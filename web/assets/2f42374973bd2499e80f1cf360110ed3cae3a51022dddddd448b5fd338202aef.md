# Deterministic permanence does not give class-uniform stochastic tightness

## Result and status

For one mass-action network, every positive deterministic trajectory in the positive stoichiometric class \(B+C=2\) converges to one equilibrium. The same lattice compatibility plane contains infinitely many closed, irreducible, positive-recurrent stochastic classes whose stationary laws leave every common compact set. This refutes an implication from one deterministic global attractor to tightness uniformly over stochastic communicating classes.

The CTMC construction and balance equations below are independently reconstructed exactly. The deterministic global-attractor implication is established external mathematics. Historical novelty is unknown. Astra's U record supplied only a summary, not its original proof/code packet; this reconstruction does not upgrade the source-reported fixture checks.

## Exact interfaces

The deterministic model is the autonomous mass-action ODE
\[
\dot x=\sum_r k_r x^{y_r}(y'_r-y_r),\qquad k_r=1,
\]
on \(x\in\mathbb R^4_{>0}\), with reaction diagram
\[
2C\to A+B+C\to3A+2B\to2C,
\qquad 2C\rightleftarrows2C+D.
\]
Its positive compatibility class is
\[
P=\{(a,b,c,d)>0:b+c=2\}.
\]

The stochastic model is the count-valued CTMC on \(\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4\). Specify its propensity for reaction \(y\to y'\) at count \(z\) by
\[
\lambda_{y\to y'}(z)=\prod_{j\in\{A,B,C,D\}}(z_j)_{\underline{y_j}},
\qquad (q)_{\underline m}=q(q-1)\cdots(q-m+1),
\]
with zero propensity if a reactant count is insufficient. This convention absorbs the factorial divisors into the rate constants. The count plane \(B+C=2\) is invariant. No volume limit is assumed; this is the unit-volume member of the usual density-dependent family.

The failed implication is
\[
\text{one global deterministic attractor on }P
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{a tight family of stationary laws over all closed stochastic classes in }P.
\]
The obstruction is uniformity over stochastic communicating classes. The deterministic theorem and the stochastic assertion quantify over different state-space decompositions.

## Deterministic equilibrium

The four complexes are \(2C,A+B+C,3A+2B,2C+D\), joined in one linkage class. The reaction vectors span
\[
S=\{(u_A,u_B,u_C,u_D):u_B+u_C=0\},
\]
since \((1,1,-1,0)\), \((2,1,-1,0)\), and the D-birth vector \((0,0,0,1)\) span a three-dimensional space. Thus \(B+C\) is the only linear conservation law and the deficiency is \(4-1-3=0\).

At \(x^*=(1,1,1,1)\), each reaction flux is one. The complex \(A+B+C\) has one unit of incoming and outgoing flux; \(3A+2B\) has one and one; \(2C+D\) has one and one; and \(2C\) has two incoming and two outgoing. Thus \(x^*\) is complex balanced and lies in \(P\).

Craciun's proof of the Global Attractor Conjecture applies: a complex-balanced mass-action system has a global attractor in each positive stoichiometric compatibility class. Anderson's earlier single-linkage-class result is another sufficient theorem for this network. Neither result concerns the stochastic CTMC.

Its scope assumes a finite endotactic diagram, a supplied compact positive rate interval, a fixed positive class, and arbitrary measurable rate paths; it gives a class-specific compact absorber, with entry bound depending on the initial point.

## Exact stochastic obstruction

For each integer \(n\ge0\), define
\[
\Gamma_n=\{s_1(d),s_2(d),s_3(d):d\ge0\},
\]
with
\[
s_1(d)=(n,0,2,d),\qquad
s_2(d)=(n+1,1,1,d),\qquad
s_3(d)=(n+3,2,0,d).
\]
Every state has \(B+C=2\).

At \(s_1(d)\), the only enabled cycle reaction is \(2C\to A+B+C\), with rate \((2)_{\underline2}=2\); the D-birth and D-death rates are \(2\) and \(2d\). At \(s_2(d)\), the only enabled cycle reaction is \(A+B+C\to3A+2B\), with rate \(n+1\). At \(s_3(d)\), the only enabled cycle reaction is \(3A+2B\to2C\), with rate
\[
(n+3)_{\underline3}(2)_{\underline2}
=2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3).
\]
These transitions stay in \(\Gamma_n\). The phase cycle proceeds forward with positive rate, and at phase \(s_1\), D can move both up and down between all nonnegative counts. Thus \(\Gamma_n\) is closed and irreducible.

Set
\[
w_1=\frac12,\qquad
w_2=\frac1{n+1},\qquad
w_3=\frac1{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)},\qquad
q_d=\frac{e^{-1}}{d!}.
\]
The phase-edge stationary fluxes agree:
\[
w_1\cdot2=w_2\cdot(n+1)
=w_3\cdot2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)=1.
\]
The D birth/death fluxes balance because
\[
q_d\cdot2=q_{d+1}\cdot2(d+1).
\]
Therefore
\[
\pi_n\{s_i(d)\}=\frac{w_i}{w_1+w_2+w_3}\,q_d
\]
satisfies the stationary balance equations at every state. It is a probability measure because the phase weights normalize and \(\sum_d q_d=1\).

The process is nonexplosive. The phase-transition rates have a finite upper bound depending on fixed \(n\); D immigration is bounded by rate 2, and each D death reduces D. On a bounded time interval there are almost surely finitely many births, phase transitions, and deaths. An irreducible nonexplosive CTMC with an invariant probability is positive recurrent. Hence every \(\Gamma_n\) is an infinite positive-recurrent class.

Under \(\pi_n\),
\[
\Pr_{\pi_n}(A\ge n)=1,\qquad \mathbb E_{\pi_n}A\ge n.
\]
For any compact \(K\subset\mathbb R^4_{\ge0}\), let \(M_K=\sup_{x\in K}x_A<\infty\). Then \(\pi_n(K)=0\) for each integer \(n>M_K\). The family is exactly non-tight, not merely unbounded in mean. Each \(\Gamma_n\) contains the strictly positive state \((n+1,1,1,1)\).

The nonlinear quantity \(A-B(B+1)/2\) equals \(n\) on \(\Gamma_n\) and is preserved by every enabled cycle transition there. It is not a linear stoichiometric conservation law and is not invariant under the deterministic ODE. This hidden stochastic partition is the mechanism separating the models.

## Density-dependent scaling

The obstruction persists at every fixed integer volume \(V\ge1\) under the P-card intensity convention
\[
\lambda_r^V(z)=V^{1-|y_r|}\prod_j(z_j)_{\underline{y_{rj}}}.
\]
The class \(\Gamma_n\) remains closed and irreducible. Its cycle rates become \(2/V\), \((n+1)/V^2\), and \(2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)/V^4\). D births occur at rate \(2/V\), and D deaths at rate \(2d/V^2\).

The complex-balanced point in the corresponding deterministic concentration class \(b+c=2/V\) is
\[
x_V^*=(1,1/V,1/V,1).
\]
Indeed, all five deterministic reaction fluxes agree at the complex-balance nodes, and the global-attractor theorem gives convergence from every positive point in that class.

For the CTMC, restrict the product-Poisson weights with mean \(V\) to \(\Gamma_n\). The conditional distribution of D is Poisson with mean V and is independent of the phase. The three phase weights, after removing a common factor, are
\[
u_1=1,\qquad u_2=\frac{2V}{n+1},\qquad
u_3=\frac{V^3}{(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}.
\]
Their adjacent fluxes match: \(u_1(2/V)=u_2((n+1)/V^2)\) and \(u_2((n+1)/V^2)=u_3(2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)/V^4)\). The D birth/death ratio is \(q_{d+1}/q_d=V/(d+1)\), which also balances. Thus the same exact stationary law exists at every fixed V. In concentration coordinates it is supported on \(A/V\ge n/V\); for every compact K and fixed V, its mass on K is zero as soon as n exceeds V times the maximum A-coordinate of K.

This extension removes an objection based only on setting V=1. It still does not give a fixed-positive limiting concentration class as V tends to infinity: the conserved concentration \(B+C=2/V\) approaches the boundary. It is a class-uniform obstruction at each finite V, not a counterexample to the usual fixed-initial-concentration law of large numbers.

## Quantitative claim

**SS-1 (internally reconstructed).** For the specified network and count CTMC, every deterministic trajectory from \(P\) converges to \(x^*=(1,1,1,1)\). For each \(n\ge0\), there is a stationary law \(\pi_n\) supported on a closed irreducible class \(\Gamma_n\), and
\[
\forall M<\infty,\ \forall n\in\mathbb N,\quad
n>M\Longrightarrow\pi_n\{A\le M\}=0.
\]
Thus \((\pi_n)\) is not tight on the fixed stochastic compatibility plane. This is exact for every \(n\), with no finite-volume approximation.

The claim does not say that a fixed \(\pi_n\) remains far from the deterministic equilibrium after rescaling by a large volume, or that stationary laws fail to exist. It also does not contradict finite-time convergence of density-dependent chains to ODE trajectories. It blocks exchanging the stationary/infinite-time limit with a supremum over all stochastic communicating classes.

## Measurable falsifier and comparator

An idealized single-cell experiment can test this model-specific prediction. Implement or emulate the five channels above, preserve the molecule count \(B+C=2\), prepare the positive seed \((n+1,1,1,1)\) for successive \(n\), and measure A, B, C, D at single-molecule resolution across replicate trajectories. First calibrate each propensity and verify that the reachable A/B/C phases are exactly the three displayed states. The predicted stationary phase probabilities are \(w_i/(w_1+w_2+w_3)\); conditional on any phase, D is Poisson with mean one; and every sample has \(A\ge n\). For each fixed cutoff M, when \(n>M\) the predicted stationary fraction with \(A\le M\) is exactly zero. A reproducible positive fraction, or calibrated phase fluxes inconsistent with the displayed rates, falsifies this CTMC realization.

Use the same diagram, deterministic rate constants, and positive initial conditions in the mass-action ODE as the equally informed baseline; its trajectories converge to \(x^*\), independent of n. Record molecule counts, dwell times, volume convention, propensities, and run length. The phase-3 propensity grows as \(2n^3+O(n^2)\), so high-n turnover and observation bandwidth are material costs. No physical synthesis or long-run experiment was performed; high-order reactions may need a carefully validated implementation.

## Costs, unresolved gates, and failure scope

The mathematical input is a five-reaction diagram, exact rate constants, the propensity convention, a fixed count plane, and a positive initial state. The proof uses direct finite algebra for each n and no data-acquisition algorithm. It gives no mixing-time bound or uniform stochastic Lyapunov function. Experimentally, channel realization, single-molecule acquisition, conservation checks, high-order propensity calibration, equilibration, and turnover are charged.

The U fixture checks and historical priority remain unverified. The physical prediction is conditional on the calibrated ideal reaction model. The unresolved bridge is to identify a condition on stochastic communicating classes, reaction-lattice geometry, or a uniform Foster–Lyapunov drift that rules out these escaping stationary laws while preserving deterministic classwise attraction. No such condition is proved here.
