INTERFACE AND QUANTIFIERS
Hermitian matrices carry the real Hilbert-Schmidt inner product <X,Y>=Tr(XY). I denotes the identity superoperator when placed in a channel inequality. The assertion I-Gamma <= 2(I-Phi) means
  Tr[X(X-Gamma(X))] <= 2 Tr[X(X-Phi(X))]
for every Hermitian X, using one Gamma for all X. This is not an inequality of completely positive maps and not an observable-dependent choice of measurement.
A canonical pure-state EB channel in dimension d has
  Gamma(X)=d integral rho Tr(rho X) dmu(rho),
where rho is pure and integral rho dmu=I/d. It is unital, trace preserving, EB, and HS self-adjoint. The target-2 comparator may depend on Phi, but must be common to all observables. Continuous Haar orbit measures are permitted; a finite ensemble also exists by a convexity argument below.
Self-compatible means a CPTP broadcast map B exists with both output marginals equal to Phi. No assumption that its range lies in the symmetric output subspace is made.

TARGET 1: ALL SPINS, EIGENVALUES AND SHARP C=2
Write n=2j in the nonnegative integers, H_n=Sym^n(C^2), d_n=n+1. Let V:H_(2n)->H_n tensor H_n be the canonical maximal-spin isometry. Define
  A_n(X)=V* (X tensor I) V,
  A_n*(Y)=Tr_2(V Y V*).
The broadcast map in the question is B_j(X)=(d_n/d_(2n)) V A_n(X) V*. Its marginal is therefore
  Phi_j=(d_n/d_(2n)) A_n* A_n.                     (1)
This already proves HS self-adjointness and nonnegative harmonic eigenvalues. Trace preservation follows from the SU(2)-invariant identity Tr_2(VV*)=(d_(2n)/d_n)I. The range is maximal total spin 2j, whose dimension is 4j+1, and is symmetric under swapping the output systems.

Let rho_omega^(n)=|omega;n><omega;n| be the spin-coherent projector and normalized sphere measure have total mass one. Define Q_n(X)(omega)=Tr(rho_omega^(n)X); Q_n* f=integral rho_omega^(n)f(omega) domega. By Schur's lemma, integral rho_omega^(n) domega=I/d_n and the coherent canonical EB channel is
  Gamma_j=d_n Q_n*Q_n.                            (2)
Under conjugation, End(H_n) decomposes multiplicity-free as ranks ell=0,...,n; Q_n maps rank ell into the spherical harmonics of the same degree. Its integral kernel is
  (Q_n Q_n* f)(omega)=integral ((1+omega.dot.omega')/2)^n f(omega') domega'.
The Funk-Hecke eigenvalue on degree ell is
  q_ell(n)=1/2 integral_(-1)^1 ((1+t)/2)^n P_ell(t) dt
          =(n!)^2/[(n-ell)!(n+ell+1)!],           (3)
for 0<=ell<=n, and zero above n. To derive (3), insert Rodrigues' formula, integrate by parts ell times (boundary terms vanish), and use the beta integral. All q_ell(n)>0 in the represented ranks, so Q_n is injective. Set
  C_ell(n)=d_n q_ell(n)
          =n!(n+1)!/[(n-ell)!(n+ell+1)!].          (4)
Thus Gamma_j has eigenvalue C_ell(n).

The maximal-spin coherent vector factorizes exactly:
  V|omega;2n>=|omega;n> tensor |omega;n>.
Consequently
  Q_(2n) A_n = Q_n.                              (5)
On rank ell the squared singular values of Q_n and Q_(2n) are q_ell(n) and q_ell(2n). Because each rank has multiplicity one, (5) fixes the squared singular value of A_n to their ratio. Substitution in (1) gives
  lambda_ell(Phi_j) = (d_n/d_(2n)) q_ell(n)/q_ell(2n)
                   = C_ell(n)/C_ell(2n).          (6)
In the question's spin notation this is exactly c_ell(j)/c_ell(2j), with c_ell(j)=C_ell(2j). Formula (6) was derived, not assumed.

For the inequality, fix 1<=ell<=n, write a=C_ell(n), b=C_ell(2n), and use
  1/a=product_(r=1)^ell (n+r+1)/(n-r+1),
  1/b=product_(r=1)^ell (2n+r+1)/(2n-r+1).
Put u_r=2r/(n-r+1)>=0. Since 2n-r+1 >= 2(n-r+1),
  (2n+r+1)/(2n-r+1) <= 1+u_r/2.
Expanding products into subsets yields
  product_r(1+u_r/2) <= [1+product_r(1+u_r)]/2,
because every nonempty subset coefficient 2^(-|S|) is at most 1/2. Therefore
  2/b <= 1+1/a,
  1+a >= 2a/b,
  1-C_ell(n) <= 2[1-C_ell(n)/C_ell(2n)].           (7)
The rank-zero inequality is zero=zero. Equations (4),(6),(7), summed against the squared HS norms of the full Hermitian rank components, prove the target for every j. For j=0 it is trivial. For every j>0, rank ell=1 has
  C_1(n)=n/(n+2), lambda_1=(n+1)/(n+2),
and equality in (7). Hence 2 is sharp for this fixed coherent comparator. For ell>=2 the inequality is strict.

TARGET 2: EXACT PURE-ORBIT SCORE GEOMETRY AT SPIN 3/2
Let d=4 and let Pi_ell be the real HS orthogonal projector onto rank ell, ell=0,1,2,3. For a pure seed rho, twirl its canonical channel under SU(2):
  Gamma_rho(X)=4 integral rho_g Tr(rho_g X) dg,
  rho_g=U_g rho U_g*.
Schur averaging gives harmonic score
  g_ell=4/(2ell+1) ||Pi_ell rho||_2^2.              (8)
Every covariant canonical pure-state channel is an orbit mixture. Conversely, twirling any canonical comparator preserves both its canonical form and the inequality against covariant Phi. Thus orbit-mixture scores characterize the full relevant comparator class.

Let T be spin time reversal. It satisfies T^2=-I on spin 3/2 and sends rank-ell Hermitians to (-1)^ell times themselves. For a pure rho=|psi><psi|, Kramers orthogonality gives Tr(rho T rho T^-1)=0. The even and odd rank parts are orthogonal, so purity and Kramers orthogonality imply
  ||Pi_0 rho||^2+||Pi_2 rho||^2=1/2,
  ||Pi_1 rho||^2+||Pi_3 rho||^2=1/2.
Since ||Pi_0 rho||^2=1/4,
  ||Pi_2 rho||^2=1/4, hence g_2=1/5.              (9)
Also Tr(J_a J_b)=5 delta_ab, hence
  ||Pi_1 rho||^2=|<J>|^2/5,
  g_1=4|<J>|^2/15 in [0,3/5],
  3g_1+7g_3=2.                                  (10)
Every point in this interval is attained by
  |psi_p>=sqrt(p)|3/2,3/2>+sqrt(1-p)|3/2,-3/2>,
  g_1=(3/5)(2p-1)^2.
Consequently the exact covariant canonical score set is the line segment
  (g_1,g_2,g_3)=(x,1/5,(2-3x)/7), 0<=x<=3/5.     (11)
Mixtures add no new scores beyond this segment. In particular, a given harmonic vector lambda admits the required C=2 canonical comparator if and only if
  lambda_1<=4/5, lambda_2<=3/5,
  lambda_3<=9/14, 3lambda_1+7lambda_3<=6.         (12)
To see sufficiency, take x=max(0,2lambda_1-1). Necessity follows directly by eliminating x from g_ell>=2lambda_ell-1.

COMPATIBILITY BOUNDS: AN EXACT THREE-SPIN MATRIX CERTIFICATE
Start with any self-compatible Phi and symmetrize/twirl a broadcast Choi state, so it is invariant under SU(2) and the output swap. Conjugate the reference input by the unitary part of time reversal, converting its dual representation to another spin-3/2 copy. The normalized state R now acts on three identical spins A,B,C, is SU(2)-invariant, is output-swap invariant, and has unit trace. Conversely, every such state has reference marginal I/4 and is a valid normalized broadcast Choi state. General swap invariance, including the antisymmetric output sector, is retained.

Choose Hermitian orthonormal tensors T_(ell,a) spanning rank ell. Define two-spin score operators
  W_ell = [4(-1)^ell/(2ell+1)] sum_a T_(ell,a) tensor T_(ell,a).
The Choi identity gives lambda_ell=Tr(R W_ell^(AC))=Tr(R W_ell^(BC)). Hence it also equals the expectation of
  G_ell=(W_ell^(AC)+W_ell^(BC))/2.                 (13)
For the pair AC, total pair spin J=0,1,2,3 diagonalizes W_ell. Its eigenvalue table h_ell(J), with rows ell and columns J, is
  ell=0:   1,       1,        1,       1
  ell=1:   1,      11/15,     1/5,    -3/5
  ell=2:   1,       1/5,     -3/5,     1/5
  ell=3:   1,      -3/5,      1/5,    -1/35.       (14)
Independent normalization derivation of this table: put S=J_A.dot.J_C. Then W_1=-4S/15. For Q_ab=(J_aJ_b+J_bJ_a)/2-(5/4)delta_ab I, Tr(Q_ab Q_cd)=3(delta_ac delta_bd+delta_ad delta_bc-(2/3)delta_ab delta_cd). Thus sum_ab Q_ab tensor Q_ab=6 sum_a T_(2,a) tensor T_(2,a), and
  W_2=(2/15)[S^2+S/2-75I/16].
Completeness of the tensor basis gives
  I-3W_1+5W_2-7W_3=4F,
where the pair swap F has eigenvalue (-1)^(3-J). Evaluating these identities on S=[J(J+1)-15/2]/2 gives all rows of (14), without presupposing any channel harmonic profile. Equivalently h_ell(J)=4(-1)^(3+J+ell){3/2 3/2 ell;3/2 3/2 J}.

Couple output AB first to spin L, then with reference C to total K. Allowed K are 1/2,3/2,5/2,7/2,9/2. Swap parity is (-1)^(3-L), so each K multiplicity block separates into even and odd L. Up to harmless row/column phase choices, the orthogonal recoupling matrix is
  U^(K)_(L,J)=sqrt[(2L+1)(2J+1)] {3/2 3/2 L;3/2 K J}.
The matrix of G_ell in a fixed K, fixed L-parity block is the corresponding submatrix of
  U^(K) diag_J[h_ell(J)] (U^(K))^T.               (15)
The cross-parity entries cancel in (13). There are six scalar blocks and three 2x2 blocks, listed completely below. This covers all 64 Hilbert-space dimensions (the carrier multiplicities are 2,4,3,2,1).

Scalar blocks (K;L -> G_1,G_2,G_3):
  1/2;1 -> 1/3, -2/5, 0
  1/2;2 -> 3/5, 0, -2/5
  5/2;2 -> 1/15, 0, -6/35
  7/2;2 -> -2/5, 0, 1/35
  7/2;3 -> 0, -2/5, 1/7
  9/2;3 -> -3/5, 1/5, -1/35.

For K=3/2, L=(0,2),
  G_1=G_3=diag(0,2/5),
  G_2=(1/5) [[0,sqrt(5)],[sqrt(5),0]].
For K=3/2, L=(1,3),
  G_1=diag(2/15,4/5),
  G_2=(1/25)[[8,sqrt(21)],[sqrt(21),12]],
  G_3=(1/35)[[0,4sqrt(21)],[4sqrt(21),6]].
For K=5/2, L=(1,3),
  G_1=diag(-1/5,7/15),
  G_2=(1/25)[[-2,2sqrt(14)],[2sqrt(14),-3]],
  G_3=(1/35)[[0,-2sqrt(14)],[-2sqrt(14),-9]].     (16)

All matrices are exact rational/radical values from (15). Their spectra immediately give
  G_1 <= (4/5)I,
  G_2 <= (3/5)I,
  G_3 <= [(3+sqrt(345))/35]I < (9/14)I,
  3G_1+7G_3 <= 6I.                              (17)
In particular, the only rank-3 block capable of exceeding 2/5 is K=3/2,L=(1,3), with eigenvalues (3+-sqrt(345))/35. The other nontrivial rank-3 block has eigenvalues (-9+-sqrt(305))/70.
For transparency, the three 2x2 blocks of 3G_1+7G_3 have spectra {0,4}, {-2,6}, {-2,1}; the scalar spectra are among {-2,-1,1}. In the decisive K=3/2,L=(1,3) block,
  (3/5)I-G_2=(1/25)[[7,-sqrt(21)],[-sqrt(21),3]],
  6I-3G_1-7G_3=(1/5)[[28,-4sqrt(21)],[-4sqrt(21),12]],
both positive semidefinite with zero determinant and nonnegative diagonal. Thus no floating tolerance or empirical spectrum is doing the proof work.
Taking expectations in R proves all four inequalities (12). The bound on G_3 is stronger than needed.

COMMON COMPARATOR CONSTRUCTION
For the given Phi, set x=max(0,2lambda_1-1), p=(1+sqrt(5x/3))/2, and take the SU(2) orbit of |psi_p> above. Its barycenter is I/4, so it gives a valid canonical EB channel. Equations (11),(12) imply g_ell>=2lambda_ell-1 for ell=1,2,3; rank zero is equality. Orthogonal harmonic decomposition proves I-Gamma<=2(I-Phi) on every Hermitian X using this same Gamma. The construction uses only lambda_1 to choose its seed. No observable access or per-query measurement choice is used.
A finite ensemble realizing precisely the same Gamma exists: the Haar second moment is in the convex hull of |psi psi><psi psi| on Sym^2(C^4), a 10-dimensional space. Caratheodory in the trace-one Hermitian affine space (dimension 99) gives at most 100 atoms. Its partial trace also fixes the required barycenter. This is an existence assertion; no efficient exact quadrature algorithm or gate synthesis is claimed.
Sharpness within the full canonical class: take the maximal-spin cloner at j=3/2. Its lambda_1=4/5 and every canonical comparator has g_1<=3/5. Thus any uniform constant C must satisfy (1-3/5)<=C(1-4/5), or C>=2. Target 2 has sharp C=2.

FAILED SHORTCUT / EXPLICIT COUNTEREXAMPLE
The maximal-spin cloner at j=3/2 has harmonic vector (4/5,12/25,6/35). It does not dominate all compatible profiles. In the K=3/2,L=(1,3) multiplicity block, use the unit vector
  v=(sqrt(3/10),sqrt(7/10)).
The invariant extension state with multiplicity density |v><v| and carrier state I_4/4 is positive, trace one, and supported on the symmetric AB sector. Equations (16) give
  v*G_1v=v*G_2v=v*G_3v=3/5.
Its channel is the d=4 universal depolarizing cloner marginal. This exceeds maximal-spin cloner lambda_2=12/25 and lambda_3=6/35. Its valid common comparator has g=(1/5,1/5,1/5). Any proof invoking simultaneous maximal-spin cloner harmonic dominance has a serious false premise, although the target theorem survives by (17).
