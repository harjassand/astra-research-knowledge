STRONGEST CLAIM

Let (A,tau) be any finite-dimensional tracial C*-algebra and let Phi:A->A
be UCP, tau-preserving, positive and selfadjoint on L2(tau), with a supplied
two-output compatible UCP map J:A tensor A->A satisfying
J(a tensor I)=J(I tensor a)=Phi(a). For Hermitian contractions A,C and every
integer m>=1, define the spectral measure mu_A(S)=<A,1_S(Phi)A>_2 and

  Q_(m-1)(A)=integral_(0,1] (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2m-2) d mu_A(lambda).

Then the entire CP power range obeys the input-sensitive bound

  ||[Phi^m(A),Phi^m(C)]||_2
     <= 2 sqrt(min{Q_(m-1)(A),Q_(m-1)(C)}).                 (1)

In particular, with d_0=1 and d_k=k^k/(k+1)^(k+1) for k>=1,

  sup_(A,C Hermitian contractions)||[Phi^m(A),Phi^m(C)]||_2
     <= 2 sqrt(d_(m-1)) = 2/sqrt(e m)+O(m^(-3/2)).          (2)

The fixed spectral space contributes zero. A dyadic refinement specifies
which spectral bands of each input carry the remaining commutator defect.
This is an internal mathematical derivation; novelty/priority is unassessed.

FULL DERIVATION

For any Hermitian contractions x,y, take a Stinespring dilation
J(z)=V* pi(z)V, put P=VV*, Q=I-P, and write
pi_1(x)=pi(x tensor I), pi_2(y)=pi(I tensor y). These represented tensor
factors commute. Define R_x=Q pi_1(x)V and R_y=Q pi_2(y)V. The marginal
identities imply

  Phi(x)=V*pi_1(x)V,     Phi(y)=V*pi_2(y)V,
  R_x*R_x=Phi(x^2)-Phi(x)^2,   R_y*R_y=Phi(y^2)-Phi(y)^2.

With F=R_x*R_y, inserting P+Q between the commuting represented factors
gives the exact identity

  [Phi(x),Phi(y)]=F* - F.

Hence ||[Phi(x),Phi(y)]||_2<=2||F||_2. Schatten multiplication gives
||F||_2<=||x||_infinity sqrt(tau(R_y*R_y)); taking adjoints gives
||F||_2<=||y||_infinity sqrt(tau(R_x*R_x)). Thus the smaller of the two
Kadison-defect bounds applies.

Now set x=Phi^(m-1)(A), y=Phi^(m-1)(C). UCP contractivity makes both operator
norms at most one. Trace preservation and selfadjointness give

  tau(Phi(x^2)-Phi(x)^2)
    =||x||_2^2-||Phi(x)||_2^2
    =integral (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2m-2) d mu_A(lambda),

and the same formula for C. This proves (1) directly, without comparing
Phi^m(A) to Phi^(m+1)(A).

For the band form, let E_j=1_[1-2^(-j),1-2^(-(j+1)))(Phi), j>=0, and
E_*=1_{ {1} }(Phi). With a_j(A)=||E_j A||_2 and
w_(j,k)=sup_(lambda in [1-2^(-j),1-2^(-(j+1)))
                 (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2k),

  Q_k(A)<=sum_(j>=0) w_(j,k) a_j(A)^2,
  w_(j,k)<=2^(1-j) exp(-k 2^(-j)).

The fixed band E_* has zero weight. The band projections are Hilbert-space
projections, not CP maps, so this refinement is a bound and does not itself
construct a channel.

Finally, maximizing (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2k) on [0,1] gives d_k; the
maximizer for k>=1 is lambda^2=k/(k+1). Since ||A||_2,||C||_2<=1, (2)
follows. Full derivation, including a two-band corollary and comparison with
iterated power dilations, is in
work/cycle6/c05_l03/power_range_spectral_bands.txt.

no acquisition or search cost for J or its Stinespring representation is

Phi. The proof is dimension-free and has no precision dependence. It does not

Pairwise approximate commutation of a selected family remains insufficient,
as the flagged Clifford example shows; the present estimate controls every
pair in the full power range but does not perform the missing uniform EB lift.
No counterexample to that lifting step is obtained. The general broadcasting
reconstruction problem remains open.
