# Measurement and inverse-problem research: cost must follow the observation law

## Verdict

The strongest result from this lane is a precise warning about how sensing guarantees translate into physical cost. The 2026 complex phase-retrieval bound \(\beta_{\ell_2}\ge2\) is for unnormalised Euclidean error in *squared* intensities. Equality characterizes a weighted projective 2-design and costs at least \(d^2\) rows. That fact does not imply a quadratic detector or photon requirement under shot noise.

For a normalized pure state \(x\in\mathbb C^d\), a complex Gaussian frame can be polar-whitened into a Parseval frame \(B\), whose rows define a complete one-photon POVM. With \(m=O(d)\) outcomes, its square-root outcome probabilities have a dimension-independent lower separation in phase distance. The global minimum-Hellinger estimator then has uniform mean squared phase error \(O(d/N)\) from \(N\) incident photons. The rate matches the Haar-average quantum state-estimation lower bound in dimension and sample size, even against collective measurements. With known uniform detector efficiency \(\eta\), both sides scale as \(d/(N\eta)\).

This is a conditional mathematical result and a literature-based synthesis, not a priority-cleared discovery or a hardware demonstration. The direct estimator has an exponential finite-net implementation; finite-bit calibration, unknown gains/losses, dark counts, mode mismatch, optical depth, and source energy are not covered. The reusable lesson is narrower than “more channels are better”: for photon counts, use the likelihood-induced Hellinger metric, not an unrelated adversarial residual norm.

## Model and instrument

The unknown is a fixed unit pure state \(x\), up to global phase, in a known \(d\)-dimensional complex mode. Draw an \(m\times d\) matrix \(A\) with standard circular complex-Gaussian rows and define
\[
G=A/\sqrt m,\qquad S=G^*G,\qquad B=GS^{-1/2}=A(A^*A)^{-1/2}.
\]
On the source event, \(S\) is positive definite and \(B^*B=I\). If row \(j\) of \(B\) is \(b_j^*\), the effects
\[
E_j=b_jb_j^*,\qquad j=1,\ldots,m
\]
are positive, rank one, and sum to identity. A single photon produces one of the \(m\) channel labels with probability \(p_j(x)=|b_j^*x|^2\); no outcome is discarded. An isometry from \(d\) input modes to \(m\) outputs can be embedded in an \(m\)-mode unitary, followed by one counter per output. This specifies a valid ideal instrument, not a fabricated or benchmarked device.

Write \(d_\phi(x,y)=\min_{|c|=1}\|x-cy\|_2\). Xia, Xu, and Xu prove that, for fixed \(0<\zeta<0.05\), a complex Gaussian matrix has magnitude lower modulus at least \(1/\beta_0-\zeta\), where \(\beta_0=\sqrt{4/(4-\pi)}\), with failure probability at most \(4e^{-c_2\zeta^2m}\) once \(m\gtrsim \zeta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)d\). Their covariance bound also gives \(\lambda_{\max}(S)\le1+\zeta\) except with probability \(2e^{-c_1\zeta^2m}\). On the intersection,
\[
\bigl\||Bx|-|By|\bigr\|_2\ge \ell_\zeta d_\phi(x,y),\qquad
\ell_\zeta=\frac{1/\beta_0-\zeta}{\sqrt{1+\zeta}}.
\]
The whitening step follows by applying the source modulus to \(S^{-1/2}x,S^{-1/2}y\) and using the smallest singular value of \(S^{-1/2}\). The combined success probability is at least \(1-6e^{-c\zeta^2m}\) after adjusting the universal constant. For fixed \(\zeta\), \(m=C_\zeta d\) outcomes suffice. The source hides potentially large constants.

## Photon law and estimator

For \(N\) fixed independent incident photons, the full channel counts are multinomial, \(Y\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(N,p(x))\), with \(\sum_jY_j=N\). Use squared Hellinger distance \(H^2(p,q)=\sum_j(\sqrt{p_j}-\sqrt{q_j})^2\). The empirical frequencies \(\hat p=Y/N\) obey
\[
\mathbb E_x H^2(\hat p,p(x))\le(m-1)/N.
\]
For each positive channel probability this follows from \((\sqrt q-\sqrt p)^2\le(q-p)^2/p\) and the multinomial variance; a zero-probability channel contributes zero. A global minimum-Hellinger fit
\[
\hat x\in\arg\min_{\|z\|=1\ \mathrm{mod\ phase}}H(\hat p,p(z))
\]
exists by compactness. Since \(H(p(x),p(y))=\||Bx|-|By|\|_2\), the modulus and the triangle inequality give
\[
\sup_{\|x\|=1}\mathbb E_x d_\phi(\hat x,x)^2
\le \min\left\{2,\frac{4(m-1)}{N\ell_\zeta^2}\right\}.
\]
Thus a fixed-stability frame with \(m=\Theta(d)\) has \(O(d/N)\) risk, and \(N=O(d/\epsilon^2)\) incident photons suffice for expected squared phase error at most \(\epsilon^2\), up to the displayed constants.

This is an estimator existence and risk result, not a polynomial-runtime theorem. One explicit finite algorithm enumerates a \(\gamma\)-net of projective state space and minimizes the empirical Hellinger distance over it. It uses at most \((C/\gamma)^{2d}\) candidates and \(O(md)\) arithmetic per candidate; its risk is at most \(8(m-1)/(N\ell_\zeta^2)+2\gamma^2/\ell_\zeta^2\). No efficient solver with the same uniform guarantee is established.

If the incident flux is Poisson with mean \(\tau\), retaining every output gives independent \(Y_j\sim\mathrm{Pois}(\tau p_j(x))\) and exactly \(\tau\) expected total incident photons. The full count laws have the exact affinity
\[
\operatorname{BC}(P_x,P_y)
=\exp[-\tau H^2(p(x),p(y))/2]
\le \exp[-\tau\ell_\zeta^2d_\phi(x,y)^2/2].
\]
The likelihood-ratio test for two specified states therefore has each error at most \(\alpha\) once \(\tau\ge2\log(1/\alpha)/(\ell_\zeta^2\epsilon^2)\) for separation at least \(\epsilon\). This pairwise result is not a continuous-state guarantee; the multinomial MDE bound supplies the latter.

For known state-independent detection efficiency \(\eta\), the no-click category is common to all states and scales the Hellinger metric by \(\sqrt\eta\). The upper risk is at most \(4m/(N\eta\ell_\zeta^2)\) for \(N\) launched photons. For the strongest lower comparator, let \(K\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(N,\eta)\) be the detected count. Conditional on \(K\), Bruß–Macchiavello's optimal Haar-mean fidelity bound is \((K+1)/(K+d)\), even with arbitrary collective measurements. Since \(f(k)=(k+1)/(k+d)\) is concave,
\[
\mathbb E d_\phi^2\ge\mathbb E(1-F)\ge1-f(N\eta)=\frac{d-1}{N\eta+d}.
\]
At \(N\eta\gtrsim d\) this matches the upper order \(d/(N\eta)\), counting launched rather than detected photons. Unknown, nonuniform loss, dark counts, and source-energy costs are excluded.

## Why the \(d^2\) row theorem is not a photon lower bound

For nonzero rows \(a_j\), Peng–Han–Huang's interface is \(\Psi_A(x)=(|a_j^*x|^2)_j\), with the ordinary unnormalised \(\ell_2\) norm on these *squared* outputs and quotient metric \(\|xx^*-yy^*\|_*\). It contains no count law, dose, variance, calibration, or readout noise. Let \(m_+\) be the number of nonzero rows and \(S_4=\sum_j\|a_j\|^4\). Their variational formula for \(L_A\), restricted to a Haar-random ordered orthonormal pair, gives
\[
\beta_A^2\ge\max\left\{4,\frac{2d(d+1)}{m_+}\right\},\quad d\ge2.
\]
Indeed \(U_A^2\ge S_4/m_+\), while the random-pair expectation bounds \(L_A^2\le S_4/[2d(d+1)]\). Constant distortion in this specific raw-intensity metric forces \(\Omega(d^2)\) rows. Scaling the rows changes signal level and shot noise without changing \(\beta_A\). Consequently the bound cannot be read as a photon or physical detector lower bound.

The exact equality case is also model-specific. For \(a_j=t_ju_j\), put \(p_j=t_j^4/\sum_k t_k^4\) and \(\Pi_j=u_ju_j^*\). For complex \(d\ge2\), \(\beta_A=2\) iff
\[
\sum_jp_j\Pi_j^{\otimes2}=\frac{2}{d(d+1)}P_{\rm sym}.
\]
The minimum number of positive-weight rows is \(d^2\); equality at \(d^2\) is a SIC. In every dimension \(d\ge2\), Jasper–Mixon's Sidon-set construction supplies exact weighted 2-designs with \(d^2+O(d^{1.525})\) rows. The conversion to phase-retrieval rows is \(a_j=p_j^{1/4}u_j\). These are short consequences of established design results, not a new design construction. The 2026 source theorem omits the edge case \(d=1\), where every nonzero scalar sensing matrix has \(\beta=1\).

The near-optimality stability attack yields a further one-way quantitative implication. Let \(F=\sum_jp_j\Pi_j^{\otimes2}\), \(F_0=2P_{\rm sym}/[d(d+1)]\), \(N_2=\binom{d+1}{2}\), and \(\rho=(\beta_A/2)^2-1\). The internal theorem is
\[
\|F-F_0\|_F^2\le
\frac{2(d+2)(d+3)}{d(d+1)}\rho\max\{1,\rho\}.
\]
The proof combines the source's dimension-two lower-constant bound on every complex two-plane, Haar averaging over planes, a one-sided quartic bound with zero Haar mean, and the fourth Haar moment of a traceless operator supported on the symmetric subspace. For \(\rho\le1\), a partial-transpose/rank argument gives the additional necessary row-count gap
\[
\rho\ge \frac{1}{C_d}\frac{d^2-m}{m(d+1)^2},\qquad
C_d=\frac{2(d+2)(d+3)}{d(d+1)},\quad m<d^2,
\]
and the frame-potential bound \(\rho\ge C_d^{-1}(1/m-1/N_2)_+\). These bounds are not known sharp. There is no uniform reverse implication from moment closeness: deleting one vector from a complete MUB design gives relative fourth-moment error \(O(1/d)\) but \(\beta_A\ge2\sqrt2\). This is an internal proof candidate with finite sanity checks, not externally reviewed.

## Exact \(\beta\) versus physical shot noise

An independent construction makes the metric mismatch concrete. Bodmann–Haas/Sidon weighted designs can be normalized as exact one-photon POVMs with \(m=q^2+d\) channels for an odd prime \(q\ge d\), using a two-dimensional finite-field Fourier isometry and a coordinate branch. The special layer symmetry allows square-root design weights, so the POVM effects sum to identity while the induced raw-intensity row weights still form the exact design; this is not true for arbitrary weighted designs. The finite QFT construction is an ideal isometry specification, not a hardware implementation or cost benchmark.

In this exact-\(\beta=2\) POVM, two different coordinate states differ only on their two coordinate outcomes. Their squared Hellinger distance is \(2/(q+1)\). Under Poisson flux \(\tau\), the exact affinity is \(e^{-\tau/(q+1)}\), so two-error testing at level \(\alpha\) requires
\[
\tau\ge\frac{q+1}{2}\log\frac{1}{4\alpha(1-\alpha)}
=\Omega(d\log(1/\alpha)).
\]
This is a counterexample to treating deterministic raw-intensity conditioning as photon efficiency. The Parseval Gaussian POVM uses \(O(d)\) outcomes and has constant Hellinger separation even in its worst direction, with the global \(O(d/N)\) risk guarantee. These compare different objectives; neither result establishes a universal hardware advantage.

## Cycle 3 continuation: efficient reconstruction and dark counts

The polynomial-estimator gate is now closed for an alternate completed-block instrument: Huang–Li–Needell (2025), Theorem 1, supplies a convex PSD least-squares estimator with a leading-eigenvector recovery bound for iid complex Poisson phase-retrieval rows. A scaled iid-Gaussian block completes exactly to an $O(d)$-outcome Parseval POVM, preserving the exact Poisson row law and giving $O(d/(\eta\varepsilon^2))$ launched-photon scaling, with residual, loss, and no-click outcomes charged. Exactly fixed source count is obtained by conditioning and has an explicit $O(\sqrt{N}e^{-cd})$ failure term. Direct transfer to the polar-normalized correlated frame remains open.

The addendum also proves that arbitrary unknown per-port Poisson dark means can mask any selected pair of orthogonal pure states when the allowed dark-count range is at least twice the detected source flux. An independent blank exposure of relative length $r$ then needs $r=\Omega(1/(N\eta))$ in the stated worst-case background class even to distinguish that pair. For common background levels, the same source paper's heavy-tailed CVX-LS theorem gives a finite calibration law from blank counts.
