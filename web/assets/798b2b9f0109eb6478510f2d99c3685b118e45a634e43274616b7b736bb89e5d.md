## Load-bearing source

Faul, Hoessly & Xia, *Identifiability of SDEs for reaction networks*.

- Published article / DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956792526100382
- Primary arXiv v3 PDF (31 March 2026): https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07638v3
- Theorem 3.3(2), PDF pp. 5–6 / arXiv extracted lines 303–365, states same source support plus cone intersections empty **for every** source as criterion for unconfoundability.
- Theorem 5.15, PDF p. 11 / lines 1012–1017, states generator-confoundability iff source supports agree and cone intersections are nonempty **for every** source.
- Proof §7.3, PDF pp. 14–15 / lines 1381–1569: equality of polynomial drift and covariance fields yields both equations (7.3) at each source by monomial independence (lines 1449–1490); conversely choose intersection witnesses independently source by source, assemble positive global rates, and sum (lines 1491–1569).
- Proposition 7.1, PDF pp. 12–13 / lines 1074–1156, transfers the generator result to the local SDE-law definition under the stated martingale-problem/uniqueness hypotheses.
- Conclusion, PDF p. 12 / lines 1064–1070, says the source analysis assumes all species observed and identifies partial observability as open; it also notes discrete transition densities are rarely closed form and ML inference is delicate.
- The published Cambridge page currently displays the same Theorem 3.3(2) wording. A targeted web search for an erratum/correction did not surface one; comprehensive author correspondence or erratum review was not done.

The corrected negation is: for same support, unconfoundability iff **there exists** a source with disjoint cones. Independent direct witness: `0→A,A→0` versus `0→2A,A→0`, with positive rates. Source-0 projected/full signatures are `(1,1)` versus `(2,4)` rays and disjoint; source-A signatures coincide at `(-1,1)`. Equality of fields would require `alpha=2 alpha'=4 alpha'`, impossible for positive `alpha'`.

## Partial-observation and finite-data comparators

1. Li, Barahona & Thomas, *Moment-based parameter inference with error guarantees for stochastic reaction networks*, J. Chem. Phys. 162, 135105 (2025), DOI https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0251744. Publisher source: https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jcp/article/162/13/135105/3342431/Moment-based-parameter-inference-with-error. The article's abstract and Introduction describe moment intervals plus convex/SDP constraints giving parameter bounds for transient and steady data, including networks with unobserved species; coverage is conditional on moment intervals containing the true moments. Its primary model is the chemical master equation/count process, not the Langevin diffusion used here.
2. Choi, Kang & Rempala, *Inferring structure and parameters of stochastic reaction networks with logistic regression*, PLoS ONE 21(2):e0341639 (2026), DOI https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341639. Primary full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12900436/. Abstract/Introduction and discussion describe likelihood-based structure/parameter inference and a partially observed SIR example combining Bayesian logistic regression with a deterministic ODE offset; stated assumptions include unique stoichiometric signatures and a pure-birth reference reaction for the regression formulation.
3. Müller, Ramaswamy & Sbalzarini, *Global parameter identification of stochastic reaction networks from single trajectories*, arXiv:1111.4785, https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.4785. The source describes noisy observations of selected species, stochastic-simulation-based Monte Carlo parameter identification, and cases where a single trajectory is used. It is a parameter-fitting comparator, not the source-cone structural theorem.
