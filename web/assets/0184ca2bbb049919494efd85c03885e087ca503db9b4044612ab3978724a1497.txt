A. GENERAL LOCAL-TABLE CLOSURE

The arbitrary-pair theorem actually extends to every nonnegative table on the
four local selections of each two-column group, allowing different single-
column weights. Let column vectors u_l,v_l be arbitrary in C^n and supply
 b_l(empty), b_l(u), b_l(v), b_l(uv) >=0.
For a subset B of all2m columns give it weight prod_l b_l(B intersect{u_l,v_l}).
Define the ambient-hole coefficient f(U) by summing this weight times
|det V_B[[n]\U]|^2 over |B|=n-|U|. Whenever not identically zero,
 h(x)=sum_U f(U)x^U
is Hurwitz stable. Consequently INITIAL's influence<=1 and sharp1/2-FLC
statements hold under all positive fields and bit pinnings, despite a local
table that fails ordinary complementary LC. This is about ambient-hole
coefficients; it does not assert natural column-label FLC.

Proof. Use the real-stable determinant
 P(x,a,b)=det(diag(x)+sum_l[a_l u_l u_l*+b_l v_l v_l*]),
multiaffine in every variable. On the two column variables for a group apply
 T(1)=b(empty), T(a)=i b(u), T(b)=i b(v), T(ab)=-b(uv),
acting as the identity elsewhere. Its two-variable algebraic symbol is
 b(empty) s t+i b(u)t+i b(v)s-b(uv).
For s=i z,t=i w with Re z,Re w>0 this equals
 -[b(empty)zw+b(u)w+b(v)z+b(uv)].
Every nonzero nonnegative bivariate multiaffine polynomial
 azw+bw+cz+d is Hurwitz stable. If a>0, solve its root equation as
 w=-(cz+d)/(az+b).
For Re z>0,
 Re[(cz+d)/(az+b)]
  =[ca|z|^2+(cb+da)Re z+db]/|az+b|^2 >=0;
its negative cannot have positive real part. If a=0, a nonzero linear sum
of positive-real-part arguments and a nonnegative constant cannot vanish;
the cases where a coefficient is zero follow directly. Thus the symbol is
complex stable. Borcea–Branden0809.0401v3 Theorem1.1(b) proves T preserves
complex stability or zero. Applying all groups gives Q(x) stable or zero.
Cauchy–Binet identifies its coefficient on x^U as i^(n-|U|)f(U). Therefore
 i^(-n)Q(i x)=h(x)
has the asserted nonnegative coefficients and Hurwitz stability. A nonzero
f coefficient ensures Q is not the zero polynomial. Tables with zero empty
weight are permitted; the whole instance may be zero, in which case no law
is claimed. If every empty weight is positive, f([n])>0 ensures nonzeroness.

This covers hard both-or-none tables, arbitrary soft parity penalties, and
arbitrary positive two-column local auxiliary filters at the ambient-hole
level. A table on a group of THREE columns does not obey this unrestricted
closure: the single group of identity columns in dimension3 with only
empty and all-three allowed gives h=x_1 x_2 x_3+1. It vanishes at all
x_i=exp(i*pi/3), whose real parts are positive. Thus rank/two-column size is
load-bearing. All claims concern algebraic distributions, not acquisition
or a blanket physical BCS log-concavity statement.

B. THE SHARP FAMILY IMPROVES THE GLOBAL ORBIT OBSTRUCTION

Let f_r be the four-terminal ambient-hole signature constructed in
SHARP_FACTOR_THREE_AND_CANONICAL_EMBEDDING.txt, with four occupied private
core sites. Put H_r(U)=f_r(U)f_r([4]\U). Then H_r(empty)=D^2,
where D=4+12r-16r^2, and its three two-hole complementary products are
 P=16r,Q=4,R=4.
For the rational Pell family r is near1/4 and D^2 exceeds all three products.
Each pairing M of four terminals has pair-flip-orbit minimum exactly its
corresponding P,Q or R. (Its orbit consists of empty, all, and the two
complementary pairs.) Thus total orbit capacity is16r+8, and demand D^2.

Take q independent blocks of this actual rational-complex eight-site matrix,
while restricting the4q private coordinates to remain occupied. Hole
signatures factor over blocks by block-diagonal complementary determinants.
Any pairing of the4q terminals having a cross-block pair has orbit minimum0:
flipping that pair makes an odd number of holes in each of two blocks.
Internal pairings have product orbit capacities. Therefore
 sum_M min_{W union of pairs of M} H_q(W)=(16r+8)^q,
 H_q(empty)=(D^2)^q.
Any nonnegative global pairing-orbit encoding satisfying
 sum_M b_M=H_q(empty),
 b_M<=C H_q(W) for every union W of pairs of M
must therefore pay C >= [D^2/(16r+8)]^q =R(r)^q.
For the fixed Q(i) instance (a,b)=(7,4), this is >=(2.9991229...)^q;
allowing the algebraic limit yields exactly3^q. The baseline's2^q lower
bound is thus strictly strengthened in the same precisely defined global
comparison class. This is not an arbitrary Dirichlet-energy lower bound.

If an even-windable signature g approximates f_q coefficientwise within K,
including the exact zeros, pair-flip invariance bounds its initial pairing
masses above by K^2 times the orbit minima while their total is at least
H_q(empty)/K^2. Consequently
 K >= R(r)^(q/4),
and the limit has K>=3^(q/4). This credits McQuillan's established
windability definition and the baseline's prior tensor proof; the increment

No statement about scalar norm approximation, localized active-block flows,

C. COEFFICIENT POWERS: A SHARP ADDITIONAL BOUNDARY

At the complex algebraic sharp limit, f(empty)=36, f(all)=1 and all two-hole
f=2. For coefficientwise powers f^t, the square-root four-hole inequality
would require 6^t<=3*2^t, equivalently3^t<=3. It fails for every t>1.
Continuity gives a violating Q(i) Pell matrix for each such t, so no universal
Hadamard-power Hurwitz closure survives beyond t=1 in the actual canonical
class. In particular, coefficient squaring fails decisively. For0<t<=1 the
already proved square-root exchange inequality itself survives powers,
since a<=sum b_j implies a^t<=(sum b_j)^t<=sum b_j^t. That coefficient
inequality alone does not establish full stability, windability, or sampling.
No all-degree closure under concave Hadamard powers is claimed.
