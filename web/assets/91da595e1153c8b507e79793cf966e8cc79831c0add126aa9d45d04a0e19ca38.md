# Spatial heterogeneity in microscopy: exact limits and a costed sampling rule

## Exact theorem: no worst-case adaptive advantage without spatial information

Partition the specimen into M distinct, equal-cost field-of-view (FOV) locations. Under the detection alternative, exactly K locations would return a target-positive call at a fixed optical resolution, exposure, spectral panel, and classification threshold. Their locations are unknown. A policy may query at most b distinct FOVs, and it may choose its next location based on previous results. Assume the positive call is perfect for this theorem and that the policy has no auxiliary location-correlated data.

Then the largest worst-case probability of finding a positive FOV is

\[
  1-\frac{\binom{M-K}{b}}{\binom{M}{b}}.
\]

Simple random sampling without replacement attains this probability for every fixed support of K positive FOVs. No adaptive policy can guarantee more over all such supports: under the uniform prior on K-subsets, after t distinct negative queries the remaining positive set is exchangeable, so the next query has conditional hit probability K/(M-t). Multiplying the conditional miss probabilities gives the same hypergeometric expression for every adaptive policy. The uniform randomized design is therefore minimax.

This is an exact model theorem, not a statement that biological clusters are uniformly random. It says that if arbitrary placement is admitted and no validated side information is supplied, spatial clustering alone does not improve the worst-case first-hit guarantee. A known cluster-shape or location prior, an independent morphology proxy, or low-resolution scout data changes the information interface and can support a different policy.

For large M, prevalence p=K/M, and a sample that is a small fraction of the specimen, the miss probability is approximately (1-p)^b, so sensitivity 1-beta requires roughly b >= log(beta)/log(1-p) ~ log(1/beta)/p. The exact finite-population threshold is the smallest b for which the displayed hypergeometric miss probability is at most beta.

If a sampled positive FOV is detected with independent sensitivity s and there are no false positives, the finite-population miss probability is

\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{\min(K,b)}
  \frac{\binom{K}{j}\binom{M-K}{b-j}}{\binom{M}{b}}(1-s)^j.
\]

With false-positive rate alpha, resolution-dependent sensitivity, spectral unmixing, and a chosen decision threshold, the test must be calibrated as a noisy hypothesis test. For iid sampled locations with positive-call probabilities q0 under no target and q1 under the target mixture, the information per location is D(Bern(q1) || Bern(q0)). Any test with both errors at most delta < 1/2 needs b D >= 2(1-2 delta)^2 by Pinsker's inequality. This is a lower bound, not a complete operating-characteristic calculation. A binomial likelihood-ratio test or a calibrated Poisson photon-count model should set the actual threshold.

## Aggregate measurements cannot recover spatial organization

Suppose an instrument retains only L channel totals Y_j, with independent Poisson counts and means equal to the spatial integrals of intensities lambda_j(x). Any two spatial intensity maps with the same L integrals induce exactly the same joint distribution of all channel totals. In particular, a homogeneous field and a rare bright cluster on a dimmer background can have identical integrated spectra. Under equal prior probabilities, every test using only these aggregate counts has error at least 1/2 for distinguishing those maps.

The conclusion concerns spatial organization. If endmember spectra and mixing assumptions are known, aggregate spectra may estimate mixture proportions; they still do not identify where those components occur. Spatially resolved acquisition, or a genuinely informative location-correlated auxiliary signal, is necessary to learn arrangement.

## Costed stratified acquisition rule

Use strata only when they are supported by information available before confirmatory sampling: a validated coarse morphology map, independently estimated regional prevalence, or a pilot with a stated sampling design. At the selected assay settings, let stratum h contain M_h distinct candidate FOVs, of which K_h would yield a detectable positive call, and let one confirmatory FOV cost c_h.

1. Acquire a coarse whole-specimen scout and compute a frozen stratum map. Charge scout acquisition, stage movement, low-resolution pixels, spectral channels, storage, and map analysis as C_scout. If the map was acquired previously, record it as supplied information and retain its actual historical cost in the study ledger.
2. Calibrate the scout on a representative pilot with paired coarse and high-resolution reads. Charge the pilot fields and analysis to C_calibration, and estimate target prevalence, cluster overdispersion, and assay sensitivity/specificity at the chosen point-spread function, exposure, and spectral panel. Use simultaneous lower confidence bounds K_lower,h (or a posterior predictive distribution) for confirmatory design. Do not treat the scout label as the target assay unless its sensitivity and specificity were validated against high-resolution reads. On a new specimen, acquire at least one randomized confirmatory field per stratum as the exploration floor, including small or scout-negative strata.
3. Set total allocation counts n_h, including the exploration floor, and spend the remaining budget to minimize calibrated miss probability subject to C_scout + C_calibration + sum_h c_h n_h <= C. Under the fixed finite-population model with conditionally uniform sampling within each stratum, the miss probability is

   \[
   \prod_h \frac{\binom{M_h-K_h}{n_h}}{\binom{M_h}{n_h}}.
   \]

   If K_h is uncertain, optimize the worst case over K_h >= K_lower,h, or optimize a preregistered posterior predictive miss probability. Recompute the allocation only from allowed scout/pilot information. Keep randomization within strata and log inclusion probabilities so prevalence estimates can be inverse-probability weighted.
4. Stop on a prespecified sequential likelihood-ratio boundary only if the FOV calls are calibrated and the error spending accounts for repeated looks. Otherwise use a fixed final allocation and fixed threshold. For every candidate hit, retain raw pixels/spectra for independent reanalysis.

For pixel pitch Delta_x, Delta_y, P_h pixels per field, L_h channels, exposure tau_h, and location-dependent stage/focus overhead, use the ledger:

```text
C_total = C_scout + C_calibration
          + sum_h n_h * (c_move,h + c_focus,h + tau_h * P_h * L_h
                         + c_readout,h + c_analysis,h).
```

Include z-stack planes in `P_h` for 3D acquisition. Finer pixel pitch, larger PSF support, more channels, and longer exposure increase pixel/channel and photon costs. They also change per-field sensitivity `s_h` and false-positive rate `alpha_h`; calibrate both for the stated optics, background, spectral unmixing, and classifier. Thus `K_h` means fields positive at the chosen resolution/noise/analysis operating point. If false-positive control requires repeat imaging or independent validation, include those acquisitions in the same budget.
For a study with multiple specimens, report both per-specimen scout costs and the calibration-pilot cost amortized across the cohort; do not treat a reusable map or model as free. If stage travel makes cost depend on the order of fields, replace the additive field-cost approximation with the planned route cost.

For the iid gamma–Poisson count model, if target-cell counts in one FOV have negative-binomial mean m_h and shape k_h, and each target cell is detected independently with sensitivity s_h, Poisson thinning gives a detected-cell count with mean s_h m_h and the same shape. The probability that a sampled FOV contains at least one detected target cell is then

\[
  q_h=1-(1+s_hm_h/k_h)^{-k_h}.
\]

If FOVs sampled from one specimen remain spatially dependent, independent-NB multiplication is not valid. Use the finite-population hypergeometric design when the positive-FOV map or counts are known, or estimate joint spatial dependence from pilot data and simulate the full design. The iid formula should not be advertised as a cluster-robust theorem.

## Equal-budget illustration (model calculation only)

The included script considers M=1000 equal-cost high-resolution FOVs: a validated high-risk stratum has M_H=100, K_H=10, and a lower-risk stratum has M_L=900, K_L=1. A coarse scout, its analysis, and calibration cost eight high-resolution-FOV equivalents. With total budget 28, the uniform plan can acquire 28 high-resolution fields; a stratified plan has 20 confirmatory fields after scout and calibration. Exact finite-population probabilities are:

- Uniform, 28 fields: detection probability 0.269474.
- Uniform, only the 20 fields left after an equal-cost scout: detection probability 0.200173.
- Costed stratified design, exact best allocation subject to at least one field per stratum: 19 high-risk and 1 lower-risk field; detection probability 0.891608.

The improvement follows from the assumed 10% versus 0.111% detectable-positive-FOV rates and the valid scout/calibration map. It is not a claim about any particular tissue. If the proxy misses a hidden compartment or the pilot underestimates its target rate, the apparent advantage can disappear; the uniform minimax theorem remains the fallback guarantee. A production implementation should replace the known K_h values with simultaneous pilot uncertainty bounds and incorporate false-positive costs.

## Primary literature and scope

**Baker et al., “In silico tissue generation and power analysis for spatial omics,” Nature Methods 20, 393–406 (2023), [doi:10.1038/s41592-023-01766-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-023-01766-6).** This study fits a gamma–Poisson/negative-binomial model to per-FOV cell counts to capture spatial overdispersion, and a beta–binomial model for regional cell sampling. Its examples show that abundance, FOV size, local adjacency, and spatial resolution affect detection power. In one 3%-abundance example, 100 randomly sampled cells yield about 80% chance of observing at least one; the 1%-area-FOV example also reports three FOVs for 80% detection. The parameters come from pilot data or literature estimates. The authors report deviations where cell density was uneven and state that the marker combination is assumed to label cell types with 100% accuracy. These are model-conditioned predictions, not a general guarantee about biological tissue.

**Müller et al., “Adaptive particle representation of fluorescence microscopy images,” Nature Communications 9, 5187 (2018), [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-07390-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-07390-9).** Their reconstruction method places particles at variable local resolution and gives a user-controlled image reconstruction bound from an already acquired pixel image. It reports substantial storage and processing reductions on its benchmarks, and analyzes how noise limits reconstruction accuracy. This is useful for charging downstream representation/analysis, but it does not prove that an instrument can skip field acquisition and still find an unseen rare cluster.

**Cruz-Roa et al., “High-throughput adaptive sampling for whole-slide histopathology image analysis (HASHI) via convolutional neural networks,” PLOS ONE 13(5):e0196828 (2018), [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0196828](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0196828).** The study reports uncertainty-guided tile selection for a trained whole-slide classifier and validation across cohorts. This is task-specific classifier-guided tile reduction. It depends on trained scores and tissue/cancer labels, and it does not provide a worst-case guarantee for discovering an unmodeled rare region. It is relevant as a practical prior-informed comparator, with its model training and selected-region costs charged.

## Verdict, falsifiers, and next gate

The assumption-free theorem is a useful impossibility/control, but its mathematics is classical hypergeometric sampling and does not establish novelty. Adaptive acquisition gains are conditional on a validated spatial prior or proxy and on a complete budget. The strongest practical direction is to calibrate a pilot-based, cost-aware stratified rule against a uniform randomized FOV baseline on held-out specimens, using the same high-resolution assay and error threshold. Include specimen-to-specimen variation, cluster diameter relative to PSF, cell density, spectral cross-talk, photobleaching, stage time, and analysis latency. A falsifier is any held-out cohort in which the stratifier misses positive compartments often enough that its realized sensitivity at matched total cost is no better than uniform sampling, or its false-positive rate exceeds the preregistered bound.

No experiment, biological analysis, external review, or novelty search was performed here. Model status: exact conditional theorem and counterexample proved directly; illustration computed exactly. Biological status: unknown until a representative pilot and held-out validation are acquired.
