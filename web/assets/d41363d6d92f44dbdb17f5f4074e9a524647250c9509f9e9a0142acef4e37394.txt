1. EXACT THEOREM AND GEOMETRY

Fix M<infinity, rho>0 and a finite label bound m0. A square-face disk is the
union of finitely many closed side-h squares of h Z^2, with disk interior
and a simple polygonal outer boundary. Its graph is the original square NN
one-skeleton. ALL original-boundary vertices are absorbing. The walk retains
its four original NN transitions at an interior vertex; no diagonal edge,
cell-center walk, artificial killing connector, or altered conductance occurs.
The disk is contained in B(0,M).

There are two compatible sign arcs on the outer boundary, whose transition
marks are edge midpoints. A resolved contour uses the SAME fixed face chord
for a given pair of crossed-edge ports, whether two or four sign changes
occur. In a checkerboard face the two disjoint chords cut off the negative
corners. An ordered simple resolved prefix starts at one boundary mark and
ends in the interior, or a completed crosscut joins the two boundary marks.
It may visit one square face twice. Its projection to ordinary face centers
need not be simple. Unused contour loops are not removed or observed here.
Write gamma for this PHYSICAL chord curve and U for its observer component.
For a prefix U is a simply connected slit domain; for a crosscut each U is
a simply connected Jordan component. No primal-component connectedness is
asserted. A pole z_h is a live lattice vertex in U with
  dist(z_h,partial U) >= rho.

Let K contain the original-boundary vertices and BOTH endpoints of EVERY
primal edge crossed by gamma. It may also contain any subset of the extra
queried negative fourth corners at a turn cutting off a positive corner.
The terminal extra corner is joined by an uncut original edge to a negative
cut endpoint. K contains sites only. Its auxiliary connecting curves are
not absorbing. Kill original NN walk at its first vertex in K.

Boundary labels distinguish original +, original -, gamma + bank, gamma -
bank. Empty arcs may be removed. Thus there are at most four cyclic prime-
end arcs for a prefix and at most two in a completed observer component.
One may merge equal label values, but the theorem retains all these labels.
Original-boundary labels take priority when a killing site is of both types.
An interior cut endpoint has its cut sign; an extra queried corner has the
negative-bank label. The incoming segment and the connector constructed
below specify an incident access. Distinct accesses at a common Euclidean
boundary point are NEVER identified. The theorem in fact allows any fixed
partition into m<=m0 cyclic access arcs for which these terminal connectors
bear their assigned label.

Let (xi_h,l_h) be the killing site and its label. Let (xi_B,l_B) be Brownian
exit location and access label from U, with standard Brownian covariance I.
For every a>0 there are couplings such that
  sup P( |xi_h-xi_B|>a OR l_h!=l_B ) --> 0 as h-->0.             (1)
The supremum is over the above disks, every compatible ordered prefix or
crosscut, every permitted extra-corner subset, and every protected pole.
In particular it is uniform after conditioning on any deterministic realized
prefix or on a random prefix independently of the sampling walk. It is not
an assertion for a walk chosen adaptively using its own future randomness.

Quantitative form. If Ch << r << R << rho and L is a time cutoff, then a
coupling satisfies, with one absolute geometry constant C and constants
depending only on M,rho,m0,
  P( |xi_h-xi_B|>C R OR labels differ )
    <= eta_h(r,L) + C sqrt((r+h)/R) + C M^2/L
       + C m0 log(B/rho)/log(B/(C R)).                         (2)
Here B=8(M+rho+1), C R<rho/8, and eta_h(r,L)-->0 for every fixed r,L.
Constants in the logarithms may be enlarged together to make denominators
positive. No mesh-rate is asserted for eta_h. The inverse-log screen is
deliberately used instead of an unproved inward discrete Beurling variant.

Marked conclusion. If z_h-->z and the removed closed sets converge in
Hausdorff distance in a fixed enclosing ball, pass to normalized conformal
maps f_h:D_unit-->U_h and to all transition angles. After a subsequence the
maps have a nondegenerate kernel limit f:D_unit-->U and the angle marks
converge in cyclic order. The JOINT law (xi_h,l_h) converges to the Brownian
exit law (f(e^{iTheta}),l(Theta)) with Theta uniform, interpreted through
the a.e. Hardy boundary trace of f. Colliding marks delete arcs of vanishing
harmonic mass. Bounded finite-label harmonic functions converge uniformly
on compact subsets to the marked Poisson extension. The labels are part of
the limiting data; they cannot be reconstructed from the closed set alone.

2. SCOPED ESTABLISHED IMPORTS

LL: Lawler--Limic, arXiv:math/0403309v1, Theorem 1, PDF page 3. For a fixed
mean-zero lattice walk generating its lattice with a finite (3+delta) moment,
a set meeting each radial band of fixed integer width kappa gives an upper
bound C sqrt(k/n) for escape from radius 2n while avoiding its part between
radii k and n. Use square NN walk, kappa=4 (or one larger fixed integer).
The theorem kills actual sites and does not require NN-connectedness.
URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0403309

SS: Schramm--Sheffield, arXiv:math/0605337v3, equation (3.55), PDF pages 84--85.
The observer-visible frontier of the union of diameter-R paths connecting
two complementary boundary access arcs is covered by two radius-2R balls.
This is a planar separation fact, not their DGFF or SLE conclusion. Its
argument applies in the prime-end disk of a finite polygonal slit. Section 5
states that extension and the additional probability bound used here.
URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0605337v3

Other standard premises are the isotropic functional invariance principle
for square NN walk, the continuum outward Beurling projection estimate,
Riemann mapping/kernel convergence, Brownian conformal invariance and a.e.
boundary traces of bounded analytic functions. The algebraic uses and the
required bounds are derived below. No source-reported curve/GFF theorem is
imported. The original square sign law is irrelevant once gamma is fixed.

3. ACTUAL SITE GEOMETRY AND TERMINAL ACCESSES

(a) A live interpolated NN path cannot cross gamma. Every gamma passage
meets a primal edge only at its used port. Both endpoints of that edge are
in K. Thus any traversal across the strand has already reached a killing
site. All other primal edges stay in the appropriate physical component.
This holds for a twice-visited face: its two resolved chords are disjoint.
No argument identifies an ordinary dual-center revisit with a crossing.

(b) A cut endpoint k has a connector to an incident gamma port: follow the
half of its crossed primal edge. Its open part lies in U on k's incident
side, has length h/2, and ends on the correctly signed bank. If several
crossed edges are incident at k, all such bank signs agree with k's sign.
For an extra negative corner, follow its uncut edge to the negative cut
endpoint, then a half crossed edge. The length is at most 3h/2 and the
open path lies on the negative side. The optional corner case is a SINGLE
passage cutting off a positive corner; a double checkerboard passage has
all four corners already cut-adjacent. In either case the connector is in
the observer component whenever its starting site is reached from it.
At an original-boundary site, use the incoming access and the trivial
terminal connector. Transition marks lie at edge midpoints, not ambiguous
vertices. This proves the assigned labels are legal access labels.

(c) Every chord point is at distance <=C h from a cut endpoint. Every K
site is at distance <=C h from gamma or the original boundary. Consecutive
crossed edges lie in one square, so their endpoint sets can be linked by
steps <=sqrt(2)h, hence <=2h. Following the ordered strand links any cut
endpoint to the outer absorbing boundary. An extra corner first uses its
uncut edge. The outer sites form the simple NN boundary cycle. These are
routes of actual absorbing sites, not an enlargement of K by the drawing.

(d) Suppose a lattice point x is within 3r of partial U and r>=Ch. There
is k in K within C r of x. Follow the route in (c) to outer boundary and
then along that cycle to a site at distance >=rho/2 from x. Such a site
exists because the boundary surrounds B(z_h,rho); its diameter is at least
2rho up to Ch. For R<rho/10 this route reaches beyond R. Radius changes
between consecutive route sites are <=2h. Taking first crossings of band
thresholds gives a K site in every radial band of width 4h, from radius
C r through R/2. The origin of these bands is the lattice point x.
Complete the bands below C r and above R/2 arbitrarily if necessary when
applying LL; only the annular portion is used in its event. The completed
sites are NOT added to the killing set. This proves radial density for the
LL annulus using only actual K sites in that annulus.

4. TWO SEPARATE MARGINAL BOUNDARY-LAYER ESTIMATES

Use the unrestricted square NN path W_h with piecewise linear time clock
h^2/2 per step. Its coordinate variance per unit time is one; this is the
clock matching standard Brownian motion. Killing does not change the path
before its first K visit. After a walk's OWN first lattice time t_h with
distance <=2r from partial U, section 3(d) and LL give
  P( remaining live path leaves B(W_h(t_h),R) )
       <= C sqrt((r+h)/R).                                  (3)
Apply the ordinary walk strong Markov property at t_h. The route needed
for LL is deterministic after fixing the slit and the current position.
If killing occurs sooner, this event is empty. The distance from partial U
at a live lattice time just before crossing the threshold changes by <=h.

At Brownian motion's OWN first time t_B within 2r of partial U, apply
continuum outward Beurling and its ordinary strong Markov property. The
connected physical boundary reaches across the corresponding R annulus:
for a prefix it is outer boundary plus the attached slit, and for a completed
component it is the crosscut plus that component's original boundary arc.
Its diameter is at least 2rho. Therefore
  P( remaining live Brownian path leaves B(B(t_B),R) )
       <= C sqrt(r/R).                                      (4)
These bounds are statements about EACH marginal path. They do not require,
or imply, a joint Markov property for an invariance-principle coupling.

Both killed times are bounded by the unrestricted exit time from a fixed
enclosing ball. For walk, |W_j|^2-j h^2 is a martingale and the exit overshoot
is <=h, so the physical expected enclosing-ball exit time is <=C M^2.
The identical Brownian second-moment martingale gives <=C M^2. Hence the
probability either killed time exceeds L is <=C M^2/L, uniformly in the slit
and sufficiently small h. This also covers very long prefixes and many
remaining lattice components without any assumption on their exit times.

5. ACCESS SCREENING WITH AN ELEMENTARY LOGARITHMIC COST

Let one prime-end arc A of U and its complementary arc A^c be given. Let
L_R be the union of internal paths of diameter <R whose endpoints are accesses
on A and A^c. Endpoints are allowed to approach the physical boundary; the
open path is in U. Path appendages and a slightly increased R make the union
open, which removes first-entrance measurability issues. Since R<rho/4, the
observer does not belong to L_R. Its component in U minus the mixed set has
an internal frontier covered by TWO balls of radius 2R, with centers within
R of partial U. This is precisely the geometric content of SS (3.55).

For clarity, the extension to the slit retains accesses as follows. Regard
the prime-end compactification of U as a closed disk. A simple internal
crosscut joining A and A^c separates the two transition accesses. Classify
the crosscuts that meet the observer-visible frontier according to which
transition is separated from the observer. In each class, any two visible
crosscuts intersect, by planar separation: an internal route from that
transition to their union cannot reach the observer without crossing the
other crosscut. Intersection and the diameter bound put the whole class
inside a 2R ball about any one member. This reasoning uses separation in
the prime-end disk, while the diameter and ball remain Euclidean. Thus a
double access at a slit point does not cause a sign or bank identification.
General paths can be pruned to crosscuts retaining the required endpoints;
finite subcollections and then closures give the same two-ball assertion.
For endpoint limits or open unions use 2R instead of R and take limits of
the two ball centers in the compact enclosing set. This harmless fixed
factor is included in C in (2).

Here is the ONLY hitting probability needed for these balls. If |z-y|>=rho/2
and U is in B(0,M), Brownian hitting of B(y,u) before leaving U is bounded
by hitting that ball before leaving B(y,B), where B=8(M+rho+1). The radial
harmonic function in the annulus gives
  P_z( hit B(y,u) before exit B(y,B) )
       <= log(B/|z-y|)/log(B/u)
       <= log(2B/rho)/log(B/u).                             (5)
Take u=C R<rho/8. The frontier ball centers are within C R of partial U,
so their distance from z is >=rho-C R. The first entrance into the mixed
set crosses its observer-visible frontier; a union bound over its two balls
and over the m cyclic arcs therefore gives
  P_z( Brownian path enters a diameter-R mixed-label path )
       <= C m log(2B/rho)/log(B/(C R)).                      (6)
If a path connects any two different labels, apply the single-arc screen
for either of those cyclic arcs against its complement. No estimate about
unlabelled Hausdorff closeness, inward discrete stopping, or a conditional
law under the coupled filtration has entered (5)--(6).

6. PATHWISE COUPLING AND PROOF OF (1)

The functional invariance principle supplies a coupling of unrestricted
W_h and B, starting at z_h, with
  P( sup_{t<=L}|W_h(t)-B(t)|>r/4 ) <= eta_h(r,L) --> 0.
Translation of the starting point changes neither distribution of increments
nor this error; thus it is uniform in z_h. It is also independent of U.

On this coupling event and the events (3)--(4), let theta be the earliest
time either free interpolated path reaches distance r from partial U.
This is used ONLY for a deterministic path comparison. Both paths are still
alive then: killing sites are within Ch of physical boundary and h<<r;
Brownian killing has distance zero. Both have reached their OWN 2r boundary
layer by theta. For walk use the preceding lattice node, whose distance
differs by <=h, to justify this assertion even when theta is between steps.
Their two points at theta lie at distance >=3r/4 from boundary and can be
joined by the length-r/4 segment inside U.

Their future tails from theta have diameter <=2R each, by the two marginal
confinement events. Append the terminal walk connector from section 3(b).
The union of that connector, the walk tail, the interior joining segment
and the reversed Brownian tail is an internal path of diameter <=C R
between the two terminal boundary accesses. If their labels differ, its
interior contains B(theta), so the Brownian path has entered the mixed set
from section 5. This is a pathwise implication. It does not condition a
marginal future at theta or assert that theta is a Brownian stopping time.
If the labels agree, the same confinement implies |xi_h-xi_B|<=C R.

Taking the union of coupling, tail, cutoff and screening errors proves (2).
For any a>0 first take R<a/C small, then r/R small, then L large, and then
h small at these fixed parameters. Let R decrease, r/R decrease and L
increase along a diagonal if desired. All constants depend only on the
declared enclosure, clearance and finite label bound. This proves (1).

7. MARKED KERNEL LIMITS AND JOINT HARMONIC MEASURES

Let F_h be the removed set (original exterior plus the slit), restricted
to a common closed enclosure. Suppose F_h converges in Hausdorff distance
and z_h-->z with the same clearance. A compact internal path from z to any
point in its limiting open component remains separated from F_h for small
h; conversely a ball meeting the limiting removed set cannot be eventually
contained in U_h. Thus the Caratheodory kernel of the observer components
is the component U of the limiting complement containing z. Passing to a
subsequence if necessary, Riemann maps normalized by
  f_h(0)=z_h, f_h'(0)>0
converge locally to the normalized map f onto U. The derivative is bounded
below by rho (conformal radius monotonicity for B(z_h,rho)) and bounded
above by the enclosure. Hence the limit is nonconstant and univalent.

It remains to check boundary locations, not just compact harmonic values.
Write f_h(w)=sum_{k>=0} a_{h,k} w^k. The area identity for a univalent map
and the bounded enclosure give
  sum_{k>=1} k |a_{h,k}|^2 = area(U_h)/pi <= M^2.              (7)
Every finite set of coefficients converges by local analytic convergence.
The H^2 boundary tail satisfies
  sum_{k>N}|a_{h,k}|^2 <= M^2/N,
also for f by Fatou. Therefore f_h converges to f STRONGLY in H^2, so
  integral |f_h(e^{it})-f(e^{it})|^2 dt/(2pi) --> 0.           (8)
For finite polygonal slits f_h extends continuously to the prime-end
circle. For U the bounded analytic boundary trace exists a.e.; (8) uses
that trace and requires no local connectedness of the limiting boundary.

Extract the at most m0 cyclic transition angles of the labels. Choose a
consistent circular origin away from a limiting transition; repeated marks
and empty arcs are allowed. Their convergence implies the corresponding
finite step-label functions l_h(t) converge to l(t) for a.e. t. With one
uniform Theta, (8) couples Brownian physical exit points in L^2 and gives
P(l_h(Theta)!=l(Theta))-->0. Brownian conformal invariance identifies these
joint pairs with exit location and prime-end label from the corresponding
observer domains. There is zero harmonic mass at a finite transition point.
Composing these couplings with (1), or equivalently applying convergence
of bounded continuous tests on position times the finite label set, proves
the stated joint marked harmonic-measure convergence for the killed walk.

For a prescribed bounded label value c_l, the limiting function at w is
the Poisson extension of c_{l(t)} evaluated at f^{-1}(w). The converging
angle marks and locally converging inverse maps give uniform convergence
on compact subsets. For lattice harmonic functions, (1) with clearance
uniform on that compact gives the same conclusion for live grid points.
Affine interpolation inside a fixed small cell can then be used if wanted;
no boundary trace or value across a slit is manufactured by interpolation.

If two finite slit domains agree on an oriented local boundary piece and
on its accesses within a ball of radius R, their bounded label-harmonic
functions differ at a point distance r from that piece, in the concentric
half-ball, by at most C max_l|c_l| sqrt(r/R): until escape from the ball they
have identical absorbing boundary data, and outward Beurling bounds escape.
Assume the common piece reaches the relevant annulus, as for a common strand
segment. This uniform statement passes to marked limits and preserves the
incident-side identification. It does not identify different prime ends
from their Euclidean location or assert an unmarked trace continuity theorem.

8. EARLY FALSIFIERS AND STATUS

Removing cut endpoints allows live NN edges to cross gamma and invalidates
section 3(a), even though a drawn slit may be Hausdorff close to other sites.
Replacing radial-density LL by NN-connectedness falsely excludes legitimate
square diagonal endpoint connections. Killing auxiliary connectors changes
the walk and is not permitted. A side partition with arbitrarily many cyclic
arcs has no uniform m0 screening bound. A protected pole is necessary for
the screen: the observer must not be inside a diameter-R mixed-access neck.

The proof does not acquire labels from Hausdorff limits. The same geometric
slit can have its two access labels exchanged, yielding identical removed
sets but different marked harmonic functions. Angle marks are an essential
part of the limiting theorem. No source SLE, matched height gap, GFF law,
random-surface comparison or stopping-time field identity follows from (1)
alone. The bridge is a conditional sampling step for such larger arguments.

Within the exact finite square geometry and scoped standard primary imports,
the independent theorem has no identified additional geometric hypothesis.
