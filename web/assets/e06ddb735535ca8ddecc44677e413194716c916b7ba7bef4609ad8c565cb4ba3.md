# Cycle 2 attack: bulk-matched switching worlds defeat an aggregate-rate extinction certificate

**Verdict:** A finite-rate robust extinction certificate is mathematically valid when its simultaneous confidence set covers the true state-resolved rates and one common positive weight contracts every admissible model/schedule. But aggregate bulk and birth/death mean-rate intervals do not identify whether the underlying modes switch. Here two branching-process worlds have identical untreated totals, identical uninterrupted-drug totals, identical expected aggregate birth/death intensities in each constant phase, and identical first on/off cycle; the same repeated schedule is subcritical in one world and supercritical in the other. If the robust model set retains both, no common cycle-contraction weight can certify extinction. No novelty claim: the certificate and branching-process machinery are standard; the contribution here is an explicit stress test of the observation interface.

## Two branching worlds with matched bulk and event-rate means

Use column counts and two phases: withdrawal (0) and drug (1). Each cell independently gives birth, dies, or switches state with constant phase-specific rates. For a mean-count vector x, the Metzler mean generator has off-diagonal entries equal to switching rates and diagonal entry for type j equal to birth minus death minus the total rate of leaving type j.

In reversible-state model A, the drug-free S→T and T→S rates are 0.1 and 0.9 per day. Both types have drug-free birth 0.17 and death 0.10, so net growth is 0.07. Under drug, S→T and T→S are both 0.1; S has birth 0.1 and death 2.1, while T has birth 0.1 and death 0.1. Start with n cells whose A states are independently assigned S with probability 0.9. Thus

\[
M_{A,0}=\begin{pmatrix}-0.03&0.9\\0.1&-0.83\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M_{A,1}=\begin{pmatrix}-2.1&0.1\\0.1&-0.1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
E Z(0)=n(0.9,0.1)^T.
\]

The drug-phase eigenvalues are \(\lambda_F=-2.1049875621\) and \(\lambda_L=-0.0950124379\). Starting from the stated expected composition, the total drug-phase mean is

\[
N_A(t)/n=c_F e^{\lambda_F t}+c_L e^{\lambda_L t},
\quad
(c_F,c_L)=(0.8482630166,\,0.1517369834).
\]

Static model B has two non-switching clones F and L, whose n initial labels are independently assigned F with probability \(c_F\) and L with probability \(c_L\). Off drug, both clones have birth 0.17 and death 0.10. Under drug, both have birth 0.1, and their death rates are \(0.1-\lambda_F=2.2049875621\) and \(0.1-\lambda_L=0.1950124379\). Its generators are diagonal, with entries 0.07 off drug and \((\lambda_F,\lambda_L)\) on drug.

These two worlds match the following observations exactly at the level of expected counts and event intensities:

- **Untreated total:** for every starting composition, both have \(N(t)=N(0)e^{0.07t}\). Switching in A conserves total cell count.
- **Uninterrupted-drug total:** both have \(N(t)/N(0)=c_Fe^{\lambda_Ft}+c_Le^{\lambda_Lt}\) for every duration.
- **Aggregate event-rate means in each constant phase:** off drug, both have expected birth intensity \(0.17N(t)\) and death intensity \(0.10N(t)\). On drug, both have expected birth intensity \(0.1N(t)\); because their expected total trajectories and derivatives agree, expected aggregate death intensity also agrees: \(D(t)=0.1N(t)-\dot N(t)\).
- **One on/off cycle:** after 2 days on and 3 days off, both have the same expected total, \(N(5)/N(0)=e^{0.21}(c_Fe^{2\lambda_F}+c_Le^{2\lambda_L})=0.1703352886\).

The equality is of aggregate means, not the full stochastic path law. Higher moments or lineage-resolved state transitions may distinguish the worlds. Therefore the claim is specifically that confidence intervals for these aggregate mean rates, however accurately estimated, cannot by themselves identify the hidden switching structure.

## Same repeated schedule, opposite long-run control result

Repeat a 5-day cycle consisting of 2 drug days followed by 3 withdrawal days. Its mean map in A is

\[
P_A=e^{3M_{A,0}}e^{2M_{A,1}}
=\begin{pmatrix}
0.0614519774&0.9154280419\\
0.0091945285&0.1521062916
\end{pmatrix},
\quad \rho(P_A)=0.2091093859.
\]

A positive left Perron weight, normalized to minimum coordinate 1, is \(w=(1,16.0592693)^T\), with \(w^TP_A=\rho(P_A)w^T\). The associated embedded multitype Galton–Watson process is subcritical, so extinction occurs almost surely under indefinite repetition. For the expected weighted burden, each cycle contracts by 0.20911.

For B, the cycle map is diagonal:

\[
P_B=\operatorname{diag}\left(
e^{0.21+2\lambda_F},e^{0.21+2\lambda_L}\right)
=\operatorname{diag}(0.0183160945,\,1.0201759620).
\]

The L clone’s cycle offspring mean exceeds one. Its birth and death rates are both positive, so the cycle offspring distribution is non-degenerate; the one-type Galton–Watson process for L has positive probability of indefinite survival. Thus this is an actual extinction-versus-positive-survival counterexample, not just a disagreement in predicted means. The bulk totals nevertheless match at the end of the first cycle: after two cycles they are 0.0354462 in A and 0.1582062 in B, and the B burden eventually grows because the L clone has a supercritical cycle mean.

A stronger matched-control prediction uses equal exposure over 10 days:

| Schedule | Total drug / withdrawal time | Model A mean burden at day 10 | Model B mean burden at day 10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alternating: (2 on, 3 off) repeated twice | 4 / 6 days | 0.0354462 \(N(0)\) | 0.1582062 \(N(0)\) |
| Blocked: 4 on, then 6 off | 4 / 6 days | 0.1582062 \(N(0)\) | 0.1582062 \(N(0)\) |

Hence the pre-registered alternating/blocked endpoint ratio is 0.2240 in A and 1.0000 in B, at the displayed rates. This is a model-level prediction for a controlled in-vitro system, not a clinical treatment recommendation.

## What the robust certificate proves—and what it cannot prove

Let \(Z(t)\in\mathbb N^d\) be a continuous-time multitype branching process, with mean generator \(M_\theta(u)\) for rate parameter \(\theta\) and treatment action \(u\). Suppose a simultaneous uncertainty set C contains the true \(\theta\). If a single vector \(w\gg0\) and \(\delta>0\) satisfy

\[
\sup_{\theta\in C,\ u\in\mathcal U}
\sum_i w_i[M_\theta(u)]_{ij}\le -\delta w_j
\quad\text{for every type }j,
\]

then \(W=w^TZ\) has generator drift at most \(-\delta W\), including for predictable adaptive schedules that only use actions in \(\mathcal U\). Therefore

\[
E[W(t)]\le e^{-\delta t}W(0),\qquad
P(Z(t)\ne0)\le \frac{w^TZ(0)}{\min_i w_i}e^{-\delta t}.
\]

The survival bound follows because any nonzero integer population has \(W\ge\min_iw_i\). In particular, extinction is almost sure. The guarantee is conditional on the true rates lying in the confidence set. If that set is estimated from an independent calibration cohort with coverage \(1-\alpha\), a conservative joint calibration-plus-application bound is \(\alpha+B\), capped at 1, where \(B\) is the displayed survival bound. Marginal intervals need a joint-coverage correction, such as Bonferroni, before they define this set.

A less restrictive certificate applies to a specified period-T schedule. Let \(\Phi_\theta\) be its phase-ordered mean map. If there are \(w\gg0\), \(\kappa<1\) such that

\[
\sup_{\theta\in C}\sum_i w_i[\Phi_\theta]_{ij}\le\kappa w_j
\quad\text{for every }j,
\]

then after k periods

\[
P(Z(kT)\ne0)\le
\frac{w^TZ(0)}{\min_iw_i}\kappa^k,
\]

so extinction is almost sure even if an individual phase grows. For A at the specified repeated cycle, \(\kappa=0.2091094\) works.

This does **not** imply extinction for every schedule. The action set in this example includes withdrawal forever, whose dominant mean growth rate is \(+0.07\) in both models. No \(w\gg0,\delta>0\) can satisfy the pointwise all-action condition over a class that permits that schedule. “Global” must name its schedule class: the pointwise condition gives a uniform guarantee over the declared actions; the cycle condition gives a guarantee only for schedules whose full-period map is covered.

The identifiability failure defeats the robust cycle certificate as well. If aggregate-rate intervals leave both A and B in C, a common \(\kappa<1\) weight cannot exist: for B’s L coordinate, the inequality requires \(1.0201759620\le\kappa\). State- and lineage-resolved intervals could exclude B. If they do so with simultaneous coverage and a common contracting weight exists over all remaining parameter values, the schedule certificate is valid. A certificate is thus a robust decision conditional on the observation model; it is not a mechanism-discovery procedure.

## Measurement and falsification

A matched in-vitro test would start from one barcoded parental pool, split randomly between the two 10-day schedules above, and match concentration, cumulative exposure, calendar endpoint, density, media changes, passage, handling, imaging, and endpoint processing. Add a vehicle-only arm to estimate withdrawal growth and handling effects. Use a live marker first validated against functional drug sensitivity, and track viable burden, divisions, deaths, state transitions, and barcode identity. Rechallenge descendants after drug-free expansion to detect stable resistance; otherwise a heritable escape clone can be misread as reversible tolerance.

The decisive predictions at these fixed rates are:

1. The day-10 alternating/blocked burden ratio is 0.2240 in A and 1.0000 in B.
2. Starting with cells classified as T and then withdrawing drug for 3 days, A predicts \(P(T\to S)=0.9(1-e^{-3})=0.8552\); static model B predicts no within-lineage phenotype conversion.
3. State-resolved estimates fitted to each phase must yield a simultaneous rate set whose worst-case cycle map contracts under one common \(w\) before reporting an extinction certificate.

For the schedule comparison, a practical floor is 3 independent biological replicates in each schedule arm and vehicle arm (9 cultures), with a variance-based power calculation before a definitive run. Sample viable counts and state composition at days 0, 2, 5, 7, and 10. Rate acquisition also requires lineage-level time-lapse in both phases, enough cell-hours to observe births, deaths, and switches, and a barcode or equivalent lineage assay. The 0.855 conversion prediction is so far from zero that a small idealized independent-cell cohort can separate it, but actual sample size must account for shared lineages, marker misclassification, death censoring, and culture-to-culture dependence. A destructive single-cell assay at matched timepoints is a cheaper-in-time substitute for live tracking only if described as sister-lineage distributions; it does not directly observe the same cell switch. Report cell-hours, tracked lineages, cells assayed, libraries, losses to death/censoring, and the platform quote rather than inventing dollar costs.

The construction is falsified for a target system if a pre-registered matched experiment’s confidence interval excludes the predicted 0.2240 schedule ratio and state-resolved rate estimates still support the stated parameterization, or if a validated assay finds no T→S conversion near 0.855 after accounting for division, death, and marker error. If both predictions fail, the two-state constant-rate model—not merely the switching interpretation—has failed. The certificate itself is falsified if the simultaneous rate set contains a true model/schedule with cycle map failing the proposed common-weight inequality. A failed common-weight search is not generally proof of survival; in this construction the B model supplies the separate supercritical argument.

## Prior-art boundary

- Cancer phenotypic switching has already been formulated with multitype branching processes and extinction probabilities: Gunnarsson, De, Leder, and Foo, [“Understanding the role of phenotypic switching in cancer drug resistance,” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2020.110162).
- Lineage barcoding has been used to quantify phenotype dynamics during cancer resistance evolution, with lineage information improving separation of switching from selection on aggregate populations: Whiting et al., [“Quantitative measurement of phenotype dynamics during cancer drug resistance evolution using genetic barcoding,” *Nature Communications* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59479-7).
- Common linear co-positive Lyapunov functions for families of positive systems are established switched-systems machinery: Knorn, Mason, and Shorten, [*Automatica* (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2009.04.013). Xu et al. further show why strong common functions are sufficient yet can be conservative, and develop weak common co-positive functions for positive switched systems: [*Automatica* (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.automatica.2018.04.037).

**Novelty status:** no novelty claim. The extinction theorem is a standard first-moment/Lyapunov argument for branching processes and positive switched systems; cancer switching models, branching extinction, and lineage-resolved phenotype assays all have direct prior art. The useful result here is the explicit paired construction showing exactly which aggregate finite-rate evidence is insufficient, plus the quantified equal-AUC control divergence and the condition under which state-resolved confidence sets restore a valid certificate. There is no biological fit, external validation, or clinical inference.
