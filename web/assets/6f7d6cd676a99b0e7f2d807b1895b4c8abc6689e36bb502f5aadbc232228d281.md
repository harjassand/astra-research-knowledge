# Local-data obstruction for effective conductivity

## Model and claim

Fix contrast $K>1$, stripe width $L>0$, and coupon side length $0<w\le L$. In two dimensions let
\[
a^{\rm v}_{L,U}(x)=K\,\mathbf 1\{(x_1-U)\bmod 2L\in[0,L)\}
+1\,\mathbf 1\{(x_1-U)\bmod 2L\in[L,2L)\},
\]
and define $a^{\rm h}_{L,U}$ by replacing $x_1$ with $x_2$. In each ensemble, $U$ is uniform on $[0,2L)$. The coefficient is a scalar conductivity in the continuum equation $-\nabla\cdot(a\nabla u)=0$.

Each ensemble is bounded by $1\le a\le K$, stationary, ergodic under the full translation action, and has phase fraction $1/2$. Its exact homogenized tensors are
\[
A^{\rm v}_{\rm hom}=\begin{pmatrix}H&0\\0&M\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A^{\rm h}_{\rm hom}=\begin{pmatrix}M&0\\0&H\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H=\left(\tfrac12K^{-1}+\tfrac12\right)^{-1}=\frac{2K}{K+1},\quad
M=\tfrac12K+\tfrac12=\frac{K+1}{2}.
\]
Thus the directional gap is
\[
\Delta(K):=\|A^{\rm v}_{\rm hom}-A^{\rm h}_{\rm hom}\|_{\rm op}=M-H=\frac{(K-1)^2}{2(K+1)},
\]
and the high/low directional ratio is $M/H=(K+1)^2/(4K)\sim K/4$.

Let $P^{\rm v}_{L,w}$ and $P^{\rm h}_{L,w}$ be the laws of the *complete coefficient map* on one axis-aligned square coupon $Q_w=[0,w]^2$. Then
\[
\|P^{\rm v}_{L,w}-P^{\rm h}_{L,w}\|_{\rm TV}\le \frac{w}{L}.
\]
For $N$ independent coupons,
\[
\|(P^{\rm v}_{L,w})^{\otimes N}-(P^{\rm h}_{L,w})^{\otimes N}\|_{\rm TV}\le \min\{1,Nw/L\}.
\]
Consequently every estimator $\widehat A$ based on these coupons obeys
\[
\max_{j\in\{{\rm v,h}\}}\mathbb E_j\|\widehat A-A^j_{\rm hom}\|_{\rm op}
\ge \frac{\Delta(K)}2\left(1-\frac{Nw}{L}\right)_+.
\]
This applies even when the instrument receives exact, noiseless coefficient maps; any local boundary-response measurement or noisy observation is a data-processing of that map and cannot improve the bound.

## Proof

The laminate cell problem separates by direction. For vertical stripes, the corrector flux in the normal direction is constant: $J_1=a(x_1)(1+\phi'_1(x_1))$. The zero-mean derivative constraint gives $J_1\langle a^{-1}\rangle=1$, so the normal effective coefficient is the harmonic mean $H$. In the tangential direction the affine field solves the cell problem with zero corrector; averaging its flux gives $\langle a\rangle=M$. Interchanging the stripe normal exchanges the diagonal entries. The random phase is uniform on the period torus. The full translation group acts transitively on that torus, so each ensemble is stationary and ergodic.

For a coupon of width $w\le L$, a uniform phase gives probability $1-w/L$ that the coupon contains no interface. Conditional on this event, the whole coupon is constant $K$ or constant $1$, each with probability $1/2$. This common sub-probability law has mass $1-w/L$ and is identical for vertical and horizontal stripes. The remaining mixed-coupon measures have mass $w/L$ each. Therefore their total-variation distance is at most $w/L$. Product total variation is subadditive over independent factors, yielding the $Nw/L$ bound.

For any two data laws $P,Q$ and targets $A_0,A_1$, the common part of $P$ and $Q$ has mass $1-\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV}$. On that common part, the triangle inequality gives $\|z-A_0\|+\|z-A_1\|\ge\|A_0-A_1\|$. Hence the sum of the two risks is at least $\Delta(K)(1-\|P-Q\|_{\rm TV})$, and the larger risk is at least half this value. Substitution gives the stated lower bound.

## Interface and interpretation

Supplied information: the ellipticity interval $[1,K]$, exact phase fraction $1/2$, coupon shape and side $w$, and the promise that coupons are independent draws from one of the two ensembles. Acquired information: at most $N$ complete local coefficient maps, or any measurements computed from them. The hidden stripe scale $L$ is not supplied. The total mapped area is $Nw^2$; each PDE-based local interrogation adds its solve and instrument cost. The theorem is information-theoretic, so additional local computation cannot improve it.

For any fixed $K>1$ and any finite local data budget $N,w$, taking $L\gg Nw$ makes the coupon data nearly indistinguishable while the macroscopic directional conductivity gap remains fixed. No uniform finite-sample guarantee can therefore depend only on ellipticity and phase fraction over the class of stationary ergodic fields. A rate requires additional information such as a known correlation length, a finite-range/mixing hypothesis, or access to a specimen whose observation window reaches the hidden structure scale.

This is an exact theorem about the scalar steady-conduction PDE with ideal laminates. It does not establish how often such long laminations occur in manufactured materials, account for interfaces/contact resistance or non-Fourier transport, or provide an engineering error bar for a particular specimen.
