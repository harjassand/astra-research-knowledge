1. RESOURCE INTERFACE AND CUTOFF CHANNEL

The cutoff encoder is trace preserving:

  E_K(X)=P_K X P_K+Tr[(I-P_K)X]|0,0><0,0|.

Its Kraus operators are P_K and |0,0><n_1,n_2| for each discarded number
basis vector. Their effects sum to P_K+(I-P_K)=I. The embedding decoder is
an isometry. Thus this is a legal one-branch CPTP code with quantum
dimension d_K=(K+1)(K+2)/2 and no retained classical flag. For
delta=Tr[(I-P_K)rho], the gentle projection estimate gives
T(rho,P_K rho P_K)<=sqrt(delta); adding the fallback mass costs at most
delta/2. This correctly retains the cutoff-crossing coherences. The stated
2sqrt(delta) bound is conservative and valid.

For two-mode coherent |alpha>, total number is Poisson with mean
mu=||alpha||^2<=R^2. For L=K+1>=R^2, the Chernoff bound is

  delta<=exp[-L log(L/R^2)+L-R^2]
        <=(e R^2/L)^L.

The exponent is maximized over mu<=R^2 at mu=R^2. If (2.2) holds, the
tail is at most (epsilon/2)^2; the cutoff error is then at most epsilon.
Inverting L log(L/(eR^2))=Theta(log(1/epsilon)) gives
L=Theta_R(log(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon)), hence
d_K=Theta_R((log(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon))^2).

2. FINITE-SPAN LEMMA: CONSTANTS AND UNLIMITED LABELS

For normalized linearly independent pure vectors x_s, let V=span{x_s},
G=(<x_s,x_t>) and g=lambda_min(G)>0. Let b=min_{s,t}|<x_s,x_t>|>0.
For a TP channel with Kraus A_j of rank at most Q, suppose the maximum
pure-target trace distance is epsilon<=1/2 and Q<=D/2. Write

  A_j x_s=lambda_(j,s)x_s+r_(j,s),  r_(j,s) perpendicular to x_s,
  f_s=sum_j ||r_(j,s)||^2.

The pure-target effect gives f_s=1-<x_s,Phi(P_s)x_s><=epsilon; TP gives
sum_j |lambda_(j,s)|^2=1-f_s. The off-diagonal TP identity
<x_s,x_t>=sum_j <A_jx_s,A_jx_t> has two cross terms bounded by
sqrt(f_s)+sqrt(f_t) and a remainder bounded by sqrt(f_s f_t). Hence

  |1-<lambda_s,lambda_t>|<=3sqrt(epsilon)/b,
  ||lambda_s-lambda_t||^2<=6sqrt(epsilon)/b.

Fix anchor s=1 and set B_j=(A_j-lambda_(j,1)I)|_V. The triangle-square
bound and epsilon<=1/2,b<=1 give

  sum_j ||B_j x_s||^2<=2epsilon+12sqrt(epsilon)/b
                     <=14sqrt(epsilon)/b.

The frame operator F=sum_s |x_s><x_s| has least eigenvalue g on V,
because its nonzero spectrum is that of G. Thus

  sum_j ||B_j||^2_HS(V,H)<=14D sqrt(epsilon)/(b g).          (A)

On ker(A_j|_V), B_j=-lambda_(j,1)I. Since this kernel has dimension at
least D-Q, summing the resulting HS lower bounds yields

  sum_j ||B_j||^2_HS >=(D-Q)sum_j|lambda_(j,1)|^2
                       >=(D-Q)(1-epsilon)>=D/4.             (B)

Combining (A) and (B) proves epsilon>=(b g/56)^2. The proof uses only
the total input-to-output channel, TP, rank of each Kraus operator, and
finite-family Gram data. It does not assume a product channel, covariance,
Gaussianity, or bounded output energy.

Unlimited classical labels do not evade the bound if Q is the maximum
quantum register dimension in each classical branch: every branch
encoder-decoder composite Kraus operator factors through that Q-dimensional
register and therefore has rank at most Q. Summing over any number of
branches leaves the proof unchanged. A coherent quantum label would instead
be part of the quantum memory and must be counted. An integral/Kraus direct
sum is handled by replacing sums with integrals; finite-dimensional V keeps
the HS sums finite. The resource statement is thus specific and sound for
the stated classical-label/branch convention.

3. TWO-MODE GRID: LOWER MATCHES THE CUTOFF UPPER

Set r=min(R/sqrt(2),1/2)>0, M=L+1, D=M^2, and take
alpha_(a,b)=(r exp(2pi i a/M),r exp(2pi i b/M)). These points lie in the
radius-R ball. Their overlaps obey b>=exp(-4r^2). The one-mode Gram is
circulant with Fourier eigenvalues

  M exp(-r^2) sum_{n congruent ell mod M} r^(2n)/n!, ell=0,...,L.

For r^2<=1, retaining the n=ell term and then the smallest first term
gives a one-mode eigenvalue at least M exp(-r^2)r^(2L)/L!. The two-mode
Gram is a tensor product, so

  g>=D exp(-2r^2)r^(4L)/(L!)^2.

Substitution into the finite-span lemma gives exactly the scale of (4.2).
For arbitrary branch budget Q, choose L=ceil(sqrt(2Q))-1; then D>=2Q and
L=O(sqrt(Q)). Stirling (or log L!<=L log L) yields

  log(1/epsilon)<=C_R[1+sqrt(Q)log(Q+2)].

Inverting gives Q=Omega_R((log(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon))^2), matching
the one-branch cutoff. Together the lower and upper establish the claimed
Theta_R profile for PURE two-mode coherent states in a supplied fixed
Fock chart at worst-case half-trace error. Constants and exact optimizers
are not determined.

4. FINITE Sym^m(C^3) CHART AT POLYNOMIAL PRECISION

For v_m(z)=(z_1,z_2,sqrt(m))/sqrt(m+||z||^2), with ||z||<=R, the total
excitation N=n_1+n_2 in v_m(z)^tensor m is binomial with mean at most R^2.
Its mgf satisfies E e^(tN)<=exp(R^2(e^t-1)); the same cutoff gives
Q=O_R((log(1/epsilon)/loglog(1/epsilon))^2) for sufficiently small
epsilon, capped by the full carrier dimension when K>=m.

For the phase grid with both |z_i|=r, the Gram kernel is

  G_(s,t)=((m+bar z_s dot z_t)/(m+2r^2))^m.

Its Fourier eigenvalues are D times sums of multinomial probabilities
grouped by residues modulo M. If m>=4L^2, keeping the term
(n_1,n_2)=(ell_1,ell_2), 0<=ell_i<=L, gives

  g>=D exp(-2r^2-1) r^(4L)/(L!)^2.

Indeed (m/(m+2r^2))^m>=exp(-2r^2), and for s=ell_1+ell_2<=2L,
(m)_fall(s)/m^s>=exp(-1) under m>=4L^2, using
log(1-u)>=-2u for u<=1/2. Pairwise overlaps are bounded below by
[(m-2r^2)/(m+2r^2)]^m>=exp(-8r^2) (the stated constants are conservative).
The finite-span lower therefore has log error floor O_R(L log L).
For epsilon=m^(-alpha), choose L=c_alpha log(m)/loglog(m) with sufficiently
small positive c_alpha. Then m>=4L^2 eventually and the floor exceeds
m^(-alpha), forcing Q=Omega_(R,alpha)((log m/loglog m)^2). If Q is at least
order m, this lower is automatic; otherwise the grid condition holds at
the polylogarithmic threshold. This matches the cutoff upper at every fixed
alpha>0, for the supplied normal and the PURE local coherent chart only.
The finite checker verifies small cases but does not establish these
asymptotics.

5. MIXED AND UNKNOWN-GLOBAL-CHART BOUNDARIES

The pure-state preservation lemma cannot be applied to a mixed target by
choosing one of its pure decompositions. Trace distance is convex in the
states, and accurately reproducing the mixture does not require the
channel to preserve its individual ensemble members. As a simple control,
a singleton mixed-state family can be prepared exactly with a rank-one
constant channel even if one chosen pure-state decomposition has many
nonorthogonal components. This does not resolve the specific displaced
positive-temperature family: LOCAL_CHART_PROFILE proves a one-branch
O(log^2(1/epsilon)) upper there, but no matching lower. No pure lower
transfers by convexity.

Likewise the pure local result assumes a known/supplied two-mode chart (and
the finite-carrier result assumes a supplied normal e3). It supplies a
channel independent of alpha or z within that chart, but it does not
acquire an unknown global frame/plane or pay for pilots and restoration.
A code valid on an unknown global orbit must use one encoder/decoder across
all chart orientations; separate local codes cannot be selected for free.
Thus the local polylog upper refutes an epsilon^(-2) lower whose hypotheses
are only this fixed bounded local experiment, but it does not refute an
epsilon^(-2) lower for a genuinely global unknown-chart experiment. The
actual hot-spin/thermal qutrit model with cross-root terms and iid sector
transport is also outside this pure chart theorem.
