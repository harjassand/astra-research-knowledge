# Candidate mechanism and decisive experiment: preserve one functional centromere

## What the source material actually constrains

The 2026 barcoded-HAC study supplies a spatial substrate: 2-kb position codes, two CENP-A-dense intervals in each HAC, phase-associated CENP-A/H3K9me3 profiles, and HJURP/KAT7 depletion perturbations. It reports near-half CENP-A in the colcemid-arrested mitotic sample versus the thymidine G1/S sample and recovery after 3 h of release; H3K9me3 behavior depends on dense-region context/length. The corrected tagwise reanalysis finds a positive domain interaction in both line/domain pairs, but COL→CR3 log2(Phi) decreases from 0.991 to 0.559 in HAC120 and increases from 0.227 to 0.929 in HAC465. This clone-dependent reversal rejects a universal “3-hour H3K9 recovery” law from the current data. Neither the PC read counts nor source qPCR establishes a same-cell history.

The model needs a second, slower state variable. The 2026 NSMB boundary study reports that eroding H3K9me3-associated boundaries can move/expand CENP-A domains and seed new CENP-A/CENP-C domains, including functional dicentrics. The 2021 PNAS replication-stress study reports that CENP-A loss can independently damage centromeric DNA and cause structural chromosome instability. Thus chromosome success is not one scalar “centromere mark”: it requires (i) intact DNA structure, (ii) one appropriately assembled functional kinetochore, and (iii) successful segregation.

The source article itself says the two D1/D2 CENP-A sites may both contribute to HAC function and that their spacing may matter. This is an explicit open mechanism, not proof that both domains are functional or form two kinetochores. The CENP-A-dense interval count is not a kinetochore count. A CENP-C/KNL1/NDC80 co-localized focus on the HAC is the relevant operational readout.

## Candidate spatial-cycle model (not fitted or validated)

Index 2-kb codes by `i`, with local replication-completion time `r_i`, CENP-A fraction `C_i`, H3K9me3 fraction `H_i`, and local early-G1 HJURP/KAT7 activity `U_i(t)`. A minimal hypothesis class is:

1. Replication partitions old CENP-A. A testable idealization is `C_i(r_i^+) = C_i(r_i^-)/2`; the source reports an approximately half reduction in the *bulk* mitotic sample, not a per-bin deterministic reset.
2. During the M-to-G1 reload window,

   `dC_i/dt = k_load U_i(t) (C_max − C_i) g(H_i) − μ_C C_i`,

   where `g(H)` is decreasing. This represents the established roles of HJURP/KAT7 and heterochromatin antagonism, but the rate and functional form are unknown.
3. H3K9me3 can spread/recover locally while a CENP-A-poor interval remains unprotected:

   `dH_i/dt = D_H (H_{i−1} − 2H_i + H_{i+1}) + k_seed q(C_i) H_i^n (1−H_i) − k_clear U_i H_i − μ_H H_i`.

   Here `q(C)` increases as CENP-A falls. `D_H`, `k_seed`, `n`, `k_clear`, and the shape of `q` are unmeasured. The effective spatial-coupling term could reflect local propagation/recruitment, not literal diffusion of histone marks.
4. A separate ectopic-loading rule allows weak CENP-A nucleation outside the primary core when the H3K9 boundary is lost. The number of *functional* centromeres is measured independently:

   `K = number of HAC-colocalized CENP-C-positive and outer-kinetochore (KNL1 or NDC80)-positive foci`.

   Structural state `S` is independently `intact / rearranged / lost` by long-read/PFGE/ddPCR/FISH. The engineering target is `S=intact`, `K=1`, and low HAC missegregation. Neither `K` nor `S` is inferred from CENP-A ChIP signal.

The source constrains signs and initial geometry only: replication is associated with lower CENP-A; reduced HJURP/KAT7 is associated with lower CENP-A and higher H3K9me3; H3K9me3 boundaries restrict CENP-A expansion in the separate NSMB system. It supplies no `r_i`, within-cell time course, same-cell `C_i,H_i`, front velocity, reload rate, feedback exponent, domain-count threshold, or structural transition law. Consequently, the equations are a falsifiable model family, not a model fit.

## A quantitative missing lemma: replication-age-gated boundary stability

The high-ceiling target is a **topology-preserving, replication-age-gated reload**: can local CENP-A recovery after each barcode's own replication completion keep an H3K9-poor boundary segment below the size/amplitude needed to invade, while retaining exactly one functional kinetochore? This differs from maximizing bulk CENP-A. It joins local replication, a nonlinear H3K9 boundary, ectopic-domain risk, and the independently observed kinetic topology `K`.

A minimal local invasion test can be derived without pretending its rates are known. In the simplest autocatalytic closure (`n=1` in the model above), let `q_i=1−C_i/C_*` be a normalized local CENP-A deficit, clipped to `[0,1]`, and linearize H3K9 accumulation at low flank occupancy:

`dh/dt = [D L + diag(a q_i − gamma)] h`,

where `h` is the low-occupancy H3K9 profile, `L` is the discrete nearest-neighbor Laplacian, `D` is effective spatial coupling, `a` is deficit-dependent H3K9 amplification, and `gamma` is clearance. For a uniform `m`-barcode vulnerable segment with fixed low-H3K9 endpoints, the largest eigenvalue is

`lambda_m = a q − gamma − 4 D sin^2(pi / (2(m+1)))`.

For the `m×m` tridiagonal path Laplacian with diagonal `−2`, adjacent entries `1`, and endpoint conditions `h_0=h_{m+1}=0`, the eigenvectors are `v_j^(k)=sin(j k pi/(m+1))` and the eigenvalues are `2 cos(k pi/(m+1))−2 = −4 sin^2(k pi/(2(m+1)))`, for `k=1,...,m`. The `k=1` mode is largest, so adding the scalar reaction slope `a q−gamma` gives the displayed `lambda_m`. `lambda_m>0` predicts that a small H3K9 perturbation grows in the segment; `lambda_m<0` predicts local linear decay. For a nonuniform segment, the corresponding criterion is whether the largest eigenvalue of `D L + diag(a q_i−gamma)` is positive. The critical CENP-A level in the uniform approximation is

`C_crit = C_* [1 − (gamma + 4 D sin^2(pi/(2(m+1))))/a]`,

when the critical deficit is between 0 and 1. Thus a source-informed experiment can estimate `D,a,gamma` from the local time course, calculate a barcode-run-specific CENP-A threshold, and test it on a held-out boundary/clone. No numerical `C_crit` is asserted now: the source has no local replication times or rates, and the homogeneous-block approximation is only a screening model. If the actual feedback is superlinear (`n>1`), the zero-H3K9 linearization has no deficit-dependent growth term; then a finite-amplitude nucleus threshold must be measured and the displayed `lambda=0` gate is insufficient. `code/boundary_threshold.py` implements only the stated `n=1` finite-segment screen, without inserting invented biological values.

The discriminating intervention is matched-total-dose HJURP/KAT7 recruitment either (i) at a fixed wall-clock early-G1 time or (ii) aligned to each vulnerable segment's measured local replication completion, with a late-G1 equal-dose control. The prediction is conditional: if CENP-A deficit is the driver in this model, replication-age alignment should move the at-risk segment from positive to nonpositive fitted `lambda_m` more often than the fixed pulse, and held-out H3K9 edge movement should follow the predicted sign. If a pulse transiently lowers `q` but post-pulse `lambda_m` remains positive, it predicts delay only, not durable stability. Durable benefit requires persistent CENP-A recovery or a lasting rate change. This is a more stringent target than an early-versus-late mark change.

The topological endpoint remains separate: count HAC-colocalized CENP-C plus KNL1/NDC80 foci, then score missegregation and structural loss. The boundary eigenvalue predicts low-H3K9 invasion only; it does not prove that an ectopic H3K9-poor site becomes a second kinetochore, that an existing core is functional, or that DNA remains intact. Those are independent outcomes and falsifiers. The source's phase reversal across HAC120 and HAC465 suggests testing local replication-age alignment *within* each clone, not pooling a single global phase coefficient.

**Load-bearing missing lemma:** after conditioning on complete HAC structure and cell mixture, does measured local replication-to-reload delay determine the sign of the H3K9 boundary's dominant growth mode, and does crossing `lambda=0` predict a later change in `K>1` or structural loss? Establish this by fitting only training timepoints/one clone and predicting held-out barcode edges, timepoints, and a second clone. Failure rejects the proposed transfer from chromatin boundary growth to synthetic-chromosome topology.

## Why the present 2-kb endpoint profiles cannot identify a spreading law

For a discrete profile vector `h(t)` over the barcodes, suppose

`h_dot = D L h + r(t)`,

where `L` is a nearest-neighbor spatial operator and `r(t)` collects local writers/erasers, replication-age forcing, phase mixture, and recruitment. For any chosen `D≥0` and any smooth interpolation `h(t)` through the measured endpoints, `r(t)=h_dot−D Lh` reproduces those endpoints. Therefore the snapshots do not identify `D`, a front velocity, or even nonzero spatial coupling unless `r(t)` is independently constrained. The actual THYM, COL, and CR3 samples are cross-sectional synchronized aliquots rather than repeated observations of the same lineage, which weakens dynamic interpretation further.

A local-independent model (`D=0`) and a spatial-invasion model (`D>0`) can both reproduce the reported profiles with different unmeasured phase forcing. There is no estimate of a lag-dependent intervention effect from the three states. This is the load-bearing missing lemma: a causal relation from local replication age to CENP-A/HJURP/KAT7 reload and H3K9 boundary movement, then to CENP-C/KNL1 multiplicity and the distinct structural-loss outcome.

## High-ceiling candidate: topology-gated, core-only G1 rescue

The promising design question is not “how much CENP-A?” alone; it is whether the two separated D1/D2 chromatin cores form one coordinated functional kinetochore or two, and how a transient core recovery can preserve the flanking H3K9 barrier. The candidate intervention is to recruit HJURP/KAT7 with guides against unique PC sequences *inside D1, D2, or both*, only during mitotic exit/early G1, while leaving inter-core and outer H3K9 flanks untouched. This is a proposed test, not a claimed successful treatment. It is bounded by prior art: early-G1 CENP-A loading, KAT7/HJURP function, and HAC-tethered chromatin engineering are already known. The untested increment is domain-resolved, phase-gated control with simultaneous single-kinetochore topology and structure readouts.

Factorial arms: non-targeting guide; D1-only pulse; D2-only pulse; D1+D2 pulse. For each, compare a 0–3-h early-G1 recruitment pulse, a late-G1 pulse with equal integrated exposure, catalytically dead/HJURP-binding controls, and dCas9-only controls. A site-neutral guide set matched for DNA binding/guide number controls replication obstruction. Confirm equal protein dose and synchronization. Do not globally inhibit H3K9 methylation: that would remove the boundary that may prevent ectopic CENP-A domains.

The model makes a directional, not yet magnitude-calibrated, within-clone prediction: if core reload is causal, a correctly timed pulse should move the *targeted* core's CENP-A phase fold upward and H3K9me3 phase fold downward relative to the matched phase sham; a late pulse should not reproduce that pair of changes. The opposite phase evolution of HAC120 and HAC465 means the effect cannot be pooled across clones as one universal trajectory; estimate and test it within each complete, structure-admitted clone. The safety requirement is that this local response does not increase HAC-colocalized CENP-C/KNL1 foci above one or erode flanking H3K9me3, and does not increase structural rearrangement/loss. A 50% attenuation of the HAC120 COL interaction is a preregisterable engineering success threshold, not a source-derived biological prediction: `log2(Phi)` would fall from 0.991 to at most 0.495. Biological variance is unreported in the PC libraries, so an effect-size/power plan must be recalculated from a pilot with biological replicates rather than using the read-count interval.

The D1/D2 arm comparison resolves a separate topology hypothesis. If both CENP-A cores are functionally needed, pulsing only one should fail to restore one-focus segregation even if that core's ChIP signal rises; both-core control may be required. If each can independently support a kinetochore and boundaries are weakened, a second CENP-C/KNL1 focus may emerge. These alternatives are measured directly instead of collapsing them into a CENP-A intensity surrogate.

## Decisive experiment and costs

1. **Copy/structure admission:** before synchronized ChIP, long-read/optical or PFGE assembly of each HAC clone; barcode-specific ddPCR/qPCR on matched genomic DNA from each condition; FISH to distinguish intact autonomous HAC from host integration. Exclude ambiguous clones before treatment assignment. This is necessary to bound the tag-copy bias in the corrected contrast.
2. **Replication and chromatin timing:** pulse-label nascent DNA across S phase and obtain per-code replication-completion timing; collect matched THYM, COL, then 0/0.5/1/2/3/6-h post-release aliquots. Measure CENP-A, H3K9me3, H3K14ac/H3K4me2, HJURP/KAT7, and input DNA with per-sample spike-in and code-specific copy normalization. The paper's ChIP input is about `2×10^7` cells/sample; a validated CUT&RUN/targeted barcode protocol could reduce material but must first pass an orthogonal ChIP-qPCR check. An 8-time × 4-arm × 3-replicate × 2-mark ChIP design would require about `3.84×10^9` cell-assay inputs before controls, so first gate on a smaller 4-time pilot or lower-cell CUT&RUN method. Do not pretend current GEO supplies this trajectory.
3. **Same-cell topology:** on the same phase/treatment, co-image HAC DNA (LacO/FISH), CENP-A, CENP-C and KNL1/NDC80. Count outer-kinetochore-positive loci per intact HAC, not just CENP-A intensity. Start with both HAC120 and HAC465 independently because their tagwise phase interactions run in opposite directions from COL to CR3. For the binary `K=1` criterion, observing zero `K>1` events among `N` independent HAC-bearing cells gives the rule-of-three upper 95% bound `≈3/N`; e.g. `N=300` bounds the detectable extra-focus fraction at about 1%, provided clones/fields are treated as clustered rather than 300 independent replicates.
4. **Independent phenotype:** score anaphase HAC missegregation and micronuclei with HAC-specific FISH; in parallel passage replicate single-cell clones for 20–50 generations without HAC-selective drug. At fixed passages measure HAC-positive fraction *and* structure/integration state by ddPCR/FISH/long-read/PFGE. This separates segregation loss from breakage/rearrangement and from growth-selection effects. Analyze clone and culture replicates as units; a read-count Poisson interval is not a substitute.
5. **Matched comparators:** sham and catalytically dead controls receive the same guide load, recruitment protein, induction drug, synchronization, and time. Equal-dose late G1 distinguishes timing from total exposure. A D1/D2/both factorial distinguishes domain count from single-core rescue. Keep all clones and failed HAC structures in the denominator to avoid survivor-only bias.

## Falsifiers / go-no-go rules

- **Reject the phase-local reload mechanism** if early versus equal-dose late recruitment produces no replicated tagwise CENP-A/H3K9 difference after matched copy/input normalization, or if the apparent bulk change disappears in single-cell paired imaging.
- **Reject the single-domain safety claim** if a mark-recovered HAC gains extra CENP-C/KNL1 foci, even if CENP-A signal improves.
- **Reject “centromere loss” as the cause of a retention change** if the effect is instead explained by long-read/PFGE structural breaks or host integration.
- **Reject the estimated front/lag parameters** if predictions fail on held-out barcode positions, time points, or an independently assembled HAC clone.
- **Stop before stability promotion** if phase-specific copy ratios, cell-mixture differences, replication completion, or structural integrity cannot be measured closely enough to bound the bias term in `COPY_INVARIANT_TAGWISE_CONTRAST.md`.
