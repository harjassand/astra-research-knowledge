# Transient heat-transfer asymmetry: a matched-port audit

## Result

I derived an exact reciprocity theorem for finite-volume thermal networks and the weak Fourier operator. In a fixed linear material with positive heat capacity and symmetric conductivity, the heat-power-to-temperature transfer is symmetric under swapping a pair of *matched conjugate ports*, even with arbitrary conductivity and heat-capacity gradients. A transient temperature-profile asymmetry can still be observed when the two experiments start with the same temperature increment at different locations. In the point-port RC model that asymmetry is exactly the ratio of local heat capacities, because the two initial states do not deposit the same energy.

This is a rigorous capability boundary and a falsifiable experiment, not a new material effect or a novelty claim. The evidence does not establish that the recent experiment in Su et al. used matched power/temperature ports; its full-text source equation and protocol remain unavailable here. I therefore make no allegation that the published calculation is wrong. The exact source-versus-observable comparison remains `UNKNOWN` pending full text.

## Exact result

For a lumped or finite-volume material, let `C=C^T>0` be its heat-capacity matrix and `K=K^T>=0` its reciprocal conductance/loss matrix. Let port `b_i` inject heat power and measure the conjugate temperature `b_i^T T`. Then

```text
C Tdot + K T = sum_j b_j u_j(t)
H_ij(s) = b_i^T (K+sC)^(-1) b_j
H_ij(s) = H_ji(s),  s>0.
```

The proof is one line: `K+sC` and its inverse are symmetric. Laplace uniqueness gives the same equality for the causal impulse responses. A continuum Fourier conductor follows from the symmetric weak form `∫∇v·k∇T + s∫c vT` plus fixed reciprocal boundary terms. The operator `C^(-1)K` is self-adjoint in the heat-capacity inner product.

For point-like nodes with diagonal capacities `c_i`, the temperature semigroup `E(t)=exp(-C^(-1)Kt)` instead obeys `c_i E_ij=c_j E_ji`. Equal initial temperature pulses therefore satisfy `E_ij/E_ji=c_j/c_i`, independent of time, conductance, and network geometry. Equal-energy pulses restore symmetry in the receiver-temperature output. Thus the symmetric contrast for same-`ΔT` initial conditions is exactly `(c_j-c_i)/(c_j+c_i)` when the cross-response is nonzero.

The two-node exact fixture `C=diag(1,4)`, `K=[[1,-1],[-1,1]]` makes the distinction concrete. For `q=exp(-5t/4)`, `E_12=4E_21` at every `t>0`, producing same-initial-`ΔT` contrast `3/5`. But the equal-energy impulse transfer is `H=E C^(-1)`, so `H_12=H_21=(1-q)/5`. At `t=1`, the same-temperature responses are `0.570796` and `0.142699`, while equal-energy responses are both `0.142699`. ## How this attacks the capability claim

Su et al. (2025) report transient asymmetry in linear materials with spatially distributed thermal conductivity, density, and heat capacity, including a constant-diffusivity case. The publisher record describes asymmetric transient temperature fields and experiments on a perforated plate with variable hole fillers. That observable can be physically real without giving a passive two-terminal thermal diode. If trials compare equal initial temperature increments, the stored energies vary with the local heat capacity; the theorem predicts the ratio exactly for point ports. If instead the intended claim is a matched heat-power-to-temperature cross response, the theorem predicts zero directional contrast for fixed reciprocal Fourier coefficients. If heat exchange with ambient or radiation/convection is part of the measurement, that environment is an additional port and must be measured in the energy ledger. Temperature-dependent coefficients, advection, modulated properties, state-changing contacts, or unmatched ports also escape the no-go.

The strongest equally informed control is the same coupon, same pair of calibrated ports, and the same pulse waveform and integrated heat in each direction. A single same-`ΔT` initial-condition comparison is not an equal-input control because `C` changes the energy deposited. The actual utility of a same-temperature transient response for a specific thermal-management task may remain; it should be named as such rather than as directionality of the transfer law.

## Decisive experiment

Use a graded thermal coupon with two identical, spatially characterized ports. First determine the local effective heat capacities and actuator/sensor gains. In one pair of trials, deposit the same *integrated heat* at each port with a calibrated pulse and record the conjugate temperature response at the other port. Repeat with identical initial temperature perturbations and independently measure the deposited energy. Hold geometry, ambient boundary condition, contact pressure, and sensor locations fixed; record all parasitic heat loss and verify that material properties are linear over the excursion.

The model predicts:

- Equal-energy power pulses: the two matched cross-response impulse curves coincide at every lag.
- Equal initial `ΔT` point pulses: the response ratio is constant in time and equals the measured heat-capacity ratio `c_j/c_i`.
- A time-varying ratio, or a residual difference after capacity and port normalization, rejects at least one of linearity, reciprocity, stationarity, matching, or calibration. It motivates testing explicit escape mechanisms such as nonlinear surface radiation, ambient leakage, convection, thermal-history-dependent contact, or active modulation.

This experiment needs calibrated heaters, paired temperature sensors, pulse calorimetry, local volumetric heat-capacity measurement, and boundary-loss accounting. It does not require a full-field high-resolution thermal map to test the two-port theorem, although imaging may be needed to verify the spatial port profiles. No source paper reports the cost of such a paired assay; no fabricated cost estimate is included.

## Prior art, Astra boundary, and evidence status

Transient thermal rectification and the role of thermal capacitance are directly established in Herrera, Luo, and Go (2017). Su et al. (2025) provide the specific recent transient-asymmetry target. An earlier linear-metamaterial paper by Su et al. (2022) explicitly attributes macroscopic asymmetry to tailored exchange with ambient radiation/convection. Weighted time-dependent reciprocity also appears in diffusion mathematics, including the 2015 optical-diffusion relation of Martelli et al. These sources preempt any generic novelty claim; the present derivation is a model-specific operator reconstruction and protocol distinction.
