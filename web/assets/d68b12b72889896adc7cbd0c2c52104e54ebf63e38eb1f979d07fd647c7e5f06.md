# Cycle 3 — do higher offspring cumulants change resistance risk?

## Ruling

Yes, a finite-horizon threshold probability can change after matching even the full multitype offspring mean and covariance and holding the control action fixed. The size of the change depends on the burst-state prevalence and endpoint. The relevant quantity is the complete action-conditioned offspring PGF (or multitype offspring-vector PGF) propagated through the finite-horizon threshold recursion. That is standard branching-process machinery. Once the PGCC-like state and its full offspring law are supplied to the multitype baseline, it reproduces the result exactly. The calculation does not establish a new control statistic or a biological risk model.

The construction below is an exact mathematical counterexample. Its PGCC-inspired probabilities are invented, not fitted. The biological literature supports the existence of stress-associated giant cells that sometimes produce proliferative daughter lineages; it does not support this particular probability law, independent repetitions, treatment thinning, or any numerical risk estimate here.

## Interface and measurable assumptions

Time is discrete by one declared clonogenic reproductive episode; the horizon is two episodes. `Z_t` is the number of viable cells in one resistance-relevant lineage at generation `t`. The resistance endpoint is the first crossing of a declared size threshold `M`: `τ_M = inf{t : Z_t ≥ M}`. This is a lineage-expansion proxy for establishment, not clinical relapse.

After each reproduction episode, the same fixed control condition independently retains each potential clonogenic daughter with probability `q = 4/5`. It is a common comparison condition, not an optimized schedule. The model assumes independent parent reproduction, independent daughter thinning, no density dependence or immigration, and independent daughter-type assignment with probabilities `(1−π_G,π_G)`; the founder is drawn from the same type mixture. These assumptions are measurable in principle with time-lapse lineage tracking, parent-state/ploidy calls, daughter clonogenic assays, and a fixed treatment exposure; none was measured in this audit.

Use two parental types: ordinary `O` and burst-state `G`. Under A, each parent type has offspring count `K=0` or `2`, each with probability `1/2`. Under B, O parents retain that law, while G parents have `P(K=0,1,3)=(1/3,1/2,1/6)`. Every daughter independently receives type G with probability `π_G`. Both type-specific total-count laws have mean one and variance one; the G burst law has third central cumulant one rather than zero. The aggregate B PGF is the mixture

\[
f_A(s)=\tfrac12+\tfrac12s^2,\qquad
f_B(s)=(1-\pi_G)f_A(s)+\pi_G(\tfrac13+\tfrac12s+\tfrac16s^3).
\]

For the main case `π_G=1/6`, the pre-control aggregate B probabilities at `K=0,1,2,3` are `(17/36,1/12,5/12,1/36)`; its mean and variance are both one and its third central cumulant is `1/6`. A sensitivity case uses `π_G=1/2`, still with matched per-type moments.

This is a full two-type branching model, not a hidden-mode approximation. Let `h(s_O,s_G)=(1−π_G)s_O+π_Gs_G`. Before thinning its offspring-vector PGFs are

\[
F^A_O=F^A_G=\tfrac12+\tfrac12 h^2,\qquad
F^B_O=\tfrac12+\tfrac12 h^2,\qquad
F^B_G=\tfrac13+\tfrac12h+\tfrac16h^3.
\]

For type vector `X` generated by a parent with total count `K` and i.i.d. daughter type probabilities `π=(1−π_G,π_G)`,

\[
E[X]=E[K]\pi,\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(X)=E[K](\operatorname{diag}\pi-\pi\pi^\top)+\operatorname{Var}(K)\pi\pi^\top.
\]

Both parental laws have `E[K]=Var(K)=1`, so each parent type in both models has offspring-vector mean `π` and covariance `diag(π)`. After independent thinning by `q`, `E[K_q]=Var(K_q)=q`, giving mean `qπ` and covariance `q diag(π)`. Thus the full multitype conditional drift and covariance agree for every population vector, not only for the aggregate count. The initial founder has type probabilities `π`.

## Matched control, exact threshold recursion, and result

Independent daughter retention replaces either PGF by `f_q(s)=f(1-q+qs)`. At `q=4/5`,

\[
f_{A,q}(s)=\tfrac{13}{25}+\tfrac4{25}s+\tfrac8{25}s^2,
\]
\[
f_{B,q}(s)=\tfrac{569}{1125}+\tfrac{76}{375}s+\tfrac{104}{375}s^2+\tfrac{16}{1125}s^3\quad(\pi_G=1/6).
\]

For both, `E[K_q]=Var(K_q)=4/5`. Consequently, at every current count `n`, both have the same one-step conditional drift and variance:

\[
E[Z_{t+1}-Z_t\mid Z_t=n]=-n/5,\qquad
\operatorname{Var}(Z_{t+1}\mid Z_t=n)=4n/5.
\]

The post-control offspring-vector mean for each parent is `q(1−π_G,π_G)` and its covariance is `q·diag(1−π_G,π_G)` under both models. In aggregate, both have `E[K_q]=Var(K_q)=4/5`, and at any total count `n` their common one-step drift and variance are `−n/5` and `4n/5`.

For a scalar offspring PGF `f_q`, form the substochastic safe-state matrix

\[
Q(i,j)=[s^j]f_q(s)^i,\qquad 0\le i,j<M.
\]

Mass with `j≥M` is assigned to an absorbing hit state. Starting from one founder, the exact probability of hitting by generation `T` is

\[
r_M(T)=1-e_1^\top Q^T\mathbf 1.
\]

Define `h_q=1-q+q((1−π_G)s_O+π_Gs_G)`. Then the post-control type-specific PGFs are `F^A_{O,q}=F^A_{G,q}=1/2+1/2 h_q^2`, `F^B_{O,q}=1/2+1/2 h_q^2`, and `F^B_{G,q}=1/3+1/2h_q+1/6h_q^3`. For type vector `x=(x_O,x_G)`, the multitype transition is

\[
Q_B(x,y)=[s_O^{y_O}s_G^{y_G}]F^B_{O,q}(s_O,s_G)^{x_O}F^B_{G,q}(s_O,s_G)^{x_G}
\]

on safe vectors `|x|,|y|<M`, with all other mass sent to hit. The A multitype version uses `F^A` for both parent types. Both multitype DPs agree exactly with their corresponding aggregate PGFs; the reproducibility script checks equality at `π_G=1/6,1/2` and `M=3,4`.

Exact rational results for two generations from one founder:

| Threshold | Law A hit risk | Law B aggregate PGF risk | B multitype risk | Difference B−A |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `M=3` | `1024/15625 = 0.0655360` | `7351088/94921875 = 0.07744356` | same exactly | `0.01190756` |
| `M=4` | `512/15625 = 0.0327680` | `46020918784/1601806640625 = 0.02873063` | same exactly | `−0.00403737` |

At `M=3`, B's risk is `1.1817×` A's when the burst-state frequency is `1/6`; an illustrative 7.5% cutoff accepts A and rejects B. With four independent initial resistant founders, the chance that at least one reaches `M=3` by generation two is `0.23748165` for A and `0.27561112` for B. At `π_G=1/2`, B's M=3 risk is `116368/1171875 = 0.09930069` versus A's `0.065536`, a 1.5152× ratio. These are conditional toy values, not estimated state frequencies or clinical cutoffs.

The ordering reverses at `M=4`: the law with the larger third cumulant has lower risk. Therefore no threshold-independent rule such as “more skew means more resistance risk” follows. The event probability depends on the full PGF over the horizon and threshold. At `M=3`, the B hit risk decomposes as

\[
p_3+p_1p_3+p_2\Pr(K_{q,1}+K_{q,2}\ge3),
\]

where `p_k=P(K_q=k)`; this includes a one-generation burst and descendants of one- or two-cell states. For A, only the two-founder generation-one state can hit, giving `p_2(2p_1p_2+p_2^2)=1024/15625`.

The threshold DP is the decision computation, but it is not a new statistic: it consumes the standard offspring PGF. A drift/covariance-only or Gaussian moment closure is deliberately under-informed and fails here. The strongest equally informed baseline is the explicit type-specific B PGFs; it returns B's numbers exactly while matching A's full typewise first two offspring-vector moments. This is evidence against a novelty claim for “higher cumulants improve a branching risk decision.”

## Costs, experiment gate, and falsifiers

The comparison charges the same control action to both laws. `q=0.8` means one fifth of potential clonogenic daughters are removed at each reproductive episode. Over two episodes, the expected number removed per starting founder is `0.2(1+0.8)=0.36` under either law, because both have pre-control mean one and post-control mean `0.8`. This counts modeled daughter-cell kill only; no pharmacokinetics, tissue toxicity, monetary cost, or assay latency is represented.

Acquiring the input law is not free. At minimum it requires independent tracked founders, a predeclared reproductive window, continuous enough imaging to distinguish death from missed daughter release, parent-state/ploidy labels, clonogenicity follow-up, and counts of all daughters including nonclonogenic/dead progeny. The experiment must preserve the full empirical offspring-vector law by parent state; estimating only mean, variance, or a third cumulant cannot identify the threshold probability. Report founder lineages, cell-hours, tracking failures/censoring, imaging frame rate, state-call errors, and clonogenic-assay cost. Those quantities were not available here, so no acquisition cost is claimed as paid or priced.

As a planning scale only, distinguishing the two model endpoint rates at `M=3,T=2` by the usual normal-approximation two-proportion calculation

\[
n\approx \frac{\left[z_{.975}\sqrt{2\bar p(1-\bar p)}+z_{.80}\sqrt{p_A(1-p_A)+p_B(1-p_B)}\right]^2}{(p_B-p_A)^2}
\]

(two-sided 5% size, 80% power, independent lineages, no attrition) takes about 7,348 held-out founder lineages per condition for `π_G=1/6`; at the `π_G=1/2` sensitivity it is about 1,041 per condition. This is for testing the endpoint difference, not estimating all PGF coefficients; training lineages and assay failures add cost. Clustering by organoid, variable windows, false lineage merges, and uncertainty in `q` can increase the required count.

The decisive empirical test is to freeze the two conditional offspring-vector PGFs from a training set, then predict first-hit frequency on held-out, independently sampled lineages under the same fixed exposure. A falsifier for the numeric prediction is a held-out frequency outside its preregistered prediction interval or failure of state-conditioned observed offspring to match the declared law. A falsifier for the broad higher-cumulant direction is already present in the exact `M=4` result: the risk order changes with the endpoint. For ecDNA, ordinary division still produces two daughters; ecDNA segregation alone is not a higher-total-offspring-count mechanism. A separate ecDNA model would need sister-daughter state vectors and their joint inheritance PGF, not a PGCC burst count transplanted by analogy.

## Literature and novelty boundary

- Zhang et al. report time-lapse and single-cell cloning evidence that most irradiation-induced PGCCs die while some proliferative cells undergo neosis and contribute to repopulation. This supports the qualitative state/burst motivation, not the probabilities or PGF above: [Molecular Oncology (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1002/1878-0261.12913), [PubMed record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33523579/).
- Zhang et al. report PGCC formation and daughter-cell production in ovarian cancer models, including resistant progeny in the PARP-inhibitor setting: [Science Advances (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adf7195). This is biological prior art for the mechanism, not validation of this branching law.
- Deng et al. (2026) report time-lapse PGCC budding and repopulation assays across cell/xenograft/PDX models and four paired rectal-cancer specimens after chemoradiotherapy. Their paper also says progeny fate/tumorigenic potential remains to be determined and notes the limits of in vitro/xenograft evidence; it does not supply a state-conditioned offspring-count PGF: [Advanced Science (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202519698).
- Day's cancer-oriented heterogeneous-cell model explicitly includes multitype branching, mitosis, death, mutation, conversion, and PGFs: [Mathematical Biosciences (1986)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0025-5564(86)90032-5). Bozic et al. use a multitype branching process for acquired resistance under targeted therapy: [eLife (2013)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00747). These are strong prior art against claiming that full PGFs or multitype resistance extinction calculations are new.
- Bukkuri et al. model ecological and evolutionary dynamics of a state-structured population with polyaneuploid cancer-cell (PACC) and proliferative states under continuous/intermittent treatment: [Scientific Reports (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-18137-4). State-structured modeling of polyploid-associated resistance is therefore also prior art; its outputs do not validate this toy PGF.

Novelty judgment: no new branching theorem, intervention, biological parameter, or validated biomarker. The defensible result is a transparent exact witness that moment matching is insufficient for a chosen finite-horizon threshold event, accompanied by the stronger full-PGF/multitype baseline that resolves it. Priority for any narrower prospective, calibrated predictor remains unknown; this is not a novelty-clearance search.
