# Cycle 2: observation gate for the risk-vs-T certificate

## Executive result

The current union-bound risk certificate is conditional on an upper bound for the **absolute number of viable resistant founders** and a bound on the **future rate of new escape founders**. Finite tissue/plasma data supply such bounds only under a stated sampling model: known total viable burden, a lower bound on every region's probability of entering the sample, calibrated per-cell/molecule detection sensitivity, and a time-stationarity or drift bound for the source process.

Under representative sampling, zero detected resistant cells gives an exact one-sided binomial/hypergeometric upper bound. But with no spatial coverage guarantee, there is **no nontrivial uniform upper bound** on either total founders or future source rate: put an arbitrary resistant population in an unsampled, near-zero-shedding refuge. It leaves the observed biopsy, ctDNA, and aggregate imaging unchanged. The current certificate must return `UNKNOWN` for whole-tumor founder count/source when that possibility has no quantified cap.

The failure is experimentally concrete. A 2026 breast-cancer barcoding study across two lines and four PDX models directly compared tumor pieces, needle biopsies, and cfDNA; clone distributions were patchy, single biopsies varied with location, and cfDNA shedding varied across models. This supports the counterexample as a real observation-interface risk, though it does not itself calibrate a universal coverage floor. [Serrano et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44320-026-00194-w)

## 1. Finite-sample upper bound when coverage is specified

Let a timepoint contain `M` viable tumor cells (or lineage-bearing cells), of which `R` carry the resistance state relevant to the certificate. A tissue assay samples `n` cells uniformly without replacement and calls a sampled resistant cell with sensitivity `eta`; false-positive rate is `f`. Conditional on `X` resistant cells in the sample,

`X ~ Hypergeometric(M,R,n)`, and `Y | X ~ Binomial(X,eta) + Binomial(n-X,f)`.

For the observed special case `Y=0`, the exact likelihood is

`P0(R) = sum_x Hypergeom(x;M,R,n) (1-eta)^x (1-f)^(n-x)`.

A one-sided `(1-alpha)` confidence upper bound is

`R_U = max{R : P0(R) >= alpha}`.

For perfect specificity (`f=0`) and iid sampling with replacement, this simplifies to

`q_U = min(1, [1-alpha^(1/n)]/eta),   R_U = floor(M_U q_U)`,

where `q=R/M` and `M_U` is a simultaneous upper confidence bound on viable cell number. For small `q`, `q_U approximately -log(alpha)/(n eta)`. The exact finite-population hypergeometric inversion is preferable when the sample approaches a large fraction of the tumor. If sensitivity is imperfect and specificity nonzero, use the likelihood above; do not treat zero calls as zero cells.

This bound needs all four inputs: a known sampling design, an effective number of independently assayed cells, detection sensitivity, and an upper bound on total viable cells. MRI/CT volume alone does not give `M_U` without a viable-cell-density calibration. Plasma molecule counts can give another bound only if a minimum per-cell shedding/capture coefficient `kappa_min` is validated: with `C ~ Poisson(b + kappa R)` and zero mutant molecules, a one-sided Poisson bound is `R_U = max(0,[-log(alpha)-b_U]/kappa_min)`. If `kappa_min` can be zero, plasma yields no finite upper bound on R.

For multiple predeclared spatial strata `j`, allocate error budgets `alpha_j` with `sum_j alpha_j <= alpha`; invert each stratum's likelihood and sum its `R_{j,U}`. The confidence level for the total is then at least `1-alpha` by a union bound. Strata can be lesions, image-guided regions within a lesion, or tissue sectors. This only certifies cells covered by the sampling frame; unlisted lesions need their own burden/coverage cap.

## 2. No-spatial-coverage theorem and counterexample

Suppose tissue cores all come from an accessible region `A`, while region `B` is unsampled. Compare two worlds:

- `W0`: all cells in `A` and `B` are sensitive; resistant founder count is zero.
- `W1`: `A` is identical, but `B` contains `K` viable resistant founders, for any `K` up to the total-cell capacity. Let the founders in `B` have zero or arbitrarily small release into sampled plasma during the assay window.

Both worlds have identical sampled tissue genotypes, ctDNA molecule counts (after adjusting the unobserved release coefficient), and imaging total volume. Hence the observation distributions have total-variation distance zero in the zero-shedding construction, for every finite number of deeper reads or more cells from `A`. Any purported upper bound smaller than `K` has zero uniform coverage over this admissible class. This is structural non-identifiability, not an underpowered confidence interval.

The source-rate version is just as sharp. Let an unsampled sanctuary emit resistant immigrants only after the observation window, or let its event-capture/persistence probability be zero. The entire finite data history is unchanged for any future immigration rate. Thus no finite *future* source-rate bound follows from past plasma/tissue samples without a stationarity or drift assumption and a positive event-detection bound. The honest whole-tumor output is `UNKNOWN`, even if a local biopsy has a narrow interval.

A randomized spatial sampling design can restore a probabilistic guarantee. If the tumor is partitioned into `J` equal strata and all resistant founders could be concentrated in one unknown stratum, sampling `b` distinct strata uniformly gives probability `b/J` of covering that refuge. Conditional on sampling it, if resistant cells occupy local fraction `q_local` and `m` cells are assayed with sensitivity `eta`, detection probability is `1-(1-eta*q_local)^m`. Therefore

`P(miss) = 1 - (b/J) * [1-(1-eta*q_local)^m]`.

For an adversarial, nonrandom choice of `b<J` strata, miss probability can be 1. Even a randomized sample needs broad spatial coverage before greater within-core sequencing depth helps. If no lower bound on refuge size/fraction `q_local` is supplied, the within-refuge detection guarantee also collapses.

## 3. Conditional source-rate bound

Distinguish two source mechanisms because their exposure units differ.

**De novo resistance per sensitive-cell division.** Let `D = integral_0^T divisions_S(t) dt` be measured sensitive-lineage division opportunities, and suppose a new resistant founder is observed with probability at least `rho` (including survival until sample, spatial inclusion, and genotype/barcode calling). Under a Poisson rare-event model with rate `mu` per division, the number of observed new founders is `K ~ Poisson(rho*mu*D)`. With zero observed events,

`mu_U = -log(alpha)/(rho*D)`

is the exact one-sided upper bound. With `k>0`, use the one-sided Garwood Poisson upper mean `lambda_U = 0.5*chi2_(2(k+1),1-alpha)` and `mu_U=lambda_U/(rho D)`. If only total-cell curves are measured, D is unknown; if `rho` is unknown or can be zero in a sanctuary, neither formula produces a finite uniform `mu_U`.

**External/unsampled immigration per day.** For a stationary Poisson source intensity `I` events/day, each observable with probability at least `rho`, and follow-up duration `H`, zero observed arrivals gives `I_U=-log(alpha)/(rho H)`. This says nothing about a source that changes after H. A future projection requires a declared bound on source-rate drift; stationarity is an assumption to be tested, not inferred from zero prior events.

Baseline non-detection also cannot distinguish a truly de-novo event from an initially present clone below the baseline detection limit. Barcodes identify descendants only if the genotype/barcode pair is assayed at baseline with a declared detection bound. A clone that was missed in tissue and sheds weakly into plasma is an observationally equivalent pre-existing founder.

## 4. Insert these bounds into the existing risk-vs-T program

The parent certificate has the form

`P(relapse by T) <= qbar_R*m_R(T) + integral_0^T qbar_E(t)*nu(t)*m_R(t) dt + integral_0^T qbar_E(t)*I_E(t) dt`.

Use simultaneous upper confidence envelopes `m_R,U(t)`, `nu_U(t)`, `q_E,U(t)`, `q_R,U`, and `I_U(t)`, with confidence-error allocation summing to at most `alpha`. Then the finite-data risk curve is

`Risk_U(T) = min(1, q_R,U*m_R,U(T) + integral_0^T q_E,U(t)[nu_U(t)*m_R,U(t) + I_U(t)] dt)`.

If the existing exponential decline envelope applies, `m_R,U(t)=n_R,U*exp(-delta_L*t)`, with constant upper rates, this reduces to

`Risk_U(T) = min(1, q_R,U*n_R,U*exp(-delta_L*T) + q_E,U*nu_U*n_R,U*(1-exp(-delta_L*T))/delta_L + q_E,U*I_U*T)`.

Only certify durations where this **upper** risk is below the predeclared tolerance `epsilon`. The residual-founder term falls with T; the internal escape term approaches a nonzero floor; an independent stationary immigration term grows with T. Under this bound, if `delta_L*q_R,U > q_E,U*nu_U` and `0 < q_E,U*I_U < n_R,U*(delta_L*q_R,U-q_E,U*nu_U)`, the minimizing duration is

`T_star = (1/delta_L)*log[n_R,U*(delta_L*q_R,U-q_E,U*nu_U)/(q_E,U*I_U)]`.

This `T_star` is a model-based risk-bound minimum, not a treatment recommendation. If n_R,U or I_U is not identified from sampling, plotting a precise `Risk_U(T)` is false precision: the gate must remain `UNKNOWN`. If the source rate is per sensitive-cell division, substitute `mu_U*divisions_S,U(t)` for `I_U(t)` and propagate that source term; do not add both representations for the same mutation route.

A further practical issue: the risk bound may demand fewer than one expected founder. With independent post-pulse establishment probability `q_R`, the exact residual-only risk from `N` integer founders is `1-(1-q_R)^N`; the union bound is `q_R*N`. For `epsilon=0.05` and `q_R=0.25`, even one founder exceeds tolerance, so certification requires ruling out every resistant founder. If the viable population has `M=10^6` cells and cells are sampled without replacement, ruling out a single hidden founder with 95% probability requires assaying at least 95% of all cells (perfect sensitivity); ten thousand cells from one biopsy are nowhere close. This is the stress test the risk-vs-T curve needs before it is called an observation-backed certificate.

## 5. Held-out sample-size discriminator

Run a blinded preclinical validation with neutral barcodes and a resistance genotype whose barcode can be counted jointly. Use two spatial conditions with the same total burden and same true resistant-founder count: (i) founders randomly mixed throughout the tumor and (ii) founders placed in one of `J=20` image-defined regions, with the occupied region blinded. Sample `b` randomly selected regions, then `m` cells per sampled region; collect matched plasma with a fixed independent-molecule budget and whole-tumor imaging. Measure true founder counts by endpoint whole-tumor dissociation/sequencing, which is unavailable to the proposed certificate but supplies the validation truth.

Freeze the coverage/sensitivity model and `Risk_U(T)` before holdout. Test sample budgets (for example, 1, 5, 10, or 19 regions at fixed total cells; then vary cells/region at fixed region count) so the test distinguishes spatial coverage from sequencing depth. The localized model predicts that one region has 5% chance of hitting an unknown refuge no matter how many cells are sequenced from that region; 19 of 20 randomly selected regions give 95% chance to include it before within-region dropout is counted. Uniformly mixed clones instead follow the cell-level binomial/hypergeometric curve. If higher within-core depth improves uniform but not localized detection while broader region count improves both, the iid whole-tumor interface is falsified.

For a formal 95%-confidence / 95%-coverage claim on the *founder-count upper bound*, require 59 independent held-out tumors per sampling stratum with zero undercoverage. If the true undercoverage rate were 5%, the probability of observing zero misses in 59 tests is `0.95^59 = 0.0485`; fewer zero-failure tests cannot reject a 5% miss rate at one-sided 5%. Thus compare at least 59 uniform and 59 clustered cases (118 total) for that claim. Any undercoverage invalidates that exact 95/95 claim and requires a larger holdout or a wider bound. The first goal is not a small p-value for average correlation; it is coverage of the stated upper bound and calibration of source-rate limits.

To choose the total number of assayed cells, derive the required count from the desired risk gate rather than pick an arbitrary depth. If acceptable residual risk is `epsilon_R`, then the union-bound requirement is `R <= N_crit=epsilon_R/q_R,U`. Under iid sampling with total burden `M_U`, sensitivity `eta`, and zero calls, exclude `R >= N_crit` at level `alpha` only if

`n_cells >= log(alpha) / log(1 - eta*N_crit/M_U) approx M_U*log(1/alpha)/(eta*N_crit)`.

Use the exact hypergeometric inversion for finite tumors. For source calibration, to make its future risk contribution at most `epsilon_I`, need calibration exposure `D >= q_E,U*D_future*log(1/alpha)/(rho*epsilon_I)` for a per-division rate, or `H >= q_E,U*H_future*log(1/alpha)/(rho*epsilon_I)` for stationary external immigration. These expressions expose when the certificate is empirically attainable and when required acquisition exceeds a tumor census or feasible follow-up.

## 6. Measurement assumptions and decision states

- **Count unit:** distinguish resistant cells, barcode lineages, mutation events, and independent future founders. A barcode can mark many cells; lineage count is not cell count. Collision/dropout must be measured, and recurrence risk depends on viable independent founders, not raw barcode richness.
- **Sensitivity:** use genotype-specific sensitivity from blinded spike-ins, including copy number, low tumor fraction, minimum clone size, and damaged/necrotic samples. Sequencing reads are not independent cells or molecules; count unique barcodes/cells and independent UMI/duplex molecules.
- **Whole-body coverage:** list sampled lesions and image-defined spatial strata, and supply either inclusion-probability lower bounds or a finite cap on unsampled burden. A single accessible lesion does not bound unbiopsied metastases.
- **Imaging:** volumetric response constrains total burden. It does not identify resistant fractions or a viable-cell-density upper bound without a calibration model.
- **Source stationarity:** specify whether source is per resistant cell, per sensitive-cell division, or external immigrants/time. Bound future drift and probability an event persists until detection. A historical rate alone does not uniformly bound future source intensity.
- **Risk:** propagate all nuisance and assay uncertainty jointly. If any needed confidence bound is absent, output `UNKNOWN`; a non-detection is not extinction evidence.

## Gate verdict

The binomial/Poisson observation bounds are exact under their stated inclusion and detection model. The no-spatial-coverage counterexample is exact and defeats any unconditional whole-tumor risk certification. The proposed held-out assay tests whether spatial coverage and event-detection assumptions are calibrated well enough to insert finite upper bounds into the existing risk-vs-T program. Until it passes, the robust certificate applies only to the sampled/covered compartment, not the whole tumor.
