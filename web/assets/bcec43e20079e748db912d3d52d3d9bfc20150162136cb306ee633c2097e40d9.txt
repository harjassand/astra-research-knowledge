R3 — Finite-latent mutual-information guarantee for the R2 Gaussian codec

New extension
-------------
R2's conditional-law TV guarantee does imply a retained-information statement when the latent Y has a finite alphabet. This gives a precise boundary around the continuous-Y failure mode: it closes the codec-to-MI step for finite Y, with an explicit dependence on |Y|, but gives no bound when Y is continuous or has unbounded effective cardinality.

Theorem
-------
Let Y take values in a finite set of size K>=2. Suppose the observation Z conditioned on Y=y is N(0,Sigma_y), with I<=Sigma_y<=A I for every y. Apply the R2 encoder to Z and let L be its discrete label vector. Decode L using the R2 decoder to obtain X'. For an R2 tolerance 0<epsilon<=1/2, the same encoder/decoder works for all y and satisfies

  TV(P_{Y,X'}, P_{Y,Z}) <= epsilon.

Furthermore,

  I(Y;L) >= I(Y;Z) - epsilon log_2 K - 2 h_2(epsilon),

where h_2(t)=-t log_2 t-(1-t)log_2(1-t). The number of labels is D=J^m, with J=max(4,ceil sqrt(8mM(A,m)/epsilon)); thus H(L)<=log_2 D=m log_2 J bits per archived label (the actual entropy may be lower). In particular, for fixed m,A and target additive information loss eta, choosing epsilon such that epsilon log_2 K+2h_2(epsilon)<=eta gives an explicit finite-label mutual-information approximation.

Proof
-----
For each y, R2 gives TV(P_{X'|Y=y},P_{Z|Y=y})<=epsilon. Since P_Y is the same in the two joint laws, averaging the conditional distances proves TV(P_{Y,X'},P_{Y,Z})<=epsilon. The random decoder satisfies the Markov chain Y-L-X', so data processing gives I(Y;L)>=I(Y;X').

Write P=P_{Y,X'} and Q=P_{Y,Z}; their total variation is delta<=epsilon. Their Y marginals agree. Let R be the common part of the two measures, of mass 1-delta, and write P=(1-delta)R_0+delta P_1 and Q=(1-delta)R_0+delta Q_1. This decomposition is valid on arbitrary observation spaces: take the measure with density min(dP,dQ) relative to P+Q as the common part. Introduce the binary mixture indicator B for each decomposition. The conditional-entropy chain rule yields

  H_P(Y|X) = (1-delta)H_{R_0}(Y|X)+delta H_{P_1}(Y|X)+I_P(Y;B|X),

and 0<=I_P(Y;B|X)<=H(B)=h_2(delta). The analogous statement holds for Q. Every conditional entropy of the K-valued Y lies in [0,log_2 K]. The common terms cancel, so

  |H_P(Y|X)-H_Q(Y|X)| <= delta log_2 K + 2h_2(delta).

Since the Y marginals agree, this is also |I_P(Y;X')-I_Q(Y;Z)|. Monotonicity of h_2 on [0,1/2] and delta<=epsilon give the displayed bound. Finally, L has D possible values, so H(L)<=log_2 D. No assumption on the distribution of Y within its finite alphabet is used.

Cost and limits
---------------
This is an idealized stochastic encoder/decoder with continuous Cauchy CDF/inverse and exact random draws, just as R2. The alphabet-size bound counts all label coordinates. The information-loss guarantee depends on K; for fixed target eta, a sufficient small epsilon is on the order of eta/log K (up to the binary-entropy term), leading for fixed m,A to label bits on the order of (m/2)log_2(log K/eta), plus the explicit constants in M(A,m). This is not a general continuous-latent result: when K is infinite, the finite-alphabet continuity argument disappears. It also does not control a joint process (Z_T)_T or the N75 scale-integrated information. One could only extend this to a continuous Y after proving a separate finite partition/quantization approximation and charging its distortion and acquisition costs.

Diagnostic instance
-------------------
For K=1000 and epsilon=1/1000, the continuity penalty is at most (log_2 1000)/1000+2h_2(1/1000), approximately 0.0328 bits. Combined with R2's m=1,A=2 arithmetic instance, this uses J=D=10,701 labels, or log_2 D approximately 13.39 bits per label. This is a consequence of the theorem plus the exact label arithmetic; the decimals are a diagnostic, not an additional proof claim.

Provenance
----------
This extension depends on R2 (same worker c09_l05, same session) for the conditional Gaussian codec. Its finite-alphabet continuity argument is independent of the c09 peer claims. It does not repair the continuous-Y integrated-MI gap flagged in c09_s01/c09_s02 and does not import their counterexamples as proof of an impossibility theorem for the N75 field.
