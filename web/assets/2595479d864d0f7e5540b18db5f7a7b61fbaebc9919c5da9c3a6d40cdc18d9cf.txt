NEW DETERMINISTIC LEMMA.
Let finite labelled graphs Gamma immerse in a finite rose F. All components have diameter <=dL and minimum degree >=129, and girth >=L>=3. Let D0 be a short-closure constant as in source planar.tex lemma closure. Fix epsilon in (0,1/4]. Choose U, eta, K0, C, I exactly as source extraction-constants:
 U integer >=max(3,ceil(96D0/epsilon));
 eta=min(1/(32U),epsilon/(64U));
 K0=ceil((Csep/eta)^2), C=K0(U+D0), I=ceil(192C).
Assume Gamma contains NO system of at most K0 positive-length cyclically immersed closed paths, total length between L and CL, at most I disjoint interval comparisons pairing inverse words on DISTINCT underlying undirected graph edges, and at most epsilon times total length unpaired positions.
Then the group obtained by coning each component of Gamma is word-hyperbolic. Its nullword filling function over a finite presentation is <= (64/epsilon) times word length, up to the fixed cost of replacing each cone filling by a finite set of cycle relators.

PROOF: FIXED-BOUNDARY REDUCTION.
Take a nonempty cyclically freely reduced nullword w of length n in F. A finite van Kampen diagram can be converted into a cone picture with outer boundary w, finitely many inner disks whose boundaries lift to closed paths in Gamma, and complement mapping to F. This is exactly source topology.tex cone-picture construction; its proof does not use the outer boundary lift to Gamma. Minimize total inner boundary length H among such pictures, preserving w. Tighten all inner boundaries cyclically and discard constant inner disks. They are positive and cyclically immersed. At least one remains, as w is nonempty and freely reduced.
General position with respect to one midpoint per rose edge pairs all boundary letter occurrences by embedded arcs in the planar complement; inverse letters occur at paired ends. Discard circle components.
If a pairing between two INNER occurrences uses the same undirected edge of Gamma, source topology.tex inner band surgeries reduce H by >=2 while preserving the exact outer boundary. For two different inner disks, merge them through their shared cone. For a self-pair on one inner disk, split its cone annulus and retain the complementary component containing the outer boundary, capping the new inner boundary in the same cone. The discarded other component is unnecessary to the filling. The arguments use only INNER lifts, so do not need an outer lift to Gamma. Therefore every internal pairing uses distinct underlying edges. Pairings touching outer boundary may have any lifts and are simply deleted below.

PROOF: QUANTITATIVE EXTRACTION.
Suppose, towards contradiction, n<=epsilon H/64. Split every long inner path as in source planar.tex into pieces of original length <=UL and >=UL/3, append <=D0L edges to close each piece, and leave closures unpaired. Intact closed paths need no appendage. Denote the number of pieces N. Girth and U>=3 give NL<=H; closure overhead A<=3D0 H/U<=epsilon H/32.
Include the outer boundary as one extra disk vertex before counting comparison intervals. The paired-interval lemma still applies: all it uses about each boundary word is cyclic free reduction to rule out monogons and inverse-letter self-runs. That is true for w; no path in Gamma is needed on the outer boundary. Its interval-count proof therefore gives <=6(N+1)<=12H/L pairs, assuming H>=L, which holds as an inner cyclically immersed loop exists.
Delete the exterior vertex and every pairing incident to it. At most n inner original occurrences lose their partners. Remaining internal pair intervals remain disjoint inverse comparisons between distinct graph edges.
The graph on N inner pieces formed by the remaining pairing arcs is planar. Delete at most eta N separator vertices so that every surviving component contains at most K0 pieces. Deleted original length M<=eta N UL<=eta U H<=epsilon H/64, and surviving pieces lose at most M partners.
For each surviving cluster Z, let H_Z include closures, b_Z count unpaired positions, I_Z count retained interval pairs. We have
 sum H_Z >= H-M >=63H/64,
 H_Z <=CL,
 sum b_Z <=n+M+A <=epsilon H/16,
 sum I_Z <=12H/L.
Discard clusters with b_Z>epsilon H_Z; they cost <=H/16 total length. Discard clusters with I_Z>192H_Z/L; they cost <=H/16. Thus positive cluster length remains, >=55H/64. Every cluster consists solely of closed cyclically immersed paths, so girth makes its positive length >=L automatically. Choose a remaining cluster: <=K0 paths, L<=H_Z<=CL, b_Z<=epsilon H_Z, I_Z<=192C<=I. This contradicts the assumed exclusion. Therefore H<(64/epsilon)n.

PROOF: FINITE PRESENTATION AND DEHN COST.
For each component choose a maximal tree and a root. Use one rose-word relator for each non-tree edge, consisting of the root-to-start tree path, the edge, and inverse root-to-end tree path. These finite relators normally generate exactly all component loop words. An arbitrary component loop of length h is a product of at most h conjugates of these relators, by inserting the tree paths at successive vertices and cancelling. Hence each inner cone disk of boundary length h has a filling using <=h such relators; the entire cone picture fills w using <=H finite presentation cells. The map of the complement to F contributes no relation cells. Thus Dehn area <=64n/epsilon. For an arbitrary nullword, free/cyclic reduction and conjugating whiskers add no relation cells, so the same linear upper bound follows. Finite presentations with linear Dehn function present word-hyperbolic groups.
