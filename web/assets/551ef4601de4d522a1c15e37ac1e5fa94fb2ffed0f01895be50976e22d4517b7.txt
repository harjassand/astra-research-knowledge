1. EXACT POSITIVE CASIMIR HEAT SEED, INCLUDING ITS NORMALIZATION

For traceless Hermitian h with eigenvalues h_i, define

 j_+(h)=product_(i<j) sinch((h_i-h_j)/2),
 sinch(x)=sinh(x)/x, sinch(0)=1,
 g_t(h)=exp[-Tr(h^2)/(2t)] j_+(h),     t>0.

Lebesgue measure is for the Frobenius metric on the D-dimensional real
space of traceless Hermitian matrices. Then, exactly on every tensor
fundamental representation, and in fact on each finite SU(q) representation,

 [integral g_t(h) (e^h)^tensor N dh] / [integral g_t(h) dh]
       = exp(t C_N).                                     (G2)

Consequently exp(t C_N)/Tr exp(t C_N) is the positive identical-product
mixture of local states r_h=e^h/Tr(e^h) under the classical density

 p_(N,t)(h) proportional to g_t(h) [Tr(e^h)]^N.            (G3)

There is no unknown quantum-state oracle in (G3). It is an explicit density
on D real variables given q,N,t. Acquiring a finite-bit sample is a separate
cost, discussed in Section8. At t=0 the seed is (I/q)^tensor N.

Full character/Gaussian derivation of (G2).
Let x=(x_1,...,x_q), sum x_i=0, and Delta(x)=product_(i<j)(x_i-x_j).
Put rhoW=((q-1)/2,(q-3)/2,...,(1-q)/2), the Weyl vector, not a density.
For an irreducible highest weight lambda, let
 A_nu(x)=sum_(w in S_q) sign(w) exp(<w nu,x>).
The classical Weyl determinant identities are

 chi_lambda(e^x)=A_(lambda+rhoW)(x)/A_rhoW(x),
 A_rhoW(x)=product_(i<j) 2sinh((x_i-x_j)/2),
 dim(lambda)=Delta(lambda+rhoW)/Delta(rhoW).

The Hermitian eigenvalue integration Jacobian is Delta(x)^2. Since
j_+(x)=A_rhoW(x)/Delta(x), the character trace integral has Cartan
integrand

 exp(-|x|^2/(2t)) Delta(x) A_(lambda+rhoW)(x).

All Weyl alternant terms have the same integral after a permutation of
x, because Delta changes by that permutation's sign. Writing nu=lambda+rhoW,
the trace integral is thus the common representation-independent integration
constant times q! times

 integral exp(-|x|^2/(2t)) Delta(x) exp(<nu,x>) dx.

Complete the square x=y+t nu. Delta is harmonic on the trace-zero Cartan:
the invariant Laplacian sends it to an alternating polynomial of degree
deg(Delta)-2, and every nonzero alternating polynomial is divisible by
Delta. Thus that Laplacian is zero. Gaussian averaging of a polynomial is
exp((t/2)Laplacian) acting on it, so the last integral equals

 Z_Gaussian t^deg(Delta) Delta(nu) exp(t |nu|^2/2).

Divide by dim(lambda), and then by the trivial-representation integral.
The result is exp[t(|lambda+rhoW|^2-|rhoW|^2)/2]. In the declared generator
normalization the quadratic Casimir is
(|lambda+rhoW|^2-|rhoW|^2)/2. The integral is invariant under local SU(q)
conjugation, so Schur's lemma makes its irreducible restriction this scalar.
Multiplicity spaces need an explicit further observation: in the unitary
decomposition of the tensor representation, exp(J_h)=(e^h)^tensor N is
already directsum_lambda[pi_lambda(e^h) tensor I_(multiplicity_lambda)] for
every h. Integrating acts identically on all equivalent copies, rather than
inferring that fact from SU(q) invariance alone. This clarification was
identified in c07_l05's phase2 audit and applies to any finite representation.
Thus (G2) holds on the entire tensor Hilbert
space, not only its symmetric sector. Colliding eigenvalues are handled by
continuous extension; Gaussian decay makes all integrals finite. For q=2,
h=(r/2)n.sigma gives j_+=sinch(r/2) and the original qubit radial identity.

The source premise here is ordinary Weyl character/eigenvalue integration,
equivalently the Kirillov character formula. Maher's primary wrapping paper
states that formula and reconstructs its Gaussian Fourier consequence.
This report uses its positive analytic-continuation version and shows the
specific normalization above. It is established representation theory.

3. EXACT LIE DIFFERENTIAL IDENTITIES IN ALL LOCAL DIMENSIONS

v_T; local unitary rotation differentiates to z_T. Therefore

 {J_T,k_N}=B_T k_N,       i[J_T,k_N]=R_T k_N,
 {J_T^2,k_N}/2=(B_T^2-R_T^2)k_N/4.                    (G5)
