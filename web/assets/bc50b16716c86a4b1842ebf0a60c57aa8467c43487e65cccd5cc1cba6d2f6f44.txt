## Dependency and exponent ledger

1. **Moving line curves and rational-function height (Lemma 9.1, Proposition 9.2; PDF pp. 35–38).** On a Q-defined line image, the selected coordinate `s=x'_j` is an integer parameter and, in the degree-one function-field case, all `y'_i,n` belong to `Q(s)`. Their reduced numerator/denominator degrees are bounded by the fixed curve degree (and the fixed-degree trace map for `n`); no coefficient-height bound is needed. If `R=A0/B0` is nonpolynomial of degrees `a,b≤D`, more than `a+b` integral selected pairs `(s_i,v_i)` give `a+b+1` homogeneous equations `A1(s_i)-v_i B1(s_i)=0`. The polynomial `A1B0-A0B1` has `a+b+1` roots and degree at most `a+b`, so the kernel is exactly the span of `(A0,B0)`. Maximal minors give an integral representative with coefficients `X^{O_{D,A,B}(1)}` from `|s_i|≤X^A, |v_i|≤X^B`; its nonzero resultant is an integer of polynomial size. At each counted pair, `B1(s)|A1(s)`, hence `B1(s)` divides the resultant. The divisor bound and `deg B1=b≥1` give `O_ε(X^ε)` possible inputs, uniformly in the moving coefficients. If there are at most `a+b` points, the fixed degree bound already gives `O_f(1)`. This closes the claimed coefficient-uniform alternative, conditional on the paper's fixed-degree graph bounds and Lemma 4.1's bounded multiplicity per fixed `x'`.

   In the polynomial branch, `(s-τ_i)^k | n(s)-θ_i` at distinct `τ_i`; hence `deg n≥1+d(k-1)` and `deg b_i=deg n-k≥(d-1)(k-1)`. For quartics, the only loss of one degree is equality `deg n=5`; its four critical points/values put every surviving integer value in the single fixed set `E_f` of Proposition 8.2. The proof of that proposition fixes a finite family before `X` varies and gives `#(E_f∩[-2X,2X])=O_f(X^{1/5})`; Section 11 charges it once globally, not once per curve, box, or prime interval. After deletion the quartic curve degree is at least `g=4`; in degrees 5–8 it is at least `g=(d-1)(k-1)`. No extra coefficient-height dependence enters these conclusions.

2. **Quartic infinite fibers (Lemma 6.3; PDF p. 27).** On the open `a_1…a_4≠0`, substitute `b_i=(n-θ_i)/a_i^2` in the bounded-degree cut equations. A one-dimensional `n`-fiber is exactly the common zero locus in `a` of all coefficient polynomials in `n`. For `dim Z≤3`, the fiber-dimension inequality bounds this base by dimension 2. For the hypersurface case `F(a,n)=0` with positive `n`-degree, the coefficients have no common codimension-one factor (else `F` factors), again giving base dimension ≤2. Degree stays bounded. The elementary slicing bound is `O(B^2)` integer `a`-points in a radius-`B` box, uniformly in coefficients; Lemma 4.1 contributes `O_f(1)` selected points per `x'`. Thus a box costs `O_f(X^{2U})`. With `X^{m-4j}` quartic mode-Q boxes and `U=u+j+τ`, the combined exponent is `m+2u-2j+2τ`; the certificate gives `m+2u<999/1000`, so this is `<999/1000+2/50000=3122/3125<1` (maximal at `j=0`). This is a genuine uniform positive saving.

3. **Paired-box lattice grouping (Propositions 6.1–6.2; PDF pp. 25–26).** The source's stated group has `O(X^{m-dj})` boxes and transformed sizes `|x'_i|≪X^U`, `|y'_i|≪X^V`, where `U=u+j+τ`, `V=v+j+τ`. The basis changes are integral unimodular; their coefficient sizes do not alter polynomial weights because each block is transformed linearly within a single weight. Applying the advertised common-shift inequality to the affine count yields

   `m-dj + Σ_{h=1}^d E_h(U,V) ≤ m + Σ_{h=1}^d E_h(u,v) + dτ < 999/1000 + 8/50000 = 24979/25000`.

   Hence any affine-count loss `ε<21/25000` leaves a gap below one. The finite interval list and bounded component multiplicities contribute constants only.

4. **Direct surface range (Proposition 10.1 and §11.1; PDF pp. 39–44).** Put `b=d-kη`. In the range `bη/d≤2495/10000`, the strict upper grid with `e=1/10000`, `b'=e(⌊b/e⌋+1)`, `η'=e(⌊η/e⌋+1)` satisfies

   `b'η'/d ≤ 2495/10000 + 3e + e²/4 = 0.2498000025 < (4999/10000)^2 = 0.24990001`.

   Since `D=max(d,b'+kη')≥d`, the surface threshold `δ₂=√(b'η'/D)<0.4999`. With the curve threshold `δ₁=1/2`, the affine-surface count has exponent `<δ₁+δ₂+ε<0.9999+ε`. At `b=0` and the `O_f(1/log X)` endpoint overhang, the separate finite weight pair `(e,d/k+e)` covers the range. The transition `η(1-kη/d)` is nonincreasing for `η≥1`; the listed stopping indices therefore leave no gap between the direct and number-field ranges. No arithmetic mismatch found in this boundary calculation.

5. **Dyadic summation and local sieve interface (§§11.2–11.3, Proposition 2.1; PDF pp. 43–45 and 6–8).** The fixed degree bound gives `p≪_f X^{d/k}`, hence `O_f(log X)` dyadic prime intervals. Each has a common fixed saving after choosing `ε` below the margins above; the finite interval list lets one take the minimum saving. The global quintic deletion is `O_f(X^{1/5})`. Summing the per-bin `O_f(X^{1-δ})` estimates over dyadic input blocks is geometric, `O_f(N^{1-δ})`, and hence supplies Proposition 2.1's `o(X)` tail. At content primes `p^e||c`, local admissibility forces `e<k` and `ρ_f(p^k)=p^eρ_g(p^{k-e})`; for `p∤c` the root counts agree. Thus the large-prime tail for `f=scg` agrees with that for its primitive positive-leading part once `X` exceeds the prime divisors of `c`, while the finite sieve retains the original local factors. At good primes `ρ_g(p^k)=ρ_g(p)≤d`, and `Σ_p d/p^k` converges for `k≥2`, giving positivity of the admissible Euler product. No missing prime-range or content-prime transfer was detected.
