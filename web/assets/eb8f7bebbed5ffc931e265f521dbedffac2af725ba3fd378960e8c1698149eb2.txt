1. GENERATOR IDENTITY RECONSTRUCTED

Let tau_m=(I+m.sigma)/2, k_N(m)=tau_m^tensor N, F=n.J, u=n.m, v=n-u m, and r=n cross m. Pauli multiplication gives exactly

  {n.sigma/2,tau_m}=u tau_m+(v.sigma)/2,
  [n.sigma/2,tau_m]=i(r.sigma)/2.

Summing over the N tensor factors, and using partial_m tau_m=sigma/2, yields

  {F,k_N}=B_n k_N,       [F,k_N]=i R_n k_N,
  B_n=N u+v.grad_m,      R_n=r.grad_m.

These identities hold for every |m|<1 and every unit n. B_n and R_n have scalar coefficients, so they commute with constant left and right multiplication by F. Hence

  F^2 k_N = (B_n^2+i B_n R_n+i R_n B_n-R_n^2)k_N/4,
  k_N F^2 = (B_n^2-i B_n R_n-i R_n B_n-R_n^2)k_N/4.

Adding cancels both mixed terms without assuming B_n and R_n commute:

  {F^2,k_N}/2=(B_n^2-R_n^2)k_N/4.

With H=Q_C/s^2+b.J/s and C=sum_i lambda_i n_i n_i^T, this gives

  M_H k_N := {H,k_N}/2
   = sum_i lambda_i (B_i^2-R_i^2)k_N/(4s^2)
      + B_b k_N/(2s),
  B_b=N(b.m)+(b-(b.m)m).grad_m.

Expanding B_i^2-R_i^2 on a scalar test function f gives the zeroth-order coefficient N^2u_i^2+N(1-u_i^2), drift coefficient 2Nu_i v_i+(v_i.grad)v_i-(r_i.grad)r_i, and Hessian coefficient v_i v_i^T-r_i r_i^T. Therefore M_H k_N=G k_N with

  Gf=V_N f+a_N.grad f+D_C:Hess f/(4s^2),
  V_N=[N^2 m^T C m+N(Tr C-m^T C m)]/(4s^2)+N b.m/(2s),
  a_N=sum_i lambda_i[2Nu_i v_i+(v_i.grad)v_i-(r_i.grad)r_i]/(4s^2)
      +(b-(b.m)m)/(2s),
  D_C=sum_i lambda_i(v_i v_i^T-r_i r_i^T).

As G is scalar in the matrix value, it commutes with the fixed linear map M_H. Thus G^k k_N=M_H^k k_N, and the unstopped Feynman-Kac semigroup represents e^(t M_H)k_N. This supplies the semigroup step needed by the stopped Duhamel argument; an identity only at first derivative would not by itself suffice.

2. POSITIVITY CHECK

Write P=I-mm^T and [m]_x z=m cross z. Then v_i=P n_i and r_i=-[m]_x n_i, so

  D_C=P C P-[m]_x C [m]_x^T.

For any z, C>=I and C<=L_c I imply

  z^T D_C z
    >= |Pz|^2-L_c |[m]_x^Tz|^2
    >= [(1-|m|^2)^2-L_c|m|^2]|z|^2.

Let x0=r0^2=1/[16(L_c+1)]. Since L_c>=1,

  (1-x0)^2-L_c x0
    =1-(L_c+2)x0+x0^2
    >=29/32+x0^2 > 3/4.

For the upper bound, |v_i|^2=1-u_i^2(2-|m|^2)<=1 and the subtracted sum_i lambda_i r_i r_i^T is PSD; hence D_C<=sum_i lambda_i v_i v_i^T<=Tr(C)I. The lower margin also makes the principal square root smooth on the closed stopped ball. The diffusion coefficient sqrt(D_C/(2s^2)) has covariance D_C/(2s^2), whose Ito generator is D_C:Hess/(4s^2), matching G. D_C need not be PSD outside this ball; the proof does not run the process there.

3. STOPPING AND WHOLE-DENSITY ERROR

For |m|<=r0, the drift terms obey |u_i v_i|<=r0, |(v_i.grad)v_i|<=2r0 and |(r_i.grad)r_i|<=r0. With T_c=Tr C, this gives the stated uniform drift envelope

  |a_N(m)|<=T_c r0/(2sqrt(N))+3T_c r0/(4s^2)+B/(2s)=beta_N.

Choose N large enough that beta_N<=r0/8. Each coordinate martingale has quadratic variation at most T_c/(2s^2). A trajectory starting at |m_0|<=r0/2 and hitting r0 must have martingale supremum at least 3r0/8 in Euclidean norm. Resolving this into three coordinates and both signs gives the safe bound

  P(tau<=1)<=6 exp[-3r0^2 s^2/(256 T_c)]
            <=6 exp[-kappa_1 N^(3/2)],
  kappa_1=r0^2/(256L_c).

The seed heat mixture has radial density proportional to

  r^2 sinch(r/2) exp[-r^2/(4t0)+N logcosh(r/2)],
  t0=2/N-(M+1)/N^(3/2).

For N>=(M+1)^2, t0 lies in [1/N,2/N]. With a0=2atanh(r0/2), the bad event |m|>r0/2 is r>a0. The inequalities f(u)=u^2/2-logcosh(u)>=u^4/48 for u<=1 and f(u)>=delta u^2 for u>=1, delta=1/2-logcosh(1)>0, bound the unnormalized tail by C exp[-kappa_0N], kappa_0=min(a0^4/768,delta/4). Integrating the scaled radius R=N^(1/4)r over [1,2] bounds the normalizer below by (7/3)N^(-3/4)exp[-(M+1)/2-1/12]. Thus p_bad<=C_(M)N^(3/4)e^(-kappa_0N).

For H on the full N-qubit space,

  ||H||<=h_N=(L_c/4)sqrt(N)+L_c/(2sqrt(N))+(B/2)N^(1/4).

The stopped path weight is at most e^(h_N). The stopped residual in Duhamel is bounded in raw trace norm by h_N e^(2h_N)P(tau<=1). Replacing the exponentially rare bad seed mass costs at most 2e^(h_N)p_bad. The target trace is at least one after dividing by the heat-seed trace: in spin j, Tr H_j/d_j=Tr(C)j(j+1)/(3s^2)>=0, so scalar Jensen gives Tr e^(H_j)>=d_j. Normalization therefore preserves the raw error bound. Since h_N=O_(M,B)(sqrt(N)), both tails remain exponentially small. An explicit N0 can be formed from the displayed conditions (heat threshold, N delta>=2, beta_N<=r0/8, and absorption of h_N into each exponent); no polynomial runtime follows from it.
