# Neutral mtDNA turnover: exact threshold and supply accounting

## Scope and status

This note studies one exchangeable postmitotic-cell model. It does not prove that any human aging phenotype is caused by mtDNA heteroplasmy. The mathematical ingredients are the classical neutral Moran chain; the application-specific accounting below is a conditional corollary. Prior art already covers turnover-driven heteroplasmy variance, mitochondrial-network time rescaling, and exact Moran threshold/takeover calculations. **No novelty or priority claim is made.**

## Interface

There are (Nge2) mtDNA copies in one cell. Replacement attempts occur at total rate (N\rho) per year, so one turnover cycle means (N) attempted replication/degradation replacements and ρ is cycles per year. At each attempt, a parent copy is chosen uniformly, an old copy is removed uniformly and the parent is copied into its place. All copies have identical replication and removal rates. The count (K_t) is the number carrying one focal, neutral allele. Unless stated otherwise there is no mutation or recombination at that focal allele after its initial introduction, no cell division, no copy-number change, and no allele-dependent mitophagy.

For (k\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}), one attempt changes (k\) up with probability

\[
p_k=\frac{k}{N}\frac{N-k}{N}=\frac{k(N-k)}{N^2},
\]

and down with the same probability; otherwise the count stays fixed. In continuous time, each of the two transition rates is

\[
q_{k,k+1}=q_{k,k-1}=N\rho p_k=\rho\frac{k(N-k)}{N}.
\]

## Exact finite-copy threshold result

Fix a pathogenic or operational threshold (h_*\in(0,1]), represented by (m=\lceil Nh_*\rceil). Starting with (K_0=k\le m), let τ be the first time (K_t\in\{0,m\}). Then

\[
\Pr_k(K_\tau=m)=\frac{k}{m}.
\]

**Proof.** In the embedded chain, the harmonic equation for (u_k=\Pr_k(K_\tau=m)) is

\[
u_k=p_k u_{k+1}+p_k u_{k-1}+(1-2p_k)u_k.
\]

Since (p_k>0) in the interior, this reduces to (u_{k+1}-2u_k+u_{k-1}=0). With (u_0=0,u_m=1), the unique solution is (u_k=k/m). Self-loops do not change the hitting probability. This is an exact finite-(N) statement, not a diffusion or Gaussian approximation.

In particular, a single new neutral molecule reaches an (m)-copy threshold with probability (1/m); at (N=750,h_*=0.8), (m=600) and the probability is (1/600\approx0.001667). It reaches fixation with probability (1/N), the standard neutral result.

## Turnover-rate comparison

Let (Y_j) be the embedded event-count chain and (P_t\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(N\rho t)), independent of it. Then (K_t\overset{d}=Y_{P_t}). For ρ₂>ρ₁, couple (P_t^{(2)}=P_t^{(1)}+R_t), with (R_t\sim\mathrm{Poisson}(N(\rho_2-\rho_1)t)). The event that the path has hit (m) by step (j) is nondecreasing in (j); consequently

\[
\Pr_{\rho_2}\!\left(\sup_{s\le t}K_s\ge m\right)\ge
\Pr_{\rho_1}\!\left(\sup_{s\le t}K_s\ge m\right).
\]

For this genotype-blind model, faster turnover cannot improve finite-horizon threshold-crossing risk; it only advances the same embedded random walk. Eventual hit-before-loss probability (k/m) is independent of ρ. The code computes the finite-horizon probability by uniformizing this continuous-time chain and provides a concrete example.

## Mutation supply times threshold establishment

Now allow every copied genome to acquire a new, unique neutral label with probability μ per replication event (an infinite-sites model). A homeostatic cycle has (N) replication attempts, so the expected number of new labels per cell-year is (N\rho\mu). Each label starts in one copy. Its own carrier count remains a neutral Moran marginal even when other neutral labels exist, so its probability of ever reaching (m) before loss is (1/m). By linearity of expectation, for labels born during a calendar interval of length (T),

\[
\mathbb E[\#\{\text{labels born in }[0,T]\text{ that eventually hit }m\}]
=\frac{N\rho\mu T}{m}.
\]

This counts eventual threshold-reaching lineages among mutations born in the interval, including crossings after T; it is **not** the number already above threshold at time T. With m = ceil(N h*), the rate is rho times mu times N/m, approaching rho times mu divided by h* as N grows. Thus increasing copy number lowers the neutral drift rate per calendar time, but raises the number of new mutation origins in the same proportion; in this long-run establishment accounting the two N factors cancel. It does not follow that the number of dysfunctional cells is copy-number invariant: establishment times, selection, functional effect sizes, cell/tissue thresholds, multiple mutations, and observation windows matter.

### Source-parameter translation (illustrative only)

An et al. report a mutation rate of (5.0\times10^{-8}) alterations per bp replication, an average of about 750 mtDNA copies per somatic cell, and inferred homeostatic turnover of 6.5, 9.4 and 11.5 cycles/year in the reported cell types (their turnover-cycle definition is one replication/replacement per mtDNA copy on average). For (L=16{,}569) bp, the Poisson mean per replicated genome is (L u=0.00082845), giving 0.6213 new mutation labels per 750-copy homeostatic cycle under this model. At the illustrative threshold (h_*=0.8), one label has (1/600) eventual threshold probability. The rate-weighted expected successful-lineage count is 0.00673–0.01191 per cell-year over ρ=6.5–11.5/year.

These are model translations, not an estimate of clinical or tissue dysfunction. The source reports selection against high-VAF truncating variants; the infinite-sites calculation treats labels as neutral. It also uses clone-level observations and model inference rather than observing every single-copy origin. The absolute number of functionally important variants, the allele-specific selection coefficient, and the relevant tissue threshold are not supplied by this arithmetic.

## A sign-changing counterexample: selective clearance

For mutant replication fitness (1-s), with (0<s<1), wild-type fitness 1, the probability that the newborn copy carries the mutant is

\[
f_k=\frac{(1-s)k}{N-sk}.
\]

After uniform death, the one-event probabilities of a (+1) and (-1) change are (f_k(N-k)/N) and ((1-f_k)k/N). Their difference is

\[
\mathbb E[\Delta K\mid K=k]=f_k-\frac{k}{N}
=-\frac{s k(N-k)}{N(N-sk)}<0.
\]

Thus increasing the turnover clock accelerates loss of this disadvantaged allele. If a mutant instead has a replication advantage, increased event rate can accelerate its expansion. This is why the genotype-blind assumption is load-bearing. An et al. report reduced expansion of truncating variants; deletion models include mutants with a replication advantage. Neither outcome can be inferred from bulk mitophagy rate alone.

## What would falsify this model in an experiment?

In an isogenic, nondividing cell preparation carrying a validated neutral mtDNA marker, hold copy number and allele-specific replication/removal equal while perturbing the measured homeostatic replacement rate. The model predicts (i) unchanged mean marker heteroplasmy, (ii) increased heteroplasmy variance and finite-age threshold-hit probability when turnover is faster, and (iii) the same eventual hit-before-loss probability for a fixed starting copy count. Allele-specific removal, a systematic mean shift at matched copy number, or an event-count-normalized threshold probability changing with ρ would reject the genotype-blind time-rescaling model. To connect this to damage rather than a neutral barcode, the assay must separately measure mutation consequence and genotype-conditioned removal.

## Practical acquisition and costs

Required per-cell quantities are (N), a high-depth mtDNA allele count (including the one-copy regime (1/N)), turnover-cycle rate ρ, allele-specific replication/removal bias, and a separately measured functional threshold. A source-style ∼0.3% VAF limit does not reliably see one copy at (N=750) (∼0.133%); the starting allele needs error-corrected single-molecule sequencing or a known engineered marker. Turnover requires a calibrated pulse-chase or replication/degradation tracer, not a mitophagy-flux snapshot alone. Complete event histories are not assumed available. For estimating a 1/600 single-origin hit probability with binomial relative standard error at most 20%, the usual variance calculation requires roughly (1/[p(0.2)^2]\approx15{,}000) independent single-origin lineages, with additional lineages needed for attrition, censoring, and assay error. If tracking is stopped at a finite age, use the finite-time model rather than the eventual probability.

## Novelty boundary

The neutral variance law is directly established by Johnston & Jones (2016). Aryaman et al. (2019) derive network-state modulation, turnover-rate rescaling and mtDNA-copy-number effects, including de novo mutations. An et al. (2024) estimate human somatic turnover and replication-associated mutation rates and simulate lifetime drift. A 2026 npj Aging paper already derives exact Moran takeover-time distributions and demonstrates synthetic cross-sectional threshold inference. The present output is therefore a transparent finite-(N) restatement and an accounting composition, with an executable finite-horizon calculation. It is **not** asserted as a new mitochondrial mechanism, a new stochastic-process theorem, or a result about human aging.
