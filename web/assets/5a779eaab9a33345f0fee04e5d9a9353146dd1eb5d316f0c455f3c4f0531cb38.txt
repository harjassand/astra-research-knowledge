STRONGEST CLAIM

The supplied arbitrary-bistochastic-instrument Petz-square construction has an exact factor-two Dirichlet SOS, but it is not a converse for positive selfadjoint selfcompatible channels. In every matrix dimension d>=2, its corrected marginal cannot be the depolarizing channel with contraction

  lambda_d = (d+2)/(2(d+1)),

whereas that channel is explicitly selfcompatible via a symmetric two-output channel. More generally, if an instrument-Petz square equals a depolarizing channel with contraction lambda, then necessarily

  lambda < ((d+2)/(2(d+1)))^2.

The strict inequality includes the equality case, ruled out by the Kraus structure of the identity channel. For d=2, the selfcompatible lambda=1/2 channel already violates the necessary non-strict cap 4/9 by 1/18. This is an exact representability obstruction, not a counterexample to EB rounding or to bounded-Holevo broadcasting.

1. RECONSTRUCTED SOS AND ITS INTERFACE

Work first on M_d with normalized trace tau=Tr/d. A supplied finite Kraus family satisfies

  sum_y K_y^* K_y = sum_y K_y K_y^* = I.

Put L(A)=sum_y K_y A K_y^*, M_y=K_y^*K_y, p_y=tau(M_y), omitting zero effects, and define the canonical measure-prepare map

  Psi(A)=sum_y tau(M_y A) M_y/p_y.

Then L is UCP and tau-preserving, hence an L2(tau) contraction: L^*L<=I. Psi is UCP, tau-preserving, EB, and Hilbert-space selfadjoint with 0<=Psi<=I. In particular Psi^*=Psi and Psi-Psi^2>=0. These claims use the two normalization sums and the positivity of each effect; no selfadjointness or positivity of L as a superoperator is assumed.

Let C=(L+Psi)/2 and Phi=C^*C, where * is the L2(tau) adjoint. C is UCP and tau-preserving. The actual joint channel is the equal average of the instrument-append channel

  rho |-> sum_y K_y rho K_y^* tensor [M_y/p_y] / d

and its receiver swap. Each prepared state has trace one, the instrument sum makes the map trace-preserving, and both marginals are C. Applying the physical tracial Petz adjoint C^* to both receivers gives a broadcaster with marginal Phi. Thus Phi is UCP, tau-preserving, positive and selfadjoint on L2, and selfcompatible.

The identity expands exactly:

  2(I-Phi)-(I-Psi)
    = I+Psi-(L+Psi)^*(L+Psi)/2
    = (I-L^*L) + (Psi-Psi^2) + (L-Psi)^*(L-Psi)/2 >= 0.

To check the cross terms, the last square contributes
  (L^*L-L^*Psi-Psi L+Psi^2)/2;
adding the first two terms leaves
  I+Psi-(L^*L+L^*Psi+Psi L+Psi^2)/2,
which is the middle expression. Each displayed summand is positive. Therefore I-Psi<=2(I-Phi), with no requirement that Psi<=Phi and no commutativity assumption. In the bounded-likelihood experiment from the common interface this gives the stated single-EB-map estimate e_EB <= (1/2)sqrt(2 theta r), conditional only on the supplied instrument construction and its residual definition.

2. NECESSARY SPECTRAL BOUND FOR AN INSTRUMENT-PETZ SQUARE

Use the real Hilbert space of traceless Hermitian matrices, dimension m=d^2-1, with inner product tau(AB). In a tau-orthonormal basis, let T and G be the transfer matrices of L and Psi restricted to this space. Both preserve tracelessness. Kadison's inequality and trace preservation imply ||T||_op<=1, hence ||T||_F^2<=m.

Write each nonzero effect as

  M_y = p_y(I+A_y),   tau(A_y)=0,   p_y=tau(M_y).

Completeness gives sum_y p_y=1 and sum_y p_y A_y=0. The canonical map acts on the traceless space by

  G = sum_y p_y |A_y><A_y|,

so G is positive semidefinite. If B_y=M_y/p_y>=0, tau(B_y)=1 and A_y=B_y-I. Its eigenvalues are nonnegative and sum to d, so

  tau(A_y^2)=tau(B_y^2)-1 <= d-1.

Consequently g=Tr(G)<=d-1. Also ||G||_F^2>=g^2/m.

Suppose Phi=C^*C is the depolarizing channel Delta_lambda(A)=lambda A+(1-lambda)tau(A)I. The transfer matrix of C is Q=(T+G)/2, and Q^T Q=lambda I_m. For lambda>0 this means Q=sqrt(lambda) O for an orthogonal O. Thus T=2sqrt(lambda)O-G, and

  ||T||_F^2
    = 4 lambda m - 4 sqrt(lambda) Tr(O^T G) + ||G||_F^2
    >= 4 lambda m - 4 sqrt(lambda) g + g^2/m.

Here Tr(O^T G)<=g because the symmetric part of an orthogonal matrix is <=I, and G>=0. For sqrt(lambda)>=(d-1)/(2m), the right side decreases over 0<=g<=d-1, so

  ||T||_F^2 >= 4 lambda m - 4 sqrt(lambda)(d-1) + (d-1)^2/m
              = m (2sqrt(lambda)-1/(d+1))^2.

Since ||T||_F^2<=m, this implies sqrt(lambda)<=(d+2)/(2(d+1)). Below the stated range the same conclusion is automatic. Hence lambda<=lambda_d^2.

Equality cannot occur. At sqrt(lambda)=lambda_d, the preceding function is strictly decreasing at g=d-1, so equality forces g=d-1. Equality in ||G||_F^2>=g^2/m forces G=I_m/(d+1), which is positive definite. Equality in Tr(O^T G)<=g then forces O=I_m. The formula for T gives T=I_m, so L is the identity channel on all of M_d. Every Kraus operator of an identity-channel Kraus representation is a scalar multiple of I (its Choi matrix has rank one), making every M_y scalar and G=0. This contradicts G=I_m/(d+1). Therefore the necessary bound is strict.

3. AN EXPLICIT SELFCOMPATIBLE CHANNEL OUTSIDE THE CLASS

Let F be the swap on C^d tensor C^d and P_+=(I+F)/2. Define

  B_d(rho) = 2/(d+1) P_+ (rho tensor I) P_+.

This is completely positive. A Kraus family is V_j=sqrt(2/(d+1)) P_+(I tensor |j>) for an orthonormal basis |j>. Since Tr_2(P_+)=(d+1)I/2, sum_j V_j^*V_j=I, so it is trace-preserving. The output is swap-invariant. Expanding P_+ and taking the second partial trace gives

  Tr_2[P_+(rho tensor I)P_+]
       = ((d+2)rho + Tr(rho) I)/4.

Thus both marginals of B_d are Delta_lambda_d, with lambda_d=(d+2)/(2(d+1)). This depolarizing channel is UCP, trace-preserving, positive and Hilbert-Schmidt selfadjoint. The displayed symmetric channel is its full-domain broadcaster. Since 0<lambda_d<1, lambda_d>lambda_d^2, so the necessary instrument-Petz bound rules out this channel from the construction in every d>=2.

For a small exact instance, d=2 gives lambda_d=2/3 and the instrument-square cap 4/9. If preferred, Delta_(1/2) is also selfcompatible: mix B_2 with the constant product broadcaster rho |-> I/2 tensor I/2 using weights 3/4 and 1/4. The 1/2 target exceeds the 4/9 cap by exactly 1/18.
