# Poisson sample-and-hold: exact 16-state calculation

## Model

Use two binary queues \(N=(N_1,N_2)\in\{00,01,10,11\}\). A controller memory state \(Y\) stores the last perfectly observed \(N\), so the joint process \((N,Y)\) has 16 states. Independent reads occur at rate \(\nu\); a read maps \((n,y)\) to \((n,n)\), including a read cost \(c\) even when \(y=n\). Between reads, birth rates are frozen at the full-information table row indexed by \(Y\). Each occupied output dies at rate \(\gamma\).

For equal target means \(m=1/10\), take the minimum-cap full-information table from the preceding two-queue result. Its target joint mass is the crossing point
\[
p_* = q_0(1/10)=\frac{6-\sqrt{34}}{10}=0.01690481051547,
\]
and, in units of \(\gamma\), its rates and cap are
\[
r=\frac{\sqrt{34}-4}{18}=0.10171954971363,\qquad
b=2r=\frac{\sqrt{34}-4}{9}=B_*/\gamma=0.20343909942726.
\]

| Held memory \(Y\) | Frozen \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2)/\gamma\) |
|---|---:|
| 00 | \((r,r)\) |
| 10 | \((0,b)\) |
| 01 | \((b,0)\) |
| 11 | \((0,0)\) |

The plant only accepts a birth on an empty coordinate. At every memory state, the sum of offered birth rates is at most \(B_*\).

Write \(\delta=\nu/\gamma\). The exact row generator on states \(x=(n,y)\), for distinct \(x,x'\), is
\[
Q_{(n,y),(n',y')} =
\sum_{i:n_i=0}\lambda_i(y)\mathbf 1\{n'=n+e_i,\ y'=y\}
+\sum_{i:n_i=1}\gamma\mathbf 1\{n'=n-e_i,\ y'=y\}
+\nu\mathbf 1\{n'=n,\ y'=n,\ y\ne n\},
\]
with diagonal equal to minus the off-diagonal row sum. A self-resetting read has no CTMC transition term, but remains in the cost rate \(c\nu\). For every \(\nu>0\) this 16-state chain is irreducible; solving \(\mu Q=0,\ \sum\mu=1\) gives the exact stationary model. Symmetry gives equal plant marginals.

The reported scalar criterion is average loss \(L(\delta)=1-\Pr_\delta(N=11)+\kappa\delta\), with \(\kappa=c\gamma\), i.e. unit rate cost for being outside the all-full state plus cost \(c\) per read. This makes the requested read cost explicit. The finite-\(\nu\) table does **not** preserve mean \(0.1\); it applies the full-information rates using a stale state. The computed means are reported with the success probability rather than silently treating them as fixed.

## Endpoints

As \(\nu\to\infty\), \(Y=N\) except for an \(O(\gamma/\nu)\) fraction of time. The stationary plant law therefore converges to the full-information law
\[
(\pi_{00},\pi_{01},\pi_{10},\pi_{11})
=\left(\frac{14-\sqrt{34}}{10},
\frac{\sqrt{34}-5}{10},\frac{\sqrt{34}-5}{10},
\frac{6-\sqrt{34}}{10}\right),
\]
with both means \(0.1\) and \(\Pr(N=11)=p_*\). But \(c\nu\to\infty\) for any \(c>0\), so this endpoint cannot minimize the costed criterion.

The \(\nu\to0^+\) limit is singular. At \(\nu=0\) memory never changes and the invariant law depends on the initial \(Y\); the limit from positive \(\nu\) instead mixes the four frozen-memory plant chains. Under \(Y=00\), each plant coordinate has stationary occupancy \(q=r/(1+r)\). Under \(Y=10\), \(N_1=0\) and \(N_2\) has occupancy \(s=b/(1+b)\); \(Y=01\) is symmetric. Under \(Y=11\), the plant drains to \(00\). At rare read epochs, the limiting memory chain has transition matrix \(P_{yz}=\rho^y(N=z)\), where \(\rho^y\) is the frozen-\(y\) plant stationary law. Its stationary memory masses, in order \(00,01,10,11\), are
\[
\alpha=\left(\frac{14+\sqrt{34}}{24},\frac1{12},\frac1{12},
\frac{6-\sqrt{34}}{24}\right).
\]
Consequently
\[
\lim_{\nu\downarrow0}\Pr(N=11)=\frac{6-\sqrt{34}}{24}
=0.00704367104811248,
\quad
\lim_{\nu\downarrow0}E[N_i]=\frac{8-\sqrt{34}}{24}
=0.090377004381446.
\]
Thus both endpoints are exact analytically. The zero-read limit has less joint success and lower means than the target-mean baseline.

## Exact finite-rate diagnostic

sample_hold_16state.py constructs the full 16-by-16 generator and solves its stationary linear equations with 50-digit arithmetic; it does not simulate trajectories. With \(\gamma=1\), the stationary results are:

| \(\delta=\nu/\gamma\) | \(\Pr(N=11)\) | each \(E[N_i]\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0+ | 0.0070436710 | 0.0903770044 |
| 0.3 | 0.0091804360 | 0.0924094790 |
| 0.46775104 | 0.0100000000 | 0.0932120919 |
| 1 | 0.011715152 | 0.094918921 |
| 1.32208977 | 0.0123847954 | 0.0955906355 |
| 3 | 0.014185225 | 0.097390385 |
| 10 | 0.015873462 | 0.099035497 |
| 100 | 0.016789471 | 0.099894134 |
| \(\infty\) | 0.0169048105 | 0.1 |

The matched-mean no-read constant-split baseline uses \(\lambda_c/\gamma=m/(1-m)=1/9\) on each empty coordinate. It has total shared cap \(2\gamma/9=0.222222\gamma\), stationary means \(0.1\), and joint success \(0.01\). The sample-and-hold policy uses the lower cap \(B_*=0.203439\gamma\), but for finite read rate its means fall below the baseline means. Its success crosses the baseline at \(\delta\approx0.46775104\). An equal-cap constant split at \(B_*\) instead has each mean \((\sqrt{34}-5)/9\approx0.0923280\) and joint success \(0.00852446\).

For \(\kappa=0\), the computed joint success increases toward \(p_*\) as reads become frequent. For \(\kappa>0\), use \(J(\delta)=\Pr_\delta(N=11)-\kappa\delta\) (equivalent to minimizing \(L\)); large \(\delta\) is penalized without bound. A high-precision derivative solve and a broad linear/log-rate scan put the maximum of \((\Pr_\delta(N=11)-0.01)/\delta\) near
\[
\delta=1.3220897723,\quad
\Pr(N=11)=0.01238479537,\quad
\kappa_{\rm break}\approx0.001803807443.
\]
This is a numerically located break-even read-cost coefficient against the matched-mean constant-split baseline. Rates in the scan above this coefficient did not beat the baseline, while this rate does for any lower coefficient. The candidate threshold is not a certified global bound over all \(\delta\); the fixed-\(\delta\) stationary values and both endpoint limits are direct CTMC calculations. The exact model and stationary solver are reproducible in the accompanying script.

## Assumptions and prior-art boundary

Reads are independent of the plant, have constant rate \(\nu\), are perfect and instantaneous, and carry no state-dependent cost. The memory stores the latest full joint state. There is no read delay, missed read, actuator lag, switching cost, parameter uncertainty, or added observation of plant transitions. Read cost is a fixed \(c\) per read, so its average cost rate is exactly \(c\nu\). If reads are paid only when they change \(Y\), replace this by \(c\nu\Pr(Y\ne N)\); that is a different objective.

Controlled observations in continuous-time jump MDPs are established prior art: Huang, Kavitha, and Zhu study joint control of observation epochs and control trajectories, including queueing and inventory examples ([Allerton 2019 DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ALLERTON.2019.8919744), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06128)). Their problem optimizes observation timing and actions under a discounted criterion. Here \(\nu\) is fixed, the action table is fixed from the full-information stationary problem, and the lifted finite CTMC gives an exact stationary diagnostic plus a singular slow-read limit. This calculation is a specialization and comparison, not a claim that sample-and-hold control or observation-cost optimization is new.
