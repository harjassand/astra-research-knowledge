# Cycle 2: observation cost and the stationary protein-reliability frontier

## Decisive result

Under the lead's original hard peak translation-completion cap `B`, fixed per-protein loss rate `gamma`, and objective of minimizing a stationary low-copy tail `Pr(N<h)`, noisy, periodic, delayed, and even perfect continuous observation do not improve the attainable frontier. All of those controllers still induce a causal predictable accepted birth intensity `0 <= lambda_t <= B`; the lead's pathwise coupling dominates every one by the no-observation constant-rate policy `lambda=B`. That policy attains the truncated-Poisson floor. Thus the optimal observation rate for this objective and resource contract is zero. Observation precision has no positive value for the stated reliability objective.

This does **not** say observation is free in a biochemical implementation. It says that, at fixed pointwise production capacity and for this one monotone objective, sensing is unnecessary to attain the best possible low-tail distribution. To make sensing matter, charge it against another resource or impose a different objective, such as minimizing average synthesis while meeting a reliability target.

## Explicit noisy/periodic observation model

Let `N_t` be protein count in `{0,...,M}`. A controller samples at times `T_k`; these may be periodic (`T_k=k Delta`), Poisson with rate `nu`, or chosen causally after prior observations. It receives `Y_k` with kernel `K(y|N_{T_k})`; examples include binomial detection `Y_k|N=n ~ Binomial(n,p_det)` and fluorescence `Y_k=a N+eta_k`. Between observations it may hold or vary an action based on the observation history and elapsed time:

`lambda_t = phi(Y_0,...,Y_k, T_0,...,T_k, t-T_k)`, for `T_k <= t < T_(k+1)`.

Assume this makes `lambda_t` predictable, `0<=lambda_t<=B`, births add one protein, the cap is `M`, and each present protein is lost independently at rate `gamma`. The observation rate `nu`, detection probability `p_det`, noise variance, and readout energy may be arbitrary. They do not enter the following theorem.

## Theorem (observation-independent reliability floor)

For every such controller and each `h=1,...,M`,

`liminf_(t->infinity) Pr(N_t<h) >= Z_(h-1)(q)/Z_M(q)`,

where `q=B/gamma` and `Z_j(q)=sum_(n=0)^j q^n/n!`. Equality is attained by the zero-sensor controller that accepts births at rate `B` whenever `N<M`. Hence optimizing over all sensor channels, schedules, and causal controller architectures at this hard peak cap gives the same minimum tail as no sensing.

**Proof.** Let `X_t` be the capped immigration-death chain with birth intensity `B` below `M` and death rate `gamma X_t`. Use common rate-`B` Poisson candidate births, thin them according to the controller's predictable `lambda_t`, and couple deaths of molecules present in both chains. The order `N_t<=X_t` is invariant. Thus `Pr(N_t<h)>=Pr(X_t<h)` at every time. `X_t` converges to the truncated-Poisson law `pi(n)=q^n/(n! Z_M(q))`, giving the stated floor. Taking `lambda=B` makes `N` exactly this chain and requires no observation. This is the CR-1 coupling already reconstructed in the sibling `circuit_reliability/FINAL_REPORT.md`; this report applies it to explicit sensing architectures and does not claim a new coupling theorem.

If readouts cost `c_obs` per sample, a sensing policy has observation cost `c_obs nu` (or a precision-dependent `c_obs(p_det) nu`). That cost can only make a nonconstant policy less attractive for this objective, because the zero-sensor policy already attains the floor. If sensor costs reduce the available peak rate, replace `B` by the resulting verified pointwise translation bound; calibrating that resource map is an experimental obligation.

## Why the average-resource frontier is different

The lead's exact `M=10, gamma=1` example shows a useful counterexample to treating perfect state observation as costless under an **average production** contract. Consider the state-dependent rule `lambda_n=10-n`. It has stationary `Binomial(10,1/2)` law, mean accepted production `5`, and

`Pr(N<5)=193/512=0.376953125`, `Pr(N=0)=1/1024`.

To implement this exact digital state-feedback law by sample-and-hold, suppose the controller reads the exact count immediately after each protein birth or death. Its jump intensity is `lambda_n+gamma n=(10-n)+n=10` in every state, so this idealized implementation requires exactly `nu=10` readouts per time unit. Charge each readout `c` in protein-completion-equivalent resource units. The feedback policy's total average resource use is then `5+10c`.

A no-sensor constant-arrival controller with attempted rate `q` has truncated-Poisson law. Its mean accepted production is its stationary mean `m(q)=q Z_9(q)/Z_10(q)`, and its low tail is `T(q)=Z_4(q)/Z_10(q)`. At equal production cost `m=5` (`q=5.1041217944`), it has `T=0.429147964`, worse than the free-observation feedback policy. But the constant controller matches the feedback policy's **total** resource budget when `m(q)=5+10c`. The crossover solving `T(q)=193/512` is

`q*=5.4324430254`, `m(q*)=5.2824134388`, `c*=(m(q*)-5)/10=0.0282413439` resource units per read.

For example, at `c=0.03`, the equal-total-budget constant controller has `q=5.4533569390`, mean accepted production `5.3`, and `Pr(N<5)=0.373782506`, which beats the event-sensing policy's `0.376953125`. This is a reproducible **finite-model crossover**, not a globally optimal policy theorem. It demonstrates that a free-observation comparison at equal mean synthesis can reverse once sensing is charged. At the fixed peak-only cap `B=10`, however, the unsensed `lambda=10` chain has `Pr(N<5)=0.050173`, substantially below either policy; do not present the average-resource example as a violation of the peak-cap theorem.

The calculation is reproducible with [observation_cost_threshold.py](../circuit_reliability/observation_cost_threshold.py). Its event-triggered exact sensor is an accounting model: a passive biochemical feedback mechanism may sense without a digital sampling event, while a noisy fluorescent assay may cost far more. The actual cost per observation and the feasible update rate must come from a specified physical architecture.

## Prior art and biochemical boundary

Partially observed control and observation scheduling are established fields. Borkar's finite-state ergodic-control treatment formulates average-cost control under partial observation through the nonlinear filter ([1998 Systems & Control Letters paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167691198000164)); his average-cost dynamic-programming work has a published correction ([2000 SIAM paper](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/S0363012998345172), [2007 correction](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/05064610X)). Huang, Kavitha, and Zhu study continuous-time jump MDPs with jointly selected control trajectories and observation times, including a queue and inventory example; their result uses a discounted criterion and is not this stationary low-tail theorem ([2019 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06128)). Eshragh et al. study a partially observed pure-birth process where each individual is detected with probability `p`, optimizing observation times for Fisher information about a birth rate; that is parameter estimation rather than output feedback reliability ([2025 PLOS ONE paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0328707)).

Biological feedback is also established, but its observation architecture matters. Banerjee et al. built an external electrochemical/microfluidic PID loop that measured a reporter product and adjusted IPTG to regulate LacZ-alpha in *E. coli*. They report 30-second sampling for an in-vitro dye system and 1,800-second sampling for the slower in-vivo loop, and used independently measured standards to calibrate the electrochemical readout ([2017 Scientific Reports paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-12655-2), article lines 42–45, 82–104). That is an instrumented population-level external controller, not a free autonomous molecular sensor that counts every target-protein event inside each cell. Autoregulatory protein feedback and noise control are likewise established biological motifs; they do not imply a digital controller that acquires the exact integer `N_t` at no resource cost.

The present result therefore claims no new POMDP method and no new biochemical capability. A potentially new biological contribution would require a concrete circuit whose sensor/readout and actuator are internal, plus direct measurement of its observation precision, latency/update rate, resource burden, target-protein birth intensity, and degradation rate. No such architecture or dataset is supplied here.

## Decisive experiment and next gate

Use a specified single-cell reporter and measure target-protein count trajectories, not only population-average fluorescence. Calibrate the fluorescence-to-count channel, estimate its detection kernel and sampling delay, quantify translation completion and protein loss, and report controller/readout expression or ATP/resource cost. Compare (i) constant maximum expression at a matched pointwise completion cap, (ii) a defined state-dependent feedback circuit, and (iii) the same feedback controller at equal total average resource cost. For (iii), include sensing/readout cost and actuator cost in the same units. Preregister `M`, `B`, `gamma`, the tail threshold, the averaging horizon, and how cell growth/dilution is handled.

Under the ideal hard-cap model, condition (i) must weakly dominate condition (ii) for every low-copy tail. A verified counterexample would identify a failed premise—such as burst births, altered degradation/dilution, or a mismeasured cap. The equal-total-resource comparison can favor (ii) only if the reliability gain survives its measured sensing cost; the illustrative threshold above gives a concrete calculation to replace with measured parameters. This experiment tests a specified biochemical realization; the theorem itself is a model result.

## Evidence status and limits

- Observation-independent cap theorem: direct specialization of the sibling CR-1 model theorem; internally proved there by coupling, prior domination literature already exists.
- Event-sensing rate and `c*` crossover: analytic stationary laws plus a reproducible Decimal bisection; finite model only.
- Noisy/periodic controller class: covered by the causal-intensity theorem for the peak-cap tail, but not optimized at fixed average resource.
- Prior art: primary sources read and linked above; this is a bounded check, not a full literature survey.
- Nature mapping: unresolved. No molecular implementation costs, calibration, or biological time series were collected.
