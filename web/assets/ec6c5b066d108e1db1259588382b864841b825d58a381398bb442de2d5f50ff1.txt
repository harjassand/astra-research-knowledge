# Cycle-2 check: partial-trace profile beyond irreducible Casimirs

Date: 2026-10-08  
Status: theorem in RESULT.txt §1 is valid as stated; its reducible-carrier Ky–Fan replacement is valid and sharp at the projector-trace step. The Ky–Fan quantity is not, in general, the exact maximum low-band mass over rank-constrained vectors. No cycle-1 file was changed.

## 1. General projector-mass theorem

Let $H$ be finite-dimensional, $\pi:G\to U(H)$ a unitary representation of a compact group, and $U(g)=\pi(g)\otimes\overline{\pi(g)}$ its vectorized conjugation action on $H\otimes\bar H\simeq HS(H)$. Let $L\ge0$ be self-adjoint and commute with every $U(g)$. For $t\ge0$, set

\[
 \Pi_t=1_{[0,t]}(L),\qquad D(t)=\operatorname{Tr}\Pi_t,
 \qquad \Gamma_t=\operatorname{Tr}_2\Pi_t.
\]

For a positive $d\times d$ matrix $\Gamma$, write
\[
 \kappa_Q(\Gamma)=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(Q,d)}\lambda_j^\downarrow(\Gamma),
\]
where eigenvalues are listed with multiplicity. If $X\in HS(H)$, \(\|X\|_{HS}=1\), and \(\operatorname{rank}X\le Q\), then
\[
 \langle\operatorname{vec}X,\Pi_t\operatorname{vec}X\rangle
 \le \min\{1,\kappa_Q(\Gamma_t)\}.                 \tag{A}
\]

Proof: put $x=\operatorname{vec}X$, and let $P$ project onto the column space of $X$, of rank at most $Q$. Then $x\in\operatorname{ran}(P\otimes I)$, so \(|x\rangle\langle x|\le P\otimes I\). Since \(\Pi_t\ge0\),
\[
 \langle x,\Pi_t x\rangle\le \operatorname{Tr}[(P\otimes I)\Pi_t]
 =\operatorname{Tr}(P\Gamma_t)\le\kappa_Q(\Gamma_t).
\]
The left side is also at most one. The last inequality is the Ky–Fan variational principle. It is exact for this projector-trace step:
\[
 \max_{P=P^*=P^2,\,\operatorname{rank}P\le Q}\operatorname{Tr}(P\Gamma_t)
 =\kappa_Q(\Gamma_t),
\]
attained by projecting onto top eigenspaces of \(\Gamma_t\).

For any carrier, \(\Gamma_t\) commutes with \(\pi(g)\): take the partial trace of \(U(g)\Pi_tU(g)^*=\Pi_t\). Thus it lies in the commutant of the carrier representation. No Casimir identity is needed.

If \(\pi\) is irreducible of dimension \(d\), Schur's lemma gives \(\Gamma_t=(D(t)/d)I\), since \(\operatorname{Tr}\Gamma_t=D(t)\). Hence \(\kappa_Q=\min(Q,d)D(t)/d\), and (A) reduces exactly to (1.1), including its cap by one. The proof uses only positivity, self-adjointness, and conjugation invariance of \(L\); any invariant non-Casimir \(L\) is covered.

A concrete irreducible-carrier check can even use an invariant operator that is not a function of the quadratic Casimir. For the adjoint representation of SU(3), of dimension 8, the conjugation representation on $HS(H)$ has decomposition $8\otimes8=1\oplus8_S\oplus8_A\oplus10\oplus\overline{10}\oplus27$. Assign $L$ eigenvalues $2,0,1,3,3,4$ on those respective summands. This is positive and invariant, but distinguishes the two equivalent 8 copies, which every central Casimir acts on with the same scalar. Its spectral projectors are still invariant; irreducibility of the carrier still forces $\Gamma_t=(D(t)/8)I$. Thus the proof of (1.1) does not require Casimir structure, even when the invariant operator acts nontrivially on a repeated isotypic multiplicity space.

The layer-cake identity gives the corresponding reducible-carrier energy consequence
\[
 \langle X,LX\rangle\ge\int_0^\infty[1-\kappa_Q(\Gamma_t)]_+\,dt.       \tag{B}
\]
Indeed, under the spectral law of \(L\) in state \(x\), \(\mathbb E Z=\int_0^\infty(1-\langle x,\Pi_t x\rangle)dt\). Also, because \(\Gamma_t\) increases in Loewner order, \(\kappa_Q(\Gamma_t)\) is nondecreasing, so (B) implies the endpoint bound \(\langle X,LX\rangle\ge\lambda[1-\kappa_Q(\Gamma_\lambda)]_+\).

## 2. Exact multiplicity bookkeeping

Decompose the carrier, with inequivalent irreducibles indexed by \(\alpha\), as
\[
 H\simeq\bigoplus_\alpha V_\alpha\otimes M_\alpha,
 \qquad d_\alpha=\dim V_\alpha,\quad m_\alpha=\dim M_\alpha.
\]
Since \(\Gamma_t\) commutes with \(\pi\), Schur's lemma for the commutant gives
\[
 \Gamma_t=\bigoplus_\alpha I_{V_\alpha}\otimes B_{\alpha,t},
 \qquad B_{\alpha,t}\ge0\text{ on }M_\alpha.
\]
Equivalently, \(B_{\alpha,t}=d_\alpha^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}_{V_\alpha}(\Gamma_t|_{V_\alpha\otimes M_\alpha})\). If the eigenvalues of \(B_{\alpha,t}\) are \(\beta_{\alpha j}(t)\), each \(\beta_{\alpha j}(t)\) occurs as an eigenvalue of \(\Gamma_t\) exactly \(d_\alpha\) times. Therefore
\[
 \kappa_Q(\Gamma_t)=\text{sum of the largest }Q\text{ entries in }
 \bigcup_{\alpha,j}\{\underbrace{\beta_{\alpha j}(t),\ldots,
 \beta_{\alpha j}(t)}_{d_\alpha\text{ copies}}\},             \tag{C}
\]
with all \(d=\dim H\) entries used if \(Q>d\). An equivalent allocation formula is
\[
 \kappa_Q(\Gamma_t)=
 \max_{\substack{q_{\alpha j}\in\{0,1,\ldots,d_\alpha\}\\
                         \sum_{\alpha,j}q_{\alpha j}\le Q}}
       \sum_{\alpha,j}q_{\alpha j}\beta_{\alpha j}(t).
\]
The trace identity is \(D(t)=\sum_{\alpha,j}d_\alpha\beta_{\alpha j}(t)\). Thus \(D(t)\) records only the weighted sum of these eigenvalues; it does not determine their Ky–Fan partial sums.

For a tensor-product carrier \(\pi=\rho\otimes\tau\), first decompose \(V_\rho\otimes V_\tau=\bigoplus_\alpha V_\alpha\otimes M_\alpha\), including all Clebsch–Gordan multiplicities, and apply (C). More explicitly,
\[
 HS(H)\simeq H\otimes\bar H\simeq
 \bigoplus_\gamma V_\gamma\otimes W_\gamma,
\quad
 W_\gamma=\bigoplus_{\alpha,\beta}
 \mathbb C^{N_{\alpha\bar\beta}^{\gamma}}\otimes
 M_\alpha\otimes\bar M_\beta,
\]
where \(N_{\alpha\bar\beta}^{\gamma}\) is the tensor-product multiplicity in \(V_\alpha\otimes\bar V_\beta\). Every invariant self-adjoint \(L\) has the form
\[
 L=\bigoplus_\gamma I_{V_\gamma}\otimes A_\gamma,
 \qquad A_\gamma=A_\gamma^*\ge0\text{ on }W_\gamma,
\]
so \(\Pi_t=\bigoplus_\gamma I_{V_\gamma}\otimes1_{[0,t]}(A_\gamma)\) and
\(D(t)=\sum_\gamma (\dim V_\gamma)\,\operatorname{rank}1_{[0,t]}(A_\gamma)\). The arbitrary matrices \(A_\gamma\) are precisely why a non-Casimir invariant operator is allowed. Their partial trace produces the \(B_{\alpha,t}\) in (C); multiplicity spaces must not be dropped.

## 3. Counterexamples and sharpness boundary

**A tensor-product, non-Casimir failure of the naive \(QD/d\) formula.** Take \(G=SU(2)\), \(H=\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\), and \(\pi(g)=g\otimes g\). Let \(\psi_-=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2\) be the singlet and \(P_-=|\psi_-\rangle\langle\psi_-|\), a rank-one projection commuting with \(\pi(g)\). Define
\[
 \Pi_0=|\operatorname{vec}(P_-)\rangle\langle\operatorname{vec}(P_-)|,
 \qquad L=I-\Pi_0.
\]
Here \(\|P_-\|_{HS}=1\); \(\Pi_0\) is invariant, so \(L\ge0\) is invariant. This is not the conjugation Casimir: its kernel is only the line spanned by \(P_-\), whereas the Casimir kernel also contains the triplet-sector projection. At \(t=0\), \(D(0)=1\), \(d=4\), and \(\Gamma_0=P_-\), whose eigenvalues are \(1,0,0,0\). Thus \(\kappa_1=1\), and the rank-one matrix \(X=P_-\) has low-band mass one. The naive reducible substitution \(QD/d=1/4\) is false. This example simultaneously exercises a genuine tensor-product carrier and a non-Casimir invariant \(L\).

**A multiplicity check.** Let \(H=V\otimes\mathbb C^m\), \(\pi=\rho\otimes I_m\), where \(\rho\) is irreducible of dimension \(d_0\), and choose a unit vector \(a\in\mathbb C^m\). Set \(X=(I_V\otimes|a\rangle\langle a|)/\sqrt{d_0}\). Then \(\|X\|_{HS}=1\), \(\operatorname{rank}X=d_0\), and \(x=\operatorname{vec}X\) is conjugation-invariant. For \(\Pi_0=|x\rangle\langle x|\), \(L=I-\Pi_0\) is invariant and non-Casimir when \(m>1\). Directly,
\[
 D(0)=1,\qquad \Gamma_0=XX^*=(I_V/d_0)\otimes|a\rangle\langle a|.
\]
Thus the nonzero eigenvalue of \(B_{\rho,0}\) is \(1/d_0\), and it occurs \(d_0\) times in \(\Gamma_0\). Consequently \(\kappa_{d_0}=1\), matching the rank-\(d_0\) state, while the false scalar rule would give \(QD/\dim H=1/m\). This verifies the carrier-irrep multiplicity factor in (C).

The same scalar-rule failure already occurs for the reducible Casimir example \(H=\mathbf1\oplus W\) in RESULT.txt: with \(\dim W=r>1\), its zero projector has \(D=2\) and \(\Gamma=P_{\mathbf1}+P_W/r\), whose largest eigenvalue is one; a rank-one matrix supported on the trivial block has zero energy. The error is not specific to non-Casimir choices.

**What “sharp” does and does not mean.** Ky–Fan is the exact maximum of \(\operatorname{Tr}(P\Gamma_t)\) over support projections \(P\) of rank at most \(Q\). It need not equal the maximum of \(\langle x,\Pi_t x\rangle\) over normalized Schmidt-rank-at-most-\(Q\) vectors: the earlier inequality \(|x\rangle\langle x|\le P\otimes I\) can be strict. For example, with a trivial group on \(H=\mathbb C^3\), identify \(H\otimes\bar H\) with \(\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^3\) in a fixed basis, and take \(\Pi_-=(I-F)/2\), the antisymmetric projector. Then \(D=3\), \(\Gamma=\operatorname{Tr}_2\Pi_-=I\), and \(\kappa_1=1\). But every product vector \(u\otimes v\) has
\[
 \langle u\otimes v,\Pi_-(u\otimes v)\rangle
 =\tfrac12(1-|\langle u,v\rangle|^2)\le\tfrac12,
\]
with equality for orthogonal \(u,v\). Conversely, the diagonal product projector \(\sum_{i=1}^3|i,i\rangle\langle i,i|\) has the same partial trace \(I\) and has a product vector of mass one. Hence \(\Gamma\)-only information cannot improve the universal Ky–Fan bound, though a particular \(\Pi_t\) can admit a stronger Schmidt-rank analysis. RESULT.txt §1 claims a valid upper bound, not equality for every low-rank vector.

## 4. Audit outcome

1. The irreducible statement (1.1)–(1.3) is correct for every positive invariant self-adjoint \(L\), including operators acting nontrivially on repeated irreducible multiplicity spaces; Casimir structure is not used.
2. For reducible/tensor-product carriers, the stated \(\kappa_Q(t)\) replacement is correct. Equations (C) and the \(A_\gamma\) decomposition provide its full multiplicity bookkeeping.
3. Any extension replacing \(\kappa_Q(t)\) by \(QD(t)/\dim H\) is refuted by the explicit tensor-product example above (and independently by the existing \(\mathbf1\oplus W\) Casimir example).
4. The Ky–Fan expression is sharp at the partial-trace projector step and is a valid energy-profile bridge. It is not asserted, and should not be read, as the exact rank-constrained spectral mass of each \(\Pi_t\).

All statements are exact finite-dimensional linear algebra and representation decomposition; no numerical oracle or external source check was required. Novelty and application to an operational memory claim are outside this memo.
