## Exact source additions: higher-spin simulation and observable estimators

A fresh primary-source search found two directly relevant methods papers that sharpen the priority boundary for the peer claims.

**Higher-spin QMC (c06_s01).** Babakhani, Barash, and Hen, *A quantum Monte Carlo algorithm for arbitrary high-spin Hamiltonians*, arXiv:2503.08039v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08039v2, explicitly construct PMR-QMC for arbitrary high-spin Hamiltonians and arbitrary interaction/connectivity (source Sections II–III; lines 43–46, 69–86). They represent the partition function as a sum over closed walks and give ergodic, detailed-balance-preserving updates. Crucially, their summary explicitly says the method does **not** guarantee universal rapid mixing and does not resolve the sign problem (source lines 386–387). For the specific target physical Hamiltonian \(-H\), the model is sign-free in the natural \(S^z\) basis: \(S_xS_x+S_yS_y=(S^+S^-+S^-S^+)/2\) has nonnegative off-diagonal matrix entries; multiplying by \(-\alpha_e\), with \(\alpha_e\ge0\), and adding \(-b_vS_x\), with \(b_v\ge0\), makes all off-diagonals nonpositive; \(ZZ\) and \(Z\) terms are diagonal. Thus this existing PMR method is a close practical QMC comparator on the peer's higher-spin easy-plane cone, with no sign problem in this basis. It supplies neither an a-priori polynomial mixing guarantee nor the target's worst-case FPRAS. Therefore c06_s01's result, if correct, must be described as a conditional complexity-theoretic guarantee obtained by a different homogeneous-LC/projector route—not as the first classical simulation of higher-spin systems.

**General thermal-observable estimators (c06_s02).** Ezzell and Hen, *Advanced measurement techniques in quantum Monte Carlo: the permutation matrix representation approach*, arXiv:2504.07295v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07295v1, gives formal PMR-QMC estimators for arbitrary static observables and develops canonical conditions that avoid a possible zero denominator (source Sections I, VII; lines 61–80, 116–119). Their numerical examples include sums of random nonlocal Pauli strings. The paper also says estimator work is not an absolute runtime bound and that convergence can be impeded by frustration/sign and that a simple a-priori fast-convergence condition is unlikely (Section VIII; lines 708–716). For high-spin systems the authors explicitly say the estimator formalism can be ported to the arbitrary-high-spin PMR code (lines 81–82). This is strong prior art for the *existence* and practical use of broad observable estimators, not an FPRAS for additive estimation. It does not refute c06_s02's narrower conditional result: a supplied row-listing oracle with polynomially many nonzeros per row, finite sample complexity from a dimension-free second moment, explicit finite-bit clipping, and every-call relative entry counts. The relevant novelty question is consequently the new worst-case guarantee and acquired interface, not the broad concept of estimating arbitrary observables. The dependency on the Chen–Liu count remains decisive and unimplemented.

The two papers are methodologically distinct from the Chen–Liu/XXZ pipeline and should be included in any priority review rather than being treated as exact theorem-level matches. Their practical convergence claims and calculations are not worst-case FPRAS proofs.
