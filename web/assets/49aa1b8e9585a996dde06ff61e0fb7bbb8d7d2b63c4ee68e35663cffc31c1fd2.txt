REVISION R3 — BALANCING IS EASY AFTER THE RATIOS ARE ACQUIRED
Worker c02_l06. 7 October 2026. No peer artifacts read.

PURPOSE
Sharpen the interface boundary in INITIAL/R2. The target is the two-hole
matrix G_ij=g(ij)=f({i,j})/f(empty), and the required row maxima are
r_i=max_j G_ij. A convex program can calibrate coordinate scales from G,
but that is not a count-free method for obtaining G. Also, the orthogonal
example fixes diagonal leverage and the Gram normalizer, not the full DPP
kernel, which retains the input F.

1. EXACT SCALING LAW
Let D=diag(d_1,...,d_n), d_i>0, and F'=D F D. For retained R=[n]\U and
each I subset R of size |R|/2,

  det F'[I,R\\I] = (product_{k in R} d_k) det F[I,R\\I].

Therefore f_{F'}(U)=(product_{k notin U} d_k^2) f_F(U), and, whenever
f_F(empty)>0,

  g_{F'}(ij)=g_F(ij)/(d_i^2 d_j^2).

This law is exact for rational or complex F and positive real d. It is the
proper congruence action for this hole-ratio interface; scaling rows alone
or columns alone would not give the displayed coordinate-pair denominator.

2. A CONVEX PROGRAM WHEN G IS ALREADY KNOWN
Assume G is symmetric, nonnegative, and every row has at least one positive
entry. Put x_i=log(d_i^2). Consider the geometric program in log coordinates

  minimize       sum_i exp(x_i)
  subject to     x_i+x_j >= log G_ij    for each G_ij>0.

It has a minimizer. A strictly feasible point is obtained by taking all x_i
large. For existence, any finite objective sublevel gives upper bounds on
every x_i; a positive incident G_ij and its constraint then give a lower
bound on x_i from the upper bound on x_j. The feasible sublevel is closed
and bounded. Strict convexity makes the minimizer unique.

At the optimum, KKT stationarity gives

  exp(x_i) = sum_{j:G_ij>0} lambda_ij,

where lambda_ij>=0 is the multiplier of x_i+x_j>=log G_ij. Since the left
side is positive, each vertex has an incident constraint with positive
multiplier. Complementary slackness makes that edge tight. Thus every row
has a neighbor with G_ij/(exp(x_i) exp(x_j))=1; feasibility makes all other
ratios at most 1. With t_i=exp(x_i)=d_i^2, the scaled input has

  max_j g_{F'}(ij)=1 for every i.

So there is no existence obstruction to the requested [1/4,1] balance once
the exact pair-ratio matrix is supplied. More robustly, if a certified
calculation rounds each optimum t_i upward to a value ttilde_i in [t_i,2t_i],
then feasibility is preserved and every tight-neighbor ratio drops by at
most a factor 4. Hence all scaled row maxima lie in [1/4,1].

Zeros matter: omit zero-edge constraints, but a row with G_ij=0 for every j
cannot satisfy any positive lower balance bound under diagonal scaling.
The theorem is only a post-acquisition calibration. Its inputs are the very
G_ij values whose count-free acquisition is in question. It does not supply
an evaluation or gradient oracle for h(z), nor does a numerical optimizer
run without those coefficients.

Bit/cost boundary. If exact rational G values of bit length at most B are
available, the optimum coordinates have bounded dynamic range: all t_i=2^B
is feasible when G_ij<=2^B, so an optimum has t_i<=n 2^B; and a positive
entry G_ij>=2^-B plus t_j<=n 2^B implies t_i>=1/(n 2^(2B)). Thus a factor-two
upward dyadic representation, if the optimum is numerically found and
certified, has O(B+log n) bits per coordinate. This is not yet a solver
implementation or an evaluation-cost bound for the original G. A complete
algorithm must give the convex-oracle precision/cost and account for how G
was computed.

3. SCOPE CORRECTION FOR THE LEVERAGE COUNTEREXAMPLE
For V=[I,F^T] with orthogonal F, VV*=2I and every diagonal DPP leverage
score is 1/2; log det(VV*) is also constant. These are the data held fixed
by INITIAL's rational weak-block family. However, the full projection/DPP
kernel is

  V^T(VV^T)^-1 V = (1/2) [[I,F^T],[F,I]],

which contains F in its off-diagonal blocks. The examples therefore refute
inference from diagonal leverage scores or the Gram normalizer alone. They
do not make the full DPP kernel indistinguishable as the weak parameter
varies; the full kernel exposes that parameter. Since it is the original
input matrix, access to the kernel is not itself a count oracle, but no
lower bound follows from this example against algorithms that exploit its
off-diagonal structure. The block family remains a witness against the
proposed diagonal-leverage/logdet surrogates, not against every method given
F.

4. ANOTHER NATURAL POSITIVE ESTIMATOR ALSO HAS A VARIANCE GATE
For a retained even set R, the skew Pfaffian polynomial

  K_R(z)=diag(z_R)F[R,R]-F[R,R]^T diag(z_R)

has one monomial per choice of I subset R, |I|=|R|/2, with coefficient
plus/minus det F[I,R\\I]. Independent uniform unit phases kill cross terms,
so E |Pf K_R(z)|^2=f(U). This is a positive, exact representation and
avoids the signed cancellation in R1; it is not automatically a useful
Monte Carlo algorithm.

Take F as q direct-sum copies of S=[[0,1],[1,0]], n=2q. For R=[n],
Pf K_R(z)=product_{a=1}^q (z_{2a-1}-z_{2a}) up to an irrelevant sign, and
f(empty)=2^q. With independent Rademacher signs, each factor is zero unless
the two signs differ, so one sample is nonzero with probability 2^-q and
the relative variance is 2^q-1. With independent uniform unit phases,
each squared factor has mean 2 and second moment 6, giving relative second
moment (3/2)^q. This exact counterexample is also recorded in
work/cycle4/hard_bcs_transfer_attack.txt, section 7. It refutes independent
random-sign/phase averaging of this norm as a general polynomial-cost
relative estimator; it does not rule out coupled ratio estimators, structure-
aware samplers, or an FPRAS. For this particular product family the target
pair ratios themselves are easy, underscoring that estimator failure is not
a hardness proof.

5. RESULT AND OPEN INTERFACE
The convex scaling problem separates cleanly from the missing work:

  (i) Given exact positive-support G, a convex geometric program has a
      minimizer that calibrates all row maxima to 1; factor-two upward
      coordinate rounding preserves the [1/4,1] interval.
  (ii) Ordinary diagonal leverage and log det(VV*) cannot substitute for G,
       as shown by INITIAL. Full-kernel information is not held fixed.
  (iii) The log-Hessian of the hole polynomial, signed determinant averages,
        and positive random-phase Pfaffian norms are candidate value
        interfaces, but the audited examples block the obvious surrogates
        and independent Monte Carlo estimators.

Still open: a polynomial-bit method that obtains multiplicative estimates
or a balancing certificate directly from arbitrary rational-complex F,
without first computing the hard parity partition values. No general
hardness reduction or count-free acquisition algorithm is proved here.
