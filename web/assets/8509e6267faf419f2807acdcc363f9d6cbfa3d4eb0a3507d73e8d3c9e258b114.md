# Materials/energy lead: contact memory in Mg–La-inoculated Li anodes

## Verdict

This pass does **not** establish a consequential new materials result. It does produce a proved, quantitative screening bound for an explicitly restricted diffusion model and identifies a sharp, source-grounded experiment: test whether the Mg/La-rich material retained at a stripped LiMgLa|LLZTO interface becomes a history-dependent transport barrier. Ji et al. show the elemental residue; Vema et al. independently show that deposited impurities can form a resistive, pressure-dependent particle layer with strip-rest memory in a different Li|LLZO system. Whether these are the same mechanism is UNKNOWN. Generic contact-area/current localization is preempted by substantial prior art.

The strongest concrete result is the conditional design condition

\[
\kappa^2\bar jQ<\mathrm{VSC},\qquad
\bar j<\mathrm{VSC}/(\kappa^2Q_*),
\]

where \(\kappa\) is an upper bound on the maximum local current divided by nominal-area current, acquired over the protocol. This follows by comparison with the constant maximum local flux in independent semi-infinite Sand diffusion columns. If the live interface is a uniform active fraction \(\alpha\), then \(\kappa=1/\alpha\), so the allowable nominal current falls as \(\alpha^2\). The full proof and assumptions are in [conditional_contact_depletion_bound.md](proofs/conditional_contact_depletion_bound.md).

The required \(\kappa\) is not identified by a scalar impedance measurement alone. In the parallel-patch counterexample in the proof file, the same total conductance can be concentrated on an arbitrarily small active patch, leaving global EIS unchanged while peak current amplification grows without bound. A controller therefore needs spatial flux data or separately validated uniform-conductance/geometry bounds.

For Ji et al.'s reported LiMgLa operating pair, 2.2 mA cm−2 and 1.1 mAh cm−2, their Table 3 VSC implies a conditional threshold \(\kappa<1.295\), or active fraction \(\alpha>0.772\) in the uniform-area idealization. This is not evidence that their tested cell has that active fraction. The figure-2f fit treatment changes this threshold: a fit forced through the origin gives \(\kappa<1.156\), while the source's free-intercept slope and Table 3 give about 1.268 and 1.295 respectively. These alternatives are reported as a method/provenance ambiguity, not a statistical confidence interval. A source data script recreates the fit and units in [audit_sources.py](code/audit_sources.py), with machine-readable results in [source_audit.json](results/source_audit.json).

## Source reconstruction and attack

Ji et al.'s primary Supplementary Note 1 gives

\[
\tau^{-1/2}=\frac{2i}{\sqrt{\pi D_{eff}}c_0F},\qquad
Q_{max}=i\tau,\qquad
\mathrm{VSC}=iQ_{max}=i^2\tau=\frac{\pi D_{eff}c_0^2F^2}{4}.
\]

The PDF equation is correct. The earlier inverse-diffusivity transcription was my error. Directly recomputing the Table 3 values from the rounded published \(D_{eff},c_0\) recovers its VSC values within about 0–3%. VSC is thus an algebraic reparameterization of the diffusion estimate, not a separate independent validation of it.

The source-data workbook has eight LiMgLa depletion points and three LiMg points. Its Fig. 2f fitted lines include nonzero intercepts (LiMgLa: slope 0.00845084 per mA cm−2, intercept 0.00117725 s−1/2, \(R^2=0.9763\)). Canonical Sand under the stated assumptions is through-origin. The source does not make fully clear whether Table 3's effective coefficient came from the unconstrained slope or another estimator. The origin-constrained fit gives a material VSC shift; resolving that estimator is required before using the number as a high-precision controller limit. No defect in the Sand identity itself is indicated.

The abstract does explicitly state a >70%-of-full-depletion overpotential threshold, but attributes it to phase-field modeling; it is not a universal experimental law. The paper's own Supplementary Note 8 calls 70% an ideal-contact cycling estimate, then adjusts to 53% for rough interface and ~30% with fatigue and SEI growth. Its model imposes a current-area normalization \(i_{app,loc}=I_{app}\xi S_0/S(t)\). Source Data Fig. 4f consequently reports model-derived effective current, not measured local current, and diverges as contact approaches zero. For the 1 mA cm−2 LiMgLa run it is 1.3347 mA cm−2 at 1.10 mAh cm−2, 2.401 at 1.50, 3.6698 near 1.94, and 28.135 at 2.10. These values illustrate model behavior; using them in the theorem requires same-cell experimental acquisition.

Ji's Supplementary Fig. 13 maps Mg and La at the LiMgLa|LLZTO interface after partial and full stripping. It does not quantify inclusion size/fraction, whether an interconnected layer forms, or its Li-ion conductivity. The non-stripped inoculant may help preserve nucleation sites; this could be beneficial, blocking, or mixed. Vema et al.'s Joule study reports a different mechanism in nominally 99.9% pure Li against LLZO at 5–15 MPa: oxygen-rich bulk impurity particles deposit at the stripping interface, and under pressure Li creeps through the particle gaps. Their strip-rest cycles exhibit resistance recovery followed by fast return to the prior high resistance on restart. Their uniform-particle Eqs. S18–S25 offer a quantitative model gate, but they explicitly observe nonuniform patches and do not study LiMgLa cycling.

## Resulting prediction and test

The next experiment should measure whether LiMgLa's retained inclusions become a history state that VSC alone misses. At matched starting interfacial resistance and same LLZTO batch/temperature/pressure/geometry, compare pure Li, Li97.5Mg2.5, and Li97.5(Mg–1 wt% La)2.5. Strip at the paper's target 2.2 mA cm−2/1.1 mAh cm−2 and matched lower rates; include controlled rest and restart. Combine three-electrode voltage and impedance, calibrated operando contact/current maps that bound peak \(\kappa\), and blinded cryo-PFIB/SEM/EDS at preregistered capacities. Separately measure same-condition \(D,c_0\), inclusion sizes/fraction/conductivity, and creep parameters before invoking either gate.

The decision-relevant prediction is not “Mg-La particles are harmful.” It is: after matching starting resistance, composition-specific residue coverage and pressure will predict stripping capacity and restart resistance beyond \(\kappa^2\bar jQ\) and the diffusion estimate if an inclusion-memory mechanism is active. The screening limit is the minimum of the diffusion bound, the same-material particle-creep capacity, and an empirically measured cycling-fatigue limit. A residue mechanism is falsified for this protocol if no interface residue is found or if residue measures add no prediction beyond initial impedance, pressure, and measured local-current concentration.

## Costs, limits, and novelty

This controller requires an upper bound on local flux, not only global applied current or impedance; acquiring it likely requires calibrated operando imaging or validated current inference. Cryo-SEM/EDS is destructive and is suitable for mechanism calibration, not real-time control. The papers provide no costed instrumentation/runtime/cell-count plan, and no experiment or external review was performed in this pass.

The current-amplification inequality is a model-derived sufficient bound, not a claim of novelty: local hotspot phase-field work dates to at least 2022, and 2024/2026 studies already investigate geometry-, roughness-, pressure-, and time-dependent amplification. The potentially new interface is the cross-material question linking the intentional La-bearing inoculant residue to impurity-driven contact memory; no priority claim can be made. If quantitative residue conductivity/coverage cannot be acquired, stop at this conditional screening envelope. See [SOURCES.md](source_notes/SOURCES.md) and [FAILURES.md](failures/FAILURES.md).
