N06 — Evolutionary acquisition of predictive heredity
Append-only erratum v4; 2026-10-09. v1, v2, and v3 remain unchanged and frozen.

1. Refuted formula in frozen v2 §6

The shared-environment branching model in v2 has the correct per-edge offspring law and its conservative pathwise Galton–Watson lower bound remains valid. However, v2's claimed “exact extinction recursion”

    q_z = Σ_{z'} R(z,z') (1−ξ_z+ξ_z q_{z'})^B

and the conclusion “extinction from n founders in edge z has probability q_z^n” are false in general for a generation-level branching process in a common random environment. The failure is load-bearing for any use of exact extinction probabilities or multi-founder probabilities. Do not use these formulas.

Reason. Conditional on the entire future environmental path ω, the descendant trees of n founders are independent, but their common environment makes their extinction events dependent after the environmental path is averaged out. If Q_z(ω) is the conditional (quenched) extinction probability of one founder given the full future path starting at environmental state z, then the exact annealed probability from n founders is

    q_z^{(n)} = E[ Q_z(ω)^n | Z_0=z ],                           (16)

not 

    ( E[Q_z(ω)|Z_0=z] )^n = (q_z^{(1)})^n,

unless Q_z(ω) is almost surely constant under that conditional path law. Further, with offspring p.g.f. f_z and next environment z', the pathwise recursion is

    Q_z(ω) = f_z(Q_{z'}(shift ω)),

where z' is the next state on that same environment path. Consequently, the one-founder annealed recursion is

    q_z^{(1)} = Σ_{z'}R(z,z') E[ f_z(Q_{z'}(ω')) | Z_0=z' ],   (17)

where ω' is the future path after z'. It is generally not

    Σ_{z'}R(z,z') f_z(q_{z'}^{(1)}).

For Binomial(B,ξ_z), f_z(x)=(1−ξ_z+ξ_zx)^B is strictly convex for B≥2 and 0<ξ_z<1. Jensen's inequality shows the missing quenched-path variation cannot in general be replaced by the annealed mean inside f_z. Equation (16) is the correct general n-founder relation; (17) requires the distribution of the future quenched extinction probability, not just its mean.

2. Irreducible counterexample to the scalar/product claim

Let Z_t be IID with states G and D, each of probability 1/2. This is a finite irreducible stationary Markov environment. In state G, every parent has exactly four children, so f_G(x)=x^4 and mean m_G=4. In state D, a parent has zero children with probability 1/2 and two children with probability 1/2, so f_D(x)=(1+x^2)/2 and m_D=1. The stationary log mean is

    E log m_{Z_t} = (1/2)log 4 + (1/2)log 1 = log 2 > 0.

The offspring p.g.f.s have finite means, p_0+p_1<1 in both environments, and the relevant logarithmic integrability is automatic. The Athreya–Karlin BPRE criterion below therefore gives positive survival probability, hence the quenched extinction variable Q(ω) is not identically 1. If Q(ω) were a deterministic constant q under the stationary environment path law, the pathwise recursion and IID environment would force both q=f_G(q)=q^4 and q=f_D(q)=(1+q^2)/2. Their only common solution in [0,1] is q=1, contradicting positive survival. Thus Q is non-degenerate, so Var(Q)>0 and

    q^{(2)} = E[Q^2] > (E Q)^2 = (q^{(1)})^2.

This directly refutes the n-founder product claim under an irreducible common environment. For a fixed initial state G, the same conclusion follows because the subsequent IID environment is unchanged and the one-founder quenched extinction variable is Q_G(ω)=Q(shift ω)^4, a nonconstant transform of a non-degenerate variable.

The example is a counterexample to the claimed formula, not a model of the biological predictor in v2. It isolates the shared-environment dependence that the erroneous formula omitted.

3. Exact scope of surviving finite-founder claims

The following claims from v2 remain distinct and valid within their stated scope:

(a) The deterministic infinite-population log-growth identity and the mutation-score equations are unaffected.

(b) The finite-founder reproduction law with a common exogenous generation-level Markov environment is a scalar branching process in a random environment (BPRE), with per-edge p.g.f.

    f_z(x)=(1−ξ_z+ξ_zx)^B,
    ξ_z=s exp(−c)K(e'|e),  z=(e,e').

For B≥2, finite edge state space, strictly positive environmental transitions and K>0, this model satisfies the standard non-degeneracy and integrability hypotheses of the Athreya–Karlin extinction criterion: its edge environment is a finite irreducible positive-recurrent Markov chain; each mean Bξ_z is finite and positive; p_0(z)+p_1(z)<1; |log(1−p_0(z))|<∞; and log f'_z(1)=log(Bξ_z) is finite. Therefore the source-applied sign criterion remains

    λ = E log(Bξ_Z) > 0  ⇔  positive probability of non-extinction,
    λ ≤ 0            ⇔  extinction almost surely,

under those hypotheses. This does not provide the exact probability of extinction, and it does not imply fixation at finite carrying capacity. The original Athreya–Karlin paper is “On Branching Processes with Random Environments: I: Extinction Probabilities,” Annals of Mathematical Statistics 42 (1971), DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177693150. The direct full text was unavailable in the prior v2 check; the statement and hypotheses of its Theorem 4 were checked through a secondary source, so the application remains source-dependent rather than independently proved in this record.

(c) The v2 lower bound using ξ_min remains valid, including for n founders. If ξ_min>1/B, couple every actual offspring trial to an independent uniform U and count a lower-process child whenever U≤ξ_min. Since every actual edge success probability satisfies ξ_z≥ξ_min, the actual process pathwise contains a standard environment-independent Galton–Watson process with offspring Binomial(B,ξ_min). Its n founder family trees are independent and its extinction probability is q_min^n, where q_min is the smallest root of q=(1−ξ_min+ξ_min q)^B. Hence actual survival is at least 1−q_min^n. This is a conservative coupling bound, not the false exact environmental formula above.

(d) The v3 finite-founder acquisition theorem is unaffected: after mutation reaches the target, its lower process is coupled to the same kind of environment-independent Binomial(B,p_*) Galton–Watson process. Its single-target-founder survival lower bound is 1−q_*; combining with the preceding path probability gives (13). The statement applies to unbounded branching with finite founder number and shared exogenous environment, not to finite carrying-capacity fixation.

4. Correct exact representation and source boundary

For the generation-level BPRE, keep the future environment path in the state of the extinction calculation. The quenched variable Q_z(ω) is defined by the limit of nested p.g.f.s along the path (when the limit exists):

    Q_z(ω)=lim_{T→∞} f_{Z_0}( f_{Z_1}( ... f_{Z_{T−1}}(0) ... )),
    Z_0=z.

Then (16) gives the annealed extinction probability for n founders. In the finite-state Markov case, this is a distribution-valued recursion for Q_z indexed by current environment state. Tracking only the scalar first moment q_z loses the shared-path correlations needed for extinction probabilities.

Greco and Levine, “Branching in a Markovian Environment,” Markov Processes and Related Fields 29(1) (2023), 1–33, DOI 10.46298/mpfr.2023.1662, give an exact matrix-valued extinction generating function for their BPME; their event clock updates the environment after each individual reproduction, unlike the generation-level common BPRE here. It is useful as a reminder that the exact extinction object depends on the environment/reproduction lifecycle, but its formula is not transferred to the v2 model.

6. Counterexample also refutes the scalar one-founder recursion

In the IID G/D example of §2, let Q be the quenched one-founder extinction probability under the stationary path law. The pathwise identity is Q=f_{Z_0}(Q_shift), and Q_shift has the same distribution as Q and is independent of Z_0. The actual environment-conditioned one-founder probabilities therefore satisfy

    q_G^{(1)} = E[Q^4],
    q_D^{(1)} = E[(1+Q^2)/2].

A scalar substitution of the annealed mean q̄=E Q would instead use q_G^{(1)}=(q̄)^4 and q_D^{(1)}=(1+(q̄)^2)/2. The non-degeneracy proved in §2 and strict convexity of x↦x^2 and x↦x^4 imply both Jensen inequalities are strict. Thus the scalar one-founder recursion itself fails here, in addition to the n-founder product formula.

7. Algebraic clarification to §§2 and 6

The scalar recursion printed in v2 is state-indexed, so in the IID G/D counterexample its proposed right sides are

    q_G = (q_G^4 + q_D^4)/2,
    q_D = (f_D(q_G) + f_D(q_D))/2,

not f_G(E Q) and f_D(E Q) respectively. The reason these equations still fail is state-conditional quenched variation. Let Q denote the quenched extinction probability when the current IID environment state is sampled from its stationary law. If Q were constant, the pathwise recursion would require that constant to be a common fixed point of f_G and f_D; only 1 is common. The positive Lyapunov log mean gives positive survival probability by the source criterion, so E Q<1 and Q is not constant. For fixed current state z, the quenched variable is Q_z=f_z(Q') where Q' has the stationary IID future-path law and is independent of the current state. Both f_G(x)=x^4 and f_D(x)=(1+x^2)/2 are strictly increasing on [0,1], so each Q_z is non-degenerate. Therefore the correct one-founder equations are

    q_G = [E(Q_G^4)+E(Q_D^4)]/2,
    q_D = [E(f_D(Q_G))+E(f_D(Q_D))]/2,

whereas replacing each random Q_z by its mean q_z gives the displayed false scalar equations; strict Jensen makes the two sides unequal. Also q_z^{(2)}=E(Q_z^2)>(E Q_z)^2 for either fixed initial state z. This state-conditioned argument completes the counterexample to both frozen-v2 claims.

8. Erratum to §6's comparison formula

Section 6 correctly computed the actual q_G and q_D as expectations of powers, but its comparison with a single pooled mean q̄ did not match the state-indexed scalar recursion printed in frozen v2. The correct comparison is the one in §7: v2 substitutes the state-conditioned means q_G and q_D into f_G and f_D and then averages over the next environment state. The IID counterexample refutes that state-indexed recursion by strict conditional Jensen, as shown in §7. Section 6's pooled-mean sentence is superseded and should not be used as the proof against the specific v2 formula.
