STATUS

I found no exact obstruction to polynomial-output-time generation. The R06 construction is an explicit deterministic machine, and its finite loops yield a conservative effective bound. With m=n+B+1 for the affine-pencil interface, one direct dense implementation has bit time

    O(C m^503 log^3(m+2))

and write-workspace O(C m^45 log(m+2)) bits, for an absolute, mechanically computable constant C. The full dense output is O(C m^492 log(m+2)) bits. The exact minimum affine-pencil input already forces about 4.91e62 tuples in the large-characteristic branch.

INTERFACE AND QUANTIFIERS

Input is only unary n,B>=1. Output is a finite list of n-tuples of integer square matrices. After reduction to the prime subfield, the same list must simultaneously hit every family of at most B feasible affine pencils of orders at most B, over every coefficient field and every extension-coefficient instance. Feasibility means an invertible matrix evaluation exists over an extension. The generator receives no coefficients, witnesses, formula, oracle, or characteristic.

For a nonzero admissible size-s noncommutative rational formula, the R06 bridge uses B=2s+1 and at most s+1 domain/root pencils. The claimed hit is defined at every inverse occurrence in the original expression tree and has invertible root value. Formula evaluation and any exact matrix-response service are outside generator time and must be charged separately.

EFFECTIVE COST RECONSTRUCTION

Let w=n+1, Delta=4w^3B, and let M2 be the least power of two strictly greater than B Delta. Put rho2=M2/2 and d2=M2 rho2. The integral large-characteristic branch defines

    E0 = 3 M2^2 (w+M2+1),
    T0 = B d2 (d2 E0+1),
    K  = max(M2, B T0+2).

It evaluates the adjugate-normalized construction on the five-dimensional integer grid {0,...,K}^5. At each point it can compute numeric integer determinants/cofactors directly; no symbolic adjugate or field inverse is required. The number of points is (K+1)^5. Every output entry is an integer and all are written densely.

For each prime p<=K, the appended prime-specific generator chooses M_p as the least power of 2 greater than B Delta when p is odd, and the least power of 3 when p=2. Put rho_p=phi(M_p),

    D0_p = 10 B M_p^3 (w+M_p+1),
    H_p  = max(16, rho_p (B+1) D0_p).

If p>H_p^2 it uses the prefix grid {0,...,H_p^2}. Otherwise it exhaustively searches for an irreducible polynomial of degree e with Q=p^e>H_p^2. Minimality gives Q<=pH_p^2<=H_p^4 and e<=2 ceil(log2 H_p)+1. The grid is then F_Q. In either case there are at most H_p^16 four-parameter points. The coefficient algebra has prime-field dimension L_p=e rho_p; the ordinary regular-representation matrix dimension is d_p=M_p L_p. Each point is built with exact finite-field arithmetic, the singular R0 cases are discarded, and every retained tuple is emitted.

The extension search visits at most Q<=H_p^4 candidate polynomials. A deterministic irreducibility test computes the needed Frobenius powers and polynomial gcds, at cost O(e^3 log p) prime-field operations per candidate with naive polynomial arithmetic. A direct specialized implementation builds the Newton coordinate matrices and unit-triangular inverses in O((n+1)M_p^3) extension-field operations, performs the Fourier lift in O((n+1)M_p^3) coefficient-algebra operations, writes the regular representations, and uses O((n+1)d_p^3) prime-field operations for elimination and normalization. Naive multiplication in the coefficient algebra costs O(L_p^2) prime-field operations. One prime-field operation on O(log p)-bit residues costs O(log^2 p) bit operations. Dense output bits are also included.

Here is a single explicit polynomial envelope. For m=n+B+1>=3,

    M2 <= 8 m^5,       K <= 2,000,000 m^37,
    Mp <= 12 m^5,      D0_p <= 241,920 m^21,
    H_p <= 2,903,040 m^27, e <= 70m,
    L_p <= 840m^6,     d_p <= 10,080m^11.

The outer machine tests at most K candidate integers for primality; trial division gives at most O(K^2 log^2 K) bit operations. Across at most K primes, extension searches cost at most O(K H_*^4 e_*^3 log^3 K), where H_*=2,903,040m^27 and e_*=70m. Grid construction and matrix operations cost at most

    O(K H_*^16 m (M_*^3 L_*^2 + d_*^3) log^2 K),

where M_*=12m^5, L_*=840m^6, and d_*=10,080m^11. The d_*^3 term dominates and gives O(m^503 log^2 m). Emitting every dense prime-specific tuple costs at most O(K H_*^16 m d_*^2 log K)=O(m^492 log m) bits.

For the integral branch, M2<=8m^5, d2<=32m^10, E0<=1,920m^15, T0<=1,966,112m^36, and K<=2,000,000m^37. The Newton-factor products and finite unit-triangular inverses have polynomial coefficient heights; directly evaluating them at grid entries gives an integer-entry bit bound b=O(M2^3 log(K+1))=O(m^15 log m). Hadamard/minor bounds give cofactor length Lambda=O(d2(b+log d2))=O(m^25 log m). Exact fraction-preserving Gaussian elimination computes each d2-by-d2 determinant in O(d2^3 Lambda^2) bit operations. At most n d2^2+1 determinants suffice per grid point. Thus the integral branch costs O((K+1)^5 (n d2^2+1)d2^3 Lambda^2)=O(m^286 log^2 m) bit operations, plus lower-order construction and output costs. Its output has O(m^231 log m) bits. The numeric adjugate can be streamed cofactor-by-cofactor; workspace for the elimination matrix is O(d2^2 Lambda).

The largest term is the repeated prime-specific grid, so a two-pass count/header convention only changes the constant. Keeping one point's matrices and streaming the output gives O(m^45 log m) workspace; storing the entire list instead uses its O(m^492 log m) output size.

SMALLEST INPUT AND PRACTICAL STATUS

At affine-pencil input n=B=1, w=2, Delta=32, M2=64, rho2=32, d2=2048, E0=823,296, T0=3,453,153,708,032, and K=3,453,153,708,034. The integral branch alone prints

    (K+1)^5 = 490,997,805,735,020,350,923,752,387,395,914,902,495,012,994,134,412,855,390,021,875

tuples. Each tuple has 2048-by-2048 integer matrices (one variable at n=1), so that branch writes at least 2.059e69 dense integer positions. This lower bound is for the specified R06 construction, not for every possible universal list. The rational-formula corollary sets B=2s+1, so its smallest legal formula input is larger still.

This fixed-input explosion is not an asymptotic obstruction: for complexity in unary n,B, the displayed number is a constant, while the general output bounds above are polynomial. It does establish that “polynomial-time” here is purely asymptotic and provides no practical generator at small sizes. No full promised-size run was executed. The finite component diagnostic in the R evidence archive checks only 8,360 semisimple-component comparisons; it does not run this generator or establish its universal guarantee.

PROOF STATUS, FALSIFIER, AND SCOPE

The count of prime branches, extension-field candidate search, grid points, matrix sizes, entry bits, determinant calls, and dense output is now explicitly charged. No factoring oracle, field-size-dependent exhaustive scan for p>H^2, coefficients, witnesses, or formula evaluation enters generator time. The published inherited sources support the folded q-Newton and operational free-skew-field mechanisms, but do not publish the added characteristic-independent integer composition. R06 is a supplied internal construction with independent internal audits, not an externally validated theorem or priority claim.

A direct falsifier of this cost result would be a step in the specified construction whose range exceeds these parameter bounds after substitution, or a required coefficient/matrix representation with superpolynomial bit height. For example, an irreducible search over all p^e polynomials with Q not bounded by H^4, or a per-prime subroutine whose time is exponential in log p rather than polynomial in p<=K and the unary parameters, would break this derivation. The supplied construction instead bounds Q by H^4 and chooses M as a prime power, avoiding general factorization. The full branch-by-branch proof remains a candidate-level reconstruction and has not been formalized or executed at its promised sizes.

The claim does not extend to one universal point, commutative PIT, arbitrary shared circuits, or a polynomial-bit evaluator for unknown coefficients over an unrepresented field. Historical priority and the cost of querying/evaluating an unknown rational formula remain separate.
