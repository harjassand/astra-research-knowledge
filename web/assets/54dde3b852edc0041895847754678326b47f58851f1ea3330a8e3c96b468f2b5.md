# Quantum-chemistry representation for catalytic barriers and correlated states

**Verdict.** Natural-orbital compression is an empirically effective representation choice for one small, fixed-spin Ru-fragment CASCI reaction-energy calculation. The reported 22-to-16-qubit reduction preserves that model's reactant/product energy difference to 0.2 kcal/mol. It does not certify a catalytic barrier, the physical spin ground state, dynamic correlation, or the periodic surface. Nor does it show a quantum advantage: the reported ADAPT-VQE results are noiseless statevector calculations on a very small active-space problem, and the exact classical CASCI control is already available.

The sharp reusable statement from this audit is conditional: if the discarded configuration block lies a certified energy gap above the retained-space ground energy and the retained/discarded coupling has a certified operator-norm bound, then the omitted correlation energy is bounded by a closed-form function of those two quantities. For a barrier or reaction-energy difference, the error is bounded by the larger of the two state-specific truncation bounds, since both retained-space errors are variational and nonnegative. One-particle natural occupations alone do not give this guarantee. An exact two-state family keeps an omitted-configuration probability fixed while making the energy error grow without bound as the gap and coupling scale together.

The block bound is an internally reconstructed elementary matrix inequality, not a novelty or priority claim. Its useful research gate is whether the gap and coupling can be certified cheaply from molecular integrals for a realistic transition-state pair. The new Ru paper does not report those quantities, and estimating the gap by diagonalizing the discarded block would usually reintroduce the expensive problem the reduction was meant to avoid.

## Primary anchor: Ru single-atom N2 hydrogenation

Geet Gupta's 25 September 2026 preprint studies the step
`RuH2(N2)* -> RuH(NNH)*` on a Ru single-atom site on Ru(0001). Its principal reported result is a state-space reduction and reaction-energy calculation, not a correlated transition-state barrier. The paper says the hydrogenation step is a high-barrier step in prior work, but the new correlated calculations use relaxed reactant and product structures only. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30958)

The source interface is:

- **Physical structures supplied to the electronic calculation.** Periodic PBE-D3 relaxation of a `4 x 4 x 3` Ru(0001) slab with 48 slab Ru atoms, a Ru adatom, and `N2H2` adsorbate (53 atoms in the complete system); 11 Å vacuum; plane-wave cutoffs 60/480 Ry; `4 x 4 x 1` k-point mesh; scalar-relativistic ultrasoft Ru pseudopotential with 4s/4p semicore valence; Methfessel-Paxton smearing 0.02 Ry. Both structures have identical stoichiometry. The source reports geometry thresholds of `1e-5 Ry` in energy and `5e-3 Ry/Bohr` in force. At fixed charge-density dual, 60 Ry differs from its tested 70 Ry reference by about 22.46 meV/atom; the chosen k-mesh differs from 6 x 6 x 1 by 0.456 meV/atom. Cancellation in the isomeric difference is expected, not separately proven.
- **Finite correlated fragment.** An unrelaxed extraction at each geometry gives the 18-atom `Ru14N2H2` cluster, with no boundary capping and no explicit quantum embedding. It is treated as an isolated molecule. The basis is Ru def2-SVP with its effective core potential and N/H cc-pVDZ, yielding 472 contracted basis functions. A matched PBE-D3 fragment calculation gives `+25.4 kcal/mol` versus `+27.5 kcal/mol` on the periodic slab (2.1 kcal/mol difference). This checks fragmentation only at PBE-D3; it does not establish correlated fragment-size convergence.
- **State and active Hamiltonian.** A neutral finite fragment is used. UHF spin-sector scans are heavily spin contaminated and several do not converge; the lowest nominal singlet is retained as an orbital reference, not as evidence that the physical fragment ground state is a singlet. The reported CASCI energies are explicitly within the imposed singlet sector. AVAS projects Ru 4d/5s, N 2s/2p, and H 1s references; threshold 0.905 gives (16 electrons, 11 spatial orbitals) for both states. In a spin-orbital basis, the Hamiltonian is
  `H = sum_pq h_pq a†_p a_q + 1/2 sum_pqrs h_pqrs a†_p a†_q a_r a_s`,
  plus nuclear and frozen-core constants. The output is the active-space ground-state energy for each geometry and their difference `E[RuH(NNH)*] - E[RuH2(N2)*]`.
- **Compression and execution.** The CASCI one-particle density matrix is diagonalized to obtain natural orbitals. The three orbitals closest to occupation 2 are frozen, with their Coulomb/exchange contributions folded into a dressed one-electron term and a scalar. The retained problem is (10e,8o), mapped by Jordan-Wigner to 16 qubits instead of 22. The actual calculations use a noiseless statevector simulator, not a physical quantum processor. The source's IBM/IonQ compilation chart is a resource estimate only; it states that no noisy simulation or physical-device execution was performed.

The source-reported CASCI truncation scan is especially informative. Relative to the parent (16e,11o) CASCI, the retained-space energy errors (reduced minus parent) in kcal/mol are: `n_active=4`: R 29.2, P 21.9; `5`: 28.8, 19.8; `6`: 8.1, 3.8; `7`: 2.5, 1.7; `8`: 0.002, 0.205; `9`: 0.001, 0.003. At `n_active=8`, the frozen orbital occupations are `(1.99999980, 1.99999927, 1.99999859)` for R and `(1.99999988, 1.99999729, 1.99981175)` for P. The resulting CASCI reaction energy changes from `+148.2` to `+148.4 kcal/mol`, a source-reported 0.2 kcal/mol difference from partial cancellation of the two state errors.

The pairing matters: at `n_active=7`, neither state's truncation error is under 1 kcal/mol, but their difference is only `1.7 - 2.5 = -0.8 kcal/mol`; at `n_active=4`, it is `21.9 - 29.2 = -7.3 kcal/mol`. The n=8 result is a successful finite diagnostic for that model, not a uniform guarantee. The source scans the active orbital count against its parent CASCI, so it already pays for the larger CASCI and the 1-RDM used to select the natural orbitals.

The active-space qubit Hamiltonian has 20,890/20,930 Pauli terms for the 22-qubit R/P problems and 5,789/5,793 for the 16-qubit R/P problems, about a 3.6-fold term-count reduction. This is consistent with the approximate fourth-power scaling in spin-orbital count for an unstructured two-electron operator list. That count reduction does not by itself give a measurement-cost reduction: the source does not give a shot allocation, energy confidence interval, state-preparation repetition count, or noisy-device energy estimate.

## Paired truncation certificate

Here is a sufficient condition that turns a retained-space representation into a quantitative paired-energy bound. It is a finite-dimensional Hermitian block-matrix result and applies to any fixed one- and two-electron active Hamiltonian, regardless of whether energies are obtained by classical CI or a quantum eigensolver.

For each geometry `s` (reactant `R` or transition state `T`), let `P_s` project onto the retained configuration space, including the chosen fixed occupations of removed orbitals, and let `Q_s=I-P_s`. Write

`H_s = [[A_s, B_s], [B_s†, C_s]]`,

where `A_s=P_s H_s P_s`, `B_s=P_s H_s Q_s`, and `C_s=Q_s H_s Q_s`. Let `e_s = lambda_min(A_s)` and suppose a certified `gamma_s > 0` satisfies `C_s >= (e_s + gamma_s) I`. Define `beta_s >= ||B_s||_2` and

`f(gamma,beta) = (sqrt(gamma^2 + 4 beta^2) - gamma)/2 <= beta^2/gamma`.

Then the full finite-basis ground energy `E_s=lambda_min(H_s)` obeys

`0 <= e_s - E_s <= f(gamma_s,beta_s)`.

**Proof.** For any unit vector with components `x in P_s`, `y in Q_s`, the Rayleigh quotient of `H_s-e_s I` is at least
`gamma_s ||y||^2 - 2 beta_s ||x|| ||y||`.
The minimum eigenvalue of the scalar 2-by-2 matrix `[[0,-beta_s],[-beta_s,gamma_s]]` is `-f(gamma_s,beta_s)`, so `E_s >= e_s-f`. The Rayleigh-Ritz principle gives `E_s <= e_s` by restricting its minimization to `P_s`. Equality is attained by the two-dimensional block `[[e_s,beta_s],[beta_s,e_s+gamma_s]]`; the bound is sharp under the stated information.

For an activation barrier or same-stoichiometry reaction energy, define retained and full differences as `D_P=e_T-e_R` and `D=E_T-E_R`. With `delta_s=e_s-E_s >= 0`,
`D_P-D=delta_T-delta_R`,
so the truncation error satisfies
`|D_P-D| <= max(f(gamma_T,beta_T), f(gamma_R,beta_R))`.
The maximum, rather than the sum, follows because both variational truncation errors are nonnegative. If each state energy is then estimated by a non-variational measurement with certified absolute error `eta_s`, add `eta_T+eta_R`; if both estimates remain variational with known residual bounds `v_s`, the total difference error is at most `max(f_T+v_T, f_R+v_R)`.

This is useful only if `gamma_s` and `beta_s` are themselves available as certified bounds. `beta_s` may be bounded from matrix elements/integrals, although a naive determinant-space Frobenius bound can be loose. `gamma_s` is the hard part: computing the lowest eigenvalue of the discarded block exactly may be comparable to the parent CI problem. Gershgorin, interval, or other rigorous lower bounds are possible candidate services, but no such scalable certificate is supplied or tested here. The preprint reports neither `gamma_s` nor `beta_s`, so this lemma gives no numerical certification for its Ru result.

## Why near-integer occupations alone cannot certify the energy

A 1-RDM occupation constrains a probability of leaving a frozen-occupation sector, but does not control the Hamiltonian matrix elements or the energy denominator of that excursion. The exact two-state family makes this failure sharp. Let the retained state be `|0>`, the discarded state be `|1>`, and

`H = [[0,v],[v,gamma]]`, with `gamma>0`.

The ground state has discarded-state probability
`p = (1 - gamma/sqrt(gamma^2+4v^2))/2`,
and the omitted-space energy lowering is
`delta = (sqrt(gamma^2+4v^2)-gamma)/2 = gamma p/(1-2p)`.
For fixed `p in (0,1/2)`, choose `v=gamma sqrt(p(1-p))/(1-2p)`. The measured discarded probability stays exactly `p` while `delta` grows linearly without bound as `gamma` increases. Interpreting `|1>` as a singly ionized frozen spatial orbital makes its one-orbital occupation deficit exactly `p`.

The product state's third frozen-orbital deficit in the Ru source is `2 - 1.99981175 = 1.8825e-4`. Setting `p` to that value and `gamma=10 Ha` gives `v=0.13827 Ha` and `delta=0.0019115 Ha`, about `1.20 kcal/mol`; the occupation is unchanged. This is a mathematical counterexample to an occupation-only implication, not a claim that these are the Ru integrals. A system-specific operator-norm or gap bound rules out the scaling used by the toy family; an occupation list does not.

There is also a basic norm-dependent fallback: if the discarded probability is at most `epsilon`, the projected normalized state differs from the full state by at most `sqrt(2 epsilon)` in vector norm, so energy error is at most `range(H)*sqrt(2 epsilon)` after shifting away any scalar energy offset. This requires a spectral-width bound and is generally much too loose for chemical accuracy. The arithmetic checks in `compression_bounds.py` reproduce the source occupation deficits, determinant-sector sizes, paired energy differences, toy counterexample, and one illustrative block-certificate value (`gamma=0.01 Ha`, `beta=0.001 Ha` gives `0.0621 kcal/mol` per state). That illustrative certificate is not measured chemistry.

## Attack against a matched classical comparator

For the reported spaces, exact classical CASCI/FCI is the correct matched baseline. In the fixed `N_alpha=N_beta` determinant representation, the (16e,11o) parent space has `C(11,8)^2 = 27,225` determinants and the (10e,8o) reduced space has `C(8,5)^2 = 3,136`. A spin-adapted implementation can be smaller still. These are small exact-diagonalization problems, and the source itself uses CASCI references to choose orbitals, certify the truncation, and benchmark ADAPT-VQE. The adaptation does not improve on that exact answer. In the reduced space, statevector ADAPT-VQE stops at pool-gradient norm `1e-3 Ha`, but remains 5.49 kcal/mol above reduced-space FCI for R and 1.25 kcal/mol above for P; its reaction energy is 4.2 kcal/mol low. A common gradient threshold therefore fails as a paired energy-accuracy certificate on this example.

For larger active spaces, the best classical comparator depends on entanglement and basis size; DMRG and selected-CI/FCIQMC-family methods must be included rather than a single-reference DFT calculation alone. The primary resource-estimate paper by von Burg et al. studies a Ru-catalyzed CO2-to-methanol cycle and explicitly uses DMRG approximants for phase-estimation trial-state overlap. For 52–65 active orbitals it estimates about `1e10` Toffoli gates and about 4,000 logical qubits for 1 mHa energy precision, under its error-correction and gate-time assumptions. The authors conclude further algorithm improvements are needed for quantum chemistry to beat traditional methods. Those are prospective resource estimates, not experimental runs or advantage evidence. [Primary paper](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.3.033055)

A barrier-specific stress-test appears in Di Paola et al.'s Pt/Pt-Co oxygen-dissociation study. Their active-space energy profile changes substantially as the active space grows; for the Pt/Co model the activation-energy sign changes as the space is enlarged, with the best reported value `0.293 eV` at an `(8e,8o)` ADAPT-VQE calculation. They report that recovering the energy ordering needs a larger active space and that Co d-orbital magnetism was left at mean-field level to avoid an enormous active space. This is a useful representation-sensitivity warning, not an external exact benchmark or a barrier certificate. It also illustrates how the chemical question can be hidden by a representation choice: a subsystem omitted to control qubit count can host the strongest competing correlation. The 2026 publisher correction changes author affiliations only. [Primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-024-01460-x), [correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41524-025-01513-9)

The broader MOBH35 barrier benchmark provides an additional classical control boundary: local coupled-cluster variants work well in low static-correlation cases, but some can fail badly as static correlation strengthens. It supports including diagnostics and multireference controls; it does not validate any one active-space quantum representation for the Ru/Pt systems. [Primary benchmark](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jctc.1c01126)

## Full cost and precision ledger

The source-supported and missing costs are:

- **Geometry and structure search:** 53-atom periodic PBE-D3 slab relaxation at both endpoints, including cutoff and k-point convergence work. No transition-state optimization or correlated potential-energy scan is reported. A barrier calculation must additionally find/validate a first-order saddle and track the relevant spin surfaces.
- **Hamiltonian acquisition:** fragment extraction, 472 contracted basis functions, unrestricted/restricted mean-field calculations, AVAS projectors and threshold sweep, four-index integral generation/rotation, 1-RDM formation, natural-orbital sorting, and dressed frozen-orbital Hamiltonians. These are classical inputs to the VQE stage. The 16-qubit Hamiltonian is not acquired for free.
- **Classical reference and active-space selection:** parent CASCI is explicitly needed to obtain the NOs and reference energies. The determinant sectors above make this the dominant matched comparator for the example, not a difficult hidden baseline.
- **State preparation:** the paper uses a single reference determinant and a spin-conserving singles/doubles ADAPT pool of 315 candidates. R/P converged after 95/134 additions to circuits with 96/135 operators. The full-pool search is repeated adaptively; if all pool gradients are evaluated each iteration, it entails up to 29,925/42,210 gradient components before optimizer and energy evaluations. The reported costs are noiseless statevector work; no shot count, hardware success probability, error mitigation, or physical state-preparation cost is given. The supplementary material transpires hardware-targeted circuits but does not claim execution.
- **Observable and numerical precision:** the desired chemical comparison should allocate statewise error budgets so their difference is certified. The source gives no finite-shot estimator or confidence interval. The `1e-3 Ha` ADAPT pool-gradient threshold is an optimizer stopping rule, not an energy error bar: on this instance it coexists with 5.49 and 1.25 kcal/mol state-energy errors. PBE cutoff errors are expected to cancel due to same composition, but the correlated finite-fragment error is not bounded by that cancellation.
- **Dynamic correlation and finite-size boundary:** regional-core NEVPT2 gives state corrections `-1.489` and `-2.705 Ha`, reversing the CASCI reaction energy from `+148.2` to about `-614.6 kcal/mol`; its perturber block `S_ijrs` dominates and is more than twice as large for P. The source properly labels it numerically unstable and does not identify a microscopic cause. DSRG-MRPT2 with 50 retained virtuals yields reaction energies from `+92.6` to `+36.3 kcal/mol` as flow parameter `s` varies from 0.3 to 1.0 Ha^-2 (default 0.5 gives +70.5), and the virtual space is not converged. The final activation free energy is therefore unresolved. Fragment-size, spin-sector, basis, and `s`/virtual convergence are charged next steps.

## Decisive next test

On a genuine catalytic barrier path, freeze a common set of orbitals by a predeclared threshold, then evaluate the same geometries with: (i) parent CASCI/selected CI or DMRG as the strongest classical reference available for the chosen active space; (ii) the compressed-space solver; and (iii) a paired gap/coupling certificate if one can be constructed from the integrals. Use reactant, saddle, and product rather than substituting a reaction energy for the barrier. Report state energies, 1-/2-RDMs, discarded-block bounds, spin diagnostics, and the independently budgeted geometry, integral, dynamic-correlation, measurement, and state-preparation errors.

**Predeclared falsifier for an occupation-only rule:** any pair of electronic Hamiltonians with the same removed-orbital natural occupations but a barrier error above the proposed tolerance. The exact two-state family already falsifies a universal rule at fixed discarded probability; the practical molecular test is the Pt/Co active-space sweep or a fresh matched CAS/DMRG transition-state path. **Predeclared pass condition for the conditional certificate:** independently verified lower bounds on both discarded blocks and upper bounds on both couplings imply `max_s f(gamma_s,beta_s)` below the chosen truncation budget, and a parent-space diagonalization on tractable members of the same family confirms the interval contains the actual barrier error. Failure to find usable `gamma_s` is a negative result on practicality, not a failure of the matrix lemma.
