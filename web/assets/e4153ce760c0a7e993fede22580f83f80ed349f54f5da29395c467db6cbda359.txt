Status and imported premise
--------------------------
The positive results below are CONDITIONAL on the following exact premise Q8:
for every centered isotropic log-concave Lebesgue density p on R^n and every
real symmetric matrix M,

    Var_p(X^T M X) <= 8 ||M||_HS^2.                         (Q8)

This is SOURCE-REPORTED by Brayden Letwin, arXiv:2607.24164, Theorem 1.2,
and is the imported starting point of Bizeul-Klartag-Lehec (BKL),
arXiv:2610.05474v1, Lemma 4.2. Its statement was checked against the current
primary arXiv page and BKL's full TeX. Its original complete proof was not
independently validated in this task. The following implications are fully
derived here, and do not require accepting the entire new KLS proof.

If instead one assumes a universal KLS constant C_P, the same arguments work
with 8 replaced by 4 C_P. Thus none of these consequences independently
proves KLS. All calculations use real scores and densities. Covariance is
positive definite; singular distributions can be handled on their affine
support, but no singular numerical covariance inversion is claimed.

T1. ONE-STEP STABILITY UNDER AN ARBITRARY NONLINEAR OBSERVATION
------------------------------------------------------------
Let p be a full-dimensional log-concave density, with known mean m and
covariance Sigma > 0. Let q=r p be ANY density with E_p r=1, r>=0, and

    delta = E_p (r-1)^2 < infinity.

The target q need not be log-concave, unimodal, smooth, or in an exponential
family. Write G_p=N(m,Sigma), and G_q=N(m_q,Sigma_q). Then

    ||Sigma^(-1/2)(m_q-m)||^2 <= delta,                    (1)

    ||Sigma^(-1/2) Sigma_q Sigma^(-1/2)-I||_HS
        <= sqrt(8 delta)+delta.                          (2)

Set eta=sqrt(8 delta)+delta. If eta<1, Sigma_q is positive definite and

    KL(G_q || G_p) <= delta/2 + eta^2/[4(1-eta)],         (3)

    TV(G_q,G_p) <= sqrt(delta/4 + eta^2/[8(1-eta)]).       (4)

All constants are independent of n and of the condition number of Sigma in
the mathematical statement. Computing or acquiring Sigma^(-1/2) retains
its actual size and conditioning costs.

Proof. Apply the fixed affine change Y=Sigma^(-1/2)(X-m). Under p, E Y=0
and E YY^T=I. The change preserves log-concavity. Set h=r-1, b=E_p hY,
and D=E_p h(YY^T-I). Since E_p h=0, E_q Y=b and E_q YY^T=I+D.

For a unit vector u, Cauchy-Schwarz gives
|u^T b|^2 <= E h^2 E(u^T Y)^2=delta. Taking the supremum gives (1).
For a symmetric M of HS norm one,

    |Tr(MD)| = |E h(Y^T M Y-Tr M)|
             <= sqrt(delta Var_p(Y^T M Y)) <= sqrt(8 delta).

Duality of the HS norm on symmetric matrices gives ||D||_HS<=sqrt(8 delta).
Now S=Sigma^(-1/2)Sigma_q Sigma^(-1/2)=I+D-bb^T and
||bb^T||_HS=||b||^2<=delta, proving (2). All target second moments exist:
E_p r |Y_iY_j| is finite by Cauchy-Schwarz and Q8/finite fourth moments.

If eta<1, every eigenvalue of S lies in [1-eta,1+eta]. For |u|<=eta,

    u-log(1+u) = u^2 integral_0^1 t/(1+tu) dt
               <= u^2/[2(1-eta)].

The exact Gaussian relative-entropy formula therefore gives

    KL(G_q||G_p)
     = (||b||^2 + Tr(S-I-log S))/2
     <= delta/2 + ||S-I||_HS^2/[4(1-eta)],

which proves (3). Pinsker proves (4). No Gaussian approximation to q was
used. In particular TV(q,G_q) can be bounded away from zero.

T2. A SHARP LIKELIHOOD-COST CERTIFICATE
--------------------------------------
Suppose q(x)=ell(x)p(x)/E_p ell, where 0<ell_-<=ell(x)<=ell_+<infinity
on the support of p. These are supplied bounds or separately acquired
certificates, not assumed consequences of a finite sample. Let

    B=log(ell_+/ell_-), K=exp B.

Then

    delta=chi^2(q||p) <= (K-1)^2/(4K)=sinh^2(B/2).        (5)

This likelihood-only bound is sharp over distributions of ell. Thus (1)-(4)
become an explicit dimension-independent certificate using B alone. The
target can be a posterior from any bounded nonlinear likelihood.

Proof. Scale ell so 1<=ell<=K. Set a=E ell and v=E ell^2. Pointwise,
ell^2 <= (K+1)ell-K, hence

    delta=v/a^2-1 <= (K+1)/a-K/a^2-1.

For 1<=a<=K the last expression is maximized at a=2K/(K+1), with value
(K-1)^2/(4K). Equality occurs when ell takes K with probability 1/(K+1)
and takes 1 otherwise. Such an ell can be placed on measurable sets of the
specified probability under a continuous log-concave base.

A convenient conservative rule is: if 0<B<=1/4, then TV(G_q,G_p)<=B.
Indeed sinh(B/2)<=3B/5, so delta<=9B^2/25 and
eta<=3(3B/5)+9B^2/25<=189B/100<1/2. Consequently (3) is at most
[9/50+(189/100)^2/2] B^2 <2 B^2, and (4) is at most B. The exact formula
is appreciably better; its leading TV upper bound is sqrt(5) B/4.

Named task unlocked: refresh scheduling for a Gaussian moment surrogate
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Input: a sequential posterior or likelihood-tempering task, base densities
p_j which are log-concave at every refresh point; an exact-moment oracle at
those points; known per-step log-likelihood contrasts B_j; tolerance
epsilon<=1/4. Output: a cached Gaussian with TV error at most epsilon
relative to the exact Gaussian with the CURRENT posterior's moments.

Cache G at a refresh point. Add B_j to a running contrast budget, since the
oscillation of a sum of log-likelihood increments is at most the sum of
their oscillations. Until this sum exceeds epsilon, return the cached G.
T2 proves its error guarantee, without acquiring any target moments. At a
budget crossing, refresh the moments and reset. An increment too large can
be tempered; if the two endpoint densities are log-concave, all intermediate
geometric interpolants are log-concave. With total contrast L, one obtains
at most ceil(L/epsilon)+1 refreshes when segment boundaries can be chosen.

This removes a sqrt(n) penalty in the usual certificate that knows only
e^(-B)I <= Sigma_p^(-1/2)Sigma_q Sigma_p^(-1/2) <= e^B I. That certificate
gives Gaussian covariance KL <= n(e^B-1-B)/2=O(nB^2), hence a contrast step
of order epsilon/sqrt(n). T1 instead controls the ENTIRE HS covariance
change. Its covariance contribution is O(B^2), without n.

Costs and equally informed baseline: each refresh still needs n means and
n(n+1)/2 covariance entries; storing a full surrogate costs O(n^2), ordinary
factorization costs O(n^3), and the moment-oracle/sampling/precision cost is
not supplied by this theorem. No first moment or covariance is acquired for
free. Gaussian-likelihood evaluation and inverse covariance numerical
conditioning retain their costs. If B is unknown, a finite sample cannot
certify a global likelihood contrast; likelihood moments can alternatively
give a statistical upper bound on delta with its own error probability.

The strongest baseline with the SAME Q8 premise can derive T1 directly by
the short duality argument above. Therefore this is a useful explicit
certificate, not a demonstrated computational separation. Q8 was claimed
in July 2026; no dependence on the October all-order KLS mechanism is needed.
The cached Gaussian approximates the target's MOMENT GAUSSIAN only. It
does not justify posterior approximation, MCMC acceptance, original-density
warm starts, or the N75 matrix-channel MI inequality.

T3. FISHER-SPEED CONTROL FOR GENERAL LOG-CONCAVE DENSITY PATHS
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let (p_t) be a differentiable path of full-dimensional log-concave densities
with nonsingular covariance Sigma_t. Let s_t=partial_t log p_t, with E s_t=0
and I_p(t)=E s_t^2<infinity. Assume the mean/covariance derivatives exist
and are given by differentiating expectations; e.g. a regular dominated
path, or the geometric interpolation below with finite information cost.
Let G_t=N(m_t,Sigma_t). Then the exact Gaussian Fisher speed satisfies

    I_G(t) = m_t'^T Sigma_t^(-1)m_t'
             + (1/2)||Sigma_t^(-1/2)Sigma_t'Sigma_t^(-1/2)||_HS^2
            <= 5 I_p(t).                                (6)

Proof. Fix a time t and whiten at that time, without differentiating the
whitening map. The expectation differentiation formulas give

    Sigma_t^(-1/2)m_t' = E[s_t Y],
    Sigma_t^(-1/2)Sigma_t'Sigma_t^(-1/2) = E[s_t(YY^T-I)].

The terms involving m_t' in the derivative of central covariance vanish
because E Y=0. Exactly the two duality bounds in T1 imply squared norms
at most I_p(t) and 8 I_p(t). Their sum with the factor 1/2 proves (6).
The Gaussian Fisher formula follows by differentiating its log density:
the centered linear and centered quadratic Gaussian scores are orthogonal,
and their variances are respectively the displayed two terms.

Thus Gaussianization is sqrt(5)-Lipschitz for lengths of density paths
which remain log-concave. It is NOT asserted to be a contraction in the
unrestricted ambient Fisher distance between arbitrary densities.

An endpoint theorem with a computable information premise
----------------------------------------------------------
Let p,q be mutually absolutely continuous full-dimensional log-concave
densities with finite Jeffreys divergence

    J(p,q)=KL(p||q)+KL(q||p).

Let G_p,G_q be their moment Gaussians. Then, for H defined WITHOUT the
1/sqrt(2) normalization, H^2=integral (sqrt(G_p)-sqrt(G_q))^2,

    H(G_p,G_q) <= (1/2)sqrt(5 J(p,q)),
    TV(G_p,G_q) <= min{1,(1/2)sqrt(5 J(p,q))}.            (7)

Proof. Take p_t=p^(1-t)q^t/Z_t and F(t)=log Z_t. Convex combinations of
the two convex potentials prove log-concavity. The score is the centered
log(q/p), hence I_p(t)=F''(t). Integration gives

    integral_0^1 I_p(t)dt=F'(1)-F'(0)=J(p,q).

Cauchy-Schwarz yields path length integral sqrt(I_p)<=sqrt J. The
square-root Gaussian density curve has L2 speed sqrt(I_G)/2. Integrating
and using (6) gives H<=sqrt(5) sqrt J/2. The inequality TV<=H follows
directly by Cauchy-Schwarz applied to
|G_p-G_q|=|sqrt(G_p)-sqrt(G_q)|(sqrt(G_p)+sqrt(G_q)).
The formulas extend by endpoint limits if a score is not L2 exactly at an
endpoint. Along the open interpolation F'' is finite almost everywhere
when its integral J is finite. Fourth-moment domination by a constant
multiple of p+q justifies covariance limits.

T4. NO GLOBAL LINEAR KL DOMINATION, EVEN FOR LOG-CONCAVE DENSITIES
-----------------------------------------------------------------
There is no universal C such that KL(G_q||G_p)<=C J(p,q) for all log-concave
p,q. Let p=Exp(1) and q=Exp(epsilon), epsilon in (0,1), on [0,infinity).
Both are log-concave and mutually absolutely continuous. Direct integration
gives

    J(p,q)=epsilon+epsilon^(-1)-2,
    KL(G_q||G_p)=epsilon^(-2)-epsilon^(-1)+log epsilon.

The ratio is asymptotic to 1/epsilon, so it diverges. The local Fisher-speed
bound does not turn a long continuation path into a linear KL bound.

The constant in (6) cannot be one, and is at least (5+sqrt(17))/2.
For centered unit-variance X=Exp(1)-1, consider features A=X and
C=(X^2-1)/sqrt(2). Their Gram matrix is

    [[1,sqrt(2)],[sqrt(2),4]].

Its largest eigenvalue is (5+sqrt(17))/2. The derivative of moment
Gaussianization maps an L2 score to its inner products against these two
features. Taking a score in the top eigendirection attains that squared
operator norm. Its sign can be chosen with negative quadratic coefficient,
so p_t proportional to exp(-x+t s(x)) on x>=0 is log-concave for t>=0 and
has the desired one-sided derivative at zero. A small positive t gives a
strongly convex potential on the common half-line support and makes the
same lower bound a limit of interior points of this path. Sharpness of 5
versus this lower bound remains open here.

T5. HIGH-ORDER TILT ANALYTICITY ALONE DOES NOT FORCE A GAP
---------------------------------------------------------
Let sigma in (0,1), a=sqrt(1-sigma^2), and

    mu_sigma = (N(-a,sigma^2)+N(a,sigma^2))/2.

These are smooth, isotropic one-dimensional densities with subgaussian
moment generating functions E exp(tX)<=exp(t^2/2). Define
F_f(z)=E[f(X)exp(zX)]/E exp(zX). For EVERY f in L2(mu_sigma), and EVERY
integer d>=1,

    |F_f^(d)(0)/d!| <= 4^d ||f||_2,                      (8)

uniformly in sigma. Nevertheless C_P(mu_sigma) tends to infinity at least
exponentially in 1/sigma^2.

Proof of (8). The complex MGF is M(z)=exp(sigma^2 z^2/2) cosh(az), which
has no zeros on |z|<=1/2. The L2 norm of its likelihood kernel satisfies

    ||exp(zX)/M(z)||_2^2
      = exp(sigma^2 |z|^2) cosh(2a Re z)/|cosh(az)|^2.

Here |cosh(az)|^2=sinh^2(a Re z)+cos^2(a Im z)>=cos^2(1/2).
On |z|<=1/2 the square root of the displayed bound is at most
exp(1/8) sqrt(cosh 1)/cos(1/2)<2. Thus |F_f(z)|<=2||f||_2. Cauchy's
coefficient estimate gives |F_f^(d)(0)/d!|<=2^(d+1)||f||_2<=4^d||f||_2.
Analyticity for L2 f follows by Gaussian tail domination on compact disks.
Subgaussianity follows from cosh(at)<=exp(a^2 t^2/2).

Proof of gap divergence. For sigma^2<=a^2/2 set w=sigma^2/a. Let g be
-1 below -w, linear with derivative 1/w on [-w,w], and +1 above w. It is
odd, so its mean is zero. Write Q=mu_sigma([-w,w]). Its density on this
interval is at most cosh(1) exp(-a^2/(2sigma^2))/(sigma sqrt(2pi)), so

    Q <= Qbar = [2w cosh(1)/(sigma sqrt(2pi))]
                   exp(-a^2/(2sigma^2)).

Var g>=1-Q and E(g')^2=Q/w^2. Therefore, when Qbar<1,

    C_P(mu_sigma) >= (sigma^3 sqrt(2pi)/(2a cosh 1))
                     (1-Qbar) exp(a^2/(2sigma^2)).         (9)

The right side diverges. In fact the potential Hessian at zero is
1/sigma^2-a^2/sigma^4, negative for small sigma. This locates the failure
exactly at the convex Bochner step in the BKL/Song-Zhang criterion. (8)
does not refute that criterion under its stated log-concavity hypothesis.

T6. A QUANTUM CUMULANT TRANSFER FAILS AT THE TRACIAL STATE
----------------------------------------------------------
Let d>=2, tau(A)=Tr(A)/d, and let H_0 be the real Hilbert space of traceless
Hermitian d by d matrices with inner product tau(AC). Pick an orthonormal
basis (B_i), and define the quantum Gibbs log partition

    Lambda(h)=log tau(exp(sum_i h_i B_i)).

At h=0 its first derivative is zero and its Hessian is I. Let kappa_3 be
its third derivative tensor. For EVERY traceless Hermitian B with tau(B^2)=1,

    sum_{j,k} kappa_3(B,B_j,B_k)^2 = d^2/2-2.             (10)

Consequently there is no dimension-independent BKL-style third-cumulant
contraction bound for arbitrary quantum Gibbs exponential families, even
at the maximally mixed state with perfectly conditioned Fisher covariance.
For d=2^k, take nonidentity Pauli strings: each individual score is an
operator contraction with tau(B_iB_j)=delta_ij. The failure therefore
persists with bounded individual scores, without a state-conditioning issue.

Proof. Expanding exp at zero and using trace cyclicity shows

    kappa_3(B,A,C)=tau(B(AC+CA)/2)
                 = <A, P_0 L_B(C)>_tau,

where L_B(C)=(BC+CB)/2 and P_0 projects to H_0. Thus the left side of (10)
is the squared HS norm of P_0 L_B restricted to H_0. Diagonalize B with
eigenvalues b_i, sum b_i=0 and sum b_i^2=d. On the full Hermitian space,
L_B has eigenvalues b_i on diagonal coordinates and (b_i+b_j)/2 on each
of the two real off-diagonal coordinates for i<j. Hence

    ||L_B||_HS^2=sum_{i,j} ((b_i+b_j)/2)^2=d^2/2.

The full Hermitian space splits orthogonally into span{I} and H_0. In this
block decomposition L_B has zero I-to-I entry, I-to-H_0 vector B of norm
one, and adjoint H_0-to-I vector also of norm one. Removing these two
blocks subtracts 2 from the squared HS norm, proving (10).

Restricting to diagonal scores gives the separate exact formula d-2.
It follows by compressing diag(b_i) to the Euclidean hyperplane sum x_i=0;
the squared HS norm is sum b_i^2-2 sum b_i^2/d=d-2. Thus even the purely
commuting categorical model fails a dimension-uniform bound. Quantum Gibbs
families do not have the classical log-concave Lebesgue interface.

There is also a fixed-dimension conditioning obstruction. A commuting
thermal qubit with rare-state probability p has standardized third cumulant
(1-2p)/sqrt(p(1-p)), whose square diverges as p tends to zero. This example
is distinct from the tracial growing-dimension obstruction in (10).

A DIRECT QUANTUM COUNTERPART TO THE GAUSSIANIZATION STABILITY TASK
----------------------------------------------------------------
For d=2^k choose a complete nonidentity Pauli bank and one Pauli B. Let
rho_0=I/d and rho_t=(I+tB)/d, |t|<1. Define the real mean vector
m_i=Tr(rho_t B_i) and the SYMMETRIZED observable covariance
S_ij=Tr(rho_t(B_iB_j+B_jB_i)/2)-m_i m_j. This is an explicitly specified
moment-proxy interface; it does not assume simultaneous measurement of
noncommuting observables. It is also not identified with the BKM Hessian
away from t=0. Let G_t=N(m,S).

Quantum chi-square relative to rho_0, defined as
Tr((rho_t-rho_0)rho_0^(-1)(rho_t-rho_0)), equals t^2. The state's trace
distance from rho_0 is |t|/2, and its eigenvalue condition ratio is
(1+|t|)/(1-|t|), independent of d. Yet

    ||S-I||_HS^2=t^2(d^2/2-2)+t^4,
    KL(G_t||G_0)=-(d^2/8) log(1-t^2).                    (11)

Proof. m=t e_B and S=I+t K_B-t^2 e_B e_B^T, where K_B is the compression
P_0 L_B from (10). Since B^2=I and tau(B)=0, K_B e_B=0. The cross term in
the squared HS norm vanishes, proving the first formula. On the full
Hermitian space, the +1 and -1 eigenspaces of L_B each have dimension d^2/4,
and the zero eigenspace has dimension d^2/2. The two-dimensional space
spanned by I,B removes one eigenvector from each of the +1 and -1 spaces.
Consequently S has eigenvalues 1+t and 1-t each of multiplicity d^2/4-1,
1 on the anticommuting space of dimension d^2/2, and 1-t^2 in the B
direction. Thus log det S=(d^2/4)log(1-t^2), while
||m||^2+Tr(S-I)=t^2-t^2=0. The exact Gaussian KL formula proves (11).

At t=0 the state Fisher speed is one (SLD and BKM agree at the tracial
state), but the moment Gaussian Fisher speed squared is d^2/4. The
quantum Jeffreys divergence of rho_t and rho_0 is t artanh(t), independent
of d. Thus a dimension-free classical Q8-style Gaussianization stability
theorem fails in this fully bounded, uniformly faithful quantum interface.
No slow dynamics, poor covariance conditioning, rare-state tail, or
unbounded Hamiltonian score is used. Restricting the bank or changing the
covariance/proxy definition is a substantive additional hypothesis.
