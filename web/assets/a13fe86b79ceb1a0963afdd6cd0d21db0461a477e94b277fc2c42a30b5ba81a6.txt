N09 GLASS MARGINALITY — FROZEN RECORD v1
Date: 2026-10-09
Status: internally derived conditional results; physical preparation law unresolved.
Companion executable diagnostic: work/natural/n09_glass_marginality/diagnostic.py

TARGET
======

Find a preparation-resolved mechanism for the low-frequency nonphononic modes
of amorphous solids. The hard question is not whether a positive Hessian permits
small eigenvalues; it does. The hard question is what measure a specified
cooling and relaxation protocol places on the local curvature and anharmonic
jet of its final quasi-localized modes, and whether that measure explains the
frequent D_loc(omega) ~ omega^4 tail and protocol-sensitive departures.

This record does not claim a new universal law. Its original candidate
contribution is a joint, quantitative obstruction-and-test package: the exact
quench-selected measure that must be derived; a local barrier statistic that
separates fragile omega^3 modes from a lowest-well omega^4 population; and a
conditional elastic-continuum calculation showing when phonon hybridization
does or does not change a local-resonance exponent. None of these calculations
derives the actual preparation weight W_p for a glass.

CLAIM LEDGER
============

1. SOURCE-REPORTED: In many 3D computer glasses the localized nonphononic
   spectrum is reported as omega^4 across preparation histories; annealing can
   strongly reduce its prefactor. Overdamped instantaneous quenches have also
   produced exponents between about 3 and 4 in a specific model.
2. INTERNALLY DERIVED: A smooth random quartic-jet chart gives p(kappa) ~ kappa
   and D(omega) ~ omega^3. Restricting the selected quartic minimum to be the
   lowest one in that quartic polynomial adds a tau-window of width
   O(sqrt(kappa)) and gives omega^4. Neither implication follows from Hessian
   stability alone.
3. INTERNALLY DERIVED: If an omega^3 branch comes from p(kappa) ~ kappa with a
   nonzero, finite conditional cubic coefficient, its small adjacent-saddle
   barrier obeys B ~ 2 kappa^3/(3 tau^2), hence Pr(B < b) ~ b^(2/3). Bounded
   fifth- and higher-order terms do not change the leading barrier asymptotic.
4. CANDIDATE, CONDITIONAL: A dipolar local defect coupled linearly to an elastic
   continuum has relative resonance width Gamma/omega ~ omega^(d-2). In d>2
   this width is asymptotically small, so convolution with the phonon bath
   preserves a smooth defect-resonance power law, provided the renormalized
   curvature distribution is already specified and defects remain weakly
   coupled. This is not a derivation of that distribution.
5. UNRESOLVED: No derivation was found for the low-kappa quench-selected local
   jet weight for a physically specified cooling protocol. Global minimization,
   local metastability, overdamped minimization, and inertial cooling induce
   different measures. The literature supports protocol effects but does not
   close this measure-to-exponent step.

I. QUARTIC JET: EXACT ALGEBRA, CONDITIONAL EXPONENTS
====================================================

Let a smooth one-coordinate energy be expanded about a reference point s=0:

    U(s) = a1 s + a2 s^2/2 + a3 s^3/6 + a4 s^4/24.

Suppose s=s0 is a stationary point and write y=s-s0. Set
 kappa=U''(s0), tau=U'''(s0), chi=U''''(s0). Then

    U(s0+y)-U(s0) = kappa y^2/2 + tau y^3/6 + chi y^4/24.

The inverse coordinate transformation is

    a1 = -kappa s0 + tau s0^2/2 - chi s0^3/6,
    a2 =  kappa - tau s0 + chi s0^2/2,
    a3 =  tau - chi s0,
    a4 =  chi.

Direct differentiation gives

    |det d(a1,a2,a3,a4)/d(s0,kappa,tau,chi)| = kappa

on the stable branch kappa>0. Therefore, if (and only if the sampling density
in the a-coordinates is smooth and nonzero on the relevant chart, the mode
selection does not add a singular weight, and the other coordinates integrate
to a finite nonzero constant),

    p0(s0,kappa,tau,chi) = kappa p_a(a(s0,kappa,tau,chi)),
    p0(kappa) ~ C kappa,
    D(omega) d omega = p0(kappa) d kappa,
    D(omega) = 2 omega p0(omega^2) ~ 2 C omega^3.

The Jacobian is exact; the distributional smoothness and the interpretation of
p0 as the glass's mode-level sampling law are assumptions. In particular this
is not yet a derivation from a quench.

For chi>0 the quartic polynomial about the stationary point is nowhere below
U(s0) exactly when

    U(s0+y)-U(s0) = y^2/24 (12 kappa + 4 tau y + chi y^2) >= 0 for every y,
    tau^2 <= 3 kappa chi.

This is the discriminant condition for that quadratic bracket. It selects the
lowest well of the quartic truncation along this coordinate. It is stronger
than local mechanical stability, which only requires kappa>0. It is not a
criterion for the true global minimum of the full many-particle energy: higher
orders and all transverse coordinates can change the remote landscape.

If the smooth-jet factor is multiplied by an as-yet-unknown preparation and
mode-selection weight

    W_p(kappa,tau,chi) ~ kappa^q |tau|^b,

with chi supported on a compact interval bounded away from zero, then the
unrestricted tau integral gives

    p_p(kappa) ~ kappa^(1+q),       D_p(omega) ~ omega^(3+2q).

If the quartic-lowest-well restriction is also imposed, tau is integrated only
over |tau| <= sqrt(3 kappa chi), and for b>-1,

    p_p(kappa) ~ kappa^(3/2+q+b/2),
    D_p(omega) ~ omega^(4+2q+b).

The familiar omega^3 and omega^4 powers are the special case q=b=0. More
generally, if the pre-selection curvature density is kappa^a rather than the
smooth-jet value a=1, the corresponding powers are 2(a+q)+1 without the gate
and 2a+2q+b+2 with the gate. Thus the gate does not remove the need to derive
the preparation measure; it only supplies one factor when its support is
actually imposed. Choosing q or b to fit an observed exponent is parameterizing
the answer, not explaining it.

A mixture of the two quartic branches has

    D_loc(omega) = A3 omega^3 + A4 omega^4,
    beta_eff = d log(D_loc)/d log(omega)
             = 3 + A4 omega/(A3 + A4 omega),
    omega_cross = A3/A4.

This predicts how a finite fitting window can report an intermediate effective
exponent; it does not predict A3 or A4. Any nonzero A3 dominates at sufficiently
small omega, so a truly asymptotic omega^4 law requires A3=0 or a different
small-omega scaling of that contribution.

II. LOCAL STABILITY, QUARTIC LOWEST-WELL SELECTION, AND GLOBAL PREPARATION
=========================================================================

Consider

    U_epsilon(y)=epsilon y^2/2 + y^3/6 + y^4/24,  epsilon>0.

The origin is a strict local minimum for every epsilon>0, with curvature
kappa=epsilon. For epsilon<1/3, tau^2=1>3 epsilon chi, so the quartic has a
deeper displaced minimum. Thus positive Hessian curvature does not impose the
quartic-lowest-well condition. When epsilon is small, the intervening saddle is
at y=-2 epsilon+O(epsilon^2), its barrier is 2 epsilon^3/3+O(epsilon^4), and
the displaced minimum is lower. This is an exact local counterexample to
identifying mechanical stability with selection of the lowest nearby well.

There are three distinct selection notions:

  (i) local Hessian stability of a final inherent structure: every non-Goldstone
      eigenvalue is positive;
  (ii) lowest minimum of a quartic polynomial along one chosen coordinate:
      tau^2<=3 kappa chi under chi>0;
  (iii) selection by the actual cooling, thermostat, and relaxation protocol,
      which depends on the whole many-dimensional landscape and path.

They are not interchangeable. A quartic Taylor polynomial can itself fail to
represent a remote minimum of the full energy. If the question is whether a
real cooling trajectory removes a metastable soft core, that is a statement
about its path measure, activation barriers, and remaining time, not about the
sign of the final Hessian eigenvalue alone.

For deterministic overdamped minimization of a fixed energy U,

    dq/dt = -M grad U(q),       Phi(q0)=lim_(t->infinity) q(t),

the probability of ending in minimum alpha is exactly

    w_p(alpha) = integral_(B_alpha) rho_parent(q0) dq0,

where B_alpha is its attraction basin and rho_parent is the initial measure.
For an inertial quench, the state is (q,p) and the relevant basin lies in phase
space. For a noisy, finite-rate cooling schedule, the endpoint law is the
pushforward through the time-dependent Langevin/Fokker-Planck propagator; it
need not equal a static basin-volume measure. In all cases the observable
mode DOS is exactly an endpoint expectation, for example

    D_p(omega) = integral rho_parent(dz0)
                 sum_n delta(omega-omega_n(Phi_p(z0))).

The sum must also specify how quasi-localized modes are selected and counted.
The mode-level jet measure induced by the protocol can be written

    Pi_p(dtheta) = integral rho_parent(dz0)
                    sum_(n in QLM(Phi_p(z0))) delta_(theta_n)(dtheta),
    theta=(s0,kappa,tau,chi, elastic multipole, core orientation, ...).

Only under a smooth landscape chart can it be factored schematically as

    d Pi_p = kappa p_a(a(theta)) W_p(theta) dtheta.

Here W_p contains the basin/path weight, the choice of direction/eigenvector,
the QLM classification, and correlations with the surrounding elastic field.
This is the exact location of the unresolved preparation gate. The low-kappa
asymptotic of W_p is not fixed by the Jacobian or by local stability.

An explicit obstruction prevents inferring W_p from a local jet alone. Start
with any smooth potential whose germ around y=0 has the prescribed (kappa,
tau,chi), and add a C-infinity bump supported outside |y|<delta. This leaves
every derivative at y=0 unchanged while moving neighboring maxima and minima.
For gradient descent from a uniform initial distribution on a bounded interval,
the capture probability of the origin is proportional to the length of its
basin, bounded by adjacent maxima. Moving a bump changes that basin weight
without changing the local jet. In higher dimensions the basin boundary is a
global stable manifold, so its measure has still more information than a
one-coordinate Taylor expansion.

A tempting fold argument also fails as a universal selection rule. In
U(x)=x^3/3-mu x, the stationary points are x=+sqrt(mu), a minimum with
kappa=2 sqrt(mu), and x=-sqrt(mu), a maximum. The minimum's overdamped basin
intersects initial interval [-L,L] in (-sqrt(mu),L], of length L+sqrt(mu)
=L+kappa/2. As kappa tends to zero this length approaches L, rather than
vanishing as kappa. The opposite basin boundary and the initial density decide
the capture weight. A confining modification far from the fold changes the
other boundary but not the local normal form. Therefore saddle-node softness
does not by itself supply a kappa suppression factor for a quench.

III. A LOCAL BARRIER TEST FOR FRAGILE SOFT MODES
================================================

For the quartic jet, stationary points aside from y=0 solve

    kappa + tau y/2 + chi y^2/6 = 0.

Take chi bounded, tau bounded away from zero, and kappa -> 0+. The root nearest
the origin is the intervening saddle,

    y_s = -2 kappa/tau + O(kappa^2),
    B = U(s0+y_s)-U(s0)
      = 2 kappa^3/(3 tau^2) + O(kappa^4).

For a full smooth potential with a bounded fifth derivative in this
neighborhood, the fifth-order remainder at y_s is O(kappa^5); the displayed
leading barrier is unchanged. This is a local asymptotic, independent of the
height of a more distant minimum. If tau tends to zero with kappa, the scaling
changes; the finite-nonzero-tau hypothesis is essential.

If the fragile branch has p(kappa)~C kappa and a conditional tau distribution
that converges to h(tau) with 0 < integral |tau|^(4/3)h(tau)d tau < infinity,
then changing variables in B=2 kappa^3/(3 tau^2) gives

    Pr(B < b) ~ (C/2)(3/2)^(2/3) b^(2/3)
                integral |tau|^(4/3) h(tau) d tau,
    p_B(b) ~ const * b^(-1/3).

Since D(omega)~A3 omega^3 implies p(kappa)=(A3/2)kappa, an omega^3 fragile
branch predicts this barrier CDF if its cubic coefficient remains
nondegenerate. It is a quantitative, falsifiable consequence, not a new claim
that all omega^3 spectra must have that origin.

The discriminant boundary tau^2=3 kappa chi is also measurable as
r=tau^2/(3 kappa chi)=1. A decisive protocol comparison would prepare
matched-composition glasses with inertial continuous cooling, overdamped
instantaneous minimization, and slower cooling, then for each QLM compute kappa,
tau, chi, r, and the actual barrier along a *transversely relaxed* path. Report
the conditional fraction with r>1 at each frequency, D_r(omega), and the
barrier CDF. If an omega^3 part is carried by fragile quartic wells, it should
co-occur with a finite-τ population and B~omega^6, with low-barrier cumulative
scaling b^(2/3). If an omega^4 tail reflects the quartic-lowest gate, the
selected r>1 fraction must be subleading under that particular mechanism.
Match the mean inherent-state energy across protocols: existing simulations
show why energy matching alone cannot substitute for local-jet/path matching.

The transverse-relaxation qualification matters. In an eigenbasis for a soft
coordinate x and stable coordinates y, consider

    U(x,y)=kappa x^2/2 + tau x^3/6 + chi x^4/24
             + y^T A y/2 + x^2 c^T y/2 + higher terms,
    A>0.

Minimizing over y gives y*(x)=-A^(-1)c x^2/2+O(x^3). Substitution changes the
quartic coefficient to

    chi_relaxed = chi - 3 c^T A^(-1)c.

Thus a one-dimensional slice along a harmonic eigenvector need not have the
same quartic stability criterion as the relaxed reaction path. Soft acoustic
coordinates make the inverse-A limit and its size dependence another item to
control. A practical numerical test should compare the eigenvector slice,
successive nonlinear mode continuation, and the fully relaxed barrier. This
term can change the measured r and barrier; it does not by itself provide the
quench distribution of those quantities.

IV. LONG-RANGE ELASTICITY AND THE PHONON HYBRIDIZATION GATE
=========================================================

For an elastic dipole in d dimensions, the displacement field decays as
u(r)~r^(-(d-1)) outside a core. Its tail norm is

    integral_xi^L r^(d-1) |u(r)|^2 dr ~ integral_xi^L r^(1-d) dr.

It converges for d>2 and grows as log(L/xi) for d=2. This yields a finite
core-normalization scale in 3D and logarithmic normalization/participation
corrections in 2D. Published 2D/3D/4D simulations report omega^4 in all three
dimensions but a size-dependent prefactor and weaker localization in 2D. The
elastic tail can therefore change visibility and finite-size scaling without
by itself changing the local curvature exponent.

Here is a conditional hybridization calculation. Represent one local defect by
a unit-mass coordinate x with renormalized bare stiffness kappa_R, linearly
coupled to longitudinal/transverse elastic modes u_q of frequency cq. A local
force dipole has Fourier amplitude O(q); normalized plane waves contribute
V^(-1/2), so g_q=O(q/sqrt(V)). Its retarded self-energy is, up to tensor and
angular constants,

    Sigma(omega) = sum_q |g_q|^2 / (c^2 q^2-(omega+i0)^2).

The acoustic phase-space integral gives

    Im Sigma(omega) ~ integral dq q^(d-1) q^2
                         delta(c^2 q^2-omega^2) ~ omega^d.

The static shift Sigma(0) and the analytic mass renormalization are infrared
finite for d>2. Absorbing them into kappa_R and a finite Z gives

    G_x(omega)^(-1) = kappa_R - Z omega^2 - i C omega^d + higher terms.

At the resonance omega_0=sqrt(kappa_R/Z), the line width is

    Gamma(omega_0) ~ omega_0^(d-1),
    Gamma/omega_0 ~ omega_0^(d-2).

Thus in 3D the relative line width vanishes linearly with omega_0. In 2D the
coupling is marginal at this power-counting level and the real mass correction
is logarithmic. This calculation assumes a localized coordinate with a
nonzero dipole, a harmonic elastic continuum, weak linear coupling, and no
collective defect interaction that broadens resonances by O(omega). It does
not prove those assumptions for every glass or repair an unknown distribution
of kappa_R. If the map from the unhybridized to renormalized curvature is smooth
with nonzero derivative at zero, it preserves a power exponent; an additive
gap or singular renormalization would not.

A basis-invariant local spectral measure can avoid assigning a hybridized
eigenvector wholly to either a phonon or a defect. For a compact force pattern
f define

    S_f(omega)=sum_n |<f,e_n>|^2 delta(omega-omega_n)
              = <f|delta(omega-sqrt(H))|f>.

If the net force and its dipole moment vanish, its Fourier form factor starts
at q^2. A normalized acoustic mode then has squared overlap O(q^4/V); the
Debye mode count is O(V omega^(d-1)). Hence

    S_f^phonon(omega) ~ omega^(d+3),
    S_f^phonon(omega) ~ omega^6 in d=3.

If the same probe has a nonzero variance of core overlap with localized defect
resonances, their weighted contribution is C_Q A_beta omega^beta. The
single-defect calculation above gives a normalized line kernel whose relative
width tends to zero in d>2. Convolution with such a kernel preserves a smooth
power law: for |omega-omega0|/omega0=o(1), omega0^beta=omega^beta(1+o(1)), and
the kernel has unit spectral mass. The resulting 3D prediction is

    S_f(omega) ~ C3 A3 omega^3 + C4 A4 omega^4 + C_ph omega^6.

This is a conditional observable distinction: quadrupolar forcing suppresses
the Debye background more rapidly than either proposed defect tail and does
not require counting eigenvectors below the first phonon band. Multiple probe
positions/orientations are needed to keep the mean core-overlap variance
nonzero. The measured dissipative susceptibility has an extra 1/omega factor
relative to S_f. A finite-resolution experiment, disorder-dependent overlap,
and non-dilute defects can alter prefactors and crossover windows; no actual
experimental realization is claimed here.

For comparison, if QLM centers are independently Poisson distributed with
per-volume density A_beta omega^beta, then

    Pr(omega_min > omega) = exp[-V A_beta omega^(beta+1)/(beta+1)],
    omega_min,median = [(beta+1) log(2)/(V A_beta)]^(1/(beta+1)).

This extreme-value law is conditional on independent centers. At the first
phonon scale omega_1~L^(-1), the expected number of QLMs below it is

    n_Q(omega<omega_1) ~ L^(d-beta-1).

For beta=4 this is L^(-2) in 3D and L^(-3) in 2D. Thus a clean eigenvector
window below the first phonon disappears with size even though the local
resonance population can persist. This explains a measurement obstruction,
not the frequency law.

V. SOURCE COMPARISON AND THE EXACT GAP
======================================

The following are primary-source reports, not external validation of the
mechanism developed above.

* Gurarie and Chalker, arXiv:cond-mat/0204630 (submitted 2002), “Some generic
  aspects of bosonic excitations in disordered systems,” PRL 89, 136801 (2002),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0204630. Kumar, Procaccia and Singh reproduce
  the local quartic change of variables and its assumptions below. Their
  source-reported formulas at arXiv:2102.12368v2, lines 41–66, explicitly say
  smooth coefficient PDF -> p(kappa)~kappa -> omega^3, while the additional
  quartic-lowest-well condition |tau|<=sqrt(3 kappa chi) -> omega^4.

* Kumar, Procaccia and Singh, “Density of Quasi-localized Modes in Glasses:
  where are the Two-Level Systems?”, arXiv:2102.12368v2 (2021), EPL 135, 66001,
  https://arxiv.org/html/2102.12368. They report 60,000 independently prepared
  2D N=4000 glasses but only 403 selected QLMs (lines 76–93). Of these, 278
  satisfy the quartic-lowest condition and 125 violate it; in that sample the
  first group has no observed double-well profiles and the second group has
  shallow lower wells with barriers from 3.4e-9 to 4.0e-4. Their measured
  subgroup exponents agree with omega^4 and omega^3 respectively. This is a
  focused model-level separation, not a derivation of the quench-selected
  weight across materials or protocols. Their expansion uses the harmonic
  eigenvector direction (lines 82–87), so it does not settle the relaxed-path
  issue above.

* Lerner and Bouchbinder, arXiv:1705.01037v2 (29 Aug 2017), “Effect of
  instantaneous and continuous quenches on the density of vibrational modes in
  model glasses,” PRE 96, 020104(R), https://arxiv.org/html/1705.01037. They
  report a 3D inverse-power-law model, N=2000, fully overdamped minimization
  after instantaneous quenches, and continuous cooling (lines 49–53). For
  instantaneous quenches, 3<beta<=4 and beta approaches 4 as parent temperature
  approaches Tg; continuous rates below 1e-2 give beta=4, while an infinitely
  fast run gives about 3.3 (lines 59–71). An instantaneous T0=0.60 ensemble
  and continuous rate 1e-3 ensemble have mean energies within less than 1%,
  but different beta (lines 72–79). The paper attributes the effect to inertial
  self-organization and proposes the soft-potential lowest-well picture
  (lines 84–89); it does not compute W_p(kappa,tau,chi). The reported beta fits
  are in a restricted finite-size window below Goldstone modes.

* Lerner and Bouchbinder, arXiv:1711.11263v3 (30 Mar 2018), “Frustration-induced
  internal stresses are responsible for quasilocalized modes in structural
  glasses,” PRE 97, 032140, https://arxiv.org/html/1711.11263. For a radial
  pair potential, they decompose the Hessian as

      M = sum_alpha kappa_alpha grad(r_alpha) grad(r_alpha)^T
          - sum_alpha f_alpha/r_alpha Hessian(r_alpha).

  The reported small eigenvalue results from cancellation between contributions
  individually much larger than omega^2 (lines 43–49). Artificially reducing
  stresses by about 1–3% opens a gap, while slower annealing reduces measured
  macroscopic stress by about 4% and preserves a gapless omega^4 tail (lines
  65–80). This distinguishes a scalar average stress from the prepared joint
  organization of stiffness and stress. The modification of the Hessian is
  artificial; it does not derive a preparation measure.

* Kapteijns, Bouchbinder and Lerner, arXiv:1803.11383v2 (7 Aug 2018), “Universal
  non-phononic density of states in 2D, 3D and 4D glasses,” PRL 121, 055501,
  https://arxiv.org/html/1803.11383v2. They report omega^4 in d=2,3,4; a core
  size near ten particle diameters; tails r^(-(d-1)); strong hybridization
  narrowing the accessible harmonic window; size-independent prefactors in 3D
  and 4D but a growing 2D prefactor (lines 39–44, 65–85). Their elastic-tail
  calculation gives omega_g~(log N)^(-1/2) in 2D and a finite limit for d>2.
  This supports the separation between local exponent and dimension-dependent
  observability, but remains an eigenmode measurement.

* Wang et al., “Low-frequency vibrational modes of stable glasses,” Nature
  Communications 10, 26 (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6318266/.
  Swap-Monte-Carlo-prepared 3D glasses from poorly annealed through ultrastable
  have D_loc=A4 omega^4 across the studied parent temperatures, while A4 falls
  sharply below the mode-coupling temperature and the modes become more
  localized/sparser (lines 113–115, 133–139, 163–178). This is source-reported
  numerical evidence for robust exponent with preparation-dependent
  population, not a formula for A4(p).

* Schirmacher et al., arXiv:2310.02983v1 (2023), “The nature of non-phononic
  excitations in disordered systems,” Nature Communications 15, 3107 (2024),
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02983. Their GHET predicts type-I omega^2 modes in
  high-parent-temperature, marginal samples and type-II stress-related modes
  with exponent depending on small microscopic-stress statistics and potential
  cutoff tapering (abstract and DOI-linked article). Lerner and Bouchbinder,
  arXiv:2405.07851v2 (4 Dec 2024), PRE 111, L013402 (2025),
  https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07851, test this in 3D models. They report
  omega^4 rather than predicted omega^(10/3) for m=6 and omega^5 for attractive
  bead-spring m=1; the taper-induced pairwise-stress singularity disappears
  after particle-level summation, and a purported vortex mode can be explained
  as a phonon superposition (lines 42–56, 68–94). Schirmacher et al.,
  arXiv:2509.04988v3 (12 May 2026), PRE 113, 065406 (2026),
  https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04988, respond with new small-system data arguing
  for taper-dependent slopes and stress-related vortex modes; they dispute the
  finite-size interpretation (lines 214–232). This debate is unresolved here.
  It reinforces that “which stress variable, at which coarse-graining scale?”
  is part of the missing representation; pairwise cutoff statistics cannot be
  silently identified with the mode-core stress law.

* Moriel, Lerner and Bouchbinder, arXiv:2404.16996v2 (26 Nov 2024), “Experimental
  evidence for the omega^4 tail of the nonphononic spectra of glasses,” J Appl
  Phys 136, 225105 (2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16996. They infer the
  nonphononic contribution by subtracting a Debye term from measured total
  spectra and report consistency across several materials (abstract). This is
  a meaningful experimental constraint, but not direct observation of isolated
  localized eigenvectors or of the proposed local barrier distribution.

The exact obstruction has three parts. (A) The local quartic chart gives the
power only after a coefficient prior and a selected-well gate are specified.
(B) The full elastic solid renormalizes local stiffness and hybridizes its
eigenvectors; in 3D a weak dipolar bath is asymptotically narrow, but the
renormalized curvature law remains an input. (C) The cooling protocol fixes
which final minima and local defects are sampled through basin/path weights and
their correlations with frozen stress. No consulted primary source derives
that complete joint pushforward law. Mean inherent-state energy, scalar stress,
and positive Hessian curvature are each insufficient statistics according
to the cited comparisons.

VI. ATTEMPTS THAT DID NOT CLOSE THE MECHANISM
=============================================

1. “Hessian positivity forces omega^4.” Refuted by the strict local minimum
   U_epsilon above, whose omega can be arbitrarily small while a lower adjacent
   well remains. Local stability does not impose a distribution or exclude
   metastability.
2. “The change-of-variables Jacobian is the explanation.” Incomplete. It gives
   omega^3 under smooth random jets. The omega^4 step adds a selection
   inequality; neither the smooth jet law nor that inequality is shown to be
   the output of a physical cooling schedule.
3. “Gradient descent weights a fold-born soft well by its shrinking width.”
   Refuted as a universal statement by the fold normal form above: for a fixed
   broad initial interval, its basin length tends to a nonzero constant. The
   basin may shrink under a specially concentrated initial measure or a
   different global landscape, but that is precisely preparation dependence.
4. “Average stress reduction determines the tail.” The 2018 stress-rescaling
   comparison reports the opposite: a smaller artificial reduction gaps modes,
   whereas larger annealing-related stress reduction preserves them. The joint
   structure of stress and stiffness changes under preparation.
5. “A harmonic eigenvector quartic slice is the physical activation path.”
   The explicit transverse-relaxation correction chi_relaxed=chi-3c^T A^-1c
   refutes equality in general. A measured barrier or nonlinear continuation
   must be computed along a specified path.
6. “One stress-distribution theory currently resolves universality.” The 2024
   GHET paper and 2025/2026 tests directly conflict over the relevant stress
   scale, localization, finite-size corrections, and cutoff dependence. No
   conclusion here upgrades either side to established status.

VII. RUNNABLE EVIDENCE AND SCOPE
===============================

Run from the campaign workspace:

    python3 work/natural/n09_glass_marginality/diagnostic.py

The script uses only the Python standard library. It checks the Jacobian at
sample points; verifies numerically that B/kappa^3 tends to 2/3 for tau=chi=1;
prints the conditional omega^3/omega^4, phonon multipole, resonance-width, and
fold-basin exponents. Its output is an algebraic/numerical sanity check only.
It contains no simulated glass, experimental data, or independent evidence for
the smooth-jet law or W_p. The expected final line is “all algebraic diagnostics
passed; no empirical glass data were used”.

Strongest supported outcome at v1: exact derivation of what assumptions produce
the two familiar quartic-jet exponents; exact separation of local Hessian,
quartic-lowest, and protocol selection; a robust conditional low-barrier test
for a fragile branch; and a controlled d>2 elastic-hybridization argument for
local resonance exponents. The foundational requested law—the actual
