STATUS AND STRONGEST RESULT

Internally proved, with a self-contained elementary proof below. Every nonnegative
multiaffine polynomial of constant parity that is nonvanishing when all variables
have positive real part satisfies the coefficient-one square-root exchange
inequality. In particular, the proposed real-stable signed even-hole polynomials
satisfy the generalized six-hole inequality, and the theorem covers an arbitrary
shared occupied core. It is stronger than a theorem about a six-by-six Hermitian
matrix. The exchange constant one is optimal, including in the hard-BCS matrix
subclass.

This closes the specified coefficient-exchange gate. It does not prove the
determinant-state sampler's transition, microscopic two-replica energy, or
congestion bounds, and it does not establish an FPRAS by itself. Novelty of the
exchange inequality remains a separate literature question.

EXACT THEOREM / INTERFACE

Let E be a finite set. Let f:2^E -> [0,infinity) vanish on all sets of one parity,
and define the multiaffine polynomial h(x)=sum_U f(U) product_{i in U} x_i.
Assume h is nonzero and Hurwitz stable: h(z) != 0 whenever Re z_i > 0 for all i.
For all S,T in 2^E of the supported parity and every a in D=S symmetric-difference
T, the following inequality holds:

 sqrt(f(S) f(T)) <=
 sum_{j in D, j != a} sqrt(f(S symmetric-difference {a,j})
                          f(T symmetric-difference {a,j})).             (E)

If f(S)f(T)=0, (E) is immediate. If S=T, there is no a to quantify over. Otherwise
|D| is a positive even integer. No homogeneity, ordinary positive-coefficient
log-concavity, determinant representation, graph structure, or genericity is
assumed. The coefficients may be zero and the polynomial may have boundary
zeros on Re z_i=0.

For the exact form in the cycle-4 task, n=|E| is even and

 Q(x)=sum_{U even} (-1)^((n-|U|)/2) f(U) x^U

is real stable in the upper half-plane. Then
h(x)=(-1)^(n/2) Q(i x)=sum_{U even}f(U)x^U is Hurwitz stable, so (E) applies.
The rotation maps Re x_i>0 to Im(i x_i)>0. This is the place where the signed
stable polynomial enters; it is not a claim that its unsigned coefficient
polynomial is stable in the upper half-plane or log-concave.

AN ELEMENTARY ODD-DEGREE ENDPOINT LEMMA

Lemma. Let p(z)=a_0+a_1 z+...+a_d z^d be a real polynomial of odd degree d with
a_0,a_d>0 and no zero in Re z>0. Then

                       a_0 a_d <= a_1 a_(d-1).                       (L)

Proof. All its roots rho_i satisfy Re rho_i<=0; none is zero because a_0>0.
Put r_i=-1/rho_i. Then Re r_i>=0 and

 p(z)=a_0 product_i (1+r_i z),
 a_1/a_0=sum_i r_i,
 a_(d-1)/a_d=sum_i 1/r_i.

The nonreal r_i occur in conjugate pairs. Each such pair contributes
2 Re r_i>=0 to the first sum and 2 Re(1/r_i)>=0 to the second. Since d is odd,
at least one r_i is real. Every real r_i is strictly positive: rho_i is real,
nonzero, and in the closed left half-plane. Let R be the nonempty multiset of
these positive real r_i. Hence

 (a_1/a_0)(a_(d-1)/a_d)
 >= (sum_{r in R} r)(sum_{r in R}1/r)
 >= |R|^2 >= 1,

where the second inequality is Cauchy-Schwarz. This proves (L). Multiplicities
and imaginary-axis roots cause no difficulty. There is no strict-stability
perturbation assumption. For d=1 the assertion is equality.

The odd-degree hypothesis is essential: p(z)=1+z^2 has no root in Re z>0 but
has a_0 a_2=1 and a_1^2=0. The lemma needs no sign assumption on the middle
coefficients; their nonnegativity in the application is independently available.

This is the entire univariate ingredient. Asner's total-nonnegativity theorem
for Hurwitz matrices also implies this endpoint minor inequality, but that
stronger imported theorem and the alternative Pick-residue argument are not
required here.

PROOF OF (E), INCLUDING EVERY CLOSURE AND INDEX

It suffices to assume f(S),f(T)>0. Twist by all coordinates in T:

 h^T(x)=x^T h(y), where y_i=1/x_i for i in T and y_i=x_i otherwise.

Multiaffinity makes h^T a polynomial with coefficient
[x^U] h^T=f(T symmetric-difference U). Inversion 1/z maps the open right
half-plane to itself, and x^T is nonzero there, so h^T is Hurwitz stable.
Because all supported original U have the same parity as T, h^T is even.
Its coefficients are nonnegative.

Set each variable outside D=S symmetric-difference T equal to zero, producing
g(x_D). Boundary specialization preserves stability or gives the zero
polynomial, by Hurwitz's theorem (equivalently, take positive real numbers
tending to zero). Here g is not zero because its constant coefficient is
f(T)>0. In particular g remains Hurwitz stable. This specialization fixes the
common core in the coefficient data; no differentiation or compression to a
smaller matrix is used.

Fix a in D and choose arbitrary positive real numbers w_j for j in D\{a}.
Set x_a=1 and x_j=w_j z for j in D\{a}. The resulting univariate polynomial p
is nonvanishing for Re z>0 because every coordinate supplied to g then has
positive real part. Let d=|D|-1, an odd integer, and W=product_{j != a}w_j.
Its four endpoint coefficients are exactly

 a_0       = f(T),
 a_d       = f(S) W,
 a_1       = sum_{j != a} f(T symmetric-difference {a,j}) w_j,
 a_(d-1)   = W sum_{j != a} f(S symmetric-difference {a,j})/w_j.       (C)

Reason for the parity-sensitive indices: x_a=1 contributes no z degree.
At degree zero, only the empty monomial can occur because {a} is odd. At
degree d, only the full even set D can occur; D\{a} is odd. At degree one,
only pairs {a,j} occur. At degree d-1, D\{a,j} is even and is the only possible
type of monomial. Its coefficient is f(T symmetric-difference
(D\{a,j}))=f(S symmetric-difference {a,j}). For |D|=2, the endpoint and
neighbor coefficients overlap; (C) still holds and the final exchange is an
identity. Positive f(S) ensures the actual degree is exactly d.

Apply (L), substitute (C), and cancel W>0. For every choice of positive w,

 f(S) f(T) <= (sum_{j != a} A_j w_j)(sum_{j != a} B_j/w_j),
 A_j=f(T symmetric-difference {a,j}),
 B_j=f(S symmetric-difference {a,j}).                                (F)

For arbitrary A_j,B_j>=0,

 inf_{w_j>0} (sum_j A_j w_j)(sum_j B_j/w_j)
                         = (sum_j sqrt(A_j B_j))^2.                 (O)

The lower bound is Cauchy-Schwarz. For coordinates with A_j,B_j>0 choose
w_j=sqrt(B_j/A_j). If A_j>0,B_j=0 send w_j to zero; if A_j=0,B_j>0 send
w_j to infinity; if both vanish the coordinate is irrelevant. These independent
limits establish the infimum formula even when no positive finite minimizer
exists. If the common positive-product set is empty, both sums can be sent to
zero and the infimum is zero. Taking the infimum in (F) and then the square
root proves (E) without any division by a zero coefficient.

SIX-HOLE AND SHARED-CORE CONSEQUENCES

The requested case S={1,2,3,4}, T={5,6}, a=1 gives five exchange terms and
has leading constant exactly one. In hard-BCS notation, f(U)=Z_(E\U)(F),
where Z_R is the sum of squared half-size complementary minors on the even
retained set R. All terms in (E) retain whatever common occupied sites lie
outside D. A twist followed by zero specialization carries those coefficients
unchanged. Thus the proof does not assume the retained core can be removed
from the matrix F; this was the load-bearing shared-core concern in cycle 4.

The four-hole triangle is the |D|=4 special case. Squaring (E) gives
f(S)f(T) <= (sum sqrt(A_jB_j))^2. A further Cauchy-Schwarz step gives the
weaker factor-(|D|-1) sum_j A_jB_j bound. It does not give the already-refuted
coefficient-one squared-sum inequality, even for four holes.

SHARPNESS / FALSIFICATION ATTEMPTS

For disjoint pairs of coordinates, h=product_pairs(1+x_i x_j) is Hurwitz
stable: two complex numbers in the open right half-plane cannot have product
equal to a negative real number. It is even, multiaffine, and nonnegative.
Take T empty, S the union of all pairs, and a in one pair. Exactly its partner
j contributes to (E), with value one; all other terms vanish. The left side
is also one. No universal constant smaller than one can hold.

This sharp example occurs in the matrix subclass: take F_(1,2)=F_(3,4)=
F_(5,6)=1 and all other entries zero. Then Z_R(F)=1 precisely when R is a
union of these pairs, and is zero otherwise. The exchange above is exact.

Main possible failure points independently checked: right-half-plane inversion
(positive real part preserved); positive rotation of the signed Q; parity after
full twist; boundary specialization not becoming zero (f(T)>0); actual odd
degree (f(S)>0); the degree d-1 coefficient and its complement index; d=1;
boundary roots in (L); disjoint nonzero exchange supports in (O). None leaves
a gap. The even-degree counterexample 1+z^2 explains why dropping constant
parity invalidates this particular proof and endpoint inequality.
