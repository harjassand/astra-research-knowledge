EXACT THEOREM

Let rho_N be any permutation-invariant N-qubit state with exactly the
maximally mixed Schur-Weyl sector weights

 rho_N = direct_sum_j p_j rho_j tensor I_(m_j)/m_j,
 p_j=(2j+1)m_j/2^N, Tr rho_j=1,
 m_j=binomial(N,N/2-j)-binomial(N,N/2-j-1).

The matrices rho_j can be arbitrary density matrices in their spin-j spaces.
Let rho_r be any r-qubit marginal, tau_r=I/2^r, and q=r/N. Define the white
reference chi-square quantity by

 chi2(rho_r||tau_r)=2^r Tr(rho_r^2)-1.

For 1<=r<=N with q<1/2,

 chi2(rho_r||tau_r)
 <= 108 q(1+3q)/(1-2q)^(7/2).                             (A)

In particular, for r<=N/128,

 chi2(rho_r||tau_r) <= 128 r/N.                            (B)

The constants here are uniform in N,r and all choices of rho_j. This proves
the requested claim with substantially smaller C than 50000. No dynamical
solution, positivity of a coherent-state representation, finite de Finetti
approximation, or assumption of sector extremizer purity is used.

The proof is a Pauli-degree expansion plus a one-variable polynomial bound.

STEP 1: AN ELEMENTARY POLYNOMIAL GRID LEMMA

For every real polynomial P of degree at most k>=1, and any nonnegative
integer or half-integer j, the spin grid G_j={-j,-j+1,...,j} obeys

 max_(m in G_j) P(m)^2
 <= 36 k^2 (1/(2j+1)) sum_(m in G_j) P(m)^2.              (C)

If j<4k^2 this is immediate, since 2j+1<=9k^2. Otherwise let
M=max_(x in [-j,j]) |P(x)|. The classical Markov polynomial derivative
inequality, rescaled to [-j,j], is

 max |P'| <= (k^2/j) M.

At a point x_* attaining M, all points in the intersection of [-j,j] with
[x_*-j/(2k^2),x_*+j/(2k^2)] have |P(x)|>=M/2. This intersection has length
at least j/(2k^2)>=2, and contains at least j/(4k^2) spin-grid points. Thus
sum_m P(m)^2 >= j M^2/(16k^2). Since j>=4k^2>=4,
(2j+1)/j<=9/4, proving (C) with constant 36. The case j=0 was already
covered by the elementary small-grid case.

PRIMARY THEOREM CHECK: The only non-elementary classical ingredient here is
the Markov derivative inequality ||P'||_[-1,1]<=k^2||P||_[-1,1]. Lawrence A.
Harris, "A proof of Markov's theorem for polynomials on Banach spaces,"
J. Math. Anal. Appl. 368 (2010), 374-381, DOI 10.1016/j.jmaa.2010.02.050,
Theorem 1 in the author PDF (page 3), gives an independent proof containing
this as the special case X=Y=R, derivative order one, T_k'(1)=k^2. The
hypotheses are a real polynomial of degree at most k bounded on the closed
unit interval. Source: https://www.ms.uky.edu/~larry/paper.dir/proof.pdf
The author PDF is retained with its SHA-256 in this report's manifest.

STEP 2: SYMMETRIZED PAULI OBSERVABLES ARE POLYNOMIALS IN SPIN

For a real unit vector u in R^3 put Q_i=u.sigma^(i), S=sum_i Q_i=2J_u, and

 E_k(u)=sum_(A subset [N], |A|=k) product_(i in A) Q_i,
 B_k(u)=E_k(u)/binomial(N,k).

The Q_i commute with each other and Q_i^2=I. Direct multiplication gives

 S E_k = (k+1)E_(k+1)+(N-k+1)E_(k-1),
 E_0=I, E_1=S.

Indeed outside indices create each (k+1)-subset exactly k+1 times, and
inside indices create each (k-1)-subset exactly N-k+1 times. This proves
that B_k is a real polynomial of degree at most k in J_u. Its eigenvalues
in the spin-j sector are the evaluations of that polynomial on G_j.
Equivalently these are normalized Krawtchouk polynomials, but no result
about Krawtchouk polynomials is required.

With tau_N=I/2^N, distinct subset Pauli products are orthogonal and each has
normalized squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm one. Hence

 Tr(tau_N B_k(u)^2)=1/binomial(N,k).                       (D)

Write B_(k,j) for its restriction to spin j. Permutation invariance means
this observable acts as B_(k,j) tensor identity on each multiplicity space.
The exact white sector weights give

 sum_j p_j Tr(B_(k,j)^2)/(2j+1)
 = Tr(tau_N B_k(u)^2).

Apply (C), then Cauchy-Schwarz across the weights, to arbitrary rho_j:

 |Tr(rho_N B_k(u))|
 <= sum_j p_j ||B_(k,j)||
 <= [sum_j p_j ||B_(k,j)||^2]^(1/2)
 <= [36k^2 sum_j p_j Tr(B_(k,j)^2)/(2j+1)]^(1/2).

By (D), for every unit u,

 |Tr(rho_N B_k(u))|^2 <= 36 k^2/binomial(N,k).             (E)

This is precisely where the exact Schur weights are used. The arbitrary
within-sector state is controlled by a polynomial evaluation inequality;
there is no replacement of rho_j by a pure, coherent, or thermal state.

STEP 3: RECOVER ALL MIXED-AXIS PAULI COEFFICIENTS

Define the real symmetric order-k tensor

 c_(a_1...a_k)=Tr[rho_N sigma_(a_1)^(1)...sigma_(a_k)^(k)],
 a_i in {x,y,z},
 T_k=sum_(a_1,...,a_k) c_(a_1...a_k)^2.

It is real since distinct-site Pauli products are Hermitian, and symmetric
since rho_N is permutation-invariant. Its diagonal polynomial is

 f_k(u)=sum c_(a_1...a_k) u_(a_1)...u_(a_k)
       =Tr[rho_N product_(i=1)^k (u.sigma^(i))]
       =Tr(rho_N B_k(u)).

For uniform u on the unit sphere S^2, the standard spherical moment formula
is a sum of Kronecker-delta pairings divided by (2k+1)!!. It follows, for
example, by writing a standard three-dimensional Gaussian as R u, applying
Gaussian pairings, and using E R^(2k)=(2k+1)!!. Contracting the moment formula
with c tensor c gives

 E_(u in S^2) f_k(u)^2 >= [k!/(2k+1)!!] T_k.              (F)

To check the inequality sign: the k! pairings with every leg of the first
tensor paired to the second each contribute ||c||_F^2. Every other pairing
has the same number of internal pairs in each tensor and contributes the
squared Frobenius norm of an iterated tensor trace, hence is nonnegative.
There are no untracked cross terms or tracelessness assumptions.

Combining (E) and (F) gives

 T_k <= 36 k^2 (2k+1)!!/[k! binomial(N,k)].                (G)

STEP 4: SUM THE PAULI-DEGREE EXPANSION

Pauli orthogonality for the r-qubit marginal gives exactly

 chi2(rho_r||tau_r)=sum_(k=1)^r binomial(r,k) T_k.

For r<=N, binomial(r,k)/binomial(N,k)
=product_(i=0)^(k-1) (r-i)/(N-i) <= (r/N)^k=q^k.
Using (G), extending a positive series to infinity, and q<1/2,

 chi2 <=36 sum_(k=1)^infinity k^2 (2k+1)!! q^k/k!.

The generating function and its differentiated form are

 sum_(k=0)^infinity (2k+1)!! q^k/k! = (1-2q)^(-3/2),
 sum_(k=1)^infinity k^2 (2k+1)!! q^k/k!
 = (q d/dq)^2(1-2q)^(-3/2)
 =3q(1+3q)/(1-2q)^(7/2).

This proves (A). For q<=1/128 the coefficient multiplying q is bounded by

 108(1+3/128)/(1-1/64)^(7/2)
 < 108(131/128)(64/63)^4
 = 68681728/583443 <128,

proving (B) without numerical rounding.

SHARP SMALL-q RATE AND LEADING CONSTANT

Let b_N=sum_j p_j j. The degree-one coefficient is exactly
T_1=4|<J>|^2/N^2<=4b_N^2/N^2. Retaining that exact coefficient and using
(G) only from k=2 gives the refined bound

 chi2 <= 4r b_N^2/N^2
       +108q[(1+3q)/(1-2q)^(7/2)-1].                     (H)

The highest-weight state
rho_N^up=direct_sum_j p_j |j,j><j,j| tensor I_(m_j)/m_j
attains the first term, so its marginal has chi2>=4r b_N^2/N^2. Since
b_N/sqrt(N)->sqrt(2/pi), as independently derived in the original frozen
report, the white-sector minimax chi2 obeys, for every r_N=o(N), r_N>=1,

 sup_rho chi2(rho_(r_N)||tau_(r_N))
 = [8/pi+o(1)] r_N/N.                                   (I)

Here the remainder in (H) is O((r/N)^2), and the finite-N error in b_N^2/N
vanishes. This leading constant is sharp; the universal finite constant
128 is deliberately simple rather than optimal.

RESOURCE AND FRESH-PREPARATION CONSEQUENCES

Use natural logarithms. Since tau_r is scalar, Jensen's inequality on the
eigenvalue distribution of rho_r gives

 D_rel(rho_r||tau_r)
 =sum_i lambda_i log(2^r lambda_i)
 <=log(sum_i lambda_i 2^r lambda_i)
 =log(1+chi2) <=128r/N                                   (J)

for r<=N/128. Thus the local relative-entropy deficit from maximal mixing
has no multiplicative log N loss under the exact sector-weight hypothesis.

For m independent fresh preparations of the same global state, each giving
the same r-site marginal, purity multiplicativity gives

 1+chi2(rho_r^tensorm||tau_r^tensorm)=(1+chi2(rho_r||tau_r))^m.

The trace/HS Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
D(a,I/d)<= (1/2)sqrt(chi2(a||I/d)). Consequently, if an arbitrary joint
measurement on those m marginals distinguishes rho_r^tensorm from
tau_r^tensorm with trace-distance requirement d_*>0, necessarily

 m >= log(1+4d_*^2)/log(1+128r/N)
   >= [log(1+4d_*^2)/128] N/r.                           (K)

With equal priors, success probability at least 1/2+delta requires
d_*=2delta. The bound allows collective/adaptive processing across fresh
preparations; it only assumes that the preparations are independent and
the accessible quantum input from each is an r-qubit marginal. It does not
cover sequential access to many different blocks from one preparation.

WHY THE STRONGER SECTOR CONDITION MATTERS

The chi2 bound is false under the mean J^2 condition alone. For even N let
rho_N^rare be a mixture with weight p=3/(N+2) on |up>^tensorN (spin N/2),
and weight 1-p on the maximally mixed spin-zero sector. It is
permutation-invariant and has exactly <J^2>=3N/4. Every r-site marginal
satisfies rho_r>=p|up>^tensorr<up|^tensorr, hence

 chi2(rho_r||tau_r)>=2^r p^2-1.

For example r=ceil(3 log_2 N)=o(N) makes this diverge. There is no conflict
with the original trace-distance theorem: a very small weight on an
extremely pure component can have small trace distance and enormous chi2.
