2. GRID ENERGY AND MULTILINEAR INTERPOLATION

For a source vector psi let F_z=(K_m psi)(z)=psi(sort z)/sqrt(m!). Set
u_z=h^(-m/2)F_z on interior nodes, and u_z=0 whenever a coordinate is
0 or n+1. Let I_h u be the tensor multilinear interpolant on [0,1]^m;
it is symmetric and zero on every outer face. Let U_h u be the centered
nearest-node step function, including zero-valued ghost cells.

Let E_h(u) be the full labeled-cube discrete Dirichlet energy

  E_h(u)=h^(m-2) sum_{unordered grid edges {z,z+e_i}}
                   |u_{z+e_i}-u_z|^2.                 (2.1)

Grid vertices range from 0 to n+1 in each coordinate. Tangential boundary
edges have zero difference. Interior and ghost-normal edges in (2.1)
are exactly the free Dirichlet cube form divided by 2J after scaling.
archive.txt (2.3) gives

  2J(Lh)^2 E_h(u)<=L^2<psi,Hcrit psi>.                 (2.2)

Two elementary interpolation estimates, valid for arbitrary complex nodal
values with zero ghost faces, are

  integral |grad I_hu|^2 <= E_h(u),
  ||I_hu-U_hu||_2^2 <=m 3^m h^2 E_h(u).                (2.3)

For the first, each partial derivative is the difference of two opposite
face interpolants divided by h. Convexity and integrating the tensor
weights bound its squared integral by the corresponding edge differences.
Each transverse interior node is shared by two cells with integrated
weight 1/2; the weights sum to one, giving the constant one.

For the second, in a centered nearest-node cell every vertex used by the
multilinear interpolation is at coordinate distance at most one from the
chosen node. Connect it by a coordinate path of at most m edges within
that node's radius-one star. Cauchy-Schwarz and convex interpolation give
pointwise error at most m times the sum of squared edge differences in
the star. A fixed edge belongs to at most 3^m stars. Integrating cells
of volume at most h^m gives the second estimate in (2.3).

This 3^m incidence counts local Q1 stencil stars in the FULL labeled
grid, not lifts of an edge from the ordered quotient. The separate orbit
edge multiplicity may be m!, as explicitly accounted in archive.txt.

The exact source embedding and K-step differ by

  ||I_{L,m}psi-U_hu||_2^2
      =sum_y |psi(y)|^2(1-sqrt(w(y)/m!))^2
      <=<psi,1_{W>0}psi><=<psi,W psi>.                  (2.4)

If ||psi|| is bounded and <psi,A_{L,m}(g)psi><=E with g>=g_->0, then
E_h<=E/(2J), since Lh>=1, and <W><=E/(g_- L). Thus (2.3)-(2.4) show
that I_{L,m}psi and I_hu are O(L^-1/2) close, with explicit m,E constants.
This transfers interpolation compactness to the EXACT source isometry.

3. THE CONTACT-NODE TRACE ESTIMATE

Fix a pair i<j. On the collapsed cube [0,1]^(m-1), let D_{h,ij}u be the
centered nodal step function whose chosen grid node has z_i=z_j, both
nearest to s/h, and whose other coordinates are nearest to their argument
divided by h. Values on ghost cells are zero. Its L^2 norm uses ordinary
ds d(other coordinates), and exactly

  ||D_{h,ij}u||_2^2
    =h^(m-1) sum_{z_i=z_j}|u_z|^2
    =h^-1 sum_{z_i=z_j}|F_z|^2.                        (3.1)

The crucial finite-grid lemma is

  ||D_{h,ij}u-(I_hu)|_{x_i=x_j}||_2^2
                 <=m 3^m h E_h(u).                    (3.2)

Proof. On each collapsed centered cell, choose its repeated-coordinate
node. Every multilinear vertex used at a point of the diagonal lies
within one coordinate step of that node. Again connect it by at most m
edges in the radius-one full-cube star. Convexity/path Cauchy-Schwarz
give the same pointwise star bound as Section 2. A fixed full-cube edge
belongs to at most 3^m such repeated-coordinate stars. The collapsed
cell volume is at most h^(m-1). Sum and use (2.1). This proves the
explicit constant; there is no assumption that nodal contact values equal
an interpolated diagonal trace.

Again the star incidence is local full-grid stencil incidence. A fixed
edge's endpoints must lie within distance one of the selected repeated
node, giving at most 3^m choices even before enforcing z_i=z_j. No
ordered-to-labeled orbit count is used in this interpolation estimate.

Compactness of the traces can be shown without a discrete trace theorem.
Every I_hu is in H^1_0 of the cube. Extend it by zero to R^m. For any
v in H^1_0, integrate the one-dimensional derivative of |v|^2 along the
unit normal to x_i=x_j, then apply Cauchy-Schwarz. The collapsed trace
has measure dSigma/sqrt(2), and an admissible bound is

  ||v|_{x_i=x_j}||_2^2<=4||v||_2 ||grad v||_2.           (3.3)

The zero extension is H^1, so density justifies this for every form-domain
function. Consequently strong bulk L^2 convergence plus bounded H^1
norm yields strong L^2 convergence of every diagonal trace. Estimate
(3.2) then also gives strong convergence of the discrete D_{h,ij}.

Bulk H^1 compactness here can itself be checked by the product sine basis:
its coefficients satisfy sum_k pi^2|k|^2|c_k|^2=||grad v||_2^2, so
the tail with any coordinate label>M is O(M^-2), uniformly. A diagonal
subsequence of bounded finite coefficient vectors is strong L^2 Cauchy.
This supplies the collective compactness used below, rather than merely
asserting strong-resolvent convergence.

4. FORM LIMINF: THE EXACT MULTIPLICITY INEQUALITY

For a weakly ordered node y with multiplicities r_v, put

  W(y)=sum_v(r_v-1), P(y)=sum_v r_v(r_v-1)/2,
  t(y)=prod_v r_v!, w(y)=m!/t(y).

Since t>=r_v for every v, including r_v=1,

  t W>=sum_v r_v(r_v-1)=2P,
  m! W>=2w P.                                         (4.1)

Therefore the original canonical contact term dominates the full labeled
pair-contact term EXACTLY:

  gL<psi,W psi>
     =gL m! sum_y W(y)|F_y|^2
     >=2gL sum_z sum_{i<j}1_{z_i=z_j}|F_z|^2
     =2g(Lh) sum_{i<j}||D_{h,ij}u||_2^2.               (4.2)

Higher coincidence strata need not be discarded to obtain a liminf.
Their multiplicities have the correct inequality direction.

Equivalently, on a sorted tuple the labeled pair term per |psi(y)|^2 is

each with F=psi/sqrt(m!); their factor w/m!=1/prod r_v! gives precisely
that coefficient. The elementary termwise inequality is

  sum_{j>K}exp(-tau a_{L,j})

Neither a scalar heat-trace limit nor operator-norm convergence alone
