NOISY SCALAR ORACLE: EXACT ACQUISITION IN A SUPPLIED AFFINE CLASS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the stated interface; finite pair/decoder
checks are recorded separately. No novelty, priority, or broad representation-
acquisition claim is made.

RESULT

For an unknown affine function f(x)=a x+b on [0,1], with integer coefficients
a,b in {0,...,H}, direct noisy scalar queries at x=0 and x=1 recover (a,b)
exactly with probability at least 1-delta using

    n = 2 ceil(32 sigma^2 ln(8/delta))

queries, when the response has one fractional bit and a public finite clipping
range chosen below. Conversely, every learner, including an adaptive learner
allowed arbitrary real query points and unquantized observations, needs

    n >= 2 sigma^2 (1-2 delta) ln((1-delta)/delta)

queries in the worst case. Since H>=1 and 0<delta<=1/4, the minimax query
complexity is Theta(1 + sigma^2 ln(1/delta)) with universal constants. Input
query precision B=0 suffices: only the rational endpoints 0 and 1 are used.
One half-unit output grid (Q=1 fractional bit) suffices. The lower bound holds
even if the learner is given infinite response precision and arbitrary query
precision, so increasing either precision cannot improve this query-count
order. The minimum necessary output precision is not determined here.

This is acquisition of two latent parameters inside a supplied model family.
The family, its coefficient bound, and the noise law are given. The values a
and b are not. No examples, coefficients, realization, decoder, or teacher
answer are supplied. The result does not acquire the affine representation
itself and does not establish autonomous discovery of a model class.

1. LATENT CLASS AND LEGAL ORACLE

Fix an integer H>=1 and define

    F_H = { f_(a,b)(x)=a x+b : a,b in {0,1,...,H}, x in [0,1] }.

The learner is told H and that the unknown target belongs to F_H. It is also
told sigma>0, a confidence target delta in (0,1/4], and the response
quantizer. The learner does not know a or b.

At round t the learner chooses a rational point x_t in [0,1]. A general
nonendpoint rational is sent as its reduced numerator and denominator in a
canonical self-delimiting binary code; its input precision is the denominator
bit length B(x)=ceil(log_2 q) for x=p/q. The endpoint choices 0 and 1 have
dedicated one-bit codes and B=0. Choices may depend on all earlier queries
and responses and on private randomness. The oracle draws fresh independent
Z_t~N(0,sigma^2), forms

    u_t = f(x_t)+Z_t,

rounds u_t to the nearest multiple of eta=2^(-Q), and clips the rounded
integer index to [-M,M]. It returns the resulting index as a fixed-length
binary code. Ties have probability zero. This is the entire oracle response;
there is no side channel or batch/matrix output.

For the claimed upper bound take Q>=1, let

    m = ceil(32 sigma^2 ln(8/delta)),
    R = ceil(sigma sqrt(2 ln(8m/delta))),
    M = ceil(2^Q (2H+R+1)),
    r = ceil(log_2(2M+1)).

The public clip interval is [-M eta, M eta]. Each oracle answer uses exactly
r bits (unused codes are padded). Public channel parameters are shared by the
learner and oracle. If they must be transmitted, their one-time encoding cost
is added separately.

2. ACHIEVABILITY AND FAILURE PROBABILITY

Query x=0 exactly m times and x=1 exactly m times. Let y_t^(0) and y_t^(1)
denote the decoded fixed-point scalar responses. Set

    b_hat = nearest_integer( (1/m) sum_t y_t^(0) ),
    c_hat = nearest_integer( (1/m) sum_t y_t^(1) ),
    a_hat = c_hat-b_hat,

and clamp the final pair to {0,...,H}^2 if needed.

At either endpoint the true mean mu is an integer in [0,2H]. On the event
|Z_t|<=R, the response does not saturate, because M eta>=2H+R+1, and its
rounding error is at most eta/2<=1/4. Hence

    | average(y_t)-mu | <= | average(Z_t) | + 1/4.

If |average(Z_t)|<1/4, rounding returns mu exactly. Since the average noise
is N(0,sigma^2/m), the Gaussian tail bound gives, for each endpoint,

    Pr{|average(Z_t)|>=1/4} <= 2 exp(-m/(32 sigma^2)).

Across both endpoint means this is at most 4 exp(-m/(32 sigma^2))<=delta/2.
Also, for each response,

    Pr{|Z_t|>R} <= 2 exp(-R^2/(2 sigma^2)) <= delta/(4m).

There are 2m responses, so the probability of any saturation is at most
delta/2. Outside these two failure events, b_hat=b and c_hat=a+b, hence
a_hat=a. Total failure is at most delta.

The endpoint map is injective on the whole class, not just on a subfamily:

    (f(0),f(1))=(b,a+b),   b=f(0),   a=f(1)-f(0).

Thus the two queried locations identify every member of F_H. The only random
part is estimating their integer endpoint values under the declared noise.

3. ADAPTIVE LOWER BOUND

Take the two legal targets f_0(x)=0 and f_1(x)=x, which belong to F_H for
H>=1. Conditional on a query x, their unquantized analog observations are
normal with the same variance and means differing by x. Their KL divergence
is x^2/(2 sigma^2)<=1/(2 sigma^2). Quantization and clipping are deterministic
maps, so the data-processing inequality can only decrease this divergence.

For an arbitrary adaptive n-query protocol, condition on its transcript up to
each round. Its next query policy is the same under the two targets, while
the response conditional divergence is at most 1/(2 sigma^2). The chain rule
for KL therefore gives

    D(P_0^transcript || P_1^transcript) <= n/(2 sigma^2).

If the final learner identifies the full target with error at most delta
under each member of F_H, the event A="the output is f_0" has probabilities
P_0(A)>=1-delta and P_1(A)<=delta. Applying data processing once more to this
binary event yields

    D(P_0^transcript || P_1^transcript)
      >= kl(1-delta || delta)
      = (1-2delta) ln((1-delta)/delta).

Combining the inequalities proves the stated lower bound. It covers adaptive
query selection and a stronger oracle that returns unquantized real values.
No query can separate this slope pair by more than one unit per observation,
because every legal x lies in [0,1]. A zero-query learner also cannot identify
all (H+1)^2>=4 targets with error below 1/2, giving the constant lower term.

Together with the endpoint upper bound, for every 0<delta<=1/4,

    n_min = Theta(1+sigma^2 ln(1/delta)).

This is a minimax query-order match, not a claim of matching best constants.
The sufficient Q>=1 output precision and B=0 input precision are explicit;
the theorem does not prove Q>=1 is necessary. The lower bound already permits
arbitrary query precision and infinite-precision observations, so extra
precision has no asymptotic value for query count in this model.

4. TOTAL BIT AND COMPUTATION COST

For the stated fixed-length protocol, each endpoint query is encoded by one
bit. Each response uses r=ceil(log_2(2M+1)) bits. The final coefficient pair
uses 2h bits, where h=ceil(log_2(H+1)). Thus the charged interaction plus
answer cost is exactly

    C_up = 2m(1+r)+2h bits,

excluding the public problem/channel description. Add that description once
if it is not already shared. Since

    r = Theta(Q + log_2(H+R+1)),

the response width explicitly charges the numeric height, clipping radius,
and fractional precision. Any learner using this same r-bit response format
and at most n queries spends at least rn response bits. The adaptive lower
bound therefore also lower-bounds total response communication by

    C_reply >= r max{1, 2 sigma^2 kl(1-delta || delta)}.

Together the matching query bound and fixed response width give communication
cost of order r(1+sigma^2 ln(1/delta)); query-coordinate and final-answer
bits are included in C_up. If the task requires only a model index rather
than the explicit pair, the answer-code term can be removed, but the query
and response accounting is unchanged.

The learner stores two integer response sums, each using O(r+log m) bits,
and performs O(m(r+log m)) bit operations for additions, divisions by m,
integer rounding, subtraction, and range checks. This excludes the oracle's
physical Gaussian random source and the learner's external runtime overhead;
the noisy answer's r transmitted bits are counted in full.

Example: H=3, sigma=1/2, delta=0.05, Q=1 gives m=41, R=3, M=20,
r=6, and h=2. The protocol asks 82 endpoint queries, sends 574 query/response
bits, and returns the pair in 4 bits, for 578 charged bits total. This example
is deliberately conservative; it illustrates the complete code/range charge,
not the best possible constant.

5. IDENTIFIABILITY COUNTEREXAMPLE AND EXECUTABLE CHECK

Let f_0(x)=0 and f_1(x)=x. If the learner is restricted to querying x=0,
then both targets have the same analog mean 0 at every round. They therefore
induce exactly the same complete transcript law after any quantization and
clipping, regardless of the number of repetitions. No estimator can have
success probability greater than 1/2 on both targets under that restricted
query policy. This is a genuine nonidentifiability, not a small-sample issue.

At x=1 their means are 0 and 1. For Q=1 (eta=1/2), sigma=1/2, and an
interior response bin y=0, the probabilities are

    Pr_0(y=0)=Phi(0.5)-Phi(-0.5)=0.3829249...,
    Pr_1(y=0)=Phi(-1.5)-Phi(-2.5)=0.0605975... .

The laws are distinct, and the two endpoints jointly identify all targets.
The runnable check at
work/programmes/acquisition_learning/leaves/noisy_scalar_model/pair_check.py
confirms endpoint injectivity for H=3 by exhaustive enumeration of all 16
coefficient pairs, equal response laws for the pair at x=0, and unequal bin
probabilities at x=1. Its seeded 1,000-trial decoder diagnostic recorded 0
errors for target (a,b)=(2,1) at the theorem's conservative m=41. This finite
diagnostic does not prove the probability bound. Raw output is saved beside
the script as pair_check_result.json.

6. EQUALLY INFORMED COMPARATOR AND LIMITS

The comparator is an ordinary learner told exactly the same F_H, H, sigma,
delta, query rights, quantizer, and clipping rule. It uses the nonadaptive
endpoint design and sample-mean rounding above. The lower bound applies to
every equally informed adaptive learner, including any learner choosing
points from its prior noisy observations. Therefore no adaptive strategy can
improve the minimax query order here, and the standard endpoint learner
already matches it. There is no measured or theoretical advantage over an
equally informed baseline.

What is acquired is only the target's coefficient pair. The affine family,
parameter grid, scalar evaluation interface, Gaussian law, fixed-point
precision, and finite code range are supplied. If coefficients may be
arbitrary reals, exact identification from finitely many noisy queries is
impossible; if the class or noise law is unknown, this theorem says nothing
about acquiring either one. Correlated/adversarial noise, model mismatch,
unknown H, and computational restrictions on query placement are outside
scope. This small construction is not evidence of general teacher-free
representation discovery or general intelligence.

7. PRIMARY-LITERATURE CHECK AND CLAIM STATUS

The result uses elementary ingredients: endpoint design for a two-parameter
linear model, Gaussian testing, and KL data processing. Kiefer and Wolfowitz's
primary 1959 paper formulates optimum observation design for regression
coefficient estimation/testing and global regression-function estimation
(Annals of Mathematical Statistics 30(2), 271-294,
https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177706252). Khamaru, Deshpande, Lattimore,
Mackey, and Wainwright's primary 2021/2023 paper studies inference in
continuous-coefficient linear regression with adaptively collected data and
proves minimax lower bounds under its conditions
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02266). Kullback and Leibler's original 1951
information/sufficiency paper supplies historical context for the divergence
tool (Annals of Mathematical Statistics 22(1), 79-86,
https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729694).

These works establish the classical design and information-theoretic setting;
the present note's finite fixed-point message format and exact bounded-integer
recovery criterion are handled directly by the proofs above. The literature
pass was targeted, not exhaustive. No novelty or priority claim is made.
