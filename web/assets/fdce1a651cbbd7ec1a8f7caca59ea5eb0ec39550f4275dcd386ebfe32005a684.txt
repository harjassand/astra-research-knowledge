BORDENAVE–COLLINS EXPECTATION-CONSTANT AUDIT
2026-10-08

Verdict

For the U(N) model and the stated finite parameters, the reconstructed bound
c_E <= 64 exp(5/2) = 779.679613485... < 780 is valid. It is not a constant
printed in either source: it follows after supplying an explicit Gaussian
expansion and tracking the explicit path-count factors. As written, the packet
skips one needed detail: a multiplicity-three coordinate has nonzero terms
once the Gaussian factors are shifted. The bound survives, but the sentence
that its “centered Gaussian moment” vanishes is insufficient by itself.

Source scope and dates

- Bordenave–Collins, “Norm of matrix-valued polynomials in random unitaries
  and permutations,” arXiv:2304.05714v2 (10 Jan 2024), especially Lemma 5.4,
  Corollary 5.5, Theorem 5.1 and its proof, Theorem 1.1, and Lemma 9.3:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2304.05714v2
- Bordenave–Collins, “Strong asymptotic freeness for independent uniform
  variables on compact groups associated to nontrivial representations,”
  arXiv:2012.08759v2 (20 Nov 2022), Theorem 11 and §3.4, Corollary 14:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2012.08759v2

Both papers state several constants only as “numerical” or “universal.” The
number below is an internal reconstruction from the explicit unitary route;
it is not author-printed, externally reviewed, or a novelty claim.

1. One-generator estimate, with the omitted Gaussian step filled

For a generator occurring q times, a nonzero Haar expectation requires equal
numbers of U and U* factors, hence q is even and the sequence is balanced.
Theorem 11 (for N >= 4 and 2q^(7/2) <= N^2) gives exactly

  N^(q/2) |E prod U_(x_t,y_t)^(eps_t)|
    <= (1 + 3 q^(7/2) N^(-2)) E prod (G_(x_t,y_t)^(eps_t) + theta),
  theta = q N^(-1/4).

The expectation on the right factors over distinct coordinate pairs. If a
pair occurs a times without conjugation and b times with conjugation, r=a+b,
its shifted-Gaussian factor is exactly

  F_(a,b)(theta) = sum_(t=0)^min(a,b) binom(a,t) binom(b,t) t!
                    theta^(r-2t).

This formula shows why “multiplicity three has zero centered Gaussian
moment” is not enough: for (a,b)=(2,1), F=2 theta + theta^3. The required
uniform estimate is nevertheless elementary. For r=1, F=theta. For r=2 or 3,
F <= (1+theta)^r (for r=3, 2 theta + theta^3 <= (1+theta)^3). For r>=4,
use t! <= r^(r/2) and binom(a,t)binom(b,t) <= binom(r,2t); summing the even
terms gives F <= r^(r/2)(1+theta)^r. Multiplying over pairs therefore yields

  E prod (G^eps + theta)
    <= theta^(e1) (1+theta)^q q^(m4/2),

where e1 counts pairs of total multiplicity one and m4 is the sum of total
multiplicities at least four. Since (1+theta)^q <= exp(q theta), the
one-generator estimate has no extra numerical multiplier:

  |E prod U^eps| <= (1 + 3 q^(7/2)/N^2) exp(q^2 N^(-1/4))
                    N^(-q/2) theta^(e1) q^(m4/2).

This is a direct completion of the Gaussian expansion behind B–C Lemma 5.4.
It does not use Corollary 14 as a theorem with q=1: Corollary 14 carries its
own hypothesis k^(q+1) <= n^(1/4), which is not the hypothesis needed here,
and its statement itself leaves a universal constant unspecified.

2. From one generator to a path

For a closed colored path of even length m, let q_i be the number of
occurrences of generator i, including inverse letters. Nonzero contributions
have each q_i even; zero counts contribute a factor one. We have sum_i q_i=m,
q_i<=m, and sum_i q_i^2<=m^2. Thus

  prod_i (1 + 3 q_i^(7/2)/N^2)
    <= exp(3 sum_i q_i^(7/2)/N^2)
    <= exp(3 m^(7/2)/N^2) <= exp(3/2),

using the Corollary 5.5 gate 2m^(7/2)<=N^2. The Gaussian shifts multiply to
at most exp(sum_i q_i^2 N^(-1/4)) <= exp(m^2 N^(-1/4)). The remaining
multiplicity term is m^(m4/2). The source graph calculation gives
m4<=4 chi, hence m^(m4/2)<=m^(2 chi). Therefore the Corollary 5.5 prefactor
can be taken to be exp(3/2), with its displayed exp(m eta), eta=mN^(-1/4).

3. Theorem 5.1 path summation

The proof uses explicit factors from Lemmas 5.3, 5.8, and 5.9; their displayed
bounds have coefficient one. Combining them with the reconstructed path bound
produces the class summand

  (2 d m^(d+1))^4 exp(3/2) exp(m eta) ell^11 rho^ell
       [(2 d m^(d+1))^3 Delta_1]^(e1)
       [(2 d m^(d+1))^6 Delta_2]^r,

where Delta_1=eta m sqrt(2d) ell^9, Delta_2=N^(-1)m^2 ell^18, and
r=m/2-v. Theorem 5.1's ell_0 condition makes both bracketed ratios <=1/2;
the two sums over e1,r are each bounded by 2. It also gives
exp(m eta)=exp(m^2 N^(-1/4))<=e. Finally
(2 d m^(d+1))^4=16d^4m^(4d+4). Consequently its trace coefficient is

  4 * e * exp(3/2) * 16 = 64 exp(5/2).

Summing m=1,...,ell for the moment-error line costs no extra constant:
sum m^(4d+4) <= ell^(4d+5), and ell^11 yields ell^(4d+16). The same
coefficient works in Theorem 5.1's second bound. For d=64, ell=p, N=p^1104,
the displayed reduction to c/sqrt(N) is valid because
64^4 p^272 <= p^552 iff 64^4 <= p^280. This check uses the selected p only
to verify a theorem gate; it plays no role in deriving 64 exp(5/2).

4. Transfer to c_E in Lemma 9.3

Theorem 1.1 applies Jensen to the moment estimate. With C=64 exp(5/2),

  E ||A_N||_ell <= rho (1+C/sqrt(N))^(1/ell)
                 <= rho (1+C/(ell sqrt(N)))
                 <= rho (1+C/sqrt(N)),

using (1+x)^(1/ell)<=1+x/ell. Linearization preserves the relevant norms.
Thus the Theorem 1.1 constant can be chosen <=C; the authors' c-prime
notation need not enlarge it. Lemma 9.3 reuses that same upper-bound constant
on each tensor leg, so its factor (1+c_E/sqrt(N))^k is valid with c_E<=C;
for k=2 it is the square used in the packet. For the finite point,
p is even, d=64, N=p^1104, and ell=p; the source hypotheses
p<=N^(1/1104), d^70<=N, and ell<=ell_0 all hold. Also N>=4 and
2q_i^(7/2)<=2p^(7/2)<=N^2 for each q_i<=p, so Theorem 11's separate size
gate is met. These are admissibility checks, not a way of shrinking or
estimating C.

Exact repair needed in the packet

Replace the claim that multiplicity three simply vanishes by the displayed
F_(a,b)(theta) expansion and its bound F_(2,1)<= (1+theta)^3. Also describe
the constant as a reconstructed bound from Theorem 11 plus the explicit
B–C path proof, rather than as something that follows from the printed
numerical-constant statements alone. With that repair, I found no missing
multiplicative factor in c_E<=64 exp(5/2)<780. The packet's finite numerical
threshold remains conditional on its other stated free-score and Bell-quotient
inputs; this audit validates only the expectation-constant chain.
