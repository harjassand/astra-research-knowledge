# Mesoscopic SU(2) Racah contour/saddle attack

**Status: UNKNOWN.** I found an exact residue-contour representation and isolated the expected complex saddle pair in the regime

    n=2j -> infinity, 1<=l<=n, l/n -> 0,
    L=l(l+1), N=n(n+2), z=L/sqrt(N) -> infinity.

The local saddle analysis points toward |eta_l(l)| of order z^(-1/2), hence eta_l(l)->0, but I do not have a valid global contour deformation or a uniform remainder. I found no exact countersequence. This note addresses only the residual sublinear/large-z regime; it does not repeat the fixed-ratio compactness theorem in RACAH_MIDDLE_COMPACTNESS.txt.

## Scalar gate and the target regime

Use the already audited normalization

    eta_nl = (-1)^n (n+1) { n/2  n/2  l ; n/2  n/2  l }
          = sum_(r=0..l) (-1)^r t_r,
    t_r = (n+1)(n-r)!/(n+r+1)! * ((l+r)!/((l-r)! r!))^2,
    t_(r+1)/t_r = (L-r(r+1))^2/((r+1)^2(N-r(r+2))).

The coherent multiplier is

    mu_nl = product_(s=1..l) (d-s)/(d+s),   d=n+1.

Since -log(mu_nl)=2 sum_(s=1..l) artanh(s/d)>=L/d and L/d~z, this regime has mu_nl->0. Thus

    q_nl=(1-eta_nl)/(1-mu_nl)^2 = 1-eta_nl+o(1).

A vanishing q sequence is therefore exactly a sequence with eta_nl->1. It would suffice to prove eta_nl<=1-c uniformly eventually; an envelope |eta_nl|<=C/sqrt(z) would be stronger. No such bound is established here.

## Exact residue contour

For complex s define the meromorphic function

    G_nl(s) = pi csc(pi s) (n+1) Gamma(n-s+1) Gamma(l+s+1)^2
              /[Gamma(n+s+2) Gamma(l-s+1)^2 Gamma(s+1)^2].

Let 0<delta<1 and let C_(l,delta,T) be the positively oriented rectangle with real sides -delta and l+delta and imaginary sides +/-T. The only poles of G inside it are the csc poles s=0,1,...,l: the numerator-gamma poles are at s=n+1+k or s=-l-1-k, outside the rectangle, and reciprocal gamma factors introduce zeros only. At an integer r in [0,l], the residue is (-1)^r t_r. Therefore the following finite-contour identity is exact for every T>0:

    eta_nl = (1/(2 pi i)) integral_(C_(l,delta,T)) G_nl(s) ds.       (1)

This is a residue identity, not yet a useful saddle integral: the original cycle winds around l+1 poles. Any proposed deformation to steepest-descent cycles must preserve those residues and account for poles crossed at the csc lattice and, if the contour is extended far enough, the numerator-gamma pole strings. A direct rectangular contour has no branch ambiguity; steepest descent for log G additionally needs consistent log branches away from all poles and zeros.

The exact logarithmic derivative away from zeros and poles is

    D_nl(s)=G'_nl(s)/G_nl(s)
      = -psi(n-s+1)-psi(n+s+2)
        +2 psi(l+s+1)+2 psi(l-s+1)-2 psi(s+1)-pi cot(pi s),       (2)

where psi is the digamma function. Equation (2) is a useful exact saddle equation, but I have not classified all of its complex roots or their contour intersection numbers.

## Local saddle calculation (not a contour asymptotic theorem)

Put z0=l^2/n. In the target regime z0~z, z0/l~l/n->0, z0/n~(l/n)^2->0, and z0->infinity. Uniform digamma expansions on a fixed relative disk around s=+i z0 give

    D_nl(s) = -2 log(s/(i z0)) + o(1),

because psi(n-s+1)+psi(n+s+2)=2 log n+o(1),
2 psi(l+s+1)+2 psi(l-s+1)=4 log l+o(1),
psi(s+1)=log s+o(1), and cot(pi s)=-i+O(exp(-2 pi Im s)). The errors are uniform on |s-i z0|<=epsilon z0 for fixed 0<epsilon<1; their scale is O(1/l+1/n+1/z0+(z0/l)^2+exp(-2 pi(1-epsilon)z0)). The lower disk gives the conjugate equation with -i pi in place of +i pi.

Since -2 log(s/(i z0)) has one zero in that disk and is bounded away from zero on its boundary, Rouché's theorem gives one exact zero of D_nl near +i z0 and one near -i z0, for all sufficiently advanced members of any such sequence. These are genuine local saddles of G. At this leading level they are the same conjugate pair that produces J_0(2z0): the Hessian scale is |D'|~1/z0, suggesting Gaussian width sqrt(z0) and an individual contribution of order z0^(-1/2).

This calculation does **not** show that the rectangular cycle (1) deforms to just these two saddle cycles. In fact, on each real interval (r,r+1), r=0,...,l-1, the gamma ratio in G is positive and smooth, while |csc(pi s)| diverges at both ends. Thus |G| has at least one interior minimum, so (2) has at least one real stationary point in each such interval. There are already at least l additional real critical points in the pole strip. Their relevance or cancellation after a global deformation is unproved; other complex roots are not ruled out. It would be incorrect to call +/-i z0 the complete saddle inventory.

## Coalescence, turning, and why fixed-ratio formulas do not close the gap

The local complex pair is separated in the target regime: its distance is about 2z0->infinity, and it is far from the real csc poles. Its leading Bessel-model coalescence is instead at z0=0, where the pair reaches the origin/pole; that is outside the requested large-z regime.

There is a distinct coalescence in the exact three-term recoupling recurrence. Its fixed-ratio principal symbol at k/n->t has neighbor-root sum gamma(t)=(6t^2-2)/(1-t^2). As t->0, gamma(t)=-2+4t^2+O(t^4), so the two unit-circle roots merge at -1. The target has t=l/n->0, while the accumulated phase across a k-window of length l is of order l^2/n~z->infinity. A separated-root WKB estimate is not uniform here; a uniform band-edge/Bessel turning analysis would be needed. This is structural diagnosis only, not a reproof of the fixed-ratio theorem.

The existing exact Racah-to-Bessel estimate is

    |eta_nl-J_0(2z)| <= 2(z^2+3z) exp(2z)/(3 sqrt(N)).

It proves the previously admitted logarithmic band but is ineffective uniformly once z is polynomially large; for example along l~n^(3/4), z~n^(1/2), its right side grows exponentially. A direct expansion of the coefficient product around the Bessel terms also has a saddle-scale logarithmic correction of order z^3/L~z^2/n. This need not tend to zero throughout the target regime (it grows along l~n^(3/4)). It may be mainly a phase correction, but treating it as a small error is invalid. A proof would need to retain the full phase, establish the complete steepest-descent cycle decomposition, and bound all residual arcs/pole contributions uniformly in l,n.

The fixed-ratio Ponzano-Regge theorem is not used: its nondegenerate fixed-shape regime does not supply a uniform remainder as l/n->0. Chen, Ismail, and Simeonov derive an exact circle integral for a Racah family and prove a small-index asymptotic under their f=o(sqrt(m)) hypothesis. I do not verify a parameter identification that places this two-equal-l symbol inside that theorem's assumptions; in the natural identification f=l, the target has l/sqrt(n)->infinity, opposite to the small-index scale. Neither source supplies the missing global contour control for (1).

High-precision evaluations along sample power sequences showed oscillatory values with decreasing scale, consistent with but not proving the saddle prediction:

**Decisive falsifiers / next proof obligation.** A sequence of exact pairs with l/n->0, z->infinity and eta_nl->1 would refute the desired uniform gap and force q_nl->0. Conversely, proving a global deformation of (1) with all pole/saddle cycles included and a uniform O(z^(-1/2)) remainder would close this scalar gate in the mesoscopic regime. No such sequence or proof is supplied here. The channel consequence also remains conditional on the prior actual-broadcaster scalar inequality; a scalar failure alone is not an N93 physical counterexample.
