# Cycle 3: pulse-order separation under exact CLE matching

This addendum links to the metastable-action analysis in [FINAL_REPORT.md](FINAL_REPORT.md) and the partial-observation/growth analysis in [CYCLE2_REPORT.md](CYCLE2_REPORT.md). It attacks a different identifiability question: can two jump processes remain indistinguishable by their full-state chemical-Langevin drift and covariance under every allowed intervention, yet become distinguishable by a short controlled pulse word because their hidden phase assigns higher-order jumps differently?

All quantities below are generated from a deliberately constructed finite-state model. They are not cell-fate data or evidence that a biological system has the proposed hidden modes, burst reactions, or parity reporter.

## Model and strict CLE constraint

The observed species are counts (X=(X_1,ldots,X_4)in\mathbb N^4). There is a hidden phase (H\in\mathbb Z_3) with clockwise transitions (h\to h+1\pmod 3) at an unknown rate \(\kappa\), and initial prior (H_0\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,1,2\}). The prepared observed state is (X_0=(0,0,0,0)). The readout only reports the parity
\[
P(X)=\sum_{i=1}^4 X_i\pmod 2,
\]
with independent flip probability \(\epsilon\) at each post-pulse snapshot. (H) is never observed.

There are two pulse actions (A,B). During an action at commanded rate \(\lambda=2r\), a marked Poisson burst occurs, adding one of the fixed stoichiometry vectors (v\in\{0,1\}^4) to (X). The mark law is one of
\[
K_{\rm iid}(v)=1/16\quad(v\in\{0,1\}^4),\qquad
K_{\rm even}(v)=\tfrac18\,\mathbf 1\{\textstyle\sum_i v_i\text{ is even}\}.
\]
The empty mark is a zero-stoichiometry event. It can be removed from the state-changing generator without changing any observation law.

Two hidden-mode mechanisms use those same reaction channels and rates:

- (M_+): for both actions, use \(K_{\rm iid}\) when \(H=0\), and \(K_{\rm even}\) otherwise.
- (M_-\): action (A) uses \(K_{\rm iid}\) only at (H=0\); action (B) uses it only at (H=2\). All other action/mode pairs use \(K_{\rm even}\).

When no pulse is active, (X) is frozen and the hidden phase continues its common cycle. For arbitrary nonnegative, piecewise-constant pulse controls (u_A(t),u_B(t)), the full-state generator is
\[
\mathcal L_m^u f(x,h)=\kappa[f(x,h+1)-f(x,h)]
 +\lambda\sum_{a\in\{A,B\}}u_a(t)\sum_v K^m_{a,h}(v)[f(x+v,h)-f(x,h)].
\]
This is a fixed-stoichiometry jump model on (\mathbb N^4\), with hidden state appended as a separate, common reaction subsystem.

For every proper coordinate subset (I\subsetneq\{1,2,3,4\}), both mark laws have
\[
\mathbb E\prod_{i\in I}v_i=2^{-|I|}.
\]
Therefore, at every (x,h) and separately for either action, both mechanisms have the same chemical-Langevin coefficients
\[
b_i=\lambda u_a/2,\qquad A_{ii}=\lambda u_a/2,\qquad A_{ij}=\lambda u_a/4\quad(i\ne j),
\]
plus the same hidden-phase drift/covariance. In particular, all first-, second-, and third-order jump tensors match. The fourth distinct-coordinate moment does not: \(\mathbb E_{\rm iid}[v_1v_2v_3v_4]=1/16\) and \(\mathbb E_{\rm even}[v_1v_2v_3v_4]=1/8\). Since the action-specific moments match at each hidden state, summing any allowed simultaneous mixture of (A,B) also preserves equality. Thus this construction meets the strict pointwise (b=Sa), (A=S\operatorname{diag}(a)S^\top) equality constraint; the difference is in the jump kernel beyond the third tensor and in how each action is coupled to hidden phase.

## Exact finite-state interface

The parity and hidden phase form a closed six-state CTMC (Z=(H,P)\in\mathbb Z_3\times\mathbb Z_2). Use column generators (Q[destination,source]). During pulse action (a), (h\to h+1) occurs at rate \(\kappa\); parity (p\to1-p) occurs at rate \(r=\lambda/2\) only when that action is in its \(K_{\rm iid}\) phase. Under (K_{\rm even}), parity never changes. The active phases are
\[
M_+:(h_A,h_B)=(0,0),\qquad M_-:(h_A,h_B)=(0,2).
\]
The initial six-vector (\mu) has mass (1/3) on each ((h,0)).

For a two-pulse word (AB) with durations (\tau_A,\tau_B), let (D_p) project onto parity (p). The exact endpoint table is
\[
E_{p_1p_2}=\mathbf1^\top D_{p_2}e^{\tau_BQ_B}D_{p_1}e^{\tau_AQ_A}\mu.
\]
For a gap \(g\), insert (e^{gQ_{off}}) between (D_{p_1}) and (e^{\tau_BQ_B}), where (Q_{off}) has only the common hidden-phase cycle. The latent pulse-increment table is (J_{uv}=E_{u,u\oplus v}): (U=P_1\oplus P_0=P_1), (V=P_2\oplus P_1), and (P_0=0) is known exactly.

With noisy endpoint snapshots (Y_j=P_j\oplus E_j), (E_j\stackrel{iid}{\sim}\mathrm{Bernoulli}(\epsilon)), the observed table is obtained by applying the two binary-symmetric emission matrices to (E). The binary event
\[
Z_{obs}=\mathbf1\{Y_1=1,Y_2=0\}
\]
is precisely the event that both increment estimates \(\widehat U=Y_1\), \(\widehat V=Y_1\oplus Y_2\) equal 1. It is an observable even though the pulse increments themselves are not observed without error.

## Minimum word and robust separation theorem

At length zero, the observed species are frozen, so passive data agree exactly. A one-letter (A) word has the same law in both mechanisms. A one-letter (B) word also has the same (X)-only law: shifting all hidden phases by two maps its active phase 0 to phase 2, and the initial hidden prior and cycle generator are rotation-invariant. The same conjugacy proves that (AA) and (BB) do not separate the mechanisms. A mixed two-letter word is therefore minimal in pulse count.

At \(\kappa=0\), write
\[
q(\tau)=\Pr(\text{odd number of parity toggles in an active pulse})=\frac{1-e^{-2r\tau}}2.
\]
For equal durations, (M_+) has \(\Pr(U=V=1)=q^2/3\), since both actions are active exactly when the fixed hidden phase is 0. Under (M_-\), actions (A) and (B) are active in disjoint phases, so \(\Pr(U=V=1)=0\). One-pulse increment marginals are equal. With (P_0) known exactly and endpoint error \(\epsilon\), the (Z_{obs}) probability gap is ((1-2\epsilon)q^2/3).

A continuous unknown-rate certificate was computed for \(\tau_A=\tau_B=0.38\), (r=1) (hence commanded \(\lambda=2\)), \(\epsilon=0.08), and unknown clockwise switching rate \(\kappa\in[0,3]\). A grid of 30,001 values with spacing (h=10^{-4}) gave
\[
\min_{grid}p_+(Z_{obs})=0.07729737177,\quad
\max_{grid}p_-(Z_{obs})=0.07214662552.
\]
For either model, Duhamel's formula and the column-generator induced 1-norm give
\[
\left|\frac{d}{d\kappa}p_m(Z_{obs})\right|\le4\tau=1.52,
\]
because \(\|\partial_\kappa Q\|_1=2\), there are two pulse semigroups, and the projection/emission maps are stochastic contractions. Every point is within (h/2) of a grid point. Thus, continuously on the full interval,
\[
p_+(Z_{obs})\ge0.07722137177,\quad
p_-(Z_{obs})\le0.07222262552.
\]
The probability ranges have certified gap at least (\Delta=0.00499874625), with midpoint threshold (0.07472199865). This is the lead quantitative lemma: for the stated observation model and calibration, an unknown switching rate anywhere in the compact interval is not a nuisance that needs point estimation before classification.

For independent replicate wells, use \(\widehat p_N=N^{-1}\sum_i Z_{obs,i}\). Hoeffding's inequality gives, under either mechanism,
\[
\Pr(\text{threshold misclassification})\le e^{-N\Delta^2/2}.
\]
Thus (N\ge\lceil2\log(1/\delta)/\Delta^2\rceil) suffices. At \(\delta=0.05\), the certified sufficient size is 239,779 independent AB wells. This is a conservative one-bit bound, not a lower bound or an assertion of practical feasibility; the full four-category snapshot likelihood can be more efficient. To make the finite-data rule concrete, a seeded synthetic binomial run at \(\kappa=1\) produced 19,422 event-positive wells under \(M_+\) (frequency 0.0809996) and 16,251 under \(M_-\) (frequency 0.0677749); the fixed threshold classified both correctly. These are simulated outcomes generated from the model, not laboratory observations.

### Actuation and reporter uncertainty debit

The nominal (N=239{,}779) bound assumes (r=1), (epsilon=0.08), and commanded durations are exact. To charge finite control and observation calibration uncertainty, suppose separately verified bounds are supplied:
\[
|\widetilde\tau_a-0.38|\le10^{-5},\quad |r_a-1|\le10^{-4}\ (a=A,B),\quad |\widetilde\epsilon-0.08|\le10^{-4}.
\]
These tolerances are assumptions for this calculation, not measured calibrations. For \(\kappa\le3\), the column-generator norm obeys \(\|Q_a\|_1\le2(3+1.0001)\); Duhamel bounds each event-probability perturbation by
\[
B=2(3+1.0001)(2\cdot10^{-5})+2(0.38001)(2\cdot10^{-4})+2\cdot10^{-4}=0.000512008.
\]
The last term uses \(|\partial_\epsilon p(Z)|\le2\). Debit both mechanism ranges by \(B\), giving robust plus lower bound 0.076709364, robust minus upper bound 0.072734634, and robust gap \(\Delta_{rob}=0.00397473025\). The midpoint threshold remains 0.074721999 under these symmetric bounds. The 5% sufficient sample size becomes 379,244 independent AB wells. The robust seeded synthetic draws at \(\kappa=1\) give 30,794 event-positive wells under \(M_+\) (frequency 0.0811984) and 25,773 under \(M_-\) (frequency 0.0679589), correctly on opposite sides of the same threshold. Again, these are simulated counts.

The controller's declared per-well resource debit is
\[
C_{well}=c_{culture}+c_{verified\ start}+2c_{snapshot}
 +\lambda_{max}(\tau_{A,max}+\tau_{B,max})c_{dose/unit}+c_{handling},
\]
with \(C_{total}=N C_{well}+C_{reporter\ calibration}+C_{actuation\ calibration}\). The pulse exposure is \(\lambda\tau=0.76\) event opportunities per action and 1.52 total per AB well. At the robust sample size this is 379,244 (A) pulses, 379,244 (B) pulses, and 758,488 endpoint snapshots. At worst-case calibrated durations/rates it uses 0.760096 event opportunities per action per well, 1.520192 total per AB well, and 576,523.70 across all wells. Initial-state verification and reporter/actuation calibration are charged separately. No currency or empirical biological cost is assigned. The high count is a conservative one-bit bound; full-likelihood design may reduce it.

## Sequential adaptive design and abstention

A finite acquisition wrapper can use the exact four-category endpoint law rather than only (Z_{obs}). Let the allowed word menu be (\mathcal W=\{AB,BA\}\times\{0.25,0.38,0.50\}). For each word, evaluate the six-state matrix-exponential likelihood over the nuisance set; the certificate allows \(\kappa\in[0,3]\) and the bounded duration, rate, and reporter-error intervals above. Each batch consists of independently randomized founder wells, each assigned exactly one word before treatment.

At stage (t), choose a word maximizing
\[
\frac{\inf_{\theta_+\in C_+,\theta_-\in C_-}
  \mathrm{TV}(p_{+,\theta_+}(w),p_{-,\theta_-}(w))}{C_{well}(w)},
\]
where (C_m) is the current parameter confidence set. After (n_t) wells on that word, let \(\widehat p_t\) be its four-category frequency vector and use the coordinate radius
\[
r_t=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2\cdot4/\delta_t)}{2n_t}},\qquad
\delta_t=\frac{\delta}{t(t+1)}.
\]
Retain \((m,\theta)\) only if every observed batch's predicted category probabilities lie within its corresponding coordinate radius of that batch's frequencies. For continuous nuisance intervals, use interval subdivision; inflate each grid cell's category radius by its half-width times the Duhamel derivative bound 2*(tau_A+tau_B) for hidden-rate variation, with corresponding bounds for pulse rates, durations, and reporter error. Conditional Hoeffding plus \(\sum_{t\ge1}\delta_t=\delta\) keeps the true mechanism/nuisance pair in the confidence set with probability at least (1-\delta), even though the next word is chosen adaptively from earlier batches. Stop and choose the only surviving mechanism; if both survive at the resource cap, if both are empty, or if all surviving word-separations are zero, return `UNKNOWN`.

This is adaptive experiment acquisition across replicate wells. The within-well separating intervention is a preassigned fixed (AB) word. No feedback-optimal claim is made about choosing (B) after seeing (Y_1) in the same well. Such a policy must propagate the noisy observation through the hidden-mode belief state and recompute the action likelihood; it is outside this report's theorem. Pulse-rate, duration, and reporter-error uncertainty must be included in \(\theta\); the bounded intervals in the preceding subsection preserve a positive range gap, while wider calibration intervals may close it and force `UNKNOWN`.

## A necessary high-switching abstention

There is no uniform finite-sample guarantee over unbounded \(\kappa\) for a fixed finite menu of pulse words whose durations are bounded away from zero and whose dose rates are bounded. As \(\kappa\to\infty\), the hidden phase mixes between action events. For either action, its fast-switching averaged mark law is
\[
\bar K_a=\tfrac13K_{iid}+\tfrac23K_{even},
\]
under both (M_+) and (M_-), since each action is in its (K_{iid}) phase for one third of the hidden cycle. The controlled averaged (X)-generator is consequently identical for the two mechanisms under every fixed finite pulse schedule. For a finite horizon, the potential burst count is Poisson with mean bounded by (\lambda T), uniformly in (\kappa). Truncate that count at (K), paying its common Poisson tail; conditional on at most (K) distinct event times, the hidden-phase transition kernel between events converges to the uniform projector as (\kappa\to\infty). The finite mark-sequence laws therefore converge to the same iid action-averaged law. Letting (K\to\infty) shows total-variation convergence for any finite collection of endpoint snapshots, including after the noisy emission channel. The exact six-state calculation confirms this: for (AB,\tau=0.38,r=1,\epsilon=.08), TV is 0.01030 at \(\kappa=3\), 0.001064 at 10, 0.0001226 at 30, and (1.12\times10^{-5}) at 100. Accordingly, if the rate confidence set reaches the fast-switching regime, the finite-menu controller must return `UNKNOWN` rather than force a mechanism choice. This does not rule out rate-dependent durations or dose rates that scale with \(\kappa\); that would be a separate cost-sensitive control problem and is not established here.

## Literature boundary

The chemical Langevin equation is an approximation to the chemical master equation under its stated time-scale and population conditions; equal (b,A) is not equality of the jump generator. Gillespie's primary CLE paper is the relevant approximation boundary: [J. Chem. Phys. 113 (2000), 297–306](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.481811).

Pulse and input selection for discriminating competing biochemical models is established prior art. Bandara et al. optimize initial conditions, stimulus inputs, and structural perturbations for model separation, while noting that their SOS objectives do not directly incorporate measurement noise: [BMC Systems Biology 4 (2010), 38](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2873315/). Skanda and Lebiedz optimize measurement times, initial conditions, and perturbations for deterministic biochemical ODE discrimination: [Bioinformatics 26 (2010), 939–945](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btq074). Vanlier et al. use predictive distributions and Jensen–Shannon divergence for Bayesian experiment design in biochemical networks: [BMC Systems Biology 8 (2014), 20](https://doi.org/10.1186/1752-0509-8-20). Those works rule out novelty claims for generic pulse selection or adaptive experiment design.

The narrower candidate contribution is the explicit counterexample satisfying pointwise equality of the full-state CLE drift/covariance (indeed, matching third jump moments too), plus a minimum two-action word and a finite-noise, bounded-hidden-rate separation certificate. We did not find a primary source in this bounded search that states this exact construction or threshold bound. Novelty remains `UNKNOWN`; this is not a comprehensive search. It does not outperform landscape-control methods at steering a calibrated biological landscape. OLAC and the 2025 landscape-control method from Cycle 1 optimize model-derived transition actions/occupancies; the present construction instead shows that a diffusion-level landscape can be insufficient to identify the controlled jump mechanism. The scopes are complementary, and no benchmark was run.

## Biological falsifier and boundary

A prospective falsification would require a system in which (i) four count reporters can be initialized at the stated parity, (ii) two interventions act on a hidden three-phase process, (iii) endpoint parity error and pulse rates are calibrated, and (iv) replicate founder wells are independent. At the declared pulse duration/dose, the nominal model predicts (p(Z_{obs})\ge0.07722) for (M_+) and (p(Z_{obs})\le0.07223) for (M_-), provided \(\kappa\le3\); under the stated pulse and reporter calibration tolerances the bounds are 0.07671 and 0.07273. With enough independent wells, a confidence interval wholly in the gap rejects both models; a rate outside the certified range, a reporter-error interval that closes the gap, or inadequate well budget forces `UNKNOWN`. A held-out AB/BA reversal and single-action controls test the claimed phase association. No biological species, actual drugs, cell lines, fate labels, lineage trajectories, or empirical values for \(\lambda,\kappa,\epsilon\) were supplied. Thus biological validity, intervention safety, and actionable cell-fate control remain `UNKNOWN`.
