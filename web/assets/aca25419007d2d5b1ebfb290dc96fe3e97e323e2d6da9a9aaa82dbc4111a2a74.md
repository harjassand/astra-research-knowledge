# Cycle 2: one-lag identifiability of switching and net growth

**Status:** the unrestricted one-lag identification claim is false for (K\ge3), even with all (K) pure starts, exact fractions, exact absolute totals, and strictly positive switching rates. A conditional recovery theorem, finite-τ error bounds, a bounded-noise sample planner, and a robust finite-action reach–avoid rule are derived and executable below. The construction is a special case of the classical Markov embedding problem; the proposed inference/control machinery is standard in substance. No biological data were analyzed. Novelty/priority is **not claimed**.

This report follows the earlier endpoint/path-reward result in [FINAL_REPORT.md](FINAL_REPORT.md). That earlier result concerns two-state path-reward aliases; this cycle concerns multi-state switching/growth separation from one endpoint matrix.

## Interface and observation contract

For one fixed treatment, let expected phenotype counts follow

\[
\dot x(t)=A x(t),\qquad A=Q+\operatorname{diag}(r),\qquad
Q_{ji}=q_{i\to j}\ (j\ne i),\quad Q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{i\to j}.
\]

Thus (Q) is a column-generator and (r_i) is the net division-minus-death rate. Birth and death separately are outside this mean-count interface. From pure inoculation (e_i), the expected normalized state-count column at time τ is

\[
M(\tau)e_i=e^{A\tau}e_i.
\]

If the culture readout gives fractions (p_{ji}(\tau)) and total fold change (s_i(\tau)=N_i(\tau)/N_i(0)), then the corresponding state-count column is (M_{ji}=s_i p_{ji}). With random cultures, form this product **within each independent replicate** and average it; multiplying a separately averaged fraction by a separately averaged total need not be unbiased because the two measurements can be dependent. The independent unit is the randomized well/founder inoculation, not the descendants within a well.

The supplied quantities in the executable design are (K), treatment/horizon, a bound (B\ge\|A\|_1), desired rate precision, confidence level, an observation support/tail assumption, a finite action set, and costs. Acquired quantities are the (K) verified pure starts per action; independent replicates; all (K) fractions and absolute totals at the selected time; and reporter calibration if state assignment is imperfect. The script charges every action–start–replicate for culture, pure-start sorting/verification, an all-state fraction panel, and an absolute-count read, plus a one-time calibration cost. It does not count descendants as independent samples.

If starts have known mixture matrix (W), recovering (M) instead requires τ-end count matrix (MW) and — at minimum — full column rank of (W); error is amplified by the conditioning of (W^{-1}). Pure starts set (W=I). If the assay has known confusion matrix (C), use (C^{-1}) on state counts when it is invertible and charge calibration; the error bound is multiplied by at most \(\|C^{-1}\|_1\). A rank-deficient or unknown reporter leaves additional aliases.

## Exact identifiability result and its failure

Once (M(\tau)) is known, (K) pure starts reveal every column. Under a no-alias condition, the rates follow from a matrix logarithm:

\[
A=\tau^{-1}\operatorname{Log}M(\tau),\qquad
q_{i\to j}=A_{ji}\ (j\ne i),\qquad
r_i=\sum_j A_{ji}.
\]

Here Log is the principal matrix logarithm. For example, the sufficient bound \(\tau\|A\|_1<\pi\) puts the spectrum of \(\tau A\) strictly inside the principal-log strip, so \(\operatorname{Log}(e^{\tau A})=\tau A\). With \(|r_i|\le R\) and outgoing switching sum \(\sum_{j\ne i}q_{i\to j}\le\Lambda\), a directly checkable sufficient bound is \(B=R+2\Lambda\), since

\[
\|A\|_1=\max_i\left(|r_i-\lambda_i|+\lambda_i\right)\le R+2\Lambda.
\]

For (K=2), this no-alias condition is unnecessary for exact mathematical identification from (M(\tau)): every admissible (2\times2) Metzler matrix has real eigenvalues, and the real exponential is one-to-one on those eigenvalues; the repeated-eigenvalue/Jordan case is also uniquely recovered by the principal log. For (K\ge3), unrestricted one-lag identification fails.

**Three-state alias.** Let clockwise and counterclockwise rates be (u,v>0) on the directed three-cycle, with (r=0). The generator has eigenvalues

\[
0,\qquad -\tfrac32(u+v)\pm i\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}(u-v).
\]

Keeping (s=u+v) fixed and replacing (d=u-v) by \(d+4\pi/(\sqrt3\tau)\) shifts the two nonreal eigenvalues by \(\pm2\pi i/\tau\). The cycle generators share Fourier eigenvectors, so their exponentials at τ are exactly equal. Whenever both rate pairs remain positive this gives distinct valid irreducible generators with the same (M(\tau)).

At τ=1, (s=10,d=0) gives ((u,v)=(5,5)). The shifted pair is 
\((u',v')=(8.6275987285,1.3724012715)). Both have positive rates and their endpoint matrices agree up to (2.4\times10^{-15}) in the executable check, while their generators differ by (3.63) in max norm. Every pure-start fraction and total is identical. Therefore a theorem claiming all (K(K-1)) switching rates and all (K) net-growth rates are globally identified from one unrestricted finite lag is refuted.

For (K>3), a block-diagonal embedding of this example gives the same counterexample on the full allowed class. The (K=3) example itself is irreducible. A stronger claim restricted to irreducible models at every larger (K), or to a biological rate box that excludes all branch aliases, needs its own proof; it is not used here.

**Why (K) starts are necessary in the worst case.** If one pure source state (k) is omitted, choose a model where no started state can reach (k), then vary any rates leaving (k). All measured endpoint columns are unchanged at every time. Thus (K) linearly independent initial conditions are necessary for a uniform all-rates guarantee. This does not mean pure starts are uniquely optimal: any known full-rank mixture design can supply the same (K)-dimensional input span, with its conditioning charged.

**Why absolute totals are needed.** For two states with only (0\to1), at τ=1 compare model A (q_{0\to1}=1,r=(0,0)) with model B (q'_{0\to1}=e-1,r'=(e-2,-1)). From start 0 both have endpoint expected counts \((e^{-1},1-e^{-1})\), and from start 1 both have fraction entirely in state 1. Hence all pure-start fractions agree although the switching rate differs. The absolute total from start 1 is 1 in A and \(e^{-1}\) in B, separating these two models. More generally fractions alone discard each endpoint column scale. Even with absolute totals, only net rates (r_i=b_i-d_i) can be inferred from the mean equation: replacing ((b_i,d_i)) by ((b_i+c,d_i+c)) leaves (r_i) unchanged.

## Finite-τ estimator and error

Write

\[
e^{A\tau}=I+\tau A+E_\tau,
\qquad \|E_\tau\|_1\le R_\tau:=e^{B\tau}-1-B\tau.
\]

For an endpoint estimate \(\widehat M\) with entrywise error at most \(\epsilon_M\), the simple estimator \(\widehat A=(\widehat M-I)/\tau\) obeys

\[
\max_{i\ne j}|\widehat q_{i\to j}-q_{i\to j}|
\le \frac{\epsilon_M+R_\tau}{\tau},\qquad
\max_i|\widehat r_i-r_i|
\le \frac{K\epsilon_M+R_\tau}{\tau}.
\]

The first bound is finite-τ truncation plus measurement error; as τ→0 the truncation part is at most \(B^2\tau/2+O(B^3\tau^2)\), while measurement error is amplified as (1/\tau). For two candidate models whose off-diagonal rate vectors differ by sup norm Δ, the maximum difference of their endpoint matrix entries is at least \(\tau\Delta-2R_\tau\). With independent endpoint error at most \(\epsilon_M\) per model, this is a robust distinction only when \(\tau\Delta>2(R_\tau+\epsilon_M)\). This is a local finite-lag guarantee; it does not remove exact aliases.

If using the log estimator, put \(\alpha=e^{B\tau}-1\) and suppose \(\alpha+\eta<1\), where \(\|\widehat M-M\|_1\le\eta\). A series perturbation bound gives

\[
\left\|\frac{\log\widehat M}{\tau}-A\right\|_1
\le \frac{\eta}{\tau(1-\alpha-\eta)}.
\]

This follows by expanding \(\log(I+X)=\sum_{m\ge1}(-1)^{m+1}X^m/m\), using \(\|M-I\|_1\le\alpha\), \(\|\widehat M-M\|_1\le\eta\), and \(\|X^m-Y^m\|_1\le m\rho^{m-1}\|X-Y\|_1\) for \(\rho=\alpha+\eta<1\). It is a sufficient near-identity perturbation certificate, stricter than the exact \(B\tau<\pi\) no-alias condition.

## Sampling, noise, precision, and cost

For each treatment action (a\in\{1,\ldots,A_0\}), pure start (i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}), and replicate well (m\), define \(Y^{a,i,m}_j=N^{a,i,m}_j(\tau)/N^{a,i,m}(0)\), which has expectation (M^a_{ji}(\tau)\). Assume the wells are independent and each coordinate is unbiased with known support \([0,C]\). Then for (n) independent wells per action and pure start, Hoeffding plus a union bound gives

\[
\Pr\!\left(\max_{a,i,j}|\widehat M^a_{ji}-M^a_{ji}|>\epsilon_M\right)
\le 2A_0K^2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2n\epsilon_M^2}{C^2}\right).
\]

For target off-diagonal rate error εq, set \(\epsilon_M=\tau\epsilon_q-R_\tau\); a finite design exists only when this is positive. The sufficient replicate count is

\[
n=\left\lceil\frac{C^2}{2(\tau\epsilon_q-R_\tau)^2}
\log\frac{2A_0K^2}{\delta}\right\rceil.
\]

The script searches a supplied finite grid of τ values and chooses the least charged cost among points meeting this error target. Its cost is \(A_0 K n\) independent wells, each charged for treatment/culture, pure-start sorting or verification, one panel containing all (K) fractions, and the absolute-total assay, plus the specified one-time calibration cost. In the included illustrative settings (K=3,A_0=2,B=2,\epsilon_q=0.4,C=1.5,\delta=0.05), a 100-point grid on ([0.005,0.2]) selects τ=0.09167 and requires 20,975 wells per action and pure start (125,850 total); estimated cost is 503,405 units under the explicitly supplied per-well cost vector. This large Hoeffding count is a conservative accounting result for that chosen precision/support bound, not a biological recommendation.

**Noise limitation:** the expected-count ODE does not define the sampling distribution. The sample guarantee additionally assumes independent wells and bounded unbiased normalized count coordinates. A clipping/saturation assay adds a bias term unless the clipping probability is bounded; unbounded branching counts require a variance/tail model and a Bernstein or likelihood bound instead. The script reports its [0,C] assumption and does not silently treat descendant cells as independent. Under a calibrated reporter confusion matrix, replace εM by the observed error multiplied by \(\|C^{-1}\|_1\), and charge for calibration. Costs for sorting, culture, treatment, reporter calibration, all-state fraction assay, absolute counts, and technical/batch replication must be measured locally; the example uses declared synthetic cost weights.

## Finite-action robust reach–avoid rule

For a state-level event, let (T) be target states, (U) unsafe states, μ the initial distribution, and (H) the horizon. Under action (a), make (T\cup U) absorbing in (Q_a), obtaining (Q_a^{\rm abs}). Then

\[
V_a(Q_a)=\sum_{j\in T}[e^{Q_a^{\rm abs}H}\mu]_j
\]

is the probability of reaching (T) before (U) by (H). If every off-diagonal (q_{i\to j}) lies within εq of an estimated nonnegative rate vector, Duhamel's formula yields

\[
|V_a(Q)-V_a(\widehat Q)|\le H\|Q^{\rm abs}-\widehat Q^{\rm abs}\|_1
\le 2H(K-1)\epsilon_q.
\]

For a finite action set, compute (L_a=\max(0,V_a(\widehat Q)-2H(K-1)\epsilon_q)) and (U_a=\min(1,V_a(\widehat Q)+2H(K-1)\epsilon_q)). Choose the maximin action \(\arg\max_a L_a\); certify a unique best action only if its lower bound exceeds every competitor's upper bound, and certify a target threshold θ only if the selected action's lower bound is at least θ. Otherwise report `UNKNOWN` for a threshold guarantee (or report the maximin choice without claiming it is uniquely optimal). If the rate confidence region is not a coordinate box, use a certified optimizer or return `UNKNOWN`; do not evaluate only arbitrary samples and call that a robust bound.

The executable illustration reports action B as maximin, but its intervals overlap and its lower bound is below 0.5, so it certifies neither unique optimality nor a 0.5 target guarantee. This is the intended conservative behavior. The calculation is for an individual cell-state CTMC. A population-level reach/avoid objective involving division, death, clone size, or extinction requires a lineage branching model and observation/censoring likelihood; the first-moment equation alone cannot determine those event probabilities.

The saved [CYCLE2_RUN.json](CYCLE2_RUN.json) verifies numerically: the two positive three-cycle generators have endpoint matrices equal to roundoff; omitting a source that is unreachable from the starts hides its outgoing rate changes; the actual short-τ linear-estimator error lies below the analytic remainder bound; the near-identity matrix-log reconstruction has max error below (3\times10^{-14}); and the grid/sample/cost and reach–avoid routines return the values stated above. These are finite numerical checks, not proof of code correctness for arbitrary matrices or biological validity.

Mathematical no-go falsifier: the cycle pair is a direct analytic counterexample; it is not an empirical hypothesis. Conditional identification applies only if the supplied rate bound truly rules out branch aliases (or another injectivity condition is proved). A biological model is falsified if independently measured times reject semigroup consistency \(M(t+s)=M(t)M(s)\) beyond calibrated uncertainty, if no admissible bounded generator fits all pure-start columns, or if direct lineage data show history/age-dependent switching, state-label confusion, impure starts, time-varying rates, or growth/censoring behavior outside the stated process. Independent cell counts within a shared well are not a valid replication substitute.

## Prior art and strongest controls

The direct literature comparison is adverse to broad novelty claims:

- The **Markov embedding problem** is the exact mathematical issue here: decide whether a discrete-time matrix is (e^{\tau A}), and list or distinguish admissible generators. The three-state branch alias is a special, easily calculated example, not a new phenomenon. See Baake and Sumner, [“The embedding problem for Markov matrices”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00818), and the later classification work, [“Embedding of Markov matrices for (d\le4)”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11607033/).
- **CellTrans** explicitly requires a full-rank starting composition matrix (pure starts are its default), infers transition probabilities from proportion trajectories, and focuses on similar/equal proliferation/death; it notes that unequal rates require an extended model. Its input setup and initial-matrix rank requirement already cover the basic need for (K) independent starts. [Buder et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1177932217712241).
- **Transcompp** fits state transitions and optional phenotype-specific proliferation from multi-time single-cell or bulk fraction snapshots, across flexible initial compositions and replicates, with uncertainty resampling. It is a stronger multi-time population-snapshot comparator than the one-lag design. [Jagannathan et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa021).
- **Gunnarsson, Foo, and Leder** model stochastic division, death, and switching; provide likelihood-based confidence intervals; and treat fractions or absolute cell counts across one or more times. Their analysis directly covers rate separation and the fact that number data can identify net birth while division/death separation needs richer stochastic data. This is the closest prior art to the biological target, and it makes a generic “estimate switching and net growth” claim untenable. The specific globally unrestricted one-lag matrix alias above is not presented here as a theorem from their paper. [Gunnarsson et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtbi.2023.111497).
- **KCA** infers transition dynamics from state correlations on lineage pedigrees using endpoint single-cell labels; the paper uses pair correlations for reversible networks and higher-order correlations for irreversible structure. **Tree-HMM** methods infer hidden-state switching while using genealogy and division/censoring structure. They are much richer observation channels than bulk endpoint proportions. [Hormoz et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.015); [lineage tree HMM 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-022-04208-9).
- **Active timepoint selection** is also established: Huynh and van der Schaar (ICML 2026) select measurement times for learning measure-valued trajectories under sparse destructive measurements. The finite-grid τ selection here is only an elementary Hoeffding-cost variant and is not claimed novel. [PMLR 306 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/huynh26a.html).

The strongest equal-budget control for the proposed observation design is to spend the same (A_0Kn) independent cultures and the same fraction/absolute-count panel cost on multiple/adaptively selected lags, or to use lineage-resolved observation with the same founder and imaging budget where available. A one-lag full-rank endpoint matrix cannot defeat exact embedding aliases; additional lags, sufficiently short bounded-rate observations, or lineage pedigrees supply the additional information. No controlled biological head-to-head was run, so practical superiority is **UNKNOWN**. The first report's endpoint/path-reward baseline remains at [FINAL_REPORT.md](FINAL_REPORT.md).

## Claim disposition

The claim that one finite endpoint lag plus all (K) pure starts always identifies (Q) and (r) is **REFUTED** for (K\ge3). (K) starts are necessary in the worst case and sufficient under an explicit principal-log injectivity/rate bound. Finite-τ bias, bounded-noise sample counts, the cost interface, and confidence-set reach–avoid enclosures are mathematically derived under their stated assumptions and finite-checked. Fraction-only ambiguity and birth/death confounding are explicit. No biological validity, empirical efficiency, broad novelty, or one-lag unbounded-rate identification is claimed.
