CURIE–WEISS ISING: CRITICAL FLUCTUATIONS AND NONUNIFORM GAUSSIAN LIMITS
8 October 2026

STATUS

The scalar Curie–Weiss calculation is an established theorem, not a new result. The finite-volume law and thermodynamic pressure are exact. At the critical point the total magnetization has scale N^(3/4) and a quartic, non-Gaussian limit; at zero-field coexistence the finite-volume law is a symmetric mixture of two phases. The ordinary one-phase Gaussian approximation fails uniformly at criticality and fails globally at coexistence. Gaussian fluctuations remain valid after conditioning on one stable phase.

I independently derive below a compact-parameter finite-size window with both temperature and field, including uniform total-variation convergence after lattice jitter. Existing primary results cover the zero-field critical law and the temperature crossover; Verbeure–Zagrebnov study field and Fourier-mode variants. I found no basis for a novelty claim. The result is only a continuum limit for one rescaled order parameter in an all-to-all model; it is not a spatial continuum limit or a theorem for finite-range Ising systems.

1. MODEL, INPUTS, AND EXACT FINITE-N LAW

For N spins sigma_i in {-1,+1}, let M_N=sum_i sigma_i and take the dimensionless Gibbs measure

    P_(N,beta,h)(sigma) = Z_(N,beta,h)^(-1)
      exp[ beta M_N^2/(2N) + h M_N ].

Equivalently, the pair interaction is beta/N times sum_(i<j) sigma_i sigma_j, up to the constant beta/2. The supplied inputs are N, beta, h, and this complete-graph interaction. No samples, parameter-learning oracle, spatial geometry, or unknown interaction coefficients are acquired. The total magnetization is sufficient for the Gibbs weight. For M in {-N,-N+2,...,N},

    P(M_N=M) = Z^(-1) binom(N,(N+M)/2)
                   exp[ beta M^2/(2N) + hM ],
    Z = sum_(M=-N,-N+2,...,N) binom(N,(N+M)/2)
                   exp[ beta M^2/(2N) + hM ].                         (1)

Thus an exact 2^N-configuration sum reduces to N+1 positive terms.

2. THERMODYNAMIC LIMIT

Set m=M/N and

    I(m) = 1/2[(1+m)log(1+m)+(1-m)log(1-m)],  -1<=m<=1,
    s(m) = log 2 - I(m).

For every fixed beta>=0 and h in R, Stirling's formula and the N+1-term sum give

    lim_(N->infinity) N^(-1)log Z_(N,beta,h)
       = sup_(|m|<=1){log 2 - I(m) + beta m^2/2 + hm}.              (2)

The magnetization density m_N=M_N/N concentrates on the maximizers in (2); the large-deviation rate is the displayed variational function subtracted from its maximum. Interior stationary points solve

    atanh(m) = beta m + h, equivalently m=tanh(beta m+h).           (3)

At h=0 there is one maximizer m=0 for beta<=1. For beta>1 there are two symmetry-related maximizers +/-m_beta, where m_beta is the unique positive solution of m=tanh(beta m). At beta=1, m=0 remains the only thermodynamic maximizer, but the quadratic curvature vanishes. Near zero,

    I(m) = m^2/2 + m^4/12 + m^6/30 + O(m^8),
    I(m)-m^2/2 = m^4/12 + O(m^6).                                 (4)

Balancing N m^4 at order one gives m_N of order N^(-1/4), hence M_N of order N^(3/4). This derivation supplies the thermodynamic limit and the finite-size scale from the same exact entropy function.

3. CRITICAL FINITE-SIZE LAW, INCLUDING A TEMPERATURE AND FIELD WINDOW

Fix any compact K subset R^2. For (a,b) in K, set

    beta_N = 1 + a/sqrt(N),   h_N = b/N^(3/4),
    X_N = M_N/N^(3/4),
    Delta_N = 2/N^(3/4).

Let U_N be independent and uniform on [-Delta_N/2,Delta_N/2]. Define nu_(a,b) on R by the normalized Lebesgue density

    p_(a,b)(x) = C_(a,b)^(-1)
       exp[-x^4/12 + a x^2/2 + b x],
    C_(a,b) = integral_R exp[-x^4/12 + a x^2/2 + b x] dx.          (5)

Claim, derived here: for every fixed compact K,

    sup_(a,b in K) d_TV( Law_(N,beta_N,h_N)(X_N+U_N), nu_(a,b) ) -> 0.
                                                                         (6)

All moments of fixed finite order also converge uniformly on K. This uniform-in-(a,b) jittered-TV statement is internally derived here; it is stronger in metric and parameter uniformity than the b=0 distributional limits I inspected in the primary literature. No rate for (6) is claimed.

Proof. Write m=M/N=x N^(-1/4). The exact unnormalized mass is the binomial coefficient in (1) times the exponential factor. Uniformly for |x|<=R, (a,b) in K, Stirling gives

    log binom(N,N(1+m)/2)
      = N log 2 + (1/2)log(2/(pi N)) - N I(m)
          - (1/2)log(1-m^2) + O_R(N^(-1)).

Combining with beta_N M^2/(2N)+h_N M and (4), the parameter-dependent part of the log mass is

    a x^2/2 + b x - x^4/12 + O_(K,R)(N^(-1/2)).                  (7)

The magnetization lattice has spacing Delta_N in x. On bounded intervals, (7) and Riemann sums give the density in (5), uniformly over K. For global control, the even-power series for I yields I(m)>=m^2/2+m^4/12 on [-1,1]. The residual exponent is therefore bounded above by

    -x^4/12 + (sup_K |a|)x^2/2 + (sup_K |b|)|x|.

Stirling's prefactor is uniformly bounded in an interior interval |m|<=m_0<1; outside that interval the entropy gap is order N and dominates the O(sqrt(N)) temperature perturbation and O(N^(1/4)) field term. A lower bound on the partition sum from |x|<=1 then gives uniform integrable quartic tails, including every fixed polynomial moment. Compact convergence plus these tails proves (6) and moment convergence. The jitter cells have exactly the lattice spacing; the omitted tails beyond the two finite-N endpoints vanish under the same estimate. 

For b=0 and a=0, (5) is the classical critical law p(x) proportional to exp(-x^4/12). For fixed a, b=0, it is the crossover law exp(-x^4/12+a x^2/2). The sign a>0 gives a two-peaked finite-size profile; it does not yet represent two macroscopically separated phases because M/N still tends to zero in this N^(-1/2) window.

4. VARIANCE, SUSCEPTIBILITY, AND THE FAILED GAUSSIAN SCALE

At beta=1,h=0, let X have density proportional to exp(-x^4/12). Direct gamma integrals give

    E[X^(2r)] = 12^(r/2) Gamma((2r+1)/4)/Gamma(1/4),
    C2 := Var(X) = E[X^2]
       = sqrt(12) Gamma(3/4)/Gamma(1/4)
       = 1.170828656607529...,
    E[X^4] = 3,  E[X^4]/E[X^2]^2 = 2.1884396152264767... .        (8)

Moment convergence in (6) yields

    Var(M_N) = (C2+o(1)) N^(3/2).                                 (9)

Use dimensionless susceptibility chi_N := partial_h E[M_N/N] at h=0, with h the coefficient in (1). The finite-N identity chi_N=Var(M_N)/N gives

    chi_N = (C2+o(1)) sqrt(N).                                    (10)

In the finite-size window, N^(-1/2)chi_N converges uniformly on compact (a,b) sets to Var_(nu_(a,b))(X). For a=b=0 it is C2. If physical field H is defined by an exponent beta H M, the physical susceptibility has the additional factor beta.

The standard high-temperature theorem at fixed 0<=beta<1 is M_N/sqrt(N) -> Normal(0,(1-beta)^(-1)); it predicts chi_N -> (1-beta)^(-1). That approximation is not uniform as beta approaches 1. The relevant subcritical crossover is 1-beta_N much larger than N^(-1/2). At beta_N=1-c/sqrt(N), c>0 fixed, (6) instead gives a non-Gaussian density proportional to exp(-x^4/12-c x^2/2) on the N^(3/4) scale. At beta=1, M_N/sqrt(N)=N^(1/4)X_N is not tight: for every fixed R, P(|M_N|/sqrt(N)>R)->1. Even after variance standardization, M_N/sqrt(Var M_N) converges to X/sqrt(C2), whose kurtosis is 2.18844 rather than the Gaussian value 3. Thus the failure is not repaired by simply inserting the diverging susceptibility into a Gaussian variance.

5. COEXISTENCE: A SECOND UNIFORMITY FAILURE

Fix beta>1 and h=0. Equation (2), symmetry, and concentration on the two maximizers imply

    M_N/N -> (delta_(m_beta)+delta_(-m_beta))/2,
    E[M_N]=0,
    Var(M_N)/N^2 -> m_beta^2,
    chi_N/N -> m_beta^2.                                          (11)

The finite-N ensemble remains symmetric, while a fixed positive field selects the positive phase after the thermodynamic limit. More explicitly, for h_N=c/N with fixed real c, a two-well Laplace expansion gives

    M_N/N -> w_+(c) delta_(m_beta)+w_-(c)delta_(-m_beta),
    w_+(c)=e^(c m_beta)/(2 cosh(c m_beta)),
    w_-(c)=e^(-c m_beta)/(2 cosh(c m_beta)).                       (12)

Consequently, lim_(h downarrow 0) lim_(N->infinity) E[M_N/N] = m_beta, whereas E_(N,beta,0)[M_N/N]=0 for every N. The field window 1/N resolves the coexistence weights. A single Gaussian about the finite-volume mean zero cannot describe (11): M_N/sqrt(N) escapes toward two opposite values of magnitude m_beta sqrt(N), and the susceptibility is order N. If conditioned on the positive phase, the local variable (M_N-Nm_beta)/sqrt(N) is Gaussian with variance

    v_beta = 1/[I''(m_beta)-beta]
           = (1-m_beta^2)/[1-beta(1-m_beta^2)].                   (13)

The Gaussian approximation breaks globally across the mixture; it survives within a selected stable phase.

6. STATISTICAL-MECHANICAL CONTINUUM AND PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON

The continuum in (5) is a weak/density limit of a scalar rescaled order parameter. The Curie–Weiss Hamiltonian assigns equal 1/N coupling to every pair and has no spatial coordinate, gradient energy, or finite interaction range. Its quartic expansion is a controlled mean-field Landau rate function. It does not prove the same coefficient, exponent, or fluctuation law for a local lattice model or a spatial continuum field theory.

Primary-source comparison:

* Ellis and Newman, “Limit theorems for sums of dependent random variables occurring in statistical mechanics,” 1978, established non-Gaussian limits when the thermodynamic rate function has a degenerate minimum; type k gives scale N^(1-1/(2k)) and an exp(-constant x^(2k)) law. The Curie–Weiss critical point is type 2. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00533049.
* Ellis, Newman, and Rosen, “Limit theorems for sums of dependent random variables occurring in statistical mechanics. II. Conditioning, multiple phases, and metastability,” 1980, analyze conditional fluctuations and multiple phases. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00536186.
* Eichelsbacher and Löwe, “Stein’s method for dependent random variables occurring in statistical mechanics,” submitted 13 August 2009 and published in Electronic Journal of Probability 15 (2010), Theorems 1.2–1.4, prove O(N^(-1/2)) Kolmogorov bounds at fixed high temperature and at criticality, and the beta_N=1+gamma/sqrt(N) quartic crossover for zero field. Their fixed-beta Gaussian constant depends on beta; the N^(-1/2) temperature window is the crossover. https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.1909 ; https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v15-777.
* Verbeure and Zagrebnov, “Gaussian, non-Gaussian critical fluctuations in the Curie-Weiss model,” 1994, derive q-mode laws with temperature and fading fields. Their q=0 total critical mode is quartic/non-Gaussian, while nonzero Fourier modes can have Gaussian internal fluctuations. They also caution that models can share a critical index but differ in rate functions and fluctuation distributions. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02186760 ; author-uploaded text: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/225435472_Gaussian_non-Gaussian_critical_fluctuations_in_the_Curie-Weiss_model.

The prior explicitly classifies quartic criticality as established and makes no transfer to local-lattice universality. See READ_LEDGER.txt. 7. COSTS, BASELINE, AND FALSIFIER

Inputs supplied: N, beta, h, and the exact complete-graph model. Acquired inputs: none. There is no query interface or inference stage. The exact sum (1) has N+1 magnetization values. A recurrence for adjacent weights is

    w(M+2)/w(M) = [(N-M)/(N+M+2)] exp[2 beta(M+1)/N+2h].

Thus all weights and moments use O(N) scalar arithmetic operations and O(N) memory, or O(1) memory for streamed moments/normalization. Numerically, log weights and log-sum-exp are needed because the unscaled exponents are O(N); p-bit arithmetic is charged per recurrence/sum. This is an arithmetic count, not a bit-complexity theorem. Equation (1) already eliminates explicit enumeration of 2^N spin configurations. No physical Gibbs preparation, mixing time, pair-coupler implementation, or cost of emitting N spins is included.

Strongest equal-data baseline: the exact binomial law (1), evaluated for the same supplied N,beta,h, plus the Ellis–Newman/Eichelsbacher–Löwe asymptotics and rates. The exact baseline gives the full finite-N answer and costs O(N) weight operations; this report improves neither prediction nor computation. The finite check script below only evaluates that baseline in float64 and supports the constants in (8)–(10); it is not proof.

Finite diagnostic: the float64 exact-sum script gives Var(M)/N^(3/2)=1.13872 at N=64 and 1.16782 at N=8192, tending toward C2=1.17083; the standardized fourth moment increases from 2.05616 to 2.17708 toward 2.18844. This checks the coefficient and the non-Gaussian direction on these sizes only.
