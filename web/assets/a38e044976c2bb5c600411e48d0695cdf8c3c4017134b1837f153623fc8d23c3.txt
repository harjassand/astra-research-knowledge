1. Exact interface, with the row denominator retained
----------------------------------------------------
Use the proper uniform bounded-span q=2 marked row-urn code and its projection
in outputs/research/sol_hidden_renewal/RESULT.txt, Sections 1--2. Given positive
integer gaps w=(g_1,...,g_m), set N=1+sum_t g_t. The observed binary block has
events at positions 1, 1+g_1, ..., N, so it starts and ends at an event.
For a hidden path u=(u_0,...,u_m) in {0,1}^{m+1}, let

 c_{i,d,g}=#{t: (u_{t-1},u_t,g_t)=(i,d,g)},
 r_i=#{t=1,...,m: u_{t-1}=i},
 a(u)=product_{i,d,g} c_{i,d,g}!.

Its exact evidence is

 Z(w)=sum_u a(u)/(r_0! r_1!),
 Q_N^bin(x(w))=Z(w)/(2K_N).

K_N is the number of bounded-span four-colored count pools, plus the separate
allzero mode. K_N is computed exactly by the colored-partition DP already
banked. Full span forces the pool to equal the observed counts; no extra
unused items fit. This is why this query theorem does not directly cover a
censored prefix. Numeric gap labels are actual durations in the raw binary
input; m<=N-1. No succinct, exponentially encoded horizon is assumed.

Main restricted theorem. For every such word with every gap multiplicity at
most two, rational 0<epsilon<1 and 0<delta<1/2, an algorithm returns a
nonnegative rational Zhat satisfying

 Pr[(1-epsilon) Z(w)<=Zhat<=(1+epsilon) Z(w)]>=1-delta

in worst-case bit time polynomial in N, epsilon^{-1}, log(delta^{-1}) and
the bit lengths of the rational accuracy parameters. Dividing by 2K_N gives
the same relative guarantee for Q_N^bin, with no inverse likelihood cost.
The m=0 block is handled exactly: Z=2. No zero testing is needed because Z>0.
The FPRAS below is a theorem-level composition; this packet does not contain
an executable implementation of the published Ising algorithm or a practical
runtime claim for a local computer.

2. Permutation expansion and the canonical weighted bond counterexample
----------------------------------------------------------------------
For each gap g, let O_g be its occurrence set. The number of permutations
pi_g of O_g preserving its ordered transition-pair colors is
product_{i,d} c_{i,d,g}!. Consequently, for arbitrary multiplicities,

 a(u)=sum_{(pi_g)} product_g product_{t in O_g}
     1[u_{t-1}=u_{pi_g(t)-1}] 1[u_t=u_{pi_g(t)}].

For multiplicity two, pi_g is identity or swap. Let a doubled gap occur at
positions a,b, and assign it a bond bit sigma_g. The selected bond adds BOTH
equality edges {a-1,b-1} and {a,b} on vertices {0,...,m}. A gap occurring once
has no bond. Let E_sigma be the union of the selected edges, kappa_sigma its
number of components, and

 U(sigma)=2^{kappa_sigma}, W=sum_sigma U(sigma)=sum_u a(u).

If component C contains a_C origins among vertices {0,...,m-1}, then
sum_C a_C=m. Vertex m is an endpoint and contributes zero to these sizes.
The row-weighted bond mass is exactly

 B(sigma)=sum_{T subset components} 1/[s_T! (m-s_T)!],
 s_T=sum_{C in T} a_C,
 Z=sum_sigma B(sigma).

Compute B by the nonnegative coefficient DP for product_C (1+z^{a_C}).
This costs O(m^2) integer operations with polynomial bit length. A component
of origin size zero contributes a factor two, as required for a free endpoint.

Unweighted log-supermodularity DOES survive overlapping edge groups. Graphic
component count kappa(E) is decreasing and supermodular on edge sets. For
group subsets A,B, E_{A union B}=E_A union E_B while E_{A intersection B}
is a subset of E_A intersection E_B. Therefore

 kappa(E_A)+kappa(E_B)
 <=kappa(E_A union E_B)+kappa(E_A intersection E_B)
 <=kappa(E_{A union B})+kappa(E_{A intersection B}).

Thus U(A)U(B)<=U(A union B)U(A intersection B), even with shared edges.

The row-weighted B fails this inequality. Take the legal word w=(1,1,2,2),
m=4 and N=7. Its binary trajectory is 1110101. Its two edge groups are
{0-1,1-2} and {2-3,3-4}. Exact weighted masses are

 B(00)=35/6, B(10)=5/6, B(01)=5/4, B(11)=1/12.

Hence B(00)B(11)=35/72 < 25/24=B(10)B(01). This specifically blocks applying
a ferromagnetic/FKG theorem directly to the canonical weighted bond law.
It does not block a different variable representation or an FPRAS.

There is also no positive log-supermodular auxiliary-variable lift whose
marginal on THESE canonical Boolean bond coordinates is B. Marginalizing a
log-supermodular Boolean weight preserves log-supermodularity. One elementary
proof eliminates one auxiliary bit: for arbitrary x,y put C=x meet y and
D=x join y; write A_b=f(x,b), B_b=f(y,b), C_b=f(C,b), D_b=f(D,b).
The diagonal products are bounded by C_0D_0 and C_1D_1. For the two cross
products X=A_0B_1 and Y=A_1B_0, log-supermodularity gives X,Y<=U=C_0D_1,
XY<=(C_0D_0)(C_1D_1)=UV with V=C_1D_0, and U>=V. These inequalities imply
X+Y<=U+V: if a cross product is at most V this is immediate; otherwise use
t+UV/t<=U+V for V<=t<=U. Summing diagonal and cross inequalities proves
(C_0+C_1)(D_0+D_1)>=(A_0+A_1)(B_0+B_1). Iterate the elimination. Nonnegative
zero cases follow by the same inequalities. This claim preserves the stated
coordinates; it does not exclude gauges, signed transforms or other encodings.

3. Every conditional unweighted partition reduces to ferromagnetic Ising
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let a bond status be free, forced identity, or forced swap. Its original
four-port tensor on the starts A,C and ends B,D is respectively

 1+1[A=C]1[B=D], 1, or 1[A=C]1[B=D].

Apply T=2^{-1/2}[[1,1],[1,-1]] to every tensor port. Internal identity edges
are invariant under T^T T=I. The transformed tensors are respectively

 4*1[A=B=C=D=0]+1[A=C]1[B=D],
 4*1[A=B=C=D=0],
 1[A=C]1[B=D].

Thus ALWAYS wire the two starts together and the two ends together for every
doubled label, including forced bonds. These wires plus the internal word
edges create the Fourier graph components. A free bond gives between its
start and end wire components the interaction

 M=[[5,1],[1,1]].

A forced identity pins both wire components to zero and contributes a scalar
4. A forced swap imposes the same pair wires without any interaction.
A singleton gap has the all-ones two-port tensor, which transforms to
2*1[A=B=0]; it pins both endpoints and contributes 2. The two free word-end
vectors transform to sqrt(2)e_0, pinning their components and contributing
another factor 2. Therefore for every partial bond assignment h,

 W_h=2^{1+#singletons+2#forced-identities} Z_M(G_h; specified spins zero).

G_h has O(m) vertices and edges. Multiple edges, isolated vertices and
self-loops are allowed; their scalar contributions or fields are retained.
Every pin favors the same spin zero. All W_h are positive, since constant
original hidden paths satisfy every selected equality and all free factors
are at least one. This is an exact, sign-free partition identity after the
specified orthogonal basis change. The selected-edge graph used for correction
in Section 4 is DIFFERENT from this fully wired Fourier graph.

For completeness, the source theorem can be used with zero field alone,
so a nonuniform-field extension need not be assumed. Choose rational r>1
close to a=5^{1/4}, and replace M by M_r=[[r^4,1],[1,1]]. Use spins s,t in
{-1,+1}, with zero corresponding to +1. Then

 M_r(s,t)=r^{1+s+t+st}.

For each interaction edge vw, introduce the three zero-field ferromagnetic
Ising edges vw, v* and w*, where * is one common ghost vertex. Each has
Boltzmann factors r for equal spins and r^{-1} for unequal spins. Identify
every pinned vertex with *. Conditional on s_*=+1, the triangle product is
r^{s_vs_w+s_v+s_w}, so multiply by r per original interaction. The two ghost
spin choices contribute equally by global spin flip. If e is the number of
free interactions, this proves

 Z_{M_r}(G;pins zero) = r^e Z_Ising(H;zero field)/2.

Remove loops with their factor r and merge parallel edges with multiplicity
t by using interaction r^t; all t sum to O(m). All remaining couplings are
ferromagnetic. There are O(m) sites, O(m) interactions before merging, and
the prefactors are known positive rationals. Cases with no edges are exact.

If |log(r^4/5)|<=rho/(8 max(1,e)), every configuration weight, and therefore
the partition itself, changes by a factor between exp(-rho/8) and exp(rho/8).
A dyadic r meeting this requirement is obtained by rational bisection of
r^4=5 using O(log(m/rho)) bits. Use a slightly smaller tolerance in the Ising
FPRAS to obtain relative tolerance rho for the ORIGINAL W_h. This charges
the irrational constant approximation and does not assume exact real numbers.

Primary imported theorem. Jerrum and Sinclair, 'Polynomial-time approximation
algorithms for the Ising model', author preprint revised December 1991,
Theorem 5, printed pp.12--13 (PDF pp.14--15), gives an FPRAS for arbitrary
ferromagnetic interaction matrix Vij and uniform external field B, including
B=0. The proof's runtime is polynomial in site count and inverse accuracy,
independent of the magnitudes of individual interaction weights. Its Section
3, printed p.8 (PDF p.10), explicitly uses exact unit-cost real arithmetic.
We do not silently turn that convention into a bit-model assertion.

Bit-model repair for THESE instances. With rational r, every high-temperature
edge activity for a merged multiplicity t is

 lambda=(r^{2t}-1)/(r^{2t}+1),
 cosh(t log r)=(r^t+r^{-t})/2.

Both are rational of polynomial bit length. Theorem 5's annealing schedule
uses only the rational vertex penalties mu_k=(v-k)/v down to 1/v and finally
mu=0, where v is the number of Ising sites. Samples are needed only at mu>=1/v.
The sampled observables are (mu_{k+1}/mu_k)^{#odd}, with exponent at most v;
all are exact rationals with polynomial bit length, including the final
zero-field observable 1[#odd=0]. Products and averages use polynomially
many such terms. Theorem 2 provides a polynomial deterministic number of
Metropolis steps for each sample, uniformly in the edge activities.

Each ideal transition probability is a rational computable with polynomial
bit operations. Simulate it by a b-bit dyadic approximation. If the total
number of simulation steps in a generator is T, choose
b>=ceil(log_2(2T/tau)) plus the bits required for finite uniform edge choice.
The one-step kernel error is at most tau/(2T); coupling all steps adds at
most tau/2 to total variation. Allocate the other tau/2 to the published
mixing bound. This yields the required generator accuracy with fixed finite
randomness and worst-case polynomial bit cost. Approximate uniform edge
choice by a dyadic categorical distribution with the same allocated error.
No unbounded rejection loop is needed. Median repetition amplifies confidence
using O(log(delta^{-1})) independent trials. Thus the imported real-arithmetic
algorithm gives a polynomial-BIT W_h FPRAS for this rational family, with no
inverse W_h or inverse branch-probability dependence.

4. A polynomial lower bound for the row correction
-------------------------------------------------
Let C_m=binom(m,floor(m/2))/m! and put

 g(sigma)=B(sigma)/(U(sigma) C_m)
 =E_{uniform independent component colors}
       [binom(m,R_0)/binom(m,floor(m/2))].

Then 0<g<=1. For mu_U(sigma)=U(sigma)/W,

 Z=W C_m E_{mu_U} g.

We prove the uniform lower bound E g>=p0=1/[1024(m+1)^3] for m>=1. This is
the load-bearing step permitting relative rather than merely additive error.

First fix sigma and let a_max=max_C a_C. Independently assigning each
component color zero or one gives

 E R_0=m/2,
 Var(R_0)=(1/4)sum_C a_C^2 <=m a_max/4.

The elementary binomial type bound gives, for p=r/m,

 binom(m,r)>=exp(m H(p))/(m+1),
 binom(m,floor(m/2))<=2^m.

For the first inequality, r is a mode of Binomial(m,r/m), and its probability
is at least 1/(m+1); endpoint cases hold exactly. Write t=2p-1. The positive
series

 D(p||1/2)=sum_{k>=1} t^{2k}/[2k(2k-1)]

has coefficient sum log 2, so D<=t^2 log 2. Therefore the ratio inside g is
at least (m+1)^{-1} exp[-4 log(2)(r-m/2)^2/m]. Jensen's inequality gives

 g(sigma)>=(m+1)^{-1}exp[-4 log(2) Var(R_0)/m]
          >=2^{-a_max}/(m+1).

Second control a_max under mu_U. The POTENTIAL simple equality graph E_all
obtained by selecting every doubled-label group has degree at most two:
vertex t is in at most one start equality, belonging to gap t+1, and one end
equality, belonging to gap t. If an edge is owned by two distinct groups,
both possible incidences at each endpoint coincide with that edge. It is
therefore an isolated two-vertex component. This overlap exception is why
one must not count its two owners as two obligatory selected bonds.

Every simple path of length L>=2 in E_all consequently has a UNIQUE group
owner for each edge, and requires at least ceil(L/2) distinct selected bonds.
For any fixed values of any other bonds, adding a bond adds equality edges,
so its U weight cannot increase. Summing over unspecified bonds preserves
this comparison. Thus

 Pr_muU[sigma_j=1 | any partial assignment of other bits]<=1/2,
 Pr_muU[a specified set of t bonds are all selected]<=2^{-t}.

A degree-at-most-two graph has at most 2(m+1) directed simple paths of any
fixed positive length. If a selected component has at least L+1 vertices,
it contains a simple path of length L, including the cycle case. Union bound:

 Pr_muU[max component size >=L+1]
 <=2(m+1) 2^{-ceil(L/2)} <=2(m+1) 2^{-L/2}, L>=2.

Take L0=ceil(log_2(64(m+1)^2)). The bad probability is at most 1/4. On the
remaining probability at least 3/4, a_max<=component size<=L0. Also
2^{-L0}>=1/[128(m+1)^2]. Hence

 E_muU g >=3/[512(m+1)^3] >=p0.

If L0 exceeds m, the bad event is empty, and the same bound holds. All
ceilings and small-m cases are covered. No equilibrium assumption about
the hidden source, and no independence of different bonds, is used here.

5. Actual FPRAS composition, errors and rare likelihoods
------------------------------------------------------
The W_h FPRAS in Section 3 supplies an approximate mu_U sampler by sequential
bond self-reduction. With a partial assignment h, approximate both positive
branch partitions W_{h0},W_{h1} and normalize. Adding one group imposes at
most two equalities, so termwise U_0/4<=U_1<=U_0, and the same inequality
holds after summing unspecified bonds. Thus every selected-branch conditional
lies in [1/5,1/2]; no exponentially rare branch must be observed empirically.

For any target TV tolerance xi, let L be the number of doubled labels. L=0
is exact. Otherwise set the relative tolerance and per-call failure chance
of each branch estimate to xi/(16L). On the event both estimates are good,
normalization changes the Bernoulli conditional by at most twice that
relative tolerance. The two bad-estimate chances sum to at most xi/(8L).
Use a finite-bit Bernoulli approximation with error at most xi/(4L).
Sequential coupling bounds the entire sampled law's TV distance from mu_U
by at most xi. Bad calls use any valid fallback branch probability, so their
effect is included in this unconditional bound. All calls have polynomial
worst-case cost. Fresh randomness for each complete self-reduction produces
independent identically distributed samples from one law nu within xi of mu_U.

Set xi=epsilon p0/32. Compute g exactly for each sample by the component-size
subset DP. If mu=E_muU g and mu'=E_nu g, boundedness gives

 |mu'-mu|<=xi<=epsilon mu/32, mu'>=p0/2.

Take T=ceil(1536 epsilon^{-2} p0^{-1} log(8/delta)) independent samples.
A rational upper bound to the logarithm, e.g. ceil(log_2(8/delta)), can replace
it in the implementation. Bounded-variable multiplicative Chernoff bounds
give sample mean gbar within relative epsilon/8 of mu' with failure at most
delta/2. Combined with the TV bias, gbar is within relative epsilon/4 of mu.
Independently estimate W within relative epsilon/8 with failure at most
delta/4. Return

 Zhat=What C_m gbar.

The combined relative error is less than epsilon for 0<epsilon<1, and total
failure is at most delta. This is a relative FPRAS for the original evidence
with its row-factorial denominators intact, rather than a heuristic proposal.

Cost. p0^{-1}=O(m^3), T=O(m^3 epsilon^{-2} log(delta^{-1})), and each sample
uses O(m) counting calls. Their inverse relative tolerance is O(m^4/epsilon)
and their log confidence cost is O(log(m/epsilon)). Every Ising instance has
O(m) sites/interactions; all rational weights and random-step precision have
polynomial bit length. The published polynomial is high degree. This proof
does not claim practical speed or provide a benchmark.

For exact g arithmetic, let d_r be coefficients of product_C(1+z^{a_C}).
Then g=[sum_r d_r binom(m,r)]/[2^{kappa} binom(m,floor(m/2))]. Every sample
g has common denominator 2^{m+1}binom(m,floor(m/2)); scaling its numerator by
2^{m+1-kappa} gives an integer with O(m) bits. The sample average has O(m+log T)
bits in this common representation. C_m has O(m log m) bits, and K_N has
polynomial bit length and is exactly computable. Returning Zhat/(2K_N)
therefore preserves relative accuracy for probabilities that can be extremely
small. No additive likelihood tolerance, inverse Q probability, exponentially
many random trials, or uncharged postselection event appears in this leaf.

6. Precise obstruction to the same unrestricted proposal
-------------------------------------------------------
For arbitrary multiplicities retain the permutation expansion of Section 2.
Give a permutation tuple pi weight U(pi)=2^{kappa(pi)} and let W=sum_pi U(pi).
Its exact row correction is again

 g(pi)=E_component colors binom(m,R_0)/binom(m,floor(m/2)),
 E_muU g =m! Z/[binom(m,floor(m/2)) W].

Now take ONE observed gap label repeated m times. Constant hidden paths
contribute m! each to W, so W>=2m!. On the other hand, classify hidden paths
by their four transition counts C_{00},C_{01},C_{10},C_{11} summing to m.
There are binom(m+3,3) such types. For a fixed count type, draw a start mark
uniformly and sample its outgoing row urns without replacement, stopping
when the current row empties. This is a proper law allowing unused rows.
Every path using exactly the type has probability

 (1/2) product_{i,d} C_{id}! /[R_0! R_1!].

These length-m complete paths are disjoint outcomes, so their sum is at most
one. Thus their unnormalized contribution to Z is at most two. Summing types,

 Z<=2 binom(m+3,3),
 E_muU g <=binom(m+3,3)/binom(m,floor(m/2))
          <=binom(m+3,3)(m+1)/2^m.

This is an exponential upper bound on rejection acceptance when proposing
pi from mu_U and accepting with probability g(pi). Such a rejection correction
requires exponentially many expected proposals. The polynomial lower bound
in Section 4 cannot hold for arbitrary multiplicities. A small mean by itself
does not prove an exponential relative variance bound. For this proposal and
this exact correction observable, the stronger bound can in fact be proved
as follows. It is not a lower bound on all algorithms.

Let m=2k, k>=3, and C=binom(2k,k). Fix the hidden run with k zero origins
followed by k one origins and a final one: u=0^k 1^{k+1}. Its transition
classes have positions 1..k-1 of color 00, position k of color 01, and
positions k+1..2k of color 11. Consider permutations that fix position k,
are a single full cycle on positions 1..k-1, and a single full cycle on
positions k+1..2k. There are

 T=(k-2)!(k-1)!

such distinct permutations. The start equalities of the first full cycle
connect vertices 0..k-2 and its end equalities connect 1..k-1. Their overlap
is nonempty because k>=3, so together they connect all zero vertices. The
second cycle similarly connects all one vertices k..2k. No equality crosses
the two classes. Thus every one of these permutations has EXACTLY two
components, each with k origins. Its U is four and its correction is

 g=(1+C)/(2C).

Their contribution alone gives

 E_muU g^2 >= T(1+C)^2/(W C^2),
 E_muU g^2/(E_muU g)^2 >= T(1+C)^2 W/[(m!)^2 Z^2]
   >=(1+C)^2/[2 C k^2(k-1) binom(m+3,3)^2]
   >=C/[2 k^2(k-1) binom(m+3,3)^2].

Here T/m!=1/[C k^2(k-1)], W>=2m!, and Z<=2binom(m+3,3) are the previous
bounds. Since C>=2^m/(m+1), the final expression grows exponentially up to
a polynomial denominator. Therefore Var_muU(g)/(E_muU g)^2 is exponential
along this family. This rigorously blocks a uniform polynomial relative-
variance guarantee for the standard unweighted importance observable.
It does not prove that all estimators, clipping/control-variate schemes,
different proposals, or approximation algorithms require exponential time.

The one-label likelihood is exactly computable by a polynomial transition-
count DP because its observed gap alphabet is fixed. Thus this construction
is a precise proposal obstruction, not approximation hardness of Z. In
general, permutation equality graphs also lose the selected-pair graph's
degree-two/isolated-overlap structure, and no Ising subroutine for their full
higher-arity factorial tensors has been supplied.

7. Scope, source comparison and next gate
----------------------------------------
The restricted FPRAS applies to the exact-hard full-span family with doubled
original labels and singleton padding. It shows that exact hardness of that
family does not obstruct efficient relative approximation. It does NOT prove
a relative oracle for all Q_N^bin prefixes: shorter prefixes allow unused
pool items, varying total row counts and the censored terminal gap moment,
none of which are included in the full-span W_h graph identity. It also does
not supply general-q partitions, an approximate final suffix-average oracle,
or any predictor for every hidden renewal source.

The parent packet outputs/research/root_relative_prediction_compiler/PROOF.txt
does convert a uniform polynomial-bit relative CHILD-marginal oracle into
an O_q(n^{-1/2}) expected-risk predictor, with explicit smoothing/failure costs.
This leaf is not such an oracle. A proof covering arbitrary multiplicities
and censoring, or a different efficient code preserving the redundancy bound,
is still the decisive generic hidden-source gate.

The published Ising subroutine is standard and attributed. The bond expansion
and Hadamard M identity already occurred in our exact-hardness packet; only
the restricted correction/approximation construction is newly reconstructed
here. No claim of external novelty is made. The primary source bounds below
concern the imported theorem, not priority of the whole derived interface.
