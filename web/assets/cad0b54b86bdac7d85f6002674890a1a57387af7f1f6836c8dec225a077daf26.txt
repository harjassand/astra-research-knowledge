3. NONDESTRUCTIVE COUPLING LOCALIZATION, WITH LAMBDA CANCELLED

Measure particle number M on EVERY input copy. Target states are number

The conditional quantum state in sector m is

             sigma_(g,m)=exp(-tau A_m(g))/z_m(g),

independent of lambda. Sort copies into sector groups. Given the counts

4. INVERSE-SLOPE POSITIVITY AND ITS ACTUAL ACQUISITION

Finite z_(L,2)(g) is decreasing and convex in g. Differentiating the finite
trace gives z'=-tau Tr[L W exp(-tau A_g)]. The second derivative is
tau^2 integral_0^1 Tr[B exp(-s tau A_g)B exp(-(1-s)tau A_g)]ds>=0,
B=L W. In an A_g eigenbasis each summand is a positive coefficient times
|B_ij|^2. Continuum z2(g) is the pointwise limit of these functions, and
therefore also decreasing and convex. No unbounded trace derivative is
needed for the continuum inverse.

The continuum ground energy E(g) is strictly increasing at positive g.
Here is the exact argument from kinetic_second_moment.txt. A ground
minimizer u_g can be nonnegative by taking its absolute value. If its
contact norm vanished, its weak eigen-equation would be the free one.
Its positive overlap with the unique strictly positive free ground would
force it to be that free ground, whose contact norm is 3, a contradiction.
Thus V u_g!=0. Rayleigh comparison with the ground at b>a gives
E(a)<=E(b)-(b-a)||Vu_b||^2<E(b). Min-max also makes every higher ordered
eigenvalue nondecreasing. Hence

       d_infty:=z2(g_+)-z2(2g_+)>0.                     (4.1)

For a convex decreasing function, any secant inside [g_-,g_+] is at least
the negative slope of its secant on [g_+,2g_+] in absolute value. Thus

 z2(a)-z2(b)>=(b-a)d_infty/g_+.

The positive number d_infty is NOT supplied. The following finite PUBLIC
setup algorithm acquires a certified lower bound d_*>0 and a fixed L0.
Use the explicit fixed-m heat trace bound H_2(L,R_aux) from
sol_attractive_critical/quantitative_rate.txt (7.1), with public coupling
upper bound enlarged to 2g_+. It bounds absolute canonical trace error.
At stage j take L_j=2^(j+4), R_aux,j=4j/tau. Compute both finite two-body
traces at g_+,2g_+ with certified error <=2^-j. If

 [computed z_(L_j,2)(g_+)-computed z_(L_j,2)(2g_+)]
                   -2^(1-j)-2H_2(L_j,R_aux,j)>0,

take any smaller positive rational as d_*. The test terminates: fixed-m
constants are finite, H_2 tends to zero, and (4.1) is strict. Every stage
uses a known matrix of dimension binom(L_j-1,2); matrix acquisition and
certified heat evaluation are charged in acquisition.txt. No spectrum is
given to the algorithm, and the terminating stage is not asserted small.

Now choose fixed R_aux so 12 B_2 exp(-tau R_aux/4)<d_*/4, where B_2 is the
public half-heat trace bound in (7.1). The remaining terms in 2H_2(L,R_aux)
are bounded by explicitly decreasing powers of L. Choose PUBLIC integer
L0>=max(16,4) so for EVERY L>=L0, 2H_2(L,R_aux)<=d_*/2.
This is a scalar arithmetic test, not a nonuniform convergence oracle.
Then z_(L,2)(g_+)-z_(L,2)(2g_+)>=d_*/2. Use, uniformly L>=L0 and continuum,

                    kappa=d_*/[2g_+(z1^*)^2].         (4.2)

This proves (3.3). The derivative lower bound is used only as an inverse

5. QUADRATIC LEAKAGE UNDER AN ESTIMATED ENERGY CUTOFF

This lemma is reusable for ANY common-form family A_g=T+g V^*V with
T>=0, g,h>=g_->0 and compact resolvent. Let P_h be any spectral projection
retaining ALL A_h energies <=R and only energies <=2R, R>=1; its exact
outer threshold can vary by number sector. For an A_g eigenvector psi
of energy E<=R/2, and A_h eigenvectors chi_j with E_j>R, weak form testing
gives

 (E_j-E)<chi_j,psi>=(h-g)<Vchi_j,Vpsi>.

The sign is immaterial. The form bound A_h>=h V^*V implies

 sum_j |<Vchi_j,Vpsi>|^2/(E_j+1)
  =||(A_h+1)^-1/2 V^*Vpsi||^2
  <=||Vpsi||^2/h <= E/(hg) <=E/g_-^2.

The equality means the bounded adjoint of V(A_h+1)^-1/2 acting on Vpsi;
there is no claim that V^*Vpsi belongs to the Hilbert space. Since
(E_j+1)/(E_j-E)^2<=8/R on E_j>R,E<=R/2,R>=1, it follows that

             ||(I-P_h)psi||^2<=8(g-h)^2 E/(g_-^2 R).  (5.1)

Splitting the true Gibbs energy at R/2, using (2.2), yields

 Tr[(I-P_h)rho_g]<=S_half exp(-tau R/4)
                              +8(g-h)^2 C_E/(g_-^2 R). (5.2)

At finite L, V=sqrt(LW) is a finite operator. In continuum the argument
above takes place on the common form domain, at each finite number sector.
Summing uses the finite grand-canonical energy bound C_E. No finite-rank
or fixed-d approximation is assumed before establishing this inequality.

7. EXACT TP PROTOCOL AND FULL PRODUCT-STATE ERROR

Let 0<epsilon<=1, p=(epsilon/12)^2, eta=p/4, h as in Section 3, and
delta=C_loc h. Assume h<=p_*/2; this is an explicit lower threshold on n.
Choose r>=2 such that n t_r<=p/4, using the scalar tail in (2.2), and an
integer R satisfying

 R>=1,
 R>=(4/tau)log(4n S_half/p),
 R>=32n delta^2 C_E/(g_-^2 p).                         (7.1)

The encoder first measures and counts M on all copies. If a number exceeds
r, use a fixed failure flag. Otherwise compute g_hat from the counts,
sort the quantum copies, and in each group project onto the acquired
estimated-coupling energy space P_(g_hat,m). Any failed projection leads
to the same failure flag. The all-success conditional state is a tensor
product of identical normalized projected states inside each m group.
It can be treated by exact Schur compression, Section 8.

Crucially the estimate depends on the counts, so one does NOT multiply
a false independent one-copy error bound. Average over the count blocks.
On the good event |g-g_hat|<=delta. The union bound and (5.1) show that
the average all-projection failure probability is at most

 eta+n t_r+n S_half exp(-tau R/4)+8n delta^2 C_E/(g_-^2 R) <= p. (7.2)

The true-energy and mean-energy terms average to exactly n times their
single-copy grand-canonical values, even though the estimate is correlated
with the states. Dropping the bad-count contribution costs at most eta.
No conditional inverse probability or number-sector normalization loss is
introduced. Number tails are discarded by actual failure events.

In the direct sum over classical count blocks, this protocol is a single
block-dependent projection followed on failure by a fixed density. For
the normalized pre-projection state Omega, the gentle projection bound
gives ||Omega-P Omega P||_1<=2sqrt(p). Adding the failure output costs p.
The sorting, discarding of order records and uniform reshuffling are TP
and exact on the pre-projection family. Therefore the ideal recovered
whole n-copy state has full trace error <=2sqrt(p)+p<=3sqrt(p)=epsilon/4.
The remaining 3epsilon/4 is available for finite circuit approximation.
Encoder and decoder are specified on arbitrary states by the same
measurement, failure replacement and TP extension; no postselection.

For fixed epsilon, (7.1) has R=O(log n) because n delta^2 is a PUBLIC

8. EXACT SECTORWISE SCHUR SUFFICIENCY AND THE MEMORY COUNT

For any density sigma on C^d and any k>=0, Schur-Weyl duality gives

 (C^d)^tensor k = direct_sum_alpha U_alpha tensor V_alpha,
 sigma^tensor k = direct_sum_alpha R_alpha(sigma) tensor I_(V_alpha).

This follows because sigma^tensor k commutes with every permutation,
and the commutant algebra is direct_sum End(U_alpha) tensor I_V. Its
blocks need not be normalized. Measure alpha, retain its conditional
U_alpha density, discard the maximally mixed multiplicity factor. Decode
by appending I_V/dim V and inverse Schur transformation. This is EXACT
on sigma^tensor k for every sigma, including rank-deficient sigma and
degenerate spectra. There is no Q-LAN regularity or nondegeneracy gate.

The Young diagram has d row lengths <=k, requiring <=d ceil(log2(k+d))
classical bits. Weyl's formula bounds

 dim U_alpha=product_(i<j)(alpha_i-alpha_j+j-i)/(j-i)
                                  <=(k+d)^[d(d-1)/2].

Equivalently, its Gel'fand-Zetlin quantum register has d(d-1)/2 bounded
integer entries. Thus each group needs at most
[d(d-1)/2]ceil(log2(n+d)) qubits, plus at most one harmless padding qubit.
Groups with zero copies or d0 do not reach the success branch; d1 has no
quantum memory. Summing uses sum_m d_m^2<=(sum_m d_m)^2<=D(R)^2.
The counts take (r+1)ceil(log2(n+1)) classical bits and the coupling label
has O(log n+log(1/epsilon)) bits at fixed public parameters. This proves
(1.1). All dependencies on d are retained while d grows with n.

The decoder's multiplicity preparation is PUBLIC, independent of g and
lambda: sample a uniform integer indexing standard Young tableaux of the
known shape, then unrank it. Branch counts are exact integer dimensions
given by the hook-length formula, so sampling/unranking are finite
algorithms. An expected constant number of rejection trials samples a
uniform integer; a fixed truncation gives an explicitly budgeted small
error. Transient entropy, qubits and gates of the reconstructed n-copy
output are charged; they are not part of persistent archive memory.
