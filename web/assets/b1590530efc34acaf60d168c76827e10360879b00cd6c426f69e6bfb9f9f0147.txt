Primary source reads and extent
1. Onsager (1949), scan https://fig.if.usp.br/~marchett/fluidos/statistical-hydrodynamics_onsager.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02780991. Read pp. 283–286; Fourier cascade and increment regularity argument. Treated as heuristic, not a weak-solution theorem.
2. Eyink (1994), journal page https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0167278994901171; DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(94)90117-1. Read primary abstract only; it says *-Hölder is stronger than ordinary Hölder and describes band-averaged flux/local triadic interactions. CET’s paper was separately read for its footnote reporting Eyink’s weighted Fourier norm. Full Eyink proof not read.
3. Constantin, E, Titi (1994), author-hosted scan, https://web.math.princeton.edu/~weinan/papers/misc1.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099744. Read theorem and commutator proof, pp. 1–3; browser extraction included Besov increment, stress identity, energy equality, and O(epsilon^(3alpha-1)) residual. Source calls for periodic boundary conditions.
4. Duchon, Robert (2000), https://www.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~prazak/uceni/431-20/lit/Jean_Duchon_2000_Nonlinearity_13_312.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/13/1/312. Read Euler Proposition 2 and Proposition 3, pp. 3–4; extracted the 1/4 cubic-increment defect, kernel-independent distributional limit, local energy equation, and C(t)|r|sigma(|r|) criterion. Source’s base domain is T^3 and base solution class is L^3_tL^3_x.
5. Cheskidov, Constantin, Friedlander, Shvydkoy (2008), https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0759 (v1 dated 2007-04-05), author-hosted paper https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~acheskid/papers/onsager.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/21/6/005. Read Theorem 3.3, dyadic flux proof, and §3.4 kinematic counterexample, including the authors’ explicit caveat that the counterexample field is not an unforced Euler solution.
6. Isett (2018), https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.08301 (posted 2016-08-30), Annals paper DOI https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2018.188.3.4. Read abstract and theorem claim confirming for every alpha<1/3 a compact-time-supported weak Euler solution in C_tC_x^alpha with nonconstant total kinetic energy.
7. Isett (2024), https://msp.org/apde/2024/17-6/apde-v17-n6-p09-s.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.2140/apde.2024.17.2123. Read official journal metadata/abstract and endpoint statement: construction approaches the critical exponent across scales but does not attain C^(1/3); endpoint described as open.
