# Battery transport subtask: tracer data do not identify ionic conductivity

## Verdict

The exact mathematical result is a constructive non-identifiability theorem:
within a reversible, translation-invariant two-ion jump model, one can hold the
entire equilibrium position law and each ion's complete single-particle path
law fixed while varying the bulk charge conductivity from arbitrarily close to
zero to arbitrarily close to twice the Nernst–Einstein estimate. The hidden
quantity is the synchronized cross-correlation of cation and anion motion.

This does **not** establish a new transport principle for a real battery
material. Green–Kubo/Einstein–Helfand conductivity, Haven ratios, and the role
of ion cross-correlations are established prior art. The toy construction is a
precise counterexample to a tracer-only inference and a model-design knob, not
a fitted prediction for an electrolyte. Novelty and priority are unresolved;
the present line is suitable as a scoped rejection of a tracer-only claim, not
as the headline frontier discovery.

## Reconstructed source theorem and prior art

France-Lanord and Grossman, *Correlations from Ion Pairing and the
Nernst–Einstein Equation*, Phys. Rev. Lett. **122**, 136001 (2019), give the
Nernst–Einstein expression in Eq. (1) and the exact linear-response
Einstein-form conductivity in Eq. (3). In three dimensions, with signed
valences \(z_i\), their Eq. (3) is
\[
 \sigma_E=\lim_{t\to\infty}
 \frac{e^2}{6 V k_B T\,t}
 \sum_{i,j}z_i z_j\,\mathbb E[\Delta x_i(t)\cdot\Delta x_j(t)].
\]
Their text states that NE is recovered by retaining only \(i=j\); the
off-diagonal terms encode correlations. The paper treats NE as exact at
infinite dilution and warns of failure in concentrated/correlated systems.
Its *cluster* Nernst–Einstein formula is an approximation that assumes
noninteracting clusters. In its PEO/LiTFSI simulation, the authors report a
tenfold NE overestimate, but also large uncertainty in the collective
Einstein estimate; that number is specific to their force field, state point,
cluster definition, and trajectory, not a universal correction.

The mechanistic principle is therefore not new. Walz and van der Spoel's
primary study, *Microscopic origins of conductivity in molten salts unraveled
by computer simulations* (*Communications Chemistry* 4, article 9 (2021)),
reports that the composition with the highest simulated Li-ion mobility need
not have the highest simulated conductivity, and attributes the difference to
carrier density and cross-ion correlations in the studied LiF/Cl/I systems
(abstract; Results, conductivity and cross-correlation sections). A 2026
primary methods study by Zaby et al. compares Green–Kubo and Einstein–Helfand
estimates for [EMIm][DCA] and LiFSI/DME; it reports that the self-only estimate
misses a substantial fraction of collective transport in those simulations
(Results, inverse Haven ratio) and discusses the long trajectory,
unwrapped-coordinate, current-correlation, and uncertainty costs
(Introduction and Methods). These are source-reported simulation findings,
not claims about every solid electrolyte.

## The quantitative model result

The full derivation is in [MODEL_PROOF.md](MODEL_PROOF.md). For \(M\) independent
charge-neutral pairs at density \(\nu=M/V\), the reversible jump process has
two mode rates: \(a\) for common-direction pair hops and \(b\) for
opposite-direction charge hops. For every split with \(a+b=\lambda\), each
ion's full marginal path is the same nearest-neighbour random walk, with
\(D_+=D_-=a_0^2\lambda\), and the zero-field equilibrium position law is
unchanged. Yet
\[
  \sigma=4\beta e^2a_0^2b\nu,\qquad
  \sigma_{NE}=2\beta e^2a_0^2(a+b)\nu,\qquad
  \sigma/\sigma_{NE}=2b/(a+b).
\]
The individual trajectories do not identify the split. At fixed \(\lambda\),
the cross-diffusion coefficient is \(C_{+-}=a_0^2(a-b)\), and adding this
single synchronized cross term recovers the exact conductivity.

More generally, for one monovalent cation/anion pair with scalar long-time
self-diffusivities \(D_+,D_-\) and cross coefficient \(C\), covariance
positivity gives
\[
  (\sqrt{D_+}-\sqrt{D_-})^2\leq D_++D_--2C
  \leq(\sqrt{D_+}+\sqrt{D_-})^2.
\]
This interval is sharp for reversible correlated Brownian diffusions with a
positive-semidefinite block diffusion matrix. It does not assert that every
lattice material realizes every endpoint. For equal tracer diffusivities, the
sharp model-class interval is \([0,2\sigma_{NE}]\), so NE is not a universal
upper bound without an additional sign assumption on correlations. In
particular, some materials may have negative cross coefficients, while the
studied molten-salt examples above had NE above the exact value.

## Model boundary and information contract

The theorem's supplied data are stronger than ordinary experiments provide:
the full equilibrium law and the complete one-ion path marginal are fixed.
The missing information is the *joint* motion of unlike ions. The exact
electrochemical response is a weighted sum over all ion–ion covariances, not
just their self-MSDs.

The jump witness is a mathematical model with labeled, noninteracting pairs
and concerted nearest-neighbour displacements. The concerted transition is
allowed regardless of pair separation, so the generator is not a model of a
short-range physical interaction. It omits Coulomb forces, exclusion,
disorder, lattice chemistry, grain boundaries, interfaces, and electronic
leakage. The reversible Markov theorem is exact under those assumptions; any
connection to nature is conditional and untested.

## Design and measurement consequence

Within the model, improving self-diffusion alone is insufficient: conductivity
depends on the charge-odd mode \(b\), while a change in the charge-even mode
\(a\) can change or preserve tracer diffusion without equivalent charge
transport. This suggests a measurable design question—whether a material's
dominant migration events transport charge or move oppositely charged species
in correlated neutral motions—but supplies no answer for a specific chemistry.

For a named material, acquire at one matched temperature, composition, phase,
and sample condition: carrier number density and valence; tracer \(D_i\) (for
example by PFG-NMR or validated simulation); the species-resolved long-time
cross-displacement matrix (or total charge-current autocorrelation); and direct
bulk conductivity. The decisive comparator is exact Einstein–Helfand or
Green–Kubo transport against the self-only Nernst–Einstein baseline, with
impedance corrected for geometry, grain boundaries, electrode polarization,
and electronic leakage. A fitted two-mode model must also pass an independent
trajectory/impedance comparison; non-Poisson waiting times, strong
inter-pair interactions, or an inconsistent collective conductivity reject
that mechanism.

All cost-sensitive information is material: NE needs self trajectories only;
EH needs long unwrapped trajectories and a stable collective-MSD linear window;
GK needs well-resolved current/velocity frames and a stable integral plateau.
Independent replicas, block uncertainty, correlation-time convergence,
finite-size checks, and force-field or electronic-structure validation are
needed before a simulation-derived number is treated as a material property.
The 2026 methods paper specifically cautions about noisy GK tails, EH fit-window
choice, force-field dependence, and the larger sampling burden from
cross-correlations.

## Evidence classes and remaining gates

- **Established external/source theorem:** the Green–Kubo/Einstein–Helfand
  collective conductivity identity and NE diagonal approximation, as stated
  in the cited primary paper.
- **Internally proved model result:** the exact jump-process construction,
  its invariant law and one-ion path marginals, and the conductivity ratio in
  `MODEL_PROOF.md`.
- **Not established:** mathematical external review; novelty/priority; a
  battery-material parameter fit; an experimental validation; or an
  improved material-design outcome.
- **Decisive next gate:** a source-priority search for the strong
  “same full one-ion path law, different conductivity” formulation, followed
  by an atomistic trajectory and impedance comparison in a named electrolyte.
