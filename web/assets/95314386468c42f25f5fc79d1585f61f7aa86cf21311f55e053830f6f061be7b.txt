CYCLE 4 LEAF — SPARSE COUNTEREXAMPLE AND SUPPORT-SWITCH STRESS TEST
Date: 2026-10-08

RESULT / STATUS
---------------
An exact sparse, weakly reversible, order-five network gives failure of
uniform tightness of classwise stationary laws, even though all classes lie
in one deterministic stoichiometric slice and all five rates range over the
positive compact box [1,2]^5. The failure is across distinct stochastic
communicating classes, indexed by a nonlinear pathwise label n. Each fixed
class is irreducible, nonexplosive, and positive recurrent. For each fixed
class, its stationary laws over [1,2]^5 are uniformly tight. This is not a
within-class recurrence or tightness failure.

No exact within-one-communicating-class counterexample to rate-box uniform
stationary tightness or V-geometric ergodicity was found. The sparse
paired-edge family with positive catalyst immigration/death and positive
paired substrate channels instead admits a common compact-box geometric
drift candidate (see the Cycle 4 sparse-switch audit and Cycle 3
geometric-rate-box proof). My independent exact test of its support-switch
potential found no mixed-escape defect. This result does not extend to
arbitrary weakly reversible networks.

1. Exact weakly reversible family and compact rate box
----------------------------------------------------
Species are A,B,C,D. Use standard stochastic mass-action propensities with
falling factorials and five positive rate constants kappa_1,...,kappa_5:

  R1: 2C       -> A+B+C       rate kappa_1 (C)_2
  R2: A+B+C    -> 3A+2B       rate kappa_2 A B C
  R3: 3A+2B    -> 2C          rate kappa_3 (A)_3 (B)_2
  R4: 2C       -> 2C+D        rate kappa_4 (C)_2
  R5: 2C+D     -> 2C          rate kappa_5 (C)_2 D.

The complex graph has the directed 3-cycle
2C -> A+B+C -> 3A+2B -> 2C
and the reversible edge 2C <-> 2C+D. It is one weakly reversible linkage
class with four complexes. The reaction vectors of R1, R2, and R4 are
linearly independent; R3=-(R1+R2), and R5=-R4. Thus the stoichiometric
rank is three, the deficiency is 4-1-3=0, and the largest source has
molecularity five. The deterministic conservation law is B+C.

The rate parameter is theta=(kappa_1,...,kappa_5) in the nondegenerate
compact box Theta=[1,2]^5. All class and stationary formulas below hold for
every positive theta, so the compact-box quantifier costs nothing extra in
the derivation.

2. Closed classes inside B+C=2
------------------------------
On the slice B+C=2, set

  n = A - B(B+1)/2.

Every enabled reaction preserves n on this slice. For each n>=0, define

  Gamma_n = {(n,0,2,d), (n+1,1,1,d), (n+3,2,0,d) : d in N_0}.

Write x_0=(n,0,2,d), x_1=(n+1,1,1,d), x_2=(n+3,2,0,d). From x_0, R1
moves to x_1 at rate q_0=2 kappa_1; R4 and R5 change only d, with birth
rate 2 kappa_4 and death rate 2 kappa_5 d. From x_1, R2 moves to x_2 at
rate q_1=(n+1) kappa_2. From x_2, R3 returns to x_0 at rate
q_2=2 kappa_3 (n+1)(n+2)(n+3). No other channel is enabled on these
states. Therefore Gamma_n is closed. The 3-cycle connects its phases, and
the birth/death pair at phase zero adjusts d in either direction, so
Gamma_n is irreducible.

These are distinct stochastic communicating classes inside the same
deterministic compatibility slice B+C=2. The pathwise label n is nonlinear
and is not an additional linear stoichiometric conservation law.

Nonexplosion is direct for each fixed n and theta. The three phase rates
are finite constants on the finite phase set. D immigrations occur at a
constant rate 2 kappa_4<=4; on each finite time interval their count is
almost surely finite. D deaths can occur at most the initial D count plus
the number of immigrations, and phase jumps have bounded rate for fixed n.
Thus no finite-time explosion is possible.

3. Exact stationary family and tightness failure
------------------------------------------------
The phase CTMC has the exact stationary weights

  w_0 = 1/(2 kappa_1),
  w_1 = 1/((n+1) kappa_2),
  w_2 = 1/(2 kappa_3 (n+1)(n+2)(n+3)),
  Z_n = w_0+w_1+w_2,   nu_n(i)=w_i/Z_n.

Indeed w_i q_i=1 for i=0,1,2, so each directed phase flux is 1/Z_n.
Conditional on any phase, D has the same immigration-death generator
scaled by 2: immigration rate 2 kappa_4 and death rate 2 kappa_5 d.
Its stationary law is Poisson with mean mu=kappa_4/kappa_5. The product

  pi_(n,theta) = nu_n tensor Pois(kappa_4/kappa_5)

is invariant: the phase-generator balance cancels separately at every d,
and the Poisson birth/death balance cancels at phase zero. It is a
probability law; irreducibility and nonexplosion then give positive
recurrence and uniqueness on Gamma_n.

For every theta in Theta and every state in Gamma_n, A>=n. Any compact
subset K of the countable lattice N_0^4 is finite; let M_K be its maximum
A-coordinate. If n>M_K, then

  pi_(n,theta)(K)=0    for every theta in [1,2]^5.

Thus the family of stationary laws over all recurrent stochastic classes
inside the single deterministic slice B+C=2 is not uniformly tight,
uniformly over the positive compact rate box. Equivalently, for any
norm-like V on the full lattice,

  pi_(n,theta)(V) >= inf{V(x): A(x)>=n} -> infinity.

There can be no common stationary V-moment bound over these classes for
any common coercive V.

The quantifiers matter. Fix n. Within Gamma_n, A,B,C range over only three
phase values, and D is Poisson with mean kappa_4/kappa_5 in [1/2,2]. Hence
the stationary laws over Theta are uniformly tight on this fixed class;
for example, P(D>m)<=2/(m+1) by Markov's inequality. So this construction
does not refute rate-box tightness inside one fixed communicating class.
It also does not by itself refute class-dependent V-geometric ergodicity.
It rules out uniform tightness and common coercive stationary-moment
bounds when the quantifier ranges over the infinitely many class labels.

For kappa_i=1, c=(1,1,1,1) is a complex-balanced equilibrium: every
directed complex flux equals one. The network is weakly reversible and
deficiency zero; this is a sparse order-five instance. The stationary
phase/Poisson formula above was derived directly, so it does not rely on
transferring an ambient product-Poisson law onto the nonlinear classes.
The standard complex-balanced product-form result is only background prior
art, not evidence of novelty for this class construction.

4. Independent stress test of the sparse support-switch potential
-----------------------------------------------------------------
I tested the candidate on a different sparse graph from the earlier
fixture. Let catalysts A_1,A_2,A_3 and substrates B_1,B_2,B_3 have edges

  E={(1,1),(2,1),(2,2),(3,2),(3,3)}.

For each j, include 0 <-> A_j at alpha_j=delta_j=1. On each edge include

  A_j <-> A_j+B_i,              p_ji=1, q_ji=2,
  A_j+B_i <-> 2A_j+B_i,         u_ji=v_ji=1, k_ji=1,

with stochastic mass-action propensities. This graph has no isolated
coordinates and every B_i has a nonempty catalyst neighborhood. Take
lambda_j=epsilon_i=1, R=6/5, K=2, and

  h_i(a)=1{sum_(j in N(i)) a_j=0},
  V(a,b)=sum_j a_j + sum_i R^(b_i)(1+h_i(a)) + 2 1{a=0}.

Here chi_j=delta_j-sum_(i~j) p_ji(R-1) is 4/5, 3/5, 4/5 for j=1,2,3,
respectively, so the small-gain margin is strict. The boundary weight also
satisfies K>(H+1)/alpha_sum=4/3, where H=3 and alpha_sum=3.

Directly summing reaction increments gives the general identity

 LV = H - sum_j lambda_j delta_j a_j
    + sum_(j,i) [p_ji(R_i-1)a_j R_i^(b_i)
                -q_ji(1-R_i^-1)a_j b_i R_i^(b_i)
                +lambda_j k_ji u_ji a_j b_i
                -lambda_j k_ji v_ji(a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i]
    -K alpha_sum 1{a=0} + K sum_j delta_j 1{a=e_j}
    + sum_i epsilon_i R_i^(b_i)[-alpha(N(i))1{h_i=1}
       +sum_(j in N(i))delta_j 1{a restricted to N(i)=e_j}].

This displays the only support-switch surcharge: death of the last local
catalyst. On that indicator, the same catalyst has a_j=1, so the negative
substrate-removal term on that edge is
-q_ji(1-R_i^-1)b_i R_i^(b_i); it dominates the O(R_i^(b_i)) surcharge for
large b_i. If the local support is absent instead, catalyst immigration
contributes -epsilon_i alpha(N(i))R_i^(b_i). For a large catalyst with all
adjacent b_i=0, the strict chi_j margin gives negative linear drift. If an
adjacent b_i is bounded and positive, the reverse autocatalytic term has
degree k_ji+1>=2 in a_j and dominates the positive terms that are linear
in a_j. If both catalyst and substrate counts grow, the q-removal term
dominates the positive edge terms. These cases cover the mixed escape
patterns tested here; they are not inferred from a finite grid.

The independent script verify_independent.py builds the CTMC reaction list
and computes LV by direct V(y)-V(x) increments using exact fractions. It
compares that answer with the displayed formula on all 4^6=4096 states
with every count in {0,1,2,3}; all identities match exactly. It separately
checks seven mixed sequences at scales n=4,8,16,32. At n=16 and n=32 all
seven have LV<0. The sequences include an inactive high B_1 with unrelated
high A_3, a sole-neighbor last-catalyst death switch at high B_2, two active
neighbors at high B_2, high A_2 with a bounded positive neighboring B_1,
high A_2 with zero neighboring substrates, simultaneous high A_2/B_2,
and A=0 with all three substrates high. At n=4 and n=8 not all selected
states have negative drift, as expected for a finite Foster region.

This exact finite identity check is not an all-state theorem. The asymptotic
case split above and the common compact-box drift argument in
outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle4/leaves/sparse_switch_adversarial/AUDIT.txt
provide the broader candidate proof for this paired-edge family. Under
that drift, the uniform finite-path minorization in
outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle4/leaves/uniform_mixing/ROOT_AUDIT.txt
gives uniform V-geometric convergence over compact positive rate boxes on
each active irreducible class, conditional on the internal drift proof.
Neither result applies to general WR networks.

5. Scope boundary, costs, and falsifiers
---------------------------------------
The N77 construction is the decisive counterexample for the across-class
uniform-tightness claim. It is a failure of a uniform statement over
stochastic class labels, not a failure of an individual class's stationary
law. The sparse paired-edge support-switch theorem is a positive result for
a different reaction architecture; it does not subsume N77, whose channels
do not include the paired catalyst birth/death architecture and whose
classes are separated by a nonlinear label.

Exact derivation cost is constant-size algebra per class: three phase
rates, three reciprocal phase weights, and one Poisson ratio. Uniform
non-tightness follows from A>=n without simulation. The two scripts are
dependency-free Python: verify_n77_box.py checks all 32 corners plus the
center of [1,2]^5 for n=0,...,30 (3,069 exact phase-flux identities and
21,483 Poisson balance identities); verify_independent.py checks 4,096
support-switch states plus 28 selected sequence endpoints. These are
finite corroborations, not replacements for the symbolic proofs.

Falsifiers: any enabled channel from a displayed phase leaving Gamma_n, a
nonzero phase-flux residual w_i q_i-w_(i+1)q_(i+1), or a failure of the
Poisson local-balance identity would invalidate the stationary family. For
the switch test, any disagreement between direct CTMC increments and the
formula is a sign/propensity error; the rational grid found none. A genuine
within-fixed-class counterexample would need unbounded support inside one
irreducible class and a rate sequence in one compact positive box whose
stationary mass escapes (or a proven failure of common geometric rates).
No such example was found here. Whether arbitrary WR networks admit such
within-class failures remains UNKNOWN from this search.
