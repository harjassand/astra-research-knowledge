FINITE-H TASK QUOTIENTS, AND WHY HANKEL TAIL IS NOT TASK RISK
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact finite-task quotient theorem and exact four-hidden-state counterexample proved; no novelty, external review, or acquisition advantage claimed.

Question and interface
----------------------
An HMM's Hankel rank is the dimension of a real linear realization of its
joint word probabilities. It is not automatically the number of predictor
states needed for a chosen loss. This note defines a finite-horizon,
task-dependent state-compression target directly from conditional future
laws, proves its KL projection identity, and gives a counterexample to
turning a small unnormalized Hankel singular tail into worst-history task
risk. It does not repeat a finite-rank witness-horizon or realization
theorem.

Let Sigma be a finite output alphabet. Fix a context depth m and a future
task horizon H. A raw context is h in Sigma^m with pi(h)=Pr[X_1...X_m=h]>0;
the future task outcome F is X_(m+1)...X_(m+H) in Sigma^H. Write

    p_h(x)=Pr[F=x | X_1...X_m=h],   x in Sigma^H.

The HMM transition, emission, hidden state, predictive basis, and all p_h
are unknown to the learner. The mathematical quotient below is defined
from the observable law. The acquisition baseline uses only independent
complete raw strings of length m+H and sees every symbol. It receives no
conditional-law, hidden-state, model-entry, or basis oracle.

Theorem 1: exact finite-H predictive quotient and KL projection
--------------------------------------------------------------
Choose any distribution pi on the positive-probability contexts (the
natural choice is their process probability). A K-label predictive
compression consists of a deterministic encoder c:Sigma^m -> {1,...,K}
and one decoder law q_z in Delta(Sigma^H) per label. Its task loss is
expected conditional KL

    L(c,q)=sum_h pi(h) KL(p_h || q_(c(h))).

For each nonempty cell C={h:c(h)=z}, define its mass and barycenter by

    pi(C)=sum_(h in C) pi(h),
    p_C(x)=sum_(h in C) pi(h)p_h(x)/pi(C).

Then the exact identity

    L(c,q) = sum_C pi(C) I(Hist;F | c(Hist)=C)
             + sum_C pi(C) KL(p_C || q_C)

holds. Consequently the unique loss-minimizing decoder on each cell is
q_C=p_C, and the optimal K-label quotient risk is

    D_K^(m,H) = min_(partitions C of supported contexts, |C|<=K)
                  sum_C pi(C) I(Hist;F | c(Hist)=C).

The number of predictive labels needed for average-KL tolerance epsilon is
K_avgKL^(m,H)(epsilon)=min{K: D_K^(m,H)<=epsilon}.

This finite optimization is a rank-free truncation target for the stated
task: it depends on the chosen history distribution, future horizon, loss,
and allowed label count, not on the Hankel rank. It is exact as an
information projection, not an efficient acquisition algorithm.

If pi is a task weighting fixed by the application, it is supplied as part
of the loss definition. If pi(h)=Pr[h] is the process-induced context law,
the same raw histories estimate it; it is not a free conditional oracle.

Proof. Fix a cell C. Expand the left-hand contribution:

    sum_(h in C) pi(h) KL(p_h||q_C)
      = sum_(h in C) pi(h) sum_x p_h(x) log p_h(x)
        - pi(C) sum_x p_C(x) log q_C(x).

Add and subtract pi(C) sum_x p_C(x) log p_C(x). This gives

    sum_(h in C) pi(h) KL(p_h||p_C)
        + pi(C) KL(p_C||q_C).

The first term divided by pi(C) is the conditional mutual information
between the history and H-step future inside that cell. Summing cells
proves the identity. KL nonnegativity makes q_C=p_C optimal, with equality
for any other q_C only when q_C=p_C on the support. Minimizing over the
finite set of partitions with at most K cells proves the D_K formula.

At zero loss, histories may share a cell exactly when their H-step future
laws agree. Thus D_K=0 iff K is at least the number of distinct p_h among
positive-mass contexts. For nonzero loss, D_K is the precise average-KL
complexity curve. It may favor merging rare contexts; that is correct for
this pi-weighted objective and may be wrong for an application demanding a
uniform guarantee.

Worst-context alternative. For uniform-history total variation, define

    N_TV^(m,H)(epsilon) = min{|Q|: Q subset Delta(Sigma^H),
        for every supported h some q in Q has TV(p_h,q)<=epsilon}.

This is exactly the minimum number of decoder laws needed for a
worst-context TV guarantee: a cover assigns each history to one center; in
the other direction, any K-label decoder with worst loss at most epsilon
places its K output laws at the centers of such a cover. The finite
context set guarantees the cover exists and its size is at most
|Sigma|^m. For d=|Sigma|^H future outcomes, quantizing each probability
coordinate into bins of width epsilon/d gives the explicit loose bound
N_TV<=min{|Sigma|^m,(ceil(d/epsilon)+1)^d}. A finite task list
G={g_1,...,g_s}, 0<=g_j<=1, has the analogous
quotient by the prediction vectors
(E[g_j(F)|h])_(j=1)^s and its l-infinity covering number. These quantities
can differ sharply from linear Hankel rank.

The quotient is finite-H and task-specific. It does not necessarily define
a recurrent stochastic machine: a cell can split after an observation, so
the partition need not be closed under updates. A deployment claim that
stores only the label and updates it indefinitely needs a separate
congruence/closure condition and must charge the update rule. No such claim
is made here.

Counterexample: a vanishing finite Hankel tail with constant conditional risk
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix 0<p<=1/4. Consider a four-hidden-state, time-homogeneous HMM with
hidden states C_mark, R_mark, C_tail, R_tail. Initially the hidden state is
C_mark with probability 1-p and R_mark with probability p. C_mark emits
marker b and transitions to C_tail; R_mark emits marker a and transitions
to R_tail. C_tail is absorbing and emits 0. R_tail is absorbing and emits
0 with probability 1/4 and 1 with probability 3/4 at every step. The
alphabet is {a,b,0,1}. No hidden state is observed.

Take the legal Hankel submatrix M with row prefixes b,a and one-symbol
suffixes 0,1; its entries are raw joint prefix probabilities f(uv):

                       suffix 0       suffix 1
    prefix b             1-p              0
    prefix a              p/4           3p/4

These are ordinary raw strings, not a supplied basis or matrix-entry
oracle; a retaining learner estimates the four cells from its complete
length-two paths.

Thus M=[[1-p,0],[p/4,3p/4]]. Its best rank-one Frobenius approximation M_1
is the leading singular component. By Eckart-Young its residual is the
smallest singular value, and

    ||M-M_1||_F = sigma_2(M)
      = |det(M)|/sigma_1(M)
      = (1-p)(3p/4)/sigma_1(M) <= 3p/4,

because sigma_1(M)>=1-p. The finite Hankel tail therefore tends to zero
linearly with p. Yet det(M)=3p(1-p)/4>0, so this block has exact rank two
for every p>0. The full process Hankel rank is exactly three for every
p>0. The minor with rows (empty,b,a) and columns (empty,0,1) is

    [[1,   0,     0],
     [1-p, 1-p,   0],
     [p,   p/4, 3p/4]],

whose determinant is 3p(1-p)/4. Conversely every nonempty history begins
with a or b and thereafter lies in one of the two absorbing tail laws, so
all Hankel rows lie in the span of the empty row and those two tail rows.
Thus the full linear rank remains three as p tends to zero.

At depth m=1 and future horizon H=1, the exact predictive
quotient also has two classes: p_a=(1/4,3/4) and p_b=(1,0) differ. Under
natural context weights pi(a)=p and pi(b)=1-p, the one-label KL quotient
has barycenter p_C=(1-3p/4,3p/4) and risk

    D_1 = p KL(Bern(3/4)||Bern(3p/4))
          +(1-p) log(1/(1-3p/4)) -> 0 as p -> 0.

By contrast, the best one-label worst-context TV radius is exactly 3/8,
because TV(p_a,p_b)=3/4. Thus even within one fixed rank-two family, the
number of states for a specified average loss can collapse while the
worst-context state requirement remains two.

Now use the natural conditional decoder from this joint block: normalize
the approximating row M_1[a,:] to predict the next symbol after history a.
This row is nonzero. To bound its prediction, write

    M^T M = [[A,C],[C,D]],
    A=(1-p)^2+p^2/16, C=3p^2/16, D=9p^2/16.

Let v=(v_0,v_1) be the top right singular vector with positive coordinates,
and let t=v_1/v_0. Its top eigenvalue lambda_1 obeys lambda_1>=A, hence

    t = C/(lambda_1-D) <= C/(A-D).

For p<=1/4, A-D=(1-p)^2-p^2/2 >= 1/2, so
t<=3p^2/8<=3/128<1/32. Since M_1 is rank one, its row for a is a
positive scalar multiple of v^T. The normalized rank-one prediction of 1
after a is therefore

    q_1(1|a)=v_1/(v_0+v_1)<=t<1/32.

The true conditional law is Bernoulli(3/4). For binary laws TV is the
absolute difference of their probabilities of 1, so

    TV( P(X_(m+1)|a), q_1(.|a) ) >= 3/4-1/32 = 23/32.

For every p>0, a is a reachable history. Therefore a finite legal
Hankel-block approximation has tail at most 3p/4 -> 0 while its
row-normalized, rank-one conditional prediction has worst-history TV at
least 23/32. No function of this unnormalized Frobenius tail that tends to
zero can universally upper-bound this conditional task risk. The missing
factor is context mass: the joint row is O(p), while conditioning divides
by p. In general, if a joint row r has mass p and an approximation rhat
has ||r-rhat||_1<=eta<p/2, then normalization only gives the coarse bound
TV(r/p,rhat/||rhat||_1)<=eta/p. A positive mass floor is needed to turn
joint-probability error into uniform conditional error.

This counterexample concerns a finite Hankel submatrix and a specified
rank-one SVD projection followed by row normalization. It does not claim
that every rank-one predictive model fails every task: a predictor chosen
directly for the single history a could output Bernoulli(3/4). The result
is that a small unnormalized spectral tail alone does not certify that a
generic low-rank projection preserved the conditional task. The task
quotient above states what the complexity target must depend on.

Raw-data acquisition and same-data full-path baseline
-----------------------------------------------------
For the displayed process, each iid raw trajectory of length two is a
complete pair (marker, tail symbol). With N trajectories, the full-path
baseline stores all pairs, estimates the joint table, and obtains
P(1|a) as N_(a1)/(N_(a0)+N_(a1)). It sees the same data as any learner that
forms M and truncates it; a retaining baseline can simulate that learner's
compression and has no less information. The SVD construction above is
not an acquisition advantage. It is a counterexample to the loss
interpretation of the tail.

The rarity cost is real. Compare the same four-state family with R_tail
emitting Bernoulli(3/4) versus Bernoulli(1/4). Both have context mass p.
One length-two trajectory has KL

    p KL(Bern(3/4)||Bern(1/4)) = (p/2) log 3.

Le Cam's bound requires Omega(1/p) full raw trajectories for constant-error
distinguishing. Conversely, the retaining baseline sees about Np rare
contexts; conditional Bernoulli concentration estimates P(1|a) to error
epsilon with N=O((p epsilon^2)^(-1) log(1/delta)) trajectories when p is
known or lower-bounded, including the separate binomial event of seeing no
rare context. Compression cannot remove this information requirement.

For a general finite context/future table, let M=|Sigma|^m and d=|Sigma|^H.
With N iid raw strings of length m+H and a minimum raw context probability
Pr[Hst=h]>=p_min on contexts whose predictions must be certified, count
histories and futures from the complete strings. This is a data-support
condition separate from any externally selected task weight pi. A union
bound and binomial lower-tail bound give
uniform TV error epsilon with probability at least 1-delta for

    N >= max{ (8/p_min) log(2M/delta),
              (d^2/(4 p_min epsilon^2)) log(4Md/delta) }.

Indeed, Chernoff gives all context counts at least N p_min/2 except with
probability delta/2 under the first term. Conditional on those counts,
Hoeffding with coordinate error 2 epsilon/d and a union bound over Md
context/future cells gives total-variation error at most epsilon except
with probability delta/2 under the second term.

This is a deliberately simple histogram bound. It charges every symbol
(N(m+H)), every context/future counter (O(Md log N) bits), and all
conditional-law entries (O(Md) arithmetic to form). For KL quotient risk,
estimating rare future cells also needs a positive probability floor or a
separately analyzed smoothing rule; otherwise small TV error need not
control KL. A decoder with K labels stores O(Kd) probabilities plus
O(M log K) bits of context labels; at b bits per probability its numeric
payload is O(Kdb) bits. Exhaustively finding the best partition
may require examining up to sum_(j=1)^K S(M,j) assignments, where S(M,j)
is a Stirling number of the second kind; the information projection is
not an efficient learner by itself.

The fair baseline is the same-data complete-path learner, not a learner
given the Hankel block for free. It can retain all N strings, compute the
same joint table, select the same SVD directions, or directly estimate the
task conditionals. Any claim of lower sample use, faster runtime, smaller
storage, or reusable state needs its own comparator and cost ledger.

Interpretation and failure gates
--------------------------------
* Hankel rank is linear dimension of unnormalized word probabilities.
  The quotient risk D_K is an average-KL rate-distortion curve over a
  specified context distribution; N_TV is a worst-context covering
  number. None is determined by the others without additional margins or
  structural assumptions.
* Small joint Hankel tail is compatible with constant conditional error
  when the approximated row has small probability. Average-KL may still be
  small because it weights that row by p; worst-history TV does not.
* No minimum context mass means no uniform conditional sampling guarantee.
  No future-cell floor means no generic KL continuity guarantee from TV.
* A finite-H quotient is not a finite-state update law. An online predictor
  needs update closure, and raw observations do not supply closure for free.
* The same-data full-path baseline weakly dominates any representation-only
  statistic in information. A representation may save storage or
  computation, but this note proves no net acquisition win.

The counterexample is a falsifier for any proposed theorem asserting that
unnormalized finite Hankel Frobenius tail alone controls maximum conditional
TV risk after standard rank-d SVD truncation and normalization. A valid
repair must include an explicit history-mass/conditioning term, use a
task-weighted loss such as D_K, or certify a task-specific predictive cover.
It does not falsify the exact finite-H quotient theorem, which takes the
target loss and its weights as inputs.

Selective primary-source prior art
----------------------------------
* Carlyle and Paz, “Realizations by Stochastic Finite Automata,” JCSS 5(1),
  26-40 (1971), doi:10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3. Finite Hankel rank
  characterizes finite-dimensional real-weighted word realizations; this
  is linear realization complexity, not a target-loss quotient.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000071800053
* Littman, Sutton, and Singh, “Predictive Representations of State,”
  NeurIPS 14 (2001). Predictive state representations use predictions of
  future tests as coordinates; they motivate task-dependent prediction
  vectors, but do not prove the finite weighted-KL partition identity here.
  https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2001/file/1e4d36177d71bbb3558e43af9577d70e-Paper.pdf
* Shalizi and Crutchfield, “Computational Mechanics: Pattern and
  Prediction, Structure and Simplicity,” Journal of Statistical Physics
  104 (2001), 817-879. Their causal states identify histories with equal
  entire conditional futures and establish exact predictive minimality;
  this note instead gives a finite-H, loss-weighted approximate quotient.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907176
* Hsu, Kakade, and Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden
  Markov Models,” COLT 2009; arXiv:0811.4413. This is an iid raw-observation
  spectral baseline under singular-value conditions. It does not make
  unnormalized matrix error a uniform conditional-risk guarantee.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413
* Tishby, Pereira, and Bialek, “The Information Bottleneck Method,”
  Proceedings of the 37th Allerton Conference (1999), pp. 368-377.
  Their target-relevant compression objective is close prior art for a
  task-weighted quotient. The finite deterministic partition and the
  conditional-KL barycenter identity used here are derived explicitly
  above.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057

The KL barycenter identity is proved directly above; it is standard
information-projection algebra, not a novelty claim. No external proof
review, empirical validation, or priority claim is made.
