# Cycle 2: independent referee of L02 operator Jensen, exposure, and threshold transfer

**Verdict.** I find no counterexample in the requested load-bearing steps. The zero-spectrum Jensen inequality follows from a positive resolvent integral after an ε-shift, and the ε→0 passage is in operator norm. The mixed-exposure law is normalized by the transported vector norm; omitting that norm would be wrong. The component barycenter, KL drift, selection argument, ceiling rounding, and the constants 2048/1024 also check. This is an independent reconstruction of these steps from the complete L02 proof source, not external or formal validation of the whole theorem.

## 1. Target interface and exact claim

Let G be a finite two-player game with finite question sets X,Y, answer sets A,B, question law μ, and Boolean predicate V. A commuting strategy is a unit vector ψ on any Hilbert space H and local POVMs M_x^a,N_y^b with [M_x^a,N_y^b]=0 for every x,y,a,b. Write v=ωᶜᵒ(G), d=|A||B|.

On a positive-mass component j of the pair support, let μ_j be the conditional edge law. On L²(μ_j), P_X and P_Y are conditional-expectation projections onto functions of the respective local question; define P_j=(P_X+P_Y)/2 and let σ_j be the gap of I−P_j away from constants. Let σ=min_j σ_j, with σ=1 if every component is a singleton. The source target, for v<1, 0<δ<1−v and every k, is

    Pr[W_k ≥ ceil((v+δ)k)]
      ≤ exp(−σ δ³ k / (2048(1+ln d))).

For connected support the denominator improves to 1024(1+ln d). The strategy may use arbitrary joint POVMs on complete question and answer strings. No tensor-product factorization across repetitions is assumed.

## 2. Resolvent maps and the zero-spectrum limit

For a bounded positive T on H, define F_T:H→L²((0,∞),ds;H) by

    (F_T h)(s) = T(T+sI)⁻¹ h.

For h∈H, the spectral theorem and Tonelli for nonnegative integrands give

    ||F_T h||² = ∫ ⟨h,T²(T+sI)⁻²h⟩ ds = ⟨h,Th⟩.

Thus F_T is everywhere-defined and bounded, with F_T*F_T=T. When 0≤T≤I it is a contraction. This is a vector-valued L² map; no eigenbasis, finite rank, or lower spectral bound is being used.

The bounded column needed for transport is

    (V_T h)(s) = T^(1/2)(T+sI)⁻¹ h.

For each fixed h, the map in s is strongly measurable and

    ||V_T h||² = ∫ ⟨h,T(T+sI)⁻²h⟩ ds = ||1_(0,∞)(T) h||².

So V_T is a bounded partial isometry on all of H and V_T*V_T=P_T, the support projection of T. Also F_T=V_T T^(1/2). The operator-norm bound on the integrand of V_T need not be square-integrable near s=0; the valid argument is vectorwise spectral integration. This distinction matters when the spectrum accumulates at zero.

### Jensen inequality

Take a finite positive ensemble A_l≥0 with q_l>0, sum_l q_l=1, and B=sum_l q_l A_l. Put D_l=A_l−B. First assume A_l,B≥εI. Write R_l(s)=(A_l+sI)⁻¹ and R_B(s)=(B+sI)⁻¹. The resolvent identity, with its order retained, is

    R_l = R_B − R_B D_l R_B + R_B D_l R_l D_l R_B.

It is verified by multiplying by A_l+sI; it does not require A_l to commute with B. Since sum_l q_l D_l=0 and
f(t)=t ln t=∫₀∞[t/(1+s)−t/(t+s)]ds,
functional calculus gives

    sum_l q_l f(A_l) − f(B)
      = ∫₀∞ s sum_l q_l R_B D_l R_l D_l R_B ds.                 (J1)

The integrand is positive: R_B D_l R_l D_l R_B is a positive congruence. For each s,

    F_A_l(s)−F_B(s) = s(R_B−R_l),
    (F_A_l(s)−F_B(s))² = s² R_B D_l R_l² D_l R_B.

Since s R_l² ≤ R_l, the Gram integrand is bounded above by the integrand in (J1), term by term. Integration yields

    sum_l q_l (F_A_l−F_B)*(F_A_l−F_B)
      ≤ sum_l q_l f(A_l)−f(B).                                  (J2)

For the general bounded case, replace every A_l by A_l+εI and B by B+εI. The weights still give B+εI=sum_l q_l(A_l+εI), and D_l is unchanged. For fixed ε>0, the integrands in (J1) are O(s) near zero and O(s⁻²) at infinity in operator norm, so the identities and integrals are norm-convergent.

To remove the shift, for every scalar t≥0,

    ||F_(t+ε)−F_t||²
      = ε² ∫₀∞ s²/[(t+ε+s)²(t+s)²] ds
      ≤ ε² ∫₀∞ (ε+s)⁻² ds = ε.

By functional calculus this is a bound on the operator between the common L² codomains:
||F_(T+εI)−F_T||≤√ε. The finite sums of Gram operators in (J2) therefore converge in norm. Since f(t)=t ln t, f(0)=0, is continuous on [0,2], the right side also converges in norm for effects. Letting ε↓0 proves (J2) when zero belongs to the spectrum, including continuous spectrum accumulating at zero. In particular, restricting to supp B and treating B⁻¹ as bounded would be invalid, but this proof does not do that.

### Bounded transport and algebra membership

Let A be a von Neumann algebra on H, T∈A an effect, and positive G_a∈A with sum_a G_a=T. Then 0≤G_a≤T. On ran(T^(1/2)), define

    q_a(T^(1/2)u,T^(1/2)v)=⟨u,G_a v⟩.

If T^(1/2)u=0 then G_a^(1/2)u=0, so the form is well-defined. Cauchy–Schwarz and G_a≤T give |q_a(ξ,ζ)|≤||ξ||||ζ||. It extends to P_T H and defines a positive contraction C_a supported on P_T, with

    G_a=T^(1/2) C_a T^(1/2),     sum_a C_a=P_T.

This proves factorization without using an unbounded inverse. For R_ε=T^(1/2)(T+εI)⁻¹/², the regularizations satisfy

    (T+εI)⁻¹/² G_a (T+εI)⁻¹/² = R_ε C_a R_ε ∈ A,
    0≤R_ε C_a R_ε≤I,        R_ε → P_T strongly.

The strong limit is C_a because C_a=P_T C_a P_T. Thus C_a∈A by strong closure. The convergence need not be norm convergence; it remains valid with zero as an accumulation point.

For D∈A′, every coefficient T^(1/2)(T+sI)⁻¹ commutes with D, so V_T D=(I⊗D)V_T. Hence

    Q_a=V_T C_a V_T* + 1_(a=a*) (I−V_TV_T*)

commutes with I⊗A′, and therefore lies in B(L²) von Neumann-tensored with A. The Q_a are positive and sum to I; direct substitution using V_T*V_T=P_T gives F_T*Q_aF_T=G_a. If T=0, all C_a=0 and Q assigns the default outcome a*.

For commuting algebras A,B on H, transport Alice with a fresh L²_s factor and Bob with a distinct L²_t factor. Their effects lie in B(L²_s)⊗I_t⊗A and I_s⊗B(L²_t)⊗B. These algebras commute for all inputs because the ancillas are distinct and A commutes with B. If T,G_a∈A and S,K_b∈B, the common unnormalized vector

    Ψ_(T,S)(s,t)=T(T+sI)⁻¹ S(S+tI)⁻¹ψ

has norm²=⟨ψ,TSψ⟩, and successive pullbacks give

    ⟨Ψ_(T,S),Q_a R_b Ψ_(T,S)⟩ = ⟨ψ,G_a K_b ψ⟩.

Thus the construction preserves the joint probability, not merely each marginal. It does not identify commuting strategies with tensor-product strategies.

## 3. Mixed exposure: exact law and error budget

Fix a tested set C⊊[k], event E_C that all coordinates in C are won, p=Pr(E_C)>0, and m=k−|C|. Put d_C=d^|C|, L=ln(d_C/p), τ=L/m. Order the live coordinates. Expose

    Ω_j=(X_C,Y_C,X_1,…,X_j,Y_(j+1),…,Y_m),   j=0,…,m,

under the original product law μ^⊗k. Conditional on Ω_j, the two full question strings are independent: at each live coordinate one side is fixed and its mate is drawn from the corresponding conditional law, independently across coordinates. No answer conditioning has yet been imposed.

For tested answer strings α,γ, let M_X^α and N_Y^γ be the repeated-strategy effects summed over all remaining answers with these tested answers. Their conditional averages F_j^α=E[M_X^α|Ω_j] and H_j^γ=E[N_Y^γ|Ω_j] remain in their original commuting algebras. Let θ(r,α,γ) be the indicator that these tested answers win C. Write χ(A,H)=⟨ψ,AHψ⟩. Conditional independence gives the exact identity

    E_Ωj sum_(α,γ) θ χ(F_j^α,H_j^γ) = p.                       (E1)

At mode i, set r₀=(X_C,Y_C,X_<i,Y_>i); it is independent of the fresh pair z=(x,y). Given r₀ and x, define F_x^α=E[M_X^α|r₀,X_i=x]; given r₀ and y define H_y^γ=E[N_Y^γ|r₀,Y_i=y]. Then F_y^α=E_(x|y)F_x^α and H_x^γ=E_(y|x)H_y^γ. For r=(r₀,α,γ), set

    w_i(r)=Pr(r₀) θ(r,α,γ).

This weight is intentionally unnormalized and uses counting measure over α,γ. The exact conditional law is

    Pr(r,x,y | E_C)
      = w_i(r) μ(x,y) ||Ψ_(F_x^α,H_y^γ)||² / p.                (E2)

Indeed ||Ψ_(F_x,H_y)||²=χ(F_x,H_y), which is exactly the tested-answer mass after averaging the remaining questions. Summing the numerator in (E2) gives p by (E1). Normalizing w_i alone would discard the strategy-dependent tested-answer likelihood. For finer coordinate answers a,b, effects G_x^(α,a),K_y^(γ,b) sum to F_x^α,H_y^γ. The joint pullback above reproduces ⟨ψ,G_x^(α,a)K_y^(γ,b)ψ⟩ exactly. Dividing by ||Ψ||² yields the conditional fine-answer law when this norm is nonzero; zero norm carries no probability.

The exposure potentials are controlled by scalar spectral Jensen, without a density matrix or trace. For effects F,H from the two commuting algebras, A↦χ(A,H)=⟨H^(1/2)ψ,A H^(1/2)ψ⟩ is a normal positive functional on Alice's algebra. Let g=χ(I,H)≤1 and h=χ(F,H). The spectral measure of F in vector H^(1/2)ψ and convexity of t ln t imply

    −χ(F ln F,H) ≤ h ln(g/h) ≤ −h ln h,

with the value zero at h=0. Since −t ln t is concave, there are d_C tested answer pairs and their total flagged mass averages to p; Jensen gives

    −pL ≤ U_j ≤ 0,       −pL ≤ Z_j ≤ 0,
    U_j=E sum θ χ(F_j ln F_j,H_j),
    Z_j=E sum θ χ(F_j,H_j ln H_j).                              (E3)

For example, at coordinate i, after reindexing conditional averages,

    U_i−U_(i−1)
      = E_(r₀) sum_(α,γ) θ sum_y μ_Y(y)
          χ(E_(x|y)[F_x^α ln F_x^α]−F_y^α ln F_y^α, H_y^γ) ≥ 0,

    Z_(i−1)−Z_i
      = E_(r₀) sum_(α,γ) θ sum_x μ_X(x)
          χ(F_x^α, E_(y|x)[H_y^γ ln H_y^γ]−H_x^γ ln H_x^γ) ≥ 0.

Apply (J2) to F_x at fixed y, then evaluate the positive Gram inequality under χ(·,H_y); it bounds E||Ψ_(F_x,H_y)−Ψ_(F_y,H_y)||² by the first potential increment. The reversed application bounds E||Ψ_(F_x,H_y)−Ψ_(F_x,H_x)||² by the second. Telescoping (E3) over i gives total squared-error budget 2pL. With w_ir=w_i(r)/m, vectors f_ir(z)=Ψ_(F_x,H_y), a_ir(x)=Ψ_(F_x,H_x), and b_ir(y)=Ψ_(F_y,H_y), this yields

    sum_(i,r,z) w_ir μ(z)||f_ir(z)||² = p,
    sum_(i,r,z) w_ir μ(z)(||f_ir(z)−a_ir(x)||²+||f_ir(z)−b_ir(y)||²) ≤ 2pτ,
    sum_(i,r,z) w_ir μ(z)⟨f_ir(z),W_ir(z)f_ir(z)⟩ = p sbar.     (E4)

Here sbar is the average live-coordinate win probability conditioned on E_C. W_ir(z)=sum_(a,b) V(z,a,b)Q_(r,x)^aR_(r,y)^b is an effect: it is a sub-sum of the positive commuting POVM products.

## 4. Component barycenter and conditioning drift

On each connected support component, P_j=(P_X+P_Y)/2 has constants as its fixed space. Conditional expectation is the least-squares projection onto local functions. Apply the scalar Poincare inequality to each vector coordinate in the finite-dimensional span of the finitely many values of f_ir; the ambient Hilbert space itself may be infinite-dimensional. For any local anchors a(x),b(y),

    σ_j Var_(μ_j)(f)
      ≤ (E||f−P_X f||²+E||f−P_Y f||²)/2
      ≤ E(||f−a(x)||²+||f−b(y)||²)/2.

Let λ_j=μ(component j), β_ir,j=E_(μ_j)f_ir, and define

    P_j=sum_ir w_ir E_(μ_j)||f_ir||²,
    E_j=sum_ir w_ir Var_(μ_j)(f_ir),
    P̂_j=sum_ir w_ir||β_ir,j||²,
    T_j=sum_ir w_ir E_(μ_j)⟨f_ir,W_ir f_ir⟩,
    T̂_j=sum_ir w_ir E_(μ_j)⟨β_ir,j,W_ir β_ir,j⟩.

Then sum_j λ_jP_j=p, sum_j λ_jT_j=p sbar, P̂_j=P_j−E_j, and E:=sum_j λ_jE_j≤pτ/σ.

For P̂_j>0, the vector

    P̂_j^(−1/2) sum_ir sqrt(w_ir)|ir>⊗β_ir,j

is a unit state independent of the fresh questions. Use the same finite flag register for both players, with block-diagonal POVMs Q_(r,x),R_(r,y). Every Alice block commutes with every Bob block by the transported-algebra argument; disjoint flag blocks multiply to zero. This is a legal commuting strategy for the component game G_j, so T̂_j/P̂_j≤v_j=ωᶜᵒ(G_j), or T̂_j≤v_jP̂_j when P̂_j=0 as well. Since 0≤W≤I,

    |T_j−T̂_j| ≤ (sqrt(P_j)+sqrt(P̂_j))sqrt(E_j) ≤ 2sqrt(P_jE_j),
    T_j ≤ v_jP_j+2sqrt(P_jE_j).                                (B1)

The first line is the quadratic-form difference bound followed by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. Every positive-mass local question identifies its component. Tensoring near-optimal commuting representations for the finitely many component games gives a legal strategy for G, hence sum_j λ_jv_j≤v. This tensors distinct strategies; it does not factor Alice and Bob in the original representation.

For the conditional component drift, let Q be the law of the vector of live component labels J_1,…,J_m conditioned on E_C. From (E2), its average marginal is exactly

    λ̃_j := (1/m)sum_i Pr[J_i=j | E_C] = λ_jP_j/p.

Because E_C is an event of probability p, the density of Q with respect to λ^⊗m is at most 1/p; thus D(Q||λ^⊗m)≤ln(1/p). The chain rule gives sum_i D(Q_i||λ)≤D(Q||λ^⊗m). Convexity of relative entropy and Pinsker then give

    TV(λ̃,λ) ≤ sqrt(ln(1/p)/(2m)).

Using 0≤v_j≤1, (B1), and Cauchy–Schwarz across components,

    sbar ≤ v + sqrt(ln(1/p)/(2m)) + 2sqrt(τ/σ).                (B2)

The first error is the genuine disconnected-component drift; it is zero for connected support.

## 5. Selection, ceiling rounding, and constants

For any coordinate i, fix the other question pairs and sum the repeated POVM over all other answer coordinates. This gives complete one-round POVMs; every Alice/Bob pair still commutes because the original string effects commute for every full input. Hence each coordinate's marginal success is at most v, and E W_k≤vk. If v=0 this makes every win indicator zero almost surely. Assume v>0. Fix q=v+δ, q₀=v+δ/2, and let p_H=Pr[W_k≥ceil(qk)]. If p_H=0 there is nothing to prove. For any ε∈(0,1), put

    ℓ=ceil(ln(4/(εp_H))/ln(q/q₀)).

Draw ℓ independent uniform indices from [k], with replacement, and let C be their distinct set. For fixed realized outcomes with W wins, the probability every sampled index is a win is (W/k)^ℓ. Therefore

    E_draw a_C = E(W/k)^ℓ ≥ p_H q^ℓ = A₀,
    E_draw b_C = E[1_(W<q₀k)(W/k)^ℓ] ≤ q₀^ℓ ≤ εA₀/4,

where E_draw is over the independent uniform index draws, a_C=Pr(E_C), and b_C=Pr(E_C,W<q₀k). The last inequality is exactly the definition of ℓ. Sets with a_C<A₀/2 contribute at most A₀/2 to E_draw a_C; sets with b_C>εa_C contribute at most E_draw b_C/ε≤A₀/4. Hence some fixed C satisfies

    |C|≤ℓ,       p_C=a_C≥A₀/2,       Pr(W<q₀k | E_C)≤ε.        (S)

Now set ε=η=δ/16, r=ln(q/q₀), R=ln(d/q), T₀=σδ²/128, and c=δT₀/[16(1+ln d)]. Since q<1, σ≤1, and δ<1,

    r ≥ δ/(2q),       q ln(1/q)≤1/e,
    c/r ≤ T₀/8 ≤ η/2,
    c(1+R/r) ≤ 3T₀/16.

For the last inequality, use δ(1+R/r)≤δ+2q ln d+2q ln(1/q)≤3(1+ln d). Put t=1+ln(4/η)/r and B₀=ln2+Rt. Choose an integer

    k₀ ≥ max(2t/η, 16B₀/T₀).

Assume k≥k₀ and p_H≥exp(−ck). Then ℓ≤ck/r+t≤ηk, so C is proper and u=|C|/k≤η. By (S), on E_C the residual W−|C| is nonnegative and, outside a conditional event of probability at most η, W≥q₀k. Therefore

    E[W−|C| | E_C]/(k−|C|)
      ≥ (1−η)(q₀−u)/(1−u)
      ≥ (1−η)(q₀−η)/(1−η)
      = q₀−η = v+7δ/16.                                       (C1)

The middle inequality uses that (q₀−u)/(1−u) decreases in u because q₀<1. The tested-set probability in (S) gives

    ln(1/p_C)+|C|ln d
      ≤ ln(1/p_H)+ln2+ℓR
      ≤ ck(1+R/r)+B₀.

Since k−|C|≥(1−η)k, it follows that

    τ(C) ≤ [c(1+R/r)+B₀/k]/(1−η)
          ≤ T₀/[4(1−η)] < T₀.

Apply (B2) with m=k−|C|. The drift is at most sqrt(τ/2)≤sqrt(T₀/2)≤δ/16, and the variance term is strictly below 2sqrt(T₀/σ)=δ/sqrt(32). Their sum is <δ/4. This contradicts (C1), so p_H<exp(−ck) for k≥k₀. Here c=σδ³/[2048(1+ln d)].

For k<k₀, take N independent tensor copies of any fixed k-repetition commuting strategy on H^⊗N. Product questions, product state, and product block measurements make the N block outcomes independent. If every block has at least ceil(qk) wins, the total is at least N ceil(qk)≥ceil(qNk). Choose N with Nk≥k₀; the large-length result gives p_H^N≤exp(−cNk), hence p_H≤exp(−ck). This works for infinite-dimensional H because the commuting model is closed under finite tensor products. Taking suprema is uniform. For connected support, the drift in (B2) vanishes; choose T₀=σδ²/64 and the same transfer gives c=σδ³/[1024(1+ln d)]. Finally, the all-wins event is a subset of every threshold event with δ<1−v; letting δ↑1−v yields the stated all-wins endpoint.

## 6. Objections checked and status

- A bounded inverse on supp(T) or supp(B) is not assumed; it may fail when positive spectrum accumulates at zero.
- The F_T column limit is norm-convergent in the ε approximation, whereas R_ε→P_T is only strong in general. The proof uses each mode at the right topology.
- Mixed-exposure weights w_i are not a probability distribution. The missing mass is exactly ||Ψ||², and (E2) sums to p.
- The vector barycenter state uses a shared classical flag and is independent of fresh questions. The flag and transported POVMs are legal in the unrestricted commuting model.
- The likelihood-ratio bound is relative to λ^⊗m and yields exactly sqrt(ln(1/p)/(2m)); it cannot be dropped for disconnected support.
- The ceiling inequalities have the needed direction: W≥ceil(qk) implies W/k≥q, and N ceil(qk)≥ceil(qNk). The tested-set lower bound gives the same rounding-safe residual threshold q₀k−|C|.

No counterexample was found in these steps. The source-level result remains an internally proved candidate: this audit does not certify historical priority, a named verifier gap, sparse-PCP/Hamiltonian consequences, or formal/external validation.
