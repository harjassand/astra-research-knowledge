AN EXPLICIT RATIONAL GAUSSIAN COUNTEREXAMPLE TO UNIFORM RARE-HERALD NOISE ROBUSTNESS
Root derivation, 8 October 2026. INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE; independent checks pending; priority UNKNOWN.
This is a conditioning instability theorem, not a computational-hardness theorem and not a refutation of a specified N81 compiler instance.

THEOREM
Let R tend to infinity through integer squares, fix an integer a>=1, and set lambda=2^(-R^2), eta=1/R, nu=R^(-a). Start with a two-mode squeezed vacuum sqrt(1-lambda^2) sum_{n>=0}lambda^n |n>_S|n>_I and R-1 vacuum ancillas. Split idler I uniformly across R herald modes with a rational real passive interferometer. Apply identical pure loss of transmissivity eta to all R+1 modes. Herald exactly one photon in each of the R idler outputs; keep the signal S. Compare this ideal conditional signal sigma_R with the conditional signal tilde_sigma_R when independent phase-insensitive additive Gaussian noise of mean photon number nu is inserted in EVERY mode immediately before the loss.

Then sigma_R converges in trace norm to the diagonal Poisson(1) state, while tilde_sigma_R converges in trace norm to the vacuum. Consequently
    T(sigma_R,tilde_sigma_R) -> 1-exp(-1).
The ideal conditional signal mean photon number tends to 1. All inputs have rational covariances with polynomial description length. The noisy Gaussian input is strictly mixed. Both experiments have the same vacuum-loss contraction and, for every fixed p>1,
    Tr[(T*T)^p]=(R+1)R^(-p)->0.
Thus even inverse-polynomial all-mode noise need not preserve a rare binary-herald conditional law, despite bounded ideal conditional energy and every such vanishing power-spectrum certificate.

PROOF OF THE IDEAL CONDITIONAL LAW
Let N be the common input photon number, with geometric probability (1-lambda^2)lambda^(2N). Photon-count probabilities can be evaluated in this diagonal mixture: the herald is number diagonal, passive mixing conserves total number, and pure loss is phase covariant. Given N=n, exactly R idler photons survive with probability binom(n,R)eta^R(1-eta)^(n-R), and the uniform fanout assigns them one per mode with probability R!/R^R. Conditional signal loss is independent given N.

Put x=lambda^2(1-eta). The exact herald probability is
    p_R=(1-lambda^2)lambda^(2R) eta^R (R!/R^R) (1-x)^(-R-1).
Conditional on the herald, K=N-R has negative-binomial law
    P(K=k)=binom(R+k,R)(1-x)^(R+1)x^k.
The signal photon count conditional on K is Bin(R+K,eta). Coupling the first R Bernoulli trials shows
    T(sigma_R, diag Bin(R,1/R)) <= P(K>0)
        =1-(1-x)^(R+1) <=(R+1)x ->0.
The binomial law tends to Poisson(1) in total variation, hence the diagonal states converge in trace norm. Its exact mean is
    eta[R+(R+1)x/(1-x)] ->1.

PROOF OF THE NOISY CONDITIONAL LAW
The pure squeezed input is half-trace distance lambda from global vacuum; appending vacua and applying the passive splitter preserve this distance. Apply the same additive-noise-plus-loss channel to both inputs. Contractivity gives distance at most lambda between the actual noisy global output and a product of R+1 thermal states of mean t=eta nu=R^(-a-1).
For that product thermal reference, the binary herald has probability
    p_0=[t/(1+t)^2]^R,
and leaves the signal exactly thermal with mean t. Here
    log(lambda/p_0)=-R^2 log2+(a+1)R log R+2R log(1+t)->-infinity.
In particular lambda<p_0 eventually.

For two global states of trace distance delta, applying an instrument that retains the heralded output and records a scalar failure gives
    ||A-B||_1+|Tr A-Tr B|<=2delta.
Writing p=Tr A, q=Tr B therefore yields
    T(A/p,B/q)<=delta/p<=delta/(q-delta).
Use delta=lambda and q=p_0. Thus
    T(tilde_sigma_R, thermal(t)) <=lambda/(p_0-lambda)->0.
Since T(thermal(t),vacuum)=t/(1+t)->0, the noisy conditional state tends to vacuum. The reverse triangle inequality, together with the ideal limit, gives the displayed limiting gap.

RATIONAL DESCRIPTION AND COSTS
Write R=m^2. The uniform vector u=(1/m,...,1/m) has rational entries and norm one. The Householder matrix I-2vv^T/(v^Tv), v=e_1-u, is rational orthogonal and sends e_1 to u (R>1). The two-mode squeezed covariance has rational entries proportional to (1+lambda^2)/(1-lambda^2) and 2lambda/(1-lambda^2); lambda has R^2+1 binary denominator bits. The interferometer, loss amplitude sqrt(eta)=1/m, and additive covariance nu I are all rational. A dense covariance description and its bit arithmetic therefore have polynomial size/cost in R. No efficient generation of a successful herald is claimed. This is a rational channel/covariance description, not a claim that every optical Stinespring gate has rational amplitudes: the environment coupling sqrt(1-eta) may be algebraic, and a gate realization must charge its approximation precision.
The ideal herald is extraordinarily rare: log(1/p_R)=Theta(R^3), while log(1/p_0)=Theta(R log R). Preparing either conditional state by rejection charges its respective inverse herald probability. Retained conditional energy is not an acquisition-cost bound. The noise moves the event into a different, much more probable mechanism; unconditional states can remain arbitrarily close while normalized herald outputs differ by a constant.
