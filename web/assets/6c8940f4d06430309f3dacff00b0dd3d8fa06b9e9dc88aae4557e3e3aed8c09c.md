# Connected fixed-metric topological-entropy power counterexample

## Exact conclusion

The total-volume substitution `h_mu(phi_1) -> h_top(phi_1)` in the factor-four power bound fails even when the manifold is connected, the Riemannian metric is fixed and flat, and the steady divergence-free field is nowhere zero. On a fixed unit flat `T^3`, there are smooth fields `X_epsilon` and smooth residual strain-viscosity forces such that

```text
h_top(phi_1^epsilon) >= h_0 > 0      (same h_0 for every epsilon),
W_epsilon = O(nu epsilon^2) -> 0,
V = 1.
```

Thus for sufficiently small `epsilon`, `W_epsilon < 4 nu V h_top(phi_1^epsilon)^2`; indeed no positive constant times `nu V h_top^2` can lower-bound work uniformly on this class. The metric and physical time-one clock are fixed. No nonvanishing or connectedness loophole is used.

## Imported entropy seed

Berger–Turaev, *On Herman’s Positive Entropy Conjecture*, Theorem A and §3.4, construct a smooth standard-area-preserving disk diffeomorphism `f` that is the identity near the boundary and has a positive-area stochastic island; hence `h_area(f)>0` and `h_top(f)>0`. Section 3.4 explicitly extends the map by identity outside a disk. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/1704.02473 . This existence theorem is imported, not reproved, and no finite-bit implementation is claimed. The same positive-area disk-map seed appeared in c08_l08's frozen INITIAL; its source origin is attributed there, while the shrinking fixed-metric suspension and power calculation below are this post-exposure construction.

## Suspension and spatial compression

Embed the disk in a coordinate chart of the flat unit torus `T^2`. The map `f`, being area-preserving and identity near the boundary, admits a smooth area-preserving isotopy `f_z`, `0<=z<=1`, from the identity to `f`, supported in a fixed compact disk `K`. Reparameterize the isotopy to be constant near `z=0` and `z=1`, so its Eulerian velocity

```text
V_z = (d f_z/dz) o f_z^(-1)
```

is smooth, one-periodic in `z`, standard-area-divergence-free, and supported in `K` for every `z`. The usual path-connectedness of the compactly supported area-preserving disk group supplies the isotopy; equivalently, `f` is a compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of the disk.

For every sufficiently small `epsilon>0`, let `D_epsilon` be the image of a disk containing `K` under the chart dilation `psi_epsilon(x)=epsilon x`; choose the chart so its closure lies in one coordinate patch. Define the conjugate isotopy and vector field

```text
f_(epsilon,z) = psi_epsilon o f_z o psi_epsilon^(-1),
V_(epsilon,z)(x) = epsilon V_z(x/epsilon),
X_epsilon(x,y,z) = ∂_z + V_(epsilon,z)(x,y).
```

Extend `V_(epsilon,z)` by zero outside `D_epsilon`. Because `V_z` is supported strictly inside `K`, this extension is smooth; periodicity in `z` makes `X_epsilon` an autonomous smooth vector field on the fixed `T^2 x S^1`. Its third component is exactly one, so it is nowhere zero. The flat divergence is

```text
div X_epsilon = ∂_x V_epsilon^x + ∂_y V_epsilon^y + ∂_z(1)
               = (div_(x,y) V_z)(x/epsilon) = 0.
```

Starting at the section `z=0`, the `z` coordinate advances at unit speed and the transverse equation is the isotoped dynamics. Therefore the section is invariant under the time-one map and

```text
phi_1^epsilon|_(T^2 x {0}) = f_epsilon
  = psi_epsilon o f o psi_epsilon^(-1)
```

on the disk, extended by identity outside it. It follows that

```text
h_top(phi_1^epsilon) >= h_top(f_epsilon)=h_top(f)=:h_0>0.
```

The conjugacy is spatial only; there is no time slowdown or speed-up, so the entropy per fixed physical unit remains `h_0`.

Every section `T² x {z0}` is invariant under the time-one map. The return map on that section is conjugate by the area-preserving partial isotopy map to `f_epsilon`; hence it has the same metric entropy. Since the base coordinate `z0` is fixed by the time-one map, the entropy of the product-volume system is the integral of these conditional entropies over `z0`. Thus

```text
h_mu(phi_1^epsilon) = epsilon² (Area(D)/Area(T²)) h_D,
h_D = h_(area on D normalized to probability)(f) > 0.
```

The map is identity on the complement of `D`, so that complement contributes zero entropy. This explicitly exhibits why the prescribed-volume entropy can vanish while topological entropy does not.

## Exact strain scaling and power

Use the same flat product metric for every `epsilon`. With coordinates `i,j` in `{x,y}`, the nonzero derivatives are

```text
∂_i V_epsilon^a(x,y,z) = (∂_i V_z^a)(x/epsilon,y/epsilon),
∂_z V_epsilon^a(x,y,z) = epsilon (∂_z V_z^a)(x/epsilon,y/epsilon),
```

and the derivatives of the background component `X^z=1` vanish. Let `S_epsilon=Def X_epsilon` and let `A,B>=0` be the fixed integrals of the horizontal-gradient and z-derivative terms of the base family over `K x S^1`. Changing variables in the transverse plane gives exactly

```text
∫_(T^3) |S_epsilon|² dVol = epsilon² A + epsilon^4 B.
```

In particular the energy is `O(epsilon²)`. The constant `A` is positive: if all horizontal symmetric derivatives vanished, each compactly supported `V_z` would be a Euclidean Killing field and hence zero, forcing `f=id`, contrary to positive entropy. All fields are smooth for every fixed `epsilon`; higher derivatives can grow as `epsilon` shrinks, so this is not a uniform all-orders regularity bound.

For fixed `nu>0`, define a smooth force by the admitted residual law, with `p=0` for simplicity:

```text
F_epsilon = ∇_(X_epsilon) X_epsilon
             - 2 nu div(Def X_epsilon).
```

It is a smooth field for each `epsilon`. Closedness and flat divergence-freeness yield the exact work identity

```text
W_epsilon = ∫ <F_epsilon,X_epsilon> dVol
           = 2 nu ∫ |S_epsilon|² dVol
           = 2 nu (epsilon² A + epsilon^4 B).
```

In the flat incompressible case, `2 div(Def X_epsilon)=Delta X_epsilon`; the transverse Laplacian of `epsilon V_z(x/epsilon)` is `epsilon^(-1) Delta_(x,y)V_z(x/epsilon)`. A nonzero compactly supported `V_z` cannot be horizontally harmonic, so the residual force's supremum norm grows on the order of `nu/epsilon` (the advective term is only `O(epsilon)`). This is a relevant physical boundary: integrated signed power tends to zero while peak sustaining force grows. The theorem charges `∫<F,X>` only; it has no uniform pointwise-force, force-gradient, actuator, or acquisition budget.

Hence `W_epsilon/(4 nu V h_top(phi_1^epsilon)^2) <= (epsilon² A+epsilon^4B)/(2 h_0²) -> 0`, proving the stated failure. The correct metric-entropy theorem remains consistent: `h_mu(phi_1^epsilon)=O(epsilon²)`, so its squared right side shrinks faster than work.

## Scope, attribution, and proof status

This is stronger than the earlier disconnected Sol-plus-flat counterexample and the separate connected metric-rescaling construction: it uses a single fixed flat connected manifold, one fixed metric, one fixed clock, and nowhere-zero fields. Its admitted force may have high spatial derivatives, but the theorem imposes no uniform force-regularity or actuator-cost cap. A class with a supplied uniform derivative envelope, a fixed lower bound on the volume of a positive-entropy region, or an acquired actuator interface is a different question.
