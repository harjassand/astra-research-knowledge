ENTROPY DISSIPATION, DIRICHLET COMPARISON, AND TRACE DISTURBANCE
Cycle05 literature memo — narrow primary-source comparison
Status: scoped source audit; no priority or novelty claim

EXACT INTERFACE UNDER REVIEW

Let M_d be finite, tau=I/d, and Phi:M_d->M_d be bistochastic, CP, and
Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint. Let Psi:M_d->M_d be one fixed bistochastic CP
map. Assume the supplied full real-Hermitian quadratic-form comparison

  <H,(I-Psi)H>_HS <= C <H,(I-Phi)H>_HS   for every Hermitian H.

Write T(rho,sigma)=||rho-sigma||_1/2,
  D=D(rho||tau) in nats,
  b=T(Phi(rho),rho), and e=T(Psi(rho),rho).
The candidate inequality is e^3 <= (27C/4) D b, uniformly over d, Phi,
Psi, and density matrices rho. Psi need not be entanglement-breaking for
this inequality. The parent’s separate C4 result supplies such a Psi that
is canonical EB, but that is not a premise needed for this comparison.

The proposed semigroup route is available at the level of hypotheses:
P_t=exp[-t(I-Phi)]=exp(-t) sum_{n>=0} t^n Phi^n/n! is a bistochastic CP
semigroup and is Hilbert–Schmidt symmetric. It gives a route to reversible
QMS results. It does not by itself turn the one-step trace displacement b
into an entropy-dissipation or trace-stability estimate.

EXACT FORM IDENTITIES AND A FAILED INTERMEDIATE LEMMA

For h=rho-tau and delta_G=rho-G(rho), any bistochastic G fixes tau and
Tr(delta_G)=0. Therefore

  E_G(h):=<h,(I-G)h>_HS = Tr(h delta_G) = Tr(rho delta_G).

For Phi, E_Phi(h)>=0 because Phi is a bistochastic CP HS contraction and
self-adjoint. The target premise compares E_Psi(h)<=C E_Phi(h), using the
real quadratic form (equivalently its symmetric part if Psi is not
self-adjoint). Also E_Phi(h)=Tr(rho delta_Phi)<=2b. These identities are
valid, but they do not themselves control the trace norm e.

The tempting composition would be to add the dimension-free lemma

  e^3 <= (27/8) D(rho||tau) E_Psi(rho-tau).                    (*)

That lemma is false by local scaling, so it cannot establish the proposed
27C/4 bound. For a concrete commuting example take d=2, G=Psi to be the
completely depolarizing channel, H=diag(1,-1), and
rho_epsilon=I/2+epsilon H for 0<epsilon<1/2. Then
e=epsilon, D(rho_epsilon||tau)=2 epsilon^2+O(epsilon^4), and
E_Psi(rho_epsilon-tau)=2 epsilon^2. Hence the left side of (*) is
epsilon^3 while its right side is (27/2)epsilon^4+O(epsilon^6), which
fails for sufficiently small epsilon for any fixed coefficient. This is
not a counterexample to the target: taking Phi=Psi gives b=epsilon and
the target right side is order epsilon^3.

Thus the exact source-level gap is sharper than a generic
entropy-times-energy conversion. A valid proof needs a scale-compatible
bridge that uses the trace defect b (or an equivalent first-order
quantity) directly. The target still follows as a formal consequence if
that bridge is proved; it is not reduced to (*). None of the audited
primary theorems below supplies the needed bridge. This failure is a
counterexample to one proof route, not a novelty or impossibility claim
about the target theorem.

THREE STRONG PRIMARY MECHANISMS AND THEIR LIMITS

1. Complete-positivity mixing gives relative-entropy decay for symmetric
   semigroups. Gao, Junge, LaRacuente, and Li, Theorem 1.1 of
   arXiv:2209.11684v2 (2023; journal version, “Complete positivity order
   and relative entropy decay,” Forum Math. Sigma 13 (2025), e31), assume a
   symmetric QMS T_t with fixed-point conditional expectation E and a
   finite complete-positivity mixing time: for some t,
   (1-eps)E <=_cp T_t <=_cp (1+eps)E. They derive a complete modified
   logarithmic Sobolev inequality with alpha >= 1/(2 t_cb(0.1)); in
   particular D(T_t(rho)||E(rho)) <= exp(-2 alpha t)D(rho||E(rho)).
   Their Theorem 1.2 gives the discrete analogue for a symmetric CP unital
   map under a two-sided CP-order mixing hypothesis on an even power.

   This is the closest route through the supplied Phi: Poissonization gives
   a symmetric QMS. But the premise is map order in the CP cone and a
   mixing-time quantity. The target supplies only a quadratic-form
   comparison between I-Psi and I-Phi. The conclusion controls entropy
   after evolution toward a fixed-point conditional expectation; it does
   not bound T(Psi(rho),rho), which is a different channel’s one-step
   trace displacement. Thus CP-order mixing/entropy decay does not prove
   the target without an additional bridge that retains b and its constants.

2. Reversible QMS trace-distance ergodicity is obtained through a weighted
   chi-square expansion. Bertini, De Sole, and Posta, Theorem 2.5 of
   arXiv:2206.11923v2 (2023; J. Funct. Anal. 286 (2024), 110340), assume an
   ergodic QMS reversible with respect to a faithful state sigma. If
   -L=sum_j lambda_j <f_j,.>_sigma f_j is its sigma-GNS spectral
   decomposition, then

     d_tr(rho P_t,sigma)^2 <= (1/4) sum_{j>=1} exp(-2 lambda_j t)
                                             |rho(f_j)|^2,

   and the uniform bound replaces |rho(f_j)| by ||f_j||_infinity. The proof
   passes through D_chi2(rho||sigma)=Tr[(rho-sigma)sigma^{-1}(rho-sigma)]
   and d_tr^2 <= D_chi2/4.

   This gives a genuine trace-norm conclusion for semigroup mixing, but its
   object is distance to stationarity after time t, not a one-step
   displacement of a common Psi. The uniform estimate exposes operator
   norms of the GNS-normalized eigenfunctions, so it carries spectral and
   representation data rather than the desired universal constant. It
   supplies neither a fixed-map Dirichlet comparison nor the target cubic
   trace-defect estimate.

3. Nontracial detailed balance has a modular entropy-dissipation calculus.
   Carlen and Maas, arXiv:1609.01254v3 (2017; J. Funct. Anal. 273(5)
   (2017), 1810–1869), define sigma-DBC as self-adjointness in the
   sigma-GNS inner product (Definition 2.10). Their compatible inner
   products are modularly weighted; GNS is s=1 and KMS is s=1/2, and they
   note that detailed-balance conventions can differ (Section 2.2–2.4).
   For sigma-DBC generators they express the dual generator through
   state-dependent logarithmic means,

     -L^dagger(rho) = sum_j partial_j^dagger(
         [rho]_{omega_j} partial_j(log rho-log sigma)),

   (Theorem 5.10). Under an additional gradient/intertwining condition,
   the dual semigroup is a gradient flow of D(rho||sigma) (Theorem 7.6),
   and the generalized log-Sobolev estimate is

     D(rho||sigma) <= (1/(2 lambda))
       Tr[-L^dagger(rho)(log rho-log sigma)]

   (Equation 8.17, with the paper’s stated assumptions).

   This is the strongest audited nontracial extension of the entropy side:
   it tracks relative modular structure and entropy production. Its
   dissipation is nonlinear in rho through [rho]_omega and log gradients;
   it is not the fixed sigma-Hilbert–Schmidt Dirichlet form in the target,
   and it gives no trace-displacement estimate for Psi. A nontracial version
   of the target would need a new comparison of those modular means with
   the fixed quadratic form and the trace norm, with explicit dependence
   (or independence) on sigma_min. These theorems do not provide that
   bridge.

DECISIVE MATHEMATICAL GATE

The exact target remains e^3 <= (27C/4)D b under full real-Hermitian form
order. A proof must state whether Psi is self-adjoint; if it is not, it
must use the real symmetric quadratic form and explain how the comparison
is preserved. It must cover boundary states or give a justified limiting
argument and remain independent of d. A semigroup derivation must identify
how it retains the first-order trace defect b; substituting D times a
quadratic energy has the wrong near-tau scale. Entropy decay at fixed
positive time does not supply that bridge by itself.

The first sharp falsification route is to restrict to commuting channels
and states, then test small reversible Phi and bistochastic Psi satisfying
the form order for a ratio e^3/[C D b] exceeding 27/4. A numerical
violation would need exact rational channel/state data and a certified
ratio before it counts. No such test was run in this literature task.
Passing the classical case would still leave the noncommutative statement
open.

The source audit is deliberately narrow. It establishes the nearest
primary mechanisms and the exact conversion they leave open; it does not
claim that a broader search proves absence or novelty.
