# Learning acquisition: a robust static task-space reduction, and its remaining sequential gap

**Status: partial mathematical reduction; no breakthrough or canonical HMM compiler.**

The strongest result reconstructed in this pass is a constant-factor, bicriteria
compression of a finite conditional-prediction table in the norm appropriate to
*all bounded future tasks*. Combining this with a sharp elementary Hellinger
identity gives a teacher-error constant independent of the HMM width and of the
length of the sequence. The exponentially many future columns of such a table
can be acquired approximately from teacher evaluation and simulation, for a
finite list of prefixes. This is stronger than a raw-probability Frobenius
projection, but does not acquire a recurrent state that works on fresh prefixes.

The underlying Hellinger and semidefinite ingredients are established tools.
The application assembled here has not undergone an external correctness or
priority review; no novelty claim is made. The primary-source audit identifies
the exact theorem that already removes the older fidelity obstruction and the
stronger promise that prevents its direct application here.

## 1. Exact target and opportunities

The canonical target, read from the prior packet's `work/inputs/02_TARGET.md`, is:

* Unknown possibly time-inhomogeneous HMM law P on [q]^H, at most r hidden states.
* Independent complete P trajectories.
* Evaluation, at any explicitly chosen prefix, of exact rational probabilities
  from one fixed polynomially evaluable teacher Q, description bound L.
* Complete-law KL(P || Q) <= zeta, 0 <= zeta <= 1. Q may have arbitrary rank.
* Polynomial acquisition in r,q,H,L,1/epsilon,log(1/delta), without inverse zeta,
  inverse singular-value, or inverse latent-mass dependence.
* Teacher-free finite-precision recurrence of dimension poly(r,q), bounded
  stream-specific retained bits, bounded states, and simultaneous norm-bounded
  linear readouts of every bounded future task with MSE <= C zeta + epsilon^2.
* Subsequent explicit convex regression for a supplied downstream task.

The teacher is an acquired prediction service, not true chosen-prefix
conditioning. Full-prefix storage or later calls to Q are forbidden in deployment.

Three opportunities were screened:

1. Robustify the low-rank conditional-query learner through teacher-generated
   trajectories, whose conditional error is controlled by complete-law KL.
2. Replace Frobenius table compression by the infinity-to-two task norm. This
   yielded the usable deduction below.
3. Make Mao's low-degree counterexample uniformly samplable by sampling its
   random subspace and evaluation points. This runs into the inherited rare
   relation / large low-degree norm obstruction; high probability of a good
   parameter draw is insufficient. I did not produce a uniform sampler.

Release family 140's memory--sample lower bound for exact Gaussian regression
was checked at catalogue-interface depth. It concerns a continuous spherical
signal, finite retained messages, and fresh Gaussian rows; it does not remove
HMM conditioning or construct compact predictive states. A discretization has
an exponentially large latent-state alphabet, so its lower bound does not
contradict a polynomial-in-r compiler. No consequence here relies on its proof.

## 2. What primary literature already supplies

[Liu--Moitra, Model Stealing for Any Low-Rank Language Model](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07536),
Theorem 1.3, learns any exactly low probability-rank law from arbitrary-prefix
conditional samples in polynomial resources, without a fidelity parameter.
It uses conditional-density sketches, barycentric spanners, and convex KL
projection during each change of basis. Its sampler makes no further source
queries after acquisition.

This is already a removal of the fidelity assumption in
[Mahajan--Kakade--Krishnamurthy--Zhang, COLT 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v195/mahajan23a/mahajan23a.pdf).
It is not a removal of the current misspecified-teacher acquisition obstruction.

The precise stronger promise appears in Liu--Moitra Lemma 6.3: their simulated
density law is uniformly conditionally close to a rank-S law for **every queried
prefix**. Algorithm 3 includes all forced one-symbol extensions of previously
selected spanning prefixes, as well as ordinary sampled prefixes. Complete-law
KL(P || Q) does not give that promise for the forced extensions. The proof's
use of uniform conditional closeness is therefore load-bearing.

Astra N25 supplies the explicit four-state paired-bit counterexample: a teacher
can have arbitrarily small complete-law KL from P while its synthetic splice
logit rectangle has rank 2^(k-1) and constant mass under independently spliced
marginals. That is a counterexample to the proposed transfer, not a negative
result against every compiler. The exact source read was
`work/astra/web/pages/d-473caf7c9b06a990.html`.

[Neykov, Polynomial-Time Near-Optimal Estimation over Certain Type-2 Convex Bodies](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.22714),
Section 2, uses the established fractional-projector relaxation of Kolmogorov
widths, citing Varadarajan et al. (2007). The PSD Grothendieck inequality is
Rietz/Nesterov; see the primary
[Briet--de Oliveira Filho--Vallentin paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.5765).
The reduction below specializes these tools to a prediction-table zonotope;
it is not presented as a new semidefinite principle.

Exact copies of the selected primary sources are in `sources/`. I technically
read the stated Liu--Moitra interfaces and spanner/projection algorithms, Neykov's
width relaxation and approximate quadratic oracle, and Mao's construction and
nonuniform-sampler statement. This was not a complete certification of any paper.

## 3. Sharp conditional error on teacher trajectories

Define squared Hellinger distance in the **full** convention

    H2(P,Q) = sum_w (sqrt(P(w)) - sqrt(Q(w)))^2
            = 2 - 2 sum_w sqrt(P(w)Q(w)).

Let h range over cells of any prefix stopping-time partition; fixed cuts are a
special case. Write p_h=P(h), q_h=Q(h), and

    A_h = sum_f sqrt(P(f|h) Q(f|h)),
    v_h = TV(P(f|h),Q(f|h)).

On a P-zero cell, choose any conditional extension; the Hellinger decomposition
below is unaffected. Independent randomization in the stopping rule can be
adjoined to both laws.

**Lemma.** For either R=P or R=Q,

    E_R[v_h^2] <= H2(P,Q) <= KL(P || Q).

**Proof.** Cauchy--Schwarz applied to sqrt(p_f)-sqrt(q_f) and
sqrt(p_f)+sqrt(q_f) gives v_h^2 <= 1-A_h^2. Furthermore,

    p_h + q_h - 2 sqrt(p_h q_h) A_h
      = (sqrt(p_h)-sqrt(q_h)A_h)^2 + q_h(1-A_h^2)
      >= q_h v_h^2.

Sum over h. The left side is exactly H2(P,Q), including the conditional future
Hellinger contribution. Exchange P and Q to obtain the P version. Finally,
Jensen gives KL(P || Q) >= -2 log affinity(P,Q) >= 2(1-affinity(P,Q)).
This proves the claim. The constant one cannot be reduced uniformly: put Q mass
alpha on a P-zero prefix and use disjoint conditional laws there; as alpha goes
to zero, both the averaged squared-TV contribution and global H2 are alpha+o(alpha).

**Useful consequence.** At each cut t, all true conditional futures of an r-state
HMM lie in the linear span of its r state-conditioned future laws. On a P-zero
prefix, choose a fixed mixture of those r laws. Thus Q's conditional-future
profiles, sampled on its own ordinary trajectories, are within zeta in averaged
squared TV of an r-dimensional family. This has no inverse-zeta or horizon
factor. It applies to stopping decisions made from the observed teacher prefix,
not to conditioning the query distribution on a rare selected-basis event.

It does not supply true conditionals, identify which prefixes are inaccurate,
or bound arbitrary forced/spliced query distributions. If query-prefix law R is
dominated by kappa times Q's stopped-prefix law, the same calculation gives the
weaker kappa*zeta bound. The density factor is charged; it can be arbitrarily
large in the N25 paired-bit example.

## 4. Static task-norm compression: exact finite-matrix theorem

For a real n-by-m matrix M, define

    ||M||_(infinity -> 2)^2
       = max_{s in {-1,+1}^m} ||M s||_2^2.

This is the squared worst-case loss over bounded tasks. Let

    OPT_r(M) = min_{Pi orthogonal projector, rank(Pi)<=r}
                   ||(I-Pi) M||_(infinity -> 2)^2.

**Theorem.** Given an explicitly encoded finite matrix M, a polynomial-size SDP
and spectral rounding produce an orthogonal projector Pi of rank at most 2r with

    ||(I-Pi) M||_(infinity -> 2)^2 <= pi * OPT_r(M).

An approximate rational implementation gives the same statement with an additive
accuracy allowance. All n*m entries, bit lengths, SDP accuracy, workspace, and
eigendecomposition are charged. This is a bicriteria result: rank doubles.

The SDP is

    minimize sum_j z_j
    subject to 0 <= X <= I, trace(X) <= r,
               Diag(z) - M^T (I-X) M >= 0.

All inequalities on symmetric matrices are PSD inequalities. For fixed X, the
minimum over z is the dual of

    SDP(C) = max_{S>=0, diag(S)=1} trace(C S),
    C = M^T (I-X) M >= 0.

For PSD C,

    max_sign s^T C s <= SDP(C) <= (pi/2) max_sign s^T C s.

For completeness, the upper inequality can be proved directly. Factor feasible
S as the Gram matrix of unit vectors v_j, draw a standard Gaussian G, and let
s_j=sign(<G,v_j>). Then E[s_i s_j]=(2/pi)arcsin(S_ij). The power series of arcsin
has positive coefficients, beginning with x; every odd Schur power of S is PSD.
Its trace inner product with PSD C is nonnegative. Therefore

    E[s^T C s] >= (2/pi) trace(CS).

The best sign vector is at least that expectation.

Let Pi_* minimize OPT_r and use X=Pi_* as a feasible comparator. The SDP optimum
is at most (pi/2) OPT_r. From the acquired X, retain its largest 2r eigenvectors
(or all of them when n<=2r). On every omitted eigenvector, lambda(X)<=1/2 because
trace(X)<=r. Hence

    I-Pi <= 2(I-X).

For every sign vector s, this implies

    ||(I-Pi)Ms||^2 <= 2 s^T M^T(I-X)Ms <= 2 SDP(C),

which proves the theorem. This proof uses no singular-value lower bound. Standard
weak SDP/eigenvector accuracy enters as additive error, not inverse conditioning.

### Consequence for a fully available teacher table

At a fixed cut, let nu(h)=Q(h), and define

    M_Q[h,f] = sqrt(nu(h)) Q(f|h),
    M_P[h,f] = sqrt(nu(h)) P(f|h).

The chosen P-zero conditional extension keeps rank(M_P)<=r. For every signed
task s,

    ||(M_Q-M_P)s||^2 <= 4 E_Q[TV_h^2] <= 4 zeta.

The projector onto the column space of M_P is a legal rank-r comparator, so
the acquired projector satisfies

    ||(I-Pi)M_Q||_(infinity -> 2)^2 <= 4 pi zeta.

Include a constant feature. A task g in [0,1] is written as
g=(1+s)/2. The teacher-task compression MSE is at most pi*zeta, and the source
teacher MSE at most zeta. Squaring their sum gives the universal bound

    source-task MSE <= (2 pi + 2) zeta,

plus the chosen SDP and sampling accuracy allowances. This holds simultaneously
for all bounded tasks. No factor r or H multiplies zeta.

On n equally weighted supplied table rows, an orthonormal basis V for Pi yields
states m_i=(1,V_i)/sqrt(2), norm at most one. The readout for g is

    theta_g = (1/sqrt(2), V^T [Q(2g-1)]/sqrt(2)),

with norm at most sqrt((n+1)/2). Thus bounded states and bounded linear readouts
are explicit on this finite row interface. Bounded-label regression over these
known states is an ordinary convex least-squares problem.

For nonuniform rows, the direct lookup coordinates divide by sqrt(nu(h)); a
minimum-row-mass bound would then be needed for this particular normalization.
This is one reason not to treat the fully available table theorem as the canonical
state construction.

## 5. Acquisition of future columns for a finite prefix list

The exponential number of futures is removable **for a finite supplied or sampled
list of n prefixes**. This does not yet generalize the states to fresh histories.

Let q_i be Q's conditional future law at prefix h_i. Teacher evaluation computes
q_i(f) by multiplying at most H exact rational next-token probabilities; teacher
simulation samples from q_i. Use the mixture

    mu(f) = (1/n) sum_i q_i(f),
    v(f) = (q_1(f)/mu(f),...,q_n(f)/mu(f))/sqrt(n).

On the support of mu, each ratio is at most n and

    ||v(f)||_2 <= sqrt(n).

Sample k independent futures from mu and form columns v(f_j)/k of an n-by-k
matrix M_hat. The corresponding exact conditional table M has columns
(q_1(f),...,q_n(f))/sqrt(n). For a vector a,

    ||M^T a||_1 = E_mu |<a,v(F)>|,
    ||M_hat^T a||_1 = (1/k) sum_j |<a,v(f_j)>|.

An alpha-net of the n-dimensional unit sphere, Hoeffding's inequality, and the
sqrt(n) Lipschitz bound give uniform additive error eta with probability 1-delta
using, conservatively,

    k = O(n [n log(1+sqrt(n)/eta) + log(1/delta)] / eta^2).

This is uniform over all unit a. Consequently, it is uniform over all orthogonal
projectors Pi, since

    ||(I-Pi)M||_(infinity -> 2)
        = sup_{||a||=1} ||M^T (I-Pi)a||_1.

Apply the finite SDP to M_hat. Its rank-2r output therefore has true task-norm
error at most

    sqrt(pi) * sqrt(OPT_r(M)) + (sqrt(pi)+1)*eta

in norm; its squared error is O(OPT_r(M)+eta^2) with a universal constant.

All ratios are bounded on the acquired mixture support, avoiding an uncharged
rare-future density denominator. Stored arrays have n*k entries; probability
evaluation uses O(n*k*H) teacher-token calls, and simulation costs O(k*H). Exact
products have encoded bit length charged in L and H. Approximate rational density
and sampling arithmetic can be budgeted additively because the teacher returns
exact rationals; finite bit bounds must still be implemented in any deployment.

For n independently teacher-sampled prefixes, the averaged squared-TV error has
expectation at most zeta. A scalar Hoeffding bound provides zeta+a on that list
with n=O(a^-2 log(1/delta)), with no inverse zeta. However, the SDP states are
coordinates on this acquired list of rows. This is a finite data representation,
not an evaluable feature map on every new prefix.

## 6. Load-bearing gaps and failed transfers

The remaining implication is precise:

> Starting from finite sampled-prefix task-norm frames, acquire polynomial-size
> evaluable feature maps for fresh prefixes and stable updates that use only the
> previous compact state and the next observed symbol, with total actual-path
> loss O(zeta)+epsilon^2 and no r/H multiplier on zeta.

The static SDP does not solve that implication. It chooses a subspace of functions
on the training-prefix list. Evaluating a new row by its full prefix or by a teacher
call would violate the deployed-memory interface. Independently good frames at
successive cuts do not supply acquired change-of-basis maps, nor prove stability
under normalization and accumulated finite-precision error.

The following shortcuts were rejected:

* Reusing Liu--Moitra's proof with ordinary teacher KL substituted for uniform
  conditional closeness. Its forced basis extensions are not controlled.
* Treating an empirical orthogonal projection as an acquired recurrent update.
  It does not evaluate a fresh prefix and does not propagate information forward.
* Using release 140's exact-Gaussian streaming lower bound to infer hardness for
  this HMM interface. Continuous versus r-state latent descriptions and teacher
  query access do not match.
* Sampling Mao's nonconstructively selected code subspace and point set and
  discarding their small failure probability. Bounded low-degree L2 advantage is
  sensitive to rare relations and cannot be justified by a TV/w.h.p. statement.

The decisive falsification for the direct conditional-query transfer remains the
paired-bit synthetic-splice construction in N25. A decisive positive next result
would be the quoted stable acquisition/update lemma, implemented without calls
to true chosen-prefix conditionals, a full-history register, an exponential
table, or general nonconvex optimization oracle.

## 7. Computational evidence and practical costs

`check_static_compression.py` computes the SDP and enumerates every signed
future task on 12 small probability-table contamination fixtures. It also checks
100 unequal-prefix-mass/zero-source-mass Hellinger fixtures.
`diagnostics.json` records `ALL_DIAGNOSTICS_PASSED`; all 12 SDP fixtures have
rank two true tables and rank four retained spaces. Every bounded task on each
7-column fixture is enumerated to check the norm-bounded readout and source MSE
bound. The run took about 0.11 seconds after environment setup.

This is numerical finite evidence only. Clarabel issued an accuracy warning on
at least one solve; its output is not an exact primal/dual certificate, and the
script accepts numerical `optimal_inaccurate` status within the asserted numeric
tolerances. The displayed all-dimensional algebra is the proposed theorem
argument; solver status is not used to certify it.

The local diagnostic used an isolated uv cache inside this worker directory and
downloaded roughly 31 MiB of package wheels (numpy, scipy, cvxpy, highspy plus
small dependencies). There was no cloud computation, publication, repository
mutation, or latent oracle. The theorem's general SDP has n-by-n and k-by-k PSD
constraints; polynomial worst-case resources can still be impractical when
prefix and accuracy sample budgets are large. No Apple-M4 runtime or memory
capability was inferred from the small fixture timings.

## 8. Strongest supported consequence

The static robust acquisition problem should be posed in the bounded-task
operator norm, where a constant-factor, rank-doubling convex relaxation exists,
rather than insisting on Frobenius or square-root tensor compression. It also
has a polynomial finite-prefix column acquisition mechanism from the actually
supplied teacher service.

This narrows the unresolved acquisition debt to fresh-prefix features and
teacher-free sequential closure. It does not remove that debt or resolve the
canonical proposition. No unrestricted representation learner, uniform low-degree
counterexample sampler, or consequential scientific breakthrough was established.
