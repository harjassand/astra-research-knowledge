# Family 113 molecular-pairing transfer audit

## Verdict

There is a clean, narrow molecular interface for the release’s perfect-matching result: count (and, source-reported, approximately sample) complete one-to-one pair assignments in a supplied, explicitly listed, unweighted simple compatibility graph. This is useful for feasibility and combinatorial design-space size. It does not predict solution-phase assembly yield. Yield also depends on association free energies, concentrations, partial complexes, strand indistinguishability, kinetics, and any non-pairwise or geometric interactions.

I found two conditional interfaces that survive. First, the unweighted FPRAS gives a relative approximation to the number of full pairings and therefore an additive-accurate *combinatorial* entropy. Second, the separate perfect-matching entropy result bounds the Shannon entropy and weighted partition function of a saturated, pair-additive Gibbs model when its edge marginals are supplied. Neither statement is an equilibrium-yield theorem, and the release results are treated here as source-reported rather than independently verified.

## Reconstructed release interface

The pinned release is [OpenAI Math snapshot `adc7f124`](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a). The relevant paper is [*A Fully Polynomial Randomized Approximation Scheme for Perfect Matchings in General Graphs*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/A-Fully-Polynomial-Randomized-Approximation-Scheme-for-Perfect-Matchings-in-General-Graphs-September-23-2026/main.pdf), Theorem 1.1 (printed pp. 2–3). It takes a finite simple undirected graph (G=(V,E)) with vertices and edges explicitly listed, and rational (0<\varepsilon<1), (0<\delta<1/2). Writing

\[
Z(G)=|\{M\subseteq E:M\text{ covers every vertex exactly once}\}|,
\]

the paper claims a classical randomized estimate \(\widehat Z\) with
\[
\Pr[(1-\varepsilon)Z(G)\le\widehat Z\le(1+\varepsilon)Z(G)]\ge1-\delta,
\]
exact zero output on infeasible graphs, and worst-case bit time polynomial in the full encoding length, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(\log\delta^{-1}\), including unsuccessful random tapes. This is an *unweighted* count of labeled edge subsets on a *simple* graph. It does not directly accept an implicit compatibility oracle, real-valued edge energies, compressed parallel-edge multiplicities, or a thermodynamic ensemble.

The same paper’s §10.1 (pp. 41–42) gives a constant-fibre reduction for exactly-(k) matchings in an explicit simple host graph with (n) vertices and (m) edges. With (q=n-2k\), add (q) labeled dummy vertices, join each to every original vertex, add no dummy–dummy edges, and retain original edges. The resulting simple graph has at most (2n) vertices and at most (m+n^2) edges, and
\[
Z(H_k)=q!\,|M_k(G)|.
\]
Thus the theorem also covers a *specified cardinality* of disjoint pairs after graph construction and exact division by (q!\). Its §10.2 (pp. 42–43) claims an approximate uniform sampler with TV error \(\tau\), exact feasibility handling, and polynomial worst-case bit time; for an input graph with (m_0) edges it uses at most (2m_0) counting calls and (2m_0+1) exact matching-feasibility tests. These sampler statements are also source-reported.

The separate paper [*Entropy and Face Dimension of the Perfect-Matching Polytope*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Entropy-and-Face-Dimension-of-the-Perfect-Matching-Polytope-September-23-2026/main.pdf), Theorem 1.1 and §6, has a different interface. For a loopless labeled multigraph on (2m\ge2) vertices, let (x_e\) be feasible edge marginals of a perfect-matching law, let
\[
F(x)=-\sum_e x_e\ln x_e,\qquad B(x)=-\sum_e(1-x_e)\ln(1-x_e),
\]
and let (H(x)\) be the maximum Shannon entropy of any perfect-matching law with those marginals. It claims
\[
F(x)-(2-2/m)B(x)\le H(x)\le F(x).
\]
For finite dimensionless edge activities (a_e>0), set \(\theta_e=\ln a_e\), \(Z_w=\sum_{M\in PM(G)}\prod_{e\in M}a_e\), and use the Gibbs law \(p(M)\propto\exp(\sum_e\theta_e1_{e\in M})\). Its edge mean (x\) makes this law maximum entropy among laws with those marginals, so
\[
\sum_e\theta_ex_e+F(x)-(2-2/m)B(x)\le\ln Z_w\le\sum_e\theta_ex_e+F(x).
\]
This is a mathematical entropy/partition-function bound, not a weighted FPRAS. The sharper pointwise inequality does not by itself compute (x), (\theta), or (Z_w). The source also gives an (O(n))-additive-log weighted variational bracket in §6. The release’s entropy formalization summary is in [the family-113 Lean scope note](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/lean/docs/113.md).

All of these release claims remain **source-reported** in this audit. The local release text and Lean-scope note were inspected; no independent proof review, Lean build, or algorithm replay was performed.

## Concrete molecular model and its exact boundary

Take (2m) distinguishable, monovalent oligonucleotide strands (or one-site molecular constructs). A vertex is one physical strand instance. Put an undirected edge (uv\) in (G) exactly when the specified design rule admits one duplex/contact between those two instances. Each strand may be in at most one duplex, and a *fully paired* state is represented completely by its selected edge set. A perfect matching is then exactly a complete one-to-one partner assignment. To meet the theorem’s direct input model, list every vertex, including isolates, and every allowed edge; do not hide candidate-pair tests behind an oracle. The graph must be simple: a pair has at most one modeled contact state. If conformations are distinct microstates, or identical species are quotiented rather than labeled, an extra multiplicity/symmetry model is needed. If a molecule has multiple simultaneously usable binding sites, ordinary graph matching on molecules does not encode that valence; hypermatching or another constraint model is needed.

On this interface, with probability at least (1-\delta\), the release FPRAS would approximate the number of complete assignments. Defining (S_{\rm comb}=k_B\ln Z(G)\), the same success event gives
\[
|k_B\ln\widehat Z-S_{\rm comb}|\le k_B[-\ln(1-\varepsilon)].
\]
For example, (\varepsilon=0.01\) gives an additive error at most (0.010051k_B\) in the *log count*. This is a rigorous conditional deduction from the source-reported relative-count guarantee. It is useful when one wants to know whether a complete pairing exists, compare the size of two feasible assignment spaces, or sample a combinatorial candidate uniformly. It is a physical configurational entropy only under the additional condition that the measured ensemble is uniform over these complete states (or an equivalent equal-free-energy model), with partial states and other degrees of freedom excluded from the quantity being discussed.

For an explicitly prescribed (k)-pair design space in a graph with (n) candidate strands, the (H_k) reduction above gives the analogous count. Its graph-construction cost is (O(m+n^2)) vertices/edges in the worst case, plus (q!\) arithmetic with (O(n\log(n+1))) bits. This still counts possible pair sets, not their equilibrium weights.

## Counterexamples to using count or entropy as yield

**Total assembly yield already fails on (K_2\).** Let (A+B\rightleftharpoons AB\), with equal total concentration (c) of each strand. The explicit compatibility graph is the same single-edge graph (K_2\) for both conditions, so it has one perfect matching, (Z=1\), and combinatorial entropy (\ln Z=0\). Let (x=[AB]/c\) be the fraction of each strand bound and (K_a\) the association constant. Ideal equilibrium mass action gives
\[
K_a=\frac{[AB]}{[A][B]},\qquad \kappa=K_ac,\qquad x=\kappa(1-x)^2,
\]
so
\[
x=\frac{\sqrt{1+4\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{1+4\kappa}+1}.
\]
At (c=1\,\mu\mathrm M\), illustrative values (K_a=10^4\,\mathrm M^{-1}\) and (10^{10}\,\mathrm M^{-1}\) give (\kappa=0.01\) and (10^4\), hence (x\approx0.009804\) and (x\approx0.990050\), respectively. The graph, PM count, and count entropy are identical; the predicted bound yield differs by about two orders of magnitude. These are model parameters, not reported measurements. Sequence-dependent nearest-neighbor free energies and condition-dependent DNA association constants are established physical inputs, not quantities encoded by a binary edge; see SantaLucia and Bielec et al. below.

**The conditional fully assembled distribution also fails to be uniform when energies vary.** On (C_4\) with edges (12,23,34,41\), there are exactly two perfect matchings and \(\ln Z=\ln2\). Give edges (12,34\) dimensionless activity (K\) each and (23,41\) activity (1\) each. Within the fully paired subensemble, the two product weights are (K^2\) and (1\), so the first assembly has probability (K^2/(K^2+1)\). At (K=100\), it has probability (0.999900\), and the actual conditional Shannon entropy is about (0.00102\) nats instead of the uniform combinatorial value (\ln2\approx0.69315\). The unweighted count is still two. This is a direct counterexample to interpreting (\ln|PM(G)|\) as the Gibbs entropy or to inferring which assembly dominates from the count alone.

## Prior art and equally informed baselines

- On the chemistry side, Dirks et al. formulate partition functions for multiple interacting nucleic-acid strands and compute equilibrium complex concentrations subject to test-tube mass conservation; their model explicitly handles distinguishability and symmetry. This is closer to physical yield than a simple compatibility graph. SantaLucia’s nearest-neighbor work supplies sequence-dependent DNA duplex free energies. Bielec et al. experimentally study temperature-, ionic-strength-, and length-dependent kinetic/equilibrium constants, and report that equilibration at low concentration can take days even for short oligos. Together these sources establish the key missing inputs and the kinetic caveat; they do not validate the illustrative numerical (K_a\) values above.
- On the counting side, Jerrum–Sinclair (1989) give an FPRAS for the monomer–dimer partition function (all partial matchings) under their stated weight encoding, while also treating permanent approximation on a restricted bipartite class. Jerrum–Sinclair–Vigoda (2004) give an FPRAS for the permanent of every nonnegative matrix, hence weighted bipartite perfect-match counts. The family-113 paper itself emphasizes that an all-matching FPRAS does not isolate the perfect-matching contribution with relative error when near-perfect/perfect ratios are unrestricted. For planar graphs, Pfaffian methods provide an exact-counting baseline. Thus family 113’s source-reported advance matters most for arbitrary nonbipartite *unweighted perfect-matching counts*, not the ordinary weighted thermodynamic partition function and not bipartite or planar special cases already covered by established methods.
- The family-113 entropy paper attributes its pointwise entropy statement to the perfect-matching entropy conjecture in Anari–Oveis Gharan–Vinzant’s FOCS 2018 work; in the bipartite/permanent setting, stronger coefficient-one entropy comparisons were already known through Schrijver/Gurvits inequalities. The released general-graph bound is mathematically relevant to nonbipartite saturated pairing ensembles, but it does not turn binary compatibility into an energy model.

## Complete costs and measurable gate

For (n=2m\) strand instances and (m_G=|E(G)|\), the *algorithmic* input is the explicit labeled edge list, of encoding length (L=O((n+m_G)\log(n+1))\) bits under standard labels, plus binary rational (\varepsilon,\delta\). The source claim is worst-case polynomial bit time in (L,\varepsilon^{-1},\log\delta^{-1}\), but no usable exponent, benchmark, implementation audit, or energy figure was established here. The cost of obtaining the graph is external: without a sparse generator or supplied compatibility table, evaluating all candidate pairs can require \(\binom n2\) sequence/condition tests. If one pair test costs (C_{\rm pair}\), charge up to \(\binom n2 C_{\rm pair}\) before invoking the FPRAS. Pair-energy calibration, concentrations, temperature/salt, strand copy counts, labeling/symmetry decisions, assay acquisition, synthesis, and instrument/compute energy are additional and unmeasured. The theorem does not acquire or validate those data.

For a uniform sample from the complete combinatorial space, the source’s deletion sampler charges up to (2m_G\) FPRAS calls and (2m_G+1\) deterministic feasibility tests, with its specified polynomial dependence on (\tau^{-1}\). For a (k)-pair sample, first expand (H_k\); its edge count is at most (m_G+n^2\). This is an equally informed **classical algorithmic** baseline for uniform combinatorial designs. For an equilibrium-yield claim, the fair baseline instead includes mass-action/partition-function modeling with the same sequence, concentration, condition, and kinetic information supplied to every method; comparing that physical prediction to a graph-only count would not be equally informed.

The fastest decisive follow-up is a one-pair (K_2\) assay: hold the compatibility graph fixed, measure the association constant and bound fraction over at least two controlled concentration/condition settings, and compare against (x=\kappa(1-x)^2\). For the richer surviving entropy interface, test a saturated system where every complete state is a matching and pair energies are additive: measure edge marginals and independently estimate the conditional distribution over complete products, then test whether its entropy and log partition lie in the displayed pointwise bracket. Significant partial-complex population, kinetic trapping, cooperative contacts, geometry-dependent energies, or nonconstant symmetry multiplicities falsify that restricted physical mapping.

## Compact N36 carry-forward note

The previously inspected Astra N36 converse is a theorem about a different interface, not a microscopy result. For one blind observation (X\sim N(\theta,I_d)\), \(\theta\in\{\pm r e_i:i\le d\}\), a shared retained hybrid state of total dimension (D=\sum_jd_j\), and every later mode-indexed measurement required to reproduce the corresponding scalar Gaussian output law within TV (\varepsilon\), the source’s strengthened theorem claims
\[
\ln D\ge \frac d2\ln(r/\varepsilon)-(5/4+\ln2)d.
\]
At (r=128\varepsilon\), this is (0.48287d\) nats. Its proof uses bounded proper loss, TV-to-regret control, and a Holevo/SSA argument across incompatible later POVMs. We did not find a physical imaging acquisition map satisfying this one-sample blind encoder and all-later-query full-Gaussian-TV interface. A camera’s digitized pixels or a localization-only decision task does not instantiate it automatically; no D-versus-resolution or molecule-localization claim is carried forward. See the pinned [Astra N36 card](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/8ede33c2f09a8abe07b430892b3ac4bb71c27c83/work/agents/measurement_inverse/astra/cards/N36-query-gaussian-converse.txt) and proof pages recorded in the earlier lane’s local source map.
