# Literature novelty assessment: radial planar Coulomb no-Monge candidate

**Checked 2026-10-09.** Candidate is still conditional: the five-annulus finite/angular interval certificate and its global extension are not complete, so every novelty statement below is conditional on a correct proof.

## Bottom line

If proved as stated, this would give a **new symmetry-constrained nonattainment example**: a (C^\infty_c\), absolutely continuous, rotation-invariant common marginal on \(\mathbb R^2\), supported on five annuli, for which no pair of marginal-preserving Borel maps induces a Coulomb optimizer. It would go beyond the existing planar-radial results that only rule out the small prescribed Seidl/co-motion map family, and beyond older radial results that produce some non-graph optimal plans without ruling out a different graph optimizer.

It should **not** be advertised as the first Coulomb no-Monge example in \(\mathbb R^2\), or as a new equality-of-infima theorem. The 2026 OpenAI/math family-373 manuscript claims smooth nonattainment for all inverse-power costs \(|x-y|^{-s}\), every \(d\ge2\), hence includes a (nonradial) smooth \(d=2,s=1\) example; its general Riesz result is broader in dimension/exponent. And equality of Kantorovich and Monge infima for nonatomic identical marginals was already established for Coulomb by Colombo–Di Marino. The candidate's incremental claim is the **radial planar restriction plus exclusion of every graph optimizer**.

Assessment: **meaningful specialist advance, not transformative by itself**. Rotational symmetry is central in the strictly-correlated-electron / radial DFT literature, so proving failure of every co-motion map even in that symmetry class would close a clean gap. But a five-annulus, highly engineered density is an existence example, not evidence that typical radial densities or ground-state densities lack Monge optimizers. It does not establish physical ground-state representability or a general failure theorem.

## Exact prior-work distinctions

1. **Seidl templates only — Colombo–Stra (2015 preprint; journal 2016).** Their paper defines the general Monge problem, the cyclic Monge problem, and Kantorovich problem separately. The planar-radial counterexamples disprove optimality of the DDI/Seidl construction and, in their other example, all maps in the four-map \(\{III,DDI,DID,IDD\}\) class. This is not a theorem excluding arbitrary pairs \((T_2,T_3)\). The first counterexample uses a radial law concentrated in a small neighborhood of the unit sphere; the result is about those special maps, not arbitrary maps. Primary source: [Colombo & Stra, *Counterexamples in multimarginal optimal transport with Coulomb cost and spherically symmetric data*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.08522), especially §§1–2.4 and the proof in §5; published in *Math. Models Methods Appl. Sci.* 26 (2016), DOI [10.1142/S021820251650024X](https://doi.org/10.1142/S021820251650024X).

2. **All four prescribed cyclic Seidl maps — Bindini–De Pascale–Kausamo (arXiv 2020; journal 2022).** They rigorously reduce the planar, three-marginal radial problem to a one-dimensional *radial cost* (not the 1D Coulomb cost), give a condition under which DDI is optimal, and construct smooth radial counterexamples for which none of the four \(I/D\) cyclic templates is optimal (Theorems 1.1, 1.3–1.5). Theorem 1.5 still quantifies over the prescribed forms \((\mathrm{Id},S,S^2)_\#\rho\), \(S\in\{I,D\}^3\), not every Borel Monge pair. Primary source: [arXiv:2011.05063v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.05063), DOI [10.3934/cpaa.2022015](https://doi.org/10.3934/cpaa.2022015).

3. **Radial non-graph optimizers are weaker than no Monge optimizer — Pass (2013).** Pass proves radial reduction and constructs optimal measures with higher-dimensional support / nonuniqueness in specified cases. The dimension argument for a non-graph optimizer uses \(d\ge3\) (support dimension at least \(2d-2>d\)); it does not give the target planar \(d=2,N=3\) no-map result. More generally, exhibiting one optimal plan that is not a graph does not exclude another graph optimizer. The family-373 manuscript makes this distinction explicitly. Primary source: [Pass, *Remarks on the semi-classical Hohenberg–Kohn functional*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.2766), especially §§3.2 and Propositions 3.2.1–3.2.4; published in *Nonlinearity* 26 (2013), 2731–2744, DOI [10.1088/0951-7715/26/9/2731](https://doi.org/10.1088/0951-7715/26/9/2731).

4. **Equality of infima is old.** Colombo–Di Marino prove equality of the Coulomb Kantorovich minimum and cyclic-Monge infimum for identical non-atomic marginals; this supplies approximating maps but does not guarantee an attaining map. Since arbitrary map pairs include cyclic maps, equality also gives equality of the unrestricted Monge infimum. Thus the candidate's equal-infima clause is useful/necessary to state the nonattainment phenomenon sharply, but is not by itself novel. Primary author version: [Colombo & Di Marino, *Equality between Monge and Kantorovich multimarginal problems with Coulomb cost*](https://cvgmt.sns.it/paper/2127/), paper PDF [here](https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/2127/MultiColDim.pdf); published in *Ann. Mat. Pura Appl.* 194 (2015), 307–320, DOI [10.1007/s10231-013-0376-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10231-013-0376-0).

## How the 2026 family-373 result changes the claim

The OpenAI/math manuscript [*A counterexample to the Monge ansatz for the three-marginal Coulomb cost*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/A-counterexample-to-the-Monge-ansatz-for-the-three-marginal-Coulomb-cost-September-25-2026/paper.pdf) is pinned here to commit `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb`, merged 2026-10-08 05:20 UTC. Its Theorem 1.1 constructs a smooth compactly supported \(\mathbb R^3\) marginal with smooth compactly supported square root and proves no arbitrary measure-preserving Borel pair attains; its Theorem 7.1 gives the same general no-Monge/equal-infima phenomenon for every inverse-power exponent \(s>0\) in each \(d\ge2\). In particular, it already claims an unstructured/nonradial smooth planar example for \(d=2,s=1\). Its construction uses five separated ball components, not a rotationally symmetric density.

The initial repository commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a` (2026-10-06) and the Oct. 8 revision `fd4aeeb...` contain **byte-identical** family-373 PDF (SHA-256 `46caabb46552913329e6a1a6226ad2dc196bd231d117562d0495f19a5832ae70`). In the Oct. 8 revision, the comparator challenge file has a `sorry`, but that file is only the pinned target statement. The actual implementation exists under [`lean/OAI/Analysis/CoulombTransport`](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/lean/OAI/Analysis/CoulombTransport): `Conclusion.lean` invokes `FiveComponentGeometry.exists_full_coulomb_conclusion`, and the implementation is split across the library. I did not independently run/compile it; the `sorry` in the comparator alone is not evidence that no proof exists.

This source is an October 2026 repository preprint, not a peer-reviewed journal result. Treat its mathematical claim as a contemporaneous claimed result, and do not conflate the available implementation files with an independently audited or compiled proof. Its own family note scopes the formalized Coulomb theorem to \(\mathbb R^3\); the paper's all-dimension inverse-power theorem is separate from that stated comparator.

The January 2026 reference [Friesecke, arXiv:2601.02616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02616) is **not** a Coulomb nonattainment theorem. Its main result resolves mass-splitting for the time-discrete generalized-Euler cost. §3.2 discusses the prior three-particle Coulomb example in \(\mathbb R^3\) and says that for Coulomb, outside \(d=1\), universal existence of a Monge optimizer is not known; that is precisely an example of an optimal mass-splitting/non-graph plan, not exclusion of all graph optimizers.

## Smoothness and support qualifications

- Colombo–Stra / Bindini–De Pascale–Kausamo already have **radial planar** examples rejecting prescribed Seidl maps; do not describe the five-annulus result as the first smooth radial Coulomb counterexample of any kind.
- Pass's non-map support argument is in higher dimension; it is not the exact \(\mathbb R^2\) arbitrary-map obstruction.
- Family 373 has **smooth compactly supported** marginals (and the base \(\mathbb R^3\) statement also has smooth square root) but is nonradial. Its inverse-power extension includes the planar Coulomb exponent for some nonradial density.
- The candidate's five annuli are mutually separated, so it is a disconnected, deliberately tailored compact support. This is a stronger regularity/geometry package than singular spherical-shell idealizations, but does not imply positive density on a connected or convex domain. Avoid implying generic physical realizability.

## Yukawa / screened Coulomb and Riesz extensions

A bounded targeted search of primary OT/DFT literature for “Yukawa,” “screened Coulomb,” “multi-marginal,” “Monge,” “co-motion,” and radial variants found no prior radial no-Monge example. The relevant primary hit was Cotar–Friesecke–Pass, [*Infinite-body optimal transport with Coulomb cost*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00526-014-0803-0): it mentions the Yukawa potential \(e^{-\epsilon |x-y|}/|x-y|\) in an integrability/finite-energy example, not a three-marginal Monge-attainment result. This is a bounded negative search, not a proof that no such paper exists.

If the candidate's perturbation argument proves a radial planar Yukawa counterexample for one screening parameter, spatial rescaling \(x\mapsto tx\) changes \(e^{-\kappa|x-y|}/|x-y|\) by a positive global factor and sends \(\kappa\mapsto t\kappa\); this would transfer the result to all \(\kappa>0\). That would be a new radial-symmetry extension based on the present search. Likewise, a radial open interval of Riesz exponents near \(s=1\) would be new only in the symmetry-restricted class; family 373 already claims the nonradial result for every \(s>0,d\ge2\). Both conclusions remain conditional on completing the proof/certificates.
