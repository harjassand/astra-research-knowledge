§2: sine transform
------------------
On one site-coordinate, h has diagonal 4J and off-diagonals -2J, with
Dirichlet ghosts phi_0=phi_L=0. For phi_i=sin(pi i/L),
h phi=epsilon_1 phi, epsilon_1=4J(1-cos(pi/L)). On an m-subset X,
f(X)=product_{i in X}phi_i is positive. Summing the unrestricted
one-coordinate equations gives m epsilon_1 f. For each occupied adjacent
pair, the two forbidden coordinate hops have off-diagonal contributions
-2J phi_{i+1}/phi_i and -2J phi_i/phi_{i+1}; the hard-core graph also has
four fewer diagonal units of J (two blocked moves, each removing 2J from
the degree). Removing the blocked hops therefore changes the quotient by

 2J(phi_{i+1}/phi_i + phi_i/phi_{i+1} - 2) >= 0.

The endpoint-killing terms are exactly the one-particle ghost terms. The
positive-ground-function identity for a weighted graph then gives

 <psi,Hcrit,m psi>
  = 2J sum_{X~Y} f(X)f(Y)|psi(X)/f(X)-psi(Y)/f(Y)|^2
    + sum_X (Hcrit,m f)(X)/f(X) |psi(X)|^2,

so Hcrit,m >= m epsilon_1 and A_L >= c_L N. The factor 2J and the sign of
the correction agree with the exact occupation form. The S=1 case (L=2)
also has the stated doubled endpoint field and one-particle energy 4J.

§3: critical trace bound
------------------------
For a block of ell spins, an i,j transposition is a word of 2(j-i)-1
nearest-neighbor swaps with each bond used at most twice. Telescoping the
unitary product, applying Cauchy--Schwarz, and using
|| (I-P)psi ||^2=2< I-P > gives

 <I-P_ij> <= 4(j-i) sum_{a=i}^{j-1}<I-P_{a,a+1}>.

At bond a, sum_{i<=a<j}(j-i)=ell*a*(ell-a)/2 <= ell^3/8. Thus
H_complete <= ell^3/2 H_neu,ell. In total-spin T=ell/2-k,

 H_complete=2J k(ell+1-k),
 H_neu,ell >= 4J k(ell+1-k)/ell^3 >= 2J k/ell^2.

The spin-ell/2-k multiplicity is binom(ell,k)-binom(ell,k-1), hence at
most binom(ell,k), and its irrep dimension is at most ell+1. This yields
Tr exp(-beta H_neu,ell) <= (ell+1)(1+exp(-2J beta/ell^2))^ell.

Dropping positive inter-block bonds and endpoint killing gives a form/min-max
comparison to the tensor sum of block Hamiltonians, not an exponential
operator-monotonicity assertion. For b<=S/ell+1 blocks, this proves

 log T_L(t) <= b log(ell+1)+S exp(-2J t L^2/ell^2).

With ell=floor(L sqrt(Jt/log(L+1))) for sufficiently large L, the exponent
is at least 2 log(L+1), while S/ell is at most
2 sqrt(log(L+1)/(Jt)); the stated C_t log(L+1)^(3/2) bound follows. The
packet's onset is a sufficient computable large-L threshold; finitely many
smaller L are individually finite. These bounds are uniform over all
particle sectors because they are full spin-space traces.

§4: contact density
-------------------
For D(i,y)=<n_i n_y>, D(i,i)=0. If y is distinct from i,i+1, pair each
configuration with exactly one of i,i+1 occupied and y occupied to the
configuration obtained by that hop. Cauchy--Schwarz on the difference of
squared amplitudes gives

 |D(i+1,y)-D(i,y)| <= sqrt(2 E_i^y [D(i+1,y)+D(i,y)]),

where E_i^y is the edge Dirichlet mass with y in the common occupied set.
Each exclusion hop has exactly m-1 common occupied sites, so
sum_{i,y}E_i^y=(m-1)sum_{X~Y}|psi(X)-psi(Y)|^2
<= (m-1)<Hcrit>/(2J). Also sum_{i,y}D(i,y)=m(m-1), and along any selected
paths sum of D(i,y)+D(i+1,y) is at most 2m(m-1), since each D entry is
counted at most twice.

For a bond y,y+1 in the left half, choose x>y minimizing D(x,y), giving a
baseline at most (2/S)sum_x D(x,y). Telescope from the target D(y+1,y) to
D(x,y). The path starts with the comparison i=y+1; the target edge itself
has y=i and is excluded from the difference inequality. For a bond in the
right half, write D(y+1,y)=D(y,y+1), choose x<=y minimizing D(x,y+1), and
telescope left. Each half's baselines total at most 2m(m-1)/S. Cauchy on
the path differences gives per half
(m-1)sqrt(2m/J)*sqrt(<Hcrit>). Summing both halves proves exactly

 <W> <= 4m(m-1)/S
        +4(m-1)sqrt(m/(2J))*sqrt(<Hcrit>).

Thus for m,E=O(log(L)^(3/2)) and <A_L><=E, the right side is
O(log(L)^3/L). This estimate requires no positive contact coupling.

§5: gap cube and near-isometry
------------------------------
For x_1<...<x_m set y_a=x_a-a+1. Weakly ordered y lie in
1<=y_1<=...<=y_m<=n=L-m. If r_v are multiplicities, the labeled orbit has
w=m!/product_v r_v! elements. The map
K psi(z)=psi(sort(z))/sqrt(m!) therefore obeys

 ||K psi||^2=sum_y |psi(y)|^2/product_v r_v!,
 1-||K psi||^2 <= <W> for normalized psi.

The latter is pointwise: the contact count for a weak tuple is
sum_v(r_v-1), zero on distinct tuples and at least one whenever the norm
loss is nonzero. For an allowed chamber hop v -> v+1, the number of labeled
cube edges is w r_v, so the cube-form coefficient relative to the single
source edge is w r_v/m! = 1/[(r_v-1)!r_{v+1}! product_{u!=v,v+1}r_u!] <= 1.
The lower and upper ghost counts have the same form. This verifies the form
inequality <Kpsi,B_{L,m}Kpsi><=<psi,A_L psi>, including repeated-coordinate
edges and both boundaries.

Combining the contact bound with this norm formula gives the packet's eta_L
in (19). On the span of the first j source eigenvectors with a_j<=E,
every vector has norm loss at most eta_L and cube energy at most source
energy. K is injective on that span. Min-max gives b_j<=a_j/(1-eta_L),
which is exactly (20); summing only the source eigenvalues below E gives
(21). This restricted min-max use is valid even though K is not an
isometry. The target symmetric cube dimension is binom(L-1,m), equal to the
source m-sector dimension.

§6: global cutoff and Bose product
-----------------------------------
For mu<lambda_c, select c_0 in (max(mu,0),lambda_c), set
d=(c_0-mu)/2 and a=min(1/2,d/c_0). Since A_L>=c_0N eventually,
(1-a)A_L-(mu+d)N>=0, proving A_L-mu N>=a A_L+dN. The two tail estimates
follow by extracting dN on N>M and half of aA_L on A_L>E. The choices in
(25) make each tail at most exp(-C log(L+1)^(3/2)); they include the full
spin-space trace T_L, so no binomial multiplicities are omitted.

On the retained space m<=M_L, A_L<=E_L, the eta_L from §5 is
O(log(L+1)^3/L). The one-particle cube energies satisfy
L^2 epsilon_{k,L-m} >= L^2 epsilon_{k,L-1}; for k>=2,
L^2 epsilon_{k,L-1}>=8J k^2. Taking eta<=1/4 gives higher-mode gaps at
least 6Jk^2. The first-mode gap remains strictly positive because
L^2 epsilon_{1,L-1}=c_L -> lambda_c and mu<lambda_c; imposing
(1-eta)c_0-mu>=d gives the explicit first factor. Hence the retained sum
is dominated by the finite-mode ideal Bose product and then by the finite
constant (27), since mu<lambda_c<24J. Dominated convergence of the mode
products gives the limsup (28). Sector min-max handles every g_L>=0, and
the fugacity shift by s proves the exponential tail (29).
