# Canonical enzyme-kinetics models versus the C8 input

**Finding.** The source-supported Goldbeter–Koshland covalent-modification
cycle is a compact, scientifically important Michaelis–Menten (MM) network
with positive sparse rational rates, exact enzyme/substrate structure, and a
published parameterized simulation. It can be represented directly in the
C8 JSON input. The current no-expansion recognizer returns `REFUTED` for its
declared P/N33 essential-source predicate, with an exact tie witness. That
formal outcome is not a biological failure: the reduced model is confined to
the conserved-total line \(W+W^*=1\), while the witness is an ambient
positive-quadrant growth direction outside that compatibility class.

The published coNP-hardness construction does **not** preserve this canonical
MM-cycle structure. It uses dimension and channel count that grow with a SAT
instance, formula-specific supports and jump vectors, guard channels with
negative exponents, and no biochemical moiety/enzyme conservation constraints.
The theorem therefore does not establish hardness for this two-channel
Goldbeter–Koshland family, or for a restricted canonical Hill/MM subclass.

## Source-grounded model

Goldbeter and Koshland's original 1981 PNAS paper studies a reversible protein
modification cycle. Their Eqs. 1–2 give the mechanism

\[
W+E_1 \rightleftharpoons WE_1 \longrightarrow W^*+E_1,
\qquad
W^*+E_2 \rightleftharpoons W^*E_2 \longrightarrow W+E_2.
\]

Here \(E_1\) and \(E_2\) are distinct converter enzymes; the target moiety
passes between unmodified \(W\) and modified \(W^*\). Eqs. 3–6 show the
explicit complex-state balances and conservation of target and enzyme totals.
The reduced rate law follows their QSSA treatment after the assumption that
the productive enzyme–substrate complexes are negligible in the target total.
With normalized target fractions \(w=[W]/W_T\) and
\(w^*=[W^*]/W_T\), \(w+w^*=1\), write the two channel rates as

\[
r_1(w,w^*)=\frac{V_1}{W_T}\frac{w}{K_1+w},\qquad
r_2(w,w^*)=\frac{V_2}{W_T}\frac{w^*}{K_2+w^*},
\qquad
K_i=K_{mi}/W_T,\quad V_i=k_i E_{iT}.
\]

The state order is \((w,w^*)\); the channel jumps are
\(\nu_1=(-1,+1)\) and \(\nu_2=(+1,-1)\). Thus both channels preserve
\(w+w^*\). The reduced rate network has the exact positive-rational encoding

| Channel | Rate \(P/Q\) | Jump |
|---|---|---|
| kinase conversion | \(((3/25)w)/(1/100+w)\) | \((-1,+1)\) |
| phosphatase conversion | \(((1/10)w^*)/(1/100+w^*)\) | \((+1,-1)\) |

The fixture freezes a published illustrative simulation setting at the upper
endpoint of the first Fig. 5 sweep: \(W_T=100\,\mu\mathrm M\),
\(K_1=K_2=10^{-2}\), \(V_2=10\,\mu\mathrm M\,\mathrm s^{-1}\), and
\(V_1/V_2=1.2=6/5\). Hence \(K_{m1}=K_{m2}=1\,\mu\mathrm M\),
\(V_1=12\,\mu\mathrm M\,\mathrm s^{-1}\), and the normalized rate
coefficients are \(V_1/W_T=3/25\,\mathrm s^{-1}\) and
\(V_2/W_T=1/10\,\mathrm s^{-1}\). Fig. 5 also reports a second ratio sweep
from 0.5 to 0.8. With its fixed \(V_2\), these give the printed illustrative
\(V_1\) ranges \([5,12]\) and \([5,8]\,\mu\mathrm M\,\mathrm s^{-1}\),
respectively.

These figure settings are simulation inputs, not measured confidence bounds.
The paper's general domain is positive \(K_i,V_i,W_T\); it does not give a
validated compact parameter box for a named kinase/phosphatase pair. The
primary-source equation and parameter details are transcribed in
[`goldbeter_koshland_1981.md`](source_cards/goldbeter_koshland_1981.md), and
the exact rational input is
[`goldbeter_koshland_1981_mm.json`](fixtures/goldbeter_koshland_1981_mm.json).

The underlying explicit mechanism is separately encoded in
[`goldbeter_koshland_1981_mass_action_mechanism.json`](fixtures/goldbeter_koshland_1981_mass_action_mechanism.json).
Its six mass-action arrows are W+E1 association, WE1 dissociation,
WE1 catalysis, W*+E2 association, W*E2 dissociation, and W*E2 catalysis.
It conserves \(W+W^*+WE_1+W^*E_2\), \(E_1+WE_1\), and
\(E_2+W^*E_2\). The source does not supply one numerical tuple of all six
microscopic rate constants for the fixture; all six coefficients are set to
one as a support-only representative. Because C8's predicate ignores positive
coefficient magnitudes, this placeholder is not used as biological
parameter evidence. Goldbeter–Koshland separately examine non-negligible
complexes and nonproductive cross-binding; those effects are omitted from this
minimal six-arrow mechanism and from the two-rate QSSA fixture.

## Exact recognizer replay

Run from the repository root with:

```sh
python3 work/agents/c8_implicit_rational_geometry/child_models/replay_goldbeter.py
```

The saved replay is
[`goldbeter_recognizer.json`](replay/goldbeter_recognizer.json). It used Z3
4.15.4 and the current
[`recognize_sparse_rational.py`](../recognize_sparse_rational.py) exact
support recognizer. Both fixture outputs are `REFUTED` under the declared C8
P/N33 rule, which treats every outward member of an exact top tie as a
failure.

For the reduced MM instance, the verified direction witness is
\(\omega=(1/4,1/2)\): both rates have \(\tau=0\), while
\(\omega\cdot\nu_1=1/4\) and \(\omega\cdot\nu_2=-1/4\). This is an exact, coefficient-independent support
calculation. But the positive log-ray \((e^{t/4},e^{t/2})\) is incompatible
with \(w+w^*=1\); no trajectory in the fixed-target-total compatibility
class can follow that unbounded direction. For the explicit mechanism, the
saved second witness similarly refutes the all-positive-orthant predicate at
an exact tie between WE1 dissociation and WE1 catalysis. That direction also
does not encode a physically accessible escape from the fixed target and
enzyme-total polytope.

The reduced-rate calculation can be checked by hand. For an arbitrary
direction \(\omega=(a,b)\), the support scores are
\(\tau_1=a-\max(0,a)=\min(a,0)\) and
\(\tau_2=b-\max(0,b)=\min(b,0)\). At \((a,b)=(1/4,1/2)\), both
coordinates are positive, so both saturating rates have asymptotic exponent
zero; the opposite jumps then give opposite nonzero projections, one outward.
For the explicit-mechanism witness \((-1/2,1/2,0,0,-1,0)\), WE1
dissociation and WE1 catalysis have the same mass-action source exponent
(zero) and opposite nonzero projections; the outward catalytic member is
therefore a top tie under C8's all-top-inward rule.

Therefore these runs establish that the *ambient exact support predicate*
does not automatically inherit its interpretation from a conserved
biochemical model. They do not show that either model is unstable, transient,
or nonpermanent on a compatibility class. This distinction is especially
important here: the idealized QSSA model has a compact one-dimensional
compatibility class, and the explicit mechanism has fixed finite moiety
totals.

## Canonical restrictions versus the hardness construction

The source model imposes the following restrictions:

- The QSSA/MM form has fixed dimension two and two opposing conversion
  channels. Each numerator is a single substrate monomial; each denominator
  is a positive constant plus that same substrate monomial.
- The two jumps are exact opposites, enforcing target-total conservation.
  The explicit mechanism additionally enforces fixed kinase and phosphatase
  totals through enzyme-bound occupancy states.
- Enzyme capacities and Michaelis constants are coupled physical parameters:
  \(V_i=k_iE_{iT}\) and \(K_i=K_{mi}/W_T\). They are not arbitrary
  edge-by-edge support design variables. A measured kinase activity or input
  regulator may change \(V_1\), but the source's illustrative sweep is a
  one-dimensional ratio change, not an independent schedule on a growing
  family of channels.
- If the conserved target is eliminated by setting \(w=1-w^*\), the forward
  numerator becomes \(1-w^*\), with a negative coefficient. That reduced
  one-coordinate formula no longer obeys C8's positive-coefficient
  support-only input promise. Keeping both species preserves positivity but
  retains the invariant and exposes the ambient-direction mismatch above.

By contrast, C8's 3SAT reduction has \(n+1\) coordinates and
\(3n+m+1\) channels. It uses clause-specific supports, arbitrary literal
incidence in reaction vectors, paired coordinate guards, and rates such as
\(X_i/Z^2\) and \(1/(X_i Z^2)\). The latter use negative exponents; the
construction has no target/enzyme conservation laws, no fixed converter
topology, no shared enzyme-pool accounting, and no requirement that each
reaction vector be compatible with a source complex and product complex.
Its rational-rate hardness therefore demonstrates hardness for the general
sparse positive generalized-rational interface, not for the Goldbeter–Koshland
cycle or for enzyme MM/Hill models that preserve these canonical restrictions.

A Hill-shaped *system output* also need not correspond to a Hill exponent in
any one channel. Huang and Ferrell's primary MAPK study reports an observed
effective input/output slope near \(n_H=4.9\) for p42 MAPK, while attributing
the response to cascade structure and assumptions about distributive
phosphorylation and partial saturation. That source is recorded in
[`huang_ferrell_1996_mapk.md`](source_cards/huang_ferrell_1996_mapk.md); it is
not presented as another extracted rational fixture or as evidence that each
reaction has Hill exponent 4.9.

## Limitations and next useful gate

This is a two-source model-coverage audit, not a systematic survey of
published biochemical models. The exact Goldbeter–Koshland fixture uses
source-reported simulation settings rather than a measured joint parameter
interval. The explicit mass-action fixture's unit coefficients are declared
placeholders. C8's `REFUTED` result is supported by a rational witness replay;
it says nothing about empirical fit, full enzyme-complex approximation error,
or invariant-class permanence. A meaningful next test would formalize a
conservation-restricted support predicate or analyze the reduced scalar
dynamics directly, then compare that criterion with a genuinely measured
kinase/phosphatase parameter box. No restriction-preserving hardness result
is established here.
