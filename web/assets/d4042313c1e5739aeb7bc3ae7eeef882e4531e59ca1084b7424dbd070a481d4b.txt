1. Finite-rank realization and Hankel context
---------------------------------------------
J. W. Carlyle and A. Paz, “Realizations by Stochastic Finite Automata,”
Journal of Computer and System Sciences 5(1), 26–40 (1971), DOI
10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3.
Primary record: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3
Publisher metadata/abstract: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000071800053

The publisher abstract states the finite-rank characterization for string
functions and finite-state sequential realization. Used only as background
for the Hankel/linear-representation language. The short finite witness
argument in RESULT.txt (stabilization of reachable/observable spans by r-1)
is proved there directly. The publisher full text was not retrieved in this
pass.

2. Observable spectral learner and equal-data baseline
-------------------------------------------------------
D. Hsu, S. M. Kakade, and T. Zhang, “A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden
Markov Models,” COLT 2009; journal version, JCSS 2012.
Original author PDF: https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~djhsu/papers/hmm.pdf
arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.4413

The original PDF was opened and inspected. Its observable representation
section gives an operator parameterization from observable probabilities
(PDF pp. 2–5). Figure 1 explicitly takes a supplied state count m and N
independent observation triples, estimates P_1, P_{2,1}, and P_{3,x,1}, then
uses an SVD and pseudoinverses (PDF pp. 6–7). The sample-complexity discussion
assumes independent samples and says a long sequence can discount effective
sample size by the mixing rate (PDF pp. 3 and 20). Its perturbation appendix
states the singular-value perturbation inequality (PDF p. 22). This is a
strong same-data spectral/observable-operator control, but its rank parameter
and probability tables are inputs/estimates of a different form; no result
from that paper is used to prove rank-free stopping here.

3. Empirical processes under beta mixing
----------------------------------------
B. Yu, “Rates of Convergence for Empirical Processes of Stationary Mixing
Sequences,” Annals of Probability 22(1), 94–116 (1994), DOI
10.1214/aop/1176988849.
Primary DOI: https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176988849
Project Euclid record: https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-probability/volume-22/issue-1/Rates-of-convergence-for-empirical-processes-of-stationary-mixing/10.1214/aop/1176988849.full

Abstract/metadata were checked: the paper studies empirical processes for
stationary absolutely regular sequences and describes block/coupling methods.
This note needs only a finite family of word indicators, so after a block
coupling it applies the elementary independent Bernoulli Hoeffding inequality
and a union bound; no VC-class theorem is invoked. Direct full-text retrieval
was unavailable in this browsing pass.

4. Berbee coupling
------------------
H. C. P. Berbee, Random Walks with Stationary Increments and Renewal Theory,
Mathematical Centre Tracts 112 (1979), ISBN 978-90-6196-182-6.
CWI repository record: https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/12694

The CWI primary record and full-text link were checked. Its PDF is 42 MB and
the browser fetch failed due the size limit, so the theorem text itself was
not inspected. The coupling inequality used here is explicitly stated with
its beta convention. It also follows by sequentially coupling each separated
block to its stationary marginal, with mismatch probability bounded by the
absolute-regularity coefficient, and taking a union bound. A later source
that states the block version and cites Berbee/Yu is available at
https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/document/3440591, but it is not treated as
the primary source.

5. Independent bounded-variable concentration
----------------------------------------------
W. Hoeffding, “Probability Inequalities for Sums of Bounded Random Variables,”
Journal of the American Statistical Association 58(301), 13–30 (1963), DOI
10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830.
Publisher DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830

The primary bibliographic record was checked. The employed two-sided bound
for Bernoulli indicators is the standard consequence
Pr(|mean-p|>x) <= 2 exp(-2 n x^2). The theorem is derived from this finite
class bound after coupling; no other concentration theorem is needed.
