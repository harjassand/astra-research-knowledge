# Reaction-selectivity lead: downstream commitment after reversible super-reduction

## Result

The best distinct chemistry line is Edgecomb et al.'s 2026 cyclopropyl-ketone/styrene photoreductive annulation. It is separate from the repository's active H2-source TST, DAR energy, and mScarlet/flavin RF work. The source reports selective formal [3+2] coupling despite roughly a 1-V redox mismatch: a difficult-to-reduce cyclopropyl ketone is fragmented and pulled toward annulation while the easier styrene radical anion is returned by back electron transfer (BET) before it becomes hydrogenated product.

The selectivity mechanism and its two-channel steady-state equation are already in the published SI, so neither is a new result here. I derived a conditional $N$-channel extension that acts as a static setpoint compiler: given measured intervals for each channel's capture flux and effective product-commitment/BET ratio, it either certifies an acceptor concentration that meets a target product fraction while maximizing target throughput under a finite two-state catalyst pool, returns **REFUTED** for an impossible selectivity floor, or **UNKNOWN** when the target's commitment advantage is not established. An exact-rational implementation and certificate are in [kinetic_commitment_gate.py](kinetic_commitment_gate.py) and [kinetic_commitment_certificate.json](kinetic_commitment_certificate.json); the proof and interface are in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).

This is a completed mathematical design component, not a validated chemical design capability. Its chemistry-side bottleneck is real: the source gives conflicting Ket-1 electron-capture constants, the two-channel model's equal/diffusion-limited SET premise is not confirmed by those measurements, and the effective commitment hazards and in-reaction acceptor concentration are not measured in the catalytic mixture. The extension's novelty and experimental utility remain **UNKNOWN**.

## Exact design rule

For channel $i$, write the source flux into a reduced intermediate as $\alpha_iD$, where $\alpha_i=k_{ET,i}[S_i]$, and let product commitment and BET hazards be $c_i$ and $b_iA$. Under constant concentrations and quasi-steady intermediates,

\[
v_i=D\alpha_i\frac{c_i}{c_i+b_iA}
=D\alpha_i\frac{q_i}{q_i+A},\qquad q_i=\frac{c_i}{b_i}.
\]

The target share is monotone increasing in $A$ whenever its $q_j$ exceeds every competitor's $q_i$. With independent rectangular rate intervals, the script uses the target's lower $\alpha_j,q_j$ and each competing channel's upper values to form a robust lower bound. When $D+A=C_T$, target throughput decreases strictly with $A$, so the minimum feasible acceptor setting is optimal among static settings meeting the selectivity floor. This supplies a quantitative rate-acquisition-to-setpoint pipeline once the premises are measured.

As a direct read of the source's disputed measured SET ratio, equal substrate concentrations give $g=\alpha_{Ket-1}/\alpha_{styrene}=2/15$ if the SI prose value $4\times10^9$ is used against styrene's $3\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{M^{-1}s^{-1}}$. In the high-BET limit, a 90% target fraction then requires an effective commitment-scale ratio $q_{Ket-1}/q_{styrene}\ge67.5$; matching the source's 70:7 product odds requires at least 75. The SI Fig. 5c dimensionless illustration $\beta=bA/c_2=560$ yields the finite-BET chemical-rate gates $u=k_1/k_2\ge76.5957$ for 90% and $u\ge86.4198$ for 10:1 odds under $g=2/15$. These are conditional thresholds, not estimates of the reaction's in situ rates. If the conflicting caption value is used instead, $g=1$ and the same toy model gates fall to 9.1304 and 10.1633. The factor-of-eight swing is why the SI conflict cannot be silently resolved.

## Source boundary and actual inputs

Edgecomb et al. report in [Nature 656 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10897-7) that super-potent photoreductants can make outer-sphere SET diffusion-limited and make product distribution depend on downstream chemistry and BET. The official supplementary file is [available here](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10897-7/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10897_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

The SI's perylene/390-nm screen reports 70% annulation product A and 7% styrene hydrogenation B by GC, an A:B ratio of 10:1 among those two products; these numbers are not isolated yields, a rate ratio, or Faradaic efficiency. The stated screen consumes a 0.3-mmol substrate charge at about 0.075 M, 7.5 mol% perylene, 390-nm Kessil illumination, 1-mA electrolysis to roughly 1 F/mol, about 7.75 h, a divided cell, Zn sacrificial anode, reticulated-vitreous-carbon cathode, 0.4-M anodic/cathodic electrolytes, dry/degassed DMF, nitrogen handling, and chromatographic workup. One faraday per mole on 0.3 mmol is 28.9 C (about 8.0 h at 1 mA). The SI does not report the integrated cell-voltage history, lamp wall power, incident/absorbed photon count, quantum yield, or energy per mole of isolated product. I charge the named current, catalyst, electrode, solvent, electrolyte, gas, time, and workup inputs; full optical/electrical energy remains unpriced.

The detailed conditional evidence and exact SI locators are in [SOURCE_NOTES.md](SOURCE_NOTES.md). Nearby primary work already covers super-reduction plus PCET mitigation of BET ([Bains et al., Science 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adw1648)) and the formal alkyl-cyclopropyl-ketone [3+2] transformation with SmI2 ([Mansell et al., JACS 2024](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.4c03073)). Thus this packet does not claim priority for the broad chemistry, the transformation, or the two-channel algebra.

## Independent checks and decisive experiment

Run **python3 kinetic_commitment_gate.py** to reproduce all exact-rational thresholds and the output certificate. The script also constructs a three-channel rectangular-uncertainty example, certifies an acceptor-setpoint bracket by rational bisection, and refuses to claim monotonicity when target commitment ordering is not established. It compiles with Python and the emitted JSON parses. These are mathematical interface checks; no chemistry was rerun, no new electronic-structure calculation was made, and no external reviewer or experiment validated the extension.

The decisive experiment is one same-condition kinetic dataset in the actual solvent, substrates, electrolyte, light/current protocol, and catalyst state: determine $k_{ET,i}[S_i]$, channel-specific BET hazards, productive first-passage hazards (including the full multistep Ket-1 path), and $[A]$; then compare predicted product fractions and target flux with time-resolved GC at held-out light/potential or acceptor settings. Measure current and cell voltage over the run, Zn loss, and incident/absorbed photon flux in parallel. Resolve the SI's Ket-1 capture conflict first. A held-out product split outside the interval-robust bound, or a matched-information redox-potential/Marcus baseline with a better selectivity-throughput-energy point, rejects the proposed design capability.
