# Independent audit: transient thermal nonreciprocity and matched ports

**Verdict.** The proposed fixed-operator reciprocity theorem is correct for the stated paired source/readout, with a necessary distinction between body-power inputs and initial-temperature perturbations. A two-node counterexample shows that equal initial temperature jumps need not be reciprocal: different heat capacities encode different deposited energies. Equal-energy power impulses and properly paired terminal variables restore reciprocity. The 2022 primary paper gives a closely related, explicit *terminal* reciprocity observable (its published Eq. 4), and therefore preempts a generic claim that changing which boundary is hot creates terminal nonreciprocity in its physically mass-conserving, periodic-steady-state model. It does not prove the full LTI all-time theorem or settle the 2025 paper’s observable.

For the 2025 paper, the accessible ScienceDirect indexed abstract/introduction says that a Dirac-function heat source and Green-function method are used, and describes swapping source and receiver / transient spatial-profile asymmetry. The article text and equations were inaccessible (ScienceDirect 403; no author copy obtained). **UNKNOWN:** whether its pair is equal power or equal initial ΔT, whether the receiver is temperature or a conjugate boundary flux, how profiles are normalized, and the precise rectification formula. This boundary is insufficient to diagnose an error in its source definition or to say its claimed observable equals the matched-port transfer matrix.

## Conditional theorem and proof

Let \(\eta(x)=\rho(x)c_p(x)>0\) be volumetric heat capacity, and let \(K\) be the weak Fourier operator with symmetric conductivity \(k(x)=k(x)^T\succ0\). Include any fixed Robin loss \(h(x)\ge0\) in its form:
\[
a(u,v)=\int_\Omega \nabla u\cdot k\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega_R} huv\,dS,
\qquad m(u,v)=\int_\Omega\eta uv\,dx.
\]
Fixed homogeneous Dirichlet/Neumann/Robin conditions are applied to the unforced remainder of the boundary. The body-source model is \(C\dot T+KT=q\). For port profiles \(b_j\), drive \(q(x,t)=b_j(x)p_j(t)\), and read the paired weighted temperature \(y_i(t)=\int b_i(x)T(x,t)\,dx\), with identical profile and normalization in the source and readout roles of each port. Then for \(\Re s>0\),
\[
H_{ij}(s)=\int_\Omega b_i(K+sC)^{-1}b_j\,dx=H_{ji}(s).
\]
Indeed the weak form \(a_s(u,v)=a(u,v)+s m(u,v)\) is symmetric (complex-bilinear, not sesquilinear); with \(a_s(u_j,v)=\int b_jv\), we get \(H_{ij}=a_s(u_i,u_j)=a_s(u_j,u_i)=H_{ji}\). Laplace uniqueness gives equal paired impulse responses. Symmetric Robin losses preserve the identity. Anisotropic symmetric \(k\) is allowed.

The units/normalization are part of the interface: for a one-watt distributed input, take \(\int b_jdx=1\), and measure the temperature average with the same \(b_i\). A separate boundary-port version follows by replacing body sources with Neumann power-flux functionals and paired boundary-temperature traces; the Dirichlet-to-Neumann map is symmetric for Dirichlet temperature drives and total conjugate reaction fluxes. Do not combine variable Dirichlet forcing with a theorem that assumes those same Dirichlet values are fixed and homogeneous. Initial states must be reset or their free response subtracted. Nonconjugate weighting, different spatial profiles, advection, nonsymmetric conductivity, time-varying operators, nonlinear constitutive laws, or powered feedback fall outside the theorem.

For the free initial-temperature propagator \(G_T(x,y,t)=[e^{-C^{-1}Kt}](x,y)\), the correct identity is
\[
\eta(x)G_T(x,y,t)=\eta(y)G_T(y,x,t).
\]
A point energy impulse and an initial temperature delta are not the same input: integrating \(C\dot T+KT=b\delta(t)\) gives \(C\Delta T=b\). Unit deposited energy produces initial jump \(C^{-1}b\), not a unit temperature jump at every source location.

## Exact independent two-node diagnostic

Use \(C=\operatorname{diag}(1,4)\), \(K=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\). For paired unit-power node ports,
\[
(K+sC)^{-1}_{12}=(K+sC)^{-1}_{21}=\frac1{s(4s+5)},\qquad h_{12}(t)=h_{21}(t)=\frac{1-e^{-5t/4}}5\quad(t>0).
\]
For unit initial temperature jumps instead,
\[
[e^{-C^{-1}Kt}]_{12}=\frac45(1-e^{-5t/4}),\qquad
[e^{-C^{-1}Kt}]_{21}=\frac15(1-e^{-5t/4}),
\]
so their ratio is 4, exactly \(C_2/C_1\). The two cases have equal ΔT but unequal stored energies; equal-energy initialization divides the source-2 jump by 4 and restores equality. This is a counterexample to *unweighted initial-temperature Green symmetry*, not to power/temperature port reciprocity. The executable `code/two_node_reciprocity.py` checks the exact formulas numerically.

## Primary prior and source boundaries

- **2022, exact terminal comparator:** Li et al., “Reciprocity of thermal diffusion in time-modulated systems,” *Nature Communications* 13, 167 (2022), [DOI/full article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-27903-3), [open primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.11164). Its published Eq. (4) defines reciprocity by swapping fixed hot/cold Dirichlet temperatures at terminals 1 and 2 and comparing time-averaged terminal heat fluxes:
  \[
  (\langle q_{f,2}\rangle,\langle q_{f,1}\rangle)^T
  =-(\langle q_{b,1}\rangle,\langle q_{b,2}\rangle)^T,
  \]
  i.e. \(\langle Q_{b,1}\rangle=-\langle Q_{f,2}\rangle\), \(\langle Q_{b,2}\rangle=-\langle Q_{f,1}\rangle\) with the article’s signs. Here the source is a pair of boundary temperature constraints (hot/cold swapped); the measured receivers are average fluxes at both terminal ports. It is a direct prior for the *terminal* observable, but its theorem concerns harmonic-periodic steady state in mass-conserving time-modulated material, including the required convective/material-derivative term, not the fixed-operator impulse response for all times. Their primary text also warns that freely changing density without the associated mass motion omits a convective term. It is therefore the strongest same-information operational baseline, not a derivation of the 2025 experiment.
- **2015, explicit nonlinear/switching comparator:** Li et al., “Temperature-dependent transformation thermotics: From switchable thermal cloaks to macroscopic thermal diodes,” *PRL* 115, 195503 (2015), [primary arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/html/1505.00971), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.115.195503). It specifies \(\tilde R_1(T)=R_1[1-(1+e^{\beta(T-T_c)})^{-1}]\) (type A) or \(R_1/(1+e^{\beta(T-T_c)})\) (type B), then
  \(\tilde\kappa_r=\kappa_0(r'-\tilde R_1)/r'\), \(\tilde\kappa_\theta=\kappa_0r'/(r'-\tilde R_1)\) (Eq. 3). Its layered realization uses \(\kappa_1(T)=\kappa_a+(\kappa_0-\kappa_a)/(1+e^{\beta(T-T_c)})\), \(\kappa_2(T)=\kappa_b-(\kappa_b-\kappa_0)/(1+e^{\beta(T-T_c)})\) (Eq. 4). Rectification (maximum reported ratio 30 for that numerical parameter set) relies on temperature-dependent properties or SMA-triggered copper/EPS connectivity. This is outside fixed linear time-invariant Fourier diffusion.
- **2017, formula-level limit:** Herrera, Luo & Go, “Thermal Rectification Under Transient Conditions: The Role of Thermal Capacitance and Thermal Conductivity,” *J. Heat Transfer* 139, 091301 (2017), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1115/1.4036339). The primary abstract available here establishes a two-slab composite, transient rectification several times steady-state under some conditions, and an essential role for diffusivity plus temperature-dependent conductivity or thermal capacitance/nonlinearity. A later primary article describes their calculation as a hot-side boundary-temperature impulse from a uniformly cold initial condition with linearized \(k(T)\). The 2017 publisher PDF was blocked (403); its exact equation and rectification-ratio formula were **not** reconstructed, so this is an abstract/model-class comparison, not equation-level validation. Its nonlinear material/boundary-step protocol is not the fixed linear matched-port benchmark.

The 2025 primary indexed record is Su, Li, Guenneau & Xiong, “Transient nonreciprocity heat transfer with inhomogeneous materials,” *Int. J. Heat Mass Transfer* 239, 126570 (2025), [DOI/article page](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijheatmasstransfer.2024.126570). Indexed text reports transient spatial-inversion asymmetry for inhomogeneous \(k,\rho,c_p\), symmetric steady state, Dirac-source/Green-function framing, and a perforated metal-plate experiment (15 cm plate, 30 segments, 0.2–0.48 cm holes filled with differing substances). Those indexed snippets do **not** expose source normalization, receiver functional, exact observable, or equations. No claim of source-definition error or contradiction is supported by the material available in this audit.

## Strongest baseline, costs, and discriminating experiment

The fair theoretical baseline uses the same sample, coefficients, geometry, initial state, and time window. Compare (i) equal initial ΔT at two locations, (ii) equal deposited joules at those locations, and (iii) conjugate terminal drives/readouts: equal power-in/weighted-temperature-out or swapped Dirichlet temperatures/conjugate flux-out. Record both ΔT and calibrated energy, temperature field, terminal heat flux, boundary losses, contact resistances, pulse timing, and motor/heater/control power if any. Temperature averaging, source-profile normalization, and sensor gain must be frozen before the forward/reverse comparisons.

For the two-node discriminator, capacities 1 and 4 predict an apparent 4:1 response under equal ΔT and exactly 1:1 under equal deposited energy; these are parameter-free outputs of the same reciprocal model. For a plate, place matched localized heaters and paired temperature averages at candidate ports, drive each with the same measured joule pulse, and swap source/receiver. In a second terminal test swap hot/cold boundary temperatures and compare cross-paired terminal fluxes as in 2022 Eq. 4. The decisive counter-result is statistically resolved inequality under correctly normalized energy/conjugate ports, after fixed-baseline free evolution and parasitic exchange are removed, in a verified linear, reciprocal, time-invariant sample. Equal-ΔT asymmetry alone is not that result.

Costs are measurement and control costs rather than a new material design: two directional runs per port pair, calibrated power/temperature/flux sensors, baseline reset, calorimetric energy balance, and finite-element reconstruction of the actual nonuniform source shape. If a sample is rotated, modulated, or SMA-switched, meter the actuator and switching energy; these alter the model class. No numeric experimental cost or 2025 material-level performance prediction is defensible without the article’s full protocol and device data.
