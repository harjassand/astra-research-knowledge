This append-only note corrects v1's q=5 curvature threshold, proves the entire rate region for that source, gives a maximal envelope criterion for when the one-sided converse certifies Slepian–Wolf optimality, and tests the converse on a non-doubly-symmetric binary modulo-sum source with a known matching structured code. v1 remains preserved; its q=5 threshold of 16 was a valid but needlessly weak sufficient bound, not the correct threshold for that example.

1. Model and candidate converse retained from v1
================================================

The model is iid finite-alphabet (X_i,Y_i), separate encoders observing only X^n and Y^n, no feedback, no encoder cooperation, no decoder side information, and a decoder that must recover Z^n=f^n(X^n,Y^n) with block error tending to zero. Rates are n^{-1}log_2|M_X| and n^{-1}log_2|M_Y|. All claims below are vanishing-block-error claims. A fixed codebook selected in an achievability proof is known to the parties and does not require run-time common randomness or a transmitted seed.

For a Latin-square function f, set W_Y(y|x)=P(Y=y|X=x) and W_Z(z|x)=sum_y W_Y(y|x)1{f(x,y)=z}. The candidate internally derived one-sided bound is, for every λ≥1,

    R_X + λR_Y >= H(X,Y) + min_{U-X-Y}{λH(Z|U)-H(Y|U)}
                 = H(X,Y) - cav(phi_λ)(P_X),
    phi_λ(p) = H(p W_Y) - λ H(p W_Z).                         (1)

Here U ranges over finite auxiliaries; equivalently, cav(phi)(P_X) is the maximum of E[phi(P_{X|U})] over posterior decompositions with mean P_X. Carathéodory gives a finite support of at most q+1 posterior laws for q input symbols. The symmetric inequality follows by interchanging X and Y. v1 contains the complete candidate derivation. Its function-specific step uses only that x↦f(x,y) is one-to-one for each y; the tensorization uses the conditioned Csiszár sum identity and iid source coordinates, not a group law. The proof remains an internal derivation, not an external proof validation.

2. Corrected q=5 example: threshold 1 and the full SW region
============================================================

Use the order-5 Latin square in v1:

        2 0 3 1 4
        3 1 4 0 2
        4 2 0 3 1
        1 3 2 4 0
        0 4 1 2 3

It has diagonal g=(2,1,0,4,3), a permutation. Let X be uniform and P(Y=y|X=x)=4/5 at y=x and 1/20 at each other y. Both X and Y are uniform. For W_Y, row x has its 4/5 entry at y=x. Since row x of f permutes the y symbols, W_Z(·|x) has its 4/5 entry at z=g(x) and 1/20 elsewhere. Therefore, with output permutation π(x)=g(x),

    W_Z(z|x) = W_Y(π^{-1}(z)|x) for every x,z.                (2)

Thus pW_Z is a relabeling of pW_Y for every input law p, and their output entropies agree identically. In (1),

    phi_λ(p) = (1-λ) H(pW_Y).

Since entropy is concave, phi_λ is convex for every λ≥1; at λ=1 it is identically zero. In particular the curvature ratio from v1 is exactly 1: the two Hessian quadratic forms are equal after the output permutation. The one-sided converse gives, for every λ≥1,

    R_X + λR_Y >= H(X,Y) + (λ-1)H(Y|X)
                 = H(X) + λH(Y|X).                            (3)

For the reverse direction, the same diagonal permutation works. Given Y=y, X has probability 4/5 at x=y and 1/20 elsewhere; Z=f(X,y) has probability 4/5 at z=g(y) and 1/20 elsewhere. Hence W_{Z|Y} is W_{X|Y} with outputs permuted by g. The symmetric support inequality is exact for every λ≥1 as well.

At λ=1, (1) yields R_X+R_Y≥H(X,Y). There are also the two genie cutsets

    R_X >= H(X|Y),     R_Y >= H(Y|X).                           (4)

For example, if the decoder is additionally given Y^n, it can simulate the Y encoder and recover X^n from the decoded Z^n because x↦f(x,y) is a permutation; Fano's inequality gives the first bound. The other Latin invertibility gives the second. Slepian–Wolf coding attains (4) together with the sum bound, so the complete capacity region is exactly

    R_X >= H(X|Y),  R_Y >= H(Y|X),  R_X+R_Y >= H(X,Y).          (5)

Numerically, H(X)=H(Y)=log_2 5=2.321928095 and H(X|Y)=H(Y|X)=1.121928095, so H(X,Y)=3.443856190 bits per symbol. This is a full-region result for the displayed source, not just the λ≥16 face. The square is not isotopic to Z_5: `latin_square_check.py` exhaustively checks all 120^3 symbol/row/column relabelings. This proves only the stated finite non-isotopy fact, not a general theorem about non-group functions.

3. Maximal envelope criterion for the SW converse
=================================================

For any Latin-square f, row x of W_Y and row x of W_Z are permutations of one another. Thus their conditional entropies agree for each x. For every input prior p,

    Δ(p) := H(pW_Y)-H(pW_Z)
          = I_p(X;Y)-I_p(X;Z).                                  (6)

The one-sided λ=1 bound certifies the SW sum constraint exactly when cav(Δ)(P_X)=0. If P_X has full support, this certificate condition has a simple necessary-and-sufficient channel form:

    cav(Δ)(P_X)=0  <=>  Δ(p)<=0 for every input prior p
                   <=>  W_Z is more capable than W_Y.             (7)

For the forward implication from the right, every posterior decomposition has average Δ≤0, while U=X gives average zero because Δ(δ_x)=0 by Latin invertibility. For the reverse implication, suppose Δ(p)>0 for some p. Choose ε>0 small enough that P_X-εp is coordinatewise nonnegative. Use an auxiliary with probability ε posterior p, and otherwise reveal X exactly (singleton posterior δ_x with weight P_X(x)-εp(x)). Its mean posterior is P_X and its average Δ is εΔ(p)>0, so cav(Δ)(P_X)>0. This rare-pooling witness makes precise why a single-letter auxiliary can expose the gap missed by the deterministic SW corner.

If (7) holds, (1) gives R_X+R_Y≥H(X,Y); together with the Latin genie cutsets and Slepian–Wolf achievability, the full capacity region is (5). The criterion is maximal for this particular one-sided envelope proof. Failure of (7) means this converse does not certify SW; it does not prove that the operational region is smaller. The symmetric-channel condition with X,Y interchanged is another sufficient route to the same SW region.

There is also a differential sufficient test. Define the output Fisher quadratic form

    J_W(p;d) = sum_o ((dW)_o)^2/(pW)_o,
    sum_x d_x=0.

Then

    D²phi_λ(p)[d,d] = [λJ_{W_Z}(p;d)-J_{W_Y}(p;d)]/ln 2.      (8)

Pointwise dominance J_{W_Y}≤λJ_{W_Z} makes phi_λ convex and yields the corresponding SW support face. A stochastic degradation W_Y=W_Z Q implies J_{W_Y}≤J_{W_Z} by data processing for the Fisher metric, so it is a structural sufficient condition at λ=1; common output-permutation equivalence gives equality. For general positive W_Y and full-rank W_Z, the finite Hessian threshold in v1 remains valid. Convexity/Hessian dominance is sufficient for (7) but not necessary; (7) is the exact envelope criterion.

4. A non-equivalent binary case with an exact structured sum rate
=================================================================

This is a known-case challenge to the converse, not a new priority claim. Let Y~Bernoulli(7/10) and Z~Bernoulli(1/5) independently, and set X=Y⊕Z. The full-support joint law in (x,y) order is

                 y=0      y=1
        x=0      6/25     7/50
        x=1      3/50    14/25.

It has P_X(1)=31/50, P_Y(1)=7/10, and P_Z(1)=1/5. The source is not doubly symmetric. The induced channels are

    W_Y(·|0)=(12/19, 7/19),    W_Y(·|1)=(3/31, 28/31),
    W_Z(·|0)=(12/19, 7/19),    W_Z(·|1)=(28/31, 3/31).

Neither identity nor output complementation maps W_Y to W_Z, so these are not output-permutation-equivalent channels. Yet the Latin converse has a tight λ=1 envelope. Let t=P(X=1), a=7/19, s=315/589, r=160/589. Then

    P(Y=1)=a+st,       P(Z=1)=a-rt,
    phi_1(t)=h(a+st)-h(a-rt).

Writing q_Y=a+st and q_Z=a-rt, concavity is equivalent to

    F(t) := s²q_Z(1-q_Z)-r²q_Y(1-q_Y) >= 0.

The quadratic terms cancel, and exact arithmetic gives

    F(t) = 10500/212629 - (6300000/204336469)t,
    F(1) = 10500/566029 > 0.

Hence F(t)>0 on [0,1], so phi_1''(t)<0 throughout; its upper concave envelope is itself. At the actual prior t=31/50, the output marginals are 7/10 and 1/5. Because Y and Z are independent and (X,Y)↔(Y,Z) is bijective,

    H(X,Y)=h(7/10)+h(1/5),
    cav(phi_1)(P_X)=h(7/10)-h(1/5).

The converse (1) therefore gives R_X+R_Y>=2h(1/5). This is attained by the classical K–M linear syndrome code: choose a public binary m×n matrix A, both encoders send AX^n and AY^n, and the decoder adds them to obtain AZ^n and source-decodes the iid Bernoulli(1/5) sequence. For any δ>0, a random A with m/n>h(1/5)+δ has expected decoding error tending to zero: for each other typical z'^n, Pr[A(z'^n-z^n)=0]=2^{-m}, and the typical set has at most 2^{n(h(1/5)+o(1))} words. Fix one successful A for each n; no matrix seed is sent at run time. Thus the exact sum-rate is

    C_sum = 2h(1/5) = 1.443856190 bits/symbol.

The Slepian–Wolf minimum sum is H(X,Y)=1.603218994, so structured coding improves it by 0.159362804 bits/symbol. The source's row channels, support, and entropy values are independently checked by `xor_nondsbs_exact.py`. Nair–Wang (2020, Proposition 2 and Remark 7) already establish the more general exact-sum family containing this example; this calculation validates the Latin-square converse and envelope algebra on a channel pair that is not output-equivalent. It does not solve the full rate region for this source.

5. Known-source boundary and unresolved harder case
==================================================

Primary-source status: Nair and Wang, “On optimal weighted-sum rates for the modulo sum problem,” ISIT 2020, DOI 10.1109/ISIT44484.2020.9174208, Theorem 4 and Lemma 2 (PDF pp. 1–2, lines 198–240 and 280–340), prove the modulo-sum weighted converse and its envelope criteria. Their Proposition 2 and Remark 7 (PDF pp. 3–4, lines 470–557) prove exact K–M sum-rate optimality for a non-DSBS family including the example above; their Theorem 4 specializes to field addition. They cite the original K–M construction: Körner and Marton, “How to encode the modulo-two sum of binary sources,” IEEE TIT 25(2), 219–221 (1979), DOI 10.1109/TIT.1979.1056022. Tsujino and Watanabe, arXiv:2606.25570v2 (12 Aug 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25570, study adjacent-parity multi-letter AH coding and give an eventual-improvement criterion over SW. The literature check supports that arbitrary-source binary XOR capacity remains unresolved; none of the sources above proves the candidate below.

For the v1 full-support binary law theta=(0.379,0.008,0.246,0.367), exact enumeration of the known adjacent-parity AH expression at block length m=3 gives 1.599998181, below SW sum 1.614684731 and below 2H(Z)=1.635113273. A coarse 1000-point posterior/chord search for the one-letter envelope gives the outer sum bound about 1.477807364, far below this AH point. The finite search is a diagnostic, not an exact optimization, and no matching converse or exact capacity is obtained. `ah_parity.py` reproduces the coding-side entropy calculation. The arbitrary-source binary modulo-sum capacity gap remains unresolved in this work.
