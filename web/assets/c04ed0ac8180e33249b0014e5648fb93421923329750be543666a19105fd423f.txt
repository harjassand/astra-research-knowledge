QUANTITATIVE OSCILLATION COST FOR A SMOOTH THREE-ELECTRON COULOMB MARGINAL
7 October 2026. Candidate derivation, not externally reviewed or formally certified.

The source changed mechanism is release 373's five-component Coulomb splitting
certificate, not its headline alone. The quantitative conclusion below is not
stated in that manuscript. The exponent and regularization mechanism already
occur for a two-marginal line-segment toy example in Carioni--Del Grande--Iglesias--
Schonberger, arXiv:2609.05062, Example 4.6. Thus this is a physical smooth
three-particle realization and sharp complexity transfer, not a newly discovered
regularization exponent. Priority for this exact Coulomb statement is unverified.

THEOREM INTERFACE
There exists a probability density rho in C_c^infinity(R^3), with sqrt(rho)
also in C_c^infinity, for which the following holds. Put mu=rho dx and
c(x,y,z)=1/|x-y|+1/|x-z|+1/|y-z|. Let E be the Coulomb Kantorovich minimum
with all three marginals mu. For two arbitrary Borel mu-preserving maps T2,T3,
write Delta(T)=integral c(x,T2(x),T3(x)) dmu-E, and, for 0<s<1/2, write

 R_s(T)=double integral [|T2(x)-T2(x')|^2+|T3(x)-T3(x')|^2]
                     /|x-x'|^(3+2s) dmu(x)dmu(x').

For each fixed s there exist positive c_s,C_s,delta_s,epsilon_s such that

  c_s delta^(-s) <= inf{R_s(T): Delta(T)<=delta} <= C_s delta^(-s)
                 (0<delta<delta_s),

and

  c_s epsilon^(1/(1+s)) <= inf_T[Delta(T)+epsilon R_s(T)]
                        <= C_s epsilon^(1/(1+s))
                 (0<epsilon<epsilon_s).

All maps preserve the complete marginal exactly. No neural architecture,
digital representation, compiler, optimizer, runtime, or ground-state external
potential is supplied. Constants depend on the fixed constructed density and s.
The regularizer measures spatial oscillation of deterministic co-motion maps;
it is not electronic kinetic energy and this theorem makes no such identification.

GEOMETRIC INPUT AND ITS ELEMENTARY CONSTRUCTION
We use the local certificate of release 373, whose construction is elementary
calculus. It can equivalently be retained as an explicit imported hypothesis:
there is an open ball B and four smooth diffeomorphisms H1=Y1,H2=W1,H3=Y2,H4=W2
onto disjoint components near e1,-e1,e2,-e2, with B near zero. A smooth potential
u on the five components has slack S=c-sum u >=0, with zero set exactly the
permutations of (x,Yk(x),Wk(x)). After restriction to a compact cube Omega
contained in B, there are constants kappa,gamma>0 such that:
(i) correct component triples have quadratic slack coercivity transverse to
the corresponding smooth equality graph;
(ii) all other component patterns have slack >=gamma;
(iii) on correct component products S and its Hessian are bounded and DS=0
on the equality graph.

For completeness the local construction is: at (0,a,-a), a=e1 or e2,
c=5/2, grad_x c=0, grad_z c=(5/4)a. Set u0(x)=5/2-K|x|^2/2 and
v_a(z)=(5/4)a dot(z+a)-K|z+a|^2/2. For fixed y near a minimize
F_a(x,y,z)=c-u0-v_a in (x,z). Its Hessian is M_a+K I6, positive for large K.
The mixed y derivative consists of the two invertible blocks
-D^2(|b|^-1) at b=-a and -2a, because D^2(|b|^-1)=|b|^-3(3 bb^T/|b|^2-I).
As K tends to infinity, K(M_a+K I)^-1 N_a tends to N_a; both coordinate
blocks of the minimizing derivative are consequently invertible for large K.
The implicit and inverse function theorems give x=X_a(y),z=Z_a(y), both
diffeomorphisms. The minimum value defines the y-potential. Restricting small
convex product neighborhoods makes the Hessian uniformly positive, giving
quadratic coercivity. The two branches share u0. Other distinct-center triples
have strictly positive slack (the mixed pair has cost 2+1/sqrt(2)>5/2); repeated
components have a singular pair cost at least 1/(2r), excluding them when r is
small. Compact restrictions give a uniform positive gamma. Invert X_a to
parameterize both branches by central x. This establishes the stated input
using only the displayed Hessian, standard local calculus and finite strict gaps.

Choose Omega=(-b,b)^3 inside the common ball. Let nu have product density
w(x)=product_i f(x_i), with f positive on (-b,b), smooth and flat at endpoints,
and sqrt(f) smooth and flat (for example normalized exp[-2/(b^2-t^2)]).
Extend by zero. Set H0=Id and

  mu=(1/3)nu+(1/6)sum_{j=1}^4 (Hj)_#nu.

The diffeomorphisms are smooth with uniformly bounded derivatives and inverse
derivatives on a neighborhood of closure(Omega). Thus rho and sqrt(rho) are
smooth and compactly supported. Choosing branch k uniformly and permuting
coordinates uniformly supplies an optimizer supported on S=0; hence
E=3 integral u dmu and Delta=integral S(x,T2(x),T3(x)) dmu.
The geometry is not assumed to be a physical electronic ground-state density.

LOWER BOUND: EXACT MARGINALS FORCE FINE BRANCH MIXING
It suffices to use T=T2. For nu-a.e. central x, its output lies in one of the
five compact components. Let Sj={x:T(x) in Hj(closure Omega)}, j=0,...,4.
For j>=1 put z_j(x)=Hj^-1 T(x) on Sj. Quadratic coercivity on correct component
patterns, and the positive gap plus bounded diameter on all other patterns,
give

  nu(S0)<=C Delta,
  sum_{j>=1} integral_Sj |z_j(x)-x|^2 dnu(x)<=C Delta.                 (L1)

Exact marginal preservation gives the crucial submeasure inequality

  r_j=(z_j)_#(nu|Sj) <= (1/2)nu, j=1,...,4.                           (L2)

Indeed central source mass is nu/3 while the entire target component has
measure (Hj)_#nu/6; contributions from other sources are nonnegative. This
retains the exact supplied-versus-acquired interface: approximate marginals
would add an error term and are not included in the theorem.

Choose nested fixed cubes Q0 compactly inside Q1 compactly inside Q2 inside
Omega, where w>=m>0. Let zeta be smooth, supported in Q2 and equal to one on
Q1. For j>=1 define F_j=zeta w 1_Sj. Their fractional Sobolev seminorms obey

  sum_j [F_j]_(H^s(R3))^2 <= C(1+R_s(T)).                            (L3)

Proof: on Q2, different branch labels have outputs in distinct separated
components, so |1_Sj(x)-1_Sj(x')|^2 <= C|T(x)-T(x')|^2. The density w is
bounded below on Q2, allowing conversion to the weighted R_s integral.
Variation of zeta w contributes only a fixed smooth H^s seminorm. Pairs
outside Q2 are harmless because supp(zeta) is separated from its complement
outside Q2. This also covers the label S0.

Take a compactly supported smooth probability mollifier eta_h. For h smaller
than the fixed Q0-Q1 separation,

  ||F_j-F_j*eta_h||_(L1(Q0)) <= C h^s [F_j]_Hs.                     (L4)

This is the standard Fourier estimate |1-hat eta(h xi)|<=C min(1,h|xi|)
followed by Cauchy--Schwarz on Q0. By transporting nu|Sj to r_j using z_j,
the L1 translation estimate for the smooth kernel gives

  ||(nu|Sj)*eta_h-r_j*eta_h||_L1 <= C h^-1 sqrt(Delta).               (L5)

For x in Sj intersect Q0, use F_j(x)=w(x), F_j*eta_h=(nu|Sj)*eta_h, and
r_j*eta_h <= w*eta_h/2 from (L2). Summing over j and integrating gives

 integral_(Q0 minus S0) [w-(w*eta_h)/2]
     <= C h^s sqrt(1+R_s(T))+C sqrt(Delta)/h.                        (L6)

The left side is >=(1/2)nu(Q0)-C Delta-C h, by smoothness of w and (L1).
Set h=A sqrt(Delta), choosing the FIXED A sufficiently large, then restrict
Delta sufficiently small. The final term in (L6) is below a quarter of the
fixed positive mass and h is within the required interior scale. Hence

  1+R_s(T) >= c Delta^-s.

Shrinking the Delta threshold once more absorbs the 1, proving
R_s(T)>=c Delta^-s. Delta=0 with finite R is impossible by taking h down to
zero directly in (L6). This lower proof applies for every 0<s<1; s<1/2 is
needed for the discontinuous exact upper construction below.

UPPER BOUND: EXACT LOCAL QUANTILE STRIPES
Partition (-b,b) in the first coordinate into N physical intervals I of length
h=2b/N. For r=2 or 4, split each I into r subintervals J_l of equal f-mass.
Let F_I be the normalized conditional CDF of f on I. On J_l define

 psi_r(t)=F_I^-1(r F_I(t)-l), l=0,...,r-1,
 Psi_r(x)=(psi_r(x1),x2,x3).

On each stripe psi_r is monotone onto I, |psi_r(t)-t|<=h, and the pushforward
of nu restricted to that stripe is exactly (1/r)nu restricted to its cell.
The possibly unbounded derivative near a flat-density endpoint causes no
problem: its total variation on each stripe is the interval length h.

For a central source x, use r=4 and, for l=0,1,2,3, set the target pair
respectively to
 (H1(Psi4 x),H2(Psi4 x)), (H2(Psi4 x),H1(Psi4 x)),
 (H3(Psi4 x),H4(Psi4 x)), (H4(Psi4 x),H3(Psi4 x)).
For source Hj(x), j>=1, use r=2. Its two choices are
 (H0(Psi2 x),Hj'(Psi2 x)) and (Hj'(Psi2 x),H0(Psi2 x)),
where j' is the opposite member of the same pair (1<->2,3<->4).
These define Borel T2,T3 on the five support components.

Check marginals exactly: central source contributes nu/12 to each outer
component under either map. Its opposite outer source contributes a second
nu/12, giving the required nu/6. Each of the four outer sources contributes
nu/12 to the central component, totaling nu/3. The cellwise conditional laws
are unchanged by the quantile expansions, so this calculation holds for
every Borel subset, not only component masses.

Each produced triple is O(h) away from the equality triple with the original
central parameter x, after permuting coordinates as needed. The slack S has
zero first derivative and bounded Hessian on the separated component products.
Taylor's theorem therefore gives Delta<=C h^2. No singular cost passage or
only-linear cost oscillation estimate is used.

On each stripe, T2,T3 are compositions of uniformly smooth Hj with a monotone
one-coordinate expansion. The interior BV contribution is O(1); branch jumps
across at most 4N stripe surfaces contribute O(N). Smooth source coordinate
changes Hj^-1 preserve this bound up to fixed constants. Extension by zero
outside the five compact Lipschitz component domains has BV norm O(N) and
bounded L-infinity norm. Therefore, for either extended map U,

  integral |U(x+z)-U(x)|^2 dx <= C min(1,N|z|).

Integrating this bound against |z|^(-3-2s) dz yields [U]_Hs^2<=C N^(2s),
precisely because 0<s<1/2. Since rho is bounded, the weighted R_s is also
<=C N^(2s). Taking N of order delta^-1/2 proves the sharp accuracy bound;
taking N of order epsilon^-1/(2+2s) proves the regularized upper bound.
The lower regularized bound minimizes delta+c epsilon delta^-s, while maps
with delta above the fixed threshold already cost a fixed positive amount.

FAILURES / LIMITS
- No new general regularization phenomenon or exponent is claimed.
- No direct identification with electron kinetic energy or finite-coupling DFT.
- No connected-support smooth density version proved; the five components are
  essential to the source certificate and the branch-label estimates.
- Exact preserving continuous maps cannot use these branches from a connected
  central component. Fractional maps may jump; this is why s<1/2 is substantive.
- No architecture-size/bit/runtime bound follows from the fractional seminorm.
- Approximate marginals require tracking additional error in (L2); not done.
- No independent human proof review or exhaustive novelty search completed.
- No finite experiment is used as proof; the bounds are analytical.
