N20 — PHASE II NATURAL SCIENCE RECORD
Persistent heredity in protocells before templated genomes
Frozen record: v1.txt (append later records; do not overwrite)
Status: internally derived finite-partition theorem + candidate chemical architecture; no integrated experimental validation and no priority claim.

QUESTION
Can a driven, thermodynamically consistent autocatalytic reaction/compartment network preserve and select more than one distinguishable chemical state through growth, fission, and finite-molecule dilution, without postulating a genome-copying operation?

EXECUTIVE RESULT
I derive an exact reliability/branching condition for a collective autocatalytic state whose chemistry has several alternative minimal seed cores. A daughter inherits the state if it receives every catalyst in at least one seed core; the inherited core then rebuilds the full state by explicit core-catalysed reactions from activated feedstock. No catalyst molecule alone makes a copy of itself. With r disjoint seed cores, each containing k distinct essential species and one copy of each at fission, independent equal partition gives per-daughter state retention

    q(k,r) = 1 - (1 - 2^(-k))^r.

Sibling outcomes are correlated, so their exact offspring probabilities are not Binomial(2,q):

    P0 = (1 - 2^(1-k))^r,
    P2 = 1 - 2(1 - 2^(-k))^r + (1 - 2^(1-k))^r,
    P1 = 2[(1 - 2^(-k))^r - (1 - 2^(1-k))^r].

The mean number of active daughters is 2q. The state-bearing population is supercritical exactly when q>1/2, i.e.

    r > ln(1/2) / ln(1 - 2^(-k)).

For k=2, three alternative two-species cores suffice: q=37/64, mean offspring=37/32, and ultimate survival probability is 5/9. A single tracked daughter retains the state for g generations with probability q^g, if each active daughter restores the same fission composition before its next division. Thus this is not immortality: finite lineages eventually lose state with probability one whenever q<1, while a branching population can have positive survival probability.

The potentially useful mechanism is *seed-family redundancy*: fission need not preserve the full molecular composition. It need only leave one complete catalytic hyperedge that can reconstruct that composition. The construction is chemically explicit at reaction-network level and obeys local detailed balance when each synthesis step is coupled to a fuel/waste pair. Its weakest point is chemical realization: the cross-catalytic network in which any one of several minimal cores reconstructs the same large state has not been identified in a plausible prebiotic protocell. Treat the architecture as a design theorem and experimentally testable candidate, not a discovery of a natural system.

1. REACTION-LEVEL ARCHITECTURE

For a chemical state s, let its full catalytic organization contain r disjoint candidate seed cores

    C_(s,a) = {X_(s,a,1), ..., X_(s,a,k)},    a=1,...,r.

Each C_(s,a) is a minimal collectively autocatalytic set: all k species are required for the active reaction route; no single member catalyses its own production. Any complete C_(s,a) can catalyse production of every catalyst species X_(s,b,j), including members of its own core, from corresponding precursor P_(s,b,j) and activated fuel F_(s,b,j):

    C_(s,a) + P_(s,b,j) + F_(s,b,j)
          <=> C_(s,a) + X_(s,b,j) + W_(s,b,j).

Here C_(s,a) written on both sides means the k catalyst molecules are present in the catalytic complex and returned unchanged. In species-level stoichiometry they cancel; the net chemical transformation is P+F <=> X+W. For a concrete two-member core, the minimal autocatalytic edges are

    X_(s,a,1) + P_(s,a,2) + F_2 <=> X_(s,a,1) + X_(s,a,2) + W_2,
    X_(s,a,2) + P_(s,a,1) + F_1 <=> X_(s,a,2) + X_(s,a,1) + W_1.

To make the seed-family property hold, an intact core also catalyses production of the other cores' members by reactions of the same form. This is a collective bootstrap: an intact core has the whole set of required catalysts, while no member alone reproduces itself. Catalytic reaction propensities vanish if any required core member is absent. At the level of a more detailed mechanism, C can be a transient multi-molecule catalytic complex, with association/dissociation steps included explicitly.

The whole state s also catalyses a growth reaction that adds a membrane-forming amphiphile L:

    C_(s,a) + P_L + F_L <=> C_(s,a) + L + W_L.

The state affects growth through its catalytic rate v_s or through its effect on membrane composition/permeability. Fission occurs after B amphiphile additions (or after an equivalent surface-area threshold). State s is a chemical reaction organization, not an abstract phenotype label: the reporter products and rate constants must be measured to identify it.

Multiple heritable alternatives are distinct state families s=1,...,K, each with its own catalyst species and seed hypergraph. A state may be an enantiomeric/metabolic alternative, a different catalytic subnetwork, or another chemically distinguishable basin. A catalyst network is not assumed to copy arbitrary strings. The reaction list supplies the proposed route from one inherited complete seed to the larger network state.

2. THERMODYNAMIC ACCOUNTING

For each net product-forming step P+F <=> X+W, choose species with elemental and charge vectors satisfying b(P)+b(F)=b(X)+b(W) (buffered protons/ions must be listed as chemostats if they participate), and define the forward affinity

    A = mu_P + mu_F - mu_X - mu_W.

At fixed chemical potentials, local detailed balance requires the ratio of forward to reverse propensities (including activities) to satisfy

    ln(a_forward/a_reverse) = beta A,    beta = 1/(k_B T).

The catalyst complex appears on both sides and does not supply free energy. Sustained net synthesis requires positive reservoir affinity and finite fuel supply; at equilibrium A=0 there is no net forward flux and the proposed growth/repair cycle cannot be maintained. Reversible reactions are retained in the physical model; an irreversible forward-only network is only a high-affinity kinetic limit, not a thermodynamic statement.

Each net new catalyst X consumes one activated precursor P and one F molecule in this stoichiometry. If a daughter is restored to r copies of each of k catalyst species, the *net number of synthesis turnovers* is the deficit in those species. With no reverse turnovers or degradation, this is also the exact fuel count in the one-fuel-per-copy model; in a reversible/degrading realization the gross forward fuel consumption can be higher. If several catalyst classes require different fuel stoichiometries, multiply each net turnover by its own coefficient. Chemical work/free-energy draw is set by the reservoir chemical potentials and reverse fluxes; without a specific chemistry there is no defensible numerical joule-per-copy value. If each parent cycle adds exactly B membrane molecules L, it consumes B corresponding precursor/fuel units; expected membrane additions to viable daughters are 2qB per mother fission. Mechanical fission also costs work. If a spherical compartment grows from volume V (area A0) to 2V and then divides into two volume-V daughters, its growing membrane area reaches 2^(2/3)A0 before fission and 2A0 after division. At constant tension gamma the net area increase from one parent to both daughters is A0, with an additional fission-path barrier; detailed work depends on membrane mechanics. A tensionless ideal membrane can make the tension term small, but not make fuel synthesis free.

For the stated independent-partition model, the exact expected number of catalyst-production fuel molecules required to restore every viable daughter to r copies of each of the k catalyst species is

    E[F_seed | one parent] = kr [1 - (1 - 2^(-k))^(r-1)].

Derivation: let M=kr total seed molecules; S be the subset entering daughter 1; V(S) indicate that S contains at least one whole core. A viable daughter with n_j inherited copies of species class j must synthesize r-n_j copies of that class, so C(S)=V(S)(M-|S|)+V(S^c)|S|. For any one molecule v, conditioned on v entering daughter 1, its own core is complete with probability 2^(-(k-1)); each other core is complete with probability 2^-k. Therefore

    E[|S| 1_V(S)] = M/2 * [1 - (1 - 2^(1-k))(1 - 2^(-k))^(r-1)].

By symmetry, E[C]=2[Mq-E(|S|1_V(S))], which simplifies to the formula above. This counts catalyst synthesis only. Membrane synthesis, fuel regeneration, repair, and fission energy are additional costs.

3. EXACT STOCHASTIC PARTITION AND PERSISTENCE

At fission, every one of the kr catalyst molecules is independently assigned to one of two equal daughters. A specified k-member core lands wholly in a specified daughter with probability a=2^-k. The r cores are disjoint, so the probability a specified daughter receives no complete core is (1-a)^r. Thus

    q = P(daughter active) = 1 - (1-a)^r.

For both daughters to be active, at least one core must be wholly assigned to daughter 1 and at least one to daughter 2. A specified core is split between daughters with probability b=1-2a. Inclusion-exclusion gives

    P2 = 1 - 2(1-a)^r + b^r,
    P0 = b^r,
    P1 = 2[(1-a)^r - b^r].

This explicitly handles the negative correlation between sisters. The mean is P1+2P2=2q. For a two-type (active/inert) Galton-Watson process with this offspring distribution, extinction probability z is the smaller solution of

    z = P0 + P1 z + P2 z^2.

If 2q<1, extinction is certain. If 2q=1, extinction is certain except for the degenerate case P1=1 (one viable daughter is guaranteed every generation, so the state persists without population growth). If 2q>1 and P2>0, the nontrivial root is z=P0/P2, so survival probability is

    S = 1 - P0/P2 = (2q - 1)/P2.

Example k=2,r=3: a=1/4, b=1/2, q=37/64, P0=1/8, P1=19/32, P2=9/32, mean=37/32, S=5/9. Seed restoration consumes 21/8=2.625 expected fuel molecules per parent fission (for unit fuel stoichiometry per catalyst species). A specific tracked daughter retains the state with probability 37/64 per generation, so after g generations its state-retention probability is (37/64)^g. The population can persist with probability 5/9 even though any chosen lineage has a positive per-generation loss chance.

The supercritical threshold follows exactly:

    2[1-(1-2^-k)^r] > 1
    iff r > ln(1/2)/ln(1-2^-k).

For k=2 the minimum is r=3; for k=3 it is r=6; for k=4 it is r=11. For per-daughter fidelity at least 1-epsilon, it is necessary and sufficient in this seed model that

    r >= ceil[ln(epsilon)/ln(1-2^-k)].

For fixed k this is O(log(1/epsilon)) alternative cores, and each core costs k molecules at fission. For fixed r, a large k makes the probability of inheriting any whole core small; redundancy must compensate. This is a reliability tradeoff, not a free bypass of chemistry.

If instead all K catalyst species are individually obligatory and there are n copies of each at fission, without alternative seed cores, the exact per-daughter closure probability is

    q_all = (1 - 2^-n)^K.

Thus q_all>1/2 requires n > -log2(1-2^(-1/K)); to achieve q_all>=1-epsilon requires n >= ceil[ln(1-(1-epsilon)^(1/K))/ln(1/2)] (equivalently find the least integer n with (1-2^-n)^K >= 1-epsilon). For large K and small epsilon, n is about log2(K/epsilon). The seed-family construction changes the inheritance condition from “all K components survive” to “at least one complete seed survives,” but it pays for multiple alternative catalytic cores and assumes every core can rebuild the common state.

For arbitrary overlapping seed families E on a finite set of M molecules, the exact generalization is the reliability polynomial

    q(E) = 2^-M * sum_{S subset [M]} 1{there exists e in E with e subset S}.

Use S^c for the other daughter to obtain P0/P1/P2. This formula is exact and can be exhaustively enumerated for small networks; its computational cost grows as 2^M. The disjoint-core expression above is the closed form for a useful design family.

Kinetic completion and degradation can be added without changing the partition logic. Let phi_s(S) be the probability that a daughter starting from inherited subset S reconstructs the required catalyst composition and reaches its membrane-division threshold before losing the state. It is computed from the finite chemical master equation with the chosen forward, reverse, degradation, and membrane-growth propensities; it is not an assumed replication probability. Replace q by

    qbar_s = 2^-M sum_S phi_s(S).

The exact offspring probabilities are then

    P2 = 2^-M sum_S phi_s(S) phi_s(S^c),
    P1 = 2^-M sum_S [phi_s(S)(1-phi_s(S^c)) + (1-phi_s(S))phi_s(S^c)],
    P0 = 2^-M sum_S (1-phi_s(S))(1-phi_s(S^c)),

when the two daughters' post-fission reaction trajectories are independent conditional on the partition. Mean offspring is 2 qbar_s and the supercritical condition remains 2qbar_s>1. This is the correct gate for any finite-rate realization; an exact chemical master-equation calculation is still needed for a specific chemistry.

4. FINITE DISTINGUISHABILITY

Let two states s,t produce a measurable reporter by converting each of M finite precursor molecules independently with probabilities p_s and p_t over a fixed observation interval. The observed count Z is Binomial(M,p_s) or Binomial(M,p_t). With equal priors, the exact minimum classification error is

    Pe(s,t) = (1/2) sum_{z=0}^M min{Bin(M,p_s)[z], Bin(M,p_t)[z]}.

The Bhattacharyya bound is

    Pe(s,t) <= (1/2) [sqrt(p_s p_t) + sqrt((1-p_s)(1-p_t))]^M.

Thus a sufficient finite-molecule condition for pairwise error <= epsilon is

    M * {-ln[sqrt(p_s p_t)+sqrt((1-p_s)(1-p_t))]} >= ln[1/(2 epsilon)].

The script computes the exact sum and bound. For K states, require the pairwise bound for every pair; a conservative union bound gives total misclassification <= epsilon if each pair has error <= epsilon / binom(K,2). A chemical distinction without a reporter or other measured output is not an operationally established distinguishability claim.

5. GROWTH, MUTATION, AND SELECTION

Let tau_s be the random time for an active s-compartment to add the B membrane molecules that trigger fission. In the simplest finite reaction-level growth model, membrane additions are independent Poisson events at rate v_s, so tau_s is Erlang(B,v_s) and E[e^(-alpha tau_s)]=(v_s/(v_s+alpha))^B. If each active daughter reconstructs the seed family before the next fission and there is no state switching, the Bellman-Harris Euler-Lotka equation is

    2 q_s E[e^(-alpha_s tau_s)] = 1,

giving

    alpha_s = v_s[(2q_s)^(1/B)-1],   provided 2q_s>1.

For a Poisson wrong-state-switch hazard mu_s per unit time during growth, multiply the transform by e^(-mu_s tau_s), so

    alpha_s = v_s[(2q_s)^(1/B)-1] - mu_s.

Positive alpha_s is the population-growth condition. In a resource-unlimited comparison, state s is selected over t when alpha_s>alpha_t. A faster catalytic growth rate v_s need not win if its core has lower q_s, higher mutation, or larger B/resource cost. Under shared finite resources, this condition is necessary only for low-density invasion; stable coexistence requires additional negative frequency dependence, spatial refugia, or resource partitioning.

For a state-independent generation time tau and K state types, let U_ij be the probability that a viable daughter of parent type i has type j. The mean offspring matrix is

    M_ij = 2 q_i U_ij.

The multitype population is supercritical iff spectral radius rho(M)>1 (under the standard irreducibility/non-degeneracy conditions). The state proportions in the large-population expectation are governed by the Perron eigenvector. For differing Erlang growth times, alpha is the real solution of

    rho( D(alpha) M ) = 1,
    D(alpha)_ii = (v_i/(v_i+alpha))^B.

For Poisson switching during growth, use D(alpha)_ii=((v_i)/(v_i+alpha+mu_i))^B for retention-only mutation; for a full type-switch matrix integrated over the growth time, put the corresponding matrix-valued transition transform in each row. These equations distinguish state retention, total population growth, and selection; they are not interchangeable.

For one state with per-cycle switch probability u_s independent of cycle duration, same-state expected offspring is 2q_s(1-u_s), so same-state supercriticality requires

    u_s < 1 - 1/(2q_s).

If u_s=1-e^(-mu_s tau_s), this is mu_s tau_s < ln(2q_s). The model therefore states a measurable error budget: dilution and chemical switching jointly determine whether a state-bearing lineage can invade.

6. WHAT IS AND IS NOT SHOWN

The finite-partition theorem is exact for independent molecule assignment and seed-sufficiency. It gives a constructive route around the strict compositional requirement: a daughter may lose most of its mother’s chemical inventory and nevertheless inherit a complete hyperedge that rebuilds the state. The contribution of the seed family is directly visible in q(E), not a deterministic multistability claim. The fission step is a finite stochastic channel, and sibling correlation is included.

The full physical capability remains conditional on four substantial premises:
(a) At least two distinct minimal autocatalytic cores can be made from accessible substrates without templated genome copying.
(b) Any intact core can bootstrap all state-defining catalyst species, but cores of different states do not freely bootstrap each other.
(c) The catalytic state accelerates membrane growth enough that divisions occur, while fuel conversion and waste removal keep the reaction affinities positive inside the compartment.
(d) Catalyst leakage, degradation, spontaneous seed nucleation, and reverse reaction rates leave qbar above one half and mutation below the selection margin.

No arbitrary reaction-graph autocatalysis implies these premises. In particular, a formal RAF/closed set is not automatically a kinetic replicator, a growth mechanism, or a viable protocell. A root core that can rebuild arbitrary chemistry would also be an information-bearing catalyst; the construction avoids templated polymers but does not remove the need for molecular specificity.

7. COUNTEREXAMPLES AND FAILURE CASES

1. One copy of each member of a two-species core (k=2,r=1) has q=1/4; mean active daughters is 1/2. A parent’s complete autocatalytic closure can be destroyed by fission with probability 1/2, and the population is subcritical. “Autocatalytic” alone does not imply heredity.
2. Two alternative two-member cores (k=2,r=2) have q=7/16<1/2, still subcritical. Redundancy is not automatically enough; the exact threshold is r>=3 for k=2.
3. If a cell is at equilibrium with no maintained fuel affinity, the forward network cannot sustain net catalyst and amphiphile production. A static pair of attractors cannot replace thermodynamic drive.
4. For any finite r,k with q<1, a particular chosen daughter lineage retains its state only with probability q^g; this tends to zero as g grows. The construction gives probabilistic population persistence, not an immortal individual lineage.
5. If one core member is much more leaky/degraded than the rest, the nominal seed hyperedge may almost never survive; use the finite-rate qbar, not the ideal q. If spontaneous false-state nucleation is high, identity is lost even when a complete original core survives.
6. If all state variants compete for one limiting substrate and their cycles are fully irreversible, the faster cycle may exclude the others. Multiple heritable variants do not imply stable polymorphism.
7. A faster catalytic cycle may consume membrane precursor or energy used by the rest of the network, reducing actual division output. Selection is on viable daughter number per unit time and resource, not on autocatalytic flux in isolation.

8. ALTERNATIVES CONSIDERED

A. Single-molecule master autocatalyst. One root X with X+P+F <=>2X+W is the simplest exact regenerative memory; per-daughter retention is 1-2^-n if n X are present. It is a useful bound and a direct non-genomic autocatalytic route, but it risks merely relocating the “replication function” into one assumed self-producing molecule. It also makes the whole state depend on one chemical identity.

B. One obligatory multi-species set. This uses q=(1-2^-n)^K and is the direct compositional-heredity baseline. It has a clear copy-number requirement but offers no repair when any essential catalyst vanishes. The correlated-partition threshold above gives an exact benchmark.

C. Redundant catalytic seeds (the candidate above). Any full seed set restores the same downstream organization. Its gain is topological redundancy; its cost is multiple distinct seed species and cross-bootstrap chemistry. A natural hypercycle may realize a subset of this pattern, but the bootstrap-to-whole-state condition must be tested explicitly.

D. Enantiomeric autocatalysis. R and S versions provide a simple pair of chemically distinguishable, potentially mutually competing states. Soai’s reaction experimentally shows product autocatalysis and amplification of enantiomeric excess, but the zinc reagents are not a demonstrated prebiotic protocell chemistry. A future test could couple a prebiotic-compatible chiral autocatalyst to vesicle growth and then measure its fission transmission; this report does not claim that experiment has been done.

E. Persistent membrane phase or bound catalytic aggregate. Physical co-partition of an intact catalytic cluster can make seed loss less likely than independent binomial partition, but this is an additional mechanism and requires measured cluster dissociation, growth, and fission statistics. The independent-molecule model deliberately does not grant that advantage.

10. STRONGEST CONSEQUENCES ACTUALLY SUPPORTED

1. A finite catalytic state can be population-supercritical across stochastic fission even when the inherited unit is a small complete catalytic core rather than the full chemical composition. For k=2 core members, r=3 disjoint alternative roots are the minimum for mean active offspring >1 under independent 1:1 partition.
2. The exact distinction between a viable daughter and two viable sisters matters: their inheritance is correlated by conservation of the parent’s molecule inventory. The corresponding branching survival probability is (2q-1)/P2, not the independent-Bernoulli formula.
3. A useful experimental discriminator follows directly: titrate the number of alternative catalytic seeds while holding total catalyst molecules and membrane-growth chemistry fixed; measure (a) number of viable daughters, (b) state-specific reporter reconstruction, (c) expected catalytic-fuel use, and (d) state-specific Malthus growth. The disjoint-core formula predicts q and P0/P1/P2 without fitted inheritance parameters, provided independent molecular partition is verified.
4. The proposed work-around is not costless: at high fidelity it requires more seed cores, fuel to reconstruct missing catalysts, and a chemically selective bootstrap. The true foundational constraint shifts from copying every component to creating and preserving at least one *complete regenerative seed*.

11. DECISIVE NEXT EXPERIMENT / THEORY GATE

Find an experimentally demonstrated, non-template-assisted autocatalytic reaction set with two or more distinct minimal subsets C_a such that any one C_a catalyses (directly or through an explicit finite reaction sequence) production of every state-defining catalyst. Measure the complete stoichiometry, forward/reverse rate constants, affinities, degradation, and cross-bootstrap matrix. Encapsulate that network in a compartment whose growth is coupled to one measured catalytic output; carry out controlled division with molecule-resolved or validated fluorescence reporters. Test independent partition against measured co-partition, then compare observed q/P0/P1/P2 and multi-generation state switching with the exact predictions. Failure of all alternatives to bootstrap the same state, or qbar<=1/2 after finite kinetics and leakage, refutes this candidate for that system.

The decisive theoretical gate is a finite chemical master equation with local detailed balance, substrate depletion, reverse fluxes, seed degradation/leakage, mutation, and an explicit membrane-division clock. The present partition formula supplies its boundary condition; it does not replace that generator.
