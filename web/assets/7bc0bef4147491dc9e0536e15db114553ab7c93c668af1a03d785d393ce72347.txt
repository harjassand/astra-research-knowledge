REGULAR SU(m) WEAK GIBBS CARRIERS: MULTIPLICITY-SAFE MEMORY TRANSFER
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE; no external/formal or priority validation.

Main result. For each fixed m, a supplied irreducible SU(m) carrier whose
Dynkin labels remain in a fixed regular cone has optimal blind full-state
weak Gibbs recovery error comparable to its unrestricted Casimir rank
minimum divided by N^2, uniformly for EVERY quantum branch dimension Q.
Adjoint multiplicity m-1 is explicitly handled. The uniform Gram proof
uses central contractions and highest-vector variance, not a presumed
semiclassical normalized-trace limit or a scalar first posterior moment.

This packet proves the representation hypotheses of the independently
audited abstract transfer at outputs/research/ROOT_COVARIANT_GIBBS_TRANSFER.txt.
Sections 8--10 recall the operational proof, so the conclusion is not a
rank-energy-only inference. Section 13 gives a classical-polynomial frame
criterion for other simple groups, with its remaining input distinguished
from the proved SU(m) case. No iid-sector or weight-wall transfer is used.

1. SUPPLIED INTERFACE, RISK, AND UNIFORM QUANTIFIERS

Fix m>=2, real constants 0<c<=C<infinity and 0<h0<=h1<infinity, and a
nonzero traceless Hermitian fundamental matrix H of size m. All are fixed
BEFORE the carrier scale N or branch dimension Q. Let N>=1 and integer
Dynkin labels a=(a_1,...,a_(m-1)) satisfy

                  cN <= a_i <= CN  for every i.                 (1.1)

Let V_a be the irreducible unitary SU(m) carrier, U_g its representation,
and d=dim V_a. Use Hermitian fundamental generators t_A, A=1,...,q,
q=m^2-1, normalized by Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2. The carrier Hermitian
generators are T_A; write

  H=sum_A x_A t_A,    r^2=|x|^2=2 Tr_fund H^2>0,
  B_g=U_g(sum_A x_A T_A/N)U_g^dagger,
  tau_(h,g)=exp(h B_g)/Tr exp(h B_g),  h in [h0,h1].              (1.2)

The carrier, normalization N, H and h interval are supplied. One unknown
state in this orbit is supplied. The encoder is blind to g and may also
be blind to h. It is an arbitrary quantum instrument with classical label
y and memory K_y of dimension <=Q in EVERY branch, followed by a
trace-preserving decoder K_y->V_a. Classical labels and local ancillas
are unrestricted; there is no entanglement assistance or quantum side
channel. The output carrier is V_a. Define

  e*_Q=inf_legal Phi sup_(g,h) (1/2)||Phi(tau_(h,g))-tau_(h,g)||_1,
  E(A)=sum_A ||[T_A,A]||_HS^2,
  Lambda_a(Q)=min_(rank A<=Q,||A||HS=1) E(A).                    (1.3)

All matrices, including nonnormal ones, are admitted in Lambda. The
rank-constrained unit sphere is compact, so its minimum exists. The
fixed-h minimax and Haar-average converses obey the same bounds below.
The single upper code works for every h in the supplied interval.

If H has distinct eigenvalues, its SU(m) orbit is the full flag. The
result also permits nonzero H on a fundamental spectral wall: simplicity
of the Lie algebra makes the adjoint orbit second moment coercive for
every nonzero H. This is separate from regularity of the carrier labels.
The stabilizer of tau inside represented SU(m) equals the centralizer of
H: logarithm of a positive matrix is injective, and the nontrivial
Lie-algebra representation is faithful. An ambient U(d) centralizer is a
different group. No purification or inter-carrier risk is being measured.

2. THE THEOREM AND EXPLICIT FIXED CONSTANTS

Put p=m-1, q=m^2-1, M=m(m-1)/2, and define

  C_F=q/(2m),
  K_0=m(m-1)^2 C^2/2,       K_rho=m(m^2-1)/24,
  beta_0=2 sqrt(K_0 K_rho), K=K_0+beta_0,
  b=sqrt(C_F K),            B0=r sqrt(K),
  A_0=2m sum_(s=1)^p b^(2s),
  D_0=(2^p/m)(c/2)^(m(m-1)),
  kappa=D_0/A_0^(p-1),
  u_s=sqrt(2m)b^s,          v_s=q sqrt(beta_0) s b^(s-1),
  J_st=u_s v_t+u_t v_s+v_s v_t,
  J_0=(sum_(s,t=1)^p J_st^2)^(1/2),
  N_0=ceil(max(1,(2J_0/kappa)^2)),
  g0=kappa/(2q),            g1=(A_0+p kappa/2)/q,
  C_0=sum_(s=1)^p s^2 b^(2s-2),
  gamma=r^2/q,
  rho=r sqrt(g1 C_0)/(q g0).                                    (2.1)

All quantities are finite and positive and depend only on the fixed
interface. The deliberately loose cutoff N_0 is a proof constant, not a
useful implementation threshold. Define

  c_*=(h0^2 gamma/2)exp(-6h1 B0),
  C_large=(1/4)exp(2h1 B0)[h1 rho+gamma h1^2],
  C_small=N_0^2(CN_0+1)^M/C_F,
  C_*=max(C_large,C_small).                                    (2.2)

For every instance of (1.1), every N>=1 and integer 1<=Q<=d,

      c_* Lambda_a(Q)/N^2 <= e*_Q <= C_* Lambda_a(Q)/N^2.        (2.3)

For N>=N_0 the upper constant C_large alone suffices. The same conclusion
holds at each supplied h in [h0,h1]. At Q=d the identity code and both
energy bounds are zero. No rank profile is assumed in this theorem.

3. WEIGHT NORMALIZATION AND HIGHEST-VECTOR CONCENTRATION

Write l=(l_1,...,l_m) for the traceless usual highest weight divided by
N, so l_i-l_(i+1)=a_i/N and sum l_i=0. For a unit highest vector psi put

  mu_A=<psi,T_A psi>/N,       xi=sum_A mu_A t_A=diag(l_i/2),
  eta_i=l_i/2,
  |mu|^2=2 Tr xi^2=(1/2)sum_i l_i^2.                           (3.1)

The factor 1/2 in xi is essential for Tr(t_A t_B)=delta/2. In particular
xi is not diag(l_i). Cartan generators have their highest-weight means;
off-diagonal generators have zero means.

The quadratic Casimir is

  sum_A T_A^2=C_N I,
  C_N=N^2|mu|^2+N beta(l),
  beta(l)=2<mu,mu_rho>,
  l_(rho,i)=(m+1-2i)/2,     |mu_rho|^2=K_rho.                 (3.2)

This is the usual C2(lambda)=(lambda,lambda+2rho) in the generator
metric (long-root squared length 1). It follows as well by evaluating
the Casimir on psi: Cartan means give |Nmu|^2 and each positive-root
raising/lowering pair gives its linear highest-weight contribution.
Explicitly, t_x=(E_ij+E_ji)/2 and t_y=(E_ij-E_ji)/(2i) have highest-state
sum of squared-generator expectations N(l_i-l_j)/2 for i<j. Summing
these contributions gives N sum_i l_i(m+1-2i)/2=N beta(l), verifying
the factor in (3.2) without a convention-dependent Casimir import.

The width of l is at most (m-1)C, so |l_i|<=(m-1)C and |mu|^2<=K_0.
Consequently 0<beta(l)<=beta_0, C_N<=KN^2, ||T_A||<=sqrt(K)N and
||sum x_A T_A/N||<=r sqrt(K)=B0. The last bound is operator Cauchy--
Schwarz against sum T_A^2. Also ||xi||<=b and eta_i-eta_(i+1)>=c/2.

For delta_A=T_A/N-mu_A I one has the EXACT total variance identity

           sum_A ||delta_A psi||^2=beta(l)/N.                 (3.3)

This controls every polynomial of fixed degree uniformly on the fixed
regular cone. It does not require a global coherent-state resolution or
an asymptotic normalized-trace oracle.

4. THE EXACT ADJOINT MATRIX-POWER FRAME AND ITS ENERGY CONTROL

On C^m tensor V_a define the Hermitian matrix

  X=sum_A t_A tensor T_A/N,
  Y_(s,A)=2 Tr_fund[(t_A tensor I)X^s],    1<=s<=p.             (4.1)

The trace here is the partial trace over the FUNDAMENTAL factor. It is
not divided by m or d. Y_(1,A)=T_A/N. These are Hermitian: partial trace
is cyclic against operators on its traced factor. X is invariant under
g tensor U_g; therefore each family transforms as the real adjoint.
All powers in (4.1) are ordered noncommutative matrix powers.

The row/column operator Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives

  ||X||<=sqrt(C_F C_N)/N<=b,
  ||[X,I tensor F]||HS^2=E(F)/(2N^2).                          (4.2)

For any matrix Z on C^m tensor V, fundamental Parseval gives

  sum_A ||2 Tr_fund[(t_A tensor I)Z]||HS^2<=2||Z||HS^2.       (4.3)

Indeed t_A and I/sqrt(2m) are an orthogonal full fundamental matrix
basis with squared norm 1/2. Expanding Z in it proves (4.3), including
its scalar-block remainder. The commutator power identity and Schatten
ideal bound imply

  ||[X^s,I tensor F]||HS<=s b^(s-1)||[X,I tensor F]||HS,
  E_(Y_s)(F):=sum_A||[Y_(s,A),F]||HS^2
        <=s^2 b^(2s-2) E(F)/N^2.                             (4.4)

This is an exact inequality for ALL F, not a diagonal/coherent-filter
estimate. For the frame hypotheses of the abstract transfer the sum of
these constants is C_0 in (2.1).

Normalization control at degree two:
  Y_(2,A)=[(1/2)d_ABC T_B T_C-(m/4)T_A]/N^2.                 (4.5)
The linear correction comes from i f_ABC T_B T_C and cannot be dropped
in an exact finite-N assertion. The degree-two leading classical symbol
is still the traceless part of xi^2.

5. CENTRAL GRAM: AN EXACT TRACE REDUCTION AND A QUANTITATIVE LIMIT

Adjoint covariance and irreducibility give a real symmetric matrix G_N
such that

  (1/d)Tr_V(Y_(s,A)Y_(t,B))=G_(N,st) delta_AB.               (5.1)

The trace in (5.1) is normalized by d, not the fundamental dimension.
Define the Hermitian invariant contraction

  Z_st=(1/2)sum_A[Y_(s,A)Y_(t,A)+Y_(t,A)Y_(s,A)].             (5.2)

It commutes with the irreducible representation, so Schur's lemma makes
it scalar. Consequently

          q G_(N,st)=<psi,Z_st psi>                         (5.3)

EXACTLY. This identity is the reason a highest-vector approximation
controls the normalized-trace Gram without importing a trace asymptotic.

The commuting classical symbols are

  z_(s,A)=2 Tr_fund(t_A xi^s),
  H_st=sum_A z_(s,A)z_(t,A)
       =2[Tr xi^(s+t)-(Tr xi^s)(Tr xi^t)/m].                 (5.4)

For every fundamental unit vector u, tensor Cauchy--Schwarz and (3.3)
give

  ||(X-xi tensor I)(u tensor psi)||<=sqrt(C_F beta(l)/N).

The ordered telescoping identity, valid without commutation of X and xi,

  X^s-(xi tensor I)^s
    =sum_(j=0)^(s-1) X^j(X-xi tensor I)(xi tensor I)^(s-1-j),

therefore bounds its action on u tensor psi by
s b^(s-1)sqrt(C_F beta(l)/N). Diagonalize the fundamental Hermitian
t_A and take the partial trace in (4.1). This yields

  ||(Y_(s,A)-z_(s,A)I)psi||
       <=2||t_A||_1 s b^(s-1)sqrt(C_F beta(l)/N).

Since ||t_A||_1^2<=m Tr(t_A^2)=m/2, summing over q generators gives

  (sum_A||(Y_(s,A)-z_(s,A)I)psi||^2)^(1/2)<=v_s/sqrt(N),
  (sum_A|z_(s,A)|^2)^(1/2)<=u_s,                           (5.5)

with exactly the constants in (2.1): 2qm C_F=q^2. Expanding the inner
products <Y_(s,A)psi,Y_(t,A)psi>, taking real parts as in (5.2), and
using Cauchy--Schwarz proves

  |q G_(N,st)-H_st|
     <=(u_s v_t+u_t v_s)/sqrt(N)+v_s v_t/N,
  ||G_N-H/q||op<=J_0/(q sqrt(N)).                           (5.6)

Every coefficient is fixed before N. Covariance alone would not prove
(5.6) or rule out a degenerate multiplicity Gram.

6. REGULAR VANDERMONDE CONDITIONING AND COMPLETE MULTIPLICITY

Let V be the m-by-p scalar matrix V_(i,s)=eta_i^s and P_0=I-11^T/m.
Then H=2V^T P_0 V. A polynomial of degree <=m-1 that is constant at
all m distinct eta_i must itself be constant; hence H is positive
definite. The augmented Vandermonde matrix with columns 1,V has squared
determinant equal to the Schur complement of its constant-column block:

  det H=(2^p/m)prod_(i<j)(eta_i-eta_j)^2>=D_0,
  Tr H<=A_0,
  lambda_min(H)>=det H/(Tr H)^(p-1)>=kappa.                  (6.1)

The lower bound uses eta_i-eta_j>=(j-i)c/2>=c/2 and is deliberately
loose. For p=1 the denominator is 1. By (5.6), for N>=N_0,

           G_N>=g0 I,       Tr G_N<=g1.                    (6.2)

These are actual uniform Gram inequalities on the regular cone.

It remains to prove that the p families span the ENTIRE adjoint
isotypic component, rather than an unknown subspace of it. The exact
adjoint multiplicity in End(V_a) is p when every a_i>=1:

  V_a tensor C^m is a sum of m distinct irreducibles, multiplicity one,
  C^m tensor (C^m)^*=1 plus ad,
  dim Hom(V_a,V_a tensor ad)=m-1.                            (6.3)

For clarity the first identity needs no unexamined Pieri convention.
In the Weyl character alternant, multiply A_(lambda+rho) by sum_i e_i.
Permutation symmetry gives sum_i A_(lambda+rho+epsilon_i). Adjacent
entries of lambda+rho have differences a_i+1>=2, so all m shifted
alternants remain strictly decreasing; their associated highest weights
lambda+epsilon_i (with the harmless common trace shift removed) are
dominant and distinct. Dividing by A_rho proves the stated direct sum.
Frobenius reciprocity identifies

  Hom(V_a,V_a tensor C^m tensor (C^m)^*)
             with End_G(V_a tensor C^m),

whose dimension is m. The trivial summand contributes one, proving
(6.3). Finally (6.2) makes the p adjoint families linearly independent.
Their span has dimension pq, the dimension of the complete adjoint
isotypic component. The representation hypothesis is therefore proved.

7. FINITE-SCALE PATCH, WITHOUT AN UNPROVED SMALL-N GRAM

The frame proof is quantitative for N>=N_0. The conclusion for N<N_0
does not presume that a higher matrix power family is independent there.
Every nontrivial SU(m) representation has Casimir at least C_F. This
follows from the positive inverse Cartan matrix and the fundamental
values i(m-i)(m+1)/(2m), minimized at i=1,m-1. Thus the commutator
Laplacian on End(V_a) has gap at least C_F off its scalar kernel.

For ||A||HS=1 and rank A<=Q, |Tr A|^2<=Q by the singular-value bound,
so E(A)>=C_F(1-Q/d). For Q<d,

                  Lambda_a(Q)>=C_F/d.                      (7.1)

The Weyl dimension formula gives

  d=prod_(i<j)[1+(a_i+...+a_(j-1))/(j-i)]<=(CN+1)^M.         (7.2)

A reset channel through one-dimensional quantum branches has error at
most 1. Hence, when 1<=N<N_0 and Q<d,

  e*_Q<=1<=C_small Lambda_a(Q)/N^2.                          (7.3)

At Q=d the identity channel has zero error. The converse below already
holds for every N>=1. This proves (2.3) with fixed all-N constants.

8. ARBITRARY-CODE CONVERSE: NO FIRST POSTERIOR INFERENCE

A legal unlimited-classical-label Q-memory channel has composite Kraus
operators A_i of rank<=Q and sum_i A_i^dagger A_i=I. Conversely the
polar support factorization of any such Kraus family is a legal hybrid
code. This exact characterization is PRIOR WORK: Bisio, D'Ariano,
Perinotti and Sedlak, PRA85,032333 (2012), Section V.2, arXiv:1112.3853v1
lines 294--296. It is recalled for the resource interface, not claimed
as a new result. Countable or continuous labels give the same positive
integral identities in finite dimension; no unitality is assumed.

For fixed h let W_g=d tau_(h,g) and M_0=exp(2h1 B0). Irreducibility
makes int W_g^2 dg=(Tr W_e^2/d)I. Expanding commutator squares yields
the exact self-score identity

  Tr W_e^2-int Tr[W_g Phi(W_g)]dg
         =(1/2)sum_i int||[W_g,A_i]||HS^2 dg.                (8.1)

Both commutator-square terms average to the same scalar second moment,
using only sum_i||A_i||HS^2=d. This is valid for NONUNITAL channels.
The effect W_g/M_0 and the exponential divided-difference inequality
on [-B0,B0] imply

  e(Phi;h)>=h0^2 exp(-6h1 B0)/(2d)
                         sum_i int||[B_g,A_i]||HS^2 dg.     (8.2)

Indeed Z_h/d is between exp(-hB0) and exp(hB0), so the entrywise
commutator coefficient of W_g compared to B_g has modulus at least
h exp(-2hB0). The effect contributes exp(-2h1B0). Finally adjoint
Schur orthogonality gives

  int||[B_g,A]||HS^2 dg=gamma E(A)/N^2.                      (8.3)

Apply the rank minimum to each A_i and sum its norm weight /d to obtain
the lower constant c_* in (2.2). This converse uses neither multiplicity
one nor a claim that a Gibbs first harmonic lies solely in span T_A.

9. MULTIPLICITY-SAFE LINEAR DOUBLE-COMMUTATOR ESTIMATE

For positive F put S=Tr F^2, F_g=U_g F U_g^dagger and

  D_F(X)=int [F_g,[F_g,X]]dg,
  Phi_F(X)=(d/S)int F_g X F_g dg.                            (9.1)

The latter is CPTP: Haar F_g^2=(S/d)I. Its memory branches factor
through rank F. Covariance, self-adjointness and the COMPLETE adjoint
frame yield real coefficients theta_s with

                    D_F(T_A/N)=sum_s theta_s Y_(s,A).

Taking trace inner products against all q components of Y_t gives

  qd (G_N theta)_t=v_t(F),
  v_t(F)=sum_A Tr([Y_(t,A),F]^dagger[T_A/N,F]).               (9.2)

Here v_t(F) is not the numerical variance constant v_t of (2.1). The
group integral disappears by simultaneous real orthogonal rotation of
the two tensor families. Cauchy--Schwarz and (4.4) bound its modulus by
sqrt(C_t)E(F)/N^2, C_t=t^2 b^(2t-2). Consequently

  ||theta||_2<=sqrt(C_0)E(F)/(qd g0 N^2).

The normalized Gram gives
||sum_A x_A Y_(s,A)||_1<=d r sqrt(G_(N,ss)). Thus, for N>=N_0,

                 ||D_F(B_e)||_1<=rho E(F)/N^2.              (9.3)

The trace norm is LINEAR in energy. Replacing (9.3) by a generic
square-root commutator estimate would not establish the claimed rate.
No separate operator bound on each higher tensor is needed in this
step. Mixing among the m-1 copies is included in G_N inverse.

10. FULL NONCOMMUTING GIBBS UPPER AND POSITIVITY REPLACEMENT

For an arbitrary matrix A=U diag(sigma) V^dagger, the scalar Casimir
identity gives

  E(A)-[E(|A|)+E(|A^dagger|)]/2
     =sum_(A,i,j) sigma_i sigma_j
            |(U^dagger T_A U)_ij-(V^dagger T_A V)_ij|^2>=0.  (10.1)

Both moduli have the same rank and HS norm as A. A positive minimizer
F of Lambda_a(Q) therefore exists. This step proves existence, not an
algorithm to solve the nonconvex optimization.

Since Phi_F-I=-(d/(2S))D_F, it suffices to control D_F(exp(hB_e)). Put
C_B=(B_e+B0 I)/(2B0), so 0<=C_B<=I, and ell=2hB0. One has

  ||D_F(C_B)||_1<=rho E(F)/(2B0 N^2),
  int||[F_g,C_B]||HS^2 dg=gamma E(F)/(4B0^2 N^2).            (10.2)

Apply the noncommutative derivation [F_g,.] twice to an ORDERED product
C_B^k. The k terms with a double derivative are bounded by k times the
first line of (10.2). Each cross term is bounded by Schatten Holder and
||C_B||<=1; there are k(k-1) such ordered terms. Hence

  ||D_F(C_B^k)||_1
     <=[k rho/(2B0)+k(k-1)gamma/(4B0^2)]E(F)/N^2.           (10.3)

No powers have been commuted across a derivative. Sum the exponential
series, divide by Tr exp(ell C_B)>=d, and include the half-trace-distance
factor. The scalar Gibbs shift cancels its normalization, giving

  (1/2)||Phi_F(tau_(h,e))-tau_(h,e)||_1
      <=exp(2hB0)[h rho+gamma h^2] E(F)/(4SN^2).             (10.4)

Covariance makes this uniform in g, and h<=h1 makes it uniform over the
interval. Take E(F)/S=Lambda_a(Q), and then use Section 7 at small N.
This proves the FULL density error, including all higher harmonics and
noncommuting contributions, rather than just a first observable moment.

11. EXACT FIRST HARMONIC: A COMPLETE FRAME, NOT A SCALAR POSTERIOR

For regular N>=N_0 define x_A(g) as the real adjoint coefficients of
gHg^dagger. Put Y_(s,x)=sum_A x_A Y_(s,A), and
m_s(h)=Tr[tau_(h,e)Y_(s,x)]. Since the map
A->int x_A(g)tau_(h,g)dg is an adjoint tensor, completeness and (5.1)
give the exact posterior formula

  int x_A(g)tau_(h,g)dg
      =(1/(qd))sum_s (G_N^(-1)m(h))_s Y_(s,A).              (11.1)

To check the coefficient, take trace against Y_(t,A), sum over A, and
use sum_A x_A(g)R_(AB)(g)=x_B. Thus qd G_N times its coefficient vector
equals m(h). There are p=m-1 entries, not one. The moment vector m(h)
depends on all Gibbs powers. The already banked SU3 carrier (1,1) with
H=diag(2,-1/2,-3/2) has a nonzero second adjoint coefficient at order
h^2 (regular_su3/RESULT.txt Section 11); therefore scalarization is
actually false for Gibbs states. Neither (8.2) nor (9.3) assumes it.

13. CLASSICAL-POLYNOMIAL FRAME PRINCIPLE FOR OTHER SIMPLE GROUPS

Here is a proved sufficient-condition theorem, separating the remaining
classical Lie-theoretic input from its quantum representation consequence.
Fix a compact connected simple group, an invariant real generator metric
on its Lie algebra g, and a compact set K of regular dominant normalized
weights. Suppose the following FINITE CLASSICAL INPUT is supplied:

  p=rank g real homogeneous equivariant polynomial maps
  z_s:g^*->g, degree e_s>=1, whose value Gram
  H_st(lambda)=<z_s(mu_lambda),z_t(mu_lambda)>
  has smallest eigenvalue >=kappa_K>0 for lambda in K.          (13.1)

The coefficient lists, degrees, and conditioning bound are charged input.
For V_(N lambda) define Hermitian Y_(s,A) by PBW symmetrization of each
real polynomial component z_(s,A), with generators T/N. Symmetrization
is equivariant, so these are exact adjoint tensor families. Their
invariant Hermitian contractions are central and their normalized trace
equals a highest-vector expectation, exactly as in (5.3).

The scalar Casimir supplies uniform ||T_A/N||<=sqrt K and highest-vector
total variance beta/N. Ordered monomial telescoping against the means
mu gives ||(Y_(s,A)-z_(s,A)(mu)I)psi||<=L_s/sqrt N, uniformly on K:
in each telescoping term the rightmost remainder acts directly on psi;
all earlier factors have bounded operator norm and all later factors
are scalar means. Fixed finite polynomial coefficients give fixed L_s.
The same expansion of central contractions therefore proves

                G_N=H(lambda)/q+O_K(N^(-1/2)).               (13.2)

Likewise each commutator of a monomial is a sum of e_s ordered terms.
Schatten ideals and fixed coefficient sums give
E_(Y_s)(F)<=C_s E_T(F)/N^2 for EVERY F, with fixed C_s. No normalized
trace asymptotic or vanishing higher-copy assumption is used.

Finally the exact adjoint multiplicity is p at sufficiently deep regular
weights. The Weyl character identity for any Weyl-invariant weight sum is

  chi_lambda chi_ad
     =sum_(mu weights of ad) multiplicity(mu)
                                    A_(lambda+rho+mu)/A_rho.

If lambda+mu is dominant for every adjoint weight, each term is

abstract operational theorem applies. The finite-N patch uses the

14. PRODUCTS OF TYPE-A SIMPLE FACTORS AND A COERCIVITY FAILURE

The theorem does apply unconditionally to a fixed product of SU(m_l)
factors (and its central quotients when the selected representation
descends), with each carrier factor in a fixed regular cone at common
scale N. The irreducible total carrier is a tensor product. Assume H_l
has NONZERO component in every simple factor and bounded weak scale.

For factor l let E_l be its commutator energy on all matrices of the
total carrier. Its complete adjoint frame is Y_(l,s,A) tensor identities
on the other factors: each other End(V_j) has trivial multiplicity one.
The normalized total trace Gram and commutator bounds are unchanged;
the coefficient estimate (9.2) is valid on the total d-dimensional
carrier with E_l in place of E. Set

  E=sum_l E_l,   gamma_l=|x_l|^2/q_l,
  gamma_min=min_l gamma_l>0,    gamma_max=max_l gamma_l,
  B0=sum_l |x_l|sqrt(K_l),      rho=max_l rho_l.

The group-orbit energy is sum_l gamma_l E_l/N^2, bounded below by
gamma_min E/N^2 and above by gamma_max E/N^2. The frame trace norm is
at most sum_l rho_l E_l/N^2<=rho E/N^2. The scalar TOTAL Casimir gives
the same positive-modulus replacement. Therefore all-Q recovery obeys
e*_Q comparable to the total Casimir rank minimum /N^2, with

  c_*=(h0^2 gamma_min/2)exp(-6h1 B0),
  C_large=(1/4)exp(2h1 B0)[h1 rho+gamma_max h1^2].             (14.1)

The small-N reset patch uses the total carrier dimension and the minimum
positive factor Casimir. No unproved representation-sector transport is

The nonzero-component condition cannot be omitted. On G_1 times G_2
