Cycle 2 leaf memo — product-group Casimir profile
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE for the exact spectral/projector profile and the stated asymptotic transfer counterexample. Novelty/priority UNKNOWN. This does not change the SU(m) symmetric-power theorem or claim its hypotheses extend to product groups.

Question
Test whether the SU(m) symmetric-power order law E_Q ~ N Q^(-1/(m-1)) can be transferred to a different compact-group representation. I use G_n=SU(2)^n acting on H_n=(C^2)^{tensor n} by the external tensor product of n fundamental representations. This is irreducible as a representation of G_n. I derive every Casimir multiplicity and low-energy partial trace, then show that substituting the total highest-weight size n into the SU(2) law gives the wrong order for Q about n.

1. Exact representation and Casimir spectrum
At site i let S_{i,a}=I tensor ... tensor (sigma_a/2) tensor ... tensor I for a=1,2,3. This is the convention Tr_fund(S_a S_b)=delta_ab/2. On Hilbert-Schmidt operators define the positive conjugation Casimir

    L(X)=sum_{i=1}^n sum_{a=1}^3 [S_{i,a},[S_{i,a},X]],
    E(X)=<X,LX>_HS=sum_{i,a} ||[S_{i,a},X]||_HS^2.

Locally, End(C^2)=1 direct-sum 3 under conjugation: the scalar matrices have Casimir 0, and the traceless matrices form spin 1 with Casimir 1(1+1)=2. Therefore

    End(H_n) = tensor_{i=1}^n (1 direct-sum 3)
             = direct-sum_{S subset [n]} (tensor_{i in S} 3_i),

where factors outside S are trivial. The summands are pairwise inequivalent irreducible representations of the product group. For |S|=r, L has eigenvalue 2r and the summand dimension is 3^r. Thus the exact eigenvalue multiplicity at 2r and cumulative low-energy dimension are

    m_r = binom(n,r) 3^r,
    D_r = sum_{s=0}^r binom(n,s) 3^s,     r=0,...,n.

In particular D_n=4^n=dim End(H_n), while the carrier dimension is d_n=dim H_n=2^n.

2. Every low-energy partial trace, explicitly
Use vec: End(H_n) -> H_n tensor conjugate(H_n). On one site, let Pi_0 project onto the normalized invariant vector |Omega>=(|0,bar0>+|1,bar1>)/sqrt(2), and Pi_1=I_4-Pi_0 project onto the traceless/adjoint sector. Then

    Tr_2(Pi_0)=I_2/2,
    Tr_2(Pi_1)=2 I_2-I_2/2=3 I_2/2.

For a subset S, the projector Pi_S onto its tensor-product sector has

    Tr_2(Pi_S)=(3/2)^|S| (1/2)^(n-|S|) I_{H_n}
               =3^|S|/2^n I_{H_n}.

Consequently the projector Pi_{<=2r} onto all eigenvalues at most 2r has the exact partial trace

    Gamma_r=Tr_2(Pi_{<=2r})=(D_r/2^n) I_{H_n}.             (2.1)

This computes the full operator, not only its trace. Its trace is D_r, as required.

3. Exact rank-Q projector profile
Let X in End(H_n) satisfy ||X||_HS=1 and rank(X)<=Q. Let P be the orthogonal projection onto ran(X), so rank(P)<=Q. Under vectorization, vec(X) has Schmidt rank rank(X), and |vec(X)><vec(X)| <= P tensor I. By positivity and (2.1),

    <vec(X),Pi_{<=2r} vec(X)>
       <= Tr[(P tensor I) Pi_{<=2r}]
       = Tr(P Gamma_r)
       <= Q D_r/2^n.

The mass is also at most one. Layer cake for the spectral law of L in state vec(X) now gives the exact partial-trace lower profile

    E(X) >= Phi_n(Q)
           := 2 sum_{r=0}^{n-1} [1-Q D_r/2^n]_+.          (3.1)

Equivalently, D_r=4^n Pr(Bin(n,3/4)<=r), so

    Phi_n(Q)=2 sum_{r=0}^{n-1}
                [1-Q 2^n Pr(Bin(n,3/4)<=r)]_+.

The equality here is for the profile computed from the exact invariant projectors; (3.1) is a rigorous lower bound on the optimum rank-Q energy, not a claim that every Q attains equality. The pointwise consequence is

    E(X) >= 2r [1-QD_r/2^n]_+,   0<=r<=n.

4. Explicit low-rank upper benchmark
For Q=2^k, 0<=k<=n, fix any unit vector |0> in C^2 and set

    X_k = (I_{2^k}/sqrt(2^k)) tensor (|0><0|)^(tensor (n-k)).

Then ||X_k||_HS=1 and rank(X_k)=2^k. Identity factors have zero commutator energy. On one active qubit,

    sum_{a=1}^3 ||[sigma_a/2, |0><0|]||_HS^2=1,

because the a=3 term is zero and the a=1,2 terms each equal 1/2. Hence E(X_k)=n-k. For arbitrary integer 1<=Q<=2^n, choosing k=floor(log_2 Q) gives the feasible bound

    inf_{rank X<=Q, ||X||_HS=1} E(X) <= n-floor(log_2 Q).      (4.1)

One exact optimum follows by matching the profile and construction: at Q=2^(n-1), the r=0 term of (3.1) is 1 and D_1=1+3n makes every r>=1 term vanish. Thus Phi_n(Q)=1, while X_{n-1} has energy 1, so the rank-d_n/2 minimum is exactly 1.

For Q=1, (3.1) and (4.1) already show rank-one energy is Theta(n): for example, taking r=floor(n/8) in the argument below gives a linear lower bound, while X_0 has energy n.

5. Concrete failure of direct SU(2) order-law transfer
The transfer being tested is the parameter-only extrapolation that replaces the symmetric-power degree N by total highest-weight size N_tot=sum_i 1=n for the product highest weight (1,...,1), keeps m=2, and predicts energy of order N_tot/Q for Q<=N_tot/8. Take Q_n=floor(n/8).

Write r_n=floor(n/8). The terms a_s=binom(n,s)3^s increase for s<=r_n, so

    D_{r_n} <= (r_n+1) binom(n,r_n) 3^r_n
             <= (n+1) 2^(beta n),
    beta=H_2(1/8)+(log_2 3)/8 < 0.742 < 1,

where H_2 is binary entropy. Thus

    epsilon_n := Q_n D_{r_n}/2^n
          <= n(n+1) 2^(-(1-beta)n) -> 0.

For each s<=r_n, D_s<=D_{r_n}, so (3.1) yields

    inf_{rank X<=Q_n, ||X||_HS=1} E(X)
       >= 2(r_n+1)(1-epsilon_n)
       = (n/4)(1-o(1)).                                    (5.1)

But the direct total-weight substitution N_tot/Q_n is bounded (tending to 8). Therefore no rank-Casimir order law depending only on this total weight and Q can transfer from SU(2) symmetric powers to these product-group representations. The discrepancy is at least linear in n.

This does not contradict the SU(m) result: SU(2)^n is a different group, its highest weight has n independent coordinates, and (5.1) shows that the number of simple factors matters. It falsifies only the explicit direct-transfer rule above. A product-group theorem would need parameters that retain the weight vector/factor geometry, rather than its scalar sum.

6. Status, cost, prior boundary
The derivation is finite-dimensional and exact: the inputs are G_n, its external tensor-product fundamental representation, the fixed generator normalization, and rank budget Q. No numerics, oracle, sampling, approximation, or precision cost is used. The key proof ingredients are local 1+3 decomposition, tensor-product multiplicities, explicit partial traces, and the same vectorization/layer-cake argument used for invariant Dirichlet forms generally.

Closest checked prior in the parent cycle-2 source ledger: Alagic–Russell, “Uncertainty Principles for Compact Groups,” arXiv:math/0608702v2 (2008), https://arxiv.org/html/math/0608702v2. Their theorem gives compact-group support/Fourier-rank uncertainty; it is a relevant rank-sensitive baseline, not a source for the specific product Casimir spectrum or the comparison (5.1). This memo makes no novelty or priority claim. The mathematical derivation is self-contained; no exhaustive literature audit was performed.

Falsifier for the stated profile: an error in the local spin-1 Casimir normalization, multiplicity sum D_r, product partial trace (2.1), or layer-cake interval width 2 would invalidate (3.1). Falsifier for the transfer counterexample: a violation of the exact decomposition/profile or of the binomial-entropy bound leading to (5.1). Neither computation is represented as external validation.

Scope boundary: the exact lower profile does not determine the exact minimizer for every Q; only the displayed Q=2^k upper construction and Q_n order separation are established. This is not an operational memory theorem, channel advantage, log-Sobolev result, or KLS conclusion.
