# Independent scout: robust reaction control from affine geometry

Status: new conditional transfer, plus an unconditional exact controlled-network example. The general theorem depends on the release's arbitrary-tolerance affine-minimum lemma. I read its complete induction and all subsequent proof sections, but have not established external correctness or priority. This report does not certify the other release families.

## Result and significance

The most consequential overlooked interface I found is family 149. Its stated theorem concerns fixed-rate weakly reversible mass-action systems. Its geometry is independent of the rates. This permits three successive deductions:

1. One common convex absorbing set works for arbitrary measurable rates in a supplied compact positive interval, rather than only a fixed rate vector.
2. The same set works under arbitrary switching among finitely many reaction graphs with the same stoichiometric span, provided each graph satisfies the directional endotactic condition below. Individual edges may be switched off when a mode changes.
3. The flux argument extends from weak reversibility to all endotactic graphs. Conditional on the affine lemma, this proves the known Extended Permanence Conjecture for variable-rate endotactic mass-action systems, and its common-span switching version.

The incremental mechanism is minimal-essential-source dominance, replacing the source's directed-return-path argument. Time variation and switching require no dwell time and no common equilibrium. The result is a deterministic concentration theorem; molecule-count stochastic safety is a separate question being pursued by the statistical-mechanics worker.

Other opportunities considered but not adopted: family 090 would offer material design from triangular crystallization, but quantitative defect stability is not supplied by energy minimality alone; family 144 gives smooth dynamics with a shift-like Koopman representation, but acquiring its observable/basis remains a separate input problem; family 376 gives fluid reachability undecidability, whose immediate compiler consequence would not by itself provide a new finite-resource controller. None was deeply audited here.

## Exact interface

Let the species count be d. There are finitely many graph modes G_a=(C_a,R_a), with complexes in Z_{≥0}^d. Let ν_e=y'_e-y_e and assume every mode has the same stoichiometric span

    S = span{ν_e : e in R_a}.

Fix a positive class P=(c+S)∩R_{>0}^d, and supplied bounds 0<κ≤K<∞. For an arbitrary measurable mode a(t) and arbitrary measurable active rates k_e(t)∈[κ,K], consider

    x'(t) = f_{a(t),k(t)}(x(t))
           = Σ_{e in R_{a(t)}} k_e(t) x(t)^{y_e} ν_e.

The directional endotactic property used is: for every r∉S⊥, restrict to reactions with r·ν_e≠0; among their source complexes minimizing r·y_e, every reaction has r·ν_e>0. This is the usual endotactic condition applied in direction -r, after omitting reactions with zero projection. Weak reversibility implies it.

The conclusion is existence of a nonempty compact convex K_P⊂P, common to all admissible control paths, such that every positive initial point gives a global positive Carathéodory solution and enters K_P in finite time. The entry bound depends on the initial point, while K_P depends only on the graph family, P, κ, and K. In particular, one common positive lower and finite upper concentration bound applies after entry.

For open-loop measurable coefficients, ordinary Carathéodory existence and uniqueness hold because the vector field is locally uniformly Lipschitz in x. For arbitrary discontinuous feedback, the correctly scoped claim is that every absolutely continuous solution of the associated controlled differential inclusion obeys the safety conclusion; existence and uniqueness of a particular feedback implementation require their own hypotheses. Convex mixtures of admissible mode fields retain all derivative inequalities.

The edge list, rate bounds, class, and control path are supplied. There is no network identification, kinetic parameter estimation, controller optimization, or stochastic-to-deterministic approximation hidden in the theorem.

## Source geometry and read depth

Pinned source: [family 149 manuscript directory](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Uniform-Permanence-in-Weakly-Reversible-Mass-Action-Systems-October-5-2026).

All six decisive TeX files were downloaded with SHA-256 provenance to `sources/149/`; `sources/149/manifest.json` records exact URLs and bytes. The full affine induction, trapping argument, classwise iteration, introduction, and references were read. No Lean build was run.

The affine lemma supplies finitely many labels, independent of h,

    F_h(x) = min_λ (r_λ·x + b_λ(h)),  r_λ∈[-1,1]^d,

whose active slopes approximate p at x_i=h^{p_i}, p∈[-1,1]^d, with any prescribed positive tolerance E(r). The critical tolerance includes coordinate directions and every pairwise complex difference:

    D = {e_i} ∪ {z-y : y,z∈C, z≠y},
    E(r) = min({1/2} ∪ {|r·v|/(2||v||_1) : v∈D, r·v≠0}).

Because the label set is finite, choose

    H = min({1} ∪ {|r_λ·v|/2 : v∈D, r_λ·v≠0}) > 0.

Activity implies that each nonzero comparison r·v has the same sign as p·v and |p·v|≥H. In particular,

    r·u > r·w  =>  x^u ≤ h^H x^w.                 (1)

For sufficiently small h, the labels active at c have slope zero. The plateau K_h={F_h=F_h(c)} is a compact convex polytope containing c, with K_h⊂int[h^{1/2},h^{-1/2}]^d. Every active label at a class point outside K_h lies outside S⊥. These geometric statements use no rate constants.

Internal audit observations: the affine proof permits approach to cube faces from outside; its local offset shifts use strict limiting exponent gaps; all zero comparison products are omitted from the tolerance; and its compactness arguments use finitely many fixed slope tolerances rather than continuity of E. I did not find an invalid inference in the complete induction. The compact-cover choice and uniform small-h threshold remain nonquantitative. The independent reaction-control auditor is checking this same dependency.

## Reconstructed supporting lemma: cut flux

This lemma is independent of the affine construction. Suppose a finite weakly reversible graph is given, r orders its complexes, and a positive point x satisfies (1) whenever r·u>r·w. Let all active rates belong to [κ,K], and let m_R be the number of active non-self-loop edges. If

    h^H m_R K ≤ κ/2,

then every cut between consecutive distinct r-levels has nonnegative net upward flux, strictly positive when some reaction crosses it.

Proof. If there is a downward crossing, its return path contains an upward crossing w→w'. Each downward source u lies strictly above w in r-level, so the entire downward flux is at most h^H m_R K x^w. The selected upward reaction contributes at least κ x^w. Its surplus is at least κ x^w/2. If there is no downward crossing, every crossing contribution is upward and nonnegative. Writing each signed r·ν_e as the sum of the intervening level gaps gives exactly

    r·f(x) = Σ_j (a_{j+1}-a_j) Q_j(x).

If r∉S⊥, at least one edge crosses a cut, hence the sum is positive. This proof remains valid pointwise for time-varying rates and for any active weakly reversible mode. No averaging or switching-frequency assumption appears.

## Stronger deduction: endotactic dominance with an explicit margin

Let C be the union of the finitely many mode complexes and let

    M = max_{y∈C} ||y||_1,
    L = Σ_{e∈R_union} ||ν_e||_1.

Assume S≠{0}, so L>0. Fix an active affine slope r∉S⊥ and an admissible mode. Select a minimal r-source w of the r-essential graph and one reaction e_w leaving it. Endotacticity gives d_w=r·ν_{e_w}>0. Since ν_{e_w}∈D, d_w≥2H.

Any negative projected reaction e has r·y_e>r·w; an equal minimal source would violate the directional condition. Equation (1) therefore gives x^{y_e}≤h^H x^w. Keeping just one positive flux and bounding every negative flux,

    r·f(x)
      ≥ [κ d_w - h^H K Σ_{d_e<0}|d_e|] x^w
      ≥ [2κ H - h^H K L] x^w.

Choose h_P small enough that h^H K L≤κ H for every 0<h≤h_P. Because x∈B_h=[h,h^{-1}]^d and the source degree is at most M, x^w≥h^M. Hence

    r·f(x) ≥ δ_h := κ H h^M > 0.               (2)

For r∈S⊥ the derivative is zero. The bound is uniform over mode, active rates, and active points. It also holds for convex combinations of admissible fields. It supplies the source's required strict increase without a compactness minimum over rates. The degenerate S={0} case has f=0 and P={c}.

This extension was found together with the reaction-control prior-art worker, who identified the minimal-essential-source argument. It is not being presented as my sole independent discovery.

## Finite entry and a quantitative, explicitly conditional bound

For an absolutely continuous trajectory, the minimum of finitely many affine functions is absolutely continuous. At almost every time where all relevant derivatives exist,

    (F_h∘x)' = min_{λ active} r_λ·x'.

Equivalently, integrate the active derivative inequality directly. Thus F_h is nondecreasing inside B_h and rises at least δ_h per unit time in B_h∩(P\K_h). Since it has global maximum M_h=F_h(c), K_h is invariant. Its positive distance from the boundary of B_h supplies global positive continuation for solutions beginning there.

Let

    A_h = M_h - min_λ[b_λ(h)
                         + h Σ_{r_{λ i}>0} r_{λ i}
                         + h^{-1} Σ_{r_{λ i}<0} r_{λ i}].

This is exactly M_h-min_{B_h}F_h, not a numerical estimate. Any trajectory that remains in B_h enters K_h within A_h/(κ H h^M).

Choose h_0≤h_P so small that ||log x^0/log h_0||_∞<H. Coordinate sign comparisons then force every active slope at x^0 to be zero, hence x^0∈K_{h_0}. Define h_{j+1}=min(√h_j,h_P). The inclusions K_{h_j}⊂int B_{√h_j}⊂B_{h_{j+1}} permit successive entry. The number of stages is

    N = max(0, ceil log_2[log(1/h_0)/log(1/h_P)]),

and a uniform control-independent entry bound is

    T(x^0) ≤ Σ_{j=1}^N A_{h_j}/(κ H h_j^M).        (3)

Finally K_P=K_{h_P}∩(c+S). Formula (3) is computable given the affine labels, offsets, H, and an admissible h_P. The source does not currently provide an efficient finite-bit acquisition of these objects. No polynomial-time chemistry-control algorithm follows merely from this formula.

## Exact rational safety-certificate acquisition

A separate constructive consequence avoids treating an arbitrary real polytope as a free input. Suppose the complexes, κ,K,c, and a compact initial uncertainty polytope A⊂P are rational. Choose a rational basis B for S and write x=c+Bz.

Conditional on the affine lemma, choose h so small that every point of A lies in the interior of K_h. Remove inequalities whose normal belongs to S⊥; they are constant on P and do not define proper relative facets. Every remaining facet of K_h∩P has the strict common inward margin (2). Compactness and the finite facet family make this an open property of the normals and offsets. Rational perturbations therefore give a bounded rational polytope Q containing A in its relative interior, contained strictly in the positive orthant, and having strictly inward drift on every proper facet for all admissible rates and modes.

More explicitly, take a sequence of rational perturbations. Boundedness and positive distance from coordinate boundaries persist. If any new facet violated an inward inequality, choose its offending points and control vectors along a subsequence. Compactness gives a limit on the corresponding original facet, contradicting its strict uniform margin. Tangential class constraints have already been removed, so this does not require preserving an irrational zero comparison after perturbation.

This proves existence of a total computable, possibly extremely slow, certificate compiler:

1. Enumerate bounded rational polytopes Q in z coordinates.
2. Verify A⊂Q and c+BQ⊂R_{>0}^d using rational linear programming.
3. For each mode and each facet n_j·z=b_j, decide the first-order real polynomial sentence

       ∀z∈Q, ∀k∈[κ,K]^{R_a}:
       n_j·z=b_j  =>  n_j·g_a(z,k)<0,

   where g_a=(B^TB)^{-1}B^T f_a(c+Bz,k).
4. Return the first verified polytope.

All polynomial coefficients are rational, so exact real quantifier elimination decides each candidate. Conditional existence ensures eventual termination. A common rate box can be checked directly rather than enumerating exponentially many rate vertices. Soundness of any accepted certificate is unconditional and does not depend on the affine lemma. The theorem supplies termination for the promised endotactic input class.

This is a safety compiler for a prescribed compact initial set. It does not by itself give a finite certificate that the returned Q absorbs every initial point of an unbounded class. The reaction-certificates worker is separately investigating global-absorption certificates; an all-scale rationalization gap should not be hidden. No useful worst-case bit-complexity or retained-certificate size bound has been proved. Quantifier elimination and polytope enumeration can be prohibitively costly.

## Unconditional worked controller and exact certificate

Consider two species A,B. The controller may choose between

    mode 1: 0 ↔ A+B,  A ↔ B,
    mode 2: 0 ↔ A,    0 ↔ B,

and may independently choose every active rate in [1,2]. Both graphs are weakly reversible and have S=R^2. Mode 1 has two linkage classes. The mode-1 field is

    a' = k_0-k_1ab-k_2a+k_3b,
    b' = k_0-k_1ab+k_2a-k_3b.

For a≤1/4,

    a' ≥ (1-2a)(1+b) ≥ 1/2.

For a≥4,

    a' ≤ (2-a)(1+b) ≤ -2.

Identical bounds hold for b, and mode 2 gives the same inequalities without the factor 1+other. Consequently Q=[1/4,4]^2 is common and invariant, and every positive initial point enters it by

    T ≤ max_{i∈{a,b}} {2(1/4-x_i^0)_+, (x_i^0-4)_+/2}.

Once a coordinate enters its interval it cannot leave, regardless of the other coordinate or subsequent control switches. Initial-point-dependent larger rectangles give global positive existence before entry.

The executable `verify_switching_example.py` checked all 256 facet/control vertices with exact rational arithmetic. Each facet field is separately affine in the free concentration and in each rate, so its minimum on the facet/rate rectangle is attained at a vertex. This multiaffinity argument, together with exact vertex checking, is a finite proof certificate for this example. The minimum common inward margin is 1/2; mode-1 lower and upper facet margins are respectively 5/8 and 5/2. Results are saved in `switching_example_certificate.json`.

For a periodic switching/rate schedule, the period map is a continuous self-map of Q. Brouwer supplies at least one positive periodic solution. For example, mode 1 with rates (2,1,1,2) has equilibrium (2,1), while mode 2 with rates (1,2,2,1) has equilibrium (1/2,2). Alternating those modes illustrates forcing without a common equilibrium. This establishes existence, not uniqueness or attraction of the periodic orbit. The general novelty, if the affine dependency survives external review, lies in obtaining the common set for arbitrary high-dimensional endotactic graphs; this two-species consequence and Brouwer step have older precedents.

## Spatial reactors: a second exact control consequence

The common convex region also supplies a reaction/transport interface. Consider any finite number of compartments with concentration vectors x_z and common nonnegative mixing coefficients across species:

    x'_z = f_z(t,x_z) + Σ_w m_{zw}(t)(x_w-x_z),  m_{zw}(t)≥0.

At a supporting facet r·x_z+b=M_h of K_h, all neighboring compartment states satisfy r·x_w+b≥M_h. Thus every mixing contribution to the inward derivative is nonnegative:

    r·Σ_w m_{zw}(x_w-x_z)≥0.

The reaction contribution is also nonnegative by the active-slope argument. Consequently K_h^N is invariant for arbitrary compartment topology and arbitrary bounded measurable mixing coefficients. Choose h small enough to contain the compact positive initial concentration range. The resulting lower/upper concentration bounds are independent of N and the topology. This is all-time safety depending on the initial range, rather than a classwise absorbing-set claim.

For this invariance statement, individual modes need not share a stoichiometric span: a facet normal that annihilates the active mode simply has zero reaction derivative. Equal spans were required above to guarantee strict increase and absorption. A shared transport operator, such as identical scalar diffusion and advection for every species, preserves the same convex region by the standard parabolic invariant-region argument. Thus smooth equal-diffusion reaction systems on periodic or no-flux domains obtain uniform pointwise bounds and global continuation, conditional on the affine lemma. The PDE conclusion requires the usual regularity ensuring local solution existence.

The explicit two-species example is stronger because its certified region is rectangular. It permits species-dependent mixing coefficients:

    x'_{z,i}=f_{z,i}(t,x_z)+Σ_w m^{(i)}_{zw}(t)(x_{w,i}-x_{z,i}).

At a global coordinate minimum the mixing term is nonnegative; at a maximum it is nonpositive. Let m_i^0 and M_i^0 be the smallest and largest initial concentrations of species i across compartments. The exact reaction inequalities give the topology-independent entry bound

    T ≤ max_i {2(1/4-m_i^0)_+, (M_i^0-4)_+/2}.

Every compartment thereafter remains in [1/4,4]^2, regardless of the independent local mode/rate controllers. The same maximum-principle calculation applies to arbitrary positive species diffusion coefficients on periodic or no-flux domains. This is an unconditional consequence for this specific reactor motif; no general unequal-diffusion endotactic theorem is claimed.

The general common-transport restriction is necessary for an arbitrary convex region: take K={(a,b):a≥1,b≥1,a+b≤3}, two compartments initially (2,1) and (1,2), and symmetric mixing of A alone at rate m>0. The second compartment immediately has (a+b)'=m>0 and exits K. Equal transport would average the complete concentration vectors and preserve K.

Convex invariant-region transfer is an established method, not a new diffusion mechanism. [Reder, 1982](https://www.numdam.org/item/?id=AIF_1982__32_1_71_0), whose references also identify Chueh, Conley, and Smoller (1977), is a primary precedent. The possible new ingredient would be obtaining the required interior convex region for arbitrary endotactic controlled reactions. The explicit 0-complex reactions represent maintained feed/reservoirs; concentration bounds do not bound cumulative chemical feed or energy over a long operational horizon.

## Decisive falsification and limits

The first mathematical falsification target is the arbitrary-tolerance affine-minimum lemma. A counterexample to its activity approximation would invalidate the general permanence and compiler-termination claims while leaving the explicit example intact. The relevant complete source is `sources/149/build/sections/affine.tex`.

The second target is the directional endotactic equivalence: an admissible endotactic graph, slope r, and negative essential reaction at a minimal r-source would invalidate the extension. The independent prior-art and audit workers are checking this exact interface.

The assumptions have concrete boundaries. With 0↔A, k_forward(t)=e^{-t}, and k_reverse=1, the concentration is (a_0+t)e^{-t}→0; omitting the uniform positive lower rate bound fails. With forward rate e^t and reverse rate 1, the concentration diverges; omitting the upper bound fails. If a graph mode has zero stoichiometric span and freezes the state, a controller that remains in it prevents one fixed compact set from absorbing all initial points in the larger union class. Common mode span is sufficient; no necessity theorem for weaker persistent-excitation conditions is claimed.

Resource accounting: this scout downloaded roughly 44 KiB of exact release TeX plus two primary prior HTML documents; no whole-release proof ingestion or remote mutation occurred. The exact example needs only small integers/rationals and 256 evaluations. General geometry acquisition, number/height of facets, h_P, quantifier-elimination cost, and certificate length remain unbounded in the current theorem. The reactor's concentration counts are not memory-cost bounds, and deterministic permanent concentrations do not prohibit eventual extinction in a finite-molecule Markov chain.
