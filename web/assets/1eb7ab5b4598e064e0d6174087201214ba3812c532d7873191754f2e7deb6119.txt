LEAF 16 — BBGKY hierarchy, kinetic limit, and uniformity
Read date: 2026-10-08 (Australia/Brisbane)
Status: exact scoped derivation completed; no general uniform-in-time propagation-of-chaos claim.

RESULT

1. Model, state, and target

For N particles on R with coordinates z_i=(q_i,p_i), take the mean-field Hamiltonian

  H_N = (1/2) sum_i p_i^2 + (1/(2N)) sum_{i<j} (q_i-q_j)^2
      = (1/2) sum_i p_i^2 + (1/2) sum_i (q_i-qbar_N)^2,
  qbar_N = N^{-1} sum_i q_i.

Hamilton's equations are qdot_i=p_i and pdot_i=-(q_i-qbar_N). This is a globally Lipschitz, quadratic pair-interaction model; the center-of-mass coordinate is unconfined. Initial data are exactly iid with one-particle law gamma=N(0,I_2) on R^2, with independent standard Gaussian q and p. The norm used below is quadratic Wasserstein W_2 on the displayed Euclidean space; the fixed bounded observable is phi(q)=cos(q).

The kinetic reference solves the Vlasov equation

  partial_t f + p partial_q f + (-q + m_q[f_t]) partial_p f = 0,
  m_q[f_t] = integral q f_t(dq dp),     f_0=gamma.

The solution is f_t=gamma: its mean is zero and harmonic rotation preserves the standard Gaussian. Thus its q marginal and every k-fold kinetic product position law are standard Gaussian.

2. Exact BBGKY hierarchy

Let f_N^{(k)} be the normalized k-particle phase-space marginal and Z_k=(z_1,...,z_k). For 1<=k<N, integrating the Liouville equation gives

  partial_t f_N^{(k)}
  + sum_{i=1}^k (p_i partial_{q_i}-q_i partial_{p_i}) f_N^{(k)}
  + (1/N) sum_{i=1}^k (sum_{j=1}^k q_j) partial_{p_i} f_N^{(k)}
  + ((N-k)/N) sum_{i=1}^k partial_{p_i} integral q_{k+1} f_N^{(k+1)}(Z_k,z_{k+1}) dz_{k+1}=0.

Gaussian tails justify the integrations by parts. At k=N the final term vanishes and the internal term restores the exact force -q_i+qbar_N. At k=1 the equation is

  partial_t f_N^{(1)} + p partial_q f_N^{(1)} - q partial_p f_N^{(1)}
  + (1/N) q partial_p f_N^{(1)}
  + ((N-1)/N) partial_p integral q_2 f_N^{(2)}(z,z_2) dz_2=0.

The Vlasov closure replaces the last conditional field by m_q[f_t]=0 and drops the finite-N internal contribution. At level one the closure defect has two explicit flux pieces, N^{-1}q f_N^{(1)} and ((N-1)/N) E[q_2|z_1] f_N^{(1)}. The second piece is the first pair-correlation term in the hierarchy; it is not an unspecified higher-order remainder in this model.

3. Exact finite-N solution and hierarchy error

Write Qbar_0=N^{-1}sum_i q_i(0), Pbar_0=N^{-1}sum_i p_i(0). The solution is

  q_i(t)=Qbar_0+t Pbar_0 +(q_i(0)-Qbar_0) cos(t)+(p_i(0)-Pbar_0) sin(t),
  p_i(t)=Pbar_0 +(p_i(0)-Pbar_0) cos(t)-(q_i(0)-Qbar_0) sin(t).

It follows that the first k phase points are centered Gaussian with block covariance, in particle-major ordering,

  C_{N,k}(t)=I_{2k}+(1/N) J_k tensor B_t,
  B_t=[[t^2,t],[t,0]].

The diagonal particle block is I_2+B_t/N; each off-diagonal block is B_t/N. The relative coordinates contribute (I_k-J_k/N) tensor I_2, while the common displacement contributes the remaining block. Let

  mu_+(t)=(t^2+|t| sqrt(t^2+4))/2,   mu_-(t)=(t^2-|t| sqrt(t^2+4))/2.

These are the eigenvalues of B_t. Since J_k has eigenvalues k,0,...,0, the exact full phase-space factorization cost is

  W_2(L(z_1(t),...,z_k(t)), gamma^{tensor k})^2
       = (sqrt(1+(k/N)mu_+(t))-1)^2
         +(sqrt(1+(k/N)mu_-(t))-1)^2.                 (1a)

The square-root arguments are positive for k<=N. On every |t|<=T this gives a full phase-space bound C_T k/N, where C_T=sup_{|t|<=T} sqrt(mu_+(t)^2+mu_-(t)^2)<infinity; this follows by rationalizing each square-root difference and using denominator >=1. This is a proved equality, not a Gronwall estimate.

For the position projection alone,

  Sigma_{N,k}(t)=I_k+(t^2/N) J_k,

because the relative coordinates contribute I_k-J_k/N and the common displacement Qbar_0+tPbar_0 contributes (1+t^2)J_k/N. Thus its exact factorization error is

  W_2(L(q_1(t),...,q_k(t)), gamma_q^{tensor k})
       = sqrt(1+k t^2/N)-1.                         (1)

It yields the sharper low-order position estimates

  W_2(one-body q marginal, gamma_q) = sqrt(1+t^2/N)-1 <= t^2/(2N),
  W_2(two-body q marginal, gamma_q^{tensor 2}) = sqrt(1+2t^2/N)-1 <= t^2/N,

and, for any k<=N and 0<=t<=T,

  W_2(k-body position marginal, gamma_q^{tensor k}) <= k T^2/(2N).   (2)

The inequality uses sqrt(1+x)-1<=x/2. Thus fixed-k chaos holds with an explicit O(k T^2/N) position error on bounded time intervals, and the full phase-space error is O_T(k/N). No spatial or temporal discretization is used; the solution and covariance are exact. If one truncates the BBGKY hierarchy at level k by factorizing the next marginal, (1a) is the exact full phase-space cost and (1) the exact position-projection cost for this initial state.

4. Uniformity failures and the first uncontrolled hierarchy term

The q-q covariance of distinct particles is exactly

  Cov(q_1(t),q_2(t))=t^2/N.                              (3)

It is zero initially, O(1/N) at each fixed t, and order one at t_N=sqrt(N). Equation (3) is the first pair-correlation term that blocks uniform-in-time factorization. It is generated by the conserved random center-of-mass momentum Pbar_0, whose variance is 1/N, through the common displacement t Pbar_0. The relative coordinates remain harmonic and bounded; the secular contribution is entirely the neutral translation mode.

For the bounded 1-Lipschitz observable phi(q)=cos(q),

  | E phi(q_1(t)) - integral phi d gamma_q |
    = exp(-1/2) (1-exp(-t^2/(2N))).                       (4)

At t=sqrt(N), this is exp(-1/2)(1-exp(-1/2))=0.23865... independently of N. Hence there is no uniform-in-time one-body convergence, even in bounded-Lipschitz observables. Likewise, at any fixed t>0 with k_N/N -> alpha>0, (1) converges to sqrt(1+alpha t^2)-1>0: convergence is not uniform over all hierarchy levels k<=N. For fixed k, (1) tends to zero iff t_N=o(sqrt(N)); at t_N=c sqrt(N) the limit is sqrt(1+k c^2)-1.

The conditional moment in the k=1 BBGKY term makes the failure concrete. Let C_1(t)=[[1+t^2/N,t/N],[t/N,1]] be the covariance of (q_1,p_1). Gaussian conditioning gives

  E[q_2 | q_1=q,p_1=p] = [t^2/N, t/N] C_1(t)^{-1} (q,p)^T.

It vanishes at t=0 and is small at fixed t for large N, but its q coefficient is not uniformly O(1/N) as t grows. The stronger witness (3)-(4) does not depend on interpreting a pointwise conditional coefficient.

5. Stronger scoped result after removing the neutral mode

Define centered observables qtilde_i=q_i-qbar_N(t), ptilde_i=p_i-pbar_N. Their k-particle phase-space law is Gaussian with covariance (I_k-J_k/N) tensor I_2, independently of t, because the relative dynamics is a rotation and the initial law is isotropic. In particular, the centered k-position marginal has covariance I_k-J_k/N. Its exact distance to gamma_q^{tensor k} is

  W_2(L(qtilde_1,...,qtilde_k), gamma_q^{tensor k})
       = 1-sqrt(1-k/N) <= k/N, for 1<=k<=N.             (5)

For the full centered phase-space block the corresponding distance is sqrt(2)(1-sqrt(1-k/N))<=sqrt(2)k/N. These bounds are uniform for every t in R. Centered particles are still dependent (their distinct-particle q covariance is -1/N), so (5) is a finite-k marginal comparison, not exact independence. It identifies the precise scope in which a uniform-in-time chaos estimate is true for this model: after quotienting out the common translation/boost mode.

6. Equally informed baselines and charged costs

A same-initial-particle synchronous coupling to the kinetic harmonic path q_i^V(t)=q_i(0)cos(t)+p_i(0)sin(t) has the exact discrepancy

  q_i(t)-q_i^V(t)=Qbar_0(1-cos(t))+Pbar_0(t-sin(t)),
  E|q_i(t)-q_i^V(t)|^2=((1-cos(t))^2+(t-sin(t))^2)/N.

This is an equally informed, explicit baseline using the same initial sample. Its W2 upper bound is the square root of this quantity; the optimal marginal W2 in (1) is sharper at fixed t because it may recouple the two Gaussian laws rather than preserve particle labels. Both reveal order-one error at t~sqrt(N). Independently, the classical Dobrushin stability baseline can be derived directly here. For b(z,mu)=(p,-q+m_q[mu]), |b(z,mu)-b(z′,nu)|<=|z-z′|+W_1(mu,nu). Couple characteristics from any initial coupling and let D(t) be their expected distance; then D′(t)<=2D(t), so W_1(mu_t,nu_t)<=exp(2t)W_1(mu_0,nu_0). The empirical measure of the N particles is itself a measure-valued Vlasov solution from the iid empirical initial measure. By exchangeability and convexity of W_1 this gives W_1(f_N^(1)(t),gamma)<=exp(2t) E W_1(mu_N(0),gamma), for t>=0. This is an equally informed, general stability baseline using the same sample and state; it does not furnish an all-time O(1/N) bound. The same-initial-particle coupling above is a more concrete model-specific baseline. The exact law (1) supplies the sharp position-marginal answer and localizes the growth to the center-of-mass mode.

Accounting: pair force scale is exactly 1/N; retaining f_N^(k) requires the next marginal f_N^(k+1), so a BBGKY closure discards its conditional field. The exact k-position factorization cost is (1), with bounded-time upper cost kT^2/(2N); no grid, solver, Monte Carlo, or quadrature cost is charged because none is used. The all-N hierarchy level has order-one raw W2 error at fixed nonzero t, while the per-coordinate normalized error has a different scale; do not conflate these norms.

8. Falsifier, limitations, and restart

Falsifier: any contrary direct solution or simulation of the stated Hamiltonian and iid Gaussian initial state that fails the covariance identity Sigma_{N,k}=I_k+(t^2/N)J_k, or any proposed uniform-in-time uncentered estimate that survives the cos(q) witness (4), would falsify this leaf. A sharper uniform bound is possible only after changing the target/norm/state or removing the center mode; equation (5) exhibits one such scoped change.

Limitations: this is a one-dimensional quadratic, globally Lipschitz mean-field Hamiltonian with an unconfined center of mass and a special Gaussian initial law. It establishes no generic kinetic theorem, quantum-to-classical limit, collision/Boltzmann limit, or behavior for singular forces. The source audit is bounded as described above. Novelty and priority are not claimed.

Restart: (i) independently rederive the BBGKY marginal equation and covariance block; (ii) perturb the model with a confining external harmonic trap and determine whether the center-of-mass secular witness disappears; (iii) extend from Gaussian initial data only after identifying the empirical mean/variance terms exactly; (iv) compare an explicit Dobrushin finite-time bound against (1) with constants fixed; (v) keep uncentered, centered, fixed-k, and full-N claims separate. Do not promote the finite-time O(k/N) estimate to a uniform-in-time theorem.
