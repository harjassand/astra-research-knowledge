# Stationary-law comparison for local-resampling particle systems

## Verdict

There is a clean quantitative comparison statement for finite-volume, possibly nonreversible, single-site resampling dynamics. It bounds stationary disagreement coordinate by coordinate with the Green matrix `(I-C)^(-1)`, where `C_ij` measures the effect of input site `j` on the update at site `i`. A weighted contraction condition also gives a direct infinite-volume uniqueness criterion. The generator proof is short and explicit, but the finite result is a specialization of established Dobrushin / invariant-Markov-kernel comparison machinery; I do not claim novelty.

The main scope trap is update-clock mismatch. If the local update kernels are identical but site rates differ, stationary laws can still differ for a nonreversible system. The exact two-site example below rules out any perturbation estimate whose forcing term charges kernel changes alone. For compatible Gibbs heat-bath dynamics, clock changes preserve the Gibbs law; that special property does not extend to general nonreversible IPS.

## Finite-volume model and comparison theorem

Let `V` be finite and `Ω={0,1}^V`. At site `i`, a rate-`λ_i` clock replaces the spin by a Bernoulli draw with parameter `q_i(η)`, where `η∈Ω` is the current configuration. The generator is

`Lf(η)=Σ_i λ_i [Σ_{a∈{0,1}} q_i(a|η) f(η^{i←a}) - f(η)]`.

Compare a second system with kernels `q̃_i` and the **same** rates `λ_i`. Assume a common nonnegative influence matrix `C` bounds both families:

`||q_i(.|η)-q_i(.|ζ)||TV ≤ Σ_j C_ij 1{η_j≠ζ_j}`,

`||q̃_i(.|η)-q̃_i(.|ζ)||TV ≤ Σ_j C_ij 1{η_j≠ζ_j}`.

Here `TV(P,Q)=sup_A |P(A)-Q(A)|`. Define the same-configuration perturbation

`δ_i=sup_η ||q_i(.|η)-q̃_i(.|η)||TV`.

**Theorem.** If `ρ(C)<1`, each process has a unique stationary distribution, `μ` and `μ̃`. There exists a coupling of them for which the coordinate disagreement probabilities `p_i=P(X_i≠Y_i)` satisfy

`p ≤ δ + Cp`, and therefore `p ≤ (I-C)^(-1)δ = Σ_{n≥0} C^n δ`.

Consequently, for every finite `S⊂V`,

`||μ_S-μ̃_S||TV ≤ Σ_{i∈S} [(I-C)^(-1)δ]_i`,

and for every real observable `f`,

`|μf-μ̃f| ≤ Σ_i osc_i(f) [(I-C)^(-1)δ]_i`,

where `osc_i(f)=sup{|f(η)-f(ζ)|: η_j=ζ_j for all j≠i}`. These bounds are dimension-free for observables whose support and oscillations are fixed and whose propagated forcing remains bounded.

### Coupling proof

Use common rate-`λ_i` clocks for the two systems and a maximal coupling of the two Bernoulli draws at each clock ring. For a coupled state `(η,ζ)`, the expected new disagreement at updated site `i` is exactly `||q_i(.|η)-q̃_i(.|ζ)||TV`. By the triangle inequality this is at most `δ_i+Σ_j C_ij 1{η_j≠ζ_j}`. Thus the coupled generator applied to `D_i=1{η_i≠ζ_i}` is bounded above by

`λ_i[-D_i + δ_i + Σ_j C_ij D_j]`.

The finite coupled chain has an invariant law. Under `ρ(C)<1`, Perron–Frobenius gives positive `w` and `α<1` with `Cw≤αw`. The same-coupling drift with `δ=0` contracts the weighted maximum disagreement `max_i P(X_i≠Y_i)/w_i` at rate at least `λ_min(1-α)`, proving uniqueness of each marginal stationary law. Hence the marginals of any invariant law of the coupled chain are `μ` and `μ̃`. Taking stationary expectations in the coordinate drift gives `p≤δ+Cp`. Since `ρ(C)<1`, iteration and the vanishing remainder `C^n p` yield the resolvent bound. A coupling union bound gives the marginal TV estimate; telescoping `f` over disagreeing coordinates gives the observable estimate.

### Boundary perturbation

For a finite region `Λ` with frozen exterior boundary on `B`, let `C_{ΛΛ}` bound internal influences and `C_{ΛB}` bound the effect of exterior spins on inside updates. If the two boundary conditions are `b,b′`, then

`δ ≤ C_{ΛB} 1{b≠b′}`,

so

`p_Λ ≤ (I-C_{ΛΛ})^(-1) C_{ΛB} 1{b≠b′}`.

For a local observable, sum the corresponding coordinates weighted by `osc_i(f)`. The matrix powers enumerate directed chains of influence from changed boundary sites to the observable. This is a finite-volume boundary-sensitivity estimate; it does not itself assert a limit exists.

## Infinite-volume statement and phase boundary

Let `V` be countable, with finite spin space, finite-range update kernels, and rates satisfying `0<λ_min≤λ_i≤λ_max<∞`. Assume the graphical construction defines a Feller process on the compact product space. If there are weights with `0<w_min≤w_i≤w_max<∞` and

`Cw≤αw` for some `α<1`,

then the common-clock coupling satisfies

`sup_i P(X_i(t)≠Y_i(t))/w_i ≤ exp[-λ_min(1-α)t] sup_i P(X_i(0)≠Y_i(0))/w_i`.

Compactness and the Feller property give at least one invariant law; the local coupling contraction forces any two invariant laws to agree on every finite cylinder, hence the invariant law is unique. For two systems with the same rates and common `C`, the stationary coupling again obeys `p≤(I-C)^(-1)δ`, with the series converging in the weighted norm.

If every `q_i` is the conditional resampling kernel of one Gibbs specification, every Gibbs measure is invariant under this heat-bath process. The contraction condition therefore rules out Gibbs phase coexistence within its regime. It is only sufficient: the low-temperature two-dimensional ferromagnetic Ising model has nonzero spontaneous magnetization and distinct plus/minus infinite-volume states, while every finite-volume positive-rate heat-bath chain still has a unique stationary law. Thus finite irreducibility, infinite-volume uniqueness, and boundary-condition independence are separate claims.

## Rate-mismatch counterexample

On two sites, take the same update kernels in both systems:

`q_1(1|x_2)=1/5+(3/5)x_2`,

`q_2(1|x_1)=1/10+(1/5)x_1`.

All update probabilities lie strictly between zero and one, so the finite chain is irreducible. The influence matrix has `C_12=3/5`, `C_21=1/5`, and spectral radius `sqrt(3/25)<1`. Let `p=P(X_1=1)`, `q=P(X_2=1)`, and `r=P(X_1=X_2=1)` at stationarity. The coordinate stationarity equations give

`p=1/5+(3/5)q`, `q=1/10+(1/5)p`, hence `p=13/44`, `q=7/44`.

Applying stationarity to `x_1x_2` gives

`r=(λ_1 E[X_2 q_1(1|X_2)] + λ_2 E[X_1 q_2(1|X_1)])/(λ_1+λ_2)`
`  =(λ_1·7/55 + λ_2·39/440)/(λ_1+λ_2)`.

Thus `r=19/176` for rates `(λ_1,λ_2)=(1,1)` and `r=151/1320` for rates `(2,1)`, although `δ_1=δ_2=0`. The stationary laws differ. The two conditional kernels are incompatible with a common joint Gibbs law: their conditional odds-ratio changes are `16` and `27/7`, respectively. This example is not a Gibbs heat-bath process and shows why the theorem must match clocks or charge their difference.

For unequal rates, a valid coarse repair is to couple clocks at the common rate `m_i=min(λ_i,λ̃_i)` and treat the unmatched rate `e_i=|λ_i-λ̃_i|` as extra forcing. The same drift argument gives

`p ≤ (I-C)^(-1) g`, where `g_i=δ_i+e_i/m_i`,

provided `ρ(C)<1`. This bound is conservative and may exceed one; coordinatewise minimum with one is valid. The two-site example proves that a rate-forcing term cannot generally be omitted.

## Prior art and novelty boundary

Rebeschini and van Handel, *Comparison Theorems for Gibbs Measures*, J. Stat. Phys. 157 (2014), [arXiv:1308.4117](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.4117), provide the strongest directly relevant prior art checked here. Their Proposition 3.2 is a general comparison principle for invariant laws of Markov kernels using a Wasserstein influence matrix; their Theorem 2.4 gives the local-update Dobrushin comparison bound, and the paper treats finite or countable sites. In finite volume, the stationary law of the continuous-time generator above is also invariant for the random-scan kernel `G=Σ_i (λ_i/Σ_jλ_j)U_i`, where `U_i` is the local replacement update. The resolvent conclusion is therefore a direct specialization of their general comparison machinery after translating the coupling coefficients. No priority claim is supported.

Dobrushin’s primary uniqueness / phase-transition sources include “The problem of uniqueness of a Gibbsian random field and the problem of phase transitions,” *Functional Analysis and Its Applications* 2 (1968), [DOI 10.1007/BF01075682](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01075682), and “The description of the random field by its conditional distributions and its regularity conditions,” *Theory of Probability and its Applications* 13 (1968), [MathNet record](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=tvp&option_lang=eng&paperid=839&wshow=paper). For the coexistence obstruction, Griffiths’ primary paper “Peierls Proof of Spontaneous Magnetization in a Two-Dimensional Ising Ferromagnet,” *Physical Review* 136 (1964), [DOI 10.1103/PhysRev.136.A437](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.136.A437), gives a rigorous low-temperature spontaneous-magnetization argument.

## Evidence status and open gap

- The finite generator comparison, clock-mismatch extension, and two-site calculation are algebraically reconstructed in this report; no code or numerical experiment was used.
- The infinite-volume statement assumes a well-defined finite-range Feller graphical process and uniformly bounded positive update rates. Those assumptions are explicit; no assertion is made for unbounded rates, long-range non-summable influences, or general spin-flip generators whose update law depends on a separate jump mechanism.
- The Dobrushin comparison prior-art match is strong enough to reject novelty for the finite resolvent theorem. Whether the exact infinite-volume nonreversible generator formulation appears verbatim in another primary source was not established; the coupling proof itself is elementary and does not justify a priority claim.
- Dobrushin / Griffiths source records support the uniqueness/coexistence boundary. No external proof review or novelty search over the entire IPS literature was performed.
