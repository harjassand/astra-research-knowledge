Unforced 3D Navier–Stokes: energy closure and the missing critical bound
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: PROVED CONDITIONAL CRITERION + PROVED NO-GO FOR ENERGY-ONLY SCALE-FREE SMALLNESS; GLOBAL REGULARITY OPEN.

Target and scope
----------------
Fix viscosity nu>0 and consider the unforced incompressible equations on R^3:

  partial_t u + (u . grad)u + grad p = nu Delta u,
  div u = 0,     u(0)=u_0 in L^2_sigma(R^3).

This note concerns only this unforced finite-energy problem. The rescaled
examples below are ordinary smooth solutions of this equation; no forced
construction is used, and no singularity or global regularity conclusion is
inferred from them.

Exact Leray–Hopf energy statement
--------------------------------
For a Leray–Hopf solution, the initial-time energy inequality is

  (1/2)||u(t)||_2^2 + nu int_0^t ||grad u(s)||_2^2 ds
      <= (1/2)||u_0||_2^2                         for a.e. t>0.

In the standard strong energy-inequality formulation there is a full-measure
set S of starting times such that, for every s in S and every t>=s,

  (1/2)||u(t)||_2^2 + nu int_s^t ||grad u(r)||_2^2 dr
      <= (1/2)||u(s)||_2^2.

The global-initial-time version is enough for all estimates here. For smooth,
decaying solutions, integration against u gives the identity for every
0<=s<=t:

  (1/2)||u(t)||_2^2 + nu int_s^t ||grad u(r)||_2^2 dr
      = (1/2)||u(s)||_2^2.

The cancellation is exact: int (u.grad)u . u = int u.grad(|u|^2/2)=0,
int grad p . u=0, and int Delta u . u=-||grad u||_2^2. Leray's 1934
construction encodes this energy-dissipation inequality in its weak solution
class; the inspected original text explicitly says energy boundedness does
not by itself yield regularity (see READ_LEDGER.md).

Scaling and the critical quantities
-----------------------------------
For each lambda>0, the transformation

  u_lambda(x,t)=lambda u(lambda x,lambda^2 t),
  p_lambda(x,t)=lambda^2 p(lambda x,lambda^2 t)

preserves the same equation and viscosity on R^3. For 1<=p,q<=infinity,

  ||u_lambda||_{L^q_t L^p_x}
      = lambda^(1-3/p-2/q) ||u||_{L^q_t L^p_x}.

Thus 2/q+3/p=1 is the scale-critical line. Examples are L^infinity_t L^3_x
and L^5_t L^5_x (equivalently L^5 in space-time). Also invariant are
||u(t)||_{dot H^(1/2)} and the normalized local quantities built with
parabolic cylinders. By contrast,

  ||u_lambda||_{L^infinity_t L^2_x} = lambda^(-1/2)||u||_{L^infinity_t L^2_x},
  ||u_lambda(0)||_2^2 = lambda^(-1)||u(0)||_2^2,
  ||grad u_lambda||_{L^2_t L^2_x} = lambda^(-1/2)||grad u||_{L^2_t L^2_x},
  int ||grad u_lambda||_2^2 dt = lambda^(-1) int ||grad u||_2^2 dt.

The Leray–Hopf controls are therefore subcritical under concentration: they
shrink under precisely the rescaling that leaves a critical norm unchanged.
Energy interpolation does give u in L^(10/3)((0,T)xR^3), but that exponent
is subcritical and does not reach the L^5 Serrin quantity.

The tested closure mechanism and its no-go
------------------------------------------
Named mechanism E->S5: infer scale-invariant smallness of
||u||_{L^5((0,T)xR^3)} from only the two global energy-budget numbers

  E_0=||u_0||_2^2,       D_T=nu int_0^T ||grad u||_2^2 dt,

by interpolation or an energy-only estimate uniform in the time interval.
This is the attractive closure because smallness of a critical quantity can
be fed into a regularity argument. It fails as a scale-free mechanism.

Take any nonzero divergence-free Schwartz field phi with Fourier support in
an annulus 1<=|xi|<=2. Standard local strong existence supplies a nonzero
smooth unforced solution v on [0,tau] for some tau>0. For every lambda>=1,
set T_lambda=tau/lambda^2 and define u_lambda by the transformation above.
Then u_lambda is a smooth finite-energy unforced solution on [0,T_lambda],
and exact change of variables gives

  ||u_lambda(0)||_2^2 = lambda^(-1)||phi||_2^2,
  D_(T_lambda)(u_lambda)
      = lambda^(-1) nu int_0^tau ||grad v(s)||_2^2 ds,
  int_0^(T_lambda) int_R3 |u_lambda|^5 dx dt
      = int_0^tau int_R3 |v|^5 dx dt =: c_phi > 0.

The last constant is positive since v is continuous and v(0)=phi is
nonzero. The energy equality also holds exactly for every member of this
family. Hence E_0+D_T tends to zero while the critical L^5 norm remains
c_phi^(1/5)>0.

Consequently, for every C<infinity and alpha>0, no estimate of the form

  ||u||_{L^5((0,T)xR3)} <= C (E_0+D_T)^alpha

can hold uniformly over smooth unforced solutions and arbitrary finite T.
More generally, no scale-free bound F(E_0,D_T) with F(a,b)->0 as
a+b->0 can provide critical smallness. This is a no-go for the named
energy-only closure route, not a no-go for estimates that use additional
scale-invariant structure, and not evidence of blowup. The Fourier-shell
choice makes the same obstruction visible at t=0:
||phi_lambda||_{dot H^(1/2)}=||phi||_{dot H^(1/2)} although
||phi_lambda||_2^2=lambda^(-1)||phi||_2^2.

Equal-information baseline: with only E_0 and D_T, the Leray–Hopf inequality
and interpolation supply the subcritical energy class (including
L^infinity_t L^2_x, L^2_t dot H^1_x, and L^(10/3)_{t,x}); the rescaling test
shows that this same information cannot force the required scale-free L^5
smallness. Adding a Serrin norm changes the information available, so it is
listed separately as the successful conditional baseline below, not counted
as a same-information improvement.

Precise missing bound
--------------------
The missing input for this route is a bound that survives the parabolic
rescaling, such as a finite L^5_t L^5_x norm on the interval (for the
continuation criterion below), or pressure-aware local quantities such as
r^(-2) int_(Q_r)|u|^3 and r^(-2) int_(Q_r)|p-(p)_(B_r)(t)|^(3/2) that appear
in suitable-weak-solution epsilon-regularity methods. The energy inequality
controls neither of these by a scale-free function tending to zero with
(E_0,D_T). The equal-information energy interpolation is exactly

  int_0^T ||u(t)||_(10/3)^(10/3) dt
     <= C (sup_t ||u(t)||_2)^(4/3) int_0^T ||grad u(t)||_2^2 dt.

This yields the subcritical L^(10/3) spacetime bound from the same energy
inputs. At the critical exponent, Gagliardo–Nirenberg instead gives

  ||u(t)||_5^5 <= C ||u(t)||_2^(1/2) ||grad u(t)||_2^(9/2).

Energy controls sup_t ||u(t)||_2 and the time integral of ||grad u||_2^2;
it does not control the 9/2 power on the right. A new proof would need a
frequency-localized flux/cancellation estimate or a genuinely critical norm
bound, rather than interpolation of the global energy budget alone.

Conditional regularity criterion proved here
---------------------------------------------
Theorem. Let nu>0 and 0<T<infinity. Let u be a maximal strong solution of
the unforced 3D equations on R^3 with divergence-free u_0 in H^1(R^3), smooth
for t>0. If

  int_0^T ||u(t)||_5^5 dt < infinity,

then

  sup_(0<=t<T) ||grad u(t)||_2^2 < infinity,
  int_0^T ||Delta u(t)||_2^2 dt < infinity,

and the H^1 strong solution continues beyond T. Thus a finite maximal
strong-solution time T requires divergence of the critical L^5_t L^5_x
integral. This is a conditional theorem; it does not establish that every
finite-energy Leray–Hopf solution has finite L^5 norm.

Proof. Write Y(t)=||grad u(t)||_2^2, Z(t)=||Delta u(t)||_2, and
U(t)=||u(t)||_5. Pair the equation with -Delta u. The pressure term vanishes
because div Delta u=0. Integration by parts and Holder give

  (1/2)Y' + nu Z^2
     <= |int (u.grad)u . Delta u|
     <= U ||grad u||_(10/3) Z.

Interpolating grad u between L^2 and L^6, then using Sobolev on grad u,

  ||grad u||_(10/3)
     <= C ||grad u||_2^(2/5)||grad u||_6^(3/5)
     <= C Y^(1/5) Z^(3/5).

Therefore the nonlinear term is at most C U Y^(1/5) Z^(8/5). Young's
inequality with conjugate exponents 5/4 and 5 yields

  Y' + nu Z^2 <= C nu^(-4) U^5 Y.

Gronwall gives, for all t<T,

  Y(t) <= Y(0) exp(C nu^(-4) int_0^t U(s)^5 ds).

The assumed finite L^5 integral bounds Y uniformly on [0,T). Integrating the
differential inequality then gives int_0^T Z^2<infinity. The standard local
H^1 strong-solution theory has a lifespan bounded below in terms of nu and
the H^1 norm. Restarting at times approaching T therefore gives a uniform
continuation interval, contradicting maximality at T. This proves the
criterion. The exponent and viscosity power are dimensionally consistent:
nu^(-4)||u||_5^5 has units of inverse time.

Costs, falsifier, and limits
----------------------------
Analytical cost: one standard local-existence fact for smooth finite-energy
data, one Sobolev interpolation, Young's inequality, Gronwall, and H^1
continuation. No numerical computation is used; the rescaling identities are
exact. Source review cost: the original Leray PDF was inspected at the
introduction and energy-dissipation passage; Serrin's publisher record was
inspected for bibliographic metadata. The conditional estimate above is
derived in this note rather than claimed from a transcription of Serrin's
paywalled paper.

Falsifier for E->S5: produce a valid proof of a uniform estimate from only
(E_0,D_T) that forces L^5 smallness as E_0+D_T->0; the exact smooth rescaling
family above must then violate at least one stated hypothesis or one of the
three scaling identities. Any proposed estimate allowed to depend on T,
critical-scale ratios, profile information, or local pressure/flux data is a
different mechanism and is not ruled out by this no-go.

Falsifier for the conditional theorem: a smooth maximal H^1 solution with
finite int_0^T||u||_5^5 but unbounded H^1 norm or no H^1 continuation would
contradict the displayed differential inequality or the stated local
continuation theorem.

Restart
-------
Keep the proved criterion and rescaling no-go fixed. The next useful attack
is a theorem that derives a scale-invariant local bound from the actual
nonlinear dynamics, with pressure included, or a counterexample to a named
candidate flux estimate. First state that estimate with all scale factors
and quantifiers; test it against concentration rescalings before attempting
to sum it over scales. Do not turn the rescaling no-go into a claim about
singularity or global regularity.
