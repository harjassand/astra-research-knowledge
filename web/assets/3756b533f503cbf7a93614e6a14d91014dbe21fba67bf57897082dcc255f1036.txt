DECISIVE CONCLUSION
Finite polynomial regularization, Kesten tree polynomials, and the Christoffel
minimum-residual principle are published classical machinery. Their direct
composition gives explicit finitely supported real h for the stated T_m, with
degree r=O(eta^-2) and m=O(eta^-4). I did not locate a separately stated published
theorem with exactly this T_m/eta formulation. That absence is not novelty evidence;
the construction below is an elementary specialization of established results.

EXACT SPECIALIZATION: OUR DERIVATION FROM CLASSICAL MACHINERY
Let d=m+1, Q=T_m T_m*, m>=1. In the regular representation of F_m,
the self-adjoint block operator B=[[0,T_m],[T_m*,0]] is the adjacency
operator of the d-regular bipartite tree on two copies of F_m. Edges
are labelled by 1,V1,...,Vm. A cycle would give a nonempty freely
reduced relation, so the graph is a tree. This elementary realization
also transfers all polynomial L2 identities to any free-Haar system
with the same tracial *-distribution.

Set F0=1,F1=x,F2=x^2-d,Fn=xF(n-1)-mF(n-2), n>=3.
Then Fn(B)delta_o is the indicator of the distance-n sphere.
For n>=1, that sphere has d*m^(n-1) vertices, while
F(2j)(0)=(-1)^j*d*m^(j-1). Define the polynomial
G_j(y)=F(2j)(sqrt(y)); this is a real degree-j polynomial.
The G_j are orthogonal for the Q spectral measure, with
||G_0||^2=1 and ||G_j||^2=d*m^(2j-1) for j>=1.

Thus the Christoffel kernel at zero has
K_r(0,0)=1+r*d/m. Put kappa=1+r*d/m and

 q_r(y)=[1+sum_{j=1}^r (-1)^j*m^(-j)*G_j(y)]/kappa,
 p_r(y)=(1-q_r(y))/y,
 h=T_m* p_r(Q) delta_e.

Because q_r(0)=1, p_r is a real polynomial of degree r-1; the
quotient means polynomial division, not an unbounded inverse.
Hence h is real and finitely supported. Direct spectral calculus gives
T_m h-delta_e=-q_r(Q)delta_e. Simon's variational principle gives

 ||T_m h-delta_e||_2^2=m/[m+r(m+1)].                 (A)

It is exactly minimal among residuals q(Q)delta_e with deg q<=r and
q(0)=1; this does not assert joint norm-constrained optimality.

The coefficient of h at each vertex in the odd sphere 2j+1 is

 c_j=(-1)^j*(r-j)/[m^(j+1)*kappa], 0<=j<r.

This follows by solving the radial adjacency equations:
d*c_0=1-1/kappa and c_(j-1)+m*c_j=(-1)^(j+1)*m^(-j)/kappa,
with c_r=0. Summing squares over the odd spheres yields

 ||h||_2^2=(m+1)*r(r+1)(2r+1)/[6(m+r(m+1))^2].    (B)

In particular (A)<1/r and (B)<r/(3m). An entirely explicit strict
choice is r=ceil(eta^-2) and any integer m>r/(3eta^2). Both norms
are then <eta. There is no weak-convergence threshold and no final
nonconstructive density approximation. Degree r and m imply exact
support size

 (m+1)*sum_{j=0}^{r-1}m^(2j),

which is exponential in r for m>1. The construction is effective but
not polynomial in expanded support size as a function of 1/eta.
For the supplied prefix realization, each prefix p_j has ambient
word length j^2+3j, so h's support words have length at most
(2r-1)(m^2+3m); w_m has length m^2+3m+1.

For fixed m, letting r tend to infinity makes the residual vanish
but makes ||h||_2^2 grow asymptotically as r/[3(m+1)]. This agrees
with the Kesten hard-edge fact that the exact inverse is not L2:
the Q density behaves as const*x^(-1/2), so integral x^(-1) dmu
diverges. Finite approximants never assume an exact L2 inverse.
