## Target whose priority is being assessed

The strengthened internal candidate is the all-particle Gibbs density

\[
 \rho_N=\frac{\exp\{Q_{\alpha I+A_N}/N+F_{b_N}\}}
 {\operatorname{Tr}\exp\{Q_{\alpha I+A_N}/N+F_{b_N}\}},
 \qquad Q_C=\sum_{a,b}C_{ab}F_aF_b,
\]

on the full tensor product of N fundamental SU(d) sites. For each fixed
d>=2, central ferromagnetic coupling alpha>0, and field bound B<infinity,
c04_s01 gives an explicit positive radius delta_*(d,alpha,B) such that the
actual finite-N state is fully separable for every N>=1 whenever A_N is real
symmetric with norm at most delta_* and the supplied one-body field is within
the stated bound. The neighborhood radius may be extraordinarily small.
The local state factors in a separable decomposition may differ by site; no
exact iid-product decomposition of the perturbed state is claimed. The
statement is for the whole density, not only fixed marginals or a symmetric
spin sector. The primary internal statement and proof are in
`work/cycle6/c04_s01/revisions/FULL_RANK_STOPPING.txt` and
`work/cycle6/c04_s01/revisions/OPEN_NEIGHBORHOOD.txt`; the separately
reconstructed absorption proof is in
`work/cycle6/c04_s02/revisions/EXACT_FULL_SEPARABILITY.txt` and is stated for
N>=d+1. These are internal candidates pending the assigned technical audits.

## Findings

I did not find a reviewed primary source that directly proves this exact
uniform-in-N, fixed nonzero anisotropic open-neighborhood statement at
arbitrary fixed effective temperature and bounded field. The closest sources
subsumed sublemmas or nearby regimes, but their theorems have material scope
gaps. Accordingly, novelty/priority is **UNKNOWN**; the comparison below is
not an exhaustive literature search and is not a novelty certification.

### General separable-ball prior art: Gurvits–Barnum (2003)

Gurvits and Barnum, “Separable balls around the maximally mixed multipartite
quantum states,” arXiv:quant-ph/0302102, Phys. Rev. A 68, 042312 (2003),
prove that an m-partite positive matrix within Frobenius radius
`2^{-(m/2-1)}` of the identity is fully separable. Their normalized-state
corollary gives a much larger generic identity-centered ball than the crude
`||E||_1 <= d^{-2N}` ball reconstructed in the c04 absorption argument (for
qubits, their normalized radius is approximately `2^{-(3N/2-1)}`). The paper
explicitly says its proof does not give a separable representation. This
subsumes any priority claim for merely proving a generic multipartite
identity-centered separable ball. It does not itself establish that the
model-specific anisotropic Gibbs state lies in or can be absorbed into that
ball uniformly in N; that step needs a product-mixture approximation with a
sufficient white-noise component and an error/rate comparison.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0302102 and
https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0302102 (abstract, introduction and Corollaries
1 and 3). The paper states at its introduction that its argument is
nonconstructive as a decomposition, despite the separability criterion.

### Exact general high-temperature results

Bakshi, Liu, Moitra and Tang, “High-Temperature Gibbs States are Unentangled
and Efficiently Preparable,” FOCS 2024, Theorem II.4, prove exact full
separability for qubit Pauli Hamiltonians under the low-intersection condition
at `beta < 1/(100 Delta K)`. For all-to-all two-body interactions, the dual
interaction degree Delta grows with N, so this theorem does not yield a
constant-temperature guarantee for the present mean-field scaling. The paper
also emphasizes that a field restriction is material: Remark II.6 gives a
two-qubit weak-interaction example where sufficiently large one-local terms
can leave the Gibbs state entangled.

Putterman, Zlokapa and Cotler, “When quantum thermal states look classical,”
arXiv:2607.28536v2 (5 October 2026), Theorem 1(a), improve the structural
high-temperature scope to arbitrary long-range qubit Pauli Hamiltonians of
locality k and per-site absolute interaction strength s. They prove exact
separability for all system sizes when `beta <= 1/(72 s k)`; their Hamiltonian
class includes fields as one-site terms counted in s. This is strong and
close prior art for the all-to-all qubit specialization. Writing the present
exponent in their convention puts the effective central coupling and field
into s, with k=2 for the pair-interaction expansion. Their theorem therefore
covers a high-temperature/effective-coupling region, not arbitrary fixed
`beta*alpha`; it also does not cover fundamental SU(d>2) as stated. Neither
the theorem nor its upper-bound examples establish the target’s specialized
low-temperature neighborhood around an integrable isotropic Casimir.

Primary sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16850 and the FOCS proceedings
PDF at https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2024-Papers/pdfs/FOCS2024-1oojWxXs5YAKfs3z3lBRMF/167400b027/167400b027.pdf;
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28536 and full v2 text
https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28536. Putterman et al. define the long-range
class at §2.1 / Eq. (12), state the all-N high-temperature separability bound
in Theorem 1(a), Eq. (22), and explicitly describe it as a temperature
threshold. These sources are close prior art, not direct subsumption.

### Closest collective-spin thermal-state study (2026)

Mathé, Usui, Gühne and Vitagliano, “Estimating the best separable
approximation of non-pure spin-squeezed states,” Quantum 10, 2078 (2026),
arXiv:2504.07814v2, treat the full permutation-invariant Hilbert space of
fully connected spin-1/2 XXZ thermal states, including all total-spin sectors.
Their model is `H=g/N(J_x^2+J_y^2)+g_z/N J_z^2+h J_z`. It is therefore a very
close physical/model comparator, and includes anisotropic directions away
from the isotropic XXX point. But the paper's result is an SSI-derived lower
bound on the best separable approximation plus numerical/iterative upper
bounds from candidate separable states. Sections 3.3–3.4 report finite-size
computations (including N=8 and N=200) and asymptotic lower-bound formulas for
special cases. They say a zero lower bound often comes with a numerically
close separable ansatz; this is not an exact all-N separability proof. The
large-N completeness statement is only for matching first and second
collective moments, not equality of the full density matrix. No uniform
anisotropy ball or arbitrary-fixed-temperature theorem appears in the paper.
It also observes entanglement at nonzero temperature in some ordered FM XX
regions; that is a useful boundary warning and is compatible with a
sufficiently small, model-specific neighborhood around the isotropic point.

Primary source: https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2026-04-21-2078/;
full article https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07814. I downloaded and inspected the
35-page article PDF. The exact XXZ model is defined in §2.4 (Eq. 20); the
thermal-state numerics are in §3.3; the analytic results in §3.4 are
asymptotic SSI/BSA lower bounds, not exact decomposition theorems.

### Mean-field/de Finetti and 1991 critical-fluctuation sources

The official Springer page for Fannes, Kossakowski and Verbeure, “Critical
fluctuations for quantum mean-field models,” J. Stat. Phys. 65, 801–811
(1991), describes a Ginzburg–Landau-type approximation for *local Gibbs
states* leading to exact thermodynamics, and computation of spin-fluctuation
laws for some spin-1/2 models, including abnormal fluctuations at criticality.
That is highly relevant to the critical/local fluctuation lineage. Springer
exposes only the abstract and marks the paper as subscription content; the
direct PDF request did not supply full text and the accessible ResearchGate
record offered no full-text file. Thus the abstract does not support a claim
that the paper proves this whole-density exact separability theorem, but the
unread body leaves historical overlap unresolved. Do not assert that FKV
1991 lacked the result.

Official source/access record: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01053756.

Fannes and Vandenplas, “Finite size mean-field models,” arXiv:quant-ph/0605216,
give finite de Finetti-style approximation for low-order marginals; their
finite-N theorem bounds two-site marginals, not the entire N-body state by an
iid mixture. Infinite-volume exchangeability/de Finetti results and local
marginal limits likewise do not imply finite-N full separability of the
whole Gibbs density. Primary source: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0605216.

## Priority conclusion and decision gate

The exact isotropic Casimir/product-mixture identity and the generic
identity-centered separable ball each have substantial prior art. The
high-temperature separability region is also directly anticipated in broad
qubit Pauli classes. The particular candidate that remains distinguishable
in the reviewed sources is the *combination*: an explicit, fixed nonzero
anisotropy neighborhood around the ferromagnetic fundamental-SU(d) Casimir,
at any specified finite coupling/temperature and bounded field, with exact
full separability of the entire finite-N state uniformly for every N in the
stated range. I found no direct primary-source proof of that combination.

This is not a publication-priority verdict. The load-bearing source gate is
still the inaccessible full text of FKV 1991 and a broader targeted search
for exact Gibbs separability of anisotropic fully connected XXZ/collective
models. In particular, the closest 2026 XXZ paper was published before this
workspace's 2026-10-08 audit date but is numerical/variational rather than a
uniform theorem. Candidate correctness is separately under active internal
audit; priority evidence cannot substitute for checking the logdet/SDE
argument, all-N constants, and the separable-ball absorption inequality.
