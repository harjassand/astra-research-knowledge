1. Exact model and conventions
------------------------------
Let L>=2, S=L-1, n_i=(I-Z_i)/2, N=sum_{i=1}^S n_i, and
W=sum_{i=1}^{S-1} n_i n_{i+1}; there is no wraparound contact. Keep BOTH
endpoint fields, also when S=1:

 Hcrit = J sum_{i=1}^{S-1}(I-X_i X_{i+1}-Y_i Y_{i+1}-Z_i Z_{i+1})
       + J(I-Z_1)+J(I-Z_S),       J>0,
 B_L(kappa,lambda_c)=L^2 Hcrit-kappa W-lambda_c N,
 lambda_c=2 J pi^2,               beta=tau L^2.

All results below use this S=L-1 convention. In the m-subset basis X of
{1,...,S}, an allowed move exchanges an occupied and empty neighboring site.
The exact Hcrit matrix has diagonal 2J[d(X)+b(X)], off-diagonal -2J per
allowed move, where d(X) is the number of allowed moves and
b(X)=1_{1 in X}+1_{S in X}. Thus both endpoint fields contribute, including
twice on the single site when S=1. W is diagonal and counts only internal
adjacent occupied pairs.

2. Exact one-magnon counterexample
----------------------------------
In the m=1 basis |i>, i=1,...,L-1, the matrix is the Dirichlet path operator
with diagonal 4J and off-diagonal -2J. Its exact eigenvalues are

 epsilon_{k,L}=4J(1-cos(pi k/L)),   1<=k<=L-1.

Consequently the exact lowest shifted energy is

 E_{L,1}=4J L^2(1-cos(pi/L))-2J pi^2.

Since 1-cos x < x^2/2 for x>0, E_{L,1}<0 for every finite L>=2. Taylor
expansion gives

 E_{L,1}=-J pi^4/(6L^2)+J pi^6/(180L^4)+O(L^-6).

Therefore no a>0 can satisfy E_{L,m}>=a m^2/L uniformly over all m>=1.
This is an exact spectral refutation, not a numerical extrapolation. It also
shows why a proposed “positive curvature” must exclude m=1 or include a
finite-size correction. At m=1, W=0 for every kappa.

3. Fixed-m projected-sine derivation: an upper bound only
---------------------------------------------------------
Set phi_i=sin(pi i/L), f_m(X)=product_{i in X} phi_i, and
q_i=phi_{i+1}/phi_i+phi_i/phi_{i+1}-2>=0. Summing the one-particle Dirichlet
equations and deleting the two blocked hops for every occupied neighboring
pair gives the exact identity

 (Hcrit f_m)(X)/f_m(X)=m epsilon_{1,L}
                  +2J sum_{i:i,i+1 in X} q_i.

With probability mu_m(X)=f_m(X)^2/sum_Y f_m(Y)^2, the Rayleigh quotient is
therefore exactly

 R_{L,m}=m(c_L-lambda_c)
       +2J L^2 sum_i q_i mu_m(i,i+1 both occupied)
       -kappa sum_i mu_m(i,i+1 both occupied),
 c_L=L^2 epsilon_{1,L}.

This f_m is an exact fixed-number projection of a one-mode product vector.
It is a valid sector Rayleigh trial, so E_0(B_{L,m})<=R_{L,m}; it is not a
lower bound. For fixed m, write p_i=phi_i^2/sum_j phi_j^2
=2 sin^2(pi i/L)/L. Elementary-symmetric-polynomial normalization of the
distinct-site product measure yields, for fixed m,

 mu_m(i,i+1 occupied)=m(m-1)p_i p_{i+1}[1+O_m(L^-1)],
uniformly in i; this relative form is used for the endpoint-weighted sum.

The Riemann sums are

 sum_i p_i p_{i+1}=3/(2L)+O(L^-2),
 L^2 sum_i p_i p_{i+1}q_i=pi^2/(2L)+O(L^-2),
 c_L-lambda_c=-J pi^4/(6L^2)+O(L^-4).

For the second sum, q_i=(pi/L)^2 cot^2(pi i/L)+ lower-order terms away
from the endpoints, and the weighted integrand is
sin^4(pi x)cot^2(pi x)=sin^2(pi x)cos^2(pi x), whose integral is 1/8.
The endpoint terms are controlled by the vanishing sin^4 weight. Hence

 R_{L,m}=binom(m,2)(lambda_c-3 kappa)/L+O_{J,kappa,m}(L^-2).
                                                        (A)

For 0<=kappa<kappa_t=lambda_c/3, (A) is positive at fixed m>=2 and
contains an m(m-1)/L coefficient. But (A) remains only an upper bound on
the sector ground energy. The coherent-profile threshold kappa_t is a
variational threshold; neither (A) nor the coherent-state calculation gives
a matching sector lower bound. At kappa=0, the sine ground-state transform
also gives the exact lower bound

 E_0(B_{L,m}) >= m(c_L-lambda_c)=-m J pi^4/(6L^2)+O_m(L^-4),

which is far too weak to establish the positive 1/L curvature. Together
with (A), it does prove E_0(B_{L,m})->0 for each fixed m at kappa=0. The
fixed-m critical heat/form limit gives the same zero ground limit for bounded
kappa, but it does not determine a first correction. No step here promotes
the fixed-m quantifier to m=m(L).

The distinction from an unprojected coherent state is essential. A
spin-coherent product is a superposition of number sectors, so its expectation
is a probability-weighted average of sector expectations. It cannot be read
as the ground energy in any one m-sector. Projection of the sine product to
fixed m gives the Rayleigh upper bound above, not a sector lower bound.

4. Sector heat traces are not sector ground energies
----------------------------------------------------
For fixed m, the critical continuum sector heat trace is the ideal symmetric
Dirichlet-boson expression

 Z_m^B(tau)=sum_{1<=k_1<=...<=k_m}
   exp[-tau lambda_c sum_{a=1}^m(k_a^2-1)].                 (B)

The all-first-mode configuration contributes 1, while excited mode tuples
give additional terms. Thus E_0(B_{L,m})->0 only implies that the ground
Boltzmann term tends to 1; it does not imply Z_{L,m}->1. For m=1 there is
also an exact finite-L trace:

 Z_{L,1}(tau)=sum_{k=1}^{L-1}
   exp{-tau[4J L^2(1-cos(pi k/L))-lambda_c]}.

At tau=0.05, formula (B) gives Z_1^B=1.052145987571115 and
Z_2^B=1.054845894553744 (J=1). The dense m=2 diagnostic at L=32 gives
Z_{L,2}=1.027717235 at the same tau, while its ground energy is 0.570997195;
the trace includes excited levels and is not exp(-tau*0.570997195).
Finite-L trace data are in DIAGNOSTICS.json. The fixed-m continuum trace
limit is supported by the exact gap-coordinate/form-convergence argument in
the prior local chain analysis; it is not inferred from the lowest eigenvalue.

--------------------------------
The runnable dense script constructs the exact-convention occupation matrix
and uses NumPy symmetric eigvalsh. Its mathematical entry formula is
integer-valued times J; the actual assembly, lambda_c=2J pi^2 shift, and
diagonalization use float64, so the reported eigenvalues are numerical, not
certified algebraic intervals. At kappa=0, the m=2 values are:

 32             465                0.5709972       18.271910

These are finite dense eigenvalues (floating diagonalization of the specified
matrix), and L E_0 trends toward lambda_c=19.739209. That is evidence for the
m=2, kappa=0 coefficient, not a proof of its limit. At L=32, m=2, dense
values for kappa=0, lambda_c/12, lambda_c/4 are respectively
L E_0=18.271910, 13.196980, 2.997249. The projected-sine limiting
coefficients from (A) are lambda_c, 3lambda_c/4, lambda_c/4 respectively;
the finite ground data are below these trial upper coefficients as expected.

An exploratory L=24 scan for m=2,...,8 is in GROWING_M_L24.json. The m=2
entry is dense. The m>=3 entries are single-pass Lanczos Ritz values from
the legacy scanner without full reorthogonalization, so they are not
certified intervals and are included only to expose finite-size behavior.
They show that the fixed-m coefficient is not a reliable uniform formula
at these m/L values. No growing-m asymptotic is claimed.

----------------------------------------------
Primary-source comparison: Napiórkowski and Seiringer's treatment of the
quantum Heisenberg chain supplies relevant spin-deficit, gap-map, and

Inputs/costs: J=1 in numeric runs; lambda_c=2 pi^2; tau=1 for the ground
scan and tau=0.05 for heat traces; kappa in {0,lambda_c/12,lambda_c/4} for
the L=32 two-particle check. Dense diagonalization reaches dimension 1081

it prove the coefficient in (A) for sector ground energies. Fixed-m
heat-trace convergence, coherent-product energy, and growing-m ground
curvature are three separate claims.
