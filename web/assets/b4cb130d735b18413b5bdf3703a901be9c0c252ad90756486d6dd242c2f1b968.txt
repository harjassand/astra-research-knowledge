TEACHER-FREE RECOVERY OF BOUNDED NONCOMMUTATIVE RATIONAL FORMULAS
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: conditional constructive result for equivalence classes; exact syntax recovery is impossible. The efficient descriptor extension remains an internally derived candidate.

DECISION

There are three different tasks. (1) Identity testing asks whether a supplied formula denotes zero. (2) Equivalence-class recovery asks for any representation of the same element of the free skew field. (3) Syntax-tree recovery asks which exact parse tree the hidden teacher used. Exact black-box matrix evaluations can support (1) and, with finite bounded-height enumeration, (2); they can never solve (3), even with unlimited exact queries. Matrix input access and exact inverse-domain status matter. Scalar evaluations at scalar inputs do not suffice for noncommutative functions, and unbounded numerical noise defeats exact recovery.

MODEL AND NOTATION

Let F=Q, let x_1,...,x_n be noncommuting variables, and let Phi be an admissible rational formula of tree size at most s. An inverse-height bound is not needed separately (it is at most s); no polynomial degree bound is assumed. For the finite-enumeration construction below, assume a known coefficient-height bound H: every rational constant is encoded in lowest terms with numerator and denominator of at most H bits. Define formula equivalence as equality on every matrix tuple in the intersection of the two expression domains, at all finite dimensions; this is the free-skew-field equivalence used in the primary rational-identity literature.

The exact matrix oracle accepts an explicitly specified tuple A=(A_1,...,A_n) of rational square matrices, and returns the full matrix Phi(A), or an exact UNDEFINED flag if an inverse occurrence in the hidden tree fails. It supports every dimension requested by the algorithm. A query uses rational entries of explicitly charged bit length. This is an active, noncommuting matrix oracle; it is not passive scalar data or evaluations only on commuting scalars.

N51 supplies the needed finite hitting list over Q: for formula-size bound S, a deterministic list H(n,S) of rational matrix tuples, polynomial in n,S to generate, such that every nonzero admissible size-S rational formula has a defined, invertible value at at least one tuple, preserving its original inverse occurrences. Write L(n,S) for its list length, d(n,S) for its largest matrix dimension, and b(n,S) for its largest entry bit length. The released theorem claims all three polynomially bounded. Its construction is extremely large in constants; no practical runtime is implied.

1. IDENTITY TESTING WHEN THE FORMULA IS SUPPLIED

Query every tuple in H(n,s) and evaluate the supplied formula exactly. Ignore UNDEFINED responses. If any defined response is nonzero, the formula is nonzero. If every defined response is zero (including the case that all responses are UNDEFINED), the formula is an identity. Soundness of the zero answer follows by contraposition: a nonzero admissible formula has a defined invertible value at one list tuple. The admissibility promise is essential; a nowhere-defined expression is not covered.

This is a polynomial-list black-box identity test conditional on N51. A currently published comparator already gives deterministic quasipolynomial black-box rational identity testing for general formulas: Arvind–Chatterjee–Mukhopadhyay, arXiv:2309.15647v4, Theorem 2. Their parameters for inversion height theta are list size (ns)^{O(theta^5 log s)} and matrix dimension s^{O(theta)}; balancing reduces general size-s formulas to theta=O(log s), hence quasipolynomial bounds. This result is identity testing, not formula reconstruction. The N51 release's polynomial list is a newer source claim; no priority claim is made here.

2. EXACT EQUIVALENCE-CLASS RECOVERY BY FINITE ENUMERATION

The following direct reduction is enough to turn any domain-preserving hitting list into a teacher-free equivalence-class learner. It needs no intermediate-gate access.

Algorithm. Set S=2s+O(1), query the hidden oracle on every tuple in H(n,S), and store each full exact output or UNDEFINED flag. Enumerate every rational formula tree of size at most s over the n variables with constants of height at most H. For each candidate Psi, evaluate its own tree on the same list. Retain Psi if, at every list point where the hidden oracle was defined, Psi is also defined and its full matrix output is exactly equal. Return any retained candidate as a representative of the hidden equivalence class.

Why it works. The hidden tree is among the enumerated candidates, so at least one candidate survives. Suppose a candidate Psi computes a different free-skew-field element. The difference Delta=Phi-Psi is a nonzero rational formula of size at most 2s+O(1). It is admissible: take a common multiple N of dimensions at which Phi and Psi are respectively defined. Repeating each regular tuple block-diagonally shows that each formula's domain in Mat_N(Q)^n contains a nonempty Zariski-open set. The intersection of two nonempty Zariski-open subsets of this irreducible affine space is nonempty over the infinite field Q. Apply the N51 list at that size. At a tuple where Delta has a defined invertible value, every inverse occurrence from both original trees is defined, and Phi(A) differs from Psi(A). The hidden response is defined there, so Psi is rejected. Thus every survivor is equivalent to Phi. Equivalent candidates may have different domains; the rule deliberately ignores points where the hidden oracle is undefined, so domain differences do not invalidate the returned function class.

Costs and scope. The query count is L(n,2s+O(1))=poly(n,s); query dimension is at most d(n,2s+O(1))=poly(n,s); query-entry bit length is b(n,2s+O(1))=poly(n,s). Each response is a full d-by-d matrix and its exact rational encoding is charged. The candidate count is at most M=2^{O(s(H+log(n+s)))} under a fixed operator/leaf grammar. Candidate evaluation takes O(s) matrix operations per tuple, so the simple learner uses at most O(M L s d^omega) field operations with standard dense linear algebra, plus exact rational bit costs. The query cost is polynomial, but this exhaustive decoder is exponential in sH; it is a proof of identifiability and a terminating learner, not an efficient implementation. It does not need a degree bound. In the absence of known H this enumeration does not terminate.

For Q inputs of height H, query entries of at most b bits, and D-by-D matrices, rational output heights have a polynomial upper bound P=poly(s,D,H,b). One route to this bound is the standard size-O(s) linear-pencil representation of a rational formula: after substitution, Phi(A) is an entry/block of an inverse of a matrix of order O(sD), and determinant height bounds give P polynomial in the encoded input. The same bound applies to a size-O(s) difference. Consequently every nonzero rational entry at a N51 witness has magnitude at least 2^{-P}. This gives an absolute-precision sufficient condition for a noisy-output variant of the finite-enumeration learner: if oracle errors are entrywise at most eta<2^{-P-2}, candidate responses are computed exactly, and the oracle's defined/undefined status is exact, a false candidate differs by more than the matching tolerance at its witness. This is only a worst-case separation bound, not a practical condition estimate; the exponent and constant in P were not calculated here.

3. MORE EFFICIENT REPRESENTATION LEARNING CANDIDATE

The local S evidence package contains a stronger derived construction, “N51 CONSEQUENCE: DETERMINISTIC ACQUISITION OF AN NC RATIONAL DESCRIPTOR” (source page d-f4fc10a8fa770e96). It learns an affine matrix-inverse descriptor alpha(C-sum_i D_i x_i)^{-1} beta from exact full-matrix queries, without receiving the formula tree or a coefficient-height bound for its algebraic query count. Its claimed descriptor order is q<=R=(s+1)d, where d is the acquired regular-centre dimension. The procedure obtains a regular centre from the inverse-node domains; perturbs all n d^2 matrix entries independently; represents one output entry as a recognizable noncommutative series of dimension R; uses a finite graded feature map to distinguish every residual in that R-dimensional space; and acquires a residual basis by breadth-first closure over k=n d^2 letters. It then specializes the generic-entry variables back to x_i and takes the reachable/observable quotient.

With L0=|H(n,2s+1)|, L1=|H(k,R^2+1)|, and e_max the largest feature-tuple dimension, the package gives query count Q<=L0+(1+kR)L1+R and maximum original-oracle query dimension at most d R(R+1)e_max. All are polynomial in n,s if N51's polynomial list theorem is accepted. The descriptor pencil is claimed to be invertible at every point in the original formula tree's domain, so the learned descriptor evaluates to the same function at every such point. This is domain containment, not equality of the two syntactic domains. The proof route is plausible and specific, but this leaf has not independently re-proved its residual-feature injectivity or invariant-quotient specialization. The N51 generator and full learner are unexecuted; only a reduced toy replay is recorded. Treat this as INTERNALLY-PROVED-CANDIDATE, not an externally checked theorem. The descriptor is a reusable representation, not a polynomial-size scalar formula or the original syntax tree.

For exact Q bit complexity, a bound on hidden constant height H is needed to turn the field-operation statement into a polynomial bit-time statement. The package claims polynomial total bit costs in n,s,H using linearization/determinant bounds. For an abstract coefficient field, exact field operations and equality are oracle capabilities; a binary bit bound is inapplicable. Actual oracle evaluation time, complete matrix-reply encoding, and every dense response bit remain charged.

4. PARTICULAR SYNTAX TREE: UNCONDITIONALLY UNIDENTIFIABLE

No query algorithm can recover the teacher's exact parse tree from values and definedness, even with unlimited exact full-matrix access. The distinct equal-size trees x+0 and 0+x have the same value on every matrix tuple and are both defined everywhere. Therefore they induce identical transcripts under every adaptive evaluation strategy. A canonical representative can be selected after recovering the function class, but the hidden original tree cannot be inferred. If the target is syntactic provenance, it must be supplied by metadata or a syntax-revealing oracle; more value queries do not help.

5. OUTPUT ACCESS AND NOISE BOUNDARIES

Scalar substitutions alone cannot identify general noncommutative formulas: [x_1,x_2]=x_1x_2-x_2x_1 is zero on every scalar tuple but nonzero on some matrix tuples. A trace-only matrix readout also loses information, since every matrix commutator has trace zero. By contrast, a scalar readout of a full matrix-valued evaluation can simulate full output if the service allows conjugated input tuples: f(P A_i P^{-1})=P f(A) P^{-1}; a polynomial number (at most D^2) of first-diagonal readouts with known permutation/shear conjugations recovers every entry. Conjugation preserves all inverse domains. This raises the query count and does not compress response information.

Without a separation promise, exact recovery from bounded additive noise is impossible, even for constants: 0 and rational delta I are indistinguishable when |delta| is below the noise level. Unbounded coefficient heights permit arbitrarily small delta. With height H and rational query points, the P-bit bound above supplies a conservative precision scale, but exact definedness flags or certified distance from singular inverse gates are still required. Neither N51 nor N68 proves a stable numerical decoder. N68's Prony/Hankel recovery is explicitly exact and can be badly conditioned.

6. N68 CONTROL: THE SPARSE DIVISION-FREE SUBCLASS

N68 is a useful narrower positive control. For f=sum_{j<=s} c_j w_j in Q<x_1,...,x_n> with at most s nonzero ordered words and no degree/word-length bound, the local N68 derivation constructs one exact full-matrix query of dimension 2s and entry bit length O(s log(n+1)). A base-(n+1) affine action assigns distinct integer codes alpha(w) to all words. Column sums of f(A) return 2s power moments sum c_j alpha(w)^k. Exact Hankel rank and Prony recovery find the support and coefficients; deterministic Sturm isolation and base decoding cost polynomial in n,s and the actual response bit length. Dimension 2s is optimal for this universal one-query learner over Q. The ECCC primary paper itself proves a one-query identity-testing tuple of dimension s for s-sparse noncommutative polynomials; the 2s-dimensional acquisition and decoder are a derived consequence, not the paper's stated theorem.

This does not solve rational-formula learning: inverse gates can encode functions that are not sparse polynomials, and expanding a size-s formula may give exponentially many words. N68 supplies neither inverse-domain tests nor a noisy decoder. A scalar first-diagonal service can acquire its 2s moments using 2s conjugated scalar queries, but arbitrary unknown coefficients still require output information; finite bit-packing is not free.

7. FORMULA-SUPPLIED BASELINE

If the tree is supplied, evaluating Phi at one D-by-D tuple takes O(s) matrix-gate operations and checks each inverse where it occurs; with dense linear algebra this is O(s D^omega) field operations, plus rational bit growth. There is no acquisition step. If identity testing is needed, deterministic polynomial-time white-box rational identity testing is known from noncommutative-rank algorithms; Arvind et al. summarize this baseline and contrast it with their quasipolynomial black-box result. A teacher-free learner instead pays many large matrix queries and full-response costs to obtain a reusable descriptor/class. The present construction makes no claim that this is cheaper than a supplied formula for evaluating a particular input.

EXACT GAPS / FALSIFIERS

1. N51 release scope: the official September 24 OpenAI Math preprint's abstract is Q-specific and claims a polynomial-size rational-matrix list with no separate nesting or constant-height bounds. The local N51 card extends the mechanism to all coefficient fields/extensions and a strong simultaneous affine-pencil property; that extension is SOURCE-DERIVED/UNREVIEWED here and is not needed for the Q-only enumeration theorem. The exact polynomial exponents are not recorded in this leaf. Full list generation has not been run; the local all-field cost diagnostic already reports about 4.91e62 grid points and 2.06e69 dense integer entries for n=B=1. This is an asymptotic construction, not a practical query generator.
2. The descriptor learner requires independent review of the regular-centre construction, graded residual-feature injectivity, exact BFS closure, and invariant-quotient domain argument. The local record reports a 70-case toy replay with 120 calls; this does not run the promised-size generator or establish a general theorem.
3. Prior-art priority for descriptor learning and for the hitting-list result is UNKNOWN. Existing recognizable-series/minimal-realization methods are standard. Arvind et al. provide quasipolynomial black-box identity testing, not the reconstruction theorem stated here. N68's older sparse interpolation comparator has degree dependence. No novelty claim follows from a finite source search.
4. No noisy stability, scalar-only general rational-formula learner, passive trajectory learner, or polynomial-size scalar-formula reconstruction is established. The matrix-inverse lower bound in Hrubeš–Wigderson (2015) rules out inferring a small scalar formula merely from a small matrix descriptor in general.
5. A decisive external check is an independent proof review of N51 and the descriptor's domain-preserving quotient, followed by execution on a moderate exact generator. Practicality likely requires a much smaller domain-preserving hitting set.
