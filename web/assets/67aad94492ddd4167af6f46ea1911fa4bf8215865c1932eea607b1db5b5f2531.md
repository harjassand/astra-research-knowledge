# Disordered transport: a quantitative local-data limit

## Verdict

I reconstructed a complete model theorem showing that local microscopy cannot support a uniform finite-sample estimate of bulk directional conductivity from ellipticity and phase fraction alone. Two stationary, ergodic, uniformly elliptic binary media can have the same 50/50 phase law, nearly indistinguishable full coupon maps, and a fixed, high-contrast gap in their exact homogenized conductivity tensors. The separation is explicit and the information bound is quantitative.

This is a defensible candidate theorem, not a verified priority claim or a new engineering law. The qualitative warning that stationary ergodicity alone supplies no convergence rate is established prior art. The narrower contribution here is an explicit two-phase laminate pair and a local-observation total-variation/Le Cam lower bound. I found no exact match in the scoped source search, but the lower bound follows from elementary laminate homogenization and a standard coupling; novelty remains unresolved.

Full derivation: [laminate_local_data_lower_bound.md](proof/laminate_local_data_lower_bound.md). Finite discrete audit: [check_stripe_coupon_bound.py](scripts/check_stripe_coupon_bound.py).

## Candidate theorem

Take scalar conductivities 1 and K>1 in alternating stripes of width L, with a uniformly random phase. One ensemble has stripes normal to e1; the other has stripes normal to e2. Both are stationary and ergodic, satisfy 1≤a≤K, and have exactly one-half of each phase. Their homogenized tensors are

\[
A^{\rm v}_{\rm hom}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{2K}{K+1},\frac{K+1}{2}\right),\qquad
A^{\rm h}_{\rm hom}=\operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{K+1}{2},\frac{2K}{K+1}\right).
\]

For a square coupon of side w≤L, the laws of the entire coefficient maps under the two ensembles have total-variation distance at most w/L: with probability 1−w/L the coupon contains no interface, and the shared part of both laws is the same 50/50 mixture of constant-high and constant-low coupons. For N independent coupons, the distance is at most min(1,Nw/L). Every estimator of the tensor therefore has worst-case expected operator-norm error at least

\[
\frac{(K-1)^2}{4(K+1)}\left(1-\frac{Nw}{L}\right)_+.
\]

At K=100, the two principal conductivities are 200/101≈1.98 and 101/2=50.5, a ratio 10201/400≈25.50. For N=20 coupons of side w=10 and hidden stripe width L=2000, the data-law distance is at most 0.1, while the risk lower bound is about 21.83 in conductivity units. The theorem still holds if each coupon yields an exact, noiseless pixel map; any local boundary measurement or noisy sensor output is less informative by data processing.

The necessary design gate from this pair is explicit: without an upper bound on the correlation scale L, finite local data cannot guarantee fixed accuracy uniformly over stationary ergodic media. For a confidence claim that distinguishes the two targets with error at most δ, a necessary condition is Nw/L≥1−2δ, unless the model class rules out these long-range laminates. This is not sufficient for accuracy in other media.

## Proof and audit status

The normal coefficient is the harmonic mean because the normal flux is constant across layers; the tangential coefficient is the arithmetic mean because the affine tangential field has zero corrector. Rotating the layers swaps the entries. For the observation bound, the no-interface event has probability 1−w/L and leaves the same constant-coupon sublaw in each ensemble. The remaining mass is w/L, so total variation is at most w/L. Product subadditivity gives Nw/L. The common-part argument and triangle inequality give the estimator-risk bound. Full derivation is in the linked proof file.

The exhaustive finite-pixel script checks four periods and coupon widths. It reproduces the exact discrete analogue TV=(w−1)/L and the K=100 harmonic/arithmetic calculations. This is a scoped arithmetic check; the continuum statement rests on the proof, not the enumeration.

## Interface and costs

Supplied: contrast interval [1,K], exact 50/50 phase fraction, coupon shape/side w, and independent coupon sampling. Acquired: coefficient or sensor data from N local windows. With pixel size h, a full map costs N(w/h)^2 scalar readings, before calibration and specimen preparation. If a local boundary-value solve is also performed, its mesh, solver tolerance, and runtime must be charged separately; the information lower bound holds even if that solve is exact and free. Knowing L, or measuring large enough windows to expose L, is an additional acquired input. Repeated measurements of one small coupon do not enlarge its spatial window.

For a standard representative-volume computation, one must also charge the number and size of generated or cut specimens and each corrector solve. Gloria’s i.i.d. algorithm explicitly trades regularization T, outer box L, proxy box R≥L, and number of realizations N against error and compute. The 2019 RVE-selection theorem assumes finite-range dependence; its local-dependence structure and expected sample statistics are premises, not quantities inferred for free from a phase fraction. This branch does not claim an algorithmic runtime bound for the general RVE PDE solve.

## Prior art reconstructed

- [Gloria and Otto, “An optimal variance estimate in stochastic homogenization of discrete elliptic equations” (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.1291) treats i.i.d. edge coefficients. It proves central-limit-scale variance for smooth spatial averages of corrector energy, with a logarithmic correction in dimension two. Its stochastic-independence premise excludes the hidden-period ensemble here.
- [Gloria, “Numerical approximation of effective coefficients in stochastic homogenization of discrete elliptic equations” (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2011018) gives a computable proxy for the regularized corrector and analyzes the cost/error tradeoff in T, L, R, and N for i.i.d. conductances. This is the strongest direct algorithmic baseline located for conductivity estimation.
- [Fischer, “The Choice of Representative Volumes in the Approximation of Effective Properties of Random Materials” (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00205-019-01400-w) rigorously analyzes RVE selection under uniform ellipticity/boundedness, stationarity, and finite-range dependence. It gives an explicit fluctuation/bias interface and extends the analysis to strongly elliptic systems. The stated finite-range premise is what the stripe scale violates when L is unknown. Its useful proof spans are the assumptions at §§2–2.1 and Theorem 2: pp. 644–647 in the published pagination / HTML lines 334–405.
- [Gloria, Neukamm, and Otto, “Quantitative estimates in stochastic homogenization for correlated coefficient fields” (2021)](https://doi.org/10.2140/apde.2021.14.2497) addresses slowly decaying correlations under a multiscale logarithmic-Sobolev hypothesis and derives rates with critical dimension/correlation-decay regimes; it also covers systems such as linear elasticity. Its rates require a quantified correlation law, which is absent in the candidate lower-bound class.
- [Ravichandran, Nguyen, and Schneider, online 2025 / issue 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00466-025-02694-2) is recent computational prior art for linear elastic particle composites. It separately measures random error and systematic error for periodized and snapshot ensembles, finds snapshot bias can exceed and decay more slowly than random error, and reports that fixing phase fraction reduces variance without changing systematic bias. Their FFT study is a finite family of generated spherical/fiber microstructures, not a universal local-data guarantee. It makes the present distinction practically timely but does not prove the laminate lower bound.
- [Torquato, “Exact Expression for the Effective Elastic Tensor of Disordered Composites” (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.79.681) expresses effective elastic tensor expansions through n-point structure correlations. It motivates treating higher-order spatial organization as meaningful, but this branch proves only scalar heat/electrical conduction, not an elasticity analogue.

No cited quantitative rate was transferred outside its own independence, finite-range, correlation-decay, periodization, or material-generation assumptions. In particular, exact volume fraction, an RVE diagnostic, and an observed small variance do not bound the systematic error for an arbitrary unknown ensemble.

## Model versus material

The prediction is exact for the continuum, scalar, steady diffusion equation with perfectly bonded ideal layers and no contact resistance, convection, finite-size phonon effects, or fabrication defects. It establishes that an inference guarantee needs a correlation-scale or other structural premise; it does not say that an industrial material contains long laminates, nor predict an actual material’s conductivity without imaging and constitutive calibration.

## Discriminating falsifier and next gate

The mathematical falsifier is direct: any claimed estimator that promises fixed directional accuracy over all stationary ergodic fields using only K, phase fraction, and N local windows is contradicted by choosing L≫Nw. A physical model check is to fabricate two 50/50 layer specimens at contrast K=100, rotate the layers by 90 degrees, map coupons much smaller than L, and measure steady in-plane conductivity along e1 on specimens much larger than L. The scalar Fourier model predicts about 1.98 versus 50.5, up to discretization and interface corrections; a failure beyond those measured corrections rejects the physical ideal-laminate model, not the information-theoretic inference proof.

Next theoretical gate: derive an analogous explicit pair for an elasticity tensor, or replace the exact laminate pair with isotropic ergodic ensembles whose scalar effective conductivities differ while finite local patch laws remain close. Either would broaden application. The current result remains a candidate counterexample mechanism with unresolved priority; it does not establish a new general stochastic-homogenization rate or an actual-material forecast.
