PRIMARY-SOURCE COMPARISON: REGULAR SU(3) CARRIERS, FLAGS, END(V), AND FILTERS
Checked: 2026-10-08
Scope: targeted comparison of primary sources; this is not a comprehensive search or a priority/novelty determination.

SETUP AND INTERFACE

Write V_(a,b) for the SU(3) irrep with Dynkin labels a,b >= 0. It is regular exactly when a,b>0. Its dimension and dual are

  d_(a,b) = (a+1)(b+1)(a+b+2)/2,
  V_(a,b)^* = V_(b,a),
  End(V_(a,b)) ~= V_(a,b) tensor V_(b,a).

The highest-weight line of a regular carrier has compact stabilizer T^2, so its pure coherent-state orbit is SU(3)/T^2, the full flag. If one Dynkin label is zero, the stabilizer grows and the orbit is a partial flag (CP^2). This distinction is built into the flag/Borel-Weil sources below, not a separate novelty claim.

For a Gibbs family generated by a regular Cartan element H, rho = Z^{-1} exp(-beta d pi_(a,b)(H)), beta>0, its stabilizer inside the represented SU(3) is exactly the centralizer of H. Indeed, the matrix exponential is injective on Hermitian matrices, and d pi_(a,b) is injective on su(3) because its kernel is an ideal and the representation is nontrivial. If H has three distinct fundamental eigenvalues, that centralizer is T^2, so the SU(3) conjugacy orbit is SU(3)/T^2. Accidental equal energies can enlarge the ambient U(d) centralizer without enlarging this represented SU(3) stabilizer. A Gibbs state of a central Casimir is scalar by Schur's lemma and has a trivial orbit. The coherent-state papers below construct rank-one orbits and use coherent-state resolutions in thermal/path-integral calculations; they do not by that fact prove a theorem about arbitrary Gibbs-state orbit information or spectra.

CLOSEST EXACT RESULTS

1. SU(3) coherent states on the full flag.

M. Mathur and D. Sen, “Coherent States For SU(3),” arXiv:quant-ph/0012099v1 (submitted 2000-12-19); J. Math. Phys. 42 (2001), 4181–4196, DOI 10.1063/1.1385563. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0012099v1 ; version record: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0012099

For every (N,M), their six-boson construction gives normalized coherent vectors in the carrier (N,M), with d=(N+1)(M+1)(N+M+2)/2. The states have a resolution

  integral dOmega |z,w>_(N,M) <z,w|_(N,M) = I/d,

where dOmega is their SU(3) group measure normalized to total mass one; multiplying the integral by d gives I. The coherent-state stationary subgroup is generally U(1)xU(1), hence the projective orbit is SU(3)/T^2. For the boundary families (N,0) and (0,M), the stabilizer is U(1), giving CP^2 instead. For mixed representations they use all six oscillators; the state labels parametrize an eight-real-dimensional SU(3) group chart with phase redundancy, not a six-dimensional quotient chart.

Exact overlap: this directly covers the regular highest-weight coherent orbit and a normalized resolution of identity. It also develops a path-integral formalism for Hamiltonians built from SU(3) operators. Its result is not a spectral theorem for End(V_(a,b)), a rank-constrained Casimir inequality, or a general theorem about full-rank Gibbs-state orbits.

2. Flag Toeplitz/Berezin calculus, End multiplicities, and asymptotic saturation.

M. Dawson and Y. Hernández-Eliseo, “A Representation-Theoretic Approach to Toeplitz Quantization on Flag Manifolds,” arXiv:2406.10804v2 (v2 dated 2025-02-12; v1 submitted 2024-06-16); J. Funct. Anal. 288(9) (2025), 110877. Primary text/version: https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10804v2 ; record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10804

Their parabolic convention says the compact stabilizer L is determined by the support of lambda. For SU(3), lambda=a omega_1+b omega_2 with a,b>0 has full support, S=empty, and L=T; a zero coefficient gives a partial flag. This matches the regular/boundary split above.

With v_lambda a unit highest-weight vector and Haar probability measure, the Toeplitz quantization is, under their End(V_lambda) identification,

  T_f = d_lambda integral_G f(g) |pi_lambda(g)v_lambda><pi_lambda(g)v_lambda| dg.

Their trace convention is Tr(T_f)=d_lambda integral f. The covariant Berezin symbol of an operator A is gL -> <A pi(g)v_lambda, pi(g)v_lambda>. The factor d_lambda belongs in T_f; the coherent vector in the covariant symbol is unit norm. For Delta_lambda(g)=<v_lambda,pi(g)v_lambda>, Schur orthogonality gives integral |Delta_lambda|^2=1/d_lambda, so d_lambda |Delta_lambda|^2 is a unit-mass kernel and B^lambda f=d_lambda f*|Delta_lambda|^2.

Two exact finite-dimensional statements are especially close to “filters.” First, their Theorem 3.10 says a highest-weight vector tensored with a lowest-weight vector is cyclic in the tensor product; applied to V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*, v_lambda tensor bar(v_lambda) is cyclic. Proposition 3.11 then makes T^lambda:C^infinity(G/L)->End(V_lambda) surjective. Proposition 5.2 identifies the symbol-side kernel and an isomorphic finite-dimensional subspace of symbols. Thus every operator, including every isotypic projector, has a smooth Toeplitz preimage, but a symbol preimage need not be unique.

Second, for an irreducible pi occurring in End(V_lambda), they define K_pi^lambda as the span in V_pi^L of the projections of v_lambda tensor bar(v_lambda) onto its individual copies. Those projected vectors are linearly independent, and

  mult_G(pi, End(V_lambda)) = dim K_pi^lambda <= dim(V_pi^L).

For the full flag L=T, the upper bound is the zero-weight multiplicity of pi. Their Proposition 6.3 gives the Berezin transform on this pi block as (d_lambda/d_pi) times a concrete operator B_pi^lambda on K_pi^lambda built from those projected vectors. This is a multiplicity-space matrix, not a general scalar multiplier; when a representation occurs more than once, replacing it by one scalar would erase the copy-label data. This is an exact covariant-symbol formula, though it does not by itself give an explicit closed-form matrix for every (a,b,pi).

Their Theorem 4.5 states: for closed H<=G, Toeplitz operators with H-invariant symbols commute if and only if pi_lambda restricted to H is multiplicity-free. For SU(3), the U(3)->U(2) branching is multiplicity-free, so the corresponding U(2)-invariant-symbol family is commuting. This is a precise commuting-filter result under its branching hypothesis.

Their Theorem 7.1 gives an approximate identity on G/L when lambda_n is supported on Pi\S and, for every C>0, min_(Pi\S) lambda_n >= C log n eventually, while max_(Pi\S) lambda_n <= n^D eventually for some D>0. On the SU(3) full flag this requires both Dynkin coefficients to grow superlogarithmically, with their maximum polynomially bounded. Their Corollary 7.4 further proves, for each fixed pi and sufficiently large n,

  mult_G(pi, V_lambda_n tensor V_lambda_n^*) = dim(V_pi^L),

provided the lambda_n representations are self-conjugate. For SU(3), self-conjugacy means a_n=b_n. The approximate-identity assertion itself does not require self-conjugacy, but the stated multiplicity-saturation corollary does; Observation 7.2 explicitly flags removing self-conjugacy as an unproved possible extension. This is eventual saturation for each fixed pi, not a simultaneous all-pi finite decomposition formula.

3. Covariant symbols of weight projectors.

D. Bar-Moshe, “A Method for Weight Multiplicity Computation Based on Berezin Quantization,” arXiv:math-ph/0306056v3 (v3 dated 2009-09-25; v1 submitted 2003-06-23); SIGMA 5 (2009), 091, DOI 10.3842/SIGMA.2009.091. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/math-ph/0306056v3 ; record: https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0306056

For compact semisimple G, maximal torus T, and a Borel-Weil carrier pi_lambda on G/T, Proposition 1.2 gives the principal Berezin symbol of the projector onto weight m as

  L_m^lambda(zeta,bar z) = integral_T (m-lambda)(h) exp(K^lambda(h.z,bar z)) dmu(h).

Here torus Haar measure has total mass one. Proposition 1.3 reconstructs the weight multiplicity as the rank of a Hermitian form from this polynomial symbol on the largest Schubert cell. The Liouville measure on G/T has total mass V in the paper's convention, and the section inner product carries the factor N^lambda/V.

Exact overlap: this is directly a covariant-symbol filter for weight subspaces and multiplicities inside one carrier. It is not the same multiplicity problem as decomposing End(V_lambda) under conjugation; its output is weight multiplicity gamma^lambda(m), not mult(pi, V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*). Keep the line-bundle/Borel-Weil sign convention and the Liouville-volume factor when comparing formulas.

4. Exact SU(3) tensor-product multiplicities.

A. D. Berenstein and A. V. Zelevinsky, “Triple Multiplicities for sl(r+1) and the Spectrum of the Exterior Algebra of the Adjoint Representation,” J. Algebraic Combin. 1 (1992), 7–22. Primary scan: https://pages.uoregon.edu/arkadiy/triple.pdf

Their Theorem 1 identifies dim(V_lambda tensor V_mu tensor V_nu)^sl(r+1) with the number of nonnegative integer arrays satisfying stated linear boundary constraints. For r=2 (sl_3), the BZ triangle has nine nonnegative entries, six outer constraints, and two independent hexagon constraints: one integer parameter remains. Therefore an SU(3) triple multiplicity is the number of integers in a bounded interval. They define the invariant triple multiplicity, so to get the output multiplicity in V_lambda tensor V_mu, use the dual output: dim(V_lambda tensor V_mu tensor V_nu^*)^G.

J. Rasmussen and M. A. Walton, “su(N) tensor product multiplicities and virtual Berenstein-Zelevinsky triangles,” arXiv:math-ph/0010051v3 (v3 dated 2001-12-12; v1 submitted 2000-10-31); J. Phys. A 34 (2001), 11095–11105, DOI 10.1088/0305-4470/34/49/324. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/html/math-ph/0010051v3 ; record: https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0010051

They give an explicit integer-point/polytope formula for all su(N) tensor multiplicities. In su(3), their Eq. (24) is a single sum of 1 over integer eta from an explicit maximum of three affine expressions to a minimum of six affine expressions (using ordinary and dual Dynkin labels); equivalently, the multiplicity is the positive part of upper endpoint minus lower endpoint plus one. It applies directly to End(V_(a,b)) after substituting lambda=(a,b), mu=(b,a), and dualizing the requested output label. This is an exact, all-parameter antecedent for the one-dimensional interval-count structure. The current local note `spectrum_exact.txt` instead uses a GL(3) Littlewood–Richardson lift and its row-tableau variable; the coordinates differ, but both count the same coefficient.

As a second exact route, L. Bégin, P. Mathieu, and M. A. Walton, “su(3)_k fusion coefficients,” arXiv:hep-th/9206032v1 (submitted 1992-06-08), Mod. Phys. Lett. A 7 (1992), 3255–3266, DOI 10.1142/S0217732392002640, gives a closed formula for all su(3)_k fusion coefficients and states that the k->infinity limit gives all ordinary su(3) tensor coefficients. Primary record: https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9206032. This is a fusion-level formula whose stable/infinite-level limit returns the ordinary tensor multiplicity; Rasmussen–Walton/BZ is the more direct finite ordinary-tensor statement.

A useful warning against assuming multiplicity one: V_(1,1) is the regular adjoint 8, and the LR/BZ count gives two adjoint copies in 8 tensor 8. A carrier GT basis therefore cannot be mistaken for a multiplicity-free SU(3) decomposition of End(V).

5. Gelfand–Tsetlin branching and quantization of the flag.

I. M. Gelfand and M. L. Tsetlin, “Finite-dimensional representations of the group of unimodular matrices,” Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR 71 (1950), 825–828 (Russian); English reprint in I. M. Gelfand, Collected Papers, vol. II (Springer, 1988), 653–656. English scan: https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Gelfand/Gelfand7.pdf

For U(3), lift (a,b) to the polynomial highest weight Lambda=(a+b,b,0). The U(3)->U(2) restriction has one copy for each interlacing (mu_1,mu_2), Lambda_1>=mu_1>=Lambda_2>=mu_2>=Lambda_3; U(2)->U(1) adds one copy for each nu_1 with mu_1>=nu_1>=mu_2. The resulting GT patterns label a basis, equivalently the joint spectral labels of the nested GT commutative algebra. This yields exact carrier-level projectors/filters onto branching-pattern basis vectors.

V. Guillemin and S. Sternberg, “The Gelfand-Cetlin system and quantization of the complex flag manifolds,” J. Funct. Anal. 52(1) (1983), 106–128, DOI 10.1016/0022-1236(83)90092-7. Primary journal page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022123683900927

Their result constructs action-angle variables and Bohr–Sommerfeld sets for the collective Gelfand–Cetlin system; applying Sniatycki quantization gives multiplicity formulas that the authors prove are the correct multiplicities of the associated representations on complex flag manifolds. This is an exact geometric-quantization theorem on flags.

Mismatch: GT labels resolve a carrier basis and Guillemin–Sternberg quantizes the integrable system. Neither statement makes V_lambda tensor V_lambda^* multiplicity-free, nor does it alone label the multiple copies of a fixed SU(3) irrep in End(V_lambda). For those copy multiplicities, use the SU(3) tensor-coupling/BZ or LR data; then a GT basis can be used inside the coupled factors. Dawson–Hernández-Eliseo's Theorem 4.5 is the closer theorem for commuting Toeplitz filters, with the explicit multiplicity-free restriction condition.

NORMALIZATION / SCOPE CHECKLIST

- Coherent-state dimension: Mathur–Sen's D(N,M) agrees with d_(a,b) for N=a, M=b. Their normalized full-group measure gives integral |z,w><z,w|=I/d; Dawson's quantization inserts d_lambda in T_f so T_1=I.
- Flag measure: Dawson uses normalized Haar/quotient measure, v_lambda of norm one, Tr(T_f)=d_lambda integral f, and integral |Delta|^2=1/d_lambda. Bar-Moshe instead writes total Liouville mass V and uses N_lambda/V in the section inner product; torus Haar is unit mass.
- Tensor coefficients: BZ/Rasmussen–Walton count integer couplings with ordinary/dual Dynkin-label conventions; the output weight must be dualized in the invariant-triple formulation. These are dimensionless integer multiplicities.
- Casimir/energy: the present spectral note uses Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2 and C2(p,q)=(p^2+pq+q^2+3p+3q)/3. The cited flag and tensor multiplicity theorems do not fix this quadratic-Casimir operator normalization or state the cumulative Casimir-projector/rank profile in this convention. BZ's root normalization (long roots of squared length 2) is used for weight-coordinate combinatorics, not as an assertion of this operator scaling.
- Gibbs orbit: for positive-temperature Gibbs states generated by a regular Cartan H with three distinct fundamental eigenvalues, the stabilizer inside the represented SU(3) is its centralizer T^2. Accidental equal eigenvalues of the represented density matrix may enlarge its ambient U(d) centralizer, but do not enlarge this SU(3) orbit stabilizer. This follows from injectivity of the Hermitian exponential and faithfulness of d pi on su(3); it should not be attributed to the coherent-state resolution theorem.

OVERLAP ASSESSMENT (SCOPED, NOT A NOVELTY CLAIM)

Strongest prior overlap: full-flag coherent states and their resolution are explicit in Mathur–Sen; finite End(V_lambda) decomposition multiplicities are exactly the classical SU(3) LR/BZ coefficients, with a one-parameter integer interval formula; Dawson–Hernández-Eliseo give a representation-theoretic flag Toeplitz/Berezin calculus, surjective quantization, the exact multiplicity-space matrix, commuting families under a multiplicity-free subgroup restriction, and eventual saturation for fixed pi along self-dual regular sequences. Bar-Moshe gives an exact covariant-symbol method for carrier weight projectors; GT gives exact branching-pattern carrier filters.

These theorems do not themselves assert the finite cumulative Casimir rank profile in `spectrum_exact.txt`, a rank-constrained Dirichlet lower bound, or a general Gibbs-orbit information theorem. That statement is only a comparison of the cited theorem contents, not evidence of absence from the broader literature and not a priority/novelty conclusion. The exact local profile should be compared against the above classical multiplicity and flag-symbol machinery before any sharper claim.
