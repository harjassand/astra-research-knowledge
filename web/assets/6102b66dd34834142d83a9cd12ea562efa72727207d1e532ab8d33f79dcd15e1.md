# Combined-row support of the complement-LCU projector

**Result.** For the legal augmented BMVZ family with the two repeated BJSW
singleton seeds, the *combined* canonical projector
`C0 = I - VV*` has a row with at least

\[
  \left\lceil\frac{5^R}{D}\right\rceil
\]

nonzeros, where `R` is the BMVZ repetition count and `D=M+1` is the LCU label
dimension (the `M` positive terms plus the identity label). This holds for
every exact label-preparation unitary `A`, so it survives cancellations in
the complete formula (1). Since `D=poly(n)` and `R` grows as a positive
polynomial in the BMVZ input parameter, this is superpolynomial in the
instance size. It is a lower bound for this canonical projector, **not** for
every positive exact enforcer with the same kernel.

## Exact relative-reflection row

Use the actual added five-qubit seed vectors

\[
 \phi=(|00010\rangle-|10010\rangle)/\sqrt2,\qquad
 \psi=(|00010\rangle-|01110\rangle-|10110\rangle-|11010\rangle)/2.
\]

Write `z=00010`, `u=10010`, and let `v1,v2,v3` be the other three basis
strings in the support of `psi`. Their singleton layers have weight `1/4`
each; BMVZ's small-layer repetition rule gives, in the actual output,

\[
 Q_\phi=(I-|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|)^{\otimes R},\quad
 Q_\psi=(I-|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)^{\otimes R},\quad
 R_\phi=2Q_\phi-I,\quad R_\psi=2Q_\psi-I.
\]

Take the source data row to have `z` in all `R` seed blocks and any fixed
computational-basis string on the original and dummy registers. The pair
`R_phi R_psi` acts as identity on those other registers. On one seed block,
the row of `Q_phi Q_psi` indexed by `z`, restricted to
`{z,u,v1,v2,v3}`, is exactly

\[
 (3/8,\;1/2,\;1/8,\;1/8,\;1/8),
\]

while the corresponding rows of `Q_phi` and `Q_psi` are respectively
`(1/2,1/2,0,0,0)` and `(3/4,0,1/4,1/4,1/4)`. Every entry outside these five
columns is zero. These values follow by multiplying the two rational
projectors `I-|phi><phi|` and `I-|psi><psi|`; thus they require no floating
point approximation.

For a repeated-block column string `y`, let `a,b,c` count its symbols from
`{z}`, `{u}`, and `{v1,v2,v3}`, respectively, so `a+b+c=R`. The exact entry
of the relative reflection row is

\[
 (R_\phi R_\psi)_{z^{\otimes R},y}
 =4(3/8)^a(1/2)^b(1/8)^c
 -2\,\mathbf1_{c=0}(1/2)^{a+b}
 -2\,\mathbf1_{b=0}(3/4)^a(1/4)^c
 +\mathbf1_{b=c=0}.
\tag{4}
\]

Equation (4) uses the full product identity
`R_phi R_psi = 4 Q_phi Q_psi - 2 Q_phi - 2 Q_psi + I`; it is not a
pre-cancellation union estimate. If `b,c>0`, only the first term remains
and is nonzero. If `b>0,c=0`, cancellation would require
`2(3/4)^a=1`, which has no integer solution. If `b=0,c>0`, cancellation
would require `2^(1-R)=1`, impossible for `R>=2`. For `b=c=0`, the entry is
`1+4(3/8)^R-2(1/2)^R-2(3/4)^R`: it is `-1/16` at `R=2`, positive at
`R=3`, and positive for `R>=4` since
`2(3/4)^R+2(1/2)^R < 1`. Hence every one of the `5^R` columns in this local
alphabet is nonzero for `R>=2`; columns outside it vanish. The selected row
of `R_phi R_psi` has **exactly `5^R` nonzeros**.

An exact rational enumeration of (4) for `R=1,...,6` returned supports
`1,25,125,625,3125,15625`. The `R=1` cancellation is a useful warning against
using the tensor-factor support union; the exact full-row statement starts
at `R=2`.

## Extracting this pair from the combined `C0` row entries

Let `A` be the unitary label preparation in the interface note and let
`chi_j` be its LCU amplitudes. For distinct term labels `j,k`, define the
bilinear transform of the *actual combined entries* by

\[
 T_{j,k}(x,y)=\sum_{a,b=0}^{M}
 A_{ja}\,\overline{A_{kb}}\,(C_0)_{(a,x),(b,y)}.
\]

Substitute equation (1) from `00_INTERFACE_AND_TENSOR_FORMULA.md`. The identity
term vanishes because `sum_a A_{ja}\overline{A_{ka}}=0`. Unitarity also gives
`sum_a A_{ja}\overline{A_{pa}}=delta_{jp}` and
`sum_b \overline{A_{kb}}A_{qb}=delta_{kq}`. Therefore, exactly,

\[
 T_{j,k}(x,y)=-\chi_j\overline{\chi_k}(R_jR_k^*)_{x,y}.
\tag{5}
\]

Take `j=phi`, `k=psi`. Both amplitudes are nonzero (`|chi_phi|=|chi_psi|`
`=1/(2 sqrt(2))`), and the data row selected above gives `5^R` distinct
values of `y` for which (5) is nonzero. Thus for every such `y`, at least one
physical entry `(C0)_(a,x),(b,y)` is nonzero. If `s` is the maximum number of
nonzeros in a physical row of `C0`, the `D` source-label rows `(a,x)` together
contain at most `D s` nonzero physical entries. They must cover all `5^R`
distinct output data strings, so

\[
             s\ge \left\lceil 5^R/D\right\rceil.
\tag{6}
\]

This is a full-list argument: all other BMVZ clauses and any dummy terms are
present in (1), but label orthogonality isolates the selected seed-pair
coefficient after they have been combined. No assumption on their supports,
commutation, or signs is used. It also does not depend on choosing a favorable
completion of the first column of `A`.

The BMVZ source audit at
`outputs/research/games_hamiltonians/cycle2/bmvz_bjsw_source_audit.md`
establishes the legal augmentation, the positive singleton weights, and the
exact repeated clauses. Its source interface is BMVZ Definitions 1.6--1.9
and Theorem 7.3 / Theorem 7.4 in [arXiv:2510.01333v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01333v1).
The result is for that deliberately augmented hard family; it makes no claim
that this exact seed pair occurs in an unmodified canonical BMVZ reduction
input.

## What this does not prove about arbitrary enforcers

For an arbitrary positive `C` with `ker(C)=ran(V)`, its rows are image-orthogonal
relations, but they need not equal rows of `I-VV*` or obey (1). In fact, this
image has a nonzero relation of support at most `2D`. In the branch
coordinates before the final label rotation, choose every seed block to be a
computational basis string outside the five seed strings, for example `11111`.
Both seed reflections fix this data vector. The two branch frame vectors
`chi_phi R_phi|x>` and `chi_psi R_psi|x>` are then proportional, giving a
two-entry kernel relation. Rotating back by `A*` spreads it to at most `2D`
physical entries. Thus a proof that tries to show every nonzero kernel vector
has superpolynomial support cannot work for this image. The sparse relation
does not imply that any particular enforcer has a row of that form, and it
does not give a row-sparse positive enforcer with a constant gap.

**Status:** PROVED for the exact combined-row lower bound (6) on the canonical
projector `C0`; UNKNOWN whether the same legal image admits some other
polynomial-row positive enforcer with exact kernel and a size-independent
gap. A resolution of that remaining question must use the full frame's
dependency geometry and the gap requirement, not the minimum weight of a
single dependency. The entry oracle for (1) remains polynomial, but the row
list for `C0` is necessarily superpolynomial by (6), so specified-entry
access is not sparse-row access.
