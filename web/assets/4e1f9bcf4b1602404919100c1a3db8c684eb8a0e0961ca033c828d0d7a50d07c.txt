PRIOR ART AND COMPARISON

The most consequential new source is Ruelle 1982, “Large Volume Limit of the Distribution of Characteristic Exponents in Turbulence,” primary PDF https://www.ihes.fr/~ruelle/PUBLICATIONS/%5B73%5D.pdf. Theorem 2.1 and Corollary 2.2 establish Navier–Stokes state-space positive-exponent/KS-entropy bounds controlled by viscous dissipation; in 2D the information-production rate is bounded by a fixed multiple of dissipation at fixed viscosity. Ruelle 1984, https://repo-archives.ihes.fr/FONDS_IHES/I_Prepublications/RUELLE/1977-1990/P_84_01/P_84_01_web.pdf, extends related estimates to time-dependent forcing. These make the broad “fluid entropy versus dissipation” theme established prior art. Their entropy is that of the velocity-field PDE dynamics (a state-space system on divergence-free vector fields), while H here is the finite-dimensional particle-map entropy for a supplied steady velocity. Their constants depend on dimension, viscosity, domain/volume and spectral estimates; they are not the sharp 4νV coefficient. A steady PDE state can have zero state-space entropy while its particle flow has positive entropy.

The primary Ruelle inequality is exact and hypothesis-matched: https://www.ihes.fr/~ruelle/PUBLICATIONS/%5B51%5D.pdf, Theorem 2(b), C¹ map on compact manifold, any invariant probability, no ergodicity assumption. Pesin 1977 primary text https://www.mathnet.ru/php/getFT.phtml?jrnid=rm&option_lang=eng&paperid=3219&what=fullteng assumes a C² measure-preserving diffeomorphism in a smooth measure equivalent to Riemannian volume for its entropy formula. It applies to the smooth nowhere-zero candidate, but should not be casually applied to μ restricted to a positive-volume zero set.

Hydrodynamic “entropy” must be separated by object. The 2010 controlled vortex-array paper calls a thermodynamic global quantity “hydrodynamic entropy”: APS abstract https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.81.066302. The 3D turbulence preprint by Biswas–Manoharan–Joy, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20973v3, defines a practical “topological entropy” through material-line length growth and uses log-normal/CLT, decorrelation, independence and stationary/ergodic sampling steps. Its numerical 3D Navier–Stokes comparison is useful nearby prior art, not a rigorous volume-KS-versus-power theorem. For a general volume-preserving 3D map, material-line stretching and metric entropy are not interchangeable: Pesin entropy sums all positive exponents, while line stretching probes a top stretching direction. They coincide in the candidate autonomous 3D case because the neutral flow direction and volume preservation force χ,0,−χ.
