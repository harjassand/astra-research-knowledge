# Immunology discrimination lead: adaptive B-cell mechanical proofreading

## Verdict

I do not find a defensible new immune-sensing principle in this lead. The high-ceiling candidate, adaptive mechanical proofreading based on the running maximum size of a local BCR–antigen cluster, is already a peer-reviewed 2026 *PRX Life* paper. I reconstructed its single-cluster model, proved one exact hard-threshold fact, and attacked the paper's local dose-sensitivity story with global dose-distribution and matched-dose comparisons. The resulting evidence is useful as a negative/qualification package, not as a new biological discovery.

The mechanism's operational benefit is narrower than universal dose invariance: in the published model it can reduce nuisance-dose misranking when affinity cases draw different antigen quantities, but the effect depends on the dose prior and is reversed when high- and low-affinity cases are compared at the same antigen quantity. Prior-specific thresholds, longer contacts, and right censoring matter. The source's mobility experiments independently show that membrane mobility shifts the timing and spatial pattern of force-mediated capture, a major omitted covariate for any dose-only controller.

## Why this was the candidate

Xu, Jiang, and Wang proposed adaptive force generation `F(mmax)` in which a B cell's force rises with its largest observed local BCR–Ag–tether cluster. The authors model force-sensitive Bell dissociation at the tether and BCR interfaces, antigen extraction/rebinding, and termination when the cluster disappears; their paper compares it with a prescribed time-ramp `F(t)`. The primary article, supplement, and authors' source scripts are preserved and indexed in [SOURCES.md](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c7_immunology_discrimination/source_notes/SOURCES.md).

In the one-cluster model, states are extracted antigens `n`, tethered BCR–Ag–tether complexes `m`, and an initial finite ligand count `L`. Association from the free or rebindable pool raises `m` at total rate `(L-m)kon`; tether rupture extracts an antigen, while BCR rupture loses the tethered complex; both decrease `m`. Force raises both Bell off-rates according to the per-bond load `F/m`. This is a clean model of an information bottleneck—how to compare affinity while antigen amount varies—but it assumes one uniform localized cluster, homogeneous binding/tether parameters, a common association rate, and a prescribed controller. It has no explicit antigen diffusion/mobility, multiple spatial clusters, measured force-generation cost, or biological controller state.

## Exact but limited result

In the ideal `β=∞` step-force limit, before force onset the marginal `m` process is an exact birth–death chain: `λ_m=(L−m)kon`, because new binding and rebinding together exhaust the same `L−m` pool, and `μ_m=m(ka0+kb0)`. The gambler's-ruin recurrence gives

`P_1(hit mc before 0) = [1 + Σ(j=1..mc−1) Π(r=1..j) μ_r/λ_r]⁻¹` for `L≥mc`, and zero for `L<mc`.

At the paper's `mc=60` rates this is 0 at `L=59` and .998068 at `L=60`. The proof and numerical evaluation are in [finite_dose_threshold.md](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c7_immunology_discrimination/proof/finite_dose_threshold.md). This is only a threshold-reachability statement in the hard-step idealization. It does not prove score robustness. At the paper's actual `β=5`, the force ramp is soft and nonzero below the threshold; for `L≤30`, the maximum force is bounded by about 24.2 pN at `mc=60`, but no state is an exact no-force state.

## Local sensitivity versus global performance

The source-style local sensitivity probe at `L=100`, `ΔL=24`, and `ΔEb=.5 kBT` was rerun with 5000 trajectories per condition. For `F(mmax)` (`mc=60`), normalized affinity sensitivity `αE=2.771`, whereas for `F(t)` (`tc=60`) it is 3.516. Dose sensitivity per ligand is .00878 versus .08784; across 24 ligands the dose score shift is .211 versus 2.108. Thus the affinity-to-dose sensitivity ratio is about 7.9× more favorable for the running-maximum policy. That advantage is partly due to reduced response to *both* signals: local affinity sensitivity is lower too. Full output: [local_vs_global_results.json](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c7_immunology_discrimination/code/local_vs_global_results.json).

I then measured the source's global rank metric across three dose priors, choosing `mc` from a seven-value scan and giving the time-only controller a stronger prior-aware 5×7 `F0 × tc` grid. Screen candidates were validated on fresh streams. For independently redrawn low/high affinity doses, the best validated time-only AUCs were .675 for `Normal(100,30)`, .598 for uniform 5–195, and .607 for uniform 5–60. The published adaptive force schedule (`F0=800 pN`) gave .767, .679, and .625 with prior-scan thresholds 60, 50, and 30, respectively. The first two adaptive gains persist despite the stronger time-only baseline and longer time-only schedules; the low-dose CIs overlap and its adaptive censor-completion envelope [.542,.761] crosses the time result. About 16–21% of adaptive low-dose trajectories hit the 1800 s cutoff. The exact validation results are in [time_policy_sweep_results.json](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c7_immunology_discrimination/code/time_policy_sweep_results.json).

The task changes when the dose is shared within an affinity pair. With matched `L`, time-only control had higher AUC in all three priors: .876 vs .806 for `Normal(100,30)`, .860 vs .735 for uniform 5–195, and .730 vs .660 for uniform 5–60. There was no time-policy censoring in these runs. The model thus supports a narrower interpretation: `F(mmax)` can protect *between-encounter ranking* when each receptor sees a different hidden dose, but it is not a generally more selective affinity comparator at a common dose. This does not refute the paper's defined independent-dose task; it shows why local `αL` alone is insufficient and why the experimental comparison must declare which nuisance structure it targets.

These outcomes are finite computations, not globally optimized controllers. The time-only schedule search covers 35 combinations and independently validates its top two; adaptive `mc` was selected on a seven-value scan but retained the source `F0=800 pN` rather than being jointly tuned over force ceiling and threshold. Adaptive duration is about 116 s vs 223 s for the AUC-leading time schedule under the source-normal prior, 328 s vs 459 s over uniform 5–195, and 440 s vs 363 s over uniform 5–60. The simulator's `Σ(F/m)x` Bell-barrier proxy is lower in the adaptive runs, but it omits displacement and is not physical work or ATP. A fair engineering claim requires force–displacement and contact-time matching.

## Model–nature boundary

McArthur et al. measured B-cell extraction on mobile DOPC and less-mobile DPPC bilayers using DNA-based tension sensors and live imaging. Mobility changed the magnitude, location, and timing of force, extraction probability/speed, and affinity discrimination. Extraction began around 8 s on DPPC and 200 s on DOPC; mean time to the first internalized cluster was 74 vs 421 s; total antigen acquired could become similar by 45 minutes because mobile DOPC permits recruitment of a larger accessible antigen pool. Those experiments make mobility a concrete covariate and endpoint confound. They do not show that force onset tracks `mmax` or validate the adaptive controller.

## Next decisive experiment

The proposed experiment is a factorial B-cell/BCR-affinity × ligand-density × measured-mobility assay, holding antigen valency, tether sequence/strength, and BCR density fixed or measured. Time-resolved imaging should jointly observe local cluster maxima, DNA tension-sensor output, extraction events, contact lifetime, total antigen uptake, and delivery to MHC-II compartments. Train the `F(mmax)` policy and a prior-aware optimized `F(t)` control on a subset of densities; lock both before evaluation on held-out low/high densities and mobility conditions. Compare both independently varying and matched-dose affinity pairs. Match measured force-displacement work and contact duration rather than the Bell-barrier proxy.

The candidate passes only if measured force onset tracks cluster maximum after conditioning on dose and mobility, and the locked controller beats the equally informed time-only policy on held-out density at the same duration/true-work budget with low censoring. The experiment and explicit failure gates are in [EXPERIMENT.md](/Users/harjas/Documents/Codex/2026-10-08/use-the-astra-research-knowledge-repository-2/work/agents/c7_immunology_discrimination/restart_gates/EXPERIMENT.md).

## Recommendation to the root effort

Treat adaptive mechanical proofreading as prior art and this package as a careful model audit. Its strongest transferable insight is experimental: independently varying antigen dose and presenting-membrane mobility can make endpoint antigen uptake a misleading proxy for affinity-selective sensing. Do not elevate the exact step-gate lemma or Monte Carlo ranking results into a natural law or a new immune capability. If a new-systems capability is still required, the useful unresolved target is a spatial, mobility-aware controller with measured force and time budgets, then a held-out test against the strongest dose-aware time schedule.
