## 1. Optimized arithmetic supply lemma

For real u >= 2 define

`ell(u) = ceil((5/6) log u / log 2)+1`,

and let P(u) be the product of the first ell(u) rational primes. There exists an absolute u_0 such that every odd integer `u >= u_0` has a representation as a sum of at most nine positive rationals `e_i/t_i`, where every `e_i | P(u)^2`, every `t_i` is a positive integer, and every `t_i <= 3 P(u)^2`.

Proof. Let A be the positive divisors of P(u), with `N = |A| = 2^ell(u) >= 2u^(5/6)`. For each prime p <= u let H_p be the reduction of A modulo p and call p bad if `|H_p| <= p^(5/6)`.

For any bad p, Cauchy-Schwarz applied to the residue fibres gives at least `N^2/|H_p| - N` ordered pairs of distinct members of A with difference divisible by p. Because `N >= 2u^(5/6)`, this number is at least `N^2/(2u^(5/6))`. Each nonzero difference has absolute value below P(u), hence at most `log P(u)/log 2` distinct rational prime factors. Counting pair-prime incidences yields

`#bad primes <= 2u^(5/6) log P(u)/log 2 = O(u^(5/6) log u log log u)`.

Quantitative Vinogradov gives at least `c_0 u^2/(log u)^3` ordered prime triples summing to each sufficiently large odd u, with absolute c_0 > 0. This follows from the standard asymptotic and a uniform positive lower bound for the odd singular series. At most `3u * #bad primes` such triples contain a bad prime: choose its position and value, then the next prime has at most u choices and the last is determined. The exponent 5/6<1 makes this upper bound `o(u^2/(log u)^3)`. Thus there is a triple `u = p_1+p_2+p_3` in which each prime is good for the same A.

For one good prime p, write H=H_p and h=|H| > p^(5/6). Let U=AB with A and B independent uniform members of H, and let `e_p(x)=exp(2 pi i x/p)`. For nonzero t modulo p, additive orthogonality gives

`|sum_{a,b in H} e_p(tab)|^2 <= h sum_{a in F_p} |sum_{b in H} e_p(tab)|^2 = p h^2`.

Consequently `|E e_p(tU)| <= sqrt(p)/h < p^(-1/3)`. Three independent copies U_1,U_2,U_3 therefore satisfy

`Pr(U_1+U_2+U_3=0) >= [1-(p-1)/p]/p > 0`.

The strict positive margin comes from p-1 rather than p nonzero frequencies, so the endpoint exponent works. Lift the six witnessing residues to divisors of P(u). The three resulting products e_1,e_2,e_3 are positive divisors of P(u)^2 and their sum is divisible by p. Set `t=(e_1+e_2+e_3)/p`. Then t is a positive integer, `t <= 3 P(u)^2`, and `p=e_1/t+e_2/t+e_3/t`. Do this independently for each of the three good primes. Nine terms give u. Zero residues cause no problem: the lifted integer products are still positive. Repetitions among witnesses and denominators are allowed at this stage.

Quantitative Vinogradov was checked at Theorem 1, printed p.3, equations (1.3)-(1.4), of A. V. Kumchev and D. I. Tolev, *An invitation to additive prime number theory* (2005), author-hosted at https://tigerweb.towson.edu/akumchev/a17.pdf. We use it as prior mathematics, not as a theorem proved by this investigation. Only that excerpt, rather than the entire survey, was read.

## 2. A simultaneous supply with controlled auxiliary integer

Fix a positive integer r. It will be chosen large once the desired C is specified, and then held fixed as b tends to infinity. Put

`L = log log b`, `Y = b^(r+1)`,

`K = P(Y)^2 * floor(L)!`.

For sufficiently large b, every odd integer `1 <= s <= Y` is a sum of at most nine positive rational divisors `e/t`, with `e | K` and `t <= 3K`. Every integer `1 <= s <= Y` has a representation of the same kind with at most ten terms.

For large odd s use Section 1 and the monotonicity of ell(s). For bounded initial s, eventually s divides floor(L)! and the one-term representation s=s/1 works. For a large even s, apply the odd supply to s-1 and append 1=1/1. This covers the entire interval simultaneously; its threshold may depend on r.

Here is a self-contained primorial estimate, supplied independently by frontier_geometry2 and reconstructed again here. The central binomial coefficient satisfies `binom(2m,m)>=4^m/(2m+1)`. For each prime p, its exponent in that coefficient is <=floor(log(2m)/log p), since the summands in Legendre's factorial valuation difference are zero or one. Therefore `log binom(2m,m)<=pi(2m)log(2m)`. This implies `pi(x)>=c_1 x/log x` for some absolute c_1>0 at all sufficiently large x. Inverting gives `p_j<=C_1 j log(2j)`, after enlarging C_1 to cover bounded j. Also `p_j>=j+1`. Summing logarithms between these bounds gives

`log(product_(j<=ell) p_j) = ell log ell + O(ell log log(2ell))`.

Thus `log P(Y) = (5/(6log 2)+o(1)) log Y log log Y`, without a separate PNT input. The factorial contributes only `O(L log L)`. Hence

`log K = (5(r+1)/(3log 2)+o(1)) log b L`.

In particular K > b for all sufficiently large b. No primality or coprimality restriction on K is imposed.

## 3. Prepare an unreduced greedy denominator

Starting from (R,q)=(a,b), use ordinary greedy steps until the remainder is zero or q >= T=bK:

`n=ceil(q/R)`, `(R,q) <- (nR-q,nq)`.

Keep each pair unreduced. In every active step n >= 2, n <= q, and `0 <= R_new < R`. At a positive stop,

`0 < R < b`, `bK <= q < (bK)^2`.

The number j of prefix terms is at most `1+ceil(log_2(log T/log 2))`, so

`j <= (1/log 2 + o(1)) L`.

For completeness, a positive greedy remainder x changes to a value at most x^2/(1+x); the first positive remainder is below 1/2. Together with the positive integer numerator, this gives the displayed crossing bound. Each remainder is smaller than the last reciprocal subtracted, so the prefix denominators are distinct and the final R/q is smaller than every prefix reciprocal.

## 4. Divisor density and odd grouping

The following elementary invariant holds: if q is obtained from b by multipliers `n <= q_old+1`, then for every real `1 <= w <= q` there is a divisor d of q in `[w/b,w]`. Initially d=1 works. In a step q_new=n q_old, use the old invariant if w <= q_old; if w >= n, use n times a divisor selected at w/n. The only remaining possibility is `q_old < w < n`; then q_old is a divisor and `w < n <= q_old+1 <= b q_old`. These cases cover all w. They do not require gcd(n,q_old)=1.

The greedy q has this invariant. Let `X=RK`. By the stop rule `0 < X < bK <= q`. While X > Y choose d | q in

`[X/b^(r+1), X/b^r]`,

by applying density at `w=X/b^r` (which lies in [1,q]). Let s be the largest odd positive integer at most X/d. For sufficiently large b this is positive, and

`b^r-2 < s <= Y`,

`X_new = X-ds` is an integer with `0 <= X_new < 2d <= 2X/b^r`.

Thus every nonfinal grouping step shrinks X by factor at most 2/b^r, and uses an odd supply coefficient. If a positive final X <= Y remains, use d=1 and coefficient s=X. There are at most

`G <= log X/(r log b-log 2)+1`

nonfinal groups. This bound deliberately ignores the earlier stop at Y; the ignored improvement affects only a bounded number of groups at fixed r. A zero remainder needs no final group.

For each group, supply `s=sum e_i/t_i`, where e_i | K. Its contribution converts exactly to unit fractions:

`ds/(qK) = sum_i 1/[(q/d)(K/e_i)t_i]`.

Both q/d and K/e_i are integers; t_i need not divide either q or K. Every nonfinal group uses at most nine terms and the final group at most ten. Consequently the tail length is <=9G+10, uniformly in a.

## 5. Coefficient and all quantifiers

Using `X < bK` and Section 2 gives

`j+9G+10 <= [(16+15/r)/log 2 + o(1)] L`.

Given C > 16/log 2, first choose a fixed sufficiently large integer r so that

`(16+15/r)/log 2 < C`.

Finally let b exceed the thresholds in Sections 1-4 and the asymptotic threshold for the strict remaining gap. This order of choices is essential: r is fixed before b grows. This proves the length bound with repeated denominators. If the greedy prefix terminates earlier, its own shorter expansion already satisfies the bound.

## 6. Remove repeats without increasing length

Any positive k-term unit-fraction expansion of a fixed x in (0,1) can be made distinct with exactly k terms by Takenouchi's classical replacements:

`2/(2p) = 1/(p+1) + 1/[p(p+1)]`,

`2/(2p+1) = 1/(p+1) + 1/[(p+1)(2p+1)]`.

A repeated denominator 2 and denominator 1 are impossible since x<1. Every allowed replacement increases the sum of denominators strictly, by (p-1)^2 or 2p^2. There are finitely many nondecreasing k-tuples with sum x: the first denominator is <=k/x, and after selecting it induction bounds the remaining positive sum with k-1 terms. Thus replacement must terminate at distinct denominators.

Normalize the tail alone. Its sum R/q is less than each prefix reciprocal, and every positive reciprocal in any representation of that tail is <=R/q. Hence normalized tail denominators cannot collide with prefix denominators. No extra term is used. This proves the stated distinct version if the previous candidate steps survive independent audit.

## 7. Complete size and acquisition interface

Before removing repeats, every tail denominator is at most

`3qK^2 < 3b^2 K^4`.

Prefix denominators are below T=bK. Thus the repeated representation has O(log log b) terms, maximum denominator `exp(O(log b log log b))`, and total bit volume `O(log b (log log b)^2)`, with constants depending on C. The sharper maximum-denominator estimate has not been proved to survive normalization.

Nevertheless any sorted positive k-term expansion of a/b obeys `n_i <= (kb)^(2^(i-1))`: after i-1 terms its positive remainder is at least `1/(b product_{h<i} n_h)`, and at most k/n_i. Multiplying inductively yields the bound. Therefore the sum of denominator bit lengths is at most `(2^k-1) log_2(kb)+k`. With the above coefficient, this is `O((log b)^(17+delta))` for every fixed delta>0 after adjusting C and the threshold. This is a polynomial certificate-size existence bound; its exponent is large.

Acquisition has not been made polynomial in input bit length. A direct exhaustive implementation enumerates `2^ell(Y)`, of order `b^(5(r+1)/6)`, primorial divisors, plus prime triples up to Y and residue witnesses. These searches are exponential in log b. Vinogradov and the elementary estimates imply eventual thresholds but this note gives no usable numerical b_0(C). Distinctness normalization terminates, yet its runtime may also be large. These are limitations of this construction, not lower bounds against every possible algorithm.

The bottleneck is specifically supply acquisition and cleanup. An exactly computable cutoff variant makes the auxiliary integer polynomial to form in log b for fixed r: replace floor(L)! by `M!`, where `M=floor(log_2 floor(log_2 b))`, obtained from integer bit lengths. M tends to infinity and is O(L), the only factorial properties used here. The primorial index can be obtained exactly by finding the least integer h with `2^(6h)>=Y^5`, then taking ell=h+1. Only O(log b) small primes are multiplied. This avoids an unstated exact real-logarithm oracle. Divisor selection also need not factor q: follow its recorded multiplier history recursively through the three cases in Section 4, with depth <=j. Once supply witnesses are available, greedy preparation, odd grouping, and reciprocal conversion require only polynomial bit arithmetic. The cutoff refinement was independently supplied by frontier_geometry2.

## Prior art, downstream use, and next falsifiable steps

The primary older comparator checked is Tenenbaum-Yokota (1990), *Length and Denominators of Egyptian Fractions, III*, theorem on printed p.151: it gives length `(1+epsilon) log b/log log b` with maximum denominator `4b(log b)^2 log log b`. It already gives polynomial-size distinct certificates and much smaller denominators, using more terms. Our shortest-length route and its acquisition cost therefore serve a different interface; it should not replace that result in denominator-sensitive applications.

For length alone, the nearer comparator is Vose's O(sqrt(log b)) uniform construction. Original Vose (1985) full text was inaccessible through the publisher redirect; no original-proof reading is claimed. Its precise distinct-divisor statement was read in van Doorn-Tang (24 May 2026 revision), Lemma 2.2, printed pp.3-4: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.22083v2. That recent paper also proves an exp(c k^2) lower bound for the smallest denominator missing from exact k-term decompositions of one. The common auxiliary divisor method is therefore established prior art, while external priority for our parameter optimization remains open.

The reusable mechanism is: a simultaneous positive rational-divisor supply under K, an unreduced greedy denominator with multiplicative divisor density, and odd coefficient grouping. It could support exact probability or combinatorial constructions where the number of reciprocal pieces is costly. Any such application must charge denominator sizes and supply acquisition.

## 9. Why three products is the best choice in this size-based family

For an integer k>=3, the same argument permits k bilinear products with image threshold `alpha=1/2+1/k`, taking `ell(u)=ceil(alpha log u/log 2)+1`. The Fourier error is <=(p-1)/p at that endpoint, while alpha<1 still makes the bad-prime count negligible compared with quantitative Vinogradov's prime triples. Odd integers then need at most 3k rational-divisor terms. At fixed grouping r, the resulting leading length coefficient is

`[1+6k alpha(r+1)/r]/log 2 = [7+3k+(6+3k)/r]/log 2`.

This increases with k, so k=3 gives the smallest coefficient in this particular size-based Fourier plus three-prime construction. For k=2 its nominal threshold is alpha=1; no image can have h>p, and the bad-prime counting comparison no longer gives a negligible exceptional set. The separate F_127 example also excludes a two-product lemma merely assuming h>p^(5/6). These facts do not prove a universal lower bound of 16/log 2; they identify what a better construction must change.

The classical worst-case lower argument gives the remaining gap explicitly. Append 1/b to a k-term expansion of (b-1)/b, and let s=k+1 be the new length. Sorted positive denominators for a sum of one obey `d_j<=s^(2^(j-1))`, so `b<=s^(2^(s-1))=(k+1)^(2^k)`. Hence `log log b<=k log 2+log log(k+1)`. Together with any O(log log b) upper bound, this implies `N(b)>=(1/log 2-o(1))log log b` for the worst-case minimum length N(b). Our current coefficient leaves a factor16 gap; no optimal constant has been determined.
