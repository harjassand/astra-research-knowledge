# Cycle 2: audit of the September 2026 aging-clock preprint

Audit date: 2026-10-08. The current version is Tanigawa & Iwaki, v2 posted 2026-09-18, *A generator-matrix model quantifies the limited contribution of measured biomarkers to human mortality acceleration*, medRxiv DOI [10.64898/2026.07.05.26356402](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.05.26356402). It remains a preprint. Its September version supersedes the July version and changes the title, sample, methods disclosure, and clock-reversal statistics. The code archive is version 1.2.0, Zenodo DOI [10.5281/zenodo.22825428](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22825428).

## Reconstructed mortality model

The headline NHANES fit is not a fitted person-level CTMC/phase-type first-passage likelihood. The v2 Supplementary Methods describe the primary estimator as a joint cross-sectional biomarker regression plus proportional-hazard Gompertz survival likelihood, sharing a per-axis slope parameter. The model is

\[
 z_{ij}\mid a_i \sim N\!\left(b_j+\lambda_j(a_i-a_c),\sigma_j^2\right),\qquad
 \gamma=\gamma_{base}+\sum_j\beta_j\lambda_j,\qquad
 f_{latent}=\frac{\gamma_{base}}{\gamma_{base}+\sum_j\beta_j\lambda_j}.
\]

Here `lambda_j` is estimated from the cross-sectional age gradient in one-time measured axes and then reused as each person's within-follow-up biomarker drift; `beta_j` is the conditional association with mortality. The `gamma_base` term is the residual Gompertz acceleration after imposing that shared-drift structure. The 1.0.0 archive inspected locally implements this structure: `bayes_unified_generator.py` has an observed biomarker likelihood `z ~ Normal(b + lambda*(age-60), sigma)` and an Aalen/Poisson-process survival contribution with the same `lambda` entering follow-up hazard acceleration. This agrees with the v2 methods description.

The model reports NHANES `n=23,512`, 4,829 deaths, latent fraction 92.7% [92.2, 93.2]; HRS 91.5% [89.7, 93.3]; and an alternative discretized mean-field re-specification 89.1% [88.0, 90.0]. The latter is explicitly not an independent person-level phase-type likelihood. Its state propagation is a point-estimate consistency check, and the depicted repair transitions are not in that script. The full person-level phase-type likelihood was not computationally tractable. Thus the title and schematic describe a mechanistic interpretation broader than the primary fitted likelihood.

Priors in the v2 supplement are `beta_j ~ Normal(0, 0.5)`, `lambda_j ~ Normal(0, 0.03)`, `sigma_j ~ HalfNormal(1)`, `log(lambda0) ~ Normal(log(10^-3), 2)`, and `gamma_base ~ Normal(0.08, 0.03)`, with the last prior anchored to an AnAge cross-species relation. The supplement says the latent fraction is robust to that prior centre and presents model-generated parameter recovery across true shares 60–95% (40 simulated datasets; absolute bias at most 1.3 percentage points; mean bootstrap 95% coverage 0.93). This is calibration under the assumed model, not a test that its cross-sectional slopes equal within-person drift in humans. The paper itself identifies selective survival, unmodelled assay error, cohort effects, frailty and proportional-Gompertz misspecification as limitations. It also reports age-band residual misfit and calls aggregate expected-count agreement weak evidence because a fitted baseline nearly forces it.

The Lean 4 theorems in the supplement prove algebraic properties of `a/(a+b)` under sign assumptions (bounds, monotonicity, and limits). They do not prove that empirical `gamma_base` is damage, that the decomposition is causal, or that a posterior is identified from the actual data. The simulation-based recovery tests numerical behavior within the proposed generative family; the paper explicitly leaves full Bayesian simulation-based calibration for later.

## Two-clock intervention reconstruction

The v2 version corrects the earlier analysis for repeated samples nested within donors. It calculates within-donor treatment-control clock differences, averages over days/experiments within donor, then uses one-sample t intervals/tests over donors. The exposure is in-vitro reprogramming of human fibroblasts, not an in-vivo intervention and not a functional regeneration endpoint.

| Dataset/protocol | Independent donors | Horvath difference (years) | DamAge difference (clock units) | Interpretation bounded by design |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GSE165179, transient maturation-phase reprogramming (MPTR) | 3; 13 matched donor × day × experiment pairs | −9.7 [−18.1, −1.3], p=.038 | +13.1 [−18.0, +44.2], p=.21 | DamAge interval admits a sizeable decrease; no equivalence margin was set. |
| GSE165178, Sendai protocol, SSEA4+ vs CD13+ | 4; 11 matched donor × day pairs | −22.4 [−28.0, −16.7], p=.001 | −2.6 [−7.2, +2.1], p=.18 | No detectable average DamAge change; low donor count does not establish resistance. |
| Sendai day 9 / 11, partial window | 4 | −9.8 (p=.16) / −6.2 (p=.45) | +16.6 (p=.19) / +5.8 (p=.54) | Neither clock is resolved at these time points. |
| Sendai day 15, approaching full iPSC identity | 4 | −47.2 (p=.001) | −24.4 (p=.030) | Both clock scores decrease near identity loss; this contradicts universal DamAge irreversibility. |
| GSE142439, mRNA reprogramming, independent group | donor structure unresolved in public IDs | weak and normalization-sensitive | weak and normalization-sensitive | Authors report paired Horvath delta −3.40 years with original values vs −0.20 with their processing; underpowered. |

DamAge is intercept-free here, so its score differences are arbitrary clock units. The paper now states correctly that nonsignificance is not equivalence. It also says DamAge resistance could be a construction effect: CpGs selected by the causality-enriched clock may not overlap with reprogramming-responsive CpGs. The observed result is therefore a clock-score dissociation with substantial uncertainty, not a measurement of irreversible molecular damage or proof that the score represents the human mortality-model residual.

## Measurement and model-identification context

The original Horvath clock is a methylation-based predictor trained to estimate chronological age over many tissues/cell types; its source paper explicitly states that the biological meaning of the resulting age prediction was not established. Ying et al. construct DamAge from CpGs with putative causal links to aging outcomes using epigenome-wide Mendelian randomization; this enrichment is a design property, not a direct assay of DNA lesions or a calibrated cell-intrinsic damage state. Xiao et al.'s EpiTrace work provides an independent measurement example: chromatin-accessibility loci and methylation shifts can move on different time courses, and 5-azacytidine-induced global demethylation shifts DNAm age while accessibility-derived mitotic age behaves differently. These primary studies motivate a measurement model with direct intervention-to-assay effects and multiple readouts, not a single interchangeable `biological age` variable.

Phase-type mortality models have a separate known identifiability issue: different `(initial distribution, transient generator)` pairs can give the same survival distribution; for nonredundant representations, equivalent pairs are related by a similarity transformation preserving the all-ones vector. The 2023 phase-type identifiability paper states this explicitly and gives examples. A joint biomarker likelihood can identify a *restricted parameterization* conditional on its measurement and drift assumptions; it does not by itself identify the biological meaning of latent state coordinates.
