LEAF 13 — ONSAGER FLUX THRESHOLD
Retrieved and audited 2026-10-08 (Australia/Brisbane)

DECISION

For a distributional, divergence-free 3D Euler solution on the periodic torus, spatial filtering gives the exact resolved-energy balance

  ∂t (|ū_ℓ|²/2) + div[((|ū_ℓ|²/2+p̄_ℓ)ū_ℓ)+τ_ℓ ū_ℓ]
      = τ_ℓ : ∇ū_ℓ = -Π_ℓ,
  τ_ℓ = (u⊗u)̄_ℓ - ū_ℓ⊗ū_ℓ,   Π_ℓ := -τ_ℓ:∇ū_ℓ.

The commutator estimate is cubic in velocity increments and costs one inverse length:

  ∫_0^T∫_{T³} |Π_ℓ| dxdt ≤ C_{G,α} ℓ^{3α-1} ∫_0^T A_α(t)^3dt,

where A_α(t)=sup_{0<|r|≤1}|r|^{-α}||u(t,·+r)-u(t,·)||_3 and 0<α<1. Thus any weak solution with u∈L³_t B^α_{3,∞}, 1/3<α<1, has vanishing flux and conserves total kinetic energy; α≥1 follows by embedding into any α′∈(1/3,1). At the critical exponent, an O(ℓ^{1/3}) increment bound gives only O(1); a little-o increment modulus suffices. The strongest cited endpoint theorem is CCFS's R³ result for L³_t B^{1/3}_{3,c(N)}∩C_w,t L², where the high-frequency dyadic Besov coefficients tend to zero; the periodic analogue follows from the same dyadic estimate. The plain B^{1/3}_{3,∞} norm bound does not make this argument vanish.

The threshold in the estimate is sharp as a commutator scaling statement. An explicit divergence-free Fourier triad has a nonzero filtered flux, and the rescaled family u_N=N^{-α}v(Nx), filtered at ℓ=N^{-1}, has flux exactly proportional to N^{1-3α}. These fields are smooth test fields, not anomalous Euler solutions. Separately, Isett proves actual non-conservative weak Euler solutions for every α<1/3; the exact C^{1/3} endpoint remains open in the latest primary endpoint paper located in this pass. No claim about an endpoint dissipative solution follows from the scaling example.

1. SETUP AND QUANTIFIERS

Fix arbitrary T>0 and work on T³=(R/2πZ)³. Let (u,p) be a weak incompressible Euler solution with u∈C_w([0,T];L²(T³))∩L³((0,T)×T³), div u=0, and p∈L^{3/2}((0,T)×T³) (the mean-zero pressure is obtained from the Riesz transforms of u⊗u). The equations are

  ∂t u + div(u⊗u)+∇p=0,   div u=0.

Take any fixed G∈C_c^∞(R³), G≥0, G even, ∫G=1, and set G_ℓ(r)=ℓ^{-3}G(r/ℓ), ū_ℓ=G_ℓ*u and p̄_ℓ=G_ℓ*p, with periodic convolution. All assertions below hold for every such kernel; constants may depend on G and α, never on ℓ or u except through the displayed norms. Let R_G be such that supp G⊂B(0,R_G).

2. DERIVATION OF THE FILTERED BALANCE

Filtering the distributional Euler equation in space commutes with ∂t and ∇ and gives

  ∂t ū_ℓ + div(ū_ℓ⊗ū_ℓ)+∇p̄_ℓ = -div τ_ℓ.

Contract with ū_ℓ. Since div ū_ℓ=0,

  ū_ℓ·div(ū_ℓ⊗ū_ℓ)=div(|ū_ℓ|²ū_ℓ/2),
  ū_ℓ·∇p̄_ℓ=div(p̄_ℓū_ℓ),
  -ū_{ℓ,i}∂_jτ_{ℓ,ij}=-∂_j(ū_{ℓ,i}τ_{ℓ,ij})+τ_{ℓ,ij}∂_j ū_{ℓ,i}.

This proves the stated local balance in distributions (and as an L¹-in-time identity after spatial integration). In particular, for a.e. t,

  (1/2) d/dt ||ū_ℓ(t)||²_2 = ∫_{T³} τ_ℓ:∇ū_ℓ dx.

The resolved flux Π_ℓ is defined with the minus sign so positive Π means transfer out of resolved scales. With the Duchon–Robert convention below, the limiting defect D is +lim Π_ℓ and the unfiltered local energy equation is ∂t(|u|²/2)+div[u(|u|²/2+p)]+D=0. At finite ℓ, the Duchon–Robert cubic increment density and Π_ℓ need not be pointwise equal; they yield the same limiting distributional defect, differing at finite scale by divergence terms.

The exact stress increment identity, for δ_r u(x)=u(x+r)-u(x), is

  τ_ℓ = ∫G_ℓ(r) δ_{-r}u⊗δ_{-r}u dr - m_ℓ⊗m_ℓ,
  m_ℓ = ∫G_ℓ(r)δ_{-r}u dr.

This follows by expanding u(x-r)=u(x)+δ_{-r}u in (u⊗u)̄_ℓ and ū_ℓ⊗ū_ℓ. Also, because ∫∇G_ℓ=0,

  ∇ū_ℓ(x)=∫∇G_ℓ(r)δ_{-r}u(x)dr.

3. QUANTITATIVE INCREMENT ESTIMATE AND CONSERVATION

For 0<α<1 define kernel moments

  M_{G,s}=∫|G(z)||z|^s dz,   N_{G,α}=∫|∇G(z)||z|^α dz.

If A_α(t)<∞, Minkowski and Hölder give the explicit estimates

  ||∇ū_ℓ(t)||_3 ≤ N_{G,α} ℓ^{α-1} A_α(t),
  ||τ_ℓ(t)||_{3/2} ≤ [M_{G,2α}+M_{G,α}²] ℓ^{2α} A_α(t)².

Thus C_{G,α}=N_{G,α}[M_{G,2α}+M_{G,α}²] is one valid constant and

  ∫_0^T∫|τ_ℓ:∇ū_ℓ| ≤ C_{G,α}ℓ^{3α-1}||A_α||_{L³(0,T)}³.

On the torus, the spatial Besov increment characterization gives A_α(t)≲||u(t)||_{B^α_{3,∞}} for 0<α<1. For 1/3<α<1 this tends to zero; α≥1 follows by lowering the Besov exponent. More generally, if

  ω_3(ℓ):=sup_{|r|≤ℓ}||δ_r u||_{L³((0,T)×T³)}=O(ℓ^α),

then a compactly supported filter gives ∫|Π_ℓ|≤C_Gℓ^{-1}ω_3(R_Gℓ)^3=O(ℓ^{3α-1}); the same conclusion follows from ω_3(ℓ)=o(ℓ^{1/3}). At α=1/3, an O(ℓ^{1/3}) bound alone yields only O(1), not decay. For example, the logarithmic improvement ω_3(ℓ)≤Cℓ^{1/3}(log(e/ℓ))^{-β}, any β>0, makes this bound tend to zero at rate O((log(e/ℓ))^{-3β}).

To turn flux decay into all-time conservation, integrate the resolved balance between arbitrary s,t∈[0,T]. Then | ||ū_ℓ(t)||²_2-||ū_ℓ(s)||²_2 |≤2∫_s^t∫|Π_ℓ|. For each fixed time, ū_ℓ(t)→u(t) strongly in L² by approximate-identity convergence; weak continuity supplies the time representatives. Letting ℓ→0 proves ||u(t)||²_2=||u(s)||²_2 for every s,t. This is a qualitative result unless a scale modulus A_α or ω_3 is supplied; at the endpoint c₀ condition there is generally no uniform decay rate without its tail modulus.

4. DUCHON–ROBERT LOCAL DEFECT CRITERION

Duchon and Robert give a local version under the weaker basic assumption u∈L³((0,T)×T³). With the above smooth even nonnegative unit-mass mollifier, set

  D_ℓ^{DR}(u)(x,t)=(1/4)∫∇G_ℓ(r)·δ_r u(x,t)|δ_r u(x,t)|²dr.

They prove D_ℓ^{DR}→D in distributions, independently of G, and

  ∂t(|u|²/2)+div[u(|u|²/2+p)]+D=0.

Their Proposition 3 gives a direct sufficient increment condition: if for a.e. t and all small r,

  ∫_{T³}|δ_r u(t,x)|³dx ≤ C(t)|r|σ(|r|),

where C∈L¹(0,T), σ(a)→0 as a↓0, then D=0. Consequently the total energy is conserved. Besov α>1/3 yields σ(a)=a^{3α-1}; the condition also admits endpoint little-o moduli. On a torus there is no spatial boundary flux.

5. SOURCE COMPARISON

Onsager’s 1949 paper is a heuristic statistical/Fourier cascade argument: it describes how an infinite cascade could remove total energy from finite wave numbers and says a velocity increment condition stronger than |δ_r u|≲|r|^n for n>1/3 would imply conservation. It is not a theorem for distributional weak Euler solutions. Original source: “Statistical Hydrodynamics,” Il Nuovo Cimento 6 (1949), 279–287, DOI 10.1007/BF02780991.

Eyink’s 1994 Physica D paper gives an early rigorous Fourier/local-transfer proof. Its abstract states conservation for a *-Hölder condition stronger than ordinary Hölder and emphasizes local triadic interactions; it uses a band-averaged flux because the ordinary flux can miss the distance energy is transported in wavenumber. The CET paper’s footnote records Eyink’s condition as the weighted Fourier norm ||u||_{C_*^α}=Σ_k |k|^α|û_k|. This is a positive result but not the sharp standard-Hölder/Besov statement. Source: “Energy dissipation without viscosity in ideal hydrodynamics I: Fourier analysis and local energy transfer,” Physica D 78 (1994), 222–240, DOI 10.1016/0167-2789(94)90117-1. Only the primary abstract and CET’s footnote were inspected here; the full paywalled proof was not.

Constantin–E–Titi (1994) replace the more restrictive Fourier summability with the concise spatial commutator estimate and obtain the sharp supercritical Besov condition L³_t B^α_{3,∞}, α>1/3. Duchon–Robert (2000) reformulate the same limiting loss as a local distributional defect from a cubic velocity-increment expression, making endpoint little-o criteria transparent. Cheskidov–Constantin–Friedlander–Shvydkoy (2008) use Littlewood–Paley flux locality to reach the endpoint B^{1/3}_{3,c(N)} and exhibit a critical B^{1/3}_{3,∞} field with nonvanishing flux, while explicitly not claiming it is an Euler solution. Isett (2018) proves actual nonconservation for every α<1/3 by convex integration. This separates the historical heuristic, sufficient commutator theorem, critical kinematic obstruction, and actual PDE existence result.

6. ENDPOINT AND STRONGEST COMPARATOR

Theorem 1 of Constantin–E–Titi (1994) states, on the periodic box, that for every α>1/3 a weak solution u∈L³([0,T];B^α_{3,∞})∩C([0,T];L²) conserves energy for every t. Their proof uses the stress identity above, ||τ_ℓ||_{3/2}≲ℓ^{2α}||u||²_B, ||∇ū_ℓ||_3≲ℓ^{α-1}||u||_B, and the resulting ℓ^{3α-1} error.

The stronger endpoint baseline relevant here is Theorem 3.3 of Cheskidov–Constantin–Friedlander–Shvydkoy (2008), stated on R³: every weak Euler solution u∈L³([0,T];B^{1/3}_{3,c(N)})∩C_w([0,T];L²) conserves energy. Their c(N) endpoint is defined by the dyadic tail condition 2^{q/3}||Δ_q u||_3→0 as q→∞. In particular B^{1/3}_{3,p}, p<∞, is included. Their dyadic flux proof has the bound |Π_Q|≤C(K*d²)^{3/2}(Q), with d_q=2^{q/3}||Δ_q u||_3 and exponentially localized K; the tail condition forces Π_Q→0. The same estimate adapts to the periodic torus with periodic Littlewood–Paley blocks, though that periodic version is not imported as a separate source theorem here. No rate follows from c₀ alone absent a quantitative tail modulus.

In the same paper, §3.4 constructs a divergence-free vector field in B^{1/3}_{3,∞}(R³) whose Littlewood–Paley energy flux has positive liminf. The authors explicitly warn that this field is not an unforced Euler solution, so it is a kinematic flux example, not a dissipative solution. This independently marks the gap between B^{1/3}_{3,∞} and the c₀ endpoint for flux estimates.

7. EXPLICIT SCALING OBSTRUCTION

Use the periodized Gaussian filter with Fourier multiplier Ĝ_ℓ(k)=exp(-ℓ²|k|²/2) on T³, and the smooth divergence-free field

  ψ(x,y)=cos x+cos(2y)+cos(x+2y),
  v(x,y,z)=(∂_yψ,-∂_xψ,0).

Direct Fourier contraction of τ_1(v):∇v̄_1 gives the spatial mean

  F₀ := ⟨τ_1(v):∇v̄_1⟩
      =2[-(1/4)e^{-1}+e^{-4}-(3/4)e^{-5}]
      = -0.157415363306881… ≠ 0.

For reproducibility, with ψ_k=1/2 on k=(1,0),(0,2),(1,2) and their negatives, the 2D Euler vorticity equation in this convention is ∂tω_k=Σ_{p+q=k}(p^⊥·q)|q|²ψ_pψ_q, p^⊥=(p_2,-p_1). It gives Re(ψ̄_k∂tω_k)=(-1/4,1,-3/4) on the three positive modes; the negative modes contribute the same values. The Gaussian weights in the resolved energy are respectively e^{-1}, e^{-4}, e^{-5}; summing gives F₀ above. Thus Π_1(v)=-F₀>0. This is the instantaneous Euler transfer for a smooth initial state, not anomalous loss of total energy.

For every integer N≥1 and α∈(0,1), let v_N(x)=N^{-α}v(Nx), with filter width ℓ_N=N^{-1}. Its finitely many Fourier modes have amplitudes N^{-α}, so ||v_N||_{B^α_{3,∞}} is bounded above and below by constants independent of N. The Gaussian multipliers at ℓ_N match those for v at ℓ=1, while one derivative contributes N and the quadratic stress contributes N^{-2α}. Hence

  ⟨τ_{ℓ_N}(v_N):∇v̄_{ℓ_N}(v_N)⟩=N^{1-3α}F₀.

At α=1/3 this is a nonzero constant; below 1/3 it grows in magnitude. This verifies that a bound using only the B^α_{3,∞} norm cannot gain a vanishing factor at or below 1/3. Each v_N is smooth; for fixed N its flux tends to zero as ℓ→0. It is not a counterexample to conservation for a weak Euler solution. It only falsifies the inference that the cubic commutator estimate, from the stated norm bound alone, forces vanishing below/at threshold.

8. ACTUAL ANOMALOUS SOLUTIONS VS METHOD OBSTRUCTION

The negative direction below threshold is a separate PDE existence theorem, not a consequence of the triad scaling. Isett (Annals 2018) constructs, for every α<1/3, weak 3D Euler solutions in C_t C_x^α, compactly supported in time on R×T³ and therefore not conserving total kinetic energy. This completes the below-threshold flexible side of Onsager's conjecture. Isett's endpoint paper, published in Analysis & PDE 17 (2024), explicitly describes the exact C^{1/3} endpoint as open and proves constructions whose scale-wise Hölder regularity approaches 1/3 without attaining it. The exact critical C^{1/3} question is described as open in that endpoint paper. I also located no result establishing a dissipative solution in the broader plain B^{1/3}_{3,∞} class; the open C^{1/3} question alone does not settle that broader class. The sufficient c₀ endpoint theorem remains valid.

9. COSTS, FALSIFIERS, AND STATUS

There is no computational algorithm or runtime claim. The proof cost is one spatial mollification and Hölder/Minkowski estimates; the only scale dependence is ℓ^{3α-1} or the supplied endpoint modulus, with constants displayed above. The theorem statements quantify over every T>0, every weak solution in the stated class, and every admissible spatial mollifier. Endpoint c₀ yields qualitative convergence; a numerical convergence rate requires quantitative dyadic tail data. No finite-time computation or external proof certification was performed.

Useful falsifiers for this leaf are: (i) a sign or factor error in the filtered energy equation, checked by re-contracting the filtered momentum equation; (ii) a change in the exact triad Fourier mean, reproducible from its six wave vectors and Gaussian multipliers; (iii) a weak solution in a claimed sufficient regularity class with changing energy; or (iv) a newer primary result resolving the exact endpoint, which would update the status but not the commutator calculation. The explicit fields in §7 do not satisfy the Euler-solution premise and cannot falsify an energy-conservation theorem.

STATUS: Reconstructed classical commutator derivation; source theorem for the endpoint c₀ class; source theorem for anomalous solutions strictly below 1/3; exact endpoint dissipative-solution status remains UNKNOWN/open in the primary literature located for this pass. No novelty claim.

RESTART: Preserve the sign convention Π_ℓ=-τ_ℓ:∇ū_ℓ and D=lim Π_ℓ. Next useful work is to independently verify the explicit triad calculation by a symbolic/Fourier audit, then check for post-2024 primary papers on exact C^{1/3} or B^{1/3}_{3,∞} dissipative solutions before using any current-status statement beyond this scoped audit.
