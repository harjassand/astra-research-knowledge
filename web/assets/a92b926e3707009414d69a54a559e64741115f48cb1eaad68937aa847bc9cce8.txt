Outcome and scope

The unrestricted inequality remains UNKNOWN. No exact counterexample, and no
proof for arbitrary Hilbert dimension and arbitrary positive witness Q, was
found. The new internally proved partial result is noncovariant: every actual
bistochastic k-output broadcaster obeys the exact inequality for every Q of
rank at most k. For two outputs this covers rank at most two and, more broadly,
every factor bank partitioned into two commuting groups. It yields ONE canonical
EB comparator on any supplied score subspace of dimension at most two, together
with a finite approximate compiler from the supplied broadcaster. Iterating the
broadcaster gives a GENERIC factor 2 ceil(log2 r) comparator on every fixed
r-dimensional score span, r>=2, and hence O(log d) on all H0. Combining the
rank-two result with the usual singlet-star trace bound also proves the complete
inequality for every selfadjoint bistochastic selfcompatible qubit channel,
including channels with signed L2 spectrum. No external validation or novelty
claim is made for these elementary partial theorems.

A possible counterexample to the requested two-output inequality must therefore
have d >= 3 and witness rank >= 3. It must survive the true common pure-1-design
optimization and supply an actual compatible channel. Separate variance minima,
independent star maxima, and finite numerical relaxations do not suffice.

A. Exact target, conventions, and equivalent deficit

Let tau(X)=Tr(X)/d and H0={A=A*:tau(A)=0}, with real inner product
<A,B>=tau(AB). Let Phi:M_d->M_d be UCP and tau-preserving. Actual two-output
selfcompatibility means that a UCP map J:M_d tensor M_d -> M_d is supplied or
exists, with J(X tensor I)=J(I tensor X)=Phi(X). The requested full channel
theorem additionally assumes Phi is selfadjoint and positive as an L2(tau)
operator. Section B needs neither of these two extra assumptions. Sections C
and D use selfadjointness for the operator comparison; positivity is not needed
for the qubit theorem or for the common-span existence theorem.

For Q >= 0 on H0 write Q=sum_i |A_i><A_i|, and put

 t(Q)=Tr_H0 Q=sum_i tau(A_i^2),
 E_Phi(Q)=sum_i tau[A_i(A_i-Phi(A_i))],
 h1(Q)=max_{pure finite designs} sum_y p_y sum_i [Tr(P_y A_i)]^2,

where P_y has rank one, p_y >= 0, sum p_y=1, and sum p_y P_y=I/d. Compactness
of the pure-state moment body and Caratheodory allow finite ensembles. Since
sum_y p_y Tr(P_y A_i^2)=tau(A_i^2), the EXACT deficit is

 Delta1(Q)=t(Q)-h1(Q)
          =min_{pure 1-designs} sum_y p_y sum_i Var_{P_y}(A_i).

The target is 2 E_Phi(Q) >= Delta1(Q), equivalently
2 sum_i tau[A_i Phi(A_i)]-t(Q) <= h1(Q). The minimum over ONE pure state is
only a lower bound on Delta1; it cannot replace this design-average deficit.

For a pure design define Psi(X)=d sum_y p_y Tr(P_y X)P_y. It is UCP, trace-
preserving, entanglement-breaking, and positive selfadjoint on L2(tau). Its
quadratic form is tau[A Psi(A)]=sum_y p_y [Tr(P_y A)]^2. Also 0 <= Psi <= I
as L2 operators: the upper bound is the scalar variance inequality averaged
over the design. The desired full theorem is, by finite-dimensional separation,
the existence of one such Psi satisfying Psi >= 2 Phi-I on H0. This separation
does not license substituting different designs for different observable
components of a fixed Q.

B. Exact measurement lemma for two commuting groups

THEOREM B.1. Let J be an actual two-output UCP broadcaster with bistochastic
marginals Phi. Suppose a finite bank of Hermitian observables {A_i} can be
partitioned as I1 disjoint-union I2, with pairwise commuting A_i within each
group. They need not commute across the groups. Then there is a finite pure
1-design, explicitly obtained from J and these observables, such that

 sum_y p_y sum_i Var_{P_y}(A_i) <= 2 E_Phi(Q),
 Q=sum_i |A_i><A_i|.

Thus the requested target holds for this Q. Tracelessness is only needed to
place Q in H0; the measurement calculation itself allows arbitrary Hermitian
observables. No covariance, positivity of the L2 spectrum, or selfadjointness
of Phi is assumed.

PROOF. Choose a common orthonormal eigenbasis for group 1, with rank-one
projections F_a, a=1,...,d, and a common orthonormal eigenbasis for group 2,
with projections G_b. For i in group 1 write A_i=sum_a alpha_i(a) F_a; for
i in group 2 write A_i=sum_b beta_i(b) G_b. An empty group can be measured in
an arbitrary basis. Define the input POVM

 M_ab=J(F_a tensor G_b).

Each M_ab is positive and sum_ab M_ab=I. Its marginal first and second meter
moments are, for i in group 1,

 sum_ab alpha_i(a) M_ab=Phi(A_i),
 sum_ab alpha_i(a)^2 M_ab=Phi(A_i^2),

and the analogous equalities with beta_i(b) hold for group 2. Give outcome
(a,b) the scalar estimate c_i(a,b)=alpha_i(a) or beta_i(b), as appropriate.
The trace-averaged mean-square meter error is

 L_i=sum_ab tau[(A_i-c_i(a,b)I) M_ab (A_i-c_i(a,b)I)]
    =tau(A_i^2)-2 tau[A_i Phi(A_i)]+tau[Phi(A_i^2)]
    =2 tau[A_i(A_i-Phi(A_i))].

The last equality uses tau preservation. Every term in the first expression
is nonnegative; no operator commutation with M_ab was used, since the trace
permits the expansion.

Spectrally refine each input effect:

 M_ab=sum_z m_abz P_abz,  m_abz>=0,  rank(P_abz)=1.

Discard zero eigenvalues. Put p_abz=m_abz/d and y=(a,b,z). Then
sum_y p_y P_y=I/d and sum_y p_y=1, so this is exactly a pure 1-design.
For each resulting pure state and each observable,

 Tr[P_y(A_i-c_i(y)I)^2]
   =Var_{P_y}(A_i)+(Tr(P_y A_i)-c_i(y))^2
   >=Var_{P_y}(A_i).

Multiplying by p_y and summing y identifies the left meter expression L_i.
Summing i proves the theorem. There are at most d^2 original joint outcomes
and at most d eigenvectors per effect, hence at most d^3 pure outcomes. QED.

COROLLARY B.2 (all rank-two witnesses). Every Q >= 0 on H0 of rank at most
two has a factorization with at most two nonzero Hermitian factors. Put one
factor on each output. Each singleton group commutes, so B.1 applies. This is
a proof for all such witnesses, not just for a tested observable pair.

COROLLARY B.3 (k receivers). For an actual UCP k-output broadcaster with every
marginal Phi bistochastic, the same proof using a joint product PVM gives the
inequality for every factor bank partitionable into k commuting groups, in
particular every Q of rank at most k. There are at most d^(k+1) pure outcomes.
The input broadcaster representation already has exponential size in k in a
generic dense representation; its acquisition is not supplied for free.

The scalar posterior means in this proof depend on the INPUT effects M_ab,
not on a Kraus representation of the marginal Phi. The construction therefore
does not make the invalid N92 own-effects transfer to the original channel.

C. One common comparator on a two-dimensional score subspace

THEOREM C.1. Let Phi be selfadjoint and bistochastic and actually selfcompatible.
For every supplied real subspace S subset H0 of dimension r<=2, there is ONE
canonical pure-design EB Psi such that

 P_S Psi P_S >= P_S(2 Phi-I)P_S.

Every full-rank PSD witness supported on S still has rank at most two. Hence
B.2 proves its support inequality. Apply separation in the space of real
symmetric operators on S to the compact set of compressed pure-design moment
operators minus its PSD cone. If the required comparison failed, a separating
PSD Q supported on S would violate B.2. This proves existence. Although the
direct B.1 design may vary with Q, separation now gives one common Psi on S.
It does not give comparison on all of H0 when dim H0>2.

The same statement holds for dim S<=k with k actual receivers. A fixed design
for arbitrary factor groups gives bounds for diagonal/group-block losses;
cross terms between the groups require a separate argument. A decomposition
of an arbitrary Q into rank-two pieces cannot be summed: h1 is subadditive,
and different pieces can demand different pure designs.

There is a constructive approximate version of C.1 from a supplied J and an
orthonormal tau basis G_1,...,G_r of S. Write T_ab=tau[G_a Phi(G_b)] and
R=2T-I_r. For a design let M_ab=sum_y p_y Tr(P_y G_a)Tr(P_y G_b).
Then 0<=M<=I_r. In the positive-spectrum target, 0<=T<=I_r, so every loss
L=M-R has norm at most B=2. With signed selfadjoint Phi, norm at most B=4
is safe. The following exact-real arithmetic scheme returns one design:

 1. Given losses L_1,...,L_(t-1), set
    Q_t=exp[-eta sum_(s<t) L_s]/Tr exp[-eta sum_(s<t) L_s].
    This is a real symmetric witness density on S.
 2. Factor Q_t into at most r<=2 factors in the G basis. Apply B.1 to those
    factors and J to obtain a pure design, and compute its M_t.
 3. Put L_t=M_t-R. The oracle guarantee is Tr(Q_t L_t)>=0.
 4. Return the uniform mixture of the T constructed designs.

For eta B<=1, Golden-Thompson and
exp(-eta L)<=I-eta L+2 eta^2 B^2 I give the standard potential estimate

 lambda_min[(1/T) sum_t L_t]
   >=(1/T) sum_t Tr(Q_t L_t)-log(r)/(eta T)-2 eta B^2.

Indeed log Tr exp[-eta sum_t L_t] is at most
log r-eta sum_t Tr(Q_t L_t)+2 eta^2 B^2 T and at least
-eta lambda_min(sum_t L_t). For 0<epsilon<=1, choose
eta=epsilon/(4B^2) and T>=8B^2 log(r)/epsilon^2. The resulting mixed design
satisfies M_bar>=R-epsilon I_r. The r=1 case is handled directly by an
eigenbasis and requires no iterations. Thus the approximate common-measurement
construction does not require an oracle optimizing h1.

Cost and acquisition boundary. J, Phi, S and valid exact compatibility are
supplied inputs; finding J or learning an unknown channel is not part of this
compiler. Each iteration produces at most d^3 pure states. A direct dense
implementation uses O(d^8) arithmetic per iteration as a safe unoptimized
bound for pullbacks, eigendecompositions, and moments; storing a dense Choi J
requires O(d^6) scalars and the unreduced mixed design O(T d^4) scalars. The
number of iterations is O(B^2 log(r)/epsilon^2). Exact input matrices and
spectral data must be representable. For algebraic inputs the finite spectral
operations are algebraic. The matrix-exponential update may be replaced by a
rational PSD witness density Q_t within trace norm alpha of the ideal density
formed from the approximate previous losses. If each approximate loss has
operator error at most beta, its norm is at most B+beta; its ideal-density
payoff is at least -alpha B-beta, since B.1 still gives nonnegative payoff
for the actual rational Q_t and its exactly constructed design. Apply the
regret bound with B+beta. Passing back to true averaged losses costs another
beta, so the final slack is at most the regret term plus alpha B+2beta.
For example use regret tolerance epsilon/2, alpha<=epsilon/(8B) and
beta<=epsilon/8. Computing the accumulated matrix exponential to this alpha
must itself charge its size, spectrum and precision. Precision/bit complexity
and nearly degenerate spectral calculations are charged, not declared
uniformly polynomial without an input-size/conditioning analysis. Numerical
effect refinements with no barycenter/positivity repair are not exact
certificates. Compactness yields an exact design; Caratheodory
allows at most d^2+r(r+1)/2 states when only its barycenter and compressed
moment operator must be retained. No implementation of the full optimizer
or its certified finite-precision update is claimed in this run.

Restricted physical consequence. For any valid states rho_x=(I+H_x)/d with
H_x in the SAME S, the common Psi obeys

 tau[H_x(I-Psi)H_x]<=2 tau[H_x(I-Phi)H_x]+epsilon tau(H_x^2),
 T(rho_x,Psi(rho_x))
   <=(1/2) sqrt[2 E_Phi(H_x)+epsilon tau(H_x^2)].

The second inequality uses ||Z||_1<=sqrt(d) ||Z||_2 and
(I-Psi)^2<=I-Psi. It is a uniform EB reconstruction statement for supplied
two-dimensional tracial score banks. It does not validate N93's preceding
smoothing/Petz reduction or address arbitrary score rank or noncentral
physical reference weights.

C.2. Generic logarithmic-score-rank rounding

THEOREM C.2. For every finite d, every selfadjoint bistochastic actually
two-output-selfcompatible Phi, and every fixed real score subspace S subset H0
of dimension r>=2, there exists ONE canonical pure-design EB Psi such that

 P_S(I-Psi)P_S <= 2 ceil(log2 r) P_S(I-Phi)P_S.                 (C.2)

No positivity of the L2 spectrum or covariance is required. r is the dimension
of the FIXED common score span S, not the rank of one selected witness Q.
For dim S=1 an eigenbasis design fixes the generator exactly; for dim S=0
the statement is vacuous. This is a dimension-dependent theorem; it does not
establish the unrestricted c=2 conjecture.

PROOF. Let m=ceil(log2 r), k=2^m, and let J be an actual UCP two-output
extension of Phi. Define the binary-tree Heisenberg channels

 J_0=Id,  J_l=J composed with (J_(l-1) tensor J_(l-1)).

Each J_l maps M_d^(tensor 2^l) into M_d and is UCP, by tensoring and composing
UCP maps. Inductively EVERY individual output marginal is exactly Phi^l:
if a leaf observable is X and all other leaf factors are I, its branch gives
Phi^(l-1)(X), and the final root marginal applies Phi once more. Thus J_m is
an actual k-output broadcaster with common marginal Phi^m. Phi^m is CP,
unital, tau-preserving and L2-selfadjoint. One may discard k-r leaf outputs,
leaving an actual r-output broadcaster with the same marginal Phi^m.

Every PSD witness Q supported on S has rank(Q)<=r. Apply B.3 to the r-output
extension: 2 E_(Phi^m)(Q)>=TrQ-h1(Q). Separation as in C.1, now on the FULL
r-dimensional S, yields one common canonical Psi satisfying

 P_S(I-Psi)P_S<=2 P_S(I-Phi^m)P_S.

Bistochastic UCP contraction and selfadjointness put every L2 eigenvalue
lambda of Phi in [-1,1]. For every integer m>=1,

 1-lambda^m=(1-lambda) sum_(j=0)^(m-1) lambda^j
           <=m(1-lambda),

because each lambda^j<=1 and 1-lambda>=0. At lambda=1 both sides are zero.
This does not require lambda>=0 and remains true for negative eigenvalues,
including lambda=-1. Functional calculus therefore gives

 0<=I-Phi^m<=m(I-Phi),
 E_(Phi^m)(Q)<=m E_Phi(Q) for EVERY PSD Q.

Compressing this operator inequality to S and combining the previous bound
proves (C.2). QED.

Taking S=H0 gives a FULL-DOMAIN canonical EB comparator with
c(d)=2 ceil(log2(d^2-1)) for d>=2. This is O(log d), with an exact integer
coefficient. The qubit theorem D improves the generic c(2)=4 to 2. A proof
of c=2 in all d still requires a new argument; no logarithmic factor was
silently removed.

Acquired approximate comparator. The same witness-density compiler in C.1
works on r-dimensional S by replacing its oracle with spectral measurements
of the at most r factors of Q on the r selected tree leaves. Let T_m denote
the compressed Phi^m and R_m=2T_m-I_r. The losses M-R_m have norm at most 2
for positive Phi and at most 4 for signed selfadjoint Phi. O(B^2 log r / eps^2)
iterations give one pure design with

 P_S(I-Psi)P_S<=2m P_S(I-Phi)P_S+eps I_S.

All finite-precision qualifications from C.1 remain. The primitive J and S
are supplied, and the tree uses k-1<2r two-output calls; no unknown channel
or extension is learned. After discarding unused leaves, each oracle has at
most d^r product PVM outcomes and d^(r+1) refined pure states. A safe naive
evaluation cost using the primitive dense J is O(k d^(r+6)+r^3) arithmetic
per oracle, by applying at most k-1 d^2-input pullbacks per outcome and then
diagonalizing the input effects. Unreduced design storage over T iterations
is O(T d^(r+2)), besides O(d^6) for the primitive J and O(r d^2) for S. Thus
this is an EXPLICIT finite compiler but not an efficient general one: cost
is exponential in r log d before compression. A Caratheodory representative
needs at most d^2+r(r+1)/2 pure states once the barycenter and compressed
moment matrix are retained; finding and certifying that reduction has a
further linear-algebra/precision cost. The short final existence certificate
does not erase acquisition cost.

Honest reconstruction consequence. For any collection of valid states
rho_x=(I+H_x)/d with all H_x in the SAME r-dimensional S,

 T(rho_x,Psi(rho_x)) <= (1/2) sqrt[2m E_Phi(H_x)],

or with approximate comparator add eps tau(H_x^2) under the square root.
Consequently for a sequence with dimensions d_n and score spans S_n of
dimensions r_n, canonical EB reconstruction follows whenever

 sup_x E_(Phi_n)(H_x) ceil(log2 max(2,r_n)) ->0

(and the approximate compiler tolerance times the squared score norm also
vanishes). No bound on family cardinality is used. For fixed r this is a
dimension-free reconstruction theorem from small broadcaster energy.

Approximate score-span version. Suppose a supplied orthogonal projection
P_S has residual ||H_x-P_S H_x||_(2,tau)<=delta uniformly. For positive Phi,
E_Phi(P_S H_x)^(1/2)<=E_Phi(H_x)^(1/2)+delta, since 0<=I-Phi<=I. The
common comparator from C.2 and L2 contraction of Psi then give

 T(rho_x,Psi rho_x)
  <=delta+(1/2)sqrt[2m (sqrt(E_Phi(H_x))+delta)^2
                       +eps ||P_S H_x||_(2,tau)^2].

For signed Phi use sqrt(2)delta in the inner energy bound, because
0<=I-Phi<=2I. Positivity of P_S H_x+I is not needed in this estimate; only
the original rho_x must be a state. Both S and the uniform residual bound
are SUPPLIED certificates. Bounded Holevo capacity has not been shown here
to acquire such an S, control r, or bound this tail. Replacing log r by a
capacity parameter without that missing mechanism would change the theorem.

C.3. Exact obstruction to replacing score dimension by bounded capacity

For d=2^n, let G_1,...,G_D, D=d^2-1, be all nonidentity Hermitian Pauli
operators, orthonormal in tau and obeying G_i^2=I. Fix 0<a<1 and consider
the 2D-state family

 rho_(i,+)=(I+a G_i)/d,  rho_(i,-)=(I-a G_i)/d.

Each state has d/2 eigenvalues (1+a)/d and d/2 eigenvalues (1-a)/d, hence
the SAME entropy log d-f(a), where, in nats,

 f(a)=[(1+a)log(1+a)+(1-a)log(1-a)]/2.

For every prior, its average state has entropy at most log d. The Holevo
capacity of the family is therefore at most f(a), and the uniform prior
attains f(a), because the +/- pairs have average I/d. Thus its capacity is
EXACTLY f(a), independent of d, although its tracial score span is ALL H0
of dimension D. For a=1/2 this is approximately 0.130812 nats.

This also obstructs a uniform small L2 score tail of bounded rank. For every
rank-r orthogonal projection P_S on H0,

 sum_(i=1)^D ||(I-P_S)G_i||_(2,tau)^2=D-r,
 max_(i,+/-) ||a G_i-P_S(a G_i)||_(2,tau)
    >=a sqrt(1-r/D).

Therefore a uniform residual certificate delta<a requires
r>=D[1-(delta/a)^2]. Fixed capacity alone cannot acquire a fixed-dimensional
score space with a uniformly vanishing tail, even with a fixed likelihood
sandwich (1-a)I/d<=rho<= (1+a)I/d. This is an obstruction ONLY to that
dimension/tail substitution. No channel nearly fixing this family is supplied,
so this example does NOT refute the bounded-Holevo approximate-broadcasting
target, nor rule out a different use of selfcompatibility and near fixation.

D. Complete qubit theorem, including signed spectrum

THEOREM D.1. In d=2, every bistochastic, L2-selfadjoint, actually selfcompatible
Phi has a canonical pure-design EB Psi with Psi>=2Phi-I on H0. Positivity of
Phi as an L2 operator is not required.

PROOF. Use Pauli observables X,Y,Z, orthonormal in tau. A selfadjoint qubit
channel has a real symmetric Bloch matrix T. Choose orthonormal eigenaxes,
changing an axis sign if necessary so that the change of coordinates is a
proper rotation, and write its eigenvalues lambda_1,lambda_2,lambda_3. UCP
bistochastic contraction gives lambda_i<=1 (and |lambda_i|<=1).

Every canonical pure design has a real PSD Bloch moment M of trace one,
since every pure qubit has a unit Bloch vector. Conversely any PSD M of trace
one is such a moment: if M=sum_i mu_i e_i e_i^T, use the six pure states
with Bloch vectors +/-e_i, each of weight mu_i/2. The first moment vanishes
and the second equals M. Thus it suffices to prove

 sum_i (2lambda_i-1)_+ <=1.

For each pair of eigenaxes, Q=|e_i><e_i|+|e_j><e_j| has h1(Q)=1, since the
sum of two squared coordinates of a unit Bloch vector is at most one and a
pair of opposite pure states in that plane attains it. B.2 gives
lambda_i+lambda_j<=3/2.

The remaining trace constraint is sum_i lambda_i<=2. Here is an independent
standard star derivation, also fixing all normalizations. For arbitrary d,
an orthonormal tau basis G_i of H0 obeys
sum_i G_i^T tensor G_i=d^2 P_Bell-I. Therefore

 W_I=d^2(P_RA tensor I_B+P_RB tensor I_A)-2I_RAB.

The two Bell projectors have overlap singular value 1/d, and the largest
eigenvalue of their sum is 1+1/d. One can check this directly on vectors
v_b=|Bell>_RA|b>_B and w_b=|Bell>_RB|b>_A: their Gram cross term is
<v_b,w_c>=delta_bc/d, so v_b+w_b and v_b-w_b have eigenvalues 1+1/d and
1-1/d; the remaining orthogonal space has eigenvalue zero. The normalized
Choi expectation gives

 2Tr_H0 Phi=Tr(Omega W_I)<=d^2+d-2.

At d=2 this is Tr T<=2. No marginal relaxation is invoked: Omega is the
actual broadcaster's normalized Choi state, and the bound is valid for it.

If zero or one lambda_i exceeds 1/2, the positive-part sum is at most one
by contraction. If exactly two exceed 1/2, the pair bound gives their sum
of positive parts at most one. If all three do, the trace bound gives
sum_i(2lambda_i-1)<=1. Put r_i=(2lambda_i-1)_+ and choose
mu_i=r_i+(1-sum_j r_j)/3. Then the six-state design above has
M=diag(mu_i)>=diag(2lambda_i-1), proving the theorem. QED.

Given the exact 3x3 Bloch matrix and its compatible-channel promise, the
selector is acquired by a constant-size eigendecomposition and six states;
it need not acquire J. Degenerate eigenspaces allow arbitrary orthonormal
eigenaxes. Precision and exact representability still have their usual cost.
The universal qubit cloner has lambda_i=2/3 and requires mu_i>=1/3 for c=2.
A canonical comparator with a constant c<2 would require sum mu_i>1, so c=2
is sharp in this class. N92 already states the positive-spectrum qubit theorem
with this axis-dephasing selector and pair/triple constraints. That case is an
independent reconstruction, not new progress. The signed-spectrum statement is
made explicit here; no external novelty is claimed for either formulation.

E. A fully physical Pauli-cloner search reduction

This reduction was reconstructed independently rather than treating an
unconstrained star eigenvalue as a channel. Let d=2^n, N=d^2, and label Hermitian
Paulis P_v by v in GF(2)^(2n), with commutation character
chi_v(u)=(-1)^[u,v]. Define real symmetric matrices on R^N

 F_uv=chi_u(v)/d,  D_v=diag_u chi_v(u),
 (X_v a)_u=a_(u+v),  H_q=(1/2)sum_(v!=0) q_v(D_v+X_v), q_v>=0.

F is an orthogonal involution, F D_v F=X_v, so H_q commutes with F. For
strictly positive q, H_q is an irreducible Metzler matrix. Its top eigenvector
a is unique, strictly positive and normalized. F a is a top eigenvector,
and (Fa)_0=sum_u a_u/d>0; uniqueness forces Fa=a, rather than -a. For weights
with zeros, add positive epsilon weights and take a convergent subsequence.
Thus a>=0, Fa=a and H_q a=lambda_max(H_q) a are available for every q>=0.

Let |B_v>=(I tensor P_v)|Bell> and define the normalized four-party state

 |omega_a>_RABE=sum_v a_v |B_v>_RA tensor |B_v^*>_BE.

Recoupling the Bell pairs gives
|omega_a>=sum_u (Fa)_u |B_u>_RB tensor |B_u^*>_AE.
The overlap of these two Bell-pair bases is
Tr(P_u P_v P_u P_v)/d^2=chi_u(v)/d, establishing the identity including the
complex-conjugation convention for imaginary Paulis. Tracing E produces an
actual compatible normalized Choi state, not just pairwise necessary bounds.
All one-party marginals are I/d, and both RA and RB marginals have Bell
probabilities p_v=a_v^2. The common Pauli channel is CP, bistochastic and
selfadjoint, with Pauli eigenvalues

 lambda_v=sum_u chi_v(u)a_u^2
         =<a,D_v a>=<Fa,X_v Fa>=sum_u a_u a_(u+v)>=0.

It therefore also meets the positive L2 requirement. For Q diagonal in the
Pauli basis, with factors sqrt(q_v) P_v,

 sum_v q_v lambda_v=lambda_max(H_q),
 2E-equivalent objective=2lambda_max(H_q)-sum_v q_v.

The first-moment constraint is handled exactly on the design side: a Pauli
orbit of ANY pure seed is a pure 1-design, while the squared Pauli means are
unchanged by conjugation. Hence

 h1(Q)=max_{unit psi} sum_v q_v <psi|P_v|psi>^2.

Each stabilizer-state orbit is a legal design and yields a lower bound
max_L sum_(v in L\{0}) q_v, over maximal commuting Pauli label subspaces L.
Replacing the true h1 optimum by this lower bound is valid for a NO-VIOLATION
screen, but a positive gap against it would NOT be a counterexample. The
finite tests below found no such gap. This reduction is a physically valid
candidate class; it is not a proof that every compatible channel has this
form, nor a general c=2 theorem for it.

1. Qutrit SDP screen: 16 fixed witnesses (isotropic plus seeded PSD Wishart
matrices of ranks 2,...,8). The broadcaster variable is a 27x27 Hermitian
PSD Choi state, with ALL three one-body marginals I/3, output-swap symmetry,
a real symmetric transfer matrix, and initially PSD transfer matrix. Its
objective is Tr(Omega W_Q)-TrQ. A separate bosonic-PPT moment SDP supplies
an UPPER relaxation of h1 with mean I/3; finite pure-state moment LPs supply
numerical LOWER candidates. No violation was found, even against those lower
candidates. Repeating without transfer-matrix PSD gave the same optima to
solver accuracy; this does not certify positivity is generally automatic.
SCS floating optima/PSD residuals are finite evidence, not exact upper bounds
or a proof. The isotropic equality agrees with the exact universal cloner.

5. Single-state uncertainty is insufficient. Delta1 is a barycentric convex
roof, and replacing it by min_pure sum Var removes a required constraint.
Arbitrary sums of rank-two/group bounds similarly remove the common POVM.
No claim rests on these reductions.

6. Kraus/Petz-square transfer is insufficient. N92's supplied-instrument
comparison applies to its Petz-square likelihood map. An arbitrary Kraus
choice for a marginal need not round that marginal; even Fourier-unitary
Kraus representations of dephasing can have constant effects. The lead's
universal-cloner own-effects obstruction is another exact failure of this

7. Noncommutative Grothendieck factor 2 does NOT directly identify h1. The
primary Naor-Regev-Vidick rounder optimizes a bilinear four-tensor and outputs
two unitaries from vector-valued unitaries. It does not output rank-one POVM
effects, their weights, or the barycenter I/d. Under the natural channel-
correlation objective Q=I_H0, a unitary identity has value d^2-1 while the
canonical moment optimum is d-1, a ratio d+1. A multiplicative factor-two
unitary guarantee cannot alone imply the required additive inequality. A
new bridge using actual two-output constraints and pure-design completeness
would still be needed. This is an interface obstruction, not a theorem that
Grothendieck's method cannot be adapted.

G. Quadratic-transport formulation and exact remaining gate

Define the positive bipartite cost

 C_Q=(1/2) sum_i (A_i^T tensor I-I tensor A_i)^2.

For the normalized Choi coupling J_Phi with both marginals I/d,
Tr(J_Phi C_Q)=E_Phi(Q). The minimum of this cost over ALL separable couplings
with these same marginals is EXACTLY Delta1(Q), not merely bounded by it.
To prove this, refine a separable coupling into products P_y^T tensor R_y
of pure states, with weights w_y. Let a_yi=Tr(P_y A_i), b_yi=Tr(R_y A_i).
Both pure marginal ensembles have barycenter I/d. The elementary inequality
sum_i a_yi b_yi <= (sum_i a_yi^2+sum_i b_yi^2)/2 gives correlation at most
h1(Q). The canonical coupling sum_y p_y P_y^T tensor P_y attains h1(Q).
This proves the equality of minimum separable cost and Delta1.

Thus the unrestricted gate is the special, additive integrality-gap claim

 min_{ACTUAL two-extendible, bistochastic, selfadjoint positive couplings}
      Tr(J_Phi C_Q)
     >=(1/2) min_{separable, same marginals} Tr(sigma C_Q)

for every PSD Q. C_Q is a sum of matched observable-square differences, not
an arbitrary positive transport cost. A general separability-versus-extension
gap result must preserve this structure, all one-party marginals, and the
channel selfadjoint/positive conditions to apply. The primary separable-QOT
paper below studies related costs and Fisher/variance relations; its checked
interface does not supply this exact two-extendibility comparison.

The strongest next attack is a barycenter-preserving rounder for this special
cost, or an exact Q of rank at least three and actual extension with cost
strictly below half its separable minimum. A finite SDP witness would require
exact feasibility repair for Omega and an exact dual operator majorant or
semialgebraic certificate for h1, with a strict residual margin. A sample of
pure states, stabilizer lower bound, or pairwise star inequalities cannot
provide the needed h1 upper certificate.
