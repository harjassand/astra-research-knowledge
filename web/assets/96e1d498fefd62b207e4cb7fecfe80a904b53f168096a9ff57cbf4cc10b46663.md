# Cycle 4: finite-sample and nuisance audit of the information-starvation frontier

## Verdict

The power-law calculation in `RESULT_AND_RESTART.md` is correct **conditional on a calibrated observation map and a fixed background model**. The apparent information gain from a background-free molecular counter is real in that ideal regime: finite-sample distinguishability scales as `M epsilon`, rather than `M epsilon^2`. The current formulation does not yield a uniform theorem for ctDNA, however. An unknown time-varying background can exactly absorb the clone signal; an unknown clone-specific release/copy/spatial factor can exactly trade off against cell fraction; and at clinical molecule inputs the typical observation can be zero even when expected Fisher information is positive.

Thus the defensible result is a conditional boundary plus a no-go: molecule counting improves the exponent only above a measured, stable background and only for the molecule-weighted clone signal. Without drift and transfer bounds, finite or infinite depth cannot identify whole-tumor resistant-cell fraction from plasma alone. The relevant mathematical ingredients (Fisher pullback, Poisson/binomial information, Hellinger/Le Cam, and the moment inequalities in §6) are standard; the biologically useful addition is an explicit, physically calibrated nuisance gate, not a priority-cleared theorem.

## Exact finite-separation result

For a single marker, let `M` be the number of independent genome-equivalent opportunities, and suppose the exact assay model is

`C | H=i ~ Binomial(M, p_i)`, `p_i = b + a epsilon g_i`,

with `0<=p_i<=1`. Here `epsilon` scales the rare-cell state, `g_i` is its response under parameter value `i`, `a` is a known signal-to-molecule conversion, and `b` is a known stable background. The exact Bhattacharyya affinity of the two finite-sample laws is

`rho = [sqrt(p_0 p_1) + sqrt((1-p_0)(1-p_1))]^M`.

Set `D = -2 log(rho)`. Then the exact total variation distance obeys `TV(P_0,P_1) <= sqrt(1-exp(-D))`, and every test with type-I error `alpha` and type-II error `beta` obeys

`alpha + beta >= 1 - TV(P_0,P_1) >= 1 - sqrt(1-exp(-D))`.

For independent timepoints, multiply the affinities (equivalently sum the `D_t`). In the rare-VAF regime, the binomial is approximated by `Pois(mu_i)` with `mu_i=M p_i`; then

`D_Pois = (sqrt(mu_1)-sqrt(mu_0))^2 = M(sqrt(p_1)-sqrt(p_0))^2`.

For two simple Poisson hypotheses, the exact TV is also directly computable. If `mu_1>mu_0`, let `k*=ceil((mu_1-mu_0)/log(mu_1/mu_0))`; then

`TV = P_{mu_1}(C>=k*) - P_{mu_0}(C>=k*)`.

The Hellinger expression is a convenient finite-sample lower bound, not a normal or local-KL approximation. For target `alpha+beta<=gamma`, a **necessary** condition is

`D >= -log(1-(1-gamma)^2)`.

At `alpha=beta=.05`, this is `D>=1.66073`. With `b=0`, `p_i=a epsilon g_i`, so the necessary molecule budget scales as

`M >= 1.66073 / [a epsilon (sqrt(g_1)-sqrt(g_0))^2]`.

With fixed `b>0` and `a epsilon max(g_i)<<b`,

`D = M a^2 epsilon^2 (g_1-g_0)^2/(4b) + O(M epsilon^3)`,

so the necessary budget is approximately

`M >= 6.64292 b / [a^2 epsilon^2 (g_1-g_0)^2]`.

This verifies the proposed crossover at `a epsilon ~ b` for a **known** background. These are necessary conditions from a TV upper bound; exact Poisson/binomial TV or a constructive Neyman–Pearson test gives the sharper answer for a specified alternative.

For the important boundary `H0: p=0` versus `H1: p=a epsilon`, with zero background and perfect specificity, the optimal rule is call positive iff `C>=1`. It has type-I error zero and miss probability `exp(-M a epsilon)`. Thus 95% detection requires exactly

`M a epsilon >= log(20)=2.99573`.

This one-sided detection result is the source of the familiar `3/f` molecule requirement when `f` is the **molecule-level VAF**. It is not a requirement in resistant-cell fraction unless the physical cell-to-VAF map is known.

## Unknown or drifting background: exact no-go and bounded-drift gate

The candidate's statement that nuisance estimation “can only lower” information is too weak to support a finite guarantee. Two sharp obstructions apply.

**Arbitrary drift gives exact observational equivalence.** At times `t`, take clone signals `a epsilon g_0(t)` and `a epsilon g_1(t)`, with `g_0(0)=g_1(0)` so the same baseline is observed. Under hypothesis 0 choose `b_0(t)=B+a epsilon[g_1(t)-g_0(t)]`; under hypothesis 1 choose `b_1(t)=B`. For `g_1(t)>=g_0(t)` and `B>=0`, both backgrounds are valid and

`b_0(t)+a epsilon g_0(t) = b_1(t)+a epsilon g_1(t)`

at every time. Every binomial/Poisson observation has exactly the same law under both hypotheses, for every `M`; the minimax sum of errors is one. An arbitrary drifting background therefore destroys uniform identifiability, not merely one power of information.

**Bounded common-baseline drift gives a quantitative margin.** Suppose the baseline background is `b0`, while at the post-baseline time each hypothesis may have its own drift in `[-d,d]`. For `g1>g0`, the two allowed post-baseline rates are intervals

`I_0=[b0-d+a epsilon g0, b0+d+a epsilon g0]`,
`I_1=[b0-d+a epsilon g1, b0+d+a epsilon g1]`.

The least-favourable separation is `delta=(a epsilon(g1-g0)-2d)_+`. If `delta=0`, the composite hypotheses overlap and no amount of sequencing guarantees discrimination. If `delta>0` and the rates are nonnegative, a least-favourable Poisson pair uses `q0=b0+d+a epsilon g0` and `q1=b0-d+a epsilon g1`; its Hellinger exponent is

`D_min = M (sqrt(q1)-sqrt(q0))^2`.

The .05/.05 test target requires `D_min>=1.66073` as a necessary condition. The bound must include baseline-control uncertainty in `d`; an untreated baseline alone does not bound post-treatment drift. If `b` is stable but its value is unknown, it can still be handled with multiple timepoints only to the extent that the signal trajectory has a component outside the nuisance direction.

Specifically, for `C_t~Pois(M_t[b+a r_t(theta)])` with one *stable but unknown* background parameter `b`, the local efficient Fisher information after eliminating `b` is

`I_(theta|b) = sum_t M_t a^2 (d_theta r_t)^2/(b+a r_t)`
`              - [sum_t M_t a (d_theta r_t)/(b+a r_t)]^2 / [sum_t M_t/(b+a r_t)]`.

When `b` dominates and `r_t=epsilon g_t`, this is approximately

`(a^2 epsilon^2/b) [sum_t M_t u_t^2 - (sum_t M_t u_t)^2/sum_t M_t]`,

where `u_t=d_theta g_t`. It is quadratic and equals zero for one observation or for a time-constant sensitivity direction. If there is a free `b_t` at every timepoint, nuisance projection removes all `theta` information in this additive channel. Hence a “matched baseline” helps only with a justified stability/variation bound or contemporaneous negative-control measurements.

## Typical samples can be zero despite positive Fisher information

With a background-free Poisson counter, `C~Pois(mu)` and `I_theta=mu (d_theta log mu)^2` is an **expectation over repeated samples**. If `mu<<1`, most individual samples contain no molecules and do not estimate the parameter. For zero versus a rare signal, `P(C=0|H1)=exp(-mu)`; at `mu=.05531`, the zero sample has likelihood ratio `exp(-.05531)=.946` against the alternative and occurs 94.6% of the time.

The 2026 primary plasma-input analysis combined 5,238 specimens from three cohorts and reported median input `5,531` genome equivalents; its evaluated evidentiary thresholds included 1, 3, 5, and 10 mutant molecules. Under the optimistic single-locus model `C~Pois(M f_VAF)`, the median input gives:

| Nominal molecule VAF | Mean mutant molecules | `P(C=0)` | `P(C>=1)` | `P(C>=3)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.1% (`10^-3`) | 5.531 | 0.00396 | 0.9960 | 0.9135 |
| 0.01% (`10^-4`) | 0.5531 | 0.5752 | 0.4248 | 0.01874 |
| 0.001% (`10^-5`) | 0.05531 | 0.9462 | 0.05381 | 0.0000271 |

At `0.01%`, 95% probability of seeing at least 1, 3, 5, or 10 mutant molecules requires mean counts `2.996, 6.296, 9.154, 15.705`, respectively. That corresponds to about `29,957, 62,958, 91,535, 157,052` independent genome equivalents at one locus. These ignore capture loss, error-control thresholds, and uncertain VAF; they are lower bounds. The source reports that fewer than 10% of its real samples satisfy any listed 95%-detection criterion at 0.01% VAF.

At a median-sized 5,531-GE sample, the two known-background Poisson laws with rates `b=10^-4` and `b+a epsilon=1.1*10^-4` have means `.5531` and `.60841`. Their exact `TV` is only `0.03095`; therefore every test has `alpha+beta>=0.96905` and equal-prior error at least `0.48453`. The Hellinger upper bound gives `TV<=0.03629`, consistent with this exact value. This is a finite-separation statement: nonzero expected Fisher information is not evidence that a typical sample resolves the change.

## ctDNA signal is not resistant-cell fraction

For lesion `ell` and clone `j`, define `G_(j,t)=sum_ell eta_(ell,j,t) N_(ell,j,t)`, the expected plasma genome-equivalent contribution after clone- and lesion-dependent release, vascular access, fragment recovery, and assay capture. Let `m_(j,t)` be mutant-copy multiplicity and `C_(j,t)` total copy number at the locus; let `G_(N,t)` be normal-cell genome equivalents. The physical VAF is

`v_t = [sum_j G_(j,t) m_(j,t)] / [2 G_(N,t) + sum_j G_(j,t) C_(j,t)]`.

For a private resistant-clone variant, only that clone enters the numerator. Thus VAF depends on tumor fraction, per-lesion/clone shedding, copy number and mutant multiplicity, and spatial representation. A linear model `b+a r` is an adequate local approximation only after these quantities have been measured or shown stable. Raw sequencing reads do not replace independent genome equivalents: PCR duplicates do not create additional tumor molecules.

There is an exact two-clone counterexample even with a perfect assay. Ignore normal cfDNA for the moment, take two diploid clones with a heterozygous variant private to `R`, and let `q=N_R/(N_R+N_S)` and `s=eta_R/eta_S`. Then

`VAF = s q / [2(s q + 1-q)]`.

At equal shedding, `q=0.20` gives VAF `0.10`, while `q=1/3` gives `1/6`. Holding the cellular composition at `q=0.20` but changing relative shedding to `s=2` also gives exactly `1/6`. A plasma VAF shift alone cannot distinguish cell selection from clone-specific release. Allowing normal cfDNA or copy-number/multiplicity changes adds further degrees of freedom.

This nuisance is observed rather than merely hypothetical. A primary single-cell/ctDNA study reports that a clone-specific ctDNA shedding index in a matched NSCLC patient was correlated with clone S-phase fraction; its direct ctDNA shedding comparison used one patient and four blood samples, so it establishes a failure mode, not a population calibration. A 2026 primary barcoding study directly compared spatial tumor pieces, needle biopsies, and plasma in six breast-cancer models and found clone patchiness, biopsy-location effects, and model-dependent shedding. A refuge with zero/unknown sampling or plasma inclusion probability makes the whole-tumor signal unbounded even if local assay error is zero.

## Final boundary and falsifier

The information-starvation frontier has three regimes:

1. **Calibrated signal-dominated molecules:** `a epsilon >> b` and stable `a,b`; required unique molecules scale at least as `epsilon^-1` for a fixed fractional alternative.
2. **Background-dominated molecules:** `a epsilon << b`, known/bounded background; required molecules scale at least as `b epsilon^-2/a^2`.
3. **Unbounded nuisance or mapping:** if two allowed nuisance paths make `b_0+a_0 r_0=b_1+a_1 r_1` at every observed time, the laws coincide and no amount of read depth identifies the clone-cell trajectory. With bounded drift the separation gate is `a epsilon |Delta g|>2d`; below it the composite hypotheses overlap.

The prospective discriminating check is an independent, held-out paired tissue/plasma set with per-sample GE, clone-specific CCF and marker copy/multiplicity, multi-region/lesion representation, matched normal/error controls, and repeated timepoints. Estimate the transfer residual

`d_t = logit(plasma clone share)_t - logit(tissue clone share)_t`

only where both fractions are estimable, and freeze an upper bound on its between-time drift before fitting clone-growth effects. If the allowed transfer drift (including tissue sampling uncertainty) reaches the hypothesized signal separation, declare the cell-fraction claim `UNKNOWN`, irrespective of nominal Fisher information. A plasma-only trajectory then identifies molecule-weighted clone abundance, not a whole-tumor resistant-cell fraction.
