2. Exact overlapping claims in the October 3 candidate

Model: stationary finite irreducible CTMC, deterministic two-label observation, ordinary time reversal, all microscopic variables even. The cost is the stationary path irreversibility rate
  sigma(Q) = sum_{i<j} (F_ij-F_ji) log(F_ij/F_ji),  F_ij=pi_i Q_ij.
Under local detailed balance this is the steady EPR in units k_B per time. The release’s theorem targets exact stationary two-label alternating-renewal laws that have at least one finite CTMC realization.

The path-comparison lemma is an exact match to N10 v1, Section 2, and root hidden_dissipation_v1:
  Q^dagger_ij = pi_j Q_ji/pi_i,  R=(Q+Q^dagger)/2,
  D(P_Q^[0,T] || P_R^[0,T]) <= T sigma(Q)/4.
PAPER.tex, Lemma “Dimension-free path comparison,” equations (the path comparison and random-clock comparison), and its proof around source lines 1360–1434 show that R has the same pi and statewise exit rates; the holding-time terms cancel; and the path-KL rate is sum_{i != j} F_ij log(2F_ij/(F_ij+F_ji)). Pairing both directions gives the scalar inequality
  x log(2x/(x+y)) + y log(2y/(x+y)) <= (x-y)log(x/y)/4.
With u=(x-y)/(x+y), the right minus left, divided by (x+y)/2, is -log(1-u^2)-u atanh(u), whose derivative is u/(1-u^2)-atanh(u) >= 0. This is the same symmetrization, same factor 1/4, and same weak-current sharpness mechanism reconstructed in N10 v1. Classification: independently rederived by N10/root, but already explicit in this dated candidate; do not present the symmetrization or factor-1/4 inequality as a new result.

The candidate’s quantitative observable is different in detail from N10 v1’s endpoint-pair table. Let B be a visible block, p its stationary mass, and H(t)=Pr(the process stays in B throughout a stationary interval of length t). Use two exponential clocks and ask whether the entire past/future intervals stayed in B, conditioned on Y_0 in B. Write a=E[z e^{-zE} persistence]/p=z Hhat(z)/p and b=E[z^2(E_-+E_+) persistence]/p=-z^2 Hhat'(z)/p. Under a reversible comparison, conditional past/future tests are independent and have the same conditional success probability given X_0, so b>=a^2. The manuscript proves the KL projection of a symmetric positively associated binary test law is minimized at the independent table with the target marginal, giving the test mutual information I(a,b), and then
  sigma >= 2 z p I(a,b)  when b<a^2.
Its deterministic-window form in the release is sigma >= (2p/t) I(a,b), with a=H(t)/p and b=H(2t)/p. These are uninterrupted-residence tests; equal endpoint labels are explicitly not substituted for persistence. The source also gives explicit polynomial/rational lower estimates on I and a simultaneous deterministic-error-box corollary, but expressly does not supply a dependence-aware confidence procedure or sample-size guarantee. Therefore the broad mechanism “a time-symmetric visible process can force a dimension-free positive EPR floor” and a fixed-window negative-covariance certificate are prior candidate results. N10 v1’s endpoint pair-MI inequality remains a distinct valid statistic/derivation, but its existence phenomenon is not a new principle.

The release’s main added theorem is stronger than the first Aznagulov preprint in one exact direction. For finite-realizable stationary ergodic two-label alternating-renewal P, it states
  V_infinity(P)=0  iff  both complete dwell laws are hyperexponential
  iff  P has a finite reversible exact realization.
The proof (PAPER.tex, Theorem 9 and proof around lines 1822–1899) takes any exact sequence Q_k with sigma(Q_k)->0, replaces each by its reversible additive symmetrization R_k, and uses the path-KL bound plus Pinsker to make the target persistence H_P uniformly close at each t to reversible persistence H_Rk. The latter is a positive finite spectral mixture from the self-adjoint killed generator on B. Compactness after x=e^{-lambda} yields a positive finite-measure limit. Since one fixed finite CTMC realizes P, H_P satisfies a constant-coefficient ODE by Cayley–Hamilton; uniqueness of Laplace transforms forces the limiting measure onto finitely many rates. The stationary dwell density then has a finite positive exponential-mixture form. Conversely, for two hyperexponential dwell laws, the paper gives a finite reversible bipartite reset construction with rates Q_{A_i B_j}=lambda_i beta_j and Q_{B_j A_i}=mu_j alpha_i and equal cross-edge stationary fluxes. Its worked q=1/20 ring has a certified, nonoptimal fixed-window floor >0.017568198744 nats/time. The paper explicitly says the optimum remains unknown and the result is a candidate awaiting independent review.

3. What the two Aznagulov v1 preprints do and do not establish

2609.14623v1 defines the exact-law finite-CTMC optimization V_n(P) and V_infinity(P) under ordinary reversal of even variables. Its Theorem 9 proves V_n(P_q)>0 for every feasible fixed finite state count n and every 0<q<1. The proof (HTML Section 8.8, equations 9.1–9.3) uses the reversible killed-block formula
  f_B(u)=r^{-1}<t,e^{u Q_BB}t>_{pi|B}
       = r^{-1} sum_l |<t,psi_l>|^2 e^{-lambda_l u},
so every finite reversible realization has a positive exponential-mixture dwell density. The q-family target has a negative residue in its rational dwell transform. The preprint then invokes its fixed-dimension attainment theorem to turn “no reversible realization” into V_n>0. It does not establish a floor uniform in n; it explicitly leaves open whether V_n tends to a positive number or to zero. This is exact antecedent for finite-dimensional impossibility, but not for the October 3 candidate’s all-dimension zero-cost dichotomy. The latter’s spectral-compactness/Cayley–Hamilton closure is an added proof in the inspected candidate.

2609.37205v1 studies a different finite-bandwidth task. It assumes a fully observed finite-generator irreversible alternative and a detector whose snapshots have gaps at least tau_0; the composite null consists of stationary observations of all finite reversible hidden chains with deterministic labels, arbitrary hidden dimension/rates/mixing (HTML lines 33–40). Two randomized exponential clocks recover exact generator identifiability at every finite gap (equations 6–7). For normal alternatives its sharp instance-wise snapshot cost is Theta(exp(2 gamma_irr tau_0) log(1/alpha)); its prescribed antisymmetric sampled-current test is controlled against every hidden reversible null, with no hidden mixing bound (abstract, equation 3, and equations 6–8). This is a finite-data distinguishability theorem, not an EPR lower-bound theorem. It does not resolve a full observed law that is exactly time-reversal invariant, and its “two clocks” are randomized sampling delays rather than the release’s two persistence indicators. It contains additive reversibilization in the generator notation, but the inspected v1 text does not give the path-KL <=T sigma/4 lemma used in the release.

4. Comparison with frozen N10 and root candidates

N10 v1. The central edge/path inequality is the same as the October 3 candidate and is reclassified as independently rederived prior-candidate material. Its 3-state singleton-observed driven ring is also an alternating-renewal law in the candidate’s target class. After rate rescaling, the ring a=10,b=1 is the same biased three-cycle family at q=1/10 (complementing the visible block only swaps the labels); the release’s theorem covers every 0<q<1 and its persistence certificate already proves a dimension-independent positive floor for this kind of reversible-output renewal target. Consequently N10 v1’s qualitative “reversible observed path but strictly positive required EPR” wedge is not a new discovery. N10 v1’s endpoint-pair certificate 4 I(Y_0;Y_t)/t is not the same formula as the release’s persistence-test certificate and can be retained as an independent alternative derivation, with no priority claim. The Hoeffding protocol in v1 uses N independent stationary pair preparations; the candidate release explicitly stops short of a statistical CI/sample-complexity method, so that operational calculation is not duplicated there, but it is a conventional concentration step and has not been established as an efficient assay.

N10 v2. The 20-state ring×telegraph construction and its six-time reflected covariance witness are not the release’s alternating-renewal theorem: the product-gated output has not been shown to have independent alternating dwell sequences, so do not silently apply the renewal classification to it. The release’s general persistence certificate may apply to other finite-realizable binary processes if their chosen persistence covariance is negative; that possibility must be tested before claiming the v2 example is outside all of the release’s quantitative coverage. What is distinct in v2 is the specific internally checked witness structure: the whole output law is reversible; every individual binary lag table has positive covariance and lies in the finite equilibrium pair-table closure; yet one six-time reflected Gram form is negative, excluding a common all-even equilibrium Markov completion. The release’s inspected proof tests uninterrupted persistence events and does not state this single-lag-closure versus common multi-time-semigroup construction.

Root v1–v2 midpoint and copositive inequalities. The release uses the same hidden midpoint conditional independence idea for two binary persistence tests and the same additive path-KL budget. Its proof applies directed KL from Q to R and the explicit information projection I(a,b). Root v2/v4 instead compares conditional forward and reversed-past half-path laws, uses their Jeffreys divergence and the sharp inequality J>=4 TV atanh(TV), then applies midpoint algebra. The formulas sigma T>=g(4 Delta/R^2) for a scalar reflected statistic and sigma T>=g(delta/M) for a symmetric copositive word witness do not appear in the three inspected sources. This is a genuine theorem-level extension relative to this bounded comparison, but it uses the same broad symmetrization/midpoint ingredients; no historical-priority claim is licensed.

Root v1/v2 reflected-word CP tests and v3 single-lag closure. The release’s binary conditional persistence calculation has a two-variable second-moment/positive-association consequence, but it does not state the finite-alphabet completely-positive cone as the exact closure of all finite reversible-CTMC lag-t tables at fixed observed marginal. Root v3’s reset-chain sufficiency proof for that one-lag closure and root’s arbitrary finite reflected-word CP/copositive certificates were not found in the audited paper or either Aznagulov v1. This is only a bounded negative search, not evidence of historical novelty. Classical CP/mixture-of-iid algebra and reflection positivity are known; the distinctive candidate is the CTMC closure/thermodynamic use and finite construction, still pending broader primary-literature review.

Root v5 standard-Borel extension. No such extension is asserted by the inspected finite-CTMC candidate or the two finite-generator preprints. Root v5 is conditional on its explicit pair of path-Jeffreys identities; those identities must be proved for each proposed infinite-state model. It remains an internal conditional candidate, with no priority or physical-generalization claim.

Do not infer global optimality, statistical calibration, empirical support, or priority from this audit.
