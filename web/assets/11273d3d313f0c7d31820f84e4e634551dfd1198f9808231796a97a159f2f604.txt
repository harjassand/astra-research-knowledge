Leaf 07 — Boltzmann-to-fluid limit: entropy does not control the energy flux
Date: 2026-10-08

STATUS
Elementary counterexample proved for a precise implication used in the relative-entropy/compactness route: relative entropy at the hydrodynamic scale, even with entropic convergence of the initial fluctuation to zero, does not bound or make uniformly integrable the order-three energy-flux moment. It therefore cannot by itself justify the energy-flux passage required for general (acoustic) initial data. This does not show that the incompressible Euler limit itself fails without the cited tail hypothesis; the constructed data are in fact well-prepared for the older velocity-only theorem. Historical novelty and external proof review: UNKNOWN.

1. Concrete model and source theorem

Take the normalized flat torus T^3=(R/Z)^3, with dx(T^3)=1, and M(v)=(2 pi)^(-3/2) exp(-|v|^2/2). Use the hard-sphere Grad-cutoff collision kernel

  b(z,omega)=|z dot omega|,  omega in S^2,
  v'=v-[(v-v_*) dot omega]omega,
  v_*'=v_*+[(v-v_*) dot omega]omega,

in the standard gain-minus-loss Boltzmann operator Q. This kernel is nonnegative, has at most linear relative-speed growth, and its angular integral is 2 pi |z|. The equation is

  epsilon partial_t f_epsilon + v dot grad_x f_epsilon
       = epsilon^(-2) Q(f_epsilon,f_epsilon),
  x in T^3, t>0, v in R^3,
  f_epsilon(0,x,v)=f_epsilon^in(x,v)>=0.

This is Saint-Raymond's incompressible Euler scaling with q=2 in epsilon partial_t f+v dot grad_x f=epsilon^(-q)Q: Mach number and Strouhal number are epsilon, and Knudsen number is epsilon^2. After division by epsilon, the transport/acoustic time is 1/epsilon and the collision prefactor is epsilon^(-3). The entropy inequality, when available, reads H(f_epsilon(t)|M)+epsilon^(-3) int_0^t int_T^3 D(f_epsilon) <= H(f_epsilon^in|M); after division by epsilon^2 the dissipation weight is epsilon^(-5).

Write

  H(f|M)=int_T^3 int_R^3 [f log(f/M)-f+M] dv dx,
  D(f)=1/4 int (f'f_*'-ff_*) log(f'f_*'/(ff_*)) b dv dv_* d omega.

The directly relevant primary-source result is Saint-Raymond, “Hydrodynamic limits: some improvements of the relative entropy method,” Theorem 2.1, Ann. Inst. H. Poincare Anal. Non Lineaire 26 (2009), 705–744, DOI 10.1016/j.anihpc.2008.01.001. In the paper's full kernel class, b is measurable and a.e. positive, satisfies 0<b(z,omega)<=C_b(1+|z|)^beta and int_S2 b(z,omega)domega >= C_b^(-1)|z|/(1+|z|), for beta in [0,1]. The hard-sphere kernel above is an instance. Its assumptions include, as epsilon tends to zero:

  (a) epsilon^(-2) H(f_epsilon^in|M) <= C_in;
  (b) g_epsilon^in=(f_epsilon^in/M-1)/epsilon converges weakly locally to g^in and
      epsilon^(-2) H(f_epsilon^in|M) -> (1/2) int_T^3 int_R^3 M |g^in|^2 dv dx;
  (c) the solution family obeys int_R^3 M ((f_epsilon-M)/M)^2 dv <= C
      for almost every (t,x), uniformly in epsilon.

For q>1 (we fix q=2), that theorem gives relative weak compactness of g_epsilon in L^1_loc(dt dx;L^1(dv)). Put rho^in=int M g^in dv, u^in=int v M g^in dv, theta^in=(1/3)int (|v|^2-3)M g^in dv, and let P be the Helmholtz projection onto divergence-free vector fields. Every limit is an infinitesimal Maxwellian

  g=u dot v + (theta/2)(|v|^2-5),

where u is a Lipschitz incompressible Euler solution and theta is transported by u, with u(0)=P u^in and theta(0)=(3 theta^in-2 rho^in)/5. The fast acoustic corrector has initial fields (rho^in+theta(0), u^in-Pu^in, theta^in-theta(0)). The theorem states strong L^1_loc convergence after subtracting that corrector. On T^3 there is no boundary condition. The theorem is conditional on the target solution's lifespan. Assumption (c) is a separately imposed solution-level large-velocity control. It is not among the initial-data assumptions (a)–(b); this construction shows their failure to imply the analogous bound at t=0, while positive-time regularization is unresolved here.

The same paper’s proof chain is explicit. The entropy inequality gives a uniform normalized entropy and, after division by epsilon^2, an epsilon^(-q-3) entropy-dissipation budget. A modulated entropy inequality compares f_epsilon with a local-Maxwellian approximation containing the acoustic and relaxation-layer corrections. The moment equations then require control of the energy flux (a third velocity moment) for general initial data. Saint-Raymond states that relative entropy alone cannot control g_epsilon |v|^3, and uses a Chapman–Enskog-type decomposition plus the extra tail control and entropy dissipation; in the proof, the non-equilibrium flux is bounded by entropy and dissipation terms, with a vanishing factor epsilon^((q-1)/2). For q=2 this factor is epsilon^(1/2). Compactness and the moment equations then identify Euler plus the transported thermal mode and acoustic corrector. The Lipschitz bound is used in the modulated-entropy/Gronwall closure.

2. Explicit counterexample to the omitted flux inference

For every sufficiently small epsilon, set

  a(x)=1+(1/2) sin(2 pi x_1),
  R_epsilon=epsilon^(-4),
  delta_epsilon(x)=epsilon^12 a(x),
  U_epsilon(x)=-delta_epsilon(x) R_epsilon/(1-delta_epsilon(x)) e_1,
  T_epsilon(x)=1-delta_epsilon(x)R_epsilon^2/[3(1-delta_epsilon(x))^2].

Write N(U,T I) for the density of a 3D Gaussian with mean U and covariance T I, and define the smooth positive initial datum

  f_epsilon^in(x,v)=(1-delta_epsilon(x)) N(U_epsilon(x),T_epsilon(x)I)(v)
                         +delta_epsilon(x) N(R_epsilon e_1,I)(v).

For small epsilon, T_epsilon>0 and uniformly T_epsilon=1+O(epsilon^4), U_epsilon=O(epsilon^8). Direct Gaussian moment calculation gives, for each x,

  int f_epsilon^in dv=1,
  int v f_epsilon^in dv=0,
  int |v|^2 f_epsilon^in dv=3.

Thus all local hydrodynamic fields initially equal those of M. The relative entropy H_x= int f_epsilon^in log(f_epsilon^in/M) dv is bounded using convexity of relative entropy under mixtures:

  H_x <= (1-delta) D(N(U,T I)||M)+delta D(N(R e_1,I)||M)
      <= C (|U|^2+|T-1|^2)+delta R^2/2
      <= C epsilon^4,

uniformly in x. Here D(N(U,T I)||M)=|U|^2/2+(3/2)(T-1-log T), and D(N(R e_1,I)||M)=R^2/2. Hence epsilon^(-2)H(f_epsilon^in|M)=O(epsilon^2)->0.

Also ||f_epsilon^in(x,.)-M||_L1(dv)<=C epsilon^4 uniformly in x: the core Gaussian changes by O(epsilon^4) in total variation and the tail has mass O(epsilon^12). On every compact velocity set M is bounded below, so g_epsilon^in=(f_epsilon^in/M-1)/epsilon ->0 strongly in L^1_loc(dx dv). Therefore these data converge entropically at order epsilon to g^in=0, in precisely the initial-data sense of condition (b).

Nevertheless their energy flux is large. For V distributed as N(U,T I), E[V|V|^2]=U(|U|^2+5T). Consequently

  J_epsilon(x):=int (|v|^2/2)v f_epsilon^in(x,v) dv
       =(1/2)delta_epsilon R_epsilon
          [R_epsilon^2+5(1-T_epsilon)-|U_epsilon|^2] e_1
       =(a(x)/2)e_1+O(epsilon^12).

The Maxwellian has zero energy flux. Thus the third moment of the fluctuation is

  int (|v|^2/2)v M g_epsilon^in dv = J_epsilon/epsilon
       = a(x)e_1/(2 epsilon)+O(epsilon^11),

which diverges. This is divergence in distributions in x as well: with phi(x)=cos(2 pi x_1),

  int_T^3 [int (|v|^2/2)v M g_epsilon^in dv] dot grad(phi) dx
       = -pi/(4 epsilon)+O(epsilon^11).

So neither weak local convergence of g_epsilon^in nor entropic convergence at order epsilon implies even boundedness of this energy-flux pairing. In particular there is no estimate of this flux by a quantity tending to zero with epsilon^(-2)H alone. The tail carries physical energy only epsilon^4, below the epsilon^2 entropy scale, but its speed epsilon^(-4) amplifies the third moment enough to leave an O(epsilon^(-1)) fluctuation flux.

The initial-time analogue of the theorem’s pointwise L^2(M^{-1}) tail condition (c) fails dramatically for this family. Since f_epsilon^in >= delta N(R e_1,I),

  int M ((f_epsilon^in-M)/M)^2 dv
     = int (f_epsilon^in)^2/M dv -1
     >= delta^2 exp(R^2)-1
     >= (epsilon^24/4) exp(epsilon^(-8))-1 -> infinity.

This pins down what the added condition buys in the proof: the energy-flux compactness step does not follow from initial entropy convergence alone. The theorem's bound is imposed on the solution for almost every positive time, so the initial-time calculation by itself does not prove that an actual solution violates that solution-level hypothesis after an instantaneous relaxation. For a classical solution starting from these smooth data, the local energy identity would give epsilon partial_t E_epsilon+div_x J_epsilon=0 and hence an initial derivative of size epsilon^(-1); this is the corresponding initial-layer signal. The derivative statement is conditional on a solution class with local energy balance. The counterexample itself is only the explicit initial-data/moment obstruction, and does not assume a classical solution exists uniformly in epsilon.

3. Comparison and scope control

The older primary theorem by Saint-Raymond, “Convergence of Solutions to the Boltzmann Equation in the Incompressible Euler Limit,” Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. 166 (2003), 47–80, DOI 10.1007/s00205-002-0228-3, handles well-prepared data without an additional tail assumption: for smooth divergence-free u^in, it assumes epsilon^(-2)H(f_epsilon^in|M_{1,epsilon u^in,1})->0 and renormalized solutions of the same epsilon-scaled equation with q>1, and identifies the weak fluctuation limit u dot v on the lifespan of the Lipschitz Euler solution. The 2009 theorem weakens the initial profile restrictions to admit acoustic waves and a transported thermal field, and gives a stronger corrected convergence statement, but in exchange assumes the nonuniform high-velocity control (c). Our constructed data satisfy the 2003 well-prepared entropy hypothesis with u^in=0; therefore this counterexample is consistent with that theorem. It does not show Euler convergence fails. It shows why the broader energy-flux argument cannot silently omit its tail/integrability input.

Falsifier for the narrow claim: an error in the explicit entropy or Gaussian moment calculations above, or a proof that (a)–(b) alone uniformly bound the displayed distributional energy-flux pairing, would falsify this result. The latter is ruled out by the constructed sequence if its algebra is correct. A separate theorem identifying the same general-data limit while directly handling this sequence could show that condition (c) is not necessary for the limit itself; that remains open here. Whether the actual solution from this datum instantaneously acquires a positive-time bound of type (c) is also open in this leaf.

4. Scaling, precision, and dependency ledger

  Fluid scaling: Mach/Stokes parameter epsilon; Knudsen number epsilon^2; collision exponent q=2.
  Collision entropy budget: epsilon^(-3) int D before fluctuation normalization; epsilon^(-5) int D after division by epsilon^2.
  Tail scale: speed R=epsilon^(-4); local mass delta=epsilon^12 a(x); tail energy delta R^2=epsilon^4 a(x).
  Entropy precision: H=O(epsilon^4), hence H/epsilon^2=O(epsilon^2)->0.
  Flux precision: physical energy flux J=O(1); fluctuation flux J/epsilon=O(epsilon^(-1)); fixed smooth test pairing is -pi/(4 epsilon)+o(1).
  Tail-condition diagnostic: relative chi-square >= (epsilon^24/4) exp(epsilon^(-8))-1.
  Proof’s dissipation gain: epsilon^((q-1)/2)=epsilon^(1/2) for q=2, conditional on its tail and solution hypotheses.
  No finite-precision computation, solver, archive, or numerical diagnostic is used. All displayed costs are asymptotic powers or exact Gaussian identities.
