N17 — Phase-space mixing spectra and the identifiability boundary
Version: v1 (frozen first derivation; 2026-10-09)
Status: internally derived conditional results; no empirical validation of a new discriminator.

TARGET

Can collisionless phase-space structure provide a model-independent, sharp distinction between dark-matter microphysics and baryonic feedback, beyond density-profile fits? The proposed route is to replace a central proxy such as rho/sigma^3 by the full excess-mass spectrum of the dark-matter distribution function, and to determine exactly which parts of that spectrum are invariant under arbitrary gravitational forcing.

Result: the spectrum gives an exact one-sided test of specified collisionless warm-relic models if the dark-matter distribution itself is measured or otherwise bounded. Fine-grained Casimirs also give an in-principle test for collisional evolution if the initial and final dark-matter distributions are known. Neither is an observable of stellar tracers. All stellar phase-space responses factor through the total gravitational-potential history and tracer birth/selection model. Therefore a stellar-only snapshot, even with complete positions and velocities, cannot provide a universal classifier of SIDM versus feedback or of warm versus cold initial conditions. It can constrain the total potential under additional equilibrium assumptions, and age-resolved tracer histories can constrain potential evolution, but neither labels the dark-matter collision operator without extra inputs.

This is a scoped identifiability result, not a claim that SIDM and feedback are indistinguishable in every dataset. With fixed baryonic histories, fixed initial conditions, and a measured time-dependent total potential, particular models can predict different tracer distributions. The theorem below says the model label is not encoded in a gravity-only tracer observable when those histories produce the same total potential and the same tracer initial data.

2. REPRESENTATION: THE DARK-MATTER EXCESS-MASS SPECTRUM

Let f(x,v) be a mass phase-space density, normalized so that rho(x) = integral f(x,v) d^3v. For every threshold eta >= 0 define

    D_f(eta) = integral_Gamma (f(x,v) - eta)_+ d^3x d^3v,
    (q)_+ = max(q,0).

D_f(eta) has units of mass. It is the total excess mass in phase-space cells whose density exceeds eta. At regular thresholds,

    -dD_f/deta = Vol_Gamma{(x,v): f(x,v) > eta}.

Thus the full curve D_f(eta) gives the phase-space volume above every density threshold, and hence the histogram of phase-space density values. It does not retain where those values sit in phase-space; it is complete only modulo measure-preserving rearrangements. This is precisely the quotient relevant to collisionless mixing.

The statistic is stronger than one number such as rho/sigma^3: it retains every threshold eta. For a normalized positive coarse-graining kernel K, f_bar = K*f, convexity of C_eta(q)=(q-eta)_+ and Jensen's inequality give

    D_fbar(eta) <= D_f(eta), for every eta,

provided coarse-graining preserves phase-space measure and the same full domain is included. This inequality is componentwise in eta, not merely an entropy or central-density comparison.

3. EXACT COLLISIONLESS DERIVATION, INCLUDING BARYONIC FEEDBACK

For collisionless dark matter,

    partial_t f + {f,H} = 0,       H(x,v,t) = |v|^2/2 + Phi_tot(x,t),

where { , } is the canonical Poisson bracket. Phi_tot may be time-dependent and may include an arbitrarily time-dependent baryonic contribution. Hamiltonian flow has zero phase-space divergence. For differentiable C with C(0)=0 and vanishing boundary flux,

    d/dt integral C(f) dGamma
      = - integral C'(f) {f,H} dGamma
      = - integral {C(f),H} dGamma
      = 0.

Approximating C_eta(q)=(q-eta)_+ by smooth convex functions yields

    D_f(t)(eta) = D_f(0)(eta)    for every eta

for the fine-grained collisionless distribution. A time-dependent feedback potential changes orbital energies and spatial density, but it does not change this fine-grained level-set spectrum. If only a coarse-grained final f is available, phase mixing gives

    D_fbar(t)(eta) <= D_f(0)(eta).

This remains true for arbitrary gravitational forcing by baryons. It is not a statement that a central density or a moment proxy is conserved: those can change substantially under collisionless evolution. The theorem is about the full fine-grained distribution, or the one-sided coarse-grained inequality.

An illustrative two-cell mixing calculation makes the one-sided nature explicit. Start with phase-space densities (2,0) on two cells of unit volume and mix them to (1,1). For 0 < eta < 1, D_initial=2-eta and D_final=2(1-eta), so D_final-D_initial=-eta. For 1 <= eta < 2, D_initial=2-eta and D_final=0. The spectrum falls under collisionless coarse mixing; this fall is not evidence for SIDM.

4. QUANTITATIVE WARM-INITIAL-CONDITION WITNESS

For a fermionic relic whose primordial occupation per quantum state is bounded by q_max, the mass phase-space density in velocity coordinates obeys

    f_mass <= f_max = g q_max m_chi^4 / h^3,

where g is the internal-state degeneracy, m_chi the particle mass, and h Planck's constant. For a zero-chemical-potential Fermi-Dirac relic, q_max=1/2, so

    f_max = g m_chi^4 / (2 h^3)
          = g m_chi^4 / [2 (2 pi hbar)^3].

The factor m_chi^4 follows from d^3p=m_chi^3 d^3v and multiplying number density by particle mass. Since collisionless Hamiltonian transport preserves f and coarse-graining averages it,

    f_bar(x,v) <= f_max,
    D_fbar(eta) = 0 for every eta >= f_max.

Therefore, if the dark-matter f_bar were measured over a specified resolution kernel and its lower confidence bound had positive excess mass above the model's f_max, that fermionic relic model would be ruled out independently of any baryonic feedback history. This is a one-sided, sharp falsifier for the stated production model. It does not positively identify warm dark matter when the bound is respected: cold dark matter can also be mixed to low f, and warm models with different production functions have different q_max or transfer functions.

Common proxy caveat. For an isotropic Maxwellian with one-dimensional dispersion s,

    f(0) = rho / (2 pi s^2)^(3/2),
    rho/s^3 = (2 pi)^(3/2) f(0).

The conversion is an isotropic-Maxwellian assumption, not an identity for an arbitrary halo. For an anisotropic Gaussian with principal dispersions s_1,s_2,s_3,

    f(0) = rho / [(2 pi)^(3/2) s_1 s_2 s_3].

A line-of-sight dispersion alone does not determine the product s_1 s_2 s_3. Consequently a measured rho/sigma_los^3 is not a model-independent estimate of the maximum six-dimensional f. Full velocity-tensor information and a distribution-function model would be needed even before comparing it with f_max.

5. WHAT SIDM CHANGES, AND WHAT WOULD BE REQUIRED TO SEE IT

For elastic self-scattering, write

    partial_t f + {f,H} = C[f].

The collisionless Casimirs are no longer conserved in general:

    d/dt integral C_0(f) dGamma = integral C_0'(f) C[f] dGamma.

For a closed classical elastic Boltzmann system with microreversible kernel B >= 0, its fine-grained Boltzmann entropy

    S[f] = -k_B integral f ln(f/f_ref) dGamma

obeys the H-theorem

    dS/dt = (k_B/4) integral d^3x d^3v_1 d^3v_2 dOmega
              B (f_1 f_2 - f'_1 f'_2)
                ln[(f_1 f_2)/(f'_1 f'_2)] >= 0.

The sign follows from (a-b) ln(a/b) >= 0. Equality holds when the local velocity distribution is Maxwellian (or when the collision rate vanishes). Collisionless gravitational evolution instead conserves this fine-grained integral exactly. In principle, a strictly positive fine-grained entropy production measured for the dark matter over a known closed interval is inconsistent with pure Vlasov evolution, while baryonic gravity alone cannot produce it.

This is not currently a stellar-tracer observable. A finite-resolution estimate of S or D also rises/falls under collisionless filamentation and coarse-graining. A valid test would require: (i) direct dark-matter phase-space information at two times or a known initial f; (ii) control of phase-space resolution and mixing; (iii) accounting for particles crossing the chosen spatial/velocity boundary; and (iv) exclusion of non-gravitational sources or sinks. Even then, entropy production identifies collisional/non-Hamiltonian evolution, not a unique value or velocity dependence of sigma/m without a specified collision kernel.

Exact finite snapshot counterexample. Let f_P(x,v) be the isotropic Plummer equilibrium, with rho(r)=3M[1+r^2/a^2]^(-5/2)/(4 pi a^3), Phi(r)=-GM/sqrt(r^2+a^2), relative binding energy E_b=-[|v|^2/2+Phi(r)] and mass DF

    f_P(E_b) = (24 sqrt(2)/(7 pi^3)) [a^2/(G^5 M^4)] E_b^(7/2), E_b>0,
    f_P=0, E_b<=0.

This has finite total mass M. Use the same f_P as Cauchy data at time t0 in a Vlasov model and an elastic-SIDM Boltzmann model with nonzero cross section. The two models have exactly the same f, rho, and Phi at t0; f_P is valid instantaneous initial data for both, though C[f_P] will generally be nonzero and the SIDM model will evolve away from it. Thus even a perfect single-epoch measurement of the full dark-matter f cannot identify the collision operator from the state alone; temporal information or an independently specified history is needed. This example is not a claim that a Plummer halo is a stationary SIDM equilibrium.

Equilibrium caveat. A local Maxwellian f=A(x) exp[-|v-u|^2/(2s^2)] is both a collisionless stationary DF when it is a function of the integrals of motion and a null state of an elastic collision operator by detailed balance. The self-gravitating isothermal sphere has infinite mass when extended to infinity. A Maxwellian in a finite reflecting box is an exact all-time shared equilibrium, but the reflecting boundary is artificial for an isolated halo. A lowered/truncated-isothermal King DF is not generally a collisional null state because elastic collisions can repopulate the truncated velocities. No finite isolated-halo equilibrium conclusion is inferred from these idealized constructions.

Finite-halo weak-scattering limit. In a prescribed, finite gravitational potential, let a particle's integrated collision hazard be

    tau_coll = integral_0^T n_chi(x(t)) <sigma_tot(v_rel) v_rel> dt.

Its probability of at least one scatter is 1-exp(-tau_coll) <= tau_coll. Couple an SIDM trajectory and a collisionless trajectory from the same initial point so that they agree until the first collision. The coupling inequality gives total-variation distance between their one-particle endpoint distributions <= 1-exp(-tau_coll) <= tau_coll. Thus for tau_coll << 1, every bounded one-particle phase-space statistic differs by at most 2 tau_coll in expectation under the same prescribed potential. This is an exact single-particle bound for that setting. It is not a uniform theorem for a self-gravitating population whose changed density significantly changes Phi; applying it to a halo requires a stability/response bound on that potential. It does show why no finite-precision universal discriminator can be uniform down to arbitrarily small SIDM cross section in regular, weakly collisional systems.

6. STELLAR-TRACER FACTORIZATION AND THE NO-GO BOUNDARY

For a collisionless stellar cohort a born at time t_a,

    partial_t f_*,a + v dot grad_x f_*,a
                 - grad_x Phi_tot dot grad_v f_*,a = 0.

If two dark-matter models produce the same Phi_tot(x,t) over the cohort's history, and the cohort has the same birth DF and survival law, uniqueness of Hamiltonian transport gives exactly the same f_*,a at the observation time. The measured catalogue has the form

    g_a(y) = integral K_a(y|z) q_a(z) f_*,a(z,t0) dz,

where z=(x,v), q_a is the selection probability, and K_a includes projection and measurement errors. Matched selection and measurement kernels therefore give exactly the same g_a. Stellar data depend on the DM microphysics only through its contribution to Phi_tot(t), plus any assumed coupling beyond gravity. They do not directly observe C[f_chi], f_chi(x,v), or its Casimirs.

The implication is conditional but decisive: if SIDM+feedback and collisionless-DM+feedback realize the same total potential history, no gravity-only stellar tracer statistic can separate them. If their potential histories differ and birth distributions are constrained, the tracer distributions can differ; that is a model-specific history test, not an invariant microphysics label. At a single epoch, the Plummer construction above gives exact overlap even for direct f_chi measurements. Stellar ages or multiple populations help only after birth distributions, star-formation history, selection, and potential evolution are constrained independently.

7. MASS-ANISOTROPY, EQUILIBRIUM, AND FULL POSITION-VELOCITY DATA

For a spherical, steady, non-streaming tracer with number density nu(r), radial dispersion sigma_r^2, and beta(r)=1-sigma_t^2/(2 sigma_r^2), the Jeans equation gives

    M(<r) = - r sigma_r^2/G
             [d ln(nu sigma_r^2)/d ln r + 2 beta(r)].

The line-of-sight second moment is

    Sigma(R) sigma_los^2(R)
      = 2 integral_R^infty [1-beta(r) R^2/r^2]
             nu(r) sigma_r^2(r) r dr / sqrt(r^2-R^2).

With only nu and sigma_los, M, beta, and sigma_r are not separately fixed: choose an admissible beta and solve the Jeans/projection system, or choose another admissible beta and obtain another mass profile fitting the same projected moments. This is the standard mass-anisotropy obstruction. Two chemo-dynamical populations add constraints and can estimate a mass slope under equilibrium/sphericity assumptions; they still measure a common gravitational potential, not the DM collision operator.

If all three velocity components and the 3D tracer density are measured perfectly, beta and sigma_r can be formed directly. Under the spherical steady assumptions, the Jeans equation then fixes the total enclosed mass. This can remove the usual moment-level mass-anisotropy degeneracy. It still does not reveal whether that mass was produced by a collisionless or self-interacting dark component with the same density, nor does it determine the dark-matter velocity DF. Baryonic mass subtraction and stellar mass-to-light ratios remain additional inputs.

The equilibrium assumption is itself testable only with additional information. Away from equilibrium, the time-dependent collisionless Boltzmann equation contains partial_t f_* and streaming terms. One stellar snapshot does not supply partial_t f_*. A sufficiently complete time series could infer acceleration from the CBE where grad_v f_* spans the required directions, but it would infer grad Phi_tot, not the DM collision operator. Age-selected cohorts do not by themselves provide a known initial DF; they have different birth conditions and selection functions.

8. SELECTION AND EXTREMAL CASES

The observation operator is non-injective whenever q_a(z)=0 on any region: changing f_* there leaves g_a unchanged. Projection from 6D to sky position and line-of-sight velocity adds further null directions. If selection is known and strictly positive on the region of interest and full 6D data are available, selection correction may recover the stellar DF on that support; it still does not recover f_chi. If q_a is unknown, an apparent deficit of central or high-velocity tracers can be either selection or dynamics.

Limiting cases that block a universal classifier:

- Warm bound inactive: if all directly measured coarse f lies far below f_max, the fermionic occupancy inequality excludes nothing; cold DM can produce the same low coarse f through mixing.
- Anisotropic velocity ellipsoid: rho/sigma_los^3 does not determine f_max without the two transverse velocity dispersions.
- Known spherical equilibrium and full 3D stellar velocities: total mass may be identifiable while dark-matter microphysics remains unidentified.
- Unknown equilibrium status: a steady Jeans inversion can be biased by time-dependent streaming or tidal debris.
- Empty selection support: no inference can distinguish models in the unobserved region.
- Exact present-state overlap: the same finite Plummer f_chi at one epoch is valid data for both collisionless and SIDM kinetic equations; only the time derivative differs.
- SIDM equilibrium: entropy production is zero in a local Maxwellian state, so absence of a measured entropy change does not rule out SIDM.

Primary phase-space occupancy argument from Vlasov evolution. The explicit f_max formula here is stated for a specified fermionic primordial occupation; it is not universal across warm-DM production mechanisms.
- Walker & Peñarrubia (2011), “A Method for Measuring (Slopes of) the Mass Profiles of Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies,” ApJ 742, 20, https://doi.org/10.1088/0004-637X/742/1/20. Uses chemo-dynamically distinct stellar populations to estimate an enclosed-mass slope under spherical equilibrium; this supports the limited statement that multiple populations constrain gravity, not particle microphysics directly.
- Diakogiannis, Lewis & Ibata (2014), “Resolving the mass–anisotropy degeneracy of the spherically symmetric Jeans equation – I. Theoretical foundation,” MNRAS 443, 598–609, https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stu1153. Their equations make explicit that a projected second-moment fit depends on assumed mass and anisotropy; their reconstruction is unique only for the assumed mass model. This supports the scoped Jeans statement above.

Their 16 isolated-dwarf hydrodynamic simulations report overlapping core outcomes for combinations of SIDM and feedback, and candidate differences in stellar/gas histories within that suite. This is simulation evidence about that model family, not empirical confirmation or a universal signature.
- Hayashi et al. (2021), “Probing dark matter self-interaction with ultrafaint dwarf galaxies,” Phys. Rev. D 103, 023017, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.103.023017. Their analysis of 23 UFDs found no object preferring nonzero SIDM in their phenomenological halo model, with some model-dependent constraints. This is an empirical example of current stellar-kinematic inference depending on halo modeling, not a general exclusion of SIDM.

10. ALTERNATIVES, GAPS, AND STRONGEST SUPPORTED CONSEQUENCES

Alternative A — use central Q=rho/sigma^3. Rejected as a universal statistic: it assumes a relation between a low-order velocity moment and the maximum of a six-dimensional distribution. The anisotropic-Gaussian counterexample above makes the missing information explicit. The D-spectrum is the mathematically correct level-set object, but it requires f_chi.

Alternative B — distinguish SIDM by an isothermal DM core versus a feedback-made core. Rejected as model-independent: density and even stellar-response differences depend on cross section, velocity dependence, star-formation history, halo assembly, and selection. The primary Burger et al. simulation suite reports both overlap and some conditional history differences; those results motivate model-specific tests only.

Alternative C — use stellar action diffusion as an SIDM signature. Actions are approximately invariant under slow potential evolution and can change under impulsive changes, but both feedback and SIDM can produce evolving Phi_tot. The action statistic therefore classifies a potential history only after the baryonic forcing and birth actions are constrained. It does not isolate C[f_chi] without those controls.

Strongest quantitative consequences actually established here:

1. Direct-DM warm relic test: if a measured coarse-grained D_f(eta) is strictly positive for any eta >= g m_chi^4/[2 h^3] for a zero-chemical-potential fermionic relic, that specified collisionless relic is impossible under the stated kinetic assumptions, regardless of gravitational baryonic feedback.
2. Direct-DM SIDM test: if an independently known initial state evolves with strictly positive fine-grained Boltzmann entropy production in a closed system, pure Hamiltonian collisionless evolution is excluded. This does not yield sigma/m without a kernel and cannot be inferred from coarse stellar data.
3. Stellar-tracer no-go: matched total potential histories and matched tracer birth/selection inputs yield exactly the same stellar phase-space observables for any two dark-sector models. Even exact equilibrium 6D stellar kinematics can at best constrain total gravity and its mass decomposition given baryonic inputs.

Unresolved: any practical route to a true 6D dark-matter distribution; whether a realistic finite halo's potential response to SIDM can be bounded tightly enough to turn the weak-scattering coupling estimate into a self-consistent stellar prediction; and whether a specific observed sample has enough independent baryon-history information to test a restricted SIDM-versus-feedback model family. No claim of an achieved observational discriminator is supported.
