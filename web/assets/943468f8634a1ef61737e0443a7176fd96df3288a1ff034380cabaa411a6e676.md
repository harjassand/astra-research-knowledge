# Cycle 2: lattice saturation, Markov connectivity, and N77 escape

## Finding

The reversible detailed-balanced completion in the metabolic-thermo candidate retains the N77 escape mechanism. Its reaction-vector lattice is already saturated, so lattice saturation alone does not remove hidden stochastic components. The decisive distinction is between (a) generating the full integer stoichiometric lattice and (b) having feasible reaction moves connect every nonnegative integer fiber. N77 satisfies the first and fails the second because its complexes impose catalyst/availability thresholds.

A sufficient, algebraically checkable strengthening is: the reaction set contains a **catalyst-free reversible Markov basis** for the saturated integer stoichiometric lattice. Then every integer fiber inside a real stoichiometric class is one irreducible CTMC class. If that class is positive recurrent, it has one invariant probability, so there cannot be a family of stationary laws indexed by hidden components of that deterministic class. This connectivity condition proves no Lyapunov drift, nonexplosion, positive recurrence, or quantitative tail estimate. Across a family of rate constants, stationary laws can still escape even with the same Markov-basis reaction vectors.

## Independent reconstruction of the reversible candidate

Use internal species (A,B,C,D) and the four reversible pairs, with all effective directional constants equal to one:

\[
2C\rightleftarrows A+B+C,\qquad
A+B+C\rightleftarrows 3A+2B,\qquad
3A+2B\rightleftarrows 2C,\qquad
2C\rightleftarrows2C+D.
\]

The net reaction vectors for the displayed forward directions are

\[
\nu_1=(1,1,-1,0),\quad \nu_2=(2,1,-1,0),\quad
\nu_3=(-3,-2,2,0),\quad \nu_4=(0,0,0,1),
\]

with \(\nu_1+\nu_2+\nu_3=0\). The rank is three, and the only real linear conservation is \(B+C\). The unit activity vector is detailed balanced for every reversible pair.

For each integer \(n\ge0\), define

\[
\Gamma_n=\{(n,0,2,d),(n+1,1,1,d),(n+3,2,0,d):d\in\mathbb N_0\}.
\]

On the fiber \(B+C=2\), write \(b=B\), so \(b\in\{0,1,2\}\) and \(C=2-b\). The only enabled main-network phase moves are:

- \(b=0\): \(2C\to A+B+C\) sends \(A\mapsto A+1,b\mapsto1\), and the reverse of \(3A+2B\rightleftarrows2C\) sends \(A\mapsto A+3,b\mapsto2\);
- \(b=1\): the first pair's reverse sends \((A,b)\mapsto(A-1,0)\) when \(A\ge1\), and the second pair's forward direction sends \((A,b)\mapsto(A+2,2)\) when \(A\ge1\);
- \(b=2\): the second pair's reverse and the third pair's forward direction are enabled when \(A\ge3\), returning to phases \(b=1) and \(b=0\), respectively.

For \(H(A,B)=A-B(B+1)/2\), each of those enabled moves preserves \(H\), including the reverse directions; the fourth pair changes only \(D\). For \(n\ge0\), the three nonnegative phase populations with \(H=n\) are exactly \((n,0,2),(n+1,1,1),(n+3,2,0)\). The phase edges are bidirectional and connect all three phases. At phase zero, \(D\) has immigration rate 2 and death rate \(2d\), so every \(d\) communicates with every other \(d\). Therefore each \(\Gamma_n\) is closed and irreducible.

For fixed \(n\), phase-change rates are bounded, while D-births have rate at most 2 and D-deaths cannot increase D. Thus births are dominated by a rate-2 Poisson process and there are finitely many phase jumps on bounded time intervals; the chain is nonexplosive.

The measure \(\rho(x)=\prod_i1/x_i!\) is reversible for the all-unit stochastic mass-action rates. Indeed, for \(x'=x-y+y'\),

\[
\rho(x)\,x^{\underline y}=\frac1{(x-y)!}
=\frac1{(x'-y')!}=\rho(x')\,(x')^{\underline {y'}}.
\]

Restricting \(\rho\) to \(\Gamma_n\) and normalizing gives a stationary probability. The phase weights, after removing the common factor \(1/n!\), are

\[
\left(\frac12,\frac1{n+1},
\frac1{2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}\right),
\]

and \(D\) has independent \(\operatorname{Poisson}(1)\) marginal. Irreducibility and nonexplosion then give positive recurrence and uniqueness on \(\Gamma_n\). Since \(A\ge n\) on \(\Gamma_n\), this stationary family is not tight: for every compact \(K\subset\mathbb R^4\), its A-coordinate is bounded, so \(\pi_n(K)=0\) for all sufficiently large \(n\).

### Exactness inside the fiber \(B+C=2\)

The \(\Gamma_n\), \(n\ge0\), are closed and contain all states in this fiber with \(H\ge0\). They do **not** exhaust the whole fiber. For every \(d\ge0\), the four states

\[
(0,1,1,d),\quad (0,2,0,d),\quad (1,2,0,d),\quad (2,2,0,d)
\]

have no enabled reaction and are separate absorbing singleton classes. This is because the middle complex needs at least one A, the \(3A+2B\) complex needs at least three A, and the D-exchange pair needs two C. There are other communicating classes outside the fiber \(B+C=2\), since the full nonnegative state space has fibers with other values of \(B+C\). Thus the stated \(\Gamma_n\) family is a closed infinite family in one deterministic class, not an exhaustive list of all global closed classes.

## Saturation is already true and still fails

Let \(L=\sum_r\mathbb Z\nu_r\) be the reaction lattice and \(S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\nu_r\}\). The integer kernel of the primitive conservation row \(K=(0,1,1,0)\) is

\[
M=\ker_{\mathbb Z}K=\{(p,q,-q,r):p,q,r\in\mathbb Z\}=S\cap\mathbb Z^4.
\]

Every \(\nu_r\) lies in \(M\). The minor of the stoichiometric matrix using rows \((A,B,D)\) and columns \((\nu_1,\nu_2,\nu_4)\) is \(-1\). Hence the rank-three column lattice is primitive/saturated and \(L=M\). This is an exact integer calculation; no finite-state enumeration is needed.

Nevertheless,

\[
x=(0,0,2,0),\qquad x'=(1,0,2,0)
\]

lie in the same integer fiber and satisfy \(x'-x=e_A=\nu_2-\nu_1\in L\), but \(x\in\Gamma_0\) and \(x'\in\Gamma_1\). Both classes are closed, so the states cannot communicate. The nonlinear phase invariant \(H\) is not a lattice invariant: it distinguishes communicating components even after both the real stoichiometric class and reaction-lattice coset have been fixed. This is the decisive counterexample to “saturation implies one stochastic class per deterministic class.”

## Algebraic Markov-basis gate

Choose the integer conservation matrix \(K=(0,1,1,0)\), so its fibers are precisely the nonnegative sets with fixed \(B+C\). In the polynomial ring \(\mathbb Q[a,b,c,d]\), the toric ideal for this fiber relation is

\[
I_K=\ker\bigl(a\mapsto1,\; b\mapsto t,\; c\mapsto t,\; d\mapsto1\bigr)
=\langle a-1,\;b-c,\;d-1\rangle.
\]

The binomials from the actual reversible complexes generate

\[
J_{\rm rxn}=\langle c^2-abc,\;abc-a^3b^2,\;a^3b^2-c^2,\;c^2-c^2d\rangle.
\]

They belong to \(I_K\), but do not generate it. At \((a,b,c,d)=(0,1,0,2)\), every generator of \(J_{\rm rxn}\) vanishes, whereas \(a-1\), \(b-c\), and \(d-1\) do not. Therefore \(J_{\rm rxn}\subsetneq I_K\). This source-complex ideal diagnostic reflects the shared catalysts, but the standard Markov-basis theorem is applied to primitive move binomials after cancelling common factors. Those primitive binomials are

\[
c-ab,\qquad c-a^2b,\qquad a^3b^2-c^2,\qquad 1-d.
\]

Their ideal \(J_{\rm prim}\) is also strictly smaller than \(I_K\): at \((a,b,c,d)=(0,1,0,1)\), all four primitive binomials vanish, but \(a-1\) and \(b-c\) do not. Hence the reaction-vector move set itself is not a Markov basis. The actual enabled-reaction graph is even more restrictive, because common catalyst factors remain in the source complexes. For example, \(2C\to A+B+C\) has move \(e_A+e_B-e_C\), but it is enabled only when \(C\ge2\), although the primitive negative part of that move needs only \(C\ge1\).

The Diaconis–Sturmfels Markov-basis theorem says a finite set of moves connects every nonnegative integer fiber exactly when its primitive move binomials generate the toric ideal. For its direct use as a reaction-network connectivity certificate, the actual reversible reactions must realize the basis moves with no extra common catalysts: for each basis vector \(u\), include \(u^-\rightleftarrows u^+\), not merely \((g+u^-)\rightleftarrows(g+u^+)\) for a nonzero catalyst vector \(g\). Then each standard feasible Markov move is an enabled reaction, the reaction graph contains a connected spanning move graph on every fiber, and positive directional rate constants make each fiber irreducible.

This yields a concrete sufficient hypothesis:

1. The reaction lattice equals the saturated lattice \(S\cap\mathbb Z^d\), checked by Smith normal form or the gcd of maximal minors.
2. A computed Markov basis for that lattice is represented by catalyst-free reversible reaction pairs with positive rates, checked by a Gröbner-basis/toric-ideal certificate and inspection of the source complexes.

Under these assumptions every nonempty integer stoichiometric fiber is one stochastic communicating class. If it is positive recurrent, its stationary probability is unique. Thus there can be no N77-style collection of stationary laws escaping across hidden closed components *inside that fiber*.

The condition is algebraically checkable, but it is stronger than vector-lattice saturation and may require adding reactions absent from the chemical model. It does not imply physical-time nonexplosion or positive recurrence. It also supplies no uniform tail bound over a changing rate-constant family.

## Explicit Markov-basis completion and limits

For this example, add reversible pairs

\[
\varnothing\rightleftarrows A,\qquad B\rightleftarrows C,
\qquad \varnothing\rightleftarrows D,
\]

with unit rate constants. These reactions preserve \(B+C\) and contribute the catalyst-free Markov moves \(e_A\), \(e_B-e_C\), and \(e_D\). Their binomials \(1-a,b-c,1-d\) generate \(I_K\), so the integer fiber \(B+C=2\) becomes one irreducible class. All pairs remain detailed balanced at the unit activity vector. The normalized product-Poisson law on that fiber has independent \(A,D\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(1)\), and \(B\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(2,1/2)\), with \(C=2-B\). This completion demonstrates that a catalyst-free Markov-basis condition can actually eliminate the hidden-component escape mechanism; it is not merely a statement about the abstract lattice.

For this augmented fiber, nonexplosion is direct: \(B,C\) are bounded by 2; total D-birth rate is bounded; and the total rate of reactions that increase A is at most linear in A, with jumps bounded by 3. A linear-rate pure-birth comparison rules out infinitely many upward A jumps on a bounded time interval; downward A jumps and D deaths cannot cause explosion. Thus the normalized detailed-balance law is a genuine stationary probability on the unique class.

It still gives no structural tail control across varying rates. For the reversible one-species network \(\varnothing\rightleftarrows X\), the moves \(\pm1\) are a saturated Markov basis and the sole deterministic class is \(\mathbb R_{\ge0}\). With birth rate \(\theta\) and death propensity \(x\), the unique stationary law is \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\theta)\). As \(\theta\to\infty\), these laws are not uniformly tight on count space. Thus Markov connectivity removes hidden class labels; stationary-tail control still needs kinetic bounds, and parameter-uniform tightness requires hypotheses on the rates.

## Claim boundary

- The reversible candidate's N77 escape construction is internally verified from the listed network; this report does not claim historical novelty.
- Saturation is necessary to eliminate distinct lattice cosets inside a real stoichiometric class, but is not sufficient to eliminate reaction-enabled communicating components.
- A catalyst-free reaction Markov basis is a sufficient connectivity condition. A bare generating set of the reaction-vector lattice, or a Markov basis represented only by catalyst-bearing complexes, is not enough.
- Connectivity eliminates cross-component stationary escape only after fixing one deterministic integer fiber. It does not prove existence of an invariant law or a quantitative stationary-tail estimate.
- The Markov-basis completion above is a mathematical open-network modification; it changes the model and is not presented as a natural biochemical pathway.
