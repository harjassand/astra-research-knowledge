SOL ROBUST INITIALIZER — INDEPENDENT ADVERSARIAL AUDIT
Version 2; 2026-10-09. Append-only audit of frozen
work/sol/robust_recovery/v1.txt. Candidate proof, not external validation.

AUDIT VERDICT

The finite-variance initializer argument in V1 survives this independent algebraic review under its stated model and its real-arithmetic black-box use of DKKP (2023), Theorem C.1. I found no counterexample to the conditional-variance population bound, strong-global-adversary initialization event, MOM scale estimate, uniform-in-L argument, fractional-deletion bound, or 21 epsilon to 20 epsilon survivor transfer. The Rayleigh-to-angle implication and the proposed repeated-call majority-ball confidence amplification are also valid when each DKKP call uses independent randomness and is conditioned on a fixed admissible input.

There are two domain endpoints that V1 does not cover: epsilon=0 is outside DKKP Theorem C.1 and makes log(d/epsilon) undefined; d=1 is also outside its d>2 statement and has no transverse eigengap. Both admit simple repairs below. d=2 can be lifted to dimension 3 by adding an independent learner-generated Gaussian sensing coordinate and a zero signal coordinate, then projecting the initializer back without renormalizing. The paper’s “zero mean” remark does not invalidate the random-sign construction: the formal finite-set theorem is stated in terms of stability alone, and a realized finite inlier sample need not itself have zero empirical mean.

V1 remains only an initializer result. It correctly leaves the one-global-adversary refinement interface unresolved. In particular, sample splitting after a noncausal adversary has inspected the full clean sample only bounds per-batch corruption counts; it does not establish that future clean batches are independent of iterates that can encode them through corrupted earlier rows.

1. MODEL AND SOURCE THEOREM CHECK

Use the V1 notation u=||x*||^2>0, R>=sigma/u, y=(a^T x*)^2+zeta, a~N(0,I_d), E[zeta|a]=0, E[zeta^2|a]<=sigma^2. The adversary may inspect the whole clean initializer batch and replace at most epsilon m pairs. It acts before the learner’s optional independent signs. This conditional second-moment assumption is essential to the displayed V1 bounds; it is weaker than a conditional fourth moment but is not just an unconditional variance assumption.

The imported primary result was checked live at https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/diakonikolas23a.html and its official PDF, accessed 2026-10-09. Definition 2.4 (PDF pp. 4–5; local robust_pca23.txt lines 340–381) defines stability by every [0,1]-valued weight of retained mass at least 1-epsilon and normalized weighted second moment within (1±gamma)Sigma. Theorem C.1 (PDF pp. 18–19; local lines 1793–1806) explicitly requires d>2, 0<20 epsilon<gamma<gamma0, a finite uniform input P=(1-epsilon)G+epsilon B, and (20epsilon,gamma)-stability of G. It returns a top-variance direction with probability at least .99 in O(md gamma^-2 log^4(d/epsilon)) time. Appendix C.3 states that the probability can be boosted by repeated calls; its suggested selector estimates target variance, while V1’s majority-ball selector is an independently valid alternative that does not need access to Sigma.

The theorem’s formal C.1 hypothesis refers only to Definition 2.4 stability. The surrounding remark says stability is intended for mean-zero distributions, and the main subgaussian theorem starts from a zero-mean law, but the actual inlier G in the robust finite-set model is a realized empirical measure and is generally not exactly centered. Thus the sign xi in V1 makes the underlying clean transformed law mean zero, while finite-sample stability itself is proved from sign-invariant outer products. It is harmless and unnecessary for this formal call. No exact finite-sample centering claim should be attached to one random sign per row.

The live current PMLR page for Das, Chatterjee and Batra is volume 306, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/das26a.html (checked 2026-10-09). The local source gives Question 4.8 and Definition 4.6 at work/applied/a07_robust_optimization/research/das26a.txt lines 923–990. Its displayed target for X=Y a is inconsistent with that X’s untruncated second moment, as V1 already notes. V1 instead derives and uses its own target S_L for the different bounded feature sqrt(g_L(Y))a; that target check is internally coherent.

2. LINE-BY-LINE ALGEBRA THAT PASSES

Population transform. Let f_L(y)=clip(y,[-L,L]), g_L(y)=L+f_L(y), so 0<=g_L<=2L. For the Gaussian signal and conditional centering,

  E[y aa^T] = u I + 2 x*x*^T,
  E[y^2 aa^T] <= (3u^2+sigma^2)I + 12u x*x*^T.

The second display follows by conditioning on a, removing the cross term, bounding E[zeta^2|a] by sigma^2, and using E[(a^T x*)^4 aa^T]=3u^2 I+12u x*x*^T. Since |clip_L(y)-y| <= y^2/L, the population Q_L=E[g_L(y)aa^T] obeys

  ||Q_L-S_L||op <= (15u^2+sigma^2)/L,
  S_L=(L+u)I+2x*x*^T.

The eigenvalues are L+3u along x* and L+u on its orthogonal complement, gap 2u for d>=2. Conditional heteroskedasticity and arbitrary dependence of zeta on a are allowed. This is a valid route because V1 proves stability relative to S_L and pays the clipping error explicitly; it does not assert Q_L=S_L.

MOM scale. E[y]=u and Var(y)=2u^2+E[zeta^2]<= (2+R^2)u^2. With K=ceil(32 epsilon m+32 log(2/delta_s)), K<=m/2, block length b=floor(m/K)>=m/(2K). A clean block fails the sqrt(8v/b) deviation threshold with probability at most 1/8 by Chebyshev. Hoeffding gives P{at least K/4 clean bad blocks}<=exp(-K/32)<=delta_s/2. A replacement adversary can touch at most epsilon m<=K/32 disjoint blocks, even when it chooses indices after seeing the complete clean sample. Thus strictly more than half the blocks are within the threshold and the median obeys |h-u|<=4 sqrt(vK/m). The ceiling contributes the +1 in V1 (3.1). Truncating the median at zero does not change it on the event |h-u|<=nu u with nu<1. The sufficient conditions in (3.2) correctly imply the relative scale event. No independence between h and the PCA data is used.

Uniform adaptive deletion. For k=ceil(beta m), the Gaussian row-matrix norm union bound over all k-subsets gives, simultaneously for any index weights q_i in [0,1] with sum<=beta m,

  ||m^-1 sum_i q_i a_i a_i^T||op
    <= [sqrt(k)+sqrt(d)+sqrt(2(k log(em/k)+t_D))]^2/m.

For fixed v, the deletion linear program is maximized by the largest k values (with a possible fractional endpoint); PSD ordering permits extending fewer rows to k. This proves the fractional and data-adaptive form. Since g_L<=2L pointwise, the deleted feature covariance is <=2Lmax G_k for every response-dependent deletion and every L in the allowed interval.

The scalar Bernstein step is also sound despite response tails: for fixed unit v, Z=g_L(y)(v^T a)^2 satisfies 0<=Z<=2L G^2 pointwise, where G is a one-dimensional standard Gaussian; no independence between the multiplier and G is needed. E Z^j<=(4L)^j j!, hence E|Z-EZ|^j<=(8L)^j j!, supporting the stated Bernstein scale. The 1/4-net then controls operator norm. Finally, L -> g_L(y) is 2-Lipschitz for every fixed y, so for Ahat=m^-1 sum aa^T,

  ||Qhat_L-Q_L||op <= E_m+2 Delta (||Ahat||op+1)

between deterministic mesh points. The grid depends only on the fixed unknown parameter u in the proof, not on computed data; no algorithmic mesh is required. The event is uniform on the scale interval that contains Ah, so choosing L=Ah from the corrupted batch is valid.

Stability normalization. With q_i=1-w_i, alpha=m^-1 sum q_i, D=m^-1 sum q_i g_L(y_i)a_i a_i^T, and Qhat_L=m^-1 sum g_L(y_i)a_i a_i^T,

  Q_w=(Qhat_L-D)/(1-alpha).

Subtracting S_L gives numerator (Qhat_L-Q_L)+(Q_L-S_L)-D+alpha S_L. Thus the four error terms in V1 (6.1)—uniform empirical fluctuation, population clipping bias, deletion, and alpha||S_L||—are exactly the terms needed. Since 1-alpha>=1-beta and lambda_min(S_L)>=Lmin+u, condition (6.1) implies ||Q_w-S_L||op<=gamma(Lmin+u), hence both Loewner inequalities. The normalization factor is not missing.

Survivor transfer. If the adversary replaced alpha_0 m rows, alpha_0<=epsilon, and a weight w retains at least 1-20epsilon of the survivor distribution, extend it by zero on changed rows. The clean full-sample retained mass is (1-alpha_0)G[w]>=(1-alpha_0)(1-20epsilon)>=1-21epsilon. Therefore full-sample (21epsilon,gamma)-stability implies survivor (20epsilon,gamma)-stability with the same target. The mixture padding for fewer than epsilon m replacements is exact: if actual fraction is alpha_0 and P=(1-alpha_0)G+alpha_0 B_0, then for epsilon>alpha_0 set B=(alpha_0/epsilon)B_0+(1-alpha_0/epsilon)G. This gives P=(1-epsilon)G+epsilon B and leaves G stable.

Rayleigh and confidence. If a unit DKKP output v obeys v^T S_L v>=(1-C_P gamma)(L+3u), then

  2u(1-|<v,x*/sqrt(u)>|^2) <= C_P gamma(L+3u),
  dist(v,x*/sqrt(u))^2 <= C_P gamma(L+3u)/u.

The second line uses dist(v,w)^2=2(1-|<v,w>|)<=2(1-|<v,w>|^2). With L<=A(1+nu)u and gamma=alpha/[A(1+nu)+3], the squared radius is at most C_P alpha. Choose alpha so this is <=1/81^2. The scale bound gives |sqrt(h/u)-1|<=nu, and the vector identity sqrt(h/u)v-w=(sqrt(h/u)-1)v+(v-w) yields V1’s initializer distance bound nu+3rho0, without a missing scale factor.

For fixed input P, each DKKP call has success probability >=.99 over its own randomness. Independent seeds give independent calls conditional on the clean-data event. At least a strict majority succeed except with probability <=exp(-0.48 J_P); a successful set lies within pairwise distance 2rho0 in the projective metric min(||v-w||,||v+w||). A minimum-radius ball containing a strict majority has radius <=2rho0 and intersects the strict-majority success set, so its selected center is within 3rho0 of the target. With J_P=ceil(8 log(2/delta_P)), the stated failure allocation is valid. Pairwise selection costs O(J_P^2 d). Any failed-call sentinel can be replaced by an arbitrary unit vector; the successful strict majority argument is unchanged.

3. REQUIRED ENDPOINT REPAIRS AND EDGE CASES

Zero contamination. The frozen formula beta=21epsilon and k=ceil(beta m) is undefined at epsilon=0 (k=0 makes k log(em/k) undefined), and DKKP Theorem C.1 requires its input contamination epsilon to be strictly positive. The data theorem is not refuted, but the stated algorithm is not defined at this endpoint. One repair covering also 0<epsilon<1/m is:

  epsilon_alg = max(epsilon,1/m),  beta_alg=21 epsilon_alg.

Prove base stability at beta_alg, use k=ceil(beta_alg m)>=21, and require 20epsilon_alg<gamma. Actual corruption alpha_0<=epsilon<=epsilon_alg. For any (20epsilon_alg)-deletion of G, the full-clean retained mass is at least (1-alpha_0)(1-20epsilon_alg)>=1-21epsilon_alg, so the same transfer applies. Pad the mixture to epsilon_alg using the exact B formula above. When epsilon_alg=1/m, the extra deletion term is O(log m/m); the epsilon-dependent PCA runtime is now log^4(dm), finite. The displayed m>=C(1+R^2)^2[d+log((1+R^2)/delta)] regime absorbs log(m)/m in the deletion term and ensures 20/m<gamma after increasing C. State this explicit margin. Alternatively, branch to ordinary top-eigenvector computation when no point was corrupted, but the floor repair integrates more cleanly with one theorem.

d=1. The imported theorem’s d>2 hypothesis is not met, and S_L has only one eigenvalue so there is no second-eigenvalue gap to invoke. Phase retrieval in one dimension has no direction to estimate: output x0=+sqrt(h). On the scale event, phase/sign distance is |sqrt(h)-|x*||/|x*|<=nu. This covers d=1 without robust PCA.

d=2. After the adversary acts, draw fresh independent z_i~N(0,1) and set a_i^+=(a_i,z_i), x_aug=(x*,0). For clean rows, (a_i,z_i) is N(0,I_3), y_i=((a_i^+)^T x_aug)^2+zeta_i, and conditional centering/variance bounds remain valid by conditioning through the independent z_i. Transformed corruptions are still arbitrary outliers. Apply the d=3 theorem and return the first two coordinates of x0_aug without renormalizing. Orthogonal projection is contractive, so dist(x0,x*)<=dist(x0_aug,x_aug). The basin guarantee is preserved and runtime changes by only a constant dimension factor.

sigma=0. Conditional second moment zero forces zeta=0 almost surely. Choosing R=0 makes the scale, clipping and stability formulas regular for every u>0; Var(y)=2u^2 remains nonzero because of the Gaussian design, so the MOM argument is valid. This initializer is still only an approximate basin point at finite m. V1 correctly does not infer exact recovery after a finite number of optimization steps by setting the noise term to zero.

q -> 0. For u>0 and fixed sigma>0, R=sigma/u diverges. The stated sufficient conditions then require epsilon log(e/epsilon)=O(R^-2) and m=O(R^4 d) up to confidence factors; there is no uniform resource bound as q tends to zero at fixed absolute noise. If sigma=0, R may remain zero and the real-arithmetic scale-invariant bound is unchanged as q decreases. At q=0, the direction gap 2u vanishes and R=sigma/u is undefined; the theorem must exclude this case. A finite-precision result also needs a signal-scale/encoding lower bound because absolute rounding does not remain relative as u tends to zero.

4. NONCAUSAL ADVERSARY AND FULL-ALGORITHM LIMIT

For the initializer, V1 handles one global strong adversary correctly: MOM uses a good-block event on the original iid sample and loses at most one block per changed row; the design deletion event is uniform over all weights; the L event is uniform on a deterministic interval; the PCA theorem accepts any fixed corrupted outlier component. The scale and PCA observations are not treated as independent.

The downstream refinement claim is not available from this initializer proof. A noncausal adversary that sees all clean rows can place information about a future batch into a corrupted row assigned to an earlier batch. Iterates depending on that corrupted row are then dependent on later clean observations. Randomly partitioning the already-corrupted sample and proving that each part has at most twice the corruption fraction does not repair this dependence. A valid end-to-end proof needs a uniform-in-iterate event over the full parameter set, a private sampling mechanism whose independence survives the adversary model, or another explicit mechanism. V1 states this as unresolved and must retain that status until the proposed separate refinement is fully derived and audited.

5. SAMPLE, COST, AND BASELINE AUDIT

Set R>=sigma/u as a supplied dimensionless upper bound; in the AISTATS fourth-moment model, sigma<=K4 gives R<=r_up when r_up>=K4/u is supplied. With fixed nu=1/27, A=C_L(15+R^2), gamma=alpha/[A(1+nu)+3], Delta=alpha u/1000, the stability terms scale as follows:

  clipping bias: O(u/C_L);
  full-data covariance fluctuation: O(Au sqrt(t/m)+Au t/m), t=Theta(d+log((1+R^2)/delta));
  net mesh: O(alpha u) since ||Ahat||op=O(1) at this sample size;
  deletion: O(Au[epsilon log(e/epsilon)+d/m+t/m]), plus O(Au log(m)/m) when k is constant;
  normalization: O(epsilon A u).

Thus the exact finite condition is still V1 (6.1), with beta replaced by beta_alg for the zero endpoint. A sufficient large-R regime is epsilon log(e/epsilon)<=c/(1+R^2) and m>=C(1+R^2)^2[d+log((1+R^2)/delta)], with absolute constants enlarged for the fixed basin and DKKP constants. The MOM scale requirement is O((2+R^2)log(1/delta)), dominated by this m. At epsilon=0 or epsilon<1/m, epsilon_alg=1/m introduces only the stated log(m)/m deletion term. These are sufficient, not minimax-sharp, bounds.

The per-call DKKP time is O(md gamma^-2 log^4(d/epsilon_alg)); confidence boosting multiplies it by O(log(1/delta)). Preprocessing is O(m); majority-ball selection is O(d log^2(1/delta)). Since gamma^-2=Theta((1+R^2)^2), the main runtime is O(md(1+R^2)^2 log^4(d/epsilon_alg) log(1/delta)) in real arithmetic. The offline C.1 theorem gives a time bound, not a complete bit-complexity or streaming-memory bound for this application. Storing the m transformed vectors costs O(md) words in a direct implementation. High-confidence number of samples is not multiplied by the number of PCA repetitions; only runtime is. Finite precision and arbitrary-size adversarial coordinates remain outside the claim.

For total failure probability delta, one explicit allocation is delta_s=delta_C=delta_A=delta_D=delta_P=delta/5; then the four clean-data event failures and the amplified fixed-input PCA failure sum to at most delta. This makes every logarithm in the sample statement O(d+log((1+R^2)/delta)); the PCA repetition factor contributes only log(1/delta) to runtime.

The V1 comparison phrase “from roughly r_up^{-4}” is correct only if it specifically means the older Kong robust-PCA initializer in Das–Batra Theorem 4.1: its displayed tolerance is epsilon<=C(1+r_up^2)^-2, i.e. large-r_up order r_up^-4 (local das26a.txt lines 670–703), with a polynomial/roughly quadratic-in-sample robust PCA cost. A second baseline, their stability-based DKKP initializer Theorem F.5, has epsilon<=C(16+r_up^2)^-4, i.e. r_up^-8, and m2>=C n^5(16+r_up^2)^4 log(4n/delta) (local lines 2813–2845). V1 should name the first comparison explicitly and mention the second separately; saying the new tolerance improves “the original route” without naming which is ambiguous. For R<=r_up, the new epsilon log(e/epsilon)<=c/(1+R^2) condition is asymptotically much less restrictive than either displayed prior tolerance at large comparable ratios, but it also has an R^4 d sample factor and is not asserted optimal.

6. CLAIM STATUS AND OPEN GATES

Internally checked in this audit: all displayed V1 initialization inequalities listed in Sections 2–3 above; strong-global-adversary validity for the initializer; same-data L selection; survivor transfer; conversion of DKKP Rayleigh guarantee to phase distance; independent-call majority amplification.

Repair required before stating a fully general initializer theorem: cover epsilon=0 with epsilon_alg=max(epsilon,1/m) or a no-corruption branch; cover d=1 directly and d=2 via augmentation (or state d>2 only); clarify the baseline theorem in the contamination comparison; explicitly combine delta_s, delta_C, delta_A, delta_D, and delta_P into total delta; retain R as a supplied bound and q>0.

Not established here: a new theorem beyond V1; optimal epsilon/noise/sample tradeoffs; a full one-global-adversary end-to-end refinement algorithm; finite bit complexity; independent external verification or novelty/priority.
