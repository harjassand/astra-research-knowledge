# Exact obstruction to uniform element abundance from regular antichain generators

**Status:** Complete proof of a restricted auxiliary counterexample. No resolution of Frankl's union-closed sets conjecture; no assertion of historical novelty. This record has not undergone specialist peer review or formalization.

## The original conjecture

Frankl (1979) conjectures that every finite nontrivial union-closed family of sets `F` has *some* element belonging to at least `|F|/2` members. A counterexample requires **every** ground element to have frequency less than `|F|/2`. Our construction does not satisfy that condition.

## The auxiliary assertion disproved

> If a finite union-closed family `F` is generated by an irredundant antichain of generators, and every ground element belongs to exactly two generators, then every ground element occurs in at least half the sets in `F`.

This is false, even asymptotically in a strong sense.

## Explicit theorem

For every integer `k >= 1`, there exists a finite union-closed family `F_k` on `6k+1` ground elements generated by `4k+2` pairwise incomparable sets with the following properties:

1. Each ground element belongs to exactly two generators.
2. The generating family is irredundant.
3. `|F_k| = 16^k + 2*8^k + 4^k - 2^(k+1)`.
4. One ground element belongs to precisely `2*8^k + 4^k - 2^(k+1)` members.
5. The frequency ratio in (4) tends to **zero** as `k -> infinity`.
6. The family also contains elements occurring in at least half its members, so this is not a counterexample to Frankl.

For `k=1` the singled-out frequency is exactly one half. For every `k>=2` it is strictly less than one half.

## Construction

Construct a simple graph `G_k` with two central vertices `u` and `v` joined by edge `c=uv`. To each central vertex attach `k` disjoint pairs of new vertices; for every pair `a,b` attached to `u`, insert the triangle edges `ua,ub,ab`, and likewise at `v`. There are `4k+2` vertices and `6k+1` edges. Every vertex has degree at least two.

Set the universe `X=E(G_k)`. For each vertex `w` let `S_w` be its **edge star**: the set of graph edges incident to `w`. Define

    F_k = { union(S_w for w in U) : U subset of V(G_k) }.

Empty union is allowed.

This family is union-closed because the union associated to `U` and to `T` is the union associated to `U union T`. Every ground edge `xy` occurs in exactly the two stars `S_x` and `S_y`. The stars are pairwise incomparable: in a simple graph with minimum degree >=2, every `S_x` has an edge not incident to another fixed vertex `y`, so `S_x` cannot be a subset of `S_y`. Thus the generator system is also irredundant.

## Essential bijection and proof of cardinality

The complement of a star-union generated by a vertex set `U` is precisely the set of graph edges *induced* by `T=V(G_k)\U`:

    E(G_k) \ union_{w in U} S_w = E(G_k[T]).

Two choices of `T` may yield the same induced edge set, because isolated vertices can be added or deleted. Remove all vertices isolated in `G_k[T]`, obtaining a vertex set `R`. Either `R` is empty or every vertex of `G_k[R]` has an internal neighbor. Moreover, `E(G_k[T])=E(G_k[R])` and `R` is *recoverable* as the set of endpoints of its induced edge set. Thus the members of `F_k` are in bijection with the vertex subsets `R` containing no isolated vertices in `G_k[R]`, including the empty subset.

Count these `R` according to which central vertices they contain:

- Both `u` and `v`: the central edge supports the two hubs, and each of the `2k` petal pairs may be in any of four states (`00`,`01`,`10`,`11`). This gives `16^k` subsets.
- Exactly `u` (or exactly `v`): among its `k` petal pairs, at least one satellite must be selected, giving `4^k-1` choices; with the other hub excluded, each of its `k` pairs must be `00` or `11`, giving `2^k` choices. Both orientations give `2(4^k-1)2^k`.
- Neither `u` nor `v`: each of the `2k` pairs must be `00` or `11`, giving `4^k` choices.

Summing yields

    |F_k| = 16^k + 2(4^k-1)2^k + 4^k
          = 16^k + 2*8^k + 4^k - 2^(k+1).

The distinguished central edge `c=uv` is **absent** from a star-union if and only if it belongs to its complementary induced edge set. This happens exactly when both `u,v` are in the unique isolate-free representative `R`, a case counted `16^k` times. Hence the number containing `c` is

    |F_k| - 16^k = 2*8^k + 4^k - 2^(k+1).

The ratio is asymptotic to `2^(1-k)`, hence tends to zero. In particular, for `k=2` there are 392 members and `c` occurs in 136, giving `17/49 < 1/2`.

## Why the original Frankl conjecture survives: explicit injection

Fix a petal pair `a,b` with hub `u`; the internal edge `ab` is a ground element. In the isolate-free representation, its absence from a member of `F_k` is equivalent to `a,b` both belonging to `R`.

Define an injection from isolate-free `R` containing both `a,b` into isolate-free `R'` not containing both:

- If `u in R`, delete `b` and retain `a`. The retained `a` is still supported by `u`; the deleted `b` supported no vertex other than `a,u`. All other vertices retain their neighbor.
- If `u not in R`, delete *both* `a,b`. They form an isolated two-vertex component of `G_k[R]`, hence deleting them cannot isolate any remaining vertex.

These operations are injective within their separate cases (the membership of `u` is unchanged and specifies the inverse), and their images are disjoint. Thus at most half of the isolate-free subsets contain both `a,b`. It follows that the edge `ab` occurs in **at least half** the members of `F_k`. This explicitly prevents `F_k` from refuting Frankl.

## What would actually resolve the conjecture

Either give a general proof that every finite nontrivial union-closed family has an abundant element, or provide an explicit finite union-closed family in which every element is rare, with exact closure and frequency certificates. The present result only refutes a stronger, auxiliary *every-element* statement. It gives no proof of the all-family conjecture or the unresolved automorphism-transitive subcase.
