EXECUTIVE RESULT
----------------
The proposed global denominator (1+B)^(-D) is not a safe generator
correction: a low-count B_i removal changes the correction attached to a
different, high, inactive B_l. The resulting cross-coordinate term can make
LV tend to +infinity. A per-face correction

  V(a,b)=1+A+sum_(i in I+) R^(b_i)[1+epsilon_i h_i(a)/(1+b_i)^D]+K I_0,
  h_i(a)=1{sum_(j in N(i))a_j=0},  I_0=1{A=0},

removes that cross term. With D>=m, K>=1, a common R>1 sufficiently close
to one, and the stated finite-degree toggle grammar, the fixed-class
generator admits constants C,c>0 such that

  LV <= C - c [ A + sum_(i in I+) R^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D ]
     <= C' - c' V/(1+log V)^D.                                  (1)

The first inequality is the useful generator certificate; constants depend
on the fixed network, rates, epsilon_i, K, D, and the fixed isolated-
substrate labels. It is not a common bound over unbounded class labels.
Equation (1) is enough for physical-time nonexplosion and positive
recurrence in every irreducible fixed-label class. For D>=1 it is a
subgeometric, not geometric, Foster bound; the endpoint m=D=0 gives a
geometric bound. Under the usual petite/irreducible
conditions it gives stretched-exponential convergence at the rate scale

  r(t) = phi(H_phi^{-1}(t)),
  phi(v) ~ v/(log v)^D,
  H_phi(v)=int_1^v ds/phi(s) ~ (log v)^(D+1)/(D+1),

so r(t) grows on the scale exp(c t^(1/(D+1))) / t^(D/(D+1)), up to constants
in the exponent. The associated convergence bound decays like 1/r(t), a
stretched-exponential scale with a polynomial factor. This is a certificate
guarantee, not a claim that the chain's actual convergence rate cannot be
geometric. A simple isolated-label ray
shows the local V itself cannot satisfy LV<=C-cV in the full stated family.

1. MODEL AND CLASS INTERFACE
----------------------------
Counts are a_j for A_j and b_i for B_i. The base channels, with stochastic
mass-action falling-factorial propensities, are

  0 <-> A_j                                      rates alpha_j, delta_j a_j;
  A_j <-> A_j+B_i                               rates p_ji a_j, q_ji a_j b_i;
  A_j+B_i <-> (k_ji+1)A_j+B_i                  rates u_ji a_j b_i,
                                                v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i,

for (j,i) in a finite support E and fixed k_ji>=1. All displayed rates are
strictly positive and finite. Added channels are a finite list of pairs

  A_j+z_B <-> A_l+z_B,       A_j+z_B <-> z_B,

with identical substrate vector on the two sides, total substrate degree
|z_B|<=m, and positive finite rates. The first kind preserves A=sum_j a_j;
the second changes A by one. In particular, each added propensity is bounded
by a constant times (1+B)^m after fixing isolated substrate labels, where
B=sum_(i in I+)b_i. Isolated B counts are invariant under both the base and
added channels. Every result below is within one fixed isolated-label
class. The base network makes each such active class irreducible by the
usual clear-to-zero/build-from-zero paths; added reversible channels do not
remove those paths.

For the active coordinates, choose one R>1 and finite eps_i>0, D>=m, and
K>=1. The function V is positive and norm-like because
V>=1+A+sum_i R^(b_i); the correction is bounded above by
sum_i eps_i R^(b_i), and K I_0 is bounded. Thus its sublevel sets are finite
in each fixed-label class. If I+ is empty, Section 5 gives the separate
linear immigration/death argument.

2. EXACT LOCAL GENERATOR STRUCTURE
----------------------------------
Write c_R=R-1 and d_R=1-R^(-1). The uncorrected base terms are

  H - sum_j delta_j a_j
  + sum_(j,i in E) [p_ji c_R a_j R^(b_i)
                    -q_ji d_R a_j b_i R^(b_i)
                    +k_ji u_ji a_j b_i
                    -k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i],

where H=sum_j alpha_j. The K-correction contributes exactly

  -K H I_0 + K sum_j delta_j 1_{a=e_j}.

For each active substrate i, catalyst immigration/death switches h_i and
contributes

  epsilon_i R^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D
     [-alpha(N(i)) h_i
       +sum_(j in N(i)) delta_j 1{a restricted to N(i) is e_j}],       (2)

where alpha(N(i))=sum_(j in N(i))alpha_j. The positive death switch in (2)
is present only when the local neighborhood has exactly one molecule.
For a substrate-preserving catalyst toggle, b is unchanged, so its support
switch contribution for i is exactly +/- epsilon_i W_i, where

  W_i=R^(b_i)/(1+b_i)^D.

At a positive switch h_i:0->1, the source has exactly one local catalyst.
Therefore the catalyst factor in a transfer/deactivation propensity is
exactly one, and its remaining substrate factor is at most C(1+B)^m. The
reverse support switch h_i:1->0 contributes negatively. The local
denominator is the key: changing b_i in a base substrate reaction cannot
change any h_l correction for l!=i.

More explicitly, for A_j->A_j+B_i and A_j+B_i->A_j, the required source
has j in N(i), hence h_i=0 before and after; no local correction for i
changes. Every l!=i has b_l and all catalyst counts unchanged. The
autocatalytic pair leaves b fixed; its forward direction can only switch
some h_l from one to zero, while its reverse leaves at least one A_j and
cannot switch h_l from zero to one. Consequently, the only positive
support-switch terms are the last-neighbor deaths in (2) and the explicitly
listed new toggles.

3. DRIFT BOUND WITH DEGREE-m TOGGLES
-----------------------------------
Choose R>1 close enough to one that, for each catalyst j,

  chi_j=delta_j-sum_(i:(j,i) in E) p_ji(R-1)>0.                     (3)

For an edge (j,i), its substrate bracket

  g_ji(n)=p_ji c_R R^n-q_ji d_R nR^n+k_ji u_ji n

satisfies g_ji(n)/R^n -> -infinity. Choose M large enough that for each
edge and n>=M, g_ji(n)<=-c_0 nR^n, for one c_0>0. If h_i=0, then
A_i=sum_(j in N(i))a_j>=1 and the edge block gives a negative term of order
nR^n A_i. The positive base last-neighbor correction is at most
max_j(delta_j epsilon_i) R^n/(1+n)^D and is absorbed by that sink for
large n. If h_i=1, all incident edge terms vanish and (2) gives the direct
negative face term -alpha(N(i))epsilon_i W_i. Thus every high substrate
face has a negative component of at least order W_i; an active high face
actually has the stronger nR^n sink.

Here is a precise catalyst split for the remaining base terms. For each
catalyst j and incident edge (j,i), g_ji(n) is bounded above on n>=0,
because it tends to -infinity. Let G_ji=max(0,sup_n g_ji(n)) and
G_j=sum_i G_ji. Choose c_0>0 below one quarter of min_(j,i) q_ji d_R and
a finite threshold N_ji so that for n>=N_ji,
g_ji(n)<=-(G_j+2)-2c_0 nR^n. If any incident edge is above its threshold,
that edge's negative coefficient pays for every possible positive
coefficient on the other edges, leaving a strict linear-in-a_j margin and
a reserved c_0 nR^n high-face term. If no incident
edge is above threshold, all incident b_i are bounded. When at least one is
positive, the reverse autocatalytic term
-k_ji v_ji (a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i dominates all remaining linear-in-a_j gains
for large a_j; the finitely many smaller a_j values contribute a constant.
When all incident b_i are zero, the coefficient is exactly
-delta_j+sum_i p_ji(R-1)=-chi_j<0. Taking the minimum over finite j gives a
negative A margin, while the reserved high-face terms give the active
substrate margins. The positive base last-neighbor corrections are bounded
for low b_i because they force local catalyst count one; for high active
faces and comparable-coordinate added switches they are absorbed by the
reserved q-removal term. The negative
immigration correction supplies -alpha(N(i))epsilon_i W_i on every inactive
face. Thus, after adding a finite constant for low faces, the base part has
the claimed catalyst and substrate drift margins.

It remains to check the new channels, whose rates may be unbounded. For each
new channel r, its substrate monomial obeys

  phi_r(b) <= C_r(1+B)^m.                                            (4)

Here and below C_r absorbs fixed isolated labels. The positive A-increment
from z_B->A_j+z_B is at most C(1+B)^m. When A=0 its total V increment is
1-K plus nonpositive support-correction changes, hence is nonpositive for
K>=1. For A>0 it is bounded by (4). A positive K-boundary jump from
A_j+z_B->z_B can occur only at a=e_j and is also bounded by (4). Deactivation
away from A=e_j has a negative A increment. Transfers have zero A
increment.

For a positive support switch in coordinate i, the added-channel generator
term is at most C(1+B)^m W_i. Let b_* = max_i b_i and s=|I+|. Then
B<=s b_*. There are two cases. If b_i>=b_*/2, then D>=m implies
(1+B)^m/(1+b_i)^D is bounded by a constant depending only on s,m,D; the
positive switch term is O(R^(b_i)), while the active pre-switch q-removal
sink is at least c b_i R^(b_i), so it is absorbed for large b_i. If
b_i<b_*/2, the positive term is at most C(1+b_*)^m R^(b_*/2), whereas the
negative block on a coordinate attaining b_* is at least
c R^(b_*)/(1+b_*)^D, whether that coordinate is inactive (its immigration
switch) or active (its stronger q sink). The ratio is at most a polynomial
times R^(-b_*/2), hence tends to zero. This controls all positive
support-switch terms. Likewise, the A-birth terms in (4) are dominated by
the max-coordinate negative block since
R^(b_*)/(1+b_*)^D divided by (1+B)^m tends to infinity. The finite number of
channels lets one choose a common threshold and reserve a positive fraction
of the substrate margins.

Finally, for b_i<=M the W_i are bounded and enter C. The scalar
R^n/(1+n)^D is eventually increasing. If b_*=max_i b_i, then each W_i is
bounded by a finite constant plus R^(b_*)/(1+b_*)^D; summing over the finite
set I+ gives the required comparison with the max-face term, up to a finite
additive constant. Combining the base catalyst margin and the high-face/
toggle estimates proves, for constants C,c>0,

  LV <= C-c_A A-c_S sum_i W_i.                                      (5)

Let S=sum_i R^(b_i). The function V is comparable to 1+A+S, with constants
depending on K and max eps_i. Since b_i<=log_R S and
W_i>=R^(b_i)/(1+log_R S)^D,

  A+sum_i W_i >= c' V/(1+log V)^D

outside a finite set. Absorbing the finite set into C in (5) proves (1).
This is a fixed-network all-state case split, not an event-chain estimate.

4. PHYSICAL-TIME NONEXPLOSION AND RECURRENCE
---------------------------------------------
The total rate is not globally bounded. It contains the base terms

  H+sum_j delta_j a_j
   +sum_(j,i)[p_ji a_j+(q_ji+u_ji)a_jb_i
                    +v_ji(a_j)_(k_ji+1)b_i]

and added terms bounded by finite sums of
a_j(b)_(z_B), a_l(b)_(z_B), and (b)_(z_B), which can grow like a_j B^m or
B^m. Thus a bound on the number of reactions in the embedded event chain
does not bound elapsed physical time; high-rate toggles can have very short
holding times.

The proof uses the physical-time generator inequality (1). It implies the
global upper bound LV<=C. For L>V(x), set

  tau_L=inf{t>=0: V(X_t)>=L}.

On {V<L}, V bounds every active coordinate; this is a finite state set.
The network has finitely many reactions, finite stoichiometric jumps, and
finite propensities at every state, so the set of one-jump targets from
{V<L} is finite and the exit rates there have a finite maximum. Stopped
Dynkin, first on these finite localizations, gives

  E_x V(X_(t wedge tau_L)) <= V(x)+Ct,
  P_x(tau_L<=t) <= (V(x)+Ct)/L.                                     (6)

An infinite number of jumps cannot occur while the chain remains in a finite
set, because its total rate there is bounded. Since V is norm-like on the
fixed class, an explosive path would have to exit every finite V-sublevel
set in finite time. Sending L to infinity in (6) rules this out. This is
physical-time nonexplosion; no uniform global bound on q(x) is asserted.

Because V/(1+log V)^D tends to infinity, (1) gives LV<=-1 outside some
finite sublevel set C. Stopped Dynkin at tau_C wedge tau_L, followed by
nonexplosion and Fatou, gives E_x tau_C<=V(x)-1 for x outside C (a harmless
additive adjustment is allowed if the set is enlarged). The countable-state
continuous-time Foster theorem then gives positive recurrence in each
irreducible fixed-label class. The exact interface in Anderson–Kim,
Theorem 3.1, is: a countable-state CTMC, a finite K, a positive V, and
AV<=-1 off K imply positive recurrence of every state in each closed
irreducible component and finite expected time to enter the union of such
components. Here nonexplosion is proved separately above, and the base
paths give one irreducible class per fixed isolated-label vector.

For a subgeometric conclusion use the stronger explicit drift (1), not just
LV->-infinity. With phi(v) comparable to v/(1+log v)^D, replace it on a
bounded interval by an increasing differentiable concave function. For
D>=1, phi'(v)->0 and

H_phi(v)=int_1^v ds/phi(s) ~ (log v)^(D+1)/(D+1),
r_*(t)=phi(H_phi^(-1)(t))
       = exp(Theta(t^(1/(D+1))))/t^(D/(D+1)) up to constants.

The continuous-time extended-generator theorem of Douc–Fort–Guillin,
Theorem 3.11, converts LV<=-phi(V)+b1_C into its process drift condition.
Their Theorems 3.1, 3.9 and 3.10 then provide subgeometric modulated
return-time bounds, positive Harris recurrence with pi(phi(V))<infinity,
and subgeometric convergence when an irreducible skeleton is available.
For this nonexplosive irreducible countable CTMC every fixed positive-time
skeleton is irreducible and aperiodic: a finite path can be placed inside
any positive time interval and a no-jump interval has positive probability.
The set C is finite and petite (indeed a finite-state minorization follows
from common finite paths and bounded rates on C).

The return-time conclusion is not an exponential moment. It is the
subgeometric modulated-moment/ergodicity conclusion associated with r_*;
in particular the usual first hitting-time bound follows from LV<=-1
outside C. The certificate gives pi(V/(1+log V)^D)<infinity, not
pi(V)<infinity. It does not establish a positive exponential moment for
return times. Also, if one has only LV(x)->-infinity with no lower envelope
such as phi(V), the safe consequence is the -1 Foster theorem and finite
mean hitting/return time after nonexplosion; no particular subgeometric
rate, higher return-time moment, or stationary V-moment follows from that
limit alone.

5. DEGENERATE SUPPORTS AND QUANTIFIER BOUNDARIES
------------------------------------------------
If I+ is empty, all B coordinates are fixed labels. On each fixed-label
class the A-process is a linear immigration/death/migration chain: the base
0<->A_j channels give a strictly negative linear drift in A, added transfers
preserve A, added deactivations decrease A, and reverse deactivations add
at a finite label-dependent constant rate. The finite K I_0 term only
changes finitely many boundary increments. Thus LV<=C-cA for V=1+A+K I_0
outside a finite set; localized Dynkin proves nonexplosion and the chain is
positive recurrent (geometric drift is available). If J is empty as well,
each substrate vector is a singleton class and the claim is trivial.

For nonempty E, isolated substrate labels are omitted from V and fixed.
Their factorials in new toggle propensities are finite constants, so the
proof holds classwise. Constants can grow with those labels; there is no
uniform full-state tightness or uniform rate-box statement across unbounded
isolated labels. The catalyst set may contain isolated A_j; their
immigration/death drift remains active and is included in A.

When m=0 and D=0, the same first inequality gives a geometric Foster bound
(and the usual exponential-type ergodicity conclusions under the same
regularity assumptions). For D>0 even at m=0, the displayed logarithmic
weakening is the conclusion furnished by this particular comparison. The
local denominator requires D>=m to absorb a support-switch rate whose
substrate degree is at most m when its high coordinates are comparable to
the corrected coordinate. For D<m, a same-face toggle can contribute
Theta(b_i^(m-D)R^(b_i)) at the last-catalyst boundary, while the base
q-removal sink is only Theta(b_iR^(b_i)); for m-D>1 this can defeat the
proposed bound. The conclusion above is only for D>=m.

6. TWO EXACT TESTS
------------------
6a. Why the global denominator fails
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
This report retains the global-denominator obstruction separately from
the local proof. Take disjoint active edges (A1,B1),(A2,B2), and the state

  a1=n^2, a2=a3=0, b1=1, b2=n.

Then h_1=0 and h_2=1. In the candidate with correction
epsilon_2 R^(b2)/(1+B)^D, the B1-removal channel
A1+B1->A1 has rate q_11 n^2 and changes B from n+1 to n. It therefore
raises the untouched B2 correction by

  epsilon_2 R^n [(n+1)^(-D)-(n+2)^(-D)]
    = epsilon_2 D R^n n^(-D-1)(1+o(1)).

The reverse B1-creation channel has the opposite denominator increment,
but choosing q_11>p_11 leaves a positive leading coefficient. Its net
contribution is Theta(R^n n^(1-D)). The B2 immigration switch contributes
only -Theta(R^n n^(-D)); it is smaller by a factor n. The old local edge
terms, A-death, and reverse autocatalysis are polynomial in n and cannot
cancel this exponential term. Hence LV(x_n)->+infinity for every fixed
finite D. A permitted high-rate transfer A1+mB2<->A3+mB2 has zero V-jump
on this ray (A1 remains present and A2 remains zero), and a permitted
deactivation contributes only polynomial order. The shared denominator
therefore cannot certify nonexplosion or recurrence. Root also stores the
direct exact generator replay at
outputs/research/reaction_dynamics/cycle5/GlobalDenominatorObstruction.txt.

The local denominator removes exactly this mixed-face effect: the B1
removal changes only b1-dependent terms, and their h_1 is zero because A1
is the required source catalyst. The high inactive B2 correction is
unchanged.

6b. Local denominator is not geometrically strong in the general class
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Take catalysts A1,A3 and substrates B,C, with base support E={(A1,B)};
C is an isolated substrate label fixed at 2. Include all positive base
paired channels, and add the two degree-two pairs

  A1+2C <-> 2C,             A1+2B <-> A3+2B,

with all rates equal to one. The second pair has rates growing as
a_j(b)_2 when B grows. Along x_n=(A1=0,A3=0,B=n,C=2), the transfer pair
is disabled, while the reverse of A1+2C<->2C creates A1 at the constant
rate (2)_2=2. The only other enabled transitions that affect V are the
base immigrations 0->A1 and 0->A3. Set K=1 and use the local candidate
with epsilon=1, R=3/2, D=m=2. Directly,

  LV(x_n)=-3 (3/2)^n/(1+n)^2.                                     (7)

The exact Fraction checker in evidence/check_local_ray.py verifies (7) on
n=0,...,30 and separately verifies that the total exit rate grows at least
as n(n-1) on the active transfer ray (A1=1,A3=0,B=n,C=2). Thus LV(x_n)
tends to -infinity, but LV(x_n)/V(x_n)->0. No constants C,c>0 can make
this V satisfy LV<=C-cV. The degree-two channel that creates A1 uses only
the frozen C label, so its rate does not grow with n and cannot cancel the
inverse polynomial. This is a counterexample to a geometric inequality for
this candidate, not to recurrence. It shows why D>=m alone cannot promise
geometric drift. If every inactive-face activator instead contains m
copies of the same diverging B_i and D=m, that channel contributes order
R^(b_i) and can repair this particular ray; the general extension allows
other z_B, so that stronger premise is absent.

Indeed this fixture is not a recurrence counterexample by another route:
each displayed reversible pair has equal unit forward/reverse constants,
so the deterministic activity vector with every coordinate one is detailed
balanced. The stochastic product-Poisson law with unit means is reversible;
conditioning on the invariant C=2 coordinate gives a stationary law in
this fixed-label class. The example isolates a weakness of this particular
Lyapunov geometry, not a stochastic instability.

7. EVENT CLOCK, N35/N76, AND SCOPE
----------------------------------
N35 is a controlled finite-horizon safety result for an acquired inward
molecular polytope; its source card explicitly disclaims all-time survival,
nonexplosion, and ergodicity. It is not used here. N76's falling-factorial
potential bounds upward changes in the embedded reaction-event chain, not
elapsed time. Neither statement supplies (1) or (6). The present route
charges physical holding times by proving a generator drift and controlling
finite-sublevel exit rates. An event-chain estimate alone could be
compatible with arbitrarily short holding times at high B and would not
prove physical-time recurrence.
