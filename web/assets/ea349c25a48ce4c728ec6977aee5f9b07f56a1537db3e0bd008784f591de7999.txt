EXACT HARD-ENERGY ASYMPTOTIC EXPONENT FOR THE QUTRIT EXCITED BLOCK
Status: algebraic derivation, independently checkable; external novelty not assessed.

Matrix E = [[1/2, sqrt(2)/4], [sqrt(2)/4, 3/4]], with costs 1,2.
For k excited sites, a hard energy Q permits Hamming weight j <= Q-k.
Let p_Q be the smallest achievable expectation of E^{tensor k}, optimized
over k <= Q and all states in that hard downset. Q is a nonnegative integer.
The ground state has E=1 and costs 0, so extra ground sites have no effect.

RESULT
  lim_{Q->infinity} [-log p_Q]/Q = log((1+sqrt(17))/2).
The exponent is approximately 0.940613642107209 per unit hard energy.
The associated exponential decay base is (sqrt(17)-1)/8.
This is substantially larger than the all-separable exponent log 2, if
that comparator is established under the same hard-support interface.

1. EXACT FINITE SYMMETRIC-BLOCK INVERSE
Write E=(1/2)L L^T, L=[[1,0],[s,1]], s=1/sqrt(2).
In the orthonormal Dicke basis |j>, j=0,...,n,
  T_{ij}=<i|Sym^n(L)|j>
        = binom(n-j,i-j) sqrt[binom(n,j)/binom(n,i)] s^{i-j}, i>=j,
and T_{ij}=0 for i<j. Thus T is lower triangular.
For m=min(n,Q-n), compression to j<=m factors exactly:
  A_{n,m} = 2^{-n} T_{n,m} T_{n,m}^T.
There are no omitted columns in these rows, because T is lower triangular.
Sym^n(L^{-1}) replaces s by -s. The inverse of the leading principal
triangular section has the same property. If J=diag((-1)^j), then
  T_{n,m}^{-1}=J T_{n,m} J.
Consequently,
  lambda_min(A_{n,m})^{-1}=2^n ||T_{n,m}||_2^2.             (1)

2. EXACT GENERATING FUNCTION
Let R_{n,i}=sum_{j=0}^i T_{ij}^2, the squared Euclidean norm of row i.
Let M=L L^T=[[1,s],[s,3/2]]. By multiplicativity of the symmetric power,
  R_{n,i} = [Sym^n(M)]_{ii}.
For D_z=diag(1,z),
  sum_i R_{n,i} z^i = tr Sym^n(D_z M).
If a,b are the eigenvalues of D_z M, this trace equals sum_{i=0}^n
a^{n-i}b^i. Its ordinary generating function in n is therefore
  sum_{n>=0} v^n sum_i R_{n,i}z^i
    = 1/[(1-va)(1-vb)]
    = 1/[1-v(1+3z/2)+v^2 z].
This holds first for positive z and then as a formal power-series identity.
No asymptotic representation theorem is imported.

Define S_Q=sum_{n+i=Q, 0<=i<=n} 2^n R_{n,i}. Substituting v=2u,z=u,
  sum_{Q>=0} S_Q u^Q
    = 1/[1-2u-3u^2+4u^3]
    = 1/[(1-u)(1-u-4u^2)].                               (2)
Let lambda=(1+sqrt(17))/2 and lambda_-=(1-sqrt(17))/2.
Coefficients of (1-u-4u^2)^{-1} are
  c_Q=(lambda^{Q+1}-lambda_-^{Q+1})/sqrt(17).
Then S_Q=sum_{r=0}^Q c_r. In particular log(S_Q)/Q -> log lambda;
all coefficients are nonnegative and |lambda_-|<lambda.

3. SPECTRAL SANDWICH AND OPTIMIZATION OVER SITE NUMBER
Let B_Q=max_{0<=n<=Q} 2^n ||T_{n,min(n,Q-n)}||_2^2.
Every summand of S_Q is an admissible row squared norm, and a matrix's
spectral norm is at least the Euclidean norm of any of its rows. There
are at most Q+1 summands, so
  B_Q >= S_Q/(Q+1).
The spectral norm squared is at most the squared Frobenius norm. Summing
over all admissible n and i yields
  B_Q <= sum_{q=0}^Q S_q.
Both sides have log exponent log lambda. By (1), the optimum over the
symmetric sectors has exactly the claimed exponent.

The parent task has an independent exact finite-Q proof that the minimum
over the whole computational downset lies in the permutation-invariant
sector: after alternating-sign conjugation, its inverse is a nonnegative
irreducible permutation-invariant matrix, whose Perron vector is symmetric.
It need not be strictly positive: entries vanish when the union of the
row/column subsets exceeds the cutoff. Irreducibility follows because
every subset has a strictly positive connection to the empty subset.
Combining that argument with this report identifies p_Q above exactly.
Even without importing that finite-Q argument, the Schur blocks supplied
in the task suffice for the asymptotic conclusion: a block with r
two-row boxes is 4^{-r}A_{n,m}, with cost n+m+3r. The symmetric-sector
growth rate log lambda exceeds log(4)/3, so r cannot improve the best
asymptotic exponent. Uniform polynomial sandwiches handle r growing
with Q, rather than just fixed r.

4. EXPLICIT SADDLE AND A DICKE-SPACE WITNESS
The entry T_{ij} has squared value
  T_{ij}^2 = (n-j)! i! / [(n-i)! j! (i-j)!^2] * 2^{-(i-j)}.
For i/n -> x and j/n -> y, its log per n tends to
  F(x,y)=(1-y)log(1-y)+x log x-(1-x)log(1-x)-y log y
          -2(x-y)log(x-y)-(x-y)log 2.
At fixed x, maximizing in y gives
  2(x-y)^2=y(1-y),
  y=(1+4x-sqrt(1+8x-8x^2))/6.
The maximum over x<=t increases to t=2/3, then stays at log 2.
At an interior optimum of [log 2+F(x,y)]/(1+x), one also has
  x(1-x)^2=2y(1-y)^2.
The maximizing values are exactly
  x_*=(7-sqrt(17))/10,
  y_*=(5-sqrt(17))/10,
  x_*-y_*=1/5.
At these values,
  [log 2+F(x_*,y_*)]/(1+x_*) = log((1+sqrt(17))/2).
Thus one may choose n approximately Q/(1+x_*) and m approximately
x_*n. The asymptotic fractions are n/Q=10/(17-sqrt(17)) and
m/Q=(7-sqrt(17))/(17-sqrt(17)). Integer rounding changes the log rate
by O(log Q/Q), as follows directly from factorial bounds.

An explicit state giving this exponent can be obtained without solving
the entire eigenproblem. Choose m=floor(x_*n), j=floor(y_*n), and define
  |psi> = T_{n,m}^{-T}|m> / ||T_{n,m}^{-T}|m>||.
Its Rayleigh quotient in A_{n,m} is exactly
  <psi|A|psi> = 2^{-n}/sum_{i=0}^m T_{mi}^2
             <= 2^{-n}/T_{mj}^2.
Equivalently, its Dicke amplitudes are proportional to (-1)^i T_{mi},
i=0,...,m. The global sign (-1)^m has no effect.
All amplitudes are Dicke amplitudes within the hard cutoff. The limiting
exponent of the last bound is log lambda. It is an explicitly specified
state family; no statement about efficient physical preparation is made.
