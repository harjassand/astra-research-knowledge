for every isotropic log-concave law in every finite dimension,
||kappa_(d+1)(u,.,...,.)||_HS^2 <= b ||u||^2. The X-coordinate vectors after

EXACT CONSTANT INTERFACE

For isotropic mu define

  F_f(z) = E_mu[f(X) exp(<z,X>)] / E_mu[exp(<z,X>)],
  T_d^mu f = (1/d!) nabla^d F_f(0).

Let

  B_d = sup_{all dimensions, isotropic log-concave nu, |u|=1}
         ||kappa_(d+1)^nu(u,.,...,.)||_HS,
  A_d = sup_{all dimensions, regular isotropic log-concave mu,
             Var_mu(f)=1} ||T_d^mu f||_HS.

Constants are extended nonnegative reals. The exact universal relation is

                            A_d = B_d / d! .

For any regular mu and centered unit-variance f with bounded Hessian, let
X_1,...,X_N iid mu, eta_beta independent Laplace(beta),
F_N=N^(-1/2) sum_i f(X_i), sigma_beta^2=1+2/beta^2, and
S=(F_N+eta_beta)/sigma_beta. The law of Y=(S,X_1,...,X_N) has density

  exp[-sum_i V(x_i) - beta |sigma_beta s - N^(-1/2)sum_i f(x_i)|].

It is log-concave once beta ||Hess f||_infty/sqrt(N) <= a, where
Hess V >= a I. Indeed its potential is the maximum, over signs, of
sum_i (V(x_i) +/- beta f(x_i)/sqrt(N)) minus the corresponding linear
function of s; each branch is convex under that inequality.

The law is centered and Cov(S)=1. Its X block is I_(nN), and its covariance
C is positive definite: any nonzero S coefficient has an independent Laplace
noise variance contribution 2/(beta^2 sigma_beta^2), while a nonzero X-only
coefficient has positive variance under the independent isotropic copies.
Set Z=C^(-1/2)Y. Z is isotropic log-concave. For coordinate basis vectors
e_S,e_(i,j), define u=C^(1/2)e_S and v_(i,j)=C^(1/2)e_(i,j). Then

  |u|^2 = C_SS = 1,
  <v_(i,j),v_(k,l)> = Cov(X_i,j,X_k,l) = delta_ik delta_jl.

Thus all v_(i,j) form an orthonormal frame. No condition on <u,v_(i,j)> is
needed: the directional cumulant assumption permits every unit first-slot
vector, including one not orthogonal to the frame.

Writing Lambda for the log MGF of Y, differentiation at w=0 gives

  partial_w Lambda(0,z_1,...,z_N)
       = (1/(sigma_beta sqrt(N))) sum_i F_f(z_i).

Therefore, for any ordered j_1,...,j_d and replica labels i_1,...,i_d,

  kappa_Y(e_S,e_(i_1,j_1),...,e_(i_d,j_d))
    = d!/(sigma_beta sqrt(N)) (T_d f)_(j_1,...,j_d)
      if i_1=...=i_d, and 0 otherwise.

The N same-replica blocks are disjoint. Their squared sum is exactly
  (d!)^2 ||T_d f||_HS^2 / sigma_beta^2.
Affine cumulant covariance says this is a restriction of
||kappa_(d+1)^Z(u,.,...,.)||_HS^2 to an orthonormal frame in the remaining
slots, so

  (d!)^2 ||T_d f||_HS^2 / sigma_beta^2 <= b.

For each finite beta choose finite N >= (beta ||Hess f||_infty/a)^2.
Uniformity of b over all dimensions is essential. Let beta increase to get
||T_d f||_HS <= sqrt(b)/d! with no factor 2. Smooth compactly supported
functions, centered and normalized, are dense in centered L2(mu); continuity
of T_d in L2 follows from finiteness of log-concave polynomial moments and
the Riesz representation of each tensor entry. This extends the result to
all f in L2. Constants do not matter because T_d kills constants.

Conversely, for linear f(x)=<u,x>, isotropy gives Var(f)=|u|^2 and
T_d f=kappa_(d+1)(u,.,...,.)/d!. Hence B_d/d! <= A_d for regular nu.
Regular isotropic log-concave approximation with convergence of moments up to
order d+1 extends this to every isotropic log-concave nu. This approximation
step is needed for literal equality of the two suprema; without it the direct
statement proved is equality for the regular directional-cumulant class.

ADVERSARIAL CHECKS AND LIMITS

1. Whitening does not lose the exact mixed block. The affine tensor identity
   is kappa_Y(v_1,...,v_(d+1)) =
   kappa_Z(C^(1/2)v_1,...,C^(1/2)v_(d+1)); use of C^(1/2), not C^(-1/2),
   is correct for tensor arguments.
2. The normalized S coordinate remains variance one for arbitrary centered
   unit-variance f, including a nonzero linear component. Cross-covariances
   affect C but do not enter the Gram matrix among the transformed X vectors.
3. Added Laplace noise is essential for C positive definite. At infinite beta
   and a purely linear f, the covariance would become singular; the proof only
   uses finite beta and then takes a scalar bound limit.
4. The source suspension's beta-Hessian/sqrt(N) condition is sufficient: the
   absolute-value coupling is a maximum of two convex branches. No hidden
   convexity of |nonlinear g| is assumed.
5. The hypothesis must cover arbitrary dimension. N grows with beta and with
   the approximant's Hessian bound. It is not a fixed-dimension consequence.
6. The factor d! is correct: T_d is the Taylor coefficient tensor
   nabla^d F_f(0)/d!, while the mixed cumulant takes d derivatives of F_f.
   Ordered tensor indices match the source Hilbert-Schmidt convention.
7. The proof uses only a subset of full tensor entries. Terms whose remaining
   slot includes S, and mixed-replica terms from the general cumulant, are
   omitted from the lower bound and can only increase the full squared norm.

The exact equivalence is a universal worst-case statement across measures and
dimensions. It does not say that, for each fixed mu, the largest T_d response
is attained by a linear observable. A standard Gaussian demonstrates this
distinction below. Nor does a local tilt-response bound imply an archive
codec, control the full posterior field, or close the c09 joint-posterior
memory gate.

One concrete, deliberately narrow downstream consequence is a local
zero-memory codec for a known exponential-tilt family. If the universal
tilt-response bound is ||T_d f||_HS <= R^d ||f||_2 for every d, let
L_z(x)=exp(<z,x>-Lambda(z)) be the likelihood ratio of the tilt of a regular
isotropic strongly log-concave reference mu. The degree-d L2 Taylor coefficient
g_d(z) of L_(t z) at t=0 obeys, for every f in L2(mu),

  <f,g_d(z)> = <T_d f,z^tensor d>,
  ||g_d(z)||_2 <= R^d ||z||^d.

Strong log-concavity makes t -> L_(t z) analytic as an L2-valued function near
zero. The coefficient bound gives its Taylor series radius at least
1/(R||z||), so for R||z||<1,

  ||d mu_z/dmu - 1||_2 <= R||z||/(1-R||z||),
  TV(mu_z,mu) <= (1/2) R||z||/(1-R||z||).

Thus, when the parameter domain is the supplied ball ||z||<=r with Rr<1,
an encoder can ignore X (D=1) and a decoder that can sample the acquired
reference mu has uniform TV error at most (1/2)Rr/(1-Rr). To meet epsilon it
suffices that Rr <= 2epsilon/(1+2epsilon). The acquired costs are a correct
reference sampler and a certified parameter-radius bound; no input-dependent
record is used. This only handles a very small neighborhood of one reference
law and is not a compression result for the unit parameter ball or for the
general c09 statistical-memory family. A dimension-free local tilt bound also
follows from standard log-concave tail control, so this corollary is not
claimed as novel or field-breaking; it is the only direct archive consequence
I found from this transfer.

FINITE WITNESS COMPILER AND ACQUIRED COSTS

The exact transfer gives a constructive refutation procedure. Suppose an
acquired regular isotropic mu and a centered unit-variance smooth f with
||Hess f||_infty <= M satisfy a certified lower bound
||T_d f||_HS >= alpha, and a proposed universal directional bound is b.
If q=d! alpha/sqrt(b)>1, choose

  beta^2 = 4/(q^2-1),
  N = max(1, ceil(4 M^2/(a^2(q^2-1)))).

Then sigma_beta^2=(q^2+1)/2<q^2, and the constructed isotropic log-concave
law in dimension nN+1 satisfies

  ||kappa_(d+1)^nu(u,.,...,.)||_HS^2
     >= (d!)^2 alpha^2/sigma_beta^2
     >= 2 b q^2/(q^2+1) > b.

This is an explicit finite witness against b, rather than an appeal to the
beta-to-infinity limit. If q=1+delta with small delta, then N is
O(M^2/(a^2 delta)); the witness dimension is O(n M^2/(a^2 delta)). The
regularity margin a, Hessian cost M, and excess response margin delta are
load-bearing input quantities.

Whitening cost is structured but can be ill-conditioned. Put
r=E[X f(X)] and let g=(r,...,r)/sqrt(N) in the X block. The covariance is
exactly

  C = I + (e_S g^T + g e_S^T)/sigma_beta.

It differs from identity only on span{e_S,g}; the nontrivial eigenvalues are
1 +/- |r|/sigma_beta, and |r|<=1. Thus C^(-1/2) can be applied with a 2x2
matrix function plus O(nN) vector work, rather than a dense (nN+1)-cube
decomposition. Its condition number is at most
(sigma_beta+1)/(sigma_beta-1), which is Theta(beta^2) in the worst case as
beta grows; ||C^(-1/2)|| is then Theta(beta). For q near one the finite
witness has beta^2=Theta(1/delta), replica count Theta(M^2/(a^2 delta)),
and whitening condition number Theta(1/delta). The exact symbolic theorem
has no numerical error, but a numerical certificate must pay for this
conditioning and for acquiring r.

The input needed by this witness procedure is not free: a representation or
sampler for mu; a certified lower curvature a; an explicitly represented f;
certified centering and L2 normalization; a global Hessian bound M; and a
certified lower bound alpha for T_d f. For fixed n,d, the symmetric tensor
T_d f has binomial(n+d-1,d) entries, and direct integration involves tilted
moments through order d. This costs at least that many output values and
input-specific integration/sampling precision; no universal sample bound is
claimed. If an arbitrary L2 witness is approximated by compactly supported
smooth functions, M may diverge as alpha approaches its target. Constructing
or simulating one suspension sample costs N base samples, N evaluations of
f, one Laplace sample, and whitening; full tensor enumeration in the lifted
dimension would scale like (nN)^d and is unnecessary for the selected-block
lower witness. Costs are not uniform in dimension, d, curvature, or margin.

EXACT SANITY EXAMPLE: A NONLINEAR OBSERVABLE ON A GAUSSIAN BASE

Take mu=N(0,1), f(x)=(x^2-1)/sqrt(2). Then Ef=0, Var(f)=1,
||f''||_infty=sqrt(2), and

  F_f(z)=E[f(X)e^(zX)]/E[e^(zX)] = z^2/sqrt(2),
  T_2 f=1/sqrt(2).

The base Gaussian has kappa_3=0, so linear observables on this fixed base
have zero second-order tilt response. For beta=4 and N=32, a=1 satisfies
beta ||f''||/sqrt(N)=1, so the suspension is log-concave. Since
E[X f(X)]=0, its covariance is exactly identity without any whitening.
Each mixed entry kappa_(S,i,i)=sqrt(2)/(sigma_beta sqrt(N)); their N
disjoint squares sum to 2/sigma_beta^2=16/9. Hence this finite-dimensional
isotropic log-concave law has a third-cumulant directional norm squared at
least 16/9, despite the base Gaussian having zero third cumulant. This
independently checks the factorial, replica count, and mechanism using exact
Gaussian moments. Letting beta grow and N>=2 beta^2 yields the lower
universal value b_2>=2 from this example alone; it is not a sharpness result.
`python3 work/cycle6/c09_l09/revisions/suspension_exact_sanity.py` checks the
finite rational threshold instance b=3/2, beta^2=12, N=24, and selected
cumulant lower bound 12/7>3/2. This is exact arithmetic for the displayed
constants, not an independent machine proof of log-concavity or the tensor
identity; those are derived above.

For the more useful search reduction, a certified non-linear f with response
above a proposed universal threshold immediately produces a finite
isotropic-log-concave cumulant counterexample at the displayed dimension and
conditioning cost. The reverse test only needs linear f. This can reduce
universal-constant falsification to a response-tensor search, but the tensor
has binomial(n+d-1,d) entries and acquisition may dominate.

DOWNSTREAM TRANSFER TEST

This mechanism supplies a precise bridge between a one-shot observable tilt
operator and cumulants, but not the statistical-memory result sought in c09.
The c09 Sol reports give: (S01,S02) counterexamples to generic covariance or
Poincare-to-mutual-information substitutions; (S03) a log-covariance-field
variance bound for a distinct all-scale channel. c09_L01 additionally audits
the KLS preprint's localization and information interfaces; its reported
finite-horizon inverse-covariance estimate narrows a regularity concern but
does not verify the full imported proof. The available Luna reports
give: (L02) a posterior-covariance channel bound conditional on identifying
the actual N75 field; (L03) a joint-posterior thickness condition and positive
finite-label codec; (L04) a same-Fisher product/shared-scalar log-vs-log^2
pair; (L05) a supplied common scalar statistic codec; (L06) fixed blind
encoder/decoder and hybrid-memory interface corrections; (L07) an exact
two-mode singular posterior witness; (L08) effective-tail and acquired-cost
obstructions; (L10) finite Fisher-jet indistinguishability. None establishes that N75's actual
likelihood field equals the tilt-response tensor, nor that local derivatives
at z=0 control the full posterior information integral or memory cost.
The new suspension candidate therefore does not repair the c09 archive gate.

The KLS source's criterion in Section 3 uses all arbitrary-observable
Taylor tensors in a sufficient Poincare hypothesis, and Section 7 transfers
universal cumulant bounds to that hypothesis. Removing the factor 2 improves
the resulting Poincare constant from at most 2 C K to at most C K when the
source's b=K^d(d!)^2 bound is used: exact equality gives
||T_d f||^2<=K^d||f||^2; the criterion then has R=sqrt(K). This is only a
constant-factor improvement, not a KLS advance beyond the imported claim.
The stronger capability demonstrated here is finite witness conversion, not
a new archive codec or an efficient acquisition method.
