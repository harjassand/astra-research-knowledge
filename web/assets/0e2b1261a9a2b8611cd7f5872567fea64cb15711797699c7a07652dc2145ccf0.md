## Trace 087: usable primitive and remaining foundation

The exact symmetric source is [The symmetric Mahler conjecture and its equality cases](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/The-symmetric-Mahler-conjecture-and-its-equality-cases-September-22-2026/paper.pdf). Source PDF pages 7–14 were used to reconstruct the lens, homogeneous-zero mass, and boundary-feasibility argument. Page 13 was visually checked to preserve powers, Jacobian factors, and the direction of the limit inequality.

Exact primitive: supplied real nonzero rows `b_1,...,b_m` span `R^d`; supplied conformal bijection `F` maps the disk to a bounded planar domain, has F(0)=0, and is continuous on the closed disk. For every independent d-row basis I, set `Z_I=B_I^{-1}(F(e^{i theta_i}))`, with independent uniform angles. Let P_I be the probability that every remaining row coordinate lies in the closed planar domain. Then `sum_I P_I >=1`. No acquisition of rows or domain is part of this statement, and no extra-boundary genericity assumption is needed for this lower bound.

The proof reconstructs the normalized isolated-zero mass and the high-power change of variables. These local calculations appear sound in the restricted reconstruction; this is not an external correctness or priority clearance for the full Mahler package. The immediately deduced witness law has acceptance at least `1/#independent_bases`. Uniform sampling over supplied independent bases thus has expected at most that many trials in an ideal exact-real model. Enumerating the basis list can cost `binom(m,d)` rank tests, and numerical implementation additionally owes domain evaluation, boundary decisions, input precision, and basis conditioning. The source's lens sampler derives from [Gross's conformal Skorokhod embedding](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.00852); no originality is claimed for the boundary law.

For the sharp volume-product application, the next link is the exact feasible-simplex identity `S=(d!/4^d) integral_A |Sigma_X| dX`, and `Sigma_X subset A^polar`. Extra-row boundary events must have probability zero for the no-overlap identity. The equality classification additionally uses the lifted body, a common-feasibility witness, endpoint lens scaling, and a classical metric-median classification. Those further sections were not independently checked here. Part III's nonsymmetric entropy/Riccati proof is a separate chain: the summary ends by retaining uniform scalar inequalities as dependencies, so the elementary symmetric primitive must not be used as a certificate for it.

Decisive failure test: a legal row/domain instance with `sum P_I<1` would refute the primitive; merely mishandling closed-domain boundary events could also reverse the limiting inference. For the full symmetric inequality, a failure of extra-boundary nullity or simplex interior disjointness would break the volume conversion even if the mass primitive survived. For the general argument, scalar positivity and the covariance-normalization existence are separate load-bearing obligations.

## Trace 159: the cost invariant, repaired history, and audit targets

The exact source is [Quasipolynomial Bounds for Arithmetic Progressions](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/Quasipolynomial-Bounds-for-Arithmetic-Progressions-September-23-2026/paper.pdf), 198 pages. Only its interface, sections 4.6 and 10, and immediate weighted consequences were read selectively. Its appendices implementing sampling, detection, positivity, globalization, and relative lifting were not fully audited.

The summary's Part I targets a summable polylogarithmic density bound, then records an unresolved mesh/truncation circularity concern (summary PDF p24). Part II uses that earlier architecture as input and reorganizes precision. This historical concern is not by itself a disproof of the final paper: the final text explicitly discusses the absence of width circularity in section 4.6 and gives a descending budget schedule in section 10.3. The remaining question is whether every supporting lemma actually respects the advertised dependence.

For each fixed progression length k, cell depth D depends only on k. Old determining integer coordinates remain exact in later polynomials. Precision Q_j=log(2/w_j) is allowed to change by

`Q_j(new) <= Q_j(old) + P_j(p,d_*,Q_{>j}(old))`,

where P_j is fixed and excludes the current precision from the added term; p is a logarithmic inverse-density scale, and d_* is polynomially forecast before precision choices. Fixed-depth descending envelopes prove polynomial total precision over O_k(p) rounds. That elementary implication was independently proved in `lemmas.md`; it is not the proof that the mathematical increment actually attains this recurrence.

The deepest reusable target is an absolute fresh-coordinate bound independent of box dimension plus relative lifting of rank at most d+d0, rather than an arbitrary polynomial in old rank. The source points to Appendices C/G/H/I for shift comparison, cube comparison, positive return, and relative lifting. The sharp schedule also requires large internal moduli/heights to disappear from output relative width losses. A lemma that brings current Q_j, its current rank threshold, or a large restored orbit period back into that loss falsifies the stated quasipolynomial iteration. Fixed k is essential: no uniform growing-k complexity follows.

Prior art separates the inverse theorem from the density-increment cost. [Leng–Sah–Sawhney's quasipolynomial inverse theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17994) is the supplied structural input. Their [Szemerédi bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17995) give, for k>=5, a stretched exponential in log log N, not the released log N saving. A quasipolynomial inverse theorem alone does not prove the release's quantitative conclusion.

Strongest consequences, conditional on the released density bound: every progression-free set has uniformly bounded harmonic sum; even divergence of `sum_{a in A}(log(2+a))^B/a` for any fixed B>=0 forces every fixed-length progression. The sharp density result also implies quasipolynomial coloring thresholds. These are source consequences, not new results of this pass. The reciprocal-sum comparator cannot replace the quantitative proof when using the stronger weighted tails or coloring rates.
