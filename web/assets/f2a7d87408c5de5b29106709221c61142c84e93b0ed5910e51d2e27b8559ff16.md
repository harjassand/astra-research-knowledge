### Fresh stochastic endpoint audit: fixed-order integrability closes noncircularly

A fresh primary-source check closes the stochastic expectation/interchange gate for each fixed dimension and tensor order on the compact-support localization setup. The distinction between constants is essential. The theorem's target (b_r=K^{r-1}((r-1)!)^2) is not needed to justify the Ito expectations and would be circular if invoked before the induction proves orders (m,m+1). Instead, a deliberately crude (C_{n,r}<\infty), independent of the target theorem, follows from uniform one-dimensional isotropic log-concave moment bounds and the moment-cumulant partition identity.

Indeed, for isotropic log-concave \(\nu\), unit vectors \(v,w_2,\ldots,w_r\), and each partition block of \(\{1,\ldots,r\}\), Holder and \(\|\langle X,w\rangle\|_p\le c p\) bound the associated product moment by a constant depending only on \(r\). The finite partition formula therefore gives \(|\kappa_r^\nu(v,e_{i_2},\ldots,e_{i_r})|\le J_r\). Summing the \(n^{r-1}\) remaining-coordinate entries yields

`R_r^nu <= C_{n,r} I`, with `C_{n,r}=n^(r-1) J_r^2` (a larger `n^r J_r^2` is also safe).

This crude estimate is uniform over all isotropic log-concave laws, so it applies uniformly in time and over the whitened posteriors \(\nu_t\), without assuming the theorem being proved. With \(q_t=\langle A_tu,u\rangle\) for fixed deterministic unit \(u\), BKL's affine identity (PDF p.21, eq. (77)) gives pathwise \(\mathcal E_r(t)\le C_{n,r}q_t\). The quantity \(q_t\) need not decrease pathwise; only its mean does. For compact support diameter \(D\), the posterior has the same essential support, so \(0\le q_t\le D^2\) (in fact \(D^2/4\)), and hence \(A_t\preceq D^2I\). BKL's noise inequality and symmetry of \(H_{i,t}\) give \(\sum_i H_{i,t}^2\preceq D^2\sum_iH_{i,t}A_t^{-1}H_{i,t}\preceq8D^2A_t\preceq8D^4I\); therefore \(\sum_i\|H_{i,t}\|_{HS}^2\le8nD^4\). This deterministic finite-horizon quadratic-variation bound makes the covariance stochastic integral square-integrable in BKL's SDE, so \(\mathbb E A_t=e^{-t}I\) follows without an expectation interchange gap. Thus \(\mathbb E q_t=e^{-t}\), \(\mathbb E q_t^2\le D^2e^{-t}\), and \(\mathbb E\mathcal E_r(t)\le C_{n,r}e^{-t}\).

For the energy local martingale, BKL's normalized first variation (PDF pp.22–23, eq. (88)) is \(g_i=2\langle\widetilde T,\widetilde W_i\rangle-\langle\widetilde T,\Sigma_{\widetilde H_i}\widetilde T\rangle\). Equations (90)–(91) give, with no missing dimension or factor-two term,

`d<M>_t/dt = sum_i g_i^2 <= 8 E_m(t) E_{m+1}(t) + 16(m-1)^2 E_m(t)^2`.

Substituting the *crude* \(C_{n,m},C_{n,m+1}\) envelopes and applying Tonelli yields

`E<M>_infinity <= D^2 [8 C_{n,m} C_{n,m+1} + 16(m-1)^2 C_{n,m}^2] < infinity`.

The energy martingale is therefore genuinely square-integrable. On finite horizons the remaining drift terms are integrable: the energy terms have the exponential envelopes, and the mixed \(L_m\) term is absolutely integrable by Cauchy-Schwarz in \(dt\otimes dP\) and the lower-order induction estimate. Local Ito identities can be stopped and limits passed using this bracket bound and those drift bounds. Consequently the terminal term obeys \(\mathbb E\mathcal E_m(T)\le C_{n,m}e^{-T}\to0\) for every \(T\to\infty\); the nonnegative energy integral converges by monotone convergence and the mixed term converges absolutely. This is stronger than selecting only a subsequence \(T_k\), and it validates the integrated-energy expectation interchange in the compact-support induction.

This auxiliary closure is fixed-(n,m), and its martingale bracket also depends on the original support diameter (D). It does not provide a uniform bracket estimate through BKL's compact-support approximation to a noncompact law. That approximation remains a separate source-proof gate. The source pointers, exact normalization, and superseded conditional (b_r)-only attempt are recorded in `kls_covariance/LOCALIZATION_GATE_AUDIT.md`; the independent fixed-order moment argument and the same limit-order check are in `kls_proof_audit/FINAL_REPORT.md`, lines 165–206. This closes only the stochastic endpoint conditional on BKL's imported third-cumulant/Letwin input and the stated localization setup; it is not external validation of the KLS theorem.

## Astra N75: covariance-only heat-flow plateau and missing information step

The pinned Astra card, full supplied summary, and source map are preserved as `N75.txt`, `U-sources.txt`, and `U-package.html`. Although N75's linked technical note and verification ZIP are absent from that package, the exact predecessor channel argument is recoverable in OpenAI/math Family 093, “A dimension-free logarithmic Sobolev inequality for subgaussian log-concave measures,” at the pinned commit `adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a`, PDF path `preprints/A-dimension-free-logarithmic-Sobolev-inequality-for-subgaussian-log-concave-measures-September-23-2026/paper.pdf`. The local copy's Git blob hash matches the pinned tree; its SHA-256 is `767a5eb7cd819af7e33c8b867c41403aac36ec015c89c1d2beb186abca9ec89e`. The extracted text is `kls_covariance/source_family093/paper.txt`.

The summary reports the following partial spectral result. For a centered compactly supported log-concave density `mu`, with optimal Poincaré constant `P_0`, covariance operator norm `sigma^2`, and `mu_s = mu * N(0,sI)`, it claims for `0 < s < P_0`:

`P_0 <= P(mu_s) <= P_0 + sigma^2 s/(P_0-s)`.

The summarized proof uses a first eigenfunction, a heat-flow contact identity, and conditional Jensen for `b_s(Y)=E[X|Y]`; it says no proposed subgaussian LSI is needed for this spectral plateau. If a normalized hypothetical KLS counterexample had `P_0=1` and `sigma^2 -> 0`, then `P(mu_s) -> 1` at every fixed `s<1`.

The original Family 093 channel proposition is explicit. For a sequence `j` of stationary laws `eta_j` of `Y_{j,t}`, let `R_j(y)` be the positive-semidefinite matrix field and `Hhat_{j,i}` the original Sylvester matrices. Its hypotheses include:

`R(eta_j) <= lambda_j^{-1}`, `lambda_j -> 1`, `E tr(R_j^2) -> 1`, `E|D R_j|^2 -> 1`,

`D_i R_j = (Hhat_{j,i} R_j + R_j Hhat_{j,i})/2`, and `sum_i E||[Hhat_{j,i},R_j]||_HS^2 = o(1)`,

where `R(eta_j)` is the logarithmic-Sobolev constant in that paper. For fixed `t>0`, `L>=1`, define `f_L(q)=q/(1+q/L)`, `C_{j,T}(y)=I+f_L(T R_j(y)^2)`, and `Z_{j,T}=C_{j,T}(Y_{j,t})^(1/2)G` with independent standard Gaussian `G`; then `I<=C_{j,T}<= (1+L)I` and Proposition 7.2, equations (7.6)–(7.8), proves

`limsup_{j->infinity} integral_0^infinity I(Y_{j,t}; Z_{j,T}) dT/T^2 <= 9/4`,

uniformly over each fixed `t,L`. The proof applies the LSI to square-root posterior likelihoods, computes their Gaussian covariance Fisher information, and integrates an eigenbasis/Sylvester multiplier. Comparable eigenvalue pairs have multiplier at most 9; noncomparable pairs have a `4 log(1+L)` bound multiplied by the commutator defect, which vanishes as `j->infinity` at fixed `L`.

The missing N75 step is now narrowly identified: retain this specialized channel and matrix-field/Sylvester assumptions, but replace the LSI hypothesis `R(eta_j)<=lambda_j^{-1}` by a Poincaré bound `P(eta_j)<=lambda_j^{-1}` and prove the same scale-integrated mutual-information bound (or another absolute bound sufficient downstream). Poincaré applied to the square-root likelihood yields an affinity/Hellinger estimate, not the required additive KL/mutual-information estimate across diffuse spectral blocks. No counterexample satisfying all the specialized channel hypotheses was found. **The covariance-only replacement remains open; the original LSI proposition is source-reconstructed, not externally re-proved.**

## Astra N76: exact discrete boundary correction, no recurrence result

For population `x in N^d`, finite complex set `C`, and available complexes `y <= x`, define `x_under_y = product_i x_i!/(x_i-y_i)!`, with zero when disabled. The source summary defines

`F(x) = min_{y<=x} log((x-y)!)`, `M(x)=max_{y<=x} x_under_y`.

For every enabled reaction `y -> y'`, the target complex `y'` is available after the reaction and leaves the same residual `x-y`. Also `F(x)=log(x!)-log M(x)`. Thus exactly

`F(x-y+y') - F(x) <= log(M(x)/x_under_y)`.

For positive fixed rate constants `k_y` on a finite network, the embedded event chain therefore has `E[exp(Delta F)|x] <= C_rate`, where one valid crude bound is `C_rate=(sum_y k_y)/min_y k_y`: the total propensity is at least `k_* M(x)`, while each jump’s exponential factor is at most `M(x)/x_under_y`. The resulting exponential maximum envelope is measured in **reaction events**.

This is a precise boundary-safe upward-change control. It is not negative drift, does not bound physical-time holding-time accumulation, and proves neither nonexplosion nor positive recurrence. The load-bearing missing mechanism is control of effective slow motion between fast cycles together with elapsed-time costs. Source’s 8,034-state and 25,456-transition checks are reported only; not replayed here.

## Astra N77: explicit failure of uniform tightness across stochastic classes

For unit mass-action constants under falling-factorial propensities, take

`2C -> A+B+C -> 3A+2B -> 2C`, and `2C <-> 2C+D`.

For each integer `n >= 0`, the closed class is

`Gamma_n={(n,0,2,d), (n+1,1,1,d), (n+3,2,0,d): d>=0}`.

The A/B/C phase cycle has event rates `2`, `n+1`, and `2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)`. It is finite and irreducible for each fixed `n`. The D coordinate has immigration/death rates `2` and `2d` in phase `(n,0,2)`, and is unchanged in the other two phases. The product of the phase law with weights proportional to `(1/2, 1/(n+1), 1/[2(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)])` and a Poisson(1) D law satisfies detailed balance on each edge pair/cycle flow and is a stationary probability. The process is nonexplosive: the A/B/C phase is a finite-rate cycle for fixed `n`, D births have bounded rate 2, and D deaths only decrease D. Hence every `Gamma_n` is individually irreducible positive recurrent.

On `Gamma_n`, the pathwise nonlinear invariant is `A - B(B+1)/2 = n`, and the stationary law has `E[A] >= n`. All these classes lie in the same linear stoichiometric plane `B+C=2`, and each contains a strictly positive state `(n+1,1,1,1)`. The deterministic mass-action network is complex-balanced at `(1,1,1,1)` and the positive stoichiometric class `B+C=2` has the common global attractor by the established complex-balanced global-attractor theorem. The stationary laws nevertheless escape every compact set as `n -> infinity`.

This refutes **class-uniform tightness** of stochastic stationary laws inferred only from one deterministic attractor. It does **not** refute componentwise positive recurrence; each `Gamma_n` is positive recurrent. Product form/global-attractor mechanisms are established prior art; novelty of the example is unverified. Full source proofs and finite code were not supplied in the Astra packet, so the reconstruction above is an independent derivation, not validation of the source’s reported checks.
