## Result of the bound search

I did not obtain an effective upper bound on \(Z\), nor an explicit positive-integer charge that is proved to work. The paper’s stated result is finite-but-nonnumerical: it explicitly says that no effective numerical charge bound is asserted. The obstacle is specific: the finite-sufficient-tests proof produces an unspecified radius \(\tau\) of good couplings, and every charge bound reduces to bounding that radius effectively.

Let \(B\subset(0,1]\) be the bad set of (7.9), and

\[
C_B=\sup_{0<t\le1}t^{-1}\mathbf 1_B(t),\qquad Z=2+\lceil C_B\rceil.
\]

Lemma 7.4 proves \(B\cap(0,\tau)=\varnothing\) for some \(\tau>0\), hence \(C_B\le1/\tau\) and \(Z\le2+\lceil1/\tau\rceil\). Thus any proved explicit \(\tau\) gives both an upper bound on the paper’s \(Z\) and a direct valid charge, for example \(Z_0=\lfloor1/\tau\rfloor+1\), since \(1/Z_0<\tau\). No value or computable lower bound for \(\tau\) is supplied.

The exact loss of effectiveness has several independent layers:

1. **The spectral continuation radius.** At \(\lambda=0\) the relevant \(S_z=1/2\) three-electron determinant is isolated by the one-particle splitting \(d_*=\epsilon_e-\epsilon_b>0\). Proposition 2.2 proves only \(0<d_*<100/D\) (with \(D=10^{200}\)); it gives no lower bound. Proposition 2.3 uses analytic perturbation and the contour Neumann series
   \[
   (H_{m,\lambda}-\zeta)^{-1}=R_0(\zeta)(I+\lambda V_{ee}R_0(\zeta))^{-1},
   \]
   but leaves the contour norm—and hence an admissible radius governed by the small isolated-level gap—uncomputed. The text expressly says no lower bound uniform in \(D\) is asserted; more to the point for the fixed \(D\), it also supplies no numerical fixed-\(D\) radius. \(d_*\) is the near-degenerate bonding/antibonding splitting of the one-electron two-centre Coulomb operator, so an effective perturbation argument first needs a certified positive lower bound for this splitting and explicit Coulomb-resolvent constants.

2. **The exterior radius \(M\).** The uniform tail estimate (3.45) has a remainder \(\rho_D(R)\) with \(\rho_D(R)\downarrow0\) but no rate or evaluable bound. Lemma 7.2 chooses \(M\) by “sufficiently large” \(j\) after invoking this limit and scalar \(o(1)\) terms. Consequently \(M\), which sets the compact cylinder and the region on which later tests must be controlled, is finite but not effectively bounded in the paper.

3. **The all-minimizer margin.** Proposition 4.2 proves convergence of every folded angle minimizer, its multiplier, and its derivatives by a sequential compactness argument. This yields eventual satisfaction of the strict tests but no modulus such as \(\sup_{\theta\in\mathrm{Min}_t}\|\theta-\theta_*\|_{C^1(K)}\le\omega(t)\) with computable \(\omega\). In addition, the positive margin \(\alpha\) in (7.7) is only known to be \(>0\), with no numerical lower bound. This blocks turning the last test of (7.9) into a numeric interval even if the eigenstate continuation radius were known.

4. **The plane-channel constants.** Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.4 establish the nonzero equatorial channel coefficient and uniform remainders, but the radius at which the remainder is dominated and the \(D\)-dependent constants are not evaluated numerically. The positivity/direct-amplitude requirements in the regularity test therefore also remain qualitative.

These are not one redundant missing estimate. A lower bound for \(d_*\) alone would not compute \(M\), \(\alpha\), the channel comparison range, or the uniform convergence rate over all minimizers. Conversely, the source already has a useful effective *last-stage* criterion: Lemma 4.7 supplies finite sufficient inequalities and explicit slack (including the strip derivative constant \(<10^{30}\)) once \(M,\alpha\), and the density/angle data pass those tests. The missing step is to prove those tests for a named interval \(0<t<\tau\) with fully numerical constants.

## Theorem/interface and proof reconstruction

Theorem 1.1 sets \(D=10^{200}\) and asserts existence of a finite integer \(Z\) such that three electrons with equal nuclear charges \(Z,Z\) at \(\pm(D/Z)e_z\) have an absolute Coulomb ground state whose density is not the ground-state ensemble density of any noninteracting three-electron Hamiltonian with one real spin-independent local potential in \(L^{3/2}(\mathbb R^3)+L^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\). The integer is defined by (7.10), not numerically evaluated.

After scaling by \(1/Z\), this is the unit-charge two-centre molecule at separation \(2D\), with electron repulsion coefficient \(\lambda=1/Z\). At zero coupling, the determinant \(b\uparrow,b\downarrow,e\uparrow\) is the \(S_z=1/2\) state. Coupling produces an odd direct-removal channel with a nodal zero on the median plane and a complementary ionic channel whose leading planar amplitude is nonzero of order \(\lambda A(r)r^{-4}\). For the density \(n\), a folded angle minimum defines orbitals \(g=\sqrt n\cos\theta\), \(h=\sqrt n\sin\theta\); the weighted spectral-gap certificate makes their paired/single determinant kinetic-minimizing among all ensembles with density \(n\). Thus a hypothetical local-potential representation forces the potential \(V=\Delta g/(2g)\) up to a constant. Translation limits of any \(V\in L^{3/2}+L^\infty\) yield an exterior form bound and imply exponential \(H^1\) integrability of \(g\) at an exponent \(a>k\). Since \(\theta=0\) on the median plane, the nonzero \(\lambda A(r)r^{-4}\) planar density tail contradicts the trace consequence of that integrability. Section 7 chooses an integer coupling \(1/Z\notin B\) so the finite sufficient tests guarantee the hypotheses of that fixed-density obstruction.

This reconstruction supports the source’s stated nonnumerical existence mechanism; it does not establish the connection as a new assay-design mechanism. No direct biology/assay transfer follows from the Kohn–Sham example.

## Quantitative route and decisive next gate

The closest outside lead for the *first* obstacle is the H\(_2^+\) literature. The paper’s one-electron operator is exactly the clamped two-centre Coulomb problem, with internuclear separation \(R=2D\). Harrell’s double-well analysis and Graffi–Grecchi–Harrell–Silverstone’s H\(_2^+\) \(1/R\) work analyze the exponentially small gerade/ungerade splitting; the often-quoted asymptotic begins \(d_*(R)\sim (4/e)R e^{-R}\). This identifies the scale and suggests a path to a gap lower bound. I did not find an evaluated remainder that certifies a lower bound at the fixed \(R=2\cdot10^{200}\): the cited statements are asymptotic or use implicit \(O(\cdot)\) constants. Harrell’s general translated-well theorem is relevant precedent, but its bound still requires quantitative evaluation of the single-well tails and does not, as retrieved here, give a plug-in numerical lower bound for this Coulomb case. The semiclassical smooth-well results likewise do not immediately supply the required constants for this singular Coulomb operator.

The decisive next gate is a **numerical, rigorous lower bound for \(d_*(D)\)** at \(D=10^{200}\), preferably by an explicit Herring-flux / prolate-spheroidal ODE argument with a remainder bound valid at that \(D\). Combine it with explicit bounds on \(\|V_{ee}R_0(\zeta)\|\) to get a concrete spectral-branch radius. This will test whether that route is viable, but it is not sufficient alone. In parallel, an effective proof must expose a computable majorant for \(\rho_D(R)\) and the scalar exterior remainders, then compute \(M\) and a positive lower bound on \(\alpha\). Finally it must obtain a quantitative stability bound for *all* angle minimizers and their \(C^1\) derivatives, so the strict tests (7.9) hold uniformly below one explicit \(\tau\). Only then can the formula above produce a charge bound.

As a caution about relying on analyticity alone, the two-level family \(A_\delta(t)=\operatorname{diag}(0,\delta-t)\) has a simple isolated ground state at \(t=0\) for every \(\delta>0\) and an analytic family, but the ground-state isolation radius is only \(\delta\). This does not counterexample the fixed Coulomb model; it shows why a qualitative statement “isolated and analytic” without a quantitative gap cannot furnish a uniform effective radius.
