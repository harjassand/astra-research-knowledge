# Coupled-flux Chernoff certificate for finite-horizon safety

**Status.** Internally reconstructed conditional lemma and algorithm sketch. The proof is a direct refinement of the pinned Astra N35 stopped exponential-supermartingale argument. It is not a priority claim; the mechanism is close to standard exponential-martingale/CTMDP methods. Its possible value is representing measured, coupled enzyme/resource constraints without replacing them by a looser independent rate box.

## Exact model and interface

Let there be $d$ species and $R$ ordinary molecular reactions $y_r\to y'_r$, with $y_r,y'_r\in\mathbb N^d$, jumps $\nu_r=y'_r-y_r$, and stoichiometric matrix $S=[\nu_1\ \cdots\ \nu_R]$. If the closed network has an integer vector $w>0$ with $w^\top S=0$, then $w^\top Z_t^V=w^\top Z_0^V$; at fixed volume $V$, the count class is finite. More generally, the safety certificate below only requires a supplied compact positive polytope $Q\subset c+\operatorname{im}S$, with rational coordinate bounds $0<x_j\le U_j$.

For each concentration $x\in Q$, the admissible enzyme/resource allocations form a nonempty compact set $K(x)\subseteq\prod_r[0,\bar k_r]$. A key application case is a measured rational polytope such as $A(x)k\le p$, where $p$ is the protein/turnover budget and the rows couple pathways that compete for the same enzyme, ATP, redox, or transport capacity. The controller may choose any predictable $k_t\in K(X^V_{t-})$, including history-dependent choices. Concentration jumps are $\nu_r/V$, with exact molecular intensities

\[
V k_{r,t}\phi^V_{y_r}(X^V_{t-}),\qquad
\phi^V_y(x)=V^{-|y|}\prod_j(Vx_j)_{y_j}.
\]

The safe set is a compact polytope

\[
Q=\{x\in c+\operatorname{im}S:g_i(x)=a_i^\top x+b_i\ge0,\ i=1,\ldots,m\},
\]

with proper relative facets. For each facet supply a collar width $\ell_i>0$. Write $\delta_{ir}=a_i^\top\nu_r$. For $\theta>0$, define the controlled exponential-generator function

\[
H_i(x,k;\theta)=\sum_{r=1}^R k_r x^{y_r}
             (e^{-\theta\delta_{ir}}-1).
\]

This is the support function that sees the *joint* admissible resource set. It does not take a separate worst case for every reaction.

## Theorem

Suppose there are $\theta_i,\eta_i>0$ such that

\[
\sup_{\substack{x\in Q\\0\le g_i(x)\le\ell_i}}
\sup_{k\in K(x)}H_i(x,k;\theta_i)\le-\eta_i
\tag{1}
\]

for every facet $i$. Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
J_i&=\max_r|\delta_{ir}|,\\
D_i&=\sum_r\bar k_rE_{y_r}(U)
                    |e^{-\theta_i\delta_{ir}}-1|,\\
C_i&\ge\max\left\{0,\sup_{x\in Q,k\in K(x)}H_i(x,k;\theta_i)\right\},\\
c_i&=\theta_i\ell_i,\qquad c_* =\min_i c_i,\\
\Lambda_Q&\ge\sup_{x\in Q,\,k\in K(x)}\sum_r k_r x^{y_r},\\
V_0&=\max_i\left\lceil\frac{2D_i}{\eta_i}\right\rceil\vee1,
\end{aligned}
\]

where $C_i$ and $\Lambda_Q$ may be any finite certified upper bounds on the displayed suprema, and

\[
E_y(U)=\sum_{j:y_j\ge2}\binom{y_j}{2}
   U_j^{y_j-1}\prod_{q\ne j}U_q^{y_q}.
\]

For $V\ge V_0$, any predictable allocation policy in $K(X^V_{t-})$, and an initial lattice point with $X^V_0\in Q$,

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)\le
\sum_{i=1}^m e^{-\theta_iVg_i(X^V_0)}
 +T\sum_{i=1}^m(VC_i+D_i)e^{-c_iV}
\tag{2}
\]

for every finite $T$, where $\tau_Q=\inf\{t:X^V_t\notin Q\}$. If every initial gap satisfies $g_i(X^V_0)\ge d_i>0$, then at $T=e^{c_*V/2}$,

\[
\Pr(\tau_Q\le e^{c_*V/2})\le
\sum_i e^{-\theta_i d_iV}
 +e^{-c_*V/2}\sum_i(VC_i+D_i).
\tag{3}
\]

The bound is uniform over all admissible predictable controllers. It is a finite-horizon statement; it does not imply survival forever or stochastic permanence.

## Proof

For a fixed facet define $\Phi_i(x)=e^{-\theta_iVg_i(x)}$. The exact predictable generator satisfies

\[
\frac{\mathcal L_t\Phi_i(x)}{\Phi_i(x)}
 =V\sum_r k_{r,t}\phi^V_{y_r}(x)
                  (e^{-\theta_i\delta_{ir}}-1).
\]

On the count lattice, the falling-factorial estimate gives $0\le x^{y_r}-\phi^V_{y_r}(x)\le E_{y_r}(U)/V$. Hence replacing the molecular propensity by its macroscopic monomial changes the displayed generator ratio by at most $D_i$, uniformly over all feasible allocations. In facet $i$'s collar, condition (1) therefore yields

\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi_i/\Phi_i\le -V\eta_i+D_i\le -V\eta_i/2\le0
\]

when $V\ge V_0$. Outside the collar, $\Phi_i\le e^{-c_iV}$, and the definition of $C_i$ gives

\[
\mathcal L_t\Phi_i\le (VC_i+D_i)e^{-c_iV}.
\]

Summing over facets bounds the generator of $\Phi=\sum_i\Phi_i$ everywhere in $Q$ by the second summand in $2$, per unit time. At a first exit at least one facet gap is negative, so $\Phi(X^V_{\tau_Q})\ge1$. A single jump changes facet $i$'s gap by at most $J_i/V$; the stopped potential is bounded by $\sum_i e^{\theta_iJ_i}$. The total pre-exit rate is at most $V\Lambda_Q$, so the stopped jump process is nonexplosive. Applying the compensated counting-process/Dynkin formula up to $T\wedge\tau_Q$, then using nonnegativity and the exit lower bound, proves $2$. Substitution of $T=e^{c_*V/2}$ gives $3$.

The strict exponential margin (1) is checkable from measured coupled flux constraints. A sufficient ordinary inward-drift condition is

\[
\inf_{x\in\text{collar}_i,k\in K(x)}
 a_i^\top\sum_r k_rx^{y_r}\nu_r>0,
\]

because bounded rates and bounded jumps make the derivative of $H_i(x,k;\theta)$ at $\theta=0$ uniformly negative. Direct optimization over $\theta_i$ and $K(x)$ usually gives a sharper exponent than the separate rate-box/quadratic-variation bounds in N35.

## Certificate algorithm and costs

1. Supply the integer reaction list, volume $V$, initial molecule counts, safe thresholds, and a conservation matrix $C$ with $C^\top S=0$. Positive rows of $C$ give pool bounds; a strictly positive row $w$ makes the entire closed count class finite. Compute exact rational left-kernel bases and check the initial integer reaction-lattice coset.
2. Supply the kinetic law and a *joint* allocation set $K(x)$, including enzyme abundance, $k_{cat}$, ATP/redox turnover, transport and controller constraints. Proteome budgets are inequalities on rates; ATP/ADP and NAD$P$H pools are stoichiometric invariants only when the modeled network is closed with respect to those moieties.
3. Specify $Q$, its relative facets, and the intended finite-horizon hazard set. Search rational $\theta_i,\ell_i$, then certify $1$ and $C_i$. For fixed $x$, the inner maximization over a polytope in $k$ is a linear program. For constant $K$, checking its vertices reduces the outer objective to finitely many mass-action polynomials multiplied by certified rational intervals for exponentials. Rational interval subdivision or real-algebraic optimization can prove strict margins. A failed margin yields either `UNKNOWN` (subdivision/precision exhaustion) or an explicit outward-feasible state/allocation.
4. Compute $D_i,C_i,V_0,c_*,\Lambda_Q$ and output $2$–$3$. Once the certificates are acquired, the constants require $O(mR)$ reaction/facet terms plus polynomial evaluation. Certificate acquisition has no general polynomial-time bound and may be exponential in species/reaction dimension; controller evaluation and input assays are extra costs. The state-space Bellman alternative below costs one continuous-time control solve on every reachable molecule-count state.

Inputs that require real acquisition are absolute metabolite counts/concentrations and cell volume; conserved adenylate and nicotinamide totals; calibrated reaction stoichiometry; enzyme abundances and $k_{cat}$ intervals; measured isotope-tracing flux constraints; and initial-condition and intervention logs. No experimental data or assay costs were obtained here. Open uptake/secretion, nucleotide synthesis, dilution, or degradation can remove the positive conservation law; then cumulative feed and fuel must be bounded explicitly or the finite-state reduction is invalid.

## Independent exact-control representation

When there is a strictly positive integer conservation vector (w), a closed finite-volume class

\[
\Omega_V=\{z\in\mathbb N^d:w^\top z=w^\top z_0\}
\]

has at most $\prod_j(\lfloor(w^\top z_0)/w_j\rfloor+1)$ states. For a bad-state set $D\subset\Omega_V$, the worst-case finite-horizon reachability probability $u(t,z)$ is the solution of the finite-state Bellman equation

\[
\partial_tu(t,z)=\sup_{k\in K(z/V)}
  \sum_r V k_r\phi^V_{y_r}(z/V)
        [u(t,z+\nu_r)-u(t,z)],
\]

with $u(0,z)=\mathbf1_D(z)$ and $u(t,z)=1$ on $D$. Each right-hand side maximizes a linear function over the supplied flux polytope. This gives the exact robust risk (up to the numerical ODE solve) and a worst-case allocation policy. It is the standard finite-state CTMDP route, not a claimed new theorem. It can benchmark the conservative analytical certificate $2$ on small networks. Its cost grows with Ω and with the time interval; the barrier certificate avoids enumerating all states once $Q$ and its strict collars are supplied.

## A coupled-rate witness and an attack

Take $2A\to A+B$ and $2B\to A+B$, so $A+B=V$ and $x=A/V$. Set $Q=[1/4,3/4]$. The coupled feasible allocation set

\[
K=\{(k_A,k_B)=(q,q):1\le q\le16\}
\]

can represent a co-regulated pair of opposing branch capacities under one bounded enzyme-allocation variable. Its deterministic drift is

\[
\dot x=q[(1-x)^2-x^2]=q(1-2x).
\]

For collar width $\ell=1/32$, the left collar ends at $x=9/32$, and the inward drift is at least $7/16$; the right collar is symmetric. Thus the whole coupled set has a strict margin. In the exponential-generator test with $\theta=1/2$,

\[
h(x)=x^2(e^{1/2}-1)+(1-x)^2(e^{-1/2}-1)
\]

is increasing on the left collar because $h'(x)=2x(e^{1/2}-1)+2(1-x)(1-e^{-1/2})>0$. Certified bounds $e^{1/2}-1<649/1000$ and $e^{-1/2}-1<-393/1000$ give $h(9/32)<-2427/16000<-3/20$. Since $h<0$ there, its supremum over $q\in[1,16]$ occurs at $q=1$, so $\eta=3/20$ is valid. On all $Q$, $h''(x)=2(e^{1/2}+e^{-1/2}-2)>0$, so its maximum is at an endpoint. The rational exponential enclosures give $h(1/4)<0$ and $16h(3/4)<5.448<5.45$; thus $C_i=109/20$ is a certified upper bound for both facets. Since $E_2(1)=1$, one may take $D=16(649+394)/1000=2086/125$ and $V_0=223$. Also

\[
\Lambda_Q=16\max_{x\in[1/4,3/4]}(x^2+(1-x)^2)=10.
\]

At $V=3000$ with the compatible center start $A_0=B_0=1500$, both initial gaps are $1/4$, $c_*=1/64$, and $3$ yields

\[
T=e^{23.4375}\approx1.5093\times10^{10},\qquad
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)<2.17\times10^{-6}
\]

in the stipulated model time units. A rational reciprocal-Taylor enclosure verifies the right side is $<217/10^8=2.17\times10^{-6}$; its Decimal diagnostic is $2.16877191\times10^{-6}$. The thinning candidate count $V\Lambda_QT$ is approximately $4.52791405\times10^{14}$ per run, so no full simulation was run. This is a bound, not a simulated observation. The exact coupled constraint is load-bearing: if replaced by the independent box $k_A,k_B\in[1,16]$, then at $x=1/4,k_A=16,k_B=1$, $\dot x=-7/16$, outward, and for $\theta=1/2$ the rational enclosure gives $H_L>1470431/3440640>0$. Thus the independent box fails the same exponential collar certificate even though the coupled set passes.

The bound still does not imply all-time safety. The same pinned Astra appendix gives a closed network $A+B\to2A$ at rate 2 and $A\to B$ at rate 1, with $\dot x=x(1-2x)$ and inward margins on $Q=[1/4,3/4]$, whose finite count chain reaches the absorbing state $A=0$ almost surely. This explicitly falsifies an attempted inference from deterministic inwardness or an exponential horizon to stochastic permanence.

## Falsifier and novelty boundary

The algebraic falsifier is a single feasible pair $(x,k)$ in a declared collar for which the certified $H_i(x,k;\theta_i)\le-\eta_i$ fails. A rate vector outside the measured allocation set, violated pool invariant, insufficient-reactant propensity mismatch, or incompatible initial integer totals also invalidates the certificate's application. An experimental falsifier is a replicated first-exit frequency whose valid lower confidence bound exceeds $2$, under logged initial molecule counts, volume, flux allocations and feed, after accounting for observation and model error.

The genuine new work would have to be a practical acquisition/optimization theorem for strict coupled-flux certificates from enzyme/proteomics data, with provable complexity and a nontrivial experimentally checked network where it materially improves the best equally informed control. The present result does not supply that. Conservation-law enumeration, constrained CTMDP reachability, exponential Lyapunov arguments, and N35's finite-volume safe-polytope proof already cover the core mechanisms. Treat this as a mathematically explicit, application-targeted refinement to test, not as a demonstrated frontier advance.
