FIXED-RANK INTERBLOCK EXTENSION — SIGNED TAGGED TENSOR
c02_l05, 7 October 2026. Deeper revision; INITIAL remains immutable.

THEOREM CANDIDATE
Suppose F=G+XY^T over rational complex entries, where G is block diagonal on a supplied partition B_1,...,B_m, each block has size b=O(log n), and X,Y are n-by-r with fixed r. Then every coefficient
 c_k(F)=sum_{I,J disjoint, |I|=|J|=k}|det F[I,J]|^2
is computable deterministically in polynomial bit time in n and the input length; when c_k>0, an exact sample from the corresponding determinant-squared law is computable by a Las Vegas algorithm in expected polynomial bit time. A direct signed tensor has effective charge/parity bond dimension at most 4(k+1)^2 per ordered tag pair and
 T_r^2,   T_r=sum_{p=0}^r binom(n,p)^2 binom(r,p),
ordered tag pairs. Thus for fixed r its size is polynomial, with a large n^{4r} factor. For rank one, the smaller three-moment recurrence and O(m^2) defect categories from `rank_one_block.txt` improve this representation.

No nonnegative local encoding is claimed. The tensor keeps complex phase and cofactor signs until the final scalar coefficient; all intermediate weights are exact rational complex values. The final coefficient and every conditional completion mass are nonnegative because they equal sums of determinant squared weights.

MINOR-EXPANSION IDENTITY
Let I=(i_1<...<i_k), J=(j_1<...<j_k), and for equal-size position subsets A,B subset [k], use R=I_A and C=J_B. For p=|A|=|B|, the matrix minor expansion and Cauchy–Binet give
 det((G+XY^T)[I,J]) =
 sum_{p=0}^{min(k,r)} sum_{A,B subset [k], |A|=|B|=p}
   (-1)^(sum A+sum B) det(G[I\R,J\C])
   sum_{S subset [r], |S|=p} det X[R,S] det Y[C,S].
Equivalently, enumerate tags tau=(p,R,C,S), with R,C subsets of [n], |R|=|C|=p and S subset [r], |S|=p. The tag coefficient is
 kappa_tau=det X[R,S]det Y[C,S].
A tag contributes only when R subset I and C subset J. Since G is block diagonal, det G[I\R,J\C] vanishes unless
 |I_t|-|R_t| = |J_t|-|C_t| for every block t;
when these equalities hold it is the product of the square local determinants. Thus the determinant is a sum of T_r local-product terms, each retaining its signed cofactor phase.

LOCAL TRANSFER FOR A TAG PAIR
To compute c_k, expand the squared determinant as a sum over ordered tag pairs (tau,tau'). The global scalar kappa_tau overline(kappa_tau') is factored out. For a block assignment I_t,J_t (disjoint), the local contribution is zero unless both tags satisfy membership and residual-balance conditions:
 R_t,R'_t subset I_t; C_t,C'_t subset J_t;
 |I_t|-|R_t|=|J_t|-|C_t|;
 |I_t|-|R'_t|=|J_t|-|C'_t|.
Otherwise put d_t=det G_t[I_t\R_t,J_t\C_t] and d'_t=det G_t[I_t\R'_t,J_t\C'_t]. The amplitude expansion sign for tau is (-1)^(sum of positions of R in I plus positions of C in J). Within block t, the position sum splits into a local part plus prefix-count terms:
 sigma_I(R)=sum_t( sigma_{I_t}(R_t) + |R_t| sum_{s<t}|I_s| ),
and the analogous formula holds for C and for tau'. Here sigma_{I_t} is the sum of within-block positions in the ordered list I_t. Consequently the product of the two tag signs is computed by a four-state parity transfer tracking the prefix parities
 (sum_{s<t}|I_s| mod 2, sum_{s<t}|J_s| mod 2).

For each tau,tau', form a 4-by-4 matrix of bivariate polynomials indexed by incoming/outgoing row/column parities. Its block-t entry is the sum over at most 3^b disjoint choices I_t,J_t satisfying the two validity tests, with monomial x^|I_t| y^|J_t|, coefficient d_t overline(d'_t), and sign
 (-1)^[ sigma_{I_t}(R_t)+sigma_{I_t}(R'_t)+sigma_{J_t}(C_t)+sigma_{J_t}(C'_t)
       + a(|R_t|+|R'_t|)+b(|C_t|+|C'_t|) ],
where (a,b) is the incoming parity state. The outgoing state adds (|I_t|,|J_t|) mod 2. Multiplying these matrices over blocks and extracting [x^k y^k] from initial state (0,0) to final state (k mod2,k mod2) gives the exact cross-term sum for this tag pair. Summing kappa_tau overline(kappa_tau') times these cross terms over all tag pairs is exactly c_k(F).

The signs do not require a positive representation. They are finite exact rationals, so cancellations are computed rather than estimated.

ORDERING AND EXACT-BIT DETAILS
The block partition need not initially be contiguous in the coordinate order. Apply a simultaneous permutation P to rows and columns so the blocks are contiguous, and replace X,Y by P^T X,P^T Y. The legal disjoint pairs are carried bijectively to legal pairs. Each minor changes only by a row permutation sign times a column permutation sign, so its squared magnitude and c_k are invariant. Sampled subsets are mapped back by P. The displayed prefix-parity formula is for this contiguous block order.

For rational complex data, let D be the product of all positive denominators appearing in the real and imaginary parts of G,X,Y; its bit length is at most the input length L. A tag term with update order p has denominator dividing D^(k+p): it uses p entries from X, p from Y, and k-p from G. Each cross-tag transfer term therefore has denominator dividing D^(2k+p+p') and hence D^(2k+2r). This gives a shared denominator of polynomial bit length O((n+r)L), with polynomial-size numerators. This avoids an uncontrolled denominator-growth assumption when adding rational transfer entries. If c_k>0, each conditional completion mass is an exact nonnegative rational; integerizing the at-most-3^b child masses and using rejection from a power-of-two range gives an exact sampler in expected polynomial bit time. The algorithm is not a sampler when c_k=0, because that sector has no target distribution.

PHASE-CANCELLATION / POSITIVITY LOSS
The tag-pair tensor must retain off-diagonal tag pairs. In `tag_dephasing_check.py`, G has two 2x2 diagonal blocks with G[1,2]=G[3,4]=1 (one-based notation), while H=XY^T has rank exactly two and only H[1,4]=H[3,2]=1. The legal I={1,3}, J={2,4} minor has tag amplitudes +1 from p=0 and -1 from p=2; its p=1 terms vanish. The resulting F has rank one, so c_2(F)=0, while the diagonal-only tag contraction contributes 1+1=2 on that pair. Therefore simply deleting cross-tag interference has an unbounded multiplicative error (even infinite at a zero coefficient) on a minimal-rank, off-block update fixture. This is a precise failure of that dephasing shortcut, not a lower bound against every possible nonnegative tensor or sampler.

CONDITIONAL SAMPLING
For any fixed prefix of block assignments, define its completion mass as the sum of |det F[I,J]|^2 over all valid suffixes with final row and column counts k. Reuse the tag-pair expansion, multiply the fixed-prefix local contributions and prefix parity, then run the suffix four-state bivariate transfer. Summing all tag pairs yields that exact completion mass. For each candidate disjoint assignment (I_t,J_t) in the next block, compute its child completion mass by fixing that block too. These child masses are nonnegative rationals and sum to the parent mass. Sample the next block from these masses by clearing denominators and exact rejection sampling; repeat. This is an exact conditional sampler, not a signed-weight sampler. The algorithm may encounter cancellations internally, but it never samples a negative term.

COST AND BOND DIMENSION
There are at most T_r tags and T_r^2 ordered pairs. For fixed r, T_r=O(n^(2r)2^r). Each block enumerates at most 3^b disjoint row/column assignments; local determinants have order at most b. A tag-pair DP tracks row and column counts up to k and two parities, so its effective bond state has at most 4(k+1)^2 entries. Polynomial convolution across m blocks and all tag pairs is therefore
 T_r^2 * poly(n,3^b,b,L),
where L is the rational input bit length. If b=O(log n) and r is fixed, this is polynomial in n and L. The exact sampler uses the same suffix tables and at most 3^b candidate branches per block. Numerators/denominators have polynomial bit length: each local determinant has poly(b,L) bits, each transfer sums at most polynomially many terms, and the number of blocks and tags is polynomial for fixed r. A decomposition may be supplied or recovered by exact rank factorization of F minus its block diagonal part; the latter cost is charged as polynomial preprocessing.

The exponent grows with r. This is not a polynomial result for variable r, and it is not an efficient practical algorithm claim for r>=2.

EXACT CHECK
`rank2_block_tensor_check.py` builds the 4-state parity transfer explicitly for two 2x2 blocks and rank r=2, sums all 69^2 ordered tag pairs, and matches direct determinant enumeration for k=0,1,2. It checks local sign decomposition, rectangular-block balance, and exact cancellations on a small integer instance. `rank2_minor_expansion_check.py` separately verifies the minor/Cauchy–Binet expansion on all 19 legal minors of that instance. `tag_dephasing_check.py` verifies complete cancellation against positive diagonal-tag mass for a minimal-rank update. These are finite fixtures only; the universal transfer derivation above is the theorem candidate.

RELATION TO POSITIVITY / OLD RESULTS
This signed network resolves the interface by carrying phase tags, not by replacing F with absolute values or by invoking the signed-hole stability inequality as an oracle. It avoids the common-orientation projection whose norm can be 2^-q on the swap-block family. It also does not contradict the F4 even-windability obstruction: the internal tensor entries are signed/complex. If a nonnegative matching-circuit terminal signature is required instead, the supplied F4 direct-sum lower bound still gives comparison cost >=2^q (or coefficientwise K>=2^(q/4)).

The all-size signed ambient-hole exchange and OA113 aggregate capacity remain independent baseline lemmas. This fixed-r class has its own exact coefficient/sampler route, so it does not establish an OA113-style energy inequality for arbitrary F. Dense unrestricted F may have rank n off the chosen blocks; the tag count then becomes exponential.
