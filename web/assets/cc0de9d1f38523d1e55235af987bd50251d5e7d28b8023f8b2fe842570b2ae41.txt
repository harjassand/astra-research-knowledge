STRONGEST CLAIM
For the critical-spin family
  rho_N(A,b) = exp(2 J^2/N + Q_A/N^(3/2) + b.J/N^(3/4)) / Z,
there is a rational sequence of growing parameters for which the whole density stays a constant trace distance from every fully separable state. This is a physical failure of extending the product-preparation conclusion beyond its fixed compact parameter interface, not a numerical precision defect. A second exact axis-anisotropy family shows that noncompact anisotropy can move the spin radius from the N^(3/4) critical scale to macroscopic order. The finite-bit sampler's constants also grow superpolynomially with the public bounds; that is a separate limitation of this implementation, not a lower bound on all possible algorithms.

1. EXACT PHYSICAL COUNTEREXAMPLE: STRONG ANTIFERROMAGNETIC GROWTH
Take N=4k^2, so sqrt(N) is an integer, set b=0 and
  A_N = -(N+2) sqrt(N) I_3.
Then Q_(A_N)=-(N+2)sqrt(N) J^2, and the exponent is exactly
  2J^2/N - (N+2)sqrt(N) J^2/N^(3/2) = -J^2.
Thus rho_N = exp(-J^2)/Tr exp(-J^2). The supplied finite-bit construction does not cover this sequence: with M_N=||A_N||=(N+2)sqrt(N), its seed coefficient is t0=2/N-M_N/N^(3/2)=-1.

Let d_(N,j) be the multiplicity of spin j for N even:
  d_(N,j) = binom(N,N/2-j) (2j+1)/(N/2+j+1),  j=0,...,N/2.
The exact sector probability is proportional to
  (2j+1) d_(N,j) exp[-j(j+1)].
For fixed j, d_(N,j)/d_(N,0) -> 2j+1. Also d_(N,j)/d_(N,0) <=2j+1, so the relative sector weights are dominated by the summable sequence (2j+1)^2 exp[-j(j+1)]. Hence the probability that the spin-j sector is observed converges to
  q_j = (2j+1)^2 exp[-j(j+1)] / Z_inf,
  Z_inf = sum_(l>=0) (2l+1)^2 exp[-l(l+1)].

Use the following valid randomized-axis measurement: draw n uniformly from S^2, measure every qubit in the sigma.n basis, and record the event E that the total spin component is zero. Conditional on sector j, rho_N is maximally mixed on the spin-j irrep and E has probability 1/(2j+1). Therefore
  Pr_rhoN(E) -> c_* = [sum_(j>=0)(2j+1)exp[-j(j+1)]] / Z_inf
       = 0.6220455028... .

For every fully separable state, Pr(E) <= 8/sqrt(N). Proof: by convexity it is enough to take a pure product with Bloch vectors u_i, |u_i|=1. For fixed n, the local outcomes are independent Bernoulli variables with p_i=(1+n.u_i)/2. Write V(n)=sum_i p_i(1-p_i)=(N-n^T S n)/4, S=sum_i u_i u_i^T. Fourier inversion and
  |1-p+p exp(it)|^2 = 1-4p(1-p)sin^2(t/2)
  <= exp[-4p(1-p)sin^2(t/2)]
show that the atom at total magnetization zero is at most min(1,c/sqrt(V)), c=sqrt(pi)/(2sqrt(2)); here sin(|t|/2)>=|t|/pi for |t|<=pi. Let lambda_1>=lambda_2>=lambda_3 be eigenvalues of S, sum lambda_i=N. If lambda_1<=N/2 then V>=N/8 for every n. Otherwise lambda_2<=N/4; writing z=n.e_1 gives
  N-n^T S n >= N/4 (1-z^2), hence V>=N/16 (1-z^2).
Averaging min(1,sqrt(2pi)/(sqrt(N)sqrt(1-z^2))) over uniform z in [-1,1], with the polar caps split off, gives <=8/sqrt(N). Mixtures preserve this bound.

Since trace distance dominates the probability difference of any measurement event,
  inf_(sigma fully separable) T(rho_N,sigma) >= c_* - o(1).
This rules out any asymptotically vanishing whole-density product-mixture preparation error on this growing-parameter sequence. It is not a claim against general entangled-state preparation.

The same obstruction tolerates a growing traceless anisotropy. Let
  D_N=N diag(1,-1,0),  A_N=-(N+2)sqrt(N) I_3 + D_N.
Then ||D_N||=N -> infinity, and K_N=-J^2+Q_(D_N)/N^(3/2). Since J^2 commutes with Q_(D_N) and |Q_(D_N)|<=N J^2, on spin j the eigenvalues of K_N lie between
  -(1+N^(-1/2))j(j+1) and -(1-N^(-1/2))j(j+1).
For fixed j the perturbation norm is O(j^2/sqrt(N)); for N>=4 the sector tails are dominated by (2j+1)^2 exp[-j(j+1)/2]. The same dominated-convergence argument gives the same limiting q_j and c_*. The randomized-axis event has conditional probability 1/(2j+1) for any state inside each sector because its axis average is I/(2j+1) on V_j. Thus this noncompact anisotropic perturbation does not remove the constant separation. The dominant scalar term still supplies the antiferromagnetic mechanism; this does not assert that D_N alone causes entanglement.

2. PHYSICAL SCALING CHANGE FROM A GROWING ONE-AXIS ANISOTROPY
For N a square, take A_N=sqrt(N) diag(1,0,0), b=0. The exponent is
  K_N = 2J^2/N + J_x^2/N.
J^2 and J_x commute, so the exact joint (j,m_x) weight is
  d_(N,j) exp[2j(j+1)/N + m_x^2/N].
Writing x=2j/N in [0,1] and v=m_x/j in [-1,1], the large-N exponent per spin, up to a parameter-independent constant, is
  Phi(x,v)=h((1-x)/2)-log(2) + x^2/2 + x^2 v^2/4,
where h(p)=-p log p-(1-p)log(1-p). The maximum over v is at |v|=1. At v=1,
  Phi(x,1)=x^2/4-x^4/12+O(x^6)>0
for sufficiently small x>0, while Phi(0,1)=0. The compact variational problem therefore has its maximizers at x>0; the spin radius is macroscopic rather than O(N^(3/4)). This is a physical scaling failure of the compact critical law, though it is not a separability lower bound: ordered ferromagnetic phases can still admit product-mixture descriptions.

The diagnostic work/cycle6/c07_l04/anisotropy_scaling.py computes exact integer spin multiplicities and floating-point normalized sector sums for N=256,1024,4096. The median j/N is 0.4297, 0.4297, 0.4292. These finite values illustrate the variational prediction only; the Laplace argument is the asymptotic derivation.

3. DISTINCT NUMERICAL/COST BOUNDARY
The finite-bit construction and audit in work/cycle1/spin_finite_precision_compiler.txt and work/cycle1/spin_finite_precision_audit.txt assume fixed public rational M,B, and explicitly require t0>0. Its sufficient resampling budget contains
  V_star = 3 exp(B^2/4) [exp(384(D_w+1/24)^2) I_4 + sqrt(pi)/4] / L_base,
  D_w=(6M+1)/2,  L_base=(7/3)exp(-M/2-1/12),
with R=ceil(V_star/(4 eta^2)); it also has N0 >= (8(D_w+1)/delta_HS)^2. Thus the stated route's constants include exp(Theta(M^2)) and exp(B^2/4) factors. If M_N=N^alpha for any fixed alpha>0, this sufficient finite-bit budget is superpolynomial in N, even in regimes where t0>0 (for example A_N=-sqrt(N)I gives t0=1/N). This does NOT prove an intrinsic resource lower bound. If M_N>=2sqrt(N), the selected positive-heat seed has t0<=0 and that specific construction is outside its domain. The strong antiferromagnetic example above fails physically as well as leaving the construction's domain.

A bounded field-growth illustration: the compact critical observation has quartic density proportional to exp[-4|x|^4/3+b.x]. If b_N=(log N)e_z, its saddle has |x|=(3 log N/16)^(1/3), so the critical coordinate J/N^(3/4) drifts to infinity; the corresponding spin remains submacroscopic. The supplied constants contain exp(B^(4/3)), which at B=log N is superpolynomial. This is a boundary calculation, not a claimed uniform finite-N decoder theorem for growing b.
