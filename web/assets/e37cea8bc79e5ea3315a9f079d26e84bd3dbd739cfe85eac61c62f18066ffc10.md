# Kohn–Sham nonrepresentability: boundary under nonlocal transfer

## Verdict

The pinned source's exact statement is a no-go only for a **single real, spin-independent local multiplier** `v ∈ L^{3/2}(R^3)+L^∞(R^3)`, with ground-state ensembles allowed. It does not establish a quantitative distance from the target density to nearby local-potential ensemble densities, and it explicitly disclaims spin-dependent and nonlocal potential classes.

There is a direct counterexample to extending the no-go to a broad, precisely specified nonlocal class. Conditional only on the source's Proposition 5.1, its own kinetic-minimizing determinant can be made an exact noninteracting ground state by an engineered spin-independent nonlocal one-body **quadratic-form perturbation with relative form bound zero**. The represented density is exactly the obstructed Coulomb density, and the determinant retains the exact constrained-search kinetic energy `Tens[n]`. This is a mathematical scope counterexample, not a claim that the constructed operator is a conventional Fock, screened-exchange, hybrid, or pseudopotential operator.

The approximate-local question remains `UNKNOWN`: the fixed-density trace contradiction does not itself give a positive L1, H1, or energy-graph distance to the set of local-potential ensemble densities. Spin-dependent local potentials and narrower physically structured nonlocal kernels also remain outside this result.

## Pinned-source interface

The local source is `work/agents/c9_frontier_transfer/sources/kohn_sham.pdf`, *A Coulomb ground-state density without Kohn–Sham ensemble representation* (45 pages; pinned local copy). Theorem 1.1 states that a specified three-electron two-centre Coulomb ground-state density is not a ground-state ensemble density for any real, spin-independent `v ∈ L^{3/2}+L^∞`. The proof is ensemble-wide: Section 5 supplies a constrained kinetic-energy certificate and identifies the only possible regular local potential; Section 6 forces an exterior potential limit and a weighted H1 trace that conflicts with the nonzero planar density tail. The local potential class and exact density are load-bearing. The charge is exactly specified through a nonnumerical spectral formula, but the source gives no effective numerical bound; this is an existence construction rather than a demonstrated ordinary molecule or practical calculation.

Proposition 5.1 gives the exact transfer data used below. Set

`g = sqrt(n) cos(theta)`, `h = sqrt(n) sin(theta)`, and `u0 = g/sqrt(2)`, `u1 = h`.

The source gives `||g||_2^2=2`, `||h||_2^2=1`, orthogonality of `u0,u1`, `n=|g|^2+|h|^2`, and the determinant

`Phi = (u0 up) wedge (u0 down) wedge (u1 up)`

with `T[|Phi><Phi|] = Tens[n]`, where `Tens[n]` is minimized over **all** three-electron ensembles with density `n`. The orbitals lie in H1, which is all the form construction requires.

## Exact nonlocal representation construction

Let the spatial one-particle space be `L2(R^3)` and the spin-orbital space be `H=L2(R^3;C^2)`. Let `P_sp` be the spatial orthogonal projection onto `U=span{u0,u1}`, and set

`P = P_sp tensor I_spin`, `Q = I-P`.

For `psi ∈ H1(R^3;C^2)`, write

`t[psi] = (1/2) integral |grad psi|^2`.

For any fixed `C>0`, define the one-body generalized-KS Hamiltonian by its closed quadratic form

`q_NL[psi] = t[Q psi] + C ||Q psi||_2^2`, with form domain H1.

Equivalently, relative to the free kinetic form `t`, the nonlocal form potential is

`k[psi] = q_NL[psi]-t[psi]`
`       = C||Q psi||_2^2 - t[P psi] - 2 Re t(P psi,Q psi)`.

This is explicitly nonlocal: it depends on global projections onto the two source orbitals and includes finite-rank derivative couplings. It is spin-independent because the same spatial projection acts on both spin components.

### Form admissibility and spectral ground space

Since `P` has finite rank and its range is contained in H1, there is a finite `M` with `t[P psi] ≤ M ||P psi||_2^2`. Cauchy–Schwarz for the kinetic form gives

`2|t(P psi,Q psi)| ≤ eta t[Q psi] + eta^{-1} t[P psi]`

for every `eta>0`. Also `t[Q psi] ≤ 2t[psi]+2t[P psi]`. Combining these estimates, for each `epsilon>0` there is a finite `B_epsilon` such that

`|k[psi]| ≤ epsilon t[psi] + B_epsilon ||psi||_2^2`.

Thus `k` has relative form bound zero with respect to the free kinetic energy. The form `q_NL` is closed: `q_NL[psi]+||psi||^2` controls `||Q psi||_{H1}^2+||P psi||_2^2`, which is equivalent to the H1 norm because `P` is finite rank with H1 range.

Its kernel is exactly `P H=U tensor C^2`; on the orthogonal complement, `q_NL[psi] ≥ C||Q psi||^2`. Hence the one-body Hamiltonian is nonnegative, has ground energy zero, and has a gap of at least `C` above its finite-dimensional ground orbital space.

Second-quantize this one-body operator for three noninteracting fermions. The determinant `Phi` occupies only orbitals in `P H`, so it has many-body energy zero and is a ground state. Its spin-summed density is

`n_Phi = 2|u0|^2 + |u1|^2 = |g|^2+|h|^2 = n`.

Therefore `|Phi><Phi|` is a pure ground-state ensemble representing the exact density excluded by Theorem 1.1 in the local multiplier class. Moreover its ordinary kinetic expectation remains `Tens[n]`, exactly the source's ensemble constrained-search minimum. Thus the counterexample survives even if one compares both density and the noninteracting kinetic-energy value: their discrepancy is zero.

## Transfer boundary

This construction refutes the blanket extension “the density is not representable by any noninteracting ground-state ensemble” and refutes extension to the class of all symmetric, infinitesimally kinetic-form-bounded nonlocal one-body perturbations. It does **not** refute the stated scalar-local theorem.

The class is deliberately broad. The engineered form cancels kinetic couplings to a selected finite-dimensional occupied subspace and puts a positive projector penalty on its complement. That is enough for a mathematical generalized one-body Hamiltonian, but it is not shown to arise from Coulomb Fock exchange, screened exchange, a local-plus-Fock hybrid, or a standard pseudopotential family. Those physically structured nonlocal subclasses need separate tests. The spin-independent construction also says nothing about whether a spin-dependent **local** multiplier can represent this density.

The source's exact tail proof is not a stability theorem. Its angle, ensemble kinetic certificate, and formal potential are tied to the exact density. Without a separately proved closedness or quantitative separation result for a specified local-potential family, no positive distance in L1, H1, weighted tail, or energy-graph topology follows. No such local approximate-representation bound is claimed here.

## Primary prior-art boundary

Harriman's explicit orbital construction shows why density representability by orbitals is distinct from ground-state v-representability: arbitrary admissible densities can be decomposed into orthonormal orbitals, but the construction does not supply a common local ground-state potential. Gilbert's nonlocal-potential extension makes the one-particle density kernel, rather than only its diagonal density, the natural variable for nonlocal external operators. Seidl–Görling–Vogl–Majewski–Levy provide the standard physical GKS precedent using a screened nonlocal exchange part together with a local density component. These works motivate the distinction; the finite-rank form construction above is our internal counterexample and should not be attributed to those papers.

The pinned source itself also distinguishes exact ensemble representability from a determinant-only inversion and explicitly says it makes no assertion for larger distributional, spin-dependent, or nonlocal classes. Ullrich–Kohn's ground-state ensemble framework is consistent with allowing degenerate determinants in the local theory; our counterexample uses a pure state lying in a degenerate nonlocal ground space.
