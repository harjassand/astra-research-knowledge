INDEPENDENT AUDIT: PARITY-RESOLVED SCHUR SPIN-BAND LOWER MASS
8 October 2026

Scope and verdict
-----------------
This note reconstructs only Section 4 of
`outputs/research/sol_collision_precision/quantum_tail_precision.txt`.
The Schur-band mass inequality (4.3), including its numerical constants,
follows from the exact Schur law, an elementary Stirling lower bound, and a
parity count. No local-limit approximation is used. I find no gap in this
section under the conditions stated below. The later score/tail argument is
outside this audit.

1. Conditions and notation
--------------------------
Let n be an integer with n>=4, let Q be a positive integer, and let
0<r<=1/2. Put t=atanh(r), a=(1+r)/2, b=(1-r)/2, and assume the radius
envelope

    r sqrt(n) <= c1.

For the selected band require Q<=n/128. The Schur label is the integer
L=2j in

    Lambda_n={L in {0,...,n}: L congruent to n mod 2}.

The selected input band j in [16Q,32Q] is equivalently
L in [32Q,64Q]. Q is an integer because it is a branch dimension; this
integrality is used in the exact parity-point count below. The band-mass
bound itself does not require Q>=1/r. That additional condition appears in
Section 3 and is needed when this mass is combined with the score deficit.

Since Q<=n/128, every L<=64Q<=n/2. Hence for k=(n-L)/2 we have
k>=n/4>=1 and n-k>=(n/2)>=2. These are the conditions needed for the
factorial bounds and for x=L/n to lie in [0,1/2]. In the full critical
regime c0<=r sqrt(n)<=c1, the upper envelope implies r<=1/2 whenever
n>=4c1^2. Thus the source's “sufficiently large n” condition supplies
r<=1/2. The lower envelope c0 is not used in the band-mass calculation;
it ensures nontriviality/feasibility of the later resource range.

2. Exact Schur identity and first lower bound
---------------------------------------------
The spin-L/2 multiplicity is

    m_L = binom(n,k)-binom(n,k-1)
        = [2(L+1)/(n+L+2)] binom(n,k),
    k=(n-L)/2,

with binom(n,-1)=0 at L=n. In the product state
rho_r^{tensor n}, the eigenvalue on a total-spin-L/2 magnetic vector is
(ab)^(n/2) exp(2tm), because a/b=exp(2t). Summing over m=-L/2,...,L/2
therefore gives the exact Schur probability

    P_L=m_L (ab)^(n/2) sinh((L+1)t)/sinh(t).              (A)

This has the continuous t=0 value m_L 2^(-n)(L+1). Set

    pi_0(L)=2^(-n) binom(n,(n-L)/2),
    ab=(1-r^2)/4.

Substituting the exact multiplicity into (A) yields the equivalent identity

    P_L = [2(L+1)/(n+L+2)] pi_0(L)(1-r^2)^(n/2)
                         * sinh((L+1)t)/sinh(t).           (B)

For t>=0,

    sinh((L+1)t)/sinh(t)
       = sum_(u=0)^L exp((L-2u)t) >= L+1.                 (C)

The inequality follows by pairing the terms exp(x)+exp(-x)>=2, with an
unpaired central 1 when L is even; at t=0 equality holds. Also
n+L+2<=2(n+1) for 0<=L<=n. Hence (B) gives

    P_L >= [(L+1)^2/(n+1)] pi_0(L)(1-r^2)^(n/2).         (D)

For the common thermal factor, use

    log(1-u)>=-u/(1-u), 0<=u<1.

With u=r^2<=1/4, this gives

    (n/2)log(1-r^2)
      >= -(n/2) r^2/(1-r^2)
      >= -(2/3)nr^2 >= -(2/3)c1^2,

and therefore

    (1-r^2)^(n/2)>=exp(-2c1^2/3).                         (E)

3. Central-binomial Stirling bound
----------------------------------
Let x=L/n in [0,1/2], p=k/n=(1-x)/2. Apply

    sqrt(2 pi s)(s/e)^s <= s!
      <= sqrt(2 pi s)(s/e)^s exp(1/(12s))

to n!, k!, and (n-k)!, using the lower bound for n! and upper bounds for
the denominator factorials. Since k>=n/4 and n-k>=n/2, the total
Stirling correction satisfies

    1/(12k)+1/(12(n-k)) <= 1/(3n)+1/(6n)=1/(2n)<=1/8<1/6.

Consequently

    pi_0(L) >= [2 exp(-1/6)/sqrt(2 pi n(1-x^2))] exp(-n I(x))
             >= [2 exp(-1/6)/sqrt(2 pi n)] exp(-n I(x)),

where

    I(x)=[(1+x)log(1+x)+(1-x)log(1-x)]/2.

The first displayed denominator is equivalently
sqrt(2 pi n(1-x^2)); it is written only to make the binomial variance
factor explicit. Since 1-x^2<=1, removing that factor weakens the
prefactor as shown. Also I(0)=I'(0)=0 and

    I''(x)=1/(1-x^2)<=4/3, 0<=x<=1/2,

so two integrations give I(x)<=2x^2/3. Finally

    2 exp(-1/6)/sqrt(2 pi) > 1/2,

and hence the claimed uniform lower bound follows:

    pi_0(L) >= [1/(2 sqrt(n))] exp[-2L^2/(3n)].          (F)

4. Count of parity-allowed points and band sum
----------------------------------------------
The endpoints 32Q and 64Q are even integers. If n is even, the allowed
L's are even and both endpoints occur, giving 16Q+1 points. If n is odd,
the allowed L's are odd; the points are 32Q+1, 32Q+3,...,64Q-1, giving
exactly 16Q points. Thus in either case

    # (Lambda_n intersect [32Q,64Q]) >=16Q.               (G)

For each such L, combine (D), (E), and (F). Use n+1<=2n,
L+1>=32Q, and L<=64Q:

    P_L >= [(32Q)^2/(2n)] [1/(2 sqrt(n))]
            exp(-2c1^2/3) exp[-2(64Q)^2/(3n)]
         = 256 exp(-2c1^2/3) Q^2/n^(3/2)
            exp[-8192 Q^2/(3n)].

Summing over at least 16Q allowed points gives

    P(16Q<=j<=32Q)
      >=4096 exp(-2c1^2/3) Q^3/n^(3/2)
                       exp[-8192 Q^2/(3n)].               (H)

This reproduces (4.3), including both constants 4096 and 8192/3.

5. Conditions needed when used in the full argument
----------------------------------------------------
For (H) alone it is enough that n>=4, Q is a positive integer,
Q<=n/128, 0<r<=1/2, and r sqrt(n)<=c1. If Q is also required to obey
the preceding score section's Q>=1/r, the interval is nonempty only when
1/r<=n/128, equivalently rn>=128. Under r>=c0/sqrt(n), this follows
for n>=(128/c0)^2 (with endpoint rounding handled by the integer Q).
The band mass itself does not use this lower-Q condition.

For fixed c0,c1 and the source's large-n critical regime, r<=1/2 follows
from r<=c1/sqrt(n) once n>=4c1^2. The text should make explicit that this
large-n choice is part of the hypotheses before using (E). No other hidden
condition on parity or on the location of the band is needed.

The bound can be very small and need not be <=1 for all formal parameter
values; it is a valid lower bound regardless, and in the stated regime the
later use only needs positivity with its displayed scale. No approximation
claim is made.
