DEPENDENT RAW-PATH CONCENTRATION AND A RANK-FREE PREDICTIVE-RISK CERTIFICATE
Cycle 3 result | 2026-10-08

STATUS
======

PROVED HERE: for one arbitrary finite-alphabet trajectory, a delayed-martingale theorem controls every entry of a universal finite prefix/suffix feature tensor simultaneously around its predictable pathwise compensator. It handles overlapping future windows by a residue-class decomposition, and handles all post-hoc feature/basis selection within the declared finite universal dictionary through one Frobenius-norm event. A separate rank-free confidence sequence certifies the path-average conditional risk of an online predictor, with no mixing, rank, singular-gap, or independent holdout assumption.

OBSTRUCTION PROVED: this certificate is not a confidence statement for stationary/future risk of a fitted predictor. Even among stationary, irreducible, two-state output HMMs, an unknown arbitrarily slow transition makes the stationary risk unidentifiable from any fixed finite path with uniform high confidence. A one-parameter lower bound quantifies the required trajectory length when the transition rate is fixed.

NOT CLAIMED: a population-Hankel concentration theorem without a mixing/ergodicity promise; a uniform confidence bound for an uncountable retrospective predictor class without norm/entropy/code control; an efficient realization learner; or novelty/priority. The martingale ingredients are standard. The exact-delayed-feature application and the two-state obstruction are internally derived and not externally reviewed.

1. Data interface and universal feature family
==============================================

Let Sigma be a known alphabet of size a>=2 and let X_1,...,X_T be one observed path. It may come from any HMM with unknown hidden state count, initial law, transition matrix, and emissions. The results below in fact need only the observed filtration F_t=sigma(X_1,...,X_t), not an HMM assumption. There are no resets, entry queries, supplied word dictionary, mixing promise, singular-value margin, or hidden-state observations.

To make “all features” finite and charge them, let maximum past and future word lengths P,Q>=0 satisfy P+Q<=T. These are horizon caps, not a supplied feature dictionary. Enumerate every raw suffix-of-past word u in U_P=Sigma^{<=P} and every raw prefix-of-future word v in V_Q=Sigma^{<=Q}. Their counts and the full coordinate count are

  p=sum_(i=0)^P a^i,   q=sum_(j=0)^Q a^j,   K=pq.

Set n=T-P-Q+1 and index the n valid centers by t=P,...,T-Q. For a word u, let A_t(u)=1 when the |u| symbols ending at t equal u; A_t(empty)=1. For v, let B_t(v)=1 when X_(t+1)...X_(t+|v|)=v; B_t(empty)=1. Form the whole matrix contribution

  Z_t[u,v]=A_t(u) B_t(v),       u in U_P, v in V_Q.

At every center and for every length pair (i,j), exactly one cell with |u|=i, |v|=j is 1. Thus each Z_t has m=(P+1)(Q+1) ones and ||Z_t||_F=sqrt(m). Cells are dependent: they share symbols, and many coordinates reuse the same prefix/suffix events. No cell-independence model is used. The empirical tensor/matrix is Zbar=n^(-1)sum_t Z_t. The statistic for (u,v) is an overlapping-window count of the contiguous word uv.

The natural predictable reference is

  Gamma_t = E[Z_t | F_t],       Gbar = n^(-1)sum_t Gamma_t.

For each cell, Gamma_t[u,v]=A_t(u) Pr(X_(t+1:t+|v|)=v | F_t). This conditional probability uses the entire observed history and the unknown HMM filter; Gbar is a random, path-dependent compensator. It is generally not computable from the raw path alone. This is deliberate: concentration around Gbar is distribution-free, while replacing it by a fixed stationary Hankel matrix would require additional assumptions. For a stationary source E[Zbar]=H_PQ, where H_PQ[u,v]=Pr(X_1...X_|uv|=uv), but the theorem below does not assert that Zbar is close to H_PQ.

2. Delayed martingale theorem for matrices, tensors, and vectors
=================================================================

Theorem 1 (overlapping-window concentration around the predictable compensator). Let H>=1 be a maximum future lookahead. Let Z_t be any finite-dimensional vector, matrix, or tensor statistic with coordinates in [0,1], measurable with respect to F_(t+H), and let Gamma_t=E[Z_t|F_t]. Suppose ||Z_t||_F<=B almost surely (for vectors use Euclidean norm; for tensors use the flattened Frobenius norm). For n consecutive valid centers, define Zbar=n^(-1)sum Z_t and Gbar=n^(-1)sum Gamma_t. For every delta in (0,1), with probability at least 1-delta,

  ||Zbar-Gbar||_F <= (2B/n) [ sqrt(2 H n x) + (2/3) H x ],
  x=log(2H/delta).                                                   (1)

Consequently every coordinate, every submatrix/subtensor, and every post-hoc linear statistic <A,Zbar-Gbar> with ||A||_F<=1 obeys the same bound on this one event. For ||A||_F<=c_A, multiply the bound by c_A. For a matrix, the operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm. If Z_t is the complete raw prefix/suffix indicator matrix in Section 1, use B=sqrt(m); then (1) is

  ||Zbar-Gbar||_F <= 2 sqrt(m) [ sqrt(2 H x/n) + 2H x/(3n) ].         (2)

The feature dictionary size K does not enter this simultaneous concentration radius: the full vector-norm event controls every coordinate and every data-selected subset. K still enters the enumeration, output, and storage costs. A finite family of M distinct feature maps/statistics can be concatenated into a direct-sum tensor; then use its actual B and H in (1). If a candidate is an arbitrary nonlinear function of the data rather than a bounded-norm linear statistic of this tensor, it is not covered without an additional complexity bound.

Proof. Fix a residue class modulo H among the center indices. Consecutive centers t_j in that class differ by H. Put D_t=Z_t-Gamma_t. It is measurable with respect to F_(t+H), has conditional mean zero given F_t, and ||D_t||_F<=2B by conditional Jensen. Therefore within one residue class the partial sums of D_t form a Hilbert-space martingale with increments bounded by c=2B. The Frobenius space is a Hilbert space, hence (2,1)-smooth. The exponential martingale inequality for 2-smooth-space martingales (Pinelis, Theorem 3.1) gives, for a class with n_r centers, for lambda>0 and s>0,

  Pr(max_partial ||sum D_t||_F >= s)
    <= 2 exp(-lambda s + n_r (exp(lambda c)-1-lambda c)).

For 0<=lambda c<3, exp(lambda c)-1-lambda c <= (lambda c)^2/[2(1-lambda c/3)]. The usual scalar Bernstein optimization therefore gives tail at most 2 exp(-x) at threshold

  s_r = c sqrt(2 n_r x) + (2/3)c x.

Take x=log(2H/delta) and union bound over the H residue classes. On the resulting event, the norm of the total sum is at most the sum of the H class norms. Since sum_r n_r=n, Cauchy-Schwarz gives sum_r sqrt(n_r)<=sqrt(Hn), so

  ||sum_t D_t||_F <= c sqrt(2Hnx) + (2/3)c Hx.

Divide by n and substitute c=2B to obtain (1). The residue classes are not asserted independent. The decomposition uses only the observed filtration, so unknown hidden-state dependence, overlapping past contexts, and arbitrary nonstationarity are allowed. The dependence penalty H is the maximum unobserved lookahead; overlap on the already observed past is absorbed into F_t.

For the raw indicator matrix, each length pair contributes exactly one 1, so B=sqrt((P+1)(Q+1)). Flattening a tensor does not change its Frobenius norm, and the proof is unchanged. For every matrix cell c, the event ||Zbar-Gbar||_F<=epsilon implies |Zbar_c-Gbar_c|<=epsilon simultaneously, regardless of which c or submatrix is selected after the complete path is seen. This is how candidate multiplicity is handled for linear feature statistics; it is not a license to select an arbitrary nonlinear statistic without a bound.

An anytime version follows by applying (1) at each n and allocating delta_n=delta/[n(n+1)]. For fixed H and feature map, with probability at least 1-delta, simultaneously for every n>=1,

  ||Zbar_n-Gbar_n||_F <= (2B/n) [sqrt(2Hn x_n)+(2/3)H x_n],
  x_n=log(2H n(n+1)/delta).                                        (3)

No fresh validation batch is used or needed for (1)-(3). The pathwise compensator remains the target.

Universal caps from the path length. No word dictionary or maximum feature horizon needs to be supplied. For a length-T path, the complete finite family of admissible raw past/future caps is

  C_T={(P,Q): P,Q>=0, P+Q<=T},     S_T=|C_T|=(T+1)(T+2)/2.

Apply (1) to each cap with failure budget delta/S_T and take a union bound. Then, with probability at least 1-delta, every cap matrix selected after seeing the path obeys its own (1), with x_{P,Q}=log(2 max(1,Q) S_T/delta), n_{P,Q}=T-P-Q+1, and B_{P,Q}=sqrt((P+1)(Q+1)). If Q=0, all features are already F_t-measurable and Z_t=Gamma_t, so the error is exactly zero. This finite union covers every raw word pair (u,v) whose total observed window length is at most T, including adaptive choice of P,Q, words, rows, columns, and linear combinations controlled by the corresponding Frobenius norm. The selection cost is the additional log S_T term. Words with total length exceeding the observed path are not acquired.

If both the cap and stopping prefix n are selected from the same path, allocate delta_(P,Q,n)=6 delta/[pi^2 S_T n^2]. The simultaneous radius is (1) with x_(P,Q,n)=log(2 max(1,Q) S_T n^2 pi^2/(6 delta)); the Q=0 case remains exact. This is simply a union over the finite cap family and the summable n^{-2} schedule, not a validation-data split.

3. Rank-free certificate for online predictive risk
===================================================

Theorem 2 (prequential conditional-risk confidence sequence). Before observing X_t, an online forecaster outputs p_t in the probability simplex on Sigma. p_t may be any F_(t-1)-measurable function of all past observations, including features discovered from this same path. Let e_x be the one-hot vector for symbol x and use normalized multiclass Brier loss

  ell_t = (1/2)||p_t-e_(X_t)||_2^2 in [0,1],
  r_t = E[ell_t | F_(t-1)],
  Lbar_n = n^(-1)sum_(t=1)^n ell_t,
  Rbar_n^path = n^(-1)sum_(t=1)^n r_t.

For one fixed n, with probability at least 1-delta,

  |Lbar_n-Rbar_n^path| <= sqrt(log(2/delta)/(2n)).                  (4)

For M predictors run in shadow (each prediction is made before X_t), the same event holds simultaneously for every predictor with log(2M/delta) in place of log(2/delta). More generally, give predictor m a code/prior weight w_m>0 with sum_m w_m<=1. Then with probability at least 1-delta, for every predictor m and every n>=1,

  |Lbar_(m,n)-Rbar_(m,n)^path|
      <= sqrt( log(pi^2 n^2/(3 delta w_m)) / (2n) ).               (5)

For uniform weights w_m=1/M, this charges retrospective candidate multiplicity by log M. A single actual online policy, even if it adaptively discovers and uses features, is predictable as one policy sequence and needs no M term. A retrospective choice among an uncountable class is not covered without a prior/description-length, covering-number, or other complexity bound.

Proof. For each predictor and time, ell_t is F_t-measurable, lies in [0,1], and E[ell_t-r_t|F_(t-1)]=0. The differences are martingale differences whose conditional range length is at most 1. Conditional Hoeffding's lemma gives E[exp(lambda(ell_t-r_t))|F_(t-1)]<=exp(lambda^2/8). Multiplication of the resulting exponential supermartingale and optimization in lambda gives the two-sided fixed-n radius sqrt(log(2/delta)/(2n)). A union bound gives the M-candidate claim. For (5), assign failure budget delta_mn=6 delta w_m/(pi^2 n^2); the sum over m,n is at most delta because sum_(n>=1)n^(-2)=pi^2/6. Substituting delta_mn into the fixed-n bound gives (5). This is a rank-free, no-mixing certificate for the average conditional risk along the same observed sequence of online forecasts.

Interpretation boundary. Equation (4) certifies the average conditional Brier risk Rbar_n^path at the realized histories t=1,...,n, not the expected stationary risk of a final frozen predictor on fresh histories. It also does not identify the conditional means r_t from data without a model. The confidence interval is computable from recorded forecasts and realized losses, and its center estimates this pathwise risk. A pathwise risk certificate can be useful for an online learner, but it is not a transferable population guarantee.

For a q-symbol prediction target with deterministic label predictions, the analogous 0-1 loss yields the same theorem. Brier loss is used here because it evaluates probability forecasts and is bounded without clipping. Log loss would be unbounded unless a positive probability floor were imposed and charged.

4. Unknown mixing obstruction for stationary predictive risk
============================================================

Theorem 3 (no uniform finite-path certificate for stationary risk without a mixing bound). Fix pi in (0,1) and an integer T>=1. Let P_0 be the one-state HMM that emits 0 forever. For 0<rho<1, let P_(rho,pi) be the stationary two-state HMM with hidden state S_t in {0,1}, deterministic emission X_t=S_t, and transition matrix

  P_(rho,pi) = [[1-rho*pi,       rho*pi],
                [rho*(1-pi), 1-rho*(1-pi)]].

Its stationary law is (1-pi,pi), and its second eigenvalue is 1-rho, so it is irreducible and aperiodic for 0<rho<1 but its mixing time diverges as rho tends to zero. For the fixed predictor g(x-history)=0, stationary one-step 0-1 risk is 0 under P_0 and pi under P_(rho,pi). Yet

  P_(rho,pi)(X_1...X_T=0^T) = (1-pi)(1-rho*pi)^(T-1),
  P_0(X_1...X_T=0^T)=1.

Let any data-only procedure output a confidence interval C(X_1:T) for this predictor's stationary risk, with diameter strictly less than pi. If coverage under P_0 is at least 1-delta, then under P_(rho,pi) its failure probability is at least

  (1-pi)(1-rho*pi)^(T-1) (1-delta).                                (6)

Proof. P_0 produces only z=0^T. The assumed coverage implies the procedure's (possibly randomized) interval on z contains 0 with probability at least 1-delta. Since its diameter is less than pi, that same interval cannot contain pi. Under P_(rho,pi), the observed path equals z with the probability shown, and conditional on z the procedure has the same output distribution as under P_0. On the event that it outputs an interval containing 0, it misses the true risk pi. Multiplying the probabilities proves (6). Letting rho approach zero makes the lower bound tend to (1-pi)(1-delta), which is large for ordinary confidence levels; for pi=1/4 and delta=0.05 it tends to 0.7125. Thus no fixed finite T gives a uniform high-confidence stationary-risk interval of diameter below pi over this unknown-mixing HMM class.

The exact path-law total variation between P_0 and P_(rho,pi) is 1-(1-pi)(1-rho*pi)^(T-1), because P_0 is a point mass at 0^T. The two-point testing error is therefore at least half the overlap mass. To make (6) compatible with failure at most delta, it is necessary that

  (1-rho*pi)^(T-1) <= delta / ((1-delta)(1-pi)),

when the right side is below 1. Equivalently,

  T >= 1 + log(delta/((1-delta)(1-pi))) / log(1-rho*pi).

For small rho this is order (rho*pi)^(-1) log((1-pi)(1-delta)/delta). The mixing time is order rho^(-1). Since rho has no positive lower bound and cannot be upper-certified from a constant all-zero sample path, the sample cost has no uniform finite bound. This obstruction is exact at the level of overlap for this pair; no claim is made about optimal constants for broader loss/model classes.

5. Candidate multiplicity, precision, and complete cost ledger
=============================================================

Horizon and feature count. The raw path has a^T possible full strings, but the algorithm never enumerates that path space. For any cap P,Q with P+Q<=T, it covers every raw suffix/prefix word pair in the universal dictionary of size

  K = ((a^(P+1)-1)/(a-1)) ((a^(Q+1)-1)/(a-1));

each local matrix contribution has only m=(P+1)(Q+1) nonzero cells. K is exponential in the feature horizons; m is quadratic in the number of allowed lengths. The theorem also covers all S_T=(T+1)(T+2)/2 admissible cap pairs simultaneously with the log S_T correction above, so the algorithm can choose its maximum feature lengths after seeing the path. This universal cap family contains every raw word-pair window available anywhere in the finite path. Feature maps outside these raw coordinates still require a norm/entropy/code budget.

Dependent-window effective sample. There are n=T-P-Q+1 overlapping centers. Formula (2) has leading radius O(sqrt(m Q log(Q/delta)/n)) for the full matrix and no feature-count log, with lower-order O(sqrt(m) Q log(Q/delta)/n). It behaves like an effective n/Q windows for a Q-symbol unobserved future block. This is a martingale-delayed concentration rate, not an independence claim or a mixing estimate. Past-window overlap of length P incurs boundary loss in n but no extra dependence factor because the past is conditioned on. For the one-step risk theorem Q=1: the radius is O(sqrt(log(1/delta)/n)), independent of HMM mixing.

Candidate sets. For the complete raw indicator tensor, the Frobenius event controls all K entries and every post-hoc selected submatrix simultaneously; no union over a later row/column basis is needed. For M distinct candidate tensors not embedded in one direct-sum object, union bound over M, adding log M. For risk predictors, the time-uniform bound (5) has log(1/w_m), which is log M under a uniform finite family. A concrete no-dictionary baseline is every stationary context map on suffixes of length <=P with b_pred-bit probabilities: there are at most

  M <= (2^b_pred+1)^(a p)

maps, since there are p contexts and at most (2^b_pred+1)^a coordinate vectors per context. This is an overcount that makes the code cost explicit. A predictor whose parameters/features are represented by at most B_code bits has at most 2^(B_code+1) descriptions and pays B_code log 2 in place of log M. Predictors with unrestricted real parameters or unbounded program length have no uniform retrospective guarantee. An actually selected online predictor is handled directly by predict-before-observe and pays no post-hoc class factor.

Raw acquisition and same-data full-path baseline. The learner reads exactly one path of T symbols, costing T ceil(log2 a) input bits (plus protocol/reset cost, here zero resets). The baseline receives and may retain this same full path; it can compute every listed feature without querying an entry. For one selected cap, a streaming pass has n centers and updates m active counters per center, so O(nm) counter increments; each word comparison can be maintained incrementally with tries/prefix codes. Materializing all K rational cell frequencies costs O(K) writes and O(K log(n+1)) bits for numerator counts plus one denominator n. The empirical statistics are exact rationals C_c/n. The model-dependent compensator Gbar is generally unobserved, so do not serialize or pretend to compute it. If the algorithm needs to score every cap while discovering features, the explicit worst-case work is O(U_T) counter increments and the counter storage is O(K_T^all log(T+1)) bits, where

  U_T = sum_((P,Q) in C_T) (T-P-Q+1)(P+1)(Q+1),
  K_T^all = sum_((P,Q) in C_T) (sum_(i=0)^P a^i)(sum_(j=0)^Q a^j).

Both are finite and generally exponential in T; this is the honest cost of materializing the entire universal feature family. The probability theorem itself does not require materializing all caps: the same stored path can be revisited for a chosen cap, at O(T) storage bits, or the selected cap's counters can be kept online.

For the rank-free certificate of one online forecaster, each time step needs O(a) arithmetic to form a Brier loss from a recorded probability vector and one observed symbol. Store cumulative exact losses with O(log n+2 b_pred) bits when probabilities are on a common b_pred-bit dyadic grid; store the common denominator once. To evaluate M forecasts in shadow costs O(n M a) arithmetic and O(M log n) counter bits, in addition to each model's parameter/feature memory. For one adaptive policy it is O(n a) scoring work and a single counter. A b_pred-bit simplex grid incurs at most sqrt(2a) 2^(-b_pred) extra Brier loss error relative to an unquantized forecast; add this to the confidence radius if the target forecast is unquantized.

The log/square-root radii can be rounded upward to dyadic precision s bits. If R is the displayed exact radius and R_s=2^(-s) ceil(2^s R), then R_s-R<2^(-s). With rational delta, a certified integer upper bound on log z is ceil(log_2 z), because log z<=log_2 z for z>=1; an integer-square-root calculation then produces a conservative rational/dyadic radius. This costs O(s+log z) bit arithmetic for the radius. If dense matrix entries are additionally rounded to b_mat fractional bits, their Frobenius rounding error is at most sqrt(K)2^(-b_mat-1); to keep it below epsilon_mat require b_mat>=ceil(log_2(sqrt(K)/(2 epsilon_mat))). Exact count storage avoids that loss. Any subsequent SVD/linear algebra introduces separate numerical error; the risk certificate requires no SVD, rank, or singular-gap calculation.

6. Counterexamples and scope ledger
===================================

1. Treating all sliding windows as independent is false even for an iid-symbol process: length-Q windows overlap in Q-1 symbols. Under the general HMM interface, dependence can extend far beyond Q through a persistent latent state. The residue/martingale proof does not assert independent windows.
2. A fixed stationary Hankel target is not the same as Gbar. Stationarity makes the empirical count unbiased, but not concentrated at finite T without additional dependence control; Theorem 3 supplies the obstruction.
3. The stationary risk confidence sequence (5) concerns a predictable online sequence and its same-path conditional risk. Reusing the entire path to choose a final predictor and then calling its apparent same-path loss an independent test estimate is invalid. Retrospective finite-class selection pays log M; no fresh holdout is assumed here.
4. A matrix/tensor feature selection among coordinates in the declared universal dictionary is covered by the full-norm event. A selection outside the cap, arbitrary high-amplitude linear combinations, or nonlinear post-hoc search needs an explicit norm/entropy/code budget.
5. Any fixed mixing rate estimate from a no-transition path is unsafe: the two-state alternative can have arbitrarily small rho while preserving the same observed path with probability close to 1-pi. No such estimate is used or claimed.

UNKNOWN: optimal constants and the sharp Q-dependence for arbitrary HMM window tensors; whether a useful smaller feature norm or conditional-variance process can be estimated online without reintroducing hidden mixing assumptions; finite-state-specific improvements to the delayed-martingale bound; minimax risk for broader finite-HMM output channels under a stated mixing estimator; external review; novelty/priority.
