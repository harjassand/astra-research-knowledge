Main reconstructed statement
----------------------------
Let K/Q be a separable quartic field with distinct conjugates theta_1,...,theta_4. Let Y be a geometrically irreducible surface over Q in the rational arithmetic-box coordinates, meeting the open set a_1a_2a_3a_4 != 0 and satisfying

    a_i^2 b_i = n - theta_i   (1 <= i <= 4),

where n is nonconstant and algebraic over C(a_1,...,a_4). Give each a-coordinate weight U>0 and each b-coordinate weight V>0, and set D0=min(3U,2V,U+V). Then the source's pair-image argument yields

    H_Y(T) >= (T^2/2) min{ (2U+V)D0/(U^2 V^2), 3/U^2 } - O(T),

uniformly over bounded-degree moving varieties and rational integral coordinate changes, conditional on the pair-image lemma in Proposition 7.1. The key independent check is that the source's two difficult branches—quadratic a-image and plane a-image—do close under the stated hypotheses.

Proof audit of Proposition 7.1's weighted complete intersection
---------------------------------------------------------------
For h selected independent a-coordinates, write the irreducible relation as F_1(a_1,...,a_h,n)=sum_I a^I f_I(n). Factor f_I(n)=prod_i(n-theta_i)^(nu_i,I) g_I(n), put e_i=min_I(I_i+k nu_i,I), and substitute n=a_1^k b_1+theta_1. After clearing denominators, the source's C_1(a,b) has weight

    D=max_I{ U|I|+(kU+V)deg f_I } - U sum_i e_i.

The exponents I-e are distinct Laurent a-monomials after imposing a_i^k b_i=q, and each nonzero coefficient contributes a nonzero q-polynomial. Thus the weight-D initial form is nonzero on the open a_i != 0. Also D>=min(U,V): C_1 vanishes on the nonempty relation variety, so it cannot be a nonzero constant, and every positive-weight monomial has weight at least min(U,V).

Scale U,V to positive integers A=L U, B=L V and D to E=L D. Homogenizing the h-1 equations a_i^k b_i+theta_i=a_1^k b_1+theta_1 and C_1 with a variable t of weight 1 gives h equations in 2h+1 weighted variables. On t != 0 and all a_i != 0 the dehomogenized coordinate ring is the domain C[a_1^{-1},...,a_h^{-1},n]/(F_1), hence an integral component of dimension h+1 after restoring t. The affine strata with some a_i=0 have dimension at most h-1: then n=theta_i, every other a_j is nonzero, b_j=(theta_i-theta_j)/a_j^k, and the surviving C_1 Laurent monomials are distinct and nonzero. After restoring t their dimension is at most h.

At t=0, the first h-1 equations say all products a_i^k b_i equal q. Where all a_i are nonzero, the initial form of C_1 is a nonzero Laurent polynomial in the free a_i and q, so this stratum has dimension at most h. Where some a_i=0, q=0 and the locus is a union of coordinate h-planes, also dimension at most h even if C_1 vanishes on one of them. Krull's height bound gives every component of the h-equation scheme dimension at least h+1. The t=0 and affine-boundary estimates therefore leave one top-dimensional component, and the all-nonzero open is integral and generically reduced. A complete intersection in a polynomial ring is unmixed; hence there are no embedded components, and generic reducedness plus uniqueness of the top component makes the homogenized ideal prime. In particular it is t-saturated.

The filtered Hilbert series is therefore

    (1-z^(kA+B))^(h-1) (1-z^E) / ((1-z)(1-z^A)^h(1-z^B)^h).

At z=1 its pole order is h+1, with leading coefficient (kA+B)^(h-1) E/(A B)^h. At a nontrivial root of unity where only one of the A- or B-denominator factors vanishes, the pole order is at most h. If both vanish, the order is 2h, but gcd(A,B) divides kA+B and E: E is an integer combination of A and B because D is selected from integer-linear combinations of U and V. The h numerator factors cancel at least h orders, again leaving at most h. Partial fractions thus leave only O(T^(h-1)) periodic terms. Rescaling gives the claimed leading Hilbert coefficient (kU+V)^(h-1)D/(U^hV^h), uniformly when degrees and weights are fixed. This resolves the weighted-infinity and periodic-root issue in the named gate, subject to the source's construction of F_1 and C_1.

Quartic surface alternatives (Proposition 8.1)
-----------------------------------------------
The a-image S has dimension two: n is algebraic over its a-coordinate field and each b_i=(n-theta_i)/a_i^2. Split by deg S.

1. If deg S>=3, a generic projection to an affine plane has function-field degree deg S and no points lost at infinity. A primitive element separates the extension in characteristic zero; 1,r,r^2 are independent over the two projection coordinates. Counting their monomial multiples gives H_Y(T)>=3T^2/(2U^2)-O(T), the second term in the target minimum.

2. If deg S=2, its projective span is a P^3 and S is a quadric. Its affine direction is a rational hyperplane in K, hence Tr_{K/Q}(gamma alpha)=0 for gamma!=0; under the four embeddings this gives a relation sum_i sigma_i(gamma) ell_i=0 with every coefficient nonzero. Thus the four lines ell_i=0 in the projective direction plane have no triple intersection and their six pair intersections are distinct. The nonzero quadratic part Q_2 cannot vanish at all six: if it did, its restriction to each ell_i=0 would have three roots and each line would divide Q_2. Choose i,j with Q_2 nonzero at ell_i=ell_j=0. Projection to (a_i,a_j) then has generic degree two. If Proposition 7.1's D>=D0 alternative holds, its Hilbert coefficient gives the first target term. If D<D0, its extracted equation has the form c_i b_i+c_j b_j=S_ij(a_i,a_j), deg S_ij<=2, and implies n in C(a_i,a_j). Since the selected pair projection has degree two and every b is determined by a and n, [C(Y):C(a_i,a_j)]=2. The source's doubling clause applies; D>=V gives 2D>=2V>=D0, again yielding the first target term.

3. If deg S=1, S is an affine plane. The a_i are nonconstant affine linear functions; joining i,j when their gradients are independent gives a connected complete multipartite graph. If any edge has D>=D0, use Proposition 7.1. Otherwise every edge gives c_i b_i+c_j b_j=S_ij with nonzero constants c_i,c_j and degree at most two. Substitution yields

    B_ij n = c_i theta_i a_j^2 + c_j theta_j a_i^2 + a_i^2 a_j^2 S_ij,
    B_ij = c_i a_j^2 + c_j a_i^2 != 0.

If n is polynomial, each a_i^2 divides n-theta_i. A general affine line meets the four distinct zero lines at distinct points, which become four critical points of n with four distinct critical values theta_i. But deg n<=4, so n' has degree at most three: contradiction. If n is nonpolynomial, any irreducible denominator factor Q of n divides every B_ij. Since c_i c_j !=0, B_ij factors into two lines through the intersection of a_i=0 and a_j=0, transverse to each. The fixed factor Q lies on every edge; connectedness forces all four zero lines through a common point O. Center the plane at O and scale coordinates by t. The limit of n(t x,t y) at t=0, from each edge identity, is the same rational map

    R_ij = (c_i theta_i a_j^2 + c_j theta_j a_i^2)/(c_i a_j^2+c_j a_i^2)

on P^1 of directions. For every edge it is a nonconstant fractional-linear transform of (a_i/a_j)^2, hence a degree-two map, ramified exactly at a_i=0 and a_j=0 with values theta_i and theta_j. Since the edge graph is connected and the theta_i are distinct, the common map has four distinct ramification points, contradicting Riemann-Hurwitz (total ramification for degree two on P^1 is two). So some edge has D>=D0.

Costs, controls, falsifier
-------------------------
This is a symbolic proof audit; no computation or acquired numerical data are needed. All constants depend on fixed K, the quartic roots, fixed U,V and bounded variety degree; there is no uniformity in the polynomial f or coefficient height. The matched baseline is the source's direct surface method in the complementary large-prime interval; the geometry here improves only its number-field range. A decisive falsifier would be an explicit geometrically irreducible Y satisfying the stated quartic equations for which (i) all three degree cases hold, (ii) no independent pair reaches D0, and (iii) the displayed Hilbert lower bound fails; the plane argument rules this out under Proposition 7.1. Remaining risk is a dependency mismatch in the affine variety/coordinate hypotheses or in the moving-descendant uniformity, not a finite numerical margin.
