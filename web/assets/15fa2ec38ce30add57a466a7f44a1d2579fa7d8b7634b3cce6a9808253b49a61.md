# Cycle 3: burst offspring versus a full multitype branching baseline

## Finding

Matching the chemical-Langevin drift and infinitesimal covariance does not in general determine finite-horizon or eventual extinction risk. A continuous-time branching counterexample below has identical mean trajectory, variance at every time, and CLE coefficients under two burst-offspring laws, yet their eventual resistant-lineage establishment probabilities are 0.634 and 1. The finite-horizon survival probabilities differ by 21.2 percentage points at one event-rate time unit and 35.9 points at five.

This demonstrates a limit of a diffusion/moment closure, not a gap in a full branching-process baseline: the full offspring probability-generating function distinguishes the two laws exactly. A model that tracks the PGCC state or uses its complete burst-size law already represents the mechanism. ecDNA segregation during ordinary binary division is even more constrained: if every division yields exactly two daughters and all daughter types/copy-number states are resolved, the full mean and covariance of the multitype offspring vector determine its distribution. The candidate's genuine data gap is therefore measurement of hidden mother states, full daughter-state outcomes, and event timing; it is not a new branching equation.

## Exact counterexample with matched CLE coefficients

Consider a continuous-time Markov branching process containing one resistance-relevant type R. Each cell has a replacement event at rate c: it is removed and replaced by K R offspring, independently of other cells. K=1 is a lineage-continuing event with no net count change. Compare:

| Offspring law | K values and probabilities | E[K] | Var(K) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A, burst-and-loss | 0 with probability 1/3; 3 with probability 2/3 | 2 | 2 |
| B, persistent-or-large-burst | 1 with probability 2/3; 4 with probability 1/3 | 2 | 2 |

Let Y=K-1 be the population jump caused by one event. Both laws satisfy

    E[Y]=1,        E[Y^2]=3.

At population size n, the event rate is cn, so the exact infinitesimal drift and variance are

    E[dN | N=n]/dt = c n,
    Var[dN | N=n]/dt = 3 c n.

They induce the same CLE coefficients dN = cN dt + sqrt(3cN) dW. More strongly, their first two population moments agree at every time from the same initial count. Starting from N(0)=1,

    E[N(t)] = exp(ct),
    E[N(t)^2] = 4 exp(2ct) - 3 exp(ct),
    Var[N(t)] = 3 exp(ct)(exp(ct)-1).

Thus even the full mean and variance time courses do not distinguish these laws.

Their exact branching probability-generating functions are different:

    f_A(s) = 1/3 + (2/3)s^3,
    f_B(s) = (2/3)s + (1/3)s^4.

For a type-R founder, the finite-time PGF F(t,s)=E[s^{N(t)} | N(0)=1] solves

    dF/dt = c(f(F)-F),        F(0,s)=s.

The finite-horizon extinction probability is q(t)=F(t,0); persistence to horizon t is 1-q(t). For law A,

    dq_A/dt = (c/3)(1 - 3q_A + 2q_A^3),        q_A(0)=0,
    ct = 3 integral_0^{q_A(t)} du/(1 - 3u + 2u^3).

For law B, q_B(t)=0 for every finite t: every event leaves at least one R descendant. Numerically integrating the exact scalar ODE for A gives:

| Horizon | Law A: extinction by horizon | Law A: R persists to horizon | Law B: extinction | Law B: R persists |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ct=1 | 0.212388 | 0.787612 | 0 | 1 |
| ct=5 | 0.358785 | 0.641215 | 0 | 1 |

Eventual extinction q is the least solution of q=f(q). For A,

    q_A = (sqrt(3)-1)/2 = 0.3660254,
    P_A(eventual nonextinction/establishment) = 0.6339746.

For B, the least solution is q_B=0, so eventual establishment probability is 1. Equal drift, equal diffusion coefficient, and even equal finite-time first and second moments therefore leave a 36.6-percentage-point difference in eventual establishment. These values are mathematical fixtures, not estimates for any cancer or PGCC system.

Here “establishment by horizon t” means at least one resistant descendant remains at t; “eventual establishment” means the lineage never becomes extinct. A clinical size threshold is a different endpoint and should be computed from the full process's hitting probability.

## What the strongest full branching baseline already captures

For m cell types, let a type-i cell experience event class ell at rate c_iell and be replaced by an offspring vector Z^(i,ell) in N^m. Write its joint offspring PGF as

    f_iell(z) = E[product_j z_j^(Z_j^(i,ell))].

The parent is removed at the event; Z includes all its daughters. With independent cell-level event clocks and rates that do not depend on population density, the population is a continuous-time multitype branching process. Its exact generator is

    L g(x) = sum_i x_i sum_ell c_iell
                E[g(x - e_i + Z^(i,ell)) - g(x)].

The mean drift matrix and infinitesimal covariance depend only on the first two jump moments:

    A_{ji} = sum_ell c_iell (E[Z_j^(i,ell)] - 1{i=j}),
    Q(x)  = sum_i x_i sum_ell c_iell
                  E[(Z^(i,ell)-e_i)(Z^(i,ell)-e_i)^T].

A full multitype baseline retains every f_iell, not merely A and Q. For constant rates, its finite-time PGFs satisfy

    dF_i(t,z)/dt = sum_ell c_iell [f_iell(F(t,z)) - F_i(t,z)],
    F_i(0,z) = z_i.

The eventual extinction vector for all descendants in the modeled type set is the least solution q in [0,1]^m of

    q_i = sum_ell c_iell f_iell(q) / sum_ell c_iell.

Under a time-varying treatment schedule, replace c_iell and f_iell by their time-dependent values in the PGF ODE. Time-to-horizon extinction, a post-treatment establishment probability, and finite-threshold risk can then be obtained from the full PGF or the corresponding branching generator. The burst example is fully resolved by this baseline as soon as K's law and the cell event rate are supplied. It is the Gaussian/moment closure that fails.

A PGCC can be represented as an additional mother type or a sequence of states such as arrested giant -> budding/bursting -> daughter phenotype/copy-number types. If the mother has a heritable or persistent latent state, its transition and delay distribution must be retained. Marginalizing that state generally produces a non-Markovian lineage, not an ordinary one-type branching process. A finite-state Markov representation is recovered by adding the hidden state to the type; age-dependent branching is needed when waiting times are not memoryless. If a shared microenvironment or tumor-level feedback couples different mothers, independent branching is no longer exact and a common-environment or interacting-particle model is needed.

## Boundary case: ordinary binary ecDNA segregation

ecDNA inheritance is biologically relevant because its copy number and co-inheritance can produce distinct daughter states. But ordinary mitosis has exactly two daughters. If each daughter is assigned one of a finite or countable set of fully observed types (including the joint ecDNA copy-number/oncogene states), let Z be the offspring-type count vector. Since sum_j Z_j=2, its full distribution is already determined by its second factorial-moment matrix:

    P(Z=e_j+e_k) = E[Z_j Z_k],                 j != k,
    P(Z=2e_j)   = E[Z_j(Z_j-1)]/2.

Equivalently, the full vector mean plus covariance determines the second factorial moments and hence every pair outcome. Therefore two ordinary binary-division offspring laws cannot have identical full multitype first and second moments but different PGFs under this complete type resolution. In the simpler one-type binary case K in {0,1,2}, normalization, mean and second moment determine all three probabilities: p_2=E[K(K-1)]/2, p_1=E[K]-E[K(K-1)], and p_0=1-p_1-p_2. This identifiability statement is per division event with the event clock known; matching only aggregated CLE coefficients while event rates are unknown is weaker. If the baseline observes only total count or a coarse “resistant/sensitive” label while copy number or daughter pairing is hidden, those are not the full multitype moments; hidden states can then be aliased even though the coarse binary offspring law itself is determined. The result also assumes the division clock is separately known and the full offspring moments are conditional on parent type.

The ecDNA complication is thus state resolution and sister-pair measurement. A parent with different ecDNA copy numbers may have different daughter-type distributions and treatment-dependent fitness. If cell types are indexed by all relevant joint ecDNA species/copy states, the full branching kernel captures it. If those states are collapsed, average drift/covariance may not suffice for a coarse resistance endpoint, but the claim depends on what moments were actually observed.

## What branching data are sufficient

For the Markov multitype branching baseline, the sufficient model inputs for exact extinction and finite-horizon probabilities are:

1. **Type definition and initial distribution.** Include every heritable or persistent variable predictive of daughter outcomes or resistance establishment: genotype, phenotype, ecDNA species and relevant copy-number state, PGCC state, and any required age/phase state. Record the starting number or distribution of founders by type.
2. **Event timing and event classification.** Estimate per-cell, type- and environment-specific hazards and waiting-time laws for death, ordinary division, state transition, budding, multipolar division, or burst. Event times matter for a finite horizon; an eventual offspring distribution alone does not fix when offspring appear.
3. **Complete joint offspring vectors.** For every event and parent type, record the joint number of daughters in each offspring type, including zero-offspring events, daughter sister-pair dependence, and the upper tail of burst size. For ecDNA, record both daughters' joint species/copy counts, not only a population mean or marginal copy-number histogram.
4. **Treatment-dependent transition kernels.** Measure how event hazards, offspring-state distributions, and delays vary across the pulse and post-pulse environments. Include mutation or phenotype switching among daughters.
5. **Endpoint and censoring.** Define extinction, persistence by a fixed time, eventual survival, or first passage to an establishment threshold. Track enough lineage history to distinguish these endpoints and handle right censoring.

If environment varies jointly across cells, or one PGCC burst produces correlated daughters whose later fate shares a persistent maternal factor, the state must include the common factor or the model must explicitly represent dependence. Fitting only E[Z] and Cov(Z) is not sufficient for the counterexample class. Conversely, if the complete offspring-vector PGF and event clock are known, the burst adds no additional mathematical information beyond those branching inputs.

## Primary-literature boundary

Primary ecDNA studies establish unequal and sometimes coordinated inheritance, not the particular matched-moment counterexample above. Lange et al. combined random-segregation modeling, live-cell ecDNA tagging, daughter-cell measurements, and patient analyses; they quantified roughly 100 daughter-cell pairs per cell line and linked random ecDNA inheritance to copy-number heterogeneity and adaptation ([Lange et al., Nature Genetics 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-022-01177-x)). Hung et al. measured coordinated asymmetric inheritance of distinct ecDNA species, reporting daughter-cell co-segregation correlations of R=0.42–0.64 in tested lines ([Hung et al., Nature 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07861-8)). These observations make joint daughter-state recording important, while their models already make inheritance/offspring distributions explicit rather than relying only on CLE moments.

Primary PGCC studies report delayed daughter production and noncanonical reproduction in particular preclinical systems. Niu et al. used fluorescent time-lapse tracking after paclitaxel in ovarian cancer cell lines and reported PGCC nuclear budding/fragmentation, followed by mitotically competent proliferating daughters ([Niu et al., Oncogenesis 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/oncsis.2016.75)). Zhang et al. later tracked olaparib-induced PGCCs in ovarian/breast cell lines and organoids and reported escape from senescence through restitutional multipolar endomitosis and other noncanonical divisions, generating resistant daughters ([Zhang et al., Science Advances 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adf7195)). These establish biological plausibility for an expanded reproductive-state model, not a calibrated burst-size distribution or a measured relapse-probability advantage. PGCC observations are context-dependent and should not be generalized to every tumor.

Cancer resistance and treatment modeling already use multitype branching processes: for example, Bozic et al. model genotype resistance with a multitype branching process ([Bozic et al., eLife 2013](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.00747)); stochastic continuous/pulsed resistant-clone models also predate this candidate ([Foo & Michor, PLoS Computational Biology 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000557)). Thus the mathematical novelty boundary is strict: demonstrating a difference between two moment-matched laws is elementary PGF dependence on higher moments, and modeling a PGCC burst is not new once its full offspring kernel is specified. No cited study establishes the numerical laws A or B above, and no data were fit here.

## Bottom line for the mechanism audit

- **Yes:** higher offspring moments can materially alter finite-horizon and eventual resistant-lineage risk while matching CLE drift and covariance; the explicit burst pair demonstrates it.
- **No:** this does not defeat the strongest full multitype branching model, which takes the complete event/offspring law as input and computes the correct PGF.
- **Ordinary ecDNA mitosis:** with exactly two daughters and fully resolved daughter types, first two multitype moments already determine the offspring kernel; the interesting uncertainty is hidden/coarse state and measured sister-pair dependence.
- **PGCC bursts:** variable daughter number permits the explicit higher-moment counterexample, but its law and timing must be directly estimated; current primary reports do not identify them sufficiently to claim a quantitative cancer-risk shift.
- **Unresolved:** whether any specific ecDNA/PGCC tumor has a measured offspring law whose full-PGF predictions are both identifiable and materially different from its matched CLE prediction. No intervention or clinical claim is made.
