RESULT

Use trace distance D(a,b) = (1/2)||a-b||_1. Let rho_N be an arbitrary
permutation-invariant state of N qubits, with J = (1/2)sum_i sigma^(i), and
Tr(rho_N J^2) = 3N/4. Write rho_r for any r-qubit marginal, tau_r=I/2^r,
a_r=E|epsilon_1+...+epsilon_r| for independent uniform signs, and
h_n=binomial(n+3,3). Then the following explicit bound holds:

 D(rho_r,tau_r)
 <= min{1, 2(1-h_(N-r)/h_N)
             + |Tr(rho_N J)| a_r/(N+4)
             + 9r(N+2)/[(N+4)(N+5)]}.                         (1)

In particular, |Tr(rho_N J)| <= sqrt(3N/4) gives uniform convergence to zero
for every r_N=o(N). This uses only the mean J^2 constraint; the stronger
Schur-Weyl sector-weight constraint is unnecessary for convergence.

Equation (1) is sharp to leading order. Define the two minimax distances

 M_mean(N,r)=sup D(rho_r,tau_r), over the states just described;
 M_weights(N,r)=the same supremum with exact initial maximally mixed
               Schur-Weyl weights q_j=(2j+1)m_(N,j)/2^N fixed.

For every r_N -> infinity with r_N=o(N),

 M_mean(N,r_N) = [sqrt(3/(2*pi))+o(1)] sqrt(r_N/N),           (2)
 M_weights(N,r_N) = [2/pi+o(1)] sqrt(r_N/N).                 (3)

Thus both the sublinear threshold and the square-root order are sharp, with
the exact leading minimax constants shown. These statements concern state
classes, and make no assumption that the extremizers are reached by a given
Lindblad evolution.

The exact finite sharpness statement is stronger. Let beta_N be the largest
possible sum_j q_j j subject to sum_j q_j j(j+1)=3N/4 and sum_j q_j=1, with
j ranging over the allowed N-qubit spin sectors. Let b_N=sum_j p_(N,j)j for
the fixed maximally mixed weights. For N>=r>=6, either minimax quantity M,
with B=beta_N or B=b_N respectively, satisfies

 B a_r/N - (15/2)r/N <= M <= B a_r/N + 15r/N.               (4)

For the upper bound before simplification one may replace the right side by
2(1-h_(N-r)/h_N)+B a_r/(N+4)+9r(N+2)/[(N+4)(N+5)].
Negative lower bounds of course may be replaced by zero.

PROOF OF THE UPPER BOUND

1. Symmetric purification and one fixed de Finetti measure.

Let |Omega> = tensor_i (|00>+|11>) on physical/ancillary qubit pairs and
|Psi>=(sqrt(rho_N) tensor I)|Omega>. This is normalized. Every permutation
matrix U_pi is real and commutes with sqrt(rho_N), so |Psi> is invariant
under U_pi tensor U_pi. After grouping each physical/ancillary pair as one
four-dimensional site, |Psi> belongs to Sym^N(C^4).

For Haar probability measure dv on unit vectors v in C^4 set

 dmu_N(v)=h_N |<v^tensorN|Psi>|^2 dv,
 sigma(v)=Tr_anc |v><v|=(I+s(v).sigma)/2.

The coherent-state resolution of identity makes mu_N a probability measure.
The finite coherent-state de Finetti bound, in its explicit full-N measure
form, gives

 D(rho_r, integral sigma(v)^tensorr dmu_N(v))
 <= 2(1-h_(N-r)/h_N) <= 6r/(N+1).                         (5)

PRIMARY THEOREM INGREDIENT: Christandl, Koenig, Mitchison and Renner,
"One-and-a-half quantum de Finetti theorems," arXiv:quant-ph/0602130v4,
Theorem II.2 and Corollary II.3, PDF pages 2-3. Its hypothesis here is a
normalized pure vector in Sym^N(C^D), with 1<=r<=N. Its proof uses precisely
the full-N Haar coherent-state outcome measure above. Its trace-distance
convention includes 1/2. The exact bound is
2[1-dim Sym^(N-r)(C^D)/dim Sym^N(C^D)]. We set D=4 and trace out the paired
ancillas. This stated source ingredient, not a newly claimed de Finetti theorem,
is the only non-elementary input to (5).
Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0602130

It is essential that one fixed full-N measure is used: a bare existence
theorem with independently chosen measures for different marginal sizes
would not by itself justify the next moment identities.

2. Exact moments of that measure.

For a Hermitian operator A on C^D, S_A=sum_i A^(i), and an N-site symmetric
state gamma, the Haar coherent-state measure satisfies

 E <v|A|v> = [<S_A>+Tr A]/(N+D),

 E <v|A|v>^2 =
 [<S_A^2>+<S_(A^2)>+2(Tr A)<S_A>+(Tr A)^2+Tr(A^2)]
 /[(N+D)(N+D+1)].                                       (6)

These identities follow directly by diagonalizing A. In a symmetric
occupation basis indexed by n_1+...+n_D=N, the operator-valued Haar integral
of a diagonal function is diagonal. The coherent-state weight for that basis
vector induces a Dirichlet distribution with parameters n_i+1. Its first
and second moments are
E x_i=(n_i+1)/(N+D) and
E x_i x_j=(n_i+1)(n_j+1+delta_ij)/[(N+D)(N+D+1)].
Summing lambda_i x_i, where the lambda_i are the eigenvalues of A, gives (6).
Unitary covariance then gives it for arbitrary A. This also supplies an
independent short derivation rather than relying on an unspecified measure.

Apply (6) to A_a=sigma_a tensor I_anc, a=x,y,z. Here D=4, Tr A_a=0,
A_a^2=I_4, and S_(A_a)=2J_a on the physical systems. Therefore

 E s(v) = 2<J>/(N+4),
 E |s(v)|^2 = [4<J^2>+3N+12]/[(N+4)(N+5)]
             = 6(N+2)/[(N+4)(N+5)].                       (7)

No covariance sign, isotropy, magnetization, sector-weight, or dynamical
assumption is hidden in these identities.

3. Product expansion with a dimension-independent trace-norm remainder.

For any qubit sigma=(I+s.sigma)/2 with t=|s|, write

 sigma^tensorr = tau_r + L_r(s) + R_r(s),
 L_r(s)=2^(-r) sum_i s.sigma^(i).

After a common rotation this is a commuting calculation in the z basis.
Against independent uniform signs, the likelihood ratio is
L=product_i(1+t epsilon_i), its linear part is t S_r, and
S_r=sum_i epsilon_i. Orthogonality of sign monomials gives

 E(L-1-tS_r)^2=(1+t^2)^r-1-rt^2.

If rt^2<=1 this implies E|L-1-tS_r|<=rt^2, since
exp(x)-1-x <= (e-2)x^2 <= x^2 for 0<=x<=1. If rt^2>=1, use instead
E|L-1-tS_r|<=2+t sqrt(r)<=3rt^2. Consequently, for all r,t,

 ||R_r(s)||_1 <= 3r |s|^2.                               (8)

The averaged linear term depends on E s, and has the exact norm

 (1/2)||E L_r(s)||_1=(|E s|/2)a_r.                        (9)

Combine (7)-(9), convexity of trace norm, and (5). This proves (1).

For comparison, a simpler non-sharp consequence obtained from product
fidelity is
D(rho_r,tau_r) <= 2(1-h_(N-r)/h_N)
                  +sqrt[3r(N+2)/((N+4)(N+5))].
The expansion bound (1) resolves the leading constant and shows that if
<J>=0 the bound improves to O(r/N).

4. Optimize the linear coefficient over spin sectors.

Schur-Weyl decomposition of a permutation-invariant qubit state is

 rho_N=direct_sum_j q_j rho_j tensor I_(m_j)/m_j,
 Tr rho_j=1, q_j>=0, sum_j q_j=1.

The collective spin acts as spin j on its irrep, so |<J>_rhoj|<=j. Hence
|<J>|<=sum_j q_j j. With the mean constraint, the maximum is beta_N. It is
obtained by a mixture of the two adjacent allowed j values bracketing the
solution g_N=(sqrt(1+3N)-1)/2 of j(j+1)=3N/4, with their weights chosen to
give that expectation. This follows because j is a concave function of
j(j+1), whose discrete upper concave hull joins adjacent points. Thus
beta_N=sqrt(3N)/2+O(1).

With fixed sector weights, the maximum is b_N=sum_j p_j j. Both maxima are
attained by highest-weight states with all spin vectors pointing along z.

PROOF OF SHARPNESS AND THE FINITE LOWER BOUND

5. Explicit extremal constructions.

For any chosen weights q_j satisfying the mean constraint define

 rho_N^up(q)=direct_sum_j q_j |j,j><j,j| tensor I_(m_j)/m_j. (10)

This is a valid permutation-invariant state. It has <J^2>=sum q_j j(j+1),
<J_z>=sum q_j j=:B, and <J_x>=<J_y>=0.

For the mean-only case take the adjacent-sector optimizer defining beta_N.
A particularly simple subsequence is j=3k and N=4j(j+1)/3=4k(3k+1), with
just that one sector present. The N-qubit spin parity and j<=N/2 conditions
are satisfied for k>=1, and the mean constraint is exact.

For the stronger case take exactly

 p_(N,j)=(2j+1)m_(N,j)/2^N,
 m_(N,j)=binomial(N,N/2-j)-binomial(N,N/2-j-1),              (11)

using zero for out-of-range binomial coefficients and the allowed parity
j=0,1,...,N/2 for even N or j=1/2,3/2,...,N/2 for odd N. These are exactly
the maximally mixed state sector weights, so (10) with (11) satisfies the
strict constraint without changing any sector probability.

6. Marginal z measurement and controlled binomial approximation.

In sector j the state (10) has fixed total z magnetization 2j. Because it is
permutation-invariant, its computational-basis diagonal is uniform on the
strings with K=N/2+j plus signs. Thus the r-qubit z-measurement outcome
count is hypergeometric Hyp(N,K,r). Its full bit-string distance is exactly
the count-distribution distance, since conditional on the count both laws
are uniform. Let H be this hypergeometric mixture and Bprod the mixture of
independent Bernoulli bit strings with p_j=K/N=(1+t_j)/2, t_j=2j/N.

PRIMARY CLASSICAL INGREDIENT: H. R. Kunsch, "The difference between the
hypergeometric and the binomial distribution," May 1998, theorem on PDF
page 2, proves TV(Hyp(N,K,r),Bin(r,K/N))<=(r-1)/(N-1), provided
r(K/N)(1-K/N)>=1. Its TV convention is sup_A |P(A)-Q(A)|. Source:
https://stat.ethz.ch/~kuensch/papers/hypergeom.pdf
This author manuscript contains the proof, not only a quotation of Ehm.

For r>=6 this condition holds whenever j<=N/4. The exceptional weight is
bounded by Markov's inequality:
P(j>N/4)<=4E t_j^2, and E t_j^2=4E j^2/N^2<=3/N.
Therefore

 TV(H,Bprod) <= (r-1)/(N-1)+12/N.                         (12)

Apply the same elementary expansion (8) to this classical product mixture,
this time keeping the lower triangle inequality. Its average linear
coefficient is E t_j=2B/N. Hence

 TV(Bprod,uniform r-bit strings)
 >= B a_r/N - (3/2)r E t_j^2
 >= B a_r/N - (9/2)r/N.                                 (13)

A measurement cannot increase trace distance. Combining (12)-(13) proves

 D((rho_N^up(q))_r,tau_r)
 >= B a_r/N - (9/2)r/N - (r-1)/(N-1) -12/N.              (14)

For N>=r>=6, (r-1)/(N-1)<=r/N and 12/N<=2r/N, giving the lower side of
(4). The upper side follows from (1), 2(1-h_(N-r)/h_N)<=6r/(N+1), and
9r(N+2)/[(N+4)(N+5)]<=9r/N. For fixed r<6, the ordinary collision coupling
between with-replacement and without-replacement samples gives an O(1/N)
replacement for (12), so the same leading term remains valid.

7. Leading constants.

The exact Rademacher absolute moment is

 a_r=r binomial(r-1,floor((r-1)/2))/2^(r-1),
 a_r=[sqrt(2/pi)+o(1)] sqrt(r) as r->infinity.             (15)

Together with beta_N=sqrt(3N)/2+O(1), (4) gives (2), since
(r/N)/sqrt(r/N)=sqrt(r/N)->0.

For (11), Stirling's formula gives convergence of j/sqrt(N) to the density

 f(x)=8 sqrt(2/pi) x^2 exp(-2x^2), x>=0.

Indeed m_j=(2j+1)binomial(N,N/2-j)/(N/2+j+1), and the lattice spacing is
1/sqrt(N). The exact second-moment bound E j^2<=3N/4 supplies uniform
integrability of j/sqrt(N), so its first moment converges as well:

 b_N/sqrt(N) -> integral_0^infinity x f(x) dx=sqrt(2/pi).

Then (4) and (15) give (3). This asymptotic derivation is included here;
the constant is not imported from an unchecked sector-asymptotics source.

LINEAR-SIZE MARGINAL SHARPNESS WITHOUT A CLT

8. A direct constant lower bound with exact preserved weights.

For the state (10) with weights (11), measure S_r=sum_i Z_i and put
X=S_r/sqrt(r). Permutation invariance and <S_N>=2b_N give

 E_rho X=2sqrt(r)b_N/N,
 E_rho X^2=1+(r-1)(2N-4b_N)/[N(N-1)],
 E_tau X=0, E_tau X^2=1.                                (16)

The second identity follows from <S_N^2>=4E j^2=3N-4b_N. For any two
probability laws P,Q, weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives

 |E_P X-E_Q X|^2
 <= [E_P X^2+E_Q X^2] sum_x (P(x)-Q(x))^2/(P(x)+Q(x))
 <= 2 TV(P,Q)[E_P X^2+E_Q X^2].

Thus (16) gives the completely explicit witness bound

 D(rho_r,tau_r)
 >= [4r b_N^2/N^2] /
    {2[2+(r-1)(2N-4b_N)/(N(N-1))]}.                     (17)

If r/N->c in (0,1], its limit is

 liminf D(rho_r,tau_r) >= 2c/[pi(1+c)] >0.               (18)

Therefore the sublinear condition is sharp even with exact initial sector
weights. At r=N the stronger fact D(rho_N,tau_N)->1 also follows directly:
the support of (10) has rank sum_j m_j=binomial(N,floor(N/2)); every nonzero
eigenvalue equals (2j+1)/2^N>=2^(-N). Hence
D(rho_N,tau_N)=1-binomial(N,floor(N/2))/2^N ->1.

OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE AND LIMITS

Every POVM on r=o(N) qubits has outcome statistics that approach those of
the maximally mixed r-qubit state uniformly over the constrained class.
No such marginal has a constant-bias test that distinguishes the global
state from the maximally mixed state. This constrains robust detection of
global entanglement through mesoscopic marginals.

Trace-distance convergence alone does not assert that a growing-r marginal
is separable: separability balls around I/2^r shrink with the dimension.
An entanglement witness can still detect a signal whose measurement bias
vanishes. The result also does not prove any dynamical reachability,
entanglement-depth claim, or relative-entropy/resource estimate.
