# Primary-source notes

These sources ground the mathematical mechanisms and the novelty boundary. Source reports are not external verification of every derivation in proof.md.

1. J.-R. Chazottes, P. G. da Silva, C. Giardina, and F. Redig, “Relative entropy and waiting times for continuous-time Markov processes,” Electronic Journal of Probability 11 (2006), DOI [10.1214/EJP.v11-374](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/electronic-journal-of-probability/volume-11/issue-none/Relative-entropy-and-waiting-times-for-continuous-time-Markov-processes/10.1214/EJP.v11-374.full). The paper studies finite-state CTMC path-space relative entropy and compares Markov chains. It is the closest source mechanism for the path-KL rate; proof.md reconstructs the general rowwise formula directly from the path likelihood.

2. L. H. Koopmans, “Asymptotic Rate of Discrimination for Markov Processes,” Annals of Mathematical Statistics 31(4) (1960), 982–994, DOI [10.1214/AOMS/1177705671](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177705671). The source treats likelihood-ratio error rates for discrete-time-parameter Markov processes. It is prior art for Markov discrimination exponents; it is not cited as a CTMC theorem.

3. E. Gassiat, A. Cleynen, and S. Robin, “Finite state space non parametric Hidden Markov Models are in general identifiable,” arXiv:1306.4657 (submitted 2013), [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4657), Theorem 2.1. The theorem identifies a known-size finite HMM up to hidden-state permutation from three consecutive observations if the hidden transition matrix has full rank and the emission distributions are linearly independent. That is a positive identifiability result under assumptions. Deterministic duplicated block emissions in Counterexample A violate the linear-independence condition.

4. P. Metzner, E. Dittmer, T. Jahnke, and C. Schütte, “Generator estimation of Markov jump processes,” Journal of Computational Physics 227(1) (2007), 353–375, DOI [10.1016/j.jcp.2007.07.032](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2007.07.032). The paper treats generator estimation from incomplete/discrete observations and explicitly flags that the matrix exponential need not be injective for generators with complex eigenvalues. Counterexample B supplies an exact three-state rate pair illustrating this boundary.

5. Classical testing facts used in proof.md (Neyman–Pearson optimality, KL data processing, total-variation/Hellinger inequalities) are proved in-line for the particular risk bound or applied in their standard form. The report makes no novel priority claim for these inequalities.

## Source boundary

The exact CTMC path divergence, hidden-readout bound, Fisher contraction, Chernoff semigroup, and explicit counterexamples are internally derived/reconstructed in this lane. They are standard mathematical mechanisms or direct consequences; they have not received independent external proof review. The synthetic discriminators are proposed model checks, not executed experiments or biological claims.
