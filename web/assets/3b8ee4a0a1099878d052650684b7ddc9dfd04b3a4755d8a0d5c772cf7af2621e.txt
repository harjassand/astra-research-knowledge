T18 — Four-dimensional Kakeya maximal obstruction: two-frame curvature candidate
Frozen record: v1 | 2026-10-09 | status: candidate mechanism; primary target unresolved

Claim status
------------
No new Kakeya theorem is proved here. The exact four-dimensional target, the current source-reported frontier, one exact frame-change identity, a scale-critical bush calculation, and counterexamples to a simpler directional-covariance invariant are recorded. The proposed three-frame curvature statistic is an invention candidate, not a proved inequality and not a priority claim.

1. Exact target and interface
-----------------------------
For 0<delta<1, let T be a finite family of unit delta-tubes in R^4 with pairwise delta-separated directions, and put f_T=sum_{T in T} 1_T. Up to the usual duality, the dimension-four Kakeya maximal conjecture is

    ||f_T||_{L^{4/3}(R^4)} <= C_epsilon delta^{-epsilon}.

Equivalently, the maximal average over unit delta-tubes maps L^4(R^4) to L^4(S^3) with norm O_epsilon(delta^{-epsilon}). The unrestricted target is the full power delta^{-epsilon}, for every epsilon>0. The source-reported 4D estimate at dimension d<4 has the form

    ||f_T||_{L^{d/(d-1)}(R^4)}
       <= C_epsilon delta^{-(4/d-1+epsilon)},

and does not reach d=4.

Use the standard slope chart on a fixed direction patch with v_4 bounded away from zero. Write each centerline as

    (M_i(t),t),   M_i(t)=b_i+t u_i,   b_i,u_i in R^3.

At delta=1/N, a tube is represented by its occupied time bins and spatial cells. For a weighted indexed family, let n be total index weight, I the weighted number of occupied time-cell incidences, E the set of occupied spacetime cells, m=I/|E|, k=I/n, and lambda=k/N. This is an explicit supplied/acquired interface: the tubes and their direction separation are supplied; the shading, multiplicity profile, and moving-box concentration must be controlled in the proof.

For an affine moving box B with orthogonal widths a/N,b/N,c/N over all t in [0,1], let n_B be the weight of indices whose entire traces M_i([0,1]) lie in B, and define

    Delta_box = sup_B n_B/(abc),  1<=a<=b<=c<=N.

The natural line-space endpoint target is: for every p>3 and eta>0,

    m <= C_{p,eta} N^eta lambda^{-p} Delta_box.                 (1)

For unit weights and N^{-1}-separated slopes, endpoint differences force the slopes of lines contained in B into a box of volume O(abc/N^3), hence n_B<=Cabc and Delta_box=O(1). Since |E|=nk/m, (1) gives

    |E| >= C^{-1}N^{-eta} n N lambda^{p+1}.

At p down to 3 this is the density-four estimate |E|>=N^{-o(1)} nN lambda^4, which is the tube-union form corresponding to the L^4 maximal endpoint. The familiar subcritical loss predicted by this reduction is delta^{-(p-3)/4-epsilon}. A complete reduction for the 4D weighted model is not supplied here; (1) is the intended geometric target, not an established implication in this record.

3. Broad part and the candidate curvature invariant
---------------------------------------------------

At a spatial cell Q, write V_Q for the directions of tubes meeting Q and m(Q)=#V_Q. A fixed-threshold broad/narrow split is available without polynomial partitioning. If no 3-plane carries all but a fixed fraction of V_Q, compactness of S^3 gives four fixed-size direction sectors with determinant bounded below and each carrying a fixed fraction of m(Q). The endpoint multilinear Kakeya inequality controls the broad contribution after summing over the finite sector choices (Bennett–Carbery–Tao, arXiv:math/0509262v1; Guth, arXiv:0811.2251v2). The constants depend on the broadness threshold. This does not control the narrow part when its approximating 3-plane changes with Q or across scales.

To isolate that missing transport, parameterize line trajectories in R^3 by M(t)=b+tu. For theta=(theta_1,theta_2), define a frame at (t,theta) by

    X = M_1(t)+theta_1 M_2(t)+theta_2 M_3(t),
    Y = M_2(t),  Z = M_3(t),
    U = u_1+theta_1 u_2+theta_2 u_3,
    V = u_2,      W = u_3.

For a second frame (t+tau,theta+eta), direct expansion gives the exact transition

    X' = X + tau U + eta_1 Y + eta_2 Z + tau eta_1 V + tau eta_2 W,
    Y' = Y + tau V,       Z' = Z + tau W,
    U' = U + eta_1 V + eta_2 W,
    V' = V,                W' = W.                              (2)

The associated 6x6 linear map is unipotent. The two bilinear terms in X' are the new feature relative to the 3D line-space calculation, which has only one transverse shear parameter.

Take a base frame 0 and two comparison frames i=1,2 with gaps (tau_i,eta_i), eta_i in R^2. Define the **three-frame curvature matrix**

    C_3 = [ tau_1 eta_{1,1}   tau_1 eta_{1,2} ]
          [ tau_2 eta_{2,1}   tau_2 eta_{2,2} ],

and the scalar curvature

    K_3 = det(C_3)=tau_1 tau_2 det(eta_1,eta_2).                 (3)

This is an oriented area in the two independent normal-shear directions, weighted by the two time gaps. It is invariant under changing the base only up to the expected re-expression of the frame gaps; its scale, rather than exact numerical value, is the intended invariant. If |K_3|>=kappa, then, after conditioning on the other terms in (2), the two cross-term observations give independent linear forms in (V,W); solving for both coordinates costs O(kappa^{-1}) when the frame gaps are bounded. If K_3=0, the two rows are collinear and only one normal-velocity combination is exposed. This statement is just the determinant formula and 2x2 inversion; it makes no Kakeya estimate by itself.

The candidate multiscale mechanism is to stratify by the dyadic size of K_3 and couple that curvature budget to the six coordinate entropy rates of (X,Y,Z,U,V,W). In a high-curvature stratum, two frame comparisons should force entropy into both V and W. In a low-curvature stratum, the normal shifts are effectively one-dimensional; a line-space box/plany argument should then control concentration. The missing theorem is a uniform weighted entropy-transport inequality that sums these strata with only N^{o(1)} total loss and yields (1) for every p>3. Neither the exponent allocation among the six widths nor this summation inequality has been proved.

4. Stress tests and rejected shortcut
-------------------------------------

The simpler proposal “use det(sum v v^T) as the local four-dimensional uncertainty” fails as an entropy detector. Here v is a unit direction and the matrix is the uncentered directional second moment.

**Bush.** Let a delta-net of S^3 (about delta^{-3} directions) index tubes all passing through the origin. On a shell |x|~r, delta<=r<=1, the multiplicity is comparable to r^{-3} and the shell volume is comparable to r^4. Thus

    integral_{|x|~r} f^{4/3} dx ~ r^4 (r^{-3})^{4/3} ~ 1.

There are about log(1/delta) dyadic shells, so ||f||_{4/3}^{4/3}~log(1/delta). This saturates the conjectured endpoint up to the permitted delta^{-epsilon} loss. Any proposed scale sum must allow one unit of contribution per scale.

**Hairbrush.** Fix a root segment R={s e_1:0<=s<=1}. Choose a delta-net V_delta in a direction patch with v_4 in [1/3,2/3] and |v_1|<=1/2. For each v, take the tube through v_4 e_1 in direction v. It meets R. Near the root point t e_1, the tubes with |v_4-t|<=C delta contribute about delta^{-2} directions; these directions lie on a two-dimensional spherical slice v_4=t. Yet their uncentered moment matrix is positive definite (on a compact subpatch, all four eigenvalues are bounded below by a constant times the local count): a nonzero linear functional cannot vanish on an open patch of that affine spherical slice. So full linear span does not imply three-dimensional angular entropy. The root's delta-neighborhood contributes only O(delta^{1/3}) to the L^{4/3} integral, so this is a diagnostic counterexample to the statistic, not to Kakeya.

**Regulus/product.** Let u_j=(cos(2pi j/M),sin(2pi j/M)) and w_k=(cos(2pi k/M),sin(2pi k/M)), M~delta^{-1}, and use directions v_{jk}=u_j tensor w_k in R^4. They are delta-separated, number ~delta^{-2}, and lie on the rank-one quadric x_1 x_4-x_2 x_3=0, a ruled product surface in S^3. Their second moment tends to (1/4)I_4, despite their two-dimensional angular entropy. If all tubes pass through the origin, multiplicity on the delta-neighborhood of the resulting three-dimensional cone at radius r is ~r^{-2}; the shell contribution is O(delta r^{1/3}), and the total L^{4/3} integral is O(delta). Again a full determinant does not measure angular entropy or incidence cost.

**Planebrush.** In the plany configuration from the 4D literature, directions through each fine cube lie within O(delta) of a two-plane that may depend on the cube. The local second moment is close to rank at most two, so the determinant test calls it narrow. This is the right local classification but supplies no relation between the planes at adjacent scales. The unresolved part is precisely a transport rule for the plane field and its associated tube counts.

The determinant shortcut is therefore rejected. The three-frame matrix (3) records a different quantity: curvature of the sequence of normal frames. Its validity as a useful invariant remains untested against fully specified realizations of the four stress-test families, because those examples do not by themselves specify the extremal event-frame law.

There is also a direct limitation of K_3 as a stand-alone classifier. For the product/regulus tube family, the coordinate formula permits generic three-frame gaps with tau_1,tau_2 nonzero and linearly independent eta_1,eta_2, giving K_3 nonzero while the direction set still has only about delta^{-2} elements and its bush has vanishing L^{4/3} mass. This is an algebraic stress test, not a realizability claim about the event-frame law used in a proof. Thus curvature has to be coupled to occupied-cell entropy and incidence weights; nonzero curvature alone does not force an extremal four-dimensional tube family.

5. What is proved and what remains
----------------------------------

Internally derived exactly: the tube/line-space endpoint interface; the box-counting reason for the abc denominator; the frame-change identity (2); the determinant and inversion consequence (3); the bush logarithm; and the hairbrush and product/regulus counterexamples to local second-moment isotropy as an entropy proxy.

Source-reported only: Moreira's d=3.067379... estimate; the OpenAI Math 3D maximal and 4D Hausdorff claims; all imported 3D maximal, Furstenberg, and multilinear inequalities. None was independently proof-audited here.

Unresolved exact inference: prove the density inequality (1), or an equivalent four-dimensional maximal estimate. In particular, one needs a uniform estimate for low-curvature frame triples, a quantitative entropy gain for high-curvature triples, and a summation over dyadic curvature classes with only N^{o(1)} loss. A nonzero determinant alone is insufficient: the selected frames can be correlated with the line parameters, and conditioning can erase the apparent entropy gain. No external correctness, novelty, or priority claim is made.

No computational experiment was necessary or run; all displayed checks are exact geometric/counting derivations. There is no added test script.
