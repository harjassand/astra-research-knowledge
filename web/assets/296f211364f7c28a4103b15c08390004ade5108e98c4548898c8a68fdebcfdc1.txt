T07 — Dynamics, geometric complexity, and transport cost
Frozen record v1 — 2026-10-09
Status: candidate mechanism diagnosed; target remains unresolved. This record does not claim a proof of Bressan's endpoint mixing-cost conjecture or of endpoint propagation for the logarithmic Sobolev seminorm.

0. Result of this finite investigation

The most direct scale-counting quantity for binary mixing is the logarithmic difference seminorm

  H(ρ) = ∫_{T^d} ∫_{|h|<r_*} |ρ(x+h)-ρ(x)|² |h|^{-d} dh dx,   r_*<inj(T^d)/2.

It has the right geometric strength: if ρ takes the values ±1 and every ball at scale ε contains a fixed positive fraction of both signs, then H(ρ) is at least c(d,κ) log(1/ε)-C(d,κ). For a smooth divergence-free flow its formal variation is the quadratic commutator

  dH/dt = ⟨ρ,[u·∇,M]ρ⟩,

where M is the Fourier multiplier associated with the difference kernel; at high frequency M(k)=c_d log|k|+O(1). This gives a plausible bridge from geometric mixing to action, but the required action-only endpoint bound |dH/dt|≲||∇u||_1 is exactly the missing assertion. The pair-kernel absolute-value estimate has a logarithmically divergent sum over scales and cannot provide that bound. In the requested intermittent stress test, a high-frequency binary laminate inside a compactly supported affine strain patch does not refute the endpoint estimate: the trace-free angular cancellation removes the apparent log-frequency accumulation, and both H-growth and action scale like aδ² per unit time. That calculation is local and does not control arbitrary multiscale/aligned laminates or the return-flow region.

A primary preprint dated 2026-08-08 provides a close, but stronger-assumption, endpoint theory: weighted-maximal-operator/Osgood flow regularity and mixing lower bounds under an extra superlinear Orlicz assumption on Db. Its theorem yields sublogarithmic functions of ε for weaker Orlicz scales and the classical log bound at L log L; it does not give a bound from L¹ action alone. The same paper explicitly leaves open an L¹ control of the Lusin distortion function K that would imply local Hlog regularity. The present campaign's exact action-only target remains open in the sources checked, and the present work makes no decisive new theorem.

1. Exact target and conventions

Let d≥2 and use the unit flat torus T^d with normalized Lebesgue measure. Let u∈C^∞([0,T]×T^d;R^d) be divergence-free and let X solve ∂t X(t,a)=u(t,X(t,a)), X(0,a)=a. The action considered here is the homogeneous seminorm

  A_T(u) = ∫_0^T ∫_{T^d} |∇u(t,x)| dx dt.

For smooth u this equals the time-integrated total variation of u, up to the chosen matrix norm. A spatially constant velocity has zero action, as it should: it only translates the tracer. If instead one writes the full W^{1,1} norm, its zeroth-order term is irrelevant to the conjectured obstruction and must be removed or quotiented by translations.

Take E⊂T^d with |E|=1/2 and finite perimeter, and set ρ_0=2 1_E−1. It is transported by ρ_t=ρ_0∘X(t,·)^{-1}; it stays ±1 almost everywhere and has mean zero. Fix κ∈(0,1/2), for example κ=1/3. Say ρ_T is geometrically mixed at scale ε when for every x∈T^d,

  κ ≤ |{ρ_T=1}∩B_ε(x)|/|B_ε| ≤ 1−κ.

The sharp endpoint target is a constant c=c(d,E,κ)>0 such that every sufficiently small ε and every smooth divergence-free u satisfying this mixing condition obey

  A_T(u) ≥ c log(1/ε).

The quantifiers are uniform in T and u. This is the periodic Bressan mixing-cost assertion. It is stronger than an Lp result for p>1, than an asymptotic-in-time statement for periodically repeated flows, and than a bound with an additional L log L or other superlinear spatial regularity cost. All constants and the scale normalization must be kept fixed when comparing variants.

2. The candidate geometric invariant and its mixing lower bound

Define

  H_{r_*}(ρ) = ∫_{T^d}∫_{|h|<r_*} |ρ(x+h)-ρ(x)|² |h|^{-d} dh dx.

The seminorm is finite for BV data; for binary ρ, |ρ(x+h)-ρ(x)|²=2|ρ(x+h)-ρ(x)|, so it is a scale-integrated phase-disagreement count. In Fourier variables, with e_k(x)=exp(2π i k·x),

  H_{r_*}(ρ)=Σ_{k∈Z^d} m_{r_*}(k)|ρ̂(k)|²,
  m_{r_*}(k)=∫_{|h|<r_*}|e^{2π i k·h}−1|² |h|^{-d}dh.

For |k|r_*≫1, m_{r_*}(k)=2|S^{d−1}| log|k|+O_{d,r_*}(1), so this is a logarithmic frequency moment, not a power Sobolev norm.

Here is the exact geometric implication, also established in Bruè–Colombo–Johansson, Lemma 6.5, in the notation of their torus seminorm. If ρ∈{−1,1} a.e. and mix_κ(ρ)=ε≤r_*=1/5, then

  H_{r_*}(ρ) ≥ c(d,κ) log(1/ε) − C(d,κ).

The derivation is direct. For r>ε, the mixing condition implies at every x that a fixed fraction of B_r(x) has the sign opposite to ρ(x), so

  average_{B_r(x)} |ρ(z)−ρ(x)| ≥ c(κ).

Writing the ball average in polar layers and integrating dr/r from ε to r_* yields a logarithmic lower bound. Fubini and the polar-coordinate identity bound this averaged quantity by C(d)H_{r_*}(ρ), up to a harmless large-scale constant. Thus any action estimate propagating H immediately yields the desired geometric mixing barrier.

This is stronger than a bare L² negative-Sobolev interpolation: it records one unit of phase disagreement at each logarithmic scale. Its weakness is temporal, not geometric.

3. Exact smooth commutator and pair-kernel identities

For a smooth radial cutoff χ supported in [0,r_*), equal to 1 on [0,r_*/2], set w(r)=χ(r)r^{−d} and

  H_χ(ρ)=∫∫_{T^d×T^d} |ρ(y)−ρ(x)|² w(dist(x,y)) dxdy.

Choose χ≥0. Then H_χ dominates the hard-cutoff seminorm integrated only to r_*/2, so the mixing lower bound in §2 also applies to H_χ, with constants changed only by the fixed cutoff radius.

The cutoff makes the outer boundary contribution explicit and avoids treating a sharp cutoff distributionally. Write M_χ for the Fourier multiplier whose symbol is the corresponding increment weight. For smooth ρ and smooth divergence-free u, A=u·∇ is skew-adjoint on L², ∂tρ=−Aρ, and differentiation gives the exact identity

  d/dt H_χ(ρ_t) = ⟨ρ_t,[A,M_χ]ρ_t⟩.

Indeed, with the real L² pairing, H=⟨ρ,Mρ⟩ and
  dH/dt=−2⟨Mρ,Aρ⟩=⟨ρ,(AM−MA)ρ⟩.
If û and ρ̂ are torus Fourier coefficients and m(k) is the symbol of M, this becomes

  dH/dt = Re Σ_{k,l∈Z^d} ρ̂(k)^* [2π i l·û(k−l)] [m(l)−m(k)] ρ̂(l).

The complex pairing is understood with the real part; for a log multiplier m(k)=log(2+|k|), the bracket is a log-frequency difference. The formula is exact at the smooth level. Extending it to merely W^{1,1} velocities and transported binary data requires proving the commutator is integrable and passing to the regular-Lagrangian-flow limit; neither step is automatic from this display.

There is also an exact Lagrangian identity. Since X preserves volume,

  H_χ(ρ_t)=∫∫ |ρ_0(a)−ρ_0(b)|² w(dist(X_t(a),X_t(b))) da db.

At times and pairs before torus cut-locus issues, differentiating the radial kernel and changing back to Eulerian variables yields

  dH_χ/dt = ∫∫ |ρ_t(y)−ρ_t(x)|²
       [w'(r)/r] (y−x)·(u(t,y)−u(t,x)) dxdy,
  r=|y−x|,

with w'(r)/r = −dχ(r)r^{−d−2}+χ'(r)r^{−d−1}. This includes the cutoff-annulus term exactly. For the singular inner part, taking absolute values gives the upper bound

  C_d ∫_{|h|<r_*} |h|^{−d−1}
       ∫_{T^d}|ρ(x+h)−ρ(x)|² |u(x+h)−u(x)| dx dh.

This estimate explains the endpoint obstacle. Since the tracer is binary, its squared increment is at most 4, while W^{1,1} only supplies

  ∫|u(x+h)−u(x)|dx ≤ |h| ||∇u||_1.

Substitution leaves C||∇u||_1∫_0^{r_*}dr/r, a divergence over small scales. Using finite perimeter of the current tracer gives an increment estimate proportional to |h|Per({ρ=1}), but combining that with ||u||∞ still leaves a scale factor tied to Per(ρ), and does not remove the alignment of strain concentration with the disagreement set. The missing estimate must use the signed trace-free cancellation in the pair formula or a different invariant. Replacing it by a maximal-function L¹ bound is invalid: the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator has only weak-(1,1) control, and its strong local integrability needs L log L.

For comparison, if M is the ideal log multiplier, the smooth commutator formula reads

  d/dt Σ_k log(2+|k|)|ρ̂(k)|²
    = Re Σ_{k,l} ρ̂(k)^* [2π i l·û(k−l)]
                    [log(2+|l|)−log(2+|k|)]ρ̂(l).

This expression retains cancellation that the absolute pair estimate discards. No action-only bound on it has been proved here.

4. Intermittent affine-strain stress test, including the return flow

This calculation addresses a specific failure mode: arbitrarily high-frequency binary lamellae placed inside an arbitrarily small region where the strain is large. Work in d=2 and choose χ_0∈C_c^∞((-1,1)^2), χ_0=1 on [−1/2,1/2]^2. For patch size δ>0 and strain a>0, define a stream function and velocity

  ψ_δ(x,y)=a x y χ_0(x/δ,y/δ),
  u_δ=(∂_y ψ_δ,−∂_x ψ_δ).

Then div u_δ=0, supp u_δ⊂(−δ,δ)^2, and u_δ=(a x,−a y) in the central square. Scaling gives |∇u_δ|≤C a throughout the support and

  ||∇u_δ||_{L¹(T²)} ≤ C a δ².

The annular compensating return flow is included in this estimate; no non-periodic affine field is being charged as if it were globally admissible.

Put a square-wave binary laminate of stripe spacing N^{−1} in the central patch, with Nδ≫1, and set it equal to either sign outside the patch, smoothing only the description of the patch boundary if needed while retaining a binary set. Its logarithmic disagreement energy contributed by interior pairs has size

  H_patch(ρ_N) ≍ δ² log(Nδ),

up to constants from the fixed patch geometry. To see the lower estimate, take h=rω with C/N≤r≤cδ and |ω_y| bounded below; a fixed positive fraction of x-positions within the central patch cross an interface, uniformly up to harmless stripe-period oscillations. The kernel contributes dr/r, giving log(Nδ).

In the affine core, the flow map is (x,y)↦(e^{at}x,e^{−at}y). A stripe pattern initially normal to y becomes frequency N e^{at} in y. For a short time at≤c_0, use a central subpatch whose trajectories stay inside the affine core; its area remains comparable to δ². Therefore the high-frequency contribution changes by order δ² at. The action over that interval is order atδ². Thus the one-patch affine hotspot has matching H-growth/action scaling, uniformly in N and δ. This does not disprove an endpoint estimate.

The exact cancellation behind the scaling can be seen in the inner pair kernel. Write z=rω and A=diag(a,−a), so u(x+z)−u(x)=Az and

  z·(u(x+z)−u(x))/|z|² = ω·Aω = a(ω_1²−ω_2²),
  ∫_{S¹}ω·Aω dω = (|S¹|/2)tr A = 0.

For the square-wave laminate, the x-averaged squared increment at displacement z is 4q_N(z_2), where q_N(s) is the fraction of phases changed by a shift s. For |s|≤(2N)^{−1}, q_N(s)=2N|s|; for general s it is a bounded period-N function with mean 1/2. At r≫N^{−1}, the angular oscillatory error is O((Nr)^{−1/2}) by stationary phase in the two angular directions where ω_2 is extremal. The constant mean multiplies the zero angular average of ω·Aω. Hence the radial integral over N^{−1}≤r≤cδ is bounded by

  C aδ² ∫_{1/N}^{cδ} (Nr)^{−1/2} dr/r ≤ C aδ²,

while for r≤N^{−1}, q_N(rω_2)≤2Nr|ω_2| and the integral is

  C aδ² ∫_0^{1/N} Nr dr/r ≤ C aδ².

These are scaling estimates for the periodic laminate in the affine core; boundary/cutoff errors are supported in an area O(δ²) with strain O(a), but an all-pattern uniform estimate for those errors has not been established here. The affine flow multiplies the local stripe frequency by e^{at}; this makes the one-patch model scale-consistent with H-growth of order aδ² per unit time and action of order aδ² per unit time, but the full H-variation including all cross-patch and return-flow pairs has not been bounded here. The calculation demonstrates why taking absolute values before angular averaging manufactures a false log N in the affine-core term. It also shows the test actually required of any universal argument: preserve cancellation after localizing in x, allowing arbitrary pattern alignment and scale separation, and include the return region.

For nested or intermittent patches, a disjoint affine model with patches of areas δ_j² and rates a_j would give total action ∑a_jδ_j² and the same order of log-energy growth ∑a_jδ_j² per unit time. This is only a scaling model. It does not cover overlapping hotspots, changing patch locations, nonlinear cut-and-shuffle stages, or laminates correlated across all scales. Those are the unresolved stress cases, not evidence either for or against the bound.

5. Other constructions tested at the level of obstruction

Cut-and-shuffle. A single alternating laminate of spacing ε has interface length on the order of ε^{−1} in two dimensions and Hlog of order log(1/ε), but specifying a final pattern says nothing about the least incompressible action needed to generate it. A baker-type map suggests successive doubling, but its discontinuous cuts must be realized by an admissible smooth velocity and the transition cost must be counted. Conversely, a one-dimensional rearrangement/BV-map lower bound does not imply the two-dimensional flow conjecture. I found no valid way to sum stage costs at the endpoint without reintroducing a scale loss.

Thin filaments. Stretching a single interface produces long filaments but only mixes a thin neighborhood of that interface. To satisfy the every-ball criterion throughout the domain, filament neighborhoods must cover essentially all of the torus; this is why a local construction is not itself a counterexample. A perimeter estimate under ||∇u||∞ yields exponential perimeter growth, but replacing ||∇u||∞ by ||∇u||1 is the same concentration/alignment problem as in the commutator calculation.

Intermittent mixing. A high strain on a tiny patch can have small global L¹ action, but it modifies only that patch. The explicit stream-function test above checks the local candidate invariant and finds no violation in the affine model. Any global low-action counterexample would need to transfer this intermittency across enough patches and scales to meet the every-ball condition; the cost of that transfer is exactly what remains unaccounted for here.

6. Primary-source comparison and exact scope

The following facts were checked in primary papers/preprints online on 2026-10-09; source-reported claims remain source-reported, not independent proof validation.

(a) Crippa–De Lellis, “Estimates and regularity results for the DiPerna–Lions flow,” J. Reine Angew. Math. 616 (2008), author-hosted PDF: https://www.math.ias.edu/delellis/sites/math.ias.edu.delellis/files/Estimates_ODEs.pdf. Their theorem gives the logarithmic mixing-cost lower bound for p>1 under their mixing definition and conjectures the p=1 endpoint. The original statement's norm is ∫_0^T ||Db(t)||_{L^p}dt; the endpoint action is ∫|Db|, not the full norm of u. This source pins the classical comparison, not an endpoint solution.

(b) Elia Bruè, Maria Colombo, Carl Johan Peter Johansson, “Lyapunov exponents, entropy and mixing for DiPerna-Lions flows,” arXiv:2510.02921v1 (submitted 2025-10-03), https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02921. §1.4, equations (1.8)–(1.9), lines 153–159 in the web version, define the torus seminorm used above and state propagation for p>1, explicitly leaving p=1 open. Lemma 6.5, lines 990–1015, gives the binary-tracer geometric inequality. Their Theorems 1.4–1.5 are asymptotic results in a periodic-in-time setting and p>1; they do not settle the finite-time L¹ action target. In §6.1 their general map argument has an error term proportional to ε^{(p−1)/p}; the vanishing step degenerates at p=1.

(c) Henrique Borrin and João Fernando Nariyoshi, “Quantitative Osgood regularity for DiPerna–Lions flows,” arXiv:2608.08337v1 (submitted 2026-08-08), https://arxiv.org/html/2608.08337. Theorem 1.1, §1.2, lines 109–145, proves a quantitative Osgood modulus outside an exceptional set under b∈L¹_tW^{1,1}_{loc}, growth and compressibility assumptions; the functions depend on the integrability modulus of Db. Its geometric mixing Theorem 6.1, lines 1948–1952, assumes a balanced finite-perimeter partition and a divergence-free field with the paper's support/DiPerna–Lions assumptions, but also an extra finite superlinear Young-function condition Db∈L¹_tL^{Φ_0}_x and linked Φ,Ψ hypotheses (Assumption 3.1, lines 606–644). It proves Ψ(ε^{−d})≤C(∫||Db||_1+∫||Db||_{L^{Φ_0}}). For Llog^bL it yields log(ε^{−d})^b lower bounds, 0<b≤1; the log bound at b=1 uses LlogL. It does not produce log(1/ε) from L¹ alone. §7, Question 1, lines 2118–2131, explicitly asks whether a global W^{1,1} regular Lagrangian flow has an L¹ Lusin-distortion control K such that |X_t(x)−X_t(y)|≤exp(K(x)+K(y))|x−y|; the authors say an affirmative answer would yield local Hlog regularity and possibly Bressan, while a negative answer suggests genuinely Osgood endpoint behavior. That is a sharp nearby open question, not something resolved here. The article's Osgood theorem also warns that the W^{1,1} and BV flow regimes must not be conflated: Proposition 4.3 gives a BV example for which their Osgood estimate fails.

(d) Elia Huysmans and Felix Said, “Mixing Estimates for Passive Scalar Transport by BV Vector Fields,” arXiv:2504.03023v1 (submitted 2025-04-03), https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03023. Theorem A, lines 257–264, provides a finite-time W^{-1,1} estimate for autonomous divergence-free BV velocity fields with tetration dependence. Their Remark 2.8, lines 265–270, traces the tower loss to local regularization and a pigeonhole argument. This is a genuine endpoint estimate in a restricted autonomous class, but it does not establish the sharp log lower barrier for general time-dependent smooth/W^{1,1} flows.

(e) Current-status search on 2026-10-09 used exact-phrase and arXiv-targeted web queries for Bressan's conjecture, W^{1,1} endpoint, and mixing-cost lower bounds. No later primary proof was found. The latest directly relevant primary sources above still identify the action-only endpoint as open. Search absence is not a proof of novelty or priority.

7. Claim ledger

Source-reported: (i) the p>1 logarithmic mixing-cost lower bound; (ii) the binary-tracer Hlog lower bound from geometric mixing; (iii) p>1 propagation of Hlog; (iv) the p=1 propagation and finite-time Bressan endpoint are open in the checked papers; (v) the 2026 Osgood/Orlicz results and their extra assumptions.

Internally derived at the smooth level: the commutator identity and Fourier formula in §3; the stream-function compact strain construction and its action scaling; the affine trace-free angular cancellation and the one-scale square-wave laminate scaling test in §4. These calculations have not been independently reviewed and do not extend to arbitrary rough fields.

Candidate: Hlog is the right geometric invariant to turn endpoint propagation into the desired binary mixing barrier. A cancellation-preserving, scale-summed commutator estimate could provide the missing mechanism.

Unresolved: an action-only bound H(ρ_T)≤H(ρ_0)+C(d)A_T(u) for general smooth divergence-free u, or an alternative invariant with equivalent geometric coercivity; passage to W^{1,1} regular Lagrangian flows; a uniform estimate surviving arbitrary scale separation, local strain alignment, moving hotspots and return flows; a construction violating the proposed barrier; and the L¹ Lusin-distortion question of Borrin–Nariyoshi. No bound or counterexample has been established here.

8. Exact next mathematical gate

A successful continuation must do one of the following, with all quantifiers and constants uniform in the scale:

1. Prove a cancellation-retaining estimate for the commutator in §3 that is bounded by ∫||∇u||_1 for every binary tracer (or a new invariant whose binary mixing lower bound is c log(1/ε)); the proof must explicitly include the high-frequency patch laminate and its compensating return flow and cannot insert a strong L¹ maximal-function estimate.
2. Construct a smooth divergence-free sequence and binary initial half-set with A_T=o(log(1/ε)) that meets the every-ball mixing condition; account for transition layers, return flow, all unmixed regions, and the exact action.
3. Prove or refute the stronger Lusin-distortion control K∈L¹ for W^{1,1} incompressible regular Lagrangian flows, then verify carefully whether its forward/inverse estimates really imply the required Hlog propagation and finite-time geometric barrier.

A proof only for p>1, a bound with LlogL (or another superlinear Orlicz cost), an asymptotic repeated-map statement, an autonomous/BV special case, a local hotspot computation, or an interpolation inequality does not pass this gate.
