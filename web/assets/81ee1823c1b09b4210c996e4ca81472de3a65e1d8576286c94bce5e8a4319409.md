# Counterexamples and scope failures

These examples are deliberately inside or adjacent to the model interface. They prevent a neutral, fixed-copy result from being promoted into a universal claim about mitophagy, mitochondrial repair, or aging.

## 1. Selective clearance reverses the drift sign

Let mutant replication fitness be 1-s with 0<s<1, wild-type fitness 1, and removal be uniform. If the current mutant count is k, the mutant-parent probability is (1-s)k/(N-sk). The exact one-attempt expected mutant-count change is

    -s*k*(N-k)/(N*(N-s*k)),

which is negative for every interior state. Increasing the turnover event rate speeds mutant loss. This is a direct counterexample to “more turnover always increases mutant burden.” The selection coefficient must be measured rather than inferred from total mitophagy flux.

## 2. Replicative advantage reverses the conclusion the other way

For a deletion with mutant fitness 1+s, the mutant-parent probability exceeds its frequency at every interior state. Uniform removal then gives positive expected mutant-count drift. Faster turnover accelerates expansion. Deletion-bearing mtDNA and selective replication are modeled in prior work; the 2026 deletion-accumulation paper emphasizes that the sign may depend on the deletion’s effect on transcription-linked replication feedback. This is not a prediction for every deletion or tissue.

## 3. Equal mean heteroplasmy does not determine threshold burden

Take functional threshold h*=0.8. Population A has every cell at heteroplasmy 0.5, so no cell is above threshold. Population B has half of cells at 0 and half at 1, also with mean 0.5, but half are above threshold. Thus a bulk mean, or a bulk damage total without its single-cell distribution, cannot identify the fraction of threshold-crossing cells. This is an elementary distributional alias, not a new result.

## 4. Same turnover flux can represent different genotype-conditioned kernels

Two interventions can have the same total number of mtDNA replacement events per day and opposite net effects on a focal mutant: one removes mutant-bearing units preferentially, the other replicates mutant genomes preferentially. The total turnover rate alone does not distinguish their transition matrices. The model’s decisive data are copy number plus allele-conditioned birth/removal rates, not only mitophagy reporter flux or mitochondrial mass.

## 5. Finite age and eventual threshold risk are different quantities

The neutral hit-before-loss probability k/m is an eventual probability. It does not say the threshold will be reached by an animal’s age, by the endpoint of a culture experiment, or before a lineage is lost. The supplied finite-time CTMC calculation changes substantially with rho*T/N; the 2026 npj Aging work already derives first-passage distributions for its deletion model. Using k/m as a finite-age disease probability is an invalid inference.

## 6. Functional threshold is mutation- and tissue-specific

The 80% threshold used in the source-parameter illustration is an explicit example. Mitochondrial disease thresholds vary with mutation, tissue, respiratory demand and complementation. An operational threshold in an assay must be acquired from the same allele and tissue context. Treating h*=0.8 as universal would be a biological counterexample to the model-to-nature connection, not a model theorem failure.
