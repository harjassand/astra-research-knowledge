# Source notes and prior-art boundary

## Primary model source

Kocka, Husain & Agudo-Canalejo, “Nonequilibrium protein complexes as molecular
automata,” arXiv v2 (27 March 2026), subsequently published in *PRX Life*.

- [Full text, arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.15603v2)
- [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/dgw7-s8gl)
- [Official APS abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prxlife/abstract/10.1103/dgw7-s8gl)
- [Code and data record, DOI 10.5522/04/31497577.v1](https://doi.org/10.5522/04/31497577.v1)

Useful source locations in the HTML:

- Lines 31–38: version, title, abstract, and theoretical molecular-automata
  capability claim.
- Lines 58–77: binary ring; local-triplet state changes; spontaneous detailed
  balance; enzyme reaction and local detailed-balance ratio; equal-node-energy
  and common-fuel simplifications; forward/backward rate formula; reverse
  channels.
- Lines 80–86: strongly driven limit, active/absent enzyme truth table, 256
  rule sets, and asynchronous single-subunit updates.
- Lines 107–116: reported exponential relaxation of Rule 166 with increasing
  complex size and proposed stopwatch interpretation.
- Lines 117–130: bistable-rule set; Rule 232 perfect single-bit splitting in
  the idealized attractor sense; Rule 166 lack of perfect single-flip correction;
  finite-affinity and spontaneous-noise comparisons.
- Lines 126–129: source defines \(\tau_{10}\) from probability mass in the
  exact all-zero state, not a majority-decoded first-passage time.
- Lines 149–160: authors’ discussion says no extant complex is known to
  implement molecular automata and concrete realizations remain future work;
  the cited switchable assemblies are presented as ingredients.
- Lines 176–189: full-state master-equation construction and explicit
  numerical definition of source \(\tau_{10}\) as the time at which
  \(P(X_t=0^N)\) reaches 0.9.

The closed-form run-length recurrences in `PROOF.md` are reconstructed from
this source model. They do not establish priority or a new biomolecular result.

## Experimental ingredients are not an implementation

The model paper cites Pillai et al., “De novo design of allosterically
switchable protein assemblies,” *Nature* 632 (2024),
[DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07813-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07813-2),
and KaiC-family assemblies as possible ingredients. These sources support
switchable assemblies or phosphorylation dynamics, not eight local-triplet
specific enzyme channels implementing a selected rule. This lane’s bounded
frontier scan found no concrete molecular automaton implementing Rule 166 or
232. The source authors explicitly leave such a realization open.

## Rejected candidate route

Facilitated dissociation with a designed ternary intermediate and switchable
off-rates is already directly demonstrated by Broerman et al., *Nature* (2025),
[DOI 10.1038/s41586-025-09549-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09549-z).
It was rejected as a new lead because it is established prior art, not an open
capability gap.

## Boundaries

- Source-reported: rule enumeration, exponential Rule 166 spectral relaxation,
  splitter probabilities, and the finite-noise simulations described in the
  paper.
- Internally reconstructed: run-length CTMC reduction, closed-form first-
  passage/readout and fuel-count formulas, exact first-exit rates, and the
  Rule 232 finite-affinity clean-repair threshold.
- Finite arithmetic replay: the specific matched-capacity \(N=7\) comparison
  in `exact_rate_results.json`, solved over rational rates with zero algebraic
  residual.
- Unknown: mapping any designed protein/RNA sequence or enzyme concentration
  to the assumed local specificity, prefactors, equal node energies, uniform
  chemical potential, and spontaneous-flip rate; cross-reactivity; finite
  substrate/fuel depletion; subunit turnover; expression/assembly cost; and
  experimental memory performance.
- Not claimed: formal proof that a Rule 166 or Rule 232 protein complex can be
  built; source-code equivalence is not a molecular realization; no novelty or
  priority clearance.
