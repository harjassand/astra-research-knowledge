# Complement-LCU image: alternative enforcer audit

**Status: unresolved for the exact image.** I find neither (i) a positive
constant-gap polynomial-row enforcer for the fixed complement-LCU image, nor
(ii) an arbitrary-enforcer lower bound. I do prove that the active-flag
seed-pair row-mass witness does not transfer to this image, and give a
rowwise approximate-sparsity lemma for the fixed BJSW local alphabet. Thus the
cycle-2 Chebyshev method has no superpolynomial obstruction to exploit here.
This is a limitation of that method, not evidence that a sparse enforcer
exists.

## Fixed interface and costs

Use the exact interface in `00_INTERFACE_AND_TENSOR_FORMULA.md`. The augmented
target is $H=\sum_{j=1}^M\pi_j h_j$, where each BMVZ clause is

\[
h_j=I-Q_j,\qquad Q_j=\bigotimes_{r=1}^R Q_{j,r},\qquad
Q_{j,r}=I-P_{j,r},
\]
and the dummy terms are included as factors acting on their own constant-size
registers. Add the zero projector and the LCU identity label as specified in
the interface, so all weights are positive and sum to one. With
\(R_j=I-2h_j=2Q_j-I\), preparation unitary $A$, and
\(\chi=A|0\rangle\), set
\[
U=(A^*\otimes I)\,\mathrm{SELECT}(R_j)\,(A\otimes I),\quad
V|\psi\rangle=U(|0\rangle\otimes|\psi\rangle),\quad
B=(I-|0\rangle\langle0|)\otimes I.
\]
Then $V^*BV=2H-H^2$, so $H\le V^*BV\le2H$, with an at-most-one-sparse diagonal
test of $O(\log M)$ label locality. $V$ and SELECT have polynomial circuit
size when the term generator and controlled local projectors have polynomial
access. Exactness uses the stated algebraic-gate model; finite-gate synthesis
is approximate and needs a separate error charge.

The canonical enforcer $C_0=I-VV^*=UBU^*$ is an exact positive projection,
has kernel exactly \(\mathrm{ran}(V)\), and has gap one on the entire physical
space. Formula (1) of the interface gives polynomial-time specified-entry
evaluation. The established row bound is only
\[
1\le \max_i\operatorname{nnz}(C_{0,i,*})
\le \min\{(M+1)2^N,\ O(M^3 2^{10R})\}.
\]
No row-list algorithm with polynomial output length is known. Entry access
does not supply one.

## Why the active-flag witness is not an LCU witness

Write $c_{j,a}=\overline{A_{ja}}\chi_j$, so
\[
K_a=\sum_j c_{j,a}R_j,\qquad (VV^*)_{(a,x),(b,y)}=(K_aK_b^*)_{x,y}.
\]
For every output label $a\ne0$, orthogonality of column $a$ of $A$ to
\(\chi\) gives \(\sum_j c_{j,a}=0\), and hence the exact identity
\[
K_a=-2\sum_j c_{j,a}h_j.                                      \tag{4}
\]
Also $K_0=I-H$. Consequently every data-dependent block of $VV^*$ is a
linear combination of $h_j$ and $h_jh_k$, plus a scalar identity term
whose only data-basis support is the diagonal. In particular, the
nonzero-label/nonzero-label blocks are
\[
K_aK_b^*=4\sum_{j,k}c_{j,a}\overline{c_{k,b}}\,h_jh_k.
\]
They are not the active-flag block
\(J_\phi U_\phi U_\psi^*J_\psi^*\). A relative decoder unitary must not be
inserted into this formula; the exact LCU blocks are the displayed products
of OR projectors.

For the forced seed pair let $t=00010$, $u=10010$, and let $(v_1,v_2,v_3)$
be $(01110,10110,11010)$. Directly from the supplied seed kets, the one-copy
rows are
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{nonzero columns} & \text{entries}\\ \hline
\langle t|Q_\phi & t,u & 1/2,1/2\\
\langle t|Q_\psi & t,v_1,v_2,v_3 & 3/4,1/4,1/4,1/4\\
\langle t|Q_\phi Q_\psi & t,u,v_1,v_2,v_3 & 3/8,1/2,1/8,1/8,1/8
\end{array}
\]
The last line follows from
\(\langle t|Q_\phi Q_\psi=(1/2)\langle t|Q_\psi+(1/2)\langle u|Q_\psi\)
and \(u\notin\mathrm{supp}(\psi)\). Thus its row squared norm is $7/16$.
For $x=t^{\otimes R}$,
\[
h_\phi h_\psi=I-Q_\phi^{\otimes R}-Q_\psi^{\otimes R}
                    +(Q_\phi Q_\psi)^{\otimes R}.
\]
The off-diagonal row norms of the three nonidentity terms are exactly
\[
\sqrt{2^{-R}-4^{-R}},\qquad
\sqrt{(3/4)^R-(9/16)^R},\qquad
\sqrt{(7/16)^R-(9/64)^R}.
\]
Therefore the off-diagonal row norm of $h_\phi h_\psi$ is at most their
sum, which tends to zero and is bounded above by
$(2^{-1/2})^R+(\sqrt3/2)^R+(\sqrt7/4)^R$. Its exact row support can
still grow exponentially, but that support carries vanishing squared mass on
this row. The cycle-2 witness instead obtained constant mass across $8^R$
entries of a relative Clifford unitary. The LCU seed block has different
algebra and cannot inherit that conclusion. The existing exact seed checker
verifies the Clifford relative-unitary statement; the three displayed
projector rows above are exact rational calculations from the same kets.

## Rowwise approximate-sparsity lemma for the fixed local alphabet

This lemma explains why the preceding loss is structural for the current
row-mass method. It is not a lower bound on arbitrary enforcers.

Assume the BJSW hard instance uses a fixed finite set of constant-arity
local projectors, as in the audited seed family, and include the fixed dummy
projectors. Equivalently, assume a uniform $\rho<1$ with this property: for
every local computational row and every one-copy factor $Q=I-P$ or $QQ'$
(including $P=0$), if that factor's row has at least two nonzero entries,
then its row $\ell_2$ norm is at most $\rho$. This condition is automatic for
a fixed finite local alphabet. Each factor is a contraction; if a row of
$Q$ or $QQ'$ has norm one, the basis vector is fixed by all involved
projections, so the row has support one. The maximum over the finite
remaining rows is therefore strictly below one. For five-local terms and
pairs of terms, group each copy over the union of at most ten acted-on
qubits. The local row-support cap is then the constant $d=2^{10}$; spectator
coordinates do not affect it.

**Claim.** For every computational-basis row $i$ of $P=VV^*$ and every
\(0<\varepsilon<1\), there is a column set $S_i$ of size
\[
|S_i|\le \operatorname{poly}(M,1/\varepsilon)
\]
such that
\[
\sum_{q\notin S_i}|P_{iq}|^2\le\varepsilon^2.             \tag{5}
\]
The polynomial degree depends only on the fixed local alphabet through
$d,\rho$, not on $R$, the data dimension, or the choice of $A$.

**Proof.** Consider a tensor product $S=\bigotimes_r S_r$ in which each
one-copy factor is $Q_{j,r}$ or $Q_{j,r}Q_{k,r}$. Let $t(S,x)$ be the
number of copies whose row factor at data string $x$ has support at least
two. Tensorization gives row support at most $d^{t(S,x)}$ and row norm at
most $\rho^{t(S,x)}$. For a cutoff $t_0$, keep a term’s full row if
$t(S,x)\le t_0$, and replace it by zero otherwise. The retained row has
support at most $d^{t_0}$; the discarded row has norm at most
\(\rho^{t_0}\).

Expand $h_j=I-Q_j$ and
\(h_jh_k=I-Q_j-Q_k+Q_jQ_k\). Each has at most four tensor-product rows
of the form just treated (the identity row has support one). From (4),
\(\sum_j|c_{j,a}|\le1\) by Cauchy-Schwarz. The coefficients in each block
$K_aK_b^*$, or in its $K_0=I-H$ expansion, therefore have total
absolute value bounded by a universal constant (at most sixteen after
expanding the $h_j,h_jh_k$ terms). There are $M^2$ possible term pairs. For each
fixed label block, retain the union of low-$t$ supports over all pairs; it
has size $O(M^2d^{t_0})$, while the omitted row norm is at most
$O(\rho^{t_0})$ by the coefficient $\ell_1$ bound and triangle
inequality. There are $M+1$ output-label blocks. Taking their union gives
support $O(M^3d^{t_0})$ and total omitted row norm
$O(\sqrt{M+1}\rho^{t_0})$. Choose
\[
t_0=\left\lceil\frac{\log(C\sqrt{M+1}/\varepsilon)}
                         {\log(1/\rho)}\right\rceil
\]
for a sufficiently large alphabet-dependent constant $C$. Then the
omitted norm is at most $\varepsilon$, and $d^{t_0}$ is a fixed power of
$M/\varepsilon$. This proves (5). $\square$

One consequence is that any constant-mass row-mass witness for this $P$
must put at least half its mass on a polynomial number of entries. If a row
has selected mass at least $m>0$, and every selected entry has squared
magnitude at most $a$, apply (5) with $\varepsilon^2<m/2$. Then
\[
\frac{m}{2a}\le |S_i|\le\operatorname{poly}(M).
\]
If the candidate row bound is $s\ge n^\kappa$ and $M\le n^c$, the
Chebyshev/row-mass parameter
$D=\log(m/(2a))/\log(s+1)$ is bounded by a constant depending on
$c,\kappa$ and the fixed local alphabet; it does not grow with $R$. This
may exclude some small row-degree exponents for a specified target gap, but
choosing a larger polynomial exponent can make $D\le1$, where the displayed
Chebyshev bound is vacuous. For constant or subpolynomial row degree the
row-mass argument can still give a nontrivial bound. Thus (5) does not force
a vanishing gap for the class of all polynomial row degrees. The exact
projector rows may still have exponentially many nonzeros: (5) is an
$\ell_2$-approximation statement, not exact sparsity.

The finite-alphabet hypothesis matters. If local projectors are allowed to
vary with input size so that nontrivial local row norms approach one, a
uniform $\rho<1$ need not exist. The claim is for the audited fixed BJSW
local gate/projector set plus the stated dummies, or for any family with an
explicit uniform $\rho$ certificate.

## What this does and does not settle

The exact compression/kernel/gap package for $(V,B,C_0)$ is complete
except for row access to $C_0$. The polynomial-time entry formula is not a
row-list proof. The exact projector calculation above blocks transferring the
active-flag $m=1/16$, $a=(1/16)8^{-R}$ witness to this $V$. The approximate
row lemma further says that, for the fixed BJSW alphabet, any constant mass
in any row of $VV^*$ is concentrated on polynomially many columns, so the
previous dimension-free row-mass/Chebyshev argument cannot rule out all
polynomial-row enforcers.

No alternative $C\ne C_0$ with an exact kernel, a full-space constant
Loewner gap, and a polynomial-time row-list oracle is constructed here. No
lower bound for arbitrary positive $C$ follows either: a sparse positive
matrix may couple label and data registers in ways not represented by the
canonical projector or a term-graph/star Laplacian. N26's positive local
syndrome budget theorem also does not apply to arbitrary such $C$; its
positive-term decomposition, support, and normalized-budget premises are not
implied by row sparsity. The unresolved gate is exactly an all-state
polynomial-row positive matrix with kernel $\mathrm{ran}(V)$ and a uniform
positive spectral floor, or a new invariant covering every such matrix.

The falsifiable next checks are: (a) enumerate the exact $C_0$ row support
for increasing seed depth under the fixed BJSW LCU preparation; this tests
only $C_0$; (b) attempt a concrete sparse relation matrix for the LCU frame
\(\{K_a^*|x\rangle\}\), with an exact kernel and a full-space gap proof;
and (c) if claiming an arbitrary-$C$ obstruction, exhibit a row-mass or
other invariant not contradicted by (5). Finite row counts alone establish
neither a polynomial row algorithm nor a general lower bound.
