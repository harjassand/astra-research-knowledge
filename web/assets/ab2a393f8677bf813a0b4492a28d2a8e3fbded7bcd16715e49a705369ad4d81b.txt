ROUND 6 REVISION 02 — PRIMARY STABILITY/LIFTING SCOPE AND FIXED-RANK CHANNEL COROLLARY
Worker c05_l01. 2026-10-07. Cross-phase Sol baseline exposure was authorized
by control/CROSS_PHASE.json after all Sol INITIALs froze. INITIAL.txt remains
unchanged. This report corrects one bibliographic error in INITIAL and adds an
independently derived fixed-range-rank consequence. It does not settle the
unrestricted full-image CP-range lifting gate.

RESULT

The primary theorems checked give dimension-independent stability for each
fixed finite tuple, not a modulus uniform in the number of tuple entries. An
abelian-ultraproduct lifting theorem is stronger in its own exact setting, but
it lifts trace-preserving *-homomorphisms from countably generated abelian
von Neumann algebras into ultraproducts of diffuse coordinate algebras. It is
not a lifting theorem for a UCP map whose whole operator-ball image is only
approximately commuting. None of the audited sources supplies a single
trace-preserving EB map approximating an unrestricted, growing-dimensional CP
image in normalized infinity-to-one trace norm.

There is, however, a channel-level positive corollary for fixed real range
rank. It follows from the fixed-tuple stability theorem and a maximum-volume
basis argument. It uses one output pinching of the original map, so CP and
trace preservation are kept exactly. Its tolerance is independent of matrix
dimension when the range rank is fixed, but the available theorem's
commutator threshold depends on that rank; this does not cover ranks growing
with dimension.

PRIMARY THEOREM AUDIT

1. Bansil–Kachkovskiy, “On almost commuting matrices with respect to the
normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm,” arXiv:2608.31000v1, 2026-08-31,
https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31000v1 .

Theorem 1.1: for each pair of selfadjoint contractions in M_d with normalized
trace, there are commuting approximants, one commuting with the original
first matrix, and

  ||X-X'||_(2,d)^2 + ||Y-Y'||_(2,d)^2 <= 24 ||[X,Y]||_(2,d)^(2/3).

The constant is independent of d. It is a pair result, and the selected
approximating pair depends on the pair. The paper explicitly says its
Theorem 1.1 conclusion is not immediately iterable. Theorem 1.3 treats a
fixed N-tuple Y_1,...,Y_N relative to one X, with a sqrt(N) dependence. Its
Corollary 1.4 treats a fixed N-tuple with a C(N) and exponent
2/3^(N-1). Both are dimension-independent for fixed N, not uniform in N.
They approximate tuples, not a CP map, and do not impose positivity,
unitality, trace preservation, or compatibility on the tuple corrections.
The source's setting is M_d with normalized trace; it does not state the
general weighted direct-sum tracial interface.

Primary-source details directly checked: the abstract and Theorems 1.1–1.3
and Corollary 1.4 in the arXiv v1 HTML. In particular, Theorem 1.3's bound
is 24 sqrt(N) (sum_j ||[X,Y_j]||_(2,d)^2)^(1/3), and the corollary's
constant and exponent depend on N.

2. Hadwin–Li, “A Note on Approximate Liftings,” Operators and Matrices 3
(2009), 125–143, arXiv:0804.1387v1,
https://arxiv.org/html/0804.1387v1 .

Bibliographic correction: the frozen INITIAL called this Hadwin–Shulman. The
primary paper's title page names Don Hadwin and Weihua Li. Preserve that
INITIAL as frozen; this revision records and uses the corrected attribution.

Theorem 4.2 states that for every fixed finite n, p in [1,infinity), and
epsilon>0, there is delta>0 independent of the tracial C*-algebra such that
an n-tuple of contractions with small normality defects and pairwise
commutators is close, in the sum of p-norm errors, to a commuting normal
n-tuple. The proof constructs one tracial ultraproduct of a sequence of
counterexample algebras and uses a fixed finite tuple there. This justifies
dimension-free fixed-n stability; it does not establish a delta uniform as
n grows with matrix dimension. For Hermitian inputs the normality defects
are zero. The theorem is not CP-map stability, but the pinching corollary
below converts its fixed-rank conclusion into an EB map.

Theorem 4.1 says a tracial ultraproduct of tracial C*-algebras is a von
Neumann algebra. This is structural closure, not a coordinate lift.

Theorem 4.4 assumes every coordinate M_i is a diffuse von Neumann algebra
with faithful trace, and A is a commutative countably generated von Neumann
subalgebra of the tracial ultraproduct. It constructs trace-preserving
*-homomorphisms pi_i:A->M_i representing every a in A. Corollary 4.5 gives
the analogous statement for a commutative countably generated unital
C*-algebra. The source proof uses exact coordinate projections of every
trace, available from diffuseness. Finite matrix coordinate algebras M_d
are not diffuse. Moreover, the map in the broadcasting gate is UCP, not a
*-homomorphism; approximate commutation of its range does not turn that map
into a homomorphism. The theorem therefore does not directly supply an EB
map with a uniform full-ball channel error. The entire image-generated
abelian algebra can also have no uniform finite-generator bound as d grows.

3. Hadwin–Shulman, “Tracial Stability for C*-Algebras,” arXiv:1607.04470v2,
https://arxiv.org/html/1607.04470v2 .

This paper formulates tracial stability in terms of lifting unital
*-homomorphisms from a fixed separable C*-algebra into tracial ultraproducts,
equivalently stability of a fixed finite set of algebraic relations in its
stated settings. Its finite-generator and trace-approximation hypotheses
are not a theorem about a sequence of UCP channel maps with growing
operator-ball image, nor about EB approximation in the induced
infinity-to-one norm. It is relevant conceptual background, but does not
close the present interface.

4. Raynaud, “On ultrapowers of non commutative L_p spaces,” J. Operator
Theory 48 (2002), 41–68,
https://jot.theta.ro/jot/archive/2002-048-001/2002-048-001-003.html .

The journal abstract identifies an isometric identification of ultrapowers
of Haagerup L_p spaces with the associated L_p space of the von Neumann
ultraproduct. This is an L_p-space identification. No lifting of CP maps,
EB maps, or simultaneous channel approximants is stated there.

FIXED-RANGE-RANK EB CONSEQUENCE (DERIVED HERE)

Let (A,tau) be finite-dimensional with faithful normalized trace, and let
T:A->A be UCP and tau-preserving. Let R be the real vector space
T(A_sa), with real dimension r. Suppose the entire Hermitian unit-ball
image has pairwise normalized-L2 commutator at most delta.

Choose a real basis X_1,...,X_r from the compact image
S=T({H=H*, ||H||<=1}) that maximizes absolute determinant in any fixed
coordinates on R. Write each X in S as X=sum_j c_j X_j. Replacing the jth
basis column by X gives determinant c_j times the maximum determinant;
maximality implies |c_j|<=1 for every j. This is the only coefficient
bound used below.

Apply Hadwin–Li Theorem 4.2 with p=2 to X_1,...,X_r. For any eta>0, its
fixed-r theorem supplies delta_HL(r,eta)>0, independent of A and its
dimension, such that if delta<delta_HL(r,eta), there are commuting normal
contractions Z_j with sum_j ||X_j-Z_j||_2<eta. Put
Y_j=(Z_j+Z_j*)/2. A commuting family of normal operators has commuting
real parts; Y_j are selfadjoint contractions and
||X_j-Y_j||_2<=||X_j-Z_j||_2. Let D=C*(Y_1,...,Y_r), an abelian
subalgebra, and let E_D be its tau-preserving conditional expectation.

For X=sum_j c_jX_j in S, since E_D(Y_j)=Y_j and |c_j|<=1,

  ||X-E_D(X)||_1
    <= sum_j |c_j| ||(I-E_D)(X_j-Y_j)||_1
    <= 2 sum_j |c_j| ||X_j-Y_j||_2
    <= 2 sum_j ||X_j-Y_j||_2 < 2 eta.

Here E_D is contractive on L1, so ||I-E_D||_(1->1)<=2, and normalized
L1<=L2. If Q_z are the minimal projections of D, then

  E_D T(A)=sum_z tau(T^*(Q_z)A) Q_z/tau(Q_z),

where T^* is the tau-adjoint. The effects T^*(Q_z) are positive and sum to
I, and the preparations Q_z/tau(Q_z) are trace-one positive. Thus E_D T is
one full-domain measure-and-prepare (EB), UCP, tau-preserving map and

  ||T-E_D T||_(infinity->1, Hermitian) < 2 eta.

The output pinching preserves the original CP map's domain dependence and
trace exactly. For fixed r this proves a dimension-independent qualitative map-level modulus
under full-image commutator control. The threshold is the fixed-r
Hadwin–Li delta; no effective formula is supplied by its compactness proof.

For M_d and r>=2, Bansil–Kachkovskiy Corollary 1.4 also gives an explicit
but rank-dependent version. If its tuple bound is
sum_j ||X_j-Y_j||_(2,d)^2 <= C(r) delta^(2/3^(r-1)), then the same argument
and Cauchy–Schwarz yield

  ||T-E_D T||_(infinity->1,Hermitian)
    <= 2 sqrt(r C(r)) delta^(1/3^(r-1)).

This is useful for fixed r, and becomes nonuniform as r grows. For a general
map r can be as large as the real dimension of A_sa (d^2 for M_d); no
dimension-independent conclusion follows from these fixed-tuple theorems.

WHY THE FULL CP IMAGE REMAINS A GAP

The exact power-channel input is a commutator estimate for every pair of
outputs, with the supremum taken over the entire operator-norm input ball.
Pair stability gives a potentially different abelian algebra for each
pair. A basis-and-net conversion needs a number of outputs or a coefficient
profile controlled uniformly in dimension. The fixed-tuple theorems provide
no such control when range dimension grows. Hadwin–Li's abelian lift requires
a *-homomorphism, countable generation, diffuse coordinate algebras, and
pointwise equality in a tracial ultraproduct; it is not a lift of the
original channel map. Pointwise tracial convergence by itself is not the
uniform supremum over all operator-norm inputs in the desired map metric.

STATUS / NEXT GATE

Primary source scope confirms a fixed-rank EB lift, not a dimension-free lift
for arbitrary growing CP ranges. The load-bearing missing statement remains:
from uniform normalized-L2 pairwise commutator smallness on the full CP
image of the operator ball, derive one EB channel whose
normalized-infinity-to-one error tends to zero uniformly in dimension and
trace; or exhibit a qualifying selfcompatible channel sequence for which
that distance stays positive. No such theorem or qualifying counterexample
was found in this audit. This is bounded primary-source coverage, not a
novelty certification or a proof of nonexistence.
