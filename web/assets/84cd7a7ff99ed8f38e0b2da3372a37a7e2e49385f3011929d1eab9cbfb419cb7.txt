## Ambient state space and exact class classification

Write the nonnegative integer slice of the closed affine compatibility class as

\[
E=\{(a,b,c,d)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4:b+c=2\}.
\]

The deterministic theorem concerns the positive part
\(E_{\mathbb R}^{+}=\{(A,B,C,D)\in\mathbb R_{>0}^4:B+C=2\}\). The CTMC is
defined on \(E\), the lattice points in its nonnegative closure; it can and
does hit boundary states with \(B=0\) or \(C=0\). Thus “same compatibility
class” means the same stoichiometric affine class/its closure, not that the
stochastic chain stays inside the strictly positive deterministic set.

Every state of \(E\) has exactly one of three phases:

* \((b,c)=(0,2)\), with \(N=a\);
* \((b,c)=(1,1)\), with \(N=a-1\);
* \((b,c)=(2,0)\), with \(N=a-3\),

where \(N(a,b)=a-b(b+1)/2\). On \(E\), the first core reaction is enabled
only in phase 0 (its rate is \((c)_2=2\)); the second is enabled only in
phase 1 with \(a\ge1\) (rate \(abc=a\)); and the third is enabled only in
phase 2 with \(a\ge3\) (rate \((a)_3(b)_2\)). When enabled, each advances to
the next phase and preserves \(N\). The two D reactions are enabled only in
phase 0, where their rates are 2 and \(2d\). These conditions classify every
state, including all boundary cases:

\[
E=\Bigl(\bigsqcup_{n\ge0}\Gamma_n\Bigr)
\sqcup\{(0,1,1,d):d\ge0\}
\sqcup\{(a,2,0,d):a=0,1,2,\ d\ge0\}.
\]

The last four forms (one phase-1 state and three phase-2 states, for each d)
are singleton absorbing classes: every core reaction is disabled, and the D
reactions require two C molecules. For \(n\ge0\), the three states in phases
0, 1, 2 with \(N=n\) are exactly

\[
\Gamma_n=\{(n,0,2,d),(n+1,1,1,d),(n+3,2,0,d):d\ge0\}.
\]

All three cycle rates are positive for every \(n\ge0\). From any phase one
can reach phase 0, change d in either direction as needed, and continue around
the cycle. Therefore \(\Gamma_n\) is closed and irreducible. The displayed
union is disjoint and exhaustive, not just a list of convenient subfamilies.

For completeness, the cycle propensities at these states are \(2,n+1,2P_n\),
where \(P_n=(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)\). The factorial weight
\(1/(a!b!c!d!)\) restricted to \(\Gamma_n\), after removal of the common
\(1/n!\), has phase factors \(1/2,1/(n+1),1/(2P_n)\) and D factor \(1/d!\).
The three cycle fluxes agree exactly, and phase-0 D birth/death fluxes agree
because \(2/d!=2(d+1)/(d+1)!\). The total weight is finite. The chain is
nonexplosive: within a fixed \(\Gamma_n\), cycle rates are bounded and D births
are bounded by a rate-2 Poisson process; deaths cannot exceed the initial D
count plus births. Hence the normalized law is the unique stationary law on
\(\Gamma_n\), and \(\Gamma_n\) is positive recurrent.

With \(Z_n=P_n+2(n+2)(n+3)+1=n^3+8n^2+21n+19\), the phase masses are

\[
p_0(n)=P_n/Z_n,\quad p_1(n)=2(n+2)(n+3)/Z_n,\quad p_2(n)=1/Z_n,
\]

and \(D\sim\mathrm{Pois}(1)\) independently. The polynomial identities
\(Z_n-P_n=2n^2+10n+13\) and \(Z_n\sim n^3\) give

\[
p_0(n)\to1,\quad p_1(n)\sim2/n\to0,\quad p_2(n)\sim n^{-3}\to0.
\]

Thus \(\pi_n(B=0)=p_0(n)\to1\) and \(\pi_n(BC=0)=1-p_1(n)\to1\). Since
\(A\ge n\) on \(\Gamma_n\), for any compact \(K\subset\mathbb R_{\ge0}^4\)
with \(A\le M_K\) on K, \(\pi_n(K)=0\) whenever \(n>M_K\). This is the
strong form of the surviving counterexample: the sequence of component
stationary laws is not tight, although every member is individually tight
and positive recurrent.

The strict-positive lattice mass is another exact boundary diagnostic. The
only states in \(\Gamma_n\) with A,B,C,D all positive are phase 1 with D>0,
so

\[
\pi_n(A,B,C,D>0)=p_1(n)(1-e^{-1})\longrightarrow0.
\]

Each class nevertheless contains such states, including \((n+1,1,1,1)\). This
is a uniform stationary-boundary failure over components; it is not a claim
that any one positive recurrent component lacks positive states.

## Surviving and falsified statements

**Falsified:** deterministic global attraction to one positive equilibrium in
one positive stoichiometric class does not imply uniform tightness over all
closed stochastic components in the nonnegative lattice slice, nor a uniform
positive stationary-mass lower bound for the strict-positive lattice states
of those components.

**Survives:** each fixed \(\Gamma_n\) is positive recurrent with one tight
stationary law; all components with n in a fixed finite set have a uniform
tail bound (only finitely many A/B/C values occur and D is always Poisson(1));
the deterministic ODE converges to \((1,1,1,1)\) from every positive initial
condition; and the component family is not uniformly tight. No recurrence
failure, explosion, or failure of existence of a stationary law has been
proved.

There is also a tight stationary law on all of E: the product-Poisson law
\(\mathrm{Pois}(1)^{\otimes4}\) conditioned on \(B+C=2\). The conditioning
event is invariant because \(B+C\) is conserved, so this is a stationary
mixture over the closed components above. Its exact mass on \(\Gamma_n\) is

\[
q_n=\frac{e^{-1}}{2n!}\left(\frac12+\frac1{n+1}+\frac1{2P_n}\right),
\]

obtained by summing \(e^{-2}/(2a!b!c!d!)\) over the three phases and all
\(d\). In particular, the global mixture suppresses large-n components
with a factorial tail even though their component-conditioned laws escape.
More generally, every fixed probability mixture of the component stationary
laws and absorbing point masses is a probability on a countable lattice and
hence is tight. The counterexample is exactly a failure of *uniformity over
the set of component laws*, not a failure of tightness of each law or of every
chosen mixture.

## Strongest remaining mathematical target and limits

The useful statement is a quantifier obstruction at the bridge from a
deterministic compatibility class to stochastic components. Linear
conservation fixes \(B+C\) but says nothing about the nonlinear integer
invariant N; in the integer slice the enabled reactions alternate by phase,
while in the deterministic relative interior all three core rates are
strictly positive at once and N has nonzero derivative. The example therefore
cannot turn N into a deterministic first integral or a general stochastic
invariant.
