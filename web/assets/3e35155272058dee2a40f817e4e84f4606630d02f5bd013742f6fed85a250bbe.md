# Independent audit of the dimension-uniform Gaussian scalar upper

7 October 2026. Audited `../sparse_gaussian_upper/report.md`, principally
Sections 2-4 and 6, after the scalar channel became a premise for the weak
ball and severe-axis archive laws. Mathematical arguments were reconstructed;
finite diagnostics were not used as proof premises.

## Verdict

**PASS for the ideal stochastic-kernel theorem**

    Q_0=P_0,
    chi^2(Q_t||P_t)<=K t^2/J^4,  |t|<=1,

with an absolute finite K. The drift, endpoints, tail denominator, and
reversibility steps survive inspection. The proof uses x-linear hats on a
Cauchy-spaced grid, which differs from my earlier Cauchy-coordinate hat proof.
There is no hidden dimension or support-size dependence in K.

**PASS for finite-encoder reference preservation and its mathematical
stability bound**, provided the decoder is rebuilt from the actual finite
partition A_j. Its quantization precision is O(log J), and the quoted
stability calculation has the correct powers and exponential factor.

**The bit-complexity assertion is an execution outline**, rather than a
supplied implemented bitstream compiler. Two initially ambiguous conventions
were corrected during this audit: buffered finite-precision node selection
and expanded supports in rejection proposals. I inspected the revised
definitions and found both repairs valid. I have not independently implemented
the computable-real sampler or verified its complete expected-bit complexity.

This is internal mathematical review, not formal verification, historical
novelty clearance, or external certification. The companion sparse converse
is outside this audit; matching leading constants require it separately.

## 1. Mesh and signed posterior drift

Write delta=pi/J and tau=tan(delta). Tangent addition gives, exactly,

    h_+=tau(1+z^2)/(1-z tau),
    h_-=tau(1+z^2)/(1+z tau),
    h_+-h_-=2z tau^2(1+z^2)/(1-z^2 tau^2).

The difference is genuinely second order. It must not be replaced by either
width. The flat asymmetric tent centroid is

    integral s a(s)ds / integral a(s)ds=(h_+-h_-)/3.

For Gaussian weighting v(s)=exp(-zs-s^2/2), the mesh condition gives
e^-1<=v<=e. With H=max(h_-,h_+),

    |Cov(s,v)|<=sup|v'| Var(s)<=e(|z|+H)H^2.

Dividing by E v>=e^-1 yields the report's weighting drift. Combining it
with mesh asymmetry proves

    |E_Gamma_z(X-z)|<=C J^-2 (1+|z|)^5.

The displayed value of C_1 is a valid conservative choice. Its first term
bounds |z|(1+z^2); its second bounds (|z|+1)(1+z^2)^2.

The global mesh condition is also valid. With u=log J,
S=8+4sqrt(u), and S'=2/sqrt(u),

    d/du log[S(1+S^2)]<=3S'/S=6/(S sqrt(u))<1

for u>=16 log 2. Both summands of the left side of (10) decrease thereafter;
at J=2^16 their sum is less than one. The support span of an interior tent is
2H<1 in this range. This latter fact follows from (10) and S>=20, not merely
from the weaker printed H<1.

All nodes needed when |x|<=T are interior: B>S-1 and the endpoint support
starts at L>B-1>S-2=T. Nodal expectation errors and ordinary interpolation
errors therefore both apply on the central region. Transferring the nodal
bound to x costs only absolute factors, since neighboring nodes are within
one of x. The resulting pointwise estimate

    |Tf_t(x)-f_t(x)|<=C |t| J^-2 e^|x| (1+|x|)^5

has no supremum-cutoff factor. Its polynomial remains inside the final
Gaussian integral. This is why no logarithmic degradation enters the rate.

## 2. Exterior rays and the chi-square direction

For the positive endpoint with support [L,infinity), B-L<=1/B. Its
normalizer is at least Q(B), whereas its exponential numerator is at most
exp(1/2)Q(L-1). Mills inequalities give

    E_Gamma_+ e^Y
      <=[(1+B^2)/(B(L-1))]
         exp(1/2+(B^2-(L-1)^2)/2).

The exponent equals B+B(B-L)-(B-L)-(B-L)^2/2, and is at most B+1.
The prefactor is bounded by four because L-1>=B/2. At any point where this
hat is active, B<=x+1. Thus the endpoint expectation is <=4e^2 e^x. Reflection
handles the negative endpoint; finite interior hats are even easier. This
proves T(e^|.|)(x)<=C e^|x| for the actual unbounded-support channel.

Any hat whose decoder can enter |y|>T has its entire encoder support within
|x|>T-1. The tent span is below one; the exterior-ray supports begin beyond
T. Therefore the localized exponential-moment statement (20) is valid.

Positive Jensen followed by self-adjointness yields

    integral_(|y|>T) phi(y)e^|y| [Tf_t(y)]^2 dy
      <=integral phi(x) f_t(x)^2
                     T(e^|.| 1_(|.|>T))(x)dx.

This is the correct weighted direction. Since
|f_t(x)|<=|t|(1+|x|)e^|x|, its bound is an explicit Gaussian tail with
weight (1+|x|)^2 e^(3|x|). Completing the square gives exactly

    integral_u^infinity (1+x)^2 e^(3x) phi(x)dx
      =e^(9/2)[(u+5)phi(u-3)+17Q(u-3)].

For u=T-1=5+4sqrt(log J), its exponential factor is <=J^-8; the remaining
polynomial is harmless. This proves the required O(t^2 J^-4) tail bound.

Finally, the squared error is relative to the SHIFTED law:

    chi^2(Q_t||P_t)=integral phi(x)[Tf_t(x)-f_t(x)]^2/L_t(x)dx,
    1/L_t(x)<=exp(|x|+1/2).

The central polynomial times exp(3|x|) is integrable against phi. The tails
have just been controlled with the same weight. No denominator is silently
dropped or replaced by the null denominator. For J<2^16,
chi^2(P_0||P_t)=exp(t^2)-1<=(e-1)t^2, so the universal constant can absorb
these finitely many J. K is very conservative, but independent of dimension.

## 3. Rational nodes and finite-encoder stability

Rational perturbations rho<=J^-5 are much smaller than every mesh width,
whose minimum is of order J^-1. They add at most 4rho to adjacent-width
asymmetry, and O(rho) to support endpoints. The extra drift is dominated by
the original J^-2 estimates. Gaussian oscillation and endpoint moment bounds
survive after increasing absolute constants.

The finite-prefix partition has sup_x sum_j|A_j(x)-a_j(x)|<=C J2^-b,
and the difference vanishes outside a known bounded interval. Exact null
preservation follows algebraically from decoding with phi A_j/alpha_j^A.
It would fail if the original decoder were reused after rounding the encoder.

For any bounded f, put F=||f||_infinity and
eta=integral phi sum_j|A_j-a_j|. Write the signed output as
sum_j Gamma_j b_j, where b_j=integral phi a_j f. The exact normalization
identity is

    alpha_j(Gamma_j^A-Gamma_j)
      =phi(A_j-a_j)+(alpha_j-alpha_j^A)Gamma_j^A.

Its weighted total variation, together with |b_j|<=F alpha_j, proves

    integral phi e^|y| |T_Af-T_af|dy<=C F e^(S+2) eta.

No inverse rare-label mass appears. Since |T_Af-T_af|<=2F, the weighted
squared-error discrepancy is <=C F^2 e^(S+2)eta.

Truncate f_t only for this estimate at |x|<=S+2, obtaining
F<=|t|(S+3)e^(S+2). The complementary f_t tails satisfy O(t^2 J^-4) weighted
L2, both directly and after either reversible operator. Hence the stability
term is at most

    C t^2 (S+3)^2 e^(3(S+2)) J2^-b.

Equation (30) makes it O(t^2 J^-4), exactly as asserted. This establishes
the finite-encoder version mathematically. Because S=O(sqrt(log J)), the
required b is O(log J).

## 4. Two execution clarifications

The following issues were sent to the author and have now been incorporated
in the source report. The updated report specifies indices 1,...,J-1,
dyadic nodes tested against rational s in [S-1/4,S-1/8], a floor-prefix
encoder with right probability rounded down, and expanded proposal supports.
The revised cutoff still leaves B>S-1 and L>T because the largest adjacent
width at J>=2^16 is below 1/20. The proposal intervals expanded by 2^(1-b)
contain the actual finite-function supports, including the negative ray.
These corrected definitions remove both implementation ambiguities.

1. **Finite node selection.** Specify j=1,...,J-1. Computing a kept-node table
   by the exact comparison z_j<=S_J does not by itself prove the claimed
   J poly(log J) bit bound: nearly equal computable reals can require high
   comparison precision. Use a buffered finite-precision cutoff instead.
   For example approximate the upper Cauchy cutoff index to error below 1/4,
   choose a deterministic nearby integer, retain its symmetric nodes, and
   increase the known cutoff by a few mesh widths if necessary. Every relevant
   endpoint moves by O((1+S^2)/J)<1, and the two-unit T-to-S gap leaves ample
   room. This avoids a separation theorem for the original transcendental
   cutoff comparison and preserves all estimates with an absolute margin.
2. **Use actual finite supports in proposals.** A fixed-point floor readout
   may move a negative endpoint's support to the right of its ideal endpoint;
   nearest rounding can expand either endpoint support. A rejection proposal
   restricted to the original ideal ray would then miss positive A_j mass.
   Define L_A (or the negative reflected boundary) from the actual finite
   function A_j, and propose on its entire ray. The endpoints differ by
   O(2^-b), so the same Mills bound gives constant acceptance. Alternatively
   specify symmetric truncation/readout conventions that make the proposed
   rays contain the complete actual supports. This is a sampler convention,
   not a mathematical obstruction to exact reference preservation.

For an interior label, a uniform proposal on its actual support followed by
exp[-(y^2-m^2)/2]A_j(y) has the correct conditional density. Gaussian
oscillation is bounded and the stepwise hat has average near one half, so
acceptance is uniformly positive. For a positive ray starting at L_A, an
exponential proposal L_A+E/L_A accepted with exp[-(Y-L_A)^2/2]A_j(Y) is also
exact: multiplying its proposal density by the acceptance probability gives
a constant times phi(Y)A_j(Y). Its acceptance is L_A alpha_j^A/phi(L_A),
bounded below by the corrected endpoint Mills estimate.

The report's polynomial expected output-prefix cost is plausible in the
declared computable-real/random-bit model. Evaluating A_j(Y) requires resolving
only finitely many dyadic boundaries, and an independent uniform acceptance
threshold makes comparisons terminate almost surely. But a full complexity
verification should bound arithmetic precision, support-boundary ambiguity,
and unbounded exponential proposals explicitly. I did not audit a supplied
implementation because none is supplied. The ideal kernel theorem and finite
encoder stability do not depend on that additional compiler claim.

## 5. Consequences this audit supports

KL(Q||P)<=chi^2(Q||P), product KL additivity, and Pinsker imply

    TV(product_i Q_theta_i, N(theta,I_d))
      <=sqrt(K/2) ||theta||_2/J^2.

Exact null reconstruction prevents inactive-coordinate error accumulation.
Thus the growing-dimension weak-ball upper has an absolute O(d) additive
bit cost. No sparse-support factor is introduced.

For known weak axes a_i<=1, choose J_i^2 proportional to a_i/epsilon on kept
axes; use the exact null decoder on omitted axes. On the ellipsoid
sum(theta_i/a_i)^2<=1, the total KL is bounded by a constant times epsilon^2,
independently of the number of axes. Exponential axes then give the reported
leading upper coefficient by summing log(a_i/epsilon). This audit certifies
that upper mechanism; the companion lower proof remains a separate premise
for equality of leading coefficients.

The finite-prefix decoder outputs a procedural random real, not a completed
floating-point number. A finite/countable digital output law has TV one
against a continuous Gaussian. Prefix readout or procedural output must
therefore remain explicit. Retained input-dependent temporary precision
would count toward the archive if it were kept. Grid-table setup bits are
input-independent resources and should still be reported separately from
the statistical memory dimension.
