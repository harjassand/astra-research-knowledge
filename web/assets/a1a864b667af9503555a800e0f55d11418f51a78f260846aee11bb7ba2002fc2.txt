SU(3) WALL WEIGHTED COUNT: HEAT-TRACE REDUCTION TO A SHIFTED
MISSING-LABEL SPECTRUM
Date: 2026-10-08
Status: exact operator and heat-trace reduction; the uniform shifted
tridiagonal-eigenvalue estimate needed to prove the count remains open.
No weighted-count counterexample is produced.

1. TARGET AND SOURCE NORMALIZATION

Let a>=b>=1, N=a+b, B=b+1, lambda=(a,b), and supply V_lambda.
Use Hermitian generators with Tr_fund(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2 and

  C2=(a^2+ab+b^2+3a+3b)/3,
  C3=(a-b)(2a+b+3)(a+2b+3)/18,
  alpha=C3/C2,
  S_A=(D_A-alpha T_A)/N,
  C_S=Delta/N^2,
  Delta=C2(C2/3+1/4)-C3^2/C2.

On End(V_lambda), define L_T=sum_A ad(T_A)^2,
L_S=sum_A ad(S_A)^2, and

  L_w=L_T/N^2+L_S/(N sqrt(C_S)).

The desired estimate, with the scalar mode removed, is

  D_w(t)=Tr 1_(0,t](L_w)
      <= C d[(N^2/B)t^2+N^3 t^3], 0<t<=c0/N,            (1.1)

where d=(a+1)B(N+2)/2 and C,c0 are independent of a,b,N.
Writing T=N^2t, the same target is

  D_w(T/N^2)<=C[T^2+(B/N)T^3], 0<T<=c0N.                (1.2)

The exact identity

  C_S/[b(b+2)] = a(a+2)(N+1)(N+3)/(12 C2 N^2)            (1.3)

implies sqrt(C_S) comparable to B uniformly for a>=b>=1, and the
factor in (1.3) tends to 1/4 when b/N->0. For a crude explicit
uniform comparison, N>=2 and a>=N/2 give
  1/64 <= a(a+2)(N+1)(N+3)/(12C2N^2) <= 5/2;
also (sqrt(3)/2)B<=sqrt(b(b+2))<=B. Thus absolute comparison
constants in sqrt(C_S)~B are available on the full stated carrier
range, not only asymptotically.

2. BLOCKWISE HEAT-TRACE FORMULA

Decompose the diagonal SU(3) representation

  End(V_lambda)=direct_sum_nu V_nu tensor M_nu.

Then L_T acts by c_nu=c2(nu) on V_nu and L_S=I_(V_nu) tensor K_nu
for a positive matrix K_nu on M_nu. The two invariant operators
commute. Thus, for s>0, the exact heat trace is

  H_w(s)=Tr e^(-sL_w)
        =1+sum_(nu!=0) dim(V_nu)e^(-s c_nu/N^2)
                            Tr_(M_nu)e^(-s K_nu/(N sqrt(C_S))). (2.1)

The summand 1 is the scalar mode. The elementary heat-to-count bound
is

  D_w(t)<= e[H_w(1/t)-1].                                 (2.2)

So a sufficient heat estimate, with no Tauberian regularity issue, is

  H_w(s)-1 <= C d[(N^2/B)s^(-2)+N^3 s^(-3)],
                         s>=N/c0.                         (2.3)

Indeed, each eigenvalue <=t contributes at least e^(-1) to H_w(1/t).

3. A MULTIPLICITY-SAFE CUBIC LOWER BOUND

Put M=sum_A ad(T_A)ad(S_A). Same-index covariance gives
[T_A,S_A]=0, so M is self-adjoint; it is SU(3)-invariant and preserves
each full c_nu-isotypic block, including its multiplicity coordinates.
On a block with c_nu>0,

  L_S >= M^2/c_nu.                                        (3.1)

Proof: for psi in that block, project ad(S_A)psi back to the block
before applying ad(T_A); the latter preserves the block and has row
operator norm sqrt(c_nu), since sum_A ad(T_A)^2=c_nu I there. Hence

  ||M psi||^2 <= c_nu sum_A||P_nu ad(S_A)psi||^2
               <= c_nu <psi,L_S psi>.

This is a quadratic-form comparison and makes no assumption that M,
L_S, or an individual multiplicity coordinate is diagonal in the same
basis.

There is an exact expression on V_lambda tensor V_lambda^*. Let X_A
act by T_A on the first factor, let Y_A act by the dual generators on
the second factor, and K_A=X_A+Y_A be the diagonal generator. Let
D_X,A=d_ABC X_BX_C and D_Y,A=d_ABC Y_BY_C. Then

  M=[2C3+(A3-B3)-alpha L_T]/N,                            (3.2)
  A3=d_ABC Y_A X_B X_C,  B3=d_ABC X_A Y_B Y_C.

To verify (3.2), write the ad(S_A) action as
(D_X,A-D_Y,A-alpha K_A)/N and expand K_A(D_X,A-D_Y,A).
The pure cubic terms are dX^3=C3 and dY^3=-C3; the remaining two
terms are A3-B3. This establishes the claimed blockwise scalar/cubic
operator with the stated Tr_fund normalization.

4. IDENTIFICATION WITH THE SU(3) DIAGONAL-CENTRALIZER MISSING LABEL

The mixed cubic in (3.2) can be matched exactly to the standard
diagonal-centralizer generator. Use complex matrix units e_ij in the
two tensor factors and the polarized traces of Crampé, Poulain
d'Andecy, and Vinet, where

  T^(a1,...,ak)=e_(i2 i1)^(a1)e_(i3 i2)^(a2)...e_(i1 ik)^(ak).

Their missing-label generator is

  X_miss=1/2(T^(1,1,2)-T^(1,2,2))+1/3(l_1-l_2).          (4.1)

In the present normalization the source's quadratic and cubic
central values are k=2C2 and l=2C3. On the two factors lambda and
lambda*, l_1=2C3 and l_2=-2C3. The defining-basis Fierz identity
Tr(t_A t_B)=delta_AB/2 gives

  T^(1,1,2)=2A3-3 X_A Y_A,
  T^(1,2,2)=2B3-3 X_A Y_A.

For example, this follows by expanding e_ij=2(t_A)_(ji)t_A and using
Tr(t_A t_B t_C)=(d_ABC+i f_ABC)/4; the f-contraction is
f_ABC X_A X_B=(3i/2)X_C. The common quadratic term cancels in the
difference. Therefore

  X_miss=A3-B3+4C3/3,
  M|_(M_nu)= [X_miss,nu + 2C3/3-alpha c_nu]/N.            (4.2)

This is an exact affine relation, not an asymptotic replacement. The
dual-factor orientation matters for the sign of l_2 and of the cubic
shift.

For nu=(p,q), the tensor-product parameters in the source are

  (m1,m2)=(a+1,b+1),
  (m1',m2')=(b+1,a+1),
  (m1'',m2'')=(p+1,q+1).

The source's tridiagonal theorem (§1.3, Eqs. (17)-(18)) gives an
explicit tridiagonal matrix for X_miss on M_nu. Its dimension is the
Littlewood-Richardson multiplicity m_nu. In particular, define

  ell=N+1-(p+2q)/3,
  n  =N+1-(2p+q)/3.

For precision, the source defines, when ell<=n,
  (xi_1,xi_2,xi_3)=(ell,m2,m1'+ell-n),
and, when n<=ell,
  (xi_1,xi_2,xi_3)=(n,m1',m2+n-ell),
with (xi_4,xi_5,xi_6)=(ell-m2',n-m1,0). Put
xi_a=min(xi_1,xi_2,xi_3), xi_b=max(xi_4,xi_5,xi_6),
Lambda=sum_(i=1)^3 xi_i-sum_(i=4)^6 xi_i+2|ell-n|, and
d_m=xi_a-xi_b=m_nu. In the stated tridiagonal basis, the off-diagonal
entries are

  (X_miss)_(j,j+1)=(j+xi_b-xi_1)(j+xi_b-xi_2)(j+xi_b-xi_3),
  (X_miss)_(j+1,j)=(j+xi_b-xi_4)(j+xi_b-xi_5)(j+xi_b-xi_6).

The diagonal entries use E_(j,k)=sum_(i=1)^6
(xi_i-j-xi_b+1/2)^k and are

  (X_miss)_(j,j)=-[(7/2)E_(j,1)^3-18E_(j,1)E_(j,2)+18E_(j,3)]/108
                 -E_(j,1)(Lambda^2+2)/24.

For n<=ell, the source uses the parameter-exchanged basis described
there. The theorem states that the matrix is diagonalizable with
distinct eigenvalues, while the authors explicitly note that no exact
general eigenvalue formula is known. Source:
N. Crampé, L. Poulain d'Andecy, L. Vinet, “The missing label of
su(3) and its symmetry,” arXiv:2110.03521v1, §1.3 and Appendix A,
https://arxiv.org/html/2110.03521.

Normalization check: for lambda=(3,1), nu=(1,1), the source parameters
give ell=n=4, (xi_1,...,xi_6)=(4,2,2,0,0,0), and Lambda=8. The two
diagonal entries from the displayed E-formula are -617/54 and -23/54,
so Tr X_miss=-320/27. Independently, (4.2) gives
Tr X_miss=N Tr M-2 sigma_nu=-320/27. This checks the shift and the
source-to-Hermitian normalization in a non-self-dual carrier.

5. EXACT REDUCTION OF THE HEAT GATE

Let x_(nu,r) range over the real spectrum of X_miss,nu and put

  sigma_nu=2C3/3-alpha c_nu.

From (3.1)-(4.2) and the eigenvalue min-max principle, each block heat
trace is bounded by the heat trace of the scalar shifted cubic
operator. Thus the exact inequality is

  H_w(s)-1 <= sum_(nu!=0) dim(V_nu)e^(-s c_nu/N^2)
       sum_r exp[-s(x_(nu,r)+sigma_nu)^2/
                         (c_nu N^3 sqrt(C_S))].           (5.1)

This uses only the exact weighted L_w and the proved comparison (3.1).
It is valid for every a>=b>=1 and every s>0. In the target window
s>=N/c0, a sufficient remaining scalar estimate is

  sum_(nu!=0) dim(V_nu)e^(-s c_nu/N^2)
       sum_r exp[-s(x_(nu,r)+sigma_nu)^2/
                         (c_nu N^3 sqrt(C_S))]
   <= C d[(N^2/B)s^(-2)+N^3s^(-3)].                       (5.2)

Equation (5.2) is an exact heat-kernel version of the missing weighted
spectral profile. It reduces the difficult matrix K_nu problem to the
sublevel/heat distribution of one explicit tridiagonal missing-label
operator per output irrep, with its carrier-dependent shift sigma_nu.
No direct diagonalization of L_w or CPV spectral ansatz is used.

6. WHAT THE REDUCTION DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

The comparison (3.1) is rigorous and multiplicity-safe, but may be
strict. To prove (5.2), one needs a uniform estimate on how many
eigenvalues x_(nu,r) lie within a window of -sigma_nu, with width
about

  sqrt(c_nu N^3 sqrt(C_S)/s).

For near-wall carriers sqrt(C_S)~B/2, while the Casimir heat factor
localizes c_nu at scale N^2/s. The required estimate must therefore
simultaneously capture the output Casimir variable and the shifted
missing-label spectrum. A global Weyl-dimension estimate or the
unshifted count of x_(nu,r) is insufficient: the shift depends on
both C3(lambda) and c_nu. The source's tridiagonal formulas make the
finite matrix exact but do not supply the needed uniform near-root
count; their paper specifically says general closed eigenvalue
formulas are unavailable.

As a consistency check, the exact adjoint block has one mode of
weighted eigenvalue (3+o(1))/N^2 and a second of order 1/(NB) when
b/N->0. A shifted-cubic heat estimate must retain both modes; a
uniform ellipticity replacement L_S>=cL_T would incorrectly discard
the first. The adjoint block is finite-dimensional and cannot decide
(5.2).

The known ordinary-Casimir shortcut is rigorously false: for
b=floor(N^(1/4)) and T~b^2, the exact stable zero-weight count has
D_T(T)>=cT^3, while T^2+(B/N)T^3=o(T^3). This refutes only the attempt
to drop L_S from the heat/count estimate. No weighted counterexample
sequence is obtained here, and (5.2), hence (1.1), remains unresolved.
