Claim: Let A be a complete synchronizing deterministic finite automaton on m>=2 states with at least two letters a,b. The source constructs B by adjoining m-1 proxy states p_i and retaining every original transition, with p_i a=q_i a and p_i b=q_{i+1}b; every further letter maps p_i into the original image Qc. Every nonempty word has exactly the same image from B as from A, so their nonempty reset languages and reset thresholds agree. The pair-specific unit merges form a spanning path, forcing the pair-distance MST weight K(B)=|Q_B|-1=2m-2. Applied to the classical m-state Cerny family, |Q_B|=2m-1 and rt(B)=(m-1)^2=(|Q_B|-1)^2/4, so rt(B)/K(B)=(|Q_B|-1)/4 and no universal constant C gives rt<=C K (even with a fixed additive constant).
Interface: Complete deterministic automata; at least two alphabet letters; the core automaton is synchronizing. Pair distance is the shortest word merging a specified pair; K is the minimum spanning-tree weight using these distances. The construction is not strongly connected: the original states form a closed subautomaton.
Proof spine: For every letter c, the new states map into Qc while original states still have image Qc; induction on a nonempty word gives Q_B w=Q_A w. The pairs {q_i,p_i} merge under a and {p_i,q_{i+1}} under b, giving a unit-weight spanning path. Since every edge weight is at least one, this attains the |Q_B|-1 MST lower bound. The classical Cerny threshold then yields the unbounded ratio.
Costs: The extension is written with O(m times alphabet-size) transition assignments. Pair distances are polynomial-time computable by reverse search on unordered pairs. This does not provide a short-reset algorithm or improve general reset-word upper bounds; exact subset BFS appears only as a finite check in the source.
Failure: This refutes the specific proposal that a fixed multiplicative rescaling of pair-distance MST universally pays for a single common reset word. It does not refute the Cerny conjecture, all polynomial-size potentials, or the same claim restricted to strongly connected automata. Historical priority is UNKNOWN.
Closest comparator: The construction uses the classical Cerny family and the source cites established reset-threshold literature. The exact extension formulation was not independently checked against prior art during intake; no novelty or priority claim is made.
