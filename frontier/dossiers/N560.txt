Claim: For each nonzero net jump nu, the infinitesimal endpoint intensity has the finite expansion j_nu(x)=sum_alpha kappa_(alpha,alpha+nu) product_i binom(x_i,alpha_i). For every candidate alpha with |alpha|_1<=k and alpha+nu>=0 coordinatewise, the aggregate coefficient is isolated by the multivariate forward difference (Delta^alpha j_nu)(0). Under bounded reaction orders and the source interface, this identifies observable directed reactant/product complexes and their aggregate rates.
Interface: Uses the same exact-reset, independent-run, complete labeled endpoint, calibrated-time stochastic mass-action contract as N559. It concerns infinitesimal jump intensities reconstructed through short-time endpoint laws; no reaction-event trajectory is assumed. Finite-sample rate recovery is epsilon-accurate under an explicit positive rate floor and known bounds.
Proof spine: Expand each jump intensity in the binomial/Newton basis. The identity Delta binom(x,a)=binom(x,a-1) makes evaluation at zero after Delta^alpha isolate the coefficient at alpha. The displayed alternating finite sum gives the inversion.
Costs: Candidate seed states scale as (k+1)^k per recovered support; endpoint sample costs depend on short-time bias, confidence, rate floors, number of possible jumps and full-state readout. The source provides conservative batch and arithmetic bounds, not a cost-free exact real-rate oracle.
Failure: Net-zero reactions are invisible, identical (alpha,beta) channels are only recovered through summed rates, and incomplete/latent species observation invalidates the contract. Theorem concerns non-null bounded-order channels; no simultaneous estimation of arbitrary hidden rates is claimed.
Closest comparator: Finite differences in the binomial basis are standard. The contribution claimed by the source is their use with positive-hazard support peeling and a fully charged active endpoint interface, not a new difference calculus.
Open verification: Independently check the short-time bias and union-bound constants, and assess whether realistic endpoint assays can recover all labeled non-null jumps at the required precision.
