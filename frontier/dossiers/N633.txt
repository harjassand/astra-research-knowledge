Claim: Let n>=1 qubits start in |0^n> and a reference with H_R=sum_{r>=0} r|r><r| start in any finite-mean state rho of mean mu. Under an arbitrary joint unitary commuting with H_S+H_R, retain all outputs and compare the final joint state with |+^n><+^n| tensor the same original rho, uncorrelated. If D is their trace distance, then for every s>0, D >= exp(-s mu)[1-((1+exp(-s))/2)^n], and D >= n/[8e(mu+n)]. Thus for 0<epsilon<=1/(16e), D<=epsilon requires mu>=n/(16e epsilon). A supplied sine-profile reference and explicit conserving interactions attain D<=min(1, pi sqrt(n(n+1))/(2(L+1))) with mean energy n+(L-1)/2, giving optimal Theta(n/epsilon) scaling for this exact joint-return task.
Interface: All system outputs and the reference are retained; the target contains the same uncorrelated reference. U may be arbitrary subject to exact total-energy conservation. Trace distance is 1/2 trace norm. The upper construction assumes the coherent sine reference is supplied; it is not constructed from free inputs.
Proof spine: The invariant bounded effect exp[-s(H_S+H_R)] has expectations L_rho(s) and L_rho(s)((1+exp(-s))/2)^n on actual and target states. Jensen gives L_rho(s)>=exp(-s mu), yielding the all-protocol lower bound. For the upper bound, sector Hadamards yield 2^(-n/2) sum_x |x> T^|x| eta; telescoping shift error and W~Bin(n,1/2) give the uniform overlap bound.
Costs: Mean resource scaling is Theta(n/epsilon) in the stated small-error regime, with a supplied sine reference of mean n+(L-1)/2; no gate-synthesis or reference-preparation complexity is bounded.
Failure: The lower bound is for the entire joint output/reference comparison, not the reduced system channel or a channel diamond norm. The construction prepares no reference and charges no arbitrary-unitary synthesis; initial-reference precision contributes additional error. A fixed-pure-reference overlap optimum is also source-derived but does not imply efficient synthesis.
Closest comparator: The source cites Chiribella, Yang and Renner (2021), “Fundamental Energy Requirement of Reversible Quantum Operations,” https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.11.021014, plus finite sine-reference and catalytic-coherence antecedents. Citation and exact priority comparison were not rechecked in intake; no novelty is claimed.
