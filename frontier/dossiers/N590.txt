Claim: Let rational linearly independent p-by-n matrices A_i have Frobenius Gram H_ij=tr(A_i A_j^T), s(lambda)=lambda^T H lambda=||A_lambda||_F^2. If integer r>m satisfies the collective stable-rank bound ||A_lambda||_op^2<=s(lambda)/r for every lambda, then the homogeneous positive dual v(lambda)=det(I+A_lambda A_lambda^T)^(-1/2) obeys exp(-s/2)<=v(lambda)<=(1+s/r)^(-r/2). Either rational certificate M_p=sum_ij(H^(-1))_ij A_iA_j^T<=I_p/r or M_n=sum_ij(H^(-1))_ij A_i^T A_j<=I_n/r suffices. An exact ideal sampler proposes lambda=sqrt(r)H^(-1/2)Z/sqrt(V), V~chi-square_(r-m), accepts by v/h_r, then samples the conditional Gaussian X and projects Y onto ker F(X); its success is at least a_(r,m)=(2/r)^(m/2)Gamma(r/2)/Gamma((r-m)/2). Separately, the source states that for rational homogeneous g=c=0 inputs, a native M_p certificate and r>=4(m+1)^2 yield a finite-random-bit algorithm with W1 joint-law error<=epsilon and residual norm<=epsilon, using time/bits polynomial in input bit length, dimensions and log(1/epsilon). This finite-output theorem is not exact finite-bit equality conditioning and is not TV approximation to a continuous law.
Interface: The exact sampler assumes independent standard Gaussian prior coordinates, rational A_i, full-rank input family and a native collective stable-rank certificate. The finite-output theorem is narrower: homogeneous zero target, rational inputs, M_p certificate, r>=4(m+1)^2, rational 0<epsilon<1, independent uniform random bits, and W1 plus a residual guarantee. The theorem and the accompanying implementation are distinct evidence: the source prototype handles two 128x128 int64-numerator matrices with denominator 4096 and recomputes a native certificate r=62>=36 (m=2); arbitrary dimensions and rational encodings are not implemented.
Proof spine: The rational Gram matrix converts the span to conceptual Frobenius-orthonormal coordinates. PSD certificates bound both matrix-side operators and imply ||A_lambda||_op^2<=s/r. Concavity of log(1+u) yields r log(1+s/r)<=log det(I+A_lambda A_lambda^T)<=s; hence the Student-t envelope. Integrating the lower Gaussian bound yields the exact acceptance lower bound. The source’s finite-output proof separately charges the trial cap, normal tails, small singular values, arithmetic/coupling errors and output residual to obtain W1 control; it is a written analytic candidate, not certified by the finite examples.
Costs: The PSD sufficient verifier and per-trial matrix work are polynomial in dimensions and rational bit lengths; it is not a complete recognizer. Expected exact trials are at most 1/a_(r,m); r=Omega(m^2) suffices for a dimension-independent bound, while subspace restriction consumes up to a+b units of r. Finite-output polynomial bit/time is claimed only under the stated homogeneous theorem and independent-uniform-bit model. The included implementation is fixed-input-format prototype evidence; its three saved single draws and timings are not benchmarks and do not prove the law or uniform performance.
