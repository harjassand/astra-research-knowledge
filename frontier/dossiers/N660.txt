N660-finite-message-sld-reliability-secrecy-bound | A finite-message SLD bound couples decoding reliability to trace-distance secrecy

Interface: Uniform k-message code, k>=2, receiver average error epsilon and delta=one-half trace distance from product of the uniform message and environment marginal. Environment dominates receiver in measured chi-square for the block channel actually used.
Failure: No arbitrary adaptive wiring, automatic environment-versus-receiver order from a scalar self-coefficient, newly established capacity mechanism, or claim of a new asymptotic strong converse. Novelty of the finite-k refinement remains UNKNOWN.

Claim (source-reported). For a uniform k-message code, k>=2, let epsilon be receiver average decoding error and delta=(1/2)||rho_XE-(I_X/k) tensor rho_E||_1. If the environment E dominates receiver B in measured chi-square for the block channel under consideration, then
  delta >= [(1-epsilon-1/k)_+]^2 / (1-1/k).
N659 supplies an actual environment decoder with success at least h_k(1-epsilon) when 1-epsilon>=1/k; secrecy bounds every decoder's success by 1/k+delta. The positive-part bound is trivial outside that range. This uses N659's source-reported theorem, not the N402/N403 Choi coefficient formula.

Reported comparison. Under the same blockwise binary midpoint-MMSE dominance hypothesis, the manuscript compares the bound with delta >= (1-2 epsilon)^2/2 for epsilon<=1/2. It agrees for k=2; for k>2 its difference is (k-2)[1/(2k)-epsilon^2/(k-1)]>0 throughout that range. At epsilon=0 the bound gives delta>=1-1/k. The primary-source comparison and originality have not been independently checked by this intake.

Composition contract. Complete SLD dominance tensorizes by replacing one channel at a time and treating other outputs as an untouched reference. Hence N659's bounds apply to parallel block codes with the same encoder; the source reports transfer of vanishing decoding errors and no decrease in achievable reliability exponents. Ordinary one-use dominance alone is not asserted to tensorize. The code's environment-versus-receiver order must be established separately; a scalar self-contraction coefficient does not supply it.

Counterexample to broader wiring (source lines203-204). For the binary erasure/BSC pair in N659, mapping the erasure flag to a fair bit yields BSC bias a^2 on the source side while the target remains bias a. SLD order reverses at the uniform input. Arbitrary common adaptive output controls are therefore excluded. Retained memory may be an untouched reference for a one-use inequality without suffering multiplicative contraction.

Boundary. Capacity monotonicity and zero-capacity consequences are not claimed new. The source distinguishes this finite-code bound on separate decoding/secrecy errors from an existing asymptotic private strong converse using an ideal-key criterion.
