Claim: For algebraically closed K, one fixed polynomial map F:K^n→K^n of coordinate degree at most d and nonconstant polynomial h of degree e, if L=min{t≥0:h(F^t(a))≠0} is finite, the source candidate proves L≤T_n−1. With Δ_0=e,T_0=1 and Δ_(i+1)=Δ_i^(n+2)d^((n+1)T_i), T_(i+1)=(Δ_i+1)T_i for i=0,…,n−1, zero values at t=0,…,T_n−1 imply all-time zeroness. The upper horizon is independent of coefficient heights and has tower height growing with dimension.
Interface: A single autonomous polynomial map, specified initial point and polynomial output; the target is the length of the initial zero block, with L=∞ if all outputs vanish. The geometric statement is over an algebraically closed field as in the source. For the stated rational-input computational consequence, coefficients and initial point are rational and characteristic zero; polynomials are explicitly encoded.
Proof spine: A coherent return-device invariant tracks one actual iterate F^τ on each irreducible component, certifies intermediate survival, separates safe and exiting components, and descends dimension with a Bézout-based degree budget. It does not replace the orbit with independently selectable transitions.
Costs: The source derives a coefficient-height-uniform primitive-recursive horizon. For rational encoded inputs it reports exact evaluation/decision bit work primitive recursive in input length; no polynomial-time or practical-cost claim is made.
Failure: This is initial-block finite determination for one fixed map, not the Skolem question of an arbitrary later zero. Arbitrary control words, general partially defined rational maps and practical efficiency are outside scope. The literature comparison is targeted and does not establish priority.
Closest comparator: The packet compares the dimension-preserving Novikov–Yakovenko bound, polynomial-recursive zeroness results, effective difference elimination and invertible polynomial automata; it identifies a distinct unrestricted singular/dimension-collapsing scope but does not rule out an equivalent result elsewhere.
