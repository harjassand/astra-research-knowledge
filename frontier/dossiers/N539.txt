Claim: Let P0=Bern(a)^d with 0<a<=1/2, target A={1}, p=a^d and integer 0<=k<d. Set D_k=sum_(j=0)^k binom(d,j)((1-a)/a)^j and K=p^-1-D_k. If D_k<=K, the source constructs nonnegative P* with every marginal on at most k coordinates exactly equal to P0, P*(A)=2p and chi-square(P*||P0)=1/K<=2p. This divergence is minimal among laws satisfying those marginal and event-mass constraints. An adaptive assay revealing at most k distinct coordinates from each fresh specimen has identical transcript laws under P0 and P*, even with unlimited specimens. A separate positive regime assumes known product structure and explicit polynomial-size DNF failure modes and composes established product-estimation/weighted-witness methods.
Interface: Known d,a,k and a rare conjunction under a Bernoulli product baseline. The negative pair is indistinguishable from supplied low-order marginals; the assay reveals at most k distinct coordinates per fresh specimen. Reusing a specimen to observe more coordinates changes the contract. The positive regime requires true product structure and explicit DNF representation.
Proof spine: In L^2(P0), split the point-evaluation reproducing kernel at the all-ones vector into degrees <=k and >k. The high-degree residual is orthogonal to every function of <=k coordinates. Adding it to the likelihood ratio doubles target-event mass while preserving all those marginals. Its squared norm gives the chi-square value; Cauchy-Schwarz proves optimality. Transcript coupling handles the stated adaptive fresh-specimen assay.
Costs: The source gives a full-vector relative-estimation lower bound of Omega(1/(epsilon^2 p)) under its stated conditions despite exact supplied marginals through order k. P* has a compact exact rejection sampler with reported acceptance 1/2; constructing it does not reveal which real-world law holds. The positive product/DNF regime charges measurements and arithmetic and uses known methods.
Failure: D_k<=K is required. The divergence optimum is not by itself a finite-sample minimax theorem for every assay. Product and explicit-DNF assumptions are essential to the positive regime. No real failure law or general rare-event capability is established.
