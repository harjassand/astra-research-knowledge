Claim: Under the source's weak-Hall, local-diffusive model with the same scalar zero-field longitudinal conductivity in every domain and fixed single-domain coefficients a,c, tetragonal symmetry gives K0=diag(a,a,c) and K(n)=aI+(c-a)nn^T. First-order arithmetic averaging is exact under those assumptions: for Q=E[nn^T], tr Q=1, K_eff=aI+(c-a)Q and b_hat·K_eff b_hat=a+(c-a)E[(n·b_hat)^2]. For the three ideal SrTiO3 tetragonal variants and B along [111], every squared projection is 1/3, so the model predicts S_[111]=(2a+c)/3 independent of domain populations. Orthogonal full-tensor slopes sum to tr K_eff=2a+c, invariant under pure orientation redistribution in this model. These are conditional deductions, not measured values of a,c or evidence for a microscopic mechanism.
Interface: Assumes weak Hall response, local diffusion, a common scalar zero-field longitudinal conductivity, fixed a,c across intervention, and arithmetic averaging at first order. Arbitrary inhomogeneous anisotropic composites are outside scope. B projection and contact-normal geometry must not be conflated.
Proof spine: The axial-vector transformation laws make K transform as an ordinary rank-two tensor. Tetragonal D4h symmetry fixes diag(a,a,c); the domain director gives the rank-one form. The uniform zero-field corrector under common scalar longitudinal conductivity makes the first-order effective Hall tensor the arithmetic mean, from which the [111] and trace identities follow.
Costs: The source digitizes plotted vector markers from a published figure and reports descriptive finite-field statistics. Its +/- perturbation range for extraction is a sensitivity interval, not a confidence interval or experimental error model. No suitable matched before/after [111] or full three-axis raw dataset was located in the bounded search.
Failure: The analysis neither statistically rejects the microscopic scattering explanation nor establishes a new natural mechanism. Contact gains, temperature mismatch, heat-flow changes, spatially varying longitudinal anisotropy, domain walls and other scattering changes remain possible. A raw Hall angle is not automatically the zero-field slope.
Closest comparator: The source identifies prior SrTiO3 domain-orientation work and known Hall-sign-inversion examples in inhomogeneous media. It makes no novelty claim; citations and figures were not independently reanalysed during intake.
Open verification: Acquire matched-temperature full-tensor Hall measurements in [111] and three orthogonal geometries with calibrated contact/longitudinal response; then test the invariants against spatially inhomogeneous transport and the source-reported uncertainty.
