Symbols and resource contracts LOCAL to this card.

Claim: Let C be a nonzero closed convex semialgebraic cone of completely positive maps on M_d, invariant under arbitrary CP pre- and postcomposition. The source candidate proves: if every member of C has some entanglement-breaking power, then there is N(C) such that every length-N(C) word of independently chosen members of C is entanglement breaking. Under these hypotheses pointwise eventual EB, a common equal-map EB exponent and a common arbitrary-word EB length are equivalent. The source applies this to PPT maps and to the cone CP ∩ 2-CoPos (maps that are both CP and 2-copositive), whose factors can include maps that are not PPT.
Interface: Finite-dimensional maps on M_d; every factor lies in the stated mapping cone and the full closed/convex/semialgebraic/CP-invariance assumptions are required. Words are serial compositions with factors allowed to vary by position; factors may be non-TP.
Proof spine: CP input/output filters isolate a transported cross coefficient in a two-dimensional triangular map. A nonzero coefficient would preserve qubit NPT at every power, contradicting the pointwise hypothesis; normalized boundary and positive-anchor inductions then yield a uniform arbitrary-word length.
Costs: The conclusion is finite existence of N(C), with dimension-only applications for PPT and CP plus 2-copositive cones. No practical numeric exponent or efficient decomposition is supplied.
Failure: The cone hypotheses cannot be dropped for an arbitrary compact set/class. This is serial composition, not tensor powers. Source says the later cone and unrestricted-word arguments were not covered by earlier focused reviews; targeted dependency/algebra checks are source-reported only.
Closest comparator: The source credits established mapping-cone and k-EB composition results and compares restricted mixed-map results. Its novelty search is explicitly targeted, not exhaustive.
Open verification: Independently reconstruct the transported cross-coefficient reduction, rectangular moving-support boundary, normalization and two-sided anchor steps for the full CP mapping-cone theorem; check each PPT and CP-plus-2-copositive interface and conduct a focused prior-art review.
