## Source-reported selectivity bound

For the report's freely mixing radical model,

\[
r_{AB}=k_{AB}ab,\qquad r_{AA}=\tfrac12 k_{AA}a^2,\qquad r_{BB}=\tfrac12 k_{BB}b^2,
\]

it derives the integrated desired cross-selectivity bound

\[
S_{AB}\leq \frac{k_{AB}}{k_{AB}+\sqrt{k_{AA}k_{BB}}}.
\]

The derivation is conditional on the stipulated local mass-action rates applying throughout. Equal cross- and homocoupling coefficients give a 50% ceiling in this model; reaching 99% by kinetic discrimination alone requires \(k_{AB}\geq99\sqrt{k_{AA}k_{BB}}\). This is not a universal chemical-synthesis limit: preassociation, catalysts, persistent radicals, heterogeneous interfaces and other mechanisms can leave the model's assumptions.

The report says it ran four synthetic feed schedules with equal A/B inputs, equal reaction coefficients and first-order loss 0.05; the best desired fraction was 50%, while alternating or sequential feeds did worse. The code and inputs were not attached or independently executed in this intake.

## Proposed mechanism and model comparisons

The proposed reusable molecular compartment separates precursor loading, a reactive state, and leaving-group/product release. In a simple equimolar, fixed-degeneracy equilibrium example, the report estimates that an 11.4 kJ/mol preference gives 99% occupancy. With \(M\) competing alternatives, it gives the illustrative scale \(RT\ln[99(M-1)]\), about 22.8 kJ/mol for 100 alternatives and 34.2 kJ/mol for 10,000. These are model calculations, not universal energy requirements; photon fractions and other physical mechanisms differ.

A one-knob confinement model is reported to fail as a monotone design rule: if stronger confinement slows productive fragmentation and escape together, its synthetic rates produce a finite optimum. For the report's example, the yield rises from 12.9% to 31.5% and then falls at higher confinement. This is a limitation of that rate dependence, not a no-go for molecular cages.

The proposed alternative uses orthogonal reaction-state gates. For the stated synthetic case \(q=0.99765\) and target 0.99, the report requires \((1+\beta)(1+\epsilon)\leq1.00773\) and reports a model yield of 99.07%. Its example assumes an escape-rate reduction from about \(6\times10^9\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) to \(6\times10^6\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), while the productive coupling rate is about \(10^9\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\). No such rates were measured, and the report leaves open whether partner identity can be retained without suppressing bond-forming motion.

## Comparator, decisive test and costs

The source compares the proposal with substrate-paired photoactivated complexes, iron-porphyrin/carbon-nitride radical sorting, persistent radical pairing on Ag(111), molecular cages, and kilogram-scale nickel cross-electrophile manufacture. These are report-referenced comparisons only; this intake did not audit the cited literature. Its revised novelty claim—independent reaction-state switching and partner memory—remains unestablished.

The proposed discriminator is isotope crossover between two equivalent labeled precursor pairs. Under the report's ideal mixture model, the mismatched-product fraction is \((1-m)/2\); vary the mixing-to-illumination delay and compare the same conditions with and without a host, using time-resolved absorption and product-isotope analysis. This is a proposed experiment, not an acquired result.

The report estimates 268 kJ/mol incident photon energy per product at 450 nm and model yield 0.99071; at an assumed 30% wall-plug efficiency, that becomes about 894 kJ/mol electrical input for illumination alone. A 10 mM host operating at 1 M/h would need about 101 cycles per host per hour, or roughly 36 seconds per cycle. These are illustrative estimates and omit synthesis, separation, host recovery, heating and other process costs; no practical advantage follows.
