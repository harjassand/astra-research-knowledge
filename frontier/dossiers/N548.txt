N548-conditional-generator-identifiability-and-invisible-current-gauge | Full generator recovery is conditional on rank, while two stationary laws leave an invisible current

Claim: Let s_i=∇log p_i and T_i=∇²p_i/p_i for d+1 controlled experiments, and define W(x)=[s_1−s_0,…,s_d−s_0]. For i=1,…,d define h_i=D:(T_i−T_0)−∇·u_i−u_i·s_i+∇·u_0+u_0·s_0. Under the source’s smoothness and exact-law assumptions, if the pairwise matrix features identify constant D and W(x) is invertible almost everywhere, subtracting stationary Fokker–Planck equations identifies b(x)=W(x)^{−T}h(x) almost everywhere. Conversely, for a compatible pair of positive stationary densities on T², known D and controls do not identify general b: adding v(x)=p_0(x)^{−1}S∇(p_1/p_0), for a nonzero constant skew matrix S, preserves both stationary laws but changes their currents.
Interface: The sufficient theorem assumes full-state, exactly known positive stationary densities, common smooth b, constant D, known controls, full rank in the D-feature system and almost-everywhere pointwise rank of W. The counterexample is on the flat two-dimensional torus and retains the same D and controls. Neither statement is a finite-sample result.
Proof spine: Subtract the stationary Fokker–Planck equations to obtain d linear equations in b(x); invert W wherever it has full rank. For the obstruction, p_0v=S∇r has zero divergence, and p_1v=rS∇r also has zero divergence because S is skew and Hessians are symmetric.
Costs: The nonparametric formula uses density scores, second derivatives, pointwise matrix inversion and conditioning. Statistical error, regularization, regions where W is nearly singular, mixing, finite samples and partial observation have no guarantee.
Failure: A singular or poorly conditioned W prevents stable drift recovery. The two-regime gauge refutes unrestricted full-drift identification from those two laws, but it does not refute recovery of D or the d+1 rank-conditional theorem. A radial or latent projection does not satisfy the full-state interface.
Closest comparator: The source cites prior Fokker–Planck algebra and intervention-identifiability work; no complete proof-level priority audit is reported. N498 is a useful projection-loss contrast, not a theorem premise.
