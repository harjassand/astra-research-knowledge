Claim: Let A be a finite subset of SO(3) with positive gate law nu and sphere averaging operator Q. Under the absolute L2 spectral-gap condition ||Qf||_2<=lambda||f||_2 for mean-zero f, lambda<1, the source defines F_m(T) as the supremum l1,2 operator norm over arbitrary time-inhomogeneous m-by-m row-stochastic positive lifts and proves F_m(T)<=2 exp(-c_nu T/m), c_nu=(1-lambda^2)log(2)/98305. For m>=16 a fixed lift gives (1-8/m)^T<=F_m(T), so 1-rho_m=Theta_nu(1/m).
Interface: Gates are drawn independently from the fixed positive law. Every row-stochastic transition may depend on the current gate and time; no commutativity, reversibility or stationary-kernel assumption. The theorem is conditional on the stated spectral gap; concrete constants are not optimized.
Proof spine: A matched-Gaussian convex-order Lyapunov controls the evolution of the conditional-mean measure for every stochastic encoder. A spherical isoperimetric/profile argument and accumulation over time yield the upper bound. A polytope containing the ball supplies a fixed positive lift with eigenvalue 1-8/m for the lower bound.
Costs: The result bounds an operator profile, not total runtime, memory bits, precision, or data acquisition. The constant c_nu is small and nonoptimized. The external spectral gap needed by the concrete gate corollary is separate and imported in the packet.
Failure: A one-step contraction claim is false for finite alphabets: F_m(1)=1 for m>=5. The proof requires accumulation. No gap-free uniform polynomial rate follows from density alone; the packet gives a Liouville rotation counterexample.
