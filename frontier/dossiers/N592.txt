Claim: Let n independent two-state continuous-time Markov chains have rational stationary target probabilities p_i in (0,1), rational relaxation rates lambda_i>0, target-to-other rates (1-p_i)lambda_i and other-to-target rates p_i lambda_i, and the stationary product initial law. Let H be the first time all components occupy their designated joint target, with H=0 if initially there. For rational epsilon,delta in (0,1/2), the source specifies a randomized Turing algorithm outputting a nonnegative rational Hhat and proves an internal coupling claim P(|Hhat-H|>epsilon H)<=delta. Its asserted expected bit complexity is polynomial in total input bit length, n, epsilon^-1 and log(delta^-1), with no polynomial dependence on inverse target probability q^-1=(product_i p_i)^-1 or on rate ratios. It also derives sup_t |P(Hhat<=t)-P(H<=t)| <= delta-log(1-epsilon)/e. These are source-reported proof claims, not independently reconstructed by intake.
Interface: The target is one joint state and the initial law is the stationary product distribution. Only one approximate hitting time is returned. The source does not provide a trajectory, arbitrary-start hitting law, arbitrary target-set law, relative rare-tail error, exact continuous sampling, or total variation for the unquantized continuous law. Total variation is stated only for a requested finite binning with a separately charged boundary-error term.
Proof spine: The classical stationary renewal transform gives a geometric sum of exponential increments. The construction stratifies the activated Bernoulli rate sum by its maximum active rate, realizes the law as a Cox/Poisson mixture, and replaces only sufficiently large stratum sums by sample means. The finite-bit specification uses bounded dyadic uniforms, slack at the branch threshold, capped finite Poisson tables, explicit coupling budgets, and charged rational bit lengths; it avoids a Poisson oracle whose mean scales like q^-1.
Costs: The ideal-real construction reports O(n^3 epsilon^-2 log(4n/delta)) elementary increment work. The finite-bit theorem reports expected bit complexity polynomial in input bit length, n, epsilon^-1 and log(delta^-1); rare target probability and extreme rate ratios affect arithmetic representation length, not a polynomial number of simulated transitions. The source Python programs are floating-point demonstrations and do not implement or certify the finite-bit specification.
Failure: Brown’s exact stationary hitting-time representation is established prior art, and non-enumerative product-chain approximations already exist. The source only identifies the maximum-rate-stratified compressed coupling and its encoded complexity as potentially distinct; priority is UNKNOWN. Its numerical checks are finite floating-point observations, not the theorem proof. No unquantized TV or relative rare-tail guarantee follows.
