Claim: On R^d, suppose p_i(x)=Z_i^{−1}p_0(x)exp(a_iᵀx) for i=1,…,d, with A=[a_1,…,a_d] nonsingular, known constant forces U=[u_1,…,u_d], a common drift and constant D≻0. Under consistency and normalizability, the source derives B=UA^{−1}, D=sym(B), K=skew(B), and b(x)=B∇log p_0(x), with stationary current J_i=K∇p_i. Thus an arbitrary nonlinear log p_0 is allowed within this exact-tilt family and the circulation can be nonzero. Fewer than d independent tilt directions cannot guarantee full generator identification in the worst case within the family.
Interface: The tilt relation is exact, A is full rank, controls are known constant forces, and D is constant symmetric positive definite. The identification result assumes exact stationary laws; classifier estimates of tilt slopes from independent stationary samples are a separate statistical procedure. Estimating b or entropy production still requires score information.
Proof spine: Use the pairwise identity with q=1 and constant w=a_i−a_j to recover the symmetric part of UA^{−1}; the score-difference matrix is A, so the stationary Fokker–Planck subtraction gives b. Conversely the skew part produces a divergence-free stationary current. If fewer directions are observed, perturb B by a nonzero E annihilating all observed a_i while preserving positive definiteness of sym(B+E).
Costs: No finite-sample rate for estimating A, B, D or b is proved. Accuracy depends on force amplitudes, density overlap and conditioning of A; physical preparation of independent stationary regimes and time to equilibrium are not charged.
Failure: The formula is specific to exact exponential tilts with invertible A. It does not extend automatically to arbitrary density ratios, unknown/state-dependent diffusion, changing drift across interventions, latent state, or finite-sample high-dimensional estimation.
Closest comparator: The source recognizes OU intervention and broader stationary SDE identifiability literature; exact priority for this nonlinear-base affine-tilt formula is UNKNOWN. The route is a conditional structural result, not a general identification algorithm.
