Claim: For compactly supported probability measures mu,nu in R^d, define Phi_(r,eta)(mu)=D(mu*gamma_r||gamma_r)-D(mu*gamma_r||eta*gamma_r), for every r>0 and every finitely supported probability eta. The source proves nu<=cx mu if and only if Phi_(r,eta)(nu)<=Phi_(r,eta)(mu) for all such probes. The raw scalar profile D(mu*gamma_r||gamma_r) alone is incomplete; a reflected two-point pair has identical scalar profiles but incomparable convex order.
Interface: All measures are compactly supported; the probes range over all positive Gaussian scales and all finite eta. The theorem characterizes an infinite family of real-valued probes and supplies no oracle, finite query bound or efficient decision algorithm.
Proof spine: Forward direction follows because Gaussian addition preserves convex order and the probe’s log-likelihood is convex. For the converse, small-scale finite log-sum-exp probes approximate any finite maximum of affine functions uniformly on the compact supports; supporting affine approximations recover every finite convex test function.
Costs: The probe family is infinite and may require precision and many affine pieces. For supplied finite supports, standard martingale-coupling linear feasibility already decides convex order; the result is a complete reformulation, not an efficient new capability.
Failure: One scalar E_r profile is not enough; reflection preserves it while the two call functions cross. Replacing the whole probe family by a restricted small family requires separate approximation/cost guarantees.
Closest comparator: Gaussian mixtures, convex duality and finite martingale-coupling tests have established precedents; the source describes this as a useful reorganization and does not claim a new primitive.
Open verification: Independently check the log-sum-exp limit and supporting-affine approximation, then determine whether any restricted probe family yields a useful finite computational interface.
