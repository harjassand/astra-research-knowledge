## Source-reported result

For a stationary, time-reversal-invariant nuclear state, a position-dependent electron-scattering operator (A(R)), and the complete signed nuclear energy-transfer spectrum, the 17:44 report gives

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} E S_A(E)\,dE
=\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\sum_i\frac{\langle|\nabla_i A|^2\rangle}{M_i}.
\]

This includes energy gain, energy loss and all phonon orders; it is not a centroid of a selected peak. The explicit potential term cancels from the double commutator, but the expectation still depends on the nuclear position distribution. The report also gives a thermal-harmonic second moment and says it checked the moments against a thermal quantum oscillator. Neither calculation was independently checked here.

Under the source's fixed-force, in-plane harmonic graphene model (300 K; 288/800-atom periodic cells; published Perebeinos–Tersoff force matrix; coherent pupil and Lobato–Van Dyck carbon scattering factor), the specified two-label rearrangement predicts about +7.8% and −7.2% full-moment changes at the two moved labels, with about +0.08% at unchanged site (b). The corresponding model optical-band centroids shift about +2.05, −2.09 and +0.677 meV. The last values are one-phonon model centroids in 145–210 meV, not all-background experimental spectra. The reported reader assumes 60 keV, 32 mrad convergence, a 12 mrad detector at a chosen 110 mrad offset, 28 meV resolution and unit efficiency; the calculation is not experimentally calibrated and omits several physical contributions recorded in the source intake.

## Material correction and cost

At (b), the two-label centroid change is 0.6766 meV versus 0.7364 meV for a single stationary label with a spatial scan. Thus most of the visible shift survives without the second label; the connected four-spectrum effect is much smaller. The report's ideal counting-noise-only comparison requires about (2.9\times10^{12}) primary electrons per spectrum, about 18 hours each or 74 hours total at 7 pA, before background, drift, preparation and survival costs. The source explicitly withdraws the small motif's optical response as a device justification. Single-isotope vibrational response is not the proposed new operation; the possible distinction is controlled post-assembly intervention and verification.

The proposed operation retains a 24-exchange closed Si route over (a,b,f), followed by a 16-hop parking path to about 2 nm from the intervention. Reported ideal counts are 72 exchanges for before/edit/after ending parked, 88 with Si returned to its start, and 128 for before/edit/undo/reference ending parked. These exclude failed attempts, recovery, initial isotope identification, registration and imaging. No successful physical operation or transfer probability is established.

The source estimates (8.9\times10^9) primary electrons per mass-moment spectrum (about 3.4 minutes at 7 pA; about 13.5 minutes for two changed sites before/after) and peak fluence (1.2\times10^{10}\,e/\text{Å}^2) per spectrum. These are model-based counting estimates, not calibrated identification probabilities or safe-dose limits. Energy-window truncation, energy referencing, coherent probe halo, electronic background, detector statistics, structural survival and the full visited isotope inventory remain consequential.
