Claim: The packet’s weak stationary-generator regression replaces density derivatives with empirical weak-form generator moments only after supplying an eight-function Fourier drift basis: on its synthetic 2D torus model, 12 repetitions with 80,000 samples per regime (240,000 total) report mean drift-coefficient error 4.01% and D error 5.50%. The source itself demotes this strategy as established weak Fokker–Planck/Stein prior art. In a separate 25-repetition paired same-sample comparison on the 3D quartic model, at 32,000 samples per regime the logistic density-ratio estimate had 1.78% mean B error versus 1.62% for score moments that additionally use the known quartic potential family; the reported paired 95% interval for the logistic-minus-score error difference is [−0.048,+0.360] percentage points and includes zero. The experiments establish no general estimator dominance.
Interface: The weak-moment linear system assumes a supplied finite drift basis and known test functions; data are generated by a numerical stationary simulator. The matched comparator reuses the same sample populations, while the score-moment method receives the quartic potential family. Reported intervals describe repetitions of one synthetic distribution, not population-science uncertainty.
Method and comparison scope: Weak integration by parts expresses stationary generator moments using observed positions and known test functions. The paired score-moment comparator uses a_i=−E_{p_i}[∇log p_0] after fitting the supplied-form quartic potential. The source reports errors and comparison intervals, but this intake does not independently run either method.
Costs: The finite drift basis and quartic family are supplied structural information. The simulations omit physical acquisition and equilibration. No nonasymptotic guarantee, real-data confirmation, or broad efficiency comparison is given.
Failure: Without the basis or potential-family information, the source’s shortcuts do not solve generic high-dimensional score acquisition. The weak method is prior art, and the score estimator sometimes outperforms the logistic estimator; do not claim a new dominant algorithm.
Closest comparator: The source cites published weak Fokker–Planck/Stein methods and a matched same-data parametric score-moment comparator. This card records an explicit prior-art demotion and negative dominance result, not a novelty claim.
Open verification: Verify the matched information sets and uncertainty calculation, audit the cited weak-form prior art, and require an acquisition-complete comparator before claiming algorithmic advantage.
