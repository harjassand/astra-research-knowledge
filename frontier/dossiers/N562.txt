Claim: For the specified scalar cubic J1–J2 exchange trial, J1=4J2 gives a quartic R-point line junction: unweighted soft volume scales as epsilon^(3/4), which under the report’s alpha=0.88 conditional formula suggests T^0.78. A forward intraband current weight proportional to |k| instead yields N_1(epsilon)~(3pi/2)epsilon log(1/epsilon), hence Delta rho=C1 T log(T0/T)+C2 T+o(T), not a pure leading T law. Separately, at J1=2J2 the two-coupling scalar spectrum cannot make R and X both minima; a J3 example gives competing isolated minima, and a force-balance model shows a fluctuation spectrum alone is not a resistance mechanism.
Interface: These are scoped calculations for the displayed scalar exchange, smooth-band and force-balance models. The J1–J2 lattice is a trial model rather than an established microscopic model of Ce3Pd20Si6; the actual band, internal multipolar structure, electron-coupled operator, vertex, incommensurability and momentum sink remain unresolved.
Proof spine: Factor the exact exchange polynomial at J1=4J2; compute the quartic junction volume. Then include the current-vertex weight in a finite-cutoff soft-volume integral, whose m=1 term has a logarithm. Compare R/X corner energies for the J1=2J2 candidate and use coupled drift force-balance to expose dependence on momentum relaxation.
Costs: The report’s scale and exponent calculations are conditional. No raw arrays, residual-resistance fit, joint covariance, confidence interval, or material-specific coupled transport calculation was performed. Source-reported numerical diagnostics were not rerun by this intake.
Failure: Does not exclude other Kondo-breakdown or nonperturbative explanations. The J3 counterexample refutes the inference that competition alone forces a soft continuum; it is not an empirically validated replacement model.
