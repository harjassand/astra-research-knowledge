Proposed electrochemical loading of a mixed H/D population onto supported graphene, selective partial oxidative stripping and separately qualified product collection. Source experimental antecedents concern reversible isotope-dependent bonding/removal; mixed-isotope hazards, inventory and product purity are not measured.

For a stripping-only interval with nonincreasing N_H,N_D, no readsorption/exchange and a uniform hazard ratio k_H<=r_max k_D, integration gives log(odds enrichment)<=(r_max-1)[-log(D recovery)]. This is elementary kinetics, not a novel theorem. Waveform changes do not evade a genuinely uniform hazard bound; exchange, replenishment, spatial heterogeneity and coadsorption interactions require a new mass-balanced model.

The proposed first go/no-go test starts from a measured surface D fraction0.10, targets fraction>=0.95 and retains>=half the initial D at room temperature with resolved isotope mass balance. It requires hazard ratio at least1+log(171)/log(2), approximately8.41785 in the stated stripping model. This is a chosen experimental threshold, not an observation or sufficient commercial-value criterion.

The reported operating coverage around1e14 atoms/cm^2 is an operating point, not a maximum storage theorem. Inventory, current and collection arithmetic expose scale costs. The source24.3pL carrier-H limit applies to one specified single-cycle liquid-collector calculation; it does not exclude multi-cycle accumulation, isotope-exchange removal or a separately measured gas/aprotic collector.

Pure-H and pure-D voltammograms and a zero-point-energy Boltzmann factor do not supply a mixed-isotope stripping ratio, recovery yield or full-cycle energy advantage. Established graphene isotope pumping and other electrochemical isotope separation remain strong comparators. No fitted physical model, new experiment, separator endurance or practical advantage is asserted.
