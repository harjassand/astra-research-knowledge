Claim: For D harmonic traceless-Hermitian k-by-k coordinate/momentum pairs, m=k^2-1 and G=-i sum_a[X_a,P_a], the positive dual on traceless Hermitian Lambda is v_D(Lambda)=det_R(I+L_Lambda L_Lambda*)^(-D/2). If the eigenvalues mu_a sum to zero, Weyl reduction gives v_D=product_{a<b}[1+(mu_a-mu_b)^2]^(-D), and the normalized eigenvalue density is proportional to product_{a<b}[(mu_a-mu_b)^2/(1+(mu_a-mu_b)^2)^D] on the trace-zero hyperplane. The source proves that the normalizing integral of this real-D family is finite iff D>1+1/k. In particular D=2 is normalizable for every finite k>=2, despite the generic stable-rank condition r>m failing at sufficiently large k. The gap density has a positive half-Cauchy product envelope and so an ideal-arithmetic rejection sampler exists, but no favorable acceptance scaling in k is established. The physical target is the classical high-temperature, zero-coupling harmonic Spin Matrix Theory phase-space ensemble with a common positive oscillator frequency; it is not interacting BFSS/BMN dynamics.
Interface: Here D is the number of independent harmonic matrix pairs in the physical model and is a positive integer. The integrability theorem separately extends the eigenvalue density to real D>0. k is gauge SU(k) size, independent of D. The law is residual/delta-conditioned, not intrinsic orbit-volume or Hausdorff surface measure; no additional spatial angular-momentum constraints are included. The commutator representation has structural stable rank r=Dk when explicitly supplied or accompanied by a checkable representation witness; the generic Casimir certificate alone returns only r<=D. At D=9, fixed SU(3)/SU(4) constants are source-reported, but the large-k D=9 regime is not efficient under these bounds.
Proof spine: Diagonalize Lambda. Each unordered eigenvalue pair contributes two real commutator singular directions of magnitude |mu_a-mu_b|, yielding the determinant product. Weyl’s change of variables contributes the Vandermonde square; ordered gaps give a positive density. For D=2, bounding nonadjacent factors by one and retaining adjacent Cauchy factors proves finite normalization at each k. For real D, the source proves necessity by a large-radius cone and sufficiency by ordered-gap sectors, assigning each pair to its largest intervening gap and integrating the resulting logarithmic variables; the threshold is D>1+1/k.
Costs: The gap dimension is k-1, followed by an independent Haar eigenbasis. The D=2 half-Cauchy rejection proposal establishes existence in ideal arithmetic only; its acceptance as k grows is not bounded favorably. The generic stable-rank envelope gives explicit fixed-rank constants for D=9 and SU(3)/SU(4), but r=Dk eventually fails r>m at fixed D. The finite-output theorem as originally stated requires r>=4(m+1)^2 and does not literally cover these D=9 examples; the source gives a checked fixed-(D,k) argument for r>2m with constants, but no implemented multiprecision physical sampler or practical timing claim.
Failure: Finite normalization is not efficient acquisition. The D=2 squared inverse-determinant L2 control used by the source’s finite-output argument diverges logarithmically near the origin for every k, so that precision proof cannot be extended merely by sharpening stable-rank constants. No replacement Lp or small-ball argument is proved. At fixed physical D=9, large gauge rank is not efficient under these bounds. The result is not a new physical law, quantum singlet sampler, interacting BFSS/BMN solution, or established historical novelty.
Closest comparator: The source identifies the target with Harmark–Orselli (2014) classical zero-coupling Spin Matrix Theory and distinguishes it from interacting classical matrix dynamics and Gaussian quantum thermal-circle methods. It also cites Cauchy-determinant and difference-eigenvalue-model antecedents; these citations and the physical convention were not independently checked during intake. No direct speed comparison or historical novelty evidence is claimed.
