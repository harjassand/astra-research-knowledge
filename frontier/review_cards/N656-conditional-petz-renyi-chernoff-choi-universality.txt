Claim (conditional source-reported theorem candidate). Assume the N402 state-independent signed-lift certificate and N403 rectangular sharpness identity. For each finite channel \(\mathcal N:A\to B\), let \(J\) be its unnormalized Choi matrix and \(J^+\) its Moore–Penrose inverse. The manuscript claims that one Choi objective
\[
 M(\mathcal N)=\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal D(B)}\left\|\operatorname{Tr}_B[(I\otimes\sigma)J^+(I\otimes\sigma)]\right\|_\infty,
 \qquad \operatorname{ran}(I\otimes\sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}J,
 \quad \eta=1-M^{-1},
\]
gives \(\eta_{D_f}^c=\eta_{D_f}^{\Delta}=\eta_{I_f}=1-M^{-1}\) for (i) nonaffine modular Petz generators normalized by \(f(1)=f'(1)=0\), operator convex, with \(f(0+)<\infty\), and finite \(\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x\); the same equality is claimed for each fixed Petz Rényi order \(0<\alpha<1\) and quantum Chernoff divergence. An infeasible range constraint means \(M=\infty\). The conditional information is the manuscript's explicit difference definition; it is not an optimized or sandwiched Rényi CMI. Sharpness uses a reference of dimension at most \((\dim B)^2+1\) and a binary classical auxiliary.

Proof spine reported: integrate resolvent quadratic forms against the positive operator-convex representation for the upper bound; use N403's rectangular optimum and ordered rare-flag limits for a matching lower witness. Rényi and Chernoff follow through their stated Hellinger kernels. This is a finite-dimensional Choi-data capability conditional on N402/N403, not a formula for arbitrary channel pairs, adaptive channel discrimination, or fixed-equilibrium entropy decay.

Boundary/failure evidence: at Petz order zero, qubit depolarizing shrinkage \(1/2\) has all three coefficients zero, while each fixed interior order is claimed to give \(3/8\); the rare-flag limit is therefore nonuniform at zero. A proposed extension to all \(\alpha>1\) is false: the supplied diagonal Petz order-10 example has ratio \(0.3874364\ldots>3/8\), and order ten is outside the usual data-processing range. The classical multihypothesis exponent matches an established Chernoff result; the proposed noncommuting transfer fails on the supplied support-mismatch example. No historical-priority certification is claimed.
