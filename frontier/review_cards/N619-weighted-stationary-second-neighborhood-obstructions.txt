One four-vertex witness has arcs 0->1,0->3,1->2,2->0,3->1,3->2, stationary law (4,3,4,2)/13, outdegrees (2,1,1,2), and exact second-neighborhood sizes (1,1,2,1), giving expectations 19/13 and 17/13. These examples obstruct broad stationary-weighted extensions; neither refutes the existential Seymour conjecture, the minimum-outdegree formulation, or a row-regular triangle-free statement.
Interface: Finite strongly connected oriented digraphs; pi is stationary for the uniform-outneighbor transition. In the first witness outdegree is variable and directed triangles exist. The second witness is triangle-free but variable-degree, with exact second neighborhoods defined to exclude the start and first outneighbors.

For the triangle-free example, arrange four two-vertex blocks cyclically, put one internal arc in each block and all arcs to the next block; the exact stationary weights are 3/28 on each u_i and 4/28 on each v_i, while each exact second neighborhood has size two.
