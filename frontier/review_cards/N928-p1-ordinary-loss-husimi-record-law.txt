Ordinary loss yields a Husimi-law predictor for sparse complete records

STATUS | source_derived_unreviewed
REPORTED STATUS | Source-reported proof and exact finite benchmark; not independently validated in this integration. Novelty UNKNOWN; physical acquisition NOT_ACQUIRED; general speedup NOT_ESTABLISHED.
EXACT CLAIM | For arbitrary physical initial density operators, number-conserving hopping/density interactions, ordinary equal pure loss at a fixed rate d>0, and calibrated sparse observation with detected fraction O(theta), the canonical physical Husimi law gives a finite positive predictor for the complete permitted record under the same implemented feedback policy, with explicit truncation, tail, event-mass, grid/particle, numerical and calibration costs.
PROOF SPINE | A backward-effect argument starts from the physical Husimi law and avoids derivatives of the preparation amplitude. Ordinary loss bounds accumulated nonlinear sensitivity and the remaining count budget. The result predicts classical records; it does not reconstruct the quantum state from a classical mixture and does not claim an exact positive diffusion.
LIMITS | Grid cost is O_m((1+R/delta)^(2m)); particle needs can depend exponentially on the record alphabet. Mean energy alone gives no uniform O(theta) bound. Initial Husimi weights and phase reference require a fresh calibrated acquisition. The generic bound can be vacuous on the source's exact nonlinear benchmark and is not an economical many-mode theorem.
EXACT BENCHMARK | The separate source reports an outward exact-arithmetic certificate for a two-mode open-Kerr first-port record: TV from the correctly damped nonlinear limit <=0.000538488; TV from the old phase clock >=0.072192509; TV from the unchanged weak-bath predictor >=0.280862244. Equal pooled count-path laws for Kerr-on/off models are also reported, while marked first-port records separate them. These are finite model calculations, not laboratory data; no historical novelty is claimed.
DISTINCTION | The older P1 weak-bath result is N927. It uses theta-scaled bath rates and a different purification/amplitude-distribution contract. This result fixes the loss rate independently of theta and uses a physical Husimi law. It does not invalidate, generalize, or inherit the earlier assumptions.
