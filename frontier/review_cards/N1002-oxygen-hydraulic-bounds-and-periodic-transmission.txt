EXACT CLAIM
In the locally Newtonian extraction-bed model, p/kappa=q*A(M/q), and nondecreasing oxygen-dependent viscosity gives differential gain1<=E=2-g/A<=2; uniform low viscosity dominates at fixed pressure. Spatially localized resistance can change that bound but raises separate pressure/information requirements. The successor is a proposed periodically driven Maxwell transmission H=i*omega*tau/[beta+i*omega*tau*(1+beta)] with opposed metering ports. Static-preload creep cannot reopen a gate; the periodic device needs real sensing, balanced forces, delivery floor, work and reset/cyclic stability.

INTERFACE AND ASSUMPTIONS
Known fixed geometry and uptake law, nonnegative locally Newtonian viscosity and declared resource budget for the hydraulic result. The successor assumes the explicit Maxwell/output-spring topology, pressure-balanced port law, periodic drive, actual local/downstream-to-upstream oxygen information and acquired oxygen conversion kinetics, rather than substituting stress-relaxation time for ligand response.

BOUNDARIES
These models omit major biological vessel/injury effects. Oxygen extraction alone cannot distinguish latent recoverability. Desired-sign rheology and hydrogel valves have antecedents; no broad novelty claim. The published very-low-oxygen material threshold is not a healthy-tissue setpoint. Thin ribbons preserve ideal axial stiffness and shorten diffusion paths but cannot remove ligand kinetic floor or oxygen inventory. An ordinary powered sensor/actuator has a different topology and need not obey the direct-transmission volume bound. No reopening of blocked vessels or tissue-recovery/clinical capability.
