They describe finite classical channels, but complete SLD comparison permits every finite untouched quantum reference, including noncommuting conditional reference states. PROVISIONAL CLASSICAL CHARACTERIZATION
The transcript reports the equivalence

    M >=^c_SLD N  iff  there exists a real J in R^{m x n} such that
        M J = N,
        J 1_n = 1_m,
        diag(N_{x,:}) - J^T diag(M_{x,:}) J >= 0 for every input x.

J is a signed observable reconstruction and may contain negative entries; it is not required to be a stochastic post-processing. The reported certificate removes the arbitrary finite quantum reference from the classical-channel comparison. If the target channel has n outputs, reference dimension n+1 suffices to test the complete order.

The source's proposed contribution is the necessity of one common signed variance-dominating lift for arbitrary finite classical channels, together with the reference bound. Sufficiency of this kind of lift is prior work. This does not establish the common-lift converse for arbitrary noncommutative quantum channels.

BINARY-INPUT ALGORITHM
For a two-row channel W, define

    I_W(p) = sum_y (W_{0y}-W_{1y})^2 /
                    (p W_{0y} + (1-p) W_{1y}),  0 < p < 1,

omitting identically zero columns. The transcript reports

    M >=^c_SLD N  iff  I_M(p) >= I_N(p) for every p in (0,1).

After clearing positive denominators, exact Sturm sign counting decides the resulting rational polynomial inequality in time polynomial in the rational input length. This algorithm is specific to binary-input channels. The general finite characterization yields a polynomial-size SDP, but the source does not claim a polynomial-time exact feasibility algorithm at arbitrary singular boundaries.
