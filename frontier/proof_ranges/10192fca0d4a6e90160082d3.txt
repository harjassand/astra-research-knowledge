## 3. The elementary exact-null upper bound

Since the vacuum is fixed and the one-excitation Hamiltonian is I+C,

    U_H(t)psi=(|vac>+exp(-it)exp(-itC)|a>)/sqrt(2).

Let nu be the probability spectral measure of C at a and let

    F(t)=<a,exp(-itC)a>=integral exp(-itx) dnu(x).

After undoing K at time h, the expectation of X_a is Re F(h). Hence

    p=(1/2) integral [1-cos(hx)] dnu(x).

Since |x|<=2 and h<=1/2, |hx|<=1. The elementary bounds

    1-cos y >= y^2/4  for |y|<=1,
    1-cos y <= y^2/2  for every real y

give

    p >= h^2 <a,C^2 a>/8.

The squared distance of the two full state vectors at time t is

    ||U_H(t)psi-U_K(t)psi||^2 = 1-Re F(t)
                            <= t^2 <a,C^2 a>/2.

Partial trace contracts trace distance, and pure-state trace distance is bounded by vector distance. Therefore E_T^2<=4T^2p/h^2, proving (6). Exact local agreement implies the measured X outcome has p=0 at h. The probability of no rejection in N shots is (1-p)^N<=exp(-Np), proving (7).

No candidate many-body simulation or filter is used. Each successful positive-certificate shot requires a fresh vacuum bath supplied by the experimental interface. If its actual reset cost is C_reset, preparation/readout/gate cost is C_local, and native duration is h, the total resource bill includes N(C_reset+C_local) and native duration Nh. Neither vacuum preparation nor gate calibration is asserted to be universally free in the laboratory. This proof is a survival/second-moment argument; that ingredient is not claimed as new.
