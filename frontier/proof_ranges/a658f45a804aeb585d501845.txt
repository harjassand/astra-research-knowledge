INDEPENDENT ABSTRACT GOOD-CENTER RECTIFIABILITY BASELINE
=======================================================
Authoring status: language-model-assisted internal candidate proof.
Date: 2026-10-09. Worker: complexity_proof_recon, existing Sol/max.

0. Result, distinction from the older dimension theorem
-----------------------------------------------------
The claimed conclusion in this baseline is affirmative: under the exact
measure and frequency conventions below, the full good-center set is
countably (m-1)-rectifiable and has sigma-finite H^(m-1). This is stronger
than a Hausdorff-dimension bound. The proof first obtains finite critical
measure on countably many pieces; it never assumes that finiteness in order
to apply a rectifiability theorem.

The new mechanism is an augmented-center covariance estimate. A center z
is lifted to (2P(z,t),z), so varying frequencies are part of the covariance
rather than an additive error. A uniform SECOND eigenvalue gap for the
integrated PSD block then gives a best-(m-1)-plane estimate linear in the
frequency drop. A maximal-density normalization closes the packing step
using a primary discrete Reifenberg theorem. All centers, including
exceptional centers for arbitrary proposed center measures, are covered by
the same countable decomposition.

The older internally audited abstract dimension proof and previous own
work were available background. No conclusion from that proof is needed
as an axiom below. In particular, global positivity of every ball-height
is not imported: positivity is required only where a good-center frequency
is defined. Radial identities are derived directly from the distributions.

1. Exact theorem and notation
----------------------------
Let U=B_10 in R^m, m>=1. The local version on any connected open set follows
by countable room covers. Let nu and d be nonnegative locally finite Radon
measures, f an R^m-valued locally finite Radon measure, beta a symmetric
m-by-m locally finite Radon measure, and mu a signed locally finite Radon
measure. Require the matrix-valued measure

              T = [ nu   f^t ]
                  [ f    beta ]                              (1.1)

to be positive semidefinite: its pairing with every continuous compactly
supported vector field v is nonnegative. Equivalently, after domination by
one positive scalar measure, its matrix Radon--Nikodym density is PSD a.e.
All the following identities hold as distributions on U:

  grad nu=2f,  div f=d=tr beta,  div beta=grad mu.               (1.2)

Here (div beta)_i=sum_j partial_j beta_ij. Require as measure inequalities

            2mu<=d,              |mu|<=C d,                   (1.3)

with one finite C>=0. Assume nu is not the zero measure, although the proof
at a good root uses the more local nonzero height condition.

Fix an integer p>=4 ONCE. Set w=y-z and

  phi_z,r(y)=r^-m (1-|w|^2/r^2)_+^p,
  psi_z,r(y)=2p r^-m (1-|w|^2/r^2)_+^(p-1),
  H(z,r)=int psi_z,r dnu,
  A(z,r)=r^2 int phi_z,r dmu,
  D(z,r)=r^2 int phi_z,r dd,
  R(z,r)=int psi_z,r dbeta[w,w].                               (1.4)

At H>0, write P=A/H, n=D/H, q=R/H and dot=r partial_r. This P normalization
is part of the theorem: a differently normalized frequency cannot be
substituted without checking its radial identities and lower threshold.

Fix N>=1/2. The canonical good set consists of all z in U such that there
is rho_z>0, B_rho_z(z) compactly contained in U, and FOR EVERY 0<r<rho_z,

               H(z,r)>0,          1/2<=P(z,r)<=N.             (1.5)

The conclusion is countable (m-1)-rectifiability in the usual sense of a
cover by countably many Lipschitz images up to an H^(m-1)-null remainder,
and sigma-finiteness of that critical Hausdorff measure. Any Borel subset
Z of this canonical good set inherits both conclusions. Even an arbitrary
subset is contained in the same countable finite-measure rectifiable cover
up to a critical outer-measure-null set. No bound is imposed at centers
outside the good set. If N<1/2, the good set is empty.

We use the diameter-gauge normalization H^k=lim_delta inf sum diam(U_i)^k.
Changing to the standard omega_k/2^k normalization changes only constants.
Write k=m-1. Constants in the proof depend only on m,p,N,C; the spectral
gap constant is obtained by compactness and is not asserted to have an
explicit formula. The cutoff rho_z is allowed to vary pointwise.

2. Radial identities and the exact PSD defect
-------------------------------------------
The kernels vanish to sufficient order at their boundaries; ordinary
smooth approximation justifies their distributional use for Radon data.
From grad nu=2f and div f=d, integration by parts gives

  int psi_z,r w dot df = r^2 int phi_z,r dd = D,
                         dot H=2D.                          (2.1)

For completeness, differentiating H under scaling produces
-int div_y(psi_z,r w) dnu, which equals 2 int psi_z,r w dot df.

Test div beta=grad mu with the vector phi_z,r w. Since
grad phi_z,r=-(psi_z,r/r^2)w, the result is

     int psi_z,r |w|^2 dmu = m A-D+R.

Differentiating A therefore yields

                         dot A=2A-D+R.                      (2.2)

PSD of T and Cauchy--Schwarz give D^2<=HR. Also 2A<=D follows from (1.3).
Consequently, at every good center and good radius,

 n>=2P>=1,     q>=n^2,
 dot P=(q-n^2)+(n-1)(n-2P)>=0.                               (2.3)

The derivative lower bound needed later is stronger than monotonicity:

          dot P >= (q-n^2)+(n-2P)^2.                        (2.4)

Indeed n-1>=n-2P>=0 exactly because 2P>=1. For the continuous vector field
v_z,t(y)=(-2P(z,t),y-z) in R^(m+1), its PSD energy is

 int psi_z,t v_z,t^t dT v_z,t
     =H(z,t)[q-4Pn+4P^2]
     =H(z,t)[(q-n^2)+(n-2P)^2]
     <=H(z,t) dot P(z,t).                                   (2.5)

There is no division by n-2P, n-1, or dot P. Equality/zero-defect radii are
included. In particular, the threshold P=1/2 is included, not approached
only from above. The statement would NOT follow merely by giving P a
nonnegative derivative unrelated to (2.5). The exact radial identities, or
at least the residual bound (2.5) with the mass controls below, are load
bearing. Here all are consequences of (1.1)--(1.5).

3. Root doubling and compactness bounds
--------------------------------------
Work first at a good root 0, with (1.5) valid through radius 8. Normalize
H(0,2)=1 by multiplying the entire tuple by that positive constant's inverse.
Since q>=n^2 and P<=N, (2.3) gives, with K=2N+1,

        dot P=q-(2P+1)n+2P >= q-K sqrt(q) >= q/2-K^2/2.

For any a<b<=8,

 int_a^b q dt/t <=2[P(0,b)-P(0,a)]+K^2 log(b/a),
 int_a^b n dt/t <=sqrt(log(b/a) int_a^b q dt/t).               (3.1)

As dot log H=2n, these give upper and lower bounded-ratio comparisons for
H. In particular, every H(0,t), 1/2<=t<=6, lies between positive constants
depending only on N (and fixed ratios), under H(0,2)=1.

They also bound the tuple's total variation on B_3. The positive lower
bound of psi_0,5 on B_3 bounds nu(B_3) by a multiple of H(0,5). Select one
u in [4,5] where n(0,u) is bounded using (3.1). The positive lower bound of
u^2 phi_0,u on B_3 then bounds d(B_3) by a multiple of D(0,u)=n(0,u)H(0,u).
Since beta is PSD and tr beta=d, every beta component's variation is
bounded by d(B_3). PSD bounds |f|(B_3) by a dimensional multiple of
sqrt(nu(B_3)d(B_3)); |mu|(B_3)<=C d(B_3). These are uniform local weak-star
compactness bounds, with the distributional identities and inequalities
preserved in limits. H(0,1/2)>=c_N>0 prevents inner collapse.

The exact spatial/amplitude scaling used at a general root x and radius r
is as follows. Let F(y)=(y-x)/r and h=H(x,2r). The transformed tuple is

 nu'=r^-m h^-1 F#nu,   f'=r^(1-m) h^-1 F#f,
 beta'=r^(2-m) h^-1 F#beta,
 d'=r^(2-m) h^-1 F#d,  mu'=r^(2-m) h^-1 F#mu.                 (3.2)

This is a positive congruence of the matrix measure by diag(1,r I_m),
times r^-m/h. It preserves every identity and (1.3), gives H'(0,2)=1,
and P'(z,t)=P(x+rz,rt). No weight or dimension-dependent amplitude is
unaccounted for.

4. Uniform second-eigenvalue gap for the integrated block
--------------------------------------------------------
Choose one fixed smooth nonnegative chi, supported in B_(3/2), and equal
to one on B_1. Define the PSD (m+1)-by-(m+1) matrix

                           G=int chi dT.

CLAIM: under the normalized good-root conditions of Section 3, its second
largest eigenvalue satisfies

                        lambda_2(G)>=c(m,p,N,C)>0.           (4.1)

Proof by contradiction. Otherwise take a normalized sequence with that
eigenvalue tending to zero. Section 3 gives weak-star convergence of the
tuple on B_3 along a subsequence. Testing with the fixed kernels preserves
H(0,2)=1, H(0,1/2)>=c_N, and A(0,r)>=H(0,r)/2, A(0,r)<=N H(0,r)
at each r<2. No quotient is taken in a limit before proving its height is
positive. The bounds (3.1) also give a positive limiting height at each
fixed smaller radius, with constants allowed to depend on that fixed ratio.
In particular the limit has nonzero nu in B_(1/2). The limiting G has rank
at most one and has nonzero (0,0) entry.

Let its range be spanned by w=(a,b), a scalar and b in R^m. Necessarily
a!=0, since the (0,0) entry is nonzero. For each constant vector orthogonal
to w its integrated chi-weighted PSD energy is zero. Positivity then makes
the matrix measure itself annihilate that vector wherever chi>0. Therefore
on B_1 the entire limiting block is a positive scalar measure times ww^t.
Writing v=b/a gives there

             f=v nu,   beta=v v^t nu,   d=|v|^2 nu.          (4.2)

The first identity (1.2) says grad nu=2v nu. The second says simultaneously

                  div f=2|v|^2 nu=|v|^2 nu.

Thus v=0 because nu is nonzero locally. Distributionally grad nu=0 on the
connected ball, so nu is a positive constant multiple of Lebesgue measure;
f=beta=d=0 there. The domination |mu|<=C d makes mu=0 there as well. Hence
P(0,r)=0 for r<1, contrary to the inherited P(0,r)>=1/2. This proves (4.1).

This argument rules out rank ONE, not merely a zero block, which is the
precise gap needed for codimension one. No harmonic factorization of T,
pointwise differentiability of nu, or quantitative unique continuation is
used. The condition |mu|<=C d is important here: it rules out an uncharged
constant mu in a rank-one limit.

5. Augmented-center covariance and the L2 plane estimate
-------------------------------------------------------
At scale one let the tuple be normalized as in Section 3. Let eta be ANY
finite positive Borel center measure supported on good centers in B_1.
Require those centers' cutoffs to include 4 and the root's to include 8.
Zero eta is trivial. At each fixed t in [3,4], put

                 w_z(t)=(2P(z,t),z) in R^(m+1),
                 Sigma(t)=int (w_z-wbar)(w_z-wbar)^t deta(z),

where wbar is the eta-mean. Its spatial principal block is the usual
unnormalized center covariance Q=int (z-zbar)(z-zbar)^t deta. Its smallest
spatial eigenvalue is exactly the best-hyperplane squared error:

               lambda_m(Q)=inf_(affine L^(m-1))
                                  int dist(z,L)^2 deta(z).   (5.1)

Cauchy interlacing for the m-by-m principal submatrix gives
lambda_m(Sigma)>=lambda_m(Q). If E is the span of the top m eigenvectors
of Sigma, then Sigma>=lambda_m(Q) Pi_E. Since E has codimension one,

 Tr(Sigma G)>=lambda_m(Q) Tr(Pi_E G)
                >=lambda_m(Q) lambda_2(G)>=c lambda_m(Q).    (5.2)

For v_z,t(y)=(-2P(z,t),y-z), the covariance in z is Sigma (both coordinate
differences have the same minus sign). The variance identity and PSD give

 int deta(z) int chi v_z,t^t dT v_z,t
     =Tr(Sigma G)+eta(R^m) int chi vbar_t^t dT vbar_t
     >=Tr(Sigma G).                                         (5.3)

On spt chi, z in B_1 and t in [3,4], psi_z,t is bounded below by a positive
fixed constant, since |y-z|<=5/2<3. Thus chi<=C_p psi_z,t. Also the whole
support of psi_z,t lies in B_5, where psi_0,6 has a positive lower bound.
Consequently H(z,t)<=C_p H(0,6)<=C_(p,N). Equations (2.5), (5.2), (5.3)
now yield, FOR EACH t in [3,4],

                 lambda_m(Q)<=C int dot P(z,t) deta(z).

Integrating dt/t gives

       lambda_m(Q)<=C int [P(z,4)-P(z,3)] deta(z).             (5.4)

All integrands used for Tonelli are nonnegative. No lower bound on H at
the moving centers, independent of their individual positivity, is needed.

Restore general scales via (3.2) and eta'=r^-k F#eta. If x is good, the
tuple is available on B_(10r)(x), its root cutoff includes 8r, and every
center supporting eta in B_r(x) has cutoff including 4r, then

 beta^k_eta,2(x,r)^2
 :=r^-k-2 inf_(affine L^k) int_(B_r(x)) dist(z,L)^2 deta(z)
 <=C r^-k int_(B_r(x)) [P(z,4r)-P(z,3r)] deta(z).             (5.5)

For unrestricted finite eta, apply the derivation to eta restricted to the
ball; this is exactly the beta-number on the left. The center x need not
belong to the support, but must itself meet the good-root bounds. Later it
is always a center of the tested measure. The estimate is independent of
eta's cardinality, total mass, or prior Hausdorff-density behavior.

7. Packing on a common small-frequency-range piece
-------------------------------------------------
Let E be a Borel set of good centers contained in B_(R0)(a), with
B_(1000R0)(a) compactly contained in U. Suppose there is a common rho with
1000R0<rho and, for EVERY z in E and EVERY 0<r<rho,

           H(z,r)>0,   1/2<=P(z,r)<=N,
                      alpha<=P(z,r)<=alpha+delta.            (7.1)

Take delta>0 to be selected below from only m,p,N,C. For 0<h<=R0 take ANY
finite disjoint collection {B_h(z_i)}, z_i in E. Let

       eta=sum_i h^k delta_(z_i),
       A_*=sup_(x in R^m,t>=h) eta(B_t(x))/t^k.               (7.2)

For a nonempty finite packing, 1<=A_*<infinity. This definition creates a
growth constant; it does not assume its uniform boundedness. All centers
lie in B_(R0)(a), so radii t>4R0 cannot be responsible for values above
A_*/2: compare with the ball B_(2R0)(a) containing all atoms. Choose t in
[h,4R0] and x with eta(B_t(x))>=A_* t^k/2. Choose an atom z0 in that ball,
put R=2t, S=2R, and restrict eta'=eta|B_R(z0). Then

  R<=8R0, S<=16R0, h<=R/2,
  A_* R^k/2^(k+1)<=eta'(R^m)<=A_* R^k.                      (7.3)

Rescale by T0(y)=(y-z0)/S and set

              eta_tilde=(A_* S^k)^-1 (T0#eta').

It is a generalized packing measure of disjoint balls of equal radius
s=h/S<=1/4, centered in B_(1/2); these balls are contained in B_1. Their
coefficients are b_i=1/A_*. Moreover

 ell:=2^(-(2k+1))<=eta_tilde(B_1)<=2^-k<=1,
 eta_tilde(B_t(y))<=t^k for every y and t>=s.                 (7.4)

The latter follows immediately from the defining supremum A_* in (7.2),
even after restriction. Below s, beta at every atom center is zero because
the original balls were disjoint and another atom cannot be in that ball.

Define P_tilde(y,t)=P(z0+Sy,St). Apply (5.5) to eta_tilde at its atom
centers, for s<=t<=2. Its spatial PDE normalization is harmless by (3.2).
All needed rooms exist: 8St<=16S<=256R0<rho and B_(10St) at an atom is
contained in B_(321R0)(a), which is in U. Write
W(y,t)=P_tilde(y,4t)-P_tilde(y,3t)>=0. Tonelli and (7.4) give

 I:=int deta_tilde(x) int_s^2 beta^k_eta_tilde,2(x,t)^2 dt/t
 <=C int deta_tilde(y) int_s^2 W(y,t)
                    eta_tilde(B_t(y))/t^k dt/t
 <=C int deta_tilde(y) int_s^2 W(y,t) dt/t.                  (7.5)

For any nondecreasing F taking values in an interval of length delta,

 int_a^b [F(4t)-F(3t)] dt/t
    <=log(4/3)[F(4b)-F(3a)]<=delta log(4/3).                (7.6)

This follows by integrating its nonnegative logarithmic derivative, or
Stieltjes measure, over strips of width log(4/3). Every radius in (7.6)
is within (7.1). Thus I<=C_1 delta eta_tilde(B_1)<=C_1 delta.

Markov with M=sqrt(delta) gives exceptional eta_tilde mass
Gamma<=C_1 sqrt(delta). Primary DR, with b=1/A_* and zero truncation, yields

 ell<=eta_tilde(B_1)<=c_m/A_*+(c_m+C_1)sqrt(delta).

Choose delta so (c_m+C_1)sqrt(delta)<=ell/2. Then

               A_*<=2c_m/ell=:A0(m,p,N,C).                 (7.7)

This controls EVERY finite equal-radius packing without a cardinality or
preexisting measure bound. In particular, sum_i h^k<=A0(2R0)^k.

To get a local bound, for b<=R0/4 repeat with E intersected with B_b(x),
using an enclosing ball of radius 2b centered at an E point. Its 2000b room
lies in B_(R0+2000b)(a), hence inside B_(1000R0)(a); and 2000b<rho.
For b>R0/4 the total bound controls the mass with only a factor 8^k.
A maximal finite
disjoint h-ball packing centered in that subset yields a covering by 2h
balls. Such a finite maximal packing exists by the ordinary ambient volume
bound, even when E is not closed. The uniform packing bound and h down to
zero give

                  H^k(E intersect B_b(x))<=A1 b^k           (7.8)

for all x and b>0 (enlarging A1 by fixed dimensional constants). In
particular H^k(E)<infinity. There is no attempt to infer finite critical
measure from the older dimension bound, and no hypothesis of sigma-finite
H^k was inserted into DR.

8. Jones square function, density, and countable decomposition
------------------------------------------------------------
Set eta=H^k|E, a finite Borel measure. Its growth bound (7.8) is uniform.
Use (5.5) at x in E for 0<r<=R0. Tonelli, (7.8), and (7.6) give

 int_E int_0^R0 beta^k_eta,2(x,r)^2 dr/r deta(x)
     <=C A1 int_E int_0^R0 [P(y,4r)-P(y,3r)] dr/r deta(y)
     <=C A1 delta log(4/3) eta(E)<infinity.                  (8.1)

Thus the small-scale Jones integral is finite for eta-a.e. x. Remaining
radii in any fixed bounded interval away from zero contribute finitely by
the trivial distance bound and finiteness of eta(E). After rescaling E into
B_1 this meets exactly Import R's integral through radius 2.

Here is a self-contained positive-upper-density check for the diameter
normalization of H^k. If eta(B_r(x))/r^k had limsup zero on a set of positive
eta mass, some subset F of that set would have positive eta mass and satisfy
eta(B_r(x))<=epsilon r^k, all x in F, all r<1/j, with fixed epsilon<1.
Use arbitrarily fine covers of F by sets U_i, select x_i in F intersect U_i,
and enclose that intersection in B_(diam U_i+o(1))(x_i). The ball bound and
countable subadditivity give

                      eta(F)<=epsilon H^k(F).

But F is a measurable subset of E and eta(F)=H^k(F)<infinity, a
contradiction. The sets defined by the ball tests are Borel. Therefore
positive upper k-density holds eta-a.e.; finite upper density follows from
(7.8). Import R now gives countable k-rectifiability of E.

It remains to cover EVERY good center by countably many such E. At each
good z, (2.3) implies existence of

                         P0(z)=lim_(r down to 0) P(z,r)
                               in [1/2,N].                 (8.2)

Choose a common-cutoff level 1/j for the good bounds, shrink it if needed
for distance to the domain boundary, and partition P0 into half-delta
bins [i delta/2,(i+1)delta/2). Since P(z,r) decreases to P0(z) as r decreases,
each point has a further cutoff 1/l below which P(z,r) lies in
[i delta/2,(i+2)delta/2], an interval of length delta. Restrict to countably
many sufficiently small spatial room balls, with radius R0 less than
1/(1000l) and the corresponding interior room. The resulting sets satisfy
(7.1) and cover the entire canonical good set, not just a center-measure
full subset. All these tests can be made at rational radii: kernels and
frequencies are continuous where H>0; dot H=2D>=0 extends positive rational
height tests to all radii. The canonical good set, P0, and its pieces are
Borel. Each piece has finite H^k and is countably k-rectifiable by Sections
7--8. The countable union proves both claimed conclusions for m>=2.

9. Critical dimension zero and zero-frequency edges
--------------------------------------------------
For m=1, beta=d and div beta=grad mu mean mu=d+c dx with a constant c on
the connected interval U. Then 2mu<=d implies 0<=d<=-2c dx, so c<=0. If
c=0, d=mu=0 and nu is an affine density; its nonnegative nonzero affine
profile has no zero inside U, and P is zero, hence there are no good points.

If c<0, write d=w dx. Domination gives |w+c|<=Cw, which implies

               (-c)/(C+1)<=w<=-2c a.e.

The distributions grad nu=2f and div f=d now make nu a C^(1,1) nonnegative
density u with u''=2w>=2(-c)/(C+1)>0. It is strictly convex. At a good z,
dot log H=2n>=2 implies H(z,r)<=H(z,r0)(r/r0)^2 for r<r0. Since the
psi-kernels form a positive approximate identity and u is continuous,
this forces u(z)=0. A nonnegative strictly convex function on a connected
interval has at most one interior zero. Therefore the good set has at
most one point: it is countably 0-rectifiable and has finite H^0. The proof
does not apply a positive-dimensional Reifenberg theorem at k=0.

At a hypothetical spatial dimension m=0, tr beta=d=0 and |mu|<=Cd gives
mu=0, so P=0 at nonzero height; there is no good center. More generally,
no homogeneous or residual branch with zero energy is used as a paying
branch: d=0 on an inner ball forces mu=0 there, and its root cannot satisfy
P>=1/2. The zero-defect equality cases in dimensions m>=1 remain included
through (2.4), without division by the defect. These cover the s=0 or
zero-dimensional edge of a dimension-reduction interpretation.

10. Assumption ledger and exact boundary of the result
------------------------------------------------------
* Pointwise in all centers: every claimed good center must have (1.5) at
  EVERY sufficiently small radius. There is no a.e.-center replacement.
* The cutoff can depend on the center. Countable decomposition removes
