2. An exact restriction on proposed chemical histories
The useful starting point is to track four molecular populations, not just one abundance ratio: L and D glutamate, each containing either \(^{14}\mathrm N\) or \(^{15}\mathrm N\).
Let \(L_j,D_j\) denote those populations and define
\[
n_j=L_j+D_j,\qquad
m_j=L_j-D_j,\qquad
e_j=\frac{m_j}{n_j}.
\]
Here \(e_j\) measures the handedness imbalance within one isotope class.
Isotope delta values first need conversion to heavy-atom fractions:
\[
R(\delta)=R_{\rm AIR}\left(1+\frac{\delta}{1000}\right),
\qquad
q(\delta)=\frac{R(\delta)}{1+R(\delta)}.
\]
It is \(q\), not \(\delta\), that mixes linearly.
At the published central values, the calculation gives
\[
e_{14}=0.0527903,\qquad e_{15}=0.0175575.
\]
Both isotope classes therefore retain an L excess at those central values, but the heavy-nitrogen class has less.
Racemization does not erase every kind of information independently
For a closed pool undergoing symmetric racemization,
\[
\dot L_j=k_j(D_j-L_j),\qquad
\dot D_j=k_j(L_j-D_j).
\]
Therefore
\[
n_j(t)=n_j(0),\qquad
m_j(t)=m_j(0)e^{-2K_j},
\qquad
K_j=\int k_j(t)\,dt.
\]
When racemization is isotope-independent, both isotope classes’ handedness imbalances contract by the same factor. Consequently, one cannot simply assume that extensive racemization erased an abundance imbalance while leaving an arbitrary isotope–handedness difference intact.
There is also a sharp positivity constraint:
\[
\boxed{
2K\leq-\ln\!\left(\max\{|e_{14}|,|e_{15}|\}\right).
}
\]
At the printed central values, this allows at most 4.24 handedness-halving times since the relevant initial state.
This is not an age estimate for Bennu’s molecules. It assumes a closed pool without later formation, exchange, selective destruction or protected compartments. It is also a central-value calculation, not a confidence bound.
A source-mixture invariant
Consider a racemic background plus one L-biased source, followed by isotope-independent racemization. With \(d=D/L\), the heavy-nitrogen fraction associated with the excess is
\[
\boxed{
q_{\rm excess}=\frac{q_L-dq_D}{1-d}.
}
\]
The subsequent racemization cannot change this ratio under the stated assumptions. At the three published central values, the source would require
\[
\delta^{15}N_{\rm excess}\approx-590.5\,‰.
\]
That initially looked like a strong discriminator against an ordinary terrestrial L-amino-acid contribution. However, this is an inferred source composition under a model—not a measured nitrogen reservoir. The estimate becomes unstable as the abundance ratio approaches one.
Static isotope-of-excess calculations already have meteoritic precedents. I have not relabelled that existing operation as a new foundational principle. Wiley Online Library
3. Measurement uncertainty defeats the apparent exclusion
I tested the exact source-mixture relation against the measurement summaries rather than treating their central values as exact.
For illustration, restrict the source to a nitrogen isotope value between \(-20\) and \(+30\,‰\), a typical terrestrial range discussed in the analytical literature—not an absolute bound on all terrestrial material. Analytical Science Journals
One fitted combination is:
Quantity	Published central value	Compatible model value
D/L	0.900	0.8124
D nitrogen isotope value	\(277\,‰\)	\(275.82\,‰\)
L nitrogen isotope value	\(190\,‰\)	\(220.29\,‰\)
Source isotope value	Not measured	\(-20\,‰\)


Each fitted measurement lies within one of its respective quoted spreads. Those spreads have different definitions, so this is not a joint confidence statement.
I also repeated the calculation using \(0.1/\sqrt6\) as an illustrative standard error for the D/L mean. A typical-isotope source remained compatible with modest shifts in the nitrogen measurements. The full calculations retain the assumptions explicitly; no calibrated likelihood was reconstructed from unavailable raw data.
This prevents a claim that the published summaries require exotic chemistry. It does not establish contamination, negate the reported blanks, or overturn the experimental paper’s results for other molecules.
