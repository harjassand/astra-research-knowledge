# Exact finite seed and compressed shared-quantile coupling

Status: newly derived corollaries and an independent reconstruction of the parent compiler interface. The whole-path rounding and simultaneous filler proof are in `FINITE_POPULATION_THEOREM.md`; the parent and another worker independently reconstructed those arguments. This note does not change the previous source statements. No priority claim is made.

## 1. Model and parameters

Let a deterministic automaton have state set S of size d, alphabet A of size m, start s0, and transition f(s,a). Write the prescribed positive rational marginal as

    mu(a)=u_a/Q,   sum_a u_a=Q,   u_min=min_a u_a>=1.

Here Q is an integer common denominator, not a state set. Take a population P>=1 and put

    beta=dQ/(P u_min+dQ),
    r=d/(2P),
    W=dQ^2,
    R=Q(P u_min+dQ).

The relaxed histogram graph has H=binom(P+d-1,d-1) vertices. An edge e has allocation k_e(s,a), label counts v_e(a)=sum_s k_e(s,a), and |v_e(a)-P mu(a)|<=d.

## 2. A single quantile repairs arbitrarily many marginals

The normal branch is the uniform law on P particle words along any relaxed graph path. At an occurrence of edge e its marginal is v_e(a)/P. The filler marginal is

    q_e(a)=[mu(a)-(1-beta)v_e(a)/P]/beta
           =[u_a(P u_min+dQ)-Q u_min v_e(a)]/(dQ^2).

Its numerator is nonnegative: the relaxed budget gives

    u_a(P u_min+dQ)-Q u_min v_e(a)
    >= dQ(u_a-u_min)>=0.

The numerators sum to W. Fix an alphabet order and let q_e cumulative sums be b_e(a)/W. Sample one integer J uniformly from {0,...,W-1}. At EVERY occurrence of edge e output the letter whose half-open cumulative interval contains J/W. This gives marginal q_e at each occurrence, even when edge occurrences are repeated or adaptively queried. The same J may be reused everywhere because the model prescribes only one-coordinate marginals. No conditional transition probability, independence, or joint filler law is specified.

Mix this filler law with the P normal words in weights beta and 1-beta. Each input marginal is exactly mu. If g is the normal terminal histogram, the repaired law p' obeys TV(p',g)<=beta. This is one ordinary finite joint input law, not a collection of independently chosen answers to queries.

## 3. Uniform integer seed, exact quotas, bounded support

Every normal word has mass

    (1-beta)/P=u_min/(P u_min+dQ)=Q u_min/R.

Every one of the W quantile filler words has mass

    beta/W=1/R.

Consequently the complete repaired coupling is the uniform law on R words, with repetitions: take Q u_min copies of every normal word and one copy of every quantile word. At every coordinate its number of occurrences of letter a is exactly

    Q u_min v_e(a)+[u_a(P u_min+dQ)-Q u_min v_e(a)]
    =u_a(P u_min+dQ)=R mu(a).

This exact count verifies the law directly, independently of the mixture calculation. Its terminal law has denominator dividing R.

At most P+W distinct words are needed. If one allocation is chosen for every ordered histogram pair, then q_e depends only on that graph edge. Partition [0,1) at ALL such edge cumulative breakpoints. There are at most

    K<=min(W,(m-1)H^2+1)

nonempty intervals; each interval gives just one filler word. The support bound improves to P+K. Replacing different allocations on repeated occurrences of the same histogram edge by the canonical allocation does not change ANY histogram along the path, including the terminal histogram.

Combining this with whole-path rounding proves the following exact statement.

**Finite-seed theorem.** For every n>=1 and every terminal law p attainable at horizon n with each Xi~mu, there exists a joint input law at the SAME horizon with each Xi~mu exactly, terminal law p', and

    TV(p,p')<=d/(2P)+dQ/(P u_min+dQ),

such that p' is realized by a uniform R-word law and at most P+min(W,(m-1)H^2+1) distinct words. R and the support bound do not depend on n. No approximation to the full joint input law is asserted.

For P=ceil(2dQ/(u_min epsilon)), the terminal bound is at most 3epsilon/4 and

    R<=dQ^2(2/epsilon+1)+Q u_min.

Thus the separate inverse-smallest-marginal factor cancels from the exact seed denominator. The common denominator Q may be exponentially large in its binary encoding; this is not a polynomial-size population assertion in that encoding.

An exact uniform seed in {0,...,R-1} can be sampled by binary rejection using fewer than 2 ceil(log2 R) random bits in expectation (R=1 needs none). This is an expected-cost statement; a non-dyadic exact law need not admit a deterministic bound on the number of fair coin flips.

## 4. Exact-quota graph corollary

Take the population graph on R particles with EXACT label counts R mu(a) at every edge. Every path defines an actually feasible coupling. The finite-seed theorem gives an inner approximation at the same horizon:

    I_(R,n) subset R_n,
    sup_(p in R_n) dist_TV(p,I_(R,n))<=r+beta.

Here I_(R,n) consists of the empirical terminal histograms of exact-quota graph paths of length n from R delta_s0. This corollary can be useful as a certificate with no filler branch. It does not improve the efficient compiler automatically: its population R can be substantially larger than P. The smaller relaxed graph plus the explicit filler remains the principal algorithm.

## 5. Parent Boolean-power witness DAG: normal branch

Choose one relaxed allocation per ordered edge. Give each histogram x canonical slots 1,...,P, grouped by state. For edge e:x->y, divide the x-state slots into its allocation bins (s,a), and send each source slot bijectively to canonical destination slots grouped by f(s,a), ordered by source state, letter and rank. This gives a true permutation sigma_e of P slots and a letter function a_e on source slots.

A length-2^j reachability witness for (x,y) chooses a midpoint z and concatenates the witnesses (x,z,2^(j-1)) and (z,y,2^(j-1)). Equal midpoint histograms give identical canonical slot spaces, so

    sigma_node=sigma_right composed with sigma_left.

There are at most H^2 nodes at each level and O(log n) levels. A general binary horizon is a concatenation of at most log2 n+1 power witnesses. Reusing nodes and edges is harmless: they denote deterministic slot maps, not fresh random draws.

Sample one initial slot uniformly. A coordinate query descends the concatenation DAG. When skipping a left child, update the slot by its stored permutation. At a leaf output a_e(current slot). This is exactly one deterministic word for the selected initial slot. It gives consistent repeated and adaptive coordinate queries, and the source slot at every leaf remains uniform. Cache all node permutations using O(P H^2 log n) slot entries; a coordinate uses O(log n) table lookups, plus ordinary index arithmetic. The terminal state is the state block containing the final slot.

## 6. Filler branch: terminal state and random access

For a sampled quantile interval I, every graph edge e has one fixed filler letter a_e(I). Its automaton transition is the TOTAL state map

    F_(e,I):s -> f(s,a_e(I)).

These maps act on every automaton state; no relation to the histogram of e is required. Compose them on the SAME witness DAG. Thus the midpoint histogram identities that justify normal particle permutations impose no restriction on filler trajectories. Repeated edges use the same letter, as required by the shared quantile law.

Composing d-state maps at all witness nodes costs O(d H^2 log n) table entries/work for one sampled interval. It gives the exact terminal state of that filler word and a coordinate query still descends to one leaf. No independent-coordinate cache is needed. The terminal state and all queried letters belong to the same deterministic filler word.

## 7. Exact terminal law without traversing n positions

The edge CDF breakpoints have common denominator W. Sort the at most (m-1)H^2 breakpoints and retain the K nonempty intervals. Let their integer widths be b_I, so sum_I b_I=W. On each interval the entire filler word and its terminal state are constant. Compose maps as above for each interval, and add mass

    beta b_I/W=b_I/R

at that terminal state. Add the P normal contributions Q u_min/R at their terminal states. This computes the EXACT terminal law of the compiled coupling in time polynomial in the explicit compiled graph, P, d, m, log n and rational bit lengths, with no iteration over n or W required. A direct bound for the map compositions is O(K d H^2 log n); the factor K is required when computing the whole law, whereas one sampled filler trajectory needs only O(d H^2 log n). The interval computations can be streamed to avoid storing all K map tables simultaneously.

An interval can be sampled with its exact rational mass rather than sampling an infinite real. Equivalently, the uniform integer seed of Section 3 chooses a normal word or a filler interval according to its integer multiplicity. All sampling statements include the finite-seed/random-bit cost. Outputting the complete length-n word still requires Omega(n) output work; the result supplies compressed storage, exact terminal evaluation, and random coordinate access.

## 8. Gates checked and limits

The critical checks are: the marginal repair constrains only unconditional single-site laws; canonical midpoint slot maps compose; filler maps are defined on ALL states; repeated graph edges reuse the same quantile; rational interval widths give the correct complete-law denominator; and whole-terminal-law compilation includes the K interval factor. Each gate reconstructs directly from the definitions above.

No claim is made for prescribed conditional transition kernels, blind PFA controls, approximation of the full input joint law, or a sampler that emits n symbols in sublinear output time. No imported theorem is needed beyond the whole-path integral-flow rounding proved in the companion note.
