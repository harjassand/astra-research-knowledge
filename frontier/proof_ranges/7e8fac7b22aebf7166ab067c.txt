# A coupled product class and the exact obstruction to general phase averaging

Date: 15 October 2026. Separate continuation after the frozen shear theorem.

## 1. An exact failure of unrestricted density averaging

The conditional-density theorem relies on the phase gradient being invariant in the averaged coordinate. Removing that hypothesis is false even for a convex integrand.

Take F(A)=|A|^4 on the two-dimensional torus and fields independent of x:

    theta(y)=sin y,
    r(y)=1+epsilon cos(2y),
    phi=r exp(i theta),
    R=(E_y r^4)^(1/4).

For |epsilon|<1 the amplitude is smooth and positive. Direct exact integration gives

    E F(Dphi)
      =3/8+epsilon+(53/16)epsilon^2
             +(7/4)epsilon^3+(427/64)epsilon^4,
    E F(D(R exp(i theta)))=(3/8)E r^4.

Their difference is

    epsilon(209epsilon^3+56epsilon^2+70epsilon+32)/32.

At epsilon=-1/100 this equals

    -31305391/3200000000 <0.

Thus replacing the density by its transverse average **increases** the energy. The original field is one-dimensional and has nonnegative convex energy; this is a counterexample only to the proposed averaging operation, not to quasiconvexity.

Consequently the new shear theorem cannot be extended to arbitrary phases by simply averaging a different coordinate. An orbit average must retain the local phase-gradient coefficients. A phase such as theta=sin x sin y has no nonzero constant direction along which its gradient is invariant: its Hessian at (pi/2,pi/2) is -I, which annihilates no nonzero direction.

Nonlinear changes to phase coordinates introduce a nonconstant spatial Jacobian and right-coordinate transformation of the matrix gradient. Left-output invariance does not remove those terms. No general energy-decreasing phase-coordinate change is proved here.

## 2. A genuinely coupled phase class with separable complex factors

There is nevertheless a different exact compatible class beyond shear phases.

### Theorem 2.1

Let F be a left-SO(2)-invariant rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic, with minimum rank-one value mu>=0. Let z(x) and w(y) be arbitrary complex periodic W^(1,4) functions of their respective coordinates. Put

    phi(x,y)=z(x)w(y).

Then

    E F(Dphi) >= c_* mu E|Dphi|^4,
    c_*=(1+2*3^(1/3))^(-3)>1/60.

If either z or w has an exact half-period sign reversal, then the same bound holds for every affine Jensen gap E[F(B+Dphi)-F(B)].

The phase can vary nonlinearly in both coordinates. The amplitude is restricted by complex product separability, so the theorem does not cover arbitrary amplitudes at a prescribed coupled phase.

### Proof

First suppose both factors avoid zero. Write z=R exp(i alpha), w=S exp(i beta), using only their well-defined logarithmic derivatives; a globally periodic branch of alpha or beta is unnecessary. Remove the output rotation exp(i(alpha+beta)). The two gradient columns are

    RS (Z(x)|W(y)),
    Z=z'/z, W=w'/w.

By quartic homogeneity the energy is averaged with the product weight R^4 S^4. Let M_z=E R^4 and M_w=E S^4 and normalize these two independent weights to probabilities. Periodicity gives

    E_(R^4) Re Z=0, E_(S^4) Re W=0.

Let their complex weighted means be i tau and i eta. For each fixed W, apply rank-one convexity in the first full column, retaining the quartic Bregman bound mu/3. Then apply the same argument in the second column:

    E F(Dphi) >= mu A0+(mu/3)A1+(mu/3)A2,

where

    A0=M_z M_w (tau^2+eta^2)^2,
    A1=M_z M_w E_(R^4) |Z-i tau|^4,
    A2=M_z M_w E_(S^4) |W-i eta|^4.

The averaged matrix (i tau|i eta) has rank at most one. This justifies the first term. The two tangent terms vanish under the corresponding conditional mean.

The original gradient is the sum of the rotated/scaled mean matrix and its two one-column deviations. Their integrated fourth powers are exactly A0,A1,A2. Minkowski and weighted Holder give

    (E|Dphi|^4)^(1/4)<=A0^(1/4)+A1^(1/4)+A2^(1/4),
    A0+(A1+A2)/3
       >=(A0^(1/4)+A1^(1/4)+A2^(1/4))^4
             /(1+2*3^(1/3))^3.

This proves the bound.

For zeros, approximate each factor by smooth curves and then by small constant complex translations that avoid zero. A smooth closed curve has planar area zero, so such translations exist arbitrarily close to zero. Strong one-dimensional W^(1,4) convergence gives uniform convergence of the factors and strong W^(1,4) convergence of their product map in two dimensions. Quartic energy passes to the limit.

If one factor has a half-period sign reversal, Dphi is antipodal under that shift. Odd background terms vanish; the constant rank-one-positive Hessian quadratic is nonnegative on every periodic gradient by Fourier/Parseval. This is exactly the same all-background transfer used in the frozen shear theorem. QED.

### Explicit coupled example

Take

    z(x)=cos x+i sin(3x),
    w(y)=1+(1/2)exp(iy).

The first factor avoids zero and changes sign under x->x+pi. The phase of the second factor is non-affine, as is the phase of the first. Thus the full phase generally depends nonlinearly on both coordinates, while the exact all-background theorem applies.

The separate checker computes its G energy and fourth gradient norm exactly from Laurent coefficients. This finite example illustrates the theorem; it is not its proof.

### Why density mixtures do not close the general problem

A general positive density might be approximated by sums of separable densities. That observation does not transfer Theorem 2.1 to arbitrary amplitudes. For a fixed phase, density convexity gives an **upper** bound on the energy of a convex mixture by the mixture of component energies. Positive energy on the separate components does not provide the required lower bound on their mixture. No tensor-density completion theorem is asserted.

### Convex amplitude elimination with concentrated positive densities

search_concentrated_density.py used a periodic cardinal cubic B-spline density with 8 knots in each coordinate. Coefficients have fixed sum64 and lower bound10^-4, so density positivity follows from the nonnegative partition of unity rather than sampled positivity. This permits substantial concentration beyond the incoming Fourier l1 cap.

Three structurally chosen phases were tested:

    shear control: theta=y+(1/2)sin(2x),
    separable fold: theta=sin x+sin y,
    crossed fold: theta=sin x sin y.

The density objective is convex by the incoming theorem. Spectral projected gradient and an Armijo line search were bounded by1500 iterations; actual completed counts were5,141,63. Six-point cell Gauss objectives were reevaluated with ten points per cell. Final exploratory values were about7.52788,3.05516,1.00277, respectively. No negative candidate was found.

The saved Frank-Wolfe gaps concern only the floating quadrature finite-dimensional programs. They are not continuum lower certificates. The separable-fold density reached maximum coefficient about11.45 while maintaining positive coefficients; no absence of a concentrating counterexample follows.

### Resonant soft-Hessian maps with every affine direction optimized

After reading phase_geometry/TRANSLATION_GAUGE_AND_SOFT_OPERATOR.md, a complementary many-mode attack used

    phi=((2/3)v_y,v_x)

with48 real Fourier coefficients for scalar v and frequency indices at most3 in each coordinate. Thus the mixed-derivative compatibility and soft relation are exact at representation level. A32x32 spectral sum resolves every quartic product for this representation. Arithmetic remains floating.

For every coefficient iterate, all four affine-background entries were minimized by the exact moment formula

    B_opt=-H^(-1)b,
    H=E D^2G(Dphi), b=E DG(Dphi).

Numerically nearly singular H was rejected rather than silently divided by. Gradient formulas used the envelope derivative and were checked independently by a central directional derivative before optimization.

Three deterministic seeds completed67,106,82 iterations. The smallest obtained optimized Jensen-gap/fourth-norm ratio was about2.16695, positive. No negative rational reconstruction was attempted because no negative candidate existed. The corresponding fixed-I soft discriminant ratio was about0.0018367, also far below the negative-scaling threshold1, but this is an observed candidate value rather than an upper bound.

The results are in soft_hessian_search.json. They do not supply a global lower bound for the Hessian class, establish the optimum of the Fourier program, or close the nonlinear cubic/K estimate.

These bounded failures do not justify more identical optimization starts. A further attack needs a new compensated Hessian inequality or geometry giving a negative candidate.
