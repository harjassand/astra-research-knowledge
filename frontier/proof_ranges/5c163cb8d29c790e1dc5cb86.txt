## 4. Witness/state pair and the linear cone obstruction

Take a realization with ||G||<5, Tr G^2>9d^2/40 and q_k<1/64, and put

\[
 \rho=\frac{I+G/6}{d^2},\qquad W=I-32G. \tag{10}
\]

The exact symmetries and partial traces in Section 2 give rho^Gamma=rho, trace one, and both marginals I/d. Its spectrum is in [1/(6d^2),11/(6d^2)]. For a normalized vector of Schmidt rank at most k,

\[
 \langle x,Wx\rangle\ge1-32q_k>1/2. \tag{11}
\]

Since W^Gamma=W, both W and W^Gamma are k-block positive. Every tau=A+B^Gamma in C_k consequently satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W\tau)\ge1/2,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(G\tau)\le1/64.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W\rho)
 =1-\frac{32}{6d^2}\operatorname{Tr}G^2
 <-1/5,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(G\rho)>3/80.
\]

Since ||G||<=5, duality of operator and trace norm gives

\[
 \frac12\|\rho-\tau\|_1
 \ge\frac{(3/80)-(1/64)}{10}=7/3200.
\]

This proves Theorem B's state claim. Under the Choi convention, S_k corresponds exactly to SP_k: a pure Choi vector corresponds to a Kraus operator with rank equal to that vector's Schmidt rank; sum the rank-one Choi decompositions. The channel statement follows with J(Lambda)=d rho, without importing a conjectural channel theorem.

The witness W itself defines the map P with J(P)=W/d. It is k-positive and k-copositive by (11). This equivalence can also be seen directly by compressing the first Choi factor to k-dimensional subspaces: those compressions are positive iff all Schmidt-rank-at-most-k quadratic forms are nonnegative.

A decomposable Choi matrix A+B^Gamma has nonnegative pairing with every PPT state. The negative pairing with rho therefore proves that P is indecomposable. Its partial traces are I, making P unital and trace preserving. Its input and output transpose invariances follow term by term from (1).

Explicitly,

\[
 P(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\frac{I}{d}
       -\frac{32}{d^2}\sum_{a,b}g_{ab}\operatorname{Tr}(S_aX)S_b. \tag{12}
\]

For any decomposable map D,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[\rho J(D)]\ge0,\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}[\rho J(P)]\le-1/(5d),\quad
 \|\rho\|_\infty\le11/(6d^2).
\]

It follows that ||J(P)-J(D)||_1>=6d/55. Evaluate P-D on one half of a normalized maximally entangled state to conclude

\[
 \|P-D\|_\diamond\ge\frac1d\|J(P)-J(D)\|_1\ge6/55. \tag{13}
\]

Finally K(d)>=floor(d/C)+1 follows from the counterexample for that k. K(d)<=d follows because every positive Choi matrix has Schmidt number at most d and every PPT map is CP. This is an asymptotic-order answer, not an exact finite-dimensional classification.

## 5. An elementary dimension-asymmetric separable ball

**Lemma.** If E=E* acts on C^r tensor C^b and

\[
 \|E\|_\infty\le\epsilon\le\frac1{2r-1},
\]

then I+E is separable. The dimension b does not enter the bound.

**Proof.** Write E as r-by-r blocks E_ij. Each block has operator norm at most epsilon. For i<j define

\[
 H_{ij}=(E_{ij}+E_{ij}^*)/2,\quad
 K_{ij}=(E_{ij}-E_{ij}^*)/(2i),
\]

both Hermitian contractions of norm at most epsilon. Let P_ij=|i><i|+|j><j|, X_ij=|i><j|+|j><i|, and Y_ij=i(|i><j|-|j><i|). The two matrices

\[
 \epsilon P_{ij}\otimes I+X_{ij}\otimes H_{ij},\quad
 \epsilon P_{ij}\otimes I+Y_{ij}\otimes K_{ij}
\]

are separable. For example the first equals

\[
 \frac{P_{ij}+X_{ij}}2\otimes(\epsilon I+H_{ij})
 +\frac{P_{ij}-X_{ij}}2\otimes(\epsilon I-H_{ij}),
\]

a sum of positive tensors. The analogous identity holds for Y and K. Sum these over i<j. Their off-diagonal blocks reproduce all of E's off-diagonal blocks. The remaining diagonal block i is

\[
 I+E_{ii}-2\epsilon(r-1)I\succeq[1-(2r-1)\epsilon]I\succeq0.
\]

Those diagonal blocks are themselves separable tensors. This proves the lemma. No commutativity of the blocks is assumed. The result is not claimed to be an optimal or historically new separable-ball radius.

## 6. From small Schmidt norm to genuine r-EB maps

Now choose the good realization under d>=C r^3 and q_r(G)<=1/(64r). Keep W, rho, P from (10),(12).

For every complex isometry U:C^s -> C^d with s<=r, any vector in the range of U tensor I has Schmidt rank at most s. Therefore

\[
 \|(U^*\otimes I)G(U\otimes I)\|_\infty\le q_s(G)\le q_r(G).
\]

Consequently the first-factor compression of W is

\[
 (U^*\otimes I)W(U\otimes I)=I_s\otimes I_d+E_U,
\qquad \|E_U\|_\infty\le1/(2r)\le1/(2s-1).
\]

By Section 5 **every such compression is separable**, not merely positive or PPT.

To see that this is precisely enough for r-EB, take any pure input vector psi in C^r tensor C^d, of Schmidt rank s<=r. It can be written as

\[
 \psi=(A\otimes V)\Omega_s,
\]

where V:C^s -> C^d is an isometry, A:C^s -> C^r is arbitrary, and Omega_s=sum_i e_i tensor e_i is unnormalized. Direct expansion of the matrix units gives

\[
 (\operatorname{id}_r\otimes P)(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)
 =(A\otimes I)(V^T\otimes I)J(P)(\overline V\otimes I)(A^*\otimes I). \tag{14}
\]

The middle factor is the compression with U=overline V, divided by d. It is separable; the outer local filter preserves separability. Every positive input is a finite sum of such pure projectors, proving r-EB.

The adjoint map has coefficient matrix g^T. Its Choi perturbation is obtained by swapping the two tensor factors of G (because G is real). Swap preserves the Schmidt ranks in (6), the operator norm, and the exact transpose symmetries. The same argument proves r-EB for P*.

Indecomposability and the diamond-norm gap follow from Section 4, since q_r<=1/(64r)<=1/64. This proves Theorem A.

## 7. Why this is stronger than high Schmidt number alone

A PPT state can have a large Schmidt number and still be expressible as A+B^Gamma with low-Schmidt A,B. It is not enough to exhibit high Schmidt number for rho, or even separately for rho and rho^Gamma. The critical property here is **one separating witness valid on both low-Schmidt cones at once**, followed by the stronger separable-compression property in Theorem A.

The fixed-reference conclusion is about all inputs with a reference of that size, including complex Schmidt bases and arbitrarily singular local filters. It is not a collection of sampled input tests. Conversely, letting r tend to infinity while also changing d and P does not construct a fixed finite-dimensional tensor-stable map.

The theorem makes no claim that P tensor P, let alone every tensor power of P, is positive. Neither k-positivity nor r-EB at finite r licenses that inference. It also does not prove that the physical PPT channel Lambda has a non-entanglement-breaking cube. Failure of a sufficient low-Schmidt decomposition is not failure of that cube conclusion.

## 8. Existence, exact coefficients, and computational costs

The proof specifies an ordinary finite-dimensional Gaussian distribution and proves a positive-probability open set of successful coefficients. It is an existence construction, not a stored example at d=67,108,864.

Rational realizations also exist. The space V_d tensor V_d has a basis of rational matrices; rational coordinates are dense. Strict positivity, the negative witness pairing, and the strict uniform low-rank bound are open conditions in this finite-dimensional space. A sufficiently close rational perturbation therefore retains every conclusion. The orthonormal basis is used only to prove Gaussian estimates, not to assert irrational coefficients are necessary.

In principle one can enumerate rational matrices in that subspace and test the strict uniform low-rank bound using real quantifier elimination: real and imaginary coordinates, unit norms, rank minors and the squared bilinear inequality form polynomial constraints. Positivity and the moment conditions are also finite algebraic tests. Such an enumeration eventually terminates. It has not been executed; no useful time, memory, bit-complexity, or laboratory preparation bound is asserted. A practical high-probability random draw still requires O(d^4) real coefficients to store densely.

The theorem's universal inequalities are justified analytically by the net proof. Small-dimensional numerical low-rank searches cannot certify them, and the supplied software expressly does not treat those searches as certificates.
