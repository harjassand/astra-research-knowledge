Let R=p tensor q be any nonzero real rank-one matrix. Choose U in SO(2)
with `Uq=|q|e1`. Then

\[
URU^T=(\operatorname{tr}R,\ R_{21}-R_{12})\otimes e_1. \tag{1}
\]

Indeed the first coordinate of `|q|Up` is p.q, and the second is
`det(q,p)=p2 q1-p1 q2`. These are the two displayed quantities.

There are exactly three cases, up to multiplying the direction by a
nonzero scalar (which preserves an acoustic zero):

1. `R21-R12 != 0` and `tr R=0`: the direction becomes e21. A 90-degree
   rotation conjugates it to -e12, the inherited shear contact.
2. `R21-R12=0`: R is symmetric and rank one. Hence it is a nonzero
   real multiple of p tensor p; orthogonal conjugation and scaling give
   e11, the pinned nonshear contact. Its trace is necessarily nonzero.
3. Both quantities are nonzero: scaling (1) gives `(t,1) tensor e1`,
   with `t=tr R/(R21-R12) != 0`, precisely the generic theorem in REPORT.md.

No nonzero rank-one direction has both quantities zero: a symmetric
trace-zero real rank-one matrix would have its only nonzero eigenvalue
equal to zero, a contradiction. This also follows from
`|R|^2=(tr R)^2+(R21-R12)^2` on `det R=0`.

For any orthogonal U, define `F_U(A)=F(U^T A U)`. If F is left-SO(2)
invariant, then so is F_U, since `U^T Q U in SO(2)` for every Q in SO(2).
Also

\[
H(A)=(|A|^2-2\det A)^2=H(U^TAU). \tag{2}
\]

The chain rule preserves the acoustic inequality and its zeros. For QC,
transform the domain and perturbation by
`Omega_U=U Omega`, `phi_U(x)=U phi(U^T x)`. Then
`Dphi_U(x)=U Dphi(U^T x) U^T`, and Lebesgue measure, boundary trace,
Sobolev norms, and all background matrices are preserved. Consequently
the normalization preserves **existence of completion along the same H**,
not merely along a transported completion polynomial.

## Strict conformal LH alone gives finite-H QC completion

Let G be any left-SO(2)-invariant real homogeneous quartic. Suppose

\[
h_G(I;R):=\tfrac12D^2G(I)[R,R]\ge\delta |R|^2
\quad\text{for every rank-one }R,
\qquad\delta>0. \tag{3}
\]

Write `G(X)=T(X,X,X,X)` and let M bound the four-linear norm of T.
For any background B, rotate the target to write `B=sI+E`, with
`s>=0` and E anticonformal. The exact acoustic expansion and the tensor
bound give

\[
h_G(sI+E;R)\ge
\left(\delta s^2-12\sqrt2 M s|E|-6M|E|^2\right)|R|^2.
\]

Young's inequality therefore gives

\[
h_G(sI+E;R)\ge
\left(\frac\delta2s^2-C|E|^2\right)|R|^2,
\qquad C=144M^2/\delta+6M. \tag{4}
\]

For rank-one R, `|CR|^2=|R|^2` and `|CE|^2=2|E|^2`. Hence

\[
h_H(sI+E;R)
=2|CE|^2|CR|^2+4(CE.CR)^2
\ge4|E|^2|R|^2. \tag{5}
\]

Taking

\[
\alpha_0=(C+\delta/4)/4
\]

makes `R_0=G+alpha_0 H` satisfy

\[
\tfrac12D^2R_0(B)[R,R]\ge
\frac\delta4|B|^2|R|^2,
\]

because `|B|^2=2s^2+|E|^2`. Thus R_0 satisfies the strict homogeneous
Legendre-Hadamard premise of Iwaniec--Kristensen (2005), Theorem 3.1
at p=4. H is strongly quasiconvex by their elliptic-operator Lemma 4.1,
or by the explicit Cauchy-Riemann proof in REPORT.md. Their theorem now
gives finite `beta>=0` such that `R_0+beta H` is quasiconvex.
This uses the classical theorem only after its strict hypothesis has
actually been established.

The constants M and delta are mathematical bounds here. Acquiring an
effective delta from arbitrary coefficient data is a separate algorithmic
issue; no oracle or optimal completion threshold is asserted by this lemma.

## Conditional full-projection composition

Let V be the full nine-dimensional invariant space and pi the quotient
map by `span{H}`. Assume the two pinned exact existence statements on
the shear and parallel contact strata, together with REPORT.md's generic
theorem. Then

\[
\pi(\mathrm{ROC}\cap V)=\pi(\mathrm{QC}\cap V). \tag{6}
\]

To see the nontrivial inclusion, suppose `G+alpha H` is ROC for some
finite alpha. Since `D^2H(I)=0`, its conformal acoustic form is exactly
that of G. It is nonnegative on rank-one directions. If it is strictly
positive on every unit rank-one direction, compactness provides delta>0
and (3)--(5) give a QC completion. Otherwise a nonzero rank-one acoustic
zero exists at I. Apply the exhaustive normalization (1). The corresponding
contact-span theorem puts the quartic in one of the three exact contact
slices; that slice's existence equivalence supplies a QC completion.
Equation (2) transports it back along the same H. The reverse inclusion
is the usual QC-to-ROC implication.

This composition establishes equality of existential projections only.
It makes no statement that a particular ROC coefficient alpha already
gives QC, no least-alpha statement, and no effective cost claim for finding
a contact or a strictness certificate from arbitrary real coefficients.
The root is responsible for admitting and independently auditing the full
composition and its inherited premises before promoting (6).
