## 9. Experiments, failed transfers, and the remaining mechanism

Two distinct exploratory routes were run before the analytic argument was isolated.

**Constructive dissipation.** The computation allowed unequal orientation terms, general cubic differential primitives, variable positive input metrics, and output-rotating diffusion. Some numerical certificates succeeded after allowing a real variable metric. The output-rotating terms were not demonstrated to add power in the retained comparisons: the same successful inputs also admitted purely real variable-metric numerical certificates. Other searches retained significant residuals. Neither residuals nor finite iterations prove infeasibility, and no full-cone heat inclusion was obtained.

A separate attempt combined a general polynomial SOS polyconvex support certificate with a corrected-heat component. This was motivated by the fact that a polynomial polyconvex function need not admit a convex polynomial lift at any degree, as emphasized in [4]. Some sampled inputs admitted the combined numerical construction; a universal decomposition was not established. Polynomial lift restrictions are not identified with polyconvexity.

**Compatible counterexample search.** Quartic polarization was used to eliminate the affine background, leaving a finite Fourier optimization over actual gradient coefficients. One retained full-cone numerical candidate was tested with cutoff three, a 13x13 dealiased grid, eight starts, and 800 Adam steps per start. The smallest directly reevaluated Jensen gap was approximately 0.0188792416110866. No negative gap was found. The numerical solve used a 10^-12 identity regularizer; the reported backgrounds were then evaluated directly. The candidate's general mathematical status and the search minima were not promoted to exact theorems. These observations are not lower bounds over the search class and not QC certificates.

The new exact results remove the singular-cubic coupling and classify the corresponding invariant quartic exceptions. They do not establish the positive-definite inequality

    c >= (1/2)b^T H^{-1}b

for every compatible gradient. That is the remaining nondegenerate obstruction, not a theorem inferred from the current computations.

The strongest retained extension is **quantitative closure near the nullspace**, combined with the genuinely resonant fourth-order information that distinguishes gradients from arbitrary determinant-balanced laws. The distinct counterexample route remains exact Fourier acquisition after minimizing the affine background, now with the theorem that an actual violation must have H>0 and rank C>=3. Neither route is claimed completed.
