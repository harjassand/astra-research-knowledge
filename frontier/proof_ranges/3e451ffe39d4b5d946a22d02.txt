Telegraph bridge
The initial sign is uniform. The endpoint differs from it with probability
\[
\frac{1-e^{-\lambda T}}2.
\]
For a midpoint with the same definitions of \(h,a\):
- Opposite endpoints give either midpoint sign with probability \(1/2\).
- Equal endpoints \(x\) give midpoint \(-x\) with probability\[
  \boxed{
  \frac{(1-a)^2}{2(1+a^2)}.
  }
  \tag{9}
  \]
These formulas follow by multiplying the two half-interval transition probabilities and conditioning on the endpoints. They again require only one independent uniform variable per visited node.
Thus both Gaussian and telegraph values admit
\[
\boxed{O(\log M)}
\tag{10}
\]
innovation queries per coherent time lookup.
This is a construction of the joint law, not merely its covariance.
4. Approximating the physical channel without controlling an entire path uniformly
For two deterministic noise paths \(x,y\), Duhamel’s formula and unitarity give
\[
\|U_x(T)-U_y(T)\|
\le
\int_0^T\|H_x(t)-H_y(t)\|\,dt.
\]
Consequently, for any coupling of two noise processes,
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi_X-\Phi_Y\|_\diamond
\le
2\int_0^T\sum_a\alpha_a
\,\mathbb E|X_a(t)-Y_a(t)|\,dt.
}
\tag{11}
\]
The same estimate holds with intervening instruments: dilate the instruments, retain their records, and telescope the complete evolution. No error is divided by the probability of a measurement outcome.
Grid error
Replace each process by its left-endpoint value in each cell. For Gaussian modes,
\[
\mathbb E|X(t)-X(s)|
\le\sqrt{2\lambda|t-s|}.
\]
For telegraph modes,
\[
\mathbb E|X(t)-X(s)|
=1-e^{-\lambda|t-s|}
\le\lambda|t-s|.
\]
Set
\[
C_G=\sum_{\mathrm{Gaussian}\ a}\alpha_a\sqrt{\lambda_a},
\qquad
C_R=\sum_{\mathrm{telegraph}\ a}\alpha_a\lambda_a.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi-\Phi_{\mathrm{grid}}\|_\diamond
\le
2\sqrt2\,T\sqrt\Delta\,C_G
+
2T\Delta C_R.
}
\tag{12}
\]
A sufficiently large power of two satisfying
\[
M\ge
C\left(
1+\frac{T^3C_G^2}{\varepsilon^2}
+\frac{T^2C_R}{\varepsilon}
\right)
\tag{13}
\]
controls this error. Additional conditions below ensure convenient segmentation and control-time rounding.
Equation (13) may demand an enormous grid. Only \(\log M\), not \(M\), is used in the circuit.
Finite Gaussian innovations
A finite-bit normal generator can be made explicit.
Take a uniform \(b\)-bit integer, convert it to the midpoint of its uniform quantile interval, apply the inverse normal distribution function, and truncate to \([-R_Z,R_Z]\). Enforce antisymmetric rounding between opposite quantiles.
For
\[
R_Z=O(\sqrt{\log(1/\eta)}),
\qquad b=O(\log(1/\eta)),
\]
the resulting variable \(Z_b\) has a coupling to a standard normal \(Z\) with
\[
\mathbb E(Z-Z_b)=0,
\qquad
\mathbb E|Z-Z_b|^2\le\eta^2.
\tag{14}
\]
For completeness, if \(f\) is the clipped quantile function, monotonicity gives a midpoint-discretization squared error at most
\[
4R_Z^2\,2^{-b}.
\]
The omitted Gaussian tail has exponentially small second moment. The clipped inverse CDF can be evaluated by bisection and finite-precision evaluation of the normal CDF in polynomial bit complexity; no quantile table is stored.
Every exact Gaussian grid value is a linear combination
\[
X_j=\sum_\ell c_{j\ell}Z_\ell,
\qquad
\sum_\ell c_{j\ell}^2=1.
\]
Under independent innovations, the mean-zero approximation errors in (14) therefore imply
\[
\mathbb E|X_j-Y_j|^2\le\eta^2
\tag{15}
\]
uniformly in \(j\), rather than an error proportional to the total number of grid points.
The stable recursion (8) also bounds arithmetic-error accumulation by a polynomial in the tree depth and working precision.
For telegraph bridges, finite-bit uniforms and threshold rounding can be coupled with the exact process. A union bound over its \(O(rM)\) potential nodes costs only
\[
b=O(\log(rM/\varepsilon))
\]
precision bits. It does not require visiting those nodes.
A deterministic norm cap
The simulation query budget must be fixed before choosing a seed. Gaussian paths are unbounded, so impose a final cap
\[
B=
\left\lceil
\sqrt{2\log\frac{64(1+ST)}{\varepsilon}}
\right\rceil.
\]
Clip Gaussian field values to \([-B,B]\).
Using
\[
\mathbb E|X-\operatorname{clip}_B(Y)|
\le
\mathbb E(|X|-B)_+
+\mathbb E|X-Y|,
\]
equation (11) bounds the resulting channel error by choosing
\[
\eta=O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{1+ST}\right).
\]
Every finite seed now defines a Hamiltonian satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\|H(t)\|\le\Lambda
=
\alpha_0+B\sum_{\mathrm{Gaussian}\ a}\alpha_a
+\sum_{\mathrm{telegraph}\ a}\alpha_a.
}
\tag{16}
\]
The Gaussian clipping argument uses marginal moments, not a supremum over the entire fine grid.
5. Simulating the gridded Hamiltonian without visiting its cells
This step uses established truncated-Dyson simulation, but there is a useful exact identity that avoids a differentiability assumption on the noise. arXiv
Consider a segment of duration \(d\), containing equally sized cells with constant Hamiltonians \(H_j\). For independent uniform cell indices \(j_1,\ldots,j_k\), sorted into nondecreasing order,
\[
\boxed{
\int_{0<t_1<\cdots<t_k<d}
H(t_k)\cdots H(t_1)\,dt_1\cdots dt_k
=
\frac{d^k}{k!}
\mathbb E\!\left[
H_{j_{(k)}}\cdots H_{j_{(1)}}
\right].
}
\tag{17}
