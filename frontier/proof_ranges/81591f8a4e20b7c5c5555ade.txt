ACQUISITION-AWARE STATIONARY FIRST-HIT CDFS
2026-10-10. Internal candidate theorem/proof; no novelty or independent-validation claim.
Companion to RESULT.txt. This note accounts for local parameter acquisition and
physical observation time instead of treating product-chain parameters as free.

Main stability theorem
----------------------
Let Q and Qhat be two finite reversible continuous-time chains on the same
state space and with the same nonempty target B. Write pi, pihat for their
stationary probabilities, m_x=pi(x), mhat_x=pihat(x), and c_xy=pi(x)Q_xy,
chat_xy=pihat(x)Qhat_xy for their undirected edge conductances. Assume the two
chains have the same possible edges and, for some kappa,rho>=0,

  exp(-kappa)<=mhat_x/m_x<=exp(kappa) for every x,
  exp(-rho)<=chat_xy/c_xy<=exp(rho) for every edge.       (1)

Start each chain in its OWN stationary law. If F and Fhat are the CDFs of
first hitting B, including the time-zero atom, then

  sup_{t>=0}|Fhat(t)-F(t)|
       <= 2[exp(kappa)-1]+[exp(rho)-1]/2.                 (2)

The right side can of course be capped at one. It has no dependence on the
stationary target mass pi(B), the killed spectral gap, the cardinality of the
state space, the time t, or the numerical waiting time. For kappa,rho<=1 it
is at most 4kappa+rho. This is a stationary-start assertion; arbitrary
nonstationary starts need separate assumptions and may have much larger
condition numbers.

Proof of (2) by interpolating masses and conductances
-----------------------------------------------------
Put C=B^c and extend any function on C by zero on B. Let M and Mhat be the
diagonal matrices of m_x and mhat_x restricted to C. Let L and Lhat be the
killed Dirichlet matrices, defined by

  u^T L v = sum_{unordered edges {x,y}} c_xy
                         [u(x)-u(y)][v(x)-v(y)],       (3)

with the zero extensions used on B. For theta in [0,1], interpolate the
FULL-state masses and conductances linearly: m_theta=(1-theta)m+theta mhat,
c_theta=(1-theta)c+theta chat. They define a valid reversible CTMC with
generator Q_theta(x,y)=c_theta(x,y)/m_theta(x) for x!=y and row sums zero.
Although this intermediate chain need not be a product chain, it is used
only in the proof. Its killed matrices are M_theta=(1-theta)M+theta Mhat and
L_theta=(1-theta)L+theta Lhat.

Let u_theta(t)=exp(-t M_theta^{-1}L_theta)1_C. Probabilistically,
u_theta(t,x)=P_x^{theta}(H_B>t). Hence 0<=u_theta(t,x)<=1 and
partial_t u_theta(t,x)<=0 for every x and t. Its stationary-start survival
is S_theta(t)=1_C^T M_theta u_theta(t), so F_theta(t)=1-S_theta(t).

Write M'=Mhat-M and L'=Lhat-L. Differentiating the killed forward equation
M_theta partial_t u_theta+L_theta u_theta=0 gives

  M_theta partial_t(partial_theta u_theta)
       +L_theta partial_theta u_theta
       =-M' partial_t u_theta-L' u_theta,
  partial_theta u_theta(0)=0.

Reversibility of the intermediate chain and variation of constants yield

  partial_theta S_theta(t)
    =1^T M' u_theta(t)
      -int_0^t u_theta(t-s)^T M' partial_s u_theta(s) ds
      -int_0^t u_theta(t-s)^T L' u_theta(s) ds.         (4)

The ratio bounds (1), along the entire interpolation, imply

  |M'_{xx}|<=alpha_M (M_theta)_{xx}, alpha_M=exp(kappa)-1,
  |c'_{xy}|<=alpha_L c_theta(x,y), alpha_L=exp(rho)-1.  (5)

The first term of (4) has absolute value at most alpha_M because
0<=u_theta<=1 and sum_C m_theta<=1. For the second, pointwise monotonicity
of u_theta in time and 0<=u_theta<=1 give

  |int_0^t u_theta(t-s)^T M' partial_s u_theta(s)ds|
    <=alpha_M sum_C m_theta(x)int_0^t[-partial_s u_theta(s,x)]ds
    =alpha_M sum_C m_theta(x)[1-u_theta(t,x)]<=alpha_M.  (6)

For the third term, (3) and edgewise Cauchy-Schwarz imply

  |v^T L' w|<=alpha_L [v^T L_theta v]^{1/2}
                          [w^T L_theta w]^{1/2}.

Apply Cauchy-Schwarz to the time convolution. Energy dissipation says

  int_0^t u_theta(s)^T L_theta u_theta(s)ds
      =[||1_C||_{M_theta}^2-||u_theta(t)||_{M_theta}^2]/2<=1/2.

Consequently the last integral in (4) has absolute value <=alpha_L/2.
Integrating |partial_theta S_theta(t)| over theta proves (2), simultaneously
for every t. This argument uses only positivity, reversibility, and the
matched stationary start; it does not invert a small killed eigenvalue.

Sharper fixed-stationary-measure form estimate
-----------------------------------------------
If pi=pihat, write A=-D_pi^{1/2}Q_CC D_pi^{-1/2} and Ahat analogously.
Suppose (1-delta)A<=Ahat<=(1+delta)A with 0<=delta<1. The form difference
E=Ahat-A obeys |v^T E w|<=delta||A^{1/2}v||||A^{1/2}w||. Duhamel and
Cauchy-Schwarz in time give, for any Euclidean a,b,

  |a^T[exp(-tAhat)-exp(-tA)]b|
     <=delta ||a||||b||/[2 sqrt(1-delta)]             (7)

uniformly in t. Indeed, the two squared-energy time integrals are bounded
by ||a||^2/[2(1-delta)] and ||b||^2/2, respectively. For stationary starts,
a=b=sqrt(pi_C), so ||a||||b||=pi(C)<=1. Equation (7) is independent even
of log[1/pi(B)]. The differing-pi theorem above cannot be obtained by
naively comparing the two symmetrized A matrices in Loewner order: their
near-null directions move when pi changes. Interpolating physical masses
and conductances is what preserves the no-rarity stability.

Product-chain specialization
----------------------------
For independent two-state coordinates, let p_i,pihat_i in (0,1) and
lambda_i,lambdahat_i>0. Define local logarithmic errors

  kappa_i=max(|log(pihat_i/p_i)|,
              |log[(1-pihat_i)/(1-p_i)]|),
  kappa=sum_i kappa_i,
  nu=max_i |log(lambdahat_i/lambda_i)|,
  kappa_max=max_i kappa_i,
  rho=nu+kappa+kappa_max.                               (8)

The product stationary masses satisfy the first inequality in (1).
For an edge flipping coordinate i, with other bits x_{-i}, its conductance
is exactly

  c_i(x_{-i})=lambda_i p_i(1-p_i) pi_{-i}(x_{-i}).      (9)

Taking the ratio of (9) for the two models gives the second inequality in
(1) with rho in (8). Thus (2) is an explicit stability bound computable
from aggregate local errors. If all local logs in (8) are at most xi, then
kappa<=n xi, rho<=(n+2)xi, and the first-hit CDF error is at most
4n xi+(n+2)xi whenever (n+2)xi<=1. In particular a local log accuracy
xi=epsilon/[10(n+2)] is enough for stationary first-hit CDF error <=epsilon/2,
irrespective of how exponentially small pi(B) is.

Concrete local-transition calibration
-------------------------------------
Here is a fully specified acquisition interface. The unknown true product
chain belongs to a supplied class

  a<=p_i<=1-a,  lambda_0<=lambda_i<=lambda_1

for known positive rational a<=1/2 and 0<lambda_0<=lambda_1. Each
coordinate can be prepared independently in state 0 or 1, then observed
after a chosen physical duration; preparations and binary observations are
counted. No target-hit observation is used. Set R=lambda_1/lambda_0,
tau=1/lambda_1, and c_*=lambda_0/(2lambda_1)=1/(2R).

For each coordinate, perform N independent trials prepared at 0 and N
independent trials prepared at 1, each observed after tau. The respective
transition probabilities are

  A_i=P_tau(0,1)=p_i c_i,
  B_i=P_tau(1,0)=(1-p_i)c_i,
  c_i=A_i+B_i=1-exp(-lambda_i tau)>=c_* .            (10)

Let Atilde_i,Btilde_i be empirical frequencies. Fix desired CDF error
epsilon in (0,1/2), confidence failure zeta in (0,1/2), and set

  xi=epsilon/[20(n+2)],
  d=a c_* xi/100,
  N=ceil[log(4n/zeta)/(2d^2)].                       (11)

Hoeffding and a union bound give, with probability >=1-zeta,
|Atilde_i-A_i|,|Btilde_i-B_i|<=d for every i. Define

  ctilde_i=Atilde_i+Btilde_i,
  ptilde_i=Atilde_i/ctilde_i,
  lambda_i^*= -log(1-ctilde_i)/tau.                    (12)

On the good event, all denominators are positive, all ptilde_i lie in
(0,1), and ctilde_i<3/4. The log errors in ptilde_i and 1-ptilde_i are
<xi/4: this follows from |Atilde_i/A_i-1|<=xi/100,
|Btilde_i/B_i-1|<=xi/100, and |ctilde_i/c_i-1|<=xi/50.
For the rates, d[log(-log(1-c))]/dc =
1/[(1-c)(-log(1-c))]<=4/c on c<3/4. Since
|ctilde_i-c_i|<=2d and c_i>=c_*, their log-rate error is <xi/4.
Evaluate the logarithm in (12) to a positive binary rational
lambdahat_i with additional log-relative error <=xi/4, and put
pihat_i=ptilde_i. The resulting local log errors are <=xi. Computing
these rational estimates uses polynomial bit operations in the model-class
encoding, log N, and log(1/xi). A certified log series or interval routine
works because ctilde_i stays away from 1 on the good event.

Thus with probability >=1-zeta, the TRUE and ESTIMATED stationary-start
first-hit CDFs differ uniformly in t by at most epsilon/4 (using (2) and
the deliberately conservative xi in (11)). A pointwise estimate at a
binary-rational t follows by running the supplied-model deterministic
compiler in RESULT.txt on the rational estimates to tolerance epsilon/4.
This gives total pointwise error <=epsilon/2 on the good calibration event.
If a rational CDF approximation valid uniformly over all t is desired,
run RESULT.txt's finite-support CDF preprocessing to its own uniform
tolerance epsilon/4; its computational cost is polynomial in 1/epsilon.

Calibration costs: 2nN preparations, 2nN binary observations, and total
physical observation duration 2nN tau, with

  N=O[n^2 R^2/(a^2 epsilon^2) * log(n/zeta)].        (13)

No term involves 1/pi(B), the number of full states, or a wait for a hit.
Preparations are an explicit resource, not hidden inside a sample oracle.
If each preparation requires physical time T_reset, add 2nN T_reset to the
duration; no bound on that device-specific cost is inferred here.
If a stationary physical start is needed after calibration, a fresh product
chain begun arbitrarily can be allowed to run for

  T_mix=lambda_0^{-1} log(4n/epsilon),

because its law is then within epsilon/4 in total variation of pi by
coordinate mixing and a union bound. That start-law discrepancy changes
the first-hit distribution by at most epsilon/4. The claimed physical-time
polynomial requires a^{-1}, R, and lambda_0^{-1} to be polynomially bounded
in the problem size; if very small rates or probabilities are supplied
only through short binary encodings, (13) exposes their inverse numerical
cost instead of disguising it as bit complexity.

Scope and validation
--------------------
The stability theorem is a self-contained mathematical derivation, not an
independently certified historical novelty claim. The calibration result is
conditional on the stated reset-and-observe interface and known class bounds.
Without controlled preparations or a comparable local data source, one
cannot infer the same sample complexity from an unobserved rare trajectory.
The deterministic boundary-resolvent CDF compiler is constructed in
RESULT.txt; this note adds acquisition and stationary-start perturbation
control. Source and theorem-status cautions in RESULT.txt remain in force.

A floating-point sanity check used n=6, one all-ones target, p_i=.1 and
lambda_i=1, perturbed to pihat_i=.101 and lambdahat_i=1.005. The target masses
were 1.0000e-6 and 1.06152e-6. Across 1001 times from zero to 1e7, the
largest CDF difference was .0232834 near t=185468, below the bound .161789
from (2). This finite check illustrates the rarity-independent bound; it is
neither a proof nor a certification of the numerical compiler.
