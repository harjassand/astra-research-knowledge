WORKING DERIVATION — CONSERVATION-COMPATIBLE FINITE MEMORY
13 October 2026. Internally derived candidate; priority UNKNOWN.

Original target remains deterministic 3D point-Coulomb relaxation on the
collisional scale. The construction below does NOT derive that limit. It
addresses a concrete sub-obstruction: a positive finite-time resonance
replacement need not conserve energy. Sections 2-8 supply screening in a
radial diagnostic; Section 9 removes that supplied length for an exact
continuum Fourier calculation. Neither construction acquires screening
from particles. No molecular simulation advantage is claimed.

1. FAILURE OF POSITIVITY ALONE

Use Fourier measure dk (no (2pi)^-3 prefactor; this convention is local).
For u=v-v*, define the raw finite-time tensor

  B_T(u)=int dk k k^T |Vhat(k)|^2 F_T(k.u),
  F_T(omega)=(1-cos(T omega))/(pi T omega^2).

F_T is nonnegative, normalized, and is the transform of a triangular time
window. B_T is positive. But B_T(u)u is nonzero at finite T.
For Q_B(f)=div_v int B(v-v*)[f* grad f-f grad_* f*]dv*,
the weak form is

  <psi,Q_B(f)> = -1/2 int ff* [grad psi-grad_*psi*]^T
                         B(u)[grad log f-grad_*log f*] dv dv*.

For a Maxwellian with covariance theta I, its kinetic-energy production is

  dE/dt = 2/(theta T) int_0^K k^2 |Vhat(k)|^2
                              [1-exp(-theta T^2 k^2)] dk > 0.

Derivation: rotational symmetry splits B=a P_u+b e_u e_u^T. Its longitudinal
coefficient obeys r^2 b = (4/T) int k^2 Vhat^2[1-sinc(Tkr)]dk.
For u~N(0,2 theta I), E sinc(Tk|u|)=exp(-theta T^2 k^2).
This is a failure of using B_T as an instantaneous velocity-only collision
generator, not a contradiction of the actual memory dynamics: microscopic
potential/field energy can exchange with kinetic energy.

2. TRANSVERSE COMPLETION

Let phi be an even probability density on R, phi>=0, and
F_T(omega)=T phi(T omega). Put P_u=I-u u^T/|u|^2 for u!=0 and

  A_T(u)=int dk (P_u k)(P_u k)^T |Vhat(k)|^2 F_T(k.u).

At u=0 the value can be chosen arbitrarily for the weak formula (a null set
for absolutely continuous f). The tensor is symmetric, even, positive and
A_T(u)u=0. Its collision operator therefore conserves mass, momentum and
kinetic energy exactly, and dissipates int f log f. All Maxwellians are
stationary. These structural identities use the standard Landau weak form;
positivity/projection and the H-theorem mechanism are known mathematics.

For radial Vhat and a finite ultraviolet cutoff K, direct angular integration
gives, with r=|u|,

  A_T(u)=a_T(r) P_u,
  a_T(r)=pi/r int_0^K k^3 Vhat(k)^2 R_phi(Tkr) dk,
  R_phi(a)=int_{-a}^a (1-x^2/a^2) phi(x) dx.

The infinite-time resonance tensor has
  a_inf(r)=pi/r int_0^K k^3 Vhat(k)^2 dk.

If mu2=int x^2 phi(x)dx<infinity, then

  0<=1-R_phi(a)
    =int_|x|>a phi(x)dx + a^-2 int_|x|<=a x^2 phi(x)dx
    <=mu2/a^2.

Moreover a^2[1-R_phi(a)] -> mu2. The second term tends to mu2;
the first multiplied by a^2 is bounded by the vanishing second-moment tail.
Thus smoothing the positive window cannot improve the leading T^-2
transverse deficit within this operation, unless its frequency variance is zero.

For Vhat(k)=1/(k^2+kappa^2),
  int_0^infinity k Vhat(k)^2 dk = 1/(2 kappa^2),
and hence, UNIFORMLY IN THE FINITE CUTOFF K,

  0<=a_inf(r)-a_T(r)<= pi mu2/(2 T^2 kappa^2 r^3).

The tensors themselves diverge logarithmically as K->infinity. This bound
does not define an uncut finite collision operator. It controls their difference
uniformly and preserves, rather than removes, the Coulomb ultraviolet problem.
