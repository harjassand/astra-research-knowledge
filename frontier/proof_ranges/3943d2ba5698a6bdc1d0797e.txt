## 2. An exact positive lift into bipartite problems

The first mechanism was to represent a general perfect matching through a compatible vertex bipartition.

For \(S\subseteq V\), let \(G_S\) contain exactly the edges of \(G\) crossing between \(S\) and \(V\setminus S\), retaining their weights. Define

\[
Z_S=\sum_{M\in\mathcal M(G_S)}w(M).
\]

A fixed perfect matching has exactly \(2^n\) compatible choices of \(S\): independently choose one endpoint of each matching edge.

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\sum_{S\subseteq V}Z_S=2^n Z_G.}
\tag{1}
\]

This is positive throughout. There is no cancellation, exponentially precise subtraction, or inaccessible input representation.

An equivalent polynomial is

\[
P_G(x)=
\sum_{M\in\mathcal M(G)}
w(M)\prod_{\{u,v\}\in M}(x_u+x_v).
\tag{2}
\]

Its coefficient at a squarefree monomial is a bipartite matching partition function:

\[
[x^S]P_G(x)=Z_S.
\]

Also,

\[
P_G(\mathbf 1)=2^n Z_G.
\]

**Equation (2) is not an efficient evaluation oracle.** Evaluating it at \(\mathbf 1\) is precisely the original counting problem, up to the known factor \(2^n\). What is tractable is an individual coefficient through a bipartite instance.

### Both conditional distributions are accessible

Define a joint distribution

\[
\Pi(M,S)=
\frac{w(M)}{2^n Z_G}
\mathbf 1\{M\subseteq E(G_S)\}.
\tag{3}
\]

Its matching marginal is exactly \(\pi_G\).

Its conditional operations are:

\[
S\mid M:
\quad
\text{independently choose one endpoint of every edge of }M;
\]

\[
M\mid S:
\quad
\text{sample a weighted perfect matching of the bipartite graph }G_S.
\]

The first requires \(n\) fair bits. The second has polynomial-time approximate implementations using existing bipartite counting and sampling machinery. Neither conditional requires \(Z_G\). [ACM Digital Library](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/1008731.1008738)

This gives an explicit alternating sampler:

\[
M
\longrightarrow
\text{random compatible }S
\longrightarrow
\text{new matching }M'\text{ in }G_S.
\tag{4}
\]

The decisive question is whether this sampler moves efficiently between different parts of the matching space.

### Why independent bipartitions do not already solve counting

Let \(S\) be uniformly random among all subsets of \(V\), and consider the idealised estimator

\[
Y=2^n Z_S.
\]

Equation (1) gives \(\mathbb E Y=Z_G\).

For matchings \(M,N\), let \(c(M,N)\) be the number of components of their union, counting a shared edge as a doubled-edge component. Every other component is an even alternating cycle.

There are exactly \(2^{c(M,N)}\) bipartitions compatible with both. Therefore,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathbb E Y^2}{Z_G^2}
=
\mathbb E_{M,N\sim\pi_G}
2^{c(M,N)}.
}
\tag{5}
\]

This calculation reveals an exponential cost immediately. When \(G\) has a unique perfect matching, \(c(M,M)=n\), so the relative second moment is \(2^n\).

More directly, a random bipartition is compatible with that matching with probability \(2^{-n}\). Polynomially many trials will usually return only zeros—even with an exact oracle for every bipartite count.

Thus the positive identity does not, by itself, produce an efficient estimator.
