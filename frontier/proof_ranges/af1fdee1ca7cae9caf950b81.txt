\paragraph{Acquisition cost changes the conclusion.}
At the target rate, an hour contains $2.62\times10^{10}$ particles. As an illustrative reliability requirement, bounding the probability of \emph{any statistical coverage failure} by $0.01$ per hour needs a per-particle one-sided Gaussian guard of about $7.17$ standard deviations, by the union bound. This does not require independence between particles, but the assumed calibrated Gaussian tail itself must hold. With $95\%$ dense-sphere acceptance and no material uncertainty, the normalized random standard deviation must be about $0.115\%$ or less. With the material margins in (T10), it must be about $0.046\%$ or less, before further systematic error. A nominal $0.2\%$ single-particle standard error is insufficient for that industrial reliability target. Other explicitly stated lot-level risk targets give different guards.

For harmonic acceleration amplitude $a$ at frequency $f$, the resolved displacement is $a/(2\pi f)^2$. At the assumed force scale it is $249\,\mathrm{nm}$ at $1\,\mathrm{kHz}$ and $24.9\,\mathrm{pm}$ at $100\,\mathrm{kHz}$; resolving a $1\%$ coefficient change requires respectively $2.49\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $0.249\,\mathrm{pm}$. A square-wave or impulsive protocol has a different transfer function and must be analyzed explicitly. Faster cycling helps freeze orientation while shrinking mechanical readout. Even $1\%$ electrical anisotropy at the assumed field can rotate a freely moving grain appreciably in a millisecond. Initial spin survives average induced-torque cancellation. Optical centroid precision is also not automatically center-of-mass acceleration precision: an off-center void or irregular scattering pattern produces a rotating centroid offset. These are unclosed measurement conditions, not a proof that every instrument is impossible.

Independent isotropic probes can remove one orientation bias but incur statistical cost. For $u$ uniform on the unit sphere and a tensor $\alpha$ fixed before that draw,
\[
 X=\frac{3u^T\alpha u}{\Tr\alpha},\qquad
 \mathbb EX=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}X=\frac65\left(
       \frac{\Tr(\alpha^2)}{(\Tr\alpha)^2}-\frac13\right)\le\frac45.
\]
The fourth-moment identity $\mathbb E u_i u_j u_k u_l=(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})/15$ proves the formula. Worst-case $0.2\%$ relative standard error requires about $200{,}000$ independent probes even with noiseless readout. A separately acquired near-isotropic class is easier. This familiar random-trace estimator does not remove gradient-squared force, torque-dependent sampling or physical sensing cost.

The resource analysis also exposes particle interactions, singulation and gas loading. A conservative $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ neighbor-exclusion radius in a Poisson beam with $95\%$ isolated grains gives density at most $9.8\times10^7\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and about $0.0074\,\mathrm{m^2}$ beam area at $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Isotropic axis cycling can cancel the leading pair interaction of identical spheres, so this conservative estimate is not a universal throughput bound. At $0.1\,\mathrm{Pa}$, the assumed Epstein drag model predicts a small bias, but even $1$ standard liter per minute of carrier gas would require order $1.7\times10^4\,\mathrm{L/s}$ pumping speed. Dry feed, particle-addressed actuation and calibrated drive/readout energy remain design requirements. The detailed models and their assumptions are preserved with the technology notes; none describes a built instrument.

The comparator is not an uninspected sieve. \href{https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19890004301}{NASA/MIT's 1986 free-fall vacuum AC dielectrophoretic separator} establishes prior hardware of this general type. \href{https://patents.google.com/patent/CN113499989A/en}{CN113499989A, published 2021}, describes hollow-powder sorting by size bins and airflow deflection. A patent is disclosure of a design, not verified performance. Existing polarizability-based particle sorting is also established. No matched $1\%$ closed-void, $1\,\mathrm{kg/h}$ measurement comparison has been completed, and no superiority over the strongest powder-screening technology is established.

\paragraph{Reproducible falsification and next milestone.}
Use individually identified, connected conductive reference particles spanning solid, shielded hollow, elongated and asymmetric shapes. Acquire independent ground-truth closed-void volumes by sufficiently resolved tomography or destructive sectioning, plus material/composition bounds. First measure the three-axis four-sign force coefficient on a fixed reference body; vary charge, position and orientation to expose uncancelled terms. Then test freely moving particles with the same blind acceptance threshold and coverage rule. A false acceptance above the admitted void threshold beyond the declared error coverage falsifies either the physical premises or their acquisition. Show that the claimed nuisance bounds hold on the accepted particles; bulk averages are insufficient.

The next engineering milestone is a single-particle apparatus that certifies the required response accuracy and valid uncertainty bound on a blinded reference set. It precedes a parallel powder sorter. Pumping, electrode-drive and recovery losses, illumination and readout, tracking computation, interparticle fields, powder delivery, rejection actuation and cleaning must be charged at the demonstrated accepted-mass rate. No net-energy advantage or manufacturing feasibility has been proved. Even perfect feedstock screening would not remove pores created subsequently by keyholing, lack of fusion or dissolved-gas evolution during printing.
