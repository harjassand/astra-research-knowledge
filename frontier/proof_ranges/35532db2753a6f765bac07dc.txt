## 6. Frozen-reservoir lifting theorem

The central issue in combining the objects is that microscopic transitions inside a module may change whether its reservoirs are good. Multiplying its flow by k_e evaluated on the unmodified gate field would not in general preserve the source identity.

For a coarse edge e=(u,v), define Gamma^{e+} to be the gate field with **both u and v forced good**, keeping every other block gate unchanged. Put

\[
F_e(\omega_{Q_e^c})=k_e(\Gamma^{e+}). \tag{15}
\]

This coefficient is independent of all occupations inside Q_e, including both reservoirs and both ports. In a fixed tiling define the lifted microscopic current

\[
J_b^{\mathrm{tile}}(\eta)
=\sum_{e: b\subset Q_e}F_e(\omega_{Q_e^c})H_b^e(\eta_{Q_e}). \tag{16}
\]

At most four modules contribute to a bond. Finally average over the L squared translated tilings. The resulting J is covariant under every unit lattice translation, not only block translations.

### Theorem

Suppose a finite module obeys (4), with legality in arbitrary exteriors, product-equilibrium port/reservoir independence, square-symmetric orientations, and finite C(p) in (6). If (12) holds, (15)–(16) construct a legal, antisymmetric, square-integrable globally admissible current. It satisfies

\[
\langle a,J\rangle=\frac{2\chi}{L},
\quad
\|J\|^2\le\frac{2\chi C(p)}{L^2}\mathcal K(b).
\tag{17}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{D(p)\ge\frac1{C(p)\mathcal K(b(p))}.} \tag{18}
\]

### Proof of exact global admissibility

Legality follows term by term. Antisymmetry holds because an exchange inside Q_e changes H antisymmetrically but does not change its coefficient F_e.

On nonzero module demands both endpoint gates are good, so

\[
F_e g_u g_v=k_e(\Gamma). \tag{19}
\]

We prove conservation in expectations against every local test function, not by asserting that an infinite pointwise sum telescopes.

For a local test f, antisymmetry and translation covariance transform its pairing with Gf into minus twice the sum over microscopic bonds of E[J_b f]. Only bonds touching the support of f have nonzero expectations; all other terms vanish by exchange symmetry.

Substitute (16), group the finitely many relevant module terms, and use (4) and (19). The result, apart from the harmless common factor, is

\[
\sum_{e=(u,v)}\mathbb E[k_e(\Gamma)(\sigma_u-\sigma_v)f]. \tag{20}
\]

Condition on all reservoirs. The port occupations sigma are independent Bernoulli-p variables, whereas k is now fixed. Set

\[
m_u(\omega)=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{ports}}[(\sigma_u-p)f].
\]

The sequence m_u has finite support: it is zero at every port on which f does not depend. Conditional expectation turns (20) into

\[
\sum_{e=(u,v)}k_e(m_u-m_v)
=\sum_u m_u\operatorname{div}_{\mathrm{space}}k(u)=0.
\]

Every effective sum is finite, and integrability follows from the energy bound below. This proves (2). In particular, conservation is not checked merely against the finite functions used to discover H.
