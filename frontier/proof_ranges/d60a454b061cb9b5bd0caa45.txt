At \(g=0\), its photon-number distribution is

\[
 p_B(n)=\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)}\,
 \frac{\operatorname{haf}(B[n])^2}{\prod_i n_i!}.
\tag{2.1}

Here \(B[n]\) repeats row and column \(i\), \(n_i\) times. A diagonal entry of \(B\) may join distinct copies of the same mode. Odd-order hafnians are zero. Photon collisions are retained.

More generally, use the real Bargmann density kernel

\[
 R(\xi)=c\exp\!\left(\tfrac12\xi^TK\xi+\ell^T\xi\right),
 \quad \xi=(z_1,\ldots,z_m,w_1,\ldots,w_m),
\tag{2.2}
\]

with rational symmetric \(K\geq0\), rational \(\ell\geq0\), and real ket/bra exchange symmetry. Write

\[
 X=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad \rho(XK)<1.
\]

A quantum interpretation additionally requires that (2.2) represents a positive density operator. The general constructor does **not** attempt a complete physicality test. `pure_kernel` gives a physically valid input family; `trace_modes` produces physical mixed kernels by tracing modes out of an already physical input. Nonnegativity and convergence alone define a normalized positive diagonal count law, but should not be advertised as sufficient for every quantum-state constraint.

The pure subclass corresponds to \(K=\operatorname{diag}(B,B)\), \(\ell=(g,g)\). The normalizer of the general kernel is

\[
 c^{-1}=\det(I-XK)^{-1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\tfrac12\ell^T(I-XK)^{-1}X\ell\right).
\tag{2.3}
\]

Let \(H\) be the observed modes and \(h_i\geq0\) their prescribed counts. Write \(h=\sum_{i\in H}h_i\), \(s=|H|\), and \(U=H^c\). The desired output is

\[
 N_U\sim\mathcal L(N_U\mid N_H=h).
\]

A zero-probability herald is rejected as an invalid conditioning event. The working exact matching recurrence detects it exactly.

**Excluded interfaces.** Generic signed or complex interference matrices, approximate-input tomography without an error model, arbitrary non-Gaussian inputs, arbitrary quantum measurements, and the ordering of individual photon arrivals are not covered. A supplied local-phase gauge can sometimes reduce a state to the nonnegative class, but finding such a gauge is not assumed free. The algorithm samples a measurement distribution, not a classical description of the entire quantum state.

## 3. Positive Gaussian marginalization

Subscripts below refer to doubled sets of modes. Let \(D_z\) be diagonal on the doubled unobserved set, with the same physical marking variable on its ket and bra copy. Put

\[
 M=X_UK_{UU},\qquad
 J(z)=D_z(I-MD_z)^{-1}X_U.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For positive \(z\), this is also \((X_UD_z^{-1}-K_{UU})^{-1}\). The formula extends to zero markings by continuity. Define

\[
 S(z)=K_{HH}+K_{HU}J(z)K_{UH},
\]
\[
 t(z)=\ell_H+K_{HU}J(z)\ell_U,
 \qquad v(z)=\ell_U^TJ(z)\ell_U.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Gaussian contraction of unobserved ket/bra indices gives

\[
 R_H(\xi_H;z)=c\det(I-D_zM)^{-1/2}
 e^{v(z)/2}
 \exp\!\left(\tfrac12\xi_H^TS(z)\xi_H+t(z)^T\xi_H\right).
\tag{3.3}
\]

One can prove (3.3) either by completing the quadratic form in the complex Gaussian trace integral or by expanding in Wick contractions. Nonnegativity makes the path expansions below absolutely convergent for \(0\leq z\leq1\).

Let \(r=(h_1,\ldots,h_s,h_1,\ldots,h_s)\). Let \(L_r(S,t)\) be the sum of weights of all partitions of these **labeled** tokens into pairs and singletons. A pair of types \(a,b\) has weight \(S_{ab}\); a singleton of type \(a\) has weight \(t_a\). This is a loop hafnian, with singleton weights kept separate from pair weights between repeated copies.

Taking the observed Fock coefficient in (3.3) proves the conditional probability generating function

\[
 F_h(z)=
 \left[\frac{\det(I-M)}{\det(I-D_zM)}\right]^{1/2}
 \exp\!\left(\frac{v(z)-v(1)}2\right)
 \frac{L_r(S(z),t(z))}{L_r(S(1),t(1))}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

The factors \(\prod h_i!\) and the full-state normalizer cancel. This is why the sampler does not need the herald success probability.

For diagnostics only,

\[
 \Pr(N_H=h)=
 \sqrt{\frac{\det(I-XK)}{\det(I-M)}}
 e^{[v(1)-v_{\rm full}(1)]/2}
 \frac{L_r(S(1),t(1))}{\prod_i h_i!}.
\tag{3.5}
\]

The implementation never feeds (3.5) into a rejection step.

## 4. Count-compressed reconstruction theorem

### 4.1 Elimination

Take an entrywise nonnegative matrix \(W\), with an internal set \(U\) and a boundary set that is never traversed internally. Suppose \(\rho(W_{UU})<1\). For an internal vertex \(v\), set

\[
 p_v=1-W_{vv}>0,
 \quad V_{ij}=\frac{W_{iv}W_{vj}}{p_v},
 \quad W'_{ij}=W_{ij}+V_{ij}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Record \(p_v\) and

\[
 \theta^{(v)}_{ij}=\frac{V_{ij}}{W'_{ij}},
\tag{4.2}
\]

taking the ratio to be zero when both numerator and denominator vanish. Repeat until all internal vertices have been removed. The final boundary matrix is

\[
 W_{HH}+W_{HU}(I-W_{UU})^{-1}W_{UH}.
\]

### 4.2 Reverse step

Suppose the reduced graph currently contains directed edge counts \(C_{ij}\). Independently, draw

\[
 R_{ij}\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(C_{ij},\theta^{(v)}_{ij}),
 \qquad K_v=\sum_{ij}R_{ij}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Retain \(C_{ij}-R_{ij}\) direct edges. Replace the routed edges by

\[
 C_{iv}=\sum_jR_{ij},\qquad C_{vj}=\sum_iR_{ij}.
\]

Draw the self-loop count

\[
 C_{vv}\sim\operatorname{NegBin}(\alpha+K_v,p_v),
 \qquad N_v=K_v+C_{vv}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

The negative-binomial convention is

\[
 \mathbb E[z^{\operatorname{NegBin}(a,p)}]
 =\left(\frac{p}{1-(1-p)z}\right)^a.
\]

The shape is **\(\alpha+K_v\), not just \(K_v\)**. The additional shape accounts for the loop soup. For photon simulation, \(\alpha=1/2\).

### 4.3 Proof

Write \(a=W_{vv}\). Each reduced edge \(i\to j\) is a mixture of a direct edge of weight \(W_{ij}\) and an excursion through \(v\) of total weight \(W_{iv}W_{vj}/(1-a)\). Therefore the direct/excursion choice has probability (4.2). For a multiplicity of identical reduced edges, their independent choices aggregate exactly to (4.3).

Each of the \(K_v\) routed excursions makes one compulsory visit to \(v\), followed by a geometrically distributed number of self-loops with generating function \((1-a)/(1-az)\). Loops wholly removed at \(v\), independently, have generating function

\[
 \exp\!\left(\alpha\sum_{k\geq1}\frac{a^k(z^k-1)}k\right)
 =\left(\frac{1-a}{1-az}\right)^\alpha.
\]

Multiplying these factors gives a negative binomial of shape \(\alpha+K_v\). The marked effective excursion weight is

\[
 \frac{W_{iv}W_{vj}z}{1-az}.
\]

This is exactly the marked version of the Schur-complement update (4.1). Inductively undoing all vertices recovers the original path weights and determinant factor. This proves the joint occupation law, not merely its first moments.

In the absence of boundary paths, the result has PGF

\[
 \mathbb E[z^N]=
 \left[\frac{\det(I-M)}{\det(I-D_zM)}\right]^\alpha.
\tag{4.5}
\]

The argument permits arbitrary finite graphs, collisions, repeated edges, self-loops and nonreversible nonnegative internal matrices. There is no loop-length cutoff and no mixing-time assumption.

**What is inherited.** Schur-complement path compression and binomial/negative-binomial reconstruction have an established kinetic path-sampling antecedent [3]. The loop measure and determinant law are classical [4,5]. The present statement composes the loop immigration term, arbitrary boundary paths and the Gaussian conditioning interface. No claim that Schur elimination or kinetic path sampling itself is new is made.

## 5. Complete Gaussian sampling procedure

Use one additional boundary vertex \(\star\), representing the displacement source. Order the vertices as doubled \(U\), doubled \(H\), then \(\star\). Construct

\[
 W=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 X_UK_{UU}&X_UK_{UH}&X_U\ell_U\\
 K_{HU}&K_{HH}&\ell_H\\
 \ell_U^T&\ell_H^T&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Eliminate only doubled \(U\). The final boundary block is

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}S(1)&t(1)\\t(1)^T&v(1)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Perform the following operations:

1. Sample a pair/singleton matching of the \(2h\) observed tokens, with weights \(S(1),t(1)\).
2. Record each paired token pair as one directed boundary edge, choosing either orientation. Record each singleton as an edge to \(\star\).
3. Independently draw \(P\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(v(1)/2)\), and add \(P\) self-edges at \(\star\).
4. Reverse elimination by (4.3)–(4.4), with \(\alpha=1/2\).
5. Sum ket and bra occupation counts for each unobserved physical mode. Do not report the ghost or boundary occupations.

All boundary path functions are symmetric after physical variables are identified on ket/bra copies, so choosing a fixed orientation for each matched pair introduces no bias. The Poisson source contributes

\[
 \exp\!\left(\frac{v(1)}2\left[\frac{v(z)}{v(1)}-1\right]\right)
 =e^{[v(z)-v(1)]/2}.
\]

The zero-rate case is interpreted as no source paths. Combining this with (4.5) and the weighted matching law yields exactly (3.4). This completes the distributional proof of the algorithm in ideal scalar arithmetic.

For pure undisplaced GBS with no herald, there are no boundary paths, and the construction reduces to \(\alpha=1/2\) loops on

\[
 M=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\B&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The resulting PGF is

\[
 \frac{\sqrt{\det(I-B^2)}}{\sqrt{\det(I-(D_zB)^2)}},
\]

which is the PGF of (2.1). This also explains even total photon parity. It does not assert a positive mixture of coherent quantum states.

### Exact matching backend

For type multiplicities \(r\), select the first occupied type \(a\). The executable recurrence is

\[
 Z(r)=t_a Z(r-e_a)
 +\sum_b (r_b-\mathbf1_{a=b})S_{ab}
 Z(r-e_a-e_b),\qquad Z(0)=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Negative-multiplicity terms are absent. Conditional branch probabilities are the summands divided by \(Z(r)\). Rational arithmetic makes these choices exact in the unbiased-random-bit model. The multiplicity factor counts the possible partner labels; no extra singleton multiplicity is required because the distinguished token is fixed.

The number of memoized states is bounded by

\[
 R_h=\prod_{i\in H}(h_i+1)^2.
