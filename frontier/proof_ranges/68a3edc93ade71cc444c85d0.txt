## Algebra and positivity

With `L=(1-exp(-mu*h))/mu`, `v=exp(-mu*h)`, `z*=D/L`, and `u=(1-z*)/(1-z*v)`, the conditions `0<D<L<h` give `0<z*<1`, `0<u<1`. Both vector fields leave `[0,1]` invariant; output ethane rates `1-z` and `beta` are nonnegative. Static positive/negative equilibria are 0/1, giving endpoint rates 1/beta. The pulse return map has multiplier `u*v<1`, and `z* = 1-u+u*v*z*` follows by substitution. Its periodic total is exactly `h-z*L+d*beta=P*rho`.

For an isolated pulse, the end-preparation states are `1-u` and 0. Positive evolution multiplies their difference by `exp(-mu*t)`, yielding the stated integral. For matched periodic conditioning, the pre-pulse state is `z*v`. The executed pulse returns it to `z*`; a positive sham of the same duration produces `z*v*exp(-mu*d)`. Their difference is exactly `z*(1-exp(-mu*P))`. The stated conditioned contrast therefore has the correct history and observation clock.

As `mu -> 0`, `z* -> D/h` in `(0,1)`, `1-u = z*(1-v)/(1-z*v)=O(mu)`, and `1-u*v=(1-v)/(1-z*v)=O(mu)`. The fixed-horizon output integrals vanish, while the e-fold conditioning cycle count diverges. Positive washout time likewise scales as `1/mu`. No finite maximum conditioning/washout horizon follows from these endpoint/mean observations.

For a 10-second common hold with `T=h`, the conditioned formula simplifies to `D*(1-exp(-mu*P))`. This is useful for checking the result. The isolated contrast approaches D as `L` approaches D from above. **Do not extend that limiting range statement to both contrasts:** in this family the conditioned contrast remains strictly below `D*(1-exp(-mu_max*P))`, where `L(mu_max)=D`. The reviewed theorem itself claims the D limit only for the isolated contrast, which is correct.
