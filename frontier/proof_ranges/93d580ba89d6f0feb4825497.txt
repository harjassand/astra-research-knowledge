## 5. What can be obtained with finitely many copies?

The closure statement has a concrete finite-certificate consequence.

Let

\[
u(h)=\sum_{i\in E}h(\{i\}).
\]

### Finite approximation theorem

Suppose a rational linear inequality

\[
L(h)\ge0
\]

is valid on \(\mathcal K_E\). Then, for every rational \(\varepsilon>0\),

\[
\boxed{L(h)+\varepsilon u(h)\ge0}
\]

has a finite-copy certificate.

### Proof

Normalize by \(u(h)=1\), and consider

\[
B_\varepsilon=
\left\{
h\in\Gamma_E:
u(h)=1,\ L(h)\le-\varepsilon
\right\},
\]

where \(\Gamma_E\) is the Shannon cone.

This set is compact. It is disjoint from \(\mathcal K_E\). Hence every \(h\in B_\varepsilon\) fails some finite-copy program.

Because each program-feasibility cone is closed, the complements of these cones form an open cover of \(B_\varepsilon\). A finite subcover suffices.

Combine those finitely many programs using fresh auxiliary variables. The combined program excludes every member of \(B_\varepsilon\). By homogeneity, every initial profile admitting it satisfies

\[
L(h)+\varepsilon u(h)\ge0.
\]

The resulting implication is a finite rational polyhedral implication, so a rational Farkas certificate exists. The case \(u(h)=0\) is trivial because the polymatroid must then be identically zero. ∎

This also shows that any inequality **strictly positive on the normalized nonzero copy model** already has an exact finite-copy certificate. The unresolved issue concerns boundary inequalities.

### An explicit bound for a one-step MAXE certificate

The approximation need not always be obtained through compactness alone.

Suppose a one-step MAXE proof has been reduced to a rational certificate

\[
L(f)+\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_jd_j(g)\ge0,
\qquad \lambda_j\ge0,
\tag{1}
\]

valid whenever \(f,g\) satisfy the Shannon constraints and agree on the protected marginals. Here \(d_j(g)\) are the required separator conditional mutual informations. Setting all \(d_j(g)=0\) proves \(L(f)\ge0\).

Such a form is obtained from a Farkas certificate by moving the separator-equality terms to the left and using \(d_j\ge0\) to make their coefficients nonnegative.

Put

\[
\Lambda=\sum_{j=1}^{q}\lambda_j.
\]

For every positive integer \(T\), a fixed finite-copy construction proves

\[
\boxed{
L(f)+\frac{\Lambda}{T}\bigl(u(f)-f(E)\bigr)\ge0.
}
\tag{2}
\]

To prove this, build a tree of depth \(T\). At each node, include one conditional-copy replacement for each of the \(q\) separators.

Every node profile agrees with \(f\) on the protected marginals. If \(L(f)=-\delta<0\), equation (1) implies that at every node some separator has

\[
d_j(g)\ge\frac{\delta}{\Lambda}.
\]

Following such a child increases total rank by at least \(\delta/\Lambda\). After \(T\) levels,

\[
g_T(E)\ge f(E)+\frac{T\delta}{\Lambda}.
\]

But all singleton ranks remain fixed, so \(g_T(E)\le u(f)\). Rearrangement gives (2). If \(\Lambda=0\), the original certificate already proves \(L\ge0\) without enforcing the separator equalities.

This construction is finite but expensive. With \(m=|E|\), it uses at most

\[
m\sum_{t=0}^{T}q^t
\]

ground-set labels. A full Shannon formulation on \(N\) such labels has \(2^N-1\) rank coordinates.

Thus the result is **not** a polynomial-time entropy algorithm. It is a resource-explicit conversion from a one-step MAXE certificate to finite-copy certificates with controlled error.
