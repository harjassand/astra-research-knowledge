## 2. A sharp a-posteriori norm certificate

Suppose Y>=0 is bounded and an ACQUIRED whole-operator bound satisfies

    ||Y-F_P(Y)|| <= eta,   eta>=0.

Then

    ||Y-X_P|| <= R_a(eta)
               = (eta+sqrt(eta^2+4eta/a))/2.                    (2)

Proof. Put d=||Y-X_P||. The triangle inequality and (1) imply
d<=eta+d/(1+ad). Thus a d^2-a eta d-eta<=0, whose nonnegative
root is (2). No spectral gap or compactness is used. Conversely, for any e>=0,

    eta <= a e^2/(1+ae)  implies  ||Y-X_P||<=e.                 (3)

The dependence is sharp: Phi=Id, P=0 has X_P=0, and Y=eI has
eta=e-g_a(e)=a e^2/(1+ae) and error exactly e. It works in finite
and infinite dimension. For small e the required norm residual is quadratic,
eta~a e^2, rather than a linear accuracy promise. This is a sufficient worst-case
precision requirement; problem-specific structure can improve it.

This is the same scalar stability mechanism as the source's outside-g_a
essential-norm floor, now applied to a fixed-point approximation. It does not
make a nonzero residual certify compactness. On an infinite-dimensional H the
sharp example Y=eI is noncompact for every e>0.

If the supplied source is approximate instead, Q>=0 and ||P-Q||<=zeta, the source's
inside-g_a perturbation proof gives

    ||X_P-X_Q|| <= S_a(zeta)
                 = (sqrt(zeta^2+4zeta/a)-zeta)/2.               (4)

Therefore an approximate solve for F_Q has error at most
R_a(eta)+S_a(zeta) relative to X_P. Outside and inside errors have
different signs in their root formulas and must not be interchanged.
