STEADY-STATE RESPONSE TO ARBITRARY RECURRING GLOBAL-GATE JUMPS
Internal theorem candidate, 2026-10-10. No exact-output or novelty claim.

This is a genuinely different operation from the balanced-gate hitting-CDF
extension in COUPLED_EXTENSION.txt. The gate network here need NOT preserve
the product stationary law. Its repeated jumps change the stationary law;
the result gives affordable additive stationary observables and finite
response differences. It does not claim a poly-log hitting-CDF query for
this unbalanced class.

Theorem and input

Let Q0=sum_i Q_i be the tensor-coordinate sum of explicitly supplied
irreducible rational local CTMC generators on d_i states. Let pi0 be their
product stationary law. Give k explicit global configurations G and any
rational k by k row-generator Gamma, with no balance condition. Define

    Q=Q0+E_G Gamma E_G^T.                              (1)

Q is irreducible and its stationary law pi may be nonproduct. Give a rational
observable in product-sum form

    g(x)=sum_{ell=1}^r c_ell prod_i g_{ell,i}(x_i),      (2)

with explicitly supplied rational local arrays, and rational epsilon>0.
There is a deterministic algorithm returning a rational answer within
epsilon of pi g in bit time polynomial in the full input length, k, r,
and log(1/epsilon), without constructing N=prod_i d_i states. Running it
twice estimates the finite steady-state response between any two supplied
gate policies to additive epsilon. No hidden stationary law or mixing-time
oracle is supplied. Exact rational pi g may have a prohibitively long
expanded representation, and the theorem does not produce an unbiased
single-sample estimator or an infinitesimal derivative guarantee.

Explicit mixing certificate, with only polynomial bit length

Discard one-state local factors. Let Lambda=sum_i max_x[-Q_i(x,x)] and
rG=max_j[-Gamma(j,j)], so every exit rate of Q is at most Lambda+rG.
Let qmin be the least positive offdiagonal rate in any Q_i, and let
L=sum_i(d_i-1). From any product state x there is a directed path to a
fixed reference state x* using at most L local-coordinate transitions:
each irreducible local directed graph has a simple path of length <=d_i-1.

Set M=2(Lambda+rG), P=I+Q/M, p=min(qmin/M,1/2), and T=L/M. P has
self-loop probability at least 1/2 and each required local path edge has
probability at least qmin/M. Uniformization over time T has Poisson mean
L; the event of exactly L clock rings followed by the selected path and
self-loop padding gives, for every x,

    [exp(TQ)](x,x*) >= e^(-L) L^L/L! p^L
                     >= (p/4)^L =: eta.            (3)

Here e<4 and L!<=L^L. M,T,p,eta are explicit positive rationals, and
log(1/eta) is polynomial in the input length. Doeblin contraction yields

    ||alpha exp(tQ)-pi||_TV <=(1-eta)^floor(t/T)     (4)

for any initial law alpha. Put the computable rational bound
H_g=sum_ell |c_ell| prod_i max_x |g_{ell,i}(x)| >=||g||_infty.
If H_g=0, output zero. For any s>0, the Abel resolvent

    A_s(g):=s*pi0^T(sI-Q)^(-1)g
           =int_0^infty s e^(-st) pi0^T exp(tQ)g dt

satisfies, by (4), splitting the integral into intervals of length T,

    |A_s(g)-pi g| <=2 H_g sT/eta.                 (5)

Choose rational s=epsilon*eta/(8 H_g T), obtaining bias <=epsilon/4.
The numerical value 1/s may be huge, but log(1/s) is polynomial in the
binary input and log(1/epsilon).

Small exact resolvent formula at positive s

Let R0(s)=(sI-Q0)^(-1) and R(s)=(sI-Q)^(-1). The usual finite-rank
resolvent identity, valid because s>0, reads

    R=R0+R0 E_G J(s) Gamma E_G^T R0,
    J(s)=[I-Gamma E_G^T R0 E_G]^(-1).              (6)

The inverse is only k by k and needs no inverse of Gamma. Because pi0 is
stationary for Q0, pi0^T R0=pi0^T/s. Thus the scalar computed is

    A_s(g)=pi0 g + pi0(G) J(s) Gamma v(s),
    v(s)=E_G^T R0(s)g,                             (7)

where pi0(G) is the row of the k listed product stationary masses.
The only nontrivial base queries are the k by k matrix
M0(s)=E_G^T R0(s)E_G and the k-vector v(s). All are one-dimensional
Laplace integrals of products of local heat entries:

    M0(j,l)=int_0^infty e^(-st)
                prod_i [exp(tQ_i)]_{g_j(i),g_l(i)} dt,

    v_j=sum_ell c_ell int_0^infty e^(-st)
                prod_i [exp(tQ_i)g_{ell,i}]_{g_j(i)} dt. (8)

So each integrand is evaluated with local d_i by d_i exponentials and
O(n(k^2+kr)) local selected contractions; no product-state enumeration.

An exact CONDITION bound removes the dangerous apparent Woodbury pole.
Since R(s) is a Markov resolvent, its entries are nonnegative and every
row sums to 1/s. The identity

    J(s)=I+Gamma E_G^T R(s)E_G                 (9)

therefore gives

    ||J(s)||_infty <=1+2rG/s,                  (10)

while ||M0(s)||_infty<=1/s and ||v(s)||_infty<=H_g/s.
Consequently finite-precision perturbation bounds for (7) require only
polynomially many guard bits in log(1/s), log rG, log H_g, k and epsilon.
For example, it suffices to make each M0 and v entry accurate to
epsilon divided by a sufficiently large fixed polynomial in
k, 1/s, 1+rG, 1+H_g, and max_j pi0(g_j)^(-1). Every such polynomial has
polynomial bit length. This condition bound holds despite the altered,
unknown stationary pi.

Bit-complexity of the positive-s base entries

For a selected entry in (8) with absolute tolerance delta, truncate the
time integral below vmin=delta/(16 H0 max(1,H_g)) and above

    vmax=(1/s) ceil(log_2(32 H0 max(1,H_g)/(s delta))),

where H0=1/prod_i min_x pi_i(x), computed from local stationary solves.
The real-time lower/upper tails are <=delta/8 each. Split the remaining
range into O(log(vmax/vmin)) dyadic intervals. The local directed-cycle
sector bound from RESULT.txt implies that every local complex semigroup
exp(zQ_i) is contractive in L2(pi_i) when
|Im z|<=Re z/(32d), d=max_i d_i. Therefore the integrand on each dyadic
interval is analytic and bounded by a computable H0-dependent rational
on a Bernstein ellipse of parameter 1+1/(64d). The elementary Gaussian
quadrature estimate (6.1) of RESULT.txt gives geometric error with
O(d[log(1/delta)+input bit length]) nodes per interval. Approximate
Legendre nodes/weights and local matrix exponentials by polynomial-bit
arithmetic, using scaling and squaring for exponentially large t. Thus
the k^2+kr entries in (8), followed by (7), are computed to the requested
precision in polynomial bit time. The bias allocation (5) then completes
the theorem.

This class is operationally substantive: Gamma may direct 0^n to 1^n
whenever the all-zero configuration occurs, without a balancing reverse
jump. Such a jump shifts the stationary law and can change a product-sum
observable such as the expected number of active coordinates. It can recur
arbitrarily often. At long physical horizons, direct simulation and
uniformization pay for time or inverse precision, whereas (7) pays for
their binary logarithms. This is a finite-output accuracy statement, not
a universal symbolic exactness result.

Familiar baseline and novelty boundary

The algebraic rank-k update is standard Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury, and
Markov-chain finite perturbation methods are established; see Hunter,
The Computation of Key Properties of Markov Chains via Perturbations
(2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05247 . The additional guarantee here
comes from the explicit product heat interface, a rational Doeblin
certificate for the coupled chain, analytic dyadic quadrature, and the
positive-s stochastic-resolvent condition bound (9). This is internally
proved for the stated input; no literature-priority claim is made.
