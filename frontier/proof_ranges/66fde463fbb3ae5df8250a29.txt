## 6. From the classification to a genuine global Jensen theorem

### Lemma 6.1: gradients confined to a planar matrix subspace

Let G=Dphi be a mean-zero periodic planar gradient taking its values in a real linear subspace L of matrix space, dim L<=2. Then every continuous rank-one-convex quartic satisfies

    E F(A0+G)>=F(A0) for every A0.                           (6.1)

**Proof.** If det vanishes identically on L, every direction in L is rank one. The restriction of F to A0+L is convex, so ordinary Jensen applies.

If det|L is nonzero semidefinite, its mean zero forces G almost everywhere into its nullspace, of dimension at most one. This is either the zero field or a rank-one line, and Jensen again applies.

The remaining case is dim L=2 with det|L indefinite. Its rank-one cone is the union of two distinct lines spanned by R1=a1 tensor b1 and R2=a2 tensor b2. Their input directions b1,b2 must be independent: otherwise the whole plane L would have rank at most one.

For each Fourier frequency k, compatibility gives G_hat(k)=i phi_hat(k) tensor k. Its real and imaginary parts belong to L and are rank-one matrices with the same real input direction k. Hence that coefficient lies on one of the two rank-one lines, and k is parallel to the corresponding b_j. Any direction that occurs can be represented by a primitive integer frequency k_j. Thus

    G(x)=R1 g1(k1.x)+R2 g2(k2.x),

with mean-zero scalar periodic profiles. Since k1,k2 are independent integer vectors, the map from the spatial torus to its two phases sends Haar measure to product Haar measure. Integrate first over phase one and apply rank-one Jensen, then over phase two. This proves (6.1). Smooth approximation preserving L gives the W^{1,4} case. QED.

The general use of rank-one subspaces and low-wave laminate geometry has substantial prior art [3]. No novelty is claimed for that background. The new classification is what forces this geometric situation for arbitrary F in the full cone when its averaged Hessian degenerates.

### Theorem C: global positivity on every singular averaged-Hessian face

Let F be any left-SO(2)-invariant ROC homogeneous quartic, with all nine coefficients unrestricted. Let phi be periodic W^{1,4}, or belong to W^{1,4}_0(Omega;R^2) for a bounded Lipschitz domain. If

    H=E D^2F(Dphi) is singular,

then

    integral [F(A0+Dphi)-F(A0)] >=0 for every constant A0.    (6.2)

**Proof.** If F belongs to E, use its explicit polyconvex decomposition (4.1): ordinary Jensen controls the convex fourth power, and determinant preservation followed by scalar Jensen controls gamma q^2.

If F is not in E, Theorem B forces rank C<=2. Since C is the second moment, every vector in ker C has zero squared pairing with G almost everywhere. Thus G takes values in ran C, a subspace of dimension at most two. Lemma 6.1 supplies the positive Jensen construction.

For zero-boundary phi, extend it by zero into a larger periodic square. Homogeneity gives D^2F(0)=0, so the new averaged Hessian is a positive scalar multiple of the original one and remains singular. The relative energy is zero outside Omega. Apply the periodic conclusion and use density. QED.

This is an actual global variational inequality, not a test of an acoustic Gram matrix. It includes arbitrary numbers of Fourier modes in the allowed profiles and does not assume a fixed bandwidth. It does not classify all homogeneous gradient Young measures obtained as limits of nearly singular fields; that quantitative/limit issue remains explicit.

### Consequence for the unresolved counterexample mechanism

Any actual compatible counterexample inside the nine-coefficient cone must have

    rank C>=3 and H>0,

and F cannot belong to E. In that sector the affine background can be minimized exactly by (3.8). A cubic term cannot exploit an exactly flat averaged-Hessian direction: it vanishes there by Theorem A, and the complete compatible variational gap is nonnegative there by Theorem C.

This excludes exact flat-face escape, not all near-flat sequences. It supplies neither a universal lower bound for the positive-definite Schur remainder nor an a priori lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of H for normalized candidates.
