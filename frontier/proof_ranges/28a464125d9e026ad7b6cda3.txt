## 5. The important negative result: real-frequency contrast does not imply a large equilibrium effect

For the same ideal dielectric reference, the largest relative difference of r_p(i xi) over xi=0.01–1e5 cm^-1 and q=1e6–4e8 rad/m is about 4.13e-4 (0.0413%). The response difference near a real polariton resonance is therefore a poor surrogate for the size of the equilibrium perturbation. No target response is calculated from the 13% witness contrast.

A useful independent harmonic bound makes this explicit. For a common positive stiffness matrix K and two positive mass matrices satisfying

    r^-1 M_A <= M_B <= r M_A,

the zero-temperature displacement covariance obeys

    C(M) = hbar/pi integral_0^infinity (K + xi^2 M)^-1 dxi,
    r^-1/2 C(M_A) <= C(M_B) <= r^1/2 C(M_A).

Proof: bound the resolvent by matrix monotonicity, integrate, and substitute sqrt(r) xi for uniformly scaled M. No weak-coupling or well-separated-mode assumption enters this finite-dimensional harmonic result. The code checks the integral against normal modes and tests the bound for 60 positive stiffness matrices, including stiff/soft spectra.

For moving boron masses 10 and 11 while leaving every other mass unchanged, r=1.1 is a conservative nominal-mass bound. The factors are about 0.95346 and 1.04881. This bounds displacement covariance only when K and the coordinate/coupling definition are unchanged. It is NOT a bound on Tc, critical susceptibility, all electromagnetic observables or anharmonic electronic order. Actual isotope-dependent equilibrium geometry violates the common-K hypothesis.

In a classical configurational equilibrium distribution with a fixed potential and position-independent masses, mass permutation changes no configurational expectation: momentum integrals only supply normalisation factors. Hence a claim of a large classical equilibrium material change driven solely by mass reassignment would be inconsistent.

The mass bound is a guard against hidden gain. It does not make the material sensitivity needed for an important measurement available for free.
