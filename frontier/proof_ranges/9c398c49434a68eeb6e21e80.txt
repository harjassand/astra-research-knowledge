2026-10-15. Root derivation, internally checked only. This is a synthesis of classical grounded Green functions, killing and Kron reduction, not a historical novelty or algorithmic advantage claim. It identifies the exact receiving operation needed by a scalar-capacity approach.

Let a finite connected reversible graph have positive conductances c and masses w. Fix singleton terminals s,t, let I exclude them, K=L_II, b_i=c_is, and h_I=K^{-1}b. Put h_s=1,h_t=0. Then N=w_s+w_I^T h_I and E_s tau_t=N/C_st. All graph coefficients are supplied, including every mass. Empty I gives N=w_s.

Add a third terminal a and edges a--i of conductance lambda w_i for every i!=t. Retain s,t,a and eliminate I by the exact Schur complement. Denote its positive a--s conductance by g_as(lambda). With D=diag(w_I),

    g_as(lambda)/lambda = w_s+w_I^T(K+lambda D)^{-1}b =: N_lambda.

This follows by the offdiagonal Schur formula: the I--a column is -lambda w_I and I--s is -b, so the new offdiagonal is -lambda w_s-lambda w_I^T(K+lambda D)^{-1}b. Every term of the resulting conductance is nonnegative. No subtractive finite difference is needed for this transfer coefficient.

Let r_j be any supplied path-resistance upper bound from j to the wired boundary {s,t}; shortest resistance paths give one O(m log n) construction. Define B=sum_(j in I) w_j r_j. Then, for every lambda>0,

    N_lambda <= N <= w_s+(1+lambda B)(N_lambda-w_s)
                     <= (1+lambda B)N_lambda.

Proof: K and K+lambda D are nonsingular M-matrices, so h_lambda=(K+lambda D)^{-1}b is nonnegative and h-h_lambda=lambda K^{-1}D h_lambda. Let u=K^{-1}w_I, the mean exit-time vector for {s,t}. The Green column K^{-1}_{.,j} attains its maximum at j by the discrete maximum principle, and K^{-1}_{jj}=R_eff(j,{s,t})<=r_j. Hence 0<=u_i<=sum_j w_jr_j=B. Therefore N-N_lambda=lambda u^T D h_lambda<=lambda B(N_lambda-w_s), as claimed. For I empty take B=0.

Thus lambda<=epsilon/B turns a relative certificate for the *positive transfer conductance* g_as into a relative certificate for N, and a separately certified C gives a directional-mean interval. The extra terminal changes the information retained: two-terminal capacity alone is insufficient.

## Why this is not a new fast solver

The required object is an offdiagonal three-terminal response coefficient. A generic Loewner/spectral or additive-energy guarantee need not control a very small offdiagonal coefficient relatively. Reconstructing g_as by subtracting three scalar wired-terminal capacities can require much higher precision than any individual capacity. The incoming deletion/contraction scalar-capacity theorem does not automatically certify this new coefficient.

The bound B can be enormous. A heavy source-attached leaf with mass M and edge delta has exit time M/delta, forcing lambda about epsilon delta/M. Classical graph transformation can instead transfer its full mass to the attachment exactly without needing this small probe. Therefore the auxiliary construction is a precise positive interface and finite-bias theorem, not an established improvement over mass-carrying elimination or a direct selected-output solve.

No physical rate uncertainty, missing-path acquisition, first-passage distribution or tail guarantee is supplied. Root's primary comparisons: Trygubenko–Wales2006, DOI10.1063/1.2198806 (exact path/holding-time elimination); Dörfler–Bullo2013, arXiv1102.2950 (grounded/self-loop Kron framework); the actual pinned PyGT source acquires the stronger mean-preserving operation.
