## 6. The exact compositional requirement forces decoupling

The missing condition can be stated without an entanglement measure.

Suppose a successful joint state \(\Omega_{SC}\) has marginals \(\sigma,\tau\). Require that, after every nonzero-probability local product measurement outcome \(E_A\otimes E_B\) on \(S\), the conditional catalyst state remains \(\tau\).

Then
\[
\operatorname{tr}_S[(E\otimes I)\Omega]
=
\operatorname{tr}(E\sigma)\,\tau
\tag{33}
\]
for every product effect \(E\). Zero-probability outcomes satisfy the same equality automatically.

Product effects span the operator space. Hence
\[
\boxed{\Omega=\sigma\otimes\tau.}
\tag{34}
\]

The converse is immediate.

Thus, in this setting, protecting the return guarantee against arbitrary subsequent free postselection is exactly an **uncorrelated-return requirement**.

And with a finite catalyst, that stronger requirement cannot create any entangled state from a free input—even probabilistically.

Indeed, an exact successful transformation
\[
\tau\longmapsto p\,\sigma\otimes\tau
\]
could be repeated to produce \(\sigma^{\otimes n}\) on a nonzero successful branch, while keeping its Schmidt number bounded by \(\operatorname{SN}(\tau)\). But for every entangled \(\sigma\), the same fixed informationally complete measurement argument gives a constant \(\kappa_\sigma>0\) with
\[
E_R(\sigma^{\otimes n})\ge n\kappa_\sigma.
\tag{35}
\]
This contradicts the bounded Schmidt-number ceiling.

The conclusion preserves the earlier results rather than weakening their assumptions:

The complementary-spectrum construction used exact infinite support, so it has no finite Schmidt-number ceiling. The tensor-universality construction consumes a nonfree source state at each conversion. The channel-removal results concern universally usable operations with their stipulated whole-protocol return conditions. None is contradicted by the finite, success-only, correlated state preparations here.
