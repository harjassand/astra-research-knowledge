## 4. A more general proved representation principle

The construction establishes something beyond the undecidability consequence.

### Affine fixed-locus representation theorem

Every affine scheme of finite presentation over \\(\mathbb Z\\) is isomorphic to a scheme-theoretic retract of the fixed-point scheme of a quadratic integral symplectic polynomial automorphism of some affine space.

Proof. Present the scheme as the common zero locus of finitely many integer polynomials. Replace those equations by quadratic arithmetic-circuit equations. This replacement preserves the represented functor on commutative rings.

For the quadratic system \\(Q(z)=0\\), construct

\\[ H(z,\lambda)=\sum_i\lambda_iQ_i(z) \\]

and the associated symplectic shear \\(F_H\\).

There is a projection

\\[ \rho:\operatorname{Fix}(F_H)\to V(Q), \qquad (z,\lambda,p)\mapsto z, \\]

because the fixed-point equations include \\(Q_i(z)=0\\).

There is also a section

\\[ \sigma:V(Q)\to\operatorname{Fix}(F_H), \qquad z\mapsto(z,0,0). \\]

Both are polynomial morphisms defined over \\(\mathbb Z\\), and

\\[ \rho\circ\sigma=\operatorname{id}\_{V(Q)}. \\]

Thus \\(V(Q)\\), and hence the original affine scheme, is a scheme-theoretic retract. \\(\square\\)

This theorem identifies the precise mechanism:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Arbitrary algebraic constraints} \longrightarrow \text{quadratic gates} \longrightarrow \text{cubic Hamiltonian} \longrightarrow \text{quadratic symplectic fixed points}. } \\]

It is valid over arbitrary commutative rings. The undecidability consequence is one application over \\(\mathbb Z\\).

## 5. Premise testing and limits

An elementary fixture checks the construction.

Take the quadratic system

\\[ y-x^2=0,\qquad y-2=0. \\]

Its Hamiltonian is

\\[ H=u(y-x^2)+v(y-2). \\]

Then

\\[ \nabla H=(-2ux,\ u+v,\ y-x^2,\ y-2). \\]

A fixed point forces \\(x^2=2\\). There are real fixed points, but no rational or integer fixed points. Direct symbolic checks confirmed the gradient identities, symplectic condition and inverse composition.&#x20;



The important limitation is that the theorem exploits exact arithmetic. It does not establish undecidability of trajectory simulation, finite-precision physical measurement, or fixed-point existence on compact symplectic manifolds.

Indeed, trajectory simulation for these maps is explicit. The dynamical difficulty is eliminated; all computational hardness is concentrated in deciding whether a polynomially described fixed locus contains an integer point.

There is also no computable function \\(B(n)\\) that always bounds the height of a smallest integer fixed point whenever one exists. Otherwise one could enumerate every integer point of height at most \\(B(n)\\) and decide \\(K\\), contradicting undecidability.

## 6. Source verification and actual novelty

Two distinctions matter.

First, the theorem above does not rely on OpenAI's claimed resolution of Hilbert's tenth problem over \\(\mathbb Q\\). Its undecidability statement follows from the classical, established integer theorem. The October 2026 rational manuscript supplies a related arithmetic-circuit normalization, but the rational decision claim itself is not formalized in the pinned Lean catalogue, and I have not independently verified its long proof.

The same construction gives an exact reduction from rational polynomial solvability to rational fixed-point existence for quadratic symplectic shears. Consequently, if the OpenAI rational-solvability theorem is correct, it transfers to this dynamical formulation. That is a conditional consequence, not an independently certified solution of the rational problem.

Second, the Hamiltonian shear itself is a classical object. Koch and Lomelí study Hamiltonian flows whose trajectories depend linearly on time, including the relationship between cubic Hamiltonians and quadratic symplectic maps.&#x20;
