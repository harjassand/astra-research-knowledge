## 4. Contradiction witnesses and an exact overlap formula

Return to the general preprocessed system, with \(U>0\), and define the affine code

\[
\mathcal C=\{c\in\mathbb F_2^m:A^Tc=0,\ b^Tc=1\}.
\]

If \(r_A=\operatorname{rank}A\) and \(k=m-r_A\), then \(|\mathcal C|=K=2^{k-1}\). The functional \(b^T\) is nonzero on \(\ker A^T\), because the full system is inconsistent. For a retained set \(S\), let

\[
N(S)=|\{c\in\mathcal C:\operatorname{supp}c\subseteq S\}|.
\]

For inconsistent \(S\), the same argument restricted to \(S\) gives the exact identity

\[
N(S)=2^{|S|-\operatorname{rank}A_S-1};
\tag{4.1}
\]

for consistent \(S\), it is zero. Thus one Gaussian rank computation counts every overlapping contradiction witness without enumerating them.

Assign witness weights

\[
w(c)=\prod_{e:c_e=1}p_e,\qquad Q=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}w(c)=\mathbb E_\mu N(S).
\]

Sample \(c\) with probability \(w(c)/Q\), then independently retain every edge outside its support with its original probability. The marginal retained-set law is

\[
\pi(S)=\frac{\mu(S)N(S)}Q.
\]

Every sample is inconsistent. For \(Y=1/N(S)\),

\[
\mathbb E_\pi Y=U/Q,\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_\pi Y}{(\mathbb E_\pi Y)^2}
\le Q/U-1\le K-1.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The first inequality uses \(0<Y\le1\), so \(\mathbb E Y^2\le\mathbb E Y\). This is the classical union-mixture/Karp–Luby construction; the rank formula is its useful algebraic realization here.

## 5. A retention-mass bound on certificate overlap

### Theorem 5.1

For every binary affine input with independent retention,

\[
\boxed{\quad Q\le UP,\qquad P:=\prod_{e=1}^m(1+p_e)\le\exp\!\left(\sum_ep_e\right).\quad}
\tag{5.1}
\]

When \(U>0\), this is equivalently \(Q/U\le P\). When \(U=0\), one has \(Q=0\), and no quotient is asserted. The bound has no dependence on \(U^{-1}\), coefficient rank, equation arity, or the number of witnesses.

**Proof.** The case \(U=0\) is immediate from \(Q=\mathbb E_\mu N(S)=0\), so assume \(U>0\). Let \(\mathcal M\) be the family of inclusion-minimal contradictory supports, ordered in any fixed way. For each inconsistent \(S\), let \(C\) be the first member of \(\mathcal M\) contained in \(S\). This partitions the inconsistent event. A contradiction witness on a minimal support must use every coordinate of that support, so over \(\mathbb F_2\) it is the indicator \(1_C\).

First, there is no nonzero vector \(d\) supported inside \(C\) with \(A^Td=0\) and \(b^Td=0\). Otherwise, the all-one indicator \(1_C\) is a contradiction witness, and \(1_C+d\) is a contradiction witness with strictly smaller support, contradicting minimality. Consequently, the map

\[
\{c\in\mathcal C:\operatorname{supp}c\subseteq S\}\longrightarrow
\mathbb F_2^{S\setminus C},\qquad c\longmapsto c|_{S\setminus C}
\]

is injective: two witnesses agreeing off \(C\) differ by a forbidden zero-right-side dependency on \(C\). Therefore

\[
N(S)\le2^{|S\setminus C|}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Condition on \(C\subseteq S\). Outside \(C\), retention is still a product measure. The further condition that no earlier minimal contradictory support is contained in \(S\) is a **decreasing** event in those outside retention bits. Harris correlation implies that conditioning a product measure on a decreasing event cannot increase the mean of the increasing random variable \(2^{|S\setminus C|}\). Thus

\[
\mathbb E[N(S)\mid C\text{ is first}]\le
\prod_{e\notin C}(1+p_e)\le P.
\]

All conditioning events of positive mass are legitimate. The outside-empty configuration avoids every earlier minimal support, since one minimal support cannot properly contain another. Averaging over the partition yields

\[
Q/U=\mathbb E[N(S)\mid S\text{ inconsistent}]\le P.
\]

Finally \(1+p_e\le e^{p_e}\). ∎

For completeness, the Harris inequality needed here is elementary: for increasing functions \(f,g\) of independent binary coordinates, \(\operatorname{Cov}(f,g)\ge0\). Induct on the number of coordinates. Conditional covariance on the last coordinate is nonnegative by induction, and covariance of the two conditional means is nonnegative because both means increase with that last bit. Apply this to \(f=2^{|S\setminus C|}\) and \(g=-1_D\), where \(D\) is the decreasing avoidance event.

The proof uses the special affine-code injectivity in (5.2). For arbitrary monotone DNF witnesses this injectivity need not hold: \(C=\{1,2\}\), \(D_1=\{1,3\}\), \(D_2=\{2,3\}\) give the same restriction outside \(C\). The inequality is therefore not being asserted for arbitrary certificate families.

This exponential retention-mass dependence cannot simply be removed from this particular estimator. Take \(m_0\) copies of the left coefficient \(a=1\) with right side zero, and \(m_1\) copies with right side one, all retained with fixed probability \(p\). This is one connected coefficient-matroid block. Then

\[
U=[1-(1-p)^{m_0}][1-(1-p)^{m_1}],
\]

\[
Q=\tfrac14[(1+p)^{m_0}-(1-p)^{m_0}]
               [(1+p)^{m_1}-(1-p)^{m_1}].
\]

For large balanced \(m_0,m_1\), \(Q/U\) is asymptotic to \((1+p)^m/4\), where \(m=m_0+m_1\). A large upper bound alone does not prove large actual variance. To compute that variance, define

\[
B_i=(1-p/2)^{m_i}-(1-p)^{m_i},\qquad i=0,1.
\]

When both row types are present, \(N(S)=2^{|S|-2}\). Independence of their retained counts therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E_\pi[Y^2]
=\frac1Q\mathbb E_\mu[1/N(S);\ S\text{ inconsistent}]
=\frac{4B_0B_1}{Q},
\]

\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_\pi(Y)}{(\mathbb E_\pi Y)^2}
=\frac{4QB_0B_1}{U^2}-1.
\]

For \(m_0=m_1\to\infty\), the relative variance plus one is asymptotic to

\[
[(1+p)(1-p/2)]^m,
\]

whose base is \(1+p(1-p)/2>1\). Thus this estimator's actual relative variance is exponential. The event itself has the easy closed-form probability above; this is an estimator limitation, not an instance-hardness argument.
