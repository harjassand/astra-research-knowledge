## 5. Constructing exact marginals—not just proving they exist

The walk itself need not be unbiased. Exact unbiasedness is constructed afterward from the objective-aware algorithm.

For fixed \(A,x\), define the good bases
\[
\mathcal G=
\left\{
\mathbf1_B:
B\text{ a basis},
\ \|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le C
\right\}.
\]

The one-basis algorithm supplies the response rule
\[
\forall c\in\mathbb Q^n,\qquad
\text{return }y\in\mathcal G
\text{ with }c^\top(y-x)\le0.
\]

An ellipsoid decomposition converts that rule into an explicit convex combination. This is a classical metarounding-type conversion, not a newly invented part of the spectral method. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/rsa.10033)

Here is the constructive argument.

Suppose a finite collection \(S\) of returned bases does not contain \(x\) in its convex hull. Then a strictly separating vector can be scaled to satisfy
\[
(y-x)^\top c\ge1
\qquad(y\in S).
\]

Because the coordinates of \(y\) are Boolean and \(x\) has a known rational denominator, determinant bounds give a uniform polynomial-bit bound on such a separating vector. After another constant scaling, its feasible region contains a ball of explicitly bounded radius inside a known outer ball.

At each ellipsoid center \(c\), the rounding algorithm returns a basis violating the proposed separating inequality:
\[
(y-x)^\top c\le0.
\]

Record that basis and make the corresponding ellipsoid cut. Polynomially many cuts reduce the enclosing volume below the ball that would exist if \(x\notin\operatorname{conv}(S)\). Therefore the recorded bases must contain \(x\) in their convex hull.

Finally solve the explicit rational linear program
\[
\sum_{y\in S}\lambda_y y=x,\qquad
\sum_{y\in S}\lambda_y=1,\qquad
\lambda_y\ge0.
\]
A basic feasible solution has at most \(n+1\) support points.

This constructs **exact** marginals. It does not invoke an optimizer over the unknown set of all good bases.

The resulting distribution is also a compact certificate: basis feasibility, rational probabilities, exact marginals, and every discrepancy inequality can be checked without replaying the spectral walk.

There is one unavoidable sampling qualification. A probability such as \(1/3\) cannot be implemented with a uniformly bounded number of fair random bits. The distribution is constructed deterministically in polynomial time; exact sampling from it uses **expected** polynomial time through rational-probability rejection sampling.
