The old pointwise exponential separation cannot hold uniformly throughout a polynomial-radius unrestricted rate neighborhood.
Using its notation, add
\[ \Delta Q = \rho\left( |f\rangle\langle d_1| + |c_\ell\rangle\langle f| \right), \qquad 0<\rho\le\frac1{16}. \]
Each physical matrix entry changes by at most \(2\rho/n\). The perturbation preserves positivity, rapid mixing, uniform stationarity, the instantaneous observed generator, and the label-preserving reflection that gives observed time symmetry.
Nevertheless, with \(\varepsilon=1/(16r)\), the autocorrelation becomes
\[ C_\rho(t) = e^{-t}\cosh\!\left(\sqrt{2\varepsilon\rho}\,t\right). \]
Therefore,
\[ \boxed{ C_\rho(2t)-C_\rho(t)^2 = e^{-2t}\sinh^2\!\left(\sqrt{2\varepsilon\rho}\,t\right) \ge 2\varepsilon\rho\,t^2e^{-2t}. } \tag{17} \]
A balanced two-state Markov process satisfies \(C(2t)=C(t)^2\). When \(\rho\) is inverse-polynomial in \(r\), bounded pair statistics detect the violation with polynomially many samples.
This refutes a uniform exponential lower bound for every model in such a neighborhood. It does not invalidate the earlier exact construction, and it does not imply that every perturbation makes passive inference easy.
The broader result avoids that fragile objective: it measures the actual system’s controlled-closure residual, whether or not passive concealment survives.
Positive \(\Gamma\) alone does not imply dissipation. A reversible system can have hidden rate heterogeneity. Conversely, \(\Gamma=0\) does not exclude an invisible dissipative component.
A dissipation certificate requires comparing the positive residual with passive curvature.
For a balanced binary observable \(f\), define
\[ \lambda_f=-C_f'(0),\qquad d_f=\|(I-P)Qf\|_\pi^2, \qquad k_f=C_f''(0)-\lambda_f^2. \]
Under ordinary stationary time reversal, with all microscopic variables even and exit rates at most \(\Lambda\),
\[ \boxed{ \sigma(Q)\ge \frac{(d_f-k_f)^2}{32\Lambda^3}. } \tag{18} \]
The proof combines the response-completion identity with current conservation, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the scalar entropy-production inequality
\[ (x-y)\log(x/y)\ge\frac{2(x-y)^2}{x+y}. \]
For the passive-equivalent systems with an equilibrium binary observed law, \(k_f=0\). The active certificate then gives a positive dissipation floor for realizations matching the specified observation-and-intervention interface. It cannot give that floor from the passive law alone, which has an equilibrium realization.
There is also a definite limit to what \(\Gamma\) identifies. In the four-state example, the family
\[ Q_\beta = J-I+\varepsilon\frac{df^\top}{4} +\beta\frac{dd^\top}{4} \]
has the same entire passive law and the same \(\Gamma\), while its higher controlled responses vary with \(\beta\).
Thus:
\[ \boxed{ \Gamma\text{ is a closure and error certificate, not a complete hidden-system description.} } \]
The unresolved general problem is identifying useful hidden dynamics when the residual is large, and acquiring the kinetic control law without assuming it from the outset.
The strongest supported capability is experimental certification of a reduced controlled state without reconstructing the microscopic system. Its guarantees cover arbitrary finite hidden models under the stated acquisition assumptions, and complete feedback-controlled trajectories under the stated regularity bounds.
The access obstruction and the perturbation counterexample also delimit the claim: active information is not determined by passive dynamics plus thermodynamic rate ratios, and the original exponential separation is not uniformly robust to unrestricted polynomial-sized rate errors.
