## 1. The obstruction and the retained principle

For a real symmetric matrix-valued driver, consider

\[
\dot U(t)=S(t)U(t),\qquad U(0)=I,\qquad S(t)^T=S(t).
\]

The elementary estimate obtained by taking norms before integrating is

\[
\|U(t)\|\leq \exp\!\left(\int_0^t\|S(s)\|\,ds\right).
\]

It discards cancellation in time. Set

\[
B(t)=\int_0^t S(s)\,ds.
\]

One cannot simply replace the exponent by \(B(t)\): ordered products do not reduce to the exponential of an integral. However, after the exact transformation \(V=e^{-B}U\), the first commutator is skew-symmetric and does not change Euclidean length. The symmetric part of the transformed generator starts with

\[
\frac13[B,[B,S]].
\]

The retained principle is therefore specific: **once the integrated symmetric driver is accounted for, a rigorous upper bound on additional amplification can be charged to a double-commutator action.** This is an upper-bound principle, not an assertion that every nonzero double commutator causes growth.

All unqualified matrix norms below are Euclidean operator norms. For a symmetric matrix \(B\), let

\[
r(B)=\lambda_{\max}(B)-\lambda_{\min}(B)
\]

be its spectral width. Define the entire even function

\[
c(r)=\begin{cases}
\dfrac{r\cosh r-\sinh r}{r^3},&r>0,\\[4pt]
\dfrac13,&r=0.
\end{cases}
\]

Its expansion is

\[
c(r)=\frac13+\frac{r^2}{30}+\frac{r^4}{840}+\cdots.
\]
