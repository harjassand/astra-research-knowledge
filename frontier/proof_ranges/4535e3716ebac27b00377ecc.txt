## 8. Exact finite unital moments and bounded-score proof

Rotation invariance gives zero input magnetization and isotropic pair correlations. Summing them after T proves (5), hence (6) using C_N<4. For a general affine channel r->c+Tr there is also the exact identity

\[
\sum_a\operatorname{Var}_{\Lambda^{\otimes N}R_N}J_a
=\frac{N(3-\|T\|_F^2-|c|^2)}4+\frac{\|T\|_F^2C_N}{3}.    \tag{27}
\]

This yields a sufficient condition with ||T||_F^2+|c|^2>1, but is not a complete nonunital criterion (Section 9).

For the bounded score, first take an arbitrary product state with Bloch vectors r_i and fix an axis a. The scaled local outcomes are independent, supported on +/-1/(2 sqrt N). Let their sum have mean mu_a and variance

\[
c_a=\frac1{4N}\sum_i(1-r_{ia}^2),\quad
0\le c_a\le1/4,\quad\sum_ac_a\ge1/2.                    \tag{28}
\]

A scalar Lindeberg replacement by a Gaussian of the same mean and variance, for f(x)=exp(-t x^2), gives the uniform estimate

\[
\left|\mathbb E f(J_a/\sqrt N)
-\frac{e^{-t\mu_a^2/(1+2tc_a)}}{\sqrt{1+2tc_a}}\right|
\le t^{3/2}/\sqrt N.                                    \tag{29}
\]

Details of constants: the centered summands have absolute third moments summing to at most 1/(4 sqrt N). Matching independent Gaussians have absolute third moments summing to sqrt(2/pi)/(4 sqrt N). Taylor's remainder is bounded by one sixth of their sum times ||f'''||_infinity; ||f'''||_infinity<=12 t^{3/2}. Their product is <t^{3/2}/sqrt N. This proof is uniform in arbitrary means and heterogeneous mixed product factors.

Discarding the Gaussian mean factor increases the right side. The convex decreasing function c->(1+2tc)^(-1/2) has its maximal sum over (28) at a permutation of (0,1/4,1/4). Consequently

\[
\sum_a\mathbb E e^{-tJ_a^2/N}
\le1+2/\sqrt{1+t/2}+3t^{3/2}/\sqrt N.                    \tag{30}
\]

Mixture linearity extends this to all full-separable states. For the target, e^{-x}>=1-x and (5) give

\[
\sum_a\mathbb E_{\omega_N} e^{-tJ_a^2/N}
\ge3-t\left[\frac{3-s}{4}+\frac{sC_N}{3N}\right].         \tag{31}
\]

Use (1+x)^(-1/2)<=1-x/2+3x^2/8 for x>=0 and t=2delta/3. The difference between (31) and (30) is at least

\[
\frac{\delta^2}{12}
-\frac{2sC_N\delta}{9N}
-3(2\delta/3)^{3/2}/\sqrt N.                             \tag{32}
\]

If N delta>=4096, s<=3 and C_N<4 show that the last two terms are at most

\[
\delta^2\left[\frac{8}{3\cdot4096}+\frac2{64}\right].
\]

The remaining gap is >delta^2/24. Dividing by three proves (8)-(9). Hoeffding's inequality applied to [0,1] scores at the midpoint of a gap g=delta^2/72 gives total error <=2exp(-m g^2/2), proving (10).

This test does not infer finite-N trace distance from weak convergence or from filtering. It directly bounds finite-N measurement laws. No entangling measurement is required: one common chosen axis and N local measurements suffice.

**Optional entropic corollary.** Randomizing a pass bit with probability equal to the score, data processing and binary Pinsker give E_R>=2g^2=delta^4/2592 nats. The same lower bound holds for the regularized relative entropy to full separability across N laboratories: sequential local tests leave any laboratory-separable alternative laboratory-separable conditional on every past history, so the KL chain rule gives m times the one-copy lower bound even with arbitrary local cross-copy quantum correlations. This is a small positive bound, not a logarithmic growth claim after fixed noise.
