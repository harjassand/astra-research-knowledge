PIECEWISE-CONTROLLED PRODUCT-CHAIN SURVIVING-STATE COMPILER
2026-10-10. Constructive mathematical candidate, with a small numerical check.
No priority or independent-validation claim. No repository files changed.

Interface and inherited primitive
---------------------------------
There are n binary coordinates. On segment k of duration T_k>=0, coordinate i
has rational stationary probability p_{ki} in (0,1) and rational relaxation
rate lambda_{ki}>0. The control schedule is explicitly supplied and depends
only on the segment index and survival, not on an unobserved path. A fixed
list B={b_1,...,b_m} of distinct n-bit targets is explicit. Allow B_k to
vary by segment at no mathematical cost, provided each list is explicit.
Initially the law is a rational product Bernoulli law. All times, parameters,
and requested accuracy 0<epsilon<=1/4 have binary-rational encodings.

The one inherited computational lemma is MARKED_EVENTS.txt in the adjacent
work/target_set_resolvent directory: for any rational product starting law,
target b, rational t>=0 and alpha>0, it claims a rational alpha-accurate
marked first-hit sub-CDF F_b(t), in deterministic time polynomial in input
bits, m, and log(1/alpha). Its derivation charges target-set resolvent
conditioning, rare hits, and all precision. The theorem below is conditional
on the correctness of that internal primitive. For signed product mixtures,
call it separately on each product component and sum with signed weights.

Statement
---------
There is a deterministic algorithm which returns rational coefficients c_r
and rational Bernoulli parameters q_{ri} defining a signed product mixture

    tilde_nu_K(x)=sum_{r=1}^R c_r prod_i q_{ri}^{x_i}(1-q_{ri})^{1-x_i}

such that ||tilde_nu_K - nu_K||_1 <= epsilon, where nu_K is the exact
UNNORMALIZED law of the configuration still alive after all K control
segments. The algorithm never forms a 2^n-state vector. Its bit operation
count, output bit length, R, and sum_r |c_r| are polynomial in the explicit
input length, K, n, m, and 1/epsilon. More specifically, the elementary
geometric-age construction has

    R <= 1 + sum_k m(G_k+O(1)),
    G_k = O((nK/epsilon) log(1+T_k Lambda_k K/epsilon)
                        + (nK/epsilon) log(1+Lambda_k/gamma_k)),
    sum_r |c_r| <= 1+2K,

with gamma_k=min_i lambda_{ki}, Lambda_k=sum_i lambda_{ki}. The logarithms
are shorthand for rational bit-length expressions; no physical-time loop or
factor 1/pi_k(B) occurs. The number of CDF calls is polynomial in R,m,G_k;
each requested precision has polynomially many bits.

A stronger fixed-ellipse interpolation variant, derived below, replaces
1/epsilon in the rank by polylog(1/epsilon): with

    D=O(n+log(K/epsilon)),
    L_k=O(log(1+T_k Lambda_k K/epsilon)),

it achieves R <= 1+sum_k m[1+L_k(D+1)] and coefficient l1 at most
1+O(K log(D+2)), plus arbitrarily small rational numerical error. Its CDF
query count and bit complexity remain polynomial in input bits, K,n,m,
log(1/epsilon). This second tier interpolates both the product heat kernel
and the marked CDF, then integrates two rational polynomials exactly. Its
detailed finite-bit construction is given below, but has NOT been separately
coded or interval-certified. The elementary tier is implemented numerically
in demo.py.

Exact boundary identity and the signed-measure invariant
---------------------------------------------------------
Let P_t be the unrestricted product-chain transition kernel and Q_t the
killed kernel on B^c. For any finite signed initial measure zeta, let
F_b^zeta be its signed first-hit measure at b, including a time-zero atom
zeta(b). The strong Markov identity, linear in zeta, is

    zeta Q_T = zeta P_T - sum_b int_[0,T] (delta_b P_(T-s)) dF_b^zeta(s). (1)

Here zeta Q_T is extended by zero on B. A product law stays product under
P_T, and delta_b P_u is product for every u. Thus only the integral needs
compression. Equation (1) applies to signed zeta even though neither its
first-hit measure nor its output need be nonnegative.

The marked first-hit map from an initial state to (b,s) is a substochastic
Markov kernel. Consequently, for ANY signed state measure zeta,

    sum_b |F_b^zeta|([0,T]) <= ||zeta||_1.                 (2)

This is the key invariant. The coefficient l1 C=sum_r|c_r| can be much
larger than ||zeta||_1 because components cancel, but the AGGREGATED signed
exit measure still has total variation bounded by the state norm. If
||tilde_nu_k-nu_k||_1<=epsilon, then ||tilde_nu_k||_1<=1+epsilon. Form all
marked-bin weights by summing CDF contributions from the old components
BEFORE expanding any age kernel. Their absolute sum is at most 1+epsilon
up to numerical error. Expanding each old component separately would use
the weaker bound C and generate a multiplicative rank recurrence.

Elementary geometric-age construction
-------------------------------------
For fixed b and segment parameters, write K_b(u)=delta_b P_u. If
q_i(u)=p_i+(b_i-p_i)exp(-lambda_i u), tensor-product telescoping gives

    ||K_b(u)-K_b(v)||_1 <= 2 sum_i |q_i(u)-q_i(v)|,
    ||dK_b(u)/d(log u)||_TV <= sum_i lambda_i u exp(-lambda_i u)
                                      <= n/e.             (3)

Also ||K_b(u)-delta_b||_1<=2 Lambda u and
||K_b(u)-pi||_1<=2n exp(-gamma u), where pi is the current product
stationary law. Choose a local kernel tolerance xi=epsilon/(64K), and
rational cutoffs

    u0=xi/(2 Lambda),
    U=ceil(log_2(2n/xi))/gamma,
    h=xi/(2n).

Use delta_b for ages u<=u0, pi for ages u>=U, and the left-endpoint
K_b(a) on middle bins [a,min((1+h)a,U,T)]. By (3), every age has L1
kernel error <=xi (the log-step error is <=(2n/e)h<xi). If T lies inside
a bin, truncate that bin at T. For a bin [a,b] with b<T, its signed weight
is F_b(T-a)-F_b(T-b); the final oldest bin ending at age T has weight
F_b(T-a), thereby including the time-zero atom. Endpoints are rational.
The number of bins is at most 2+ceil(log(U/u0)/log(1+h)), truncated by T;
for h<=1/2, log(1+h)>=h/2, giving the stated polynomial bound. Even
astronomically large rational times cost only their bit length.

For current tilde_nu, propagate its R product terms freely. For each b and
age bin, append ONE correction product with coefficient minus the
AGGREGATED signed bin weight. Equation (2) bounds the analytic binning
error by xi(1+epsilon). Compute each aggregate weight to sufficiently small
rational error so their total l1 error is <=epsilon/(16K). Since a weight
is the difference of at most two CDFs, it suffices, for example, to ask
each product-component CDF query for absolute precision

    alpha <= epsilon/[128 K m(G+2) max(1,C)],           (4)

with harmless slack for endpoints, where C=sum_r|c_r| is known exactly.
Approximate every exponential used in the free and correction product
parameters sufficiently well that their combined L1 error is
<=epsilon/(16K); the factor C is charged in the free-part precision.
Rational exponentials and their certified errors can be obtained in time
polynomial in argument bit length and log(1/alpha), using range reduction
and truncation; probabilities can be clamped into [0,1]. The total local
state error can be made <=epsilon/K.

If e_k is the L1 error after k segments, Q_t is a substochastic positive
kernel and hence an L1 contraction. Therefore e_{k+1}<=e_k+epsilon/K and
e_K<=epsilon. If C_k is the coefficient l1, the aggregated marked-bin
measure plus rounding gives

    C_{k+1} <= C_k + ||tilde_nu_k||_1 + epsilon/(16K)
            <= C_k + 1+epsilon+epsilon/(16K).

Starting at C_0=1 and using epsilon<=1/4 gives C_k<=1+2k. The number of
terms satisfies R_{k+1}<=R_k+m(G_k+O(1)), not R_k(1+mG_k). These two
inequalities close the finite-bit induction, including the precision cost
of evaluating the signed CDF sums.

Fixed-ellipse interpolation: stronger accuracy dependence
---------------------------------------------------------
The geometric grid proves the operation with minimal analytic machinery.
The following interpolation makes the number of product terms logarithmic
in 1/epsilon. It uses the same CDF primitive; no first-hit density oracle
is assumed.

For one stage, let tau=epsilon/[64K Lambda(1+epsilon)]. Separate the
time-zero marked atom exactly: its correction is
sum_b tilde_nu(b) K_b(T). The positive-time hit mass in either window
(0,tau] or (T-tau,T] is at most Lambda tau ||tilde_nu||_1, by the bound
on each state's total jump rate. Omit those two windows; their total L1
effect is <=epsilon/(16K). If T<=2tau, all positive-time hits can simply
be omitted and there is no interior calculation.

Otherwise partition the interior age interval [tau,T-tau] into rational
intervals I=[a,b] of width w=b-a at most

    w <= min(a,T-b)/32.                                 (5)

An elementary multiplicative walk from both ends constructs
L=O(log(1+T/tau)) such cells. The Bernstein ellipse E_2(I), with focal
interval I and parameter rho=2, lies strictly in Re u>0 and Re(T-u)>0
by (5). For Re u>=0, one one-bit row of K_b(u) has complex entrywise l1
at most 3, so ||K_b(u)||_1<=3^n. Chebyshev-Lobatto interpolation on I
at D+1 nodes u_j therefore has uniform vector L1 error

    sup_(u in I) ||K_b(u)-sum_j L_j(u)K_b(u_j)||_1
        <= 4*3^n*2^(-D).                                (6)

Choose D=ceil(log_2(256*3^n K/epsilon)); this makes the combined
interpolation error, after integrating against the TOTAL signed marked
hit variation bounded by 1+epsilon, much smaller than epsilon/K.
Each K_b(u_j) is a product law. The exact signed coefficient of that law
in this cell is

    c_(b,I,j) = int_(s in (T-b,T-a]) L_j(T-s) dF_b^+(s), (7)

where F_b^+(t)=F_b(t)-F_b(0) excludes the separated atom. The Chebyshev
Lebesgue constant satisfies sup_u sum_j|L_j(u)|=O(log(D+2)); hence, over
ALL targets and cells,

    sum_(b,I,j) |c_(b,I,j)|
       <= O(log(D+2)) sum_b |F_b^+|((0,T])
       <= O(log(D+2))(1+epsilon).                       (8)

Thus the signed coefficient budget remains additive across stages:
C_K=1+O(K log(D+2)), allowing an exact rational rejection sampler below.

The coefficients in (7) are ACQUIRED from CDF values. Stieltjes
integration by parts, with a<b<T, gives the exact formula

    c_(b,I,j) = L_j(a)F_b^+(T-a)-L_j(b)F_b^+(T-b)
                 + int_a^b L_j'(u)F_b^+(T-u)du.        (9)

For a product initial law, the killed-chain spectral representation in
MARKED_EVENTS.txt extends F_b^+(z) analytically to Re z>=0, with modulus
at most 2M_b, where M_b=Lambda/(gamma*pi(B)*pi_*), and
pi_*=prod_i min(p_i,1-p_i). For a signed R-term input of coefficient l1
C, the bound is 2CM_b. Its logarithm has polynomial input bit length;
there is no iteration proportional to M_b. Interpolate the scalar analytic
function u -> F_b^+(T-u) on the SAME ellipse by a Chebyshev polynomial
P_Q(u). Uniform scalar error is at most 8 C M_b 2^(-Q). The degree-D
Lagrange basis satisfies the Markov-inequality estimate

    int_a^b |L_j'(u)|du <= 2D^2 Leb(D),                (10)

where Leb(D)=O(log(D+2)) is its Lebesgue constant. Substituting P_Q for
F_b^+(T-u) in (9) thus incurs at most
16 C M_b D^2 Leb(D) 2^(-Q) error per coefficient. Choose
Q=O(log(C M_b m L K D/epsilon)); all these logarithms have polynomial
input size. The integral of L_j'(u)P_Q(u) is then computed EXACTLY by
rational polynomial arithmetic. Only Q+1 marked-CDF values per target
and cell are needed, each acquired by summing the R product-component
CDF calls at the same rationalized time. Endpoint CDFs are queried too.

The ideal Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes can be approximated by rationals within
a small inverse polynomial fraction of cell width. Their normalized
minimum spacing is Omega(1/D^2); the Chebyshev Vandermonde matrix is a
scaled discrete cosine transform, and its entries change by at most D^2
times the normalized node perturbation. Choosing perturbation O(D^-4)
preserves invertibility and changes the real-axis Lebesgue bound by at
most a constant factor. Further polynomial-bit precision makes this
perturbation's interpolation error as small as desired. The real-time
CDF is Lambda||tilde_nu||_1-Lipschitz away from its atom, so rationalized
query times and finite CDF accuracy are charged by ordinary error
propagation through the polynomial basis. Ask each product-component
CDF query for enough precision that, after multiplying by C,
interpolation and (10), the total error in all mL(D+1) coefficients is
<=epsilon/(16K). Approximate product parameters similarly. This
establishes the polylog-accuracy rank and bit-complexity bounds. An
implementation should certify these node and polynomial errors before
treating this stronger tier as a software-verified result.

Operational full terminal-state sampler
---------------------------------------
The mixture is not merely a list of observable marginals. Its rational
coefficients and product parameters permit an exact rational sampler for
an approximation to the ENTIRE terminal outcome (alive configuration or
one symbol DEAD), without enumerating 2^n states.

Let s_tilde=sum_r c_r and append a deterministic DEAD product component
with coefficient c_dead=1-s_tilde. This signed measure v has total mass
exactly one. The true augmented law p has alive part nu_K and dead mass
1-s, where s=sum_x nu_K(x). If the alive L1 error is eta, then

    ||v-p||_1 <= 2eta.                                  (11)

Set A=sum_r|c_r|+|c_dead|. Choose one component with probability |c|/A,
sample its rational product law (or DEAD), and call the resulting outcome
x. Its proposal probability is a(x)/A, with
a(x)=sum_r|c_r|P_r(x)+|c_dead|1_{x=DEAD}. Compute exactly the signed
weight v(x) and a(x) by rational arithmetic, then accept x with rational
probability max(v(x),0)/a(x). The accepted law is v_+/sum_x v_+(x).
Since sum_x v(x)=1, sum_x v_+(x)>=1; acceptance is at least 1/A, so
expected proposals are at most A=O(K log(D+2)) for the interpolation
version, or O(K) for the geometric version. Each proposal costs polynomial
work in R,n and the rational bit lengths. If N=sum_x v_-(x), then
sum v_+=1+N; direct L1 comparison shows

    TV(v_+/(1+N),p) <= ||v-p||_1/2 <= eta.               (12)

Indeed replacing v by v_+ removes exactly N from its L1 discrepancy to
nonnegative p, while normalizing v_+ adds exactly N, proving (12).
This samples the full configuration law plus absorption, with no rare-
survival penalty. Directly rejecting DEAD from this sampler would cost
inverse survival mass, but the following prefix method avoids that cost.

Conditional-on-survival prefix sampler
--------------------------------------
Suppose the true terminal survival mass s=sum_x nu_K(x)>0 and a rational
lower bound 0<s0<=s is supplied. Run the fixed-ellipse compiler with alive
L1 error eta<=epsilon*s0/n. No positivity is required of tilde_nu.
Generate a configuration bit by bit. At a prefix h of length i-1, compute
the two signed child masses directly from the product mixture:

    v(hb)=sum_r c_r prod_(j<=i) q_(rj)^{(hb)_j}
                             (1-q_(rj))^{1-(hb)_j},  b=0,1. (13)

The unqueried coordinates sum to one. Maintaining each component's
current prefix product makes both child masses cost O(R) operations,
with no 2^n summation. Set a_b=max(v(hb),0), choose the next
bit with rational probability a_b/(a_0+a_1), and choose arbitrarily if
both a_b vanish. Repeat to depth n. The algorithm uses O(nR) product
updates and exact rational Bernoulli draws, regardless of s.

To bound its error, let p(hb) be true child masses and p(h)=sum_b p(hb).
At every prefix, clipping and normalization give

    p(h) TV(q(.|h),p(.|h)) <= sum_b |v(hb)-p(hb)|.       (14)

If both clipped masses vanish, the left side is at most p(h), which is
also bounded by the right side. For each depth, summing the right side
over all prefixes contracts the global L1 error under coarse-graining;
after normalizing the true conditional law, it is at most eta/s.
A sequential coupling over n bits therefore yields

    TV(output, nu_K/s) <= n eta/s <= epsilon.           (15)

The fixed-ellipse compiler's cost is polynomial in log(1/eta), so this
conditional sampler is polynomial in input bits, K,n,m, log(1/epsilon),
and log(1/s0). It has no multiplicative 1/s rejection factor. The
lower-bound encoding and the accuracy needed relative to rare survival
are explicit: if log(1/s0) is enormous, the stated runtime grows with it.

Conditional multistate nonreversible corollary
----------------------------------------------
The state-compiler algebra does not use two-state reversibility. The
nonreversible product-chain sector candidate in
work/nonreversible_boundary/RESULT.txt gives complex-time contraction
on |Im z|<=Re z/(2d) for independent local chains of maximum state count
d, including their killed compression. On balanced age/time cells, a
smaller fixed-relative ellipse rho=1+1/(64d) stays in both contraction
sectors. A target-started product heat row then has l1 norm at most
sqrt(H), with H the reciprocal minimum product stationary mass. The
same two-level interpolation has degree
O(d(log H+log(K/epsilon))) and additive rank. The first-hit CDF scalar
has an analogous accretive killed-chain complex bound
O(Lambda H/lambda0), with polynomial-bit logarithm. This extension is
CONDITIONAL on a marked-by-target finite-bit CDF primitive for the
nonreversible model; the cited file proves only the unmarked hitting
CDF. It is not part of the binary-interface theorem here.

Matched baseline and numerical check
------------------------------------
A straightforward product-mixture method applies the hit correction
independently to each old component. With G age kernels per target, its
term count obeys R_{k+1}=R_k(1+mG) before compression. The aggregated
marked measure in (2) changes this to R_{k+1}=R_k+mG. A full-state
method stores 2^n probabilities. This is a representation/rank advantage
over these explicit baselines, not an established asymptotic separation
from the strongest tensor-network or specialized trajectory algorithm.
CDF acquisition, precision, state evaluation, and sampling are charged
above. The gain requires few explicit targets, independent coordinates
within each control segment, and a control schedule compatible with the
stated interface.

Run `python3 work/controlled_product/demo.py`. It computes all n=3
states only to check the identity, using uniformization with Poisson-tail
error below 2e-15 per propagation. It then executes the signed-product
compiler for three different control segments and B={1,6}. With a coarse
age grid (ratio 1.5, minimum age .04), the final mixture has 55 terms,
coefficient l1 1.812, and L1 error 0.02424 against exact killed
propagation; a componentwise expansion would have 6,859 terms. With a
finer grid (ratio 1.2, minimum age .01), it has 159 terms, coefficient
l1 1.822, and L1 error 0.01007; componentwise expansion would have
154,495 terms, using the actual 26,27,26 bin counts across segments.
`python3 work/controlled_product/cheb_diagnostic.py`
checks the stronger coefficient-acquisition formula on one n=3 segment,
again with an explicit-state floating marked-CDF oracle. It produces 303
terms with an L1 error 0.010345 at omitted edge width .01, and 771 terms
with error 0.001040 at width .001. These errors match the measured
omitted-window first-hit mass to about 1e-13; the interior two-level
interpolation residual is below that diagnostic resolution. The numbers
check the algebra on small instances; they do not certify the general
finite-bit CDF primitive or polynomial node-rounding bounds.
