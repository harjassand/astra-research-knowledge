7. A regime where the total-gate improvement survives
Take a fixed number of collective modes and bounded-degree interaction graphs:
\[
r=O(1),\qquad |E_0|+|E_{\mathrm{ex}}|=O(n).
\]
Suppose
\[
\sum_\mu|c_{\mu i}|^2=\Theta(\gamma)
\]
for each emitter, and the per-emitter drive, pump, dephasing and exchange scales are \(O(\gamma)\).
At
\[
T=\Theta(1/\gamma),\qquad k_0=n,
\]
we have
\[
s^2=\Theta(\gamma n),\quad
g^2=\Theta(\gamma n^2),\quad
hT=O(n),\quad
R=O(n+\log(1/\varepsilon)).
\]
For polynomially specified accuracy and coefficients,
\[
Q_{\mathrm{sim}}=\widetilde O(n^{3/2}),
\qquad
G_{\mathrm{sim}}=\widetilde O(n^{5/2}).
\tag{29}
\]
The current general simulator, instantiated with the same constructed encodings and the same removal of \(H_0\), gives
\[
G_{\mathrm{general}}
=
\widetilde O\!\left(
D_{\mathrm{in}}\,[1+(g^2+h)T]
\right)
=
\widetilde O(n^3).
\tag{30}
\]
Equation (30) is an instantiation of its published upper bound, not a lower bound on every possible specialized algorithm. arXiv

Input loading and simple state preparation are near-linear; the correlation-map output and classical processing are quadratic per shot. Neither removes the square-root gate improvement.
The large collective rate is real
It is not merely a poor normalisation convention. For example, include a mode
\[
L_1=\sqrt{\gamma/2}\sum_i\sigma_i^-.
\]
On a \(q\)-excitation Dicke state,
\[
\langle L_1^\dagger L_1\rangle
=\frac{\gamma}{2}q(n-q+1),
\]
which is \(\Theta(\gamma n^2)\) for \(q\) near \(n/2\). A second nonuniform mode, disordered diagonal interactions and local exchange can break the permutation-symmetric dynamics without removing this large rate.
The useful distinction is between that instantaneous collective rate and the much smaller sustained excitation-supply budget established by (4).
The advantage is not guaranteed for the initial cooperative flash alone. At \(T=\Theta(1/(\gamma n))\), the bounds can be comparable. The stronger application is late-time populations, delayed emission, and driven correlations on longer physical times. Nor does the result certify that a chosen \(T\) is sufficient to reach a stationary state.
