Author: gb_finite_rate_genesis. Date: 2026-10-16. Status: internally derived; external correctness and historical priority UNKNOWN.

## 1. The source question and the new answer

`finite_rate_joint_syndrome_2026-10-15/joint/REPORT.md` explicitly leaves open whether the single-parity factor

    epsilon_i(T) = J_i^2 [ T/gamma_i
                        - (1-exp(-2 gamma_i T))/(2 gamma_i^2) ]

multiplies across simultaneous disjoint-parity interactions sharing an arbitrary finite bath. It proves the larger positive majorant B instead. Under its exact single-parity hypotheses, the sharp epsilon PRODUCT DOES hold. In fact an explicit latent-history bad effect, containing the endpoint event, has this bound. No physical measurements or new resources have to be added to the specified exact Lindblad evolution to prove the endpoint assertion.

The proof is an application of the previously supplied deletion-null instrument lemma to an exact Poisson-pinch representation. It does not prove that an unspecified physical detector realizes the stipulated generator.

## 2. Endpoint theorem

Let Z_1,...,Z_k be commuting Hermitian involutions on a finite joint memory-bath Hilbert space. A reference can be tensored throughout. Let

    d rho/dt = -i[H_0(t) + sum_i V_i(t), rho]
               + sum_i gamma_i(t) (Z_i rho Z_i-rho),       (1)

on a fixed deterministic interval [0,T]. Assume, almost everywhere:

    [H_0(t),Z_i]=0,
    {V_i(t),Z_i}=0,
    [V_i(t),Z_j]=0 for i != j,
    ||V_i(t)|| <= j_i(t),

where j_i is nonnegative integrable and gamma_i nonnegative integrable. All propagators exist. There is no bound on H_0, no commutation assumption on bath coefficients, and no bath reset. The initial joint state is supported in a specified joint Z sector s; let P_s denote its projector.

Define

    E_i = integral_0^T integral_0^T j_i(t) j_i(u)
            exp[-2 integral_min(t,u)^max(t,u) gamma_i(v) dv] dt du.  (2)

For any selected set S, let P_flip(S,s) project onto outputs whose selected parities all differ from s, with no restriction on the other parities. If Phi_T is the channel of (1), then

    P_s Phi_T^*(P_flip(S,s)) P_s
        <= product_(i in S) min(1,E_i) P_s.                 (3)

This is an operator effect inequality, uniform over arbitrary bath states, allowed initial joint correlations, and entangled references. It is not a product-state assertion or a product of observed marginals. For constant j_i=J_i and gamma_i>0,

    E_i = epsilon_i(T)
        = (J_i^2/gamma_i)
            [T-(1-exp(-2 gamma_i T))/(2 gamma_i)].           (4)

At gamma_i=0 use E_i=(J_i T)^2. Thus E_i <= min((J_i T)^2,J_i^2 T/gamma_i) when gamma_i>0. A selected interaction that flips two selected parities violates the hypotheses; the supplied X_1 X_2 counterexample still applies.

No final physical measurement is needed to define the Born effect in (3). If the endpoint parity instrument is actually performed, (3) bounds its genuine outcomes. Neither reading gives a fictitious local fault instrument inside an otherwise unspecified evolution.

## 3. Exact Poisson-pinch representation, including concurrent H

Put D_i=(Id+Ad Z_i)/2. The identity

    gamma_i(Ad Z_i-Id) = 2 gamma_i(D_i-Id)                  (5)

is exact. The solution of (1) is the average of Hamiltonian evolution interrupted by nonselective D_i at independent inhomogeneous Poisson times of intensity lambda_i(t)=2 gamma_i(t).

This follows directly from the no-event/event probabilities in dt, or from the convergent Dyson expansion of the bounded channel jump generator. The Hamiltonian remains present at every non-event time. There is no splitting approximation, no assumption of a noiseless finite measurement window, and no change to (1).

Choose the projective Kraus representation of each D_i and keep its outcome in an isolated latent record. These are labels of a legitimate mathematical dilation of the exact channel, not newly acquired syndrome data or controller inputs. Its independent Poisson times are representation randomness. Later operations never act on the latent records. For finite integrated rates, each trajectory has finitely many cuts almost surely; the averaged dilation can use a direct integral. One can also truncate counts and pass by dominated convergence.

## 4. Conditional cut theorem

Fix all Poisson times, but do NOT condition and normalize their syndrome outcomes. For parity i, its cuts and the interval endpoints partition [0,T] into gaps B. Its initial label is s_i. At T add the terminal parity projector used in the Born event; resolving all terminal parities is harmless when summing unused outcomes. Define the latent mismatch flag d_(i,B) when the measured parity at the two ends of gap B differs. Let b_i be the OR of its gap flags.

Split V_i into its restrictions V_(i,B). Let

    a_(i,B) = integral_B j_i(t) dt.

There is one common dilation for all real interpolations multiplying these restrictions by z_(i,B) in [0,1]. Delete a selected restriction. Inside its gap, H_0, every other V_j, and all other parity pinchings preserve Z_i. The next Z_i measurement must therefore repeat the actual entering label, on EVERY joint input and for EVERY setting of the other interpolation parameters. Later isolated records cannot change this fact. Thus the fixed output projector Q_(i,B) onto its mismatch obeys

    Q_(i,B) U_(z_i,B=0) W = 0.                            (6)

All Q are diagonal in the same retained-record basis and commute, even when two gap flags share a boundary label. This is whole-input deletion nullness, not a conditional probability statement.

For a set C of selected gap restrictions, apply multivariate fundamental theorem of calculus to the selected projected dilation. Every anchored boundary term is killed by (6). In the mixed Duhamel derivative each selected V occurs once, with chronological unitary/isometric propagators between them. The sum over their possible time orders partitions the Cartesian insertion-time cube, so

    || (product_(c in C) Q_c) U W || <= product_(c in C) a_c.

Squaring yields the positive effect bound product a_c^2. No bath factors are commuted or reordered. Shared-bath noncommutativity is fully retained.

Union over choosing one witness gap for each selected parent i gives, conditional on cut times eta,

    effect(all b_i=1, i in S | eta)
        <= product_(i in S) min(1, sum_B a_(i,B)^2) I.      (7)

Capping one factor at one is justified by applying the same estimate to the smaller selected set; it is not algebraically replacing an arbitrary product bound by a stronger one. The endpoint-flipped event for i implies b_i=1.

## 5. Average before interpreting the bound

The cut families are independent across i. The right side of (7) depends only on each own cut family, while the operator inequality holds uniformly before averaging. Therefore

    effect(all selected b_i=1)
       <= product_(i in S) E_eta_i min(1,sum_B a_(i,B)^2) I
       <= product_(i in S) min(1,E_eta_i sum_B a_(i,B)^2) I. (8)

Expand a squared gap action as a double integral. Two times u,t lie in one gap exactly when there is no Poisson cut between them. That probability is exp[-integral_min^max lambda_i]. Hence the expectation is exactly (2). Equations (3)-(4) follow.

For homogeneous lambda and gap lengths ell_B,

    E sum_B ell_B^2
       = 2[T/lambda-(1-exp(-lambda T))/lambda^2].          (9)

One can alternatively condition on N=n cuts: uniform order statistics give Dirichlet gaps and E[sum ell_B^2 | N=n]=2T^2/(n+2). Averaging N~Poisson(lambda T) gives (9).

The rate improvement is not asserted after fixing every cut trajectory. A trajectory with no cuts has the action-squared budget J_i^2 T^2. Nor does (8) imply a small normalized probability after postselecting another latent bad flag or a bath outcome.

A_i are Hermitian, ||A_i(t)||<=j_i(t), and may share arbitrary physical memory/bath supports algebraically. Let K_z,xi be the Walsh coefficient of the propagator in initial bits f.

    H_tilde(t)=H_base(t)+sum_i X_(q_i) tensor A_i(t)

    E_xi sum_(z: z_i=1 for every i in S) K_z,xi^dagger K_z,xi
