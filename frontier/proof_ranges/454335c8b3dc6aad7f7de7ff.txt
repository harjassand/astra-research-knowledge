TARGET-SET RESOLVENT: INTERNAL CANDIDATE THEOREM AND PROOF
2026-10-10.  Status: internally derived proof, not independent certification or priority claim.

Statement and interface
-----------------------
Let X be the product of n independent two-state continuous-time chains. Coordinate i has stationary probability p_i in (0,1) of state 1 and relaxation rate lambda_i>0, all binary-encoded rationals. Thus 0->1 has rate lambda_i p_i and 1->0 has rate lambda_i(1-p_i). Let B be a nonempty list of m distinct explicit n-bit configurations, and let the initial law mu be a product Bernoulli law with binary-rational parameters r_i in [0,1] (deterministic initial configurations are included). Write H_B=inf{t>=0:X_t in B}. Given a nonnegative binary rational t and epsilon in (0,1/2), a deterministic Turing algorithm returns a rational number Fhat(t) satisfying

    |Fhat(t)-Pr_mu(H_B<=t)| <= epsilon

in time polynomial in the total input bit length, m, and log(1/epsilon). It never enumerates the 2^n states. The same construction works whenever the initial law permits poly-time evaluation, to requested precision, of mu^T P_u(:,b) for b in B and has a polynomial-bit upper bound on ||D_pi^{-1/2}mu||. In particular, any explicitly given mixture of polynomially many product laws works.

A separate deterministic preprocessing algorithm produces a finite-support distribution whose CDF is within epsilon uniformly on [0,infinity), with number of support points and bit cost polynomial in the input length, m, and 1/epsilon. It can then draw from that rational distribution using fair bits with polynomial expected cost. This is an absolute Kolmogorov guarantee. It is not exact sampling, a total-variation guarantee, or a relative guarantee for arbitrarily tiny upper survival tails. For any fixed binary-rational t>0, the lower-tail CDF value also admits a relative eta approximation in time polynomial in the input length and log(1/eta), using the explicit positive bound near the end of this note.

Notation and two elementary spectra
-----------------------------------
Let pi(x)=prod_i p_i^{x_i}(1-p_i)^{1-x_i}, q=pi(B), pi_* = prod_i min(p_i,1-p_i), gamma=min_i lambda_i, Lambda=sum_i lambda_i. All have polynomial-length rational encodings; in particular log(1/q) and log(1/pi_*) are polynomial in the input length. Let D=diag(pi), Q the product generator, and A=-D^{1/2}QD^{-1/2}. Then A is real symmetric positive semidefinite, with spectrum exactly {sum_{i in S} lambda_i:S subset [n]} inside [0,Lambda]. Its heat kernel has entries

    [exp(-u A)]_{ab} = prod_i k_i(u;a_i,b_i),

where k_i(0,0)=1-p_i+p_i exp(-lambda_i u),
      k_i(1,1)=p_i+(1-p_i) exp(-lambda_i u),
      k_i(0,1)=k_i(1,0)=sqrt(p_i(1-p_i))(1-exp(-lambda_i u)).

Each requested entry costs O(n) elementary evaluations at any prescribed precision.

Let C=B^c, A_C be the principal submatrix on C, a=D_C^{-1/2}mu_C, and b=D_C^{1/2}1_C. The survival function is

    S_mu(t)=Pr_mu(H_B>t)=a^T exp(-t A_C)b,  t>=0.           (1)

Here ||b||<=1 and ||a||^2=sum_{x in C}mu(x)^2/pi(x)<=1/pi_*. Use the rational bound M:=1/pi_* >=||a||||b|| in the algorithm; its logarithm has polynomial input bit length. The spectrum of A_C is inside [gamma q,Lambda]: for v supported on C, Cauchy-Schwarz gives (E_pi v)^2 <=(1-q)||v||_pi^2, so Var_pi(v)>=q||v||_pi^2. The product chain Poincare inequality E(v,v)>=gamma Var_pi(v) gives A_C>=gamma q I; the upper bound follows by compression of A. This lower bound is needed only to cap the time range for the sampler. The single-time CDF algorithm needs only A_C>=0.

Small target resolvent and boundary renewal identity
----------------------------------------------------
For real s>0 put R_B(s)=[(sI-Q)^{-1}]_{B,B} and w_mu(s)=mu^T(sI-Q)^{-1}[:,B]. These are m x m and 1 x m, respectively. The strong Markov property at H_B gives, for x arbitrary and y in B,

    R_{xy}(s)=sum_{z in B} E_x[exp(-sH_B)1_{X_H=z}] R_{zy}(s).

Consequently

    f_mu(s):=E_mu exp(-sH_B)=w_mu(s) R_B(s)^{-1}1_B,
    h_mu(s):=(1-f_mu(s))/s = int_0^infty exp(-st)S_mu(t)dt
             =a^T(sI+A_C)^{-1}b.                         (2)

The convention exp(-s infinity)=0 applies. For stationary mu=pi, w_pi=pi_B^T/s and, with K_B(s)=D_B^{1/2}R_B(s)D_B^{-1/2} and beta=(sqrt(pi(z)))_{z in B},

    f_pi(s)=beta^T K_B(s)^{-1}beta/s.                       (3)

The symmetrized K_B(s) is a principal compression of (sI+A)^{-1}; therefore

    (s+Lambda)^{-1}I <=K_B(s)<=s^{-1}I.                  (4)

The condition bound is kappa(K_B)<=1+Lambda/s. At rare-event time scales s can itself be of order q, so this condition number may be numerically large; exact-rational or certified high-precision arithmetic charges its logarithm, which has polynomial bit length. No loop count proportional to 1/q or Lambda/gamma is used.

Both inputs to (2) have product heat-kernel integrands. For z in B,

    R_B(s)_{xy}=int_0^infty exp(-su)P_u(x,y)du,
    w_mu(s)_z=int_0^infty exp(-su) prod_i [pi_i(z_i)
                                      +(mu_i(z_i)-pi_i(z_i))exp(-lambda_i u)]du.

The second product is a probability at each real u even though its exponential-basis coefficients can have signs. The O(n) entry evaluation and m x m solve use no full-state vector.

Certified positive quadrature and finite-bit resolvent evaluation
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Here is one explicit quadrature lemma. For any rational s>0 and relative tolerance delta in (0,1/2), a list of positive nodes u_j and positive weights v_j, of length poly(log((s+Lambda)/s),log(1/delta)), can be constructed such that

    |sum_j v_j exp(-(s+x)u_j) - 1/(s+x)|
           <= delta/(s+x)  for every x in [0,Lambda].     (5)

One construction is the log-time trapezoid. Set h=1/J with the integer J=10+ceil(log_2(1/delta)), which is computable by integer comparisons, initially taking every integer j and u_j=exp(jh), v_j=h u_j. Poisson summation and the Mellin transform of u exp(-alpha u) give

    alpha h sum_{j in Z}exp(jh)exp(-alpha exp(jh))
       =sum_{k in Z}Gamma(1-2 pi i k/h)alpha^{2 pi i k/h}.

For real alpha>0, the k=0 term is 1; the rest has modulus at most 2 sum_{k>=1}|Gamma(1+2 pi i k/h)|. The identity |Gamma(1+iy)|^2=pi y/sinh(pi y) bounds this by O(sqrt(J)exp(-pi^2 J)) <=delta/4 with the chosen J. Truncate the negative tail where u_min <=delta/[C(s+Lambda)]; its absolute contribution is <=2u_min. Truncate the positive tail after u_max >=(2/s)log[C(s+Lambda)/(s delta)]; monotonicity of exp(v)exp(-s exp(v)) beyond this point bounds its absolute contribution by s^{-1}exp(-s u_max), after an absolute-constant adjustment. Such truncation indices can be chosen conservatively from integer bit lengths and rational upper bounds, so no exact transcendental comparison is needed. Thus both tails are <=delta/[4(s+Lambda)], establishing (5). The number of retained j is O(J[log((s+Lambda)/s)+log(1/delta)+log log(1/delta)]). A dyadic-interval Gauss-Legendre construction gives the same positive-weight conclusion without the Mellin identity.

Applying (5) to the spectral calculus of A and compressing gives the Loewner statement

    (1-delta)K_B(s) <=sum_j v_j exp(-s u_j)[exp(-u_j A)]_{B,B}
                   <=(1+delta)K_B(s).                    (6)

For the cross row w_mu, the same scalar approximation has full-operator error at most delta/s; multiplying by D^{-1/2}mu (norm <=M) and a selected basis vector bounds each suitably symmetrized cross-entry error by M delta/s. This is enough for arbitrary-precision evaluation of (2).

All data in the quadrature can be replaced by rational approximations with certified entry error. The exp, sqrt and pi evaluations require precision polynomial in log(1/delta), bitlength(s), log((s+Lambda)/s), log(1/pi_*), n and m. Arguments lambda_i u_j that are too large can be certified as exp(-lambda_i u_j) below the requested error and set to zero; otherwise ordinary range reduction and Taylor series suffice. To make the solve bound explicit, write c=w_mu D_B^{-1/2}, beta=D_B^{1/2}1_B, and A_0=s+Lambda. Then f_mu=c K_B^{-1}beta, ||beta||<=1, ||K_B^{-1}||<=A_0, and ||c||<=sqrt(m)/(s sqrt(pi_*))<=C_0:=m/(s pi_*). If rational approximations obey ||Ktilde-K_B||<=eta_K<=1/(2A_0), ||ctilde-c||<=eta_c, and ||betatilde-beta||<=eta_b<=1, the inverse norm is at most 2A_0 and direct subtraction gives

    |ftilde-f_mu|<=4A_0 eta_c+4C_0 A_0^2 eta_K+C_0 A_0 eta_b.

For requested |htilde-h_mu|<=tau, choose eta_c<=s tau/(12A_0), eta_K<=s tau/(12C_0 A_0^2), and eta_b<=s tau/(3C_0 A_0), additionally shrinking them to the harmless norm assumptions. Product quadrature and rational sqrt/exp approximations deliver these entry tolerances using polynomially many bits. Invert Ktilde with exact rational arithmetic; its determinant and inverse have polynomial bit length because its entries, dimension m, and certified eigenvalue lower bound do. This proves the oracle bound:

    Given rational s>0 and rational tau>0, h_mu(s) can be computed
    to absolute error tau in time polynomial in n,m,input bits,
    bitlength(s), and max(0,log(1/tau)).                  (7)

The statement remains valid when tau is exponentially small in a polynomial of the input length. In the stationary case, (6) alone gives relative error O(delta) for f_pi(s) by order reversal under matrix inversion; because 0<=f_pi(s)<=1, this yields absolute O(delta/s) for h_pi(s). This is the particularly clean rarity-uniform conditioning claim.

Stationary positive-mixture route (simpler, slower in epsilon). When mu=pi, a=b in (1), so the spectral theorem gives S_pi(t)=int_{[gamma q,Lambda]}exp(-rt)nu(dr) for a positive measure nu of total mass 1-q. Hence h_pi(s)=int nu(dr)/(s+r). For t>0 choose N=ceil(100/epsilon), k=N-1, s=N/t, Delta=s/N^3, and form

    A_N(t)=s^N/[k! Delta^k] sum_{j=0}^k(-1)^j binom(k,j)h_pi(s+j Delta)
          =int prod_{j=0}^k [s/(s+r+j Delta)]nu(dr).

The product differs from (1+rt/N)^(-N) by at most k(k+1)Delta/(2s)<=1/(2N), uniformly in r. Also sup_{x>=0}|(1+x/N)^(-N)-exp(-x)|<=1/N: for x<=N/2 use x-N log(1+x/N)<=x^2/(2N) and maximize x^2 exp(-3x/4)/(2N); for x>=N/2 use (3/2)^(-N)<=1/N. Thus |A_N(t)-S_pi(t)|<=3/(2N). Evaluating every h_pi(s+j Delta) from (6) to relative f_pi error delta=epsilon/[100(2N^3)^k] makes the finite-difference numerical error <=epsilon/100, since the coefficient l1 amplification is at most (2N^3)^k/k!. Required precision O(N log N+log(1/epsilon)) is polynomial in 1/epsilon. This gives an independent, entirely real-axis absolute-CDF algorithm. The following spectral-polynomial construction improves the epsilon dependence to polylogarithmic and applies to nonstationary product starts.

Uniform spectral polynomial for exp(-t A_C)
-------------------------------------------
For a fixed rational t>0 set s=1/t and Y=s(sI+A_C)^{-1}=(I+t A_C)^{-1}. Its spectrum lies in [0,1]. Define g(y)=exp(1-1/y) for y>0 and g(0)=0. Spectral calculus gives

    exp(-t A_C)=g(Y).                                      (8)

There is a constructive degree d=O(log^2(1/xi)) polynomial P_d with rational coefficients and

    sup_{0<=y<=1}|P_d(y)-g(y)|<=xi,                         (9)

constructible in time poly(log(1/xi)). Here is an explicit proof rather than an approximation-theorem black box.

For any y in (0,1], Cauchy's formula on |z-y|=y/2 gives Re(1/z)>=2/(9y), hence

    |g^(k)(y)| <= e k! (2/y)^k exp(-2/(9y))
                <= e 9^k(k!)^2.                           (10)

The function and all derivatives extend as zero at y=0. Put G(theta)=g((1+cos theta)/2)=exp[-tan^2(theta/2)], with G(pi)=0; it is a smooth even 2 pi-periodic function. All nonzero derivatives of (1+cos theta)/2 have magnitude <=1/2. Faa di Bruno's formula, partial Bell polynomials, and S(k,l)<=l^k/l! for Stirling numbers of the second kind imply

    ||G^(k)||_infty
      <= e sum_{l=1}^k 9^l(l!)^2 2^{-l} S(k,l)
      <= e k(9e/2)^k(k!)^2 <=40^k(k!)^2, k>=1.           (11)

Write G(theta)=c_0+sum_{j>=1}c_j cos(j theta). Repeated integration by parts gives

    |c_j| <=2*40^k(k!)^2/j^k <=2(40k^2/j)^k.

For j>=80 take k=floor(sqrt(j/80)), so |c_j|<=2^{1-k}. Therefore the tail sum beyond degree d is at most C(1+sqrt(d))exp(-c sqrt(d)). Choose d=80K^2 with the first integer K for which the explicit grouped-tail bound 1000(K+1)2^{-K} is below xi/[100(d+1)]. Then K=O(log(1/xi)) and the exact Chebyshev truncation c_0+sum_{j=1}^d c_j T_j(2y-1) has error much smaller than xi.

For constructive coefficients, sample G on a uniform periodic mesh of M_grid>=4d points and take a discrete cosine/Fourier transform. Every alias of an index j<=d has magnitude >=M_grid-d>=3d; its total error is bounded by the already chosen coefficient tail. Compute each sampled exp and cosine to xi/[100(d+1)] using ordinary rational interval approximations and round the Fourier coefficients to dyadics. This yields (9). If P_d(y)=sum_{r=0}^d alpha_r y^r, then the recurrence T_{j+1}(2y-1)=2(2y-1)T_j(2y-1)-T_{j-1}(2y-1) shows ||coeff(T_j(2y-1))||_1<=7^j. Since |c_j|<=2 and the rounded coefficients can be kept <=3,

    A_pow:=sum_r |alpha_r| <=4*7^{d+1}.                (12)

Thus log A_pow=O(d), so conversion to the power basis and exact dyadic coefficient storage have polynomial bit cost.

Accessing powers of Y using only real boundary resolvents
---------------------------------------------------------
Let m_r=a^T Y^r b. The zeroth moment m_0=mu(C)=1-mu(B) is computed exactly from the product law and explicit B. For r>=1, h_mu(s)=a^T(sI+A_C)^{-1}b from (2). Choose a positive rational Delta and form

    mtilde_r = s^r /[(r-1)! Delta^{r-1}]
               * sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(-1)^j binom(r-1,j) h_mu(s+j Delta).

The elementary divided-difference identity gives, exactly,

    mtilde_r = a^T [prod_{j=0}^{r-1} s(s+j Delta+A_C)^{-1}]b.   (13)

All factors commute. For any x>=0, the scalar product in (13) lies between 0 and (s/(s+x))^r, and their difference is at most r(r-1)Delta/(2s). Consequently

    |mtilde_r-m_r| <= M r(r-1)Delta/(2s).              (14)

Choose xi=epsilon/(10M), construct P_d, set A_pow as in (12), and choose

    Delta/s <=epsilon/[100 M A_pow d^2].

Then the total error after weighting (14) by the alpha_r is <=epsilon/200. To charge evaluation error, put U=2s/Delta and request each of the at most d(d+1)/2 scalar values h_mu(s+j Delta) to absolute error

    zeta=epsilon/[100 A_pow s U^{d-1}].               (15)

The binomial sum magnifies any one-moment error by at most s U^{r-1}/(r-1)!, so (15) makes the weighted numerical error <=epsilon/100. Although zeta can be tiny, its required precision is O(log(1/epsilon)+log M+d+d log U+|log s|) bits, polynomial in the input length and log(1/epsilon). Formula (7) supplies every scalar value in that bit complexity. Finally

    S_hat(t)=sum_{r=0}^d alpha_r mtilde_r,
    Fhat(t)=1-S_hat(t)

has absolute error at most M xi+epsilon/200+epsilon/100<epsilon, with rational output. This proves the single-time theorem. The algorithm uses only positive-real resolvent arguments. The cancellation in the high finite differences is paid for explicitly by the O(d log U) precision bits in (15); ordinary floating point is not a certificate.

Known sharper rational approximation. Sachdeva and Vishnoi, "Faster Algorithms via Approximation Theory", Section 7, Theorem 7.1 (https://theory.epfl.ch/vishnoi/Publications_files/approx-survey.pdf), present a proof of Saff-Schonhage-Varga's exponential-accuracy negative-pole approximation: a degree-d polynomial p_d satisfies sup_{x>=0}|exp(-x)-p_d((1+x/d)^{-1})|<=O(d 2^{-d}), with coefficients bounded by d^{O(d)} and computable in poly(d) bit/arithmetic cost to the stated accuracy. Substituting s=d/t, Y=s(sI+A_C)^{-1}, and p_d for P_d in (8)-(15) yields degree d=O(log(M/epsilon)) instead of the elementary Gevrey-Chebyshev d=O(log^2(M/epsilon)). Its coefficient l1 norm is exp(O(d log d)), so the finite-difference precision cost remains polynomial. This approximation is established prior art; the candidate contribution here is the target-boundary compiler and complete rarity/bit-cost accounting. The self-contained Gevrey argument above does not depend on the cited theorem.

Relative approximation of the lower-tail CDF
--------------------------------------------
Fix t>0 and let tau=min(t,1/Lambda), rho=min_i lambda_i min(p_i,1-p_i)>0. Select the most probable bit value of each product-initial coordinate; the resulting configuration x has mu(x)>=2^{-n}. Select any target a in B. Flip, in a fixed order, each of the d<=n coordinates on which x and a differ, and allow no other jump before tau. Every prescribed edge has rate at least rho, and the total exit rate at every intermediate state is at most Lambda. Integration over the ordered d-jump simplex gives

    F_mu(t)>=2^{-n} exp(-Lambda tau) (rho tau)^d/d!
            >=2^{-(n+2)}(rho tau)^n/n! =: L(t)>0.        (16)

The last inequality uses Lambda tau<=1, rho tau<=1, d<=n, and exp(-1)>=1/4. L(t) is rational with polynomial bit length, even when the event is extremely rare in numeric value. To obtain a multiplicative eta approximation to F_mu(t), call the absolute CDF algorithm with tolerance eta L(t)/2. Its running time remains polynomial in the input length and log(1/eta). At t=0, F_mu(0)=mu(B) is computed exactly.

Uniform CDF sampler (optional corollary)
---------------------------------------
First take F(0)=mu(B) exactly. For t>0, the hitting density is at most Lambda: conditional on being outside B at time t, the instantaneous entrance rate into B is at most the total exit rate, which is at most Lambda. Thus F is Lambda-Lipschitz on (0,infinity). Also (1) and A_C>=gamma q I give S_mu(t)<=M exp(-gamma q t). Choose a rational endpoint T with M exp(-gamma q T)<=epsilon/16, for example a deliberately loose rational T=16/[epsilon pi_* gamma q]. Its binary length is polynomial.

Use the single-time oracle at precision alpha=epsilon/32. Recursively bisect [0,T]. For an interval [a,b], stop if its estimated CDF increment is at most epsilon/2, or if b-a<=epsilon/(4Lambda); otherwise bisect. A stopped interval has true increment at most epsilon/2+2alpha or epsilon/4. Any split interval has true increment at least epsilon/2-2alpha, so at each depth there are O(1/epsilon) split intervals. The maximum depth is O(log(T Lambda/epsilon)), hence the number of CDF queries and leaves is polynomial in input bits and 1/epsilon. Put the exact mass mu(B) at time 0. Round the other endpoint CDF approximations to dyadics, clamp them to [0,1], take their running maximum (starting from exact mu(B)), and place each increment of this monotone sequence at the corresponding right endpoint. Set the final value at T to 1. The resulting step CDF differs from the true CDF by O(epsilon) inside every leaf because its true CDF increment is O(epsilon), and by O(epsilon) after T. Tighten the constants, or run this construction with epsilon/4 internally, to achieve the stated epsilon. All masses and support times are rational with polynomial bit length. A uniform random integer with rejection samples the finite distribution exactly with expected polynomial fair-bit cost.

Scope and literature boundary
-----------------------------
This construction assumes the product generator and initial law are supplied exactly as binary-rational parameters; its claimed cost includes product-kernel evaluation, positive quadrature, m x m inversions, polynomial coefficient acquisition, cancellation precision, and CDF queries. It does not learn the generator from trajectories. The single-time guarantee is absolute, although (6) yields relative accuracy for the stationary hitting-time Laplace transform. No historical novelty, practical speed, optimal exponent in epsilon, or broad physical applicability has been established here. The closed proof should be independently checked before a strong theorem or discovery claim is made.
