THEOREM
Let W be the smallest linear subspace of R^n containing e1 such that
KW is contained in W and T(u,v,w)=alpha*u*v*w belongs to W whenever
u,v,w belong to W. Componentwise products are intended.

(i) If W=R^n, the zero-initial IO germ determines K and alpha, globally
among all n-dimensional models of this form, modulo hidden signed
permutations fixing e1. A finite IO word jet suffices.
(ii) If W is proper, complete IO data do not determine K, even if all
alpha_i are supplied exactly. Thus W=R^n is also necessary for full
recovery in this unrestricted SPD model class.

LEMMA 1 — A PAIRED DIRECTION HAS AN OBSERVABLE SUPPORT EULER FIELD
Suppose v is nonzero and paired with z_v. Put beta_i=alpha_i v_i^2.
Set q0=v, z0=z_v and recursively
  q_(m+1)=-[v,[v,[q_m,f]]]/6,
  z_(m+1)=-L_v^2 F z_m/6.
Differentiating the cubic proves q_m,i=v_i beta_i^m and z_m=q_m dot x.
Consequently
  p_m=L_v z_m(0)=sum_(v_i != 0) v_i^2 beta_i^m.
This is a positive atomic moment sequence. Repeated beta values simply
aggregate their positive weights; zero v_i contribute nothing. Since
alpha_i>0, its distinct atoms are exactly the distinct positive beta_i.

Let r be their number, <=n. The Hankel matrices
H_l=(p_(i+j))_(0<=i,j<l) have rank min(l,r). With p0,...,p_(2n-1),
detect r and solve the positive-definite H_r system
  sum_(j=0)^(r-1) c_j p_(i+j)=-p_(i+r), 0<=i<r.
Then P(t)=t^r+sum_(j=0)^(r-1)c_j t^j is the monic support polynomial.
P(0)!=0. For j=1,...,r define
  D_j=-[v,[q_(j-1),f]]/6=diag(beta_i^j)x.
If P(t)=sum_(j=0)^r c_j t^j with c_r=1, form
  E=-sum_(j=1)^r (c_j/c0) D_j.
Its i-th diagonal coefficient is 1-P(beta_i)/P(0): this is 1 when
v_i!=0 and 0 when v_i=0. Hence E=P_S x where S=support(v).
All coefficients used in E are rational functions of the moments;
individual atom roots are unnecessary at this stage.

LEMMA 2 — K PRESERVES PAIRED CONSTRUCTIBILITY
For the E=P_S x just obtained, let
  R=-[v,f]=Kv+3alpha*x^2*v.
Direct differentiation yields
  [E,R]=3alpha*x^2*v-P_S Kv.
Thus C=R-[E,R]=Kv+P_S Kv is constant, and
  [C,E]=P_S C=2P_S Kv.
Therefore Kv=C-[C,E]/2 is a constructible constant field.

Its paired scalar is
  z_(Kv)=-(I+L_E/6-L_E^2/6) F z_v.
Indeed -Fz_v=(Kv) dot x+sum_i alpha_i v_i x_i^3.
L_E has eigenvalue 0 on linear terms outside S, 1 on linear terms
inside S, and 3 on the displayed cubic. The polynomial
1+t/6-t^2/6 equals 1 at t=0,1 and 0 at t=3. This proves the identity.
It does not presume that individual coordinates in S are already known.

LEMMA 3 — T PRESERVES PAIRED CONSTRUCTIBILITY
For paired u,v,w,
  T(u,v,w)=-[u,[v,[w,f]]]/6,
  z_T=-L_u L_v F z_w/6.
The constant field is alpha_i u_i v_i w_i in coordinate i, and the
scalar is exactly its Euclidean inner product with x.

FINITE BASIS CLOSURE
Start with the pair e1,h. Given a basis of paired directions, form
K v_i and T(v_i,v_j,v_k). Gram entries are observable:
  <v_i,v_j>=L_(v_i) z_(v_j)(0).
Use exact Gram ranks to add any independent candidate. If no candidate
increases rank, the current span is K-invariant and T-closed by linearity
and trilinearity, hence equals W. At most n-1 additions occur.
If e1 is cyclic for K, the K operation alone generates a basis, but
cyclicity is not necessary: cubic products may add missing directions.

FULL COORDINATE RECOVERY ONCE THE BASIS HAS SIZE n
Choose a paired q=SUM_(r=0)^(n-1)t^r v_r with all q_i nonzero and all
beta_i=alpha_i q_i^2 distinct. Such a choice can be found by exact
Hankel-rank tests using only the integers
  t=0,1,...,n^2(n-1).
For a full basis, each polynomial q_i(t) is nonzero; each
sqrt(alpha_i)q_i(t) +/- sqrt(alpha_j)q_j(t) is also nonzero because
different rows of an invertible basis matrix cannot be proportional.
There are n^2 such polynomials of degree <=n-1, so at most n^2(n-1)
bad real t values. Full moment Hankel rank n is precisely the desired
condition. This is finite, though badly conditioned choices are possible.

Use Lemma 1 on q. The now-distinct beta_i and weights w_i=q_i^2 are
recovered from p0,...,p_(2n-1) by Prony and Vandermonde inversion.
Choose hidden signs q_i=+/-sqrt(w_i); recover alpha_i=beta_i/w_i.
The measured coordinate is anchored: alpha1=-G^3 Fh(0)/6 is known,
q1=Gz_q(0) is known with its sign, and beta1=alpha1 q1^2 singles out
the corresponding distinct atom.

Define q_m,i=q_i beta_i^m and z_m=q_m dot x. Then
  B_mr=-L_(q_r) F z_m(0)=q_m^T K q_r.
Let Q_mi=q_i beta_i^m for 0<=m<n. Its determinant is
  product_i q_i * product_(i<j)(beta_j-beta_i),
which is nonzero. Hence K=Q^(-1)B Q^(-T).
All sign and ordering choices amount exactly to a hidden signed
permutation fixing the measured coordinate; alpha is permuted likewise.

NECESSITY
If W is proper, f maps W into W: K preserves W and
alpha*x^3=T(x,x,x) belongs to W for x in W. The input e1 also lies
in W. Every zero-initial trajectory therefore stays in W.
For any epsilon>0 define K_epsilon=K+epsilon P_(W perpendicular).
This is real symmetric positive definite, agrees with K on W, and
has exactly the same vector field along every such input trajectory.
It consequently has identical IO for every admissible input, while
its trace differs by epsilon*(n-dim W). Signed permutations preserve
trace. Thus these are nongauge collisions, proving impossibility.

CONSERVATIVE UNIFORM FINITE WORD-DEPTH BOUND
Let A_d be the polynomial algebra generated by binary IO word functions
of length <=d. Say a paired field has depth d if its operator shifts
A_l into A_(l+d), and its scalar belongs to A_(d-1).
The initial e1,h pair has d0=1. Given depth d:
  moments for support: <=4nd+2n-2;
  E operator shift: <=n(2d+1);
  K pair depth: <=(4n+1)d+2n+1;
  T triple pair depth: <=3d+1.
Define d_(r+1)=(4n+1)d_r+2n+1. After at most n-1 additions all basis
pairs have depth <=d_(n-1). The final q Prony moments and K matrix
need no more than
  J_n=4n d_(n-1)+2n-2
binary IO word depth, where
  d_r=((6n+1)(4n+1)^r-(2n+1))/(4n).
The bound is intentionally crude: J_1=4, J_2=114, J_3=3208.
It establishes finiteness, not useful acquisition complexity.
