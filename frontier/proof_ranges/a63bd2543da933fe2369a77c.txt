## 1. Observation model

Fix a product with exactly \(n\) tracked carbon atoms. Write its unordered entry-age multiset as
\[
\Xi=\sum_{i=1}^n\delta_{A_i},\qquad A_i\geq 0.
\]
For convenience, represent this multiset by an exchangeable lift \((A_1,\ldots,A_n)\): randomly permute its atoms, with multiplicity retained. Let \(\mu\) be the law of this lift. For a product collected at time \(t\), \(p(a)\) denotes the *actual, calibrated* heavy-label probability measured at the declared feed boundary at clock time \(t-a\). Shifting the measured feed waveform relative to product collection programs \(p\). Conditional on \(\Xi\), distinct entering carbon molecules receive independent labels with these probabilities. The measured product statistic is the whole-product heavy-carbon count \(K\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\).

The conditional probability generating function and its population average are
\[
G_{\mu,p}(z)
=\mathbb E_\mu\prod_{i=1}^n\bigl(1-p(A_i)+zp(A_i)\bigr),
\qquad
P_{\mu,p}(K=k)=[z^k]G_{\mu,p}(z).
\tag{1}
\]
For a fixed \(p\), these \(n+1\) values are expectations of symmetric functions of the unordered ages. They do not label carbon positions or identify the pathway that produced the ages.

The model assumes a known number \(n\), one tracked carbon per independently labelled feed molecule, isotope-neutral ancestry dynamics, an accounted carbon inventory, calibrated boundary waveform, and a calibrated whole-parent-product count channel. If one of these conditions fails, (1) is not the observation law for the claimed ancestry distribution.
