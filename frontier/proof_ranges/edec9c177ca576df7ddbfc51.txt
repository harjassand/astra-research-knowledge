Define
$$
c_N(x_1,\ldots,x_N)=\sum_{1\le i<j\le N}\phi(|x_i-x_j|),
$$
with value +∞ at a collision. Then there is a smooth compactly supported probability density ρ on ℝᵈ, with √ρ ∈ C_c∞, such that, for μ=ρ dx:

1. The equal-marginal Kantorovich problem for c_N has a finite attained minimum.
2. Every optimal plan fails to be a graph over any one of its N coordinates.
3. The infimum over (N−1) μ-preserving Borel maps equals the Kantorovich minimum, and finite-cost graph plans approach the value.

The density depends on N,d,φ and the chosen radius. Its construction uses 2N−1 disjoint smooth component patches; it does not give connected support or a density strictly positive throughout a connected domain. This is a counterexample to a universal “an optimal Monge plan always exists” assertion; it says nothing about arbitrary prescribed μ, genericity, uniqueness, or minimizers for standard physical densities.

The assumptions include φ_s(r)=r^(−s) for every s>0, and screened Coulomb/Yukawa φ_κ(r)=e^(−κr)/r for every κ>0. For Yukawa,
$$
\phi_\kappa'(r)=-e^{-\kappa r}(\kappa/r+1/r^2)<0,\quad
\phi_\kappa''(r)=e^{-\kappa r}(\kappa^2/r+2\kappa/r^2+2/r^3)>0.
$$

## Reconstruction

### 1. A finite center code: two rotated regular polygons

Embed a plane in ℝᵈ. Choose R>0, and let A={a_{0,j}} for j=0,…,m−1 be the regular m-gon of radius R, with angles 2πj/m. Let B={a_{1,j}} be the same polygon rotated through π/m. Their union is the regular 2m-gon. Each branch configuration is the origin plus all vertices from one of the two polygons. The polygon centroid identity Σⱼ a_{k,j}=0 gives
$$
c_N(0,a_{k,0},\ldots,a_{k,m-1})
=C_*:=m\phi(R)+\sum_{j<\ell}\phi(|a_{k,j}-a_{k,\ell}|),
$$
with the same value for both k, and ∇ₓ c_N(0,a_{k,0},\ldots)=0.

The crucial center-level strictness is a self-contained Jensen argument. For angular separation t∈(0,2π), set F(t)=φ(2R sin(t/2)). With r(t)=2R sin(t/2), r''=−r/4, so
$$
F''(t)=\phi''(r)(r')^2-\phi'(r)r/4>0.
$$
For any m distinct points on the circle, order them cyclically and let their gaps be g₁,…,gₘ>0, Σᵢ gᵢ=2π. For q=1,…,m−1, set sᵢ,q=gᵢ+⋯+gᵢ₊q₋₁ cyclically. Then Σᵢsᵢ,q=2πq. Since F is strictly convex,
$$
\sum_i F(s_{i,q})\ge mF(2\pi q/m).
$$
The pair-energy equals ½Σ_qΣ_iF(sᵢ,q), so it is minimized uniquely by the regular m-gon. Among m-subsets of the 2m-gon A∪B, only the two parity classes A,B are regular m-gons. Every mixed selection has strictly higher outer pair-energy. Since each selected outer center has distance R from the origin, its central interactions are all mφ(R); thus the only distinct-center equality patterns with the origin are the two prescribed branches.

All distinct-center patterns without the origin have positive cost, whereas the outer center potentials below are zero. If any two variables lie in the same sufficiently small component ball, their mutual cost tends to +∞ uniformly as the radius shrinks, and dominates the bounded sum of dual potentials. There are finitely many center patterns, so all noncontact inequalities persist after shrinking the component balls.

### 2. Lift the center code to exact local equality manifolds

Fix branch k, choose one polygon vertex aₖ,₀ as anchor y, and minimize over the remaining m variables
$$
w=(x,z_1,\ldots,z_{m-1})\in(\mathbb R^d)^m,
$$
where x is the central point and zⱼ are the other polygon vertices. Let bₖ,ⱼ be the gradient of c_N in zⱼ at the center configuration. Use local potentials
$$
u_0(x)=C_*-\frac K2|x|^2,\qquad
v_{k,j}(z)=b_{k,j}\cdot(z-a_{k,j})-\frac K2|z-a_{k,j}|^2.
$$
For Fₖ=c_N−u₀−Σⱼvₖ,ⱼ, the w-gradient vanishes at the center configuration. Its Hessian there is Mₖ+K Iₘd, positive definite for large K.

Let h(v)=φ(|v|). At any nonzero v, the eigenvalues of D²h(v) are φ″(|v|) radially and φ′(|v|)/|v| tangentially, so every such Hessian is invertible. The mixed derivative Dᵧ∇w c_N is a stack of m invertible d×d blocks: the central-variable block is −D²h(aₖ,₀), and the block for zⱼ is −D²h(aₖ,ⱼ−aₖ,₀). As K→∞,
$$
K(M_k+K I_{md})^{-1}D_y\nabla_w c_N\longrightarrow D_y\nabla_w c_N.
$$
Hence for all sufficiently large K, every d×d component block of the stationary-branch derivative is invertible. The implicit function theorem and inverse function theorem give smooth local diffeomorphisms Xₖ(y) for the central point and Zₖ,ⱼ(y) for every other polygon vertex. Positive definiteness on small product balls makes the stationary point the unique minimizer for each fixed y. Its minimum value supplies the anchor potential vₖ,₀(y), giving an exact local supporting inequality with equality precisely on that branch.

Both branches have the same central potential u₀. At each outer center the corresponding vₖ,ⱼ (including the anchor minimum potential) vanishes. Combining these local inequalities, the strict center-level gaps above, and the collision barrier produces one bounded potential u on the disjoint union of the 2N−1 component balls such that
$$
c_N(x_1,\ldots,x_N)\ge \sum_{i=1}^N u(x_i)
$$
on the whole support product, with equality exactly on permutations of the two local branch families.

Reparameterize each branch by central point x: invert Xₖ, and compose its inverse with each Zₖ,ⱼ. This yields 2m smooth diffeomorphisms Hₖ,ⱼ:B→ℝᵈ, k∈{0,1}, j=0,…,m−1, whose open images lie in the distinct outer component balls and such that
$$
(x,H_{k,0}(x),\ldots,H_{k,m-1}(x))
$$
is an equality tuple for every x∈B.

### 3. Smooth marginal, optimal coupling, and exact mass obstruction

Choose g∈C_c∞(B), g≥0, ∫g²=1, and write ν=g²dx. Set
$$
\mu=\frac1N\nu+\frac1{2N}\sum_{k=0}^1\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(H_{k,j})_\#\nu.
$$
The coefficients sum to one because 1/N+2m/(2N)=1. Each image measure has smooth density gₖ,ⱼ², where
$$
g_{k,j}(y)=g(H_{k,j}^{-1}y)\,|\det DH_{k,j}^{-1}(y)|^{1/2}.
$$
The image of supp g is compactly inside Hₖ,ⱼ(B), and all component supports are disjoint. Thus ρ and √ρ are both smooth and compactly supported.

Sample x∼ν, choose one of the two branches uniformly, and uniformly permute the resulting N-tuple. Its law π₀ has all marginals μ and lies on the equality set, so it saturates the supporting inequality. Any other coupling also lies in the support product and satisfies the inequality. Therefore π₀ is a finite optimal coupling. Every optimal coupling must be concentrated on the equality set.

Now let a candidate optimal graph use any coordinate as its source, and restrict to the event that this source coordinate is in B. Equality forces any one of its other coordinates to take one of the 2m values Hₖ,ⱼ(x). For any Borel selection set S⊂B,
$$
\mu(S)=\nu(S)/N,\qquad
\mu(H_{k,j}(S))=\nu(S)/(2N).
$$
If a μ-preserving map T selected Hₖ,ⱼ(x) on S, then
$$
\nu(S)/N=\mu(S)\le\mu(T^{-1}(H_{k,j}(S)))
=\mu(H_{k,j}(S))=\nu(S)/(2N),
$$
forcing ν(S)=0. The finitely many selection sets cannot cover B up to full ν-measure. This rules out a graph over any coordinate for every optimal coupling, not merely for the exhibited π₀.

### 4. Equality of infima without attainment

The source's cell-reallocation proof (§6) generalizes unchanged. The finitely many compact component supports have a positive minimum separation η. Partition each into cells of diameter at most δ, and let qᵢ₁…ᵢN be the masses of π₀ on cell products. Split each source cell into pieces of these exact masses, then use Borel maps between absolutely continuous probabilities (e.g. the source's explicit Rosenblatt/conditional-quantile construction) to realize the prescribed target-cell laws for each of the other N−1 coordinates. The resulting graph coupling has exactly the same cell-product masses as π₀, and only uses products of distinct component supports. On these products the cost is Lipschitz; if L=sup|φ′| over their compact distance range, the cost oscillation in a cell product is at most N(N−1)Lδ. Thus finite-cost graph plans approach ∫c_N dπ₀, while no graph plan attains it.
