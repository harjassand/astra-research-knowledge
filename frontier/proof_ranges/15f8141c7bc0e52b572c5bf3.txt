## Appendix A. A failed sunflower surrogate, with exact proof

A probability law on subsets is R-spread if Pr(S subset X) <= R^(-|S|) for every finite S. The proposed shortcut was that sufficiently large constant spread might force disjoint samples. It is false even for the existence of a disjoint pair.

Take q>=2 and m>=q^q disjoint blocks, each of size q. A set in the family fills one block completely and chooses exactly one element in each other block. Choose the full block uniformly and all other choices independently and uniformly. Every set has size k=m+q-1; there are m q^(m-1) distinct sets.

Any two members intersect: the second member meets the full block of the first.

Set R=q/(1+(q-1)/m). If S has at least two elements in each of two blocks, its inclusion probability is zero. If S has u>=2 elements in one block and s singleton elements in other blocks, the inclusion probability is m^(-1)q^(-s), at most R^(-(u+s)) because m>=q^q>=R^u and R<=q. If S has singleton elements in t distinct blocks, its inclusion probability is

    q^(-t) [1+(q-1)t/m]
      <= q^(-t) [1+(q-1)/m]^t
      = R^(-t),

by the binomial inequality. Thus the family is R-spread with arbitrarily large R, yet pairwise intersecting. The construction does not rule out sunflowers with a common core.

A second rejected move was to define an entropy-minimising coupling supported on sunflower triples and hope for a universal O(k) divergence bound. For a sunflower-free k-uniform family, such a coupling with uniform identical marginals is necessarily diagonal and its divergence from the independent product is 2 log |F|. Merely asking for the needed O(k) bound therefore reinstates the difficult exponential sunflower bound instead of supplying a mechanism.
