Let \(S\subseteq\{0,1\}^n\) have pairwise Hamming distance at least \(\delta n\). Let \(P\) be supported on \(S\), with
\[ \max_xP(x)\le2^{-h}. \]
For a uniform \(k\)-dimensional code, \(h=k\).
Let \(Q\) be a mixture of \(L\) Gaussian polynomial-threshold generators, each with coordinate degrees at most \(D\ge1\). Components may have different latent dimensions, means, covariances, and coefficients.
For \(0<\eta<1\), define
\[ K(D,\delta,\eta) = \left(\frac2\eta\right)^{ \cot^2\left(\frac{\pi\delta\eta^2}{4D}\right) }. \]
\[ \boxed{ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q) \ge 1-\eta-LK(D,\delta,\eta)\,2^{-h}. } \]
No component is assumed to lie inside \(S\). The theorem allows arbitrary leakage and arbitrary component biases.
Two established analytic inequalities supply the inputs.
For correlated standard Gaussians \(G,G_\rho\), with
\[ \rho=\cos\theta, \]
Kane’s degree-\(D\) polynomial-threshold noise bound gives
\[ \Pr[F_i(G)\ne F_i(G_\rho)] \le\frac{D\theta}{\pi}. \]
This inequality is imported, not a new result of this investigation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.2709)
Gaussian hypercontractivity gives, for every measurable set \(A\),
\[ \Pr[G\in A,\ G_\rho\in A] \le \Pr[G\in A]^{2/(1+\rho)}. \]
This follows from the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck \(L^{1+\rho}\)-to-\(L^2\) inequality. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.2410)
Now consider one mixture component, with atom probabilities \(q_x\). Put
\[ \theta=\frac{\pi\delta\eta^2}{2D}, \qquad a=\frac{1-\rho}{1+\rho} =\tan^2(\theta/2), \]
and
\[ \tau=\frac1K =\left(\frac\eta2\right)^{1/a}. \]
Call an atom in \(S\) heavy when \(q_x>\tau\). There are at most \(K\) heavy atoms. Let \(T\) be the remaining target atoms, and set
\[ q=Q_j(T). \]
The probability that both correlated outputs lie in \(T\) is at least \(q^2\). Indeed, the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck correlation operator is positive semidefinite:
\[ \langle1_A,P_\rho1_A\rangle = \|P_{\sqrt\rho}1_A\|_2^2 \ge(\mathbb E1_A)^2. \]
Split this joint event into equal and unequal outputs.
If the outputs are unequal, they differ in at least \(\delta n\) coordinates. Summing the noise bound over coordinates gives
\[ \Pr[\text{both in }T\text{ and unequal}] \le\frac{D\theta}{\pi\delta} =\frac{\eta^2}{2}. \]
For equal outputs, hypercontractivity applied to each atom’s inverse image gives
\[ \Pr[F(G)=F(G_\rho)=x]\le q_x^{1+a}. \]
Consequently
\[ \sum_{x\in T}q_x^{1+a} \le q\tau^a =\frac{\eta q}{2}. \]
Combining the two parts,
\[ q^2\le\frac{\eta q}{2}+\frac{\eta^2}{2}. \]
The positive root is \(\eta\), so
\[ Q_j(T)\le\eta. \]
Take the union \(H\) of the heavy sets across all components. It has at most \(LK\) points, while
\[ Q(S\setminus H)\le\eta, \qquad P(S\setminus H)\ge1-LK2^{-h}. \]
The difference on this event proves the theorem. \(\square\)
Since \(\tan x\ge x\),
\[ \log_2K \le \frac{16D^2\log_2(2/\eta)} {\pi^2\delta^2\eta^4}. \]
For fixed \(D,\delta\), targets with \(h\ge Rn\), and subexponentially many components,
\[ L=2^{o(n)}, \]
the total-variation distance tends to one.
For a simple finite consequence, take \(\eta=\tfrac12\). If
\[ d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P,Q)\le\frac14, \]
then necessarily
\[ \boxed{ \log_2L+ \frac{512}{\pi^2}\frac{D^2}{\delta^2} \ge h-2. } \]
For uniform good codes and polynomially many components, this forces
\[ D=\Omega(\sqrt n). \]
The constants can be large; the displayed bound may be numerically vacuous at small block lengths. Its content is the resource scaling and its independence from latent dimension.
