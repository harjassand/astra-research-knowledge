Result: product interventions can replace microscopic kinetic calibration
I derived an experimental completion theorem for unknown, heterogeneous interventions.

Its central operation is:

Combining controls multiplicatively makes inner products of their unknown microscopic rate changes observable. Those inner products certify whether a data-derived combination of controls can recover the hidden predictive residual.

The experiment does not reconstruct the microscopic generator or estimate each transition’s response coefficient. It can also start from a reproducible nonstationary distribution.

This enlarges the earlier calibrated-field result in three respects: gains may differ arbitrarily between hidden transitions, the adequacy of the selected controls is assessed from measured quantities rather than assumed, and the resulting certificate can support prediction under a finite library of separately tested control actions.

The result is conditional on an explicit access interface. Product composition must be justified, and unobserved internal transitions must either remain unchanged or be covered by an activity counter. Thermodynamic rate ratios alone still cannot supply this information.

The potentially original contribution is this acquisition-to-prediction mechanism—not Gram matrices, lumpability, or response theory themselves. The proofs below are internally supported; independent review and historical priority remain unresolved. Response dependence on the chosen kinetic perturbation, and response-based tests of Markovian models, are established prior work.

Previously, recovery assumed a specified microscopic field rule. The earlier proof explicitly required that rule, rather than deriving it from observed energy differences.
