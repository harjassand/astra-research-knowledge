## 2. The mathematical mechanism

### An exact harmonic response to symmetric cubic noise

Let \(N_t\in\mathbb N^m\) consist of independent immigration–death coordinates:
\[
n\longrightarrow n+e_i\quad\text{at rate }1,\qquad
n\longrightarrow n-e_i\quad\text{at rate }n_i.
\]
This autonomous environment is nonexplosive and positive recurrent, with product \(\operatorname{Pois}(1)\) stationary distribution.

Conditional on \(N_t=n\), give \(Z_t\ge1\) birth and death rates
\[
\begin{aligned}
b_n(z)&=\eta+\lambda(n)z(z-1)+H(n)z(z-1)^2,\\
d_n(z)&=\mu(n)z(z-1)+H(n)z(z-1)^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where \(\eta>0\), \(\lambda,\mu\ge1\), and \(H>0\). Write \(D=\lambda-\mu\).

The symmetric \(H\)-term contributes **nothing** to the ordinary drift:
\[
\mathcal LZ=\eta+D(n)z(z-1).
\]

But consider the harmonic-number observable
\[
\ell(1)=0,\qquad
\ell(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{z-1}\frac1j.
\]
Since its forward and backward increments are \(1/z\) and \(-1/(z-1)\), direct substitution gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L\ell(z)
=(D(n)-H(n))(z-1)-\mu(n)+\frac{\eta}{z},
\qquad z\ge2.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Thus symmetric noise can change the sign controlling escape without changing the ordinary drift.

### The threshold is proved, including equality

For this process,
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\exists n:\ D(n)>H(n)
&\Longrightarrow \mathbb E_{n,z}\tau<\infty
\quad\text{from every state},\\
D(n)\le H(n)\ \forall n
&\Longrightarrow\text{nonexplosion and positive recurrence}.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{2}
\]

**Nonpositive case.** Equation (1) gives \(\mathcal L\ell\le\eta\) everywhere and \(\mathcal L\ell\le-1/2\) for sufficiently large \(z\). Therefore
\[
F(n,z)=1+\sum_i n_i+2(m+1)\ell(z)
\]
has finite sublevel sets, globally bounded-above generator, and strictly negative generator outside a finite set. Stopped Dynkin estimates exclude explosion; the finite-set return-time argument gives positive recurrence. This includes \(D=H\).

**Positive case.** Freeze a favorable environment state and put
\[
\alpha=\frac{\lambda-\mu}{H}>1.
\]
For the resulting birth–death chain define auxiliary weights
\[
\pi_1=1,\qquad
\pi_j=\frac{b_1\cdots b_{j-1}}{d_2\cdots d_j}.
\]
First-step recurrence gives the expected time to pass from \(j\) to \(j+1\):
\[
u_j=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\pi_i}{b_j\pi_j}.
\]
Now
\[
\frac{b_{j-1}}{d_j}
=1+\frac{\alpha-3}{j}+O(j^{-2}),
\qquad
\pi_j\asymp j^{\alpha-3}.
\]
Hence
\[
u_j=
\begin{cases}
O(j^{-\alpha}),&1<\alpha<2,\\
O(j^{-2}\log j),&\alpha=2,\\
O(j^{-2}),&\alpha>2.
\end{cases}
\]
All three cases are summable, so
\[
M:=\mathbb E_1\tau=\sum_{j\ge1}u_j<\infty.
\]
Because the chain starting at one must pass through every finite higher level,
\[
\sup_{z\ge1}\mathbb E_z\tau\le M.
\]

This uniform bound closes the recurrent-exposure argument. At each visit of \(N_t\) to the favorable state \(v\), the probability of remaining there for \(2M\) and exploding during that interval is at least
\[
\delta=\tfrac12 e^{-2Mq(v)}>0.
\]
Survival through \(k\) visits is at most \((1-\delta)^k\). The environment has finite mean return cycles, so the total expected lifetime is finite.

No assertion that a rare favorable state is “eventually enough” is left unsupported.
