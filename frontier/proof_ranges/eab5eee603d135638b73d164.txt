## Why this avoids a whole-network reconstruction, and when it cannot

The necessary reconstruction domain is the target's **connected reaction neighborhood**, not all dislocations in the crystal. The boundary record detects a line that has left the local field; only that line's route must be followed to a sink. A complete graph of unrelated dislocations is unnecessary if the target's local closure and a separately measured device performance envelope suffice. In practice, if the target is joined to a specimen-spanning junction network, following the products can become nearly a whole-network reconstruction. The certificate then correctly loses its hoped-for acquisition advantage.

There is an exact observation-equivalence obstruction to a stronger global claim. Let the structural assay image only \(U\), report only the signed net Burgers content of \(\Omega_{\rm act}\), and take one terminal electrical measurement on a current path that excludes a mechanically inactive region \(W\subset\Omega_{\rm act}\). State A contains no line in \(W\). State B is identical in \(U\) and contains an unobserved closed perfect-dislocation loop in \(W\); its signed flux through a complete outer circuit is zero, and it can have no stacking-fault area if it is a perfect loop. In the ideal readout geometry, the loop is outside the measured current path, so the chosen scalar output is exactly identical. Yet the internal defect networks differ. Thus this finite observation map is non-injective even before finite resolution/noise is added. To certify that *no* harmful defect remains in a global claim, either widen the structural assay to the entire relevant active volume at sufficient class/resolution, or narrow the claim to the tested target and service envelope. No new scalar observable avoids that missing information universally.

There is also a practical, not just topological, blind space. TEM contrast depends on diffraction vector, thickness, tilt and overlap. A fault can vanish when \(g\cdot R\) is integral; a line can be invisible for a given \(g\); a single projection confounds depth. Therefore, the presence of multiple selectable reflections and a calibrated detection floor are part of the certificate, not optional improvements.
