## Result in one paragraph

The candidate operation is to invert a mechanically balanced isotope laminate, thereby changing its dynamic surface response while preserving isotope inventory, nominal geometry, contact chemistry and specified static mechanical response coefficients. A complementary eight-layer h10BN/h11BN pair matches linear-laminate stretching, extension–bending and bending coefficients even when the two isotopes have different elastic constants. A reference Maxwell calculation gives measurable near-field spectral contrast while a mass-only dielectric model has identical static reflection. The first intended scientific task is a controlled test of dynamical-environment sensitivity in the organic superconductor kappa-(BEDT-TTF)2Cu[N(CN)2]Br, abbreviated kappa-ET. Its response to this particular intervention remains unmeasured. Large optical contrast is NOT a prediction of large equilibrium electronic change.

The proposed contribution is this constrained experimental substitution, not isotope heterostructuring, cavity control, Thue–Morse optics, transfer matrices or fluctuation–dissipation theory, all of which have prior art. Specialist novelty review remains necessary.
