## 4. The apparent universal operation does not compose

The finite theorem initially suggests a strong new operation: arbitrary full-rank entangled states become preparable from a free input, while the catalyst marginal is restored exactly.

The next question is whether those outputs can be used as ordinary resources without invalidating the return claim.

They cannot, in general.

Let \(\Phi\) be a Bell state in an active \(2\times2\) sector, and let \(v\) be an orthogonal, locally flagged product vacuum. Define the two-qutrit state
\[
\theta=\frac12\Phi+\frac12v.
\tag{21}
\]

The construction above produces \(\theta\) from a free input with catalyst \(\theta\), succeeding with probability \(1/2\). The successful joint state is
\[
\Omega
=
\frac12v_S\otimes\Phi_C
+
\frac12\Phi_S\otimes v_C.
\tag{22}
\]
Both marginals are \(\theta\).

Separately, \(\theta\) can be converted to \(\Phi\) by locally projecting onto its active sector, with probability \(1/2\).

Thus, writing \(\rightsquigarrow\) for the state-level marginal-return relation,
\[
\text{free}\rightsquigarrow\theta,
\qquad
\theta\rightsquigarrow\Phi.
\tag{23}
\]

But Theorem 1 gives
\[
\boxed{\text{free}\not\rightsquigarrow\Phi}
\tag{24}
\]
with any finite catalyst.

The physical composition explains the failure. Conditioning the output of (22) on the active sector selects
\[
\Phi_S\otimes v_C.
\]
The catalyst is vacuum, not \(\theta\). The combined successful protocol has consumed the entanglement that the first marginal description called “returned.”

### A general obstruction to state-only bookkeeping

This is not peculiar to Bell states.

For any finite-dimensional target \(\chi\), form
\[
\theta_\chi=\frac12\chi_{\rm active}+\frac12v.
\]
The support criterion permits
\[
\text{free}\rightsquigarrow\theta_\chi,
\]
and local flag projection permits
\[
\theta_\chi\rightsquigarrow\chi.
\]

Consequently:

\[
\boxed{
\text{The transitive closure of stochastic marginal-return reachability is universal.}
}
\tag{25}
\]

This is a statement about the **formal closure**, not physical universal generation.

It also rules out a nontrivial scalar resource monotone on states that is simultaneously nonincreasing under every such marginal-return transformation and ordinary stochastic LOCC. Those inequalities would imply
\[
M(\chi)\le M(\text{free})
\]
for every \(\chi\), while discarding gives the reverse inequality.

The role of external correlations in catalysis is already an established research subject. The claim here is the explicit failure of this success-conditioned state relation to support nontrivial transitive resource accounting—not a claim that correlations were previously overlooked.
