## 4. A positive spectral construction with optimally small contamination

Let m=2d and define the shifted Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes

    x_j=delta+(1-delta)[1-cos(j pi/m)]/2,   j=0,...,m.

For each j, let w_j be the Lagrange interpolation coefficient at zero:

    w_j=product_(k!=j) (-x_k)/(x_j-x_k).

For every polynomial p of degree at most m,

    p(0)=sum_j w_j p(x_j).                                     (8)

The nodes are positive and increasing, so sign(w_j)=(-1)^j, and sum w_j=1.

Define q(x)=T_m((1+delta-2x)/(1-delta)), where T_m is the Chebyshev polynomial. Then q(x_j)=(-1)^j. Consequently,

    Lambda=sum_j |w_j|
          =T_m((1+delta)/(1-delta))
          =cosh(2m artanh(sqrt(delta))).                        (9)

Put A=(Lambda+1)/2. Define two positive probability measures:

    nu_g=[delta_0 + sum_(j odd) |w_j| delta_(x_j)]/A,
    nu_b=[sum_(j even) w_j delta_(x_j)]/A.                       (10)

Each has d+1 support points. Their moments agree through degree 2d by (8). The good measure has mass 1-rho at zero, where

    rho=1-1/A=(Lambda-1)/(Lambda+1)
       =tanh^2(m artanh(sqrt(delta)))
       <=eta^2/9.                                              (11)

For the last inequality, sqrt(delta)=eta/(8d), artanh z<=4z/3 for z<=1/2, and tanh z<=z. Also

    w_0=product_(k>0) x_k/(x_k-delta) >=1,

so nu_b has mass at least 1-rho at x_0=delta.

For all real t,

    1-Re integral exp(-itx)dnu_g(x) <=2rho.                      (12)

At t_star=pi/delta<T,

    Re integral exp(-it_star x)dnu_b(x) <=-1+2rho.               (13)

Thus one response stays close to an exact onsite rotation for all time, while the other accumulates an almost opposite phase before T. Moment matching alone would not prove the active-access lower bound; Sections 6-7 prove the necessary channel statement.

### 4.1 Exact extremal meaning of the contamination

The value in (11) is optimal for the following purely polynomial problem. If a probability measure mu_g has mass 1-rho at zero and all its other mass in [delta,1], a probability measure mu_b is supported in [delta,1], and their moments agree through degree m, then

    rho >= (Lambda-1)/(Lambda+1).

Indeed, q(0)=Lambda while |q(x)|<=1 on [delta,1]. Matching q expectations gives

    (1-rho)Lambda-rho <= integral q dmu_g
                       = integral q dmu_b <=1.

The measures (10) attain equality. This is an explicit application of Chebyshev extremality, not a separate claim of a newly discovered approximation theorem.
