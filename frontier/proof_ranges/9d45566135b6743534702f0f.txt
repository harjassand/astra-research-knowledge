## 8. Finite coefficients and robustness actually proved

Use a dyadic step q=2^(-b) satisfying

    q<=min{eta/(96T), eta/(1200d^3)}.

Round common diagonal coefficients once and reuse them on both arms. Round hopping magnitudes and the central hopping down to the grid, and construct the other hypothesis by reflection. The active matrix changes by operator norm at most 3q, since at most two hopping errors and one diagonal error enter each row. All hopping row sums remain at most one.

Equation (16) and q<=eta/(1200d^3) keep every arm hopping positive. Also q<=gamma/6 keeps the central hopping positive. The endpoint difference remains bounded by one. Thus the controlled channel estimate is unchanged. Reflection isospectrality is exact, rather than approximately preserved.

Duhamel bounds the target-trajectory change through T by 3qT<=eta/32. The margins in (20) retain (2), and the vacuum gap remains at least 1-gamma-3q>0.9. Since all coefficients are bounded and b=O(log(d/eta)), the family admits a polynomial-size finite classical specification.

The Jacobi construction uses d-dimensional polynomial orthogonalization of explicitly specified positive measures. The diagnostic implementation uses arbitrary-precision numerical arithmetic and then matched dyadic rounding. It is not a general interval-certified compiler. The theorem proves existence of the displayed finite-precision family; it does not claim a compiled quantum circuit or a laboratory realization.

This is a SHARED-BULK perturbation statement. Independent changes near the observed ports can reveal the hypothesis immediately, and constant errors can accumulate over the long prediction horizon. We do not claim robustness to arbitrary fixed-strength disorder, finite-temperature background excitations, or decoherence. The center hopping is gamma=eta/(16T), and the construction is inhomogeneous rather than translation invariant.
