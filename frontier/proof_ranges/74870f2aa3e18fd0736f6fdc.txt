## Candidate mechanism and work definition

For a molecule with Hamiltonian H and state ρ, the ideal ergotropy is

\[
\mathcal W(\rho,H)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho H)-\min_U\operatorname{Tr}(U\rho U^\dagger H).
\]

This is the largest energy removable by a unitary on that molecule, with its eigenvalues fixed. A pure high-$J$ rotor can have nearly all its energy above the lowest allowed state as ergotropy. A passive thermal distribution has zero ergotropy. Neither rotational energy nor a nonzero ergotropy value guarantees that a practical optical interaction can extract it. A measured Raman coherence is not by itself a measurement of ergotropy.

A concrete candidate is a phase-matched sequence of stimulated rotational anti-Stokes Raman transitions, $J\to J-2$, driven with a seeded probe or a cavity mode. If a probe photon at frequency $\omega$ exits at $\omega+\Delta E_J/\hbar$, the useful energy is the *increment* $\hbar\Delta\omega=\Delta E_J$, after subtracting the incoming seed. For high-$J$ CO₂, this would require returning the rotational ladder through many steps. A time-reversed optical-centrifuge waveform is a second possible implementation: drive the polarization chirp backwards, with the molecular phase and the field phases controlled, so the rotor exerts negative torque on the field. In either version, the extracted sideband must be directed into a stored, reusable mode and reduce the replenishment work in a subsequent matched cycle.

The required closed balance is

\[
\Delta E_{\rm rotor}+\Delta E_{\rm vib}+\Delta E_{\rm chem}+\Delta E_{\rm translational}
+\sum_b (E_{b,\rm out}-E_{b,\rm in})+Q_{\rm bath}=0.
\]

All incident and outgoing fields must be counted, including seeds, pump light, sidebands, fluorescence, and cavity loss. A bright blue-shifted photon can be coherent while its energy comes mostly from the applied pumps. A red-shifted Stokes photon ordinarily deposits energy in the molecule. Spontaneous photons and CARS probe signals do not establish a reusable field. A loss of Raman coherence does not by itself show loss of ergotropy; population-resolved molecular energy and bath heating are needed too.
