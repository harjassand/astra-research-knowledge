Result: a local prediction capability with a sharp acquisition limit
I derived an explicit way to certify the equilibrium consequences of a local modification without reconstructing the unchanged bulk. I also derived a matching counterexample showing that, even when the local calculation is exact, the required physical information can remain exponentially expensive to acquire.

This does not complete the civilisation-level North Star. The concrete progress is a proved connection between a locally computable certificate, an implementable prediction procedure, and its unavoidable information cost. The broader thermal-update idea is established prior work; historical novelty of the particular certificate and combined result remains unverified.

The complete derivations, resource accounting, executed checks, and remaining obstruction are preserved here:

1. Selecting the obstruction
I compared three capabilities rather than starting from a preferred mathematical representation.

Scientific capability	Relevant specialised methods	Obstruction that matters here
Certified ground-state energies and phase selection	Variational and tensor-network calculations, lower-bound relaxations, quantum energy estimation	An accurate-looking candidate does not automatically provide a tight error certificate. Worst-case electronic-structure hardness also survives substantial physical restrictions.
Reliable long-time transport and response	Quantum simulation and tensor-network influence-functional methods	Avoiding a full wavefunction does not necessarily remove long-time complexity: it can reappear in the temporal correlations that must be represented.
Predicting local equilibrium design changes	Quantum belief propagation, equilibrium certification hierarchies, local entropy methods	The important questions are whether the update can be computed locally, whether its error can be certified, and whether the reference experiment contains enough information about the modified system.
The first row is constrained by an actual worst-case hardness result, not merely by the size of Hilbert space. The second already has methods that replace spatial-state representations with temporal ones. The third already has rigorous local-observable certification methods, so “certify observables without reconstructing the state” would not itself be a new contribution. 

I developed several mechanisms before concentrating on the third task.

Local marginal and entropy certificates led back to the question of when a finite, affordable calculation gives a useful bound. Dissipative witnesses led to an analogous issue: a small stationarity residual is insufficient without control over hidden slow processes. The mechanism developed most deeply was instead:

Use one reference equilibrium ensemble to predict specified changes to the material, while certifying how much knowledge of the surrounding material the calculation actually needs.

Here, “one reference” means one reference Hamiltonian—not one physical specimen. Fresh independent specimens are consumed.
