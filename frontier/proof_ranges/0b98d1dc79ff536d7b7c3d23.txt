Use a common denominator 2^b, with b polynomial. A uniform b-bit block is decoded to atom j according to a disjoint
integer interval of length equal to its numerator. Each interval has a polynomial-size binary comparator automaton.
Replace each atom-labelled transition by such a b-bit interval recognizer. The accepted bit strings encode the product
atom law exactly. Multiple nondeterministic witness paths do not multiply the count of a bit string.
The resulting ordinary NFA and unary input length are polynomial. Its accepted-word fraction equals the rounded
probability under the dyadic law. Total variation changes the event probability by at most tau rho/10, hence only O(tau)

approximates log Pr(A_i) to O(tau).
The external counting theorem [1] counts accepted words, not accepting runs, and also supplies polynomial-time

7 Conditional samples and the polynomial union correction
For a generated accepted atom word, jitter each X_j independently according to the original q_j law restricted to its
atom interval, and E according to its comparator cell. For the exact atom masses this produces q times the independent
E law conditioned on the rounded acceptance event. That acceptance event is a subset of {E>=R}; its mass differs by
O(tau) relatively, so conditioning changes the desired tilted joint law by O(tau) in total variation. Dyadic-law and NFA-
generator errors can also be budgeted as O(tau), because the normalizer is >=rho/2.
Project to the non-anchor X_j, then sample
  X_i=(T-L)_+ + Z, Z~Exp(lambda),
and sample every activity outside V_i independently from its original exponential law. This is within O(tau) total
variation of the original environment conditional on A_i. The true ideal conditional law follows directly from the
likelihood identity in Section 1. B_j conditionals are elementary memorylessness.
Now use the ordinary union-of-at-most-2n-events estimator: select a component with probability proportional to its
probability, sample its conditional environment, evaluate the number M(X) of true components containing X, and
average 1/M(X). The ideal mean is U/W and lies in [1/(2n),1]. Choosing all component count and conditional-sampling
errors tau=O(epsilon/n), with appropriate constants and polynomial sample count, gives relative O(epsilon) error in U
and hence additive O(epsilon) error in log U. Component weights are normalized in log scale without ever writing
exp(-lambda T) as a huge denominator. Optional omission of negligible components must use the retained-component
membership count throughout. Require total discarded component weight at most epsilon*W/(100K), which ensures
lost union mass at most epsilon*U/100 because W<=K*U. It suffices to drop only components whose true log gap from
the largest component is at least log(100K^2/epsilon); when gaps are computed approximately, add an explicit guard at
least twice the known log-estimation error. Alternatively keep every component and implement log-scale categorical
sampling to controlled total-variation accuracy.

Finite precision sampling
A discrete atom sample is not itself a continuous conditional sample. The jitter must use lazy continuous random bits or
an explicitly bounded finite-precision coupling. Membership checks for the true A_i/B_j can use interval arithmetic and
be refined until unambiguous, except on null boundaries. A worst-case polynomial-bit truncation uses the following
anti-concentration argument; the product tilted densities have polynomial-bit logarithmic sup bounds and conditioning
normalizer >=rho/2, and the shifted anchor adds an independent exponential. Section 9 below supplies a polynomial
precision bound from these density and conditioning estimates; continuous sampling and exact-real comparisons are
not assumed as oracles.

9 Finite precision membership and sampler implementation
Here is an explicit anti-concentration bound for the ideal jittered sampler from Section 7. Its conditioning is the
rounded acceptance event under the exact q/E product law; its probability is at least rho/2 for sufficiently small tau.
Before that conditioning, non-anchor class coordinates are independent q_j variables on [0,T], and coordinates outside
V_i are independent original exponentials. The anchor is then X_i=(T-L_i)_+ +Z with independent Z~Exp(lambda_i).
Let D=max(1,max_j lambda_j,max_(j in V_i except i)(delta_j+1/T)). For any fixed nonempty original path P and any
u>0:
    If P contains i, conditional on all other base variables its duration is Z plus a fixed shift. Therefore Pr(|duration(P)-
     T|<=u)<=2 lambda_i u.
    If P does not contain i, choose any j in P. Under the unconditioned independent base law its sum has density at
     most D, by conditioning on the other coordinates. Imposing the rounded-acceptance conditioning raises this strip
     probability by at most 2/rho. Hence Pr(|duration(P)-T|<=u)<=4Du/rho.
The same bounds apply to strips |X_j-T|<=u. A DAG with n activity edges has at most 2^n distinct path edge sets.
Across at most n anchored component tests, a coarse union bound therefore makes every true component-membership
test unambiguous, except with probability zeta, when
  u = zeta rho / [100 n 2^n D].
This rational has polynomial bit length. Empty paths have length zero and cannot meet T>0, so they create no boundary
issue. For a B_j conditional sampler, memorylessness gives the analogous bounds directly, without the rho
denominator.
Compute each non-anchor base coordinate and Z to an enclosing interval of width at most u/(10n^2). Longest-path and
positive-part computations are 1-Lipschitz in the sum norm, so the anchor interval and then every path-sum interval
have width less than u. If any component-membership test remains ambiguous, return an arbitrary indicator or sample
and charge the entire event to failure zeta. This is a controlled finite-precision coupling; no assertion of TV proximity
between a discrete vector and a continuous law is made.
All required coordinate magnitudes have polynomial bit length with probability 1-zeta: class coordinates are bounded
by T; outside exponentials and Z can be capped for computation at J/lambda_j, where integer J>=log(10n/zeta), at total
exceptional probability O(zeta). The anchor is at most T+Z.
Conditional jitter of a truncated exponential on a rational interval [a,b] is explicit. For delta>0 use
  a - (1/delta) log(1-U(1-exp(-delta(b-a)))), U uniform(0,1),
and for delta=0 use a+(b-a)U. If delta(b-a) exceeds a polynomial logarithmic precision cutoff, replace the interval law
by a+Exp(delta), charging its exponentially small truncation tail. Otherwise certified exp/log or monotone CDF
bisection computes the displayed quantile. Excluding U within kappa of an endpoint costs at most 2kappa and bounds
the required inverse-CDF derivative; all logs of the input scales, interval widths, target width, and kappa are
polynomial. Choosing kappa=zeta/poly(n) gives a worst-case polynomial-bit sampler whose generated intervals couple
to the ideal continuous variables within the requested widths except with probability O(zeta).
Dyadic atom-law error and approximate NFA word generation are first coupled at the atom-word level. On agreement
the same conditional jitter kernel is used, so their O(tau) TV error remains O(tau) before finite-precision comparisons.
The above anti-concentration analysis is needed only for the ideal exact-law word sampler.

10 Explicit robust union estimator error accounting
Let K<=2n be the number of nonempty components, p_r their true probabilities, W=sum p_r, and pi_r=p_r/W. Suppose
every estimated p_r has relative error at most a, and every conditional sampler is within TV b of the true conditional
law. The estimated selection probabilities differ from pi in TV at most 2a (for a<=1/4). The ideal variable Z=1/M(X),
where M counts the true containing components, lies in [1/K,1] and has mean U/W>=1/K. Approximate selection and
sampling change its mean by at most 2a+b, and membership-computation failures at probability zeta add at most zeta.
