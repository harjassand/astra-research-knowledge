# Exterior receiving theorem: collision endpoints and marked-label parity

Status: self-contained proposed mathematical derivation plus finite exact diagnostics. The determinant, Pfaffian, fixed/free-endpoint and exterior-representation algebra is classical. Historical novelty of the quantitative receiving composition is UNKNOWN. No external theorem audit or physical workload validation occurred.

## 1. Models, conventions and outputs

Vertices are ordered `0,...,n-1`. For an undirected weighted graph let

\[
 L_{xx}=d_x=\sum_y r_{xy},\qquad L_{xy}=-r_{xy}\quad(x\ne y),
 \qquad K(t)=e^{-tL}.
\]

Rates are fixed, nonnegative, symmetric and independent of the state. Set `1 <= k <= n` and `X=(x_1<...<x_k)`. Distinguish three outputs:

1. **Collision model.** Identical independent walkers start at X. Stop at their first simultaneous occupation of one vertex. The endpoint is the sorted occupied set, with an additional cemetery outcome for prior collision. On a path, sorted positions also identify each initial walker because order cannot change before collision. On a cycle they do not identify the cyclic rotation of labels or winding.
2. **Conditional collision endpoint.** The complete sorted endpoint, or its number of sites in a supplied bin B, conditional on no collision before t. A certified positive survival lower bound is required for a certified relative/conditional error.
3. **Interchange parity.** In the original interchange process, select the labels initially at X. If their final sites in that label order are `(sigma(x_1),...,sigma(x_k))`, let `s_X(sigma)` be the sign of the permutation sorting that sequence. This is a binary endpoint observable on every graph. It is not their full joint endpoint law.

An optional nonnegative site potential `v_x` replaces L by `L+diag(v)`. In the collision model this means independent deaths at the corresponding site hazards. Equivalently its surviving endpoint weights include

\[
 \exp\{-\int_0^t\sum_{i=1}^k v_{X_i(u)}\,du\}.
\]

Thus nonnegative potentials also supply exact Laplace transforms of total exposure until collision. Death and collision share a cemetery outcome unless a richer output is separately constructed. All finite-bit executions below use integer rates, integer potentials and t=1. The structural theorem also permits identical directed nearest-neighbor walkers and time-dependent rates/potentials; those extensions are not implemented by the unit-horizon prototype.

## 2. All-k generator identity on paths and cycles

For an n-by-n matrix A let `dGamma_k(A)` act on wedges by applying A to each one of k factors and summing. The ordered basis is `e_Y=e_y1 wedge ... wedge e_yk`, with `y_1<...<y_k`. Its diagonal at Y is `sum_{y in Y} A_yy`. Replacing y by z outside Y has coefficient `A_yz` times the sign needed to reorder the resulting wedge. Replacements into Y vanish.

The killed independent-walker generator H has that same diagonal with A=L+diag(v), off-diagonal `-r_yz` for each move to an unoccupied z, and killing rate

\[
 2\sum_{\{y,z\}\subset Y}r_{yz}+\sum_{y\in Y}v_y.
\]

Both directions of an attempted meeting contribute, explaining the factor 2.

**Path.** A nearest-neighbor noncolliding move never passes another coordinate. Its wedge reordering sign is +1. Hence

\[
 H=d\Gamma_k(L+\operatorname{diag}v).
\]

**Cycle.** Cut the cycle at `(n-1,0)`. Let A have ordinary degree/potential diagonal and ordinary negative off-diagonals except on this wrap edge, where

\[
 A_{0,n-1}=A_{n-1,0}=-(-1)^{k-1}r_{0,n-1}.
\]

An allowed wrap move passes k-1 other occupied sites. The wedge sign `(-1)^(k-1)` cancels the displayed twist, so again H=`dGamma_k(A)`. Thus odd k uses the ordinary periodic one-particle matrix, and even k the antiperiodic one. The diagonal still includes both collision directions.

For any A, differentiation of the wedge action gives

\[
 e^{-t d\Gamma_k(A)}=\bigwedge^k(e^{-tA}).
\]

Consequently, with `K=e^(-tA)`, the collision-model subprobability at sorted endpoint Y is

\[
 \mu_X(Y)=\det K_{X,Y}. \tag{2.1}
\]

This proves nonnegativity of these particular k-minors in the path/cycle contract, without assuming positivity of other-order minors on a twisted cycle. It also proves the formula directly for potentials.

For time-dependent nearest-neighbor matrices, take products of short-time propagators and use `wedge^k(UV)=wedge^k(U) wedge^k(V)`. Passing to the finite-dimensional evolution limit gives (2.1) with the time-ordered one-particle kernel. Identical walker generators are essential; arbitrary particle-dependent diffusion rates do not share this construction.

## 3. Positive cover for even-k cycles

Let C be the ordinary Laplacian on the 2n-cycle, with each edge weight and potential repeated twice. Set `T=[I;-I]`. Entrywise,

\[
 CT=TA,\qquad e^{-tC}T=T e^{-tA}.
\]

The first n rows therefore give

\[
 K_{xy}= [e^{-tC}]_{x,y}-[e^{-tC}]_{x,y+n}. \tag{3.1}
\]

Since cover rows are nonnegative and substochastic, the signed projected rows satisfy `||K_x||_1 <= 1`. If a nonnegative substochastic cover approximation has row error epsilon, the projected signed row also has l1 error at most epsilon and norm at most one. The projection has l1 operator norm at most one. The same fixed intertwiner works with time-varying cycle rates and repeated potentials.

The cover preserves all-k collision positivity after taking k-minors. Its one-particle projection need not itself be a stochastic kernel.

## 4. Pfaffian compression, including odd k and a count polynomial

For any k-by-n matrix M over a characteristic-zero commutative ring define the skew matrix `J_uv=sign(v-u)` and the row sum vector `c=M 1`. Then

\[
 \sum_{|Y|=k}\det M_Y=
 \begin{cases}
  \operatorname{Pf}(MJM^T),&k\text{ even},\\
  \operatorname{Pf}\begin{pmatrix}MJM^T&c\\-c^T&0\end{pmatrix},&k\text{ odd}.
 \end{cases} \tag{4.1}
\]

Here is a proof, so no external identity is a premise. Put `ell_y=sum_i M_iy xi_i` in an exterior algebra. The bivector `sum_{u<v} ell_u wedge ell_v` has coefficient matrix MJM^T. Its kth-degree top coefficient is the Pfaffian. Expanding by distinct y-columns gives `sum_Y Pf(J_Y) det M_Y`. Every even principal `J_Y` has Pfaffian 1: expansion in its first row gives an alternating sum of an odd number of ones. This proves the even formula. For odd k append one row and one last column, with a single 1 at their intersection. Every nonzero maximal minor includes that column. The even formula for the enlarged matrix is precisely the bordered odd formula in (4.1).

For a supplied bin `B subset {0,...,n-1}` set `w_y(z)=z` on B and 1 elsewhere, and take

\[
 M_{iy}(z)=K_{x_i,y}w_y(z).
\]

Then (4.1) computes

\[
 G_X(z)=\sum_{j=0}^k p_j z^j,
 \qquad p_j=\Pr_X(\tau>t,\ |Y_t\cap B|=j), \tag{4.2}
\]

with deaths included in tau if potentials are present. No binomial-state enumeration or interpolation is required: evaluate the Pfaffian exactly over the polynomial ring. At z=1 this is survival. Replacing w_y by any real terminal weights of absolute value at most 1 gives the corresponding multiplicative endpoint observable, with the same error proof below. For a fixed number of bins, multivariate polynomials give joint counts, but their output coefficient count must be charged; arbitrary many bins can require an exponential-size output.

## 5. A full-vector receiving bound with no factorial factor

Let the true one-particle rows be `a_1,...,a_k`, and supplied approximate rows `b_1,...,b_k`, with

\[
 \|a_i\|_1,\|b_i\|_1\le1,\qquad \|a_i-b_i\|_1\le\epsilon_i,
 \qquad \delta=\sum_i\epsilon_i.
\]

The rows may be signed, as on an even-k cycle. Define signed endpoint measures by determinants of their respective row matrices. Then

\[
 \sum_{|Y|=k}|\det a_Y-\det b_Y|\le\delta. \tag{5.1}
\]

To prove this, let `F(v_1,...,v_k)=prod_i a_i(v_i)`. The map from F to its signed sum over permutations of each distinct unordered endpoint tuple is an l1 contraction: each distinct tuple occurs in exactly one endpoint set, and the triangle inequality discards signs. It maps F to the determinant measure. The tensor-product telescoping identity gives

\[
 \|\bigotimes_i a_i-\bigotimes_i b_i\|_1
 \le\sum_i\|a_i-b_i\|_1
       \prod_{h<i}\|b_h\|_1\prod_{h>i}\|a_h\|_1
 \le\delta.
\]

This proves (5.1). Aggregating endpoints by bin counts is another l1 contraction, so the **entire coefficient vector** in (4.2) has total absolute error at most delta. The error is not k! epsilon, binomial(n,k) epsilon, or a bound obtained by multiplying independent determinant-entry errors. Neither approximate minors nor approximate coefficients need be nonnegative.

The k dependence is unavoidable in this row-only contract: k distinct unmoving walkers with a uniform small site-death rate have survival `exp(-kvt)` and one-row loss `1-exp(-vt)`. The ratio of survival loss to one-row loss tends to k as vt tends to zero.

### Two generators

If K and K' are valid one-particle kernels for the same path/cycle k contract, then (5.1) holds with

\[
 \theta=\sum_{i=1}^k\|K_{x_i,:}-K'_{x_i,:}\|_1. \tag{5.2}
\]

After adding collision/death cemetery mass, their complete killed-endpoint laws have TV distance at most theta: the living l1 difference is at most theta and the cemetery difference at most theta. For conditional endpoints, if both survival masses are at least s0, the TV difference is at most theta/s0. These are observable-specific perturbation certificates. They do not give full interchange endpoint TV.

## 6. Receiving a conditional count sampler

Compute exact rational signed coefficients `a_j` from the approximate row matrix. Let `s_hat=sum_j a_j`. A certified survival lower bound is

\[
 s_0=\max(0,s_{\rm hat}-\delta).
\]

For conditional use require s0>0 and verify that the clipped total below is positive; this is automatic for the displayed lower bound computed from s_hat. Set `b_j=max(a_j,0)` and `B=sum_j b_j`. Clipping cannot increase distance to the true nonnegative coefficient vector, so `sum_j |p_j-b_j|<=delta`. If `s=sum_j p_j>=s0`, then

\[
 \operatorname{TV}\left((p_j/s)_j,(b_j/B)_j\right)
 \le\frac{\sum_j|p_j-b_j|+|s-B|}{2s}
 \le\delta/s_0. \tag{6.1}
\]

In the integer implementation, `a_j=N_j/Q^k`, so sample integer weights `max(N_j,0)` directly. Draw a uniform integer below their positive sum using `ceil(log2(sum))` fair bits and reject integers outside the range. Acceptance is at least 1/2. With A attempts and fallback to a fixed count, the additional TV error is at most `2^-A`, and the deterministic fair-bit bound is `A ceil(log2(sum))=O(Akp)`. Thus no rare-event rejection with expected cost 1/s is needed. Rare survival instead raises the required kernel precision.

For an unconditional count-plus-cemetery receiver, the raw cemetery mass is `1-s_hat>=0` because the determinant signed measure has l1 norm at most one. Clip negative living coefficients and normalize all outcomes. If m is the exactly measured total negative living mass, its TV error is at most `delta+m<=2delta`. When all coefficients are nonnegative, it is at most delta. These are valid repairs of a signed approximation; assuming rounded minors remain positive would be incorrect.

## 7. Fixed-prefix block Pfaffians

Let F=`(f_1<...<f_s)` be forced first endpoints and let every remaining endpoint be in a suffix `B_a={a,...,n-1}`, with `f_s<a`. For a k-by-n matrix M define

\[
 Z(F,a)=\sum_{Z\subset B_a,\ |Z|=k-s}\det M_{[F,Z]}.
\]

Write `C=M_F` and `B=M_{B_a}J_{B_a}M_{B_a}^T`. If k-s is even,

\[
 Z(F,a)=(-1)^{s(s-1)/2}
  \operatorname{Pf}\begin{pmatrix}B&C\\-C^T&0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{7.1}
\]

For odd k-s, append a new row and a free last column having a single 1 at their intersection, exactly as in Section 4, and apply (7.1) to the enlarged matrix. The block order is at most 2k in both cases.

Proof: in the exterior proof of (4.1), wedge the forced column forms `ell_f1,...,ell_fs` with the free-column bivector power. Introducing s auxiliary variables eta turns this expression into the Pfaffian of the block in (7.1). Moving each eta past the subsequent forced forms contributes the sign `(-1)^(s(s-1)/2)`. The dummy free last column handles odd free degree without changing any selected minor. This is a fixed/free-endpoint form of the classical minor-summation formula.

With integer approximate rows M/Q, every Z has the same denominator Q^k, even with the ghost row. Thus all prefix masses are exactly consistent marginals of **one** signed endpoint measure. The consistency is essential for the following sampling error proof.

Suffix skew blocks can be precomputed by

\[
 B_a=B_{a+1}+m_a t_{a+1}^T-t_{a+1}m_a^T,
 \qquad t_a=m_a+t_{a+1},
\]

in O(nk^2) integer operations and O(nk^2 p) bits. Thereafter each prefix mass requires a Pfaffian of order at most 2k, with no scan through n sites.

## 8. Direct conditional sampling of the complete sorted endpoint

The endpoint-set space is traversed by a binary tree, but only one sampled path is visited. After prefix F, binary-search the next site in `[lo,hi]`, where `lo=last(F)+1` (or 0) and `hi=n-(k-|F|)`. A search node has signed mass

\[
 Z(F,lo)-Z(F,hi+1).
\]

At midpoint m its child masses are

\[
 a_L=Z(F,lo)-Z(F,m+1),\qquad
 a_R=Z(F,m+1)-Z(F,hi+1). \tag{8.1}
\]

Choose the child in proportion to their clipped positive masses. If both clipped masses vanish, take a fixed feasible child. At a singleton append that site to F and repeat. The output always consists of k distinct ordered sites. Each next-site search has depth at most `ceil(log2 n)`, so

\[
 D\le k\lceil\log_2 n\rceil
\]

binary decisions suffice. At most 3D prefix queries are made before caching, and repeated queries are cached in the implementation.

**TV theorem.** Let p be the true living endpoint measure, a the determinant signed approximation, `||p-a||_1<=delta`, and `p(total)>=s0>0`. The ideal positive-repair tree sampler Q satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(p/p(total),Q)\le D\delta/s_0. \tag{8.2}
\]

At a node E with true mass p(E)>0, the two child masses p_L,p_R form a nonnegative vector. Clipping the signed child masses and normalizing gives local conditional TV error at most

\[
 (|p_L-a_L|+|p_R-a_R|)/p(E).
\]

This follows from the same normalization inequality as (6.1). If both clipped masses are zero, then `|p_L-a_L|+|p_R-a_R|>=p(E)`, so the bound remains valid for the fixed fallback. Multiply local error by the true conditional probability p(E)/s of reaching the node. At each decision depth, the child events partition the endpoint-set space; hence their total absolute signed-mass error is at most `||p-a||_1<=delta`. A maximal coupling that proceeds until the first mismatched decision bounds total TV by the sum over depths, giving D delta/s. Padding completed paths with no-op decisions deals with unequal depths.

Using A-attempt bounded-fair-bit rejection at each positive node adds at most `D 2^-A`. Therefore the fully implemented receiving contract is

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{TV}(\text{true endpoint}\mid\tau>t,\ \text{delivered sample})
 \le D(\delta/s_0+2^{-A}).} \tag{8.3}
\]

The count receiver (6.1) is sharper when only counts are required. The full-endpoint receiver is useful when actual correlated positions matter. On a cycle this remains the unordered endpoint set, not the complete labelled/winding endpoint.

## 9. Exact integer one-particle enclosure and all costs

For the implemented constant-rate integer input, let c be the cover-state count: n for a path or odd-k cycle, 2n for an even-k cycle. Include site killing in the diagonal and set `Lambda=max_i C_ii`. Choose `N=2^m>=Lambda`, `Q=2^p`, `p>=m`, and

\[
 M_0=Q(I-C/N),\qquad M_{j+1}=\lfloor M_jM_j/Q\rfloor
\]

entrywise. Every stored matrix is nonnegative and Q-substochastic. Each product rounds away less than c/Q in each row l1 norm. The error recurrence gives `(2^j-1)c/Q` for the jth stored power, and at most cN/Q for a binary query with at most N Euler steps.

Uniformize the transient-plus-cemetery process at Lambda. On one 1/N interval, the TV distance between Bernoulli(Lambda/N) and Poisson(Lambda/N) event counts is at most `(Lambda/N)^2`. Couple N intervals, then convert TV to row l1. The true heat row and its integer approximation therefore differ by at most

\[
 \epsilon=2\Lambda^2/N+cN/Q. \tag{9.1}
\]

Signed cover projection preserves this bound. Set delta=k epsilon for equal precision. All Pfaffians, mass differences, clipping, certificates and sampler selectors thereafter use exact integers or rationals. Approximate determinants may have either sign; certificates use (5.1) before interpreting outputs.

To obtain delta<=eta, choose

\[
 N\ge\max(\Lambda,4k\Lambda^2/\eta),\qquad Q\ge2kcN/\eta,
\]

rounded upward to powers of two with p>=m. Thus m and p grow with rate bit length and `log(k/eta)`, not with the numerical event count N. For conditional count error alpha, take eta<=alpha s0; for complete endpoint error alpha, take eta<=alpha s0/D, reserving a separate fair-bit fallback budget. There is no uniform certified positive s0 for arbitrary inputs.

Let M(b) denote b-bit integer multiplication cost, Div(b) exact integer division cost, and `A(b)=M(b)+Div(b)+O(b)`. An O(b^2) bound for A is sufficient, and does not assume Python's division matches its fastest multiplication algorithm. With schoolbook dense matrix products, reusable one-particle power preprocessing costs `O(c^3 m A(p))` bit operations and `O(c^2 m p)` power-storage bits. A unit-time-only computation, as executed here, needs only the current square and `O(c^2 p)` bits. A general cached dyadic query for k selected rows costs `O(k c^2 m A(p))`; retrieving unit-time rows after squaring costs O(kc).

Scalar survival costs `O(nk^2 A(p)+k^3 A(O(kp)))` after receiving rows. Prefix suffix-block preprocessing costs `O(nk^2 A(p))`; each mass query costs `O(k^3 A(O(kp)))`, and a complete endpoint sample uses O(D) such queries. Suffix blocks occupy `O(nk^2 p)` bits. Intermediate skew-elimination entries are Pfaffians of selected submatrices, and have `O(kp)` bits (add logarithmic accumulation allowance if using a less structured implementation). Since every child event's signed mass has absolute value at most Q^k, every selector uses at most kp+1 bits per attempt; a conservative deterministic complete-sample bound is `A_attempt D(kp+1)` fair bits, distinguishing the attempt count A_attempt from arithmetic cost A(b).

For the one-bin count polynomial, fraction-free skew elimination uses O(k^3) polynomial operations. With naive degree-k polynomial multiplication/division this is `O(k^5 A(O(kp)))` bit operations and `O(k^4 p)` working bits, plus the O(nk^2) low-degree skew-block construction. The k+1 rational output coefficients alone can occupy O(k^2 p) bits. Stronger polynomial arithmetic is possible but not implemented or claimed.

The fraction-free Pfaffian update is

\[
 a'_{ij}=\frac{a_{h,h+1}a_{ij}-a_{h,i}a_{h+1,j}+a_{h,j}a_{h+1,i}}
                  {\text{previous pivot}}.
\]

Initial denominator is 1, with row/column pivot swaps if necessary. Skew block elimination over the fraction field proves the Pfaffian condensation identity behind this update: a determinant-one congruence makes a nonsingular skew prefix block diagonal with its Schur complement, then the four-index Pfaffian expansion gives the displayed relation. Its numerators divide exactly in the integer polynomial ring because the resulting entries are sub-Pfaffians. If the active pivot row is identically zero, the remaining Pfaffian is zero. Every division and polynomial remainder is checked exactly in code; recursive expansion and explicit minor enumeration serve only as separate small checks.

## 10. An original-interchange endpoint observable on every graph

Let `P_e` be the one-particle permutation matrix of a transposition e. The exact identity

\[
 I-\bigwedge^k P_e=d\Gamma_k(I-P_e) \tag{10.1}
\]

holds for every k: P_e has exactly one -1 eigendirection and is +1 on its complement. Both sides act as 2 on wedges containing that direction and as 0 on all others. This rank-one property is special; `I-wedge(P)` does not equal `dGamma(I-P)` for arbitrary permutations P.

The interchange generator in this representation is therefore dGamma_k(L), on **every** weighted graph. Taking its semigroup and reading the wedge matrix entries gives

\[
 \mathbb E[s_X(\sigma_t)\,1_{\sigma_t(X)=Y}]=\det K(t)_{X,Y}. \tag{10.2}
\]

Summing over Y gives an exact Pfaffian for `E s_X(sigma_t)`. Thus the endpoint parity distribution is

\[
 \Pr(s_X=-1)=(1-\operatorname{Pfaffian\ expression})/2. \tag{10.3}
\]

This is an observable of the original symmetric interchange generator, including branching graphs. Weighted columns give `E[s_X prod_{x in X}w_{sigma(x)}]`; the count polynomial gives signed bin-count coefficients `Pr(even,N_B=j)-Pr(odd,N_B=j)`. Those coefficients are **not** the unsigned count distribution. No hidden measurement of labels is supplied; their identities must be part of the stated output.

The receiving theorem gives parity TV error at most delta/2. For two rate arrays r,q on an arbitrary graph, their binary endpoint parity laws satisfy

\[
 \operatorname{TV}(\text{parity}_r,\text{parity}_q)
 \le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^k\|K_r(x_i,:)-K_q(x_i,:)\|_1. \tag{10.4}
\]

This is an executable observable-specific endpoint perturbation operation. Certified heat rows turn it into exact rational intervals; it does not require killed-pair positivity on a branch. It says nothing about full permutation, all-color or full tagged endpoint TV.

On a path, the ordinary parity expectation also equals collision survival by Sections 2 and 10. On an odd-k cycle the same is true. For even-k cycles, the antiperiodic construction corresponds to endpoint order sign multiplied by `(-1)^(number of selected-label wrap traversals)`. It is a trajectory-dependent twist, not ordinary endpoint parity. Along a path, or with this corrected cycle twist, the sign equals `(-1)^(number of swap-clock firings having two selected endpoints)`. An internal selected swap contributes -1; a single selected move does not. Conditional on the exclusion-occupancy path, averaging these signed internal clocks gives killing potential `2 sum internal r`, exactly H. This explains the positive collision interpretation and its precise distinction from ordinary interchange tagged motion.

## 11. Branching obstruction and the high-occupancy exception

Suppose a positive three-leaf star is present and `2<=k<=n-2`. Hold k-2 spectator sites outside its four vertices fixed and traverse

`(1,2)->(0,2)->(2,3)->(0,3)->(1,3)->(0,1)->(1,2)`.

This loop exchanges two labelled walkers without collision, so exterior reordering signs multiply to -1. Every physical edge is traversed once in each orientation. Any fixed reversible U(1) edge gauge contributes product 1, and any diagonal configuration-state gauge telescopes to 1. They cannot turn the exterior generator into the positive physical killed generator. This rules out that restricted one-particle gauge construction, not sparse pair methods, higher covers or all algorithms.

The occupancy qualifier is essential. For k=1 all graphs work. For k=n, H is a scalar. For k=n-1, use the signless one-particle Laplacian `Q=D+W`. Under the Hodge complement basis, `dGamma_(n-1)(Q)` is

\[
 (\operatorname{tr}Q)I-Q^T=2R I-D-W,
\]

where R is total edge rate. The diagonal at hole h is `2R-d_h` and off-diagonal is `-r_hz`, exactly the one-hole killed generator. This works on arbitrary graphs, even nonbipartite ones. A signed-edge double cover can enclose its one-particle kernel. However, the physical hole chain already has n states, so this exception supplies no asymptotic state compression.

Ordinary exterior kernels on a branching graph still represent the signed interchange observable in (10.2). The obstruction prohibits changing its interpretation to collision probability.
