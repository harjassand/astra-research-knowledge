## Null-Lagrangian gauge: exact limit

For a zero-boundary or periodic gradient perturbation \(X=D\varphi\), \(\mathbb E X=0\) and \(\mathbb E\det X=0\). For every constant background \(B\),

\[
 \det(B+X)-\det B=\operatorname{cof}B:X+\det X,
\]

so its mean is zero. Adding any linear combination of the ordinary null Lagrangians \(1\), \(A_{ij}\), and \(\det A\) to \(F\) leaves \(g_\mu(B)\), and hence \(\Delta_\mu\), unchanged for every \(B\). A constant determinant gauge cannot repair a negative profile or turn a correlated decomposition into a positive one.

The pointwise indefinite-Finsler representation \(D^2F(A)=P_A+\lambda_AJ\) does not supply such a gauge: \(\lambda_A\) generally varies with \(A\). Multiplying a null Lagrangian by a state-dependent coefficient is not a null Lagrangian; the derivative of that coefficient contributes to the spatial integral. A variable gauge must be shown to arise from an exact/closed differential form. The closed heat certificates for the recorded sharp slices do this for those slices only; their existence is not a universal selection theorem.
