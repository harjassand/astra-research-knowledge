## 2. A joint identity for arbitrary profile loops

Let F:R^(m x 2)->R be C^1 and rank-one convex. Work on a rectangle periodic in x and y, with x period L. Write E_y for normalized averaging in y. For a smooth periodic potential phi define

    P=(phi_x | 0),       Q=(0 | phi_y).

Both are rank-one matrices pointwise, although P+Q need not be rank one. For a rank-one increment Z put

    R_F(C,Z)=F(C+Z)-F(C)-DF(C)[Z] >= 0.

Fix an arbitrary constant affine matrix B. Direct subtraction gives

    F(B+P+Q)-F(B)
      = R_F(B+Q,P)+R_F(B,Q)
        +DF(B+Q)[P]+DF(B)[Q].                         (2.1)

The last term has zero y-average at each x.

View u(x)=phi(x,.) as a closed curve in the vector space of periodic target-valued profiles. Define a one-form on this profile space by

    alpha_(F,B)(u)[v]
       = E_y DF(B+(0|u_y))[(v|0)].                    (2.2)

Then

    L J_F(B,Dphi)
      = integral_0^L E_y [R_F(B+Q,P)+R_F(B,Q)] dx
        + integral_u alpha_(F,B),                    (2.3)

where J is the probability-normalized Jensen gap on the rectangle. Equation (2.3) is a global identity. It uses neither polar coordinates nor absence of zeros, and it imposes no affine-phase or frequency-separation condition.

The circulation is genuinely joint: alpha is evaluated at the complete profile u, not at one carrier at a time. It may have either sign. The positive remainders retain the full carrier interactions as well.

### Finite profile coordinates

For computation and explicit constructions choose arbitrary smooth periodic profiles psi_1,...,psi_d in R^m and write

    phi(x,y)=sum_j a_j(x) psi_j(y),

where a:[0,L]->R^d is a closed path. Then

    alpha_j(a)=E_y DF(B+(0|sum_i a_i psi_i'))[(psi_j|0)]. (2.4)

Every smooth periodic potential is approximable in W^(1,4) by such fields: first take a finite Fourier sum in y, then approximate the periodic coefficient curves in x by polygonal curves. This density statement does not impose any circulation cancellation on the approximants.

For a polynomial F of degree at most four, alpha_j is a polynomial in a of degree at most three. Thus the signed part of (2.3) depends only on the integrals

    integral_a a^beta da_j,      |beta|<=3.            (2.5)

These are a subset of finite-order polynomial path data, not the full path signature. Calling them polynomial circulation moments makes the exact scope clear.
