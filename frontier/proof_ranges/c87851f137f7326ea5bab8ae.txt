1. INTERFACE AND THE PROPOSED ALLOCATION

Let H_j carry irreducible spin j, d=2j+1, tau=Tr/d, and
  L_w=sum_i w_i J_i^2, V_w=j(j+1)sum_i w_i/3,
  k_j(w)=max_{sum_y p_y pi_y=I/d} sum_y p_y sum_i w_i <Ji>_{pi_y}^2.
All pi_y are pure; the ensemble and support are unrestricted.
Its exact convex-roof dual is
  k_j(w)=min_G tau(G),
  <psi|G|psi> >= sum_i w_i <psi|Ji|psi>^2 for every pure psi.       (A1)
For nonnegative weights every feasible G is PSD. A minimizer exists:
there is a strictly feasible scalar envelope; closed feasible sublevels
are bounded because G>=0 and trace is bounded. Convex-roof duality at
the interior reference I/d gives equality in (A1).

Time-reverse the Choi input so all registers carry the same spin-j
representation. Since Theta Ji Theta^-1=-Ji, the star is
  W_w=-sum_i w_i Ji_A (Ji_B+Ji_C).                              (A2)
The support objective is Theta invariant; an optimal envelope may be
averaged with its Theta transform. In this convention both L and G
are time-reversal even. Put K_w=L_w+G_w and consider
  D_alpha(w)=alpha K_A+(1-alpha)(K_B+K_C)/2-W_w.                 (A3)
Operator positivity would imply the correct tracial bound because the
three single marginals are I/d and the allocation fractions sum to one.
The source spin-one theorem proves (A3) with alpha=3/5 for its exact
optimizer. Positivity of (A3) is a stronger statement than the legal
joint-Choi/tracial-state support inequality. Failure of (A3) is NOT a
legal channel falsifier unless a legal tracial state also violates it.

At w=(1,1,1), exactly k=j^2. Every maximizing pure state is coherent:
|<J>|<=j, and equality makes the state the unique top eigenvector of
J along its mean-spin direction. Thus G_0=j^2 I is feasible and optimal.
It is the UNIQUE optimal dual: every coherent state is active, and
averaging its nonnegative envelope slack gives tau(G)-j^2=0; the
coherent symbol is injective on B(H_j), forcing G=j^2 I.

The isotropic star W_0 has largest eigenvalue j(2j+1)=V+k. Its equality
space E is BC spin 2j coupled with A spin j to total spin T=j. Let P_E
be its projection. Consequently D_alpha(1,1,1) vanishes on E for every
alpha, so positivity near isotropy imposes nontrivial compressed
first-order obligations.

2. ENVELOPE DIFFERENTIABILITY DERIVED FROM SATURATION

Let w_i(epsilon)=1+epsilon h_i, and L'=sum_i h_i Ji^2. The covariance
set of pure tracial ensembles is compact. At isotropy every maximizing
ensemble uses coherent states. For j>=1 the tracial barycenter fixes
their second directional moment to I3/3: averaging
  <n|Ji Jj+Jj Ji|n>/2
   =j(j-1/2)n_i n_j+(j/2)delta_ij
against such an ensemble gives tau((JiJj+JjJi)/2)
=j(j+1)delta_ij/3. Therefore EVERY isotropic support optimizer has spin
covariance j^2 I3/3. The directional derivative of the compact support
function is unique, so
  k_j(1+epsilon h)=j^2+epsilon (j^2/3)sum_i h_i+o(epsilon).       (A4)
This is a consequence of the optimizer face, not a smoothness assumption.

The only operator whose coherent symbol is j^2 sum_i h_i n_i^2 is
  G'= (2j/(2j-1))L' - (j^2/(2j-1))sum_i h_i I.                 (A5)
Indeed the displayed coherent second moment proves this equality, and
injectivity makes the operator unique.

For ANY optimal G_epsilon in (A1), define
  H_epsilon=G_epsilon-j^2 I-epsilon G'.
Its coherent symbol is pointwise nonnegative, because the envelope must
dominate the coherent-state score. Its averaged symbol equals
  tau(H_epsilon)=k_j(w)-j^2-epsilon(j^2/3)sum_i h_i=o(epsilon).
The coherent canonical map Psi_j has positive multipole eigenvalues
  c_l=(2j)!(2j+1)!/[(2j-l)!(2j+l+1)!], l=0,...,2j.
Thus Psi_j(H_epsilon)>=0 and, writing c_min=c_{2j},
  ||H_epsilon||_op <= ||H_epsilon||_HS
      <= c_min^-1 ||Psi_j(H_epsilon)||_HS
      <= (d/c_min) tau(H_epsilon)=o(epsilon).                  (A6)
HS here is the ordinary Frobenius norm. This explicitly justifies
  G_epsilon=j^2 I+epsilon G'+o(epsilon)
for every optimal envelope, without importing global optimality of any
guessed formula. The first-order K envelope is consequently
  K'=(4j-1)/(2j-1)L'-(j^2/(2j-1))sum_i h_i I.                 (A7)
The argument excludes j=1/2, whose operator space has no rank-two
harmonic and whose covariance behavior is different; it gives no
allocation constraint there.

3. EXACT EQUALITY-SPACE COMPRESSION AND FORCED ALLOCATION

Set n=2j (integer n>=2), and first take h=(-1/2,-1/2,1).
The highest total-spin vector e=|T=j,M=j; BC spin n> has expansion
  e=sum_{r=0}^n (-1)^r sqrt[p_r]
       |j,r-j>_A tensor |n,n-r>_BC,
  p_r=binom(2n-r,n)/binom(2n+1,n+1).                           (A8)
The phase is irrelevant globally. This follows directly from the
Schwinger-boson polynomial (a_up b_down-a_down b_up)^n b_up^n.
The binomial hockey-stick identity yields
  E r=n/(n+2), E[r(r-1)]=2n(n-1)/[(n+2)(n+3)].
Hence exact second moments are
  R_A=<JA_z^2>=n(n^3+n^2+6n+4)/[4(n+2)(n+3)],
  R_S=< (JB_z+JC_z)^2>=n(n+1)^3/[(n+2)(n+3)],
  R_B=R_C=n(2n^4+5n^3+5n^2+2n-2)
                  /[4(n+2)(n+3)(2n-1)].
The last formula also follows from hypergeometric splitting of the
maximal BC spin n into two spin n/2 factors:
  <JB_z^2|S_z=m>=[(2n-2)m^2+n^2]/[4(2n-1)].

For this h, exactly
  ell_A=<e|L'_A|e>=n^2(n-1)^2/[4(n+2)(n+3)],
  ell_B=<e|L'_B|e>
      =n(n-1)^2(n+1)(2n+3)/[4(n+2)(n+3)(2n-1)],
  omega=<e|W'|e>=n^2(n-1)(n+1)/[2(n+2)(n+3)].                 (A9)
The target equality-space representation is itself spin j. Every
traceless quadratic perturbation has one rank-two component. Rotating
the axis and using this multiplicity-free component gives, for general
trace-zero h,
  P_E L'_A P_E = c_A L'_{tot},
  P_E L'_B P_E = P_E L'_C P_E = c_B L'_{tot},
  P_E W' P_E = c_W L'_{tot},
where L'_{tot}=sum_i h_i J_{tot,i}^2 acts on E and
  c_A=n(n-1)/[(n+2)(n+3)],
  c_B=(n-1)(n+1)(2n+3)/[(n+2)(n+3)(2n-1)],
  c_W=2n(n+1)/[(n+2)(n+3)].                                  (A10)

Substituting (A7) into (A3) gives the exact compression
  P_E D'_alpha P_E
    = -3(2n+1)/[(n+2)(n+3)]
        [alpha-(n+1)/(2n+1)] L'_{tot}.                        (A11)
For nonzero trace-zero h, L'_{tot} is a nonzero traceless Hermitian
operator when j>=1, hence has both positive and negative eigenvalues.
An operator inequality surviving arbitrary sufficiently small
anisotropy must therefore have
  alpha=alpha_j=(n+1)/(2n+1)=(2j+1)/(4j+1).                   (A12)
This necessity already holds for a single sign of the small perturbation:
any nonzero compressed rank-two coefficient is indefinite. With unequal
output allocations beta,gamma and alpha+beta+gamma=1, the equality space
has equal B,C compressions, so the same reference fraction is forced;
only their sum enters. Output-swap averaging permits beta=gamma.

The fixed source fraction 3/5 has highest-vector tangent deficit
  <e|D'_{3/5}|e>
     =-3n(n-1)(n-2)/[20(n+2)(n+3)].                          (A13)
It is negative for every j>1. At j=3/2 it is exactly -3/100.
For general j, alpha_j tends to 1/2 as j grows. Its dependence on j
changes the proof allocation but does not change the desired factor two.

Allowing separate reference fractions a for L and b for G does not
give universal spin-independent constants either. The compressed
condition is
  (n-1)a+n b=(2n-1)(n+1)/(2n+1)=n-1/(2n+1).                  (A14)
No fixed a,b satisfy this for all integer n>=2: the left side is affine
in n, whereas the right side is not. For one j this is a necessary line
of allocations; a=b=alpha_j is a natural point on it, not a sufficiency
theorem.

4. EXPLICIT RATIONAL NEAR-ISOTROPIC FAILURE AT j=3/2

h=(-1/2,-1/2,1). At j=3/2, G'=3L'/2 has eigenvalues +/-9/4.
For a pure state with r=|<J>| choose its mean-spin direction n and

  1-|<psi|n>|^2 <= j-r.
Put D=j^2-r^2. Then
  |<G'>-j^2 h(n)|<=2||G'|| sqrt(j-r),
  |j^2 h(n)-r^2 h(n)|<=D ||h||,

  G_bar=j^2 I+epsilon G'+(2||G'||^2/j)epsilon^2 I
       =j^2 I+epsilon G'+(27/4)epsilon^2 I
is a feasible envelope for EVERY pure state. Therefore
  0<=k_j(w)-j^2<=27epsilon^2/4.
Since d=4 and c_min=c_3=1/35, the exact inverse-symbol estimate gives
  ||G_opt-j^2 I-epsilon G'||_op<=945epsilon^2                 (A15)
for EVERY optimal envelope. This includes any time-reversal-averaged

Take the exact rational weights

For j>=1 the EXACT scalar allocation interval that passes this entire
single-observable operator control is
  (j-1)/(2j-1)<=alpha<=j/(2j-1).                            (A19)
To prove sufficiency and necessity, put a=m_A and
delta_b=m_B+a, delta_c=m_C+a, which are integers. The deficit is
  D=(2alpha-1)a(delta_b+delta_c)
                      +(1-alpha)(delta_b^2+delta_c^2).
Each output contribution separates. For alpha>=1/2 the only negative
linear direction has delta opposite to a; a unit step is possible only
when |a|<=j-1, yielding alpha<=j/(2j-1). For alpha<=1/2 the negative
direction is the same sign as a, with maximal |a|=j, yielding
alpha>=(j-1)/(2j-1). Larger integer steps only increase the quadratic
cost. Boundary one-step product vectors attain these limits, proving
necessity. At j=1 this is the full interval [0,1]; at j=1/2 the direct

The necessary alpha_j lies strictly inside (A19) for all j>=1:
