Result: a polynomial-time route to the full finite-ring transport response
I derived a randomized classical algorithm that approximates the full finite-ring transport coefficient to arbitrary additive accuracy, with polynomial total resources at fixed positive dephasing and fixed local interaction bounds.

The interacting coherence correction is included—not truncated at a prescribed Pauli degree, replaced by a local detuning statistic, or evaluated only to leading order in strong dephasing.

The central operation is to insert complete occupation measurements that make the simulated trajectories restartable, then remove those measurements through a rigorously controlled extrapolation. An exact constrained representation proves that this extrapolation has a domain of analyticity independent of chain length.

Two qualifications are essential. The result is a theoretical finite-volume approximation theorem, not yet a practical solver or a high-precision theorem for the infinite chain. The derivation has not received independent proof review, and historical novelty of the combined algorithm remains unverified.
