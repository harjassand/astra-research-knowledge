## 2. Why the complete block operation is LOCC

Write a product-Kraus decomposition
\[
\mathcal N(X)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{r}
(A_j\otimes B_j)X(A_j\otimes B_j)^\dagger.
\tag{7}
\]

Discard zero terms, and put
\[
a_j=\|A_j\|_F,\qquad
b_j=\|B_j\|_F,\qquad
\lambda_j=a_j^2b_j^2.
\]

Trace preservation gives
\[
\sum_j A_j^\dagger A_j\otimes B_j^\dagger B_j=I_{AB},
\]
so
\[
\sum_j\lambda_j=D.
\tag{8}
\]

Alice and Bob sample a shared index \(j\) with probability \(\lambda_j/D\). They apply the local success filters
\[
A_j/a_j,\qquad B_j/b_j.
\]
These are valid contractions because the operator norm is bounded by the Frobenius norm.

If both filters succeed, they announce flag \(1\). Otherwise, they discard their systems, prepare the fixed product output of \(\mathcal R\), and announce flag \(0\).

The success subchannel is exactly
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_j\frac{\lambda_j}{D}
\left(\frac{A_j}{a_j}\otimes\frac{B_j}{b_j}\right)
X
\left(\frac{A_j}{a_j}\otimes\frac{B_j}{b_j}\right)^\dagger
&=\frac1D\mathcal N(X).
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
\]

Its success probability is \(1/D\) for every input. After resetting on failure, the failure subchannel is
\[
\left(1-\frac1D\right)\mathcal R.
\]
This proves (4).

The general observation that separable operations can be realized through stochastic LOCC, and its connection with normalization and causal order, has substantial antecedents. The construction is not being presented as a newly discovered equivalence between separability and stochastic implementability. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08835?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Why announcing the flag earlier changes the task

Suppose a causal LOCC implementation issued flag \(1\) before receiving the input.

Conditioned on that flag, all its shared memory would still be separable: it began without entanglement and has used only LOCC. Its subsequent action on the fresh quantum input would therefore be an LOCC channel.

But the required subsequent channel is \(\mathcal N\), which is outside \(\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\). This is impossible.

The obstruction is also robust. If a sequence of causal LOCC implementations approximated the early-flag process, their probabilities \(q_j\) of flag \(1\) would converge to \(p>0\), and their conditional channels \(\mathcal L_j\) would satisfy
\[
q_j\mathcal L_j\longrightarrow p\mathcal N.
\]
Because the input arrives after the flag, each \(\mathcal L_j\) is a trace-preserving LOCC channel. Dividing by \(q_j\) then gives
\[
\mathcal L_j\longrightarrow\mathcal N,
\]
contradicting \(\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\).

**The same division is not valid operationally for a flag obtained after processing the input.** In that case the normalized successful branch can be separable without being LOCC.
