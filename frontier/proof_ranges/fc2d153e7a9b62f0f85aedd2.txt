## 5. Why exact marginals follow

Fix \(A,x\), and let
\[
\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}
=
\left\{
y\in\mathcal V:
\|A(y-x)\|_\infty\le4096k^3+48k
\right\}.
\]

The objective-aware result says
\[
\forall c\in\mathbb R^n,\quad
\exists y\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}
\text{ with }c^\top y\le c^\top x.
\]

Suppose
\[
x\notin\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}).
\]
Strict separation would give a \(c\) satisfying
\[
c^\top x
<
\min_{y\in\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}}c^\top y,
\]
contradicting the objective-aware result.

Thus
\[
x\in\operatorname{conv}(\mathcal V_{\mathrm{good}}).
\]

A convex decomposition of \(x\) supplies the required distribution. Every support point is feasible and has bounded error, while its expectation is exactly \(x\). At most \(n+1\) support points are needed.

This avoids trying to preserve exact marginals through every intermediate analytic operation.

## 6. Why bounded exchanges are necessary

The converse is independent of the spectral machinery.

Take an edge \([u,v]\) changing \(k\) coordinates, and let
\[
x=\frac{u+v}{2}.
\]

Any distribution on vertices with expectation \(x\) must be supported on this edge. To see this, take a linear functional exposing the edge. Its value at \(x\) is maximal over the polytope. An average of vertex values can equal that maximum only if every positive-probability vertex also maximizes the functional.

The only vertices on the edge are \(u,v\), so the distribution must choose each with probability \(1/2\).

Now use the one-row matrix
\[
a_j=v_j-u_j.
\]
Its columns have absolute value at most one, and
\[
a^\top(u-x)=-\frac{k}{2},
\qquad
a^\top(v-x)=\frac{k}{2}.
\]

Therefore
\[
R(P)\ge\frac{k}{2}.
\]

Combining this with the upper bound proves the proposed uniform classification.

The unbiasedness requirement matters here: it forces rounding to respect the exposed face containing the fractional point.
