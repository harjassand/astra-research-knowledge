4. The joint error–acquisition theorem
Suppose a certified ratio envelope is available on the positive-reference-work support:
\[
0<a\le r_e\le b,
\qquad R=b/a.
\]
No such envelope is required on zero-reference-work edges.
The local responses then obey
\[
a\le u_K\le b,
\qquad
b^{-1}\le v_K\le a^{-1}.
\]
Define
\[
A=\mathbb E_wu,
\qquad B=\mathbb E_wv,
\qquad \Delta=AB-1.
\]
The global deterministic enclosure has endpoint ratio \(1+\Delta\). I derived
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_w(u)}{A^2}\le R\Delta,
\qquad
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_w(v)}{B^2}\le R\Delta.
}
\]
Thus, when the acquired local representation gives a tight physical-response enclosure, the quantities needed to estimate that enclosure also have controlled relative variance.
Proof of the variance connection
For any positive random variable \(X\) with mean \(m\),
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{(X-m)^2}{X}\right]
=m\left(m\mathbb E[X^{-1}]-1\right).
\]
If \(X\le b\), this implies
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{m^2}
\le
\frac bm\left(m\mathbb E[X^{-1}]-1\right).
\]
Apply this to \(u\). Because \(uv\ge1\),
\[
A\mathbb E[u^{-1}]-1\le AB-1=\Delta,
\]
and \(b/A\le R\). The reciprocal argument proves the bound for \(v\).
No spatial independence or stationarity assumption is needed. The target coefficients can be arbitrary and fixed; the randomness comes from the acquisition algorithm.
Separating two kinds of unresolved error
Define the probability distribution
\[
q_K=\frac{w_Ku_K}{A}.
\]
The discrepancy decomposes exactly as
\[
\boxed{
\Delta=
\chi^2(w\Vert q)
+
A\mathbb E_w\!\left[v-\frac1u\right].
}
\]
Both terms are nonnegative.
The first measures redistribution of response work between cells. The second measures incompatibility between a cell’s prescribed reference potential and prescribed reference current.
This identifies two different reasons a certificate can remain wide. Increasing the sample count addresses neither intrinsic defect. Changing the reference or enlarging the locally acquired response is necessary.
Sampling complexity and its qualification
The implementation uses an established empirical Bernstein confidence bound, with a failure budget distributed across successive stopping stages. The concentration inequality and variance-adaptive stopping are prior art. arXiv
Combining those bounds with the variance theorem gives, up to logarithmic factors, an acquisition count of the form
\[
\widetilde O\!\left(
\frac{R\Delta}{\varepsilon^2}
+
\frac{R-1}{\varepsilon}
\right).
\]
This does not mean arbitrary \(\Delta\) permits an \(\varepsilon\)-accurate answer. The intrinsic discrepancy must already lie below the accuracy budget, with room for statistical error.
For example, when \(\Delta\le\varepsilon/4\) and \(R\) is controlled, the count becomes approximately
\[
\widetilde O(R/\varepsilon)
\]
rather than a range-only \(\varepsilon^{-2}\) count.
Those are sampled cells. A sampled cell containing thousands of coefficients is not a unit-cost microscopic query.
