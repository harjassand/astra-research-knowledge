2. The mathematical distinction that makes the test possible
An isotope measurement can count molecules with exactly one heavy atom, rather than count all heavy atoms. Those quantities need not determine one another.
Consider the two native backbone carbons. Let \(X_1,X_2\) indicate whether each is \(^{13}\mathrm C\), and define
\[
p=\frac{\mathbb E(X_1+X_2)}2,
\qquad
c=\Pr(X_1=X_2=1).
\]
The probabilities of zero, one and two heavy carbon atoms are exactly
\[
P_0=1-2p+c,\qquad
P_1=2(p-c),\qquad
P_2=c.
\]
Define the carbon-only ratios
\[
r=\frac{P_1}{2P_0},
\qquad
t=\frac{P_2}{P_0}.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
p=\frac{r+t}{1+2r+t},
\qquad
c=\frac{t}{1+2r+t}.
}
\tag{1}
\]
This inversion does not require independent isotope placement or identical abundances at the two positions.
The consequence is precise: the singly substituted ratio \(r\), by itself, does not determine the mean heavy-carbon abundance \(p\). The doubly substituted ratio \(t\) supplies the missing information in this two-carbon model.
For fixed \(p\) and \(r\),
\[
\boxed{
t=\frac{p(1+2r)-r}{1-p}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{p}{1-p}-r
=
\frac{c-p^2}{(1-p)(1-2p+c)}.
\tag{3}
\]
Thus a low singly substituted ratio at fixed mean abundance requires positive correlation between the two heavy-carbon indicators.
This is not reproduced merely by making two independent positions unequal. If their probabilities are \(p+h\) and \(p-h\), direct substitution gives
\[
r-\frac{p}{1-p}
=
\frac{h^2}{(1-p)[(1-p)^2-h^2]}
\geq0.
\]
That change goes in the opposite direction. The published positional interpretation instead assigns a lower mean to the backbone and compensates with an enriched carboxyl carbon.
The crucial experimental qualification
These equations concern native-carbon probabilities after the relevant instrumental and chemical corrections. I did not independently reconstruct those probabilities from the original spectra.
For the numerical comparison, I used the explicit working interface
\[
r=R_{\rm VPDB}\left(1+\frac{\delta_{\rm backbone}}{1000}\right),
\qquad R_{\rm VPDB}=0.011100,
\]
and converted the whole-molecule isotope value to an atom fraction. The reference convention comes from the supplement, but applying its corrected isotope value to this probability model remains an unverified interface. Derivatizing carbons, fragmentation response and correlations with other isotope systems require separate treatment. NASA Technical Reports Server
The predictions below are therefore conditional benchmarks, not a calibrated reinterpretation of the experiment.
3. Two explanations predict substantially different additional signals
I constructed two complete isotope distributions.
In the first, the backbone positions are independent and relatively \(^{13}\mathrm C\)-poor, with a \(^{13}\mathrm C\)-rich carboxyl position maintaining the observed whole-molecule average. Its backbone double-substitution ratio is
\[
t_{\rm positional}=r^2.
\]
In the second, all three native carbon positions have the same average heavy-carbon abundance. The two backbone positions are positively correlated, with their pair probability fixed by equation (2).
These distributions match the same specified whole-molecule average and singly substituted backbone ratio, but predict:
Predicted—not observed—native two-carbon quantity	Bennu benchmark	Murchison benchmark
\(P_2/P_0\), independent-backbone model	\(0.00008632\)	\(0.00011261\)
\(P_2/P_0\), equal-marginal correlated model	\(0.00204686\)	\(0.00069593\)
Ratio between those predictions	23.71	6.18


A probability distribution realizing the correlated benchmark can be written explicitly. Mix independent three-carbon molecules with heavy-carbon probability \(p_{\rm low}=r/(1+r)\), together with a fraction
\[
\lambda=\frac{p_{\rm bulk}-p_{\rm low}}{1-p_{\rm low}}
\]
of molecules carrying three heavy carbons. For Bennu’s benchmark, \(\lambda\approx0.001921\).
This proves mathematical feasibility. It does not identify an actual source, contaminant or reaction. The three-heavy-carbon construction is also not unique; it is only one distribution consistent with the specified pair constraints.
The calculation nevertheless turns an ambiguous explanation into a quantitative discriminator: the equal-marginal carbon-correlation hypothesis has to account for a much larger native doubly substituted population.
4. Testing a physical origin for that correlation
I investigated a particular source-mixture mechanism: molecules form within reservoirs having different heavy-carbon abundances, with carbon atoms sampled independently inside each reservoir. Molecules subsequently mix without exchanging their atoms.
Let \(P\) be the heavy-carbon atom fraction of the reservoir from which a molecule formed. Then
\[
p=\mathbb E[P],\qquad
c=\mathbb E[P^2],
\]
so
\[
\boxed{c-p^2=\operatorname{Var}(P).}
\tag{4}
\]
This provides a direct physical requirement, rather than an adjustable correlation parameter.
How extreme must a source be?
If every reservoir satisfies \(P\leq B\), then \(P^2\leq BP\), hence
\[
\boxed{B\geq\frac{c}{p}.}
\tag{5}
\]
The benchmark requires a reservoir reaching at least 18.05% \(^{13}\mathrm C\) for Bennu and 6.155% for Murchison. These are conditional source requirements—not measured reservoir compositions.
A sharper result applies when sources lie in a specified interval \(a\leq P\leq b\). Since
\[
(P-a)(b-P)\geq0,
\]
taking expectations yields
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}(P)\leq(p-a)(b-p).
}
\tag{6}
\]
The bound is sharp: mixtures of the two endpoints attain it.
Comparison with independently measured organic material
A separate Bennu study reports organic-material isotope compositions spanning \(-326\) to \(+364\,‰\) in its comparison with aggregate material. I used that range as a defined source class, not as a bound on every carbon-bearing phase or historical precursor. Nature
At Bennu’s benchmark whole-molecule abundance, equation (6) permits covariance of at most
\[
1.40165\times10^{-5}.
\]
The correlated benchmark needs
\[
1.88210\times10^{-3},
\]
which is 134.28 times larger.
Equivalently, source mixing confined to that interval can depress the apparent backbone value by at most 1.306‰, not the benchmark’s 175‰.
That bounded source-mixture explanation fails.
The restriction cannot legitimately be extended to every Bennu source. The observations concern other aliquots and finite-resolution regions. Rare presolar phases have much more extreme isotope compositions; their existence does not demonstrate that they supplied these amino acids or preserved the required atom-pair correlations. Nature
I therefore did not rescue the model by inserting an unmeasured extreme precursor and calling it an explanation.
