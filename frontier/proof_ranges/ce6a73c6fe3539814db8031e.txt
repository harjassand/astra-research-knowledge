The output sought is repeated restoration of a programmed nanoscale frame after unknown damage using common repair reagents, without imaging each defect or supplying a library of sequence-specific replacement staples. This would compound manufacturing effort if it preserved arbitrary programmed topology. The existing strand-exchange self-healing comparator already treats unknown staple damage; designed polymerase gap filling is also demonstrated. Both are credited, not assumed absent.

Root's redesign separates persistent routing information from sacrificial duplex runs. Retain covalent junctions encoding the desired strand connectivity. Between junctions, arrange complementary continuous templates so a short gap in either backbone has an intact opposing run, an eligible endogenous 3-prime hydroxyl, a suitable downstream end, and no ambiguous template branch. Supply appropriate polymerase, ligase, nucleotide substrate, cofactors, and end processing if needed. Repair would use the surviving template sequence and preserved route. It must halt at the correct boundary rather than displace intact neighboring strands. This is a proposed engineering arrangement of known DNA repair chemistry; novelty and operation on an origami frame are UNKNOWN.

The first important correction is that sequence complementarity alone does not encode arbitrary origami connectivity. At a deleted routing junction with two incoming and two outgoing surviving arms, the pairings AB/CD and AD/CB can share every surviving sequence and arm position. A common bath sees the same damaged input in both target worlds. Any randomized repair kernel has identical output law, and averaged exact-target success is at most one half. For k erased independent routing bits, exact success averaged over the equally likely targets is at most 2^(-k). Repeated enzyme cycles cannot restore erased information. This is a conditional information argument about identical complete inputs; it is not an assertion that actual DNA damage always erases routing or that sequence-specific replacement strands cannot repair it.

A sufficient abstract condition for unique repair is that each damaged interval has an eligible primer and a unique, continuous, complementary template walk between retained routing vertices, with intact endpoints identifying the intended join. Local extension and ligation then have a uniquely specified *target*. This graph condition does not establish enzyme accessibility, fidelity, end chemistry, a ligase's acceptance of the nick, mechanical retention, or its rate. A crossover generally breaks a naive continuous-template argument. Persistent junction protection is an additional acquired and manufactured resource, with its own lifetime and damage cost.

For a single reciprocal-template patch, a minimal independent-damage model has states 0 (both copies intact), 1 (one damaged but repairable), and F (both lost). Let each copy fail at rate lambda, and a singly damaged patch repair at rate mu. Then 0 -> 1 at 2 lambda, 1 -> 0 at mu, and 1 -> F at lambda. Solving the two first-step equations gives the exact mean absorption time from 0:

    E[T_F] = (mu + 3 lambda)/(2 lambda^2).

Without repair it is 3/(2 lambda); for rapid repair it scales as mu/(2 lambda^2). This is a standard reliability benefit of redundancy and repair, not a new abstract operation. For N independent patches, survival is S_patch(T)^N, not exp(-T/E[T_F]) except as a limiting approximation. Every routing vertex adds its own survival factor. The code uses the exact 2-by-2 generator to avoid treating the mean lifetime as an exponential law.

The real tests are whether this design restores scaffold as well as staples after blinded random eligible gaps, retains target geometry and function, and beats optimized replacement-staple repair or protection alone at matched total manufacture, reagent, energy, time and fidelity. Include polymerase-only, ligase-only, nonmatching DNA, undamaged-enzyme, and route-erasure controls. Natural or designed damage with blocked ends and gaps across junctions must remain in the denominator. A surviving fluorescence signal is insufficient as the sole topology endpoint. No such test was performed here.
