## Explicit preparation counterexample

This is a review qualification of a substitution already disclaimed by the
source, not an additional claim of scientific novelty. Use beta=1 and four
classical states in order (label0,b0),(label1,b0),(label0,b1),(label1,b1), with

    H=diag(0,0,-10,10),   V=diag(1/5,-4/5,1/5,-4/5).

The label-changing T is exactly zero, so a correct equilibrium histogram would
give exact reweighting. A trapped preparation uniformly occupying the first
two states instead reports p=(1/2,1/2), remains stationary under H, and gives

    Delta_wrong=1/5-log[(1+e)/2]<0.

The genuine global Gibbs probability of label1 is

    p1=(1+exp(-10))/(2+exp(10)+exp(-10))<1/512.

Consequently its true design change is

    Delta_true=1/5-log[1+p1(e-1)]>1/5-1/256>0.

The strict bounds use e>2, e<3, exp(1/5)<5/4, and log(1+x)<x. Numerically the
false estimate is -0.4201145 and the true change is +0.1999220. A local label
histogram can therefore look stationary and have no label coherence while
missing the free-energy-dominating branch. A calibrated equilibrium discrepancy
or preparation/mixing guarantee remains substantive acquired information.
Small beta*kappa alone does not provide it.
