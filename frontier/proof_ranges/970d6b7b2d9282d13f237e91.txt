## Outcome

The strongest result from this investigation is a **closure-preserving uniformization theorem**: an increasing probability distribution on a union-closed family can be represented, to arbitrarily small local error, by the uniform distribution on a larger, genuinely union-closed family. The construction has a polynomial-time randomized realization for explicitly given rational probabilities.

This yields an exact consequence:

> **Uniform and increasing-weighted union-closed families have the same optimal universal element-frequency bound.**

It also identifies exactly which distributions resist every entropy-increasing self-coupling.

**I did not obtain the decisive half-frequency theorem, a better universal frequency constant, or an independently major application of the construction. External novelty is not established either.** What follows is a proved intermediate capability, an exact failed continuation, and the remaining mathematical obstruction—not a claim that the mission is complete.

## 1. Selected obstruction

The initial candidates were linear-size sample compression, dimension-free concentration for log-concave measures, and union-closed abundance. I selected the third because it exposes a particularly sharp mismatch between a simple algebraic operation and a difficult global consequence.

The target is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F\subseteq 2^{[n]}\text{ finite and union-closed},\quad
\mathcal F\ne\{\varnothing\}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\max_i\frac{|\{A\in\mathcal F:i\in A\}|}{|\mathcal F|}
\ge\frac12.
}
\tag{UC}
\]

Here the family is nonempty. This is Frankl’s union-closed sets conjecture. Entropy growth under union has already produced constant lower bounds. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09055)

The independent-copy entropy method has a sharp threshold at

\[
\frac{3-\sqrt5}{2}\approx0.381966,
\]

and dependent coupling methods already go beyond it. Thus merely proposing correlated copies would not constitute a new mechanism. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08824) Recent work also explicitly constructs global couplings, rather than only coordinatewise protocols. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08291)

The question I pursued was more structural:

**Can one remove the uniform-distribution restriction without losing the actual union operation, distinctness of sets, or coordinate frequencies?**

An increasing-weighted version had already been proposed in the Polymath discussion. Its condition is

\[
p(A)\le p(B)\qquad(A\subseteq B,\ A,B\in\mathcal F).
\tag{1}
\]

The weighted formulation itself is therefore prior art, not a discovery here. [Gowers's Weblog](https://gowers.wordpress.com/2016/02/13/func3-further-strengthenings-and-variants/)

The construction below shows how to move back from that weighted formulation to actual uniform families.
