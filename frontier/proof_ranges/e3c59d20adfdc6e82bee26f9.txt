## 9. Interventions that cross the proved boundary

### 9.1 One additional far-end preparation

Suppose the experimenter may also prepare |+> at L_d with vacuum elsewhere and read that qubit. The two hypotheses have an onsite difference a_delta>=1/2 there. In the one-excitation sector ||C_s||<=2. Taylor expansion with its unitary remainder gives

    exp(-itC_s)=I-itC_s+R_s(t),     ||R_s(t)||<=2t^2.

Therefore the imaginary parts of the two endpoint return amplitudes differ by at least

    a_delta t-4t^2 >=t/4,      0<t<=1/16.                             (27)

After undoing K, this is a difference in a one-qubit Y expectation. With t_0=min(tau,1/32), O(t_0^(-2) log(1/alpha)) shots identify the supplied binary alternative. There is no dependence on d or T at fixed t_0 and confidence, and no microscopic reconstruction is required. For the dyadic variant the endpoint difference remains bounded below by an absolute constant, with the same order and an adjusted constant.

This is an explicit escape for this family, not a theorem that one far-end probe identifies every unknown Hamiltonian.

### 9.2 Waiting for the requested prediction time

Allowing one coherent experiment of duration t_star=pi/delta also defeats the explicit pair: the same initial product state and one-qubit rotating-frame X readout give a constant signal separation. This uses native time of order T. It is testing the system at the forecast time, not certifying that forecast from short experiments.

### 9.3 Systematic errors in the simple positive certificate

If calibration and readout errors can change each ideal rejection probability by at most beta, an upper confidence bound p_U on the observed rejection rate gives

    E_T <=(2T/h) sqrt(p_U+beta).

To certify error epsilon by this route requires beta=O(h^2 epsilon^2/T^2). Long-horizon model tolerance is not the same as tolerance to SPAM error. The lower bound already holds for ideal, noiseless experiments; hardware error cannot be its explanation.
