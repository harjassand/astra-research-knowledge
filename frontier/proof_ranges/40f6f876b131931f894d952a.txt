0. PRECISE RESULT THAT THE CONSTRUCTION SUPPORTS

On the circle T_P, P=2.002, use the physical halfspace multiplier

  K exp(2 pi i n x/P)=kappa |2 pi n/P| exp(2 pi i n x/P), kappa>0.

Let U=(1-x^2)_+^(3/2), periodically extended, and let 0<epsilon<=1. Define
W_*=epsilon U, b=B=1.51 kappa, a=c by v2 (1.2),(1.3). In particular,
c is positive periodic C^(1,1/2), c_min>=.01 epsilon kappa, c_max<4.6 epsilon
kappa. The gap arch is part of this exact material construction. The operator
H=K-B has full-domain form h and constrained coercivity

  h[v]>=.01 kappa ||v||_2^2 when integral(c v)=0.

Define C=integral(c W_*)=-h[W_*]>0, lambda_*=1/C, q_*=W_*/C, and
g=Hq_*+c lambda_*>=0 with g q_*=0. These are exact identities, not profile
fits. Given positive periodic physical data V0,theta0 in C^(1+alpha),
0<alpha<1/2, define without a future blow-up time

  rho0=integral(c V0), q0=V0/rho0, y0=1/(rho0 theta0),
  F0=integral c[q_* log(q_*/q0)+q0-q_*]+integral B y0.

Set the integrand q_* log(q_*/q0) to zero where q_*=0. Assume F0<ell, where
ell obeys v2 (4.1) if epsilon<1, or v2 (5.3) if epsilon=1. These conditions
define a nonempty open subset of physical C^(1+alpha) data and admit positive
slip outside the equilibrium active patch and finite positive state everywhere.

The full aging-law, zero external loading, fixed-normal-stress equations

  V_t/V=-K V/c+(B/c)(V-1/theta), theta_t=1-V theta

then have a unique solution for t0<=t<T, where T is finite and determined by
the data. At every t<T, V and theta are strictly positive; for positive elapsed
time the solution is classical. Its continuum similarity limit satisfies

  (T-t)V -> W_* in H^(1/2)(T_P) and in every finite Lp(T_P).

The aging exponent satisfies log(theta(x,t))/log(T-t)->W_*(x) for almost every
x. The exact time-origin gauge is determined by the initial amplitude clock,
not prescribed by a codimension-one similarity initial-data condition.

For epsilon<1 the time interval obeys

  1/(rho0 lambda_*) <= T-t0 <= exp(D)/(rho0 lambda_*),
  D=2 F0[3+Q/(lambda_*-Q)+B_max/sqrt(B_min eta)], Q=||q_*||_infty.

For epsilon=1 the time is finite, lambda(tau)->lambda_*, and
(T-t)rho->1/lambda_*. No explicit upper time bound is inferred from a finite
integral of lambda_- alone. Both cases allow the gapless elastic tip mobility.
The critical case allows the pointwise memory gap to close at x=0. This is a
limiting free-support selection theorem on the full periodic continuum; exact
finite-time zero fronts are invariant under the positive original model and
are not asserted to move. Thresholded active sets converge in measure when
the target threshold level set has measure zero. Hausdorff edge convergence
and nonperiodic locked outer-boundary continuation remain outside this theorem.

1. FIXED LOCAL EXISTENCE SPACES AND TRACE

All spaces below are on the circle of fixed length P. Let p=4 and

  E=B^(3/4)_(2,4),
  X0=L2 x E, X1=H1 x E,
  MR4(0,S)=W^(1,4)((0,S);X0) intersect L4((0,S);X1).

The y component is an ODE in E. X1 is densely and continuously embedded in X0.
The real interpolation trace is exactly

  (X0,X1)_(3/4,4)=E x E,
  MR4(0,S) embeds C([0,S];E x E).

E embeds H^(1/2) and C^gamma for every 0<gamma<1/4, and is a multiplication
algebra. To check the less immediate first inclusion, write a_j=2^(3j/4)
||Delta_j f||_2. Then sum 2^j||Delta_j f||_2^2=sum 2^(-j/2)a_j^2 is bounded
by (sum a_j^4)^(1/2)(sum 2^(-j))^(1/2). The other inclusions follow the
one-dimensional Bernstein estimate and the standard dyadic product
decomposition: the low-high terms use E subset L-infinity, and the high-high
terms are summable since 3/4>1/2. Positive C^(1+alpha) physical data belong to
E, as do their reciprocal/multiplied normalizations.

2. FROZEN MULTIPLIER IS SELF-ADJOINT IN ONE FIXED WEIGHTED HILBERT SPACE

Freeze a strictly positive continuous qbar and set abar=qbar/c. Work in the
fixed Hilbert space L2(abar^(-1)dx), whose norm is equivalent to ordinary L2.
The closed nonnegative densely defined form

  e(u,v)=<K^(1/2)u,K^(1/2)v>_L2, domain H^(1/2),

has associated nonnegative self-adjoint operator Abar=abar K. Its operator
domain is exactly H1. Indeed the form identity for u in the operator domain
gives Ku=abar^(-1)f in distributions with f in weighted L2, hence Ku in L2,
and the Fourier multiplier implies u in H1. Conversely every H1 function
satisfies this form identity with f=abar Ku. The graph norm is equivalent to
the H1 norm because abar and abar^(-1) are bounded. No differentiability of
abar is used for the domain assertion.

By the spectral theorem, -Abar generates an analytic contraction semigroup on
this weighted Hilbert space. The maximal-Lp result proved by L. de Simon in
1964, pp207--223, therefore applies for every 1<p<infinity, in particular p=4.
One can first shift Abar by 1 to remove its constant zero mode, use the
half-line result for the shifted operator, and remove the shift on every
finite interval by the transformation u(t)=exp(t)v(t). Equivalent fixed norms
then transfer the estimates to the original L2 space. This is not an assertion
that a time-dependent operator is self-adjoint in a changing Hilbert space.
The zero operator on E has maximal-L4 regularity by Bochner integration.
Thus diag(abar K,0) has maximal-L4 regularity on the fixed pair (X1,X0).

3. NONAUTONOMOUS MR, CLAMP, AND THE EXACT AMANN INTERFACE

For a continuous positive path q(t) in E,

  ||[(q(t)-q(s))/c]K u||_2 <= C||q(t)-q(s)||_E ||u||_H1.

Freezing at time s and choosing a short interval makes this perturbation
smaller than the inverse bound of the frozen MR operator. A Neumann-series
argument gives MR on that interval. A finite partition of a compact time
interval and the E trace at each interface give MR for the whole path.
This argument proves the continuous-coefficient MR implication needed here.
Amann's 2005 paper p5 cites this implication as Proposition 7.1 in reference
[5], which is the DIFFERENT paper 'Maximal regularity for nonautonomous
evolution equations', Adv. Nonlinear Studies 4 (2004),417--430. Proposition 7.1
is not being attributed to the 2005 paper.

To make the abstract maps defined on all MR paths, choose a smooth bounded
scalar clamp chi, bounded below by a positive constant, and equal to the
identity on a neighborhood of the initial range q0(x)/c(x). Set

  A(q,y)=diag(chi(q/c)K,0),
  lambda(q,y)=-h[q]-integral(B q y),
  R(q,y)=(q[(B/c)(q-y)-lambda], y(q-y-lambda)).

The clamp makes every frozen operator uniformly positive; every MR path has
a continuous coefficient in the operator norm L(X1,X0). Thus A(q,y) has MR
for every such path by the preceding partition argument. A is bounded and
Lipschitz on bounded MR sets into L-infinity((0,S);L(X1,X0)); the topology on
Amann's MR class is the induced operator-norm topology, not a demand for a
single inverse estimate over all arbitrary paths.

The scalar quadratic form is locally Lipschitz on E:

  |h[q1]-h[q2]| <= C(||q1||_H^(1/2)+||q2||_H^(1/2))
                          ||q1-q2||_H^(1/2).

The bilinear memory integral has the same property in E x E. Since E is an
algebra and 1/c is a multiplier on E, R is locally Lipschitz E x E -> X0.
The continuous MR trace implies R is bounded and Lipschitz on bounded MR
sets into L-infinity((0,S);X0). Both A and R are Volterra maps, since their
value at t uses only (q(t),y(t)). These checks establish assumption (1.2) in
Amann's 2005 paper with its upper integrability exponent equal to infinity.
Theorem 2.1, not Theorem 3.1, gives a unique maximal MR solution; Corollary
2.2 gives its MR continuation criterion. Theorem 3.1 concerns dependence on
data. The author survey uses different numbering and is not the primary
theorem reference used in this addendum.

For sufficiently small S, the E trace remains in the neighborhood of the
positive initial range, so the clamp is inactive and q>0. The memory equation
has positive solution y=y0 exp(integral(q-y-lambda)); its E solution is positive
at each spatial point. Thus the MR solution solves the actual unclamped
normalized equations. Continuation below is always performed in a fresh
positive neighborhood where the local clamp is inactive.

4. PUTTING THE ACTUAL MR SOLUTION IN THE CLASSICAL SCHAUDER CLASS

This step is necessary because Dong--Jin--Zhang Theorem 1.6 is an a priori
estimate for solutions already in C^(1,1+gamma), not a stand-alone existence
theorem. It must not be applied to a hypothetical weak nonlinear limit.

Fix a compact interval [0,S] of the local MR solution. In its q equation set

  a(t,x)=q(t,x)/c(x),
  f(t,x)=q(t,x)[(B/c)(q(t,x)-y(t,x))-lambda(t)].

These are now fixed coefficients and forcing of a linear equation. They are
continuous jointly in (t,x), uniformly C^gamma in space for any gamma<1/4,
and a has a positive lower bound. Continuity of lambda follows from the E
trace and the preceding quadratic-form estimate. No time Holder modulus for
lambda is assumed.

Extend the coefficients slightly in time and mollify to smooth a_n,f_n and
smooth q0,n. Positivity and upper/lower ellipticity bounds are preserved, and
spatial C^gamma seminorms stay uniformly bounded. Uniform convergence a_n->a,
Lp convergence f_n->f, and E convergence q0,n->q0 can be arranged. Solve

  q_n' + a_n K q_n=f_n, q_n(0)=q0,n

by the nonautonomous MR result just established. These linear solutions are
smooth: spatial difference quotients commute with K, and their equations have
forcing f_n,x-a_n,x Kq_n, already in L4 L2. Repeated MR estimates yield every
spatial Sobolev order; differentiating in time gives the corresponding smooth
time regularity. Smooth data and coefficients remove the initial compatibility
issue. This is a linear bootstrap, and does not presume nonlinear classical
existence.

The MR inverse for a_n stays uniformly bounded because a_n->a in
L-infinity((0,S);L(H1,L2)) and the inverse for the fixed path a is bounded.
The difference q_n-q solves a linear equation with forcing
f_n-f+(a-a_n)Kq and its small initial trace difference. Hence q_n->q in MR4,
and therefore in C([0,S];E). In particular the q_n have uniformly bounded
global spatial C^gamma norms.

Apply Dong--Jin--Zhang Theorem 1.6 to these SMOOTH linear solutions after
lifting their periodic fields to R. The coefficient kernel is exactly
a_n(t,x)kappa/(pi |z|^2); it satisfies the order-one symmetric cancellation,
ellipticity and spatial Holder assumptions (1.11), and f_n has the same
spatial Holder bounds. The theorem allows merely measurable time dependence.
On each interval [s,S], s>0, its estimate gives uniformly bounded spatial
C^(1+gamma) norm and spatial C^gamma norm of q_n'. Covering the circle by
cylinders gives the global periodic bound. One may decrease gamma to avoid
endpoint exponents and the noninteger regularity requirement.

Compact interpolation now gives q_n->q uniformly in C^(1+gamma') for
gamma'<gamma on interior intervals: uniform C^(1+gamma) bounds together with
uniform C0 convergence suffice at each time with constants uniform in time.
The order-one symmetric integral K:C^(1+gamma')->C^gamma' is continuous, so
Kq_n->Kq uniformly. It follows from the equations that q_n'->f-aKq uniformly.
Thus the actual q is C1 in time, C^(1+gamma') in space and satisfies the
equation classically for t>0. This establishes the precise strong-to-classical
interface. It neither identifies a nonlinear weak-energy defect nor assumes
classical regularity as an input to its own proof. The memory ODE propagates
the corresponding spatial regularity from the C^(1+alpha) initial y0.

The identities in v2 may now be differentiated on intervals bounded away
from zero. MR continuity in E, hence in C0 and H^(1/2), passes their integrated
versions to the initial time. The energy chain rule also follows directly by
Fourier truncation, since Kq and q' belong to L4 L2 on each compact interval.
For each finite interval, q has a positive lower bound, so the entropy chain
rule has no singular denominator. There is no unproved entropy inequality
lost in an approximation limit.

5. GLOBAL RESTART WITHOUT A CIRCULAR TIME-REGULARITY ASSUMPTION

Suppose the maximal normalized time Smax were finite. The v2 entropy, maximum
and energy estimates give q<=Mbar, y<=Ybar, and bounded lambda on [0,Smax),
with constants independent of derivatives. At a spatial minimum Kq<=0, giving

  min q(t)>=min q0 exp[-(lambda_*+(B_max/c_min)Ybar)t].

The y ODE likewise gives min y(t)>=min y0 exp[-(Ybar+lambda_*)t]. Thus both
remain bounded below on the finite interval. Their lower bounds need not stay
positive as normalized time tends to infinity.

The already classical q therefore fits Schwab--Silvestre Theorems 1.1/7.1:
the lifted symmetric kernel has order one, pointwise positive bounds, zero
drift, and bounded forcing. This supplies a uniform space-time C^gamma bound
away from t=0. The memory ODE supplies a uniform spatial C^gamma bound for y
by a spatial difference estimate and Gronwall; scalar lambda cancels in that
comparison. Hence a=q/c and f above have uniformly bounded spatial C^gamma
norms. Decrease gamma below the local classical exponent, alpha and 1/2 if
needed. Dong--Jin--Zhang Theorem 1.6 now gives uniform C_x^(1+gamma) bounds
and uniform C_x^gamma bounds for q' near Smax, without assuming a Holder
modulus in time for lambda. This application is legitimate by Section 4.

The y ODE propagates C^(1+gamma) spatial bounds by differentiating in x and
using the q bound; its time derivative is bounded in that space on the finite
interval. Bounded q' in C0 gives a uniform C0 limit at Smax. Compact spatial
interpolation gives a positive C^(1+gamma') limit for q for any gamma'<gamma.
The same holds for y. These limits belong to the trace E x E and have positive
minima, so Section 3 restarts the solution, contradicting maximality.
Consequently Smax=infinity. Local MR uniqueness makes the restarted solution
unique; the dependence theorem gives continuous dependence before blow-up.

6. FINAL PHYSICAL RECONSTRUCTION

The normalized identities, estimates, and energy-limit proof in v2 are
unchanged. Given the global solution just established, solve rho'=rho lambda,
rho(0)=rho0, t'=1/rho and set V=rho q, theta=1/(rho y). This exact change of
variables gives the physical equations, with initial values V0,theta0.
The mass constraint integral(cq)=1 is preserved because lambda=-h[q]-I.
In the strict case the integrable lambda deficit gives the explicit time
bounds; in the critical case lambda->lambda_*>0 gives finite physical time.
In both cases the tail integral for t implies (T-t)rho->1/lambda_*.

The normalized obstacle energy gap tends to zero; constrained coercivity then
gives q->q_* in H^(1/2). This proves the stated blow-up profile convergence.
V diverges in every open interior subinterval of the active patch in the
energy/finite-Lp sense; in particular its norm is unbounded, so T is a true
finite maximal physical time. At finite physical times theta remains finite
and positive. The a.e. state exponent in v2 follows by Fubini from the
integrable v^2+y^2 error and the exact memory equation, not from dropping it.
