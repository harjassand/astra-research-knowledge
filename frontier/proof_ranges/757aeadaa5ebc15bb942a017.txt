\[ \boxed{ K_{n,L}(S,T)=\frac{1}{1+L|S\cap T|}. } \]
The remaining argument is an exact calculation of this kernel’s rank.
Define the subset-incidence matrix
\[ Z_{S,U}=\mathbf1_{\{U\subseteq S\}}. \]
Ordering subsets by size makes \(Z\) triangular with diagonal entries one, so it is invertible.
For
\[ f(k)=\frac1{1+Lk}, \]
let
\[ d_k=\Delta^k f(0). \]
Then
\[ \begin{aligned} d_k &=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{k-j}\binom kj\frac1{1+Lj}\\ &=\int_0^1(t^L-1)^k\,dt\\ &=(-1)^k \frac{k!L^k}{\prod_{j=0}^{k}(1+Lj)}. \end{aligned} \]
In particular, every \(d_k\) is nonzero.
Newton expansion gives
\[ f(|S\cap T|) = \sum_{U\subseteq S\cap T}d_{|U|}. \]
Hence
\[ K_{n,L} = Z\, \operatorname{diag}(d_{|U|}:U\subseteq[n]) \,Z^{\mathsf T}. \]
Both outer matrices are invertible and every diagonal entry is nonzero. Therefore
\[ \boxed{\operatorname{rank}K_{n,L}=2^n.} \]
Equivalently,
\[ \det K_{n,L} = \prod_{k=0}^{n}d_k^{\binom nk}\ne0. \]
This is a standard incidence-factorization technique from meet-matrix mathematics; I am not claiming the technique itself as new. The proposed contribution is its use inside the explicit, nonredundant cancellation separation. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2007/37580)
Because a sum of \(t\) separated functions has rank at most \(t\), the obstruction holds even when the proposed compression allows signed real factors—not merely rectangle indicators.
Let
\[ D_n(S,T)=\mathbf1_{\{S\cap T=\varnothing\}} \]
be the disjointness matrix. For \(L=8\),
\[ \|K_{n,8}-D_n\|_{\max}\le\frac19. \]
Thus an approximation to \(K_{n,8}\) with error \(1/9\) approximates \(D_n\) with error \(2/9\).
The needed lower bound is an approximate-rank lower bound, not merely a communication lower bound. Here is the precise interface.
For \(n=4m\), index the ground set by \((\ell,b)\), where \(\ell\in[2m]\) and \(b\in\{0,1\}\). Restrict rows to
\[ S_x=\{(\ell,x_\ell):\ell\in[2m]\}. \]
Partition \([2m]\) into \(m\) pairs. A restricted column chooses one position \(v_j\) from each pair and bits \(w_j\), giving
\[ T_{V,w}=\{(v_j,1-w_j):j\in[m]\}. \]
Then
\[ D_n(S_x,T_{V,w}) = \operatorname{NOR}_m(x|_V\oplus w). \]
This is a NOR pattern matrix. Converting from Boolean to sign entries changes the approximation error to \(4/9\) and increases rank by at most one. Sherstov’s approximate-rank theorem, with the \(\Omega(\sqrt m)\) approximate degree of NOR, then yields \(2^{\Omega(\sqrt m)}\). Restriction handles general \(n\). The full constants and conventions are preserved in the research record. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.4291)

Instead of sharing the surviving value across all active positives, cancel each negative contribution against its matching nested positive contribution. The remainder is a single-row predicate.
Write
\[ Y_i=\{w_T:i\in T\}. \]
After cancellation, group the \(L\) diagnostic rows for coordinate \(i\) into
\[ \{d_{ia}:a\in[L]\}\times Y_i. \]
A positive partition consists of one rectangle covering all core rows in the distinguished and target columns, one rectangle for each of the other \(2Ln\) basis columns, and these \(n\) diagnostic rectangles. They are disjoint and total
\[ 1+2Ln+n. \]
Thus the same instance has
\[ \boxed{ \text{structure-aware output width }O(n) \quad\text{versus}\quad \text{equal-sharing intermediate rank }2^n. } \]
The successful operation uses differences of nested local predicates. Those differences need not be members of the original column basis.
