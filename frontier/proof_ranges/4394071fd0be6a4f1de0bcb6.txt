## Attempted go/no-go and decisive scope

The physical mechanism is plausible in an isolated, phase-prepared rotor: state-selective stimulated anti-Stokes transitions can transfer molecular rotational energy into an optical sideband, and a cavity can capture that sideband. The optical-centrifuge loading itself is a coherent Raman operation, so its ideal unitary reverse is not forbidden. This is the credible candidate, not a claimed invention or achieved technology.

The same-regime gate is not passed. In dense CO₂ the only demonstrated return-window proxy is rotational coherence and energy-transfer dynamics, not work survival; the collision interval is about 167 ps and vibrational hot bands are visible by 500 ps. The centrifuge test uses tens of mJ-scale laser systems and 10 Hz operation, while no count of activated molecules or calorimetric net field gain was reported. In the only chemistry experiment, rotation was continually prepared in a trapped ion and the observed reaction was rare; no recovery pulse, coherent sideband, recovered work, or holding-power cost was measured. There is no basis for assigning one set of $p,r,h,\eta$ to either experiment.

A decisive next discriminator would stay in one CO₂ or reaction-compatible apparatus and perform paired loading/unloading runs with calibrated field-energy measurements across every incident and output mode. It would use correctly tuned, detuned, phase-reversed, no-load, and no-unload controls; independently measure the full state-resolved rotational and vibrational populations; measure gas heating; and show that the collected sideband replaces part of the next-cycle pump at fixed product amount and selectivity. For chemistry, product identity and carbon/oxygen balance must be measured independently. Until that is done, coherent spectroscopy, a reversed alignment trace, or light escaping a cavity counts as output observation, not reusable work.
