## 6. Real-input results and exact countermodels

The narrow-bound experiment uses 58 samples from 0.01 through 904.7 Hz and epsilon=0.1 mOhm for each real and imaginary component. This error budget is neither measured instrument uncertainty nor an established bound on governing-model error.

| Query | Checked continuum outer interval, mOhm | Internal NNLS prediction, mOhm |
|---|---:|---:|
| Q_10 | [62.18499257164905, 62.73598921007930] before outward display rounding | approximately 62.38484 |
| Q_1000 | [100.41253233665253, 529.10492665402530] before outward display rounding | approximately 111.43733 |

The exact rational bounds, rather than rounded decimal renderings, are authoritative in results/continuum_certificates.json. The Q_10 interval has width approximately 0.5510 mOhm, about 0.882% of its midpoint.

The broad Q_1000 interval is not merely an ineffective upper bound. Two explicit positive models are supplied whose frequency-domain feasibility is verified using exact rational arithmetic. Each has maximum component error strictly below 0.1 mOhm, yet their Q_1000 values are approximately 100.769677 and 528.747375 mOhm. Both have positive series inductance.

Therefore, under this EIS-only model and error interface, the same supplied measurements support both sides of a 200 mOhm decision threshold. Any point estimator has worst-case absolute error at least half their separation, approximately 213.989 mOhm. This is an information limitation for the stated interface, not a lower bound on methods using additional pulse or pseudo-open-circuit data.

### The full spectrum rejects the tighter model

The positive-RC model has a nonincreasing real part as frequency increases. In the retained input,

- Re Z at 10,000 Hz is 38.03 mOhm;
- Re Z at 5,484 Hz is 37.00 mOhm.

Their 1.03 mOhm ordering violation forces any uniform real-component error budget to be at least 0.515 mOhm, irrespective of the number of relaxation modes. Series inductance and capacitance do not alter this real-part condition. An exploratory full-band grid fit required approximately 0.543 mOhm, but that latter optimum is not asserted as an exact continuum minimum.

Thus the narrow lower-frequency certificate is not a certificate for the entire measured spectrum, nor evidence that the actual cell belongs to the assumed RC class. The frequency restriction is an exploratory modeling choice, not a physically established remedy for the rejected full-band model.
