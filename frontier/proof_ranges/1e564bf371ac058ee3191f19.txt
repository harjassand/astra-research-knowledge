# A finite algebraic criterion for uniform eventual entanglement breaking

9 October 2026. Integrated strengthening of the mapping-cone uniformization theorem. The equivalence below removes its infinite-time premise and gives an exact decision procedure. It uses the already checked corner mechanism and uniformization proof; no additional numerical audit is needed. Priority and external certification remain open.

## The criterion

Let C be a closed convex CP mapping cone on M_d. For a proper nonzero orthogonal projection P, put Q=I-P. Call P invariant for Phi when

 Q Phi(P) Q=0.

For a CP map this is equivalent to preservation of the P-supported matrix corner. Define its cross transfer by

 T_(Phi,P)(X)=P Phi(P X Q) Q.

The following are equivalent, without any semialgebraicity assumption:

1. Every Phi in C has a finite entanglement-breaking power.
2. Every Phi in C and every proper nonzero invariant P satisfy T_(Phi,P)=0.

If C is additionally semialgebraic, these are equivalent to:

3. There is one common finite N such that Phi^N is EB for all Phi in C.

Moreover, if condition 2 fails, a block-unitary postfilter produces an element F in C for which every positive power F^n is NPT. Thus the negative side has a finite persistent-coherence witness, rather than an unbounded search through powers.

## Necessity and the negative witness

Suppose T_(Phi,P) is nonzero. In adapted coordinates its restriction to Hom(QH,PH) is a square linear operator T. Products U_P tensor conjugate(U_Q), with U_P,U_Q unitary, span the full matrix algebra on this space. The nondegenerate trace pairing therefore supplies such a product for which

 tr[(U_P tensor conjugate(U_Q))T] != 0.

Put U=U_P direct_sum U_Q and F=Ad_U Phi. It belongs to C and preserves P. Its cross transfer S has nonzero trace, so S has a nonzero eigenvalue. The invariant-corner triangular structure makes the cross transfer of F^n exactly S^n, which is nonzero for every n.

Every PPT CP map with invariant P has zero cross transfer: a product vector in Q(output) tensor conjugate(P(input)) has zero quadratic form against the positive partially transposed Choi matrix, hence is in its kernel. The corresponding matrix elements annihilate all cross coefficients. Therefore F^n is NPT for every n, and in particular cannot be EB.

A finite witness consists of Phi,P,U with: Phi in C; P=P*=P^2 and 0<trP<d; U*U=I and UP=PU; Q Phi(P) Q=0; and the displayed cross-operator trace nonzero. The trace can equivalently be taken on all M_d for X->P U Phi(P X Q) U* Q, since that operator vanishes off the cross-input subspace. These are finite polynomial conditions in real and imaginary matrix entries.

## Sufficiency by dimension induction

The property in condition 2 passes to every compressed cone: embed its member into M_d, and embed its invariant projection. The embedded cross-transfer identity restricts back to the smaller corner.

Proceed by induction on d. Dimension one is immediate. Given Phi in C:

- If its spectral radius is zero, Cayley-Hamilton gives Phi^(d^2)=0.
- If Phi is reducible, select a proper invariant P. Condition 2 gives zero cross transfer. The positive split Phi=F+G with GF=0 from the full proof applies. Each compressed map has a finite EB power by lower-dimensional induction. The exact bound N_P+N_Q+1 then gives a finite EB power of Phi.
- If Phi is irreducible with positive spectral radius, faithful left Perron normalization gives a CPTP map Theta in C by a telescoping CP similarity. Its right invariant state is faithful. If Theta is primitive, its powers converge to X->tr(X)sigma for sigma>0. The latter has an interior separable Choi matrix, so sufficiently large powers are EB. To see the interior assertion directly, filter the elementary separable ball around I tensor I by sigma^(1/2) on the output. No uniform mixing rate is needed for this pointwise argument.
- If Theta is irreducible and periodic with period h>=2, its hth power has the cyclic Kraus block structure proved in the full theorem. Condition 2 kills every cross block; the result is a direct sum of maps on smaller corners. Lower-dimensional induction gives a finite EB power. Undo the CP similarity.

This proves condition 1 without invoking any PPT-specific eventual-EB theorem. The equivalence with condition 3 in the semialgebraic case is the general uniformization theorem. The implication 3=>1 is immediate.

## Exact decidability and a terminating exponent search

Suppose C is given by a finite first-order semialgebraic description over the real algebraic numbers. It may equivalently be given by a rational quantifier-free formula. If needed, the CP, cone, convexity, closedness and mapping-cone promises are themselves first-order real-algebraic properties, although checking them can be expensive.

Condition 2 is a finite sentence in the first-order theory of real closed fields. It quantifies over the entries of Phi and P; the zero cross-transfer identity is imposed on the d^2 matrix units. Quantifier elimination decides it exactly.

If the answer is negative, semialgebraic sampling can produce real-algebraic Phi,P,U satisfying the finite witness conditions above. Nonzero trace then certifies NPT at every power without testing those powers.

If the answer is positive, enumerate N=1,2,... . At each N use quantifier elimination to decide

 forall Phi in C: J(Phi^N) is separable.

Separability is an existential real-algebraic formula: a separable d-by-d Choi matrix is a sum of at most d^4 positive rank-one product matrices, with zero terms allowed. The map power is polynomial in the entries of Phi. The uniformization theorem proves that this search terminates. Consequently both the infinite-time cone property and a valid common exponent are computable from the algebraic input description.

This is exact computability, not a practical runtime bound or a numerically robust test. Near-zero equalities and high-degree elimination can be prohibitive.

## Finite generator input

There is a concrete finite input model. Given finitely many CP maps Phi_1,...,Phi_s with real-algebraic entries, their closed convex CP mapping cone is semialgebraic and has an effectively obtainable first-order description.

Indeed, a CP pre/postfiltered generator expands into terms Ad_A Phi_j Ad_B. In the real vector space of Hermiticity-preserving maps, of dimension d^4, conic Caratheodory bounds every finite conic combination by d^4 such terms. Nonnegative coefficients can be absorbed into A. Thus the finite conic hull is a finite union of polynomial images described by existential real variables A_l,B_l and discrete choices j_l. Its closure is semialgebraic and effectively definable by

 forall epsilon>0 exists an element of that hull within epsilon.

The closure is a CP mapping cone because composition is continuous and the finite hull already has the required invariance. The decision criterion therefore applies to this finitely generated, closed family.

On the negative side, a returned witness can lie only in the closure of the finite filter hull; the procedure does not promise an exact finite filtering circuit realizing a limiting witness. On the positive side, its bound covers all members of the closed family and hence every actual finite filtering mixture. It does not assert a bound on arbitrary nonstationary products with different maps at each time.

## Significance and scope

This strengthening converts an infinite-time premise into a finite algebraic dichotomy: either a persistent cross-corner coherence exists after a filter, or all cone members have a common finite EB index when the cone is semialgebraic. It supplies an exact algorithmic capability even when no PPT assumption is available. The main theorem, not generic compactness or distance to an ideal, is what makes the positive exponent search halt.

No claim is made that this decides eventual EB for an arbitrary individual channel without passing to its entire generated closed mapping cone. That stronger individual problem is different. No assertion about the optimal exponent, the PPT-square conjecture, practical complexity, or priority is made.
