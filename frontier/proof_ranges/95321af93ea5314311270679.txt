# Independent construction: inverse of a local observable without global mixing

Date: 2026-10-12. Internal reconstruction and invention attempt. Historical novelty: **UNKNOWN**. No claim below is externally verified. The direction was selected before screening information from other workers.

## 1. Why a generic transition-simulation solution is impossible

Let the state space be {0,1}, let f(x)=x, and take 0<epsilon<=1/4. Kernel P0 has transition probabilities P0(0,1)=epsilon and P0(1,0)=epsilon. Kernel P1 has P1(0,1)=2epsilon and P1(1,0)=epsilon. Both are irreducible reversible two-state chains. Their stationary means are respectively 1/2 and 2/3, a difference Delta=1/6.

The interface is a transition simulator: an algorithm may adaptively choose either state and request a transition from it. It is not given exact transition probabilities. Let T be its number of requests, and Z its output. Couple the algorithm's internal random bits and couple transition replies maximally until the first unequal reply. At state 1 the replies can always agree; at state 0 their mismatch probability is epsilon. If no mismatch occurs before the P0 run stops, both transcripts and outputs agree. Thus the mismatch event D has probability at most epsilon E0[T]. This statement holds for a random stopping time by summing the conditional mismatch probabilities over requests made before stopping.

If the outputs are unbiased under both kernels, then

    Delta = |E[Z1-Z0]| <= E[(|Z1|+|Z0|) 1_D]
          <= sqrt(2(E[Z1^2]+E[Z0^2]) Pr(D)).

Writing M=max(E0[Z^2],E1[Z^2]) and C=max(E0[T],E1[T]), we obtain

    C M >= Delta^2/(4 epsilon) = 1/(144 epsilon).

Consequently no universal bounded-variance, bounded-work, unbiased stationary estimator exists in this interface. The observable is bounded and has Dirichlet energy O(epsilon), so small *positive* Dirichlet energy does not repair the obstruction. A negative-Sobolev/source condition would have to exclude it.

Exact probability queries change the example: query the two rates and compute the stationary ratio. They must not be conflated with simulation access. This bound does not exclude symbolic local-rule algorithms.

## 2. New attempt after the collision: symbolic support growth

Let X be a finite state space and L a continuous-time Markov generator on X. X need not be enumerated by the algorithm. Let I be a finite collection of indices, and suppose functions phi_i:X->R satisfy |phi_i|<=1. They need not be linearly independent. Suppose exact local calculation proves the identities

    L phi_i = -d_i phi_i + c_i 1 + sum_j b_ij phi_j.                 (2.1)

Every row is finitely supported, d_i>=gamma>0, and I has an integer grade g(i)>=0. For a fixed A>=0 suppose

    b_ij != 0  =>  g(j)>=g(i)+1,
    r_i := sum_j |b_ij| / d_i <= A/(g(i)+1),
    |c_i|/d_i <= U.                                              (2.2)

The grade is a semantic property derived from the local generator, not a relabeling of path depth. In particular every individual reachable row must satisfy (2.1)-(2.2). The theorem below also holds for a countably infinite I when the displayed uniformly convergent sums and the action of L can be interchanged; this note only claims the finite-state, finite-I case to avoid unverified domain assumptions.

Define D=diag(d_i), B=(b_ij), K=D^{-1}B, and u_i=c_i/d_i. For every path i0=i,i1,...,in with nonzero coefficients,

    product_{k=0}^{n-1} r_{ik}
      <= A^n / product_{k=0}^{n-1}(g(i)+k+1)
      <= A^n/n!.                                                (2.3)

Hence the absolute row-sum norm of K^n is at most A^n/n!, and

    R := sum_{n>=0} K^n

converges absolutely with row-sum norm at most exp(A). Here it is also a finite sum because strictly increasing grade makes K nilpotent. No lower bound on the spectral gap of L is used.

### Theorem 2.1: stationary mean and Poisson operation

For any stationary probability pi of L, put m_i=pi(phi_i). Integrating (2.1) gives m=u+Km. Iteration gives

    m = sum_{n=0}^{N-1} K^n u + K^N m.

Because |m_i|<=1 and (2.3) tends to zero, the remainder vanishes. Therefore all stationary probabilities agree on these observables, and

    m = R u = (D-B)^{-1} c.                                     (2.4)

For each x, define

    h(x) = R D^{-1} phi(x) = (D-B)^{-1} phi(x).                   (2.5)

Apply L to the finite linear combination and use (2.1):

    L h = (D-B)^{-1}[-(D-B) phi+c] = -phi+m 1.

Thus -L h_i=phi_i-m_i and |h_i(x)|<=exp(A)/gamma. This is a complete local symbolic certificate of a bounded observable-specific inverse. It does not give the stationary law, the global mixing time, or an inverse on other observables.

For an irreducible finite chain, the centered Poisson solution equals h_i-pi(h_i), and the integrated transient response is h_i(x)-pi(h_i). In the centered example in Section 4, the supplied formula has pi(h_i)=0, so h_i itself is the integrated response. In the general theorem, calling h_i an integrated response without subtracting pi(h_i) would be wrong.

## 3. A bounded unbiased evaluator

For A>0, sample N~Poisson(A), independently of all subsequent path choices. Start at index i. At a row k with s_k=sum_j |b_kj|>0, sample successor j with probability q_kj=|b_kj|/s_k and multiply the path weight by

    b_kj/(d_k q_kj) = sign(b_kj) r_k.

If the row has no successors before step N, output zero. Otherwise after N steps the path weight is W_N. For any terminal values t_k bounded by T, return

    Y_t = exp(A) N!/A^N W_N t_{I_N}.                            (3.1)

Conditionally on N=n, E[W_n t_{I_n}]=(K^n t)_i. Since Pr(N=n)=exp(-A) A^n/n!, E[Y_t]=(R t)_i. The pointwise bound (2.3) yields |Y_t|<=T exp(A). Hence all moments are finite. The number of symbolic row visits is at most N+1 and has expectation at most A+1. Early termination only decreases it.

Use t=u for the stationary mean, obtaining |Y|<=U exp(A). Use t_k=phi_k(x)/d_k for the Poisson value, obtaining |Y|<=exp(A)/gamma. If A=0, K=0, so return t_i deterministically.

Resource accounting: these are counts of **symbolic row visits**, not full runtime. Each row needs exact d_k, exact outgoing coefficients or a correct sampler proportional to their absolute values, and the exact sum of their absolute values. The terminal function must be evaluated. Bit precision, generating a Poisson variable, evaluating exp(A), and all preprocessing must be charged. Formula (3.1) is mathematically unbiased in the exact real-arithmetic/randomness model; a finite-bit implementation must either use exact computable-real randomness/output or explicitly bound numerical bias. A floating-point pilot does not certify exact unbiasedness.

For approximate values, independent averages plus a median-of-means construction give absolute error eta with failure probability delta using O(exp(2A)/(gamma^2 eta^2) log(1/delta)) Poisson-value replicates. This is independent of the global gap but exponential in the declared coefficient parameter A. No polynomial claim in arbitrary A is made.

## 4. A non-product example whose target evolution uses a slow coordinate

Take active spins x_0,...,x_m, passive spins y_0,...,y_{m-1}, and one hidden control spin z, all in {-1,+1}. Each site has an independent Poisson refresh clock. The y spins refresh to independent fair signs at rate 1. The z spin refreshes to a fair sign at rate epsilon>0. For k<m, x_k refreshes at rate 1 with conditional mean

    a x_{k+1} y_k z,       0<|a|<=1.

The root x_m refreshes to a fair sign at rate 1. This is an explicit nonnegative local Markov rule depending on at most three other spins. For |a|<1 the chain is irreducible immediately; at |a|=1, irreducibility also follows by scheduling fair passive/root/z refreshes and copies, but the strict-noise case is sufficient here.

Let

    phi_k = x_k z^{k mod 2} product_{j<k} y_j,
    d_k = k+1+epsilon (k mod 2).

Directly applying each local refresh gives, for k<m,

    L phi_k = -d_k phi_k+a phi_{k+1},
    L phi_m = -d_m phi_m.                                       (4.1)

All passive factors already in phi_k contribute -1 each. The z factor contributes -epsilon when present. The only active spin in phi_k is x_k; replacing it multiplies by the fresh y_k and toggles z. Therefore g(k)=k and r_k=|a|/d_k<=|a|/(k+1). The construction has A=|a|<=1 and gamma=1, independent of m and epsilon.

The exact solution for f=x_0 is

    h = sum_{k=0}^m [a^k / product_{j=0}^k d_j] phi_k.            (4.2)

Every phi_k has stationary mean zero by (4.1), so pi(h)=0 and pi(f)=0. Formula (4.2) is a nontrivial integrated transient response even though the stationary mean is fixed by symmetry. Its value can be evaluated unbiasedly with at most |a|+1 expected symbolic row visits and output magnitude at most exp(|a|).

The generator has the exact eigenfunction Lz=-epsilon z, so it has an arbitrarily slow mode and global equilibration cannot be independent of 1/epsilon. The target's evolution does depend on z, so this is not a disjoint product of an unrelated fast and slow chain. The cancellation of the slow mode is certified by the observable algebra.

For m=1, stationarity applied to q=x_0 x_1 y_0 z gives

    L q=-(3+epsilon)q+a,
    pi(q)=a/(3+epsilon).

Thus the continuous-time asymptotic variance of the centered integral of x_0 is

    sigma^2=2 pi(x_0 h)
           =2[1+a^2/((2+epsilon)(3+epsilon))].                   (4.3)

This remains bounded as epsilon decreases to zero. It is an example of a nontrivial observable response and variance calculation, not an advance in arbitrary equilibrium sampling.

## 5. The gates that remain hard

1. **Prior art.** Weighted Neumann random walks, stochastic duality, finite moment closure, and auxiliary-function stationary bounds are old. The operation above has not been established as historically new. Its present status is a precise internal theorem and a candidate compilation interface.
2. **Applicability.** Fresh independent damped factors make the grading work. A consequential feedback system with repeated variables can erase these factors and destroy the grade condition. No general discovery or change-of-basis procedure is established.
3. **Acquisition.** A compact generator does not automatically give compact observable closure. Fourier extraction from an interaction block of d spins can cost 2^d. A supplied exact row sampler is a separate interface unless derived explicitly.
4. **Meaning of the source.** The explicit example computes a Poisson response and asymptotic variance. Its stationary mean alone is easy. This is not a completed solution to the initial universal stationary-mean ambition.
5. **Precision.** A bounded exact-real estimator has a finite-bit approximation with controlled error, but exact unbiased finite-bit implementation is not proved here.

## 6. Immediate counterexamples and next mathematical attack

The two-state simulator obstruction survives all generic proposals. Another local counterexample is an active spin x which refreshes at rate 1 to copy a hidden fair-refresh spin z of rate epsilon. Then Lx=-x+z and Lz=-epsilon z. The centered source x has Poisson solution h=x+z/epsilon. Its asymptotic variance is 2[1+1/(epsilon(1+epsilon))]. Thus dependence on a slow hidden coordinate is not harmless by itself; the support-growth condition does real work.

The next attack is to replace strict grade growth with an exactly resummed within-grade block and quantify the loss when passive variables are reused. A valid extension must expose both the block-solve acquisition cost and the norm of its inverse; calling a hard inverse a 'resummation' would merely move the obstruction.
