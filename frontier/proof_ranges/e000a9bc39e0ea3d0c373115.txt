\[ Q_a(z) = Q+(z-1)K_a^+ +(z^{-1}-1)K_a^-. \]
Hence
\[ F_{ab}(z) := \left. \frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} \mathbb E_z[e_b(X_t)] \right|_{t=0} = \pi^\top Q_a(z)^2e_b \]
is a Laurent polynomial with powers between \(-2\) and \(2\).
On that five-dimensional polynomial space, direct interpolation gives
\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} \chi_{ab}''(0) ={}&-\frac16\left[F_{ab}(2)-F_{ab}(1/2)\right]\\ &+\frac9{10}\left[F_{ab}(3/2)-F_{ab}(2/3)\right]. \end{aligned} } \tag{5} \]
The identity follows by checking the five monomials \(z^{-2},z^{-1},1,z,z^2\).
Only four fixed rate multipliers are needed:
\[ 2,\quad \frac12,\quad \frac32,\quad \frac23. \]
Every microscopic rate changes by at most a factor of two. The amplitudes do not shrink as the requested accuracy increases.
The remaining estimation problem is short-time curvature, not an infinitesimal intervention. Finite field amplitude and sampling uncertainty are substantive issues in existing response-based tests; the present extraction makes the amplitude choice explicit rather than treating susceptibility as a free oracle. \[ \boxed{ \Gamma=0 \iff \text{the observed labels form an autonomous controlled state.} } \tag{6} \]
More precisely, the following are equivalent:
\[ \Gamma=0 \iff (I-P)QP=0 \iff \sum_{j:\ell(j)=b}q_{ij}=L_{ab} \]
for every microscopic state \(i\) in label \(a\) and every \(b\ne a\).
The last condition says that microscopic states sharing the same observed label have identical total transition rates toward every other label.
When it holds, every initial microscopic distribution produces the observed Markov dynamics \(L\). Furthermore, any known label-dependent rate multiplication
\[ q_{ij}^{u}(t) = c_{\ell(i),\ell(j)}(t,Y_{[0,t)})q_{ij} \tag{7} \]
produces observed conditional rates
\[ L_{ab}^{u}(t)=c_{ab}(t,Y_{[0,t)})L_{ab}. \]
The policy may be randomized and depend on the entire observed history. It may not inspect the hidden state.
The proof is immediate from positive definiteness of the stationary inner product: \(\Gamma=0\) forces every \(b_a=0\). The aggregate rates then determine the observed jump intensities independently of the hidden conditional distribution.
A small certificate is also useful.
Set \(d=\operatorname{tr}\Gamma\), and let \(\ell_{\min}\) be the smallest positive off-diagonal rate of \(L\). Suppose
\[ e^{-H}\le c_{ab}\le e^H. \]
For an explicit, globally mixing uncertainty class, assume supplied bounds
\[ \boxed{ \lambda\pi_j\le q_{ij}\le\Lambda\pi_j \qquad(i\ne j), } \tag{8} \]
where \(0<\lambda\le\Lambda\).
This permits nonreversibility, nonuniform stationary probabilities, arbitrary hidden dimension, and arbitrary internal rate variation consistent with the bounds. It is nevertheless a genuine regularity assumption—not something inferred automatically from the observed data.
Start the microscopic system in stationarity and the reduced model in \(\mu\). For every compatible feedback policy and every finite horizon \(T\),
\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{TV} \left( \mathsf P_Q^{u,[0,T]}, \mathsf P_L^{u,[0,T]} \right) \le \sqrt{ \frac{\Lambda}{\lambda} e^{3H} \frac{T\,\operatorname{tr}\Gamma}{2\ell_{\min}} }. } \tag{9} \]
The laws here contain the whole observed trajectory, including transition times. Therefore the same bound controls every bounded path statistic and every event measurable from that trajectory.
Also,
\[ \ell_{\min}\ge\lambda\min_a\mu_a. \]
No minimum microscopic probability \(\min_i\pi_i\) appears.
Construct a hidden comparison chain by retaining within-label transitions and rescaling transitions from hidden state \(i\), in label \(a\), into label \(b\):
\[ \bar q_{ij} = q_{ij}\frac{L_{ab}}{r_{ib}}, \qquad r_{ib}=\sum_{\ell(j)=b}q_{ij}. \]
Zero-rate cases can be completed without losing forward support. The comparison chain is exactly lumpable and has observed generator \(L\).
For
\[ \Psi(x,y)=x\log(x/y)-x+y, \]
the path likelihood ratio reduces to an integral of
\[ c_{ab}\Psi(r_{ib},L_{ab}). \]
Use
\[ \Psi(x,y)\le\frac{(x-y)^2}{y}. \]
Under (8), even with feedback, the controlled microscopic marginal satisfies
\[ \Pr(X_t=i) \le \frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}e^{2H}\pi_i. \]
This follows from the scalar differential inequality
\[ p_i'(t) \le \Lambda e^H\pi_i-\lambda e^{-H}p_i(t). \]
Consequently,
\[ D_{\mathrm{KL}} \left( \mathsf P_Q^{u,[0,T]} \middle\| \mathsf P_L^{u,[0,T]} \right) \le \frac{\Lambda}{\lambda}e^{3H} \frac{T\,\operatorname{tr}\Gamma}{\ell_{\min}}. \]
