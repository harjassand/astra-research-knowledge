First,
\[ N:=V-V_q\succeq0. \]
This follows directly from positivity of measurement noise. For \(f=u\cdot Y\), \(A=A_u\), and measurement effects \(E_y\), unbiasedness gives
\[ \sum_y f(y)E_y=A. \]
Consequently,
\[ \sum_y(f(y)I-A)E_y(f(y)I-A) = \sum_yf(y)^2E_y-A^2 \succeq0. \]
Taking the state expectation proves \(u^TNu\ge0\).
The crucial cancellation is exact:
\[ \boxed{N_{ac}=0\quad\text{when }S_a\cap S_c=\varnothing.} \tag{5} \]
For disjoint supports, the product measurement reproduces the quantum second moment, including any long-range correlation. It adds no cross-covariance error.
For overlapping supports, direct evaluation gives
\[ |N_{ac}| \le \left(\frac3{\eta^2}\right)^k-1. \]
Each candidate overlaps at most \(1+k(\Delta-1)\) candidates, counting itself. Hence
\[ 0\preceq N\preceq\nu_{\mathrm{meas}}I, \]
where
\[ \nu_{\mathrm{meas}} = [1+k(\Delta-1)] \left[\left(\frac3{\eta^2}\right)^k-1\right]. \tag{6} \]
This step applies to arbitrary states. It does not assume Gibbs structure or independent quantum outcomes.
For any Hermitian \(A\), the Brooks–Harris inequality gives
\[ 0\le \operatorname{Var}_\rho(A) -\operatorname{Cov}^{\mathrm{BKM}}_\rho(A,A) \le \frac{\beta}{12} \operatorname{Tr}\rho[A,[H,A]]. \tag{7} \]
This is an established thermal correlation inequality. The variance-minus-susceptibility quantity is also an established object, often called quantum variance. Neither is being claimed as new. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.2882)
For completeness, its spectral proof uses the logarithmic and arithmetic means of two Gibbs probabilities:
\[ L_{ij}=\frac{p_i-p_j}{\log p_i-\log p_j}, \qquad T_{ij}=\frac{p_i+p_j}{2}. \]
With \(x=(\log p_i-\log p_j)/2\),
\[ T_{ij}=L_{ij}x\coth x, \qquad 0\le x\coth x-1\le\frac{x^2}{3}. \]
The double-commutator weight is \(4x^2L_{ij}/\beta\), giving (7).
Now define \(W\) through
\[ u^TWu=\operatorname{Tr}\rho[A_u,[H,A_u]]. \]
The needed coefficient-space bound is
\[ \boxed{ 0\preceq W\preceq4gk(k+\ell)\Delta I. } \tag{8} \]
To prove the upper bound, expand
\[ [P_a,[H_X,P_c]]. \]
Its norm is at most \(4\|H_X\|\), and it vanishes unless
\[ X\cap S_c\ne\varnothing, \qquad S_a\cap(X\cup S_c)\ne\varnothing. \]
For fixed \(c\), the total interaction weight touching \(S_c\) is at most \(kg\). Each relevant \(X\cup S_c\) meets at most \((k+\ell)\Delta\) candidate supports. The resulting nonnegative coefficient kernel therefore has row sums bounded by \(4gk(k+\ell)\Delta\).
Its column sums have the same bound: split the second intersection condition into \(X\cap S_a\ne\varnothing\) or \(S_c\cap S_a\ne\varnothing\). These contribute at most \(4kg\ell\Delta\) and \(4k^2g\Delta\), respectively.
The row/column Schur bound proves (8). Combining (7) and (8),
\[ 0\preceq V_q-C \preceq \frac{\beta g}{3}k(k+\ell)\Delta I. \tag{9} \]
Adding (6) and (9) proves (4).
The exact Gibbs response identity is
\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial h_b}\mathbb E_hY_a =-\beta C_{ab}. \tag{10} \]
Let \(s=\nabla_h\log p_h\) be the classical outcome score. Because the features do not depend on \(h\),
\[ \operatorname{Cov}(Y,s)=-\beta C. \]
The joint covariance matrix of \(Y\) and \(s\) is positive semidefinite. Its Schur complement gives
\[ \boxed{F_{\mathrm{loc}}\succeq\beta^2CV^{-1}C.} \tag{11} \]
Set \(R=V-C\), so \(0\preceq R\preceq\nu I\). The exact identity
\[ C(C+R)^{-1}C-(C-R) = R(C+R)^{-1}R \succeq0 \tag{12} \]
requires no commutativity between \(C\) and \(R\). It follows that
\[ F_{\mathrm{loc}}\succeq\beta^2(C-R). \]
Finally, the SLD Fisher weights for Gibbs parameters are
\[ \beta^2\frac{L_{ij}^2}{T_{ij}} \le\beta^2L_{ij}, \]
so \(F_Q\preceq\beta^2C\). Measurement information is bounded above by SLD information. These facts complete (1)–(3).
