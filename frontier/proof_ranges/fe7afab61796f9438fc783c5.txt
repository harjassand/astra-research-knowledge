## 1. Model and exact real closure

For `i=1,...,r`, consider complex amplitudes `x_i,y_i,c`, real controls `k=Kv`, and a supplied real `r×d` matrix `K`:

\[
i\dot x_i=k_i\bar y_i c,\qquad i\dot y_i=k_i\bar x_i c,
\qquad i\dot c=\sum_i k_i x_i y_i.
\tag{1}
\]

Write `a_i=|x_i|²`, `e_i=|y_i|²`, `n=|c|²`, `s_i=a_i+e_i`, `b_i=x_i y_i`, and define

\[
z_i=b_i\bar c,\quad p_i=\operatorname{Im}z_i,\quad
S_{ij}=\operatorname{Re}(b_i\bar b_j),\quad
R=SK,\quad P=K^Tp,\quad L=K^T(k\odot s).
\tag{2}
\]

The real state is

\[
\pi_K=(a,e,n,p,R)\in\mathbb R^{3r+1+rd}.
\]

**Theorem 1 (exact polynomial factorization).** For every admitted real control history, the image of (1) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot a_i=\dot e_i&=-2k_i p_i,&
\dot n&=2k^Tp,\\
\dot p_i&=(Rv)_i-k_i s_i n,&
\dot R_{i\alpha}&=-k_i s_i P_\alpha-L_\alpha p_i.
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]

All expressions are polynomial in the state and linear in `v`. They remain defined when any amplitudes vanish, including `c=0`. With dense `K`, evaluation and storage cost `O(rd+r)`. The state image has algebraic constraints and is a proper subset of its ambient Euclidean space.

**Proof.** Direct differentiation gives

\[
\dot b_i=-ik_i s_i c,\qquad
\dot z_i=i\sum_jk_jb_i\bar b_j-ik_i s_i n.
\]

Taking imaginary parts gives the equation for `p`. If `B_α=Σ_j K_jα b_j` and `Q_iα=b_i bar B_α`, then

\[
\dot Q_{i\alpha}=-ik_i s_i\overline{\sum_jK_{j\alpha}z_j}
                  +iL_\alpha z_i.
\]

Its real part is `-k_i s_i P_α-L_α p_i`. The action signs follow from `2 Re(bar x_i dot x_i)=-2 k_i Im z_i`; the central sign is the opposite. No division was used. ∎

The root's complex `(z,Q)` closure is therefore correct but excessive for this action task. `Re z` and `Im Q` do not enter (3). The involution

\[
(x,y,c)\longmapsto(\bar x,\bar y,-\bar c)
\tag{4}
\]

preserves (1) under every real control, preserves actions, sends `z→-bar z` and `Q→bar Q`, and leaves `(p,R)` unchanged. Full phase-orbit separation is stronger than necessary here.

An equivalent real geometry is useful. Put `h=-ic`, and identify each complex number with a vector in `R²`. Then

\[
\dot b_i=k_i s_i h,\qquad \dot h=-\sum_i k_i b_i,
\quad p_i=-b_i\cdot h,\quad R_{i\alpha}=b_i\cdot B_\alpha.
\tag{5}
\]

The real subspace `span{h,B_1,...,B_d}⊂R²` is constant along a trajectory: every derivative of these reference vectors lies in their span. Each `b_i` component perpendicular to that subspace is constant. Thus the reduction deliberately forgets components that never influence the admitted action experiment. When both `h` and all `B_α` vanish, the state is an exact equilibrium for every admitted control, even if individual pairs are populated.

## 2. Global existence, realizability, and feedback

**Theorem 2 (global physical factorization).** Let `v∈L¹_loc([0,∞);R^d)`. For every finite initial amplitude vector, (1) has a unique global absolutely continuous solution. The unique solution of (3) initialized at its realizable image equals `π_K` of that solution for all time. In particular, its image remains realizable and cannot escape the physical constraint set.

**Proof.** The amplitude field is polynomial and satisfies the Carathéodory local uniqueness bounds on bounded sets, with integrable envelope proportional to `|v(t)|`. The positive definite quantity

\[
E=\sum_i(a_i+e_i)+2n
\tag{6}
\]

is conserved for all real controls. It bounds every amplitude and prevents finite-time escape. The polynomial reduced field has the same local existence and uniqueness property on bounded sets. The lifted amplitude solution supplies a global reduced solution; uniqueness identifies every reduced solution with it. ∎

This theorem does **not** assert that arbitrary, physically inconsistent ambient coordinates have global solutions. Polynomial vector fields alone do not ensure that. For example, the formal `r=d=1,K=v=1` reduced system has the exact solution

\[
a=e=-(1-t)^{-2},\quad n=(1-t)^{-2},\quad
p=(1-t)^{-3},\quad R=(1-t)^{-4},
\]

which blows up at `t=1` and is unphysical because two actions are negative. Physical constraint preservation is proved by the lift, rather than inferred from arbitrary numerical initial data.

For feedback `v=φ(t,π_K)`, assume the usual Carathéodory bounds: on each compact state set, both its value bound and its state-Lipschitz constant have an integrable time envelope. The amplitude closed-loop field then has the corresponding local existence and uniqueness bounds, (6) still bounds it, and the same proof gives a unique global closed-loop factorization. Autonomous locally Lipschitz feedback satisfies this on every energy shell. Controller internal states can be appended if their own evolution is well posed.

Continuity alone is insufficient. For `r=d=1,K=1`, initialize `x=y=1,c=0` and use `v=√max(p,0)`. Under unit control the ordinary solution has `p(τ)=τ+O(τ³)` near `τ=0`. Reparameterize it by `dτ/dt=√p(τ)`. Besides the stationary solution, nontrivial solutions can depart after any waiting time, because `∫₀^τ du/√p(u)` is finite. The feedback is continuous but not Lipschitz; uniqueness fails. A bang-bang/sign prescription likewise requires an explicit solution concept and cannot inherit the preceding theorem automatically.
