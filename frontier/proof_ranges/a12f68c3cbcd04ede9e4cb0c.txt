## Prior art and the complete process-level comparator

Electrochemical Cu/C removal from molten iron is already published prior art. Paeng, Judge, and Azimi's 2024 study reports reducing Cu from 0.43 wt% to 790 ppmw (0.079 wt%) with an oxysulfide electrolyte while also removing carbon ([*Resources, Conservation and Recycling* 206, 107654](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2024.107654)). The later Paeng–Azimi 2025 techno-economic study targets HRC at 710 ppmw Cu, <10 ppmw S, and 197 ppmw C from 100% scrap feeds at 0.43 or 0.21 wt% Cu ([article DOI](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.4c02665); local 11-page supporting PDF: `primary/paeng2025-tea-si.pdf`). Its SI gives four one-tonne-basis scenarios, material/energy flows, OPEX, and plant CAPEX. The four case identifiers are preserved as the SI labels; the accessible SI alone does not define the main-text scenario names.

The SI also lists 1,440–1,940 kg of retained flux in cases using it, 60–480 kg of flux residue, and 40.8–56.0 kg of coal or CaC₂ in cases that use a reductant/desulfurizer. Case 4's negative OPEX is after a modeled credit for newly produced Fe; it is not a negative total plant cost. The SI's 2024 unit prices include calcium-aluminate flux $200/t, pyrite $250/t, Fe₂O₃ $104/t, CaC₂ $650/t, ZrB₂ electrode $0.13/g, and waste disposal $24/t. Plant component capital costs include $1,584.33/t capacity for the electrorefining cell, $96.88/t desulfurization, $278.52/t EAF, $273.30/t casting, and $246.53/t hot-strip mill. The article reports levelized HRC costs of $941.73/t for 0.43 wt% Cu scrap and $783.24/t for 0.21 wt% Cu scrap; at then-current HRC price its NPV/IRR are −$5.67m/4.9% and $225m/9.8%, respectively, with electrolyte and scrap costs significant. This is a modeled Cu/C industrial comparator, not a measured energy cost for the 2026 Sn/FeS screen. It is the strongest process-level baseline retrieved here; it already includes product quality, electricity, flux, coproduct Fe credits, OPEX, CAPEX, and an economic result.

The table transcribes the SI's reported case streams and totals; it is not an independent process simulation, nor an elemental closure by this audit. The comparator supplies a much more complete modeled energy/material/economic accounting than the 2026 batch screen, while remaining a modeled Cu/C route with its own assumptions.
