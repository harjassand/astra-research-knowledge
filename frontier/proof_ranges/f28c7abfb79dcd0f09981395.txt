8. What was actually tested
The packet separates proof checks from implementations.
The constant-resolution envelope was tested on 240 random small instances, covering 16,732 spin configurations. All assertions passed. Five four-spin rank-one mass checks, spanning \(K=2^{-40}\) to \(2^{40}\), used the same 611 cells and gave ideal acceptance probabilities between approximately \(0.8772\) and \(0.8824\).
The rejection-free mixture was checked against the complete three-spin output distribution at three interaction strengths. Observed TV errors were approximately
\[
4.26\times10^{-4},
\qquad
4.21\times10^{-8},
\qquad
2.42\times10^{-7},
\]
below the fixture’s proved ideal bound of approximately \(0.03077\).
A separate rank-one product-base prototype compiles and samples without enumerating spin configurations:
Twenty-spin fixture	Compiled cells	Preprocessing	Time for 256 draws
\(K=1/20\)	63,297	4.17 seconds	0.125 seconds
\(K=2^{60}\)	63,297	6.96 seconds	0.101 seconds


These are single-run measurements, not competitive benchmarks. An eight-spin instance also passed a separate exhaustive partition check.
There is an important numerical qualification. An initial all-mpmath moderate-coupling version exceeded a 120-second execution cap. The delivered faster prototype uses a hybrid SciPy/mpmath interval routine. That routine does not yet implement the theorem’s certified, uniform input-bit error contract. General rank and the correlated-residual pipeline are also not implemented.

Completion assessment
The result is more than a constant improvement or a formal extension of the retained Gaussian-crossing family. It supplies a different representation for a discrete equilibrium computation:
\[
\boxed{
\text{arbitrarily strong positive low-rank interaction}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{finite mixture of bounded-field easy models}.
}
\]
The envelope and mixture-error arguments are complete. The proposed fixed-rank bit-complexity theorem has an explicit algorithmic reduction, including acquisition of geometric guarantees, precision, randomness, memory, and output costs.
The remaining validation gates are independent proof review, historical priority, and a certified general implementation with matched benchmarks. The mathematical escalation question is whether weighted-cell aggregation can improve the \(n^{O(r)}\) dependence on a meaningful parameterised or structured growing-rank class; parameterised hardness must be checked rather than assuming such an improvement exists.
The retained Gaussian results are not premises of this construction. The packet is a local research handoff; no GitHub files were changed.
