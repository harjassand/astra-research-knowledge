\subsection{The operation and its exact mathematical power}

Let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^3$ be a bounded connected filled conductor with $C^{1,\beta}$ boundary, $0<\beta<1$. Filling means filling only electrically shielded \emph{closed} cavities, not taking a convex hull or closing open surface recesses. Write $V=|\Omega|$, $m$ for particle mass, and $\alpha$ for its free-space, static, SI electric polarizability tensor: an applied uniform field $e$ induces dipole $p=\alpha e$. It is positive definite. In this convention a solid conducting sphere has $\alpha=3\epsilon_0 V I$.

We import the perfect-conductor endpoint Hashin--Shtrikman inequality
\[
 V\Tr(M^{-1})\le1,\qquad \alpha=\epsilon_0M.
 \tag{T1}
\]
An explicit primary route is Theorem 4.2 of \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2608.20466v1}{Colbrook--Sadeghi, version 1, 20 August 2026}; the paper identifies it as the endpoint of classical finite-contrast bounds. This imported electrostatic inequality is not a new result of this investigation. The following use as a one-sided mass/void certificate is an internal deduction; historical application novelty remains unknown.

\begin{techtheorem}[Conditional low-void acceptance]\label{thm:void}
Suppose the particle is a connected perfect conductor made of one known material of mass density $\rho$, with electrically shielded closed void fraction $\phi$. Then, with $R=\Tr(\alpha)/m$,
\[
 R\ge\frac{9\epsilon_0}{\rho(1-\phi)},\qquad
 \phi\le 1-\frac{9\epsilon_0}{\rho R}.
 \tag{T2}
\]
Consequently an acquired upper bound $R\le R_U$ certifies $\phi\le\phi_*$ whenever
\[
 R_U\le\frac{9\epsilon_0}{\rho(1-\phi_*)}.
 \tag{T3}
\]
This implication holds for every admitted external shape. It is an acceptance certificate; a large response does not prove a void.
\end{techtheorem}
\begin{proof}
For the three positive eigenvalues of $M$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\Tr(M)\Tr(M^{-1})\ge9$. Equation (T1) therefore gives $\Tr(\alpha)\ge9\epsilon_0 V$. Electrostatic uniqueness makes the exterior response unchanged when shielded empty cavities are filled with conductor. Since $m=\rho(1-\phi)V$, division and rearrangement prove the claims.
\end{proof}

An elongated dense conductor can have large $R$ through shape alone; interpreting large $R$ as porosity is invalid. The direction of the bound removes this ambiguity by accepting only small $R$. Dense irregular particles may be rejected, so useful yield is an independent engineering quantity. Open pores, disconnected conducting fragments, conductive bridges that break during the measurement, and unknown inclusions require a different physical model.

\begin{techtheorem}[Explicit uncertainty budget]\label{thm:voidrobust}
Suppose, for each admitted particle separately, that metal density is at most $\rho_{\max}$, nonmetal mass fraction is at most $\eta_{\max}$, and measured physical polarizability satisfies
\[
 \Tr(\alpha_{\rm phys})\ge(1-\epsilon_\alpha)
       \Tr(\alpha_{\rm PEC}),\qquad 0\le\epsilon_\alpha<1.
\]
Let $\phi$ be the closed gas volume fraction of the filled particle, and suppose a correctly covered upper confidence bound $R_U$ on $\Tr(\alpha_{\rm phys})/m$ is acquired. Then
\[
 \phi\le1-\frac{K}{R_U},\qquad
 K=\frac{9\epsilon_0(1-\eta_{\max})(1-\epsilon_\alpha)}{\rho_{\max}}.
 \tag{T4}
\]
The confidence is exactly that of the measurement bound and of its explicitly admitted premises.
\end{techtheorem}
\begin{proof}
The metal volume is at most $(1-\phi)V$, while its mass is at least $(1-\eta_{\max})m$. Thus
$V\ge(1-\eta_{\max})m/[\rho_{\max}(1-\phi)]$.
Apply (T1), the trace bound in the preceding proof, and the physical-response lower bound, then use $R\le R_U$. If the resulting right side is negative, the admitted premises and measurements are inconsistent; that is not a valid automatic acceptance.
\end{proof}

Bulk composition or oxide assays do not provide these \emph{per-particle} premises. A hollow particle with a denser shell can have exactly the same exterior polarizability and total mass as a solid particle of the nominal alloy. A sufficiently reduced electrical response can also hide a mass deficit. Such particles cannot be excluded by this measurement alone. The certificate deliberately exposes rather than solves that acquisition obstruction.

\subsection{Realizable fields and an exact finite-body projection}

The attempted full isotropic force construction fails a Maxwell constraint. For three fields with $E_a(0)=E_0e_a$, their local gradients $G_a$ must be symmetric and trace-free in source-free quasistatic vacuum. Requiring $\sum_aG_a\alpha e_a=c\Tr(\alpha)e_z$ for every symmetric $\alpha$ forces
\[
 (G_a)_{ji}=c(\delta_{jz}\delta_{ia}+\delta_{iz}\delta_{ja}
                         -\delta_{az}\delta_{ji}),
\]
whose trace is $-c\delta_{az}$. Thus this exact full-vector construction is impossible for nonzero $c$. A scalar measurement avoids that requirement.
