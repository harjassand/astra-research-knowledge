The resource distinction is important. Gate evaluation on a supplied configuration requires \(O(L^2)\) work and memory. A sampled successful route has \(O(L)\) macro instructions, but expanding it can cost \(O(L^3)\). The scalar certificate has \(O(L)\) updates with growing integer bit lengths. Affordable exact pointwise evaluation of the fully summed module current is not proved.
The certificate avoids that pointwise evaluation because the requested output—an energy upper bound—can be obtained directly.
The calculation exposed a second obstruction: concentrating a typical configuration into a specified macroscopic empty frame.
Suppose a positive-mass preparation kernel sends equilibrium restricted to an event \(G\), of probability \(\gamma\), into an output density \(r\) supported on an event \(F\), of probability \(\varepsilon\).
Mass conservation and Cauchy–Schwarz imply
\[ \boxed{ \mathbb Er^2\ge\frac{\gamma^2}{\varepsilon}. } \tag{12} \]
Additional history labels cannot remove this marginal concentration after those labels are forgotten.
For one specified \(L\)-square frame,
\[ \varepsilon=q^{2L-1}. \]
At \(q=0.2\), \(L=4096\), this is approximately
\[ 10^{-5725.26}. \]
Two specified frames have probability approximately
\[ 10^{-11450.53}. \]
The balanced strip moment satisfies
\[ V_m(p)=\frac1q+O(m^{-1}) \]
for fixed \(p\). Consequently, the paired frame estimate retains essentially the \(q^{-4L}\) concentration scale, up to factors polynomial in \(L\).
That explains why removing factorial history counting improves the exponent from 52,114 to 11,593 but does not produce a usable bound.
There is an important qualification:
This is a limitation of positive-density preparation into a specified rare frame, not a no-go theorem for every signed source–sink current.
Preparation and return currents may cancel. A representation that proves and exploits those cancellations could escape the preparation estimate. So could a construction that transports through distributed, smaller active regions rather than preparing a whole block boundary.
I have not proved that either possibility is impossible. I have also not constructed the required low-energy composition.
The next object should be a balanced moving-interface current, or a cancellation-aware signed composition, rather than another larger full-frame module.
The required theorem is now precise:
Construct a port-independent gate family with probability high enough for the retained completion, and a legal source–sink grammar satisfying (6), whose whole-current second moment is controlled by the active cooperative interfaces—not by the empty perimeter of the completed block.
The construction must prove how different interface locations and histories recombine. Normalising by the current number of active fronts is not enough: that count generally changes during microscopic moves and does not satisfy the conserved-division lemma.
A particularly consequential target would be an explicit estimate of the form
\[ C_L(1-q)\le \exp(A/q) \]
with suitably controlled gate and completion constants, or a numerically informative module cost at \(p=0.80\).
The significance of that target can be assessed against the specialised baseline. For two-dimensional KA, established bounds have the form
\[ C\,e^{-\lambda q^{-1}\log^2(1/q)} \le D(1-q)\le C'e^{-\lambda'/q}, \]
with positive constants. The present full-frame family does not improve this scaling. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.08495v2)
The preserved tools now address parts of the missing construction: exact cylinder intersections, division by genuinely conserved multiplicity, balanced path kernels with optimal endpoint densities, composable moment bounds, explicit port-label accounting and the existing nonlocal lift.
What remains unresolved is their composition into a low-concentration cooperative current. There is no proved convergence of this dual family to the optimal current, no new two-sided accuracy theorem and no quantitatively useful dense prediction.
The configuration graph is no longer the acquisition bottleneck. The remaining bottleneck is constructing a globally useful current without concentrating transport through an exponentially rare macroscopic frame—or proving cancellations that remove that concentration from the final signed current. The report supplies the explicit constructions and proofs; independent review and publication-priority assessment remain outstanding.
