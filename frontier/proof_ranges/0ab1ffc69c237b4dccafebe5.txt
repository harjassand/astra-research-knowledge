## 4. Nonmonomial features genuinely escape a monomial restriction

The preceding bounds do not justify a broader claim that monomial representations are optimal. I obtained an explicit counterexample to that extrapolation.

Let
\[
a=t+t^4,\qquad
b=t^2+t^6,\qquad
c=t^3+t^5,\qquad
d=t^7,
\]
and define
\[
W=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{1,a,b,c,d\}.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{W^2=\mathbb Q[t]_{\le14},}
\]
where \(W^2\) means the linear span of pairwise products.

Thus five features, multiplied only quadratically, span every polynomial of degree at most fourteen.

### Exact certificate

There are fifteen unordered products of the five displayed generators. Their coefficient matrix in the basis
\[
1,t,\ldots,t^{14}
\]
is a \(15\times15\) integer matrix with determinant
\[
-1.
\]

The full matrix is included in the results file. The research note also gives explicit quadratic reconstruction formulas for every monomial, independently exposing the algebra behind the determinant certificate. For example,
\[
t^8=ab-ac+b^2+bc-cd-ad-c-d.
\]

Consequently, the curve
\[
t\longmapsto(t,t^2,\ldots,t^{14})
\]
is contained in the image of an explicit quadratic polynomial map
\[
\Gamma:\mathbb C^4\to\mathbb C^{14}.
\]

### Five monomial features cannot do this

Suppose a five-dimensional space spanned by monomials had a square containing all fifteen powers \(1,t,\ldots,t^{14}\).

There are only fifteen unordered pair products. Their exponents would therefore have to be distinct and equal exactly
\[
\{0,1,\ldots,14\}.
\]

The generator exponents must lie between \(0\) and \(7\). Producing exponents \(0,14,1,13\) forces the generator set to contain
\[
0,\quad7,\quad1,\quad6.
\]
But then
\[
0+7=1+6
\]
is a collision between distinct unordered pairs, a contradiction.

Six monomial features suffice: the exponent set
\[
\{0,1,2,4,6,7\}
\]
has pairwise sums covering \(0,\ldots,14\).

Therefore the exact minimum feature dimensions in this example are
\[
\boxed{
5\text{ for polynomial features},\qquad
6\text{ for monomial features}.
}
\]

This is a genuine representational distinction. It is **not** a scalable computational speedup. The investigation did not obtain an efficient construction of analogous features for an arbitrary succinct circuit, nor a general zero test operating on them.

It does establish why the additive obstructions above cannot be promoted into a universal impossibility theorem.
