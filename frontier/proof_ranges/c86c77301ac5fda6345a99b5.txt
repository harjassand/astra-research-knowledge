Let
\[ Y=\{y\in\mathbb R^D:Ay\le b\}, \qquad q(y)=\frac12y^\top Hy+g^\top y, \]
where all entries are rational, \(H=H^\top\succeq0\), \(Y\neq\varnothing\), and \(q\) is bounded below on \(Y\).
Let \(P\) select \(d\) observed coordinates. Define
\[ \boxed{ F(x)=\min_{\substack{y\in Y\\Py=x}}q(y), } \tag{1} \]
with \(F(x)=+\infty\) when the fiber is infeasible.
The supplied input is the matrices defining this quadratic program—not an expanded list of regions of \(F\). The latent dimension and all constraints count toward input size.
For a rational initial state \(x_0\in\operatorname{dom}F\), consider
\[ \dot u(t)+\partial F(u(t))\ni0, \qquad u(0)=x_0. \tag{2} \]
Let \(z_*\) be a minimizer of \(F\), and choose a positive rational radius \(R\) with
\[ \|x_0-z_*\|\le R. \]
A suitable minimizer and radius can be acquired from the supplied quadratic program.
For every rational \(0<\epsilon<1\), there is a deterministic algorithm returning rational times and states
\[ 0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T_*, \qquad v_0=x_0,\ldots,v_N=s, \]
such that the polygonal curve \(v\), extended by \(v(t)=s\) after \(T_*\), satisfies
\[ \boxed{ \sup_{t\ge0}\|v(t)-u(t)\|\le\epsilon R. } \tag{3} \]
Moreover,
\[ s\in\arg\min F, \qquad \boxed{\|s-u_\infty\|\le\epsilon R,} \tag{4} \]
where \(u_\infty\) is the equilibrium selected by the exact flow.
The running time and output length are polynomial in the explicit input bit length and \(\epsilon^{-1}\). This uses the established polynomial-bit algorithm for rational convex quadratic programming as an imported computational primitive. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0041555380900981)
The accuracy dependence is on reciprocal error, not merely the number of accuracy bits. For absolute accuracy \(a\), the charged ratio is \(R/a\). This is not a claim of arbitrary precision at logarithmic cost.
No bound on the number of active-regime changes is assumed.
The proof starts with a classical variational object, then makes its construction and computational cost explicit.
