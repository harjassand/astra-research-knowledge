## 6. Quantitative discretisation and statistical costs

The following elementary derivation is included so that no convergence-rate oracle is supplied for free.

Let H=DF and G=-HF+Delta F, where Delta differentiates twice in the real-noise directions. Suppose

\[
\sup_t E\|H(X_t)\|_F^2\le H_2,
\qquad \sup_t E\|G(X_t)\|^2\le G_2.
\]

The bounds below acquire H2 and G2 from coefficients and coordinate moments.

Couple (4.2) and (3.3) with identical Brownian increments. On one step, their endpoint mismatch has residual

\[
\mathcal R_k=\int_{t_k}^{t_k+h}(s-t_k)G(X_s)ds
+\sqrt2\int_{t_k}^{t_k+h}(s-t_k)H(X_s)dB_s.
\]

The drift part has squared expectation at most h^4 G2/4. The martingale part has squared expectation at most 2h^3 H2/3 and has zero cross term with the old adapted error. The resolvent inequality and Young’s inequality give, for h<=1,

\[
E\|e_{k+1}\|^2\le q_h E\|e_k\|^2+q_h^2h^3D_0,
\quad
D_0=G_2/(4\lambda)+G_2/2+4H_2/3.
\]

Summing the geometric series from identical initial states proves

\[
\sup_N(E\|X_{Nh}-Y_N\|^2)^{1/2}
\le h\sqrt{D_0/\lambda}. \tag{6.1}
\]

### Explicit local constants

Set s=2, q=1+2D, g=max(a,c), and

\[
a_0=\max_i\sum_j|A_{ij}|+(u_0+v_0)c_1,
\]
\[
F_p=a_0K_p+qgs^3K_{3p}^3,
\]
\[
H_{loc}=a_0+3qgs^3K_4^2,
\]
\[
G_{loc}=a_0F_2+3qgs^3K_8^2F_4+6qgs^2K_2.
\]

Minkowski, Holder, and the finite support of each quartic give H2<=n Hloc^2 and G2<=n Gloc^2. For example, a quartic Hessian row times F contains (v.X)^2 (v.F); its L2 norm is bounded by s^2 K8^2 times s F4. All terms are explicitly accounted for.

Define

\[
C_{step}=\left[\frac1\lambda\left(
\frac{G_{loc}^2}{4\lambda}+\frac{G_{loc}^2}{2}+
\frac{4H_{loc}^2}{3}\right)\right]^{1/2}.
\]

Then the strong error is at most h sqrt(n) Cstep, and for W

\[
|E W(X_{Nh})-E W(Y_N)|
\le n c_1\sqrt{v_2}\, C_{step}h. \tag{6.2}
\]

A conservative stationary-start coupling gives continuous-time mixing bias at most n c1 v2 exp(-lambda T). The discrete chain has analogous bias at most n c1 v2 q_h^N.

For either real or imaginary part of the endpoint estimator iW(Y_N), Gaussian Poincare applied to the innovations gives

\[
\operatorname{Var}\le n c_1^2v_2/\lambda. \tag{6.3}
\]

This is a variance bound for independent restarted endpoints, not permission to treat consecutive chain observations as independent.

### Parameter quadrature

Put g(t)=i f_W(it). It is analytic whenever it lies in Omega, and |g(t)|<=K=n c1 v2/2. Choose a Bernstein ellipse of radius R>1 for [0,1] whose imaginary semiaxis (R-R^{-1})/4 is below u0 and whose real extent lies within (-v0,v0). On that ellipse the same bound applies. M-point positive Gauss–Legendre quadrature has error bounded by

\[
\frac{4K R^{-(2M-1)}}{R-1}. \tag{6.4}
\]

This follows by approximating g with a polynomial of degree 2M-1 via its Chebyshev series and using positive weights with total mass one. Thus M=O(log(n/epsilon)) for fixed rectangle width. Nodes, weights, and their finite precision are additional computations; a Cauchy derivative bound on a smaller ellipse controls node-rounding error.

### Endpoint count

At every node, use independent trajectories from zero. With variance bound v, a block average over at least 8v/tau^2 endpoints has probability at most 1/8 of coordinate error exceeding tau. Medians of an odd number of blocks give exponential confidence amplification. Both real and imaginary coordinates and all quadrature nodes are union-bounded. There is no inverse-average-phase term.

An explicit conservative allocation uses h<=epsilon/[8n c1 sqrt(v2) Cstep], a burn-in making the mixing error <=epsilon/8, quadrature error <=epsilon/8, and per-coordinate median error <=epsilon/16. Root residuals, Gaussian generation, and quadrature rounding use the remaining budget. The constants can be loosened to rational upper bounds, as in `audit.py`.

For bounded degree, bounded local coefficients, and fixed positive margins, the ideal numbers scale as:

* M=O(log(n/epsilon)) parameter nodes;
* O(n epsilon^{-2} log(M/delta)) independent endpoints per node;
* O(n epsilon^{-1} log(n/epsilon)) implicit steps per endpoint;
* O(n) coefficient arithmetic per sparse drift evaluation.

With the bounded-domain solver below this gives a tilde-O(n^3 epsilon^{-3}) arithmetic construction for absolute error in L, with constants determined by the displayed quantities. Tildes hide logarithms and finite-bit factors, NOT inverse certificate margins or arbitrarily large coefficients.

For error epsilon in the intensive L/n, the step requirement instead has h=O(epsilon), the endpoint variance is O(1/n), and the ideal sparse work is tilde-O(max(n,epsilon^{-2}) epsilon^{-1}), with a minimum number of endpoints for confidence amplification. This is a different and weaker output contract: it does not estimate Z to relative epsilon.
