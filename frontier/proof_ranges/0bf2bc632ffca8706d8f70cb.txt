6. Reconstructing the trajectory and counting entries without a time grid
Once the local position and inward velocity have been sampled, the rest of the trajectory is acquired by Gaussian regression.
Let \(c\) be the complete Fourier-coefficient vector, with covariance \(\Sigma\), and let \(H_{\mathrm{obs}}c=b_{\mathrm{obs}}\) encode the sampled position and normal velocity. Draw an independent original Gaussian coefficient vector \(c_0\), and set
\[
\boxed{
c=
c_0+
\Sigma H_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top
(H_{\mathrm{obs}}\Sigma H_{\mathrm{obs}}^\top)^{-1}
(b_{\mathrm{obs}}-H_{\mathrm{obs}}c_0).
}
\tag{23}
\]
This gives the exact conditional Gaussian law in the ideal-real construction. All regression matrices are computed from the input; their inversion and multiplication costs are charged.
For a facet proposal, rotate the trajectory by an independent uniform phase. Stationarity makes this the required unanchored crossing law. The initial-region component is not randomly rotated.
A finite implementation can preserve the planted facet equality exactly: round latent inputs first, then apply the rational affine parameterization and regression. Uniform phase can be generated from two independent Gaussian variables \(g,h\), using
\[
\cos\theta=\frac{h^2-g^2}{h^2+g^2},
\qquad
\sin\theta=\frac{2gh}{h^2+g^2}.
\tag{24}
\]
For rational \(g,h\), these identities preserve the unit-circle relation exactly.
Exact crossing count for finite coefficients
Put \(z=\tan(t/2)\). For each facet,
\[
P_i(z)
=
(1+z^2)^d
\left[a_i^\top X(2\arctan z)-b_i\right]
\tag{25}
\]
is a rational polynomial of degree at most \(2d\).
At a simple real root, the signs of the other \(P_j\) determine whether the point lies on the actual polyhedral boundary, and the derivative sign determines whether the crossing is inward.
Sturm sequences and exact sign determination therefore compute \(W\) in polynomial bit complexity. The point \(t=\pi\), corresponding to infinity in this parameterization, is handled separately. Exact real-root isolation is established algebraic computation, not another assumed oracle. arXiv
In an anchored facet proposal, one root is planted at \(z=0\). That factor is removed before discriminant tests; it is not mistakenly treated as a numerical degeneracy.
Why arbitrary coefficient rounding is unsafe
A path can make an extremely narrow excursion:
\[
f(t)=(1+2^{-b})\cos(t-\phi)-1.
\]
Its positive interval has width of order \(2^{-b/2}\). A fixed grid can miss it, and careless rounding can remove it.
The finite-bit proof must therefore control changes in crossing topology under the proposal distribution, not merely assert that tangencies have probability zero.
7. Finite precision without an inverse-probability penalty
The relevant bad events are algebraic: a multiple root, simultaneous crossings of two facets, a root at an endpoint used by the counter, or degeneration of the phase parameterization.
After eliminating the deliberately planted equality, these events are zeros of nonzero polynomials in the sampled latent variables. Their degrees and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in \(L,m,d\). Full positive-definite Fourier blocks and genuine facets ensure that no additional crossing degeneracy is forced.
Two estimates make finite precision sufficient.
First: the proposal density is not exponentially large in the rarity exponent
For each local distribution, subtracting \(q_*\) gives density proportional to \(e^{-f}\). The constructed interior ball has polynomial-bit radius and \(f\le1\), so its normalizer is at least
\[
2^{-\operatorname{poly}(L)}.
\]
Hence the normalized density is at most \(2^{\operatorname{poly}(L)}\). Gaussian regression variables obey analogous bounds.
Equation (19) and Gaussian tails confine the latent variables to a box of polynomial-bit radius except with any prescribed small probability.
Crucially, this bound does not divide an unconditional estimate by the original tiny facet probability.
Second: a polynomial is unlikely to be extremely close to zero
For a nonzero univariate polynomial with leading coefficient \(c\) and degree \(h\),
\[
\operatorname{length}\{x:|P(x)|\le u\}
\le
2h(u/|c|)^{1/h}.
\]
To see this, factor over the complex numbers. If the product of the \(h\) root distances is at most \(u/|c|\), at least one distance is at most \((u/|c|)^{1/h}\).
Conditioning successively on variables gives a corresponding multivariate bound. Comparing the bounded joint density on the latent box with uniform measure produces, for each nonzero bad-event polynomial \(P\),
\[
\Pr(|P|\le u)
\le
2^{\operatorname{poly}(L,m,d)}
u^{1/\operatorname{poly}(m,d)}
\tag{26}
\]
after incorporating its polynomial-bit coefficient lower bound.
Thus
\[
\boxed{
b_{\mathrm{work}}
=
\operatorname{poly}\!\left(
L,m,d,\log(K_*/\varepsilon)
\right)
}
\tag{27}
\]
bits suffice to preserve the required crossing count except with probability \(O(\varepsilon/K_*)\).
Polynomial derivative bounds on the same box turn the small-ball estimate into a rounding-stability bound. No minimum crossing separation is supplied as a promise.
For a conditional waveform output, adding precision polynomial in \(\log\zeta^{-1}\) controls the requested uniform waveform error.
