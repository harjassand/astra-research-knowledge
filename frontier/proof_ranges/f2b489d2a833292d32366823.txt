# Full amplitude minimization does not convexify the remaining phase

Date: 15 October 2026. Worker: phase_geometry.

Status: internally derived analytic construction, accompanied by exact finite
constant checks. External correctness review and historical novelty are UNKNOWN.
This is a representation obstruction for the convex energy `F(A)=|A|^4`.
It is not a counterexample to quasiconvexity or to Morrey's conjecture.

## 1. The reduced functional being tested

Write normalized integrals on a `2*pi` cell as `<...>`. For a prescribed real
phase theta and a nonnegative amplitude r, set

    u = r (cos theta, sin theta),
    E_theta(r) = < (|grad r|^2 + r^2 |grad theta|^2)^2 >.

The equality is the exact chain rule for `F(A)=|A|^4`. Define the reduced value

    J(theta) = inf { E_theta(r): r >= 0, <r^4> = 1 },       (1.1)

with periodic amplitudes, or with a specified common boundary value below.
Every infimum in this note is over the entire indicated Sobolev amplitude
class, rather than a Fourier truncation. The fixed-phase density convexity
used below is the supplied perspective theorem, which here also follows
directly from convexity of `|A|^4` on fixed-direction density interpolations.

The incoming note disproves *joint* phase-density convexity at a selected
density. That does not by itself disprove convexity after minimizing out the
density. The construction below does disprove the latter.

## 2. A quantitative one-dimensional lemma

For a `2*pi`-periodic nonnegative `r` with `<r^4>=1`, put

    L_t(r) = < (r'^2 + t^2 cos^2(y) r^2)^2 >,
    lambda_0 = 1/(4*pi)^4.

For every `0 <= z <= 1`,

    L_(z/4)(r) >= lambda_0 z^4.                          (2.1)

Proof. Let `d=<r'^4>`. If `d >= lambda_0`, the claim is immediate. Otherwise,
the one-dimensional Sobolev estimate gives

    osc r <= integral |r'| <= 2*pi*d^(1/4) < 1/2.

Because `sup r >= 1`, we have `r >= 1/2`. Consequently,

    L_(z/4)(r)
      >= (z/4)^4 <r^4 cos^4 y>
      >= (z/4)^4 (1/16) (3/8)
       = 3 z^4/32768.

The elementary bound `pi>3` implies
`lambda_0 < 1/20736 < 3/32768`. This proves (2.1).
No spectral theorem or numerically estimated eigenvalue is used.

### Exact high-frequency scaling

For an integer `N>=1`, minimizing

    < (r'^2 + (z*N/4)^2 cos^2(Ny) r^2)^2 >

at mass one has value exactly `N^4` times the corresponding value at `N=1`.
Indeed, average the density `rho=r^4` over translations by `2*pi*j/N`.
Fixed-phase density convexity decreases the energy, keeps the mass, and makes
the amplitude `2*pi/N` periodic. The change of variable `t=Ny` now factors out
`N^4`. Conversely every base-cell amplitude yields an admissible amplitude
`r(y)=s(Ny)`, proving equality rather than merely a bound.

Thus, for any nonnegative periodic r of mass m,

    < (r'^2 + (z*N/4)^2 cos^2(Ny) r^2)^2 >
       >= N^4 lambda_0 z^4 m.                           (2.2)

The same bound holds for amplitudes whose endpoint traces at `0` and `2*pi`
agree, because they define periodic Sobolev amplitudes. It does not require
that their derivatives match at the endpoints.

## 3. A short periodic counterexample, with explicit N=32

Let chi be a real periodic `C^1` cutoff, `0<=chi<=1`, which is zero on
`A=[0,pi/2]` and one on `B=[pi,3*pi/2]`. Such a cutoff can be chosen smooth.
Set

    theta_0(y) = (1/4) sin(32 y),
    theta_+(y) = 2 chi(y) theta_0(y),
    theta_-(y) = 2(1-chi(y)) theta_0(y).

Then `theta_0=(theta_++theta_-)/2`. All phases are real, periodic, have zero
winding, and obey `|theta_+|,|theta_-|<=1/2`.

Use the amplitudes

    q_A(y) = (32/3)^(1/4) sin(2y),   y in [0,pi/2],
           = 0,                    otherwise,
    q_B(y) = q_A(y-pi).

They belong to `W^(1,4)`, and exact integration gives

    <q_A^4> = <q_B^4> = 1,
    <q_A'^4> = <q_B'^4> = 16.                           (3.1)

On the support of q_A, theta_+ vanishes. On the support of q_B, theta_- vanishes.
Therefore `J(theta_+)<=16` and `J(theta_-)<=16`. In contrast, (2.2) gives

    J(theta_0) >= 32^4/(4*pi)^4 = (8/pi)^4 > 16,          (3.2)

where the last inequality uses only `pi<4`. Thus J is not convex.

The same proof applies on the two-dimensional torus: averaging the density
over x translations eliminates x dependence without increasing energy.

There are no nonzero winding numbers, large phase values, negative amplitudes,
sampled positivity conditions, or finite-dimensional optimization premises.
Replacing supported amplitudes by positive density mixtures shows that the
infimum over strictly positive amplitudes has the same endpoint upper bounds.

For every prescribed phase-amplitude bound eta>0, replace `1/4` by a positive
epsilon small enough that `2*epsilon<eta`, use the analogous positive
one-cell bound `min(lambda_0,3*epsilon^4/128)`, and then choose a sufficiently
large integer N. Therefore no `L^infinity` neighborhood of the zero phase
gives convexity of the fully reduced periodic functional. This assertion does
not claim a bound on derivatives of the phase.

## 4. The obstruction with common affine boundary and positive amplitudes

This construction meets the boundary setting of the incoming phase reduction.
Let

    Omega = (0,2*pi)^2,
    N = 1024,
    delta^4 = 1/(4*N^4),
    psi(x) = sin(x/2).

Use normalized area integrals. Define

    theta_0(x,y) = (1/4) psi(x) sin(Ny),
    theta_+ = 2 chi(y) theta_0,
    theta_- = 2(1-chi(y)) theta_0.                        (4.1)

Choose chi as above with `|chi'|<=1`. For example, on the two transition arcs
of length `pi/2`, use the cubic step `H(t)=3t^2-2t^3`, reversing its direction
on the second transition. Its derivative is at most `3/pi<1`, and the resulting
periodic cutoff is `C^1`. A smooth cutoff with the same plateaus and derivative
bound exists by smoothing within the strict derivative margin.

The phases vanish on every boundary side, have midpoint theta_0, and obey
`|theta_+|,|theta_-|<=1/2`. Consequently all their directions lie strictly in
one open half-plane.

For each phase define the complete reduced value

    J_delta(theta) = inf { E_theta(r):
        r>=0, r|boundary=delta, <r^4>=1 }.                (4.2)

The corresponding vector map has the same affine boundary `u=delta*e_1`
for every phase: here `B=0` and `c=delta*e_1`. The subproblem is attained by
the supplied fixed-phase existence theorem. We do not assume that its
minimizer is positive; the endpoint competitors below are uniformly positive.

### Endpoint upper bounds

Put

    p(x) = (8/3)^(1/4) sin(x/2),
    r_A(x,y) = p(x) q_A(y),
    r_B(x,y) = p(x) q_B(y).

Both r_A and r_B vanish on the boundary, have fourth mass one, and are supported
in the respective flat-phase strips. Exact separated integrations yield

    <p^4> = 1,       <p'^4> = 1/16,      <p'^2 p^2> = 1/12,
    <q_A^4> = 1,     <q_A'^4> = 16,      <q_A'^2 q_A^2> = 4/3.

Hence

    <|grad r_A|^4> = <|grad r_B|^4>
      = 1/16 + 2(1/12)(4/3) + 16 = 2345/144.            (4.3)

Now take positive densities

    rho_+ = (1-delta^4) r_A^4 + delta^4,
    rho_- = (1-delta^4) r_B^4 + delta^4,
    r_+ = rho_+^(1/4),   r_- = rho_-^(1/4).

They have fourth mass one, boundary amplitude delta, and satisfy `r_+,r_->=delta`.
Fixed-phase density convexity and the flat supports give

    E_theta_+(r_+) <= (1-delta^4)(2345/144) + delta^4 E_theta_+(1),
    E_theta_-(r_-) <= (1-delta^4)(2345/144) + delta^4 E_theta_-(1).

The derivative bounds `|psi'|<=1/2`, `|chi'|<=1` imply

    |partial_x theta_+|, |partial_x theta_-| <= 1/4,
    |partial_y theta_+|, |partial_y theta_-| <= (N+1)/2,
    |grad theta_+|, |grad theta_-| <= N,     N>=2.

Thus `E_theta_+(1),E_theta_-(1)<=N^4`, and

    J_delta(theta_+), J_delta(theta_-)
       <= 2345/144 + 1/4 = 2381/144 < 17.               (4.4)

### Midpoint lower bound for every amplitude

Suppose an amplitude admissible in (4.2) has `E_theta_0(r)<=17`. The chain-rule
energy gives `<|partial_x r|^4><=17`.

Let `a=1/4` and remove the two x-boundary strips of width a. For each fixed y,
the boundary value r=delta and Holder's inequality imply, on the left strip,

    r(x,y)^4 <= 8 delta^4 + 8 x^3 integral_0^a |partial_x r(t,y)|^4 dt.

Integrating x and treating the right strip in the same way bounds their total
normalized fourth mass by

    M_boundary <= (8a/pi) delta^4 + 2a^4 <|partial_x r|^4>
                <= 1/24 + 17/128 < 1/2.                (4.5)

We used `pi>3` and `delta^4<=1/16`, both vastly weaker than the chosen constants.
At least half of the fourth mass therefore lies in `a<=x<=2*pi-a`. On this
interior region concavity of sine gives `psi(x)>=a/pi`. Consequently

    <r^4 psi^4> >= a^4/(2*pi^4) = 1/(512*pi^4).          (4.6)

For almost every fixed x, the y-amplitude has equal endpoint values delta and
defines a periodic Sobolev amplitude. Apply (2.2) there with `z=psi(x)`, then
integrate x. Dropping the other nonnegative terms in the full energy gives

    E_theta_0(r)
      >= N^4 lambda_0 <r^4 psi^4>
      >= N^4/(131072*pi^8)
       > N^4/2^33 = 128,                                (4.7)

where `pi<4` and `N=2^10` were used. This contradicts `E_theta_0(r)<=17`.

Attainment of (4.2) now gives

    J_delta(theta_0) > 17
      > (J_delta(theta_+)+J_delta(theta_-))/2.             (4.8)

Even without using attainment, the argument rules out every admissible
competitor of energy <=17 and proves the non-strict bound `J_delta(theta_0)>=17`,
which already suffices for the strict nonconvexity inequality because (4.4)
is strictly below 17.

The endpoint construction uses C^1 phases and positive C^1 amplitudes, well
inside the analytic Sobolev domain. It may be approximated by smooth phases
and densities preserving their fixed boundary, plateaus, and mass; the strict
gap leaves room for this approximation. The derivative bound on chi has a
strict margin, and smooth approximations of the compactly supported bumps
converge strongly in W^(1,4), so (4.3)-(4.4) persist with an arbitrarily small
increase. The midpoint itself is smooth and is unchanged by this smoothing.

## 5. What the construction diagnoses

Global amplitude minimization can choose spatially different places to store
the mass for different phases. Each endpoint phase has a macroscopic strip
where it is flat. Their midpoint has a fast oscillatory gradient on essentially
the entire domain. Changing the phase can therefore switch the location of
the preferred amplitude. This effect survives strict amplitude positivity,
the fixed mass, a common affine boundary, zero winding, and a half-plane chart.

It refutes the following potential continuations:

* Inferring convexity of the reduced phase value from convexity of the two
  separate blocks or from globally solving the amplitude block.
* Treating a half-plane chart or small phase values alone as a global phase
  convexity hypothesis.
* Excluding phase nonconvexity solely by proving that trial amplitudes stay
  uniformly positive; the positive endpoint densities here satisfy that.

It does not refute a phase-lowering operation using additional structure,
a nonconvex but globally controlled phase inequality, an adequate nonlinear
nonlocal flux certificate, or quasiconvexity. In this example every actual
energy is nonnegative because F is convex. No mixed-phase Jensen gap below
the affine energy has been constructed.

## 6. Reproducibility and attribution

Run `python3 verify_phase_geometry.py` in this worker directory. It checks the
trigonometric mass and energy constants, cutoff derivative bound, all rational
threshold comparisons, and the exact G acoustic/soft-potential calculations
described in `TRANSLATION_GAUGE_AND_SOFT_OPERATOR.md`. It does not establish the
analytic compactness, density averaging, or Sobolev arguments by executing them.
Those arguments are proved above rather than delegated to the checker.

The underlying hidden-convexity and Picone machinery is prior work:
[Brasco and Franzina, arXiv:1403.0280v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1403.0280v2),
Sections 2 and 3. The present counterexample is internally derived here; no
comprehensive search establishing its priority was performed.

The currently relevant special quasiconvexity theorem has a smaller symmetry
class: [Bruno and Pasqualotto, arXiv:2608.06367v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1)
prove the Dacorogna-Marcellini result and the both-left-and-right rotational
quartic result. Neither that theorem nor this counterexample closes the full
left-invariant quartic phase problem.
