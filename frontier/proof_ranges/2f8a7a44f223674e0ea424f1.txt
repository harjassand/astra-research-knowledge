## 3. Full-rank compatible covariance realization

Let $r_\pm=R_\pm/\sqrt D$ and
\[
C_e=\tfrac12(r_+r_+^T+r_-r_-^T).
\]
The two nonzero eigenvalues are $(1\pm r_e)/2$, where $r_e=(R_+:R_-)/D$. For small $e$, $r_e>0$, so the anisotropy is
\[
\tau_e=1-\lambda_{\max}(C_e)=\frac{13e^2}{D}.
\]
The averaged Hessian is $H_G(C_e)=Q_e/(2D)$, hence
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e))}{e^4}\to\frac{65}{6},\qquad
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e))}{\tau_e^2}\to\frac5{78}.
\]

The two-mode covariance is realized by smooth periodic gradients for $e=1/(2m)$, $m\in\mathbb N$. Set
\[
K_+=(1,m),\quad K_-=(-1,m),\quad a_+=(1,3e),\quad a_-=(1,-3e)
\]
and
\[
\varphi_m(x,y)=
\frac{a_+\sin(2\pi K_+\cdot(x,y))+a_-\sin(2\pi K_-\cdot(x,y))}
{2\pi m\sqrt D}.
\]
Differentiating gives exactly
\[
D\varphi_m=r_+\cos(2\pi K_+\cdot(x,y))
+r_-\cos(2\pi K_-\cdot(x,y)).
\]
The integer frequency matrix with rows $K_+,K_-$ has determinant $2m\ne0$, so its torus homomorphism is onto and pushes Haar measure to Haar measure. The two phases are independent uniform phases. Their cosine means vanish, second moments are $1/2$, and cross moments vanish. Every cubic monomial has an odd power of at least one centered phase, so $b_G=\mathbb E DG(D\varphi_m)=0$. Consequently $C_{D\varphi_m}=C_e$ and $H_G(D\varphi_m)=H_G(C_e)$ exactly. The pointwise bound $|D\varphi_m|_F\le2$ gives uniformly bounded gradients and fourth moments, hence fourth-moment equiintegrability. This two-mode construction has rank-two covariance.

There is a full-rank version using two smaller modes. For $m\ge3$, with $e=1/(2m)$, add to the potential
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
e^3\sin(6\pi x)/(6\pi)\\
e^3\sin(8\pi x)/(8\pi)
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its gradient adds $e^3E_{11}\cos(2\pi(3x))+e^3E_{21}\cos(2\pi(4x))$, where $E_{11}$ and $E_{21}$ are the row-major coordinate vectors for the matrices with a single $1$ in positions $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$. The four integer frequencies are $K_+,K_-,(3,0),(4,0)$. They are pairwise distinct up to sign for $m\ge3$, so their cosine modes are orthogonal and
\[
C_e^{\rm full}=C_e+\frac{e^6}{2}(E_{11}E_{11}^T+E_{21}E_{21}^T),
\qquad M_2:=\mathbb E|D\varphi_m|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}C_e^{\rm full}=1+e^6.
\]
The four amplitude vectors span $\mathbb R^4$; the exact determinant of the matrix with columns $r_+,r_-,e^3E_{11},e^3E_{21}$ is $6e^7/D$. Hence $\det C_e^{\rm full}=9e^{14}/(4D^2)>0$. The covariance is balanced as well: $\mathbb E\det(D\varphi_m)=(C_e^{\rm full})_{14}-(C_e^{\rm full})_{23}=0$ in row-major coordinates.

The four phases are not jointly independent on a two-torus, and independence is not needed. Since $DG$ is homogeneous cubic, its mean vanishes if no signed sum of three selected frequencies is zero. A signed sum has zero $y$-coordinate only if the number of old modes $K_\pm$ is even and their signed $m$-coordinates cancel. With one or three old modes the $y$-coordinate is a nonzero odd multiple of $m$. With two old modes, cancellation leaves their signed $x$-sum in $\{-2,0,2\}$, which cannot be canceled by the remaining $x$-frequency $\pm3$ or $\pm4$. With no old modes, three signed values from $\{3,4\}$ cannot sum to zero. Thus there is no cubic resonance and $b_G=0$ exactly.

Quadratic dependence of $D^2G$ on its argument and Fourier orthogonality give
\[
H_G(C_e^{\rm full})=H_G(C_e)+\frac{e^6}{2}\big(D^2G(E_{11})+D^2G(E_{21})\big).
\]
Both added Hessian matrices are positive definite (the first four leading principal minors are positive), and $H_G(C_e)\succ0$ for $e>0$ by the exact acoustic blocks above. In particular $C_e^{\rm full}$ and $H_G(C_e^{\rm full})$ are both positive definite. Weyl's inequality and the $O(e^6)$ covariance/Hessian perturbations imply
\[
\tau_e^{\rm full}:=1-\lambda_{\max}(C_e^{\rm full}/M_2)=13e^2+O(e^4),\qquad
\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e^{\rm full}))=\frac{65}{6}e^4+o(e^4).
\]
Consequently
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_G(C_e^{\rm full}))}{M_2(\tau_e^{\rm full})^2}\longrightarrow\frac{65}{1014}=\frac5{78}.
\]
This upgrades the covariance obstruction to actual smooth compatible gradients with full-rank $C$ and $H$, bounded gradient (indeed $|D\varphi_m|_F\le2+2e^3$), and uniformly integrable fourth moments: no lower bound $\lambda_{\min}H_G(C)\ge c M_2\tau^\alpha$ can hold along this family for $\alpha<2$. The pairwise acoustic-modulus bound above remains a pair-test upper bound, not a universal modulus theorem. These fields have growing Fourier bandwidth and do not give strong-$L^4$ convergence to a fixed one-line field, a Schur-loss discontinuity, or a negative Jensen gap.
