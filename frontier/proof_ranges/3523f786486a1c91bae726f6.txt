## 4. An exact exponential mixing obstruction

The sampler in (4) can be analysed completely on an even cycle.

Let \(G=C_{2n}\), with unit weights. It has exactly two perfect matchings, \(M_0\) and \(M_1\).

Starting from \(M_0\), there are \(2^n\) equally likely compatible bipartitions. Only two are also compatible with \(M_1\): the two proper two-colourings of the entire cycle.

On either shared bipartition, the exact conditional matching distribution chooses \(M_1\) with probability \(1/2\). Therefore,

\[
K(M_0,M_1)=2^{-n}.
\]

By symmetry,

\[
\boxed{
K=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-2^{-n}&2^{-n}\\
2^{-n}&1-2^{-n}
\end{pmatrix}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

The spectral gap is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{gap}(K)=2^{1-n}.}
\tag{9}
\]

Starting at \(M_0\), the total variation distance after \(t\) steps is exactly

\[
\left\|K^t(M_0,\cdot)-\pi_G\right\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
=
\frac12(1-2^{1-n})^t.
\]

Thus constant-accuracy sampling takes \(\Theta(2^n)\) steps.

This obstruction does not depend on numerical precision. Even an arbitrary conditional procedure that always returns a matching of \(G_S\) cannot switch phases unless the shared bipartition was drawn, an event of probability \(2^{1-n}\).

Nor does repeating the same conditional operation polynomially many times remove that exponential event.

This is a failure of the proposed mechanism, not evidence that counting this family is difficult: an even cycle is trivial to count directly.

## 5. Pursuing the global-move repair

The immediate repair is to flip a whole alternating cycle:

\[
M' = M\triangle C.
\]

The even-cycle example is then solved in one move. I therefore investigated whether an implementable global-cycle proposal could replace the problematic bipartition overlap.

### Simple alternating walks still encounter exponential barriers

Consider the following family \(H_r\).

Start with a cycle on \(2r\) base vertices. For each base edge \(uv\), retain \(uv\) and add a parallel length-three path

\[
u-a-b-v.
\]

The resulting graph has \(6r\) vertices. All its edges belong to some perfect matching.

Each four-cycle gadget has two relevant states:

- Neither terminal is matched inside it: use \(ab\), giving one configuration.
- Both terminals are matched inside it: use either \(\{uv,ab\}\) or \(\{ua,bv\}\), giving two configurations.

Exactly one incident gadget must cover each base vertex. Consequently, the active gadgets form one of the two perfect matchings of the base cycle.

The graph therefore has two equally weighted phases, each containing \(2^r\) perfect matchings:

\[
Z_{H_r}=2^{r+1}.
\]

Now consider an alternating-walk proposal that chooses a nonmatching edge uniformly, follows the forced matching edge, and stops or rejects on its first repeated matching pair.

To change phases, it must traverse the entire base cycle. At each newly traversed active gadget—apart from a possible initial gadget—there is a choice among three nonmatching edges. One choice closes a local alternating loop before the global traversal finishes.

Thus a phase-changing proposal has probability at most

\[
\left(\frac23\right)^{r-1}.
\tag{10}
\]

A rejection filter cannot increase that probability.

The bipartition proposal also remains exponentially suppressed. Compatibility with the other phase imposes opposite-colour constraints across its \(r\) active base edges. Under a random orientation of the current matching, these constraints have rank at least \(r-1\), giving

\[
\Pr(\text{bipartition permits the other phase})
\le 2^{1-r}.
\tag{11}
\]

Therefore a combination of these two proposal mechanisms, making only polynomially many attempts per step, still has exponentially small phase-crossing probability. Any such chain preserving the uniform target has an exponential worst-case mixing time.

This is a stronger failure than the original long-cycle example: allowing long moves is not enough when generating those moves repeatedly chooses local detours.

### But this family can be compressed exactly

The next representation change does remove this obstruction.

For a gadget with weights

\[
w_{uv}=d,\quad
w_{ua}=p,\quad
w_{ab}=q,\quad
w_{bv}=s,
\qquad q>0,
\]

its inactive weight is \(q\), and its active weight is \(dq+ps\).

Removing \(a,b\), replacing the edge \(uv\) by weight

\[
d+\frac{ps}{q},
\]

and multiplying the partition function by \(q\) is exact.

For \(H_r\), every gadget becomes an edge of weight \(2\). The entire graph reduces to a weighted cycle, whose count is \(2^{r+1}\).

This is a real constructive elimination operation with explicit rational arithmetic. It also means the preceding obstruction **cannot** justify rejecting algorithms that first integrate small separable regions.

What remains unproved is a general replacement for this elimination when the interacting region has many boundary vertices. Explicitly storing all boundary matching states introduces exponentially many entries. I did not obtain a representation closed under the necessary operations with polynomial construction and update cost.

There is relevant prior warning here: the failure of perfect/near-perfect matching chains in general graphs is not merely a missing analysis of the bipartite proof. Štefankovič, Vigoda, and Wilmes established torpid mixing for all hole-pattern weighting schemes within the JSV-type chain they study. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.07504v1)
