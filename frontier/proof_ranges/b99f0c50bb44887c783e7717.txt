## 5. The full-cone averaged-Hessian rank gap

Let J_det be the symmetric 4x4 matrix representing the determinant in row-major coordinates, with (J_det)_{14}=(J_det)_{41}=1/2 and (J_det)_{23}=(J_det)_{32}=-1/2. Let

    K={ C>=0 : tr(J_det C)=0 }.

Because D^2F(X) is quadratic in X, its average depends only on C=E XX^T. Denote this linear map by H_F(C). Every C in K is the second moment of a finite centered law, for example by its spectral decomposition with symmetric +/- atoms. Theorem A gives H_F(C)>=0.

### Theorem B: exact exceptional-class characterization

For every ROC F in the full left-SO(2) quartic cone, the following are equivalent:

1. There is C in K of rank at least three with H_F(C) singular.
2. There are three linearly independent rank-one matrices R1,R2,R3 such that sum_j D^2F(Rj) is singular.
3. F belongs to E in (4.1).

In particular,

    F not in E and rank C>=3  =>  H_F(C)>0.                  (5.1)

This characterizes an exact exception, not a sufficient inequality imposed on a chosen coefficient slice.

**Proof of 1 => 3.** Choose A!=0 in ker H_F(C). By Lemma 2.1,

    D^2F(A)[X,X]=P_A(X)+lambda_A det X, P_A>=0.

The moment identity and reciprocity give tr(P_A C)=0. For two PSD matrices this implies ran C is contained in ker P_A. Hence rank P_A<=4-rank C<=1. Lemma B proves F in E.

**Proof of 2 => 1.** Take C=sum_j vec(Rj)vec(Rj)^T. It is PSD of rank three and has zero determinant moment. By definition its image under H_F is the displayed Hessian sum.

**Proof of 3 => 2.** Write F=gamma q^2+|ell(A)|^4, where ell is a complex-linear functional of the two complex columns. Choose A!=0 in its real kernel. For rank-one R,

    D^2F(R)[A,A]=2gamma[(cof A):R]^2.

There are three linearly independent rank-one matrices in the hyperplane (cof A):R=0. If A is invertible, reduce to A=I; one possible triple is

    R1=[[0,1],[0,0]], R2=[[0,0],[1,0]],
    R3=[[1,1],[-1,-1]].

They are trace-free, rank one, and independent. If A has rank one, reduce it to e_11; use e_11,e_12,e_21. Invertible input/output coordinate changes preserve rank and the polar determinant condition, so these constructions cover every nonzero A. For gamma=0 any independent triple of rank-one matrices suffices. Each D^2F(Rj) is PSD by reciprocity, and vanishes in the A direction. Their sum is singular. QED.

### Sharp compatible example for the exceptional class

The trace-free triple above is not merely an arbitrary law. It is realized by

    phi(x,y)=(sin y+sin(x+y), sin x-sin(x+y)),
    Dphi=R1 cos y+R2 cos x+R3 cos(x+y).

For F=q^2+|w|^4 in the complex coordinates of Section 4, the resulting covariance has rank three and the averaged Hessian has rank three with I in its kernel. The verifier checks both ranks exactly. Thus the exception is necessary even for an actual three-direction resonant gradient.
