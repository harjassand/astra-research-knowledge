### 8.3 All finite classical channels

Let \(W(y|x)\) be a stochastic matrix, viewed as a measure-and-prepare quantum channel. Its Choi matrix is diagonal. Averaging the center over output phases makes it diagonal without increasing the convex objective. Formula (8.3) reduces to
\[
\boxed{\quad
\eta(W)=1-\min_{\theta\in\Delta_X}
\sum_{y:\,W(y|x)>0\ \forall x}
\left(\sum_x\frac{\theta_x}{W(y|x)}\right)^{-1}.
\quad} \tag{8.5}
\]
An empty sum is zero. This is contraction with quantum side information. It is not, in general, the classical KL contraction coefficient. The formula remains sharp when the input register is required to be classical. To see this in the proof, \(\mathbf A\) and \(\mathbf G\) are block-diagonal in the input alphabet. Dephasing \(C\) in that alphabet preserves every minimax objective in (4.4). The weighted projection in (4.3) then supplies a block-diagonal \(B\). The coherent-flag states (7.1) are consequently classical on the channel input, as required.

For the exclusion channel on \(k\ge3\) symbols,
\[
W_k(y|x)=\frac{\mathbf1_{y\ne x}}{k-1},
\]
every output column has a zero, so \(\eta(W_k)=1\). In contrast, the ordinary classical KL coefficient is \(1/(k-1)\); a self-contained proof is in CLASSICAL_EXAMPLES.md. A finite rational ternary example with a qubit reference is certified in exact_triangle_certificate.py.

For a symmetric channel with diagonal \(a>0\), off-diagonal \(b>0\), and \(a+(k-1)b=1\), symmetry and convexity yield
\[
\eta(W)=1-\frac{k^2}{1/a+(k-1)/b}. \tag{8.6}
\]
For example \(k=3,a=3/5,b=1/5\) gives \(8/35\), rather than the ordinary classical value \(1/5\).
