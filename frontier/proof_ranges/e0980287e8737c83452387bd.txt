## 1. Two-contact classification reconstructed

Let $F$ be a homogeneous quartic on $\mathbb R^{2\times2}$, rank-one convex, and let $q(A)=\det A$. In the full 35-coefficient planar quartic space, reciprocity gives
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=D^2F(X)[A,A].
\]
Applying the indefinite non-strict Finsler lemma to the right side yields, for each fixed $A$,
\[
D^2F(A)[X,X]=P_A(X)+\lambda_Aq(X),\qquad P_A\succeq0.
\]
For any balanced second-moment matrix $C\succeq0$ with $\operatorname{tr}(J_{\det}C)=0$, this implies $H_F(C)\succeq0$. If $A\in\ker H_F(C)$, then $\operatorname{ran}C\subseteq\ker P_A$. At rank-one acoustic contacts, global nonnegativity of $D^2F(A+tY)[R,R]$ forces its first derivative to vanish. Cubic tensor symmetry then gives the cubic closure used in the classification. For arbitrary balanced laws, the same argument gives $b_F\in\operatorname{ran}H_F(C)$, so the background minimum is the exact Schur expression, but its sign is not implied.

For the complete left-SO(2)-invariant nine-coefficient quartic cone, the source theorem classifies the rank-two singular case as follows. If some balanced $C$ of rank at least two has singular $H_F(C)$, choose $A\in\ker H_F(C)$; then the Finsler remainder has rank at most two. The proof separates rank-one and invertible $A$, then rank-one, generic, and singular top-block cases after normalization. Rank-one $A$ reduces to a convex binary quartic plus determinant terms, with acoustic PSD tests eliminating the nonconvex cross terms. For invertible $A$, normalize $A=I$, write the quartic in complex variables, and split by the rank of the positive remainder and the determinant form on its kernel plane. An indefinite determinant restriction is contradicted by the cubic-closure coefficient $-4\lambda^3a^3/(r^2p^3)$; a totally null plane yields the convex sum $f|z+w|^4+(g-f)|w|^4$; singular top-block cases are excluded by rank-one acoustic tests. The surviving integrands are exactly
\[
\mathcal P_2=
\{\gamma(\det A)^2+|\alpha x_{\mathbb C}+\beta y_{\mathbb C}|^4:\gamma\ge0\}
\;\cup\;
\{a|Ar|^4+|\alpha x_{\mathbb C}+\beta y_{\mathbb C}|^4:
a\ge0,\ r\in\mathbb R^2\},
\]
where $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb C$ are arbitrary. Conversely, each displayed class supplies two independent rank-one contacts. Thus, within this scope, existence of a rank-two balanced singular Hessian, existence of two independent rank-one Hessian contacts, and membership in $\mathcal P_2$ are equivalent. In particular, if $F\notin\mathcal P_2$, every balanced $C$ of rank at least two has $H_F(C)\succ0$.

The larger nullspace-closure statement applies to all 35 planar quartic coefficients, but the final exceptional-class and two-contact classification is only for the nine-coefficient left-SO(2) cone. This theorem does not prove nonnegativity of the positive-definite Schur remainder, full Morrey, or quasiconvexity for the whole cone.
