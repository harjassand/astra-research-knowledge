## 7. A reference-pairing-only contrast

Take two independent copies of

\[
 \rho_{RSE}=|0\rangle\langle0|_S\otimes|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|_{RE}.
\]

Both data registers are blank `|0>`. Exchanging them leaves the **entire physical density operator unchanged**. The allowed future operation swaps each resident `E_i` into `S_i`. It retrieves a Bell pair between resident `R_i` and `S_i`.

- Scoring `S_i` against resident `R_i` gives Bell success one, before or after data exchange.
- Scoring after the derangement against donor `R_j`, `j != i`, gives a maximally mixed two-qubit state and Bell success `1/4`.

The candidate's donor-tracked contrast is consequently `3/4`, exactly as predicted by its tagged product-marginal identity. The physical routing state change is zero. NumPy checks verify both facts without fitting a model.

This is not a counterexample to the algebra. The logical task changed which external reference is associated with the data delivered to a site. If the intended task explicitly follows original job identities, the loss of association with resident environmental storage can be operationally meaningful. It is, however, a counterexample to the claim that every tracked contrast establishes changed local processor behavior caused by the physical swap. The assay must name the joint routing-and-reference-assignment intervention, and its decision must depend on that logical task.

A useful controlled test records both resident-reference and donor-reference scores when those measurements are separately implementable. It can separate local physical response from changes in task association. Reference readout requires its own gate/distance/storage accounting; bookkeeping does not create a remote Bell measurement for free.
