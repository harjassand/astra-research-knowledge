## 6. The attempted stronger routes and their obstructions

### A. Exact finite proof does not follow from arbitrarily small error

It would be incorrect to erase the \(\varepsilon u(h)\) term merely because \(\varepsilon\) can be made arbitrarily small.

For an elementary illustration, consider

\[
x+\frac{1}{k}y\ge0,\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,
\]

together with \(y\ge0\).

All these inequalities imply \(x\ge0\). No finite subcollection does: if its largest index is \(K\), then

\[
y=1,\qquad x=-\frac{1}{2K}
\]

satisfies that finite subcollection while violating \(x\ge0\).

In coefficient space, \((1,0)\) belongs to the closure of the generated cone but not to its finite conic hull.

This is **not** an entropy counterexample. It identifies the logical gap in claiming that the conservativity theorem automatically supplies an exact finite-copy certificate.

To close that gap, one needs an additional property of the actual copy-proof cones—not general compactness or rationality.

### B. Small rank gain does not control the change of the whole profile

I also examined a possible convergence argument: repeatedly remove separator defects, hoping that a small total-rank increase forces a small change in the remaining entropy coordinates.

That stability premise is false.

Let \(U,V\) be independent fair bits, and define three coordinates

\[
A=U,\qquad B=U,\qquad C=(U,V).
\]

Then

\[
I(A;B\mid C)=0.
\]

Introduce \(A'=V\). At the entropy-profile level,

\[
(A',C)\quad\text{and}\quad(A,C)
\]

have identical profiles:

\[
H(A')=H(A)=1,\qquad
H(C)=2,\qquad
H(A',C)=H(A,C)=2.
\]

Moreover,

\[
I(A';A,B\mid C)=0,
\]

because all these variables are determined by \(C\). Thus this is a valid conditional-copy witness under the entropy constraints.

Nevertheless,

\[
H(A,B)=1,
\qquad
H(A',B)=2.
\]

The replacement changes a marginal entropy by one bit while its total-rank gain is zero.

Consequently, no general estimate of the form

\[
\|g'-g\|
\le C\,I_g(A;B\mid C)
\]

can hold for arbitrary entropy-level copy witnesses.

This falsifies that proposed stability mechanism. It does not rule out a more carefully selected convergent construction. The maximum-rank proof avoids needing such stability altogether.

### C. Quantized ranks explain one boundary—but do not remove it

When a class admits copy extensions with all ranks in a fixed lattice \(\eta\mathbb Z\), every positive defect costs at least \(\eta\). Repeatedly removing a positive defect must then terminate after at most

\[
\frac{u(h)-h(E)}{\eta}
\]

updates.

That gives exact finite completion in such a class.

The unresolved step is obtaining an analogous uniform quantization or finite-termination principle for general copy-consistent real profiles. Neither rational input data nor the existence of rational witnesses to individual finite programs establishes it.

## 7. Does this reach the foundational objective?

The decisive entropy-cone target remains

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal K_E
\stackrel{?}{=}
\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}},
}
\]

where \(\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}}\) is the almost-entropic cone.

Actual entropy profiles admit conditional copying, so

\[
\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}}\subseteq\mathcal K_E.
\]

The missing result is either a reverse-inclusion theorem or a rigorously constructed profile in

\[
\mathcal K_E\setminus\overline{\Gamma_E^{*}}.
\]

The investigation established neither.

What it does establish, subject to the proof-system definitions above, is a restriction on one proposed route:

> **Repeatedly adding the specified MAXE and GMAXE separator rules cannot bridge a gap between the closed copy hierarchy and the almost-entropic cone. Those rules already hold throughout the copy hierarchy’s model class.**

This is more than a new inequality or another representable example: it concerns an entire method of generating inequalities. But it is not a characterization of information, a separation from the copy hierarchy, or an efficient general entropy procedure.

**The supported output is therefore a candidate original conservativity theorem, with the proof and finite approximation mechanism given above.** The targeted sources checked did not provide the same closed-hierarchy theorem, but that is not an exhaustive priority determination, and the argument has not received independent verification.
