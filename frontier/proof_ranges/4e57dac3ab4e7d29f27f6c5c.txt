### Theorem B: linear decomposable-Schmidt complexity is necessary for PPT maps

For every k>=1 and d>=C k, there exists a real density matrix rho on C^d tensor C^d satisfying

\[
 \rho^\Gamma=\rho,\quad \rho_A=\rho_B=I_d/d,\quad
 \frac{I}{6d^2}\preceq\rho\preceq\frac{11I}{6d^2},
\]

and

\[
 \inf_{\tau\in\mathcal C_k}\frac12\|\rho-\tau\|_1\ge\frac7{3200}.
\]

The channel Lambda with J(Lambda)=d rho is unital, trace preserving, and PPT, but is not in SP_k+transpose o SP_k. Here SP_k consists of CP maps admitting a Kraus decomposition whose operators all have rank at most k.

If

\[
 K(d)=\min\{k:\operatorname{PPT}(d,d)\subseteq\operatorname{DSP}_k(d,d)\},
\]

then for d>=C,

\[
 \lfloor d/C\rfloor+1\le K(d)\le d.
\]

Thus K(d)=Theta(d). This establishes the asymptotic order, **not** the exact minimum for each d. In particular it does not decide the separate d=4,k=2 case. It rules out dimension-independent bounded-Schmidt decompositions of all PPT maps and the proposed universal inclusion in DSP_2, not the conclusion of the PPT-cubed conjecture itself.

## 2. The exact random subspace and its covariance

Let V_d be the real Hilbert space of traceless real symmetric d-by-d matrices with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product. Its dimension is

\[
 h=\frac{d(d+1)}2-1.
\]

Choose an orthonormal basis S_1,...,S_h. Take independent real standard normal coefficients g_ab and put

\[
 G=\frac1d\sum_{a,b=1}^h g_{ab} S_a\otimes S_b. \tag{1}
\]

Exactly, G is real symmetric and

\[
 G^\Gamma=G=G^{\Gamma_A},\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}_A G=\operatorname{Tr}_B G=0.
\]

For every Hermitian matrix H,

\[
 \operatorname{Var}[\operatorname{Tr}(GH)]
 =d^{-2}\sum_{a,b}|\operatorname{Tr}[(S_a\otimes S_b)H]|^2
 \le d^{-2}\|H\|_2^2. \tag{2}
\]

This is just orthogonal projection onto a real Hilbert subspace. It applies to complex Hermitian H; no restriction of the later Schmidt vectors to real coordinates is made. Also,

\[
 d^2\operatorname{Tr}(G^2)=\sum_{a,b}g_{ab}^2\sim\chi^2_{h^2}. \tag{3}
\]

An equivalent numerical generation is to draw an isotropic real symmetric Gaussian matrix A on dimension d^2, orthogonally project it onto V_d tensor V_d, and divide by d. The projector first takes (A+A^Gamma)/2 and then removes the two local identity sectors. Both implementations were checked against each other on small dimensions.

## 3. Uniform Gaussian estimates, with explicit constants

Write m=d^2. Three kinds of events are used.

### 3.1 Global operator norm

A 1/4-net of the real unit sphere in R^m has at most 9^m elements. For a real symmetric matrix, its norm is at most twice the largest absolute quadratic form on that net. By (2), each quadratic form is a centered Gaussian of variance at most 1/m. Therefore

\[
 \Pr\{\|G\|_\infty>5\}
 \le2\exp[-(25/8-\log9)m]\le2e^{-m/2}. \tag{4}
\]

Real vectors suffice **only** for this global norm because G is real symmetric. The restricted estimate below uses complex vectors.

### 3.2 Hilbert–Schmidt mass

For a chi-square variable with L degrees of freedom, the elementary moment-generating-function Chernoff bound gives

\[
 \Pr\{\chi^2_L<0.9L\}\le e^{-L/400}.
\]

Indeed, optimizing E exp(-t chi-square) and using -log(1-u)-u>=u^2/2 gives this inequality. Since h>=d^2/2 for d>=2, (3) implies

\[
 \Pr\{\operatorname{Tr}G^2<9d^2/40\}\le e^{-d^4/1600}. \tag{5}
\]

### 3.3 Restricted bilinear norm

Define

\[
 q_k(G)=\sup_{\substack{\|x\|=\|y\|=1\\\operatorname{SR}(x),\operatorname{SR}(y)\le k}}
                  |\langle x,Gy\rangle|. \tag{6}
\]

For any eta>0,

\[
 \Pr\{q_k(G)>\eta\}
 \le4\exp\left[36dk-\frac{d^2\eta^2}{16}\right]. \tag{7}
\]

Here is a full net proof, including the often problematic uniform extension step.

**Rank-net size.** Identify vectors with d-by-d matrices by vectorization. A rank-at-most-k unit Frobenius matrix has SVD U diag(s) V*, where U,V are complex d-by-k isometries and s is a nonnegative unit vector in R^k. The set of isometries has an eta_0-net in the operator norm of size at most (1+2/eta_0)^(2dk): use disjoint eta_0/2 balls in the ambient real 2dk-dimensional normed space, all contained in the ball of radius 1+eta_0/2. Choose the net centers within the isometries. The s set has a Euclidean eta_0-net of size at most (1+2/eta_0)^k, again with unit centers.

The three factor approximations give Frobenius error at most 3 eta_0. Thus the rank set has an epsilon-net, with centers still of rank at most k and norm one, of cardinality

\[
 (1+6/\epsilon)^{(4d+1)k}.
\]

Set epsilon=1/8, giving 49^((4d+1)k).

**Extension without a dimension logarithm.** Split the singular values of a rank-at-most-2k vector into two groups of at most k. The sum of the norms of the two pieces is at most sqrt(2). Applying this separately to x and y proves

\[
 q_{\min(2k,d)}(G)\le2q_k(G).
\]

Approximating each of x,y by its rank-k net center gives

\[
 q_k(G)\le Q_{\rm net}+2\epsilon q_{\min(2k,d)}(G)
 \le Q_{\rm net}+4\epsilon q_k(G).
\]

Therefore q_k<=2 Q_net. No sampled low-rank optimization is substituted for this universal bound.

**Gaussian tail.** For fixed complex x,y, the real and imaginary parts of <x,Gy> are Gaussian quadratic pairings with Hermitian test matrices (yx*+xy*)/2 and (yx*-xy*)/(2i), respectively. Each test has HS norm at most one. Hence

\[
 \Pr\{|\langle x,Gy\rangle|>u\}\le4e^{-d^2u^2/4}.
\]

Union over two nets, with u=eta/2, gives

\[
 4\exp\left[2(4d+1)k\log49-d^2\eta^2/16\right].
\]

For d>=2 the entropy is at most 36dk, since 2(4d+1)<=9d and log49<4. This proves (7).

### 3.4 The two regimes

For d>=C k, using eta=1/64 in (7) yields

\[
 \Pr\{q_k>1/64\}\le4e^{-d^2/131072}. \tag{8}
\]

For d>=C r^3, using eta=1/(64r) and k=r yields

\[
 \Pr\{q_r>1/(64r)\}\le4e^{-d^2/(131072r^2)}. \tag{9}
\]

For example, the exponent comparison in (9) is

\[
 \frac{d^2}{65536r^2}-36dr
 \ge\frac{d^2}{131072r^2},
\]

using d>=2^23 r^3. In either regime, the sum of the relevant failure probability and (4),(5) is less than one. Indeed at the smallest allowed d each exponent already exceeds 100. Thus the intersection of all required good events has positive probability. Boundaries can be avoided; one can take all useful inequalities strict.

## 4. Witness/state pair and the linear cone obstruction

Take a realization with ||G||<5, Tr G^2>9d^2/40 and q_k<1/64, and put

\[
 \rho=\frac{I+G/6}{d^2},\qquad W=I-32G. \tag{10}
\]

The exact symmetries and partial traces in Section 2 give rho^Gamma=rho, trace one, and both marginals I/d. Its spectrum is in [1/(6d^2),11/(6d^2)]. For a normalized vector of Schmidt rank at most k,

\[
 \langle x,Wx\rangle\ge1-32q_k>1/2. \tag{11}
\]

Since W^Gamma=W, both W and W^Gamma are k-block positive. Every tau=A+B^Gamma in C_k consequently satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W\tau)\ge1/2,\qquad
 \operatorname{Tr}(G\tau)\le1/64.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W\rho)
 =1-\frac{32}{6d^2}\operatorname{Tr}G^2
 <-1/5,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(G\rho)>3/80.
\]

Since ||G||<=5, duality of operator and trace norm gives

\[
 \frac12\|\rho-\tau\|_1
 \ge\frac{(3/80)-(1/64)}{10}=7/3200.
\]

This proves Theorem B's state claim. Under the Choi convention, S_k corresponds exactly to SP_k: a pure Choi vector corresponds to a Kraus operator with rank equal to that vector's Schmidt rank; sum the rank-one Choi decompositions. The channel statement follows with J(Lambda)=d rho, without importing a conjectural channel theorem.

The witness W itself defines the map P with J(P)=W/d. It is k-positive and k-copositive by (11). This equivalence can also be seen directly by compressing the first Choi factor to k-dimensional subspaces: those compressions are positive iff all Schmidt-rank-at-most-k quadratic forms are nonnegative.

A decomposable Choi matrix A+B^Gamma has nonnegative pairing with every PPT state. The negative pairing with rho therefore proves that P is indecomposable. Its partial traces are I, making P unital and trace preserving. Its input and output transpose invariances follow term by term from (1).

Explicitly,

\[
 P(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)\frac{I}{d}
       -\frac{32}{d^2}\sum_{a,b}g_{ab}\operatorname{Tr}(S_aX)S_b. \tag{12}
\]

For any decomposable map D,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[\rho J(D)]\ge0,\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}[\rho J(P)]\le-1/(5d),\quad
 \|\rho\|_\infty\le11/(6d^2).
\]

It follows that ||J(P)-J(D)||_1>=6d/55. Evaluate P-D on one half of a normalized maximally entangled state to conclude

\[
 \|P-D\|_\diamond\ge\frac1d\|J(P)-J(D)\|_1\ge6/55. \tag{13}
\]

Finally K(d)>=floor(d/C)+1 follows from the counterexample for that k. K(d)<=d follows because every positive Choi matrix has Schmidt number at most d and every PPT map is CP. This is an asymptotic-order answer, not an exact finite-dimensional classification.
