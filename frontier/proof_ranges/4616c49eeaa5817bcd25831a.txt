## 9. What is now established and what is not

The new main result reaches beyond rank-one neighborhoods: arbitrary scalar amplitudes depending on all spatial coordinates, including internally resonant and concentrating amplitudes, admit a positive integral representation. Its quartic corollary controls every affine background uniformly. Explicit full-rank fields demonstrate that it is not a consequence of small transverse mass.

The assembly theorem preserves complete quartic optimization for exactly specified resonance-separated packets. It admits arbitrarily many overlapping packets, all with their internal nonlinear correlations retained. Its canonical resonance-component reduction restricts where a counterexample must occur. It is not a decomposition theorem for all gradients into proved-positive pieces.

Two exact compatible tests mark the obstruction: cross-packet energy can decrease even for determinant square, and the variable-phase curvature correction can have either sign even for a convex quartic. Neither gives a negative quasiconvexity gap. They prohibit two tempting but invalid global assemblies.

The complete nine-coefficient implication remains unresolved in this investigation. The missing operation would control connected cross-packet resonances, or the signed phase-curvature term and its positive Bregman/acoustic budget, without discarding their correlation. Arbitrary planar potentials cannot be assumed to have affine phase or a resonance-separated positive decomposition. Zeros and winding add further issues to any attempt to use a global polar representation.

This direction now has an analytic global exclusion rather than only contact asymptotics. Repeating contact classification or one-carrier optimization is not productive. Concentrated effort is warranted only if directed at the signed cross-carrier interaction or an independently checkable compatible negative profile. No compute-calibrated expected probability of success is asserted.
