## Acquisition budget and present method limits

The budget is target-class and specimen specific; the primary-method sources do not provide a universal scan time, dose, or cost per area. The correct report is measured instrument time, electron/X-ray dose, preparation loss, analysis effort, beamtime, control count, and functional cycling for the actual run. A useful work-plan cost expression is

\[
C_{\rm assay}=C_{\rm selection+preparation}+2\sum_{g\in G}C_{\rm registered\ scan}(A,r,\tau)
+C_{\rm local\ tilt/4D-STEM}+C_{\rm boundary\ follow-up}
+C_{\rm fiducial\ strain/path}+C_{\rm performance\ cycles}+C_{\rm matched\ controls}+C_{\rm analysis}.
\]

The factor 2 denotes baseline and post-treatment; dynamic tracking adds frames and beam dose. For the finite ZnS/f.c.c. example, the structural cover starts with three reflections, one additional to the cited two-beam ZnS set, and may add opposite conditions for sign, plus a separate scan for each registered target/timepoint. The catalog-wide minimum is an explicit weighted set-cover problem over visible line/fault classes; the weights are scan time/dose and orientation-change cost. It can be solved exactly for a small known material catalog, but set-cover optimality does not certify real-world detectability.

**Equal-target-information comparator.** To test whether this certificate actually saves acquisition over full reconstruction, process the same pre/post target-plus-collar volume by (a) conventional intensity tomography and (b) targeted object-based line tomography, supplemented by the same multi-g/fault and path-strain measurements. Require both pipelines to return the same decision variables — target line lengths by Burgers class, fault areas by translation class, junction products, every collar-boundary crossing, and uncertainty at the same spatial cutoff — with matched calibration and blinded review. Charge all tilts, setup, dose, analysis and failed scans. Conventional tomography is reported at roughly 50–100 projections, while the object method reports selected line reconstruction with 2–3 views; these literature figures do not establish equal sensitivity or lower cost for this certificate. The cited object pipeline's incomplete/false detections make that comparison an experiment still to be run. If the lower-view path cannot meet the same blind-set and sensitivity thresholds, it has not delivered equal target information and this proposed acquisition advantage fails.

Relevant primary-method limits are:

- **ECCI/SEM:** A [primary 2017 ECCI study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-09756-3) describes few-nanometre lateral resolution, a visibility depth of order 100 nm, diffraction-condition dependence, and a required local orientation accuracy better than 0.1°. Its dipole analysis used five distinct \(g\) vectors and ±\(g\) pairs for selected reflections. The [ZnS/GaP v2 optical-glide methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07908v2) provide a closer platform-specific count: eight ECCI micrographs for pre/post images at four 365 nm intensities, 148 misfit dislocations, 30 kV/2.4 nA imaging, and 350 °C cycles. That work also measures relaxation with HRXRD symmetric (400) and asymmetric (224)/(\(\bar2 24\)) reciprocal-space maps; use those for population/strain baselines, not to assign a single line's topology. ECCI is cheaper over broad near-surface areas than making a TEM foil, but is not a whole-thickness assay and its longest-line mobility sample is selection-limited.
- **4D-STEM/CBED:** [Niermann et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10866901/) show 3D location/type classification from one 4D-STEM measurement by comparing measured pattern variation with multi-beam calculations. In their GaN example, thickness and line depth were fit to about 5 nm calculation steps for a 132 nm foil. The paper identifies a mid-plane mirror ambiguity, and the method remains subject to the \(g\cdot b\) coverage and fidelity of the assumed strain/elastic model. Cost is one diffraction pattern at each probe position, detector/data volume, and model fitting; a single projection is not a 3D network map.
- **Targeted dislocation tomography:** A [primary object-based TEM study](https://academic.oup.com/mam/article/28/3/633/6889418) says conventional intensity tomography can require roughly 50–100 projections; its node/line method reports good positional accuracy with as few as three images and can reconstruct selected objects from as few as two tilts. Its example pipeline recovered about 80% of true line content while also producing false lines; manual matching of lines across views was the slowest step. It explicitly warns that projected crossings can be fake junctions, and the demonstrated network omitted extended/partial line detail from its simplified reconstruction. These costs/evidence argue for target-local use plus explicit sensitivity and junction checks, not a claim of completeness.
- **Bulk X-ray option:** Dark-field X-ray microscopy penetrates bulk material and is an appropriate complement when a TEM foil cannot represent subsurface motion. The [2025 DFXM Burgers-vector paper](https://journals.iucr.org/j/issues/2025/01/00/nb5385/nb5385.html) reports multi-peak comparison for one edge-dislocation example and develops single-peak Burgers-vector extraction from rocking-tilt scans; it also states practical difficulty of multi-peak scans. Treat the single-peak assignment as a modality-specific method, not an already validated general assay. A bulk survey's volume, reflections, rocking angles and time must be charged.
- **Growth/strain comparator:** The [forward-graded ZnS\(_y\)Se\(_{1-y}\)/GaAs study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11664-010-1101-z) is credited by the supplied ledger for X-ray-based dislocation-density changes and an overshoot-interface annihilation/coalescence route. The source does not supply a same-defect before/after topology, and a growth-stack result cannot be used as evidence for a post-growth carrier-gated reaction.
- **Beam and thermal regime:** In the [2023 ZnS electric-field study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41563-023-01572-7), the authors report dislocation motion during an interval with the electron beam off. The optical-glide methods instead anneal at 350 °C and image the same location after cooling. Accordingly, charge a matched beam-only/readout exposure control, do not assume the beam is passive, and keep the electric-field and optical/350 °C arms separate unless a joint same-specimen control set is acquired.
- **Fault contrast:** In diffraction, the fault phase shift is \(2\pi g\cdot R\). A fault contrast can disappear when \(g\cdot R\) is an integer. The [4H-SiC primary methods paper](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jap/article/116/10/104905/386748/A-method-to-determine-fault-vectors-in-4H-SiC-from) notes that phase-based fault-vector solutions may not be unique; couple fault contrast with the line Burgers circuit and a different g condition.

A sample-level no-defect statement cannot be obtained by multiplying nominal microscope resolution by an unmeasured detection sensitivity. Report the imaged volume, spatial/contrast threshold, orientations, failed acquisitions, thickness, missing region, and detection calibration. If a boundary or interior is outside all modalities, preserve it as `UNKNOWN`.
