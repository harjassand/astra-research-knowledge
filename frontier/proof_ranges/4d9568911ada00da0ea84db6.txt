1. CLASSIFICATION OF POINTWISE DERIVATIVE-CANCELLING STEIN MATRICES

This exact lemma restricts the simplest local completion interface. Assume
K(y,w) is C2 in w and C1 in y, and a candidate Stein matrix is K(y,g(y)).
At a fixed y,w, the unknown Hessian contribution to rowwise Euclidean
divergence is
 sum_(j,k) (partial K_ij/partial w_k)(y,w) H_kj.
Suppose it vanishes identically for every symmetric matrix H, as it must
if the algorithm obtains a derivative-free formula by universal algebraic
cancellation with no additional value differences. Then, for each row i,
 partial_(w_k) K_ij + partial_(w_j) K_ik =0.          (1.1)
For j=k this includes partial_(w_j)K_ij=0. Thus K_i is a Killing field in
w-space. On connected R^d it has the form
 K_ij(y,w)=b_ij(y)+sum_k A_ijk(y) w_k,
 A_ijk(y)=-A_ikj(y).                                (1.2)
Proof without an external theorem: differentiate (1.1), commute mixed
partials, and cycle the indices to obtain
 partial_(w_l)partial_(w_k)K_ij
 =-partial_(w_l)partial_(w_j)K_ik
 =+partial_(w_j)partial_(w_k)K_il
 =-partial_(w_k)partial_(w_l)K_ij,
so each second derivative is zero. The affine derivative is skew by (1.1).
The same result follows for weak derivatives under appropriate Sobolev
regularity, but no weak extension is needed for this scoped lemma.

Write K=K0+Kg, where K0(y)=b(y) and Kg_ij=sum_k A_ijk g_k. Then
 (Kg g)_i=sum_(j,k) A_ijk g_j g_k=0.                 (1.3)
Consequently the g-dependent part of a stationary skew flow
 ydot=-K(y,g(y))^T p
is tangent to F-level sets:
 g(y) dot [-Kg(y)^T p]=0.                           (1.4)
Known g-independent K0 may move F, as the harmonic Gaussian rotation does;
it does not invalidate the classification of the unknown-field-dependent
part. This explains R20's level-set restriction for ALL smooth pointwise
matrices with universal Hessian cancellation, rather than only its chosen
rank-two coefficients. It is NOT a lower bound for arbitrary finite-value
algorithms, secant constructions, seed-dependent gates, or Brownian-path
completions. A successful local design must escape this pointwise interface.

2. DEPENDENT MARTINGALE APPROXIMATION: EXACT COUPLED ERROR IDENTITY

Use the Brownian representation from r16_brownian_gaussian_completion.txt:
M_t=u(t,B_t), H_t=Du(t,B_t), dM_t=H_t dB_t, M_0=mu, M_1=g(B1).
Let Hhat_t be ANY predictable symmetric process on the same filtration,
possibly augmented by independent randomness, with ||Hhat_t||op<=L and
sigma>L. Let W be a fresh independent Brownian motion and define
 Shat_t=(sigma^2 I-Hhat_t^2)^(1/2),
 Zhat=integral Hhat_t dB_t + integral Shat_t dW_t,
 Xhat=g(B1)+integral Shat_t dW_t.                    (2.1)
The quadratic variation of Zhat is exactly sigma^2 t I, so Levy's theorem
makes Zhat a centered N(0,sigma^2 I) vector, regardless of how well Hhat
approximates H. On the displayed coupling,
 Xhat = mu+Zhat + integral (H_t-Hhat_t)dB_t.           (2.2)
The terminal error is NOT independent of Zhat in general. Nevertheless
Ito isometry gives the valid bound
 W2(Law(Xhat),N(mu,sigma^2 I))
 <=[E integral ||H_t-Hhat_t||F^2 dt]^(1/2).           (2.3)
This identifies precisely what a direct predictable-integrand approximation
would have to acquire. An operator-norm error delta gives only delta sqrt(d)
in (2.3), so exponential accuracy in the number of acquired steps would be
needed for a polylog query theorem. Randomizing an unbiased H estimate does
not by itself exploit the linkage to g(B1): its extra conditional variance
appears inside the right-hand integral.

For example take a fresh conditional independent A_t with E[A_t|B_t]=H_t
and norm<=L, and average n independent samples to form Hhat_t. Then
 E||H_t-Hhat_t||F^2 = n^-1 E||A_t-H_t||F^2.
The direct bound therefore has worst size L sqrt(d/n). This is an interface
calculation, not a lower bound against dependent endpoints or final-observable
compression. Any improved construction must use cancellation beyond (2.3),
or a much stronger acquisition of H than iid conditional samples.

If a final independent Gaussian reserve of covariance eta^2 I is added to
both sides of a coupling, the Gaussian convolution inequality gives TV at
most W2/(2 eta), by Pinsker applied to shifted Gaussians and convexity. The
reserve also increases the target variance and must be budgeted from sigma;
it is not a free smoothing operation. Taking completion noise S with
L<S<sigma and eta^2=sigma^2-S^2 is legitimate. The exact algebra (2.2) then
uses S in place of sigma and the reserve transfers its error to TV.

A separate numerical warning: even on a subinterval away from t=1, the
function t->H(t,B_t) is generally only a stochastic continuous process,
not an analytic deterministic-time function. High-order scalar quadrature
cannot be claimed from analyticity of the heat-smoothed function H(t,x)
alone; Brownian path increments or an appropriate stochastic expansion must
be included in the acquisition analysis.

5. EXACT BROWNIAN OPERATOR REGULARITY FROM GRADIENT SYMMETRY

Let s=1-t and H(t,x)=D P_s g(x). Then H is symmetric and ||H||op<=L.
For every v, full symmetry of the third potential derivatives gives
 D H(t,x)[v] = D_x(H(t,x)v).
Writing A=Dg, defined a.e., Gaussian integration by parts yields
 D_x(Hv)=s^(-1/2) E[(A(x+sqrt(s)Z)v) Z^T].
The coordinate Gaussians Z_j form an orthonormal family in L2, so the
Hilbert-valued Bessel inequality gives the EXACT one-slot estimate
 ||D H(t,x)[v]||F^2 <= s^(-1) E||A(x+sqrt(s)Z)v||^2
                         <= L^2 ||v||^2/s.           (5.1)
No Hessian oracle is supplied or queried in this argument. The derivative
is an analysis object. For T_j=partial_j H, all T_j are symmetric and
 v^T (sum_j T_j^2) v = sum_j ||T_j v||^2
                      = ||D H[v]||F^2,
so
 sum_j T_j(t,x)^2 <= L^2/(1-t) I.                    (5.2)

Along Brownian motion, the backward heat equation makes H_t=H(t,B_t)
a bounded self-adjoint matrix martingale for t<1, with
 dH_t=sum_j T_j(t,B_t)dB_t^j.
For 0<=t<u<1 set V_tu=L^2 log((1-t)/(1-u)). Then, conditionally on F_t,
 Pr(||H_u-H_t||op>=a | F_t)
 <= min(1,2d exp(-a^2/(2V_tu))) for a>0.             (5.3)
The deterministic upper bound 2L also applies. In particular, for p>=1,
 (E[||H_u-H_t||op^p|F_t])^(1/p)
 <= C L min(1,sqrt(log((1-t)/(1-u))
                           [log(2d)+p])).           (5.4)
Here C is an absolute constant, not dependent on d,g,t,u.

Direct proof of (5.3), avoiding any unacquired matrix concentration oracle:
for self-adjoint M,A and real lambda,
 D^2 tr exp(lambda M)[A,A]
 =lambda^2 integral_0^1 tr(exp((1-r)lambda M) A
                           exp(r lambda M) A)dr
 <=lambda^2 tr(exp(lambda M) A^2).
Diagonalize M in the proof only, and apply convexity of exp to the scalar
factors exp((1-r)lambda m_i+r lambda m_j); the symmetry |A_ij|=|A_ji|
gives the last inequality. Ito's formula and (5.2), applied to the martingale
H_v-H_t over v in [t,u], imply
 E[tr exp(lambda(H_u-H_t))|F_t]
 <= d exp(lambda^2 V_tu/2).
Markov's inequality, optimization lambda=a/V_tu, and repetition for -H
give (5.3). Integration of this tail gives (5.4). This is a mathematical
proof using spectral calculus; the algorithm is not given a diagonalization
of H_t or a query-free way to evaluate it.

A useful noncommutative consequence for a completion scale S>L:
 R(H)=(S^2 I-H^2)^(1/2), m=sqrt(S^2-L^2).
For symmetric H1,H2 of operator norm <=L,
 ||R(H1)-R(H2)||op <= (L/m)||H1-H2||op.               (5.5)
Indeed Delta=R(H1)-R(H2) solves
 R(H1)Delta+Delta R(H2)=H2^2-H1^2.
The Sylvester inverse has the integral representation
 Delta=integral_0^infinity exp(-rR(H1))(H2^2-H1^2)
                              exp(-rR(H2))dr,
which bounds its norm by ||H2^2-H1^2||/(2m). Expanding the square difference
without assuming commutativity gives ||H2^2-H1^2||<=2L||H2-H1||.

This gives dimension-logarithmic averaged OPERATOR perturbation of the
conditional Brownian Hessian and its square-root completion on short
logarithmic-time intervals. It does not give an acquired H_t, an acquired

particular, (5.4) cannot be substituted directly into an Ito isometry while
omitting the factor sqrt(d). Any positive sampler compiler using these

8. FINITE POSITIVE STEIN-COVARIANCE PROPOSAL: EXACT RANDOMIZATION ACCOUNTING

The following extends R21's local leading-error cancellation identity.
Assume g,K smooth, delta_gamma K=g-mu, K bounded, and define
 Gamma=sym(K Dg^T).
Let Gammahat(G,xi) be a symmetric conditional unbiased estimator of Gamma,
independent of a fresh standard Gaussian Z, and assume
 ||Gammahat||op<=c<S^2 everywhere. Then the FINITE positive proposal
 X=g(G)+(S^2 I-Gammahat(G,xi))^(1/2) Z               (8.1)
has mean mu and EXACT covariance S^2 I. It is generally not Gaussian.
Positivity follows from the strict matrix bound, rather than from a signed
Taylor expansion. The covariance statement uses Gaussian Stein:
 Cov(g)=E K Dg^T=E Gamma=E Gammahat.
These are interfaces; no bounded Gammahat action oracle is assumed acquired.

For a fixed t in R^d let H=t^T(g-mu), q=t^T Gamma t and qhat=t^T Gammahat t.
The characteristic factor, with the target Gaussian removed, is
 E exp(i s H+(s^2/2)qhat).
Conditionally on G, E[qhat-q]=0, so
 E_xi exp((s^2/2)qhat)=exp((s^2/2)q)(1+V_s),
 V_s=E_xi[exp((s^2/2)(qhat-q))-1-(s^2/2)(qhat-q)]>=0.
The exact Taylor remainder satisfies
 V_s <= (s^4/8)E_xi[(qhat-q)^2 exp((s^2/2)|qhat-q|)]. (8.2)
Thus random covariance adds its first new Hermite term at order four; it
cannot be discarded on the basis of conditional unbiasedness.

More explicitly, the contracted third and fourth cumulants of t^T X are
 kappa_3(t^TX)=-E[(K^T t) dot grad q],
 kappa_4(t^TX)=-3E[H (K^T t) dot grad q]
                 +3E Var(qhat|G).                 (8.3)
Proof: conditional Gaussian moments give kappa3=E H^3-3E Hq and
kappa4=E H^4-6E H^2q+3E qhat^2. Stein integration by parts yields
 E H^3=2E Hq, E H^4=3E H^2q,
 E Hq=E[(K^Tt) dot grad q],
 E H^2q=E[q^2+H(K^Tt) dot grad q].
Substitution gives (8.3), including the strictly nonnegative covariance
randomization contribution. These identities explicitly separate a tied
Stein correction from arbitrary conditional covariance matching.

For a deterministic Gamma, R21's exact characteristic differential identity
is recovered:
 M_s'-i(t dot mu)M_s
 = (i s^2/2) E[(K^Tt) dot grad q
                 exp(i s t dot g+(s^2/2)q)].         (8.4)
For random Gammahat differentiable in G (and justified domination), the
exact corresponding right-hand side is
 s E[(qhat-q) exp(i s t dot g+(s^2/2)qhat)]
 +(i s^2/2) E[(K^Tt) dot grad_G qhat
                      exp(i s t dot g+(s^2/2)qhat)]. (8.5)
The first term in (8.5) begins with covariance-estimator variance. A positive
higher-order compiler would have to cancel/control both terms, not replace
Gammahat by its conditional expectation inside the exponential.

One additional exact identity: put A=(S^2 I-Gammahat)^1/2 and
D_G X=Dg+D_G(AZ). Before conditioning on X, a valid Stein matrix is
 T=K (D_G X)^T + A^2.
Its symmetric part is
 sym T=S^2 I+(Gamma-Gammahat)+sym[K(D_G(AZ))^T].      (8.6)
For scalar test potentials psi,
 E[(X-mu) dot grad psi(X)]=E[sym T : Hess psi(X)].
The full nonsymmetric T is required for general vector Stein tests. In the

NOT automatically square its incoming discrepancy: its Stein matrix has

reintroduce a linear error. A proof of a positive superlinear update remains

 E[J_s(c;v)J_s(c;v)^T]
 <=E_Z[Dg(c+sZ) C Dg(c+sZ)^T]
 <=L^2||C||op I.                                   (10.3)
For C=I and every w, convex Jensen gives the sharper mgf fact
 E exp(w dot J_s(c;v)) <=exp(L^2||w||^2/2).          (10.4)
The mean is zero because E[J|v]=H_s(c)v. Indeed J_s(c;v) is convex-order
dominated by N(0,L^2I): first use Rao-Blackwell, then condition on X so
Dg(X)v is Gaussian with covariance <=L^2I. The same argument for (9.3)
gives a proxy b^2L^2I, and
 E[v J_K(c;v)^T]=K_b(c) for v standard Gaussian.     (10.5)
All these statements hold conditionally on an acquired/adaptive center c,
provided v and the internal action seeds are fresh.

This avoids a spurious factor d in any FIXED-DIRECTION variance bound for
an actual Gaussian action. It still allows trace variance of order d and
does not by itself give the target JOINT Gaussian channel with cross block
K_b(c). In particular, nonlinear action samples cannot be substituted for
K_b inside a covariance square root. A positive completion must preserve
or exploit the Rao-Blackwell linkage, the queried endpoint values, and the
OU/Brownian endpoint, rather than retaining only its first two moments.

11. FULL RESOLVENT REGULARITY AND RETAINED SOURCE COUPLING

For raw globally L-Lipschitz conservative g, the full kernel
 K_1(y)=integral_0^1 E Dg(uy+sqrt(1-u^2)Z) du
is C^1 despite the possible discontinuity of Dg. Indeed for b<1, K_b is
smooth, ||K_1-K_b||op<=L(1-b), and the one-slot Frobenius bound gives
 ||(DK_c-DK_b)[v]||F
 <=L integral_b^c u/sqrt(1-u^2) du ||v||
 <=L sqrt(1-b^2)||v||,  b<c<1.                    (11.1)
The derivatives converge uniformly, so K_1 is C^1 and
 ||DK_1[v]||F<=L||v||,
 sum_j (partial_j K_1)^2 <=L^2 I,
 delta_gamma K_1=g-mu.                            (11.2)
The divergence identity follows first for b<1, with M_b-mu on its right,
then locally uniformly as b increases to one. This does not give uniform
higher derivatives: the naive second-derivative integral already diverges
logarithmically. Smooth covariance completion follows at the first-derivative
level from the Sylvester square-root formula in section 5, but arbitrarily
high-order time quadrature is not justified by raw resolvent analyticity.

The exact secant action (9.1) admits an equivalent generation which RETAINS
an explicit Gaussian source point. Given acquired c,v!=0 and u=v/||v||,
draw X~N(c,s^2I) independently of v, and two fresh scalar normals A2,A3.
Put q=u dot (X-c)/s, R=s sqrt(q^2+A2^2+A3^2), and
 Zperp=(X-c)/s-q u.
The two queried endpoints c+sZperp plus/minus R u have exactly the same
joint law as in (9.1). The Gaussian point X is now available and its scalar
coordinate q is one of the three coordinates defining the radius. Conditional
on the radius, q divided by R/s is uniform on [-1,1], proving the same
Rao-Blackwell identity. Retaining X may matter for a coupled endpoint
completion; it does not make the endpoint action itself Gaussian.

12. EXACT LATENT POSITIVE FILLING AND ITS ACQUISITION BOUNDARY

The convex-order argument gives a concrete POSITIVE latent Gaussian filling,
not just an abstract order statement. Let V,W be independent standard
Gaussian vectors, use the hidden Gaussian source X from section 10, and put
 A=Dg(X),
 Y=A V+sqrt(L^2 I-A^2) W.                          (12.1)
The hidden source X is independent of V in the joint law. Since A is
symmetric and ||A||op<=L, the square root exists. Conditional on X,
 Y~N(0,L^2 I), so Y has that law and is independent of X. If
 T=(V,Zperp,R) is the acquired secant sigma-field (with c fixed), then
 E[Y|T]=E[A V|T]=J_s(c;V).                        (12.2)
Thus (J_s,Y) is an explicit martingale coupling to the isotropic Gaussian,
and the same construction with the random scale in (9.3) fills a K_b action
to N(0,b^2L^2I). For that version A=b Dg(X) and the latent scale belongs to T.

Equation (12.1) is NOT an acquired algorithm: A and its square root are
unqueried. Sampling a fresh LGaussian independently of T preserves the
marginal Gaussian but destroys (12.2), which is exactly the dependence
needed in a mean-cancellation construction. First and second moments of J
do not restore this conditional law. A source-coupled positive compiler
would have to reproduce enough of (12.2), or another exact cancellation,
using the available endpoints with a deterministic polylogarithmic query
cap. No such compiler is proved here.
