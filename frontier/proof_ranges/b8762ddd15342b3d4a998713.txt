## 1. The precise theorem

Fix any rational \(0<\varepsilon<1\). The construction produces one fixed objective
\[
f(z)=\frac{\mu}{2}\|z\|^{2}
+\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_j\max\{0,b_j^\top z\}^{2},
\]
where \(\mu>0\), \(a_j\ge0\), and all coefficients are rational.

This is positive quadratic regularisation plus a finite nonnegative sum of squared ReLU terms—not an arbitrary computational oracle disguised as a loss.

The objective is globally continuously differentiable with Lipschitz gradient. It is \((1-\varepsilon)\)-strongly convex and \((1+\varepsilon)\)-smooth, giving the condition-number bound
\[
\kappa(f)\le \frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}.
\]
Thus the bound approaches one as \(\varepsilon\) approaches zero.

Its unique minimizer and minimum are
\[
z_*=0,\qquad f(z_*)=0.
\]

Ordinary full-batch gradient descent, with the fixed learning rate \(1\), has update
\[
G(z)=z-\nabla f(z)
\]
and satisfies
\[
\|G(x)-G(y)\|\le\varepsilon\|x-y\|.
\]
Every trajectory therefore converges rapidly to the known minimizer.

Nevertheless, there is a polynomial-time encoding \(E\) of finite programs and inputs into rational starting points such that
\[
\Lambda(E(w))
:=
\lim_{t\to\infty}
-\frac1t\log_2
\frac{f(G^tE(w))}{f(E(w))}
\]
exists and equals
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(E(w))
=
\begin{cases}
2k-2,&\text{the encoded computation accepts},\\[2mm]
2k,&\text{it never accepts},
\end{cases}}
\]
for one fixed integer \(k\).

The objective, dimension, coefficients, learning rate and two possible answers are all fixed. **Only the rational initial point varies.**

An algorithm approximating \(\Lambda\) within \(1/3\) would distinguish the two cases. A program that accepts precisely when another program halts then turns that algorithm into a halting decider, contradicting the classical undecidability result. [University of Virginia Computer Science](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/Turing_Paper_1936.pdf)

The central separation is therefore
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{Known minimizer, near-unit conditioning,}\\
\text{and globally contractive gradient descent}
\end{gathered}
\quad\not\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{computable instance-specific asymptotic rate}.}
\]

Here “rate” means the asymptotic average logarithmic decay above. It does not mean that the one-step ratio \(f(z_{t+1})/f(z_t)\) necessarily has a limit.
