## 2. The finite construction

Suppose
\[
\sigma=w\omega+(1-w)\chi,
\qquad 0<w<1,
\tag{5}
\]
where \(\omega\) is separable.

Give each catalyst subsystem an extra orthogonal vacuum vector. Embed \(\chi\) in the active subspace and write
\[
v_C=|\perp\perp\rangle\langle\perp\perp|,
\qquad
\tau=w\chi_C+(1-w)v_C.
\tag{6}
\]

Alice and Bob locally test whether the catalyst is active. They declare failure on the vacuum sector. The success probability is \(w\), and conditioned on success they possess \(\chi\).

They then use shared randomness:

With probability \(w\), they keep \(\chi\) in the catalyst and prepare \(\omega\) on the output. With probability \(1-w\), they transfer \(\chi\) to the output by local swaps and reset the catalyst to vacuum.

The successful joint state is
\[
\boxed{
\Omega
=
w\,\omega_{AB}\otimes\chi_C
+
(1-w)\,\chi_{AB}\otimes v_C.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Its marginals are exactly
\[
\operatorname{tr}_C\Omega=\sigma,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}_{AB}\Omega=\tau.
\tag{8}
\]

Everything is finite-dimensional and LOCC.

The resource has not been cloned. On one branch it remains in the catalyst; on the other it moves to the output. The restored marginal averages over those alternatives.

To obtain (5) from a normalized product vector
\[
|ab\rangle\in U_\sigma,
\]
choose a sufficiently small \(w>0\) satisfying
\[
w\le
\frac{1}{\langle ab|\sigma^{+}|ab\rangle},
\tag{9}
\]
where \(\sigma^+\) is the inverse on the support. Then
\[
\sigma-w|ab\rangle\langle ab|\succeq0.
\]
Normalizing the remainder gives \(\chi\), with \(\omega=|ab\rangle\langle ab|\).

Conversely, any positive separable component contributes product vectors to the support. This proves the constructive direction and the geometric equivalence.
