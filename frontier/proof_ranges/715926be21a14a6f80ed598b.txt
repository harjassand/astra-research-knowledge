Fix d>=2, 0<kappa<=1/d, A>=1, and B>=0. For each N let rho_N be a
permutation-invariant density operator on (C^d)^tensorn, with one-body
state tau_N=rho_1 satisfying tau_N>=kappa I. Assume that, for every
Hermitian H with ||H||_op<=1 and every real t,

 Tr rho_N exp[t sum_(i=1)^N(H_i-Tr(tau_N H)I)]
          <= A exp(B N t^2).                            (H)

The constants are fixed uniformly in N; tau_N may vary with N. There is
no Young-sector assumption. Define tau=tau_N, sigma_r=tau^tensorr,

 chi_P^2(rho_r||sigma_r)=Tr rho_r^2 sigma_r^(-1)-1,
 q=r/N,
 L=e(B+2)(d^2+1)/kappa,
 y=Lq/(1-q).

For every integer 2<=r<N with y<1,

 chi_P^2(rho_r||tau^tensorr) <= A^2 y^2/(1-y).          (T1)

In particular, with the explicit constants

 c=1/(2L+1),  C=8 A^2 L^2,

one has for every integer 1<=r<=cN,

 chi_P^2(rho_r||tau^tensorr) <= C(r/N)^2,
 D(rho_r||tau^tensorr) <= log(1+chi_P^2)
                       <= C(r/N)^2,                   (T2)

where the relative entropy uses natural logarithms. At r=1 both
quantities are exactly zero. Thus (T2) applies eventually to every
sequence r_N=o(N), with constants depending only on d,kappa,A,B.

The quadratic rate is attained by a fully separable family satisfying
(H); see the construction below. Concentration by itself is therefore
not an entanglement witness.

PROOF

1. A legal complex Gram bound from full rank of the one-body state.

Choose a Hermitian, traceless Hilbert-Schmidt orthonormal basis
F_1,...,F_m, m=d^2-1, with Tr F_a F_b=delta_ab. Put

 mu_a=Tr tau F_a,  A_a=F_a-mu_a I,
 G_ab=Tr tau A_a A_b.

The matrix G is complex Hermitian. It contains the imaginary
commutator contributions and is not replaced by a real covariance
matrix. For v in C^m, put U=sum_a v_a F_a and alpha=Tr tau U. Then

 v^dagger G v
   =Tr tau (U-alpha I)^dagger(U-alpha I)
   >=kappa Tr (U-alpha I)^dagger(U-alpha I)
   =kappa[sum_a |v_a|^2+d|alpha|^2]
   >=kappa sum_a |v_a|^2.                              (P1)

The third equality uses Tr U=0. Therefore G>=kappa I. This inequality
is valid for all complex v even though the concentration assumption is
used only for Hermitian H and real t.

For k distinct sites define the real tensor

 d_(a_1,...,a_k)=Tr rho_N A_(a_1)^(1)...A_(a_k)^(k),
 T_k=sum_(a_1,...,a_k) d_(a_1,...,a_k)^2.

The tensor is real because the product acts on distinct sites and is
Hermitian. It is symmetric by permutation invariance. Also d_1=0.

Products of I and A_a form a complex basis of the r-site operator space.
They are orthogonal across different supports under the inner product
<U,V>_sigma=Tr sigma U^dagger V, sigma=tau^tensorr. A size-k support has
Gram matrix G^tensork. Let delta=rho_r-sigma and R=delta sigma^(-1).
Then cyclicity of trace gives exactly

 <R,R>_sigma=Tr delta^2 sigma^(-1)=chi_P^2,
 <A_S,R>_sigma=Tr A_S^dagger delta=d_S.

The constant coefficient is zero. Finite-dimensional Gram Parseval and
(P1) therefore give

 chi_P^2
   =sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k) d_k^dagger(G^(-1))^tensork d_k
   <=sum_(k=2)^r binomial(r,k) kappa^(-k) T_k.           (P2)

There is no assumption that rho_r commutes with tau^tensorr. The exact
absence of k=1 follows from choosing the actual one-body marginal tau.

2. The new concentration-to-coefficient estimate.

For a real unit vector u in R^m set H=sum_a u_a F_a. Since
||H||_op<=||H||_HS=1, (H) applies to this Hermitian direction. Define

 Q_i=H_i-Tr(tau H)I, S=sum_i Q_i,
 P_u(z)=Tr rho_N product_(i=1)^N(I+zQ_i),  z in C.

The Q_i commute because they act on different sites. They have real
eigenvalues a_i in [-2,2]. For every real a and complex z, the elementary
inequality 1+x<=exp(x) gives

 |1+za|^2=1+2a Re z+a^2|z|^2
          <=exp(2a Re z+a^2|z|^2),

including points at which 1+za=0. Joint functional calculus therefore
gives the operator inequality

 |product_i(I+zQ_i)|
       <=exp[(Re z)S+(|z|^2/2)sum_i Q_i^2]
       <=exp(2N|z|^2) exp[(Re z)S].

The product is normal and diagonal in a joint spectral basis. Positivity
of the diagonal weights of rho_N implies
|Tr rho_N product_i(I+zQ_i)|<=Tr rho_N |product_i(I+zQ_i)|,
without any assumption that rho_N commutes with the Q_i. Applying (H)
only at the real parameter t=Re z yields the uniform circle bound

 |P_u(z)|<=A exp[(B+2)N|z|^2].                          (P3)

No complex-parameter version of (H), scalar substitution into a
noncommuting covariance, or sector-weight control has been used.

Let

 f_k(u)=sum_(a_1,...,a_k)d_(a_1,...,a_k)u_(a_1)...u_(a_k).

Permutation invariance gives the exact polynomial expansion

 P_u(z)=sum_(k=0)^N binomial(N,k) f_k(u) z^k.

For any R>0, Fourier orthogonality on the circle gives the coefficient
identity

 binomial(N,k) f_k(u)
   =[1/(2pi R^k)]int_(0)^(2pi) P_u(R exp(i theta))exp(-ik theta)dtheta.

Use (P3) and optimize R^2=k/[2(B+2)N]. For k>=1 this proves

 |f_k(u)|
   <= A[2e(B+2)N/k]^(k/2)/binomial(N,k).                (P4)

The circle identity is an elementary identity for a polynomial, so no
analytic continuation theorem or unverified concentration hypothesis
is needed.

3. Recover all tensor coefficients from all real directions.

Write

 alpha_(m,k)=m(m+2)...(m+2k-2).

For uniform u on the unit sphere in R^m, its 2k-th moment is the sum
over pairings divided by alpha_(m,k). One derivation is to take a
standard real Gaussian vector g=R u: its moment pairings follow by
expanding exp(|s|^2/2), and its radial moment is
E R^(2k)=m(m+2)...(m+2k-2), obtained by radial integration or induction.

Apply that identity to f_k(u)^2. The k! pairings connecting every index
of one copy of d_k to an index of the other contribute k! T_k. Every
other pairing contracts the same number of index pairs internally in
both real symmetric tensors and then gives the squared Frobenius norm
of the corresponding iterated trace. Those terms are nonnegative.
Consequently

 E f_k(u)^2 >= [k!/alpha_(m,k)] T_k.

Combining this identity with the uniform estimate (P4) yields

 T_k <= A^2 alpha_(m,k)/k!
          *[2e(B+2)N/k]^k/binomial(N,k)^2.             (P5)

This step is why concentration in every Hermitian direction controls
the full local state: permutation invariance makes its coefficient
tensors symmetric, and the sphere contraction controls their complete
Frobenius norms, including components produced by coherences.

4. Sum all degrees at least two with explicit constants.

For 1<=k<=r<N,

 binomial(r,k)/binomial(N,k)<=q^k,
 1/binomial(N,k)<=k!/[N(1-q)]^k.

The first bound follows term by term from
(r-l)/(N-l)<=r/N; the second follows from the falling factorial since
N-l>=N-r+1>=N(1-q). Insert (P5) into (P2):

 chi_P^2
   <=A^2 sum_(k=2)^r alpha_(m,k)
            *[2e(B+2)q/(kappa k(1-q))]^k.             (P6)

For m>=3 and k>=2,

 alpha_(m,k)<= (m+2k-2)^k <=[k(m/2+1)]^k.

The last inequality is equivalent to
(k-2)(m/2-1)>=0. Thus (P6) is bounded by

 A^2 sum_(k=2)^r [Lq/(1-q)]^k.

If y=Lq/(1-q)<1, extend this finite nonnegative sum to infinity to get
(T1). If q<=1/(2L+1), then y<=1/2. Also q<=1/2, since the feasible
kappa<=1/d implies L>1. Hence

 chi_P^2<=2A^2 L^2 q^2/(1-q)^2<=8A^2 L^2 q^2,

proving the first estimate of (T2). The proof applies for every N and
r in the explicit range, not just asymptotically.

5. Relative entropy in the noncommuting case.

Let rho_r have eigenvalues lambda_i and eigenvectors |i>, and sigma_r
have positive eigenvalues nu_j and eigenvectors |j>. The weights
lambda_i |<i|j>|^2 form a probability distribution. Jensen's inequality
for the logarithm gives

 D(rho_r||sigma_r)
   =sum_(i,j)lambda_i |<i|j>|^2 log(lambda_i/nu_j)
   <=log sum_(i,j)lambda_i^2 |<i|j>|^2/nu_j
   =log Tr rho_r^2 sigma_r^(-1)
   =log(1+chi_P^2)<=chi_P^2.

Terms with lambda_i=0 contribute zero. This proves the KL consequence
without a commutativity assumption.

AN ATTAINING QUADRATIC-RATE FAMILY; NO ENTANGLEMENT CLAIM

The following fully separable family shows that the exponent 2 cannot
be improved for the concentration class in general. Put

 D_0=diag(1,-1,0,...,0),
 tau_(+,N)=(I+D_0/sqrt(N))/d,
 tau_(-,N)=(I-D_0/sqrt(N))/d,
 rho_N=(tau_(+,N)^tensorN+tau_(-,N)^tensorN)/2.

This is permutation invariant and has tau_N=I/d>=kappa I for every
feasible kappa<=1/d. It is fully separable by its displayed construction.

To verify (H) for all Hermitian H with ||H||<=1, first observe the
elementary one-copy bound

 Tr eta exp[t(H-Tr eta H I)]<=exp(t^2/2)

for every state eta. Indeed Tr eta exp(tH) is the ordinary moment
generating function of a distribution on [-1,1] obtained from the
spectral projectors of H. The second derivative of its log is the
variance under exponential tilting and is at most 1; its centered log
has value and first derivative zero at t=0. Integrating the second
derivative proves the bound for positive and negative t.

For the two product branches, their one-copy means differ from the
mean under I/d by +/-Tr D_0 H/[d sqrt(N)]. Product factorization and
the previous bound therefore give

 Tr rho_N exp[t sum_i(H_i-Tr H/d I)]
    <=exp(Nt^2/2) cosh[t sqrt(N) Tr D_0 H/d]
    <=exp[(1/2+2/d^2)Nt^2].

Here |Tr D_0 H|<=2, and cosh(x)<=exp(x^2/2) follows by integrating
(log cosh x)''=sech^2 x<=1. Thus (H) holds with A=1 and
B=1/2+2/d^2, uniformly over all directions and both signs of t.

The marginal has the same two-branch form. Direct product-trace
calculation, with tau_N=I/d, gives exactly

 chi_P^2(rho_r||(I/d)^tensorr)
    =[(1+2/(dN))^r+(1-2/(dN))^r]/2-1
    >=2r(r-1)/(d^2 N^2).

Only even binomial terms remain, and they are nonnegative. For growing
sublinear r the leading coefficient is 2/d^2 times (r/N)^2. For fixed
r>=2 the leading term is 2r(r-1)/(d^2 N^2). This establishes sharpness
of the order for admissible fixed concentration parameters; it does
not optimize the universal constant in (T2).

To check the stated limits directly, the terms of even degree >=4 are
at most sum_(k>=4)(2r/(dN))^k/k!=O_d((r/N)^4) when r/N tends to zero.
For fixed r this is O_(d,r)(N^-4); for growing r the degree-two term
divided by (r/N)^2 tends to 2/d^2.
