\[ F(x,y)=\frac12x^2+\max(0,x-y), \qquad (x(0),y(0))=(1,0). \tag{23} \]
Before the hinge becomes inactive,
\[ x(t)=2e^{-t}-1,\qquad y(t)=t. \]
Let \(\alpha\) be the unique root
\[ 2e^{-\alpha}=1+\alpha, \qquad \alpha\approx0.374822528183623. \]
After that time,
\[ x(t)=\alpha e^{-(t-\alpha)}, \qquad y(t)=\alpha. \]
Hence the dynamically selected equilibrium is
\[ u_\infty=(0,\alpha). \]
But the minimizer set is
\[ \arg\min F=\{(0,y):y\ge0\}, \]
and the metric projection of the initial state is \((0,0)\). Jumping to that minimizer gives an order-one error.
Nor does taking a fixed number of very large implicit steps fix the problem. For sufficiently large step \(h\), the first proximal state is exactly
\[ (x_1,y_1)= \left(\frac1{h+2},\frac1{h+2}\right). \]
Subsequent equal-sized steps keep that second coordinate. Sending the physical horizon to infinity with a fixed number of equal steps therefore selects the wrong limit.
The compiled trajectory preserves information that static minimization and indiscriminate large stepping discard.
For the nonlinear example above, I constructed and checked a rational certificate with 535 intervals, terminating at time approximately \(21.09755\), then remaining at the exact minimizer
\[ s= \left(0,\frac{805659543}{2147483648}\right). \]
Its action upper bound is approximately
\[ J=9.5720456943\times10^{-6}. \]
The exact rational comparison
\[ \boxed{2J\le\frac1{40000}} \]
passes. Consequently, the certificate proves
\[ \boxed{ \sup_{t\ge0}\|v(t)-u(t)\|\le0.005. } \]
This is an all-time mathematical certificate, not a numerical comparison at selected sample times. The endpoint’s actual error, bounded using a rational Taylor-series bracket for \(\alpha\), is below \(0.000341932\).
