## 3. Conditional entropy theorem

**Theorem 1.** Under (CB),

    log |F_p^N/W| <= h_0 N,
    h_0 = log 3 + (4/3) log 2.

Consequently,

    p^(codim W) <= K_0^N < 8^N,
    K_0 = 3 * 2^(4/3) = 7.559526299369239...

The prime p = 2 also satisfies the stated cardinality bound trivially, without requiring this construction.

### Proof: construction of the joint generator

For a state z in F_p^N, put

    x_i(z) = z_i when z_i != 0, and x_i(z) = 1 when z_i = 0.

Use a solution of (1) for x(z). For each legal direction w, introduce transitions

    z -> z+w and z -> z-w,

at rate lambda_{x(z),w}/2 each. Duplicate destinations have their rates added. Every transition remains in the same coset of W. The total rate is finite; indeed sum_w lambda_{x,w} <= sum_i r_i because every legal direction is nonzero.

Let Q denote this finite-state generator. For a scalar function f:F_p->R and the coordinate projection pr_i, direct evaluation gives

    Q(f o pr_i)(z) = r_i Lf(z_i),                              (2)

where

    Lf(b) = [f(0)+f(2b)-2f(b)]/2,    b != 0,
    Lf(0) = [f(1)+f(-1)-2f(0)]/2.

Indeed, each direction active at a nonzero coordinate replaces its value by 0 or twice its value, with equal rates. At a zero coordinate, the two destinations are 1 and -1. Summing the active rates gives exactly r_i.

Equation (2) is the essential interface. It does not assert independence of coordinates. It proves that every coordinate has the correct generator in ordinary elapsed time, not merely when observed at its own jumps.

### Proof: the scalar stationary law

Every nonzero b resets to zero at rate 1/2. From zero the scalar process enters +1 or -1, and successive non-reset moves double the current value. Therefore its unique recurrent class is

    Omega = {0} union {+2^k, -2^k : k >= 0} mod p.

All states reach this class; within it every state can reach zero and every state can be reached from zero. Hence L has a unique stationary law pi.

An explicit description of pi is:

* output 0 with probability 1/3;
* with probability 2/3, draw a uniform sign S in {-1,+1} and an independent integer T >= 0 with Pr(T=k)=2^(-k-1), then output S 2^T mod p.

To verify stationarity directly, let nu be the law of S 2^T. The geometric identity gives nu = (1/2)law(S) + (1/2)law(2Y), where Y has law nu. Combined with pi(0)=1/3 and pi on nonzero states equal to (2/3)nu, this is exactly pi L=0. Modular collisions only combine masses; the verification does not require the signed powers to be distinct.

The sign entropy is log 2 and the geometric entropy is 2 log 2. Encoding the mixture before reducing modulo p gives

    H(pi) <= h(1/3) + (2/3)(log 2 + 2 log 2)
          = log 3 + (4/3)log 2
          = h_0.                                               (3)

Here h denotes binary entropy. Passing from the encoding to a residue cannot increase entropy.

### Proof: quotient entropy without independence

For each coset c, choose any stationary probability measure mu_c for the restriction of Q to c. Such a measure exists on every nonempty finite state space; irreducibility of the joint chain is unnecessary.

Stationarity and (2) imply, for every i and every f,

    0 = E_{mu_c} Q(f o pr_i) = r_i E_{mu_c} Lf(Z_i).

Since r_i > 0, the i-th marginal is stationary for L and therefore equals pi.

Now choose a coset label C uniformly from F_p^N/W and sample Z from mu_C. The coset label is determined by Z. Every coordinate of Z has marginal pi. Thus

    log |F_p^N/W| = H(C)
                  <= H(Z)
                  <= sum_i H(Z_i)
                  = N H(pi)
                  <= h_0 N.

This proves the theorem. No mixing estimate, product distribution, or independence approximation is used. QED.

### A weaker sufficient hypothesis

It actually suffices to choose a separate fixed positive vector r^(c) for each coset, provided it belongs to C(W,x(z)) for every z in that coset. The same proof works because multiplying a scalar generator by a positive constant does not change pi. The common global vector (CB) is a convenient stronger interface, not a logically necessary condition for the entropy conclusion.

The counterexample in Section 5 defeats even this coset-specific fixed-clock relaxation in at least one coset. It does not exclude arbitrary other stochastic constructions or other proofs of the quotient bound.

## 4. Why a correct jump chain is not enough

A coordinate can have the right transition law at its successive jumps while its occupation law at ordinary times is wrong, because the waiting time can depend on its current state or on other coordinates.

For a two-state illustration, the embedded jump chain alternates 0,1,0,1,... regardless of rates. The continuous-time chain with rate epsilon from 0 to 1 and rate 1 from 1 to 0 has stationary law

    pi(0)=1/(1+epsilon),  pi(1)=epsilon/(1+epsilon).

Thus the jump chain alone does not determine the physical-time marginal. This elementary example establishes the logical distinction; it is not itself a counterexample to every claim of the withdrawn paper. The exact generator identity in Theorem 1 avoids the inference entirely.
