## Decisive failed thermal transfer: exact check

Let K=[diag(1,a)]^tensor n with a=1/4 and r=3/2. Each factor has polynomial product_i(1+a z_i w_i), strictly zero-free in radius r because a r^2=9/16<1. After T factors, M=[diag(1,b)]^tensor n, b=a^T.

Its best rank-one relative operator error is b. But its normalized Gibbs-like state has trace distance

D(M/Tr M, |0^n><0^n|)=1-(1+b)^(-n).

For n=4096, T=4, the operator error is exactly 1/256 while trace distance is approximately 0.999999883902; the lower bound D>999/1000 was checked with exact rational arithmetic. The omitted partition mass is exponential in n at fixed b. Splitting a fixed total imaginary time into more slices leaves the total b unchanged. Therefore small relative operator error alone cannot establish normalized finite-temperature sampling, partition-function accuracy or state preparation.

This is an elementary semantic counterexample, not a claimed novel result, not an objection to Theorem B, and not a lower bound on every algorithm for local observables. Product systems themselves are easy. It is a decisive rejection of the direct proposed inference.
