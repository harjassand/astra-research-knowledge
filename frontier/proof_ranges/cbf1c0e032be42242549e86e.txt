## Theorem D: an exact full-separability bound

For every t>=0,

\[
\max_{\sigma\in\mathrm{SEP}_N}\operatorname{tr}(\sigma e^{-tJ^2})
=b_N(t):=\frac1{\binom{2n}{n}}\sum_{j=0}^n m_{N,j}e^{-tj(j+1)}. \tag{D1}
\]

The bound is attained by the pure product state with n spins up and n spins down along any one common axis, and also by its common-rotation/permutation twirl. It is an optimization over **all** fully separable states, not only invariant states. At t=infinity it gives the singlet-projector bound 1/(n+1).

More generally, for any conjugation-invariant probability measure mu on SU(2),

\[
\left|\int\prod_{i=1}^N\langle r_i|U|r_i\rangle\,d\mu(U)\right|
\le\int|\langle\uparrow|U|\uparrow\rangle|^{N}d\mu(U). \tag{D2}
\]

The right side is exactly the expectation of the corresponding averaged tensor-power operator on the balanced product. This statement about an integral does not claim that every central positive operator has such a positive group measure.

### Proof of the extremality statement

For a pure product, apply triangle inequality and Holder with N equal exponents:

\[
\left|\int\prod_i\langle r_i|U|r_i\rangle d\mu\right|
\le\prod_i\left(\int|\langle r_i|U|r_i\rangle|^N d\mu\right)^{1/N}.
\]

Every factor has the same value, because pure qubit states are a single SU(2) orbit and mu is conjugation-invariant. For SU(2), the two diagonal entries of U in any orthonormal basis are complex conjugates. The balanced product therefore has integrand exactly |U_00|^(2n), attaining the bound pointwise. Mixed product factors and arbitrary separable mixtures follow by convexity.

To obtain e^(-tJ^2), one does not need to assume a signed Fourier representation is positive. For an integer L, let each independent rotation have angle a_L=sqrt(6t/L), with a uniformly random spatial axis. Its distribution is conjugation-invariant. In the N-qubit representation,

\[
\mathbb E e^{ia_L\widehat u\cdot J}=I-tJ^2/L+O_N(L^{-2}).
\]

The tensor-power average of the product of L independent rotations is the Lth power of this matrix, which tends in operator norm to e^(-tJ^2). The convolution measure remains a conjugation-invariant **probability** measure. Apply (D2) at each L and pass to the limit. This also reconstructs the required positive heat-measure representation without importing a new research theorem.

Finally, the diagonal entries of P_{N,j} are equal on computational strings of magnetization zero, because site permutations act transitively on those strings. The trace of P_{N,j} on that magnetization subspace is m_{N,j}; the subspace dimension is binom(2n,n). Thus the balanced-product spin probabilities are m_{N,j}/binom(2n,n), proving (D1).

## Independent finite-size entanglement check

The t=infinity case proves max_SEP tr(sigma P_{N,0})=1/(n+1). For R_N, that probability is m_{N,0}/Z_N. Set q_j=m_{N,j}/binom(2n,n). Since sum_j q_j=1,

\[
Z_N/\binom{2n}{n}=\sum_jq_j(2j+1)f_j\le1.
\]

For every even N>=4, q_2>0 and (2*2+1)f_2=1/3<1, so this inequality is strict. Hence R_N has singlet probability >1/(n+1) and is entangled. For N=2 equality holds. This supplies an all-size proof independent of the C_N calculation. For N=4 the exact target singlet probability is 3/8, exceeding the separable maximum 1/3.
