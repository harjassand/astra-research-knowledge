ALL-POSITIVE-PARAMETER NONLINEAR ROW-STRESS CALIBRATION
Internal exact result, 2026-10-16

Scope and status
----------------
The result below is a restricted conformal Jensen theorem, for every r>0,
and explicit nonseparated stress-conservation examples. It does not prove the
unrestricted quasiconvexity of J_r. Historical originality and external
mathematical validation are not established. The exact Gram identity and
positive definiteness have an executable rational certificate. The analytic
integration argument is given explicitly below.

1. Integrand and fixed stress
----------------------------
For A=[[a,b],[c,d]], put u=a^2+c^2, v=b^2+d^2, j=ad-bc and

 J_r(A)=3r/(8(r+2))*(u+v-2j)^2 + r(r-1)/6*u^2+j^2+r*u*j.

For arbitrary real s and every r>0 define

 S_1(b)=-(r+2)s^2 b + 7r/(6(r+2))*b^3,
 S_2(a)=(r+2)s^3 +(5r/2+2)s^2 a +7r/4*s*a^2
                       +r(4r+3)/(12(r+2))*a^3.

Let C_r=(r+2)(r+3)/6 and L_r=(r+2)(2r+3)/3. The pointwise residual is

 G_{r,s}(A)=J_r(sI+A)-C_r*s^4-L_r*s^3*a-S_1(b)c-S_2(a)d.

The certificate proves, for EVERY real a,b,c,d,s and EVERY r>0,

 G_{r,s}(A) >= r/(512(r+2)) *
                  [5/8*|A|^4+s^2(a^2+c^2+d^2)].                (P)

The constant 1/512 is an exact certified lower bound, not an optimized
constant. Here |A| is the Frobenius norm. The stresses differ substantially
from the r=1 cubic-decoupling calibration; their choice is part of the new
all-r construction.

2. Exact algebraic certificate
------------------------------
Set h=ad-bc+s(a+d) and use the ordered polynomial vectors

 z0=(h, rcs, rcd, rc^2, rbc, ras, rad, rac, rab, ra^2),

 z1=(ds, d^2, cs, cd, c^2, bd, bc, b^2,
                         as, ad, ac, ab, a^2,
                         r(a^2+2as+c^2), ras, rcs).

ALL_R_STRESS_EXACT_CERTIFICATE.json contains constant rational symmetric
matrices Q0 (10 by 10) and Q1 (16 by 16), encoded together with a block
split at index 10. It gives the polynomial identity

 (r+2)G_{r,s}(A)=z0^T Q0 z0 + r*z1^T Q1 z1.                    (I)

Its four disjoint positive-definite principal blocks, with zero entries
between the blocks, have zero-based index lists

 [0,3,4,5,6,9], [1,2,7,8],
 [10,11,14,16,17,18,19,22,23,24], [12,13,15,20,21,25].

The certificate stores all exact LDL pivots. verify_all_r.py independently
reconstructs J_r and (I), verifies every coefficient and every LDL pivot,
and verifies Q1-(1/512)I positive definite by 16 further rational pivots.
No floating-point sign or numerical floor is a premise of this proof.
The discovery used numerical semidefinite feasibility, then replaced it
with exact rational coefficients and exact identities.

To obtain (P), discard the nonnegative Q0 term in (I) and use Q1>=I/512.
The first thirteen entries of z1 have squared sum

 a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4
 +a^2b^2+a^2c^2+a^2d^2+b^2c^2+b^2d^2+c^2d^2
 +s^2(a^2+c^2+d^2).

The first ten terms equal (|A|^4+a^4+b^4+c^4+d^4)/2 and are at least
5|A|^4/8, by Cauchy-Schwarz. The last three entries of z1 add nonnegative
squares. This proves (P) exactly. At r=0 the residual has the useful anchor
G_{0,s}(A)=h^2, but the theorem and its strict coercivity concern r>0.

3. Restricted Jensen theorem for a stress-conservation class
-----------------------------------------------------------
Use normalized averages on T^2=(R/2pi Z)^2. Let f,g be periodic W^{1,4}
functions and set a=f_x,b=f_y,c=g_x,d=g_y. Suppose the vector field

 S(f)=(S_1(f_y),S_2(f_x))

is divergence free in distributions. Then

 <J_r(sI+D(f,g))>-J_r(sI)
 >= 5r/(4096(r+2))*<|D(f,g)|^4>
       +r*s^2/(512(r+2))*<f_x^2+g_x^2+g_y^2>.                (T)

Proof. Periodicity gives <f_x>=0. The stress is in L^{4/3}, so its
vanishing distributional divergence gives <S(f).Dg>=0, first for smooth
g and then for W^{1,4} g by density and Holder. Integrating (P) gives (T).
The Sobolev expressions are integrable because J_r is quartic and the
stress is cubic. No restriction is placed on g beyond periodic W^{1,4}.
Equality in the Jensen inequality forces D(f,g)=0 almost everywhere,
because the first constant on the right of (T) is strictly positive.

For smooth f the stress-conservation equation is exactly

 K_{r,s}(f_x,f_y)*f_xy=0,

 K_{r,s}(a,b)=r/[4(r+2)] *
    [6(r+2)s^2+14(r+2)sa+(4r+3)a^2+14b^2].                 (D)

For nonsmooth f the divergence formulation is primary: multiplying
f_xy by a discontinuous coefficient need not be defined.

4. All-r separable first-row corollary
-------------------------------------
Take f(x,y)=F(x)+H(y), with periodic W^{1,4} functions F and H. Then
S_1(f_y) depends only on y and S_2(f_x) only on x, so their divergence is
zero in distributions. Therefore (T) holds for EVERY r>0, EVERY real s,
EVERY such F,H, and EVERY periodic W^{1,4} g.

This is the requested parametric extension of the r=1 surviving mechanism.
Its two first-row profiles may be arbitrary; it is not a finite-mode claim.
It also establishes C_r^2<=4A_rQ_r for each such fixed compatible field,
by applying the Jensen inequality for every s and minimizing the resulting
quadratic in s. This uses the original integrand, with no completion term.

5. Explicit nonseparated stress-conservation examples for every r>0
------------------------------------------------------------------
The divergence condition is strictly broader than f=F(x)+H(y). The
following construction is exact, lives on the same square torus, and
allows arbitrary g as before.

Fix r>0, s!=0 and let

 A_r=(8r+13)/(6(r+2)), t_+=1/4,
 t_-=[-(92r+181)+sqrt(912r^2+6024r+8373)]/[8(8r+13)].

Then t_-<0<t_+. Let theta=-t_-/(t_+-t_-), which lies strictly between
zero and one. Choose a periodic continuous piecewise affine F on R/2pi Z
whose derivative is t_+ on an interval of length 2pi*theta, and t_- on its
complement. The mean derivative is zero, so this F exists and is Lipschitz.
Set

 f(x,y)=s*F(2x+y).

This f is nonseparated whenever F is nonconstant. Its gradient is
(a,b)=s(2t,t) with t taking precisely t_- and t_+.

For these gradients the normal stress flux is

 2S_1(st)+S_2(2st)
   =(r+2)s^3+2r*s^3*P_r(t),
 P_r(t)=t[3/2+(7/2)t+A_r*t^2].

The quotient [P_r(t)-P_r(1/4)]/(t-1/4) equals

 A_r*t^2+(7/2+A_r/4)t+(3/2+7/8+A_r/16).

Its discriminant is

 (304r^2+2008r+2791)/[192(r+2)^2]>0.

The displayed t_- is one of its two negative roots. Its sign is certified
by (92r+181)^2-(912r^2+6024r+8373)
=4(8r+13)(236r+469)>0. Hence P_r(t_-)=P_r(t_+). The normal flux is the
same in both gradient states, so its distributional derivative along
2x+y vanishes. Thus div S(f)=0, including the interfaces, and (T) applies
for every arbitrary periodic W^{1,4} g.

These examples exhibit membership in the nonlinear stress-conservation
class and receive the explicit quantitative margin (T). Their qualitative
Jensen inequality is already covered by the triangular-gradient-measure
theorem of Harris, Kirchheim and Lin, after a fixed change of input
coordinates, row/column swaps, and affine translation. They are not a new
qualitative Jensen class. Reference: Theorem 2.4 in "Two-by-two upper
triangular matrices and Morrey's conjecture", arXiv:1708.07849v2,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07849v2. No conclusion is asserted for every
one-phase first row, every piecewise affine first row, or every smooth
first row from the stress equation alone. The full two-profile theorem
in Section 4 does not satisfy the triangular-support hypothesis in
general; failure of that hypothesis does not establish nonlaminateness.

6. Exact defect formula for an unrestricted smooth first row
-----------------------------------------------------------
For arbitrary smooth periodic f,g the same pointwise certificate yields

 <J_r(sI+D(f,g))>-J_r(sI)
 >= 5r/(4096(r+2))*<|D(f,g)|^4>
       +r*s^2/(512(r+2))*<f_x^2+g_x^2+g_y^2>
       -<g*K_{r,s}(f_x,f_y)*f_xy>.                           (E)

Indeed <S(f).Dg>=-<g div S(f)> and (D) gives the exact divergence.
The last term is the unresolved mixed-derivative defect for general f;
its sign is not controlled by the present argument. A coarse bound on it
is ||g-<g>||_4 ||K(f_x,f_y)f_xy||_{4/3}. This is a sufficient estimate,
not a proof of the unrestricted sharp inequality.
