## 1. Exact setting

All norms and averages below use normalized Lebesgue measure on
`T^2 = (R / 2 pi Z)^2`. Let `phi = (f,g)` be an actual periodic map in
`W^{1,4}(T^2; R^2)`, and write

\[
 a=f_x,\quad b=f_y,\quad c=g_x,\quad d=g_y,\qquad
 q=\sigma a+d,\quad \delta=\|(q,c)\|_2,
 \qquad \sigma>0.
\]

In particular, `E_x a = E_x c = 0`, where `E_x` averages over the horizontal variable, and `average(q) = 0`. Compatibility holds distributionally:

\[
 a_y=b_x,\qquad q_x=\sigma a_x+c_y.
\]

The assumptions about exact gradients matter. A periodic row-curl-free matrix field may have nonzero harmonic parts. The constant field

\[
 (a,b,c,d)=(1,0,0,-\sigma)
\]

is curl-free and has zero defect, but its cubic `a^3` has average one. Thus the source's result cannot be enlarged to all periodic curl-free fields with arbitrary means. It applies to the gradient perturbations in its conformal-background expansion, whose potentials are periodic.

For a bounded domain, the safe zero-boundary class is `W_0^{1,4}`. Its zero extension is an exact Sobolev gradient in a surrounding square. Passing between normalized and unnormalized norms introduces the same area factor on both sides, since `1/2 + 2/4 = 1`. Hence the finite cubic inequalities transfer without a domain-area loss. No assertion about an arbitrary notion of pointwise boundary vanishing is needed.

## 2. Reconstruction of the mixed derivative estimate

For `1 < p_0,p_1 < infinity` and
`1/t = (1/p_0 + 1/p_1)/2`, the claimed estimate is

\[
 \|\psi_{xy}\|_t
 \leq C_{p_0,p_1}\|\psi_{xx}\|_{p_0}^{1/2}
                         \|\psi_{yy}\|_{p_1}^{1/2}.       \tag{A1}
\]

Here is a complete reduction to the usual periodic multiplier theorem and scalar three-lines lemma. Start with a trigonometric polynomial and put
`Q = (I-E_x)(I-E_y)`. Both conditional expectations are contractions on every `L^p`; `Q` has norm at most four. It removes the coordinate frequency axes, changes neither mixed derivative, and only decreases the needed endpoint derivative bounds up to an absolute constant.

For `u=Q psi`, define

\[
 F(z)=e^{(z-1/2)^2}|D_x|^{2(1-z)}|D_y|^{2z}u.
\]

On nonzero integer frequencies the multiplier `m_tau(n)=|n|^{i tau}` has modulus one and dyadic discrete variation at most `C |tau|`. Set its value at zero to zero. The one-dimensional periodic Marcinkiewicz theorem gives

\[
 \|m_\tau(D)\|_{L^p\to L^p}\leq C_p(1+|\tau|).
\]

Applying such operators one coordinate at a time is legitimate by scalar Fubini: no unproved vector-valued multiplier estimate is required. On `Re z=0`, `F(z)` is a product of two such imaginary-power multipliers applied to `-u_xx`; on `Re z=1`, it is a product applied to `-u_yy`. Their norms grow at most quadratically in `|Im z|`, while the Gaussian has modulus `exp(1/4-(Im z)^2)`. Consequently

\[
 \sup_\tau\|F(i\tau)\|_{p_0}\leq C\|\psi_{xx}\|_{p_0},
 \qquad
 \sup_\tau\|F(1+i\tau)\|_{p_1}\leq C\|\psi_{yy}\|_{p_1}.
\]

To make the varying target exponent explicit, let a simple dual function `h` satisfy `||h||_{t'}=1`, and set, on its nonzero support,

\[
 h_z=\operatorname{phase}(h)
      |h|^{t'((1-z)/p_0'+z/p_1')}.
\]

The boundary norms are `||h_{i tau}||_{p_0'}=1` and
`||h_{1+i tau}||_{p_1'}=1`; also `h_{1/2}=h`.
Apply the scalar three-lines lemma to `average(F(z) h_z)`. Duality gives

\[
 \||D_x||D_y|u\|_t
 \leq C\|\psi_{xx}\|_{p_0}^{1/2}
         \|\psi_{yy}\|_{p_1}^{1/2}.
\]

Two periodic Hilbert transforms convert its left side to the mixed derivative. Their boundedness is another immediate application of the same one-dimensional multiplier theorem to the sign multiplier. If either endpoint derivative is zero, every off-axis Fourier coefficient is zero, so the inequality is still valid.

For distributions with the two endpoint derivatives in the specified spaces, convolve with two-dimensional Fejer kernels. These approximants are trigonometric polynomials, their endpoint derivatives converge strongly in their finite `L^p` spaces, and (A1), applied to differences, makes their mixed derivatives Cauchy in `L^t`. The limit is the distributional mixed derivative. Thus the stated density step is valid. Arbitrary sharp Fourier cutoffs are unnecessary.

There is no endpoint use at `p=1` or `p=infinity`. The optional extension in the source should be read with **finite** `s>2`, so `p=s/(s-2)` is also strictly between one and infinity. Neither limiting endpoint follows from this proof.

## 3. Hodge step, finite cubic class, and necessity

Subtract the irrelevant means of the two potentials. Define periodic zero-mean `chi` and `psi` by

\[
 \Delta\chi=q,\qquad \Delta\psi=\sigma b-c.
\]

The standard Fourier Hodge decomposition is

\[
 (\sigma f,g)=\nabla\chi+(\psi_y,-\psi_x).
\]

It has no harmonic remainder because the vector-field means were subtracted. Its derivative identities are exactly

\[
 \sigma a=\chi_{xx}+\psi_{xy},\quad
 \sigma b=\chi_{xy}+\psi_{yy},\quad
 c=\chi_{xy}-\psi_{xx}.                               \tag{A2}
\]

The periodic second-order Riesz multipliers `partial_ij Delta^{-1}` are bounded on each finite interior `L^p`. In particular, writing `M=||D phi||_4`,

\[
 \|\psi_{xx}\|_2\leq C_\sigma\delta,\qquad
 \|\psi_{yy}\|_4\leq C_\sigma M.
\]

Apply (A1) at `(p_0,p_1)=(2,4)`, where `t=8/3`, and use ordinary interpolation for `chi_xx`:

\[
 \|\psi_{xy}\|_{8/3}^2\leq C_\sigma\delta M,
 \qquad
 \|\chi_{xx}\|_{8/3}^2
 \leq\|\chi_{xx}\|_2\|\chi_{xx}\|_4
 \leq C_\sigma\delta M.
\]

Equation (A2) proves

\[
 \boxed{\|a\|_{8/3}^2\leq C_\sigma\delta M.}           \tag{A3}
\]

This is a nonlinear estimate. No linear estimate across the rank drop has been used.

For a homogeneous cubic, introduce the invertible polynomial-coordinate change

\[
 \widetilde P(a,b,c,q)=P(a,b,c,q-\sigma a).
\]

The source's criterion is precisely that the coefficients of `b^3` and `a b^2` in `P tilde` are zero. Then

\[
 \widetilde P=qP_2+cR_2+a^2(\lambda a+\mu b).
\]

Hölder bounds the first two terms by `C delta M^2`. For the remaining two, the exponents are exact:

\[
 \|a^2v\|_1\leq\|a\|_{8/3}^2\|v\|_4
 \quad (v=a,b),\qquad 2/(8/3)+1/4=1.
\]

Use (A3). This proves the absolute bound, which implies the signed bound.

For necessity of the signed bound, the source's two sequences work without a hidden incompatible matrix prescription. The pure shear has `b=cos y+cos 2y`, zero defect, and `average(b^3)=3/4`. For the second test let

\[
 \psi=-\cos(x+y)-\cos y-\tfrac12\cos(x+2y),
\]
\[
 f_N=N^{-1}\psi_y(x,Ny),\qquad
 g_N=-\sigma N^{-2}\psi_x(x,Ny).
\]

Putting `A=psi_xy`, `B=psi_yy`, `R=psi_xx`, its exact derivatives are

\[
 a_N=N^{-1}A(x,Ny),\quad b_N=B(x,Ny),\quad
 c_N=-\sigma N^{-2}R(x,Ny),\quad q_N=0.                \tag{A4}
\]

The gradients are uniformly bounded even in `L^infinity`, and integer `N` preserves every torus average. Exact Fourier multiplication gives

\[
 \langle AB^2\rangle=\tfrac32,\quad
 \langle R^2\rangle=\tfrac58.
\]

Thus `average(a_N b_N^2)=3/(2N)` but
`delta_N=sigma sqrt(5/8)/N^2`. After excluding `b^3`, all other monomials have order `N^{-2}` or smaller. A nonzero `a b^2` coefficient contradicts the claimed signed estimate.

There are `binomial(6,3)=20` homogeneous cubic monomials in four variables, and these are two independent coefficient conditions. The finite signed and absolute classes are therefore both exactly 18-dimensional. Strong `W^{1,4}` convolution approximation preserves compatibility and all terms; it gives the source's stated Sobolev extension.

## 4. Horizontal heat: identity and a direct Sobolev justification

Write `P_t=exp(t partial_x^2)`, and apply it to each gradient entry. For smooth fields, compatibility gives

\[
 a a_xq_x-a b_xc_x-\sigma a a_x^2
 =a(a_xc_y-a_yc_x)=\det D(a^2/2,c).
\]

Its periodic integral vanishes. Also

\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\langle a_t^3\rangle
 =-6\langle a_t a_{t,x}^2\rangle.
\]

Since `E_x a=0`, horizontal heat sends `a_t` strongly to zero. Combining these facts gives exactly

\[
 \boxed{\langle a^3\rangle
 =\frac6\sigma\int_0^\infty
   \langle a_ta_{t,x}q_{t,x}-a_tb_{t,x}c_{t,x}\rangle\,dt.} \tag{A5}
\]

The source's quartic has Hessian entries

\[
 K_{aa}=(1+\theta)a^2+b^2,\quad K_{ab}=2ab,
 \quad K_{bb}=a^2+4b^2/\theta.
\]

Subtracting `a^2(X^2+Y^2)` gives precisely
`theta a^2 X^2 + 4abXY + 4b^2Y^2/theta + b^2X^2`,
the claimed sum of two squares. Thus the factors in the source's heat budget and final constant `18/sigma^2` are correct.

There is a direct interpretation for `W^{1,4}` potentials that avoids relying on an unspecified convergence of singular time integrals. For each positive time, horizontal heat derivatives of all entries belong to `L^4`. Compatibility gives

\[
 (a_t)_y=(b_t)_x\in L^4,\qquad
 (c_t)_y=(q_t)_x-\sigma(a_t)_x\in L^4.
\]

Hence `a_t,c_t` belong to full `W^{1,4}` for positive time. The Jacobian identity is valid by Sobolev density, since `a_t^2/2` belongs to `W^{1,2}` and `c_t` to `W^{1,4}`.

For arbitrary `a,b` in `L^4`, differentiate the quartic heat energy on a compact positive-time interval and integrate over `y`. The chain rule and horizontal integration by parts yield the budget below. The quadratic heat-energy identity applies to `q,c` in `L^2`. Consequently both terms on the right of (A5) are absolutely integrable in full space-time, by Cauchy--Schwarz:

\[
 \int_0^\infty\langle|a_ta_{t,x}q_{t,x}|\rangle dt
 \leq
 \left(\int_0^\infty\langle a_t^2a_{t,x}^2\rangle dt\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\int_0^\infty\langle q_{t,x}^2\rangle dt\right)^{1/2},
\]

and similarly for `a_t b_{t,x} c_{t,x}`. Integrate first on `[epsilon,R]`, then let `epsilon` decrease to zero and `R` increase to infinity. Strong heat continuity in `L^4` supplies the initial cubic limit, and `E_x a=0` supplies the terminal limit. Thus (A5) has a canonical absolutely convergent heat-integral meaning for the full claimed Sobolev class.

## 5. A stronger explicit heat bound

This is an internal deduction from the source's valid budget. Let

\[
 A=\langle a^4\rangle,\quad B=\langle b^4\rangle,
 \quad B_0=\langle(E_xb)^4\rangle,
 \quad M=\langle a^2b^2\rangle,
\]
\[
 D=\langle(q-E_xq)^2+c^2\rangle.
\]

The exact terminal heat state is `(a_t,b_t) -> (0,E_x b)` strongly in `L^4`. Thus the quartic budget is

\[
 \int_0^\infty\langle a_t^2(a_{t,x}^2+b_{t,x}^2)\rangle dt
 \leq\frac{1+\theta}{12}A+\frac12 M+
           \frac{B-B_0}{3\theta}.                     \tag{A6}
\]

Conditional Jensen gives `B-B_0 >= 0`. Parseval, or the exact quadratic energy identity, gives

\[
 \int_0^\infty\langle q_{t,x}^2+c_{t,x}^2\rangle dt
 =\frac12D,                                           \tag{A7}
\]

where `E_x c=0` has been used. Apply space-time Cauchy--Schwarz to (A5), then optimize the positive scalar `theta`:

\[
 \boxed{
 |\langle a^3\rangle|^2
 \leq\frac{18}{\sigma^2}\,D
 \left[\frac A{12}+\frac M2+
          \frac13\sqrt{A(B-B_0)}\right].}              \tag{A8}
\]

One-sided limits give (A8) when `A=0` or `B=B_0`. It improves the reported bound because `D <= average(q^2+c^2)` and `B-B_0 <= B`. It remains a non-sharp signed cubic estimate and does not resolve the target inequality.

## 6. Uniform integrability: proof and the exact 15-dimensional class

First reconstruct the source's claim. Suppose `D phi_n` is bounded in `L^4`, its fourth powers are uniformly integrable, and `delta_n -> 0`. Periodic exact gradients satisfy the determinant-null identity

\[
 \sigma\langle a_n^2\rangle
 =\langle a_n q_n-b_n c_n\rangle.                      \tag{A9}
\]

For smooth maps this follows by integration by parts; strong Sobolev convolution extends it. The right side tends to zero by the bounded `L^2` norms of `a_n,b_n` and the vanishing defect. Therefore `a_n -> 0` in `L^2`, and `c_n,q_n,d_n` also tend to zero in `L^2`. They tend to zero in measure. Uniform integrability of fourth powers implies

\[
 a_n,c_n,q_n,d_n\longrightarrow0\quad\hbox{strongly in }L^4. \tag{A10}
\]

The source's target cubic is

\[
 \widetilde C_r
 =q[2aq-(r+2)a^2+rc^2-2bc]-\kappa a^3-\kappa ac^2,
 \qquad\kappa=r(r+2)/3.
\]

Every quadratic factor paired with `q` or `c` tends to zero in `L^2` by (A10) and boundedness of `b` in `L^4`. The pure cubic satisfies

\[
 \frac{|\langle a_n^3\rangle|}{\delta_n}
 \leq C_\sigma\|D\phi_n\|_4\|a_n\|_4\longrightarrow0
\]

by (A3). For fixed `r>0`, `delta` and `sqrt(Q_r)` are equivalent norms because `kappa>0`. This proves the source's asserted little-o conclusion. No coercivity of `J_r` is needed for this implication with its stated boundedness and uniform-integrability assumptions. The further inference from `A_r=1` to an `L^4` bound requires the separate pointwise coercivity claim; this analytic audit does not substitute the finite verifier for that claim.

### New theorem: complete characterization of the little-o property

For a real homogeneous cubic `P`, the following are equivalent.

**(i)** For every sequence of smooth periodic potentials such that

\[
 \sup_n\|D\phi_n\|_4<\infty,\quad
 \{|D\phi_n|^4\}\text{ is uniformly integrable},\quad
 0<\delta_n\longrightarrow0,
\]

one has

\[
 \frac{\langle P(D\phi_n)\rangle}{\delta_n}
 \longrightarrow0.                                   \tag{A11}
\]

**(ii)** In the coordinates `(a,b,c,q)`, the coefficients of the following five monomials in `P tilde` vanish:

\[
 \boxed{b^3,\quad ab^2,\quad qb^2,\quad cb^2,\quad a^2b.} \tag{A12}
\]

**(iii)** There is a representation

\[
 \widetilde P=qP_2+cR_2+\lambda a^3,                    \tag{A13}
\]

where neither homogeneous quadratic `P_2` nor `R_2` has a `b^2` term.

**(iv)** There is a finite constant `K`, depending on `sigma` and `P`, such that every smooth periodic potential satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \|P(D\phi)\|_1
 \leq K\,\|(q,c)\|_2\,\|D\phi\|_4\,\|(a,c,q)\|_4.
 }                                                     \tag{A14}
\]

There are exactly 15 independent allowed cubic coefficients. The same equivalence holds if all sequences in (i), and all maps in (iv), are allowed to be `W^{1,4}` rather than smooth.

**Sufficiency and the refined bound.** The algebraic equivalence of (ii) and (iii) is immediate by assigning each monomial containing `q` or `c` to one of those factors. Put `w=(a,c,q)`. Every quadratic in (A13) contains at least one component of `w`; hence its absolute value is at most `C |w| |D phi|` (with constants depending on `sigma` and the polynomial). Hölder bounds the first two terms of (A13) in absolute `L^1` by

\[
 C\delta\|D\phi\|_4\|w\|_4.
\]

The remaining term obeys

\[
 \|a^3\|_1\leq\|a\|_{8/3}^2\|a\|_4
 \leq C_\sigma\delta\|D\phi\|_4\|w\|_4
\]

by (A3). This proves (iv), including its absolute-value form. Under the assumptions of (i), (A10) gives `||w_n||_4 -> 0`, so (iv) proves even the stronger conclusion

\[
 \frac{\|P(D\phi_n)\|_1}{\delta_n}\longrightarrow0.
\]

**Necessity.** All tests below are actual periodic compatible gradients, uniformly bounded in `L^infinity`, hence uniformly integrable in the required sense. Coefficients are eliminated in the displayed order, so previously eliminated monomials cannot cancel the next test.

* To eliminate `b^3`, take `f=sin y+(1/2)sin 2y` and `g=epsilon sin y`. Then `a=c=0`, `b=cos y+cos 2y`, and `q=epsilon cos y`. The `b^3` average is `3/4`, while `delta=epsilon/sqrt(2)`. All other monomials are bounded or vanish after division by the defect.
* To eliminate `a b^2`, use (A4). Its normalized average diverges linearly in `N`, while every remaining cubic has bounded normalized average or tends to zero.
* To eliminate `q b^2`, take `f=sin y` and `g=(epsilon/2)sin 2y`. Then `a=c=0`, `b=cos y`, and `q=epsilon cos 2y`. Hence `average(q b^2)=epsilon/4`, `delta=epsilon/sqrt(2)`, and the quotient tends to `sqrt(2)/4`, multiplied by that coefficient. All surviving terms with at least two `q` factors tend to zero after normalization.
* To eliminate `c b^2`, use the construction (A4) with
  `psi=(1+cos x)(1+cos y)`. Exact averages are
  `average(R B^2)=-1/2`, `average(A^2 B)=0`, and
  `average(R^2)=3/4`. Thus
  `average(c_N b_N^2)=sigma/(2N^2)` and
  `delta_N=sigma sqrt(3)/(2N^2)`. Their quotient is `1/sqrt(3)`. The possibly surviving `a^2 b` coefficient contributes exactly zero in this test; all other surviving cubics are `o(delta_N)`.
* Finally use the original three-mode `psi` in (A4). Besides the averages already recorded, it satisfies
  `average(A^2 B)=1/2`. With the previous four coefficients zero,
  `average(a_N^2 b_N)/delta_N = sqrt(2/5)/sigma`, while all remaining cubic terms are `o(delta_N)`. Therefore the `a^2 b` coefficient must vanish.

This proves necessity, the dimension count, and the equivalence of (i)--(iv). Strong convolution approximation extends (iv) to `W^{1,4}`. It also proves the signed/absolute little-o equivalence. Merely strengthening the source's restriction to `P(a,b,0,-sigma a)=lambda a^3` is insufficient: the additional `q b^2` and `c b^2` coefficients must also vanish.

The target cubic has none of the five forbidden monomials, so the reported nonconcentration theorem is an instance of this exact class. The 18-dimensional boundedness class contains cubics such as `q b^2`, `c b^2`, and `a^2 b` whose normalized averages remain nonzero along explicitly nonconcentrating near-kernel sequences.

### Target-specific refinement: only the first column needs uniform integrability

The target has a stronger property than a general member of the 15-dimensional class. In its displayed representation, every quadratic multiplied by `q` or `c` contains `a` or `c`. For example, write the last term as `c(-kappa a c)`. Hölder and (A3) therefore give, for its local cubic density `mathcal C_r`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|\mathcal C_r(D\phi)\|_1
 \leq K_r\,\|(q,c)\|_2\,\|D\phi\|_4\,\|(a,c)\|_4.
 }                                                     \tag{A15}
\]

The constant is finite for fixed `r>0`; no sharp value is asserted. To check the only potentially troublesome term, `2a q^2`, pair one `q` in `L^2` and bound `||a q||_2 <= ||a||_4 ||q||_4`, with `||q||_4 <= C_sigma ||D phi||_4`. All other non-pure terms are even more directly of this form. The pure `a^3` term uses (A3).

Suppose now only that `D phi_n` is bounded in `L^4`, `Q_r -> 0`, and

\[
 \{(a_n^2+c_n^2)^2\}\text{ is uniformly integrable}.
\]

Equation (A9) and the defect give `a_n,c_n -> 0` in measure, so this weaker uniform-integrability assumption implies `||(a_n,c_n)||_4 -> 0`. Equation (A15) proves

\[
 \frac{C_r(\phi_n)}{\sqrt{Q_r(\phi_n)}}\longrightarrow0.
\]

No uniform integrability of `|b_n|^4`, `|d_n|^4`, or `|q_n|^4` is needed. Conversely, if a bounded near-kernel sequence has `|C_r|/sqrt(Q_r)` bounded away from zero, (A15) forces `||(a_n,c_n)||_4` bounded away from zero along that subsequence. Since `(a_n,c_n)` still tends to zero in measure, its fourth powers fail uniform integrability. Thus the necessary concentration is specifically in the first column.

## 7. Limits, sharpness, and source distinctions

The concentration scaling in the source is correct in two dimensions:
`phi_epsilon(x)=epsilon^{1/2} phi((x-x_0)/epsilon)` gives
`A_r` unchanged, `Q_r` multiplied by `epsilon`, and `C_r` multiplied by `epsilon^{1/2}`. Thus a nonzero normalized cubic can persist through concentration. The 15-dimensional theorem assumes uniform integrability and does not eliminate this mechanism.

The source's interpolation and heat arguments prove finiteness, not the constant required for `C_r^2 <= 4 A_r Q_r`. No conclusion about general nonscalar full-rank backgrounds follows from the analytic results audited here. Fixed `r>0` and fixed `sigma>0` matter; no estimate uniform as `r` or `sigma` approaches zero is claimed.

`verify_exact.py` in this directory uses exact rational Fourier arithmetic to check all trigonometric averages used in the necessary sequences and the dimension counts. It does **not** prove (A1), Riesz-transform boundedness, density, time integrability, or uniform-integrability convergence. Those arguments are written above. This audit did not rerun or rely on the source packet's finite verifier as an analytic proof.
