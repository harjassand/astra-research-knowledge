## 8. Best-method comparison and limits of the novelty claim

Existing hBN-versus-other-cover comparisons [1] are simpler and have already revealed a large contrast. They change cover chemistry and mechanical response. The proposed pair instead seeks a more restricted spectral intervention, at substantially greater fabrication cost.

Target isotope substitution directly probes mass sensitivity but changes the material under investigation. Here the target isotope inventory is unchanged. This is not automatically a better probe of pairing.

The hovering-cavity quantum Hall experiment [5] already offers same-sample, in-situ tuning of electromagnetic coupling. Its reversibility is superior to separately transferred isotope patches. This proposal's distinct goal is frequency/momentum reweighting at nearly matched static loading, not the first example of external control of quantum matter.

Isotope-stack spectral engineering [2], Thue–Morse multilayer optics [8,9] and fluctuation–dissipation [7] are prior art. The proposed simultaneous mechanical/static matching and surface-spectrum substitution requires priority review. A different control construction is not yet a demonstrated scientific advantage.
