### Concrete finite-gate process

Let

\[
 R_x=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&3/5&-4/5\\0&4/5&3/5\end{pmatrix},\quad
 R_z=\begin{pmatrix}3/5&-4/5&0\\4/5&3/5&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \quad A=\{I,R_x,R_x^{-1},R_z,R_z^{-1}\}.
 \tag{4}
\]

For x,u in the unit ball and a word of length t, write G_t=g_t...g_1 and

\[
 p_*(+\mid x,g_1,\ldots,g_t,u)=\frac{1+(1/3)u\cdot G_tx}{2}.
 \tag{5}
\]

A simulator uses a preparation distribution p_x, transition P_{t,T}(g_t), and a terminal response h_{t,T,u}(i) in [0,1]. Its only persistent record of earlier inputs is its m-valued state. Fresh independent randomness is permitted. A correlated seed, a stored gate word, or an external accumulated rotation is not free. The time index and deadline are supplied as in the previous model, but carry no past-gate information. Preparations need not be affine functions of x. Matrices and probabilities may be exact real numbers; description length, acquisition, sampling precision, and running time are not counted here.

**Theorem B (fixed finite alphabet, exact and approximate).** For the process (5), uniformly for 0<=epsilon<=1/72,

\[
 \frac{c_\nu}{\log24}T\le m_*(T;\epsilon)\le16(T+1).
 \tag{6}
\]

Here nu is uniform on the five gates in (4), and (SG) follows from the established theorem verified in Section 7. The upper bound is exact (epsilon=0), works for every x,u, and in fact works for every rotation, not only the finite alphabet. The lower bound needs only the six preparations +/-e_1,+/-e_2,+/-e_3 and the single final query u=e_3. A separate simulator designed solely for these six preparations and that one query still obeys the lower bound.

Thus the state count is Theta(T), and the state-register bit count is log_2 T+O(1), not Theta(T) bits. The static *full* x,u observation family has four-state minimum, as proved in the previous packet. Do not confuse that full static family with the six-preparation, single-query restriction: the latter has a two-state static realization.
