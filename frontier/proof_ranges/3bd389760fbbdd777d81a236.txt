## Adversarial laws and singular checks

All independent checks below were freshly implemented in `adversarial_check.py`; they use SymPy exact rational arithmetic. They are finite examples, not a proof of the analytic estimates.

1. **Rare transverse spike at a simple transverse contact.** Use the Dacorogna–Marcellini endpoint
   $F_{DM}(X)=|X|^4-(4/\sqrt3)|X|^2\det X$, $R=e_{12}$, and the centered scalar law has $s=1$ with probability $1/3$ and $s=-1/2$ with probability $2/3$. Independently let $Y=y e_{11}$, with $y=0$ with probability $1-p$, and $y=\pm10$ each with probability $p/2$, where $p=10^{-10}$. Every $X=sR+Y$ is rank one. Its covariance is $\operatorname{diag}(10^{-8},1/2,0,0)$, so $R$ is the unique covariance maximizer. Exactly,
   \[
   m_2=1/2,\;m_3=1/4,\;m_4=3/8,\quad
   \delta_2^2=2\cdot10^{-8},\quad \delta_4^4=1/375000.
   \]
   Thus $\delta_2\ll\delta_4$, a deliberate rare-spike stress. A normalized contact null vector is $A=(-1/2)e_{12}+ (\sqrt3/2)e_{21}$; take the complementary basis $e_{11},(\sqrt3/2)e_{12}+(1/2)e_{21},e_{22}$. In this orthonormal frame the actual block gives
   \[
   h=1/50000000,\quad
   \sigma=150000001/7500000056250000,\quad
   \sigma/h=1200000008/1200000009.
   \]
   The regressed inverse quadratic $\ell^TH^{-1}\ell$ (before the factor $1/2$ in the gap) is exactly $1/135000000900000000$. The complete affine gap is $4375015187500101/15000000100000000>0$, exceeding the pure-line reference $7/24$ by $45475000303/45000000300000000$. This confirms that the actual Schur denominator stays close to $h$ in this adversarial concentration regime. A scale family $p=10^{-10k}$, $|y|=10^k$ has both relative norms tending to zero while $\delta_2/\delta_4\to0$.

2. **Concentrated nonzero normal covariance correlation.** Put mass $(1-p)/2$ at each $\pm e_{12}$, and mass $p/4$ at each matrix with coordinates $(2u,s,4s,2u)$, for $s,u\in\{\pm1\}$ and $p=10^{-8}$. The law is centered and has determinant zero pointwise. For $R=e_{12}$, $q(Y)=4$ on rare atoms and $z=\mathbb E(sY)=(0,0,4p,0)$. The exact identity (4) holds, and the covariance maximizer is still $e_{12}$: for unit vectors satisfying $q=0$, setting $r=4p,t=16p$, the objective has the same value as $b^2+tc^2+r(a^2+d^2)+4rbc$, and
   \[
   1-\widehat R^TC\widehat R\ge2(1-3r)|bc|+(1-t)c^2\ge0.
   \]
   Equality is only the line $\mathbb Re_{12}$. This directly probes the quadratic-normal regression when the correlation is nonzero.

3. **Singular line and zero law.** For the same $F_{DM}$, the two-point law has $X=e_{12}$ with probability $1/3$ and $X=-e_{12}/2$ with probability $2/3$. It has $\operatorname{rank}H=3$, $b\in\operatorname{ran}H$, and exact minimum $7/24$, matching (18). The zero law has rank-zero $H$ and zero gap. No pseudoinverse or hidden regularization is needed.

4. **Tangent falsifier to any moment-only extension.** Independently reconstructed the exact balanced law for
   \[
   G=3(U+V-2q)^2+\tfrac{35}{24}U^2+q^2-\tfrac52qU,
   \quad U=a^2+c^2,\;V=b^2+d^2.
   \]
   At $A_0=I,R_0=e_{12}$, $D^2G(I)[X,X]=\tfrac12(3a-2d)^2+\tfrac56c^2-q(X)$: the PSD Finsler remainder has rank two, not three, so assumption (A) fails. With $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, $m=9$, $w=1-3\varepsilon/2$, and $\tau=9m^2\varepsilon/(2w)$, place weights $w/2,w/2,\varepsilon,\varepsilon/2$ at $\pm(0,1,\tau,0)$, $m(1,0,0,3/2)$, and $-2m(1,0,0,3/2)$. The law is centered and determinant-balanced; $H\succ0$ and the fitted line is $e_{12}$, yet the full optimized gap is exactly
   \[
   -5801410605116157185784799329826986224787/
   2664768988592396062935563721800235200000<0.
   \]
   More strongly, an independent exact symbolic calculation in `adversarial_check.py` gives, along the same fixed-$m=9$ family as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$,
   \[
   \delta_2^2=\frac{243\varepsilon(4335\varepsilon+26)}{2(3\varepsilon-2)^2}\longrightarrow0,
   \quad
   \delta_4^4=\frac{59049\varepsilon(38259189\varepsilon^3+9126\varepsilon^2-6084\varepsilon+1352)}{8(3\varepsilon-2)^4}\longrightarrow0,
   \]
   while the affine trial restricted to $B=tI$ has optimized value $c-\beta^2/(2h)\to-3/4$. Since the unrestricted infimum is no greater than this trial, sufficiently small members remain negative while satisfying arbitrarily strong relative near-line bounds. The law is supported in a two-dimensional matrix subspace; it is not a compatible-gradient counterexample. It decisively blocks extending the theorem by determinant moment algebra alone across tangent contacts.
