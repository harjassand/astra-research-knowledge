## 1. The decisive computational statement

Let \(M\) be an arbitrary matroid on \(n\) elements, supplied by an **independence oracle**. Its base polytope is
\[
P(M)=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf1_B:B\text{ is a basis of }M\}.
\]

The input is a rational matrix \(A\in\mathbb Q^{m\times n}\) and a rational point \(x\in P(M)\), with
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{ij}^{2}\le1
\qquad(j=1,\ldots,n).
\]

### Proposed theorem: constructive exactly unbiased matroid rounding

There is a deterministic algorithm that constructs bases
\[
B_1,\ldots,B_t,\qquad t\le n+1,
\]
and nonnegative rational probabilities \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_t\), satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\lambda_\ell=1,
\qquad
\sum_{\ell=1}^{t}\lambda_\ell\mathbf1_{B_\ell}=x,
}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\|A(\mathbf1_{B_\ell}-x)\|_\infty\le C
\quad\text{for every support point}.
}
\]

Here \(C\) is an absolute constant. The proof supplies the conservative value \(C=262336\); its magnitude is not the advance.

The algorithm uses **polynomially many independence-oracle queries and polynomially many ordinary bit operations**, measured in \(n,m\), and the total binary input length. Its output distribution has polynomial encoding length.

The proof first establishes the stronger directional statement needed to construct that distribution:

> For every rational cost vector \(c\), a deterministic polynomial-bit algorithm returns a basis \(B\) with
> \[
> \boxed{
> c^\top\mathbf1_B\le c^\top x,
> \qquad
> \|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le C.
> }
> \]

This does not assume a matrix representation of the matroid, an oracle for discrepancy-constrained optimization, or an oracle for nonlinear minimization.

The spectral engine remains inherited from the previous proof and Guo–Fang–Lu’s **20 September 2026** preprint. Their stated algorithmic model is exact real arithmetic. The additional claim here is the transfer to general matroids with rational bit complexity and constructive exact marginals. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23540v1)
