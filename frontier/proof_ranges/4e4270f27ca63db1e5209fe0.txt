## 1. The operational target

Let \(\Omega=B_1\subset\mathbb R^n\), \(n\ge3\). A scalar conductivity \(\sigma\) equals one outside an interior ball \(B_r\), where \(0<r<1\), and obeys \(1/2\le\sigma\le3/2\). A boundary voltage \(f\) produces the weak solution
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\sigma\nabla u_{\sigma,f})=0,
 \qquad u_{\sigma,f}|_{\partial B_1}=f.
\]
The scalar readout is the energy \(Q_\sigma(f)=B_\sigma(f,f)\), or the real/imaginary part of a pairing
\[
 B_\sigma(f,h)=\int_{B_1}\sigma\nabla u_{\sigma,f}\cdot\overline{\nabla u_{\sigma,h}}\,dx.
\]
A complex pairing costs two real readouts. This note gives the experiment generous access to all boundary voltages. Every lower bound therefore also applies to voltages supported on a fixed open patch \(\Gamma\).

Use the exact background-energy norm
\[
 \|f\|_E^2=\int_{B_1}|\nabla u_{1,f}|^2dx
 =\sum_{\ell\ge1}\ell\|f_\ell\|_{L^2(S^{n-1})}^2.
\]
Here \(f_\ell\) is the spherical-harmonic component of degree \(\ell\), and constants are quotiented out. This is the homogeneous \(H^{1/2}\) norm. The dissipated energy satisfies
\[
 \tfrac12\|f\|_E^2\le Q_\sigma(f)\le\tfrac32\|f\|_E^2.
\]
Thus this norm charges a physical excitation resource, rather than allowing unbounded high-frequency voltages for free. The requested output is a finite representation of \(\sigma\), with error in \(L^2(B_1)\), in already calibrated Euclidean coordinates.

## 2. Explicit smooth real pair

Write \(x=(\rho\cos\theta,\rho\sin\theta,z)\), \(z\in\mathbb R^{n-2}\). Fix a nonzero, real, rotation-invariant \(\psi\in C_c^\infty(B_r)\), with support separated from the rotation axis \(\{\rho=0\}\), and normalize \(\|\psi\|_\infty\le1\). For an integer \(k\ge1\), integer \(s>n/2\), and \(0<a\le1/2\), put
\[
 h_k(x)=\psi(\rho,z)\cos(k\theta),\qquad
 t_k=ak^{-s},\qquad \sigma_k^\pm=1\pm t_kh_k.
\]
These coefficients are smooth and uniformly elliptic and equal one on a fixed boundary collar. Because the support avoids the axis, differentiation gives
\[
 \|h_k\|_{H^s}\le C_{\psi,s}k^s,
 \qquad \|\sigma_k^\pm-1\|_{H^s}\le C_{\psi,s}a.
\]
The angular integral of \(\cos^2(k\theta)\) is independent of \(k\), so their separation is exactly
\[
 \Delta_k:=\|\sigma_k^+-\sigma_k^-\|_2
 =\sqrt2\,a\|\psi\|_2k^{-s}.                    \tag{1}
\]
Choosing \(a\le\eta/C_{\psi,s}\) puts all of them in any prescribed positive \(H^s\) radius \(\eta\).
