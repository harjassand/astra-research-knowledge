2. NONVANISHING NORMALIZED-LOG COEFFICIENTS — FULL PROOF

Define g(u)=log(1+u)/u, with g(0)=1, and h(u)=log g(u). Then

  h(u)=sum_{k>=1} (-1)^k b_k u^k,             b_k in Q, b_k>0.

Proof: put v=-u. The identity

  v/(-log(1-v)) = integral_0^1 (1-v)^t dt

holds analytically for |v| small and therefore as rational-coefficient formal
series. For k>=1 and 0<t<1, the coefficient (-1)^k binom(t,k) of v^k in
(1-v)^t is strictly negative. Thus the integral is 1-B(v), where every
positive-degree coefficient of B(v) is strictly positive. Consequently

  h(-v)=log((-log(1-v))/v)=-log(1-B(v))=sum_{j>=1} B(v)^j/j

has a strictly positive coefficient at every v^k. This proves the assertion.
The finite integral may alternatively be interpreted by integrating polynomial
binomial coefficients coefficientwise; all resulting coefficients are rational.
Once nonzero in Q, they remain nonzero in any characteristic-zero field.

Prior-art qualification: this ingredient is classical. [BL, equation (20)]
gives b_k=C_{2,k}/(k k!), where
  C_{2,k}=sum_{j=1}^k |s(k,j)|/(j+1)>0.
No new coefficient sequence or positivity theorem is claimed here.

3. BINOMIAL CONTACT — EXACT STATEMENT AND PROOF

Theorem A. If 0 != Q=c x^alpha+d x^beta in F[x_1,...,x_n] has at most two
monomials and degree at most D, then

  ord_0 Q(ell_1,...,ell_n) <= D+n-1.

More precisely, after combining identical terms, if |alpha| != |beta|,
the contact is min(|alpha|,|beta|). If the degrees agree at d_0 and the two
leading Taylor coefficients fail to cancel, the contact is d_0. If they cancel
and alpha!=beta, put e_i=alpha_i-beta_i and
m=|{i:e_i!=0}|. Then m>=2 and the contact is exactly

  d_0 + min{k>=1: sum_i e_i i^{-k} != 0},     with that k <= m-1.

Proof: the unequal-degree and uncancelled cases follow from
ell_i(z)=(z/i)(1+O(z)). In the cancelled equal-degree case, set
A_alpha=prod_i i^{-alpha_i} and G_alpha=prod_i g(z/i)^{alpha_i}.
Then ell^alpha=A_alpha z^{d_0}G_alpha and G_alpha(0)=1. Cancellation means
d A_beta=-c A_alpha, so the remaining contact is d_0 plus the contact of
G_alpha-G_beta. Their unit ratio satisfies

  G_alpha/G_beta=exp(S(z)),
  S(z)=sum_i e_i h(z/i)
      =sum_{k>=1} (-1)^k b_k (sum_i e_i i^{-k}) z^k.

For any nonzero S with S(0)=0, exp(S)-1 has exactly the same order as S.
Multiplication by G_beta does not change order. The coefficient lemma gives
the exact moment formula above. Finally sum_i e_i=0 because the total degrees
agree. If the first m-1 positive moments also vanished, the Vandermonde matrix
on the m distinct numbers 1/i, with exponents 0,...,m-1, would annihilate the
nonzero vector of supported e_i. This matrix is invertible. Hence the first
nonzero positive moment has index at most m-1. This proves Theorem A.

Sharpness is explicit. For n>=2 put
  e_i=(-1)^{i-1} binom(n-1,i-1) i^{n-2},
  alpha_i=max(e_i,0), beta_i=max(-e_i,0), d_n=sum_i alpha_i.
Finite differences give sum_i e_i i^{-k}=0 for k=0,...,n-2, whereas the
(n-1)st moment is 1/n. Therefore
  Q_n=(prod_i i^{alpha_i}) x^alpha-(prod_i i^{beta_i}) x^beta
has degree d_n and contact d_n+n-1. For n=3 this is
  27 x_1 x_3^3-16 x_2^4,                    degree 4, contact 6.

Q_n is NOT a counterfamily to N250. Repeated squaring gives O(n^2 log n)
arithmetic operations for its powers and scalar normalizations, but d_n grows
exponentially (indeed faster) in n, and its contact is d_n+n-1. D is part of
the requested bound. The normalization constants also have very large bit
length. Charging only n and arithmetic circuit size while forgetting D and
the scalar encoding would manufacture a false counterexample.

4. A FACTOR CLASS WITH EXPONENTIAL DERIVATIVE DIMENSION

Theorem B. Let 0 != P be a product of nonzero affine linear polynomials and
polynomials with at most two monomials, with arbitrary repetitions/powers.
If deg P=D, then

  ord_0 P(ell_1,...,ell_n) <= nD.

No factorization need be supplied to the black box; this is a class promise.
To prove it, an affine form with nonzero constant has order 0. A homogeneous
nonzero affine form L=sum c_i x_i has order at most n because its first n
Taylor coefficients are (-1)^{k-1} sum c_i i^{-k}/k, and the matrix with
entries i^{-k}, k=1,...,n, is invertible. A nonconstant binomial of degree d
has order <=d+n-1<=nd by Theorem A. Constants have order 0. Orders and
degrees add under products of nonzero polynomials and nonzero scalar series.
Summing the factor bounds proves Theorem B.

The nD bound is sharp: the affine form
  L_n=sum_{i=1}^n (-1)^{i-1} binom(n-1,i-1)i^{n-1}x_i
has contact n; its first nonzero Taylor coefficient is (-1)^{n-1}/n^2.
Thus L_n^D has contact nD. The class also contains prod_i x_i, whose total
derivative dimension is 2^n. Consequently this proof removes the specific
linear-state obstruction on a nontrivial product class, rather than merely
lowering the constant in a derivative-dimension bound.

The product-class black-box consequence is unconditional but scoped. Take
L=nD+1 and
  phi_i(z)=sum_{j=1}^{nD} (-1)^{j-1} z^j/(j i^j).
Composition modulo z^L agrees with the logarithmic composition. Theorem B
therefore makes P(phi(z)) a nonzero univariate of degree <=nD^2. The scalar
points phi(t), t=0,...,nD^2, give at most nD^2+1 rational hitting points.
For D=0 the origin suffices. Coordinate denominators can be cleared by
(nD)! i^{nD}; output lengths and construction time are polynomial in n,D.
The exact evaluation cost and the encoding of arbitrary circuit constants
remain separate. This class already has elementary PIT methods; this fixed
logarithmic curve bound is research capital, not a general PIT breakthrough.
Priority of the exact direct-contact corollary is UNKNOWN.

5. RATIONAL PRIMITIVE CLASSIFICATION — INCLUDING z-DEPENDENT READOUTS

Let a_1,...,a_N be distinct nonzero complex numbers and let
lambda_i(z)=log(1+z/a_i), with their germs at zero. These logarithms are
algebraically independent even over C(z): a loop around -a_i adds 2*pi*i
to lambda_i only. Continuing any alleged polynomial relation around all
integer combinations of these loops produces a polynomial in the integer
translation parameters vanishing on Z^N; at an ordinary point that polynomial
is zero. Varying the point proves every rational-function coefficient zero.
Clearing denominators extends injectivity to rational functions.

Theorem C. For T(z,x) in C(z,x_1,...,x_N), suppose
  d/dz T(z,lambda_1(z),...,lambda_N(z)) = h(z) in C(z).
Then, as a rational function,
  T(z,x)=r(z)+sum_i c_i x_i,                r in C(z), c_i in C.
The converse is immediate.

Proof: continue the scalar germ around a loop about -a_i, avoiding the finite
poles of h. Its derivative remains h, so the difference between the continued
and original germ is a constant d_i. The logarithms' continuation gives
  T(z,lambda+2*pi*i e_i)-T(z,lambda)=d_i.
Algebraic independence over C(z) promotes this to a rational-function identity
  T(z,x+2*pi*i e_i)-T(z,x)=d_i.
Subtract sum_i (d_i/(2*pi*i))x_i. The remaining rational function is periodic
in each x_i with a nonzero additive period. A one-variable rational function
over a characteristic-zero field with a nonzero additive period is constant:
any finite pole would generate infinitely many poles under translations; with
no finite poles the function is polynomial, and comparison of its highest
degree term rules out a positive degree. Apply this over
C(z,x_1,...,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},...,x_N) for each i. The remainder lies in C(z).
This proves the theorem. Possible isolated poles of the displayed rational
germ may be avoided; a rational derivative supplies analytic continuation away
from its own finite poles, so apparent singularities do not invalidate the loop
argument. Constants d_i are loop integrals of the single-valued derivative h.

Corollary C1. If g(z) is a nonzero rational function and an ordinary branch
of log g(z) can be written as T(z,lambda(z)) with T rational, then
  T(z,x)=c+sum_i m_i x_i,                 m_i in Z,
and g has no finite zero or pole outside {-a_1,...,-a_N}.

Proof: apply Theorem C to h=g'/g. Its finite poles are simple, with integer
residues, and it is O(1/z) at infinity. The derivative r'(z) of a rational
function cannot have a simple-pole term: a finite pole of r would give a pole
of order at least two in r'. Therefore r has no finite poles and is polynomial.
The O(1/z) behavior of g'/g and of sum c_i/(z+a_i) forces r'=0. Comparing
residues gives c_i=m_i=ord_{-a_i}g in Z, and excludes all other finite poles
of g'/g. This establishes the claim. At g(0)!=0, one can write equivalently
  g(z)/g(0)=prod_i (1+z/a_i)^{m_i}.

This proof is an elementary monodromy/primitive-extension observation. Exact
priority is not claimed; no external correctness review has been obtained.
It is stronger in readout scope than N250's stated affine-log pullback premise.

6. GROWING-ALPHABET CONTACT AMPLIFICATION CANNOT BE FREE

Corollary C2. Let R in C(z) be nonconstant and regular at 0, set b=R(0), and
let a!=0 satisfy b!=-a. If
  log(1+R(z)/a)-log(1+b/a)
is any rational function of z and N distinct ordinary source logarithms
lambda_i(z), then its contact at zero, namely
  r=ord_0(R(z)-b),
is at most N.

Proof: Corollary C1 makes that analytic function an affine combination of the
lambda_i, with its constant chosen to vanish at zero. The first N derivatives
of such a nonconstant combination have the invertible Vandermonde matrix on
1/a_i, so its order is at most N. The outer logarithm has a nonzero derivative
at b, and hence its contact is exactly the local ramification r. This proves
the alphabet-size charge, without a fixed-puncture-set premise.

If a fixed seed analytic germ F(t) has contact q at t=b, the composed germ
F(R(z)) has contact qr<=qN. Iteration does not evade this charge: apply it to
the final composite rational map and the final logarithmic alphabet. Thus
rational pullback, even with growing alphabets and z-dependent rational
readouts, cannot turn a seed of polynomially bounded contact into contact
superpolynomial in that seed bound and the final alphabet size. It does not
bound a seed P(ell) whose own contact is already unknown; it is not a proof
of N250. It concerns exact representations, not approximation or unrelated
transcendental generators.

The local bound is sharp. Let L_N be the affine form in Section 4 and set
g(z)=prod_i(1+z/i)^{[x_i]L_N}, R(z)=a(g(z)-1). Then
log(1+R(z)/a)=L_N(lambda(z)) has contact N. For N=2 a simpler instance is
  R(z)=z^2/(z+1),                         a=4,
  log(1+R(z)/4)=2 log(1+z/2)-log(1+z),    contact 2.
The bound concerns local ramification, not the global rational degree:
R(z)=(1+z)^M-1 has global degree M but log(1+R)=M log(1+z) and local
ramification 1. Conflating these quantities would overstate the obstruction.

7. ATTACKS THAT DO NOT CLOSE THE MAIN GATE

A. Linear-state expansion. N246 is a valid barrier to a faithful polynomial
linear realization; the product example's contact n disproves using it as a
large-contact counterexample. The family 116 construction requires a small
ordered coefficient representation. No new commutative homomorphism or
circuit-dependent ordered encoding was produced.

B. Interpolation contact. With M=binom(n+D,D), the first M-1 Taylor conditions
on the M polynomial coefficients have a nonzero null vector, giving contact
at least M-1. Algebraic independence ensures the resulting germ is nonzero.
A dense expression costs O(DM+nM) arithmetic operations in a straightforward
encoding. No polynomial-size compression was established. The four imported
multiaffine fixtures of orders 1,3,7,15 remain finite examples.

C. Binomial moment cancellation. The explicit sharp family from Section 3
has succinct powers but enormous degree. Its contact is linear in D, so it
does not disprove the uniform polynomial in n,s,D.

D. Pairwise separation is insufficient for arbitrary sums. For every M>=2,
the three analytic units f_1=1, f_2=1+z, f_3=2+z+z^M have order-at-most-1
nonzero linear combinations within every pair, yet f_3-f_2-f_1=z^M.
These are not logarithmic monomials and not a counterexample to N250; they
refute the abstract inference that a pairwise contact bound controls a whole
linear span. Extending Theorem A from binomials to arbitrary sparse sums needs
a genuine many-term invariant or Wronskian argument with controlled complexity.

E. Pullback amplification. Sections 5–6 strengthen the fixed-alphabet no-go.
Allowing rational rather than affine readouts and growing alphabets does not
make this exact rational-pullback route free of an alphabet-size charge.
