Without (8), the unforced stationary bound still holds with marginal factor one. Under general bounded controls and exit rates at most \(\Lambda\), a factor
\[ e^{2\Lambda T\sinh H} \]
is sufficient. That can be exponentially large in the controlled horizon; it is not an uncharged polynomial guarantee.
There is a broad class where the passive impossibility is stronger than the old exponential lower bound.
Suppose
\[ \boxed{PQ(I-P)=0.} \tag{10} \]
This is the opposite invariance condition from controlled autonomy: the stationary reverse generator \(Q^*\) preserves the observed function space.
Then the complete stationary observed process is exactly the Markov chain \(L\). Nevertheless,
\[ (I-P)QP \]
can be nonzero, so \(\Gamma\) can be positive.
Thus two hidden systems can have:
\[ \text{identical complete passive path laws} \]
but
\[ \Gamma_0=0,\qquad \Gamma_1\ne0. \]
No amount of passive observation distinguishes them. Calibrated active experiments recover their different certificates.
For uniform \(\pi\), let \(J\) be uniform averaging and \(Z\) an arbitrary matrix. Set
\[ \boxed{ Q=J-I+\varepsilon(I-P)Z(I-J), } \tag{11} \]
with \(\varepsilon\) small enough to retain positive off-diagonal rates.
Every such generator satisfies (10), and all have the same observed refresh generator
\[ L=\mathbf1\mu^\top-I. \]
But \(Z\) can change both the coupling from observed to hidden variables and the internal hidden dynamics.
This class requires no nilpotence, special spectrum, long observation word, or paired ladder amplitudes. It contains relatively open sets within its linear passive-equivalence constraints, with a positive rate margin.
The active acquisition theorem applies more widely still: a perturbed model need not remain in this passive-equivalence class for its actual \(\Gamma\) to remain measurable.
Take four states, uniform stationarity,
\[ f=(1,-1,1,-1)^\top,\qquad d=(1,-1,-1,1)^\top, \]
and
\[ Q=J-I+\frac14\frac{df^\top}{4}. \]
Observing \(f(X_t)\) gives exactly the stationary binary chain with flip rate \(1/2\), at every collection of times. Yet
\[ \Gamma= \frac1{64} \begin{pmatrix} 1&-1\\ -1&1 \end{pmatrix}. \]
Under the calibrated sign-potential pulse,
\[ \chi(t) = 1-e^{-t} -\frac1{32}\left[1-(1+t)e^{-t}\right], \]
whereas the equilibrium two-state realization has response \(1-e^{-t}\).
This is not merely delayed passive detection. There is no passive distinction to detect.
The statistical conclusion is minimax: a class containing these matched models cannot admit a uniformly consistent passive autonomy test. It does not assert that every perturbed alternative remains individually hard to detect.
Now consider a different implementation of the same potential change:
\[ \boxed{ q_{ij}^{u,\mathrm{clock}}(t) = e^{-u_{\ell(i)}(t,Y_{[0,t)})}q_{ij}. } \tag{12} \]
All outgoing rates from the current observed label receive the same multiplier.
For a fixed potential vector \(u\), both this rule and the geometric rule satisfy
\[ \frac{q_{ij}^{u}}{q_{ji}^{u}} = \frac{q_{ij}}{q_{ji}} e^{u_{\ell(j)}-u_{\ell(i)}}. \tag{13} \]
They therefore impose identical changes in forward/reverse rate ratios. Their symmetric kinetic factors differ.
Dependence of nonequilibrium response on this implementation is known. The stronger statement below concerns the information in all adaptive experiments, not just a response coefficient. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.4394)
Suppose \(|u_a|\le H\). Every observed trajectory under a well-posed, nonanticipating source-clock policy through physical time \(T\) can be generated from an unforced observed trajectory through time at most \(e^H T\), together with the policy’s independent random seed.
Consequently, for any two hidden models,
\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{TV} (\text{controlled data}_0,\text{controlled data}_1) \le \operatorname{TV} \left( \text{passive data}_0^{[0,e^HT]}, \text{passive data}_1^{[0,e^HT]} \right). } \tag{14} \]
Given an unforced observed path \(y(s)\), define operational time by
\[ \tau'(t) = e^{-u_{y(\tau(t))}(t,\text{controlled history})}, \qquad \tau(0)=0, \]
and output \(y(\tau(t))\).
This multiplies every outgoing microscopic intensity by the specified clock factor. The transformation can be constructed from the observed path alone; it never needs the hidden state. Moreover,
\[ \tau(T)\le e^HT. \]
It is therefore a common data-processing map from passive to controlled observations, which proves (14). ∎
