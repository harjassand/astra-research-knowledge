damped trajectory. Its relevant nonlinear clock is (1-exp(-dt))/d, rather than

These are three-outcome record statements, not distances between quantum states.

1. PHYSICAL MODEL AND EXPOSED RECORD
==================================
Set hbar=1 and D[a](rho)=a rho a^dagger-{a^dagger a,rho}/2. Let
  H = chi N1 N2,
  d rho/dt = -i[H,rho] + d D[a1](rho) + d D[a2](rho),  d>0.       (1)
The baths are independent vacuum Markov loss channels in this supplied model.
Their ordinary loss rate d does NOT shrink with theta.

Interfere the two output loss fields on a balanced real passive mixer. During
an observation window the detected jump operators are
  L_plus/minus = sqrt(eta(t)*d/2) (a1 +/- a2).
The remaining output loss is unobserved. Summing all observed and unobserved
channels gives exactly the dissipator in (1), because a unitary output mixing
does not change the sum of loss dissipators. This requires access to coherent,
phase-stable mixing of these output modes. It is a specified instrument, not
an inference from ordinary local count access alone.

The latch exposes only one of three symbols at the completed window:
  0 : no detected output event;
  + : the first detected event used the plus port;
  - : the first detected event used the minus port.
The event time is not exposed; later detections are ignored. Neither the latch
nor an unexposed trigger changes the control during the window. Detection can
be gated on at a fixed t0>0, while loss and Kerr act from time zero.

Initial state:
  rho_init = Delta Gamma(R),
  R = [[r,s],[s,r]],  0<s<r, r+s<1,
  Delta=(1-r)^2-s^2.
Gamma is bosonic second quantization, so Tr Gamma(R)=1/det(I-R).
This is a positive, normalized, zero-mean gauge-invariant Gaussian state with
occupation covariance R(I-R)^(-1). The preparation is JOINT: s encodes an
actual intermode phase correlation. Its replacement by product marginals is
not allowed. The initial bath state and Markov dynamics are supplied premises.
No microscopic material implementation or acquired calibration is asserted.

2. EXACT COHERENCE GENERATING FUNCTION, INCLUDING HIDDEN LOSS
===========================================================
Let rho_0(t) be the unnormalized state conditional on no detected event from
the start of the chosen schedule through t. Then
  dot rho_0 = -i[chi N1N2,rho_0]
              +d(1-eta(t)) sum_i ai rho_0 ai^dagger
              -(d/2){N1+N2,rho_0}.                           (2)

Define the weighted number-difference-one sector
  c_nm(t)=sqrt((n+1)(m+1)) <n,m+1|rho_0(t)|n+1,m>,
  F(x,y,t)=sum_(n,m>=0) c_nm(t) x^n y^m.
In particular, Tr(a1^dagger a2 rho_0)=F(1,1,t). Direct substitution into (2)
gives the exact recurrence
  dot c_nm = [-d(n+m+1)-i chi(n-m)] c_nm
             +d(1-eta(t))[(n+1)c_(n+1,m)+(m+1)c_(n,m+1)].      (3)
The square-root Fock weights have cancelled; no occupation truncation was made.
Thus
  partial_t F = [d(1-eta(t))-(d+i chi)x] partial_x F
                +[d(1-eta(t))-(d-i chi)y] partial_y F-dF.

With
  f_plus(x,t)=exp(-(d+i chi)t)x
             +int_0^t d(1-eta(v)) exp(-(d+i chi)v) dv,
  f_minus(y,t)=exp(-(d-i chi)t)y
              +int_0^t d(1-eta(v)) exp(-(d-i chi)v) dv,
the exact solution is
  F(x,y,t)=exp(-dt) F(x->f_plus(x,t), y->f_minus(y,t), 0).     (4)
Differentiating (4) directly verifies the time-dependent-efficiency version;
one must not reverse the order of the loss and Hamiltonian intervals.

For the stated Gaussian preparation,
  F(x,y,0)=Delta*s/[1-r(x+y)+(r^2-s^2)xy]^2.                  (5)
One derivation uses the exact positive Gaussian coherent-state preparation:
if its covariance is V=R(I-R)^(-1), then F is the Gaussian integral of
  alpha1^* alpha2 exp[-(1-x)|alpha1|^2-(1-y)|alpha2|^2].
The determinant is [1-r(x+y)+(r^2-s^2)xy]/Delta and V12=s/Delta;
the two-mode Gaussian first moment yields (5). This argument describes the
initial state only; it does not assert that later Kerr states remain positive
coherent mixtures.

Let f(t)=f_plus(1,t). The needed exact no-detection coherence is real:
  C0(t)=Tr(a1^dagger a2 rho_0(t))
       =exp(-dt) Delta*s /
        [1-r(f+conj(f))+(r^2-s^2)|f|^2]^2.                   (6)
For constant efficiency,
  f=exp(-(d+i chi)t)
    +d(1-eta)[1-exp(-(d+i chi)t)]/(d+i chi).
For eta(t)=eta on [t0,T] and zero before t0, t in [t0,T],
  f=E(t)+d[1-E(t)-eta(E(t0)-E(t))]/(d+i chi),
  E(t)=exp(-(d+i chi)t).                                    (7)

The hidden histories have a precise interpretation. If L is an exponential
photon lifetime of rate d, then
  f(t)=E[ exp(-i chi min(L,t)) w(L,t) ],
where w=1 if L>t and w=1-eta(L) if L<=t. This is the characteristic function
of the time that a photon survives and contributes Kerr phase, weighted by
the absence of its detection. Equation (3) makes this interpretation exact in
the selected coherence sector. Freezing every occupation until the final time
would replace min(L,t) by t and is wrong. This lifetime representation is not
a claim that the complete nonlinear quantum state is a classical photon model.

Since |f|<=1, the real denominator in (6) is strictly positive:
  |1-rf|^2-s^2|f|^2
  >=[1-(r+s)|f|][1-(r-s)|f|] >= Delta > 0.
The defining sums and the characteristic solution therefore converge for these
inputs. The initial state has exponential occupation tails and finite moments;
pure loss cannot create an occupation explosion.

3. EXACT RECORD LAW AND AN INFORMATION-EQUIVALENCE CHECK
======================================================
Write
  zeta(t)=1-int_0^t eta(v)d exp(-dv) dv.
The exact no-detection probability is
  S(t)=Delta/[1-2r zeta(t)+(r^2-s^2)zeta(t)^2].               (8)
This is independent of chi, because the number-diagonal populations obey the
ordinary death process. The total detected intensity is eta d N; the signed
port intensity is 2 eta d Re(a1^dagger a2). Therefore
  B(T)=p_plus-p_minus=int_0^T 2 eta(t)d C0(t) dt,
  p0=S(T), p_plus=(1-S(T)+B(T))/2,
  p_minus=(1-S(T)-B(T))/2.                                  (9)
Equation (9) is a positive three-outcome law as it comes from the completely
positive first-event instrument. The sole remaining scalar integral can be
certified without any physical Fock cutoff.

There is a stronger passive equivalence than equality of mean count curves.
For any disjoint time bins Bj, let pj=int_Bj eta(t)d exp(-dt)dt. The joint PGF
of the complete POOLED detected counts Kj (ignoring output-port marks) is
  E product_j yj^(Kj)
   = Delta/[1-2r zeta+(r^2-s^2)zeta^2],
  zeta=1+sum_j pj(yj-1).                                    (10)
Each initial photon is detected once in one bin or never. The initial joint
number law and the diagonal death generator prove (10). Equivalently the
backward effect algebra of pooled counting remains number diagonal. Thus every
finite partition, and hence the full pooled count path law, is the same for
chi=0 and chi!=0. Mixing and retaining the first-port mark detects information
absent from the entire pooled path law. No unknown joint preparation was
silently exchanged when comparing the two models.

4. THE LARGE-OCCUPATION LAW WITH ORDINARY LOSS
===========================================
Fix alpha>beta>0, d>0, g real and kappa>=0, and take
  r=1-alpha theta, s=beta theta,
  chi=theta g, eta=theta kappa on [t0,T].
Assume theta(alpha+beta)<1 and theta kappa<=1. Initial mean occupation per
mode is alpha/[(alpha^2-beta^2)theta]-1, so it diverges as theta^(-1).
Let
  Delta_bar=alpha^2-beta^2,
  u(t)=1-exp(-dt), v(t)=exp(-dt0)-exp(-dt).
For fixed t0,T, expansion of the EXACT lifetime characteristic gives
  f(t)=1-theta[kappa v(t)+i(g/d)u(t)]+O(theta^2),
uniformly in t in [t0,T]. Consequently
  D(f)/theta^2 -> Q(t)
   =[alpha+kappa v(t)]^2-beta^2+(g/d)^2 u(t)^2.               (11)
The lower bound D(f)/theta^2>=Delta_bar permits dominated convergence of (9).
Thus
  S_cl(T)=Delta_bar/{[alpha+kappa v(T)]^2-beta^2},
  B_cl(T)=2 kappa Delta_bar beta int_(u(t0))^(u(T))
       du/{[alpha+kappa(u-u(t0))]^2-beta^2+(g/d)^2u^2}^2.     (12)
These formulas are exact in the limiting model. Equation (12) alone is not a
finite-theta error certificate; the separate exact finite instance supplies one.

The positive leading law starts with the SUPPLIED JOINT density
  p_init(z1,z2)=Delta_bar/pi^2 *
    exp[-alpha(|z1|^2+|z2|^2)+2 beta Re(z1^*z2)].
Its deterministic amplitudes satisfy
  dot zi=-(d/2)zi-i g |zj|^2 zi, i!=j,
  zi(t)=exp(-dt/2) zi(0)
       *exp[-i(g/d)|zj(0)|^2(1-exp(-dt))].                  (13)
The detected port intensities are
  lambda_plus/minus=(kappa d/2)|z1(t)+/-z2(t)|^2
during the chosen window. Integrating exp(-integrated total intensity) against
the joint p_init and its interference moment gives exactly (12).

For this equal-loss, pure cross-Kerr example only, the whole leading marked
process is the old undamped process on the deterministic clock
  s=(1-exp(-dt))/d.
This clock saturates at 1/d. The assertion is not made for general hopping,
unequal losses, drives or noncommuting Hamiltonians: their controlled damped
flow must actually be solved.

An unchanged old weak-bath law instead retains constant amplitudes, nonlinear
clock t, and intensities kappa d |z|^2. Even a partial repair which damps the
amplitudes but still uses phase exp[-ig|zj(0)|^2 t] fails. That repaired model
is a well-defined positive classical predictor with precisely the same pooled
count law as (13), but its first-port balance is
  B_wrong=2 kappa d Delta_bar beta int_(t0)^T
    exp(-dt)dt/{[alpha+kappa v(t)]^2-beta^2+g^2 t^2}^2.       (14)
Comparing (12) with (14) isolates an error in nonlinear phase, rather than
merely observing that ordinary loss depletes the field.

6. WHAT A CURRENT EFFECTIVE STATE MUST RETAIN
===========================================
Ordinary loss changes phase dynamics through the time integral of the surviving
other-mode intensity. A current description with occupations alone cannot
recover the marked record. Two loss schedules with the same total attenuation
need not have the same integrated intensity and therefore need not have the
same relative phase, despite identical final occupation laws.

This does NOT force retention of the entire microscopic loss history in the
leading regime. For the permitted number-conserving controls and counts, a
joint positive distribution of current intensities I1,I2 and relative phase
phi=arg(z1)-arg(z2), together with the actual controller, is sufficient:
  dot Ii=-d Ii, dot phi=g(I1-I2),
  lambda_plus/minus=(kappa d/2)
       [I1+I2 +/-2 sqrt(I1 I2) cos(phi)].                    (15)
No-count propagation weights that distribution by survival; a revealed port
weights it by its path likelihood. The global phase is immaterial for this
specified instrument class. It would matter after expanding the class to
phase-referenced drives or suitable quadrature instruments.

The probability distribution is not generally summarized by its covariance.
For example, the equation for E[z1^*z2] includes
  -d E[z1^*z2] + i g E[(I2-I1)z1^*z2],
which needs a higher mixed moment. Repeated marked observations change the
joint intensity-phase distribution by non-Gaussian likelihood factors.
The two Gaussian parameters of the initial preparation do not remain an
exact posterior closure under arbitrary feedback. The single-record formula
above is not promoted into such a claim.

The exact finite-theta lifetime characteristic retains the loss-shot-noise
correction. At fixed macroscopic time with occupation O(theta^-1), fixed d
and chi=theta g, that correction vanishes in the displayed record limit, while
the deterministic damping of phase is order one. A finite-error general
quantum-record theorem still needs its independent generator/residual argument;
coefficient scaling and the one solvable instance do not provide it.
