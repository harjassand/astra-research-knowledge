THE DECISIVE CHANGE
A two-saddle Fourier local-CLT approximation is unnecessary. Fold the real Gaussian moment integral onto the positive half-line. This combines the parity-related saddles before numerical approximation and leaves a positive integrand, including arbitrarily small displacement at odd n. Its normalized width and complex analyticity bounds are uniform in the coefficient order and displacement.

COEFFICIENT THEOREM
Let a,c>=0 be rational, n>=0 an integer encoded in binary, and p>=1. Write H_n=[z^n]exp(a z^2/2+c z). There is a deterministic algorithm polynomial in total coefficient input length L, log(n+1), and p which either recognizes H_n=0 or produces an enclosure of log H_n of absolute width<=2^(-p). Equivalently one obtains a relative-accuracy enclosure in floating mantissa/exponent representation. Exact rational H_n is not the output: its numerator/denominator may require Omega(n) bits.
Zero/elementary cases: H_0=1; a=0 gives c^n/n!; c=0 gives H_even=(a/2)^(n/2)/(n/2)! and H_odd=0; a=c=0 handled directly.

POSITIVE INTEGRAL IDENTITY
For a,c>0, set d=c/sqrt(a), s=(-1)^n and m=(d+sqrt(d^2+4n))/2. Then m>=sqrt(n), m>=d and m-d=n/m. Since n!H_n is the n-th moment of N(c,a),
 H_n = a^(n/2)/(n!sqrt(2pi)) integral_0^infty t^n exp[-(t-d)^2/2] b_s(t) dt,
 b_s(t)=1+s exp(-2dt).
For even n the bracket is positive and >=1. For odd n it is positive for t>0 and equals -expm1(-2dt). There is no subtraction of two large integrated quantities.
Normalize at m:
 G(t)=exp{n[log(t/m)-(t-m)/m]-(t-m)^2/2} b_s(t)/b_s(m),
 I=integral_0^infty G(t)dt.
The prefactor can be evaluated stably in logarithms:
 log H_n=(n/2)log a-logGamma(n+1)-(1/2)log(2pi)+n log m
          -(1/2)(n/m)^2+log b_s(m)+log I.
Using m-d=n/m avoids subtracting close large numbers.

UNIFORM REAL BOUNDS
The base logarithm phi(t)=n log t-(t-d)^2/2 has phi'(m)=0 and phi''(t)=-n/t^2-1<=-1. Thus its normalized exponential is <=exp[-(t-m)^2/2].
Even parity satisfies b_+(t)/b_+(m)<=2. Odd parity uses concavity of b_-(t)=1-exp(-2dt): b_-(t)/b_-(m)<=max(1,t/m). Hence, for m>=1, the tails outside |t-m|<=R are bounded by 6 exp(-R^2/2) when R>=1.
For |t-m|<=1/2, n/m^2<=1 and log(1+u)-u>=-u^2 for |u|<=1/2 give a base ratio>=exp(-3/8). Both parity ratios are >=1/2 on that interval. Therefore I>=exp(-3/8)/2>1/4. This converts absolute integral error into relative coefficient error uniformly, even when odd H_n tends to zero as c tends to zero.

UNIFORM COMPLEX BOUNDS
Choose integer R=ceil sqrt(2(p+16)). If n<16(R+1)^2, evaluate the finite positive coefficient sum directly; it has O(p) terms, so numerical n does not enter the large-order branch.
Otherwise m>=4(R+1). On every complex radius-1 disk centered in [m-R,m+R], write z=t+iy. Then t>=m/2 and |y|<=1. The normalized base has modulus
 <=exp[-(t-m)^2/2+(1+n/t^2)y^2/2] <=exp(5/2).
For even parity |b_+(z)|/b_+(m)<=2. For odd parity,
 1-exp(-2dz)=2dz integral_0^1 exp(-2dzv)dv,
so |b_-(z)|<=|z|b_-(t)/t. Concavity yields |b_-(z)|/b_-(m)<=|z|/min(t,m)<2 throughout these disks. Therefore |G(z)|<32. No factor inverse in numerical c or exp(d^2) appears in the bound.

CERTIFIED EXPLICIT QUADRATURE
Partition [m-R,m+R] into exactly 4R panels of width1/2, with centers m-R+(2j+1)/4. Integrate the degree-K Taylor polynomial on each panel, K=p+R+16. By Cauchy's estimate on its radius-1 disk, the pointwise Taylor remainder is <=(32/3)4^(-K). Integrated remainder over all panels is <22R4^(-K). Add the explicit nonnegative tail bound6exp(-R^2/2). These choices leave a conservative margin below the requested relative error after I>1/4.
Taylor coefficients are computable using finite formal power-series arithmetic. For a center m+u and local variable v, the normalized base log coefficients are:
 l0=n[log(1+u/m)-u/m]-u^2/2;
 l1=-u[1+n/(m(m+u))];
 l2=-n/[2(m+u)^2]-1/2;
 lj=(-1)^(j+1)n/[j(m+u)^j] for j>=3.
Exponentiation uses f_j=(1/j)sum_{i=1}^j i*l_i*f_(j-i). The parity factor is an elementary exponential series; its odd constant term uses expm1, not 1-exp with unchecked cancellation. Interval arithmetic carries every coefficient, and a radius equal to the analytic Taylor-plus-tail bound is added to the integral enclosure.
There are O(sqrt p) panels, O(p) coefficients per panel and O(p^2) elementary formal-series operations per panel. All algebraic/transcendental inputs are evaluated to polynomially many bits, conservatively O(p+K log(K(R+1))+L+log(n+1)). Large cancellations n log m-logGamma(n+1) require O(log n+log L) guard bits, not O(n). Small odd displacement can be factored as b_-(t)=2dt*sinhc(dt)*exp(-dt), or evaluated with certified expm1; its bit cost depends on L. Standard rigorous log/exp/logGamma algorithms complete the bit-polynomial existence proof. Particular Arb library performance is separately measured, not formally bounded by these finite tests.

PARITY LOGCONCAVITY AND THE SAMPLING CONSEQUENCE
E_j=H_(2j) is the convolution of (a/2)^q/q! with c^(2r)/(2r)!. O_j=H_(2j+1) is the same first sequence convolved with c^(2r+1)/(2r+1)!. Each factor sequence is nonnegative logconcave with no internal zeros. Convolution preserves logconcavity: the associated Toeplitz matrices have nonnegative 2-by-2 minors, and Cauchy-Binet preserves them under multiplication. Limits handle a=0 or c=0. Thus both parity sequences of H_n are logconcave.
For displaced single-herald matching, the cross count k has w(k)=b^k H_(h-k)^2/k! up to a common factor. Its adjacent ratio within one parity is
 w(k+2)/w(k)=b^2/[(k+1)(k+2)] [H_(h-k-2)/H_(h-k)]^2.
The H ratio decreases as k increases, hence each parity restriction is logconcave. The full k law may be bimodal; the earlier h=2 counterexample remains valid. Combining the two parity envelopes in one rejection table does not require either parity's normalizer.
Exact mode comparison would be an unnecessary hidden precision gate: rational weights can be extremely close. Instead bisect the monotone log ratio, certifying its sign unless its enclosure lies within [-delta,delta], delta=1/[4(h+1)]. Stop at such an approximate anchor. This costs only O(log h) extra coefficient accuracy bits. Logconcavity implies every rise away from this anchor is at most exp(delta*h)<2 over the entire support. Multiplying nearest-endpoint dyadic rectangle heights by2 then dominates the full block. Charging each outer block to its predecessor loses at most another factor2, giving total envelope mass<=12 times the true mass. A common reference for both parity peaks can be chosen to within a constant factor by certified coarse log-weight comparisons; refine only to a fixed absolute accuracy, so ties cause no stall. Certified grid rounding and capped rejection give an epsilon-TV cross-count sampler in polynomial bit time.
Given k, let n=h-k. The two own-pair counts are conditionally independent with weights
 v(q)=(a/2)^q c^(n-2q)/[q!(n-2q)!], 0<=q<=floor(n/2).
Their ratios ((a/2)/c^2)*(n-2q)(n-2q-1)/(q+1) decrease strictly, so the original unimodal finite-bit routine applies. Boundary counts are C00=q0,C11=q1,C01=k, singleton edges to ghost n-2q0 and n-2q1. Add the source Poisson ghost self-count and reverse the inherited Schur elimination. This analytically removes the binary-large-h restriction also for nonnegative displacement and one herald. The separate implementation/audit status must be checked before claiming completed executable composition.

Every one of S+4 slots receives 3epsilon/[4(S+4)], and all capped uniform tickets together receive epsilon/4. Adaptive coupling gives the stated epsilon TV bound under the ideal fair-bit model and valid numerical enclosures.
The reference is the maximum of two coarsely enclosed logweight UPPER endpoints, extracted inside a high-precision context and stored as an exact dyadic.
