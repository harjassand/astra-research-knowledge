# Cross-flux proposal: targeted prior-art check

**Verdict.** The capacity enclosure is classical Dirichlet–Thomson theory; the related complementary-energy error identity is the Prager–Synge/hypercircle idea ([review](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.00440)). Berman–Konsowa already optimizes over probability laws on reactive paths and over unit flows, with the harmonic current attaining the path-flow variational bound ([den Hollander & Jansen, 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.1305), especially Theorems 1.1 and 1.4). TPT already uses divergence-free reactive current and conserved flux through separating/isocommittor surfaces ([E, Ren & Vanden-Eijnden, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cplett.2005.07.084); [Metzner, Schütte & Vanden-Eijnden, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1137/070699500)). I found no reason to claim the variational enclosure, flux normalization, or a new capacity principle here. I did not find this exact continuous cross-flux sampling rule as a named algorithm; coarea gives it directly, so that alone is not a novelty claim.

Let \(C=\rho D\), \(u|_A=0,u|_B=1\), and let \(j\) be divergence-free in the transition domain, with unit flux from \(A\) to \(B\), no side leakage, and \(j\cdot\nabla u\ge0\). Then \(q=j\cdot\nabla u\) integrates to one. For regular levels \(\Sigma_s=\{u=s\}\), coarea yields
\[
q(x)\,dx = ds\,(j\cdot n_s)\,dS,\qquad n_s=\nabla u/|\nabla u|.
\]
If each level separates \(A,B\), its total flux is one. Thus the proposed sampler is: draw \(s\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\), then draw on \(\Sigma_s\) from normalized normal-flux measure. Drawing a streamline by source flux and intersecting it at uniform \(u\) gives this law when streamlines are well-defined, monotone, and reach \(B\). This is a continuous transport/coarea construction, not by itself a certificate that the conditional surface or streamline sampler is tractable.

There is a necessary condition missing from “\(q\ge0\), \(\int q=1\).” Define energy measures \(d\mu_u=\rho\nabla u^TD\nabla u\,dx\) and \(d\mu_j=j^TC^{-1}j\,dx\). The weights
\[
a=\frac{d\mu_u}{q\,dx},\qquad b=\frac{d\mu_j}{q\,dx}
\]
obey \(\mathbb E_q a=\mathcal E(u)\), \(\mathbb E_q b=\mathcal R(j)\), and \(ab\ge1\) only when **both** energy measures are absolutely continuous with respect to \(q\,dx\). Only then does \([1/\mathbb E_q b,\mathbb E_q a]\) enclose capacity. Without this overlap, the expectations omit unseen energy. On a graph with two parallel \(A\!\to\!B\) edges of conductances \(c_1,c_2\), take \(u_A=0,u_B=1\) and unit flow \(j=(1,0)\). Then \(q=(1,0)\), while \(\mathrm{cap}=c_1+c_2\); samples see only edge 1, so the apparent \(\mathbb E_q a=c_1\) misses \(c_2\) (arbitrarily large). Feasibility alone therefore does not give a valid MC upper bound or rarity-independent relative guarantee.

At the exact pair \(u=h\), \(j=C\nabla h/\mathrm{cap}\), the proposal is \(q=\rho\nabla h^TD\nabla h/\mathrm{cap}\), and \(a\equiv\mathrm{cap}, b\equiv1/\mathrm{cap}\) on its support. This is the zero-variance ideal, not evidence that an approximate pair has coverage or concentration.

**Residual opening, conditional and concrete:** construct a generator-aware, exactly feasible pair and a sampler that certifiably covers both energy measures, then prove useful tail/dominance bounds for \(a,b\) and a finite-cost confidence enclosure whose width is controlled by the primal–dual gap. For example, certified bounds \(a\le A_*, b\le B_*\) give Hoeffding radii \(r_a=A_*\sqrt{\log(4/\delta)/(2n)}\), \(r_b=B_*\sqrt{\log(4/\delta)/(2n)}\), hence the valid \(1-\delta\) interval \([1/(\bar b+r_b),\bar a+r_a]\); relative accuracy still requires the resulting endpoints to be close, and useful sample cost requires known bounds relative to the means. Required charged inputs include acquisition of \(\rho,D\) and field evaluations, learning/solving for \(u,j\), verifying boundary traces, divergence, unit flux, sign, overlap and tails, and sampling/validating the coarea or streamline map. Unit-flux normalization avoids the equilibrium partition function for sampling \(q\); it does not supply these checks or control weight tails. A global scale in \(\rho\) cancels from \(q\), but scales capacity and may still be needed for an absolute rate unless it cancels against a separately certified basin normalization. If the model admits exact energy integration, use it directly; sampling adds no certificate. A Piola/invertible-flow parameterization could enforce some constraints, but support coverage, tail control, and acquisition complexity remain the theorem-level obligations. Without stated structural assumptions, the two-channel example rules out the proposed general guarantee.
