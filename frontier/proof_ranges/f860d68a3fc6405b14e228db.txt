## 6. Contradiction and what is left open

Choose tests by source Lemma 7.1 using the unchanged bound \(\sum_k B_k^{16}<\infty\). Then \(H(C)\le\log\#\mathcal D_{\log n}=o(N)\) for the sampled cell C. Since (5.3) gives list entropy greater than .994N, eventually \(H(U|C),H(V|C)\ge.99N\). Conditional subadditivity gives average one-entry entropy at least \(.99\ell_i\). Given C and an entry index, the two inputs are independent under the pair law. The unchanged binary symplectic estimate in source Lemma 7.2 implies
\[
 \mathbb E_\rho Z\le.62. \tag{6.1}
\]
The catch-all cell causes no additional entropy cost beyond H(C); the full pair-law marginals remain exactly \(\widetilde\nu\).

For the lower bound, let \(a:M\to\mathcal A\) be the finite symbol observable transported from X by J on D (extend it arbitrarily off D). Choose Borel representatives of its atoms and disjoint compact cores \(C_b\subset D\cap a^{-1}(b)\) with \(\mu(M\setminus C)<\delta_c\), where \(C=\bigcup_b C_b\); inner regularity gives these cores. Distinct nonempty cores have positive pairwise distance. The main input law \(\widetilde\nu_K\) misses C at any time by at most \(\omega_K(\delta_c)\), while the bad input component contributes at most \(1-p<\epsilon\). For every t, \(\sigma\le2\widetilde\nu\) and (4.1) give \((S^t)_*\sigma(C^c)\le2p\omega_K(\delta_c)+2(1-p)\). If two shadowing points are both in C and closer than the minimum separation between distinct cores, they have the same symbol. Thus each half's expected symbol-mismatch fraction is at most \(3\omega_K(\delta_c)+3\epsilon\). Choose \(\epsilon+\omega_K(\delta_c)<d_*/20000\); Markov's inequality, at the source threshold \(d_*/100\) on each half, makes the combined failure of (7.7)–(7.8) at most .03.

The source graph-exception event has \(\mu_0\)-mass \(\alpha_{i,D}\to0\). For the switched output, use \(\sigma\le2\widetilde\nu\). Let E be the graph-exception event in X and charge output outside D at the same time. On K the code is defined and the event has mass at most \(\omega_K^X(\alpha_{i,D})\), where \(\omega_K^X(s)=\sup_{\mu_0(B)\le s}\lambda_K(B)\to0\); the entire bad component has mass \(1-p\), covering both exception and coding-domain failure. Hence
\[
 \sigma(J^{-1}E\cup D^c)\le2p\,\omega_K^X(\alpha_{i,D})+2(1-p).
\]
Choose \(1-p<.002\), then a sufficiently late test so the first term is below .005; the total is below .01. Together with \(\rho(G)\ge.99\) and mismatch failure at most .03, at least .95 of the original pair law has valid coding, symbol matching, and graph relations. On that set the source recognition argument yields \(Z\ge.96\). Hence
\[
 \mathbb E_\rho Z\ge.95\cdot.96=.912>.62,
\]
contradicting (6.1).

All coding-domain failures are included: D is invariant; the main component is absolutely continuous with respect to invariant \(\mu\), so all its shifts stay in D a.s.; the input bad component has mass \(1-p\); and output bad mass is bounded by \(2(1-p)\) from \(\sigma\le2\widetilde\nu\). The system is fixed before the hypothetical model; K, bump, and all model-dependent constants are selected afterward, and source test selection absorbs them at late scales.

## 7. Significance and limit

The source's exact limitation is that it rules out smooth positive invariant densities but does not claim an obstruction for every invariant measure equivalent to volume. The modified construction above goes further: it rules out the **entire absolutely-continuous class**, including densities that vanish on positive volume. It thereby forces any smooth model, if one exists, to use a purely singular invariant probability. This is a meaningful, independently consequential boundary for the smooth-realization problem. It leaves all singular invariant measures open and is not a claimed historic resolution of arbitrary-measure smooth realization.

The theorem is stated for closed manifolds. No boundary or singular-measure extension is claimed.
