## A fully N687-compatible rare-bump example

The rare-coordinate scaling is realizable by a conservative globally Lipschitz field, rather than only by an abstract product law. Set phi(t)=(1-|t|)_+ and w=d^(-1/4), and define:

\[
g_i(x)=Aw\,\phi(x_i/w),\qquad
F(x)=Aw^2\sum_{i=1}^d\int_0^{x_i/w}\phi(s)\,ds.
\]

Then g is the gradient of F, F is C^{1,1}, and:

\[
\|g(x)-g(y)\|_2^2\le A^2\|x-y\|_2^2.
\]

For standard Gaussian input G, the coordinates X_i=g_i(G) are independent and satisfy |X_i|<=Aw. Here phi_N denotes the standard normal density; the exact one-coordinate integrals give:

\[
\Pr(X_i\ne0)=\Pr(|G_i|<w)\le\sqrt{2/\pi}\,w,
\quad \mathbb E X_i=A\phi_{\rm N}(0)w^2+O(Aw^4),
\quad \operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\tfrac23A^2\phi_{\rm N}(0)w^3+O(A^2w^4),
\]

For fixed A and sigma, the choice w=d^(-1/4) gives:

\[
\|m\|_2\longrightarrow A/\sqrt{2\pi},\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(\mathcal L(g(G)+Z),N(m,\sigma^2I_d))=O(d^{-1/4})
\]


Independence in the rare-coordinate claim is essential. A finite check with all coordinates equal to one rare centered Bernoulli variable has the same bounded and rare coordinate marginals, but at d=10^4 and w=0.1 its exact one-plus-chi-square value is 1.51e33; incorrectly multiplying marginal bounds would suggest a small exponent 0.0041. General N687 fields need not have independent coordinates; use the vector-subGaussian theorem unless independence has been proved for the particular field and input distribution.
