## 3. Every stochastic encoder gives a convex-order evolution

Let X_0 lie on the unit sphere, initially correlated with an m-state variable S_0. Generate independent gates with law nu, independent of (X_0,S_0), and let X_t=g_t X_{t-1}. Update S_t with an arbitrary kernel P_t(g_t), using fresh randomness.

Define

\[
 q_i(t)=\Pr(S_t=i),\quad v_i(t)=\mathbb E[X_t\mid S_t=i],\quad
 \mu_t=\sum_iq_i(t)\delta_{v_i(t)}.
\]

Zero-weight states may be assigned any vector. Every mu_t has at most m atoms in the unit ball. Put

\[
 M_t=\int\|v\|^2d\mu_t(v),\qquad
 R_t=\int\|v\|d\mu_t(v).
\]

**Lemma 2.** With Qmu the average of the rotated measures,

\[
 \mu_t\le_{cx}Q\mu_{t-1},\quad
 M_{t-1}-M_t=
 \mathbb E\|g_tv_{S_{t-1}}(t-1)-v_{S_t}(t)\|^2\ge0.
 \tag{12}
\]

**Proof.** Given S_{t-1} and g_t, the new state is conditionally independent of X_{t-1}. Therefore, if U=g_tv_{S_{t-1}}(t-1),

\[
 \mathbb E[U\mid S_t]=\mathbb E[X_t\mid S_t]=v_{S_t}(t).
\]

The law of U is Qmu_{t-1}. Conditional Jensen proves convex order, and conditional Pythagoras proves the variance identity. QED.

This is an unconditional coupling argument. It does not incorrectly assume that the output-state distribution is independent of the current gate. In general that independence is false.

Combining Lemma 1 with (12), stochastic merging can only decrease E_r further after gate averaging.

## 4. Spectral mixing produces a definite entropy drop

Let J be orthogonal projection onto radial functions in L2(R^3,dx), and let F=sqrt(f_mu), so ||F||_2=1. Applying (SG) on each sphere and integrating in the radial variable gives

\[
 \|QF\|_2^2\le\|JF\|_2^2+\lambda^2\|F-JF\|_2^2.
 \tag{13}
\]

**Lemma 3.**

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)-\mathscr E_r(Q\mu)
 \ge\beta\|\sqrt{f_\mu}-J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2^2.
 \tag{14}
\]

**Proof.** Rotations preserve gamma_r and commute with Gaussian smoothing. Therefore

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)-\mathscr E_r(Q\mu)
 =h(Qf)-h(f)=\sum_g\nu(g)D(g_\#f\|Qf).
\]

For densities p,q,

\[
 D(p\|q)\ge\|\sqrt p-\sqrt q\|_2^2.
\]

One proof is Jensen: D(p||q)>=-2 log integral sqrt(pq)>=2(1-integral sqrt(pq)). Averaging this bound gives

\[
 \sum_g\nu(g)D(g_\#f\|Qf)
 \ge2-2\langle QF,\sqrt{Qf}\rangle
 \ge2-2\|QF\|_2
 \ge1-\|QF\|_2^2.
\]

Use (13) and ||F||_2=1. QED.

The key feature is that the same E_r is decreased by both steps: the rotations' averaging and the encoder's convex-order contraction. Neither operation can recharge the other one's loss.

## 5. An m-atomic law with nonzero moment cannot be angularly smooth at scale m^{-1/2}

**Lemma 4.** Suppose mu has at most m atoms in the unit ball and integral ||v||^2 dmu >=a, where 0<a<=1. Set

\[
 r=\frac{a}{8\sqrt{3m}}.
\]

Then

\[
 \|\sqrt{f_\mu}-J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2^2\ge a/16.
 \tag{15}
\]

**Proof.** Set s=sqrt(a/2) and L=2sqrt(3). The mu-mass of centers with norm at least s is at least a/2, since E||V||^2<=s^2+Pr(||V||>=s). Let E be the union of the Euclidean balls of radius Lr about those centers. Markov's inequality gives Pr(||Z||<=L)>=3/4 for standard Z in R^3. Hence

\[
 \int_E f_\mu\ge3a/8\ge a/4.
\]

A ball of radius b about a point of norm at least s, with b<s, is seen from the origin inside an angular cap with sine of angular radius at most b/s. Its normalized spherical area is at most (b/s)^2/2. This holds for its intersection with every sphere centered at the origin. Here

\[
 \frac{(Lr)^2}{s^2}=\frac{a}{8m}<1.
\]

Consequently E occupies at most angular fraction a/16 of each such sphere. Because J sqrt(f_mu) is radial,

\[
 \int_E(J\sqrt{f_\mu})^2\le(a/16)\|J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2^2\le a/16.
\]

The reverse triangle inequality on E now yields

\[
 \|\sqrt{f_\mu}-J\sqrt{f_\mu}\|_2
 \ge\sqrt{a/4}-\sqrt{a/16}=\sqrt a/4.
\]

Squaring proves (15). QED.
