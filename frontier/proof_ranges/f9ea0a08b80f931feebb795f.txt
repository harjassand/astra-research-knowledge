## 4. A different construction: exact graph-basis witness evaluation

The block calculation did not settle (T). A separate constructive search used complex graph-basis dyads, including different left and right graphs. The evaluation below proves exactly what the attached integer searches test; it does not prove positivity for every graph or every rank-two matrix.

Let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\), \(G\) a symmetric zero-diagonal matrix over \(\mathbb F_3\), and

\[
Q_G(z)=\sum_{i<j}G_{ij}z_iz_j,\qquad
|G_a\rangle=3^{-n/2}\sum_{z\in\mathbb F_3^n}\omega^{Q_G(z)+a\cdot z}|z\rangle.
\tag{10}
\]

These vectors form an orthonormal basis by character orthogonality.

### Same-graph closed formula

Fix \(\delta,v\in\mathbb F_3^n\), \(v\ne0\), and set
\(X=|G_0\rangle\langle G_{-\delta}|\),
\(Y=|G_v\rangle\langle G_{v-\delta}|\).
For each \(S\), write \(R=[n]\setminus S\),
\(M=G_{S,R}\), \(r=\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_3}M\), and \(b=-\delta_S\).
Set \(a_S=3^{-r}\) when \(Mk=b\) is soluble, and zero otherwise. Set \(b_S=0\) unless this system is soluble and \(v_R\) belongs to the row space of \(M\). If both conditions hold, choose any solution \(k_0\) and set

\[
b_S=3^{-r}\omega^{v_R\cdot k_0}.
\]

This is independent of the solution because \(v_R\) annihilates \(\ker M\). With

\[
A=\sum_S(-1/2)^{|S|}a_S,\qquad B=\sum_S(-1/2)^{|S|}b_S,
\]

one has, for all complex \(\alpha,\beta\),

\[
q_n(\alpha X+\beta Y)=A(|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2)+2\operatorname{Re}(\overline\alpha\beta B).
\tag{11}
\]

**Proof.** Direct summation over the traced variables gives

\[
(\operatorname{Tr}_S|G_a\rangle\langle G_b|)_{x,y}
=3^{-|R|}\omega^{Q_{G_R}(x)-Q_{G_R}(y)+a_R\cdot x-b_R\cdot y}
\mathbf1\{M(x-y)=b_S-a_S\}.
\tag{12}
\]

Here the labels \(a,b\) in (12) are arbitrary basis labels, distinct from the scalar quantities \(a_S,b_S\) defined above. For the diagonal norm, count the \(3^{|R|-r}\) admissible differences and \(3^{|R|}\) choices of \(y\), obtaining \(3^{-r}\). For the cross inner product, the phase remaining on the difference is \(v_R\cdot(x-y)\). The character sum over \(\ker M\) vanishes unless \(v_R\in\operatorname{row}M\); otherwise its value is \(3^{-r}\omega^{v_R\cdot k_0}\). Summing with the coefficients in (1) proves (11). □

Thus this whole two-dimensional dyad span is nonnegative exactly when \(A\ge|B|\). Writing \(A=a/L\), \(B=(p+q\omega)/L\), the test becomes the integer inequality
\(a^2\ge p^2-pq+q^2\), together with \(a\ge0\).

### Different left and right graphs

For graphs \(G,H\), take
\(X=|G_0\rangle\langle H_{-\delta}|\),
\(Y=|G_v\rangle\langle H_{w-\delta}|\), with \(v,w\ne0\).
Every linear combination still has rank at most two. The required two-by-two matrix is

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}q_n(X)&b_n(X,Y)\\\overline{b_n(X,Y)}&q_n(Y)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its positivity is equivalent to positivity of the diagonals and the determinant. By (4), each summand in these three entries is a finite quadratic character sum. To make the evaluation reconstructible, write \(z=(x,y)\in\mathbb F_3^{2n}\),
\(A_0=\operatorname{diag}(2G,2H)\), and let \(P_S\) swap corresponding \(x,y\) coordinates in \(S\). The swap expectation
\(\langle G_a\otimes H_d,F_S(G_c\otimes H_b)\rangle\) equals

\[
3^{-2n}\sum_z\omega^{z^TAz+\ell^Tz},\quad
A=P_S^TA_0P_S-A_0,\quad
\ell=P_S^T(c,b)-(a,d).
\tag{13}
\]

Diagonalize by a congruence over \(\mathbb F_3\):
\(E^TAE=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0)\), with each \(d_i\in\{1,2\}\), and \(\ell'=E^T\ell\).
If any of the last \(2n-r\) coordinates of \(\ell'\) is nonzero, the sum is zero. Otherwise, completing squares and using
\(\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_3}\omega^{t^2}=1+2\omega\) and
\(\sum_t\omega^{2t^2}=-(1+2\omega)\) gives exactly

\[
\omega^{-\sum_{i=1}^r d_i(\ell_i')^2}
\frac{(-1)^{\#\{i:d_i=2\}}(1+2\omega)^r}{3^r}.
\tag{14}
\]

Equations (13)–(14), summed over \(S\) with coefficient \((-1/2)^{|S|}\), evaluate every entry of \(K\). They include singular quadratic forms. Since \((1+2\omega)^2=-3\), the result lies in \(\mathbb Q(\omega)\). There are \(2^n\) subsets: this is an exponential-time formula, not a polynomial-time arbitrary-witness algorithm.
