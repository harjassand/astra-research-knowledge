2. Transfer-function construction

Let a = 2*pi*0.01 per second, delta = a/40 = pi/2000 per second, and A = 125
mOhm. Let B(s) be the common positive RC baseline whose exact compact nodes,
positive masses, and positive series-inductance coefficient are retained in
verified_hard_pair.json. Define

  Z_+(s) = B(s) + 2A*delta/(s+delta),
  Z_-(s) = B(s) + 2A*delta/(s+2delta).

The extra term in Z_+ is a positive RC mode of resistance 2A and relaxation time
1/delta. The extra term in Z_- is a positive RC mode of resistance A and
relaxation time 1/(2delta). Both are finite positive RC models; no ideal slow
endpoint or resonant mode is used for the difference. Their transfer difference
is

  D(s) = Z_+(s)-Z_-(s)
       = 2A*delta^2 / ((s+delta)(s+2delta)).                    (1)

Let d(t) be the unit-step-response difference and g(t) its derivative. Direct
integration gives

  d(t) = A*(1-exp(-delta*t))^2,
  g(t) = 2A*delta*(exp(-delta*t)-exp(-2delta*t)) >= 0.         (2)

The first-order early response cancels exactly. In particular d(t) is of order
t^2 at the origin, even though the two positive RC responses are each of order
t. The absence of a signed impulse or a high-frequency discrepancy prevents an
arbitrarily fast switch from exposing this pair immediately.

The common baseline cancels for every input. For zero incremental initial
conditions and any bounded measurable current i, their voltage difference is

  Delta v(t) = integral_0^t g(t-u)*i(u) du.                  (3)

Their common inductance causes no difference, including for finite ramps.
For peak-only ideal switches, its shared switching impulse is excluded or
subtracted using the common known L; the observation here is the regular
incremental voltage. Finite-slew inputs starting from zero avoid that impulse.
