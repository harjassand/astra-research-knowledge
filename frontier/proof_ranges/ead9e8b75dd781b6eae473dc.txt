## 3. Relation to the existing norm budget and robust observation errors

The concave function phi(a)=beta a-f(a/K) on [0,K] has derivative

    phi'(a)=beta-atanh(a/K)/K.

Its maximum is at a_*=K tanh(beta K), with

    max phi=log cosh(beta K).

Therefore the new bound never exceeds either beta a or log cosh(beta K).
For a binary label this never worsens either of the source's two budgets,
and can be strictly better than their minimum. For multiple labels take
the minimum with the source's finer graph value log C_norm; cosh(beta K)
need not improve that graph value.

For a confidence interval tau in [tau_minus,tau_plus], form the physically
feasible interval

    a in [max(0,-tau_plus), min(K,-tau_minus)].

If it is empty the observations/calibration are inconsistent. Maximize phi
on this interval: the maximizer is a_* clipped to its endpoints. With only
tau_minus, use the interval [0,min(K,-tau_minus)]. It is unsafe simply to
insert -tau_minus into a nonmonotone phi. Certified norm/temperature errors
must also be optimized over their uncertainty sets. A negative resulting
upper bound contradicts the physical contract and must not become C<1.

Set C_activity=exp(max phi), then take the minimum with any independently
proved source norm budget. The source histogram theorem applies with this C
unchanged. Histogram and kinetic confidence failures require their usual
joint allocation. Measurements from unverified equilibrium preparations do
not establish the premise of (2).
