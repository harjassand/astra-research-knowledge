## Finding

The ZnS studies establish two real control levers: electric-field polarity moves selected charged partials in opposite directions, and 365 nm illumination changes the relative glide mobility of Zn-rich and S-rich dislocations. **No same-platform ZnS operation in the audited studies demonstrates selective annihilation.** They do not show an illumination- or field-selected annihilation reaction. The closest direct semiconductor precedent is GaAs/Si under recombination-enhanced glide: Callahan et al. imaged one threading-dislocation pair reacting and interpreted the event as annihilation, leaving a short interfacial segment. That experiment did not compare reaction outcomes under a chosen carrier gate and a matched control.

Thus, “carriers can move dislocations,” “carriers can change which core class moves faster,” and “carriers select a reaction that leaves a retained repair” are three different claims. The first two have primary experimental support. Carrier-assisted area filtering is also prior art: Hughes et al. report a beam-driven decline in the count of mobile threads leaving an imaged AlGaAs/Si area. The third claim—selection among reaction outcomes with the product verified after the gate is removed—remains unshown in the sources inspected. A narrower, testable contribution would be a measured change in the *outcome distribution of a geometrically identified, compatible dislocation pair* under a carrier condition, with its product checked after the stimulus is removed.
