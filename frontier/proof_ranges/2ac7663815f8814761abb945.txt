## 7. An unavoidable finite-precision floor

Suppose the single-operator comparison is only known with an additive residual:

\[
 A\preceq g_a(C+BB^*)+\varepsilon I,
 \qquad B\text{ compact},\quad\varepsilon\ge0. \tag{7.1}
\]

Let `||T||_ess` denote the norm modulo compact operators. In the quotient algebra, set `m=||T||_ess^2`. Taking norms gives

\[
 m\le\frac{m}{1+am}+\varepsilon.
\]

Solving the scalar quadratic yields

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|T\|_{\mathrm{ess}}^2
 \le\frac{\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+4\varepsilon/a}}2.
 \quad} \tag{7.2}
\]

The bound is attained on an infinite-dimensional space by `B=0` and

\[
 T=\sqrt{m_*}\,I,
 \qquad
 m_*=\frac{\varepsilon+\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+4\varepsilon/a}}2.
\]

Thus the unresolved singular-value scale is asymptotically `(epsilon/a)^(1/4)`. An arbitrarily small nonzero residual does not certify compactness. In finite dimensions, the same identity block can have arbitrarily large multiplicity. This is a precision obstruction, not merely a poor bound from one numerical implementation.

For a rank-`r` source, the finite-dimensional interlacing recurrence becomes

\[
 \lambda_{j+r}\le g_a(\lambda_j)+\varepsilon,
\]

whose scalar limiting floor is the same `m_*`.
