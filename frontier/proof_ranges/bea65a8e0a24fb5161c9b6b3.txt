## Exact scalar construction

Let f0(q)=a q^2/2-b q^4/4+c q^6/6, b,c>0,
a=3b^2/(16c), qB^2=3b/(4c). At coexistence,
f0(q)=(c/6)q^2(q^2-qB^2)^2 >= 0.

Control f(q;u,h)=f0(q)+u q^4/4-hq. A simple protocol uses U=b:

1. q=0, h=0; raise u from 0 to U.
2. u=U; increase q from 0 to qB and apply h=a q+c q^5.
3. q=qB; lower u from U to 0 with h=u qB^3.

Stage 2 has f''=a+5c q^4 >= a. During stage 3,

f(q;u,u qB^3)-f(qB;u,u qB^3)
= f0(q)+u (q-qB)^2(q^2+2qB q+3qB^2)/4 >= 0.

Thus the selected state is a global minimum throughout, unique except at the
ungated coexistence endpoints; its local curvature never vanishes.

For 0<U<=b, the middle-stage global curvature bound is
mu=a-9(b-U)^2/(20c). The same construction works whenever mu>0,
using h=a q+(U-b)q^3+c q^5 in stage 2. U=0.55b gives mu=0.514a.

The more general elementary principle is tangent compensation: if F0 has equal
global minima A and B, G is convex and minimized at A, and F0+UG is strongly
convex, the auxiliary potential UG enables a unique-equilibrium traversal. During
removal at B use bias h=u grad G(B); the remaining energy difference contains
u times the nonnegative Bregman divergence of G. Implementing all components of
h and G is a separate physical requirement. This is not a historical novelty claim.
