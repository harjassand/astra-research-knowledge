## 5. The sample cost cannot be substantially improved

### Theorem — Universal lower bound

Any universal processor using \(n\) source samples and approximating every flagged target above to half-diamond error \(\varepsilon\) must satisfy
\[
\boxed{\varepsilon\ge\frac1{n+2}.}
\tag{10}
\]

Combined with (6), this establishes worst-case sample complexity
\[
\boxed{\Theta(1/\varepsilon)}
\]
independently of dimension.

### Proof

It suffices to choose a known, fixed channel: **SWAP on two qubits**.

Then
\[
M=\sigma,
\qquad
\mathcal F_\sigma(\rho)=\sigma\rho\sigma.
\tag{11}
\]

For a pure program \(\sigma=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\), the ideal success probability on \(|\phi\rangle\) is
\[
|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|^2.
\]

Any processor’s success probability is described by an effect
\[
0\le T\le I
\]
on the \(n\) program qubits and one information qubit. Twirling this effect by simultaneous qubit rotations cannot worsen its uniform error.

On
\[
\operatorname{Sym}^n(\mathbb C^2)\otimes\mathbb C^2,
\]
the rotation representation has two inequivalent irreducible components, each occurring once. Thus the twirled effect has the form
\[
T=a\Pi_++b\Pi_-,
\qquad 0\le a,b\le1,
\]
where \(\Pi_+\) projects onto the fully symmetric component.

For input
\[
|\psi\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes|\psi\rangle,
\]
success probability is \(a\), so
\[
a\ge1-\varepsilon.
\]

For input
\[
|\psi\rangle^{\otimes n}\otimes|\psi^\perp\rangle,
\]
the squared projection onto the fully symmetric component is \(1/(n+1)\). Its success probability is therefore at least
\[
\frac{a}{n+1},
\]
although its ideal success probability is zero. Hence
\[
\varepsilon
\ge\frac{1-\varepsilon}{n+1},
\]
which proves (10).

This is a programmable-measurement argument. Generalized swap tests and programmable projective measurements are established prior machinery; neither that interpretation nor the lower-bound technique is claimed new here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02546)
