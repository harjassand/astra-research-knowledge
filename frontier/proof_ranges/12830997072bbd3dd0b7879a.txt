## 1. Actual kernel rows and necessary notation

For fixed r<-2 the inherited certificate has S_r positive definite and
corrected dissipation

\[
\mathcal D_r(A,z)=(C_r^T(A\otimes z))^TS_r(C_r^T(A\otimes z)).
\]

The eight rows in the construction are exactly the actual exported rows.
With X=(a-d,b+c), Y=(-3b+(2r+1)c,(2r+1)a+3d), they imply

\[
X_i\,\psi_{j,yy}=0,\qquad
Y_i\,\psi_{j,xx}=0,
\qquad i,j\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{S1}
\]

These are **outer-product equations**, with all four products in each
family. A two-component Hadamard-product interpretation would be too weak
for the stated analytic case split. For the all-background heat application,
the map in (S1) is the full map \(\Psi(x)=Bx+\eta(x)\), so A=D Psi and
z=D² Psi=D² eta. It is not the perturbation gradient alone.

`check_kernel_and_scaling.py` reads C directly from the pinned JSON, rather

## 2. Analytic case split, with a stronger local consequence

If X is not identically zero on a connected analytic domain, one analytic
component is nonzero on an open set. Equation (S1) makes both components of
Psi_yy vanish there. Analytic continuation makes Psi_yy=0 everywhere.
Similarly, nonidentically-zero Y forces Psi_xx=0 everywhere.

The exceptional strain planes are handled correctly. X identically zero
is the ordinary Cauchy-Riemann system, so each component of Psi is harmonic.
Y identically zero is the anisotropic Cauchy-Riemann system

\[
\Psi_{2,y}=k\Psi_{1,x},\qquad
\Psi_{2,x}=-\Psi_{1,y}/k,\qquad k=-(2r+1)/3>1,
\]

whose cross derivatives give k² Psi_xx+Psi_yy=0 componentwise. The two
strain planes intersect only in A=0: their combined coefficient determinant
is 4(r+2)², nonzero on this branch. Thus there is no omitted exceptional
parameter or strain-plane intersection.

The construction's growth-based rigidity argument is sufficient. The full
rows in fact yield a stronger local statement:

**Any real-analytic map on a connected open set with zero full Gram density
is affine.** If both X and Y are nonidentically zero, (S1) gives both pure
second derivatives zero. If exactly one strain plane vanishes identically,
the remaining nonzero plane gives one pure second derivative zero, and the
corresponding harmonic/elliptic equation gives the other. If both vanish,
the gradient itself is zero. Hence in every case

\[
\Psi(x,y)=A x+B y+vxy+c.
\]

Use actual additional row ell_4. On this bilinear map it is

\[
\ell_4=A\cdot v+|v|^2y.
\]

Its vanishing on an open set forces v=0. This additional identity is checked
exactly. The original decay/sublinear-growth hypotheses are then needed
only to identify the allowed affine map: Bx+Schwartz has no nonconstant
perturbation, a sublinear affine map has zero gradient, and a periodic
perturbation of Bx is constant. The weaker construction proof remains sound
without using this strengthening.

## 3. Strict Jensen positivity and nonsmooth zero-boundary data

For compactly supported W1,4 data, all relevant L2,L3,L4 norms are finite.
Zero extension of a bounded-Lipschitz W0^(1,4) perturbation is a compactly
supported W1,4 map; no derivative trace is required. Positive anisotropic
heat time makes the perturbation analytic and Schwartz, even though the
initial data need not be smooth. Gaussian convolution of compactly supported
L1 data and its differentiated kernels gives this directly.

Use the relative quartic energy at the full affine background. For t>0,
integration by parts and the global exact-form primitive give

\[
E'(t)=-\int\mathcal D_r(B+D\eta_t,D^2\eta_t)\le0.
\]

The primitive pullback contains a derivative of the heat field and is
Schwartz despite the constant background. Standard Gaussian decay gives
E(infinity)=0. Strong heat continuity in L2,L3,L4 gives E(t)->E(0).
If E(0)=0, monotonicity and the zero infinite-time limit force E(t)=0 at
every t>0. Thus the continuous nonnegative dissipation vanishes everywhere
at any positive time. The analytic rigidity above makes D eta_t=0.

Heat is injective on these gradients as tempered distributions. Its Fourier
multiplier is nonvanishing; inversion can be performed locally on compact
frequency supports, without treating the exponentially growing inverse
multiplier as a bounded global operator. Therefore D phi=0 initially, a
contradiction. This proves the claimed strict inequality for every nonzero
individual gradient at every fixed affine background. The same argument
uses periodic heat and the periodic affine-rigidity case on a torus.

This proof is valid for nonsmooth W0^(1,4) perturbations by applying heat
directly to the zero extension. Merely approximating a strict smooth
inequality and passing to a limit would not preserve strictness; that
incorrect shortcut is not being used.

The rank-one-valued bound is also correct. Convexity of a scalar quartic
g(t)=at⁴+bt³+ct²+dt+e gives c>=3b²/(8a), and hence
g(1)-g(0)-g'(0)>=a/3. The exact rank-one formula for J_r gives
J_r(R)>=alpha|R|⁴, so compatible rank-one-valued perturbations have margin
alpha/3. This does not itself control general rank-two gradients.

## 4. Critical heat identity: correct renormalization

For smooth angular h, let phi(rho,theta)=rho^(1/2)h(theta). This initial
map has sublinear growth, but its fourth-power gradient energy has logarithmic
divergences. It must not be substituted into the finite-energy compact-data
proof. The construction correctly works with positive-time **truncated**
energies instead.

Constant anisotropic heat commutes with scalar dilation. Its exact scaling
is psi_t(x)=t^(1/4)psi_1(x/sqrt(t)). Therefore

\[
E_R(t)=\int_{|x|<R}J_r(D\psi_t)
=E_{R/\sqrt t}(1).
\tag{S2}
\]

For smooth h, derivatives of the initial homogeneous map on far annuli
obey |D^m phi|=O(R^(1/2-m)). Split the Gaussian convolution into displacements
of size at most R/2 and its tail. Taylor expansion and the vanishing first
Gaussian moment give

\[
D\psi_1(R e_r)=R^{-1/2}[A_h(\theta)+O(R^{-2})],
\quad D^2\psi_1=O(R^{-3/2}).
\]

The tail containing the nonsmooth origin is exponentially small. All bounds
are uniform in theta for each fixed smooth h. No uniform bounds over all
smooth approximants are needed later.

Consequently R times the radial derivative of E_R(1) tends to I_r(h).
Differentiating (S2) yields

\[
\lim_{R\to\infty}E_R'(t)=-I_r(h)/(2t).
\tag{S3}
\]

Equivalently, E_R(t)-I_r(h)log R has a finite limit of the form
C-I_r(h)log(t)/2. This explains the renormalization without ever asserting
that the full energy is finite.

The differentiated energy also has an integration-by-parts expression. The
heat flux and the cubic primitive pullback are both O(R^-3) on the circle;
after integrating over its O(R) length they vanish as O(R^-2). The bulk
corrected density is O(R^-4), absolutely integrable at infinity, and smooth
at the origin for t>0. Passing to the limit gives precisely

\[
I_r(h)=2t\int_{\mathbb R^2}
\mathcal D_r(D\psi_t,D^2\psi_t).
\tag{S4}
\]

The factor 2t, the sign, and the use of radial scaling with anisotropic
diffusion are all correct. Full right rotational invariance is unnecessary.
Thus the principal analytic identity survives the attempted falsification.

## 5. Rough angular profiles and zero-energy rigidity

If h_n->h strongly in W1,4(S1), then the angular energies and N(h_n) converge
by polynomial continuity. The one-dimensional Sobolev estimate gives uniform
convergence and a common bound |phi_n(x)|<=C|x|^(1/2). Gaussian convolution
therefore makes all positive-time heat derivatives converge uniformly on
compact sets. The limiting Gram density is nonnegative and continuous.

Fatou applied to (S4) gives exactly the required one-sided inequality

\[
I_r(h)\ge2\int\mathcal D_r(D e^L\phi,D^2 e^L\phi)\ge0.
\tag{S5}
\]

An equality for rough angular data was not assumed or needed. If I_r(h)=0,
the heat density vanishes. The analytic field has sublinear growth, so its
gradient is zero. Local Fourier inversion for the nonvanishing heat multiplier
then gives zero initial gradient; degree-one-half homogeneity makes phi=0.
Thus h=0. The origin singularity, infinite full energy, and nonsmooth angular
derivative do not invalidate this step.

## 6. Compactness proves a positive angular infimum without a minimizer

N(h)=integral(|h|²/4+|h'|²)² is equivalent to the fourth power of the W1,4
norm. A sequence N(h_n)=1 is bounded in W1,4, hence has a uniformly convergent
subsequence and weakly convergent derivatives in L4. For fixed h and theta,
v -> J_r(h tensor e_r/2+v tensor e_theta) is convex because differences are
rank one. Its continuous convex integral functional on L4 is weakly lower
semicontinuous. Uniform convergence of h_n changes its energy by at most
C||h_n-h||infinity times a uniformly bounded cubic integral.

If the normalized infimum were zero, the limit would satisfy I_r(h)<=0.
Equation (S5) and zero-energy rigidity force h=0. Thus h_n->0 uniformly.
The pointwise rank-one Bregman lemma, applied with base h_n tensor e_r/2,
then gives

\[
I_r(h_n)\ge\frac\alpha3\int|h_n'|^4
-C\|h_n\|_\infty^3\int|h_n'|.
\]

The last term tends to zero, so h_n' tends strongly to zero in L4. This
contradicts N(h_n)=1. Therefore the fixed-r angular infimum is strictly
positive. The argument specifically handles potential weak derivative-norm
loss. It does **not** require or establish attainment by a minimizer, nor
does it produce a numerical value or computable lower enclosure for delta_r.

## 7. Annular concentration scope

For fixed logarithmic-width cutoff transitions on exp(-T)<rho<1,
the main homogeneous annulus contributes exactly T I_r(h) and T N(h).
Each transition contributes O_r(N(h)), uniformly in T. This follows directly
from the formula for the cutoff gradient and scale invariance of the fourth
power at degree one half. Thus the angular margin excludes zero-ratio
annular sequences at B=0, even for varying normalized angular profiles.

At a fixed affine B and N(h)=1, the L2 and L3 gradient integrals remain
bounded uniformly in T, since rho^-1/2 is radially in L2 and L3 near zero.
The affine linear term integrates to zero. The lower-order cross terms are
therefore O_(r,B)(1), whereas the principal quartic term is at least delta_r T.
The same exclusion holds in this explicitly normalized scope.

The bounded-cross-term argument alone does not cover arbitrary T-dependent
profile amplitudes at fixed B, nor growing affine backgrounds. Rescaling a
small profile amplitude to N=1 changes the effective background. Such
amplitude/background escapes must not be silently included in the annular
claim. Nor does this result cover general two-dimensional concentration,
oscillation, or multiscale gradient fields.
