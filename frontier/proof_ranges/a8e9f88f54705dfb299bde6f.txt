3. A time-dependent physical discretization with controlled activity
Set
\[
A(t)=\frac12\mathsf L(t)^\dagger\mathsf L(t)+iH(t),
\]
and define
\[
a=\frac{g^2}{2}+h_0,
\qquad
D=a^2+g\ell_L+\ell_H,
\qquad
\lambda=g^2T.
\tag{19}
\]
Use a uniform grid of \(N\) cells, width \(\Delta=T/N\), with right endpoints \(t_j\). A single step has Kraus operators
\[
\begin{aligned}
K_{0,j}&=R_j=(I+\Delta A(t_j))^{-1},\\
K_{\mu,j}&=\sqrt\Delta\,L_\mu(t_j)R_j,\\
K_{\mathrm{err},j}&=\Delta A(t_j)R_j.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
Equation (7) proves exact trace preservation. The final outcome is an artificial numerical-error outcome, not a physical jump. Its probability is small:
\[
\|K_{\mathrm{err},j}\|^2\le\Delta^2a^2.
\tag{21}
\]
On this outcome the algorithm stops and returns an error-flagged state; it does not discard the execution.
An interval containing \(n\) grid cells is compiled using
\[
B_j=\sqrt{\Delta/2}\,\mathsf L(t_j),
\qquad
H_j=\Delta H(t_j).
\]
Thus its first-event query cost is
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left(
1+g\sqrt{n\Delta}+h_0n\Delta
\right).
}
\tag{22}
\]
Crucially,
\[
B_j/(g\sqrt{\Delta/2})=\mathsf L(t_j)/g.
\]
Making the grid finer does not require a new, stronger matrix oracle.
Local channel error
For \(a\Delta\le1/2\), the recorded single-cell instrument differs from the exact physical instrument by at most
\[
\boxed{32D\Delta^2}
\tag{23}
\]
in diamond norm, when exact jump times are reported by their grid-cell index.
Here are the components of the bound.
The exact no-jump propagator \(U_j\) satisfies
\[
\|U_j-R_j\|
\le
\frac{\Delta^2}{2}
\left(a^2+g\ell_L+\ell_H\right).
\tag{24}
\]
Freezing \(A(t)\) gives the Lipschitz contribution; comparing the exponential with the resolvent gives the \(a^2\) contribution.
For a single jump at time \(s\) within the cell, compare
\[
U(t_j,s)L_\mu(s)U(s,t_{j-1})
\quad\text{with}\quad
L_\mu(t_j)R_j.
\]
Using the stacked map, their operator-norm difference is bounded by
\[
\Delta(3ag+\ell_L).
\]
After integrating and converting to the corresponding completely positive maps, this contributes at most
\[
\Delta^2(6ag^2+2g\ell_L).
\]
The total probability of two or more jumps is bounded by
\[
\sum_{k\ge2}\frac{(g^2\Delta)^k}{k!}
\le\frac e2g^4\Delta^2,
\]
and the numerical branch contributes at most \(\Delta^2a^2\). These bounds sum to less than (23). They remain valid after tensoring with an arbitrary reference system.
The analysis compares complete instruments before conditioning. No error bound is divided by the probability of an observed jump.
Controlling expected jump count, not just channel error
Expected running time requires an additional estimate. Let \(K_{\mathrm{disc}}\) count physical jumps in the discrete process, stopped on a numerical-error outcome. Then
\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E K_{\mathrm{disc}}-\nu_\rho
\right|
\le
64T\Delta D(1+g^2T).
}
\tag{25}
\]
To see this, write \(\sigma_{j-1}\) for its unnormalised active state. Its jump probability in cell \(j\) is
\[
p_j
=
\Delta\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
\mathsf L(t_j)^\dagger\mathsf L(t_j)
R_j\sigma_{j-1}R_j^\dagger
\right].
\]
The accumulated state error is at most \(32T\Delta D\). Also,
\[
\|R_j-I\|\le a\Delta,\qquad
\|\dot\rho(t)\|_1\le4a,
\]
and
\[
\|\mathsf L(t)^\dagger\mathsf L(t)
-\mathsf L(s)^\dagger\mathsf L(s)\|
\le2g\ell_L|t-s|.
\]
Comparing \(\sum_jp_j\) with the integral in (3) gives (25).
Choose \(N\) to be a power of two satisfying
\[
N\ge
\max\!\left\{
8(1+\lambda),\;
2aT,\;
\frac{1024T^2D(1+\lambda)}{\varepsilon}
\right\}.
\tag{26}
\]
The zero-generator case is handled directly.
This makes both the accumulated physical-channel error and the expected-count error small. The large \(N\) enters clock registers, arithmetic and requested precision through \(\log N\); it does not become the number of sequential oracle calls.
