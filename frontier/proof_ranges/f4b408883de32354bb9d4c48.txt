\section{Confined solid-iron export: a physical architecture with missing admission}

\subsection{Mechanism and its demonstrated antecedents}

The desired operation reduces iron upstream of a gangue-retaining boundary,
deconstructs admitted metal at a contact, transports atoms through a confined
path and rebuilds/harvests metal downstream. It seeks to avoid melting gangue,
not merely reproduce fine-ore reduction.
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/1302.5709v2}{Coh et al. (2013)} experimentally moved
largely crystalline Fe through smaller CNT constrictions; their surface
reconstruction explanation and possible refining application are prior art.
\href{https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms9071}{Melinte et al. (2015)}
demonstrated export onto graphene and reverse admission of prepared external
nanoparticles. Their interface is nearby, conductive and nanoscale. Carbon
reaction, carbide formation and tube changes prevent treating the wall as
inert. Neither result demonstrates raw-ore extraction or bulk throughput.

A permanently continuous, hot 3 nm Fe shell is not a justified material.
\href{https://www.nature.com/articles/srep46725}{Fe-film dewetting on alumina}
is experimentally used to form catalyst particles. The more credible mutation
is an independently conducting capillary with a confined moving/regrowing Fe
plug. It must repeatedly acquire fresh metal from actual reduced ore, retain
source contact as the domain shrinks, reject impurities, harvest downstream
metal and preserve or restart a transport root. Those are required services,
not consequences of surface diffusivity.

\subsection{Governing model and conditional flux}

For capillary radius $r$, active length $L$, open fraction $f$, surface molar
density $\Gamma_s$, diffusivity $D_s$ and temperature $T$, stipulate
\begin{align}
 \mu&=\mu_0+\gamma V_m\mathcal K+V_m\sigma_h+RT\log a_{\rm Fe},\\
 J_{\rm mol}&=\frac{2f}{r}\frac{D_s\Gamma_s}{RT}
          \left[Z^*F E-\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial x}\right].\label{eq:ironflux}
\end{align}
Here $\mathcal K$ is mean-curvature sum, compression raises $\mu$, $V_m$ is
molar volume, $F$ is Faraday's constant, and $Z^*$ is effective migration
charge. All circumference active, uniform coefficients, connected metal,
open source/sink and no poisoning are assumed.

The \href{https://doi.org/10.2320/matertrans1960.20.523}{Nii--Yoshihara clean-$\alpha$-Fe measurement}
supplies $D_s=10\exp[-228000/(RT)]\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ in dry purified hydrogen
at 1073--1163 K. It does not measure a dirty ore contact or Fe/CNT interface;
moisture changes the kinetics. We use its law only for optimistic arithmetic.

For $T=1163\,\mathrm K$, $r=L=30\,\mathrm{nm}$, $f=0.2$, a 0.3 nm mobile
atomic layer, assumed $Z^*=10$, a 3 nm conducting Fe shell and assumed
conductivity $10^6\,\mathrm{S/m}$, the model gives:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Conditional quantity & Value\\
\midrule
Clean free-Fe $D_s$ & $5.75\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$\\
Passive flux with maintained $100\,\mathrm{J/mol}$ drop & $22.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}h^{-1}}$\\
Field for $10\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-2}h^{-1}}$ without opposition & $1.54\,\mathrm{kV/m}$\\
Voltage across active layer & $46.1\,\mu\mathrm V$\\
Projected current density & $64.5\,\mathrm{MA/m^2}$\\
Active-layer electricity & $0.297\,\mathrm{MWh/t\,Fe}$\\
Added electricity if effective contact $R_A=10^{-12}\,\Omega\mathrm{m^2}$ & $0.416\,\mathrm{MWh/t\,Fe}$\\
Initial shell inventory / depletion time without feed & $9.92\,\mathrm{mg/m^2}$ / $3.57\,\mathrm{ms}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Increasing both $r,L$ from 30 to 100 nm cuts passive flux by about 11.1 and
raises the target-flux active loss to about $3.19\,\mathrm{MWh/t}$ under the
same supplied coefficients. Neither value is a device simulation.
A full Fe plug has larger conducting cross-section and, if one keeps this
same hot free-surface model, about $4.76$ times the thin-shell local loss.
A separately conducting CNT is a different model: its measured current cannot
be combined with the Fe-shell $Z^*$ and free-surface $D_s$ as though all were
one validated interface.

\subsection{Exact failed interfaces}

\paragraph{Admission and recovery.}
Path conservation gives
\[
 \dot N_{\rm path}=S_{\rm feed}-J_{\rm export}-Q_{\rm loss},\qquad
 \int J_{\rm export}\,dt\le N_{\rm path}(0)+\int S_{\rm feed}\,dt.
\]
A short metal burst may simply drain the seed or shell. A gangue cage can admit
reducing gas while containing no metal-transport path to the boundary. In this
admitted counterexample, metallization can be complete and export zero.
Recovery is bounded by the source-connected iron fraction; grade and
metallization alone do not establish 94\% recovery. Fracture, liberation or
surface transport over gangue must be constructed and charged.

\paragraph{Stress, curvature and purity.}
The example's electromigration work is only about $44.5\,\mathrm{J/mol}$.
A hydrostatic difference of about $6.27\,\mathrm{MPa}$ cancels it in
\eqref{eq:ironflux}. Open growing surfaces may relieve stress, but constrained
contacts can stall. An assumed $\gamma=1\,\mathrm{J/m^2}$ gives a spherical
30 nm curvature scale near $473\,\mathrm{J/mol}$; a maintained favorable
$100\,\mathrm{J/mol}$ drop is not automatic. Coarsening or a new narrow entry
can reverse it. A small pore rejects large fragments, not automatically
surface-mobile or dissolved S, P, Ni, Cu, C or scaffold species. Atomwise
reconstruction is not a universal purification theorem.

\paragraph{Electrical topology and collection.}
Let total online area be $A$, projected current density $j$, local voltage $v$,
and product mass flux $m$. Dividing into $N$ equal cells and connecting them
\emph{electrically} in series gives
\[
 I=\frac{jA}{N},\qquad V=Nv,\qquad P=jAv,\qquad \dot M=mA,
\]
provided every cell has independent material feed and product access. Passing
the same atoms through all $N$ cells instead gives $\dot M=mA/N$.
A conducting common product rail shorts differing node potentials.
One concrete escape is isolated batch compartments, de-energized and equalized
before common harvesting. Downtime, duplicate cartridges and service costs
remain within the system boundary.

Contacts of specific resistivity $\rho_c$ and actual area at most $gA/N$
satisfy, by Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contact}
 \frac{P_c}{A}\ge\frac{\rho_cj^2}{g}.
\end{equation}
Series count cancels. A flare can enlarge real contact area only with an
explicit spreading and fabrication construction.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional conductor-volume/storage cost]\label{thm:wire}
Suppose $N_g$ connections in the equal-cell batch architecture each carry
$I=jA/N$ between node planes separated by at least $H$, with conductor
conductivity at most $\sigma$. If their total heat is at most $\eta jAv$, then
\[
 \frac{V_{\rm cond}}{A}\ge
 \frac{jH^2}{\sigma\eta v}\left(\frac{N_g}{N}\right)^2.
\]
If a run lasts $\tau$, feed contains $C_{\rm Fe}$ recoverable Fe mass per
volume, and product density is $\rho_{\rm Fe}$, then in the normal storage-stack
geometry one may substitute
$H\ge m\tau(C_{\rm Fe}^{-1}+\rho_{\rm Fe}^{-1})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every transverse plane of a connection carries net current $I$.
Cauchy--Schwarz over its conducting area, then along the span, gives
$P_lV_l\ge I^2H^2/\sigma$. Summing and applying Cauchy--Schwarz again gives
$P_{\rm total}V_{\rm cond}\ge(N_gIH/\sqrt\sigma)^2$.
Insert the heat allowance. Feed and product storage require respective
heights at least $m\tau/C_{\rm Fe}$ and $m\tau/\rho_{\rm Fe}$; their sum gives
the stated substitution. Branching or detours do not remove the net-plane
current bound within this geometry.
\end{proof}

For illustrative $j=60\,\mathrm{MA/m^2}$, $v=30\,\mu\mathrm V$,
$H=200\,\mu\mathrm m$, $\sigma=10^7\,\mathrm{S/m}$ and $\eta=0.1$, the bound
is about 80 litres of conductor per online square metre when $N_g/N\simeq1$.
It is a specified-geometry lower bound, not a universal no-go for all reactors.
Continuous electrically disconnected product packets might reduce storage
height, but their formation, collection and source isolation are an additional
unproved operation. A $300000$-cell illustrative circuit lowers terminal
current to $200\,\mathrm A$ at $9\,\mathrm V$, while retaining these internal
costs and separate feed/product compartments.

\subsection{Complete energy boundary and a falsifiable milestone}

An illustrative 45\%-Fe hematite-plus-inert-gangue feed at 94\% export recovery
requires $2.364\,\mathrm t$ ore per tonne exported Fe and leaves about
$0.843\,\mathrm t$ gangue. Stoichiometric hydrogen is at least
$54.15\,\mathrm{kg/t}$ if only exported Fe is reduced, and about
$55.87\,\mathrm{kg/t}$ for the declared 97\% metallization.
With the explicit assumed heat capacities and fusion duty in the model,
avoided gangue heating from 890 to $1600^\circ\mathrm C$ plus melting is only
$0.260\,\mathrm{MWh/t\,Fe}$. The optimistic $0.297\,\mathrm{MWh/t}$ active
export electricity already matches that benefit before contacts. Heating ore,
hydrogen generation, gas recycling, membrane manufacture/replacement,
liberation, purity finishing, Fe losses, gangue service and final Fe melting
are additional required costs. This calculation establishes no energy advantage.

The next useful engineering milestone is sustained \emph{feed-derived} atom
admission, rather than another flux extrapolation. On a supported coupon,
isotope-label the oxide Fe, record all seed/collector Fe inventory and meter
all electrical contacts. Controls include bias off/reversed, blocked pores,
preloaded Fe without ore and a prepared pure-Fe source. Require isotope-resolved
export exceeding 100 times accessible preloaded path Fe, repeated replenishment
and harvesting, full Fe/gangue/carbon balance and product impurity analysis.
Then test a declared real untreated fine ore. The 100-fold criterion is a
proposed falsification gate, not a claimed measurement.
If the path only discharges its own Fe, consumes its wall, fails source contact
or co-transports unacceptable impurities, the proposed extraction interface
fails even if nanoscale migration remains real. No such test has been run.
