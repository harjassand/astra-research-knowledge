## Consequences, scaling, and limits

For any strip joining two arbitrary complex profiles in this family,
integrating (1) gives

\[
 W_0(U,V,\tau)-\tfrac12\int|D\phi|^4
 \ge\mathcal P(V)-\mathcal P(U),\qquad\tau>0.
\]

It permits negative transitions. In particular it includes the source's
edge (5/4,2)→(2,5/4), τ=1/2, whose raw action is −1431/1280. It replaces
the finite 81-profile/five-width graph certificate by a continuous one,
and strengthens that family's 1/4 margin to 1/2. The conceptual advance is
the continuous, four-real-dimensional storage inequality, not the constant.
The original finite certificate remains preserved as an earlier exact result.

For any positive integer k, the same assertion holds with e^{±iky} and
potential k³𝒫. Insert kr,ks in (2): L(k r,k s,p,q,t,h) has storage derivative
k³(P_rp+P_sq). Negative k merely exchanges the two carriers and the potential
accordingly. The assertion does not sum across distinct k: their nonlinear
resonances are precisely the missing interaction.

To reach arbitrary profiles one needs a storage functional on complete
transverse profiles controlling all such resonances, or a compatibility-
preserving reduction to already certified families with a justified energy
comparison. The source's finite-difference loop operation supplies neither;
it changes the gradient and can add unbounded connector cost. Density of
Fourier/polygonal profiles does not transfer a one-frequency certificate to
their sums. A failure of a proposed polynomial storage ansatz is likewise
not an exact negative compatible cycle.

The general potential/closed-cycle method is established variational machinery,
as are SOS certificates and Fourier compatibility. This record claims an
explicit internally checked certificate and its stated family consequence;
it does not claim historical priority for the method. The scope is materially
broader than a single fixed carrier or finite graph, but remains far from a
universal quasiconvexity theorem or a civilisation-enabling capability.
