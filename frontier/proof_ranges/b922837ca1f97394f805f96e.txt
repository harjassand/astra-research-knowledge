## 1. Contract and independent premises

Throughout, normalized averages are over the square torus of period 2 pi.
Let

\[
V=D\psi=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\quad
j=ad-bc,
\]
\[
J(V)=J_1(V)=\frac18\big((a-d)^2+(b+c)^2\big)^2+j^2+(a^2+c^2)j.
\]

No coefficient is added to J. All the functional identities below hold for
periodic W^{1,4} maps. Smooth maps suffice for their initial derivation; strong
W^{1,4} approximation passes every cubic and quartic integral to the limit.
There is no orientation, injectivity, sign-of-determinant, Fourier-bandwidth,
or small-amplitude assumption.

The supplied reports were data, not proof premises. Two elementary premises
are reconstructed here.

**Pointwise coercivity.** Put u=a^2+c^2, v=b^2+d^2, and w=ab+cd. Then

\[
J=\frac{(u+v)^2}{8}+\frac32j^2+\frac12(u-v)j,
\quad uv=w^2+j^2.
\]

If S=u+v>0, set x=(u-v)/S and y=2j/S. The constraint gives
x^2+y^2<=1. Hence

\[
\frac{J}{S^2}=\frac18+\frac18(3y^2+2xy)
\ge\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}{16}>0.                 \tag{1}
\]

The last step is the smaller eigenvalue (3-sqrt(13))/2 of the matrix
[[0,1],[1,3]]. Thus J(V)>=((5-sqrt(13))/16)|V|^4.

**Rank-one convexity at the unchanged coefficient.** J is invariant under
left SO(2). It is therefore enough to consider a rank-one direction
e_1 tensor (1,z). Write the background entries as

\[
(a,b,c,d)=\tfrac12(P+X,Y-Q,Y+Q,P-X).
\]

Direct differentiation of J gives the acoustic matrix, in coordinates
(P,Q,X,Y),

\[
G(z)=\begin{pmatrix}
2&-z&-1/2&-z\\
-z&(z^2+1)/2&0&z^2/2\\
-1/2&0&(z^2+1)/2&z\\
-z&z^2/2&z&2z^2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first 2 by 2 block has determinant 1 and is positive definite. Its
Schur complement is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}3(z^2+1)/8&3z/4\\3z/4&3z^2/2\end{pmatrix},
\quad \det=9z^4/16.
\]

Both diagonal entries and the determinant are nonnegative. Thus G(z) is
positive semidefinite for every real z. The direction with first input
coordinate zero follows by continuity and homogeneity. This is an independent
r=1 rank-one-convexity reconstruction. No external claimed planar equivalence
is admitted.

## 2. Separate the exact acoustic-null function

For any periodic phi, define

\[
m(y)=\langle\phi_1(\cdot,y)\rangle_x,
\quad \psi=(\phi_1-m,\phi_2),\quad h(y)=m'(y).
\]

Then <psi_1>_x=0, <h>_y=0, and

\[
D\phi=D\psi+h(y)e_{12}.                              \tag{2}
\]

Conversely, every periodic psi with <psi_1>_x=0 and every mean-zero
h in L^4(y) produce an admissible phi: choose a periodic W^{1,4} primitive
m of h and set phi=(psi_1+m,psi_2). This is an exact decomposition; no
component of the original admissible class is discarded.

For V=Dpsi put

\[
e=2a+d,\quad q=e^2+c^2,
\]
\[
C(V)=a^3+5a^2d-4abc+ac^2+2ad^2-2bcd+c^2d
     =(a^2+c^2)(a+d)+2ej,
\]
\[
A=\langle J(V)\rangle,
\quad C=\langle C(V)\rangle,
\quad Q=\langle q\rangle,
\]
and the following functions of y:

\[
P=\left\langle\frac32(b+c)^2+\frac12(a-d)^2+2c^2\right\rangle_x,
\quad M=\langle ce\rangle_x,
\quad N=\langle\partial_bJ(V)\rangle_x.                 \tag{3}
\]

The cubic source is explicitly

\[
\partial_bJ=
\frac12(b+c)^3+\frac12(a-d)^2(b+c)+2c^2(b+c)
-c(2ad+a^2+3c^2).                                    \tag{4}
\]

The horizontally averaged b and c are zero. Therefore the cubic term in h
in the quartic expansion averages to zero, and direct algebra gives

\[
\langle J(V+h e_{12})\rangle
=A+\left\langle\frac{h^4}{8}+\frac{P h^2}{2}+Nh\right\rangle_y,
\]
\[
\langle C(V+h e_{12})\rangle=C-2\langle Mh\rangle_y,
\quad \langle q(V+h e_{12})\rangle=Q.                 \tag{5}
\]

For completeness, before horizontal averaging the omitted cubic term is
h^3(b+c)/2. This is why subtracting the horizontal mean of the first map
component, rather than simply postulating an arbitrary shear, is necessary.

The full pointwise conformal expansion is

\[
J(sI+V)-J(sI)=J(V)+sC(V)
+s^2\{q(V)+3\det V\}+s^3(5a+3d).                   \tag{6}
\]

The linear derivative means and determinant mean of a compatible periodic
gradient vanish. The determinant mean still vanishes after (2), because
<c>_x=0. Thus the *actual* Jensen energy is exactly

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_\psi(h,s)
=A+\left\langle\frac{h^4}{8}+\frac{Ph^2}{2}+Nh\right\rangle_y
-2s\langle Mh\rangle_y+Cs+Qs^2.}                     \tag{7}
\]

## 3. The inner problem is jointly convex

This is the load-bearing operation. It is stronger than separate convexity
in a rank-one direction. Pointwise in x and y,

\[
\begin{split}
s^2q-2shce+\frac{h^2}{2}
\left\{\frac32(b+c)^2+\frac12(a-d)^2+2c^2\right\}
={}&(se-hc)^2+s^2c^2\\
&+\frac34h^2(b+c)^2+\frac14h^2(a-d)^2.
\end{split}                                         \tag{8}
\]

Consequently

\[
\mathcal G_\psi(h,s)=\left\langle h^4/8+Ph^2/2\right\rangle_y
-2s\langle Mh\rangle_y+Qs^2                         \tag{9}
\]

is nonnegative and convex jointly in the whole function h and the scalar s.
The linear terms Nh and Cs in (7) do not change convexity.

If Q=0, compatibility and <psi_1>_x=0 force V=0. Indeed c=0 implies
psi_2 depends only on y; e=0 and <a>_x=0 then imply d=a=0, and psi_1 is
zero. In this degenerate case (7) is simply <h^4>/8.

For V nonzero, Q>0. The inner objective is strictly convex: distinct h
are separated by the strictly convex fourth-power term, and with h fixed
distinct s are separated by Qs^2. It is coercive. For example,

\[
-2s\langle Mh\rangle
\ge-\frac Q2s^2-\frac2Q\|M\|_{4/3}^2\|h\|_4^2;
\]

the fourth-power term absorbs the last term and the linear sources. The
direct method on L^4_0(y) times R gives a unique global minimizer. Here
P is in L^2, N in L^{4/3}, and M in L^2, so all terms are defined. This
argument also establishes existence in the Sobolev class, beyond smooth
profiles.

## 4. Exact convex-conjugate criterion, including all scalar backgrounds

Define

\[
g_P(t)=t^4/8+Pt^2/2,
\quad R_P(z)=\text{the unique real root of }t^3/2+Pt=z.
\]

P>=0, so R_P is single valued and increasing. Its convex conjugate is

\[
g_P^*(z)=\frac38R_P(z)^4+\frac P2R_P(z)^2.             \tag{10}
\]

An optional closed expression is

\[
R_P(z)=\operatorname{cbrt}\left(z+\sqrt{z^2+(2P/3)^3}\right)
+\operatorname{cbrt}\left(z-\sqrt{z^2+(2P/3)^3}\right),
\]

where real cube roots are meant. The implicit monotone-root definition is
preferable for both exact arguments and numerical stability.

For each fixed s the unique optimal shear is

\[
h_s(y)=R_{P(y)}\big(\lambda_s-N(y)+2sM(y)\big),
\quad\langle h_s\rangle_y=0.                         \tag{11}
\]

The mean-zero condition determines the single real multiplier lambda_s.
Equivalently, lambda_s minimizes
<g_P^*(lambda-N+2sM)>_y. Thus

\[
\inf_{\langle h\rangle=0}\mathcal E_\psi(h,s)
=A+Cs+Qs^2-\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}
\langle g_P^*(\lambda-N+2sM)\rangle_y.                \tag{12}
\]

The simultaneous optimum in h and s is characterized by only two scalar
closure equations:

\[
\boxed{
h=R_P(\lambda-N+2sM),\qquad
\langle h\rangle=0,\qquad
2Qs+C-2\langle Mh\rangle=0.}                         \tag{13}
\]

These equations determine the unique global optimum, not an arbitrarily
selected stationary point. The original unknown shear function is recovered
pointwise from the scalar inverse R_P. The outer class of psi is still
infinite dimensional. Computing its one-dimensional integrals or proving
uniform inequalities for them is not a free finite calculation.

The completely optimized defect has a useful operator form. On mean-zero
h define

\[
\mathcal T_\psi(h)=\left\langle h^4/8+Ph^2/2\right\rangle
-\frac{\langle Mh\rangle^2}{Q},
\quad L=N+\frac CQM.
\]

The quadratic part is nonnegative by (8), so T_psi is strictly convex. Its
quadratic operator is multiplication by P minus the rank-one operator

\[
\boxed{\mathcal K_\psi h=Ph-\frac2Q M\langle Mh\rangle.} \tag{14}
\]

Eliminating s in (7), then taking the conjugate on the mean-zero space, gives

\[
\boxed{\Delta(\psi)=\inf_{h,s}\mathcal E_\psi(h,s)
=A-\frac{C^2}{4Q}-\mathcal T_\psi^*(-L).}             \tag{15}
\]

The central unproved source inequality is now precisely

\[
\boxed{A\ge\frac{C^2}{4Q}+\mathcal T_\psi^*
\left(-N-\frac CQM\right)
\quad\text{for every compatible psi with }\langle\psi_1\rangle_x=0.} \tag{16}
\]

**Equivalence and counterexample reconstruction.** Equation (16) is
equivalent to quasiconvexity of J at I, not just a sufficient test. Equations
(2) and (7) cover every original map, and (15) optimizes its omitted acoustic
component and affine scalar globally. Quasiconvexity at I implies the
inequality at every sI by quartic homogeneity, since for s nonzero

\[
J(sI+D\phi)-J(sI)=s^4\{J(I+D(\phi/s))-J(I)\}.
\]

The s=0 case follows from (1). Conversely, if Delta(psi)<0, solve (13),
integrate the resulting mean-zero h, and use (2). The optimizing s cannot
be zero, because (1) makes every zero-background energy nonnegative.
Rescaling the resulting phi by 1/s gives a compatible negative Jensen map
at I. A negative certified value of (15) therefore has the exact required
variational meaning.

The use of a square torus does not weaken the all-domain question. A
zero-boundary W^{1,4} map on a bounded Lipschitz domain can be extended by
zero, isotropically rescaled with its amplitude to preserve its gradient,
and placed inside a torus cell. In the reverse direction, tile a smooth
periodic map on a large square and cut it off in a boundary strip; the
quartic boundary cost is subleading compared with the area. Then use
density. These usual localization steps preserve the fixed affine
background and the integrand coefficients.

No proof of (16) has been obtained. A mere pointwise supporting inequality
cannot establish it: the missing information lies in the compatible outer
sources C and N, and their relation to the variable positive operator K.

## 5. A symmetry class is excluded, but the surviving obstruction is explicit

If V(x+pi,y)=-V(x,y), all cubic horizontal averages vanish: C=N=0.
Equations (7)-(8) then give

\[
\mathcal E_\psi(h,s)=A+\langle h^4/8\rangle
+\left\langle(se-hc)^2+s^2c^2
+3h^2(b+c)^2/4+h^2(a-d)^2/4\right\rangle\ge0.         \tag{17}
\]

Thus an arbitrary acoustic shear combined with any collection of odd
horizontal harmonics cannot violate the conformal inequality. This is a
no-go result for that microstructure architecture, not the main advance.
An interacting candidate needs nonzero cubic source moments in the
remaining gradient.

A much broader compatible architecture can also be excluded analytically.
Let F and G be arbitrary smooth periodic real functions, with <F>=0, and set

\[
\psi(x,y)=\left(-\tfrac12G'(y)F(x),\ G(y)F'(x)\right).       \tag{18}
\]

Its gradient entries are

\[
a=-G'F'/2,\quad b=-G''F/2,\quad c=GF'',\quad d=G'F'.
\]

Thus e=0 and M=0 identically. Direct integration by parts gives

\[
C=\frac18\langle(F')^3\rangle_x\langle(G')^3\rangle_y
 =2\langle abc\rangle=2\langle a(b+h)c\rangle               \tag{19}
\]

for every h(y). Indeed <G'G^2>=<F'F''>=0,
<FF'F''>=-<(F')^3>/2, and <GG'G''>=-<(G')^3>/2.
The arbitrary shear does not change the last integral.

On the whole pointwise constraint d=-2a, one has the exact SOS

\[
\begin{split}
8\{J(a,b,c,-2a)-\tfrac94a^2b^2\}={}&
97\left(a^2+\frac{30}{97}bc\right)^2+2a^2c^2\\
&+(b^2+2bc-c^2)^2+\frac{264}{97}b^2c^2.                 \tag{20}
\end{split}
\]

Consequently, writing A(h)=<J(V+h e_12)> and Q=<c^2>, Cauchy-Schwarz
and (19)-(20) give

\[
C^2\le4\langle a^2(b+h)^2\rangle Q\le\frac{16}{9}A(h)Q.
\]

Thus for every scalar background and every admissible shear,

\[
\mathcal E_\psi(h,s)\ge A(h)-C^2/(4Q)\ge\frac59A(h)\ge0.    \tag{21}
\]

No Fourier restriction or parity of G is required. This excludes the entire
separable divergence-free potential architecture, including its nonlinear
optimal shear, with a strict margin. It does not exclude nonseparable
potentials.

### The surviving scalar-Hessian obstruction

The full e=0 compatible subspace has the representation

\[
\psi=(-\theta_y/2,\theta_x),\qquad
 a=-\theta_{xy}/2,\ b=-\theta_{yy}/2,\ c=\theta_{xx},\ d=\theta_{xy}.
                                                               \tag{22}
\]

Constants of the map do not affect the energy. The horizontal-mean part of
theta can be removed and represented by the already eliminated h(y)e_12,
so <theta>_x=0 can be imposed. The potential theta may now be fully
nonseparable. The conformal source is

\[
C_\theta=\left\langle\theta_{xy}^3/8+
                         \theta_{xy}\theta_{xx}^2/2\right\rangle,
\quad Q_\theta=\langle\theta_{xx}^2\rangle.             \tag{23}
\]

The variable N_theta and P_theta are still exactly (3)-(4), evaluated at
(22), and M_theta=0. The complete nonlinear-shear and scalar-background
minimum is therefore

\[
\boxed{\Delta_\theta=A_\theta-C_\theta^2/(4Q_\theta)
-\inf_\lambda\langle g_{P_\theta}^*(\lambda-N_\theta)\rangle_y.} \tag{24}
\]

The minimizing shear is h=R_P(lambda-N), with its required mean-zero
constraint; s=-C/(2Q). For arbitrary nonseparable theta, the inequality
Delta_theta>=0 is unproved. It is a precise scalar-potential subproblem of
the full source inequality (16), with an explicit construction of any
violating map. The pointwise SOS (20) still holds, but the identity (19)
that connects it to C no longer holds. The exact unaccounted cubic source is

\[
C_\theta-2\langle abc\rangle
=\frac18\left\langle
\theta_{xy}^3+4\theta_{xy}\theta_{xx}^2
-4\theta_{xx}\theta_{xy}\theta_{yy}\right\rangle.       \tag{25}
\]

This quantity is not a null Lagrangian. It is the explicit compatibility
obligation left by the separable proof, not an arbitrary matrix-law defect.

### A nonseparable exact fixture: active mean constraint and failed cancellation

Take the genuinely compatible three-wave potential

\[
\theta=U\cos x+V\cos(x+y)+T\cos(2x+y).
\]

Here

\[
C=-3TUV/2,\quad 2\langle abc\rangle=-3TUV/8,
\quad Q=(16T^2+U^2+V^2)/2.
\]

Thus C=8<abc>, rather than 2<abc>. This explicitly disproves the proposed
extension of the separable cubic cancellation to arbitrary Hessian
potentials. Its full one-dimensional reduction is particularly concrete:

\[
P=P_0+P_1\cos y,\quad N=N_0+N_1\cos y,
\]
\[
P_0=\{439T^2+28(U^2+V^2)\}/16,\quad P_1=11UV/4,
\]
\[
N_0=85TUV/16,\quad N_1=T(31U^2+30V^2)/16,
\]
\[
1024A=21675T^4+4016T^2U^2+9616T^2V^2
       +48U^4+512U^2V^2+300V^4.                       \tag{26}
\]

The exact optimum is

\[
h(y)=R_{P_0+P_1\cos y}(\ell-N_1\cos y),\quad
\langle h\rangle=0,\quad s=3TUV/\{2(16T^2+U^2+V^2)\}.
\]

The multiplier is lambda=N_0+ell. If P_1N_1 is nonzero, ell is not zero.
For example, if both are positive, pair points with cos y=z and cos y=-z.
At ell=0 the positive root occurs with the smaller P and has greater
magnitude than the negative root; their sum is positive. Thus <h>>0 and
ell must be negative. Merely subtracting the mean of N would violate the
original gradient's mean-zero constraint. The unique-root shear generally
has infinitely many vertical harmonics even though theta has only three

This fixture itself is analytically positive, and is not left disguised as
an unchecked counterexample. Define

\[
B=U^2/2+9V^2/8+21T^2/2,\quad\kappa=(5-\sqrt{13})/16.

Pointwise coercivity and Jensen give min_h A(h)>=kappa B^2, since

\[
C^2/(4Q)=\frac{9T^2U^2V^2}{8(16T^2+U^2+V^2)}
\le\frac3{139}B^2.

To verify the latter bound, put x=U^2, y=V^2, z=T^2. All coefficients of

\[
\boxed{\Delta_\theta\ge(\kappa-3/139)B^2>0}

for nonzero (U,V,T). Positivity follows already from sqrt(13)<4 and

## 6. The opposite determinant-sign contact

Let D=diag(1,-1). Its acoustic-null shear is e_21. Subtract the vertical
mean of phi_2 and write Dphi=Dpsi+h(x)e_21 with <h>_x=0. Define

\[
f=a-2d,\quad Q_D=\langle f^2+b^2\rangle,
\quad C_D=\langle DJ(V):D\rangle,
\]
\[
P_D=\langle(a+d)^2/2+3(c-b)^2/2+2b^2\rangle_y,
\quad M_D=-\langle bf\rangle_y,
\quad N_D=\langle\partial_cJ(V)\rangle_y.
\]

The counterpart of (7) has exactly the same coefficients and signs after
these replacements. Its pointwise positive quadratic identity is

\[
s^2(f^2+b^2)+2shbf+h^2\{(a+d)^2/4+3(c-b)^2/4+b^2\}
=(sf+hb)^2+s^2b^2+3h^2(c-b)^2/4+h^2(a+d)^2/4.        \tag{24}
\]

Its cubic h^3 coefficient is (c-b)/2, whose vertical average is zero.
The pointwise expansion at sD has quadratic term
Q_D(V)-3 det V and linear term s^3(3a-5d). Consequently every existence,
conjugate, mean-zero, scalar-optimization, and reconstruction argument in
Sections 2-4 applies. This operation handles the two opposite-sign contact
geometries separately. It does not assert that their remaining source
inequalities hold, or that either conformal family controls every full-rank
background.

## 7. A decisive limitation: the two axis eliminations do not combine convexly

One cannot simply declare both acoustic shears part of a single convex inner
problem. Consider the genuinely compatible map

\[
\phi=(H\sin y,K\sin x).
\]

At zero background its exact energy is

\[
\langle J(D\phi)\rangle
=\frac3{64}(H^4+K^4)+\frac7{16}H^2K^2.              \tag{25}
\]

At H=K=1 the Hessian in (H,K) is
[[23/16,7/4],[7/4,23/16]], with eigenvalue -5/16 in direction (1,-1).
Thus the two-shear optimization is not jointly convex even in this smallest
compatible example. The energy itself is positive. This is a failure of
the proposed iterative convex-operation extension, not a quasiconvexity
counterexample.
