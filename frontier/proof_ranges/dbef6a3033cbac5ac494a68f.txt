## Outcome

The decisive target I pursued was **a polynomial-time relative-approximation algorithm for weighted perfect matchings in general graphs**.

**I did not establish that algorithm, or another breakthrough meeting your completion standard.** The investigation produced an exact positive representation with implementable conditional operations, then exposed why those operations do not combine into an efficient global algorithm. I also pursued global alternating-cycle updates rather than stopping at the first obstruction.

The surviving mathematics is below. I have not established external priority for these identities and obstructions, so I am not presenting them as new discoveries merely because I derived them here.

## 1. Target selection and the actual computational task

I initially considered sparse linear-system solving, deterministic parallel matching, and approximate counting. The numerical route did not produce a mechanism that removed the cost of constructing a suitable preconditioner. The literature check also mattered: the bipartite matching problem already has a deterministic NC result reported in 2026, so treating that special case as an untouched target would have been mistaken. [ECCC](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/119/)

My first concrete counting mechanism concerned intersecting two matroids. That target also had to change: Chen and Liu’s preprint, submitted **5 October 2026**, already states an FPRAS for counting common bases from independence oracles, using softened intersection, a Poincaré inequality, and external-field optimisation. That is prior work, not an outcome of this investigation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06724)

The remaining target was the following.

Given an explicitly represented graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(|V|=2n\) and nonnegative rational edge weights \(w_e\), compute

\[
Z_G=\sum_{M\in\mathcal M(G)}w(M),
\qquad
w(M)=\prod_{e\in M}w_e,
\]

where \(\mathcal M(G)\) is its set of perfect matchings.

The desired output is \(\widehat Z\) satisfying

\[
\Pr\!\left[(1-\varepsilon)Z_G
\le \widehat Z\le
(1+\varepsilon)Z_G\right]\ge 1-\delta
\]

in time polynomial in the explicit input length, \(\varepsilon^{-1}\), and \(\log \delta^{-1}\).

A corresponding sampler should output an actual perfect matching, with distribution close in total variation to

\[
\pi_G(M)=\frac{w(M)}{Z_G}.
\]

The bipartite version has an established FPRAS. That supplies a genuine computational component—not an assumed counting oracle for the original general-graph problem. [ACM Digital Library](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/1008731.1008738)
