## 1. Setup, provenance, and contract

All Hilbert spaces are separable and complex. All operators appearing before a compactness conclusion are bounded. The symbol `<=` in operator formulas means the Loewner order. For `a>0`, set

\[
 g_a(t)=\frac{t}{1+at},\qquad t\ge0,
 \qquad
 f_a(t)=\frac{t}{1-at},\qquad 0\le t<a^{-1}.
\]

These functions are applied to self-adjoint operators by continuous functional calculus. The identity

\[
g_a(X)=a^{-1}\big[I-(I+aX)^{-1}\big]
\]

makes its order monotonicity immediate.

The starting single-operator comparison is

\[
 A=T^*T\preceq g_a(TT^*+BB^*)=g_a(C+P),
 \quad C=TT^*,\quad P=BB^* . \tag{1.1}
\]

A comparison of exactly this form occurs in Balasubramanian–Kasiviswanathan, arXiv:2610.07728v2, Section 7, Lemma 7.1 [1]. Their source-response/spectral-radius argument is **not** claimed as a contribution here. Their claimed KLS theorem is not a premise of any proof in this note and was not independently validated in this run.

The candidate contribution is the collection of statements below, with their exact scope. It is not a claim that the component ideas of operator monotonicity, spectral interlacing, trace concavity, fixed-point iteration, weighted shifts, or displacement structure are new. In particular, classical matrix subadditivity [2] and existing displacement-rank/singular-value analysis [3] are relevant prior art. A limited search is not historical-priority clearance.

**Input/output contract for the constructive part.** Given `a`, finitely many bounded operators `V_i` satisfying `sum_i V_i V_i^* <= I`, and a bounded source `B`, construct the unique positive solution of a specified operator recursion, with norm error at most `epsilon`. The theorem supplies an iteration count and a representation-space bound. It does not promise inexpensive application of arbitrary input operators, inexpensive word-space Gram matrices, or a small representation when the word space grows rapidly.
