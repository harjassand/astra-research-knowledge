## Status and scope

The full implication from planar rank-one convexity to quasiconvexity is **not established here**. No compatible negative quasiconvexity gap is constructed.

This investigation proves a global Jensen inequality for an infinite-dimensional family of internally resonant gradients: arbitrary real scalar amplitudes multiplying a periodically rotating target vector, and more generally a convex-loop carrier. The main positive identity holds for every C² rank-one-convex integrand on R^(m x n), without homogeneity or rotational symmetry. For planar left-SO(2)-invariant quartics it yields an explicit fourth-order coercivity bound, uniform over all affine backgrounds. Neither a near-line condition nor a bounded-background condition is imposed.

A second theorem gives an exact assembly rule, after complete affine optimization, for Fourier packets independent through degree four. Its condition preserves all internal packet resonances. A separate compatible example proves that unrestricted assembly fails: even a polyconvex energy has a strictly negative interaction increment. A variable-phase identity identifies the signed term absent from the successful modulation theorem.

These statements have analytic proofs below. `verify_global_compatibility.py` checks nine groups of finite algebraic identities and exact examples. It does not certify the analytic proofs, establish historical priority, or formally verify them in a proof assistant. The earlier tangent verifier was replayed (12 groups passed). The new main theorem does **not** use the earlier contact classification, Hodge estimate, or near-line theorem as premises.

The techniques used here—rank-one supporting inequalities, integration by parts, convexity, Fourier moment matching, and quadratic minimization—are established mathematical tools. Their use here supplies the stated constructions; no claim of historical invention of those tools, or of priority for the complete combination, is made.
