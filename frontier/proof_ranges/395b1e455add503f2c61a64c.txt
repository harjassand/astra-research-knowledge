# Ordinary-bit PSPACE terminal-gap theorem

Status: complete internal complexity argument, conditional only on the
finite-population approximation proved in `FINITE_POPULATION_COMPILER.md`.
That approximation's flow rounding and exact marginal repair are reconstructed
there. This result concerns the numerical decision/approximation capability;
it makes no polynomial-space claim for the explicit random-access sampler DAG.
No lower bound or historical novelty is claimed.

## Exact promise problems

An input contains an explicitly listed total deterministic finite automaton
`f:S x A -> S`, its start state `s0`, rational probability laws `mu` on `A`
and `nu` on `S`, and rational numbers `a>=0` and `gamma>0`. All integers,
numerators, denominators, and table indices have ordinary binary encodings.
The complete transition table is supplied and read; there is no table,
adjacency, coupling, or optimization oracle. Remove zero-mass letters exactly.
Let `d=|S|`, `m=|supp(mu)|`, and `alpha=min_{mu(a)>0}mu(a)>0`.

Two variants are considered:

1. **Binary fixed horizon.** The input also supplies a positive integer `n` in
   binary. Let `R_n` be the terminal laws from length-`n` joint letter laws with
   `X_t~mu` at every coordinate. Put `v=inf_{p in R_n}TV(nu,p)`.
2. **Arbitrary positive horizon.** No horizon is supplied. Put
   `v=inf_{n>=1,p in R_n}TV(nu,p)`.

The promise is either `YES: v<=a` or `NO: v>=a+gamma`. A deciding machine must
answer accordingly on these promised inputs; it may answer either way in the
intervening gap. There is no exact cutpoint, exact closure-membership, or
existence-of-an-exact-optimal-finite-horizon claim.

**Theorem. Both promise problems have deterministic polynomial-space
algorithms in the total ordinary binary input length.** In either variant,
given rational `epsilon>0` in binary, a rational approximation to `v` with
absolute error at most `epsilon` can also be output using polynomial work
space and polynomially many output bits. Runtime is not asserted polynomial.

## Precision and graph acquisition

Choose the integer

    P = ceil(4d/(alpha*gamma)).

The finite-population graph consists of histograms `x in N^d`, `sum_s x_s=P`.
An arc exists exactly when a nonnegative integer allocation `k_{s,b}` satisfies

    sum_b k_{s,b}=x_s,
    y_u=sum_{s,b:f(s,b)=u}k_{s,b},
    |sum_s k_{s,b}-P*mu(b)|<=d     for every positive-support letter b.

It has `H=binom(P+d-1,d-1)` vertices. The entire graph need not be stored.

Let `L_mu,L_gamma` denote the encoding bits of the relevant rational data.
A positive supplied rational has numerator at least one. Consequently

    alpha >= 2^(-O(L_mu)),    gamma >= 2^(-O(L_gamma)),
    log2(P+1) = O(log(d+1)+L_mu+L_gamma).

The ceiling is computed by integer multiplication and division on these
polynomial-bit operands. There is no assumption that `gamma` or `alpha` is
inverse-polynomial, and neither is treated as a unit-cost real. Exponentially
small rational gaps produce exponentially large populations whose binary
representations still have polynomial length.

Write `b=ceil(log2(P+1))`. A histogram occupies `O(d*b)` bits and a complete
allocation `O(d*m*b)` bits. For `mu(b)=r_b/s_b`, its budget is tested as the
integer inequality

    |s_b*sum_s k_{s,b}-P*r_b| <= d*s_b.

Every operand has polynomially many input bits. Nonnegative integer allocations
can be enumerated as weak compositions of `P` into `d*m` cells using
`O(d*m*b)` bits, or nondeterministically guessed in that workspace. Their
source and destination histograms are then computed by additions and table
lookups. This supplies adjacency with full input acquisition accounted for;
it is not an oracle.

The vertex count is also an explicitly computable polynomial-bit integer:

    log2 H <= O(d*log2(P+d)).

Compute `H` successively from `binom(P+j,j)` for `j=1,...,d-1`, multiplying
by `P+j` and dividing by `j`. Every division is exact and every intermediate
integer has `O(log H+log(P+d))` bits. Since the state/alphabet lists and table
are explicit, `d,m` are bounded by the input length.

## Exact objective comparisons

Let `V` be the least common multiple of the denominators of `nu`, and set
`w_s=V*nu(s)`. Its binary length is at most the sum of those denominator
lengths, hence polynomial in the input. For any histogram,

    TV(nu,x/P) = T(x)/(2*P*V),
    T(x)=sum_s |P*w_s - V*x_s|.

The numerator is between zero and `2PV`; the denominator and numerator have
`O(log P+log V)` bits. Comparison with the rational threshold
`c=a+gamma/2` is an exact cross-multiplication of polynomial-bit integers.
No floating-point precision or comparison oracle is used.

## Nondeterministic polynomial-space reachability

For the fixed horizon, store the current histogram, one allocation, and a
binary counter. Starting at `P delta_s0`, perform exactly `n` transitions,
guessing and validating an allocation at each step. At the final step accept
if `T(x)/(2PV)<c`. Every branch halts after exactly `n` transitions, and uses

    O(d*m*b + log n + inputbits)

work bits, up to ordinary arithmetic workspace of polynomial size.

For arbitrary positive horizon, use a counter up to `H` and test the objective
after each of steps `1,...,H`. A reachable final histogram distinct from the
initial one has a simple path of length at most `H-1`. A positive return to
the initial histogram has a nonempty cycle of length at most `H`. Thus a
successful positive-length path exists if and only if this bounded procedure
has an accepting branch. It never accepts at step zero. Its work space is

    O(d*m*b + log H + inputbits),

again polynomial. The nondeterminism guesses mathematical allocations; it is
not a probabilistic algorithm and does not presume a source of exact random
bits. NPSPACE=PSPACE gives deterministic polynomial-space deciders.

One can see the deterministic space bound directly, without invoking an
unimplemented adjacency routine. For exact `n`, recursively test

    E(x,y,l) = OR_z [E(x,z,floor(l/2)) AND E(z,y,ceil(l/2))],

with equality at `l=0` and the explicit allocation enumeration at `l=1`.
Enumerate the `H` midpoint histograms in polynomial workspace. Recursion
depth is `O(log n)`; retaining its histogram and length records costs
`O((d*b+log n)*log n)` bits, plus the reusable adjacency workspace. For
unbounded positive reachability, add one dummy start whose outgoing arcs are
those of the initial histogram, with no incoming arcs, and apply ordinary
bounded-length recursive reachability to this `H+1` vertex graph. The space
is polynomial in `d*b,log H,inputbits`. This is a fully supplied integer
algorithm in both variants.

## Why the graph decision answers the promised original problem

Let `v_P` be the minimum of `TV(nu,x/P)` over exactly length-`n` reachable
histograms, or over positive-length reachable histograms in the unbounded
variant. The approximation theorem gives

    v-beta <= v_P <= v+r,
    r=d/(2P),       beta=d/(P*alpha+d).

This follows from whole-flow rounding `R -> G` with radius `r` and
exact-marginal repair `G -> R` with
radius `beta`; it does not assume a symmetric radius `r`. It remains valid
for an unattained unbounded infimum by applying the inequalities to an
arbitrarily near-optimal terminal law and taking the infimum.

The chosen population satisfies

    r <= alpha*gamma/8 <= gamma/8,
    beta < d/(P*alpha) <= gamma/4.

Therefore

    v<=a        implies v_P<=a+r<a+gamma/2,
    v>=a+gamma  implies v_P>=a+gamma-beta>a+gamma/2.

The nondeterministic machines above accept exactly when some admissible
histogram has objective below `a+gamma/2`, equivalently `v_P<a+gamma/2`.
Their deterministic simulations therefore decide the promised original
problem. This finishes the ordinary-bit PSPACE proof.

## Polynomial-space numerical output

There are two complete constructions.

First, set `P=ceil(2d/(alpha*epsilon))`. Enumerate all terminal histograms,
decide reachability in polynomial space as above, and retain the smallest
integer numerator `T(x)` among reachable histograms. Its common denominator
is `2PV`, so this yields the **exact** finite-graph optimum `v_P` with
polynomial-bit output. The interval

    [max(0,v_P-r), min(1,v_P+beta)]

contains `v` and has width at most `r+beta<=3epsilon/4`. Output its midpoint.
All interval arithmetic has polynomial-bit operands. Reachability calls reuse
the same work tape; enumeration time can be enormous without increasing
space. Nonemptiness is assured because the product input law exists, or by
rounding its layered flow. This numerical algorithm need not construct a
single sampler permutation or store a witness DAG.

Second, a promise-gap decider alone supports an ordinary binary search with
overlapping safe updates. For `0<epsilon<1`, maintain `l<=v<=u`, initially
`[0,1]`. Fix `gamma=epsilon/4`. While `u-l>epsilon`, put

    a=(l+u)/2-gamma/2.

Ask the gap decider. A YES answer implies `v<a+gamma`, since the decider
must answer NO whenever `v>=a+gamma`; update `u=min(u,a+gamma)`. A NO answer
implies `v>a`, since it must answer YES whenever `v<=a`; update
`l=max(l,a)`. The gap's arbitrary behavior is harmless. Both updates preserve
the invariant and reduce width to at most `(u-l)/2+gamma/2`. After
`O(log(1/epsilon))` iterations the width is at most `epsilon`. The midpoint
has the required error. When a loop runs, `u-l>epsilon>gamma`, so its `a`
is nonnegative. Rational endpoints have only polynomially many bits: each
step adds one dyadic division and the fixed rational `gamma`. If `epsilon>=1`,
the trivial output zero suffices. A binary rational `epsilon>0` has
`log(1/epsilon)` bounded by its denominator encoding length. Reusing the
decider's workspace makes the whole approximation polynomial-space.

## Hardness assessment

There is no completed PSPACE-hardness reduction here. The exponential number
of implicit histogram vertices alone does not establish hardness of this
particular graph family. Two immediate conventional routes are unsound:

* A probabilistic-automaton control word must be independent of transition
  coins. This model constrains only one-coordinate marginals and permits the
  future letters to be correlated with earlier branching letters. Importing
  such a reduction silently removes an essential independence condition.
* An intersection-of-DFAs reduction requires all branches to read the same
  word. Conditional row allocations here can feed different words to
  different state populations. For example, two components accepting only
  `a` and only `b` respectively have empty length-one intersection. A half-half
  component mixture can nevertheless feed `a` to the first and `b` to the
  second with overall letter marginal `(1/2,1/2)` and acceptance one. A first
  marginally uniform branching letter produces this component mixture from
  a deterministic start, and a perfectly correlated second letter supplies
  the two different controls. The common-word reduction has failed.

A bounded Petri-net/population reachability reduction would additionally have
to enforce synchronized legal machine steps, prevent conditional row choices
from cheating, and retain a polynomially represented positive terminal gap
despite the additive `d` quota allowance. None of these obligations is solved
by the present graph construction. Thus **PSPACE upper bound: proved
internally; PSPACE hardness/completeness: UNKNOWN.**
