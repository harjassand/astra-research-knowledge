## 3. Completed rank-preserving block identity

All identities also apply when the remaining physical dimensions are unequal. Write
\(H=\bigotimes_{i=1}^m\mathbb C^{d_i}\), and define \(q_{\boldsymbol d}\) as in (1). Empty products mean \(H=\mathbb C\) and \(q_\varnothing(z)=|z|^2\).

Let

\[
b_{\boldsymbol d}(A,B)=\sum_{S\subseteq[m]}(-1/2)^{|S|}
\operatorname{Tr}\big[(\operatorname{Tr}_S A)^*\operatorname{Tr}_S B\big],
\]

so \(q_{\boldsymbol d}(A)=b_{\boldsymbol d}(A,A)\). Let \(F_i\) exchange the two copies of the \(i\)-th factor of \(H\otimes H\), and set

\[
R_{\boldsymbol d}=\prod_{i=1}^m(I-\tfrac12F_i).
\tag{3}
\]

These commuting factors are positive definite; each has eigenvalues among \(1/2,3/2\). In particular, \(R_{\boldsymbol d}\succeq2^{-m}I\).

### Lemma: crossed swap contraction

For arbitrary \(u,v,x,y\in H\),

\[
b_{\boldsymbol d}(uv^*,xy^*)
=\langle u\otimes y,R_{\boldsymbol d}(x\otimes v)\rangle.
\tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** For each subset \(S\), directly contracting the row and column indices yields

\[
\operatorname{Tr}\big[(\operatorname{Tr}_Suv^*)^*\operatorname{Tr}_Sxy^*\big]
=\langle u\otimes y,F_S(x\otimes v)\rangle,
\qquad F_S=\prod_{i\in S}F_i.
\]

For example, on a traced single slot the contraction is
\(\langle u,v\rangle\langle y,x\rangle\); on an untraced slot it is
\(\langle u,x\rangle\langle y,v\rangle\). The same index matching works for entangled vectors across the remaining slots; no factorization of these vectors is assumed. Summing over \(S\) proves (4). In particular,

\[
q_{\boldsymbol d}(xv^*)=\langle x\otimes v,R_{\boldsymbol d}(x\otimes v)\rangle\ge0.
\tag{5}
\]

### Theorem: qutrit lifting identity

For arbitrary \(u,v,x_0,x_1,y_0,y_1\in H\) and real \(\varepsilon\), put

\[
C_\varepsilon=\sum_{a=0}^1
(e_a\otimes u+\varepsilon e_2\otimes x_a)
(e_a\otimes v+\varepsilon e_2\otimes y_a)^*,
\qquad D=x_0y_0^*+x_1y_1^*.
\tag{6}
\]

Both matrices have rank at most two. Exactly,

\[
\boxed{
q_{(3,\boldsymbol d)}(C_\varepsilon)
=\varepsilon^2\sum_{a=0}^1
\big\|R_{\boldsymbol d}^{1/2}(x_a\otimes v-u\otimes y_a)\big\|^2
+\frac{\varepsilon^4}{2}q_{\boldsymbol d}(D).
}
\tag{7}
\]

**Proof.** For any matrix with first-factor blocks \(C_{ij}\), separating the traced and untraced first slot in (1) gives

\[
q_{(d,\boldsymbol d)}(C)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}q_{\boldsymbol d}(C_{ij})
-\frac12q_{\boldsymbol d}\left(\sum_iC_{ii}\right).
\tag{8}
\]

The matrix in (6) has blocks

\[
C_\varepsilon=
\begin{pmatrix}
uv^*&0&\varepsilon uy_0^*\\
0&uv^*&\varepsilon uy_1^*\\
\varepsilon x_0v^*&\varepsilon x_1v^*&\varepsilon^2D
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Writing \(A=uv^*\), its first-slot trace is \(2A+\varepsilon^2D\). Expanding (8) cancels the constant terms and leaves

\[
\varepsilon^2\sum_a\big[q(x_av^*)+q(uy_a^*)-2\operatorname{Re}b(A,x_ay_a^*)\big]
+\tfrac12\varepsilon^4q(D).
\]

Equations (4) and (5) turn each bracket into the squared norm in (7). This proves the identity for arbitrary complex vectors, without a lower-copy positivity premise. □

### Exact consequences and limits

The coefficient of \(\varepsilon^2\) is nonnegative for every number of remaining sites. This is the Hessian coefficient only along the displayed transverse family; it is not a statement about every tangent direction of every rank-two matrix.

If \(u,v\ne0\) and this coefficient vanishes, positive definiteness of \(R\) implies
\(x_a\otimes v=u\otimes y_a\) for each \(a\). Equality of these simple tensors gives \(x_a=c_au\), \(y_a=c_av\), with the same complex scalar. Hence
\(D=(|c_0|^2+|c_1|^2)uv^*\) has rank at most one and (7) is nonnegative for all \(\varepsilon\).

If \(q_{(3,\boldsymbol d)}(C_\varepsilon)<0\), then \(\varepsilon\ne0\) and necessarily \(q_{\boldsymbol d}(D)<0\). Therefore, a counterexample at the smallest possible copy count cannot belong to this entire lifting family. This is a restricted minimal-counterexample exclusion, not an exclusion of all counterexamples.

The missing coverage is substantive: arbitrary pairs of vectors in \((\mathbb C^3)^{\otimes n}\) do not possess the repeated upper-block structure in (6). No transformation preserving (1) that imposes this structure on an arbitrary rank-two matrix was proved. Also, the sign of \(q_{\boldsymbol d}(D)\) for general rank-two \(D\) remains precisely a lower-copy instance of the original problem.
