4. The microscopic mechanism tested
I then pursued a mechanism whose isotope dependence could be derived rather than inserted as a fitted number.
The candidate was nuclear-spin-dependent radical-pair chemistry. Such isotope effects have theoretical precedents; they are not new physics invented in this session. Wiley Online Library
The specific model assumes two electron spins initially in a singlet, one nitrogen nucleus, zero magnetic field, and an isotropic coupling
\[
H=a\,\mathbf I\cdot\mathbf S_1.
\]
Singlet and triplet states leave through separate reaction channels with rates \(k_S,k_T\).
These assumptions deliberately exclude other nuclear couplings, exchange interactions, spin relaxation and additional reaction pathways. None of those exclusions has been established for a glutamate reaction in Bennu.
Exact quantum calculation
Conservation of total angular momentum reduces the populated sector to
\[
H_I=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&v\\
v&-a/2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
v=\frac{a\sqrt{I(I+1)}}2.
\]
Integrating the density-matrix equations gives the singlet product yield
\[
Y_S=
\frac{k_S(k_T+w)}
{k_Sk_T+w(k_S+k_T)},
\]
where
\[
w=\frac{2v^2A}{A^2+a^2/4},
\qquad
A=\frac{k_S+k_T}{2}.
\]
For the two nitrogen isotopes and this restricted model, I derived
\[
\boxed{\frac{Y_{15}}{Y_{14}}\leq\frac{14}{9}.}
\]
The report contains the algebraic certificate: after clearing positive denominators, the inequality becomes a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients. Full density-matrix calculations independently checked the reduced calculation in five parameter cases, with maximum absolute disagreement approximately \(5.6\times10^{-16}\).
That agreement verifies those internal calculations. It does not verify that the assumed reaction occurs.
Connecting spin yields to surviving molecules
A spin-dependent yield alone is not a chemical explanation. I therefore explicitly modelled what happens to the molecule: a singlet event returns glutamate with randomized handedness; a triplet event destroys it.
Under those additional assumptions,
\[
n_j=n_j(0)e^{-F(1-Y_j)},\qquad
m_j=m_j(0)e^{-F},\qquad
e_j=e_0e^{-FY_j}.
\]
Here \(F\) is integrated activation exposure.
Matching the published central values requires substantial prior chirality:
\[
\boxed{e_0\geq0.3829.}
\]
The mechanism does not generate handedness from an initially racemic population. It requires at least a 38% initial imbalance in this model. The best constant-parameter survival found numerically was approximately
\[
2.46\times10^{-6},
\]
with an almost completely one-handed initial population.
I also allowed mixtures of more than 31,000 reaction-yield settings to test whether changing conditions could substantially reduce the inferred destruction. The discretized optimum was similar. This was not a certified global optimization over all possible chemistry.
