8. Comparison with current certification and learning algorithms
The comparison changes when “local access” is unpacked.
Normalized-Frobenius certification. Bluhm and coauthors give \(O(1/\varepsilon)\)-total-time tolerant certification for fixed-locality Hamiltonians, including ancilla-free variants. Their target is normalized Frobenius distance between generators, with general evolution access and candidate-dependent processing. That is not the promise of accepting every accurate one-qubit trajectory under a short local-port interface. The distinction is not simply the presence of an ancilla. arXiv
Single-qubit-operation certification. Shin and Tong’s eigenphase-engineering work supplies learning and tolerant certification using single-qubit operations and forward dynamics. Those operations are not confined to the protected ports here. Small evolution steps also do not imply a small total coherent duration for one controlled experiment. arXiv
A single readout qubit can already suffice under stronger control. Liu and coauthors’ 1 October 2026 work learns many-body Hamiltonians through one measurable qubit using controls throughout the register and a connected reference backbone. They explicitly construct coherent information routing. This is a particularly important comparison: restricting readout alone is not the obstruction established here. Their systemwide controls are absent from my lower-bound interface. arXiv
Very restricted settings can still provide broad information. Gong and coauthors’ 5 October 2026 work reconstructs generic two-local Hamiltonians using uniform preparation and measurement settings across the system, with separate computational-basis results for specified interaction families. Such settings are experimentally restricted, but spatially global; they do not leave the inaccessible bath fixed in vacuum. arXiv
Structured local-probe reconstruction. Quantum Probe Tomography provides positive results using local probes, structured Hamiltonian families and thermal-state access. Those assumptions differ from the inhomogeneous worst-case family and fixed reset interface here. The new theorem does not exclude those positive guarantees. arXiv
Accordingly, the result is not “local measurements cannot certify quantum dynamics.” It is a statement about the joint costs of where one may intervene, how long each system may evolve, which bath states can be prepared, and whether approximately correct predictions must be accepted.
9. What has actually advanced
The new contribution is a self-contained separation between an exact predictive certificate and genuine tolerant forecasting under ordinary active real-time access.
It is stronger than the previous fixed-input imaginary-time obstruction in several specific respects: the experiments may be actively controlled; the target is only one qubit; the complete Hamiltonian alternatives are already known; full spectra coincide; and the proof bounds whole controlled quantum experiments.
The package contains 2,502 finite numerical assertions, including 690 multiprecision checks, controlled interacting-chain checks, the exact-null certificate and the far-end escape. It also supplies an explicit dyadic ten-qubit instance. These are diagnostics, not independent verification or a laboratory demonstration.
The broad positive objective remains unestablished: I have not produced a universally tolerant, efficient certifier from short local-port experiments. Instead, the theorem proves that such a guarantee is impossible over the stated class.
The resulting principle is precise: a sound certificate that accepts exact agreement need not be convertible into a procedure that reliably accepts approximately correct long-time predictions. Under restricted physical access, acquiring that tolerance can itself require superpolynomial information—even when there is only one unknown microscopic bit.
