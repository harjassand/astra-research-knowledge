6. SHARP RAYS ARE NONEXPOSED IN THE FULL NINE-DIMENSIONAL CONE K

They ARE exposed inside C: the nonnegative functional obtained by adding
the two block-kernel quadratic forms vanishes only on their ray. Exposure
does not automatically pass from an exposed face to its ambient cone.
For these rays it actually fails.

Let T be the compact set of acoustic evaluation functionals
ell_(A,eta,xi)(F)=D^2F(A)[eta tensor xi,eta tensor xi], with
|A|=|eta|=|xi|=1. The invariant quartic Q0(A)=|A|^4 satisfies

 ell_(A,eta,xi)(Q0)
 =8[A:(eta tensor xi)]^2+4|A|^2|eta tensor xi|^2 >= 4.

Consequently cone(T) is closed: evaluation at Q0 bounds the total mass
of any convergent nonnegative conic combination, and conv(T) is compact
in finite dimension. Bipolar duality gives K*=cone(T). Caratheodory's
theorem permits finite conic combinations; no distributional derivative
of an acoustic evaluation is being included as a positive functional.

Suppose L>=0 on K and L(J_r)=0. Write L as a nonnegative finite sum of
normalized acoustic evaluations. Every evaluation used with positive
weight must vanish on J_r. After rotating its target vector to e1, the
determinant and kernels in Section 4 show its only possible contacts are

 p=0, A proportional to I; or q=0, A proportional to A_r.

For j^2, the acoustic form in this target normalization is 2(dp-cq)^2.
It vanishes at both contact families. Hence every term of L annihilates
j^2 and L(j^2)=0. Since j^2 and J_r are independent, L cannot expose the
ray J_r in K.

In fact its smallest exposed face in K is exactly cone{J_r,j^2}. It is
exposed by ell0+ell_r, where ell_r(F)=D^2F(A_r)[e11,e11]. The first zero
puts F in C. For z=(r+2,-3), the second zero is

 (1/2)ell_r(F)=(1/9) z M2 z^T=0.

Since M2 is positive semidefinite, M2 z^T=0, so

 alpha=3delta/[8(r+2)], beta=(r-1)delta/6.

The first LMI now forces delta=t r and gamma=t+s for t,s>=0. Thus
F=t J_r+s j^2, as claimed. The preceding dual argument proves that no
smaller exposed face contains J_r.
