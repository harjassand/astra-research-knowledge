## 6. Tempting but misleading lossless-Hall variant

For identical wet devices and an electrical two-port Y=d I+h J, with J=[[0,-1],[1,0]],

    H=kappa I+gamma J,
    kappa=K-C^2(G+d)/((G+d)^2+h^2),
    gamma=C^2 h/((G+d)^2+h^2).

An ideal lossless electrical gyrator d=0 would permit

    max |gamma|/kappa = Z/(2 sqrt(1+Z)),
    max eta_work_transfer = eta_c(Z)^2.

The hydraulic off-diagonal responses have opposite signs but equal magnitudes: this is a gyrator, not an isolator. Ordinary ohmically contacted Hall plates do not become lossless terminal gyrators merely because bulk longitudinal conductivity approaches zero. Known contact-loss obstructions invalidate the naive efficient-DC-machine interpretation. Capacitive Hall coupling changes the finite-frequency problem, not the DC conclusion.
