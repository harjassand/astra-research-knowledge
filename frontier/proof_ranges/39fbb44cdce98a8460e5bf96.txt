### Why the stronger source statement also holds

Suppose the consumed source \(\rho\) has support spanned by product vectors. Choose a separable state \(\zeta\) with exactly the same support. There are finite constants \(a,b>0\) such that
\[
a\rho\preceq\zeta\preceq b\rho.
\tag{18}
\]

Assume a successful operation converts \(\rho\otimes\tau\) to \(p\Omega\), with output support \(U\), catalyst marginal \(\tau\), and \(U\) product-free.

Replace the source by the free state \(\zeta\). The resulting output lies between \(ap\Omega\) and \(bp\Omega\). In particular, it remains supported on
\[
U\otimes\operatorname{supp}\tau.
\]

Let \(\Phi\) be its induced, unnormalized map on the catalyst. Then
\[
ap\,\tau\preceq\Phi(\tau)\preceq bp\,\tau.
\tag{19}
\]

For any positive \(X\) supported on \(\operatorname{supp}\tau\), one has \(X\preceq c\tau\) for some finite \(c\). Positivity therefore shows that applying the same operation to \(\zeta\otimes X\) still produces an output supported on \(U\), with memory supported on \(\operatorname{supp}\tau\).

Moreover,
\[
\Phi^n(\tau)\succeq(ap)^n\tau,
\tag{20}
\]
so no finite iteration vanishes.

We have again obtained arbitrarily many outputs in \(U^{\otimes n}\), starting only from the finite catalyst and freely preparable inputs. The same contradiction proves the stronger statement. ∎

For algebraic input matrices, the support criterion is a finite decision problem: finding a product vector in \(U_\sigma\) amounts to solving finitely many polynomial equations for nonzero vectors \(a,b\). No search over unbounded catalyst dimensions is necessary in this model. This does **not** decide the earlier tensor-universality problem, whose nonfree inputs and exact product-return requirements are different.
