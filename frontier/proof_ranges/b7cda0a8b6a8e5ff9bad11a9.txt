The one concrete escape worth discriminating is a timed export into a physically isolated reservoir. It changes the common-solution assumption without postulating a coating. Obtainable valves do not acquire the requisite salt response: precipitation may occur on exposed faces before export, or continue from nuclei carried into the reservoir. A useful interval between contact dissolution, export and competing precipitation has not been measured on this NH4SCN platform. No calcite kinetics are transferred.

The decisive experiment is an isothermal interface pulse, not a refrigerator build. On one NH4SCN specimen, fix contact stress and temperature and vary only solution residence time before isolation. Measure actual contact area, force-displacement work, gross retreat, exported dissolved inventory, precipitation everywhere, calorimetric heat and pumping work. Subject each exported inventory to the specified warm regrowth stroke and measure restored crystal geometry, composition and solvent inventory. Compare exposed-face, zero-stress and inert mechanical controls. All are proposed measurements.

Reject the intervention unless the exported composition exceeds the measured warm-regrowth equilibrium threshold and the whole cold heat balance is positive. In a restricted constant-partial-enthalpy model,

  Qc approximately equals Delta_h_diss*n_retained - W_contact - E_other_cold.

With W_contact approximately sigma*v_s*n_gross, a necessary condition is

  n_retained/n_gross > (sigma*v_s + E_other_cold/n_gross)/Delta_h_diss.

No numerical NH4SCN contact parameters are assigned. Use calibrated calorimetry over this approximation. Geometry not restored, irreversible redistributed salt, separator work, solvent loss and damage remain costs even when retreat is rapid.
