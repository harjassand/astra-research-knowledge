## 3. Consequence of OpenAI family 004: bounded-degree smooth rational-point undecidability

OpenAI's manuscript Hilbert's tenth problem over the rational numbers claims that rational solvability of arbitrary integer-coefficient polynomials is undecidable.

Its Section 7 derives two additional statements: the rational-solvability problem has Turing degree \\(0'\\), and its restriction to polynomials of degree at most four retains that degree. The reduction to degree four is a standard arithmetic-circuit construction; the undecidability itself depends on the manuscript's much deeper preceding argument.

Source: OpenAI family 004, original Section 7, pinned revision.

Applying the theorem above to a polynomial of degree at most four gives

\\[ \deg H_f\le2(4)+3=11. \\]

We therefore obtain the following precise conditional consequence.

Corollary, conditional on the correctness of OpenAI family 004. No algorithm decides whether an arbitrary smooth, geometrically integral affine hypersurface over \\(\mathbb Q\\), of total degree at most eleven and unrestricted dimension, has a rational point.

The proof is an effective many-one reduction:

\\[ \mathrm{H10}(\mathbb Q) \ \le_m\ \mathrm{H10}\_{\deg\le4}(\mathbb Q) \ \le_m\ \mathrm{SmoothIntegralAffine}\_{\deg\le11}(\mathbb Q). \\]

The first reduction is the arithmetic-circuit normalization. The second is \\(f\mapsto H_f\\). Constant inputs can be handled separately by fixed smooth geometrically integral hypersurfaces with a rational point or without one.

Under the same assumption, the target decision problem has Turing degree \\(0'\\): the reduction gives hardness, while enumeration of rational tuples gives the upper bound.

The distinction matters: this does not establish a many-one reduction of the halting problem to \\(\mathrm{H10}(\mathbb Q)\\). OpenAI's Section 7 explicitly distinguishes its Turing reduction from that stronger assertion.

### A further implication already supplied by published mathematics

Bjorn Poonen proved in 2009 that an algorithm deciding rational-point existence on smooth projective geometrically integral varieties over a number field would also decide rational-point existence on arbitrary varieties.

Consequently, if OpenAI family 004 is correct, rational-point existence is also undecidable for smooth projective geometrically integral varieties over \\(\mathbb Q\\).&#x20;

That is a historically consequential implication, but its logical transfer was already established in the literature; it is not a newly discovered bridge.

Nor does it establish the corresponding result for smooth projective hypersurfaces of bounded degree. Projective completion introduces rational points at infinity, and the affine construction above does not prevent them.

## 4. An unconditional undecidability result requiring no OpenAI premise

There is also a separate consequence of the classical Davis–Putnam–Robinson–Matiyasevich theorem.

Theorem. Deciding the existence of an integer point on a smooth, geometrically integral affine hypersurface over \\(\mathbb Q\\) of total degree at most five is undecidable. As a computably enumerable decision problem, it is many-one complete.

### Proof

Given an arbitrary integer polynomial \\(f\\), introduce arithmetic-circuit variables. Its equation \\(f=0\\) becomes a finite system

\\[ q_1(\mathbf Z)=\cdots=q_s(\mathbf Z)=0, \qquad \deg q_i\le2, \\]

with integer solutions equivalent to those of the original equation.

Now construct the single hypersurface

\\[ \boxed{ U\left(1+\sum\_{i=1}^s q_i(\mathbf Z)^2\right)=1. } \\]

For integer inputs,

\\[ 1+\sum_i q_i(\mathbf Z)^2 \\]

is a positive integer. Its product with the integer \\(U\\) equals one exactly when \\(U=1\\) and all \\(q_i=0\\).

Thus integer solvability is preserved.

Let \\(G=1+\sum_iq_i^2\\). The coordinate ring is

\\[ \mathbb Q[\mathbf Z,U]/(UG-1) \cong \mathbb Q[\mathbf Z,G^{-1}]. \\]

It is the coordinate ring of a nonempty principal open subset of affine space. Hence the hypersurface is smooth and geometrically integral. Its degree is at most five.

The reduction is effective. DPRM makes ordinary integer polynomial solvability many-one complete among computably enumerable sets, and integer solutions can be enumerated. The same classification follows for these quintic hypersurfaces. \\(\square\\)

This establishes an exact restricted undecidability theorem without relying on any claim from the October 2026 release.

Its historical novelty is another matter.
