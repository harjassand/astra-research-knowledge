## What selects the sign

For two inversion-related polar variants, write their polarizations as \(\mathbf P_s=s\mathbf P_0\), with \(s\in\{-1,+1\}\). Their electric-field contribution to free-energy density is

\[
f_s=f_0-\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf P_s,
\qquad f_{+}-f_{-}=-2\,\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf P_0.
\]

Thus the sign-selecting operation is an inversion-odd field component along the polar axis. A scalar intensity mask alone is inversion-even; it specifies where and how much light is delivered, but not which of two otherwise symmetric variants is lower in energy. In Fe:LiNbO₃, absorbed visible light creates photovoltaic charge, and the conductive liquid screens surface charge. The crystal/liquid boundary, c-axis orientation, and charge transport provide the sign asymmetry. The 2025 structured-light system modulates amplitude and exposure with an SLM; it does not lock a carrier-envelope phase or a relative \(\omega/2\omega\) phase. Calling this a coherent-phase-selective operation would therefore misstate its control variable.

This known field-coupling identity is a mechanism check, not a new law. It also explains why the route cannot simply be transferred to GaAs: GaAs antiphase-boundary polarity and the polar LiNbO₃ order parameter are different structural coordinates, defects, and switching kernels. The incoming GaAs hypothesis still requires a measured phase-odd retained boundary response in its own specimen and geometry.
