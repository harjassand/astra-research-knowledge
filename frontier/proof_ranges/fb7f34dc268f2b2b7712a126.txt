## 4. Executed obstruction: a subsolution is not an acquired response

Take H=C, a=1, Phi=Id and P=1/2. The unique fixed point is X_P=1/2.
Both A_0=0 and A_1=1/2 satisfy A<=g_1(A+P), with exactly the same
source and propagation data. They are also actual scalar operator comparisons:
T_i=sqrt(A_i) and B=sqrt(1/2) satisfy T_i*T_i<=g_1(T_i T_i*+BB*).

Consider actions u in {0,1} and loss (u-2A)^2. At A_0 the unique optimum
is u=0; at A_1 it is u=1. A decision procedure given only the comparison
data cannot distinguish them. If it randomizes with probability p of choosing
u=1, its two regrets are p and 1-p, hence worst-case regret at least 1/2.
This is an exact information-interface counterexample, not a runtime lower bound
for algorithms given T or additional observations.

Thus the source's A<=X_P theorem cannot by itself provide two-sided losses for
an unspecified A. If Q>=0, a one-sided statement survives:

    Tr(QA) <= Tr(QY)+||Q||_1 R_a(eta).

For the scalar comparison, an exact rank or Schatten certificate is still a
valuable upper bound on an already specified T. It supplies no acquisition
oracle for the target, its polar factor, task losses or a physical observable.
In particular this fixed-point result does not close the N764/N768 interacting
inverse/rank or loss-acquisition obligations.
