## Two targets that must be distinguished

For ordered volumes f_i at chemical axes n_i with local order S_i, a corrected, integrated, incoherent kinematic superlattice measurement has weights

    a_i = f_i S_i^2,
    W = sum a_i n_i n_i^T,
    Q_order = W/trace W.

The source proposal instead requests

    Q_volume = M/trace M,
    M = sum f_i n_i n_i^T.

The first is an experimentally plausible first-stage readout; it is not generally the second. Extra energies change Fe/Ni contrast, but multiply the same S_i^2 and do not separately recover f_i and S_i.

An exact indistinguishable pair has only x and y axes. World A has f=(1/2,1/2) and S^2=(9/25,9/25). World B has f=(3/4,1/4) and S^2=(6/25,18/25). Both have total ordered volume 1 and identical a=(9/50,9/50), hence identical superlattice poles at every energy in the factorized model. Yet Q_volume differs. Even perfect signal and full angular coverage cannot remove this ambiguity from those data alone.

There is a stronger false positive for the proposed loading contrast. Keep f_x=f_y=1/2 and both axes permanently fixed. Under x loading choose S=(4/5,2/5); under y loading choose S=(2/5,4/5). Both final states have phase volume 1 and total superlattice weight 2/5. Q_order changes from diag(4/5,1/5,0) to diag(1/5,4/5,0). Thus the apparent D_x-D_y is 6/5, although the volume-axis contrast is exactly zero. A retained, cyclically repeatable order-weighted signal can therefore arise by selective order/disorder without axis reorientation. Matching total phase fraction and total diffraction order does not rule this out. Exact rational checks are saved in `measurement_results.json`.
