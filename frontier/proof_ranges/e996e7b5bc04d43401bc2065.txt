## 8. What would make this decision-relevant

The present E/O laws are abstract and are not fitted to a catalyst. A consequential use needs a concrete pair of physically credible catalyst histories that:

1. both fit the strongest existing pulse, periodic, and other transient data under the same prior calibration information;
2. predict a separation in one selected functional such as (6) that exceeds calibrated experiment and model uncertainty;
3. imply different actions under an explicit catalyst/process objective, such as a change in product selectivity, stability, throughput, or energy use; and
4. can be discriminated by a measured input waveform and full-parent-product count channel at lower total cost or higher reliability than optimized pulse, multipulse, periodic, nonlinear isotope-frequency, and relevant spectroscopic controls.

The actual acquisition ledger must charge the boundary waveform and its transfer delay, isotope purities, chemical-feed constancy, isotope dose, prehistory and washout, old-carbon residual, stationary-state validation, isotope effects on kinetics, product separation, natural-isotope and fragmentation correction, detector response and covariance, normalization, observation time, switching bandwidth, and calibration. Raw ion counts are not independent product samples. The ancestry quantity is entry at the declared boundary; it is not automatically surface residence, bond-formation time, or a spatial location.

No current result supplies the catalyst-specific hypotheses, measured waveform, response matrix, acquisition covariance, total-cost comparison, or action map. Therefore the result is a rigorous ideal identifiability boundary plus a scoped synthetic control calculation, not a validated scientific decision aid.
