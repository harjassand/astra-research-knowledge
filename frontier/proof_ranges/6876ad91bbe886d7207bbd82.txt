I committed to the following target:
\[ \boxed{\chi(G)\le 2h(G)\quad\text{for every finite graph }G,} \]
where \(\chi(G)\) is the chromatic number and \(h(G)\) is the largest order of a complete-graph minor.
This investigation did not settle that bound. It produced an exact contact-completion certificate, a concrete application that survives a recently constructed obstruction, and a structural observation that changes how a second approach should be pursued. The principal remaining obligation is a global theorem about chromatic-critical graphs—not an improvement to the constants in the local certificate.
The complete proofs and reproducible checks are preserved in the RESEARCH_NOTE.md and astra_genesis_contact_completion.zip.
I screened three distinct objectives: simultaneous basis exchange, invariant subspaces of Hilbert-space operators, and conversion of chromatic complexity into clique minors.
For simultaneous basis exchange, the missing step was coordinating many locally valid exchanges without destroying previously established bases. Results for random bases do not supply that operation for arbitrary inputs. For invariant subspaces, the obstacle I considered was preserving a nonzero, proper, closed subspace when passing from approximate structures to a limit. I found more concrete leverage in the graph problem: its central requirements—connectedness, disjointness and pairwise contact—can be separated and tested exactly. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.17121)
The current sources also changed the appropriate target. Wood’s 7 October 2026 preprint states the universal bound
\[ \chi(G)\le 19{,}885{,}160\,h(G), \]
refining the new linear theorem of Norin and Steiner. Thus merely establishing some absolute constant is no longer an appropriate objective. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10831v1)
Separately, the supplied OpenAI archive claims counterexamples to exact Hadwiger, \(\chi(G)\le h(G)\). I have not independently audited that manuscript, and Wood’s introduction still describes the exact conjecture as open. I therefore distinguish the archive claim from independently reconciled mathematical consensus. Neither source establishes the factor-two target pursued here.  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.10831v1)
The obstruction selected for attack was:
How can one create many mutually adjacent connected branch sets while preserving their disjointness, without replacing ordinary contact by a stronger—and potentially false—requirement?
A \(K_t\)-minor can be represented by \(t\) disjoint connected vertex sets, with an edge between every pair of sets. Call these sets branch sets.
The smallest nontrivial branch sets are edges. A matching whose edges are pairwise touching therefore gives a clique minor.
Let \(H\) be a graph on an even number \(N\ge4\) of vertices, and set \(G=\overline H\). Define
\[ D=\Delta(H), \qquad C=\max_v c_H(v), \]
where \(c_H(v)\) counts the induced four-cycles of \(H\) containing \(v\).
Choose \(x,y\in(0,1)\), and put
\[ L=(1-x)^D(1-y)^C. \]
If
\[ \boxed{ \frac1{N-1}\le xL^2, \qquad \frac1{(N-1)(N-3)}\le yL^4, } \tag{1} \]
then \(G\) has a pairwise-touching perfect matching. Consequently,
\[ h(G)\ge N/2. \]
Choose a perfect matching \(M\) uniformly from all perfect matchings of \(K_N\). There are two kinds of failure.
A matching edge might belong to \(H\), rather than \(G\). Each specified forbidden edge occurs with probability
\[ p_1=\frac1{N-1}. \]
Alternatively, two legal matching edges might fail to touch. If their endpoint pairs are \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{c,d\}\), this happens precisely when
\[ ac,ad,bc,bd\in E(H), \qquad ab,cd\notin E(H). \]
Thus their four vertices induce a four-cycle in \(H\), and the matching contains its two diagonals. Each specified event of this kind has probability
\[ p_2=\frac1{(N-1)(N-3)}. \]
This is an exact characterization of contact failure, not a surrogate based on density or average connectivity.
These events specify partial matchings inside a uniform perfect matching. The Lu–Székely negative-dependency theorem permits the lopsided Lovász local lemma in this probability space. We may use a dependency graph joining all events whose vertex supports intersect. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/0905.3983v4)
Each vertex participates in at most \(D\) first-kind events and \(C\) second-kind events. Assign activities \(x\) and \(y\), respectively. An event supported on \(r\) vertices has a local-lemma neighbour-product at least \(L^r\). Conditions (1) therefore imply simultaneous avoidance of every failure event.
The resulting matching consists of legal edges, and every pair touches. Contracting its edges gives \(K_{N/2}\). \(\square\)
For any sequence of even-order graphs satisfying
\[ \Delta(H_N)=o(N) \]
and
\[ \boxed{ \limsup_{N\to\infty} \frac{\max_v c_{H_N}(v)}{N^2} < \frac1{4e}, } \tag{2} \]
the complement has a pairwise-touching perfect matching for all sufficiently large \(N\).
To see this, choose \(c<1/(4e)\) above the limsup and set
\[ x=A/N,\qquad y=B/N^2. \]
