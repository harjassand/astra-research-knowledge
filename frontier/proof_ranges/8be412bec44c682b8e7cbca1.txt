## Exact statements and mechanisms

### AIPS Proposition 5.1

In *A hunt for sharp L^p-estimates and Rank-One Convex Variational Integrals*, arXiv:1403.1095v1, §5 first introduces the wider radial-linear notation `f(x)=Lambda(|x|)x`, allowing matrix-valued `Lambda`. Proposition 5.1 itself restricts to scalar `Lambda:[0,R]->R`, with `f:B_R->R^n`, and assumes continuous rank-one-concave `E:R^(n×n)->R`. It concludes

```text
integral_{B_R} E(Df) <= |B_R| E(Lambda(R) I).
```

Equivalently, with `F=-E` and `u=f-Lambda(R)x`, this is the Jensen inequality for `Du` at affine datum `B=Lambda(R)I`. The radial profile is Lipschitz as set out in §5.1; the theorem is not for arbitrary matrix-valued `Lambda`.

The proof mollifies `E` while preserving rank-one concavity, then for each radius `t` defines the difference between the energy of `Df` over `B_t` and that of the affine map `x -> Lambda(t)x`. Since

```text
Df(x) = Lambda(|x|) I + Lambda'(|x|) x⊗x/|x|,
```

the increment from `Lambda(|x|)I` is rank one. Rank-one concavity gives its tangent upper bound. Integrating on `|x|=t` uses

```text
integral_{|x|=t} x⊗x = (|S^{n-1}|/n) t^(n+1) I,
```

which cancels the derivative of the reference affine energy and makes the shell-energy difference monotone. This is a clean global Jensen result for radial maps and implies their gradient distributions are laminates by the usual Jensen characterization. It is not an explicit laminate splitting tree.

### FL modulation theorem

The pinned source theorem assumes `F:R^(m×n)->R` is C² rank-one convex, `V:R->R^m` is C² periodic and satisfies `V''(theta)=-a(theta)V(theta)` with `a>=0`, and `r` is an arbitrary smooth real periodic scalar function of all `n` coordinates. With `theta=k·x`, `W=V'`, and

```text
phi = r V(theta),   P = r W⊗k,   Q = V⊗grad(r),
```

both `P` and `Q` are rank one. For every constant `B`, the source proves

```text
integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
 = integral R_F(B+P,Q)
   + integral a(theta) r^2 integral_0^1 t D^2F(B+tP)[V⊗k,V⊗k] dt >= 0.
```

Here `R_F(C,Q)=F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C)[Q]`. Rank-one convexity makes the first term nonnegative; the second is nonnegative because the Hessian in a rank-one direction is nonnegative. The equality comes from differentiating the source's vector primitive and integrating its divergence over the periodic cell. The argument extends in the source to smooth zero-boundary amplitudes; the stated quartic extension to `W^(1,4)` is by strong approximation.

The key difference from AIPS is not merely replacing a radial formula by a loop symbol. In AIPS, isotropy of the sphere integrates the rank-one radial increment into a scalar reference derivative. In FL, an ODE for the target loop turns phase differentiation into a scalar nonnegative curvature weight and yields an exact gap decomposition for arbitrary `B`. AIPS states Proposition 5.1 directly for continuous rank-one-concave integrands; FL's exact identity is derived for C² integrands, while its source separately extends the bounded-gradient Jensen inequality to continuous rank-one-convex tests by convolution. FL works for arbitrary `m,n`, arbitrary `B`, and full-coordinate amplitudes. The source's separate planar quartic corollary gives a uniform `mu_F/12` fourth-order gap for its modulation class; this is a quantitative strengthening on that class, not a general Morrey theorem.

### Convex-loop parameterization and limits of the comparison

The source also derives the oscillator condition for a smooth strictly convex closed planar curve enclosing the origin after reparameterizing at constant areal speed: `det(V,V')` is constant, so `V''` is parallel to `V`, and positive curvature gives `V''=-aV` with `a>0`. Thus the loop mechanism is broader than the circle/ellipse example, but still relies on a scalar spatial phase `k·x` and the special oscillator relation.

AIPS's nontrivial radial field has its target line varying around rays from a center; an FL field has a carrier line constant along parallel affine hyperplanes. A linear change of spatial coordinates does not turn radial angle into an affine scalar phase. Conversely, the displayed FL example has modulus varying in one Cartesian direction while the loop phase varies in another, so it is not a radial map. These examples rule out the direct identification of the two natural classes; they do not rule out an indirect representation through a more general laminate theorem.
