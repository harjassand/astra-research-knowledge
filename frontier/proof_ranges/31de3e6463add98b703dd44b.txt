The strongest extension is an operational readout theorem, not a faster Gibbs-learning algorithm.
I obtained a self-contained finite-dimensional proof of the following statement:
For bounded-locality, bounded-incidence Hamiltonian parameter directions, one fixed randomized single-qubit measurement loses at most an extensive amount of Fisher information relative to optimal quantum readout—even with calibrated, independent detector errors.
The consequence is sharper than a conditioning statement: when equilibrium parameter sensitivity is superextensive, this fixed local measurement preserves its leading information. Moreover, a measured scalar variance can certify that near-optimality without evaluating a partition function.
This result does not assume the earlier global covariance candidate. Its complete derivation is below and in the manuscript. Independent proof review and original priority remain unestablished.

I examined the predecessor’s decisive ordering:
\[ \langle \mathcal G_iP_a,\mathcal G_jP_c\rangle_K = \langle P_a,\mathcal G_i\mathcal G_jP_c\rangle_K, \]
followed by local approximation.
I did not find a decisive defect in moving the exact thermal adjoint before truncation, or in the corresponding support-counting argument. That does not independently validate the complete Gaussian locality, frequency-tail, and uniform-constant construction. The repository appropriately still records N711 as an unreviewed candidate with an open independent-reconstruction gate. 
The important limitation is that its auxiliary operator need not generate a completely positive evolution. Thus its coercivity does not automatically provide a physical Gibbs sampler, rapid equilibration, or inexpensive optimization.
The productive change was to investigate a different quantity:
\[ \boxed{\text{measured covariance}-\text{thermal response covariance}.} \]
Neither covariance needs to have decaying correlations or bounded largest eigenvalue. Their difference can nevertheless be bounded uniformly. That is the cancellation mechanism used here.
Let
\[ H(h)=H_{\mathrm{bg}}+\sum_{a=1}^{m}h_aP_a, \qquad \rho_h=\frac{e^{-\beta H(h)}}{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H(h)}}. \]
The \(P_a\) are distinct, canonical-sign, nonidentity Pauli strings satisfying
\[ |\operatorname{supp}P_a|\le k, \qquad \max_i\#\{a:i\in\operatorname{supp}P_a\}\le\Delta. \]
As before, candidate incidence includes zero-valued coefficients.
The background is fixed with respect to \(h\). At the operating point, assume the full Hamiltonian has a decomposition
\[ H=\sum_X H_X, \qquad |X|\le\ell, \qquad \max_i\sum_{X\ni i}\|H_X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le g. \]
This permits a background outside the parameter dictionary. It also permits many weak background interactions: bounded combinatorial degree of the background is unnecessary.
The state must be the unrestricted, full-rank Gibbs state at known \(\beta>0\). No commutativity, absence of frustration, high-temperature condition, spectral gap, or correlation-decay assumption is imposed.
On every copy, independently choose \(X\), \(Y\), or \(Z\) uniformly at each site, measure that single-qubit observable, and retain both the basis and outcome.
Allow calibrated independent sign errors with site visibility
\[ \eta_i\in[\eta,1],\qquad \eta>0. \]
A visibility \(\eta_i\) means a sign-flip probability \((1-\eta_i)/2\).
The corresponding unbiased feature for \(P_a\) is
\[ Y_a= \prod_{i\in S_a} \left[ \frac{3s_i}{\eta_i} \,\mathbf1\{b_i=(P_a)_i\} \right], \qquad S_a=\operatorname{supp}P_a. \]
Thus
\[ \mathbb E_hY_a=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_hP_a). \]
This is a local-Pauli classical-shadow measurement, with the noise correction explicit; that measurement primitive is established prior work, not the invention claimed here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08953)
Let \(C\) denote the BKM covariance matrix:
\[ u^TCu= \int_0^1 \operatorname{Tr} \left(\rho^t(A_u)_c\rho^{1-t}(A_u)_c\right)\,dt, \qquad A_u=\sum_a u_aP_a. \]
Let \(F_{\mathrm{loc}}\) be the classical Fisher matrix of the complete recorded measurement outcome, and let \(F_Q\) be the symmetric-logarithmic-derivative quantum Fisher matrix for the equilibrium parameters \(h\).
These concern changes in the Gibbs state as the Hamiltonian parameters change—not unitary phase encoding. Thermal Hamiltonian-parameter quantum Fisher information has been studied previously; the new target here is the information retained by a specified, parameter-independent local measurement. [Quantum Steampunk](https://quantumsteampunk.umiacs.io/public/docs/LPGP_24_Estimation_PRL.pdf)
Define
\[ \nu= \bigl[1+k(\Delta-1)\bigr] \left[\left(\frac3{\eta^2}\right)^k-1\right] + \frac{\beta g}{3}k(k+\ell)\Delta. \]
Then
\[ \boxed{ \beta^2C(C+\nu I)^{-1}C \preceq F_{\mathrm{loc}} \preceq F_Q \preceq\beta^2C. } \tag{1} \]
More strongly,
\[ \boxed{ 0\preceq\beta^2C-F_{\mathrm{loc}} \preceq\beta^2\nu I, } \tag{2} \]
and therefore
\[ \boxed{ 0\preceq F_Q-F_{\mathrm{loc}} \preceq\beta^2\nu I. } \tag{3} \]
The constant \(\nu\) is independent of \(n,m\) at fixed physical parameters.
For an extensive collective direction with \(\|u\|_2^2=O(n)\), equation (3) says that even optimal quantum readout can gain at most \(O(n)\) additional Fisher information over this fixed local measurement.
The theorem does not require a lower bound on \(\lambda_{\min}(C)\). It remains valid independently of whether N711 ultimately survives review.
Write
\[ V=\operatorname{Cov}(Y) \]
for the covariance of the measured features, and let
\[ (V_q)_{ac} = \frac12\operatorname{Tr}\rho \{(P_a)_c,(P_c)_c\} \]
be ordinary symmetrized quantum covariance.
The proof establishes
\[ \boxed{0\preceq V-C\preceq\nu I} \tag{4} \]
