## 2. A matched-Gaussian relative-entropy lemma

For a compactly supported probability mu on R^d, let gamma_r be N(0,r^2 I), f_mu=mu*gamma_r, and

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)=D(f_\mu\|\gamma_r).
 \tag{7}
\]

For V~mu and independent standard Gaussian Z,

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)=\frac{\mathbb E\|V\|^2}{2r^2}-I(V;V+rZ).
 \tag{8}
\]

This follows by expanding the Gaussian cross entropy and using I(V;V+rZ)=h(V+rZ)-h(rZ). In particular, for mu supported in the unit ball,

\[
 0\le\mathscr E_r(\mu)\le\frac1{2r^2}.
 \tag{9}
\]

Write eta <=cx mu if integral phi d eta <= integral phi d mu for every finite convex phi. This is the established convex order.

**Lemma 1 (convex-order reverse triangle).** If eta <=cx mu, then

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)\ge\mathscr E_r(\eta)+D(f_\mu\|f_\eta).
 \tag{10}
\]

**Proof.** The function

\[
 \ell_\eta(z)=\log\frac{f_\eta(z)}{\gamma_r(z)}
 =\log\int\exp\!\left(\frac{z\cdot v}{r^2}-\frac{\|v\|^2}{2r^2}\right)d\eta(v)
 \tag{11}
\]

is convex, as a log-Laplace transform. Common independent Gaussian addition preserves convex order: for convex phi, x -> E phi(x+rZ) is convex. Thus f_eta <=cx f_mu. Direct subtraction yields

\[
 \mathscr E_r(\mu)-\mathscr E_r(\eta)-D(f_\mu\|f_\eta)
 =\int(f_\mu-f_\eta)\ell_\eta\ge0.
\]

Compact support bounds ell_eta by affine growth in norm, and all densities have Gaussian tails; the integrals are finite. This proves the lemma. Gaussian convolution is injective, so equality in E_r monotonicity forces mu=eta. QED.

The reference Gaussian must match the convolution Gaussian. The lemma is not a statement that arbitrary KL divergences are monotone in convex order. Nor is mutual information alone monotone in that order (Section 10).
