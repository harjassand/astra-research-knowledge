Convex energy does not make asymptotic prediction computable
The strongest result from this investigation is a complete constructive proof candidate for an impossibility principle:

A finite-dimensional Hamiltonian system can have globally strongly convex energy, with its Hessian arbitrarily close to the identity, while a fixed quadratic observable converges on every encoded input to a value that no algorithm can determine.

The construction preserves bounded trajectories, a common input energy and a constant output gap. It does not obtain its undecidability by making the observable’s limit fail to exist.

The argument below imports one published polynomial-ODE simulation theorem. Historical originality and independent verification of the resulting theorem have not been established.
