8. Complete algorithm, error allocation and resource costs
The finite algorithm is:
Check covariance positivity and the danger-region interior.
Remove redundant facets.

Compute Gaussian regression data for the initial-region and facet components.
Approximate each component mass μ_i to relative error O(ε).
Construct its log-concave sampler.

Repeat O((md+1) ε⁻² log(1/δ)) times:
    choose a component using its normalized estimated mass;
    sample its local position/velocity variables;
    reconstruct a conditional Fourier trajectory;
    apply a uniform phase only for a facet component;
    count its entries algebraically;
    accumulate 1/W, treating a flagged numerical failure as score zero.

Return log(sum μ_i) + log(mean score).

Component masses and mixture probabilities are computed in the logarithmic domain. Terms too small to affect the error budget may be omitted with a charged total-mass bound; extremely small exponentials need not be written in fixed-point form.
Relative mass errors of \(O(\varepsilon)\) cause only relative \(O(\varepsilon)\) error in the ideal mixture. Component sampling and crossing-count errors are set to \(O(\varepsilon/K_*)\), because
\[
\mathbb E_Q(1/W)\ge1/K_*.
\]
This distinction prevents an unnecessarily severe precision requirement.
Let \(D_c=r(2d+1)\) be the coefficient dimension. Let \(I_{\mathrm{conv}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{conv}}\) denote the paid costs of the explicit convex integration and sampling constructions above—not free oracle calls.
A conservative bound is
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{total}}
\le{}&
\operatorname{poly}(L)
+(m+1)I_{\mathrm{conv}}
\\
&+
O\!\left(
K_*\varepsilon^{-2}\log\delta^{-1}
\right)
\left[
S_{\mathrm{conv}}
+\operatorname{poly}(D_c,m,d,b_{\mathrm{work}})
\right].
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{28}
\]
Every term is polynomial in the stated input and accuracy parameters. The conservative convex-body construction can have a high polynomial overhead; no near-linear or low-degree runtime is claimed.
For a strict worst-case polynomial execution bound, expected-time sampling subroutines are capped using their proved expectation bounds. Their small timeout probability is included in the bounded-score error budget. Confidence in the integration stage is amplified by independent repetitions, giving logarithmic dependence on \(\delta^{-1}\), rather than paying \(1/\delta\).
Memory. Dense regression preprocessing can conservatively store \(O((m+1)D_c^2)\) rational numbers, plus the convex-sampler workspace. Proposal trajectories are processed one at a time. No table of all time points or all Monte Carlo trajectories is required.
Randomness. All draws are implemented with finite fair-bit procedures at the precision in (27). There is no exact-real random-number oracle in the bit-complexity claim.
Outputs. A probability estimate occupies polynomially many bits in logarithmic or scientific notation. One conditioned trajectory has \(O(rd)\) coefficients. Requesting every entry time or a densely sampled waveform additionally pays for that output.
Acquisition. The covariance matrices, mean and facets are explicit inputs. The algorithm does not infer them from measurements or certify a physical Gaussian approximation for free.
9. Strongest consequence: probability and conditioned failure histories
The construction supports two distinct scientific outputs.
First, it estimates continuous-time entry probabilities for a supplied multivariate spectral model. This includes simultaneous threshold failures such as
\[
X_1(t)>u_1,\ldots,X_r(t)>u_r
\]
and general linear combinations representing prescribed danger modes.
Second, inverse-multiplicity acceptance produces trajectories conditioned on entering the danger region. In the ideal construction,
\[
\Pr(\text{accept})=p/M\ge1/K_*,
\]
so conditioned sampling costs at most \(K_*\) proposals on average—not \(1/p\).
The sampled crossing is not necessarily the first crossing. The complete waveform must be examined to obtain a first-entry record or a sequence of recrossings.
A polynomially supplied union of polyhedral danger regions is also covered. Count initial-state and entry witnesses separately for each region, even if the regions overlap. The same correction works with
\[
K_*
=
\sum_{\ell}
(1+m_\ell d).
\tag{29}
\]
This permits an explicit list of failure modes without inclusion–exclusion over their overlaps. It does not permit an exponentially expanded Boolean description to be treated as a short list.
Comparison under matched assumptions
Prior capability	What it already handles	Distinction in this construction
Efficient scalar Gaussian excursions	Rare high thresholds without a growing rare-event simulation penalty	A changing multivariate polyhedral conjunction is not a scalar Gaussian supremum
Vector Rice/Slepian methods	Boundary-crossing intensities and crossing-conditioned descriptions	Multiplicity correction gives the full entry probability and conditioned trajectory law
Truncated-Gaussian minimax tilting	Strong simulation and estimation methods for linear restrictions	Used as a relevant competitor, not assumed to have a uniform polynomial worst-case bound for every input
General log-concave integration	Polynomial sampling and normalization with specified geometric access	The required facet-flux distribution and its geometric data are constructed from the physical input
Exact algebraic root algorithms	Certified crossing counts for finite coefficients	Proposal-specific precision bounds connect those counts to the original continuous Gaussian law


The first three comparisons are supported by the scalar-excursion, vector-crossing and minimax-tilting literature; none is an obsolete direct-Monte-Carlo baseline. arXiv
The result is a uniform complexity guarantee for a specified model class, not evidence that its conservative implementation is faster than well-tuned specialized algorithms on every instance.
