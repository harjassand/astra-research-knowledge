\section*{2. Finite private codes: a concrete improvement}

Suppose the environment $E$ dominates receiver $B$ in measured $\chi^2$ for the block channel under consideration. A uniform $k$-message code with receiver average error $\varepsilon$ and secrecy
$\delta=\tfrac12\|\rho_{XE}-I_X/k\otimes\rho_E\|_1$ obeys
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \delta\ge\frac{[(1-\varepsilon-1/k)_+]^2}{1-1/k}.
 \quad} \tag{9}
\]
Indeed (3) gives an actual environment decoder with success at least $h_k(1-\varepsilon)$ when $1-\varepsilon\ge1/k$, and secrecy bounds every decoder's success by $1/k+\delta$. If the given error is larger, the displayed positive-part bound is trivial.

Hirche [1, Theorem 4.1] proves $\delta\ge(1-2\varepsilon)^2/2$ for $\varepsilon\le1/2$ from binary midpoint-MMSE dominance at each blocklength. That hypothesis already supplies the local SLD inequalities: encode $\sigma\pm tH$ at faithful $\sigma$ and cancel the exact $t^2$ midpoint form. The local-to-global argument above therefore makes (9) applicable under the same blockwise hypothesis. At $k=2$ it reproduces the prior bound. For $k>2$ it is strictly stronger throughout $\varepsilon\le1/2$; the difference is
\[
 (k-2)\left[\frac1{2k}-\frac{\varepsilon^2}{k-1}\right]>0.
\]
At zero decoding error it forces $\delta\ge1-1/k$, the largest possible cq secrecy defect. For example $k=1024$, $\varepsilon=.01$ gives $\delta\ge.9791$, whereas the stated pairwise bound gives $.4802$.

Complete SLD order tensorizes: replace one channel at a time, treating the other output as the untouched reference. Hence (3)--(4) hold for every parallel block code with the same encoder. They transfer vanishing classical and quantum decoding errors, and do not decrease achievable reliability exponents. Capacity monotonicity and zero-capacity consequences are not claimed as new. The existing general exponential private strong converse [5] uses a different ideal-key criterion; (9) is a finite-code bound on separate decoding and trace-distance secrecy errors.

\textbf{Closest antecedents and significance.} B\'eny [3, Eqs.~(5)--(6)] already defines the Bures metric adjoint; at maximally mixed logical input it is exactly (7). Barnum--Knill [4] already establishes squared near-optimal recovery and pretty-good discrimination. The transpose/Petz decoder has at least as much maximally mixed entanglement fidelity as this SLD decoder: its eigenbasis kernel $1/\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j}$ dominates $2/(\lambda_i+\lambda_j)$. The SLD score is useful here because it respects the assumed channel order. We claim neither a better recovery map nor a new near-optimal-decoding paradigm. No explicit primary statement of the cross-channel bounds (3)--(4) or the $k$-dependent refinement (9) was located in the focused review. These are scoped theorem candidates with direct proofs, not a certified historical first or a foundational breakthrough.
