\section{Exact cost of compressing a unitary}
All norms in this section use the original dimension $n$, including matrices acting on a corner.
\begin{lemma}[Polar repair formula]
Let $P$ be an orthogonal projection, $Q=I-P$, $U\in U(n)$, and $A=QUQ|_{Q\mathbb C^n}$. Put $T=QU^*PUQ|_{Q\mathbb C^n}$. Then $0\leq T\leq I_Q$ and
\[
\inf_{V\in U(Q\mathbb C^n)}\|A-V\|_{2,n}^2
=\frac1n\tr\bigl(I_Q-\sqrt{I_Q-T}\bigr)^2.
\]
Consequently this squared distance lies between $\tr(T^2)/(4n)$ and $\tr(T^2)/n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$A^*A=I_Q-T$. If the singular values of $A$ are $s_j$, the nearest-unitary squared Frobenius distance is $\sum_j(1-s_j)^2$. Indeed, expand $\|A-V\|_F^2$ and maximize $\operatorname{Re}\tr(V^*A)$ using a singular-value decomposition; completing the polar partial isometry on its kernel attains the maximum $\sum_j s_j$. If $t_j$ are the eigenvalues of $T$, then $s_j=\sqrt{1-t_j}$. For $0\leq t\leq1$,
\[
\frac{t}{2}\leq1-\sqrt{1-t}=\frac{t}{1+\sqrt{1-t}}\leq t.
\]
Squaring and summing proves the bounds.
\end{proof}

This shows why a small rank loss cannot be treated as free. The controlling quantity is a fourth-power leakage expression, $\tr[(B^*B)^2]/n$ with $B=PUQ$, rather than rank alone.

\begin{proposition}[An exact amplification example]
For every integer $q\geq2$, there are matrices in dimension $n=q^4$ and an error $E$ with $\|E\|_{2,n}=\eps=q^{-2}$ such that deleting a fraction $\eps$ of the dimensions kills $E$ exactly, but the compressed unitary requires repair of size $\sqrt{\eps}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose orthogonal $k=q^2$ dimensional spaces $B,C$. Let $U$ swap $B,C$ and fix the remaining space, $E=q^{-1}P_B$, and $Q=I-P_B$. Then $EQ=0$ and $\|E\|_{2,n}=q^{-1}\sqrt{k/n}=q^{-2}$. On $Q\mathbb C^n$, $QUQ$ has exactly $k$ zero singular values and all other singular values equal to one. The preceding formula gives optimal repair distance $\sqrt{k/n}=q^{-1}$.
\end{proof}
This example disproves a norm-only compression lemma. By itself it says nothing about a particular group's stability.
