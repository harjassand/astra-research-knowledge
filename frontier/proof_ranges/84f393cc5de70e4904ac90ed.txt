The remaining issue is whether (11) survives finite-precision acquisition of \(Y_0\). Ordinary rounding does not suffice.
Exact conservation repair
Choose a spanning tree and let \(T\) be its routing operator:
\[
BT=I.
\]
Applying \(T\) means accumulating vertex imbalances along the tree; it costs \(O(n)\), without storing a dense matrix.
For a column \(r\) corresponding to one row of \(R\), form a dyadic approximation
\[
a\approx W^{1/2}r,
\]
and approximately solve
\[
Lv=Ba.
\]
Instead of storing the provisional flow \(a-WB^\top v\), store
\[
\boxed{
j=a-WB^\top v-T(Ba-Lv).
}
\tag{13}
\]
Then, in exact arithmetic on the stored finite numbers,
\[
\boxed{Bj=0.}
\tag{14}
\]
Set the corresponding row of the implemented sketch \(Y\) to \(j^\top W^{-1}\).
This uses one Laplacian solve per row, followed by sparse operations and a tree traversal. Numerically stable, finite-precision, nearly-linear Laplacian solvers are established algorithms and are charged as preprocessing subroutines. arXiv
Why this repair gives relative—not merely absolute—accuracy
Let
\[
E=(Y-Y_0)W^{1/2}.
\]
Both the ideal and repaired normalized rows lie in the cycle space, so
\[
E=E\Pi.
\tag{15}
\]
Therefore, if the solves and square roots are accurate enough that \(\|E\|\le a_0\), then for every \(x\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|(Y-Y_0)x\|
&=\|E\Pi W^{-1/2}x\|\\
&\le a_0\sqrt{x^\top\Gamma x}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{16}
\]
This remains small even when \(x^\top\Gamma x\) is tiny. In particular, it is exactly zero on every true null direction.
Combining (16) with the ideal embedding yields
\[
\boxed{
(1-\theta)\Gamma_{SS}
\preceq
\widehat\Gamma_S:=Y_S^\top Y_S
\preceq
(1+\theta)\Gamma_{SS}
}
\tag{17}
\]
after reserving constant fractions of the error budget.
Thus the stored sketch has precisely the right local kernels on the common success event. No post-damage spectral-gap bound is used.
