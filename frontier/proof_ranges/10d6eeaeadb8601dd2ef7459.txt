2. Interface and the already independent upper endpoint.

Throughout T(rho,eta)=||rho-eta||_1/2. All channels act on the full matrix
algebra. Sigma is a faithful stationary density matrix and
  PhiSharp = Gamma_sigma Phi* Gamma_sigma^{-1},
  Gamma_sigma(X)=sqrt(sigma) X sqrt(sigma),
  S=PhiSharp Phi.
Phi is CPTP but is not assumed KMS symmetric. The same S is used for every
tested state; it depends only on Phi and sigma.

The frozen independent proof gives, for every Hermitian X with
-sigma<=X<=sigma,
  ||X-SX||_1^2 <= 2 ||X-Phi X||_1.                          (1)
Its load-bearing input is the Hilbert-Schmidt contraction of
  T_sigma(X)=sigma^{-1/4} Phi(sigma^{1/4} X sigma^{1/4})
             sigma^{-1/4},
obtained from finite Schatten interpolation. The weighted transform of S is
T_sigma* T_sigma, a positive contraction. If x is the weighted transform of
X, then
  ||(I-T_sigma* T_sigma)x||_2^2
    <= ||x||_2^2-||T_sigma x||_2^2
    <= 2 Tr[sigma^{-1/2} X sigma^{-1/2}(X-Phi X)]
    <= 2 ||X-Phi X||_1.
Schatten Holder converts the weighted Hilbert-Schmidt norm to physical trace
norm with factor ||sigma^{1/4}||_4^2=1. This is the proof of (1), with no
inverse-minimum-eigenvalue error factor.

Every binary-CQ state with B marginal sigma has the form
  rho=|0><0| tensor A + |1><1| tensor (sigma-A),
  0<=A<=sigma.
Take X=2A-sigma. Since Phi sigma=S sigma=sigma, classical block additivity
gives
  b=T(rho,(id tensor Phi)rho)=||X-Phi X||_1/2,
  e=T(rho,(id tensor S)rho)=||X-SX||_1/2.
Equation (1) yields
  e^2<=b,  hence e<=sqrt(b).                              (2)
This includes arbitrary binary priors, local dimensions, and faithful sigma.
It does not require self-compatibility. When Phi has a supplied broadcaster,
postprocessing both outputs by PhiSharp broadcasts S, so compatibility is
also retained. Binary-CQ mutual information is at most ln 2.

3. Audit of the supplied legal fixed-qubit construction.

For every integer K>=2 set
  v=K^{-2}, c=sqrt(v(1-v)), sigma=diag(3/4,1/4),
  tau_0=[[1-v/3,-c/3],[-c/3,v/3]],
  tau_1=[[v,c],[c,1-v]].
Both outputs have trace one. Tau_1 is positive of rank one because its
determinant is v(1-v)-c^2=0. Tau_0 has positive diagonal entries and
  det(tau_0)=(1-v/3)v/3-c^2/9=2v/9>0.
Thus
  Phi_K(X)=X_00 tau_0+X_11 tau_1
is a full-domain measurement-preparation CPTP channel. The actual broadcaster
  B_K(X)=X_00 tau_0 tensor tau_0+X_11 tau_1 tensor tau_1
is CPTP and has both marginals Phi_K for EVERY matrix input X.
The weighted average
  (3/4)tau_0+(1/4)tau_1=diag(3/4,1/4)=sigma
checks exact stationarity, including off-diagonal cancellation.

The genuine stationary Petz map is
  Phi_KSharp(X)
    =sum_j sigma_jj Tr[tau_j sigma^{-1/2} X sigma^{-1/2}] E_jj.
It is CPTP and fixes sigma. In particular,
  Phi_K(E_01)=0,
  Phi_KSharp(E_01)=diag(-c/sqrt(3),c/sqrt(3)).
This verifies the adjoint orientation and shows that the raw channel is
not KMS symmetric. The round trip S_K=Phi_KSharp Phi_K is sigma stationary,
KMS self-adjoint, and positive in the weighted Hilbert-Schmidt space.
Its full-domain broadcaster is (Phi_KSharp tensor Phi_KSharp) B_K.
The fact that tau_1 is rank deficient causes no inverse-state problem:
only the fixed faithful sigma is inverted.

4. Audit of faithful inputs, information, and the forward residual.

Let
  rho_+=[[5/8,c/6],[c/6,3/8]],
  rho_-=[[7/8,-c/6],[-c/6,1/8]],
  rho_K=(1/2)|+><+| tensor rho_+
        +(1/2)|-><-| tensor rho_-.
Both conditional states have trace one, their average is exactly sigma,
and the reference marginal is I_2/2.

Since v<=1/4, c^2<=3/16. The smaller conditional determinant obeys
  7/64-c^2/36>=7/64-(3/16)/36=5/48.
A trace-one positive matrix has lambda_min=det/lambda_max>=det. Thus
lambda_min(rho_+),lambda_min(rho_-)>=5/48 and
lambda_min(rho_K)>=5/96. The tested joint states are uniformly faithful.
Sigma is fixed and has lambda_min=1/4. Only channel output states may
approach the boundary as K increases; the target hypotheses allow this.

The exact information budget in nats is
  I(R:B)_{rho_K}
   =D(rho_K||(I_2/2) tensor sigma)
   =S(sigma)-(S(rho_+)+S(rho_-))/2
   <=S(sigma)<ln 2.
There is no growing dimension or diverging input information budget.

For a convenient conditional parametrization set
  rho(q)=[[1-q,h(q)],[h(q),q]],
  h(q)=(4c/3)(q-1/4), m=1-4v/3.
Direct application of Phi_K gives off-diagonal coefficient h(q) and tail
probability
  q'=v/3+(1-4v/3)q=1/4+m(q-1/4).
The preservation of h(q) arises from preparation conditioned on the input
diagonal; it does not claim preservation of arbitrary input coherences.

For q_+=3/8 and q_-=1/8, h(q_+)=c/6 and h(q_-)=-c/6. The changes of tail
probability are -v/6 and +v/6 respectively. The raw error in each block is
diagonal and traceless, with half-trace norm v/6. Hence
  b_K=T(rho_K,(id tensor Phi_K)rho_K)
     =v/6=1/(6K^2).                                      (3)

5. Exact Petz error, an analytic consistency bound, and the sharp exponent.

For real Hermitian X the tail entry of the Petz output is
  (Phi_KSharp X)_11
    =(v/3)X_00+(1-v)X_11+(2c/sqrt(3))X_01.
Applying this to Phi_K rho(q) gives
  (S_K rho(q))_11=1/4+ell(q-1/4),
  ell=m^2+8c^2/(3sqrt(3)).
All off-diagonal entries of S_K rho(q) vanish. A traceless Hermitian qubit
matrix with diagonal x,-x and off-diagonal h has half-trace norm
sqrt(x^2+|h|^2). The two conditional Petz errors therefore have the same
norm, and classical block additivity gives
  r_K=T(rho_K,(id tensor S_K)rho_K)
     =sqrt((1-ell)^2/64+c^2/36).                          (4)

The supplied lower estimate follows immediately:
  r_K>=c/6
      >=sqrt(3)/(12K)
      >=1/(7K),                                         (5)
where the last comparison is equivalent to 147>=144.

An additional analytic audit check supplies a matching upper estimate
without a numerical replay. Write
  1-ell=A v-B v^2,
  A=(8/3)(1-1/sqrt(3)),
  B=16/9-8/(3sqrt(3)).
One has 0<A<4/3 and B>0. For 0<v<=1/4,
  A-Bv>=A-B/4=20/9-2/sqrt(3)>0.
Consequently 0<1-ell<4v/3, and (4) gives
  r_K^2<=v^2/36+v(1-v)/36=v/36,
  r_K<=1/(6K).                                          (6)
In fact (4) shows K r_K -> 1/6. Equations (3)-(6) are an exact all-K
certificate: r_K is Theta(K^{-1}) while b_K=Theta(K^{-2}).

For any exponent alpha>1/2 and any fixed polynomial prefactor bounded on
the fixed information interval 0<=I<=ln 2,
  r_K/[P(I+1)b_K^alpha]
is unbounded, at least on the scale K^{2alpha-1}. Equivalently one can
use the single fixed budget C=ln 2 in P(C+1).
The independently derived bound (2) holds throughout the binary-CQ
subclass, while this fixed-qubit, fixed-sigma, balanced-binary family
rules out every larger universal exponent. Thus exponent 1/2 is sharp
for binary-CQ references under the stationary-margin interface.

This is a universal-exponent statement, not an optimal-prefactor result.
Indeed r_K/sqrt(b_K)->1/sqrt(6), so this witness does not saturate the
prefactor one in (2). Phi_K varies with K; no claim is made about a fixed
single channel admitting such a violating sequence.

6. Exactly what this comparison establishes and does not establish.

The lower family has local dimension 2, reference dimension 2, broadcaster
output dimension 4, fixed sigma, fixed bounded information, uniform
faithfulness of tested inputs, full-domain CPTP maps, and explicit legal
broadcasters. S_K is one fixed prescribed Petz round trip for both conditional
blocks. There is no label-dependent or state-dependent recovery choice.
The conditional quantum states generally do not commute with sigma.

The source construction is a genuine obstruction to Petz residual transfer
with exponent >1/2, including for self-compatible raw channels. It does not
obstruct exponent <=1/2. It also does not refute raw broadcasting-to-EB:
Phi_K itself is EB and preserves these states with the smaller raw error
b_K. An existential EB conclusion and a bound for this prescribed Petz
round trip are distinct interfaces.

The independent operator-order and dimension-charged lemmas remain valid.
For arbitrary quantum references, rho<=M(tau_R tensor sigma) gives
e<=sqrt(Mb), and a reference-dimension-r bound gives e<=r sqrt(b).
No mutual-information budget conversion eliminating M or r has been proved.
Their failure to yield the desired conversion is not a counterexample.

7. Pure-joint closure and precise primary-source antecedent.

The unchanged independent baseline proved for a pure rho_RB with
rho_B=sigma that
  e<=b+sqrt(b).
The proof compares entanglement fidelities of Phi and PhiSharp and then
uses trace-distance contraction and a triangle inequality. This proof was
written before the source-only memo gave the exact Barnum-Knill singleton
consequence. The pure subcase must therefore be labelled source antecedent,
not a newly established historical claim.

Primary source: Barnum and Knill, "Reversing quantum dynamics with near-optimal
quantum and classical fidelity," Theorem 3, Eq. (13), with transpose recovery
Eq. (12):
  https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0004088v2
  https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0004088
  https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1459754
The primary PDF had also been inspected during the bounded independent
source pass. The theorem assumes a commuting input ensemble. For the
singleton ensemble {sigma} this assumption is vacuous. Its reversal
Kraus operators sigma^{1/2} K_i^* Phi(sigma)^{-1/2} are exactly those of
PhiSharp because Phi(sigma)=sigma. Taking the identity competing recovery
gives
  F_e(sigma,PhiSharp Phi)>=F_e(sigma,Phi)^2.
For any purification rho_RB of sigma, measurement with the projector rho
implies F_e(sigma,Phi)>=1-b. The pure-state trace/fidelity inequality then
yields
  e<=sqrt(2b-b^2)<=sqrt(2b).
Thus a fully valid combined pure bound is
  e<=min{1, b+sqrt(b), sqrt(2b-b^2)}.
Neither bound needs an information budget.

The source theorem does not close the present arbitrary mixed-extension
gate. Every mixed extension is obtained by a channel on the purifying
reference, and contraction gives observed mixed b<=purification b.
This direction does not turn small observed mixed b into small
purification error. Applying transpose recovery to the whole mixed input
instead generally uses its full joint density matrix and produces a
different recovery map, not id_R tensor PhiSharp.

For a concrete scope check take sigma=I_2/2, Phi conjugation by Pauli X,
and a trivial reference with rho=sigma. Its observed b is zero and its
Petz round trip is exactly the identity, while F_e(sigma,Phi)=0.
This is not a counterexample to residual transfer. It demonstrates why
observed mixed residual cannot simply replace purification fidelity in the
Barnum-Knill argument.

8. General gate and the charged smoothing failure.

The still-open target is a bound for arbitrary mixed quantum rho_RB with
rho_B=sigma and I(R:B)<=C, of the form
  T(rho,(id tensor PhiSharp Phi)rho)
      <=poly(C+1) b^alpha
with universal alpha>0 and one fixed local Petz round trip. If such a
universal exponent exists, the present legal lower witness forces
alpha<=1/2.

No entropy-loss shortcut is asserted. The parent has separately noted that
bounded information and small raw residual need not make entropy loss small.
No global quantum power claim follows from the scoped binary or pure lemmas.

The order-smoothing attempt is also properly charged. If
T(rho,rho_tilde)<=epsilon and rho_tilde<=M Omega, contraction only guarantees
  b_tilde<=b+2epsilon.
Applying the order bound and transferring back gives
  e_rho<=2epsilon+sqrt(M(b+2epsilon)).
The smoothing error enters the forward residual before multiplication by M.
An exponential-in-1/epsilon order bound does not by itself give a logarithmic
rate, much less a power rate. Additional channel-compatible stability would
be needed. This is a failed proof shortcut, not a general impossibility.
