## 5. Local, connected, interacting, isospectral realization

### 5.1 Jacobi matrices differing at only one boundary

For either measure in (10), orthonormalize 1,x,...,x^d. Multiplication by x in this basis is a real symmetric tridiagonal Jacobi matrix. After a fixed alternating sign gauge, write them as A_g,A_b with negative neighbouring couplings and seed e_0.

The diagonal at j depends on moments through degree 2j+1. The bond from j to j+1 depends on moments through degree 2j+2. Equality of moments through 2d therefore makes every bond and all diagonal entries except the last identical. Both spectra lie in [0,1]. Trace comparison gives the exact difference

    A_b-A_g=a_delta |d><d|,     a_delta=(1+delta)/2.                        (14)

To verify the trace calculation, the even-indexed nodes have sum (d+1)(1+delta)/2; the odd-indexed nodes have sum d(1+delta)/2; nu_g additionally has eigenvalue zero. The corresponding cosine sums are zero. Endpoint spectral weights are the measures in (10).

Every bond has magnitude at most 1/2: subtracting I/2 gives an operator with norm at most 1/2, bounding each off-diagonal entry.

### 5.2 No exponentially small coefficients are required

The Chebyshev-Lobatto barycentric weights have magnitudes proportional to c_j, where c_0=c_m=1/2 and the interior c_j=1. This follows by differentiating the nodal polynomial proportional to (1-y^2)U_(m-1)(y) at its roots y=cos(j pi/m). The Lagrange coefficients at zero consequently obey

    |w_j|=kappa c_j/x_j

for a common positive kappa. Since w_0=kappa/(2delta)>=1, every |w_j|>=delta. As A<=2, every atom in either measure has weight at least delta/2.

The smallest gap between eigenvalues within either measure is at least 1/(8d^2). This follows from the smallest consecutive Chebyshev-node spacing, using sin y>=2y/pi on [0,pi/2]; the additional zero in nu_g is no closer to its next support point.

We use an elementary Jacobi estimate. If a Jacobi matrix has simple eigenvalue gaps at least Delta and first-site weights at least alpha, every bond b satisfies

    |b| >= Delta sqrt(alpha)/3.                                (15)

Split the chain across b. The left principal block has fewer eigenvalues than the full matrix. Intervals of radius Delta/3 around its eigenvalues can cover at most one full eigenvalue apiece. Choose a full eigenvalue lambda not covered. Its normalized eigenvector v then satisfies

    ||v_left|| <= 3|b|/Delta,
    alpha <= |v_0|^2 <= ||v_left||^2.

This proves (15), and here yields

    eta/(300d^3) <= |b_j| <=1/2.                                (16)

### 5.3 Reflection doubling and a weak connecting bond

Let gamma=eta/(16T). On two arms define the one-excitation corrections

    C_g=A_g direct-sum A_b
        -gamma(|L_0><R_0|+|R_0><L_0|),
    C_b=P C_g P^*,                                             (17)

where P exchanges L_j and R_j. The graph is a path with ordering L_d,...,L_0,R_0,...,R_d. Only the two outermost diagonal coefficients differ between C_g and C_b. The hopping row sums are at most one. Also

    -gamma I <=C_g,C_b<=(1+gamma)I.

Promote this to the spin Hamiltonians

    H_s=sum_x [1+(C_s)_(xx)] n_x
        +sum_(edges xy) (C_s)_(xy)(sigma_x^+ sigma_y^-+sigma_y^+ sigma_x^-)
        +sum_(edges xy) n_x n_y,     s in {g,b}.                 (18)

The density-density terms have strength one. Hence these are genuinely interacting Hamiltonians, although the requested prediction lies in the invariant vacuum-plus-one-excitation sector, where those interaction terms vanish. We do not claim a hard interacting finite-density prediction theorem.

Every edge has nonzero hopping. Reflection exchanges H_g and H_b exactly, proving full many-body isospectrality. In particular,

    H_b-H_g=a_delta(n_(L_d)-n_(R_d)),                                 (19)

up to exchanging the labels g and b.

On a path, the Jordan-Wigner transformation maps the hopping and onsite part to a quadratic fermion Hamiltonian with one-particle matrix I+C_s. Diagonalizing this matrix writes that part as sum_j lambda_j f_j^dagger f_j with lambda_j>=1-gamma. Therefore it is at least (1-gamma)N. The remaining density interactions are nonnegative. The vacuum is consequently the unique ground state, with gap at least 1-gamma>0.9. This proves the stated gap without an assumed many-body gap conjecture.

### 5.4 The prediction gap

Set a=L_0. First omit the central hopping in the one-excitation sector. Equation (12) gives

    sup_t ||U_(H_g)(t)psi-U_K(t)psi|| <=sqrt(2rho)<eta/2.

Equation (13), tested by X_a after undoing the candidate phase, gives

    D(rho_(H_b)^a(t_star),rho_K^a(t_star)) >=1-rho.

Restoring the connecting bond changes the one-excitation generator by operator norm gamma. Duhamel's identity bounds its state-vector effect by gamma t, and hence its trace-distance effect by at most gamma t. Since gamma T=eta/16,

    E_T(H_g) <eta/2+eta/16<eta,
    E_T(H_b) >=1-rho-eta/16>3/4.                                (20)

Notice that the common gap is a gap above the vacuum. The two long-lived responses use closely spaced EXCITED energies. A vacuum gap does not rule out this mechanism.
