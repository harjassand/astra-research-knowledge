7. LINEARLY TRANSFORMED ISOTROPIC QUARTICS DO NOT EXPLAIN THESE RAYS

Consider any two-sided SO(2)-invariant rank-one-convex quartic
Q(A)=a0|A|^4+b0|A|^2 det A+c0(det A)^2, and invertible real P,D.
The operation Q(P A D) preserves rank-one convexity and quasiconvexity
when Q is quasiconvex. Average this function under left SO(2), obtaining E.
On rank-one matrices A=x tensor n,

 E(x tensor n)=a0 k_P |x|^4 [n^T D D^T n]^2,

where k_P>0. Rank-one convexity forces a0>=0; if a0=0, evaluating the
e11 acoustic form at diagonal matrices forces b0=0, so E is a multiple
of j^2.

For J_r, the same rank-one restriction is

 |x|^4 {alpha(n1^2+n2^2)^2+beta n1^4}.

With alpha>0 this is a square of a real quadratic form in n only when
beta=0. Indeed the odd coefficients force its mixed term to vanish,
and the n1^2 n2^2 coefficient then forces its two diagonal coefficients
to be equal. In the allowed sharp ranges beta=0 occurs only at r=1.

At r=1 the restriction is radial, forcing D D^T to be scalar. Then
Q(P A D) and its left average are right-SO(2)-invariant. J_1 is not:
its expansion contains (u-v)j/2. Thus E cannot be proportional to J_1.

Since J_r is extreme in K, a positive finite sum of these left averages
could equal J_r only if every nonzero average were proportional to J_r.
The preceding obstruction rules this out for every allowed r. Therefore
neither a single two-sided-invariant quartic under P A D nor positive
finite combinations of such transformed quartics prove its QC by simple
inheritance. Adding arbitrary convex quartics does not rescue that finite
decomposition: after left averaging, extremality would force a convex
multiple of J_r, contradicting Section 5.

This is a precise exclusion of these standard linear-transform and
positive-combination routes. It does not exclude a different analytic
consequence of the literature, nonpolynomial constructions, nonlinear
changes of variables, or a future direct QC proof.

Transposition must not be treated as a general QC-preserving operation.
For this isotropic source class specifically Q(B^T)=Q(B), so
Q(P A^T D)=Q(D^T A P^T), reducing it to the already covered operation.

8. SCOPE OF THE AUDIT

The structural identities above are independently checked by
verify_structural.py and its exact receipt. They establish ROC,
extremality with the stated ambient cone, nonpolyconvexity, and the
nonexposure distinction. They do not themselves establish QC of any
nonpolyconvex J_r. Other workers' heat certificates have separate
provenance and validation. No historical-first claim is made here.
