## 3. Proving the nonreversible theorem

The second amplification uses the KMS adjoint rather than assuming it is itself broadcastable.

Define

\\[ \Omega\_\sigma(A)=\sigma^{1/2}A\sigma^{1/2}, \qquad T^\sharp=\Omega\_\sigma^{-1}T\_\*\Omega\_\sigma. \\]

Because \\(\sigma\\) is faithful and stationary, \\(T^\sharp\\) is unital, completely positive and \\(\sigma\\)-preserving.

Consider the multiplicative reversibilization

\\[ \Gamma=T^\sharp T. \\]

It is KMS-self-adjoint, positive and contractive.

It is also self-compatible. Indeed,

\\[ \mathcal B\_\Gamma^\*=T^\sharp\circ\mathcal B^\* \\]

is completely positive and unital, and both of its marginal channels equal \\(\Gamma\\).

Theorem A applies to \\(\Gamma\\). Hence there is a physical EB channel \\(\Psi\\) satisfying

\\[ I-\Psi\preceq4(I-\Gamma). \tag{9} \\]

Set \\(H_T=(T+T^\sharp)/2\\). The exact identity

\\[ 2(I-H_T)-(I-\Gamma) =(I-T)^\sharp(I-T)\succeq0 \tag{10} \\]

gives

\\[ I-\Gamma\preceq2(I-H_T). \\]

Combining (9) and (10),

\\[ \boxed{ I-\Psi\preceq8(I-H_T). } \\]

This proves Theorem B. \\(\square\\)

The important point is that no claim that \\(T^\sharp\\) is self-compatible is needed. Only the composite \\(T^\sharp T\\) must be self-compatible, and its broadcaster is explicit.
