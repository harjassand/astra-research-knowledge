## Conditional structural-domain mechanism

Use kappa^A_ij = epsilon_ijk h_k. In a nonmagnetic fixed structural state,
Onsager reciprocity allows h(B)=K B+O(B^3). Both h and B are axial vectors.
For every orthogonal structural transformation R, the two determinant signs
cancel and K transforms as an ordinary tensor:

    K' = R K R^T.

Tetragonal D4h symmetry gives K0=diag(a,a,c). A domain with tetragonal director n
has

    K(n)=a I+(c-a) n n^T.

Antiparallel n and -n produce the same tensor. Antiphase variants related solely
by a lost translation do not reverse a homogeneous bulk transport coefficient.
The simple ferromagnetic-domain analogy therefore does not itself establish
cancellation of a field-induced Hall signal in a nonmagnetic material.

### Required effective-medium assumptions

The following conclusions assume weak Hall response, a local diffusive
constitutive law, the same scalar zero-field longitudinal conductivity kappa0
everywhere, and fixed a,c across the intervention. With a prescribed average
gradient E, write the local gradient E+grad(phi). The zero-field corrector is
uniform. At first order in B,

    <j> = -kappa0 E - <kappa^A> E,

because <grad(phi)>=0. Thus arithmetic averaging is exact at this order under
these assumptions, rather than being assumed for a general composite.

Let Q=<n n^T>, tr Q=1. Then

    K_eff = a I+(c-a)Q,
    Bhat . K_eff . Bhat = a+(c-a)<(n.Bhat)^2>.

### Derived constraints

* The projected coefficient is between a and c. Same-sign strictly nonzero
  principal coefficients cannot cancel exactly in this model.
* **Same-sign is not implied by symmetry, Onsager reciprocity, or positive entropy
  production.** The antisymmetric thermal conductivity is nondissipative.
* A counterexample is K0=diag(1,1,-2). Equal axis populations give K_eff=0.
* Isotropic populations give K_eff=(2a+c)I/3. If a,c have the same sign, the
  retained magnitude is at least one third of the largest principal magnitude.
  A strongly textured population can nevertheless give a tiny signal if one
  principal coefficient is tiny. “Random” must not be substituted for a measured
  population.
* Exact isotropic cancellation needs c=-2a. That model predicts a zero in the
  single-domain conventional Hall projection at cos^2(theta)=1/3, approximately
  54.7356 degrees from the tetragonal axis.

These are deductions under assumptions, not measurements of a and c.

## Sharpest unperformed discriminating protocol

For the three ideal SrTiO3 tetragonal variants, n is along pseudocubic x,y,z.
For B along [111], (n.Bhat)^2=1/3 in every domain, independent of populations.
Therefore

    S_[111] = (2a+c)/3.

Within the specified model, annealing-induced repopulation cannot change the
low-field conventional Hall-conductivity slope in a transport plane perpendicular
to [111]. A change would require at least one assumption to fail. It would not
by itself identify which microscopic mechanism changed.

More generally, full-tensor measurements in three orthogonal conventional Hall
geometries give

    Sx+Sy+Sz = tr K_eff = 2a+c,

which pure orientation redistribution preserves even for continuously rotated
domains under arithmetic averaging. Trace preservation is necessary but not
sufficient for that explanation.
