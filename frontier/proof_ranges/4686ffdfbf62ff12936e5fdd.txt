Internally prestressed separator: explicit construction and resource bounds
Root invention and continuum derivation, 12 October 2026

Status: independently reconstructed existing architecture, rejected as a new
mechanism. The materials worker's subsequent full methods/supplement retrieval
found that Cui et al. already use a contracting NiTiNb shape-memory alloy ring
around the LLZO pellet. This corrects the scout's earlier implication of a
removable external fixture. Root's circular tensile-ring load path is therefore
directly anticipated by the strongest relevant demonstration. Numerical material
values below are declared illustrative assumptions, not acquired measurements.

Desired operation
------------------
Maintain approximately 330 MPa biaxial in-plane compression in an ion-conducting
garnet separator through a self-contained load path. Recent
experiments support stress steering of lithium cracks in particular LLZTO
geometries. They do not establish that 330 MPa is sufficient for all chemistry,
defects, current, capacity or cycle life. Root's proposed implementation is a
tiling of compressed ion-conducting cores, each surrounded by a closed tensile
ring, rather than prescribing a favorable stress field without its load path.
The basic ring operation is already the published experimental method. Tiling
does not establish a new operation or an improvement over that method.

Exact necessary balance
-----------------------
Let a bounded composite body be at static equilibrium, div(sigma)=0, with zero
external traction and no body force. Assume the Cauchy stress is symmetric and
interfaces transmit traction. For each i,j, the divergence theorem gives

  integral_Omega sigma_ij dV
    = integral_boundary x_i * traction_j dS = 0.

Thus an entire free-standing single-phase separator cannot have sigma_xx<0
everywhere (or sigma_yy<0 everywhere). Residual surface compression is balanced
by tension elsewhere. This does not prohibit useful local compression.

If ceramic volume fraction f has sigma_xx,sigma_yy<=-P, and all remaining
load-bearing material has tensile normal stress <=S, then necessarily

  f*P <= (1-f)*S,      f <= S/(P+S).                         (1)

Void fractions cannot carry the balancing stress. Equation (1) is necessary,
not sufficient: maximum principal stress, fracture, fatigue, interfaces and
electrochemical stability may impose stricter conditions. Uniform thermal
expansion in a free homogeneous body supplies no residual stress.

Constructive circular cell
--------------------------
Take a ceramic disk of radius a and a same-thickness elastic annular ring with
outer radius b. Work in small-strain isotropic plane stress, with no body force,
outer radial traction zero, and ideal frictionless interference contact giving
pressure P at a. Perfectly bonded phases with different thickness strains require
a three-dimensional interface correction; that model is not asserted here.
The exact classical axisymmetric solution is

  ceramic: sigma_rr=sigma_theta_theta=-P,
  ring:    sigma_rr=A-B/r^2, sigma_theta_theta=A+B/r^2,
  A=P*a^2/(b^2-a^2), B=A*b^2.

The ceramic therefore has the desired uniform in-plane compression in this
ideal model; the tensile ring closes its force path. With f=a^2/b^2,

  max ring hoop stress = P*(1+f)/(1-f).

A ring tensile limit S requires S>P and

  f <= (S-P)/(S+P),                                        (2)

which is stricter than the general balance bound. S is an allowable principal
tensile stress, not automatically a measured uniaxial yield stress. At the ring's
inner edge the principal stresses are (-P,S,0); a ductile von Mises criterion
would instead test sqrt(S^2+P^2+P*S). Plane-stress displacement
compatibility requires an initial radial interference delta satisfying

  delta/a = P*[(1-nu_c)/E_c
                + ((1+f)/(1-f)+nu_r)/E_r].                 (3)

Indeed the disk contracts by P*a*(1-nu_c)/E_c and the inner ring expands by
P*a*[((1+f)/(1-f)+nu_r)/E_r]. Their sum is the required interference.
