Candidate theorem: robust binary tracking along a supplied direction schedule

INPUT / GAME. Fix v_1,...,v_N in the Euclidean unit ball of R^d. The complete
direction schedule is supplied before compilation. At round t a target
a_t in [-alpha,alpha] is revealed, the controller chooses epsilon_t in
{-1,+1}, and then an adversary chooses xi_t in [-eta,eta], after seeing
epsilon_t. The actual error increment is (epsilon_t-a_t+xi_t)v_t. Exact
past error state is available before the next decision. Targets and errors
may depend on the entire preceding transcript. Put m=1-alpha-eta>0.

CLAIM. There is a deterministic causal sign policy, depending on the full
direction schedule, for which every prefix error has norm at most
C m^-3 sqrt(d), with C absolute and independent of N. This is an existence
theorem. No efficient general compiler is supplied by the analytic proof.

Source input: the filtered-path energy lemma of OpenAI/math family 097.
This note reconstructs its use and strengthens its sign-selection lemma.

1. A robust interval lemma

Let beta>=0, eta>=0, and ell>0 with ell+beta<3+eta. If an open interval
I=(A,B) lies in (-ell,ell) and has length greater than 2(1+eta), then for
every q in [-beta,beta] at least one sign epsilon has
  q+epsilon+[-eta,eta] subset I.
The sign is chosen before the point in [-eta,eta].

Proof. Suppose both proposed closed intervals fail containment. If
B<=q-1+eta, or A>=q+1-eta, the length of I is less than or equal to
ell+beta-1+eta<2+2eta. Otherwise failure of the negative interval forces
A>=q-1-eta, and failure of the positive one forces B<=q+1+eta. Again
B-A<=2+2eta, a contradiction. Strict interval length ensures containment
of the closed perturbation interval, including its endpoints.

2. Robust coordinate-section transform

Suppose K is bounded, open, symmetric and convex, and for every positive
diagonal Q its first Dirichlet energy satisfies lambda_Q(K)<=kappa^2 trQ.
The source's simultaneous-density separation proof gives one f>=0,
supported in K, ||f||_2=1, with ||partial_i f||_2<=kappa for every i.

For coordinate e, lift to B={(y,s):y+se in K, |s|<ell}; let I_y be its
vertical fiber, L(y)=|I_y|, and D_r={y:L(y)>2r}. These are bounded symmetric
open convex sets when nonempty. Rearrange the lifted density
Psi(y,s)=(2ell)^-1/2 f(y+se) 1[|s|<ell] in each s-fiber. L2 contraction of
one-dimensional symmetric rearrangement preserves every horizontal energy.
The source's exact layer-cake formula is
  E|s| = (8ell)^-1 int_{[-ell,ell]^2} int min(f(y+re)^2,f(y+r'e)^2) dy dr dr'.
The translate estimate gives
  E|s| >= ell/2 (1-2ell kappa).

Choose parameters so this lower bound exceeds r=1+eta. The source's
Minkowski convexity argument makes h_Q(u)=lambda_Q(D_u) nondecreasing and
convex. Slice Rayleigh quotients and Jensen then give
  lambda_Q(D_r)<=kappa^2 trQ.
The robust interval lemma implies that for every y in D_r and every
|q|<=beta, one epsilon has y+(q+epsilon+xi)e in K for ALL |xi|<=eta.
Thus T_e(K)=D_r preserves all diagonal energy bounds and supports the
correct decision order: target/shift, then sign, then adversarial error.

Construct K_N=K, K_(t-1)=T_(e_t)(K_t) backwards for the distinct coefficient
axes. At step t, given x in K_(t-1) and an allowed current shift q, choose
the robust sign. Every subsequently chosen xi leaves the updated point

it does not choose a separate signing after seeing the complete error path.

3. Apply the transform to the source's contracting filter

Choose
  delta=m/4,
  beta=alpha+delta,
  ell=2+2eta+m/2,
  kappa=m/(16ell^2).
Then ell+beta=3+eta-m/4 and
  ell/2 (1-2ell kappa)=1+eta+3m/16 >1+eta.
Because 0<=alpha,eta and alpha+eta<1, ell is bounded by an absolute constant.

The source filtered energy lemma supplies absolute A0,B0,H0. Let
  b_t=sigma v_t,
  C_t=(I-b_t b_t^T)^(1/2)=I-gamma_t b_t b_t^T,
  gamma_t=1/(1+sqrt(1-||b_t||^2)).
Define R_0 as projection onto d initial coordinates and
R_t x=C_t R_(t-1)x+b_t x_(d+t). The filtered body D satisfies
  |x_j|<B0, ||R_t x||<A0 sqrt(d) for all t,
  lambda_Q(D)<=H0 trQ.

Set the first d coefficient coordinates to zero. The predictor row p_t is
  p_t x=gamma_t b_t^T R_(t-1)x^0,
where x^0 zeroes the initial coordinates. It uses only earlier noise axes.
For the contribution r_i of one earlier noise coordinate, symmetry gives
  ||r_(i-1)||^2-||C_i r_(i-1)||^2=(b_i^T r_(i-1))^2.
Therefore each column of the predictor matrix has squared norm at most
sigma^2 and sum_t p_t Q p_t^T<=sigma^2 trQ for every diagonal Q.

Take Dstar=max(1,sqrt(2H0)/kappa),
sigma=min(1/2,kappa delta/(sqrt(2) pi)), and
  K=(2Dstar D) intersect {|p_t x|<delta for every t}.
The reconstructed Brownian-slab energy bound yields
  lambda_Q(K)<= [H0/Dstar^2+pi^2 sigma^2/delta^2] trQ<=kappa^2 trQ.

At round t the target is revealed. Prescribe
  q_t=clip_delta(p_t x^(t-1))-a_t, so |q_t|<=beta.
Use the robust coordinate policy and then allow the adversary's xi_t.
The assigned coefficient is
  x_(d+t)=epsilon_t-a_t+xi_t+clip_delta(p_t x^(t-1)).
The final x lies in K for every adversarial play. Since p_t depends only
on earlier coordinates, its final bound shows clipping was never active.
Consequently the contractions cancel exactly:
  R_t x=R_(t-1)x+b_t(epsilon_t-a_t+xi_t).
Thus R_t x=sigma sum_(i<=t)(epsilon_i-a_i+xi_i)v_i simultaneously for all t.
Membership in 2Dstar D gives the norm bound 2Dstar A0 sqrt(d)/sigma.
Here Dstar=O(m^-1), sigma^-1=O(m^-2), proving C m^-3 sqrt(d).

The coefficient-history policy can be replaced by a policy depending on
the current actual error state, current target and time: the finite-horizon
safety objective depends on the past only through its current state after
the already-safe prefixes. A winning continuation can be selected at every
reachable winning state. This Markovization does not supply an efficient
representation or avoid acquiring the state feedback.

4. Sharp interface obstructions

If alpha+eta>1, let every v_t be one fixed unit vector, take a_t=alpha and
xi_t=-eta. Every possible increment is at most 1-alpha-eta<0, so its norm
grows at least N(alpha+eta-1). Saturation cannot be hidden by the theorem.
Section7 below resolves the boundary alpha+eta=1 by a different fixed-schedule
adversary, with the target still revealed before the sign.

If directions may adapt to the actual state, already d=2,a=xi=0 is
impossible: choose a unit v_t perpendicular to the current state. Then
every sign gives ||s_t||^2=||s_(t-1)||^2+1, hence ||s_N||=sqrt(N).

If errors can be arbitrary transverse vectors, let v_t=e_1 and inject
eta e_2 every round. The second coordinate grows Neta. Scalar errors
along the supplied direction are essential. Exact direction calibration
and adequate state feedback are acquired physical interfaces, not free
consequences of the mathematical construction.

5. A constructive fixed-dimension, finite-precision compiler

This gives a general finite-horizon algorithm, rather than merely proposing
to evaluate the analytic backward bodies. Its dependence on dimension is
exponential. Let 0<tau<=1 and let the supplied v_t be rational unit-ball
vectors. Put
  M=ceil(8Nd/tau), K=ceil(max(8N/tau,8/m)), delta=1/K.
Round each M v_t coordinate to the nearest integer to obtain r_t, and use
  vtilde_t=2r_t/(2M+d).
The elementary triangle inequality gives ||vtilde_t||<=1 and
||vtilde_t-v_t||<=d/M<=tau/(8N).

Acquire scalar approximations ahat_t and xihat_t with absolute errors at
most delta/8; the latter is acquired after actuation, before the following
decision. Set
  atilde_t=delta round(ahat_t/delta),
  xitilde_t=delta round((xihat_t+atilde_t-ahat_t)/delta).
Ties may be resolved either way. Then
  |atilde_t-a_t|<=5delta/8,
  |(epsilon-atilde+xitilde)-(epsilon-a+xi)|<=3delta/4.
The rounded target index is at most ceil(K alpha)+1 in absolute value.
Also |xihat+atilde-ahat|<=eta+5delta/8, so the rounded offset index is at
most ceil(K eta)+1. These finite alphabets include every possible rounded
play, including arbitrary measurement errors within the promised bound.

Compile the finite game containing ALL target/error multiples of delta with
absolute integer indices at most ceil(K alpha)+1 and ceil(K eta)+1,
respectively. Its target and error bounds are at most alpha+2delta and
eta+2delta, so the remaining margin is at least m-4delta>=m/2.
The analytic theorem therefore supplies a winning radius
  R=O(m^-3 sqrt(d))
for every horizon N, with an absolute implicit constant.

Every virtual state lies on the lattice (1/L)Z^d, L=K(2M+d). At a proposed
radius B, enumerate its ball S_B and perform the EXACT backward recursion
  W_N=S_B,
  W_(t-1)={s in S_B: for every target A there exists epsilon such that
              s+(epsilon-A+Xi)vtilde_t is in W_t for every error Xi}.
All shifts are integer lattice shifts; all ball tests compare integer
squared norms. If zero is winning, retain an action for each winning
state/target. Otherwise double B. The analytic theorem guarantees that
doubling terminates at B=O(m^-3 sqrt(d)). Each retained policy is independently
checked by its universal successor conditions. The finite game itself is
standard robust dynamic programming, not a new synthesis algorithm.

The state count is at most S=(1+2BL)^d. A direct implementation takes
O(N S K^2 d) integer operations and O(N S K) action bits plus polynomial
input/transition storage; the integer bit lengths are polynomial in the
input description, logN, log(1/tau), log(1/m), and logB. Thus, for fixed d,
it is polynomial in N, tau^-1 and m^-1, rather than exponential in N.
It is not polynomial in d, not logarithmic in N, and not a polynomial-bit
precision algorithm in tau because its state count is polynomial in tau^-1.

The real and virtual prefix errors differ by at most
  N(2d/M+3delta/4)<=11tau/32<tau
using |epsilon-a+xi|<=2 and ||vtilde||<=1. Therefore the compiled finite
policy tracks the continuous original game within B+tau. N feedback
measurements at absolute precision 1/(8K), with O(log(N/tau)+log(1/m))
bits for bounded scalar inputs, are required; this is not a theorem
obtaining unknown actuator errors without measuring them.

6. Infinite periodic finite-alphabet operation

For a period of P rational directions and rational finite target/error
alphabets, phase-augment the lattice state. The robust predecessor map
  F(W)_p={s safe: forall target exists epsilon forall error,
                    successor in W_((p+1) mod P)}
is monotone. Starting from all safe phase states gives a decreasing chain;
it stabilizes after at most P|S| strict state removals. A fixed point with
the phase-zero origin is an independently checkable controller for every
horizon, with one table lookup per step. Safety-game fixed points are
classical. The source-derived value here is the radius existence argument
and its quantitative dimension/margin dependence, not the fixed-point
algorithm itself.

The exact implemented example in safety_game.py uses the repeated period
  (1,0),(3/5,4/5),(0,1),(4/5,-3/5),
and target/error alphabets {-1/4,0,1/4}. Radius 43/20 wins indefinitely;
42/20 loses. The invariant certificate checks 140220 exact transitions.
The same-interface nominal greedy feedback needs radius 50/20; 49/20
loses. These radii are optimal within the specified integer twentieth
radius grid, not over every real radius or every continuous noise law.

No preselected sign string can replace feedback in this experiment or in
general. For any fixed signs and any direction w, choose actuator offsets
xi_t=+eta sign(w dot v_t) for all t, or their negatives. One of those two
allowed plays has absolute terminal projection at least
eta sum_t |w dot v_t|. On a repeated spanning schedule this grows linearly
with the horizon. This is an exact open-loop versus feedback separation;
ordinary sigma-delta feedback must still be included as the competent
baseline, as the finite comparison above does.

7. Blind current targets and a sharp boundary obstruction

The positive theorem is stronger than its initial game statement: the
current target need not be revealed before the sign. Combine -a_t+xi_t
into a single postdecision drift zeta_t with |zeta_t|<=gamma=alpha+eta<1.
Apply the construction with alpha'=0, eta'=gamma. Its same radius bound
C(1-gamma)^-3 sqrt(d) applies. Feedback of the actual error is still
required before the following round; the target is not learned for free.

Conversely, alpha+eta=1 is impossible in general even when targets ARE
revealed before the sign. The following fixed rational direction schedule
strengthens a boundary observation sent by root, and independently checks
its decision order for every alpha,eta>=0 with alpha+eta=1.

Block j contains the fixed rational unit vectors
  w(t)=((1-t^2)/(1+t^2),2t/(1+t^2)), t=k/(16j), -16j<=k<=16j,
and their negatives. This is a 1/(16j)-net of the circle: t in [-1,1]
and negation cover the circle, ||w'(t)||<=2, and nearest grid distance
in t is at most 1/(32j). Its order is fixed independently of the controller.

At current state s and direction v, a non-activating round chooses
  a=-alpha sign(s dot v), xi=eta sign(s dot v).
Both choices respect the decision order (the target uses only past state
and the current supplied direction). The actual coefficient is
epsilon+sign(s dot v), hence either zero or twice the outward sign.
The squared norm never decreases; a nonzero step increases it by at least4.

Inductively require squared norm at least j/2 at the end of block j.
If that level is already reached, use non-activating rounds throughout.
Otherwise the starting squared norm is at least (j-1)/2, and any nonzero
non-activating step reaches the level immediately. If none occurs, the
state stays fixed. The rational net then supplies a scheduled direction
with |s dot v|<=(1+eta)/4, because ||s||<sqrt(j/2).
At that direction reveal a=0 and, after the sign, choose xi=eta epsilon.
The squared norm increases by at least (1+eta)^2/2>=1/2. Thereafter use
non-activating rounds. This proves the induction against every controller,
including one knowing the entire direction schedule.

After J blocks the squared norm is at least J/2. Their total length is
32J^2+34J, yielding an unbounded Omega(N^(1/4)) lower bound on this fixed
schedule. Together with the saturation example for alpha+eta>1, strict
margin is the sharp qualitative feasibility region for this game.

center may differ from the next true state by delta per coordinate.

For each phase and sign enumerate every integer offset m such that
  m delta in (epsilon+[-1/2,1/2])v + [-2delta,2delta]^2.
Exact rational interval intersection computes this finite set, including
boundary cases. It overapproximates every physically possible next
measurement center: current state uncertainty and next feedback error
each contribute delta. A cell is safe only if all its corners lie in
the radius-three Euclidean ball. Greatest-fixed-point safety synthesis
returns a phase/cell sign table.
