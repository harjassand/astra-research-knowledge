## 5. Acquisition from actions, precision, and concrete control

The coordinates need not be measured using separate coherent sensors: exact action **jets under known probes** suffice. This is an operational mathematical interface, whose finite-precision cost is substantial.

For one fixed nonzero coupling vector `κ`, initialize repeated identical states and use constant unit control. If every `κ_i≠0`,

\[
p_i=-\frac{\dot a_i(0)}{2\kappa_i},\qquad
R_i=-\frac{\ddot a_i(0)}{2\kappa_i}+\kappa_i s_i n.
\tag{12}
\]

For general `K`, choose `d` linearly independent probe vectors `v^(j)` with every component of `Kv^(j)` nonzero. Such vectors exist because a finite union of row hyperplanes cannot contain an open set. The corresponding second derivatives give `R_i·v^(j)` and recover each row `R_i` by a linear solve. This needs `d` distinct identically prepared probe settings, control calibration, all relevant modal-action channels, and a conditioned probe matrix. The symmetric finite-interval implementation below requires fresh forward/reverse copies, hence at least `2d` such probe runs before averaging. The procedure does not acquire a copy's unknown initial state without disturbing it. Repeated preparations, weak-probe extrapolation, or an independently validated reset mechanism are indispensable.

For one scalar action with deterministic measurement error at most `ε_a`, symmetric first/second derivative estimates at `±τ` have errors

\[
|\widehat{\dot a}-\dot a|\le M_3\tau^2/6+\epsilon_a/\tau,
\quad
|\widehat{\ddot a}-\ddot a|\le M_4\tau^2/12+4\epsilon_a/\tau^2,
\tag{13}
\]

assuming known bounds `|a'''|≤M_3, |a''''|≤M_4` on that probe interval. Such bounds can be obtained explicitly from the known energy: for constant coupling with `κ=max_i|k_i|`, conservative choices are

\[
M_3=24\kappa^3 E^{5/2},\qquad M_4=120\kappa^4 E^3.
\tag{13a}
\]

To verify (13a), use the weighted amplitude norm `||u||_E²=Σ_i(|x_i|²+|y_i|²)+2|c|²` and write the quadratic field as a symmetric real-bilinear `F(u)=B(u,u)`. The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives `||B(u,w)||_E≤κ||u||_E||w||_E`. Thus `||u'||≤κE`, `||u''||≤2κ²E^{3/2}`, `||u'''||≤6κ³E²`, and `||u''''||≤24κ⁴E^{5/2}` by differentiating the quadratic field. Leibniz differentiation of `|x_i|²` gives the stated scalar constants. They are loose bounds, not assumed unknown sensor inputs.

Reverse-time samples can be implemented by fresh copies with the opposite coupling only in the drift-free model (1). The optimal curvature bound from (13) is `2√(M_4 ε_a/3)`; recovering `R` further amplifies errors by `1/(2|κ_i|)` and probe-matrix inverse conditioning. Finite nonzero `τ` supplies an approximation, not exact derivatives. Statistical averaging has additional sample costs determined by its noise model. No conditioning-independent or sample-efficient sensor theorem follows.

The generic dimension floor in Corollary 3.1 also applies to the number of continuous scalar measurement values in an exact finite sensing transcript: fewer than `3r+1` cannot identify arbitrary generic predictive states. For known constants the floor is `2r`. This statement assumes the preparation/control/measurement transcript depends continuously on the initial state; it is not a lower bound for arbitrary discontinuous encodings or for a single requested scalar output.

Two concrete actuation rules use exactly this state:

* **Instantaneous transfer:** with `||v||₂≤V`, `dot n=2v·P`, so the instantaneous maximum is `2V||P||₂`. The regularized feedback `v=V P/√(η²+||P||²)` is smooth for `η>0` and makes `dot n≥0`. It stalls at `P=0`, including every zero-central-mode initial condition, so it is not a global reachability or optimal-transfer theorem.
* **Starting from `c=0`:** form the symmetric positive semidefinite `d×d` matrix `T=K^TR`, whose entries are `Re(B_α bar B_β)`. Its rank is at most two. The startup curvature is `ddot n=2v^TTv`; a finite initial pulse along a top eigenvector maximizes it at `2V²λ_max(T)`. `T=0` means all admitted collective amplitudes vanish and the entire state is an equilibrium. Eigenvector selection at degeneracy is a prescribed-pulse choice, not an unexamined smooth-feedback claim.

For the original two-triad boundary pair `(b_1,b_2)=(1,1)` versus `(1,-1)`, this controller detects startup curvatures `8` versus `0` under equal couplings. It demonstrates phase-sensitive prediction and a local control choice; preparation of either loop phase and implementation of the coupling signs/strengths are separate physical tasks.

## 6. Damping, detuning, and changing couplings

Add known linear terms

\[
\dot x_i=-(\gamma_{xi}+i\omega_{xi})x_i-ik_i\bar y_i c,
\quad
\dot y_i=-(\gamma_{yi}+i\omega_{yi})y_i-ik_i\bar x_i c,
\]
\[
\dot c=-(\gamma_c+i\omega_c)c-i\sum_i k_i b_i.
\tag{14}
\]

Set `A_i=γ_xi+γ_yi+i(ω_xi+ω_yi)`, `A_c=γ_c+iω_c`. Then

\[
\dot b_i=-A_i b_i-ik_i s_i c,\qquad
\dot z_i=-(A_i+\bar A_c)z_i+i\sum_jk_jb_i\bar b_j-ik_i s_i n.
\tag{15}
\]

Nonnegative damping makes (6) nonincreasing, so the same global existence argument holds under locally integrable coefficients. Bounded negative damping gives a Grönwall energy bound on finite intervals. Arbitrary additive forcing is a different interface and is not covered.

**Real closure failure under even uniform detuning.** If the pair-center detuning is `δ=(ω_x+ω_y)-ω_c`, then `dot p` contains `-δ Re z`. With `r=1`, `a=e=n=1`, `K=1`, the states `z=+1` and `z=-1` have identical old `(actions,p=0,R=1)`. At `δ=1,k=1`, their `dot p` values are `-2` and `0`. Their action accelerations differ. Consequently, the half-sized real closure is not sufficient once this reflection-breaking drift is admitted.

**An exact spectral-hull construction.** For fixed diagonal `D=diag(A_i)`, let the complex linear space

\[
U=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{K,DK,D^2K,\ldots,D^{r-1}K\}
\tag{16}
\]

have dimension `m`, and choose columns of `W` as a basis. Include `z_i` and `Q_iα=b_i overline(B_α)` with `B_α=Σ_j W_jα b_j`. If `DW=WM`, the column vector `B=W^Tb` satisfies `dot B=-M^TB-iLc`. With `Z_α=Σ_jW_jα z_j`, `L_α=Σ_jW_jα k_j s_j`, direct differentiation gives

\[
\dot Q_{i\alpha}=-A_iQ_{i\alpha}
 -\sum_\beta\overline{M_{\beta\alpha}}Q_{i\beta}
 -ik_i s_i\bar Z_\alpha+i\bar L_\alpha z_i.
\tag{17}
\]

Equation (15) uses the `Q` columns expressing each admitted real `k` in `U`. This is an exact globally polynomial closure of size `O(rm)`, including damping/detuning, with no division by an amplitude. For time-dependent diagonal coefficients, use a common space invariant under every admitted diagonal matrix; its joint spectral classes determine the hull. With `g` distinct fixed diagonal values, `m` is the sum of the ranks of `K` restricted to their coordinate groups, at most `min(r,gd)`.

For distinct `A_i` and one column `K` whose entries are all nonzero, the Vandermonde matrix in (16) has rank `r`. Thus generic inhomogeneous linear drift destroys small-`d` savings **within this collective-linear-sketch construction**. This is not a lower bound on every nonlinear predictor: the O(r) amplitude model remains available. Uniform `A_i` makes `m=d`; uniform nonzero pair-center detuning still requires the complex rather than real closure.

**Proposition 6.1 (sharp detuned information).** For fixed coefficients (14), suppose every row of `K` is nonzero and every pair-center detuning `δ_i=Im(A_i+bar A_c)` is nonzero. Then `(actions,z,Q_U)` is exactly the all-action behavioral quotient, where `U` is (16). Its displayed coordinates remain redundant; the claim concerns fibers, not a quadratic dimension lower bound.

**Proof.** Sufficiency follows from (15)–(17) and the damped energy bound. For necessity, action behavior under arbitrary piecewise constant controls determines the values of all iterated Lie derivatives of the outputs under drift `f_0` and the control vector fields `f_α`. To see this directly, concatenate short constant-control segments and differentiate the resulting output with respect to their separate durations at zero; affine combinations of constant controls recover each drift/control word. First, `L_{f_α}a_i=-2K_iα p_i` recovers `p_i` using a nonzero row entry. Then

\[
L_{f_0}p_i=-\operatorname{Re}(A_i+\bar A_c)p_i-\delta_i\operatorname{Re}z_i
\]

recovers `Re z_i`. Writing `Q_iα=R_iα+iJ_iα`, the control derivatives satisfy `L_{f_α}p_i=R_iα-K_iα s_i n` and `L_{f_α}Re z_i=-J_iα`; thus every complex `Q` column for `K` is determined. Finally, for any fixed weight column `w`, the drift alone gives

\[
L_{f_0}Q_{i,w}=-A_iQ_{i,w}-Q_{i,Dw}.
\]

Repeated drift derivatives recover the columns for `DK,D²K,...`, hence the whole hull. Equality of all action responses therefore forces equality of the asserted state, including at zero amplitudes. ∎

This additionally explains why arbitrarily small nonzero detuning can change exact indistinguishability: the reflection symmetry is broken. Estimating the recovered coordinate from a drift derivative divides by `δ_i`, so no uniform finite-precision sensing claim as `δ_i→0` follows.

If `K=K(t)` is differentiable and real, differentiating `Q` adds `b_i overline(Σ_j dot K_jα b_j)`. A moving basis with `dot K=K C(t)` closes with known coefficient mixing. A genuinely moving span generally needs its derivative directions too; instantaneous rank alone is insufficient. More generally, a differentiable basis `W(t)` closes (14) whenever `dot W-DW=W C(t)` and every admitted coupling lies in `span W(t)`. This is a dynamical invariant-subspace condition, not a free way to hold arbitrary real couplings in a small space under arbitrary detuning.
