4. Exact agreement has a polynomial local certificate
The contrast is explicit.
Consider the common ambient class of excitation-preserving Hamiltonians that fix the vacuum and have one-excitation restriction
\[
H\big|_{\text{one excitation}}=I+C,\qquad \|C\|\le2.
\]
The hard chains belong to this class. It also contains nontrivial exact-null models differing from \(K\) outside the predictive sector.
Choose
\[
h=\min\{\tau,1/2\}\le T.
\]
Prepare \(\psi\), evolve for time \(h\), undo the known candidate phase on qubit \(a\), and measure \(X_a\). Let \(p\) be the probability of the minus outcome.
Then
\[
\boxed{
E_T(H)\le \min\left\{1,\frac{2T}{h}\sqrt p\right\}.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Proof
Let \(\nu\) be the spectral measure of \(C\) at the initially excited site. Write
\[
F(t)=\langle a|e^{-itC}|a\rangle
=\int e^{-itx}\,d\nu(x).
\]
The experiment gives
\[
p=\frac12\int[1-\cos(hx)]\,d\nu(x).
\]
Because \(|hx|\le1\),
\[
p\ge \frac{h^2}{8}\int x^2\,d\nu(x).
\]
Meanwhile, the squared distance between the full state vectors obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|U_H(t)\psi-U_K(t)\psi\|^2
&=1-\Re F(t)\\
&\le\frac{t^2}{2}\int x^2\,d\nu(x).
\end{aligned}
\]
Taking reduced states cannot increase trace distance, so
\[
E_T^2\le \frac{4T^2p}{h^2}.
\]
This proves (5).
Consequently, zero rejections in
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
=
\left\lceil
\frac{4T^2}{h^2\varepsilon^2}
\log\frac1\alpha
\right\rceil
}
\tag{6}
\]
shots give a sound \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy certificate with error probability \(\alpha\). Exact agreement gives \(p=0\).
The procedure uses one-qubit preparation, a one-qubit phase correction and one-qubit measurement. It does not learn the Hamiltonian or simulate a many-body candidate.
But it is not genuinely tolerant: it may reject an already accurate model. The lower bound shows that repairing this defect can be fundamentally expensive.
The ambient class permits zero hopping, so exact agreement is a nonempty promise. The lower-bound alternatives themselves have nonzero hopping throughout. I am not comparing against a vacuous exact-null problem.
