I read the complete Genesis note. Its nominal hard family has a common spectrum and positive spectral weights, and its two real-time return probabilities differ by \(9/32\). The missing bridge is vector agreement, rather than scalar moment agreement alone.
Suppose
\[ A_+^jv=A_-^jv \qquad(0\le j<\ell), \]
and both spectra lie in \([3/8,5/8]\). Expanding around \(A=\tfrac12I\) gives
\[ \|e^{-sA_+}v-e^{-sA_-}v\| \le 2e^{-3s/8}\frac{(s/8)^\ell}{\ell!}. \tag{19} \]
For \(0\le s\le\beta/2\),
\[ \|e^{-sA_+}v-e^{-sA_-}v\| \le 2\min\!\left\{ \frac{(\beta/16)^\ell}{\ell!}, 3^{-\ell} \right\}. \tag{20} \]
For Genesis’s Jacobi realization, matching scalar moments through degree \(m-1\) implies the required vector identities through
\[ \ell=\frac{m+1}{2}. \]
The reason is specific and provable: the coefficients of \(x^j\) in the shared orthonormal-polynomial coordinate basis depend only on moments through degree \(2j\).
Adding the same reference and normalization as Genesis then gives the complete-state bound
\[ \boxed{ \sup_{0\le\tau\le\beta} D(\Psi_+(\tau),\Psi_-(\tau)) \le 2\sqrt3 \min\!\left\{ \frac{(\beta/16)^\ell}{\ell!}, 3^{-\ell} \right\}. } \tag{21} \]
The adaptive pure-snapshot fidelity argument consequently yields
\[ N\ge \frac1{108} \max\!\left\{ 9^\ell, \left(\frac{16\ell}{e\beta}\right)^{2\ell} \right\}. \tag{22} \]
So the nominal Genesis isospectral family does support a complete-quantum-snapshot lower bound of the same exponential order, but now through an explicit proved interface bridge. Its scalar-noise constants were not simply transferred.
This is a subsidiary result. The active-verification theorem does not depend on it.
The new result changes the question from reconstructing a future signal out of one repeatedly observed input to testing the action of the dynamics on a precisely chosen family of inputs.
The proved contribution is:
For a specified initial state and a supplied candidate model, coherent short-filter interventions can certify the entire real-time trajectory with quadratic acquisition cost, without identifying the Hamiltonian outside the predictive subspace.
That statement applies to arbitrary noncommuting finite-dimensional Hamiltonians. It is substantially broader than the original transport construction.
It is not the first Hamiltonian-certification result, nor the first separation between verification and learning. Existing real-time certification and thermal-learning results must remain credited under their own access models. [SIAM](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611978971.53)
