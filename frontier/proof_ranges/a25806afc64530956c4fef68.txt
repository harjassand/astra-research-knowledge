7. Resource and verification accounting
The preparation theorem has the following complete operational scope.
Resource	What is actually required
Input acquisition	Explicit initialization and one-step implementation circuits. An entry-evaluation circuit alone is insufficient.
Representation	One \(m\)-bit or \(m\)-qubit state; no expanded transition matrix or density matrix.
Runtime	\(O(1+KB/\delta^2)\) rule opportunities per prepared output, multiplied by actual rule costs. Numerical \(B\), not merely \(\log B\), is charged.
Memory	One state, one rule’s workspace, initialization workspace, and \(O(\log T+\log K)\) counter bits, plus supplied circuit storage.
Randomness	Padding the rule count and averaging horizon to powers of two permits bounded fair-bit selection with only constant-factor overhead.
Verification	The algorithm does not verify the existence of the common reference, its entropy bound, or universal decoder soundness. These remain explicit promises.


For implementation error, suppose initialization is within distance \(\eta_0\), and every implemented rule is within \(\eta\) of its target in maximum-row TV or half-diamond norm. The prepared-state error is at most
\[
\eta_0+T\eta.
\]
Since each residual changes by at most twice the state error,
\[
r_i(\widetilde\rho)
\leq r_i(\bar\rho_T)+2d(\widetilde\rho,\bar\rho_T).
\]
Taking \(T\geq8KB/\delta^2\), together with
\[
\eta_0\leq\frac{\delta}{16},
\qquad
\eta\leq\frac{\delta}{16T},
\]
leaves a valid residual margin.
These are accuracy requirements, not a claim that arbitrary maps can be synthesized cheaply. Actual synthesis and arithmetic costs must be included.
Finally, estimating an additional bounded observable to error \(\varepsilon\) and failure probability \(\zeta\) requires
\[
O\!\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\log\frac1\zeta\right)
\]
independent prepared outputs and measurements. That multiplies the full preparation cost.
