## A law-mixture operation that is valid

For a probability law \(\mu\) with finite fourth moment, set

\[
 g_\mu(B)=\int [F(B+X)-F(B)]\,d\mu(X),\qquad
 \Delta_\mu=\inf_B g_\mu(B).
\]

If \(\mu=\sum_j\theta_j\mu_j\) is a convex mixture of laws, then \(g_\mu(B)=\sum_j\theta_jg_{\mu_j}(B)\). Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\Delta_\mu\geq\sum_j\theta_j\Delta_{\mu_j}.}
\]

This follows by taking the infimum of the sum: \(\inf_B\sum_j\theta_jg_j(B)\geq\sum_j\theta_j\inf_Bg_j(B)\). It does not require independence between the random variables used to define the component laws. Thus a genuine probability mixture of covered centered, determinant-balanced compatible laws with nonnegative gaps stays nonnegative, with the same optimized affine background.

This operation does not apply to pointwise superposition \(X=X_1+X_2\). The law of a sum is generally not the convex mixture of the laws of its summands; quartic polarization creates mixed terms. Nor does conditioning a gradient on an arbitrary spatial region produce a centered determinant-balanced gradient law: subtracting the local mean changes the boundary data, and the determinant null-Lagrangian identity is tied to the full affine trace. A general decomposition into covered pieces therefore needs a compatibility-preserving construction and a uniform error bound in \(B\), not just a measure-theoretic partition.

The same infimum argument proves superadditivity for exact disjoint-support profile gluing: if zero-boundary compatible profiles have disjoint supports, their background-gap functions add, so the optimized gap of their sum is at least the sum of the individual optimized gaps. This rules out a negative gap created solely by gluing separately nonnegative disjoint profiles through one escaping affine background. It says nothing about a single resonant profile or about decomposing an arbitrary gradient into disjoint profiles.
