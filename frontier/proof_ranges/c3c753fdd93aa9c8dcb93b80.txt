I derived a way to certify a macroscopic transport improvement in an interacting quantum chain using only a local energy-detuning statistic—not the equilibrium distribution, partition function, or a reconstructed many-body state.
The result changes the information interface rather than contradicting the earlier lower bound. It uses controlled local preparations and a specified physical dephasing model. Its central mathematical step is an exact treatment of undamped populations, which prevents a transport certificate from concealing an unknown global relaxation time.
The completed derivation includes the infinite-system, long-time second-moment limit. It is not merely a finite-cluster calculation or a large-noise approximation. However, historical novelty, independent proof validation, and superiority over the strongest specialised computational methods remain unestablished. The broader high-precision prediction capability is still open.

The previous rare-information obstruction survives a decision-only reformulation. Place a decision threshold between the two modified-system expectations in the earlier construction: a successful decision procedure must still distinguish the nearly identical reference states. Asking for one bit instead of a precise expectation does not automatically eliminate the acquisition cost.    research_note
I therefore examined three different routes: equilibrium decision certificates, observable-specific dynamical residuals, and transport certificates built around the distinction between populations and coherences.
The first route already has substantial prior art: certified equilibrium algorithms can return rigorous observable intervals without reconstructing the entire state. The unresolved issue is affordable convergence in the difficult regimes—not inventing the idea of an interval certificate. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-51592-3)
The second route fails when a small residual is converted into an error bound by dividing by an unknown relaxation rate. Separating Hamiltonian evolution from a degenerate dissipator is also established in quantum hypocoercivity; that separation alone is not a new principle. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.11503v2)
The productive route was more specific:
Enforce the undamped population constraints exactly, then certify only the current response required for transport.
This leads to a local-to-macroscopic theorem and an experimentally specified decision procedure.
Consider an infinite, translation-invariant spin chain with
\[ H=-\frac{t}{2}\sum_i(X_iX_{i+1}+Y_iY_{i+1})+H_{\mathrm{diag}}, \]
where \(H_{\mathrm{diag}}\) contains arbitrary bounded, finite-range interactions diagonal in the occupation basis. These interactions need not be weak. They generally do not commute with the hopping part.
The physical dynamics is
\[ \mathcal L O = i[H,O]+\gamma\sum_i(Z_iOZ_i-O), \qquad d=4\gamma>0. \]
Here \(d\) is the decay rate of a coherence differing on two sites. Dephasing is part of the target system, not a mathematical regularizer that can later be removed without cost.
The response is defined in the infinite-temperature product state at mean occupation \(1/2\). With \(n_i=(I-Z_i)/2\) and susceptibility \(\chi=1/4\), define
\[ D= \lim_{T\to\infty} \frac{1}{2T\chi} \sum_x x^2 \left\langle e^{T\mathcal L}(n_x-\tfrac12),\,n_0-\tfrac12 \right\rangle . \]
This is the long-time coefficient of the second moment of the density response. It is not, by definition alone, a Gaussian hydrodynamic limit.
Now define a local quantity:
\[ \Xi = \mathbb E_{\mathrm{uniform}} \left[ \big( H_{\mathrm{diag}}(\eta^{i,i+1}) -H_{\mathrm{diag}}(\eta) \big)^2 \;\middle|\; \eta_i\ne\eta_{i+1} \right]. \]
The configuration \(\eta^{i,i+1}\) is obtained by exchanging two neighbouring occupations. Thus \(\Xi\) is the mean squared energy mismatch encountered by one allowed hop.
Only spins within the interaction range enter this quantity.
For the stated model, the limit defining \(D\) exists, is finite, and satisfies
\[ \boxed{ \frac{2t^2d}{d^2+\Xi} \;\le\; D \;\le\; \frac{2t^2}{d}. } \]
The proof does not assume a global mixing rate, a many-body spectral gap, integrability of the interacting Hamiltonian, or \(d\) much larger than the interaction strength.
The limitation is equally important: it proves a positive finite second-moment transport coefficient, not a convergence rate, Gaussian spreading profile, finite-temperature result, or zero-dephasing limit.
Take
\[ H_{\mathrm{diag}} = U\sum_i(n_i-\tfrac12)(n_{i+1}-\tfrac12) + V\sum_i(n_i-\tfrac12)(n_{i+2}-\tfrac12). \]
The local detuning calculation gives
\[ \Xi=\frac12\big[(U-V)^2+V^2\big]. \]
For \(t=1,\ U=4,\ V=1\), this is \(\Xi=5\). The interaction is not being removed: the nearest-neighbour interaction is four times the hopping amplitude, and the next-nearest-neighbour interaction is nonzero.
At \(d=2\),
\[ \frac49\le D\le1. \]
For the same Hamiltonian at \(d=16\),
\[ D\le\frac18. \]
Consequently,
\[ \boxed{ \frac{D(d=2)}{D(d=16)} \ge\frac{32}{9}>3.55. } \]
This certifies an extensive physical design change—changing the dephasing throughout the system—without calculating either transport coefficient precisely.
Noise-dependent enhancement and suppression of transport are known phenomena. The potential contribution here is the explicit certificate and its information requirements, not the qualitative observation that noise can alter transport. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09367)
The main difficulty is that dephasing damps coherences but leaves every diagonal population observable undamped. Treating dephasing as a gap on the entire operator space would give false results.
Write the negative generator as
\[ B=-\mathcal L=S+A, \qquad A=-i[H,\cdot], \]
where \(S\) is positive and self-adjoint, while \(A\) is skew-adjoint.
Complex conjugation splits Hermitian observables into real-symmetric and imaginary-antisymmetric parts. The current lies in the latter. Let \(P\) project onto diagonal observables, and let \(S^+\) invert dephasing only on its damped subspace.
For a finite periodic chain, define
\[ \kappa_n=\frac1n\langle J_n,B^{-1}J_n\rangle, \]
with the inverse taken outside the conserved fixed-point space.
