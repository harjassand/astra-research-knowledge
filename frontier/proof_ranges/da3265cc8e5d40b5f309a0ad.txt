## 4. Applying the theorem to the entire copy hierarchy

For precision, a *finite-copy program* here permits Shannon constraints, finitely many conditional-copy steps, and ordinary deterministic coordinate operations such as duplication, grouping, restriction, and relabelling. **Taking a limit is not counted as a finite-copy program.**

For a program \(P\) starting on \(E\), let

\[
Q_P(E)
\]

be the cone of initial profiles admitting a polymatroidal realization of that program.

Each \(Q_P(E)\) is a polyhedral cone: the program introduces finitely many rank coordinates, Shannon inequalities, and homogeneous linear equalities, after which the auxiliary coordinates are projected out.

Define

\[
\mathcal K_E=\bigcap_P Q_P(E).
\]

Thus \(h\in\mathcal K_E\) means:

> Every prescribed finite copy construction can be realized starting from \(h\).

This is stronger than merely admitting one copy, or all copies up to some fixed depth.

The important point is to prove that \(\mathcal K\) itself admits copies **whose resulting profiles remain in \(\mathcal K\)**. That does not follow by swapping quantifiers without justification.

### Lemma — A copy can be chosen consistently with every finite continuation

The family \(\mathcal K\) is closed and stable under conditional copying.

### Proof

Closedness follows because each \(Q_P(E)\) is closed.

Now fix \(h\in\mathcal K_E\) and a desired first copy step. Let \(W\) be the set of possible polymatroidal witnesses for that first step.

The set \(W\) is nonempty. All original singleton ranks are fixed, and each copied singleton has the rank of its source. Subadditivity therefore bounds every witness coordinate. Consequently, \(W\) is compact.

For each finite continuation program \(R\), let

\[
W_R\subseteq W
\]

be the first-step witnesses that admit continuation \(R\). This is closed: the relevant extension constraints form a finite polyhedral system, and their projection onto the first-witness coordinates is polyhedral.

The family \(\{W_R\}\) has the finite-intersection property. Indeed, take finitely many continuations \(R_1,\ldots,R_s\). Perform the required first copy and then run the continuations using disjoint fresh auxiliary labels.

When processing a later continuation, requiring a new copy to be independent of all previously introduced variables is stronger than its original requirement. Restricting back to that continuation therefore preserves the required copy constraints.

This combined construction is itself a finite-copy program. Since \(h\in\mathcal K_E\), it has a realization. Its first-step witness lies in

\[
W_{R_1}\cap\cdots\cap W_{R_s}.
\]

Compactness now gives

\[
\bigcap_R W_R\ne\varnothing.
\]

A witness in this intersection admits every finite continuation and hence belongs to \(\mathcal K\). ∎

Restriction and relabelling are inherited by running a program while retaining any unused original coordinates, then restricting its witness. Deterministic coordinate operations are included explicitly in the program definition; their rank pullbacks are fixed rather than freely chosen.

The central theorem therefore applies to \(\mathcal K\).

### Consequence — MAXE does not shrink the closed copy model

Every \(h\in\mathcal K_E\) has the prescribed MAXE completion **inside \(\mathcal K_E\)**.

For generalized MAXE, first duplicate coordinates according to the prescribed map and then protect the designated transversals. Applying the theorem preserves each transversal’s original profile and enforces every separating independence. This is precisely the duplication-plus-completion structure of GMAXE. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12439v2)

It follows that allowing MAXE and GMAXE, even interleaved with further copies, does not exclude any initial profile in \(\mathcal K\).

Equivalently, if \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{copy}}\) denotes the cone of finite-copy-provable linear inequalities, and \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{mixed}}\) additionally permits these MAXE rules, then

\[
\boxed{
\overline{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{copy}}}
=
\overline{\mathcal D_{\mathrm{mixed}}}.
}
\]

The closures here are in the finite-dimensional space of inequality coefficients.

**This is the main structural conclusion.** The specific marginal-gluing rules cannot distinguish a profile that survives the entire finite-copy hierarchy.
