For a supplied linear observable \(c^\top x\), the finite output gives
\[ \boxed{ \left| \sup_{t\ge0}c^\top u(t) -\max_i c^\top v_i \right| \le\epsilon R\|c\|. } \tag{24} \]
A linear functional attains its maximum on a polygonal path with constant tail at a node. This permits margin-based safety verification for every future time, rather than checking a finite collection of simulation times.
The input representation accommodates quadratic energies, unilateral linear constraints, threshold penalties, and internal variables eliminated through convex optimization. The mathematical pathway is to certify long-time behavior of an already validated convex relaxation model without resolving every time scale or expanding every active regime.
However, acquiring a physically faithful energy model and controlling spatial discretization error are separate tasks. The theorem does not establish that a given material, circuit, or continuum equation is adequately described by such a model.
Three attempted extensions expose the remaining obstruction.
Latent variables cannot simply be given their own dynamics. For
\[ F(x)=\min_z\left[\frac12(x-z)^2+\frac12z^2\right]=\frac{x^2}{4}, \]
the observed flow is \(x(t)=e^{-t/2}\) from \(x_0=1\). Joint gradient descent in \(x,z\), even initialized at the minimizing \(z=1/2\), has a different second derivative and therefore a different trajectory. The primal–dual construction avoids this representation failure.
An unknown future input still has to be acquired. For \(x'=u(t)\), where \(u\) is either zero or a unit pulse in one of \(N\) intervals, distinguishing terminal state zero from one requires \(\Omega(N)\) interval queries under that access model. Bounded total input or work does not eliminate the query cost. Supplying the pulse location explicitly would be a different input model.
Persistent oscillation requires a different representation. A rotation can maintain unit norm forever; an eventually constant trajectory cannot approximate it. This does not prove rotations are hard to simulate—their matrix-exponential representation is easy. It shows that dissipation-based time allocation and a constant tail cannot simply be transferred to phase-sensitive dynamics.
Similarly, small multiplicative changes in a nonconvex energy can change which branch a trajectory follows. A small energy discrepancy is not, by itself, a reliable dynamical error measure outside the convex setting.
The completed mathematical consequence is an end-to-end, finite-bit approximation and certification theorem for the entire future of implicitly represented convex relaxation systems. It preserves dynamical equilibrium selection and yields all-time, margin-based observable verification.
The unresolved escalation is a comparably acquired representation that preserves oscillatory phase, nonconvex branch selection, and controlled responses without hiding their computation in an oracle. That is necessary before this approach could support claims about substantially broader technological systems.
