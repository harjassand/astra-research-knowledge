## Interface

Let (\Phi:M_d\to M_d) be CPTP, unital and self-adjoint on the real Hilbert space of Hermitian matrices with inner product (\langle A,B\rangle_\tau=\operatorname{Tr}(AB)/d). Self-compatibility means a CPTP channel (\mathcal B:M_d\to M_d\otimes M_d) has both marginals equal to (\Phi). A common EB comparator is a full-domain measure-and-prepare CPTP map; the operator order is on the Hilbert–Schmidt space.

No positivity assumption on (\Phi) as a Hilbert–Schmidt operator is needed. Kadison Schwarz and trace preservation make a unital CPTP map an HS contraction; self-adjointness gives real spectrum in ([−1,1]). The proof assumes the broadcaster and (\Phi) are supplied as finite-dimensional channels. The Choi reference is only a mathematical representation. It does not assume any output side register or data-acquisition procedure.

## Tree measurement lemma

Symmetrize the supplied broadcaster over its two outputs if needed. Reapply it to every receiver at each level of a full binary tree of depth

\[
m=\lceil\log_2(d^2)\rceil,\qquad K=2^m.
\]

The resulting (K)-output CPTP channel has every one-receiver marginal (\Phi^m). This follows by induction: a leaf at depth (j) has marginal (\Phi^j), since the same broadcaster is applied once more and either child marginal is (\Phi).

For any Hermitian (A=\sum_y a_yP_y), measure its spectral PVM on one receiver. The induced input effects are (E_y=\Phi^m(P_y)), because (\Phi) is Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint. Trace preservation gives (p_y=\tau(E_y)=\tau(P_y)). Define the canonical EB map associated with an input POVM (M=(M_z)) by

\[
\Psi_M(X)=\sum_z\frac{\tau(M_zX)}{\tau(M_z)}M_z,
\]

omitting zero effects. It is CPTP, unital, entanglement-breaking, and Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint. Its quadratic form is the classical score (\langle A,\Psi_M(A)\rangle_\tau=\sum_z\tau(M_zA)^2/\tau(M_z)).

For the receiver's marginal POVM (E_y=\Phi^m(P_y)), self-adjointness and (A=\sum_y a_yP_y) yield

\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_y\frac{\tau(E_yA)^2}{p_y}
-\left(2\langle A,\Phi^m(A)\rangle_\tau-\|A\|_{2,\tau}^2\right)
&=\sum_y\frac{(\tau(E_yA)-a_yp_y)^2}{p_y}\\
&\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

If a joint POVM on all (K) receiver systems refines this marginal measurement, its canonical score is at least the marginal score by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. For any frame (A_1,\ldots,A_r) with (r\le d^2\), measure the spectral PVM of (A_i) on receiver (i), and measure the identity on unused receivers. The product POVM pulled back through the tree channel is one input POVM (M); its canonical EB map satisfies the score inequality for every member of the frame simultaneously.

If only an \(r\)-dimensional real test subspace is relevant, the same argument needs \(K=2^{\lceil\log_2 r\rceil}\) leaves and gives the restricted comparison constant \(2\lceil\log_2 r\rceil\). This follows by applying cone separation inside that subspace. The full channel can be treated on the traceless space of dimension \(d^2-1\), since both channels fix the identity.

## From a frame to one full-channel comparison

Write (Q\succeq0) for any positive operator on the real (d^2)-dimensional Hermitian space, and decompose it as (Q=\sum_{i=1}^r w_i|A_i\rangle\langle A_i|), where (r\le d^2), (w_i\ge0). The frame POVM above gives one (\Psi_M) with

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q\bigl(\Psi_M-[2\Phi^m-I]\bigr)\ge0.
\]

Kadison Schwarz and trace preservation make (\Phi) a Hilbert–Schmidt contraction, so self-adjointness gives spectrum in ([−1,1]). For (λ∈[0,1]), Bernoulli gives (λ^m≥1−m(1−λ)); for (λ<0), the inequality is equality at (m=1), and for (m≥2) its right side is at most (−1) while (λ^m≥−1). Thus (\Phi^m\succeq I-m(I-\Phi)), hence

\[
2\Phi^m-I\succeq I-2m(I-\Phi).
\]

Therefore, for every (Q\succeq0), some canonical measurement channel satisfies

\[
\operatorname{Tr}_{HS}Q\bigl(\Psi_M-[I-2m(I-\Phi)]\bigr)\ge0.
\]

The set of canonical measurement channels is convex: mix POVMs and retain the random coin in the outcome. It is compact by representing each POVM effect as \(M_z=d p_z\rho_z\), with \(\sum_zp_z\rho_z=I/d\), and taking the continuous image of the weak-* compact set of probability measures on the compact state space with that barycenter. Every such measure’s first and second moments have a finite-support representative by Carathéodory, so it gives the same channel as a finite-outcome canonical map. If no single \(\Psi\) in this compact convex set satisfied \(\Psi\succeq I-2m(I-\Phi)\), separation from the positive-semidefinite cone would give a \(Q\succeq0\) for which the displayed supremum is negative, contradicting the frame construction. Thus a single full-domain canonical EB map satisfies

\[
I-\Psi\preceq 2m(I-\Phi),\qquad m=\lceil\log_2(d^2)\rceil.
\]

The separation step is essential: separate measurements for separate frame elements do not suffice. Here the whole tree POVM handles one finite frame at once, and compact-convex separation turns the resulting all-frame support inequalities into one universal comparator.
