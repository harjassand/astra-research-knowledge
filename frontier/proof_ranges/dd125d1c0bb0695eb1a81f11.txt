Constructed: nonlocal transport certificates from finite source–sink modules
I constructed an explicit nonlocal completion for the original hard, two-dimensional Kob–Andersen model. It converts a finite, exactly checked configuration-space flow into a globally admissible current, with a proved and computable bound on the nonlocal contribution.
The resulting certificate is positive for every particle density \(0<p\le 0.40\). For example,
\[
\boxed{D(0.30)\ge 0.008932514941.}
\]
This is a broad lower bound, not an accurate prediction or a solution of dense glassy transport. Positivity itself was already established using cooperative-dynamics and percolation methods. The contribution developed here is the constructive certificate interface, its explicit completion-error theorem, and an executed hard-KA instance. Independent publication priority remains unverified. arXiv

1. What changed: preserve the defect, then complete it
The earlier no-go theorem remains intact: a finite-range, exactly conserved legal current cannot carry positive transport in box-closed cooperative dynamics. Increasing its dictionary cannot fix that obstruction.    cray_genesis_report
The new construction does not search for such a current.
Instead, its finite object is a legal current with a precisely specified source–sink defect:
\[
\partial H
=
(\text{left port occupation}-\text{right port occupation})
\times \text{reservoir gates}.
\]
A separate, genuinely nonlocal spatial current connects these defects. Their cancellation is proved after assembly, rather than imposed on each finite module.
I examined three alternatives before developing this construction. Direct projection of a conservation defect transfers the difficulty into an uncontrolled inverse: small residuals do not imply small correction energy without a quantitative spectral estimate. Weak-limit constructions likewise require a bounded-energy sequence that still has to be built. Unbounded routing is more concrete, but it is already established prior art, and forward path length alone does not control inverse congestion. Shapira’s recent Thomson-principle paper explicitly identifies unbounded paths as necessary for cooperative models. arXiv
The selected operation separates the difficulties:
Construct legal source–sink transport inside finite blocks. Construct global spatial conservation around arbitrarily large obstacles. Join them through coefficients that remain invariant during every microscopic module transition.

The last requirement is essential. A naive multiplication of the two currents would generally fail.
2. The finite object: a source–sink module for hard KA
The model remains the square-lattice hard KA rule: an exchange requires a vacant neighbor other than the exchange partner at both endpoints. No forbidden transition is softened.
Write \(p\) for particle density, \(q=1-p\), and \(\chi=pq\). The target is the infinite-volume bulk variational diffusion coefficient
\[
D(p)=\frac{1}{2\chi}
\inf_{f\ \mathrm{local}}
\mathbb E_p\sum_{i=1}^{2}c_i(a_i+G_i f)^2,
\]
with horizontal displacement field
\[
a_i=(\eta_0-\eta_{e_i})\mathbf1_{\{i=1\}}.
\]
This is collective diffusion, not tagged-particle self-diffusion. The underlying variational duality is not the invention; a Thomson-type lower-bound formulation is already available. arXiv
Ports and reservoir gates
Partition the lattice into \(3\times3\) blocks. Each block’s center is its port; its other eight sites form its reservoir.
A reservoir is declared good when there is at least one vacancy on each of its four outer sides, and at least one vacancy among the four neighbors of the center. This rule never reads the port occupation.
Exactly 154 of the 256 reservoir patterns are good. Their probability is the explicit polynomial
\[
\gamma(p)
=
q^8+8pq^7+28p^2q^6+52p^3q^5+49p^4q^4+16p^5q^3.
\tag{1}
\]
Because the reservoirs are disjoint, their gate values are independent under the known Bernoulli equilibrium.
For two adjacent blocks \(u,v\), I constructed a finite current \(H\) on their \(6\times3\) union satisfying
\[
\boxed{
\sum_{b\subset Q_{uv}}H_b(\zeta)
=
(\zeta_{s_u}-\zeta_{s_v})g(\omega_u)g(\omega_v).
}
\tag{2}
\]
Here \(H_b(\zeta)\) is the outgoing current along a configuration-graph edge. Thus the sum is a configuration-space divergence, not a spatial divergence.
Every transition used by \(H\) is legal even with the entire exterior occupied. It is consequently legal in any actual exterior configuration: additional vacancies cannot disable a KA move.
Importantly, \(H\) can pass through intermediate states whose gates are bad. Only its source–sink defect has the gated form in (2).

A sparse electrical-flow solve proposes \(H\). It is then rounded, and the integer divergence residual is repaired along a spanning forest.
The final witness satisfies every source equation exactly. Numerical convergence is not a correctness assumption.
The objective also includes assembly congestion. A microscopic bond inside a block can belong to four pair modules; a bond crossing a block interface belongs to one. With those multiplicities \(w_b\), define
\[
C(p)
=
\frac{\mathbb E_{p,Q}\sum_b w_bH_b^2}
{2\chi\,\gamma(p)^2}.
\tag{3}
\]
This is an explicitly evaluable rational function. Its coefficients are recomputed by the checker from the stored currents—not accepted from a numerical optimizer.
