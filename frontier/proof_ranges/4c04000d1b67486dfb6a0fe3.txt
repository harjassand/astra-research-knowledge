## 4. Calculated optical intervention, with its scope

The validation geometry is a laminate in vacuum, not a fitted kappa-ET stack. The quantity

    I(z,nu) = integral q^2 exp(-2qz) Im r_p(q,nu) dq

is an evanescent quasistatic dipole-kernel proxy. It is not full radiative LDOS, a superfluid susceptibility or a prediction of the target's electron–phonon kernel. The integral uses q from 1.2e6 to 1e9 rad/m, above the light line across the tested band.

At 1470 cm^-1, 20 nm witness height and nominal damping:

    (I_A-I_B) / ((I_A+I_B)/2) = approximately -0.13295.

The difference remains about -0.13086 at six times the nominal damping. A Gaussian spectral witness with 10 cm^-1 standard deviation, integrated over 1430–1510 cm^-1, gives about -0.13397. This is not dependent on resolving one infinitesimal resonance.

At q=51 rad/micrometre, the nominal Im r values are approximately 2.0047 and 8.1703. These are local resonant response amplitudes, not energy-gain factors. They are not presented as a superconductivity enhancement.

The ideal static reflection discrepancy is below 3e-16 numerically. Using the slightly non-mass-scaled published h11 in-plane endpoints gives a maximum relative DC mismatch near 1.92e-5 over the tested q grid. Neither number is a real fabrication tolerance or an experimental guarantee.

The reference code verifies passivity on the sampled evanescent grid, homogeneous-layer subdivision invariance, elastic moment equality, and numerical convergence. The 10,000 versus 20,000 point q-grid witness difference is below 1e-12 for the nominal case. The sampled imaginary-frequency maximum is not a rigorous global maximum.
