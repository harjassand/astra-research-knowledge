Before retaining a general quartic heat-flow programme, I tested its simplest hidden assumption.
Take
\[ F(A)=(\det A)^2. \]
This is quasiconvex: the average determinant is fixed by the boundary data, and Jensen’s inequality applies to its square.
Nevertheless, its energy need not decrease under componentwise heat evolution.
On the \(2\pi\)-periodic torus, let
\[ h(x,y)=\sin x+\sin y, \qquad v_0=(h,h^2). \]
Initially,
\[ \det Dv_0=0 \]
everywhere.
The exact heat evolution is
\[ \begin{aligned} v_t^1&=e^{-t}(\sin x+\sin y),\\ v_t^2&= 1-\frac12e^{-4t}(\cos2x+\cos2y) +2e^{-2t}\sin x\sin y. \end{aligned} \]
Its Jacobian is
\[ \det Dv_t = 2e^{-t}(e^{-2t}-e^{-4t}) \cos x\cos y(\sin x-\sin y). \]
Hence, using normalized torus average,
\[ \boxed{ \left\langle(\det Dv_t)^2\right\rangle = \frac12e^{-2t}(e^{-2t}-e^{-4t})^2>0 \qquad(t>0). } \tag{12} \]
The energy rises from zero.
Therefore the statement
Every rank-one-convex planar quartic admits a direct heat-monotonicity proof
is false. A viable operation must at least separate the determinant-square contribution.
The quartic setting has additional structure absent from the nonpolynomial Burkholder family.
For a homogeneous quartic \(F\), symmetry of its four-linear polarization gives
\[ \boxed{ D^2F(A)[H,H]=D^2F(H)[A,A]. } \tag{13} \]
Consequently, if \(F\) is rank-one convex and \(R\) has rank one, then
\[ D^2F(R)[H,H]\geq0 \qquad\text{for every }H. \tag{14} \]
Thus the full Hessian is positive semidefinite at every rank-one matrix.
This yields an exact classification.
