10. Executed checks
I created and executed small-case diagnostics. They do not implement the general convex-body integration stage.
The Monte Carlo tests used one or two independent coordinates,
\[
X_i(t)=C_i+A_i\cos t+B_i\sin t,
\]
with variances \(1/4,3/4,3/4\), and the simultaneous-threshold region \(X_i>u\). Each proposal test used 40,000 samples.
For one coordinate, the probability has an analytic formula. Across thresholds from \(-1\) to \(100\), the largest observed relative discrepancy was about \(0.34\%\), consistent with the reported Monte Carlo uncertainty.

The last row is an estimator diagnostic, not an independently known exact probability. Its reported relative standard error was approximately \(1.69\times10^{-4}\). The component normalizers and samplers in this special case are explicit; the test therefore does not demonstrate performance of the general acquisition algorithm.
Additional exact checks verified Gaussian regression constraints and conditional covariance identities. They also confirmed two crossings in near-grazing rational examples with excess amplitudes \(2^{-20},2^{-80},2^{-200}\), and \(2^{-1000}\); the chosen 1,024-point grids missed all four excursions.
A position–velocity correlation test changed the relevant flux integral from approximately \(0.0168\) to \(0.1603\) while keeping the marginal variances fixed. This exposes the error in factorizing the facet position and inward velocity.

11. Scope and remaining uncertainty
The guarantee concerns the supplied stationary finite Fourier model. It does not automatically transfer to nonstationary dynamics, arbitrary infinite-bandwidth processes, singular spectral covariances, nonlinear failure boundaries, or microscopic physical models that have not been reduced to the stated input.
Spectral truncation deserves particular caution. A small absolute model error need not give a small relative error in an extremely rare probability. This algorithm does not make that certification free.
Likewise, the polynomial dependence on dense degree \(d\) is essential. A frequency of size \(2^b\) supplied succinctly does not receive a polynomial-in-\(b\) crossing-count guarantee here.
The construction evaluates a supplied model or an explicitly supplied list of designs. It does not globally optimise arbitrary design spaces, learn unknown covariances, or give relative accuracy for every subsequent rare condition imposed on an accepted trajectory.
Established outcome
The surviving mechanism is
\[
\boxed{
\text{convex acquisition of inward boundary flux}
\;+\;
\text{bounded entry multiplicity}
\;+\;
\text{algebraic continuous-time counting}.
}
\]
Together, these give a polynomial-time construction for multivariate continuous-time polyhedral failure probabilities and failure-conditioned trajectories, with acquisition and precision charged rather than assumed.
The unresolved questions are whether this complete theorem is historically new, how its best implementation compares with specialized reliability methods, and how far the finite-spectrum model can be certified for demanding physical workloads. The mathematical construction does not currently depend on an unresolved committor, an unprovided rare-event sampler, or an uncharged fine time grid.
