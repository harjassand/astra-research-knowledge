RADIAL POSITIVITY, UNIQUENESS, AND ATTAINED WEAK DUAL CERTIFICATES
Independent analytic reconstruction and extension, 15 October 2026

Principal findings

For a homogeneous quartic polynomial F with a positive rank-one lower
value mu, every nonzero fixed-mass amplitude minimizer on a connected
planar domain is strictly positive in its interior and is unique. No
smoothness of the minimizer is assumed. The proof uses its legitimate
signed Euler equation, a logarithmic Caccioppoli estimate, and 4>2 Morrey
embedding. For a torus or strictly positive boundary amplitude, this also
produces an attained exact dual certificate in a specified weak flux
space. The source's positivity gap is therefore closed in this setting.

The zero-boundary distinction is essential for dual attainment. An exact
convex example has a positive interior minimizer and a supremum of smooth
flux certificates equal to its minimum, but no maximizing flux in the
natural unweighted L^(4/3) flux space. A separate defect in the source's
short mu>0 classification proof is repaired without changing its conclusion.

These are fixed-direction results. They do not bound the radial minimum
uniformly over directions and do not prove full quasiconvexity. Analytic
correctness has not been externally certified; historical novelty UNKNOWN.

Let Omega be a bounded connected Lipschitz domain in R^d, d<4, or a
connected flat d-torus, d<4. The application of interest is d=2. Let
n in W^(1,infinity)(Omega;R^m) be a fixed unit vector field. Smooth n from
the incoming application is a special case. Let F:R^(m x d)->R be a real
homogeneous quartic polynomial that is rank-one convex. Put

  mu = min_{rank R=1, |R|=1} F(R),

and assume mu>0. No output invariance is needed for the radial theorem.
Define

  f(x,t,xi)=F(n(x) tensor xi + t Dn(x)),
  J(r)=integral_Omega f(x,r,grad r).

For prescribed nonnegative admissible boundary trace g and M>0, the
primal problem is

  I(n,g,M)=min {J(r): r>=0, r in W^(1,4), Tr r=g,
                         integral r^4=M}.                         (1.1)

On a torus the trace requirement is omitted. Nonempty admissibility is
assumed. For existence alone d<4 is unnecessary; that restriction enters
the elementary positivity and boundary-flux proofs below.

2. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE SOURCE'S BASIC ANALYTIC CLAIMS

2.1 Hidden convexity and its zero extension

For any fixed x, a -> F(Dn+n tensor a) is convex: differences lie in a
rank-one linear space, and the definition of rank-one convexity applies
to every segment in that space. Its perspective

  h_x(rho,z)=rho F(Dn+n tensor z/(4rho)), rho>0,

is jointly convex. Since D(rn)=r[Dn+n tensor grad(log r)], its integral
is exactly J(r) when rho=r^4. Thus

  r_t=[(1-t)r_0^4+t r_1^4]^(1/4)

has J(r_t)<=(1-t)J(r_0)+t J(r_1). This remains true for nonnegative
W^(1,4) amplitudes. One may first replace r_i by
(r_i^4+epsilon^4)^(1/4), whose gradients converge strongly to grad r_i,
and then pass to the limit. In particular grad r_i=0 almost everywhere
on {r_i=0}, as for any Sobolev scalar function on a level set.

Alternatively the pointwise interpolation derivative and Holder give

  |grad r_t|^4 <= (1-t)|grad r_0|^4+t|grad r_1|^4.

This proves that the density domain {rho>=0: rho^(1/4) in W^(1,4)} is
convex. Its fixed trace g^4 and fixed mass M are affine conditions. One
must retain this domain; rho is not an unrestricted Sobolev variable.

2.2 Exact Picone expression and the quartic remainder

For positive r,s put C=Dn+n tensor grad(log r) and
Q=n tensor grad(log(s/r)). Direct differentiation and quartic Euler
homogeneity give

  F(D(sn))-(1/4)DF(D(rn)):D(n s^4/r^3)
    =s^4 [F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C):Q].                            (2.1)

Along a rank-one line, the polynomial f(t)=F(C+tQ) is a globally convex
quartic. If its leading coefficient A=F(Q)>0 and cubic coefficient L,
nonnegativity of f'' gives C_2>=3L^2/(8A). Consequently

  f(1)-f(0)-f'(0) >= A/3.

For A=0, f'' globally nonnegative forces its cubic coefficient zero and
the quadratic coefficient nonnegative. Hence in every case

  F(C+Q)-F(C)-DF(C):Q >= F(Q)/3 >= (mu/3)|Q|^4.             (2.2)

The factor 1/3 is universally sharp for convex quartic line restrictions:
f(t)=(t-1/3)^4 has leading coefficient one and remainder exactly 1/3
at t=0, increment one. Formula (2.1) therefore has the stated quantitative
right side (mu/3) r^4 |grad(s/r)|^4.

For r bounded away from zero in W^(1,4), and d<4, both r and s are
bounded. The quotient test s^4/r^3 is W^(1,4), so (2.1) is meaningful
almost everywhere and is integrable without a smooth-reference assumption.
For boundary data that allow r=0 on the boundary, this global quotient
test may fail; the uniqueness proof below avoids it.

2.3 Coercivity and attainment

Put K=||Dn||_infinity. Polynomial quartic growth and Young's inequality
give constants c>0,C<infinity depending only on F,mu,K such that

  f(x,t,xi) >= (mu/2)|xi|^4-C|t|^4.                        (2.3)

Indeed compare F(n tensor xi+tDn) to F(n tensor xi), use
|DF(A)|<=C_F|A|^3, and absorb the mixed cubic terms against
mu|xi|^4. At fixed mass this bounds minimizing sequences in W^(1,4).
Rellich compactness gives strong L4 convergence along a weak W^(1,4)
subsequence; it preserves mass and nonnegativity. The trace is weakly
continuous. The scalar integrand is convex in xi and continuous in t,
with fourth growth and (2.3), so weak lower semicontinuity with strong
amplitudes proves attainment. For example this follows by adding
C t^4 to make the integrand nonnegative and using its pointwise convex
support representation in xi; subtract the strongly convergent mass term.

The source's existence and fixed-direction convexity conclusions are
therefore correct. They do not imply joint convexity with respect to n.

3. THE SIGN CONSTRAINT IS REMOVABLE, SO THE WEAK EULER EQUATION IS LEGAL

Because F is a quartic polynomial, F(-A)=F(A). For any real Sobolev r,

  D(|r|n)=sign(r) D(rn) almost everywhere,
  J(|r|)=J(r),    integral |r|^4=integral r^4.

On {r=0}, both gradients in this equality are zero almost everywhere.
If Tr r=g>=0 then Tr|r|=g. Thus a minimizer of (1.1) is ALSO a minimizer
among all real r having the same trace and fourth mass. There is no
one-sided obstacle variation left to prevent a full Euler equation.

The energy and mass are continuously Frechet differentiable on the
affine W^(1,4) trace class. The derivative of mass on the variation space
W^(1,4)_0 is h -> 4 integral r^3 h. This is nonzero: M>0 and r>=0 ensure
that r^3 is a nonzero distribution, and some compactly supported smooth
h pairs nontrivially with it. The Banach-space Lagrange-multiplier theorem
therefore gives a real lambda such that

  integral A(x,r,grad r).grad psi + B(x,r,grad r) psi
      =4 lambda integral r^3 psi                         (3.1)

for every psi in W^(1,4)_0, with periodic tests on a torus. Here

  A=f_xi=DF(D(rn))^T n,
  B=f_t=DF(D(rn)):Dn.

No test with nonzero boundary value is used on the nonzero-boundary
problem, and no assumption that r itself is an admissible zero-boundary
test is made. In particular J(r)=lambda M is asserted only on a torus or
when g=0; in general a boundary contribution remains.

4. NEW THEOREM: STRICT INTERIOR POSITIVITY WITHOUT REGULARITY ASSUMPTIONS

Theorem 4.1. Under the hypotheses of Section 1, every minimizer with M>0
is strictly positive at every interior point, using its continuous
Sobolev representative. It is bounded away from zero on compact subsets.
On a connected torus it has a positive global minimum. On a bounded
domain with continuous g>=g_0>0 on the whole boundary, it also has a
positive global minimum on the closure.

Proof. Convexity in xi and (2.3) imply the radial derivative coercivity

  A(x,t,xi).xi >= f(x,t,xi)-f(x,t,0)
                  >= c_0|xi|^4-C_0|t|^4,                 (4.1)

where c_0>0. Polynomial growth and bounded Dn also give

  |A(x,t,xi)|+|B(x,t,xi)| <= C_1(|xi|^3+|t|^3).           (4.2)

These include all lower-order terms produced by the moving direction.
The constants depend on F,mu,||Dn||_infinity, not on r or epsilon.

Fix 0<=eta in C-infinity_c(Omega) and epsilon>0. The function

  psi=eta^4/(r+epsilon)^3

is an allowed W^(1,4)_0 test: the reciprocal composition is Lipschitz
on [0,infinity) for fixed epsilon and r is nonnegative. Substitute it
into (3.1). Exact differentiation yields

  3 integral eta^4 A.grad r/(r+epsilon)^4
    =4 integral eta^3 A.grad eta/(r+epsilon)^3
      +integral eta^4 B/(r+epsilon)^3
      -4 lambda integral eta^4 r^3/(r+epsilon)^3.          (4.3)

Let G=|grad r|/(r+epsilon). Since 0<=r/(r+epsilon)<=1, (4.1)-(4.2)
bound the left side below by 3c_0 integral eta^4 G^4-C integral eta^4.
The absolute values on the right are at most

  C integral eta^3(G^3+1)|grad eta|
  +C integral eta^4(G^3+1)+4|lambda| integral eta^4.

Young's inequality, first for G^3|grad eta| and then for G^3, absorbs a
fixed fraction of the eta^4 G^4 term. Thus

  integral eta^4 |grad log(r+epsilon)|^4
    <= C integral [|grad eta|^4+(1+|lambda|)eta^4],         (4.4)

uniformly in epsilon. All terms in (4.3) are finite for each epsilon;
no negative power of r itself has been used.

For a ball B_(2R) compactly contained in Omega choose eta=1 on B_R,
|grad eta|<=C/R. Since d<4, Morrey-Poincare on B_R gives

  osc_(B_R) log(r+epsilon)
      <= C R^(1-d/4)||grad log(r+epsilon)||_(L4(B_R))
      <= C [1+(1+|lambda|)^(1/4) R].                      (4.5)

Constants can depend on F,mu,||Dn||_infinity and d, but not on r,
epsilon or its positive lower bound. In particular

  sup_(B_R)(r+epsilon)
      <= exp(C[1+(1+|lambda|)^(1/4)R]) inf_(B_R)(r+epsilon).

W^(1,4) functions have continuous representatives when d<4. If r is
positive at one point of B_R, let epsilon tend to zero in the preceding
ratio bound to obtain positivity and a lower bound throughout B_R.
M>0 supplies a positive point somewhere. Chains of overlapping interior
balls in the connected domain propagate positivity everywhere. Compact
sets give a uniform lower bound by continuity. On a torus, compactness
gives a global positive minimum. For positive continuous boundary data,
the Sobolev extension theorem and Morrey give continuity on the closure;
the continuous boundary representative equals g. Positivity there and
in the interior, plus compactness, gives the asserted global bound. QED.

The restriction d<4 belongs to this elementary proof. General Harnack
theorems may permit more dimensions under their precise structural
assumptions; no such extension is claimed or needed for the planar task.

5. NEW THEOREM: UNIQUE AMPLITUDE MINIMIZER, INCLUDING ZERO BOUNDARY DATA

Theorem 5.1. The minimizer of (1.1) is unique on a connected domain, for
every admissible nonnegative boundary trace and M>0.

Proof. Suppose r_0,r_1 are minimizers. Both are positive in the interior
by Theorem 4.1. For 0<t<1 set rho_i=r_i^4, rho_t=(1-t)rho_0+t rho_1,

  w_0=(1-t)rho_0/rho_t,   w_1=t rho_1/rho_t,
  a_i=grad(log r_i),      a_t=w_0 a_0+w_1 a_1.

The interpolated amplitude is feasible, so hidden convexity is an
equality after integration. Pointwise, however, (2.2) supplies the exact
positive bound on its convexity deficit

 (1-t)F(D(r_0 n))+tF(D(r_1 n))-F(D(r_t n))
   >= (mu/3)rho_t w_0 w_1(w_0^3+w_1^3)|a_1-a_0|^4.      (5.1)

To see this, use the convexity remainders of each
Dn+n tensor a_i about Dn+n tensor a_t, multiply by w_i, and sum; the
linear terms cancel. All coefficients are positive in the interior.
The integrated deficit is zero, so a_1=a_0 almost everywhere there.
On each compact subset, r_1/r_0 is Sobolev with zero gradient, hence is
constant. Connectedness makes the constant global, and equal fourth
mass makes it one. No quotient boundary test was needed. QED.

Thus zeros of the amplitude do not create interior dead cores or
multiple amplitude minimizers in the planar positive-mu problem. Boundary
zeros remain possible, as the next sections deliberately distinguish.

6. NEW THEOREM: EXACT ATTAINED DUAL IN A NATURAL WEAK FLUX SPACE

This section assumes either a connected torus, or the bounded-domain
problem with g>=g_0>0 continuous on the boundary. By Theorem 4.1 its
unique minimizer r has r>=c>0 on the whole compact domain.

Set

  f_x(a)=F(Dn+n tensor a),
  f_x^*(j)=sup_a [j.a-f_x(a)],
  X={j in L^(4/3)(Omega;R^d): div j in L1(Omega)}.

For a torus X is periodic. Quartic coercivity in a makes f_x^* finite
and bounds its absolute value by C(1+|j|^(4/3)), uniformly in x. Thus
f_x^*(j) is integrable for every j in X. Let

  q_j=-(1/4)div j-f_x^*(j) in L1.

Define the boundary term without assuming a classical normal trace.
Choose any H in W^(1,4) with boundary trace g^4 and set

  B_g(j)=(1/4)[integral j.grad H+integral H div j].         (6.1)

This is independent of the extension H. The difference of two extensions
lies in W^(1,4)_0 and is approximable by compactly supported smooth
functions in W^(1,4), hence uniformly since d<4. Pairing j with their
gradients and div j with their values passes to the limit. On a torus
the boundary term is defined to be zero.

The attained primal-dual identity is

  I(n,g,M)
    = max_{lambda in R, j in X, q_j>=lambda almost everywhere}
                      [lambda M+B_g(j)].                 (6.2)

Proof of weak duality. For any feasible amplitude s put rho=s^4.
It belongs to W^(1,4) and is bounded because d<4. At rho>0, Fenchel gives

  rho f_x(grad rho/(4rho))
       >= (1/4)j.grad rho-rho f_x^*(j).

At rho=0 both rho and grad rho vanish almost everywhere and the same
inequality holds with both sides zero. Integration and (6.1) give

  J(s)>=integral q_j rho+B_g(j)>=lambda M+B_g(j).          (6.3)

Proof of attainment. Put a=grad(log r) and

  j_*=partial_a f_x(a).

Since r is bounded above and below and grad r is L4, a is L4 and j_* is
L^(4/3). For a compactly supported smooth density variation zeta, use
psi=zeta/(4r^3) in the weak equation (3.1). This is an allowed W^(1,4)_0
test because r>=c>0. The chain rule, or direct simplification of (3.1),
gives in distributions

  f_x(a)-a.j_*-(1/4)div j_*=lambda.                       (6.4)

Every term except div j_* is L1, so div j_* is L1 and j_* belongs to X.
Differentiable convexity gives f_x^*(j_*)=a.j_*-f_x(a).
Thus q_(j_*)=lambda exactly, almost everywhere. The pointwise Fenchel
inequality is an equality at r, and (6.1) therefore yields

  J(r)=lambda M+B_g(j_*).

This proves both equality and attainment in (6.2). It does not assume
an abstract Slater condition or a classical boundary normal derivative.
The maximizing pair is unique in this specified class: equality in (6.3)
with the strictly positive minimizer forces Fenchel equality, hence
j=j_* since f_x is differentiable, and then q_j=lambda fixes lambda.

On a torus the minimum is lambda M. On a nonzero-boundary problem the
boundary term is essential and cannot be silently discarded. The source's
smooth-flux sufficient condition is recovered when j is smooth; this
theorem proves exactness in X, not smoothness of the optimal flux or an
automatic interval-certifiable smooth approximation.

7. EXACT OBSTRUCTION: BOUNDARY ZEROS CAN DESTROY UNWEIGHTED DUAL ATTAINMENT

Take Omega=(0,1)^2, n=e_1 constant, and the explicitly convex quartic
F(A)=|A|^4, so mu=1. Prescribe g(x,y)=x on the whole boundary and M=1/5.
The map r_*(x,y)=x is feasible, with J(r_*)=1. For any real Sobolev
competitor with this boundary, the average gradient is e_1 by integration
by parts. Convex Jensen gives integral |grad r|^4>=1, with equality only
for r=x. Thus it is also the unique constrained minimizer. Its weak
Euler multiplier is lambda=0 and it is strictly positive in the interior.

Here f(a)=|a|^4, and pointwise Fenchel equality at rho_*=x^4 requires

  j_*=4|grad(log x)|^2 grad(log x)=4 e_1/x^3.

But integral |j_*|^(4/3) diverges at x=0. Consequently NO maximizing
flux belongs to X, if the dual class in (6.2) is retained for this
zero-boundary problem.

Nevertheless its supremum is exact. For every epsilon>0 the smooth flux

  j_epsilon=4 e_1/(x+epsilon)^3

has f^*(j_epsilon)=3/(x+epsilon)^4 and div j_epsilon=-12/(x+epsilon)^4.
Thus q_(j_epsilon)=0 identically, and its fixed boundary term is

  B_g(j_epsilon)=1/(1+epsilon)^3 -> 1=J(r_*).

This is an exact example of nonattainment, not merely a concern about
boundary regularity. The strictly positive boundary after a large target
translation in the original affine-boundary application avoids it.

8. REPAIR OF THE SOURCE'S mu>0 CLASSIFICATION SHORT ARGUMENT

For the full planar left-SO(2)-invariant homogeneous quartic class, the
classification conclusion is correct: a rank-one-convex member that
vanishes on a nonzero rank-one matrix is polyconvex. The displayed claim
that Hessians at only e_12 and e_11 force all three orientation coefficients
to vanish is insufficient. The following gives the complete repair.

By invariance, F(a tensor b)=|a|^4 h(b), where h is a nonnegative convex
binary quartic. Its fourth root is the gauge of a convex symmetric unit
ball, hence a seminorm. If h has a nonzero null direction, the seminorm
is constant on parallel lines in that direction. A real invertible input
coordinate change therefore makes h(b)=alpha b_1^4, alpha>=0.

Write u=|column1|^2, v=|column2|^2, w=column1.column2, q=det A. For
completeness, an invariant real quartic is a quadratic in u,v,w,q, with
the sole degree-four relation uv=w^2+q^2. This follows by writing the
two output columns as complex z_1,z_2: rotation invariance of a degree-four
monomial requires two holomorphic and two antiholomorphic factors, so
the invariant space is the nine-real-dimensional space of Hermitian
quadratic forms on (z_1^2,z_1 z_2,z_2^2). One real basis is
u^2,v^2,uv,w^2,uw,vw,qu,qv,qw. On rank-one matrices z_j=b_j z, its first
six terms give the binary monomials b_1^4,b_2^4,b_1^2 b_2^2,b_1^3 b_2,
b_1 b_2^3, with uv and w^2 coinciding. Equality of the binary restriction
to alpha b_1^4 forces the coefficients of v^2,uw,vw to be zero and those
of uv,w^2 opposite. Since uv-w^2=q^2, the resulting full polynomial is

  F=alpha u^2+gamma q^2+q(Lu+Mv+Nw).                      (8.1)

At A=e_12 and H=(s,t) tensor (x,y), the coefficient of tau^2 in
F(A+tau H) is

  gamma x^2 t^2-2M x y s t-N x^2 s t.                    (8.2)

Rank-one convexity for every s,t,x,y forces M=0, then N=0, then gamma>=0.
It does NOT constrain L at this matrix.

At A=diag(1,q) and H=e_21, after M=N=0, det(A+tau H)=q and
u(A+tau H)=1+tau^2. The coefficient of tau^2 is

  2alpha+Lq.                                              (8.3)

Since q is arbitrary, rank-one convexity forces L=0. The remaining
F=alpha u^2+gamma q^2 is polyconvex, and invertible input changes preserve
polyconvexity. This completes the classification. Compactness of the
unit rank-one set then gives mu>0 for every non-polyconvex member.

The insufficiency of the two named rank-one matrices has an exact witness.
F=u^2+q^2+qu has at e_12 the nonnegative acoustic coefficient x^2t^2;
at e_11 it has

  x^2(6s^2+2t^2)+y^2t^2+2xy st
    =(yt+xs)^2+5x^2s^2+2x^2t^2 >=0.

Yet (8.3) at q=-3 is -1. This quartic is NOT rank-one convex globally;
it disproves the shortened inference, not the repaired classification.

9. IMPLICATION FOR ARBITRARY AFFINE-BOUNDARY TESTS AND THE REMAINING GAP

Every bounded smooth test u=Bx+phi can be translated by a constant c so
u+c stays away from zero, with smooth fixed n=(u+c)/|u+c| and boundary
g=|Bx+c|>=g_0>0. The unique amplitude minimizer of the same fourth mass
is positive on the closure and lowers the original compatible energy.
It has an attained weak dual (6.2) and the legal nonsmooth Picone comparison.
Thus the source's amplitude-zero and weak-duality-attainment issues can
both be removed for this exact representation of every smooth test.

What has not been proved is

  lambda_(n,g,M) M+B_g(j_(n,g,M)) >= |Omega|F(B)

uniformly over the resulting direction fields and masses. The maximum
in (6.2) gives complete certificates for each radial problem, but defining
j_* through its unknown minimizer does not give a phase-independent
construction or establish this inequality. Interior positivity and
uniqueness do not control the sign of the ground-state/boundary expression.
Joint phase-density convexity remains false even for convex F, as in the
incoming exact Hessian example. No full-QC inference is made.

11. DERIVED A POSTERIORI ERROR CERTIFICATE FOR THE UNIQUE RADIAL MINIMIZER

The new positivity and uniqueness theorem makes the scalar Euler residual
an error certificate for the attained amplitude problem, with no appeal
to an optimizer success flag. Assume the torus or positive-boundary setting.
Let s be any positive smooth feasible amplitude with fourth mass M and
the correct boundary values. Require its flux j_s to belong to X and its
residual g_s below to be L-infinity; smooth n and s suffice. Put sigma=s^4
and use its computed flux
j_s=partial_a f_x(grad log s) and residual

  g_s=f_x(grad log s)-(grad log s).j_s-(1/4)div j_s.

Suppose verified bounds ell<=g_s<=u hold almost everywhere. Weak duality,
with the actual boundary term retained, gives

  ell M+B_g(j_s) <= I(n,g,M) <= J(s),
  0<=J(s)-I(n,g,M)
       <= integral (g_s-ell)s^4 <= M(u-ell).               (11.1)

For the unique minimizer r_*, integrated Picone with reference s and the
same boundary yields

  J(r_*)-J(s)
    >= integral g_s(r_*^4-s^4)
       +(mu/3) integral s^4 |grad(r_*/s)|^4.

Both densities have mass M, so the linear integral is >=-M(u-ell).
Since J(r_*)-J(s)<=0,

  integral s^4 |grad(r_*/s)|^4 <= 3M(u-ell)/mu.           (11.2)

Thus a nearly constant verified scalar residual proves both nearly
optimal energy and relative-gradient accuracy. If s>=s_0>0, (11.2)
bounds the unweighted L4 gradient of w=r_*/s. With prescribed positive
boundary values, w-1 has zero trace, so Poincare and Morrey control its
W^(1,4) norm and supremum by the right side to the fourth-root power.
On a connected torus, Morrey controls osc(w); the identity
integral s^4 w^4=integral s^4 and w>=0 puts 1 between its minimum and
maximum, so ||w-1||_infinity<=osc(w). Multiplication by smooth s then
gives a W^(1,4) error bound for r_*-s, with constants depending on the
domain, s_0, and the supplied W^(1,infinity) norm of s.

This is a direct certified stopping criterion for a fixed-direction
amplitude solve. It does not prove convergence of a particular numerical
scheme, guarantee that a smooth nearly optimal reference is discoverable
at a specified cost, or bound the minimum uniformly over directions.

12. SIMPLE EXACT NONCONVEXITY AFTER COMPLETE AMPLITUDE MINIMIZATION

This independent example distinguishes reduced-phase nonconvexity from
the source's joint Hessian at a nonstationary density. It is a periodic
winding-three example, not a negative compatible energy or an affine-
boundary counterexample. No further generalization is asserted here.

Take F(A)=|A|^4 on the two-dimensional 2*pi torus, and let

  Lambda(theta)=min_{r>=0, integral r^4=1} integral |D(r e^(i theta))|^4.

Choose a smooth periodic chi such that chi=-1 on [0,pi/2], chi=+1 on
[pi,3pi/2], and chi(y+pi)=-chi(y). For an explicit smooth construction,
put chi=-1 on [0,pi/2] and chi=-1+2S(2y/pi-1) on [pi/2,pi], where S is
any smooth step flat at both endpoints, then extend by half-period
antisymmetry. Its mean is zero. Let h'=chi be a smooth periodic primitive,
and set theta_0=3y+3h(y), theta_1=3y-3h(y). Their midpoint is 3y, and
both phases represent smooth periodic directions with the same winding.

For theta_0, use an amplitude proportional to phi(y)=sin^2(2y) on
(0,pi/2), extended by zero outside; it belongs to W^(1,4), and the phase
is constant on its support. For theta_1 shift phi by pi. Exact integrals
are

  integral_0^(pi/2) phi^4=35pi/256,
  integral_0^(pi/2) |phi'|^4=3pi.

After normalizing fourth mass, both trial energies equal 768/35. Thus
Lambda(theta_i)<=768/35. At the midpoint,

  |D(r e^(3iy))|^2=|grad r|^2+9r^2,
  integral |D(r e^(3iy))|^4>=81 integral r^4=81.

A constant amplitude attains this bound, so Lambda(3y)=81>768/35.
Consequently the exactly amplitude-minimized phase objective is not
convex. By Theorems 4.1 and 5.1, the endpoint minima are attained by
strictly positive unique amplitudes despite the convenient zero-supported
trial used to bound them. Removing amplitude zeros and optimizing that
block completely therefore does not supply phase convexity.
