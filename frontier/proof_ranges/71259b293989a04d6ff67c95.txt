## 5. E/O age laws and single-pulse separation

Let (A_j=C+(j-1)\tau+\tau X_j), (j=1,\ldots,4). These ages are ordered because (A_{j+1}-A_j=\tau(1+X_{j+1}-X_j)\ge0). Reversing the ages gives the ordered entry times, and
\[
L_4=(A_2-A_1)+(A_4-A_3)
=\tau[2+X_2-X_1+X_4-X_3].
\]
The eight even strings yield lengths (0,2\tau,4\tau) with multiplicities (1,6,1); the eight odd strings yield (\tau,3\tau) with multiplicities (4,4). Every proper coordinate marginal agrees because every assignment on at most three bits has exactly the same number of even and odd completions.

For a step or rectangular pulse, let (m_k\in\{0,1\}) record whether the age node (C+k\tau), (k=0,\ldots,4), lies inside the pulse's translated age interval. A rectangular interval makes the node sequence (m_0,\ldots,m_4) have at most two transitions. Atom (j) samples node (j-1+X_j), so its pulse membership is (m_{j-1+X_j}), which varies with (X_j) only if (m_{j-1}\ne m_j). At most two coordinates affect the entire vector of atom-label probabilities. The complete isotope-count law is therefore a function of at most two (X_j)'s and agrees under E and O. This argument includes pulse boundaries coincident with age nodes: the convention for whether a boundary point is inside still yields one contiguous node block and at most two transitions.

For a general normalized deterministic isotope mean (u), set (u_k=u(t-C-k\tau)). Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta(t,C)
&=\mathbb E_E\prod_{j=0}^3u_{j+X_{j+1}}-\mathbb E_O\prod_{j=0}^3u_{j+X_{j+1}}\\
&=\frac18\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^4}(-1)^{|x|}\prod_{j=0}^3u_{j+x_j}\\
&=\frac18\prod_{j=0}^3(u_j-u_{j+1}).
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus for an (M)-Lipschitz waveform, ( |\Delta|\le(M\tau)^4/8\). Smooth waveforms can distinguish E/O; the exact equality result is only for the stated step and one-pulse family.
