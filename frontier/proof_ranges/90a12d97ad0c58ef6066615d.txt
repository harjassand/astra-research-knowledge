## 3. An exact scalar-source index

Return to (1.1). Boundedness gives

\[
 a\|A\|\le a g_a(\|C+P\|)<1.
\]

Order reversal of inversion gives the exact equivalence

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 A\preceq g_a(C+P)
 \ \Longleftrightarrow\
 f_a(A)\preceq C+P.
 \quad} \tag{3.1}
\]

Indeed, the first inequality is equivalent to
`I-aA >= (I+a(C+P))^{-1}`; invert it, subtract `I`, and divide by `a`. The argument is reversible.

The polar decomposition `T=U A^{1/2}` makes `C=UAU^*`, so this is a special case of the source recursion with one contraction `U`. This is a mathematical identification, **not** a cheap procedure to acquire `U` from an implicit `T`; constructing the polar factor may itself require difficult spectral information.

### Lemma 3.1 — compact source forces compact target

If `B` is compact and (1.1) holds, `T` is compact.

#### Proof

In the quotient by compact operators, `P` vanishes. Denote the quotient map by `pi`, and put `m=||pi(T)||^2`. The positive operators `pi(A)` and `pi(C)` both have norm `m`. Functional calculus and the order comparison imply

\[
 m\le g_a(m)=\frac{m}{1+am}.
\]

Hence `m=0`, which is exactly compactness of `T`. ∎

For a prescribed finite or countably infinite positive sequence

\[
 a^{-1}>\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots>0,
 \qquad \lambda_j\longrightarrow0\text{ in the infinite case},
\]

define `r_a(lambda)` to be the least possible rank of a source `B` in (1.1), over all bounded operators whose positive singular-value squares are the prescribed list. Arbitrary zero singular values and enlargement of the Hilbert space are permitted. For the empty list set `r_a=0`.

### Theorem 3.2 — exact reciprocal-window formula

With `xi_j=1/lambda_j`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 r_a(\lambda)=\sup_{x>0}\#\{j:x\le\xi_j<x+a\}.
 \quad} \tag{3.2}
\]

The answer may be infinite. In the finite-rank case it is equivalently the least integer `r` satisfying

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \lambda_{j+r}\le\frac{\lambda_j}{1+a\lambda_j}
 \quad\text{for every applicable }j.
 \quad} \tag{3.3}
\]

#### Necessity

If `rank P=r`, min–max applied on a codimension-`r` kernel yields

\[
 \lambda_{j+r}(C+P)\le\lambda_j(C)=\lambda_j.
\]

Equation (3.1) then implies `f_a(lambda_{j+r})<=lambda_j`, precisely (3.3). Taking reciprocals gives `xi_{j+r}>=xi_j+a`. This is equivalent to having no more than `r` reciprocal eigenvalues in any half-open interval `[x,x+a)`. The half-open endpoint is important when equality occurs.

#### Sufficiency and explicit realisation

Suppose the interval count is finite, equal to `r`. On `ell^2(N)`, padding a finite list by zeros, define

\[
 Te_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j}\,e_{j+r},
 \qquad
 Bu_j=\sqrt{f_a(\lambda_j)}\,e_j,
 \quad 1\le j\le r.
\]

Then `T^*T` has the prescribed diagonal. In the first `r` coordinates, `C+P` equals `f_a(lambda_j)`; in later coordinates it equals `lambda_{j-r}`. Equation (3.3) therefore proves (3.1) coordinatewise. The source has exactly rank `r`.

If the interval supremum is infinite, necessity excludes all finite-rank sources. An infinite-rank source still exists: use diagonal `T` and `BB^*=f_a(A)-A`, as in Section 5.1. ∎

**What this realisation preserves.** It preserves singular values, including their multiplicities. It need not preserve the eigenvalues of a pre-existing nonnormal operator, locality, tensor-product structure, or any differential interpretation. No claim for a fixed ambient dimension with prescribed kernel dimensions is made.

### Spectral counting and heat-trace consequences

For any actual rank-`r` certificate, repeated use of (3.3) gives

\[
 \lambda_{j+kr}\le\frac1{\lambda_j^{-1}+ka},\qquad 1\le j\le r.
\]

Thus `T` is in weak Schatten class `S_{2,infinity}`: in elementary terms its singular values obey a `j^{-1/2}` bound after grouping by `r`.

For `H=A^{-1}` on the support of `A`,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}
 \le\frac{\sum_{j=1}^r e^{-\beta/\lambda_j}}{1-e^{-a\beta}}
 \le\frac{r e^{-\beta/\|T\|^2}}{1-e^{-a\beta}},
 \qquad\beta>0.
\]

These are genuine consequences of the certificate. They do not supply a cheap certificate for an arbitrary inverse operator.
