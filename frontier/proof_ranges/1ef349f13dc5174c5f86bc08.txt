# Accessible passive stiffness: bounded support design, coexistence and acquisition

15 October 2026. Independent mathematical continuation of the supplied transport checkpoint. These statements concern the stated classical models. No named material, transition ensemble, accessible fixture, kinetic response, historical originality, or physical capability has been acquired here. The matrix and optimization tools are standard; the explicit constructions and their use as falsifiers are the present research output.

## 1. Coordinates that a fixture can actually reach

Let u be n stable host coordinates and let H be a common positive definite host Hessian. For each fixed constrained well or transition ensemble s, assume

    E_s(u;S)=E_s^0 + (1/2)u^T H u - f_s^T u
                       + (1/2)(U^T u)^T S(U^T u).

Here U is a fixed, full-column-rank n-by-r map describing actual accessible attachment coordinates q=U^T u. The support has S>=0, a common zero rest coordinate, and no state-dependent chemistry, preload, change of f_s, or change of H. Any stable modes retained at a transition state are constrained modes: this is not a Gaussian integration over an unstable saddle direction. The constrained ensembles and their measures are fixed. Temperature T>0 is common and classical. Reservoir driving is a separate paid boundary.

Define

    G=U^T H^-1 U >0,       p_s=U^T H^-1 f_s,
    a_s=G^-1/2 p_s,        L=G^1/2 S G^1/2,
    Q=I-(I+L)^-1.

Then 0<=Q<I, with exact inverse

    S=G^-1/2 [(I-Q)^-1-I] G^-1/2.                    (1)

The matrix Q is dimensionless; a_s has units square-root energy. A component of a microscopic force outside the accessible relaxation subspace has no support response in this model. Replacing U by arbitrary atomic coordinates silently supplies an unbuilt apparatus.

Woodbury's identity and the Gaussian partition function give

    F_s(S)-F_s(0) = (1/2)a_s^T Q a_s
                         -(k_B T/2) log det(I-Q).    (2)

To see the force term, write the Woodbury subtraction as H^-1 U G^-1/2 Q G^-1/2 U^T H^-1. The determinant lemma gives det(H+USU^T)/det(H)=det(I+L)=det(I-Q)^-1. Both identities hold for singular S because I+L is positive definite. They prove (2) without an approximation in S.

For an initial well A and a constrained exit ensemble s, put

    C_s=(1/2)(a_s a_s^T-a_A a_A^T).

The barrier shift is exactly

    Delta B_s = tr(Q C_s).                           (3)

The common entropy term in (2) cancels. This cancellation requires the same H, U, constrained dimension and measure for both ensembles. It is not a general finite-temperature theorem about a physical migration path.

## 2. The attainable body, rather than an unlimited barrier-shift cone

If every PSD support on the measured r ports is constructible, the finite barrier vectors are the linear image

    B_fin={ (tr(Q C_1),...,tr(Q C_m)): 0<=Q<I }.

Its closure B is the image of 0<=Q<=I, a compact convex body. The cone obtained from arbitrary PSD Q describes directional signs or infinitesimal responses, not unbounded finite barrier changes. The Q=I boundary requires diverging stiffness along the relevant modes. No finite spring reaches it.

A finite hardware cap 0<=S<=S_max is equivalent to

    0<=Q<=Q_max=I-(I+G^1/2 S_max G^1/2)^-1.          (4)

This follows from operator monotonicity of X -> I-(I+X)^-1 and of its inverse Q -> (I-Q)^-1-I. A finite S_max has Q_max<I, so its compact body is attained with finite support. For W=sum_j w_j C_j, its exact support function is

    sup_{0<=Q<=Q_max} tr(WQ)
       = tr[(Q_max^1/2 W Q_max^1/2)_+].              (5)

Proof: Q=Q_max^1/2 Z Q_max^1/2 on the range of Q_max, with 0<=Z<=I. In the eigenbasis of the compressed W, choose Z=1 on its positive eigenspaces and Z=0 on its negative eigenspaces. Zero eigenvalues are arbitrary. Formula (5) also covers singular Q_max. For an uncapped ideal support set Q_max=I; the value is a supremum if the selected eigenvalue of Q is 1.

Let A_j be signed benefit matrices (for example A_U=-C_U for lowering a useful barrier and A_H=C_H for raising a damage barrier). Desired nonstrict benefits d_j are feasible in the capped full-PSD body if and only if

    for every lambda_j>=0:
    sum_j lambda_j d_j
       <= tr[(Q_max^1/2 (sum_j lambda_j A_j)
                               Q_max^1/2)_+].       (6)

Necessity follows by weighting the target inequalities and applying (5). For sufficiency, the compact image of the cap body minus the nonnegative orthant is closed and convex. If d is outside it, a separating hyperplane must have a nonnegative normal lambda because the set is unbounded in every negative coordinate direction. The separation contradicts (6). This is a semidefinite feasibility certificate, not acquisition of any A_j.

## 3. Actual support catalogs and a local accessibility certificate

A common passive catalog is

    S(kappa)=sum_l kappa_l v_l v_l^T,
    0<=kappa_l<=kappa_l,max,

where each fixed v_l is a reachable attachment direction. Coupled springs may enlarge the cone, but their coupling and mounting must be demonstrated. Equation (1), membership of S(Q) in this cone, and the real caps/resource constraints are the exact realizability test. In general the legal Q set from a restricted cone is not assumed convex. The full-PSD body in section 2 is then an optimistic relaxation: its impossibility certificates still rule out the catalog, but its feasible points may be inaccessible and its separation criterion need not be sufficient for the restricted catalog.

For mechanically independent normalized ports (G=I and diagonal catalog S), Q=diag(chi_l), chi_l=kappa_l/(1+kappa_l). The finite cap body is a box in chi and the barrier body is a box's linear image. All simultaneous targets are then a genuine linear program. No arbitrary cross spring is needed for the explicit construction below.

An exact accessibility counterexample makes the relaxation boundary concrete. Choose H=U=I, a_A=(1,1), a_U=(1,-1). The useful benefit matrix -C_U is [[0,1],[1,0]]. Every diagonal catalog Q has zero useful benefit. The full-PSD choice Q=(1/4)[[1,1],[1,1]] has useful benefit 1/2 and finite S=(1/2)[[1,1],[1,1]]. It requires the coupled direction (1,1), absent from that diagonal catalog. Thus a favorable mathematical direction can be completely useless to the actual available independent ports; obtaining its cross coupling is a separate physical interface.

At any current support L, an incremental passive catalog support dS gives

    dQ=(I+L)^-1 G^1/2 dS G^1/2 (I+L)^-1.             (7)

Thus define z_l=(I+L)^-1 G^1/2 v_l and the acquired directional benefit columns

    D_jl=z_l^T A_j z_l.

Ignoring finite caps for this local sign test, a nonnegative incremental catalog direction produces strictly positive benefits for every j if and only if there is no nonzero lambda>=0 with D^T lambda<=0. This is the finite-cone theorem of alternatives (or a separating-hyperplane proof). It can be tested directly from measured rate-level response columns as well. It does not certify a finite nonlinear improvement, a support-removal direction at a boundary, or a global yield optimum.

## 4. What rank one can and cannot do

For exactly two strict directional criteria tr(AQ)>0 and tr(BQ)>0, unrestricted PSD feasibility implies a rank-one direction with the same signs. Here is an elementary rank-reduction proof. Start with any feasible Q of rank r>=2 and work in its range. There is a nonzero symmetric D preserving the two linear forms because r(r+1)/2>2. Choose the sign of D with tr(D)<=0; D then has a negative eigenvalue. Increase t>=0 in Q+tD until it first reaches the PSD boundary. Both forms stay fixed and the rank drops. Iterate to rank one. Rescale this direction to Q<I if needed; strict signs survive. This proof preserves two form values but not a cap or a finite target after rescaling.

Equivalently, the unrestricted two-sign margin is

    max_{Q>=0,tr Q=1} min(tr(AQ),tr(BQ))
       = min_{0<=lambda<=1} lambda_max(lambda A+(1-lambda)B).

The minimax identity follows from compact convex bilinear minimax. A nonzero nonnegative combination of A and B that is negative semidefinite certifies impossibility; its absence certifies strict feasibility. These are standard quadratic/PSD alternatives, not a historical novelty claim.

For m simultaneous strict signs, the same rank-reduction argument only ensures a direction with r(r+1)/2<=m. Three criteria can genuinely require rank two.

**Exact two-port coexistence construction.** In dimension two choose H=U=I and

    a_A=(1,0),  a_U=(0,1),
    a_H+=(1/2,1),  a_H-=(1/2,-1).

Use two independent finite passive springs

    S=diag(1,2/3),    Q=diag(1/2,2/5).

Equation (3) gives

    Delta B_U=-1/20,  Delta B_H+=Delta B_H-=1/80.      (8)

All numbers are synthetic energies in a common chosen unit. For every rank-one Q=t vv^T, t>0, a useful decrease requires |v_2/v_1|<1 (and v_1 cannot vanish). Both damage increases require |1/2+v_2/v_1|>1 and |1/2-v_2/v_1|>1. Together these require |v_2/v_1|>3/2, a contradiction. Therefore every rank-one support fails the three signs, even with arbitrary rank-one orientation and strength; the displayed independent two-spring fixture satisfies them at finite strength. This is an exact conditional advantage over a rank-one apparatus class, not evidence that a membrane has these force projections.

**Two targets can also require rank two at a finite ceiling.** Set a_A=e_1, a_U=0, a_H=e_2 and demand -Delta B_U>=1/5 and Delta B_H>=1/10. This requires Q_11>=2/5 and Q_22-Q_11>=1/5, hence tr Q>=1. A finite rank-one Q<I has tr Q<1 and cannot comply. The full-rank Q=diag(2/5,3/5) has finite S=diag(2/3,3/2) and meets both targets. A rank-one trace-one limit would require infinite stiffness. Thus the two-sign rank-one statement must not be extended to fixed quantitative targets and resource ceilings.

## 5. Optimal orientations with explicitly declared resources

The support function gives the optimal orientation for a linear barrier objective under an isotropic support cap. Two additional budgets produce finite optima. These are optimization of the stipulated model resource, not an assertion that it equals manufacturing, actuator, or service cost.

First, establish S reversibly and isothermally while holding the system in A, beginning with S=0 and the same reference coordinate. The conditional free-energy work is

    C_A(Q)=tr(P_A Q)-(k_B T/2)log det(I-Q),
    P_A=(1/2)a_A a_A^T.                              (9)

It is convex and diverges as an eigenvalue approaches 1 at T>0. Maximize a linear objective tr(WQ) subject to C_A(Q)<=E and 0<=Q<I. The feasible set is convex. For Lagrange multiplier lambda>0 put D=W-lambda P_A. The optimum for that multiplier shares eigenvectors with D and has eigenvalues

    q_i=max(0, 1-lambda k_B T/(2 d_i))
         if d_i>lambda k_B T/2, else 0,              (10)

where d_i are eigenvalues of D. This follows by maximizing d_i q_i+(lambda k_B T/2)log(1-q_i), whose derivative is d_i-lambda k_B T/[2(1-q_i)]. The entropy term makes the maximizer unique for lambda>0, apart from irrelevant basis choices within eigenspaces. Choose lambda to meet the budget when an improving direction exists; otherwise Q=0 suffices. Orientation depends on lambda because establishment in A has a directional relaxation cost. The minimum reversible establishment work is not the full apparatus energy. Unloading in another state, switching, hysteresis, dissipation, maintenance and replacement require their own ledger.

Second, declare a dimensionless total normalized stiffness budget

    tr(GS)=tr[Q(I-Q)^-1]<=B.                         (11)

For a linear objective tr(WQ), an optimizer shares eigenvectors with W; if d_i are W's eigenvalues, the Lagrangian optimum has

    q_i=max(0,1-sqrt(lambda/d_i)) for d_i>lambda,
    q_i=0 otherwise.                                (12)

This comes from d_i-lambda/(1-q_i)^2=0. The matrix trace identity uses cyclicity and does not assume G and S commute. This normalized stiffness cost is a declared design metric; a nonisotropic physical spring-volume or trace(S) budget generally does not have formula (12). Catalog, local stress and geometry constraints can invalidate both unrestricted orientations.

## 6. State-dependent Hessians and finite-temperature sign reversal

If H_s varies by ensemble, put G_s=U^T H_s^-1 U. Then the exact harmonic shift is

    Delta F_s=(1/2)f_s^T[H_s^-1-(H_s+USU^T)^-1]f_s
                         +(k_B T/2)log det(I+S G_s). (13)

There is no shared Q and no automatic determinant cancellation. More generally, for fixed constrained ensembles with an added energy (1/2)q^T S q, let M_s(S)=<qq^T>_s. Differentiation under a finite partition function gives

    dF_s=(1/2)tr(dS M_s),
    d^2F_s[P,R]=-(beta/4)Cov_s(q^T P q,q^T R q).     (14)

Each F_s is concave in S on its domain; a barrier difference F_s-F_A need not be convex or concave. The local attainable sign cone uses the actual matrices (M_s-M_A)/2 at the current S. It can change with stiffness, temperature and ensemble. Positive semidefinite dS only means the added energy is nonnegative; it does not force every barrier difference upward.

An entropy-only one-dimensional example has all f_s=0, H_A=1, H_U=4, H_H=1/4 and S=1. It gives

    Delta B_U=(k_B T/2)log(5/8)<0,
    Delta B_H=(k_B T/2)log(5/2)>0.

Thus a common-host force-only null is not a universal stiffness null. Conversely a locally favorable sign need not persist. In units k_B T=1, choose H_A=1,f_A=0,H_U=4,f_U=3. Then

    Delta B_U(kappa)=(9/2)[1/4-1/(4+kappa)]
                            +(1/2)log[(1+kappa/4)/(1+kappa)],
    Delta B_U'(0)=-3/32,
    Delta B_U(1)=9/40+(1/2)log(5/8)=-0.0100018...,
    Delta B_U(3)=27/56+(1/2)log(7/16)=+0.06880357....

These are synthetic counterexamples to extending a local covariance sign into a global operating window. At low temperature the classical approximation may fail. A moving saddle/dividing surface or opening an alternate pathway is beyond the fixed-ensemble derivation. Actual rates additionally require dynamical transmission and prefactors. Under an otherwise valid transition-state rate network, well free-energy terms still cancel from lifetime jump rewards as row-time changes even when the Hessians differ; it is the saddle responses and dynamical corrections that then replace the common-host saddle matrices.

## 7. Preload is a different intervention

An actual spring centered at r contributes (1/2)(q-r)^T S(q-r). Besides stiffness, it adds force USr and a constant (1/2)r^T S r. Equations (2)–(3) require r=0 fixed. State-dependent r, maintaining constant strain by a servo, electrostatic fields, altered surface charge, adsorption, chemical functionalization, support adhesion or a pressure-driven flow can change f_s, H_s, driving and prefactors. Their effects cannot be credited to the zero-preload passive stiffness theorem. Comparing a fixture to an unsupported sample must retain its lost active area, contact resistance, pore blockage, roughness, local stress, fabrication yield and new damage routes.

## 8. Correct transport target and missing data

The transport_damage worker independently derives a finite a.s.-absorbing CTMC result. At the current support let v be continuation reward, nu cumulative occupation time, and A_it=g_it+v_destination-v_i. The exact rate-level directional sensitivity is

    dY=sum_i,t nu_i k_it A_it d log k_it.              (15)

Under the fixed-prefactor common-host barrier law, the Bellman sum cancels every well term. With P_t^dagger=(1/2)a_t a_t^T, w_t=sum_origins nu_i k_it A_it and fixed rho,

    dY=tr(dQ W_Y),
    W_Y=-beta sum_t w_t P_t^dagger.                  (16)

This is the signed global saddle objective that can be inserted into the accessible directional/capped calculations. A nominally useful hop can have adverse continuation value. Every damage channel must appear. Equations (5), (10) or (12) applied to W_Y choose a first-order direction or surrogate, not a globally optimal finite Y(Q): weights vary and finite network yield is generally nonlinear/nonconcave. Recompute the complete changed network and cost after any finite proposal. Formula (16) belongs to transport_damage; the accessible support body and constructions above are the present worker's independent derivations.

In particular, the three-sign construction in section 4 proves a barrier-constraint advantage over the rank-one fixture class. It does not prove higher lifetime yield than every rank-one fixture. A rank-one intervention that worsens one small damage channel could still outperform it on a weighted total yield; the full target and any separate channel limits decide that comparison.

No microscopic transition-state oracle is necessary to test an operational response. Real small catalog perturbations can acquire eventwise rate slopes, and (15) includes their prefactor, recrossing, chemistry and contact effects. They cannot identify the microscopic source of a rate change by themselves. A TST interpretation obeys

    d log k_it/dS = d log prefactor_it/dS
                           -(beta/2)(M_t^dagger-M_i).

Inferring saddle covariance from rates requires a dynamical/prefactor assumption or independent measurement. Equilibrium covariance in a well, net conductance alone, or a favorable vibrational spectrum does not supply it. Temperature sweeps do not generically separate barriers and prefactors when entropy, solvation or pathways change with temperature.

There is a quantitative acquisition cost even in an ideal channel-resolved model. Allocate independently observed at-risk exposures E_+ and E_- to legal perturbations +delta and -delta of one already installed port (negative perturbation means reducing its positive stiffness, not installing a negative spring). Assume exactly k_+=k_0 exp(delta*s), k_-=k_0 exp(-delta*s), independent homogeneous Poisson counts, and expected counts m_+=E_+ k_+, m_-=E_- k_-. For nuisance theta=log k_0 and slope s, the Fisher matrix is

    I=[[m_++m_-, delta(m_+-m_-)],
       [delta(m_+-m_-), delta^2(m_++m_-)]].

Its profiled information for s is

    I_s,eff=4 delta^2 m_+ m_-/(m_++m_-).             (17)

Near zero slope with balanced total exposure E, this equals delta^2 k_0 E. Under the regular parametric/unbiased Cramer-Rao premises, Var(s_hat)>=1/I_s,eff; the asymptotic standard-error scale is therefore 1/[delta sqrt(k_0 E)]. Rare damage slopes require exposure growing as 1/k_0 as well as inverse squared perturbation and precision. Increasing delta is limited by finite-change nonlinearity and specimen damage. This is a favorable identifiable Poisson model, not a universal confidence bound for hidden, correlated or nonstationary pathways. It explains why microscopic-force-free identification is possible in principle without making acquisition cheap.

## 9. Decisive obtainable test and matched comparator

Use one actual device class with two separately calibrated support ports, verified controllable stiffness at fixed rest coordinates, and a specified accepted transport endpoint. At the same temperature, reservoirs, voltage/pressure affinity, active-area boundary and fabrication distribution, randomize finite support conditions including each individual port, both ports, a sham fixture and the best matched strain/pressure/chemical baseline. Calibrate force-displacement or thermal compliance, preload, geometry, drift and support caps. Record state-conditioned channel events and at-risk exposure, including empty-state and fixture-induced failure routes, rather than only survivor conductance. If those event labels are unobservable, directly compare accepted cumulative output and renewal throughput over complete lifetimes; do not claim an identified hidden rate network.

Estimate local response columns from these legal perturbations, select a direction using the complete lifetime/cost objective, and predeclare a finite confirmation condition. Require accepted output per specimen, lifetime, reset time, delivered flow/current, selectivity, active area, fabrication yield and total input cost under a matched task. A support that merely shifts well clocks can fail the lifetime-yield test. An unchanged damage clock can overturn a protective occupied-state result. A decrease in prefactor or active area can defeat a barrier gain. If only fraction phi of baseline active area remains and local linear conductance gains g, total membrane conductance before access resistance is phi*g times baseline; phi*g>1 is necessary for a conductance advantage at that boundary. Other geometries and nonlinear operation require their actual circuit/fluid model.

For a homogeneous rare damage channel with zero events in predeclared at-risk exposure E, a one-sided 1-alpha confidence bound is h<=-log(alpha)/E. Charge this exposure, destructive samples, calibration, support installation, feedback (if any), reset/replacement, conditioning, heating/cooling, pressure/electrical/chemical work and disposal. No-event observation is not a zero-hazard proof. Use simultaneous channel coverage and a censoring model if multiple pathways matter.

The strongest current falsifier is simple: at fixed matched boundaries, no legal support direction improves the acquired signed lifetime response beyond its resource cost, or its finite confirmation fails once all damage routes and active area are included. That result rules out the declared fixture/device regime without claiming a universal no-go for mechanogating. A positive complete-lifetime result would establish an operational effect in that regime; microscopic attribution and scalable device advantage would remain separate obligations.
