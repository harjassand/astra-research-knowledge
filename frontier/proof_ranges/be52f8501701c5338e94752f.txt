2. First mechanism: spectral growth does not certify enough distinct neighbours
A natural attempted strengthening was
\[
\rho(A)\ge \frac n3
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{a directed triangle}.
\]
If true, it would imply the desired outdegree theorem because \(\rho(A)\ge\delta^+(A)\). It is false.
Exact spectral substitution theorem
Let \(G\) be a strongly connected loopless digraph with adjacency matrix \(B\), order \(N\ge2\), and spectral radius \(r>0\).
Replace each vertex by a transitive tournament \(T_m\), and replace each original arc by all possible arcs between the corresponding blocks. The resulting adjacency matrix is
\[
C=B\otimes J_m+I_N\otimes T_m.
\]
Then
\[
\boxed{
\rho(C)=
\frac{1}{(1+1/r)^{1/m}-1}.
}
\]
Moreover, the construction preserves directed girth and satisfies
\[
\delta^+(G[T_m])=m\delta^+(G).
\]
Proof. Choose a positive vector \(b\) with \(Bb=rb\). Put
\[
q=(1+1/r)^{1/m},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac1{q-1},
\qquad
z_a=q^{m-1-a},\quad 0\le a<m.
\]
The geometric-series identities give
\[
\sum_a z_a=\frac{\lambda}{r},
\qquad
T_mz=\lambda(z-\mathbf1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
C(b\otimes z)
=b\otimes\left(r\Bigl(\sum_a z_a\Bigr)\mathbf1+T_mz\right)
=\lambda(b\otimes z).
\]
This eigenvector is positive. Diagonal conjugation therefore turns \(C\) into a nonnegative matrix with every row sum equal to \(\lambda\). Its spectral radius is exactly \(\lambda\).
A vertex in position \(a\) of block \(i\) has outdegree
\[
m\,d_G^+(i)+(m-1-a),
\]
which proves the minimum-degree formula.
No directed cycle lies within a transitive block. The inter-block transitions of any directed cycle project to a nonempty closed directed walk in \(G\), while every shortest cycle of \(G\) lifts to the substituted graph. Thus directed girth is preserved. \(\square\)
A 20-vertex counterexample
Take \(G\) to be the directed four-cycle and write \(D_m=C_4[T_m]\). Then
\[
|V(D_m)|=4m,\qquad
g(D_m)=4,\qquad
\delta^+(D_m)=m,
\]
but
\[
\rho(D_m)=\frac1{2^{1/m}-1}.
\]
For \(m=5\),
\[
\rho(D_5)>\frac{20}{3}.
\]
This does not depend on numerical approximation:
\[
23^5=6\,436\,343
>
6\,400\,000=2\cdot20^5
\]
implies \(2^{1/5}<23/20\), hence the claimed inequality.
In fact,
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\rho(D_m)}{|V(D_m)|}
=\frac1{4\log2}
\approx0.3606737602>\frac13.
\]
The positive eigenvector above has largest-to-smallest coordinate ratio
\[
2^{(m-1)/m}<2.
\]
Thus even a bounded ratio of Perron-vector coordinates does not rescue this spectral implication.
These are not Caccetta–Häggkvist counterexamples: their minimum outdegree density is only \(1/4\). They distinguish walk growth from the minimum number of distinct outneighbours.
The transitive-tournament substitution itself is prior mathematics: the OpenAI Seymour source explicitly uses this construction and cites an earlier application. I therefore do not count the construction or this spectral calculation as an established original advance.
