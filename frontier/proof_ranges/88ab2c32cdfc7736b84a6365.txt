## The question and exact quadratic form

Let \(H=(\mathbb C^d)^{\otimes n}\), where \(d\ge3\). Identify \(C\in\operatorname{End}(H)\) with its vectorization across the two parties and put
\[
 Q_n(C)=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}}(-1/2)^{|S|}\|\operatorname{Tr}_S C\|_F^2.
\]
The endpoint Werner state \(\rho_d=(2I-F)/(2d^2-d)\) is positive and NPT for \(d>2\), and
\[
 \langle\operatorname{vec}(C),(\rho_d^\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\operatorname{vec}(C)\rangle
  =\big(2/(2d^2-d)\big)^n Q_n(C).
\]
Thus all-copy nondistillability of this particular state requires \(Q_n(C)\ge0\) for **every** \(n\) and **every complex rank-at-most-two** \(C\). This requirement remains open; the results below do not settle it.

For local matrix units \(E_{ij}\), the polarized quadratic form satisfies
\[
 B_n(E_{a b},E_{c e})=\prod_{r=1}^n\left(\delta_{a_r c_r}\delta_{b_r e_r}-\tfrac12\delta_{a_r b_r}\delta_{c_r e_r}\right),\quad Q_n(C)=B_n(C,C),
\]
with \(B_n\) conjugate-linear in its first variable.

## Theorem 1: exact local support-overlap test

Fix nonzero subspaces \(U_i,V_i\subseteq\mathbb C^d\), with orthogonal projections \(P_i,R_i\), and let \(\mathcal C\) be all operators \(C\) whose range lies in \(\bigotimes_i U_i\) and whose adjoint range lies in \(\bigotimes_i V_i\). Then
\[
 Q_n(C)\ge0\quad \text{for every }C\in\mathcal C
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}(P_iR_i)\le2 \quad\text{for each }i.
\]
**Proof.** In vectorized coordinates, the one-site Hermitian operator for \(Q_1\) is \(K=I-\tfrac12|\Omega_d\rangle\langle\Omega_d|\), where \(|\Omega_d\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^d|jj\rangle\) is unnormalized. Its compression to \(U_i\otimes\overline{V_i}\) is \(I-\tfrac12|\eta_i\rangle\langle\eta_i|\), where \(\eta_i=(P_i\otimes\overline{R_i})\Omega_d\) and \(\|\eta_i\|^2=\operatorname{Tr}(P_iR_i)\). The full compressed operator, after permuting slots, is the tensor product of these local compressions. Each factor is positive semidefinite exactly when its overlap is at most two, proving sufficiency. If an overlap exceeds two, its local compression has a strictly negative eigenvalue. Every other local compression has a strictly positive eigenvalue (on a space of dimension one this follows from \(\operatorname{Tr}(P_iR_i)\le1\); otherwise it has an eigenvalue one). A product of a negative eigenvector at the offending site and positive eigenvectors elsewhere has negative expectation. This proves necessity. \(\square\)

This classifies positivity on **entire product-defined operator spaces**, without an assumption on matrix rank. It does not classify arbitrary rank-two matrices, whose left and right local supports may overlap in more than two dimensions.
