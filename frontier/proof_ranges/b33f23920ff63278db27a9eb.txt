7. Receipts and reproducibility

explore_joint_pair.py generated candidate common baselines with a joint LP.
Its optimization output is not trusted as a continuum or feasibility proof.

verify_hard_pair.py independently reads the retained candidate, converts all
nodes and coefficients to exact Fractions, checks nonnegative components and
positive common inductance, evaluates all 116 observed components exactly, and
uses mpmath interval arithmetic at 55 decimal digits for the two Q values and
the critical-time brackets. It does not import SciPy or the grid optimizer.
The analytic impulse-positivity argument covers every input and every time.
Authoritative numerical enclosures are directed 30-digit decimal STRINGS made
from the exact binary interval endpoints. A first display implementation used
float(endpoint) and one nextafter; an independent audit found that mpmath can
truncate by more than one ulp. That display defect was corrected before this
packet was frozen. No float conversion is used for the retained enclosures.

The common baseline contains 19 finite positive modes. The normalized total
masses are about 71.368916315 mOhm (minus) and 71.225030246 mOhm (plus), each
below the source's conditional 71.4268 mOhm upper mass. The hidden normalized
nodes are exactly 20/21 (minus) and 40/41 (plus). Both models have the same
positive L about 1.59603490288e-7 H. Exact coefficient/error rationals are in
verified_hard_pair.json. The data SHA256 is retained there too.

To run the checker:
  python3 verify_hard_pair.py

To regenerate the exploratory candidates with isolated dependencies:
  uv run --with scipy --with mpmath python explore_joint_pair.py
  python3 verify_hard_pair.py

No original input or source result was modified. The checker establishes stated
arithmetic facts conditional on the model and interval-library correctness; it
is not a proof-assistant formalization or an external laboratory validation.

8. Strongest-method confrontation and novelty status

Brown and Grabovsky, On feasibility of extrapolation of completely monotone
functions, arXiv:2401.15178v2 / DOI 10.1137/24M1652325, already derive a sharp
asymptotic power-law exponent for relative-L2 extrapolation of positive
exponential mixtures. Their global extrapolation theorem is materially deeper
than the finite-pair construction here. Their exact norm/class is different, so
their exponent is not silently asserted for our acquisition contract.

Casini, Garulli, and Vicino, On input design in l-infinity conditional set
membership identification (Automatica 42, 2006), already formulate optimal
bounded-input worst-case identification through radii of feasible system sets.
Worst-case input design and midpoint information arguments are established
ideas. Grabovsky's Stieltjes reconstruction and Lawrence's spectral duality
already cover positivity-based inverse uncertainty; this is not a novelty claim
for linear-functional bounds.

Huang et al.'s 2024 hybrid time/frequency method, binary multi-frequency EIS,
and closed-loop/Fisher-information designs are entitled to their strongest
versions. The lower bound above explicitly gives them all bounded current
waveforms and the complete voltage path, rather than comparing only NNLS or
three hand-selected frequencies. It is an information bound under deterministic
voltage error, not an empirical finding that those methods fail on real cells.
