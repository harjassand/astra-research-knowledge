## Quantitative finite obstruction

Assign arbitrary permutations \(X_{iuv},L_{buj},R_{bvk}\in\mathrm{Sym}(D)\). Assume every triangle factorization has normalized Hamming error at most \(\varepsilon\), and every nondegenerate rectangle evaluated in these assigned permutations has fixed-point fraction at most \(\varepsilon\). Define \(Y_i=(P(X_{iuv}))_{u,v\in[r]}\), an \(rD\times rD\) integer matrix, where \(P\) denotes the permutation matrix.

**Lower rank.** Expanding the real trace moments gives

\[
\operatorname{tr}(Y_iY_i^T)=r^2D,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}((Y_iY_i^T)^2)
\le [2r^3-r^2+r^2(r-1)^2\varepsilon]D.
\]

There are exactly \(2r^3-r^2\) degenerate index choices; their products are identity even for arbitrary assignments. Each other trace counts fixed points of a rectangular permutation. Cauchy--Schwarz on the positive eigenvalues therefore yields

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb R}Y_i
\ge \frac{r^2D}{2r-1+(r-1)^2\varepsilon}.
\]

Each row has exactly \(r\) unit entries. The source's rank-under-reduction argument remains valid without any representation assumption: for an integer matrix of real rank \(d\) and row norm at most \(\sqrt r\), choose a nonzero \(d\times d\) minor. Hadamard bounds its absolute determinant by \(r^{d/2}\); if its rank modulo \(q\) is \(d'\), integer row operations make \(d-d'\) rows divisible by \(q\), so \(q^{d-d'}\le r^{d/2}\). Since \(q>r\), \(d'\ge d/2\). Thus

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}Y_i
\ge D\alpha(\varepsilon),\qquad
\alpha(\varepsilon)=\frac{r^2}{2[2r-1+(r-1)^2\varepsilon]}.
\]

This argument is uniform in \(D\), including \(q\mid D\).

**Upper rank with errors.** For permutations \(p,p'\), their permutation-matrix difference has nonzero columns only where \(p(j)\ne p'(j)\). Consequently

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{F}(P(p)-P(p'))\le Dd_H\(p,p'\)
\]

over **every** field \(F\). This is the crucial error bound: it concerns support and rank, so no dimension-dependent conversion from an operator norm occurs.

For a Cartesian block \(b\), form the exact matrix factorization from the assigned \(L,R\) permutations. For each fixed \(u,v\), the shifts \((\alpha_b(i)+v,\beta_b(i)+u)\) enumerate all coordinate pairs once. Its inner dimension is \(D\), and its factor order is preserved. The difference from \(\sum_{i\in I_b}Y_i\) is a sum of \(r^2|I_b|\) embedded matrix differences, each of rank at most \(\varepsilon D\). Therefore, with \(Z(z)=\sum_{i\in I(z)}Y_i\),

\[
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_q}Z(z)
\le\begin{cases}
2D+r^2|I(z)|\varepsilon D,&z\notin E,\\
rD,&z\in E.
\end{cases}
\]

**Certificate.** The interpolation identity still holds exactly for these arbitrary \(Y_i\):

\[
-\bigoplus_iY_i=\sum_{z\in P}H(z)\otimes Z(z).
\]

Rank subadditivity and \(\operatorname{rank}H(z)\le1\) imply, after canceling \(D>0\),

\[
N\alpha(\varepsilon)\le B+r^2S_0\varepsilon.
\]

Since \(\alpha(\varepsilon)\ge r/4-r^2\varepsilon/8\), this forces

\[
\Delta\le r^2(N/8+S_0)\varepsilon.
\]

Set

\[
\varepsilon_*=
\min\left\{\tfrac12,
\frac{\Delta}{2r^2(N/8+S_0)}\right\}>0.
\]

For \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_*\), the inequality would give \(\Delta\le\Delta/2\). Hence **no such assignment exists, in any finite dimension**. Although the source constants are enormous, they are fixed; soficity imposes no minimum allowed approximation error.

## Why soficity supplies the forbidden assignments

Use the finite-set definition in [Arzhantseva--Păunescu, Definition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.2626v2#S2): for any finite set of group elements and any \(\delta>0\), a sofic group has a permutation model whose multiplication errors are below \(\delta\), while every nonidentity listed element moves a fraction exceeding \(1-\delta\).

Choose one finite set containing the identity, all labeled edge-loop elements, the inverses needed in the rectangles, and every rectangular prefix product. Also include every triangle product; it equals the corresponding right-edge element. Set \(\delta<\varepsilon_*/8\). Assign \(X=\phi(x)\), \(L=\phi(\ell)\), \(R=\phi(\rho)\).

The exact group equality \(\ell x=\rho\) gives the triangle assignment error below \(\delta\). No chosen spanning-tree edge needs an exactly normalized model.

If the definition does not explicitly require \(\phi(1)=\mathrm{id}\), approximate multiplication of \(1\cdot1\) gives \(d_H(\phi(1),\mathrm{id})<\delta\). Each inverse substitution then costs at most \(2\delta\). A rectangle contains two inverses, and comparing its four-factor evaluation with \(\phi(w)\) uses three prefix multiplications. Total comparison error is below \(7\delta\). Because \(w\ne1\), \(\phi(w)\) has fixed-point fraction below \(\delta\); the actual rectangle evaluation has fixed-point fraction below \(8\delta<\varepsilon_*\). This produces exactly the forbidden assignment.

No permutation-stability theorem is used. In general, failure of residual finiteness alone would be insufficient: [Arzhantseva--Păunescu, Example 7.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1410.2626v2#S7.SS1) discusses sofic non-residually-finite Baumslag--Solitar groups, while their Theorems 4.2--4.3 explain the additional stability route. Hyperbolicity does not itself provide that stability; [Alekseev--Thom, Corollary B](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15494v1) even gives a residually finite hyperbolic group failing flexible permutation stability. Here the stronger, perturbation-resistant rank certificate supplies the obstruction directly.

## Independent geometric shortcut and remaining source gate

The source's link-combinatorics can be checked without its lengthy Euclidean thinness proof. Give every attached triangle a hyperbolic metric with angles

\[
(27\pi/50,\ 9\pi/50,\ 9\pi/50)
\quad\text{at }(O,V,W).
\]

Their sum is \(9\pi/10<\pi\), so the hyperbolic triangle exists. All triangles use one shape, and each edge type receives the corresponding common length. This changes the metric, not the fundamental group.

At \(O\), each block's link is simple complete bipartite; a cycle has at least four edges, hence length at least \(108\pi/50>2\pi\). At \(V,W\), the link projection to the punctured incidence graph is locally injective: at an \(x\)-direction a label belongs to at most one block at each point, and at an \(L\)- or \(R\)-direction the shift equation determines at most one array coordinate because \(s_b,t_b\ge r\). Therefore reduced link cycles project to reduced incidence cycles and have at least 14 edges, giving \(126\pi/50>2\pi\). A reduced link path between two distinct same-label \(x\)-directions projects to an internally reduced closed walk, which contains a simple cycle; its length is also at least 14 edges, or the directions are disconnected. Each rectangular turn thus has link distance greater than \(\pi\).

The classical link criterion and its two-dimensional form are **Bridson--Haefliger (1999), Theorem II.5.2 and Lemma II.5.6, pp. 206--207**; Theorem II.5.4 gives CAT(-1) for the simply connected cover. I checked the original pages both as text and as rendered images in this [primary reference](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/bridsonhaefligerx.pdf). Finite shapes and a finite base complex give a complete locally finite cover. If a strict polyhedral-complex convention requires cells to intersect in a single face, subdivide each hyperbolic triangle; the metric and original links remain unchanged, and the additional vertex links satisfy the usual \(2\pi\) condition. The deck action is proper and cocompact, so the group is word-hyperbolic. A finite-order deck transformation would fix a point in this CAT(0) space and hence be identity, giving torsion-freeness. A nonconstant closed local geodesic cannot be nullhomotopic, giving the nontrivial rectangles; the latter two consequences are also recorded in **II.4.13, p. 201**. This verifies the geometry **assuming the stated incidence data and cell gluings**. The geometric-action implication is independently recorded in [Kim--Walsh (2016), Lemma 3.9](https://academic.oup.com/jtopol/article/9/1/117/2459625).
