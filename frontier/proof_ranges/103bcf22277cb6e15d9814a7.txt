## Selective poisoning: real in another reaction, not a high-temperature MTO result

Ezenwa et al. 2026 report co-feeding 2,6-di-tert-butylpyridine (DTBP) to selectively titrate **extracrystalline acid sites** in MFI during toluene methylation. The primary paper, *Polymethylated Aromatics Deactivate Intracrystalline Acid Sites in MFI Zeolites during Low-Temperature Toluene Methylation*, DOI [10.1016/j.jcat.2025.116653](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2025.116653), uses 403 K, 4 kPa toluene, 65–68 kPa DME, <1% conversion, and DTBP partial pressures of roughly 0.005–0.040 kPa. It validates DTBP against MCM-41 and reports external-site poisoning without a significant initial-rate change under those conditions. The intervention separates site-location contributions during deactivation; it does not tag a methylbenzene chemical state.

The paper explicitly contrasts this low-temperature reaction with high-temperature MTH. Its own mechanism is that bulky polymethylbenzene species accumulate and block intracrystalline transport at 403 K, whereas the same types of species may dealkylate under high-temperature MTH. Therefore, this result cannot establish DTBP stability, access selectivity, neutrality, competitive adsorption, or an interpretable response at 673 K in methanol-to-hydrocarbons over H-ZSM-5. Nor does MFI poisoning establish anything in HSAPO-34’s CHA cages. I found no same-regime demonstration of selective internal HCP poisoning or post-formation chemical-state marking.
