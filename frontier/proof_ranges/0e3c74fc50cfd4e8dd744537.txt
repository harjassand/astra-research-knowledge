This is a local obstruction to a polyconvex supporting hyperplane, not
an inference from failure of a particular Gram certificate.

If a finite-valued F on all matrices is polyconvex, it has a convex
minor potential g(A,z) with F(A)=g(A,det A). This potential can be taken
finite everywhere: given any (A,z), choose B with det B=z-det A; then
(A,z) is the midpoint of (A+B,det(A+B)) and (A-B,det(A-B)). Thus a
supporting hyperplane exists at (I,1).

For smooth F its pullback must have the form

 F(A) >= F(I)+DF(I):(A-I)
         +lambda[det A-1-tr(A-I)].

For J_r, its Hessian in the e12 direction is zero. A nonnegative support
gap must have a positive-semidefinite Hessian, so the e12-e21 cross
entry of D^2(J_r-lambda det)(I) must vanish. That entry is
-(r+2)+lambda. Therefore lambda=r+2 is forced.

The resulting support gap is

 G_r(A)=J_r(A)-(r+2)j-(2/3)r(r+2)a+(r+2)(r+1)/2.

The Hessian of this gap has null direction K_r=diag(1,-r-1). Along it,
the exact polynomial identity is

 G_r(I+t K_r)
 = -r(r+2)t^3/3
   +(r+2)(9r^3+36r^2+40r+12)t^4/24.

For every allowed r, the cubic coefficient is nonzero (indeed negative).
Thus the gap is negative for sufficiently small positive t. This refutes
polyconvexity at I for every J_r, r>0 or r<-2.

The same cubic argument applies when alpha is increased while beta,
gamma=1,delta=r stay at this first-LMI boundary. H contributes only
fourth-order terms near I. Thus all these first-boundary members are
nonpolyconvex at I as well.
