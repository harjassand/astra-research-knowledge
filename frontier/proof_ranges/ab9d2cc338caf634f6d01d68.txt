## 8. Exact consequences

### 8.1 A strict-contraction criterion

Let \(P\) be the support projection of \(J\), and set
\[
B_0=\ker\operatorname{Tr}_A(I-P). \tag{8.1}
\]
For \(b\in B\),
\[
\langle b,\operatorname{Tr}_A(I-P)b\rangle
=\sum_i\|(I-P)(|i\rangle\otimes b)\|^2.
\]
Thus \(B_0\) is exactly the largest output subspace with
\(A\otimes B_0\subseteq\operatorname{supp}J\). The theorem gives
\[
\eta_D^c<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B_0\ne\{0\}. \tag{8.2}
\]
Equivalently, there is a density \(\sigma\) and \(\varepsilon>0\) with
\(J\succeq\varepsilon I\otimes\sigma\): the channel has a positive completely-positive replacer component. The connection to Doeblin minorization is prior context [3]; the equality formula determines more than that qualitative bound.

### 8.2 A matrix harmonic dual and exact products

When \(B_0\ne0\), compress \(J^+\) to \(K\succ0\) on \(A\otimes B_0\). Then
\[
\boxed{\quad
\beta(\mathcal N):=1/M(\mathcal N)
=\min_{\theta_A\text{ a state}}
\operatorname{Tr}\left[\operatorname{Tr}_A((\theta_A\otimes I)K)\right]^{-1}.
\quad} \tag{8.3}
\]
To prove it, express (3.3) as
\(\min_\sigma\max_\theta\operatorname{Tr}K(\theta\otimes\sigma^2)\).
Convex minimax exchanges the two optimizations. For
\(H_\theta=\operatorname{Tr}_A((\theta\otimes I)K)\succ0\), weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\[
\min_{\sigma\succeq0,\operatorname{Tr}\sigma=1}\operatorname{Tr}H_\theta\sigma^2
=1/\operatorname{Tr}H_\theta^{-1},
\]
attained by \(\sigma=H_\theta^{-1}/\operatorname{Tr}H_\theta^{-1}\). This proves (8.3).

For two channels with finite \(M_i\), their common output subspaces multiply. Indeed, with \(S_i=\operatorname{Tr}_{A_i}P_i\), the eigenvalues of \(S_i\) are at most \(d_i\); the eigenspace of \(S_1\otimes S_2\) at \(d_1d_2\) is exactly the product of the individual top eigenspaces. Hence \(B_{0,12}=B_{0,1}\otimes B_{0,2}\).

Product choices in the primal (3.3) give \(M_{12}\le M_1M_2\). Product choices in the dual (8.3) give \(\beta_{12}\le\beta_1\beta_2\), which is the reverse inequality for \(M\). If a factor has \(\beta=0\), fixing the other input gives coefficient one for the product. Therefore
\[
\boxed{\quad\beta(\mathcal N_1\otimes\mathcal N_2)=\beta(\mathcal N_1)\beta(\mathcal N_2),\quad}
\]
\[
\boxed{\quad\eta(\mathcal N_1\otimes\mathcal N_2)
=1-(1-\eta(\mathcal N_1))(1-\eta(\mathcal N_2)).\quad} \tag{8.4}
\]
Here \(\eta\) denotes any of the three equal coefficients, not the ordinary classical coefficient or a fixed-state contraction constant.
