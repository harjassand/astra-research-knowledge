Let a finite reversible network have endpoints A,B and M intermediate nodes
z_1,...,z_M. Its only edges are A--z_i and z_i--B. Both edges of channel i have
the same positive conductance w_i. Fix gamma>0 and epsilon in (0,1).

An algorithm knows M,gamma,epsilon and the following promise: either all
w_i=gamma/M (null), or one unknown channel J has
w_J=gamma/M+2*epsilon*gamma, with all others unchanged. Its permitted acquisition
operation is a named-channel query i returning w_i, at unit cost. It may choose
queries adaptively and randomly and may stop randomly. Endpoint aggregate rates,
weighted-neighbor samples, and a full graph listing are not supplied.

The required output is an interval [L,U] with L>0 and U/L<=1+epsilon containing
cap(A,B) with probability at least 1-alpha on every promised instance, where
0<alpha<1/2. Then its expected number of queries on the null instance is at least

                     M*(1-2*alpha).                          (1)

Proof of the capacity formula
-----------------------------
With endpoint potentials u_A=0,u_B=1, channel i has energy
w_i*u_i^2+w_i*(1-u_i)^2. This is minimized independently at u_i=1/2, yielding
cap(A,B)=sum_i w_i/2. Thus c_0=gamma/2 on the null and
c_1=(1+2*epsilon)*gamma/2 on every alternative. No admissible relative-width
interval can contain both numbers.

This is a legitimate finite continuous-time reversible Markov chain: choose
uniform stationary probability pi=1/(M+2), set q_xy=c_xy/pi for the edges and
diagonal rates to minus the row sums. The statement is nevertheless about the
declared conductance acquisition oracle, not unrestricted access to this CTMC.

Proof of the query lower bound
-----------------------------
Run the algorithm on the null, retaining its internal randomness. Independently
choose a uniform hidden J, and run the alternative with the same randomness.
Until J is queried the transcripts coincide. If D is the set of distinct queries
made by the null run, coupling failure has probability E_0|D|/M. Therefore the
total variation distance between the null transcript/output and the uniform
mixture of alternative transcripts/outputs is at most E_0|D|/M.

Let E be the event that the output interval has the required width and contains
c_0. Null correctness gives P_0(E)>=1-alpha. On any alternative E is a failure
event, because it excludes c_1, hence P_J(E)<=alpha. Their mixture has the same
upper bound. Total variation is at least 1-2*alpha. Combining these inequalities,
E_0 Q >= E_0|D| >= M*(1-2*alpha), proving (1). If E_0 Q is infinite the claim
is automatic. Random stopping does not evade the coupling argument.

Implication
-----------
Even a tiny capacity gamma/2 admits classical exact variational certificates.
