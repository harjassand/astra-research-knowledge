The replacement repair is elementary but addresses that issue directly.
Suppose a rational candidate \(z\) lies in an original face \(F\), and it satisfies a fixed rational affine constraint
\[ Cy=d. \]
In the rounding algorithm, this is the temporary cost slice \(c^\top y=c^\top x_0\), whose right-hand side comes from the original input.
Choose a dyadic mesh \(h=2^{-p}\) and enclose \(z\) coordinatewise:
\[ \ell_j=h\lfloor z_j/h\rfloor,\qquad I_j=[\ell_j,\ell_j+h]. \]
Now return a vertex of the ordinary rational linear-programming polytope
\[ \boxed{ F\cap\{y:Cy=d\}\cap\prod_j I_j. } \]
This is not a nonconvex optimization problem.
The intersection contains \(z\), so it is nonempty. Its returned point \(\widehat z\) satisfies
\[ \|\widehat z-z\|_\infty\le h, \qquad \|\widehat z-z\|_2\le\sqrt n\,h, \]
while preserving the affine constraint exactly and remaining in \(F\).
A vertex of the intersection is determined by at most \(n\) independent facet, face, slice, and cell equations. Boolean polytopes admit integer facet and affine-hull descriptions with \(O(n\log n)\)-bit coefficients, by determinant bounds. The cell endpoints have \(O(p)\) bits. Therefore Cramer’s rule bounds the output encoding polynomially in the original data and \(p\), independently of the candidate’s denominator length.
Two distinctions are essential.
The returned point need not be dyadic. A slice such as \(y_1=1/3\) may prohibit that. The enclosing cell is dyadic; the returned point is rational.
The cell is discarded after repair. Subsequent directions use the minimal face of the original polytope, not the artificial faces of the clipped cell. The repaired point may enter a smaller original face; that is permitted and useful.
For explicitly listed rational inequalities, I implemented this operation without an LP solver: repeatedly choose a rational direction annihilating the active constraints and push to the first boundary. Each push adds an independent active constraint, so at most \(n\) pushes reach a vertex of the clipped slice. In the general oracle model, ordinary exact rational LP supplies the same operation.
This removes the need for the earlier, more elaborate affine-anchor repair.
The geometric starting point survives scrutiny:
\[ \boxed{\dim F\ge N/k,} \]
where \(N\) is the number of coordinates varying on an original face \(F\).
Indeed, edge directions span the face tangent. A basis of \(\dim F\) such directions must cover every varying coordinate, and each direction has support at most \(k\). This remains true after every original-face transition.
The constructive proof uses that reserve differently from the old global-minimization argument.
Work in signed coordinates \(Q=2P-\mathbf1\). Set
\[ K=64k,\qquad R=\lfloor\sqrt K\rfloor,\qquad H=32R^2,\qquad \eta=\frac1{Kn}. \]
Retained row coefficients \(b_{ij}\) give
\[ p_{ij}=b_{ij}^2,\qquad q_i=\sum_jp_{ij}. \]
Delete a coefficient whenever
\[ p_{ij}>q_i/K, \]
until every retained row is sufficiently spread out. Large rows, with \(q_i>R^2\), have their tracked sums held constant. Medium rows use rational dyadic scales \(r_i\) satisfying
\[ q_i\le r_i^2<4q_i. \]
For a medium row, let
\[ G_i(y)=\sum_jp_{ij}(1-y_j^2) \]
and introduce the two barriers
\[ u_{i\sigma}(y) = \frac{\sigma d_i-H}{r_i} +\frac{RG_i(y)}{2r_i^2} +12R-e, \qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\}. \]
Here \(d_i\) is the rational tracked discrepancy and \(e\) is a small numerical allowance. The maintained condition is \(u_{i\sigma}\le0\).
The spectral matrix is
\[ M= \sum_{i,\sigma} 32K r_i^{-4}e^{u_{i\sigma}/(2R)}p_ip_i^\top +B+\eta\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top, \]
where \(B\) is the diagonal contribution from large rows. The potential is
\[ \boxed{ \Phi(y)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(M)-8\bigr)_+^3 -\eta\|y\|_2^2. } \]
The norm includes frozen coordinates. The spectral matrix acts on the remaining coordinates.
The retained-row and energy-corrected spectral machinery builds on recent constructive Komlós methods; it is not presented as an independently invented framework. Those methods already establish polynomial-time ordinary signing, including a polynomial-bit construction in Li’s work. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30044)
Write \(M=M_0+\eta\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\). The matrix \(M_0\) is positive semidefinite and entrywise nonnegative, so it has a nonnegative unit top eigenvector \(v_0\), with
\[ (\mathbf1^\top v_0)^2\ge1. \]
