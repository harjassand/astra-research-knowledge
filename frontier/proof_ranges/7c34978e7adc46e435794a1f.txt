## 4. Exact consequence: increasing weights do not change the universal abundance constant

For \(c\in[0,1]\), consider two statements.

**Uniform abundance:** Every finite nontrivial union-closed family has an element occurring in at least a \(c\)-fraction of its members.

**Increasing-weight abundance:** For every probability distribution \(p\) with nontrivial union-closed support and increasing weights,

\[
\max_i\Pr_{A\sim p}(i\in A)\ge c.
\]

### Theorem 2

These statements are equivalent.

### Proof

The weighted statement immediately implies the uniform one.

For the converse, apply Theorem 1 to \(p\). Let

\[
r_k(i)=\Pr_{Q_k}(i\in A_1),
\qquad
p_i=\Pr_p(i\in A).
\]

The one-block bound gives, simultaneously for every \(i\),

\[
|r_k(i)-p_i|
\le
\varepsilon_k
:=
\sqrt{\frac{\log(4L_k)}{2k}}.
\]

Permutation invariance means that every lifted coordinate \((j,i)\) has frequency \(r_k(i)\). Applying uniform abundance to the actual lifted set family therefore yields

\[
c\le\max_i r_k(i)\le\max_i p_i+\varepsilon_k.
\]

Letting \(k\to\infty\) proves the weighted statement. ∎

**This is a universal equivalence across all finite families and ground-set sizes. It is not a claim that reweighting one fixed family cannot reduce its largest frequency.**

There is also a quantitative counterexample reduction. A weighted counterexample satisfying

\[
\max_i p_i\le c-\eta
\]

produces a finite uniform counterexample once the local error is smaller than \(\eta\). A block length of order

\[
k=
O\!\left(
\eta^{-2}\log\frac{b+1}{\eta}
\right),
\qquad b=\lceil\log_2m\rceil,
\]

suffices. The randomized construction above can find the corresponding compact family description with controlled failure probability.

The increasing-weight condition cannot simply be weakened to

\[
p(A\cup B)\ge\min\{p(A),p(B)\}.
\]

A September 2026 preprint constructs distributions satisfying that weaker condition, with zero weight at the empty set, for which every element has arbitrarily small weighted frequency. That result concerns a different weighted class and does not contradict Theorem 2. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25101v1)
