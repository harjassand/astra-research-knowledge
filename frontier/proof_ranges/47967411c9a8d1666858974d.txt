## Independent calculations

The separate global amplitude transfer-matrix implementation in `analysis/independent_checks.py` does not import producer optical code. It reproduces the eight-layer reference points and independently calculates the seven-layer candidate. A separate cross-implementation check compares it with the unchanged producer recursion across the complete 121-row frequency CSV and 801 static-wavevector points; the largest relative complex-reflection difference is below 3.0e-14 in the frequency grid and 3.9e-15 in the static sweep. The producer's finite-check tolerances and grid ranges are listed in `replay/replay_receipt.md`. For an 80 nm internal stack, 5 nm h¹⁰BN caps, a 20 nm witness height, and the source Lorentz inputs in vacuum:

| Design/model | Nominal 1470 cm⁻¹ witness | Sixfold damping | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Source 8-layer 50:50 pair | -13.295% | -13.086% | Reproduced reference calculation |
| 7-layer h10 positions {0,4,5} pair, d = 80/7 nm | -12.600% | -12.595% | New mirror-matched model scenario |

These are differences divided by the pair mean, exactly as in the source. They are not predicted target changes. Two passive, synthetic oscillator spectral weights centered at 1470 and 1572 cm⁻¹ give opposite signs when integrated against the ideal pair's delta Im(r_p) at fixed q=51 μm⁻¹, despite identical ideal static reflection. This establishes a precise identifiability limit: static equality plus a local optical witness does not determine a target-weighted response when the target's frequency weighting is unknown. The oscillator weights are mathematical countermodels, not κ-ET parameters.

To expose the omitted substrate boundary, the independent code also replaces the *terminal* vacuum with synthetic, real, nondispersive isotropic permittivities from 1 to 20. For these fixtures, the source design remains between -13.295% and -13.261%, and the seven-layer design between -12.600% and -12.566%. Ideal static reflection remains equal to roundoff at all five synthetic loads. These cases show only behavior under simple model loads; they cannot stand in for measured, anisotropic, complex, temperature-dependent κ-ET data. No material values were invented for κ-ET.

Local one-at-a-time \(±1%) mathematical probes show the largest witness sensitivity to the assumed h¹¹BN in-plane LO and TO endpoints (up to about 2.7 percentage points of contrast from these probes); this is not an uncertainty interval. The proposal has no joint h¹¹BN damping, high-frequency permittivity or normal-axis fit. Thickness/cap probes are likewise not fabrication tolerances. The full parameter table is preserved in `analysis/independent_results.json`.

The ideal mass-scaled model has equal static response to floating-point roundoff. The published-endpoint reference leaves a maximum relative static-reflection residual 1.91893e-5 over the tested grid. Equal ε(0) for every hBN layer makes order irrelevant at zero frequency even with a different terminal substrate, but actual isotope films require measurement because structural renormalization can alter their static tensor.

The Lorentz form with positive damping is the source model's causal linear response under its stated time convention. The validator checks nonnegative evanescent Im(rp) only on a sampled finite frequency/wavevector grid; it does not certify passivity over all bands or acquire a full measured dielectric. The source's q integral is truncated to 1.2e6–1e9 m⁻¹. Its imaginary-frequency comparison samples xi from 0.01 to 1e5 cm⁻¹ and q from 1e6 to 4e8 m⁻¹; there is no tail estimate or rigorous continuum supremum. In the unloaded symmetric vacuum geometry, reversing the stack reflects the boundary-value problem and preserves its mode spectrum, while exchanging the fields seen from the two faces. Thus the one-face reflection witness can differ even when the isolated mode spectrum agrees. A one-sided substrate load removes that symmetry and requires the full loaded stack to be measured or recomputed.
