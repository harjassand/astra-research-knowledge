all-spectrum, crossing-jet, and fixed-area interface needed by a countable library.

VERDICT

I reconstructed Lemma 2.3 and found no counterexample or missing hypothesis in
its stated finite-cutoff claim. The key fact is weaker than full Strong Arnold
transversality: every unwanted positive repeated eigenspace admits SOME nonscalar
first variation inside the two tangent constraints retaining the designated
double. Its proof by equal product spans, equal nodal sets, and a nodal-domain
energy test is sound in this real scalar, connected, smooth surface setting.

The paper proves a finite-q conclusion, not an all-spectrum one. The additional
countable interface can nevertheless be reconstructed, with the explicit Baire
spaces and endpoint qualifications below. In particular, a single exact-double
metric can have every other eigenvalue simple, and a smooth curve through it can
have no other repetition anywhere while retaining its value and prescribed
nonzero first derivative. These are internal existence proofs, not external
validation of the manuscript, quantitative algorithms, or novelty findings.

Dependence on Lemma 2.2 is limited to its actual small finite-pool perturbation
argument for a compact path, rather than bare existence of some joining path.
The distinct assigned audit of Lemmas 2.1-2.2 remains relevant to that premise.

FINITE LEMMA: RECONSTRUCTION

Work on the connected closed surface S^2 over real functions. Let
  rho_w = e^(2w)/integral e^(2w) dmu,
  h0(w) = rho_w g0,
where dmu is the unit round normalized measure. The Dirichlet form q is
independent of w in dimension two. Its mass form m_w changes by
  m'_w(f,g)[xi] = 2 integral fg (xi - integral xi dmu_w) dmu_w.
At a positive scalar eigencluster lambda I, the first cluster matrix is therefore
  D_ij[xi] = -2 lambda integral u_i u_j (xi - mean_w xi) dmu_w.
Changes of orthonormal frame contribute zero at a scalar matrix.

For an orthonormal real pair u,v in the chosen exact double, define
  U1=u^2-v^2, U2=2uv,
  K={xi: integral U1 xi dmu_w = integral U2 xi dmu_w = 0}.
The U's have zero mean and are independent. To check the latter directly, a
nontrivial relation alpha U1+beta U2=0 is a nonzero traceless real quadratic
form in (u,v). Its zero set is two distinct lines. On a small connected open set
where (u,v) is nonzero, continuity confines (u,v) to one line. A nonzero linear
combination of u,v then vanishes on that open set and hence everywhere by
unique continuation, contradicting orthonormality. Thus K has codimension two.
The chosen double has a local two-component traceless constraint F_d with
surjective derivative and ker DF_d=K.

Claim: for every different positive repeated cluster E_mu, there is xi in K
whose compression to E_mu is nonscalar. Suppose not. Choose any orthonormal
pair a,b in E_mu and put A1=a^2-b^2, A2=2ab. Scalarity for every xi in K means
both A functionals vanish on K. The annihilator of the kernel of the surjection
xi -> (integral U1 xi, integral U2 xi) is precisely the span of its components.
Since A1,A2 are independent by the same argument, their span equals the U span.
Consequently, as identities of smooth functions,
  Q(a,b) = T Q(u,v), Q(x,y)=(x^2-y^2,2xy), T in GL(2,R).
There is no unknown additive constant: this is an identity of functionals on
the full smooth space, and the product functions all have zero mean.

The set a=0 is the inverse image under Q(a,b) of the closed negative horizontal
ray, including its origin. An invertible linear T takes the inverse image of
this ray to another closed ray. Identifying R^2 with C, Q is complex squaring;
the preimage of any closed ray is one real unoriented line. Hence for some
(c,d) != (0,0),
  {a=0}={f=0}, f=cu+dv.
This equality includes simultaneous zeros, so no division by u,v,a,b is needed.

Let Omega be a connected component of this common nonzero set and change
signs so a,f>0 there. Both restricted functions lie in H^1_0(Omega), even with
a singular nodal boundary. Indeed (f-epsilon)_+ on Omega has compact support
inside Omega: the boundary has f=0 and the ambient sphere is compact. These
truncations converge in H^1 by dominated convergence of functions and gradients,
and interior smoothing approximates each compactly supported truncation. Do the
same for a. Their weak eigenvalue equations may now be tested against each other:
  q_Omega(a,f) = mu integral_Omega af dmu_w
               = lambda integral_Omega af dmu_w.
The integral is positive, forcing mu=lambda, a contradiction. Thus the claim
holds for every unwanted repeated eigenspace, of any finite multiplicity.

To integrate the retaining tangent, choose smooth zeta_1,zeta_2 for which
DF_d on their span is invertible. In the three-parameter family
  w(t,x)=w+t xi+x_1 zeta_1+x_2 zeta_2,
the finite-dimensional implicit-function theorem solves F_d=0 by x(t), with
x(0)=0 and x'(0)=0 because xi is in K. The corrected curve therefore has
tangent xi; the unwanted matrix is mu I+tD+o(t), D nonscalar. Spectral continuity
then separates its distinct first-order D groups for sufficiently small t!=0.

Extend the finite list through q+1 to the complete cluster containing q+1;
call its last position q'. Preserve the gap after q', the two isolation gaps
of the designated double, and every previously strict internal gap. Under
each unwanted split, sum_j binomial(m_j,2) strictly decreases. No clusters can
merge across a preserved gap; the designated cluster's contribution stays one.
There are at most sum_j binomial(m_j,2)-1 successful unwanted splits. Finite
iteration therefore removes every unwanted repetition through q, including
the gap after q. Each finite-dimensional repair can be as small as desired in
any given finite list of C^r seminorms. This proves density in the C-infinity
topology, not just in the C^2 norm used to define U.

No full m-by-m surjectivity for unwanted high-multiplicity eigenspaces has
been assumed or established. A single nonscalar D suffices. Likewise the
argument does not prove a codimension-two non-crossing rule for paths entirely
confined to the exact-double slice. Only POINT density in that slice is needed.

EXTRA COUNTABLE INTERFACE: EXPLICIT PROOF

1. A Baire exact-double slice.

Choose a small spectral neighborhood W of w* with exactly two eigenvalues
inside the contour for the chosen double and strict outer isolation gaps.
Its smooth real 2-by-2 cluster matrix M_d defines
  F_d(w)=(M_11-M_22,2M_12).
The exact-double set D={w in W:F_d(w)=0} is precisely the designated double
locus in this neighborhood; isolation holds there and fixes its positions.

Here is a sufficient chart without invoking a general Frechet inverse theorem.
Use C^2(S^2) as a Banach space. On fixed L^2(dmu), the normalized conformal
operator can be written
  A(w)=rho_w^(-1/2)(-Delta_0)rho_w^(-1/2), domain H^2.
It is a smooth C^2-parameter family of bounded maps H^2->L^2. Positivity,
elliptic compact resolvent, the resolvent identity and finite-rank real frame
construction make F_d a smooth map to R^2 on a C^2 neighborhood. Split
  C^2 = ker DF_d(w*) direct-sum span{zeta_1,zeta_2}.
The Banach implicit-function theorem gives a graph
  w=w*+z+chi_1(z)zeta_1+chi_2(z)zeta_2, z in ker DF_d(w*).
Restrict z to smooth functions. The kernel in C-infinity is a closed Frechet
space, and the graph map and its inverse are continuous in that topology:
the correction has only two fixed smooth directions and its coefficients are
continuous already in C^2. Thus D is locally homeomorphic to an open subset
of a complete metrizable Frechet space and is a Baire space. Smooth dependence
along smooth finite-parameter families also follows from the Banach chart.

For q>=i+1 let D_q require every inequality lambda_j<lambda_{j+1}, j<=q,
except j=i. D_q is relatively open by finite spectral continuity. The finite
lemma above, applied at each point of D and staying in the local chart, makes
it dense in D. Baire gives a parameter w_d in every prescribed neighborhood
of w* with exactly this one double and every other eigenvalue simple.
This is a countable extension newly reconstructed here; Lemma 2.3 itself has
only a finite-q quantifier. It supplies no simultaneous spectral gap over q.

2. Crossing curves fixing value and first derivative.

After choosing w_d, choose a smooth direction xi with nonzero traceless
cluster compression B=M'_d[xi]-(tr M'_d[xi])/2 I, equivalently DF_d(w_d)[xi]!=0.
If a fixed direction at w* already has
this property, w_d can be taken close enough to retain it by continuity.
Take a small interval J=[-a,a] and work in the affine relative space
  C={gamma in C-infinity(J x S^2): gamma(0)=w_d,
                                      partial_tau gamma(0)=xi}.
These are closed linear evaluation conditions after translation, so the
underlying affine space is complete and metrizable. Requiring gamma(J) in U
and chosen isolation neighborhoods defines an open Baire subset. It is
nonempty for sufficiently small a. Endpoint values are allowed to vary here;
alternatively they may be fixed at already all-spectrum-simple parameters.

For any such curve, use the chosen local Riesz frame and write
  M_d(tau)=c(tau) I+tau C(tau), C(0)=B.
The trace-free
quotient is smooth by the integral formula for division by tau. Its two
eigenvalues are distinct near zero, hence the designated pair has gap at least
c_gamma |tau| and is simple for small tau!=0. Its two eigenlines extend smoothly
across zero and exchange ordered positions. All other finitely many gaps
through any specified q are strict near zero because they are strict at w_d.

Let C_q mean every finite gap through q is strict for tau!=0, with exactly
the designated exception at tau=0. This is open in C. Near zero use continuity
of the divided cluster matrix in the C^1 path norm, together with the fixed
value and first derivative. Away from a small neighborhood use positive
minimum finite gaps on compact subintervals. Spatial C^2 control gives the
required finite operator/spectral continuity.

C_q is dense in C as follows. For any trial curve and any finite collection
of desired smooth seminorm bounds, choose a small central interval where it
already satisfies C_q. If endpoints are free, first make them simple through
q by arbitrarily small smooth changes supported near the endpoints; continuity
then gives simple small endpoint intervals. If they are fixed and already
all-spectrum simple, this step is unnecessary. Apply the small finite-direction
avoidance perturbation separately to the two remaining compact legs. A cutoff
vanishes near their simple endpoints and is one on every possible bad portion.
At each bad point its two traceless equations have rank two in the finite
perturbation parameters, by the unrestricted real pair splitting formula.
Cover exact repeated blocks by countably many local codimension-two zero sets
as in source (2.9). With one time parameter their projection to the parameter
space has measure zero: each is locally a Lipschitz image of dimension D-1
inside R^D. An arbitrarily small parameter therefore avoids them all. This
perturbation is supported away from zero and fixes the prescribed crossing
jets. It can be arbitrarily small in the stated finite seminorms.

Consequently intersection_q C_q is residual and nonempty. One curve has the
designated exact double only at zero, all other spectrum simple at every
phase, and the fixed nonzero first jet. There need not be one central interval
on which every trial curve is already all-spectrum simple; its finite-q
central interval is allowed to shrink as q grows. This is why Baire is needed.

The same proof works with any further prescribed finite-order or full Taylor
jets of a given smooth reference curve at zero: those evaluation conditions
are closed, and every density perturbation is supported away from zero. It
does not preserve a fixed OPEN NEIGHBORHOOD of zero. If that frozen segment
has an unwanted high-energy repetition, no permissible perturbation can
remove it. Also one cannot fix the original value w* while removing other
degeneracies it already has; select the all-other-simple w_d first.

3. All-simple joining paths and splicing.

Unrestricted finite simplicity is open/dense, hence an all-spectrum-simple
base exists in any open smooth neighborhood. In the relative smooth path
space joining any two all-spectrum-simple endpoints inside convex U, each
finite-q all-simple condition is open/dense by the same small path proof.
Baire yields an all-spectrum-simple joining path. Fixed constant endpoint
intervals are permitted because the prescribed endpoint metrics are already
all-spectrum simple. Join each crossing segment endpoint to a common base
this way. Smooth reparametrization can be the identity near the crossing and
constant near joins; it preserves the crossing jets and the all-simple
spectrum on the join intervals. No eigenvalue branches need be analytic.

4. Exact angular area and crossing controls.

The source's normalized conformal metric has total area 4pi, not yet a fixed
pointwise area form. The explicit Moser map Phi_w gives h(w)=Phi_w^*h0(w) with
  dvol_h(w)=4pi dmu
at every parameter, including the Baire-selected smooth curves. Solving the
fixed mean-zero Poisson problem, forming a positive density vector field, and
integrating its flow depend continuously in C-infinity and smoothly along
smooth parameter curves. Thus taking a smooth limit or fixing phase jets of
w also transports those properties to h. The variation caused by the gauge
is a commutator with the scalar eigenspace operator; its compression vanishes.
Neither the splitting rank nor the exact double is changed.

For a continued gauged eigenbasis e_1,e_2 at a crossing, let
u_a=e_a compose Phi_w^(-1) be the corresponding pre-gauge eigenfunctions.
Choose the conformal parameter direction xi_control=u_1 u_2. Its off-diagonal
derivative, before and after the gauge, is
  -2 lambda integral (u_1 u_2)^2 dmu_w != 0.
The product cannot vanish identically, by unique continuation. The scalar
normalization term does not enter the off-diagonal entry. This supplies the
required control after the reference curve and its first jet have been fixed.
It does not alter those reference jets until the later controlled metric is
deliberately perturbed. At finitely many crossing types all positive gaps,
slopes, and control coefficients have positive minima individually; no
uniform lower bound across infinitely many spectral positions follows.

HYPOTHESES, FAILED STRONGER INFERENCES, AND REMAINING GATES

The proof uses real scalar eigenfunctions, real symmetric cluster matrices,
smooth positive metrics on a connected compact boundaryless surface, and
unrestricted smooth conformal directions. The simple zero mode cannot be
the chosen double. Connectedness supplies unique continuation and simplicity
of constants; compactness gives finite clusters and the nodal truncations.
The two-dimensional conformal Dirichlet invariance is the variation formula
used here; no extension to arbitrary dimensions or complex/magnetic/Hodge
operators is established. The real Q-squaring and ray argument is essential.

Antipodally even directions suffice only for pair splitting within a fixed
parity. Cross-parity products are odd and unavailable there; one cannot
import full-spectrum path avoidance or constrained splitting into that
restricted family. Lemma 2.3 explicitly allows unrestricted directions,
which is the correct interface. Stronger rotational symmetry is an immediate
obstruction to splitting the real sine/cosine azimuthal pairs. Keeping a
fixed finite-dimensional direction pool for the whole infinite spectrum is
also unsupported; each finite-q density proof may choose a new finite pool.

The countable proof requires smooth relative spaces. C^2 convergence alone
does not produce a smooth metric or preserve all prescribed phase jets, and
bare endpoint point-density does not establish density of good curves.
Conversely, the finite-pool proof provides arbitrarily small perturbations
in every specified finite set of smooth seminorms, which is enough for
Frechet Baire. Endpoint values fixed at nonsimple high eigenvalues invalidate
the all-spectrum good-path claim; they must be free or already fully simple.

There is no unclosed spectral existence gate within this Lemma-2.3/countable
interface reconstruction, conditional on the pair splitting and the stated
finite-path perturbation proof. The source's generation of the initial
isolated doubles at all desired adjacent positions (Lemmas 2.4-2.5), the compact
library organization, and the subsequent Ricci/PDE/transfer construction
were not proved by this audit. No formal proof, external expert validation,
certified numerical spectrum, or historical priority check was produced.
