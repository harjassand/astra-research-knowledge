## 2. Constructive positive recursion

Let

\[
 \Phi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_iXV_i^*,\qquad
 \sum_{i=1}^{d}V_iV_i^*\preceq I,
 \qquad P=BB^*.
\]

Define

\[
 \mathcal F(X)=g_a(\Phi(X)+P),\qquad X\succeq0. \tag{2.1}
\]

### Theorem 2.1 — explicit acquisition and norm convergence

There is a unique bounded positive solution

\[
 X_* = \mathcal F(X_*).
\]

Starting from `X_0=0`, the iterates `X_{k+1}=F(X_k)` increase to `X_*` in operator norm and satisfy

\[
 \boxed{\quad 0\preceq X_*-X_k\preceq\frac{1}{a(k+1)}I.\quad} \tag{2.2}
\]

More precisely, for positive `X,Y`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|\mathcal F(X)-\mathcal F(Y)\|
 \le g_a(\|X-Y\|).
 \quad} \tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently, for any positive initial value `Y_0`,

\[
 \|Y_k-X_*\|
 \le\frac{\|Y_0-X_*\|}{1+ak\|Y_0-X_*\|}.
\]

If a bounded positive target `A` is only a subsolution,

\[
 A\preceq\mathcal F(A),
\]

then

\[
 \boxed{\quad A\preceq X_*\preceq X_k+\frac{1}{a(k+1)}I.\quad} \tag{2.4}
\]

#### Proof

Put `delta=||X-Y||`. Positivity and subunitality of `Phi` give

\[
 \Phi(X)+P\preceq\Phi(Y)+P+\delta I.
\]

For every positive `Z`, functional calculus gives

\[
 g_a(Z+\delta I)-g_a(Z)
 =\delta(I+aZ)^{-1}(I+aZ+a\delta I)^{-1}
 \preceq\frac{\delta}{1+a\delta}I.
\]

Apply monotonicity, then exchange `X` and `Y`, proving (2.3). The scalar iterate is

\[
 g_a^{\circ k}(t)=\frac{t}{1+akt}.
\]

Every image of `F` is between zero and `a^{-1}I`. The sequence starting at zero is monotone. For `m>k`, its tail obeys

\[
 \|X_m-X_k\|
 \le g_a^{\circ k}(\|X_{m-k}\|)
 \le g_a^{\circ k}(a^{-1})
 =\frac1{a(k+1)}.
\]

It is therefore norm-Cauchy. Norm-continuity of `F` shows that its limit is a fixed point. Two positive fixed points at distance `delta` would obey `delta<=g_a(delta)`, forcing `delta=0`. Monotonicity and the tail estimate prove (2.2). Iterating (2.3) proves the general convergence estimate. Finally, a positive subsolution increases under iteration and all its iterates converge to the same fixed point. Thus `A<=X_*`, proving (2.4). ∎

### Theorem 2.2 — the representation is generated by source words

Suppose `rank B=r<infinity`. Let

\[
 W_k=\operatorname{span}\{V_{i_\ell}\cdots V_{i_1}u:
 u\in\operatorname{ran}B,\ 0\le\ell<k\},
 \qquad W_0=\{0\},\quad D_k=\dim W_k.
\]

Then `ran X_k` is contained in `W_k`; in particular `rank X_k<=D_k`. The fixed point is compact, and for every positive subsolution `A`,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \lambda_{D_k+1}(A)\le\frac1{a(k+1)}.
 \quad} \tag{2.5}
\]

Eigenvalues are ordered nonincreasingly and padded with zeros. Such a positive `A` is also compact.

#### Proof

The support inclusion follows inductively because `Phi(X_k)+P` is supported on `W_{k+1}`, and `g_a(0)=0`. Equation (2.2) approximates `X_*` by finite-rank operators in norm, proving compactness. A positive operator dominated by a compact positive operator is compact: for finite-rank projections onto the leading eigenvectors of `X_*`, the norm of `(I-Q)A^{1/2}` tends to zero, so `A^{1/2}` is compact. Apply min–max to (2.4), using `rank X_k<=D_k`, to obtain (2.5). ∎

If the `V_i` commute, the number of distinct monomials of degree less than `k` is `binomial(d+k-1,d)`, so

\[
 D_k\le r\binom{d+k-1}{d}.
\]

Without relations, only the generally much larger bound

\[
 D_k\le r(1+d+\cdots+d^{k-1})
\]

is automatic. More generally, an actually proved estimate `D_k<=C k^s` yields eigenvalue counting of order at most `t^{-s}` through (2.5). This is a consequence of an acquired source algebra, not an assumption on the unknown target eigenvectors.

**Computational costs.** Achieving the uniform norm error in (2.2) needs `k=O((a epsilon)^{-1})` iterations. This is not logarithmic dependence on accuracy. Each step must apply the `V_i`, acquire the generated span, and evaluate a finite-dimensional inverse on it. The size, conditioning, coefficient precision, and cost of acquiring that span remain charged. For dense `N x N` inputs, ordinary dense matrix operations provide an implementation, but do not remove `N` from the cost. In an infinite-dimensional representation, computability and cost of source-word inner products must be established from that representation; they are not free oracles.

If positive approximate iterates incur norm errors `eta_j` per step, nonexpansiveness gives the safe bound

\[
 \|\widetilde X_k-X_*\|
 \le\frac1{a(k+1)}+\sum_{j=1}^k\eta_j.
\]

Thus both truncation and rounding must be paid. The supplied floating-point script is a diagnostic, not an interval-certified implementation.

### Source perturbations

For fixed `Phi`, let `X_P` and `X_Q` be the fixed points for positive sources `P,Q`. If `||P-Q||<=eta`, the same modulus argument gives, with `delta=||X_P-X_Q||`,

\[
 \delta\le g_a(\delta+\eta),
 \qquad
 \boxed{\quad
 \delta\le\frac{\sqrt{\eta^2+4\eta/a}-\eta}{2}.
 \quad} \tag{2.6}
\]

This estimate is attained by `Phi(X)=X`, `P=eta I`, and `Q=0`, with scalar fixed points. It applies to perturbations **inside** `g_a`. Section 7 treats a different error, added **outside** `g_a`; the signs of the two formulas differ.
