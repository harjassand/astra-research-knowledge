## 6. A representation obstruction and its explicit replacement

### 6.1 Finite scalar-source rank fails on a simple product spectrum

On a Hilbert basis indexed by `alpha in N_0^n`, define

\[
 Ae_\alpha=\frac1{|\alpha|+2}e_\alpha.
\]

This definition is sufficient; no physical model or unknown spectral theorem is required. At total degree `m`, the eigenvalue multiplicity is

\[
 M_{n,m}=\binom{n+m-1}{m}.
\]

The reciprocal eigenvalues are the integers `m+2`. For `a=1`, a half-open window of length one contains one degree level. On the cutoff `|alpha|<=m`, Theorem 3.2 therefore gives exactly

\[
 \boxed{\quad r_1=\binom{n+m-1}{m}.\quad} \tag{6.1}
\]

For the full space and `n>=2`, this tends to infinity, despite the fixed operator norm `||A||=1/2`. At `n=m=20`, the minimum scalar-source rank is `68,923,264,410` and the full cutoff dimension is `137,846,528,820`.

Thus a dimension-free operator norm does not imply a small-rank source representation. This is an exact obstruction to a proposed route, not evidence against a general concentration theorem or a general computational class separation.

### 6.2 The same operator has an explicit finite-channel source recursion

The operators in this construction are the standard symmetric multivariable shifts, not newly invented operators; see the established multivariable operator theory in [6]. The calculation below places them in the present recursion and checks the resulting costs. Define

\[
 V_i e_\alpha=
 \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i+1}{|\alpha|+1}}e_{\alpha+e_i},
 \qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]

These operators commute. A direct coefficient calculation shows

\[
 \sum_i V_iV_i^*=I-|e_0\rangle\langle e_0|.
\]

Set `P=|e_0><e_0|` and `Phi(X)=sum_i V_i X V_i^*`. For degree `m>=1`,

\[
 \Phi(A)e_\alpha
 =\sum_i\frac{\alpha_i}{m}\frac1{m+1}e_\alpha
 =\frac1{m+1}e_\alpha.
\]

At the vacuum, the source equals one. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\quad A=g_1(\Phi(A)+P).\quad} \tag{6.2}
\]

A rank-one source and `n` explicitly given propagation operators suffice. There is no contradiction with (6.1), which constrains one endomorphism `T` with `T^*T=A` and the paired operator `TT^*`, not a general multi-channel map.

The generated space `W_k` is exactly the degrees less than `k`, with

\[
 D_k=\binom{n+k-1}{n}.
\]

The iterates reproduce `A` exactly on those degrees and vanish on the others; their norm error is exactly

\[
 \|A-X_k\|=\frac1{k+2}.
\]

This also proves the universal `1/k` convergence order in Theorem 2.1 is sharp. The representation has changed from a huge scalar source to a small list of formulas, but the multiplicities survive in the generated spaces. For large `n` and `k`, those spaces remain large. The example does not establish a general tensor-compression theorem or a speedup over the explicit diagonal baseline.

### 6.3 Why a small number of channels is still not a spectral-compression guarantee

For the product example, the multiplicity formula immediately gives

\[
 A^{1/2}\in S_q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q>2n.
\]

Thus the single-operator Schatten law of Section 4 cannot be transferred to arbitrary row maps merely because the source has rank one.

There is an even sharper example. On the Hilbert basis of finite words over `d>=2` letters, let `V_i` prepend letter `i`, and let `P` project onto the empty word. The `V_i` have mutually orthogonal ranges and satisfy `sum V_i V_i^*=I-P`. Define

\[
 Ae_w=\frac1{|w|+2}e_w.
\]

Again `A=g_1(Phi(A)+P)`, by the same one-step scalar recurrence. Each degree has `d^m` vectors. The operator is compact, but `Tr(A^s)=infinity` for every finite `s>0`, because `sum_m d^m/(m+2)^s` diverges. Therefore compactness and a rank-one source coexist with failure of every finite Schatten bound in the multi-channel setting. The missing information is precisely the growth of generated spaces, not the number of initially supplied formulas.
