10. A MATCHED COMPARATOR THAT LIMITS THE WINDOW CLAIM

The leading-log limit is NOT specific to finite frequency variance. If phi
is any even probability density with finite absolute logarithmic moment,
the exact error formula in Section 9 still applies. Its tail tends to zero
by logarithmic integrability, and its truncated second-moment term tends to
zero by dominated convergence. Thus J_phi(A)=log A-c_phi+o(1) without a
finite mu2. Finite variance buys the explicit A^-2 rate, not existence of
the leading-log generator. This distinction prevents overvaluing the
cosine optimization as if it created the Landau limit.

For the Fejer comparator, its characteristic function is 1-|t| on [-1,1].
The same logarithm identity gives
  E log|X|=1-gamma,  c_Fejer=3/2-gamma=0.9227843350984671... .
At EQUAL maximum lag L, its large-K radial coefficient is
  a_Fejer=pi/r[log(KLr)-c_Fejer+o(1)],
whereas cosine uses T=L/2 and gives
  a_cos=pi/r[log(KLr)-(log 2+c_cos)+O((KLr)^-2)].
The cosine offset log 2+c_cos=0.9815721015668906... is actually larger
than c_Fejer by 0.0587877664684235... . Hence cosine is not uniformly
superior even among these finite-lag Coulomb coefficients. Neither offset
is the physically derived subleading screened constant. The valid advantage
is its sharp finite-variance angular/error control in the stated class;
the universal leading-log leverage belongs to finite-memory radial
elimination itself. The next decisive task remains microscopic acquisition,
not optimization of this subleading number.
