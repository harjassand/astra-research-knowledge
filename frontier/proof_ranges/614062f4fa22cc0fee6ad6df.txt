## 1. Audit and change of representation

The preceding near-line theorem concerns a compatible gradient X satisfying two relative smallness conditions about a *single fixed matrix line*. Its proof relies on a covariance-fitted line, a weighted positive Schur expansion, and an L²/L⁴ mixed-derivative estimate. The replayed symbolic checks verify its contact algebra and weighted polynomial coefficients, not its analytic interpolation, uniform-remainder argument, or exhaustion of all contact types.

A spatial partition by the locally dominant matrix direction does not preserve gradients: if X=Dphi, then cutting it by an indicator generally creates curl at the interfaces. Likewise, the constant background selected by complete optimization cannot be minimized independently on arbitrary spatial pieces without accounting for interfaces and cross terms. Critical concentration does not remove this difficulty.

The new route does not attempt such a partition. It builds a *potential-level family* for which a rank-one tangent remainder and a positive acoustic integral exactly represent the global gap. It then investigates which different families may be superposed without changing the sign of the fully optimized quartic interaction.

Throughout, matrix inner products are Frobenius. Periodic cells carry normalized Lebesgue measure when expectations are used. Integral identities are valid with normalized or unnormalized measure.
