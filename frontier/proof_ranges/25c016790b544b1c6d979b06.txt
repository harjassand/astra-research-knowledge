## 7. Exact interfaces that change the conclusion

### 7.1 Observable path dual witnesses

For any fixed bounded predictable vector critic f(t,H_t), the visible jump compensator identity is

    E int f dot dE(Y_t) = E int f dot rbar_t dt.

Therefore

    L(f) = (2/T) E int f dot [dE(Y_t)-vhat(t,H_t)dt]
                         - (1/T) E int ||f||^2 dt,

    D_cond = sup_f L(f),
    D_cond-L(f) = (1/T) E int ||e_t-f(t,H_t)||^2 dt.    (12)

The supremum is achieved by f=e, whose norm is at most 2sqrt(2)R. Every frozen critic gives an observable lower bound on D_cond without any history matching or hidden state information. This is an exact falsification operation. Absence of a detected critic gives an upper certificate only if a supplied approximation or complexity promise ensures that the search can approximate e. In the hard family, the sign pattern xi is precisely the missing exponential critic description/acquisition.

A conservative finite acquisition bound is explicit. For a critic with ||f||<=F, its single-path score Z_f obeys

    |Z_f| <= (2sqrt(2)F/T) N_T + 2sqrt(2)FR+F^2,

where N_T is visible jump count, dominated by Poisson(lambda), lambda=RT. Zero the score when N_T>L, without conditioning or discarding resets. The absolute truncation bias is at most

    (4sqrt(2)FR+F^2) P(Poisson(lambda)>=L).

The elementary exponential-moment bound makes this <=epsilon/2 by taking

    L >= (e-1)lambda + log[2(4sqrt(2)FR+F^2)/epsilon].

The retained score is bounded by

    K_L=(2sqrt(2)F/T)L+2sqrt(2)FR+F^2.

For a frozen finite library of h critics, n>=8 K_L^2 epsilon^-2 log(2h/zeta) independent episodes estimates all L(f) to epsilon with probability at least 1-zeta. Numerical integration of the predictable critics and predictor is additionally charged. If the critic is fitted, use independent validation or a justified uniform complexity bound. This operation can efficiently disprove a proposed predictor when a simple witness is available; it does not bypass (9) for universal completeness.

### 7.2 Independent conditional replicas

Suppose there is an extra operation: after an unprobed random stop with observed history H_U, prepare X_1 and X_2 independently from the posterior law Law(X_U | H_U), keeping the same history, time and target action. Then

    D_cond = E <r(X_1)-vhat(H_U), r(X_2)-vhat(H_U)>.    (13)

Independent short continuations give its rate cross-moment. In particular the mixed endpoint derivative of

    E <E(Y^1_{h_1})-E(Y_U), E(Y^2_{h_2})-E(Y_U)>

at (h_1,h_2)=(0,0) is E||rbar_U||^2. Tagged first derivatives acquire the predictor cross terms. Finite time jets can be acquired as in the main note, now from a joint conditional-preparation experiment. Gains are unnecessary for this particular cross-moment once the conditional replica operation is supplied.

Copying the actual hidden state is different: two independent future branches from the same X_U give E||r_U||^2, and substituting this in (13) yields D_raw. They are not independent posterior draws conditional only on the visible history. Likewise applying two gain factors on one edge produces a same-state moment. Neither operation supplies (13).

Ordinary reset/rejection can realize conditional replicas only at its actual matching cost. Exact matching of continuous histories need not even have positive probability. In this finite family the relevant context is discrete and uniform; matching a specified context by fresh resets costs N in expectation, whereas pooling accidental matches yields the sqrt(N) aggregate-testing scale of Sections 4 and 6. A native conditional reset/resampling primitive would remove that cost and is a genuinely stronger acquisition interface. Its correctness cannot be inferred from the product law.

### 7.3 A supplied finite conditional closure

Let C=C(U,H_U) be a known observable context with at most L states, including enough information to make the predictor C-measurable. Suppose a supplied closure guarantee gives

    delta_C^2 = E ||rbar_U-E[rbar_U | C]||^2 <= delta^2.

Let mu_c=P(C=c) and m_c=E[1{C=c}(r_U-vhat)]. Then

    D_cond = sum_{c:mu_c>0} ||m_c||^2/mu_c + delta_C^2. (14)

The first moments m_c are acquired by context-tagged first-time jets with the tag and predictor frozen at the stop, or from the compensator identity in (12). The masses are ordinary occupation observations. No product probe or hidden reconstruction is needed.

There is no need to divide by arbitrarily small measured masses: since ||r_U-vhat||<=2sqrt(2)R, omitting all contexts with mu_c<eta/L loses at most 8R^2 eta in (14). A robust empirical version uses simultaneous mass errors <=eta/(4L), retains cells with muhat_c>=eta/(2L), and divides only by muhat_c-eta/(4L)>0. Every retained true mass is at least eta/(4L), and omitted total mass is below 3eta/4. Moment confidence intervals supply a conservative upper bound for each retained numerator. Thus fixed L, a supplied closure error delta and charged tagging/predictor computation give a polynomial finite acquisition procedure with controlled truncation, statistical and jet errors.

The closure error is a substantive supplied or independently justified gate. It is not certified by observing the finite cell moments alone; the counterexample has exponentially many contexts, and a small unvalidated representation cannot silently stand in for them. Predictor quantization, if needed to make it C-measurable, requires an additional explicit error allowance.

## 8. Source and scope receipts

The source of product-gain completion is the full original Astra N718 proof, pinned at revision `5fc72a144d77c31b400efc0768afd7833c702362`, read in the main investigation. Its off-diagonal product law, selective neutrality, bounded-rate requirement and independent repeated preparation are at lines 43-64, 96-108, 265-302; its nonstationary same-state Gram identity is at lines 124-168. The present obstruction is not imported from that source. It targets a stronger conditional functional that the source does not claim to acquire. The original source remains unreviewed.

High-cardinality conditional distribution testing is an established subject. [Canonne, Diakonikolas, Kane and Stewart, Testing Conditional Independence of Discrete Distributions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.11560) gives general upper and lower bounds with explicit dependence on conditioning-domain size. Its full theorem is not imported here; the simpler paired-sign birthday proof in Section 4 is self-contained. [Chakraborty, Fischer, Goldhirsh and Matsliah, On the Power of Conditional Samples in Distribution Testing](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2012/154/) explicitly distinguishes independent conditional sampling from ordinary sampling. Its conditional oracle is broader than the posterior-replica interface in Section 7.2; this is a conceptual interface comparison, not an attribution of a new sampling oracle. The two public abstracts were reopened on 2026-10-12.

The contributed claim is scoped: under the actual reset/random-stop/product-gain interface, a universal removal of conditional hidden-rate variance meets an exponential history-association acquisition barrier, even with exact known source synthesis. The exact counterexample, transcript domination, finite collision lower bound and observable alternatives are internally reconstructed here. This does not establish a first result in conditional-independence testing, a new general martingale duality, a physical demonstration, or a foundational capability already achieved.

Separate verification files check exact finite mixture probabilities, generator/probe identities, rate and norm bounds, projection and terminal hazard algebra, collision-statistic moments and marked numerical occupation integrals. These checks supplement the proofs above. They do not independently validate all conditional statements, physical interfaces or historical priority.
