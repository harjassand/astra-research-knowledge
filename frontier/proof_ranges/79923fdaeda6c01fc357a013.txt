## Theorem 2: second-order stability of the canonical zero witnesses

Let \(n\ge2\) be even, \(d\ge3\), \(a=0^n\), \(b=1^n\), and
\[
 C_0=|a\rangle\langle a|-|b\rangle\langle b|.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{rank}(C_0)=2\), \(Q_n(C_0)=0\). For **every twice differentiable path** \(C(t)\) of rank-at-most-two complex matrices with \(C(0)=C_0\),
\[
 Q_n(C(t))=t^2 H+o(t^2)\quad\text{with } H\ge0.
\]
The assertion is only second-order: it does not say \(Q_n\) is nonnegative in a neighborhood of \(C_0\).

**Proof.** Write \(P=\operatorname{span}(a,b)\) and \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\) on \(P\). Since \(PC_0P=J\) is invertible, every nearby rank-two matrix has a block-Schur parameterization
\[
 C(t)=(I+X(t))M(t)(I+Y(t))^*,
\]
where \(M(t):P\to P\), \(X(t),Y(t):P\to P^\perp\), \(M(0)=J\), \(X(0)=Y(0)=0\). Indeed, the invertible upper-left \(2\times2\) block determines the lower-right block by the rank-two Schur identity. Put \(M(t)=J+tM_1+O(t^2)\), \(X(t)a=t x+O(t^2)\), \(X(t)b=t z+O(t^2)\), \(Y(t)a=t y+O(t^2)\), \(Y(t)b=t w+O(t^2)\), with \(x,y,z,w\perp P\). The first-order term is
\[
 T=M_1+xa^*+ay^*-zb^*-bw^*.
\]
The second-order term has, modulo terms inside \(P\times P\) or off its diagonal blocks, the component \(xy^*-zw^*\). All the omitted terms are \(B_n\)-orthogonal to \(C_0\). Directly from the matrix-unit formula, \(B_n(C_0,C_0)=0\), \(B_n(C_0,T)=0\); hence
\[
 H=Q_n(T)+2\operatorname{Re}B_n(C_0,xy^*-zw^*).
\]
For each string \(c\notin\{a,b\}\), define \(m_0(c)=\#\{i:c_i=0\}\), \(m_1(c)=\#\{i:c_i=1\}\),
\[
 \alpha_c=2^{-m_0(c)},\quad\beta_c=2^{-m_1(c)},\quad
 A_c=\prod_i(\mathbf1_{c_i=0}-\tfrac12)-\prod_i(\mathbf1_{c_i=1}-\tfrac12).
\]
Let \(\mathcal B=\{0,1\}^n\setminus\{a,b\}\), and let \(\mathcal D\) be the remaining strings, i.e., those containing a symbol other than zero or one. For \(c\in\mathcal B\) set \(\bar c=(1-c_1,\ldots,1-c_n)\), \(\sigma_c=(-1)^{m_0(c)}\). Matrix-unit multiplication gives the **exact** decomposition
\[
\begin{split}
 H={}&2^{-n}|(M_1)_{aa}+(M_1)_{bb}|^2+|(M_1)_{ab}|^2+|(M_1)_{ba}|^2\\
 &+\sum_{c\in\mathcal B}\alpha_c\left(|x_c-\sigma_c\overline{w_{\bar c}}|^2+|y_c-\sigma_c\overline{z_{\bar c}}|^2\right)\\
 &+\sum_{c\in\mathcal D}\Big[\alpha_c(|x_c|^2+|y_c|^2)+2A_c\operatorname{Re}(x_c\overline{y_c})\\
 &\hspace{35mm}+\beta_c(|z_c|^2+|w_c|^2)-2A_c\operatorname{Re}(z_c\overline{w_c})\Big].
\end{split}
\]
To verify the decomposition, note that local off-diagonal matrix units are \(B_1\)-orthogonal to everything except themselves, while any pair of diagonal units has pairing \(\delta_{ij}-1/2\). The only off-block cross pairings occur between \(E_{ca}\) and \(E_{b\bar c}\), or between \(E_{ac}\) and \(E_{\bar c b}\), with \(c\in\mathcal B\). Their normalized coefficient is \(\sigma_c\alpha_c\). The \(C_0\)-curvature pairing vanishes for \(c\in\mathcal B\), and is \(A_c\) for diagonal \(E_{cc}\) with \(c\in\mathcal D\).

For even \(n\), the binary terms vanish because \(A_c=0\) on \(\mathcal B\). On \(\mathcal D\), parity gives \(A_c=0\) or \(|A_c|=2^{1-n}\). Because every \(c\in\mathcal D\) has \(m_0(c),m_1(c)\le n-1\), one has \(|A_c|\le\alpha_c\) and \(|A_c|\le\beta_c\). Thus each \(\mathcal D\) bracket is nonnegative by \(2|uv|\le|u|^2+|v|^2\); the other terms are manifest sums of squares. Consequently \(H\ge0\). \(\square\)

### Scope and unclosed obligation

Theorem 2 proves that an arbitrary smooth rank-two witness curve cannot make \(Q_n\) negative at order one or two near this family of zero witnesses. It does **not** eliminate negative terms of higher order or distant negative rank-two witnesses, so it does not prove four-copy, general even-copy, or all-copy undistillability. Similarly, Theorem 1 is a product-support characterization, not a characterization of all rank-two supports. Establishing an NPT bound-entangled state still requires universal positivity \(Q_n(C)\ge0\) for **all** \(n\) and **all complex rank-two** \(C\), or another complete construction.

### Verification and originality qualification

The algebraic second-order coefficient was independently expanded by matrix-unit contractions and its proposed sum-of-squares formula was checked with exact rational random tangent instances for \(n=2,4,6\), and with complex Gaussian-integer instances for \(d=3\), \(n=2,4,6\), and \(d=4\), \(n=4\). Those are diagnostics, not substitutes for the proof. No exhaustive priority search, proof-assistant certification, external specialist review, or historic novelty determination was completed.
