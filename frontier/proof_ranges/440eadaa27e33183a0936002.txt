## 4. Why the oracle and numerical interface is polynomial

There are three places where a seemingly constructive proof could still conceal an unavailable resource. The manuscript addresses each.

### Matroid faces and line endpoints require no additional oracle

For a rational point \(y\), separation over the base polytope reduces to minimizing
\[
S\longmapsto r(S)-y(S).
\]

A rank evaluation takes at most \(n\) independence queries by greedy augmentation. Exact submodular minimization is polynomial-time, so the separation procedure is explicitly reducible to the stated independence interface. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0004089)

The minimal face is also recoverable without enumerating tight sets.

After deleting zero coordinates and contracting one coordinates, let \(T_j\) be the smallest tight set containing \(j\). It is found by minimizing
\[
r(S)-y(S)+\varepsilon_0|S|,
\qquad j\in S,
\]
where a rational denominator bound for \(y\) determines a sufficiently small positive \(\varepsilon_0\).

Two coordinates belong to the same block when they occur together in every tight set. These blocks can be recovered from the \(T_j\). The face’s tangent equations are exactly
\[
\sum_{j\in C}h_j=0
\]
for the recovered blocks \(C\), together with zero motion on fixed coordinates.

Their right-hand sides in the face description are integers. No fractional-coordinate block can be a singleton, giving the dimension bound \(s/2\).

Exact line endpoints are one-variable rational linear programs using the same separator. Precision resets are linear programs with that separator plus explicit equations and box constraints.

### Tiny coefficients do not force an uncharged precision requirement

A medium-row matrix contribution is evaluated in the normalized form
\[
\boxed{
w_{i\sigma}p_ip_i^\top
=
\frac{K^2}{2}e^{u_{i\sigma}/(2K)}
\left(\frac{p_i}{r_i^2}\right)
\left(\frac{p_i}{r_i^2}\right)^\top.
}
\]

Here
\[
\left\|\frac{p_i}{r_i^2}\right\|_2^2\le\frac1K,
\qquad
\|\nabla u_{i\sigma}\|_2\le2.
\]

Thus the calculation does not multiply an enormous \(r_i^{-4}\) by an underflowing exponential. Very negative exponents can be truncated with a certified error; the remaining exponentials need only polynomially many precision bits.

The spectral matrix also has a known gap:
\[
\lambda_1(M)-\lambda_2(M)\ge\eta.
\]

The positive rank-one term \(\eta\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top\) supplies this gap. It bounds the required eigenpair and reduced-resolvent precision. Approximate derivatives can consequently be computed by rational arithmetic to the prescribed tolerances; no exact exponential or exact eigenvector oracle is used.

### Denominator bounds do not recur through the whole trajectory

After each reset, state coordinates have a fixed polynomial encoding bound determined by the original input and the chosen grid precision.

A single unreset direction and endpoint may have a larger polynomial encoding length, but they are reset before the next iteration.

Offsets remain bounded-bit as well: each row absorbs at most \(n\) removed-coefficient contributions, and cleanup occurs **after** reset. The absorbed coordinates therefore already have the uniform encoding bound.

This is the distinction between a polynomial real-arithmetic operation count and the polynomial-bit claim made here.
