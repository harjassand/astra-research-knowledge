CRAY result — analytic anchoring for cancellation-dominated computation
The strongest result is a candidate coefficient-checkable principle for certifying which integral a complex stochastic computation actually evaluates.
For an explicit interacting-field family, the construction replaces estimation of an exponentially small oscillatory average by estimation of ordinary moments of a contractive stochastic process. The important step is not introducing another stochastic representation. It is proving that the representation computes the intended answer without assuming an inaccessible correctness certificate.
This investigation does not establish the requested major operational breakthrough. The mathematical mechanism survives the checks below, but its generality, historical originality, and practical advantage remain unresolved. The strongest specialised comparator won the executed connected-system benchmark, and the conservative error guarantee is prohibitively expensive.

1. The obstruction selected
The independent screen covered goal-directed inverse inference, response computation in chaotic systems, multireference imaging, and oscillatory field integrals. I selected the last because it presented two distinct obstructions that a single mechanism might address.
The first is cancellation. Consider
\[
Z(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}e^{-V(x)+\zeta W(x)}\,dx.
\]
At \(\zeta=i\), contributions have different complex phases. Sampling from the positive reference law proportional to \(e^{-V}\) gives the estimator
\[
\frac{Z(i)}{Z(0)}=\mathbb E[e^{iW(X)}].
\]
Writing this expectation as \(a\), the sample mean from \(N\) independent observations has relative mean-squared error
\[
\boxed{\frac{|a|^{-2}-1}{N}.}
\]
An exponentially small average phase therefore makes this particular estimator exponentially expensive.
The second obstruction is correctness after avoiding cancellation. Complex Langevin methods replace the oscillatory integral with a positive stochastic process on complex variables. But the process can converge and still reproduce the wrong integration cycle. Stationarity, small residuals, and several standard diagnostics do not settle that question. This remains an explicit issue in the primary literature. DOI
The operational question became:
Can the correct integration cycle, mixing rate, and estimator variance be certified from the supplied coefficients before trusting the simulation?

2. The proposed operation: anchor the proof, not the noisy data
The construction uses a complex parameter \(\zeta\) and the process
\[
dX_t=-\nabla S_\zeta(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dB_t,
\qquad
S_\zeta=V-\zeta W,
\]
where \(B_t\) is ordinary real Brownian motion, while \(X_t\) takes complex values.
The proposed correctness operation is analytic anchoring:
Establish a common domain in which the stochastic processes remain confined and contract uniformly. Prove that their stationary polynomial expectations are holomorphic functions of \(\zeta\). Identify those functions on a real interval, where the process samples an ordinary positive Gibbs distribution. Then extend the identity to the complex target.
This does not extrapolate a finite set of noisy real-parameter measurements. The algorithm runs at the required complex parameter values. Analytic continuation transports the correctness proof, not the measured data.
Complex Langevin itself is established. So are implicit solvers and earlier invariant-strip analyses. Neither is claimed as an invention here. arXiv
The candidate contribution is the combination of an acquired multivariate coefficient certificate, an argument selecting the real integration cycle, and quantitative computational bounds.
3. An explicit model where the required certificate is obtainable
Let \(G\) be a graph with \(n\) vertices and maximum degree \(D\). The supplied input consists of rational coefficients:
\[
A=A^\top,\qquad C=C^\top,\qquad a,c>0.
\]
The matrix \(A\) is strictly row-diagonally dominant with positive diagonal. Off-diagonal entries of \(C\) lie on graph edges. Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
V(x)={}&\frac12x^\top Ax+\frac a4\sum_i x_i^4\\
&+\frac c4\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}
\left[(x_i+x_j)^4+(x_i-x_j)^4\right],
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
W(x)=\frac12x^\top Cx.
\]
The matrix \(C\) can have full rank. This is not another fixed-rank construction.
The primary output is
\[
L=\log\frac{Z(i)}{Z(0)},
\]
with the logarithm continuously continued from \(\zeta=0\). Additive error \(\epsilon\) in this complex logarithm gives relative error at most \(e^\epsilon-1\) in the represented ratio. The procedure also supports fixed-degree holomorphic observables and the less demanding intensive output \(L/n\).
The finite certificate
Choose an open parameter rectangle
\[
|\Re\zeta|<u_0,\qquad |\Im\zeta|<v_0,
\qquad v_0>1.
\]
Compute
\[
m=\min_i\left(A_{ii}-\sum_{j\ne i}|A_{ij}|\right),
\]
\[
c_1=\max_i\sum_j|C_{ij}|,
\qquad
c_2^2=\max_i\sum_j C_{ij}^2,
\]
and
\[
m_*=m-u_0c_1,\qquad
\theta_*=\min\left\{\frac{3a}{1+2D},\,3c\right\}.
\]
The construction accepts a radius \(r>0\) when
\[
\boxed{\lambda=m_*-(3a+24cD)r^2>0}
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
\eta=m_*r-(a+8cD)r^3
-\frac{v_0^2c_2^2}{4r\theta_*}>0.
}
\]
These are directly checkable coefficient inequalities. They contain no unknown stationary law, partition function, spectral gap, or experimental oracle.
The radius itself need not be supplied. Setting \(s=r^2\) reduces its acquisition to
\[
m_*s-(a+8cD)s^2-\frac{v_0^2c_2^2}{4\theta_*}>0,
\]
intersected with
\[
0<s<\frac{m_*}{3a+24cD}.
\]
This is a concave quadratic feasibility problem. The delivered code either acquires a rational radius or rejects this sufficient test.
