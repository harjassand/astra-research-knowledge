The complete analytic proof is in the attachment. The following gives its structure, including the dimension count and the finite termination argument.

### 4.1 Strengthen the target by retaining one objective

Rather than first constructing an unbiased distribution, prove that for every \(c\in\mathbb R^n\), there is a good vertex \(y\) with
\[
c^\top y\le c^\top x.
\]

A separation argument will then produce exact marginals.

Pass to the sign polytope
\[
Q=2P-\mathbf1,\qquad z_0=2x-\mathbf1.
\]
Set
\[
K=16k.
\]

The sign-coordinate target is
\[
c^\top v\le c^\top z_0,
\qquad
\|A(v-z_0)\|_\infty\le2K^3+6K.
\]
Dividing by two recovers the stated Boolean-coordinate bound.

### 4.2 Track row errors without accumulating a loss at every stage

For each row, retain coefficients \(b_{ij}\) and an offset, so that the tracked row value is an affine function \(d_i(z)\). Write
\[
p_{ij}=b_{ij}^{2},
\qquad
q_i=\sum_jp_{ij}.
\]

Whenever
\[
p_{ij}>\frac{q_i}{K},
\]
discard that retained coefficient and put its current contribution into the offset.

The entire history of these discards costs at most \(4K\) in any row. The reason is a telescoping estimate. At a discard,
\[
\sqrt q-\sqrt{q-a^2}
=
\frac{a^2}{\sqrt q+\sqrt{q-a^2}}
\ge\frac{|a|}{2\sqrt K}.
\]
The first discard occurs only when \(q<K\). Hence the total absolute discarded mass is less than \(2K\), and each discarded coordinate can subsequently change by at most two:
\[
\big|[A(z-z_0)]_i-d_i(z)\big|\le4K.
\]

The resulting bound is for the **whole construction**, not a per-stage error summed over a growing number of stages.

### 4.3 Use a spectral barrier with feasible-direction freedom

The analytic part uses energy-corrected row barriers and a positive Gram matrix formed from squared retained coefficients. Those ingredients come from the spectral-signing proof, rather than from the failed final-output quartic objective. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.23540v1)

Let \(F\) be the current minimal feasible face, with \(s\) nonconstant coordinates. The construction maintains a twice-differentiable potential
\[
\Phi(z)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(M(z))-1\bigr)_+^3
-\eta\|z\|_2^2,
\qquad
\eta=\frac1{Kn}.
\]

Its invariants imply
\[
\Phi(z)\le0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\lambda_{\max}(M(z))<2.
\]

They also keep tracked row errors below \(2K^3\). The negative quadratic term supplies strictly negative curvature when the spectral part can be made locally flat or concave.

The key additional claim is that this curvature argument still works **inside the current feasible face while preserving the objective equation**
\[
c^\top z=c^\top z_0.
\]

At an interior candidate minimum, the constraints on a direction consume at most:

- \(5s/K^2\) dimensions for protected rows and tight row barriers;
- \(4s/K+2/K\) dimensions for the relevant spectral directions;
- one dimension for the objective.

The face-dimension lemma therefore leaves a subspace of dimension at least
\[
\frac{s}{k}
-\frac{5s}{K^2}
-\frac{4s}{K}
-\frac2K
-1.
\]

For \(K=16k\) and \(s>K\), this is strictly greater than
\[
\frac{5s}{K}.
\]

That is sufficient for the restricted-trace curvature argument. In the resulting direction \(h\),
\[
L'[h]=0,\qquad L''[h,h]\le0,
\]
where \(L=\lambda_{\max}(M)\). Consequently, when \(L>1\),
\[
\Phi''[h,h]
=
3(L-1)^2L''[h,h]-2\eta\|h\|_2^2<0.
\]
When \(L\le1\), the spectral term has zero Hessian and the same strict negativity follows directly.

The full proof derives the required matrix inequalities and checks that sufficiently small moves in either sign remain feasible. Thus a relative-interior point cannot be a local minimum while \(s>K\).

### 4.4 Obtain finite progress without assuming convergence

This is where the argument changes from a rounding trajectory to a face-descent proof.

Keep the current row data fixed. Within the compact face \(F\), minimize \(\Phi\) subject to the maintained row constraints, the objective equation, and
\[
L(z)\le2.
\]

The current point is feasible, so the set is nonempty. A global minimum exists by compactness. Its potential is still nonpositive, which ensures \(L<2\).

The preceding negative-curvature argument says that this minimum **cannot lie in the relative interior of \(F\)** when \(s>K\). It must lie in a proper face.

Restrict to that smaller face and update the retained coefficients. The proof checks that these updates cannot increase the potential.

Every stage strictly decreases face dimension. Therefore there are at most \(n\) such stages—even when a stage does not immediately make an individual coordinate integral.

Eventually the current face has at most \(K\) nonconstant coordinates. Choose its minimum-cost vertex. This preserves the objective inequality and adds at most \(2K\) to any row.

The total sign-coordinate error is therefore
\[
\underbrace{2K^3}_{\text{tracked row}}
+
\underbrace{4K}_{\text{all discard errors}}
+
\underbrace{2K}_{\text{terminal rounding}}
=
2K^3+6K.
\]

This establishes the proposed objective-aware upper bound.

**An important limitation remains:** the compact minimization problem is generally nonconvex. Existence of its minimum is not an efficient algorithm for finding it. The proof does not hide that computational problem behind an oracle assumption.
