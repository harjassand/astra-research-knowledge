## 7. Exact conclusion and limits

1. The paper's **implemented local replacement** is a one-slice thermal force on an otherwise noncompact, unconstrained path. This equation-level prescription lies inside the incoming finite-anchor theorem's scope for fixed positive mass, fixed anchor count, and bounded massive-source moments.
2. The theorem and independent first-order calculation both say that one fixed thermal slice does not carry a finite bulk thermal response as the auxiliary domain grows. The derivative tends to the vacuum tadpole; the finite-β target has the Bose-enhanced tadpole.
3. This does **not** refute the initial-state Schwinger–Keldysh contour, nor a future implementation that keeps its exact KMS constraint or derives an $O(1)$ nonlocal correction. Such a model is outside the finite-anchor theorem and remains to be checked.
4. A Fourier normalization or correctly transformed integration variable cannot repair the missing thermal factor. An altered mobility/noise could change the sampler, but then correctness must be re-established.
5. The PR authors' plotted ω=0 discrepancy has the sign predicted by the weak-coupling test, but no source code/raw data reproduction was available. The finite-λ numerical explanation remains **unknown**.
6. For a real temperature-correct response output available today, M–T's positivity-constrained direct functional bounds from standard compact thermal Euclidean data are a concrete alternative, conditional on a reliable sampling/error model and reflection positivity. They provide an interval, not a recovered exact spectrum or a free data source.

No new efficient many-body algorithm, generic thermal solver, physical-device result, or priority claim is established here.
