## Concrete obtainable interface

The existing interface is suitable for an isotope-coded C4 campaign with a detector modification:

1. Retain the published 4 mm quartz fixed bed, 100 mg H-ZSM-5, 400 °C, 1 bar, 20 mL NTP/min He, and 7.6 μmolC methanol pulses. The feed is single-carbon 12C/13C methanol. The source used two saturators near 18 °C, a roughly 2 mL sample loop, and a four-way valve upstream of that loop to select the isotope stream. Feed each code/control condition the same number of pulses, total methanol carbon, pulse interval, and total run time; use the existing 75 s and 225 s intervals as initial candidate scales.
2. Define the feed boundary at the catalyst-bed inlet. Measure p13(t) and total methanol flow there on a matched bypass, using a fast isotope-sensitive MS/IRMS channel and an inert tracer. Do not infer it from the valve command. The paper gives no measured inlet waveform or line/loop dead-volume response. As an order-of-magnitude check only, sweeping 2 mL at 20 mL/min is 6 s; this is not an observed switching edge.
3. Condition the catalyst under a fixed 12C feed, then run a repeated balanced isotope code until both product isotope distributions and total C4 yield repeat cycle-to-cycle. Measure and charge all startup output; the observed 29 at% 13C aromatic carryover shows that five heavy pulses alone are not a clean start. Monitor total conversion, C4 selectivity, tracer response, and their drift throughout. The source establishes quasi-steady response for its five-pulse switch protocol, not stationarity under a new coded run or immunity to deactivation.
4. Resolve and quantify the pooled C4H8 products with a GC-MS channel on the existing C1–C4 separation (or split the GC effluent between FID and a mass analyser). Measure all five carbon-isotope counts through molecular-ion channels or validated fragments retaining the full carbon skeleton (nominal parent ions m/z 56–60 for C4H8). Calibrate the full response matrix A[m,k], natural-abundance contributions, background, fragmentation/cross-talk, isotope-dependent response, and repeat-injection covariance using known standards and blanks. The current C1–C4 FID gives total species yield only; the current upstream quadrupole's m/z 39–47 mixture cannot substitute.
5. Test isotope neutrality directly. The paper assumes the molar product response composition does not change across the 12C/13C switch, but gives no quantitative carbon-isotope kinetic-effect bound for this catalyst/feed. Compare pure-label and mixed-label states at matched total methanol flow and dose, and test whether conversion, C4 yield, product identity, or code-conditioned selection changes. If it does, the simple ancestry identity needs a calibrated chemistry-response model or fails for this platform.
6. Pre-register the target parity and compare it against a single switch, optimized two/multipulse sequences, and periodic coding at the same total isotope dose, catalyst time, preparation, and achieved C4-count accuracy. Measure independent replicate catalyst runs and the joint covariance of fitted P[k], the inlet waveform, and detector calibration. The raw estimand is q = P[0] - P[1] + P[2] - P[3] + P[4]; correct for measured feed contrast only after testing the label-independence assumptions. A parity difference can resolve a pre-specified pair of ancestry models only if their predicted ΔQ exceeds the measured systematic-plus-statistical error. One scalar cannot uniquely recover the MTH mechanism.

This is a standard, physically obtainable isotope-switch plus GC-MS adaptation, not a built or executed apparatus. The needed calibration standards, inlet response, switch transition, product-count covariance, code-state stationarity, isotope-effect bound, and cost are currently **UNKNOWN**.
