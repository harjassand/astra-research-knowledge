1. Necessity has a direct exact witness
--------------------------------------
Let

  A0 = [[0,(2r+1)/3],[1,0]],   R=e1 tensor e1.

Direct differentiation gives

  D^2 F_(alpha,r)(A0)[R,R]
    = 16(r+2)^2/9 * (alpha-alpha_*(r)).

For r in I, the prefactor is strictly positive. Every alpha below the claimed
floor therefore fails the Legendre-Hadamard condition. This is a concrete
rank-one-convexity obstruction and is independent of certificate feasibility.
It also makes necessity of the quasiconvexity threshold immediate.

2. The two acoustic contacts force the correct Gram support
----------------------------------------------------------
At alpha=alpha_*, write the target-e1 rank-one acoustic form as

  D^2F(A)[e1 tensor (n,m),e1 tensor (n,m)] = vec(A)^T M_r(n,m) vec(A).

The exact determinant is

  det M_r(n,m) = 9 m^4 n^4 r^4.

The original contact is at A=I and (n,m)=(0,1). It has the exact flat curve

  A_old(t)=[[1,-(r+1)t],[(r+1)t,1]],  xi_old(t)=(t,1),

  D^2F(A_old(t))[e1 tensor xi_old(t)]^2
    = (2/3) r(r+1)^2(r+2)t^4.

The sharp floor adds a second contact, at A=A0 and xi=(1,0). An exact flat
curve through it is

  A_new(t)=[[-3(r+1)t/(2r+1),(2r+1)/3],[1,(r+1)t]],
  xi_new(t)=(1,t),

  D^2F(A_new(t))[e1 tensor xi_new(t)]^2
    = 6 r(r+1)^2(r+2)t^4/(2r+1)^2.

The notation [R]^2 in these displays means the repeated Hessian direction
[R,R]; it does not square the Hessian value.

For each curve, apply a target rotation R_theta simultaneously to A and e1,
and form the compatible rank-one second jet

  z=(R_theta e1) tensor xi tensor xi,
  Z=vec(R_theta A) tensor z.

On this jet B1 and B2 are proportional, so every alternating two-form
correction vanishes. For any constant positive input diffusion D, the heat
value is (xi^T D xi) times the corresponding acoustic value and is O(t^4).
If a positive-semidefinite Gram matrix Q represents the corrected heat
density, it follows that QZ(0)=0 and QZ'(0)=0. Target rotations supply three
independent vectors for each of these two orders. The original curve forces
six null vectors; the new curve forces six more. Their combined rank is 12

twelve-dimensional kernel. Its bottom twelve rows are the identity. The
portable verifier checks both acoustic identities, annihilation of all twelve

was 18-dimensional and only incorporated the first contact.

5. Analytic implication: quasiconvexity at every affine background
----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix r, a constant B, and a compactly supported smooth perturbation phi. Evolve

  phi_tau = exp(tau(d1 partial_xx+d2 partial_yy)) phi,
  A_tau=B+D phi_tau.

Use the relative quartic energy

  E(tau)=integral [F(A_tau)-F(B)-DF(B):D phi_tau].

The closed homogeneous quadratic two-form has the global polynomial primitive
eta=i_E omega/4. Its pullback by A_tau is exact. Derivatives of the heat
perturbation are rapidly decreasing, and the primitive pullback has zero
boundary integral at infinity. Thus its integral vanishes. Integration by
parts and the exact certificate give

  E'(tau) = -integral Z_tau^T C_r S_r C_r^T Z_tau <= 0.

For p=2,3,4, the positive anisotropic Gaussian has the usual two-dimensional
estimate ||D phi_tau||_p <= C tau^(-(1-1/p)) ||D phi||_1. Quartic Taylor growth
therefore gives E(tau)->0 as tau->infinity. Strong L2, L3, and L4 continuity
at tau=0 gives E(0)>=0. The integrated linear term is zero. Strong W1,4
approximation proves the affine Jensen inequality on every bounded Lipschitz
domain for W0^(1,4) perturbations. The usual periodic heat/Stokes argument
also gives the periodic version.

For alpha>alpha_*, add (alpha-alpha_*)H, which is convex. This proves the
claimed exact quasiconvexity threshold. Quasiconvexity implies rank-one
convexity; the explicit negative acoustic witness proves failure below it.
