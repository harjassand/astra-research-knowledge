Introduce the complex matrix coordinates
\[ z=\frac{A_{11}+A_{22}}2 +i\frac{A_{21}-A_{12}}2, \qquad w=\frac{A_{11}-A_{22}}2 +i\frac{A_{21}+A_{12}}2. \]
Write
\[ r=|z|,\qquad s=|w|,\qquad R=r+s. \]
For \(p>2\), use the rank-one-convex sign of the Burkholder integrand:
\[ F_p(A)=\big((p-1)s-r\big)R^{p-1}. \tag{4} \]
Work initially on
\[ \mathcal U=\{A:r>0,\ s>0\}. \]
Let
\[ \omega_z=d(\Re z)\wedge d(\Im z),\qquad \omega_w=d(\Re w)\wedge d(\Im w), \]
and define
\[ K_p=2p(p-1)R^{p-2}, \qquad \Omega_p=K_p(\omega_z+\omega_w). \tag{5} \]
At a given matrix, rotate domain and target so that \(z=r\), \(w=s\), and express the compatible jets in those coordinates as in (1).
\[ \boxed{ \begin{aligned} &E_{F_p}(A;B_1,B_2)+\Omega_p(B_1,B_2)\\ &\quad= p(p-2)R^{p-3} \left[ (p-1)s(a^2+b^2) +\frac{R^2}{4r}\big((c-e)^2+(b-d)^2\big) \right]. \end{aligned}} \tag{6} \]
The right-hand side is nonnegative.
At the diagonal representative,
\[ \begin{aligned} 2z_x&=a+e+i(d-b),& 2z_y&=b+f+i(e-c),\\ 2w_x&=a-e+i(d+b),& 2w_y&=b-f+i(e+c). \end{aligned} \]
The radial Hessian coefficients are
\[ \begin{aligned} F_{rr}&=p(p-1)R^{p-3}\big[-r+(p-3)s\big],\\ F_{rs}&=p(p-1)(p-2)sR^{p-3},\\ F_{ss}&=p(p-1)R^{p-3}\big[r+(p-1)s\big], \end{aligned} \]
with angular coefficients
\[ \frac{F_r}{r} =pR^{p-2}\frac{-r+(p-2)s}{r}, \qquad \frac{F_s}{s}=p(p-1)R^{p-2}. \]
Substitution and collection of coefficients gives (6). The supplied symbolic script verifies it with \(p\) left as a parameter.
However,
\[ d\Omega_p=dK_p\wedge(\omega_z+\omega_w)\neq0. \]
It would be incorrect to integrate away this correction using Stokes’ theorem.
The next result shows that a more elaborate closed correction cannot repair this.
For every \(p>2\), there is no \(C^1\) closed two-form \(\omega\) on \(\mathcal U\) satisfying
\[ E_{F_p}(A;B_1,B_2)+\omega_A(B_1,B_2)\geq0 \tag{7} \]
for every \(A\in\mathcal U\) and every compatible pair.
The form need not be polynomial, homogeneous, exact, or bounded near the excluded axes.
Suppose such a certificate exists.
The energy is invariant under independent rotations of \(z,w\), induced by domain and target rotations. Average the certificate over this compact symmetry group. This preserves positivity and closedness.
Also average using simultaneous conjugation of \(z,w\). Because the corresponding domain reflection reverses the orientation of the spatial jet pair, the appropriate action on the correction is minus pullback. After this averaging, the general surviving form is
\[ \omega = rU\,dr\wedge d\theta +rV\,ds\wedge d\theta +sW\,dr\wedge d\phi +sZ\,ds\wedge d\phi, \tag{8} \]
where \(z=re^{i\theta}\), \(w=se^{i\phi}\), and \(U,V,W,Z\) depend only on \(r,s\).
Now use a basic property of positive-semidefinite quadratic forms:
A direction with zero quadratic value must have zero mixed coefficient with every other direction.
For the corrected quadratic form in \(a,b,c,d,e,f\), the direction with only \(f\neq0\) has zero quadratic value. Its mixed coefficients force
\[ U-V=K_p,\qquad W-Z=-K_p. \tag{9} \]
After imposing (9), the direction with \(c=e\) and all other coordinates zero is another null direction. Its mixed coefficient with \(a\) forces
\[ Z=K_p,\qquad W=0. \tag{10} \]
These are necessary conditions for any averaged positive completion, not choices made to simplify the calculation.
Closedness of (8) requires
\[ \partial_r(sZ)-\partial_s(sW)=0. \]
But the forced coefficients give
\[ \boxed{ \partial_r(sZ)-\partial_s(sW) = 2p(p-1)(p-2)sR^{p-3}>0. } \tag{11} \]
This contradiction proves the theorem. ∎
The nonzero exterior derivative is forced by positivity along null directions. It is not an accident of the explicit correction in (5).
There are two useful extensions and boundaries.
First, constant anisotropic diffusion does not rescue this certificate class. A certificate for a positive-definite spatial diffusion tensor \(M\) can be averaged over domain rotations. The tensor becomes \((\operatorname{tr}M/2)I\), yielding the impossible isotropic certificate.
Second, singular corrections confined to \(z=0\) or \(w=0\) cannot fix this particular problem: the contradiction already occurs where both coordinates are nonzero.
This does not disprove Burkholder quasiconvexity. It rules out closed two-form completions for the specified constant-diffusion approach. Nonlinear flows, nonlocal operations, and integrated arguments without such a pointwise certificate remain outside the theorem.
