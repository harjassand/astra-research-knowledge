4. The main mathematical construction
The observational obstacle
We observe angular positions and proper motions much more directly than the stars’ small separation along the line of sight. Multiplying both angular motions by one common distance introduces a spurious relative velocity when their true distances differ.
To leading order, this has the form
\[
w_x=v_x-\beta z,\qquad w_y=v_y,
\]
where \(z\) is the unknown depth difference, \(x\) is aligned with systemic transverse motion, and \(\beta\) describes the corresponding angular-motion shear.
Using independently inverted parallaxes does not automatically solve the problem: their uncertainty can exceed the binary’s physical separation.
The construction sought to cancel \(z\) algebraically, rather than infer it through an eccentricity or orbital-size prior.
Exact identity under a specified observation model
Assume a fixed observer plane and constant \(\beta\). Let
\[
L=xv_y-yv_x,\qquad
\tau=\dot L=x a_y-y a_x.
\]
Choose a differentiable function \(g(y)\), an antiderivative \(G'(y)=g(y)\), and define
\[
H(y)=yg(y)-G(y).
\]
Then \(H'(y)=yg'(y)\).
Construct the observable
\[
W_g
=-g'(y)w_y(xw_y-yw_x)-\beta H(y)v_z.
\]
The key identity is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\left[g(y)L+\beta zH(y)\right]
=-W_g+g(y)\tau.
}
\tag{1}
\]
The proof is short but contains the useful cancellation. First,
\[
xw_y-yw_x=L+\beta yz.
\]
Also,
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(gL)=g'v_yL+g\tau,
\]
and
\[
\frac{d}{dt}(\beta zH)
=\beta v_zH+\beta zyg'v_y.
\]
Adding them and substituting the observed angular momentum proves (1).
For a stationary ensemble with integrable terms and no boundary contribution,
\[
\boxed{\langle W_g\rangle=\langle g(y)\tau\rangle.}
\tag{2}
\]
Thus central dynamics predict zero without specifying the eccentricity distribution. The price is a real assumption: the sampled ensemble must satisfy the stationarity condition, and the observation model must be valid.
A directional statistic
Let \(e_x,e_y\) be the projected components of the specified Galactic-field direction. Choosing
\[
g(y)=-e_xe_y|y|
\]
gives, almost everywhere,
\[
\boxed{
S_{\rm sh}
=\frac{e_xe_y}{G_NM}
\left[
x\,\operatorname{sgn}(y)w_y^2
-|y|w_xw_y
+\frac{\beta}{2}y|y|v_z
\right].
}
\tag{3}
\]
The ideal central-force prediction is
\[
\langle S_{\rm sh}\rangle=0.
\]
For the external-field force above, an additional axisymmetry assumption about the intrinsic position distribution gives a nonnegative expectation. The report supplies the sign proof.
Equation (1) is exact for the stated fixed shear model. Its implementation on actual astrometry is not an exact instrument-level theorem. Finite-angle terms, time-dependent observer geometry and the estimation of systemic motion require further control. I retained those limitations rather than interpreting the algebraic cancellation as a completed force measurement.
The report also derives a separate projected-torque moment with an explicit angularly averaged external-field prediction. It is not the same statistic as (3); its illustrative prediction was not compared directly to the numbers below.
5. What the observations show
After harmonizing the overlapping HARPS stellar masses to their primary-table values, I obtained:
Observational cohort	Pairs	Mean \(S_{\rm sh}\)	Descriptive standard error
Archive/primary-clean-table intersection	33	\(0.003898\)	\(0.010779\)
All primary HARPS-table pairs	32	\(0.013064\)	\(0.020731\)
Primary pairs absent from the archive	24	\(0.019391\)	\(0.026908\)


These are my calculations from the acquired numerical measurements, with pair-level values and executable code in the package.
The displayed errors are sample standard deviations divided by \(\sqrt N\). They are not confidence intervals for a gravitational law. The 32-pair cohort overlaps the first; the 24-pair subset is disjoint but has different selection and includes higher-acceleration systems.
No compelling directional-torque evidence emerges from these calculations. That is not an exclusion of the external-field mechanism: the samples are small, heterogeneous and not selection-calibrated.
I also tested explicit measurement-error scenarios. Assuming diagonal Gaussian errors, a \(40\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) radial-velocity floor, and 5% or 10% independent component-mass uncertainties moved the 33-pair mean only to approximately \(0.00394\) or \(0.00392\). Those sensitivity calculations do not control missing covariance, selection or astrophysical contaminants.
A decisive counterexample to overinterpretation
The limitation can be proved rather than merely listed.
Let \(\mathcal L\) denote phase-space evolution, \(f\) a stationary parent population, and \(s\) the probability that an object enters the catalogue. From
\[
W_g=g\tau-\mathcal LF
\]
and integration by parts,
\[
\boxed{
\langle W_g\rangle_s
=
\langle g\tau\rangle_s
+
\frac{\int fF\,\mathcal Ls}{\int fs}.
}
\tag{4}
\]
The second term is a selection contribution. It need not vanish when the physical torque does.
For an explicit example, take an exactly Newtonian circular orbit with \(G_NM=a=1\):
\[
x=\cos\varphi,\quad y=\sin\varphi,\quad
v_x=-\sin\varphi,\quad v_y=\cos\varphi.
\]
Choose \(\beta=0\) and \(e_x=e_y=1/\sqrt2\). Then
\[
S_{\rm sh}
=\frac12\operatorname{sgn}(\sin\varphi)\cos\varphi,
\]
whose uniform-phase average is zero.
Now use the inclusion probability
\[
s(\varphi)=\frac{1+\epsilon\sin2\varphi}{2}.
\]
This selection is invariant under exchanging the two stars. Direct integration gives
\[
\boxed{
\langle S_{\rm sh}\rangle_s=\frac{2\epsilon}{3\pi}.
}
\]
With \(\epsilon=0.1\), selection varies only between \(0.45\) and \(0.55\), yet
\[
\langle S_{\rm sh}\rangle_s=0.02122066
\]
despite exactly zero physical torque.
This is a constructed counterexample, not a claim about the actual catalogue. It establishes that a signal from this statistic would not identify new gravity without selection control. The primary sample’s construction includes orbital-inference-dependent cuts, so sampled stationarity cannot simply be assumed. arXiv
