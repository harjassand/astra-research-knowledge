Result: a sharp, constructive criterion for counting continuity
The strongest outcome is a finite criterion that determines when approximating a reversible Markov kernel controls a specified nonlinear graph count.
The criterion has both directions. A positive certificate gives a quantitative continuity bound. A negative certificate produces an actual nonnegative, symmetric kernel with every row integral exactly one—not merely an abstract entropy obstruction.
It also yields an explicit irrational threshold:
\[
\boxed{
p_c(\text{two triangles sharing one vertex})
=
\frac{1+\sqrt{17}}2
\approx 2.5615528128.
}
\]
Below this threshold, kernels can converge strongly in \(L^p\) and in operator norm while the corresponding graph count diverges.
The candidate original contribution is the sharp equivalence and its counterexample construction. The proof uses a published normal-polymatroid theorem, credited below. I have not established historical priority or obtained independent review. This is a general analytic classification, not a claim to have resolved a major named conjecture.
1. The obstruction
Let \(H=(V,E)\) be a finite simple graph, with \(n\) vertices and \(m\geq1\) edges. For a nonnegative symmetric kernel \(W:[0,1]^2\to[0,\infty)\), define
\[
t(H,W)
=
\int_{[0,1]^V}
\prod_{uv\in E}W(x_u,x_v)\,dx_V.
\]
Assume exact normalization:
\[
\int_0^1W(x,y)\,dy=1
\qquad\text{for almost every }x.
\]
Thus \(W\) describes a reversible Markov transition density with uniform stationary measure. Unlike an ordinary bounded graphon, \(W\) may take values much larger than one. Graph densities in Markov spaces are an established framework, not introduced here. arXiv
The question is:
How much moment control makes a global approximation of \(W\) sufficient to approximate \(t(H,W)\)?

For unrestricted \(L^p\) graphons, the classical counting lemma works when \(p>\Delta(H)\), and the unrestricted assertion fails at and below the maximum degree. Exact marginal normalization removes some of those counterexamples, but it does not eliminate concentration. arXiv
The surviving difficulty is multiscale concentration: different edges of the same pattern can exploit different large-value scales. Tracking only maximum degree, average density, or a single concentration scale misses this interaction.
The representation developed here records the probability cost of concentrating every subset of the pattern’s vertices.
2. The concentration number
A set function \(d:2^V\to\mathbb R\) is admissible when \(d(\varnothing)=0\), it is increasing, and it is supermodular:
\[
d(S)+d(T)
\leq
d(S\cup T)+d(S\cap T).
\]
For a fixed \(p>1\), define \(\gamma_H(p)\) by the following linear program:
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_H(p)=\min\quad &d(V)\\[2mm]
\text{subject to}\quad
&k_e\geq0,\qquad \sum_{e\in E}k_e=1,\\
&d(\{u,v\})\geq p\,k_{uv},\\
&d(\{u,v\})-d(\{u\})\geq k_{uv},\\
&d(\{u,v\})-d(\{v\})\geq k_{uv}
\qquad(uv\in E),\\
&d\text{ is increasing and supermodular.}
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(k_e\) represents an edge’s amplification scale. The quantity \(d(S)\) represents the probability cost of concentrating the vertices in \(S\). Normalizing \(\sum k_e=1\) makes the objective a cost per unit total amplification.
For fixed rational \(p\), this is an ordinary rational linear program. It has
\[
2^n-1+m
\]
variables, and \(O(n^2 2^n+m)\) elementary inequalities suffice.
The consequential part is that its optimum controls the original analytic problem exactly.
3. The theorem
For \(p>1\) and \(B>1\), let
\[
\mathcal M_{p,B}
=
\left\{
W\geq0:
W=W^\top,\ 
\int W(x,y)\,dy=1,\ 
\|W\|_p\leq B
\right\}.
\]
Use the cut norm
\[
\|F\|_\square
=
\sup_{S,T}
\left|
\int_{S\times T}F(x,y)\,dx\,dy
\right|.
\]
Theorem — entropy-deficit completion
If \(\gamma_H(p)>1\), every \(H\)-density is finite on \(\mathcal M_{p,B}\), and
\[
\boxed{
|t(H,U)-t(H,W)|
\leq
C_{H,p,B}\,
\|U-W\|_\square^{
\frac{\gamma_H(p)-1}{m-1+\gamma_H(p)-1}
}
}
\tag{2}
\]
for cut-norm differences at most one.
If \(\gamma_H(p)\leq1\), then for every \(B>1\) there are bounded, finite-step kernels \(W_L\in\mathcal M_{p,B}\) such that
\[
\boxed{
\|W_L-1\|_1\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
\liminf_L t(H,W_L)>1=t(H,1).
}
\tag{3}
\]
The strict inequality in the positive criterion is therefore necessary, including at the boundary.
If \(\gamma_H(p)<1\), the counterexamples can satisfy the stronger conclusions
\[
\boxed{
\|W_L-1\|_p\longrightarrow0,\qquad
\|T_{W_L}-J\|_{2\to2}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
t(H,W_L)\longrightarrow\infty.
}
\tag{4}
\]
Here \(T_W\) is the integral operator of \(W\), and \(J\) is the constant-kernel operator.
The assumptions \(p>1\) and \(B>1\) matter. At \(B=1\), normalization and Jensen’s inequality force \(W=1\). The theorem does not classify the distinct endpoint question of strong-\(L^p\) continuity when \(\gamma_H(p)=1\).
