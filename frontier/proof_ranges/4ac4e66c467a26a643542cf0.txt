## Reconstructed theorem and proof

Let $V=\mathbb R^{2\times2}\cong\mathbb R^4$ with the Frobenius product and row-major coordinates. Write

\[
q(X)=\det X=X^T JX,\qquad J_{14}=J_{41}=\tfrac12,\quad
J_{23}=J_{32}=-\tfrac12,
\]
with all other entries zero. Thus $J^2=I/4$, $q(R)=0$ exactly for rank-one $R$, and $|JR|=1/2$ for unit $R$ on that cone. Let $F:V\to\mathbb R$ be a homogeneous quartic satisfying the differential rank-one-convexity condition

\[
D^2F(A)[R,R]\ge0\quad\text{for every }A\in V\text{ and every }R\text{ with }q(R)=0.
\]

Fix a unit rank-one $R_0$, and suppose $F(R_0)>0$. Set $M_A=D^2F(A)$. The simple transverse contact assumptions are

\[
\ker M_{R_0}=\mathbb R A_0,\quad |A_0|=1,\quad
P_0:=M_{A_0}-\lambda_0J\succeq0,\quad \ker P_0=\mathbb R R_0,
\tag{A}
\]
for some scalar $\lambda_0$. Since $M_{R_0}\succeq0$ by reciprocity, its other three eigenvalues are strictly positive.

For a probability law $X$ on $V$, write

\[
C=\mathbb E[XX^T],\quad H=\mathbb E M_X,\quad
b=\mathbb E DF(X),\quad c=\mathbb E F(X).
\]
Assume $\mathbb EX=0$, $\mathbb E q(X)=0$, and $\mathbb E|X|^4<\infty$. The theorem states that constants $\delta_0,\eta>0$, depending only on $F,R_0,A_0$, exist such that if

\[
X=s_0R_0+Y_0,\quad Y_0\perp R_0,\qquad
\|Y_0\|_2\le\delta_0\|s_0\|_2,\quad
\|Y_0\|_4\le\delta_0\|s_0\|_4,
\tag{B}
\]
then

\[
\inf_{B\in V}\mathbb E\{F(B+X)-F(B)\}\ge\eta\,\mathbb E|X|^4.
\tag{C}
\]
The zero law has gap zero. Constants do not depend on the size of the optimizing $B$ or on the scale of the nonzero law.

### Identities and positivity of the averaged Hessian

Quartic polarization gives

\[
M_A[X,X]=M_X[A,A],\qquad M_AA=3DF(A),\qquad
DF(A)\cdot A=4F(A),\quad M_A[A,A]=12F(A).
\tag{1}
\]

For each fixed $Z\in V$, the indefinite non-strict Finsler lemma applied to $M_Z$ gives $M_Z=P_Z+\lambda_ZJ$, $P_Z\succeq0$. Therefore

\[
Z^THZ=\mathbb E M_X[Z,Z]=\mathbb E M_Z[X,X]
=\mathbb E P_Z[X,X]+\lambda_Z\mathbb E q(X)\ge0.
\tag{2}
\]

This proves $H\succeq0$. The order matters: fix $Z$, use quartic reciprocity, then average over $X$. Directly averaging $M_X=P_X+\lambda_XJ$ would leave the uncontrolled term $\mathbb E\lambda_XJ$ and is not a valid proof of (2).

Expanding at $X$ and using centeredness also gives the exact background identity

\[
\mathbb E\{F(B+X)-F(B)\}=c+b\cdot B+\tfrac12B^THB.
\tag{3}
\]
The only omitted Taylor term is $\tfrac16\mathbb E D^3F(X)[B,B,B]$; $D^3F(X)$ is linear in $X$, so that term vanishes. When $H\succ0$, the optimized value is $\Delta=c-\tfrac12b^TH^{-1}b$.

### Covariance-adaptive line and exact normal regression

Maximize $R^TCR$ over the compact set $\{R:|R|=1,q(R)=0\}$, choosing the sign near $R_0$. Set

\[
s=X\cdot R,\quad Y=X-sR,\quad m_j=\mathbb E s^j,\quad
v=\mathbb E|Y|^2,\quad t=\|Y\|_4,\quad
\delta_2=\sqrt{v/m_2},\quad \delta_4=t/m_4^{1/4}.
\]
At a constrained maximum, the Lagrange multiplier equation is

\[
CR=m_2R+\theta JR,
\]
because $R\cdot JR=q(R)=0$. Hence $z:=\mathbb E(sY)=CR-m_2R=\theta JR$. Determinant balance gives

\[
0=\mathbb E q(X)=\mathbb E q(Y)+2R^TJz
=\mathbb E q(Y)+2\theta|JR|^2,
\]
so

\[
z=-\frac{\mathbb E q(Y)}{2|JR|^2}JR,\qquad |z|\le L v.
\tag{4}
\]

For the determinant form, $|JR|=1/2$; the uniform bound follows because $q(Y)$ is quadratic. This identity says that the only fitted covariance correlation lies in the normal direction of the determinant cone and is second order in $Y$.

The fitted line remains near the original line. If $\vartheta$ is the angle between them, maximality gives $\|Y\|_2\le\|Y_0\|_2$, while projection onto $R^\perp$ gives

\[
\sin\vartheta\,\|s_0\|_2\le\|Y_0\|_2+\|Y\|_2\le2\|Y_0\|_2.
\]
Also $\|Y\|_4\le\|Y_0\|_4+\sin\vartheta\|s_0\|_4$, and $s=X\cdot R$ differs from $s_0=X\cdot R_0$ by $O(\delta_0)\|s_0\|_p$, for $p=2,4$. Thus $m_2,m_4>0$ for nonzero $X$, the scalar norms are comparable, and both $\delta_2,\delta_4=O(\delta_0)$.

### Moving spectral frame and uniformly feasible Finsler scalar

By (1), $M_R\succeq0$ for every rank-one $R$. The averaged Hessian has the exact expansion

\[
H=m_2M_R+D^3F(R)[z,\cdot,\cdot]+\mathbb E M_Y,
\qquad \|H-m_2M_R\|\le Lv,
\tag{5}
\]
using (4) and the fact that $M_Y=O(|Y|^2)$.

For $R$ near $R_0$, the least eigenvalue $\epsilon$ of $M_R$ is simple. Choose its unit eigenvector $A_R$ continuously with $A_R\cdot A_0>0$. Spectral-gap perturbation gives

\[
|A_R-A_0|\le L|R-R_0|,\qquad
0\le\epsilon\le A_0^TM_RA_0=R^TP_0R\le L|R-R_0|^2.
\tag{6}
\]

For each such $A_R$, Finsler gives at least one $\lambda_R$ with

\[
P_R:=M_{A_R}-\lambda_RJ\succeq0.
\]
Every feasible choice is uniformly controlled. First, testing $P_R\succeq0$ on fixed unit vectors $x_+,x_-$ with $q(x_+)>0>q(x_-)$ bounds $\lambda_R$, since $M_{A_R}$ is uniformly bounded. By reciprocity, $q(R_0)=0$, and $M_{R_0}A_0=0$,

\[
R_0^TP_RR_0=M_{A_R}[R_0,R_0]
=M_{R_0}[A_R,A_R]\le L|A_R-A_0|^2.
\]
Positive semidefiniteness gives $|P_RR_0|^2\le\|P_R\|R_0^TP_RR_0$, hence $|P_RR_0|\le L|A_R-A_0|$. Since $P_0R_0=0$, subtracting the two identities

\[
P_RR_0-P_0R_0=(M_{A_R}-M_{A_0})R_0-(\lambda_R-\lambda_0)JR_0
\]
shows $|\lambda_R-\lambda_0|\le L|A_R-A_0|$. Therefore $P_R\to P_0$ uniformly over feasible choices. The kernel and compactness assumptions in (A) imply, after shrinking the neighborhood,

\[
\|P_R\|\le L,\quad
P_R(Y,Y)\ge\nu|Y|^2\quad\text{whenever }Y\perp R,\quad
P_R(R,R)=M_{A_R}[R,R]=M_R[A_R,A_R]=\epsilon.
\tag{7}
\]

This is the required uniform feasible scalar statement. It does not assume a continuous formula for $\lambda_R$; all feasible choices near the contact converge to $\lambda_0$.

### Actual Schur lower bound

Use the moving orthonormal decomposition $V=\mathbb RA_R\oplus A_R^\perp$ and write

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}h&k^T\\k&D\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Equations (5)–(6) yield $D\succeq(d/2)m_2I_3$ and $|k|\le Lv$, where $d>0$ is the spectral gap of $M_{R_0}$ off $A_0$. For $h$, use the **same** scalar from (7):

\[
h=\mathbb E M_X[A_R,A_R]
=\mathbb E M_{A_R}[X,X]
=\mathbb E P_R[X,X],
\]
since $\mathbb E q(X)=0$. Writing $X=sR+Y$,

\[
h=m_2\epsilon+2P_RR\cdot z+\mathbb E P_R[Y,Y].
\]
For $P_R\succeq0$, $|P_RR|^2\le\|P_R\|\epsilon$. Thus (4) and (7) give

\[
m_2\epsilon+(\nu-L\sqrt\epsilon)v\le h\le m_2\epsilon+Lv.
\]
After reducing the neighborhood, $h\ge m_2\epsilon+(\nu/2)v$. The actual Schur complement after eliminating all three complementary affine directions is

\[
\sigma=h-k^TD^{-1}k
\ge m_2\epsilon+(\nu/2)v-Lv^2/m_2
\ge c_0(m_2\epsilon+v).
\tag{8}
\]
The last step uses $v/m_2=\delta_2^2\ll1$. Since $h\asymp m_2\epsilon+v$, also

\[
\sigma\ge(1-L\delta_2^2)h.
\tag{9}
\]

This is a bound on the real Schur denominator, not on its unreduced entry $h$. If $m_2\epsilon+v>0$, then $H\succ0$. Completing the square and using $|k|^2/[m_2(m_2\epsilon+v)]\le L\delta_2^2$ gives the congruence-scale lower bound

\[
H\succeq c\,\operatorname{diag}(m_2\epsilon+v,m_2 I_3),
\]
after decreasing $c>0$. The block inverse consequently satisfies

\[
w^TH^{-1}w\le L\left(\frac{w_A^2}{m_2\epsilon+v}
                    +\frac{|w_D|^2}{m_2}\right).
\tag{10}
\]
Indeed the exact inverse quadratic form is
$(w_A-k^TD^{-1}w_D)^2/\sigma+w_D^TD^{-1}w_D$; the cross term is bounded using $|k|\le Lv$, $D\succeq c m_2I$, and (8).

### Full cubic regression and inverse-metric control

Set

\[
u=\frac{m_3}{3m_2},\qquad \ell=b-uHR,\qquad S=m_4^{1/4}.
\]
For the exact line law $X=sR$, Euler’s identity gives $b=m_3DF(R)$, $HR=3m_2DF(R)$, so $\ell=0$ in **all four components**. For general $X=sR+Y$, quartic expansion gives the exact vector identity

\[
\ell=M_R\big(\mathbb E[s^2Y]-2uz\big)
+\tfrac12\mathbb E\{sD^3F(R)[Y,Y,\cdot]\}
+\mathbb E DF(Y)-u\mathbb EM_YR.
\tag{11}
\]
This subtracts the complete pure-line cubic before any inverse is used.

Using $|u|\le S^2/(3\sqrt{m_2})$, $ |z|\le Lv$, Hölder, and $\delta_2,\delta_4\le1$,

\[
|\mathbb Es^2Y|\le S^2\sqrt v,\quad
\mathbb E|s||Y|^2\le S\|Y\|_{8/3}^2\le S t\sqrt v,
\]
\[
\mathbb E|Y|^3\le t^2\sqrt v,\quad \mathbb E|Y|^2=v.
\tag{12}
\]
Write $\ell=\ell_AA_R+\ell_D$ with $\ell_D\perp A_R$, the scalar and three-dimensional components in the moving frame. The eigenvector relation $M_RA_R=\epsilon A_R$ supplies an extra factor $\epsilon$ on the first term of (11) in its $A_R$-component. Equation (12) then yields

\[
|\ell_D|\le LS^2\sqrt v,\qquad
|\ell_A|\le LS^2\sqrt v\,(\epsilon+\delta_2+\delta_4).
\tag{13}
\]
In fact the final vector in (11) vanishes: for every $W$, reciprocity gives $W\cdot\mathbb E(M_YR)=M_R[\mathbb EY,W]=0$, since $\mathbb EY=0$. Combining (10) and (13), with

\[
\frac{\epsilon^2v}{m_2\epsilon+v}\le\epsilon\frac v{m_2}
\]
and bounded $\epsilon$, gives the following component estimates:

\[
\frac{\ell_A^2}{m_2\epsilon+v}
\le LS^4\left(
\frac{\epsilon^2v}{m_2\epsilon+v}
+\frac{v}{m_2\epsilon+v}(\delta_2^2+\delta_4^2)
\right)
\le L(\delta_2^2+\delta_4^2)m_4,
\qquad
\frac{|\ell_D|^2}{m_2}\le L\delta_2^2m_4.
\]

Here $v/(m_2\epsilon+v)\le1$, and $\epsilon^2v/(m_2\epsilon+v)\le\epsilon v/m_2\le L\delta_2^2$.

Therefore

\[
\ell^TH^{-1}\ell\le L(\delta_2^2+\delta_4^2)m_4.
\tag{14}
\]

The same estimates resolve the fully reduced cubic numerator. Indeed, $\sqrt\sigma\,\delta_2\gtrsim\sqrt v\sqrt{\epsilon+\delta_2^2}$ and $\sqrt\sigma\gtrsim\sqrt v$; these absorb respectively the $\epsilon\sqrt v$, $\delta_2\sqrt v$, and $\delta_4\sqrt v$ pieces of $\ell_A$. Also $|k^TD^{-1}\ell_D|\le LS^2v^{3/2}/m_2\le L\sqrt\sigma S^2\delta_2$. Hence

\[
|\ell_A-k^TD^{-1}\ell_D|
\le L\sqrt\sigma\sqrt{m_4}\,(\delta_2+\delta_4),\qquad
\ell_D^TD^{-1}\ell_D\le L\delta_2^2m_4.
\tag{15}
\]
Thus neither the numerator nor denominator is replaced by a fixed-frame or unreduced surrogate.

### Final minimization and the singular case

By (3), change the unrestricted affine origin from $B$ to $-uR+Z$. Then

\[
\Delta=Q-\tfrac12\ell^TH^{-1}\ell,\quad
Q=c-ub\cdot R+\tfrac12u^2R^THR.
\tag{16}
\]
Polynomial expansion and the same Hölder bounds imply

\[
|c-F(R)m_4|\le L\delta_4m_4,\quad
|b-m_3DF(R)|\le LS^2\sqrt v,\quad
\|H-m_2M_R\|\le Lv.
\]
Substituting Euler’s identities from (1) into (16),

\[
\left|Q-F(R)\left(m_4-\frac{2m_3^2}{3m_2}\right)\right|
\le L(\delta_2+\delta_4+\delta_2^2)m_4.
\]
With (14),

\[
\left|\Delta-F(R)\left(m_4-\frac{2m_3^2}{3m_2}\right)\right|
\le L(\delta_2+\delta_4)m_4.
\tag{17}
\]
Because $m_3^2\le m_2m_4$, the leading term is at least $F(R)m_4/3$. Continuity and $F(R_0)>0$ give $F(R)\ge F(R_0)/2$ nearby. Choosing the two ratios small makes $\Delta\ge c_1m_4$. Finally,

\[
\mathbb E|X|^4=m_4+2\mathbb Es^2|Y|^2+\mathbb E|Y|^4
\le(1+\delta_4^2)^2m_4,
\]
which proves (C).

The sole singular case in this neighborhood is $\epsilon=v=0$: then $Y=0$ almost surely and $X=sR$. Here $H=m_2M_R\succeq0$ may be singular, but $b=uHR$. Thus $B=-uR$ is a global minimizer without using an inverse, and direct Euler calculation gives

\[
\Delta=F(R)\left(m_4-\frac{2m_3^2}{3m_2}\right)\ge F(R)m_4/3.
\tag{18}
\]
The zero law is separate and has zero gap. This closes the only inverse edge case required by the theorem.
