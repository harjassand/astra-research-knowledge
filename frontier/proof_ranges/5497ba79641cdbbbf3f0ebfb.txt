## 5. Two mechanisms force two different exponents

### 5.1 Normal diagonal example: the `2p` obstruction

Let `Te_j=t_j e_j`, where `0<=a t_j^2<1`, and take

\[
 Be_j=\frac{\sqrt a\,t_j^2}{\sqrt{1-at_j^2}}e_j.
\]

Then `C=A` and `P=f_a(A)-A`, so (1.1) is an equality. As `t_j` tends to zero, the source singular values are asymptotic to `sqrt(a) t_j^2`. Hence

\[
 B\in S_p\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\in S_{2p}.
\]

Sequences at the summability threshold rule out every universally smaller output exponent. Uniformly scaling a finite diagonal example to zero makes the ratio in (4.3) tend to one, proving sharpness of its constant.

### 5.2 Harmonic weighted shift: repeated nonnormal propagation

On `ell^2(N_0)`, fix `b>0` and define

\[
 \lambda_k=\frac{b^2}{1+ab^2(k+1)},\qquad
 Te_k=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,e_{k+1},\qquad
 Bz=bz e_0.
\]

Now

\[
 A=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\ldots),
 \qquad
 C+P=\operatorname{diag}(b^2,\lambda_0,\lambda_1,\ldots).
\]

The recurrence `lambda_k=g_a(lambda_{k-1})`, with initial source `b^2`, proves equality in (1.1). The source has rank one, while

\[
 s_k(T)\asymp k^{-1/2}.
\]

Thus `T` is compact but not Hilbert–Schmidt. Finite source rank alone does not imply trace-class or Hilbert–Schmidt response.

This operator also has spectral radius zero. Its weights decrease, so

\[
 \|T^m\|^2=\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda_k,
\]

whose `m`-th root tends to zero. A small eigenvalue spectrum is therefore not the same thing as a small singular-value spectrum.

### 5.3 Direct sums force the `p+2` obstruction

Take an orthogonal sum of the preceding shifts with parameters `b_i` tending to zero, and sources on their root vectors. The singular values of `B` are `b_i`. For every `q>2`, write `alpha=q/2`. Monotone integral comparison gives

\[
 \frac{b_i^{q-2}(1+ab_i^2)^{1-\alpha}}{a(\alpha-1)}
 \le\sum_{k\ge0}
 \left[\frac{b_i^2}{1+ab_i^2(k+1)}\right]^\alpha
 \le\frac{b_i^{q-2}}{a(\alpha-1)}.
\]

Hence

\[
 T\in S_q\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (b_i)\in\ell^{q-2}.
\]

For example, a suitably scaled sequence

\[
 b_i=(i+1)^{-1/p}[\log(i+2)]^{-2/p}
\]

belongs to `ell^p` but to no `ell^s` with `s<p`. It rules out any universally valid `q<p+2`. For `q<=2`, one block already fails. At `q=p+2`, a single block with `b` tending to zero makes the ratio in (4.2) tend to one. This proves sharpness of that constant.

Together the normal and nonnormal examples prove the full exponent `max(p+2,2p)` is optimal, not just an artifact of the proof.
