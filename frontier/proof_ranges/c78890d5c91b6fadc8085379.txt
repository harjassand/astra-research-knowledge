5. The other completed test: adding more force mediators does not rescue the calcium explanation
Before the gallium investigation, I tested the calcium isotope-shift data against a broader force construction.
Suppose a weak extra interaction is additive over neutrons and has an isotope-independent electronic response. At first perturbative order, its contribution to transition \(j\) has the form
\[
\delta\nu_j^{\mathrm{new}}(A,40)
=
X_j(A-40).
\]
A sum—or an integrable continuum—of mediator potentials merely changes \(X_j\). It does not create another independent isotope pattern under these assumptions.
After eliminating the leading nuclear-radius contribution between two transitions, I tested
\[
y=Fx+K\mu+Q\mu_2+C\gamma,
\]
with \(x,y\) the measured 729 nm and 570 nm shifts,
\[
\mu=\frac{1-1/R}{m_{40}},
\qquad
\mu_2=\frac{1-1/R^2}{m_{40}^2},
\qquad
\gamma=A-40.
\]
The four-isotope algebraic solution required
\[
Q_{\mathrm{required}}
=
+1.979934960\ \mathrm{GHz\,u^2}.
\]
Using the published central recoil coefficients, the corresponding physical recoil combination was
\[
Q_{\mathrm{recoil}}
=
-1.012869689\ \mathrm{GHz\,u^2}.
\]
The opposite-sign discrepancy was not removed by the measurement-error sensitivity checks I performed. However, this calculation deliberately omitted nuclear polarization. The primary experiment already identifies nuclear polarization as a potentially adequate Standard Model contribution to the observed nonlinearity. arXiv
The conclusion is therefore limited: adding more mediators within this factorized force class does not make the incomplete explanation sufficient. It is not an exclusion of arbitrary new physics, and the exact four-parameter fit is not evidence for a force.
