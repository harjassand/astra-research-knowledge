I established a robust obstruction to a candidate mathematical operation, together with an exact alternative on the same instances. I did not establish a universal new primitive or resolve the log-rank conjecture.
The main finding is:
A Boolean matrix can have a small, nonredundant signed representation and a small positive representation, yet a natural rule for passing between them creates an exponentially complicated intermediate object.
The separation survives constant entrywise approximation. It also survives requiring the starting representation to come from linearly independent actual columns of the matrix—not merely from an artificially padded decomposition.
This identifies a genuine failure of a proposed operation. It does not show that cancellation is intrinsically expensive: the same examples admit inexpensive cancellation when their global structure is used.

I initially considered coordinated discrepancy rounding, adaptive covariance conditioning, and normal forms for algebraic degeneration. None yielded a decisive new operation independent of nearby Astra work. I therefore pursued a different question:
When does a short algebraic description imply a short operational description?
For a Boolean matrix
\[ M:X\times Y\longrightarrow\{0,1\}, \]
let \(r(M)\) be its real rank, and let \(\pi(M)\) be the minimum number of disjoint all-one rectangles partitioning its one-entries. Here a rectangle is an arbitrary Cartesian product \(A\times B\).
The log-rank conjecture, in its partition formulation, asks whether
\[ \log \pi(M)\le \operatorname{polylog}(r(M)+2). \]
Its communication formulation asks whether two parties can evaluate \(M(x,y)\) using only polynomially many bits in \(\log r(M)\). The signed-rectangle formulation below is already established prior work, not an invention of this investigation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02583)
One strong existing approach uses semidefinite programming and spectral discrepancy estimates to obtain communication complexity \(O(\sqrt r)\). That remains very different from a polylogarithmic dependence. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18524?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
A particularly useful algebraic starting point is the theorem of Hambardzumyan, Lovett and Shirley: every rank-\(r\) Boolean matrix has a presentation
\[ M=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf1_{P_i} -\sum_{j=1}^{q}\mathbf1_{N_j}, \qquad p+q=O(r\log(r+2)). \]
Thus the missing capability can be formulated as converting short signed rectangle descriptions into short positive, disjoint descriptions. [DROPS](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.7)
My investigation focused on the conversion itself.
At each cell, write
\[ P(x,y)=\#\{\text{active positive rectangles}\}, \qquad N(x,y)=\#\{\text{active negative rectangles}\}. \]
The Boolean promise says
\[ P(x,y)-N(x,y)\in\{0,1\}. \]
A natural proposal is to distribute the surviving value among the active positive contributions:
\[ u_i(x,y) = \mathbf1_{P_i}(x,y)\frac{M(x,y)}{P(x,y)}, \]
with value zero when \(P=0\).
This is not just one arbitrary averaging formula. It is forced by the following requirements: the allocation is nonnegative, conserves \(M\), uses only the active labels at that cell, and treats the active positive labels exchangeably. Swapping two active positive labels leaves that local information unchanged, so their allocated residuals must agree.
The intended procedure was then to compress each nonnegative residual into a short sum of separated functions,
\[ u_i(x,y)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{t_i}a_\ell(x)b_\ell(y). \]
The failure is that local normalization can create enormous separated rank. The Boolean sum may remain simple while its individually allocated pieces become difficult to represent.
The scope matters: this does not exclude globally informed algorithms, algorithms that choose a different allocation, or algorithms that retain a compact symbolic expression without separately factorizing the residuals.
For every \(n\ge1\) and integer \(L\ge1\), there is a Boolean matrix \(M_{n,L}\) satisfying
\[ r(M_{n,L})=1+2Ln, \]
with a signed presentation of length
\[ s=1+3Ln, \]
and a disjoint positive partition of size at most
\[ 1+2Ln+n. \]
The signed presentation comes from a linearly independent, inclusion-maximal dissociated family of actual columns, with coefficients in \(\{-1,0,1\}\). There are no duplicate positive-negative rectangles.
Nevertheless, the equal-sharing residual \(U_0\) of one positive term satisfies
\[ \boxed{\operatorname{rank}(U_0)=2^n.} \]
For \(L=8\), the failure persists under approximation:
\[ \boxed{ \|A-U_0\|_{\max}\le\frac19 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{rank}(A)\ge 2^{\Omega(\sqrt n)}. } \]
The approximate statement uses the published pattern-matrix theorem through an explicit reduction given below. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0906.4291)
In particular, with \(L=8\),
\[ r(M)=16n+1,\qquad s=24n+1,\qquad \pi(M)\le17n+1, \]
while the exact intermediate rank is \(2^n\).
Use three kinds of rows:
\[ c_S\quad(S\subseteq[n]),\qquad d_{ia},\ e_{ia}\quad(i\in[n],\,a\in[L]). \]
Define Boolean column vectors by this table:
\[ \begin{array}{c|ccc} \text{row}&v_0&v^+_{ia}&v^-_{ia}\\ \hline c_S&1&\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}&\mathbf1_{\{i\in S\}}\\ d_{jb}&0&\mathbf1_{\{(i,a)=(j,b)\}}&0\\ e_{jb}&0&\mathbf1_{\{(i,a)=(j,b)\}}& \mathbf1_{\{(i,a)=(j,b)\}} \end{array} \]
The important identity is
\[ v^+_{ia}-v^-_{ia}=\mathbf1_{\{d_{ia}\}}. \]
For every \(T\subseteq[n]\), include another column
\[ w_T = v_0+\sum_{i\in T}\sum_{a=1}^{L} \bigl(v^+_{ia}-v^-_{ia}\bigr). \]
These columns are Boolean because the differences are indicators of distinct diagnostic rows, disjoint from the support of \(v_0\).
Let \(M_{n,L}\) contain all the \(v\)-columns and all the \(w_T\)-columns.
Suppose a linear combination of the \(v\)-columns vanishes.
Evaluation at \(d_{ia}\) forces every coefficient of \(v^+_{ia}\) to vanish. Evaluation at \(e_{ia}\) then forces every coefficient of \(v^-_{ia}\) to vanish. Finally, evaluation at \(c_\varnothing\) forces the coefficient of \(v_0\) to vanish.
Thus these \(1+2Ln\) actual columns are linearly independent. They span the remaining columns, proving the rank formula.
They are also inclusion-maximal dissociated: every additional \(w_T\) satisfies the displayed signed subset-sum relation. Consequently, this construction meets the actual-column interface used in the signed-representation theorem, rather than exploiting arbitrary redundant factors.
On the submatrix indexed by \(c_S\) and \(w_T\), the matrix is identically one. But its signed presentation has
\[ P(c_S,w_T)=1+L|S\cap T|, \qquad N(c_S,w_T)=L|S\cap T|. \]
