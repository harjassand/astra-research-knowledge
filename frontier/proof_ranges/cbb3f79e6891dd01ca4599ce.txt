4. Realization by local, gapped Hamiltonians
The measures above are not merely abstract probability distributions.
For either bulk measure \(\nu_\pm\), orthonormalize
\[
1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}
\]
in \(L^2(\nu_\pm)\). Multiplication by \(x\) becomes a real symmetric tridiagonal matrix \(H_\pm\).
Tridiagonality follows because \(xp_j\) is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree below \(j-1\). Choosing positive leading coefficients makes neighbouring couplings nonzero. A diagonal sign change makes all off-diagonal entries negative.
The first basis vector represents the constant polynomial. Therefore,
\[
\langle e_0,e^{-sH_\pm}e_0\rangle=G_{\nu_\pm}(s),
\]
\[
\langle e_0,e^{-itH_\pm}e_0\rangle=A_{\nu_\pm}(t).
\]
Use the total Hamiltonian and fixed preparation
\[
\mathcal H_\pm=0\oplus H_\pm,
\qquad
\psi=\frac12e_{\mathrm{ref}}+\frac{\sqrt3}{2}e_0.
\tag{11}
\]
This is a nearest-neighbour chain plus one known isolated reference level. Preparation requires a fixed two-site superposition, not an unknown eigenstate.
Engineered nearest-neighbour coherent-transfer systems are established prior art. The local realization itself is not the proposed new capability. APS Journals
What is well conditioned
The two nominal Hamiltonians have exactly the same spectrum, so every partition function
\[
\operatorname{tr}e^{-s\mathcal H_\pm}
\]
is identical. The full spectrum can therefore be supplied without helping to distinguish the pair.
The ground state is unique, its energy is zero, and the gap above it is \(3/8\). Its preparation weight is exactly \(1/4\). The remaining energies lie in \([3/8,5/8]\), and their preparation weights satisfy (3).
There is also a polynomial lower bound on every chain coupling.
Lemma. If a Jacobi matrix has eigenvalue spacing at least \(\Delta\) and every endpoint spectral weight is at least \(\alpha\), then every neighbouring coupling \(b\) satisfies
\[
|b|\ge\frac{\Delta\sqrt\alpha}{3}.
\tag{12}
\]
To prove it, split the chain at \(b\). The left principal block has fewer eigenvalues than the complete matrix. Since the full eigenvalues are \(\Delta\)-separated, some full eigenvalue \(\lambda\) is at distance at least \(\Delta/3\) from the left block’s spectrum. For its normalized eigenvector,
\[
\|v_{\mathrm{left}}\|\le\frac{3|b|}{\Delta}.
\]
Its endpoint component has squared magnitude at least \(\alpha\), proving (12).
Here,
\[
\boxed{
|b|\ge\frac1{24m\sqrt{m+1}}.
}
\tag{13}
\]
Thus neither exponentially weak hopping nor exponentially small spectral weights are needed.
The excited-state spacings are \(O(1/m)\); they are not claimed uniformly separated. The ground-state gap is uniform. Distinguishing these two kinds of gap is important.
5. Robustness: locality replaces exact coefficient tuning
Moment matching also has a spatial interpretation.
For \(m=2r-1\), the two Jacobi matrices have identical diagonal coefficients through index \(r-2\) and identical bonds through distance \(r-1\). Their near-boundary dynamics agree.
A closed walk starting at the observed endpoint needs at least \(m\) matrix factors to encounter the first potentially different diagonal coefficient. This gives a robustness statement that does not depend on the exact binomial identities.
Perturb the bulk matrices by real symmetric tridiagonal matrices \(E_\pm\) satisfying
\[
\|E_\pm\|\le\frac1{64t_m},
\tag{14}
\]
while preserving their common near-boundary coefficients. A common perturbation \(E_+=E_-\) is sufficient. Keep the reference unchanged.
The perturbed bulk spectra remain in \([1/3,2/3]\), and their endpoint moments still agree through degree \(m-1\).
Uniform imaginary-time bound
Taylor expansion at zero gives a bulk difference bounded by \(2B^m/m!\).
For a bound uniform over all imaginary times, expand \(e^{-sx}\) around \(x=1/2\). On \([1/3,2/3]\), its degree-\((m-1)\) remainder is at most
\[
e^{-s/3}\frac{(s/6)^m}{m!}.
\]
Its supremum over \(s\ge0\) is at most \(2^{-m}\). Consequently, including the reference,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le s\le B}
|G_+^{\mathrm{pert}}(s)-G_-^{\mathrm{pert}}(s)|
\le
\frac32
\min\!\left\{\frac{B^m}{m!},2^{-m}\right\}.
}
\tag{15}
\]
The reference still guarantees \(G\ge1/4\), so the logarithmic difference is at most four times this.
Real-time separation remains
Duhamel’s formula gives
\[
\|e^{-it(H+E)}-e^{-itH}\|\le t\|E\|.
\]
Under (14), each return probability changes by at most \(1/32\). The difference between the two hypotheses therefore remains at least
\[
\boxed{\frac7{32}.}
\tag{16}
\]
The same exponential-order lower bound follows in both noise models.
This is a meaningful but structured robustness theorem. It is not a full unrestricted perturbation neighbourhood. A change at the observed endpoint can create a first-order imaginary-time difference and make detection much easier.
Also, arbitrary perturbations need not preserve the nominal common spectrum or the spectral-weight floor. Free complete-spectrum side information is asserted for the nominal isospectral family, not simultaneously for every perturbed pair.
