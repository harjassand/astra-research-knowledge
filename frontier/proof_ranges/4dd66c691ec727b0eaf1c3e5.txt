# Sparse, fixed-design Poisson compilers for local generator errors

16 October 2026. Independent mathematical derivation and small-matrix verification. No canonical source was changed.

## Result and scope

There is a useful escape from the full-patch `2 nu/d²` diamond-ball obstruction, provided the physical error interface supplies a **known sparse Pauli coefficient pattern with coefficient bounds**. It does not require the error itself to be Lindblad.

For traceless Hermitian Pauli strings `P_a`, write the allowed error as

`E(rho) = sum_ab C_ab (P_a rho P_b - {P_b P_a,rho}/2) - i[H,rho]`,

where `C=C†` may be indefinite and `H=sum_p h_p P_p`, with real `h_p`. Suppose, before the error is selected,

`|C_ab| <= M_ab`, `M_ab=M_ba>=0`, and `|h_p|<=u_p`.

There is an explicit fixed, implemented Pauli-dephasing floor `F`, using only original Pauli labels, their pair products at allowed nonzero coefficient positions, and Hamiltonian labels, such that for every allowed error

`F+E = sum_e lambda_e (Phi_e-Id)`,

with every `Phi_e` CPTP, every `lambda_e` independent of the unknown coefficients, and

`sum_e lambda_e <= 2 sum_ab M_ab + 2 sum_p u_p`.                         (R)

The ordered sum `sum_ab` counts each offdiagonal pair twice. Each correlated-pair proposal has at most four Kraus operators and is supported on the union of its two Pauli supports. Thus weight-`k` Pauli noise labels require at most weight-`2k` pair events and product floors. A bounded geometric diameter and bounded incident rate are separate necessary hypotheses: small body count alone does not imply locality.

This is a coefficient-structured robustness neighborhood, not an improved arbitrary diamond-norm ball. The fixed floor must really be present in the generator. Its strength and support, and all proposal rates, are charged. Dense or high-weight expansions of correction-jump errors can destroy the advantage.

The Choi criterion, generalized Schur complement, Lindblad gauge shifts, and Poisson-channel semigroups used below are established machinery. The sparse fixed-design synthesis and its application to the supplied memory construction are derived here; historical priority for this precise packaging has not been established. The known single-Pauli Hamiltonian/dephasing calculation is used only as an ingredient.

## 1. Exact necessary and sufficient criterion for any finite-rate compiler

Use normalized Hilbert–Schmidt inner product `<A,B>=Tr(A†B)/d`, normalized vectorization `|A>=(A tensor I)|Omega_d>`, and the normalized Choi matrix. Choose a complete traceless orthonormal basis `{F_a}`; the basis may be complex. An incomplete specified noise basis is embedded in this complete basis with zero rows and columns.

For

`L(rho)=sum_ab C_ab(F_a rho F_b†-{F_b†F_a,rho}/2)-i[H,rho]`,

define

`S=sum_ab C_ab F_b†F_a`, `s=Tr(S)/d=Tr C`,

`H0=H-Tr(H)I/d`, `B0=-(S-sI)/2-iH0`, `b_a=<F_a,B0>`.

All identity components of `H` are irrelevant and removed. Jumps containing identity components must first be put into a traceless representation, including the accompanying Hamiltonian gauge change. For Hermitian `C`, `S` is Hermitian.

**Criterion.** An exact representation `L=lambda(Phi-Id)` with finite `lambda>0` and CPTP `Phi` exists if and only if

1. `C>=0`, and
2. `b in Ran C`.

When these hold, the least rate is

`lambda_min = s + b† C^+ b`,                                             (1)

apart from the harmless zero-generator convention: `L=0` has infimum zero and can be represented at every positive rate by `Phi=Id`. All rates `lambda>=lambda_min` work for a nonzero generator. Here `C^+` is the Moore–Penrose inverse. In particular, a small nonzero component of `b` in the null space is fatal at every finite rate, not merely expensive.

**Proof.** With `G=-S/2-iH0`, the Choi matrix of `L` is

`J(L)=sum_ab C_ab |F_a><F_b| + |G><I| + |I><G|`.

In the orthonormal decomposition `span{|I>} direct-sum traceless space`,

`J(lambda Id + L) = [[lambda-s, b†], [b, C]]`.                            (2)

A positive block matrix has `C>=0`. If `v in ker C`, positivity of its restriction to the plane generated by `(1,0)` and `(0,v)` forces `v†b=0`; hence `b in Ran C`. With that condition, completing the square gives

`[z*,x†] J(lambda Id+L) [z,x]^T`

`= (x+C^+ b z)† C (x+C^+ b z) + (lambda-s-b†C^+b)|z|²`.

This proves necessity and sufficiency. Trace preservation of `Id+L/lambda` is automatic. Choi positivity then proves it is a channel. No commutation assumption is used.

Multiple finite-rate CPTP clocks cannot evade a failure of this criterion on the same system: if `L=sum_j lambda_j(Phi_j-Id)` and `Lambda=sum_j lambda_j<infinity`, then `L=Lambda(Phi_bar-Id)` for the CPTP convex average `Phi_bar=sum_j lambda_j Phi_j/Lambda`. The same argument covers a finite-intensity distribution of channel marks. Enlarged-system models with residual continuous dynamics or state-dependent trajectories are outside this criterion.

### Optimal Lindblad identity-shift gauge

Factor `C=VV†`, and let `L_j=sum_a V_aj F_a`. Find the minimum-norm solution `z=V^+ b` of `Vz=b`. Define `alpha_j=conj(z_j)` and `K_j=L_j+alpha_j I`. Then

`sum_j alpha_j* L_j = B0`, `sum_j |alpha_j|²=b†C^+b`,

`sum_j K_j†K_j = lambda_min I`.

Moreover `L(rho)=sum_j K_j rho K_j†-lambda_min rho`. Thus `K_j/sqrt(lambda_min)` are Kraus operators of the optimal proposal channel. At any larger rate add the Kraus operator `sqrt(lambda-lambda_min) I` before dividing by `sqrt(lambda)`.

This optimizes all identity-shift gauges, and the Choi proof shows the rate is globally optimal among all CPTP proposal representations of this generator, not merely among the displayed gauges. The non-scalar no-jump drift is removable exactly when `B0` belongs to the complex span of the traceless jumps. Merely eliminating the Hamiltonian is insufficient; the Hermitian part `-(S-sI)/2` must also be removable.

### Scalar jump-intensity special case

If `S=sI`, then `b` is the coefficient vector of `-iH0`. Finite-rate compilation holds exactly when `H0` belongs to the noise range, and

`lambda_min=s+h†C^+h`.

A scalar `S` without this Hamiltonian range condition does not suffice. If also `H0=0`, rate `s` works directly with channel Kraus `L_j/sqrt(s)`.

## 2. Unavoidable obstruction and a physically meaningful repair

For amplitude damping `L=kappa D[sigma_minus]`, with `sigma_minus=|0><1|`, the jump range is `span{sigma_minus}` while `B0=(kappa/4)Z`. Therefore no finite-rate exact channel compiler exists.

An explicit witness avoids relying on the range calculation. In the computational Choi basis, the `|00>,|11>` principal block of `J(Id+L/lambda)` is

`(1/2) [[1, 1-kappa/(2lambda)], [1-kappa/(2lambda), 1-kappa/lambda]]`.

Its determinant is `-kappa²/(16lambda²)<0` for every finite positive `lambda`. Likewise a nontrivial pure Hamiltonian has `C=0`, `b!=0`, and cannot compile. Taking a smaller time step never makes the exact Euler map CP in these examples.

Even purely dissipative random-unitary semigroups need not admit finite-rate compilation. For a qutrit, take the Hermitian jump `A=diag(-1,0,1)` and `L=D[A]`. Its noise range is `span{A}`, but `B0=-(A²-(2/3)I)/2` lies outside it. This dephasing semigroup has the usual Gaussian random-unitary representation, yet no finite-rate CPTP compiler. More generally a single traceless Hermitian jump with zero Hamiltonian passes the criterion only if `A²` is affine in `A`, equivalently its spectrum has at most two distinct values.

Add real implemented dephasing and a longitudinal Hamiltonian:

`L'=kappa D[sigma_minus]+gamma D[Z]-i[hZ, .]`, `gamma>0`.

Now the exact minimum rate is

`lambda_min=kappa/2+gamma+(kappa²/16+h²)/gamma`.                            (3)

Optimizing the added dephasing gives `gamma=sqrt(kappa²/16+h²)` and rate `kappa/2+2sqrt(kappa²/16+h²)`. At `h=0`, `gamma=kappa/4` yields rate `kappa` and exactly

`L'=kappa(R0-Id)`, `R0(rho)=|0><0| Tr rho`.

Thus a one-qubit reset channel is a precise repair, with the additional dephasing explicitly charged. It is nonunital, so random-unitary machinery alone would miss this useful case.

## 3. Sparse repair for a specified correlated Lindblad generator

For a Pauli basis and `C>=0`, the only new directions in `B0` beyond Hamiltonian labels arise from nonidentity Pauli products `P_bP_a` with nonzero `C_ab`. Write `B0=sum_p b_p P_p`.

Adding `sum_p gamma_p D[P_p]`, with `gamma_p>0` wherever `b_p!=0`, changes `C` to `D=C+diag gamma` and changes `S` only by `(sum gamma)I`. Consequently `b` is unchanged. The exact optimum is

`lambda_min=Tr C+sum gamma+b†(C+diag gamma)^+b`,                           (4)

and the simple upper bound

`lambda_min <= Tr C+sum gamma+sum_{b_p!=0}|b_p|²/gamma_p`                 (5)

follows from `D>=diag gamma`. The pseudoinverse inequality remains valid with zero directions: use `b†D^+b=sup_x(2 Re(b†x)-x†D x)` and the support of `b`.

Choosing `gamma_p=|b_p|` gives

`lambda_min <= Tr C+2 sum_p |b_p| <= sum_ab |C_ab|+2 sum_p |h_p|`.        (6)

The second bound uses Pauli products, the triangle inequality and the fact that diagonal products contribute only to the identity. It is independent of patch Hilbert-space dimension, but depends on coefficient norm and basis sparsity.

For a calibrated generator, the optimal diagonal floor on a specified allowed set is the convex semidefinite problem

`minimize Tr C+sum gamma+q`

subject to `gamma>=0` and `[[q,b†],[b,C+diag gamma]]>=0`.

Thus identity-shift gauge optimization is solved exactly by (1), and optimal choice of allowed dephasing floor is an SDP in coefficient space. The elementary choice in (6) is not claimed globally optimal. A numerical optimization in the accompanying test reduces one noncommuting correlated two-qubit example from `1.4392465286` to `1.3544505733`; this is only a numerical illustration, not a certified global optimum.

## 4. Fixed-design robustness, including signed errors

A calibrated floor chosen from actual unknown coefficients would not establish the desired robustness neighborhood. Here the floor is chosen solely from the a-priori bounds `M,u` in Result (R).

Let the allowed set of Pauli labels contain original noise labels, Hamiltonian labels and the nonidentity products at allowed offdiagonal positions. No repeated multiplication closure is needed. Extend `C,M` by zeros on new labels. For each label define

`r_p=sum_q M_pq`,

`tau_p=sum_{a!=b: P_bP_a is a phase times P_p} M_ab`,

`beta_p=tau_p/2+u_p`.

One may improve the latter to `sqrt((tau_p/2)^2+u_p²)`, since the coefficients of `S0` and `H0` are real. Either choice bounds `|b_p|` uniformly. Install the fixed floor

`F=sum_p (r_p+beta_p) D[P_p]`.                                           (7)

For every allowed Hermitian `C`, diagonal dominance gives `C+diag r>=0`: the negative contribution of each offdiagonal term is bounded by its absolute value times the sum of the two squared coordinate magnitudes, and `C_pp>=-M_pp`. Thus

`D=C+diag(r+beta)>=diag beta>=0`.

If `beta_p=0`, then every admissible `b_p` is zero. For any `v in ker D`, `0=v†D v>=sum beta_p|v_p|²`, so `v` vanishes on every positive-beta direction and `v†b=0`. Therefore `b in Ran D`, including singular cases and exact diagonal cancellations.

By (1) and the same variational inequality as in (5),

`lambda_min <= Tr C+sum r+2 sum beta`

`<= sum_a M_aa+sum_ab M_ab+sum_{a!=b}M_ab+2sum_p u_p`

`=2sum_ab M_ab+2sum_p u_p =: Lambda`.                                   (8)

Use `Phi=Id+(F+E)/Lambda`, with identity thinning if its minimum rate is smaller. `Lambda` and the floor are fixed before the error is chosen. If all bounds are zero, simply omit the zero generator and its clock.

If every admissible `C` is known positive semidefinite, the `r` part may be omitted; the same proof gives the improved ceiling `sum_ab M_ab+2sum_p u_p`. That assumption cannot be applied silently to perturbations formed by subtracting two correction generators.

## 5. Pairwise local implementation of the fixed-design result

The aggregate compiler in Section 4 could have a large union support if diagonalized globally. The following decomposition preserves pairwise support and needs no global coefficient diagonalization. Use the conservative `beta=tau/2+u` floor in (7); the sum of the floors below is exactly that floor, with duplicate labels added.

### Diagonal noise coefficient

For a real coefficient `c=C_aa`, `|c|<=M_aa=M`, install `M D[P_a]`. The resulting generator `(M+c)D[P_a]` has the fixed proposal rate `2M`, with channel

`Phi_a=((M-c)/(2M))Id+((M+c)/(2M))Ad_Pa`.

Zero `M` terms are omitted.

### One complex correlated edge

Take distinct nonidentity Hermitian Paulis `P,Q`, coefficient `z=C_ab`, `|z|<=M`, and define `QP=omega R`, where `R` is the fixed Hermitian Pauli product and `omega` is a phase in `{1,-1,i,-i}`. The edge generator is

`E_z(rho)=z(P rho Q-{QP,rho}/2)+conj(z)(Q rho P-{PQ,rho}/2)`.

Install the fixed floor `M(D[P]+D[Q]+D[R])`. Its coefficient matrix on `{P,Q,R}` has the `P,Q` block `[[M,z],[conj(z),M]]` and the `R,R` entry `M`. The scalar jump intensity is `s=3M`; its residual is `b_R=-a`, with `a=Re(z omega)`. Therefore its exact least rate is

`lambda_edge=3M+a²/M<=4M`.                                              (9)

This includes commuting and anticommuting pairs, arbitrary complex correlations, and singular boundary blocks `|z|=M`. Its input-independent fixed clock may always run at `4M`.

An explicit four-Kraus representation at that fixed rate is as follows. Write `z=t exp(i theta)`, `0<=t<=M`, with arbitrary `theta` at `z=0`, and set

`K_+=sqrt((M+t)/2) (P+exp(-i theta)Q)`,

`K_-=sqrt((M-t)/2) (P-exp(-i theta)Q)`,

`K_R=sqrt(M)R-(a/sqrt(M))I`,

`K_0=sqrt(M-a²/M)I`.

Then `sum K†K=4M I`, and `Phi_edge(rho)=sum K rho K†/(4M)` has exactly the desired generator. Every Kraus operator is supported on `supp(P) union supp(Q)`. Zero Kraus operators can be dropped.

### Hamiltonian coefficients

For `h_pP_p`, `|h_p|<=u_p`, install `u_p D[P_p]` and use fixed rate `2u_p`. This is the already-known matched single-Pauli calculation, included solely to complete the general compiler. Its actual least rate is `u_p+h_p²/u_p`.

Summing these independent clocks gives exactly (R). No simultaneous commutation or Trotter limit is required: the generator of independent clocks is the sum, and event ordering handles overlapping noncommuting channels.

A body-count increase is real. For example, `P=XXII`, `Q=IIZZ` produces `R=XXZZ`. With signed offdiagonal coefficients `z=M=0.03` and all original diagonal coefficients zero, the fixed floor has rates `0.03` on each of those three Paulis, its completed noise matrix is singular of rank two, and its exact minimum rate is `0.12`, equal to the uniform bound. This explicitly tests both higher-weight products and the singular range issue.

## 6. How this plugs into the supplied memory construction

Keep the completed ideal correction channels `kappa(C_v-Id)` unchanged. Replace the full-patch depolarization floor by the physically installed floor (7), or its explicit termwise version, for a separately specified allowed coefficient interface. The perturbed generator is then an exact sum of ideal correction proposals and the fault proposals above.

The source's conditional-on-schedule Kraus-span proof can use these proposal channels without modification in principle: their schedules are input-independent and their supports are bounded. All the stated envelope, eroder, LSS, topology and recovery premises remain necessary; this work does not reprove them.

The quantitative geometric hypothesis is a bounded total rate of proposal supports intersecting each relevant coarse guard, not merely a small sum over an arbitrarily named patch. A sufficient interface is uniformly bounded pair-support diameter, uniformly bounded number of coefficient positions touching a fine cell, and uniformly small incident sums of `M_ab,u_p`. More generally weighted incident-rate bounds can replace a count bound. Charge the larger product-support birth boxes when applying the existing envelope argument.

For an arbitrary bounded-body operator basis, the universal criterion in Section 1 still applies, but the sparse fixed-floor recipe specifically exploits Hermitian Pauli involutions. Expand each original bounded-body operator into Paulis first and charge the resulting coefficient bounds and any Hamiltonian gauge terms from identity components. For an operator of operator norm at most one on at most `k` qubits, its normalized Pauli coefficient one-norm is at most `2^k`; thus a generic basis conversion can cost up to `4^k` in the coefficient-matrix one-norm. This depends on body count rather than the full patch dimension, but is not free.

A signed difference `D[L+delta L]-D[L]` is generally not Lindblad by itself. After expressing both traceless jumps in the same basis, its coefficient matrix is a difference of positive matrices; identity parts can also generate Hamiltonian terms. Section 4 deliberately permits this. However, the Pauli coefficient support of a general correction projector or a general perturbation on an 11-body (or larger) patch may be dense. Finite support does not imply a small coefficient norm or few allowed matrix entries. An apparatus-level model must establish these assumptions; this is not a result for all arbitrary diamond-bounded local perturbations.

For perspective, with norm-one Pauli strings,

`||E||_diamond <= 2sum_ab |C_ab|+2sum_p |h_p|`.

This follows termwise from the norm bound for left/right multiplication and the anticommutator. Its direction matters: small coefficient norm implies a small diamond norm; a small arbitrary diamond norm does not supply a dimension-independent coefficient bound. Consequently there is no contradiction with the fixed-baseline minimax audit.

## 7. Prior-art comparison and novelty boundary

1. **Complete positivity and block-matrix test.** Choi's 1975 paper supplies the Choi/Kraus criterion. The singular Schur-complement condition is established matrix analysis, with a primary antecedent in Arthur Albert (1969). Equations (1)–(2) are a direct, self-contained application; no novelty claim is attached to them. [Choi](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(75)90075-0); [Albert](https://doi.org/10.1137/0117041). Albert's bibliographic record was verified, but access did not expose the full article; the needed statement is proved above.

2. **GKSL and gauge freedom.** The canonical generator form is due to Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan and Lindblad. Albert, Bradlyn, Fraas and Jiang give the identity-shift gauge explicitly in Appendix A, Eq. (A4), and explain the traceless convention. The optimized gauge above is ordinary least squares applied to this freedom. [GKS](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.522979); [Lindblad](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01608499); [Albert et al., Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.08079#page=23).

3. **Poisson quantum renewal / “uniformization.”** For a channel `Phi`, `exp[t lambda(Phi-Id)]=exp(-lambda t) sum_n (lambda t)^n Phi^n/n!` is the standard compound-Poisson expansion. Vacchini's *Quantum renewal processes*, Eq. (11) in the arXiv version, explicitly obtains `exp[t(L0+lambda(E-1))]` from exponential waiting times. Setting `L0=0` gives the present proposal architecture. Crucially, the general renewal construction allows continuous dynamics between events; it does not assert that every arbitrary Lindbladian is a finite-rate channel minus identity. [Vacchini](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.00693#page=8), published [Scientific Reports 10, 5592 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-62260-z).

4. **Random-unitary semigroups are broader in one direction and narrower in another.** Aniello, Kossakowski, Marmo and Ventriglia characterize random-unitary/twirling semigroups, including Hamiltonian and Gaussian components and a closed cone of unitary-channel differences; see Proposition 5.2 and Section 5. Those limits need not be a finite-rate compound-Poisson representation. Pure Hamiltonians already show the distinction. Conversely our CPTP proposals can be nonunital, as the reset example shows, so they need not be random-unitary. [Aniello et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.3507), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8113/43/26/265301).

The focused searches did not identify a primary source presenting precisely the fixed sparse Pauli floor, signed coefficient uncertainty, pairwise four-Kraus compiler and bound (R) together. That is not evidence of historical originality. The defensible contribution here is an explicit derived structured alternative and its exact interface to the supplied proof, while all underlying machinery remains standard.

## 8. Reproducibility and checks

Run `python structured_noise_compiler/check_compiler.py` from the current workspace; it uses only existing NumPy and SciPy. Full output is in `check_results.json`.

Verified checks:

- Amplitude-damping Choi minor is negative at rates `0.1,1,10,100`; the analytic determinant is reproduced. A qutrit Hermitian-jump dephasing example also fails the range criterion and has a negative Euler-channel Choi eigenvalue.
- At `kappa=0.7`, adding `gamma=0.175` gives rate `0.7` and exactly the reset channel. With `h=0.19`, the optimized rate is `0.8666236541`.
- A complex, rank-two, noncommuting two-qubit noise example is repaired, its Kraus representation reproduces the generator, and a rate just below (1) gives a negative Choi eigenvalue.
- Sixty signed random coefficient samples on one, two and three qubits obey one fixed preselected floor and rate bound per family. Complex offdiagonal entries, many zero-beta directions, and exact singular cancellations are included. Choi positivity errors are at floating-point scale, worst approximately `-1.12e-16`.
- A four-qubit example verifies a weight-four product floor from two weight-two inputs and saturates the stated signed bound.
- Twenty-four commuting/anticommuting complex edge examples verify the explicit four-Kraus formulas, with maximum error `1.76e-16`.
- Two noncommuting compiled generator terms reproduce the exact matrix exponential through their Poisson-series representation to error `2.17e-16`.

These finite checks catch sign, normalization, support and singular-case mistakes; the general claims rest on the proofs above.
