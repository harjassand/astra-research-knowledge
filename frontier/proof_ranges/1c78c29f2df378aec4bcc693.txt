Let \(y_*\) and \(\lambda_*\ge0\) be rational global optimality witnesses:
\[ Hy_*+g+A^\top\lambda_*=0, \qquad \lambda_*^\top(b-Ay_*)=0. \]
For every feasible \(y\),
\[ \boxed{ q(y)-m_*= \frac12(y-y_*)^\top H(y-y_*) +\lambda_*^\top(b-Ay). } \tag{17} \]
Both terms are nonnegative. Therefore the complete latent minimizer set is the rational polyhedron
\[ Y_*= \left\{ y: Ay\le b,\; H(y-y_*)=0,\; \lambda_*^\top(b-Ay)=0 \right\}. \tag{18} \]
Its observed projection is \(S=\arg\min F\). That projected polyhedron need not be expanded.
Write (18) as inequalities \(By\le c\), using both signs for equalities. Clear denominators with an integer \(D_0\), and set
\[ U=\max\!\left(1,\max_{ij}|(D_0B)_{ij}|\right), \qquad K=D_0(DU)^{D-1}. \]
Then
\[ \operatorname{dist}(y,Y_*) \le K\|(By-c)_+\|_2. \tag{19} \]
This is a deliberately loose Hoffman bound, not a claim to compute the optimal constant. Hoffman’s error-bound principle is established prior work. [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/nist-research-library/journal-research-volume-49)
For completeness, the explicit constant follows from integer linear algebra. Every independent row submatrix of \(D_0B\) has a positive integer Gram determinant, hence determinant at least one. Its largest singular value is at most \(DU\), which bounds its smallest singular value below by \((DU)^{-(D-1)}\). Applying this to independent active normals at the nearest point proves (19).
Let \(\overline H=\sum_{ij}|H_{ij}|\). Equation (17) then gives
\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{dist}(x,S)^2 \le C^2(F(x)-m_*), \qquad C^2=K^2(2\overline H+E_0), } \tag{20} \]
on the relevant energy sublevel.
The numerical value of \(C\) may be enormous. Its binary encoding length is polynomial, and its ingredients are acquired from the original rational quadratic program.
For the true flow,
\[ \frac12\frac d{dt}\|u(t)-z_*\|^2 \le-(F(u(t))-m_*). \]
Energy monotonicity therefore gives
\[ F(u(t))-m_*\le\frac{R^2}{2t}. \tag{21} \]
Choose
\[ T_*=\frac{8C^2}{\epsilon^2}. \]
Equations (20)–(21) imply
\[ \operatorname{dist}(u(T_*),S)\le\frac{\epsilon R}{4}. \]
Construct a finite-horizon candidate with error at most \(\epsilon R/4\), and project its terminal state \(v_N\) onto \(S\). This projection is another convex quadratic program, performed in the latent representation (18). Call the result \(s\). Then
\[ \|v_N-s\|\le\frac{\epsilon R}{2}. \]
Replace only the last endpoint of the polygonal candidate by \(s\), and continue constantly thereafter. The modification costs at most \(\epsilon R/2\) in state error.
For every later time, distance to the fixed minimizer \(s\) is nonincreasing. Thus
\[ \|u(t)-s\| \le \|u(T_*)-s\| \le\frac{3\epsilon R}{4}, \qquad t\ge T_*. \]
This proves the all-future theorem.
Since only \(\log T_*\) enters the mesh size, and \(\log C\) is polynomially bounded in the input encoding, the complete construction is polynomial in input length and reciprocal accuracy.
A feasible finite primal–dual path with action bound \(J\), together with a certified minimizer \(s\), proves an all-future error bound of
\[ \boxed{\sqrt{2J}+\|v_N-s\|.} \tag{22} \]
The large convergence constant is needed to prove that a polynomial-size construction exists. A verifier can check the returned action and terminal distance directly.
