## The storage inequality

Define an invariant potential on the four-real-dimensional profile space by

\[
 \mathcal P(\alpha,\beta)
 =-\frac{16}{3}|\alpha|^4+\frac{25}{4}|\beta|^4
   -\frac{11}{6}|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2.
\]

The stronger pointwise assertion is

\[
 \mathbb E_y G(D\phi)-\tfrac12\mathbb E_y|D\phi|^4
 \ge \frac{d}{dx}\mathcal P(\alpha,\beta). \tag{1}
\]

For its proof, at each x use a constant output rotation and a constant
y-translation to make α=r and β=s real. The two phases can be fixed
independently by these two symmetries. Transform their **actual** derivatives
by the same constants and write α'=p+it, β'=q+ih. There is no differentiation
of an x-dependent coordinate change. At zero amplitudes any phase may be
chosen; the resulting polynomial inequality still applies. These symmetries
preserve G, the gradient norm, and the invariant storage derivative.

In this gauge the complex columns are

\[
 X=(p+it)e^{iy}+(q+ih)e^{-iy},\qquad
 Y=ir e^{iy}-is e^{-iy}.
\]

Set u=|X|²,v=|Y|²,j=Im(conj(X)Y). Let L=E_y G(X|Y), N=E_y(u+v)² and
P(r,s)=−16r⁴/3+25s⁴/4−11r²s²/6. Exact Laurent averaging yields

\[
 L-P_rp-P_sq-N/2=z_{12}^TQ_{12}z_{12}+z_9^TQ_9z_9, \tag{2}
\]

with

\[
 z_{12}=(r^2,rp,rt,p^2,pt,t^2,s^2,sq,sh,q^2,qh,h^2),
 \quad z_9=(rs,rq,rh,ps,pq,ph,ts,tq,th).
\]

The complete rational matrices are in `CONTINUUM_STORAGE_CERTIFICATE.json`.
Every leading principal minor is strictly positive. The separate executable
`verify_continuum_storage.py` reconstructs L and N directly from the columns,
checks every coefficient in (2) and Sylvester's criterion by exact arithmetic.
The certificate uses rational numbers with small denominators. Numerical SDP
optimization discovered a proposal; it is not a premise of (2) or positivity.

Because P depends only on squared radii, its derivative in this gauge equals
the derivative of the invariant potential, even when t,h are nonzero.
Equation (2) proves (1). In one dimension W^{1,4} amplitudes are bounded and
absolutely continuous, so the polynomial chain rule is valid. Integrating
over the closed x path makes the potential difference zero and proves the
zero-background bound. Polygonal paths are included without a density limit.
