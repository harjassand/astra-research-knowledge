## 2. Fixed foundational target T

T is the existential statement that a finite-dimensional bipartite state can have negative partial transpose while remaining undistillable:

\[
\exists d\ge3\ \exists \rho\in M_d(\mathbb C)\otimes M_d(\mathbb C):
\quad \rho\succeq0,\quad \operatorname{Tr}\rho=1,\quad \rho^\Gamma\not\succeq0,
\]
\[
\forall n\ge1\ \forall\psi\text{ with }\operatorname{SR}_{A^n:B^n}(\psi)\le2:
\qquad \langle\psi,(\rho^\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\psi\rangle\ge0.
\]

The rank-two distillation criterion and the Werner partial-trace formulation are imported established formulations; see Costa Rico and the references in the July 2026 papers. The investigation did not alter T to a finite-copy assertion.

A candidate strategy used

\[
\rho_*=(2I_9-F_3)/15,
\]

where \(F_3(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x\). The stronger statement that this particular state is undistillable would prove T. Conversely, finding it distillable would reject this candidate, **not** disprove existential T.

### Elementary checks and the exact remaining obligation

The symmetric and antisymmetric eigenspaces of \(F_3\) have dimensions six and three. Thus \(\rho_*\) has eigenvalues \(1/15\) and \(3/15\), is positive, and has trace one. Set \(\Omega=\sum_{j=0}^2 e_j\otimes e_j\). Since \(F_3^\Gamma=\Omega\Omega^*\),

\[
\rho_*^\Gamma=(2/15)(I-\tfrac12\Omega\Omega^*),
\qquad \rho_*^\Gamma\Omega=-\Omega/15.
\]

It is therefore NPT, without any imported conjecture.

For \(C\in M_{3^n}(\mathbb C)\), use row/column tensor factors indexed by the resource copies, and let \(\operatorname{Tr}_S C\) contract corresponding row and column indices on the subset \(S\). Define

\[
q_n(C)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}(-1/2)^{|S|}\|\operatorname{Tr}_S C\|_F^2.
\]

Vectorize \(C\) as \(\psi_C=\sum_{x,y} C_{xy}e_x\otimes e_y\). Its Schmidt rank is \(\operatorname{rank}C\). Expanding the tensor product and contracting each \(\Omega\Omega^*\) gives exactly

\[
\langle\psi_C,(\rho_*^\Gamma)^{\otimes n}\psi_C\rangle=(2/15)^n q_n(C).
\]

The unproved candidate obligation is therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad q_n(C)\ge0\quad\text{for every }n\ge1\text{ and every complex }C\text{ of rank at most two}.\quad}
\]

At one copy, \(|\operatorname{Tr}C|\le\|C\|_1\le\sqrt{2}\|C\|_F\) proves the inequality. The July two-copy theorem supplies \(n=2\). Neither statement supplies an induction. Partial trace need not preserve matrix rank: for example, the partial trace of a rank-one maximally entangled projector is a full-rank scalar identity.

## 3. What the recent sources do and do not provide

Fraser–Huber–Pozsgay–Vona and Bharti–Gajjala–Haug report sharp two-copy partial-trace inequalities. Their relevant arguments were examined, including the balanced rank-one decomposition and the auxiliary-qubit formulation. No all-copy theorem is imported from those papers.

Wu–Zou prove three-copy inequalities on the normal rank-two sector and specified nonnormal support sectors. Their nuclear-norm/exterior-square estimates and product-reference Schur argument were examined. Their normal-sector conclusion does not cover arbitrary complex rank-two matrices.

The 4 October anonymous candidate at the third pin claims unrestricted three-copy qutrit undistillability. Its written proof constructs matched moments of types (3,1), (2,2), (1,3), uses whole-replica partial transposes and a coupled quartic Gram map, and reduces to a 2,374-dimensional rational source space. A displayed identity with 716 positive multipliers is the decisive certificate. The written physical lifting and the finite source-space structure were read; the stored rational matrices and all 716 positivity checks were not independently replayed here. The repository explicitly claims neither four-copy nor all-copy undistillability. The earlier access failure to its Zenodo proof was subsequently resolved by finding its linked GitHub manuscript.

The OpenAI zero-key construction uses states of the form \((L\otimes M)(\chi\chi^*)\), with both local maps completely positive and completely copositive. Such a state is PPT: transposing Bob's output gives \((L\otimes(T\circ M))(\chi\chi^*)\succeq0\). Its tensor-closed coherence argument therefore does not itself construct an NPT state. No transfer removing that PPT condition was proved.
