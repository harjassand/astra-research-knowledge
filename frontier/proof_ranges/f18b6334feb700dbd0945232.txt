# Logarithmic contact: independent reconstruction and determinant-route falsification

Research date: 2026-10-08 UTC. Scope: characteristic-zero arithmetic circuits evaluated at ell_i(z)=log(1+z/i). No general contact bound or small-circuit counterfamily is obtained. All computations are exact and local. No external writes.

## Consequential finding

Let P4 be the primitive integer multi-affine polynomial in `extremal_multiaffine_4.json` (coefficient masks use x1 as bit 0). It is the unique nonzero polynomial, up to scale, in the 16-dimensional multi-affine space satisfying the first 15 Taylor vanishing conditions. Its contact is exactly 15.

**Proposition (finite, narrow obstruction).** P4 cannot be expressed as a read-once determinant of any size over C. More generally, it cannot be expressed as

    det(A0 + x1 A1 + x2 A2 + x3 A3 + x4 A4)

with constant matrices and rank(Ai)=1 for i=1,...,4, regardless of matrix size.

This invalidates one natural compression of the existing maximal-contact fixtures. It says nothing against general determinants with repeated variables, general arithmetic circuits, other high-contact polynomials, or the size-sensitive logarithmic contact conjecture. The coefficient-space/principal-minor machinery is classical; novelty of this specific application has not been certified.

## 1. Independent reconstruction and exact fixtures

For n variables, the coefficient of z^k in ell_i is (-1)^(k+1)/(k i^k). Products were generated by truncated exact rational convolution. The (2^n-1) by 2^n matrix of coefficients z^0,...,z^(2^n-2) has a one-dimensional kernel for n=2,3,4,5. Integer normalization gives contact orders 3,7,15,31, respectively. Primitive coefficient maximum bit lengths are 3,11,116,1075. The n=5 fixture is a finite extension, not evidence of polynomial circuit size.

The independently reconstructed n=2,3,4 coefficients agree with the pinned Astra fixture source `updates/AP/package/pit/contact_examples.json` as exposed by immutable source d-de03f8ff1da7598d. Source text is preserved at ../frontier/d-de03f8ff1da7598d.txt. The n=4 leading nonzero coefficient after substitution is

    [z^15] P4(ell) = 106094131743479867224150599577643825 / 144618323705856.

## 2. Reduction from arbitrary-size rank-one determinants

Suppose f=det(A0 + sum_i xi ui vi^T) in n variables and the coefficient of x1...xn is nonzero. Then the columns u1,...,un and rows v1^T,...,vn are each linearly independent: a dependent family cannot contribute a nonzero n-fold mixed determinant term. Choose invertible constant left and right changes of basis taking U=[u1 ... un] to [I_n;0] and V^T to [I_n 0]. After accounting for a nonzero overall scalar, the determinant is

    det([[diag(x)+B, C], [D, E]]).

The full monomial coefficient is det(E), so E is invertible (empty E allowed). Schur complementation gives

    f / [x1...xn]f = det(diag(x)+B-C E^(-1)D).

Every read-once determinant is already a rank-one-restricted symbolic determinant. This proves that it suffices to exclude the n by n diagonal-plus-constant form. No assertion that general multilinear determinant representations have rank-one variable matrices is made.

## 3. Principal-minor data

Normalize q=P4/c_[4], where c_[4]=3216992424277409444056193764900. For I subset [4], write m_I=[x_[4]\\I]q. If q=det(diag(x)+A), then m_I=det A[I,I]. Let

    a_i=m_{i},
    r_ij=a_i a_j-m_{ij},
    t_ijk=m_{ijk}-a_i a_j a_k+a_i r_jk+a_j r_ik+a_k r_ij.

Thus r_ij=Aij Aji and t_ijk is the sum of the two directed 3-cycle products. Reduction modulo 101 is legitimate because c_[4] is a unit. It gives

    (a1,a2,a3,a4) = (51,9,44,83),
    (r12,r13,r14,r23,r24,r34) = (13,18,7,57,10,34),
    (t123,t124,t134,t234) = (91,54,82,37).

## 4. Compact classical polynomial-identity certificate

Put a=t123, b=t124, c=t134, d=t234. The Nanson cycle-sum identity says the determinant of the following matrix is zero for any principal-minor data of a 4 by 4 matrix:

    [a r14, b r13, c r12, 2 d r12 r13 r14+c b a]
    [b r23, a r24, d r12, 2 c r12 r23 r24+d b a]
    [c r23, d r13, a r34, 2 b r13 r23 r34+d c a]
    [d r14, c r24, b r34, 2 a r14 r24 r34+d c b]

This is the first four rows of the classical 5 by 4 Nanson matrix recorded in Lin–Sturmfels, arXiv:0812.0601v3, Section 3. Its entries are polynomials over Z in the principal minors. For this fixture it reduces modulo 101 to

    [31 63 56 71]
    [48  1 77 26]
    [28 60 64 31]
    [57 12 18 86],

whose determinant is 5 modulo 101. Therefore the rational value of the universal polynomial relation is nonzero. This directly excludes complex principal-minor realizability, with no inference from merely having no F101-points. It also bypasses any concern that a complex solution could fail to reduce modulo 101.

Data: `nanson_certificate_mod101.json`. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/0812.0601v3, Section 3, displayed Nanson matrix.

## 5. Independently derived quotient-algebra certificate

This second certificate does not rely on the classical identity. All r1j are nonzero, so diagonal similarity permits A12=A13=A14=1, A21=r12, A31=r13, A41=r14. Put u=A23, v=A24, w=A34. The pair-product constraints force A32=r23/u, A42=r24/v, A43=r34/w. The four triple-cycle constraints imply

    r13 u^2-t123 u+r12 r23=0,
    r14 v^2-t124 v+r12 r24=0,
    r14 w^2-t134 w+r13 r34=0,
    g := r24 u^2 w^2+r23 r34 v^2-t234 u v w=0.

All rational coefficients lie in R=Z_(101). The quadratic leading coefficients are units; after normalization these are monic in their separate variables. Their constant terms are units too. Thus every complex candidate u,v,w is algebraic over Q and integral over R, and its inverses are integral as well. Reducing at a prime over 101 would give a solution over an algebraic closure of F101. This is an algebraic-closure statement, not just an F101-point check.

More directly, form the finite free rank-8 R-algebra B=R[u,v,w]/(the three monic quadratics), with basis

    (1,w,v,vw,u,uw,uv,uvw).

Multiplication by g has determinant congruent to 17 modulo 101, as recorded in `norm_certificate_mod101.json`. Hence its determinant is a unit of R and g is invertible in B. The equation g=0 cannot hold in any characteristic-zero specialization to a field. This proves the proposition independently of the Nanson identity. The script also obtains the unit Groebner basis over F101 as a redundant check.

## 6. Reconstructed restricted contact bound; already known

Let V be an R-dimensional polynomial space invariant under coordinate translations. Choose a basis p1,...,pR and define fj(z)=pj(ell(z)). Algebraic independence of the logarithms follows by independently continuing around each pole -i: a putative polynomial relation vanishes on a full translation lattice, hence is zero. Therefore the fj are linearly independent and their Wronskian W is nonzero.

Translations act unipotently on V, so their determinant is one. Write the Wronskian symbolically in x=ell(z), with rational z coefficients. Replacing x by x+c multiplies its columns by the translation matrix, preserving W. Polynomial translation invariance forces W to be independent of x. In particular, it can be evaluated at x=0, leaving a rational function of z.

Let Q(z)=product_i(z+i), and S=R(R-1)/2. The row of r-th derivatives, after setting x=0, has denominator Q^r and numerator degree at most (n-1)r. Indeed each term is a constant times a product of derivatives ell_i^(k), and the total derivative weight is r. Thus

    W(z)=N(z)/Q(z)^S,       deg N <= (n-1)S.

If f is a nonzero element of the span of the fj and ord_0 f=m, replacing a basis element by f gives ord_0 W >= m-(R-1). Since Q(0) !=0,

    ord_0 f <= (n-1) R(R-1)/2+R-1.

This is a genuine contact argument, not the false inference that linear-realization dimension by itself bounds contact. However, it exactly matches the already reconstructed multiplicity lemma in Astra and the Bhardwaj–Saptharishi/Moura route. It is not new progress on the open gate. Products of independent linear forms can have small circuits and exponentially large translation closure, so this proof does not address the decisive case.
