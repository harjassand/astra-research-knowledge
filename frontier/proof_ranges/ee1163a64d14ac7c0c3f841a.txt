## Demonstrations, and what each actually establishes

| Primary experiment | Observed physical output | Missing for molecular work recovery in useful chemistry |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ optical centrifuge, 380 Torr, 300 K study (2023) | Coherent rotational CARS ladder resolved to $J=364$, collision-dependent hot bands and rotational-to-vibrational transfer. The detected CARS is a probe/readout. | No useful CO production, calibrated molecule count in high-$J$ states, controlled unloading, net optical gain, or replenishment-work reduction. Hot bands appear after about 500 ps. |
| SiO⁺ + H₂, optical pumping in an ion trap (2023) | Extreme rotational excitation up to $j=170$ raises the measured reaction rate by about 3.6×. | No optical energy input/recovery ledger or repeated work-recycling cycle. Ultra-dilute reagent gas, slow single-ion reaction rate, and incoherent pump preparation do not match the dense neutral centrifuge regime. |
| Para-H₂ coherent two-photon emission (2014/2015) | Vibrational coherence plus an external mid-IR trigger produces a directed, coherent partner at 5.048 μm. The 2015 article estimates $6\times10^{11}$ photons/pulse; this gives 23.6 nJ/pulse at that wavelength. The partner is about $1.6\times10^{-4}$ of the 150 μJ trigger energy from the reported photon count; the paper itself reports the ratio as approximately $10^{-4}$. | No chemistry, no total balance across all fields, and no cycle proving that output reduces the work needed for a later operation. The two visible preparation fields are 5 mJ each; first anti-Stokes intensity at 1577 nm is reported about 50× the partner intensity, so partner-channel power is not a complete output ledger. The estimated photon count uses an assumed equal detector responsivity at 5048 and 4587 nm. |
| D₂ CW rotational Raman cavity (2011) | High-finesse cavity generates coherent rotational Stokes/anti-Stokes sidebands; the authors report up to seven sidebands and over 50 mW combined at 10 Torr. | The Stokes ladder pumps molecules into rotation; the paper's 60 μW first anti-Stokes output is 0.34% of the reported 17.5 mW pump output, not a recovery efficiency. The 77 K D₂ cavity is not a high-$J$ CO₂ reaction platform. |
| CO₂/O₂ dense-gas thermalization (2015 PRX; oxygen rather than the CO₂ reaction sample) | A 0.9-bar O₂ sample was centrifuged with 30 mJ pulses; rotation-to-translation relaxation was tied to a thermal channel. The model estimates 2% of molecules reach $J\approx91$, each with 1.5 eV, and a 140 K bulk gas rise. | This is direct evidence that rotational work can become bath heat in a dense gas, not evidence that it cannot first be extracted. It measures refractive, birefringence, density and acoustic signals, not reusable light. |
| Helium-droplet centrifuge deceleration, arXiv v1 (25 Aug 2026) | Molecular alignment follows the rotating drive and returns during the deceleration sweep; the authors interpret the dynamics as rapid molecule-to-superfluid thermalization. Effective model times are about 10 ps for CS₂ and 100 ps for OCS. | The measured signal is fragment-angle alignment, not a captured optical output mode. The paper studies molecule–bath coupling; the observed reversal does not measure returned work. It is a superfluid droplet regime, not the chemistry regimes above. |

The 2015 para-H₂ demonstration is the strongest physically observed example of energy from a prepared molecular coherence appearing in a coherent emitted channel. The estimated partner energy is about 24 nJ/pulse, or 0.24 μW at 10 Hz. The two visible preparation fields together are 10 mJ/pulse, or 100 mW average; the external trigger is 150 μJ/pulse, or 1.5 mW average. These comparisons are not efficiencies because there are other sidebands and the full energy flow through the target was not measured. They do establish a real, controlled coherent output path whose practical limitation is presently severe. The system is p-H₂ at 78 K and 60 kPa, with a calculated ideal-gas density of $5.57\times10^{19}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$: dense gas, but a different molecule, temperature, excited degree of freedom, and no chemical output.

The phase and noise conditions also do not transfer automatically. The CO₂ experiment used phase-locked optical components and a 65 ps CARS probe, but the spectra were averaged over hundreds to thousands of shots; a spectroscopic fringe is not a cycle-level phase-referenced power measurement. In p-H₂, the preparation fields were synchronized to better than 50 ps, the trigger jitter was about 0.8 ns, the coherence estimate was 0.04 against a stated maximum of 0.5, and the Raman HWHM used for decoherence was 65 MHz. The authors measured output dependence on trigger timing and energy, which establishes control of the emission process, but not persistent phase lock to a work-storage load. The SiO⁺ reaction rate was inferred through a Ba⁺ fluorescence proxy and secular excitation; the authors report that their mass resolution cannot separate SiO⁺ from SiOH⁺ and that laser drift contributes noise. The D₂ study's high-finesse cavity required active locking and alignment, while its generated sidebands were not tested as a stored input for a later cycle.

The primary papers therefore occupy incompatible corners. CO₂ supplies high density and an optically prepared high-$J$ distribution but no chemistry or extraction. SiO⁺ supplies a real reaction-rate change but at ultrahigh vacuum with low event rate and continuous incoherent pumping. p-H₂ supplies controlled coherent emission in dense gas but no reaction. D₂ supplies cavity sidebands but Stokes pumping and a different rotational regime. The droplet result is a bath-coupling study. Combining the best density, reaction rate, coherence, and recovery number from these separate systems would not describe any demonstrated apparatus.
