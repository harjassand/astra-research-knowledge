## 4. Strengthening the obstruction: balanced bottlenecks near Gaussian

The preceding family establishes the exact codimension boundary, but its inner component becomes rare as \(n\) grows. The next construction removes that limitation.

Fix \(n\ge5\). Let \(a>0\) be the median of \(\chi_n^2\), and choose a smooth function
\[
0\le\psi\le1,\qquad
\operatorname{supp}\psi\subset[-1,1],
\qquad
\psi=1\ \text{on }[-1/2,1/2].
\]

For small \(\delta>0\), define
\[
w_{\delta,\varepsilon}(x)
=
1-(1-\varepsilon)
\psi\!\left(\frac{|x|^2-a}{\delta}\right),
\]
and
\[
d\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}
=
\frac{w_{\delta,\varepsilon}}c\,d\gamma_n,
\qquad
c=\mathbb E_{\gamma_n}w_{\delta,\varepsilon}.
\tag{16}
\]

This suppresses a thin annulus around a Gaussian median radius. Its width is controlled by \(\delta\); its residual density is controlled independently by \(\varepsilon\).

### Four-coordinate integration preserves almost Gaussian curvature

The density of \(\chi_4^2\) is
\[
h(z)=\frac z4e^{-z/2},\qquad z\ge0.
\]
For \(q=|y|^2\), set
\[
H(q)=
\int_0^\infty
\psi\!\left(\frac{q+z-a}{\delta}\right)h(z)\,dz.
\]

The codimension-four marginal has density proportional to
\[
e^{-q/2}\bigl[1-(1-\varepsilon)H(q)\bigr].
\tag{17}
\]

The relevant bounds are
\[
0\le H\le\frac\delta e,\qquad
|H'|\le\frac\delta2,\qquad
H''\ge-\frac\delta2.
\tag{18}
\]

Here is why the last bound, which matters most, holds. Integration by parts twice gives
\[
H''(q)
=
\frac14\psi\!\left(\frac{q-a}{\delta}\right)
+
\int_0^\infty
\psi\!\left(\frac{q+z-a}{\delta}\right)
\left(\frac z{16}-\frac14\right)e^{-z/2}\,dz.
\]
The boundary term is nonnegative. The negative part of the integrand is bounded by \(1/4\), and its support has length at most \(2\delta\). This proves the third inequality in (18). The first two follow similarly from
\[
\|h\|_\infty=\frac1{2e},
\qquad
\|h'\|_\infty\le\frac14.
\]

Write \(b=1-\varepsilon\). For the logarithm of (17),
\[
\ell'=-\frac12-\frac{bH'}{1-bH},
\qquad
\ell''=
-\frac{bH''}{1-bH}
-\frac{b^2(H')^2}{(1-bH)^2}.
\]
Also, \(H\) and its derivatives vanish for \(q>a+\delta\).

Using (18), both radial and tangential Hessian eigenvalues are at most
\[
-1+\tau_\delta,
\qquad
\tau_\delta=
\frac{\delta[1+2(a+\delta)]}{1-\delta/e}.
\tag{19}
\]
Thus every codimension-four marginal is \((1-\tau_\delta)\)-strongly log-concave, uniformly in \(\varepsilon\).

Crucially,
\[
\tau_\delta\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{as }\delta\downarrow0,
\]
regardless of how deep the annular bottleneck becomes.

### Relative entropy and isotropic normalization

Let
\[
M_n=\|\text{density of }\chi_n^2\|_\infty.
\]
The Gaussian mass affected by the annulus satisfies
\[
m:=
\mathbb E_{\gamma_n}
\psi\!\left(\frac{|X|^2-a}{\delta}\right)
\le2\delta M_n.
\]

Because \(0<w_{\delta,\varepsilon}\le1\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
D(\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|\gamma_n)
&=
\mathbb E_{\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}}
\log\frac{w_{\delta,\varepsilon}}c\\
&\le-\log c\\
&\le-\log(1-m)\\
&\le4\delta M_n
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]
for sufficiently small \(\delta\).

Rotational symmetry gives covariance \(\beta I\), with
\[
|\beta-1|
\le
\frac{(|n-a|+\delta)m}{n(1-m)}
=O_n(\delta),
\tag{21}
\]
uniformly in \(\varepsilon\).

Let \(\widehat\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\) be the isotropic rescaling. Its projected curvature lower bound becomes
\[
\beta(1-\tau_\delta)=1-O_n(\delta).
\]
Moreover, direct entropy calculation gives
\[
D(\widehat\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|\gamma_n)
=
D(\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}\|\gamma_n)
-\frac n2(\beta-1-\log\beta).
\tag{22}
\]
The subtracted quantity is nonnegative.

Choosing \(\delta\) sufficiently small therefore establishes the entropy and marginal-curvature requirements of Theorem C, **uniformly over every \(0<\varepsilon\le1\)**.

### A balanced test function with arbitrarily small energy

Define \(f\) to be \(0\) for
\[
|x|^2\le a-\delta/2,
\]
to be \(1\) for
\[
|x|^2\ge a+\delta/2,
\]
and linear in \(|x|^2\) between them.

The sets
\[
A=\{|x|^2\le a-\delta\},
\qquad
B=\{|x|^2\ge a+\delta\}
\]
lie outside the modified annulus. Because \(a\) is a median,
\[
\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}(A),
\ \nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}(B)
\ge\frac12-\delta M_n
\ge\frac13
\]
when \(\delta\) is small. Hence
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}}f
\ge\frac19.
\tag{23}
\]

Throughout the transition region, \(\psi=1\), so the density multiplier is exactly \(\varepsilon\). Also,
\[
|\nabla f|^2=\frac{4|x|^2}{\delta^2}.
\]
For \(\delta\le1\) and \(c\ge1/2\),
\[
\int|\nabla f|^2\,d\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon}
\le
8(a+1)M_n\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}.
\tag{24}
\]

After isotropic rescaling, \(\beta\le2\) for small \(\delta\), so (23)–(24) yield
\[
\boxed{
C_P(\widehat\nu_{\delta,\varepsilon})
\ge
\frac{\delta}{144(a+1)M_n\,\varepsilon}.
}
\tag{25}
\]

First choose \(\delta\) to secure the prescribed Gaussian proximity and marginal curvature. Then choose \(\varepsilon\) small enough to make (25) exceed the prescribed \(K\).

This proves Theorem C.
