## Finding

Astala–Iwaniec–Prause–Saksman (AIPS), Proposition 5.1, is a close and substantive antecedent: it proves a Jensen-type inequality for gradients of radial maps against every rank-one-concave integrand. Its actual input is `f(x)=Lambda(|x|)x` on a ball and its affine boundary datum is `Lambda(R) I`. The proof is a shell-by-shell monotonicity argument: a rank-one supporting inequality is integrated over each sphere, and the isotropic spherical second moment cancels the derivative of the affine comparison energy.

The FL theorem has a different structured family and a different identity. It permits an arbitrary real amplitude depending on every spatial coordinate, multiplying a prescribed periodic target loop with affine phase; its affine comparison matrix is arbitrary. The proof splits the gradient into two rank-one terms and uses `V''=-aV`, `a>=0`, to construct an exact divergence primitive. The resulting gap is the sum of a rank-one Bregman remainder and a nonnegative acoustic-curvature integral. The AIPS proof does not supply this identity by a direct specialization, while the FL proof does not reproduce AIPS's radial shell monotonicity. Both are positive Jensen constructions; the shared tangent inequality alone is not a novelty distinction.

The natural families are not equivalent by the obvious coordinate identifications. For a nonzero radial AIPS map, the target line is spanned by `x`; for a circle-loop FL map it is spanned by `V(k·x)`, fixed along affine-phase hyperplanes wherever the amplitude is nonzero. The FL example

```text
phi(x,y) = (1 + 0.5 cos x) (cos y, sin y)
```

has nonradial amplitude and affine phase, whereas a nontrivial radial map has angular phase `arg(x)`, which is not affine. This establishes a concrete mechanism/input distinction, not a theorem that the FL family is new among all laminate or Jensen constructions.
