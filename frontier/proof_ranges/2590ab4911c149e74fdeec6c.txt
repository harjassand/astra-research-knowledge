## 3. Target and normalization

Let eta be in {0,1}^{Z^2}, with 1 denoting a particle. The equilibrium is product Bernoulli measure mu_p; q=1-p and chi=pq. For a nearest-neighbor bond {x,y}, the hard rate is

\[
c_{xy}(\eta)=
\mathbf 1\{\exists z\sim x,\ z\ne y:\eta_z=0\}
\mathbf 1\{\exists z\sim y,\ z\ne x:\eta_z=0\}.
\]

An exchange of equal occupations does nothing. This rate is invariant under exchanging the two endpoint occupations. It is exactly the hard rule: no softening parameter or artificial transitions are introduced.

In the horizontal direction, put a_i=(eta_0-eta_{e_i}) delta_{i1}, and

\[
G_i f=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^2}\nabla_{0,e_i}\tau_z f.
\]

For local f this sum is finite. The mathematical target is

\[
D(p)=\frac1{2\chi}\inf_{f\ \mathrm{local}}
\mathbb E_p\sum_{i=1}^2 c_i(a_i+G_i f)^2. \tag{1}
\]

Rate-one simple exclusion has D=1 in this convention. This is the collective/bulk coefficient, not tagged-particle self-diffusion. Identifying (1) with a hard-constraint hydrodynamic limit under specified initial conditions is a separate theorem; no such additional hydrodynamic assertion is made here. The existing variational and Thomson principles are background, not the invention [1,3,4].

If J is antisymmetric under its own bond exchange, legal (zero wherever the hard rate is zero), square integrable, translation covariant, and satisfies

\[
\mathbb E\sum_i J_i G_i f=0\quad\text{for every local } f, \tag{2}
\]

completion of the square and optimal scalar rescaling give

\[
D(p)\ge \frac{\langle a,J\rangle^2}{2\chi\|J\|^2},
\quad \|J\|^2=\mathbb E\sum_i J_i^2. \tag{3}
\]

The hard rates equal one on the support of J, so this is the c^{-1} energy norm. None of the construction below assumes a spectral gap or a supplied completion-error estimate.
