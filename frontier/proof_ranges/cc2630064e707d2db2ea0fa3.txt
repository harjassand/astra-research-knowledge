# Independent material and metrology derivations

15 October 2026. Conditional internal research; physical capability and historical originality UNKNOWN. The calculations are independent reconstruction and new conditional deductions, **not a replay of an acquired producer bundle**. SI angular frequency is `omega=2 pi f`; quoted THz labels from a source remain its reported labels.

## A. A loaded parallel-wall exchanger cannot remove its normal pressure load by offset balancing

Consider two parallel channels sharing a wall, with the same longitudinal coordinate from left to right. Let `0` and `L` bound the actual shared interaction region and define its local drops

\[
\Delta P_i=p_i(0)-p_i(L).
\]

The donor is pressure driven with `DeltaP1>0`. Let receiver flow have the same local direction, `Q2>0`, as passive positive cross-drag, while delivering work against `DeltaP2<0`. The pressure difference across the separator is `delta(x)=p1(x)-p2(x)`. Its endpoints satisfy, without assuming a linear pressure profile,

\[
\delta(0)-\delta(L)=\Delta P_1-\Delta P_2
=\Delta P_1+|\Delta P_2|.
\]

By the triangle inequality,

\[
\boxed{\max\{|\delta(0)|,|\delta(L)|\}
\ge\frac{\Delta P_1+|\Delta P_2|}{2}.}
\]

Changing the relative offset between the channels' mean pressures translates both endpoint differences by a constant; it cannot change their separation. The endpoint lower bound is attained when `delta(0)=-delta(L)=(DeltaP1+|DeltaP2|)/2`. For linear profiles, this also minimizes the largest normal pressure anywhere on the wall. For nonlinear profiles an interior extremum can require still more strength. A pressure offset applied equally to both channels does not change `delta` at all.

For a donor drop of 1 bar and receiver load of 0.2 bar over the shared region, at least one end of the shared wall sees at least 0.6 bar normal differential pressure, however the relative offset is selected. This is an operating-point example, not a fabricated membrane specification. External port drops cannot be substituted for these local drops when access regions or unshared channel segments carry appreciable pressure changes. Ordinary series loss downstream increases the adverse rise required in the driven receiver region; it does not eliminate it.

Thus normal mechanical stability/compliance cannot be independently set to zero while retaining these two hydraulic work conditions. Support, bonding, pressure safety, wall deflection, changed electronic scattering and permeation must be qualified together. This theorem does not prohibit supported walls, nonparallel routing, body-force-driven donors or a different port architecture. Those changes have their own complete force/work boundaries. Uniform-velocity theory or molecular simulation alone does not acquire this pressure-loaded fixture.

## B. What a published unloaded velocity ratio says about work

For a steady linear reciprocal device, `Q=L DeltaP`, with `L` symmetric positive definite. Suppose the full channels, including access and bulk friction, are identical so `L11=L22`. At zero receiver pressure,

\[
r=Q_2/Q_1=L_{12}/L_{11},\qquad
q^2=L_{12}^2/(L_{11}L_{22})=r^2.
\]

The source's standard reciprocal-converter result therefore becomes

\[
\boxed{\eta_{\max}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-r^2}}{1+\sqrt{1-r^2}}
=\frac{r^2}{(1+\sqrt{1-r^2})^2}.}
\]

To verify the optimization directly, write `DeltaP2=-x DeltaP1`. Positive useful output requires `0<x<r`, and the power ratio is `eta(x)=x(r-x)/(1-r x)`. Differentiation gives `r-2x+r x²=0`; its admissible root is `x=(1-sqrt(1-r²))/r`, which yields the expression above. The result includes both channels' diagonal losses through the acquired full matrix `L`, not merely a microscopic interface ratio.

For `r<<1`, `eta_max≈r²/4`. An unloaded ratio `r=0.1` thus permits at most 0.2513% work efficiency in this symmetric linear reduction; `r=0.4` gives about 4.36%. The paper's term “tunnelling efficiency” is a velocity ratio, not an energy efficiency. For unequal channels or nonuniform velocity profiles, one velocity ratio does not identify `q²`; both port responses and their full diagonal laws are required. The molecular simulations' velocities are not automatically the installed device's terminal volume flows.

Coquinot et al. give an approximate scaling, their Eq.10,

\[
r\simeq A\exp[-(\gamma\tau_{sl}+d/\ell)],
\quad A=\lambda_{qf}/(\lambda_{qf}+\lambda_{cl})\le1.
\]

Their real-carbon discussion estimates `gamma tau_sl~1`, `ell~4 nm`. **Within that approximate ansatz**, at `d=10 nm` and even `A=1`, `r<=exp(-3.5)=0.03020`; the symmetric work ceiling is about 0.02281%. For `A=0.5`, it is about 0.00570%. This is not a rigorous material upper bound: the authors explicitly describe the estimate as approximate, discuss neglected perpendicular electron transport, and do not measure the wet two-liquid coefficient matrix. Nor should the thick-wall asymptotic formula be treated as an exact thin-layer theorem. It screens the interpretation of a published estimate, not nature as a whole. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-024-01842-8).

## C. Energy-transfer evidence does not identify momentum-transfer strength

Use a restricted but useful common-channel model: two internally equilibrated, isotropic reservoirs at common temperature `T`, coupled by a passive, velocity-independent equilibrium transmission `mathcal T(q,omega)>=0`. A small temperature difference transfers energy; a small relative tangential velocity Doppler-shifts the Bose populations. Let

\[
d\Gamma=\frac{d^2q}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{d\omega}{2\pi},\quad
n=(e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT}-1)^{-1}.
\]

The thermal boundary conductance per area and linear friction coefficient are

\[
h=\int d\Gamma\,\mathcal T
\frac{\hbar^2\omega^2}{k_BT^2}n(n+1),
\]

\[
\lambda=\int d\Gamma\,\mathcal T
\frac{\hbar^2q_x^2}{k_BT}n(n+1).
\]

Rotational symmetry gives `<qx²>=q²/2`. Defining the normalized heat-transfer weight by the integrand of `h`,

\[
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{T h}{2}
\left\langle\frac{q^2}{\omega^2}\right\rangle_h.}
\]

The units are correct: `T h / (omega/q)^2` is `Pa s/m`. If all transferring modes have known phase velocities `v_min<=omega/q<=v_max`,

\[
\frac{T h}{2v_{max}^2}\le\lambda\le\frac{T h}{2v_{min}^2}.
\]

Without the momentum-resolved spectral weight, `h` alone cannot identify `lambda`. A countermodel uses identical frequencies, occupation factors, transmission and mode measure, but replaces `q1` by `q2`: heat transfer is identical while friction changes by `(q2/q1)^2`. Our executable example has a factor `10^6`. This is a logical nonidentifiability witness within the channel class, not a claim that those two materials have been obtained.

The 2023 graphene/water cooling experiment is even farther from a DC work acquisition: it measures picosecond cooling of optically heated electrons, with initial temperatures 623–1241 K, and interprets its water/isotope-specific result through libration-mode coupling. It does not measure the equilibrium two-liquid `h`, the spectral moment above, DC friction or loaded receiver flow. [Yu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41565-023-01421-3).

The active-wall Keldysh theory with inelastic scattering need not admit this single-transmission reduction. The identity is not asserted as a universal bound on that theory. It proves that even the favorable shared-channel reduction does not permit a heat-only observation to substitute for acquired momentum transport. One can qualify useful macroscopic work through a direct port measurement without first reconstructing every microscopic mode; quantum-mechanism attribution needs stronger spectroscopy and intervention evidence.

## D. Arbitrarily slow high-q Drude modes are not acquired graphene plasmons

As a diagnostic, consider a **long-wavelength** local monolayer graphene model at temperature `T`, chemical potential `mu`, and real nondispersive background `epsilon_eff`:

\[
\sigma(\omega)=iD/\omega,\quad
D=\frac{e^2}{\pi\hbar^2}
2k_BT\ln[2\cosh(\mu/(2k_BT))],
\]

\[
\omega_p^2(q)=Dq/(2\epsilon_0\epsilon_{eff}).
\]

At neutrality the effective Drude energy is `2 kBT ln2=35.84 meV` at 300 K; tuning `mu` to zero does not remove this thermal scale. The finite-temperature intrinsic-plasmon literature also emphasizes the long-wavelength requirement and Landau damping. [Das Sarma and Li](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.87.235418).

For an illustrative **ordinary frequency** `f=0.3 THz`, the local collisionless expansion requires `q vF/omega<<1`. At `q=2.5 nm^-1`, using `vF=10^6 m/s`, this parameter is about 1326. Thus the slow/high-q simulator mode cannot simply be declared a local sharp monolayer-graphene plasmon. Formal substitution into the local dispersion is not a valid high-q prediction.

The code varies a diagnostic background permittivity from 2 to 80 to show the issue, without assigning those values to a wet device. Actual frequency-dependent liquid screening, nonlocal response, scattering, confinement and thermal carrier distributions must be used. Multilayer graphite, interlayer modes, flat-band materials, MXenes and acoustic plasmons can differ; this calculation is not their exclusion. It rejects an arbitrary favorable Drude substitution only.

## E. Mechanical and permeation counterfeits must be bounded by work

For a circular small-slope tension membrane of radius `a` and tension `S`, a uniform normal differential pressure gives

\[
w(r)=\Delta p(a^2-r^2)/(4S),\quad
\Delta V=\pi a^4\Delta p/(8S),\quad
C_V=\pi a^4/(8S).
\]

Stored elastic energy is `U_el=C_V Delta p²/2`. This is the work available upon release from that stored state, not a bound on indefinitely driven membrane pumping. Under a pressure hold, receiver flow caused only by settling volume is proportional to `dV/dt`; a purely stationary wall cannot continue that displacement indefinitely. A general driven-mechanical nuisance budget uses the measured boundary power

\[
W_{mech}=\int dt\int_{wall}\mathbf t\cdot\dot{\mathbf u}_{wall}\,dA,
\]

or a conservative pressure-volume bound `P_max TV(V)` for a measured compliant volume coordinate. Oscillations, creeping supports, rectification and active vibration require their continuing mechanical work, not a one-shot elastic allowance.

For a leak with bounded transverse volume flow and pressure, its hydraulic energy throughput is bounded by

\[
W_{leak}^{U}=\int |\Delta p_\perp|\,|Q_\perp|\,dt.
\]

Chemical/electrochemical transport and thermal sources need additional correctly defined budgets, without double-counting electrical energy already contained in electrochemical potentials. A tiny high-pressure leak can power a larger low-pressure receiver circulation; comparing output volume directly with leaked volume is not enough. Compare **work**.

An observation `W_out > W_mech^U+W_leak^U+W_other_known^U`, with uncertainty and consistent boundaries, excludes those bounded counterfeit routes. It does not by itself uniquely prove a quantum-electronic mechanism. Ideal graphene impermeability to atoms/molecules does not certify a fabricated wetted wall's proton, defect or edge leakage. [Hu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14015).

## F. A different intervention: remove friction before trying to harvest it

The physically different route is to suppress an existing dissipative wall spectrum rather than add a second-fluid work path. A measured total pressure-drop reduction at fixed useful throughput would directly test that endpoint. The 2024 glycerol/mica experiment provides an actual spectral/friction tuning precedent, including a nonmonotonic friction coefficient and strongly temperature-dependent slip. It does not supply a thin-wall power converter or a fixed-temperature electronic switch. [Lizée et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50232-0).

For a Navier-slip slit of height `h`, length `L`, viscosity `eta`, slip `b=eta/lambda`,

\[
Q/w=\frac{h^3\Delta P}{12\eta L}(1+6b/h)
=\frac{h^3\Delta P}{12L}\left(\frac1\eta+\frac6{h\lambda}\right).
\]

Hence a large cooling-induced slip-length increase is not the same as increased permeance; viscosity and friction must be measured together, and refrigeration is part of the complete task cost. A fixed-temperature back-side electronic/phononic intervention could be scientifically useful if it changes `lambda` while the actual wetted face, contact chemistry and mechanics are controlled. N802 already warns that a statically matched optical spectrum does not acquire the loaded target response; no qualified switching material or new operation is claimed here. No extraordinary new capability was established.
