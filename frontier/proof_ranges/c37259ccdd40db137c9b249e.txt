## 3. Pushing beyond a shrinking-coordinate encoding

A construction that merely asks whether an exponentially small coordinate is positive would leave the operational meaning of the output questionable. I therefore made the computation change **the actual exponential decay of the loss**.

The bridge has three parts.

### Encode finite programs without hiding exponential input costs

A Turing-machine tape can be represented by two finite stacks. For an alphabet of size \(a\), assign odd digits \(d_j=2j-1\) and choose \(B=4a\). Encode a stack as
\[
x=\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}d_{w_r}B^{-r}.
\]

A stack of \(\ell\) symbols requires \(O(\ell)\) bits—not an exponentially long initial number.

The possible top symbols occupy separated rational intervals. Push and pop are affine on their respective branches:
\[
\operatorname{push}_d(x)=\frac{d+x}{B},
\qquad
\operatorname{pop}_d(x)=Bx-d.
\]

Continuous piecewise-linear plateau functions select the appropriate symbol interval. These functions have finite ReLU representations. Adding a scale coordinate homogenises the affine operations without computing a division by that scale.

The proof note gives the branch-selection formulas and both-stack transition rules explicitly.

### Convert acceptance into persistent geometric growth

Construct a fixed finite-depth homogeneous ReLU network \(F\) implementing one transition of a fixed universal interpreter.

Before acceptance, its scale coordinate remains one, and its encoded configurations stay in a fixed bounded set.

Upon acceptance, it resets to a canonical accepting vector \(q_*\) and thereafter obeys
\[
F(sq_*)=\Gamma s q_*.
\]

Let the feedforward depth be \(\ell\). Choose
\[
\Gamma=4^\ell.
\]

There is no circular choice here: the circuit architecture is fixed first, with \(\Gamma\) treated as a coefficient parameter. Changing its numerical value does not change the depth.

A block-cyclic pipeline converts the successive layers into one fixed recurrent matrix \(W\). With a suitable finite encoding \(P\),
\[
R^\ell P(q)=P(Fq),
\qquad R(u)=\sigma(Wu).
\]

Therefore
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|R^tP(q_0)\|^{1/t}
=
\begin{cases}
4,&\text{acceptance occurs},\\
1,&\text{acceptance never occurs}.
\end{cases}
\]

### Transfer the growth distinction into loss decay

The gradient-exchange construction inserts one recurrent update every two gradient steps. Consequently,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\|z_t\|^{1/t}
=
\begin{cases}
2\rho,&\text{acceptance occurs},\\
\rho,&\text{acceptance never occurs}.
\end{cases}
\]

Both cases still contract rapidly toward zero.

Since \(f(z_t)=\|z_t\|^2/2\) and \(\rho=2^{-k}\), the two loss exponents are exactly
\[
2k-2
\quad\text{and}\quad
2k.
\]

This produces a **fixed gap in the requested numerical output**, rather than requiring an increasingly accurate sign measurement of a vanishing coordinate.

It does not eliminate the growing precision needed to follow the computation internally. That cost is accounted for below.
