For completeness, the first row follows by Taylor expansion of the standard
Gaussian density around the true mean. For D=sample_mean−mu, E D=0 and
E||D||^2<=R^2/n. In every unit direction v, the L1 norm of the second
directional derivative of the Gaussian density is E|Z^2−1|<=2, where Z is
standard normal. The linear Taylor term cancels after expectation, the
integral remainder has weight integral_0^1(1−t)dt=1/2, and TV adds a factor
1/2. Hence TV(Law(sample_mean+G),N(mu,I))<=R^2/(2n).

## Acquisition and precision ledger

- Known R is a substantive input promise. Finite observed samples cannot certify
  an almost-sure bound for an arbitrary unknown distribution. A scientific source
  must derive it from the measurement/model interface, or pay for its verification.
- iid acquisition includes simulator/measurement setup, burn-in where applicable,
  and generation/read cost C_X. A correlated trajectory is not the promised oracle.
- No law learning, mean learning, hidden representation, or decoder is supplied.
  Preprocessing chooses m using only R and epsilon, in O(log m) scalar steps.
- Each proposal generates d Gaussian coordinates. Each oracle sample requires a
  d-coordinate inner product and norm check in the diagnostic implementation.
- Expected acquisitions are <=2e^2m. Expected Gaussian vectors/proposals are
  <=e^2m. Working memory is O(d) when factors are accumulated online. Output is d
  real coordinates, so writing it costs at least Omega(d).
- The theorem is in an ideal random-real/arithmetic model. A float vector has
  finite support and TV distance 1 from a continuous Gaussian. A deployment must
  specify rounded-target output or an exact-real random-bit representation and
  prove its arithmetic acceptance error. That bit-level gate is not closed by
  this Python implementation.
- If the acquired X is independently rounded with ||X_tilde−X||<=eta almost
  surely, then the procedure targets mu_tilde=E X_tilde. Pinsker bounds
  TV(N(mu_tilde,I),N(mu,I))<=eta/2. This controls oracle rounding bias only;
  it is not a complete numerical-analysis proof for Gaussian/probability generation.
