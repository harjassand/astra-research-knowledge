## 5. The stronger routes this construction rules out

### A resource-preserving causalization theorem is false

A tempting route to the unrestricted parallel result was:

> If a complete LOCC operation satisfies the no-backward-signalling equations of a causal process, it should admit an LOCC realization respecting that causal order.

The flagged construction is a counterexample.

Equation (3) is exactly the relevant no-backward-signalling condition. Equation (4) supplies an LOCC implementation of the complete channel. But any implementation respecting the earlier flag deadline needs a non-LOCC conditional channel.

This does not contradict the established theorem that semicausal operations are semilocalizable. That theorem permits a quantum system to be transmitted in the causal realization; it does not assert preservation of a zero-entanglement LOCC implementation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0104027?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The gap is therefore not an omitted application of a standard causal-realization theorem. **The unrestricted realization theorem does not preserve the resource restriction needed here.**

### Input-independent success does not justify free normalization

Another proposed bridge was to normalize a postselected branch whenever its success probability is independent of the input.

Here the successful branch satisfies
\[
\mathcal S=p\mathcal N,\qquad
\mathcal S^\dagger(I)=pI.
\tag{17}
\]
It belongs to an LOCC instrument. Its normalized channel \(\mathcal S/p=\mathcal N\) is nevertheless outside \(\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}\).

Thus:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Uniform success probability}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{deterministic LOCC implementation of the normalized branch}.
}
\tag{18}
\]

The distinction survives a fixed success probability \(1/9\). It is not caused by arbitrarily rare events or a limiting precision issue.

These two failures are the same obstruction viewed from different directions: **normalizing an outcome obtained after processing the input is not equivalent to selecting a usable operation before receiving that input.**

### This does not refute the original memoryless parallel conjecture

The construction is not a counterexample to
\[
\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}
\quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
E_{\mathrm{par}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathcal N)>0.
\tag{19}
\]

The early-flag object has an additional interaction stage inside each use. Replacing an ordinary memoryless channel by that two-stage process changes the task.

What has been disproved is the **general causalization lemma that could otherwise have been used to infer (19)**. The more restricted statement about arbitrary collective simulations of \(\mathcal N^{\otimes n}\) remains unresolved in this investigation.

## 6. What remains indispensable

A route to (19) must control collective block implementations without assuming that their normalized conditional operations are free.

One sufficient missing result would be a block-level testing theorem: for every
\[
\mathcal N\notin\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}},
\]
construct allowed tests with acceptance events \(A_n\) such that
\[
P_{\mathcal N^{\otimes n}}(A_n)\longrightarrow1
\]
while
\[
\sup_{\mathcal L_n\in\overline{\mathrm{LOCC}}}
P_{\mathcal L_n}(A_n)
\le e^{-c_{\mathcal N}n}
\tag{20}
\]
for some \(c_{\mathcal N}>0\).

The tests would need to remain valid against arbitrary collective LOCC across all \(n\) inputs. Combined with the retained resource-accounting argument, such a separation would rule out sublinear entanglement budgets, including exactly returned catalysts.

**No such general testing theorem is proved here.** In particular, the sequential conditional-probability argument cannot simply be reused: the counterexample above shows why a normalized branch can lie outside LOCC even when its probability is input-independent.

The rank-drop tensor-universality theorem remains intact but does not fill this gap. It concerns existence of exact postselected conversions and finite-dimensional algebra representations. It does not provide a method for issuing success before the input, preserving failed inputs for retry, or implementing the normalized successful branch as a free trace-preserving operation.

Accordingly, the two earlier results do not by themselves establish a new computational capability or an undecidability–decidability dichotomy.
