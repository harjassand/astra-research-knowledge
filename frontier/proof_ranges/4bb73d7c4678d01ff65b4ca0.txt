## 3. Turning the curvature argument into a finite algorithm

Work in sign coordinates:
\[
Q=2P(M)-\mathbf1,\qquad z_0=2x-\mathbf1.
\]

Retain the previous coefficient-removal accounting and spectral potential
\[
\Phi(z)=\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(M(z))-1\bigr)_+^3-\eta\|z\|_2^2.
\]

For the constructive proof, take
\[
K=64,\qquad \eta=\frac1{Kn},
\]
and replace exact square-root row scales by rational upper scales satisfying
\[
q_i\le r_i^2<4q_i.
\]

These scales can be chosen as dyadic multiples of original coefficients. No exact square-root operation is needed.

### Enough curvature survives all the algorithmic constraints

Let the current minimal matroid face have \(s\) nonconstant coordinates. Its dimension is at least \(s/2\).

Protect every medium-row barrier whose value satisfies \(u_{i\sigma}\ge-1\). Under the spectral invariant \(L=\lambda_{\max}(M)<2\), the dimension costs are bounded as follows:
\[
\text{large and protected-row equations}
\le\frac{129s}{K^2},
\]
and
\[
\text{spectral equations used in the proof}
\le\frac{4s}{K}+\frac2K.
\]

Reserve two additional equations: one for the objective and one for an approximate potential gradient.

The surviving dimension is at least
\[
\frac{s}{2}
-\frac{129s}{K^2}
-\frac{4s}{K}
-\frac2K
-2
>
\frac{5s}{K}
\qquad(s>K).
\]

That is enough for the inherited restricted-trace argument. It supplies a nonzero feasible direction with
\[
\boxed{
h^\top\nabla^2\Phi(z)h
\le-2\eta\|h\|_2^2.
}
\]

The important improvement is quantitative: the negative curvature has an inverse-polynomial margin.

The exact high-eigenvalue subspace is used **only in this existence proof**. The algorithm does not compute an exact spectral cutoff. Instead, it forms the rational constraint space and uses a certified approximate Hessian. Rational symmetric elimination finds a negative quadratic-form direction within that space.

### Full steps and short steps have different certificates of progress

Normalize the resulting rational direction so that
\[
\frac12\le\|h\|_2\le1.
\]

Compute the exact maximal feasible lengths in both directions:
\[
t_+=\max\{t\ge0:z+th\in Q\},
\qquad
t_-=\max\{t\ge0:z-th\in Q\}.
\]

For a fixed inverse-polynomial step size \(\tau\), put
\[
t=\min\{\tau,t_+,t_-\}.
\]

When \(t=\tau\), take a full step. When \(t<\tau\), choose the sign that reaches the corresponding endpoint. That short step hits a **proper matroid face exactly**.

Apply the precision reset inside the reached face, then perform coefficient cleanup.

Thus every iteration has one of two outcomes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{a full step decreases the potential by a fixed polynomial amount;}
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\text{a short step strictly decreases face dimension.}
}
\]

There can be at most \(n\) short steps. This remains true when a face shrinks without immediately making a coordinate integral.

### Explicit precision and termination budgets

The manuscript gives the following conservative choices:
\[
R=16K^2(m+n+1),\qquad
H=2^{16}R^6\eta^{-3},\qquad
\tau=\frac{\eta}{128H},
\]
where \(H\) bounds the relevant smoothness constants.

Choose derivative accuracy
\[
\epsilon=\frac{\eta\tau}{1024(n+1)},
\]
and set
\[
N=\left\lceil\frac{256n}{\tau^2}\right\rceil+n+1,
\]
\[
\delta=
\min\left\{
\frac{\eta\tau^2}{2^{16}H(n+1)},
\frac1{128N}
\right\}.
\]

These parameters are fixed before the walk.

For an exact local move, Taylor’s theorem gives
\[
\Phi(z+\sigma th)-\Phi(z)
\le
\epsilon t-\frac{\eta t^2}{32}.
\]

The precision reset increases the potential by at most \(H\delta\), and cleanup cannot increase it. Consequently, a full step decreases the potential by at least
\[
\gamma=\frac{\eta\tau^2}{64},
\]
while a short step increases it by at most
\[
\frac{\eta\tau^2}{512(n+1)}.
\]

Since there are at most \(n\) short steps,
\[
-\frac1K\le\Phi\le\frac1{512K}.
\]
This also preserves \(L<2\).

The number of full steps is therefore less than
\[
\frac{65n}{\tau^2}.
\]

For fixed \(K\), the resulting local-update bound is
\[
O\!\left(n^9(m+n)^{12}\right).
\]

That is an extremely conservative polynomial, not a practical running-time claim. Each update also contains polynomial-time linear-algebra and oracle subroutines.

### The reset does not create a cumulative discrepancy loss

A protected row barrier has zero first derivative in the chosen direction and nonpositive second derivative, so it does not increase along the exact move.

An unprotected barrier starts below \(-1\) and remains safely below zero over the short prescribed step. The precision reset changes any barrier by at most \(2\delta\). Over the entire computation,
\[
u_{i\sigma}\le2N\delta\le\frac1{64}.
\]

This small positive allowance still implies the same tracked-row bound
\[
|d_i|<2K^3.
\]

The earlier coefficient-removal error is a whole-history bound, not a per-iteration cost. Precision resets do not change that accounting: each removed coefficient contributes only its coefficient times the difference between its removal-time and current coordinates. The inherited bound is \(4K\) per row. proof

At termination, at most \(K\) coordinates remain nonconstant. A minimum-cost vertex of the final face adds at most \(2K\) sign-coordinate error. Hence
\[
\|A(v-z_0)\|_\infty
\le
2K^3+4K+2K.
\]

Returning to Boolean coordinates gives
\[
\|A(\mathbf1_B-x)\|_\infty\le K^3+3K,
\]
and
\[
c^\top\mathbf1_B\le c^\top x.
\]

That completes the proposed one-basis construction.
