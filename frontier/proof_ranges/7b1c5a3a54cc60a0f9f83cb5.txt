 Fast forwarding conservative interacting transport by
 rate saturation

 Proof candidate draft | 12 October 2026
 Internally derived mathematical proof candidate. No novelty or priority claim, implementation, benchmark, external
 certification, or formal verification is asserted.

 1 Actual computational task
 Input: an explicitly supplied undirected graph on n vertices, m edges with nonnegative binary-rational exchange rates
 w_e, an initial permutation of n distinguishable labels (or an initial multicolor occupancy configuration), a
 nonnegative binary-rational horizon T, and accuracy 0<epsilon<=1. At rate w_{uv}, the labels at u and v exchange.
 This is the continuous-time interchange process. Coloring some labels occupied and others vacant gives symmetric
 simple exclusion, preserving both hard-core exclusion and particle conservation. Arbitrary static spatial disorder in
 edge rates is allowed, including exponentially separated scales.
 Output: one full endpoint configuration whose law is within total-variation epsilon of the exact law at time T. The
 proposed algorithm is polynomial in n,m, the bit lengths of T and the rates, and 1/epsilon. It does not enumerate n!
 permutations or binomial(n,k) configurations and does not infer the full distribution from one-particle marginals.
 This is an endpoint sampler, not a relative estimate of an exponentially rare event, a microscopic trajectory, a
 current-count sampler, a nonequilibrium interacting-potential model, or a reaction-network simulator. The physical
 assumption is unbiased, configuration-independent pair exchange with exclusion; rates are supplied physical inputs,
 not inferred for free. The input/output size is polynomial in the explicitly supplied graph, not in a succinctly encoded
 astronomically large lattice.

 2 Claim uniformly cap rates above a polynomial threshold
 Rescale time so the target horizon is 1, with rates r_e=T w_e. Let r_e^K=min(r_e,K). Write mu and mu_K for the
 endpoint laws from the same deterministic permutation under original and capped rates. Put c=n^2/2. For K>=2c,

   ||mu-mu_K||_TV <= (m c^2/K) [6+2 log(K/c)].                 (1)
 The bound is uniform in every original rate, including rates far above K, and in the starting permutation. By projection
 it also holds for every multicolor/exclusion endpoint law. Choosing K=O((m n^4/epsilon) log(2mn/epsilon)) suffices;
 a deliberately loose explicit choice K=(12 m n^3/epsilon)^2 makes the cap contribution at most epsilon/4 for
 n>=2,m>=1. Indeed log(K/c)+3<=3 sqrt(K/c), so (1) is at most 6m c^(3/2)/sqrt(K)<=3m n^3/sqrt(K). T=0 and
 edgeless graphs are trivial.
 Unlike clean-scale averaging, the argument does not require a gap between adjacent rate scales, and does not
 recursively enumerate or simulate equilibration regions.

 3 The local discrepancy operation
 Consider two interchange processes whose initial configurations differ only by exchanging the labels at the endpoints
 x,y of one edge e of current rate w>0.
 Couple common edge clocks identically except when an edge joins the two discrepancy positions. At such an edge,
 use independent rate-w clocks for the two processes. A single jump then coalesces the configurations; afterwards use
 common clocks everywhere. Before coalescence, only two labels disagree. Their two positions evolve as independent
 continuous-time random walks on the weighted physical graph, killed when they meet. Every other label remains
 matched.
 This coupling is an instance of the established interchange coupling described by Aldous and Fill, Chapter 14.5; the
 rate-weighted version follows from the same generator construction:
 https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch14.S5.html
