2. DENSITY, GENERATOR, AND FIXED-TRIANGLE NORMALIZATION

The fundamental square has area ONE and one vertex per translation
cell. For compactly supported smooth f,
       n^-2 sum f(x/n) -> integral f.
Thus the square density is ONE. The two triangles in a cell do NOT
produce a factor two in vertex averages.

In free-coordinate Euclidean space, the equilibrium diffusion has
Brownian bracket 2 I dt and generator Delta. For density exp(<beta,h>)
the form is integral grad F.grad G exp(<beta,h>)dh, so integration by
parts gives generator Delta+beta.grad with the same normal reflection.
Consequently exp(t n^-2 sum f h) gives physical drift t n^-2 f,
not t n^-2 f/2. This is exactly the (B2) convention used in P4.

At one square-lattice vertex the global hat has SIX incident triangles,
each of area 1/(2n^2). On each triangle its barycentric integral is
one third the area. Hence
       integral eta_{n,x}=n^-2.
The six triangles occur in three pairs reflected through x/n. The fixed
same diagonal is invariant under this half-turn. The hat values match
under the reflection, so
       integral (y-x/n) eta_{n,x}(y)dy=0.                   (2.1)
The support radius is at most sqrt(2)/n. Taylor's formula therefore gives
       |integral f eta_{n,x}-n^-2 f(x/n)|
            <= C ||D^2 f||_infinity n^-4.                  (2.2)
For an interior test, all needed hats lie inside the free macrobuffer
eventually. The zero-boundary edge constraint and (G1) give the
deterministic bound |h_x|<=C_D' n, by a lattice path to the exterior of
the bounding box. There are O(n^2) relevant vertices. Thus
       |<Phi_n,f>-X_n(f)|<=C_{D,f}/n                       (2.3)
for EVERY feasible configuration, including under every tilt. The same
holds for centered fields, with at most twice the constant if one had
centered explicitly. No pointwise variance estimate is used.

Combining (2.3) with the supplied mean response gives
       E_{n,t,f} X_n(f) -> (t/A_T) Q_D(f),
       Q_D(f)=integral_D f G_D f.                          (2.4)
The coefficient is A_T^-1. The triangular coefficient (v A_T)^-1
becomes precisely A_T^-1 here, because v=1, not because of an adjustment
to the Brownian variance or the number of mesh triangles.

4. SCALAR LOG MGF, INCLUDING THE EXACT FIXED-t GATE

Fix real f in C_c^infinity(D). For X_n=X_n(f), the weights n^-2 f(x/n)
have uniformly bounded absolute sum, maximum O(n^-2), and support
diameter O(n). Choose a connected exterior zero-pin square cycle of
diameter comparable to n lying with that support in an O(n) box. It is
a subset of E_n by (G1). Apply (M) at R=n to obtain a_f>0,M_f<infinity
with
       sup_n E_n exp(a_f |X_n|)<=M_f.                     (4.1)
Finitely many small n can be absorbed by compactness or the deterministic
bound; the macro theorem already covers them if the fixed constants
are enlarged. Since E_n X_n=0, Jensen gives E_n exp(t X_n)>=1.

For finite n compactness permits differentiation:
       L_n(t)=log E_n exp(t X_n),
       L_n'(t)=E_{n,t,f} X_n.
By (2.4)--(3.1), for every fixed |t|<tau_f with tau_f>0,
       L_n'(t)-> t Q_D(f)/A.                              (4.2)
Choose 0<tau<min(tau_f,a_f/2). For |t|<=tau,
       |L_n'(t)|
       <= E_n[|X_n| exp(a_f |X_n|/2)]
       <= (2/(e a_f)) E_n exp(a_f|X_n|)
       <= (2/(e a_f)) M_f.                                (4.3)
Here the denominator of the derivative is >=1, and
x exp(-a_f x/2)<=2/(e a_f) for x>=0. Thus a single integrable bound on
the derivative works throughout the fixed interval. Integrating (4.2)
and using dominated convergence, also for negative t, gives
       L_n(t)-> t^2 Q_D(f)/(2 A), |t|<=tau.                (4.4)

The exponential bound gives tightness of X_n. If a subsequence converges
in law to Z, exp(t X_n) is uniformly integrable for |t|<a_f/2, since its
second moment is bounded by (4.1). Therefore
       E exp(tZ)=exp(t^2 Q_D(f)/(2A))
on an open interval about zero. Uniqueness of a probability law from
its MGF on such an interval identifies Z as the centered normal with
variance Q_D(f)/A. Every subsequence has that law, so
       X_n(f) => Normal(0,Q_D(f)/A).                       (4.5)
For f=0 the statement is immediate. The deterministic (2.3) transfers
the result to <Phi_n,f>.

A susceptibility derivative at t=0 alone would NOT prove (4.2): a

8. CONSTRUCTION OF THE LIMIT AND IDENTIFICATION OF ZERO EXTENSIONS

Let {u_j,lambda_j} be a Dirichlet Laplacian eigenbasis in L2(D), and
let xi_j be independent standard real normals. Define the zero-extended
Gaussian field formally as
       Z=A^-1/2 sum_j xi_j lambda_j^-1/2 (u_j extended by zero).
This series actually converges in L2 of H^{-s}(T_tor) for every s>1.
Indeed, by Parseval and Tonelli,
  sum_j lambda_j^-1 ||u_j^ext||_{H^{-s}}^2
   =sum_k (1+|k|^2)^-s sum_j lambda_j^-1 |<e_k|D,u_j>|^2
   <=lambda_1(D)^-1 sum_k (1+|k|^2)^-s ||e_k|D||_2^2
   <infinity.                                             (8.1)
The variance of every L2(D) volume test g is
       E<Z,g>^2=A^-1 <g,G_D g>
                <=A^-1 lambda_1(D)^-1 ||g||_2^2.           (8.2)
Thus for the same boundary cutoff,
       E|<Z,(1-chi_delta)phi>|^2<=C delta ||phi||_infinity^2,
since the boundary strip's area is O(delta). This tends to zero.

Section 5 identifies each finite set of compact interior pairings with Z.
For an arbitrary fixed smooth torus test phi, first use chi_delta phi
as an interior test, then apply (7.2) and (8.2). Characteristic functions
differ by at most |t| times the expected absolute strip contribution.
Sending n->infinity then delta->0 proves scalar convergence for phi.
Apply the same argument to arbitrary finite linear combinations to
obtain all joint smooth-torus pairings. Hence boundary-supported extra
laws are excluded at the level needed for zero-extension identification.

For each fixed s>1, Section 6 gives H^{-s} tightness. Any subsequential
Hilbert-space limit has the same finite-dimensional Fourier-coordinate
