4. Acquiring the coefficients without a hidden oracle
For each probe, prepare the reset ensemble \(\nu\), evolve without further resets, and stop at an independent time
\[
T\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\rho).
\]
Record the endpoint measurements and the number \(C_j\) of tagged intervention events.
The endpoint law is
\[
\rho\int_0^\infty
e^{-\rho t}
e^{(L_0+\sum_j u_jB_j)t}\nu\,dt
=
\pi(u).
\]
Thus independent finite episodes produce independent observations of the reset system’s stationary law, without burn-in. This is an established resetting/resolvent identity, not a newly invented resetting principle. arXiv
The required flux is obtained directly:
\[
\boxed{r_j=\rho\,\mathbb E C_j.}
\]
Why tagged events matter
Estimating the same flux from endpoint occupancy would use
\[
s\,1_{\{X=a\}},
\]
whose variance is
\[
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(s\,1_{\{X=a\}}\right)=sr-r^2.
\]
A fast transition can have ordinary flux while its source occupancy becomes extremely small. Multiplying rare endpoint observations by the large rate then becomes statistically expensive.
Counting transitions avoids that particular penalty.
To control the counting cost, impose an ejection condition: every added transition lands outside the entire active source set. Between two controlled transitions, at least one background transition must then occur.
Let
\[
\Lambda=\Lambda_b+\rho.
\]
Competing background and reset clocks give a geometric domination of the episode’s intervention count. From its moments, I derived the simultaneous-error ingredient
\[
\boxed{
|\widehat r_j-r_j|
\le
\Lambda
\left(
2\sqrt{\frac{t}{n}}+\frac{t}{n}
\right)
}
\]
for \(n\) independent episodes, with the usual exponentially small failure probability in \(t\).
The bound does not grow with the numerical intervention strength. Expected controlled-event work per episode is at most \(\Lambda/\rho\).

Endpoint observations at the controlled source	0
Tagged intervention events	50,736

The simulator used integer competing-clock races, with at most 27-bit race integers in this test. It did not perform \(10^8\) control-event steps.
This establishes a finite operational example of the changed measurement statistic. It does not establish that event counting itself is historically new or optimal among all specialised estimators.
5. Strong controls, uncertainty, and the global certificate
Strong rates can be capped with an explicit error
Under the reset, distinct-source, and ejection assumptions, let
\[
v_j=\min(u_j,K).
\]
I derived
\[
\boxed{
|J(u)-J(v)|
\le
\frac{\Lambda(1+\Lambda/\rho)}{K}.
}
\]
The proof uses a stationary Poisson equation. Coupling at the next common reset bounds the oscillation of its solution by \(1/\rho\). At a capped source, the local Poisson equation bounds the output consequence of an additional transition. Summing against actual intervention flux closes the estimate because total added flux is at most \(\Lambda\).
This allows arbitrarily large supplied rate bounds to be replaced by a finite cap, with the approximation charged explicitly.
It does not remove dependence on reset time, background activity, or the cost of informative experiments.
Noisy measurements yield an outer optimisation problem
Suppose all endpoint means have error at most \(\eta\), all measured fluxes have error at most \(\eta_r\), and
\[
r_j^-=\widehat r_j-\eta_r>0.
\]
With \(A=\sum_j\alpha_j\), every true achievable design lies in the LP defined by
\[
\alpha\ge0,\qquad 0\le z\le1,
\]
\[
\left|
z_i-\widehat z_i^0-\widehat F_{i\cdot}\alpha
\right|
\le \eta(1+2A),
\]
\[
r_j^-\alpha_j\le K_jz_j,
\qquad
\sum_jr_j^-\alpha_j\le\Lambda.
\]
Its optimistic objective is
\[
\widehat w^0+\widehat d^T\alpha+\eta(1+2A).
\]
This supplies a global upper bound. A proposed control is tested on fresh episodes to obtain an achieved-output lower bound. Their difference is the regret certificate.
If a calibration flux cannot be resolved above zero, the implementation stops rather than inventing a usable denominator.
