## 1. Definitions and the precise result

All vector spaces are complex, except spaces of Hermitian matrices, viewed as real Euclidean spaces with inner product `Tr(XY)`. Put `D=d^2`. On `C^d tensor C^d`, let `Gamma=id tensor transpose`, in one fixed product basis. Write `||.||_F`, `||.||_op`, and `||.||_1` for Frobenius, operator, and trace norms.

Let `S_k` be the compact convex set of trace-one positive matrices with Schmidt number at most `k`. Define

\[
\mathcal D_k=\operatorname{conv}(S_k\cup S_k^\Gamma).
\]

Equivalently, `X in D_k` exactly when

\[
X=A+B^\Gamma,\qquad A,B\in\mathsf{SN}_k^+,
\qquad \operatorname{Tr}A+\operatorname{Tr}B=1.
\]

Some members of `D_k` are not positive. The distance below is a geometric trace-norm distance to this entire compact set, and therefore also bounds distance to its positive part. The corresponding map cone is

\[
\mathcal{DSP}_k=\mathcal{SP}_k+\top\circ\mathcal{SP}_k,
\]

where `SP_k` consists of completely positive maps admitting Kraus matrices of rank at most `k`. Its Choi matrices are precisely the unnormalized cone generated by `D_k`.

**Theorem.** For every integer `d >= 2^24` and every integer

\[
1\le k\le \lfloor d/2^{24}\rfloor,
\]

there is a strictly PPT density matrix `rho` whose two marginals are `I/d`, such that

\[
\inf_{X\in\mathcal D_k}\frac12\|\rho-X\|_1\ge\frac1{576}.
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently there is a trace-preserving unital PPT channel `Phi:M_d -> M_d` outside `DSP_k`. There is also an indecomposable map which is simultaneously `k`-positive and `k`-copositive, with strict margins on all unit vectors of Schmidt rank at most `k`. The channel can be chosen so that `Phi^2` is entanglement breaking.

The coefficient `2^-24` is deliberately conservative. It is not an optimal slope and requires an enormous dimension even for `k=2`. No explicit matrix at that dimension has been computed.

If

\[
k_*(d)=\min\{k:\mathcal{PPT}(d,d)\subseteq\mathcal{DSP}_k(d,d)\},
\]

then

\[
\lfloor d/2^{24}\rfloor+1\le k_*(d)\le d
\tag{2}
\]

for the stated large dimensions. Thus `k_*(d)=Theta(d)`. The lower bound already holds when the input class is restricted to unital trace-preserving PPT channels. In particular no fixed `k`, including `2` or `3`, covers all dimensions. The `d=4, k=2` instance remains undecided here.

## 2. The Gaussian space and exact channel normalization

Use the subspace

\[
\mathcal H_0=\{G=G^*:\operatorname{Tr}_A G=\operatorname{Tr}_B G=0\}.
\]

Products of traceless Hermitian basis matrices on the two factors give an orthonormal basis. Hence its real dimension is

\[
m=(d^2-1)^2=(D-1)^2.
\]

Let `G` be a standard real Gaussian in `H_0`: its coefficients in that orthonormal basis are independent `N(0,1)`. Equivalently project a standard GUE matrix `H` orthogonally using

\[
P_0(H)=H-\frac{\operatorname{Tr}_B H\otimes I}{d}
       -\frac{I\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_A H}{d}
       +\frac{\operatorname{Tr}H}{d^2}I.
\tag{3}
\]

For every Hermitian `Z`,

\[
\operatorname{Var}\operatorname{Tr}(GZ)
=\|P_0(Z)\|_F^2\le\|Z\|_F^2.
\tag{4}
\]

Partial transpose is a real Hilbert-space isometry, fixes the identity, is self-adjoint, and preserves `H_0`. In particular `G^Gamma` has the same distribution as `G`; independence is neither true nor needed.

Define

\[
\rho=\frac1{d^2}\left(I+\frac{G}{9d}\right),\qquad
C_\Phi=d\rho=\frac Id+\frac{G}{9d^2}.
\tag{5}
\]

The two partial traces of `C_Phi` are exactly `I`. If `G` and `G^Gamma` have operator norm at most `8d`, both `rho` and its partial transpose have smallest eigenvalue at least `1/(9d^2)`. Equation (5) is therefore the Choi matrix of a strictly PPT, unital, trace-preserving channel. No post hoc filtering or Sinkhorn theorem is required.

## 3. A covering bound for low Schmidt rank vectors

Let `T_k` be the unit vectors of Schmidt rank at most `k`. By vectorization, these are `d` by `d` matrices of rank at most `k` and Frobenius norm one. For `0 < epsilon <= 1`, there is an `epsilon`-net with

\[
N(T_k,\epsilon)\le(1+6/\epsilon)^{r}
\le(7/\epsilon)^r,
\qquad r=(4d+1)k.
\tag{6}
\]

Here is a direct proof, including the dependence on `k`. Write a singular value decomposition `X=U diag(s) V^*`, padding zero singular values when necessary. Both `U,V` have orthonormal columns, operator norm one, and `s` has Euclidean norm one. A maximal `delta`-separated set in each Stiefel manifold, for the ambient operator norm, has at most `(1+2/delta)^(2dk)` points. Indeed disjoint ambient norm-balls of radius `delta/2` lie in the radius `1+delta/2` ball; compare their real `2dk`-dimensional volumes. The centers stay in the manifold. The same argument gives `(1+2/delta)^k` centers for the singular-value vectors, with centers in their nonnegative unit sphere.

The Frobenius error in replacing `U`, `s`, and `V` is at most `3delta`: use `||AB||_F <= ||A||_op ||B||_F` for the two Stiefel replacements. The resulting centers still have norm one and rank at most `k`. Set `delta=epsilon/3`. Multiplying the three counts proves (6). There is no hidden `log(k)` factor in this argument.

## 4. Direct Gaussian chaining with explicit constants

For any centered Hermitian Gaussian satisfying (4), define

\[
H_k(G)=\sup_{v\in T_k}|\langle v,Gv\rangle|.
\]

**Lemma.** For all `u >= 0`,

\[
\Pr\{H_k(G)>40\sqrt r+8\sqrt u\}\le e^{-u}.
\tag{7}
\]

Put `X_v=Tr(G vv^*)`. Its variance is at most one. Moreover

\[
\mathbb E(X_v-X_w)^2
\le\|vv^*-ww^*\|_F^2
=2(1-|\langle v,w\rangle|^2)
\le2\|v-w\|^2.
\tag{8}
\]

Take nets `T_j` of radius `epsilon_j=2^-j`, for `j=0,1,...`, with the counts in (6). Approximate each `v` by `pi_j(v) in T_j`. Consecutive approximants are at distance at most `3epsilon_j`.

Apply the one-dimensional Gaussian tail `Pr(|Z|>a) <= 2 exp[-a^2/(2 Var Z)]`. At level zero use

\[
a_0=\sqrt{2(r\log7+u+\log4)}.
\]

The union-bound failure probability is at most `e^-u/2`. At each level `j >= 1`, consider only pairs of net points whose distance is at most `3epsilon_j`; their count is bounded by `|T_j||T_(j-1)|`. Use

\[
a_j=6\,2^{-j}
\sqrt{r[2\log7+(2j-1)\log2]+u+(j+2)\log2}.
\]

The union-bound failure probability is at most `2^(-j-1)e^-u`. These sum to another `e^-u/2`. On the complementary event, continuity in this finite-dimensional space and the telescoping chain give

\[
H_k(G)\le a_0+\sum_{j\ge1}a_j.
\]

For `r >= 1`, bound the expression inside the square root of `a_j` by `r(5+3j)+u`. Use `sum_(j>=1) 2^-j=1`, `sum_(j>=1) j2^-j=2`, and `sqrt(j) <= j`. Then

\[
a_0+\sum_{j\ge1}a_j
\le[3+6(\sqrt5+2\sqrt3)]\sqrt r
    +(\sqrt2+6)\sqrt u
<40\sqrt r+8\sqrt u.
\]

This proves (7) without assuming any independence between different vectors, scales, or partial transposes.

With `u=r`, apply the lemma to `G` and `G^Gamma` and take a union bound. With probability at least `1-2e^-r`,

\[
H_k(G),H_k(G^\Gamma)\le48\sqrt r\le\frac d{36}.
\tag{9}
\]

The last inequality holds for `k <= d/2^24`, since `r <= 5dk` and the exact integer inequality

\[
5\cdot48^2\cdot36^2=14929920<2^{24}
\]

is sufficient.

## 5. Operator norm and energy estimates

For a Hermitian matrix on `C^D`, a `1/4`-net of the complex unit sphere has at most `9^(2D)` points and

\[
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le2\max_{v\text{ in the net}}|\langle v,Gv\rangle|.
\]

Each quadratic form has variance at most one. Thus

\[
\Pr\{\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}>8d\}
\le2\exp[(2\log9-8)d^2]\le2e^{-3d^2}.
\tag{10}
\]

The same estimate holds for `G^Gamma`. The joint failure bound is `4e^(-3d^2)`.

Let `S=Tr(G^2)`. It is chi-squared with `m=(D-1)^2` degrees of freedom. For `d >= 4`, `D >= 16` and `m >= (225/256)D^2`. Its elementary Gaussian Laplace transform gives

\[
\Pr\{S<D^2/2\}
\le e^{D^2/4}\,2^{-m/2}\le e^{-D^2/32}.
\tag{11}
\]

For the last inequality it suffices to use `log2 >= 2/3`; then the exponent is at most `-(11/256)D^2`, which is stronger. Combining (9)--(11), the event

\[
H_k(G),H_k(G^\Gamma)\le d/36,\quad
\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}},\|G^\Gamma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le8d,
\quad S\ge d^4/2
\tag{12}
\]

has probability at least

\[
1-2e^{-r}-4e^{-3d^2}-e^{-d^4/32}>0
\]

in the theorem's range.

## 6. Separation of the entire decomposition cone

For every `X in D_k`, linearity, self-adjointness of partial transpose, and the definition of Schmidt number imply

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(GX)
\le\max(H_k(G),H_k(G^\Gamma))\le d/36.
\tag{13}
\]

This controls arbitrary mixtures and arbitrary numbers of terms; it does not assume that `X` or the partial-transposed summand is positive. In contrast the random state has

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(G\rho)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}G^2}{9d^3}
\ge d/18.
\tag{14}
\]

Hölder's trace inequality and `||G||_op <= 8d` now give

\[
\|\rho-X\|_1\ge
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[G(\rho-X)]}{\|G\|_{\mathrm{op}}}
\ge\frac1{288}.
\]

This proves (1). The Choi scaling in (5) proves the channel assertion.

A strict dual witness is

\[
W=I-\frac{30}{d}G.
\tag{15}
\]

For every unit vector of Schmidt rank at most `k`, both `W` and `W^Gamma` have expectation at least `1-30/36=1/6`. These are exactly the Choi block-positivity conditions for a map to be `k`-positive and `k`-copositive. Meanwhile

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(W\rho)\le1-30/18=-2/3.
\]

If `W=P+Q^Gamma` for positive `P,Q`, its pairing with every PPT state would be nonnegative. It is therefore indecomposable. This proves the asserted dual obstruction directly; Proposition 5.12 in the 2026 paper is consistent with it but is not needed as a premise.

All these inequalities have strict margins. Rational Hermitian matrices in `H_0` are dense, because (3) has rational coefficients. Small rational approximations preserve positivity, block-positivity, negative pairing, and the square certificate below. Exact rational examples therefore exist, although no particular large example is materialized in this packet.
