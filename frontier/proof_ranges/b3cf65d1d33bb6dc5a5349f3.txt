## 4. The finite source–sink module

### Ports and independent reservoirs

Tile the lattice by L by L square blocks, with one fixed port per block. The remaining sites of a block are its reservoir. A gate g(omega) in {0,1} depends only on reservoir occupations, never the port. Different blocks' gates are therefore independent under mu_p.

For adjacent blocks u and v, let Q be their union. A module is a collection H_b(zeta) of currents on bonds contained in Q, satisfying:

- antisymmetry under its bond exchange;
- legality even when all sites outside Q are occupied;
- the exact configuration-space source identity

\[
\sum_{b\subset Q}H_b(\zeta)
=(\zeta_{s_u}-\zeta_{s_v})g(\omega_u)g(\omega_v).
\tag{4}
\]

The sum in (4) is the divergence of a signed current on the finite configuration graph: H_b(zeta) is its outgoing value from configuration zeta. It is not a spatial divergence. Crucially, it is **not required to vanish**.

Legality with occupied exterior is sufficient for legality in any exterior configuration in KA: adding exterior vacancies cannot disable a permitted move. Equation (4) also allows H to pass through states whose gates are bad; its divergence, rather than its support, is restricted to good endpoint reservoirs.

### The executed 3 by 3 gate

Take L=3 and the center as port. A reservoir is good exactly when each of the following five sets contains at least one vacancy:

- each of the four outer sides of the 3 by 3 block;
- the four nearest neighbors of its center.

These five sets do not contain the port. Index the eight reservoir sites row by row, omitting the center. The corresponding forbidden fully occupied bit masks are 7, 41, 90, 148 and 224. Thus the gate can be evaluated by five bit operations; no learned classifier or fitted equilibrium law is involved.

Exactly 154 of the 256 reservoir patterns are good. Their counts by particle number are

\[
(1,8,28,52,49,16,0,0,0).
\]

Consequently their exact probability is

\[
\gamma(p)=q^8+8pq^7+28p^2q^6+52p^3q^5+49p^4q^4+16p^5q^3,
\quad b(p)=1-\gamma(p). \tag{5}
\]

The gate is invariant under square rotations/reflections and decreases when vacancies are filled. Its validity is not inferred from these properties: the actual source identity (4) is verified on every finite configuration.

### Constructing and checking H

The horizontal module is a 6 by 3 rectangle with 18 sites, ports at zero-based coordinates (1,1) and (4,1). Its graph contains 262,144 configurations and 998,400 unoriented legal configuration edges. There are 47,432 nonzero source entries: two port orientations for each of 154 squared reservoir pairs.

For a microscopic bond within one block, define its assembly-overlap weight w_b=4. For a bond crossing the middle interface between the blocks, put w_b=1. These are exactly the numbers of pair modules that can cover the bond in a fixed tiling.

A sparse electrical-flow solve proposes a module minimizing sum w_b H_b^2 subject to its sources. The configuration graph conserves particle number. Product-equilibrium weights are constant within each fixed-particle-number sector, so the same proposed flow can be evaluated at every density.

Floating-point optimization is only a proposal. Each edge current is rounded to denominator 2^40. Its integer divergence residual is then routed through a deterministic spanning forest, making (4) exact. All component-root residuals must be zero; otherwise construction fails.

The independent checker `verify_module.py` does not use the graph-component labels or the numerical optimizer. It checks every stored transition against the original hard KA rule with occupied exterior, rejects duplicate edges, checks all 262,144 source equations using exact integer arithmetic, recomputes both energy polynomials with Python integers, and verifies the transport normalization polynomial. A proved int64 bound is checked before vectorized incidence accumulation; energy sums use arbitrary precision.

The vertical module is the coordinate transpose of the horizontal module. Square symmetry of the gate makes its source identity and energy identical.

Define the exact module cost

\[
C(p)=\frac{1}{2\chi\gamma(p)^2}
\mathbb E_{p,Q}\sum_{b\subset Q} w_b H_b^2.
\tag{6}
\]

If F_e is the stored integer current on an unoriented configuration edge e, n(e) its particle count, d_0=2^40 and

\[
E_n=\sum_{e:n(e)=n} w_e F_e^2,
\]

then

\[
C(p)=\frac{\sum_{n=0}^{18}E_n p^nq^{18-n}}
{d_0^2\chi\gamma(p)^2}. \tag{7}
\]

The factor two from the two configuration-edge orientations cancels that in (6). All 19 integers E_n are supplied and independently recomputed. At p=0.30, C is approximately 4.319126590793206. This decimal is descriptive; the lower-bound evaluation uses (7) as an exact rational number.
