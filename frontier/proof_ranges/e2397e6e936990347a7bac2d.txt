# Independent application check: hyperbolic property (T) envelope

Frozen before reading the algebra lead's application derivation. The assignment supplied the target interface L=G*P, F=P, Q=G and U a finite generating set of G. I read only the primary theorem statement, its definition of smallness and its stated provenance in the downloaded Minasyan source.

## Exact conditional statement

Let G be a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group. Let P be any torsion-free non-elementary word-hyperbolic group with property (T). Assuming Minasyan's published quotient theorem, there exists a torsion-free non-elementary word-hyperbolic group K with property (T), and an injective homomorphism G -> K whose image is quasiconvex. If G is nonsofic, then K is nonsofic. This does not validate any proposed G.

## Premises and independent proof

1. Put L=G*P with a finite generating set formed from generators of the factors. Free products of hyperbolic groups are hyperbolic; free products of torsion-free groups are torsion-free. Both free factors are quasiconvex (indeed their Cayley subgraphs are convex for the union generating set). F=P is non-elementary.

2. Minasyan's smallness condition is that F is not contained in A Q^{-1} Q B for ANY finite A,B subsets of L. Here Q=G is a subgroup, so Q^{-1}Q=G. For any a,b in L, P intersects aGb in at most one element. Indeed, if h1=a g1 b and h2=a g2 b belong to P, then h1 h2^{-1}=a(g1 g2^{-1})a^{-1} belongs to P intersect aGa^{-1}. In the Bass-Serre tree of G*P, distinct factor vertices have stabilizers P and aGa^{-1}; their intersection fixes the path between them, whose edge stabilizers are trivial. Thus the intersection is trivial and h1=h2. The two vertices are of different factor type and cannot coincide. A finite union of aGb therefore meets P in finitely many points and cannot cover infinite P. This establishes the EXACT smallness condition, rather than replacing it by a weaker informal almost-disjointness condition.

3. Apply Minasyan Theorem 2.6 to L,F,Q and U={g1,...,gm}, where the gi generate G. It supplies wi in P and a quotient eta:L->K by the normal closure of gi wi. Its conclusions give torsion-free non-elementary hyperbolicity, injection on all of Q, and quasiconvexity of eta(R) for each quasiconvex R contained in Q. Taking R=G gives the required quasiconvex embedding.

4. The quotient relations say eta(gi)=eta(wi)^{-1}, so eta(G) is contained in eta(P). Since L is generated by G and P, eta(P)=K. Property (T) passes to quotients, proving the claimed property of K. Soficity passes to subgroups; hence nonsofic G embedded in K forces K nonsofic. No quotient-closure assertion for soficity is used.

No obstruction was found to this application. Centralizer and conjugacy preservation are extra source conclusions, not needed for the argument. A theorem statement, its imported facts and this corollary were checked; the entire small-cancellation theorem was not reconstructed or formally verified.

## Significance and cost boundary

The preservation of hyperbolicity and the entire embedded G is a stronger interface than merely passing to a property-(T) quotient that preserves one witness. But the transfer is a routine corollary of a published theorem, and it retains the complete unverified nonsoficity/construction premise. No explicit K, selected words wi, finite presentation or useful word-length bound has been acquired. It supplies no inherited CAT(-1), two-dimensional complex, cubulation or cohomological-dimension claim.

The 2021 source itself says Theorem 2.6 is essentially a special case of Minasyan, On Residualizing Homomorphisms Preserving Quasiconvexity (2005), Theorem 1. That older theorem is described as permitting surjectivity on F and injection on Q. Accordingly, this is established-method transfer, not a new quotient mechanism. Exact historical use for nonsofic candidates remains UNKNOWN. The general corollary should not be described as an independent new nonsofic construction.
