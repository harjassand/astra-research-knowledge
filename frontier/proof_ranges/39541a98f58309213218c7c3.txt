# Fresh local Pauli randomization supplies the reset channel in law

14 October 2026. Internal compatible-interface derivation. This is established
Pauli depolarization mathematics applied to the source echo operation, not a
novel physical primitive. Historical priority of the complete protocol remains
UNKNOWN. No physical gate/error budget has been acquired.

The source assumes that before every trial the collar is replaced by I_S/d,
decoupling it from an arbitrary retained outside state. For ell spin qubits,
d=2^ell, this channel can also be implemented by fresh independent local Pauli
gates with their outcomes pooled, rather than by dissipative reset of each spin.

For ANY correlated rho_SO, including an entangled conditional state left by the
preceding trial, the exact identity is

    (1/4^ell) sum_(P in {I,X,Y,Z}^ell)
        (P⊗I)rho_SO(P†⊗I)
        = I_S/d ⊗ Tr_S rho_SO.                            (1)

Proof: expand rho_SO=(1/d)sum_Q Q⊗T_Q in the Pauli operator basis. Averaging
PQP† preserves Q=I and cancels every nonidentity Pauli by equal commuting and
anticommuting signs. The surviving coefficient T_I is Tr_S rho_SO. QED.

For trial j let F_(j-1) contain all past outcomes, keys and controller records.
Draw the CURRENT key P_j independently of the host and this history. Apply it
locally, then perform the same predeclared coherent-branch echo and readout for
every key. Conditional on F_(j-1), but averaging over P_j, (1) supplies exactly
the producer reset state. Thus the per-trial probability lower bound, including
the sharper spectral-weight version, holds for

    Pr(Y_j=1 | F_(j-1)).

Arbitrary memory between trials remains allowed. The key may be logged after
the trial, and even used for later adaptive decisions, provided all assumptions
and candidate/statistical budgets hold. The proof does not condition on the
current key or guarantee the failure bound in each individual key branch.
Postselecting outcomes by the current key, changing the echo based on that key,
or retaining one fixed key through a block is not covered by this argument.

Resources are 2ell fresh uniform random bits and at most ell chosen single-spin
Pauli gates per trial (a hardware Y decomposition can use more physical pulses).
The label still requires known preparation/readout. In a fixed-charge spin
encoding these gates preserve each site's charge, as assumed by the source;
they need not preserve magnetization or arbitrary canonical fermion sectors.
Actual pulse durations, controller memory/randomness and eventual key erasure
remain in total time/energy accounting. This is an operational replacement of
the collar channel, not a claim of free thermodynamic entropy disposal.

Ideal instantaneous local gates make (1) exact. During finite pulses the host
can evolve and become correlated with the selected control; gate dependence,
crosstalk, timing and readout can defeat the ideal channel. The experimentally
realized averaged preparation map must therefore have an independently
calibrated, state-uniform error bound folded into gamma. This observation
reduces one proposed engineering burden; it does not establish source-level
gamma on a device or matched resource superiority.

Primary prior context: Mosca, Tapp and de Wolf, *Private Quantum Channels and
the Cost of Randomizing Quantum Information*, arXiv:quant-ph/0003101v2 (2000),
shows the 2ell-bit Pauli randomization construction and its entropy cost:
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0003101 . Full HTML Sections1 and4 were inspected;
