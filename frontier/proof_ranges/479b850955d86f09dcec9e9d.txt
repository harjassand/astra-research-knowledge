The observation ladder extends to a broader result:
Calibrated interventions can recover a positive matrix measuring whether the observed variables constitute an autonomous state for future control. That matrix can be inaccessible even from an infinite passive record. When it is small, it bounds the error of an entire reduced-model trajectory under every compatible feedback policy over a specified horizon.
The construction does not require a ladder, nilpotence, uniform stationary probabilities, or knowledge of the number of hidden states.
There is also a precise limitation: two interventions can impose identical thermodynamic rate ratios while having completely different information content. One permits the recovery above; the other is only an observable change of clock and cannot reveal information absent from passive observation.
The candidate advance is the complete measurement-to-controlled-prediction theorem, including finite-amplitude acquisition, statistical costs, and the access obstruction. Historical priority remains unresolved. Response-based tests of Markovian models and dependence on the kinetic implementation of a perturbation are established prior work, not discoveries claimed here. [APS Journals](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevX.13.021034)
An observed process can be exactly Markovian in stationarity without its observed state being sufficient for predicting interventions.
These are different assertions:
\[ \text{“The passive record follows a Markov law”} \]
and
\[ \text{“The observed state determines future dynamics under control.”} \]
The theorem addresses the second assertion. It does not misclassify a stationary Markov signal as non-Markov merely because its hidden realization responds differently to intervention.
Let \(Q\) be the generator of an arbitrary finite irreducible continuous-time Markov chain, with stationary distribution \(\pi\). Observe only a label
\[ Y_t=\ell(X_t)\in\{1,\ldots,k\}. \]
The microscopic state count may be unknown. Write
\[ e_a(i)=\mathbf1_{\{\ell(i)=a\}},\qquad \mu_a=\Pr_\pi(Y=a),\qquad M=\operatorname{diag}(\mu). \]
The stationary two-time probability matrix is
\[ C_{ab}(t)=\mathbb E_\pi[e_a(X_0)e_b(X_t)]. \]
Its first derivative determines the stationary averaged generator
\[ \boxed{L=M^{-1}C'(0).} \tag{1} \]
This is a valid \(k\)-state generator. But averaging alone does not establish that \(L\) predicts the observed process under intervention.
The missing quantity is the variation of microscopic aggregate rates within each observed label.
Let \(P\) denote conditional expectation onto functions of the observed label, using the inner product
\[ \langle f,g\rangle_\pi=\sum_i\pi_i f_i g_i. \]
For each label define
\[ b_a=(I-P)Qe_a. \]
This is the part of the instantaneous evolution of observable \(e_a\) that leaves the observed function space. Define its Gram matrix
\[ \boxed{\Gamma_{ab}=\langle b_a,b_b\rangle_\pi.} \tag{2} \]
Consequently, \(\Gamma\succeq0\).
The Gram matrix itself is not a new mathematical object. The operational result is that it can be recovered without observing the \(b_a\), the hidden states, or \(Q\).
A pulse acting on observed label \(a\) must change microscopic rates according to
\[ q_{ij}^{h,a} = q_{ij}\exp\!\left[\frac h2(e_a(j)-e_a(i))\right], \qquad i\ne j, \tag{3} \]
with the diagonal adjusted to preserve row sums.
This is a specific kinetic rule: it holds the symmetric rate prefactors fixed. It is not implied by knowing the energy difference alone.
Start each pulse from the unperturbed stationary distribution and measure
\[ \chi_{ab}(t) = \left. \frac{\partial}{\partial h} \mathbb E_h[e_b(X_t)] \right|_{h=0}. \]
With time derivatives evaluated at zero,
\[ \boxed{ \Gamma = -2\chi''(0)-C''(0) -C'(0)^\top M^{-1}C'(0). } \tag{4} \]
Thus a combination of passive correlations and controlled responses recovers a positive hidden-rate-variation matrix.
Let \(Q^*\) be the stationary adjoint and \(S=(Q+Q^*)/2\). Differentiating the rates in (3) gives the finite-state response identity
\[ \chi_{ab}(t) = -\int_0^t \langle Se_a,e^{sQ}e_b\rangle_\pi\,ds. \]
This is the established nonequilibrium response mechanism; the entropic and kinetic contributions to response have substantial prior literature. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.5306)
Therefore,
\[ \chi_{ab}''(0)=-\langle Se_a,Qe_b\rangle_\pi, \]
whereas
\[ C_{ab}''(0) =\langle Q^*e_a,Qe_b\rangle_\pi. \]
Subtracting gives
\[ -2\chi_{ab}''(0)-C_{ab}''(0) = \langle Qe_a,Qe_b\rangle_\pi. \]
The last term of (4) is precisely the Gram matrix of \(PQe_a\). Orthogonal decomposition then proves (4). ∎
Relative to observed and hidden function spaces, write schematically
\[ Q= \begin{pmatrix} L & C_{\mathrm{hid}}\\ B_{\mathrm{hid}} & D_{\mathrm{hid}} \end{pmatrix}. \]
Passive two-time derivatives contain
\[ L^2+C_{\mathrm{hid}}B_{\mathrm{hid}}, \]
and higher derivatives contain directed return products such as
\[ C_{\mathrm{hid}}D_{\mathrm{hid}}^jB_{\mathrm{hid}}. \]
These can vanish despite a substantial \(B_{\mathrm{hid}}\). The earlier ladder delayed such returns.
Response completion instead acquires
\[ B_{\mathrm{hid}}^*B_{\mathrm{hid}}. \]
A directed return can cancel or be absent. A squared norm cannot vanish unless the outward coupling itself vanishes.
That is the mechanism that survives beyond the ladder.
Equation (4) appears to require arbitrarily weak fields. That requirement can be removed.
