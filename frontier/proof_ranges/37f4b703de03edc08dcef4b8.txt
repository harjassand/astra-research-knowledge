FINITE MODERATE-FREQUENCY CONSTRUCTION FOR OSCILLATORS

Consider explicitly the different model
  qddot+gamma*qdot+Kq+diag(alpha)q^3=e1*u, y=q1,
with known scalar gamma>0, mass I, K symmetric SPD, n known. Its resolvent
is q(s)=(s^2+gamma*s+K)^-1e1 and the same cubic formula holds. The port
high-pair term starts at R^-4 and hidden terms at R^-8. The same A is
obtained by the order-eight port-subtracted limit. Actually measuring this
limit is unattractive: raw measurement error is amplified by Omega^8.

Precise conditional algorithmic claim: given n, upper/lower bounds m,M,
alpha_max, a_min, delta, b and the known gamma satisfying the conditions
below, every system in this class with eigenvalue gap >=delta, overlaps
>=a_min and hidden beta separation >=b (including zero) can be reconstructed
to any prescribed epsilon, up to hidden signed permutation, with probability
>=1-zeta under the stated independent noise model. The algorithm receives
real scalar drive/readout access, not K, eigenvectors, Lambda, a or H3.
It acquires the latter modal quantities and cubic samples as described
below. Total duration, samples, drive/frequency precision, arithmetic,
storage and inverse signal tolerances are polynomial in n,M,m^-1,
alpha_max,a_min^-1,delta^-1,b^-1,gamma^-1,epsilon^-1 and log(zeta^-1).
For a fully data-independent sufficient choice, use the KNOWN lower bound
Delta0=min(delta/(2sqrt(M)),sqrt(m)) and impose gamma<=Delta0/(400n).
Choose the calibration sample count from Delta0, not an unknown measured gap.
(Coordinator clarification after final report: actual-gap diagnostics can
use less conservative promises, but are separate from this uniform theorem.)
This is a self-audited candidate theorem, with conservative rather than
practically favorable costs. No physical experiment verifies its premises.

Instead, fix low imaginary s,t and c=(s-t)/2. As a rational function of R,
F_R has the known 4n poles
  z_l=p_j,+-c, p_j,--c, c-p_j,+, c-p_j,-,
  p_j,+/-=-gamma/2 +/- i*sqrt(lambda_j-gamma^2/4).
They are distinct for distinct lambda and positive gamma. Therefore
  F_R=sum_l r_l/(R-z_l),
and its R^-m coefficient is c_m=sum_l r_l*z_l^(m-1).
Choose 4n finite imaginary R_j=i*nu_j, form
  C_jl=1/(i*nu_j-z_l), C*r=F.
No tensor is estimated. Define the known scalar moments
  m1=sum ai^2 lambda_i, m2=sum ai^2 lambda_i^2,
  d=2c+gamma, e=c^2+gamma*c,
  p8=d^4-4d^2(e+m1)+3e^2+6m1*e+m1^2+2m2.
This p8 is the R^-8 coefficient of q1(c+R)q1(c-R). Exactly,
  c4=alpha1*q1(s)q1(t),
  c8=p8*c4+B(s,t),
  B(s,t)=[z^7-p8*z^3]^T C^-1 F.
Thus unknown alpha1 cancels linearly. This is the explicit desired-target
functional suggested by the coordinator, with O(n) rather than quartic-
dimensional interpolation per low-frequency pair. It uses 4n^3 complex
H3 values before symmetry savings; all input frequencies are O(sqrt(M)).

CONDITIONING PROOF FOR FINITE RESIDUE RECOVERY

Let m<=lambda_i<=M, gamma<=sqrt(m), and let Delta be the minimum spacing
among imaginary parts of the 2n single-oscillator poles. Thus Delta is at
least min(delta/(2sqrt(M)),sqrt(m)), where delta is the eigenvalue gap.
For this paragraph assume Delta>=400*n*gamma. Cluster the imaginary pole
centers whenever adjacent centers differ by less than h=100*n*gamma.
Any cluster has at most two poles, because same-side centers are Delta-
separated. A two-pole cluster necessarily contains one left and one right
pole, whose real parts are -gamma/2 and +gamma/2.

For a singleton sample its imaginary center. For a pair with imaginary
separation >=gamma, sample both centers. Its scaled Cauchy block has
diagonal entries +/-2 and off-diagonal magnitudes <=1/sqrt(1.25), hence
minimum singular value >1.1. For a closer pair sample the mean center
plus and minus gamma/2. Direct 2x2 Cauchy determinant evaluation gives
|det(gamma*C_block)|>=0.64 and norm<=4, so sigma_min>=0.16. A singleton
has singular value 2. Entries outside these blocks have magnitude at most
gamma/(h-gamma); their row and column sums are less than .041. Therefore
  sigma_min(gamma*C)>=.1, ||C^-1||2<=10*gamma.
All constants are deliberately conservative. The same construction can
be used at less weak damping, with sigma_min(C) checked from calibrated
linear data; the universal bound above then need not apply.

At low frequencies s_j=i*sqrt(lambda_j), put
  V_ij=ai/(lambda_i-lambda_j+i*gamma*sqrt(lambda_j)).
Scaling rows by 1/ai and columns by i*gamma*sqrt(lambda_j) gives I+E,
with ||E||2 <= (n-1)*gamma*sqrt(M)/delta. If this is <=1/2,
  ||V^-1||2<=2*gamma*sqrt(M)/a_min.
Alternatively |E_ij|<=(2/sqrt(3))*gamma/Delta, since
|lambda_i-lambda_j|=|omega_i-omega_j|*(omega_i+omega_j) and
sqrt(lambda_j)<=2omega_j/sqrt(3). Thus the Delta promise itself gives
||E||2<1/2 even when the displayed delta-based bound is loose. Hence uniform H3
error epsilon_H produces
  ||delta B||2 <= n*L_B*epsilon_H,
  ||delta A||2 <= n*||V^-1||2^2*L_B*epsilon_H,
where L_B=max_(s,t)||C^-T(z^7-p8*z^3)||1. It is directly computable from
linear calibration and bounded by C0*n*gamma*(1+M)^(7/2). Consequently
the target functional has polynomial gain; full quartic inversion is
neither needed nor asserted. The beta gap b (including separation from
zero) converts A error to coordinate/K error by ordinary symmetric-matrix
eigenvector perturbation, with a further polynomial factor O(M sqrt(n)/b).
Since beta>=b and alpha<=alpha_max, the nonzero port couplings obey
k_r^2>=b/alpha_max, so unknown alpha recovery also has polynomial gain.

FROM REAL INPUT/OUTPUT TO AN H3 VALUE: EXPLICIT QUERY INTERFACE

No H3 tensor/kernel oracle is supplied in the physical statement. For any
real frequencies omega1,omega2,omega3, command the single real waveform
  u_theta(t)=eta*sum_(j=1)^3 cos(omega_j*t+theta_j),
with independently programmed phase labels theta_j in {0,pi/2,pi,3pi/2}.
Run 64 experiments to a common known snapshot time T, then average outputs
with weight exp[-i(theta1+theta2+theta3)]/64. Its linear contribution is
zero and cubic contribution is exactly
  (3/4)*eta^3 H3(i*omega1,i*omega2,i*omega3)
  *exp[i*(omega1+omega2+omega3)*T].
At degree three, the selected phase character requires one positive-
phase factor from each programmed label, yielding 6*(eta/2)^3. This remains
valid for equal or conjugate frequencies: the 64 phase tuples command
legal resultant scalar waveforms, even when some tuples are redundant.
The identity uses controlled phase variables, not separation by an
infinite-time temporal Fourier transform. Fifth and higher orders cause
an O(eta^5) error, not zero error. Clock, phase and drive calibration must
be accurate to the resulting polynomial error tolerance.

Here is a conservative explicit small-signal resource bound. The causal
oscillator Green operator has position L-infinity gain
  G<=4/(gamma*sqrt(m)).
For bounded three-tone drive, choose eta with
  eta^2 <= 1/(432*alpha_max*G^3).
A contraction argument on the ball ||q||<=6G*eta gives a unique bounded
small response. The cubic Taylor remainder in position is at most
  23328*alpha_max^2*G^7*eta^5,
so after phase normalization the H3 error is at most
  31104*alpha_max^2*G^7*eta^2.
Choose eta^2 <= epsilon_H/(124416*alpha_max^2*G^7) to allocate at most a
quarter of the H3 error budget here. Variation-of-constants plus the same
small Lipschitz constant gives decay at least gamma/4; a conservative
settling time is
  T >= (4/gamma)*log[256G/(eta^2*epsilon_H)].
Waiting with u=0 for a comparable interval supplies an approximate reset;
hidden state resets are not an uncharged operation.

The displayed transient prefactor follows from the explicit Green matrix:
||G'(t)||<=2exp(-gamma*t/2), so steady velocity gain is <=4/gamma.
Total steady forcing is <=6eta on the contraction ball; hence
||q*||<=6G eta and ||v*||<=24eta/gamma. The homogeneous position mismatch
envelope is <=2||q*||+2||v*||/sqrt(m)<=24G eta. The small nonlinear
Lipschitz constant adds a factor at most two after weighting by
exp(gamma*t/4). This gives48G eta exp(-gamma*t/4), or normalized cubic
error <=64G eta^-2 exp(-gamma*t/4), proving the stated settling bound.

With independent zero-mean sub-Gaussian readout noise of scale sigma, repeat
each phase N times, where
  N = O(sigma^2*log(256*n^3/failure_probability)
        /(eta^6*epsilon_H^2)).
This is a sample bound, not a claim that real instrument noise is always
independent. Bounded adversarial noise instead needs hardware precision
O(eta^3*epsilon_H); averaging does not remove it. Total scalar snapshots
are 64*(4n^3)*N before calibration, duration O(T) per preparation/run,
bounded force <=3eta, and bandwidth O(sqrt(M)). All gains are polynomial
in inverse gaps, inverse damping, inverse overlap and target accuracy.
