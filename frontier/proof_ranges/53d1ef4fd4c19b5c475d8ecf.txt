## Degree, dimension, and gauge

For \(d\) amplitude coordinates and a polynomial energy of degree at most
\(p\), the profile one-form has polynomial coefficients of degree at most
\(p-1\); its curvature is an exact, hence closed, polynomial 2-form with
coefficient degree at most \(p-2\). Pairings with all component moments
\(M_{ij,\beta}=\int_\Sigma a^\beta\,da_i\wedge da_j\),
\(i<j,|\beta|\le p-2\), are a sufficient finite cancellation certificate.
In two amplitude dimensions every polynomial 2-form is closed, so the exact
universal count is
\(\binom{2+p-2}{2}=\binom p2\); degree four gives six.
Equivalently, degree-at-most-three polynomial 1-forms in two variables have
20 coefficients, but the 14-dimensional space of exact forms
\(dG\), \(\deg G\le4\), has zero loop integral; the quotient has dimension 6.

For the abstract class of all polynomial 1-forms, the minimal curvature
space in \(d\) amplitude dimensions has dimension
\[
 d\binom{d+p-1}{d}-\binom{d+p}{d}+1.
\]
Indeed, it is the image of exterior differentiation from polynomial
1-forms of degree at most \(p-1\); the kernel consists of exact 1-forms
\(dG\), where \(G\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(p\), modulo
constants. In dimensions \(d\ge3\), this can be smaller than the raw list of
pair moments because curvature 2-forms obey \(d\Omega=0\). This is an
abstract polynomial one-form count; full attainability by the particular
matrix-profile map is not claimed here. The two-amplitude quartic case is
fully attained by the explicit convex examples above.

Adding an exact gauge \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha+dG\) changes no closed-loop action
and leaves \(d\alpha\) unchanged. Conversely, if another one-form preserves
the action on every closed amplitude loop in \(\mathbb R^2\), its difference
from \(\alpha\) has zero periods and is exact, so the curvature cannot change.
An energy null-Lagrangian that preserves the original Jensen functional
therefore cannot erase this signed obstruction by a coboundary; it can only
redistribute terms in an identity. For the determinant null Lagrangian in
two output dimensions, the induced profile form is
\(\alpha_{\det}(u)[v]=\mathbb E_y\det(v,B_2+u_y)\); integration by parts
shows its curvature is zero directly. Removing a nonzero curvature while
keeping every closed-loop action would require a genuine domination or
positive-remainder argument, not a gauge change.
