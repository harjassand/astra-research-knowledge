Use three harmonic imposed potentials, with DC amplitudes $E_0$ and $g$:
\[
 \Phi_1=-E_0x-\tfrac g2(x^2-y^2),\quad
 \Phi_2=-E_0y-gxy,\quad
 \Phi_3=-E_0z-gxz.
 \tag{T5}
\]
Each satisfies Laplace's equation. In the induced-dipole model at the origin, equal exposure gives
\[
 \overline F_x=\frac{E_0g}{3}\Tr\alpha,\qquad
 \overline F_y=0,\qquad
 \overline F_z=\frac{E_0g}{3}\alpha_{xz}.
 \tag{T6}
\]
The transverse term is real and cannot be suppressed by calling the force isotropic. At position $(x,y,z)$, the same model gives
\[
 \overline a_x=\frac g{3m}
       [(E_0+gx)\Tr\alpha+gz\alpha_{xz}].
 \tag{T7}
\]
Since $|\alpha_{xz}|\le\Tr\alpha/2$, tracked coordinates and a positive denominator yield the conservative bound
\[
 R\le\frac{3\overline a_x}
 {g(E_0+gx-|gz|/2)}.
 \tag{T8}
\]
Equations (T6)--(T8) alone do not remove finite-particle multipoles. At radius $15\,\mu\mathrm m$ and field length $E_0/g=1\,\mathrm{mm}$, the geometric ratio is already $1.5\%$, exceeding the target error budget. The following ideal operation removes that specific approximation.

\begin{techtheorem}[Field-sign projection, exact for finite linear conductors]\label{thm:fieldprojection}
Hold one finite conductor's position, orientation, net charge and electrostatic environment fixed. Assume free-space linear electrostatics and an exactly affine externally imposed field $E_{\rm ext}(x)=e+Gx$, where $G$ is symmetric and trace-free. Let $F(se,tG)$ be its total force for signs $s,t\in\{-1,1\}$. Then
\[
 \frac14\sum_{s,t=\pm1}st\,F(se,tG)=G\alpha e.
 \tag{T9}
\]
For the three fields (T5), averaging the projected $x$ components gives exactly $E_0g\Tr\alpha/3$, without a point-dipole or near-spherical assumption.
\end{techtheorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $q$ and $p$ be the total charge and laboratory dipole of the full charge distribution. Its internal self-force sums to zero. Integrating the affine external field over charge gives exactly $F=qe+Gp$. Linear electrostatics at fixed $q$ gives $p=p_q+\alpha e+B:G$ for some fixed linear gradient response $B$. Translation contributes a term linear in $G$ of the same parity. Hence
\[
 F(se,tG)=sqe+tGp_q+stG\alpha e+G(B:G).
\]
Multiplication by $st$ and summation cancels every term except $G\alpha e$. Direct differentiation of (T5) gives the three scalar diagonal entries in (T6), proving the final assertion.
\end{proof}

This projection is an explicitly signed \emph{measurement}, not a passive positive deflection sorter. Equal four-sign exposure has zero mean desired force; the four responses must be resolved and combined, then a separate actuator must sort the particle. A moving or rotating particle is not the same fixed configuration in all exposures. Finite electrodes change the free-space response and introduce higher field derivatives; finite conductivity, oxide, charging and gas drag add further terms. The theorem does not certify their size. A real apparatus needs a calibrated bound on each residual or an experiment that removes it. Field-sign demodulation is a familiar general technique; no claim that its abstract algebra is new is made.

\begin{techtheorem}[Obstruction to universal positive-force cancellation]\label{thm:positiveforce}
Consider a prescribed positive-exposure ensemble of affine fields $(e_k,G_k)$, with $w_k>0$, in a fixed laboratory frame, or centered on a particle's known center of mass. No such ensemble can both cancel its gradient-squared force for every admitted closed-cavity conductor and retain a nonzero universal $x$-directed trace force.
\end{techtheorem}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to consider neutral spherical conducting shells with one off-center spherical gas cavity, uniform metal density and fixed void fraction $0<\phi<1$. Their exterior static response is that of a solid sphere, $\alpha=aI$, $a>0$. Place each mass center at the origin. Moving the cavity through an open set moves the outer sphere's electrical center through an open set of offsets $d$, without changing $m$ or $a$. Indeed, if the cavity center relative to the outer center is $r_c$, then $d=\phi r_c/(1-\phi)$.

Spherical harmonic separation gives exactly $p=a(e+Gd)$: the imposed quadratic potential about the sphere center contributes no dipole there. Hence $F=aGe+aG^2d$. Cancelling the second term's $x$ component for every allowed $d$ requires $e_x^T(\sum_k w_kG_k^2)=0$. In particular,
\[
 0=e_x^T\Bigl(\sum_k w_kG_k^2\Bigr)e_x
   =\sum_k w_k\lVert G_ke_x\rVert^2.
 \tag{T9a}
\]
Every $G_ke_x$ therefore vanishes. Symmetry of $G_k$ now forces $e_x^TG_k\alpha e_k=0$ for every $\alpha$, so no desired $x$ trace force survives.
\end{proof}

This is a structural obstruction to a specified universal positive-exposure architecture. It does not rule out field centering on a separately acquired electrical center, bounded residuals, shape-dependent calibration, signed metrology or another force mechanism. Center-of-mass tracking alone does not close it.

\subsection{Numerical scale, competing designs, and the hard engineering gate}

For a homogeneous $30\,\mu\mathrm m$ diameter Al sphere with assumed $\rho=2700\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $E_0=10^6\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $g=10^9\,\mathrm{V/m^2}$, (T6) predicts
\[
 a=\frac{3\epsilon_0E_0g}{\rho}=9.84\,\mathrm{m/s^2}.
\]
A one-millisecond positive kick followed by ten milliseconds of drift would produce $103\,\mu\mathrm m$ displacement; $1\%$ void fraction changes it by about $1.04\,\mu\mathrm m$. This is only a force-scale illustration. The signed projection has a different trajectory and photon budget; the positive-kick estimate cannot be used as its achieved precision.

Each such solid particle has mass $3.82\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{kg}$. Processing $1\,\mathrm{kg/h}$ therefore requires about $7.28\times10^6$ individually admitted particles per second. If nonmetal mass, physical-response reduction and density upper margin each cost $0.2\%$, (T4) requires
\[
 R_U/R_{\rm sphere}\le1.00406
 \quad\hbox{to certify }\phi\le1\%.
 \tag{T10}
\]
That leaves roughly $0.4\%$ total headroom for shape and measurement. For ideal prolate spheroids of aspect ratios $1.1$ and $1.2$, the trace-to-mass ratios are respectively $1.00294$ and $1.01091$ times the sphere value. Thus a valid certificate can reject perfectly dense powder. Throughput, accepted mass yield, false-acceptance coverage, energy per accepted kilogram and material compatibility must all be measured together.
