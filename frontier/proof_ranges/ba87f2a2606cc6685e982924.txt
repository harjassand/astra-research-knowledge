## 3. Geometry and code-only variance

Fix a finite union (A\subset\mathbb R) of at most (m) disjoint intervals, length (\ell), and define
\[
H(s)=|A\cap(A+s)|,\qquad \delta(s)=\ell-H(s)=|A\setminus(A+s)|.
\]
Write (Z_A(t)=(-1)^{N(t+A)}). Since (N((t+A)\triangle(t+s+A))) is Poisson with mean (2r\delta(s)),
\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Z_A(t),Z_A(t+s))
=e^{-4r\delta(s)}-e^{-4r\ell}\ge0.
\]
It is zero when (H(s)=0).

For (s>0), project (A) onto the circle (\mathbb R/(s\mathbb Z)). For almost every residue (y\in[0,s)), the fiber (B_y=\{k:y+ks\in A\}\subset\mathbb Z) is finite. The fiber of (A+s) is (B_y+1), and every nonempty finite integer set satisfies \(|B_y\setminus(B_y+1)|\ge1\). Integrating the fiber counts yields
\[
\delta(s)\ge |\pi_s(A)|.
\]
If a component interval has length at least (s), its projection is the whole circle, of measure (s). Otherwise each component projects to an arc whose measure is its length, and the union has measure at least the longest component length, at least (\ell/m). Therefore
\[
\delta(s)\ge\min(s,\ell/m).
\]

If (H(s)>0), then (s\in A-A). For component lengths (l_1,\ldots,l_q), (q\le m), the sum of lengths of the (q^2) interval differences is
\[
\sum_{i,j}(l_i+l_j)=2q\ell\le2m\ell.
\]
The positive half of the symmetric difference set consequently has measure at most (m\ell). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}(Z_A(0),Z_A(s))\,ds
&\le \int_0^{\ell/m}e^{-4rs}\,ds
   +m\ell e^{-4r\ell/m}\\
&\le \frac1{4r}+\frac{m^2}{4er}
=\frac{1+m^2/e}{4r},
\end{aligned}
\]
using (y e^{-y}\le e^{-1}). For the stationary time average, evenness and nonnegativity of the covariance give
\[
\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac1T\int_0^T Z_A(t)\,dt\right)
\le \frac{2}{T}\int_0^\infty\operatorname{Cov}(Z_A(0),Z_A(s))\,ds
\le\frac{1+m^2/e}{2rT}.
\]
If (\ell=0), (Z_A\equiv1), so the variance is zero.

For a fixed code-independent stationary mixture (\mu(dA)), set (q_A=e^{-2r|A|}) and (G(t)=\int Z_A(t)\,\mu(dA)). Then
\[
\frac1T\int_0^T G(t)dt-\int q_A\mu(dA)
=\int\left[\frac1T\int_0^T Z_A(t)dt-q_A\right]\mu(dA).
\]
Jensen's inequality followed by the uniform fixed-(A) bound proves the same code variance bound for this *ensemble conditional-mean signal*. The mixture need not have bounded age span or a finite hidden state-space. This conclusion concerns the variance over the code of the mixture mean; it is not a bound on chemical or detector fluctuations.
