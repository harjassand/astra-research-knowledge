## 2. Reconstructing the actual update

### Contour and thermal information

PR start from the thermal Schwinger–Keldysh path integral: a compact Euclidean field φ_E on τ̄ ∈ [0,β] samples the density matrix, and its endpoints sew to the forward/backward real-time branches (P7, contour construction before Eq. (1)). They then move the real-time endpoint to infinity, argue that finite-time correlations decouple from the endpoint, rotate the forward branch into the upper complex half-plane, and use a new, noncompact imaginary-time coordinate τ for the dynamical branch (P7, contour discussion preceding Eq. (2)). That τ is distinct from the compact thermal τ̄.

The observable is $G^{++}(\omega)=\langle\phi^+(\omega)\phi^+(-\omega)\rangle$, which P7 says can be obtained from the forward branch in equilibrium (P7 Eq. (3)). The initial condition would impose a Fourier-space relation between the noncompact field and φ_E(τ̄0). P7 explicitly writes the relation schematically as Σ_l φ+(ω_l)=φ_E(τ̄0), then argues that its highest-frequency quadratic penalty makes that constraint irrelevant in the continuum limit and that one mode has vanishing weight at infinite spatial volume. On this basis the authors lift the Matsubara/initial constraint from the frequency-local quadratic action S0. They state that temperature then enters solely through the interaction term (P7, paragraphs before Eq. (4)).

This is the decisive scope point: the exact sewn contour motivates the construction, but the implemented frequency-space S0 is *unconstrained*. The infinite-spatial-volume limb of that rationale is absent in the 0+1-dimensional toy model; the temporal-anchor comparison here instead analyzes the update they implement. The paper's practical temperature channel is not a full sampled KMS boundary condition on the noncompact path. It is a local substitution during the interaction drift.

### Drift and single-slice replacement

For φ⁴ theory, P7 Eq. (5) gives the frequency-space Langevin update

\[
\partial_s\phi^+(\omega_l)=-\frac{\delta S_0}{\delta\phi^+(\omega_l)}-
\sum_j\frac{\delta S_E^{\rm int}}{\delta\phi^+(\tau_j)}
\frac{\delta\phi^+(\tau_j)}{\delta\phi^+(\omega_l)}+\eta(\omega_l),
\]

with \({\cal P}(\omega_l)=(2-2\cos(2\pi l/N_\omega))/a^2\) and

\[
S_0=\sum_l\Delta\omega\,\tfrac12[\mathcal P(\omega_l)+m^2]
|\phi^+(\omega_l)|^2.
\]

The source is independently Langevin-updated on the compact lattice, with noise covariance $2\delta_{ij}/a$. At the distinguished initial-condition slice \(\tau_0\), PR replace \(\phi^+(\tau_0)\) by \(\phi_E(\bar\tau_0)\) **when evaluating the local \(\phi^3\) interaction drift** (P7 Eq. (5) and following text). P8 says the same thing directly: the compact and noncompact fields are updated concurrently with independent Gaussian noises, and the value of \(\phi_E(0)\) is imprinted by replacing \(\phi^+(0)\) in the interaction drift. P8 calls this “an additional constraint on the drift” supplied by the thermal density matrix.

For \(0+1\) dimensions there is one such temporal site. With the periodic inverse DFT, and after changing from the paper's frequency coordinates to the real path coordinates, the specified update is the anchored SDE

\[
dX=-[K_aX+g_a(I-P)X^3+g_a P Y^3]ds+\sqrt2\,dW,
\quad K_a=a(-\Delta_a+m^2),\quad g_a=a\lambda/6,
\]

where $P$ selects the initial slice, $Y$ is the compact thermal source, and the source and target noises are independent. In multiple spatial dimensions $P$ selects that time slice at each spatial site. The DFT is periodic on its finite auxiliary grid, so this is a one-slice anchor on a cycle; the paper itself identifies the resulting finite-volume/convolution artifact and says it vanishes as $N_\omega\to\infty$ (P7 around Eqs. (4)–(5)).

### Fourier normalization and what is intensive

P7 does not spell out a finite-box forward/inverse DFT normalization independently of its action equation. Its Eq. (6) permits an action-consistent convention. Let $F_l=N_\omega^{-1/2}\sum_j e^{-2\pi i lj/N_\omega}x_j$ be the unitary DFT and $\Delta\omega=2\pi/(N_\omega a)$. The convention

\[
\phi_l=\sqrt{a/\Delta\omega}\,F_l
=\frac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_j e^{-2\pi i lj/N_\omega}x_j
\]

gives the position-space quadratic action $a\sum_j[(\nabla_a x_j)^2+m^2x_j^2]/2$ exactly as P7's frequency action $\sum_l\Delta\omega[\mathcal P_l+m^2]|\phi_l|^2/2$. Thus the intensive finite-box spectral coefficient is

\[
\Delta\omega\,\langle|\phi_l|^2\rangle
=a\,\langle|F_l|^2\rangle.
\]

An unnormalized mode product contains a box/Dirac-delta factor and must not be compared across box sizes without this measure. A harmless (2\pi) convention factor can be moved between the transform and frequency measure; it cannot change a thermal-to-vacuum ratio or the drift's invariant law. This action-derived normalization does not establish the authors' exact internal code convention, because no code was available.
