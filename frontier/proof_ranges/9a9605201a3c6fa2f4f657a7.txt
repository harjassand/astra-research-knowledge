## Exact statement

Set

\[
h=20,\qquad c=100^{-20}=10^{-40},\qquad r=10^{42},\qquad q=2^{521}-1,
\quad m=q^{20},\quad k=\lfloor q/100\rfloor.
\]

For any $S\subset\mathbb F_q$ of size $k$, there exist distinct marked points $a_i\in S^{20}$, affine lines $\ell_i\ni a_i$, a label set $I$ of size $N$, and $E\subset P=\mathbb F_q^{20}$, with all of the following properties simultaneously:

1. The simple bipartite graph with vertices $P\sqcup I$ and edges $z\sim i\iff z\in\ell_i\setminus\{a_i\}$ has girth at least 14.
2. $N\ge cm/4$, $|E|\le m/(2r)$, and every $z\notin E$ has degree at least $4r^2$.
3. Starting with every mark of $S^{20}$, at most $6q^{13}$ labels are discarded, adding at most $6q^{14}$ points to the exceptional set.
4. Every regular incidence set has a partition into two Cartesian blocks with both dimensions at least $r$.
5. There exist $N\times N$ matrices $H(z)$ over $\mathbb F_q$, each of rank at most one, such that
   \[
   \sum_{z\in\ell_i\setminus\{a_i\}}H(z)=-E_{ii}\qquad(i\in I).
   \]
6. The specified shifted triangle wiring gives immersed $V,W$ links in the punctured incidence graph, simple complete-bipartite components in the $O$ link, and every same-label turn between distinct $x$ germs has at least 14 link edges, or infinite distance.

In particular,

\[
\Delta=Nr/4-2m-r|E|\ge15m/4>0.
\]

All data and the prime are fixed before any permutation-model dimension is considered.

## Prime and numerical inequalities

The exponent 521 is prime by trial division up to 22. Recomputing $s_0=4$, $s_{j+1}=s_j^2-2\pmod q$ for 519 iterations gives $s_{519}=0$. The Lucas–Lehmer criterion proves that $q$ is prime. Its entry is independently listed by [GIMPS](https://www.mersenne.org/primes/).

Only the following generous inequalities are needed:

\[
q>r,\quad q\ge4000,\quad cm\ge4,\quad
q\ge64r^2/c,\quad q^6\ge24r,\quad cq^7\ge24.
\]

They follow already from $q>10^{156}$. The last bound follows since $2^{520}=(2^{10})^{52}>10^{156}$. Every inequality is also checked by exact integer arithmetic in `exact_checks.py`; no floating-point comparison enters a proof gate.

## Independent choices and point degrees

Let $M_0=k^{20}$. Independently for each mark $a\in S^{20}$, choose one of the

\[
J=(q^{20}-1)/(q-1)
\]

projective directions uniformly, and take the line through $a$ in that direction. These random variables are indexed by **marks or labels**, not by the geometric lines that happen to result.

For fixed $z$, a mark $a\ne z$ contributes exactly when its chosen direction is the unique direction of $z-a$, with probability $1/J$. The label at $a=z$, if present, never contributes because its own mark is punctured. Thus the initial degree is exactly binomial with $M_0-\mathbf1_{z\in S^{20}}$ trials, and its mean is

\[
\mu_z=(M_0-\mathbf1_{z\in S^{20}})/J.
\]

Bernoulli's inequality and $q\ge4000$ give

\[
M_0\ge cm(1-100/q)^{20}\ge cm(1-2000/q)\ge cm/2.
\]

Since $cm\ge4$, we have $M_0-1\ge cm/4$. Also $J\le2q^{19}$, so

\[
\mu_z\ge cq/8\ge8r^2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(d_z)\le\mu_z.
\]

Chebyshev yields

\[
\Pr(d_z<4r^2)\le\frac{\mu_z}{(\mu_z-4r^2)^2}
\le\frac4{\mu_z}\le\frac{32}{cq}.
\]

Let $B$ count such low-degree points. Linearity of expectation gives $\mathbb E B\le32m/(cq)$. Degrees at different points are highly dependent; **no independence between those degrees is used**.

## Cycle count including coincident lines

For $2\le s\le6$, a simple incidence cycle of length $2s$ has distinct point vertices $z_1,\ldots,z_s$ and distinct labels $a_1,\ldots,a_s$. There are at most $m^s$ ordered point tuples. The line required of label $a_j$ is forced by distinct consecutive points $z_j,z_{j+1}$. There are at most $q$ choices of a mark on that line, hence at most $q^s$ ordered mark tuples.

For a candidate tuple whose marks are distinct and whose incidences respect puncturing, all $s$ prescribed independent direction variables take their required values with probability exactly $J^{-s}$. Impossible tuples contribute zero. We may bound the number of admissible tuples by $q^s$; we do **not** apply the independence formula to repeated-label tuples. Counting ordered representations only overcounts cycles. Consequently,

\[
\mathbb E C_{2s}\le m^sq^sJ^{-s}\le q^{2s},
\qquad \mathbb E C\le5q^{12},
\]

where $C=\sum_{s=2}^6 C_{2s}$ counts unoriented simple cycles.

If several distinct marks choose the same geometric line, their random direction choices are still different independent variables. This includes 4-cycles coming from two coincident lines with different marks and two unpunctured common points. Parallel lines, the same direction at different labels, and forbidden marked endpoints cause no uncounted case. The bipartite graph is simple because there is at most one edge for each point-label pair, even if geometric lines coincide.

## One simultaneous acceptance event

Accept an outcome when

\[
B\le m/(4r),\qquad C\le q^{13}.
\]

There is no independence assumption between these two events. Markov and a union bound give

\[
\Pr(\text{reject})\le\frac{128r}{cq}+\frac5q
=\frac{128r/c+5}{q}<2\cdot10^{-73}<1/2.
\]

This slightly stronger cycle gate also implies the fixed-parameter source's $C\le m/(24rq)$, because $q^6\ge24r$. It joins the original $6q^{13}$ deletion count to the later fixed-parameter degree budget without an asymptotic limit.

Delete every label on any of those short cycles. At most $6C\le6q^{13}$ labels are deleted. Let $E$ be the union of the original low-degree points and every point of every deleted full line. Then

\[
|E|\le B+6qC\le m/(4r)+6q^{14}\le m/(2r).
\]

Outside $E$, no discarded label was incident, so the surviving degree is unchanged and is at least $4r^2$. Deletion cannot create a cycle; every original short cycle lost all its labels, so the remaining graph has no cycles of length at most 12. Bipartiteness gives girth at least 14.

Finally,

\[
N\ge M_0-6q^{13}\ge cm/2-cm/4=cm/4,
\]

and $cr=100$ gives

\[
\Delta\ge (cr/16-2-1/2)m=15m/4.
\]

Every surviving label still has its original full line with only its **own** mark punctured. The exceptional set changes where triangle relations are imposed; it does not remove its points or incidences from the interpolation sum. Independent repeated trials succeed in fewer than two trials in expectation. A deterministic finite alternative enumerates all $J^{M_0}$ direction assignments and chooses the first acceptable one.

## Cartesian partitions

Let $d\ge4r^2$. Set $t=r+(d\bmod r)\in[r,2r-1]$ and $s=[d-(r+1)t]/r$. Integrality follows from $t\equiv d\pmod r$. Moreover,

\[
s\ge\frac{4r^2-(r+1)(2r-1)}r
=2r-1+1/r\ge r.
\]

Partition the $d$ labels into blocks of sizes $rs$ and $(r+1)t$. Their dimensions may be chosen $(r,s)$ and $(r+1,t)$. Give each block $b$ an arbitrary bijection $i\mapsto(\alpha_b(i),\beta_b(i))\in\mathbb Z/s_b\times\mathbb Z/t_b$. Index blocks separately at distinct points. No independence, randomness, or special ordering of these bijections is necessary.

## Interpolation with all required incidences retained

For each retained mark $a_i=(a_{i1},\ldots,a_{i20})$, define

\[
f_i(X)=\prod_{j=1}^{20}\prod_{a\in S\setminus\{a_{ij}\}}
\frac{X_j-a}{a_{ij}-a}.
\]

The denominators are nonzero. These polynomials isolate their respective marks among the entire original grid, hence also among the retained marks. Set $f=(f_i)_{i\in I}$ and $H(z)=f(z)f(z)^T$. Then $\operatorname{rank}H(z)\le1$, $H(a_i)=E_{ii}$, and every entry restricted to any affine line has degree at most

\[
40(k-1)<q-1.
\]

For a polynomial of degree below $q-1$, its sum over $\mathbb F_q$ is zero: the constant sums to $q=0$, and each positive monomial degree below $q-1$ has zero power sum. Parameterizing a line by a nonzero direction is a bijection from $\mathbb F_q$ to its $q$ points, even when some direction coordinates vanish. Hence

\[
\sum_{z\in\ell_i\setminus\{a_i\}}H(z)
=-H(a_i)=-E_{ii}.
\]

Deleting whole labels does not damage this identity: it restricts the polynomial vector to retained marks and leaves each retained line's puncturing rule intact. **Deleting other marks or exceptional incidences from a retained line would generally damage it.** These incorrect substitutions are explicitly caught by the included negative controls.

For arbitrary equally sized matrices $Y_i$, put $Z(z)=\sum_{i:z\sim i}Y_i$. Finite rearrangement now proves the exact required tensor identity

\[
-\bigoplus_iY_i=\sum_{z\in P}H(z)\otimes Z(z).
\]

The Cartesian bijection also gives the advertised local factorization: for each fixed $u,v\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}$, the pairs $(\alpha_b(i)+v,\beta_b(i)+u)$ enumerate the whole Cartesian product once. Thus summing the formal products $L_{b,u,\alpha+v}^{-1}R_{b,v,\beta+u}$ is the $(u,v)$ block of

\[
\left(\sum_jL_{b,u,j}^{-1}\right)_u
\left(\sum_kR_{b,v,k}\right)_v.
\]

This factorization preserves multiplication order; it does not assume commutativity. This paragraph verifies the incidence-to-rank interface only, without rerunning the permutation-rank argument.

## Actual cell attachments and local link injection

Use distinct vertices $O,V,W$, edges $x_{iuv}:V\to W$, $L_{b,u,j}:O\to V$, and $R_{b,v,k}:O\to W$. For every $b,i\in I_b,u,v\in[r]$, attach one triangle with boundary

\[
L_{b,u,\alpha_b(i)+v}\ x_{iuv}\ R_{b,v,\beta_b(i)+u}^{-1}.
\]

All three vertices and edge types are different, so the boundary embeds. Distinct edge indices are not identified. Finite cells may share unions of faces; barycentric subdivision is available if a strict polyhedral convention requires single-face intersections. A regular point exists because $|E|<m$, so there are $L,R$ edges and the complex is connected.

At $O$, fix one $L_{b,u,j}$ direction and one $R_{b,v,k}$ direction. The two equations $\alpha_b(i)=j-v$, $\beta_b(i)=k-u$ specify exactly one label in that block. Hence the link is a disjoint union of simple complete bipartite graphs, with no parallel edges and girth at least four.

At $V$, map each $x_{iuv}$ germ to label $i$, each $L_{b,u,j}$ germ to its block's point $p(b)$, and each triangle corner to the incidence edge $i\sim p(b)$. Local injection must be checked at **both** kinds of germ:

- At fixed $x_{iuv}$, fixing the projected point selects at most one block containing $i$, because the incidence set is partitioned; $i,u,v$ then specify one corner.
- At fixed $L_{b,u,j}$, fixing the projected label $i$ requires $v\equiv j-\alpha_b(i)\pmod{s_b}$. At most one $v\in\{0,\ldots,r-1\}$ solves it since $s_b\ge r$.

The $W$ proof fixes $R_{b,v,k}$ and uses $u\equiv k-\beta_b(i)\pmod{t_b}$, with $t_b\ge r$. Thus these maps are genuine graph immersions. Vertex injection is neither claimed nor needed. Cyclically reduced link walks stay cyclically reduced under these maps and have at least 14 edges.

For turns, take a positive reduced link path between distinct same-label $x$ germs. Its image is closed and has no internal immediate reversal. Reduction across its closing seam is not guaranteed. Choose a shortest positive subinterval of that image with equal endpoints. It has distinct internal vertices; it cannot have length one because the incidence graph has no loops, or length two because that would be an internal reversal of the original path. It is a simple cycle, of length at least 14. Therefore the whole path has at least 14 edges. This addresses the closing-seam issue directly.

## Geometric consequence of the verified gate

Give the triangles common hyperbolic angles $(27\pi/50,9\pi/50,9\pi/50)$ at $(O,V,W)$. The sum is $9\pi/10<\pi$, so the shape exists and shared side types have matching lengths. Link cycles have length at least $108\pi/50$ at $O$ and $126\pi/50$ at $V,W$. Rectangle turns have distance at least $126\pi/50>\pi$, or infinite distance. A compatible subdivision leaves the metric unchanged; its new face links have circumference $2\pi$, and new edge links consist of length-$\pi$ arcs between two directions, with no cycle shorter than $2\pi$.

The standard finite-shape link criterion and simply connected local-to-global theorem therefore apply. They give a CAT(-1) universal cover; finite shape and local finiteness give the proper cocompact deck action. The corresponding rectangles are nontrivial closed local geodesics. These are the classical consequences used by the downstream argument. The relevant primary statements are [Bridson–Haefliger, II.5.2, II.5.4, II.5.6, printed pp. 206–207](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/bridsonhaefligerx.pdf#page=229), whose text was checked in this run. This use of standard geometry does not depend on the source's separate long Euclidean-thinness proof.

## Acquisition and bit cost

Let $L=\lceil\log_2q\rceil=521$, $T_0=M_0(q-1)$, $V_0=m+M_0$, and $\Delta_0\le\max(M_0,q-1)$. These are algorithmic size parameters, not feasible machine budgets.

- A trial samples $M_0$ independent projective directions. Exact uniform sampling can use uniform nonzero vectors in $\mathbb F_q^{20}$ followed by normalization, since every projective direction has $q-1$ representatives.
- Listing all punctured incidences takes $O(M_0qh)$ field operations and writes $\Theta(T_0hL)$ coordinate/index bits in a straightforward representation. Field operations have polynomial bit cost in $L$; they are not unit-cost operations on arbitrarily large integers. Counting and addressing use $O(hL)$-bit integers.
- A conservative exhaustive enumeration of walks of length at most 12 costs $O(V_0\Delta_0^{12})$ graph operations. Constant-length simplicity and canonical-orientation tests avoid duplicate cycle counts. The bit factor is polynomial in $hL$. Early rejection can reduce some trials' work but is not needed for the finite bound.
- Forming $E$, deleting labels, partitioning regular incidence lists and assigning coordinates are finite table operations. Output counts are at most $M_0r^2+2M_0(q-1)$ edges and $M_0r^2(q-1)$ triangles. The edge bound uses $r(s_b+t_b)\le2s_bt_b$.
- Expected trial count is bounded by $1/(1-p_{\rm reject})<2$. This multiplies the above astronomical per-trial work. Deterministic enumeration is finite with a much worse worst-case bound. No polynomial-in-$\log m$ acquisition result follows.

Here $m$ has 3,137 decimal digits, $M_0$ has 3,097, $T_0$ has 3,254, and the triangle upper bound has 3,338. Direct construction is not feasible. The present native CPU work consists only of finite integer inequalities, the 519-step prime check, and bounded toy fixtures. It does not execute this large acquisition algorithm or emit a presentation.
