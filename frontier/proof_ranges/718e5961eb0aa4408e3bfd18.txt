Application: nonlinear members in an elastic truss
Let \(x_e\) be axial member forces, \(A\) the equilibrium matrix, and
\[
H=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\ell_e}{E_ea_e}\right).
\]
Then \(x^\top Hx\) is twice the complementary elastic energy. Complementary-energy optimisation, including nonlinear cable models, is established mechanics; it is not a new physical formulation. ScienceDirect
After preprocessing the bilateral elastic structure, a query may choose arbitrary \(k\) members and impose, for example,
\[
x_e\ge0,\qquad
|x_e|\le u_e,\qquad
x_e=0,
\]
or additional compliance and convex piecewise-linear constitutive response represented by squared-hinge costs.
These respectively model tension-only restrictions, force limits, complete member removal, and specified monotone nonlinear constitutive modifications within the stated static model. The method does not simulate path-dependent plastic history merely because a force cap is present.
The load vector can range over the entire supplied load basis. Capacity parameters can also be optimisation variables when their costs and coupling constraints give an explicit convex query.
For a two-dimensional, bounded-range truss layout with geometric separators, nested dissection gives a baseline factorization cost of \(O(N^{3/2})\) arithmetic and \(O(N\log N)\) work per solve. These are the specialised baseline costs used here, not dense cubic solves. SIAM
There are concrete layouts with an inexpensive exact equilibrium repair: horizontal rows anchored at one boundary and vertical columns anchored at another provide two forest-routing systems. Additional diagonal members need not make the stiffness matrix a scalar graph Laplacian.
For such a layout, with \(M,p=O(N)\), bounded local descriptions and fixed \(d\),
\[
\boxed{
P=\widetilde O(N^{3/2}+Nq),
}
\tag{25}
\]
followed by small QP queries independent of \(N\) in arithmetic dimension. Precision factors remain charged.

A small fixed interface known beforehand should normally use ordinary condensation. The proposed advantage is for many different, adaptively selected interfaces, when \(q\ll M\), and when local outputs rather than full fields are required for most candidates.
High accuracy can make \(q\propto\varepsilon^{-2}\) expensive. No performance claim is made where that preprocessing exceeds the cost of the intended workload.
9. Exact feasibility does not remove every singular-limit obstruction
Two boundaries are decisive.
Changing the quadratic bulk is not automatically covered. Suppose a scalar bulk energy is \(E(y)=y^2\), approximated by \(\widehat E(y)=(1+\theta)y^2\). Subtracting \((1-\rho)y^2\) produces the convex physical energy \(\rho y^2\), but the compressed energy becomes \((\rho+\theta)y^2\). Relative error is \(\theta/\rho\).
Thus even a convex final model can defeat this approximation if a query cancels the baseline energy. Changes expressible as additional nonnegative complementary energy—including selected-member weakening and removal—remain covered. Arbitrary negative quadratic corrections, changing equilibrium geometry, and near-buckling bulk models do not.
Optimisation does not make combinatorial search disappear. The theorem solves continuous convex design on a query-selected support. It can evaluate an explicit candidate list with one uniform guarantee. It does not search all \(\binom Mk\) supports in polynomial time, solve nonconvex robust design automatically, or infer constitutive parameters from measurements.
Likewise, exact feasibility concerns the supplied rational model. Uncertain geometry or an uncertain contact gap cannot be promoted into an exact physical constraint merely through arithmetic.
10. Executed checks and research status
I implemented small dense diagnostics for the new construction:
- 129 exact support-kernel checks and 40 exact conditional-energy identities;
- 24 full-versus-reduced coupled convex design problems, including variable loads and shared capacity budgets;
- singular-limit examples with coefficients down to \(2^{-200}\);
- full-state refinement followed by exact interface correction.
For the 24 design tests, the largest true objective ratio of the reduced-design candidate to the direct optimum was approximately \(1.00814\); the largest feasibility residual was \(1.3\times10^{-16}\). The full-state refinement example had energy ratio approximately \(1.0000000246\).
These numbers are diagnostics, not an achieved theorem-wide tolerance or performance benchmark. The deliberately small diagnostic sketch had a worst observed geometry distortion of about \(35.9\%\), and it was larger than the small host. It does not demonstrate compression at scale.

Established outcome
The strongest surviving operation is:
\[
\boxed{
\text{Compress the minimum feasible extension energy uniformly,
while retaining its exact domain.}
}
\]
That supports a genuine increase in computational scope: query-time nonlinear constraints, changing load combinations, continuous convex design, and controlled recovery of full feasible states, rather than only scalar resistance updates.
The remaining barrier to a broader physical breakthrough is precise: the argument relies on a fixed quadratic bulk and on local modifications that do not cancel its approximation error. Extending it to globally changing constitutive geometry or nonconvex instability would require a different representation—not another use of the same inverse identity. Independent priority assessment and large-scale comparison are still required before treating the present capability as a major established advance.
