## 7. Originality and completion status

The broad connection between causal restrictions, separable operations and entanglement is **not new**. Akibue, Owari, Kato and Murao explicitly relate the LOCC–separable-operation gap to communication outside a prescribed causal order. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.96.062331?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Likewise, describing a resource as a channel together with its spacetime interface has established antecedents. Chitambar, Gour, Sengupta and Zibakhsh develop a framework in which input-to-output timing is part of the resource specification. The general assertion that timing affects nonlocal implementation cost cannot be claimed as this investigation’s discovery. [APS Journals](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.104.052208?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The precise deductions supplied here are the early-versus-late flag construction, the finite-block routing inequalities (10)–(11), and the resulting exact cost identity with universally returned catalysts. **I have not established that these precise formulations are absent from prior work.**

Their mathematical status is also separate from historical priority: the constructions and reductions are explicit, while the positive-rate conclusion uses the retained sequential-cost theorem. Independent verification of the combined argument remains outstanding.

**The historic mission remains incomplete.** The strongest surviving result is an exact obstruction to resource-preserving causalization: an input-independent flag can conceal a nonfree normalized operation, and requiring that flag before the input can turn a zero-cost block operation into a positive-cost causal process.

That result identifies a substantive failure in a proposed route to the larger theorem. It does not replace the missing collective block obstruction with a weaker result and call the original problem solved.
