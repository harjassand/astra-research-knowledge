## 6. Why the comparison is global and many-body

Both rate systems are symmetric, with the same uniform stationary measure pi on each connected configuration sector. Their Dirichlet forms obey

    0.97 E_*(f,f) <= E_M(f,f) <= E_*(f,f).

Taking an infimum over potentials with specified boundary values proves the same comparison for every reversible capacity between two sets. Taking the variational infimum that defines the spectral gap gives the corresponding gap comparison. On mean-zero functions in a connected sector, inverse quadratic forms are bounded in the reverse order, with factor at most 1/0.97.

For an antisymmetric displacement label d_xy, a reversible finite-state Markov additive process has directional transport form

    C_q(v)= (1/2) inf_f sum_x pi_x sum_y q_xy
                      [v.d_xy+f(y)-f(x)]^2.                       (3)

Applying (2) inside this infimum gives

    0.97 C_*(v) <= C_M(v) <= C_*(v)                               (4)

for every direction and every finite configuration graph. Normalization per physical volume does not change the comparison. Finite periodic interacting exclusion systems are included. A thermodynamic variational limit inherits the inequalities when that limit exists; existence or a quantum hydrodynamic limit is not assumed proved.

For simple exclusion hops on a periodic lattice with |T_xy|>=T_min and local hop detunings bounded by W, the oracle rates satisfy

    q_* >= epsilon^2 T_min^2/max(eta,W).

Therefore the matched rates dominate a constant-rate symmetric exclusion process. On a one-dimensional n-site ring in the K-particle sector, the associated current coefficient is at least

    0.97 epsilon^2 T_min^2/max(eta,W) * K(n-K)/[n(n-1)].            (5)

This follows directly from (3): constant-rate exclusion has zero configuration-wise total signed drift, so the cross term in f vanishes and a constant corrector minimizes the form. Equation (5) is a volume-independent positive bound at fixed nonzero density.

This absolute bound is not itself a unique new advantage: setting one sufficiently large uniform dephasing strength also gives a standard positive comparison bound. The distinctive candidate contribution is nearly optimal **configuration-adaptive** broadening without evaluating the exponentially many conditional responses.

These statements do not establish monotonicity of every individual first-passage time or arbitrary driven nonequilibrium current. They do not address finite-temperature detailed balance or selected pure quantum phases.
