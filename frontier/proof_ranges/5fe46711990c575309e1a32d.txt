### Theorem B: channel-universal survival

Fix **any** qubit completely positive trace-preserving map Lambda, applied identically and independently at all sites. The sequence R_N is independent of Lambda. Exactly one of the following occurs:

1. Lambda tensor Lambda is entanglement-annihilating, meaning it maps **every physical two-qubit input** to a separable output. Then Lambda^{tensor N}(R_N) is fully separable for every even N.
2. Lambda tensor Lambda is not entanglement-annihilating. Then Lambda^{tensor N}(R_N) is entangled for every sufficiently large even N. These outputs remain in SEP_1(N) and two-producible, so they are bound entangled and have depth exactly two.

Equivalently,

\[
\{\Lambda:\Lambda^{\otimes2}\text{ is EA}\}
=\bigcap_{N\ge2,\ N\text{ even}}
\{\Lambda:\Lambda^{\otimes N}(R_N)\in\mathrm{SEP}_N\}.       \tag{4}
\]

The non-EA side is eventually true, not just true along a selected subsequence. N may depend badly on the fixed channel. No uniform bound for channels approaching their boundary is implicit. For arbitrary nonunital channels the proof gives binary entanglement survival, not a uniform trace-distance gap or efficient laboratory certification.

This is also an exact characterization of channels which annihilate all singleton-separable, two-producible states at all even sizes: the annihilators are precisely the two-qubit EA channels. Channels outside that set are detected by the **one fixed sequence** (1). It does not characterize channels annihilating arbitrary higher-depth states or the different notion of entanglement-breaking channels.
