This construction passes a stronger test.
Define
\[ F_+=\prod_{j=1}^{r}Y_j, \qquad F_-=\prod_{j=1}^{r}Y_{-j}. \]
Using the exact lower-order matching and equation (7),
\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Cov}(F_-,F_+\mid Y_0=-1) = -\tau_r, \qquad \tau_r=\varepsilon^{2r}e^{-2r}>0. } \tag{17} \]
For any reversible hidden Markov realization, stationary past and future are independent and identically distributed conditional on the present microscopic state. Their conditional product covariance, after conditioning only on the observed present, is consequently nonnegative.
Equation (17) therefore excludes every finite reversible exact realization, regardless of hidden dimension.
The argument can be strengthened from “no exact equilibrium realization” to “no sequence of exact realizations with dissipation tending to zero.”
For any finite stationary generator \(G\), let
\[ R=\frac12(G+G^\dagger) \]
be its additive reversibilization. The stationary microscopic path laws on an interval of length \(L\) satisfy
\[ D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathsf P_G^L\|\mathsf P_R^L) \le\frac L4\,\sigma(G). \tag{18} \]
The comparison preserves stationary probabilities and exit rates. Its proof reduces to
\[ x\log\frac{2x}{x+y} +y\log\frac{2y}{x+y} \le \frac14(x-y)\log\frac xy. \]
This comparison and conditional past–future tests appear in the recent hidden-dissipation-floor research candidate. They are credited and reproved in the accompanying note; its broader claims are not assumed as independently validated results. 
Let \(\mu\) be the common conditional mean of \(F_\pm\) under our process. The bounded observable
\[ Z=\mathbf1_{\{Y_0=-1\}}(F_--\mu)(F_+-\mu) \]
has expectation \(-\tau_r/2\) under the target process and nonnegative expectation under the reversible comparison. Its range has width at most four.
Thus total variation is at least \(\tau_r/8\). Pinsker’s inequality and equation (18), with \(L=2r\), give
\[ \boxed{ \sigma(G) \ge \frac{\varepsilon^{4r}e^{-4r}}{16r} >0 } \tag{19} \]
for every finite all-even hidden exact realization of the observed path law.
This bound is not sharp. Its significance is its independence from hidden dimension: the dissipation cannot be removed by replacing the model with an increasingly elaborate equilibrium-like realization.
A self-contained current-based calculation also gives, for any finite all-even stationary chain under the specified conservative intervention,
\[ \left| \chi(t)-[C(0)-C(t)] \right|^2 \le \frac12M^2\,\sigma\,t\,\operatorname{Var}(f), \tag{20} \]
where \(M=(\max f-\min f)/2\).
For balanced binary observations,
\[ \boxed{ \sigma \ge \frac{2}{t} \left| \chi(t)-[C(0)-C(t)] \right|^2. } \tag{21} \]
The proof uses divergence-free stationary currents, Cauchy–Schwarz, and
\[ \int_0^t\mathcal E(P_sf)\,ds \le\frac12\operatorname{Var}(f). \]
The general response–dissipation connection predates this investigation; equation (20) is the explicit finite-state form used here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9705002)
At time one, the constructed response therefore certifies an inverse-polynomial dissipation floor
\[ \sigma\ge2c_*^2\varepsilon^4. \]
It does not recover the actual entropy-production rate to fixed relative accuracy. That stronger estimation problem remains unresolved here.
