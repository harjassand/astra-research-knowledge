PREANALYSIS.md predates raw outcome inspection, although published figures were known. Extraction was developed on discovery and frozen before validation parsing. An initial freeze had a logical error: it could label a brief initial speed excursion an event without the required persistence. Frozen observation rule:
- Use the raw long-axis/PCA revolution field at 4 kHz; centroid-angle tracking failed in some discovery traces.
- Normalize sign to each measured 1–9-s baseline, not the forcing label.
- Velocity: degree-1 Savitzky–Golay derivative over 401 samples, then 100-Hz analysis grid.
- Primary activity threshold: 5% baseline; fixed sensitivities 2.5%, 7.5%.
- Persistence: 14 of 15 successive 10-ms samples above threshold.
- Primary edge exclusion 100 ms; sensitivity 200 ms. Both exceed the derivative's ~50-ms lookahead.
- Initial activity or an onset within 50 ms of the guard is immediate/unresolved.
- A resolved later onset additionally requires initial 100-ms mean speed below half threshold and onset at least 100 ms after the guard.
- Stop at the first detected event; later pulses are not independent first arrivals.

Ten synthetic positive/negative-direction cases passed timing/classification checks. This is numerical QA, not biological calibration.

## Identifiability limits

Zero observed rotation does not independently establish zero bound or torque-capable stators. Glass contact and internal bearing barriers can mask torque. Forced direction also does not establish switch conformation; opposite-sign autonomous baselines occur in these raw traces.

A latent chemical precursor and an already engaged but mechanically pinned state can have the same inactive/active emissions and transition generator. Relabeling that hidden state leaves the distribution of those observed trajectories identical. Distinguishing them requires justified physical restrictions or another observable.

A fixed sinusoidal pinning model has conditional speed-plateau invariants, derived in sources/pinning_plateau_discriminant.md. They were **not tested on validation cells**. Even equal increments do not exclude general mechanical masking. This is not claimed as a new law.
