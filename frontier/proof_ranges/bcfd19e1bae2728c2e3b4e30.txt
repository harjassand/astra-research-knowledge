## 7. The E/O benchmark under arbitrary controls

Take the supplied synthetic four-carbon ages
\[
A_j=C+(j-1)\tau+\tau X_j,\quad j=1,\ldots,4,\qquad C\geq0,
\]
with \(X\) uniform on even parity in model E and odd parity in model O. Every proper coordinate marginal of \(X\) agrees between the two models.

Let \(u(t)\in[-1,1]\) be any deterministic normalized isotope waveform, \(p(t)=(1+a u(t))/2\), and put \(u_k=u(t-C-k\tau)\), \(k=0,\ldots,4\). Expanding the parity-class averages in (1) gives the *entire count-generating-function difference*
\[
G_E(z)-G_O(z)
=\frac{a^4}{128}(z-1)^4\prod_{k=0}^{3}(u_k-u_{k+1}).
\tag{8}
\]
Thus all five count-bin differences are a fixed multiple of \([1,-4,6,-4,1]\). The corrected parity contrast difference is
\[
\Delta_{\rm corr}(t,C)
=\frac18\prod_{k=0}^{3}(u_k-u_{k+1}).
\tag{9}
\]
This is a fourth-order finite difference across the four adjacent age intervals. It applies to arbitrary shaped, overlapping, multipulse, and periodic deterministic controls in the stated model; the control changes the four increments. If \(u\) is \(M\)-Lipschitz, the magnitude is at most \(\min\{2,(M\tau)^4/8\}\).

An ideal two-level single step or one rectangular pulse has at most two changes across the five sampled age nodes. Then at least two factors in (8) vanish, so **the complete whole-product count distributions agree** in E and O. This is the exact, finite-count lower witness for that restricted control class: its per-product divergence is zero, so no number of repeated products can distinguish the two laws with that single-pulse design.

An equally informed square wave satisfying \(u(t-\tau)=-u(t)\) has \(|u_k-u_{k+1}|=2\) for all four gaps and product \(16\), for every common \(C\) and output phase. It attains the amplitude bound \(|\Delta_{\rm corr}|\leq2\); the raw parity gap is \(2a^4\). With \(a=49/50\), the ideal per-product count-law total variation is \(a^4=0.92236816\), so the even/odd-count classifier has error \((1-a^4)/2=0.03881592\) under either specified model. This is a finite exact toy-model counterexample to any claimed superiority of the telegraph implementation. It assumes a known temporal scale \(\tau\), ideal square-wave transitions, the stipulated synthetic laws, and a calibrated count channel.

For comparison, the source's telegraph choice \(r\tau=2\) gives corrected parity gap
\[
\frac{(1-e^{-2r\tau})^4}{8}=0.116090719\ldots,
\]
and switches at mean rate \(2/\tau\), while the tuned square wave switches once per \(\tau\) and has the same mean heavy fraction. A pair of aligned finite pulses also creates the alternating grid pattern when \(C\) is known. A continuous periodic drive preserves the square-wave relation under unknown common \(C\). These comparisons are per the synthetic model, not a total-cost optimization: finite mixing, transition slope, isotope dose, switch wear, prehistory, collection time, and detector precision must be matched on a real platform.

The source paper on isotope frequency response establishes a linear isotope-composition modulation competitor, while the cited H-ZSM-5 work already uses sequences of \(^{13}\mathrm C\)-labelled and unlabelled methanol pulses with online MS and fast-scanning pulse GC. Equation (8) provides the nonlinear count response that a fair shaped/multisine/multipulse benchmark must optimize. It does not establish priority over the full nonlinear isotope-transient literature.
