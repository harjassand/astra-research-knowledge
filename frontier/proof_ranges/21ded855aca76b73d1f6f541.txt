## 5. The new boundary rules out the previous H-subtraction route

For A0=[[0,(2r+1)/3],[1,0]],

    D2(F_r-epsilon H)(A0)[e11,e11]
       =-16(r+2)^2 epsilon/9<0, epsilon>0.

Hence F_r-epsilon H is neither rank-one convex nor quasiconvex. This is a
direct acoustic argument; extremality is not needed to justify it.

The earlier anisotropic subtraction epsilon(a-d)^4 is also impossible at
the sharp floor. If it were quasiconvex, rotate outputs and average the four
rotations 0,pi/4,pi/2,3pi/4. Left invariance of F_r and

    average (a'-d')^4 = (3/8)[(a-d)^2+(b+c)^2]^2=(3/8)H

would imply quasiconvexity of F_r-(3epsilon/8)H, a contradiction.

These exclusions do not disprove a pure-fourth-order Jensen gap
integral[F_r(B+Dphi)-F_r(B)]>=c integral|Dphi|^4 by some other argument.
Such a gap is weaker than subtracting a fixed positive density from F at
every affine background. The reduced Gram's positive pivots alone establish
neither such a gap nor its absence.
