# SOL — Track B: Bernoulli Noncollapse and Measurable Rigidity

Foundational problem unresolved

The investigation has not established \\(2^G\not\cong_G4^G\\) or disproved universal Bernoulli collapse. Two rigorous obstructions substantially narrow the available strategies. Neither is a historic-scale breakthrough.

## 1. A no-go theorem for subgroup Rokhlin entropy

Proposition. Let \\(G\\) be countably infinite, with

\\[ s_G=h\_{\sup}^{\mathrm{Rok}}(G)<\log 2. \\]

Then, for every infinite subgroup \\(H\leq G\\),

\\[ \boxed{ h_H^{\mathrm{Rok}}(2^G) = h_H^{\mathrm{Rok}}(4^G). } \\]

Here the actions are restricted from \\(G\\) to \\(H\\).

Proof. There are two cases.

If \\([G:H]=m<\infty\\), the restrictions are Bernoulli \\(H\\)-shifts with base entropies \\(m\log2\\) and \\(m\log4\\). Seward's formula gives

\\[ h_H^{\mathrm{Rok}}(2^G) =\min(m\log2,s_H). \\]

A generating partition for the \\(G\\)-action can be joined over \\(m\\) coset representatives to generate the restricted \\(H\\)-action. Consequently,

\\[ h_H^{\mathrm{Rok}}(2^G) \leq m h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(2^G) =m s_G\<m\log2. \\]

Therefore \\(s_H\<m\log2\\), so both restricted shifts have Rokhlin entropy exactly \\(s_H\\).

If \\([G:H]=\infty\\), both restrictions are Bernoulli \\(H\\)-shifts with infinite Shannon base entropy. Seward's infinite-base theorem says their Rokhlin entropy is \\(0\\) when \\(s_H=0\\), and \\(\infty\\) when \\(s_H>0\\). Again the values agree.

This proves the proposition, using Seward's Theorems 1.10 and 1.12.&#x20;

Consequence: Even collecting Rokhlin entropy across every infinite subgroup cannot separate these shifts. This includes attempts to exploit cyclic subgroups, amenable subgroups, or finite-index restrictions.

## 2. Ordinary Koopman spectral invariants also coincide

For any countably infinite torsion-free group \\(G\\), the Koopman representations associated with the fair binary and four-symbol Bernoulli shifts satisfy

\\[ \boxed{ L_0^2(2^G)\cong\_{\mathbb C[G]}L_0^2(4^G) \cong\lambda_G^{\oplus\aleph_0}, } \\]

where \\(\lambda_G\\) is the left regular representation.

To prove this, write the Bernoulli \\(L^2\\)-space as its orthogonal decomposition indexed by finite coordinate subsets. A nonempty finite subset \\(F\subset G\\) has trivial stabilizer under left translation: a group element stabilizing \\(F\\) acts as a finite permutation, so some positive power must be the identity. Torsion-freeness makes the element itself trivial.

Each orbit of finite subsets therefore contributes copies of the regular representation. There are countably infinitely many such contributions.

Thus the binary and four-symbol shifts have unitarily equivalent Koopman representations, despite their different base entropies.

Consequence: Spectral multiplicity, ordinary Koopman weak containment, and invariants determined solely by the Koopman representation cannot distinguish these actions. Any spectral strategy must retain additional structure, such as the multiplication structure of \\(L^\infty(X)\\).

## 3. A stronger no-go theorem: factor-monotone invariants

There is another important obstruction from Lewis Bowen's existing work.

Bowen's theorem: For every countable nonamenable group, every nontrivial Bernoulli shift factors onto every other nontrivial Bernoulli shift. This includes groups for which sofic entropy is unavailable.&#x20;

In particular, there are measure-preserving equivariant factor maps in both directions:

\\[ 2^G\twoheadrightarrow_G4^G, \qquad 4^G\twoheadrightarrow_G2^G. \\]

Consequently, suppose \\(I\\) is any conjugacy invariant satisfying

\\[ Y\twoheadrightarrow_G Z \quad\Longrightarrow\quad I(Y)\geq I(Z). \\]

Then necessarily

\\[ \boxed{I(2^G)=I(4^G).} \\]

The proof is simply to apply monotonicity to both factor maps.

This eliminates an entire class of possible entropy-like invariants before constructing them. Any successful binary–four-symbol separator must distinguish isomorphism from mutual factorability.

That distinction is critical: factor maps in both directions do not provide inverse maps or a measurable conjugacy.

## 4. Why the characteristic-two defect does not transfer to biased Bernoulli measures

There is a precise obstruction to reusing the existing linear splitting to prove \\(s_G=0\\).

Let \\(b\in\mathbb F_2[G]\\) be the noninvertible one-sided inverse from the inherited construction, and let

\\[ r=|\operatorname{supp}(b)|. \\]

Since \\(b\\) is not invertible, \\(r\geq2\\).

Give the input coordinates independent Bernoulli-\\(p\\) distributions. A coordinate of the image under the linear cellular automaton \\(T_b\\) is the parity of \\(r\\) independent input bits.

Its probability of being \\(1\\) is exactly

\\[ q_r(p)=\frac{1-(1-2p)^r}{2}. \\]

For every \\(0\<p<1\\) with \\(p\ne\tfrac12\\),

\\[ |1-2q_r(p)|=|1-2p|^r<|1-2p|. \\]

Because binary Shannon entropy increases strictly as its parameter approaches \\(1/2\\),

\\[ \boxed{H(q_r(p))>H(p).} \\]

Thus \\(T_b\\) does not preserve the biased Bernoulli-\\(p\\) measure.

This explains why an entropy-compression argument valid for Haar measure cannot simply be applied to Bernoulli bases with arbitrarily small positive entropy.

The result does not rule out a different nonlinear construction, nor does it establish that \\(s_G>0\\).

## 5. Testing measurable rigidity and operator-algebraic approaches

The main remaining distinction is whether the action carries a measurable structure that a conjugacy must preserve but an arbitrary factor map need not.

A potentially relevant direction is to study the crossed products

\\[ M_k=L^\infty(k^G)\rtimes G,\qquad k=2,4. \\]

Any measurable \\(G\\)-conjugacy induces an isomorphism between these crossed products preserving the canonical copies of \\(L(G)\\).

Therefore, a proof that no such inclusion-preserving isomorphism exists would establish \\(2^G\not\cong_G4^G\\).

The difficulty is that this reformulation does not provide an invariant that can currently be computed differently for \\(M_2\\) and \\(M_4\\). Known rigidity results have hypotheses that must be checked independently. In particular, Popa's rigidity results for noncommutative Bernoulli shifts cannot be transferred directly to classical commutative Bernoulli shifts, and results requiring property (T) cannot be applied without establishing that property for the chosen group.&#x20;

There is also a problem with trying to recognize the coordinate-wise bit-flip group intrinsically. A measurable Bernoulli conjugacy need not preserve the original coordinate presentation. Without a theorem forcing preservation of that presentation, algebraic invariants of finite-support coordinate changes are not invariants of the underlying measurable action.

No verified rigidity mechanism survived these tests.

## 6. The decisive missing mathematical statement

The results above leave two genuinely different targets.

Target A — Direct binary–four separation

Find an invariant \\(I\\), defined without a chosen Bernoulli coordinate presentation, satisfying

\\[ I(2^G)\ne I(4^G). \\]

The invariant must withstand mutual factorability, arbitrary measurable conjugacies, identical ordinary Koopman representations, and identical subgroup Rokhlin entropy profiles.

Target B — Positive supremal Rokhlin entropy

Construct a free ergodic p.m.p. action \\(G\curvearrowright(Y,\nu)\\) and prove a strict lower bound

\\[ h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(Y,\nu)\geq\delta>0. \\]

This would establish \\(s_G>0\\) and disprove universal Bernoulli collapse by comparing base entropies on opposite sides of \\(s_G\\). It would not distinguish \\(2^G\\) from \\(4^G\\).

The positivity route is not merely a routine entropy estimate. The existence of a countable group whose Rokhlin entropy vanishes for every free ergodic action remains an open foundational question in the 2026 literature.&#x20;

| Verified historic-scale result           | None                                     |

The strongest contribution of this investigation is the systematic elimination of three broad invariant strategies: subgroup Rokhlin entropy, ordinary Koopman spectral data, and factor-monotone quantities. The parity calculation adds a precise obstruction to transferring the algebraic splitting away from Haar measure.

These deductions are valid under their stated hypotheses, but their novelty relative to the full literature has not been established.

The conclusion is that Track B remains open. None of the tested mechanisms currently justifies a claim of binary–four nonisomorphism, universal noncollapse, or a 10/10 foundational breakthrough.
