T13 — COMMON FACTORS, SHIFT COMPATIBILITY, AND THE FINITE-STATE GAP
Frozen record v1 | 9 October 2026
Status: internally derived structural lemma and exact finite-state criterion; no novelty claim. A countable-state counterexample proves that projective CP alone does not imply a finite hidden-state realization. The criterion does not derive the shift axiom from CP. The closure hierarchy below is convergent in the cylinder topology but is not a finite-time decision procedure.

TARGET
Given a stationary observed process on a finite alphabet, understand whether projectively consistent classical completely positive (CP) reflected-cylinder kernels force one common hidden state space and a reversible continuous-time Markov realization. Keep separate (i) common factor gluing, (ii) temporal Markov compatibility, (iii) finite hidden dimension, and (iv) closure of finite-state realizations.

CONVENTIONS AND INPUT
Let A be a finite output alphabet and D=Q_{>=0}. A one-sided path law is a probability measure on A^D with its product sigma-field; when the observed process is cadlag, rational-time cylinders determine its law. Let W_n=A^{D_n}, where D_1 subset D_2 subset ... are finite sets exhausting D. For a word/cylinder u, [u] denotes its cylinder event. A reflected kernel H(u,v) is the observed probability that the reversed-past cylinder u and future cylinder v both occur across a fixed reflection boundary. We assume:

 (i) H is nonnegative, symmetric, normalized, and projectively consistent under cylinder refinement;
 (ii) for each n, the complete-word matrix H_n=(H(x,y))_{x,y in W_n} is classically completely positive, i.e. H_n=B_n B_n^T for some entrywise nonnegative B_n;
 (iii) the row/column marginals agree with the supplied one-sided observed cylinder law.

Here CP always means the cone generated by xx^T for x>=0. It is not complete positivity of a quantum channel. The result uses CP itself; replacing it with positive semidefinite plus entrywise nonnegative is invalid in general at matrix order at least five.

1. COMMON PROJECTIVE FACTOR GLUING

Lemma 1. Under (i)–(iii), there exists one probability measure mu on K=Prob(A^D) such that for every pair of finite cylinders u,v,

                 H(u,v) = integral_K nu([u]) nu([v]) d mu(nu).       (1)

The same mu gives the observed one-sided marginals:

                 P^+([u]) = integral_K nu([u]) d mu(nu).             (2)

Proof. Fix n and write H_n(x,y)=sum_r b_r(x)b_r(y), b_r>=0. For every nonzero column b_r put s_r=sum_x b_r(x), lambda_r=s_r^2, and h_r(x)=b_r(x)/s_r. Since sum_{x,y}H_n(x,y)=1, sum_r lambda_r=1; each h_r is a probability vector on W_n. Extend h_r to a probability law nu_r on all of A^D, for example by fixing all coordinates outside D_n to one arbitrary symbol. Then mu_n=sum_r lambda_r delta_{nu_r} is a probability measure on K. Its second moments reproduce H(u,v) for cylinders supported on D_n: obtain shorter cylinders by summing over exact assignments in W_n and use projective consistency.

Let F_n be the set of probability measures mu on K satisfying (1) for all cylinders supported on D_n. The construction gives F_n nonempty. The constraints are nested because H is projectively consistent. The cylinder maps nu -> nu([u]) are continuous on K, so each F_n is closed. Since A^D is compact metrizable, K=Prob(A^D) and Prob(K) are compact. Thus the nested closed sets F_n have a common element. The first-moment identity (2) follows by summing (1) over a complete second-side cylinder partition and using the stipulated marginal consistency. This proves the lemma.

Interpretation and exact limitation. Equation (1) constructs a common classical positive feature space: the functions h_u(nu)=nu([u]) are nonnegative in L2(mu), and H(u,v)=<h_u,h_v>. It removes the factor-by-factor gluing obstruction for the single reflected cut. It does NOT construct transitions between feature points, a Markov kernel on K, or operators P_t. In particular, the feature shift nu -> theta_t nu is a deterministic shift of an entire output law; in a hidden Markov model, the conditional future law after time t is instead a mixture over hidden states. There is no reason for that mixture kernel to be encoded by H's second moments. Projective CP therefore supplies a common factor measure, not a semigroup.

2. AN OBSERVABLE SHIFT-SIMPLEX CRITERION FOR FINITE REALIZATION

The remaining compatibility has an exact formulation in terms of a finite simplex of conditional future laws. This is stronger and more concrete than asking for unspecified factors at each time.

Definition (finite reversible shift simplex, FRS). A stationary observed law P on A^D has an FRS of size N if there exist weights pi_i>0 (sum_i pi_i=1), output labels g_i in A, one-sided path laws nu_i in Prob(A^D), and a strongly continuous matrix semigroup S_t (t>=0) such that:

 (a) nu_i(Y_0=g_i)=1 for every i, and P^+=sum_i pi_i nu_i;
 (b) each S_t is row-stochastic, S_0=I, S_{s+t}=S_s S_t, and pi_i S_t(i,j)=pi_j S_t(j,i);
 (c) for every rational t>=0 and every future cylinder C,

             (theta_t nu_i)(C) = sum_j S_t(i,j) nu_j(C),             (3)

where theta_t is the push-forward by the future shift omega(s) -> omega(s+t).

The finite family nu_i is a simplex of observed conditional futures. Equation (3) says its convex hull is invariant under every time shift, with the same reversible barycentric dynamics S_t. It is stated entirely using conditional output laws and cylinder equalities; it is an observed positive-realization criterion. If the nu_i are linearly independent, the barycentric coordinates are unique, and theta_{s+t}=theta_s theta_t forces the semigroup composition law whenever the shift equations hold. For general redundant families, composition is retained explicitly in (b).

Theorem 2 (exact equivalence). P has an N-state reversible hidden CTMC realization with deterministic observation if and only if it has an FRS of size at most N.

Forward direction. Given a finite reversible generator Q, stationary pi, and output labels g_i, let S_t=exp(tQ) and let nu_i be the conditional future observed path law from hidden state i. The hidden Markov property gives (3); detailed balance gives pi_i S_t(i,j)=pi_j S_t(j,i). The first output is g_i deterministically and P^+=sum pi_i nu_i.

Reverse direction. A strongly continuous stochastic semigroup on a finite state space has a generator Q with off-diagonal entries nonnegative and row sums zero; detailed balance of S_t implies pi_i Q_ij=pi_j Q_ji. Build the stationary CTMC with generator Q and observe g_i. Let rho_i be its conditional future law from i. For any cylinder with first time 0, rho_i and nu_i agree at time 0 by (a). If the next observation time is t>0, the Markov recursion for rho_i and (3) give the same mixture over j of the suffix cylinder. Induction on the number of observation times shows rho_i=nu_i on every rational cylinder. Hence the constructed stationary observed future law is sum_i pi_i rho_i=P^+. A stationary two-sided law is determined by its nonnegative-time finite-dimensional marginals after translating the earliest queried time to zero. Thus the full observed law equals P. The hidden chain is reversible by detailed balance.

Under Theorem 2, every reflected kernel is automatically a *single compatible* CP factorization:

                 H(u,v)=sum_i pi_i nu_i([u])nu_i([v]).              (4)

This follows from conditional independence of past and future given X_0 and equality of their conditional laws under detailed balance. The converse from (1) to (3) is the unresolved compatibility step: (1) alone has no barycentric shift maps S_t.

3. PROCESS-LEVEL COUNTEREXAMPLE: CP DOES NOT FORCE FINITE HIDDEN DIMENSION

For n>=1 let lambda_n=1-2^{-n}, w_n=2^{-n}, and define a countable-state hidden chain with states (n,b), b in {0,1}. The label n is fixed forever; conditional on n, b flips in either direction at rate lambda_n. The stationary law is pi(n,b)=w_n/2. The chain is reversible, with bounded exit rate below one. Observe Y=b.

This gives a stationary reversible observed path law with

                 Cov(Y_0,Y_t) = (1/4) sum_{n>=1} w_n exp(-2 lambda_n t).

Every finite reflected word table is CP, with one common countable-state factorization using h_{n,b}(u)=Pr(Y_future in [u] | X_0=(n,b)); the countable sum converges entrywise. The factor laws are projectively compatible and the hidden state has a strongly continuous reversible Markov semigroup.

Nevertheless, no finite-state reversible CTMC can realize this observed law. For any N-state reversible CTMC and centered f=Y-EY, the spectral theorem for its self-adjoint generator gives

                 4 Cov(Y_0,Y_t) = sum_{j=1}^{r} a_j exp(-gamma_j t),

with a_j>0, distinct gamma_j>=0, and r<=N. The zero rate is allowed because a finite reversible chain may be reducible. The target has infinitely many distinct rates beta_n=2 lambda_n in [1,2) and weights w_n>0. To see this without appealing to asymptotic curve fitting, define m_k=(-1)^k (d^k/dt^k)[4 Cov(Y_0,Y_t)] at zero. Then

                 m_k = sum_{n>=1} 2^{-n} beta_n^k.

For every d and every nonzero polynomial p(x)=sum_{i=0}^d c_i x^i,

                 sum_{i,j=0}^d c_i c_j m_{i+j}
                   = sum_{n>=1} 2^{-n} p(beta_n)^2 > 0,

because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish at infinitely many distinct beta_n. Hence every finite Hankel moment matrix (m_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^d has rank d+1. A sum of r exponentials has Hankel rank at most r. Taking d=N contradicts r<=N. This is an exact observable obstruction to finite hidden dimension, beyond any one finite CP check.

Closure caveat. This countable-state law IS in the total-variation closure of finite-state reversible CTMC outputs. Truncating labels to n<=M and renormalizing gives a finite 2M-state model. The omitted mixture mass is sum_{n>M}w_n=2^{-M}; coupling the full and truncated mixtures shows path-law total variation at most 2^{-M}, uniformly on every time horizon. Thus the example proves that CP/common factors do not force an exact finite realization; it does not refute sufficiency for the closure of finite realizations.

4. A CONVERGENT CLOSURE HIERARCHY

This hierarchy is separate from exact FRS. It gives an operational characterization of the closure of finite-state reversible CTMC observations in the rational-cylinder topology; it is not claimed to decide exact finite realization in finite time.

Enumerate the rational-time cylinder events as E_1,E_2,..., with every finite cylinder eventually included. For m, let Gamma_m={E_1,...,E_m}. A capped model class M_{N,R,delta} has at most N hidden states, all active stationary masses pi_i>=delta, maximum exit rate at most R, reversible generator, and a deterministic map g into A. Parameterize it by pi and symmetric conductances c_ij=pi_i Q_ij=pi_j Q_ji>=0, with sum_{j!=i} c_ij/pi_i<=R. For fixed N,R,delta and a fixed output map, this is a compact parameter set; there are only finitely many output maps. Cylinder probabilities are continuous functions of the parameters, computed from exp(tQ).

Define

 d_{m;N,R,delta}(P) = min_{M in M_{N,R,delta}}
                  sum_{k=1}^m 2^{-k}|P(E_k)-M(E_k)|,

and d_m(P)=inf_{N,R,delta} d_{m;N,R,delta}(P). For fixed caps this is a compact finite-dimensional global optimization. An increasing exhaustion N_l,R_l -> infinity and delta_l -> 0 gives d_{m;l} decreasing to d_m. With computable input cylinder probabilities, validated interval methods can approximate each capped minimum to any requested error; no polynomial-time claim is made. The parameter count is O(N^2), plus finitely many output labels; each objective evaluation uses finite matrix exponentials.

Closure equivalence:

 P is in the cylinder-topology closure of finite-state reversible CTMC outputs
       iff d_m(P)=0 for every m.

If P is a limit of such outputs, every finite cylinder list has infimum distance zero. Conversely, if d_m=0 for all m, select for each m one finite model M_m whose error on Gamma_m is at most 1/m. For each fixed k, its E_k probability converges to P(E_k); hence M_m converges to P in the projective cylinder topology. This is a real convergence guarantee, but it has no finite stopping rule: strict positivity of d_m can be hard to certify when N, R, and 1/delta are unbounded.

For exact finite realization, Theorem 2 gives the stronger common-FRS requirement. A separate compactness corollary is available: if one fixed cap (N,R,delta) works at every finite cylinder level with exact zero residuals, compactness of that parameter set yields a single model matching all cylinders. Dropping the uniform cap invalidates this compactness inference, as the countable mixture demonstrates.

5. RELATION TO PRIMARY REFLECTION-POSITIVITY WORK

Jorgensen and Tian, “Reflection positivity and spectral theory,” arXiv:1705.05262v2 (6 June 2017), Section 7, Theorem 7.1 and Remark 7.2 (HTML lines 1228–1270; https://arxiv.org/html/1705.05262). The theorem identifies Markov property for their stationary covariance process with the covariance semigroup law, while reflection positivity gives a weaker Hankel positivity condition. Their scalar Gaussian examples include reflection-positive covariances beyond the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck exponential, but only the exponential is Markov in that scalar-process setting. This is primary evidence for the general positivity-versus-Markov distinction, not a theorem about classical CP word kernels or hidden finite CTMC realizations. The present factor-gluing lemma and FRS criterion are derived here; priority is unknown.

CLAIM LEDGER

- Common factor from all finite CP reflected kernels plus projective consistency: internally derived, proved above; factor space is a probability measure on one-sided path laws.
- CP/common factor implies compatible semigroup: unresolved and not claimed.
- FRS equivalence with finite reversible hidden CTMC: internally derived, proof supplied; exact observed-law statement uses all rational cylinder equalities and strong continuity.
- CP alone implies finite-state realization: refuted by the countable reversible mixture above.
- CP alone implies membership in the closure: unresolved here; the countable example lies in the closure and therefore is not a counterexample to that stronger assertion.
- Capped hierarchy for closure: exact convergence statement in rational-cylinder topology; finite-time decision and favorable computational complexity are not claimed.
