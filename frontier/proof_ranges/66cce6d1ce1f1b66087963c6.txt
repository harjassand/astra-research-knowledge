CUBIC PHASE KERNELS: A CONDITIONAL TRUSTED-CONTROL TRADEOFF
Owned closed operation and proof, 24 October 2026

Status
This is a conditional resource construction using established coherent Pauli
checks, classical syndrome codes and quadratic-form synthesis. It is not a new
error-correction architecture or a demonstrated hardware advantage. An equally
informed CPC/quantum-filter implementation can use precisely this protocol.
The independently derived resource statement compares all-trusted finite-gate
implementation with one explicitly counted noisy native kernel plus smaller
trusted control. Native hardware, time, interaction strength, dephasing and
classical compilation remain substantive resources. Priority is unestablished.

1. Problem, native resource and output

For n qubits and a known cubic polynomial over F2,

 f(x)=sum_(i<j<k) c_ijk x_i x_j x_k,      c_ijk in{0,1},
 U_f|x>=(-1)^f(x)|x>,                  M=binom(n,3),

suppose the native device implements the following concrete diagonal evolution:

 H_f=(pi hbar/tau) sum c_ijk N_i N_j N_k, N_i=(I-Z_i)/2,
 d rho/dt= -i[H_f,rho]/hbar
           +sum_i gamma_i(t)(Z_i rho Z_i-rho), 0<=t<=tau.

Coefficients, duration, Markovian independent local dephasing, supplied native
device and trusted gate-error bounds are MODEL INPUTS, not experimentally acquired
here. No arbitrary noisy non-Clifford processor is covered. The error-free kernel
is exactly U_f. All generators commute, including the superoperators, so the
actual native channel is

 N_f = Z_p o U_f,
 Z_p = tensor_i[(1-p_i)Id+p_i Ad_(Z_i)],
 p_i=(1-exp(-2 Gamma_i))/2,       Gamma_i=int_0^tau gamma_i(t)dt.       (1)

Thus local Z noise THROUGHOUT the native operation becomes the stated output
phase-flip channel by derivation. A local X fault during a cubic kernel instead
spreads into X_i times a suffix quadratic phase, generally not a Pauli. Such
faults are not covered by (1) and must not be silently replaced by outputweight1.

Desired output is the deterministic corrected channel on an ARBITRARY n-qubit
input, preserving an arbitrary external reference. No branch is postselected.
One invocation of the ordinary native N_f is used, with no controlled-U or U† call.

2. Explicit syndrome recovery

Take r=ceil(log2(n+1)) and a binary r-by-n matrix H whose columns are the distinct
nonzero binary vectors representing integers1,...,n. Let q_j be its rows and

 Q_j=X(q_j),           P_j=U_f† Q_j U_f.

Prepare r trusted controls in |+>^r. Apply controlled P_j before N_f in order
j=1,...,r, then controlled Q_j† after it in reverse order. Apply H to the controls
and measure them. Decode syndrome h_i into correction Z_i; syndrome0 and unused
syndromes receive identity. Controls/measurement/feedforward/reset are paid.
The complete circuit is CPTP and acts coherently on the quantum input.

Proof, including coherent correctable noise
For a control string a, write V_in(a)=P_r^a_r...P_1^a_1 and
V_out(a)=Q_r^a_r...Q_1^a_1. Since U_f V_in(a)=V_out(a)U_f, for any Z error Z(e),

 V_out(a)† Z(e) U_f V_in(a)=(-1)^(a.H e) Z(e)U_f.

The Walsh transform on the controls therefore writes syndrome H e without
learning anything about the logical state. This remains true with any reference.
If every native noise Kraus operator is in span{I,Z_1,...,Z_n}, different terms
have distinct syndromes. After correction the joint amplitude for Kraus alpha is

 sum_(e in{0,e_1,...,e_n}) c_alpha,e |H e> tensor U_f|psi>.

Tracing the classical syndrome gives U_f(.)U_f† multiplied by
sum_alpha,e |c_alpha,e|²=1. The latter follows from trace preservation and Pauli
orthogonality, not from a stochastic-noise assumption. So ANY coherent noise in
this correctable span is corrected EXACTLY, with all outcomes retained.

For the full independent dephasing channel (1), errors of weight>=2 can collide
with good syndromes. They produce nonidentity residual Paulis because the decoder
uses only weight0/1 corrections. Consequently the ideal-check corrected channel
is a Pauli mixture with total nonidentity probability

 P_bad=1-product_i(1-p_i)-sum_i p_i product_(j!=i)(1-p_j),
 D_diamond(N_corrected,U_f)=P_bad <= sum_(i<j) p_i p_j.               (2)

Here D_diamond=||.||diamond/2. The upper bound follows by a union bound over pairs
of flips. Equality with P_bad follows from the Pauli-mixture upper bound and its
maximally-entangled Choi input, whose nonidentity Pauli outputs are orthogonal
to the ideal output. For homogeneous p, P_bad=1-(1-p)^n-np(1-p)^(n-1)
and P_bad<=binom(n,2)p². The bare channel's corresponding error is1-(1-p)^n.

3. Trusted controls: quadratic derivatives, not dense monomial counting

For any q,

 P(q)=U_f†X(q)U_f=X(q) D_(g_q),
 g_q(x)=f(x+q)+f(x),                  degree(g_q)<=2.

The degree drops because the cubic homogeneous terms cancel under translation.
An F2 quadratic form has an alternating polar matrix B_q. An invertible binary
change of variables y=Lx brings it to

 g_q(x)=c+ell.y+sum_(a=1)^s y_(2a-1)y_(2a),
 s=rank(B_q)/2<=floor(n/2).

Proof/construction: find a pair u,v with u^T B_q v=1; replace every remaining
w by w+(w^T B_q v)u+(w^T B_q u)v. This decouples that pair. Repeat, then append
the radical basis. The transformed linear terms are recovered by evaluating
g_q on the new basis vectors. Gaussian elimination implements L and L^-1 with
O(n²) CNOTs and computes them in O(n³) classical bit operations.

To implement CONTROLLED D_(g_q), perform L unconditionally, then at most floor(n/2)
CCZs (control plus each quadratic pair), at most n controlled-Zs for the linear
terms, a possible Z on the control for c, and undo L. Add at most n CNOTs for
controlled X(q). Postchecks Q_j need at most n CNOTs. Therefore total trusted cost is

 O(nr) CCZ-equivalents, O(n²r) Clifford two-qubit gates,
 r=O(log n) protected syndrome qubits, plus preparation/readout/reset/feedforward.
 G_clean=O(n²log n) bounded-arity trusted primitives in total.       (3)

CCZ has a constant-size exact Clifford+T decomposition, so either finite alphabet
has the same asymptotic statement. All-to-all bounded-arity trusted connectivity
is assumed; geometric routing is an extra cost. Dense f's coefficient list costs
Theta(n³) bits. Straight derivative compilation plus normal forms costs
O(n³log n) CLASSICAL work and O(n³+n²log n) storage; it can be reused for a fixed
kernel but is not free. The theorem is about trusted QUANTUM control count,
not total work, circuit description size or wall time.

Important matched comparator correction
A dense cubic f does NOT require Theta(n³) CCZ/T gates. Grouping f=sum_i x_i q_i
and reducing each quadratic q_i gives an O(n²) CCZ upper bound, with Clifford
volume separate. This construction charges the better quadratic normal form on
both sides and makes no literal-monomial non-Clifford lower-bound claim.

4. Robust lower bound for all-trusted implementation

Comparison contract: a fixed FINITE gate alphabet of bounded arity, including
fixed-basis measurements/preparations/reset, at most poly(n) addressed qubits,
and initially fixed PRODUCT |0> ancillas. Other resource-state preparation is
compiled and counted. Every quantum primitive/control
counts. Arbitrary f-dependent prepared quantum states, free coherent classical
lookup, arbitrary analog angles, hidden many-qubit gates and uncharged quantum
oracles are excluded. Ordinary classical computation/branching may depend on
the full known f. The HYBRID side has the additional noisy native resource (1),
whose own hardware/action/runtime are explicitly charged in section6.
Fix once, independently of f, a finite Kraus decomposition of each fixed
bounded-arity alphabet CPTP primitive, including reset/discard/noisy primitives
if allowed; bounded arity bounds its Kraus rank, so each refinement adds only
O(1) outcome bits per primitive. No f-dependent Kraus decomposition is allowed.

Among the2^M cubic f, most U_f require Omega(n³/log n) expected all-trusted
bounded-arity primitives to implement within any fixed epsilon<1/16 in normalized
diamond distance, even with adaptive measurements and classical branching.

Proof A: separated target Choi states
A nonzero multilinear polynomial h of degree<=3 has Boolean relative weight
at least1/8. Induction: write h=h0+x_n h1. If h1!=0, on each input where h1=1
one of the two x_n values gives1; induction on degree gives at least2^(n-3)
ones. If h1=0, double the lower-dimensional weight. Since h(0)=0 for differences
of cubic f, 1+h is nonzero too, giving relative weight<=7/8. Thus distinct f,g have

 |Tr(U_f†U_g)/2^n|<=3/4.

These are the inner products of their normalized pure Choi vectors.

Proof B: extract a short, accurate Kraus path
For an adaptive all-trusted channel epsilon-close to U_f, its normalized Choi
state has fidelity>=1-epsilon with |U_f>. Refine Kraus branches by any final
discarded ancilla's computational-basis outcomes. If p_alpha=||K_alpha||F²/2^n
and |k_alpha>=vec(K_alpha)/||K_alpha||F, then

 sum p_alpha(1-|<U_f|k_alpha>|²)<=epsilon.

If expected quantum primitive count on this Choi input is G, Markov gives
P(length>4G)<=1/4, while P(branch infidelity>2epsilon)<=1/2. Their complements
intersect with positive mass. Hence some nonzero branch has length<=4G and
normalized Choi fidelity>=1-2epsilon. A worst-case expected gate bound also bounds
this Choi-input expectation.

One normalized branch cannot have fidelity>=1-2epsilon with two distinct target
Choi vectors: after aligning phases their inner product would be at least
1-4epsilon>3/4. This contradicts Proof A. The overlap inequality follows by
decomposing each target into its component along the branch and an orthogonal
component, then applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the latter.

Proof C: count physical branch descriptions, not controller trees
A length-L branch over N<=poly(n) addressed qubits has at most
(C N^k)^(O(L)) descriptions for fixed arity k/alphabet C, including its measurement
outcomes. Final discarded ancilla refinement adds only O(L) outcome bits: only
O(L) simple ancillas could have been touched by L bounded-arity primitives.
Untouched ancillas remain fixed and convey no extra information. Classical
randomness/branching may choose any path but cannot increase the number of distinct
quantum paths of this length. Each such path identifies at most one target by
Proof B. Thus channels with expected G primitives cover at most
2^(O(G log n)) of the2^M targets. Taking G a sufficiently small constant times
M/log n leaves an exponentially small fraction covered. This proves the claim.

The same elementary argument gives an exact-unitary bound without target
separation: each nonzero Kraus branch of an exact unitary is proportional to that
unitary. Neither counting statement gives an efficient explicit hard f. This is
a generic-family resource lower bound, not a computational-hardness assumption
or a proof of advantage over CPC supplied with the SAME native device.

5. Full hybrid accuracy and a nonvacuous parameter contract

Let each trusted primitive deviate from its specified ideal channel by normalized
diamond error eta_l on the complete working register. Preparation, measurement,
reset, classical feedforward's quantum action and all idle-memory errors must be
included. Ordinary CPTP composition or a declared uniform extended-memory error
contract is required; arbitrary correlated errors cannot be omitted. Telescoping
the complete channels gives

 D_diamond(N_hybrid,U_f) <= P_bad+sum_l eta_l+mu_native
                          <=sum_(i<j) p_i p_j+sum_l eta_l+mu_native.(4)

Here mu_native also charges deviation from the specified native Hamiltonian/
noise model. For coherent Hamiltonian calibration error delta H(t), Duhamel gives
mu_native<=int||delta H(t)||dt/hbar; termwise integrated phase errors delta phi_e
give the conservative bound sum_e|delta phi_e|. For m_f active couplers, a
worst-case common per-term phase error must therefore be O(mu_native/m_f).
Representing that control tolerance needs O(log(m_f/mu_native)) bits per angle;
this is a precision specification, not an acquired physical calibration method.
Uncertain dephasing rates require certified upper bounds for p_i in (4).

For p_i<=c/n and total trusted/idle/native-model error<=b, this is at most c²/2+b. For example,
c=.1 and b=.01 give error<=.015<1/16. Thus the SAME constant-accuracy task in
section4 is achieved with (3), conditional on its explicit native and trusted
quality inputs. No vacuous statistical-error bound is being called success here.
This is an analytic resource regime, not an acquired family of hardware gates.
It demands average trusted errors O(1/G_clean) and the native rates below.

6. Native interaction, duration, noise and energy/action ledger

For the SPECIFIED projector Hamiltonian, let m_f=sum c_ijk and
D_f=max_i sum_(j,k containing i)c_ijk. It uses m_f three-body interactions and
has operator norm pi hbar m_f/tau and integrated interaction action pi hbar m_f.
These are Hamiltonian/action costs, not claims about consumed heat or work.
Actual preparation, cooling and reset energy remain device-dependent/unacquired.
Its per-site sum of term norms
divided by hbar is pi D_f/tau. If hardware bounds this local sum by J, then

 tau>=pi D_f/J.                                                   (5)

For generic dense f, m_f=Theta(n³) and D_f=Theta(n²); in fact
D_f>=3m_f/n. A bounded local sum therefore forces tau=Omega(n²/J), instead of
providing a constant-time dense native operation for free. At fixed tau, local
coupling sums grow as n². These are costs of this explicit implementation, not
universal lower bounds on every physical Hamiltonian realizing U_f.

For homogeneous fixed dephasing rate gamma, Gamma=gamma tau and p=(1-e^-2Gamma)/2.
To meet p<=c/n at the fastest allowed tau requires approximately
gamma<=c/(n tau)=O(J/n³). At fixed gamma and bounded J, p instead tends to1/2,
P_bad tends to1, and this single-flip recovery gives no scalable accuracy.
Protected ancillas must also remain coherent during tau and check execution;
their idle errors belong in b. Couplers, compilation, waiting, resets and source
quality are not inferred from the gate-count theorem. Total native work can
remain Theta(n³) or worse; no total-runtime or energy advantage is claimed.

7. Exact finite diagnostic and limitations

cubic_recovery.py independently constructs n=5 U_f, its pre/post matrices, and
Walsh syndrome branches for ALL32 phase-error patterns. It compiles each quadratic
derivative into an explicit F2 basis/CNOT sequence and checks every input bitstring.
The full corrected channel is checked through its Choi matrix, not a fitted input.
The integrated diagonal Lindblad generator is independently checked on every
matrix unit against (1). All1023 nonzero cubic-difference polynomials for n=5
are enumerated, confirming the target Choi-overlap bound<=3/4 in this finite case.
Recorded p=.02 gives bare diamond error .0960792032, corrected exact error
.0038423872, matching the numerical Choi trace distance. Three syndrome qubits
use48 CNOT,4 CCZ,6 CZ and75 counted trusted primitives including H/prep/readout/

advantage, hardware availability, scalable error quality or novelty.

The serious conditional consequence is a family whose accurate QUANTUM operation
can need asymptotically fewer TRUSTED elementary controls when a costly noisy
native non-Clifford resource is supplied. Established CPC/quantum-filter methods
tie the construction; standard code geometry, not a new Hamming theorem, provides
the syndromes. General X/Y/leakage faults, locally connected hardware, native
acquisition and practical net benefit remain outside the established result.
