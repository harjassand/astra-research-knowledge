## 1. Main theorem

Let \(\kappa\) be the unique root in \((2.95,2.96)\) of

\[
 m(X)=48X^6-2545X^4+29712X^2-97344.
\]

More precisely,

\[
 2.95538664457927<\kappa<2.95538664457928.
\]

### Theorem A

For every real \(\ell\),

\[
 \boxed{F_\ell\text{ is quasiconvex}
 \iff F_\ell\text{ is rank-one convex}
 \iff |\ell|\le\kappa.}
\]

For \(|\ell|<\kappa\), every bounded Lipschitz domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\), every constant \(A_0\), and every \(\varphi\in W^{1,4}_0(\Omega;\mathbb R^2)\) satisfy

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_\Omega [F_\ell(A_0+D\varphi)-F_\ell(A_0)]
 \ge \frac{1-|\ell|/\kappa}{6}
       \int_\Omega |D\varphi|^4.}
\]

Every member with \(11/4<|\ell|\le\kappa\) is not polyconvex.

These are homogeneous quartics invariant under left SO(2) and under a quarter-turn of the input coordinates. They are not invariant under all input rotations: on a fixed rank-one output subspace their restriction is \(s^4+4s^2t^2+t^4\), rather than a multiple of \((s^2+t^2)^2\). The rank-one restriction is not the square of a quadratic, so an invertible input-coordinate change cannot turn an individual isotropic quartic into this family.
