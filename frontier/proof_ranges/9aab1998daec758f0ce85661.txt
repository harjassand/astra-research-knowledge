## 6. Telegraph numbers and the square-wave competitor

With (x=e^{-2r\tau}), the length multiplicities give
\[
Q_E=(1+6x^2+x^4)/8,\quad Q_O=(x+x^3)/2,\quad Q_E-Q_O=(1-x)^4/8.
\]
At (r\tau=2), (x=e^{-4}), giving (Q_E=0.12525161103782373), (Q_O=0.009160891550543754), and difference (0.11609071948727998).

Now use a balanced square wave satisfying (\sigma(s-\tau)=-\sigma(s)). Each age choice contributes
\[
\sigma(t-C-j\tau-\tau X_{j+1})
=(-1)^{j+X_{j+1}}\sigma(t-C),
\]
so the four-sign product is ( (-1)^{6+\sum_jX_j}=(-1)^{\sum_jX_j}). The corrected product parity is exactly (+1) on E and (-1) on O, for any common delay, except at code discontinuities. Its period is (2\tau), its switch rate is (1/\tau), and its duty cycle is one half. The telegraph setting (r\tau=2) switches at mean rate (2/\tau) and is also balanced. With pure isotope levels, the count laws are
\[
P_E(K=0,2,4)=(1/8,6/8,1/8),\qquad P_O(K=1,3)=(1/2,1/2).
\]
With symmetric imperfect purities (a<1), the raw parity means become (+a^4) and (-a^4), so correction by (a^{-4}) still gives (+1) and (-1). The square wave strictly wins this known-τ synthetic signal contrast and has half the expected switch rate at the comparison setting. A continuous randomized phase or endpoint-excluding acquisition avoids discrete-time aliasing at ideal jumps; finite ramps and measured mixing require a new waveform kernel.

For C2, the rectangular-pulse comparator is exact: for a fixed pair separation (D), the output times at which both atom entries lie in a pulse of width (h) form the overlap of two length-(h) intervals, of measure ((h-D)_+). Averaging over histories gives integrated doubly-labelled flux divided by constant total flux (R) equal to (\mathbb E(h-D)_+=h\,\mathbb E(1-D/h)_+\). Therefore the C2 telegraph functional is not itself a new acquisition capability over a fully collected single pulse.
