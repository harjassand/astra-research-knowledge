## 5. Exactly when can coupling increase entropy?

The uniformization result becomes more informative when combined with a complete characterization of entropy stagnation.

For a probability law \(p\) on subsets of a finite ground set, define

\[
\Delta(p)=
\max_{\substack{X\sim p\\Y\sim p}}
\bigl[H(X\cup Y)-H(p)\bigr],
\]

where the maximum is over all joint distributions with those marginals.

The diagonal coupling \(X=Y\) shows that \(\Delta(p)\ge0\).

### Theorem 3

\[
\boxed{
\Delta(p)=0
\iff
\operatorname{supp}p\text{ is union-closed and }
p(A)\le p(B)\text{ whenever }A\subseteq B
\text{ in the support}.
}
\tag{10}
\]

### Proof: the stated conditions prevent entropy gain

Let \(Z=X\cup Y\), with law \(q\). Union closure puts \(Z\) in the support of \(p\), while monotonicity gives

\[
\log p(Z)\ge\log p(X).
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(q)+D(q\|p)
&=-\mathbb E\log p(Z)\\
&\le-\mathbb E\log p(X)\\
&=H(p).
\end{aligned}
\]

In particular, \(H(q)\le H(p)\).

### Proof: a failure of either condition gives an explicit improving coupling

Start with the diagonal coupling.

If \(A,B\) have positive mass but \(C=A\cup B\) has zero mass, subtract \(\epsilon\) from the cells \((A,A)\) and \((B,B)\), and add \(\epsilon\) to \((A,B)\) and \((B,A)\). Both marginals remain unchanged.

The union law loses mass \(\epsilon\) at each of \(A,B\) and gains \(2\epsilon\) at \(C\). Writing \(a=p(A)\), \(b=p(B)\), its entropy derivative is

\[
\log\frac{(a-\epsilon)(b-\epsilon)}{4\epsilon^2}.
\]

It is positive throughout

\[
0<\epsilon\le\frac{\min(a,b)}4.
\]

Thus a finite, explicitly specified swap increases entropy.

Alternatively, suppose \(A\subsetneq B\) but \(p(A)>p(B)\). The same coupling modification transfers output mass \(\epsilon\) from \(A\) to \(B\). Choosing

\[
0<\epsilon=
\min\left\{
\frac{p(B)}2,\,
\frac{p(A)-p(B)}4
\right\}
\]

moves the two probabilities toward equality without crossing it, and strictly increases entropy. ∎

Given an explicitly listed distribution, this also decides whether an entropy-improving coupling exists: inspect support closure and comparable pairs. No optimization over the full coupling polytope is required.

### Consequence for the decisive objective

Combining Theorems 2 and 3 gives

\[
\boxed{
\text{Frankl’s conjecture}
\iff
\left[
\max_i\Pr(i\in X)<\frac12
\Longrightarrow
\Delta(p)>0
\right],
}
\tag{11}
\]

with the law concentrated on \(\varnothing\) excluded.

This identifies the remaining difficulty precisely. It is not merely a failure to search a rich enough coupling class. **The distributions that resist every coupling are exactly the increasing-weighted union-closed distributions, and their universal abundance problem is equivalent to the original one.**

Equation (11) is an exact reformulation, not a proof of either side.
