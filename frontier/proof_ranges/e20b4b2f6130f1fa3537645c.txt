## 8. The precise unproved gate

**Cover-balance gate (strong version).** For every prime p and every irredundant affine hyperplane cover with normal matrix A,

    1_N belongs to C(ker A,x), for every x in (F_p^*)^N.         (G)

A weaker sufficient gate asks only for a common strictly positive vector; a still weaker one asks for positive fixed clocks separately in each coset. All remain unproved for general irredundant covers.

The known local versatility statement does not establish (G); Sections 5 and 6 rigorously exclude that shortcut. The counterexample cannot be passed off as a counterexample to (G), because it fails the cover assumption.

### Exact falsification format

For fixed p,A,x, failure of unit balance has a rational separating certificate y with

    y dot s(w) >= 0 for every w in D(ker A,x),
    sum_i y_i < 0.

To falsify (G), supply p,A, affine offsets, a proof that every point is covered, a private point for every hyperplane, the scaling x, and the separator with an exact verification against all legal supports. The presence of affine offsets and private witnesses is essential: a bare versatile subspace is not a valid counterexample.

### Conditional consequences if the gate is proved

Theorem 1 would immediately give r log p <= h_0 N for all irredundant affine hyperplane covers. A published reduction from prime-field hyperplane covers to irredundant coset covers of finite abelian groups would then give an exponential intersection-index bound. This is a conditional use of an existing reduction, not a new proof of it. [R1, Section 4]

There is also a direct conditional matrix-avoidance consequence. Suppose M_1,...,M_k are invertible n-by-n matrices over F_p and each coordinate of each M_j x has at most m forbidden values. If no x avoids all forbidden values, the forbidden-value hyperplanes contain an irredundant subcover of size N'. Among these normals, some matrix contributes at least N'/k rows counting repetitions, hence at least N'/(km) distinct independent rows. Their rank is therefore at least N'/(km). The cover inequality would imply log p <= h_0 km. Consequently, under (G), p > K_0^(km) guarantees an avoiding vector. This does not assert a polynomial-time algorithm or the stronger forbidden-set conclusions of the withdrawn paper.
