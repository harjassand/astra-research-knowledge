ALL POSITIVE SHARP PARAMETERS: A NON-SOS LOCAL INPUT-HEAT OBSTRUCTION
15 October 2026

Theorem and scope
-----------------
For every real r>0, define

  A=[[a,b],[c,d]], u=a^2+c^2, v=b^2+d^2, j=ad-bc,
  H=(u+v-2j)^2,
  F=3r/[8(r+2)]H+r(r-1)u^2/6+j^2+r u j.

There is no constant positive-definite 2 by 2 INPUT diffusion matrix D and
no closed two-form omega on matrix space with homogeneous-quadratic
coefficients such that the corrected compatible heat density

  P(A,z)=sum_(i,k=1)^2 D_ik D^2F(A)[B_i(z),B_k(z)]
          +omega_A(B1(z),B2(z))

is nonnegative for every A and every six-component compatible second jet z.

This theorem makes no SOS assumption. It also excludes forms with C2
coefficients near A=0 if the pointwise inequality is required there, by the
homogeneous Taylor reduction below. It concerns fixed positive input
diffusion and local two-form corrections. It does not exclude output-coupled
heat, other regularization paths, nonlocal corrections, higher jets, singular
angular corrections, integrated inequalities without a pointwise correction,
or quasiconvexity itself. In particular, no quasiconvexity counterexample has
been constructed by this obstruction.

The negative branch r<-2 has a separate successful exact heat certificate,
recorded in UNIVERSAL_REPORT.txt. None of those frozen artifacts is changed.

Portable exact proof
--------------------
POSITIVE_INPUT_HEAT_OBSTRUCTION.json contains a sparse equality dual with
48 nonzero rational multipliers. verify_positive_obstruction.py regenerates
the entire 411 by 62 necessary linear system directly from F, all sixty
quadratic form coefficients, both diagonal diffusion coefficients, acoustic
contacts and explicit symmetries. It reads no discovery script, pickle,
optimizer, floating-point spectrum, or source certificate.

The equality dual proves, for every r>0 with no exceptional r=1,

  P(e21,(0,1,0,0,0,0))
    = -r(r+1)(2r+1)d2/(r+2) < 0.

All dual denominators are nonzero over r>0. The signs of the dual multipliers
do not matter: they multiply necessary equality constraints. Positivity of
d2 and of the displayed scalar gives the contradiction.

1. Symmetry averaging preserves the allowed class
-------------------------------------------------
Assume such a positive corrected density exists. Average over left target
SO(2). This preserves D, closedness and positivity, and makes P invariant
under simultaneous target rotation of A and z.

F is also invariant under A -> P0 A P0 with P0=diag(1,-1). Averaging this
paired target/input reflection eliminates the diffusion cross term while
preserving positive definiteness. Thus D may be assumed diagonal with
d1,d2>0.

Put h=(2r+1)/3>0 and K=[[0,h],[1,0]]. Exact coefficient expansion gives

  F(A K)=h^2 F(A), K^2=h I.

Hence M=K/sqrt(h) is a determinant-minus-one input involution and
F(A M)=F(A). Under this input change, the second jet transforms by
Hessian -> M^T Hessian M and the diffusion transforms by D -> M D M^T.
The two-form correction transforms by det(M) times pullback under A -> A M;
it remains closed. Averaging with this involution preserves positivity and
makes the diagonal diffusion satisfy

  d1=h d2, equivalently 3d1=(2r+1)d2.

The paired reflection and input involution generate a finite group up to
the harmless input sign, so one may average over both simultaneously. Left
target averaging is preserved by these operations. No diffusion coefficient
is set to zero. Trace normalization is unnecessary for the equality dual.

For the unnormalized K action, the density invariance is

  P(A K, K^T z K)=h^3 P(A,z),

where K^T z K denotes its action on each scalar Hessian. This explains the
1/h^3 factor in the regenerated involution constraints. A target pi/4
rotation has entries 1/sqrt(2); multiplying both A and z by its unnormalized
integer matrix contributes the factor 1/4 in that symmetry constraint.

2. Acoustic flatness gives necessary linear constraints without SOS
------------------------------------------------------------------
Use compatible jets in the order

  z=(phi1_xx,phi1_xy,phi1_yy,phi2_xx,phi2_xy,phi2_yy),
  B1=(z0,z1,z3,z4), B2=(z1,z2,z4,z5).

The two exact acoustic curves are

  A_old(t)=[[1,-(r+1)t],[(r+1)t,1]], xi_old(t)=(t,1),
  D^2F(A_old(t))[e1 tensor xi_old(t),e1 tensor xi_old(t)]
    =(2/3)r(r+1)^2(r+2)t^4;

  A_new(t)=[[-3(r+1)t/(2r+1),(2r+1)/3],[1,(r+1)t]],
  xi_new(t)=(1,t),
  D^2F(A_new(t))[e1 tensor xi_new(t),e1 tensor xi_new(t)]
    =6r(r+1)^2(r+2)t^4/(2r+1)^2.

For the rank-one second jet z=e1 tensor xi tensor xi, B1 and B2 are
proportional, so every alternating form correction vanishes. Thus along
either curve, with any target rotation, P=O(t^4).

At a zero of a globally nonnegative smooth P, its full gradient in (A,z)
vanishes and its joint Hessian is positive semidefinite. If a curve through
that zero has P=O(t^4), its tangent has zero Hessian quadratic value and
therefore lies in the Hessian kernel. Consequently the derivative of the
full gradient along the curve also vanishes at t=0. These conditions are
linear in the sixty form coefficients and in d1,d2. The checker imposes
them for both curves and every target rotation by matching the homogeneous
p,q rotation coefficients. It also imposes all closedness coefficients.

3. An additional forced zero closes the non-SOS escape
-----------------------------------------------------
The old contact is (A,z)=(I,e2), where e2 means the z2 coordinate vector.
Let J=[[0,-1],[1,0]]. At this contact, the old flat acoustic tangent is

  v=((r+1)J, e1),

where e1 now denotes the z1 coordinate vector. The target-rotation zero
curve has tangent

  w=(J,e5).

Both v and w lie in the joint Hessian kernel of the nonnegative density.
Therefore

  v-(r+1)w=(0,zbar),
  zbar=(0,1,0,0,0,-(r+1))

lies in that kernel. Since P is homogeneous quadratic in z, its zz Hessian
at A=I is independent of the base z. It follows that

  P(I,zbar)=0.

Global nonnegativity now forces the four A derivatives of P at this new
zero to vanish. These four equations are the first four rows of the exact
system. They are not consequences of an SOS assumption.

This additional zero is decisive. Before imposing its A gradient, the
generic pointwise-positive candidate face had an extra form parameter that
the SOS face omitted. In that exploratory parameterization,

  grad_A P(I,zbar)=(2w59(r+2)/3,0,0,-2w59(r+2)/3),

so the new-zero gradient kills the escape. The final equality dual is
regenerated from the unreduced sixty-coefficient system and has no pole at
r=1; it does not rely on that generic parameterization.

4. Full exact equality dual
---------------------------
Variables are the sixty quadratic two-form coefficients, in the explicit
pair/monomial orders in the JSON, followed by d1,d2. The rows of E are:

  4 new-zero A-gradient equations;
  1 natural diffusion equation;
  all target pi/4 polynomial symmetry coefficients;
  all paired-reflection polynomial symmetry coefficients;
  all input-involution polynomial symmetry coefficients;
  all zero-gradient/gradient-tangent contact equations and closedness.

No source Gram coefficient is frozen, and no Gram matrix appears in this
system. The generated system is 411 by 62 and has rank 61 over Q(r), though
rank is not a premise for the dual certificate.

Let L be the row functional P(e21,(0,1,0,0,0,0)). The sparse rational vector
lambda in the JSON satisfies the universal exact identity

  E^T lambda=L^T+[r(r+1)(2r+1)/(r+2)] e_(d2).

Every admissible pointwise-positive corrected density must satisfy E vars=0.
Multiplying by the dual therefore gives

  L vars=-r(r+1)(2r+1)d2/(r+2)<0,

contradicting positivity. The checker reconstructs every row and verifies
every rational coefficient of this equality. The 48 nonzero multipliers
have denominators containing only positive powers of r+1 and r+2, times
positive numerical constants; none has a positive real pole.

The negative jet is compatible. For instance, the polynomial map
v(x,y)=(xy,x) has Dv(0)=e21 and second jet (0,1,0,0,0,0) at the origin.
This local negative value refutes the required pointwise inequality; it
does not by itself construct an integrated dissipation or Jensen violation.

5. Smooth local coefficient extension by homogeneous Taylor reduction
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose a closed two-form with C2 coefficients near A=0 makes the same
corrected input-heat density nonnegative for all nearby A and all z.

At A=0 the quartic heat density vanishes. The constant form pullback is a
nonnegative quadratic form in z, but it vanishes on every rank-one second
jet because B1 and B2 are proportional. These jets span the six-dimensional
jet space. A positive-semidefinite quadratic form vanishing on a spanning
set is zero, and independence of the six coordinate form pullbacks forces
the constant form coefficient to vanish.

Applying positivity at tA and -tA and dividing by |t| forces the linear
Taylor coefficient to vanish. Dividing by t^2 and taking t to zero gives
a pointwise-positive corrected density using the homogeneous quadratic
Taylor part. Closedness passes to this Taylor part. Homogeneity then
extends its positivity to all A. The quadratic obstruction above rules it
out. This step requires C2 regularity at A=0; singular angular corrections
are outside its scope.
