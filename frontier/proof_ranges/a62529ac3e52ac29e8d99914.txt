## 2. Main conditional theorem

Let $G$ be a countable group and suppose $a,b\in R=\mathbb F_2[G]$ satisfy

$$
ab=1,\qquad ba\ne1.
$$

On the compact group $X=\mathbb F_2^G$, with left shift and Haar measure $\mu$, define

$$
(T_u x)(g)=\sum_{t\in G}u_t x(gt),\qquad
A=T_a,\ B=T_b,\ C=I-BA=T_r,\ r=1-ba,
\quad K=\ker A=\operatorname{im}C.
$$

Then the map

$$
\Theta:X\longrightarrow K^{\mathbb N},\qquad
\Theta(x)=(CA^j x)_{j\ge0}
\tag{2.1}
$$

has the following properties.

1. It is a continuous equivariant group epimorphism and $\Theta_*\mu=m_K^{\mathbb N}$. In particular its layer coordinates are independent variables with Haar law on $K$.
2. Its kernel is $D=\bigcap_{n\ge0}B^nX$. This subgroup is infinite and has atomless Haar measure. No finite-generation assumption on the whole group is necessary.
3. Its fibers have coset-Haar conditional measures. Consequently, if a Borel set $E\subset X$ has $\Theta|_E$ injective, then $\mu(E)=0$. Thus this specified layer coding cannot become a measurable isomorphism by removing a null set.
4. The dual module of $D$ is cyclic, flat, nonprojective, and not finitely presented. The exact sequence

   $$
   0\longrightarrow D\longrightarrow X\xrightarrow{\Theta}K^{\mathbb N}\longrightarrow0
   \tag{2.2}
   $$

   has no continuous equivariant **group-homomorphic** section. This statement does not exclude arbitrary Borel sections or an unrelated measurable conjugacy.
5. If $\mathcal F=\Theta^{-1}\mathcal B(K^{\mathbb N})$, then

   $$
   \boxed{h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X,\mu\mid\mathcal F)
   =h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X,\mu).}
   \tag{2.3}
   $$

   For the family-197 premise, the common value is the inherited $s<\log2$. Revealing the entire independent layer sequence leaves this relative entropy unchanged. This calculation is not an entropy-additivity formula. A separate continuity argument below evaluates the intrinsic entropy of $D$.
6. Under the original family-197 torsion-free premise, the Haar action on $D$ is free and mixing, and

   $$
   \boxed{h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(D,m_D)
   =h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X,\mu\mid\mathcal F)
   =h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X,\mu)
   =h_{\sup}^{\mathrm{Rok}}(G)=s.}
   \tag{2.8}
   $$

   In particular, this proper linear limit subshift attains the finite supremum, although its dual is flat and nonprojective. This is an exact comparison, not a proof that its common value vanishes or is positive.

### Proof of the algebraic and measure statements

The convention above gives $T_uT_v=T_{uv}$. Thus $AB=I$, $C^2=C$, $AC=0$, $CB=0$, and $BA+C=I$. Iterating $x=Cx+BAx$ gives the compact-group isomorphism

$$
\Phi_n(x)=(A^n x,Cx,CAx,\ldots,CA^{n-1}x)
:X\longrightarrow X\times K^n
$$

with inverse

$$
(y,z_0,\ldots,z_{n-1})\longmapsto
B^n y+\sum_{j<n}B^jz_j.
$$

It carries Haar measure to product Haar. Every finite prefix of (2.1) is therefore onto and independent. The image of $\Theta$ is compact and contains every finite cylinder pattern, so it is all of $K^{\mathbb N}$. The first $n$ layer coordinates vanish precisely on $B^nX$; intersecting proves the kernel formula. Also $\Theta(Ax)$ is the left shift of $\Theta(x)$ in the layer index. This auxiliary one-sided shift is not the original $G$-action.

Identify $\widehat X$ with the left $R$-module $R$, using finitely supported characters. A coefficient pairing gives

$$
\widehat{T_u}(f)=fu.
$$

Write $S(f)=fb=\widehat B(f)$, $U_n=\ker S^n$, $U=\bigcup_nU_n$. The map $S$ is onto because $f=(fa)b$, and noninjective because $r\ne0$ and $rb=0$. Moreover

$$
U_n=Rr_n,\qquad r_n=1-b^na^n,
\qquad a^nb^n=1.
\tag{2.4}
$$

Indeed $r_nb^n=0$, and $fb^n=0$ implies $fr_n=f$. Each $r_n$ is idempotent, so $U_n$ is a direct summand of $R$, and $R/U_n\cong R$. The inclusions are all strict: $ra^n\in U_{n+1}\setminus U_n$, since $(ra^n)b^n=r\ne0$ and $(ra^n)b^{n+1}=rb=0$.

The annihilator of $B^nX$ is $U_n$. The annihilator of their intersection is their increasing union $U$. For completeness, in the binary case a character vanishing on $D$ must vanish on some $B^nX$: otherwise the nested nonempty compact sets $B^nX\cap\{\chi=1\}$ would have nonempty intersection, contradicting its vanishing on $D$. Hence

$$
D^\perp=U,\qquad \widehat D\cong R/U.
\tag{2.5}
$$

The union $U$ is not finitely generated. A finite generating set would lie in one $U_N$, forcing $U=U_N$ and contradicting strict growth. A useful refinement is

$$
p_j=b^jra^j,\qquad
r_n=\sum_{j<n}p_j,\qquad
U=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}Rp_j\cong\bigoplus_{j\ge0}Rr.
\tag{2.6}
$$

The $p_j$ are pairwise orthogonal idempotents; $Rp_j=Rra^j$, and multiplication by $a^j$, with inverse multiplication by $b^j$ on that summand, identifies it with $Rr$. Thus $U$ is itself projective and countably generated. The nonprojective module in the theorem is $R/U$.

Since $R/U=\varinjlim_nR/U_n$ is a filtered colimit of free modules, it is flat. It is cyclic. If it were finitely presented, the kernel of the canonical epimorphism $R\to R/U$ would be finitely generated. This follows, over any unital ring, from Schanuel's lemma applied to a finite presentation. If it were projective, that epimorphism would split and $U$ would be a direct summand of the cyclic module $R$, hence cyclic. Both alternatives contradict the preceding paragraph. The projective resolution $0\to U\to R\to R/U\to0$ also gives projective dimension exactly one.

First suppose $G$ is finitely generated. Every finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_2[G]$-module is finitely presented: choose a finite vector-space basis and impose its action relations for a finite symmetric generating set. In the presented quotient every group word reduces to the span of that basis; the quotient maps onto the specified module of the same dimension, proving equality. If $D$ were finite, $\widehat D=R/U$ would be finite-dimensional and therefore finitely presented, a contradiction.

For a general countable $G$, let $H\le G$ be generated by the finite supports of $a,b$. The same witness holds in $\mathbb F_2[H]$, and the finitely generated case proves $D_H$ infinite. Right convolution reads independently within the blocks $gH$. Choosing representatives for the blocks $gH$ gives a compact-group identification

$$
D_G\cong\prod_{gH\in G/H}D_H.
\tag{2.7}
$$

Thus $D_G$ is infinite as well. Haar measure on an infinite compact group is atomless. As noted in Section 1, this infinitude also follows from the 2021 limit-set theorems.

A continuous equivariant group section of $\Theta$ would split the dual sequence and make $U\subset R$ a direct summand. An $R$-linear projection from $R$ is determined by its value on $1$, so its image is cyclic. This contradicts non-finite generation of $U$, proving the specified nonsplitting. Finite local linear defining constraints for $D$ would likewise generate $D^\perp=U$, proving it is not of finite type. The 2021 stability theorem gives the same latter conclusion directly, because $B$ is injective and nonsurjective and its descending images never stabilize.

Finally, Haar disintegration for a compact-group quotient makes the conditional measure above $y\in K^{\mathbb N}$ the translate of $m_D$ on $\Theta^{-1}(y)$. An injective Borel subset meets each fiber in at most one point, which has conditional measure zero. Integrating gives $\mu(E)=0$. This is a genuine measure-level obstruction to this coding, beyond merely counting fixed or periodic points. It does not distinguish arbitrary Bernoulli shifts up to measurable conjugacy.

### Proof of the relative-entropy identity

Let $\mathcal F_n=\sigma(Cx,CAx,\ldots,CA^{n-1}x)$, where each variable takes values in the whole compact $G$-system $K$. These are invariant sigma-algebras and $\mathcal F=\bigvee_n\mathcal F_n$.

The finite splitting makes $K^n$ a factor of the Bernoulli shift $X$. Weak containment passes to factors. By Abért–Weiss, the Bernoulli shift is weakly contained in every free p.m.p. action, hence so is $K^n$. Seward, *Weak containment and Rokhlin entropy*, Theorem 1.2 (equivalently its Corollary 6.6 version), therefore gives

$$
h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X\times K^n\mid\mathcal B(K^n))
=h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X).
$$

Transporting this through $\Phi_n$ gives $h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X\mid\mathcal F_n)=h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X)$ for every $n$. The right side is finite because the coordinate partition of $X$ has entropy $\log2$. Seward, *Krieger's finite generator theorem II*, Corollary 7.2(ii), applies to the increasing invariant sigma-algebras and yields

$$
h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X\mid\mathcal F)
=\inf_n h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X\mid\mathcal F_n)
=h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X).
$$

This proof never uses intrinsic entropy monotonicity under factors, a sofic approximation, or product entropy additivity. No intrinsic entropy value of $K^{\mathbb N}$ is inferred.

### Proof that the residual tail attains the supremum

Put $M=\widehat D=R/U$. Flatness here means flatness as a left $R$-module. Fix $g\ne1$ and let $H=\langle g\rangle$. Torsion-freeness gives $H\cong\mathbb Z$. The ring $R$ is free as a left $Q=\mathbb F_2[H]$-module. Restriction therefore makes $M$ flat over $Q$: for right $Q$-modules $N$,

$$
N\otimes_Q M\cong(N\otimes_Q R)\otimes_R M,
$$

and both tensor steps preserve injections. As $Q$ is a domain, $M$ is $Q$-torsion-free. It is nonzero because $1\notin U$ (otherwise $b^n=0$ for some $n$, contradicting $a^nb^n=1$). Any nonzero $v\in M$ gives an injection $Q\to M$, $q\mapsto qv$. Dualizing supplies a Haar-preserving $H$-factor

$$
D\big|_H\longrightarrow\mathbb F_2^H.
\tag{2.9}
$$

Thus each infinite cyclic restriction has KS entropy at least $\log2$ and is essentially free. Taking the countable intersection of the sets where each nonidentity element acts without fixed points makes the $G$-action essentially free.

It is also mixing. If $gv=hv$ for $g\ne h$ and nonzero $v\in M$, then $(1-g^{-1}h)v=0$, contradicting the preceding Laurent-ring torsion-freeness. Every nonzero character therefore has a free $G$-orbit. Distinct character matrix coefficients vanish outside at most one group element; finite linear combinations and then density in $L^2(D)$ prove mixing. In particular the action is ergodic. This also describes its mean-zero Koopman representation as a direct sum of regular representations; no entropy positivity is inferred from that spectrum.

Now let $\mu_n$ be Haar measure on $B^nX$, considered as an invariant measure on the *same* full shift $X$. These measures converge weak-* to $m_D$. Indeed, for every finite coordinate set $F$, the projected subspaces $\pi_F(B^nX)$ decrease inside the finite vector space $\mathbb F_2^F$, so eventually stabilize. Their intersection equals $\pi_F(D)$ by compactness of the nested preimage sets. Their Haar marginals are uniform, and hence eventually equal the marginal of $m_D$. Cylinder functions determine weak-* convergence.

The map $B^n:X\to B^nX$ is an equivariant compact-group isomorphism with inverse $A^n$ on its image. Consequently $h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X,\mu_n)=s$ for every $n$. Alpeev–Seward, *Krieger III*, Corollary 5.6(ii), gives upper semicontinuity on invariant measures of a finite-alphabet shift. The finitely generated case applies directly; alternatively all these measures and the limit are ergodic, so the stated ergodic-domain version suffices. It follows that

$$
h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(D,m_D)
\ge\limsup_n h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(X,\mu_n)=s.
$$

The coordinate partition makes $h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(D,m_D)\le\log2<\infty$. Because $D$ is free and ergodic, the definition of $h_{\sup}^{\mathrm{Rok}}(G)$ gives the reverse inequality

$$
h_G^{\mathrm{Rok}}(D,m_D)\le h_{\sup}^{\mathrm{Rok}}(G)=s,
$$

where the last equality is the inherited strict bound combined with Seward's binary min formula. This proves (2.8). Notice that no entropy comparison for the inclusion $D\subset X$ was assumed: the lower bound is a continuity statement about invariant measures, and the upper bound is the defining supremum over free ergodic actions.

The algebraic infinitude/non-finite-type facts have identified prior art. The exact maximizing-tail comparison (2.8) and the relative-layer application (2.3) were developed and separately audited in this run; no priority claim beyond that bounded search is made.
