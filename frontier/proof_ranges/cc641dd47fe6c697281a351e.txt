## Adaptive two-query obstruction

Take x=y=1 and independent bounds

    a in [1/4,33/4], b in [8,72], c in [1/8,5/8].

After a first exact query a=t, let Q_b(t) and Q_c(t) be the worst completion
ratios after querying b or c once more. The supplied one-step theorem computes
each Q exactly by its two possible endpoint answers. Optimal continuation is
V(t)=min(Q_b(t),Q_c(t)). Exact values are:

| First answer t | Q_b(t) | Q_c(t) | V(t) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/4 | 588111/258635 | 86903/68471 | 86903/68471 |
| 5/4 | 1281775/961419 | 40843/30603 | 1281775/961419 |
| 33/4 | 130549/125449 | 1545975/806647 | 130549/125449 |

Thus V(5/4)=1.3332116382... exceeds both endpoint values, whose maximum is
1.2691942574.... This is possible because minimization over the next query
choice does not preserve the coordinate shape in the one-step proof.

The failure also affects the initial minimax value, rather than only an
arbitrarily selected first query. Endpoint-only two-step dynamic programming
gives worst values 86903/68471, 588111/258635, and 1545975/806647 for first
queries a,b,c respectively. It therefore selects a and reports 1.2691942574....
For the actual continuous-answer game, the interior answer above gives lower
bound 1281775/961419 for first query a. The endpoint values already give larger
lower bounds for first queries b and c. Consequently the true deterministic
two-query minimax ratio is at least 1.3332116382..., strictly above the
endpoint-only answer. The exact optimum, randomized policies, and average-case
performance are not asserted.

The source's restriction to one-step minimax is essential. A valid global
algorithm needs continuous-outcome analysis or a sound approximation with an
error bound; it cannot simply recurse on endpoint answers.
