## 1. Exact claim audited

Let `G=(V,E)` be countable with finite maximum degree at most `D`, and let `Y` be binary. For every `v`, require

`P(Y_v=0 | sigma(Y_w: w outside N[v])) <= q` almost surely.                 (A1)

For `0<q<=2^(-(D+1))`, set `t=q^(1/(D+1))`, `r=alpha=1-t`. Then `Y` dominates iid Bernoulli density

`beta=r alpha=(1-q^(1/(D+1)))^2`.                                          (A2)

No independence between adjacent variables, stationarity, identical marginals, positive association or FKG inequality is used. The desired-density formulation should be read with `rho in [0,1)`, as usual for a Bernoulli parameter; the source's bare phrase `rho<1` has that implicit domain.

## 2. Independently reconstructed masking step

Introduce iid masks `X_v~Bernoulli(r)`, independent of the entire `Y` field, and define `Z_v=X_vY_v`. Prove, by induction on finite `|W|`, for each `v notin W` and each positive-probability pattern `s`,

`P(Y_v=1 | Z_W=s)>=alpha`.                                                 (A3)

For `W` empty, (A1) followed by expectation gives `P(Y_v=1)>=1-q>=1-t`. This also holds when `D=0`, since then `q=t`.

Fix `W,v,s` for the induction step. Partition the neighboring conditioned sites into `N0` and `N1` according to their zero and one values; put `M=W\N(v)` and `F={Z_M=s_M}`. Because `v notin W`, `M` is outside the **closed** neighborhood. Write `l=|N0|`, `m=|N1|`; then `l+m<=D`.

The event `Z_N1=1` is exactly `X_N1=1,Y_N1=1`. Its independent mask factor `r^m` cancels from the numerator and denominator before either is bounded. Therefore

`P(Y_v=0 | Z_W=s)`

` = P(Y_v=0,Z_N0=0,Y_N1=1,F) / P(Z_N0=0,Y_N1=1,F)`

` <= P(Y_v=0,F) / [t^l P(Y_N1=1,F)]`.                                    (A4)

The numerator was enlarged by dropping conditions. The denominator was decreased by requiring `X_N0=0`, which forces `Z_N0=0`; these masks are independent of `(Y_N1,Z_M)`. This direction of the denominator inequality is correct.

No denominator here is circularly assumed positive. The initial positive probability of `Z_W=s` implies positive probability of `F` and of `Y_N1=1,F`. Every earlier prefix of that latter event is positive as well. Requiring its independent masks to equal one retains positivity because `r>0`.

## 3. Why the weak conditional premise survives distant masking

Let `F_out=sigma(Y_w:w outside N[v])`. Independence of `X_M` from the entire `Y` field gives

`E[1{Y_v=0} | F_out join sigma(X_M)]`

`     = E[1{Y_v=0} | F_out] <= q` almost surely.                            (A5)

Since `F` is measurable in that joined sigma field, multiplying by `1_F` and taking expectation yields

`P(Y_v=0,F)<=q P(F)`.                                                     (A6)

This is the crucial conditional step. It does **not** condition the weak-dependency premise on nearby `Y_N1`, a future event, an actual microscopic input state, or a state selected from a trajectory. It conditions only on a function of the permitted distant variables and independent masks. Thus (A6) is valid even when the field has arbitrary correlations between neighbors.

## 4. Why earlier Y-one conditions may be replaced by Z-one conditions

Enumerate `N1={w1,...,wm}`. For each prefix `A={w1,...,w(j-1)}`, the masks `X_A` are independent of all `Y` and of `Z_M`; `A` and `M` are disjoint. Hence

`P(Y_wj=1,Z_A=1,F)=r^|A| P(Y_wj=1,Y_A=1,F)`,

`P(Z_A=1,F)=r^|A| P(Y_A=1,F)`.

Cancelling the positive common factor gives the exact equality

`P(Y_wj=1 | Y_A=1,F)=P(Y_wj=1 | Z_A=1,F)`.                                (A7)

The conditioning set `M union A` has cardinality at most `|W|-1`, so (A3) from the induction hypothesis applies. The chain rule then gives

`P(Y_N1=1 | F)>=alpha^m`.                                                 (A8)

No claim that conditioning on masked and unmasked **zeros** is equivalent is made; that would be false. The proof uses equality only for one conditions, where the mask event factors exactly.

Combining (A4), (A6), and (A8),

`P(Y_v=0 | Z_W=s)<=q/(t^l alpha^m)`

`                         <=q/t^D=t`.                                    (A9)

Indeed `t<=1/2` implies `alpha=1-t>=t`, so `t^l alpha^m>=t^(l+m)>=t^D`. This proves the induction. If `N1` is empty its product is one; if all conditioned sites are distant, (A6) alone proves the needed bound. The argument does not require shrinking `M` when there are no neighboring sites.

Finally, `X_v` is independent of `(Y,Z_W)` because `v notin W`, so

`P(Z_v=1 | Z_W=s)=r P(Y_v=1 | Z_W=s)>=beta`.                              (A10)

## 5. Countable construction and null histories

For finite `V`, enumerate its vertices; for countably infinite `V`, choose an enumeration `v1,v2,...`. Let

`p_n(h)=P(Z_vn=1 | Z_v1,...,Z_v(n-1)=h)`

for every positive-probability prefix `h`. On zero-probability prefixes choose any value in `[beta,1]`. With independent uniforms `U_n`, set the sequential copy

`Z'_vn=1{U_n<=p_n(Z'_v1,...,Z'_v(n-1))}`, `B_vn=1{U_n<=beta}`.

Induction on prefix length shows that `Z'` has precisely the finite-dimensional laws of `Z`. Cylinder sets determine the countable binary-product law, so its full law is that of `Z`. The `B` variables are iid Bernoulli(`beta`), and `B<=Z'` coordinatewise. Null histories cannot invalidate the coupling: they remain null under the correctly generated law; the selected values also satisfy the inequality if such a history is considered formally.

To glue `Y` to this construction, the original masking experiment supplies a joint law `mu(dy,dz)` supported on `y>=z`. Countable binary products are standard Borel spaces, so a regular conditional kernel `mu(dy|z)` exists. Sample `Y'` from that kernel given `Z'`, using independent auxiliary randomness. The `(Y',Z')` marginal is the original masking joint law, hence `Y'>=Z'>=B` almost surely. Countability lets the simultaneous coordinate inequalities be intersected with probability one. This proves the claimed domination without an unjustified infinite sequential conditioning step or a consistency assumption about separately chosen finite couplings.

At `q=0`, each marginal has `Y_v=1` almost surely by (A1); countability makes the whole field all ones almost surely. To obtain any `rho in [0,1)`, require `q<=(1-sqrt(rho))^(D+1)` as well as the displayed small-q condition, and if necessary thin the iid `beta` field independently. Thus the qualitative domination parameter approaches one as `q` approaches zero at fixed `D`.

## 6. Attaching the comparison to the original Poisson primitives

The receiver should retain the actual primitive fields, rather than merely replace its flags with an equal-in-law sequential copy. The preceding proof supplies a joint domination law `Q(dy,db)` with `b<=y`. Disintegrate it in the **other** direction as `Q(db|y)`. Given the existing primitive slab field `P_n` and its native flags `Y_n=F_n(P_n)`, sample the iid comparator from `Q(db|Y_n)` with independent auxiliary randomness. This preserves the full native primitive marginal and its correlation with its flags; its `(Y_n,B_n)` marginal is the desired domination coupling.

Choose that auxiliary sampling independently in each slab. Since native primitive slabs are independent, the resulting comparator slabs are independent. Each comparator slab has the same iid spatial Bernoulli(`beta`) marginal, hence the entire comparator is iid in space and slab index. Its complement is the iid death field needed by the memory proof. The coupling is an analysis construction, not an implemented controller.

This is an explicit elaboration of the source's coupling/gluing paragraphs, not a new theorem assumption or a substantive repair. It also shows that no coupling of torus stationary states is being asserted; the receiving proof still performs domination in infinite space and uses its separate stopped replay for the torus.

## 7. Literal fixed-primitive receiving premise

For one sector and slab, let the native flag at `z` be measurable with respect to independent primitive increments indexed by a deterministic set `S_z`. Every proposal owner whose **complete boundary-reset box** can meet the birth guard must belong to that set; include all correction-label streams used by the certificate and ordered-chain flags. The birth box radius and owner assignment are fixed before the rate threshold is chosen.

If each `S_z` is contained in the coarse radius-`g` primitive guard, connect targets within infinity distance `2g`. For a fixed target `z`, its `S_z` is disjoint from the **union** of primitive sets `S_w` at all nonneighbors. Independence of the primitive family implies independence of the corresponding sigma fields; for a countably infinite union this follows by extending cylinder-event independence with a monotone-class argument. Thus

`Y_z is independent of sigma(Y_w:w outside N[z])`.

A uniform flag marginal `P(B_z=1)<=q` therefore implies exactly (A1). Pairwise independence of distant flags alone would be insufficient. For example, two independent fair bits and their xor are pairwise independent, while the xor is determined by the joint pair; the source relies on disjoint primitive supports, which excludes this obstruction.

In the supplied pair receiver, `g=R+3` is valid after the full reset radius `q_reset` is charged and `b>=q_reset`. The already frozen `GUARD_RATE_THEOREM.md` proves the owner inclusion and the incident rate bound

`P(birth-bad)<= (nu_*/kappa) Cb[(2R+5)b+2q_reset]^4`.

The graph degree bound is then `D<=(4g+1)^4-1`, independent of `b,L,T`. Merely bounding the number of qubits does not bound `g`. Nonlocal pair supports, adapted spatial patches, state-dependent proposal locations or flags reading actual input states do not satisfy this proof's fixed-sigma-field promise.

The service flag must remain the source's **fresh deterministic halfspace certificate truncated at the current slab endpoint**. A full independent service duration may be used to derive its tail, but its measurable failure event must not read actual clearance times carried from a prior slab or future Poisson increments. The supplied flag definitions have that truncation and fixed guard; the domination lemma does not manufacture it.
