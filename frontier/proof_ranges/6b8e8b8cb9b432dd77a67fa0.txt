7. Physical costs, calibration and escape routes
No powerful filter is hidden
The positive certificate uses ordinary waiting under \(H\), one-qubit operations and a supplied fresh vacuum bath. There is no imaginary-time implementation, filter normalization or uncharged heralding failure.
If vacuum reset costs \(C_{\rm reset}\) and local preparation/readout costs \(C_{\rm local}\), its resource bill includes
\[
N_{\mathrm{exp}}(C_{\rm reset}+C_{\rm local})
\]
plus native evolution time \(N_{\mathrm{exp}}h\). Vacuum preparation is an explicit source assumption, not a universal free laboratory operation.
The lower bound grants these resources free and still applies.
Finite coefficients suffice—but not constant noise
The note proves an inverse-polynomial lower bound on every arm hopping,
\[
|J_j|\ge\frac{\eta}{300d^3}.
\]
Matched dyadic rounding with step
\[
q\le
\min\left\{
\frac{\eta}{96T},
\frac{\eta}{1200d^3}
\right\}
\]
preserves the predictive separation, the gap, the distant support of the difference and exact reflection isospectrality.
Thus only
\[
O(\log(d/\eta))
\]
bits per bounded coefficient are required.
However, the joining bond is inverse-polynomially weak, and the system is deliberately inhomogeneous. This is not a theorem for uniformly bounded-below hopping, fixed-strength disorder, finite-temperature initial states or persistent decoherence.
For the elementary positive certificate, an implementation bias of at most \(\beta\) in rejection probability gives
\[
E_T\le\frac{2T}{h}\sqrt{p_U+\beta},
\]
where \(p_U\) is a statistical upper confidence bound. Certifying error \(\varepsilon\) therefore requires \(\beta=O(h^2\varepsilon^2/T^2)\) by this route.
The tolerant lower bound already holds with ideal noiseless experiments.
One additional preparation location defeats the pair
Allow preparation of \(|+\rangle\) at the distant endpoint and one-qubit readout there. The onsite difference is at least \(1/2\).
A short-time expansion shows a difference in rotating-frame \(Y\) expectations of at least
\[
\frac t2-4t^2\ge\frac t4,
\qquad 0<t\le1/16.
\]
For \(t_0=\min\{\tau,1/32\}\),
\[
O\!\left(t_0^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)\right)
\]
shots identify the supplied binary alternative, independently of \(d\) and \(T\).
Alternatively, allowing one coherent run of duration \(t_*=\Theta(T)\) reveals the bad prediction directly.
These are exact boundaries: the theorem concerns limited spatial intervention and short fresh experiments—not an intrinsic inability to learn the system by any means.
