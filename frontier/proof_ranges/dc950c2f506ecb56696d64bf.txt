## 11. Decisive remaining theorem for dense cooperative transport

The demonstrated density interval is not the dense glassy regime. At large p the current small-block gate is too rare. For example, at p=0.80 its exact probability is only 0.08380672; asymptotically gamma(1-q)=16q^3+O(q^4). Better numerical optimization of this same module cannot change that fact. A demand-compatibility search, which allowed componentwise source balancing rather than requiring a prescribed swap path, returned the same gate in the tested cases; this is a numerical diagnostic, not an independently verified global optimality theorem.

The next consequential mathematical target is a **compressed cooperative module theorem**. For a specified dense p, construct a port-independent reservoir predicate g_L and a legal source–sink module satisfying (4), with all of the following certified as part of a compact representation:

- an explicit gate probability high enough for a proved closed-cluster tail, for example b<sqrt(5)-2 under the present completion;
- exact legality and source balance for every configuration represented by the module, including bad intermediate gate states;
- an explicit weighted energy C_L(p), including all congestion and information loss;
- construction and verification costs that avoid enumerating 2^(2L squared) configurations, together with enough quantitative strength to yield informative transport bounds.

This is not a request to assume a corrector or spectral gap. These are the finite inputs to the proved theorem above. Once supplied, global admissibility, response normalization and a completion tail follow constructively.

Good-box, frameability and cooperative-droplet mechanisms already supply important geometric ingredients and asymptotic comparisons [1,2]. Their existence is not new. The remaining task is to turn appropriate mechanisms into a compact exact source–sink/energy certificate with useful costs; no claim is made that the literature lacks every ingredient of such a theorem.

One can separately tighten the bad-cluster counting/completion bound at moderate density. That improves the current certificate but does not by itself fix the rarity of the small-block gate in dense systems. A larger finite graph without structural compression would merely move the exponential cost elsewhere.

## 12. Comparison and novelty status

Shapira's Thomson principle and legal path lifting [3] are established prior art. Shapira's bulk KA lower bound [1] already uses coarse percolation and quantifies multistep information loss. Ertul–Shapira [2] constructs port-independent coarse connectivity and frameable/good-box mechanisms for tagged diffusion; those geometric ideas are relevant, but tagged and bulk diffusion are not being identified. Arita–Krapivsky–Mallick [4] already supplies specialized finite-dimensional primal calculations.

This investigation does not claim to have invented any of those facts or to supersede their high-density scope. No external optimized implementation was benchmarked head to head, and no claim is made that the displayed numerical bounds are the best known.

The candidate methodological contribution is the combined **finite defect-module compiler, frozen-reservoir lifting identity, explicit cluster-quotient completion and finite-radius energy certificate**, instantiated and checked end to end for hard cooperative KA. The exact finite-module energy replaces a guessed routing/congestion cost in this implementation. The chordless-path automaton supplies a completely numerical nonlocal tail. Independent priority review remains necessary.

The linked Astra and OpenAI repositories were searched selectively for relevant transport/percolation capital. No retrieved repository result is used as an unverified proof dependency. There is no claimed quantum-transport transfer.
