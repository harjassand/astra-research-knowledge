5. Two further computational consequences
Complete stopped projective measurement in \(\widetilde O(\sqrt m)\) queries
For arbitrary orthogonal projectors \(P_1,\ldots,P_m\), let
\[
Q_j=I-P_j.
\]
The full first-failure isometry is
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W x
=
|\mathrm{pass}\rangle P_m\cdots P_1x
+
\sum_{j=1}^{m}|j\rangle
Q_jP_{j-1}\cdots P_1x.
}
\tag{29}
\]
Given the coherent indexed reflection oracle
\[
\sum_j|j\rangle\langle j|\otimes(2P_j-I),
\]
the construction implements \(\mathcal W\), not merely its pass block, using
\[
\boxed{\widetilde O(\sqrt m)}
\tag{30}
\]
queries. It works on one unknown input and preserves entanglement with a reference.
Here is the remaining reduction. Pad to a power-of-two number \(M\) of projectors. Choose
\[
\lambda=O(M\log(M/\eta)),
\]
and repeat the resolvent associated with
\[
B=\sqrt{\lambda/n}\,Q_j,\qquad H=0
\]
for \(n/M\) microsteps per projector, with \(n\) a sufficiently large power of two.
One group has survivor operator
\[
S_j=P_j+qQ_j,\qquad
q=(1+\lambda/n)^{-n/M}\le\eta/(8M).
\]
Its physical and defect amplitudes are proportional to the same vector \(Q_jx\). Their within-group time dependence is a known geometric vector, independent of the input. A known rotation combines the two event types, and inverse preparation of that geometric vector erases the microstep label.
The resulting group isometry is exactly
\[
x\longmapsto
|\mathrm{pass}\rangle(P_j+qQ_j)x
+
|\mathrm{fail}\rangle\sqrt{1-q^2}\,Q_jx.
\]
Its distance from the hard-projector isometry is at most \(\sqrt2q\). Telescoping the stopped sequence gives total error at most \(M\sqrt2q\), without requiring commutation.
Meanwhile,
\[
\sqrt n\,\beta=\sqrt\lambda,
\]
giving (30).
This is optimal in its leading dependence on \(m\): scalar projectors \(P_j\in\{0,I\}\) turn the instrument into unstructured search for the first failure, which requires \(\Omega(\sqrt m)\) queries. arXiv
This statement concerns coherent selector access. It does not make the cost of loading an arbitrary classical list of unrelated reflection circuits disappear.
Full evolution with a bounded number of irreversible losses
A fixed-budget version stops and flags overflow on the \((K+1)\)-st physical jump. Using \(O(K+1)\) time blocks gives worst-case cost
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left(
K+1+g\sqrt{T(K+1)}+h_0T
\right).
}
\tag{31}
\]
If the true process has at most \(K\) jumps, this simulates the entire channel, including its binned jump record, without overflow.
A sufficient structural condition is a known grading
\[
\mathcal H=\bigoplus_{e=0}^{K}\mathcal H_e
\]
such that \(H(t)\) preserves each grade and every \(L_\mu(t)\) lowers the grade by exactly one. Then \(L_\mu^\dagger L_\mu\) preserves grade, no-jump evolution preserves grade, and each physical jump decreases it.
Arbitrary noncommutation within these constraints is allowed. For \(H=0\) and fixed \(K\), the time dependence is square-root rather than linear.
More generally, if the probability of more than \(K\) jumps is uniformly at most \(p\), discarding the record gives full-channel error at most
\[
\varepsilon+2p.
\]
The factor is \(p\), not \(\sqrt p\), because this comparison is between the corresponding positive, trace-decreasing trajectory subchannels.
