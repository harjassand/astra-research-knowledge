## Relative precision differs from fixed additive width

The hidden-gate audit proves an endpoint rule when every rate measurement
returns an interval of bounded additive width. That rule does not transfer
sharply to a multiplicative uncertainty interval. Take masses x=2,y=8,

    a in [1,2], b in [1/8,513/8], c in [8,10].

Query b with legal interval [s,4s], s in [1/8,513/32]. The completion ratio is

    R(s)=2(s+216)(12s+5)/((2s+141)(9s+8)).

It is 89908/41245 at the left interval, 1421100/408889 at the right interval,
and 5698/1581 at the interior interval [6,24]. The latter is strictly larger
than both endpoints. The independent checker directly evaluates each ratio
from the grounded matrix, then verifies the displayed rational formula.
The audit gives a corrected finite candidate rule using stationary points of
this degree-two rational function. A coarser additive-width reduction remains
valid if the relative sensor implies an absolute width bound, but it need not
be sharp. Actual confidence-interval coverage and acquisition cost remain
separate physical/statistical obligations.
