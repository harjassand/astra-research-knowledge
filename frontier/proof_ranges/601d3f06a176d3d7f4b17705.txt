## 2. The mechanism: an exact gradient-exchange construction

The main mathematical obstacle is that an arbitrary computational update is not generally a gradient. Its derivatives need not satisfy the symmetry constraints imposed by a scalar potential.

The construction resolves that obstacle explicitly.

### A scalar potential that computes in one direction and copies in the other

Write \(\sigma(a)=\max\{0,a\}\), and define
\[
p(a,b)=\frac14\left[
\sigma(a+b)^2-\sigma(a-b)^2
-\sigma(b)^2+\sigma(-b)^2
\right].
\]

Direct differentiation gives the exact identities
\[
\nabla p(a,0)=(0,\sigma(a)),
\qquad
\nabla p(0,b)=(b/2,0).
\]

Also,
\[
p(a,0)=p(0,b)=0.
\]

Thus the same scalar function supplies a nonlinear forward transfer and a linear reverse transfer. There is no stop-gradient operation or independently prescribed “gradient” vector field.

### Completing the reverse transfer without changing the computation

Start with any rational recurrent ReLU system
\[
u_{t+1}=R(u_t):=\sigma(Wu_t),
\qquad u_0\ge0,
\]
where \(W\in\mathbb Q^{m\times m}\).

Define
\[
D=
\begin{bmatrix}
W\\-I
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
C=
\begin{bmatrix}
I&W-2I
\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
CD=2I.
\]

The extended map
\[
A(u)=C\sigma(Du)
=\sigma(Wu)+(W-2I)\sigma(-u)
\]
agrees with the desired computation whenever \(u\ge0\), because the extra term vanishes there.

But the extension satisfies
\[
A(u)-A(-u)=2u.
\]

That is the useful operation: **modify the map outside the valid computational region to supply the reciprocal structure required by an actual gradient, while leaving the intended computation unchanged.**

Let \(d_j^\top\) denote row \(j\) of \(D\), and set
\[
H(u,v)=
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{2m}
C_{ij}\,p(d_j^\top u,v_i).
\]
The scalar identities now give
\[
\boxed{
\nabla H(u,0)=(0,A(u)),
\qquad
\nabla H(0,v)=(v,0).}
\]

The first transfer performs the computation. The second copies its output back into the original register.

### Making the objective strongly convex

An explicit global bound is
\[
\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla H)\le K,
\]
where one may take
\[
K=
\max\left\{
1,\,
\sum_{i,j}|C_{ij}|
\bigl(\|d_j\|^2+2\bigr)
\right\}.
\]

Choose a dyadic rational
\[
\rho=2^{-k},
\qquad
\rho K\le\varepsilon,
\]
and define
\[
f(z)=\frac12\|z\|^2-\rho H(z).
\]

The Lipschitz bound immediately supplies the strong-convexity and smoothness bounds:
\[
f\text{ is }(1-\varepsilon)\text{-strongly convex},
\qquad
\operatorname{Lip}(\nabla f)\le1+\varepsilon.
\]

Ordinary gradient descent performs the exact cancellation
\[
z-\nabla f(z)=\rho\nabla H(z).
\]

Consequently, starting from \(z_0=(u_0,0)\),
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
z_{2t}&=\bigl(\rho^{2t}R^t(u_0),\,0\bigr),\\
z_{2t+1}&=\bigl(0,\,\rho^{2t+1}R^{t+1}(u_0)\bigr).
\end{aligned}}
\]

**Two genuine gradient-descent updates simulate one recurrent-network update**, with a fully specified geometric rescaling.

Moreover, every constructed iterate lies on one of the two coordinate subspaces, where \(H\) vanishes. Hence
\[
\boxed{f(z_t)=\frac12\|z_t\|^2}
\]
throughout the simulated trajectory.

The loss values on the trajectory are exactly quadratic, while its transverse gradients perform the computation.

### The signed terms can be removed

The intermediate expression contains negative squared ReLU terms. They are not necessary in the final loss.

For \(a>0\),
\[
-a\sigma(\ell)^2=a\sigma(-\ell)^2-a\ell^2.
\]

Apply this identity to every negative term. The negative contributions become a quadratic correction. The explicit bound above ensures that the remaining quadratic matrix is still positive definite.

A rational \(LDL^\top\) decomposition then expresses that quadratic as positive rational multiples of squares of rational linear forms. Finally,
\[
\ell^2=\sigma(\ell)^2+\sigma(-\ell)^2.
\]

This yields the nonnegative squared-ReLU representation stated in the theorem. The whole matrix-to-objective construction has polynomial description and rational bit complexity.
