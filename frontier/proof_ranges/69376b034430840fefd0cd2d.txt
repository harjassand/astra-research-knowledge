## 11. Evidence and limitations

Executed exact tests include:

- 30 one-dimensional prefix, 15 multidimensional source-divergence, 15 legal-routing and 75 pointwise identities;
- matrix-valued TT telescoping in dimensions 2,3,5; 36 exact divergence and 120 block-automaton suffix identities; exact routed energy contractions and rank-factor-two checks;
- dyadic multiscale completions, including dimension four at length scale 2^40;
- 40 finite Majorana commutator comparisons; 514 independently expanded coefficient comparisons for truly infinite-tail inputs; current-source and harmonic identities;
- exact infinite Hodge energy bounds, 20 face/gradient orthogonality checks, and a separately expanded whole-residual identity;
- exact compact sparse-baseline bounds and a broad full-interacting-model nonlocal certificate;
- 60 finite floating Richardson contraction diagnostics and an exact rank-one-RHS/full-rank-solution counterexample.

These are finite algorithm/algebra checks plus analytic proofs. They are not formal verification, external review, laboratory evidence, or a production high-degree transport engine. The whole result is retained as a candidate research advance with explicit scope.

## 12. Prior research and provenance

Tensor trains, tensor-network contractions, separated exponential sums, prescribed-divergence constructions, Hodge projection, Galerkin methods and inexact Richardson iterations are established tools. Historical novelty of this particular rank-preserving exclusion completion and its use in this quantum response problem remains unestablished.

Bachmayr and Kazeev [R1] explicitly analyze representation ill-conditioning distinct from ordinary matrix conditioning, construct low-rank multilevel preconditioners and give convergence/complexity results for elliptic problems. Thus a short tensor expression must not be treated as a stable efficient inverse. Bachmayr and Dahmen [R2] give adaptive convergent tensor solvers with complexity results under specified solution rank/sparsity structure; detecting such structure is not a new general idea here. Their results do not by themselves prove the rank theorem for the present constrained quantum operator.

FDAOE [R3] already uses fermionic weight and preserves long quadratic strings; this representation choice is credited. It applies artificial damping/extrapolation in its own physical setting. Operator backflow [R4] is a strong transport-specific comparator. No head-to-head superiority over those methods is established by the present Hodge benchmark.
