\subsection{Exact model and a scalar matched-baseline obstruction}

Consider a prepared target and one initially resonant accepting transition.
Its coupling is $J$ and its memory kernel is $e^{-\kappa s}$, $\kappa>0$.
All frequencies are angular frequencies. A differential level shift $q(t)$
gives $u(t)=e^{-i\theta(t)}$, $\theta'=q$. Assume a stationary or long-time
averaged autocorrelation $C$, $C(0)=1$, and a mean-square derivative with
$\langle q^2\rangle\le A^2$.
Bochner's theorem and Parseval give a probability spectral measure $\mu$ with
\[
 C(s)=\int e^{-i\omega s}\,d\mu(\omega),\qquad
 \int\omega^2\,d\mu\le A^2.
\]
In the second-order, weak-transfer limit the leakage rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
 \Gamma=2J^2\int\frac{\kappa}{\kappa^2+\omega^2}\,d\mu(\omega).
\end{equation}
This is not an exact formula for arbitrary strong coupling or nonlinear
reactive molecular dynamics. A periodic waveform is included by averaging
its starting phase. The weak-coupling limit observes times of order $J^{-2}$.

\begin{theorem}[Scalar static baseline]\label{thm:scalar}
Every admissible scalar control satisfies
\[
 \Gamma\ge\frac{2J^2\kappa}{\kappa^2+A^2}.
\]
The constant shift $q(t)=A$ attains equality.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The function $f(x)=\kappa/(\kappa^2+x)$ is decreasing and convex on
$[0,\infty)$. Jensen's inequality in \eqref{eq:overlap} gives
$\int f(\omega^2)d\mu\ge f(\int\omega^2d\mu)\ge f(A^2)$.
A constant shift has one spectral line at $A$.
\end{proof}

Thus scalar noise, synchronization and bandwidth do not improve the resonant
Lorentzian baseline at equal RMS differential shift. For multiple independent
resonant channels driven by one scalar field, the termwise bounds are attained
simultaneously by a static field. This statement does not extend automatically
to vector controls or to a different bath.

A channel with zero differential control response is exactly immune to the
waveform. For example,
\[
 V=J(bc^\dagger d^\dagger+b^\dagger cd),\quad
 H_c/\hbar=E(t)(a_b n_b+a_c n_c+a_d n_d)
\]
commutes with $H_c$ if $a_b=a_c+a_d$. Different single-mode frequencies do not
exclude such a multiphonon channel. Also, integrating \eqref{eq:overlap} over
a perfectly flat continuum of initial detunings leaves total spectral weight
unchanged. A broad bath cannot be replaced by a single accepting line without
evidence.

\subsection{The representation change: unknown orientation, fixed norm}

For one molecular species, suppose the differential linear-Stark vector is
$d_0n$, with $d_0>0$ fixed and $n\in S^2$ unknown but constant during a cycle.
A vector electric field gives
\[
 u_n(t)=e^{-id_0n\cdot\Theta(t)},\qquad \dot\Theta=E(t),\qquad
 \langle|E|^2\rangle\le E_0^2.
\]
The target population is prepared before protection. One common pump capable
of tracking all molecular orientations is not assumed or constructed.
A constant field has isotropic-ensemble leakage
\begin{equation}\label{eq:staticensemble}
 \frac{\Gamma_{\rm static}}{\Gamma_0}
 =\frac{\arctan a}{a},\qquad
 a=\frac{d_0E_0}{\kappa},\quad \Gamma_0=\frac{2J^2}{\kappa}.
\end{equation}
The near-perpendicular orientations cause its $1/a$ asymptotic tail.
This is an orientation-averaged initial rate, not a single ensemble lifetime.
Frozen orientations give $S(s)=\int e^{-\Gamma_ns}d\sigma(n)$.
For a static field the mean lifetime is already
$\int\Gamma_n^{-1}d\sigma=(1+a^2/3)/\Gamma_0$, while its worst-orientation
rate remains $\Gamma_0$. The uniform theorem addresses that vulnerable
orientation, rather than claiming a new mean-lifetime scaling.
Also, Jensen over the joint orientation/filter measure gives
$\Gamma_{\rm avg}/\Gamma_0\ge(1+a^2/3)^{-1}$ for arbitrary vector control.

The new internal construction uses a phase trajectory whose \emph{time}
measure reproduces spherical moments, including the time spent moving.
It does not assume instantaneous jumps between ideal quadrature points.

\begin{lemma}[Transit-compensated positive cubature]\label{lem:transit}
Let $\sigma$ be normalized area measure on $S^2$ and
$\{x_k,w_k\}$ a positive cubature exact through degree $2t$.
Let $\nu$ be the normalized arclength measure of a closed path visiting all
$x_k$. Set
\[
 K_t(x,y)=\sum_{\ell=0}^t(2\ell+1)P_\ell(x\cdot y),\quad
 h_t(x)=\int K_t(x,y)d\nu(y),\quad
 b_k=w_k[1-\delta h_t(x_k)],
\]
where $0<\delta\le[2(t+1)^2]^{-1}$. Then $b_k\ge w_k/2$,
$\sum b_k=1-\delta$, and for every restricted polynomial $p$ of degree
at most $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moments}
 \sum_k b_kp(x_k)+\delta\int p\,d\nu=\int p\,d\sigma.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The spherical reproducing kernel gives
$\int h_tp\,d\sigma=\int p\,d\nu$.
Moreover $|h_t|\le\sum_{\ell\le t}(2\ell+1)=(t+1)^2$.
The product $h_tp$ has degree at most $2t$, so its cubature sum equals its
sphere integral. Subtract $\delta$ times that identity from the cubature
identity for $p$ to obtain \eqref{eq:moments}. Taking $p=1$ proves the total
mass assertion. The kernel bound proves positivity.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Explicit orientation-uniform model protection]\label{thm:vector}
In the stated one-channel weak-transfer model there is a continuous periodic
phase path with finite RMS field and period $T$ such that, uniformly for
all $n\in S^2$,
\begin{align}
 \frac{\Gamma_n}{\Gamma_0}
 &\le4\epsilon_t^2+\frac{\kappa^2}{\kappa^2+\Omega^2},
 &\epsilon_t&=\frac{\pi^{t+1}}{(t+1)!},\quad\Omega=2\pi/T,\label{eq:vectorbound}\\
 \frac{\Omega}{\kappa}
 &\ge\frac{a}{\pi\sqrt2(t+1)(t+2)}.\label{eq:explicitperiod}
\end{align}
The field is bounded and piecewise smooth; its abrupt starts and stops are
removed by the finite-bandwidth construction below.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use $t+1$ Gauss--Legendre latitudes and $2t+1$ equally spaced azimuths.
The product rule is positive and exact through degree $2t$: the azimuthal
average kills every nonconstant harmonic of that degree, and the remaining
polynomial in latitude is integrated by Gauss--Legendre.
On a phase sphere of radius $R=\pi/d_0$, traverse each complete latitude ring,
join the rings along one meridian, and return on that meridian. Its length
$L$ is at most $2\pi R(t+2)$.

Spend fraction $\delta=[2(t+1)^2]^{-1}$ of the cycle traversing at constant
speed, and fraction $b_k$ dwelling at $Rx_k$. Lemma~\ref{lem:transit} gives its
time-moment identity. Its RMS field is $L/(T\sqrt\delta)$; choose
$T=L/(E_0\sqrt\delta)$. This proves \eqref{eq:explicitperiod}.
A constant translation makes $\Theta(0)=0$ if required and changes only an
irrelevant orientation-dependent constant phase.

The degree-$t$ Taylor polynomial of $e^{-i\pi n\cdot x}$ has uniform
remainder at most $\epsilon_t$, by the real integral remainder formula.
Its exact spherical average is $\sinc\pi=0$.
The moment identity therefore bounds the zero Fourier coefficient of $u_n$
by $2\epsilon_t$, for every orientation. All other Fourier lines are at
integer multiples of $\Omega$. Apply Parseval and \eqref{eq:overlap} to obtain
\eqref{eq:vectorbound}.
\end{proof}

Choosing $t$ of order $\log a/\log\log a$ gives an upper bound
$O(t^4/a^2)$, eventually below the static ensemble's $O(1/a)$.
This proves that the scalar obstruction does not exhaust the vector
interface. It is a conditional mathematical consequence, not proof of a
new physical effect or a major chemical capability.

\subsection{Finite bandwidth, acquisition and stronger prior mathematics}

Let $\Theta_K$ be the Fej\'er approximation through harmonic $K$, and
$B=K\Omega$. The multiplier is $(1-|m|/(K+1))_+$.
Parseval bounds
\[
 \|\Theta-\Theta_K\|_{L^2}\le\frac{E_0}{\Omega(K+1)}.
\]
The positive normalized Fej\'er kernel contracts the RMS and peak field.
The implemented field is bandlimited. Since
$|e^{ix}-e^{iy}|\le|x-y|$, its zero coefficient is at most
$2\epsilon_t+d_0E_0/[\Omega(K+1)]$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bandwidth}
 \frac{\Gamma_{n,K}}{\Gamma_0}
 \le\left(2\epsilon_t+\frac{d_0E_0}{B}\right)^2
       +\frac{\kappa^2}{\kappa^2+\Omega^2}.
\end{equation}
For $2\epsilon_t\le\kappa/\Omega$ and
$B\ge d_0E_0\Omega/\kappa$, this is at most $5(\kappa/\Omega)^2$.
Thus finite bandwidth is available, but it is explicitly costly.

The explicit construction has $O(t^2)$ nodes/stops, peak field
$E_0/\sqrt\delta$, and $O(K)$ Fourier coefficients after filtering.
Numerical synthesis needs charged precision in the nodes, integrals, timing
and delivered field. A differential-phase error bounded by $\eta$ adds a
carrier defect at most $\eta$, giving a contribution
$(2\epsilon_t+\eta)^2\Gamma_0$. Certified algebraic root isolation and
quadrature can approximate the supplied mathematical design; neither acquires
the molecule's actual Stark vectors, bath, rotation or reactive potential.

\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13152}{Ehler--Gr\"ochenig's original design-curve theorem}
already gives $S^2$ design curves of length $O(t)$, improving the existence
scaling to $O(t^2/a^2)$ under constant-speed traversal.
That imported theorem is distinct from our explicit travel-compensation proof;
its existence does not supply a calibrated apparatus.
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01988v1}{Continuous unitary-design control}
also already exists. Its full noncommuting controllability differs from the
orientation-unknown commuting Stark interface here. This distinction is
necessary for a valid comparison and does not establish historical originality.
\href{https://doi.org/10.1155/2011/584082}{Sugawara's 2011 laser construction}
already isolates molecular subspaces against vibrational redistribution.
No superiority over that stronger physical-control family was established.
\href{https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2220852120}{Wilcken et al.'s nanoantenna experiments}
also measure altered vibrational free-induction decays and infer coupled
population-lifetime changes through a constrained oscillator model. That is
an experimental interface with its own limits, not evidence for our waveform
or a direct chemical-yield demonstration.

\subsection{Numerical evidence and the physical stopping gate}

A reduced-model probe used 200 orientation nodes, 32768 time samples and a
64-harmonic Fej\'er cutoff. The following are \emph{ensemble-grid numerical thresholds for the initial
orientation-averaged rate}, not single-exponential lifetime improvements,
all-orientation certificates or experiments. The degree-2
transit-compensated waveform gives:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Leakage reduction & Static $A_{\rm rms}/\kappa$ & Vector $A_{\rm rms}/\kappa$ & Vector $A_{\rm peak}/\kappa$ & $B/\kappa$\\
\midrule
$10\times$ &15.044&18.869&75.697&91.600\\
$100\times$&156.44&91.764&368.127&445.465\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At $100\times$ the RMS shift is about $1.70\times$ smaller, but the peak shift
is about $2.35\times$ larger and bandwidth is substantial; the static field has
zero bandwidth and equal peak/RMS. At $10\times$ this explicit waveform also
loses on RMS. These examples do not prove optimality or rule out better curves.
The rigorous uniform bound is more conservative than this orientation grid.

No same-species, measured \emph{differential transition} Stark vector and
acceptor kernel satisfying the resonant weak-transfer assumptions was obtained.
Combining a single-mode slope from one species with a linewidth from another
would not calibrate the model. In particular, a strongly coupled, initially
detuned example cannot inherit the table's leakage predictions.
Additional accepting channels have other slope norms; a zero-slope channel
remains immune. Norm spread changes the ideal spherical carrier to
$\sinc(\pi |d|/d_0)$, producing a residual floor. Molecular rotation during a
cycle changes the phase law. Preparation, anharmonic chemistry, competing
reactions, field damage and wall-plug losses remain separate obligations.

A decisive next experiment would first measure the relevant transition slopes,
$J$, detuning and memory kernel in one declared species. Prepare the target
population, apply the recorded three-axis waveform, and measure population
lifetimes with orientation-resolved controls. Compare bias-off, best static
field and the strongest available coherent control under equal peak, RMS,
bandwidth and preparation accounting. A disagreement with the calibrated
population prediction falsifies the model-to-device bridge. Chemical selectivity
requires a subsequent product-yield experiment. No such experiment occurred.
