# Characteristic independence and the missing compatibility budget

Date: 2026-10-16. Internally derived, not externally reviewed. The unrestricted
mixed-source/discriminant problem remains OPEN. No compatible negative field is
constructed. Historical originality is UNKNOWN.

This note preserves the original integrand and gives an amplitude-unrestricted
positive result on a different infinite-dimensional class, followed by an exact
obstruction to replacing spatial compatibility by minor moments. The positive
class is a classical diagonal/characteristic reduction; the quantitative
identities and sharp restricted constant are derived here. It is not an
extension of the small separated tube to all gradients.

## 1. Exact receiving contract

Use normalized Haar average on the square torus. All potentials are real,
periodic, and in W^{1,4}. For V=D(f,g) write

\[
V=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\quad
u=a^2+c^2,\quad v=b^2+d^2,\quad j=ad-bc.
\]

For r>0 retain, without completion,

\[
J_r(V)=\alpha(u+v-2j)^2+\beta u^2+j^2+ruj,
\quad \alpha=\frac{3r}{8(r+2)},\quad \beta=\frac{r(r-1)}6.
\]

Set

\[
\sigma=r+1,\quad\kappa=\frac{r(r+2)}3,\quad
t=\frac{2r+1}3,\quad U=\sigma a+d.
\]

The inherited exact conformal expansion is

\[
E_r(f,g,s)=s^2\mathcal A+s\mathcal P+\mathcal Q,
\quad \mathcal A=\langle U^2+\kappa c^2\rangle,
\quad \mathcal Q=\langle J_r(V)\rangle,
\]
\[
\mathcal P=\langle 2aU^2-(r+2)a^2U+rc^2U-2bcU
                          -\kappa a^3-\kappa ac^2\rangle.
\tag{1}
\]

It uses the actual gradient identities <V>=0 and <det V>=0. These are
necessary, not sufficient, compatibility conditions. Section 5 makes their
insufficiency quantitative.

## 2. A complete compatible characteristic plane

Fix relatively prime positive integers m,n, put k=n/m and h=σ/k². For
arbitrary real one-variable periodic W^{1,4} profiles F,G define

\[
\theta_+=mx+ny,\quad\theta_-=mx-ny,
\]
\[
f=F(\theta_+)+G(\theta_-),\qquad
g=-\frac\sigma kF(\theta_+)+\frac\sigma kG(\theta_-).
\tag{2}
\]

With X=mF'(θ+) and Y=mG'(θ-),

\[
a=X+Y,\quad b=k(X-Y),\quad
c=-\frac\sigma k(X-Y),\quad d=-\sigma(X+Y).
\tag{3}
\]

Thus U=0 and c=-h b. There is no amplitude, concentration, sign, frequency,
or smallness restriction on either profile. Additive constants are immaterial.

Conversely every periodic W^{1,4} pair satisfying U=0 and c=-h b, for this
h, has form (2) modulo constants. Indeed, at a nonzero Fourier covector
(p,q), compatibility gives p ĝ=-h q f̂ and q ĝ=-σ p f̂. Every nonzero
coefficient lies on q/p=±k; the coordinate-axis coefficients vanish.
The two rational-line projections are coordinate averages after an integer
torus covering and are bounded on L^4. They yield the two W^{1,4} profiles.
This is a classification of the whole exact plane, not a finite-mode ansatz.

On the fixed square torus, if sqrt(σ/h) is irrational, the same Fourier
calculation leaves no nonzero integer covector. The exact plane then contains
only constant potentials. Nontrivial square-torus sharpness is asserted only
for the admitted values h=σ(m/n)². The formulas for every real h>0 also hold
on an adapted rectangular torus: take periods 2πLx,2πLy with
Lx/Ly=sqrt(σ/h), phases x/Lx±y/Ly, and X=F'/Lx, Y=G'/Lx. This last
observation is a separate domain version, not arbitrary h on the square.

The integer torus homomorphism (x,y) -> (θ+,θ-) has nonzero determinant
-2mn and is onto. It pushes normalized Haar measure to product Haar measure.
Consequently X and Y are independent real random variables and EX=EY=0.
For zero-mean F,G, the canonical coordinate-separated projection Πf is zero:
both coordinate averages of each nonconstant profile are zero. Nonzero maps
in this class therefore have R=||DΠf||4=0 and lie outside the N895 tube.

## 3. Exact all-r moment representation

Let M_j=EX^j+EY^j for j=2,3,4, and N_2=EX² EY². Direct polynomial
expansion of (1)-(3), with independence used only after expansion, gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal A=\kappa\sigma h M_2,\qquad
\mathcal P=-\kappa(1+\sigma h)M_3,\qquad
\mathcal Q=L_r(h)M_4+K_r(h)N_2.}
\tag{4}
\]

Here

\[
L_r(h)=\alpha(1+\sigma h)^2
       \left[(\sigma/h)^2+2\sigma/h+t^2\right]>0.
\tag{5}
\]

For an entirely explicit positivity proof of the interaction coefficient,
put M=r²+10r+10. The exact identity is

\[
\begin{split}
K_r(h)={}&\frac{r\sigma^2(2r+1)^2}{4(r+2)}h^2
+\frac{r\sigma(35r^2+50r+14)}{6(r+2)}h\\
&+\frac{9r\sigma^2}{4(r+2)}
  \left(\frac1h-\frac{M}{3\sigma}\right)^2\\
&+\frac{\sigma(4r^4+28r^3+95r^2+133r+64)}{2(r+2)}>0.
\end{split}
\tag{6}
\]

All displayed terms are nonnegative and the final term is positive.
Therefore (4) does not need an unverified general quasiconvexity premise or
an optimal Riesz constant. Its proof consists of a polynomial identity,
Haar independence, and an explicit positive square.

One way to reconstruct the coefficient is to set

\[
u_0=1+\sigma h,\quad u_1=2(1-\sigma h),
\quad v_0=\sigma^2+\sigma/h,\quad v_1=2(\sigma^2-\sigma/h).
\]

Then j=-4σXY and

\[
K=\alpha\{2(u_0+v_0)^2+(u_1+v_1+8\sigma)^2\}
 +\beta(2u_0^2+u_1^2)+16\sigma^2-4r\sigma u_1.
\]

This expression expands exactly to (6).

## 4. Sharp discriminant theorem on the whole plane

Cauchy-Schwarz on the disjoint union of the X and Y probability spaces gives

\[
|M_3|^2\le M_2 M_4.
\]

Combining this with K>0 and (4) gives, for every admitted pair,

\[
\boxed{\mathcal P^2\le4\rho_r(h)\mathcal A\mathcal Q,\qquad
\rho_r(h)=\frac{2(r+2)^2h}
 {9\sigma(\sigma^2+2\sigma h+t^2h^2)}.}
\tag{7}
\]

In particular,

\[
\boxed{\rho_r(h)\le\frac{r+2}{6(r+1)^2}<\frac13<1.}
\tag{8}
\]

The first inequality follows from
σ²+2σh+t²h²=(σ-th)²+2σh(t+1). Equality in that algebraic bound occurs
at h=σ/t; this h is exactly periodic on the square torus when sqrt(t) is
rational, and rational characteristic slopes approximate it otherwise.

For every real s this proves the homogeneous positive bound

\[
\boxed{E_r(f,g,s)\ge(1-\sqrt{\rho_r(h)})
                (\mathcal Q+s^2\mathcal A).}
\tag{9}
\]

If the gradient is nonzero then M4>0, so Q>0 and the inequality is strict.
If A=0 then X=Y=0 and the gradient vanishes; no division by zero is used.
When A>0, it also proves min_s E >=(1-ρ)Q.

The constant ρ_r(h) in (7) is sharp for the class at fixed r and admitted
square-torus h (or on the adapted rectangular torus just specified). Set Y=0.
Let X take values 1 and -p/(1-p) with probabilities p and 1-p. Such X is
the derivative of a periodic Lipschitz profile, after the harmless m scaling.
Then

\[
\frac{(EX^3)^2}{EX^2 EX^4}
=\frac{(1-2p)^2}{1-3p+3p^2}\longrightarrow1\quad(p\downarrow0).
\]

All inequalities preceding (7) are then equalities except this factor.
Strong W^{1,4} smoothing preserves the limiting ratio, so the same supremum
holds over smooth profiles. This is a supremum statement, not attainment by
a nonconstant mean-zero profile. Taking rational characteristic slopes
approaching sqrt(t) gives the upper value (r+2)/(6(r+1)²) as a supremum
over square-torus planes for every r>0; this does not assert an optimizing
square-torus plane when sqrt(t) is irrational.

At r=1, m=n=1, h=2, formulas simplify to

\[
f=F(x+y)+G(x-y),\quad g=-2F(x+y)+2G(x-y),
\]
\[
\mathcal A=4M_2,\quad\mathcal P=-5M_3,\quad
\mathcal Q=\frac{25}2M_4+169N_2,\quad\rho=\frac18.
\tag{10}
\]

As an exact genuinely two-profile test, take F=G=sin θ+(1/4)sin 2θ.
The profile derivative has moments (5/8,3/8,99/128). Therefore
A=5, P=-15/4, Q=10925/128, Γ=P²/(4AQ)=18/2185, and the optimized
background s*=3/8 gives E=10835/128>0. These are exact moments of an
actual compatible pair; no sampled quadrature or optimizer supplies them.

The underlying qualitative class is classical: (3) lies in the span of two
rank-one directions and an invertible left/right transformation makes this

## 5. Exact minor-moment obstruction, with no compatible-map claim

For every fixed r>0, choose x>0 and ε>0, and put

\[
H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-\sigma),\quad
B_\varepsilon=\begin{pmatrix}0&\varepsilon\\-\varepsilon^2&0\end{pmatrix},
\quad D=\sigma x^2+\varepsilon^3,
\]
\[
p=\frac{\varepsilon^3}{2D},\quad
q=\frac{\sigma x^2}{2D},\quad
y=-\frac{\varepsilon^3}{\sigma x}.
\]

Define a four-atom matrix law

\[
\nu=p\delta_{xH}+q\delta_{yH}
       +\frac14\delta_{B_\varepsilon}+\frac14\delta_{-B_\varepsilon}.
\tag{11}
\]

All weights are positive; p+q=1/2; and exactly

\[
\int V\,d\nu=0,\qquad\int\det V\,d\nu=0.
\tag{12}
\]

Every atom also has U=0 and c=-ε b. Thus even these local plane constraints
and all ordinary gradient-minor moments do not replace spatial compatibility.

Let L_H=J_r(H)=α(r+2)^4+(r+2)(r+3)/6. For the scalar diagonal part,

\[
M_3^{\rm diag}=\frac{\varepsilon^3x}{2\sigma}
                -\frac{\varepsilon^6}{2\sigma^2x},
\]
\[
M_4^{\rm diag}=\frac{\varepsilon^3x^2}{2\sigma}
 -\frac{\varepsilon^6}{2\sigma^2}
 +\frac{\varepsilon^9}{2\sigma^3x^2}.
\]

Its exact moment coefficients are

\[
\mathcal A_\nu=\frac\kappa2\varepsilon^4,\quad
\mathcal P_\nu=-\kappa M_3^{\rm diag},
\]
\[
\mathcal Q_\nu=L_HM_4^{\rm diag}
 +\frac12\{\alpha\varepsilon^4(1-\varepsilon)^4
              +\varepsilon^6+r\varepsilon^7+\beta\varepsilon^8\}.
\tag{13}
\]

For fixed x, as ε decreases,

\[
\varepsilon\frac{\mathcal P_\nu^2}
                     {4\mathcal A_\nu\mathcal Q_\nu}
\longrightarrow\frac\kappa{4\sigma L_H}>0.
\tag{14}
\]

Hence **no finite discriminant constant** holds for arbitrary matrix laws
satisfying only zero mean and zero determinant. The ratio is unbounded.
Furthermore if 0<x<κ/L_H, then

\[
\varepsilon^{-3}\int[J_r(I+V)-J_r(I)]\,d\nu
\longrightarrow\frac{x}{2\sigma}(L_Hx-\kappa)<0.
\tag{15}
\]

This is an obstruction to the moment relaxation, not a compatible negative
Jensen field. Choosing x arbitrarily small and then ε small makes every atom
arbitrarily close to zero. It still does not furnish a constant-centered
neighborhood counterexample for actual periodic gradients.

For an exact rational example set r=1, x=1/32, ε=1/256. Then

\Gamma_\nu=274861129984/112607332451>1,

The law is excluded as a homogeneous gradient Young measure by classical

## 6. A precise unresolved scalar continuation

The plane theorem removes one special slice of the mixed barrier. To retain
the full acoustic-null condition U=0 while dropping c=-h b, write

\[
f=\psi_y,\qquad g=-\sigma\psi_x,
\quad\psi\in W^{2,4}_{\rm per},
\]

modulo constants. Every U=0 periodic pair has this stream-potential form.
Then the missing restricted sharp inequality is exactly

\[
\boxed{\kappa\left\langle
\psi_{xy}(\psi_{xy}^2+\sigma^2\psi_{xx}^2)\right\rangle^2
\le4\sigma^2\langle\psi_{xx}^2\rangle
\left\langle J_r\!\begin{pmatrix}
\psi_{xy}&\psi_{yy}\\-\sigma\psi_{xx}&-\sigma\psi_{xy}
\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle.}
\tag{17}
\]

Equation (17) is OPEN here. The theorem above proves it when
σψ_xx=hψ_yy and gives a strong margin. Without that wave equation, the
characteristic variables are not independent and the quartic/cubic mixed
moments in (4) do not have their stated factorization. Extending the bound by
averaging directions would require a new signed interaction identity; Cauchy
on the minor moments is specifically ruled out by (11)-(16).

A negative smooth ψ in (17), independently integrated with exact or outward
enclosed energy, would give an actual compatible negative field for the
original conformal problem. A proof of (17) would still cover only U=0;
the general U component would require its own compatible coupling argument.
