## 6. Temperature-correct observable route: M–T certification

Mutzel and Tilloy, “Certified spectral functions from lattice Monte Carlo data,” [arXiv:2606.09791v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09791v1), provide the strongest practical comparator found for this output. Their exact finite-lattice Källén–Lehmann relation is

\[
C_\beta(\tau)=\int_0^\infty \rho_E(d\Delta)
[e^{-\Delta\tau}+e^{-\Delta(\beta-\tau)}],
\quad \rho_E\ge0,
\]

for reflection-positive actions (their Eqs. (5)–(6)); the finite-temperature response measure is $d\mu=(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})d\rho_E$. Therefore the requested regular response is the **linear functional**

\[
\chi_{\beta,A}(i\eta)=\int_0^\infty
\underbrace{\frac{2\Delta(1-e^{-\beta\Delta})}{\Delta^2+\eta^2}}_{G_\eta(\Delta)}
\,\rho_E(d\Delta),\qquad \eta>0.
\]

M–T's dual SDP bounds linear functionals over all nonnegative spectra consistent with the measured compact-Euclidean correlator and its covariance. Their hierarchy replaces the infinite pointwise kernel inequality by finite piecewise-affine envelopes; each feasible dual solution gives an outer bound even before the envelope hierarchy converges. For fixed $\beta$ and $\eta>0$, $G_\eta$ is bounded and can be enclosed by those envelopes. This returns an interval for the requested observable without claiming to recover a unique spectrum. A lattice-specific kernel should be used at finite $a$; continuum response then requires a separately controlled extrapolation.

This is temperature-correct in the sense that its input is a conventional finite-$\beta$ Euclidean correlator and its target is the thermal spectral functional. It is not a direct-frequency sampler, an acquisition shortcut, or a universal sign-problem solution. The paper assumes access to reliable configurations and approximately Gaussian errors with known covariance; its example uses $10^2$–$10^6$ configurations, 129 time-separation constraints, and $N_v=700$ envelope intervals in one reported convergence analysis. Its 2D \(\phi^4\) example reports integrated autocorrelation small compared with its measurement separation. Sampling/equilibration, autocorrelation analysis, covariance/bootstrap, the SDP, precision checks, and continuum extrapolation remain paid costs. The arXiv source archive contains paper source/figures but no runnable solver or Monte Carlo code, so this is a source-derived algorithmic comparator rather than an executed certificate.

Other primary alternatives do different jobs:

- **Tupitsyn et al., [arXiv:2102.11418v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11418v2), “Real-frequency response functions at finite temperature.”** Diagrammatic Monte Carlo analytically sums internal Matsubara frequencies using spectral representations of dressed propagators/effective interactions, then samples the remaining momentum/spectral integrals on the real-frequency axis. It computes a jellium plasmon linewidth, but needs those auxiliary spectral inputs and a controlled diagrammatic expansion/order limit; this is not a black-box general thermal solver.
- **Jeckelmann, DDMRG, PRB 66, 045114 (2002), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevB.66.045114.** Correction-vector DMRG obtains broadened spectra for ground-state, low-dimensional lattice systems. Broadening, finite-size scaling, and tensor-network resources are part of the method; this is not automatically a finite-temperature Gibbs-response algorithm.
- **PR itself.** Its rational imaginary-frequency spectral kernel can improve a reconstruction *if the input correlator is the correct thermal one*. A better-conditioned post-processing kernel does not validate the sampled thermal measure.
