## 5. Exact quartic assembly after all affine directions are optimized

This section does not assume that spatially overlapping fields are independent merely because they have different names. A precise degree-four condition is required.

### 5.1 Moment-level assembly identity

Let X_1,...,X_N be centered random matrices with finite fourth moments. Suppose all their joint polynomial moments of total degree at most four agree with those of independent variables with the same marginal laws. Define

    C_i=E X_i X_i^T, H_i=E D²F(X_i), b_i=E DF(X_i), c_i=E F(X_i).

For any homogeneous quartic F, polarization gives

    H_sum=sum_i H_i,        b_sum=sum_i b_i,
    c_sum=sum_i c_i + (1/2)sum_(i<j) tr(H_i C_j).             (5.1)

The expression is symmetric because tr(H_i C_j)=tr(H_j C_i). These are exact polynomial identities; they do not require a gradient hypothesis.

If F is rank-one convex and each X_i is a compatible periodic gradient, then H_i>=0 in every matrix dimension. Indeed,

    A:H_i A=E D²F(A)[X_i,X_i]

by quartic reciprocity. Parseval splits the right side into quadratic forms at rank-one real and imaginary Fourier coefficients. Each is nonnegative by rank-one convexity. Approximation covers W^(1,4). This proof does not import a higher-dimensional determinant S-lemma.

Suppose the individual affine quadratic problems have finite attained minima. Put

    B_i=-H_i^dagger b_i,   B_* = -(sum H_i)^dagger(sum b_i),
    Delta_i=c_i-(1/2)b_i^T H_i^dagger b_i.

The range conditions are automatic if each block already satisfies all-affine Jensen, since otherwise its affine quadratic would be unbounded below. They are also automatic when H_i is positive definite. Then

    Delta(sum_i X_i)
      = sum_i Delta_i + (1/2)sum_(i<j) tr(H_i C_j)
        + (1/2)sum_i (B_i-B_*)^T H_i(B_i-B_*).               (5.2)

Proof. Write each individual affine gap as

    Delta_i+(1/2)(B-B_i)^T H_i(B-B_i),

sum these expressions and use (5.1). Minimization gives (5.2). Every additional term is nonnegative when H_i>=0. This is elementary quadratic algebra; its role here is preserving the *complete optimized* interaction, rather than choosing one background direction prematurely.

Consequently positive blocks cannot become a negative profile under degree-four-independent assembly. This is spatially overlapping assembly, different from the earlier disjoint-support inequality.

### 5.2 Deterministic compatible realization: resonance blocks

Let each X_i=Dphi_i be a finite Fourier gradient. Partition its nonzero frequency support into packets. A signed zero-sum tuple of frequencies is called primitive if it has no proper nonempty zero-sum subtuple; multiplicities are allowed. Treat k and -k as one frequency class.

Assume every primitive zero-sum tuple of length at most four lies wholly inside one packet. Then all joint polynomial moments through degree four factor across packets.

Proof. Expand a monomial of total degree at most four into Fourier characters. A term survives spatial averaging precisely when its frequencies sum to zero. Decompose that tuple into primitive zero-sum subtuples. By assumption each such subtuple lies in one packet; hence each packet's total frequency sum is separately zero. The surviving terms are exactly those from independent phase averaging. Conversely, separately zero packet sums always survive. QED.

No restriction is placed on the number or complexity of internal resonances. Applying (5.2) gives global Jensen for arbitrary finite assemblies of packets from Sections 2–4 satisfying this cross-packet condition.

Such assemblies exist exactly, not just asymptotically. Let phi_i have maximum frequency infinity norm K_i. Replace

    X_i(x) by X_i(N_i x)=D[phi_i(N_i x)/N_i],

where N_1=1 and successive positive integers satisfy

    N_j > 4 sum_(i<j) N_i K_i.                              (5.3)

In any relation of length at most four, a nonzero net frequency from the largest-index packet has norm at least N_j, exceeding all lower-packet contributions. Thus it must vanish; induction treats the remaining packets. This retains each packet's internal law and internal resonances exactly. It changes the configuration rather than pretending to preserve the energy of an arbitrary pre-existing field.

### 5.3 A canonical obstruction reduction

For any finite Fourier potential, form the hypergraph whose vertices are frequency classes and whose hyperedges are primitive zero-sum tuples of length three or four. Its connected components satisfy the condition above. Each component is still a compatible gradient, since keeping Fourier modes of a potential preserves curl compatibility.

Therefore, if a finite Fourier gradient has a negative gap for a rank-one-convex quartic, at least one of these connected resonance components has a negative all-affine gap. To see this without assuming every individual minimum is attained, use the fixed-background form of (5.1): if every component were nonnegative for every B, their sum plus the nonnegative covariance interactions would be nonnegative for every B.

This reduces a counterexample to a primitive-resonance-connected component. It does not prove positivity inside such a component; components can be very large. A single finite Fourier counterexample would still suffice, since a smooth negative-gap potential can be approximated in W^(1,4) by finite Fourier sums at a fixed offending background.
