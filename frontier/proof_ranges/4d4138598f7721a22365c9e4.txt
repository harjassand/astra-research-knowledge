For a one-mode Fock-diagonal state with probabilities p_k, the displacement-heat entropy production in the convention used here is

J(p) = sum_{k>=0} (k+1)(p_k-p_{k+1}) log(p_k/p_{k+1}).

The relative-entropy displacement Fisher matrix is isotropic in this convention; its symplectic invariant is this same J. Each term is nonnegative. Thermal mean N gives J=log(1+1/N).

The candidate was K(J_C)>=G K(J_A)+(G-1)K(J_B), with K(j)=1/2+1/(exp(j)-1). It was intended to replace ordinary reciprocal-Fisher Stam by an inequality attaining thermal equality.

## Exact amplifier transition

At gain G=2, the two-mode squeezing unitary applied to |1,1> has reduced output probabilities

q_k = (k-1)^2 / 2^(k+2),  k>=0.

This follows from the disentangling identity U=exp(t a†b†) G^(-(N_a+N_b+1)/2) exp(-t ab), t=sqrt((G-1)/G). There are only two lowering terms for |1,1>; their amplitudes combine to (k-1)/2^((k+2)/2), up to an irrelevant common sign convention. The probabilities sum to1 by the elementary geometric moment identities. In particular q_0=1/4 and q_1=0.

## Full-rank finite-energy regularization

Let tau_1 be the thermal state of mean occupation1 and set

rho_e=(1-e)|1><1|+e tau_1,  e=1/10000.

Both input states are rho_e. They have full rank, mean occupation1, and finite entropy and Fisher value. Their probabilities are p_0=e/2, p_1=1-3e/4, and p_k=e/2^(k+1) for k>=2. The geometric tail makes the Fisher series finite.

By linearity on the product input, the output is a convex mixture whose |1,1> component has weight (1-e)^2. The remaining weight is 2e-e^2. Therefore its probabilities satisfy

q_0 >= L=(1-e)^2/4,  q_1 <= U=2e.

The output is also full rank: the input mixture includes |0,0> with positive weight, and its amplifier output is a thermal state with strictly positive probability at every k. No zero-probability divergence is used in the finite-e example.

For x>y>0, (x-y)log(x/y) increases with x and decreases with y. Consequently the k=0 contribution alone gives

J(q) >= (L-U)log(L/U).

Exact rational arithmetic gives L-U>1/5 and L/U>1024=2^10. Hence J(q)>2log2 and K(J(q))<5/6. Finiteness also holds: the vacuum-vacuum input component implies q_k>=e^2*2^(-(k+3)). Thus the absolute adjacent log ratio is bounded by a constant plus (k+4)log2. The output has finite second photon-number moment, since the Bogoliubov transform expresses its square in input operators of total degree at most four and the inputs have all these moments finite. The Fisher series is consequently dominated by a constant times sum_k (k+1)^2(q_k+q_{k+1}), which is finite.

On the other hand K(J(p))>=1/2 for each input. At G=2 the proposed right-hand side is at least3/2. Its left-hand side is less than5/6. The proposed inequality therefore fails with margin greater than2/3.

## Boundary

This example is non-Gaussian, isotropic and full rank, so correcting the earlier squeezing dependence does not rescue the mechanism. The failure comes from channel-created spectral holes that produce large local entropy production even when the entropy itself need not violate any lower bound. Neither the amplifier entropy photon-number inequality nor a global output-spectrum comparison is refuted. No priority claim is made for this route-specific obstruction.
