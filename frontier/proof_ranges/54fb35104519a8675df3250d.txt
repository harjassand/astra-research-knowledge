## 4. Optimal Schatten transfer

For `0<p<infinity`, the Schatten quantity is

\[
 \|X\|_{S_p}=\left(\sum_j s_j(X)^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]

It is a quasi-norm when `p<1`; the statements below do not require a triangle inequality for it.

### Theorem 4.1 — sharp exponent and constants

Under (1.1), if `B in S_p`, then

\[
 \boxed{\quad T\in S_{q(p)},\qquad q(p)=\max\{p+2,2p\}.\quad} \tag{4.1}
\]

More precisely,

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|T\|_{S_{p+2}}^{p+2}\le\frac{2}{pa}\|B\|_{S_p}^{p},
 \qquad0<p\le2,
 \quad} \tag{4.2}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \|T\|_{S_{2p}}^{4}\le\frac1a\|B\|_{S_p}^{2},
 \qquad2\le p<\infty.
 \quad} \tag{4.3}
\]

Both meet at `a||T||_{S_4}^4<=||B||_{S_2}^2`. The exponents and displayed constants cannot be improved universally.

### 4.1 Proof for `0<p<=2`

Put `alpha=p/2`. We use the classical trace inequality

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(X+Y)^\alpha
 \le\operatorname{Tr}X^\alpha+\operatorname{Tr}Y^\alpha,
 \quad X,Y\succeq0,\quad0<\alpha\le1. \tag{4.4}
\]

For completeness, if `0<alpha<1`, integrate the scalar identity

\[
 x^\alpha=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi}
 \int_0^\infty t^{\alpha-1}\frac{x}{t+x}\,dt.
\]

For positive matrices, order reversal of inversion implies

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\big[X(t+X+Y)^{-1}\big]
 \le\operatorname{Tr}\big[X(t+X)^{-1}\big],
\]

and likewise for `Y`. Add and integrate to obtain (4.4). At `alpha=1` it is an equality. This classical ingredient is not claimed as new; [2] contains a broader matrix subadditivity result.

Compactness follows from Lemma 3.1. Write `lambda_j=s_j(T)^2`, let `mu_j` be the ordered eigenvalues of `C+P`, and let `Q_N` project onto its leading `N` eigenvectors. Apply (4.4) on this finite-dimensional subspace, then eigenvalue domination under compression:

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^N\mu_j^\alpha
 \le\operatorname{Tr}(Q_NCQ_N)^\alpha
   +\operatorname{Tr}(Q_NPQ_N)^\alpha
 \le\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^\alpha+\|B\|_{S_p}^p.
\]

Equation (3.1) implies `f_a(lambda_j)<=mu_j`. Consequently,

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^N\left[f_a(\lambda_j)^\alpha-\lambda_j^\alpha\right]
 \le\|B\|_{S_p}^p.
\]

The scalar inequality `(1-u)^(-alpha)>=1+alpha u` gives

\[
 f_a(\lambda)^\alpha-\lambda^\alpha
 \ge\alpha a\lambda^{\alpha+1}.
\]

Letting `N` increase proves (4.2). All trace operations before this limit are finite-dimensional; no subtraction of two infinite traces is used. ∎

### 4.2 Proof for `p>=2`

Let `P_N` be the leading `N` spectral projection of `A`, and set

\[
 W_N=A^{p-2}P_N.
\]

At `p=2`, this means `W_N=P_N`, including any zero-eigenvalue issue. Spectral rearrangement gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_NC)\le\operatorname{Tr}(W_NA).
\]

One direct justification is to write `W_N` as a nonnegative linear combination of nested spectral projections and apply the min–max upper bound for the trace of `C` on each projection. `A` and `C` have the same nonzero eigenvalues with multiplicities.

Since

\[
 f_a(A)-A=aA^2(I-aA)^{-1}\succeq aA^2,
\]

(3.1) implies

\[
 a\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^p
 \le\operatorname{Tr}(W_NP).
\]

For `p>2`, finite-rank trace Hölder with exponents `p/2` and `p/(p-2)` gives

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(W_NP)
 \le\left(\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j^p\right)^{(p-2)/p}
       \|B\|_{S_p}^{2}.
\]

For `p=2`, directly use `Tr(P_NP)<=Tr P`. Rearranging and taking `N` to infinity proves (4.3). ∎
