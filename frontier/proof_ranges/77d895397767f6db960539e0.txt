## 3. The construction is computational, not only existential

The first proof chooses a sufficiently populated likelihood band by pigeonhole. That choice can be made using sampling rather than enumeration.

Assume that \(\mathcal F\) is explicitly listed and its probabilities are rational, with numerators and denominators of at most \(B\) bits.

Fix \(k\) and a failure tolerance \(\delta\in(0,1)\). Write \(L=L_k\).

### Selecting the threshold

Draw independent samples from \(\mu=p^{\otimes k}\), count their occurrences in the \(L\) bands above, and select a band with the largest count. Samples below all the retained bands are ignored in this comparison.

There is a band of true probability at least \(1/(2L)\). Standard binomial tail bounds show that

\[
T=
\left\lceil
64L\log\frac{2(L+1)}{\delta}
\right\rceil
\]

samples suffice to select, with probability at least \(1-\delta/2\), a band of true probability at least \(1/(4L)\).

For completeness, compare counts with \(3T/(8L)\). A band of probability at least \(1/(2L)\) falls below that count with probability at most

\[
e^{-T/(64L)}.
\]

A band of probability at most \(1/(4L)\) exceeds that count with probability at most

\[
e^{-T/(40L)}.
\]

A union bound establishes the claim.

For the selected threshold \(\tau\), the resulting family then satisfies

\[
\tau|\mathcal U|\ge\frac1{8L}.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus the preceding density and relative-entropy bounds hold with \(8L\) in place of \(4L\).

### Sampling uniformly without knowing the family’s cardinality

Use fresh samples \(x\sim\mu\). Reject when \(\mu(x)<\tau\). Otherwise accept with probability

\[
\frac{\tau}{\mu(x)}.
\]

For every \(x\in\mathcal U\),

\[
\Pr(\text{propose and accept }x)
=
\mu(x)\frac{\tau}{\mu(x)}
=
\tau.
\]

Therefore the accepted output is **exactly uniform on \(\mathcal U\)**. Its cardinality is never computed.

On a good threshold, the acceptance probability is at least \(1/(8L)\). Capping the rejection procedure at

\[
R=
\left\lceil8L\log\frac2\delta\right\rceil
\]

trials makes its additional failure probability at most \(\delta/2\). Combining threshold selection and capped rejection gives total failure probability at most \(\delta\). Conditional on any fixed returned threshold and successful rejection, the output distribution is exactly uniform on its specified family.

### Resource accounting

Membership in \(\mathcal U\) is checked by comparing a product of rational probabilities with a power of two. The integers involved have \(O(kB+L)\) bits; exponentially fine numerical approximations are unnecessary.

The preprocessing and sampling use

\[
O\!\left(kL\log\frac{L}{\delta}\right)
\]

draws from the original one-block distribution, and polynomially many bit operations in the explicit input size, \(k\), and \(\log(1/\delta)\). The family need not be enumerated or stored.

**The limitations are important.** The family itself may have exponentially many members. The theorem preserves local distributions, not total-variation closeness of the entire \(k\)-block law to \(p^{\otimes k}\). It does, however, provide the global domination bound (2), so an event exponentially unlikely under the product law remains exponentially unlikely under the lift, up to a polynomial factor.

This is a sampling and representation capability—not a polynomial-time procedure for listing an exponentially large family.
