EXACT STATIONARY SAMPLING WITH ADDITIVE LOCAL RESET HAZARDS
Root mathematical construction, 2026-10-10. INTERNAL THEOREM CANDIDATE.
No independent correctness certification or historical originality claim.

1. Computational operation and explicit interface

There are n local state sets of sizes d_i, with explicitly given irreducible
rational row-generators Q_i. Their independent generator is Q0=sum_i Q_i.
For each of k reset channels supply a nonnegative rational local hazard
a_{li}(x_i) and a rational product reset law nu_l=otimes_i nu_{li}. At full
state x, channel l fires at rate a_l(x)=sum_i a_{li}(x_i) and replaces the
ENTIRE configuration by an independent draw from nu_l. The generator is

 Q = Q0 - diag(a) + U V,
 a(x)=sum_l a_l(x), U(x,l)=a_l(x), V(l,y)=nu_l(y).          (1)

An update may leave the state unchanged. Formula (1) includes this correctly
on the diagonal. Interactions recur at every configuration, not only on a
small list of exceptional states. Reset laws need not equal the base
stationary law and may have zeros. Q is irreducible because Q0 is.

Claim A. Expectations of explicitly given finite sums of product observables
under the stationary law of Q are deterministically approximable to additive
epsilon in time polynomial in the entire rational input bit length, the
observable description, and log(1/epsilon), without enumerating prod_i d_i.

Claim B. There is an EXACT stationary configuration sampler with expected
bit time and expected space polynomial in the explicit input bit length.
It uses independent fair random bits, not a stationary sampling oracle or
physical trajectories. Its precision is random and unbounded with a
geometric tail. These claims follow from the detailed construction below.

These are mathematical algorithm claims. A two-state implementation now
acquires interval-certified stationary probabilities and drives an uncapped
lazy sampler; see REGENERATIVE.txt and regenerative_solver.py. The general
nonreversible, multistate local case remains a mathematical construction.
Finite executions do not validate the general theorem or historical priority.

2. Product subgenerator and a k-dimensional resolvent

Set a_i(x_i)=sum_l a_{li}(x_i), L_i=Q_i-diag(a_i), and L=sum_i L_i.
Each L_i is a local killed generator and exp(tL)=otimes_i exp(tL_i).
For positive rational s, let R0(s)=(sI-L)^(-1). Then

 R(s)=(sI-Q)^(-1)
     =R0+R0 U (I-V R0 U)^(-1) V R0.                     (2)

The middle inverse has dimension k. It exists: R0 U V is the discounted
kernel for one reset before independent killing, and s>0 discounts every
positive waiting time; alternatively the determinant identity and
invertibility of both Markov resolvents prove it directly.

All required entries of V R0 U, mu R0 U, V R0 g and mu R0 g are acquired
by real integrals of products of local matrix-exponential entries. Here mu
is any explicit product initial law. In particular,

 mu exp(tL) a_l
   =sum_i [mu_i exp(tL_i) a_{li}]
             prod_{j != i}[mu_j exp(tL_j)1],             (3)

and mu exp(tL) prod_i g_i equals prod_i[mu_i exp(tL_i)g_i].
Thus each integrand requires polynomially many local operations. There is
no sum over the full product state space or assumption of a low-rank answer.

Let Lambda=sum_i max_x[-Q_i(x,x)] and Amax=sum_i max_x a_i(x).
R0 is substochastic-resolvent positive with row sums at most 1/s, while
R is stochastic-resolvent positive with row sums exactly 1/s. The exact
inverse identity

 (I-V R0 U)^(-1)=I+V R U

therefore gives the infinity norm bound

 ||(I-V R0 U)^(-1)||_infinity <= 1+Amax/s.                (4)

Only log(1/s), not 1/s iterations, enters numerical conditioning. R0 norms,
U, V and the explicit observable bounds likewise have polynomial bit
lengths. Standard inverse perturbation with error less than half the
reciprocal of (4) supplies a certified finite-precision k-dimensional solve.

3. Fully acquired local matrix integrals

Compute each local stationary pi_i of Q_i by rational linear algebra, and
let pi0=otimes_i pi_i, H=prod_i [min_x pi_i(x)]^(-1). These are rational
with polynomial bit lengths. Put d=max(2,max_i d_i).

In L2(pi_i), stationary edge flow decomposes into directed cycles of
length at most d_i. Diagonalizing the cyclic shift on each cycle shows
|Im<f,-Q_i f>| <= cot(pi/d_i) Re<f,-Q_i f> <= (d/3)Re.
Adding the nonnegative real diagonal a_i preserves that sector bound.
The product sum -L has the same bound by summing coordinate slices.
Consequently exp(z L_i) is contractive in L2(pi_i) whenever
Re z>=0 and |Im z|<=Re z/d. Product integrands (3), including explicitly
signed product observables, have modulus at most H times their explicit
coefficient/norm bound throughout this cone. log H is charged.

For integral int_0^infinity exp(-s t) f(t)dt, choose real cutoffs
tmin <= delta/(8M) and tmax >= log(8M/(s delta))/s, where M bounds the
real/complex integrand without exp(-s t). Small and large discarded tails
are at most delta/8 each. One can use rational ceiling log2 bounds instead
of evaluating a logarithm. Split [tmin,tmax] into dyadic intervals. Each
interval has a Bernstein ellipse of parameter 1+1/(64d) inside the cone.
On it exp(-s z) is bounded by one and the integrand by M. Positive
Gauss-Legendre quadrature with polynomially many nodes in d, log M,
log(1/delta), and endpoint bit lengths therefore has error at most delta.
The number of intervals is polynomial in those same bit quantities.

Nodes, weights, square roots of pi_i, and local matrix exponentials are
computed with certified rational approximations. Scaling and squaring a
local exponential requires O(log(1+t ||L_i||)) squarings; contractivity
in the weighted norm bounds error growth, paid through guard bits. All
local dimensions are explicitly supplied. Equations (3)-(4) fix an oracle
with polynomial bit cost for mu R(s)g to any requested absolute precision.
This is the same explicit sector/quadrature construction as the adjacent
nonreversible_boundary/RESULT.txt, here used at positive real s only.

4. A computable mixing certificate, used only to select precision

Remove trivial one-state coordinates. If every coordinate is trivial,
the unique global state is sampled immediately. Otherwise put

 Dpath=sum_i(d_i-1), Rrate=2(Lambda+Amax),
 pstep=min(qmin/Rrate,1/2),
 T=Dpath/Rrate, eta=(pstep/4)^Dpath,                     (5)

where qmin is the least positive off-diagonal rate in the supplied Q_i.
Every pair x,y can be connected using at most Dpath base transitions:
in each irreducible local graph choose a simple path. Uniformize Q at
Rrate. Every selected base transition has probability at least pstep;
each self-loop has probability at least 1/2. Pad to Dpath transitions.
The probability of exactly Dpath Poisson events by T is
exp(-Dpath)Dpath^Dpath/Dpath! >=exp(-Dpath)>4^(-Dpath).
Thus, for EVERY pair x,y,

                    P_T(x,y) >= eta.                   (6)

In particular P_T(x,.)>=eta delta_y0(.) for a chosen fixed y0, giving
TV contraction by at least 1-eta per block. All quantities in (5) have
polynomial bit length, even when their values are exponentially small.
This algorithm never simulates T/eta time or constructs the full P_T.
Integrating the block mixing bound gives, for ||g||_infinity<=G,

 |s mu R(s)g - pi g| <= 2G s T/eta.                     (7)

Choose s<=epsilon eta/(8GT), evaluate mu R(s)g to epsilon/(2s), and
return s times that value. Total error is less than epsilon after ordinary
rounding. The bits of s grow with log(1/epsilon)+log(1/eta), which are
polynomial input quantities. This proves Claim A. A product-sum observable
can use G=sum_l prod_i max_x |g_{li}(x)|, computed from its explicit input.

5. Exact stationary sampling from certified prefix probabilities

Integrating (6) against stationary pi shows pi(y)>=eta for every full y.
Therefore every nonempty cylinder fixing a coordinate prefix has mass
at least eta. Such an indicator is one product observable, so Claim A
computes its stationary mass to arbitrary certified absolute precision.

At a prefix h, acquire interval bounds on pi(h,j)/pi(h) for each next
local value j. A denominator lower bound eta and absolute numerator/
denominator tolerances eta*2^(-b)/(32d) provide conditional cumulative
threshold intervals of width at most 2^(-b). All acquisition and division
costs are polynomial in input bits and b. The queried parent is always a
nonempty prefix. No conditional probability or stationary sample is supplied.

Draw one lazy Uniform[0,1] from fresh fair bits. At level b, its first b
bits specify an interval of length 2^(-b). Refine the probability intervals
and uniform bits until this uniform interval lies wholly between two
successive conditional cumulative thresholds. Output that value and move
to the next coordinate. Ties have probability zero. At level b failure to
decide has probability at most O(d 2^(-b)), since U must lie within a
constant multiple of 2^(-b) of one of at most d-1 thresholds. The thresholds
are true fixed real probabilities; the interval algorithm never makes an
incorrect comparison.

The expected work at one coordinate is bounded by a sum of the form
sum_b O(d 2^(-b)) poly(input_bits,b), hence polynomial. Summing over n
coordinates proves Claim B. Independent fair bits for different samples
give independent exact stationary samples. Runtime and precision have no
finite deterministic ceiling; expected cost and geometric continuation
tails are the actual guarantee. Finite fixed-precision executions alone
would establish only approximate sampling.

Input acquisition: all local Q_i, reset hazards and reset product laws are
explicit rational software/model data. No inference of independence,

separate stationary first-hit calibration theorem applies to its own
independent reversible interface. ACQUISITION.txt supplies a distinct reset

Preprocessing: rational local stationary solves, input norm/minorization
bounds and local matrix representations, all polynomial bit work/storage.
Per oracle query: polynomially many dyadic quadrature nodes, local matrix
exponentials and a k-dimensional solve, at explicitly chosen guard precision.
Per exact sample: polynomial expected oracle calls/arithmetic/random bits;
no direct dependence on product state count or numerical mixing time.

The standard identities used are Woodbury, uniformization, Doeblin
minorization, analytic quadrature, and lazy inverse-transform sampling.
State-dependent resetting and stochastic automata are existing fields.
The proposed contribution is their constructive polynomial-bit composition
for this explicit interaction class; originality remains UNKNOWN. Direct
state enumeration, ordinary trajectory mixing and generic tensor solvers

7. Extension: reducible local dynamics with a common full-support reset

Local irreducibility can be removed under a different explicit contract.
Allow arbitrary rational local generators Q_i, possibly reducible or zero.
Assume one supplied channel has a_l(x)>=h>0 for every configuration, and
its product reset law nu has strictly positive entries. The additive hazard
description makes h=sum_i min_x a_{li}(x) a computable lower bound whenever
it is positive. Let vmin=prod_i min_x nu_i(x)>0. These are explicit rational
inputs/bounds; log(1/h) and log(1/vmin) are charged.

This channel contains a state-independent rate-h Poisson reset to nu.
Two versions of the process can share that reset and then the same future
randomness, proving TV contraction by exp(-h t). The global Q therefore
has a unique stationary law even if the unreset local chains are reducible.
No claim is made here for arbitrary reducible local dynamics with no such
full-support reset.

To acquire stationary expectations, regularize each coordinate by adding
delta(J_i-I), where J_i has every row uniform on its local state set.
All local chains become irreducible and the preceding product-resolvent
algorithm applies. The full perturbation E has ||mu E||_TV<=n delta for
every probability row mu. Its stationary law pi_delta obeys

 pi_delta-pi = int_0^infinity pi_delta E exp(tQ) dt,
 ||pi_delta-pi||_TV <= n delta/h.                       (8)

The integral identity follows from pi_delta Q=-pi_delta E and convergence
of pi_delta exp(tQ) to pi; the integrand is a zero-mass signed measure,
so the common-reset contraction proves the inequality. For an observable
of magnitude at most G, choose rational delta<=epsilon*h/(16nG), and
compute pi_delta g to epsilon/2 by the irreducible construction. Its local
stationary weights may now be very small, but their logarithms and required
guard bits remain polynomial in input length and log(1/epsilon). Hence
Claim A persists. In fact the same common-reset coupling gives Abel bias
at most 2G s/h, avoiding the conservative path-based mixing certificate.

Exact sampling still needs a positive prefix lower bound for the ORIGINAL
chain. Put R=Lambda+Amax. Over time T=1/R, retain paths with one selected
common reset to y and no other potential event. They have total probability
at least h nu(y) T exp(-R T) >=h vmin/(4R). Thus every stationary state
has mass at least eta0=h vmin/(4R), and every nonempty prefix also does.
This argument includes reset attempts that leave the configuration unchanged;
using a uniformized potential-event representation makes them harmless.
Since R>=h>0, eta0 is well defined and has polynomial bit length.

Use the regularized stationary expectation oracle, with its explicit error
(8), at each precision level in the lazy prefix sampler. All decisions are
then certified for the unregularized stationary distribution. The same
geometric-tail argument proves exact independent samples in expected
polynomial bit time, with no numeric inverse-h simulation cost. A usual
common-reset trajectory sampler is already exact but may simulate a time
of mean 1/h; this does not establish superiority over its best analytic
or regenerative implementation.
