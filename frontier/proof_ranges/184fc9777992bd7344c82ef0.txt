## 4. A companion global class: complex one-coordinate envelopes

A related direct averaging argument covers a different class, useful for testing the limits of the numerical representation.

Let z(x) be a complex-valued periodic W^(1,4) function of one coordinate, let n be a nonzero integer, and identify

    phi(x,y)=z(x)exp(i n y)

with a real two-component potential. For an invariant quartic as in Section 3,

    inf_B E[F(B+Dphi)-F(B)] >= (mu_F/24) E|Dphi|^4.          (4.1)

Proof. Begin with smooth z having no zeros and write z=r exp(i vartheta), r>0. Remove the output rotation by invariance. The gradient becomes

    r [[r'/r,0],[vartheta',n]].

Let M=integral r^4 dx and average using the probability weight r^4/M. The weighted mean of r'/r is zero. Put tbar=integral r^4 vartheta'/M. Convexity in the first column and the rank-one Bregman estimate (3.3) give

    EF(Dphi) >= mu_F M(tbar²+n²)²
      +(mu_F/3) integral r^4 |(r'/r,vartheta')-(0,tbar)|^4.

The fourth power of the full gradient norm is at most eight times the sum of the two fourth-order expressions in this display (without coefficients), by |U+V|^4<=8(|U|^4+|V|^4). This gives the factor 1/24.

Averaging over y makes b=E DF(Dphi)=0: output rotation covariance of DF gives a full circle average of a vector. Also H>=0 by reciprocity and the Fourier/Legendre-Hadamard argument in Section 5. Thus every affine gap is at least EF(Dphi), proving (4.1).

For zeros of z, approximate by smooth curves with a small constant complex translation avoiding zero. A smooth curve has zero planar area, so such translations exist arbitrarily close to zero. Strong W^(1,4) continuity completes the proof. No winding-number restriction on z is imposed. QED.

This covers arbitrary complex envelopes of one coordinate, whereas Section 3 permits arbitrary real amplitudes of both coordinates. Neither theorem asserts positivity for general sums of differently phased carriers.
