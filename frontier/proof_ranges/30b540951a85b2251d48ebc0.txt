# Adaptive local schedules with an enforced hazard budget

Date: 2026-10-16. Worker `gf_causal_genesis`. This is the direct extension of the supplied causal-tester interface. It is distinct from the expected-action frontier in `MEAN_ACTION_FRONTIER.md`.

## Contract and theorem

There are finitely many local testers, each with its own initially-even private parity, private ledger, private immutable classical records, and independent initial auxiliary resources. A common prover has an arbitrary shared quantum bath/reference and may causally process every message jointly. It may receive copies of actual completed local records and send correlated classical commands to the testers. There is no shared initial tester resource.

Each tester follows one **fixed local policy**, represented as a finite causal circuit. A deterministic common round skeleton pads all branch-dependent timing with no-ops. Commands are measured and archived locally. The prover can influence the policy only through these typed messages. It cannot overwrite a private parity or ledger.

Before its next own gap begins, while its parity is known from the last complete cut, tester \(i\) authorizes a nonnegative cap \(a\). During that gap it permits arbitrary parity-preserving prover interactions/instruments and budgeted private odd rotations. Its local scheduling policy can change its rotation subdivisions or choose when to end the gap in response to past commands and records. On **every classical branch of that gap**, the sum of charged odd rotation angles is at most the preauthorized \(a\). A complete own-parity cut ends the gap. No postselected branch is discarded from the experiment.

Put

\[
s(a)=\sin^2(\min\{a,\pi/2\}),
\qquad h(a)=-\log(1-s(a)).
\]

For finite \(L_i\), a local ledger rejects any command that would make

\[
\sum_{\text{own gaps authorized before first mismatch}}h(a)>L_i. \tag{1}
\]

This cap must hold on **every surviving history against every prover**, not just on the realized run, an average run, or a chosen input bath. A rejected command causes no extra odd evolution; the tester can halt as good. After a first mismatch, the tester saves a permanent accept flag. What happens to its physical parity later is irrelevant to that flag.

**Theorem 2.** For every selected fixed set \(R\) and every compatible joint prover,

\[
\Pr(\text{every }i\in R\text{ ever mismatches})
\le\prod_{i\in R}(1-e^{-L_i}). \tag{2}
\]

Any terminal-all-flipped event is contained in the event in (2), provided the terminal measurement is included among the budgeted own cuts. The inequality is uniform over the full initial bath/reference input, hence yields the corresponding input-effect inequality.

For arbitrary finite \(L_i\), the bound is sharp over the broad interface: one gap with angle

\[
a_i=\arccos(e^{-L_i/2})
\]

has (h(a_i)=L_i\) and mismatch probability \(1-e^{-L_i}\). Independent qubit drives attain their product. More restrictive prescribed command palettes or round counts can have smaller values; sharpness is not asserted for every individual policy.

The local policies are independent **operations**, but neither commands, outcomes, physical bath states, nor prover actions are assumed independent. In particular, outcome feedback to a common controller is permitted in this typed interface.

## 1. A variable-time gap lemma

The fixed-angle circuit proof supplied in `causal_refresh_product/PROOF.md` scales its rotations by a complex parameter. The same argument extends to a finite classical scheduling tree with a hard action cap.

Fix one arbitrary prover strategy. Retain every instrument outcome and discarded environment in a dilation; the immutable local record makes different scheduling branches orthogonal. All parity-preserving operations are independent of the artificial scale \(z\). Every rotation has the form \(\exp(-iz\theta X)\), with a fixed nonnegative angle at its particular tree node. A message-conditioned rotation is just a different node of this fixed tree.

Let \(A_v\) be the maximum sum of remaining rotation angles along a path below node \(v\). The whole dilated subtree (W_v(z)\) satisfies

\[
\|W_v(z)\|\le e^{A_v|\operatorname{Im}z|}. \tag{3}
\]

To prove (3), work backwards. A rotation of angle \(\theta\) contributes at most \(e^{\theta|\operatorname{Im}z|}\). At a complete branching instrument \(\{K_b\}\), orthogonal outcome records give

\[
\|W_v(z)\psi\|^2
=\sum_b\|W_b(z)K_b\psi\|^2
\le e^{2A_v|\operatorname{Im}z|}
\sum_b\|K_b\psi\|^2
=e^{2A_v|\operatorname{Im}z|}\|\psi\|^2.
\]

Parity-preserving isometries have norm one. At the root \(A_v\le a\) by the branchwise cap.

The opposite-parity amplitude (F(z)\) is entire, of type at most \(a\), real-axis contractive, and satisfies (F(0)=0\). The scalar-to-operator sine lemma reproduced in the supplied proof therefore gives

\[
F(1)^*F(1)\le s(a)I. \tag{4}
\]

This is an unconditional bound for the **complete gap instrument**, uniform in its entering bath/reference. It is not a conditional sine estimate for a selected message outcome. Such normalization could invalidate it.

The analytic sine lemma is a classical Bernstein-inequality consequence and is an explicit imported premise here. The new point is the tree norm estimate (3), which handles causal variable timing without replacing pathwise action by the sum of all alternative branch actions.

## 2. Singleton ledger value

Fix any prover against one local tester. Before the first mismatch, its last own cut is still even. Conditional on its actual past local history and next authorization, its entering auxiliary/bath state may be arbitrary. The next gap has mismatch chance at most (s(a)\) by (4).

Let \(S_k\) indicate that no mismatch has occurred after \(k\) completed own gaps; halt branches are padded with zero-action gaps. Write \(H_k\) for the sum of the authorized (h(a)\) through those gaps, on the surviving history. Define

\[
Z_k=S_k e^{H_k}.
\]

Conditioning only on past records and the pre-gap authorization,

\[
\mathbb E[Z_{k+1}\mid\mathcal F_k,a]
\ge S_ke^{H_k}e^{h(a)}(1-s(a))=Z_k.
\]

The product equals one because (h(a)=-\log(1-s(a))\). If the controller randomizes the next authorization, averaging this inequality is legitimate. Finite \(L_i\) forbids an angle at or above \(\pi/2\) before success; if \(L_i=\infty\), simply use the trivial factor one.

At termination \(Z\le e^{L_i}S\), while \(\mathbb E Z\ge Z_0=1\). Therefore

\[
\Pr(\text{no mismatch})\ge e^{-L_i},
\qquad p_i\le 1-e^{-L_i}. \tag{5}
\]

The policy can reveal its own records to the prover. Once one fixes any prover strategy against this singleton policy, (4) and the ledger bound hold uniformly. No condition on another tester's future success is used.

An equivalent backwards proof assigns survival lower bound (e^{-(L_i-H)}\) to every surviving node. At a gap this lower bound is preserved because its no-mismatch probability is at least (e^{-h(a)}\), and the remaining ledger is at most (L_i-H-h(a)\). This avoids any need to interpret record probabilities as independent.

## 3. Why the adaptive policies still tensor

Every tester is one fixed finite causal circuit, including its command measurements, record messages, local timing choices, ledger checks, and acceptance flag. Its own operations do not read any other private tester register. All common control decisions are messages from the unrestricted joint prover. This is exactly the product-tester situation, despite adaptive physical clock choices.

Gutoski–Watrous Theorem 9 supplies, for each positive accept operator \(T_i\), a normalized causal operator \(R_i\) with

\[
0\le T_i\le p_i R_i.
\]

Tensor the normalized round-prefix constraints after padding the local circuits to one common finite round skeleton. Then \(\bigotimes_iR_i\) is normalized for the joint type, so

\[
\bigotimes_iT_i\le\left(\prod_ip_i\right)\bigotimes_iR_i.
\]

Pairing with any normalized compatible joint prover proves (2), using (5). The tensorization itself is established prior machinery, not a new theorem attributed to this worker. [Primary source: Gutoski and Watrous, *Toward a General Theory of Quantum Games*, Theorem 9](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/GeneralTheoryQuantumGames.pdf).

The source copy/prover/uncompute construction can supply every parity-preserving interaction: even an arbitrary returned message induces parity-diagonal Kraus operators on the private bit. Genuine classical outcome copies can be sent only after the corresponding actual instrument has created that classical record. No coherent parity is cloned or retrospectively pinched.

## 4. Qualified Hamiltonian transfer

The theorem is first proved for finite causal circuits. For a finite classical schedule tree of parity Hamiltonians, each edge has known typed endpoints and a preauthorized integrable envelope. The source sector-padding and contraction-as-an-average-of-conjugated-X construction can be applied edge by edge. There are finitely many classical branches; choose approximation errors uniformly over all of them and retain every branch record. The honest simulation preserves all branch probabilities and effects, without normalized conditioning. Each simulated edge is charged no more than its authorized cap.

For this transfer, all free controls and instruments on every branch must preserve the other active parities. If physical reservation lifecycles are used, concurrent full reservations must be disjoint on every allowed branch. Actual endpoint instruments, entry preparation, and all records must retain their original lifecycle meaning. This is a qualification to be checked, not a consequence of a scalar ledger.

The finite-tree statement does not silently cover continuous-state stopping clocks, a coherently superposed time register that can erase its history, unbounded-message precision, or a norm estimate determined only after observing the gap's success. Those require their own construction or a controlled limit.

## 5. A useful stopping policy and its costs

A simple policy accepts commands for a gap cap \(a\) only when (h(a)\) fits the remaining ledger. It may stop the coordinate immediately after its first recorded mismatch, or continue with parity-preserving dynamics until an actual required retirement instrument. This is an operation-level replacement for the fixed-schedule exclusion in the supplied theorem: refresh clocks can respond to genuine records while a uniform local certificate remains available.

The ledger is not an experiment that measures an unknown Hamiltonian norm. It must meter qualified envelopes supplied by the controller model. For a finite command alphabet and at most \(m\) gaps, it requires storage for an immutable command/record history and either exact symbolic charges or conservative finite-precision upper charges. If every numerical charge overestimates (h(a)\), the theorem remains valid; underestimation is not allowed.

Stopping can lower expected control work, but it does not remove worst-case reservation time, refresh duration, feedback latency, endpoint measurement, reset, or detector qualification. Geometry and contour arguments conditioned on random completed subsets still require an explicit reproof. A subset selected after observing the bad flags is not a fixed set \(R\) in (2).

## 6. Decisive failures of weaker hypotheses

**Mean ledger is insufficient for the product.** A common bit activates both parents with probability \(r=1/4\). Conditional on activation each uses one angle \(\pi/4\), so its own active hazard is \(\log2\) and success probability \(1/2\). Otherwise both are inactive. Then

\[
\mathbb EH_i=(\log2)/4,
\qquad \Pr(B_1B_2)=1/16,
\]

while a product of mean-ledger factors would be

\[
\big(1-2^{-1/4}\big)^2<1/16.
\]

For this one-gap externally randomized construction, the singleton Jensen bound remains true; its product does not. This is a finite classical controller construction, not a quantum-hedging subtlety.

Even that singleton mean-hazard conclusion fails for a general stopped adaptive experiment. Use two complete-cut attempts of angle \(\pi/4\), stopping on the first mismatch. Then success probability is \(3/4\), whereas the expected authorized hazard actually billed until stopping is \((3/2)\log2\), giving \(1-e^{-\mathbb EH}=1-2^{-3/2}<3/4\). The expectation is success-biased because successful histories stop earlier. This finite counterexample was supplied during the internal reconstruction by `gf_causal_adversary`; it does not affect the hard pathwise ledger theorem.

**Correlated initial tester coins are insufficient.** Giving independent-looking local policies a shared initial private activation bit changes their joint accept operator from a tensor product to a correlated tester. Their separate marginal values cannot be multiplied. Sending common commands through a prover is permitted by this theorem only because each tester still enforces its worst-case ledger against every command.

**Retrospective cost labels are insufficient.** A claimed small cap inferred after a favorable branch is not the pre-gap cap in (4). The estimate is for the complete gap map. This distinction is also why the weak-instrument action theorem has a different proof and a different constant.

**Controller readout is an additional operation.** The source model stores private refresh records inaccessible to physical controls. Revealing a genuine measurement outcome, or making a weak-herald record available, changes that interface. The theorem identifies exact sufficient conditions for the changed interface; it does not infer the required physical readout or ignore its cost.

## 7. Multi-output corollary without an independence claim

Let \(B_i\) be the permanent mismatch bits and \(q_i=1-e^{-L_i}\). Because (2) holds for every fixed subset, for nonnegative \(t_i\),

\[
\mathbb E\prod_i(1+t_iB_i)
\le\prod_i(1+t_iq_i). \tag{6}
\]

Expand both products into subset monomials; every coefficient is nonnegative. In particular, for nonnegative weights \(w_i\) and \(\lambda\ge0\),

\[
\mathbb E e^{\lambda\sum_iw_iB_i}
\le\prod_i\big[1+q_i(e^{\lambda w_i}-1)\big].
\]

This permits the ordinary optimized exponential tail bound. It does not assert stochastic domination by independent Bernoulli bits for every increasing event; threshold indicators need not have a nonnegative multilinear expansion. The corollary is an elementary consequence of subset moments, not a historical novelty claim.
