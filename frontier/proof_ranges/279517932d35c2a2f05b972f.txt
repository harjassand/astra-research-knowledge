## 2. Closure-preserving uniformization

All entropies and relative entropies below use natural logarithms, except where base two is explicitly indicated.

### Theorem 1

Let \(\mathcal F\) be a finite union-closed family with \(m\ge2\) members. Let \(p\) be a strictly positive probability distribution on \(\mathcal F\), increasing under inclusion as in (1).

For each integer \(k\ge1\), set

\[
b=\lceil\log_2m\rceil,
\qquad
L_k=2kb+1.
\]

There exists a family

\[
\mathcal U_k\subseteq\mathcal F^k
\]

that is closed under coordinatewise union and invariant under permutations of its \(k\) blocks, such that, for \(Q_k=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal U_k)\),

\[
\boxed{
Q_k(x)\le 4L_k\,p^{\otimes k}(x)
\quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal F^k.
}
\tag{2}
\]

Moreover, for every \(1\le\ell\le k\),

\[
\boxed{
D\!\left((Q_k)_{1:\ell}\,\middle\|\,p^{\otimes\ell}\right)
\le
\frac{\ell}{k}\log(4L_k)
}
\tag{3}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}\!\left((Q_k)_{1:\ell},p^{\otimes\ell}\right)
\le
\sqrt{\frac{\ell\log(4L_k)}{2k}}.
}
\tag{4}
\]

In particular, every fixed number of blocks converges jointly to independent samples from \(p\).

The construction is a product-likelihood superlevel family:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal U_k=
\left\{(A_1,\ldots,A_k)\in\mathcal F^k:
\prod_{j=1}^k p(A_j)\ge\tau_k
\right\},
}
\tag{5}
\]

for an appropriately selected dyadic threshold \(\tau_k\).

### Proof

Write

\[
\mu=p^{\otimes k}.
\]

For \(X\sim\mu\), the nonnegative random variable

\[
Z=-\log_2\mu(X)
\]

satisfies

\[
\mathbb EZ=kH_2(p)\le k\log_2m\le kb.
\]

Markov’s inequality gives

\[
\Pr(Z\ge L_k)\le\frac{kb}{2kb+1}<\frac12.
\]

Thus more than half the probability lies among points satisfying

\[
\mu(x)>2^{-L_k}.
\]

Partition those points into the \(L_k\) bands

\[
B_j=
\left\{
x:2^{-(j+1)}<\mu(x)\le2^{-j}
\right\},
\qquad 0\le j<L_k.
\]

At least one band has

\[
\mu(B_j)\ge\frac1{2L_k}.
\tag{6}
\]

Choose such a \(j\), put \(\tau=2^{-(j+1)}\), and define

\[
\mathcal U=\{x:\mu(x)\ge\tau\},
\qquad N=|\mathcal U|.
\]

Every point of \(B_j\) has mass at most \(2\tau\), so

\[
\frac1{2L_k}
\le\mu(B_j)
\le2\tau|B_j|
\le2\tau N.
\]

Therefore

\[
\tau N\ge\frac1{4L_k}.
\tag{7}
\]

For \(Q=\operatorname{Unif}(\mathcal U)\) and \(x\in\mathcal U\),

\[
\frac{Q(x)}{\mu(x)}
=
\frac1{N\mu(x)}
\le\frac1{N\tau}
\le4L_k.
\]

This proves (2), and hence

\[
D(Q\|\mu)\le\log(4L_k).
\tag{8}
\]

It remains to establish closure and the local bounds.

If \(x=(A_1,\ldots,A_k)\) and \(y=(B_1,\ldots,B_k)\) belong to \(\mathcal U\), then inclusion-monotonicity gives

\[
p(A_j\cup B_j)\ge p(A_j)
\]

for every \(j\). Consequently,

\[
\mu(x\cup y)\ge\mu(x)\ge\tau.
\]

Thus \(\mathcal U\) is union-closed. Its permutation invariance follows immediately from the product defining \(\mu\).

For an exchangeable random vector \(X_1,\ldots,X_k\), the conditional entropies

\[
h_j=H(X_j\mid X_1,\ldots,X_{j-1})
\]

are nonincreasing: conditioning reduces entropy, and exchangeability identifies the corresponding conditional distributions. Therefore

\[
H(X_1,\ldots,X_\ell)
\ge\frac{\ell}{k}H(X_1,\ldots,X_k).
\]

Since all one-block marginals coincide,

\[
\begin{aligned}
D(Q_{1:\ell}\|p^{\otimes\ell})
&=-H(Q_{1:\ell})-\ell\,\mathbb E_{Q_1}\log p(X_1)\\
&\le-\frac{\ell}{k}H(Q)-\ell\,\mathbb E_{Q_1}\log p(X_1)\\
&=\frac{\ell}{k}D(Q\|p^{\otimes k}).
\end{aligned}
\]

Combining this with (8) proves (3). Pinsker’s inequality gives (4). ∎

### Why these are actual set families

Encode a tuple by

\[
\Phi(A_1,\ldots,A_k)
=
\{(j,i)\in[k]\times[n]:i\in A_j\}.
\]

This map is injective and preserves unions. Hence \(\Phi(\mathcal U_k)\) is an ordinary union-closed family of **distinct sets** on \(kn\) elements.

There are no multiplicity weights and no extra selector element that becomes frequent merely because it records which copy was chosen.

### Scope of the operation

The underlying argument is slightly more general than union closure. For a finite binary operation \(f\), the same construction preserves closure whenever

\[
p(f(a,b))^2\ge p(a)p(b).
\]

Indeed, on product spaces this implies

\[
p^{\otimes k}(f^{\otimes k}(x,y))
\ge
\sqrt{p^{\otimes k}(x)p^{\otimes k}(y)},
\]

so every product-likelihood superlevel set is \(f\)-closed.

For union, this condition is precisely positive union-closed support together with increasing weights.
