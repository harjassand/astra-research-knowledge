# Autonomous correction: acquired aggregate-data evaluator

## Status and decision
Acquired one actual correction experiment: Li et al., two trapped Ca+ ions, a four-level spin code with error-to-motion conversion and sympathetic cooling. Public source data: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20768851 . This is a deterministic, periodically applied dissipative recovery operation, not single-qubit Zeno slowing and not a continuously autonomous Lindbladian implementation.

Run `python evaluate.py --threshold 0.75 --time-ms 29.0445`. It uses only Python standard libraries and the deposited CSVs. `evaluation.json` is its checked output. Author simulation code was downloaded as part of the archive but was NOT executed or imported. Source aggregates are preserved byte-for-byte; no raw counts have been inferred.

### What the acquired data establish
- Both normalized and explicitly unnormalized process-fidelity time series are available. Ambient data have eight matched storage times; injected-noise data have 18 physical/no-QEC times and 11 QEC times, including five exact matched QEC/physical times.
- Publisher Gaussian decay fits give QEC/physical lifetime ratios 1.4520 normalized and 1.4616 unnormalized under ambient noise, and 11.8686 normalized and 11.3615 unnormalized under injected noise. These are derived from the deposited fit coefficients, not the older manuscript headline.
- At the actual common time 29.0445 ms, ambient QEC minus physical fidelity is +0.078 normalized and +0.065 unnormalized. Across the 15 separately deposited interleaved pairs at approximately 29 ms, 11/15 favor QEC; mean improvements are 0.08853 and 0.08140 respectively. These are within-batch comparisons, not held-out validation.
- Ambient pointwise advantage is not uniform: QEC is worse at 3.2405, 16.1425, 41.9465 and 48.3975 ms, and better at 9.6915, 22.5935, 29.0445 and 35.4955 ms. Do not report a continuous measured superiority window or exact empirical crossing from these sparse nonmonotonic measurements.
- Every one of the five exactly matched injected-noise times favors QEC in both representations: 0.7555, 2.2365, 4.458, 11.1225, 15.5655 ms. No interpolation was used for these comparisons.
- A chosen useful-memory threshold Fchi=0.75 illustrates why lifetime ratio alone is insufficient. Publisher-model crossing times (QEC versus physical) are ambient normalized 27.389 vs 22.875 ms, ambient unnormalized 25.236 vs 21.368 ms; injected normalized 7.619 vs 0.823 ms, injected unnormalized 6.516 vs 0.833 ms. These are model-derived crossings, not raw observed crossings. The threshold is an evaluator choice, not the authors' criterion.

### Comparator and channel conventions
The associated April 2025 arXiv v1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16746v1) identifies the physical competitor as the least magnetic-field-sensitive pair |−1/2>_D, |+1/2>_D in the same ion, rather than the inferior auxiliary S-manifold qubit. Both compared with the uncorrected encoded logical qubit. The experiment corrects first-order dephasing; a spin echo/dynamical-decoupling optimized competitor under the same low-frequency noise and equal control budget is absent. Thus this does not establish superiority over the strongest available controlled memory protocol.

The four process-tomography input states in v1 are 0,1,+,+i; outputs undergo state tomography using maximum-likelihood estimation. Leakage is treated by adding a proportion of maximally mixed state to restore normalization. Fidelity convention is Tr(chi_measured chi_identity), with a 4x4 process matrix. Archive chi files explicitly use the Pauli basis ordered I,X,Y,Z in row-major element labels II,IX,...,ZZ; II is the identity-process fidelity (consistent with the reported values). Only real elements and marginal bootstrap errors are present; imaginary elements, count records, bootstrap samples, covariance, and a full CP/TP reconstruction prescription are absent. Do not interpret these files as executable complete quantum channels or claim they certify CP/TP. MLE is specified for state tomography; a subsequent CPTP-constrained process fit is not established by the inspected sources.

The main-versus-ED data are labelled normalized versus unnormalized tomography in the archive, not simply SPAM-corrected versus raw-shot data. The exact revised normalization reconstruction is unavailable. They cannot be silently equated to v1's mixed-state leakage treatment. Encoding/decoding fidelity is approximately 0.979 (archived chi_II); the main memory data retain differing fitted amplitudes and offsets, which must not be renormalized away. Marginal bootstrap standard deviations and fit standard errors are available. Covariance-aware confidence intervals and shot-level held-out calibration are not.

### Timing and control-cost audit
Actual CSV storage time is authoritative for evaluator comparisons. Ambient QEC times satisfy t_ms=0.015+3.2255*N. Injected QEC times satisfy t_ms=0.015+0.7405*N. Derived cycle rates are about 310.03 Hz and 1350.44 Hz, respectively; these are cycle frequencies, not calibrated Lindblad correction rates. The extra 15 microseconds and precise 5.5/25.5-microsecond increments are present in the CSV axis but not independently reconciled to a full revised control schedule.

V1 separately specifies: entropy conversion 620 microseconds (including shaped ramps); reset/cooling 90 microseconds; addressing switching 20 microseconds; initial-state pulses each have a 20-microsecond pi time; encoding 50 microseconds and inverse decoding; readout fidelity 99.7%. Ambient correction cycle is rounded to 3.2 ms, including 2.6 ms resetting/idling; injected resetting/idling reduces to 0.12 ms, yielding approximately 0.74 ms per complete cycle. Eight simultaneous 729 nm tones implement recovery, two ions and a shared motional mode are required, and 397/866 nm optical pumping dissipates entropy. No intermediate syndrome detector or feed-forward is used. Optical pumping, initial cooling, final detection wall time, total power/energy and full per-run control costs are not exhaustively reported. Therefore the archive permits storage-time/cycle-cost comparisons but NOT a full end-to-end preparation-to-final-readout throughput or energy-efficiency comparison.

V1 noise calibration: injected Gaussian magnetic field standard deviation about 16 nT, updated each 100 ms, with stated 10 Hz cutoff; ambient midnight peak-to-peak approximately 0.9 nT but occasional spikes. The archive contrasts two environments with DIFFERENT cycle rates, so it is not an independently controlled noise-rate × correction-rate response surface. Do not fit a universal scaling law to the two points. Fig3d has measured conversion-time sweeps, but its column labels/initial-state description deserve reconciliation before fitting a correction constant: the upper curve labelled logical starts around 0.96 despite the described error-state initial condition. Preserve that ambiguity rather than swap labels silently.

### Version and inference limits
The archive was published June 20, 2026, created/modified June 23, 2026, whereas available arXiv has only v1, April 23, 2025. Its ambient headline 60.9±33.0 vs 26.0±5.4 ms is NOT the archive's normalized 42.6616±9.0023 vs 29.3806±2.5840 ms. An author conference abstract at ETH's MVQC 2026 reports 42.7/29.4 ms and enhancement 1.45±0.33, supporting the existence of a revised analysis, but is not a complete revised methods paper. The Zenodo page does not expose a linked journal publication/related identifier. No matching final full paper was identified in this bounded search. Do not combine the old numerical fits with the newer archive, or infer changed methods are identical.

Simple standardized residual diagnostics are included: notably injected physical Gaussian fit has sum squared residuals ≈543 for 18 normalized points (nominal n−3=15), while ambient physical gives ≈34 for 8 points. This warns against treating reported marginal point errors and the fitted Gaussian as a complete independent-noise likelihood. No p-values or calibrated significance are claimed.

## Scientific use for Astra
This is an executable aggregate-level check of whether finite-duration autonomous correction improves same-device memory performance and whether that conclusion survives using the unnormalized reported comparator. It tests measured finite operating points, useful-fidelity threshold sensitivity and a nonzero implementation overhead. It cannot evaluate arbitrary input process composition, certify hardware-uniform finite-resource bounds, demonstrate fault-tolerant scaling, establish Markovian/non-Markovian threshold laws, or rank against ideal measurement and feedback without matched resource/latency accounting. Missing next operation is acquisition of the revised full analysis and complex process/shot-level records with independent correction-rate and noise sweeps and a matched dynamical-decoupling/control competitor. No outreach was performed.

## Exact central-estimate CP-completion obstruction (additional audit)
Run `python check_chi.py`; `chi_cp_obstruction.json` contains the results. All three deposited central chi arrays already exclude any completely positive (CP) completion, independently of the omitted imaginary entries. In a linearly independent Pauli operator basis, CP requires the Hermitian chi matrix to be positive semidefinite. Every diagonal must therefore be nonnegative:

- Fig2b: YY = −0.0016661025236856097, marginal bootstrap SD 0.0035567533690011506.
- Fig3e: XX = −0.03640368333239791, marginal bootstrap SD 0.02044493143209728.
- Fig3f: YY = −0.05635211949633728, marginal bootstrap SD 0.08301413418255371.

Choosing imaginary entries cannot repair a negative diagonal. In addition, the stored real arrays have maximum transpose discrepancies 2.1742e−7, 6.0119e−7 and 3.7933e−7 respectively. Exact Hermiticity would require these to vanish. These tiny inconsistencies are compatible with numerical reconstruction/serialization effects; their cause is not established. They are vastly smaller than the negative-diagonal obstruction. All real traces equal one to displayed floating-point precision, which does not establish positivity or trace preservation.

For a diagnostic only, symmetrizing the real arrays gives minimum eigenvalues −0.00398784, −0.07624940 and −0.06876805. No PSD Hermitian completion can have this real symmetric part: on a real test vector, the imaginary antisymmetric part contributes zero to the quadratic form. This is an additional deterministic obstruction, not a statistical rejection. Symmetrization is not applied to the source CSVs, and no repair/projection is silently performed.

Crucially, these are statistically uncertain reconstructed central estimates, NOT evidence that the physical devices enact non-CP evolution. The negative diagonals reach zero after upward changes of 0.4684, 1.7806 and 0.6788 times their respective marginal bootstrap SDs. The first and third are inside the corresponding ±1-SD marginal ranges; all three are inside ±2-SD marginal ranges. This only measures sensitivity. Marginal errors provide neither a joint covariance nor a valid multivariate confidence region, and making the diagonal nonnegative alone does not establish a feasible PSD/TP matrix. No statistical significance, physical non-CP claim, or confidence-level CP exclusion follows.

Consequently, even obtaining the missing imaginary central values would not make these fixed central arrays executable physical channels. A declared reconstruction/physicalization method plus full complex data, leakage handling and joint uncertainty is needed. An arbitrary CP projection would be a new modelling choice, not recovery of an observed channel.
