## 3. Why the representation changed

My initial approaches tried to select a good discrete output directly, either by minimizing a separable energy or by transferring a distributional operation while retaining matroid structure.

Both proposed mechanisms failed. One exact construction shows that even when
\[
A^\top A=I,
\]
global minimization of
\[
\|A\varepsilon\|_4^4
\]
can select signings with arbitrarily large maximum-coordinate error while a signing with error exactly one exists. The source bundle contains the construction and proof.

The surviving approach instead works with the **geometry of every feasible face**. It asks:

> While many coordinates remain undecided, does the current feasible face contain enough directions to escape a minimum of an error-controlling potential?

The decisive bridge is elementary.

### Face-dimension lemma

If every edge of \(P\) changes at most \(k\) coordinates, and a face \(F\) has \(s(F)\) nonconstant coordinates, then
\[
\boxed{
\dim F\ge\frac{s(F)}{k}.
}
\]

**Proof.** Edge directions of \(F\) span its translation space. Choose \(\dim F\) independent edge directions spanning that space. Their supports together contain at most \(k\dim F\) coordinates. Every coordinate outside this union is constant on \(F\). Therefore
\[
s(F)\le k\dim F.
\]
\(\square\)

This statement persists after every restriction to a face. That persistence is what makes the rounding argument possible.
