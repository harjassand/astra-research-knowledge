Simulating correlated noise without generating its time history
The strongest result of this investigation is a finite-seed quantum simulator for systems driven by correlated classical noise. It does not depend on the earlier time-history or Lindblad candidates.
The construction replaces a potentially enormous noise trajectory with a private seed that reproduces every correlation the simulation circuit can detect. It then simulates the complete noise-averaged quantum channel, including correlations across prescribed control and measurement times.
For the noise models specified below, the derived gate bound is
\[
\boxed{
G=\widetilde O\!\left(\mathcal A\,C_{\mathrm{sel}}+\mathcal A^2\right)
+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}},
\qquad
\mathcal A=1+p+T\left(\alpha_0+\sum_a\alpha_a\right).
}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}\) is the actual cost of selecting and implementing the supplied physical operators and parameters, and \(p\) is the number of control slots. The dependence on the noise correlation rates and channel accuracy is through logarithmic factors, not the number of resolved time steps or noise-switching events.
The quadratic term is real: it pays for evaluating the finite-seed function. There is no free noise oracle, uncharged random-access memory, or cryptographic pseudorandomness assumption.
This is a resource-accounted algorithmic candidate, not yet a certified original breakthrough. Its strongest consequence is a uniform way to simulate noncommuting dynamics under Gaussian and non-Gaussian colored noise while retaining environmental memory. I have not established superiority over every specialized multiscale or pseudomode algorithm.
1. The selected task and the prior-art boundary
The initial alternatives included stiff reaction-network simulation and inverse recovery from snapshots. Correlated-noise simulation offered the clearest missing operation: efficient Hamiltonian simulation can query many times coherently, but a classical procedure that generates noise sequentially does not automatically supply those queries consistently.
Several ingredients are established and must not be counted as discoveries.
Virtual Brownian trees already generate correlated paths nonchronologically from a seed, with logarithmic dependence on time resolution. Jelinčič–Foster–Kidger also construct the required Brownian integrals and establish joint-distribution guarantees in their classical query setting. arXiv
The fact that \(2Q\)-wise independence suffices to replace independent random-function values for a \(Q\)-query quantum algorithm is also established. Zhandry’s construction explicitly uses this fact and the polynomial method to avoid an additional pseudorandom-function assumption. Mark Zhandry
Likewise, truncated-Dyson simulation already implements time ordering through coherent time queries with logarithmic precision dependence. arXiv
The proposed contribution is the complete composition: explicit correlated-noise bridges, finite-bit approximation, bounded-query replacement, physical-channel error bounds, persistent memory under interventions, and paid gate costs.

2. Precise computational theorem
Consider an \(n\)-qubit Hamiltonian
\[
H_X(t)=H_0+\sum_{a=1}^{r}X_a(t)A_a,
\tag{2}
\]
where \(H_0,A_a\) are Hermitian. No commutation is assumed, either among the \(A_a\) or with \(H_0\).
The independent stationary noise modes may be of either type:
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck noise
\[
dX_a=-\lambda_aX_a\,dt+\sqrt{2\lambda_a}\,dW_a,
\qquad
\mathbb E[X_a(t)X_a(s)]=e^{-\lambda_a|t-s|}.
\tag{3}
\]
For \(\lambda_a=0\), this means one time-independent standard Gaussian variable.
Symmetric random telegraph noise
\[
X_a(t)\in\{-1,1\},
\]
with switching rate \(\lambda_a/2\), again giving covariance \(e^{-\lambda_a|t-s|}\).
Physical noise amplitudes are included in \(A_a\); unit variance does not remove their computational cost.
The target channel is
\[
\Phi_T(\rho)
=
\mathbb E_X\!\left[U_X(T)\rho U_X(T)^\dagger\right].
\tag{4}
\]
The input provides finite descriptions of the rates and actual controlled, inverse, and indexed implementations of
\[
H_0/\alpha_0,\qquad A_a/\alpha_a.
\]
The normalization factors bound the operator norms. Their preparation, selection, coefficient loading, and working-precision costs are included in \(C_{\mathrm{sel}}(b)\).
The algorithm constructs a circuit whose reduced output channel satisfies
\[
\boxed{\|\widetilde\Phi_T-\Phi_T\|_\diamond\le\varepsilon.}
\tag{5}
\]
The input may be unknown and entangled with a reference, but it is initially independent of the noise.
More strongly, insert \(p\) arbitrary supplied instruments at prescribed times. Their choices may depend on earlier recorded outcomes. The same construction approximates the complete controlled output channel, including those records, uniformly over such controllers. The controller cannot inspect the private noise seed.
Define
\[
S=\sum_a\alpha_a,
\qquad
\mathcal A=1+p+T(\alpha_0+S).
\tag{6}
\]
Then (1) holds. The suppressed factors are fixed polynomials in the parameter bit length and logarithms of
\[
r+2,\quad
1+\lambda_{\max}T,\quad
1+\mathcal A,\quad
1/\varepsilon.
\]
They do not conceal powers of a noise-grid size.
The proof follows.
