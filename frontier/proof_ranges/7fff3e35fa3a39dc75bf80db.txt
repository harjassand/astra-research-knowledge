## 6. Quantitative coercivity and non-polyconvexity

Set \(N(A)=|A|^4=(u+v)^2\) and

\[
 H_3(A)=(u+v)^2+4w^2
       =\frac{|x+y|^4+|x-y|^4}{2}.
\]

The exact identity

\[
 F_0=\frac12N+\frac12H_3+\frac32q^2
\]

shows that \(F_0-N/2\) is polyconvex: \(H_3\) is convex and \(q^2\) is a convex function of the determinant.

For \(0\le a=|\ell|/\kappa<1\), decompose

\[
 F_\ell=(1-a)F_0+aF_{\operatorname{sgn}(\ell)\kappa}.
\]

It follows that \(F_\ell-\frac{1-a}{2}N\) is quasiconvex. For any Euclidean vectors \(A,B\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 |A+B|^4-|A|^4-4|A|^2A\!:\!B
 ={}&\frac13|B|^4+6(A\!:\!B+|B|^2/3)^2\\
 &+2(|A|^2|B|^2-(A\!:\!B)^2)
 \ge\frac13|B|^4.
\end{aligned}
\]

After integration the linear term vanishes, proving the quantitative bound in Theorem A.

For the retained example \(F_*=F_{-29/10}\), this gives

\[
 \int_\Omega[F_*(A_0+D\varphi)-F_*(A_0)]
 \ge c_*\int_\Omega|D\varphi|^4,
 \quad c_*=\frac{1-(29/10)/\kappa}{6}
 \approx0.00312348553338.
\]

For non-polyconvexity let \(h=|\ell|>11/4\) and, after an output reflection if needed, use \(F_{-h}\). With \(I=\operatorname{diag}(1,1)\), \(J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\),

\[
 F_{-h}(I)=11/2-2h<0,\qquad F_{-h}(J)=11/2+2h.
\]

For sufficiently large \(t\), put mass \(1/[2(1+t^2)]\) at each of \(tI,-tI\), and mass \(t^2/[2(1+t^2)]\) at each of \(J,-J\). The mean matrix and mean determinant are both zero, while its mean energy equals

\[
 \frac{t^2}{1+t^2}[t^2(11/2-2h)+(11/2+2h)]<0.
\]

This contradicts Jensen for any convex representation in \((A,\det A)\), proving non-polyconvexity. This distribution is used only to disprove polyconvexity; it is not asserted to be a compatible gradient law. The actual gradient inequality has already been proved in Section 4.

At \(h=29/10,t=7\), this is exactly the prior witness with probabilities \(1/100,1/100,49/100,49/100\) and expected energy \(-833/250\).

### A small rational certificate for the retained example

There is also a certificate requiring no algebraic number field. For $F_*=F_{-29/10}$, take

\[
 (t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3,t_4,t_5)
 =(74/5,-73/10,37/5,6/5,16/5,6/5).
\]

The compact primitive becomes

\[
 \alpha_{\rm int}
 =\left(\frac{37}{40}(u+v)+\frac15q\right)\theta
 +\left(\frac{13}{10}(u+v)-3q\right)\zeta
 +\frac98(u-v)\theta_a.
\]

Its Gram matrix is

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}
12&13/5&54/5&-23/5&87/10&13/10\\
13/5&8&26/5&8/5&3&29/10\\
54/5&26/5&74/5&-73/10&37/5&6/5\\
-23/5&8/5&-73/10&43/5&-11/5&16/5\\
87/10&3&37/5&-11/5&7&6/5\\
13/10&29/10&6/5&16/5&6/5&4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The six leading principal minors of $G-I_6/8$ are

\[
\frac{95}{8},\quad\frac{138809}{1600},\quad
\frac{20833971}{64000},\quad\frac{1017343033}{2560000},\quad
\frac{2061679179}{102400000},\quad\frac{3181489269}{819200000}.
\]

They are all positive. Thus $G\succ I_6/8$. Lemma B now proves quasiconvexity of the retained example independently of the sharp endpoint calculations. Using

\[
 |z_+|^2+|z_-|^2=|A|^2(|X|^2+|Y|^2+|Z|^2)
\]

and $D^2|A|^4[B,B]\le12|A|^2|B|^2$ also proves $F_*-|A|^4/192$ quasiconvex. Its elementary uniform lower bound is $\int|D\varphi|^4/576$. The sharper constant $c_*$ above follows from the full-family theorem.
