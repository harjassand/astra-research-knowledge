\section{A coefficient-action failure under normalization}
A second obstruction occurs before solving a cohomological equation. Take $\Gamma=C_2=\langle s\mid s^2=1\rangle$ and, for $n\geq2$, let
\[
U_n=\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\pi/4},e^{-i\pi/4},1,\ldots,1).
\]
Then $\|U_n^2-I\|_{2,n}=2/\sqrt n\to0$, so these define asymptotic representations. But $X_n=\sqrt n E_{12}$ has normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm one, and
\[
\operatorname{Ad}(U_n)X_n=iX_n,
\qquad \operatorname{Ad}(U_n)^2X_n=-X_n.
\]
Consequently the induced conjugation operator on the full Hilbert ultraproduct of the spaces $(M_n,\|\cdot\|_{2,n})$ does not satisfy $s^2=1$. It is not a representation of $C_2$, so group-cohomology vanishing cannot be invoked on that space as though it were.

On sequences uniformly bounded in operator norm, an error $K_n\to I$ in normalized Hilbert--Schmidt norm satisfies
\[
\|K_nX_nK_n^*-X_n\|_{2,n}
\leq2\sup_n\|X_n\|_\op\,\|K_n-I\|_{2,n}\to0.
\]
The action is valid on the closed subspace generated by these sequences. This does not solve the problem: $\sqrt nE_{12}$ is orthogonal in the ultralimit to every such bounded sequence, because its pairing has modulus at most $\sup_n\|X_n\|_\op/\sqrt n$. Normalized defect vectors can concentrate in precisely this omitted component. Even the stable finite group $C_2$ exhibits the method failure.
