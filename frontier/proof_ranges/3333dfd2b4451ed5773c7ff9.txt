The first member of the main family is
\[ Q_1=\frac1{64} \begin{pmatrix} -46&16&16&14\\ 14&-48&16&18\\ 16&18&-50&16\\ 16&14&18&-48 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad f=(1,-1,1,-1), \]
with uniform stationary distribution.
Its observed process is exactly time symmetric and has
\[ \mathbb E[Y_0Y_t]=e^{-t}. \]
Nevertheless,
\[ \mathbb E[Y_0Y_sY_{s+t}] = \frac{st}{256}e^{-(s+t)}, \]
and
\[ \chi_1(t) = 1-e^{-t} -\frac1{512}[1-(1+t)e^{-t}]. \]
The larger family increases the first visible passive correlation order without increasing the order of the controlled response defect.
The dense rational generator uses
\[ O(n^2\log(r+2)) \]
bits, and can be constructed in polynomial time in \(r\) and its explicit output size. The active test uses only the observed sign, two small field values, zero-field waiting, and endpoint measurements. No reconstruction of the hidden states is required by the test.
The separation is not restricted to one isolated set of numerical rates. Replacing the ladder amplitudes by values in \([1/2,1]\), paired under the reflection symmetry, preserves the exponential passive bound and polynomial active bound up to constants.
But this is structured robustness, not robustness to arbitrary microscopic errors. Generic perturbations can introduce shorter observation sequences and make passive detection easier. An open neighborhood in the full space of rate matrices with the same separation has not been established.
Several tempting shortcuts were rejected.
An eigenvalue-only approach was unsuitable: a September 2026 counterexample exhibits finite oscillatory eigenvalue data with vanishing stationary entropy production. The present argument relies instead on observation probabilities and their interaction with control. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34352v1)
Low-order moment matching alone would not justify the full-record lower bound. The regeneration argument is essential. Likewise, an abstract orthogonal symmetry would not establish time symmetry of actual observations; the construction supplies a genuine label-preserving state permutation. Finally, treating linear response as freely measurable would conceal the principal experimental cost; finite fields, sample counts, mixing waits, and setting precision are explicitly charged.
The supported consequence is an information-theoretic separation between passive observation and controlled thermodynamic inference, with an explicit positive stochastic construction and no computational hardness assumption.
It identifies an obstruction that better passive algorithms cannot remove within the stated observation budget, and supplies a specific intervention that bypasses it. This is more than a new notation or an equivalent formulation of a difficult inference problem.
The candidate original contribution is the combination of arbitrarily deep observational concealment, exact observed time symmetry, rapid microscopic mixing, an exponential full-path lower bound, and a polynomial conservative-field test.
It is not a universal algorithm for hidden thermodynamics, a demonstrated laboratory device, or a new energy technology. The consequential next step would be a constructive theory identifying which observable–control combinations admit efficient certificates beyond this engineered family. That general characterization is not proved here.
The executed package contains 6,416 counted exact finite checks, plus separately labelled numerical entropy and response diagnostics. These are falsification tests, not independent proof validation.
