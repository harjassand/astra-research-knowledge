Small coupling singular values, exponentially large block integrals, and nonlocal fill-in must be charged.

Several counterexamples constrain the next attack. A singular coupling leaves uncovered modes. Complex couplings generated by elimination need not preserve the accretive heat geometry. A block selected using an auxiliary variable that is subsequently integrated out requires its selection event to remain inside that integral; dropping it introduces bias. Finally, bounded local factors do not imply a favourable global average phase.

That theorem must also survive stronger comparisons. Optimised contours and complex normalising flows already have successful scalar-field demonstrations. Constructive resummation and interpolation can exploit the same weak-coupling structure. Their performance cannot be dismissed without matched implementations or a genuine complexity separation. 

There are applicable cautions, but no blanket impossibility claim. Generic fermionic sign-problem constructions are NP-hard; that is not a hardness reduction for this quartic class. Separately, some complex-coupling gauge models have exponential sign problems that no contour deformation removes; that result does not exclude partial integration or prove the present target hard. 

Research decision
Stop the strongly gapped independent estimator as a breakthrough direction. It removes the previous engineered quartics and prohibitive time-stepping, but the comparator and parameter tests do not show new scientific capability.

Preserve the rank-local defect bound and exact conditional-resonance operation. The genuine local result is that exact integration removes a logarithmic sampling-variance singularity while retaining the original answer. Its ingredients—conditional expectation, Schur complements, and Fourier heat evolution—are not themselves claimed as historically new.

The strongest remaining mathematical opening is therefore a globally affordable, coefficient-acquired treatment of coupled resonances and their residual phase. That theorem is unresolved. Without it, this investigation has made local calculations reliable, not established a new affordable solution to a consequential many-body sign problem.
