## Mathematical statement accepted

Let G be a real homogeneous quartic on real 2-by-2 matrices, invariant under left SO(2), with symmetric polarization `G(X)=T(X,X,X,X)`. A supplied scalar lambda must give a positive semidefinite quadratic form

`Q(X)=(1/2)D²G(I)[X,X]-lambda det X`.

Let K be its kernel and impose `T(I,Z1,Z2,E)=0` for every pair `Z1,Z2 in K` and every E. For nonzero Q, let kappa be any positive lower bound for Q on the Frobenius-orthogonal complement of K. Let `M>0` bound T's multilinear norm; the absolute sum of coefficients of G in the expanded standard entry coordinates is valid. Then

`alpha_*=401296 M²/kappa+(9391/4)M`

is a finite sufficient completion threshold for `G+alpha H`, where `H=((a-d)²+(b+c)²)²`. Every alpha at least alpha_* gives quasiconvexity at every constant background matrix. An additional reserve nu gives the uniform test-gradient margin `4nu/1875` times `integral |Dphi|⁴`.

This is sufficient only. A failed supplied lambda, PSD check, or kernel-flatness check does not establish failure of quasiconvexity or of finite completion.

## Kernel and basis normalization

The matrix of Q must be taken in the orthonormal Frobenius entry basis `(a,b,c,d)`, so `Q(X)=x^T B x` with

`B=(1/2) Hessian G(I)-lambda D_det`,

where `x^T D_det x=ad-bc`. In particular D_det has entries `1/2` in positions (a,d),(d,a), entries `-1/2` in positions (b,c),(c,b), and zero elsewhere. Treating polynomial coefficients as unscaled off-diagonal matrix entries would be wrong; the audited specification uses the quadratic-form matrix.

Use the Frobenius-orthogonal decomposition `X=Z+Y`, with `Z in K`, `Y in K-perp`. The proof needs `|Z|,|Y|<=|X|`. An arbitrary rational kernel basis is sufficient for checking flatness but cannot replace orthogonal projection in those norm bounds. There is no obstruction to an exact rational projector: if N's columns form any rational kernel basis, then

`P_(Kperp)=I-N(N^T N)^(-1)N^T`.

The compiler need not construct this projector; it is only used in the proof. No orthonormalization or square-root oracle is needed.

For the coefficient bound, each expanded monomial coefficient is divided among its `4!/nu!` equal symmetric tensor entries. The absolute sum of all tensor entries is exactly the expanded coefficient absolute sum. Since each coordinate is bounded by its vector's Frobenius norm, `|T(X1,X2,X3,X4)|<=M product |Xi|`. This works in the standard entry basis regardless of the invariant-basis coefficients used for input. The verifier checks the combinatorial normalization for all 35 quartic monomials and all 256 ordered tensor entries.

## Universal remainder audit

Put `S(U,V,W)=T(I,U,V,W)` and `J0=sqrt(2)M`. The norm of I is sqrt(2). Kernel flatness and symmetry give

`S(E,X,X)=2S(E,Z,Y)+S(E,Y,Y)`,

`S(X,X,X)=3S(Z,Y,Y)+S(Y,Y,Y)`.

Thus the construction's bounds `3J0 |E||X||Y|` and `4J0 |X||Y|²` are valid. They are deliberately nonsharp.

Every background B has an orthogonal conformal plus anticonformal decomposition. After a left target rotation, `B=sI+E`, `s>=0`, E anticonformal, and `|C B|²=2|E|²`, with `CX=(a-d,b+c)`. The same target rotation sends `Dphi` to the gradient of the rotated vector function, preserves zero traces, and preserves G and H. The argument does not require K itself to be rotation invariant: K is applied to the increment after canonicalizing the background. Constant affine offsets in the underlying affine map have no effect on its gradient.

The exact remainder is `6T(B,B,X,X)+4T(B,X,X,X)+T(X,X,X,X)`. Its conformal quadratic part is `s²Q(X)+lambda s² det X`. The last term integrates to zero for compactly supported, zero-trace, or periodic gradient perturbations. Lambda is a constant supplied certificate; no varying determinant multiplier is introduced.

The quoted Young inequalities are valid pointwise. Their complete result is

`integral remainder_G >= (kappa/2)s² integral |Y|²-A0 |E|² integral |X|²-B0 integral |X|⁴`,

`A0=2592 M²/kappa+8M`, `B0=512 M²/kappa+3M`.

All quadratic, cubic, and quartic terms are included. In particular, the absence of uniform ellipticity on K is respected and no strict-Legendre-Hadamard theorem is used.

The H Bregman remainder has the exact lower bound

`R_H >= |CB|² |CX|²+|CX|⁴/5`.

The elementary identity proving it is checked symbolically. The L2 equality `integral |CX|²=integral |X|²` follows from the zero determinant integral.

For the L4 constant, the complex-valued Beurling estimate in Bañuelos-Janakiraman, Theorem 1.1, formula (1.4), is `||B f||p <= sqrt(2(p²-p)) ||f||p` for p>=2. At p=4 this is sqrt(24). Using `2 barpartial f=C Dphi` and the exact norm identity for the full gradient gives `K4=625/4`. The primary author PDF was independently opened and its theorem text checked: [Lp-bounds for the Beurling-Ahlfors transform](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~banuelos/Papers/Beur_Ahl.pdf). This older sufficient bound is the only sourced analytic estimate needed for the numerical constant; no claim about the present best norm is used.

Therefore the integrated H gap is at least `2|E|² integral |X|²+(1/(5K4)) integral |X|⁴`. Choosing

`alpha_*=A0/2+5K4 B0`

cancels both negative coefficients and simplifies exactly to the stated threshold. The affine linear Taylor term integrates to zero. The proof is uniform in background, domain, amplitude, and frequency.

For the stronger reserve margin, state a separate identity explicitly. If `s=|CB|²,r=|CX|²,h=CB.CX`, then

`R_H-r²/3=2(sr-h²)+6(h+r/3)²>=0`.

This supplies `nu/(3K4)=4nu/1875`. The simultaneous `/5` lower bound by itself would only give `4nu/3125`. The stronger `/3` identity is true, so this is an exposition clarification rather than a theorem gap.

Strong W^{1,4} approximation gives the zero-trace formulation on bounded Lipschitz domains, since quartic integrals are continuous under strong W^{1,4} convergence. A periodic test can be cut off over expanding unions of periods with a transition strip of vanishing relative area; after dividing by area, boundary errors vanish. Smooth periodic approximation then gives the periodic W^{1,4} case with the same constants.

## Zero-rank case

If Q=0, K is the entire matrix space. Flatness says `T(I,X,Y,E)=0` for all three arguments, hence `D³G(I)=0` and lambda=0. There is also a coordinate-free explanation: `DG(A)[I]=0` at every A, so G is translation invariant in the I direction. Left SO(2) invariance makes it translation invariant in the J direction as well, where J is a quarter-turn. Thus G depends only on its anticonformal part. The induced SO(2) action rotates that two-dimensional part; a homogeneous quartic invariant under those rotations is a multiple of its squared quadratic norm, namely `G=cH`.

Independently, the exact 20 third-derivative equations in the nine-dimensional invariant basis have rank eight and kernel exactly span H. Both descriptions agree. Consequently `alpha>=max(0,-c)` gives convex completion, and an added reserve nu again gives the stated margin. No kappa or reciprocal is formed in this branch.

## Certificate compiler and exact cost contract

For rational polynomial coefficients, rational supplied lambda, and rational requested reserve, every advertised check uses fixed-dimensional rational arithmetic. There are at most 35 expanded quartic monomials, a 4-by-4 support matrix, 15 nonempty principal minors, a kernel of dimension at most four, and at most 40 kernel-pair/coordinate flatness contractions when symmetry is used. Checking all principal minors is equivalent to PSD for a symmetric real matrix. Rank and any kernel basis can be obtained by exact elimination.

For positive rank r, the elementary symmetric coefficients of `det(I+tB)` give

`kappa=e_r(B)/e_(r-1)(B)=1/(sum_i 1/lambda_i)`.

Here the eigenvalues in the formula are precisely the r positive eigenvalues, not an additional input to the compiler. Thus kappa is positive and no larger than the least positive eigenvalue, so it is a valid lower bound on the orthogonal complement. For r=1 use e0=1. The verifier checks the harmonic identity for r=1,2,3,4 and checks the exact rational orthogonal-projector lower bound on admitted fixtures.

Because dimension and the number of polynomial coefficients are fixed, all these rational expressions have bounded algebraic degree and bounded arithmetic-expression size. For n-bit rational input, intermediate numerators and denominators and the returned threshold have O(n) bit length; total bit work is polynomial in n, with a fixed number of arithmetic operations and comparisons. The reserve bit length must be charged as part of n. This is a rational-input statement, not an algorithmic guarantee for arbitrarily presented real coefficients. Lambda is explicitly supplied. Searching for a suitable lambda, measuring coefficients, or acquiring an integrand is outside the compiler's charged input contract.

The admission predicate is unchanged by adding a multiple of H because H has zero second and third derivatives at I. Positive addition of admitted pairs `(G,lambda)` preserves PSD, intersects the kernels, and preserves flatness on that intersection. Hence its H quotient is a sufficient convex cone. No closedness, necessity, or optimal-threshold statement follows.

If K is a rank-one line and some finite H completion is rank-one convex, acoustic stationarity at I forces the flatness condition. The same implication works for a linear K every member of which is rank one, by polarization. Merely having a rank-one basis of a kernel containing nonrank-one linear combinations does not justify the condition.
