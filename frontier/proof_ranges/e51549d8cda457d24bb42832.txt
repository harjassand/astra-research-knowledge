## 3. One-dimensional primitives solve the prescribed-divergence problem

Use outgoing-minus-incoming divergence on the Cartesian orthant:

\[
\partial_\square F(g)=\sum_{i=1}^m[F_i(g)-F_i(g-e_i)],
\]

where an incoming value at coordinate -1 is zero.

### 3.1 Separated version

Suppose an absolutely summable source is explicitly represented as

\[
b(g)=\sum_{a=1}^{R}c_a\prod_{i=1}^m u_{ai}(g_i),\qquad \sum_gb(g)=0.
\]

Let m_ai=sum_s u_ai(s), and choose normalized reference sequences rho_i with sum rho_i=1. Define

\[
p_{ai}(s)=\sum_{\ell=0}^s[u_{ai}(\ell)-m_{ai}\rho_i(\ell)],
\]

\[
F_i(g)=\sum_a c_a
\Big(\prod_{j<i}u_{aj}(g_j)\Big)p_{ai}(g_i)
\Big(\prod_{j>i}m_{aj}\rho_j(g_j)\Big).
\tag{1}
\]

Then **partial_square F=b**. To prove it, take the backward difference in coordinate i, substitute the definition of p_ai, and telescope the products. For each a the result is product u_ai minus product m_ai rho_i. Summing cancels the latter because total mass is zero.

This is a prescribed-divergence construction, not the minimum-energy solution. Its energy must be computed or bounded separately. Sending a large-scale source directly to a point reference can be very expensive in energy despite its short representation.

### 3.2 Matrix-product/tensor-train version

Let

\[
b(g)=B_1(g_1)B_2(g_2)\cdots B_m(g_m),
\]

where B_i is r_(i-1)-by-r_i and r_0=r_m=1. Absolute summability of every matrix entry is assumed for the telescoping identity. For an energy-space correction, also require the primitives H_i to be square summable; this is automatic for the geometric cores below, but does not follow from absolute summability alone. Put

\[
M_i=\sum_{s\ge0}B_i(s),\quad D_i(s)=\rho_i(s)M_i,
\]

\[
H_i(s)=\sum_{\ell=0}^s[B_i(\ell)-\rho_i(\ell)M_i].
\]

Set

\[
F_i=B_1\cdots B_{i-1}H_iD_{i+1}\cdots D_m.
\tag{2}
\]

The products are ordered matrix products. No matrices are commuted. Their telescoping identity proves

\[
\partial_\square F=b-(M_1\cdots M_m)\prod_i\rho_i.
\tag{3}
\]

Thus a mean-zero source is repaired exactly. **Each F_i has the same TT bond dimensions as b.** It is not necessary to expand b into all separated rank-one components.

For rational geometric cores

\[
B_i(s)=\sum_{p=1}^{s_i}A_{ip}q_{ip}^s,\quad |q_{ip}|<1,
\quad \rho_i(s)=(1-r_i)r_i^s,
\]

all matrices are explicit:

\[
M_i=\sum_p\frac{A_{ip}}{1-q_{ip}},\qquad
H_i(s)=M_i r_i^{s+1}-\sum_p\frac{A_{ip}q_{ip}^{s+1}}{1-q_{ip}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Each repaired core has at most one additional geometric pole. The construction has no dependence on a spatial cutoff. Poles, matrix dimensions, rational bit lengths and intermediate numerator/denominator sizes are real inputs and are charged.
