3. Main theorem
Fix \(d\ge1\) and \(0<\eta\le1/8\). Set
\[
T=\frac{256d^2}{\eta^2}.
\]
There are two nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians \(H_g,H_b\) on \(2(d+1)\) qubits such that
\[
\boxed{E_T(H_g)<\eta,\qquad E_T(H_b)>3/4.}
\tag{1}
\]
Both systems have bounded interaction terms, nonzero hopping on every edge, positive density–density interactions, and a unique product vacuum ground state with gap greater than \(0.9\).
Moreover,
\[
\boxed{H_b=P H_gP^\dagger}
\]
for a reflection \(P\) of the chain. They are therefore exactly isospectral as full many-body Hamiltonians, not merely in a selected sector.
Their coefficients differ only by exchanging two distant endpoint onsite terms. The complete pair of alternatives may be supplied in advance. The only unknown information is which alternative is present.
Label the two arms from their adjacent central sites as
\[
L_0,\ldots,L_d,\qquad R_0,\ldots,R_d.
\]
Allow control of both prefixes through site \(r<d\), and write
\[
R=d-r
\]
for the distance from the controlled region to either differing endpoint.
Lower bound
For \(0<\tau\le R/2\), any procedure that accepts every \(E_T\le\eta\) system and rejects every \(E_T\ge3/4\) system, each with probability at least \(2/3\), requires
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
\ge
\frac{(2R+1)(R!)^2}{144\,\tau^{\,2R+1}}.
}
\tag{2}
\]
At fixed positive \(\eta,\tau\), with \(r\le d/2\),
\[
\boxed{
N_{\mathrm{exp}}
\ge
\exp\!\bigl(\Omega(d\log d)\bigr)
=
\exp\!\bigl(\Omega(\sqrt T\log T)\bigr).
}
\tag{3}
\]
This remains true even when roughly half the chain is controllable and complete terminal quantum readout is available.
Equation (2) is a lower bound, not a matching minimax law. No optimal tolerant upper bound is claimed.
For durations chosen at the start of each experiment, the average expected total native evolution time under the two hypotheses also satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\overline S
\ge
\frac{(2R+1)(R!)^2}{144\,\tau^{\,2R}}.
}
\tag{4}
\]
The duration statement allows adaptation between experiments, but does not assert an optional-stopping theorem for arbitrary outcome-dependent termination within a run.
