# Rough fixed directions: positivity, paid gradient budget and weak dual attainment

Research date: 15 October 2026. Separate extension of `POSITIVE_GROUND_STATE_AND_ATTAINED_DUAL.md`; the C1 core is retained independently. Internal reconstruction; external review and historical novelty UNKNOWN.

## Exact theorem

Let `Omega` be a bounded connected Lipschitz planar domain, or a connected flat two-torus. Let `F:R^(m x 2)->R` be a real homogeneous rank-one-convex quartic with the explicit positive bound

    mu=min_(rank R=1, |R|=1) F(R)>0.

Fix merely

    n in W^(1,4)(Omega;S^(m-1)),

with the periodic counterpart on a torus. No bounded derivative is required. For a nonnegative admissible amplitude trace and positive fourth mass, the fixed-direction amplitude minimum is attained. Every nonnegative minimizer is strictly positive in the interior. It is bounded away from zero on a torus, and up to the boundary when the continuous boundary amplitude is strictly positive.

The mass value is C1; in the torus or positive-boundary cases the nonnegative minimizer is unique and the weak dual from the C1 core is attained. In the planar left-invariant case its flux belongs to `L^(4/3)`, its divergence to `L^1`, and its Fenchel residual is exactly the scalar mass multiplier. There is no assertion of smooth dual attainment.

There is also a uniform **paid phase-gradient budget** lower bound. Put `K=||Dn||_4`, `M=||r||_4^4`, and `X=||grad r||_4`. For constants depending on `F,mu,Omega`,

    E_n(r)>= (mu/4)X^4-C(K^8+K^4)M.                       (R.1)

Thus a supplied bound on the actual phase-gradient norm gives a finite lower bound. It does not give nonnegativity, and no such norm bound is available uniformly over all compatible tests. No numerical complexity theorem is claimed.

## 1. Product algebra and rough coefficients

In two dimensions `W^(1,4)` embeds continuously into `C^0` and `L^infinity`, and compactly into `C^0` on the bounded domain or torus. Hence products of `W^(1,4)` fields again belong to `W^(1,4)`. The actual compatible map `u=rn` has

    Du=n tensor grad r+r Dn in L^4.

The energy `E_n(r)=integral F(D(rn))` is a differentiable polynomial functional of `r in W^(1,4)`: the map `r -> D(rn)` is bounded linear into `L^4`, and polynomial growth controls its differential.

At each point, the scalar gradient integrand

    L_n(x,s,z)=F(n(x) tensor z+s Dn(x))

is convex in `z` by rank-one convexity. Expanding about the rank-one matrix `n tensor z` and applying Young gives

    (mu/2)|z|^4-C|Dn(x)|^4|s|^4 <= L_n(x,s,z)
                <= C|z|^4+C|Dn(x)|^4|s|^4.                (R.2)

The coefficient `b(x)=C|Dn(x)|^4` belongs to `L^1`, but need not be bounded.

## 2. Uniform coercivity when the gradient budget is supplied

The planar Gagliardo-Nirenberg estimate on a bounded Lipschitz domain is

    ||r||_infinity^4
      <= C_Omega[||grad r||_4^2 ||r||_4^2+||r||_4^4].      (R.3)

A direct route to (R.3) is worth specifying. On the plane, local averaging and the `W^(1,4)` Morrey estimate give

    |v(x)|<=C[R^(-1/2)||v||_4+R^(1/2)||grad v||_4].

Choose `R=||v||_4/||grad v||_4` when both are nonzero. This proves the whole-plane product estimate. A bounded Lipschitz extension, whose derivative norm is bounded by `C(||grad r||_4+||r||_4)`, supplies the additional `||r||_4^4` term on a bounded domain. A cutoff of a finite periodic extension gives the torus version. All constants are attached to the fixed domain; no domain-independent constant is asserted.

Write `B=||Dn||_4^4=K^4`. Then (R.3) implies

    integral |Dn|^4 r^4
       <= C_Omega B[X^2 M^(1/2)+M].                       (R.4)

Insert this in the lower bound of (R.2). Young's inequality absorbs the term `C B X^2 M^(1/2)` into `(mu/4)X^4+C' B^2 M`, proving (R.1).

This estimate is uniform over directions with the stated `L^4` derivative budget, and is deliberately conservative. It does not hide phase acquisition in an oracle. The powers `K^8+K^4` are not claimed optimal.

For just one fixed direction, the weaker compactness form bound is also sufficient:

    integral b|v|^4<=eta integral |grad v|^4
                                      +C_(eta,b) integral |v|^4. (R.5)

To prove (R.5) without an explicit GN constant, suppose it fails. Choose `v_j` with left side greater than `eta||grad v_j||_4^4+j||v_j||_4^4`, normalized so its `W^(1,4)` fourth norm is one. Continuous embedding bounds the left side, so `||v_j||_4->0`. Compact embedding gives uniform convergence to zero along a subsequence. The left side tends to zero, while the gradient fourth powers tend to one, a contradiction.

## 3. Attainment and scalar multiplier without an obstacle

At fixed fourth mass, (R.1) bounds a minimizing sequence in `W^(1,4)`. Extract a weakly convergent subsequence whose amplitudes converge uniformly, say `r_j->r`. Then

    (r_j-r)Dn ->0 strongly in L^4.

The quartic difference estimate allows `r_j Dn` to be replaced by `r Dn` in the energy with an error tending to zero. With the latter field fixed, the integral is a continuous convex functional of the scalar gradient in `L^4`, hence weakly lower semicontinuous. Trace, nonnegativity and mass pass to the limit. This proves attainment with the rough direction.

The density interpolation from the C1 core is pointwise and requires only `n` unit-valued and its weak derivative. It still makes the fixed-boundary mass-value function finite and convex on positive masses. A supporting slope `lambda` consequently makes the minimizer a global minimum of

    J_lambda(r)=E_n(r)-lambda integral r^4.

For any real scalar competitor `v`, the Sobolev chain rule gives `E_n(|v|)=E_n(v)` because `F` is even under total negation. The mass and nonnegative boundary trace are preserved. Thus this is a signed minimum, not an obstacle variational inequality, and its scalar Euler equation is valid for both signs of compactly supported tests.

Uniqueness of the supporting slope follows by subtracting the Euler equations and using nonzero mass, exactly as in the C1 core. Therefore the mass value is C1 before any additional classical regularity is asserted.

## 4. A direct logarithmic proof of positivity

This proof discharges positivity without importing Harnack as a black box. The applicable primary Harnack theorem provides an independent literature route below.

Write `z=grad r` and

    A(x)=partial_z L_n(x,r,z),
    C(x)=partial_s L_n(x,r,z).

The weak Euler equation is

    integral [A.grad psi+C psi]=4lambda integral r^3 psi.  (R.6)

Convexity in `z` and (R.2) give the structural inequalities

    A.z>=c|z|^4-C0 r^4|Dn|^4,
    |A|<=C0(|z|^3+r^3|Dn|^3),
    |C|<=C0 |Dn|(|z|^3+r^3|Dn|^3).                       (R.7)

For the first inequality, use `A.z>=L_n(x,r,z)-L_n(x,r,0)` and then (R.2). The other two follow from the cubic growth of `DF`, `|n|=1`, and the chain rule.

Let `eta` be a smooth compactly supported cutoff and `epsilon>0`. The test

    psi=eta^4/(r+epsilon)^3

belongs to `W^(1,4)` for fixed `epsilon`, so is legal in (R.6). Differentiating it and moving the negative radial derivative term to the left gives

    3 integral eta^4 A.grad r/(r+epsilon)^4
      =4 integral eta^3 A.grad eta/(r+epsilon)^3
       + integral eta^4 C/(r+epsilon)^3
       -4lambda integral eta^4 r^3/(r+epsilon)^3.          (R.8)

Set `G_epsilon=|grad r|/(r+epsilon)`. Since `0<=r/(r+epsilon)<=1`, (R.7) bounds the left side below by a positive multiple of `integral eta^4 G_epsilon^4`, minus `C integral eta^4 |Dn|^4`. The two derivative terms on the right are bounded by sums of

    eta^3 |grad eta| G_epsilon^3,
    eta^3 |grad eta| |Dn|^3,
    eta^4 |Dn| G_epsilon^3,
    eta^4 |Dn|^4.

Young's inequality absorbs the first and third terms into an arbitrarily small multiple of `eta^4 G_epsilon^4`; the remaining cost is `C(|grad eta|^4+eta^4|Dn|^4)`. The multiplier term costs at most `4|lambda| integral eta^4`. Therefore, uniformly in `epsilon`,

    integral eta^4 |grad log(r+epsilon)|^4
      <= C integral [|grad eta|^4+eta^4 |Dn|^4
                                      +|lambda|eta^4].   (R.9)

Every right side is finite for `Dn in L^4`.

Choose a ball compactly inside the domain and a larger ball supporting a cutoff equal to one on it. The Morrey oscillation estimate on that ball bounds the oscillation of `log(r+epsilon)` uniformly in `epsilon`. If the ball contained both a zero of `r` and a positive value, that oscillation would diverge as `epsilon` tends to zero. Hence a ball is either entirely positive or entirely zero. Since `r` is continuous, nonzero and the domain is connected, a chain of overlapping interior balls proves positivity everywhere in the interior and a positive minimum on each compact subset.

The torus and strictly positive continuous boundary give a global positive lower bound by compactness, as in the C1 core. No derivative of `Dn`, no smoothness of `r`, and no boundedness of `Dn` was used in (R.9).

## 5. Weak dual and boundary pairing survive

For `m=2` and left-SO(2) invariance, define the global closed phase one-form `k_i=(Jn).partial_i n`. It belongs to `L^4`; local real phase lifts exist by continuity. If `r` is bounded below globally, then

    a=grad r/r in L^4,
    j=partial_a f_k(a) in L^(4/3).

The conjugate estimate from quartic coercivity gives integrability of `f_k^*(j)` through `C(|j|^(4/3)+|k|^4)`. Using the legal test `eta/r^3` in the scalar Euler equation gives

    div j=4[f_k(a)-a.j-lambda] in L^1,
    -div j/4-f_k^*(j)=lambda almost everywhere.            (R.10)

Thus the attained weak torus dual and the exact weak normal trace formula for strictly positive boundary amplitudes carry over unchanged. The Picone test `s^4/r^3-r` is still in `W^(1,4)`, so fixed-mass nonnegative minimizers are unique. For zero boundary, only interior positivity and local flux identities are asserted; the unweighted global logarithmic flux can still be singular at the boundary.

## 6. Primary-source comparison and scope

DiBenedetto-Trudinger, [Harnack inequalities for quasi-minima of variational integrals](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/17564?nt=1), Section 4, printed pp305-307, independently supplies the positivity step. Its displayed structure admits `b in L^1` when the growth exponent is larger than the dimension. Here `4>2`, `b=C|Dn|^4+|lambda|`, and additive forcing is zero. The actual hypotheses and Corollary 3 were visually checked. This source route and (R.9) agree, but the direct logarithmic proof does not depend on an unverified smooth reference.

The extension applies directly to `n=(u+c)/|u+c|` obtained from an actual planar `W^(1,4)` map translated away from zero. It supplies a weak optimal flux after amplitude minimization. The flux is an attained existence object, not a finitely acquired smooth certificate. The paid budget lower bound can be negative and the unrestricted global phase infimum retains the C1 core's `0`/`-infinity` dichotomy. No all-direction Jensen sign, full quasiconvexity, or runtime guarantee follows.
