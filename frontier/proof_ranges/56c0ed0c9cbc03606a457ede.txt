Hoeffding’s inequality gives additive variance accuracy \(\zeta\), with failure probability at most \(p\), whenever
\[ \boxed{ M\ge \frac{2L_u^4}{\zeta^2}\log\frac2p. } \tag{18} \]
The manuscript supplies the resulting rigorous confidence interval for (16). This shot bound is conservative, not claimed optimal.
Each copy requires \(n\) single-qubit measurements and \(O(n+km)\) arithmetic work. At fixed \(k,\Delta\), that is linear in system size. The scalar certificate can be processed in streaming form.
The uncharged cost is equilibrium preparation, not classical post-processing. The theorem neither supplies independent Gibbs copies nor absorbs preparation bias, detector miscalibration, or correlated sampling into its statistical guarantee.
Positive marginal visibility at each site is insufficient.
Consider the explicit commuting family
\[ H_n(t) = -\frac Jn\sum_{i<j}Z_iZ_j+tM_n, \qquad M_n=\sum_iZ_i. \]
The parameter dictionary can be the single-site \(Z_i\), so \(k=\Delta=1\). The background is two-local with incident weighted strength at most \(J\).
At \(t=0\), spin-flip symmetry gives \(\mathbb EM_n=0\). Counting configurations whose minority population is at least \(n/4\) yields
\[ \Pr(|M_n|\le n/2) \le2^{n-1}e^{-3\beta Jn/8}. \]
Thus, for fixed sufficiently large \(\beta J\),
\[ \operatorname{Var}(M_n)=\Theta(n^2), \qquad F_Q(t)=\beta^2\operatorname{Var}(M_n)=\Theta(n^2). \]
Now suppose one common detector bit flips all recorded signs with probability \(p\in(0,1/2)\). Each detector individually has positive visibility
\[ \eta=1-2p, \]
but the errors are correlated.
The noisy record distribution is
\[ \widetilde p_t=(1-p)p_t+p\,p_{-t}. \]
At \(t=0\), its derivative is multiplied by \(\eta\), while its distribution is unchanged. Therefore
\[ F_{\mathrm{common}}(0)=\eta^2F_{\mathrm{clean}}(0) \le\eta^2F_Q(0). \]
The information loss is consequently \(\Omega(n^2)\), not \(O(n)\).
This is a decisive impossibility result for replacing the theorem’s independent-error assumption by a per-site visibility condition alone.
For one qubit, take
\[ H(x,z)=xX+zZ, \qquad f(r)=\frac{\tanh(\beta r)}r, \qquad r=\sqrt{x^2+z^2}. \]
The probability of a \(Z=+1\) outcome is
\[ p_+(x,z)=\frac{1-zf(r)}2. \]
At \(x>0,z=0\), the negative log likelihood has Hessian
\[ \nabla^2[-\log p_+] = \begin{pmatrix} 0&f'(x)\\ f'(x)&f(x)^2 \end{pmatrix}, \]
and
\[ \boxed{ \det\nabla^2[-\log p_+]=-f'(x)^2<0. } \tag{19} \]
This occurs in a bounded one-site family with positive thermal covariance.
It directly refutes the inference that a strongly convex log partition function makes local-measurement likelihood optimization convex. It does not prove that every reconstruction algorithm is computationally hard.
