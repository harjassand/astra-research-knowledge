VECTOR STARK CONTROL: RECONSTRUCTION AND FINITE BANDWIDTH
Status: internally reconstructed model theorem; no originality or molecular
implementation claim. Root proposed transit compensation. This file checks
its cubature, positivity, resource accounting and a smoothing correction.

1. MODEL AND STATIC COMPARATOR

A frozen orientation u in S^2 has one differential frequency slope d=d0 u.
The control is a real vector electric field E(t), with mean |E|^2<=E_rms^2.
Let Theta'=E, q_u=d0 u.E and f_u=exp(i d0 u.Theta). Assume periodic Theta
of period T, angular fundamental Omega=2pi/T, and Fourier coefficients c_n(u).
For a weak coupling J to one resonant acceptor with amplitude memory
exp(-kappa s), the phase-averaged Golden-rule rate is

 Gamma(u)=2J^2 sum_n |c_n(u)|^2 kappa/[kappa^2+n^2 Omega^2].                 (1)

This is a specified weak-transfer model, not a general nonlinear IVR law.
Define Gamma0=2J^2/kappa and A=d0 E_rms. A constant field yields the
orientation-averaged relative rate atan(A/kappa)/(A/kappa). Its 10x and100x
rate-reduction thresholds are A/kappa=15.044233... and156.4401... .

These are orientation-averaged INITIAL rates, not a single ensemble lifetime.
With frozen orientations S(t)=average_u exp[-Gamma(u)t]. Static detuning has
mean lifetime average[1/Gamma(u)]=(1+x^2/3)/Gamma0, already quadratic in x,
while its worst-orientation lifetime remains1/Gamma0. The uniform theorem
below addresses that vulnerable-orientation gap. Finite-grid average rates
must not be promoted into uniform guarantees or a lifetime-scaling headline.

Parseval gives average_u sum_n n^2 Omega^2 |c_n(u)|^2<=A^2/3. Jensen therefore
gives the valid vector-ensemble lower bound Gamma_avg/Gamma0>=1/(1+A^2/3kappa^2).
Unlike the scalar bound, this allows order kappa^2/A^2 suppression. It does not
say every orientation can simultaneously saturate that lower bound.

2. EXPLICIT TIME MEASURE ON THE PHASE SPHERE

Take R=pi/d0, and normalized uniform measure sigma on the unit sphere.
For integer t>=1, use t+1 Gauss-Legendre z nodes and2t+1 uniform azimuths.
The N=(t+1)(2t+1) positive weights w_k sum to1 and integrate every spherical
polynomial of degree<=2t. Indeed azimuthal integration is exact up to Fourier
degree2t. Surviving monomials are polynomials in z of degree<=2t, so the
t+1-point Gauss-Legendre rule integrates them exactly.

At each z node traverse a full latitude circle. Connect consecutive rings at
azimuth0 by descending meridians, then return from the last ring to the first
along the same meridian. This is a closed piecewise smooth path through every
quadrature node. On the phase sphere its length satisfies

 L=R[sum_j2pi sin(theta_j)+2(theta_last-theta_first)]<=2pi R(t+2).           (2)

Let nu be normalized arclength along this path, including repeated segments.
The reproducing kernel for spherical polynomials through degree t is

 K_t(n,x)=sum_{ell=0}^t(2ell+1)P_ell(n.x),
 h_t(n)=integral K_t(n,x) dnu(x).

Because |P_ell|<=1, |h_t|<=(t+1)^2. Set

 delta=1/[2(t+1)^2],        a_k=w_k[1-delta h_t(n_k)].                     (3)

Then a_k>=w_k/2. Since sum_k w_k h_t(n_k)=1, sum a_k=1-delta. The time
measure rho=delta nu+sum a_k delta_{n_k} is a probability measure. For any
polynomial p through degree t, the degree2t cubature and reproducing property
give sum w_k h_t(n_k)p(n_k)=integral h_t p dsigma=integral p dnu, hence

 integral p drho=integral p dsigma.                                     (4)

Implement rho by traversing at constant speed during fraction delta of each
cycle and dwelling at node k for time fraction a_k. There is no uncharged
transit. The unsmoothed field has sharp switching and is NOT bandlimited.

3. DC CARRIER AND RMS ACCOUNTING

For each u, the Taylor polynomial of exp(i pi u.n) through degree t has real
argument remainder bounded by epsilon_t=pi^(t+1)/(t+1)!. Since
integral exp(i pi u.n) dsigma=sin(pi)/pi=0, (4) implies |c_0(u)|<=2epsilon_t.
Parseval, positivity and |n|>=1 for nonzero harmonics give

 Gamma(u)/Gamma0<=4epsilon_t^2+(kappa/Omega)^2.                           (5)

During transit speed v=E_rms/sqrt(delta), so T=L/(E_rms sqrt(delta)), and

 E_peak/E_rms=1/sqrt(delta)=sqrt(2)(t+1),
 Omega/A=2pi sqrt(delta)/(d0 L)>=1/[pi sqrt(2)(t+1)(t+2)].                 (6)

Thus t of order log(A/kappa)/loglog(A/kappa) gives a uniform-orientation
upper bound of order [kappa/A]^2 times a fourth power of that logarithmic
factor. This escapes the scalar static orientation-average1/A bottleneck.
It is an asymptotic model capability, not an efficient practical waveform.

4. FINITE BANDWIDTH: DC DEFECT MUST BE CHARGED

Let Theta_K be the Fejer convolution of the periodic vector Theta, retaining
harmonics |n|<=K with multipliers 1-|n|/(K+1). Its electric field is the same
positive-kernel convolution of E. Jensen gives RMS(E_K)<=E_rms; positivity
gives peak(E_K)<=E_peak. Its angular electric-field bandwidth is B=K Omega.
Its mean field is zero. A constant shift of Theta_K changes no |c_n|.

For every harmonic, the discarded multiplier is at most |n|/(K+1). Parseval
therefore gives

 ||Theta-Theta_K||_L2<=E_rms/[Omega(K+1)].

The pointwise Lipschitz inequality |exp(ix)-exp(iy)|<=|x-y| and Cauchy-Schwarz
then imply, UNIFORMLY in u,

 |c_0,K(u)|<=2epsilon_t+A/[Omega(K+1)]<=2epsilon_t+A/B,
 Gamma_K(u)/Gamma0<=(2epsilon_t+A/B)^2+(kappa/Omega)^2.                   (7)

For example B>=A Omega/kappa and2epsilon_t<=kappa/Omega imply the upper
bound5(kappa/Omega)^2. Bandwidth is finite but not free: this sufficient
choice requires K>=A/kappa. Its cost can be very large. Smoothing does not
preserve exact spherical moments, and ignoring its new DC term is invalid.

Numerical probes in vector_design_probe.py additionally rescale the cycle
after smoothing to USE its actual reduced RMS budget. Their field peaks and
bandwidths include that rescaling. Equation(7) conservatively retains the
original period and needs no rescaling.

5. PRIMARY COMPARATORS AND SCOPE

Ehler and Groechenig, t-Design Curves and Mobile Sampling on the Sphere,
Forum of Mathematics Sigma11 (2023), e105, Theorem5.1:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2306.13152
They prove piecewise smooth closed spherical t-design curves of lengthTheta(t),
whose trajectories are unions of circles. That known result already gives the
same sphere-moment averaging operation. Uniform-speed use on radiusR improves
the existence period to order t/A and the unsmoothed rate bound to order
kappa^2 t^2/A^2. Optimal point-design existence is imported; this is not an
explicit practical low-bandwidth molecular recipe. The elementary construction
above is a reproducible comparator, not a claimed new spherical-design result.

Yang, Leng and Li, Continuous Unitary Designs for Universally Robust Quantum
Control (2026 preprint), SecV:
 https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01988
They assume full controllability and resonant controls on all three Pauli axes.
Their analytic paths cancel arbitrary unknown static perturbations at leading
order. This is a strong prior design-based control comparator. It does not
directly instantiate our commuting Stark interface: for a fixed molecule all
three lab E components multiply the same diagonal generator with unknown
orientation-dependent coefficients. No full unitary1-design is thereby
available. This distinction leaves a specialized model interface, not an
established invention or practical advantage over the literature.

Assumptions not closed here: one nonzero fixed channel norm d0; frozen molecular
orientation over many cycles; weak transfer; exponential acceptor memory;
reliable frequency-linear Stark response across the waveform bandwidth and
peak fields; no additional resonances, heating, ionization or damage; IVR
dominates population loss. Pump-off storage of an already prepared population
does not require orientation-specific pump phase tracking. This result alone
does not demonstrate ladder climbing, chemical yield, bond selectivity or
deconstruction capability. Multiple unequal channel norms are not covered.

7. NUMERICAL ERROR SCOPE FOR THE FILTERED PROBES

The computed filtered waveform is DEFINED by the discrete Fourier coefficients
of the32768 sampled phase-sphere positions, multiplied by the Fejer factors.
It is therefore an explicit continuous trigonometric polynomial of degree K,
not a claim to have exactly evaluated the continuous convolution in Sec4.
The discrete Fejer kernel is positive and its time-dependent weights sum to1
when K<N. Thus its real-time phase lies in the radius-pi ball. The uniform
moment theorem applies to the analytical construction; the grid waveform's
actual carrier and rates are computed separately.

Orientation integration error: the200-node rule (10 Gauss z x20 azimuths)
integrates degree19 exactly in real arithmetic. Every phase autocorrelation
has orientation integrand exp(i u.D), with |D|<=2pi. Its Legendre expansion is
sum_l(2l+1)i^l j_l(|D|)P_l(u.D/|D|). For r<=2pi the power series implies

 |j_l(r)|<=r^l/(2l+1)!! * exp[r^2/(2(2l+3))].

For b_l=(2l+1) times this bound, b_(l+1)/b_l<=r/(2l+1). Therefore both
orientation measures being positive probabilities gives the absolute error

 2 sum_(l>=20)b_l <=2 b_20/[1-(2pi)/41] <1.075e-7.                       (8)

The relative-rate memory kernel integrates to1 and is positive, so the same
bound applies to its average. It is a discretization bound, not roundoff.

Time aliasing error: for any orientation, p(theta)=u.phase_K(theta) is a real
trigonometric polynomial of degree K with |p|<=pi. Applying the maximum
principle to z^K p(z) gives |p(theta+iy)|<=pi exp(K|y|). The Fourier coefficients
a_n of exp(i p) consequently satisfy |a_n|<=exp[pi exp(Ky)-|n|y]. Choose
y=log(2)/K. The coefficient L1 tail beyond the Nyquist index N/2 is bounded by

 H=2 exp(2pi-Ny/2)/(1-exp(-y)).

Discrete Fourier coefficients alias only this tail. Since the rate weights
are between0 and1 and sum_n|a_n|^2=1, the absolute rate error is<=2H+2H^2.
For K=64 this is<1.718e-72; for K=256 it is<4.295e-14. These bounds concern
aliasing of the DEFINED filtered waveform, not the sampled input's difference
from the original piecewise path. They do not cover the unfiltered probes.

Peak sampling error: project the vector field onto its direction at a true
peak. That scalar trigonometric polynomial has zero derivative at its maximum
and second derivative bounded by (2pi K)^2 times the true peak (Bernstein).
Its nearest grid point is<=1/(2N) away. Thus the true field peak is at most
the sampled peak divided by1-pi^2 K^2/(2N^2). This factor is1.00001883 for64
and1.00030129 for256. Peak table values remain labelled numerical estimates.

Run vector_probe_error_bounds.py to reproduce these analytical bound values.
Floating point implementation error has NOT been certified by interval
arithmetic; the displayed extra threshold digits are reproducibility data,
not metrological accuracy. No uniform orientation-performance check is
