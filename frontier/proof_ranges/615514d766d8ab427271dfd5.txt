Covariance matching is insufficient
Let \(X_1,X_2,X_3\in\{-1,1\}\). Compare independent signs with
\[
X_1=a,\quad X_2=b,\quad X_3=ab,
\]
where \(a,b\) are independent.
The two distributions agree on every one- and two-variable marginal. Yet three unit-duration qubit pulses
\[
H_j=(\pi/4)X_jZ
\]
multiply coherence by
\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left[-\frac{i\pi}{2}(X_1+X_2+X_3)\right].
\]
This equals \(0\) for independent signs and \(i\) for the pairwise-independent construction.
Even this commuting example has an order-one channel difference. The simulator therefore uses exact bounded-query independence, not a covariance test.
Re-seeding destroys memory
For a static Gaussian \(X\), the Hamiltonian \(H=\sigma XZ\) gives coherence
\[
e^{-2\sigma^2T^2}.
\]
Redrawing \(X\) independently in each of \(M\) equal cells instead gives
\[
e^{-2\sigma^2T^2/M}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Keeping the same seed throughout is essential.
The seed is not a full path sampler
A degree-\((k-1)\) field-polynomial family contains at most \(2^{bk}\) tables, versus \(2^{bD}\) fully independent tables on \(D\) addresses. For \(D\gg k\), their complete-table distributions can be almost maximally different.
The theorem concerns the specified bounded-query simulation and its output, not unlimited subsequent queries or recovery of an entire fine trajectory.
Fixed-amplitude and white-noise limits differ
At fixed \(\|A_a\|\), the rate dependence is logarithmic. But maintaining fixed integrated Gaussian noise strength while increasing \(\lambda_a\) requires noise amplitude proportional to \(\sqrt{\lambda_a}\). Then \(S\), and hence the leading computational cost, grows.
The theorem does not hide that physical scaling.
Finally, the model describes prescribed classical noise. It does not automatically represent a finite-temperature quantum bath with backreaction or detailed balance. Nor does it learn the noise parameters from experimental data, certify hardware oracles, or make rare conditional outputs inexpensive.
11. Established outcome and remaining barrier
Small exact and numerical checks reproduced the Gaussian bridge covariances, telegraph bridge joint laws, and sorted-Dyson identities for noncommuting matrices, with residuals at floating-point roundoff. The covariance-only counterexample above was also evaluated directly. No full quantum circuit or formal proof-assistant development was executed.
The strongest surviving construction is
\[
\boxed{
\text{explicit correlated-noise bridges}
\;+\;
\text{exact finite-query randomness replacement}
\;+\;
\text{coherent time-ordered simulation}.
}
\]
It closes a specific operational gap: the required noise values are acquired by actual circuits, globally consistently, without generating or storing the resolved time history. Finite precision, randomness, private memory, operator selection, and observable estimation are all charged.
What remains unestablished is the claim that this constitutes an independently major original advance. That requires priority assessment of the complete theorem and comparison with optimized specialized methods in a scientifically demanding parameter regime. The present result is a constructive uniform capability with explicit costs—not yet a demonstrated universal speedup or a certified historic breakthrough
