## 3. CTMC formula: correct for its exact assumptions, strongly optimistic as a frame model

Root's states `0 -> 1` at `2 lambda`, `1 -> 0` at `mu`, and `1 -> F` at `lambda` give exactly

```text
E_0[T_F] = (mu + 3 lambda)/(2 lambda^2).
```

The generator on transient states is `[[-2lambda,2lambda],[mu,-(mu+lambda)]]`. Its eigenvalues and root's survival formula are correct; `S'(0)=0` and `S(t)|_(mu=0)=2 exp(-lambda t)-exp(-2lambda t)` are independent checks. `S_patch(T)^N` is exact only if patches fail independently with constant rates and each second copy hit at the *same essential locus* ends that patch. Two nonoverlapping strand gaps need not be fatal; a single common-cause lesion or deleted routing junction can be fatal immediately. Neither complication is measured. A shared finite enzyme bath also makes `mu` load-dependent unless throughput and substrate concentration are maintained as the number of patches/devices rises.

Let a new one-copy lesion be eligible for the specified polymerase/ligase path with probability `p`. An ineligible one-copy lesion remains a state `I` that is functional while its other copy persists, but has no repair path: `0 -> R` at `2 lambda p`, `0 -> I` at `2 lambda(1-p)`, `R -> 0` at `mu`, and both `R,I -> F` at `lambda`. This retains the two-copy logic and does **not** call every ineligible hit an immediate failure. First-step equations give the exact extension

```text
E_0[T_F] = [(3-2p) mu + 3 lambda] /
           [2 lambda ((1-p) mu + lambda)].                 (1)

lim_(mu->infinity) E_0[T_F]
  = [1 + 1/(2(1-p))]/lambda,  if p<1.                  (2)
```

At `mu/lambda=1000`, the all-eligible ideal has mean `501.5/lambda`; with `p=0.99`, (1) gives only `46.5/lambda`, and even infinite repair speed tops out at `51/lambda`. The standard-library exact-rational replay is `repair_review_math.py` with `repair_review_results.json`. This is a **hypothetical countermodel**, not measured lesion chemistry. If a common end-processing bath converts `I` into `R`, that is a genuine route around this bound; its conversion rate, fidelity, side products and resource cost must be acquired. Calling end processing "available if needed" does not supply these values.

Now give each of `K` irreplaceable routing records an independent loss hazard `rho`. In this simple extension,

```text
S_frame(T) = exp(-K rho T) [S_patch(T)]^N
             <= exp(-K rho T).                            (3)
```

For 90% frame survival at horizon `T`, (3) requires `K rho T <= -ln(0.9)=0.1053605...` even with infinitely fast patch repair. If `K=100` and `lambda T=1`, then `rho/lambda <= 0.001053605...`. Root's all-eligible numerical example with `N=100`, `lambda T=1`, `mu/lambda=1000` already gives only `0.81938` patch-frame survival *before* junction loss. With no routing loss, reaching `0.9` in that exact ideal model needs approximately `mu/lambda=1894.245`. These numbers are model thresholds, not rates achieved by an enzyme or frame.

`O(1)` reagent species does not mean `O(1)` material flux. At `N` independent two-copy patches, `2N lambda T` damage events would occur over time `T` while all patches remain fully intact; state occupancy changes the actual count. If a nonvanishing fraction `q` is repaired with mean gap length `ell`, nucleotide demand scales at least as the number of those repaired events times `ell` (approximately `2N lambda T q ell` in the low-occupancy regime), plus enzyme turnover and rejects. For many devices, bath volume/replenishment scales with total attacked material. The fixed per-patch `mu` used in the CTMC silently assumes enough enzyme capacity and dNTP supply to maintain that rate.

## 4. Strongest incumbents and a decisive paired experiment

The primary [Scheckenbach et al. self-healing study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7986372/) did more than a weak unstabilized control: excess **matching staples** repaired damaged staple features under some conditions, while **nonmatching DNA** provided sacrificial protection; at high 10% FBS stress matching and nonmatching DNA gave comparable stabilization. Its authors explicitly state their exchange mechanism did not repair the long scaffold. Their readouts include AFM and DNA-PAINT geometry, while their strongest molecular inference is still narrower than arbitrary topology restoration. The candidate must beat that same-dose library/protection comparison on *scaffold damage*, its proposed point of difference.

The [designed polymerase gap-filling result](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29451771/) is a direct comparator for prearranged gaps. The use of a small set of common enzymes to process eligible gaps is established biological chemistry: a [purified-protein short-patch repair study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC419455/) reconstituted uracil-lesion repair with glycosylase, APE1, polymerase beta and ligase complex, while a [blocked-end study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3493963/) required PNKP to generate a 3′-OH before gap filling and ligation. These are **duplex DNA substrates, not origami performance demonstrations**. They make a generic common enzyme bath an incumbent chemical mechanism; the candidate's possible advance would be embedding an accessible, route-preserving reciprocal template inside arbitrary frames and sustaining it under unknown damage.

[Keller and colleagues' ligation comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31455950/) reports that ligating accessible staple ends improved origami stability under the tested urea/temperature conditions; it does not establish repair after random damage. [Covalent bonding of designed DNA assemblies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6097813/) reports greater nuclease resistance with designed crosslinks, while partial terminal/crossover ligation was insufficient for the tested full structure. A more recent [site-specific external crosslinking study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11100008/) shows why function must be matched: external crosslinks can stabilize against some insults yet leave core DNA DNase-accessible. No one of these papers is a complete incumbent for every damage class, but together they prevent comparing only against bare origami.

**Experiment.** Fabricate at least two predeclared frames, one with multiple crossovers, with a fixed target geometry and a function sensitive to route error. Randomize and blind individual, traceable molecules into: (A) reciprocal-template/protected-junction design plus active polymerase, ligase, dNTP and specified end processing; (B) the *same* design and bath composition with the active repair chemistry disabled, to isolate protection; (C) matched conventional origami with optimized excess matching staples plus an equal-mass nonmatching-DNA sacrificial control; (D) the best function-compatible ligated/crosslinked design; and (E) ordinary gap-fill/ligation on a matched design where it can act. Match exposure, temperature, total DNA mass, handling and cost. Predeclare a mixed lesion challenge including eligible scaffold and staple gaps, blocked ends, overlapping opposite-strand lesions, junction hits and a realistic random-damage regime. Do not select only successful/eligible molecules after the challenge. Measure eligibility `p`, junction survival `rho`, repair kinetics `mu`, and lesion rates from sacrificial characterization cohorts, with confidence intervals and end chemistry, rather than assuming them from the ideal CTMC.

For the **same tracked molecules**, require evidence of newly synthesized sequence spanning a known scaffold gap and a restored covalent join, a correct junction/strand-continuity map, restored 3D geometry, and recovered function over repeated blinded damage/repair cycles. Pulse-labeled nucleotide incorporation alone can mark free or irrelevant DNA; fluorescence alone can persist on a misrouted frame. Use appropriate sequence/ligation assays and single-particle imaging or equivalent complementary readouts, with before/after lineage so survival of undamaged molecules or reassembly cannot masquerade as repair. Record denominator and uncertainty for each lesion class and for the full mixed challenge.

**Kill criteria.** The proposed capability fails its stated unknown-damage scope if scaffold lesions are not covalently repaired in the same tracked frame, if route/geometry/function recovery is not established, or if success is confined to preselected eligible gaps and does not survive the full challenge denominator. It fails a new-operation advantage claim if active chemistry adds no verified recovery above (B), if the gain is matched by same-dose excess staples/sacrificial DNA or function-compatible ligation/crosslinking after full costs, or if per-defect primers/route-specific replacement species are needed in the bath. A protected route with measured `K rho T` already exceeding the target survival budget in (3) kills the claimed frame-lifetime target regardless of `mu`. These conditions do not erase a narrower proof-of-principle for designed gaps; they prevent promoting it as arbitrary, repeated, common-bath repair.
