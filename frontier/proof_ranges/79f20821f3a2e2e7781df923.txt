# Criticism of the bounded-interaction-order boundary

13 October 2026. Separate additive review of
`work/FB-agents/root/BOUNDED_ORDER_RECONSTRUCTION.md` at the source hash recorded
in `ADDENDUM_INPUT.json`. The previously frozen REVIEW.md and

not external review, historical novelty clearance or an acquired physical probe.

The theorem and error budgets are correct under the stated **complete labelled
sector-operator** input and supplied normal-ordered body-order promise. The
zero-body scalar is included, and the ordered wedge convention has no implicit
factorial. On particle number q, the q-annihilator sends |J> to vacuum and the
q-creator sends vacuum to |I>. Hence the pure q-body monomial is precisely
|I><J|, while every higher-order monomial vanishes. Recursively subtracting
already known lower-body lifts reconstructs Uq exactly. This is standard CAR
algebra and does not establish a new physical primitive.

The positive lift argument also checks including its conjugation convention.
For X=vv†, choose `B=sum_J conjugate(v_J) C_J`; the lifted operator is B†B.
Lifting the identity counts occupied q-subsets and equals `binom(N,q)I`.
Positivity applied to the two inequalities bounding a Hermitian X proves the
sharp norm factor. Equivalently, the column of annihilators divided by the
square root of that binomial gives an isometry into the q-particle coefficient
space tensored with the remaining-particle space, proving complete positivity.
The identity example attains the factor exactly. The convention is zero lift
for q>N, and the zero-body lift is the scalar identity.

The coefficient-error recursion follows by taking the norm of the supplied
q-sector error and every previously propagated lower-body lift. It is a
conservative sufficient budget, not a claim that all lower-body errors attain
their bounds simultaneously. The full N-sector error bound is the sum of their
binomially amplified norms. Binomial coefficients are nondecreasing in N, so
N=n is a safe full-Fock maximum, and an independently selected nonnegative
total charge Q permits N<=min(n,Q). No selected sector is inferred from vacuum
spectra themselves.

The relation to the earlier negative theorem is exact. Its hidden term has
order k+1. A justified promise p<=k plus complete H[q] for q<=p excludes that
alternative and determines the whole bath, rather than merely fitting one
quadratic spectral function. This does not turn a p>=2 interacting Hamiltonian
into a Gaussian environment or solve its correlated ground state. Low-order
observations cannot justify the order promise or a higher-order remainder norm
on their own.

The propagator qualification is essential and correctly stated. Full complex
operator-resolved q-particle addition/removal propagation from a prepared
stationary vacuum determines `H[q]-E0 I` by its exact derivative at zero.
Peak positions, trace spectra, unresolved intensities and noisy finite-time
measurements are weaker inputs. The scalar E0 must be known separately when
an absolute Hamiltonian is requested; it cancels in the common-bath design
contrast. No cheap derivative, calibrated coherent insertion, vacuum
preparation or interacting-material encoding is acquired by the theorem.

The dense representation and enumeration count are polynomial only for fixed
p. They include all matrix entries of the low-particle sectors, not simply p
scalar moments. For p>=2 both acquisition and the requested output precision
can be expensive; correlated solving and physical model qualification remain
open. Common omitted bath terms cannot be removed from a design contrast by
assertion. Hamiltonian-error Lipschitz bounds apply to the corresponding
whole-Hamiltonian energies/free energies; any uncoupled reference subtraction
must either cancel between designs or be accounted for separately.
