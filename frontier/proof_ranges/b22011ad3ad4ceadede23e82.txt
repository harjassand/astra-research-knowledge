Let A,B be bounded selfadjoint on arbitrary H and

 J(u,v)=1/2[exp(iuA)exp(ivB)+exp(ivB)exp(iuA)].

There exists a scalar probability nu with m_nu(g)<infinity for every
g in R^2 and a normalized POVM M with Mhat=nuhat J iff [A,B]=0.

The inverse Fourier transform T_J is a compact B(H)-valued distribution:
J is entire of exponential type supported in the rectangle
[-||A||,||A||] x [-||B||,||B||] by the operator-valued Paley-Wiener
argument (or apply the scalar theorem to matrix coefficients, retaining
the common norm bounds). It is bounded on real frequency space and is
therefore a finite-order tempered distribution. Compact support is not
an assumption about finite spectra or existence of eigenvectors.

Section 1 now gives

 L_M(s,t)=m_nu(s,t) 1/2[exp(sA)exp(tB)+exp(tB)exp(sA)] >=0.

Fix t_0!=0, set C=exp(t_0 B), and divide by the positive scalar m_nu.
Section 2, or directly (5) with D=exp(A), gives [A,C]=0.
Because B=t_0^{-1}log C, this implies [A,B]=0.
Conversely a commuting pair has a joint spectral measure; convolving it
with delta_0 or any all-exponential probability gives the required M.

Only the selected tilts are needed for necessity: it suffices that
D_nu contain {(hn,t_0):n=1,2,...} for some h>0 and t_0!=0. This is
stronger than a claim about arbitrary locally exponential noise and
weaker than requiring every exponential moment. A bounded neighborhood
of tilts does NOT by itself imply commutation.

 exp(-iuA)exp(-ivB)=exp(-ivB)exp(-iuA).
