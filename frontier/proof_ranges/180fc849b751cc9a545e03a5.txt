6. Replacing all independent innovations by a finite seed
Let the conceptual innovation oracle be
\[
O_f|x,z\rangle=|x,z\oplus f(x)\rangle,
\]
where each \(f(x)\) is an independent uniform \(b\)-bit value.
The following established bounded-query fact is the key algebraic tool. Mark Zhandry
Exact replacement lemma
A quantum circuit making at most \(Q\) calls to \(O_f\) has exactly the same averaged output channel when \(f\) is drawn from a \(2Q\)-wise independent uniform family instead of a fully independent table.
Proof. A matrix entry of \(O_f\) is linear in indicators
\[
\mathbf 1\{f(x)=y\}.
\]
After \(Q\) queries, each amplitude is a polynomial of degree at most \(Q\) in these indicators. Each output-density-matrix entry therefore has degree at most \(2Q\).
Every monomial depends on values at at most \(2Q\) distinct addresses. Its expectation is identical under the two distributions. This proves equality of the output density matrices, including arbitrary reference systems and retained measurement records. ∎
This is stronger than passing statistical tests, but weaker than reproducing a complete path distribution.
Explicit implementation
The innovation address domain has size at most \(D=O(r(M+1))\). Choose a field \(\mathbb F_{2^b}\) large enough to label every address, and let
\[
f_c(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{2Q-1}c_\ell x^\ell,
\tag{21}
\]
where the coefficients are independent uniform field elements.
The Vandermonde matrix proves the required independence. Prepare the coefficient registers uniformly using Hadamard gates. They remain private and unchanged throughout the simulation.
Each evaluation of (21) uses reversible Horner evaluation:
\[
O(Q\,\operatorname{poly}(b))
\]
gates. Its scratch registers are uncomputed after copying the function value; otherwise they could leak additional seed information and invalidate the query-interface argument.
Across all \(Q\) innovation calls, the total cost is
\[
\boxed{O(Q^2\operatorname{poly}(b))}
\tag{22}
\]
gates, with \(O(Qb)\) seed and polynomial-evaluation workspace.
Field construction need not be an uncharged randomized preprocessing step. Round \(b\) up to a power of two and build a quadratic tower. Starting from \(\mathbb F_2\), maintain an element \(a\) of absolute trace one and adjoin
\[
u^2+u+a=0.
\]
This polynomial is irreducible because the image of \(z\mapsto z^2+z\) is the trace-zero subspace. The next trace-one element is \(ua\), since its relative trace is \(a\). Repeating gives explicit polynomial-size field-arithmetic circuits.
Why the proof order matters
The error estimates in Sections 3–5 are proved under fully independent innovations. Only afterwards is the finite compiled circuit replaced by (21), using exact equality of its averaged output channel.
I do not assume that the short-seed process is globally Gaussian, globally Markovian, or close in total variation to a complete independent innovation table.
The cap (16) fixes \(q\), hence \(Q\), before the seed family is selected. There is no circular dependence between the random family and its required independence order.
Combining the approximation errors with this exact replacement proves (5).
7. Complete resource accounting
A sufficient working precision \(b\), before the harmless power-of-two rounding, is
\[
b=
O\!\left(
B_{\mathrm{in}}+m+
\log\frac{(r+2)(q+1)(1+ST)}{\varepsilon}
\right),
\tag{23}
\]
with enlarged constants for the reversible arithmetic and elementary-gate synthesis budgets.
Exponentials of very large negative arguments can be replaced by zero once below the error budget. Small differences such as \(1-e^{-x}\) are evaluated without subtractive cancellation. None of these routines requires time proportional to the numerical value of \(\lambda_{\max}T\).
The explicit gate bound is
\[
\boxed{
G=
O\!\left(
q\,C_{\mathrm{sel}}(b)
+Q^2\operatorname{poly}(b)
\right)
+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}.
}
\tag{24}
\]
Because \(m\) and \(b\) are logarithmic in the grid resolution and \(\Lambda\) differs from \(\alpha_0+S\) only by the Gaussian cap, this yields (1).
