## 8. Matching positive lift and exact finite-horizon simulation

Let 0<r_0<=1. A maximal chord-distance r_0-separated subset {u_i} of S^2 has N<=16/r_0^2 points: the disjoint caps of chord radius r_0/2 have normalized area r_0^2/16. Maximality makes it an r_0-net. Therefore, with alpha=1-r_0^2/2,

\[
 \alpha B^3\subseteq L:=\operatorname{conv}\{u_i\}\subseteq B^3.
\]

Indeed every unit support direction x has a code point with x dot u_i>=1-r_0^2/2. Let w_i=u_i/alpha and P=conv{w_i}, so B^3 is contained in P.

For every g in SO(3),

\[
 \alpha gw_i=gu_i\in B^3\subseteq P.
\]

Choose nonnegative barycentric coefficients P(g)_{ij}, summing to one, such that

\[
 \sum_jP(g)_{ij}w_j=\alpha gw_i.
 \tag{17}
\]

For a finite alphabet these are finitely many feasible linear systems. For continuous controls a fixed triangulation of the polytope gives a measurable barycentric selection; boundaries can be assigned by a fixed index rule.

The dual of T_P is

\[
 (\mathcal A_Pz)_i=\sum_g\nu(g)g^{-1}\sum_jP(g)_{ij}z_j.
\]

Equation (17) says A_P W=alpha W. Taking r_0=4/sqrt(m) for m>=16 gives N<=m and alpha=1-8/m. Padding unused states does not affect the eigenvector. Consequently ||T_P^T||>=alpha^T, proving (3).

### Exact readout compensation

Encode any x in B^3 by barycentric coordinates in P. After t gates, the expected grid vertex is alpha^t G_t x. Define

\[
 h_{t,u}(i)=\frac{1+\delta\alpha^{-t}u\cdot w_i}{2}.
 \tag{18}
\]

This is a valid [0,1] response whenever delta alpha^{-(T+1)}<=1. Its expectation is *exactly*

\[
 \frac{1+\delta u\cdot G_tx}{2}.
\]

For delta=1/3 choose r_0^2=1/(T+1), so alpha=1-1/[2(T+1)]. Bernoulli's inequality gives alpha^{T+1}>=1/2>delta. Thus N<=16(T+1) gives zero probability error at every t<=T. The shrinking internal mean is compensated by increasing the readout slope within its permitted range; there is no negative probability and no use of past-gate history at readout.

This construction uses precisely the allowed time-dependent readout. The same conclusion is not asserted for a model requiring one fixed readout at all times. The fully sharp visibility delta=1 is also outside this particular headroom argument.

## 9. Finite preparations and a single observable prove Theorem B

Take X_0 uniform on the six signed coordinate vectors, and take all gates iid uniform on (4). Its covariance is I/3 and stays so under every fixed product of rotations. Set f=e_3 dot X_T. Then E f^2=1/3 and |f|<=1.

If Y is the terminal bit, its target conditional mean as a +/-1 variable is (1/3) f. Hence the bounded score Z=(2Y-1)f has target mean 1/9. Uniform probability error at most epsilon changes its mean by at most 2epsilon. At epsilon<=1/72, the simulator must therefore satisfy

\[
 \mathbb E Z\ge1/12.
\]

But its readout depends on S_T only, so

\[
 |\mathbb EZ|
 =\left|\sum_i(2h_i-1)e_3\cdot b_i(T)\right|
 \le\sum_i\|b_i(T)\|\le F_m(T).
\]

Theorem A implies 1/12<=2exp(-c_nu T/m), which rearranges to the lower bound in (6). The exact upper bound is Section 8. QED.

An alternative direct, less sharp constant follows from R_T>=1/12, M_T>=1/144, and (16):

\[
 m\ge\frac{\beta T}{4,586,471,424}.
\]

The theorem concerns a realizable quantum experiment: prepare rho_x=(I+x dot sigma)/2, apply the SU(2) lifts, and measure the binary effect (I+(1/3)u dot sigma)/2. The Born probability is (5). This is a one-qubit versus log(T)-classical-bit separation in the stated streaming model, not a general quantum algorithmic speedup.
