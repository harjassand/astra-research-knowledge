## 11. Executed diagnostics

`checks.py` performs exact rational assertions for the 97% inequality and universal upper obstruction, a 200,003-point detuning diagnostic, four numerical Levy-integral checks, and finite four-/six-site half-filled interacting-ring solves. The finite quantum generator retains the diagonal interactions and all coherences in the number sector. A sparse bordered inverse enforces trace zero. These are floating-point calculations, not interval-certified transport proofs.

The tested Hamiltonian is

    H = sum_i v_i n_i + sum_i U_i n_i n_(i+1)
        - epsilon sum_i (sigma_i^+ sigma_(i+1)^- + h.c.),

with periodic boundaries and fixed random seed 17. All fields and interactions are stored in `results.json`.

The reported quantum quantity is the zero-frequency current quadratic form

    kappa_Q = Re Tr[J(-L)^+J]/(n dim),

not a claimed independently certified thermodynamic material conductivity. The kinetic comparison uses the corresponding finite-ring displacement variational form. The scalar baseline is **numerically optimized**, using a 129-point logarithmic grid and local refinement. It is not a rigorously certified global numerical optimizer. Since every edge rate decreases above the maximum detuning, the numerical search domain is bounded without losing a scalar maximizer.

For four sites the kinetic matched/oracle ratio is about 0.98682, and matched/numerically-optimized-scalar is about 1.13861. For six sites these are about 0.98537 and 1.27710. At epsilon=.02, the full quantum current divided by its kinetic prediction differs by approximately 0.0113% and 0.0063%, respectively. At larger epsilon the discrepancies grow; no uniform error law is inferred.

Cancelling D gives kinetic current improvements of about 7.85 and 6.20 over the matched controller. The same dominance appears in the small-epsilon full quantum tests. This is a failure of an unrestricted advantage claim, not evidence to be omitted.

`additional_checks.py` tests the finite-pulse cutoff on 42 detunings and evaluates the exact product-dimer correlation formula. At J=.05, eta=1, the product observable's quantum/kinetic integrated-correlation ratio grows from 1 for one dimer to about 125.67 for one million independent dimers. The latter calculation uses the analytic product formula, not a million-dimer Hilbert-space simulation.
