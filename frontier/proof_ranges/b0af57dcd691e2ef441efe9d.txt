# Exact workload transfer and its boundaries

Status: internal derivation, not external theorem review or historical-priority clearance.

## 1. Classical endpoint contract

Let G=(V,E) be finite, undirected, and supplied. Each edge e has a fixed nonnegative
rate w_e that does not depend on labels, colors, configuration, or time. At an
independent Poisson clock ring, exchange the two endpoint labels. Start from a
deterministic permutation. For horizon T, use dimensionless rates r_e=T w_e.

Write p_r for the resulting full permutation endpoint law. The delivered theorem
claims a bound TV(p_r,p_q)<=epsilon, uniform over initial permutations, derived
from killed-pair survival and two-sided Duhamel smoothing. A deterministic color
projection F satisfies TV(F_*p_r,F_*p_q)<=epsilon. Random initial data may also be
included: condition on the initial data, apply the uniform bound, then average.
The joint law of (saved initial data, endpoint) inherits the same bound.

This last observation permits an endpoint-derived statistic, but does not turn an
endpoint certificate into a microscopic path or multi-time-process certificate.
Independent samples at different times do not reproduce temporal correlations.

## 2. Quantum random-SWAP channel

Assume n identical local Hilbert spaces of finite dimension d. Let U_e be the
unitary SWAP on edge e, and define the time-independent generator

    G_w(X) = sum_e w_e (U_e X U_e^dagger - X).

Assume H=0 and no other Lindblad term. The same independent Poisson clocks give a
random product of SWAPs. Grouping products by their endpoint permutation proves

    Phi_w,T(X) = sum_pi p_r(pi) U_pi X U_pi^dagger,

where r=T w and p_r is exactly the classical interchange endpoint law. This is a
classical permutation-mixture mechanism, already explicit in the quantum-gossip
literature; it is not a new quantum simulation mechanism.

For every auxiliary reference R and every trace-class operator X on system+R,

    ||[(Phi_p-Phi_q) tensor Id_R](X)||_1
      <= sum_pi |p(pi)-q(pi)| ||(U_pi tensor I) X (U_pi^dagger tensor I)||_1
      = 2 TV(p,q) ||X||_1.

Taking the completely bounded trace norm gives the exact transfer

    (1/2) ||Phi_p-Phi_q||_diamond <= TV(p,q) <= epsilon.

It is dimension-independent and reference-uniform. It survives arbitrary common
CPTP pre/postprocessing by norm contractivity. The bound is generally an upper
bound, not an equality for qubits: different permutation mixtures need not be
distinguishable on the representation furnished by fixed small local dimension.

A common Hamiltonian commuting with every U_e can be factored out and leaves
the bound unchanged. A generic Hamiltonian, coherent partial SWAP, state-dependent
control, graph switching, other noise, or non-Markovian evolution is not covered
by this fixed-rate transfer without a separate argument. Piecewise-constant
segments can be treated only after giving each segment its own certificate and
charging their errors and costs; this does not come free from one endpoint call.

Sampling one permutation and applying U_pi realizes one trial of the randomized
channel. It does not output a classical approximation to the whole density
matrix. Estimating one bounded measurement probability to statistical error eta
and failure probability delta takes, for example, at least the separately
budgeted Hoeffding sufficient count ceil(log(2/delta)/(2 eta^2)); the channel
approximation contributes systematic error epsilon. Tomography and full classical
density-matrix output retain their own exponential output costs.

### Acquisition and implementation error

If the actual CPTP Markov evolution has generator G_w+Delta(t), variation of
constants and CPTP diamond-norm contractivity give

    (1/2)||Phi_actual,T-Phi_w,T||_diamond
      <= (1/2) integral_0^T ||Delta(t)||_diamond dt.

For a rate-only discrepancy, Delta=sum_e (w_actual,e-w_e)(Ad_Ue-I), the elementary
bound is T sum_e |w_actual,e-w_e|. More favorable model-specific sensitivity may
exist, but a robust interval-rate implementation has not been supplied here.
Symmetry, support, independence, Markov validity, extra noise, and rate intervals
must be acquired or controlled. Counting fast events does not acquire them.

If a sampled permutation is executed as a gate sequence whose jth implemented
gate has half-diamond error eta_j, telescoping adds sum_j eta_j. Routing, gate
durations, preparation, reset, random bits, readout, and repetitions are also
paid. The final uniform block shuffle can cost quadratically many nearest-
neighbor SWAPs on a path (a typical permutation has order s^2 inversions), even
though its classical Fisher-Yates description uses order s operations.

## 3. SSEP projection and the strongest low-order competitor

For binary colors, let eta(x) indicate occupancy. Interchange projects exactly to

    G f(eta)=sum_{x,y edge} w_xy [f(eta^{xy})-f(eta)].

Swapping two occupied labels is invisible to this binary projection. The
interchange labels are not physical vacancy-mediated tracer labels. Distinct
particle species or tagged-particle trajectories need a separate transfer; the
binary occupancy identity does not supply it.

For A subset V of size k, set f_A(eta)=product_{x in A} eta(x). Direct algebra gives

    G f_A = sum_{x in A,y outside A} w_xy
              [f_{A minus x plus y}-f_A].

Thus k-point correlations evolve under the ordinary k-particle SSEP generator on
binomial(n,k) subsets. For k=1 this is the n-state one-walker heat equation. For
k=2 the exclusion generator has reflecting blocked moves, not the killed-pair
generator used in the certificate. The nonintersection determinant in the
delivered report must not be substituted for this duality generator.

Low-degree correlations, density means, and one-site quantum reduced states
therefore have smaller same-information problems. For a quantum one-site
observable X_i, Ad_SWAPij(X_i)=X_j, so its expectation evolves by the same n-state
heat equation even when the initial global state is correlated.

In one-dimensional finite closed SSEP, a tagged physical particle can sometimes
be recovered from its conserved order and the entire endpoint occupancy, and
net transfer through a cut can be an endpoint mass difference. Such an exact
reconstruction needs its own boundary/order assumptions. It gives neither
interchange-label tracer motion nor total jump activity, heat, or microscopic
current paths. The infinite-volume and multi-time slow-bond fluctuation paper
does not by itself provide the required finite-window truncation certificate.

## 4. Exact rejection of whole-cube averaging for an actual workload

Barp et al.'s u=1 model is a rate-1 nearest-neighbor interchange on a cubic box
at horizon beta. Its cycle/shadow-length partition is a function of the endpoint
permutation, so endpoint TV directly controls its distribution and every bounded
cycle statistic. Double bars (u<1) and cycle-weighted quantum-spin ensembles are
outside this interchange reduction.

Let n=L^3, L>=80, and beta<=1. Track the label initially at x. Its jump rate is
at most 6, hence E J<=6 beta. Markov's inequality on the integer jump count yields
P(J>=13)<=6 beta/13<=6/13. With at most 12 jumps, the label lies in the coordinate
cube of radius 12 around x. That cube contains at most 25^3 vertices, including
with periodic boundaries. Therefore the original law assigns that event at
least 7/13 probability. A uniform permutation of the full cube assigns it at
most 25^3/L^3. By projection and the event characterization of TV,

    TV(p_original, p_full_uniform)
      >= 7/13 - 25^3/L^3
      >= 0.5079439603... for L>=80.

This is a proved lower bound at the actual beta=1 displayed sample and throughout
beta<=1, not a floating observation. It rules out the simplest full-cube averaging
at epsilon=.05. It is not a universal impossibility theorem for every smaller
partition or for specialized cycle-observable approximations.
