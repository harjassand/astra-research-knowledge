## 2. Exact quartic acoustic flatness for the fixed integrand $G$

Use row-major coordinates $X=(a,b,c,d)$ and the source integrand
\[
G(X)=3(u+v-2q)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+q^2-\frac52qu,
\quad u=a^2+c^2,\quad v=b^2+d^2,\quad q=ad-bc.
\]
For $e>0$, define
\[
R_\pm(e)=
\begin{pmatrix}\pm2e&1\\6e^2&\pm3e\end{pmatrix},\qquad
D(e)=|R_\pm(e)|^2=1+13e^2+36e^4.
\]
Both determinants vanish exactly. Their dot product is $1-13e^2+36e^4$, so their normalized separation is
\[
s_e^2=1-\frac{(R_+:R_-)^2}{D^2}
=\frac{52e^2(1+36e^4)}{D^2}.
\]

Let $Q_e=D^2G(R_+)+D^2G(R_-)$. In coordinate ordering $(a,d)\oplus(b,c)$ this matrix is block diagonal. Its $(a,d)$ block $A_e$ has
\[
\det A_e=80e^4(243e^4+192e^2+13),\qquad
\operatorname{tr}A_e=12(179e^4+66e^2+4).
\]
The other block $B_e$ has positive leading entry and determinant
\[
(B_e)_{11}=24(6e^2+1)(19e^2+3),\qquad
\det B_e=48(15201e^8+9752e^6+2129e^4+192e^2+6),
\]
so $B_e\succ0$. The large eigenvalue of $A_e$ tends to $48$, while $\det A_e/e^4\to1040$. Hence its small eigenvalue satisfies
\[
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(Q_e)}{e^4}\longrightarrow\frac{1040}{48}=\frac{65}{3}.
\]
For the packet's acoustic modulus
\[
\mu_G(s)=\min_{\substack{|R|=|S|=1,\ \det R=\det S=0\\1-(R:S)^2\ge s^2}}
\lambda_{\min}(D^2G(R)+D^2G(S)),
\]
the normalized pair $R_\pm/\sqrt D$ is an admissible test at separation $s_e$. Since $D^2G$ is degree two,
\[
\limsup_{e\downarrow0}\frac{\mu_G(s_e)}{s_e^4}
\le\frac{65/3}{52^2}=\frac5{624}.
\]
This is an upper-asymptotic obstruction to any lower bound $\mu_G(s)\ge c s^\alpha$ with fixed $c>0$ and $\alpha<4$. It does not establish a matching $s^4$ lower bound or any universal exponent for other integrands.
