7. DECISIVE FAILURES AND SCOPE

* Matching covariance does not establish a Gaussian law. Random covariance
  completion leaves fourth and higher cumulants. A stochastic unbiased
  action is not a random symmetric matrix available inside a square root.

* R28 gives an explicit conservative one-dimensional stress test of a frozen
  central-secant Hamiltonian rotation: g_k(x)=sin(kx)/k has vanishing mean
  Hessian, but its secant at radius1/k is cos(kX)sin(v). The resulting flow
  has variance defect (t^2/2)E cos^2(kX)[1-exp(-2)-2exp(-1)]+O(t^4),
  uniformly nonzero at small fixed t as k grows. Root checked the coefficient.
  Antithetic time reversal preserves it. This refutes that particular frozen
  source channel, not the exact chi_3 factory or the sampling objective.

* The generic expected-matrix-action interface is too weak: A=Bernoulli(p)I,
  ||v||=sqrt(d), requires Omega(d) observations to resolve p by c/sqrt(d),
  while the corresponding Gaussian output means have constant TV separation.
  This is not a lower bound for active deterministic g-value queries; their
  source/endpoints and gradient geometry must be retained.

* An exact fixed-covariance Gaussian family obtained by a fixed finite linear
  Cameron–Martin translation of one Gaussian seed must have affine mean.
  Hence nonlinear exact channels cannot also have that overly strong cache
  interface. This does not exclude approximate polylog samplers; TV does not
  control exponential tails without an additional receipt.
