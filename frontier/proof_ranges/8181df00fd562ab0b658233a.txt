## 1. Definitions and statements

All matrix algebras are over the complex numbers. Let Gamma=id tensor transpose on M_d tensor M_d. Write S_k for the cone of positive semidefinite matrices of Schmidt number at most k, and

\[
 \mathcal D_k=\mathcal S_k+\Gamma(\mathcal S_k),\qquad
 \mathcal C_k=\{A+B^\Gamma:A,B\in\mathcal S_k,\ \operatorname{Tr}A+\operatorname{Tr}B=1\}.
\]

The second set may contain nonpositive Hermitian matrices. It is the trace-one base of the first cone. It is compact: A and B lie in compact trace-bounded positive cones, and the image under the displayed continuous addition is compact. In particular, there is no hidden unbounded cancellation between the two summands.

A linear map P:M_d -> M_d is **r-entanglement breaking** (r-EB) if

\[
 (\operatorname{id}_r\otimes P)(X)\in\operatorname{SEP}(\mathbb C^r:\mathbb C^d)
 \quad\text{for every }X\succeq0.
\]

This entails r-positivity but does not require complete positivity. A map is **decomposable** if P=P_1+transpose o P_2 for completely positive P_1,P_2. Under the input-first Choi convention J(P)=sum_ij E_ij tensor P(E_ij), decomposability is equivalent to J(P)=A+B^Gamma with A,B positive semidefinite.

Set C=2^23=8,388,608.

### Theorem A: arbitrary finite-reference entanglement breaking does not imply decomposability

For every integer r>=1 and every d>=C r^3, there exists a complex-linear, real-coefficient map P:M_d -> M_d such that:

1. P is unital and trace preserving.
2. P o transpose=P=transpose o P.
3. Both P and its Hilbert–Schmidt adjoint P* are r-EB.
4. P is indecomposable. In fact,
   \[
    \inf_{D\text{ decomposable}}\|P-D\|_\diamond\ge6/55.
   \]

The diamond norm here is the completely stabilized trace norm of linear maps. Since P is not a physical channel, this last assertion must not be described as an experimentally implementable channel-discrimination protocol.

For r=2 the sufficient dimension in this conservative proof is d=2^26=67,108,864. No such giant matrix was materialized. The construction is a specified Gaussian ensemble with a proved positive probability of satisfying all of the required universal inequalities.

This gives a negative answer to Open Problem 1 of Devendra–Mallick–Sumesh, *Mapping cone of k-entanglement breaking maps*, 2021 preprint / Positivity 27,5 (2023), provided the derivation survives specialist audit. It is not a solution of PPT-cubed or NPT bound entanglement.
