3. The nonlocal operation: make a stream potential constant across obstacles
On the coarse lattice, call an edge open when both endpoint blocks are good. Let
\[
b=1-\gamma(p)
\]
be the bad-block probability.
Draw the dual edges crossing closed coarse edges. Each bad block produces a small closed-dual square; neighboring bad blocks join these into larger components.
For a dual vertex \(z\), let \(C_z\) be its closed-dual component. Define
\[
\Psi(z)=\frac{1}{|C_z|}\sum_{w\in C_z}w_2.
\tag{4}
\]
Thus the affine height \(z_2\) is replaced by its average over the entire obstacle component.
Take the rotated discrete gradient:
\[
k_h(x,y)=\Psi(x,y)-\Psi(x,y-1),
\]
\[
k_v(x,y)=\Psi(x-1,y)-\Psi(x,y).
\tag{5}
\]
Three properties follow directly.
Spatial conservation. The discrete divergence of \(k\) vanishes by cancellation of the four potential differences.
Legality. A closed edge has both dual endpoints in the same component, so its current is zero.
Nonzero mean transport. The correction
\[
\beta(z)=\Psi(z)-z_2
\]
is stationary. Once its integrability is established,
\[
\mathbb E k_h=1,\qquad \mathbb E k_v=0.
\]
The current is genuinely nonlocal: an obstacle component can be arbitrarily large. The next step controls that nonlocality explicitly.
A finite matrix certifies the infinite tail
Let
\[
R_z=\max_{w\in C_z}\|w-z\|_\infty.
\]
A component reaching distance \(r\) contains a path of at least \(r\) distinct bad sites. Choose a shortest such path. It cannot have a shortcut between nonconsecutive vertices.
A necessary local restriction is particularly useful. After a cardinal step, there are one cardinal and two diagonal continuations without a two-step shortcut. After a diagonal step, there are two cardinal and three diagonal continuations.
These counts give the matrix
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&2\\
2&3
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{6}
\]
With \(A=bM\) and \(u=(1,1)^T\),
\[
\mathbb P(R_z\ge1)\le4b,
\]
\[
\mathbb P(R_z\ge r)
\le16b^2u^TA^{r-2}u,\qquad r\ge2.
\tag{7}
\]
This overcounts the relevant self-avoiding paths, which is safe. It does not assume independence for repeated-site walks.
The tail is summable whenever
\[
\boxed{1-4b-b^2>0.}
\tag{8}
\]
No fitted percolation threshold or unspecified constant is used.
Summing (7) gives
\[
\mathbb E R_z^2
\le
m_2(b)
:=
4b+
\frac{32b^2(3-4b+b^2)}
{(1-4b-b^2)^2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Since \(|\beta(z)|\le R_z\),
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E(k_h^2+k_v^2)
\le
\mathcal K(b):=1+8m_2(b).
}
\tag{10}
\]
This supplies an explicit energy budget for the nonlocal spatial current.
4. The crucial lifting step: freeze the reservoirs inside each module
Simply multiplying \(H\) by \(k\) evaluated on the actual gate field would be wrong. A microscopic transition inside \(H\) can change that field and therefore change the coefficient multiplying the current.
The repair is to evaluate the coefficient on a modified gate field.
For a coarse edge \(e=(u,v)\), let \(\Gamma^{e+}\) be the gate configuration with both endpoint blocks forced good, leaving all other gates unchanged. Define
\[
F_e=k_e(\Gamma^{e+}).
\tag{11}
\]
This coefficient depends only on reservoirs outside the two-block module. It is therefore unchanged by every transition inside that module.
Assemble
\[
J_b^{\mathrm{tile}}
=
\sum_{e:\,b\subset Q_e}F_eH_b^e,
\tag{12}
\]
then average over the nine translated block partitions.
This produces a current covariant under every unit lattice translation, rather than only block translations.
Why the defects cancel exactly
On a nonzero source in (2), both endpoint reservoirs are good. Therefore
\[
F_eg_ug_v=k_e(\Gamma).
\tag{13}
\]
After substituting the finite source identity, the global conservation pairing reduces to
\[
\sum_{e=(u,v)}
\mathbb E\!\left[
k_e(\Gamma)(\sigma_u-\sigma_v)f
\right],
\tag{14}
\]
where \(\sigma_u\) denotes the port occupation.
Condition on all reservoirs. The current \(k\) is now fixed, and the ports remain independent Bernoulli variables.
For a local test function \(f\), put
\[
m_u=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{ports}}[(\sigma_u-p)f].
\]
Only finitely many \(m_u\) are nonzero. Equation (14) becomes
\[
\sum_{e=(u,v)}k_e(m_u-m_v)
=
\sum_um_u\,\operatorname{div}k(u)
=0.
\]
