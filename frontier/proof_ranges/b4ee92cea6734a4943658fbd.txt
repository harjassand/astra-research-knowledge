[H_0(t),Z_i] = 0,
{V_i(t),Z_i} = 0,
[V_i(t),Z_j] = 0  for i != j,
||V_i(t)|| <= j_i(t),

## 3. Universal singleton Q bound

There is an elementary proof of the one-gap sine estimate that avoids any entire-function theorem. Let a normalized vector have opposite-sector norm `sin(alpha)`, with `alpha in [0,pi/2]`. A charged rotation of angle `theta in [0,pi/2]` gives

```
||P_opp exp(-i theta X_A) psi||
  <= cos(theta)||P_opp psi|| + sin(theta)||P_same psi||
  = sin(alpha+theta),                              (3)
```

provided the induction is used while accumulated angle plus `theta` is at most `pi/2`. Starting in a definite sector, apply (3) successively, with the intervening isometries leaving sector norms unchanged. If total charged action `a<=pi/2`, the final opposite-sector norm is at most `sin(a)`; for `a>=pi/2`, use the norm bound one. This is uniform over initial memory dimension, reference, all preceding records, and every prover operation. Mixed entering states follow by convexity or purification. Rectangular isometries cause no dimension problem because the rotations act on `A tensor` the current memory and sector norms remain well defined.

For an own gap the mismatch probability is therefore at most `s_ik`. At every own-record time the conditional private parity is definite; its other memory can be arbitrary. Consider only conditional states along the event of *no preceding own mismatch*. Each next no-mismatch probability is at least `1-s_ik`, so

```
Pr(no own mismatch throughout) >= product_k(1-s_ik),
Pr(any own mismatch) <= Q_i.                       (4)
```

An endpoint opposite to the initial bit requires an own mismatch. This proves the tester value bound `p_i<=Q_i`. The argument never conditions on another tester's eventual acceptance. It permits the prover to learn whatever information the allowed message transcript reveals; isolated records themselves are not sent.

## 4. Chronological strategy representation and tensor domination

Let a particular finite circuit have `r` joint diagonal-control opportunities, in physical chronological order. All intervening rotations and prescribed pinches act locally on their owning testers; the other testers do nothing. Each tester has a message at each opportunity, including opportunities at which it has no local rotation or pinch.

Orient each tester as a Gutoski-Watrous strategy with `n=r+1` turns:

```
X_i,1 = C,
Y_i,k = C^2      for 1<=k<=r,
X_i,k = C^2      for 2<=k<=r+1,
Y_i,r+1 = C.
```

Turn 1 performs any initial local operations and sends the first copy. Turn `k` receives and uncomputes the preceding copy, performs the next local operations, and sends the next copy. The last turn receives/uncomputes the last copy, performs the remaining local operations and endpoint decision, and emits only a one-dimensional dummy output. Pinches at distinct physical nodes sit in their appropriate local operations in this same chronological sequence. A private pinch of another coordinate is never added. The common prover is a compatible co-strategy.

For `r>=1` each tester's transcript operator acts on a space of dimension `2^(2r)`, with prefixes of dimension `2^(2k-1)` for `k<=r`. The full combined transcript dimension is `2^(2rd)`. Tensor factors must be regrouped by round, not left in a coordinate-major ordering. If `r=0`, there is a single turn with one-dimensional input/output and the argument reduces to the product of scalar local probabilities.

I acquired the author-hosted June 22, 2007 primary PDF of Gutoski and Watrous, *Toward a General Theory of Quantum Games*, and read its definitions, probability formula, trace characterization, positive-domination theorem, and the relevant proofs. Printed pp. 13-14 were also rendered and visually inspected. Theorem 9 states exactly that the maximum forcing probability of a fixed positive outcome `T_i` is the minimum `p_i` for which

```
0 <= T_i <= p_i R_i,  R_i a normalized strategy.    (5)
```

The theorem explicitly has a co-strategy counterpart, but the tester orientation above uses its ordinary strategy form. Dummy one-dimensional messages are explicitly permitted in the definitions. The prover's memory is unrestricted within the finite-dimensional framework; the strategy representation removes dependence on a preselected memory cap.

For clarity, the mathematical content of the primary proof is a support/gauge duality. Positive outcome probabilities pair with compatible co-strategies by the trace inner product. The trace recursions give polar identities for the positive downward closures of normalized strategy/co-strategy sets. The relevant identity is

```
(down co-S_n)^polar = {H : H <= R for some R in S_n}.
```

It follows recursively by the partial-trace/identity adjunction

```
<Tr_W Q,Y> = <Q,Y tensor I_W>,
```

starting at `S_0=co-S_0={1}`. Positivity lets the probability supremum extend to the downward closure. The gauge of the displayed polar is precisely the least scalar in (5). Compact normalized sets make the minimum attained. This is the content actually read in Lemmas 10-12 and the proof of Theorem 9; no one-round-only statement is being extrapolated.

Each normalized `R_i` has positive prefixes satisfying, with consistent factor order,

```
Tr_(Y_i,k) R_i,k = I_(X_i,k) tensor R_i,k-1,
R_i,0=1.
```

Define `R_k` to be the canonically regrouped tensor product of the coordinate prefixes. Independence of partial traces gives

```
Tr_(tensor_i Y_i,k) R_k
  = tensor_i [I_(X_i,k) tensor R_i,k-1]
  = I_(tensor_i X_i,k) tensor R_k-1.               (6)
```

Thus the product is a normalized strategy for the combined message spaces. Independent initializations, local maps, local records and local outcome measurements give the all-accept operator `T=tensor_i T_i`. For positive factors, (5) gives

```
T <= (product_i p_i) tensor_i R_i.
```

For two factors the difference is explicitly

```
(p_1 R_1-T_1) tensor (p_2 R_2)
  + T_1 tensor (p_2 R_2-T_2),
```

a sum of positive operators. Pair with an arbitrary joint positive co-strategy `S`. Theorem 5 and normalization give `Tr((tensor_i R_i)S)=1`, so

```
Pr(all accept) <= product_i p_i <= product_i Q_i. (7)
```

Correlated prover memory and correlated outcomes are allowed. Equation (7) is a positive all-pass maximization result, not probability independence and not a minimum-failure or at-least-one-pass claim. Quantum hedging for other objectives has no bearing on this inequality.

### 5.1 Equal sector dimensions

Decompose the original space into joint sectors `H_a`, allowing unequal or zero ranks. Put `b=max_a dim(H_a)` and choose isometries `J_a:H_a -> C^b`. Their direct sum embeds the original space into `A tensor C^b`. Pad operators by zero on the orthogonal complement:

```
V_i' = J V_i J^*,  H_0' = J H_0 J^*.
```

The embedding intertwines every parity and every original own-pinch projector. The embedded original subspace is invariant. Norms are unchanged, since `J^*J=I`; probabilities and record maps on the original input space are unchanged. The padding need not preserve each original sector dimension: it is a mathematical extension used to upper-bound the original process.

The exact checker includes sector ranks `(1,2,0,1)` and verifies both odd/cross-even identities and the original-vs-padded pinch action on every matrix unit. Empty sectors do not break the construction.

### 5.2 Two conjugates for a Hermitian odd contraction

In one other-parity block, a Hermitian own-odd contraction has the square-block form

```
B = [0 K; K^* 0],  ||K||<=1,  K in L(C^b).
```

Choose `K=WP`, with `P=(K^*K)^(1/2)` and the polar partial isometry extended to a unitary `W`. Equal square dimensions guarantee this extension, including singular values zero. Define

```
U_+ = W(P+i sqrt(I-P^2)),
U_- = W(P-i sqrt(I-P^2)).
```

The functions of `P` commute and `P^2+(I-P^2)=I`, so both `U_+` and `U_-` are unitary and their average is `K`. Choose this separately in every other-parity block and put `D_+=diag(U_+,I)`, `D_-=diag(U_-,I)` in own-parity order. They are diagonal in *every* parity and

```
B = (D_+ X_i D_+^* + D_- X_i D_-^*)/2.           (8)
```

For any nonnegative `theta`, Lie splitting gives

```
exp(-i theta B)
 = lim_m [D_+ exp(-i theta X_i/(2m)) D_+^*
          D_- exp(-i theta X_i/(2m)) D_-^*]^m.    (9)
```

Every charged rotation is nonnegative and the total action is exactly `theta`, for every finite `m`, not merely in the limit. Matrix multiplication is read right-to-left for physical time, but all listed factors are allowed, and reversal of this notation changes no action count or own-gap boundary. No private record or future outcome enters the predetermined choices of the matrices.

The exact checker verifies (8) on a nonnormal complex contraction with singular values exactly `0,3/5,1`, including both endpoints of the polar construction.

### 5.3 Merely integrable time dependence and coordinate splitting

Choose finite partitions containing every actual pinch node. On a cell `I` set

```
A_i,I = integral_I V_i(t)dt,
theta_i,I = integral_I j_i(t)dt,
b_I = sum_i theta_i,I.
```

Then `||A_i,I||<=theta_i,I`. When `theta_i,I>0`, apply (8)-(9) to `A_i,I/theta_i,I`; when zero, the integrated term is zero and can be skipped. First approximate the cell evolution by `exp(-i sum_i A_i,I)` and split the coordinate exponentials by the ordinary Lie formula. If the outer order is `n` and the inner order is `m`, coordinate `i` contributes `2mn` rotations of angle `theta_i,I/(2mn)`, totaling exactly `theta_i,I`.

For the time-ordered cell propagator `U_I`, the Dyson series and the ordinary exponential have identical constant and first-order terms. Each higher-order tail has norm at most `exp(b_I)-1-b_I`; hence

```
||U_I-exp(-i sum_i A_i,I)||
  <= 2(exp(b_I)-1-b_I).                           (10)
```

Let `A=sum_I b_I` and `delta=max_I b_I`. Since `exp(b)-1-b <= b^2 exp(b)/2`, the sum of cell errors is at most `A delta exp(delta)`. Integrability allows partitions with `delta -> 0`, including the finite mandatory nodes. No pointwise continuity of `V_i` is required. Exact and approximate cell factors are unitary, so the full product error telescopes by the sum of local errors. The record isometries have norm one and preserve the estimate. At each fixed partition choose finite Lie orders to make their additional total error arbitrarily small.

Each own gap is a union of cells, because every own node is in the partition. Therefore its charged rotation sum is *exactly* its original `a_ik` throughout the approximating sequence. Rotations and free diagonal controls remain within their original gaps; no missing physical pinch is inserted. The number of comparison rounds may grow, but the scalar bound (7) does not depend on that number.

For all initial unit vectors in `P_s`, (7) holds for every approximating circuit. The norm convergence above passes it to `W`. Zero actions give no charged rotations; if any `Q_i=0`, that private parity cannot flip at all.
