# Elliptic distortion margin for the degenerate quartic G

15 October 2026. Author: qc_genesis_astra. Scope: a new deduction from the
supplied exact Heat18 certificate, plus standard Beurling-transform analysis.
External correctness review and historical novelty are UNKNOWN. This report
does not resolve the general planar rank-one-convex/quasiconvex implication.

## Result

For A=[[a,b],[c,d]], set

\[
 u=a^2+c^2,\quad v=b^2+d^2,\quad j=ad-bc,\qquad
 G(A)=3(u+v-2j)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+j^2-\frac52ju.
\]

Define the linear Cauchy--Riemann defect and its fourth power by

\[
 \mathcal C A=(a-d,b+c),\qquad H(A)=|\mathcal C A|^4=(u+v-2j)^2.
\]

The exact source certificate implies the stronger statement

\[
 \boxed{G-\kappa H\text{ is quasiconvex at every affine background},
 \qquad \kappa=\frac3{16000}.}                    \tag{1}
\]

The subtraction vanishes on the conformal plane. Nevertheless, its linear
defect is elliptic on actual compactly supported gradients. Consequently, on
every bounded Lipschitz domain Omega, for every constant B and every
phi in W0^(1,4)(Omega;R2),

\[
 \boxed{\int_\Omega [G(B+D\phi)-G(B)]
       \ge\gamma\int_\Omega |D\phi|^4,\qquad
       \gamma=\frac1{2500000}.}                  \tag{2}
\]

The same constant works on every periodic rectangle, and on every strip
(0,T) x T with zero profiles at both x ends, independently of T>0. Here and
below T as a space means R/(2 pi Z); y integration on strips is normalized.

This proves three consequences that the incoming non-strict storage note
had explicitly left open:

* a pure fourth-power, duration-independent bound on all incoming gradients;
* pairwise Lipschitz continuity of required-supply storage on every bounded
  ball of the full strong critical space W^(3/4,4)(T), modulo constants;
* uniqueness and quantitative stability of the affine minimizer.

It also gives an explicit interval of degenerate, non-polyconvex,
quasiconvex quartics. A separate exact entry strip proves that the storage
still fails both weak lower and weak upper semicontinuity at critical
regularity. Thus the improved strong topology does not repair the weak
topology or finite Fourier observation.

No optimizer for the arbitrary-duration required-supply infimum, explicit
formula for that infimum, or finite evaluation algorithm is asserted.

 Z^TCSC^TZ=\sum_{i=1}^2d_iD^2G(A)[B_i,B_i]

 \ell_0=az_0,\quad\ell_{12}=dz_0,\quad

 v_1=e_0-e_{12}+e_{16},\qquad v_2=-e_{13}+e_{17}.

Then v1 dot ell=r(z0-z4) and v2 dot ell=r(z1-z5). The heat density for

 K=12d_1v_1v_1^T+12d_2v_2v_2^T.

For clarity, the analytic implication is as follows. Put

therefore gives E(t)->0 as t->infinity, while strong heat continuity gives

## 2. Four output rotations make the margin elliptic

G is invariant under left multiplication by every rotation R_theta: u,v,j
are unchanged. Quasiconvexity is preserved by this multiplication because
R_theta(B+Dphi)=R_theta B+D(R_theta phi). Also

\[
 (R_\theta A)_{11}-(R_\theta A)_{22}
       =r\cos\theta-s\sin\theta,\qquad s=b+c.
\]

Average the four quasiconvex functions F_epsilon(R_theta A) at
theta=0,pi/4,pi/2,3pi/4. The elementary identity

\[
 \frac14\left[r^4+s^4+\left(\frac{r+s}{\sqrt2}\right)^4
                       +\left(\frac{r-s}{\sqrt2}\right)^4\right]
       =\frac38(r^2+s^2)^2
\]

proves (1), with kappa=3 epsilon/8=3/16000. This is a finite positive
average; no limiting cone closure is needed. `verify_distortion_margin.py`
also constructs the averaged rational Gram and closed form and checks their
full polynomial identity. The rotations contain sqrt(2), but their action
on the bilinear Z coordinates has rational coefficients, so the averaged
certificate is entirely rational.

## 3. Explicit elliptic coercivity and the quartic Jensen gap

For any x,h in R2,

\[
 |x+h|^4-|x|^4-4|x|^2x\cdot h\ge\frac13|h|^4.       \tag{4}
\]

Subtracting the right side leaves the exact sum
6(x dot h+|h|^2/3)^2+2(|x|^2|h|^2-(x dot h)^2), which is nonnegative.
Applying (1) and (4), and using integral Dphi=0, gives

\[
 \int [G(B+D\phi)-G(B)]\ge\frac\kappa3
                                  \int|\mathcal C D\phi|^4. \tag{5}
\]

For compactly supported phi, put f=phi1+i phi2. Then
2 partial_bar f=(phi1_x-phi2_y)+i(phi2_x+phi1_y), and
|Dphi|^2=2(|partial f|^2+|partial_bar f|^2). The Fourier multiplier identity
partial f=Beurling(partial_bar f), together with the complex-valued
Beurling bound ||Beurling||_(L4->L4)<=sqrt(24), gives

\[
 \|D\phi\|_4^2
 \le2(\|\partial f\|_4^2+\|\bar\partial f\|_4^2)
 \le50\|\bar\partial f\|_4^2
 =\frac{25}{2}\|\mathcal C D\phi\|_4^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \int|D\phi|^4\le\frac{625}{4}\int|\mathcal C D\phi|^4. \tag{6}
\]

The external analytic input is Theorem 1.1 of
[Bañuelos and Janakiraman, Lp-bounds for the Beurling--Ahlfors transform](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~banuelos/Papers/Beur_Ahl.pdf),
which states ||Beurling||_p<=sqrt(2(p^2-p)) for complex-valued functions,
p>=2. Only its p=4 specialization is used. No sharp Beurling-norm conjecture
is assumed. The multiplier identity follows directly from the symbols of
partial and partial_bar. Combining (5)-(6) gives
kappa(4/1875)=1/2500000, proving (2).

The transfer does not depend on this particular defect. More generally, if
a homogeneous quartic F satisfies quasiconvexity of F-kappa|L A|^4 for a
linear map L, and compatible zero-boundary/periodic gradients obey
||Dphi||4<=K||L Dphi||4 with a uniform constant, then its affine Jensen gap
is at least kappa/(3K^4)||Dphi||4^4. The gluing and storage proof below then
applies with that constant and the polynomial growth bounds of F. This
formulation isolates the sufficient hypothesis: an elliptic convex
subtraction can replace isotropic strict subtraction for these purposes.

The constant is independent of domain and strip width. For W0^(1,4) data,
zero extension to R2 and density prove (6). For a zero-endpoint strip of
arbitrary finite width T, first zero-extend in x, then repeat periodically in
y and multiply by eta(y/L), where eta is a fixed nonzero compactly supported
smooth cutoff. Apply (6) on R2, divide fourth-power integrals by L, and let
L tend to infinity. The extra derivative term phi eta'(y/L)/L vanishes in
the normalized L4 norm. Periodic averaging sends the main terms to the
same positive factor integral eta^4 times their one-period averages. The
factor cancels. No Poincare constant depending on T has entered. For a fully
periodic rectangle, use the same cutoff in both directions; the normalized
cutoff errors again vanish. Smooth approximation handles all Sobolev cases.

The quasiconvex inequality in (5) also holds on every zero-endpoint strip:
periodize in x, joining the two zero traces. The extension is W1,4 across
that seam, and the source periodic heat proof applies on this rectangular
torus. Thus both ingredients of (2) hold uniformly on the glued strips used
below, including strips with moving transverse means.

## 4. Incoming gradients and bounded-ball Lipschitz storage

Let X be the mean-zero W^(3/4,4)(T;R2) space, with its strong Slobodeckij
norm. Fix a bounded linear trace extension E from X to a unit slab, with
zero initial trace and terminal trace U. Write e_j(U)=||D(EU)||_j and choose
C_E with e4(U)<=C_E||U||_X. Normalized unit-slab measure is one, so e2<=e4.

Such an extension can be fixed constructively: in inward distance
t=1-x, take EU(x,y)=chi(t)(eta_t*U)(y), with a compactly supported
periodized smooth mollifier eta_t and a cutoff chi equal to one near zero
and zero at one. The zero integral of differentiated kernels bounds the
integral of the fourth power of the gradient near the boundary by
C integral_(|h|<=1)||U(.+h)-U||_4^4/|h|^4 dh; the cutoff costs at most
C||U||_4^4. This gives the asserted bounded extension on the full critical
Slobodeckij space without a frequency cutoff.

For fixed B set

\[
 f_B(A)=G(B+A)-G(B)-DG(B):A,\quad
 J_B(\Phi)=\int_0^T\mathbb E_y f_B(D\Phi),
\]

\[
 p_B(U)=\inf\{J_B(\Phi):T>0,\ \Phi(0)=0,\ \Phi(T)=U,
                 \ \Phi\in W^{1,4}((0,T)\times\mathbb T;\mathbb R^2)\}.
\]

Widths are arbitrary finite positive numbers. Means may move along a path.
Reflection of E U supplies a fixed return path R U of width one. The
following crude explicit polynomial bounds are sufficient:

\[
 |f_B(A)|\le120(|B|^2|A|^2+|A|^4),                  \tag{7}
\]

\[
 |Df_B(A)|\le180(|B|^2|A|+|B||A|^2+|A|^3).         \tag{8}
\]

Indeed, direct differentiation of the four terms of G bounds the Hessian
operator norm by (359/2)|A|^2<180|A|^2. The corresponding contributions are
144,35/2,3,15. Integrating the Hessian along a segment proves (8). The
second-order Taylor formula gives 90|B|^2|A|^2+60|B||A|^3+15|A|^4, bounded
by the right side of (7).

Put M_B(U)=120[|B|^2 e2(U)^2+e4(U)^4]. Every incoming Phi, glued to R U,
has zero outer traces. Equation (2) implies the genuinely coercive estimate

\[
 J_B(\Phi)\ge\gamma\|D\Phi\|_4^4-M_B(U).          \tag{9}
\]

In particular |p_B(U)|<=M_B(U) and p_B(0)=0. If Phi_eta is any eta-minimizer,
whose existence as an approximate minimizer follows from finiteness, then

\[
 \|D\Phi_\eta\|_4^4
        \le\gamma^{-1}(2M_B(U)+\eta).              \tag{10}
\]

These estimates have no duration factor. They control a minimizing sequence
without asserting that its arbitrary-duration infimum is attained.

Here is the full pairwise comparison. Take U,W in X with norms <=R, and set

\[
 M_{B,R}=120(|B|^2C_E^2R^2+C_E^4R^4),\qquad
 A_{B,R}=(2M_{B,R}/\gamma)^{1/4},\quad
 h=C_E\|W-U\|_X.
\]

Prepend a zero strip to Phi_eta if necessary, so its width is at least one.
Add E(W-U), translated onto its terminal unit slab. This is an admissible
incoming path ending at W; the patch has zero trace at the start of that
slab. Its gradient has L4 norm <=h. On the unit slab, Holder and (8) give,
after eta decreases to zero,

\[
 p_B(W)-p_B(U)
 \le180h\{ |B|^2(A_{B,R}+h)
               +|B|(A_{B,R}+h)^2+(A_{B,R}+h)^3\}.  \tag{11}
\]

Apply the same construction in the opposite direction for the absolute
value. Since h<=2C_E R, (11) proves

\[
 |p_B(W)-p_B(U)|\le L_{B,R}\|W-U\|_X,
\]

where a valid explicit constant is

\[
 L_{B,R}=180C_E\{ |B|^2(A_{B,R}+2C_ER)
                  +|B|(A_{B,R}+2C_ER)^2
                  +(A_{B,R}+2C_ER)^3\}.           \tag{12}
\]

This is ordinary pairwise Lipschitz continuity on every bounded critical
ball, including rough traces. It is stronger than the existing pointwise
Lipschitz assertion at a fixed W1,4 trace. It is proved for the required-supply
value p_B; no claim about every abstract dominating storage is needed.

Constants in a profile are free for the adjusted action: a constant ramp of
width L costs O_B(|c|^2/L+|c|^3/L^2+|c|^4/L^3), tending to zero. Prepending
such ramps in both directions gives p_B(U+c)=p_B(U). Concatenation yields
p_B(W)-p_B(U)<=J_B(Phi) for every compatible path from U to W. The original
unadjusted-action storage is therefore

\[
 P_B(U)=DG(B):(\mathbb E_y U\mid0)
          +p_B(U-\mathbb E_yU).
\]

At B=0, p0 is homogeneous of degree four and (12) has order R^3, as expected.
The affine mean term is retained exactly rather than silently discarded.

## 5. Minimizers, sharp power, and an explicit family

For affine Dirichlet data Bx, (2) proves that Bx is the unique minimizer,
up to any constant already allowed by the boundary class. If its excess is
at most delta, then ||Dphi||4 <=(2500000 delta)^(1/4). The same conclusion
holds for affine-periodic data modulo additive constants.

This power cannot be replaced by a quadratic lower bound near every affine
state. At B=I and phi_t(x,y)=(t sin y,0),

\[
 \mathbb E[G(I+D\phi_t)-G(I)]=\frac98t^4,\qquad
 \mathbb E|D\phi_t|^4=\frac38t^4.
\]

Thus the energy-to-gradient exponent 1/4 is sharp at this background.
Likewise G-epsilon|A|^4 still fails rank-one convexity for every epsilon>0:
its second derivative at A=I in direction e12 is -8 epsilon.

For every eta<=kappa, define

\[
 G_\eta(A)=G(A)-\eta H(A)
   =(3-\eta)(u+v-2j)^2+\frac{35}{24}u^2+j^2-\frac52ju.
\]

Since G_eta=(G-kappa H)+(kappa-eta)H and H is convex, G_eta is
quasiconvex. For eta<kappa it inherits (2) with
gamma_eta=4(kappa-eta)/1875. In particular the interval
0<=eta<=3/32000 has the uniform constant 1/5000000.

Every member is acoustically degenerate:
G_eta(I+t e12)=-1/24+(3-eta)t^4. On rank-one matrices it satisfies
G_eta(A)>=(3-eta)|A|^4. Every member is also non-polyconvex. Indeed,
G_eta(I)=-1/24 and G_eta(J0)=1271/24-16 eta for J0=diag(1,-1).
Choose a positive integer N with N^2>1271-384 eta, and give each of +/-N I
mass 1/[2(1+N^2)] and each of +/-J0 mass N^2/[2(1+N^2)]. The lifted
barycenter (mean A,mean det A) is zero, while its mean energy is

\[
 \frac{N^2(1271-384\eta-N^2)}{24(1+N^2)}<0.
\]

This is a finite-law polyconvexity separator, not a gradient law. For the
displayed nonnegative eta interval one can keep the original N=36.

## 6. An exact negative entry makes the topology sharp

Let U_plus(y)=(cos y,sin y), a0=1/4, and use the actual compatible field
Phi(x,y)=rho(x)U_plus(y). Its gradient is R_y diag(rho',rho), so

\[
 G(D\Phi)=3(\rho'-\rho)^4+\frac{35}{24}(\rho')^4
               +(\rho')^2\rho^2-\frac52(\rho')^3\rho.
\]

Take rho=a0 x on 0<=x<=1, followed by
rho=a0 exp(x-1) on 1<=x<=1+log 4. This is a W1,4 strip from zero to
U_plus. On the first piece its action is (137/120)a0^4. On the second,
the derivative is conformal and G=-rho^4/24, giving -(1-a0^4)/96. Hence

\[
 J_0(\Phi)=-\delta_0,\qquad
 \delta_0=\frac{727}{122880}>0.                     \tag{13}
\]

Its fourth-power gradient energy is finite (indeed 3853/3840). Reversing both
x and y gives the same action from U_minus(y)=(cos y,-sin y) to zero:
both gradient columns change sign and G is even. Thus
p0(U_plus)<=-delta0 and p0(U_minus)>=delta0.

For each positive integer N, set
Phi_N(x,y)=N^(-3/4)Phi(Nx,Ny), on a strip of width (1+log4)/N. Its
action and fourth-power gradient energy are unchanged. The endpoint traces

\[
 U_N^\pm=N^{-3/4}(\cos Ny,\ \pm\sin Ny)
\]

stay bounded in W^(3/4,4), converge weakly there to zero, and converge
strongly in W^(s,4) for every 0<=s<3/4. The seminorm fourth powers have
order N^(4s-3), directly from
|exp(iN(y+h))-exp(iNy)|=2|sin(Nh/2)|. Strong L4 convergence and reflexivity
identify the unique critical weak limit as zero.

At a fixed affine B, the adjusted quadratic contribution to these actions
is O_B(N^(-1/2)); the cubic contribution averages to zero because the
unscaled gradients change sign under y->y+pi. Thus the entry and exit
actions equal -delta0+O_B(N^(-1/2)). Consequently, for every B,

\[
 \limsup_N p_B(U_N^+)\le-\delta_0,
 \qquad \liminf_N p_B(U_N^-)\ge\delta_0.
\]

The now bounded-ball Lipschitz p_B is neither weakly lower nor weakly upper
semicontinuous at zero in the critical topology, and is not continuous in
any of those subcritical strong topologies. At B=0, any function of a fixed
finite Fourier projection has uniform storage approximation error at least
delta0 on a bounded critical ball containing these two sequences: for N
beyond the cutoff both inputs have measurement zero, but their true values
differ by at least 2 delta0. This conclusion makes no continuity assumption
on the function of the measurements.
