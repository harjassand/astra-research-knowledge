\section*{2. Consequence: exact sparse noise geometry}

By Section~1, every formula for $\eta$ below simultaneously applies to the complete, conditional-difference and generalized-information coefficients for regular Petz, fixed interior-order Petz R\'enyi, and Chernoff divergences, conditional on the same source dependencies.

\textbf{Exact error-basis lemma.} Let $\{U_g\}_{g=1}^{d^2}$ be any orthogonal unitary basis, with $\Tr(U_g^*U_h)=d\delta_{gh}$, and let $\N_p(X)=\sum_g p_gU_gXU_g^*$. If every $p_g>0$, then the Choi optimization is solved exactly by
\[
 \boxed{\ M(\N_p)=\frac{1}{d^4}\sum_g\frac1{p_g},\qquad
 \beta(\N_p):=M^{-1}=\frac{d^4}{\sum_g p_g^{-1}}.\ }
 \tag{1}
\]
If one probability is zero, $M=\infty$ and $\beta=0$. These are algebraic identities for the Choi objective; $\eta=1-\beta$ uses N402.

\textit{Proof.} Normalized Choi eigenvectors are $|u_g\rangle=|U_g\rangle\!\rangle/\sqrt d$, so $J=d\sum_gp_g|u_g\rangle\langle u_g|$ and
$\Tr_AJ^{-1}=\Tr_BJ^{-1}=cI$, where $c=d^{-2}\sum_gp_g^{-1}$.
For any density $\sigma$ and $T_\sigma=\Tr_B[(I\otimes\sigma)J^{-1}(I\otimes\sigma)]$,
\[
 \|T_\sigma\|_\infty\ge\frac{\Tr T_\sigma}{d}
 =\frac c d\Tr\sigma^2\ge\frac c{d^2}.
\]
The choice $\sigma=I/d$ attains equality. The block SDP has the exact primal witness $Z=J^{-1}/d^2$, $t=M$. If $p_g=0$, the range constraint forces $(I\otimes\sigma)|u_g\rangle=0$. Full Schmidt rank of $|u_g\rangle$ forces $\sigma=0$, a contradiction. No covariance argument or numerical optimizer is needed.

\textbf{Shortest-noise-word theorem.} Now suppose the basis is a projective Weyl representation of a finite label group $G$, $|G|=d^2$. For positive rates $\gamma_s$, let
\[
 L=\sum_{s\in S}\gamma_s(\Ad U_s-\id),\qquad T_t=e^{tL}.
\]
If $S$ generates $G$, let $\ell(g)$ be the shortest directed word length reaching $g$, $r=\max_g\ell(g)$, and
\[
 c_g=\frac{1}{\ell(g)!}
 \sum_{s_1\cdots s_{\ell(g)}=g}\prod_j\gamma_{s_j},
 \qquad c_e=1.
\]
Then, at fixed group and fixed rates,
\[
 \boxed{\ 1-\eta(T_t)=
 \frac{d^4}{\sum_{\ell(g)=r}c_g^{-1}}t^r+O(t^{r+1}).\ }
 \tag{2}
\]
If the jumps generate only a proper subgroup, $\eta(T_t)=1$ at every finite time.
Indeed, the compound-Poisson expansion gives
$p_t(g)=e^{-\Gamma t}\sum_{k\ge0}(t^k/k!)\sum_{s_1\cdots s_k=g}\prod_j\gamma_{s_j}
=c_gt^{\ell(g)}+O(t^{\ell(g)+1})$.
Substitute in (1): the reciprocal sum is dominated by all farthest labels. Weighted shortest-path data cost $O(|G||S|)$ arithmetic operations by BFS and dynamic programming. This is not a polynomial-in-qubit-count claim when $|G|=4^n$.

\textbf{Exact example and capability.} For one qubit with $X,Z$ jump rates $a,b>0$,
\[
 1-\eta(T_t)=(1-e^{-4at})(1-e^{-4bt})=16ab\,t^2+O(t^3).
 \tag{3}
\]
Its generator has spectral gap $2\min(a,b)>0$, yet
$\lim_{t\downarrow0}-\log\eta(T_t)/t=0$.
Thus no positive $\kappa$ can make $\eta(T_t)\le e^{-\kappa t}$ hold uniformly from time zero. This concerns arbitrary compared states with quantum references. It is compatible with positive fixed-equilibrium complete log-Sobolev constants [5].

For $n$ independent qubits with equal $X,Z$ rates $\gamma$, the exact formula, not a nonuniform small-time expansion, gives
\[
 \eta_n(t)=1-(1-e^{-4\gamma t})^{2n},\qquad
 T_n(\epsilon)=-\frac1{4\gamma}\log\!\left[1-(1-\epsilon)^{1/(2n)}\right].
 \tag{4}
\]
At $t=(\log(2n)+c)/(4\gamma)$, $\eta_n(t)\to1-e^{-e^{-c}}$.
This exactly determines the transition for this coefficient using a scalar formula instead of a growing Choi SDP. The standard CP-replacer Doeblin coefficient is
$\alpha_n(t)=(1-e^{-2\gamma t})^{2n}$; its bound $\eta_n\le1-\alpha_n$ reaches a fixed threshold around $\log(2n)/(2\gamma)$, twice the exact center. General tensor-product quantum mixing cutoffs are established prior art [6]; the scoped contribution is this exact coefficient and comparison. No improvement over every possible fixed-target or mixing method is claimed.
