## 5. Bounded noise: a probe-count-independent precision floor

Consider any deterministic adaptive protocol, allowing even discontinuous choices of the next probe, whose energy inputs satisfy \(\|f_j\|_E^2\le E_{\max}\). Suppose each scalar readout can have arbitrary error \(|e_j|\le\nu\). For a pairing, replace the energy bound by \(\|f_j\|_E\|h_j\|_E\le E_{\max}\). Let \(\delta_k\) denote the right-hand side of (6). If
\[
 E_{\max}\delta_k\le2\nu,                     \tag{8}
\]
the two media admit identical complete transcripts: at each common previous transcript, the next probe is the same; answer its response by the midpoint of the two true values. This answer is within \(\nu\) of both. Thus every reconstruction has worst-case error at least \(\Delta_k/2\), regardless of the number of probes.

For a randomized policy, the same construction yields identical transcript/output distributions when the bounded-error adversary may respond to the actual query. Hence the two disjoint success balls cannot both have probability greater than one half; this is a minimax confidence/expected-risk statement, not a fixed-coefficient pointwise error assertion for every random seed. It does **not** imply that independent centered random noise cannot be averaged away.

For fixed \(E_{\max}\), small \(\nu\), and an \(H^s\) ball containing this family, choose \(k\asymp\log(E_{\max}/\nu)\). Equations (1), (6), and (8) yield the rigorous fixed-coordinate minimax floor
\[
 \text{worst-case }L^2\text{ error}
 \gtrsim [\log(E_{\max}/\nu)]^{-s}.             \tag{9}
\]
This is a specific nonlinear precision barrier, stronger than saying a finite linearization has a kernel. To make the robust absolute error smaller than the response of this pair, normalized readout precision must reach the scale \(k^{-s}r^k\). On an order-one dynamic range, a robust \(b\)-bit accuracy promise therefore needs
\[
 b\gtrsim k\log_2(1/r)+s\log_2k-O(1).          \tag{10}
\]
Equation (10) concerns guaranteed absolute accuracy including calibration bias; it is not a standalone collision theorem for an arbitrarily aligned deterministic quantizer.

## 6. Independent Gaussian noise: exact adaptive energy accounting

Now assume a different, explicitly specified noise model:
\[
 Y_j=Q_\sigma(f_j)+Z_j,\qquad
 Z_j\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,\nu^2),
\]
where \(f_j\) is any measurable function of the previous data and independent internal randomness. The noise variance is the same under both media. Include the independent policy random seed (or an equivalent complete action/randomization history) in the transcript. For a fixed maximum number of queries, the chain rule for relative entropy of this joint transcript gives
\[
 D_{\rm KL}(P_+\|P_-)
 =\frac1{2\nu^2}\mathbb E_+\sum_j
       |Q_+(f_j)-Q_-(f_j)|^2
 \le\frac{\delta_k^2}{2\nu^2}\,
       \mathbb E_+\sum_j\|f_j\|_E^4.            \tag{11}
\]
The adaptive design does not invalidate (11): conditional on the seed and one previous transcript, the two conditional laws use the same next voltage. For observations alone, equality in (11) need not hold because the conditional laws may be mixtures; data processing gives the same upper bound from the joint transcript. For pairings, use \(\|f_j\|_E^2\|h_j\|_E^2\) in the sum. A bounded stopping time can be padded with zero probes.

If reconstruction error is strictly below \(\Delta_k/2\) with probability at least \(3/4\) under each medium, nearest-neighbor classification succeeds with that probability. Hence total variation is at least \(1/2\); Pinsker's inequality and (11) imply
\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E_+\sum_j\|f_j\|_E^4
       \ge\frac{\nu^2}{\delta_k^2}
       \ge\frac{\nu^2}{C_{n,r}^2a^2}
                  k^{2s}r^{-2k}.}              \tag{12}
\]
If \(\|f_j\|_E^2\le E_{\max}\), then \(mE_{\max}^2\) bounds the left-hand side, and \(E_{\max}\mathbb E\sum_j\|f_j\|_E^2\) does too. Thus either repetition count or the charged excitation resource must grow exponentially. For a target error \(\epsilon\), choose \(k\asymp\epsilon^{-1/s}\) so that \(\Delta_k>2\epsilon\). Up to fixed prior constants,
\[
 mE_{\max}^2\gtrsim
       \nu^2\epsilon^{-2}\exp(c\epsilon^{-1/s}).\tag{13}
\]
This theorem assumes fixed absolute Gaussian readout variance. A detector whose variance scales with excitation, measurement duration, or temperature requires its own variance-versus-resource law. No such hardware law is supplied here.

## 7. Corrected finite adaptive transcript theorem

B4's Borsuk–Ulam proof correctly gives \(\gtrsim m^{-s/n}\) separation for a *continuous full transcript*. Its continuity restriction must not be silently removed from that quantitative argument.

There is nevertheless an exact, nonquantitative extension to arbitrary deterministic adaptive policies. Let \(U\subset\mathbb R^D\) be an open family of positive conductivities, and assume each fixed probe has a \(C^1\) scalar response on \(U\). For every deterministic policy with \(m<D\) real outputs, some transcript fiber contains a positive-dimensional embedded local submanifold.

Proof: start with a connected open coordinate neighborhood \(M_0\subset U\). Inductively, \(M_j\) is a connected local manifold on which the first \(j\) outputs have fixed values, and \(\dim M_j\ge D-j\). On this one transcript the next chosen probe is fixed, even if the policy is discontinuous. Its response \(h\) restricted to \(M_j\) is \(C^1\). If \(dh\) vanishes everywhere, connectedness makes \(h\) constant, and retain \(M_j\). Otherwise choose a point with \(dh\ne0\) and retain a connected local regular level set through it; the implicit function theorem reduces dimension by exactly one. After \(m\) steps, dimension remains positive. Two distinct nearby coefficients have identical transcripts. The argument supplies no separation uniform in the policy, so it does not establish the B4 width rate for arbitrary discontinuous adaptivity.

## 8. Geometry and the gauge limitation

Set \(g_k^\pm=(\sigma_k^\pm)^{2/(n-2)}\delta\), and take the connection \(A=0\) on the trivial rank-two bundle. Restricting to one fiber component realizes exactly the scalar conductivity energy above. The metrics have the same ellipticity and regularity bounds, and fixed-chart metric coefficient distance is comparable to (1).

They are not equivalent under a diffeomorphism fixed pointwise on an open boundary patch: a diffeomorphism pulling one conformally Euclidean metric to the other must be conformal, hence Möbius for \(n\ge3\); fixing the patch forces identity. A unitary bundle gauge cannot change the metric. However, the pair is isometric by a rotation that moves boundary points. A physically marked boundary and calibrated input patterns are therefore essential to its interpretation. As B4 emphasizes, inequivalence does not itself bound below an infimum of coefficient distances over all allowed diffeomorphisms. Equations (9), (12), and (13) are proved for calibrated scalar conductivity, or for the fixed conformal representative, not for an unspecified gauge-quotient loss.

## 9. Primary-source comparison and novelty gate

* [Mandache, *Exponential instability in an inverse problem for the Schrödinger equation* (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/17/5/313) establishes exponential instability and extends it to EIT. It is the fundamental predecessor, not evidence that continuum uniqueness is operationally efficient. The usual real-potential entropy exponent is not being renamed as a discovery here.
* [Di Cristo–Rondi, *Examples of exponential instability for elliptic inverse problems* (2003 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0303126) extends the packing/covering mechanism to defects and boundary measurements, including electrode models. This forecloses any claim that finite physical measurements or inclusions are a new general instability domain.
* [Isaev, *Exponential instability in the Gel'fand inverse problem on the energy intervals* (2010 preprint), Theorems 2.3–2.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.2193) separates a real-potential entropy construction from an explicit complex angular potential. The explicit complex construction has the same \(k^{-s}\)-versus-exponential mechanism. Our use of a **real sign pair** and exact rotation covariance avoids comparing a real oscillation directly to zero, where even nonlinear terms survive. The checked source does not establish priority for our sign-pair argument; a full novelty search remains open.
* [Koch–Rüland–Salo, *On instability mechanisms for inverse problems*, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01855) makes the smoothing/entropy mechanism robust to more general geometries and coefficients. Its broader geometry coverage is not supplied by our symmetric ball example. Our concrete deliverable is the explicit pair, exact bandwidth statement, and adaptive noise/energy accounting.
* [Siegel, *Sharp lower bounds on the manifold widths of Sobolev and Besov spaces* (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jco.2024.101884) supplies the generic continuous-encoder width predecessor to B4. The \(m^{-s/n}\) bump/Borsuk–Ulam rate itself is standard.
* [Alberti–Santacesaria, *Calderón's inverse problem with a finite number of measurements* (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2019.31), and [Part II: Independent Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10950), give uniqueness/stability/reconstruction under known finite-dimensional priors. Their number of boundary inputs must not be equated without inspection to a count of real scalar energy readouts: one Neumann boundary function is infinite-dimensional information. Their finite-prior positive results do not contradict this note's smooth-class lower bound.

## 10. Resource and evidence ledger

The medium family, support radius, axis, \(\psi\), smoothness radius, and physical coordinates are *supplied prior information*. Acquiring them from an unknown specimen is not solved. For lower bounds, even supplying them does not weaken the conclusion; unknown support and geometry only make the target harder. Each experimental session charges a real scalar readout, its voltage energy, its implemented angular bandwidth, and its noise level. Repetition with independent Gaussian noise is charged in (11)–(13), rather than being treated as free precision.

If the actual voltage differs from the nominal one by \(e\), \(\|e\|_E\le\xi\), \(\|f\|_E\le A\), the elementary bound
\[
 |Q_\sigma(f+e)-Q_\sigma(f)|
 \le\tfrac32(2A\xi+\xi^2)
\]
shows what worst-case voltage-calibration accuracy is needed when this error is charged as bias. Geometry of the boundary, electrode/contact impedance, flux integration, detector offset/drift, and the absolute-noise law require additional calibration; none is inferred from PDE uniqueness. The lower bound grants perfect knowledge of these items.

The pair has a short symbolic representation: known \(\psi\), an integer \(k\), an amplitude and a sign. There is no numerical PDE solve or large uncharged output table hidden in its construction. Establishing the inequalities above uses weak energy estimates, spherical harmonics, rotation covariance and scalar Schur bounds. A numerical diagnostic, if supplied, is only a finite audit of those mechanisms, not a proof of the PDE theorem. The exact quotient-loss lower bound and a matched upper bound for arbitrary smooth geometry/transport remain open.

## 11. Next constructive refinement

An honest positive target is a known finite family of smooth radial shells, small amplitude about \(\sigma=1\), full boundary access, and \(D\) energy-normalized spherical-harmonic probes of degrees \(1,\ldots,D\). The linear response matrix is a moment/Vandermonde matrix with an explicit positive determinant for ordered disjoint shell supports. A small enough explicit parameter neighborhood should yield a contraction reconstruction with \(D\) scalar measurements, stability constant charged through that determinant, and one-dimensional radial forward solves. This would match the exact dimension obstruction on this restricted prior while displaying the conditioning and prior costs. This refinement is in progress; it is not an established upper bound in this version.
