## 6. What the telegraph parity observable contains

For even \(n=2m\), take a fair random telegraph sign \(\sigma(s)\) in feed-clock time with Poisson flip rate \(r\). Write \(a_0=p_{\rm high}-p_{\rm low}>0\) for isotope contrast and \(b=p_{\rm high}+p_{\rm low}-1\). For a product detected at time \(t\), the age-indexed input is
\[
p_t(a)=\frac{1+b+a_0\sigma(t-a)}2.
\]
Let \(K\) be the number of heavy atoms and \(X_i\in\{+1,-1\}\) their labels. Conditional independence gives
\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^n\frac{X_i-b}{a_0}\,\middle|\,A_1,\ldots,A_n,\sigma\right]
=\prod_{i=1}^n\sigma(t-A_i).
\]
After sorting \(A_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq A_{(2m)}\), the telegraph product is \((-1)^N\), where \(N\) counts flips in the reversed feed-clock intervals between \(t-A_{(2j)}\) and \(t-A_{(2j-1)}\). These disjoint intervals have total length \(L_{2m}\). The Poisson parity identity yields
\[
Q_{2m}(r)=\mathbb E e^{-2rL_{2m}},\qquad
L_{2m}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(A_{(2j)}-A_{(2j-1)}).
\tag{6}
\]
This is a positive observable-specific acquisition: it directly obtains one decision-relevant functional without identifying the full law. With \(K\) alone, use
\[
W_n(K)=\frac{(1-b)^K(-1-b)^{n-K}}{a_0^n},\qquad
\mathbb E W_n(K)=Q_n(r).
\tag{7}
\]
For \(b=0\) and even \(n\), \(W_n(K)=(-1)^K/a_0^n\). The \(n+1\) parent-product count bins suffice. At fixed \(r\), (6) is one scalar and loses the rest of the age configuration. Even knowing \(Q_n(r)\) for every \(r>0\) determines only the distribution of \(L_n\) by uniqueness of the Laplace transform, not the joint age law. For example, four ages \((0,1,10,11)\tau\) and \((0,1,2,3)\tau\) both have \(L_4=2\tau\) but different age span.

For \(N\) independent product draws conditional on the complete code, \(|W_n|\leq B_n=((1+|b|)/a_0)^n\), so the product-sampling component has variance at most \(B_n^2/N\). This is not a total campaign bound: shared-code fluctuations, reaction correlation, detector inversion, and calibration require separate accounting. In the symmetric case the conditional product term is \(a_0^{-2n}/N\). As \(a_0\) shrinks, identification remains mathematically possible but the corrected statistic becomes noisy.

Conditional Chebyshev control of this product-sampling component around the code-conditioned mean requires \(N\geq B_n^2/(\alpha\varepsilon^2)\) to keep additive error above \(\varepsilon\) with probability at most \(\alpha\). The shared-code term is not included in that count. The supplied note's separate uniform code-variance lemma is not independently audited in this subtask.
