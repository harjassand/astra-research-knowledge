## 7. What is established, and what remains missing

The main mathematical result is the complete criterion
\[
\boxed{
\text{finite stochastic marginal-return preparation}
\iff
\text{a product vector exists in the target support}.
}
\tag{36}
\]

Its converse uses a support-persistence argument that survives arbitrarily rare postselection. Its constructive direction gives an explicit finite catalyst and protocol. The stronger source version covers every source whose support is spanned by product vectors.

The geometric component is inherited from separable-approximation theory; the probability-independent ceiling uses Schmidt number; and the support-growth bound uses restricted-measurement relative entropy. Those antecedents must not be relabelled as new mathematics. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707043)

The targeted prior-art search did not establish whether the precise reachability theorem and source extension have appeared elsewhere. Broad quantum catalysis, including differing return conventions, is already an extensive subject. A comprehensive priority determination and independent verification remain outstanding. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00798)

I also checked the finite constructions using exact rational arithmetic: five flagged protocols and twenty-four capped-reservoir instances passed their normalization, marginal-return and subsequent-conditioning identities. These checks do not certify the general impossibility proof or historical originality.

**The route to a foundational computational breakthrough failed at a precise point:** the new finite operation preserves a marginal, but not the return guarantee under subsequent successful use of its output. Imposing that guarantee forces product return and eliminates its source-free entanglement-generating power. Allowing small error does not fix the problem uniformly, as the finite reservoir construction demonstrates.
