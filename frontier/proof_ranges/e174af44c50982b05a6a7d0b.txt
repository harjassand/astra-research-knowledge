I obtained a constructive theorem showing that a rapidly mixing hidden system can require exponentially long passive observation to distinguish from equilibrium, while a specified weak-field experiment distinguishes it in polynomial time.
The passive lower bound covers the entire continuously observed trajectory, including exact transition times and unlimited computational processing. It is not merely a failure of two-point correlations or a particular inference algorithm.
The candidate new mechanism is an observation ladder: a positive stochastic realization whose hidden dynamics has only a third-order nilpotent component, but whose unforced observations must traverse an arbitrarily long algebraic sequence before exposing it. A conservative perturbation accesses a much shorter sequence.
Historical priority and independent correctness remain unestablished. The complete argument and executable constructions are supplied below. Detecting hidden dissipation from apparently time-symmetric observations is already established research; that phenomenon alone is not the novelty claim. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.08765)
For every integer \(r\ge1\), construct a rational continuous-time Markov chain \(Q_r\), with between \(4r\) and \(16r\) microscopic states, and observe only
\[ Y_t=f(X_t)\in\{-1,+1\}. \]
Compare it with an equilibrium binary chain \(Z_t\) that flips sign at rate \(1/2\).
The construction gives
\[ \boxed{ T_{\mathrm{passive}}\ge16^r-2 } \]
for distinguishing the two hypotheses with equal-prior success probability at least \(2/3\).
Under the explicitly specified field intervention below, distinguishing them with error probability at most \(\zeta\) requires only
\[ \boxed{ T_{\mathrm{active}} = O\!\left( r^6\log(1/\zeta)\,[1+\log(r/\zeta)] \right). } \]
The second bound includes sampling and waiting for the system to return close to its unperturbed stationary distribution. It does not assume free resets or an exact susceptibility oracle.
This separation holds despite benign microscopic properties:
\[ \frac{7}{8n}\le q_{ij}\le\frac{9}{8n} \qquad(i\ne j), \]
uniform stationary probabilities, mixing bounded by \(e^{-t/2}\), and every nonzero eigenvalue equal to \(-1\). There are no exponentially rare microscopic states, exponentially small rates, or metastable waiting times.
The entropy-production rate satisfies
\[ \frac1{144r}\le \sigma(Q_r)\le\frac1{112r}. \]
Multiplying both generators by \(r\) makes this a constant positive dissipation interval,
\[ \frac1{144}\le \sigma(rQ_r)\le\frac1{112}, \]
while the passive lower bound remains exponential:
\[ T_{\mathrm{passive}}\ge\frac{16^r-2}{r}. \]
That rescaling increases maximum microscopic exit rates only linearly in \(r\).
The thermodynamic statements use ordinary stationary time reversal, with every microscopic state even under physical time reversal. They do not automatically apply to hidden momentum-like variables.
The efficient experiment assumes the field changes rates according to
\[ \boxed{ q_{ij}^{(h)} = q_{ij}\exp\!\left[\frac h2(f_j-f_i)\right], \qquad i\ne j, } \tag{1} \]
with diagonal entries adjusted to preserve row sums. This intervention assumption is essential. Passive observations do not determine how an unknown system responds to a field.
The general connection between response violations and entropy production is established prior mathematics. The proposed advance is the explicit, quantitatively controlled exponential passive–active separation, not the fluctuation–dissipation principle itself. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9705002)
