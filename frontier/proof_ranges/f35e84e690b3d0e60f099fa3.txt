If a selected encoding uses \(a_{\mathrm{sel}}\) ancillary qubits, a conservative workspace bound is
\[
n+O\!\left(q\,a_{\mathrm{sel}}+Q\operatorname{poly}(b)\right)
+W_{\mathrm{ctrl}}.
\tag{25}
\]
It includes seed storage, bridge evaluation, time sorting, and operator-encoding ancillas. No \(M\)-entry noise array is present.
Classical preprocessing reads the supplied coefficients and parameters, compiles their selectors, and constructs the arithmetic routines. Its cost is polynomial in their encoded size and working precision. Generating the expanded quantum circuit is charged in proportion to its gate description.
For an ordinary explicit list of \(O(n)\) bounded-locality Pauli terms and local noise operators, a scan-based selector costs
\[
C_{\mathrm{sel}}(b)=O(n\,\operatorname{poly}(b)).
\]
This requires no quantum random-access memory.
The output is a quantum channel implementation. Writing a full density matrix or a complete noise trajectory remains an exponentially or grid-sized output task where applicable.
8. Strongest scientific consequence: controlled dynamics with persistent noise memory
The same private seed is used before and after every intervention.
Consequently, a system measurement can reveal information about the noise, and future evolution retains the corresponding conditional correlations automatically. The simulator does not reset the bath, independently redraw a suffix, or estimate a classical hazard from the unknown quantum state.
This supports a concrete physical workload:
\[
H_X(t)=
\sum_{(i,j)}J_{ij}P_iP_j
+\sum_i h_iP_i
+\sum_i \sigma_iX_i(t)Z_i,
\tag{26}
\]
with bounded-degree interactions, arbitrary supplied coefficients, and prescribed control pulses. The deterministic terms may fail to commute with the noise and with one another.
Colored-noise Hamiltonians are already used to investigate excitonic transport and finite environmental memory. The existing trajectory approach generates correlated fluctuations and propagates along time steps; its scientific relevance does not by itself establish a fault-tolerant complexity bound. arXiv
For \(O(n)\) terms and modes, bounded strengths \(J,\sigma\), and \(p\) control slots,
\[
G=
\widetilde O\!\left(
n\mathcal A+\mathcal A^2
\right)+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}},
\quad
\mathcal A=1+p+O(n(J+\sigma)T).
\tag{27}
\]
At \(T=O(1/J)\), \(\sigma=O(J)\), and \(p=O(n)\), this is
\[
\boxed{G=\widetilde O(n^2)+C_{\mathrm{ctrl}}.}
\tag{28}
\]
The bound remains independent of the number of grid points used to certify noise resolution, apart from logarithms.
This is a many-body statement. I am not claiming an exponential quantum advantage by treating a single-excitation problem, whose active state space is only linear in the number of sites, as an unrestricted \(2^n\)-dimensional workload. The distinction is explicit in the excitonic-model literature. arXiv
Actual observable and control-quality estimation
Suppose an input-preparation circuit and its inverse cost \(C_{\mathrm{in}}\), and a bounded effect \(0\le F\le I\) has a coherent implementation costing \(C_F\).
Prepare the seed coherently and retain the simulator’s work registers. The complete simulation has an inverse. Compiling to channel error \(O(\delta)\), amplitude estimation yields additive error \(\delta\) with failure probability at most \(\zeta\) at cost
\[
\boxed{
\widetilde O\!\left[
\frac{G(\delta)+C_{\mathrm{in}}+C_F}{\delta}
\log\frac1\zeta
\right].
}
\tag{29}
\]
The amplitude-estimation improvement is established prior mathematics; the construction here supplies the coherent noise-averaged circuit to which it applies. arXiv
For example, prepare Bell pairs between the simulated system and a reference, apply a noisy control sequence, undo its intended ideal unitary, and measure the Bell projector. This estimates the sequence’s entanglement fidelity under the specified correlated-noise model. Bell preparation and readout cost \(O(n)\), in addition to the supplied ideal-control circuit.
When only independent input copies are available, ordinary sampling instead gives the usual \(\delta^{-2}\log(1/\zeta)\) statistical factor.
Amplitude estimation does not require enlarging the seed to cover all its repetitions: it estimates the success amplitude of the already compiled, already accurate simulation circuit. It is not using that seed as an unlimited physical-noise service.
9. Comparison against competent alternatives
The result should not be compared only with first-order trajectory stepping.
Succinct stochastic-path algorithms already exist. Bouland–Dandapani–Prakash give a quantum spectral method producing amplitude encodings of stochastic trajectories. That is a different interface from a coherent binary-value oracle needed inside a noncommuting Hamiltonian simulator; converting arbitrary path amplitudes into accurate values is not free. arXiv
Pseudomode methods are a serious alternative. The current coupled-Lindblad pseudomode theory constructs completely positive Gaussian-bath representations under explicit feasibility conditions. Such a representation can be preferable. Its mode-count and representation theorems are not, by themselves, the same total-gate bound as (24); moreover, Gaussian bath-correlation matching does not determine the non-Gaussian telegraph process covered here. arXiv
Telegraph noise has an exact finite Markovian embedding. Introduce one maximally mixed classical bath qubit per mode and use
\[
H_{\mathrm{ext}}=H_0+\sum_a A_a\otimes Z_a,
\qquad
L_a=\sqrt{\lambda_a/2}\,X_a
\]
on bath qubit \(a\). The bath remains block diagonal in the \(Z\)-basis, giving exactly the prescribed switching process.
This embedding uses \(r\) bath qubits—not exponentially many qubits. Applying the current general gate-efficient Lindblad theorem gives normalized time
\[
\tau=
T\left(
\alpha_0+S+\frac12\sum_a\lambda_a
\right).
\tag{30}
\]
That algorithm is essentially linear in \(\tau\), with logarithmic precision overhead. arXiv
The present construction trades a quadratic dependence on Hamiltonian action for removal of the leading switching-rate cost in this uniform bound. When rates are moderate, the embedding may be cheaper. When rates are large, specialized averaging limits may also be cheaper if their model-approximation errors can be certified.
Thus the defensible comparison is:
A uniform explicit-gate bound controlled by system–noise interaction strength and control count, rather than by resolved bath updates.

It is not a proved lower-bound separation from all specialized algorithms.
