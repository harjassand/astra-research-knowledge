## Strictly weaker fixed-profile certificate

Now fix the scalar target pair \(f=\cos y, g=\sin 3y\) and put
\(c=gf'-fg'\), \(R=rf'+sg'\). Exact Fourier averaging gives
\[
 (\mathbb E c,\mathbb E cf',\mathbb E cg',
   \mathbb E c(f')^2,\mathbb E cf'g',\mathbb E c(g')^2)
 =(0,0,0,\tfrac12,0,0).
\]
For any scalar polynomial quartic \(F(x,y)\),
\(F_{xy}(B_1,B_2+R)\) is a polynomial of degree at most two in \(R\).
The curvature is its average against \(c\), so the displayed identities give
\[
 K_{F,B,\psi}(r,s)=\tfrac12\lambda(F,B)r^2,
\]
where \(\lambda(F,B)\) is the coefficient of \(y^2\) in
\(F_{xy}(B_1,y)\). Thus \(M_{20}=0\) is necessary and sufficient for
cancellation for this fixed profile, all scalar quartics, and all backgrounds.
It is genuinely weaker than six-moment cancellation; the convex witnesses
above give nonzero \(\lambda\).

An explicit closed current satisfying the weaker condition is the sum of:

* a counterclockwise rectangle \([-1/2,1/2]\times[0,1]\), and
* a clockwise rectangle \([-1,1]\times[0,1/8]\).

Joining their base points by connectors and their exact reversals makes one
closed path without changing its winding current. Its exact moment vector is
\[
 (M_{00},M_{10},M_{01},M_{20},M_{11},M_{02})
 =\left(\tfrac34,0,\tfrac{31}{64},0,0,\tfrac{85}{256}\right).
\]
So the fixed-profile curvature cancels while three other area moments are
nonzero. The profile-specific conclusion is limited to this scalar target and
this pair; it is not a universal replacement for the six conditions.
