The crucial finite step is to bound the geometry of every possible collected subsystem. Write \(x=a/Q\). Its constraint normals become integer vectors

A polynomial-bit ellipsoid procedure must therefore produce a finite infeasible transcript, rather than merely approach an infeasible limit. Its collected vertices satisfy

An explicit rational linear program then extracts at most \(\dim F(x)+1\) vertices and exact weights. No compactness limit, partition function, or oracle for optimizing over \(G\) remains.
The linear-programming and strong-oracle reductions here use established rational-polyhedral machinery, not a new optimization assumption. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-97881-4)
For general Boolean polytopes, bounded facet encodings permit exact optimization–separation reductions. Minimal-face recovery, line endpoints, and clipped-cell LPs reduce to polynomially many original optimization calls.
For matroids, weighted greedy optimization uses independence queries. A direct geometric implementation uses rank queries and exact submodular minimization of rank-minus-modular functions. Each rank evaluation costs at most \(n\) independence tests, and submodular minimization is itself a polynomial-query algorithm—not a free supplied oracle. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0004089)
Once the compact rational law is constructed, exact sampling has expected polynomial random-bit cost. It is not uniform sampling over all good bases, and non-dyadic probabilities do not admit a bounded worst-case number of fair bits.
The result extends beyond Boolean decisions and arbitrary matroid bases.
Let \(f:2^E\to\mathbb Z\) be submodular, with \(f(\varnothing)=0\), and define
\[ B(f)= \{z:z(S)\le f(S)\ \forall S,\ z(E)=f(E)\}. \]
Given rational \(x\in B(f)\), put \(a=\lfloor x\rfloor\). Its nearest integer cell is a translated matroid base polytope. An explicit residual rank function is
\[ \rho(U)= \min_{T\subseteq E} \left\{f(T)-a(T)+|U\setminus T|\right\}. \]
This reduction is established in the recent base-polyhedron literature; I reconstructed its rank-function and feasible-set identities rather than assuming a new matroid oracle. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.38228)
Applying the constructive theorem gives a compact law on integer points with
\[ \boxed{ \mathbb EZ=x,\qquad Z\in B(f),\qquad Z_j\in\{\lfloor x_j\rfloor,\lceil x_j\rceil\}, } \]
and
\[ \boxed{ \|A(Z-x)\|_\infty<2209 \quad\text{on every supported outcome}. } \]
The complexity depends polynomially on input and oracle-value bit lengths, not on the numerical magnitudes of capacities. Every inequality initially tight at \(x\) remains tight in every output.
This is a hard-discrepancy consequence. It does not assert Gaussian convex-order domination for the same constructed law.
There is also a polynomial-size reduction that replaces
\[ k\max_j\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2 \]
by a more local weighted edge budget.
Let rational \(\mu_j\ge\|A_{\cdot j}\|_2\), and suppose \(\Gamma>0\) bounds
\[ \max_{[u,v]\text{ edge of }F(x)} \sum_j\mu_j|u_j-v_j|. \]
If \(d\) coordinates vary, replace coordinate \(j\) by
\[ r_j=\max\{1,\lceil d\mu_j/\Gamma\rceil\} \]
tied copies. Every varying \(\mu_j\le\Gamma\), so the lift has at most \(d^2\) coordinates. Every lifted edge changes at most \(2d\) copies.
Assign each copy the column
\[ A'_{\cdot(j,\ell)} = \frac{d}{\Gamma r_j}A_{\cdot j}. \]
The columns are normalized, and optimization over the lift requires only summing the copied objective coefficients before one original optimization call.
Projecting the constructive law back yields
\[ \boxed{ \mathbb EZ=x,\qquad \|A(Z-x)\|_\infty<2322\Gamma, } \]
with at most \(d+1\) supported vertices.
The lift never scales with the inverse of the smallest positive weight. Thus exponentially different column scales do not produce an exponential replication cost.
An exposed-edge midpoint gives the matching-order lower bound \(\Gamma/2\) for the universal weighted radius. Computing the maximum weighted edge length from an arbitrary oracle is not claimed; an upper bound is supplied explicitly.

There is a genuine weaker-oracle obstruction. Hide a perfect matching on \(2r\) elements and allow only queries asking whether a subset selects exactly one element from each hidden pair. This is a partition-matroid base family with edge support two, and \(x=\mathbf1/2\) is known.
A fixed \(r\)-subset is feasible with probability
\[ p_r=\frac{2^r}{\binom{2r}{r}} =\Theta(\sqrt r\,2^{-r}) \]
over the hidden matching. Along an all-negative adaptive transcript, the queried subsets are fixed; a union bound gives success at most \((q+1)p_r\) after \(q\) queries, including a final unqueried output.
Thus Boolean feasible-vertex membership alone can require exponentially many queries even to find a vertex, with \(A=0\). This does not apply to independence or strong convex-optimization oracles. It explains why the access model must be stated, rather than treating short edges as an algorithmic interface.
