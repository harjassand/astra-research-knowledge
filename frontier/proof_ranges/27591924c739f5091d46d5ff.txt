## 5. Why finite-outcome exact reconstruction cannot be inferred

Here is an explicit finite-capacity, zero-broadcast-error family on l^2(N). Let

sigma=sum_{n>=1}2^(-n)|n><n|,
a_n=n^(-2),
rho_n=(1-a_n)sigma+a_n|n><n|,
E={sigma} union {rho_n:n>=1}.

Joint convexity of relative entropy gives

D(rho_n||sigma) <= a_n D(|n><n| || sigma)
                = (ln2)/n <= ln2.

Donald's identity then shows C(E)<=ln2. The family is commuting and is exactly broadcast by the normal countable-outcome channel that measures the basis and prepares |n,n><n,n|, so b_2(E)=0.

Nevertheless, no finite-outcome measure-and-prepare channel fixes every member. The linear range of such a channel lies in the finite-dimensional span of its finitely many prepared states. If it fixed sigma and every rho_n, that range would contain all

rho_n-sigma=a_n(|n><n|-sigma).

These vectors are linearly independent. Indeed, for any finite linear relation, matrix entries outside its finitely many selected basis indices force the coefficient sum to vanish; then each selected diagonal entry forces the corresponding individual coefficient to vanish. This contradicts finite-dimensional range.

Thus the theorem's infimum-only endpoint wording is essential for its finite-outcome assertion. This example does have an exact countable classical reconstruction, but a general exact-channel conclusion is not needed for the transfer proof.
