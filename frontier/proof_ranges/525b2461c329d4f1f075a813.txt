## 5. Exact obstruction: versatility does not provide balanced clocks

A subspace W is called versatile here when, for every nonzero scaling x and every coordinate j, some w in W satisfies w_i in {0,+x_i,-x_i} for all i and w_j=x_j. This is the established local movement property arising in the group-ring covering argument. [R1, R4]

Consider the three-dimensional subspace of F_5^5 with row basis

    B = [1 0 0 4 3]
        [0 1 0 1 1]
        [0 0 1 2 0].

Equivalently,

    W = {(a,b,c,-a+b+2c,3a+b): a,b,c in F_5}.

It is the kernel of

    A = [1 4 3 1 0]
        [2 4 0 0 1].

### 5.1 Analytic proof of versatility

The normal columns are v_1=(1,2), v_2=(4,4), v_3=(3,0), v_4=(1,0), and v_5=(0,1). Columns 3 and 4 are horizontal. Columns 1, 2 and 5 have three distinct nonhorizontal directions.

A basic fact used twice is that any two three-element subsets of F_5 sum to F_5: for any target t, the first set and the translate t minus the second set must intersect.

Fix arbitrary nonzero x_i. To prescribe w_j=x_j for a nonhorizontal column j in {1,2,5}, choose the other two nonhorizontal coefficients from their respective three-element symmetric sets to cancel the prescribed vertical component. Then choose the two horizontal coefficients to cancel the remaining horizontal component. Both choices use the three-set fact.

To prescribe a horizontal column, examine the three scaled nonzero vertical components of columns 1, 2 and 5. There are only two sign classes in F_5^*, so two of these components agree up to sign. Their columns can therefore produce 0 or either sign of a nonzero horizontal vector h, with the vertical components cancelled. The horizontal vector is nonzero because those columns have distinct directions. Combine {0,+h,-h} with the symmetric three-set from the other horizontal column to cancel the prescribed target. This proves versatility for every scaling and every coordinate.

### 5.2 Failure of unit balance

At x=(1,1,1,1,1), the nonzero legal support vectors are exactly

    (0,1,0,1,1),
    (0,1,1,1,1),
    (1,1,0,0,1).

They satisfy

    s_1+s_4=s_2=s_5.

Every vector in their cone satisfies the same equations. The all-ones vector does not. An exact rational separating vector is

    y=(-1,1,0,-1,0),

whose dot product with every legal support is zero but whose dot product with the all-ones vector is -1. This is an exact infeasibility certificate, not a conclusion based on a numerical LP status.

### 5.3 Failure of all common positive clocks

At x'=(2,2,2,2,1), the legal support vectors are exactly

    (1,0,0,1,1),
    (1,0,1,1,1),
    (1,1,1,0,1),
    (1,1,1,1,1).

Every vector in this cone satisfies r_1=r_5.

At x''=(2,2,2,2,2), scalar invariance gives the same support cone as at the all-ones scaling, so

    r_1+r_4=r_2=r_5.

Any vector in both cones must have r_4=0. Their intersection therefore contains no strictly positive vector. Unequal but state-independent coordinate speeds do not repair the problem.

The same two cone types occur within a single coset. Indeed,

    z'  = (2,3,3,2,1),
    z'' = (3,2,2,3,3),
    A z' = A z'' = (0,2) mod 5.

Their coordinatewise symmetric sets are precisely those of x' and x'', respectively. Consequently even assigning a different fixed clock vector to each coset cannot repair this example.

### 5.4 Scope of the counterexample

These five normal vectors do not form an irredundant affine cover. Five affine lines in F_5^2 have total incidence capacity 25, so covering all 25 points would require them to be pairwise disjoint, hence parallel. These normals have rank two and are not parallel.

The example refutes the claimed implication from versatility to clock balance. It does NOT refute the cover-specific balance gate, the target exponential covering theorem, or the possibility of another operation.
