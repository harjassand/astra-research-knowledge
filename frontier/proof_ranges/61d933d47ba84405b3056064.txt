## 10. Comparison with existing access models

The following primary sources were inspected. Their results remain credited; none is imported as an unproved premise of the construction.

1. **Zubida, Yitzhaki, Lindner and Bairey, Optimal short-time measurements for Hamiltonian learning**, arXiv:2108.08824. This studies Hamiltonian learning from short-time evolution with product preparation and local measurement. It prevents claiming that local product experiments, by themselves, are a new route to generator acquisition. Our lower bound instead restricts WHERE preparation and in-run control are available and asks for tolerant prediction of one state, not reconstruction.

2. **Bluhm et al., Certifying and learning local quantum Hamiltonians**, arXiv:2603.29809v1, version inspected displaying 24 August 2026. It gives O(1/epsilon) total-time tolerant certification in normalized Frobenius norm for fixed locality, using general time-evolution access and candidate-dependent processing. Ancilla-free variants are included; lack of an ancilla is not our claimed obstruction. A Frobenius-norm tolerance condition on H-K differs from tolerating the requested one-qubit trajectory. General/systemwide control and the coherent experiment duration also differ from Section 1. The paper explicitly discusses certification from local probes as a separate question.

3. **Shin and Tong, Hamiltonian Learning and Certification via Eigenphase Engineering**, arXiv:2609.26596, submitted 22 September 2026. It develops learning and tolerant certification with single-qubit operations and forward dynamics, without ancillas. Its addressability is not confined to the protected local ports here. Small simulation steps are not a bound on the total coherent duration of a fresh controlled experiment. Our result neither subsumes nor contradicts it.

4. **Chen, Cotler and Huang, Quantum Probe Tomography**, arXiv:2510.08499. This studies local quantum probes, including learning structured, symmetry-constrained many-body systems from tunable Gibbs-state and dynamics access, with identifiability and conditioning assumptions. Our inhomogeneous worst-case chain, fixed vacuum bath, and short per-run lifetime do not satisfy that entire interface. Our theorem does not rule out its structured or generic reconstruction guarantees.

5. **Liu et al., Learning Many-Body Hamiltonians Using a Local Probe**, arXiv:2610.02157v1, 1 October 2026. This is a particularly important recent comparison: one measurable/resettable qubit suffices under fast single-qubit controls THROUGHOUT the register, a connected reference backbone, and coherent information routing. A restricted global-control variant is also given. Thus a single readout site alone is not an information obstruction. The present lower bound restricts preparation and in-run control, not just readout, and imposes a per-fresh-experiment duration cap. Its constant-strength density interactions could serve as a reference backbone if appropriate systemwide rotations were supplied; those rotations are deliberately outside our access contract.

6. **Gong, Ma, Chen, Cotler and Huang, Out-of-control Hamiltonian Learning**, arXiv:2610.06709v1, 5 October 2026. It reconstructs generic two-local Hamiltonians using uniform single-qubit preparation/measurement settings across the whole system, and gives separate computational-basis results for specified X/Z interaction families. Restricted settings do not imply restricted spatial support: preparing and measuring the entire bath, and the stated genericity/structure guarantees, differ from the vacuum-bath local-port model here.

7. **Escudero Gutierrez, Lee and Zur, Hamiltonian locality testing and certification do not achieve the Heisenberg limit**, arXiv:2610.03205v2, 9 October 2026. Its abstract states quadratic precision lower bounds for forward-only testing in normalized Frobenius norm, without the present fixed-local-family promise. We inspected the abstract, not its complete proof, and do not import its adversary theorem or identify its problem with ours. In particular, it must not be conflated with the fixed-locality positive guarantee in item 2.

8. **Gao et al., Quantum Hamiltonian Certification**, arXiv:2505.13217. Certification being less expensive than full Hamiltonian learning is prior work, not a new principle claimed here.

The meaningful comparison is joint: WHERE one may act, HOW LONG a fresh system may retain coherent influence, WHAT state family can initialize the bath, WHICH distance is certified, and WHETHER approximately correct predictions must be accepted. Merely labelling both interfaces “local measurements” erases these differences.
