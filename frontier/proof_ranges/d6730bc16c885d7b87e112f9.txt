## 8. What the mathematics changes, and what it does not

The following consequences are proved rather than assumed: exact source-rank requirements; attainable rank-optimal realisations; optimal source-to-spectrum summability exponents; explicit source-generated approximation spaces; a dimension-free iteration error with the representation cost retained; and sharp precision floors. These make a source-based compression proposal falsifiable before attributing computational power to it.

The substantive representation change is in Section 6: a scalar-source obstruction is not treated as the end of the problem. A multi-channel source calculus is explicitly constructed and proved. It removes the huge initial source rank, but not the large number of generated modes. This distinction matters for any attempted transfer to scientific computation.

There is no proof here that a general interacting Hamiltonian, arbitrary log-concave measure, or computational search problem admits an economically acquired comparison of the required form. There is also no proof that its source-word dimensions, coefficient precision, or source-word Gram calculations remain economical under interactions. Supplying these as assumptions would not meet the original mission.

An exceptional application would require a target-specific construction of the operators and source, a proof of the comparison from the original input, and an end-to-end cost smaller than the strongest relevant specialised method. No such application has been established in this run. The current record is therefore a candidate mathematical mechanism with precise boundaries, not a declaration of a historic breakthrough.
