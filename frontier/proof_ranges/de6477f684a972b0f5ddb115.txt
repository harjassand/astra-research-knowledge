Assume G is countably infinite; q is a prime power; R=F_q[G]; a,b in R with ab=1 but ba!=1. Set r=1-ba, S=supp(r), m=|S|. Then r is a nonzero proper idempotent (r^2=r); r=1 would imply ba=0 and a=aba=0, contradicting ab=1. In particular m>=2: a nonzero one-term idempotent in a group algebra is 1.

asserts finite presentation, torsion-freeness, a finite 2-dimensional classifying complex, and a,b,c in F_2[G] with ab=1, ac=0, c!=0. ## 2. Exact equivariant Haar splitting: proved from ring identities

Put X=F_q^G with coordinatewise addition, product topology, uniform product measure, and the left G shift (h.x)(g)=x(h^{-1}g). For u=sum_s u_s s define (T_u x)(g)=sum_s u_s x(gs). Then T_u T_v=T_{uv}; T_u is a continuous, shift-equivariant compact-group homomorphism. Write A=T_a, B=T_b, C=T_r=I-BA. We have AB=I; AC=0; CB=0; C^2=C; ker(A)=im(C)=:K; and ker(C)=im(B)=:Y.

The mutually inverse maps
  Phi:X -> X x K, Phi(x)=(Ax,Cx);
  Psi:X x K -> X, Psi(y,z)=By+z
are continuous G-equivariant *group* isomorphisms. Proof: ABy=y, ACx=0, CBy=0, Cz=z (z in K), and BAx+Cx=x. Therefore Phi pushes product Haar on X exactly to Haar_X x Haar_K, so the two outputs are independent. B:X -> Y is similarly a topological and measure-preserving conjugacy.

K is nontrivial because r!=0 implies T_r!=0. It is infinite, with atomless Haar: choose infinitely many disjoint translates of finite support S and observe that the corresponding coordinates of Cx for iid uniform x are independent and uniform F_q. It is a factor of a mixing Bernoulli action, hence itself mixing. This does NOT identify K as Bernoulli, even measurably. For family-197's optional c with ac=0, im(T_c) is a nonzero subgroup of K.

Topological obstruction, not measurable: augmentation eps(a)eps(b)=1, hence eps(a)!=0 and A has no nonzero constant vector in its kernel. K has exactly one fixed point whereas any nontrivial full finite-alphabet shift has more than one. The full shifts X and X x X have q versus q^2 fixed points. Neither topological fact obstructs a measure conjugacy (these fixed sets are Haar-null).

Finite-quotient audit: for any finite quotient Q of G, the images of a,b satisfy ab=ba=1 in the finite-dimensional F_q[Q], so the image of r is zero. On every finite-index-normal periodic-point space, C=0; K has no nonzero finite-orbit points, and Y contains every finite-orbit point of X. By combining finitely many quotients, some distinct pair s,t in supp(r) must have identical images in every finite group quotient. This is a valid algebraic residual-kernel consequence, not a measurable Bernoulli invariant.

## 3. Rokhlin-entropy theorem and exact dependencies

The subgroup Y=im(B)=ker(C) satisfies, for every y in Y, sum_{s in S} r_s y(s)=0. Its identity-coordinate partition alpha is generating, finite entropy log q, and uniform. The S-pattern projection is contained in a codimension-at-least-one F_q linear subspace, hence H(alpha^S) <= (m-1)log q. We invoke Seward, "Weak containment and Rokhlin entropy", Theorem 1.5 (requires a free p.m.p. action, generating partition, finite translation window) to give h_G^Rok(Y) <= inf_{T finite}H(alpha^T)/|T| <=(1-1/m)log q.

Since B conjugates X to Y and X is free ergodic for infinite G, h_G^Rok(X) is strictly smaller than log q. Invoke Seward, "Krieger's finite generator theorem for actions of countable groups II", Theorem 1.10:
  h_G^Rok(F_q^G) = min(log q, h_sup^Rok(G)).
The strictness forces the **identity** h_sup^Rok(G)=h_G^Rok(F_q^G) and the bound

  0 <= h_sup^Rok(G) <= (1 - 1/m) log q < log q.

In particular this is <log 2 for the characteristic-two release. Seward II Theorem 1.3 alone gives a weaker explicit strict inequality, but Theorem 1.5 above gives the stated coefficient. This deduction does not establish h_sup(G)=0.

**Exact finite-window linear-rank refinement (derived).** For a finite T subset G put R_T=span_Fq{g:g in T} and d_T=dim_Fq((R r) intersect R_T), where Rr is the *left* ideal. Duality identifies Rr with the finite-coordinate linear relations annihilating Y=ker T_r, so its T-projection is a uniform linear subspace of dimension |T|-d_T. Consequently
  H(alpha^T)=(|T|-d_T)log q,
  h_sup^Rok(G) <= inf_{T finite} (1-d_T/|T|)log q.
For n>=1, a^n b^n=1, r_n=1-b^n a^n!=0, Y_n=im(T_{b^n}) is likewise Bernoulli and the same formula holds with Rr_n. Thus the infimum can be taken jointly over n and finite T. A computable lower bound on d_T is rank(span_Fq{g r_n:g supp(r_n) subset T}); no oracle for the infinite ideal is needed for that sufficient estimate.

**Direct finite-matrix form (same bound).** Right multiplication by b on R has kernel Rr: if u b=0 then u=(u b)a+u r=u r, and r b=0. Thus |T|-d_T = dim_Fq span{t b:t in T}. This dimension is computed by a finite coefficient matrix for the translates t b, given an explicit witness and decidable equality of its finitely many group words. In particular
  h_sup^Rok(G) <= inf_{T finite} [rank_Fq{t b:t in T}/|T|] log q,
and likewise for b^n. A sequence (n_j,T_j) with normalized rank tending to zero would force h_sup(G)=0; no such sequence is known. This is an exact reduction to finite linear algebra, not a finite-time algorithm unless the group operations/witness can be acquired.

To force h_sup(G)=0 by this method it would suffice to find windows and n such that d_{T,n}/|T| tends to 1. No such rank-density theorem has been proved, nor are actual words a,b available for a numerical test. Iterating X ~= X x K^n does not give that density limit, entropy additivity, or X ~= X x X.

## 4. A missed theorem removes the 'infinite-only POS' loophole

Seward II, Corollary **7.7**, states the equivalence for countably infinite G of:
(i) h_sup^Rok(G)>0;
(ii) existence of a free ergodic action with finite, strictly positive Rokhlin entropy;
(iii) existence of a free ergodic action with infinite Rokhlin entropy.
Therefore **no positive-Rokhlin-entropy free ergodic action of G exists iff h_sup^Rok(G)=0**, and this includes infinite values. The earlier RESEARCH.md warning that h_sup=0 might coexist with infinite positive Rokhlin actions was mathematically wrong. Seward's unresolved implication POS=>INF concerns h_sup=+infinity, not whether h_sup is positive.

If s=h_sup^Rok(G)>0 then choose infinitely many biased two-point base distributions with distinct Shannon entropies t in (0,min(s,log 2)); Theorem 1.10 gives Bernoulli Rokhlin entropy t. Thus *uncountably many* Bernoulli shifts are pairwise nonisomorphic. If s=0, Theorem 1.10 makes every finite-Shannon Bernoulli shift have zero Rokhlin entropy, while Theorem 1.12 together with Corollary 7.7 does the same for *infinite*-Shannon bases. Hence

  all nontrivial Bernoulli shifts have identical Rokhlin entropy
    <=> h_sup^Rok(G)=0
    <=> no free ergodic action of positive Rokhlin entropy.

The common value in that case is 0. The equivalence is about *entropy*, NOT about measurable isomorphism. Full Bernoulli-isomorphism collapse implies these equivalent conditions but is not known to follow from them.

## 5. Strongest extension: explicit zero-POS groups, including a finitely presented version

Let G be as in Section 1 (non-direct-finite group ring, therefore finite h_sup). Seward II Theorem **1.11**: if P is any countable group with arbitrarily large finite subgroups and h_sup^Rok(G)<infinity, then
   h_sup^Rok(G x P)=0.
Combine with Corollary 7.7:
   EVERY free ergodic p.m.p. action of Gamma=G x P has Rokhlin entropy 0
(in particular, none has entropy +infinity). Every nontrivial Bernoulli Gamma-shift has zero Rokhlin entropy for ANY finite or infinite Shannon base, by Theorems 1.10 and 1.12.
