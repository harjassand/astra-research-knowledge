Result: exact low-rank conservation repair without a geometric grid
I derived an operation that restores exact population balance directly inside a compressed tensor representation. For the specified source format, each routed correction has tensor bond dimension at most twice that of the source, independently of how far the relative coordinates extend.
A multiscale version also controls the correction’s energy for a nontrivial family of sources spread over arbitrarily large distances. An implemented solver then uses these representations to certify an actual current-projection problem on an infinite relative-coordinate lattice:
\[
\boxed{0.42510190863487\le E_b\le0.42510190864763.}
\]
The exact interval width is approximately \(1.27518\times10^{-11}\), obtained with 136 potential variables and 306 cycle variables. Here \(E_b\) is a Hodge-projection energy appearing in the interacting-current calculation—not the full transport coefficient \(D\).
This removes geometric enumeration from an essential operation: constructing, optimizing and verifying a conservation-compatible correction. It does not yet establish the requested \(C_{\mathrm{physical}}^N\operatorname{poly}(\epsilon^{-1})\) algorithm for the full response. The remaining issue is the rank required for accurate minimum-energy corrections and successive interacting fields, rather than the rank cost of enforcing conservation itself.
The derivations remain unreviewed. Historical novelty, independent correctness and a general computational advantage are not established.

1. Why the next representation must accommodate genuinely nonlocal corrections
There is now a concrete reason not to pursue ordinary exponential support locality.
On the three-particle exclusion gap graph, with coordinates \(a,b\ge0\), the polynomial
\[
h(a,b)=(a-b)(2a+b+1)(a+2b+1)
\]
is harmonic, including at the reflecting boundaries. For the local source
\[
b=\delta_{(0,1)}-\delta_{(1,0)},
\]
its harmonic moment is
\[
\langle b,h\rangle=-12.
\]
If the corresponding pressure gradient decayed exponentially, summation by parts against \(h\) would give \(-12=0\). Thus that projection cannot have an exponentially local tail.
This is not an unrelated pathological source. With \(H_{\mathrm{diag}}=U_z\sum_iZ_iZ_{i+1}\),
\[
A_{\mathrm{diag}}^2J=-8U_z^2(J-F_{\mathrm{mid}}),
\qquad
F_{\mathrm{mid}}=\sum_i Z_{i-1}j_iZ_{i+2},
\]
and the population divergence of \(F_{\mathrm{mid}}\) is precisely the displayed gap-lattice dipole, up to the known hopping factor. Astra’s N754 source identified this obstruction; I independently recalculated its harmonic and current identities in the new code. The convention matters: the previous centered-occupation parameter \(U=4\) corresponds to \(U_z=1\). 
The correct response is not to assume that the tail is negligible. It is to represent and certify the tail without listing its coordinates.
Fermionic rather than Pauli degree remains useful for this purpose, but it is not a new representation choice: FDAOE already preserves widely separated quadratic fermionic operators.
