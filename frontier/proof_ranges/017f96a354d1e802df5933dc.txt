## Result of the investigation

I obtained a **structural theorem about the strength of entropy-proof methods**:

> **Maximum-entropy marginal completion adds no new inequalities beyond the closed consequence cone of iterated conditional copying.**
>
> This includes the generalized construction that preserves several overlapping copies of an original entropy profile.

The qualification **closed consequence cone** is essential. The argument gives arbitrarily accurate finite-copy certificates and an exact mathematical implication after taking a limit. It does **not** establish that every maximum-entropy certificate has an exact, finite-copy certificate.

Below is the mathematical argument, including a quantitative version and the obstruction encountered when pursuing the stronger conclusion. I have not established the independently major breakthrough specified in the mission, and originality of the structural theorem remains unverified.

## 1. Target selection and prior mathematics

The initial screening considered three different mechanisms.

| Candidate | Decisive objective | Mechanism sought | Status check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Matrix discrepancy | For symmetric contractions \(A_1,\ldots,A_n\), construct signs with \(\|\sum_i\varepsilon_iA_i\|=O(\sqrt n)\). | A hereditary noncommutative small-ball principle without the usual logarithmic loss. | A September 2026 manuscript already claims this theorem and an almost-input-linear arithmetic algorithm. I did not treat that claim as independently verified or pursue the same conclusion as a fresh target. |
| Entropy along heat flow | Establish an all-orders sign or positivity principle governing entropy derivatives. | A representation making the required positivity structural rather than a succession of derivative calculations. | The Gaussian complete-monotonicity formulation has a published preprint counterexample, including failure within the log-concave setting. |
| Geometry of information | Characterize the closed entropy cone, or establish a genuinely stronger mechanism for deriving its inequalities. | A marginal-completion operation whose consequences exceed conditional copying. | This offered a precise comparison problem and a route toward a much stronger characterization theorem. |

The first two status corrections are supported by the respective research manuscripts. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15025?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For the third direction, Csirmaz’s *Exploring the entropic region* explicitly asks whether the maximum-entropy methods MAXE and GMAXE can prove inequalities unavailable to iterated copying. It also distinguishes that comparison from the larger question of characterizing the entropy region. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12439v2)

I consulted Astra at commit `368dffdc40a21594b8b4a75558c5fc8d7134e90c` and OpenAI’s mathematics repository at `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb`. No unreviewed theorem from either repository is a premise of the argument below.  

An initial linear-algebra construction was not a viable novelty claim: a public manuscript dated August 24, 2026 already claims same-field MAXE completion for linear polymatroids, together with scaled-limit and generalized versions. I therefore moved from linear realizations to the entire class of objects consistent with arbitrary finite copying. [GitHub](https://github.com/kasaikenta/linear-polymatroids-maxe?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The resulting question was:

\[
\boxed{\text{Can maximum-entropy gluing exclude a profile that survives every finite copy construction?}}
\]

The argument below answers **no**, for the marginal-preservation and separator-independence rules specified here.

## 2. Mathematical setting

Let \(E\) be a finite set. A polymatroid is a function

\[
h:2^E\longrightarrow \mathbb R_{\ge 0}
\]

with \(h(\varnothing)=0\), monotonicity, and submodularity:

\[
h(S)+h(T)\ge h(S\cup T)+h(S\cap T).
\]

These are the Shannon constraints on an abstract entropy profile. Write

\[
I_h(A;B\mid C)
=
h(A\cup C)+h(B\cup C)-h(C)-h(A\cup B\cup C).
\]

For disjoint \(A,B,C\), this quantity is nonnegative.

### Conditional copying

Let \(A,C\subseteq E\) be disjoint and let \(A'\) be a fresh, labelled copy of \(A\). A conditional-copy extension of \(h\) is a polymatroid \(\widetilde h\) on \(E\cup A'\) satisfying:

\[
\widetilde h|_E=h,
\]

\[
\widetilde h|_{A'\cup C}\cong h|_{A\cup C},
\]

and

\[
I_{\widetilde h}(A';E\setminus C\mid C)=0.
\]

The marginal equality means equality on **every subset** of the indicated coordinates, not merely equality of total rank.

For actual random variables, such an extension is obtained by sampling a fresh copy of \(A\) conditionally on \(C\), independently of the remaining variables given \(C\).

### The marginal-completion rule

Fix a family \(\mathcal F\subseteq 2^E\) of protected subsets.

A partition

\[
E=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}C\mathbin{\dot\cup}B
\]

*separates* \(\mathcal F\) when every protected set lies entirely in \(A\cup C\) or entirely in \(B\cup C\).

The MAXE rule asks for a new profile \(g\) such that

\[
g|_F=h|_F\qquad(F\in\mathcal F)
\]

and

\[
I_g(A;B\mid C)=0
\]

for every separating partition. This is the entropy-profile rule in Csirmaz’s formulation; it does not specify every feature of the underlying maximum-entropy distribution. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12439v2)

That distinction matters: **the theorem below concerns this precise rule, not every conceivable use of maximum-entropy optimization.**
