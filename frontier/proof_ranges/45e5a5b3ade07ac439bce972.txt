## 8. The specific algebraic feature that makes the closure planar

Theorem A exploits one indefinite quadratic equation, not a generic expectation that all null-Lagrangian constraints control higher moments. This distinction can be seen on an actual compatible 3x2 field.

Let

    G(x,y)=[ cos x       0          ]
           [ 0           cos y      ]
           [ cos(x+y)    cos(x+y)   ].                      (8.1)

It is the gradient of (sin x,sin y,sin(x+y)). In the three-dimensional matrix subspace

    X(s,t,r)=[[s,0],[0,t],[r,r]],

the three minors are st,sr,-tr. Thus the rank-one set is the union of the three coordinate axes. The cubic C(s,t,r)=str has zero second derivative in each of those rank-one directions, yet

    E C(cos x,cos y,cos(x+y))=1/4.

All three quadratic minor means are zero. Thus the cubic-closure argument genuinely fails for this multiple-quadric geometry. This is the familiar three-direction structure behind the higher-dimensional obstruction discussed in [3], not a newly claimed counterexample to Morrey's problem. It explains why the single-quadric integrability step cannot simply be transported to arbitrary matrix dimensions.
