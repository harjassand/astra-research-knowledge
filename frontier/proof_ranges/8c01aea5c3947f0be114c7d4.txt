### Lemma — Sharp KMS correlation bound

Let \\(\omega\_{AB}\\) be any bipartite density matrix with marginals \\(\rho_A,\rho_B\\). For Hermitian \\(X,Y\\),

\\[ \boxed{ \left|\operatorname{Tr}\omega\_{AB}(X\otimes Y)\right| \le \sqrt{ \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^{1/2}X\rho_A^{1/2}X) \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_B^{1/2}Y\rho_B^{1/2}Y) }. } \tag{1} \\]

This bound holds for arbitrary bipartite quantum states, including entangled states. The constant one is optimal.

Proof.

First suppose \\(\omega\_{AB}=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\\) is pure. Choose a Schmidt decomposition

\\[ |\psi\rangle=\sum_i\sqrt{p_i}|e_i\rangle|f_i\rangle. \\]

Writing \\(X\_{ij}=\langle e_i|X|e_j\rangle\\), and similarly for \\(Y\\), gives

\\[ \langle\psi|X\otimes Y|\psi\rangle = \sum\_{i,j}\sqrt{p_ip_j}X\_{ij}Y\_{ij}. \\]

Cauchy–Schwarz therefore yields (1), since

\\[ \sum\_{i,j}\sqrt{p_ip_j}|X\_{ij}|^2 = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^{1/2}X\rho_A^{1/2}X). \\]

For a mixed state, write

\\[ \omega\_{AB}=\sum_k q_k|\psi_k\rangle\langle\psi_k|. \\]

Apply the pure-state inequality to each summand, followed by scalar Cauchy–Schwarz.

The final step uses concavity of

\\[ \rho\longmapsto \operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{1/2}X\rho^{1/2}X), \\]

which follows from Elliott Lieb’s 1973 concavity theorem.&#x20;

It gives

\\[ \sum_kq_k \operatorname{Tr}(\rho\_{A,k}^{1/2}X\rho\_{A,k}^{1/2}X) \le \operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^{1/2}X\rho_A^{1/2}X), \\]

and likewise for subsystem \\(B\\). This proves (1). \\(\square\\)

The inequality is sharp. For

\\[ \sigma= \begin{pmatrix} p&0\\\0&1-p \end{pmatrix}, \quad |\Omega\_\sigma\rangle =\sqrt p\\,|00\rangle+\sqrt{1-p}\\,|11\rangle, \\]

and \\(X=Y=\sigma_x\\),

\\[ \langle\Omega\_\sigma| X\otimes X |\Omega\_\sigma\rangle = \\|X\\|\_\sigma^2 =2\sqrt{p(1-p)}. \\]

This also exposes why the ordinary variance estimate is inadequate: \\(\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma X^2)=1\\), whereas the KMS quantity becomes arbitrarily small as \\(p\\) approaches zero.

### Reconstructing the broadcast-tree proof

We now prove Theorem A without assuming Astra's conclusion.

A useful preliminary fact is that every unital, \\(\sigma\\)-preserving completely positive map is a contraction in the KMS norm:

\\[ \\|T(A)\\|\_\sigma\le\\|A\\|\_\sigma. \tag{2} \\]

To see this, apply Lemma (1) to the bipartite state obtained by applying \\(T\_\*\\) to one half of a canonical purification of \\(\sigma\\). The resulting correlation is

\\[ \langle X,T(Y)\rangle\_\sigma, \\]

up to transpose on the purifying register. Lemma (1) bounds it by \\(\\|X\\|\_\sigma\\|Y\\|\_\sigma\\). Taking the supremum over \\(X\\) proves (2).

Because \\(T=T^\sharp\\), it has a KMS-orthonormal Hermitian eigenbasis, with eigenvalues in \\([-1,1]\\).

Choose its \\(\sigma\\)-centered eigenobservables \\(A_1,\ldots,A_m\\) satisfying

\\[ T(A_i)=\lambda_iA_i,\qquad |\lambda_i|>\frac1{\sqrt2}. \\]
