## 11. Exact qualitative classification without a spectral gap

**Theorem D (finite orbit criterion).** For a finite orthogonal alphabet in any finite-dimensional real space, with all gate weights positive and the identity included,

\[
 \rho_m=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{the generated group has a nonzero unit-vector orbit of size at most }m.
 \tag{22}
\]

**Proof.** A finite orbit gives permutation transition kernels and a nonzero dual fixed vector, so rho_m=1.

Conversely, rho_m=1 and F_m(T)<=1 force F_m(T)=1 for every T. The compact family and compact unit norm sphere imply a maximizer for F_m(m). Realize its initial b by a unit-vector variable X_0 as in Section 6. Then R_m=1 implies M_m=1, while M_0=1 and the M_t are nonincreasing. Hence E||X_t-E[X_t|S_t]||^2=0 at every time: conditional on each active state, X_t is a single unit vector.

Let C_t be the set of these active directions. For every g, gC_{t-1} is contained in C_t because each active state's stochastic row has at least one positive entry and every g has positive weight. Identity gives C_{t-1} subset C_t. There are at most m directions and m inclusions starting from a nonempty set, so at least one inclusion is equality. At that step every g maps the same finite set into itself, hence bijectively onto itself. This contains a finite group orbit of size at most m. QED.

For a dense SO(3) action no such nonzero finite orbit exists, so rho_m<1 for every fixed m even without importing (SG). This compactness argument gives no rate as m increases. The quantitative theorem is substantially stronger.

## 12. Counterexamples and unsuccessful routes

1. **One-step retention:** F_m(1)=1 for m>=5. A per-step strict-contraction claim is false for the finite alphabet.
2. **Gate-by-gate transport shortcut:** after a stochastic update, the distribution of S_t generally depends on g_t. Treating each gate as transporting the same output marginal is invalid. The proof uses the unconditional convex-order coupling instead.
3. **Mutual information alone:** let Y be uniform on +/-0.02. Conditional on Y=0.02 take X=1 with probability .02 and X=0 otherwise; use the reflected rule for Y=-0.02. Then E[X|Y]=Y, so law(Y)<=cx law(X). As r goes to zero, I(Y;Y+rZ) approaches log2, whereas I(X;X+rZ) approaches H(.01,.98,.01), which is smaller. Mean-preserving compression can increase this information quantity. The variance correction in (8) matters.
4. **Raw KL profile is incomplete:** mu=(2/3)delta_{-1}+(1/3)delta_2 and its reflection have identical E_r for every r but are incomparable in convex order. Their call functions cross at thresholds -1 and 1. A complete likelihood-probe profile is developed separately; it is not an efficient new decision algorithm.
5. **Arbitrary Gaussian reference:** N(0,.25)<=cx N(0,.5), but relative entropy to N(0,1) decreases between them. Matching the smoothing and reference Gaussian is essential in Lemma 1.
6. **Finite groups or fixed directions:** a finite orbit allows indefinite lossless positive implementation by permutations, as Theorem D requires.
7. **No gap-free universal polynomial rate:** for planar rotation by 2pi times a Liouville number, regular m-gons give rational-rotation permutation models. If p/m approximates the angle divided by 2pi, the probability error through T is at most delta*pi*T*|theta/(2pi)-p/m|. For theta/(2pi)=sum_{k>=1}10^{-k!}, denominators m_k=10^{k!} have tail error at most 2/m_k^{k+1}. Taking T_k=m_k^k makes the error vanish while m_k grows more slowly than every fixed positive power of T_k. A polygon circumscribing the unit disk has vertex norm sec(pi/m), so its readout is valid for any fixed delta<1 and sufficiently large m. Density alone therefore cannot substitute for the stated quantitative mixing assumption across arbitrary compact actions.
8. **Signed or uncharged-memory models:** the theorem does not obstruct ordinary exact real rotation coordinates, negative interpolation weights, a stored input history, or an uncharged correlated random process. These violate the positive finite-state contract or change the counted resources.
