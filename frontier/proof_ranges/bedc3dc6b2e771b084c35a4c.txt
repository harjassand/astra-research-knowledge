# Uniform finite-population approximation for ordered couplings

Status: newly derived, complete internal proof; pending independent reconstruction. Previous source files and completed theorem statements remain untouched. The elementary integral-flow lemma is standard and proved below. No novelty or priority assertion is made for that lemma or the resulting theorem before literature review.

## Perfect success and scope

The theorem gives an explicit finite horizon that approximates **every** attainable output law of an arbitrary finite deterministic automaton (hence arbitrary finite, noncommutative semigroup), while keeping every input-coordinate marginal exactly prescribed. It computes the closure of the unbounded-horizon attainable set to arbitrary total-variation accuracy, and decides promised fixed-terminal-gap questions. It does not decide exact equality at a cutpoint or exact membership of that closure.

Let Q be a set of d states, A a finite alphabet, q0 an initial state, and f:Q x A -> Q a total deterministic transition function. Let mu be a probability law on A, restrict A to its positive support, and put alpha=min_a mu(a)>0. For n>=1, let R_n be the set of laws of the terminal state f(...f(q0,X1),...,Xn) over all joint laws of (X1,...,Xn) having Xi~mu for every i. Let R=union_{n>=1} R_n, and write TV(p,q)=1/2 sum_s |p(s)-q(s)|.

For a semigroup S and supported alphabet A subset S, take Q=S union {e}, with an adjoined identity e as initial state. Then f(s,a)=sa, f(e,a)=a, and R_n is exactly the product-law polytope. One can omit the new identity if S already has a suitable identity. No commutativity, regularity, mixing, or positivity of transition matrices is assumed.

## 1. Finite configuration graph

Fix an integer D>=1. A configuration is x in Z_{≥0}^Q with sum_s x_s=D. There are

    N(D,d)=binom(D+d-1,d-1)

configurations. Put a directed edge x -> y if there are integers k_{s,a}>=0 satisfying

    sum_a k_{s,a}=x_s,
    y_u=sum_{s,a:f(s,a)=u} k_{s,a},
    |sum_s k_{s,a} - D mu(a)| <= d   for every a.

Thus D particles take one transition each; each label's population count is permitted an additive discrepancy d from its desired count. Let G_D be the empirical terminal laws x/D of configurations reachable by a **positive-length** directed path from D delta_{q0}. If a reachable configuration differs from the initial configuration, it has a simple path of length <=N-1; if it equals the initial configuration and is positively reachable, it has a directed cycle through that configuration of length <=N. Consequently every member of G_D has a witness of length between 1 and N.

All budgets are tested as exact rational inequalities when mu is rational. The graph is finite and explicitly enumerable; it uses no oracle for word acquisition or long-horizon feasibility.

## 2. Whole-path integral rounding

**Lemma.** Let F be a nonnegative real flow on a finite directed graph with integer node balances. There is an integer flow Fhat with the same balances and

    floor(F_e) <= Fhat_e <= ceil(F_e)

for every edge e.

**Proof.** Consider the subgraph of noninteger edges, ignoring directions. Every vertex incident to this subgraph has degree at least two: a single noninteger incident edge, with all remaining incident flows integral, would give a noninteger node balance. Hence, if this subgraph is nonempty, it has an undirected cycle. Adjust the flows around this cycle, with signs determined by its directed incidences, preserving every node balance. Increase the adjustment until some cycle edge first reaches its floor or ceiling. No edge leaves its original floor/ceiling interval, and at least one further edge becomes integer. Repeat. At most the original number of noninteger edges iterations are required. The same argument covers parallel edges. Loops can be rounded independently. QED.

Take any joint input law giving p in R_n. It induces a path flow on the n-layer automaton: the edge (t-1,s)->(t,f(s,a)), with label a, carries

    F_{t,s,a}=D Pr(Q_{t-1}=s,X_t=a).

Use parallel edges when different labels give the same successor. Add a terminal edge from every (n,s) to a common sink, carrying D p(s). The source balance is D, the sink balance is -D, and all other balances are zero. Apply the lemma. Decompose the integer flow into D complete q0-rooted paths.

For every input time t and letter a,

    |sum_s Fhat_{t,s,a}-D mu(a)| <= d,

because there are d relevant edges and each individual rounding error is <1 unless it is zero. The paths therefore form a configuration-graph trajectory. The rounded terminal empirical law g has

    TV(g,p) <= d/(2D).

In particular,

    sup_{p in R} dist_TV(p,G_D) <= r_D := d/(2D).             (1)

This argument rounds the **entire** path flow at once. Its error bound has no factor n.

## 3. One filler repairs all input marginals

Take any configuration-graph trajectory of length n. Assign the k_{s,a} transitions to the D particles at each step; the uniform law on the resulting D input words has each coordinate marginal mu'_t with

    mu'_t(a) <= mu(a)+d/D.

Set

    beta_D := d/(D alpha+d),
    q_t(a) := [mu(a)-(1-beta_D)mu'_t(a)]/beta_D.

Each q_t(a) is nonnegative, because

    (1-beta_D)[mu(a)+d/D] <= mu(a)

is equivalent to alpha<=mu(a). Also sum_a q_t(a)=1. Take any filler law with these coordinate marginals, for instance the independent product of the q_t. Mix the D-word law with the filler law in weights 1-beta_D and beta_D. Every coordinate marginal is now **exactly mu**, simultaneously for all t. If the D-word terminal empirical law is g, the repaired terminal law p' satisfies

    TV(p',g) <= beta_D.

The filler may have any terminal state; no assumption about an accepting language, an absorbing output, or a repair word is used. Therefore

    sup_{g in G_D} dist_TV(g,R_{<=N(D,d)}) <= beta_D,         (2)

where R_{<=N}=union_{1<=n<=N} R_n.

## 4. Uniform horizon theorem

Combining (1), loop removal, and (2),

    R_{<=N(D,d)} subset R subset [R_{<=N(D,d)}]_{eta_D},
    eta_D := d/(2D)+d/(D alpha+d).                           (3)

Here [K]_eta is the closed TV eta-neighborhood of K. Since R_{<=N} is a finite union of compact polytopes, it is closed; (3) also holds with R replaced by its closure. Thus

    d_H(closure(R), R_{<=N(D,d)}) <= eta_D.                  (4)

For any epsilon>0, choose

    D=ceil(2d/(alpha epsilon)).

Then d/(2D)<=alpha epsilon/4<=epsilon/4 and beta_D<=epsilon/2, so eta_D<=3epsilon/4<=epsilon. This is an explicit finite cutoff in the original, exact-marginal model. It is a fixed-terminal-error theorem, not a per-step perturbation theorem.

More sharply, (1) and (2) give

    d_H(closure(R),G_D) <= max(r_D,beta_D).                  (5)

The witnesses supplied by the reverse approximation all have horizon <=N.

## 5. Computation and promise decisions

Assume the transition table and mu are rationally specified. Enumerate the finite configuration graph, perform positive-length reachability from the initial configuration, and retain G_D. For any rational target law nu, compute

    v_D=min_{g in G_D} TV(nu,g),
    v=inf_{p in R} TV(nu,p)=dist_TV(nu,closure(R)).

Equations (1)-(2) imply the certified interval

    max(0,v_D-r_D) <= v <= v_D+beta_D.                      (6)

Thus v is a uniformly computable real. In particular, given rational a>=0 and gamma>0, under the promise

    YES: v<=a;     NO: v>=a+gamma,

choose D so r_D+beta_D<gamma. The computed interval (6), or the threshold v_D<a+r_D plus a safe middle threshold, decides the promise. One simple rule is choose r_D+beta_D<gamma/2 and compare v_D with a+r_D+gamma/4, adjusting the constant to put it strictly between a+r_D and a+gamma-beta_D.

A cleaner fixed rule: choose max(r_D,beta_D)<gamma/3 and compare v_D with a+gamma/2. YES implies v_D<=a+r_D<a+gamma/3, while NO implies v_D>=a+gamma-beta_D>a+2gamma/3.

For any rational terminal payoff c, its unbounded supremum is similarly computable, with additive error at most osc(c) max(r_D,beta_D), where osc(c)=max c-min c. Maximize c dot g over G_D. This includes optimal acceptance probability of any regular language at an arbitrary finite horizon.

The graph has N(D,d) vertices. A direct enumeration of all nonnegative arrays k of total D has at most (D+1)^(d|A|) cases; compute the source and target configurations and test the label budgets. Thus, for fixed automaton size and alpha bounded below, this is polynomial in 1/epsilon. No favorable bit-complexity claim is made when alpha may be exponentially small in its rational encoding. There is no exact cutpoint-decision claim: a computable optimum can still equal the threshold, and exact finite-horizon existence differs from closure membership.

## 6. Why this does not contradict the earlier obstruction

The earlier binary infix construction has an exact nonperiodic horizon set and carries unary Markov equality/cutpoint hardness. The theorem here permits a small amount of terminal mass outside the target. That mass is also the filler reservoir that repairs all per-position marginals. Its ability to repair arbitrarily many positions at once is exactly why a robust output theorem can coexist with an exact equality obstruction.

The theorem concerns arbitrary correlated letters subject only to their one-coordinate marginals. Ordinary PFAs choose a global control word independently of transition coins. A general PFA reduction cannot be imported into this coupling model without enforcing that independence.
