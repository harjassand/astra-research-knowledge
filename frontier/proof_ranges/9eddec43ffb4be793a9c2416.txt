1. Kinematics and geometric transport

Let x_C<x_N, ell=x_N-x_C, c=(x_N+x_C)/2. Assume a fixed planar
shape family, translation-invariant total mechanical energy/loading,
equal-and-opposite net endpoint forces (-F,+F), and a diagonal law with
strictly positive mobilities m_C,m_N on each traversed branch. Then

  dot{x_C}=-m_C F, dot{x_N}=m_N F,
  dot{ell}=S F, dot{c}=(m_N-m_C)F/2,
  S=m_C+m_N, A=dc/dell=(m_N-m_C)/(2S).

For an expansion ell0 -> ell1 followed by contraction back to ell0,
without uncounted motion during switching, the exact result is

  d = int_ell0^ell1 [A_out(ell)-A_in(ell)] d ell.

For constant D outward and L inward mobilities, a=ell1-ell0>0,

  d = a(A_D-A_L)
    = a(m_N^D m_C^L-m_C^D m_N^L)/(S_D S_L).

Changing only the common multiplicative mobility clock gives d=0.
If either endpoint is permanently immobile, closure of ell forces
closure of the other coordinate and gives d=0 independently of the
diagonal-law derivation. Pinning an arm only during one stroke is a
different case and does not give this null theorem.

With nonnegative diagonal coefficients, |A|<=1/2 and |d|<=a. For
m_N=m>0 and m_C^D=p>q=m_C^L>0, the displacement fraction is

  |d|/a = m(p-q)/((m+p)(m+q))
         <= (sqrt(p/q)-1)/(sqrt(p/q)+1),

with equality exactly at m=sqrt(pq). This proves displacement per span
excursion, not useful velocity, energy efficiency, or device benefit.

If M is a symmetric positive definite endpoint mobility matrix rather
than diagonal, set e=(-1,1), h=(1,1)/2. Then

  A=(h^T M e)/(e^T M e), S=e^T M e>0.

The path integral and common-scaling null remain valid; |A|<=1/2 need
not hold. M=[[1,2],[2,5]] is positive definite and gives A=1. The
diagonal bound must not be promoted to a universal crystal-line bound.
