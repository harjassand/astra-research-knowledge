### Dimension-descent extension

Let the first local dimension be \(d\ge3\), and for \(p=2,\ldots,d-1\) choose vectors \(x_{pa},y_{pa}\in H\). Replace the two factors in (6) by

\[
e_a\otimes u+\varepsilon\sum_{p=2}^{d-1}e_p\otimes x_{pa},\qquad
e_a\otimes v+\varepsilon\sum_{p=2}^{d-1}e_p\otimes y_{pa}.
\]

Define \(X_a=\sum_{p=2}^{d-1}f_{p-2}\otimes x_{pa}\),
\(Y_a=\sum_{p=2}^{d-1}f_{p-2}\otimes y_{pa}\), and
\(D=\sum_aX_aY_a^*\) on \(\mathbb C^{d-2}\otimes H\). Then

\[
q_{(d,\boldsymbol d)}(C_\varepsilon)
=\varepsilon^2\sum_{p,a}\|R_{\boldsymbol d}^{1/2}(x_{pa}\otimes v-u\otimes y_{pa})\|^2
+\varepsilon^4q_{(d-2,\boldsymbol d)}(D).
\tag{9}
\]

To prove this, apply (8). The lower-right block contributes its own first-factor quadratic form; the cross terms with \(2uv^*\) supply the displayed squares through (4). When \(d=3\), the remaining one-dimensional first factor multiplies \(q_{\boldsymbol d}\) by \(1/2\), recovering (7).
