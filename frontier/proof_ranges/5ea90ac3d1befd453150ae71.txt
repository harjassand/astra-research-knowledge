## 7. Variable phase: the exact missing signed term

The successful operation fixes an affine phase. It is important to show, rather than conceal, what changes for an arbitrary phase.

Let theta(x) be smooth and let k(x)=grad theta. Keep phi=rV(theta), W=V', V''=-aV, and now set P=rW tensor k, Q=V tensor grad r. Define the same primitive as in (2.5), but with k as an additional variable. Differentiating it gives the extra term

    E_F(x)=sum_i integral_0^r s
      D²F(B+sW tensor k)[W tensor partial_i k,V tensor e_i] ds. (7.1)

The exact identity becomes

    integral [F(B+Dphi)-F(B)]
      = integral R_F(B+P,Q)
        + integral a(theta) integral_0^r s
            D²F(B+sW tensor k)[V tensor k,V tensor k] ds
        - integral E_F.                                    (7.2)

The first two terms remain nonnegative. The last involves two different rank-one directions in a mixed Hessian pairing; rank-one convexity does not sign it. It vanishes for affine theta, yielding Theorem 2.1.

At B=0 in the planar left-invariant cone, the expression is particularly explicit. Write the orientation-odd part as

    q(Lu+Mv+Nw),     M_2(k)=Lk_1²+Mk_2²+Nk_1k_2,
    Xi(k)=M_2(k)(k_2,-k_1).

All reflection-even terms have zero first derivative in the orthogonal output direction at W tensor k. Direct differentiation gives

    E_F = (r^4/4) div Xi(grad theta).                        (7.3)

Thus phase curvature and amplitude are correlated in precisely the signed term that the affine-phase identity avoids.

The sign failure occurs even for an explicitly convex invariant quartic. Take

    F_c(A)=(u+v-2q)^2=|x_complex+i y_complex|^4,
    theta(x,y)=x+sin y,
    r(y)^4=1-(1/2)sin(2y).

This is a smooth periodic potential, since V is 2pi-periodic in theta. Here L=M=-4,N=0, and exact normalized averaging gives

    E E_(F_c)=1/4>0.                                       (7.4)

Changing the sign of the sine in r^4 changes the sign of this mean. Thus discarding the signed term, or asserting it nonpositive, is invalid even for convex F. The total Jensen gap is still nonnegative in this example; (7.4) is not a counterexample to quasiconvexity.

A nonlinear phase-straightening diffeomorphism would introduce variable input coefficients and their correlation with the amplitude. As with the prior shear conjugacy, no unrestricted reduction follows merely from changing coordinates. A global theorem would need to control (7.1) together with the positive terms, not rename it or average its coefficients independently.
