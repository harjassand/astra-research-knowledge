\section{Finite-population compilation of arbitrarily long constrained processes}\label{sec:population}

The most consequential surviving route changes the representation of an ordered coupling. A population of finitely many deterministic paths replaces the whole joint law; one correlated filler repairs every input marginal simultaneously. Unlike a local approximation of transition probabilities, its error does not accumulate with the horizon. This yields an executable terminal-optimization algorithm for binary-encoded horizons, together with succinct, exactly marginal-correct coupling witnesses. The graph can be very large; the claim concerns its explicitly accounted cost.

\subsection{The exact interface and time-uniform theorem}

Let $Q$ be a set of $d$ states, $A$ a finite alphabet, $q_0\in Q$, and $f:Q\times A\to Q$ a supplied total deterministic transition table. Let $\mu$ be a supplied rational input law. Delete its zero-mass letters, write $r=|\operatorname{supp}\mu|$, and put $\alpha=\min_{\mu(a)>0}\mu(a)$. Let $\mathcal R_n$ be the set of terminal-state laws obtained from \emph{arbitrary} joint laws on $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ with $X_t\sim\mu$ for every $t$. Correlations and dependence on the past automaton state are allowed. No independence, blind-control, or exact accepting-support restriction is imposed.

For an integer population $P\ge1$, define
\[
H=\binom{P+d-1}{d-1},\qquad a_P=\frac d{2P},\qquad
b_P=\frac d{P\alpha+d}.
\]
The configuration graph has vertices $x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^Q$ with $\sum_sx_s=P$. An allocation $z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{Q\times A}$ of total $P$ gives an edge $x\to y$ when
\[
x_s=\sum_a z_{sa},\quad y_u=\sum_{s,a:f(s,a)=u}z_{sa},\quad
\left|\sum_s z_{sa}-P\mu(a)\right|\le d\quad(a\in A).
\tag{C1}
\]
Let $\mathcal G_{P,n}$ be the histograms divided by $P$ reachable from $P\delta_{q_0}$ in \emph{exactly} $n$ graph steps, and $\mathcal G_P=\bigcup_{n\ge1}\mathcal G_{P,n}$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact marginals, uniformly approximable terminal laws]\label{thm:population}
For every $n\ge1$,
\[
\sup_{p\in\mathcal R_n}\operatorname{dist}_{\rm TV}(p,\mathcal G_{P,n})\le a_P,
\qquad
\sup_{g\in\mathcal G_{P,n}}\operatorname{dist}_{\rm TV}(g,\mathcal R_n)\le b_P.
\tag{C2}
\]
Consequently, writing $\mathcal R=\bigcup_{n\ge1}\mathcal R_n$,
\[
d_H\left(\overline{\mathcal R},\bigcup_{1\le n\le H}\mathcal R_n\right)
\le a_P+b_P.
\tag{C3}
\]
The bounds apply to every finite semigroup, including noncommutative ones: adjoin a formal initial identity and use $f(s,a)=sa$. They require neither mixing nor an algebraic structural hypothesis.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First recall, and prove, integral-flow rounding. A finite nonnegative real flow with integer node balances can be rounded on every edge into its original floor/ceiling interval while preserving those balances. In the undirected multigraph of noninteger edges, an incident vertex cannot have degree one, since its balance is integer. Adjust around a cycle with incidence signs until an edge becomes integer, staying inside all original intervals; repeat. Parallel edges cause no problem and loops round independently.

For a coupling giving $p\in\mathcal R_n$, put flow $P\Pr(Q_{t-1}=s,X_t=a)$ on each edge of the $n$-layer labelled automaton. Keep parallel labels distinct. Add terminal-to-sink edges of flow $Pp(s)$. Balances are $P,-P$, and zero. Round the \emph{whole} flow and decompose it into $P$ complete words. At each time and letter, its $d$ rounded edges have total error at most $d$, giving (C1). Each terminal sink edge has error less than one, so the empirical terminal law $g$ has ${\rm TV}(g,p)\le d/(2P)$. There is no sum of errors over $n$.

Conversely, a graph path gives $P$ full words. Their uniform law has coordinate marginals $\mu'_t(a)=c_t(a)/P$, where $c_t(a)=\sum_s z_{t,sa}\le P\mu(a)+d$. Set $\beta=b_P$ and
\[
q_t(a)=\frac{\mu(a)-(1-\beta)\mu'_t(a)}\beta
=\frac{(P\alpha+d)\mu(a)-\alpha c_t(a)}d.
\tag{C4}
\]
These are probability laws: the numerator is at least $d(\mu(a)-\alpha)\ge0$, and their sums are $d$. Mix the population law with \emph{any} law having marginals $q_t$, in weights $1-\beta,\beta$. Every input marginal becomes exactly $\mu$, and the terminal law changes by at most $\beta$. This proves (C2) at the same $n$.

Every positively reachable histogram has a positive witness of length at most $H$: use a simple path if it differs from the initial vertex, and a shortest nonempty return cycle otherwise. Delete loops in the rounded histogram path and then apply (C4) to that shorter path. This proves (C3); the finite union on its right is compact, so the assertion also holds for the closure.
\end{proof}

Choosing $P=\lceil2d/(\alpha\varepsilon)\rceil$ gives $a_P+b_P\le3\varepsilon/4$. The proof's substantive step is two-sided: rounding an entire path controls error independently of time, and \emph{every} relaxed graph walk can be repaired into an actual exact-marginal coupling. A generic grid approximation of a nonexpansive transition lacks the second property and can accumulate spurious motion indefinitely.

\subsection{A computational consequence with supplied-input costs}

For a supplied rational target law $\nu$, put
\[
v_n=\min_{p\in\mathcal R_n}{\rm TV}(\nu,p),\qquad
v_{P,n}=\min_{g\in\mathcal G_{P,n}}{\rm TV}(\nu,g).
\]
Then
\[
\max\{0,v_{P,n}-a_P\}\le v_n\le\min\{1,v_{P,n}+b_P\}.
\tag{C5}
\]
The same interval computes $\inf_{n\ge1}v_n$ using $\mathcal G_P$. In particular, arbitrary-horizon optimal terminal error is a computable real, and a fixed positive-gap promise is decidable. This does not decide equality of that real to zero, exact finite-horizon attainment, or the earlier Skolem cutpoint interface.

Acquire the graph by enumerating \emph{once} all
\[
B_P=\binom{P+dr-1}{dr-1}
\]
weak compositions of $P$ into the allocation cells; each determines its source and target uniquely. If $b$ is the rational input bit length, graph acquisition takes $B_P\operatorname{poly}(d,r,b,\log P)$ bit operations. Retain one allocation for each edge. Boolean matrix powering then computes $\mathcal G_{P,n}$ and midpoint witnesses in $O(H^3\log(n+1))$ Boolean operations, with index arithmetic charged separately. Distances to $\nu$ use rational numbers of $O(b+\log P+\log d)$ bits. Positive-length reachability computes the arbitrary-horizon version, distinguishing a zero-step initial vertex from a genuine return.

There is also an ordinary-input complexity consequence: the promised decision $v_n\le a$ versus $v_n\ge a+\gamma$, and its arbitrary-positive-horizon version, belong to PSPACE. Choose $P=\lceil6d/(\alpha\gamma)\rceil$, so $\max\{a_P,b_P\}<\gamma/3$, and test whether a reachable histogram has distance below $a+\gamma/2$. The bit length of $P$ is polynomial in the supplied rational input. A histogram and one allocation occupy $O(dr\log(P+1))$ bits; quota and distance tests use polynomial-bit integers. Exact-$n$ reachability admits a direct polynomial-space recursion: enumerate a midpoint histogram, recurse at lengths $\lfloor n/2\rfloor,\lceil n/2\rceil$, and enumerate allocations at the one-edge base case. The stack has $O(\log n)$ levels. For arbitrary positive horizons enumerate lengths $1,\ldots,H$, whose counters have polynomial bit length. This proves the upper bound without storing the exponential graph. It asserts neither PSPACE hardness nor polynomial-space storage of the compiled sampling tables.

Thus for fixed compiled accuracy the supplied-horizon dependence is polynomial in $\log n$. For fixed $d,r$ and $\alpha$ bounded below, the entire algorithm is polynomial in $1/\varepsilon$. It is not polynomial jointly in the automaton size and $\log(1/\varepsilon)$; a tiny rational $\alpha$ can itself cause exponential dependence on input bit length. No physical acquisition oracle or compressed transition table is assumed. Exact acceptance of every word would invalidate the unrestricted filler and is outside the theorem.

\subsection{A compressed joint law, exact terminal evaluation, and randomness}

The witness is operational, rather than merely an existence certificate. Order each histogram's $P$ slots by state and within-state rank. An allocation partitions each source block by letter and bijectively sends its particles into destination blocks, ordered by source, letter, and rank. Thus an edge $e$ has a $P$-slot permutation $\pi_e$ and letter vector $\ell_e$. Boolean-powering midpoint witnesses yield a concatenation DAG with $O(H^2\log(n+1))$ nodes and logarithmic depth. Cache each node's permutation using $\pi_{\rm parent}=\pi_{\rm right}\circ\pi_{\rm left}$, costing $O(PH^2\log(n+1))$ slot operations and the corresponding index storage. A single uniformly sampled initial slot now specifies one complete population word. To query its $t$th letter, descend the DAG and apply the left permutation whenever skipping a left subtree. The terminal slot also gives its exact terminal state.

There is a stronger filler than independent coordinate coins. Write $\mu(a)=u_a/M$ with integers $\sum_a u_a=M$, $u_* =\min_a u_a$, and $D_0=dM^2$. On any allocation edge,
\[
q_e(a)=\frac{w_e(a)}{D_0},\qquad
w_e(a)=P u_*u_a+dM u_a-Mu_*c_e(a).
\tag{C6}
\]
Sample \emph{one} uniform integer $J\in\{0,\ldots,D_0-1\}$ and use the inverse CDF of $q_e$ with the same $J$ at every position. Each marginal is $q_e$ exactly; all correlations are allowed. The whole filler word is deterministic given $J$, so adaptive and repeated coordinate queries need no independent coins or cache.

For fixed $J$, each leaf emits a fixed letter $a_e(J)$. Compose the $d$-state maps $T_e(s)=f(s,a_e(J))$ on the same DAG. This computes the exact filler terminal state in $O(dH^2\log(n+1))$ map-entry operations. More strongly, all leaf CDFs have at most $(r-1)E+2$ combined integer breakpoints, where $E\le H^2$ is the number of distinct retained leaf allocations. Between adjacent breakpoints every leaf's letter is constant. Evaluate the DAG once per interval and sum its interval weight. This computes the \emph{entire repaired terminal law exactly}, in
\[
O(rEdH^2\log(n+1))
\]
map-entry operations, plus sorting and rational arithmetic. There is no enumeration of the possibly enormous $D_0$ seeds. If $m_s$ is the total integer filler-interval length ending in $s$, the repaired law is
\[
p'(s)=\frac{M u_*x_s+m_s}{M(Pu_*+dM)}.
\tag{C7}
\]
Its bit length remains $O(b+\log P+\log d)$, independent of $n$. The joint law has at most $P+(r-1)E+1$ succinctly represented deterministic atoms. Equivalently, it is a uniform population of
\[
R=M(Pu_*+dM)
\]
full words: give each population word $Mu_*$ identical copies and each filler interval its integer-width number of copies. Every coordinate has exactly $R\mu(a)$ letters $a$. Thus bounded-denominator exact-quota couplings approximate every attainable terminal law uniformly in horizon. With $P=\lceil2dM/(u_*\varepsilon)\rceil$,
\[
R\le dM^2(2/\varepsilon+1)+Mu_*.
\]
One uniform integer in $\{0,\ldots,R-1\}$ therefore supplies all of its randomness. The denominator bound is polynomial in $M$, rather than its bit length; the smaller relaxed graph remains the preferable algorithm.

After compilation, each requested coordinate costs $O(\log(n+1))$ table lookups and index/quantile comparisons. Exact rational sampling of the mixture coin and branch seed uses $O(b+\log P+\log d)$ expected fair random bits, independently of $n$; integer rejection has unbounded worst-case duration. Printing all symbols still costs $\Omega(n)$ time. Certificate verification checks leaf allocations, rational CDFs, concatenation identities, and terminal evaluations in polynomial time in the explicit certificate size. The lower optimality bound additionally uses the universal flow proof, rather than finite executions alone. Full proof and resource details are in \texttt{proofs/finite\_population\_compiler.txt}; the executable solver and receipts are indexed in \texttt{proof\_index.json}.

\subsection{Exact finite-order statistics and local support constraints}\label{sec:blocks}

The primitive reaches a stronger information interface. Let $\kappa(h,a)=u_{ha}/M$ be a supplied rational law on $A^k$, $k\ge2$, whose $(k-1)$-prefix and suffix laws agree, denoted by $\lambda(h)=v_h/M$. Require every consecutive $k$-window of a word of length $n\ge k$ to have law $\kappa$. No full conditional Markov-kernel promise is made. For example, the uniform law on $000,010,101,111$ has uniform adjacent-pair marginals but $X_3=X_1$; a DFA remembering $X_1$ sees a deterministic third bit. Zero-probability blocks are forbidden, so exact local support restrictions survive.

Augment a DFA state $s$ by its last $k-1$ letters $h$, deleting histories with $\lambda(h)=0$. Initially the prefix $h$ has law $\lambda$ and the DFA state is $f(q_0,h)$. A population vertex is a histogram on these augmented states. Admit initial histograms supported on those prefix states with $|x_h-P\lambda(h)|\le1$. For each graph edge require
\[
\left|\sum_s z_{s,h,a}-P\kappa(h,a)\right|\le d,
\]
where $d$ is the \emph{original} DFA state count, and forbid zero-probability blocks. Whole-path rounding applies with source-to-prefix edges and, at the terminal layer, augmented states feeding the $d$ original terminal-state nodes before the sink. It gives the same terminal error $d/(2P)$ and per-block discrepancy $d$, independently of $n$.

Put $u_* =\min_{u_{ha}>0}u_{ha}$, $L_0=Pu_*+dM$, $F_0=dM^2$, and $\beta=dM/L_0$. At vertex $x$ and edge $e:x\to y$ define integer filler-history and filler-block counts
\[
R_x(h)=L_0v_h-Mu_*\sum_sx_{s,h},\qquad
C_e(h,a)=L_0u_{ha}-Mu_*\sum_s z_{s,h,a}.
\tag{C8}
\]
They are nonnegative: block discrepancies and $u_{ha}\ge u_*$ prove this for $C_e$; the initial prefix discrepancy and $v_h\ge u_*$ prove it initially for $R_x$. On any subsequent reachable vertex, row/column consistency propagates it. Indeed stationarity of $\kappa$ gives
\[
\sum_a C_e(h,a)=R_x(h),\qquad
\sum_{h,a:\,\operatorname{shift}(h,a)=h'}C_e(h,a)=R_y(h'),\qquad
\sum_h R_x(h)=F_0.
\tag{C9}
\]
Thus canonical $F_0$ history slots can be partitioned by $C_e(h,a)$ and bijectively matched to the next history slots. Starting with the residual prefix counts, these integral flows glue $F_0$ deterministic filler words with the required residual block marginals. Mixing them with the $P$ population words in weights $\beta,1-\beta$ repairs \emph{every} $k$-window exactly. Equivalently, use one uniform seed on $M L_0$ repeated words: each original population word has $Mu_*$ copies and each filler word one. Exact forbidden-block support is retained.

At a leaf, the filler induces $(i,s)\mapsto(\pi_e(i),f(s,a_e(i)))$ on $F_0$ slots times the original DFA states. These maps compose on the witness DAG. Its root, with initial state determined by the selected prefix, supplies the exact terminal state and all coordinate queries. Preprocessing costs $O(F_0dW)$ map-entry operations for $W$ DAG nodes, and exact terminal counting costs the same order. The factor $F_0=dM^2$ is charged: this version can be exponential in rational bit length, even when positive atoms are bounded below. It inherits polynomial dependence on $\log n$ after its compiled tables are acquired, rather than the one-coordinate compiler's stronger denominator-bit dependence. Writing $b_h=|\operatorname{supp}\lambda|$ and $H_k=\binom{P+db_h-1}{db_h-1}$, the universal approximation/cutoff argument uses the enlarged histogram graph and its admissible initial set, with total-word cutoff $k-1+H_k$ and terminal error at most $d/(2P)+d/(P\min_{\kappa>0}\kappa+d)$. Complete constructions are in \texttt{proofs/block\_marginal\_compiler.txt} and its independent companion; no implementation of this extension is asserted by the one-coordinate diagnostic script.

\subsection{Robust horizon regularity and the exact boundary}

The reachable subsets obey $V_{n+1}=\operatorname{Neighbors}(V_n)$, a deterministic map on at most $2^H$ subsets. Consequently $\mathcal G_{P,n}$ is eventually periodic, with computable preperiod plus period at most $2^H$. If its period is $L$, (C2) gives
\[
d_H(\mathcal R_{n+L},\mathcal R_n)\le2\max\{a_P,b_P\}
\]
in that tail. Thus the entire exact-marginal terminal-law sequence is effectively approximately periodic to every fixed accuracy. If, for every positive horizon, a supplied predicate has the promise $v_n\le a$ or $v_n\ge a+\gamma$, choose $\max\{a_P,b_P\}<\gamma/3$. Testing $v_{P,n}<a+\gamma/2$ yields an \emph{exactly} ultimately periodic yes-horizon language. Without the uniform gap promise this conclusion is false, as (S1) already demonstrates. The nonperiodic exact mechanism survives only through arbitrarily small distances from the decision boundary.

\paragraph{Finite-catalogue control words.}
The same operation extends to a finite supplied catalogue of rational marginal laws, and even control-dependent deterministic transition tables. At time $t$, a control symbol $b_t$ specifies its required marginal $\mu^{(b_t)}$. Use a colored graph with (C1) for that marginal, forbid zero-support labels on its edges, and let $\alpha$ be the smallest positive atom in the entire catalogue. Whole-path rounding still applies at each fixed control word. One filler of weight $d/(P\alpha+d)$ repairs every position, including varying supports. Thus a single finite colored automaton approximates all control-word terminal-law sets uniformly. Under a uniform positive-gap promise over all nonempty control words, the optimal-terminal predicate is a computably regular language: determinize reachable histogram subsets and apply (C5). This is an optimized arbitrary-correlation semantics. The established isolated-cutpoint regularity theorem for ordinary probabilistic automata, credited to \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(63)90290-0}{Rabin (1963)}, uses a different semantics; no broad regularity theorem is claimed as historically new. Fixed stochastic kernels and blind independence would require additional mathematics.

\paragraph{A long horizon can be intrinsically necessary.}
A fair-binary DFA can use a depth-$h$ binary tree to choose uniformly among $k=2^h$ disjoint cycles of distinct prime lengths $p_1<\cdots<p_k$; both letters then advance the cycle. Let $\nu$ assign mass $1/k$ to the predecessor of each entry point. At $n\ge h$, a target component is available only if $p_i\mid(n-h+1)$. Thus any attainable law has TV error at least the fraction of primes not dividing $n-h+1$. Error at most $1/4$ requires
\[
n\ge h-1+\prod_{i=1}^{\lceil3k/4\rceil}p_i.
\]
Independent fair prefix bits attain $\nu$ at a common multiple of all cycle lengths. This is a rigorous phase-alignment obstruction to a uniformly short materialized witness; standard prime bounds give stretched-exponential necessary horizons in the DFA state count. The explicit product bound needs no prime-asymptotic import. It explains the role of compressed binary-horizon computation rather than suggesting a polynomial horizon bound.

The nearest inspected primary comparison, \href{https://people.kth.se/~kallej/papers/DistributionaL_TAC023_Gao.pdf}{Gao et al., Distributional Reachability for Markov Decision Processes: Theory and Applications, IEEE TAC 69(7), 2024 (online 2023)}, uses occupation-measure polytopes and one-step sample/projection approximations; its invariant-limit analysis starts from the whole simplex. The inspected statements do not supply the time-uniform fixed-initial, exact-quota compiler above. Conversely, the unrestricted filler does not preserve conditional stochastic kernels or blind-control restrictions, so this theorem is not a solution of general MDP or probabilistic-automaton reachability. The interface comparison is recorded in the companion note. It establishes a difference in scope from checked statements, not historical priority.
