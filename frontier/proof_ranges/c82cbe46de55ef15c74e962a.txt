## CO2 thermochemical scale and conventional comparator

The activation source uses E = 6.14 eV as a high-energy rotational-state example, not as an ensemble mean or evidence of chemical yield. The primary optical-centrifuge study reports this energy only at the high-J tail at 380 Torr. It reports that a centrifugal barrier prevents dissociation at those rotational energies; its simulations find stronger rotation-to-vibration transfer for moderate rotational excitation, while states above J = 200 are not important contributors. The same experiment therefore does not provide evidence for a state that both reacts usefully and unloads efficiently.

From NIST-JANAF formation data at 298.15 K and 0.1 MPa for

    CO2(g,1 bar) -> CO(g,1 bar) + 0.5 O2(g,1 bar),

ΔG° = 257.226 kJ/mol CO and ΔH° = 282.995 kJ/mol CO. The derived reaction entropy is ΔS = 86.4315 J/(mol K), so the reversible isothermal heat duty is TΔS = 25.770 kJ/mol. It is recorded separately from work. Reversibly compressing pure CO from 1 bar to 14 bar absolute (13 barg) adds RT ln(14) = 6.542 kJ/mol. Holding the oxygen activity at its 1-bar standard state, the output free-energy floor is therefore

    ΔGchem = 263.768 kJ/mol = 2.73376 eV/CO = 2.616 kWh/kg CO.

For the idealized E = 6.14 eV input, the pure product-state model has RP = 3.40624 eV/CO. This recovery returns the excess above product chemical and pressure free energy; it does not make CO2 conversion free.

The matched system comparator retrieved is the ISPT/TNO/Air Liquide SOEC use-case model. It targets CO at 13 barg and at least 96 mol% purity, with a 95/5 CO2/CO stack feed, 750°C operation, 50% single-pass conversion, and enriched air at 30% O2. It reports 3.2 kWh/kg CO electricity: 2.7 stack, 0.2 feed heating, 0.1 air heating, and 0.2 compression. The report assumes excess steam and does not charge steam generation. This is a modeled system figure, not a demonstrated plant result.

The ideal reversible work floor leaves a conditional margin of 0.584 kWh/kg (0.61058 eV/CO) below the modeled 3.2 kWh/kg comparator. Every unmeasured loss, control, source conversion, conditioning, separation, and output cost must fit within this one margin. This is not an apples-to-apples process advantage: the thermodynamic screen assumes pure CO at 14 bar, slightly stricter than the SOEC report's minimum 96 mol% purity; activation-route heat demand and temperature, oxygen-side handling, CO separation, cooling, laser wall-plug efficiency, and throughput have not been acquired. As a deliberately conservative no-heat-credit screen, ΔH plus ideal CO compression is 2.871 kWh/kg and leaves 0.329 kWh/kg before the same additional costs. It is an enthalpy-based screen, not a full utility model.

Useful one-at-a-time thresholds against the modeled SOEC boundary are:

| Loss considered alone | Requirement to remain below 3.2 kWh/kg |
|---|---:|
| Product-state work returned to bus | ρP > 0.82075 |
| Wall-to-addressed-state loading | η > 0.90955 |
| Full-loss attrition with perfect product recovery | ℓ/p < 0.09944; if all nonproducts are attrition, p > 0.90955 |
| All remaining control, reset, conditioning, heat, separation costs | combined remainder < 0.61058 eV/CO |

These conditions spend the same margin and cannot be added independently. At 80% product return or 90% loading efficiency, modeled work rises to 3.415 and 3.416 eV/CO respectively before output conditioning. No matched measurements exist.

A separate Haldor Topsoe presentation reports a dry-CO2 stack run of about 14,000 h, 30,000 Nm3 (35.5 t) CO, 3.44 kWh/Nm3, and Faradaic efficiency of 99 ± 1%. The reported energy and mass imply about 2.91 kWh/kg at stack boundary. This is a scale and durability cross-check; it is not the same purified-product system boundary as ISPT.
