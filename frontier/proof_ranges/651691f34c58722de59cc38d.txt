Vanishing on the determinant-zero variety implies
\[ F=(\det A)\,Q(A) \]
for a quadratic polynomial \(Q\).
At a nonzero rank-one \(R\), (14) and homogeneity give
\[ D^2F(R)\succeq0, \qquad D^2F(R)[R,R]=12F(R)=0. \]
Positive semidefiniteness therefore gives
\[ D^2F(R)[R,H]=0 \]
for every \(H\). Differentiated Euler homogeneity implies
\[ D^2F(R)[R,H]=3\,DF(R)[H], \]
so \(DF(R)=0\).
But
\[ DF(R)=Q(R)\,D(\det)(R), \]
and \(D(\det)(R)\neq0\). Hence \(Q\) also vanishes on the rank-one variety and is a multiple of \(\det A\). Rank-one convexity along \(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,t)\) forces that multiple to be nonnegative. ∎
This does not establish that determinant-square is the only obstruction to closed completion. It establishes the narrower, exact statement that it is the only rank-one-convex quartic invisible on all rank-one matrices.
Write \(\Delta(A)=\det A\). Define
\[ \gamma_*(F) = \inf_{\substack{\operatorname{rank}H=1\\D\Delta(A)[H]\neq0}} \frac{D^2F(A)[H,H]} {2\big(D\Delta(A)[H]\big)^2}. \tag{16} \]
For rank-one-convex quartics this is finite and nonnegative, and, for \(\gamma\geq0\),
\[ \boxed{ F-\gamma\Delta^2\ \text{is rank-one convex} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \gamma\leq\gamma_*(F). } \tag{17} \]
The proof is direct: along a rank-one line, \(\Delta(A+tH)\) is affine, so
\[ D^2(\Delta^2)(A)[H,H] = 2\big(D\Delta(A)[H]\big)^2. \]
Thus (16) identifies exactly how much of the misleading minor energy can be removed without losing rank-one convexity.
For example,
\[ \gamma_*(\Delta^2)=1, \qquad \gamma_*(|A|_F^4)=2. \]

There is also a constructive way to measure exactly what is lost when a polynomial correction is forced to close.
Let \(G\) be a homogeneous quartic and let \(\Omega\) have homogeneous quadratic coefficients. With the Euler vector field \(\mathcal R(A)=A\), Cartan’s formula gives
\[ d(\iota_{\mathcal R}\Omega)+\iota_{\mathcal R}d\Omega=4\Omega. \]
Therefore
\[ \boxed{ \Omega^{\mathrm{cl}} = \frac14d(\iota_{\mathcal R}\Omega) = \Omega-\frac14\iota_{\mathcal R}d\Omega } \tag{18} \]
is globally exact.
It follows that the explicit coefficient inequality
\[ \boxed{ E_G(A;B_1,B_2)+\Omega_A(B_1,B_2) \geq \frac14\left|d\Omega_A(A,B_1,B_2)\right| } \tag{19} \]
is sufficient to produce a closed positive certificate.
This operation spends a quantified part of the pointwise positivity margin to cancel the exterior derivative.
The identity is standard differential-form machinery. The hard question is whether the necessary margin can be obtained after determinant removal. Proposition 1 does not supply that margin, polynomial dependence, or closedness.
The retained proof obligation is the following.
For every rank-one-convex homogeneous quartic polynomial \(F\) on \(\mathbb R^{2\times2}\), do there exist
\[ 0\leq\gamma\leq\gamma_*(F), \qquad M\succ0,\quad\operatorname{tr}M=2, \]
and a globally closed two-form \(\omega\) with homogeneous quadratic coefficients such that, for
\[ G=F-\gamma(\det A)^2, \]
one has
\[ \boxed{ \sum_{i,j=1}^{2} M_{ij}D^2G(A)[B_i,B_j] +\omega_A(B_1,B_2)\geq0 } \tag{Q4} \]
