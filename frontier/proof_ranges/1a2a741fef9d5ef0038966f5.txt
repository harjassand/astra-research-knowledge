## 5. Gauge freedoms and other lost information

**Permutation.** A product count is invariant under permutation of the \(n\) carbon atoms. The identified object is the unordered point measure \(\Xi\), not atom-to-position assignments or a bond-level genealogy.

**Common clock origin.** Absolute entry times have no coordinate-free origin. A common shift of all entry times and the laboratory time origin is a reparameterization.

**Uncalibrated common delay.** Let the actual probability at the feed boundary be \(p_{\rm cmd}(s-\delta)\), where \(\delta\) is an unknown command-to-boundary delay. The data depend on ages and \(\delta\) only through the effective shifted ages. Replacing every entry time \(s_i\) by \(s_i+c\) and \(\delta\) by \(\delta+c\) leaves every label probability \(p_{\rm cmd}(s_i-\delta)\) unchanged. Thus the ancestry common-time component cannot be separated from an unknown common transfer delay using product counts alone. Measuring the actual waveform at the declared feed boundary and synchronizing it to product collection fixes this gauge.

**Stationary-code averaging.** A stationary telegraph code has translation-invariant finite-dimensional distributions. Its averaged parity readout is therefore insensitive to a common shift of all entry times. This is useful when the common delay is nuisance variation, but it means that this code does not locate absolute entry age.

**Unmeasured label channel.** If the commanded waveform is passed through unknown mixing, dead volume, contamination, or purity drift, only the composition after that transfer is sampled by the atoms. An unknown transfer delay/shape can be traded against an ancestry-time law. The nominal command is not the input contract.

**Detector channel.** If the true whole-product count vector is \(\pi\in\mathbb R^{n+1}\) and the measured channel is \(y=A_{\rm det}\pi+b\), recovery of \(\pi\) requires a calibrated response matrix that is injective on the relevant count contrasts (full column rank is sufficient). A rank-deficient or unknown matrix leaves a further nullspace. Fragment counts, natural-isotope corrections, backgrounds, recovery, normalization, and drift must be included in that channel model; a fragment spectrum is not automatically the parent-product count law.
