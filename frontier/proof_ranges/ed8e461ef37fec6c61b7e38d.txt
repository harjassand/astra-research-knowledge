1. INTERFACE AND QUANTIFIERS

Let m be any positive integer and h=4m^2. The algorithm knows h and the two
candidate state formulas, but not the hypothesis i in {0,1}. Its only
hypothesis-dependent resource consists of independently prepared copies of
|B_i>. All ancillas, shared randomness, advice, and classical descriptions
are independent of i. No preparation-unitary query, inverse-unitary query,
controlled preparation access, label-bearing metadata, or conditional-state
oracle is supplied. Quantum measurements may be collective and adaptive.

The fixed-copy contract supplies at most N copies and requires an output
K in the nonnegative integers, unconditionally. The output law Q_i must be
within total variation epsilon=1/12 of P_i, the law of mode-2 photon count
conditioned on mode-1 count being h in |B_i>. Success flags cannot be
discarded before this error is assessed. This is a distributional sampling
contract, not high-confidence tomography of a returned distribution.
Throughout, TV(P,Q)=(1/2)sum_k |P(k)-Q(k)| and quantum trace distance is
(1/2)||rho-sigma||_1.

The event H=h specifies the mathematical target. The acquisition copies
are unconditional copies, not copies supplied after that event happened.
If exact B_i or its identifying bit is supplied, zero physical copies
suffice, so no copy lower bound applies to that different interface.

2. PHYSICAL STATE AND EXACT COUNT LAW

For real symmetric B with ||B||<1, use the normalized state
 |B> = det(I-B^2)^(1/4) exp((a^dagger)^T B a^dagger/2)|0>.
Set a=1/4, t=1/(4m), b=t^2=1/(4h)=a/h, and
 B0 = [[a,0],[0,0]],       B1 = [[a,t],[t,0]].
The eigenvalues of B1 are (a +/- sqrt(a^2+4t^2))/2. Since t<=1/4,
 ||B1|| <= (1+sqrt(5))/8 <1; ||B0||=1/4.
Define D0=1-a^2=15/16 and D1=(1-b)^2-a^2>0.

Because the creation operators commute, the unnormalized generating
function for B1 is exp(a z1^2/2) exp(t z1 z2). Its coefficient at z1^h z2^k
is zero unless 0<=k<=h and h-k is even. Otherwise it is
 (a/2)^((h-k)/2) t^k / [((h-k)/2)! k!].
Multiplication by sqrt(h! k!) and squaring gives the actual joint mass
 Pr_1(H=h,K=k) = sqrt(D1) h! a^(h-k)b^k /
                 [k! 2^(h-k) ((h-k)/2)!^2].
Thus the proposed weight with h!^2 instead of h! is valid as an
UNNORMALIZED CONDITIONAL weight, differing by the common factor
h!/sqrt(D1) from this actual joint mass.

Under B0 the second mode is vacuum, so P0 is point mass at zero.
Write k=2j, n=h/2, and divide the B1 conditional weights by the weight at 0:
 r_j = w(2j)/w(0)
     = [product_{r=0}^{j-1}(1-2r/h)^2]/(2j)!,  0<=j<=n.
Set r_j=0 for j>n and Z_h=sum_{j=0}^n r_j. Then P1(K=2j)=r_j/Z_h.
In particular r_0=1, r_1=1/2, so Z_h>=3/2 and
 TV(P0,P1)=1-P1(0)=1-1/Z_h >=1/3.
The exact equality w(2)/w(0)=1/2 holds for every allowed h.
Also r_j<=1/(2j)!, hence Z_h<=cosh(1).
As h tends to infinity, dominated convergence gives Z_h -> cosh(1).

The physical herald events have strictly positive probabilities
 p0=Pr_0(H=h)=sqrt(D0) h! a^h/[2^h (h/2)!^2],
 p1=Pr_1(H=h)=sqrt(D1) h! a^h Z_h/[2^h (h/2)!^2].
Their ratio is p1/p0=F Z_h, with F as below. Both are of order
4^(-h)/sqrt(h). Direct heralding therefore costs exponentially many
copies on average, whereas the known-family acquisition procedure below
does not need to observe H=h.

3. OVERLAP, FIXED-COPY LOWER BOUND, AND A SHARPER EXACT FORM

The mode-2 vacuum component of |B1> equals
 (D1/D0)^(1/4) |B0>.
As |B0> is itself supported on mode-2 vacuum, its overlap with |B1> is
 (D1/D0)^(1/4), a positive real number. Therefore the SQUARED fidelity is
 F=|<B0|B1>|^2=sqrt(D1/D0)
  =sqrt(1-x_h),       x_h=(8h-1)/(15h^2).
This also proves that the probability of mode-2 vacuum under B1 is F.
The distinction between amplitude overlap sqrt(F) and squared fidelity F
is essential in all powers below.

For a sampler with TV(Q_i,P_i)<=epsilon=1/12,
 TV(Q0,Q1)>=TV(P0,P1)-2epsilon>=1/6.
Every allowed fixed-copy protocol is a quantum-to-classical channel on
N copies. Contractivity of trace distance and the exact pure-state trace
distance formula imply
 1/6 <= TV(Q0,Q1) <= sqrt(1-F^N).
Consequently
 N >= log(36/35)/[-log(F)].                         (A)
One may replace 1/6 by 1-1/Z_h-2epsilon in this exact inequality.
The asymptotic coefficient in (A) is (15/4)log(36/35) h, about 0.1056 h.

The simpler proposed constant follows without asymptotics:
 1/36 <=1-F^N <=N(1-F)
        =N x_h/(1+sqrt(1-x_h)) <=N x_h <=8N/(15h).
Thus N>=5h/96. This also covers N=0 by contradiction.

4. A MATCHING ACQUISITION AND SAMPLING PROCEDURE

On each of N copies measure whether mode 2 is vacuum. Hypothesis 0 never
produces a click; under hypothesis 1 no clicks occur with probability F^N.
Choose label 1 if any click occurred and label 0 otherwise. Output zero
for label 0, and an exact sample from the known P1 formula for label 1.
Under hypothesis 0 this is exact. Under hypothesis 1 its error is
 F^N TV(P0,P1)<=F^N.
Since 1-F=x_h/(1+sqrt(1-x_h))>=x_h/2>=7/(30h),
 F^N<=exp[-7N/(30h)]. Therefore
 N>=ceil((30h/7) log(1/epsilon))
suffices for TV error epsilon. Alternatively choose the smaller exact
integer N=ceil(log(1/epsilon)/[-log F]). For fixed epsilon<1/6, fixed-copy
acquisition complexity is Theta(h) on this two-hypothesis family.

No target conditional oracle is required. An exact rational rejection
sampler for P1 is as follows (all arithmetic can use integers):
 - Propose J>=0 with probability 2^(-J-1), using fair bits until the first 0.
 - If 2J>h, reject. Otherwise set
      A_J=product_{r=0}^{J-1}(1-2r/h)^2,
      acceptance=2^J A_J/(2J)!.
 - Accept with this rational probability; on acceptance output K=2J.
The probability is at most 1 because A_J<=1 and 2^J<=(2J)!.
Proposal times acceptance is r_J/2, so accepted output law is exactly P1.
Total acceptance is Z_h/2>=3/4; the expected proposal count is
2/Z_h<=4/3. Exact rational Bernoulli trials can use unbiased random bits
and integer comparison; geometric moments give expected bit work
polynomial in log(h). There is no bounded worst-case random-bit/runtime
claim. The included Python implementation uses a seeded pseudorandom
generator for reproducible diagnostics; its algorithmic exactness refers
to the ideal fair-bit interface, not a claim about finite-seed randomness.

The physical upper bound assumes ideal independent state preparations and
ideal vacuum-versus-nonvacuum detectors. It specifies copy count and the
classical sampler; it is not a hardware demonstration or a noise-robust
detector design. Preparing or describing the unknown device is not free
if a broader application contract charges those costs.

4A. EXACT MINIMUM COPY COUNT (STRONGER RESULT)

Require exact unconditional output laws P0 and P1. Their classical squared
fidelity is P1(0)=1/Z_h because P0 is point mass at zero. Fidelity cannot
decrease under a quantum channel, so every exact N-copy sampler obeys
 F^N<=1/Z_h.
For an entirely elementary proof, let M0 be the POVM effect for output zero.
Under u=|B0>^tensor N its expectation is one, so 0<=M0<=I implies M0 u=u.
For v=|B1>^tensor N, Cauchy-Schwarz applied to M0^(1/2) gives
 |<u,v>|^2=|<u,M0 v>|^2<=<u,M0 u><v,M0 v>=P1(0).
The left side is F^N.
Conversely, set q=1-1/Z_h and s=1-F^N. Measure vacuum/nonvacuum on all N
copies. If all outcomes are vacuum, output zero. Otherwise, output a
positive sample from P1 conditioned on K>0 with probability q/s, and output
zero with the remaining probability. When F^N<=1/Z_h, q/s is at most one.
Under B0 the output is always zero. Under B1, the probability of a positive
output is s(q/s)=q, and its conditional positive-count law is exactly the
desired one. Hence this channel realizes both target laws exactly.

The exact optimal count is therefore
 N_star = ceil(log(Z_h)/[-log(F)]).
Numerical logarithms do not resolve an exact integer boundary. It can be
checked using rational arithmetic: N is sufficient precisely when
 (D1/D0)^N <= 1/Z_h^2.
Minimality is checked by failure of the corresponding inequality at N-1.
The code finds N by integer doubling/bisection using these exact rational
comparisons, without a fixed-precision logarithm seed.
Since Z_h -> cosh(1) and -log(F)~4/(15h),
 N_star/h -> (15/4)log(cosh(1)), approximately 1.62668.
This minimum is over all collective/adaptive fixed-copy quantum samplers,
under the exact count and unconditional-output interface of Section 1.

The positive-target sampler uses the Section 4 sampler and discards K=0;
the probability of a positive target count is at least 1/3, so this has
finite constant expected proposal count. Rebalancing q/s is generally
algebraic, but requires no real-number oracle. Let v=(D1/D0)^N, which is
rational, and note q/s=q/(1-sqrt(v)). For a rational u in [0,1], if u<=q
then u<q/s; otherwise
 u<q/s  if and only if  v>(1-q/u)^2.
The latter is an exact rational comparison. Generate a uniform U in [0,1]
one fair bit at a time, maintaining its dyadic interval. Accept or reject
once both interval endpoints are on the same side of q/s. This terminates
almost surely, with at most two expected random bits (endpoint coincidences
have probability zero; a threshold exactly one is recognized immediately).

For fixed h, Z_h is a finite rational sum. A common denominator divides
h^h h!, and for N=O(h) the rational v has O(h log h) bits. Consequently,
finite exact setup and the comparison sampler have polynomial(h) bit cost;
this is not a poly(log h) exact-setup claim. The approximate truncated
sampler can reduce classical setup cost, but is a different accuracy
contract. The quantum-copy count N_star remains exact.
