# Period dependence, competing mechanisms, and a finite causal operation

Status: internally derived analysis, externally unreviewed. Physical control and parameters are NOT_ACQUIRED. These results do not establish historical novelty.

## 1. The fixed-rate obstruction survives positive mixtures of scalar modes

For the N887 family, hold positive potential for h=t h0 and negative potential for d=t d0. Positive output is b+=1-z and z'=-mu z. Negative output is beta and z'=lambda(1-z). With a=lambda d0, c=mu h0, and P0=h0+d0, periodic suppression below the static duty mixture is

\[
 F(t)=\frac{(1-e^{-at})(1-e^{-ct})}{\mu t P_0(1-e^{-(a+c)t})}
 =\frac{2}{\mu P_0[t\coth(at/2)+t\coth(ct/2)]}.
\]

For k>0,

\[
\frac{d}{dt}[t\coth(kt)]
=\frac{\sinh(2kt)-2kt}{2\sinh^2(kt)}>0.
\]

Consequently F strictly decreases with t, and mean ethane strictly increases. Beta cancels from comparisons at common duty. This is the exact supplied proof, independently reconstructed here, not a new theorem about arbitrary catalysis.

The same sign holds for **any nonnegative finite or integrable parallel mixture of these modes** with a common period-independent baseline:

\[
 \bar b(t)=B-\int F_\theta(t)\,d\nu(\theta),\qquad \nu\ge0.
\]

For t2>t1, every integrand F_theta(t1)-F_theta(t2) is nonnegative. Integration preserves the inequality. Strictness requires a positive-mass nondegenerate mode. Arbitrarily slow positive-weight modes cannot repair the wrong sign. This extension does not cover negative response weights, nonlinear observations, interacting modes, different effective electrical inputs, different specimens, or protocol-dependent rate laws.

## 2. Exact finite-grid sensitivity requirements

Let b_i be the reconstructed ethane/Ain at periods P_i increasing within a duty group. A nondecreasing latent sequence y_i with symmetric absolute errors |y_i-b_i|<=epsilon exists exactly when

\[
 \epsilon\ge\tfrac12\max_{i<j}(b_i-b_j)_+.
\]

Necessity follows from b_i-epsilon<=y_i<=y_j<=b_j+epsilon. For sufficiency choose y_j=max_{i<=j}(b_i-epsilon); the stated inequalities ensure y_j<=b_j+epsilon.

For independent symmetric relative errors |y_i-b_i|<=epsilon b_i and b_i>0, the threshold is

\[
 \epsilon\ge\max_{i<j}\frac{(b_i-b_j)_+}{b_i+b_j}.
\]

The duty-10 group therefore requires at least 0.1846051928 absolute b error, or 44.77490845% independent relative b error. Duty 100 requires 0.0767608083 absolute or 28.19077083% relative error. These are deterministic sensitivity thresholds; measured errors and statistical significance remain UNKNOWN.

Because b=X(1/S-1), b increases in X and decreases in S. Independent symmetric delta-percentage-point boxes in the reported X and S produce interval endpoints by evaluating the corresponding corners. A nondecreasing fit exists iff the running maximum lower endpoint does not exceed each current upper endpoint. Exact rational bisection gives delta=8.0611055943 pp for all duty-10 points, and 4.3003021425 pp for duty 100. Varying only S gives 10.0632821834 and 5.2206739100 pp, respectively. The longest duty-10 pair alone is much less robust: 0.7445130562 pp in both X/S, 0.8973344134 pp in S alone, or 4.3717733489 pp in X alone can erase its sign.

Shared positive multiplication of all b values cannot change their ordering. Here X decreases and S increases at every duty-10 step, so common monotone calibrations of those two reported quantities also preserve the sign, provided the same material-balance definition remains valid. Protocol-dependent bias, different flows or conditions, missing reaction branches, and heterogeneous histories are separate concerns.

The workbook stores point estimates, not an acquired probability law. These calculations do not create replicate errors, calibration covariance, or an uncertainty bound from decimal precision.

## 3. A fixed-rate sequential mechanism can reverse the sign

Consider three chemical configurations W, I and R, with W+I+R=1. W is a written precursor, I inhibits ethane production, and R is the resting configuration. These are abstract labels, not identified Pd species.

Positive phase: W -> I at rate k and I -> R at rate r. Negative phase: I -> W and R -> W, each at rate w. All coefficients are fixed across all waveforms. Output is bplus-A I during positive exposure and beta during negative exposure, with 0<=A<=bplus and beta>=0. Positivity and simplex invariance follow from the Markov transition structure. Static positive output tends to bplus; static negative output is beta.

Let e_k=exp(-kh), e_r=exp(-rh), e_w=exp(-wd), and k!=r. The periodic state at the beginning of the positive phase is

\[
 W_0=\frac{1-e_w}{1-e_w e_k},\qquad
 I_0=\frac{e_w k(e_r-e_k)W_0}{(k-r)(1-e_w e_r)}.
\]

During that phase,

\[
 I(s)=I_0 e^{-rs}+\frac{kW_0}{k-r}(e^{-rs}-e^{-ks}).
\]

Thus

\[
 \bar b=\frac{h b_{+}+d\beta-AJ}{h+d},\quad
 J=I_0\frac{1-e_r}{r}+\frac{kW_0}{k-r}
 \left[\frac{1-e_r}{r}-\frac{1-e_k}{k}\right].
\]

The negative step resets I to its precursor. Frequent resets interrupt formation of I; a longer positive segment permits I to form. This is a sequential path mechanism, distinct from monotone decay of an immediately written inhibitor.

One fixed illustrative choice is k=0.5/s, r=0.1/s, w=10/s, A=bplus=0.8840362510, beta=1.75. Its characteristic transition times are 2 s, 10 s and 0.1 s. No arbitrary slow limit or separately chosen rates for each waveform is used. At h/d=10, mean b for (h,d)=(0.01,0.001),(0.1,0.01),(1,0.1),(10,1) s is respectively 0.7192220, 0.7190213, 0.7001866 and 0.5273131. The sign reverses N887's prediction. At (100,10) it rebounds to 0.8823979, approaching the static duty mixture as periods grow without bound.

This is **not a quantitative explanation of Xu's data**. A bounded descriptive fit to the seven duty-10/100 b values has RMSE 0.1019114, and its remaining 23-row prediction RMSE is 0.4455177. The selected parameter search does not prove a global optimum, but its poor result does not support promotion. It does not fit X/S separately, electrical records, spectra, or chemical state measurements. Beta=1.75 was an illustrative value between the neighboring measured static points at -0.25 and 0 V; an actual -0.1 V endpoint was not acquired.

The construction establishes a narrower fact: finite, fixed chemical rates can produce the reversed period trend without an arbitrary slow-state witness. Its state assignment and ability to describe the experiment remain unestablished.

An especially direct falsifier is the post-write response. Starting with ideal W=1,I=0, the inhibited occupancy is k(exp(-rt)-exp(-kt))/(k-r), which initially grows and peaks at log(k/r)/(k-r). For the illustrative rates this is 4.0235948 s. The fitted version predicts 1.6637671 s. N887's isolated written inhibitor instead gives an immediately present, monotonically decaying effect. Known-width fresh packets at randomized post-write delays can discriminate these shapes after accounting for packet exposure and complete collection. A single outlet transient without transport control cannot.

## 4. Why terminal waveform attenuation alone is not yet an explanation

A memoryless chemical law b=r(u) gives a cycle mean integral of r over the actual exposure occupation measure. Equal commanded duty does not imply equal occupation measure: the acquired dense trace shows settling and overshoot.

If an acquired global bound r(u)>=b_min held over every actual exposure visited, then every memoryless cycle would have mean b>=b_min. The lowest measured static b is 0.8840362510 at +1.5 V, whereas duty-10 dynamic b reaches 0.2276908882. Under the additional global bound this would exclude waveform filtering followed by a memoryless static law.

That extra premise is missing. Nine static voltage points do not bound an arbitrary response between them, the dense terminal trace briefly leaves the static command window, and terminal voltage is not an acquired local reacting-surface field. A hypothesized deep response minimum between points can evade the discrete static hull. A shared monotone static response on a fully bounded exposure domain would close that escape, but must be justified or measured. Thus electrical lag is a concrete alternative input mismatch, not an acquired full mechanism.

## 5. A finite causal operation that does not require a global washout model

The proposed operation is a randomized scheduled-pulse omission followed by a freshly tagged cohort and a common read/chase. It is unperformed. It refines existing N884 logic by specifying a history-dependent estimand that need not assert restoration of every hidden mode.

At each eligible block i, record preassignment history H_i and randomize Z_i=1 (execute the scheduled write) or 0 (duration-matched positive sham), with probability p_i known and strictly between zero and one. Enriched carbon is absent during preparation to a quantified background bound. A calibration-selected guard delay and tag-admission clock are fixed in advance and identical in both arms. At that clock admit the same finite tagged packet. Freeze subsequent external controls to the same read/chase policy through a fixed collection horizon, bounding residual fresh carbon at its end. Do not introduce arm-dependent future pulses during that policy. If actual read exposure has not matched at the prescribed clock, retain the trial and charge a conservative exposure penalty; do not discard a treatment-dependent failure after assignment.

Let Y_i(z) be eventual fresh-cohort ethane in carbon-equivalent units, bounded by known admitted fresh-carbon M. It may depend on all past history and any unmeasured current state. Consistency and sequential randomization give

\[
 E\!\left[\frac{(1-Z_i)Y_i}{1-p_i}-\frac{Z_iY_i}{p_i}\mid H_i\right]
 =E[Y_i(0)-Y_i(1)\mid H_i]=\tau_i.
\]

No scalar relaxation rate, periodic equilibrium, finite-dimensional state realization, or globally sufficient reset time occurs in this equality. Its target is the average pulse effect over the histories actually reached by the trial policy. It is not the response from a unique known equilibrium or automatically the unmodified periodic operating regime. Assignment must remain unpredictable from the current state; history-based exclusions after seeing the outcome are not allowed.

For p_i=1/2 and N eligible blocks fixed before viewing outcomes, the true inverse-probability term lies in [-2M,2M]. Conditional Hoeffding bounds give, with probability at least 1-alpha,

\[
 \frac1N\sum_i\tau_i\ge\widehat\tau-M\sqrt{\frac{8\log(1/\alpha)}N}.
\]

The target can be random through H_i. This martingale bound does not require independent reactor outcomes. It does require a valid sequential experiment and bounded, correctly assigned outcomes. Data-dependent early stopping requires a corresponding valid sequential confidence method, not reuse of this fixed-N expression.

For calibrated finite recovery intervals [Y_i^-,Y_i^+] and missing-fresh-carbon bounds u_i, replace each sham outcome by Y_i^- and each executed-pulse outcome by min(M,Y_i^++u_i). This replacement is pointwise no larger than the true contrast estimator. Subtract separately bounded exposure and isotope-assignment errors. Jointly valid measurement intervals and the randomization bound must share an explicit error-probability budget. A positive result establishes a finite, subsequent-fresh-carbon response under the tested policy. It does not identify electronic control, H inventory, adsorbates, membrane state, or a globally complete horizon.

For useful ethylene benefit reverse the treatment signs and apply the same construction to fresh ethylene amounts. Missing ethylene in the sham arm can fake benefit, so its u_0 enters the adverse bound, whereas missing ethane in the treated arm enters the ethane-deficit bound. Carbon removal and net ethylene gain must both be reported.

The proposal cannot use the observed static/periodic contrast to assume a nonzero isolated-pulse effect or choose its power. N887 already disproves that transfer under its observation contract. At a purely illustrative normalized effect delta=0.05M and alpha=0.05, the conservative displayed bound needs N>9586 before any measurement or exposure penalties. This is a cost illustration, not a proposed sample-size calculation from acquired variance.

## 6. What the finite grid bounds

With common calibrated balances, the grid determines the 30 tested means and their observed ordering. With acquired simultaneous uncertainties, it could reject a shared model class at a specified confidence level and bound the best tested performance. It does not bound an untested optimum, all slower observable modes, conditioning/withdrawal horizon, isolated-pulse effect, state carrier, or complete process work.

Finite-precision observations cannot exclude an arbitrarily slow hidden mode with amplitude beneath their uncertainty without an independent amplitude/capacity/dynamics restriction. Exact aggregate observations also do not identify an unrestricted state-space realization. These are distinct reasons for the remaining gate. The fixed-rate trend mismatch removes a particular witness family; it does not identify the surviving model.

Finally, AB versus BA is not a useful periodic ordering test for two constant segments: the two infinite periodic inputs differ only by time origin. A phase-invariant complete-cycle mean is identical. Order tests need a common finite preparation/read boundary or genuinely different segment structure, with matched actual exposures.
