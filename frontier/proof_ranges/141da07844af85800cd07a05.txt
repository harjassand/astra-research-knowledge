## 6. A compatible obstruction to unrestricted assembly

The cross-packet condition cannot be removed. Even the polyconvex quartic F(A)=(det A)^2 has negative interaction increments.

Let

    r(x)=1+a cos x,     s(x)=1-a cos x,
    phi_1=r(x)(cos y,sin y),
    phi_2=s(x)(cos 2y,sin 2y).

Both are in the class of Theorem 2.1. For their sum,

    det D(phi_1+phi_2)=r r'+2s s'+(2r's+r s')cos y.

Exact averaging gives

    E(det D(phi_1+phi_2))²
       -E(det Dphi_1)²-E(det Dphi_2)²
        =-(7/4)a²+(17/16)a^4.                               (6.1)

At a=1/2 the individual values and the total are

    c_1=17/128,    c_2=17/32,    c_sum=75/256,
    c_sum-c_1-c_2=-95/256.                                  (6.2)

Each individual cubic vector vanishes by averaging its output rotation, and H_i>=0, so Delta_i=c_i. The total optimized value is at most its zero-background value c_sum, strictly smaller than Delta_1+Delta_2. It is nonetheless nonnegative, because determinant-square Jensen proves quasiconvexity directly.

This is an actual compatible obstruction to a proposed decomposition/assembly inequality, **not** a Morrey counterexample. It shows that even individually globally positive resonant packets cannot be assembled by simply adding their optimized positive margins.
