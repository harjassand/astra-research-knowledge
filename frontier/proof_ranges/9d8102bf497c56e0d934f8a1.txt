A constructive result survived
I obtained a finite-certificate theorem that removes arbitrary quantum references from the comparison of finite classical channels. For binary-input channels, it gives an exact polynomial-time decision algorithm.

This is a positive result rather than another obstruction. However, I have not established its historical originality or the historic computational capability requested in your mission. The proof does not extend to arbitrary noncommutative quantum channels.

The central mathematical distinction is now sharper: the consistency problem can be solved for scalar channel coefficients, while a three-input example proves that simply ignoring quantum references is already invalid.

What the theorem changes
The original definition quantifies over quantum references of arbitrarily large dimension and infinitely many input experiments. Equation (2) replaces that with one finite system of matrix inequalities.

The existence of a suitable signed lift was already known to be sufficient. The constructive implication established in this derivation is its necessity for arbitrary finite classical channels, together with the finite-reference bound. I am not claiming the signed-lift concept itself as new.
