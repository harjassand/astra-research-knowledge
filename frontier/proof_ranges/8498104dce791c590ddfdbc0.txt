### Apply the same successful operation repeatedly

Suppose (1)–(2) hold, but \(U_\sigma\) contains no product vector.

Run the same successful operation on the returned catalyst repeatedly, always with fresh separable inputs, and condition on all \(n\) successes.

After each successful prefix, the catalyst marginal is again \(\tau\). Therefore the probability of the next success remains \(p\), and the complete successful event has probability
\[
p^n>0.
\tag{13}
\]

There is a subtlety: later postselection can change the states of earlier outputs through their correlations with the catalyst. It is **not** valid to say that all earlier marginals remain equal to \(\sigma\).

What survives is their support.

Once an output is supported on \(U_\sigma\), operations on other registers cannot move it outside that subspace. Every new output is also supported there. Hence the final \(n\)-output state \(\rho_n\) obeys
\[
\operatorname{supp}\rho_n\subseteq U_\sigma^{\otimes n}.
\tag{14}
\]

Let
\[
r=\operatorname{SN}(\tau),
\]
the Schmidt number of the finite catalyst. Every successful separable operation, followed by discarding the catalyst, preserves the bound
\[
\operatorname{SN}(\rho_n)\le r.
\tag{15}
\]

Schmidt number and its LOCC monotonicity are established notions. Here the relevant stronger observation follows directly from their definition: each product Kraus operator preserves an upper bound on the Schmidt rank of every vector in a decomposition, even after conditioning on success. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9911117)

A state with Schmidt number at most \(r\) satisfies
\[
E_R(\rho_n)\le\log r.
\tag{16}
\]
For each pure vector in a rank-\(r\) decomposition, its Schmidt-diagonal separable comparator gives relative entropy at most \(\log r\); convexity gives the mixed-state bound.

Equations (10), (14) and (16) imply
\[
n\kappa_{U_\sigma}\le\log r
\qquad\text{for every positive integer }n,
\tag{17}
\]
a contradiction.

This proves necessity.

**The contradiction is unaffected by an exponentially small success probability.** Each finite successful branch must obey the same Schmidt-number ceiling.
