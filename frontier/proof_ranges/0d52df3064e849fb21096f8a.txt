5. The attempted universal extension fails at common support
The obvious next attempt was to remove the common-reference assumption. That fails even for two deterministic, idempotent rules.
Let \(S\) be the satisfying set of a Boolean verifier. Add two special symbols \(a,b\), and define
\[
F_a(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S,\\
a,&x\notin S,
\end{cases}
\qquad
F_b(x)=
\begin{cases}
x,&x\in S,\\
b,&x\notin S.
\end{cases}
\]
Each rule needs at most one verifier query. Individually they have trivial fixed states: \(\delta_a\) and \(\delta_b\).
For a distribution \(\mu\), put \(s=\mu(S)\). Directly,
\[
d(\mu F_a,\mu)=1-s-\mu(a),
\]
\[
d(\mu F_b,\mu)=1-s-\mu(b).
\]
Since \(\mu(a)+\mu(b)\leq1-s\),
\[
d(\mu F_a,\mu)+d(\mu F_b,\mu)\geq1-s.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\max\{d(\mu F_a,\mu),d(\mu F_b,\mu)\}\leq\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu(S)\geq1-2\delta.
}
\]
Even constant-accuracy joint consistency therefore produces a satisfying assignment with constant probability.
But the averaged rule has the easy stationary distribution
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{bad}}=\frac12(\delta_a+\delta_b),
\]
which assigns zero probability to \(S\). Its individual residuals are both \(1/2\).
This isolates the obstruction:
Averaging can satisfy an aggregate equation through cancellation, while satisfying the individual equations requires acquiring the solution support.

Every common invariant distribution of \(F_a,F_b\) is supported on \(S\). There is no common invariant distribution dominating a full-support initialization. The finite-relative-entropy assumption fails precisely at that support restriction.
This is not an exact-equality artifact. Perturb each rule by at most \(\eta\) in maximum-row total variation. Any distribution with residual at most \(\delta\) under both perturbed rules still satisfies
\[
\mu(S)\geq1-2(\delta+\eta).
\]
Therefore, universally “softening” these rules while retaining a robust solution decoder cannot evade the compiler tradeoff without paying elsewhere.
The example is a short reduction, not a new discovery that satisfiability is hard. Its role is to falsify the proposed extension of the preparation theorem.
6. A broader geometric obstruction to repairing the method
I also tested whether a different divergence could remove the support problem while preserving the proof mechanism.
The desired replacement would be finite everywhere, contract under stochastic processing, and retain enough convexity to force progress toward simultaneous consistency.
That combination is impossible.
Theorem: disjoint-support flatness
Let \(\mathfrak D(p\|q)\) be any divergence monotone under all stochastic maps. Fix \(q=\delta_0\).
For any distributions \(p,r\) supported outside \(0\), there are stochastic maps \(A,B\) such that
\[
Aq=q,\quad Ap=r,
\qquad
Bq=q,\quad Br=p.
\]
For example, \(A\) leaves \(0\) fixed and replaces every other input by a sample from \(r\).
Data processing in both directions gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D(p\|\delta_0)
=
\mathfrak D(r\|\delta_0).
}
\]
Thus the divergence is constant—possibly infinite—on the entire face disjoint from the reference support.
If finite there, it cannot satisfy
\[
\frac{\mathfrak D(p\|q)+\mathfrak D(r\|q)}2
-\mathfrak D\!\left(\frac{p+r}{2}\middle\|q\right)
\geq c\,d(p,r)^2
\]
with a uniform \(c>0\). Three classical states already yield a contradiction. The quantum obstruction follows on diagonal states.
This rules out an everywhere-finite, uniformly curved, universally data-processing potential as a repair. It does not rule out adaptive support acquisition, non-data-processing potentials, nonlinear operations, or other algorithms.
Quantitative version
There is also a worst-case lower bound on the cost of rare reference events.
Assume \(\mathfrak D\) is normalized by \(\mathfrak D(p\|p)=0\), is convex in its first argument, satisfies data processing across finite alphabets, and has the displayed midpoint curvature \(c\).
For a uniform reference on \(2^k\) points, repeatedly mixing two distributions uniform on disjoint, equally sized subsets gives
\[
\mathfrak D(\delta_i\|u_{2^k})\geq kc.
\]
For general \(q_i>0\), a stochastic map can send \(i\) to a distinguished output while sending \(q\) to a uniform distribution on \(2^k\) points whenever \(2^k\leq1/q_i\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak D(\delta_i\|q)
\geq
c\left\lfloor\log_2\frac1{q_i}\right\rfloor.
}
\]
For \(q_i=0\), the same construction works for arbitrarily large \(k\), forcing an infinite value.
Relative entropy has
\[
D(\delta_i\|q)=\log(1/q_i),
\]
so its worst-case logarithmic rare-event cost has the correct order within this axiomatic class.
This is a point-mass, worst-case comparison—not a claim that every such divergence dominates relative entropy on all pairs. Historical originality of these elementary geometric deductions is not established.
