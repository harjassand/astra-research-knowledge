# 1. The theorem

Write
\[
\tau(X)=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(X)}d,
\qquad
\langle A,B\rangle_\tau=\tau(AB)
\]
on the real Hilbert space of Hermitian \(d\times d\) matrices.

A channel \(\Phi:M_d(\mathbb C)\to M_d(\mathbb C)\) is **self-compatible** when there is a completely positive, trace-preserving map
\[
\mathcal B:M_d\longrightarrow M_d\otimes M_d
\]
whose two marginal channels both equal \(\Phi\).

All superoperator inequalities below mean **Hilbert–Schmidt quadratic-form inequalities**, not completely positive order.

### Theorem — Common classical comparison

Suppose \(\Phi\) is completely positive, trace preserving, unital, Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, and self-compatible.

Then there exists **one finite POVM** \((E_y)_y\), with associated entanglement-breaking channel
\[
\Psi(X)
=
\sum_y\operatorname{Tr}(E_yX)
\frac{E_y}{\operatorname{Tr}(E_y)},
\]
where zero effects are discarded, such that
\[
\boxed{\Psi\succeq (2\Phi^2-\mathsf I)_+.}
\tag{1}
\]

Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
\mathsf I-\Psi
\preceq 2(\mathsf I-\Phi^2)
\preceq 4(\mathsf I-\Phi).
}
\tag{2}
\]

In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\tau\!\left[A(A-\Psi(A))\right]
\le
4\,\tau\!\left[A(A-\Phi(A))\right]
\quad\text{for every Hermitian }A.
}
\tag{3}
\]

The same \(\Psi\) works for every observable simultaneously. It is unital, trace preserving, Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, and positive as a Hilbert–Schmidt operator.

There is no spectral-gap assumption, no dimension restriction, and no requirement that \(\Phi\) have nonnegative Hilbert–Schmidt eigenvalues.

**The positive-part map in (1) is not asserted to be a quantum channel.** A separately constructed physical channel dominates its quadratic form.

# 2. Proof

## 2.1. Select the reconstructible eigenmodes

Kadison’s inequality and trace preservation give
\[
\|\Phi(A)\|_{2,\tau}^2
\le \tau(\Phi(A^2))
=\|A\|_{2,\tau}^2.
\]
Thus the self-adjoint operator \(\Phi\) has a Hermitian orthonormal eigenbasis with eigenvalues in \([-1,1]\).

Choose an orthonormal basis \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) of all its traceless eigenspaces satisfying
\[
|\lambda_i|>\frac1{\sqrt2},
\qquad
\Phi(A_i)=\lambda_i A_i,
\qquad
\tau(A_iA_j)=\delta_{ij}.
\tag{4}
\]
If there are none, the completely depolarizing channel proves (1). Assume \(m\ge1\).

Symmetrize \(\mathcal B\) under exchange of its outputs and put
\[
\sigma=\mathcal B(I/d).
\]
Both marginals of \(\sigma\) are \(I/d\). Define the real symmetric matrix
\[
T_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[\sigma(A_i\otimes A_j)\right].
\tag{5}
\]

For \(A(v)=\sum_i v_iA_i\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
0
&\le
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
\sigma(A(v)\otimes I-I\otimes A(v))^2
\right]\\
&=2\|v\|^2-2v^{\mathsf T}Tv.
\end{aligned}
\]
Therefore
\[
\boxed{T\preceq I_m.}
\tag{6}
\]

This retains the correlations between the two outputs. They are not assumed independent or separable.

## 2.2. Construct finite measurements with exactly unbiased estimators

Write
\[
A_i=\sum_a aP_{i,a}.
\]
Start with the POVM
\[
E^{(0)}_{i,a}=\frac1mP_{i,a}
\]
and real estimators
\[
z_j^{(0)}(i,a)=ma\,\mathbf1_{\{i=j\}}.
\]

Operationally, choose one observable uniformly and measure it. Then
\[
\sum_y z_i^{(0)}(y)E_y^{(0)}=A_i.
\tag{7}
\]
Its reference second-moment matrix is
\[
C_{0,ij}
=
\sum_y\tau(E_y^{(0)})
z_i^{(0)}(y)z_j^{(0)}(y)
=
m\delta_{ij}.
\tag{8}
\]

Now recursively define
\[
E^{(n+1)}_{y,z}
=
\mathcal B^*\!\left(E_y^{(n)}\otimes E_z^{(n)}\right),
\tag{9}
\]
and
\[
z_i^{(n+1)}(y,z)
=
\frac{z_i^{(n)}(y)+z_i^{(n)}(z)}
{2\lambda_i}.
\tag{10}
\]

Because \(\mathcal B^*\) is completely positive and unital, (9) is a finite POVM.

Supposing the preceding measurement has operator first moments \(A_i\), its successor satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{y,z}z_i^{(n+1)}(y,z)E^{(n+1)}_{y,z}
&=
\frac{\mathcal B^*(A_i\otimes I+I\otimes A_i)}
{2\lambda_i}\\
&=
\frac{\Phi^*(A_i)+\Phi^*(A_i)}
{2\lambda_i}\\
&=A_i.
\end{aligned}
\tag{11}
\]
Here Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjointness gives \(\Phi^*=\Phi\).

Thus **every finite-depth measurement is exactly unbiased for every selected eigenobservable**.

This is a binary broadcast tree with randomized spectral measurements at its leaves. No leaf is asked to measure incompatible observables simultaneously.

## 2.3. The full covariance matrix has a dimension-free limit bound

Set
\[
C_{n,ij}
=
\sum_y\tau(E_y^{(n)})
z_i^{(n)}(y)z_j^{(n)}(y),
\qquad
D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m).
\]

Expanding (10) produces two same-output terms and two cross-output terms. The former have reference expectation \(C_{n,ij}\), because each marginal of \(\sigma\) is tracial. Exact unbiasedness makes the latter expectations \(T_{ij}\).

Hence
\[
\boxed{
C_{n+1}
=
\frac12D^{-1}(C_n+T)D^{-1}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

This is a **matrix identity**, including off-diagonal entries.

Define
\[
\mathcal L(M)=\frac12D^{-1}MD^{-1},
\qquad
H=(2D^2-I_m)^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]
Then
\[
H\succeq I_m,
\qquad
H=\mathcal L(H+I_m).
\tag{14}
\]

The map \(\mathcal L\) preserves positive-semidefinite order: it is a congruence map. This remains true when some \(\lambda_i\) are negative.

Using \(T\preceq I_m\), induction in (12) gives
\[
C_n
\preceq
H+\mathcal L^n(C_0-H).
\tag{15}
\]

Every \(2|\lambda_i\lambda_j|>1\). Thus
\[
\bigl(\mathcal L^n(M)\bigr)_{ij}
=
\frac{M_{ij}}{(2\lambda_i\lambda_j)^n}
\longrightarrow0,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{C_n\preceq H+o(1)I_m.}
\tag{16}
\]

The initial measurement penalty was \(C_0=mI_m\). It disappears under the normalized recursion.

This is the central mechanism: **branching removes the cost of assigning different measurements to different leaves while preserving unbiased reconstruction of the slow modes.**

## 2.4. Classical regression produces one comparator on the entire observable space

For each depth, write
\[
p_{n,y}=\tau(E_y^{(n)}),
\qquad
\rho_{n,y}=\frac{E_y^{(n)}}{\operatorname{Tr}(E_y^{(n)})}.
\]
Define
\[
\Psi_n(X)=
\sum_y\operatorname{Tr}(E_y^{(n)}X)\rho_{n,y}.
\tag{17}
\]

This is a physical measure-and-prepare channel. Since
\[
\sum_y p_{n,y}\rho_{n,y}=I/d,
\]
it is unital and trace preserving. Moreover,
\[
\tau(A\Psi_n(A))
=
\sum_y p_{n,y}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{n,y}A)^2,
\tag{18}
\]
which establishes Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjointness and positivity.

Fix **any traceless Hermitian \(A\)**, not necessarily in the selected eigenspace. Put
\[
u_A(y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_{n,y}A),
\qquad
\beta_i=\tau(AA_i).
\]
By (11),
\[
\mathbb E_{p_n}[u_Az_i^{(n)}]
=
\tau\!\left(
A\sum_y z_i^{(n)}(y)E_y^{(n)}
\right)
=\beta_i.
\tag{19}
\]

For any \(v\in\mathbb R^m\), the ordinary scalar inequality
\[
\mathbb E_{p_n}
\left(u_A-v^{\mathsf T}z^{(n)}\right)^2\ge0
\]
therefore gives
\[
\tau(A\Psi_n(A))
\ge
2v^{\mathsf T}\beta-v^{\mathsf T}C_nv.
\tag{20}
\]

The channel \(\Psi_n\) in this inequality is the same for all \(A\).

We next take a limit **on the original fixed space \(M_d\)**. Canonical channels of the form (17) form a compact set: probability measures on the compact density-matrix space have continuous first and second moments, and the barycenter constraint \(I/d\) is closed. Equivalently, their moments lie in a compact finite-dimensional convex hull. Carathéodory’s theorem gives a finite ensemble for every limiting moment.

Choose a subsequence with \(\Psi_n\to\Psi\). Passing to the limit in (20), using (16), and choosing \(v=H^{-1}\beta\), gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tau(A\Psi(A))
&\ge \beta^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}\beta\\
&=
\sum_{i=1}^m
(2\lambda_i^2-1)\tau(AA_i)^2.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

Because \(A\) was arbitrary, this is full-space domination—not merely domination of the slow-mode compression. In particular, slow/fast cross terms have not been discarded.

The identity direction is fixed by \(\Psi\), and trace preservation eliminates its cross terms with traceless matrices. All remaining eigendirections have zero positive-part weight. Hence
\[
\Psi\succeq(2\Phi^2-\mathsf I)_+.
\]

Finally, for \(-1\le t\le1\),
\[
1-(2t^2-1)_+
\le2(1-t^2)
\le4(1-t).
\]
Functional calculus proves (2) and (3). \(\square\)

# 3. Consequences

## A. The arbitrary-frame comparison closes with constant four

For an arbitrary finite Hermitian frame \(B_1,\ldots,B_q\), define
\[
V=\sum_i\tau(B_i^2),
\qquad
s_\Phi=\sum_i\tau(B_i\Phi(B_i)),
\]
and
\[
k=
\max_{\sum_y p_y\pi_y=I/d}
\sum_{y,i}p_y\operatorname{Tr}(\pi_yB_i)^2,
\]
where the \(\pi_y\) are pure states.

Refining a mixed state into a pure-state ensemble increases every squared-expectation average by Jensen’s inequality, while preserving the barycenter. Thus the comparator’s ensemble can be refined to show
\[
k\ge\sum_i\tau(B_i\Psi(B_i)).
\]
Summing (3) yields
\[
\boxed{V-k\le4(V-s_\Phi).}
\tag{22}
\]

This is the finite-universal-constant statement in the selected frontier record. It is **not** the sharper conjectured inequality \(V-k\le2(V-s_\Phi)\).
