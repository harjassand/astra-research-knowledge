## 3. Exact behavioral quotient and dimension lower bound

Discard any row `i` with `K_i=0` from `(p,R)`; its two actions are constant and its phase cannot affect the remaining star. Below, assume every row is nonzero. Controls may be arbitrary piecewise constant `v` in a set containing a nonempty open subset of `R^d`. Defining the admitted class merely by a linear span without enough admissible control values would not justify the following necessity argument.

Call two initial amplitude states behaviorally equivalent when **all modal action trajectories** agree for **every common admitted prescribed control history**, starting from those states.

**Theorem 3 (minimal information, including singular strata).** Two states are behaviorally equivalent if and only if their `π_K` values coincide.

**Proof.** Equality of `π_K` is sufficient by (3) and Theorem 2. Conversely, equality of output histories includes equality of initial actions. For a constant `v`, equality of first action derivatives implies

\[
(K_i\cdot v)\,\Delta p_i=0.
\]

Since the row is nonzero and the control set has interior, `Δp_i=0`. Equality of second derivatives, using equal actions and `p`, then implies

\[
(K_i\cdot v)\,(\Delta R_i\cdot v)=0
\]

for every `v` in that open set. This is a polynomial identity, so either its first linear factor is zero or the second is zero. The first is nonzero by assumption; hence `ΔR_i=0`. The proof uses no nonvanishing amplitude hypothesis. ∎

This is a statement about exact information/fibers, rather than minimal ambient coordinate count. The displayed state is redundant.

**Corollary 3.1 (continuous state-size floor).** Any continuous exact predictor of this full action behavior, taking values in `R^m`, has `m≥3r+1` on a generic unrestricted initial-state set. If the `r` pair differences `a_i-e_i` and total energy (6) are known fixed parameters, the corresponding generic floor is `m≥2r`.

**Proof.** On a local slice fixing the global phase of a nonzero `c`, the initial actions contribute `2r+1` independent real parameters. The `r` pair-product relative phases contribute `r` more. Away from zero cosine/sine derivatives, `(actions,p)` already has full local rank `3r+1`: each `p_i=|b_i||c| sin θ_i` locally determines its own phase. Nonzero rows make these parameters behaviorally distinguishable by first action derivatives. A continuous predictive state must be injective on this local slice; invariance of dimension forbids its embedding into a lower-dimensional Euclidean space. Fixing the `r+1` independent constants removes `r+1` dimensions. ∎

The original amplitude vector has `4r+2` real coordinates. Its phase symmetry has generic dimension `r+1`, leaving the same `3r+1` generic quotient dimension; reflection (4) changes no dimension. For `d=1`, (3) stores `4r+1` real numbers, almost exactly the original amplitude count. It gains regular invariant information structure, not a large memory reduction.

On an open set where `h` and some `B=Σ_i κ_i b_i` are linearly independent, one can reconstruct the full real Gram matrix rationally from their projections. In the `d=1,K=κ` case set `T=κ^TR`, `P=κ^Tp` and

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}n&-P\\-P&T\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S_{ij}=(-p_i,R_i)M^{-1}(-p_j,R_j)^T.
\tag{7}
\]

Its denominator `nT-P²` is the squared area of the two reference vectors. It fails at the collinear/zero-reference strata where (3) stays regular. For fixed `κ`, `nT-P²=(Re Σ_i κ_i z_i)²` is conserved; this is the square of the familiar cubic Hamiltonian, consistent with the unobservability of its sign under (4).

## 4. What a newly admitted real control actually requires

It is false that **every** direction outside `col K` necessarily requires new state. Known diagonal coherences can sometimes reconstruct all missing directions. There is a sharp information criterion.

Let

\[
\mathcal D_K=\{D=D^T:\operatorname{diag}D=0,\ DK=0,
\ n_+(D)\le2,\ n_-(D)\le2\},
\tag{8}
\]

where `n_+` and `n_-` count positive and negative eigenvalues. This is a finite-dimensional semialgebraic set; testing it is not claimed inexpensive.

**Theorem 4 (exact added-control information criterion).** With every old row active, `π_K` contains sufficient initial information for all controls in `span(K,ℓ)` if and only if

\[
D\ell=0\qquad\hbox{for every }D\in\mathcal D_K.
\tag{9}
\]

Here “sufficient” means that `π_[K ℓ]` is a single-valued function of `π_K`; initialization of the enlarged polynomial predictor is then possible. A globally polynomial vector field in the **same old coordinate list** is additionally guaranteed whenever the reconstruction `Sℓ=H(π_K)` is polynomial, including the linear reconstruction case below. Condition (9) by itself establishes an information factorization and a continuous reconstruction on compact energy shells, rather than a Lipschitz formula at every singular point.

**Proof.** If old states have equal `π_K`, their two real Gram matrices have equal diagonal `a_i e_i`, equal `SK`, and rank at most two. Their difference belongs to (8): its positive inertia cannot exceed the rank of the first positive semidefinite Gram matrix, and similarly for negative inertia. Thus (9) makes their `Sℓ` equal. Theorem 3 applied to `[K ℓ]` gives sufficiency.

If (9) fails, take `D∈D_K` with `Dℓ≠0`, and its positive/negative spectral parts `S_+,S_-`. Each is positive semidefinite of rank at most two and hence is a Gram matrix of real two-vectors, or complex numbers `b_i^±`. Their diagonals agree because `diag D=0`. Realize each by setting `a_i=e_i=√((S_±)_{ii})`, choosing `x_i,y_i` with product `b_i^±`, and setting `c=0`. The resulting states have equal old actions, `p=0`, and `R=S_±K`, but different new `R` columns `S_±ℓ`. By Theorem 3 they are distinguishable with the enlarged controls. Explicitly, select `q=Kv+ℓ` so that `q_i(Dℓ)_i≠0` for an index where `Dℓ` is nonzero; this is possible because old row `K_i` is nonzero. Their initial `i`th action accelerations differ by `-2q_i(Dℓ)_i`. ∎

If an old row vanishes, a new nonzero coupling in that row usually requires its previously discarded `p_i` already at first order. The active-row assumption is essential.

**A practical sufficient linear test.** Let columns of `N` span `ker K^T`. A symmetric matrix with `DK=0` has the form `D=NAN^T`. If

\[
A\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}(NAN^T)
\quad\hbox{is injective on }\operatorname{Sym}(r-d),
\tag{10}
\]

then the known `SK` and `diag S` reconstruct the entire `S` by linear algebra. Every added real control is then supported by the old state, with a polynomial same-coordinate extension. For rank `r-1`, (10) always holds. For `K=(1,1)` it trivially holds; for `K=(1,1,1)`, write `u_i=R_i-S_ii` and recover

\[
S_{12}=(u_1+u_2-u_3)/2,\quad
S_{13}=(u_1+u_3-u_2)/2,\quad
S_{23}=(u_2+u_3-u_1)/2.
\]

Thus even the two-triad example supports arbitrary new real couplings once the old **real** state is acquired.

**First equal-coupling counterexample.** For `r=4,K=(1,1,1,1)^T,c=0`, compare

\[
b^+=(1,1,-1,-1),\qquad b^-=(1,-1,1,-1),\qquad a_i=e_i=1.
\]

Both have `p=0,R=0` and are equilibria under every old control. Add `ℓ=(1,1,0,0)^T`. Then `ℓ^Tb^+=2`, `ℓ^Tb^-=0`, and

\[
\ddot n(0)=2|\ell^Tb|^2=8\quad\hbox{versus}\quad0.
\tag{11}
\]

This is a genuine global control-span barrier. It occurs on the zero-central-mode stratum, while generic rational reconstruction (7) would hide it.
