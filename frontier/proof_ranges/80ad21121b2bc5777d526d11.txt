## 12. Evidence, exact limits and reproducibility

`check_core.py` generated `diagnostics.json` using 110-decimal-digit arithmetic and seeded NumPy/SciPy calculations. It passed 690 multiprecision and 751 floating-point assertions (1,441 total). Eighteen spectral constructions use d from 1 through 24 and eta in {0.125,0.04}. Checks include positive weights, moments, Jacobi prefixes, boundary changes, coefficient floors, connected and rounded target dynamics, and small interacting many-body systems with joint port-control pulses. The controlled simulations check uniform operator bounds over all input vectors on the two central qubits, rather than only one chosen state.

The largest multiprecision matched-moment error was about 1.0e-110; the largest reported Jacobi boundary error was about 2.1e-110. These are finite numerical diagnostics, NOT exact arithmetic, independent proof review, or proof of an untested general statement. The general arguments are Sections 3-9. `check_interfaces.py` additionally passed 1,061 floating-point assertions for the exact-null certificate, off-subspace freedom, far-end readout, and a finite dyadic example. The package therefore contains 2,502 numerical assertions in total; 690 are multiprecision, not exact arithmetic. `example_d4.json` records a ten-qubit finite coefficient instance.

The strongest established scope is a worst-case, task-specific impossibility and an exact-versus-tolerant separation. No matching upper bound for the tolerant problem is claimed. No universal tolerant certifier with all-site local access is excluded. No lower bound under all Gibbs-state preparations, translation invariance, uniform nonzero hopping, natural disorder or generic many-body inputs is proved. Classical computation is unrestricted in the lower bound, so its difficulty is acquiring the one missing bit, not simulating the fully specified small active matrices.
