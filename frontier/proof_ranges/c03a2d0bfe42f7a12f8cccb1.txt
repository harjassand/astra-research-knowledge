### A local residual bound for the lower witnesses

Let `v(t)=e^{νtΔ}u0`, `w=u-v`, and suppose on a chosen interval that

\[
\|\nabla v(t)\|_\infty\le G,
\qquad\|B(v(t),v(t))\|_2\le R.
\]

The error equation tested against `w` loses both `B(v,w)` and `B(w,w)` by incompressibility. The remaining terms give

\[
\tfrac12(\|w\|_2^2)'+\nu\|\nabla w\|_2^2
\le G\|w\|_2^2+R\|w\|_2,
\]

and therefore, using a regularization at zeros of `||w||2`,

\[
\boxed{\|w(t)\|_2\le Rt e^{Gt}.}
\tag{11}
\]

For any finite Fourier set `D`, if

\[
\|P_D[B(u,u)-B(v,v)]\|_2\le H\|w\|_2,
\]

the difference from the second Picard term

\[
V_D(t)=-\int_0^t e^{\nu(t-s)\Delta}P_DB(v(s),v(s))\,ds
\]

satisfies, provided `P_Du0=0`,

\[
\boxed{\|P_Du(t)-V_D(t)\|_2\le\tfrac12HRt^2e^{Gt}.}
\tag{12}
\]

This is an estimate for the exact PDE. No Fourier-support invariance is assumed after time zero.

## 4. The four-mode A5 pair at a nonzero time

Let `N=K+2`, `p=(N,0)`, `q=(-N+1,1)`, `k=(1,1)`, `r=p-q`, and

\[
P=N^2,\quad Q=(N-1)^2+1,\quad S=P+Q,
\quad\alpha=E/3,\quad a=\alpha N.
\]

Take `ω_{±p}=a`, `ω_{±q}=σa`, `σ∈{−1,+1}`, all other coefficients zero, and `u_j=-ij^⊥ω_j/|j|²`. The fields are real and `P_Ku0=0`. For `N≥3`,

\[
\|u_0\|_2^2=2\alpha^2(1+P/Q)<9\alpha^2=E^2.
\]

The two ordered Fourier interactions of `p,q` give

\[
c=a^2N(Q^{-1}-P^{-1})
={2\alpha^2N(N-1)\over Q}\ge2\alpha^2.
\tag{13}
\]

For the heat flow, the vorticity of `−B(v,v)` has only the four coefficients at `±k,±r`, with values `σc e^{-νSt}` at `±k` and `−σc e^{-νSt}` at `±r`. In particular,

\[
\|B(v,v)\|_2=Fc e^{-\nu St},
\qquad F=\sqrt{1+2/|r|^2}\le2,
\quad\|\nabla v\|_\infty\le4\alpha N.
\]

Write

\[
I(t)=\int_0^t e^{-2\nu(t-s)}e^{-\nu Ss}\,ds
={e^{-2\nu t}-e^{-\nu St}\over\nu(S-2)}.
\]

The leading coefficient is `σcI(t)`. By (6) applied to the difference of the two quadratic tensors, its vorticity remainder obeys

\[
|\omega_k(t)-\sigma cI(t)|
\le6\alpha Fc\,t^2e^{4\alpha Nt}.
\tag{14}
\]

Indeed the coefficient difference is at most
`|k|²(||u||2+||v||2)||w||2≤12α||w||2`, and (11) gives (14).

Set

\[
\tau_A=\min\left\{T,{1\over2\nu S},{1\over32\alpha N}\right\}
=\min\left\{T,{1\over2\nu S},{3\over32EN}\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]

For `t≤τ_A`, `I(t)≥t e^{-1/2}`, `4αNt≤1/8`, and the ratio of the bound (14) to `cI(t)` is at most
`6Fαt e^{5/8}≤24αt≤1/4` because `N≥3`. Thus

\[
\boxed{e_{K,k}^0(T;E,\nu)\ge|\omega_k^+(\tau_A)|
\ge {E^2\tau_A\over12}.}
\tag{16}
\]

The two exact target trajectories are negatives: translation by `(0,π)` changes `σ` and multiplies `ω_k` by `−1`. This justifies the minimax lower, rather than merely a lower on one solution.

For comparison, (6) gives `|curl B_k|≤2X`, so (5) gives the all-input fixed-output upper

\[
\boxed{e_{K,k}^0(T;E,\nu)
\le\min\left\{E,2E^2T,{2E^2e^{50(E/\nu)^2}\over\nu(K+1)^2}\right\}.}
\tag{17}
\]

If `νN≥(8/3)E`, both caps in (15) are at least `1/(4νN²)`: use `S≤2N²` for the viscous cap and the displayed hypothesis for the nonlinear cap. Thus (16) implies the convenient bound

\[
e_{K,k}^0\ge {E^2\over12}\min\{T,(4\nu N^2)^{-1}\}.
\tag{18}
\]

Equations (17)–(18) match the cutoff and short-time powers for fixed `E,ν` as `K→∞`. They are not a uniform sharp Reynolds-number law. The derivative lower alone was insufficient; (14) is the missing nonlinear-remainder step.
