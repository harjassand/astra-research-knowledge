# Independent audit of the universal near-kernel cubic criterion

Date: 16 October 2026. Audit role: adversarial, source-only first stage.

**Verdict.** For each fixed `sigma>0`, the proposed fifteen-dimensional class is correct for smooth periodic compatible gradients, and for periodic `W^{1,4}` gradients. The universal signed little-o property is equivalent to the corresponding absolute-integral little-o property. The five excluded coefficients are exactly those of `b^3`, `a*b^2`, `q*b^2`, `c*b^2`, and `a^2*b` in the coordinates `(a,b,c,q)`. I find no additional signed cancellation or homogeneous cubic null Lagrangian that enlarges this class.

This is an internal analytic reconstruction, not specialist review, formal verification, historical-priority clearance, or a proof of the source's sharp Jensen inequality. The source target cubic belongs to the class. The exact rational checks below concern explicitly declared fields and polynomial moments; they do not machine-verify interpolation or uniform integrability.

## 1. Exact statement and domain

Use normalized averages on the fixed square torus `(R/2pi Z)^2`. Write

\[
D\phi=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}f_x&f_y\\g_x&g_y\end{pmatrix},\qquad
q=\sigma a+d,\qquad T_\sigma\phi=(q,c).
\]

Let `P` be a fixed real homogeneous cubic. Its universal near-kernel property is:

> For every sequence of compatible gradients with bounded `L4` norm, uniformly integrable fourth powers, and `epsilon_n=||T_sigma phi_n||_2 -> 0`, one has `<P(Dphi_n)>/epsilon_n -> 0` along indices where `epsilon_n>0`.

All parameters, including `sigma`, are fixed. No uniform statement as `sigma -> 0` is made. Uniform integrability means uniform integrability of the scalar functions `|Dphi_n|^4` on this fixed finite-measure domain.

The exact periodic-potential or zero-boundary hypothesis is essential. If "compatible" means only row-curl-free fields with arbitrary harmonic or affine parts, the claimed class is false: constant gradients `diag(1,-sigma+epsilon)` have `q=epsilon`, `c=0`, uniformly bounded fourth powers, and `a^3/||T||_2=1/epsilon`. Those matrices are gradients of affine maps, but not of periodic potentials or zero-boundary maps. The classification here uses the source's exact admissible class.

The equivalent algebraic condition is

\[
\boxed{[b^3]P=[ab^2]P=[qb^2]P=[cb^2]P=[a^2b]P=0.}
\tag{1}
\]

There are twenty homogeneous cubic monomials in four independent linear coordinates. The five displayed coordinate functionals are independent, so the class has dimension fifteen. Equivalently,

\[
P=\lambda a^3+qR_2(a,b,c,q)+cS_2(a,b,c,q),
\tag{2}
\]

where `R_2` and `S_2` are homogeneous quadratics that vanish on the pure `b` axis. This decomposition need not be unique.

The proof below also gives the same classification for the zero-boundary class on any fixed bounded planar open domain containing a ball, by the explicit cutoff construction in Section 6.

## 2. Sufficiency, including the absolute version

Let `epsilon_n=||(q_n,c_n)||_2` and `M=sup_n ||Dphi_n||_4 < infinity`. Compatibility and periodicity give the determinant-null identity

\[
0=\langle a_nd_n-b_nc_n\rangle,
\qquad
\sigma\|a_n\|_2^2=\langle a_nq_n-b_nc_n\rangle.
\tag{3}
\]

Consequently `||a_n||_2^2 <= C_sigma M epsilon_n -> 0`. Also `c_n -> 0` and `q_n -> 0` in `L2`, and `d_n=q_n-sigma*a_n -> 0` in `L2`. Thus `a_n,c_n,d_n,q_n -> 0` in measure. Uniform integrability of `|Dphi_n|^4`, inherited by these fixed linear combinations, gives

\[
\delta_n:=\|a_n\|_4+\|c_n\|_4+\|q_n\|_4\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]

Every monomial in a quadratic vanishing on the `b` axis has at least one factor from `(a,c,q)`. Hence

\[
\|R_2(D\phi_n)\|_2+\|S_2(D\phi_n)\|_2
\le C_{P,\sigma}M\delta_n\longrightarrow0.
\tag{5}
\]

Hölder then controls the absolute integrals of the two defect terms by `C epsilon_n M delta_n`.

The remaining `a^3` term requires the refined interpolation estimate from the frozen source, rather than only the source's coarse cubic estimate:

\[
\|a\|_{8/3}^2\le C_\sigma\|T_\sigma\phi\|_2\|D\phi\|_4.
\tag{6}
\]

For clarity, its mechanism is a Hodge decomposition

\[
(\sigma f,g)=\nabla\chi+(\psi_y,-\psi_x),\qquad\Delta\chi=q,
\]

with `sigma*a=chi_xx+psi_xy`, `sigma*b=chi_xy+psi_yy`, and `c=chi_xy-psi_xx`. Riesz bounds give `||psi_xx||_2 <= C epsilon`, `||psi_yy||_4 <= C_sigma ||Dphi||_4`, and mixed-derivative interpolation gives `||psi_xy||_{8/3}^2 <= C ||psi_xx||_2 ||psi_yy||_4`. The `chi_xx` term obeys the same estimate by ordinary `L2`/`L4` interpolation. This proves (6), using the classical mixed-derivative input stated and proved in the source.

Apply Hölder with `2/(8/3)+1/4=1`:

\[
\frac{\|a_n^3\|_1}{\epsilon_n}
\le\frac{\|a_n\|_{8/3}^2\|a_n\|_4}{\epsilon_n}
\le C_\sigma M\|a_n\|_4\longrightarrow0.
\tag{7}
\]

Together (2), (5), and (7) prove the stronger result

\[
\boxed{\frac{\|P(D\phi_n)\|_1}{\epsilon_n}\longrightarrow0.}
\tag{8}
\]

Smooth approximation justifies the identities and interpolation for periodic `W^{1,4}` gradients. No rate depending only on `M` is asserted; the uniform-integrability assumption supplies the convergence in (4), without a specified modulus.

The extra exclusion of `a^2*b` is real. Estimate (6) only makes that monomial `O(epsilon_n)` because its remaining factor is `b_n`, which need not tend to zero. The source target has `a^3`, whose remaining factor does tend to zero.

## 3. Necessity: exact compatible witnesses

Write the five forbidden coefficients as `alpha_b3`, `alpha_ab2`, `alpha_qb2`, `alpha_cb2`, and `alpha_a2b`. All fields below are smooth and periodic. Their gradients have a uniform `Linfinity` bound, so the required fourth-power uniform integrability holds automatically.

### 3.1 The pure-shear cubic, with a nonzero denominator

For `epsilon>0`, take

\[
f_\epsilon=\sin y+\tfrac12\sin2y,\qquad
g_\epsilon=\epsilon\sin x.
\]

Then `a=q=d=0`, `b=cos y+cos2y`, `c=epsilon*cos x`, and

\[
\|T\phi_\epsilon\|_2=\epsilon/\sqrt2,\qquad
\langle P(D\phi_\epsilon)\rangle=\tfrac34\alpha_{b3}
\tag{9}
\]

**exactly for every homogeneous cubic `P`**. Every other potentially surviving monomial has zero average. Thus the required ratio cannot tend to zero unless `alpha_b3=0`. An unperturbed exact-kernel field would not suffice because the property is only tested at nonzero denominators; this perturbation handles that quantifier explicitly.

### 3.2 The `q*b^2` coefficient

Take

\[
f_\epsilon=\sin y,\qquad g_\epsilon=\tfrac\epsilon2\sin2y.
\]

Then `a=c=0`, `b=cos y`, `q=d=epsilon*cos2y`, and

\[
\|T\phi_\epsilon\|_2=\epsilon/\sqrt2,\qquad
\langle P(D\phi_\epsilon)\rangle=\tfrac\epsilon4\alpha_{qb2}.
\tag{10}
\]

This too isolates its coefficient exactly for every `P`. The normalized limit is `sqrt(2)*alpha_qb2/4`, forcing `alpha_qb2=0`.

### 3.3 A common compatible anisotropic construction

Given a fixed trigonometric polynomial `psi`, and any positive integer `N`, define

\[
f_N=N^{-1}\psi_y(x,Ny),\qquad
g_N=-\sigma N^{-2}\psi_x(x,Ny).
\tag{11}
\]

Put `A=psi_xy`, `B=psi_yy`, and `X=psi_xx`, evaluated at `(x,Ny)`. Then exactly

\[
a_N=N^{-1}A,\quad b_N=B,\quad
c_N=-\sigma N^{-2}X,\quad d_N=-\sigma a_N,\quad q_N=0,
\]

\[
\|T\phi_N\|_2=\sigma N^{-2}\sqrt{\langle X^2\rangle}.
\tag{12}
\]

These are actual gradients of periodic potentials, not separately prescribed gradient distributions. Their moments are independent of the vertical reparametrization `(x,y)->(x,Ny)`.

The following exact table supplies three seeds. It is verified using only rational Fourier arithmetic in `verify_exact_witnesses.py`.

| Seed | `<B^3>` | `<AB^2>` | `<XB^2>` | `<A^2 B>` | `<X^2>` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `psi_ab` | 0 | 3/2 | 5/4 | 1/2 | 5/8 |
| `psi_cb` | 0 | 0 | -1/2 | 0 | 1 |
| `psi_a2b` | 0 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 544627/13122 |

The seeds are

\[
\psi_{ab}=-\cos(x+y)-\cos y-\tfrac12\cos(x+2y)+\cos2y,
\tag{13}
\]

\[
\psi_{cb}=\cos x+\cos y+\cos(x+y),
\tag{14}
\]

\[
\begin{split}
\psi_{a2b}={}&\cos(x+y)+\cos(x-y)+\cos2y-\tfrac1{32}\cos4y\\
&-\tfrac8{729}\cos3x+\cos3y+\cos(3x+3y).
\end{split}
\tag{15}
\]

All coefficients and frequencies are exact. In (13), the added vertical `cos2y` cancels the `b^3` average of the source's original three-mode seed without changing the other table entries.

### 3.4 The `a*b^2` coefficient

Use (11) with (13). The exact leading expansion is

\[
\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle
=\frac{3\alpha_{ab2}}{2N}
+\frac{\alpha_{a2b}/2-5\sigma\alpha_{cb2}/4}{N^2}
+O_P(N^{-3}).
\tag{16}
\]

Both `b^3` and every `q` monomial have zero average here. Divide by `sigma*N^{-2}*sqrt(5/8)`. A nonzero `alpha_ab2` produces a term proportional to `N`, which cannot be cancelled by the bounded remaining terms. Thus `alpha_ab2=0`.

### 3.5 The `c*b^2` coefficient

Use (11) with (14). All the other forbidden moments vanish exactly. Therefore

\[
\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle
=\frac{\sigma\alpha_{cb2}}{2N^2}+O_P(N^{-3}),\qquad
\frac{\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle}{\|T\phi_N\|_2}
\longrightarrow\tfrac12\alpha_{cb2}.
\tag{17}
\]

This forces `alpha_cb2=0` independently of the other forbidden coefficients.

### 3.6 The `a^2*b` coefficient

Use (11) with (15). Every other forbidden moment vanishes exactly, including `<c*b^2>`. Therefore

\[
\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle
=\frac{2\alpha_{a2b}}{N^2}+O_P(N^{-3}),
\]

\[
\frac{\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle}{\|T\phi_N\|_2}
\longrightarrow
\frac{2\alpha_{a2b}}{\sigma\sqrt{544627/13122}}.
\tag{18}
\]

This forces `alpha_a2b=0` without relying on a sequential elimination of `alpha_cb2`.

The seven-mode seed is intentionally redundant enough to isolate this coefficient. Starting with `cos(x+y)+cos(x-y)+cos2y` gives `<B^3>=-6`, `<XB^2>=-4`, and `<A^2 B>=2`. Adding `-(1/32)cos4y` cancels `<B^3>` while preserving the last two moments. The separate triad `-(8/729)cos3x+cos3y+cos(3x+3y)` contributes `+4` to `<XB^2>` and zero to `<A^2 B>`, so it removes the remaining bad coefficient. The exact checker verifies the complete sum, including mixed resonances, rather than assuming that its components decouple.

Equations (9), (10), and (16)--(18) prove all five necessary coefficient conditions. Together with Section 2, they establish the exact classification.

## 4. Cancellation traps and signed versus absolute

1. **Exact-kernel tests miss four coefficients.** Vanishing on the pure-shear kernel only removes `b^3`. The three scales `b=O(1)`, `a=O(N^-1)`, and `c=O(N^-2)` reveal additional obstructions.
2. **A single anisotropic seed can hide a forbidden combination.** For the simple seed `cos(x+y)+cos(x-y)+cos2y`, the order `N^-2` contribution is `4*sigma*alpha_cb2+2*alpha_a2b`. That seed alone cannot distinguish combinations satisfying `alpha_a2b=-2*sigma*alpha_cb2`. The source's original three-mode seed has a different mixture, `alpha_a2b/2-5*sigma*alpha_cb2/4`. Neither isolated cancellation proves admissibility. The separate fields (14) and (15) remove this trap entirely.
3. **Ordinary first variations miss `c*b^2`.** Around a fixed pure shear `b=B(y)`, one has `<g_x B(y)^2>=0`. This identity does not imply a universal little-o bound: the anisotropic compatible sequence (14) has a nonzero normalized limit.
4. **`q*b^2` cannot be absorbed into a compatible cancellation.** The one-dimensional field (10) isolates it exactly.
5. **Concentration is unnecessary for these obstructions.** Every necessity witness is uniformly bounded pointwise. Thus uniform integrability does not make the source's entire eighteen-dimensional finite-estimate class into a little-o class; it removes precisely three additional directions, `q*b^2`, `c*b^2`, and `a^2*b`.

The signed property implies the coefficient conditions using signed exact averages. Those conditions imply the absolute property (8). Conversely, the absolute property immediately implies the signed one. Thus their admissible polynomial spaces coincide. This does not claim equality of signed and absolute optimal constants in a finite estimate, and it does not justify replacing a sharp signed variational estimate by an absolute estimate with the same constant.

## 5. No nonzero homogeneous cubic null Lagrangian

Here a cubic null Lagrangian means a homogeneous cubic `N(A)` whose average is zero for every smooth periodic gradient. There is no nonzero such cubic in the two-by-two first-gradient setting. The following elementary argument avoids importing a classification theorem.

For every integer covector `xi != 0` and vector `v`, take

\[
\phi=v\big(\sin(\xi\cdot x)+\tfrac12\sin(2\xi\cdot x)\big).
\]

Then `Dphi=(v tensor xi)*(cos(theta)+cos(2theta))`, and its cubic scalar moment is `3/4`. Hence `N(v tensor xi)=0`. Density of rational covector directions and continuity imply that `N` vanishes on every rank-one matrix. Algebraically, a homogeneous cubic vanishing on the two-by-two determinant hypersurface has the form

\[
N(A)=\det(A)(\lambda_a a+\lambda_b b+\lambda_c c+\lambda_d d).
\tag{19}
\]

The determinant is irreducible; alternatively, substitute `d=bc/a` for `a != 0` and perform polynomial division.

Take `f=sin x+sin(x+sign*y)`, `g=sin y` with `sign=+1,-1`, then interchange the two outputs. Their exact moment rows, in columns `(<a det>,<b det>,<c det>,<d det>)`, are

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/4&0&0\\
1/2&-1/4&0&0\\
0&0&-1/2&-1/4\\
0&0&-1/2&1/4
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{20}
\]

This matrix has determinant `1/16`, forcing every coefficient in (19) to vanish. The rational Fourier checker verifies these rows. In particular, multiplying the quadratic determinant null Lagrangian by a gradient entry does not produce a cubic null Lagrangian. Such products cannot silently rescue a forbidden combination.

There can of course be differential identities involving derivatives of the gradient or nonlocal representations. Those are not additional homogeneous cubics with zero average for every compatible gradient, and the explicit necessity witnesses already rule out their enlarging the class in (1).

## 6. Transfer of necessity to zero-boundary fields

This section is analytic and is not part of the finite Fourier receipt. It also shows how to avoid treating a periodic field as an automatically admissible zero-boundary field.

Fix a nonnegative, nonzero `eta` in `C_c^infinity(Omega)` for a bounded planar open domain `Omega`. For a stream seed above, use integers `M -> infinity`, set `N=M^2`, and define

\[
S_M(x,y)=N^{-2}\eta(x,y)\psi(Mx,Ny),\qquad
f_M=(S_M)_y,\quad g_M=-\sigma(S_M)_x.
\tag{21}
\]

These maps are compactly supported and satisfy `q_M=0` exactly. Uniformly on `Omega`,

\[
a_M=\eta M^{-1}A(Mx,Ny)+O(M^{-2}),\quad
b_M=\eta B(Mx,Ny)+O(M^{-2}),
\]

\[
c_M=-\sigma\eta M^{-2}X(Mx,Ny)+O(M^{-3}).
\tag{22}
\]

They remain bounded in `Linfinity`. Smooth weighted Fourier averaging gives

\[
\|T\phi_M\|_2\sim \sigma M^{-2}
\sqrt{\langle\eta^2\rangle_\Omega\langle X^2\rangle_{\mathbb T^2}}.
\tag{23}
\]

Once `b^3` and `a*b^2` have been eliminated, (22) gives the same nonzero limits for the `c*b^2` and `a^2*b` tests, multiplied by the positive factor `<eta^3>_Omega/sqrt(<eta^2>_Omega)`. All cutoff errors divided by `||T||_2` tend to zero. For the `a*b^2` test, after eliminating `b^3`, its leading order is `M^-1`, while all competing terms and cutoff errors are `O(M^-2)`, so its normalized ratio still diverges.

For the first elimination, take

\[
f_N=\eta N^{-1}(\sin Ny+\tfrac12\sin2Ny),\qquad g_N=0.
\]

Then `b_N=eta*(cos Ny+cos2Ny)+O(N^-1)`, `T=(sigma*eta_x*N^-1*(sin Ny+(1/2)sin2Ny),0)`, and `<b_N^3> -> (3/4)<eta^3>_Omega != 0`; all other cubic terms contain a small factor. The denominator is positive and comparable to `N^-1`, since a nonzero compact cutoff has nonzero `eta_x`.

For `q*b^2`, take

\[
f_N=\eta N^{-1}\sin Ny,\qquad
g_N=\eta\epsilon_N(2N)^{-1}\sin2Ny,\qquad\epsilon_N=N^{-1/2}.
\]

Here `q_N=epsilon_N*eta*cos2Ny+O(N^-1)`, `c_N=O(epsilon_N/N)`, and `b_N=eta*cosNy+O(N^-1)`. Thus `<q_N b_N^2>/epsilon_N -> (1/4)<eta^3>_Omega`, while `||T||_2/epsilon_N -> sqrt(<eta^2>_Omega/2)`. After eliminating `b^3`, the other forbidden directions are negligible in this test. This completes a sequential necessity proof in the compact zero-boundary class as well.

## 7. Source target and limits

For fixed `r>0`, `sigma=r+1` and `kappa=r(r+2)/3`, the source cubic density is

\[
P_r=2a q^2-(r+2)a^2q+r c^2q-2bcq-\kappa a^3-\kappa ac^2.
\tag{24}
\]

It satisfies all five conditions. The strengthened conclusion is therefore

\[
\frac{\|P_r(D\phi_n)\|_1}{\|T_\sigma\phi_n\|_2}\to0
\]

under the stated boundedness and uniform-integrability hypotheses. Since `kappa>0` is fixed, `sqrt(Q_r)` and `||T_sigma||_2` are equivalent, so this also recovers the source's signed nonconcentration statement.

This does not control concentrating sequences, identify the sharp constant in `C_r^2 <= 4 A_r Q_r`, prove conformal-background quasiconvexity, or extend a conformal conclusion to all backgrounds. The fifteen-dimensional criterion is an exact qualitative near-kernel classification under uniform integrability, not a sharp variational theorem.

follow from exactly the proof in Section 2, applied to a single map rather than only a sequence. Its necessity proof is valid as a sequential elimination. The completely isolated field (15) supplies an additional check independent of this order.

**The improved heat bound (A8) checks algebraically and analytically.** To avoid a notation collision, put

\[
F=\langle a^4\rangle,\quad G=\langle b^4\rangle,
\quad G_0=\langle(E_xb)^4\rangle,\quad H=\langle a^2b^2\rangle,
\]

\[
D=\langle(q-E_xq)^2+c^2\rangle.
\]

The source's compatible horizontal-heat identity pairs `(a_t*a_{t,x},-a_t*b_{t,x})` with `(q_{t,x},c_{t,x})`. The terminal state is exactly `(a_t,b_t)->(0,E_xb)`, because `E_xa=0`. Retaining the terminal value of the source's quartic budget gives, for each `theta>0`,

\[
\int_0^\infty\langle a_t^2(a_{t,x}^2+b_{t,x}^2)\rangle\,dt
\le \frac{1+\theta}{12}F+\frac12 H+\frac{G-G_0}{3\theta}.
\tag{26}
\]

Conditional Jensen gives `G-G_0>=0`. The exact quadratic heat dissipation is

\[
\int_0^\infty\langle q_{t,x}^2+c_{t,x}^2\rangle\,dt=\frac12D,
\tag{27}
\]

using `E_xc=0`. The horizontal mean of `q` may be nonzero as a function of `y`; it must be removed, as the other report correctly does. Space-time Cauchy--Schwarz contributes `(6/sigma)^2 * (D/2)`, giving `18D/sigma^2` times the right side of (26). Finally,

\[
\inf_{\theta>0}\left(\frac{\theta F}{12}+\frac{G-G_0}{3\theta}\right)
=\frac13\sqrt{F(G-G_0)}.
\]

Therefore the reported improvement is valid:

\[
\boxed{|\langle a^3\rangle|^2
\le\frac{18}{\sigma^2}D
\left[\frac F{12}+\frac H2+\frac13\sqrt{F(G-G_0)}\right].}
\tag{28}
\]

The numerical factor, terminal subtraction, and one-sided limiting cases all check. If `F=0`, the left side is zero. If `G=G_0`, conditional Jensen implies that `b` is horizontally constant; the bound follows by letting `theta` decrease to zero in (26). If `D=0`, compatibility forces `a=0`, so there is no degenerate contradiction. Positive-time smoothing and the source's quartic/quadratic budgets provide the same absolutely convergent Sobolev justification as for the original heat identity. This strengthening retains a signed cubic estimate and does not imply the sharp target inequality.

**The target-specific first-column refinement (A15) also checks.** In (24), every quadratic factor paired with a defect component contains either `a` or `c`. \|P_r(D\phi)\|_1
\le K_r\|(q,c)\|_2\|D\phi\|_4\|(a,c)\|_4.

Only uniform integrability of `(a^2+c^2)^2` is needed for the target's little-o conclusion. The determinant identity still makes `(a,c)` converge to zero in measure, and this restricted uniform integrability upgrades just that column to strong `L4`. No analogous first-column-only conclusion is claimed for every member of the fifteen-dimensional class: a term such as `q^3` may require control of `q` itself.
