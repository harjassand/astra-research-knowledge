5. Multiple loads require more than individually accurate solves
This continuation exposed an additional failure mode: exact feasibility and small error for each individual load do not suffice for arbitrary load combinations.
For example, take
\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad H=I,
\qquad
F=
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&\rho\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The exactly feasible approximate load frame
\[
\widehat X=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1\\
0&\rho\\
\eta&-\eta
\end{pmatrix}
\]
has small error in each column. But for \(a=(1,-1)\), the true energy is \(\rho^2\), while the approximate energy is
\[
\rho^2+4\eta^2.
\]
The relative error diverges as \(\rho\to0\).
Uniform load-frame construction
Instead, construct a repaired frame \(\widehat X\) satisfying
\[
A\widehat X=F
\]
and the matrix inequality
\[
\boxed{
(\widehat X-X)^\top H(\widehat X-X)
\preceq\eta^2Q_0.
}
\tag{13}
\]
This can be enforced without knowing future load coefficients. A certified lower bound \(c_-I\preceq Q_0\), followed by sufficiently accurate column solves, is enough: require each repaired column error to be at most \(\eta\sqrt{c_-/d}\) in energy norm.
For rational input, a positive bound \(c_-\) can be constructed using finite matrix bounds and exact elimination. Its acquisition and the additional precision are preprocessing costs. Nearly dependent load bases may require more bits; they are not declared well-conditioned for free.
Store
\[
\widehat D=C\widehat X,\qquad
\widehat Q_0=\widehat X^\top H\widehat X.
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
Q_0\preceq\widehat Q_0\preceq(1+\eta^2)Q_0.
\tag{14}
\]
Moreover, for every \(a,S\),
\[
e_S=(\widehat D_S-D_S)a
\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_S),
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
e_S^\top\Gamma_S^+e_S\le\eta^2a^\top Q_0a.
}
\tag{15}
\]
The proof is the minimum-energy extension inequality: \((\widehat X-X)a\) is itself a homogeneous feasible extension of \(e_S\).
Consequently,
\[
y-\widehat D_Sa\in\operatorname{range}(\widehat\Gamma_S)
\iff
y-D_Sa\in\operatorname{range}(\Gamma_S).
\tag{16}
\]
The full affine feasible domain—not just its dimension—is preserved exactly.
Uniform energy approximation
Define
\[
\widehat{\mathcal E}_S(a,y)
=
a^\top\widehat Q_0a+
(y-\widehat D_Sa)^\top
\widehat\Gamma_S^+
(y-\widehat D_Sa).
\]
Writing \(c=a^\top Q_0a\) and
\(v=(y-D_Sa)^\top\Gamma_S^+(y-D_Sa)\), equation (15) bounds the centering error by
\[
2\eta\sqrt{cv}+\eta^2c
\le\eta(c+v)+\eta^2c.
\]
Combining this with (12)–(14) gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{1-\eta-2\eta^2}{1+\theta}\,
\mathcal E_S
\le
\widehat{\mathcal E}_S
\le
\frac{1+\eta+2\eta^2}{1-\theta}\,
\mathcal E_S.
}
\tag{17}
