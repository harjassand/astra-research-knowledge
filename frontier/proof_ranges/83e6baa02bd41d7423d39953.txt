## What the cited ZnS experiments actually measured

| Study and source figures | Material and defect geometry | Stimulus and response | What remained afterward |
|---|---|---|---|
| Li et al., *Nature Materials* 22, 958–963 (2023), Figs. 1–2; Extended Data Figs. 2, 7–8; Supplementary Figs. 1–6, 9–13 and Videos 1–3 | Single-crystal sphalerite ZnS flakes. The field tests identify 30° and 90° partials. In the multi-line test, mobile B/D are 30° S-core partials with $b=a/6[2\bar{1}1]$; stationary A/C/E are 90° partials with $b=a/6[11\bar{2}]$. A separate moving 30° Zn-core line is identified with $b=a/6[1\bar{2}1]$. | A tungsten tip applied a slowly ramped field. The 30° Zn-core example began moving near 51 V, moved about 82.1 nm reversibly, and reached 306.4 nm/s maximum velocity. The authors give a field estimate of $2.4\times10^7$ V/m in the main text and Supplementary Table 2, while the Supplementary Fig. 3 caption says about $10^7$ V/m; report this as order $10^7$ V/m. For B/D, onset was around 100–104 V; the estimated local threshold was $5.1$–$6.9\times10^6$ V/m, peak drive 149 V, maximum marked-point displacement 292.6 nm, and maximum velocity 623.5 nm/s. Current was about $10^{-8}$ A in the first test and below $8\times10^{-8}$ A in the second; voltage ramp was about 1 V/s. Displacements were nonlinear because of pinning and kink motion; Supplementary Fig. 9 also shows oppositely moving dislocations in the same specimen under the same field. | At zero bias the lines stayed at their displaced positions; reversing the field moved them back. This is retained *position*, not retained defect removal. B/D approached A/C and were described as adjacent, but no reaction or line elimination was reported; the mobile partials later reversed. Extended Data Fig. 7 shows motion with the TEM beam off for about seven minutes while −149 V was applied for about 1.5 minutes. Extended Data Fig. 8 shows static dissociation structures, including partials whose projected Burgers vectors close; it is not a field-driven reaction. The test temperature is not stated in the inspected main or supplementary text. |
| Fonseca Montenegro et al., *Physical Review Materials* 10, 025003 (2026), Figs. 1–7, Tables 1–2, Appendix VIII.2 | Tensile ZnS/GaP(001), mismatch $f=0.56\%$, with {111} glide-set 60°/120° misfit dislocations and associated threading segments. The source assigns $b=a/2\langle110\rangle$; line direction [110] identifies S-rich, negative cores, while [\(\bar{1}10\)] identifies Zn-rich, positive cores. Each misfit/thread pair shares a plane and Burgers vector; the other thread in the half-loop is a neutral screw. The systematic optical study uses a 20 nm undoped film with about 6.5 threading dislocations/µm². | A 365 nm continuous-wave LED was used at 0.07, 0.09, 0.29, and 0.67 mW/cm². Films were annealed at 350 °C under the selected light condition, cooled to room temperature, and then imaged by 30 kV, 2.4 nA ECCI. Fig. 2 covers 148 misfit dislocations in eight before/after micrographs across four sequential glide cycles. Because most lines were pinned, the authors use the longest line changes to estimate unpinned glide mobility and display the full distribution. In the power series, Zn-core mobility falls by more than a factor of two and S-core mobility is comparatively flat; the Zn/S mobility ratio changes from over 10 at low power to nearly 1 at the highest power. The light/dark check in Appendix VIII.2 reports Zn-core versus S-core glide reductions of about 100× versus 5× in 20 nm films and 10× versus 2× in 25 nm films. The response is therefore selective but not an all-or-nothing switch; the weaker 25 nm response is attributed by the authors to more pinning. | After each light/heat cycle, the changed MD lengths are still visible after cooling. This is a persistent network change, but the observed effect is glide/strain relaxation, not identified annihilation or repair. In the higher-defect 25 nm film the mobility control is smaller. Exposure duration, integrated fluence, absorbed fraction, and carrier density for the optical series are not reported. The 365 nm conditions correspond by arithmetic to roughly $1.3\times10^{14}$–$1.2\times10^{15}$ *incident* photons cm⁻² s⁻¹; fluence cannot be calculated without exposure time. |

The field study also compares a modeled 3 V/nm field in its atomistic barrier calculation with experimental estimates around $10^7$ V/m. That modeled field is roughly 125–500 times the experimental field scale, so its calculated barrier reduction is not a quantitative experimental barrier measurement at the reported test field. The authors themselves note temperature dependence as open. The first field test’s inconsistent field estimates are another reason not to quote a precise universal threshold.

The optical paper reports a separate growth result, not a dynamic repair operation. Its 30 nm Al-doped ZnS films were grown on GaP. At $N_{Al}=4\times10^{17}$ cm⁻³, the reported Zn-core and S-core misfit line densities are 9.1 and 5.3 µm⁻¹; at $4\times10^{19}$ cm⁻³ they are 0.9 and 5.0 µm⁻¹. This is about a 10.1-fold Zn-core reduction while S-core density stays near its earlier value. The authors explicitly distinguish donor doping from conventional current- or field-modulated electroplasticity and state that the measurements do not provide direct spectroscopic proof of the proposed core mechanism. It is a strong same-material growth comparator, but not evidence of post-growth carrier-gated annihilation.

The factor-of-20 and factor-of-5 relative mobility shifts above are arithmetic from the paper’s stated dark/light reductions: $100/5=20$ for 20 nm and $10/2=5$ for 25 nm. They are *not* reaction-probability gains. The reproducibility appendix uses two film thicknesses and binary light/dark comparisons but does not state an independent-sample count for each condition in the accessible v2 text. The main power sweep uses sequential cycles at one location; the cycle history and changing strain matter even though the authors normalize mobility by measured strain.
