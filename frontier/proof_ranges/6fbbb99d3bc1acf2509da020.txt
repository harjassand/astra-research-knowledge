## Decisive matched experiment

First decide whether product-phase renewal, rather than applied potential, is what extends Sn extraction. Use the same 30 g nominal Fe–0.5 wt% Cu–0.2 wt% Sn–0.2 wt% Ni–0.2 wt% Cr charge and 60 g total slag at 1873 K, the same 7200 s equilibration, 2 L/min gas, and 3600 s total contact time. Compare a single 60 g static slag contact with two sequential 30 g fresh-slag contacts (each 5 wt% FeS; 1.5 g FeS per stage), removing and retaining the first spent slag after 1800 s. Total slag and FeS input, temperature-time, and total contact time are then matched; confirm the half-charge slag still covers the metal and preserves the same exposed interface. Run both arms at open circuit first, with randomized order and at least three independent charges per arm. If slag renewal passes, repeat as a 2×2 with a constant-potential arm based on the published −1 V screen; measure actual WE–CE voltage and full electrical energy. Do not infer pulse benefit from this constant-potential comparison.

For every run, weigh and assay the initial/final metal, both slag outputs, crucible wash, electrode deposits, and a captured gas/condensate train for total Fe, Sn, Cu, Ni, and S. Identify the Sn-bearing phase/species; report analytical limits and recovery uncertainty. Measure full-cell \(\int V_{WE-CE}I\,dt\), furnace energy, gas and slag use, processing time, and product throughput. The candidate survives only if a larger **closed** Sn inventory reaches captured products at the same Fe recovery and energy/material budget than in the static baseline; if staged refresh only changes concentration while the missing Fe/product balance persists, or gives no matched advantage, reject the mechanism. The comparison can establish this bounded operating effect but would not establish novelty or an industrial process on its own.
