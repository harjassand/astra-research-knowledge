## 1. The Clifford obstruction gives exactly the claimed dimension factor

I independently derived the transverse Schur complement before reading the full addendum. Put \(n=m-1\), \(z=Rs\),
\[
c=\frac{\sinh z}{s},\qquad
d=\frac{\sinh(2z)-2z}{2s^2},\qquad
k=\frac{K'(s)}s.
\]
The diagonal signal second moment is
\[
D_s=c^2I-d\Gamma_1.
\]
Consequently the transverse covariance block is
\[
(k+c^2)I-d(I_n\otimes\Gamma_1)
-c^2[\Gamma_i\Gamma_j]_{i,j=2}^m.
\]
For \(\Gamma_1\psi=-\psi\), the vector
\(v=(\Gamma_2\psi,\ldots,\Gamma_m\psi)\) is nonzero, satisfies
\((I_n\otimes\Gamma_1)v=v\), and
\([\Gamma_i\Gamma_j]v=nv\). Positivity gives
\[
k\ge d+(n-1)c^2.
\]
This is algebraically identical to the addendum's (1.2), since
\(\sinh^2z+e^{-z}\sinh z=\sinh z\cosh z\). Its leading coefficient is
\[
K'(s)\ge \frac{n e^{2Rs}}{4s}+O(1),
\qquad
\liminf_{s\to\infty}s e^{-2Rs}K(s)\ge\frac{n}{8R}.
\]
The lower-order terms are harmless upon integration. Combining this with the already justified tail-envelope Legendre estimate yields exactly \(b\le b_*\), with no missing factor two.

The existence and eigenvector requirements for the Clifford tuple are valid. For example the usual Pauli tensor construction on \((\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes d}\) gives \(2d\) generators by strings of \(\sigma_z\) followed by \(\sigma_x\) or \(\sigma_y\), and an additional generator \(\sigma_z^{\otimes d}\) when required. A second anticommuting generator interchanges the two eigenspaces of \(\Gamma_1\), so the negative eigenspace used above is nonempty. The obstruction therefore lies inside the stated class of allowed tuples.

The moment argument uses the full transverse block, not a sum of independently necessary scalar inequalities. Its block ordering and Schur-complement signs are correct. Addendum 1 supplies the positive tilted moments for general all-exponential input noise and does not assume the needed integrals without justification.

## 2. The radial quadratic term really avoids exponential amplification

At a nonzero point, rotate the physical frame so its first vector is radial. The directional norm bound is invariant under this rotation. The radial insertion \(A_r\) commutes with \(H=gA_r\), and therefore
\[
e^{-H/2}D^2\exp(H)[A_r,A_r]e^{-H/2}=A_r^2.
\]
The quadratic Taylor term has its factor \(1/2\). Thus its radial contribution is bounded by \(R^2V''/2\), while each transverse channel contributes at most
\(R^2(g/r)I(Rg)\). This verifies the addendum's (2.2)–(2.4), including every combinatorial factor.

The second-order simplex integral obeys the two stated bounds. The partition into a square and two triangles is exact. As an additional check, its explicit expression for \(z>0\) is
\[
I(z)=\frac{e^{2z}+(2z-4)e^z+3}{4z^2},
\qquad I(0)=\frac12.
\]
In particular \(I(z)\sim e^{2z}/(4z^2)\). The Duhamel derivative has two permutations; multiplying by the Taylor factor \(1/2\) leaves the coefficient in the claimed transverse term. The exponent in the integral is nonnegative, so replacing \(\|H\|\) by the larger \(Rg\) is legitimate.

No operator-convexity assertion about the exponential is needed. The proof uses operator norms and bounds the whole self-adjoint relative error by a number below one.

## 3. The complex remainder constants are valid

Write \(a=2R\), \(q=\sqrt{L^2+r^2}\), and \(\ell=\log q\), as in the source. The threshold (3.3) implies \(a\sqrt m\le q/32\). On the larger complex Euclidean ball of radius \(q/8\),
\[
\left|q(x-y)^2-q^2\right|\le\frac{17}{64}q^2.
\]
All three holomorphic branches are consequently separated from their cuts and zeros. These estimates use the fixed real comparison value \(q\) at the center; there is no illicit replacement by a complex Euclidean norm inside the analytic potential.

For completeness, with \(c=\sqrt{47}/8\), the square-root Hessian constant is bounded by
\[
\frac1c+\frac{(9/8)^2}{c^3}
=\frac{1024}{47\sqrt{47}}<4.
\]
The inherited bounds \(|h_1'|\le9\ell\) and \(|h_1''|\le9/q\) give a Hessian coefficient at most
\[
9\left(\frac{(9/8)^2}{c^2}
+\frac1c+\frac{(9/8)^2}{c^3}\right)<50.
\]
Thus \(C_H=(50+4|b|)/a\) is sufficient. Arithmetic cross-checks give approximately \(3.1780\) and \(44.1126\), respectively; the inequalities themselves have ample analytic slack.

The Cauchy circle of radius \(q/16\), centered at any point on the required complex segment, remains inside the larger ball because
\(q/32+q/16<q/8\). Hence the third-derivative entry bound is correctly
\(16C_H\ell/q^2\). Taylor's factors then give exactly
\[
\|F_x\|_\infty\le\frac{C_Hma^2}{2}\frac\ell q,
\qquad
\|F_x-Q_x\|_\infty
\le\frac83 C_Hm^3a^3\frac\ell{q^2}.
\]
In particular the \(m^3\) factor in the second estimate safely covers the coordinate sum; it is not an omitted dimension cost.

For \(J_x=e^{-F_x}-1+Q_x\), the exponential remainder and the cubic Taylor error yield (4.3) with the stated \(C_J\). Its degree-two coefficient vanishes exactly. Applying the previously proved analytic-series lemma with radius \(a=2R\) gives coefficient sum
\(S_m=m(m+1)2^m\) and the factor \(2S_m\), with no extra missing factor \(e\). The exponential estimate used to define \(C_J\) already supplies its own factor \(e/2\). Discarding the denominator and using \(e^{ag}\le e^B\) then proves the stated bound
\[
\|\text{relative analytic remainder}\|
\le C_E\ell^3/q.
\]

The radial term is smaller than the recorded \((3a/8)\ell/q\). Thus their sum is bounded by \(C_T\ell^3e^{-\ell}\), exactly as required in (4.5).

## 4. The explicit threshold is uniform in the needed variables

For each fixed \(m\ge2\) and \(R>0\), all constants in (3.2) are finite. Condition (3.3) works for every larger \(L\) and every \(r\ge0\). The constants depend on \(m,R\), through \(b_*\) as well as the displayed factors; the proof makes no claim that one fixed numerical scale works for all dimensions or all \(R\).

The individual uses of the threshold check as follows.

- \(\ell\ge e^{2|b|+4}\) gives \(\log\ell\ge2|b|+4\), hence \(\ell\le B\le2\ell\). It also ensures positivity of the potential's first and second scalar derivatives.
- \(\ell\ge\log(32a\sqrt m+e^2)\) supplies the full complex-disc clearance.
- \(\ell\ge2\log(D_0+e)\), together with \(\ell\le e^{\ell/2}\), makes \(\|F_x\|_\infty\le1\).
- \(\ell\ge2\log(16C_T+e)\), together with \(\ell^4\le e^{\ell/2}\), proves \(C_T\ell^3e^{-\ell}\le\log\ell/(16\ell)\).
- \(\ell\ge4\log(4C_S+e)\), together with \(\ell^{3/2}\le e^{\ell/4}\), supplies the inner-region bound \(T\le1/4\).

The elementary powers-versus-exponentials comparisons hold at \(\ell=64\) and persist thereafter by differentiating their logarithms. The origin is covered by continuous radial derivative values and the inner-region estimate, so no undefined radial frame is used there.

## 5. The critical gap absorbs the whole error

In the outer region \(\rho=r/q\ge1/2\), the exact factors are
\[
T(r)\le\frac{na e^{b+1}}4
\frac{e^{1/\ell}\ell}{B}(1+D_B)
\rho^{-2}e^{-(1-\rho)B}.
\]
At \(b=b_*\), the first factor is exactly one. The last factor is at most one because its logarithmic derivative is \(B-2/\rho\ge0\). This establishes uniformity when \(r/L\) changes; an argument valid only as \(r/L\to\infty\) would not have sufficed.

The inequality \(e^{1/\ell}\le1+1/\ell+1/\ell^2\) and the lower bound on \(\log\ell+b\) give
\[
\frac{e^{1/\ell}\ell}{B}
\le1-d_\ell,
\qquad d_\ell=\frac{\log\ell}{4\ell}.
\]
The simplex correction obeys \(D_B\le d_\ell/4\). To verify the endpoint in the source's monotonicity argument without relying on floating-point checks, use
\[
\frac{16\,64^3e^{-16}}{\log64}
<\frac{3^{15}}{2^{26}}<1,
\]
which follows from \(e>8/3\) and \(\log2>1/2\). Its logarithmic derivative is
\(3/\ell-1/4-1/(\ell\log\ell)<0\) above 64. Thus (5.5) holds over the entire stated range.

Combining terms gives
\[
T\le(1-d_\ell)(1+d_\ell/4)
\le1-3d_\ell/4,
\]
while the radial and analytic remainder sum is at most \(d_\ell/4\). Therefore the total relative error is at most
\[
1-d_\ell/2=1-\frac{\log\ell}{8\ell}<1.
\]
In the inner region, \(T\le1/4\) and the other terms are at most \(\log\ell/(16\ell)<1/16\), so the claimed relative error bound below \(1/2\) is valid. This proves all-space positivity, with the displayed pointwise coercivity margin. The margin tends to zero in the outer tail, precisely as the addendum acknowledges.

## 6. The result attains the envelope, not merely its asymptotic offset

The choice \(q_L(r)=\sqrt{L^2+r^2}\) is material. It flattens the center as \(L\) increases without subtracting \(\log L\) from the fixed tail offset. For each fixed finite \(L\),
\[
h_b(q_L(r))-h_b(r)
=O(L^2\log r/r)=o(1).
\]
This is an \(o(1)\) error in the potential itself. Exponentiation is therefore legitimate and gives the multiplicative asymptotic (S5). Moreover, for all sufficiently large \(r\), \(q_L(r)\ge r\) and \(h_b\) is increasing, so one can directly take
\(C=Z_L^{-1}e^{h_b(L)/a}\) in the endpoint envelope. No hidden sign in an \(o(r)\) term is being used to infer an upper bound.

The tail equivalence is for fixed \(L\), as explicitly stated in the proof; it is not asserted uniformly when \(L\) varies with \(r\). This distinction does not weaken the existence theorem, which chooses one finite \(L\) satisfying the threshold.

Positive Hessian eigenvalues, all exponential moments, derivative decay, and the norm-integrable upper bound \(\|M_A\|\le2p_Le^{R|\nabla V_L|}\) justify the remaining density and normalization claims through the inherited compact-distribution machinery. This works in arbitrary target Hilbert dimension.
