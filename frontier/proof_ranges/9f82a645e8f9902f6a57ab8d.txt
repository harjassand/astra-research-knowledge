## The closest carrier-assisted reaction precedent

Callahan et al., *Physical Review Materials* 2, 081601(R) (2018), Figs. 1–4, directly observed recombination-enhanced dislocation glide in strained GaAs on Si using ECCI. Their 3.1 µm, unintentionally p-type GaAs film ((<10^{16}\) cm⁻³) was grown on on-axis (001) GaP/Si. Cooling left about 0.2% tensile strain, providing a mechanical driving force. The glide-set α misfit has an As-rich core and [\(1\bar{1}0\)] line direction; the β misfit has a Ga-rich core and [110] direction. The associated threading segments can have intermediate line directions. The paper does not index the two Fig. 4 reaction Burgers vectors numerically in the text; its caption says they differ.

For the main imaging condition, a 30 kV SEM beam at about 3.4–3.7 nA (roughly 1.9–2.0 mA/cm²) generated an estimated $10^{17}$–$10^{18}$ electron–hole pairs/cm³. A central region received about 35 minutes of raster exposure at room temperature. The observed influence extended 2–3 µm beyond the beam region; the paper attributes this to carrier diffusion lengths around 1–3 µm. Velocities varied widely with local interactions and repeated pinning/unpinning. At higher than 30 nA, more dislocations escaped at the surface. Temperature-dependent measurements ran from −20 °C to 80 °C; a 0.2 eV activation energy was estimated from the fastest, least-pinned lines, not from the population average.

In Fig. 4(a,b), two threads on parallel glide planes, with different Burgers vectors, approach from opposite glide directions and react at roughly 70 nm plane separation. A model cited by the authors puts the GaAs interaction radius around 50–100 nm. Cross-slip was considered likely and climb unlikely to close the separation within less than one 13.5-second frame. The authors interpret the lines vanishing from view as an annihilation reaction, leaving a short dislocation segment at the heterointerface; they call this direct evidence of individual annihilation. Fig. 4(c,d) separately shows lines leaving an etched mesa edge. These are meaningful reaction and removal precedents, but the study did not compare light/carrier schedules, did not demonstrate a selected reaction over a matched alternative, and did not test survival after carrier injection was stopped. Its beam also both creates carriers and images the structure. The authors report that REDG can increase overall network complexity even while individual reactions or surface exits reduce local defect content.

This sets a hard novelty boundary: carrier-assisted dislocation annihilation and carrier-assisted removal of mobile threads from a selected observation area are both prior art. A possible new claim must concern a measured, reproducible *bias in which compatible reaction occurs*, with the product and post-gate state identified; a mobility change, an isolated annihilation event, or an area-local count decline is insufficient.
