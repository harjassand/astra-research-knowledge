## 4. Parabolic extension

Let the spatial domain be a flat torus \(\mathbb T^d\), and define

\[
\mathcal L=\partial_t+v\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta,
\qquad \nu\geq0.
\]

For the pointwise maximum estimate, take all coefficients and solutions smooth on compact subintervals of \([0,T)\). For the integrated energy estimate also assume \(\nabla\cdot v=0\). Let \(S(t,x)\) be a real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrix field, and suppose

\[
\mathcal Lz=Sz,
\qquad
\mathcal LB=S,
\qquad B(0,x)=0.
\tag{10}
\]

The auxiliary equation preserves symmetry. Define

\[
a(r)=\int_0^1e^{r\theta}\,d\theta
 =\frac{e^r-1}{r},
\]

\[
h(r)=2\int_0^1\theta e^{r\theta}\,d\theta
 =\frac{2((r-1)e^r+1)}{r^2},
\]

with \(a(0)=h(0)=1\). All coefficient functions in this note are nondecreasing for \(r\geq0\), as follows from their nonnegative power series or integral representations.

For \(r=r(B(t,x))\), put

\[
\mathcal D(t,x)=\|[B,[B,S]]\|,\qquad
\mathcal G(t,x)=\sum_{i=1}^d\|\partial_iB\|^2,
\]

\[
q(t,x)=c(r)\mathcal D
 +\nu\bigl(h(r)+a(r)^2\bigr)\mathcal G.
\tag{11}
\]

### Theorem 3 — scalar domination after the matrix gauge

For \(y=e^{-B}z\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L|y|^2\leq2q|y|^2.}
\tag{12}
\]

Consequently,

\[
\|z(t)\|_\infty
\leq
\exp\!\left\{
 \sup_x\lambda_{\max}(B(t,x))
 +\int_0^t\|q(s)\|_\infty\,ds
\right\}\|z(0)\|_\infty.
\tag{13}
\]

The norm on vector fields uses Euclidean fiber length. Equation (12) is stronger than (13): it permits any justified scalar positive-evolution or potential estimate to replace the crude time integral of the spatial supremum. Such further potential estimates are not supplied automatically by the matrix identity.

### Proof

Set \(E=e^B\), \(C_i=\partial_iB\), and use \(D\exp_B\), \(D^2\exp_B\) for the first and second Fréchet derivatives. The diffusion chain rule gives

\[
\mathcal LE=D\exp_B[S]
 -\nu\sum_iD^2\exp_B[C_i,C_i].
\]

The product rule applied to \(z=Ey\) yields

\[
\mathcal Ly=
 \left(R+\nu\sum_i\Theta_i\right)y
 +2\nu\sum_i\Gamma_i\partial_i y,
\tag{14}
\]

where \(R\) is exactly the matrix remainder (3) at each point, and

\[
\Gamma_i=e^{-B}D\exp_B[C_i],\qquad
\Theta_i=e^{-B}D^2\exp_B[C_i,C_i].
\]

The integral formulas for exponential derivatives imply

\[
\|\Gamma_i\|\leq a(r)\|C_i\|,
\qquad
\|\Theta_i\|\leq h(r)\|C_i\|^2.
\tag{15}
\]

For completeness, the first formula is

\[
\Gamma_i=\int_0^1e^{-sB}C_i e^{sB}\,ds.
\]

The second is

\[
\Theta_i=2\int_{\substack{s_1,s_2\geq0\\s_1+s_2\leq1}}
 e^{-(s_1+s_2)B}C_i e^{s_1B}C_i e^{s_2B}\,ds_1ds_2.
\]

Centering \(B\) at its spectral midpoint bounds the integrand norm in the second formula by
\(e^{r(s_1+s_2)}\|C_i\|^2\). Integrating first along \(s_1+s_2=s\) gives \(h(r)\).

Now

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L|y|^2
={}&2y^T\left(R+\nu\sum_i\Theta_i\right)y
 +4\nu\sum_i y^T\Gamma_i\partial_i y
 -2\nu\sum_i|\partial_i y|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Completing a square gives, for any vector \(w\),

\[
4y^T\Gamma_iw-2|w|^2
\leq2|\Gamma_i^Ty|^2.
\]

Combining this with (6) and (15) proves (12). The scalar maximum principle on the torus proves (13). No commutation assumption between \(B\), \(S\), and spatial derivatives of \(B\) was made. ∎
