 Total variation of a zero-mass signed measure is half its l1 norm, so

   ||d(delta_sigma P_1)/dw||_TV
     <= integral_0^1 a_w(s)a_w(1-s) ds.                       (5)
 For q=c/w<=1/2, split the integral at q and 1-q:

   integral_0^1 min(1,q/s)min(1,q/(1-s)) ds
     =-2q log(1-q)+2q^2 log((1-q)/q)
     <=q^2[4+2 log(1/q)].                                    (6)

 A one-sided smoothing estimate would give only O(1/w), which cannot be integrated uniformly up to an arbitrarily
 huge rate. The two-sided factorization gives O(log w/w^2).
 Integrating (5)-(6) from K to any rate W>=K gives

   ||Law_W(X_1)-Law_K(X_1)||_TV
     <= integral_K^infinity (c^2/w^2)[4+2log(w/c)] dw
     =(c^2/K)[6+2log(K/c)].                                  (7)

 The estimate is uniform in all other rates, so cap edges one by one and use the triangle inequality. This proves (1).

 6 Constructive sampler and bit cost
 Use K=(12 m n^3/epsilon)^2 and the following fully rational procedure:
  1. Multiply input rates by T exactly and cap at K.
  2. Let Lambda=sum_e r_e^K<=mK. If Lambda=0, return the initial state.
  3. Choose the integer D=ceil(max(1,4 Lambda^2/epsilon)).
  4. In each of D steps, independently do nothing with probability 1-Lambda/D, or select physical edge e with
     probability r_e^K/D and transpose its labels.
  5. Return the final permutation or its occupancy/color projection.
 D>=Lambda, so these are probabilities. Compare each step to the exact capped process over time 1/D. Its total clock
 count is Poisson(p), p=Lambda/D<=1. The total-variation distance between Poisson(p) and Bernoulli(p) is
 p(1-exp(-p))<=p^2. Conditional on a single event its edge is selected proportionally to r_e^K in both constructions.
 Coupling steps and ignoring multiple events gives endpoint error at most Dp^2=Lambda^2/D<=epsilon/4. Along with
 the cap error this leaves half the budget for finite random arithmetic.
 All step probabilities are binary-rational quotients of polynomial-bit rationals; combine denominators, sample the
 corresponding integer categorical law by rejection from fair bits, and truncate each rejection routine with accumulated
 failure probability at most epsilon/2. A common denominator has at most the sum of the supplied denominator bit
 lengths. The number of steps is polynomial in n,m,1/epsilon and input bit lengths, independent of numerical T or
 numerical original rates. No real clock or special conditional sampler is assumed.
 A more efficient implementation may use uniformization: draw N~Poisson(Lambda), then independently select each
 of N edges proportionally to r_e^K. Its expected swap count is Lambda<=mK. Truncating N at
 O(Lambda+log(1/epsilon)) and evaluating the Poisson masses to total error O(epsilon) gives a bounded
 polynomial-bit implementation. The deliberately conservative D-step scheme above suffices for the theorem and
 makes no practical runtime claim.

 7 What this enables and what it does not
 The output is a full interacting many-particle endpoint, including joint tracer positions and arbitrary occupancy
 correlations. With polynomially many independent samples, any bounded efficiently evaluable endpoint observable
 can be estimated to additive error. The gain is computational independence from the numerical observation time and
 from stiffness caused by enormous exchange-rate ratios. This differs from replacing transport by independent random
 walkers or computing only the mean density.
