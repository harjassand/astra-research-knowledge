6. Physical realization and finite precision
The matrices are one-excitation restrictions of the spin Hamiltonians
\[
H_\pm
=
\sum_j d_j^\pm n_j
-
\sum_j J_j
\left(
\sigma_j^+\sigma_{j+1}^-
+
\sigma_j^-\sigma_{j+1}^+
\right).
\]
All interactions are nearest-neighbor and uniformly bounded. Off-diagonal entries in the computational basis are nonpositive, so the Hamiltonians are stoquastic.
Their fermionic representation shows that many-particle energies are sums of the positive one-particle energies. Consequently, the vacuum is the unique ground state and the many-body gap is at least one.
The initial state is simply
\[
|+\rangle_0|0\rangle_1\cdots|0\rangle_{m-1}.
\]
Every subsequent state lies in vacuum plus one excitation. Across any cut, such a vector is a sum of at most two product vectors. Thus the trajectory has exact Schmidt rank at most two.
At \(t_n=\pi n\), a first-spin \(X\) measurement gives opposite deterministic outcomes under the two hypotheses. A coherent real-time experiment therefore distinguishes them in evolution time \(O(n)\), while the specified imaginary-time source requires exponentially or superexponentially many copies.
This separation is not caused by a large entanglement representation.
Exponential calibration precision is unnecessary
The construction survives matched rational rounding.
Take a slightly narrower band, using \(a=5/4\) and width \(W=1/2\), and round common bulk coefficients identically in both alternatives. If
\[
\|\widetilde A_\pm-A_\pm\|=O(1/n),
\]
then Duhamel’s formula gives
\[
\|e^{-it_n\widetilde A_\pm}-e^{-it_nA_\pm}\|
\le
t_n\|\widetilde A_\pm-A_\pm\|.
\]
Choosing the constant sufficiently small preserves a constant real-time separation.
Because the matrices are tridiagonal, this requires only \(O(\log n)\) bits per coefficient. Moreover, the rounded alternatives still satisfy
\[
\widetilde A_+^jv=\widetilde A_-^jv
\qquad (j<m).
\]
Taylor approximation gives short-window indistinguishability; polynomial approximation on a fixed complex ellipse gives an exponentially small bound uniform over all imaginary times. Together they preserve the main exponential-order separation.
This is inverse-polynomial calibration tolerance, not constant-noise robustness. It does not establish survival under fixed-strength independent disorder or nonzero decoherence over arbitrarily long evolution.
Also, \([1,2]\) is the fixed active one-excitation band. The complete many-body bandwidth is extensive.
