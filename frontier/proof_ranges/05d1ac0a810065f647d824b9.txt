# A complete weighted polynomial estimate for the double characteristic operator

Research date: 16 October 2026. Status: internally derived, unreviewed. Historical priority and external verification are UNKNOWN. The supplied cubic theorem is inherited; the argument below extends its degree and defect exponent and proves necessity by exact compatible fields. It does not prove the sharp conformal quasiconvexity inequality.

## Statement

Use normalized averages and norms on the square two-dimensional torus. Put

\[
D\phi=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad
q=\sigma a+d,\qquad T_\sigma\phi=(q,c),\qquad \sigma>0.
\]

Let \(P\) be homogeneous of degree \(m\ge2\), and set

\[
\widetilde P(a,b,c,q)=P(a,b,c,q-\sigma a).
\]

Give these variables weights

\[
\operatorname{wt}(b)=0,\quad \operatorname{wt}(a)=1,
\quad \operatorname{wt}(c)=\operatorname{wt}(q)=2.
\]

Fix \(0<\alpha\le m\) and \(1<p,s<\infty\) satisfying

\[
\frac\alpha p+\frac{m-\alpha}{s}=1. \tag{A}
\]

Then the absolute estimate

\[
\|P(D\phi)\|_1\le K\|T_\sigma\phi\|_p^\alpha
                         \|D\phi\|_s^{m-\alpha} \tag{B}
\]

holds for all smooth periodic maps, with some finite \(K\), if and only if every nonzero monomial of \(\widetilde P\) has weight at least \(2\alpha\).

For \(m\ge3\), exactly the same criterion characterizes the signed estimate

\[
|\langle P(D\phi)\rangle|\le K\|T_\sigma\phi\|_p^\alpha
                         \|D\phi\|_s^{m-\alpha}. \tag{C}
\]

For \(m=2\), the signed criterion is instead that there exists \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\) for which every monomial of

\[
\widetilde P-\lambda(aq-\sigma a^2-bc)
\]

has weight at least \(2\alpha\). The subtracted expression is the true gradient determinant. Its average is zero. It must not be replaced by only its lowest weighted part \(-\sigma a^2-bc\).

The assertion passes by convolution to maps for which \(D\phi\in L^s\) and \(T_\sigma\phi\in L^p\). If \(p>s\), this second assumption is additional. The same criterion holds for smooth compactly supported maps, or the corresponding zero-boundary classes, on any nonempty bounded planar domain. Sufficiency follows by zero extension into a surrounding square; necessity also needs the localization argument below. Constants are unchanged by isotropic rescaling. No endpoint \(p=1,\infty\) or \(s=1,\infty\) is asserted.

## 1. Quantitative sufficiency

Write \(E=\|T_\sigma\phi\|_p\), \(M=\|D\phi\|_s\), and

\[
\frac1t=\frac12\left(\frac1p+\frac1s\right).
\]

The supplied report's mixed-derivative interpolation and Hodge argument, with these endpoints, gives

\[
\|a\|_t^2\le C_0(\sigma,p,s) EM. \tag{1}
\]

For completeness, decompose \((\sigma f,g)=\nabla\chi+(\psi_y,-\psi_x)\), with \(\Delta\chi=q\). Then

\[
\sigma a=\chi_{xx}+\psi_{xy},\quad
\sigma b=\chi_{xy}+\psi_{yy},\quad
c=\chi_{xy}-\psi_{xx}.
\]

Bounded second-order Riesz multipliers imply
\(\|\psi_{xx}\|_p\lesssim E\) and \(\|\psi_{yy}\|_s\lesssim M\). The classical mixed-derivative estimate

\[
\|\psi_{xy}\|_t^2\lesssim\|\psi_{xx}\|_p\|\psi_{yy}\|_s
\]

controls the stream part. Ordinary interpolation bounds
\(\|\chi_{xx}\|_t^2\le\|\chi_{xx}\|_p\|\chi_{xx}\|_s\lesssim EM\), proving (1). The mixed-derivative estimate follows from complex interpolation of \(|D_x|^{2(1-z)}|D_y|^{2z}\), imaginary-power multiplier bounds in each coordinate, and periodic Hilbert transforms. These are classical analytic tools; this is not a new sharp multiplier claim.

Consider a monomial \(a^u b^v c^w q^z\), with total degree \(m\), and let

\[
\beta=\frac u2+w+z\ge\alpha,\qquad \rho=\frac\alpha\beta\in(0,1].
\]

Interpolate each \(a\) factor between \(L^t\), where it costs \(E^{1/2}M^{1/2}\), and \(L^s\), where it costs \(M\), using fraction \(\rho\) of the first bound. Interpolate each \(c,q\) factor between \(L^p\), where it costs \(E\), and \(L^s\), where it costs at most \(L_\sigma M\), using the same fraction \(\rho\). Here one can take \(L_\sigma=1+\sigma\). Keep \(b\) in \(L^s\).

The reciprocal Hölder exponents add to
\(\rho\beta/p+(m-\rho\beta)/s=1\). Consequently

\[
\|a^u b^v c^w q^z\|_1
\le C_0^{\rho u/2}L_\sigma^{(1-\rho)(w+z)}
       E^\alpha M^{m-\alpha}. \tag{2}
\]

Thus a valid explicit coefficient bound is the sum of the constants in (2), multiplied by the absolute coefficients of the monomials. Constants \(C_0\) are classical interpolation/Riesz operator constants, not evaluated optimally here. This proves (B), and hence (C), under the stated criterion. Subtracting the determinant proves the additional degree-two signed cases.

## 2. Exact compatible testing family

Let \(\psi,F,G\) be arbitrary smooth periodic scalar functions and put
\(k=\sigma F_x+G_y\). For an integer \(N\ge1\), write \(\varepsilon=N^{-1}\) and define

\[
f_N(x,y)=\varepsilon\psi_y(x,Ny)+\varepsilon^2F(x,Ny),
\]
\[
g_N(x,y)=-\sigma\varepsilon^2\psi_x(x,Ny)+\varepsilon^3G(x,Ny).
\]

Their exact gradients are

\[
\begin{aligned}
a_N&=\varepsilon\psi_{xy}+\varepsilon^2F_x,&
b_N&=\psi_{yy}+\varepsilon F_y,\\
c_N&=-\sigma\varepsilon^2\psi_{xx}+\varepsilon^3G_x,&
d_N&=-\sigma\varepsilon\psi_{xy}+\varepsilon^2G_y,\\
q_N&=\varepsilon^2k.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
\]

Every right-hand side is evaluated at \((x,Ny)\). These are actual periodic gradients, not prescribed matrix laws. The full gradient remains bounded in every finite \(L^s\), while \(\|T_\sigma\phi_N\|_p=O(N^{-2})\).

Every smooth mean-zero \(k\) is available: solve \(\Delta\chi=k\), then take \(F=\chi_x/\sigma\), \(G=\chi_y\). Both are smooth periodic functions. Therefore the leading variables

\[
(a,b,c,q)=(\psi_{xy},\psi_{yy},-\sigma\psi_{xx},k)
\]

have an arbitrary Hessian and an independent mean-zero scalar field.

Suppose the first nonzero weighted part of \(\widetilde P\) has weight \(j\), and denote it by \(P_j\). The covering map \((x,y)\mapsto(x,Ny)\) preserves normalized averages. The exact polynomial expansion of (3) therefore gives

\[
\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle
=N^{-j}\left\langle
P_j(\psi_{xy},\psi_{yy},-\sigma\psi_{xx},k)
\right\rangle+O(N^{-j-1}). \tag{4}
\]

There is likewise convergence after multiplication by \(N^j\) in absolute \(L^1\), in the fixed base variables. If \(j<2\alpha\), any nonzero leading signed average contradicts (C). Any nonzero leading absolute average contradicts (B).

## 3. The only possible signed cancellation

**Elementary null-polynomial lemma.** Let \(R(X,Y,Z,K)\) be a homogeneous polynomial of degree \(m\ge2\). If

\[
\left\langle R(\psi_{xx},\psi_{xy},\psi_{yy},k)\right\rangle=0 \tag{5}
\]

for all smooth periodic \(\psi\) and smooth mean-zero \(k\), then \(R\) is independent of \(K\), and

\[
R=\lambda(XZ-Y^2)\quad(m=2),\qquad R=0\quad(m\ge3). \tag{6}
\]

**Proof.** Vary \(k\) by any smooth mean-zero function. The first variation says that \(R_K(D^2\psi,k)\) is spatially constant. Choose \(\psi,k\) supported in small balls leaving an open zero region. There this constant is \(R_K(0)=0\), since \(m\ge2\). Arbitrary values of a Hessian and of \(k\) can be prescribed in a ball; the scalar mean can be corrected in a disjoint ball. Thus \(R_K\) vanishes at every \((X,Y,Z,K)\), so \(R\) is independent of \(K\).

Varying \(\psi\) gives the Euler identity

\[
\partial_{xx}R_X+\partial_{xy}R_Y+\partial_{yy}R_Z=0.
\]

At any point prescribe an arbitrary Hessian, zero third derivatives, and arbitrary fourth derivatives by a local polynomial times a cutoff. The coefficients of these fourth derivatives must vanish:

\[
R_{XX}=R_{XY}=R_{YZ}=R_{ZZ}=0,
\qquad R_{YY}+2R_{XZ}=0. \tag{7}
\]

Solving this elementary system yields
\(R=\lambda(XZ-Y^2)+\ell(X,Y,Z)+c\). Homogeneity gives (6). Conversely, the determinant Hessian has zero average by integration by parts. This planar calculation is a special case of classical higher-order null-Lagrangian theory; no priority is claimed for the lemma.

## 4. Necessity and the exact quadratic exception

For the absolute bound, a nonzero \(P_j\) cannot vanish at every arbitrary local jet. Choose \(\psi,k\) for which its leading absolute average is positive, and use (4). Hence \(j\ge2\alpha\).

For the signed bound when \(m\ge3\), the invertible substitution
\((a,b,c,q)=(Y,Z,-\sigma X,K)\) and the null-polynomial lemma ensure a nonzero leading average for every nonzero \(P_j\). Thus \(j\ge2\alpha\) is necessary.

When \(m=2\), the only undetectable leading part has weight two and is a multiple of \(-\sigma a^2-bc\). Subtract that same multiple of the true determinant \(aq-\sigma a^2-bc\), which does not change the signed integral. The residual has no weight-two cancellation; if its next weight is three, the lemma detects it because the Hessian determinant has weight two. Thus all remaining weights below \(2\alpha\) must vanish. This proves precisely the stated modulo-determinant criterion.

Degree one is deliberately excluded: every periodic linear gradient integral vanishes, so its signed classification is different.

To transfer necessity to a fixed zero-boundary domain, multiply a periodic testing map by a nonnegative smooth cutoff \(\eta(x/L)\) on a box of size \(L\). For each fixed \(N\), the additional gradient term is \(O(L^{-1})\), since the periodic potential is bounded. After division by the box area, the leading polynomial integral tends to \(\langle P(D\phi_N)\rangle\) times the positive cutoff moment \(\int\eta^m\); the norm moments tend to their corresponding periodic moments times fixed cutoff moments. Periodic averaging proves these limits. Choose \(L\) sufficiently large for each \(N\), then isotropically rescale the compact map into a ball contained in the specified domain. Relation (A) makes the ratio invariant under this rescaling. Thus the divergent periodic ratios remain divergent after localization. For the absolute bound use \(|\eta|^m\) in the same argument. Zero extension alone would not have proved this necessity direction.

## 5. Consequences and boundaries

For \(m\ge3\), define \(j(P)\) as the smallest monomial weight of \(\widetilde P\). The maximal possible defect power, among admissible interior exponent triples (A), is exactly \(j(P)/2\). Failure above it is witnessed by actual compatible fields with a ratio growing as \(N^{2\alpha-j(P)}\).

The supplied cubic theorem is the case \(m=3,\alpha=1,p=2,s=4\). Weight less than two consists exactly of \(b^3,ab^2\), reproducing its two conditions. More generally, degree \(m\) at defect power one excludes exactly \(b^m,ab^{m-1}\).

For the target cubic from the source report,

\[
C_r=q\{2aq-(r+2)a^2+rc^2-2bc\}
       -\kappa a(a^2+c^2),\qquad
\sigma=r+1,\quad\kappa=r(r+2)/3,
\]

the smallest weighted part is \(-\kappa a^3\), of weight three. Therefore

\[
\|C_r(D\phi)\|_1
\le K_r\|T_{r+1}\phi\|_2^{3/2}\|D\phi\|_6^{3/2}. \tag{8}
\]

The defect exponent \(3/2\) cannot be increased uniformly with a full-gradient norm bounded on the testing family. The receiving norm in (8) is \(L^6\), whereas the sharp conformal quasiconvexity problem requires a fixed \(L^4\)-scale quartic budget and its exact constant. Equation (8) supplies neither that sharp constant nor a solution of the quasiconvexity problem. Concentration remains load-bearing.

The theorem is a finite-bound and exact-exponent classification. Its sufficiency is a classical interpolation consequence. Its necessity uses classical null-Lagrangian structure, reconstructed here in the needed planar case. Its historical originality is not established.

## 6. An explicit sharp quadratic compensation inequality

The quadratic exception gives a result with an optimal numerical constant, beyond a symbolic membership criterion. For
\(H_\sigma=\sigma a^2+bc\), every periodic \(W^{1,2}\) map satisfies

\[
\boxed{|\langle H_\sigma\rangle|
\le\sigma^{-1/2}\|T_\sigma\phi\|_2^{3/2}
                         \|D\phi\|_2^{1/2}.} \tag{9}
\]

The constant \(\sigma^{-1/2}\) is optimal, and the defect power \(3/2\) is also optimal with these same \(L^2\) receiving norms. In contrast, the maximal absolute-estimate power for \(H_\sigma\) is one.

Indeed, the determinant identity gives

\[
\sigma\|a\|_2^2=\langle aq-bc\rangle
\le\|(q,c)\|_2\|(a,-b)\|_2
\le EM.
\]

Also \(\langle H_\sigma\rangle=\langle aq\rangle\), so
\(|\langle H_\sigma\rangle|\le E\|a\|_2\le\sigma^{-1/2}E^{3/2}M^{1/2}\).
Equality holds for \(g=0\), \(f=f(x)\) with nonzero periodic derivative: then \(E=\sigma\|a\|_2\), \(M=\|a\|_2\), and the two sides of (9) agree.

The core signed classification shows that subtracting the true determinant turns \(-H_\sigma\) into \(-aq\), whose weight is three. The exact perturbed compatible witness recorded in `VERIFICATION_RECEIPT.json` has, at \(\sigma=2\),

\[
\langle-H_2(D\phi_N)\rangle
=\frac1{2N^3}-\frac1{8N^4},\qquad
\|T_2\phi_N\|_2=O(N^{-2}),\qquad
\sup_N\|D\phi_N\|_2<\infty.
\]

For an explicit all-\(\sigma\) formula, write \(\varepsilon=N^{-1}\), \(\theta=x+Ny\), and take

\[
f_N=(-\varepsilon+\varepsilon^2/(2\sigma))\sin\theta,
\qquad
g_N=(\sigma\varepsilon^2+\varepsilon^3/2)\sin\theta.
\]

Then \(q_N=\varepsilon^2\cos\theta\), and the rank-one gradient has
\(\langle-H_\sigma\rangle=\varepsilon^3/2-\varepsilon^4/(4\sigma)\). Thus any defect power greater than \(3/2\) fails for every positive \(\sigma\). The absolute criterion sees the original weight two, and therefore has maximal power one. This sharp signed quadratic statement does not give the target cubic sharp quasiconvexity estimate.
