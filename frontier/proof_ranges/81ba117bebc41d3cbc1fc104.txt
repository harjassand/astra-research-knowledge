## 1. Main theorem: exact rational-point preservation under smoothing

Theorem. Let \\(f\in\mathbb Q[X_1,\ldots,X_n]\\) be any nonconstant polynomial of degree \\(d\ge1\\). Define the affine hypersurface

\\[ \boxed{ H_f:\quad U^3\bigl(1+f(X_1,\ldots,X_n)^2\bigr)=1. } \\]

Then:

1. \\(H_f\\) is smooth over \\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\\).
2. \\(H_f\\) is geometrically integral.
3.

# Its rational points are exactly \\[ \boxed{ H_f(\mathbb Q)

{(\mathbf x,1):f(\mathbf x)=0,\ \mathbf x\in\mathbb Q^n}. } \\]

1. Its defining polynomial has total degree \\(2d+3\\).
2. The transformation \\(f\mapsto H_f\\) is deterministic polynomial-time in the standard explicit polynomial representation.

Consequently, every affine rational hypersurface can have its entire rational-point set represented, without introducing or losing a single point, inside a smooth geometrically integral affine hypersurface.

This holds even when the original hypersurface is singular, reducible or has no rational points.

### Proof

#### A. The arithmetic mechanism

Consider the elliptic curve

\\[ E:\quad Y^2=X^3-1. \\]

This is Cremona curve 144a1, with Mordell–Weil rank zero and torsion group \\(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\\). Its complete rational-point set is

\\[ E(\mathbb Q)=\\{O,(1,0)\\}. \\]

The classification is recorded in the LMFDB.&#x20;

Suppose \\((\mathbf x,u)\in H_f(\mathbb Q)\\). The defining equation implies \\(u\ne0\\), and

\\[ \frac1{u^3}=1+f(\mathbf x)^2. \\]

Set

\\[ X=\frac1u,\qquad Y=f(\mathbf x). \\]

Then

\\[ Y^2=X^3-1. \\]

Because \\(X,Y\in\mathbb Q\\), this is an affine rational point of \\(E\\). The only possibility is \\((X,Y)=(1,0)\\).

Therefore

\\[ u=1,\qquad f(\mathbf x)=0. \\]

Conversely, every rational zero \\(\mathbf x\\) of \\(f\\) gives the rational point \\((\mathbf x,1)\in H_f\\).

This establishes the exact rational-point identity.

#### B. Smoothness

Write

\\[ F(\mathbf X,U)=U^3(1+f(\mathbf X)^2)-1. \\]

Its partial derivative with respect to \\(U\\) is

\\[ \frac{\partial F}{\partial U} =3U^2(1+f(\mathbf X)^2). \\]

At any geometric point satisfying \\(F=0\\),

\\[ U^3(1+f^2)=1, \\]

so

\\[ \boxed{\frac{\partial F}{\partial U}=\frac3U\ne0.} \\]

The Jacobian criterion therefore proves that \\(H_f\\) is smooth over \\(\overline{\mathbb Q}\\).

Crucially, no condition on the critical points of \\(f\\) is required.

#### C. Geometric integrality

Put \\(k=\overline{\mathbb Q}\\) and \\(K=k(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\\).

We show that

\\[ F=U^3(1+f^2)-1 \\]

is irreducible in \\(K[U]\\).

Since this is a cubic in \\(U\\), reducibility would imply the existence of a root \\(r\in K\\). Such a root would satisfy

\\[ r^3=\frac1{1+f^2}. \\]

Setting \\(h=1/r\\), we obtain

\\[ h^3=1+f^2, \\]

or

\\[ f^2=h^3-1. \\]

Thus the rational functions \\(h\\) and \\(f\\) define a rational map

\\[ \mathbb A_k^n\dashrightarrow E_k,\qquad \mathbf x\longmapsto(h(\mathbf x),f(\mathbf x)). \\]

Because \\(f\\) is nonconstant, we may restrict to a suitable affine line on which \\(f\\) remains nonconstant and the rational functions are defined generically. This produces a nonconstant rational map

\\[ \mathbb P_k^1\dashrightarrow E_k. \\]

Since \\(E_k\\) is smooth and projective, that map extends to a morphism. The Riemann–Hurwitz formula gives

\\[ 2g(\mathbb P^1)-2 = \deg(\phi)\bigl(2g(E)-2\bigr)+R, \\]

with \\(R\ge0\\). As \\(g(\mathbb P^1)=0\\) and \\(g(E)=1\\), it would require

\\[ -2=R\ge0, \\]

a contradiction.

Hence \\(F\\) is irreducible over \\(K\\). It is primitive as a polynomial in \\(U\\) over \\(k[\mathbf X]\\), since its constant coefficient is \\(-1\\). Gauss's lemma therefore proves that \\(F\\) is irreducible in \\(k[\mathbf X,U]\\).

Together with smoothness, this establishes geometric integrality.

Finally,

\\[ \deg F=2d+3. \\]

Squaring an explicitly represented polynomial, adding one, and multiplying by \\(U^3\\) are polynomial-time operations in its encoded input length.

The theorem follows. \\(\square\\)

## 2. The stronger structural interpretation

There is more information in the proof than merely preservation of rational solvability.

Let

\\[ B_f=\mathbb A^n\setminus V(1+f^2). \\]

Projection onto the original variables defines

\\[ \pi:H_f\longrightarrow B_f. \\]

Over \\(B_f\\), the polynomial \\(1+f^2\\) is invertible, and

\\[ U^3=(1+f^2)^{-1}. \\]

The derivative in \\(U\\) is invertible everywhere on this cover. Consequently, \\(\pi\\) is a finite étale cover of degree three, indeed a \\(\mu_3\\)-torsor.

Thus the construction establishes a more specialized theorem:

> Arbitrary affine rational hypersurface zero sets can be represented exactly as the rational points of smooth, geometrically integral affine hypersurfaces obtained from degree-three étale covers of principal open subsets of affine space.

In particular, the construction preserves the exact number of rational points, whether zero, finite or infinite.
