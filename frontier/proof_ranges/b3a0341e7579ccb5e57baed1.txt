## 3. Adaptive expected sample lower bound

Consider any randomized algorithm reading independent p-coins, with arbitrary
adaptation and a stopping time N. Its other inputs beta,B,c and model promise
are identical in the two cases. Couple its internal randomness and use the
same uniform U_j for each requested reference draw: output1 if U_j<=p. The
oracles disagree only for theta<U_j<=2theta, of probability theta.

Along the p0 run, the event N>=j depends only on previous observations and
randomness. Therefore the expected number of disagreements by its stopping
time is theta*E_0 N. If there is no disagreement before that stop, both runs
have identical transcripts and outputs. Consequently

    TV(output0,output1)<=theta*E_0 N.                          (7)

The result is trivial if E_0 N is infinite. It allows variable and unbounded
runtime; it is not merely a deterministic-cap statement.

If each case has wrong-decision probability at most alpha and UNKNOWN probability
at most delta, the event "decide positive" has probability at least1-delta-alpha
in case0 and at most alpha in case1. Hence

    E_0 N >= max(0,1-delta-2alpha)/theta
           =max(0,1-delta-2alpha)*exp(beta*B).                 (8)

Unlimited abstention evades a lower bound, as it should. Physical cost is at
least this reference-shot count times its preparation/readout cost. The theorem
does not bound protocols that prepare other gate settings or use extra complete
Hamiltonian information, quantum coherent queries, importance ensembles or
target preparations. Those change the acquired-information contract and require
their own costs. It is a precise obstruction to treating arbitrary strong
counterfactual shifts as cheaply learnable from one reference histogram.
