5. A polynomial-bit implementation of the rare convex integrals
It is not enough to write “sample a truncated Gaussian.” A remote or narrow feasible region may have an extraordinarily small Gaussian mass.
Here is a conservative explicit reduction that establishes polynomial complexity without assuming a favourable initial rejection probability.
Both the initial-region distribution and a lifted facet distribution have the form
\[
Z=\int_D e^{-q(x)}\,dx,
\tag{17}
\]
where \(D\) is a rational polyhedron, \(q\) is a rational convex quadratic, and its sublevel sets on \(D\) are bounded. In the facet case, the height coordinate is bounded by \(0\le s\le v\).
Compute
\[
q_*=\min_{x\in D}q(x),\qquad f=q-q_*.
\]
The minimization is a rational convex quadratic program; its cost is included. The factor \(e^{-q_*}\) is retained in logarithmic form:
\[
Z=e^{-q_*}J,\qquad
J=\int_D e^{-f(x)}dx.
\tag{18}
\]
Uniform energy-tail bound
Let \(x_*\) be a minimizer and \(n_D=\dim D\). Convexity implies, for \(0<a<1\),
\[
\int_D e^{-af(x)}dx
\le
a^{-n_D}\int_D e^{-f(x)}dx.
\]
Indeed, contract \(D\) toward \(x_*\) by factor \(a\), and use
\[
f(x_*+a(x-x_*))\le af(x).
\]
Taking \(a=1/2\) yields
\[
\boxed{
\Pr_{e^{-f}/J}[f>t]\le2^{n_D}e^{-t/2}.
}
\tag{19}
\]
The enormous rarity factor \(e^{-q_*}\) has disappeared.
Reduction to a bounded convex body
For an integer \(k_{\mathrm{lift}}\), define
\[
\mathcal B_k=
\left\{
(x,z)\in D\times\mathbb R^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}:
f(x)+k_{\mathrm{lift}}|z_\ell|
\le k_{\mathrm{lift}}
\text{ for every }\ell
\right\}.
\tag{20}
\]
This is convex, and
\[
\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal B_k)}{2^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}}
=
\int_D
\left(1-\frac{f(x)}{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}\right)_+^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}dx.
\tag{21}
\]
Choose
\[
t=2\left(n_D\log2+\log\frac8\eta\right),
\qquad
k_{\mathrm{lift}}\ge
\max\left\{2t,\frac{8t^2}{\eta}\right\}.
\]
On \(f\le t\),
\[
e^{-f-t^2/k_{\mathrm{lift}}}
\le
\left(1-\frac f{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}\right)^{k_{\mathrm{lift}}}
\le e^{-f}.
\]
Together with (19), this proves a relative \(O(\eta)\) approximation to \(J\). Projecting an approximately uniform sample from \(\mathcal B_k\) onto \(x\) also approximates the desired normalized density.
All geometric inputs are acquired
A strictly feasible rational point is obtained by linear programming. Moving a sufficiently small rational distance from \(x_*\) toward that point gives a centre \(x_c\) with \(f(x_c)<1/8\) and positive constraint slacks.
Those slacks and the quadratic coefficients give an explicit inscribed-ball radius at least
\[
2^{-\operatorname{poly}(L,\log k_{\mathrm{lift}})}.
\]
Coercivity of the original Gaussian quadratic, the bound \(s\le v\), and \(|z_\ell|\le1\) give an explicit containing radius at most
\[
2^{\operatorname{poly}(L,\log k_{\mathrm{lift}})}.
\]
Membership and separation use only rational linear and quadratic evaluations. Established convex-body volume and sampling algorithms therefore apply with polynomial-bit geometric parameters. Their preprocessing, rounding, sampling and confidence amplification are part of the cost. Department of Mathematical Sciences
This conservative implementation has lifted dimension
\[
O\!\left(
\frac{(r+1+\log\eta^{-1})^2}{\eta}
\right).
\tag{22}
\]
It is a complexity proof, not a claim of an efficient production implementation. Direct modern log-concave algorithms operate in the original lifted dimension \(r+1\) and are the stronger practical starting point. The polynomial guarantee does not rely on an uncharged warm start.
