## 4. Six signed area moments suffice for two joint amplitudes

Let d=2, with coordinates (r,s). Let w_a(r,s) be the integer winding number of the closed polygonal or piecewise smooth amplitude path. Define

    M_ij(a)=integral_R2 w_a(r,s) r^i s^j dr ds,
            i,j>=0, i+j<=2.                          (4.1)

The sign is the oriented winding sign; these are not moments of a positive probability distribution.

### Corollary 4.1

If all six moments

    M00, M10, M01, M20, M11, M02

vanish, Theorem 3.1 applies to

    phi=r(x) psi_1(y)+s(x) psi_2(y)

for arbitrary profiles psi_1,psi_2 and every rank-one-convex quartic.

### Proof

Green's theorem, including winding multiplicity, gives

    integral_a alpha_1 dr+alpha_2 ds
      = integral_R2 w_a(r,s)(partial_r alpha_2-partial_s alpha_1) dr ds.

The polynomial in parentheses has degree at most two. This proves the claim directly. It is also equivalent to (3.1) for polynomial one-forms: derivatives of their coefficients have degree at most two, and exact one-forms already integrate to zero.

For homogeneous quartics the curvature has total degree two jointly in B,r,s. Therefore the zero-area moment removes its quadratic-in-B part, the two first area moments remove its linear-in-B part, and the three quadratic area moments remove its B-independent part. Exact cancellation handles all affine directions together. Merely making the moments small does not, without further estimates, supply a uniform bound for arbitrarily large B.

More explicitly, with Q=(0|r psi_1'+s psi_2'),

    partial_r alpha_2-partial_s alpha_1
      = E_y { D^2F(B+Q)[(0|psi_1'),(psi_2|0)]
              -D^2F(B+Q)[(0|psi_2'),(psi_1|0)] }.       (4.2)

No sign is assigned to this curvature. The theorem controls its complete circulation by cancellation of the relevant moments.
