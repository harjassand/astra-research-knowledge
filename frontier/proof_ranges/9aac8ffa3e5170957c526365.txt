## 2. Definitions and normalization

Let \(\mathcal N:\mathcal L(A)\to\mathcal L(B)\) be completely positive and trace preserving. Write \(d=\dim A\), \(m=\dim B\), and \(\mathcal N_R=\operatorname{id}_R\otimes\mathcal N\). References are arbitrary finite-dimensional quantum systems. Logs are natural logs; the ratios are independent of their base.

For states of finite relative entropy,
\[
D(\rho\|\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma).
\]
Define
\[
\eta_D^c(\mathcal N)
=\sup_{\substack{R,\rho,\sigma:\rho_R=\sigma_R\\0<D(\rho\|\sigma)<\infty}}
\frac{D(\mathcal N_R\rho\|\mathcal N_R\sigma)}{D(\rho\|\sigma)}, \tag{2.1}
\]
\[
\eta_D^\Delta(\mathcal N)
=\sup_{\substack{R,\rho,\sigma\\0<D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_R\|\sigma_R)<\infty}}
\frac{D(\mathcal N_R\rho\|\mathcal N_R\sigma)-D(\rho_R\|\sigma_R)}
{D(\rho\|\sigma)-D(\rho_R\|\sigma_R)}, \tag{2.2}
\]
where both relative entropies before subtraction are finite, and
\[
\eta_{\mathrm{CMI}}(\mathcal N)
=\sup_{\rho_{URA}:I(U;A|R)>0}
\frac{I(U;B|R)_{\mathcal N(\rho)}}{I(U;A|R)_\rho}. \tag{2.3}
\]
Here \(I(U;A|R)=S(UR)+S(AR)-S(R)-S(UAR)\). An empty supremum is defined as zero, covering a one-dimensional input.

Use the **unnormalized** Choi matrix
\[
J=\sum_{i,j=1}^d |i\rangle\langle j|\otimes
\mathcal N(|i\rangle\langle j|),\qquad \operatorname{Tr}_B J=I_A. \tag{2.4}
\]
All occurrences of \(J^+\) denote the Moore--Penrose inverse. A normalized Choi state cannot be substituted into the following formula without changing the scale.

## 3. Exact Choi formula

**Theorem.** Let \(M(\mathcal N)\) be the value of the following finite semidefinite program:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{minimize}\quad &t\\
\text{over}\quad &\sigma\succeq0,\quad\operatorname{Tr}\sigma=1,
\quad Z=Z^*,\quad t\in\mathbb R,\\
\text{subject to}\quad &
\begin{pmatrix}J&I_A\otimes\sigma\\I_A\otimes\sigma&Z\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
\qquad\operatorname{Tr}_B Z\preceq tI_A.
\end{aligned} \tag{3.1}
\]
Set \(M=\infty\) if the program is infeasible. Then
\[
\boxed{\quad\eta_D^c(\mathcal N)=\eta_D^\Delta(\mathcal N)
=\eta_{\mathrm{CMI}}(\mathcal N)=1-\frac1{M(\mathcal N)}.\quad} \tag{3.2}
\]
When finite, \(M\ge1\). The supremum in (2.3) is approached with **binary classical** \(U\), **exactly** \(I(U;R)=I(U;A)=0\), and \(\dim R\le m^2+1\). The same reference bound suffices in (2.1).

Equivalently,
\[
M(\mathcal N)=\min_\sigma
\left\|\operatorname{Tr}_B[(I\otimes\sigma)J^+(I\otimes\sigma)]\right\|_\infty, \tag{3.3}
\]
over density matrices satisfying
\[
\operatorname{ran}(I\otimes\sigma)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}J. \tag{3.4}
\]
The range condition is essential; omitting it gives wrong answers for singular Choi matrices.

### Proof plan

A centered matrix Gram problem equals a rectangular inverse-matrix contraction problem. Stinespring factorization solves the Gram problem in terms of (3.3). Its positive optimizer gives a signed lift through an erasure channel, proving the universal upper bound. A coherent reference flag proves sharpness with an exactly fixed marginal.

## 4. The finite centered Gram problem

Put \(\Phi=\mathcal N^*\), a unital completely positive map. Choose any complex basis \(F_1,\ldots,F_n\) of \(\mathcal L(B)\), where \(n=m^2\). Let
\[
\mathbf A=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi(F_1)\\\vdots\\\Phi(F_n)\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \mathbf G=[\Phi(F_iF_j^*)]_{i,j=1}^n.
\]
Define \(\eta_G\) as the least \(\eta\) for which a scalar column \(a\in\mathbb C^n\) satisfies
\[
(\mathbf A-a\otimes I_A)(\mathbf A-a\otimes I_A)^*
\preceq\eta\mathbf G. \tag{4.1}
\]
Complete Schwarz gives feasibility at \(\eta=1,a=0\). The minimizer exists: for bounded \(\eta\), (4.1) bounds \(\|\mathbf A-a\otimes I\|\), hence bounds \(a\). Any other finite tuple of output operators is a scalar linear combination of the basis tuple, so (4.1) controls every tuple, not merely this chosen basis.

### 4.1 Rectangular quadratic forms

For \(C\succ0\) on \(R_0\otimes A\) and a block column \(B:A\to R_0\otimes A\), define
\[
q_C(B)=\operatorname{Tr}B^*C^{-1}B.
\]
Both \(\mathcal N(B)\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}_A B\) mean blockwise application. Thus \(\operatorname{Tr}_A B=0\) means that every block of the column has trace zero. Set
\[
\eta_{\mathrm{rect}}=\sup_{\substack{R_0,C\succ0,B\ne0\\\operatorname{Tr}_A B=0}}
\frac{q_{\mathcal N_{R_0}(C)}(\mathcal N(B))}{q_C(B)}. \tag{4.2}
\]
Unused output support is removed once and for all: on \(\operatorname{supp}\mathcal N(I)\), a faithful input produces a faithful output. Normalizing \(\operatorname{Tr}C=1\) does not change a ratio.

**Lemma 4.1.** \(\eta_{\mathrm{rect}}=\eta_G\), and \(\dim R_0=n\) suffices for the supremum.

**Proof.** Weighted Hilbert-space duality gives, for any block column \(F\),
\[
\sup_{\substack{B\ne0\\\operatorname{Tr}_A B=0}}
\frac{|\operatorname{Tr}B^*\Phi(F)|^2}{q_C(B)}
=\inf_a\operatorname{Tr}C(\Phi(F)-a\otimes I)(\Phi(F)-a\otimes I)^*. \tag{4.3}
\]
Indeed the annihilator of the trace-zero block columns, under the Hilbert--Schmidt pairing, consists exactly of scalar block columns \(a\otimes I\). Taking the distance to this annihilator in the dual weighted norm proves (4.3). Equivalently, it is the usual orthogonal-projection theorem applied after multiplying columns by \(C^{-1/2}\).

The dual squared norm of an unrestricted output test \(F\) is
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\mathcal N(C)FF^*=\operatorname{Tr}C\Phi(FF^*).
\]
Consequently (4.1), and its scalar compressions, imply \(\eta_{\mathrm{rect}}\le\eta_G\).

Conversely fix \(0\le\eta<\eta_G\), use the basis tuple, and let \(C\) range over density matrices on \(\mathbb C^n\otimes A\). The finite-dimensional convex minimax identity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\sup_C\inf_a\operatorname{Tr}C
[(\mathbf A-aI)(\mathbf A-aI)^*-\eta\mathbf G]\\
&\qquad=\inf_a\lambda_{\max}
[(\mathbf A-aI)(\mathbf A-aI)^*-\eta\mathbf G]>0. \tag{4.4}
\end{aligned}
\]
For completeness, the minimax hypotheses are: the density matrices form a compact convex set; the function is affine and continuous in \(C\), and convex continuous in the real and imaginary parts of \(a\). The right-hand objective is coercive in \(a\), so its infimum is attained. If that minimum were nonpositive, (4.1) would be feasible at \(\eta\), a contradiction.

Some \(C\) therefore makes the infimum positive. It can be made faithful by mixing in a small positive multiple of the identity: the infimum is concave in \(C\), and is bounded below on the density matrices by \(-\eta\|\mathbf G\|\). Positivity thus persists for sufficiently small mixing.

For this faithful \(C\), (4.3) supplies a trace-zero block column \(B\) whose dual output test already gives a ratio exceeding \(\eta\). The full output norm is at least that test value. Hence \(\eta_{\mathrm{rect}}\ge\eta_G\). This construction uses \(R_0=\mathbb C^n\). \(\square\)

## 5. Solving the Gram problem by Stinespring geometry

Choose a minimal Stinespring isometry \(V:A\to B\otimes E\), so
\[
\Phi(Y)=V^*(Y\otimes I_E)V.
\]
The Gram inequality (4.1) is equivalent to the existence of a map \(K:B\otimes E\to A\), with \(\|K\|^2\le\eta\), satisfying
\[
K(Y\otimes I)V=\Phi(Y)-\alpha(Y)I_A \tag{5.1}
\]
for a scalar complex-linear functional \(\alpha\).

Here is the factorization explicitly. With \(T_i=(F_i^*\otimes I)V\), the block matrix \(\mathbf G\) is \(T^*T\), where \(T\) is the row of the \(T_i\). If \(S=\mathbf A-aI\), define \(K(Tv)=S^*v\). The inequality \(SS^*\preceq\eta T^*T\) makes this well-defined and bounded by \(\sqrt\eta\), and extension by zero on \((\operatorname{ran}T)^\perp\) gives \(K\). Its values on the adjoint basis define \(\alpha(F_i^*)=\overline{a_i}\). This is a bijective reparametrization of the scalar centers. The converse follows by taking Gram matrices.

Set \(L=V^*-K\). Equation (5.1) becomes
\[
L(Y\otimes I)V=\alpha(Y)I. \tag{5.2}
\]
If \(t=\alpha(I)=0\), then \(LV=0\) and
\(\|V^*-L\|^2=1+\|L\|^2\ge1\). If \(t\ne0\), write \(L=tS\), so \(SV=I\), and decompose
\[
S=V^*+S_\perp,\qquad S_\perp V=0.
\]
The cross terms vanish, giving
\[
\|V^*-tS\|^2=|1-t|^2+|t|^2\|S_\perp\|^2. \tag{5.3}
\]
For fixed \(r=\|S_\perp\|^2\), the minimum over \(t\in\mathbb C\) is \(r/(1+r)\), attained at \(t=1/(1+r)\). Also
\[
SS^*=I+S_\perp S_\perp^*,\qquad \|S\|^2=1+r. \tag{5.4}
\]
Thus it remains to minimize \(\|S\|^2\) over the normalized solutions of (5.2).

### 5.1 The Choi inverse and the positive center

Write a minimal Kraus representation
\[
V=\sum_jK_j\otimes|j\rangle,
\qquad S=\sum_jL_j^*\otimes\langle j|.
\]
Use \(|K\rangle\rangle=\sum_i|i\rangle\otimes K|i\rangle\). Normalized (5.2) says that, for a possibly complex \(H\) with \(\operatorname{Tr}H=1\),
\[
\sum_j|L_j\rangle\rangle\langle\langle K_j|=I_A\otimes H. \tag{5.5}
\]
With this vectorization convention the functional in (5.2) is \(Y\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(H^*Y)\). This distinction disappears when \(H\) is positive, as it will be at the optimum.

Let \(M_K\) have columns \(|K_j\rangle\rangle\). Minimality makes the columns independent and \(J=M_KM_K^*\). Equation (5.5) has the solution
\[
M_L=(I\otimes H)J^+M_K \tag{5.6}
\]
exactly when \(\operatorname{ran}(I\otimes H^*)\subseteq\operatorname{ran}J\). It is unique for this minimal environment. Direct multiplication gives
\[
\|S\|^2=
\left\|\operatorname{Tr}_B[(I\otimes H)J^+(I\otimes H^*)]\right\|_\infty. \tag{5.7}
\]
A transpose on the remaining \(A\) factor can arise from vectorization; it leaves the operator norm unchanged.

Partial-trace cyclicity on \(B\) shows that the matrix in (5.7) depends on \(H\) only through \(H^*H\). Moreover \(\operatorname{ran}H^*=\operatorname{ran}|H|\). Replacing \(H\) by
\[
\sigma=|H|/\operatorname{Tr}|H|
\]
preserves admissibility and divides (5.7) by \((\operatorname{Tr}|H|)^2\ge|\operatorname{Tr}H|^2=1\). Hence the optimizer can be taken to be a **positive density matrix**, not merely a complex affine center.

We have proved
\[
\eta_G=1-1/M,
\]
with \(M\) given by (3.3)--(3.4). If there is no normalized solution, the only possible Gram optimum is one. When there is one, the admissible densities form a compact set and the objective is continuous, so the minimum is attained. The relation \(SV=I\) also proves \(M\ge1\).

Finally, the generalized Schur-complement criterion says
\[
\begin{pmatrix}J&A\\A&Z\end{pmatrix}\succeq0
\iff \operatorname{ran}A\subseteq\operatorname{ran}J,
\quad Z\succeq AJ^+A
\]
for \(A=I\otimes\sigma\succeq0\). Positivity of the partial trace then gives exactly (3.1).

## 6. Universal entropy upper bound

Suppose \(1<M<\infty\); the endpoints are handled below. Choose the optimal positive center \(\sigma\), its normalized \(S\), and let
\[
K=S-V^*,\quad KV=0,\quad r=\|K\|^2=M-1,
\quad\alpha(Y)=\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma Y).
\]
By (5.2),
\[
\Phi(Y)-\alpha(Y)I=-K(I-VV^*)(Y\otimes I)V.
\]
Therefore
\[
(\Phi(Y)-\alpha(Y)I)^*(\Phi(Y)-\alpha(Y)I)
\preceq r[\Phi(Y^*Y)-\Phi(Y)^*\Phi(Y)]. \tag{6.1}
\]
This holds at every reference amplification because \(K\) is tensored with the identity. Apply it to \(Y^*\) for the other multiplication order. Positivity of \(\sigma\) makes \(\alpha\) adjoint preserving.

Put \(\eta=r/(1+r)\), and let \(\mathcal E_\eta\) be the quantum erasure channel that reveals its input with probability \(\eta\). Define a generally nonpositive observable lift \(\mathcal J\) by
\[
(\mathcal JY)_{\mathrm{reveal}}
=\frac{\Phi(Y)-(1-\eta)\alpha(Y)I}{\eta},
\qquad(\mathcal JY)_{\mathrm{erase}}=\alpha(Y). \tag{6.2}
\]
It is adjoint preserving and \(\mathcal E_\eta^*\mathcal J=\Phi\). Its exact square defect is
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\Phi(Y^*Y)-\eta(\mathcal JY)_r^*(\mathcal JY)_r
 -(1-\eta)\alpha(Y)^*\alpha(Y)\\
&=\Phi(Y^*Y)-\Phi(Y)^*\Phi(Y)
 -\frac{1-\eta}{\eta}(\Phi(Y)-\alpha(Y)I)^*(\Phi(Y)-\alpha(Y)I)
\succeq0. \tag{6.3}
\end{aligned}
\]
The analogous identity holds for \(YY^*\).

### 6.1 Why the signed lift controls relative entropy

The following argument is included explicitly; no physical post-processing interpretation is being assumed for \(\mathcal J\). It is a version of the left/right-to-BKM mechanism in [2].

For a positive faithful matrix \(\omega\), define left and right multiplication \(L_\omega(X)=\omega X\), \(R_\omega(X)=X\omega\), and
\[
\mathcal A_{\omega,u}=(1-u)L_\omega+uR_\omega,\qquad0\le u\le1.
\]
Taking expectations of (6.3) and its opposite-order version in any faithful reference-input state \(\omega\) gives
\[
\mathcal J^*\mathcal A_{\mathcal E_\eta(\omega),u}\mathcal J
\preceq\mathcal A_{\mathcal N(\omega),u}. \tag{6.4}
\]
All these maps include the unchanged reference. For a positive definite operator \(A\), elementary completion of the square gives
\[
\langle z,A^{-1}z\rangle
=\sup_x\{2\Re\langle x,z\rangle-\langle x,Ax\rangle\}. \tag{6.5}
\]
Restricting the variational domain on the erasure side to \(\mathcal Jx\) in (6.5), and using (6.4), yields
\[
\langle\mathcal J^*z,\mathcal A_{\mathcal N(\omega),u}^{-1}\mathcal J^*z\rangle
\le\langle z,\mathcal A_{\mathcal E_\eta(\omega),u}^{-1}z\rangle. \tag{6.6}
\]
In an eigenbasis of \(\omega\),
\[
\int_0^1\frac{du}{(1-u)\lambda_i+u\lambda_j}
=\frac{\log\lambda_i-\log\lambda_j}{\lambda_i-\lambda_j}.
\]
Thus the integral of the quadratic form in (6.6) is exactly the Hessian \(g_\omega\) of \(\operatorname{Tr}\omega\log\omega\), also called the BKM metric. Set \(z=\mathcal E_\eta(H)\); the reconstruction identity gives \(\mathcal J^*z=\mathcal N(H)\). Hence
\[
g_{\mathcal N(\omega)}(\mathcal N(H))
\le g_{\mathcal E_\eta(\omega)}(\mathcal E_\eta(H)). \tag{6.7}
\]
Finally, for \(H=\rho-\tau\), the elementary integral remainder for this entropy potential is
\[
D(\rho\|\tau)=\int_0^1(1-s)g_{\tau+sH}(H)\,ds. \tag{6.8}
\]
Integrating (6.7) proves
\[
\boxed{
D(\mathcal N_R\rho\|\mathcal N_R\tau)
\le\eta D(\rho\|\tau)+(1-\eta)D(\rho_R\|\tau_R).
} \tag{6.9}
\]
The right side is exactly the relative entropy after erasure, since its two branches are orthogonal.

For singular states with finite input relative entropy, mix both compared states with the same faithful state. Joint convexity gives the needed upper limiting bound on relative entropy; lower semicontinuity gives the reverse one. These also apply after the channel and partial trace. Passing to the limit proves (6.9) without hidden faithfulness assumptions. If \(M=1\), (6.1) with \(r=0\) says \(\Phi(Y)=\alpha(Y)I\), so the channel is a replacer and the conclusion is immediate. If \(M=\infty\), ordinary data processing gives the upper constant one.

Subtract the reference relative entropy from (6.9) to obtain \(\eta_D^\Delta\le1-1/M\). Equal-reference pairs give \(\eta_D^c\le1-1/M\). To obtain the CMI bound, apply (6.9) with reference \(UR\), first state \(\rho_{URA}\), and second state \(\rho_U\otimes\rho_{RA}\). The two sides subtract \(I(U;R)\) and become the conditional mutual informations in (2.3).
