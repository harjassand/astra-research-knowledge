The remaining question is whether the implicit current’s energy can also be bounded without expanding its histories.
Let \(\rho_t\) be the density, relative to equilibrium, of the unnormalised positive-source measure at microscopic clock \(t\). A direct antisymmetrisation and Cauchy–Schwarz argument gives
\[ \boxed{ \mathbb E\sum_b w_bH_b^2 \le16T^2\sup_t\mathbb E\rho_t^2, } \tag{7} \]
where \(T\) bounds the path length and \(w_b\le4\) accounts for overlapping modules.
The task is therefore to derive the density bound, not supply it as an assumption.
For one framed block, define
\[ R_L(p)= 16\prod_{k=2}^{L-1} \max\{1,V_k(p)\}\max\{1,V_{k+1}(p)\}. \tag{8} \]
The factor 16 bounds the seed preparation. The remaining factors come from the exact strip moments. During a strip rotation, the additional factor is at most \(L^2\).
The proof also pays for information that could otherwise be hidden:
Seed selection. There are at most
\[ S=L^4/16 \]
pairs of seed locations. Their contributions are bounded without enumerating pivot histories.
Prepared port positions. There are at most \(N^2\) position pairs, where
\[ N=2L^2. \]
Conditioning on these positions makes each middle transposition word a fixed permutation.
The simulated occupied port. One simulated occupied token represents an actual vacancy. Forgetting its location costs an explicit factor \(N^2q/p\) in squared density. It is not treated as a free label.
Inverse preparation. The full port-transposition word is palindromic. Its reversal has the same history probability when started from the opposite port assignment. This controls the return phase without pretending that the program’s random history disappears for free.
These estimates give
\[ \sup_t\mathbb E\rho_t^2 \le S^2N^6L^2\frac qp\,R_L(p)^2. \tag{9} \]
A uniform microscopic length bound is
\[ T=256L^3. \]
Writing \(\gamma=\mathbb Eg\), the resulting module cost satisfies
\[ \boxed{ C_L(p)\le \frac{8T^2S^2N^6L^2R_L(p)^2}{p^2\gamma^2}. } \tag{10} \]
The earlier nonlocal completion supplies its explicit coarse energy factor \(\mathcal K(b)\), with \(b=1-\gamma\). Substituting a certified gate lower bound \(f\le\gamma\) gives
\[ \boxed{ D(p)\ge \frac{p^2f^2} {8T^2S^2N^6L^2R_L(p)^2\mathcal K(1-f)}. } \tag{11} \]
Every term is obtained from finite scalar calculations. No unknown spectral gap, supplied completion-error bound, global corrector or affordable inverse-history oracle appears.
The variational conversion from an admissible current to a diffusion lower bound remains the established Thomson-type principle. It is not the claimed new operation. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04636v2)
For \(p=4/5\), \(L=4096\), the exact calculation gives
\[ f\ge0.857598203285,\qquad \mathcal K(1-f)\le81.1958251981 \]
with the decimals displayed descriptively; the certificate uses rational quantities.
The resulting inverse lower bound is compared with a power of ten using integers, establishing
\[ D(4/5)\ge10^{-11593}. \]
A deterministic first-vacancy implementation of the same overall framing strategy incurred a factorial history bound and gave only \(10^{-52114}\). The balanced calculation removes that additional loss. The improvement is large algebraically, but both endpoints remain useless for numerical prediction.
The upper endpoint \(0.238144\) comes from the elementary zero-corrector formula \((1-p^3)^2\). It is not a competitive use of the specialised primal methods already available for KA. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10616)
