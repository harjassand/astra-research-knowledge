## 5. Sharp work-transfer bound for this implementation

Drive p_1 and connect the receiver to a passive hydraulic resistance R, so p_2=-R Q_2. Then

    Q_1=kappa_1 p_1,
    Q_2=b p_1/(1+kappa_2 R),
    eta(R)=R Q_2^2/(p_1 Q_1).

The maximum over R is attained at R=1/kappa_2, yielding

    eta_load,max = b^2/(4 kappa_1 kappa_2).

Define the measured whole-device coupling quantities

    chi_i = C_i^2/(K_i G_i),
    Z_i = chi_i/(1-chi_i),
    t_i = g/G_i.

Then

    b^2/(kappa_1 kappa_2)
      = product_i [chi_i t_i/((1+t_i)(1+t_i-chi_i))].

For 0<chi<1 the factor

    f(t)=chi t/((1+t)(1+t-chi))

has derivative with the sign of (1-chi)-t^2. Its unique maximum is at t=sqrt(1-chi), where

    f_max = (1-sqrt(1-chi))/(1+sqrt(1-chi))
          = (sqrt(1+Z)-1)/(sqrt(1+Z)+1)
          =: eta_c(Z).

Therefore

    eta_isolator <= eta_c(Z_1) eta_c(Z_2)/4 <= 1/4.

The first inequality is attainable within the model when both electrical matching conditions can be realized and the output hydraulic load is optimized. A fabricated pair may not permit simultaneous matching. For identical devices the bound is eta_c(Z)^2/4.

**Scope:** this is a ceiling for this specific contact-inclusive, grounded three-contact, quasistatic topology. It is not a universal 25% bound on all nonreciprocal transducers, active devices, hydraulic isolators, or finite-frequency circuits.
