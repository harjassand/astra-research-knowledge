## 4. Positivity forces every Taylor jet to be radial

Write the formal Taylor expansion at zero in the chosen basis as
\[
F_q\left(\sum_jx_je_j\right)\sim
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb N^d}c_\alpha x^\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]
Smoothness supplies Taylor expansions to every finite order, with controlled remainders near zero. Thus, along the orbit of (8), for every positive cutoff \(R\),
\[
F_q(e^{tL}u)
=
\sum_{\alpha\cdot\lambda<R}
 c_\alpha x^\alpha e^{-(\alpha\cdot\lambda)t}
+O(e^{-Rt}),
\tag{11}
\]
when \(R\) avoids the listed exponents. To see this directly, truncate the ordinary Taylor expansion at a degree \(M\) with \((M+1)\min_j\lambda_j>R\); terms above the cutoff are absorbed into the remainder. Terms of sufficiently high total degree cannot have exponent below \(R\).

Rational independence makes \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha\cdot\lambda\) injective. Its image is locally finite. Complete monotonicity, continuity at \(t=0\), Bernstein's theorem, and the extraction lemma therefore imply
\[
c_\alpha x^\alpha\geq0
\tag{12}
\]
for every sufficiently small real vector \(x\).

If any coordinate of \(\alpha\) is odd, changing that coordinate's sign in (12) forces \(c_\alpha=0\). If every coordinate is even, then \(c_\alpha\geq0\).

The basis was arbitrary. Consequently every homogeneous Taylor polynomial is invariant under reflection in every hyperplane of \(H_q\), and hence under its full orthogonal group. An orthogonally invariant homogeneous polynomial is zero in odd degree and a multiple of \(\|u\|_q^{2k}\) in degree \(2k\). The coefficients are nonnegative by (12). Therefore the Taylor jets are exactly
\[
F_q(u)\sim\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k},
\qquad a_k(q)\geq0.
\tag{13}
\]
This argument also applies when \(d=1\): the only nontrivial orthogonal transformation is reflection.

## 5. Recovering the functional globally without assuming analyticity

Take an arbitrary \(u\in U_q\), not necessarily small. Under reset,
\[
e^{tL_0}u=e^{-t}u.
\]
As \(t\to\infty\), the trajectory enters the smooth neighborhood of zero, where (13) gives the full asymptotic expansion
\[
F_q(e^{-t}u)\sim
\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k}e^{-2kt}.
\]
Apply Bernstein's theorem and the extraction lemma again. The positive representing measure is exactly
\[
\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k}\delta_{2k}.
\]
Taking \(t\downarrow0\), monotone convergence and continuity yield
\[
F_q(u)=\sum_{k\geq1}a_k(q)\|u\|_q^{2k}.
\tag{14}
\]
The sum is finite because \(F_q(u)\) is finite. This proves an actual global series identity, not merely a statement about derivatives at equilibrium. It also excludes nonzero smooth flat remainders.
