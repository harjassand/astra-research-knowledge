3. A bounded number of witnesses replaces inverse failure probability
Let \(N(X)\) count entries into \(\mathcal K\): a facet is crossed inward while the remaining inequalities hold strictly.
Define
\[
W(X)=\mathbf 1_{\{X(0)\in\mathcal K\}}+N(X).
\tag{6}
\]
Almost surely,
\[
E\iff W>0.
\]
Each scalar trigonometric polynomial \(a_i^\top X(t)-b_i\) has at most \(2d\) roots and hence at most \(d\) upcrossings. Consequently,
\[
\boxed{
1\le W(X)\le K_*:=md+1
\quad\text{on }E.
}
\tag{7}
\]
The initial-state witness is necessary: a trajectory can remain inside the region throughout the period without crossing a boundary.
Let
\[
\mu_0=\Pr[X(0)\in\mathcal K],
\]
and let \(\mu_i\) be the expected number of entries through facet \(i\). Put
\[
M=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\mu_i=\mathbb E W.
\tag{8}
\]
Construct a proposal law \(Q\) by mixing:
- the trajectory law conditional on \(X(0)\in\mathcal K\), with weight \(\mu_0/M\);
- the trajectory law seen from an inward crossing of facet \(i\), with weight \(\mu_i/M\), placed at a uniform phase.
The weighted Rice identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{dQ}{dP}(X)=\frac{W(X)}{M}.
}
\tag{9}
\]
One way to derive it is to integrate a narrow strip around each facet, weighted by positive normal velocity, and let the strip width tend to zero. Each simple crossing contributes once. Applying the same calculation with an arbitrary bounded trajectory functional proves the change-of-measure identity, not merely its normalization.
It follows that
\[
\boxed{
p=M\,\mathbb E_Q\!\left[\frac1W\right].
}
\tag{10}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\frac{M}{K_*}\le p\le M.
\]
For the ideal estimator \(Y=M/W\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_QY^2
&=M\,\mathbb E_P\!\left[\frac{\mathbf1_E}{W}\right]\\
&\le Mp\\
&\le K_*p^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\operatorname{Var}_QY}{p^2}\le K_*-1=md.
}
\tag{11}
\]
Only
\[
O\!\left(
K_*\varepsilon^{-2}\log\delta^{-1}
\right)
\tag{12}
\]
independent proposal trajectories are needed.
There is also an exact ideal conditional sampler: propose from \(Q\), then accept with probability \(1/W\). Its accepted law is \(P(\,\cdot\mid E)\), and its acceptance probability is
\[
p/M\ge1/K_*.
\tag{13}
\]
These identities are useful only if \(Q\) and \(M\) can actually be acquired. That is the next step.
4. Acquiring an inward crossing without rare rejection
Fix facet \(i\), and examine time zero. Define
\[
U=a_i^\top X(0),\qquad
V=a_i^\top X'(0).
\]
Delete one coordinate of \(X(0)\) to obtain \(Y\in\mathbb R^{r-1}\). On \(U=b_i\), the missing coordinate is recovered by a rational affine map
\[
x=x_i(Y).
\]
The conditional law
\[
(Y,V)\mid U=b_i
\]
is an explicitly computable, nondegenerate Gaussian with rational mean and covariance. Write its density as \(g_i(y,v)\).
Rice’s formula gives
\[
\mu_i
=
2\pi\,\varphi_U(b_i)
\int
v_+\,
\mathbf1_{\{a_j^\top x_i(y)>b_j,\ j\ne i\}}
g_i(y,v)\,dy\,dv.
\tag{14}
\]
Here \(\varphi_U\) is the univariate Gaussian density of \(U\).
Why a naive crossing proposal fails
Conditioning only on \(U=b_i\) and \(V>0\), then rejecting until the other inequalities hold, is insufficient.
For independent coordinates and a region requiring all \(r\) coordinates to exceed \(u\), that rejection can cost a factor
\[
\overline\Phi(u)^{-(r-1)}.
\]
The rare-event difficulty has merely moved into proposal generation.
Convex lifting of the flux
Introduce one auxiliary height \(s\), and define
\[
D_i=
\left\{
(y,v,s):
\begin{array}{l}
a_j^\top x_i(y)\ge b_j,\quad j\ne i,\\
v\ge0,\quad0\le s\le v
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{15}
\]
Then
\[
\int_{D_i}g_i(y,v)\,dy\,dv\,ds
=
\int v_+\mathbf1_{\{\text{other inequalities}\}}g_i(y,v)\,dy\,dv.
\tag{16}
\]
The set \(D_i\) is convex. The density is the exponential of a negative convex quadratic. Therefore its integral and its normalized distribution are log-concave integration and sampling problems in dimension \(r+1\).
Sampling this distribution and discarding \(s\) yields exactly the required inward-flux law. The other failure inequalities are built into the sampling distribution, rather than tested by rare rejection.
General log-concave integration and sampling are established algorithmic capabilities. Modern work by Kook and Vempala includes acquisition of starting distributions and geometric rounding; it does not require treating a stationary-distribution sampler as free. The construction here must supply the geometric and arithmetic inputs to those algorithms. arXiv
Position and velocity must remain correlated
Stationarity implies that \(U\) and its own derivative \(V\) are uncorrelated. It does not imply that the remaining position coordinates \(Y\) are independent of \(V\).
The covariance matrices \(T_j\) can create precisely those correlations. Replacing \(g_i(y,v)\) by a product of its marginals would generally change the crossing intensity and the proposed trajectory law. The lifted sampler retains the full joint Gaussian.
