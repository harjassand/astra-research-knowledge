## 3. Why no finite catalyst works for a product-free support

The decisive step combines two different controls.

A sufficiently entangled support forces an entanglement quantity to grow with the number of output registers. But a finite initial catalyst places a bound on the Schmidt number of **every successful branch**, independently of its probability.

The probability-independent bound is essential. An average-resource inequality alone would not exclude an arbitrarily rare successful branch.

### A support-persistence lemma

Let
\[
U\subseteq A\otimes B
\]
be a nonzero subspace containing no nonzero product vector. Then there exists a constant \(\kappa_U>0\) such that every state \(\rho_n\) supported on \(U^{\otimes n}\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
E_R(\rho_n)\ge n\kappa_U.
}
\tag{10}
\]

Here
\[
E_R(\rho)=\inf_{\gamma\in\operatorname{SEP}}
D(\rho\Vert\gamma),
\]
with natural logarithms and the bipartition
\(A_1\cdots A_n:B_1\cdots B_n\).

The outputs in (10) may be arbitrarily correlated. No independent-copy assumption is made.

#### Proof

Fix a product informationally complete measurement
\[
\mathsf M=\mathsf M_A\otimes\mathsf M_B.
\]
Its effects can be chosen strictly positive.

The compact set of states supported on \(U\) is disjoint from the compact set of separable states. Indeed, a separable state supported on \(U\) would supply product vectors in \(U\).

Informational completeness makes the map from states to outcome distributions injective. Therefore
\[
\kappa_U
=
\min_{\substack{\rho:\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq U\\
\gamma\in\operatorname{SEP}}}
D\!\left(\mathsf M(\rho)\middle\Vert\mathsf M(\gamma)\right)
>0.
\tag{11}
\]

Now compare \(\rho_n\) with any separable \(\gamma_n\). Measure the first output pair and discard it, retaining the classical result. Relative-entropy data processing gives
\[
D(\rho_n\Vert\gamma_n)
\ge
D(p\Vert q)
+
\sum_y p_yD(\rho_{n-1|y}\Vert\gamma_{n-1|y}).
\tag{12}
\]

The first term is at least \(\kappa_U\). Each actual conditional remainder remains supported on \(U^{\otimes(n-1)}\). Each comparator remainder is separable, because the measurement is local.

Induction gives
\[
D(\rho_n\Vert\gamma_n)\ge n\kappa_U.
\]
Taking the infimum over \(\gamma_n\) proves (10). ∎

This is an application of the restricted-measurement relative-entropy method developed by Piani, not a newly invented entropy inequality. The role here is to make the bound uniform over all correlated states in a specified tensor-product support. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.2705)
