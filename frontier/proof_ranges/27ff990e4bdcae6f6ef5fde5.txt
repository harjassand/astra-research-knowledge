## 5. A constructive spatial completion on the coarse lattice

Declare a coarse edge open when both endpoint blocks are good. Its being closed is therefore determined by an independent Bernoulli-b field of bad sites.

Draw the dual edges crossing closed primal edges. Each bad site contributes a square of four closed dual edges. These squares belong to the same connected component exactly when the associated bad sites can be joined using eight-neighbor, or star, adjacency.

For a dual vertex z, let C_z be its closed-dual component, allowing isolated vertices, and let

\[
R_z=\max_{w\in C_z}\|w-z\|_\infty.
\]

When C_z is finite, define

\[
\Psi(z)=\frac1{|C_z|}\sum_{w\in C_z} w_2,
\qquad \beta(z)=\Psi(z)-z_2. \tag{8}
\]

Then take the rotated discrete gradient of Psi. In integer labels for dual plaquettes,

\[
k_h(x,y)=\Psi(x,y)-\Psi(x,y-1),
\quad
k_v(x,y)=\Psi(x-1,y)-\Psi(x,y). \tag{9}
\]

It has zero spatial divergence identically. It is zero on a closed edge because its two dual endpoints belong to the same component and hence have the same Psi.

The correction beta is stationary. If it is integrable, stationarity gives E k_h=1 and E k_v=0, since the affine part contributes exactly the unit horizontal current.

### Explicit tail from a finite path automaton

This step does not invoke an unspecified subcritical percolation theorem.

If R_z >= r, a bad star-connected path from one of the four sites adjacent to z reaches a dual vertex at distance r. Such a path has at least r distinct bad sites. Choose a shortest path to that far site. No two nonconsecutive vertices can be star neighbors, since that would provide a shortcut.

A necessary local restriction is that a path cannot make a two-step shortcut. When the previous step is cardinal, the next step has one cardinal and two diagonal choices. When the previous step is diagonal, there are two cardinal and three diagonal choices. Thus a two-state counting matrix is

\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&3\end{pmatrix}. \tag{10}
\]

The checker independently enumerates the eight possible step directions to verify these four counts. The matrix overcounts globally chordless self-avoiding paths; this is valid because each actual path has r distinct sites and hence probability b^r. We do not assign b^r to repeated-site walks and then treat their probabilities as independent.

With u=(1,1)^T and A=bM,

\[
\mathbb P(R_z\ge1)\le4b,
\qquad
\mathbb P(R_z\ge r)\le16b^2 u^T A^{r-2}u\quad(r\ge2).
\tag{11}
\]

The largest eigenvalue of M is 2+sqrt(5). Therefore the condition for this explicit summable tail is

\[
\Delta(b):=1-4b-b^2>0,
\quad\text{equivalently }b<\sqrt5-2. \tag{12}
\]

All components C_z are finite almost surely under this condition, by (11) and a countable union over z. No unknown cluster-size constant remains.

Summing (2r-1) times (11) gives

\[
\mathbb E R_z^2\le
m_2(b):=4b+\frac{32b^2(3-4b+b^2)}{(1-4b-b^2)^2}. \tag{13}
\]

For completeness, S=(I-A)^{-1} exists with nonnegative entries,

\[
u^TSu=\frac2\Delta,
\quad
\sum_{r\ge2}(2r-1)u^TA^{r-2}u
=u^T(3S+2AS^2)u,
\]

which gives (13) by direct rational simplification.

Since |beta(z)| <= R_z, stationarity and (9) imply

\[
K:=\mathbb E(k_h^2+k_v^2)
=1+\mathbb E|\nabla\beta|^2
\le 1+8m_2(b)=:\mathcal K(b). \tag{14}
\]

The initial investigation used the weaker seven-choice self-avoiding-path count. Equation (10) replaces it; all headline bounds in this record use the stronger two-state certificate.
