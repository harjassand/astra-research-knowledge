5. Why a negative certificate produces a real kernel
This direction needs a substantive import. Abo Khamis, Nakos, Olteanu and Suciu prove that, under entropy bounds conditioned on at most one variable, optimization over polymatroids is attained by normal polymatroids. Their result credits earlier work on bag-query containment. This is the precise reason an abstract relaxed certificate can be realized here; I do not assume that arbitrary Shannon relaxations are tight. arXiv
A normal polymatroid has the form
\[
h(S)
=
\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V}
\alpha_A\,\mathbf1_{\{A\cap S\ne\varnothing\}},
\qquad \alpha_A\geq0.
\tag{7}
\]
From deficits to finite relations
Take an optimum \((d,\mathbf k)\) with \(d(V)=\gamma\leq1\). Choose a sufficiently large integer \(M\) and set
\[
h(S)=M|S|-d(S).
\]
This is a polymatroid. Its constraints become
\[
h(u)\leq M,\qquad h(uv)\leq2M-pk_{uv},
\]
\[
h(uv)-h(u)\leq M-k_{uv},
\qquad
h(uv)-h(v)\leq M-k_{uv}.
\tag{8}
\]
All conditioning sets are empty or singletons. The imported theorem supplies a normal \(h'\) satisfying these bounds with
\[
h'(V)\geq nM-\gamma.
\]
At scale \(L\), realize each coefficient \(\alpha_A\) by a shared coordinate with approximately \(2^{L\alpha_A}\) symbols. Vertex \(u\) stores the coordinates whose sets contain \(u\). Pad every vertex alphabet to size approximately \(2^{LM}\).
The resulting joint relation \(T_L\) has probability at least
\[
c\,2^{-L\gamma}.
\]
Its projection \(R_{e,L}\) onto an edge satisfies
\[
\Pr(R_{e,L})\leq2^{-pLk_e},
\qquad
\text{every row and column probability}\leq2^{-Lk_e}.
\tag{9}
\]
The shared-coordinate realization is established mathematics; the bounds above are its application to the present concentration certificate. arXiv
Combine the relations, then complete the marginals
Partition \([0,1]\) into \(n\) equal vertex-type blocks. On the block pair corresponding to an edge with \(k_e>0\), put
\[
A_L=\theta\,2^{Lk_e}\mathbf1_{R_{e,L}},
\]
and use its transpose on the reversed block pair. Set \(A_L=0\) elsewhere.
For sufficiently small fixed \(\theta>0\), (9) gives
\[
\sup_x\int A_L(x,y)\,dy\leq\theta,
\qquad
\|A_L\|_p=O_H(\theta),
\qquad
\int A_L\longrightarrow0.
\]
Now define
\[
r_L(x)=1-\int A_L(x,y)\,dy,
\qquad
\bar r_L=\int r_L,
\]
and perform the positive completion
\[
\boxed{
W_L(x,y)
=
A_L(x,y)
+
\frac{r_L(x)r_L(y)}{\bar r_L}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Every row integral is exactly one. All terms are nonnegative. The completion is bounded above and away from zero, and tends to one in \(L^1\).
Choosing \(\theta\) sufficiently small ensures \(\|W_L\|_p\leq B\) eventually for any prescribed \(B>1\).
The amplified contribution survives completion
Expand the product defining \(t(H,W_L)\).
The term using only the dense completion tends to one. Another nonnegative term uses \(A_L\) on every positive-scale edge and the dense completion on zero-scale edges.
Restricting that term to the correctly typed copy of \(T_L\) gives
\[
\text{contribution}
\geq
c_{H,\theta}\,
2^{L(\sum_e k_e-\gamma)}
=
c_{H,\theta}\,2^{L(1-\gamma)}.
\tag{11}
\]
Thus an extra positive contribution survives whenever \(\gamma\leq1\).
Zero-scale edges are supplied by the dense completion; they are not removed by an unjustified perturbation. The final object is one ordinary symmetric kernel, not a collection of colored edge oracles.
For \(\gamma<1\), replace \(\theta\) by
\[
\theta_L
=
\theta\,2^{-sL},
\qquad
s=\frac{1-\gamma}{2m_+},
\]
where \(m_+\) is the number of positive-scale edges. Then the \(L^p\) norm of \(A_L\) and all its row integrals tend to zero, but the exponent in (11) remains
\[
1-\gamma-sm_+
=
\frac{1-\gamma}{2}>0.
\]
This proves the strong-convergence/divergence statement (4).
