## Exact attainable-span proof

Write \(P(A)=A_{11}A_{12}^3\), \(G(A)=\lVert A\rVert_F^4\), and
\[
 F_\pm=G\pm\tfrac14P.
\]
These are globally convex homogeneous quartics on every \(\mathbb R^{m\times2}\).
Indeed, for any matrix direction \(H\), put
\(x=A_{11}, y=A_{12}, u=H_{11}, v=H_{12}\). Then
\[
 D^2P(A)[H,H]=6y^2uv+6xyv^2,
\]
so
\[
 |D^2P(A)[H,H]|
 \le 3y^2(u^2+v^2)+3(x^2+y^2)v^2
 \le 6\lVert A\rVert_F^2\lVert H\rVert_F^2.
\]
Meanwhile
\[
 D^2G(A)[H,H]=4\lVert A\rVert_F^2\lVert H\rVert_F^2
   +8\langle A,H\rangle^2.
\]
Therefore
\[
 D^2F_\pm(A)[H,H]\ge\tfrac52\lVert A\rVert_F^2\lVert H\rVert_F^2\ge0.
\]
Convexity at the origin follows by continuity of the Hessian. In particular,
these witnesses satisfy the stronger global-convexity condition, not just
rank-one convexity.

Take \(B_{11}=0, B_{12}=1\), all other entries zero, and profiles supported
in the first output coordinate: \(\psi_1=f e_1,\psi_2=g e_1\). For the
perturbation \(P\),
\[
 K_P(r,s)=3\mathbb E_y[(gf'-fg')(1+rf'+sg')^2].
\]
The following six finite Fourier pairs give the displayed curvature
polynomials. Each is the difference of the actual curvatures for \(F_+\) and
\(F_-\), multiplied by 2; hence they lie in the linear span of curvatures
actually attained by globally convex quartics.

| \(f(y)\) | \(g(y)\) | \(K_P(r,s)\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(\cos y\) | \(\sin 3y\) | \(\tfrac32r^2\) |
| \(\cos 3y\) | \(\sin y\) | \(-\tfrac32s^2\) |
| \(\cos y\) | \(\cos 2y\) | \(-\tfrac92r\) |
| \(\cos 2y\) | \(\cos y\) | \(\tfrac92s\) |
| \(\cos y\) | \(\sin y\) | \(-3-\tfrac32r^2-\tfrac32s^2\) |
| \(\cos y\) | \(\cos y+\sin y\) | \(-3-\tfrac32r^2-3rs-3s^2\) |

In the ordered coefficient basis \((1,r,s,r^2,rs,s^2)\), the exact
coefficient matrix has determinant \(-6561/16\). Its rank is six, so the
attainable curvature span is all of \(\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R^2)\).

Consequently, for any fixed closed amplitude loop, its circulation vanishes
for every admissible \(F\), every profile pair, and every background if and
only if all six \(M_{ij}\) vanish. For necessity, if every actual \(F_+\) and
\(F_-\) circulation is zero, their difference annihilates each row in the
table; invertibility then forces every moment to be zero. This proves that no
lower-rank linear compression of the six moments is a complete universal
zero-circulation certificate. It also shows why checking only \(B=0\) would
miss part of the answer: for a homogeneous quartic at \(B=0\), \(K\) is
homogeneous quadratic and only \(M_{20},M_{11},M_{02}\) are tested; nonzero
backgrounds produce the constant and linear curvature terms too.
