## 8. A genuine reduction, and its limits

If a polygonal profile loop visits the same profile twice, it splits into two closed compatible loops. At each fixed B the unnormalized energies add exactly. Thus a negative loop at B contains a negative subloop at the same B; repeating removes repeated vertices. Full affine optimization gives the corresponding superadditivity of the unnormalized minima. This is a profile-level compatible extraction, not cutting gradients by arbitrary spatial indicators.

For a finite profile graph, the exact negative-cycle test either supplies a negative closed compatible walk or a telescoping vertex potential. For arbitrary gradients, finite Fourier approximation in y and polygonal approximation in x is dense in W^(1,4), so this representation does not exclude a smooth negative test merely because it has many resonances. But no single finite graph covers all its approximants. Enlarging a graph is a genuine enlargement of the searched class, not a proof by finite sampling.

The finite-difference construction of Section 5 also does not preserve the original optimized interaction. If an arbitrary negative profile existed, applying that operation would replace it with a different compensated field, not rule it out. Its mathematically useful content is a way to make signed, spatially correlated interactions cancel to exactly the degree seen by the test class.

A universal positive continuation would need a profile-space function P_B such that

    L_(F,B)(U,V)>=DP_B(U)[V]

for every periodic profile U and every velocity V, where L is the full averaged strip Lagrangian. Integrating along a closed path would then prove quasiconvexity. Such a domination is not supplied here. Closed-measure and Hamilton-Jacobi theory explain why this is a natural type of question, but do not prove its existence for these nonconvex variational integrands. Naming this condition is not an additional theorem.

One must control the negative circulation by the actual nonnegative remainders and their dependence on B, rather than demand each cross term nonnegative or make current moments small without an unbounded-background estimate.
