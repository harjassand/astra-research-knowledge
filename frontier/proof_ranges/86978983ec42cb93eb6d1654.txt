4. A finite mixture that needs no latent samples
Choose
\[
L=
\lceil R\rceil+
\left\lceil\log_2\frac{8n}{\epsilon}\right\rceil,
\]
and divide \([-L,L]\) into
\[
N=\left\lceil\frac{16nL}{\epsilon}\right\rceil
\]
equal intervals.
Then
\[
b\le\epsilon/16,
\qquad
\rho\le\epsilon/8,
\]
so the ideal envelope already satisfies
\[
\|q_X-p\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\epsilon/4
\]
and has log-partition bias below \(\epsilon/2\). The remaining tolerance can pay for numerical estimation and base sampling.
There are at most
\[
m=n(N+1)
=O\!\left(\frac{n^2L}{\epsilon}\right)
\]
hyperplanes and
\[
M\le\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom mj
\]
full-dimensional cells.
For each cell, compute
\[
G_C=
\int_C
\exp\left\{
-\frac12y^\top K^{-1}y+d_C^\top y+e_C
\right\}dy
\]
and
\[
W_C=G_CZ_0(c_C).
\]
The compiled distribution is exactly
\[
\boxed{
q_X(x)=
\sum_C
\frac{W_C}{\sum_DW_D}
\,\mu_{0,c_C}(x).
}
\]
After preprocessing, drawing a sample consists of selecting a cell according to its weight and sampling one bounded-field base model.
There is no continuous latent-variable sampling at output time, no rejection, and no need to move a Markov chain between the original system’s phases.
This is the new computational representation: a finite weighted collection of easy spin laws, rather than a chain operating on the difficult global law.
