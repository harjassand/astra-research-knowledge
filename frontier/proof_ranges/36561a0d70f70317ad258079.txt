## 4. Explicit finite-prehistory obstruction

Suppose the controlled waveform begins at time zero and the feed was held at a constant probability \(p_0\) beforehand. Two four-atom age laws concentrated on entry times \((-4,-3,-2,-1)\tau\) and \((-40,-30,-20,-10)\tau\), respectively, give the same \(\operatorname{Binomial}(4,p_0)\) whole-product count law whenever all four atoms entered before the waveform began. Yet their adjacent-pair lengths are \(2\tau\) and \(20\tau\), so their \(Q_4(r)\) values are \(e^{-4r\tau}\) and \(e^{-40r\tau}\). At \(r\tau=2\), these are about \(3.35\cdot10^{-4}\) and \(1.80\cdot10^{-35}\).

This is an exact zero-information witness for an unlabelled old inventory. A finite control start cannot infer its detailed ancestry from future labels. It must instead measure/charge preparation, extend the labelled prehistory, or carry a quantitative residual-history error.
