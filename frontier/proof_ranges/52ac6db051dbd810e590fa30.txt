## 13. Scope, novelty, and the remaining foundational gate

Established mathematics used includes convex order, Gaussian mixtures, KL/Hellinger inequalities, joint spectral radius, spherical cap geometry, convex polytopes, and the Bourgain–Gamburd spectral-gap theorem. Positive realizability is already a substantial subject, including classical and quantum realization theory. Existing work also combines martingale transitions and quantization, and reverse KL triangle inequalities under shape/order conditions have precedents. These facts rule out claiming that the individual ingredients are new primitives.

The candidate contribution is the fully quantified bridge: a fixed angular spectral gap forces a sharp gap for *every* finite-state positive lift, including time-inhomogeneous encoders with unrestricted real transition probabilities, and the bridge transfers to exact interpolation range. Literature searches did not establish priority for this exact package; absence of a retrieved equivalent theorem is not a novelty certificate.

The independent Gaussian-probe exploration yields a complete reformulation of convex order but not a new efficient capability. Its positive result and counterexamples are preserved rather than inflated into a replacement foundation.

The single next mathematical gate is the following implication, restricted to symmetric lazy finitely supported laws generating a dense subgroup of SO(3):

\[
 \inf_{m\ge1}m(1-\rho_m(\nu))>0
 \quad\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}\quad
 \|Q_\nu\|_{L^2_0(S^2)}<1.
 \tag{23}
\]

The converse direction follows from Theorem A. The displayed direction is not proved or asserted as a previously unknown open problem. The missing step is converting an almost-invariant high-frequency L2 function into a finite positive lift with sufficiently small dissipation. Such functions need not be localized point clouds, so that conversion cannot simply be assumed.

A proof or a counterexample would test whether the positive-lift profile detects the full infinite-dimensional spectral obstruction or merely one consequence of it. Even an equivalence would need independently important consequences before being called foundational. No historic 9–10/10 result has been established in this packet.
