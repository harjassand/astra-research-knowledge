## 4. Restricted reconstruction: a product ray in one support plane

This section preserves a usable restriction of the Schur argument, **not a resolution of T**. The proof mechanism comes from Wu–Zou's product-reference calculation. The tensor calculation below applies to any finite number of physical sites. Priority of this formulation was not determined.

Let \(H=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\mathbb C^{d_i}\), \(d_i\ge2\), and define

\[
\lambda_i(X)=\operatorname{Tr}(X)I_{d_i}-\tfrac12X,
\qquad \Phi=\bigotimes_i\lambda_i.
\]

**Restricted statement.** The corresponding endpoint form is nonnegative on rank-at-most-two \(C\) whenever \(\operatorname{ran}C\) or \(\operatorname{ran}C^*\) contains a nonzero fully product vector.

### 4.1 Local co-Choi bounds

Normalize a product reference \(u=\bigotimes_i u_i\). By local unitary covariance it suffices to use \(u_i=e_0\). For a single local dimension \(d\), write

\[
A=\lambda(e_0e_0^*)=I-\tfrac12P,\quad P=e_0e_0^*,\quad A^{-1}=I+P,
\]
\[
\ell_a=\lambda(e_ae_0^*)=\delta_{a0}I-\tfrac12e_ae_0^*,
\qquad M(E_{ab})=\ell_aA^{-1}\ell_b^*.
\]

Use the input-first co-Choi convention

\[
\widehat J(\Psi)=\sum_{a,b}E_{ab}\otimes\Psi(E_{ba}).
\]

Put \(R=\widehat J(\lambda)=I-\tfrac12F_d\), \(S=\widehat J(M)\). The following invariant orthogonal pieces give every entry needed to check the inequality \(-R\preceq S\preceq R\).

On \(\mathbb C(e_0\otimes e_0)\), both operators equal \(1/2\). On each span of \(e_0\otimes e_a,e_a\otimes e_0\), \(a>0\),

\[
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here \(R-S=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)\) and \(R+S=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\), both positive semidefinite. On \(e_0^\perp\otimes e_0^\perp\),

\[
R=I-\tfrac12F_{d-1},\quad S=\tfrac12F_{d-1},\quad
R-S=I-F_{d-1}\succeq0,\quad R+S=I\succeq0.
\]

This proves the local bounds directly.

### 4.2 Tensorization for a fully product reference

For the full product reference, define \(A=\Phi(uu^*)\), \(\ell_a=\Phi(e_au^*)\), and the same \(M_u(E_{ab})=\ell_aA^{-1}\ell_b^*\). These objects factor across sites. After the canonical permutation grouping input and output slots, their co-Choi matrices are \(R=\bigotimes_i R_i\), \(S=\bigotimes_i S_i\).

Each \(R_i\) is positive definite. The Hermitian matrices \(T_i=R_i^{-1/2}S_iR_i^{-1/2}\) have spectra in \([-1,1]\). Consequently \(\bigotimes_iT_i\preceq I\), and congruence gives \(R-S\succeq0\). Thus \(\Phi-M_u\) is completely copositive and, in particular, positive.

For arbitrary \(z\in H\), write \(\ell_z=\Phi(zu^*)\). Positivity gives

\[
\Phi(zz^*)-\ell_zA^{-1}\ell_z^*\succeq0.
\]

Since \(A\succ0\), the Schur complement implies

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
\Phi(uu^*)&\Phi(uz^*)\\
\Phi(zu^*)&\Phi(zz^*)
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

These are precisely the two-dimensional input compressions of the Choi operator, with conjugation of the spanning vectors in the input-first convention. Complex conjugation preserves product vectors. The endpoint quadratic form is therefore nonnegative when its column space contains a product ray. The row-space version follows from \(q_n(C^*)=q_n(C)\). This completes the restricted proof.

A general two-dimensional subspace of a multipartite tensor product need not contain a product vector. No decomposition extending this argument to every allowed support plane was proved.
