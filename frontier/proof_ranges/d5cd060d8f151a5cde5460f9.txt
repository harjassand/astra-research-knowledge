# Root construction: known-operator dictionaries without a Pauli expansion

## 1. Fixed two-unitary dictionary for every specified contraction

Let A be a specified square finite-dimensional operator with ||A||<=1, supported on a known patch. Write A=UP, P=(A†A)^(1/2), extending the polar partial isometry to a unitary U on that same patch. Such an extension exists because the nullspaces of A and A† have equal dimension. Put

    V=P+i sqrt(I-P²).

The commuting positive functions give V†V=I and P=(V+V†)/2. Therefore

    A=(UV+UV†)/2.

The two coefficients have total absolute value1. For a norm-bounded known operator use ||A|| times this construction (zero operators are omitted). This is elementary polar/functional-calculus machinery; no historical novelty is asserted. Finite dimension is important for the unitary polar extension used here.

All dictionary unitaries are fixed before unknown scalar errors are selected. If A=XΠ with a known unitary X and projector Π, take U=X and V=Π+i(I−Π); no dense Pauli expansion or spectral search is needed to state that dictionary. Computing or implementing U,V for general input operators is a separate, potentially dimension-dependent cost. A promise ||unknown delta A||<=epsilon does not supply its polar direction or a fixed dictionary.

## 2. Signed correlated pair of arbitrary fixed unitaries

Let U,V be fixed unitaries on their union patch, z a complex scalar with |z|<=M, M>0, and W=V†U. Define

    E_z(rho)=z[U rho V†−{V†U,rho}/2]
             +conj(z)[V rho U†−{U†V,rho}/2].

Install the fixed physical floor

    F=M(D[U]+D[V])+(M/2)(D[W]+D[W†]),
    D[K](rho)=K rho K†−{K†K,rho}/2.

Writing z=t exp(i theta), use

    Kplus =sqrt((M+t)/2)(U+exp(−i theta)V),
    Kminus=sqrt((M−t)/2)(U−exp(−i theta)V),
    KW    =sqrt(M/2)W−conj(z)/sqrt(2M) I,
    KWdag =sqrt(M/2)W†−z/sqrt(2M) I,
    K0    =sqrt(M−|z|²/M) I.

Then exactly sum K†K=4M I, and

    F+E_z=4M(Phi_z−Id), Phi_z(rho)=sum K rho K†/(4M).

Proof: the first two Kraus operators give endpoint floors and the desired z-correlated completely positive terms. Their total jump intensity is2M I+zW+conj(z)W†. The two shifted product Kraus operators cancel that nonscalar intensity. Their CP cross terms give precisely −{zW+conj(z)W†,rho}/2. The unshifted product floors contribute M I and the scalar shifts contribute |z|²/M I; K0 supplies the remainder to4M. The combined identity shift is Hermitian, so there is no missing Hamiltonian gauge term. This proof does not require tracelessness, Hilbert–Schmidt independence or commutation of U,V.

The proposal clock is fixed independently of z. The channel mark may depend on the actual specified scalar coefficient; this is an exact representation, not a claim that an experimental controller knows that coefficient. Every Kraus operator remains supported on the union patch. When W is Hermitian, duplicate product operators can be combined and the construction reduces to the supplied four-Kraus form.

A signed real diagonal coefficient c, |c|<=M, instead uses floor M D[U] and rate2M with channel ((M−c)Id+(M+c)Ad_U)/(2M).

## 3. Uniform signed-jump replacement bound

    E=D[L+delta L]−D[L]

    |C_ab|<=M_ab=|v_a|e_b+e_a|v_b|+e_a e_b,
