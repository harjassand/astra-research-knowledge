# CRAY: Frozen-reservoir lifting and constructive nonlocal transport certificates

Research record, 13 October 2026. This is a derivation and reproducible computational certificate, not a peer-reviewed result or an independently established publication-priority claim.

## 1. Result and boundary of the result

This investigation constructs a legal, globally admissible, genuinely nonlocal current for the **original hard two-dimensional Kob–Andersen model with two-vacancy facilitation**. It provides positive, completely numerical lower bounds on its infinite-volume **bulk variational diffusion coefficient**. All model-dependent finite identities are checked exactly. The nonlocal completion is explicit and has an analytically proved, computable energy tail.

The construction has three parts:

1. A finite legal configuration-space current whose divergence is a prescribed difference of two port occupations, rather than zero.
2. A nonlocal spatial current obtained by making an affine stream potential constant on connected components of closed dual edges.
3. A frozen-reservoir lifting operation that makes the first current inherit the conservation of the second, despite changes to local gate status during legal microscopic transitions.

For the finite module constructed here, the resulting bound is positive for every particle density **0 < p <= 2/5**. The displayed lower endpoints are rounded down:

These are broad certificates, not accurate predictions. The upper column uses the zero corrector, not the strongest specialized upper-bound calculation. This investigation does **not** establish a useful result in the dense glassy regime, a speedup over the strongest simulation or variational implementation, or the first positive lower bound for KA. Existing work already proves positivity and uses cooperative droplets, coarse percolation and legal multistep comparisons [1,2].

The preserved methodological result is the explicit finite-to-nonlocal certificate transformation, its error theorem, the verified hard-KA input module, and the executed output. Whether this particular transformation is literature-new remains to be independently assessed.

## 2. Mechanisms investigated

### Direct projection of a conservation defect

One can formally project a legal current onto the orthogonal complement of microscopic correctors. That description is not an algorithm with a certified error: an arbitrarily small residual may live in an arbitrarily small spectral mode. In the elementary model A=diag(delta,1), the vector e_1 has residual delta e_1 but unit correction cost. A residual-norm argument therefore cannot silently substitute for a proved inverse or inf-sup estimate. No such estimate is assumed here.

### A hierarchy of weakly conserved currents

Weak compactness could yield a conserved limiting current if every fixed local defect tends to zero and energies remain bounded. This observation alone neither constructs the needed bounded-energy sequence nor provides a finite error certificate. It was not used as a substitute for nonlocal construction.

### Prescribed unbounded paths

Unbounded comparison paths and their information-loss/congestion analysis are already substantive prior art [1–3]. Forward path length alone does not bound the number of initial configurations whose routes traverse a given transition. The implementation below instead constructs the complete finite signed source–sink flow and evaluates its exact weighted energy. Its nonlocal part is a separate spatial operation with a proved tail.
