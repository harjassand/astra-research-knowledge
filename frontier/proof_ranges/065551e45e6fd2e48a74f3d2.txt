## 2. Main results

**Theorem 1 (active local-port tolerant lower bound).** Let d>=1 and 0<eta<=1/8. Put

    delta=eta^2/(64d^2),
    T=4/delta=256d^2/eta^2.

There are two connected nearest-neighbour Hamiltonians H_g,H_b on 2(d+1) qubits with these properties:

1. All interaction terms are bounded by an absolute constant. There are nonzero hopping terms on every edge and positive nearest-neighbour density-density interactions. The vacuum is a common unique product ground state, with spectral gap greater than 0.9.
2. H_g and H_b are related by a reflection of the chain. Their entire many-body spectra, including multiplicities, coincide.
3. All coefficients agree except for interchanging the onsite terms at the two outermost sites. The entire alternatives may be supplied in advance; their assignment is the sole unknown bit.
4. With the simple K and product psi in Section 1,

       E_T(H_g)<eta,       E_T(H_b)>3/4.                         (2)

5. Label the two arms from their adjacent central sites as L_0,...,L_d and R_0,...,R_d. Allow control of both prefixes through r, where 0<=r<d, and set R=d-r. For 0<tau<=R/2, every tolerant certifier in the stated access model uses at least

       N >= (2R+1)(R!)^2 / (144 tau^(2R+1))                     (3)

   fresh experiments in the worst case.

In particular, for fixed eta, fixed positive tau and r<=d/2, this is

       N >= exp(Omega(d log d))
         = exp(Omega(sqrt(T) log T)).                           (4)

Equation (3) is a lower bound, not a matching minimax formula. Its exact expression is preferable when tau grows with R.

For experiment durations chosen before each run, the average, under the two hypotheses, of the expected total native evolution time obeys

    Sbar >= (2R+1)(R!)^2 / (144 tau^(2R)).                       (5)

There is also a matched dyadic version with O(log(d/eta)) bits per bounded coefficient, retaining (2)-(5), exact reflection isospectrality and a gap greater than 0.9. This version has inverse-polynomial, shared-coefficient precision tolerance, not a full fixed-noise perturbation neighbourhood.

**Theorem 2 (polynomial exact-agreement certificate with one-qubit operations).** In the ambient class of Section 1.2, suppose h=min(tau,1/2)<=T. Prepare psi, evolve for time h, undo the known candidate phase on a, and measure X_a. Let p be the probability of the minus outcome. Then

    E_T(H) <= min(1, (2T/h) sqrt(p)).                            (6)

If E_T(H)=0, p=0. Thus, observing no minus result in

    ceil[4T^2/(h^2 epsilon^2) log(1/alpha)]                       (7)

independent shots is a sound certificate for E_T<=epsilon with error probability alpha. It has exact completeness and uses only a one-qubit preparation, a one-qubit phase, and one-qubit readout. It need not accept every approximately correct trajectory.

Theorems 1 and 2 concern the same experimental interface and a common ambient model class. In particular, at fixed eta and tau, allowing true model tolerance can change a polynomial exact-null task into one requiring superpolynomially many experiments. This statement does not claim that every exact Hamiltonian-verification task is polynomial.
