4. Why a positive certificate works
The proof must control unequal edge scales and mixed products. Allow each edge \(e\) its own kernel \(W_e\), with row and column integrals at most one and \(\|W_e\|_p\leq B\).
For each edge, partition its values into
\[
A_{e,0}=\{W_e\leq2\},
\qquad
A_{e,k}=\{2^k<W_e\leq2^{k+1}\},\quad k\geq1.
\]
Each slice has row and column measures at most \(2^{-k}\), and total measure at most
\[
B^p2^{-pk}.
\]
Fix an edge-level vector \(\mathbf k\). Let \(J_{\mathbf k}\) be the set of vertex tuples satisfying every selected slice constraint, and write
\[
q=\Pr(J_{\mathbf k}).
\]
When \(q>0\), condition product Lebesgue measure on \(J_{\mathbf k}\), obtaining a law \(Q\). Define the base-two relative-entropy deficits
\[
D(S)
=
D_{\mathrm{KL},2}\!\left(Q_S\middle\|\mathrm{Leb}^{S}\right).
\]
These deficits are increasing and supermodular. In particular,
\[
D(S\cup T)+D(S\cap T)-D(S)-D(T)
\]
is conditional mutual information.
A distribution supported on a set of reference probability \(a\) has relative entropy at least \(\log_2(1/a)\). Applying this to pair supports and conditional sections gives
\[
D(uv)\geq pk_{uv}-p\log_2B,
\]
\[
D(uv)-D(u)\geq k_{uv},
\qquad
D(uv)-D(v)\geq k_{uv}.
\]
The modular correction
\[
d(S)=D(S)+\frac p2\log_2(B)\,|S|
\]
removes the additive loss without disturbing supermodularity. Homogeneity of (1), together with \(D(V)=\log_2(1/q)\), yields
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(J_{\mathbf k})
\leq
B^{pn/2}
2^{-\gamma_H(p)\sum_e k_e}.
}
\tag{5}
\]
On the same event,
\[
\prod_eW_e\leq2^m2^{\sum_e k_e}.
\]
Consequently, when \(\varepsilon=\gamma_H(p)-1>0\),
\[
\int\prod_eW_e
\leq
2^mB^{pn/2}
\sum_{\mathbf k\geq0}
2^{-\varepsilon\sum_e k_e}
<\infty.
\]
The same geometric sum gives a uniform tail estimate
\[
\int\prod_eW_e\,
\mathbf1_{\{\max_eW_e>R\}}
\leq C R^{-\varepsilon}.
\tag{6}
\]
The truncation step that cannot be skipped
Pointwise truncation is not automatically Lipschitz in cut norm. The proof therefore does not truncate both kernels and assume their truncations remain close.
Instead, telescope \(t(H,W)-t(H,U)\) over the edges. In each term, leave the distinguished factor \(W-U\) untruncated and restrict every other factor to values at most \(R\).
After fixing the other vertex variables, the remaining factors separate into a function of each endpoint of the distinguished edge. This uses simplicity of \(H\). The bounded portion is controlled by the cut norm; the complementary portion is controlled by (6):
\[
|t(H,W)-t(H,U)|
\leq
4mR^{m-1}\|W-U\|_\square
+
C R^{-\varepsilon}.
\]
Balancing the two terms proves (2).
