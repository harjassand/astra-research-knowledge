INTRINSIC SINGLE-SHOT DECODING OF THE SOURCE-REPORTED GGJ qLTC FAMILY
Sol researcher6, gpt-6.1-sol / max. Frozen v1, 2026-10-09.
Status: internally derived decoder theorem on the exact source hypotheses;
application to the good family remains dependent on the GGJ construction and
parameter theorems. No independent validation of the good-qLTC claim, priority
claim, practical-resource claim, or constant-depth decoder claim is made.

0. Exact outcome and supplied information

We construct a deterministic decoder for each of the two binary CSS sectors of
the family source-reported by Gay--Granha Jeronimo (GGJ), arXiv:2609.20780v1.
On the finite-field interfaces the inputs are only

    y = delta^j e + m, or y = B_k e + m,
    B_k = (delta^{k-1})^vee on actual algebraic dual stalks.

Neither e nor m is supplied. The code's face incidence tables, actual stalk
bases, and actual incidence matrices are supplied/preprocessed. All local
lengths, stalk dimensions, and field size are fixed as the family index grows.
The guarantee is stabilizer-reduced block residual at most beta |m|, for fixed
positive linear data and syndrome tolerances. Runtime and space are O(N) in
the usual word-RAM model with O(log N)-bit addresses; field operations cost
O(1) because the field is fixed. This counts O(N) preprocessing as well as
decoding. Bit cost of address manipulation may instead be O(N log N).

The delta decoder is proved directly from GGJ's anisotropic local filling and
mixing estimates. The transpose decoder uses actual anchored arrays, local
linear splittings, and cube contractions. It has no global tensor basis and
requires no equivariance of a chosen local inverse under chart transitions.
The unsupported chart-equality gates in T14 v1 are replaced, not assumed.

1. Hypotheses and notation

Let X be a finite t-dimensional (n_1,...,n_t)-regular cubical complex satisfying
the source's strict cubical geometry and face-walk mixing inequalities.
Set n_- = min_i n_i, n_+ = max_i n_i, K >= n_+/n_-, Z_d = |X(d)|.
Let G be a cosheaf over a fixed field F of characteristic two. Every actual
stalk has a fixed basis and bounded dimension. Block weight counts nonzero
actual stalk vectors and is invariant under arbitrary changes within a block.

For every face v of dimension ell and every ell <= d < t, require

 |delta_v x|_b >= r_{d,ell} min_a |x+delta_v a|_b,
 r_{d,ell} = n_- kappa_{t-ell,d-ell} > 0.                  (1.1)

The compatible charts plus universally product-expanding constituent codes
give (1.1) by GGJ Thm4.0.1. No cross-anchor agreement occurs in (1.1).
For the transported walks W_{d,ell}, require, for all A subset X(d),

 <1_A,W_{d,ell}1_A> <= lambda |A| + beta_{d,ell}|A|^2/Z_d. (1.2)

GGJ Lem5.3.1 gives beta_{d,ell} <= binom(t,ell)2^{t-ell-1}K^d.
Define the exact source upper-bound coefficients

 b_{d,ell} = K^{d-ell+1} binom(d,ell) 2^{d-ell}
             (t-ell) binom(t-ell,d-ell) (d-ell)!
             / prod_{h=ell}^d kappa_{t-h,d-h},
 L_d = sum_{ell=0}^d b_{d,ell},
 Q_d = sum_{ell=0}^d b_{d,ell} beta_{d,ell},
 a_d = (1-lambda L_d)/Q_d.

Require lambda L_d < 1 in degrees used below. For GGJ t=4,k=2,
both G=F and its intrinsic kernel cosheaf K satisfy the hypotheses in degrees
2 and 3, and source Thm13.2.1 chooses lambda L < 1/2 for all those degrees.
The source's Q upper bounds can replace the degreewise Q_d throughout.

Define geometric bounds

 D_d = (t-d)n_+,                    0 <= d < t,
 ell_d = binom(t,d)n_+^d,
 c_{p,ell} = binom(p,ell)2^{p-ell},
 h^max_{d,ell} = binom(t-ell,d-ell)n_+^{d-ell},
 M_d = sum_{ell=0}^d
       (d-ell)! c_{d+1,ell} h^max_{d,ell}
       / [n_-^{d-ell+1} prod_{h=ell}^d kappa_{t-h,d-h}].   (1.3)

All are finite constants at fixed local parameters. D_d bounds coboundary
support inflation; ell_d bounds the support of one upward-vertex-star flip.

2. A noncocycle extension of the local-cominimal support recursion

Theorem 2.1. If x in C^d(X;G), 0 <= d < t, is locally cominimal, then

 (1-lambda L_d)|x|_b <= Q_d |x|_b^2/Z_d + M_d |delta^d x|_b. (2.1)

Consequently if |x|_b <= a_d Z_d/2, then

 |x|_b <= A_d |delta^d x|_b,
 A_d = 2 M_d/(1-lambda L_d).                             (2.2)

Here locally cominimal means no correction delta^{d-1}a, with a supported
above a single vertex, strictly decreases block weight. At d=0 the correction
space is zero and the definition is automatic.

Proof. Let A=supp x and s=delta^d x. Fix an ell-face v. Local cominimality
allows every local correction supported above v (choose any vertex of v), so
(1.1) bounds the local coboundary below by r_{d,ell}|A cap X_{>=v}(d)|.
For a (d+1)-face u containing v, the local coboundary equals s(u) plus the
external-facet contributions x(g), where g is a d-facet of u not containing v.
Each nonzero local equation either has s(u) != 0 or a nonzero external facet.
The source's cubical uniqueness lemma (GGJ Lem6.1.2) makes the assignment
from an external facet to its witness u injective at fixed v. The facet lies
in O_v(d) or N_v(d). Hence the source cocycle inequality gains just one term:

 r_{d,ell}|A cap X_{>=v}(d)|
 <= |A cap O_v(d)| + |A cap N_v(d)| + |s|_{X_{>=v}(d+1)}. (2.3)

For fixed f in X(d), sum (2.3) over ell-faces v <= f. Denote its four summed
quantities H_ell(f), O_ell(f), N_ell(f), S_ell(f). GGJ Lem6.2.1 gives

 N_ell(f) <= (d-ell+1) H_{ell-1}(f), N_0(f)=0,
 H_d(f)=1_A(f).

Unwinding from ell=0 to d gives exactly

 1_A(f) <= sum_{ell=0}^d
           [(d-ell)! / prod_{h=ell}^d r_{d,h}]
           [O_ell(f)+S_ell(f)].                         (2.4)

Multiply by 1_A(f) and sum f. The O terms are bounded by
sum b_{d,ell}<1_A,W_{d,ell}1_A>, by the source path-count estimate.
For each fixed s-supported (d+1)-face u, the number of pairs (v,f) with
dim v=ell, v<=u, v<=f, dim f=d is at most
c_{d+1,ell}h^max_{d,ell}. Dropping 1_A(f) can only increase the sum.
Thus the S terms sum to at most M_d|s|. Applying (1.2) proves (2.1).
Under the displayed small-support assumption the quadratic term is at most
(1-lambda L_d)|x|/2, proving (2.2). This argument uses the same actual-star
inequalities for F or K and has no isotropic-degree substitution.

For s=0, (2.1) also reconstructs the source distance conclusion:
every nonzero locally cominimal d-cocycle has weight >= a_d Z_d, and hence
the cohomological distance mu^d(G) >= a_d Z_d. A minimum representative of
any nonzero cohomology class is locally cominimal. GGJ Lem6.4.1 then gives

 epsilon_d >= min{ell_d^{-1}, a_{d+1} Z_{d+1}/Z_d}.       (2.5)

For completeness, when a coboundary s has weight < a_{d+1}Z_{d+1}, it is a
cocycle that cannot be nonzero and locally cominimal. Iteratively reducing s
by an upward-star d-cochain reaches zero in <=|s| steps, with a primitive of
weight <=ell_d|s|. For larger |s|, distance to ker delta <=Z_d yields the
other branch of (2.5). Thus (2.5) follows operationally from the same finite
descent, rather than requiring an efficient nearest-cocycle oracle.

3. Complete one-sided noisy decoder, with exact positive promises

Take 1 <= j <= t-2, with lambda L_j,lambda L_{j+1}<1. Set

 theta_j = a_{j+1}Z_{j+1}/2,
 C_syn,j = 1 + A_{j+1}D_{j+1},
 eps_j = min{ell_j^{-1}, a_{j+1}Z_{j+1}/Z_j},
 beta_j = C_syn,j / eps_j,
 R_j = 1+ell_j D_j.                                    (3.1)

Algorithm D_G,j(y): initialize b=y and correction f=0. Enumerate all
cochains w in C^j supported on an upward star of one vertex. While some
such w satisfies |b+delta^j w|_b < |b|_b, apply it, updating
b <- b+delta^j w and f <- f+w. Stop and return f.
Use deterministic ordering of finite local candidates and a queue of
flippable vertices. This algorithm is defined for every supplied syndrome.

Theorem 3.1. Write E=|e|_stab-red,b and M=|m|_b. If

 D_j E+M <= theta_j,
 R_j E+(ell_j+beta_j)M < a_j Z_j,                       (3.2)

then D_G,j(delta^j e+m) returns f satisfying

 |e+f|_stab-red,b <= beta_j M.                          (3.3)

Proof. Choose e of minimum weight in its stabilizer coset; this changes
neither its syndrome nor the algorithm. The initial b has weight <=D_jE+M.
Every flip strictly decreases an integer weight. The terminal b is locally
cominimal and still delta^{j+1}b=delta^{j+1}m. Its weight is <=theta_j,
so Theorem2.1 implies |b| <=A_{j+1}D_{j+1}M.
For r=e+f one has delta^j r=b+m, so |delta^j r|<=C_syn,j M.
At most |y| flips occurred, each w has support <=ell_j, giving

 |r| <= E+ell_j(D_jE+M)=R_jE+ell_jM.

By (2.5) there exists a cochain u with delta^j u=delta^j r and
|u|<=eps_j^{-1}|delta^j r|<=beta_j M. Thus r+u is a cocycle.
Its support is strictly below a_jZ_j<=mu^j by (3.2), so it is a stabilizer
coboundary. This proves (3.3). This explicitly distinguishes soundness's
distance to the whole kernel from the required stabilizer-reduced metric.

Costs. At fixed local parameters every candidate enumeration is O(1).
Initialize the flippability queue in O(|X(0)|)=O(N). A flip changes O(1)
syndrome blocks; only vertices of those changed blocks can have changed
flippability, so update O(1) queue entries. There are <=|y|<=Z_{j+1}=O(N)
strict decreases even for an input outside the promise. Thus worst-case
runtime O(N), memory O(N). No unbounded search or unknown data is used.

The same theorem applies independently to G=F and G=K in degree j=2 for
the GGJ t=4 family. Unlike the initial T14 argument, it does not require
transferring NP's constants or their component-density conventions.

4. Actual-stalk bicomplex and canonical operational maps

For 0<=r<=p<=t let

 A_{r,p}(f)=direct_sum_{u in X(p), f<=u} F(u)^*, dim f=r,
 A_{r,p}=direct_sum_f A_{r,p}(f),
 (Delta a)(g)[u]=sum_{f<g,dim f=r} a(f)[u],
 (T a)(f)[v]=sum_{u>v,dim u=p} R_{uv}^vee a(f)[u].       (4.1)

Use actual algebraic dual vectors at (f,u). GGJ Lem7.1.1 gives

 Delta^2=T^2=0, Delta T=T Delta,
 |Delta a|_anchor<=D|a|_anchor, |T a|_anchor<=|a|_anchor,
 D=t n_+.                                             (4.2)

Its proof is two intermediate faces per cubical diamond and the cosheaf
functor law; neither identity compares chart inverses across anchors.
Let K(f)=ker T_f^t. Choose basis isomorphisms iota_f:F^{dim K(f)}->K(f).
The K cosheaf incidence is restriction of topface entries; its coboundary
is Delta on the actual embedded K arrays (GGJ Lem7.1.2).
GGJ Thm7.2.1 gives local vertical exactness

 ker T_f^p=im T_f^{p+1}, dim f<=p<t.                   (4.3)

This is exactness of the transposed tensor complex inside ONE compatible
star, using the dual chart (Psi^{-1})^T, and hence holds in actual bases.
The same theorem supplies compatible charts for K from the actual dual
constituent codes. The good-family source supplies their product expansion.

Precompute, for every f,p in (4.3), a linear splitting

 S_f^p:ker T_f^p -> A_{r,p+1}(f), T_f^{p+1}S_f^p=id.

Choose any linear projection P_f^p from A_{r,p}(f) to ker T_f^p and set
L_f^p=S_f^p P_f^p. These are linear maps on every input, agree with S on
the kernel, send 0 to 0, and never increase anchor support. No minimum
weight or transition-equivariant choice is demanded. Also choose a linear
projection Pi_f:A_{r,t}(f)->K(f) and use iota_f^{-1}Pi_f as unencoding.
All are computed by finite local Gaussian elimination in O(N) total time.

For each upper p-face u choose a vertex/order of cube directions and the
ordinary cube cochain contraction H_{u}, extended to all cochains. As in
GGJ Lem7.3.1,

 Delta H+H Delta=id-P, P=0 in positive anchor degree.

On arrays regrouped by upper face this gives H_p:A_{r,p}->A_{r-1,p},
with Delta H_p a=a for positive-degree Delta-closed a, and

 |H_p a|_anchor <= H |a|_anchor, H=2^{2t}n_+^t.        (4.4)

At anchor degree0 let J_p read, at each upper p-face u, the entry at one
designated vertex of u. For Delta-closed a these entries agree; J_p is a
linear map on all arrays and

 |J_p a|_b <= G |a|_anchor, G=2^t n_+^t.              (4.5)

There is no need to discard invalid noisy arrays; linear extensions and
H_p,J_p make the entire pre/postprocessing defined on arbitrary inputs.

5. Supplied B_k syndrome to noisy delta_K syndrome

Take 1<=k<t and L=t-k. Copy the supplied y in C_{k-1}^* to vertex opinions
C(y)(v)[u]=y(u) for v<=u. This is horizontally closed. Define, operationally,

 g_0 = L^{k-1} C(y) in A_{0,k},
 g_r = L^{k+r-1} Delta g_{r-1} in A_{r,k+r}, 1<=r<=L,
 s_K = iota^{-1}Pi Delta g_L in C^{L+1}(X;K).          (5.1)

This depends only on observed y and code data. Call D_K,L(s_K), obtaining
correction c_hat in C^L(K), and reverse by

 y_L = g_L+iota c_hat,
 y_r = g_r+T H_{k+r+1}y_{r+1}, r=L-1,...,0,
 output f=J_k y_0 in C_k^*.                           (5.2)

For GGJ k=2,t=4 the K call is exactly degree L=2, so Theorem3.1 applies.

Let hats denote ideal sequences (5.1) on B_k e. Copy e to x_0 in A_{0,k};
Delta x_0=0, T x_0=C(B_k e). Inductively, only in the proof, define

 z_r=S^{k+r}(g_hat_r+x_r), x_{r+1}=Delta z_r,
                                         0<=r<L.      (5.3)

All the arguments of S lie in its kernel: Tg_hat_0=Tx_0, and induction
uses Delta T=T Delta and Delta^2=0. Also Delta x_r=0.
Thus

 c=iota^{-1}(g_hat_L+x_L) in C^L(K),
 delta_K c=iota^{-1}Delta g_hat_L.                     (5.4)

Let E=|e|_stab-red,b, M=|m|_b, and define c_e=2^k,c_m=2^{k-1}.
Choose a stabilizer-reduced e in the proof. The ideal first g_hat_0 has
support inside vertices touching e, because the syndrome vanishes at every
vertex whose local e is zero. Hence |g_hat_0|,|x_0|<=c_e E. Recursively,

 |g_hat_r|<=c_e D^r E,
 |x_r|<=(r+1)c_e D^r E,
 |c|<=P_data E, P_data=(L+2)c_e D^L.                  (5.5)

Every local L is linear and anchorwise. Therefore

 |g_r+g_hat_r|<=c_m D^r M,
 |s_K+delta_K c|<=P_noise M, P_noise=c_m D^{L+1}.      (5.6)

Faults need not obey Tm=0 or any compatibility condition. Projection Pi
fixes the ideal top syndrome and adds no new anchors; (5.6) is valid for
arbitrary adversarial measurement faults. This discharges supplied-input
and noisy-lift obligations without referring to the unknown e operationally.

6. Exact homology preservation: the missing reverse-step proof

Suppose the K decoder has

 c+c_hat=delta_K q+w, |w|<=beta_K P_noise M,
 q in C^{L-1}(K).                                    (6.1)

Such q,w exist by the reduced residual guarantee; neither is supplied to
the decoder. Let a_L=x_L+Delta iota q, and define ideal reverse values

 a_r=g_hat_r+T H_{k+r+1}a_{r+1}.

These are Delta-closed: the top is closed, and inductively
Delta a_r=Delta g_hat_r+T a_{r+1}=0. Also T a_r=Tg_hat_r.
We now prove that f_ideal=J_k a_0 differs from e by a genuine original
B_{k+1} boundary, without any tensor-image preservation assertion.

At r=L-1 put

 b_{L-1}=z_{L-1}+H_t a_L+iota q.

Then Delta b_{L-1}=x_L+a_L+Delta iota q=0, and, since T iota q=0,

 T b_{L-1}=a_{L-1}+x_{L-1}.                           (6.2)

If r>=1 and b_r is closed with T b_r=a_r+x_r, horizontal cube exactness
provides v_{r-1} in A_{r-1,k+r+1} with Delta v_{r-1}=b_r. Put

 b_{r-1}=z_{r-1}+H_{k+r}a_r+T v_{r-1}.

Its Delta is x_r+a_r+T b_r=0, while
T b_{r-1}=g_hat_{r-1}+x_{r-1}+T H_{k+r}a_r
         =a_{r-1}+x_{r-1}, using T^2=0.                (6.3)

At r=0 the closed b_0 in A_{0,k+1} has a constant actual dual value on
every (k+1)-face, so it is the copied view of h=J_{k+1}b_0.
The equality T b_0=a_0+x_0 then says B_{k+1}h=f_ideal+e.
This proves exact homological preservation. For L=1 the initial b_0
already proves it; the downward induction is empty. Global holonomy and
chosen inverse covariance are absent. The two diagonals commute in actual
stalks, and every horizontal filling is confined to ONE upper cube.

7. Noisy reverse support and the complete transpose theorem

From (6.1), the operational y_L and ideal a_L differ by
g_L+g_hat_L+iota w. Hence

 E_L:=|y_L+a_L|<=c_m D^L(1+beta_K D)M.

For r<L the difference satisfies exactly

 y_r+a_r=(g_r+g_hat_r)+T H_{k+r+1}(y_{r+1}+a_{r+1}),
 E_r<=c_m D^r M+H E_{r+1}.

Combining (4.5) and this recurrence yields

 |f+e|_stab-red,b <= C_trans M,
 C_trans=G c_m [sum_{r=0}^{L-1}(HD)^r
                  +(HD)^L(1+beta_K D)].               (7.1)

Theorem 7.1. Use Theorem3.1 for K in degree L (requiring 1<=L<=t-2).
The supplied-syndrome algorithm (5.1)-(5.2) satisfies (7.1) whenever

 D_L P_data E+P_noise M <= a_{L+1}Z_{L+1}/2,
 R_L P_data E+(ell_L+beta_K)P_noise M < a_L Z_L.        (7.2)

Every preprocessing/reverse operation processes O(N) entries in local
spaces of fixed size; the K decoder costs O(N) by Theorem3.1. Thus the
total worst-case time and memory are O(N). This discharges T1--T4 at once:
overlap agreement in the ideal is Delta=0; arbitrary noisy disagreements
have the explicitly bounded support (7.1); the top lift is into actual K;
stabilizer equivalence is the exact diagram chase (6.2)-(6.3).

8. Consequence for t=4,k=2 and binary CSS conversion

For each source-reported GGJ family member, set t=4,k=2,L=2. Its fixed
parameters give positive a_2,a_3 for F and K and comparable Z_2,Z_3,N.
Theorem3.1 and Theorem7.1 therefore give positive family-independent
constants eta_data,eta_syndrome,gamma such that both Pauli sectors satisfy

 |residual|_stab-red,b <= gamma |faulty syndrome|_b

whenever data reduced block weight <=eta_data N and faulty syndrome
block weight <=eta_syndrome N. Explicit sufficient constants are obtained
by taking half of each upper bound in (3.2) and (7.2), separately for E
and M; this avoids any strict-boundary equality issue.

GGJ Prop8.1.1 uses beta on cochains and the trace-dual binary basis on
algebraic dual vectors. The binary matrices are H_X=R_beta(delta^2) and
H_Z=R_beta(delta^1)^T. The delta^2 interface corrects physical Z errors,
and the B_2 interface corrects physical X errors, under those conventions.
Naively using the same beta coordinates for the F-linear transpose is
incorrect in general. Implement the fixed trace-dual conversions within
each block. Their costs and support inflation are fixed constants.

If B=max_f s dim_F F(f), every nonzero physical block contains >=1 bit and
every block correction has <=B bits. Thus reduced block output bound
gamma M gives binary stabilizer-reduced bound <=B gamma M, and faulty
binary syndrome-bit support bounds faulty syndrome-block support above.
The two sector decoders can be run separately and combined as a Pauli;
a constant-factor union bound on supports handles simultaneous faults.

The source-reported constant rate, relative distance, soundness, and
check/qubit degree are retained dependencies. This is an efficient
asymptotic recovery capability with finite but enormous constants; it
does not establish feasibility at realistic block lengths or hardware.
Sequential O(N) recovery alone does not satisfy the constant-depth
contraction assumption used in the separate common-bath memory theorem.
