AUDITED CLAIM AND INTERFACE

Input rational a,c>=0, binary integer n>=0, and p>=1. For nonzero
H_n=[x^n] exp(a*x^2/2+c*x), return a logarithmic interval of width at most
2^(-p-1), or a floating enclosure with binary-encoded exponent. Exact zeros
are reported separately. This interface is essential: an expanded exact
rational H_n can have exponentially many output bits in bitlength(n).

I found no mathematical counterexample to deterministic bit-polynomial
evaluation under this interface. Below are independent reconstructions of
the uniform bounds, an explicit alternative interpolation algorithm that
removes a Taylor-conditioning gap, and precise software evidence.

1. IDENTITY, PARITY, AND EXTREME PARAMETERS

For a,c>0 put d=c/sqrt(a), sigma=(-1)^n, b(t)=1+sigma*exp(-2dt),
m=(d+sqrt(d*d+4n))/2, and phi(t)=n*log(t)-(t-d)^2/2.
The Gaussian moment identity gives
 H_n=a^(n/2)/(n!*sqrt(2*pi))*integral_0^infinity exp(phi(t))*b(t) dt.
No additional exp(-d*d/2) factor is missing. Splitting the signed Gaussian
moment into positive and negative half-lines yields exactly this formula.

Set F(m+s)=exp(phi(m+s)-phi(m))*b(m+s)/b(m). Then
 log H_n=(n/2)log(a)-logGamma(n+1)-.5log(2*pi)
          +n log(m)-.5(n/m)^2+log b(m)+log I,
 I=integral_0^infinity F(t)dt.
The identity m-d=n/m is crucial; computing m-d by subtraction would be an
unnecessary numerical vulnerability when d is large.

For odd n, b is increasing, concave, vanishes at zero, and is positive for
t>0. Hence min(1,t/m)<=b(t)/b(m)<=max(1,t/m). Moreover for z=t+iy,t>0,
 b(z)=2dz integral_0^1 exp(-2dzv)dv,
 |b(z)|<=|z|*b(t)/t.
This cancels the common small displacement analytically. There is no
inverse-tiny-displacement blowup in the normalized complex integrand.
For even n, 1/2<=b(t)/b(m)<=2 and |b(z)/b(m)|<=2 when Re(z)>0.

If the rational inputs have total binary length L, nonzero d is at least
2^(-O(L)); b(m)>=min(d*m,1/2)>=2^(-O(L)), because m>=1. Thus even a direct
division by a rigorously enclosed odd b(m) costs only O(L) extra bits.
Use expm1 or cancel d when evaluating that factor.

2. REAL LOWER BOUND AND TAILS

phi'(m)=0 and phi''(t)=-1-n/t^2<=-1, so F's exponential factor is at most
exp(-s*s/2). For t in [m,m+1], n/t^2<=1, giving the lower bound exp(-s*s).
The parity factor is at least 1/2 there. Consequently
 I >= .5 integral_0^1 exp(-s*s)ds >= .5 integral_0^1(1-s*s)ds = 1/3.
This is stronger than the 1/6 bound in the submitted note and justifies the
implementation's integral.lower()>1/4 assertion once finite errors are small.

For R>=1, the omitted tails are <=4 exp(-R*R/2): in the even case use two
Gaussian tails times 2. In the odd case the left factor is <=1 and the right
factor <=1+s/m, so the tail is <=(2/R+1/m)exp(-R*R/2)<=3 exp(-R*R/2).
These bounds remain uniform for both tiny and huge displacement.

3. COMPLEX BOUNDS AND SUBMITTED QUADRATURE CONSTANTS

For the submitted algorithm m>=4(R+1). On a radius-one disk around any
real panel center in [m-R,m+R], write z=t+iy. Then t>=3m/4, |y|<=1 and
 n/t^2<=16/9. The exponential-factor modulus is bounded by
 exp(phi(t)-phi(m))*exp((1+n/t^2)*y*y/2) <= exp(25/18).
For odd n the preceding integral identity gives
 |b(z)/b(m)|<=|z|/min(t,m)<2;
the even bound is also 2. Thus |F(z)|<2 exp(25/18)<9<32.

For panels of width 1/2 and Taylor degree K, Cauchy's estimate with M=32
gives pointwise remainder <=(32/3)*4^(-K). Total interval length is 2R;
the integral error is <=(64/3)R*4^(-K)<22R*4^(-K). The implementation's
quadrature and tail bounds, 22R*4^(-K)+6 exp(-R*R/2), are conservative.
Its R=ceil sqrt(2(p+16)), K=p+R+16 leave ample error margin.

I also checked the coordinator's alternative constants: m>=2R+2 implies
t>=m/2 on unit disks, so |F(z)|<2 exp(5/2)<25<64. Unit-width panels and
degree q give error <=128R*2^(-q). On |s|<=1/2, m>=2 implies
phi''>=-25/9, so F>=.5 exp(-25/72)>.5 exp(-3/8)>1/4. Thus the proposed
smaller threshold and q=p+ceil(log2 R)+15 also work analytically.

4. EXPLICIT ALTERNATIVE THAT CLOSES THE BIT-COMPLEXITY ARGUMENT

A complex analyticity bound alone does not prove that a chosen floating
Taylor recurrence has enough guard precision. One can avoid that question
entirely with an explicit rational interpolation construction.

Retain m>=4(R+1), width-1/2 panels, and M=32 on radius-one disks. On each
panel use q+1 equally spaced rational nodes u_j=-1/4+j/(2q), j=0,...,q.
The degree-q interpolation error, by the Cauchy interpolation formula, is
 <=(4M/3)*(2/3)^(q+1), uniformly on that panel.
Indeed each numerator product factor |u-u_j|<=1/2, each contour denominator
factor |z-u_j|>=3/4, and |z-u|>=3/4 on the unit circle.
Integrated error over all panels is <=(256R/3)*(2/3)^(q+1).
Choosing q=2*(p+ceil(log2 R)+15) makes this <2^(-p)/512.

Compute the integrated Lagrange weights exactly as rationals. For the j-th
Lagrange basis, its denominator magnitude is (2q)^(-q)*j!*(q-j)! and its
numerator magnitude on the panel is at most (1/2)^q. Therefore
 sum_j integral |ell_j(u)|du <= .5*(2q)^q/q! <= .5*(2e)^q.
Across all 4R panels, sum of absolute quadrature weights <=2R*(2e)^q.
Node values accurate to absolute 2^[-p-O(q+log R)] therefore give the
required integral precision. The exact rational weights have polynomial
bit length and are constructible by polynomial arithmetic; no derivative
oracle is assumed. The node count is O(Rq)=O(p^(3/2)).

At a real node |s|<=R, F is between exp(-O(R^2)) and 2, while all log
expressions have elementary fixed-degree algebraic arguments. Computing
log1p(s/m), or its stable small-argument series, and multiplying by n costs
only O(B+L+p+q) guard bits, B=bitlength(n). Odd parity uses expm1 and the
2^(-O(L)) lower bound above. Huge d requires only binary exponent storage;
exponentially small terms can instead be discarded under an explicit error
budget. Thus this is a complete polynomial-bit-work route independently
of the submitted Taylor recurrence's precise conditioning constants.

The prefactor has magnitude O(n*(B+L)+L). Absolute accuracy in its logarithm
requires O(B+L+p) significant bits, not precision proportional to n.
logGamma at an integer can be computed by shifting an argument to O(P),
if necessary, and using O(P) Stirling terms with the explicit remainder;
take P polynomial in B+L+p. Bernoulli numbers and the O(P) shift products
are constructible in polynomial bit work. Large n does not require n!
as an expanded integer. For n<16(R+1)^2, the direct positive coefficient
sum has O(p) terms and is already a polynomial-cost alternative.

The audit-only implementation alternative_quadrature.py implements this
rational interpolation route with certified Arb arithmetic. It handles
the large-m positive branch; it is not advertised as a complete API.

6. SCOPE AND NOVELTY

The Gaussian moment identity, concavity arguments, Cauchy bounds, and
rational interpolation are standard mathematical mechanisms. This audit
does not establish historical novelty. The concrete scoped conclusion is
that binary-large-n displaced coefficient evaluation has a uniform
constructive route; tiny odd displacement is not a fatal cancellation
obstruction. Efficient evaluation alone does not establish the complete
displaced conditional sampler, physical herald preparation, tomography,
or a historic-scale breakthrough.
