## Consequence 1: an exact leading relative-entropy law

Define the ordinary, single-copy relative entropy to full separability by

\[
E_R(R_N)=\inf_{\sigma\in\mathrm{SEP}_N}D(R_N\|\sigma).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{E_R(R_N)=\ln N+O(1).} \tag{D3}
\]

More explicitly, for every fixed t>0,

\[
\ln(n+1)-\ln H(t)-tC_N\le E_R(R_N)\le\ln(n+1),
\quad H(t)=\sum_{j\ge0}(2j+1)e^{-tj(j+1)}. \tag{D4}
\]

**Lower bound.** Measurement of total spin and classical relative-entropy data processing give, for any separable sigma with spin probabilities q_j,

\[
D(R_N\|\sigma)\ge D(p_N\|q)\ge-tC_N-\ln\sum_jq_je^{-tj(j+1)}
\ge-tC_N-\ln b_N(t).
\]

The multiplicity identity

\[
\frac{m_{N,j}}{\binom{2n}{n}}=
\frac{2j+1}{n+j+1}\frac{\binom{2n}{n-j}}{\binom{2n}{n}}
\le\frac{2j+1}{n+1}
\]

implies b_N(t)<=H(t)/(n+1). Since C_N<4, the lower bound has coefficient one in ln N.

**Upper bound.** Twirl the balanced product over common rotations and all permutations. The resulting fully separable state tau_N is central, with spin law q_j=m_{N,j}/binom(2n,n) and coefficient 1/[(2j+1)binom(2n,n)] on P_{N,j}. Therefore

\[
D(R_N\|\tau_N)=\sum_jp_{N,j}\ln\left[(2j+1)f_j\binom{2n}{n}/Z_N\right].
\]

Here (2j+1)f_j<=1, while Z_N>=m_{N,0}=binom(2n,n)/(n+1). Every likelihood ratio in this sum is <=n+1, proving the upper bound. The exact displayed finite sum is generally sharper.

**Important limit:** (D3) is NOT a theorem for regularized relative entropy. The total-spin measurement is not an N-laboratory LOCC measurement. Applying it sequentially to an alternative with within-laboratory cross-copy entanglement need not preserve laboratory separability after conditioning. The usual single-copy proof cannot silently establish E_R^infinity=ln N+O(1). That remains open here.

## Consequence 2: near-unit distance despite singleton separability

The half-trace distance obeys the explicit bound

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{dist}_{\rm tr}(R_N,\mathrm{SEP}_N)
\ge\max\{0,1-7/\sqrt N-2/N\}.} \tag{D5}
\]

Use the bounded global effect e^(-tJ^2). Its target expectation is >=1-tC_N>1-4t and its separable expectation is <=H(t)/(n+1). For j>=1,

\[
(2j+1)e^{-tj(j+1)}\le\frac{3}{2t}
\left(e^{-t(j-1)j}-e^{-tj(j+1)}\right),
\]

using 1-e^(-x)>=xe^(-x). Telescoping yields H(t)<=1+3/(2t). Setting t=N^(-1/2) gives b_N(t)<=3/sqrt N+2/N, proving (D5).

This effect can be measured by resolving total spin and scoring e^(-tj(j+1)), but a full total-spin measurement is a collective operation. It is NOT the local three-axis measurement in Theorem C. Both facts coexist: a three-axis local witness certifies noisy entanglement with the stated constant gap, while the stronger near-unit noiseless distance is established using a global effect.

## Scope and verification

The balanced-product extremality proof is separate from the Gaussian replacement argument in Theorem C. Its decisive ingredients are a positive conjugation-invariant measure, Holder's inequality, the conjugate SU(2) diagonal entries, and the exact magnetization-zero multiplicity count. For odd N, higher local dimension, arbitrary central operators, signed kernels, or regularized relative entropy, the same conclusion has not been proved here.

The diagnostic `casimir_extremality_checks.py` tests dense finite-N heat operators on random product states and checks exact equality on the balanced product, then evaluates the distinct central-state relative entropy and its analytic brackets. Finite fixtures support algebra and do not certify a continuum optimization or asymptotic theorem. Historical novelty of (D1) and (D3) remains unestablished; search results did not supply a sufficiently precise priority comparison.
