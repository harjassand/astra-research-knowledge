Statement
---------
Consider k equal-frequency canonical bosonic modes with Hamiltonian
H = hbar*omega*sum_i a_i^dagger a_i. Initially their state is tau_n tensor k,
where n = (exp(hbar*omega/(k_B*T))-1)^(-1), and T>0 is the sole preparation
reservoir temperature. Work storage and Hamiltonian control are explicit
resources. All Hamiltonians return to H after preparation.

For an arbitrary final state rho, define its normal second-moment matrix by
N_ij = Tr(rho a_j^dagger a_i). Impose N_ii=n for all i. No Gaussian assumption
is imposed on rho. Let P be a rank-r orthogonal projector, 1<=r<k, with
P_ii=r/k. For a chosen 0<=nu<n, require P N P <= nu P. These are occupation
constraints on a selected set of r canonical collective modes; they do not
assert a quantum communication capacity or a complete converter specification.

Define g(x)=(x+1)ln(x+1)-x ln x, with g(0)=0, and
b=(k*n-r*nu)/(k-r). Then every admissible state satisfies

S(rho) <= r*g(nu)+(k-r)*g(b).

Consequently its free-energy increase over the initial product thermal state is
at least

Delta F >= k_B*T * [k*g(n)-r*g(nu)-(k-r)*g(b)].                 (1)

The entropy and free-energy bounds are attained by a phase-insensitive Gaussian
state with N_star=nu*P+b*(I-P). Equation (1) is therefore the exact minimum
free-energy increase in this state-preparation problem. It is a lower bound on
average preparation work, and a reversible-work infimum with ideal global
quasistatic controls. It is not a finite-time or bounded-control work equality.
At nu=0, perfect vacuum preparation requires a limiting protocol.

Proof
-----
Choose an orthonormal basis of modes adapted to ran(P) and ker(P). The total
occupation in the first r modes is t=Tr(PN)<=r*nu; the others have total
occupation k*n-t. A single bosonic mode with mean occupation x has entropy at
most g(x). One proof compares its state with tau_x by nonnegativity of relative
entropy; log(tau_x) is affine in the number operator. Subadditivity and the
concavity of g therefore give, without any Gaussian assumption,

S(rho) <= r*g(t/r)+(k-r)*g((k*n-t)/(k-r)) = F(t).

For 0<t<=r*nu<r*n,
F'(t)=g'(t/r)-g'((k*n-t)/(k-r))>0,
because g'(x)=ln(1+1/x) decreases and the first occupation is below n while
the second is above n. Continuity covers t=0. Thus F(t)<=F(r*nu).

In the adapted basis take r independent thermal modes of occupation nu and
k-r independent thermal modes of occupation b, then apply the passive unitary
that maps this basis to the physical ports. Its normal matrix is N_star, and
its diagonal is
nu*r/k+b*(1-r/k)=n.
Its selected block is exactly nu*P, and its entropy is F(r*nu), proving
attainment. In fact its individual physical-port marginals are thermal tau_n.

Initial and final mean energies are both hbar*omega*k*n, so their free-energy
difference is k_B*T times their entropy difference. The usual isothermal work
inequality gives (1). For nu>0 an ideal reversible protocol prepares each
collective thermal occupation by an isothermal frequency change followed by an
isolated adiabatic return to omega; passive mixing at equal frequency then costs
no work in the ideal mode model. Heating the complementary modes is included.
Preparing exactly nu=0 requires the infinite-gap/quasistatic limit, although the
free-energy infimum in (1) is finite. This completes the stated result.
