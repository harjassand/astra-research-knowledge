## 6. Compiling the witness distribution by a syndrome trellis

Put \(u_e=(a_e,b_e)\in\mathbb F_2^{d+1}\), and \(t=(0,\ldots,0,1)\). Fix the supplied row order. For a cut after row \(j\), write

\[
P_j=\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\ldots,u_j\},\quad
F_j=\operatorname{span}\{u_{j+1},\ldots,u_m\},
\]

\[
w_j=\dim P_j+\dim F_j-\dim\operatorname{span}\{u_1,\ldots,u_m\},
\qquad w=\max_jw_j.
\]

The feasible prefix syndromes of witnesses in \(\mathcal C\) are exactly

\[
T_j=P_j\cap(t+F_j).
\]

Since \(t\) is in the full span, this affine intersection is nonempty. Its direction space is \(P_j\cap F_j\), of dimension \(w_j\), so \(|T_j|=2^{w_j}\). Gaussian elimination constructs one offset and a basis of every \(T_j\). Enumerating those states costs \(O(m2^w)\) vector operations after polynomial linear algebra; no full \(2^{d+1}\)-state enumeration is hidden. We do not assume an optimal row ordering can be found cheaply. The width of the supplied order is computed and charged.

Let \(p_e=a_e^{\rm prob}/d_e^{\rm prob}\); the superscripts distinguish probability integers from equation coefficients. Use common prefix denominators \(D_j=\prod_{e\le j}d_e^{\rm prob}\). Let \(Z_j(s)\) be the total witness-prefix weight ending at feasible syndrome \(s\), and store integers \(G_j(s)=D_jZ_j(s)\). With absent states interpreted as zero,

\[
G_0(0)=1,\qquad
G_j(s)=d_j^{\rm prob}G_{j-1}(s)
       +a_j^{\rm prob}G_{j-1}(s+u_j).
\tag{6.1}
\]

The desired total is \(Q=G_m(t)/D_m\). Starting at \(s=t\), choose backward inclusion of coordinate \(j\) with exact rational probability

\[
\frac{a_j^{\rm prob}G_{j-1}(s+u_j)}{G_j(s)},
\]

and update \(s\leftarrow s+u_j\) if included. Induction on the recurrence proves that this samples witnesses with weight \(w(c)/Q\). Its support is then forced retained and every other row uses its original rational coin. Equation (4.1) supplies \(Y\) in one rank computation.

The trellis is standard linear-code machinery; it is reconstructed here to make the acquired representation and all costs explicit. One also has \(w\le\min\{r_A+1,k-1\}\): the first inequality follows from ambient augmented rank, and the second because the intersection at a cut is a linear image of \(\ker[u_1\ \cdots\ u_m]\), whose dimension is \(k-1\). Thus a low-nullity implementation can always use this trellis, while explicit witness enumeration gives the usual Karp–Luby baseline.

### Theorem 6.1: complete direct approximation algorithm

Let \(R=\min\{K,P\}\), computed from the rank and the supplied rational probabilities. Set

\[
n_{\rm samp}=\left\lceil3R\epsilon^{-2}\left\lceil\log_2(4/\delta)\right\rceil\right\rceil.
\]

Take this many independent witness-mixture samples and output

\[
\widehat U=Q\,\frac1{n_{\rm samp}}\sum_iY_i,
\]

clipped to \([0,1]\). In ideal exact-rational sampling, \(Y_i\in[0,1]\) has mean \(U/Q\ge1/R\). The multiplicative Chernoff bound for independent bounded variables gives relative error \(\epsilon\) with failure at most \(\delta/2\); the base-two logarithm used above is a conservative upper bound for the required natural logarithm.

There are at most \(H=2mn_{\rm samp}\) rational coin calls. To generate a rational coin with probability \(a/d\), sample a uniform integer in \([0,d-1]\) by rejection from \(\lceil\log_2d\rceil\) fair bits and test whether it is less than \(a\). Each rejection probability is at most one half. Cap every call at

\[
T=\max\{1,\lceil\log_2(2H/\delta)\rceil\}
\]

attempts; abort only if all \(T\) proposals are rejected, and return the positive fallback value one. A successful \(T\)-th proposal is accepted. A union bound makes this additional failure probability at most \(\delta/2\). Couple the capped procedure to the ideal unbounded exact sampler: they agree unless a cap abort occurs. No independence claim is made about samples conditional on avoiding every abort. Together with the ideal concentration bound, this gives a worst-case finite running-time bound and total failure probability at most \(\delta\). Since every nonaborted estimate for \(U>0\) is positive, the positive fallback also preserves the literal promise that the output is zero exactly in the preprocessed zero case.

All integer table entries have \(O(B+m)\) bits, since

\[
0\le G_j(s)\le\prod_{e\le j}(d_e^{\rm prob}+a_e^{\rm prob})\le2^jD_j.
\]

The coin probabilities also have \(O(B+m)\)-bit numerators and denominators. The values \(Y_i\) are reciprocal powers of two with exponent at most \(k-1\), so one may sum them with common denominator \(2^{k-1}\). The exact rational output has \(O(B+m+\log n_{\rm samp})\) bits. A conservative schoolbook bit-work bound is

\[
O\!\left(
(B+m+d+E_\epsilon+E_\delta)^2
+m(m+d)^3
+m2^w[(B+m)^2+d^2]
+n_{\rm samp}[md^2+mT(B+m)^2]
\right),
\tag{6.2}
\]

up to ordinary indexing overhead. The additive quadratic term charges reading the accuracy/confidence encodings and exact parameter setup for \(R,n_{\rm samp},T\); the displayed main terms also cover the probability-product work. This includes the case of a rational \(\epsilon\) with a long encoding but constant numerical value and constant sample count. The bound charges representation construction, arithmetic, rank computations, all random bits, caps and output. In particular it is polynomial in the full input length and \(\epsilon^{-1}\), with logarithmic confidence dependence apart from processing its supplied encoding, on each promised family with \(w\le c\log L\) and \(\log P\le c'\log L\), for fixed constants \(c,c'\). The simpler sufficient promise \(\sum p_e\le c'\log L\) implies the latter.
