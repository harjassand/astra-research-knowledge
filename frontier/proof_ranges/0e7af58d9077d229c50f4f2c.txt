## Scope and definition of “harmful”

Before acquiring data, specify a crystal and lattice basis, the harmful line/fault classes, the active volume, a target region \(U\), its guard collar, the spatial cutoff \(r\), the minimum reportable line/fault size, and the service conditions under which performance matters. “Harmful” is not a crystallographic label: a nonzero \(b\) alone does not establish a device penalty. The operation needs a measured functional target (for example a specified leakage, carrier lifetime, or transport metric) or a separately validated map from defect class and location to that target. Without it the result can certify structural change only.

The global active volume \(\Omega_{\rm act}\) can be larger than the locally resolved target assay. A target-local pass says that this defect/component and any products in its guard collar satisfy the stated bounds. It does not say that all remote defects in \(\Omega_{\rm act}\) were removed. A device-level retained-performance pass can qualify performance under the tested service cycle, but it cannot identify the microstructure elsewhere or promise behavior under an untested condition.
