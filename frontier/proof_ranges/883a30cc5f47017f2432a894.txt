2. Positive spectra with concealed real-time differences
The lower bound begins with a finite-difference construction, but adds a common positive background to remove an important loophole.
Let \(m\) be an odd positive integer and \(n=m+1\). Put
\[
x_j=\frac38+\frac{j}{4m},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,m.
\]
On these nodes, let \(u\) be the uniform distribution, \(u_{\mathrm{even}}\) the uniform distribution on even-indexed nodes, and \(b_+,b_-\) the binomial \(\operatorname{Bin}(m,\tfrac12)\) distribution conditioned on even and odd index.
Define
\[
\nu_\pm
=
\frac14u+\frac14u_{\mathrm{even}}+\frac12b_\pm,
\]
then add a known reference level:
\[
\boxed{
\mu_\pm=\frac14\delta_0+\frac34\nu_\pm.
}
\tag{2}
\]
Both measures have the same complete support. Every nonzero-energy weight is at least
\[
\boxed{\frac{3}{16n}.}
\tag{3}
\]
Consequently, concealment does not depend on an exponentially small overlap with some eigenmode.
The signed difference of the bulk weights is
\[
\nu_+(x_j)-\nu_-(x_j)
=
\frac{(-1)^j}{2^m}\binom mj.
\]
Finite differences annihilate polynomials of degree below \(m\). Hence
\[
\boxed{
\int x^q\,d\mu_+
=
\int x^q\,d\mu_-
\qquad(0\le q<m).
}
\tag{4}
\]
These exact local sum rules may be supplied to the estimator for free.
Imaginary-time difference
The binomial theorem gives the complete difference:
\[
\boxed{
G_+(s)-G_-(s)
=
\frac34e^{-3s/8}
\left(\frac{1-e^{-s/(4m)}}2\right)^m.
}
\tag{5}
\]
Let
\[
a=\min\!\left\{\frac{B}{8m},\frac12\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
\sup_{0\le s\le B}|G_+(s)-G_-(s)|
\le\frac34a^m.
\tag{6}
\]
The reference level ensures
\[
\frac14\le G_\pm(s)\le1
\qquad\text{for every }s\ge0.
\]
Therefore,
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{0\le s\le B}
|\log G_+(s)-\log G_-(s)|
\le3a^m.
}
\tag{7}
\]
Neither normalization nor access to relative precision removes the difference’s smallness. The measured signal itself stays above one quarter.
Real-time difference
Take
\[
t_m=4\pi m,
\qquad
\xi_m=e^{-3\pi im/2}\in\{i,-i\}.
\]
Since \(e^{-it_mx_j}=\xi_m(-1)^j\),
\[
A_+(t_m)=\frac14+\frac9{16}\xi_m,
\]
\[
A_-(t_m)=\frac14-\frac3{16}\xi_m.
\]
Thus
\[
\boxed{
p_+(t_m)=\frac{97}{256},
\qquad
p_-(t_m)=\frac{25}{256}.
}
\tag{8}
\]
The return probabilities differ by \(9/32\), despite the tiny difference in the entire available imaginary-time record.
3. Why adaptive measurements cannot bypass the bound
Suppose two Gaussian query models have conditional means differing by at most \(D\), uniformly over all allowed query locations.
The relative-entropy chain rule gives, for the complete transcript of \(N\) adaptive measurements,
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mathsf P_+\|\mathsf P_-)
\le\frac{ND^2}{2}.
\tag{9}
\]
Adaptivity does not invalidate this calculation: at a common transcript argument, the policy chooses the same next query, and the uniform mean-difference bound applies.
Pinsker’s inequality yields
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mathsf P_+,\mathsf P_-)
\le\frac{\sqrt N\,D}{2}.
\]
Equal-prior testing success of at least \(2/3\) requires total variation at least \(1/3\). Using the more permissive logarithmic measurement model and (7),
\[
\boxed{
N
\ge
\frac4{81}
\left[
\max\!\left\{2,\frac{8m}{B}\right\}
\right]^{2m}.
}
\tag{10}
\]
Predicting the return probability to error \(1/16\) distinguishes the values in (8), so this is also a prediction lower bound.
Choose the largest odd \(m\) with \(4\pi m\le T\). Then \(m=\Theta(T)\), proving the lower half of (1).
The argument covers randomized estimators, nonlinear reconstruction, adaptive temperature selection, and arbitrary subsequent processing. It is a two-point information bound, not an assumption that a particular reconstruction method performs badly. The general relationship between such bounds and functional estimation is established in optimal-recovery and statistical decision theory; that proof strategy is not claimed as new here. arXiv
A small-dimensional example
For \(m=7\), the total system has only nine levels, including the reference. Its real-time target is
\[
t_7=28\pi\approx87.96.
\]
With \(B=1\), equation (10) requires
\[
\boxed{N>10^{23}}
\]
even for the logarithmic observation model.
These are dimensionless units with spectral scale bounded by one, not laboratory seconds. The example shows that large Hilbert-space dimension is not the source of the obstruction.
