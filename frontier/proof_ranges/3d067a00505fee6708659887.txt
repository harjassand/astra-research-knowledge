FINITE POPULATION COMPILATION WITH EXACT INPUT MARGINALS
Root synthesis and independent reconstruction, 11 October 2026.
Status: complete analytic proof below; algorithm implementation is separately
recorded. Historical originality and external validation remain UNKNOWN.

1. Interface and statement

Input: an explicit total deterministic automaton (Q,A,f,q0), d=|Q|,
a rational probability vector mu, a rational target distribution nu on Q,
and a rational error tolerance. Delete zero-mass letters and let r be the
remaining alphabet size and alpha=min mu(a)>0. All correlations between
letters are allowed; only each one-coordinate marginal is prescribed.
There is no independence, blind-control, accepting-support, or per-word
constraint. Let R_n be the terminal laws of all such n-letter couplings.

For population P>=1 set
  H=binom(P+d-1,d-1), r_P=d/(2P), beta_P=d/(P alpha+d).
Construct the graph whose vertices are state histograms x of total P.
An allocation z=(z_sa) of P particles determines an edge x->y by
  x_s=sum_a z_sa, y_u=sum_{s,a:f(s,a)=u}z_sa,
provided |sum_s z_sa-P mu(a)|<=d for each a.
Let G_{P,n} be histograms/P reachable from P delta_q0 at EXACT length n;
let G_P be the union over positive n. Then, for every n>=1,
  sup_{p in R_n} dist(p,G_{P,n}) <= r_P,
  sup_{g in G_{P,n}} dist(g,R_n) <= beta_P.                (1)
Distances are TV. Both bounds are independent of n.

Consequences:
(a) The closure of union_{n>=1} R_n is within TV Hausdorff distance
    eta_P=r_P+beta_P of union_{1<=n<=H} R_n.
(b) For v_n=dist(nu,R_n), v_{P,n}=min_{g in G_{P,n}}TV(nu,g),
    max(0,v_{P,n}-r_P)<=v_n<=min(1,v_{P,n}+beta_P).         (2)
    The same interval holds for the infimum over all positive n using G_P.
(c) G_{P,n}, v_{P,n}, and a succinct repaired coupling are computable with
    dependence polynomial in the bit length of n AFTER graph acquisition.
    Exact input marginals survive, although terminal laws are approximated.
(d) For each epsilon>0 the sequence R_n is eventually epsilon-periodic
    in Hausdorff distance, with computable preperiod and period. Under a
    uniform positive terminal-gap promise, the yes-horizon set is exactly
    ultimately periodic and computably regular.

The alphabet may be elements of any explicitly supplied finite semigroup:
use an adjoined formal identity as q0, and f(s,a)=sa. No commutativity,
mixing, group structure, or regularity assumption is needed.

2. Proof of (1): global rounding instead of accumulating local errors

Standard integral-flow rounding, proved here: a finite real nonnegative flow
with integer node balances admits an integer flow with the same balances
and each edge in its original floor/ceiling interval. Noninteger edges,
viewed as an undirected multigraph, have no degree-one vertex (integer
balance would fail). Choose a cycle and adjust flows with incidence signs
until one reaches an integer endpoint. Preserve balances and all intervals.
Repeat; parallel edges are permitted, loops round independently.

For p in R_n put P times its automaton path probabilities on the n-layer
labelled graph; parallel labels remain separate. Add terminal-to-sink
edges carrying P p(s). Source and sink balances are P and -P. Round the
WHOLE flow once and decompose it into P complete words. For each time and
letter the sum of d edge-rounding errors has magnitude <=d. For each
terminal state its rounded sink flow differs from P p(s) by less than one.
Thus its histogram trajectory lies in the graph and its terminal law g
has TV(g,p)<=d/(2P), at the SAME n. No n-term error sum occurs.

Conversely any graph path gives a uniform population law on P full words.
Let c_t(a) be its label counts and mu'_t(a)=c_t(a)/P. Define one global
mixture weight beta=beta_P and filler-coordinate laws
  q_t(a)=[mu(a)-(1-beta)mu'_t(a)]/beta
        =[(P alpha+d)mu(a)-alpha c_t(a)]/d.                (3)
They are nonnegative: c_t(a)<=P mu(a)+d and mu(a)>=alpha imply the
numerator is at least d(mu(a)-alpha). They sum to one. Mix the population
law with product_t q_t in weights 1-beta,beta. Every coordinate has EXACT
law mu, at once for all t, and the terminal law changes by at most beta.
This proves (1). The filler is free to violate an accepting-support
condition; imposing one would change and potentially invalidate the theorem.

3. Arbitrary horizon, effective compactness, and robust periodicity

Each positively reachable histogram has a shortest positive witness of
length <=H: if different from the initial vertex, use a simple path of
length <=H-1; for a positive return to the initial vertex, use a shortest
nonempty cycle through it, of length <=H. Round at any n, delete loops
in the histogram path, then apply the single filler to the shortened path.
This proves (a). A finite union of R_n is compact (each R_n is the image
of a finite-dimensional compact coupling polytope), so it also approximates
the closure. P=ceil(2d/(alpha epsilon)) gives eta_P<=3epsilon/4.

For fixed P the vertex subsets V_n reachable at exact length n obey the
deterministic recurrence V_{n+1}=Neighbors(V_n). There are at most 2^H
subsets, hence a computable preperiod and period, each with sum <=2^H,
after which G_{P,n} repeats exactly. By (1), writing delta=max(r_P,beta_P),
  d_H(R_{n+L},R_n)<=2delta
throughout that tail. Choose P for 2delta<=epsilon to obtain (d).
For a promised dichotomy v_n<=a OR v_n>=a+gamma for EVERY n>=1,
choose delta<gamma/3. Then v_{P,n}<a+gamma/2 exactly classifies the two
cases by (2). This predicate is ultimately periodic. No claim is made for
horizons whose true values lie in the gap. Exact threshold equality or
exact finite-horizon attainment need not be decidable and may be nonperiodic.

4. Acquisition and numerical output

Enumerate the H histograms and ONCE all weak compositions of P into d*r
cells. There are B_P=binom(P+d*r-1,d*r-1) candidates; each determines its
source and target uniquely. Rationally test its r quotas and retain one
allocation for each distinct edge. Let L=ceil(log2(n+1)), and let b be the
total bit length of the rational input. Graph acquisition costs
  O(B_P * poly(d,r,b,log P)) bit operations,
with a conservative storage bound O(H^2*d*r*log(P+1)) bits for labelled
adjacency/witness data, plus graph indices. Hashing can reduce sparse storage
but is not needed in the bound. Explicit table reading is charged.

Boolean repeated squaring computes exact-n reachability and midpoint
witnesses in O(H^3 L) Boolean operations; ordinary index arithmetic adds
polynomial log H factors in a bit model. Minimize the rational distances
to nu over the reached histograms. Their arithmetic bit length is
O(b+log P+log d). This computes interval (2). For arbitrary positive
horizons, graph search suffices, taking care to distinguish a zero-step
initial vertex from a genuine positive return.

This is polynomial in log n for fixed compiled accuracy and automaton.
For fixed d,r and alpha bounded below it is polynomial in 1/epsilon.
It is NOT polynomial jointly in d,r and log(1/epsilon). If alpha is tiny
in its rational encoding, P can be exponential in b. These costs are part
of the algorithm, not a hidden oracle. Approximate input marginals need
separate input-error bounds; the theorem uses supplied exact rationals.

5. A succinct coupling and consistent random access

Canonical slots 0,...,P-1 at histogram x are ordered by state, then by
rank in its state block. For an edge allocation z, partition each source
block by letter. Send each (source,letter,rank) slot to the destination
block, ordered by source,letter,rank. This defines a BIJECTION pi_e of
P slots and a letter function ell_e on the source slots. The bijection is
crucial: a uniform slot stays uniform through every prefix. Every path
therefore specifies P full words without requiring them to be printed.

Midpoint witnesses of Boolean powering yield a concatenation DAG for a
chosen length-n graph path. Store nodes indexed by (level,source,target),
and O(log n) nodes for concatenating selected binary powers. There are
O(H^2 L) nodes and depth O(L). Compute each node's P-slot permutation:
  pi_parent=pi_right o pi_left.
Costs O(P H^2 L) slot operations and O(P H^2 L log(P+1)) bits of storage,
in addition to the graph. Leaf nodes retain their allocations and q_e.

Let mu(a)=u_a/Q in a common integer denominator, u_min=min u_a, and
F=d Q^2. Formula (3) has exact integer numerators
  w_e(a)=P*u_min*u_a+d*Q*u_a-Q*u_min*c_e(a),
  q_e(a)=w_e(a)/F.
Use ONE shared uniform integer J in {0,...,F-1}: at EVERY position, emit
the letter obtained by inverse-CDF sampling q_e with this same J. The
resulting filler law has every required marginal q_t. Independence is
unnecessary. A repeated query needs no cache; all queries are deterministic
given the one seed and witness. It remains a valid filler for (1).

Sample once a mixture coin of probability beta. On the population branch,
sample one uniform initial slot I. To query coordinate t, descend the DAG;
when moving to the right child update I by the left permutation. At the
leaf return ell_e(I). Each query costs O(log n) table lookups and
polynomial log P index arithmetic. The terminal state is determined by
the final canonical slot pi_root(I). On the filler branch sample J once,
and query by locating the leaf and returning its quantile letter a_e(J).

For a sampled J, define each leaf's deterministic map T_e(s)=f(s,a_e(J))
on the ORIGINAL d automaton states, and compose T_parent=T_right o T_left
on the witness DAG. In O(d H^2 log n) map-entry operations, this gives
the exact terminal state T_root(q0), fully consistent with all coordinate
queries. The maps can be retained for further samples or computed anew;
the stated per-sample preprocessing is charged.

Even the complete terminal law of the chosen repaired coupling is exactly
computable with polynomial graph/log-horizon costs. Gather all leaf-CDF
breakpoints, integers in [0,F]. There are at most (r-1)E+2 distinct ones,
where E is the number of distinct leaf allocations, E<=H^2 if one was
retained per edge. Between consecutive breakpoints every leaf has a fixed
letter. Compose the d-state maps once for each interval and allocate its
length/F to the resulting terminal state. This uses
  O(r E d H^2 log n)
map-entry operations plus sorting and O(b+log P+log d)-bit rational
arithmetic; it DOES NOT enumerate all d Q^2 seeds. The filler law has
at most (r-1)E+1 distinct deterministic words, each represented by the
same DAG. Including the population, the full coupling has at most
P+(r-1)E+1 atoms with succinct word programs. Some atoms may coincide.
If m_s is the total integer quantile-interval length ending in state s,
its exact repaired terminal probability is
  p'(s)=[Q*u_min*x_s+m_s]/[Q*(P*u_min+d*Q)].
Its rational bit length is O(b+log P+log d), independent of n.

Equivalently the complete coupling is a uniform law on
  R=Q*(P*u_min+d*Q)
possibly repeated deterministic words: each population word has Q*u_min
copies and each filler interval has its integer-width copies. Every
coordinate has exactly R*mu(a) copies of letter a. One uniform integer
U in [0,R) supplies ALL randomness: U<P*Q*u_min selects slot
floor(U/(Q*u_min)); otherwise J=U-P*Q*u_min is the filler quantile seed.
With P=ceil(2dQ/(u_min*epsilon)),
  R<=dQ^2*(2/epsilon+1)+Q*u_min.
Thus a horizon-independent exact-quota population approximates all terminal
laws. Its denominator is polynomial in Q, not in log Q. Sampling uses
O(b+log P+log d) expected fair bits via integer rejection, independent
of n, and has unbounded worst-case rejection time. No finite-worst-case
exact sampler is claimed for arbitrary rationals. Conditional on its
sampled seed, the entire word and terminal state are deterministic.

After compilation, k adaptive coordinate queries cost O(k log n) table
lookups plus index/quantile comparisons; seed storage has O(b+log P+log d)
bits, independent of n and k. Terminal-state sampling has the charged
map-preprocessing cost above, or can use the precomputed quantile intervals.
Emitting the full word still requires Omega(n) time and Omega(n log r)
output bits under a fixed-symbol encoding. This is a succinct joint-law
output interface, not a sublinear-time printer for a full length-n string.

6. Verification and mathematical provenance

A certificate records the graph allocations used by the witness DAG,
source/target/length identities at each node, the root length n, terminal
histogram, q_e, beta, and the rational cost interval. Verifying each leaf's
row, transition, and quota sums and each DAG concatenation identity is
polynomial in this explicit certificate size and rational bit length.
The universal rounding argument proves the lower optimum bound; no finite
execution certifies that universal argument by itself. The supplied coupling's
exact-marginal validity and upper cost bound admit finite algebraic checks.

Whole-flow floor/ceiling rounding is standard integral-network mathematics.
The present derived operation combines it with ONE horizon-independent
filler and finite histogram recurrence. This combination creates uniform
terminal approximation while preserving all input marginals exactly.
