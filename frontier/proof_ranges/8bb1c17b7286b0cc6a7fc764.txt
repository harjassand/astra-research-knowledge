# Universal radial endpoint and the sharp iterated-log correction

Date: 2026-10-15. Status: a complete independent proof candidate, awaiting the separate audit requested by the root agent. This file claims analytic proof, not formal verification, numerical certification, external validation, or historical novelty. Later corrections must be appended or saved in a subsequent version.

## Result

Let \(m\ge 1\), \(R>0\), and let \(A_1,\ldots,A_m\) be bounded self-adjoint operators on an arbitrary complex Hilbert space. Assume
\[
 A(u)=\sum_{j=1}^m u_jA_j,\qquad \|A(u)\|\le R|u|\quad(u\in\mathbb R^m).
\]
Write \(W_A\) for the compactly supported operator-valued distribution with Fourier transform \(e^{iA(t)}\). For \(0\le\beta\le1\), define
\[
 Q(s)=\sqrt{e^2+s^2},\qquad
 f_\beta(s)=Q(s)\bigl(\log Q(s)+\beta\log\log Q(s)\bigr)-e,
\]
\[
 V_{L,\beta}(x)=\frac{L}{2R}f_\beta(|x|/L),\qquad
 p_{L,\beta}(x)=Z_{L,\beta}^{-1}e^{-V_{L,\beta}(x)}.
\]
For every \(0<\varepsilon<1\), the explicit conservative sufficient scale
\[
 \boxed{L\ge \frac{800e^3}{\varepsilon}\,m^2(m+1)2^m R}
 \tag{T1}
\]
ensures that the operator density
\[
 M_A(x)=(W_A*p_{L,\beta})(x)
\]
satisfies, for every \(x\in\mathbb R^m\),
\[
 \boxed{(1-\varepsilon)p_{L,\beta}(x)e^{A(\nabla V_{L,\beta}(x))}
 \preceq M_A(x)\preceq
 (1+\varepsilon)p_{L,\beta}(x)e^{A(\nabla V_{L,\beta}(x))}.}
 \tag{T2}
\]
It is norm-continuous, norm-integrable, positive, normalized by \(\int M_A(x)\,dx=I\), and
\[
 \widehat M_A(t)=\widehat p_{L,\beta}(t)e^{iA(t)}.
 \tag{T3}
\]
All constants are independent of the Hilbert-space dimension and the tuple within the stated norm ball. The same scalar density works for every such tuple. It is everywhere positive, smooth, radial, strictly log-concave, and has all exponential moments; each derivative decays faster than each exponential.

For fixed \(L\), its tail has the precise logarithmic expansion
\[
 -\log p_{L,\beta}(x)
 =\frac{r}{2R}\bigl(\log r+\beta\log\log r-\log L+o(1)\bigr),
 \qquad r=|x|\longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{T4}
\]
Thus \(\beta=0\) attains the previously open \(r\log r\) endpoint. The stronger choice \(\beta=1\) attains the sharp \(r\log\log r\) correction. In particular, the latter density obeys an eventual upper bound \(p(x)\le C\exp[-r\log r/(2R)]\) with the exact critical coefficient.

For \(m\ge2\), already the Pauli pair excludes any radial all-exponential noise density with an eventual bound
\[
 p(x)\le C\exp\left[-\frac{r}{2R}
  \bigl(\log r+\log\log r+b(r)\bigr)\right],
 \qquad b(r)\longrightarrow+\infty.
 \tag{T5}
\]
Consequently no positive additional divergent correction inside the parentheses is possible under this pointwise tail-envelope contract. The coefficient of \(\log\log r\) is exactly one. For the fixed-offset envelope obtained by replacing \(b(r)\) with a constant \(b\), a necessary Pauli bound is
\[
 \boxed{b\le \log(2/R)-1.}
 \tag{T6}
\]
The construction proves existence for sufficiently negative constant offsets; it does not attain or characterize the optimal constant offset. That remaining question must not be conflated with the now-attained leading endpoint or the sharp iterated-log correction.

## 1. The inherited Weyl distribution

For real \(t,\eta\), the bounded-operator Trotter formula gives
\[
 \|e^{iA(t)-A(\eta)}\|\le e^{R|\eta|}.
\]
The inverse Fourier transform of the norm-continuous bounded function \(e^{iA(t)}\) defines a norm-continuous operator-valued tempered distribution. For example, a Fourier \(L^1\) estimate after applying \((1-\Delta)^k\), \(2k>m\), establishes finite-order continuity on each compact set. Scalarization and the Paley-Wiener-Schwartz theorem put its support in \(\overline B_R\). These arguments involve operator norms, not a sum over a Hilbert-space basis.

Compact support permits evaluation on arbitrary smooth functions by inserting a cutoff equal to one near \(\overline B_R\). In particular,
\[
 W_A(e^{g\cdot y})=e^{A(g)},\qquad
 W_A(y^\alpha e^{g\cdot y})=\partial_g^\alpha e^{A(g)}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]
These identities follow either by entire continuation of the transform or by differentiating the compactly supported distribution. Self-adjointness of the distribution follows from \(e^{iA(-t)}=e^{iA(t)*}\).

This representation and support machinery are established Weyl/Wigner calculus. The primary paper by Schwonnek and Werner explicitly gives the Fourier definition, ordered moments, compact support, and Gaussian convolution for plotting: [The Wigner distribution of n arbitrary observables](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.08342), Sections II.2-II.4 and III.1. Anderson's original calculus is an older antecedent: [The Weyl functional calculus](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(69)90013-5). No priority claim is made for these ingredients, and no inference is made that Gaussian plotting regularization proves positivity.

## 2. The two-denominator Duhamel bound

**Lemma 2.1.** Let \(H=H^*\), \(q=\|H\|\), and \(k\ge2\). For arbitrary bounded insertions \(K_1,\ldots,K_k\),
\[
 \left\|e^{-H/2}D^k\exp(H)[K_1,\ldots,K_k]e^{-H/2}\right\|
 \le 2k(k-1)\frac{e^{2q}}{(1+q)^2}\prod_{j=1}^k\|K_j\|.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

**Proof.** The iterated Duhamel formula is the sum over \(k!\) permutations of the insertions of
\[
 \int_{\substack{t_j\ge0\\\sum_{j=0}^k t_j=1}}
 e^{t_0H}K_{\pi(1)}e^{t_1H}\cdots K_{\pi(k)}e^{t_kH}\,dt.
\]
After sandwiching, the norm of the exponential factors is at most
\[
 \exp\left(q\bigl(|t_0-\tfrac12|+|t_k-\tfrac12|+1-t_0-t_k\bigr)\right).
\]
Fix \(u=t_0\), \(v=t_k\). Integrating the remaining simplex contributes
\((1-u-v)^{k-2}/(k-2)!\le1/(k-2)!\). Set
\[
 I(q)=\int_{\substack{u,v\ge0\\u+v\le1}}
 e^{q(|u-1/2|+|v-1/2|+1-u-v)}\,du\,dv.
\]
For \(0\le q\le1\), \(I(q)\le e^{2q}/2\le2e^{2q}/(1+q)^2\). For \(q\ge1\), partition the triangle into its square \(0\le u,v\le1/2\) and the other two triangles. On the square the exponent is \(2q(1-u-v)\); the square integral is at most \(e^{2q}/(4q^2)\). On the two triangles the exponent is at most \(q\), and their total area is \(1/4\). Hence
\[
 I(q)\le \frac{e^{2q}}{4q^2}+\frac{e^q}{4}
 \le\left(1+e^{-1}\right)\frac{e^{2q}}{(1+q)^2}
 \le2\frac{e^{2q}}{(1+q)^2}.
\]
Here \((1+q)^2/(4q^2)\le1\), and \((1+q)^2e^{-q}/4\le e^{-1}\) for \(q\ge1\). Multiplication by \(k!/(k-2)!=k(k-1)\) proves (2.1). \(\square\)

The function \(e^{2q}/(1+q)^2\) is nondecreasing for \(q\ge0\). Taking \(H=A(g)\), each insertion among the \(A_j\), and \(k=|\alpha|\ge2\), therefore gives
\[
 \left\|e^{-A(g)/2}\partial_g^\alpha e^{A(g)}e^{-A(g)/2}\right\|
 \le 2k(k-1)R^k
       \frac{e^{2R|g|}}{(1+R|g|)^2}.
 \tag{2.2}
\]
It is essential that two endpoint simplex variables survive when \(k\ge2\). Bounding the whole integrand by \(e^{2q}\) loses the decisive denominator. The present argument does not assert the same denominator for \(k=0\) or \(k=1\).

## 3. Applying an analytic local remainder to the distribution

**Lemma 3.1.** Let \(V\) be real-valued and smooth, with a holomorphic extension from \(x\) to the closed complex polydisc \(|z_j|\le\rho\), where \(\rho>R\), in the expression \(V(x-z)\). Put
\[
 g=\nabla V(x),\quad F_x(z)=V(x-z)-V(x)+g\cdot z,
 \quad \delta_x=\sup_{|z_j|\le\rho}|F_x(z)|.
\]
If \(\delta_x\le1\), set \(h_x(z)=e^{-F_x(z)}-1\). Then
\[
 \left\|e^{-A(g)/2}W_A(e^{g\cdot y}h_x(y))e^{-A(g)/2}\right\|
 \le 2e\,\delta_x\,
 \frac{m(m+1)(R/\rho)^2}{(1-R/\rho)^{m+2}}
 \frac{e^{2R|g|}}{(1+R|g|)^2}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]

**Proof.** Exactly \(F_x(0)=0\) and \(\nabla F_x(0)=0\). Consequently the Taylor series
\[
 h_x(z)=\sum_{|\alpha|\ge2}c_\alpha z^\alpha
\]
has no degree-zero or degree-one terms. Its complex sup norm is at most \(e\delta_x\), so Cauchy's estimate gives
\( |c_\alpha|\le e\delta_x\rho^{-|\alpha|}\).

Choose any \(a\) with \(R<a<\rho\), and a cutoff supported in \(B_a\) and equal to one near \(\overline B_R\). The series, including each fixed finite order of real derivatives, converges uniformly on \(B_a\). Multiplication by this fixed cutoff and by \(e^{g\cdot y}\) preserves that convergence. Finite-order continuity of \(W_A\) thus justifies termwise evaluation, without imposing analyticity on the cutoff. By (1.1) and (2.2), its sandwiched image has an absolutely norm-convergent series bounded by
\[
 2e\delta_x\frac{e^{2R|g|}}{(1+R|g|)^2}
 \sum_{k\ge2}k(k-1){k+m-1\choose m-1}(R/\rho)^k.
\]
Twice differentiating \((1-z)^{-m}\) shows that the sum is
\(m(m+1)z^2/(1-z)^{m+2}\) at \(z=R/\rho\). This proves (3.1). \(\square\)

For \(p=Z^{-1}e^{-V}\), the identity
\[
 p(x-y)=p(x)e^{g\cdot y}(1+h_x(y))
\]
is exact. Thus
\[
 (W_A*p)(x)=p(x)\left[e^{A(g)}+E_x\right],\qquad
 E_x=W_A(e^{g\cdot y}h_x(y)).
 \tag{3.2}
\]
The error is self-adjoint. A bound of \(\varepsilon\) in (3.1) therefore proves precisely the Loewner inequalities (T2).

## 4. Uniform analytic bounds for the explicit radial potentials

Set \(c=1/(2R)\), \(s=|x|/L\), \(Q=\sqrt{e^2+s^2}\), and \(\ell=\log Q\ge1\). Let
\[
 h_\beta(q)=q(\log q+\beta\log\log q),\quad
 b_\beta=\ell+\beta\log\ell+1+\beta/\ell.
\]
At real \(x\),
\[
 |\nabla V_{L,\beta}(x)|=c w,\qquad
 w=\frac{s}{Q}b_\beta\ge0.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Use \(\rho=2R\). Assume first
\[
 L\ge 8\sqrt m\rho/e.
 \tag{4.2}
\]
For a complex displacement \(|z_j|\le\rho\) and \(0\le t\le1\), write
\(\zeta=x/L-tz/L\). Then
\(d=|tz|/L\le e/8\le Q/8\). For
\(D=e^2+\sum_j\zeta_j^2\),
\[
 |D-Q^2|\le2sd+d^2\le(17/64)Q^2.
 \tag{4.3}
\]
In particular \(\Re D>0\), and the principal branch \(q=\sqrt D\) is holomorphic throughout a neighborhood of the closed polydisc. The branches \(\log q\) and \(\log\log q\) are also holomorphic there, because
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{47}}8Q\le|q|\le\frac98Q,\qquad
 |\arg q|<\pi/4,
\]
\[
 \Re\log q\ge\ell+\log(\sqrt{47}/8)>\ell/2.
 \tag{4.4}
\]
The following loose bounds hold uniformly for \(0\le\beta\le1\):
\[
 |\log q|\le2\ell,\quad |(\log q)^{-1}|\le2/\ell,
 \quad |\log\log q|\le4\ell,
\]
\[
 |h_\beta'(q)|\le9\ell,\qquad
 |h_\beta''(q)|\le9/Q.
 \tag{4.5}
\]
For example, the real and imaginary parts of \(\log\log q\) have absolute-sum at most \(\log\ell+\log2+\pi/2\le4\ell\). Also
\[
 h_\beta''(q)=\frac{1+\beta/\log q-\beta/(\log q)^2}{q}.
\]

Using \(|\zeta|\le9Q/8\), the complex Hessian of \(h_\beta(\sqrt{e^2+\sum\zeta_j^2})\) obeys
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|D^2f_\beta(\zeta)\|
 &\le |h_\beta''(q)|\frac{|\zeta|^2}{|q|^2}
 +|h_\beta'(q)|\left(\frac1{|q|}+\frac{|\zeta|^2}{|q|^3}\right)\\
 &\le50\frac\ell Q.
 \end{split}
 \tag{4.6}
\]
The notation \(f_\beta(\zeta)\) in (4.6) means its radial analytic extension; no complex Euclidean norm is used inside that extension. The displayed norm is the ordinary induced matrix norm after taking the holomorphic derivatives.

Taylor's formula along the straight segment gives
\[
 F_x(z)=\int_0^1(1-t)z^TD^2V_{L,\beta}(x-tz)z\,dt,
\]
and therefore
\[
 \delta_x\le \frac{25cm\rho^2}{L}\frac\ell Q
 =\frac{50mR}{L}\frac\ell Q.
 \tag{4.7}
\]
Since \(\ell/Q\le1/e\), the additional condition \(L\ge50mR/e\) makes \(\delta_x\le1\) everywhere.

At \(\rho=2R\), the combinatorial factor in (3.1) is
\[
 S_m=m(m+1)2^m.
\]
It follows that the relative error is at most
\[
 \frac{100e\,mRS_m}{L}
 \frac\ell Q\frac{e^w}{(1+w/2)^2}.
 \tag{4.8}
\]
The last scalar expression is bounded globally as follows:
\[
 e^w\le e^{b_\beta}=Q\ell^\beta e^{1+\beta/\ell}
 \le e^2Q\ell^\beta.
\]
If \(s\ge e\), then \(s/Q\ge1/\sqrt2\), so \(w\ge\ell/\sqrt2\). If \(s<e\), then \(\ell\le\log(e\sqrt2)<\sqrt2\). In either case
\[
 1+w/2\ge\ell/(2\sqrt2).
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac\ell Q\frac{e^w}{(1+w/2)^2}
 \le8e^2\ell^{\beta-1}\le8e^2.
 \tag{4.9}
\]
Combining (4.8)-(4.9) gives
\[
 \boxed{\sup_x\|e^{-A(g)/2}E_xe^{-A(g)/2}\|
 \le\frac{800e^3mRS_m}{L}.}
 \tag{4.10}
\]
Condition (T1) dominates the preliminary conditions (4.2) and \(L\ge50mR/e\), and makes (4.10) at most \(\varepsilon\). This proves positivity on all of \(\mathbb R^m\), including arbitrarily remote tails.

## 5. Strict convexity, exponential moments, and normalization

For real \(q\ge e\) and \(0\le\beta\le1\),
\[
 h_\beta'(q)>0,\qquad
 h_\beta''(q)=\frac{1+\beta/\log q-\beta/(\log q)^2}{q}>0.
\]
The radial and angular eigenvalues of the Hessian of \(f_\beta\) are, respectively,
\[
 f_\beta''(s)=h_\beta''(Q)\frac{s^2}{Q^2}
                +h_\beta'(Q)\frac{e^2}{Q^3}>0,
 \qquad \frac{f_\beta'(s)}s=\frac{h_\beta'(Q)}Q>0.
\]
At the origin these agree by continuity. Thus \(V_{L,\beta}\) is strictly convex and \(p_{L,\beta}\) is strictly log-concave.

The potential is real analytic everywhere and grows superlinearly. Its derivatives of each fixed order grow at most polynomially in \(1+\log(2+r)\) (in fact derivatives of order at least two decay, up to logarithms). The chain rule proves that every fixed derivative of \(p\) decays faster than every exponential. In particular \(Z\) is finite and all exponential moments exist.

The compact distribution convolved with this smooth density is norm-continuous. The already established upper Loewner bound gives
\[
 \|M_A(x)\|\le(1+\varepsilon)p(x)e^{R|\nabla V(x)|}.
\]
Here \(|\nabla V(x)|=O(\log(2+r)+\log\log(e^2+r))\). The right side is integrable, so \(M_A\) is Bochner norm-integrable even in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. The distributional convolution identity yields (T3). Both sides there are continuous functions of real \(t\), so evaluation at \(t=0\) is legitimate and gives
\[
 \int M_A(x)\,dx=\widehat p(0)e^{A(0)}=I.
\]
This proves the full POVM assertion without any finite-dimensional approximation.

Finally \(Q(s)=s+O(s^{-1})\), giving
\[
 f_\beta(s)=s(\log s+\beta\log\log s)-e+O(\log s/s).
\]
For fixed \(L\), \(\log\log(r/L)=\log\log r+o(1)\). The normalization contributes only a constant. Equation (T4) follows.

## 6. Pauli obstruction at the iterated-log scale

Let \(a=2R\), and use \(A_1=R\sigma_z\), \(A_2=R\sigma_x\), with any further coordinates zero. Suppose radial noise \(\nu\), with every exponential moment, makes the scalar convolution a positive normalized operator measure. Let
\[
 K(s)=\log\int e^{s x_1}\,\nu(dx),\qquad s\ge0.
\]
For the smooth densities constructed above the moment identities follow directly from the integrable domination. For a general proposed all-exponential regularizer and positive convolution, they follow by the analytic-transform/moment argument supplied in the source theorem; equivalently one may restrict this obstruction to smooth densities whose distributional convolution has exponential integrable domination. This distinction does not affect any explicit density family discussed here, but the general positive-measure moment extension must be retained when claiming the widest necessity statement.

For completeness, the necessary tilted second-moment calculation is explicit. Define
\[
 B_j(s)=e^{-sA_1/2}\left.\partial_t^j e^{sA_1+tA_2}\right|_{t=0}e^{-sA_1/2}.
\]
Radial symmetry gives transverse scalar mean zero and variance \(K'(s)/s\). Positivity of the block moment matrix and its Schur complement implies
\[
 \frac{K'(s)}s I+B_2(s)-B_1(s)^2\succeq0.
\]
Direct differentiation of
\(e^{R(s\sigma_z+t\sigma_x)}\) gives
\[
 B_1(s)=\frac{\sinh(Rs)}s\sigma_x,
\]
\[
 B_2(s)-B_1(s)^2
 =\operatorname{diag}\left(-\frac{\sinh(2Rs)-2Rs}{2s^2},
                            \frac{\sinh(2Rs)-2Rs}{2s^2}\right).
\]
Thus
\[
 K'(s)\ge\frac{\sinh(as)-as}{2s},\qquad
 \liminf_{s\to\infty}s e^{-as}K(s)\ge\frac1{4a}.
 \tag{6.1}
\]
The second assertion follows by integrating the first from zero and using
\(\int^s e^{at}/t\,dt\sim e^{as}/(as)\).

**Lemma 6.1 (tail upper bound).** If a probability density on \(\mathbb R^m\) has, for all sufficiently large \(r=|x|\), the bound
\[
 p(x)\le C\exp[-f_b(r)],\qquad
 f_b(r)=\frac r a(\log r+\log\log r+b),
\]
then
\[
 \limsup_{s\to\infty}s e^{-as}\log\int e^{s x_1}p(x)\,dx
 \le\frac{e^{-b-1}}{a^2}.
 \tag{6.2}
\]

**Proof.** Above a fixed radius greater than \(e\), \(f_b\) is strictly convex. Its Legendre maximizer \(r_s\) satisfies, with \(y_s=\log r_s\),
\[
 as=y_s+\log y_s+b+1+1/y_s.
\]
Consequently
\[
 y_s=as-\log(as)-b-1+o(1),\qquad
 r_s\sim\frac{e^{as-b-1}}{as},
\]
\[
 f_b^*(s)=sr_s-f_b(r_s)=\frac{r_s}a(1+1/y_s)
 \sim\frac{e^{as-b-1}}{a^2s}.
\]
To justify integration without losing the sharp exponential scale, take \(d_s=s^{-2}\). For the large-radius portion,
\[
 \int r^{m-1}e^{sr-f_b(r)}\,dr
 \le e^{f_b^*(s+d_s)}\int_0^\infty r^{m-1}e^{-d_s r}\,dr
 =e^{f_b^*(s+d_s)}\Gamma(m)d_s^{-m}.
\]
The bounded-radius portion of the moment-generating integral is at most \(e^{s r_0}\). Surface area, the tail constant, and \(\Gamma(m)d_s^{-m}\) contribute only \(O(\log s)\) to its logarithm, while the bounded-radius contribution is negligible on the scale \(e^{as}/s\). Replacing \(s\) by \(s+d_s\) changes the asymptotic coefficient by \(1+o(1)\). This proves (6.2). \(\square\)

Comparing (6.1) and (6.2) forces
\[
 \frac{e^{-b-1}}{a^2}\ge\frac1{4a},
\]
which is (T6). If the stronger envelope (T5) holds, it also holds with every fixed constant \(b\) beyond a sufficiently large radius. Taking any \(b>\log(4/a)-1\) contradicts (T6). In particular a coefficient \(\beta>1\) of \(\log\log r\), or a further positive divergent \(\log\log\log r\) term, is impossible under the stated envelope.

## 7. Scope, relation to the supplied proof, and remaining work

The supplied source uses a finite-order distribution estimate on an exponential times a remainder. Differentiating that exponential introduces powers of the tilt, and support enlargement introduces \(e^{a|g|}\) with \(a>R\). Those losses leave only \(c<1/(2R)\). The present proof instead expands the analytic remainder, whose first two coefficients vanish, and evaluates each coefficient using exact derivatives of \(e^{A(g)}\). The endpoint integrations in Duhamel's formula then provide \((1+R|g|)^{-2}\). This replaces a sufficient estimate; it does not contradict the earlier theorem or its obstruction.

The proof uses established Wigner/Weyl support and convolution machinery. The new work here is the specific relative analytic estimate and its endpoint application. Search and inspection of the cited primary Wigner paper were limited to identifying overlap; there has been no exhaustive historical-priority clearance.

No finite grid or numerical positivity test is used anywhere in the existence proof. The sufficient scale is explicit and deliberately large, and is not a practical noise or hardware guarantee. The result applies to bounded observables with the directional norm bound, not arbitrary unbounded observables.

Open after this proof: optimal constant offset at the \(r(\log r+\log\log r)\) scale; efficient regularization scales; exact historical priority. The universal leading endpoint and the maximum iterated-log coefficient are closed by the arguments above, conditional only on the indicated independent proof audit.
