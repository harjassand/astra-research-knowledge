N854-scalar-control-response-acquisition-and-weak-anchor-boundary | Generic scalar control-response acquisition with exact robustness obstructions
Status: source_derived_unreviewed. Mission status: NOT_ACHIEVED.

For a fixed linear apparatus with A(u)=A+P diag(u) P^T and scalar readout f(u)=c^T A(u)^(-1)b, assume all queried inverses exist, the rank-one actuator laws and their independence are calibrated, the plant/source/detector are fixed across calibration and reuse, applied control values are known, and settling/reference reset are stable. For generic coefficients, two distinct permissible positive actuator values plus the reference identify the entire future diagonal-control response map from Q(m)=(3m^2-m+4)/2 scalar configurations for m>=2 (three for m=1), up to a weighted-transpose ambiguity that preserves every allowed prediction. It does not reconstruct A or support changed sources/detectors or coupled controls. Genericity excludes algebraic exceptional sets; it is not a uniform noise-robustness theorem. A strictly binary actuator is insufficient for this stated schedule.

Two exact limitations are retained. First, the fixed-anchor schedule has no uniform robustness: exact weak-anchor examples hide an unqueried interior triple, with arbitrarily faint generic calibration separation. Second, for even m>=4, two strictly dissipative plants can agree under every intervention changing fewer than all m controls while differing on a collective response; at m=32 the two named actions are oppositely ranked. One full-support measurement separates the pair. No exponential query lower bound is claimed. Synthetic ternary and other finite checks do not establish a physical acquisition or model-fitting advantage; measured-model wave control, Virtual VNA and a 2026 read-once-determinant learner are comparators.
