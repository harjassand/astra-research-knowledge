N690-contractive-gradient-descent-rate-uncomputability | Contractive gradient descent can have uncomputable loss-decay exponents
status=source_derived_unreviewed. The pasted report provides a proof narrative and reports finite exact checks; this intake did not independently reconstruct the proof, run its code, or review priority.

For each fixed rational 0<ε<1, the report constructs a fixed rational finite-dimensional C^{1,1} objective formed from positive quadratic regularization and nonnegative squared-ReLU terms. It has unique minimizer zero, condition number at most (1+ε)/(1−ε), and the ordinary unit-step full-batch gradient map G is globally ε-contractive. Only a rational starting point varies. A polynomial-time encoding E of a finite program and input is claimed to satisfy

    Λ(E(w)) = lim_{t→∞} −(1/t) log₂(f(G^t E(w))/f(E(w)))

with two fixed values, 2k−2 if the computation accepts and 2k if it never accepts. The report concludes that approximating this actual asymptotic logarithmic loss-decay exponent to 1/3 additive accuracy is undecidable. This is not a claim that minimization is hard: the minimizer is known to be zero. Absolute-error state prediction is source-reported as easy under contraction.

The proposed gradient-exchange mechanism starts from p(a,b)=¼[σ(a+b)^2−σ(a−b)^2−σ(b)^2+σ(−b)^2], with ∇p(a,0)=(0,σ(a)) and ∇p(0,b)=(b/2,0). An extension of a recurrent ReLU map outside its valid nonnegative region supplies the reverse transfer needed for one scalar potential. Ordinary gradient descent then simulates one recurrent update every two steps while the on-trajectory loss stays exactly quadratic. The source further claims a fixed-threshold finite-horizon problem is EXPTIME-complete when T is supplied in binary, and ¼-accurate prediction of the finite-horizon logarithmic rate is EXPTIME-hard.

Reported interface boundaries are essential: exact rational bit lengths grow with time; fixed dimensionality is not fixed memory. The construction does not establish hardness at constant physical precision, for analytic/C² losses, other learning rates, or gradient flow. It establishes neither hardness of finding a minimizer nor general online-gradient universality. The source distinguishes the online-gradient result of Chatziafratis–Roughgarden–Wang (https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01280) and Welper’s stop-gradient/conditional-gradient construction (https://arxiv.org/html/2007.13664v1); historical priority remains UNKNOWN.
