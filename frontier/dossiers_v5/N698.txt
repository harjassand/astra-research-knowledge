INTERFACE
Let Q be a d-state set, A a finite alphabet, q0 an initial state, and f:Q×A→Q a supplied total deterministic transition table. The supplied input law μ on A is rational; zero-mass letters are deleted, r=|supp μ|, and α=min_{μ(a)>0} μ(a). At horizon n≥1, R_n is the set of terminal-state laws generated by arbitrary joint laws on X_1,…,X_n with every one-coordinate marginal X_t~μ. Arbitrary correlations, including dependence on the past state, are allowed. No independence, fixed stochastic kernel, blind-control, or exact accepting-support condition is assumed.

HORIZON-UNIFORM FINITE-POPULATION THEOREM (source-reported)
For integer P≥1, let H=binom(P+d−1,d−1), a_P=d/(2P), and b_P=d/(Pα+d). Vertices are state histograms x∈N^Q of total P. An allocation z∈N^{Q×A} gives an edge x→y when x_s=Σ_a z_{sa}, y_u=Σ_{s,a:f(s,a)=u}z_{sa}, and |Σ_s z_{sa}−Pμ(a)|≤d for each letter. Let G_{P,n} be the terminal histograms divided by P reachable in exactly n graph steps. Then, for every n,

    sup_{p∈R_n} dist_TV(p,G_{P,n}) ≤ d/(2P),
    sup_{g∈G_{P,n}} dist_TV(g,R_n) ≤ d/(Pα+d).

The same constants are independent of n. Also d_H(cl(⋃_{n≥1}R_n), ⋃_{1≤n≤H}R_n)≤a_P+b_P. The argument applies to every finite semigroup, including noncommutative ones, by adjoining a formal initial identity and using f(s,a)=sa; no mixing or semigroup-structure premise is needed.

PROOF MECHANISM
Scale any exact-marginal joint path law to an n-layer labelled flow of total P, add terminal-to-sink flow, and round the whole integral-balanced flow edgewise within its floor/ceiling intervals. The rounded complete paths have terminal TV error at most d/(2P); the error does not accumulate over time. Conversely, any relaxed population path has per-position letter frequencies μ'_t. A filler law with marginals q_t=[μ−(1−b_P)μ'_t]/b_P is nonnegative, and mixing this one global filler branch with the population path in weights 1−b_P,b_P restores every input marginal to μ exactly. The terminal error is at most b_P.

COMPUTATIONAL AND SAMPLING CONSEQUENCES
- For supplied rational target ν, exact rational graph distances give an interval for min_{p∈R_n} dist_TV(ν,p); P=ceil(2d/(αε)) gives a reported ε-accurate approximation. The arbitrary-positive-horizon optimum is a computable real, with the finite graph cutoff H.
- A fixed positive-gap promise (v_n≤a versus v_n≥a+γ) is in PSPACE for binary-supplied n and for arbitrary positive n. This is a membership result only: no PSPACE-hardness, exact cutpoint decision, or exact finite-horizon attainment algorithm is claimed.
- After graph acquisition, binary-horizon reachability/optimization uses O(H^3 log(n+1)) Boolean operations. The number of histogram vertices is H; candidate allocations number B_P=binom(P+dr−1,dr−1), with acquisition cost B_P·poly(d,r,b,log P) for rational input bit length b.
- A witness DAG gives a succinct exact-marginal joint law, exact terminal evaluation, and consistent adaptive coordinate queries in O(log(n+1)) lookups after compilation. Its reported atom bound is P+(r−1)E+1, E≤H². Printing all n letters still costs Ω(n); exact integer rejection sampling has expected finite random-bit cost but no bounded worst-case number of flips.
- For a stationary rational k-block law κ on A^k whose (k−1)-prefix and suffix marginals agree, an augmented-history construction preserves every overlapping k-window exactly and excludes zero-probability local patterns. Its filler uses F=dM² slots for denominator M, so costs can be exponential in the binary length of M. This extension has proof text but is not implemented by the one-coordinate diagnostic solver; it does not impose a conditional Markov kernel.
- For a fixed DFA, a uniformly positive-gap promise makes the yes-horizon language ultimately periodic. For a finite catalogue of control symbols, the corresponding uniformly gapped control-word language is regular. These are source-reported consequences under their stated promises; exact cutpoint predicates may remain nonperiodic.

Complexity is not jointly polynomial in automaton dimension and log(1/ε); small rational α, large dimensions, accuracy, and block-statistic denominators can make compilation expensive. Random-access sampler tables are not claimed to fit in PSPACE. The theorem optimizes over unrestricted correlations with exact unconditional marginals; it does not solve general stochastic-MDP reachability, blind-control, fixed-kernel, or exact accepting-support problems. It does not settle the exact cutpoint/Skolem interface retained in related semigroup work.
