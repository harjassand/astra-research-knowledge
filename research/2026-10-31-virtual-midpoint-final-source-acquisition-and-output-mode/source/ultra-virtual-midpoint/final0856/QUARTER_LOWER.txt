A QUANTITATIVE QUARTER-STEP POPULATION CERTIFICATE
Solo derivation, 31 October 2026. This is NOT a finite-data mean-rate bound.

The strict next-layer argument can be made quantitative. Put
g(r)=phi(sqrt r). For the law P_r in DIVISIBILITY.txt,

       V_4(P_r)>=max{2g(r), (8/5)[r+g(r)]}.               (Q1)

The second expression strictly improves V_2=2g(r) whenever g(r)<4r,
including the frozen CTMC source. It also holds using ONLY the exact
negative lag-four correlation-r of the fine binary observable; the rest
of the complete coarse law is not needed for this particular inequality.

Proof. Let R be a fine stationary Markov kernel, K=R^2, and suppose
<f,K^2f>=-r for f in{-1,+1}. Assume e(R)<infinity; otherwise trivial.
Set u=Kf,v=K*f,t=(u-v)/2,a=(u+v)/2, h=t f. Write eta for the symmetrized
K edge measure, z for its imbalance, and

 A=E_pi t^2=r+E_pi a^2>=r,
 D=E_eta z^2-A=E_eta[z(x,y)f(y)-t(x)]^2>=0,
 delta=e(K)-g(r)>=0.

Since g'(s)>=2 and e(K)>=g(E_eta z^2),

                  delta>=2(E_eta z^2-r)>=2D.              (Q2)

Let epsilon_x=z(x,y)f(y)-t(x),
epsilon_y=-z(x,y)f(x)-t(y). Antisymmetry gives
h(x)+h(y)=-epsilon_x f(x)-epsilon_y f(y). Each residual has mean square D,
so E_eta[h(x)+h(y)]^2<=4D. Stationarity and f^2=1 imply

             <h,Kh><=2D-A<=delta-r.                      (Q3)

For ANY real bounded observable |h|<=1 and stationary R,

                   e(R)>=-2<h,R^2h>.                     (Q4)

Indeed the symmetrized R flow gives e(R)>=2E z_R^2 and, by conditional
Jensen, E[(Rh-R*h)/2]^2<=E z_R^2 because |h|<=1. Adjointness gives
<h,R^2h>=E[((Rh+R*h)/2)^2-((Rh-R*h)/2)^2]. This proves(Q4), without
assuming h is binary or centered.

Apply(Q4) to h=t f, use(Q3), and then data processing e(K)<=2e(R):

 e(R)>=2r-2delta
      =2r-2e(K)+2g(r)
      >=2r-4e(R)+2g(r).

Hence5e(R)>=2[r+g(r)]. Multiplying by4 and taking the unrestricted
fine-root infimum gives(Q1). The argument remains valid when a right
side at an intermediate line is negative; no positive-part substitution
or extra case assumption is required.
