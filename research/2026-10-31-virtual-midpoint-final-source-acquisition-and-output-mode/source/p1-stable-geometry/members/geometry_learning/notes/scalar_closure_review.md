# Audit of task-sensitive quadratic leakage and finite whitening

31 October 2026. Analytic review only. No new data experiments or scouting.

## Verdict

The quadratic scalar-leakage claim is correct for actual f,g in the acquired subspace, a common contraction norm, and controlled leakage in both directions. Self-adjoint primitives supply the second leakage bound automatically. The proposed rational-whitener construction also has valid constants at t<=1/2.

The gain is linear dependence on the squared-defect estimation error. It is not a floor-free direct identification theorem: the scalar confidence radius is multiplied by the squared norm of the acquired whitener. That conditioning cost remains real and paid.

## 1. Projection error is second order for contained scalar tasks

Let P be the orthogonal projector onto an actual acquired span containing f,g, with ||f||,||g||<=1. Let every true source operation T_a be a contraction and assume

    ||(I-P) T_a P|| <= e_out,a,
    ||P T_a (I-P)|| <= e_in,a.

For self-adjoint T_a the two norms coincide. For an executed word a_1,...,a_H, write z_t=T_(a_t)...T_(a_1)f, p_t=Pz_t, q_t=(I-P)z_t. Then

    ||q_t|| <= ||q_(t-1)|| + e_out,a_t ||p_(t-1)||
             <= sum_(i<=t) e_out,a_i,

because the true full trajectory remains in the unit ball. Compare p_t with the trajectory generated by the compressed operations PT_aP. Its discrepancy obeys

    ||d_t|| <= ||d_(t-1)|| + e_in,a_t ||q_(t-1)||.

Therefore every contained scalar task has error at most

    sum_(i<j) e_out,a_i e_in,a_j.

With a common bound e this is binom(H,2)e^2. The bound holds simultaneously for every finite word once the per-operation leakage bounds hold. Data-dependent word selection therefore adds no new confidence allocation.

The assumptions matter. For T=[[0,1],[epsilon,0]] and P projecting onto the first coordinate, T is contractive and forward leakage is epsilon, but backward leakage is one. At length two the scalar error is epsilon, not epsilon^2. Both directions, or self-adjoint primitives, are necessary.

Containment of both task endpoints in the actual span is also necessary for this clean result. Approximate containment adds separate first-order endpoint errors.

## 2. Quadratic horizon dependence can be attained

The horizon factor is not merely a proof artifact in the general reversible-contraction class. Let c=cos(theta), s=sin(theta), P=span(e_1), and alternate

    T_+ = [[c,s],[s,-c]],
    T_- = [[c,-s],[-s,-c]].

Both are self-adjoint orthogonal contractions, each with leakage |s|. For even H, the true scalar return is cos(H theta), whereas the compressed return is c^H. Their difference has magnitude

    binom(H,2) theta^2 + O(H^4 theta^4)

as theta tends to zero with H theta small. Thus the general binom(H,2)e^2 dependence is sharp to leading order. These reflections can be realized by reversible deterministic reflection kernels on a circle with a common uniform stationary measure; their sine/cosine sector is two-dimensional. This is an analytic comparison, not a new source experiment.

## 3. Actual moment matrices and their confidence event

Let V=[v_1,...,v_r] be actual word features, including f=v_i0. For each self-adjoint primitive define

    G = V*V,
    K_a = V*T_a V,
    D_a = V*T_a^2 V = (T_a V)*(T_a V).

All three matrices have honest scalar-return experiments. G uses pairs of landmark words; K_a uses a landmark and a successor; D_a uses two successor words. No physical synthetic vector is required.

Suppose a joint acquired event gives operator errors <=eta for Ghat,Khat_a,Dhat_a. Symmetrizing Khat does not enlarge its operator error. If the acquisition only has entrywise confidence radius tau, eta=r tau is sufficient. More efficient matrix concentration can replace this conservative conversion.

A basis floor is not supplied. It can be certified by the same data. If it is very small, resolving the scalar task can still require high precision and many physical episodes. Every stage used to find independent landmarks, refine confidence, or produce a usable whitener must remain in the bill.

## 4. Rational-whitener gauge proposed by root

Let R be a computed invertible rational r by r matrix. Define

    B0 = R* Ghat R,
    nu >= ||B0-I||,
    z = eta ||R||^2,
    t = nu+z <= 1/2.

All displayed computable norms may be certified upper bounds. Let

    E = R* G R,
    U = V R,
    Q = U E^(-1/2).

Then ||E-I||<=t, so E is positive definite. Consequently Q is an isometry onto the actual span P=range(V). This condition also certifies actual independence of the r raw landmarks; it does not assume that independence for free. No numerical implementation of the unknown E or Q is required.

For each operation set

    Atilde_a = R* Khat_a R,
    Astar_a = Q* T_a Q.

Since U=Q E^(1/2),

    R* K_a R = E^(1/2) Astar_a E^(1/2).

The data perturbation is at most z. For t<=1/2,

    ||E^(1/2) Astar_a E^(1/2)-Astar_a|| <= 2t.

Indeed, ||E^(1/2)-I||<=t/(1+sqrt(1-t)), and multiplication by ||E^(1/2)||+1 gives a factor below 2t throughout this interval. Therefore

    ||Atilde_a-Astar_a|| <= z+2t <= 3t.

If the stored matrix A_a is certified contractive and a measured certificate gives d_a>=||A_a-Atilde_a||, then

    ||A_a-Astar_a|| <= 3t+d_a.

This is a linear noise/whitening bound, with no eigenvector-gap dependence. The unknown Q is merely the fixed proof gauge in which all operations are compared.

## 5. Seed and readout error

For f=V e_i0, define the computed rational vector

    b0 = R^(-1) e_i0.

Its exact physical coordinates in the Q basis are

    bstar = Q*f = E^(1/2)b0.

Because ||bstar||=||f||<=1,

    ||b0-bstar|| <= ||E^(-1/2)-I|| <= t,   t<=1/2.

The last scalar inequality follows from 1/sqrt(1-t)-1<=t on this interval.

Let the stored seed/readout vector b have a certified norm <=1 and a measured d_b>=||b-b0||. Then its coordinate discrepancy is at most t+d_b. Comparing two unit endpoint vectors contributes at most 2(t+d_b), and telescoping the stored contractions contributes H max_a(3t+d_a).

Combining with Section 1 proves the root's proposed bound:

    scalar error <= binom(H,2)e^2
                    + H max_a(3t+d_a)
                    + 2(t+d_b),

before numerical execution error. The bound requires f as the same endpoint readout and seed. Two different contained raw readouts have analogous separate endpoint terms.

## 6. Leakage certificate from an empirical residual quadratic

For each primitive form the entirely acquired rational matrices

    C_a = B0^(-1) Atilde_a,
    Rhat_a = R* Dhat_a R - Atilde_a C_a.

B0 is positive definite because nu<=t<1. Atilde is symmetric, so Rhat is symmetric. For an arbitrary coefficient vector c, the true residual from approximating T_a Uc by U C_a c is

    ||T_a Uc-U C_a c||^2.

Replacing its true G,K,D entries by their estimates gives exactly c*Rhat_a c, because C_a solves the empirical normal equations. The discrepancy of this quadratic is at most

    z (1+||C_a||)^2 ||c||^2.

Orthogonal projection onto P can only improve this residual. A unit physical input Qy has U-coordinates c=E^(-1/2)y, with ||c||^2<=1/(1-t). Therefore

    ||(I-P)T_a P||^2
      <= [lambda_max(Rhat_a)+z(1+||C_a||)^2]/(1-t).

A practical certificate can replace lambda_max by a certified upper bound and then by its nonnegative part. This avoids treating a confidence-failure-path negative radicand as meaningful. The formula is valid without a supplied exact closure promise.

Self-adjointness gives the reverse leakage bound required by the quadratic scalar theorem. Without it, D_a=V*T_a*T_aV measures only forward leakage; one must separately acquire and certify the adjoint direction.

## 7. Small empirical defects have a proved yield under exact closure

If the actual P is invariant under T_a, let Ctrue be the true coefficient operator in the U basis. Then

    ||Ctrue|| <= sqrt((1+t)/(1-t)).

The empirical residual quadratic is minimized by C_a, so its Schur complement Rhat_a is no larger in Loewner order than that quadratic evaluated at Ctrue. The true residual at Ctrue is zero. The same confidence perturbation bound therefore gives

    lambda_max(Rhat_a)
       <= z [1+sqrt((1+t)/(1-t))]^2
       <= (1+sqrt(3))^2 z,       t<=1/2.

Thus a measured O(z) defect under exact closure follows from the same confidence event. It is not a separate yield assumption. Approximate closure gives a corresponding physical residual term plus the estimation term.

At t<=1/2 the coarse estimates ||C_a||<=4 and 1/(1-t)<=2 imply that the resulting leakage certificate under exact closure is less than 65z. Better measured norms improve this considerably.

If numerical whitening precision is chosen so nu is comparable to z, stored contraction/seed modifications are O(t), and the observed defect is O(z), the entire finite-horizon scalar error is O(H^2 z). Since z=eta||R||^2 and eta can be r tau, the improvement is linear in tau with an explicit acquired conditioning factor. It does not eliminate that factor.

## 8. Acquisition and precision scope

For a fixed acquired frame and common report range, entrywise tolerance tau requires order tau^(-2) episodes per distinct scalar job, up to confidence logarithms. With O(mr^2) G/K/D jobs and a fixed acquired conditioning scale, a task target tau of order epsilon/(H^2 r ||R||^2) produces epsilon^(-2), rather than epsilon^(-4), statistical scaling. The dimensions, conditioning, return-word lengths, preparations, failed stages, and arithmetic costs remain explicit.

This is a local finite-accuracy acquisition conclusion. It does not supply a polynomial uniform bound in hidden rank alone. Extremely weak independent directions can still be expensive to establish, and unknown-rank discovery needs a finite-stage policy with a complete confidence ledger.

R, its inverse used for b0, and the empirical inverse B0^(-1) need finite rational arithmetic. B0 has eigenvalues bounded away from zero by 1-nu, but constructing a successful R may require resolving an arbitrarily small raw Gram eigenvalue. The observed t and z account for the consequence of that conditioning; their acquisition cost cannot be omitted.

The source-side scalar confidence event and all deterministic matrix certificates can cover every later word simultaneously. A new horizon, tighter epsilon, new readout outside P, new physical operation, or nonstationary source requires a new justification or new acquisition.
