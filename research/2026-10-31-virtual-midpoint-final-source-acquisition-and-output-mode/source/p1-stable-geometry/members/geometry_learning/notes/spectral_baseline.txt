SPECTRAL/CONTROL COMPARATOR AND AUDIT
31 October 2026. Child investigation: producer-workspace/spectral_baseline.

Scope. This note audits the root's construction of economical Euclidean
coordinates after the already-acquired l1-stable raw-word model. It supplies
an elementary operator-norm sampling bound, a shorter propagation proof,
readout acquisition, an actual convex optimizer, finite-precision final
certificates, and primary-source overlap. It does not claim that initial
dictionary acquisition is polynomial in latent dimension.

Main finding
============

An acquired l1-stable model can be compressed into a Euclidean-contractive
model without estimating a minimum positive Gram eigenvalue, inverting a
Gram matrix, preparing normalized linear combinations, or making additional
shifted-moment queries. The compressed model has dimension at most the true
rank of the retained raw-word vectors. Its finite-horizon error is linear in
the horizon. The construction needs a sufficiently accurate Gram matrix and
a finite convex fit whose computation and numerical certification are paid.

This is a useful representation result. It does not prove polynomial initial
acquisition in rank, an advantage over an ordinary learner retaining the same
observations, or an efficient planner for arbitrary future reward functions.

1. Exact statement and the subtle isometry extension
===================================================

Let T_a be contractions on a real Hilbert space H. Let V=[v_1,...,v_N] consist
of actual acquired raw-word features with ||v_i||<=1. Assume the preceding
acquisition stage has established, simultaneously for every generator a,

  ||M_a||_{1->1} <= 1,
  max_i ||T_a v_i - V M_a e_i|| <= rho.                         (1)

The old acquisition and all its failed searches remain charged. For a word w
of length h, c_w=M_w e_i obeys ||c_w||1<=1 and

  ||T_w v_i - V c_w|| <= h rho.                               (2)

Let G=V*V. Acquire a symmetric estimate Ghat satisfying

  ||Ghat-G||op <= eta.                                        (3)

Retain eigenvalues of Ghat strictly above 2 eta, and replace every other
eigenvalue by zero. Call the resulting PSD matrix B. Factor B=X*X with
X in R^{k x N}. Then

  k <= rank(G),
  ||G-B||op <= 3 eta.                                        (4)

Proof. A PSD rank-r G has lambda_{r+1}(G)=0, so every estimated eigenvalue
beyond r is at most eta. Negative estimated eigenvalues have magnitude at
most eta. The discarded positive eigenvalues have magnitude at most 2 eta.
Thus ||Ghat-B||op<=2 eta and the triangle inequality proves (4). No gap
between two positive eigenvalues, and no positive Gram floor, is required.

Set alpha=sqrt(3 eta). There is an isometry Q:R^k -> H' into an enlarged
Hilbert space H' such that

  max_i ||v_i-Q x_i|| <= alpha,                               (5)

where x_i=X e_i. This Q is a proof witness, not supplied to the learner.

Detail that should not be omitted: polar decomposition gives V=U G^{1/2}
with a partial isometry U. If dim(H)<N, U cannot be extended to an isometry
R^N -> H. Instead embed H into H'=H direct-sum R^N, extend every T_a by zero
on the extra summand, and extend U to an isometry J:R^N -> H'. If
B=W Lambda W* and X=Lambda^{1/2}W*, use Q=J W. Then QX=J B^{1/2}, so

  ||V-QX||op = ||G^{1/2}-B^{1/2}||op <= ||G-B||op^{1/2}.

The square-root inequality follows from monotonicity of the positive square
root: if ||G-B||<=d, then G<=B+dI, whence sqrt(G)<=sqrt(B)+sqrt(d)I;
reverse the roles for the opposite inequality. This proves (5).

The inaccessible compressed true operator A_a^*=Q* T_a Q is a contraction.
It is a feasible comparator for the implementable convex problem

  minimize over ||A||op<=1:
     F_a(A) = max_i ||A x_i - X M_a e_i||2.                    (6)

Indeed insert and subtract v_i, T_a v_i, and VM_a e_i. The three errors are
at most alpha, rho, and alpha, respectively; the last uses ||M_a e_i||1<=1.
Therefore

  min F_a <= rho+2 alpha.                                    (7)

Choose an actual computed contraction A_a with attained residual beta_a,
and beta=max_a beta_a. A completed residual certificate is sufficient;
optimization success need not be assumed. A finite optimizer below attains
beta<=rho+2 alpha+zeta in exact arithmetic, and numerical slack is explicit.

2. Removing the spurious H^2 loss
================================

Let y_w=A_w x_i. Compare y_w to X c_w, not to separately projected true
prefix states. By (6), ||A_a Xc-XM_a c||<=beta ||c||1. Since old reference
coefficients have l1 norm at most one and the new A_a contract,

  ||y_w-X c_w|| <= h beta.                                   (8)

This is the whole mechanism: l1 stability controls mixtures in the training
reference; acquired Euclidean stability controls propagation in deployment.
The previous H^2 derivation was an avoidable telescoping loss from repeatedly
projecting true states with already accumulated error.

A physical-state comparison is

  ||T_w v_i-Q y_w|| <= h rho + alpha + h beta.                 (9)

For a dictionary readout v_j, a sharper scalar bound avoids an extra linear
feature error. Since B=X*X and ||c_w||1<=1,

 |<v_j,T_w v_i> - x_j* A_w x_i|
 <= h rho + |e_j*(G-B)c_w| + ||x_j|| ||Xc_w-y_w||
 <= h rho + alpha^2 + (1+alpha) h beta.                     (10)

In particular, no positive eigenvalue of G appears in the bound. Very weak
modes can be removed when their contribution lies below the requested
horizon/accuracy resolution. This does not mean weak modes can always be
ignored: the horizon controls alpha and rho. Rank-two slow rotations retain
the earlier precision/horizon warning.

For epsilon<=1 and H>=1, the deliberately loose choice

  rho=zeta=epsilon/(16H),
  alpha=epsilon/[32(H+1)], eta=alpha^2/3

leaves substantial room for observation rounding, matrix factorization,
optimization, and deployment arithmetic: (7) gives beta<=3epsilon/(16H),
and (10) is less than epsilon/3 before those additions. A convenient
implementation can instead demand the attained beta certificate be at most
epsilon/(4H); that still leaves a large total error budget.

3. Fresh Gram sampling: N^3, not N^4, conservative shot scaling
=============================================================

Condition on the completed acquisition history and its chosen dictionary.
For each unordered pair (i,j), take n fresh reports in [-R,R], each with
conditional expectation G_ij given the entire previous transcript. Set
Ghat_ij=Ghat_ji to their average. Independence is not needed for the following
martingale-Hoeffding proof. Fresh sampling is useful because the selected
dictionary is now fixed; alternatively, a genuinely simultaneous adaptive
confidence construction may be reused.

For any fixed unit x in R^N,

  x*(Ghat-G)x = sum_{i<=j} a_ij (Ghat_ij-G_ij),
  a_ii=x_i^2, a_ij=2 x_i x_j for i<j,
  sum_{i<=j} a_ij^2 = 2 - sum_i x_i^4 <= 2.

Conditional Hoeffding gives

  P(|x*(Ghat-G)x|>t | acquisition history)
       <= 2 exp[-n t^2/(4R^2)].                              (11)

Take a 1/4-net S of the Euclidean unit sphere with |S|<=9^N. For any
symmetric E, ||E||op<=2 max_{x in S}|x*Ex|. This follows by choosing a
unit eigenvector achieving ||E||, then replacing it by a net point and
bounding the two bilinear differences by 2(1/4)||E||.

Apply (11) with t=eta/2 and union bound. It is sufficient that

  n >= 16 R^2 eta^{-2} [N log 9 + log(2/delta_G)]             (12)

to obtain (3) with conditional probability at least 1-delta_G.
The net is only used in the proof; it is not enumerated by the learner.

There are J=N(N+1)/2 jobs. Total fresh reports are

  J ceil(16 R^2 eta^{-2}[N log9+log(2/delta_G)])
    = O(R^2 N^2 (N+log(1/delta_G)) eta^{-2}).                 (13)

At eta=Theta(epsilon^2/H^2), this is, up to logarithmic terms,
O(R^2 N^3 H^4 epsilon^{-4}). The entrywise-confidence route eta=N tau
would instead give O(R^2 N^4 H^4 epsilon^{-4}); it is valid but avoidable.

Weighted returns. If job ij has a known valid bound |Y_ij|<=R_ij, take

  n_ij=ceil(16 R_ij^2 eta^{-2}[N log9+log(2/delta_G)]).

The same proof bounds sum a_ij^2 R_ij^2/n_ij, and obtains (3). The complete
new physical charge is the sum over pairs of n_ij times that job's actual
preparation, return-word execution, readout, and sampling cost. If raw words
have maximum length L and adjoints are actual primitives, the return word
has length at most 2L, in addition to preparation and measurement.

This statistical generalization does NOT manufacture a common contraction
geometry for weighted interfaces. Common stationarity and bounded endpoint
reports supply a particularly favorable unknown L2(pi) geometry. A general
weighted adjoint-return source must separately justify one common norm in
which the relevant operators contract. Its R_ij may grow exponentially in
depth; that cost remains in the sum above. Importance weights are not free.

4. The convex fit is an algorithm, not an efficient-oracle premise
================================================================

Write Y_a=XM_a and Bx=max_i||x_i||<=1+alpha. Over the spectral-norm unit ball,
F(A)=max_i||A x_i-y_i|| is convex and Bx-Lipschitz in Frobenius norm.
For a maximizing residual column j of positive norm, use the subgradient

  Z = [(A x_j-y_j)/||A x_j-y_j||] x_j*.

Its Frobenius norm is at most Bx. The Frobenius projection onto the spectral
unit ball is computed by an SVD and replacing each singular value s by
min(s,1). Start A_0=0. For T iterations use step size

  s=sqrt(k)/(Bx sqrt(T)),
  A_{t+1}=Proj(A_t-s Z_t).

Every feasible comparator has Frobenius norm at most sqrt(k), and the usual
squared-distance telescoping gives

  min_{t<T} F(A_t)-min F <= sqrt(k) Bx / sqrt(T).              (14)

Thus T=ceil(k Bx^2/zeta^2) suffices. Taking the best attained iterate is
valid; averaging feasible iterates is another option. Per iteration costs
O(N k^2+k^3) arithmetic operations for residuals and the SVD. Forming each
Y_a costs O(k nnz(M_a)), not automatically O(kN^2) when old FW columns are
sparse. Gram eigendecomposition is O(N^3) arithmetic in the conventional
dense algorithm. These are explicit finite computations. None is a planner.

The adjacent spectral_baseline.py implements this projected subgradient
algorithm. An early exit based on an attained residual is explicitly marked
as such; the theoretical iteration budget is also returned. The optimizer is
ordinary floating point. It does not claim exact-real optimization.

5. Finite precision and actual final certificates
================================================

Several distinct errors must not be hidden inside the word 'spectral':

  (a) Physical report rounding adds bias or deterministic mean error to Ghat.
      Budget it before (3), e.g. max-entry rounding b costs at most N b in
      operator norm. Bounded-report sampling controls stochastic error only.

  (b) Computing X. A finite symmetric eigensolver need not decide an exact
      equality at the threshold. Approximate eigenpairs to an absolute
      tolerance nu<<eta; include eigenvalues certified above a threshold
      in, say, [1.5eta,2.5eta], and permit arbitrary decisions inside a narrow
      certified threshold interval. All retained values stay above eta,
      so k<=rank(G); all discarded values stay O(eta), so (4) changes only
      by a numerical constant. Bounds on reconstruction and orthogonality
      residuals certify the operator error. No gap between positive target
      eigenvalues is needed. A rounded factor Xtilde with
      ||Xtilde-X||op<=nu_X changes (5) to alpha+nu_X.

  (c) Targets. Old M matrices may be exact rationals with known l1 column
      bounds. Multiplication X M must be evaluated to a known per-column
      error. Rounding M itself requires its own l1 column perturbation
      allowance; do not silently turn a floating matrix into an exact old
      acquisition certificate.

  (d) Optimization. Approximate SVD projections and objective comparisons
      can be done to error small enough for a preassigned additional zeta.
      A coarse sufficient prescription makes errors of order zeta/T per
      iteration; refined standard inexact-subgradient bounds can do better.
      The required number of bits depends logarithmically on T,N,1/zeta,
      and the finite input magnitudes, not on lambda_min^+(G). If an attained
      residual certificate already passes, a convergence proof is not needed
      for soundness; failed iterations remain charged.

  (e) Final stability. Unchecked floating singular values slightly below one
      are not an exact contraction certificate. The adjacent program uses a
      numerical SVD only as a proposal, then checks it with exact rational
      arithmetic on every binary input number. For a proposed U,S,V*, compute
      exact rational reconstruction residual and orthogonality residuals.
      Rational upper square roots give

       ||A|| <= sqrt(1+||U*U-I||F) max(S)
                sqrt(1+||V*V-I||F) + ||A-U S V*||F.

      This bound is valid even if the SVD proposal is inaccurate. The program
      scales inward, rounds to a dyadic grid, and proves both ||A_safe||<=1
      and ||A_safe-A||F<=the prescribed numerical budget using exact rational
      inequalities. It also evaluates each final column residual in exact
      rationals and returns an upper square-root bound. These certificates
      cover supplied X and target arrays; they do not certify source data or
      replace the factorization-error allowance in (a)-(c).

      The code also includes two independent geometry certificates. First,
      certified_gram_factor_error forms Ghat-X*X in exact rationals (including
      matrix-product rounding) and bounds its operator norm from a checked
      SVD proposal. Thus alpha^2 may be chosen as eta plus this attained
      factor error. Second, for retained eigenvector proposals W, exact
      positive definiteness of W*(Ghat-eta I)W proves rank(G)>=number of
      columns of W on the source confidence event. An exact-rational LDL'
      check implements this test. Numerical eigenvector orthogonality is
      not a premise. These certificates still condition on the legitimate
      source error bound ||G-Ghat||<=eta.

  (f) Deployment. For fixed stored contractions, if each matrix-vector
      multiplication incurs Euclidean error at most nu_step, total state
      error after H steps is at most H nu_step plus seed rounding. A final
      dot-product error is separately charged. Thus O(log(H/epsilon))
      additional precision, with the dimension-dependent summation factor,
      suffices. Storage/compute claims need these bits as well as arithmetic
      operation counts.

The helper was executed successfully on a 29-column, rank-two rotation fit.
It stopped after 102 iterations with a certified maximum column residual
0.001119381704. Its final certified norm upper bound was 0.999999768610.
The rounding/shrink perturbation had Frobenius bound 3.365412122e-7. Twelve
additional random matrices of dimensions 1,2,4,7 passed exact-rational final
contraction checks. The factor-error certificate was 1.016788641e-14 and its
rank-two probe certificate passed. These are algorithm/certificate
diagnostics, not a noisy physical-source experiment. The root's source
fixtures are separate.

6. Acquiring a genuinely new readout without a Gram inverse
=========================================================

Suppose a new task supplies an actual permitted readout g with ||g||<=1 and
a scalar physical interface for b_i=<g,v_i>. Charge N distinct jobs and all
their preparation/depth/report-range costs. Acquire bhat_i with simultaneous
|bhat_i-b_i|<=tau_g. Fit

  minimize over ||h||2<=1: max_i |h*x_i-bhat_i|.               (15)

The inaccessible h*=Q*g is feasible, and by (5) its objective is at most
alpha+tau_g. An additive-zeta_g optimizer therefore yields a true per-atom
readout error chi<=alpha+2tau_g+zeta_g. As before, only the attained residual
and concentration certificate are needed for soundness. This optimization
also has a direct projected-subgradient method: subgradient +/-x_j and
projection onto the Euclidean ball. T_g=ceil(Bx^2/zeta_g^2) suffices from zero.

For every later word of length h<=H from an old seed,

  |<g,T_w v_i>-h* A_w x_i| <= h(rho+beta)+chi.                (16)

There is no minimum Gram eigenvalue. Readout data acquisition, optimization,
failed attempts, and numeric precision are paid. A new arbitrary seed is
different: it can require expanding the physical dictionary and reacquiring
closure, so this cheap readout theorem must not be advertised as free new
initial-condition access.

7. What remains expensive; ordinary retained learners
=====================================================

The compression theorem consumes an actually acquired model (1). The old
compiler may require N exponential in rank at the requested rho, with bound
N roughly (1+O(1/rho))^r on its favorable confidence event. The new Gram
stage costs roughly N^3 H^4/epsilon^4 reports in the bounded case, before
physical word lengths. This is a rank-sized deployment theorem with fully
charged potentially expensive preparation, not a rank-polynomial acquisition
theorem. A large N is not erased by the fact that only k<=r coordinates
remain in a deployed predictor.

An ordinary spectral learner retaining the same acquired M and observations
can run exactly this algorithm. It is the strongest constructive comparator
here, not an algorithm to rule out. Old exact rank-two closure and signed
orthonormal-frame wins remain intact. The previous polygon obstruction also
remains intact: it forbids finite exact l1-contractive raw lifts for irrational
rotation, but does not forbid Euclidean contraction models.

Future finer accuracy or a longer horizon can require smaller rho/alpha,
additional raw words, more source samples, and more retained dimensions.
Fixed rank alone does not make those marginal costs zero. Retaining the
training dictionary/moments for further expansion has a different memory
bill from shipping only the compact deployment matrices.

8. Primary research overlap and precise qualifications
======================================================

The mathematical construction above was derived in this investigation. Its
novelty in the literature is not established. The following works preclude
broad claims that shared operators, regularized operator regression,
stability constraints, task-sensitive exploration, or train-then-distill
pipelines are themselves new.

A. Balle, Quattoni, Carreras (ICML 2012), 'Local Loss Optimization in Operator
   Models: A New Insight into Spectral Learning'.
   Primary PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.6393
   Retrieved references: turn46view0, turn48view2, turn48view3.
   Their local-loss and convex-relaxation view already learns finite operator
   models from Hankel blocks. Section 4 also acquires a basis rather than
   always supplying it: randomly split iid strings from a full-support
   distribution, then collect prefixes/suffixes. Theorem 5 assumes a finite
   rank 'strongly bounded' realization (operator norms <=1). Crucially its
   instance parameter depends on both target and sampling distribution.
   Appendix A exposes factors kappa(C_p)/sigma_min(C_p) and the analogous
   suffix covariance factor. Full support guarantees positivity, not a
   polynomial lower bound. Thus it is a real constructive comparator, but
   not a dimension-only cheap-discovery theorem. Our cutoff/approximation
   route deliberately avoids resolving every weak normalized direction.

B. Khosravi (IEEE TAC 2023), 'Representer Theorem for Learning Koopman
   Operators'.
   Primary preprint: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01681
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2023.3242325
   Retrieved: turn51view0, turn52view3.
   It formulates constrained regularized empirical operator learning and
   gives finite-dimensional Gram-based reformulations, including operator
   norm, rank, nuclear norm, and stability constraints. The source consists
   of a state sequence and measurements of given observables; Assumption 1
   requires bounded evaluation functionals (e.g. in an RKHS). This is strong
   direct prior for the finite convex regression step. It does not construct
   all charged adjoint-return words and a useful raw-word dictionary under
   our scalar source contract. Do not claim contraction regression itself
   is the contribution.

C. Liu, Netrapalli, Szepesvari, Jin, OMLE (STOC 2023).
   Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2209.14997v3
   Retrieved: turn48view0; earlier detailed source note in
   cumulative_intelligence/literature.md.
   The well-conditioned PSR theory includes reward-free transfer, but uses
   shared known core tests and a conditioning assumption. General low rank
   alone has an exponential-horizon sample obstruction in that observation
   contract. That lower bound cannot be transplanted automatically to our
   stronger Gram-return contract, which can reveal norm information absent
   from ordinary trajectory observations. Optimization/planning is also
   not automatically computationally efficient.

D. Wagenmaker, Simchowitz, Jamieson (ICML 2021), 'Task-Optimal Exploration in
   Linear Dynamical Systems'.
   Primary: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wagenmaker21a/wagenmaker21a.pdf
   Retrieved: turn48view1.
   This is a real task-sensitive experiment-design algorithm, not an
   independent-task straw comparator. Its primary setting has observed
   linear states, stable dynamics, Gaussian process noise and an input-energy
   constraint, together with smooth decision-loss conditions. It does not
   supply latent raw-word geometry under the present scalar access contract.

E. Sun, Oymak, Fazel (L4DC 2020), 'Finite Sample System Identification:
   Improved Rates and the Role of Regularization'.
   Primary: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v120/sun20a/sun20a.pdf
   Retrieved: turn46view1, turn48view5.
   Nuclear-norm Hankel regression improves low-order input-output system
   identification. Theorem 1 uses multiple zero-initial-state rollouts and
   scaled iid Gaussian design rows. Its sample-complexity convention counts
   equations; for our comparison their full input rollouts would be paid.
   It learns an LTI input-output system, not arbitrary switched word products.

F. Hazan, Lee, Singh, Zhang, Zhang (NeurIPS 2018), 'Spectral Filtering for
   General Linear Dynamical Systems'.
   Primary: https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2018/file/d6288499d0083cc34e60a077b7c4b3e1-Paper.pdf
   Retrieved: turn51view2.
   Polynomial-time online prediction without full identification is already
