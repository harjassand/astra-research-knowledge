5. Hsu, Kakade, Zhang, A Spectral Algorithm for Learning Hidden Markov Models
   (COLT 2009).
   https://www.learningtheory.org/colt2009/papers/011.pdf

Read the observable-operator construction and Theorem 6 conditions. The
algorithm uses observable first-, second-, and third-order statistics, SVD,
and matrix products. Its rank assumptions and inverse singular values in the
sample bound are real conditions. Its general principle is adopted; the
four-state binary-observation fixture need not satisfy its literal full-rank
observation-matrix condition.

6. Balle, Quattoni, Carreras, Local Loss Optimization in Operator Models:
   A New Insight into Spectral Learning (ICML 2012).
   https://borjaballe.github.io/papers/icml12.pdf

Read the local operator-loss formulation, random-basis Theorem 5, and its
Appendix A.2 proof. The paper already connects spectral learning and local
convex optimization and constructs random bases. Its strongly bounded model
and full-support sampling assumptions do not remove covariance conditioning:
the proof's acquisition parameter depends on prefix/suffix covariance singular
values and the sampling distribution. A generic rank-only cheap-basis theorem
cannot be imported from the abstract. The present direct learner is explicitly
an ordinary spectral/contraction method, not a claimed new learning paradigm.

7. Balle and Mohri, Learning Weighted Automata.
   https://cs.nyu.edu/~mohri/pub/cai.pdf

Read the reconstruction discussion and Theorem 6 statistical conditions.
Complete Hankel bases and inverse smallest singular values are charged in the
stated spectral guarantee. Exact membership/equivalence-query learning is a
different information contract from bounded noisy raw returns.

8. Khosravi, Representer Theorem for Learning Koopman Operators (arXiv 2022).
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.01681

Read the data/observable setup, bounded evaluation assumption, Gram-based
finite optimization, and operator-norm/rank/stability-constrained forms.
Finite Gram reductions and stable constrained regression are established.
That setup supplies observable evaluations along state trajectories in a
specified function geometry. Our physical return experiments must acquire
the hidden conditional-feature geometry and pay for their precision and depth.
The difference is an access/certificate contract, not a novelty claim for
spectral clipping or convex operator fitting.

9. Kimmel, Low, Yoder, Robust Calibration of a Universal Single-Qubit Gate-Set
   via Robust Phase Estimation (PRA 2015; corrected arXiv v3, 2021).
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.02677

Read the cosine/sine experiment assumptions, increasing powers, and corrected
front matter. The original pi/2 phase-branch error condition was insufficient;
the correction points to pi/3 analyses. Our proof constructs its sine
quadrature from an acquired cosine anchor and uses a stricter pi/12 error
margin with a direct nearest-branch proof. Physical word length is paid.
This adopts ordinary robust phase estimation, not a new general phase method.
