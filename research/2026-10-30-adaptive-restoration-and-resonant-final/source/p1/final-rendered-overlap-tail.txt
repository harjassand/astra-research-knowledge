12. Strongest comparisons, retained limits, and scientific status
What established methods already supply

An ordinary resampling implementation supplied with the same partition can execute this construction. Conditional-resampling ideas, positive local expansions, privacy-blanket decompositions, and subcube partitions are established tools.

The existing separation between subcube-partition complexity and randomized query complexity is relevant mathematical context. It does not establish a same-resource speedup for this cache problem. A competing method must receive the same paid cache, retained summaries, source access, and structural knowledge.

The previous fixed-edit theorem remains intact.

The new wrapper permits sample-dependent model selection when the released decision has an available positive local decomposition. It explicitly pays for the part of the cache that selection invalidates.

What remains open

The main unresolved practical issue is obtaining useful decompositions for consequential scientific decisions at affordable cost.

The general LP can be exponential. A learned model or a long numerical estimate may disclose enough about its training cache that the certified refresh cost is large. An ordinary sampler may also implement the desired released decision more cheaply than maintaining a full reusable cache.

The recursive example closes a substantial mathematical contract, but it is a structural witness. It does not establish a useful new climate, physics, energy, or general-learning workload.

The strongest completed conclusion is therefore:

Adaptive reuse can be made exact by accounting for the decision’s dependence on the cache. Positive local decompositions characterize a broad executable class; an information balance constrains every restoration algorithm; and these two sides meet in an explicit global family with an exact sublinear optimum.

The next consequential frontier is whether scientifically useful model-selection or simulation decisions admit such restorations with a complete cost advantage over equally informed ordinary methods. The present construction makes that a precise question with executable upper bounds, lower-bound tools, and a known failure mode—not an inference from marginal correctness.
