## Exact finite arithmetic: checked
For s=sqrt(N), gamma=1/s, the unreduced integer formula gives p_i=(1-gamma)w_i/(w0+w1)+gamma/2 exactly. Consequently p_i>=1/(2s) and x=eta/[(3/4)p_i]=1/(3s p_i)<=2/3. The integer update multiplies the chosen weight by q_num and the other by 2^64, equivalent to multiplying their ratio by q=q_num/2^64. Removing common powers of two does not change probabilities. All weights remain strictly positive. Each loss update increases each weight's bit length by at most 64; a bound 4+64L after L updates is valid.

The argument's dyadic bracket, odd/even alternating Taylor sums, lower-bound downward rounding and relative-deficit upward rounding are valid. The upper endpoint is below one. Alternating remainder control is therefore applicable; q<=exp(-x) and q>=exp(-x)(1-rho), with rho<1/2, follow from the displayed rational inequalities. The fixed-grid rho ledger avoids denominator multiplication. Independent tests recomputed the relevant Taylor polynomials by powers/factorials at every actual loss update, plus boundary arguments 0 and 2/3 and near-grid endpoints.

Rounded factors admit a DIRECT potential proof: the upper exponential potential inequality is preserved because factors round downward. The comparator log weight loses at most -log(1-rho)<=2rho per relevant update. This changes the governing expected regret by 2 E(sum rho)/(eta N). It does not require pretending the rounded trajectory equals ideal exact-exponential EXP3.

## Finite private coins and trajectory coupling: checked
For denominator d, b=(d-1).bit_length() samples exactly b fair bits. Accepted integers are uniform on 0,...,d-1. Acceptance probability exceeds 1/2 unless d is a power of two, when it is one. The d=1 zero-bit case is also correct. A 128-attempt cap fails with probability at most 2^-128, conditioned on any history. The explicit baseline fallback is not an exact p_i draw on the cap event. Coupling to the uncapped ideal sampler up to the first failure gives probability at most N*2^-128 of an entire adaptive trajectory mismatch. Subsequent retained-memory steering is included: a union bound is not being applied merely to isolated final scores. A bounded score changes by at most that probability; a difference of two bounded scores safely pays twice it. If expressed as FULL channel diamond norm rather than score/half-diamond convention, the extra factor two must not be dropped.

The kernel's `private_random_bits` counter counts EXPERT selection only. The correction explicitly adds 2N role bits and separately identifies 2 bits per shadow Pauli choice and ideal log2(N) terminal bits for this power-of-two horizon. Actual NumPy machine-word use for the latter choices is not instrumented. PRNG execution is reproducibility evidence, not hardware fair-coin acquisition. Integer probabilities can require O(64N) bits per draw; expected total is O(64N²), with the cap giving a finite deterministic worst-case multiplier 128. No unit-cost-real or free-randomness claim is accepted.
