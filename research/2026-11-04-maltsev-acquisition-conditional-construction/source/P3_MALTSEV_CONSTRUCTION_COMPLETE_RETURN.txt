## 1. A completed lifting mechanism

The strongest completed construction is the following. Its hypotheses are substantive acquired information; I have not proved that the preceding acquisition procedures always produce them.

### Lifting theorem

Suppose we have acquired:

1. A nontrivial partition \(q:D\to Q\) of the **whole domain**.
2. An explicit Maltsev operation \(h_B:B^3\to B\) on every block \(B\).
3. Joint compatibility of these block operations with every relation fibre: for every original relation \(R\subseteq D^r\) and every block tuple,
   \[
   R\cap(B_1\times\cdots\times B_r)
   \]
   is preserved by applying \(h_{B_i}\) in coordinate \(i\).

Given a proposed quotient Maltsev operation \(\bar m:Q^3\to Q\), we can then **decide in uniform polynomial time whether it lifts to a Maltsev polymorphism of the original language, and construct a lift when it does**.

This does not invoke a solver for the original language.

### Construction

Restrict each established indicator variable to the required output block:
\[
X_{abc}\in q^{-1}\!\left(\bar m(q(a),q(b),q(c))\right).
\]

Every preservation constraint now has a table consisting of one fibre of its original relation, possibly intersected with repeated-variable equalities.

Construct the explicit auxiliary operation
\[
\mu(x,y,z)=
\begin{cases}
h_B(x,y,z),&x,y,z\in B\text{ for one block }B,\\
z,&\text{otherwise and }q(x)=q(y),\\
x,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The two Maltsev identities hold directly. More importantly, \(\mu\) preserves every residual table: three rows from a fixed fibre have their entries in the same block at each coordinate, so \(\mu\) acts there as the jointly verified \(h_B\). It also preserves the block domains, equalities, and singleton pins.

We can therefore run the supplied-operation solver on this **residual indicator**, using the operation \(\mu\) we have just constructed. A satisfying assignment is a complete operation table for the original language; unsatisfiability correctly says that the proposed quotient operation has no lift.

The distinction that makes this noncircular is precise:

> \(\mu\) need not preserve the original unsliced relations or induce \(\bar m\). It only needs to preserve the residual fibre tables, which we have verified.

If the quotient law was acquired in a way that proves some original witness induces it—for example, because every witness is forced to induce it—the residual indicator is guaranteed satisfiable.

### Cost and transfer

The acquired block tables contain
\[
\sum_B |B|^3\le d^3
\]
entries. Grouping each relation’s rows by their block labels does not increase the total row count. Joint fibre preservation is checked in at most
\[
O\!\left(
\sum_R \operatorname{ar}(R)
\sum_{F\text{ fibre of }R}|F|^3
\right)
\le
O\!\left(
\sum_R \operatorname{ar}(R)|R|^3
\right)
\]
tuple operations, with polynomial indexing and bit costs.

The residual indicator has \(d^3\) variables and \(\sum_R|R|^3\) preservation constraints. Even materializing all its tables costs only a fixed polynomial in the complete input description. Lagerkvist’s supplied-operation solver then applies; its stated running time is \(O(d^5n^2\|I\|)\), and it returns an assignment or a correct unsatisfiability decision. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

Once the resulting original operation is verified and stored, the same solver handles arbitrarily large later networks. **The unresolved cost is acquiring the hypotheses of this lifting theorem uniformly.**

## 2. What can actually be acquired from the tables

I constructed and implemented a procedure that extracts a forced algebraic law from certain dependencies in an explicit relation. It does not receive a group law, coordinates, or an operation oracle.

Start with a nonempty relation, restrict its coordinate domains to their projections, and greedily delete coordinates while its projection remains non-full. This produces a minimally non-full relation
\[
S\subseteq D_1\times\cdots\times D_k:
\]
\(S\) is not the full product, but every proper coordinate projection is full.

Fullness is tested by counting distinct projected rows and comparing with the product of domain sizes. The Cartesian product need not be enumerated.

For each coordinate define
\[
a\mathrel{\alpha_i}b
\iff
\exists u\;\bigl((a,u)\in S\ \land\ (b,u)\in S\bigr),
\]
where \(u\) specifies all other coordinates.

Under the Maltsev promise, rectangularity proves that:

- \(\alpha_i\) is a proper equivalence relation compatible with every possible witness.
- \(S\) is saturated under replacement within these blocks.
- In the quotient relation \(S'\), any assignment to \(k-1\) coordinates extends uniquely.
- All quotient domains \(Q_i=D_i/\alpha_i\) have the same cardinality.

For \(k=2\), this exposes a quotient bijection.

For \(k\ge3\), freeze all but three quotient coordinates. The remaining relation is an explicitly tabulated Latin operation
\[
q_0:Q_1\times Q_2\to Q_3.
\]

Choose \(a_0,b_0\), and set
\[
r(a)=q_0(a,b_0),\qquad
\ell(b)=q_0(a_0,b),\qquad
e=q_0(a_0,b_0).
\]
Both \(r\) and \(\ell\) are bijections. Construct
\[
x*y=q_0\!\left(r^{-1}(x),\ell^{-1}(y)\right).
\]

This is a loop with identity \(e\). The existence of compatible quotient Maltsev operations forces it to be an **abelian group**.

The proof is short enough to expose why no coordinates are being assumed. The unknown quotient operation \(m_3\) must be a homomorphism from the third power of this loop into the loop. Put
\[
A(x)=(x,e,e),\qquad C(z)=(e,e,z).
\]
The identities give \(m_3(A(x))=x\) and \(m_3(C(z))=z\). The coordinatewise equality
\[
A(x)*C(z)=C(z)*A(x)
\]
therefore proves commutativity. Similarly,
\[
(A(x)*A(y))*C(z)=A(x)*(A(y)*C(z))
\]
holds using only the loop identity, and applying \(m_3\) proves associativity. Finally,
\[
m_3(x,y,z)=x*y^{-1}*z.
\]

Thus the quotient heap is **uniquely forced and explicitly acquired**. The other coordinate heaps follow through the section bijections.

The implementation checks the acquired group laws and quotient preservation, including cases with unequal original domain sizes and unequal block sizes. Its intermediate tables remain polynomial: if \(S\) has \(M\) rows and quotient size \(q\), then
\[
|S'|=q^{k-1}\le M.
\]

There is an important limitation. **The resulting output-class pruning is already implied by full indicator arc consistency for these raw relation charts.** Three suitable Latin-chart rows make the other output coordinates Maltsev-identity pins, forcing precisely the same quotient class. The contribution of this procedure is an explicit reusable algebraic representation and certificate—not a stronger consistency algorithm.

It also does not yet provide the whole-domain partition and jointly compatible block operations needed by the lifting theorem.
