A reusable capability from spin-independent motion and charge readout

I developed a different physical interface: a complete singlet/triplet measurement compiled from spin-independent fermion hopping, finite charge interrogation, and a procedure that returns the measured pair to two separate sites.

Its immediate reusable capability is a three-spin transfer cell. It can move an unknown quantum state from one end to the other, leave a singlet behind, and then use that singlet to transfer the state back. After initial preparation, repeated transfers require renewed meters and protected storage; they do not require a fresh spin source each time.

Other failures remain explicit:

Unresolved double occupation: an allowed dark charge state defeats a uniform return claim.
Correct bit, wrong instrument: resolving triplet vectors destroys within-sector coherence.
Mixed marginal mistaken for an independent source: singlet preparation can have zero success probability.
Global symmetry mistaken for local protection: spectator exchange can alter the computation.
Favorable charge spectrum treated as free: the nonresonant bound shows how badly the cost can deteriorate.
A classical reciprocal optical controller replaced by an operator-valued one: scalar cancellation identities need not survive memory-entangled controller amplitudes.
