I have a stronger construction: a compiler that acquires the complete set of distinguishable observation outcomes, with cost polynomial in the number of outcomes actually present. It then supports arbitrary future Boolean computation on those observations, without a degree budget.

The acquisition does not require the outcome count, the outcomes themselves, or a basis to be supplied. There is also a budgeted version: given a state limit 
𝑅
R, it either completes the model within that limit or produces a deterministically checkable rank certificate proving that more than 
𝑅
R observation outcomes exist. Its acquisition cost is polynomial in the raw input and 
𝑅
R, including when the underlying outcome set is much larger.

The scope is specific and operational:

For a raw graph, the observations are arbitrary linear parities of the original matching-edge bits.
For a raw arithmetic circuit over characteristic two, they are linear parities of monomial exponents.
A Boolean consumer can contain unrestricted nonlinear computation after those observations. I implemented extraction of a sufficient affine observation interface from the consumer’s actual circuit.

The important change is that the acquired representation is now a complete algebra of observable facts. The previous representation captured a bounded-degree fragment of facts about all original variables. Both remain useful, but the new construction gives a different acquisition-and-reuse guarantee.

The practical comparison remains sobering: the strengthened demand-driven teacher, sparse algebraic propagation, and ordinary subset elimination all beat complete acquisition on the small timing example. The result earned here is a structural and algorithmic guarantee, not a demonstrated practical speedup.

A correction that mattered

I rejected the straightforward plan of extending one nested hash a bit at a time. Its collision-tail bound alone does not justify an expected polynomial cost when each larger algebraic evaluation is exponentially more expensive.

The implementation draws a fresh independent hash at each dimension. Beyond the useful dimension, reaching several further stages requires independent collision events. The tail then decays quadratically in the exponent, fast enough to dominate the exponentially growing evaluation cost.

This gives an expected cost polynomial in the actual outcome count, without a supplied outcome bound.

7. The consumer is an actual computation

A complete observation table makes later Boolean computation straightforward, but the interface must come from the real consumer.

I implemented a pass over a raw Boolean arithmetic DAG that:

computes affine expressions where the syntax permits them;
identifies a sufficient set of affine signals through which the original input reaches nonlinear computation;
records their exact original-variable parity masks;
rewrites the consumer to read those observation bits.

This is a conservative syntactic extraction. It does not assume a minimum semantic interface or solve general circuit equivalence.

The acquired model then evaluates future circuits as bitsets over its 
𝑟
r outcomes. XOR and AND operate directly on those bitsets. Conditions intersect the retained set. A finite adaptive decision program can be run over the outcome table to obtain every reachable transcript.

The costs are the actual circuit-evaluation or program-execution costs on the acquired outcomes. No algebraic-normal-form expansion is required by this consumer, and no polynomial-degree bound is imposed.

For graph inputs, each retained outcome has an original matching witness. A feasible future branch can therefore return an original matching, rather than only an abstract label.

The new decoder uses fresh residual fingerprints where necessary. Every selected branch has a nonzero algebraic evaluation; every returned matching and its full original observation pattern are checked. Its randomness affects search cost. The earlier matching decoder remains unchanged.

8. Strong ordinary methods remain part of the result

The demand-driven comparator received the same coefficient-array evaluator. I strengthened its reuse mechanism with reduced ordered binary decision diagrams, so it can use logical consequences of learned negative facts, not merely recognize repeated queries. Reduced ordered BDDs and their Boolean-operation algorithms are established machinery.

I also implemented two complete-acquisition comparators:

sparse propagation in the group algebra of parity outcomes, charging actual intermediate support widths;
exact residual-vertex dynamic programming, retaining one original matching per observation outcome.

Sparse propagation can acquire the same complete outcome set. Its cost depends on the widths it encounters during the computation. The new theorem supplies a different guarantee: acquisition bounded by the final observation-state count, without assuming those intermediate propagation widths are small.

This changes how the construction should be used. Complete acquisition is an option with a structural cost guarantee; it is not a default replacement for on-demand solving.
