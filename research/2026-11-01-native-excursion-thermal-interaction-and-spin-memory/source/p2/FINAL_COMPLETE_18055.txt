## 1. The comparison changed the question before implementation

The two-rate conventional sensor already earns quadratic information. I therefore tested whether the returning-error machinery could support a stronger capability: coherent interaction with an unknown quantum system.

Replace the classical frequency by an unknown target operator \(B\):

\[
H=Z_S\otimes B.
\]

A returned error trajectory cancels the static unknown noise axis, but its output is

\[
U=\exp[-iA(T)Z_S\otimes B],
\qquad
A(T)=\int_0^T(-1)^{N(t)}\,dt.
\]

The random signed exposure \(A(T)\) now matters as a quantum operation.

There is a genuine success with an exact event record: stopping when \(A(t)\) first reaches a chosen positive value \(a\) gives precisely \(e^{-iaZ_S\otimes B}\). The crossing occurs in even error parity. But this requires resolved timing and a physical way to stop the interaction or hand off the state.

Finite observation exposes the failure. Even perfect parity measurements at intervals leave interior jump times unknown. Two intervals can return the error parity and cancel the unknown axis while still contracting a target coherence. An explicit checked example retains only \(0.92720819\) of that coherence. Adding noncommuting target self-dynamics makes equal signed exposure insufficient even with perfect timing.

That preserves the sensor’s achievement while identifying a limit to its coherent use: **returning the error operator does not necessarily return the intended interaction time**.

I then examined entangling force loops through harmonic mediators. The independent conventional construction was strong enough to reject several apparent inventions immediately:

- Initially hot mediators already support geometric entangling gates.
- Shaped forces can compensate damping-induced changes in closure and phase.
- Environmental measurement and feedforward already remove thermal excess noise in suitable access models.
- Encoded fixed-angle gates provide a different protection mechanism.

## 2. The physical objective and the hidden failure

Consider two qubits coupled to a harmonic mediator:

\[
S=Z_1+Z_2,
\qquad
H(t)=S\,[g(t)a^\dagger+g(t)^*a].
\]

A closed conditional oscillator trajectory produces

\[
U_{\mathrm{gate}}=e^{i\Phi S^2}
=e^{i2\Phi}e^{i2\Phi Z_1Z_2}.
\]

Thus \(\Phi=\pi/8\) gives a maximally entangling operation.

The model distinguishes two real ports:

| Physical resource | Role |
|---|---|
| Intrinsic hot port, rate \(\kappa_h>0\) | Continuously injects independent thermal fluctuations; its output is inaccessible |
| Added cold port, rate \(\kappa_c>0\) | Provides an accessible measurement of the mediator |
| Bounded force \(g(t)\) | Generates the interaction |
| Gaussian output measurement and local \(S\) rotations | Supplies observation and correction |

There is no supplied noiseless reference qubit, protected pointer, bath purification or independent \(S^2\) interaction.

The tempting strategy is to measure the cold output, infer the thermal motion and subtract the inferred phase. That can greatly reduce a classical residual variance. **The same record also contains the qubit-driven response of the mediator.** Correcting it changes the geometric interaction.

Feedback modifying both coherent drift and diffusion is established quantum-control physics. The new question here is whether the specified entangling operation survives that modification at bounded physical cost. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### The exact quantum channel

I derived the complete terminal measurement-and-feedback channel rather than treating the record as an already available classical noise value.

Write the joint conditional operation as

\[
U_s=e^{i\Phi s^2}W(sL)U_{\mathrm{free}},
\]

where \(W\) is a Weyl displacement, \(L\) describes the actual environmental displacement, and the free environment has covariance \(V\). Let \(C\) describe the measured physical outputs, \(N\) their added measurement noise, and \(k\) the feedback weights.

The delivered coherence is

\[
\rho_{ss'}
\longmapsto
e^{\,iP(k)(s^2-s'^2)}
e^{-\frac12\sigma^2(k)(s-s')^2}\rho_{ss'},
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
P(k)=\Phi-\frac12L^\top\Omega C^\top k
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\sigma^2(k)=
(L-C^\top k)^\top V(L-C^\top k)+k^\top Nk.
}
\]

Here \(\Omega\) is the canonical commutation matrix.

The second expression is the familiar variance objective. **The first expression is the gate that remains.** Optimizing only the variance can select an operation approaching zero entangling phase.

A separate Fock-space calculation integrated the physical heterodyne measurement directly. It agreed with the full complex channel to approximately \(1.7\times10^{-16}\) in its numerical fixture. Omitting the phase shift produced an error of \(0.0316\) in the same coherence.

Current primary research on physical thermal-output monitoring reinforces why the actual instrument matters: measuring a thermal output does not grant access to an arbitrary purification of its bath. I used that distinction throughout. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

## 3. A sharp bound on the interaction that survives

The complete input-output model reduces to a useful backward response \(q(t)\). For a cold-output correction weight \(f(t)\),

\[
q(t)=
\int_t^T e^{-\kappa(u-t)/2}
\big[g(u)-\sqrt{\kappa_c}f(u)\big]\,du,
\qquad
\kappa=\kappa_h+\kappa_c.
\]

Two exact identities make the obstruction visible.

The retained interaction phase is

\[
P=\operatorname{Im}\int_0^T g(t)^*q(t)\,dt.
\]

The continuing hot bath contributes at least

\[
J_{\mathrm{hot}}
\ge V_h\kappa_h\int_0^T|q(t)|^2dt,
\qquad
V_h=n_h+\frac12,
\]

where a coherence with charge gap \(s-s'\) decays as \(e^{-J(s-s')^2}\).

Define the integrated squared coupling

\[
E=\int_0^T|g(t)|^2dt.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz then gives

\[
\boxed{
J\ge \frac{V_h\kappa_h P^2}{E}.
}
\]

This is the main physical result.

**Preserving a specified nonzero interaction requires a positive thermal cost whenever fresh hot noise continues entering through an inaccessible port.** The bound already grants optimal linear processing of the cold record and ideal local phase correction.

If the force is bounded by \(|g(t)|\le F\), then

\[
J\ge \frac{V_h\kappa_h P^2}{F^2T}.
\]

Making the gate slower cannot be treated as free protection. If an unavoidable data-coherence error contributes exponent \(aT\), then for charge gap \(\Delta_s\),

\[
D_{\mathrm{total}}
\ge
aT+\frac{\Delta_s^2V_h\kappa_hP^2}{F^2T}
\ge
\frac{2|P|\sqrt{a\Delta_s^2V_h\kappa_h}}{F}.
\]

This places interaction strength, thermal noise and data storage in the same comparison.

The scope is essential. The result concerns the unencoded commuting-force interaction, continuing Markov hot noise, Gaussian observation of cold outputs and local phase feedforward. A hot **initial** oscillator alone does not satisfy its hypotheses. Neither do an accessible native bath, a quiet noise window, an encoded recovery or additional nonlinear controls.

## 4. I constructed the observer that preserves the gate

The result includes a constructive optimum.

### A test for whether thermal cancellation can retain an interaction

Suppose the physical environmental covariance is

\[
V(n)=nV_H+V_0.
\]

For a fixed finite measurement family and bounded gain, removing all leading thermal noise while retaining phase \(P_*\) requires—and within this model is sufficient for—both

\[
V_H^{1/2}(L-C^\top k)=0
\]

and

\[
\frac12L^\top\Omega C^\top k=\Phi-P_*.
\]

The first equation asks whether the hot random phase is observable. The second asks whether the correction leaves the required quantum interaction.

Together they form a linear feasibility test on the **actual available outputs**. Once feasible, the remaining noise is a constrained positive quadratic optimization. Discrete phase equivalences and permitted known local corrections must also be included; they are handled in the proof.

This gives a concrete way to assess a proposed interface before treating a low-noise record as evidence of a protected gate.

### The exact optimum at the required gate

The strongest linear Gaussian comparator has a closed-form solution. Completing the square yields a quadratic tradeoff:

\[
J_{\min}(P_*)
=
J_{\mathrm{noise\ optimum}}
+
\frac{(P_*-P_{\mathrm{noise\ optimum}})^2}{D},
\qquad D>0.
\]

The second term is the price of retaining the desired interaction.

I also derived the full continuous optimization as a linear boundary-value problem. It charges the initial bus, continuing hot input, cold input and detector noise separately. The executable solves a finite smooth readout family globally under the same phase constraint.

### An explicit construction attaining the thermal bound

For a smooth closed drive with \(g(0)=g(T)=0\), choose

\[
q_*(t)=i\frac{P_*}{E}g(t),
\]

and synthesize the physical observation weight

\[
f_*(t)=
\frac{g(t)-\kappa q_*(t)/2+\dot q_*(t)}
{\sqrt{\kappa_c}}.
\]

Then

\[
P=P_*,
\qquad
J_{\mathrm{hot}}
=
\frac{V_h\kappa_hP_*^2}{E}.
\]

The thermal part of the bound is therefore sharp.

The remaining terms are explicit. In particular, the observer weight contains \(\dot g\); its gain, detector noise and bandwidth cost can be substantial. Attaining one component of the bound does not make the whole gate accurate automatically.

## 5. The observer has a finite response time

A finite number of waveform coefficients does not prove that a real detector can acquire them. I therefore added an explicit detector model.

Use two fixed-phase homodyne channels after a 50:50 splitter, each with efficiency \(\eta\). Their reconstructed joint measurement adds quadrature variance

\[
\nu_{\mathrm{base}}=\frac1\eta-\frac12.
\]

Even perfect detectors therefore retain the heterodyne vacuum penalty.

Let each detector have finite first-order response

\[
\dot x=-Bx+Bj,
\]

followed by electronic noise. For a desired smooth weight \(f\) vanishing at both endpoints,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^T f(t)j(t)\,dt
=
\int_0^T
\left(f(t)-\frac{\dot f(t)}B\right)x(t)\,dt.
}
\]

This acquires the needed functional by weighting the filtered record. It requires no differentiation of noisy data, and the unknown initial detector memory cancels in the boundary term.

Electronic noise with quadrature spectral density \(\nu_e\) adds

\[
\boxed{
J_e=
\nu_e\left(
\|f\|_2^2+\frac{\|\dot f\|_2^2}{B^2}
\right).
}
\]

Thus finite response has an explicit derivative penalty. The finite-profile optimization remains quadratic and includes that cost.

This is a particular physical response model, with stated quantum and electronic noise. A hard stopband, saturation or a different transfer function requires its actual response in the calculation.

The construction also keeps calibration, record dynamic range, digitization, correction latency and data storage visible. For example, an unmatched hot-noise component of amplitude \(\epsilon\) produces an \(O(n_h\epsilon^2)\) contribution. Temperature-independent performance cannot be claimed with fixed mismatching calibration.

## 7. Two genuine escapes survived the comparison

### Acquiring the native environmental output

When the relevant **native output** is actually accessible, one can measure the quadrature generating its random phase while avoiding the quadrature carrying data information. The correction can then preserve the geometric phase and remove thermal excess noise, leaving detector-added noise.

This is the established environmental-erasure/no-knowledge route. Its physical access requirement is the resource that matters. A separate cold probe does not automatically supply it. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

The theory makes the remaining engineering question precise: which emitted modes can actually be collected, with what loss, phase alignment, response and noise? Missing independent hot channels and vacuum detector loss must be represented differently.

### Protecting a complete fixed-angle gate

The other collaborator constructed a decisive exception to an overbroad error-correction argument.

Consider

\[
|0_L\rangle=
\frac{|0\rangle+\sqrt3|2\rangle}{2},
\qquad
|1_L\rangle=
\frac{\sqrt3|1\rangle+|3\rangle}{2}.
\]

Both codewords have matching first and second number moments, so \(\{I,n\}\) satisfies the Knill–Laflamme conditions. Yet

\[
e^{i\pi n^2/2}
\]

implements logical \(S\), and two blocks driven through \(n_A+n_B\) acquire a logical entangling gate.

The complete finite-angle operation preserves the code even though continuously projecting its Hamiltonian would remove the useful generator. For the specified recovery, the one-block logical error under Gaussian phase variance \(v\) is

\[
p_L(v)=
\frac12-\frac9{16}e^{-v/2}
+\frac1{16}e^{-9v/2}
=
\frac9{16}v^2+O(v^3).
\]

This is established rotation-symmetric/binomial-code machinery and a real competing route. Its preparation, recovery, charge range, native losses and noncommuting faults remain paid physical requirements. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

The comparison ruled out selecting the sensor merely for quadratic information. The coherent-interaction test then exposed its unresolved clock requirement. Current Astra’s terminal-readout and complete-output results were used at their actual scopes. They did not supply missing bath access or a protected hardware service.

The surviving physical opening is now specific: **create an entangling interface whose native environmental information can be acquired or corrected while retaining the interaction, with the acquisition or recovery cheaper than the protection it replaces.** Direct native-output erasure and encoded finite-angle gates are concrete competitors for that objective.

The new theory gives that next construction a demanding, executable test: it must preserve the quantum operation, satisfy the actual observation contract, and improve the complete physical cost.
