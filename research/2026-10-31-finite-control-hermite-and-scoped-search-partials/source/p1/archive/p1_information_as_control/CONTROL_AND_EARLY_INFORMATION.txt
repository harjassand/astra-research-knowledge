P1: EARLY INFORMATION AS A DESCRIPTION OF THE REMAINING EXPERIMENT
Ordinary proof and synthesis, 30 October 2026 UTC.

Status: a foundational resource characterization and an explicit construction.
The theorems are proved here. Historical priority is unresolved. No physical
sensor, universal representation learner, or practical runtime advantage is
claimed. Earlier p1_next and p1_research artifacts are preserved unchanged.

1. THE CAPABILITY

The scientific objective is prediction at a prescribed final squared error,
including the information needed to choose a measurement. The opportunity is
that a large family of task coefficients can have a short nonlinear
description, even when no fixed low-dimensional linear representation retains
the target. Early measurements may acquire that description. A second-stage
actuator can then compute an appropriate aggregate while keeping its large
input local. Whether that aggregate is genuinely accessible remains a separate
question; software arithmetic and physical programming are not free.

The new organizing object is the distribution of the residual task coefficient
and its distortion under permitted measurement designs. A covariance matrix
determines all fixed-experiment risks in the conditional-linear setting below,
but does not determine the value of finite control. This is a precise limit on
the fixed-experiment covariance interface developed in the earlier campaign.

2. EXACT FINITE-CONTROL VALUE, INCLUDING NOISY INTERACTION

Let C in R^m have any known square-integrable law, and let V~N(0,I_m) be
independent of C. The target is C^T V. The controller initially knows C. The
final predictor retains C and receives the complete legal readout transcript.
The actuator has no additional information about C, and sees V only through
at most q unit-power linear reads (row norm at most one), each with independent
N(0,s) noise, s>0. An optimal design can use unit rows: increasing a row's
power only improves its information and leaves its scalar cost unchanged.

Controller bits, readouts and direction choices can be arbitrarily interleaved.
The actuator can choose directions from its past readouts, received bits and
independent seeds. The controller may receive readouts freely in this theorem;
return bandwidth is a separate resource. Every C-dependent actuator
distinction, including timing, requests and stopping, is charged so that the
counterfactual binary control tree has at most K=2^B leaves. A hard bound on an
unframed string length alone is insufficient. Every counterfactual branch is
legal and uses at most q reads. Independent random seeds may be revealed to
the final predictor for the lower bound.

For a matrix M with q unit rows define

  Sigma_s(M) = (I + M^T M/s)^(-1).

THEOREM 1. The infimum risk over every such interactive protocol equals

  min_{M_1,...,M_K, each with q unit rows}
       E_C min_j C^T Sigma_s(M_j) C.                           (1)

Thus one message selecting one fixed measurement design attains the best
possible value. Interleaving further readouts and control cannot improve that
value in this interface. The minimizing design may have repeated directions.

Proof. First condition on C, the legal transcript and algorithmic seeds, but
NOT on the measurement noises. Predictable adaptive Gaussian likelihoods
multiply just as fixed likelihoods do. The posterior covariance of V is
Sigma_s(M_actual). Observed choices and messages add no residual V information
once C and the numerical history are fixed. The conditional Bayes variance is
C^T Sigma_s(M_actual) C.

Only after taking this posterior-variance expression, freeze the full V,
all pre-sampled measurement-noise innovations, and algorithmic seeds. Replay
the actuator on every possible controller-bit branch. Supply the j-th noise
innovation to its j-th requested read. The resulting collection of at most K
matrices depends on the frozen replay values but not on C: whenever a bit is
needed, branch rather than invoking the real encoder. The actual trajectory
for every C belongs to this collection. Therefore its residual quadratic form
is at least the minimum over the replayed collection. C is independent of the
frozen quantities, so averaging over C for each collection is at least the
best deterministic codebook value in (1). Then average over replay values.
This proves the lower bound. Freezing the noise is a proof coupling and never
reveals that noise to the estimator.

Conversely choose a minimizing static codebook. The controller sends the index
minimizing the quadratic form for its C, and the actuator executes its stored
unit-row design. The controller computes the ordinary Gaussian posterior mean
of C^T V. This has exactly the risk in (1). Compactness of a product of unit
spheres and domination by ||C||^2 establish existence of a minimizing finite
codebook. Unused reads can be appended. QED.

The corresponding exact-read statement is

  min_{S_1,...,S_K, dim S_j<=q} E min_j ||(I-P_{S_j})C||^2.       (2)

One can prove it directly using Gaussian innovations, or bracket the noisy
optimum between the projection residual and orthonormal implementations and
let s decrease to zero. For one noisy read,

  R*(B,1,s) = [s/(1+s)] E||C||^2
              + [1/(1+s)] D_line(K),                         (3)

where D_line(K) is the optimal K-line distortion in (2).

For unlimited control, q repeats aligned with C attain the exact lower bound
s/(s+q) E||C||^2. For zero bits and C~N(0,I_m/m), q<=m, the optimum is
1-q/[m(1+s)], attained by orthonormal directions. These are ordinary Gaussian
experimental-design endpoints, not new algorithms.

Scope corrections. The final estimator must retain C. If C is uniform on
[1,2], m=q=1 and B=0, an actuator that observes V exactly but does not know C
still has target error E[V^2] Var(C)>0; (2) would give zero. If an actuator
already retains C locally, no external B-bit bottleneck applies. If public
actuator context Z is correlated with C while V remains independent of (C,Z),
the optimum is conditional: E_Z R*(B,q,s; law(C|Z)), with measurable designs.
These conditions are part of the statement, not implementation footnotes.

The theorem does not efficiently optimize a codebook. K=2^B stored dense
designs and exhaustive online selection can be prohibitively expensive. It
also does not discover the law of C, acquire C, or implement a dense read.
Targets nonlinear in the remaining V, non-Gaussian V, uncharged side
information, dependent noise, and expected read budgets need other arguments.
The trace version for a finite vector of conditional-linear targets follows
by summing the conditional variances under squared Euclidean loss.

3. WHAT THE FIRST EXPERIMENT MUST EARN

Let X be independent of V~N(0,I_m), and let

  F = g(X) + a(X)^T V,

with g and a square-integrable. An actual first experiment H observes X only,
using randomness independent of V. All later measurements access V. Define

  gbar(H)=E[g(X)|H],       C(H)=E[a(X)|H],
  J(H)=E Var(g(X)|H) + E tr Cov(a(X)|H).

THEOREM 2. When the final predictor retains H, optimal continuation risk is

  J(H) + R*(B,q,s; law(C(H))),                                (4)

under the no-public-correlated-context contract of Theorem 1. With public
context Z generated in the first phase, replace the second term by its
conditional-codebook value given Z. No optimization of H or computation of
these conditional means is granted for free.

Proof. Write

  F = gbar(H) + C(H)^T V + E0,
  E0 = g-gbar + (a-C)^T V.

For every H,V, E[E0|H,V]=0, by independence of V and the first-phase variables.
Subsequent legal transcripts are generated from H,V and independent random
seeds/noise. E0 remains orthogonal to all estimators based on them. Also

  E E0^2 = E Var(g|H) + E tr Cov(a|H) = J(H),

because E V=0 and E VV^T=I. Thus every predictor's squared error splits into
J(H) plus its error on the conditional-linear target gbar+C^T V. Subtract the
known gbar and apply Theorem 1. Extra private first-phase variables beyond C
can be treated as encoder randomization conditional on C, independent of V;
the replay lower bound still holds. QED.

This exposes two distinct effects of an early experiment: it changes the
irreducible uncertainty J(H), and it changes the distribution of descriptions
C(H) needed to control the remaining experiment. Minimizing J alone does not
optimize the second cost. Likewise a supplied coefficient feature map is not
an acquired representation merely because it makes (4) easy to evaluate.

4. SAME COVARIANCE, DIFFERENT CONTROL

For any fixed observation D of V, independent of C, even a nonlinear one,

  E Var(C^T V | C,D)
    = tr( E[CC^T] E[Cov(V|D)] ).                             (5)

Thus two coefficient laws with the same second-moment matrix have identical
risk for every fixed V experiment, when C is known to the final decoder.
Equation (5) is proved by conditioning on C,D and using independence; it is
not a claim about arbitrary fixed joint observations of the source of C and V.

Now compare C_G~N(0,I_M/M) with C_H=phi(U)/sqrt(M), where U~N(0,I_r) and phi is
the orthonormal degree-two Hermite feature map, M=r(r+1)/2. Both have mean zero
and covariance I_M/M. For C_H a scalar quantizer of the r generating variables
provides a small finite control description, with O(r) bits for fixed error.
For C_G even one controlled exact read at constant error requires Omega(M)
bits. The laws have identical fixed-experiment behavior but radically
different controlled behavior. Merely replacing the Hermite coefficient law
by a Gaussian with the same covariance destroys the relevant resource.

For hard B bits and 1<=q<M exact reads, the Gaussian source problem is classical
unequal-dimensional Grassmann quantization. Let d=M-q, a=d/2, b=q/2,
K=2^B, and let F_ab be the Beta(a,b) CDF. The elementary bounds are

  integral_0^1 (1-K F_ab(x))_+ dx <= D*
      <= integral_0^1 (1-F_ab(x))^K dx.                        (6)

The lower bound is a union bound over subspaces; the upper is the average of
a random Haar codebook and implies existence, not fast construction. At high
rate for fixed dimensions, D* is proportional to K^(-2/(M-q)). These
geometric and rate-distortion facts predate this work; see Dai-Rider-Liu.
Efficient structured quantizers such as Cube-Split are ordinary alternatives
to exponential codebook search and must not be omitted from comparisons.

An expected prefix-length variant retains a useful obstruction. For q<M,
d=M-q, and exact-read risk R<d/M for C_G, any legal prefix protocol satisfies

  E[length] ln 2 >= (d/2) [ ln(d/(M R)) - 1 + M R/d ].          (7)

For R>=d/M use the trivial lower bound zero. The complete proof is in the
independent expected-prefix audit. It conditions on the replay input and
seeds, bounds conditional mutual information by prefix length, and applies
the variational entropy inequality to a chi-square residual. This extends
the obstruction to an expected control budget, not Theorem 1's full exact
value formula to every expected resource constraint.

5. A CONSTRUCTIBLE FAMILY AND ITS UNIVERSAL FIXED COMPARATOR

The accompanying hermite_control_theorem.md proves the all-degree family

  F_c(U,V) = M^(-1/2) sum_{|a|=p} c_a phi_a(U) V_a,
  M = binom(r+p-1,p),     Var(F_c)=||c||^2/M.

Two rounds use r+1 exact reads. With IID read noise variance s and
alpha=1/(1+s), the aligned posterior-coefficient protocol has risk
(1-alpha^(p+1))Var(F_c). This matches one noisy observation of all r+M
coordinates. Every fixed q-unit-read experiment, even mixing U and V and
using repeats, arbitrary oblique directions and nonlinear decoding, has risk
at least

  max{0, Var(F_c) - (p+1)||c||_infinity^2 alpha q/M}.             (8)

The proof contracts the degree-(p+1) Gaussian Wick tensor in the posterior
mean covariance. It does not assume coordinate measurements. Equation (8)
is a universal necessary fixed-budget bound, not the exact fixed optimum.

For p=2, quantize each standardized first observation with step h and
clipping T, rounding toward zero. The message uses
r ceil(log2(2T/h+1)) bits. The normalized coefficient error obeys

  D_Q <= 4 h^2 + 4 E[G^4 1{|G|>T}],   G~N(0,1).                (9)

This expected error bound is uniform in r; no probability that all coordinates
simultaneously remain bounded is being assumed. T=6,h=.01 needs 11r bits.
Actual unknown c is acquired in M bounded synthetic function calls in the
DECLARED feature layout. Each error <=nu gives
||c-chat||^2/M <= (M+r)nu^2. This does not acquire the layout or certify that
an arbitrary function belongs to the model family. Direct-target calls are
available only on synthetic calibration inputs here; an online native target
oracle, if available, is a stronger ordinary alternative.

6. FULLY DIGITIZED READOUTS, WITHOUT A HIDDEN PRECISE CONTEXT

Let A=c*phi(G), Ahat=chat*phi(QG), M feature dimension, and alpha=1/(1+s).
The implemented direction is w=Ahat/||Ahat||, with a fixed unit fallback at
zero amplitude. Let Z=w^T V+noise and let Q_z be a nearest-grid final ADC.
The predictor uses only packed QG and the final ADC index:

  Fhat = alpha^2 ||Ahat|| Q_z(Z)/sqrt(M).

Conditional on G and the calibrated model, Z has the same N(0,1+s) law.
Thus its distribution is independent of the amplitude. Put

  k = E[A dot Ahat]/M,        t = E||Ahat||^2/M,
  a_z=E[Z Q_z(Z)],            b_z=E[Q_z(Z)^2].

The exact true risk is

  R_ADC = Var(F_c) - 2 alpha^4 k a_z + alpha^4 t b_z.            (10)

Proof. Conditional on G,V, E[F_c|G,V]=alpha A^T V/sqrt(M). Conditional on
G,Z, the Gaussian mean of V is alpha w Z. Consequently
E[F_c|G,Z]=alpha^2 (A dot w) Z/sqrt(M). Expanding E(F_c-Fhat)^2 and using
the distributional independence of Z proves (10). This argument uses G only
as an analysis variable; the implemented predictor never receives it.

The bins of Q and Q_z give k,t,a_z,b_z from univariate Gaussian integrals,
so (10) is evaluated without using Monte Carlo as a proof. For clipping at
8 and step 8/32767, Q_z has 65535 levels and fits signed 16 bits. Its error
epsilon_z satisfies step^2/4+E[Z^2 1{|Z|>8}] as an upper bound. An independent
simple certificate is

  R_ADC <= (sqrt(R_before_ADC) + alpha^2 sqrt(t epsilon_z))^2.  (11)

For balanced c=1,r=128,M=8256,s=.01, the executable gives:
  129 noisy reads; 1408 first-ADC bits; 1408 remote control bits;
  one 16-bit final ADC report; 8256 computed and applied actuator weights.
  Exact-bin risk .0295043396997; elementary uniform bound .0298513262471.
  Every fixed 129-read experiment has risk >= .9535891089109.
  Fixed risk <= .03 requires at least 2697 reads by (8).
  Reading all 8384 coordinates noisily attains .0294098520724.
These are different lower/upper statements; 2697 is not asserted sufficient.

7. PAID EXECUTION AND THE REMAINING OPERATIONAL GAP

The largest acquired run has r=64,M=2080 and unknown c in [.9,1.1]. Its
2080 scalar calibration calls materialize 4,326,400 feature entries and use
8,652,800 dense target products. The prepared model has 2080 float64 values,
16,640 bytes, whose one-time transfer is charged if remote. The calibration
contract grants absolute value error at most 1e-7; it is distinct from the
deployment's variance-.01 random read noise. That calibration precision is
not inferred from one noisy physical read per coefficient.

Per deployment the fully digitized extension uses 65 noisy scalar reads,
88 bytes of context ADC output, an 88-byte remote control packet, a two-byte
last readout, 2080 generated weights and 2080 projection multiplications.
The exact predicted MSE is .0296898865799. Across 8192 fresh trials the
measured MSE is .0299856294720 with estimated standard error .0004821124282.
Every fixed 65-read unit-row design has risk >= .8939962485039, even when
given the true coefficients. The full 2144-coordinate noisy benchmark risk
is .0295952698812. These figures concern the actual acquired c; they are not
silently substituted for the separate normalized r=128 example.

This is software execution with explicit dense arithmetic. Float64 actuator
generation and dot products are not a certified finite-precision physical
instrument. First/context and final-output communication are actually packed.
If the source has a native quadratic/direct-target evaluator, it performs the
same ordinary conditional computation. If programming the aggregate requires
exporting V first, acquisition savings vanish. A full-input implementation
also performs O(M) arithmetic. No full-cost runtime advantage is established.

7A. FINITE DENSE ACTUATOR PROGRAM, WITH ITS FULL SIZE PAID

The scalar-read construction must also price local pattern programming.
For an ideal unit vector w, round each coordinate to j_i/2^18. Let
k=ceil(sqrt(M)), D=2^19+k, and implement the rational coefficients

  wtilde_i = 2 j_i/D.

The j_i range from -2^18 to 2^18 and fit a 20-bit alphabet. Since
||2j-2^19 w||<=sqrt(M), this actual pattern satisfies

  ||wtilde||<=1,
  ||wtilde-w|| <= (sqrt(M)+k)/(2^19+k) =: delta_w.

The available action class here is a unit-power BALL; rounded patterns need
not have norm exactly one. The optimal fixed comparator is allowed the full
unit ball and in particular unit-norm rows. Its bound is unchanged.

Let t=E||chat*phi(QG)||^2/M. Coupling the same V and read noise, the decoder's
L2 change before its final ADC is at most alpha^2 sqrt(t) delta_w. The actual
last Z has conditional Gaussian variance ||wtilde||^2+s<=1+s, so the same
uniform ADC MSE bound epsilon_z applies. Independence from G is no longer
claimed. Consequently

  R_finite_pattern
    <= [sqrt(R_ideal_before_ADC)
          +alpha^2 sqrt(t)(delta_w+sqrt(epsilon_z))]^2.        (12)

Do not reuse the exact unit-pattern ADC identity (10) after this rounding.
For balanced r=128, the elementary bound in (12) is .0299713102587<.03.
The remote control is still 1408 bits, but the local dense pattern requires
165,120 bits (20,640 bytes) and 8256 coefficients to generate and apply.
The descriptor compresses incoming control, not the local actuator work.

finite_actuator.py actually packs and decodes every 20-bit pattern index. It
checks 4 sum_i j_i^2<=D^2 in integer arithmetic, verifying the rational power
cap independently of floating normalization. For the acquired r=64 model,
the local pattern is 5200 bytes, the same remote packet is 88 bytes, and
the final readout is two bytes. Across 8192 new inputs its MSE is
.0291915528022 with estimated SE .0004743011068; the analytic bound is
.0297897559625. This fresh Monte Carlo mean is not evidence that quantization
improves risk. Dense weight generation and dot products remain software
operations. The finite representation does not demonstrate a physical sensor
or a rigorously rounded hardware implementation.

8. WHY THE REPRESENTATION PROMISE CANNOT BE SILENTLY REMOVED

The side note cubic_structure_hardness.md gives an explicit reduction from
3-SAT: deciding whether an explicit homogeneous cubic has any global affine
direction is NP-hard. YES instances have a unit d with gradient F(d)=0; NO
instances have ||gradient F(d)||>1/(384N) for every unit d. For cubics,
D_d^2 F(x)=2 gradient F(d) dot x, so this is also an inverse-polynomial gap
in global Gaussian L2 directional curvature. The proof is ordinary algebra,
and an explicit 40-dimensional UNSAT cubic plus exact checks are preserved.
This does not establish hardness of all adaptive policies, approximate
representations in every metric, or any particular sensing budget optimum.

9. ATTACKS THAT DID NOT JUSTIFY A STRONGER CLAIM

Unstructured Gaussian control: the last scalar read of the preceding
quadratic example hides an ambient-dimensional direction. Classical
quantization prices that direction; extra rounds do not evade Theorem 1.
This closes an access loophole rather than producing a new practical method.

Physical shunt transfer: Astra U1050 provides a conditional rank-one shunt
model whose scalar response encodes v^T H v. The governing source explicitly
does not demonstrate the configurable apparatus. Its bounded positive-
definite H law is not the unbounded Gaussian Hermite model here. Moreover
the native energy read is already an ordinary direct-target alternative.
The transfer was examined and not credited as physical acquisition.

Posterior simplification: the companion computation branch acquires a global
curvature certificate from scalar value queries in a promised zero-hidden-
bias log-cosh class. Ordinary spectral design and classical frame completion
attain exactly the same read count. Strongly log-concave computation closes
the conditional inference problem, but the elementary normalizer bound is
enormous and a prior normalizer is not acquired for free. This useful result
does not supply the missing advantage and is retained as a companion.

10. CONSEQUENCE AND NEXT DECISIVE GATE

Within the declared conditional-linear model, a hard interactive planning
problem can be replaced exactly by a distributional design/quantization
problem. The Hermite family makes the relevant distributional distinction
constructive: low nonlinear description dimension and large fixed linear
information dimension can coexist, with final noisy accuracy preserved by
small actual messages. Covariance alone provably cannot express that fact.

The next consequential gate is a target whose source really supports the
structured aggregate, whose coefficient description can be acquired with
controlled error, and for which the best ordinary direct-target/full-input
implementation has a provably or measurably higher total cost. This note
earns the structural result and paid synthetic execution, not that gate.
