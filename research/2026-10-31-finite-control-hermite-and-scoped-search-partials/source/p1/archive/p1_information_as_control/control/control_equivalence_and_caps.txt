STATUS AND RECOMMENDATION

The useful structural result is an exact reduction: arbitrary interleaving of
B controller bits and q Gaussian linear readouts has the same optimal Bayes
risk as a single message selecting one of 2^B fixed q-dimensional subspaces.
Thus the full interactive access problem reduces to an established unequal-
dimensional Grassmann quantization problem. The sharp geometric distortion
law is prior art (Dai--Rider--Liu 2007), not a new discovery. The interactive
reduction is the candidate contribution; historical priority is unresolved.

The consequence is operational: the earlier exact final dense read uses an
unbounded-precision control interface. Constant risk reduction for fresh
isotropic inputs requires linear-in-dimension control or a linear number of
V measurements. More rounds cannot bypass this requirement. Structured source
geometry, shared side information, reuse of the same U, and efficient implicit
quantizers are real escape routes, with their resources explicitly charged.
This is an explicit interface refinement, not a contradiction of the earlier
measurement theorem. If the actuator already retains the first-stage vector
locally, external controller communication may be zero; local computation
and programming of the dense weights must then be charged instead.

1. A priced protocol model

Let U,V be independent N(0,I_m), and F=U^T V/sqrt(m), so Var(F)=1. The
controller already knows U exactly. Acquiring U itself is an additional
resource, e.g. m exact scalar coordinate reads in the earlier construction.
The B-bit price applies to U-dependent information newly available at the
V actuator. The actuator does not already retain U or its first-stage record.
Returned readouts are exact real scalars here: their number is charged, not
their digital output precision. Quantizing that return channel is a separate
resource refinement.

An actuator can obtain at most q exact scalar linear observations a^T V,
with unit directions. Its directions may depend on its independent seed,
earlier readouts, and received controller bits. The controller receives the
readouts and may send later bits based on U and the entire preceding history.
It may use independent private/public randomness. All maps are measurable.
The decoder knows U and the transcript; giving it all independent seeds can
only improve estimation and is harmless for a lower bound.

The actuator has no access to U except through counted messages. The
controller has no access to V except through the legal readouts. Feedback
from the actuator may contain any function of these readouts but no additional
V information. The hard control budget is B bits. Control timing, requests,
stopping, metadata, and variable message boundaries must not be uncharged
U-dependent signals. A standard binary protocol tree with at most B controller
branches on every path is sufficient. For each fixed V and seeds it has at
most 2^B leaves, even if the number/interleaving of readouts depends on history.

Equivalent convenient convention: a B-bit control word is padded to fixed
length, and a public protocol state determines when the next bit is requested.
Actuator-side stops based on received bits/readout history are allowed.
Controller-side decisions to stop or request a new action must be included
in the counted protocol. Merely bounding the payload string while leaving
unbounded timing/address commands free is a different access model.

Exact repeated/dependent reads add no information; orthogonalizing the
innovations preserves the transcript information. Their number of independent
directions is at most q. Zero-read or short branches can be completed for
analysis to subspaces of dimension q when q<=m. Unreachable counterfactual
histories can be totalized and capped at q reads without changing actual
almost-sure behavior. The statements below concern hard q and hard B, not
unbounded exceptional branches under an expected budget.

2. Exact interactive-to-codebook equivalence

For N>=1, 0<=q<=m, define

 D*_(m,q)(N) = min_{S_1,...,S_N: dim S_j=q}
                 E_U min_j ||(I-P_{S_j})U||^2/m.

The minimum exists: the product of N Grassmann manifolds is compact, and the
expected loss is continuous by dominated convergence. Repeated subspaces are
allowed. For q=0 it is 1; for q=m it is 0.

THEOREM. The smallest squared Bayes risk of every protocol in Section 1 is
exactly D*_(m,q)(2^B). In particular, arbitrary interleaving does not improve
the optimum over one fixed-codebook message followed by q parallel readouts.

Proof, conditional residual law. Fix U and all seeds. Controller messages
are then measurable functions of the preceding V-readout history. Every
new actuator direction is predictable in the Gaussian innovation filtration.
Therefore, for almost every completed transcript H,

  V | U,H,seeds = v_H + Z,     Z~N(0,I-P_{S_actual}),

where S_actual is the span of actual read directions. The Bayes residual
variance of F at this transcript is

  ||(I-P_{S_actual})U||^2/m.                         (A)

There is no omitted information from the controller bits: conditional on U
and the seeds, those bits are functions of already observed readouts. They
cannot expose an unread component of V. Any estimator has risk at least the
expectation of (A).

Proof, replay. Now fix the FULL latent V and all seeds as mathematical values
used only in this proof. For each complete possible controller word w, replay
the actuator. Whenever it asks for a linear read a, answer with a^T V; when
it asks for a controller bit, supply the next bit of w. The actuator's rules
use no U, so this replay constructs a subspace S_w(V,seeds) independently of
U. There are at most 2^B replayed paths/subspaces. Pad a deficient span to
dimension q if necessary. Such padding can only decrease projection error.
The actual span belongs to its replayed word, so pointwise

  ||(I-P_{S_actual})U||^2/m
      >= min_w ||(I-P_{S_w(V,seeds)})U||^2/m.

Because U is independent of the fixed V and seeds, the expectation over U
of the right-hand side is at least D*_(m,q)(2^B), for EVERY replayed codebook.
Average over V and seeds. This proves the lower bound, without pretending
the actual random subspace was independent of U.

Achievability. Fix a minimizing codebook, choose the nearest subspace to U,
and send its index in B bits. Read an orthonormal basis of that subspace and
return U^T P_S V/sqrt(m). Its risk is exactly the codebook distortion.
The same proof establishes equality of infima if one avoids the compactness
argument. No optimal interactive policy is supplied or computed.

GENERALIZATION. The replay theorem requires Gaussian V but not Gaussian U.
For ANY independent U-law with finite E||U||^2, replace D* by that law's
subspace-quantization distortion. If E||U||^2=m, the same normalization has
unit prior variance. Gaussian U is used only for the spherical laws below.
This is a precise reason source structure can reduce control cost.

3. Exact finite spherical-cap bounds

Assume 1<=q<m, let d=m-q, a=d/2, b=q/2, and let

  F_{a,b}(x)=I_x(a,b),    0<=x<=1,

be the Beta(a,b) CDF. To avoid notation confusion, this F_{a,b} is a CDF,
not the target F. For a uniform sphere direction Theta and any fixed q-plane,
||(I-P_S)Theta||^2 has precisely this beta distribution: numerator and
complement energies are independent chi-squares of dimensions d and q.
Gaussian radius is independent of Theta and has E||U||^2=m, so normalized
Gaussian distortion equals the expected spherical distortion.

For N=2^B, define

  L_N = integral_0^1 (1-N F_{a,b}(x))_+ dx,
  U_N = integral_0^1 (1-F_{a,b}(x))^N dx.

Then the exact interactive optimum obeys

                 L_N <= D*_(m,q)(N) <= U_N.        (B)

Lower proof: for any N-plane codebook, the probability that at least one
plane has squared distance <=x is at most N F_{a,b}(x), by the union bound.
Integrate the survival function of the minimum distortion.

Upper proof: draw N q-planes independently from Haar measure. Conditional
on a fixed Theta, their squared distances are independent with the above
beta law. Their average minimum distortion is U_N. At least one codebook
achieves no more than this average. This is an existence/random-code bound,
not an efficient codebook search or compact representation theorem.

The lower bound can be calculated without numerical quadrature. Set
t_N=F_{a,b}^{-1}(1/N). Integration by parts gives

  L_N=N * (a/(a+b)) * I_{t_N}(a+1,b).             (C)

At B=0, both bounds are exactly d/m, the optimal fixed q-read risk. For q=2,
F_{a,b}(x)=x^a and

  L_N=(a/(a+1)) N^(-1/a),
  U_N=Gamma(N+1) Gamma(1+1/a)/Gamma(N+1+1/a).

At m=2,q=1, equally spaced projective lines attain the cap lower bound,
giving the exact finite optimum

  D*_(2,1)(N)=1/2-[N/(2 pi)] sin(pi/N).

These closed forms are used as numerical implementation checks, not claimed
as new quantization results.

4. Rate and accuracy consequences

For fixed m,q and large N, set c=1/[a Beta(a,b)]. Near zero,
F_{a,b}(x)=c x^a(1+O(x)). Equation (B) then gives

  L_N ~ [a/(a+1)] (Nc)^(-1/a),
  U_N ~ Gamma(1+1/a) (Nc)^(-1/a).                 (D)

The ratio of these leading constants is Gamma(2+2/d). In particular,

  required B = [(m-q)/2] log_2(1/epsilon) + O_(m,q)(1)

for residual risk epsilon tending to zero. Every finite B with q<m has
strictly positive risk: a finite union of proper subspaces has Gaussian
measure zero. Zero error from one final read therefore needs an infinite-
precision control interface for a fresh full-dimensional Gaussian U.

These high-rate exponents AND constants are exactly the p=1, real-field
specialization of Dai--Rider--Liu, arXiv:0705.2278, Theorem 2. They were
rederived here before the closest source was located and are recorded as
established machinery, not a new result.

For proportional dimensions q/m->alpha in (0,1), B/m->beta>0, the beta
small-cap large-deviation rate below its mean is

  -(1/m) log F_{a,b}(x) ->
       (1/2) KL(Ber(1-alpha) || Ber(x)),  x<1-alpha.

This follows directly from the beta density and Stirling's formula; the
log-density is increasing up to its mean, so the integral has the same
exponential rate as its upper endpoint. Let r be the unique value in
(0,1-alpha) satisfying

  KL(Ber(1-alpha) || Ber(r)) = 2 beta log 2.      (E)

For x<r, N F_{a,b}(x)->0; for x>r, N F_{a,b}(x)->infinity. The lower and
upper survival integrands in (B) therefore both converge to 1_{x<r}.
Dominated convergence proves D*->r. This supplies a sharp large-dimensional
phase curve and matches root's stronger conditional-entropy converse.
At fixed positive q and B/m->beta>0, the analogous beta-density estimate
gives D*->2^(-2 beta); this fixed-q regime is already in the 2007 source's
Theorem 3. The q=0 endpoint is different and must not be inferred by continuity.

An elementary finite budget consequence is also useful. For any rank-q
projection P, ||PU||^2 is chi-square(q). Its moment generating function gives

 E max_(j<=N) ||P_j U||^2 <= q+2 sqrt(q log N)+2 log N.

For completeness, log E exp[t(chi-square(q)-q)] <= q t^2/(1-2t),
0<t<1/2. The log-sum-exp bound and t=sqrt(log N)/(sqrt(q)+2sqrt(log N))
give the displayed expectation bound. Thus

  risk >= max(0,1-[q+2sqrt(q B log 2)+2B log 2]/m). (F)

The replay theorem makes this bound valid for arbitrary interactive protocols
in the model. In particular, q+B=o(m) implies risk->1. A logarithmic-size
control program and a constant number of readouts do not retain a constant
fraction of the unit prior target variance on fresh isotropic inputs.

5. Structured controls and meaningful alternatives

The root agent owns the fuller constructive-codebook analysis. The following
simple exact reference points are retained here to make the resource
comparison concrete.

SIGN CONTROL. For q=1, choose the line spanned by sign(U). A global sign
does not change the line, so m-1 bits suffice. Encoding/decoding takes O(m)
operations and the codebook has an implicit description; no exponential
table or nearest-neighbor search is needed. Its exact risk is

  (1-1/m)(1-2/pi).

Indeed, the captured normalized energy is E(sum_i |U_i|)^2/m^2
=1/m+(1-1/m)2/pi. This gives a constant risk reduction using one V read
and linear control, while respecting the lower bound. It is not claimed
optimal; at B/m->1 the best codebook limit is 1/4, versus 1-2/pi for signs.

KNOWN SOURCE GEOMETRY. If U belongs to a known r-dimensional subspace, its
effective Grassmann dimension is r. If it has M known possible directions,
ceil(log_2 M) bits select an exact one-read measurement. The source law or
representation is an earned resource; nothing here learns it for free.
Shared predictive state or reuse of the same U across many independent V's
can amortize the program bits. That is real reuse, not new evidence.

FULL INPUT. Reading all m coordinates of V requires zero controller bits and
gives exact F at the controller. This is an equally informed ordinary
alternative, and fixes the q=m endpoint. Sending a full finite-precision U
instead permits a local linear target read, with squared error controlled by
E||U-U_hat||^2/m. Gaussian rate-distortion theory already quantifies this
full-vector communication option; its leading high-resolution bit cost is
of the same dimension order as directional programming.

DIRECT TARGET. If the actuator already has U or can directly measure U^T V,
one read and zero new controller bits may suffice. That changes the interface:
U has been co-located, programmed earlier, or used in a target-specific
physical operation. The present converse does not prohibit that resource.

RANDOM CODEBOOK COST. The random-code upper bound stores N=2^B q-planes and
normally costs O(Nmq) storage/search arithmetic. A B-bit index by itself is
not evidence of an efficient controller. Realizing a stored or generated
direction still requires its coefficients to be loaded/programmed, and finite
precision errors must be charged. The lower bound allows arbitrary pre-shared
codebooks and unbounded computation, so these costs cannot weaken it.

For any nearby projection codebook with max_j||P_j-Ptilde_j||_op<=eta,
the normalized distortion of each codebook changes by at most eta, since
|U^T(P_j-Ptilde_j)U|<=eta||U||^2. This supplies an elementary way to charge
finite codebook precision; no exact stored arbitrary-real table is assumed
cheaply constructible.

6. One final noisy read: exact transfer of the control burden

If the controller learns Y=U+eta from m independent first reads of variance
s1 and the single V read has independent noise variance s2, use alpha_i=
1/(1+s_i). A selected unit codeword w gives Bayes predictor

  alpha_1 alpha_2 (Y^T w) Z/sqrt(m),   Z=w^T V+xi.

Choose the nearest codebook line to Y. Since Y is isotropic Gaussian, the
optimal B-bit one-read risk is exactly

  1-alpha_1 alpha_2 [1-D*_(m,1)(2^B)].             (G)

Thus the ideal noisy floor 1-alpha_1 alpha_2 has an additional term
alpha_1 alpha_2 times the directional quantization distortion. Equation (B)
provides exact computable lower/upper bounds on this extra cost. The claim
here is for one final V read; arbitrary repeated noisy q-read control is not
being equated to unweighted q-subspace quantization.

7. Useful failed attacks and model boundaries

* Reusing V readouts as free control: invalid as a bypass. For fixed full V,
  each controller word still defines one U-independent replayed subspace.
  Adaptive dependence on earlier V values is explicitly covered.
* Assuming the actual span is independent of U: false. The proof instead
  fixes V and enumerates counterfactual words, then bounds their minimum.
* Ignoring message timing/stopping/addresses: invalid. Arbitrary U-dependent
  real timing or direction metadata can encode U outside the B-bit budget.
* Extrapolating to expected query count: not proved here. Rare branches with
  many measurements are a different resource. Root treats expected control
  length separately, with hard q retained.
* Dropping Gaussian V: invalid in general. For finite-support V, an exact
  real linear read with appropriately chosen coefficients can encode its
  entire state; Gaussian residual-fiber covariance is essential.
* Treating a random-code existence bound as a polynomial-time algorithm:
  invalid. Cube-Split and ordinary product quantizers are stronger relevant
  constructive baselines than an exponential lookup table.
* Declaring the cap exponent novel: invalid. Unequal-dimensional Grassmann
  quantization already establishes it, including constants and asymptotics.

8. Primary-source map and exact scope of comparisons

1. W. Dai, B. Rider, Y. Liu, 'Unequal dimensional small balls and quantization
   on Grassmann Manifolds', arXiv:0705.2278 (2007).
   https://arxiv.org/html/0705.2278
   Read abstract, definitions, Theorem 1 small-ball volume, Theorem 2
   distortion constants, Theorem 3 proportional-rate limit, Corollary 4
   random-code result. With their source dimension p=1, real field, ambient
   dimension m and reconstruction dimension q, their distortion is exactly
   ours. This is direct prior art for the geometry, not merely an analogy.
   Their retrieved statement fixes p,q in its large-n theorem; equation (E)
   above gives the directly derived proportional-q beta specialization.
   No historical novelty is asserted for that specialization either.

2. A. Decurninge, M. Guillaud, 'Cube-Split: Structured Quantizers on the
   Grassmannian of Lines', arXiv:1610.08257 (2016).
   https://arxiv.org/html/1610.08257v1
   Read abstract, Sections III--IV including real-source construction.
   It explicitly addresses exponential codebook storage/search and constructs
   implicit grids with scalar-quantizer-like encode/decode complexity. The
   real construction sends the largest-coordinate cell index and quantizes
   d-1 local coordinates. It is an established structured alternative for
   q=1; no performance improvement over Cube-Split is asserted here.

3. M. Tavassolipour, S. A. Motahari, M. T. Manzuri Shalmani, 'Learning of
   Gaussian Processes in Distributed and Communication Limited Systems',
   arXiv:1705.02627 (later TPAMI publication).
   https://arxiv.org/html/1705.02627
   Read abstract, inner-product setup and Theorems 1--2 with proof passages.
   It reduces distributed inner-product error to covariance-weighted vector
   quantization, obtains a Gaussian reverse-water-filling characterization,
   and discusses practical per-symbol quantization. It is the correct
   full-vector communication alternative. It does not price an actuator's
   finite-bit choice of a small number of exact linear V measurements.

4. K. Balasubramanian, S. Bhandari, S. P. Kasiviswanathan, 'Query-Dependent
   Reading for Quantized Inner-Product Estimation: A Minimax Separation',
   arXiv:2610.24890 (October 2026).
   https://arxiv.org/html/2610.24890
   Read abstract, introduction and formal protocol/access definitions.
   It studies a record written before the query and charges stored bits and
   returned bit probes, while address selection may depend freely on the
   query and previous returned bits. Its criterion is worst-case unit-ball
   error, not our independent-Gaussian Bayes risk. Its query-dependent gains
   therefore do not contradict our theorem: control/address bits are not
   the priced resource in that model. No claim about its later proof validity
   is made by this scope comparison.

5. N. Alon, B. Klartag, 'Optimal compression of approximate inner products
   and dimension reduction'.
   https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/compression13.pdf
   Read abstract, Theorems 1.1--1.2 and related-work model passages. Its
   collection-of-points sketches preserve all specified inner products under
   uniform additive guarantees. Such guarantees and Gaussian normalized
   target MSE should not be interchanged: typical normalized inner products
   in high dimension live at a much finer accuracy scale than a constant
   additive error on unit vectors. This is a strong ordinary sketching
   alternative within its declared access and approximation contract.

6. T. Inoue, R. W. Heath Jr., 'Grassmannian Predictive Coding for Limited
   Feedback in Multiple Antenna Wireless Systems', arXiv:1105.5782 (2011).
   https://arxiv.org/html/1105.5782v1
   Read introductory limited-feedback model and motivation. It uses shared
   temporal prediction to reduce the description of successive channel
   directions, illustrating an established structured-source/reuse escape.
   This source is contextual; none of its unretrieved performance proofs
   are used as premises of the present theorem.

9. Executable evidence

control_cap_checks.py evaluates finite cap sandwiches, checks closed-form
special cases, verifies a genuinely interleaved replay example by Monte Carlo,
and reports one-read noisy-control bounds. It does not solve optimal codebook
design or benchmark a physical actuator. Its finite integration is a numerical
calculation, not a proof of the beta bounds. Prior-art identification and
the protocol proof are preserved independently of the executable outputs.
