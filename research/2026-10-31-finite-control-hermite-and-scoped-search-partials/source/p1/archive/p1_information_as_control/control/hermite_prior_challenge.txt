FINAL PRIMARY-SOURCE CHALLENGE: HERMITE ADAPTIVE SENSING
2026-10-30. Independent source audit; no historical novelty claim established.

QUESTION AUDITED

For independent U~N(0,I_r) and V~N(0,I_M), M=binom(r+p-1,p), consider the
variance-one known polynomial target

    F(U,V) = M^(-1/2) sum_{|a|=p} phi_a(U) V_a,

where the phi_a are orthonormal degree-p multivariate Hermite polynomials.
The construction reads U's r coordinates, then reads V in the known direction
phi(U), recovering F with r+1 exact linear measurements. The audited fixed-
information bound is explained variance <= (p+1)q/M for any fixed rank-q
linear experiment on the joint (U,V) space, including mixed directions.

SEARCH VERDICT

The exact Hermite family and arbitrary mixed-input fixed-experiment bound were
not found in an inspected prior primary source during this pass. This is a
bounded search outcome, not evidence of historical novelty. Classical
information-based complexity already treats adaptive linear information for
nonlinear targets, and Jackowski's average-case multilinear paper remains a
particularly close unresolved priority check. Its full body was not obtained.

1. READABLE PRIMARY SOURCE: THE CLASSICAL ADAPTIVITY FRAMEWORK

J. F. Traub, G. W. Wasilkowski, H. Wozniakowski,
When Is Nonadaptive Information as Powerful as Adaptive Information?,
CUCS-149-84; Proceedings of the 23rd IEEE Conference on Decision and Control
(1984), pp. 1536-1540.

Full institutional copy inspected:
https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/D8NV9S9H/download

Relevant locations: Section 3; Theorem 5.1; Examples 4.1 and 5.1; references
15-17. Section 3 explicitly models sequential linear functionals, with each
next functional chosen from earlier values. Theorem 5.1 concerns a continuous
LINEAR solution operator under Gaussian input and supplies a nonadaptive
experiment of equal cardinality with no larger average error. The survey also
gives a separate average-case adaptivity separation for recovering a random
step function from point evaluations; the prior is not Gaussian and the
allowed information there is pointwise. It discusses nonlinear targets as
outside the linear no-adaptivity conclusion.

Consequence for this project: the information model and the possibility of
nonlinear adaptivity gains are classical. This source does not supply the
Hermite construction or its arbitrary joint-linear fixed lower bound. The
Gaussian linear no-adaptivity theorem is not a contradiction: F here is a
degree-(p+1) nonlinear functional of the joint Gaussian input.

Information-Based Complexity: New Questions for Mathematicians,

Relevant locations in the archive's numbered OCR: lines 0174-0237 describe
information operations and charge their evaluation; lines 0606-0626 discuss
nonlinear average-case problems; reference 13 identifies Jackowski's paper.
The authors explicitly identify Jackowski's multilinear work as an existing
nonlinear average-case contribution. They also emphasize that permitted

This is a primary source confirming the close lineage. It does not reproduce

1991 statement about the state of nonlinear theory is not a present-day claim.

3. MODERN PRIMARY NEAR-MATCH, WITH A DIFFERENT UNKNOWN OBJECT

Ben Adcock, Michael Griebel, Gregor Maier,
The Sample Complexity of Learning Lipschitz Operators with respect to
Gaussian Measures, arXiv:2410.23440.

Full primary HTML inspected:
https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23440

Relevant locations: Section 1.2, Result 3; Section 4.1's sampling definitions;
Section 4.2, Definition 4.3 and main result. The paper studies recovery of an
unknown operator F from adaptive linear samples of F and measures worst-case
L2(Gaussian-input) reconstruction error over operator classes. It obtains an
exact adaptive-width characterization for the stated classes; Hermite
coefficient approximation achieves it, so adaptivity gives no improvement
there.

The distinction is mathematical, not just terminology. Their unknown is a
member of a class of functions/operators, and measurements are linear in that
unknown function. Here the polynomial target is supplied and the unknown is
one Gaussian vector (U,V), measured linearly before computing one target
value. Neither result is a substitute for the other. The modern paper does
not establish the claimed Hermite sensing separation or refute it.

5. DIRECT-TARGET ALTERNATIVE AND WHAT THE SEPARATION ACTUALLY MEANS

For a fixed coefficient vector V, define the polynomial random function

    f_V(u) = M^(-1/2) phi(u)^T V.

If the acquisition interface supplies native point evaluation f_V(u), then
after learning U the ordinary request f_V(U) returns the target in one value
query. The aligned V measurement is exactly that value query written in
coefficient coordinates, scaled to unit norm. A native target-value service
therefore ties the ideal adaptive protocol. There is no demonstrated faster
algorithm for evaluating a fully stored dense polynomial and no demonstrated
hardware speedup from the measurement count alone.

The valid consequence is conditional on the access interface: a small initial
observation can determine which linear functional of a much larger latent
coefficient vector matters. Fixed experiments must prepare for many mutually
orthogonal Hermite coefficient features, whereas a feedback experiment can
request the relevant functional after U is known. The proof prices scalar
linear observations. Control bits, feature generation, latent coefficient
access, and construction or learning of the supplied representation must be
charged separately. The companion control and learning notes do that for
specific restricted models; none grants a general discovery advantage.

6. CLAIM LANGUAGE RECOMMENDED FOR SYNTHESIS

Use: an independently proved and audited Hermite-family separation in the
declared Gaussian linear-measurement model; a structured finite-control
construction where separately proved; a classical information-based
complexity context; exact historical overlap with Jackowski still unresolved.

Do not use: first nonlinear adaptivity separation, first Gaussian adaptive
information principle, first polynomial sensing separation, new general
learning capability, runtime acceleration, or hardware advantage. Those
claims are not established by this source audit or by the measurement theorem.
