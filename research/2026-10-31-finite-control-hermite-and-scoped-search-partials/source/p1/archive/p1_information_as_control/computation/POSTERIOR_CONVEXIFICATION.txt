P1 FRONTIER COMPANION: ACQUIRED CURVATURE CERTIFICATES FOR POSTERIOR COMPUTATION
Scientific-computation branch. 2026-10-30.

STATUS AND DECISION

This is a complete elementary construction on an explicit promised class, with
an acquired global certificate, an exact measurement-count characterization,
finite-precision value-query requirements, and a polynomial posterior sampling
and normalization closure. It is useful reusable machinery, not presently a
candidate for a major new structural discovery. Its key steps are Hessian
calculus, finite differences, classical Schur-Horn frame completion, and
established strongly log-concave computation. No priority, practical speedup,
or general computational-hardness separation is claimed.

The concrete failure is prominent: the elementary fully charged normalizer
bound below asks for approximately 3.68e28 scalar values even for the executed
two-dimensional example. That budget was NOT run. Better established
normalizers, especially Ge--Lee--Lu, are the relevant ordinary machinery.

The strongest thing earned here is that the useful subspace need not be
supplied: in the specified zero-hidden-bias class, finitely many legal scalar
potential evaluations acquire a global certificate. The strongest thing NOT
earned is the class promise itself. Matching finitely many values cannot
establish that promise. A small required number of observations is a property
of the acquired curvature profile, not an assumption that the evaluator is
already given a small nonconvex block.

All new files are confined to p1_frontier/computation. Prior p1_next and
p1_research work is unchanged. No repository integration or push occurred.

1. SCIENTIFIC QUESTION AND ACCESS CONTRACT

Can acquiring a small number of real measurements of an unknown state make
the remaining posterior a computationally controlled object, even when the
unobserved prior is multimodal? Can the directions to measure be acquired,
rather than provided as a useful representation?

Let X in R^n have density proportional to exp(-V(x)), where the exact promise is

  V(x) = (1/2) x^T H x - sum_{j=1}^m a_j log cosh(v_j^T x),
  H positive definite, a_j >= 0.

The vectors v_j, weights a_j, and their number need not be supplied to the
algorithm. The input includes n, a valid upper bound Hmax on ||H||, a valid
bound Q4 >= sum_j a_j ||v_j||^4, and a scalar potential evaluator

  O_V(x,nu) returning a value with absolute error <= nu.

One call costs T_V(n,x,nu); this cost is retained. All n coordinates of a dense
query must be transmitted unless a documented sparse-input interface exists.
The ability to request arbitrarily small nu is a substantive resource. A
fixed precision floor gives only the floor stated below. No Hessian, gradient,
hidden features, prior normalizer, or prior sampler is supplied.

Polynomial oracle counts imply polynomial execution only when the supplied
evaluator itself has the corresponding polynomial precision/input cost and
the displayed numerical parameters are bounded appropriately. This is not
an unrestricted bit-complexity theorem for exponentially large binary-encoded
curvatures, noise precisions, or condition numbers.

The prior is normalizable because the positive quadratic dominates the at-most
linear growth of log cosh. Adding a known affine term causes no change to the
curvature theorem. Zero HIDDEN biases are essential: replacing v_j^T x by
v_j^T x+b_j does not preserve the one-anchor global certificate.

After calibration, the algorithm may measure the actual fresh state X:

  Y_l = u_l^T X + xi_l,   xi_l independent N(0,sigma^2), ||u_l|| <= 1.

Directions, precision, dense input traffic, and physical observations are paid.
Repeated measurements are allowed and cost separate reads. Let M have these
row vectors and ell rows. The posterior potential is

  W_y(x) = V(x) + ||Mx-y||^2/(2 sigma^2).

The target is to construct a measurement system making W_y eta-strongly convex
for every outcome y, then sample its posterior in W2 distance or estimate its
unnormalized conditional integral to prescribed log accuracy. The target is
not to reconstruct the original prior normalizer for free.

2. GLOBAL CERTIFICATE FROM ONE ANCHOR

Define C=sum_j a_j v_j v_j^T and H0=H-C. Direct differentiation gives

  Hess V(x)=H-sum_j a_j sech^2(v_j^T x) v_j v_j^T.

Consequently

  H0 <= Hess V(x) <= H for every x, and Hess V(0)=H0.                 (1)

All inequalities are in Loewner order. In particular, the global lower
curvature matrix is attained at a known anchor, even though its eigenspaces
and the hidden representation are unknown. This property, rather than a
finite local empirical Hessian fit, is what makes the certificate global.

For any fixed measurement matrix M, every posterior W_y is eta-strongly convex
if and only if

  H0 + sigma^(-2) M^T M >= eta I.                                  (2)

Sufficiency follows from (1); necessity follows by evaluating the Hessian at
x=0. The result is independent of y. This is an exact characterization of a
uniform strong-convexity certificate, not a characterization of when ANY
possible inference algorithm can run efficiently.

3. EXACT MINIMUM NUMBER OF UNIT-POWER GAUSSIAN READS

Let d_i be the positive eigenvalues of eta I-H0, let r be their count, and put

  S=sigma^2 sum_{i=1}^r d_i.

If r=0, ell*=0. Otherwise the minimum number of rows of norm at most one
satisfying (2) is exactly

  ell* = max{r, ceil(S)}.                                          (3)

Lower bound. Write R=M^T M. If rank(R)<r, its nullspace intersects the positive
eigenspace of eta I-H0, contradicting R>=sigma^2(eta I-H0). Thus ell>=r.
Projecting that inequality onto the positive eigenspace and taking the trace
gives tr R>=S. Since tr R=sum_l ||u_l||^2<=ell, ell>=ceil(S).

Construction. For ell=ell*, choose positive frame-operator eigenvalues

  c_i=sigma^2 d_i+(ell-S)/r,  i=1,...,r.

They sum to ell. Ordered decreasingly and padded with ell-r zeros, they
majorize ell ones: the first j<=r terms have sum at least j ell/r>=j, and
for j>r their sum is ell>=j. The real Schur-Horn theorem therefore gives ell
unit vectors in the positive eigenspace with frame operator diag(c_i).
Their rows form M. Its precision dominates sigma^2(eta I-H0)_+.

This frame result and its construction are classical; see Fickus, Marks and
Poteet, source [F]. A direct implementation starts with columns sqrt(c_i)e_i
and ell-r zero columns, then rotates a pair of remaining columns with squared
norms a>=1 and b<=1 so that one new column has norm one. Set

  cos^2(theta)=(1-b)/(a-b).

The other remaining column then has squared norm a+b-1. The unprocessed
columns remain mutually orthogonal; the global frame operator is unchanged.
Lock the unit column and repeat. This costs O(ell^2+r ell) arithmetic before
the O(n r ell) dense embedding, besides the O(n^3) eigendecomposition.
Writing the matrix itself costs Omega(n ell).

A simpler ordinary comparator repeats each deficient eigenvector
ceil(sigma^2 d_i) times. Its read count is less than S+r and it has a particularly
simple implementation. The frame construction saves only that rounding
overhead; neither construction is claimed to be new matrix theory.

For adaptive Gaussian measurements, the likelihood at a fixed full transcript
is still the product of the Gaussian likelihoods with the realized directions.
The terminal Hessian is therefore again Hess V + sigma^(-2) M^T M. The same
per-transcript lower bounds apply to a policy whose every terminal posterior
is globally eta-strongly convex. This does not rule out faster adaptive
inference using a weaker, outcome-dependent property.

Noiseless projections are different: observing the r deficient eigendirections
makes each residual fiber eta-strongly convex. The dimension r is minimal
among fixed subspaces required to give that certificate for every fiber,
because the fiber through zero supplies the compression lower bound. No claim
is made about the optimal outcome-adaptive noiseless policy.

4. FINITE SCALAR-VALUE ACQUISITION, INCLUDING PRECISION

The fourth derivative of log cosh has absolute value at most 2. Thus for every
direction u,

  |D^4 V(x)[u,u,u,u]| <= M4 ||u||^4,  M4=2 Q4.

For u=e_i and u=e_i+e_j evaluate

  D_h(u)=[O_V(hu,nu)-2 O_V(0,nu)+O_V(-hu,nu)]/h^2.

Set Hhat_ii=D_h(e_i) and

  Hhat_ij=(D_h(e_i+e_j)-D_h(e_i)-D_h(e_j))/2.

There are exactly Qcal=n^2+n+1 distinct scalar evaluations if the value at zero
and the coordinate-direction values are cached. Symmetry is not used to omit
negative queries. Taylor's theorem and the actual combined coefficients give

  |Hhat_ii-H0_ii| <= M4 h^2/12 + 4 nu/h^2,
  |Hhat_ij-H0_ij| <= M4 h^2/4  + 4 nu/h^2.

For the off-diagonal expression the total absolute value-error coefficient is
4/h^2, not 6/h^2: the shared value at zero cancels partially. Hence

  ||Hhat-H0||op <= n(M4 h^2/4+4 nu/h^2).                            (4)

For target enclosure e>0, take h^2=2e/(n M4) and

  nu <= e h^2/(8n)=e^2/(4 n^2 M4).                               (5)

Then the error in (4) is at most e. A dyadic h within a factor of two below this
choice preserves the bound if nu is chosen using the actual h; thus all
calibration points have finite exact representations. If M4=0, the potential
is quadratic and any chosen nonzero h has zero truncation bias.

The cost is Qcal scalar calls, O(n Qcal) dense coordinate traffic absent sparse
input support, O(n^2) working storage, and the evaluator's requested precision
cost. The requested absolute precision scales as e^2/(n^2 M4), not just e.
Values and intermediate cancellation require enough integer and fractional
bits; arithmetic is not an exact-real unit-cost claim.

If readout error has a fixed adversarial floor nu0, minimizing the right side
of (4) over h gives this method's floor 2n sqrt(M4 nu0). This is a floor of the
displayed certificate, not a claimed minimax lower bound for every estimator.
If independent centered sub-Gaussian value noise has variance proxy s_v^2,
averaging at each of Qcal sites to error nu with overall failure <=alpha costs
at least the stated sufficient repetition count

  ceil(2 s_v^2 nu^(-2) log(2 Qcal/alpha))

per site. Hardware bias cannot be averaged away.

Guarded sensor construction. From ||Hhat-H0||<=e, design for
Hhat+R >= (eta+e+g)I, where g>0 is a reserve for numerical implementation.
Then H0+R >=(eta+g)I. Eigenvalue signs near the threshold need not be decided
exactly. A validated eigendecomposition error is added to e. If an implemented
matrix Mtilde satisfies ||Mtilde-M||<=zeta, its precision perturbation is at most

  sigma^(-2)(2||M|| zeta+zeta^2).

Choose that quantity <=g and normalize or shrink implemented rows to retain
their power cap. The exact integer optimum (3) is a real-matrix theorem; a
finite implementation uses slack and must state its actual read count.

5. ORDINARY POSTERIOR SAMPLING, WITH VALUE-ONLY GRADIENT COST

Once the guarded construction succeeds, W_y is eta-strongly convex and L-smooth
with a valid choice

  L=Hmax+sigma^(-2)||M||^2.

The data y enter the location of the posterior, not this condition number.
At zero, grad W_y(0)=-sigma^(-2)M^T y. Thus the initial distance to its minimizer
is at most R=||M^T y||/(sigma^2 eta). Large observations retain bit-length and
centering costs.

There is no free gradient oracle. By Cauchy-Schwarz,

  sum_j a_j ||v_j||^3 <= sqrt(Q2 Q4),
  Q2=sum_j a_j ||v_j||^2=tr(H)-tr(H0)
     <= n Hmax-tr(Hhat)+n e.

The third derivative of log cosh has absolute value <1. Therefore M3=sqrt(Q2 Q4)
is a uniform bound on third directional derivatives in unit directions.
A central coordinate difference at step t gives a gradient error norm at most

  sqrt(n)(M3 t^2/6 + nu/t).                                       (6)

To achieve norm error b, take t^2=3b/(sqrt(n)M3) and nu<=b t/(2 sqrt(n)).
Use a smaller dyadic t and adjust nu when finite input representation matters.
Each gradient costs 2n scalar V evaluations plus the known quadratic
likelihood arithmetic O(n ell). For M3=0 the truncation bias vanishes.

A self-contained conservative Langevin bound is useful for making every cost
explicit. For a potential U with I<=Hess U<=kappa I and ||grad U(0)||<=1,
start at zero and use

  Z_{j+1}=Z_j-h ghat(Z_j)+sqrt(2h) Ghat_j.

Suppose the gradient error is uniformly <=b, and Ghat_j can be coupled to
independent standard Gaussians with L2 error <=zeta per vector. For h<=1/kappa,

  W2(Law(Z_K),pi_U)
    <= exp(-Kh) sqrt(2(n+1)) + 2 kappa sqrt(nh)
       + b + sqrt(2/h) zeta.                                    (7)

Proof. The deterministic update z-h grad U(z) contracts by 1-h. Couple to a
stationary Langevin diffusion using the same Brownian increments. Integration
by parts gives E||grad U(X)||^2=E Delta U(X)<=n kappa. Consequently

  ||X_s-X_0||L2 <= s sqrt(n kappa)+sqrt(2ns).

The integrated drift defect in one step is at most
kappa sqrt(n)[sqrt(kappa) h^2/2+(2 sqrt(2)/3)h^(3/2)]
<=2 kappa sqrt(n)h^(3/2). Sum the geometric recurrence, including h b and
sqrt(2h)zeta per step. Strong convexity and integration by parts imply
E||X-x_*||^2<=n and ||x_*||<=1, so E||X||^2<=2(n+1). This proves (7).

For W2 accuracy delta, sufficient parameters are

  h=min{1/kappa, delta^2/(64 kappa^2 n)},
  b<=delta/4,  zeta<=delta sqrt(h)/(4 sqrt(2)),
  K=ceil(h^(-1) log(max{1,4 sqrt(2(n+1))/delta})).                 (8)

Each of the four terms in (7) is then at most delta/4. Approximate Gaussian
numbers are coupled in W2; discrete rounded draws are NOT asserted close to
continuous Gaussians in total variation. Truncating standard normals at
B=O(sqrt(log(n/zeta))) and rounding to mesh O(zeta/sqrt(n)), with sufficiently
accurate finite probabilities, supplies this coupling with finite random bits.
Arithmetic perturbations can be charged to the uniform drift/update budget.

For the original posterior, find a center c with ||grad W_y(c)||<=sqrt(eta)
by ordinary inexact gradient descent and put

  U(z)=W_y(c+z/sqrt(eta))-W_y(c),  kappa=L/eta.

The center is not supplied. Gradient descent with step 1/L, gradient error
<=eta^(3/2)/(4L), and
O(kappa log(1+L R/sqrt(eta))) iterations suffices; a final approximate-gradient
check provides the certificate. Formula (6) pays for every gradient. Sampling
U to W2 error delta yields error delta/sqrt(eta) in x coordinates.

This derivation is deliberately conservative. Stronger conventional results
are already available: Dalalyan--Karagulyan [D], Theorems 1 and 4, give the
1.65 kappa sqrt(nh) bound and more refined inaccurate-gradient guarantees.
There is no new Langevin algorithm here.

6. FULL CONDITIONAL NORMALIZER: NO HIDDEN ORACLE

The following elementary closure establishes a finite polynomial algorithm.
It is much less efficient than established log-concave normalizers and is NOT
a performance claim. It also specifies exactly which normalizer is computed.

Use the centered and scaled U from Section 5. Let a=kappa-1 and

  F(z)=U(z)-||z||^2/2,
  U_t(z)=||z||^2/2+t F(z),  0<=t<=1.

All U_t have Hessian between I and kappa I and gradient at zero at most one.
Let pi_t be their normalized densities. Put M2=2(n+1), M4'=8(n+1)^2. For every t,

  E_t ||Z||^2<=M2,  E_t ||Z||^4<=M4'.

For the fourth moment, integration by parts about the minimizer gives
E||Z-z_*||^4<=(n+2)E||Z-z_*||^2<=n(n+2); then use ||z_*||<=1 and
(u+v)^4<=8(u^4+v^4). Also

  |F(z)|<=||z||+(a/2)||z||^2,
  Var_t F <= Q=4(n+1)+4a^2(n+1)^2.

Differentiating a normalizer under the integral gives

  log integral exp(-U) = (n/2)log(2 pi)-integral_0^1 E_t F dt,       (9)
  d(E_t F)/dt=-Var_t F.

The moments above dominate the derivatives, justifying differentiation.
For requested log accuracy epsilon in (0,1), take q=ceil(4Q/epsilon)
left-endpoint temperature intervals. Their deterministic integration error is
at most Q/(2q)<=epsilon/8.

To make sampling bounded and robust, let P_R project to the Euclidean radius-R
ball and use F_R(z)=F(P_R z), where

  R=max{1,(16 M4'/epsilon)^(1/3),sqrt(8 a M4'/epsilon)}.

Its range is contained in [-B,B], B=R+a R^2/2, and its Lipschitz constant is
D=1+aR. The truncation bias is at most

  M4'/R^3 + a M4'/(2R^2) <=epsilon/8.

At each temperature use independently restarted Langevin chains from (8) with
delta=epsilon/(8D). Their bias for F_R is <=epsilon/8. Take

  Ns=ceil(32 B^2 epsilon^(-2) log(2q/alpha))

independent outputs at each temperature. Hoeffding and a union bound give
sampling error <=epsilon/4 at all q temperatures with probability >=1-alpha.
Evaluate each F_R within epsilon/8 by scalar V evaluations; absolute precision
epsilon/16 suffices for each of the two potential values entering a difference.
Combining quadrature, tail, sampling-law, statistical, and value errors gives
at most 3epsilon/4 before the final center-value error. Require the stored
W_y(c) within epsilon/16, and the final arithmetic within epsilon/16.
The final error remains less than epsilon.

The computed original-scale integral is

  log Z_y = -W_y(c)-(n/2)log eta + log integral exp(-U).              (10)

The closure uses exactly q Ns independent chains of K steps, hence
2n q Ns K scalar V evaluations for gradient computations, plus q Ns objective
evaluations, calibration, and centering. Every quantity in this count is given
above; each value call has its requested precision from (6). The powers are
poor but polynomial in n,kappa,epsilon^(-1),log(alpha^(-1)), with the stated
evaluator and arithmetic costs. Rounding input points is controlled by the
known smoothness and a bounded iterate radius: truncated Gaussian innovations
and the contractive updates give such a radius recursively. Precision can be
increased until its extra gradient/value error fits the displayed budgets.

The strong ordinary comparator is Ge--Lee--Lu [G], Theorem 1.1: with value and
gradient access, multilevel underdamped Langevin gives

  Otilde((n^(4/3) kappa+n^(7/6) kappa^(7/6))/epsilon^2)

gradient-query complexity at constant success probability, with logarithmic
confidence amplification. Their annealing-with-MALA alternative costs
Otilde(n^2 kappa epsilon^(-2) max{1,sqrt(kappa/n)}).
Those are better real-oracle algorithms and must be preferred when their
finite-precision implementation is available. They are equally informed by
the acquired curvature certificate. The elementary closure above only makes
the scalar-access existence statement self-contained.

Important normalizer distinction. Formula (10) is the integral of the
UNNORMALIZED posterior density exp(-V(x)-||Mx-y||^2/(2sigma^2)). It suffices for
normalized posterior inference. The actual observation density is

  p_Y(y)=Z_y/[Z_prior (2 pi sigma^2)^(ell/2)].

The original prior normalizer Z_prior remains unknown. Real measurements do
not provide a free means to compute it, nor a free sampler for the prior.

7. WHY THE STRUCTURAL PROMISE CANNOT BE SILENTLY ACQUIRED

A finite-precision local Hessian history does not establish (1) outside the
promised class. A simple shifted-ridge attack in one dimension is

  V_B(x)=x^2/2-a[log cosh(v(x-B))-log cosh(-vB)-v tanh(-vB)x].

At zero V_B and its gradient agree with x^2/2. On any fixed finite bounded set
of query points the difference tends exponentially to zero as B tends to
infinity. Its curvature at B is 1-a v^2, which is negative if a v^2>1.
The fourth-derivative bound 2a v^4 is independent of B. Thus even a known
smoothness bound does not let a finite precision transcript certify global
convexity on the full line unless an additional global structure/domain
restriction is supplied. The potential remains normalizable.

The exact no-free-local-Hessian issue is also retained in Astra [A2]. Its C^5
bump construction keeps a finite gradient history unchanged while changing
the Hessian at a selected query. Known derivative bounds appropriately limit
the amplitude/scale; they do not turn local empirical agreement into a
membership certificate for the zero-bias model class.

8. COMPARATORS, WHAT CHANGED, AND WHAT DID NOT

1. Same-information conventional spectral design immediately uses H0 and the
   same frame construction. No superiority over it is claimed. Measurement
   design here is the ordinary best certificate design, not a learned planner.
2. Uniform coordinate measurements give the sufficient baseline
   n ceil(sigma^2 (eta-lambda_min(H0))_+) reads. Deficient-direction sensing can
   use fewer, but input calibration and dense linear-algebra costs are paid.
3. Koehler--Lee--Risteski [K] already sample and normalize approximately low-rank
   Ising models without acquiring a real observation of the target state.
   Their Theorem 1.1 depends polynomially on n with exponent O(d_++1), and
   exponentially on the summed magnitudes of negative outliers. In the
   positive-semidefinite case it is polynomial for fixed outlier count.
   Thus a small nonconvex sector is not evidence of an unknown computational
   barrier. Their latent-Gaussian/Hubbard--Stratonovich techniques are a close
   conventional comparator for the present logcosh family.
4. Cui--Yu--Liu [C] already connect Gaussian side information and posterior
   sampling through stochastic localization/AMP in random linear models.
   Their input is an iid Gaussian design and bounded product prior, and their
   conclusion is asymptotic smoothed KL below an AMP threshold; Wasserstein
   convergence additionally assumes a dimension-free posterior covariance
   bound. They do not supply the present worst-case finite one-anchor
   certificate. Their work nevertheless prevents treating Gaussian
   observation-induced simplification as a new principle.
5. Astra [A1] already earns a common high-frequency covariance bound uniform
   over fields, jointly strongly convex bulk/fast Gaussian coordinates, and a
   paid normalizer with a fixed nonconvex sector. That construction integrates
   the original quantum normalizer; this companion conditions on real data
   and therefore changes the posterior target. It is not an improvement of
   [A1]'s original-normalizer theorem.

9. EXECUTION BOUNDARY

posterior_certificate.py implements value-only Hessian calibration, the
classical unit-frame construction, guarded measurement design, and checks of
the resulting curvature inequality. A small hidden-feature model is used only
by its evaluator and by an independently charged exact finite-mixture
reference. The code also implements value-only gradient evaluation and a
Langevin kernel and provides all conservative general-normalizer budget
numbers. Results are in posterior_results.json.

Random Hessian probes in the numerical checks are diagnostic checks of the
formula, not the global proof. Floating-point residuals are numerical checks,
not outward-rounded hardware certificates. The general normalizer's full
worst-case budget is computed and reported; it is not executed or described
as a practical algorithm. The explicit finite-mixture reference has its
enumeration cost paid, but uses the generator's hidden features. It is
validation only, not a same-information speed comparison to the value-only
construction. Same-information ordinary spectral design ties the construction.
