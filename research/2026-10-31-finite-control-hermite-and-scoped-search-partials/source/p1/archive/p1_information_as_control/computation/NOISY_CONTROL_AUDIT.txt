INDEPENDENT AUDIT: FINITE CONTROL, NOISY GAUSSIAN READS, AND A GENERAL FIRST STAGE
Scientific-computation branch, 2026-10-30.

VERDICT

The proposed exact noisy replay reduction is valid under the access contract
below. The general first-stage decomposition is also valid. The essential
corrections are explicit decoder knowledge, charged control timing, absence
of uncharged correlated actuator side information, and separation of
algorithmic random seeds from the measurement-noise innovations. The replay
freeze includes the innovations only as a mathematical coupling; the posterior
calculation must never reveal those innovations to the predictor.

The intended output is at the controller, or at a decoder retaining the same
first-stage information. The actuator only implements sensing directions and
returns its readouts. This audit does not broaden the theorem to an
actuator-only predictor.

1. CONTRACT THAT MAKES THE THEOREM PRECISE

V~N(0,I_n). C is an R^n-valued random variable independent of V with
E||C||^2<infinity. The controller knows C. The actuator initially has no
C-dependent information except a declared public context Z, if present.
For the unconditional version Z is absent or independent of C.

The actuator can take at most q measurements

  Y_t=u_t^T V+xi_t,  ||u_t||<=1,
  xi_1,...,xi_q iid N(0,s),  s>0.

The innovation sequence is external measurement noise, independent of C,V,
and all algorithmic random seeds. Its realizations are not available to either
party except through the readouts. Directions are predictable from the
actuator's received control bits, previous legal readouts and its algorithmic
seeds. Feedback can return any function of these readouts and seeds, including
the entire readout record, but no extra information about V.

The control protocol has at most B binary controller branches on every path,
so a fixed replay has at most 2^B complete control words. Fixed-length padded
words with a public state deciding when to request the next bit are a
convenient equivalent implementation. Any controller-dependent timing,
stopping, measurement requests, addresses, metadata, or message boundaries
must be included in this control alphabet. A bare payload-length bound with
free termination is insufficient.

Hard q and B bounds hold. Counterfactual branches can be totalized by stopping
or capping at q reads and B bits. This changes no actual admissible behavior
and gives a feasible replay for every complete word. The theorem is not an
expected-length or expected-number-of-measurements theorem.

The final decoder retains C, the complete readout transcript, directions, and
the protocol data necessary to calculate the posterior. Giving it additional
ALGORITHMIC seeds can only help the lower bound. This does not include xi.
One-way controller bits are priced here; exact real readout feedback is a
different channel. Finite return precision is an additional resource to pay.

2. THE GAUSSIAN POSTERIOR IDENTITY

Fix C=c and all algorithmic random seeds R. Along a realized transcript, let
M have the realized rows u_t and let y contain the measured outputs. All
controller bits are now deterministic functions of earlier readouts, and all
directions are predictable. The sequential likelihood at this transcript is,
as a function of v,

  product_t (2 pi s)^(-1/2) exp(-(y_t-u_t^T v)^2/(2s)).

Indicators that the protocol follows the realized branches depend on c,R,y;
they do not introduce an additional v-dependent factor. Stopping based on the
record does not alter that point. Bayes' rule with the standard Gaussian prior
therefore gives, almost surely,

  V | C,transcript,R ~ N(mu,P),
  P=(I+M^T M/s)^(-1),  mu=P M^T y/s.                         (1)

The conditional Bayes variance of C^T V is C^T P C. Thus any admissible
predictor has expected risk at least

  E[ C^T (I+M(C,V,xi,R)^T M(C,V,xi,R)/s)^(-1) C ].             (2)

If the decoder retains the full information in (1), its Bayes estimator
C^T mu attains (2). Unknown algorithmic seeds can only make its actual risk
larger, so using (2) is safe for a universal lower bound.

WARNING: conditioning on xi in (1) would make the observations noiseless.
For example, Y=u^T V+xi together with xi reveals u^T V exactly. The noisy
covariance in (1) is not the covariance conditional on all replay variables.

3. REPLAY AND EXACT STATIC CODEBOOK REDUCTION

Only after obtaining (2), set omega=(V,xi_1,...,xi_q,R) and fix its value for
the replay argument. For each complete word w, simulate the actuator, supplying
the bits of w when requested and the response u_t^T V+xi_t at its t-th read.
This produces a feasible row matrix M_w(omega). It is independent of C because
the actuator has no C input other than the supplied word. The same omega is
used across all counterfactual words. There are at most N=2^B matrices.

For the actual word w(C,omega), define

  ell(c,M)=c^T(I+M^T M/s)^(-1)c.

Pointwise,

  ell(C,M_actual)>=min_w ell(C,M_w(omega)).                    (3)

Omega is independent of C. For each fixed omega, averaging (3) over C gives

  E_C min_w ell(C,M_w(omega))
     >= inf_{M_1,...,M_N feasible} E_C min_j ell(C,M_j).        (4)

Averaging omega proves the universal lower bound. No false assertion that
the ACTUAL matrix is independent of C occurs in this proof.

For the converse, fix any feasible static codebook. The controller computes
the minimizing index j(C), sends it before sensing, and the actuator takes its
rows and returns the readouts. The decoder evaluates C^T mu with (1).
This achieves the codebook objective. Consequently

  optimal interactive risk
    = min_{M_1,...,M_(2^B) feasible}
        E_C min_j C^T(I+M_j^T M_j/s)^(-1) C.                  (5)

One may write inf instead of min without needing an existence argument.
For rows of norm <=1, append zero rows to make each matrix q by n. The feasible
matrix set is compact. Since 0<=ell(c,M)<=||c||^2 and ell is continuous,
dominated convergence makes the expected codebook objective continuous.
Hence a minimum exists. If every actual read must have unit rather than
at-most-unit power, short branches can instead be padded by additional unit
reads; extra independent data can only lower their risk. The optimal static
design can use all q reads.

This does not compute an optimal codebook economically. The source law,
codebook storage, numerical matrix construction, index search, and final
posterior evaluation remain separate costs. The lower bound grants arbitrary
pre-shared codebooks and computation, which strengthens rather than weakens it.

Independent but unequal fixed Gaussian noise variances s_t>0 admit the same
proof, with P=(I+M^T diag(s_t^(-1))M)^(-1) and the variances indexed by read
number. General non-Gaussian independent noise does not have covariance (1).
Correlated read noise also needs a separate conditional-innovation analysis;
the iid formula must not be copied into it.

4. PUBLIC CONTEXT AND FIRST-STAGE RANDOMNESS

If the actuator is given public context Z correlated with C, require
V independent of (C,Z) and the physical noises independent of everything else.
Condition on Z=z before replay. The appropriate optimum is

  E_Z [ inf_{M_1(z),...,M_N(z)}
           E[ min_j ell(C,M_j(z)) | Z ] ].                   (6)

The codebook is allowed to depend on the already available context. If desired,
use infima over measurable codebook families. A countable dense family of
rational feasible matrices gives measurable epsilon-minimizers by selecting
the first candidate within epsilon of the measurable infimum. Thus the
conditional expression does not require an unproved selection oracle for
the equality of infima; computational implementation is still not supplied.

Public random seeds generated BEFORE a first stage are a subtle case. Even
if a seed J is initially independent of X, the resulting conditional coefficient
C=E[a(X)|H,J] can be correlated with J, because J chooses which information H
is acquired. Such a seed is public context, not automatically randomization
independent of the final C. One must condition on it as in (6), or fix its
value when specifying the first-stage problem.

5. GENERAL FIRST-STAGE DECOMPOSITION: FULL PROOF

Let X be independent of V~N(0,I_n), with

  f(X,V)=g(X)+a(X)^T V,
  E[g(X)^2]+E||a(X)||^2<infinity.

Let H be the controller's first-stage record. The crucial structural condition
is that (X,H) is jointly independent of V and of all subsequent physical
readout noise/algorithmic coins. H may be generated by legal noisy observations
of X. The controller receives no additional X information after H. The final
decoder retains H; actuator access to H-derived information is charged through
the same control budget, except declared public context.

Set

  mu_g(H)=E[g(X)|H],  C(H)=E[a(X)|H],
  delta_g=g-mu_g,     delta_a=a-C,
  R0=delta_g+delta_a^T V.

Every subsequent legal transcript T is measurable in (H,V,xi,algorithmic
coins). Conditional on H, the residual X uncertainty is independent of these
new variables. Hence

  E[R0 | H,V,xi,coins]=0.                                     (7)

For ANY measurable predictor fhat(H,T), the cross term with R0 therefore
vanishes, yielding the exact orthogonal decomposition

  E[(f-fhat)^2]
    = E[R0^2] + E[(mu_g(H)+C(H)^T V-fhat)^2].                 (8)

Because E[V|H]=0 and E[VV^T|H]=I,

  E[R0^2]
    = E Var(g|H)+E tr Cov(a|H) =: J0.                       (9)

The cross covariance Cov(g,a|H) need not vanish. Its contribution to R0^2 is
2 Cov(g,a|H)^T V and vanishes only after averaging V. Likewise, conditional
on a realized later transcript, the irreducible variance need not equal the
constant J0. Equations (8)--(9) are the averaged exact decomposition; no
pointwise constant-residual-variance claim is needed or generally true.

Conditional on H and algorithmic coins, the Gaussian likelihood argument from
Section 2 applies. The optimal controller predictor is

  mu_g(H)+C(H)^T P M^T y/s.

The remaining risk is E[C(H)^T P C(H)]. Even if the control protocol uses all
of H rather than only C(H), replay constructs matrices independent of H.
The loss depends on H only through C. Thus the exact optimal total risk is

  J0 + min_{N=2^B static matrices} E_H min_j ell(C(H),M_j).     (10)

With public context Z, replace H by the full decoder record (H,Z) in mu_g,C,J0,
and use the conditional codebook expression (6). If H or Z contains V
information, the stated prior-I decomposition is not valid without a revised
conditional Gaussian model. If later actions acquire more X information,
J0 is no longer generally irreducible.

Neither mu_g nor C is acquired for free by this identity. For a generic
implicit model, evaluating either conditional expectation can be the original
hard inference problem. A Hermite family with explicitly available Gaussian
conditional moments earns the formula in that family; it does not establish
an arbitrary-model conditional-mean oracle.

6. THREE SMALL ACCESS COUNTEREXAMPLES

These are warnings about excluded models, not proposed extensions of the
intended theorem.

(i) If the final actuator alone must predict, take n=q=1, B=0, C uniform on
{-1,+1}. The actuator sees Y=V+xi and no sign information. Its best prediction
of C V is zero and its risk is 1. A controller retaining C has risk s/(1+s),
the value in (5). Thus the output-location condition is mathematically needed.

(ii) If free termination allows the three B=1 strings empty, 0 and 1, take
n=3, q=1, and C uniform on {e_1,e_2,e_3}. Those three strings select the three
coordinate directions and give risk s/(1+s). With only two legal control words,
the best risk is 1-2/[3(1+s)]: for unit directions u_1,u_2,
sum_i max_j u_(j,i)^2<=2, with equality for two coordinate directions.
The gap is 1/[3(1+s)]. A proper prefix-free/fixed schedule removes this loophole.

(iii) If the actuator is given Z and C=e_Z, with Z uniform on {1,2}, n=2,
q=1,B=0, a context-dependent read achieves s/(1+s). An unconditional one-matrix
codebook has best risk 1-1/[2(1+s)]. Formula (6), rather than (5), is correct.

7. ADDITIONAL SANITY CHECKS AND PRECISION INTERFACE

For q=0, (5) is E||C||^2. For B=0,q=1, its optimum is

  E||C||^2-lambda_max(E[CC^T])/(1+s).

For a deterministic nonzero C, repeating the same unit direction C/||C|| for
all q reads attains ||C||^2 s/(s+q). This confirms that q noisy reads cannot
generally be replaced by an unweighted rank-q subspace: repeats matter.

The function ell(c,M) is between 0 and ||c||^2. If a one-shot encoder has an
approximation Chat with E||C-Chat||^2=delta^2, then for any fixed codebook,

  E[ell(C,M_selected(Chat))-min_j ell(C,M_j)]
    <=4 sqrt(E||C||^2) delta+2 delta^2.                       (11)

Indeed, for every P_j in [0,I],
|C^T P_j C-Chat^T P_j Chat|<=2||C||||C-Chat||+||C-Chat||^2;
using the inequality twice and Cauchy-Schwarz proves (11).
If the final decoder also substitutes Chat and an approximation muhat_g,
then, for that one-shot matrix chosen from H, the further error is at most
delta^2+E|mu_g-muhat_g|^2. Conditional on H, the posterior mean of V has
covariance I-P and mean zero, proving this claim. These bounds offer an honest
way to pay approximate conditional-mean computation; they do not compute it.

The noisy matrix posterior is a conventional Gaussian calculation. The new
candidate, if historically distinct, is the interactive replay reduction.
This audit establishes its internal validity under the stated model, not
historical novelty. Primary comparison remains with root's/control branch's
source ledger; no new priority claim is made here.

8. EXECUTABLE INDEPENDENT CHECK

noisy_control_checks.py uses 100,000 independent replay draws. Its genuinely
interleaved example takes one noisy V read, sends a coefficient/readout-
dependent bit, and chooses the second read direction using that bit and the
first readout. The same V and external noise innovations are reused across
the two counterfactual words. Observed prediction MSE is 0.4491337 versus
mean posterior-covariance risk 0.4490451, a paired difference of 0.052 standard
errors. The replay minimum is pointwise no larger than the actual conditional
cost in every draw.

A separate example uses H=X+sqrt(0.7)Z, a(X)=(X,X^2-1), and
g(X)=X^2+0.3X. Conditional Gaussian moments supply C and mu_g explicitly.
It uses a one-bit choice between two coordinate reads, including the nonzero
conditional Cov(g,a|H) terms in its independently calculated risk. The total
risk and J0 checks differ from prediction by 2.009 and 2.064 paired standard
errors respectively. This is ordinary Monte Carlo variation, not numerical
proof. The exact equations in Sections 2--5 are the proof.

The fixed seed, all means, paired standard errors, and the closed-form access
counterexamples are retained in noisy_control_checks_results.json. No optimal
general codebook search or physical actuator is benchmarked by these checks.
