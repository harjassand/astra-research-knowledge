The strongest result is an exact connection between feedback and the cost of describing the remaining experiment

I pursued the foundational route: early information can be valuable because it gives a short description of the measurement that should come next. The relevant complexity can be much smaller than the number of coefficients involved in the target—even when their covariance has full rank and treats every direction equally.

This continuation produced three connected results:

An exact value theorem: under an explicit conditional-linear Gaussian model, arbitrary interaction with a finite control budget has exactly the value of a static finite collection of measurement designs. The proof does not construct the comparator policy.
A constructive separation: a nonlinear coefficient family needs only a small incoming description, yet every fixed linear experiment needs polynomially more measurements to achieve comparable final accuracy. The separation survives noisy reads, packed messages, finite actuator weights, and a finite final readout.
A discovery boundary: recognizing even one globally affine direction of an arbitrary supplied cubic is NP-hard, with an inverse-polynomial gap. The useful representation in the construction is therefore a substantive resource.

The result establishes a measurement-and-control capability within its declared interface. A practical advantage in total cost remains unresolved: the actuator still generates and applies a dense pattern, and an ordinary native direct-target evaluator performs the same conditional operation.

1. The capability: acquire a description of the right experiment

Suppose an early observation reveals which linear functional of a much larger unknown state matters. The subsequent task might involve thousands of coefficients, but those coefficients could be generated from a much smaller set of variables.

There are then several distinct costs:

acquiring the early information;
computing the remaining task’s coefficients;
describing the chosen measurement to the actuator;
generating and applying the actuator’s pattern;
acquiring and decoding the resulting observation.

A small description can reduce communication or measurement costs while leaving substantial local computation. Keeping those costs separate is essential.

The organizing object I obtained is the distribution of the remaining task coefficient, measured by how well feasible experiments can serve it. Covariance suffices for a large class of fixed-experiment comparisons. It does not suffice once an experiment can be selected from early information.

The underlying conditional-linear representation is established. Hastie and Tibshirani’s varying-coefficient models explicitly allow coefficients of one group of variables to depend on another group. The construction here uses that representation and proves a sensing comparison within it; it does not claim to have invented conditional linearity. Primary source

The complete isolated replay passed all eight programs.
