## 1. Chronological weighted acquisition passes

For a stationary reversible baseline source, the conditional mean of a baseline
test read on the reversed past, given the present hidden state, equals the forward
future-test function f_i of that state. This uses even reversal parity and the
actual baseline law. Independently chosen clocks and randomization can be
integrated into the test. Conditioning at the center state then gives exactly

    E[Phi_i^past w(Y_0) Phi_j^future] = <f_i, M_w T f_j>_pi.

The experiment is chronological: baseline past, controlled transition T, baseline
future. No restart at the center hidden state and no duplicate future from that
state are needed. The pulse used to estimate a particular matrix is fixed by the
experimental design; dependence on the sampled past would instead require the
corresponding declared causal kernel.

Weighting the center by h(Y_0)^2 supplies the unnormalized inner product for
pi h^2 without physically preparing the h-biased stationary distribution. This
remains valid for a reducible source: the experiment starts from the supplied
baseline stationary mixture, and the weight is an identity of measured moments.
It does not imply free stationary preparation or that a long run creates the
desired mixture.

Independent exponential durations give the resolvent S_h. Applying two such
durations successively, with the same bias and no intervention at their boundary,
gives S_h^2. The weighted self-adjointness therefore makes D_h the true squared
conditional-action Gram. Squaring one random future report would include
trajectory variance and would not supply this object.

## 3. Controlled future features have a forward physical Gram acquisition

This section independently checks the completed extension at its listed hash.

Restrict generated features to finite operator words O_i acting on constant1.
Primitive factors are visible multiplication operators and warranted physical
pulse resolvents. A word is evaluated as an ordinary chronological controlled
test; no hidden f_i value is read. The pulse adjoint in baseline L2(pi) is

    S_h^* = H_h^2 S_h H_h^-2.

This follows from H_h^2 S_h=S_h^* H_h^2. Thus the adjoint word is implemented by
reversing factor order and replacing each pulse by: visible initial weight h^2,
the SAME forward physical pulse, and visible final weight h^-2. This is a
weighted report from one forward trajectory. It is not an inverse stochastic
evolution, a negative-time pulse, a hidden conditional restart, or cloning.

Starting from baseline pi, the chronological word

    O_i^* M_w T O_j

has expectation <1,O_i^* M_w T O_j1>_pi, which equals the required inner product
<f_i,M_w T f_j>_pi. It therefore supplies the controlled-feature Gram and the
zero/one/two-pulse moment matrices. Ordinary baseline time reversal by itself
does NOT supply controlled future-feature products; the endpoint weights are
essential.

All factors must retain their actual observation and pulse interface. Positive
h on finitely many labels makes every inverse endpoint weight bounded. However,
the product of these weights can grow with word length. For a pulse ratio at
most R, each adjoint pulse contributes a worst-case report multiplier R^2;
multiple pulses can therefore produce poor statistical conditioning. The exact
identity is not a cost bound independent of feature complexity.

construction. In the population setting, iterating this enlargement terminates
