## 2. An explicit weak force-balance residual

Let rho(q,p) be a source stationary density, nu its position marginal, and

    r(q,p)=grad_p log[rho(q,p)/(nu(q) M_tau(p))],
    sigma=gamma tau E_rho |r|^2,
    C_p=A/gamma+sqrt(d tau).                                      (2)

The stationary moment bound gives (E|p|^2)^(1/2)<=C_p.
For any smooth periodic vector field phi(q), stationarity applied to
phi(q).p and momentum integration by parts give the exact identity

    <F nu-tau grad nu,phi>
       =gamma E[phi.p]-E[(pp^T-tau I):D phi]
       =tau E[r.(gamma phi-(D phi)p)].                          (3)

Consequently

    |<F nu-tau grad nu,phi>|
       <=sqrt(tau sigma/gamma)
                      [gamma ||phi||_infty+C_p ||D phi||_infty]. (4)

The matrix norm is the Euclidean operator norm. Cutoff removal uses the
finite Fisher domain and stationary momentum moments, not derivatives of F.
The independent derivation improves the square-bracket sum to
sqrt(gamma^2||phi||_infty^2+C_p^2||D phi||_infty^2): the cross term vanishes
because stationarity of |phi(q)|^2 gives E[phi.(D phi)p]=0.
The weaker displayed sum already suffices below. This distributional error
is NOT an L2 bound on F+grad U and does not contradict the shear failure.

## 3. A source-independent lower mass bound

Normalize uniform position measure u_T to mass one and fix any t0>0.
Under FREE Kramers motion started at any (q0,p0), position at t0 is a
shifted wrapped Gaussian with displacement noise variance

    v_t0=(tau/gamma^2)
          [2gamma t0-3+4 exp(-gamma t0)-exp(-2gamma t0)]>0.        (5)

Its density relative to u_T has a positive minimum m_t0. The nearest
unwrapped Gaussian term on [-pi,pi]^d gives the explicit lower value

    m_t0 >= (2pi)^(d/2) v_t0^(-d/2)
                                  exp[-d pi^2/(2v_t0)].         (6)

Mean shifts do not alter this lower bound. Let P be the forced path law
started at its stationary rho and P0 the free path law with the SAME
initial law. For Z=dP/dP0, bounded Girsanov yields

    E_P Z^(-2)=E_P0 Z^(-1)
                         <=exp[A^2 t0/(2gamma tau)].             (7)

For any position set E, Cauchy--Schwarz gives
P0(q_t0 in E)^2 <= P(q_t0 in E) E_P Z^(-2). Stationarity implies

    nu(E)>=c_mass u_T(E)^2,
    c_mass=exp[-A^2 t0/(2gamma tau)] m_t0^2>0.                    (8)

There is no hidden mixing or lower density hypothesis and no momentum
preparation in this argument. The estimate is not a pointwise uniform
lower bound on nu. Its constants use only native class parameters.

## 4. An explicit bounded-force Gibbs position approximation

Choose a nonnegative smooth Euclidean kernel kappa supported in the unit
ball, normalized for Lebesgue measure, and bounded below by kappa_min>0
on the ball of radius 1/2. For 0<delta<min(1,pi), periodize

    k_delta(q)=(2pi)^d delta^(-d) kappa(q/delta),
    nu_delta(q)=int k_delta(q-x) nu(dx),                         (9)

so nu_delta is a density relative to u_T. Set

    K0=(2pi)^d ||kappa||_infty,
    K1=(2pi)^d ||grad kappa||_infty,
    c_* =c_mass kappa_min v_d^2/[2^(2d)(2pi)^d],                 (10)

where v_d is the Euclidean unit-ball volume. Applying (8) to a ball of
radius delta/2 gives the uniform positive bound nu_delta>=c_* delta^d.
Convolving (4), and testing against e k_delta(q-.) for unit vectors e,
gives

    |(F nu)*k_delta-tau grad nu_delta|
       <=sqrt(tau sigma/gamma)
                   [gamma K0 delta^(-d)+C_p K1 delta^(-d-1)].    (11)

Since |(F nu)*k_delta|<=A nu_delta, the ordinary smoothed Boltzmann
potential U_delta=-tau log nu_delta has

    ||grad U_delta||_infty<=A+epsilon_delta,
    epsilon_delta=(sqrt(tau sigma/gamma)/c_*)
               [gamma K0 delta^(-2d)+C_p K1 delta^(-2d-1)].      (12)

Assume A>0; A=0 is already the free Gibbs source. Rescale

    U_tilde=[A/(A+epsilon_delta)] U_delta.                       (13)

It is smooth with gradient ceiling A. The oscillation of
U_delta-U_tilde is at most epsilon_delta D, where D is the torus diameter.
If a Gibbs potential changes by a function with oscillation L_energy,
Hoeffding's lemma applied to its exponential tilt gives
D(old_density||new_density)<=L_energy^2/(8tau^2).
Pinsker therefore gives TV<=L_energy/(4tau). Hence

    W1(nu,nu_U_tilde)
       <=delta+D min{1, D epsilon_delta/(4tau)}.                (14)

The first term couples each configuration to its smoothing displacement;
the second uses W1<=D TV. This is a quantitative static-fit construction
WITHOUT a force derivative bound. In fixed physical units, choosing delta
proportional to s^(1/(4d+4)) for a small entropy ceiling s gives an
O(s^(1/(4d+4))) bound. Neither exponent nor constants are claimed optimal.
Dimension, geometry and all native parameters remain fixed.
