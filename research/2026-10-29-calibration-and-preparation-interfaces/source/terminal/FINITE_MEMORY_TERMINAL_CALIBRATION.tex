\begin{abstract}
An autonomous finite-state stochastic program can have stable conditional
terminal-output laws even when execution takes an enormous time and the
terminal evidence is extremely rare. A classical absorption-forest identity
turns this statement into a bounded change of measure. If the log-likelihood
ratio of forest weights has range $\Delta$, every conditional terminal-output
law changes in total variation by at most $\tanh(\Delta/4)$. The range admits
polynomial-time certified approximation by integer arborescence optimization.
With independent row sampling and a promised lower bound $\beta$ on positive
local probabilities, calibration for every controller with at most $K$
product states takes $O(K^2\log(M/\delta)/(\beta\varepsilon^2))$ samples per
source row. A three-symbol source and a gambler's-ruin controller give a
matching $\Omega(K^2/(\beta\varepsilon^2))$ lower bound at constant confidence.

These are scoped deductions and an executable construction on classical
foundations. Historical novelty is not established. This is not a claimed
computational separation, a solution to general hidden-model acquisition,
or a new canonical publication. No external review or formal proof-checker
receipt is asserted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, opportunity selection, and status}

The selected question is: \emph{Can the numerical precision needed to reuse
an acquired stochastic model depend on the memory of the questioning
program rather than its execution time or the rarity of its evidence?}
A positive answer would allow one calibrated model to answer terminal
questions whose direct executions are prohibitively long. It would not
remove the cost of discovering that model.

Two different routes were screened. Active experimental design and
intervention learning were compared with the Astra N744 acquired-flux
construction and ordinary generator fitting and occupation-measure methods.
The N744 rank-one microscopic-source assumption cannot be silently extended
to general hidden interventions. Compact sequence-model learning was
compared with conditional-sampling HMM learning \cite{mahajan}; conditional
queries and latent-state resets are additional access. The current N25
acquisition-interface capsule also records failure of transferring
actual-path guarantees to spliced queries. Neither route earned a broad
acquisition claim from the evidence inspected.

An attempted direct theorem for arbitrary fixed-time marginals remains
incomplete. Exploratory floating-point probes are preserved separately;
they are not a proof of an all-time inequality. The completed result below
concerns autonomous stopping and terminal evidence. Section~\ref{sec:deadline}
gives a failure when an external clock is omitted from the state count.

The conventional route is strong. Absorption forests are classical
\cite{leighton,chatterjee}; same-support robustness and global model
learning have substantial prior literature \cite{chatterjee,bazille}.
The implemented predictor is the ordinary exact absorbing-chain linear
solve. No exclusive computational advantage is claimed. The useful output
here is a complete derivation, a sharp change-of-measure bound, a
computable certificate, a uniform reuse theorem with explicit acquisition
assumptions, and a matching acquisition obstruction.

\section{Explicit finite representation}

Let $V$ contain $K$ transient states and let $B$ be a finite terminal set.
Let $P,\widehat P$ be row-stochastic kernels on $V\cup B$, with absorbing
terminals and the same positive off-diagonal support in rows of $V$.
Every transient state has a path to $B$ in that support. Absorption
therefore occurs almost surely in each finite homogeneous chain.
The initial distribution $\mu$ is shared. Write $\tau$ for the first
terminal visit and $Z$ for the terminal label.

The state count includes physical or acquired model state, controller
memory, and every monitor needed to define the question. A circuit with
$b$ state bits may have $2^b$ configurations; polynomial cost in $K$
is not polynomial cost in $b$. A signed linear representation is not
automatically a positive kernel of the same size.

If nontermination is possible, identify bottom strongly connected
components disjoint from the terminals and replace them by failure
terminals. This support-based reduction preserves original terminal
probabilities and makes the remaining states transient. It is valid in
both models because support agrees. The supplied code expects an already
transient subgraph; it does not acquire an arbitrary program representation.

Self-loops are omitted in the forest expansion only \emph{after} forming
the full model--controller product. A physical self-loop may advance a
controller counter and then is not a product self-loop.

\section{The classical forest identity, with a direct proof}

A rooted directed forest $F$ selects one off-diagonal edge $i\to F(i)$
from each $i\in V$, has no directed cycle, and has every path end in $B$.
Let $\mathcal F$ be the common forest set, and define
\[
 w_P(F)=\prod_{i\in V}P_{i,F(i)},\qquad
 D_P=\sum_{F\in\mathcal F}w_P(F).
\]
Let $\rho_F(s)$ be the terminal root reached from $s$, with $\rho_F(b)=b$.

\begin{lemma}[Absorption-forest identity]\label{lem:forest}
For $s\in V\cup B$ and $b\in B$,
\[
 \Prb_P(Z=b\mid X_0=s)=
 \frac{\sum_{F\in\mathcal F}{\bf1}_{\{\rho_F(s)=b\}}w_P(F)}{D_P}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $s\in V$, put $Q=P_{V,V}$ and $L=I-Q$. Its $i$th row is
\[
 L_{i,\cdot}=\sum_{j\ne i}P_{ij}
       (e_i^\top-{\bf1}_{\{j\in V\}}e_j^\top).
\]
Expand $\det L$ by multilinearity in the rows. Each term chooses an
outgoing destination $f(i)\ne i$ for each transient vertex. If a directed
cycle is selected, its row vectors sum to zero, so its determinant vanishes.
Otherwise all paths end in $B$, and ordering vertices by distance to $B$
makes the selected matrix triangular with diagonal one. Consequently
$\det L=D_P>0$.

The terminal-$b$ absorption vector solves $Lh=P_{V,b}$. Replace column $s$
of $L$ by this right-hand side in Cramer's formula and expand in the rows.
For selected destination $j$, the new column entry is $ {\bf1}_{\{j=b\}}$.
For a selected cycle these entries are all zero; the cycle rows still
sum to zero, so its term vanishes. For a forest $F$, the selected
unreplaced matrix has determinant one and its solution is
$h_i={\bf1}_{\{\rho_F(i)=b\}}$. Cramer's rule yields the stated numerator.
An initial terminal state satisfies the identity directly.
\end{proof}

This is a proof of the classical identity used here, not an originality
claim for it. One may draw $F$ with probability $w_P(F)/D_P$, independently
draw $s\sim\mu$, and report $\rho_F(s)$. This samples the terminal law;
it does not produce an original execution history.

\section{Conditional robustness}

For each positive off-diagonal edge define
\[
 r_{ij}=\log\frac{\widehat P_{ij}}{P_{ij}},\quad
 R(F)=\sum_{i\in V}r_{i,F(i)},\quad
 \Delta=\max_{F\in\mathcal F}R(F)-\min_{F\in\mathcal F}R(F).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Sharp bounded-tilt inequality]\label{lem:tilt}
If a probability distribution $\nu'$ is obtained by reweighting $\nu$
by $r\in[a,b]$, then normalizing, where $0<a\le b$, then
\[
 \TV(\nu,\nu')\le\frac{\sqrt b-\sqrt a}{\sqrt b+\sqrt a}.
\]
The bound is attained by a suitable two-point distribution.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $m=\E_\nu r$. Then $\TV(\nu,\nu')=\E_\nu|r-m|/(2m)$.
The chord bound for the convex function $x\mapsto|x-m|$ gives
$\E|r-m|\le2(b-m)(m-a)/(b-a)$. Maximizing the resulting bound
is equivalent to maximizing $a+b-m-ab/m$, whose maximum is at
$m=\sqrt{ab}$. A distribution supported on $a,b$ with this mean
attains equality. When $a=b$, the two distributions coincide.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform terminal conditioning]\label{thm:main}
For every terminal event $E\subseteq B$ with positive probability and
every shared initial distribution,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \TV\bigl(\mathcal L_P(Z\mid Z\in E),
         \mathcal L_{\widehat P}(Z\mid Z\in E)\bigr)
 \le \tanh(\Delta/4).
 \quad}
\]
The same bound holds after any fixed coarsening of the terminal labels.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use pairs $(s,F)$ with unnormalized weights $\mu_s w_P(F)$.
Conditioning on $\rho_F(s)\in E$ cancels the forest normalization $D_P$.
The other model multiplies the remaining weights by $\exp R(F)$.
The same pairs are available in both models because the supports agree.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:tilt} with $a=\exp(\min R)$ and $b=\exp(\max R)$,
then push forward to the desired label. Total variation cannot increase
under this pushforward, and restriction to $E$ cannot enlarge the range
of $R$.
\end{proof}

There is no inverse-evidence-probability factor and no expected-duration
factor. The kernel is fixed and homogeneous; arbitrary time-varying or
adversarial transition errors are not admitted.

Adding a row constant $c_i$ to all outgoing off-diagonal log ratios adds
$\sum_i c_i$ to every forest sum and leaves $\Delta$ unchanged. Therefore
\[
 \Delta\le\sum_i\left(\max_{j\ne i:P_{ij}>0}r_{ij}
                         -\min_{j\ne i:P_{ij}>0}r_{ij}\right).
\]
In particular, valid rescaling of every exit probability from a row,
with compensating change to its holding probability, leaves terminal laws
unchanged. This is ordinary jump-chain invariance. Forced forest edges
also contribute only common factors. The log spread is a pseudometric
on fixed-support positive edge weights; its symmetry and triangle
inequality follow from the oscillation of additive log ratios.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:alpha}
If all positive off-diagonal ratios lie in $[1-\alpha,1+\alpha]$,
$0<\alpha<1$, then every conditioned terminal law has TV error at most
\[
 \tanh\left(\frac K4\log\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right).
\]
\end{corollary}

The relative probability of an event itself lies between
$[(1-\alpha)/(1+\alpha)]^K$ and its reciprocal. This does not make
an arbitrarily long printed expansion of that probability free.

\section{Computing the forest certificate}

For rational kernels, the extrema of $R(F)$ are directed-arborescence
optimization problems. Add a super-root and a zero-cost edge from each
terminal to it. Every forest corresponds to one arborescence oriented
toward that root. Negating costs exchanges maximization and minimization.
The implementation uses the classical integer cycle-contraction method
\cite{edmonds}.

Logarithms must not be treated as exact floating-point costs. For rational
$x>0$, the code encloses $\log x$ using powers of two and
\[
 \log y=2\sum_{\ell=0}^{t-1}\frac{z^{2\ell+1}}{2\ell+1}
                +\text{remainder},\qquad
 z=\frac{y-1}{y+1},\quad 1\le y\le2,
\]
with remainder in
$[0,2z^{2t+1}/((2t+1)(1-z^2))]$.
It first divides each edge likelihood ratio by a reference likelihood
ratio in its own row, exactly, removing row scaling before approximation.

Put $S=2^b$. Enclose each adjusted log ratio within width $2^{-b-4}$,
multiply by $S$, round its lower endpoint down and upper endpoint up,
and call the integer costs $\ell_e,u_e$. The exact spread lies in
\[
 \left[
 \max\left(0,\frac{\max_F\sum_{e\in F}\ell_e-
                       \min_F\sum_{e\in F}u_e}{S}\right),
 \frac{\max_F\sum_{e\in F}u_e-\min_F\sum_{e\in F}\ell_e}{S}
 \right].
\]
Each edge interval has width at most two integer units, so this interval
has width at most $4K/S$. Four integer optimizations suffice.
The rational number $\min(1,\Delta_{\rm upper}/4)$ is a safe TV bound.
The algorithm is polynomial in the explicit graph size, input rational
bit lengths, and requested log precision. Exhaustive forest enumeration
is used only as a small-instance verification oracle.

This \emph{pair} certificate requires both kernels. It is not by itself an
observable certificate for an unknown true kernel.

\section{Acquisition and reuse across controllers}

Suppose an environment has finite known state labels and actions, with
local rows $p(s',o\mid s,a)$. There are $R$ state--action rows and at most
$M$ possible cells in all rows. Assume independent reset or simulator
access to each row and a promised floor $\beta>0$ on every positive cell.
This access must be supplied or separately acquired; it does not follow
from passive hidden observations. Support may be known initially or
inferred on the high-probability event below. With unknown support,
unobserved transitions cannot be ruled out from finite data alone.
Known support permits adaptive confidence bounds, but does not provide a
uniform fixed sample budget independent of arbitrarily small probabilities.
The promised floor here supplies such a budget.

Collect $N$ independent draws from each row and use empirical frequencies.
For $0<\alpha<1$, multiplicative Chernoff bounds and a union bound give
\[
 N\ge\frac{3}{\beta\alpha^2}\log\frac{2M}{\delta}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \Prb\left(\forall p_j>0,\
       1-\alpha\le\widehat p_j/p_j\le1+\alpha\right)\ge1-\delta.
\]
Zeros are never observed; on this event no positive cell is missed.

An autonomous finite-state controller may choose actions, randomize with
known probabilities, update memory from observations, and halt with a
finite output. Each entry of the product kernel is a nonnegative linear
combination of source cells, with the same known coefficients in both
models. It inherits the same multiplicative ratio bounds and support.

\begin{theorem}[One calibration, all admitted terminal questions]
Fix $K_{\max}\ge1$ and $0<\varepsilon\le1/2$. Set
$\alpha=\varepsilon/K_{\max}$ and take
\[
 N=\left\lceil\frac{3K_{\max}^2}{\beta\varepsilon^2}
                         \log\frac{2M}{\delta}\right\rceil
\]
draws per source row. With probability at least $1-\delta$,
simultaneously for every autonomous controller with at most $K_{\max}$
transient product states and every possible terminal evidence event,
the empirical conditional terminal law is within $\varepsilon$ in TV
of the true law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the common cellwise good event, the product support and ratio bounds
hold for each controller. Collapse of nonterminating bottom components
preserves them because sums preserve multiplicative bounds.
Corollary~\ref{cor:alpha} applies with $K\le K_{\max}$.
For $\alpha\le1/2$,
$\log((1+\alpha)/(1-\alpha))\le4\alpha$, so the TV bound is at most
$K\alpha\le\varepsilon$. The cellwise good event did not depend on the
controller, initial distribution, or terminal event. They may be selected
after inspecting the data; no union bound over those choices is needed.
\end{proof}

The total acquisition count is $RN$, not $N$. Reset costs, observation
labels, inability to reach a requested row, and discovery of a state
quotient are separate costs. An unknown initial distribution can be
represented and calibrated as an additional initial row, with its state
and acquisition costs counted.

For a supplied controller, solve $(I-\widehat Q)H=\widehat R$ exactly,
then condition the resulting terminal probabilities. Rational elimination
has polynomial bit complexity in the explicit matrix and input bit
lengths. Row denominator clearing and determinant bounds give polynomial
numerator and denominator lengths. Small evidence probabilities need not
be handled by repeated rare-event simulation.

For a finite supplied library of controllers and utilities in $[0,1]$,
maximizing estimated conditional utility has true regret at most
$2\varepsilon$ relative to that library, provided every conditioning
event is possible. Apply the uniform bound to the selected and best
controllers. This is not a polynomial algorithm for searching all
finite-state controllers and does not guarantee large evidence probability.

A selected terminal label can be sampled exactly from the empirical
rational law by ordinary exact discrete sampling. It is approximate
under the true law at the stated TV tolerance. Standard exact sampling
can have unbounded rejection randomness. Emitting a full original
execution history still costs its length.

\section{Matching acquisition obstruction}

Finite-memory coin testing is classical \cite{hellman,aaronson}.
The following construction derives the accounting needed here.
Let an independent three-symbol source have probabilities
\[
 p_\theta(+)=\lambda(1/2+\theta),\qquad
 p_\theta(-)=\lambda(1/2-\theta),\qquad
 p_\theta(0)=1-\lambda.
\]
A controller performs a random walk on $-L,\ldots,L$, stays on $0$,
starts at zero, and halts at either endpoint. It has $K=2L-1$ transient
states. Its plus-output probability is
\[
 h_\theta=\frac1{1+\left(\frac{1/2-\theta}{1/2+\theta}\right)^L}.
\]
Ignore holds and solve $u_j=p u_{j+1}+(1-p)u_{j-1}$ with boundary
values $u_{-L}=0,u_L=1$. The geometric solution yields this formula;
at $p=1/2$ the solution is linear.

\begin{theorem}[Calibration lower bound]
For $0<\varepsilon\le1/16$ and $0<\beta\le1/8$, there is a one-row
source with positive cells at least $\beta$ and a controller with at
most $K$ transient states such that estimating its terminal law within
$\varepsilon$ with confidence $2/3$ requires
$\Omega(K^2/(\beta\varepsilon^2))$ raw row samples.
This is a bounded sample-budget claim without conditional-sampling access.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For odd $K=2L-1$, put $\lambda=4\beta\le1/2$ and
$\theta=2\varepsilon/L\le1/8$. Compare parameters $\theta$ and $-\theta$.
Their symbol probabilities are at least
$\lambda(1/2-\theta)\ge3\beta/2$ and the hold probability is at least
$1/2$, so the promised floor holds. Writing
$a=(1/2+\theta)/(1/2-\theta)$ gives
\[
 h_\theta-h_{-\theta}
 =\tanh\left(\frac L2\log a\right)
 \ge\tanh(2L\theta)=\tanh(4\varepsilon)>2\varepsilon .
\]
The last inequality follows from $\tanh t\ge t-t^3/3$ at $t\le1/4$.
An estimator accurate under both models would distinguish them with
error at most $1/3$.

The one-sample divergence is
\[
 D(p_\theta\Vert p_{-\theta})
 =2\lambda\theta\log\frac{1/2+\theta}{1/2-\theta}
 \le16\lambda\theta^2=64\lambda\varepsilon^2/L^2.
\]
For $N\le L^2/(1024\lambda\varepsilon^2)$, the sample-law divergence is
at most $1/16$, and Pinsker's inequality bounds sample-law TV by
$\sqrt{1/32}<1/3$. Equal-prior testing error is therefore greater than
$1/3$, a contradiction. Randomized estimators cannot increase TV.
Using the largest odd state count at most $K$ extends the order bound.
\end{proof}

Rare evidence is unnecessary for this lower bound. Exponentially many
states encoded by few bits remain an acquisition obstacle even for an
exceptionally simple source.

\section{An external-deadline failure}\label{sec:deadline}

There are three physical states $A,B,F$, initially half on each of $A,B$.
In the base kernel a live state holds with probability $1/2$ and otherwise
moves to absorbing failure $F$. In the perturbed kernel, $A$ holds with
probability $(1+\eta)/2$ and $B$ with $(1-\eta)/2$, with complementary
failure probabilities. All positive cell ratios lie in $[1-\eta,1+\eta]$.

At an externally specified time $H$, condition on still being alive.
The base posterior on $A$ is $1/2$ and the perturbed posterior is
\[
 \frac{(1+\eta)^H}{(1+\eta)^H+(1-\eta)^H}.
\]
With $\eta=1/H$, it tends to $(1+e^{-2})^{-1}$, a TV gap of about
$0.380797$ while the local error tends to zero. Base evidence has
probability $2^{-H}$.

An exact external deadline requires clock configurations in an autonomous
program. A binary counter has few bits but many configurations.
This is a conditional fixed-time counterexample; it neither proves nor
disproves the separate unconditioned fixed-time sensitivity conjecture.

\section{Executed construction and evidence}

The self-contained file \texttt{stopped\_calibration.py} uses Python's
standard library, exact fractions, and integer arborescence optimization.
It imports no previous P3 entry point or hidden initializer. Running it
writes \texttt{stopped\_calibration\_results.json}.

Local exact checks cover 48 random rational absorbing chains, with one
to four transient states and three terminals. They enumerate 11,145
forests, check the absorption identity for both kernels and every start,
verify 840 conditioned posterior inequalities, compare 96 integer
arborescence extrema with exhaustive extrema, check 12 certified log
spreads, and verify 48 exact row-speed invariances. The gambler formula
is checked at six sizes and three biases. Five deadline counterexamples
are checked exactly. These are implementation checks, not external proof
review or a substitute for the proofs above.

The acquisition example uses one raw fair-coin source and a known
95-state controller:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A gate requires 40 consecutive heads; a tail halts with failure.
\item After the gate, it waits for a run of 40 heads, with tails resetting
      the run counter.
\item It performs gambler's ruin with $L=8$ and reports $-$ or $+$.
\end{enumerate}
The state count is $40+40+15=95$. Evidence is a final $-$ or $+$ output,
with true probability $2^{-40}$. The mean waiting phase is $2^{41}-2$
steps. This is an exact expectation, not a trillion-step simulation.

Set $\varepsilon=0.1$, $\delta=0.05$, $\beta=1/4$, and $\alpha=1/950$.
A rational upper enclosure of $\log(2M/\delta)$, with $M=2$, determines
the sample budget. The seeded draw has $23{,}723{,}052$ heads among
$47{,}457{,}349$ raw coin bits. The PRNG demonstrates implementation;
physical independence remains a theorem assumption.

The universal bound holds on the common calibration event. The pair
certificate uses the known true matrix in this demonstration and is
not a data-only guarantee. For this controller, a data-only improvement
is available by intersecting the two source-cell calibration intervals:
\[
 p_{\rm lo}=\max\left(\frac{\widehat p}{1+\alpha},
                    1-\frac{1-\widehat p}{1-\alpha}\right),\qquad
 p_{\rm hi}=\min\left(\frac{\widehat p}{1-\alpha},
                    1-\frac{1-\widehat p}{1+\alpha}\right).
\]
The conditional plus probability is the known increasing function
$g(p)=1/[1+((1-p)/p)^8]$, because gate and waiting factors cancel.
This gives the interval
$[0.49484727895,\,0.50325925162]$ and the displayed error bound
relative to $g(\widehat p)$. Exact endpoints are in the results file.

Both true and empirical predictions use the same conventional
absorbing-chain linear solver and agree with independent closed forms.
Runtime fields are single local measurements, not benchmark averages.
There is no established speedup over an equally informed conventional
solver.

\section{Preserved failures, baseline, and remaining reach}

The fixed-time probe uses binary64 matrix-power derivatives and finite
searches. It tested 800 cases each at two through six states. Largest
recorded first-order sensitivities were approximately
$0.4995,0.7449,0.8554,0.9642,0.9233$ respectively.
Large-horizon numerical conditioning was not certified. These observations
do not prove an all-time inequality. The incomplete proof route is
preserved so that it cannot later be mistaken for a completed result.

The previous completed return is
\texttt{c18ca9f1-ce00-5d0a-9da8-fe05fd396e95}, supplied by Harjas.
Its governing files were
\texttt{ACQUIRED\_LONG\_HORIZON\_REGULAR\_INFERENCE.tex} and
\texttt{CONSTANT\_RANK\_QUADRATIC\_PHASE\_REFINEMENT.tex}.
Those originals were not supplied or independently replayed here.
This note does not supersede them. The identity
$O(T(x,u))=q(u+a(x))+c$, positive arithmetic representation,
expected-polynomial selected-output sampling with unbounded worst-case
precision, and full-history output cost remain user-supplied baseline
assets. The reported conventional weighted-automaton advantage is not
reversed. Exact-recognition hardness with exponentially small bias still
does not establish robust approximate-prediction hardness.

This continuation supplies a useful robustness construction and a sharp
acquisition boundary, but not a demonstrated foundational breakthrough.
The remaining question is how to acquire or certify a compact
\emph{positive} representation with reliable support and useful local
accuracy from realistic observations. This theorem transports such a
certificate once it exists. It does not obtain hidden-state resets,
solve general state discovery, remove an exponential state count, or
make arbitrary history evidence a small-memory event.
