# Decisive attached-loop manufacturing experiment

Proposed experiment only. No coupon has been built and no outcome is supplied. A positive model fixture is not a substitute for this experiment.

The decision is whether temporary routing at one proximal junction enables a useful final enclosed-loop structure to be manufactured at lower complete cost and acceptable residue than the strongest equally informed method. The first demonstrator is an 80 mm-long, 1 by 1 mm loop attached to a two-port main line through a 3 by 3 by 1 mm root chamber. A nominal 0.5 mm root partition pairs the inlet to the loop entrance and the loop return to the outlet during primary support clearance. Erasing that local partition leaves the intended final root and attached loop. Exact mouth coordinates, terminal positions, seal geometry and partition volume must be included in the fabrication drawing; a graph vertex alone does not specify their pressure field.

The leading material trial is room-temperature-cured epoxy around a cold-liquefiable F127 core, with a calcium-alginate root partition. The literature demonstrates relevant epoxy/F127 preparation and cold clearance separately from alginate valve fabrication/erasure. It does not demonstrate their sealed joint. Temperature must be measured through cure as well as wash. A transparent development coupon can expose bypass and erosion; a nontransparent target host requires an independent transfer and assay. These are proposed materials, not acquired compatibility facts.

Before a full clearance comparison, run a root-only seal coupon in the actual carrier, pressure and temperature envelope. Measure pressure-flow leakage, deformation, F127 contact/contamination, partition loss during the first wash, and root geometry after chelator exposure. Then test full loop delivery with a nonreactive tracer and physically measure distal arrival/flow. A junction pressure split in the unpartitioned specimen may already supply adequate loop flow; measure that baseline. Reject the routing claim if its apparent gain is an unmeasured mouth-pressure difference or a changed final geometry.

Freeze the endpoint and matched arms before efficacy measurements:

1. Temporary sacrificial root partition with its full two-stage wash.
2. Same final geometry and primary support with optimized steady and pulsed pressure washing. Include the measured unpartitioned loop flow.
3. Optimized vacuum/pressure cycling, with degassing, solvent, temperature and wall compliance actually allowed by the target process.
4. A reversible junction splitter/pinch or preplaced removable guide if physically available with the same final geometry and port constraints. Include localized thermoreversible F127 gating: retain a warm gel barrier at the junction during cold clearance, then cool/open it. This uses the primary support rather than a second erasure chemistry. Charge spatial heating, thermal cross-talk, actuator, fabrication and access costs, and acquire its actual scale/host transfer. Also compare direct/assembled fabrication where it meets the real product specification.

Every arm receives the same prior knowledge of geometry and materials and the same opportunity for pilot optimization. Freeze all settings on separate development coupons, randomize test specimens within fabrication lots, and blind the endpoint assay. An arbitrary low-temperature or port constraint must be justified by the actual target product; it cannot be introduced merely to disqualify an incumbent.

The endpoint must quantify both primary support and secondary partition products at the worst admitted locations, final lumen/geometry, leakage and actual resonant/reservoir function after a declared rinse and aging horizon. Effluent mass alone is insufficient: it can coexist with distal residue. Use calibrated sectioning/chemical imaging or another spatially validated assay with stated detection limits and interference controls. Fluorescent tags must be shown to track their parent materials through dissolution; tag absence alone does not establish polymer absence. Destructive sections and a total mass/species balance should challenge each other rather than share an untested calibration.

Prespecify acceptable residual mass/fraction and functional tolerances from the product decision. A 1% residue threshold, if used, is a test specification and not a demonstrated biological or thermal safety limit. Report UNKNOWN when the assay cannot resolve it. A failure-free series of 299 independent exchangeable specimens would bound a binary failure rate below 1% with one-sided 95% binomial confidence; this is an illustrative reliability calculation, not the sample size for superiority or proof of independence. Comparator superiority needs its own effect/variance-based design, using pilot information and accounting for multiple comparisons.

Record per accepted product: all fabrication/cure time and temperature, primary/secondary material and yield, rejected coupons, solvent/reagent volumes and disposal, pump/vacuum/heater/actuator energy, pressure histories, gases, assay/calibration time and cost, final dimensions, residue and function. Do not report only the successful transport interval. Measure wall chemical survival and seal leakage over the full pressure/temperature envelope; mean-coupon performance does not certify a worst-case bound.

Competing predictions are concrete. Ideal temporary routing predicts strong distal throughflow before erasure and direct export of local partition products near the main line. A leaking root predicts an early bypass and deficient loop clearance. An unpartitioned finite-mouth model predicts nonzero loop flow even without a partition. Vacuum/pressure cycling can exchange fluid despite zero mean graph flow. A reversible splitter predicts the same routing advantage without erasure chemistry. The operation is useful only if its final endpoint and complete cost beat these alternatives in a qualified target process.

Stop the candidate if the joint seal/chemistry cannot be acquired, if erasure contaminates the target, if an ordinary reversible or assembled method wins, or if no consequential product needs the admitted constraints. A positive coupon would establish a scoped manufacturing result, not foundational novelty or civilization-scale benefit. Independent replication and broader priority review would remain separate.
