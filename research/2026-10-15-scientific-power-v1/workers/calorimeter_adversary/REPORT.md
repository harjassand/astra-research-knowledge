# Independent physical audit: separator-mounted calorimetric H₂ recombination islands

**Scope.** Bounded second-candidate audit of a proposed alkaline electrolyzer separator carrying electrically isolated, gas-accessible Pt recombination islands in hydrophobic microcavities, with a suspended calorimetric/heater-null readout. This is a literature-grounded adversarial model and experiment design, not a fabrication, performance, novelty, or safety validation. The target state described here is near ambient pressure and about 80 °C; no high-pressure capability is acquired.

**Audit status: CONDITIONAL TEST CANDIDATE — NOT VALIDATED.** A conservative finite-window lower bound is physically possible in principle for H₂ consumed in the instrumented islands of the *installed* separator, but the present concept has no acquired measurement, thermal-cut, wetting, or species-closure results. A positive result must fail closed unless each condition below is bounded experimentally. The native/untouched membrane and gross unidirectional crossover are out of claim scope.

## Exact claim and accounting

Let `N_cross,net` be signed, time-integrated H₂ transport from cathode to the anode-side control volume during the window; `N_escape` the measured H₂ leaving that volume; `N_rec` H₂ consumed in the metered island control volumes; `N_store` change in the complete H₂ inventory inside the control volume; and `N_ext` net H₂ entering or being generated there by routes other than separator crossover. Conservation gives

`N_cross,net = N_escape + N_rec + N_store - N_ext`.

Since final inventory is nonnegative, a defensible lower bound is

`N_cross,net ≥ N_escape + N_rec^- - N_H2,initial - N_ext,max`,

where the initial inventory includes dissolved H₂, separator/anolyte/cavity gas and any island/catalyst hydrogen inventory. External generation/feed must be bounded, not set to zero by assumption. Reverse-direction transport is why this is a **net** bound. No inference about a pristine separator, unidirectional gross flux, or total crossover follows.

For the heat component, the proposed estimator is valid only if a lower bound `Q^-` on heat attributable to the island control volume is established after electrical, sensible/latent-flow, and storage corrections; `B` is an upper bound on every non-H₂ contribution and measurement error; and `h_max` is a conservative upper bound on exothermic heat per mole of H₂ consumed over the allowed initial/final catalyst, product, and operating states:

`N_rec^- = max(0, (Q^- - B)/h_max)`.

This is a lower bound only when `Q^- - B` is a lower bound on H₂-associated reaction heat. For product water, the vapor/liquid state matters. Adsorption, Pt–H/Pt–O state changes, liquid/KOH uptake, catalyst conditioning, other reactants, and transient heat storage cannot be silently assigned to H₂ recombination. A nonpositive numerator is a valid vacuous outcome, not evidence of zero crossover.

## Competing physical model: identical null reading without island H₂ consumption

A minimal island model is

`C_i dT_i/dt = P_h + q_H2 + q_other - Σ_j G_ij(T_i - T_j) - Hdot_flow`,

where `P_h` is the feedback-heater power, `q_H2` island recombination heat, `q_other` other local heat, `G_ij` thermal conductances to cell/anode/guard/suspension paths, and `Hdot_flow` gas/liquid enthalpy transport. At temperature null, `dT_i/dt = 0`; the measured heater power does not isolate `q_H2` when an unmeasured thermal path remains.

For one uninstrumented boundary at temperature `T_b`, write its outward heat as `G(T_i-T_b)`. The null equation contains `G(T_i-T_b)-q_H2`. For any `δ`, the alternative `q'_H2=q_H2+δ`, `G'=G+δ/(T_i-T_b)` gives the same heater reading. In particular, a zero-island-reaction model can mimic a positive inferred reaction rate by adding ordinary anode heat ingress and an admissible conductance change. That change is physically plausible when cavity wetting, electrolyte films, compression, or gas composition changes the heat path. Thus heater-power amplitude or a heater-only self-calibration does not identify reaction heat; a null controller makes the ambiguity less visible, not less real.

The strongest spatial confound is not hypothetical: Liu et al. report H₂ chemical recombination on the ordinary Ni anode components. Their supplement detects a small difference in outlet H₂ flux with oxygen versus argon carrier gas, then says quantifying this consumption during operation is challenging and estimates it with an apparent kinetic model. Heat from that existing anode reaction, along with cell Joule/OER heat, can reach the island. An active/inert island contrast alone is not a decisive control because catalyst state and reaction products can alter wetting and conductance as well as heat.

A second competing model is a positive island signal with little or no H₂ consumption: Pt surface oxidation/conditioning, adsorption and catalyst-state relaxation, water/KOH uptake, oxidation of trace contaminants, or heat brought by humid gas and condensate. If an electrolyte film reaches the island, ionic leakage or unintended electrochemistry adds both heat and a cell shunt. Conversely, water films or poisoning may suppress recombination while changing heat transfer. These models predict that a thermopile response is not, by itself, molecularly specific to H₂ consumption.

A third model preserves real H₂ recombination but denies native-separator inference: the island is an imposed H₂ sink. It lowers local H₂ partial pressure and can steepen the transport gradient, changing how much H₂ crosses. That is acceptable only for the stated installed-device claim. The separator containing the sensor must be treated as a distinct physical device, not as a passive observer of the uninstrumented membrane.

## Prior-art and closest comparators found

- **Installed separator recombination is already demonstrated as a function.** Liu et al. report an anode-side Pt gas-recombination catalyst that consumes over 95% of crossover H₂ at 1 A cm⁻² and operates over 1,000 h; their work also directly addresses alkaline separator transport. This is close functional prior art, although it does not establish a calorimetric island or the proposed lower-bound estimator. [Nature Energy article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-026-02094-7); [supplementary information](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41560-026-02094-7/MediaObjects/41560_2026_2094_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).
- **Mid-plane embedded catalyst separator is also published.** Shin et al. embed Pt/Nafion on a mesh within a polysulfone/zirconia separator and report about 60% H₂-crossover reduction, with partial catalyst-layer loss under severe dynamic cycling. This raises the prior-art bar for separator-internal recombination and flags layer retention/dynamic operation. It is not this calorimeter. [Small Methods article](https://doi.org/10.1002/smtd.202502412).
- **Electrolyzer product-line catalytic combustion sensor is disclosed.** A Yamaha patent family (priority 2019-06-28) places catalytic-combustion sensors in hydrogen and oxygen product paths; each includes catalyst, heater, and thermopile, and infers gas concentration from reaction heat. This is close calorimetric gas-sensing prior art, but external to the separator. It is a patent disclosure, not independent performance validation or a legal novelty determination. [US20220113271A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20220113271A1/en); [WO2020262475A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2020262475A1/en).
- **Electrolysis calorimetry with product recombination predates this concept.** Närger et al. describe a continuous-flow labyrinth calorimeter where a catalyst recombines gaseous electrolysis products to close the energy balance; they report 20 mW sensitivity and calibrate multiple local heater configurations to probe gradients/local heating. This is not an embedded local island, but it is a strong baseline comparator for a complete calorimetric balance. [Review of Scientific Instruments (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1141161).
- **Gas-flow microcalorimetry of H₂/O₂ recombination is established.** Lalik et al. studied supported Pd/Pt/Pd–Pt recombination using catalytic, microcalorimetric, and DFT methods; humidity/reaction water changes catalyst activity and heat signal. A separate Pd-focused flow microcalorimetry study reports large transient thermokinetic oscillations and differential heat values up to 700 kJ/mol H₂. These studies are not direct validation of Pt-on-alkaline-separator performance; they show why catalyst state, humidity, and finite-window heat accounting need explicit calibration. [Applied Catalysis A (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apcata.2015.04.029); [Industrial & Engineering Chemistry Research (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.iecr.5b00686).

This bounded search establishes substantial neighboring prior art in separator recombination, electrolyzer calorimetry, and thermopile/pellistor-like gas sensing. It does **not** determine novelty, patentability, or whether every feature combination has been anticipated.

## Gas access, selectivity, wetting, and measurement back-action

A hydrophobic gas cavity must keep gas access while excluding the actual hot concentrated KOH electrolyte. Liquid-entry pressure depends on the liquid surface tension, largest connected pore/defect, geometry and contact angle (`LEP ∝ -γ cosθ/d_max`); nominal pore size alone is not enough. Published high-temperature membrane tests document temperature-dependent LEP and sensitivity to geometry/surface properties, but they do not certify this microcavity material in 7 M KOH at 80 °C. A 100 nm commercial PTFE membrane's room-temperature water result cannot be transferred to a fabricated, catalyst-coated cavity. Actual KOH composition, cleaning/aging, compression, defects, gas/liquid pressure differential, and operating transient must be tested in situ. [Claramunt et al., Membranes (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3390/membranes11120907).

Required physical boundaries:

1. **No liquid or ionic contact with the island.** Measure hot-KOH intrusion/LEP, island-to-cell leakage current and impedance/HFR before, during, and after operation. Any wetting invalidates the gas-only heat-to-H₂ inference until its effects are bounded.
2. **No through-plane gas bypass.** The cavity must be a one-sided sampling/reaction pocket, not a gas path between compartments. Pressure-step/tracer tests must bound a cathode-to-anode shortcut below the species-balance uncertainty.
3. **Full local heat cut.** Measure suspension-leg heat flux. Hold guard and cavity boundaries isothermal with the island or measure their mismatch; for every uninstrumented path, include a demonstrated `K_max × |ΔT|` ingress bound in `B`. The ordinary anode/Ni region and cell body count as boundaries. Test during starts, stops, current and pressure changes, not only steady state.
4. **No-H₂ external-heater falsifier.** With H₂ excluded from the island but the electrolyzer at its ordinary thermal/electrical state, use a calibrated microheater at the ordinary anode/Ni location to impose equal or greater plausible local heat pulses and gradients. The calorimeter's reported lower bound must stay at zero within uncertainty. Sweep several locations and transients. Repeat with a same-geometry inert island and the active island after identical conditioning. A passing heater test is necessary, not sufficient: it does not test every chemistry, wetting or source distribution.
5. **Composition challenge and complete closure.** Measure both-side H₂/O₂ gas flow and composition (GC plus calibrated mass-flow is the strongest established comparator), dissolved gas and liquid/gas inventory changes; assay plausible liquid products such as peroxide and account for gas/liquid sensible and latent enthalpy. H₂/D₂ and O₂/¹⁸O₂ can challenge exchange/storage and leak models, but cannot prove molecular ancestry without exchange blanks: D can exchange with protons and oxygen labels can exchange into water without island recombination. Use those tracers as challenge controls only.
6. **Quantify sink back-action.** Compare pristine separator, matched cavity-only/inert island, and catalytically active island at the same current, temperature, pressure differential, and gas state; include several catalyst loadings. Concurrently track HFR, cell voltage, direct crossover/escape flow, and dissolved H₂. Interpret differences as installed-device changes. The measured island sink must be shown to access a defined fraction of the crossed gas, or the result is only a lower bound for its actual accessible island reaction, not for all recombination in the cell.
7. **Bound finite-window state changes.** Start/stop windows must include cooling and release transients or a separately measured upper bound on the final-minus-initial catalyst, gas, liquid, and adsorbed-H inventory and thermal energy. A transient heat pulse cannot simply be divided by standard water-formation enthalpy.

The gas-line catalytic sensor and conventional GC/mass-flow must be run as matched alternatives. Comparison should include total measurement uncertainty and the costs of microfabrication, isolation, thermal guard/flux metering, hot-KOH qualification, recalibration, product assays, leak tests, and loss of crossover performance—not only nominal sensor resolution.

## Decisive experiment and pass/fail logic

**Stage A — independent calorimetric identifiability before installation.** Build one island/cavity coupon in an isothermal guarded fixture. Instrument the island, suspension legs, cavity walls, guard, adjacent heater, inlet/outlet gas temperature/flow, and all electrical leads. Use blinded known electrical heat injections at the island and at multiple adjacent boundaries, including the ordinary-anode-side position; vary guard mismatch, compression, gas humidity, flow and temperature. Fit no model only to the same heater used as the sensor. Demonstrate a validated upper bound on each unmeasured heat path and show the no-H₂ adjacent-heater stress test yields `N_rec^-=0`.

**Stage B — chemically selective heat and state closure.** At near ambient pressure and 80 °C in actual 7 M KOH exposure, compare clean Pt, inert matched island, and no-Pt/poisoned controls under H₂/O₂, H₂/inert, O₂-only, and humid/inert streams. Run gas-flow microcalorimetry with independent GC and mass flow as reference. Resolve startup, steady operation, H₂ shutoff, O₂ shutoff, and cooldown; integrate the full window. Set `B` and `h_max` from the worst validated cases, including any non-H₂ exotherm, heat-storage release, wetting shift, and local heat ingress. Do not use active/inert difference alone as the heat attribution.

**Stage C — live separator and back-action.** Operate matched pristine, cavity-only/inert, and active-island separators in a standard alkaline cell. Verify no KOH ingress/ionic bridge, no through-plane gas short, and independent gas-species/mass balance. Repeat at current, temperature, differential-pressure, and start/stop points. Calculate the heat lower bound before unblinding the independent balance. The candidate passes only where (i) all inventory/source terms are closed or conservatively bounded, (ii) every thermal-path and non-H₂ term is included in `B`, (iii) `Q^- - B > 0`, (iv) the resulting net-crossover lower bound agrees with or lies below the independent integrated balance within prespecified uncertainty, and (v) the cavity/island perturbation is disclosed as the measured device state. Failure of any condition gives **no nonvacuous bound** for that window.

## Judgment

The core arithmetic can support the narrow installed-device lower bound. The present physical proposal has two decision-critical unresolved gates: heat-flux identifiability against ordinary anode/cell heat ingress, and demonstration that the hot-KOH cavity remains a one-sided gas-accessible, ionically isolated feature without an unbounded transport perturbation. The first has an exact competing thermal model; the second is material- and geometry-specific and is not established by water/PTFE literature alone. The best next step is the blinded guarded-coupon test with the no-H₂ ordinary-anode heater, then hot-KOH intrusion and gas-short qualification. Until those pass and the species balance closes, status remains **NOT VALIDATED / NO ACQUIRED CAPABILITY**.
