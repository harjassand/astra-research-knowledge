# Manufacturing C: clearance routing that disappears after use

15 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. **PHYSICAL_CAPABILITY_NOT_ESTABLISHED; NOVELTY_UNKNOWN.** No specimen was fabricated, no laboratory measurement was made, and no established method was beaten.

The surviving candidate changes a manufacturing intermediate: a small removable junction partition initially forces solvent through a full-width loop cavity. Once its primary support is removed, the partition is dissolved and its products are washed directly into the main line. The final part has the intended single-attachment loop lobe, without another final hole or a retained valve. This root-only mutation avoids the long, weak separator and distal-cleanup failures of the first construction. The proposed gain is lower clearance cost or higher yield; existing cooling baffles, temporary/dissolvable walls, thermal valves, vacuum cleaning and printing prevent a claim of absolute new access or established novelty.

The leading intervention is therefore a physical change in intermediate transport topology, followed by qualified erasure. It is a process proposal with an exact finite calculation and decisive failure tests, not another inference certificate or an achieved manufacturing capability. Detailed geometry, costs and the matched experiment follow the preserved initial-lineage notes and primary sources.

## Preserved initial lineage and why it was narrowed

The initial branched-network proposal had an Euler parity error. Pairing junctions alone does not turn arbitrary final graphs into one port-to-port path. Longitudinal partitions can duplicate selected edges, but their serial hydraulic cost is large. A two-state temporary-gate process also defeats the apparent advantage on a balanced Wheatstone network. The blind-branch target survives that particular gate comparison because deleting edges cannot give a singly attached blind branch a second flow connection. This does not exclude diffusion, surface-driven counterflow, pressure cycling or bubbles.

The earlier nominal test geometry was an 80 mm tortuous, 500 x 500 micrometre blind branch, temporarily split by a 25 micrometre septum. Its intended host contract was a monolithic nonphotocurable rigid material processed below 40 C, with no extra final hole or bonded lid. That material system was not acquired. The long-separator construction was demoted after the mechanics, erosion, material-inventory and established-method checks below. The current loop target is a different geometry; it does not establish manufacture of the earlier blind stub.

Initial chemistry was cold-liquefied Pluronic F127 core and calcium-alginate partition removed with EDTA. Primary literature establishes both component chemistries separately. It does not establish the proposed 25 micrometre watertight partition, its host seal, its pressure strength or complete cleanup. The materials investigation reports a severe deflection/feature-size gate. A stiff aluminium foil partition, followed by alkaline dissolution and local hydrogen generation, is the current mutation under primary-source screening.

`clearance_fixture.py` and `CLEARANCE_FIXTURE.json` preserve illustrative rectangular-duct clocks, ideal blind diffusion, erasure-starvation, root's independently reconstructed two-face-dose identity and aluminium stoichiometry. Their status is model arithmetic only. Core removal time, residual mass, host survival, bubble coupling, useful device performance, whole apparatus cost and superiority over vacuum-cycle cleaning remain unacquired.

## Exact localized-loop construction

The final loop has 80 mm total centerline length and a 1 x 1 mm square section. Its two ends meet a 3 x 3 x 1 mm root chamber on a two-port main line. In root coordinates x,y in [-1.5,1.5] mm and z in [0,1] mm, the main inlet is on the west face and outlet on the east, both centered at y=0. The loop entrance is on the north face and return on the south, both centered at x=0. All four mouths are 1 x 1 mm.

A full-height diagonal partition occupies |y-x| <= d/sqrt(2). At d=0.5 mm it separates west+north from south+east. The temporary path is therefore inlet -> north loop mouth -> complete loop -> south loop mouth -> outlet. After erasure all mouths share the final chamber. The diagonal strip volume is 1.996 microlitres; the loop volume is 80 microlitres. The root-only geometry was proposed by root/topology after the long separator failed. It has no longitudinal wall or buried erasure front.

The proposed sequence is to mold the alginate partition and F127 template, register the mouths, embed/cure the host, cool the entire primary support below its qualified liquefaction threshold, and wash through the forced loop to a predeclared residue endpoint. Then remove the local partition with EDTA, collect its products through the flowing main line, rinse, dry and qualify the final part. A transparent planar coupon is useful for acquiring interfaces; it cannot establish freeform opaque manufacture by assertion. Cure exotherm, wetting, interdiffusion, shrinkage and seal integrity remain unmeasured process gates.

A rigid acoustic side-lobe is one possible actual-use endpoint and gives a reason for a stiff cast host. Its complex input impedance and leakage must be measured. For an 80 mm *total* symmetric loop, the root-excited symmetric mode has two 40 mm arms; a lossless first-frequency proxy is about 2.14 kHz at sound speed 343 m/s. The earlier 80 mm blind-stub proxy cannot be carried over. Some antisymmetric loop modes may be dark at the root. No acoustic performance was simulated or measured here.

A below-40 C process cap could protect an embedded component, but no such application requirement was acquired. It must not be invented merely to exclude a stronger hot process. Both conditional low-temperature manufacture and unrestricted manufacture need comparisons. Likewise, an assembled or printed part that meets the actual functional requirement is a legitimate competitor to monolithic casting.

## Exact model boundaries and reproducible negative cases

For an ideal passive resistor graph, a connected component attached to the rest of the network at one vertex has a constant-pressure solution: positive conductances and Kirchhoff conservation imply zero net edge flow. Valve deletions cannot supply a second boundary pressure. Splitting the attachment during manufacture changes this graph property. This is a theorem about the stated model, not a universal transport impossibility.

A finite root chamber is not a point vertex. An explicit countermodel puts an 80 mm-long 1 x 1 mm loop in parallel with a 3 mm-long 3 x 1 mm root bypass. Rectangular-duct arithmetic gives R_loop/R_root=149.849 and Q_loop/Q_total=0.006629. Thus finite root pressure difference already drives unpartitioned-loop flow. The chosen north/south mouths have equal axial coordinate, and symmetry can suppress that difference, but fabrication errors, three-dimensional junction fields and deliberately asymmetric actuation must be acquired. Zero net flux also permits molecular exchange and recirculation, as the primary dead-end transport paper demonstrates.

The standard no-slip fully developed rectangular-duct fixture gives a one-volume displacement clock of 0.182 s for the 1 mm square loop at assumed viscosity 0.001 Pa s and 1 kPa, or 1.821 s at 0.01 Pa s. This omits turns, entrances, non-Newtonian rheology, moving fronts, deformation, retained boundary films and wash chemistry. A ten-volume rinse is a planning quantity, not a 99% cleanup theorem. Actual viscosity and clearance time are UNKNOWN.

The earlier 500 micrometre blind-stub split has 19 microlitres of fluid and a 77.64 s one-volume clock at assumed viscosity 0.01 Pa s and 1 kPa. Its septum-deflection screen is y=DeltaP*h^4/(32*E*d^3). Taking E=3G under an incompressible-elastic approximation and the materials investigator's source G range gives E=4.5-8.1 kPa; the 50 micrometre deflection threshold is then only 1.8-3.24 Pa. This ideal beam alarm is not a measured wall response, but strongly rejects assuming a 25 micrometre hydrogel wall carries 1 kPa. Source-supported alginate feature sizes near 434 micrometres do not acquire a 25 micrometre watertight feature. The stronger source formulation had a coarser minimum feature. A 0.5 mm root gate reduces this scale gap but is still unqualified.

The serial-splitting proposal also pays a real hydraulic penalty. Under Newtonian no-slip assumptions, circular torsional optimality gives R_j >= 8*pi*mu*L/A_j^2 for lane area A_j. If m serial lanes fit in original area A, convexity and sum A_j <= A give sum R_j >= 8*pi*mu*L*m^3/A^2. Two lanes cost at least eight circular full-area resistances before wall thickness and noncircularity. This is a conditional fluid-model bound. The topology investigator's equal-square example gives 9.83565 times the full-edge resistance even with zero wall thickness.

For equal-speed outbound/return lanes and a nonconsuming concentration step c0, root's two-face-dose identity is D(x,t)=c0[t-x/u]_+ + c0[t-(2L-x)/u]_+. For t>=2L/u this is 2*c0*(t-L/u), independent of x. I reconstructed it in the fixture. A uniform threshold above 2*c0*L/u prevents ideal erosion before full transit. Equal speeds, nondepletion and a common threshold are not acquired. More decisively, instantaneous wall solubilization followed by a blind final circuit can retain **all** its dissolved mass in distal liquid. Global activation and disappearance do not establish export.

With lane resistance per length r and cross-wall leakage conductance per length g, kappa=sqrt(2*r*g). At fixed root pressure, Q_tip/Q_intact=(kappa*L)/sinh(kappa*L), while Q_tip/Q_root=sech(kappa*L). At kappa*L=10, distal flow falls to 0.000908 of intact flow. Proximal erasure can improve measured root throughput while starving the distal region. Root-only erasure avoids the long-wall version of this failure, but premature gate leakage still reduces clearance flow and must be measured.

The exact diffusion-only model has an initially uniform dissolved residue, a clean mouth and reflecting closed end. Its mean remaining fraction is (8/pi^2)*sum exp[-(2n+1)^2*pi^2*D*t/(4L^2)]/(2n+1)^2. The 1% time is 1.781288*L^2/D: 131.95 days for L=80 mm and illustrative D=10^-9 m^2/s. This is neither a measured Pluronic diffusivity nor a lower bound on real cleaning; bubbles, wall slip, colloid phoresis and convection are absent.

## Aluminium mutation: conservation before coupling

The stiff-foil alternative reacts in alkali as 2Al + 2OH- + 6H2O -> 2Al(OH)4- + 3H2. A 25 micrometre long separator contains 2.7 mg Al and can generate at most 3.672 mL H2 at 25 C and one atmosphere, about 184 cavity volumes. It consumes at least 100.07 micromoles OH-, requiring 100 microlitres 1 M NaOH or 1 mL 0.1 M before excess and losses. One 19 microlitre preload cannot complete that reaction. A fully sealed ideal-gas countercase reaches 18.6 MPa. These are exact stoichiometric/model budgets, not useful displaced volume, venting or measured pressure.

The ideal beam and single-preload capacity constraints leave a 0.827-4.947 micrometre thickness interval at 1 kPa and 1 M NaOH. A nonempty interval of necessary constraints does not establish feasibility. Thin-film fabrication, oxide induction, early perforation, reagent expulsion, local gas blockage, aluminate precipitation and host survival remain UNKNOWN. A local root foil is a possible alternative because products are near the main outlets, but it no longer needs a claim that local gas clears a distal blind cavity.

The direct prior is [Yamashita et al. 2015](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/analsci/31/12/31_1261/_pdf), whose full paper I inspected: Al tubes/balls produced PDMS and epoxy channels. Their demonstrated NaOH process used heat; they report slow room-temperature dissolution and clogging when injection stops. The previously listed SU-8 source reports hydrogen bubbles slowing or stopping Al removal without ultrasound. Neither supports automatic gas-assisted cleanup. Aluminium gas generation was therefore not promoted into a manufacturing capability.

## Decisive physical comparison and stopping condition

First qualify a 3 mm local root coupon at the actual 0.5 mm feature. It must stay sealed under the full differential-pressure/temperature history of primary wash, then release its material into the main line without an obstructing or tethered remnant. Recovered-product chemistry, residual dry mass and geometry must agree. Dye passage alone does not qualify it. The materials report identifies crosslink leaching during water rinses and salt-dependent F127 rheology as a coupled first-stage failure, in addition to the unshown wet alginate/host seal.

Then randomize matched loop specimens across partition-and-erasure, F127-only thermal gating, optimized native flushing, vacuum-cycle cleaning and physically accessible removable-tube/valve routing. Include permitted vaporization/direct/assembled manufacture against the same actual product endpoint. A transparent instrumented coupon may acquire local mechanisms, but the claimed rigid/opaque/freeform product needs its own validation. Do not transfer a calibration result by assertion.

Predeclare whole-process time, temperature and pressure limits; dimensional and chemical thresholds; yield; and actual-use performance. An initial geometry gate could require section dimensions within 5%, no solid separator or hidden plug, and primary-plus-secondary residual below 1% of charged dry mass, together with a stricter chemistry threshold when the use requires it. Combine the global mass gate with local imaging: a tiny mass can form a fatal plug. Use independently qualified clean and deliberately retained-tip/partition controls; blind endpoint assessment where practicable.

Record pressure, local/main flow where measurable, specimen temperature, bath/pump/vacuum electricity, time, all material masses/volumes, product recovery and rejected parts. For an acoustic prototype measure complex impedance and leakage, not only resonance visibility. The candidate survives only if it achieves the actual final-use endpoint with better predeclared complete cost or yield than the strongest matched method. One successful geometry would establish only that geometry and protocol.

The decisive unresolved obligation is a physically acquired removable junction that survives primary clearance and yields a qualified product more cheaply or reliably than existing routing/cleaning. No further point-graph, gas-budget or diffusion calculation can decide that. Current disposition: preserve this narrow experimental candidate and its failures; do not promote it as a new validated operation or a civilization-changing result.
