LOCAL OCCUPANCY QUOTIENT: CERTIFICATE, COVARIANCE, AND EXECUTION INTERFACE
31 October 2026

1. SCIENTIFIC OPERATION

There are k known physical two-site modules, n=2k binary sites. A later design
request specifies each module's occupancy c_a in {0,1,2} and arbitrary site
fields h. It asks for the best orientation within that context. There are 3^k
possible occupancy contexts. The learner receives neither the useful coupling
graph nor an optimizer for it.

The unknown energy is

 E(x)=g(c_1(x),...,c_k(x))+Q(x),

where g is an entirely arbitrary unknown function on these 3^k contexts and Q
is an unknown quadratic multilinear polynomial on the 2k sites. g can be huge
and nonpolynomial. Only orientation-dependent quadratic structure is promised.
This is an explicit physical model assumption; arbitrary orientation-dependent
high-order spikes are excluded. Occupancy selection / comparison of absolute
energies between contexts is a different, unearned operation.

A source experiment prepares all 2k site values and returns an independent
absolute-energy observation with centered sigma^2-sub-Gaussian error. Every
shot and preparation is charged. A normalized bit-valued measurement must be
converted to its true energy noise scale. Readout and arithmetic must retain
the requested absolute precision despite large g; bit cost includes log of
the energy magnitude. Mathematical cancellation is not free numerical precision.

2. EXPLICIT ORBIT CONTRAST

Draw X uniformly from {-1,+1}^{2k}. Independently swap the two sites of each
module with probability 1/2, obtaining Y. Conditional on the entire occupancy
context, X and Y are independent and uniformly distributed over orientations.
The algorithm uses k fair bits for the swaps. No unknown conditional sampler,
reset oracle, or nuisance-model evaluator is needed.

The observation difference E(X)-E(Y) cancels g exactly. It also cancels every
quadratic component invariant under those local swaps. Define

 q_a=(x_a1-x_a2)/sqrt(2), s_a=(x_a1+x_a2)/sqrt(2).

q_a changes sign under a swap; s_a does not. Under independent uniform site
spins, q_a,s_a have zero mean, unit second moment, and q_a*s_a=0. The full
noninvariant degree<=2 orthonormal basis consists of

 q_a,
 q_a q_b, q_a s_b, s_a q_b  for a<b.

Its dimension is D=k+3 k(k-1)/2. All missing terms are invariant functions of
occupancy and may be absorbed into g. Denote this feature vector by phi(x).
This is a known physical coordinate transformation, not the learned forest.
The useful forest support and its coefficients must still be acquired.

For Z=[phi(X)-phi(Y)]/sqrt(2),

                  E[Z Z^T]=I_D.                          (1)

Proof: the phi components are orthonormal on the uniform original-spin cube.
Their group averages are zero. Hence E phi(X)phi(Y)^T=0, while both marginal
second moments are I. Expanding the contrast covariance proves (1). The
executable checks (1) by enumerating all X and all swap masks for k=3.

3. ORBIT RESIDUAL CERTIFICATE

Let F be any data-dependent quadratic candidate, fixed before its own fresh
validation data. It may contain only a learned forest of module interactions.
Let Pi denote averaging over independent within-module swaps. The relevant
residual is

 r_perp=(I-Pi)(Q-F).

It is still a degree-two polynomial, has conditional mean zero given context,
and its squared uniform norm is the squared Euclidean norm of its coefficients
in phi. For a fresh same-context pair,

 D_res=[E(X)-F(X)]-[E(Y)-F(Y)]

has E D_res^2=2v where v=||r_perp||_2^2. The unknown g never appears. The
Gaussian-replacement proof in OPERATIONAL_RESIDUAL_CERTIFICATE.txt gives
E r_perp^4<=15v^2. Conditional independence and Jensen yield

 E D_res^4<=120v^2,   Var(D_res^2)<=116v^2.

Use 464 pairs per block and an odd L>=8log(4/delta) independent blocks. At
each of their 2*464*L configurations average

 M=max(1,ceil[4sigma^2/nu^2 * log(4*464*L/delta)])

energy shots. Define S_cert as the median of observed block residual RMS
values plus nu. The same Chebyshev / median / bounded-noise proof gives,
with probability at least 1-delta,

       sqrt(v)<=S_cert<=sqrt(3v)+2nu.                     (2)

This is a sufficient finite constant, not a claimed optimal bill. Every shot
in the executable is counted and its measurement-noise draw is actually made.

For a context with a occupancy-one modules, direct feature calculation gives

 ||phi(x)||^2=2a+4[choose(k,2)-choose(k-a,2)]
             =2a(2k-a),                                 (3)

independently of orientations within that context. Therefore the residual
oscillation in that context is at most

 Gamma_a=2 sqrt[2a(2k-a)] S_cert.

Uniformly over all contexts, Gamma=2sqrt(2)k S_cert. If the implemented
surrogate solver has regret alpha, its true regret is at most alpha+Gamma_a.
This one event holds for arbitrary adaptive future contexts and fields. No
factor involving the probability of a rare context appears. The degree bound
and feature norm transfer, not an assumption that rare contexts were observed,
pay for this uniform conclusion.

For requested epsilon*n=2epsilon*k regret, accept S_cert<=epsilon/sqrt(2).
Taking nu=Theta(epsilon) gives O(log(1/delta)) averaged contrast probes and
O(sigma^2/epsilon^2) repetitions up to the displayed logarithms. Actual cost
also includes O(k*464*L) preparation and feature / forest evaluation work.

For adaptive candidate t, use fresh validation and a summable allocation
delta_t=delta/[t(t+1)]. Conditional on its past, (2) is unchanged. Consequently
all accepted candidates across an arbitrary search are simultaneously sound
with probability at least 1-delta. Failed data may train later candidates, but
no candidate can reuse a holdout it was optimized against as its own fresh test.

4. ACTUAL SOLVER, NOT A PREMISE

Write a forest candidate as

 F(x)=sum_a b_a q_a + sum_{a<b in T}
       [Jqq_ab q_aq_b + Jqs_ab q_as_b + Jsq_ab s_aq_b],

where T is a forest of modules. For a requested occupancy context, modules
with occupancy 0 or 2 have q_a=0 and fixed s_a=-sqrt(2) or +sqrt(2). An active
module has s_a=0 and q_a=sqrt(2) z_a, z_a in {-1,+1}.

Thus every active-active edge contributes 2Jqq_ab z_a z_b. An edge from an
active module to an inactive module contributes only a known unary field.
Requested site fields contribute (h_a1-h_a2)z_a to active modules; all remaining
terms are context constants. The active induced graph is a forest, so ordinary
two-state tree dynamic programming finds its global minimum in O(k) arithmetic
and O(k) storage and backtracks all orientations. The executable performs this
conversion and actual DP, including arbitrary adaptive contexts and fields.
It does not compare g across contexts, estimate absolute optimum energies,
or choose occupancy for the user.

5. ACQUISITION GEOMETRY FOR THE PARENT'S SPARSE CONSTRUCTION

For a module forest, expanding each group of three edge coefficients by the
orthogonal 2x2 Hadamard transform gives four original-site pair coefficients
with the same total squared norm. Split them into same-site-index edges
(1-1 and 2-2) and crossed edges (1-2 and 2-1). Each class is a two-fold cover
of a module forest and hence itself a forest on the original sites.

A union of q module forests therefore splits into 2q ordinary forests. Edge
products on each ordinary forest are independent Rademachers under uniform X.
Holder's inequality over those forests and the unary part gives

 E exp(t theta^T phi(X))
        <=exp[(2q+1)t^2||theta||^2/2].

The difference row in (1), by Cauchy-Schwarz on its two uniform marginals,
is sub-Gaussian with proxy 2(2q+1)||theta||^2. Standard sphere-net covariance
concentration and a union bound over supports then give model-RIP for all
union-q-forest vectors using O(q^2 gamma^-2 k log k) independent rows, up to
confidence factors. Unlike global-cardinality contrasts, the noninvariant
module basis has exact covariance I, so no finite-k deterministic projection
loss remains.

Projection onto module-forest coefficients is explicit: keep all q unaries;
weight each candidate module edge by Jqq^2+Jqs^2+Jsq^2; take a maximum-weight
spanning forest by Kruskal; keep all three coefficients on selected edges.
This is the exact Euclidean projection onto the group-forest model. It is not
the generally hard problem of finding an arbitrary bounded-treewidth graph.

6. STRONG ORDINARY BASELINE IMPLEMENTED HERE

Full contrast least squares learns all D noninvariant coefficients from the
same observations, independent of g's magnitude/complexity. The executable
uses m=8D independent contrast rows, solves the actual least-squares problem,
checks the realized design singular values, projects by grouped maximum
spanning forest, and independently validates the result. There is no useful
graph or solver input. This strong O(k^2) observation / polynomial arithmetic
route must be compared with the parent's sparse forest-IHT route, which aims
for O(k polylog k) observations under approximate forest structure.

The full retained least-squares route also supplies a cheaper certificate on
the finite fixture; it should not be forced to buy the generic holdout. For its
realized full-column-rank design A, let s_min be the smallest singular value.
Conditional on A, each direction of the least-squares parameter error has
sub-Gaussian proxy at most sigma^2/s_min^2. A 1/2-net of the D-dimensional
unit sphere has size at most 5^D. A union bound gives, with confidence 1-delta,

 ||theta_hat-theta|| <= 2sigma/s_min
              *sqrt[2(D log 5 + log(2/delta))].

Add this radius to ||theta_hat-theta_forest||. Multiplication by 2sqrt(2)k gives
a simultaneous all-context regret certificate from the same retained training
data, with zero additional source observations. It is valid despite adaptive
forest projection because the parameter ball is uniform. The code implements
this stronger conventional comparison. On the k=10 fixture its certificate is
0.0387108 from 2320 pilot energy shots, while the deliberately generic holdout
certificate is 0.0969682 and costs 47328 extra shots. Both meet the requested
0.4 regret. These are implementation / comparison results, not a practical
speedup claim for the generic holdout.

The intended asymptotic opening remains a sparsely acquired candidate plus
independent certification of an unknown dense tail, which can avoid learning
all D coefficients. It must be compared against sparse recovery and other
retaining methods given the same quotient observations, not against raw
regression that needlessly predicts g.

The verifier may accept a cheap candidate regardless of how it was trained;
it does not imply every good candidate can be found cheaply. Ordinary
fixed-composition contrast regression and cluster expansion are especially
relevant predecessors. The claimed organizing step is acquisition and execution
in the quotient of the actual operation, with all interfaces paid, rather than
novelty of invariance, regression, norm estimation, or tree DP themselves.

7. PRESERVED FAILURES

 - A raw global predictor of E must confront arbitrary g on 3^k contexts;
   successful fixed-context design requires no such prediction. A model can
   have enormous raw prediction error and a small certified design regret.
 - This does not allow choosing the context: changing g leaves every contrast
   unchanged but can change the best occupancy context arbitrarily.
 - Arbitrary noninvariant high-order errors can hide on rare orientations.
   Their absence is the essential residual-class promise, not certified from
   the finite holdout itself.
 - Typical-context prediction error alone is not a global guarantee; the
   quotient moment and feature-leverage proof is the required bridge.
 - The small residual norm certificate remains conservative for certain dense
   high-rank discrepancies. A stronger operation-specific spectral/SDP
   certificate could accept them, but must earn its own acquisition and solve.

sparse lattice models for predictive materials simulations, npj Computational

Author-repository PDF:

They already target preservation of scientific ground-state decisions rather
than only average energy fit, combining compressive sensing and constrained
quadratic programming. Their stated exact preservation guarantee applies to
the input-reference structures; an iterative ground-state search extends the
checked configurations. Their discussion explicitly identifies the difficulty
of general out-of-sample global guarantees. This is a strong relevant baseline,
not evidence that decision-aware model learning itself is new here.

Huang et al., Finding and proving the exact ground state of a generalized Ising
model by convex optimization and MAX-SAT, Physical Review B 94, 134424 (2016).
https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.94.134424
Exact combinatorial upper bounds and convex lower bounds are established
alternatives for the downstream ground-state computation, with their actual
search cost. They must not be replaced by an artificially weak naive sampler.

Nelson et al., Cluster expansion made easy with Bayesian compressive sensing,
Physical Review B 88, 155105 (2013).
https://bsg.byu.edu/docs/papers/BCSCE.pdf
Sparse cluster-expansion recovery and experimental/training-structure selection
are established components. The new construction adopts that type of learning
machinery; its proposed organizing distinction is learning an executable
quotient operation with a uniform later-task certificate.
