OPERATIONAL CERTIFICATES FROM NOISY RANDOM RESIDUAL ASSAYS
Ordinary proof and construction, 31 October 2026

MAIN CLAIM, WITH ITS SCOPE

A learner can acquire a tractable quadratic surrogate, then use fresh random
energy experiments to certify one bound that protects all later adaptive
inverse-design queries. The certificate covers an unknown dense quadratic
discrepancy. It does not require computing the discrepancy's NP-hard maximum,
learning every discrepancy coefficient, or assuming that an exact sparse graph
survives perturbation.

The certificate is a posteriori: it may refuse an inadequate surrogate. The
surrogate's construction and optimizer must still be paid for. A complete
forest construction below pays both interfaces using ordinary tomography,
maximum-spanning forest, and dynamic programming. The certificate may also be
used with candidates acquired by much cheaper sparse or heuristic routes.

The statistical tools (fourth-moment bounds, median-of-means, sample splitting),
forest projection, and tree dynamic programming are established machinery.
This note supplies a concrete observation-to-certified-operation construction;
it does not claim that these components or its simple norm transfer are new.
Its candidate organizing principle is to acquire a certificate of the operation
one will execute, while leaving a dense scientifically irrelevant discrepancy
unidentified. A priority claim needs stronger literature comparison.

1. EXPERIMENTAL AND COMPUTATIONAL CONTRACT

Unknown E:{-1,+1}^n -> R is a quadratic multilinear energy:

 E(x)=c+sum_i b_i x_i+sum_{i<j} J_ij x_i x_j.

J can be dense and frustrated. Its entries, graph, and any useful factorization
are not supplied. A prepared x yields an independent energy observation with
mean E(x), centered sigma^2-sub-Gaussian error. The same bound may hold
conditionally for configuration-dependent noise. Every source observation,
configuration-preparation bit, and downstream arithmetic operation is charged.

The quadratic constitutive class is a scientific assumption. Finite random
validation cannot certify this class against arbitrary high-order spikes. The
theorem permits arbitrary dense *quadratic* misspecification, not unrestricted
unknown dynamics. Absolute noise scale also matters: if a Bernoulli instrument
reports energy normalized by an O(n) range, effective sigma=O(n), and the
repetition bill below becomes O(n^2). It is not dimension-free physical access.

Training data, failed earlier validations, and internal randomness may produce
any quadratic candidate Ehat. The validator uses new independent configurations
and new independent observations after Ehat is fixed. Ehat need not be a
statistically consistent estimate, but must be evaluable at an explicitly
charged cost C_eval.

A later task supplies a known query energy g and feasible set F. An actual
algorithm produces xhat in F with

 Ehat(xhat)+g(xhat) <= min_{x in F}(Ehat(x)+g(x)) + alpha.

We do not assume this algorithm as an oracle: below, a learned forest supplies
the explicit alpha=0 algorithm for arbitrary unary g. More complex task scopes
must supply and pay a constructive algorithm of their own. No guarantee of
cheap arbitrary combinatorial constraints follows from a good surrogate.

2. FOURTH-MOMENT LEMMA (SELF-CONTAINED)

Let R=E-Ehat, let R0=R-E_uniform R, and v=E_uniform R0^2. Then

 E R0^4 <= 15 v^2.

Proof. Replace the independent Rademacher variables one at a time by standard
Gaussians. Conditional on the remaining variables, the polynomial is affine in
the current variable. The first three moments agree, and replacing fourth
moment 1 by 3 can only increase the expected fourth power, because its fourth
coefficient is the nonnegative fourth power of the affine coefficient. The
second moment is unchanged. After replacement the centered polynomial is

 Q=G^T A G + a^T G - tr(A),

where A is symmetric and in this case initially has zero diagonal. Write
s=tr(A^2), t=||a||^2. Direct Gaussian moment calculation, or differentiation of
its moment-generating function, gives

 v=2s+t,
 E Q^4=3v^2+48 tr(A^4)+48 a^T A^2 a
      <=3(2s+t)^2+48s^2+48st
      =60s^2+60st+3t^2
      <=15(2s+t)^2.

This proves the lemma. It is an elementary sharpening of the ordinary
degree-two hypercontractivity bound; no new hypercontractivity claim is made.

For independent uniform X,Y define D=R(X)-R(Y). Its unknown constant cancels.
Independence and centering yield

 E D^2=2v,
 E D^4=2 E R0^4+6v^2 <=36v^2,
 Var(D^2)<=32v^2.

3. AN EXPLICIT NOISY MEDIAN-OF-MEANS CERTIFICATE

Choose confidence allowance delta in (0,1), measurement tolerance nu>0, block
size B=128, and an odd block count

 L >= 8 log(4/delta).

Use N=B L independent pairs (X_l,Y_l), each configuration uniform on the cube.
At each of the 2N configurations average

 M=max(1, ceil[4 sigma^2/nu^2 * log(4N/delta)])

independent energy observations. For each pair subtract the exactly evaluated
surrogate difference to obtain Dhat_l. Its noise is 2 sigma^2/M-sub-Gaussian,
so

 P(|Dhat_l-D_l|>nu)<=2 exp(-M nu^2/(4 sigma^2)).

A union bound makes all N pair errors <=nu with probability at least
1-delta/2. Let each block's observed root-mean-square be

 W_j = sqrt((1/B) sum_{l in block j} Dhat_l^2),

and let W be the median of these L numbers. Report

 S = W + nu.

For exact observations, Chebyshev gives

 P( (1/B)sum D_l^2 not in [v,3v]) <=32/B=1/4.

The blocks are independent. Hoeffding's bound for their good/bad indicators
shows that more than half are good except with probability <=exp(-L/8),
which is at most delta/4 under the chosen constants. On the bounded-noise
event, the Euclidean triangle inequality changes each block RMS by at most nu;
the median changes by at most nu as well. Consequently, on a joint event of
probability at least 1-delta,

             sqrt(v) <= S <= sqrt(3v)+2nu.                 (A)

Thus this is both a sound upper certificate and a constant-factor complete
test. For any target T, accepting S<=T certifies sqrt(v)<=T. It accepts with
the same confidence if sqrt(3v)+2nu<=T. Borderline models may be rejected or
require smaller nu. Rejection is not proof that the true task regret is large.

The constants are transparent sufficient choices, not lower bounds. Median
estimation, sequential confidence bounds, known Gaussian noise, or refined
moment bounds can improve them. The construction here pays the actual bill.

Finite readout precision: if each measured energy has additional deterministic
rounding error at most rho_meas and each surrogate evaluation has error at most
rho_eval, replace nu in (A)'s additive error terms by
nu+2rho_meas+2rho_eval. Means and sums can be enclosed by rational interval
arithmetic. Comparing squared nonnegative bounds avoids an exact square-root
oracle. Bit lengths are polynomial in input coefficient heights, log n, and
requested error-bit precision; no exact real arithmetic is required.

4. THE OPERATIONAL TRANSFER

Let K=n+n(n-1)/2. The nonconstant Fourier monomials of degree one and two form
an orthonormal family of K functions, each with absolute value one. Therefore

 sup_x |R0(x)| <= sqrt(K) sqrt(v),
 osc(R):=max_x R(x)-min_x R(x) <=2 sqrt(K) sqrt(v).

On the validation event the computable number

                   Gamma = 2 sqrt(K) S

upper-bounds osc(R). For every later task (g,F) and every implemented surrogate
solver with suboptimality alpha,

 E(xhat)+g(xhat)-min_{x in F}(E(x)+g(x)) <= alpha+Gamma.     (B)

Proof. If xstar is a true optimum, use surrogate optimality and subtract the
two residual values:

 [E+g](xhat)-[E+g](xstar)
 <=alpha+R(xhat)-R(xstar)<=alpha+osc(R).

This is one deterministic uniform event after validation. Future fields,
feasible sets, and requests may depend on training data, validation data, and
all previous outputs. There is no union bound over those future requests.
The guarantee is a regret statement at the precision the task requires;
exact stochastic output semantics are unnecessary.

For extensive accuracy epsilon*n, accept Gamma<=epsilon*n. As sqrt(K) is
asymptotic to n/sqrt(2), the required S threshold is Theta(epsilon), not
Theta(epsilon/n). The number of random configurations is O(log(1/delta));
the actual source count is

 2 B L M = O(log(1/delta) *
               max{1, sigma^2 nu^-2 log(log(1/delta)/delta)}),

with displayed sufficient constants above. For nu=Theta(epsilon), no n occurs
in the source count when absolute sigma stays fixed. There are nevertheless
O(n B L) random-bit / control-preparation operations and O(B L C_eval)
candidate-evaluation operations, plus source work. This is a verification
bound, not a dimension-free learner for arbitrary quadratic energies.

5. ADAPTIVE CANDIDATE SEARCH AND LIFETIME USE

At candidate number t>=1 allocate delta_t=6delta/(pi^2 t^2). The candidate may
depend on every earlier experiment and every failed certificate. Use fresh
independent validation pairs and energy observations for this candidate, with
the parameters of section 3 at delta_t. Conditional on the full past, the proof
is unchanged. Summing conditional failure probabilities gives

 P(any false certificate at any candidate number)<=sum_t delta_t=delta.

Stop at any accepted candidate. Its bound (B) then protects all future adaptive
tasks in its executable solver scope. Failed validation observations can be
used as training data for later candidates; only each candidate's own validator
must be fresh relative to that candidate. The total observation/control/work
cost through a chosen candidate is the sum of the explicit acquisition and
validation costs actually incurred. No free successful proposal or unlimited
candidate search is implied.

Reusing an old holdout after optimizing a new candidate against it is invalid:
when fewer than K independent residual constraints were observed, there are
nonzero quadratic polynomials in their nullspace. A data-dependent candidate
can fit the old holdout perfectly while having arbitrarily large error
elsewhere. The executable counterexample constructs this nullspace explicitly.

6. FULLY CONSTRUCTED CONSUMER: LEARNED FOREST INVERSE DESIGN

Ordinary acquisition option (not claimed optimal): use the paired all-plus /
all-minus flip identities to estimate b_i, and the four-point identity

 J_ij=[E(1)-E(1^i)-E(1^j)+E(1^{ij})]/4

to estimate all pairs. Every required full configuration and repetition is
charged. At one measurement batch there are O(n^2) energy observations and
O(n^2) physical bit changes if configurations are ordered efficiently; a naive
implementation's actual transitions are counted rather than asserted cheap.
Use any chosen initial repetition count; the candidate need not come with a
parameter-wise confidence claim. Later independent validation decides whether
its operational error is small enough.

Construct a maximum-weight spanning forest with weights Jhat_ij^2 by Kruskal's
algorithm. Retain all learned unary fields and only the selected edge weights.
For the fitted full quadratic polynomial this is its best Fourier-L2 projection
onto forest supports: the omitted coefficient squared norm is minimized by
maximizing the sum of squared weights retained, a graphic-matroid optimization.
This earns a useful representation without a structure oracle. Cost is
O(n^2 log n) after coefficient estimation and O(n) retained edges.

For arbitrary future unary g(x)=sum_i h_i x_i, solve each tree by two-state
dynamic programming. With a root, a child's message conditional on parent spin
s is

 m_child(s)=min_{t in {-1,+1}} [(bhat_child+h_child)t
               + Jhat_child,parent*t*s + sum_grandchild m_grandchild(t)].

Process leaves toward the root, minimize the two root choices, then backtrack.
This is O(n) arithmetic and O(n) storage per query. It works for all real unary
fields; no bounded-field condition or truthful pinning is required. Rational
inputs can use exact comparisons with polynomial bit growth.

An exact global cardinality budget can also be earned by tree DP carrying the
number of selected vertices in addition to the root spin, with convolution of
child tables. All budgets can be solved together in O(n^2) arithmetic and
O(n^2) storage (up to the fixed spin-state factor). To see the global bound,
at a merge of disjoint partial subtrees of sizes a,b>=1 the convolution costs
O((a+1)(b+1))=O(ab). Each pair of vertices is charged only at the merge where
its two partial subtrees first meet. Summing ab over the actual tree merges is
O(n^2); a dummy root joins disconnected components with the same accounting.
One budget can cap the count tables and may use less work. Backpointers recover
an actual assignment. The companion executable implements both ordinary
two-state DP and this cardinality convolution/backtracking, and checks the
latter against full enumeration on a small dense-perturbed instance. Arbitrary
unrelated combinatorial constraints are not free.

Run section 3's fresh validator on this forest candidate. If accepted, the
actual tree algorithm solves the true unknown, possibly dense/frustrated
energy's later inverse designs to the certified regret, without ever learning
or optimizing the residual dense interaction. If rejected, refine the candidate
or stop with no unsupported global-design claim.

For an instance whose true energy is within Fourier-L2 distance eta of a forest
and whose acquisition error makes the fitted coefficients within distance zeta
of truth, the selected forest's residual distance is at most eta+2zeta. This is
the triangle inequality plus optimality of forest projection. Thus acquisition
can be made sufficiently accurate to ensure acceptance whenever
sqrt(3)*(eta+2zeta)+2nu <= epsilon*n/(2sqrt(K)).

A fully explicit conservative finite acquisition guarantee is available from
four-point estimates: make every coefficient accurate to zeta/sqrt(K), using
O(sigma^2 K/zeta^2 * log(K/delta_train)) repetitions per distinct tomography
configuration. Total source work is O(sigma^2 K^2/zeta^2 log(K/delta_train)).
This is a sufficient but unattractive O(n^4) route, not a physical lower bound.
Stronger conventional random-design quadratic regression can learn the K
Fourier coefficients with O(K polylog K) designs and O(sigma^2 K/zeta^2)
noise-dependent observations, with its regression arithmetic charged. Sparse
recovery can improve the acquisition bill when its promises are justified.
Adopt those methods rather than claim the crude tomography floor unavoidable.

7. BASELINE COMPARISON AND WHAT REMAINS UNPAID

Strongest straightforward conventional route: fit the full degree-two feature
vector by stable randomized least squares / agnostic regression, prove an L2
parameter error bound, select the maximum-spanning forest, and optimize it by
tree DP. The same Fourier norm transfer already gives the regret consequence.
Thus the existence of a forest-based reliable inverse-design service is not
by itself an original theorem against this baseline.

What the residual certificate adds operationally is an acquired a-posteriori
check that can accept any inexpensive candidate regardless of how it was fit,
including a candidate whose dense residual was never estimated. Verification
cost is independent of the full parameter count in the quadratic/extensive
accuracy regime, while full coefficient learning must pay for that dimension.
This is a verifier-versus-learner distinction, not evidence that every required
good candidate can be found cheaply. To claim a major new end-to-end advance,
one must give a consequential candidate-acquisition setting where this
distinction yields a real resource advantage over the best retaining method.

8. DECISIVE FAILURES / BOUNDARIES

(a) Tiny per-edge errors and tiny per-site average prediction error can hide
    extensive design regret. Let Ehat=0 and

    R(x)=alpha/n * sum_{i<j} x_i x_j
        =alpha/(2n)*[(sum_i x_i)^2-n].

    Each omitted edge is alpha/n and sqrt(v)/n tends to zero. Give both models
    future unary fields h_i=-alpha/4. The zero surrogate chooses all plus,
    S=sum x_i=n. The true energy is minimized at S nearest n/4 of the correct
    parity. When n is divisible by 8, regret is exactly 9 alpha n/32.
    The operational certificate measures the unnormalized residual RMS and
    rejects at an epsilon scale below a constant multiple of alpha.

(b) A valid certificate can be conservative. A high-rank random-sign dense
    residual may have small operator norm and true oscillation O(sqrt(n)),
    yet constant Fourier-L2 norm. The certificate's O(n) oscillation upper
    bound can reject it for a desired extensive error. A hierarchy using
    learned spectral or SDP residual certificates may be stronger, but those
    acquired computations must be implemented and charged.

(c) Without the low-degree constitutive promise, a residual supported at one
    configuration can evade polynomially many random probes while changing
    an optimum by an arbitrary amount. No such unrestricted robustness claim
    is earned.

(d) Passing a reused, adaptively fitted holdout is not certification. The
    nullspace counterexample is executable and exact up to displayed numerical
    tolerances.

(e) Validation never makes arbitrary planning cheap. The forest learner and
    tree DP are actual constructed operations; future constraints outside that
    solver's scope need a new paid operation.
