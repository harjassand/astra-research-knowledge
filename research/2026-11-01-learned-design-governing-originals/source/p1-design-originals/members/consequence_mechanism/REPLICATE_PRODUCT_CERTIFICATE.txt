REPLICATED CONTRAST PRODUCTS: A FINITE-VARIANCE OPERATIONAL CERTIFICATE
31 October 2026

PURPOSE

Replace the older validator's pointwise-noise confidence event and its
per-observation repetition/union-bound bill with a different experimental
question. Two independent measurements of the same residual contrast allow
unbiased estimation of its squared signal. Only a conditional variance bound
is required; the source noise need not be sub-Gaussian and need not have a
finite fourth moment. The older interval construction remains a valid fallback
and can be cheaper when noise is already negligible.

1. SETUP

Use the actual module-swap or other explicit orbit-pair sampler from
MODULE_ORBIT_CERTIFICATE.txt / ORBIT_CONTRAST_CERTIFICATE.txt. The candidate is
fixed before this fresh validation set. Let D be its exact residual contrast,
v the squared quotient norm. The established degree-two/orbit argument gives

 E D^2=2v, Var(D^2)<=116v^2.

Each individual energy shot is conditionally mean-correct, has variance at
most sigma^2, and is independent of the other shots conditional on prepared
configurations. Average l shots per raw energy readout. At each orbit pair
obtain TWO independent replicate paired residual differences

 U=D+eta_1,   V=D+eta_2,

using four raw energy readouts, hence 4l actual source shots. Conditional on
the pair, eta_1,eta_2 are independent, centered, and each has variance at most
2t where t=sigma^2/l. Candidate evaluations are deterministic or interval
enclosed, rather than new random source readings.

2. UNBIASED CROSS-PRODUCT AND VARIANCE

Set W=UV. Then E[W | pair]=D^2, so E W=2v, without knowing the actual noise
variance or subtracting an estimated noise bias. Conditional independence gives

 Var(W | pair)
   =D^2[Var(eta_1)+Var(eta_2)]+Var(eta_1)Var(eta_2)
   <=4tD^2+4t^2.

Consequently

 Var W <=116v^2+8tv+4t^2 <=116(v+t)^2.                    (1)

No fourth moment of eta is needed: the product eta_1 eta_2 has second moment
E eta_1^2 E eta_2^2 by the independent replication. One must not replace the
two independent replicates with a single noisy square; that reintroduces an
unknown positive noise bias and can require a fourth-noise-moment bound.

3. FINITE CONFIDENCE CERTIFICATE

Use B=464 pairs per block and an odd number

 L>=8log(1/delta)

of independent blocks. For each block, Chebyshev applied to (1) gives

 P(|mean W-2v|>v+t)<=1/4.

With probability at least 1-delta, the median m of block mean cross-products
satisfies

 v-t <= m <=3v+t.

Therefore return

                   S=sqrt(max(0,m+t)).

On that one event,

                  sqrt(v)<=S<=sqrt(3v+2t).               (2)

The zero-noise/zero-residual case is deterministic and is interpreted directly.
A negative sample median is permitted; clipping is valid because m+t>=v>=0
on the asserted event. There is no pointwise confidence event for any source
observation and no union-bound logarithm in the required noise averaging.

For the k-module task, multiply S by 2sqrt(2)k for the all-context regret
certificate, or by 2sqrt[2a(2k-a)] for a context with a active modules. For
requested epsilon*n=2epsilon*k regret, the norm threshold is T=epsilon/sqrt(2).
For example choosing l=max(1,ceil[8sigma^2/T^2]) ensures t<=T^2/8 and leaves
a constant acceptance margin for a candidate with v sufficiently below T^2.
If sigma is already small relative to epsilon, l=1 suffices.

The exact shot count is 4*B*L*l. Thus it is

 O(log(1/delta)*max{1,sigma^2/epsilon^2}),

with displayed sufficient constants. It has no log(number-of-probes) precision
factor and needs only finite source variance. The same configurations can be
held while obtaining their two independent replicate readouts; extra shots do
not require extra changed-control operations. Fair bits for the orbit sampler,
candidate evaluation, precision and dynamic range remain fully charged.

For candidate t_index use delta_t=delta/[t_index(t_index+1)] and fresh data.
The conditional proof and summable union bound protect arbitrary adaptive
candidate search and all subsequent adaptive tasks, exactly as before.

4. DETERMINISTIC NUMERICAL / READOUT ROUNDING

Suppose the observed replicate contrasts U_obs,V_obs differ from their ideal
unrounded values by at most a known rho each. This can include readout
quantization, surrogate evaluation intervals and numerical subtraction. Define

 C=rho(|U_obs|+|V_obs|)+rho^2,
 W_upper=U_obs V_obs+C.

Then, deterministically,

 UV <= W_upper <= UV+2C.

Use the median of block means of W_upper in place of m. The lower inequality
in (2), and hence soundness, remains valid without any additional failure
probability or pointwise probabilistic noise bound. Completeness becomes

 S^2 <=3v+2t+2 C_max,

where C_max is the largest observed block-average correction. This quantity
is explicit and recorded. Interval arithmetic can similarly enclose products,
block averages, the median, and final comparison. Large nuisance energies are
still read with enough absolute precision: rho cannot be assumed negligible
merely because the algebraic nuisance cancels. Bit cost includes dynamic range.

The prototype uses double arithmetic on moderate finite inputs and records a
conservative contrast-rounding radius 1e-8 in its comparison fixture. The
ordinary proof supports rigorous interval arithmetic; the numerical run is
not presented as a floating-point verifier for arbitrary magnitudes.

5. EXECUTABLE COMPARISON

The function statistical_validate_group_action in orbit_certificate.py performs
this experiment with actual independent noise draws. The same file retains
the older validate_group_action pointwise-noise method as a baseline/fallback.
Both receive the same acquired candidate and source information. Both use an
explicit same-orbit sampler, not an inaccessible conditional reset.

The comparison includes sigma=0.0001 and sigma=0.002 at epsilon=0.02, on an
unknown five-module forest plus a dense noninvariant tail and an arbitrary
occupancy nuisance of amplitude 10^6. Results and all source/control counts are
in orbit_certificate_results.json. At very low noise the old method can be
cheaper because it needs only two shots per pair. At sigma=0.002 the old method
requires many repeated shots to make every observation accurate simultaneously;
the replicated product estimator uses l=1, and its total shot reduction is
reported from the actual execution. These are construction comparisons, not
an assertion that either displayed constant is an optimal practical validator.

STRONG BASELINE STILL APPLIES

When the learner already paid for full contrast least squares, the same-data
parameter confidence ball in MODULE_ORBIT_CERTIFICATE.txt may certify the
operation at zero additional experimental cost. This new validator is useful
when a candidate was acquired without that full parameter representation or
when its unknown dense residual needs an independent check. It does not erase
the cost of finding such a candidate or establish a speedup over all retaining
methods by itself.
