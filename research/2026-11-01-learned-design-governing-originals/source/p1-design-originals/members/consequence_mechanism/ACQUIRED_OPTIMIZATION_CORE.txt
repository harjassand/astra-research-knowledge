ACQUIRED OPTIMIZATION CORES FROM NOISY ENERGY EXPERIMENTS
Working construction, 31 October 2026

STATUS AND SCIENTIFIC TASK

Task: after experimental acquisition, answer an unlimited adaptive sequence of
bounded external-field inverse-design problems for an unknown frustrated binary
energy. Return a configuration with a stated energy regret. Exact sampling is
not required. The operation is global optimization, not prediction or merely
evaluation of a supplied inference program.

This note provides a complete constructive bridge under a high-disorder / small
residual-core condition. It does not establish a new general algorithm for QUBO.
Single-variable persistency is established dead-end elimination (DEE); stronger
QPBO / improving-map methods should be used whenever applicable. Sparse group
discovery and exhaustive component optimization are also established ideas.
The candidate organizing contribution is to acquire a robust optimization
certificate and only the interactions incident to its uncertain core, rather
than acquire an accurate whole-system model. The whole confidence family then
has one executable reduction; no optimistic QUBO oracle is supplied.

The strongest conventional route already combines persistency, parameter
learning, and exact residual optimization. Consequently this construction is
earned supporting capital / a concrete opening, not a priority claim or by
itself evidence of a major theoretical advance. Its exact missing-next-step is
to acquire substantially stronger computational certificates, especially when
simple dominance leaves a large core, without invoking hard optimization over
every alternative confidence model.

1. MODEL AND EXPERIMENTS (NO GRAPH OR SOLVER ORACLE)

There are n binary variables x_i in {-1,+1}, with unknown energy

 E(x;h) = sum_i (b_i+h_i)x_i + sum_{i<j} J_ij x_i x_j.

Unknown J is symmetric, J_ii=0, its graph has maximum degree at most Delta, and
|J_ij| <= Jmax. These are supplied physical model bounds; the graph and all
coefficients are unknown. b_i can be arbitrary real numbers. A later query is a
known vector h with |h_i| <= H. It may depend on all previous observations and
answers. The output requirement is

 E(x_out;h) - min_x E(x;h) <= epsilon * n.

An experiment prepares a specified full configuration x and returns an
independent observation Y(x) with mean E(x;0). Its centered noise is
sigma^2-sub-Gaussian. Every energy observation and every changed control bit is
charged. This is a substantive experimental access assumption, not a consequence
of passively observing thermal samples. If this access cannot physically be
provided, this theorem does not apply. Independent measurement repetitions can
be replaced by a proved concentration statement with its actual costs.

Let x^i denote x with spin i flipped. Four energy experiments construct

 g_i(z) = [Y(x)-Y(x^i)]/2, with x_i=+1 and x_-i=z;
 Q_i(S,z) = [g_i(z)-g_i(z')]/2,

where z'_j=-z_j on S and z'_j=z_j elsewhere. Then

 E Q_i(S,z) = sum_{j in S} J_ij z_j.

Four independent energy measurements make Q noise sigma^2/4-sub-Gaussian.
No small denominator, reset-to-latent-state operation, or useful representation
is assumed. Physical configuration preparation is explicit and charged.

Likewise [g_i(+1)+g_i(-1)]/2 has expectation b_i and noise proxy sigma^2/4.
The two all-plus/all-minus baselines and their single flips can be shared among
all i. A batch costs 2+2n energy measurements and O(n) changed control bits.

2. COARSE ACQUISITION OF A ROBUST REDUCTION

Choose a coarse resolution r>0. Estimate every b_i to error at most r using
the paired all-plus/all-minus experiment. With failure allowance delta_0,

 N0 >= [sigma^2/(2r^2)] log(2n/delta_0)

independent batches suffice (round up to at least one). This follows from
P(|mean-b_i|>r) <= 2 exp(-2 N0 r^2/sigma^2) and a union bound. Let bbar_i be
the estimate, and define

 P = {i: |bbar_i| > Delta Jmax + H + r},
 a_i = -sign(bbar_i) for i in P,
 U = [n] \ P, u=|U|.

On the one acquisition event, every global minimizer, for every allowed future
h, satisfies x_i=a_i for i in P. Indeed

 |b_i+h_i| > sum_j |J_ij|,

with sign fixed by bbar_i, so flipping any contrary x_i strictly lowers E
regardless of all other spins. This is ordinary DEE with a confidence margin.
The conclusion is uniform over adaptive future fields, with no new union bound.

All interactions entirely inside P can now be discarded without learning them:
they are constant in every remaining optimization. Only the coefficients b_i
for i in U and J_ij with at least one endpoint in U can affect later choices.

This does not identify a faithful whole-system model. It acquires a reduction
certificate plus a task-sufficient partial model.

3. NOISY DISCOVERY OF SIGNIFICANT INCIDENT INTERACTIONS

Set

 tau = epsilon n / [2(1+2 Delta) max(1,u)].

We require a graph estimator with the following simultaneous properties:

 (i) every retained edge is a true nonzero edge;
 (ii) every omitted true incident edge has magnitude <= 2 tau;
 (iii) every retained coefficient and every b_i, i in U, has error <= tau.

These conditions suffice; no minimum nonzero edge size is assumed. Tiny real
interactions are paid for in the requested energy regret, not declared absent
without a consequence bound. In particular spurious weak edges cannot inflate
the computational graph on the successful acquisition event.

Here is a fully explicit sparse group-discovery construction, included to avoid
supplying a sparse-recovery oracle. For each i in U, put [n]\{i} in a balanced
binary tree of groups. At a visited group S, perform R trials. Each trial draws
independent Rademacher signs z_j, j in S, and estimates Q_i(S,z) by M repeated
four-experiment measurements. Call the group positive if any trial has absolute
estimated sum greater than tau. Split positive internal groups; inspect positive
leaves. At a leaf j, retain its estimated J_ij only if its absolute estimate is
greater than tau. Use a fresh single-coordinate estimate if needed, to error at
most tau/4. A retained estimate may be used immediately without full recovery.

There are at most 2n-1 groups per row. Set, conservatively,

 R = ceil(12 log(8 n max(1,u)/delta_1)),
 M = max(1, ceil[(8 sigma^2/tau^2)
                 log(16 n max(1,u) R/delta_1)]).

The same M bound covers final leaf estimates, allowing an additional union
bound by using the displayed conservative factor. More generally one may
choose R,M directly so the following two finite unions each cost <=delta_1/2:
all tested observation averages have error <=tau/4, and no group containing an
edge of magnitude >2tau is missed.

Proof of group detection: for S0=sum_{j in S} J_ij Z_j with variance v,
E S0^4 <= 3v^2. If S contains an edge of magnitude >2tau, then v>4tau^2.
Paley-Zygmund gives

 P(|S0| >= sqrt(v/2)) >= 1/12.

On the noise event this implies measured magnitude >tau. Failure in R trials
has probability at most (11/12)^R <= exp(-R/12). A union bound over at most
2n u groups proves that every >2tau edge is found. A zero group is never
positive on the noise event. Thus every explored positive node contains a true
edge, and there are at most 1+2 Delta ceil(log2 n) visited groups per row.
All retained leaves are true edges. Retained coefficients have error <=tau/4;
an omitted edge is <=2tau. Symmetrize two independently acquired estimates for
edges within U by averaging; averaging preserves the error bound. The set of
retained edges is still a subset of the true graph.

For a conservative unconditional work cap, stop if a row explores more than
1+2 Delta ceil(log2 n) nodes. On the successful acquisition event this never
occurs. The cap prevents an exceptional noise realization from creating an
uncontrolled amount of work.

Acquire the remaining b_i to error tau by further paired experiments. All
observations already acquired can be retained. With allowance delta_2, at most

 Nb = max(1, ceil[sigma^2/(2tau^2) log(2 max(1,u)/delta_2)])

batches for U suffice.

The sparse group route costs at most

 4 M R u [1+2 Delta ceil(log2 n)]

energy observations, plus the coarse and fine field measurements. Its control
preparation and arithmetic are explicitly polynomial: a direct implementation
uses O(M R n u [1+log(Delta+1)]) control-bit operations and comparable work to
write group signs, plus polynomial bookkeeping. This potentially quadratic
control cost must not be omitted in a practical claim. The source count is
small only when individual prepared energy measurements are an appropriate
physical resource.

Alternative established tomography, often cheaper in low measurement noise:

 J_ij = [E(1)-E(1^i)-E(1^j)+E(1^{ij})]/4.

Estimate all pairs incident to U with this four-point identity, using shared
baselines and single flips, threshold at tau, and use confidence width <=tau.
There are O(nu) possible pairs. If |Jhat-J|<=tau, retaining only |Jhat|>tau
gives no false edges and drops only edges <=2tau. An explicit implementation
can choose the cheaper route from its actual noise and control-cost model.
This is a conventional alternative, not an adversarially weakened baseline.

4. EXECUTABLE OPTIMIZER AND REGRET

Build the retained graph on U. For each connected component C, form its fitted
energy, including its learned interactions to the fixed spins a_P and the
later known fields h_C. Enumerate all 2^|C| configurations and choose a minimum.
No optimizer is supplied. Gray-code updates cost O(Delta 2^|C|+Delta |C|) per
component. Together with fixed a_P these choices give xhat(h).

There are at most Delta u true edges incident to U. On the acquisition event,
for every assignment y_U and every allowed h, the difference between true and
fitted reduced energies, after removing their irrelevant constants, is at most

 D = tau u + 2 tau Delta u = (1+2Delta) tau u.

Hence

 E(xhat(h);h)-min_x E(x;h) <= 2D <= epsilon n.

Proof: the true optimum is in the pinned face. Compare its fitted value with
the fitted minimum, then use the two uniform energy error bounds. Known h
cancels from the discrepancy. No rare probability factor or tiny global
optimality gap occurs; the accuracy is precisely the specified task regret.

The same acquired state answers every adaptive future bounded h with this
guarantee on one event of probability at least 1-delta_0-delta_1-delta_2.
Its computation per query is fully visible:

 O(n + Delta sum_C 2^|C|).

The residual component list itself is a computational certificate. A user can
set a maximum component size before acquisition and stop with the unresolved
core, rather than silently invoke an unavailable solver. This is a limitation
and an honest paid interface, not a general polynomial-time claim.

5. A NONTRIVIAL SUFFICIENT REGIME WITH NO SUPPLIED GRAPH

Assume additionally that the fixed unknown graph and couplings are independent
of independent intrinsic fields b_i. Write

 B = Delta Jmax + H + 2r,
 q = sup_i P(|b_i| <= B).

For example a density bound M_density gives q<=2 M_density B. On the coarse
confidence event U is contained in W={i: |b_i|<=B}. Thus the acquired graph is
a subgraph of the site-percolated graph G[W]. This remains true for all future
adaptive h. Couplings may have arbitrary signs and arbitrary frustration.

A graph of maximum degree Delta has at most (e Delta)^(k-1) connected sets of
size k containing a fixed vertex. Consequently

 P(v belongs to a W-component of size >=k)
   <= q (e Delta q)^(k-1).

If 2e Delta q<1, then

 E sum_C |C| 2^|C| <= 2nq/[1-2e Delta q].

Here the sum may be over acquired components and the expectation is counted
on the successful coarse-acquisition event; domination by W gives the bound
without conditioning the field law on experimental success. This establishes
linear expected enumeration work on the success event. Failed calibration must
not be allowed to contribute delta 2^n uncharged runtime; use the explicit
component-size cap below.

For q e Delta<1, with probability at least 1-delta_run, every component has
size less than

 L = 1 + ceil[ log(nq/delta_run) / log(1/(e Delta q)) ],

with a harmless max(1,nq/delta_run) inside the logarithm. Check observed
component sizes, and cap enumeration at L. This yields a polynomial worst-case
execution budget and returns the stated answer with probability at least
1-delta_0-delta_1-delta_2-delta_run. A stronger choice delta_acq small enough
that delta_acq times the explicit cap is O(n) also makes the unconditional
expected execution work O(n), up to the stated constants.

The significance is not that random fields magically solve arbitrary hard
optimization. The experimentally acquired certificate proves that all hard
choices are confined to small components, and the algorithm actually solves
them. The field-disorder hypothesis is only one sufficient way to ensure this
certificate is cheap. For a particular system the observed component sizes and
confidence bounds are the operational certificate; no random-field assumption
is needed to verify the reduction or regret statement.

6. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ADD TO THE STRONG BASELINE

Established pieces to adopt:
 - DEE / improving mappings certify persistent variables.
 - QPBO / roof duality and submodular min-cut can be substantially stronger.
 - Sparse experimental regression can recover local interactions.
 - Component enumeration / treewidth algorithms solve the residual problem.
 - Combinatorial pure exploration already focuses samples on answer changes,
   provided its optimization oracle is computationally available.

Concrete bridge earned here:
 - neither the interaction graph nor a downstream optimizer is received;
 - bulk interactions are never identified when their endpoints are pinned;
 - uncertain tiny edges are omitted with a charged optimization regret;
 - no spurious edges enlarge the learned computational core on the one event;
 - one robust face reduction works for infinitely many adaptive bounded fields;
 - the actual optimizer and all experimental interfaces are implemented;
 - statistical acquisition cost and residual computational cost are separate.

Useful failures / limits:
 - Arbitrary unbounded future h can cancel every intrinsic field; the uniform
   small-core guarantee disappears. Bounded query scope is essential.
 - A small residual core does not by itself bound measurement cost: tiny true
   decision gaps may require arbitrarily fine observations for exact output.
   The theorem requests epsilon*n regret and explicitly pays epsilon^-2.
 - High disorder alone is not a new optimization method; established percolation
   and persistency routes are serious precedents, not baselines to omit.
 - Measurement variance independent of total system energy and arbitrary
   configuration preparation are strong, explicitly paid physical assumptions.
 - Model misspecification is not solved by this theorem. Unknown higher-order
   terms require an independently acquired uniform regret / local-dominance
   remainder certificate, not an informal assertion that they are small.
 - Coefficient error in l2 or average prediction error does not certify DEE or
   protect core topology. The one-sided significant-edge graph is deliberate.
 - A generic true-model optimizer plus an active-learning confidence set can
   still leave hard alternative optimization problems. This construction avoids
   that oracle by obtaining one reduction valid throughout the entire box.
