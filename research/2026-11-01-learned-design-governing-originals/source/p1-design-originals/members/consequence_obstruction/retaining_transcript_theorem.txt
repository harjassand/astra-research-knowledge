RETAINING CALIBRATION AT THE ACTUAL TRANSCRIPT ACCURACY
====================================================

This is a conventional comparator, not a claim of a new Bernoulli factory.
Its purpose is to prevent an artificial advantage obtained by summing
per-output total-variation errors. Ordinary relative entropy accumulates
quadratically in the log-odds error, even for adaptive later queries.

1. Contract
-----------
There are m independent stationary Bernoulli sources with unknown vector
p in [0,1]^m. The sources themselves, including their acquisition and input
setting costs, are available to both procedures. A retained calibration
uses N samples from each coordinate and stores the empirical proportions
p_hat. The same calibration data D and query policy are given to an ideal
comparison procedure and to the retaining simulator.

At time t the policy, possibly depending on D and all previous output bits,
selects f_t(.;h,D):[0,1]^m -> (0,1). Assume an earned uniform certificate

    ||gradient logit f_t(x;h,D)||_1 <= L_t

for every admissible x, h, D. A certificate only along the true trajectory
does not suffice. The ideal outputs Ber(f_t(p;h,D)); the simulator outputs
Ber(f_t(p_hat;h,D)). Their respective policies see their respective output
histories, as usual in adaptive transcript comparisons. Evaluation and
rational-Bernoulli generation must actually be implemented and charged.

2. Finite-horizon confidence theorem
-----------------------------------
On ||p_hat-p||_infinity <= eta, conditional on D,

    KL(P_{1:T} || Q_{1:T}) <= eta^2 sum_t L_t^2 / 8,
    TV(P_{1:T}, Q_{1:T}) <= eta sqrt(sum_t L_t^2) / 4.

Proof. Set theta=logit f_t(p;h,D) and theta_hat=logit
f_t(p_hat;h,D). The mean-value theorem bounds |theta-theta_hat| by
L_t eta. The Bernoulli log-partition function A(theta)=log(1+exp(theta))
has A'' <= 1/4. Its Bregman divergence, which is Bernoulli KL in the
appropriate orientation, is at most (theta-theta_hat)^2/8. The conditional
KL chain rule sums these bounds at the same history h under P. Pinsker
gives the displayed TV bound. No nonadaptivity of the queries is used.

Hoeffding plus a union bound gives the confidence event with probability
at least 1-delta for

    N >= log(2m/delta)/(2 eta^2).

For L_t <= L and desired transcript TV epsilon, choose
eta=4 epsilon/(L sqrt(T)), provided eta<1; trivial cases can simply use
the inequality directly. The total calibration cost is

    m ceil[L^2 T log(2m/delta)/(32 epsilon^2)]

raw Bernoulli samples. Joint TV including the calibration transcript is
at most epsilon+delta. If only each coordinate derivative is bounded by
K, then L=mK and the displayed cost has m^3, not m^2, dependence.

3. Stronger expected-KL theorem, without a confidence failure event
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Let M_N=||p_hat-p||_infinity. Hoeffding and integration of the tail give

    E M_N^2 <= [log(2m)+1]/(2N).

Indeed P(M_N^2>s)<=min(1,2m exp(-2Ns)); integrate from zero to infinity,
splitting at log(2m)/(2N). Using an upper bound above one is harmless.

Although queries can be selected using D, their uniform sensitivity
certificate bounds KL by L_t^2 M_N^2/8 pointwise in D. Hence the joint
laws of D and the T outputs satisfy

    KL(P || Q) <= [log(2m)+1] sum_t L_t^2 /(16N),
    TV(P,Q) <= sqrt{[log(2m)+1] sum_t L_t^2 /(32N)}.

Thus N >= [log(2m)+1] sum_t L_t^2/(32 epsilon^2) is sufficient. There is
no delta term. This calculation is valid at p on the boundary too,
provided all output functions remain strictly between zero and one.

4. One infinite service, uniformly accurate on the entire transcript
-------------------------------------------------------------------
At time t, retain N_t samples per coordinate, acquiring only increments.
The ideal service receives exactly the same source transcript and query
policy. Source draws have identical conditional distributions in the two
services; only output Bernoulli draws contribute to relative entropy.
If L_t<=L and

    N_t >= c t [log(t+1)]^2,

then

    KL(P || Q) <= L^2 [log(2m)+1] C0/(16c),
    C0 = sum_{t>=1} 1/[t log(t+1)^2] < 6.

For the simple constant, the first term is 1/log(2)^2<2.082. For t>=2,
1/[t log(t+1)^2] <= 1/[t log(t)^2]. The latter is decreasing and its
sum from two is at most 1/[2 log(2)^2]+integral_2^infinity dx/[x log(x)^2]
<1.041+1.443. Total <4.566<6.

Choosing c=3 L^2 [log(2m)+1]/(16 epsilon^2), and N_t=max(1,ceil(c t
log(t+1)^2)), guarantees whole infinite-transcript TV <= epsilon.
The bound follows first for every finite prefix from the KL chain rule,
then for the infinite sequence because finite-cylinder sigma-fields
increase to the full product sigma-field and relative entropy is the
supremum of the finite-prefix relative entropies. Equivalently, use
martingale convergence for likelihood ratios and standard entropy
variational characterization. This also bounds any measurable adaptive
stopping-time output and any subsequent data processing.

By time T the total retained source draws are mN_T, namely
O(m L^2 epsilon^-2 T log(T+1)^2 log(m+1)). The storage is m integer counts
and denominators, O(m log N_T) bits, unless the policy itself asks to keep
more source information. Fresh sources are not free; their number has
been counted. A slower diverging logarithmic correction, for example
N_t of order t log(t+1) log(log(t+e))^2, also works with adjusted constants.
This is a sufficient comparator, not a statistical or computational
lower bound and not an assertion of optimality.

5. Actual execution
-------------------
For rational-program queries, evaluate f_t at rational p_hat. Standard
exact rational arithmetic and a uniform-fair-bit rational Bernoulli draw
implement the output, with their bit costs charged. At each query the
input denominator bit size is O(log N_t). If only an interval evaluator
is available, include its certified logit error beta_t in the quadratic
KL bound (L_t M_{N_t}+beta_t)^2/8. Summable beta_t^2 is sufficient in the
infinite service. An evaluator taking poly(log(1/beta_t)) work incurs
only polylog(t) additional cost for a power-law beta_t schedule. No exact
unknown-parameter evaluation is used.

6. Positive partition circuits have a dimension-free L certificate
------------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose F and G are positive weighted sums of monomials in positive
affine factors, total degree at most D, each factor at least a>0, and
each factor depends on one p coordinate with slope of magnitude at most
one. For one monomial M,

    ||gradient log M||_1 <= D/a.

The gradient of log F is a convex combination of these monomial
log-gradients, so ||gradient log F||_1<=D/a, and similarly for G.
For f=F/(F+G), logit f=log F-log G and therefore L<=2D/a. This remains
valid for positive arithmetic circuits only when their expanded
monomials have the stated degree; a compact circuit can encode very
large degree. The arithmetic evaluator still has to be charged.

7. Scope and scientific implication
----------------------------------
This does not dominate every exact factory. Exact generation can remove
epsilon^-2 dependence and can satisfy a task whose semantics truly
require exactness. It does establish that finite transcript accuracy,
and even fixed total variation on an entire infinite adaptive service,
do not intrinsically force quadratic-in-horizon calibration. A strong
retaining baseline is almost linear in horizon at fixed epsilon.

Many scientific tasks need substantially less than full transcript TV:
an expectation, a decision with a margin, or a one-sided test. In those
tasks this comparator may still overpay. Conversely, unbounded relative
error or conditioning on arbitrary tiny events is not controlled by a
small unconditioned total-variation guarantee.

No novelty or minimax-optimality claim is made for the standard KL and
retention arguments. Sample amplification offers still stronger routes
in appropriate families. Primary references:

Axelrod, Garg, Sharan, Valiant, Sample Amplification: Increasing Dataset
Size even when Learning is Impossible, ICML 2020:
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/axelrod20a.html

Axelrod, Garg, Han, Sharan, Valiant, On the Statistical Complexity of
Sample Amplification, Annals of Statistics 2024:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04315

These references are comparators for statistical experiment simulation;
the theorem above uses only the elementary stated KL, concentration,
and retention calculations and does not import a sample-amplification
theorem or claim its rate is optimal.
