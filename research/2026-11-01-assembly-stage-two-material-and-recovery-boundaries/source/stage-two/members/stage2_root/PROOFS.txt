CATALYTIC COMPLETION AS AN ASSEMBLY EXIT: PROOFS AND FAILURES
2026-11-01. Original construction and analysis in this investigation.
Status: mathematical and molecular construction; no new wet-lab result.

Purpose: recover a working physical RNA-protein machine while retaining the
chemistry of the RNA that was manufactured. Recovering its DNA sequence is a
different operation. Reverse transcription/transcription do not reproduce a
complete modification pattern or assembled state.

No variable below is assigned a favorable experimental value for the new
realization. Rates from short peptides, isolated domains, other organisms or
other immobilization geometries are not substituted.

1. LOAD PATH: WHY COLLECTION IS NOT AN EXACT CATALYTIC CERTIFICATE

A ribosome is halted at a stop codon without class-I release factors. Capture
its nascent product through a distal affinity handle outside the exit tunnel.
The connection is support--handle--peptide--ester--tRNA--ribosome, with further
noncovalent peptide/tunnel contacts. The intended event is cognate-RF-promoted
ester hydrolysis, followed by physical exit of the same manufactured subunits.

Proposition 1. Collection certifies a chosen catalytic event exactly only if
every accessible path to collection crosses that event, and the collected
object is the candidate that traversed the path.

Proof. A noncertifying path with nonzero probability is a counterexample.
A passenger is also a counterexample. Conversely the two conditions force
the identified collected object's trajectory through the event.

This physical design DOES NOT satisfy an exact cut condition. Peptidyl-tRNA
can dissociate intact; the affinity attachment can dissociate; hydrolysis can
occur without correct stop recognition. Multiple ribosomes can share a
message. completion_exit.py explicitly enumerates these bypasses.

The original simultaneous termination/recycling version adds an avoidable
bypass: RF3/RRF/EF-G can promote peptidyl-tRNA drop-off. It is rejected.
The corrected version collects a class-I-RF-only eluate FIRST and recycles it
in a separate vessel. Sufficient RF1 for a single-turnover pulse is charged;
efficient RF1 cycling on the support is not assumed. This removes one
reagent-enabled bypass, not intrinsic leakage.

Therefore the operation offers at most calibrated probabilistic enrichment.
The defining measurements are joint hydrolysis/physical-release kinetics,
drop-off, support leakage, passenger contamination and subsequent activity.
There is no supplied perfect conformation observer or ideal state filter.

2. GOOD-POPULATION YIELD AND PURITY

Let p be the incoming fraction that meets a specified future service, not
merely initial GFP fluorescence. Let a be a good particle's conditional
probability of surviving loading, capture, washing, release, collection and
recycling. Let b be a bad particle's whole-process acceptance probability.
All quantities concern the SAME population, chemical state and time horizon.

For N inputs:
  accepted good = N p a;
  accepted bad  = N (1-p) b;
  accepted purity P = p a / [p a + (1-p)b].

For a>0 and 0<p<1, P >= 1-epsilon iff
  b/a <= epsilon p / [(1-epsilon)(1-p)].

These are specification requirements, not predictions:
  p=1%, target P=99%: b/a <= 1.02030405e-4;
  p=10%, target P=99%: b/a <= 1.12233446e-3.

A large selectivity ratio against one inactive control cannot be transplanted
to a rare functional population with different defects. RISE's reported
control-specific >1000-fold selectivity is not the new gate's value of a/b.

At p=.01, a=.5 and target purity .99, b must be <=5.10152025e-5.
With zero false acceptances in independent identically distributed negative
challenges, the exact one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is
  b_upper=1-.05^(1/n).
At least58,721 such challenges are needed to put that upper bound below the
required b. This does NOT cover unrepresented defect classes, nonindependent
events, unknown p/a, or a changing batch. It exposes part of the observer cost.

3. FINITE RELEASE WINDOWS WITHOUT INVENTED KINETICS

Let A(t) be the measured cumulative correctly hydrolyzed reference population
physically recovered by time t. A collection window [u,v] is eligible only if
its measured increment in A supplies required recovery and a valid upper
bound B([u,v]) on false mass IN THAT WINDOW meets the purity bound above.
A difference of two pointwise upper CDF bounds is not such an interval bound.
With CDF bounds L_B(t)<=F_B(t)<=U_B(t), one may instead use
min(1,U_B(v)-L_B(u)), or obtain a direct simultaneous interval/density bound.
Controls must justify coverage of the manufactured population. No-RF release
alone does not bound abnormal RF-induced drop-off or noncognate termination
of a defective particle.

A release CDF normalized to already captured complexes is a CONDITIONAL
stage probability. To use it in section2, multiply by the appropriate joint
loading/capture/wash survival for each material class; do not silently replace
whole-process a,b by conditional release probabilities.

No exponential assumption is needed. Under the EXTRA assumption that good
and bad release are one-step exponentials with rates kT,kF, recovery >=eta
and false release <=epsilon from a zero-start window require
  -log(1-eta)/kT <= T <= -log(1-epsilon)/kF.
Thus a window exists iff
  kT/kF >= log(1/(1-eta))/log(1/(1-epsilon)).
For eta=.95, epsilon=.001, the required ratio is 2994.23416.
This is not a measured discrimination ratio.

Hydrolysis and physical escape can be separate. For serial rates h and e:
  F(t)=1-[e exp(-ht)-h exp(-et)]/(e-h), h!=e;
  F(t)=1-(1+ht)exp(-ht), h=e.
Hence F(t)=h e t^2/2+O(t^3), while one-step leakage begins as b t.
A very early collection can favor leakage; shorter is not automatically
cleaner. Faster chemical cleavage alone does not establish faster recovery.
This onset assumes every starting molecule is unhydrolyzed. Prehydrolyzed
but still trapped complexes add an initially linear physical-escape term.

For B--h-->H--e-->valid and B--b-->invalid, set a0=h+b:
  P_B=exp(-a0 t);
  P_H=h[exp(-a0 t)-exp(-e t)]/(e-a0);
  equal-rate limit P_H=h t exp(-a0 t);
  P_invalid=b[1-exp(-a0 t)]/a0;
  P_valid=1-P_B-P_H-P_invalid.
Eventual correctly hydrolyzed fraction <=h/(h+b). Waiting cannot remove a
competing failure. The code checks conservation and equal-rate limits with
dimensionless algebra vectors, not ribosome parameter guesses.

4. REPEATED TESTS DO NOT CREATE INDEPENDENT ERROR REDUCTION

For successive acceptances E1,...,En, exact good recovery is
  P(E1|G) product_{j>=2} P(Ej|G,E1,...,E(j-1)).
Unconditioned probabilities multiply only with the required independence or
conditional invariance. The same issue applies to bad-particle acceptance.

Counterexample: 1% of defective particles are persistent cheaters that pass
both tests; the rest pass neither. Each test's false acceptance is 1%, but
joint false acceptance is 1%, not .01%. The code retains this witness.

Repeated service tests establish reuse on the tested challenges. They do not certify
all codons, all sequences or unobserved lifetime. Ten rounds with conditional
good recovery .95 retain .95^10=59.87%; with .8 they retain 10.74%. These are
specified accounting scenarios, not measurements.

5. PASSENGERS AND THE PRICE OF SINGLE LOADING

The preserved object is an individual manufactured 50S/23S lineage (and,
when manufactured, a 30S/16S lineage), not a fixed original 70S pair.
Normal recycling can separate and re-pair subunits.

One completed tagged chain can capture all ribosomes sharing its mRNA.
Thus arbitrary bulk translation plus capture does not prove that each
recovered ribosome completed the message.

For a SPECIFIED Poisson loading process with mean lambda per isolated site:
  P(N>=2 | N>=1)=1-lambda/(exp(lambda)-1);
  fraction of individual ribosomes at multioccupied sites=1-exp(-lambda).
The second is size-weighted. To bound it by epsilon requires
lambda<=-log(1-epsilon). At epsilon=.001 this costs approximately 999.5 sites
per expected ribosome. Actual initiation need not be Poisson. This witness
shows why 'use excess message' is not an earned cheap single-particle gate.

The multioccupancy expression describes the INPUT particle distribution.
Selection can reweight occupied sites, and defective particles may have
different loading statistics. It is not an upper bound on accepted-output
passenger contamination without further selection-weighted measurements.

The concrete route needs a verified single-initiation cohort, removal of free
subunits/initiators, and monosome isolation or direct stoichiometry controls.
Preparation loss and time belong in a. If the necessary suppression cannot
be reached within budget, the service fails. Single loading is not supplied
as a perfect helper.

There is now an earned preparation lead: O'Connor et al2020
(10.1016/j.jmb.2020.05.010) purified70S initiation complexes through a1.1M
sucrose cushion and performed elongation without new subunits/initiation
machinery. Deliberately adding those ingredients generated the polysome arm.
This is evidence for a realizable single-round preparation, not a measured
contamination ceiling or recovery yield for the proposed captured reporter.

6. PURIFICATION ALONE CANNOT CREATE CORRECT OUTPUT

Proposition 2. Assume bad particles do not inhibit good ones, no repair or
new assembly occurs in selection, and selection does not improve any good
particle's output or survival. At a common deadline, selection/testing cannot
increase total correct product over direct use of the same original mixture.

Proof. Couple each good input particle to its selected counterpart. A
discarded counterpart contributes zero. A retained one after delay t_d can
produce at most its original remaining output after t_d; the original could
also produce before t_d. Sum and subtract qualification opportunity costs.
No exponential lifetime or identical-particle assumption is needed.

A physical selector may meet a purity requirement, relieve measured
inhibition, reduce downstream spending on bad machines, or protect successful
machines from a destructive reset of rejects. Those are distinct services.
A larger active percentage is not itself a manufacturing advantage.

7. SELECTIVE EXPORT PLUS REPREPARATION: A CORRECT RENEWAL ACCOUNT

Under an explicitly homogeneous re-preparation premise, let per-attempt
probabilities be p for correctly accepted output and f for falsely accepted
output. Rejection probability is 1-p-f; a rejected RNA is irreversibly lost
with probability d. Only if surviving rejects return to the same operational
starting distribution is retry probability
  r=(1-p-f)(1-d).
For r<1:
  good yield=p/(1-r), bad yield=f/(1-r);
  loss yield=(1-p-f)d/(1-r), mean attempts=1/(1-r).
Accepted purity remains p/(p+f). Retries do not cleanse a false exit.
This loop does not duplicate RNA: new RNA must come from the chemical source.

An incorrect modification or broken backbone is not repaired by repeating
an annealing cycle. More generally, let R be a substochastic retry kernel
over actual material classes, with column-distribution convention.
Expected visits from x are (I-R)^(-1)x when spectral radius(R)<1. Exit rows
times this vector give accepted yields; cost rows give expected costs.
Deterioration, replenishment and reset loss belong in the kernel/account.
These are ordinary absorbing-process identities. The unearned scientific
claim is a useful physical exit, not the mathematics.

8. THE ACTUAL RNA LIGATION TREE HAS UNEQUAL LEAF DEPTHS

Nineteen continuous pieces are connected by nine three-piece operations in
three purified tiers. Uniform operation recovery u=q*r and equal initial
amounts give limiting output u^3. This is not eighteen independent bonds
forming in one pot. The last 287-nt IVT piece enters only the final operation.

Backward accounting: for unit output, each child of an operation with
recovery u requires parent/u input. With hypothetical q=.272 and r=.8,
eighteen pieces need about97.06 copies per output; the last needs about4.60.
Total input is about255,315 nucleotide equivalents per output RNA, before
monomer synthesis, end processing, splint DNA, enzyme inefficiency and later
assembly losses. This is a finite cost exposure, not an rRNA yield estimate.
The specified q already charges RNA lost in unsuccessful ligation products;
ATP turnover, enzymes and initial fragment synthesis losses remain uncharged.

9. WHAT WOULD ESTABLISH THE OPERATION

Split one assembly batch among cognate-RF exit, competitive-affinity recovery
and puromycin recovery. Preserve RNA-chemistry identities; count original
scaffolds, active particles, recovery, complete products and correct output
over time. Compare at a common budget and deadline. Positive evidence needs
physical recovery AND a second service, plus stop discrimination, product
identity, passenger and spontaneous-release controls.

No such new physical result was obtained here. The construction and proofs
prevent occupancy, peptide-bond activity, a higher percentage, genetic
recovery, or a perfect selective-reset helper from standing in for it.
