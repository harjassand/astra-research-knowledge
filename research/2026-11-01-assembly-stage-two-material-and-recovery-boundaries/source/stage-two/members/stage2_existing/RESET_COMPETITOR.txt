RESET, RECOVERABLE FUNCTION, AND THE STRONG CONVENTIONAL COMPETITOR
Independent construction by assembly_existing, 2026-11-01.
Scope: conclusions from primary evidence and conditional mathematics; no new wet-lab result.

DECISION

An experimentally real structural reset exists. It does not yet establish that resetting
the actual failed manufacture fraction creates additional active ribosomes. Moreover,
selective export before resetting is not unique to RF-dependent on-bead release:
competitive affinity recovery can protect the same successful RNA-containing objects.
Global factors-only maturation is the strongest first comparator. If it preserves active
material while providing the same repair, selection has no yield rationale.

WHAT EARLIER RECONSTITUTION EARNS

Nierhaus and Dohme, PNAS 1974, DOI 10.1073/pnas.71.12.4713.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC433966/
The native-RNA extraction and total reconstitution route recovers particles capable
of protein synthesis with natural R17 RNA as well as poly(U). The original abstract
specifies 40 C, 20 min, 4 mM Mg, 400 mM NH4Cl, followed by 50 C, 90 min, 20 mM Mg.
This earns a once-through route from isolated native components to function, not
indefinitely repeated regeneration or repair of arbitrarily malformed chemistry.

Amils and colleagues, efficient total 50S reconstitution:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC342176/ ; PMID 27764.
Direct phenol extraction from 70S was used to minimize nuclease fragmentation;
protein isolation used acetic acid and rapid removal. The reported 50-100% activity
across functional assays is not a measured 50-100% recovery of original RNA molecules.

Preparation procedures influence reconstitution, NAR 1980:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC323967/
https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/8/5/989/1039903
RNA and protein preparation change appropriate magnesium and protein:RNA conditions.
An extracted RNA is consequently not a universally specified, memory-free reset state.

No retrieved primary study supplies repeated reset/reconstitution mass balance,
chemistry retention, and activity conversion for the actual selection-rejected
manufactured population. This is a bounded search finding, not proof of absence.

COMPETING PROCESS WITH THE SAME AVAILABLE OPERATIONS

The following integration is proposed, not claimed as already demonstrated.

1. Assemble the exact same chemically specified input batch and give it the same
   reporter translation challenge and single-cohort access conditions as the RF route.
2. Capture complexes through the accessible distal peptide tag. Competitive FLAG
   elution recovers intact RNCs. Terminate and recycle in bulk, then protect the
   resulting accepted RNA/subunits from any subsequent harsh reset.
3. Retain the uncaptured/wash material as a separate population. It includes false
   negatives, access failures, chemically defective RNA, and genuinely trapped
   assembly intermediates; do not call all of it inactive or repairable.
4. Compare continuation with EngA/ObgE against a reset branch. EDTA restoration plus
   EngA/ObgE is the strongest directly demonstrated reset for native-derived particles.
   Conventional Mg/temperature reconstitution is another complete comparator.
   Transfer of either operation to the specified new RNA chemistry must be measured.
5. Rechallenge with a distinct message and appropriately orthogonal capture scheme.
   Old peptide tags, free reporter, old mRNA, and protein complements must not supply
   a shortcut through the second selection.
6. Count original RNA lineages, intact RNA recovered, active particles, correct protein
   output, remaining service life, and resources. Each extra cycle requires real
   chelator/salt handling, protein/factor replacement where lost, energy and time.

This protects the successes from an externally imposed reset without using catalytic
RF elution. It therefore removes generic selective-reset protection as a unique
advantage of the parent's RF architecture. It does not solve the RNA chemical factory.

RF ELUTION'S NARROW POSSIBLE EXTRA FUNCTION

Competitive affinity recovery followed by bulk termination cheaply supplies recovery
of the same physical RNA and repeated translation service when termination defects
are not the scarce problem. Puromycin is another physical release control, but it
does not test canonical stop-codon hydrolysis.

An RF-only on-bead pulse, physically collected before adding recycling factors, can
condition export on prompt RF-dependent hydrolysis followed by physical escape.
This adds a discrimination against elongation-competent but termination-defective
particles. It is not a general test of fidelity, correct assembly, or long lifetime.

A stronger conventional control can also emulate this discrimination: competitive
FLAG-elute RNCs; apply the same RF-only pulse; deplete still peptide-linked complexes
using an orthogonal distal tag or remove free FLAG before recapture; collect released
ribosomes and only then recycle. This costs an additional tag/separation/matrix or
buffer exchange. It is a constructive rival, not an experimentally validated loop.
Free FLAG would inhibit direct same-matrix recapture, so that cost cannot be omitted.
Free released peptide also consumes the second matrix's capacity.

Thus RF-on-bead exit may compact two separations or avoid competitor elution, but its
advantage is empirical. Passenger contamination, nonspecific binding, tag synthesis,
release kinetics, recovery loss and readout consumption remain charged to both routes.

STATE-RESOLVED DOMINANCE RESULT: NO HOMOGENEOUS RETRY ASSUMPTION

Let x denote the full relevant state and history of an RNA-containing object, and
mu its initial distribution. Let V(x) be its expected remaining correct-service
capacity if successfully exported without reset. Let G(x) be this capacity under
global factors-only maturation, and E(x) capacity after the reset branch. Let s(x)
be acceptance probability and eta(x) in [0,1] exported survival/recovery. Compare
matched deadlines and account for reagent/factor supply; selection costs are initially
omitted, favoring the selective construction.

    Y_select <= integral [s(x) eta(x) V(x) + (1-s(x)) E(x)] dmu(x)
    Y_global  = integral G(x) dmu(x).

If G(x) >= V(x) and G(x) >= E(x) for every relevant x, then Y_global >= Y_select.
Proof: eta V <= V <= G and E <= G; a convex combination of two quantities no greater
than G is no greater than G; integrate. This does not assume identical retry
probabilities, independent cycles, homogeneous chemistry, or equal lifetimes.

The comparison is particularly immediate for selective export followed by the same
factors-only operation G on rejects: if G preserves or improves every object's usable
capacity, selection cannot increase aggregate capacity. A separate scarce purity,
assay throughput, incompatible downstream condition, or inhibitor-removal objective
would have to supply its rationale.

For EDTA versus factors-only, a necessary optimistic break-even condition is

    integral [(1-s)(E-G) + s(eta V-G)] dmu > 0.

The first term must supply enough extra repair to pay export losses and any loss of
factor-only maturation on the selected branch. Real testing, transport, and reagent
costs make the requirement stricter. If the selection step changes surviving rejected
objects, use conditional post-selection kernels rather than reusing an unconditional E.

The primary evidence does not establish the pointwise premise. Coupled assembly and
translation makes preservation-compatible factors-only maturation a serious rival;
it does not establish that the same actual rejected states are repaired without EDTA.

DECISIVE TEST AND FALSIFIERS

Split the actual rejection population from the same chemically specified batch into:
 A. no reset, matched additional time;
 B. EngA/ObgE alone under translation-compatible conditions;
 C. EDTA, restoration, EngA/ObgE;
 D. conventional Mg/temperature reconstitution.
Keep a common original-batch factors-only arm, and compare RF export with competitive
recovery. Match deadline, RNA input, protein supply, access probability and measurement
burden. Activity after treatment is insufficient: measure increment over A/B and
distinguish survival of false-negative good particles from conversion of failed ones.

The renewal rationale fails if C produces no incremental active RNA over A/B, if its
losses consume that gain, or if defects that these treatments cannot repair or
functionally compensate dominate the nonfunctional fraction. EDTA and annealing do
not themselves write missing modifications or repair broken backbones. Functional
compensation for a missing modification is a distinct possibility requiring evidence;
it must not be confused with chemical restoration. The RF selector's distinct rationale fails if competitive recovery supplies
equal or better subsequent correct-service capacity at matched purity and total cost.
Neither a geometric retry calculation nor recovery of already native particles resolves
these conditions. A selective loop may remain useful for a separately demonstrated
purity constraint; that is a different claimed capability from increased manufacture.
