The strongest opening is direct access to rare conditioned quantum states

I chose computing and preparing an interacting thermal state after a specified rare observation as the central problem.

The strongest result is a constructive separation between observation rarity and conditional computational difficulty. Under the acquired representation and fluctuation promises, the calculation pays for the observation’s curvature, the system’s fluctuations, positive-mode competition, physical accuracy and the requested output. It does not acquire an additional factor proportional to the inverse probability of the observation.

Three results make that statement possible:

A relative approximation for every supported imaginary-time matrix element, which remains valid after rare filtering.
An acquired sampler for general convex observation penalties, extending the quadratic mechanism while retaining the interacting conditional system.
A clean coherent conditional-state construction, including inverse and reflection guarantees, from expected conditional prediction loss.

I also obtained a complementary answer to the positive-mode bottleneck: pressure density admits a global retained-coordinate calculation whose outer cost has no power of system size, under the stated fixed physical promises. Relative normalizers and states still require more information.

These are ordinary proved constructions, supported by independent audits and executable checks. The new physical execution is a six-spin dense calculation. The general signed, retained-mode posterior algorithm has substantial explicit costs and has not been executed end to end; a practical advantage over strong alternatives remains unestablished.
