Result: additivity does not make a quantum resource computable

Using N675 as the accepted premise you specified, I completed a construction proving that symmetric-side-channel-assisted quantum capacity is not uniformly computable—even to fixed additive accuracy—despite being additive, convex, and single-letter.

The result survives on explicit neighborhoods of channels with strictly positive Choi matrices. An additional construction transfers it to an established assisted entanglement-distillation measure.

Scope of the resolution

The selected question is resolved: an established additive, convex, single-letter quantum communication resource can resist every uniform fixed-accuracy evaluator, including on explicitly robust full-rank instances. The same obstruction reaches an operational entanglement resource through a completed, probability-accounted local construction.

This does not settle additive-approximation computability of ordinary unassisted quantum capacity. N684’s ordinary-capacity route incurs an unknown reference-transport block length, which can destroy the computable rate margin. I examined that obstruction and retained the assisted resource convention explicitly.

The scientific contribution is the completed quantitative reduction, the impossibility of effective auxiliary truncation, and the channel-to-state transfer. It establishes a limit on universal quantum-resource optimization and certification; it does not demonstrate a new physical device or claim an efficient practical compiler.
