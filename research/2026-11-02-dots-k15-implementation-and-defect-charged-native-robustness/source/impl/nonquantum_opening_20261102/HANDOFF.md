# Independent non-quantum pass: route-closing handoff

## Result

**No new positive scientific capability was earned in this pass.** The most consequential physical opening inspected was a lithium-metal cell that permits local metal nucleation and finite growth yet excludes a spanning electronic path at specified full-cell capacity, current, cycle life, separator thickness and load. This is a meaningful missing operation, but the indispensable construction failed. Do not present it as a new battery architecture, theorem about actual cells, or device-safety certificate.

### Strongest useful findings

1. Finite biaxial compression is not a topological containment mechanism. In the elementary pressurized-crack model, opening traction is p - P sin²(theta); for every finite P and positive p there are nonhorizontal opening-admissible cracks. A finite metal-inventory bound alone does not exclude an arbitrarily thin conducting path. The explicit stress/geometry calculation and qualifications are in BATTERY_CONSTRUCTION_GATE.md.
2. Resource-matched literature is substantially stronger than a pristine unmodified separator. Existing work already includes engineered transverse cracks, biaxial prestress, and constrained-reaction self-limiting interlayers. No novelty is claimed for those ideas. Their acquired device budgets do not yet certify the desired no-spanning operation. In particular, current records must retain half-cycle capacity, separator thickness, external loads and fixture costs.
3. The attempted concrete self-insulating guard, lithiated SmNiO3, fails its absolute electronic-leakage gate. The reported saturated resistivity corresponds to about 10^-5 S/cm electronic conductivity. Its large relative ON/OFF ratio does not make it a sufficient electronic barrier; lithium-metal compatibility and near-zero-potential reversible reset are also unestablished. PRIMARY_MATERIAL_OPENING.md records source values, geometry and anisotropy caveats.

## What remains genuinely missing

A material-specific, acquired lower bound on the complete propagation/insertion free energy, or a validated stochastic crossing law, including fracture, loss of confinement, creep, bulk electronic leakage, correlated defects and finite operating area/time. Merely assuming an intact separating guard, a prescribed effective modulus, independent defects, or exact transverse growth would hide the hard part. No such positive witness was obtained.
