FINAL MATHEMATICAL AUDIT OF ROOT PROOFS.txt
1 November 2026

Verdict
-------
The operator construction is mathematically sound under its stated common-Gibbs,
positive bounded-contrast assumptions. The scalar inverse theorem, the geometric
exponential-sum construction, and the centered-energy source extension survive
independent audit. The logarithmic-stiffness work statement also has a constructive
finite-precision route, provided the support-count, backward-error, phase-tracking,
and source-conditioning details below are included.

This is an audit of a theorem and a partially implemented algorithm. It does not
establish historical novelty, a conventional-algorithm lower bound, an end-to-end
directed-rounding implementation, or a large-system high-order performance result.
The successful target-start and multiscale competitors remain part of the decision.

1. Statements that pass
-----------------------
For the even wall-parity sector of the zero-field ring, the domain-wall heat-bath
rates and their symmetrization are correct. The free-fermion cancellation is the
identity a+c=d+e. The physical gap is the sum of the two lowest positive normal-mode
energies, not necessarily the lowest single-particle energy. The no-wall target is
the pair of globally aligned spin configurations after quotienting orientation.

An endpoint-invariant local multiplier A_i preserves detailed balance with the
same Gibbs law. Consequently m K0 <= K <= M K0 holds as an operator-form comparison.
Each COMPLETE local E_i annihilates the common ground vector. This property must
not be assigned to its individual Majorana monomials.

The return identity W/[pi_b(1-pi_b)] is correct for the specified equilibrium law
conditioned outside the target. It does not require preparing that distribution in
the computation. It also does not constitute a physical preparation protocol.

The sharp Neumann relative error is correct:

    |W_k-W| <= rho^(k+1) W.

The proof uses a positive scalar spectral measure, so it applies to any nonzero
source in the orthogonal complement of the stationary vector. The Chebyshev
polynomial and its relative scalar error are also correct. Treat rho=0 separately:
the perturbation vanishes after scalar recentering and the reference inverse alone
suffices. Expressions containing 1/rho should not be evaluated in that case.

The geometric quadrature is valid. The lower bound loses at most eta/(4x) at each
end of the integral. For the upper bound, each left-sum rectangle is bounded by
(1+eta/2) times the integral over the preceding rescaled interval; these intervals
are disjoint. The resulting q depends on log(Lambda/g), not Lambda/g.

The Rtilde argument is correct. In particular,

    d = eta/[(1-eta)(1-rho)]

controls the relative perturbation of K, and the enlarged preconditioned spectral
radius is at most (1+eta)rho. There is no omitted eta=O(g/Lambda) condition. For an
explicit conservative budget at 0<delta<=1, one may take

    eta <= delta(1-rho)/16,

choose the enlarged-interval polynomial to have error at most delta/4, and reserve
the remaining error budget for finite arithmetic. These choices keep d<1 and the
enlarged radius below one. Optimizing constants is unnecessary for the theorem.

The multi-time tail bound is valid only after complete local words are assembled.
Use common finite nodes for their monomials. Separately integrating monomials to
infinite time would generally produce divergent stationary contributions.

2. Locality correction
----------------------
In the input contract, r counts wall coordinates on which A_i depends. E_i also
contains the generator acting on the two flipped walls. Therefore define

    ell_i = |support(A_i) union {i,i+1}| <= r+2,
    ell = max_i ell_i.

Use 4^ell as a conservative monomial-count bound and 4^(ell j) in the work formula.
Alternatively redefine r everywhere to mean total operator support. An unannounced
reuse of r for those two different meanings undercounts the word expansion.

The correction does not change polynomiality at fixed locality. Endpoint-invariance
and coefficient bounds can be checked by enumerating the union support, at cost
O(n 2^ell).

There is also a useful explicit coefficient bound. A complete local heat-bath term
has operator norm nu_i. Since A_i is constant on each flipped pair, it commutes
with that local term. Hence

    ||E_i|| <= (M-m)nu_i/2.

Expanding an ell-mode operator in Majorana/Pauli monomials gives the conservative
bound

    sum_alpha |coefficient_(i,alpha)| <= 4^ell ||E_i||.

Thus the total coefficient magnitude is bounded by an explicitly computable
quantity of order 4^ell (M-m) sum_i nu_i. There is no uncharged local expansion
oracle in the fixed-locality claim.

3. Finite-precision contract for Theorem 2
----------------------------------------
The input must have a finite effective encoding. Rational data, ordinary algebraic
data, or explicitly given elementary expressions with certified polynomial-cost
evaluation to p bits are sufficient. Arbitrary unspecified real parameters are
not a bit-complexity input model. Likewise, the required reference spectral
bracket must be acquired from the small explicit matrix with a controlled
residual, rather than assumed from an unverified floating eigensolver.

No relative accuracy requirement should be imposed on every single-particle
energy or on every individual eigenvector. A single normal-mode energy can be
much smaller than the physical even-sector gap. Nearly degenerate eigenvectors
can rotate sharply while the actual semigroup remains well conditioned. Requiring
their separate relative accuracy would introduce an unnecessary condition number.

Use backward error instead. Normalize the reference so c Lambda=1. Suppose the
largest quadrature time is T and the reconstructed quadratic Hamiltonian Khat
satisfies

    ||Khat-c K0|| <= zeta

in the full represented fermionic Fock space, with its scalar term retained.
For a 2n-mode quadratic coefficient residual, an explicit bound follows by summing
the corresponding operator coefficients, or by O(n) times the small-matrix norm
plus the scalar residual. Then Duhamel's formula gives

    ||exp(-Khat t)-exp(-c K0 t)|| <= t zeta exp(t zeta),
    0<=t<=T.

An orthogonal canonical factorization with a certified reconstruction residual
suffices; inverse eigenvalue separations do not occur in this bound. Certified
small-matrix spectral enclosures can be refined until the physical two-mode gap
has a positive relative bracket. This requires precision logarithmic in the
reference spectral condition number, not resolving every smaller mode relatively.

Retain the scalar offset when factoring the Hamiltonian. Do not silently reset it
to zero after an approximate diagonalization. Tiny negative energies caused by
rounding may be clipped or shifted only with the resulting operator residual
included. If a scalar factor exceeds one, its excess is bounded by the same
backward-error budget; it cannot simply be discarded.

Parity must also be retained. Particle-hole changes and orthogonal transformations
of determinant -1 alter the Fock parity convention. Use a parity-preserving
canonical circuit, or explicitly account for the transformed occupied reference
mode and the scalar change. A covariance matrix alone does not encode that bookkeeping.

Let A_abs be an explicit bound on the sum of absolute weighted word coefficients,
including the inverse polynomial coefficients, local monomials and source pairs.
Let W_lower be an explicit lower bound for the desired positive inverse quadratic
form. For the hitting-time source,

    W_lower=(1-pi_b)/(M Lambda).

Choosing, for example, zeta smaller than a constant multiple of

    delta W_lower/[A_abs (k+1) T]

controls the error contributed by all reference semigroups. The same absolute
budget applies to the individual Gaussian amplitudes. Since

    sum_l w_l <= O(g^(-1) log(1/eta)),

and local coefficient sums have the explicit bound above, log A_abs contains only
polynomially many terms of size log(Lambda/g), log s, ell, k, source coefficient
bits and log(1/eta). This gives a polynomial precision budget. The more accurate
generic scaling is O((k+1)log(Lambda/g)) for the quadrature amplification, including
the order-zero term, rather than O(k log(Lambda/g)) alone.

4. A constructive, phase-sensitive Gaussian amplitude algorithm
---------------------------------------------------------------
Bounded circuit entries by themselves are not a complete stability proof. The
following standard-chart construction makes the needed absolute-error operation
explicit and avoids assuming a perfectly conditioned BCS chart.

(a) Compile the quadratic semigroups into canonical Gaussian rotations and
normal-mode attenuations. A scalar attenuation exp(-epsilon t n) with epsilon>=0
is the vacuum-to-vacuum block of a fermionic beam splitter with a fresh vacuum
ancilla and cosine exp(-epsilon t). All beam splitters are unitary on the enlarged
space. Ancilla postselections are deferred to the final Fock amplitude. Previously
used ancillas are not reused.

(b) Expand complete local insertions in even Majorana monomials, with all scalar
coefficients and phases retained. Each such monomial is a norm-one Gaussian
unitary up to its explicit phase. A scalar word is therefore a Gaussian unitary
circuit amplitude on N=O(n(k+1)) modes, multiplied by the already charged scalar
coefficients. Polynomially many two-mode gates suffice for its Gaussian rotations.

(c) Maintain the covariance matrix AND one reference Fock amplitude with its
phase. Choose a well-supported occupation chart by successively selecting a mode
outcome of conditional probability at least 1/2. Gaussian occupation conditioning
preserves Gaussianity. In exact arithmetic, the selected Fock configuration has
probability at least 2^(-N), hence amplitude magnitude at least 2^(-N/2).

In finite precision, use a slack bound such as a selected conditional probability
at least 1/3, established by the current error budget. The resulting lower bound
is 3^(-N). These exponentially small bounds cost O(N) precision bits, not an
exponential number of operations. Sequential conditioning is used to select the
chart; it is not an uncharged physical measurement.

(d) After mapping that reference occupation to the vacuum by explicit
particle-hole transformations, its Gaussian U block has singular values at most
one and

    |det U_chart| = reference Fock probability >= 2^(-N)

in the exact construction. Thus sigma_min(U_chart)>=2^(-N); with the finite-
precision slack, replace 2 by 3. The corresponding chart inverse requires only
O(N) additional bits. The determinant equals the probability here: the amplitude
is its square root. Confusing those two quantities gives a factor-of-two error
in this auxiliary bound, though not in the polynomial conclusion.

(e) A two-mode gate makes any selected new reference amplitude a sum of at most
four old occupation amplitudes. Evaluate those old amplitudes by Pfaffians in
the old chart and retain the scalar phase. The newly chosen chart amplitude is
bounded away from zero as above. Pfaffian evaluation with polynomially bounded
matrix size and exponentially bounded chart entries needs only polynomially many
extra precision bits for a specified absolute error. Gate count, chart changes
and rounding accumulation must all be included in that error budget.

This gives polynomial work and polynomial precision for the desired ABSOLUTE
circuit amplitude error, including when the final requested amplitude is zero
or very small. The final signed sum obtains its relative accuracy from the
separate positive lower bound W_lower. Computing only a Born probability or
propagating only a covariance matrix would lose the phase information required
for that signed sum.

For the elimination step, choose a maximum-magnitude remaining skew-matrix entry
as each paired pivot, or use a division-free absolute-error controlled Pfaffian
algorithm. Complete pivoting bounds multipliers and exponential element growth;
arbitrary pivoting is not justified merely by bounded initial entries. The
resulting growth contributes polynomially many precision bits. This is an
algorithm choice within the stated polynomial work, not a new physical premise.

The construction assumes the known classical Gaussian-state operations:
canonical two-mode gate decomposition, occupation conditioning, and
phase-sensitive Fock/Pfaffian amplitudes. These are mathematical operations, not
additional physical baths or observers. This backend is constructed at the
algorithm level; the root's current code does not implement it end to end at
high order or arbitrary precision.

5. Source extension and energy observable
----------------------------------------
For a centered real classical observable A, f=(A-<A>_pi)sqrt(pi) lies in the
correct parity sector and is orthogonal to the ground vector. Reversibility gives

    integral_0^infinity Cov_pi(A(X_t),A(X_0))dt = <f,K^+ f>.

The scalar spectral arguments apply without change. What must be acquired is a
polynomial-size Gaussian/monomial representation of f, its centering, and a
positive norm bound. An arbitrary verbally described observable is not thereby
covered. Count L^2 source pairs and the source monomial degrees in the word work.

For dimensional consistency and possible cancellations, use a source condition
quantity such as

    kappa_source = (sum of absolute source coefficients)/||f||,

where the source terms are normalized Gaussian or norm-one Majorana images.
Charge log kappa_source, together with coefficient evaluation costs. A bare
log(1/||f||) misses source rescaling and can be negative for a large observable.
If f is exactly zero, chi=0 and a positive-relative-error task is inappropriate.
For a promised nonzero effective source, its norm may be certified by precision
refinement; the cost depends on the stated source condition quantity.

For the dimensionless Ising energy, apart from its irrelevant additive constant,
A=f^dag diag(2J) f. With the real canonical matrices used in the root code,

    B=U^T diag(2J) V,   F=B-B^T,

the centered source is exactly

    sum_(j<k) F_jk d_j^dag d_k^dag |g>.

The two-particle states are orthonormal. Consequently

    Var_pi A=sum_(j<k)|F_jk|^2,
    chi_A,0=sum_(j<k)|F_jk|^2/(epsilon_j+epsilon_k).

Both formulas are correct. Only pair energies enter, consistent with the even
physical gap. The same source is valid for the perturbed dynamics because the
stationary law is unchanged.

The output chi is a one-sided integrated covariance. If called a two-sided
zero-frequency power spectral density, the conventional value is 2 chi. No
conductivity, current response, conservation law or thermodynamic limit follows
from this energy autocovariance calculation alone.

Independent executable audit: audit_poisson_source.py uses physical spin energies
and the separately constructed spin-flip generator, then solves the augmented
Poisson system directly. It does not call the root's dense generator. At n=4,6,8:

    largest variance absolute error       3.33e-15
    largest integrated-response rel error 2.45e-15
    largest covariance absolute error     3.33e-15

The actual nonintegrable response also satisfies the sharp Neumann bound through
degree five in all three cases. Results are in audit_poisson_results.json. These
are floating-point checks, not directed-rounding certificates.

6. Executable limitations and repairs
------------------------------------
The original model() code computes a=nu-c and e=nu-d. For sufficiently saturated
logistic rates this can replace a physically positive reverse rate by exact zero.
For example, large negative couplings can round c to nu although a is nonzero.
Compute the complementary logistic rates directly:

    a=nu*expit(+2(J_i+J_j)),
    e=nu*expit(+2(J_j-J_i)).

This postpones floating underflow and avoids cancellation; it does not make
arbitrary finite parameters representable in double precision. Preserve analytic
a+c=d+e structure when forming the quadratic reference, and regard roundoff in
the reconstructed generator as a numerical error rather than a physical change.

The direct product formula for the parity partition function can also overflow
or cancel for large or frustrated couplings. A scaled positive two-parity transfer
recurrence or log-sum-exp evaluation avoids subtracting nearly equal partition
weights. Known current moderate-coupling examples are unaffected.

The stable first-return determinant and the first-insertion Wick implementation
have independent checks, but they are not the general high-order backend used in
the work proof. The precision stress test at two slow edges demonstrates a real
failure of double precision and a successful high-precision repair. It does not
demonstrate a total advantage: the corresponding few-sector conventional
elimination also succeeds.

7. Claim limits that should remain explicit
-------------------------------------------
At fixed locality, contrast and target accuracy, the constructed algorithm has
polynomial arithmetic/bit work in the physical size and polylogarithmic dependence
on reference spectral stiffness, with the additional input/source conditioning
quantities charged above. The dependence on accuracy and contrast is not fully
polynomial. At extensive perturbation count s=n, the n^k word count can become
prohibitive. A few nonintegrable defects are a substantially easier use case.

Rarity alone is not the advantage. The executed target-start occupation estimator
already obtains the actual nonintegrable 256-site mean without rare waiting.
Bounded common-Gibbs contrast also constrains the perturbed inverse quadratic
form between the corresponding constant multiples of the reference. It cannot
produce a new equilibrium rare-event exponential scale.

A small gap by itself does not imply that the observable is hard for simulation;
slow spectral weight must be present. Conversely, an explicit slow/fast hierarchy
can be accessible to classical conditional averaging or exact block elimination.
No theorem here excludes those algorithms, graph transformation, or low-rank
representations on the admitted class. Translation-invariant normal-mode pair
factorization is a particularly strong conventional construction. The disordered
Gaussian source and direct scalar contraction are the more relevant distinction.

The proof earns a bounded mathematical opening and a second actual Poisson
observable. It does not yet earn general energetic changes, hard constraints,
arbitrary initial/target laws, arbitrary molecular interactions, or a completed
civilisation-changing physical technology. The root's final scientific decision
states that limitation correctly.
