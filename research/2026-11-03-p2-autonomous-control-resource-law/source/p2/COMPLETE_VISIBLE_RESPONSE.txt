## 2. The central construction: closing the finite-bath error

The main obstacle was that favorable equilibrium overlap does not establish useful operation with a finite bath.

For each controller phase, let \(\pi_c\) be the known equilibrium distribution of the isolated thermal units. These distributions define a computable reference current \(J_0\). The actual joint stationary distribution generally differs from this reference.

I derived two bounds for the **same full machine**:

\[
|J-J_0|\le C\delta,
\qquad
2\gamma\delta^2\le \sigma_{\mathrm{bath}}\le AJ.
\]

Here \(\delta\) measures the actual tracking error, \(\gamma\) is the finite thermal-refresh rate, and \(C\) is calculated from neighboring equilibrium overlaps and the controller’s rate ceiling.

Combining them gives

\[
|J-J_0|
\le C\sqrt{\frac{AJ}{2\gamma}}.
\]

Solving this inequality yields an explicit positive interval:

\[
R_-(q)J_0\le J\le R_+(q)J_0,
\]

\[
q=\frac{C^2A}{2\gamma J_0},
\qquad
R_-(q)=
\frac{4}{\bigl(\sqrt{q+4}+\sqrt q\bigr)^2},
\qquad
R_+=R_-^{-1}.
\]

The lower bound remains positive at every finite positive bath rate and affinity. No infinitely fast thermal relaxation is assumed.

A separate moment bound controls occupation errors under the actual stationary law. Connecting those errors to first-record low–high–low crossings earns completed-stroke quality as well as controller current.

## 3. The rate–energy consequence

The constructive result has a matching limitation that permits interactions among the thermal units.

Let the acceptable low and high occupations differ by \(b-a\). \[

The fuel per controlled unit decreases as \(1/M\), while completed-bank throughput also decreases as \(1/M\).

Consequently, the rate of certified occupation change scales as \(A B_{\rm tot}/M\). Vanishing fuel per controlled unit cannot coexist with maintained throughput at a fixed total local-update resource in this class.

The assumptions matter: finite classical Markov dynamics, the specified controller topology, local payload updates and complete accounting of supplied drives. Collective transitions, inertial mechanisms and coherent quantum controllers change the resource contract.
