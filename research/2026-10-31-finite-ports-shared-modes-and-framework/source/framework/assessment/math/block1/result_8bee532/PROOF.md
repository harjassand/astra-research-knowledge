# A compatible resonant-wave certificate and the cusp of the sharp boundary

The main problem remains the equality of ROC and QC for the entire exceptional face. This continuation proves two statements about the admitted checkpoint polynomial

\[
P_0(z,w)=|w|^4+2|z|^2|w|^2+\operatorname{Re}(\bar z w^2)+\tfrac3{16}(|z|^2+|w|^2).
\]

First, it classifies the entire zero set of its rank-one Hessian, proves that zero quadratic Schur gap cannot produce a QC counterexample, and identifies the cusp responsible for unusually weak angular coercivity. Second, it proves the QC inequality, for **every background, amplitude, phase and three wave amplitudes**, on an explicit continuously parameterized family of actual resonant gradient fields. The latter family has nonzero cubic Schur correlation; its safety does not follow from the supplied zero-third-moment class. Neither theorem is global QC of P0.

## 1. What is retained, and what is changed

The checkpoint gives global ROC of P0, its failure of polyconvexity, and absence of every polynomial closed-form correction making ordinary heat dissipation pointwise PSD. The scientific target is still the compatible QC inequality. The additional assumption introduced by that failed method was a *local polynomial density along one particular evolution*. Its failure retains no quantitative information about interactions between distinct Fourier directions. Here compatibility is retained exactly by constructing a map and differentiating it; the proof works with its complete resonant moments and an exact polynomial determinant. No matrix-valued probability measure is substituted for a gradient.

We use the checkpoint's completed-square ROC formula and the packet's compatible moment-Schur theorem, with their stated scope. QC of the homogeneous F and the moment theorem are inherited results, not new discoveries here.

## 2. Complete characteristic locus of P0

Use real two-vectors z,w, J the positive quarter turn, and ν(b)=(b1²-b2²,2b1b2). Normalize a nonzero rank-one direction to R=(a,b), |a|=|b|=1. Put n=ν(b), c=a·n, d=Ja·n, e=a·Jn, and express a background as

\[
z=\alpha a+\beta Ja,\qquad w=\gamma b+\delta Jb.
\]

The checkpoint formula specializes exactly to

\[
P_0''(A)[R,R]=4(\alpha+2\gamma+c/4)^2+4(\beta+d/4)^2+8(\delta+e/4)^2+c^2/2. \tag{1}
\]

Consequently every zero has c=0. Changing the overall sign of R if necessary, take a=Jn. Then d=-1,e=1 and the whole zero set is

\[
A=L_b(q):\quad z=(-1/4+iq)b^2,\quad w=-(q/2+i/4)b,\qquad |b|=1,\ q\in\mathbb R, \tag{2}
\]

where vectors are identified with complex numbers. Its unique null rank-one line is generated by (Jn,b).

To prove uniqueness, compute the rotation invariants of (2):

\[
|z|^2=1/16+q^2,\quad |w|^2=1/16+q^2/4,\quad
z\bar w^2=(1+12q^2)/64+iq^3/4. \tag{3}
\]

The imaginary part determines q uniquely. Since q/2+i/4 is never zero, w then determines b uniquely. Thus **no background has two distinct null rank-one lines**. This is a global, exact statement, including q=0.

The locus is nevertheless very flat. For b±=exp(±iθ), minimization of Euclidean distance between the affine lines L_b± gives

\[
\operatorname{dist}(L_{b+},L_{b-})^2
=\frac{\sin^6\theta}{4\cos^2(2\theta)+\cos^2\theta}. \tag{4}
\]

For verification, write q±=s±p. The squared difference is

\[
s^2(4\sin^2 2\theta+\sin^2\theta)
+(-\tfrac12\sin2\theta+2p\cos2\theta)^2
+(\tfrac12\sin\theta-p\cos\theta)^2.
\]

The last two terms have least value (4), since the determinant of their offset and direction vectors is sin³θ. The denominator is strictly positive. Hence distinct characteristic lines never meet, but their separation can be cubic in their angular separation. This rules out a naive uniform angular-gap argument.

### 2.1. Exact acoustic orders at the cusp

At the representative A(q)=(-1/4,q,-q/2,-1/4), use input basis ξ=(1,-1)/√2, η=(1,1)/√2 and output basis U=(1,1)/√2,V=(1,-1)/√2. For a frequency k ξ+l η the 2-by-2 acoustic matrix is

\[
D_q(k,l)=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}
3l^2(4q^2+3)&l(4q^2k+12ql-3k)\\
l(4q^2k+12ql-3k)&12q^2k^2+32q^2l^2-24qkl+k^2+4l^2
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{5}
\]

Its determinant equals l² times

\[
4q^2(8q^2+9)k^2-12q(8q^2+3)kl+(96q^4+48q^2+9)l^2. \tag{6}
\]

For q≠0 the second factor is positive definite: its determinant is 96q⁴(4q²+3)(8q²+1). At q=0,

\[
D_0=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}9l^2&-3kl\\-3kl&k^2+4l^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det D_0=9l^4. \tag{7}
\]

Thus the soft acoustic eigenvalue vanishes to fourth angular order at the cusp and only second order at the other characteristic backgrounds. In particular at q=0 its smallest eigenvalue satisfies

\[
\lambda_{\min}(D_0)\ge\frac{18l^4}{k^2+13l^2}
\]

away from k=l=0. This is a Fourier coercivity estimate, not a global QC assertion.

### 2.2. Consequence for the compatible Schur boundary

For any nonzero smooth periodic gradient G, let H,w,q0 be the packet's moments for F,C,Q of P0 and put

\[
b=2q_0-w^TH^+w.
\]

Then **b=0 implies that G has one rank-one direction only, and its cubic Schur correlation r is zero**.

Indeed the ROC Fourier argument gives w in range H. For A*=-H^+w,

\[
b=\langle P_0''(A_*)[G,G]\rangle.
\]

Each real sine/cosine Fourier coefficient contributes a nonnegative rank-one acoustic score. If the sum is zero, every nonzero coefficient lies on a null rank-one line of P0''(A*). By the global uniqueness just proved, all coefficients have the same output polarization and the same input direction. Thus G=R h along one scalar periodic coordinate. Formula (1) also forces C(R)=0. More generally the cubic polarization identity gives

\[
DF(R)^TF''(R)^+DC(R)=C(R),
\]

because F''(R)R=3DF(R), R·DC(R)=3C(R), and DC(R) lies in range F''(R). Hence r=0 directly, for arbitrary scalar profile h and its third moment. There can be no counterexample whose sole failure is r≠0 on b=0. Multi-directional gradients always have b>0, although (4) prevents a uniform coarse angular bound.

## 3. Exact compatible resonant-wave construction

Fix e≠0 and frequencies

\[
k_1=(1,0),\quad k_2=(2,e),\quad k_3=(3,e)=k_1+k_2. \tag{8}
\]

Identify k_j with its complex coordinate and let U_j be the real vector whose complex value is i \bar k_j³. For arbitrary real amplitudes t_j and phases θ_j, define the actual map

\[
\phi(X)=\sum_{j=1}^3 t_j U_j\sin(k_j\cdot X+\theta_j),\quad
G=D\phi=\sum_{j=1}^3 t_jR_j\cos(k_j\cdot X+\theta_j),\quad R_j=U_j\otimes k_j. \tag{9}
\]

It is smooth and periodic on the rectangle with periods 2π and 2π/|e|. For rational e one may equivalently clear frequency denominators on a fixed square and divide map coefficients by that common factor. Therefore these are actual compatible test fields, with zero mean gradient.

Their conformal components are

\[
z_j=i\bar k_j^4,\qquad w_j=i k_j\bar k_j^3. \tag{10}
\]

Each individual mode has C(R_j)=0, but resonant mixtures need not. For instance at t=(1,1/16,1/81), phases zero,

\[
\langle C(G)\rangle=-\frac{5e^3(e^2+3)(e^2+8)}{648}, \tag{11}
\]

which is nonzero for every e≠0. The cubic Schur correlation is also nonzero near e=0; see below.

### 3.1. The all-amplitude certificate

Put A=t1²,B=t2²,C=t3² and T=ABC. For zero phases compute H,w,q0 as homogeneous degree-one polynomials in A,B,C. The quartic average f is degree two. Write v=t1t2t3 v0 and c=t1t2t3 c0. All these quantities are reconstructed directly from P0 in `triad_amplitudes.py` using the following exact phase moments:

* E(cos_j²)=1/2;
* E(cos_1 cos_2 cos_3)=1/4;
* E(cos_j⁴)=3/8;
* E(cos_i² cos_j²)=1/4 for i≠j;
* all other moments of degrees at most four vanish, apart from constants.

These identities are exhaustive because the integer relation module of (8) is generated by (1,1,-1), which has odd length three; there is no non-pairing relation of length four.

The receiver constructs the exact polynomial matrix

\[
N=\begin{pmatrix}
H&v_0&w\\
T v_0^T&2f&Tc_0\\
w^T&c_0&2q_0
\end{pmatrix}. \tag{12}
\]

For positive A,B,C, it is similar to the symmetric packet moment matrix by a diagonal change using √T. Direct polynomial determinant reconstruction yields **417 monomials, all with strictly positive rational coefficients**, in e,A,B,C. Every e power is even. The degrees are at most (48,5,5,5). The exact coefficient list is `triad_amplitudes_certificate.json`; the generator computes the determinant from the displayed P0, rather than trusting supplied moments or a prewritten Gram. Nonnegative coefficient signs are a finite certificate valid on the entire orthant, not samples.

For e≠0 and A,B,C>0, H is positive definite: each F''(Rj) is PSD with one-dimensional kernel, and the three different directions do not share that kernel. The already established QC of F and ROC of P0 give a≥0,b≥0 for these actual gradients. Schur elimination gives

\[
\det N=\det H\,(ab-r^2)>0. \tag{13}
\]

Thus the whole moment matrix is PSD, in fact positive definite. By the inherited exact moment theorem, **P0 satisfies its QC inequality on (9), for every background matrix and every overall amplitude**. Zero individual amplitudes follow by continuity; negative ones are covered because only their squared values and their total product enter, and the determinant contains the square of that product.

For arbitrary phases let h=cos(θ1+θ2-θ3). Only v and c change, both by the common factor h. H,w,f,q0 remain unchanged. If a0=2f-vᵀH⁻¹v and r0=c-vᵀH⁻¹w are their zero-phase values, the general phase Schur determinant is

\[
2fb-h^2\{b\,v^TH^{-1}v+r_0^2\},
\]

which is minimized at h²=1 because b≥0 and H>0. Hence the same proof covers every phase. This is an infinite compatible safe class with genuine resonant third moments and nonzero correlation.

### 3.2. Hard boundary test: almost parallel resonant waves

Take t=(1,1/16,1/81). Exact rational elimination in `triad_symbolic.py` gives, for e→0 with e≠0,

\[
a=111/4+O(e^2),\quad b=e^4/56+O(e^6),\quad r=5e^3/54+O(e^5),
\]

and therefore

\[
\frac{r^2}{ab}=\frac{1400}{80919}e^2+O(e^4)\longrightarrow0. \tag{14}
\]

Here the fields retain a two-phase resonant distribution as e tends to zero; one must not replace their moments by a one-phase e=0 field, which gains extra resonances. The exact formulas and coefficients of ab-r² are retained in `triad_exact.txt` and `triad_gap.txt`. The latter numerator has only positive coefficients as well.

This test is useful because pairwise characteristic-line distance is only cubic in angle, while an actual nontrivial three-wave resonance needs three directions. In this family the quadratic Schur gap decays at order four but the squared correlation at order six. The nearly parallel resonance therefore does not exploit the cusp into a counterexample. This observation is established for (8) and these fixed amplitude ratios; a uniform statement for arbitrary spectra has not been proved.

## 3.3. Stronger conclusion: every noncollinear triangle of characteristic waves

The same construction in fact works for **every noncollinear resonant frequency triangle**, while keeping the prescribed polarization U(k)=i \bar k³. This removes the restriction on the triangle in (8).

Choose the shortest side as k1 and the longest as k3, with signs arranged so k3=k1+k2. By a simultaneous input/output rotation preserving P0, and a common frequency scale absorbed into the wave amplitudes, normalize

\[
k_1=(1,0),\quad k_2=(p,e),\quad k_3=(p+1,e).
\]

Shortest/longest ordering gives

\[
p\ge-1/2,\qquad e^2\ge1-p^2. \tag{15}
\]

The second inequality matters only when p<0. The symmetry is exact: input rotation by β and output rotation by -3β sends z→exp(-4iβ)z,w→exp(-2iβ)w and preserves P0; it also sends U(k) to U(exp(iβ)k). Reversing any frequency does not change its rank-one R(k), and phases are already arbitrary.

`triad_wedge.py` reconstructs the same matrix (12), with p=1+ζ an indeterminate. Its exact determinant has 7,197 nonzero terms, all positive rational coefficients, in e,ζ,A,B,C; every e power is even. `verify_triads.py` then performs the exact algebraic shift ζ=p-1. This gives **5,047 positive coefficients** in e,p,A,B,C, so det N≥0 for p≥0, all e and nonnegative A,B,C.

For the remaining range -1/2≤p≤0, factor out e⁶ and set

\[
e^2=1-p^2+S,\quad S\ge0,\qquad p=-t/2,\quad 0\le t\le1. \tag{16}
\]

For every fixed monomial S^i A^a B^b C^c, express its coefficient polynomial in t in its ordinary Bernstein basis on [0,1]. The exact conversion is

\[
t^j=\sum_{k=j}^d\frac{\binom{k}{j}}{\binom{d}{j}}\binom{d}{k}t^k(1-t)^{d-k}.
\]

There are **4,616 Bernstein coefficients, all nonnegative rational numbers**; the largest polynomial degree is 21. Their full list is retained in `acute_test.json`, and `acute_test.py` reconstructs them from the shifted determinant. Consequently det N≥0 throughout (15). H remains positive definite for a noncollinear triangle with positive wave amplitudes; a,b≥0 come from the inherited homogeneous QC and ROC arguments. Thus ab-r²≥0 for every such triangle. Zero amplitudes follow by continuity. Collinear characteristic waves are one-dimensional rank-one fields, safe by ordinary Jensen along a rank-one line irrespective of any additional resonances.

Therefore the conclusion of Section 3 is valid for **every three-wave field whose frequencies have one noncollinear relation k3=k1+k2, whose output polarization is U(k)=i \bar k³, and whose wave amplitudes and phases are arbitrary**. All means and overall amplitudes are covered. This includes acute, right and obtuse frequency triangles and nonzero Schur correlation. It is a restricted compatible theorem, not the full arbitrary-polarization three-wave problem and not QC for unrestricted fields.


## 4. Remaining scope

The global question for P0 remains open, as does the full exceptional face with arbitrary M and every admitted Q. The new results exclude b=0 as a separation mechanism, expose exact angular orders that a global argument must retain, and certify an all-parameter resonant family beyond the supplied vanishing-third-moment class. They neither assert that this family exhausts gradients nor treat a finite list of safe fields as global QC.
