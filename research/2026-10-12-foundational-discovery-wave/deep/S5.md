# S5 — independent unit-flow reduction for random affine inconsistency

Date: 2026-10-12. Status: complete internal proof reconstruction, conditional only on the cited external hardness theorem; finite tests recorded separately. This is not independent external verification or an established novelty claim.

I derived the flow construction before reading S2 or the root R4 proof. The parent later reported that S2 had independently found an equivalent matroid construction and the same private-variable reinforcement. I have not read either proof. Source clones were left untouched.

## Statement proved

Let `RAI_3,1/2` take an explicitly listed family of affine equations over F2, each involving at most three distinct variables. Retain each listed equation independently with probability 1/2. Its value is the probability that the retained equations have no common solution. Relative approximation of this value is #BIS-hard under approximation-preserving reductions, assuming Feng–Fu–Guo Theorem 3. Hardness holds even when **all coefficient supports are distinct**, which is stronger than forbidding identical affine equations.

The reduction is deterministic, uses one approximation-oracle call, has size O((m+n) log((m+n)/epsilon)), and never subtracts an approximately known probability from one. Every row in the oracle instance has exactly the retention probability 1/2.

## External premise: scope checked directly

[Feng, Fu and Guo, *Approximating two-terminal network reliability*, arXiv:2608.02523v1, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02523v1#Thmtheorem3) states that, for every fixed p in (0,1), an FPRAS for undirected s–t unreliability with every edge open with probability p implies an FPRAS for #BIS. Thus p=1/2 is covered. The abstract alone would not suffice; I checked the actual theorem and Section 5.

Section 5 gives a polynomial approximation reduction from #BIS by isolating minimum cuts and then implementing a small close probability using parallel paths of length two. The final construction also removes parallel edges. Therefore the external premise is available even on simple graphs. The theorem's undirected scope matters: the F2 flow construction below models undirected connectivity, not directed reachability.

No new network-reliability hardness theorem is claimed here. Whether #BIS has an FPRAS remains open; the consequence is not an unconditional exclusion of an FPRAS.

## 1. An exact reduction when helpers can be deterministic

Let G=(V,E) be an undirected loopless graph with distinct terminals s,t. Write

    U(G) = Pr[s and t are disconnected in the independently open 1/2 subgraph].

Introduce a variable f_e in F2 for each edge. Let B be the vertex-edge incidence matrix over F2 and let d have a 1 at s and t and zero elsewhere. Impose the deterministic equations

    B f = d.                                                     (1)

For every edge e introduce a randomly retained equation

    f_e = 0,                                                     (2)

retained independently with probability 1/2. Interpret its retention as closing the edge. Thus the omitted edge equations identify the open edge set F.

For each fixed F, equations (1)–(2) are consistent if and only if s,t are connected in (V,F). One direction is witnessed by setting f to the indicator of any simple s–t path. For the other, if C is the open connected component of s and t is outside C, summing (1) over C gives 1 on the right. Every open edge inside C contributes twice and every crossing edge is closed and hence has f_e=0, giving 0 on the left. This contradiction proves the converse. Consequently the inconsistency probability is exactly U(G).

Equivalently, the image of a graph incidence matrix consists of the demand vectors whose sum is even on each connected component. This elementary flow fact is the only graph-to-linear-algebra ingredient needed; no cographic representation has to be computed.

## 2. Deterministic sparse helpers

Replace each vertex equation of degree d_v>3 by a parity chain. For distinct variables x_1,...,x_d and d>3, introduce d-3 private chain variables y_1,...,y_(d-3) and use

    x_1 + x_2 + y_1 = 0,
    y_(j-1) + x_(j+1) + y_j = 0       (2 <= j <= d-3),
    y_(d-3) + x_(d-1) + x_d = b.

The existential projection onto x is exactly x_1+...+x_d=b, and every satisfying x has a unique extension to y. Degrees 1,2,3 require a single row. Let H be the resulting deterministic helper family and D=|H|. For graphs without isolated vertices,

    D = sum_v max(1,d_v-2) <= sum_v d_v = 2m,
    number of chain variables = sum_v max(0,d_v-3) <= 2m.

The displayed chain has d-2 rows for degree d>3. In particular, a degree-four equation takes two rows and one auxiliary variable, not three rows.

Keeping the random edge equations (2) and all of H still has inconsistency probability exactly U(G).

## 3. Removing deterministic probabilities and repeated rows

Consider any nonempty helper row

    sum_(x in S) x = b,       1 <= |S| <= 3.

Partition S=P disjoint-union Q so that |P|<=2 and |Q|<=1. For every i=1,...,k, introduce a variable z_(S,i) private to this particular row occurrence and this copy, and include the two equations

    sum_(x in P) x + z_(S,i) = 0,
    z_(S,i) + sum_(x in Q) x = b.                            (3)

Both are retained independently with probability 1/2, as are all other equations. A pair is complete with probability 1/4. A complete pair enforces the original helper row after eliminating z. Every strict subset of the pair has a solution in z for **every** assignment of the core variables. Since the z variables are private, incomplete pairs cannot cooperate to impose an unintended condition. Multiple complete pairs for the same helper row only repeat that condition.

Therefore the projection of the entire retained system onto its core variables is exactly the retained edge equations plus an independently retained subfamily H' of H, where each helper row occurs with probability

    alpha = 1 - (3/4)^k.                                       (4)

These effective retention choices are independent across helper occurrences and independent of the edge-equation retention choices. No auxiliary variable is sampled or given a probability distribution: consistency existentially quantifies all auxiliary variables. The only random objects are independently retained equations.

Every row has arity at most three. All coefficient supports in the final list are distinct:

* Edge rows are distinct singleton supports {f_e}.
* Every gadget row contains its own z, so rows from different gadgets have different supports and cannot coincide with an edge row.
* Within one gadget, the supports are P union {z} and Q union {z}. P and Q are disjoint and S is nonempty, so these supports differ.

This remains true if H contains repeated rows or the original graph contains parallel edges: helper copies have different private z variables, while parallel edges have different f_e variables. The proof prohibits duplicate coefficient rows, not merely duplicate full rows including b.

## 4. Multiplicative comparison, including rare and zero events

Let q_k(G) be the inconsistency probability of the final all-random instance. Let Good be the event that every helper has at least one complete pair. With eta=(3/4)^k,

    Pr[Good] = (1-eta)^D >= 1-D eta.

Good depends only on helper-equation coins and is independent of the open graph. On Good, inconsistency is exactly the disconnection event. For every outcome, removing helper constraints from the full flow system cannot create an inconsistency. Thus a connected open graph always gives a consistent final system, even when Good fails. It follows that

    (1-D eta) U(G) <= (1-eta)^D U(G) <= q_k(G) <= U(G).       (5)

This is a multiplicative comparison. It does **not** follow merely from an additive bound |q-U|<=D eta; that weaker argument would be inadequate for rare U. Independence of Good and the original open edges is load-bearing.

If U=0, then q_k=0 exactly. If U>0 and k>=1, then Pr[Good]>0 and q_k>0. For p=1/2 and s!=t, the all-closed graph already shows U>=2^(-m)>0, but this lower bound is not used in the reduction. In a general random affine instance with positive retention probabilities, its inconsistency probability is zero exactly when the full listed family is consistent, a condition testable by Gaussian elimination.

## 5. Explicit AP reduction and size

Given a requested relative tolerance 0<epsilon<=1, choose

    k = ceil(log(4D/epsilon) / log(4/3)),

so D(3/4)^k <= epsilon/4. Query an RAI approximation oracle at relative tolerance epsilon/4 and report its answer qhat directly. On the oracle's success event,

    qhat >= (1-epsilon/4) q_k
          >= (1-epsilon/4)^2 U
          >= (1-epsilon) U,
    qhat <= (1+epsilon/4) q_k <= (1+epsilon) U.

Hence the oracle success probability is inherited without amplification or further randomized computation. The standard success probability 3/4 suffices, and other confidence requirements can be inherited in the same way. The oracle instance has

    m + 2kD rows,
    m + sum_v max(0,d_v-3) + kD variables.

The support list has O((m+n) log((m+n)/epsilon)) entries, and variable indices need only logarithmically many bits. There are no nontrivial encoded probabilities: every probability is the constant 1/2. Choosing k uses epsilon but no unknown event probability. The oracle tolerance is polynomially related to epsilon, so this is an AP reduction, not just a fixed-accuracy implication.

Preprocessing handles degenerate graphs without querying the oracle: if s=t return U=0; if s,t are disconnected in the full graph return U=1; otherwise keep only their connected component and delete self-loops. This leaves no degree-zero vertices and hence no empty helper rows. Self-loops have no effect on s–t connectivity. Distinct parallel edges are harmless as noted above. Alternatively, isolated nonterminals may simply be dropped. These operations preserve U exactly.

## 6. Prior-art and novelty assessment

The unrestricted problem is already a familiar matroid-port probability in different notation. For augmented row vectors r_i=(a_i,b_i) and tau=(0,...,0,1), a retained affine system is inconsistent exactly when tau belongs to span{r_i: i retained}. A witness linear combination gives 0=1; the converse follows from elementary elimination. Thus its probability is binary representable matroid **port reliability**, with tau as the distinguished element. A basis change can send any nonzero distinguished binary vector to tau, giving the converse representation as well. This exact equivalence is established by the elementary argument here and should not be marketed as a new conceptual discovery.

Relevant primary sources checked live:

1. [Feng–Fu–Guo 2026, Theorem 3 and Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02523v1#S5): the imported half-probability undirected unreliability hardness. Its Corollary 2 gives an FPRAS for the complementary **reliability** quantity; complementing a relative approximation does not transfer relative accuracy.
2. [Pashkovich–Skitsko–Xing, *Sequential Linear Contracts on Matroids*, arXiv:2602.03543v1, Problem 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.03543v1): explicitly defines the probability that a distinguished matroid element is not spanned by independently available elements. It places such probabilities in the existing matroid (un)reliability framework and discusses parallel copies. This is close conceptual prior art, not a source for the exact sparse affine theorem above. Its statement describing all #BIS-hard problems as equivalent should not be imported; hardness alone does not imply #BIS-easiness.
3. [Ball–Nemhauser, *Matroids and a Reliability Analysis Problem*, 1979](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.4.2.132): reliability analysis via independence systems and matroids considerably predates this work. This paper's abstract is not evidence that the exact modern AP restriction was previously proved.
4. [Hou–Liu–Ma, *Some Results on Incorrigible Sets of Binary Linear Codes*, 2021](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/transfun/E104.A/2/E104.A_2020EAL2021/_article): identifies the incorrigible-set enumerator with a Tutte specialization and derives #P-hardness. This is close erasure-decoding prior art, but block ambiguity and a distinguished bit/port are different predicates, and exact #P-hardness alone does not establish #BIS-hard relative approximation.
5. [Chen–Guo–Zhang–Zou, *Near-Linear Time Samplers for Matroid Independent Sets with Applications*, RANDOM 2024](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2024.32): gives efficient matroid-independent-set sampling and all-terminal reliability applications. It does not supply an FPRAS for the target port event or contradict the reduction.
6. [Ibrahimi–Kanoria–Kraning–Montanari, *The Set of Solutions of Random XORSAT Formulae*, SODA 2012](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1.9781611973099.62): concerns uniformly random clause ensembles and solution geometry. That randomness model differs from independent erasure of a supplied worst-case row family. Ordinary fixed-instance #XORSAT is polynomial-time solvable; the hard object here counts retention masks that yield inconsistency, not satisfying assignments of one fixed retained system.

Targeted searches included matroid ports/reliability, random XOR reliability, affine inconsistency probabilities, binary matroid approximation, and erasure-decoding complexity. I did not find a primary source explicitly stating the simultaneous restrictions **row arity at most three, all retention probabilities 1/2, and no duplicate coefficient supports**. This bounded search cannot establish priority. The defensible description is a short internally reconstructed restriction/corollary of a known network-hardness result, with a useful explicit monotone reinforcement lemma. The large conceptual and hardness ingredients are prior art. Whether the precise restriction is publishably new needs a specialist literature review; no landmark assessment is warranted from the present search.

## 7. A consequential positive structured class remains

Sparsity alone is insufficient by the theorem. A transparent positive class is a family whose nonempty row supports are **laminar**: any two supports are nested or disjoint. Its inconsistency probability can be computed exactly in polynomial time, for arbitrary independent rational retention probabilities, with arbitrary right-hand sides and repeated supports. This is a standard finite-state tree dynamic program, not a new general solver or a novelty claim.

Group equations with the same support S. Their retained local restriction on the parity x(S) is one of four sets: empty, {0}, {1}, or {0,1}. Compute the probability distribution on these four sets from the rows at S. The distinct supports form an inclusion forest. For each node, maintain the distribution of attainable parity sets in its subtree. Combine disjoint child subtrees by XOR-set addition, add a residual factor {0,1} if S contains any variable outside its children (otherwise {0}), then intersect with the node's local restriction. Child coin collections are independent and their variables are disjoint, so the four-state convolution is exact. At a root, the subtree is consistent exactly when the attainable set is nonempty; different roots are independent. Empty-support contradictory rows are handled separately. This takes O(m+number of support inclusions) four-state arithmetic operations after building the forest; a straightforward forest construction from explicit supports is polynomial. With rational probabilities, intermediate numerator/denominator bit lengths remain polynomial in the total input bit length.

The class permits unbounded rank, arbitrarily many variables, arbitrarily deep nesting, high arity, and arbitrarily rare inconsistency. Its exact nature avoids a complement-subtraction approximation problem. It is useful as a tractable structured benchmark and a falsifiable target for applications whose parity constraints actually form a hierarchy. Establishing that an intended physical or inference instance admits such a laminar representation, and charging the cost of finding it, is still necessary. Generic arity-three inputs do not inherit this property. Bounded-width affine elimination and small disjoint components are other standard positive classes, but neither is a new mathematical breakthrough.
