## Candidate 1 — fixed-precision derandomization of logspace

1. **North Star.** Determine whether the paper's fixed-precision estimator really gives a uniform deterministic, polynomial-time, logarithmic-space approximation to the acceptance probability of every fixed polynomial-time randomized logspace machine, without a promise on its acceptance probability. If so, thresholding at $1/2$ yields the claimed equality `L = RL = BPL`. The possible transferable operation is a deterministic estimator for suitable finite, ordered, substochastic transition systems; the current theorem is stated for a supplied machine, not an acquired oracles-only model.

2. **Exact question.** For a fixed randomized machine $M$ and fixed $d\ge1$, does there exist a single uniform deterministic transducer using $O(\log n)$ work bits and polynomial time that outputs $z_x$ with $|z_x-\Pr[M(x)\text{ accepts}]|\le(n+2)^{-d}$ on every input? The source asserts yes. The sharply localized independent-check question is whether the actual-versus-clean fingerprint coupling discharges its discrepancy estimate under all stated salt, endpoint-mask, and incoming-cap conditions, and whether those interfaces feed the fixed-precision theorem with the advertised costs.

3. **Importance.** If correct under the stated machine model, this collapses the standard bounded-error and one-sided randomized logspace classes to deterministic logspace. An ECCC 2026 survey predating the local September 23, 2026 preprint calls `BPL = L` a central open problem ([ECCC Report 64](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/064/download/)). The local manuscript therefore makes a major claim; the historical baseline does not establish its status after that preprint, and this investigation does not independently validate or refute the claim.

4. **Obstruction.** The source's correction hierarchy has a concise algebraic core, but that core is not the full derandomization. For a finite strictly forward matrix $C\ge0$, row sums at most one, and entry bound $f$, it constructs a nonnegative correction $E$ and $D=C-E+EC$, so
   \[
   I-D=(I+E)(I-C).
   \]
   Thus the same potential p is represented after updating its reward vector to (I+E)W, while transition entries shrink. I reconstructed the fixed-point argument: strict forward support makes the row recurrence recursive in terminal-coordinate order; the residual $T=C-E_*+E_*C\ge0$ has row sums at most one and $T(x,y)\ge\theta_1f$ wherever $E_*(x,y)>0$. Hence each corrected row has at most $1/(\theta_1f)$ supported entries. The ordered-path expansion and factorial tail then permit a fixed number of iterations independent of graph size. This argument checks algebraically under the paper's hypotheses. The remaining high-risk bridge is the estimator's clean/actual coupling and conditional environment averaging, not an identified error in this hierarchy lemma.

   A scope-limited lower bound rules out a tempting substitute: if the $N=2^T$ coin-tape acceptance bits are an arbitrary black-box Boolean function, any deterministic algorithm distinguishing acceptance density at most $1/3$ from at least $2/3$ needs at least $N/3$ queries in the worst case. After fewer queries, the all-zero transcript remains consistent with both the all-zero completion and a completion with all unqueried bits one. This does **not** refute the paper: the machine's transition structure is supplied and is exactly what its construction exploits. It does show that a claim based only on querying opaque random-tape outcomes cannot meet the theorem's polynomial-time bound.

5. **Missing capability.** An independent end-to-end reconstruction of the clean-block independence and escape/discrepancy charge, with the source's exact masks and access rules, remains absent here. In particular, the paper itself emphasizes retaining the transformed source hit and using the *actual* left incoming cap in the critical estimate. A useful transfer beyond the source theorem would also need a generic interface translating an ordered substochastic kernel and reward into the estimator's logspace access model; the hierarchy identity alone does not supply that interface. No failure of the source proof is inferred from this unfinished check.

6. **Actual first attack.** I checked the correction identity and its finite ordered-support recurrence against the source hypotheses, then isolated the black-box query barrier above. I also traced the claimed final resource bound: conditioning on one designated start costs a factor $N=2^{C_NB}$ in the eighth-moment estimate, paid by increasing precision linearly in $B$; the exact environment law is enumerated with at most $2^{O(B)}$ encodings, and every deterministic query halts within $2^{O(B)}$ configurations. The product remains $2^{O(B)}=(n+2)^{O(1)}$, with $O(B)=O(\log n)$ work bits. This is a source-level cost reconstruction, not an independent proof of every estimator subroutine.

## Candidate 2 — smooth initial forms and the cubic permanent ceiling

1. **North Star.** Find a lower-bound invariant for the permanent that survives affine-linear determinant representations and coefficientwise limits and can grow beyond the current cubic scale. The transferable operation already present is a general lower bound from a smooth first nonzero homogeneous Taylor term; its precise strength and generic ceiling can be checked directly.

2. **Exact question.** Can repeated application of the smooth-initial-form theorem alone yield a super-cubic (in particular super-polynomial) lower bound for the permanent's border determinantal complexity? No: degree and number of essential variables cap this route at cubic for affine restrictions of the $m\times m$ permanent. The next real question is whether a permanent-specific invariant, beyond the product of those two parameters, can survive border degeneration and exceed that ceiling.

3. **Importance.** The local Family 108 paper claims an Ω(m³) lower bound for exact and border determinantal complexity. Its Lean documentation states the formalized scope and the general smooth-initial-form theorem. If correct, this would extend the pre-existing quadratic lower-bound landscape: Mignon–Ressayre's primary 2004 paper proves dc(perm_m) ≥ m²/2 ([paper](https://math.univ-lyon1.fr/~ressayre/PDFs/permdet.pdf)). The local cubic source itself still describes super-polynomial determinantal complexity as open; this report does not claim that broader barrier is resolved.

4. **Obstruction.** A concrete smooth-family counterexample blocks naive iteration. Over characteristic zero, let F_{d,r} = Σ_{i=1}^{d} x_i^r, with d,r≥2. Its gradient has coordinates r x_i^{r−1}; they vanish simultaneously only at the origin, so its projective zero set is smooth. A formula computes each power in r−1 multiplications and sums the d terms with d−1 additions: dr−1 arithmetic operations. The formula-to-affine-determinant construction cited in the paper gives a determinant of order at most dr+1. Conversely, the smooth-initial-form theorem gives only (r−1)(d−1)/(4e)=Θ(rd). Thus smoothness plus this degree/dimension statistic is already tight up to constants on a smooth family; it cannot generically yield a determinant lower bound superlinear in rd.

   For any affine restriction of perm_m, degree is at most m and the number of essential variables is at most m². Substituting these into the same theorem gives at most an O(m³) lower-bound scale. This is a ceiling on this proof method, not a counterexample to Family 108's cubic theorem and not an upper bound on the permanent's actual determinantal complexity.

5. **Missing capability.** To go beyond cubic, one needs a permanent-specific structural quantity Φ with a proved lower bound Φ(perm_m)=ω(m³), plus a theorem controlling Φ for every affine determinant representation and its coefficientwise limits. No such candidate or reduction emerged. The smooth-initial-form theorem is transferable to other polynomials meeting its hypotheses, but that transfer does not evade its rd ceiling.

6. **Actual first attack.** I independently proved smoothness of the Fermat form and counted its formula: d(r−1)+(d−1)=dr−1. Combining the source's formula-to-determinant conversion with its smooth-form theorem gives matched Θ(rd) upper/lower scales. Then I used the degree-m, at-most-m²-variable bounds for affine restrictions of the permanent to get the cubic method ceiling. This is an explicit counterexample to a proposed *general escalation of the method*, not to the paper's theorem. I did not find a new invariant or a further lower bound.

7. **Exact source ranges, revisions, and costs.** At pinned OpenAI/math revision `fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb`: the PDF `preprints/A-cubic-lower-bound-for-border-determinantal-complexity-of-the-permanent-September-24-2026/A-cubic-lower-bound-for-border-determinantal-complexity-of-the-permanent-September-24-2026.pdf`, pp. 2–3 (claimed cubic constant and literature baseline), p. 5 (shifted-permanent coefficient construction and smooth restriction), pp. 6–7 (smooth-initial-form theorem and proof); `lean/docs/108.md:7–11` (formalized scope and constants); `CONTENTS.md:2679–2688`; `overview.tex:294`; and Astra's `frontier/dossiers/N79.txt:20–25` plus `frontier/cards/N79-smoothness-determinant-barrier.json:3–12` (N79 raw-card SHA-256 `100c4e4aac6e0eaf8691b5dad65ce40a72e6c16d1421ac46f0d516e31631e9ac`). N79 is explicitly `source_derived_unreviewed`, with only a supplied summary referenced and external correctness/priority unresolved; that summary file is absent from this checkout. I therefore use N79 only as a navigation/status record; the Fermat calculation above is reconstructed here.

   The relevant cost is representation size, not runtime: $F_{d,r}$ has a formula of $dr-1$ operations and an affine determinant of order at most $dr+1$; the theorem lower bound is $(r-1)(d-1)/(4e)$. For Family 108 the source states $m\ge1408\Rightarrow n\ge m^3/(5{,}529{,}600e)$, for exact and border determinant representations. No data acquisition, numerical computation, or algorithmic runtime claim is involved; the polynomial is supplied symbolically.

8. **Decisive step.** Any continuation aimed beyond cubic must exhibit and prove the permanent-specific Φ above, including its behavior under coefficientwise limits. Reapplying smoothness, degree, and essential-variable count cannot pass the Fermat counterexample. No surviving beyond-application mechanism is presently in hand, so this route is an obstruction result and a clear admission test, not a reason to claim a new algebraic breakthrough.
