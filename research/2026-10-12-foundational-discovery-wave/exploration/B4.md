## 1. North Star

Make boundary inverse problems operational: replace “the full Dirichlet-to-Neumann map uniquely determines the medium” with a theorem that specifies a prior class, boundary controls, a finite list of scalar readouts, noise and precision, reconstruction loss modulo the actual gauges, and acquisition cost. The target here is a smooth interior metric and connection from one boundary patch. A useful outcome could be either a sharp finite-query barrier or a matched stable-reconstruction guarantee under a clearly stated finite-dimensional promise.

## 2. Exact question

Let Ω be the unit ball in ℝ^n, with n ≥ 3, let Γ be any nonempty relatively open boundary patch, and take the trivial Hermitian rank-two bundle. For a smooth metric g and smooth unitary connection A, the response interface is the sesquilinear energy form
\[
  B_{g,A}(f,h)=\int_\Omega \langle d_Au_f,d_Au_h\rangle_g\,dV_g,
  \qquad f,h\in C_c^\infty(\Gamma;\mathbb C^2),
\]
where u_f is the weak solution of the connection-Laplacian Dirichlet problem with trace f. Given only q real scalar outputs of this form (a complex pairing counts as at most two real outputs), what is the minimax recovery accuracy on an a-priori H^s-bounded smooth class, modulo diffeomorphisms fixed on Γ and unitary gauges fixed there? The first exact subproblem is the scalar conformal slice $K_{s,\eta}=\{\sigma\in C^\infty(\bar\Omega):1/2\le\sigma\le3/2,\ \sigma=1\text{ near }\partial\Omega,\ \|\sigma-1\|_{H^s}\le\eta\}$ with $g=\sigma^{2/(n-2)}\delta$ and $A=0$: what is the minimax $L^2$ coefficient error from q real energy pairings? The operational extension is to impose control-norm, readout-noise, precision, and acquisition bounds, and ask whether a matched upper bound exists. For the full metric-plus-connection target, the analogous error must use a specified quotient metric.

## 3. Importance

An exact continuum uniqueness statement does not say how many boundary experiments suffice, whether the selected controls are stable to implement, or whether finite-precision readouts separate two media. This is a basic interface issue in electrical impedance tomography and geometric wave/elliptic sensing: a finite transcript is the evidence actually acquired. A lower bound that remains true for arbitrary probes prevents an optimistic choice of high-frequency controls from silently replacing the measurement budget. The ambition should stay modest until the generic information-theoretic part is separated from a genuine inverse-problem advance.

## 4. Obstruction

Here is an exact equal-data pair for every finite number q ≥ 1 of **real scalar outputs**, with no restriction on the chosen controls. Fix an integer s > n/2 and use the standard integer-order H^s norm defined by the sum of derivative L^2 norms. Choose a closed ball Q compactly contained in Ω, and choose q+1 disjoint balls of radius
\[
  r_q\asymp(q+1)^{-1/n}
\]
inside Q. Put identical rescaled smooth bumps φ_{k,q} in those balls, normalized to have disjoint-support H^s(Ω) norm one. Standard scaling gives
\[
 \|\phi_{k,q}\|_{L^2(\Omega)}\asymp r_q^s.
\]
For $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_{q+1})\in S^q\), set
\[
 \sigma_a=1+\eta\sum_{k=1}^{q+1}a_k\phi_{k,q},
 \qquad g_a=\sigma_a^{2/(n-2)}\delta,\qquad A_a=0.
\]
Choose fixed $0<\eta\le(2C_{H^s\hookrightarrow L^\infty})^{-1}$. Then every σ_a is smooth, equals one near ∂Ω, obeys $1/2\le\sigma_a\le3/2$, and has $\|\sigma_a-1\|_{H^s}=\eta$. The resulting metric coefficients have an $H^s$ bound depending only on n, s, and η, and uniform ellipticity independent of q. The conformal exponent is chosen so that
\[
 \sqrt{\det g_a}\,g_a^{-1}=\sigma_a I,
\]
and the rank-two connection-Laplacian energy with A = 0, restricted to the first fiber coordinate, is exactly the scalar conductivity energy $\int_\Omega\sigma_a\nabla u_f\cdot\overline{\nabla u_h}\,dx$.

For any fixed protocol producing q real outputs, let $T:S^q\to\mathbb R^q$ be its transcript. Uniform ellipticity and continuous dependence of the weak solution imply T is continuous for fixed controls. Borsuk–Ulam gives an $a\in S^q$ with $T(a)=T(-a)$. This also covers an adaptive protocol **if its full transcript is continuous in the coefficient**; for example, it suffices that each next control depends continuously on the previous real outputs in a trace norm for which the energy pairing is jointly continuous. It does not cover arbitrary discontinuous branching policies. If the protocol returns q complex pairings, take 2q as the real output count in this argument.

The antipodal pair is quantitatively separated in this fixed coordinate chart:
\[
 \|\sigma_a-\sigma_{-a}\|_{L^2}=2\eta\Big(\sum_k a_k^2\|\phi_{k,q}\|_2^2\Big)^{1/2}
 \asymp \eta(q+1)^{-s/n}.
\]
Because $t\mapsto t^{2/(n-2)}$ is bi-Lipschitz on $[1/2,3/2]$, the L^2 distance between metric coefficient fields g_a and g_{-a} has the same order. The pair is also **not gauge-equivalent**: if a diffeomorphism Φ fixed pointwise on Γ pulled one metric to the other, then Φ would be a conformal self-map of the ball. For n ≥ 3, Liouville's theorem makes it Möbius; fixing an open boundary patch pointwise forces Φ to be the identity, contradicting σ\(_a\ne\sigma_{-a}\). A bundle gauge cannot change the metric.

Thus no continuous transcript with finitely many real outputs can identify the gauge orbit exactly over this infinite-dimensional class. If the requested output is the representative in this fixed conformal chart, any estimator has worst-case L^2 coefficient error at least half the pair separation, so achieving error ε requires $q+1\gtrsim(\eta/\epsilon)^{n/s}$. The same indistinguishable pair persists under parameter-independent additive noise, quantization, or bounded-noise models that include zero noise. This is a query-count lower bound, not a bit-precision or experiment-time theorem.

**Gauge-loss caveat:** inequivalence proves impossibility of exact recovery modulo gauge. The displayed $L^2$ rate is for the fixed coordinate representative; this construction does not prove the same positive separation in a chosen quotient metric after taking an infimum over all boundary-patch-fixed diffeomorphisms. A quantitative quotient-loss theorem needs that extra step.

## 5. Missing capability

The missing theorem is a finite-interface stability result. Exact uniqueness sees the entire boundary form, while a lab supplies finitely many controls/readouts, each with finite energy and precision. There is no identified general upper bound that matches the lower bound here for the arbitrary smooth metric-plus-connection class. Conversely, a finite-resolution or parametrized prior can make reconstruction possible, but its basis, conditioning, control norms, noise model, and cost must be explicit. A stability modulus for the infinite DN map alone does not settle how costly it is to acquire a finite transcript.

## 6. First original attack

The construction above is the concrete attack: embed a q-sphere of smooth uniformly elliptic coefficients into the metric family, apply Borsuk–Ulam to the actual response transcript, and check that the antipodal collision survives the physical gauge. It strengthens the familiar “finite linearized probes have an infinite-dimensional kernel” observation: it gives an **exact**, finite-amplitude, equal-transcript pair and the chart-distance scale $\eta q^{-s/n}$, for arbitrary fixed controls and continuous adaptive transcripts.

This is not a new Borsuk–Ulam or Sobolev-width result. The exponent is the standard smoothness-versus-dimension scaling, and continuous nonlinear width theory already studies encoders/decoders with the same continuity restriction. The interface-specific work is only the explicit passage through the elliptic response map and the n ≥ 3 conformal gauge check. Since continuity makes the DN transcript a valid nonlinear encoder, a general manifold-width theorem may already subsume the lower bound; do not claim novelty until that implication and the gauge loss are compared carefully. Any original advance likely needs the operational refinement: a sharp quotient-metric lower/upper bound under bounded control norms and specified readout noise, or an explicit finite-dimensional promise with stable probe design.

## 7. Prior exact sources, status, and all costs

**Candidate continuum theorem under audit.** OpenAI Math result 365, [“Determination of a metric and a unitary connection from one boundary patch”](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb/preprints/Determination-of-a-metric-and-a-unitary-connection-from-one-boundary-patch-October-5-2026/paper.pdf), claims that on a compact connected smooth n ≥ 3 manifold with boundary, for the trivial rank-two bundle and arbitrary smooth g_j and A_j, equality of the full energy form for every $f,h\in C_c^\infty(\Gamma;\mathbb C^2)$ on one nonempty relatively open proper patch implies equivalence by a Γ-fixed diffeomorphism and a Γ-fixed U(2) gauge. The source states no finite measurement bound, stability modulus, Sobolev prior, or acquisition/precision guarantee. This is a candidate preprint claim, not an independently checked theorem or a premise of the Borsuk–Ulam argument. Its repository formalization `lean/docs/365.md` covers the separate bounded-measurable scalar-conductivity nonuniqueness statement only; it does not formalize this positive theorem. The source proof uses substantial boundary continuation, Green-kernel and matrix-transfer arguments, which remain unaudited here.

**Closest established boundaries.** Kenig–Sjöstrand–Uhlmann prove partial-boundary uniqueness for Schrödinger potentials and corresponding scalar EIT results, but with the full partial-data Cauchy information, not a finite list of scalar pairings ([Annals of Mathematics, 2007](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2007.165.567)). Gabdurakhmanov–Kokarev recover analytic bundle/connection/metric geometry from a boundary patch in the real-analytic category ([Proc. Royal Society of Edinburgh A, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/prm.2023.127)). Cekić's Yang–Mills connection result is a fixed-metric constrained problem; its published abstract specifies the smooth trivial-line case and the analytic higher-rank case ([J. Spectral Theory 10 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.4171/JST/302)). Rüland–Salo provide quantitative Runge approximation for second-order elliptic operators and a local-data stability application, not finite recovery of arbitrary joint smooth g and A ([IMRN 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnx301)). These sources mark meaningful neighboring results, not a proof or disproof of OpenAI Math result 365.

**Finite-measurement positive comparator and generic lower-bound theory.** Harrach proves noisy-data results and a finite-measurement version for scalar isotropic conductivity that is piecewise constant on a known pixel partition with known upper/lower bounds; it does not cover arbitrary smooth unknown geometry plus connection ([SIAM J. Math. Anal. 55 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1137/23M1544854)). Siegel's manifold-width bounds for Sobolev/Besov classes formalize limits for continuous nonlinear encoders and yield the same $q^{-s/n}$-type scaling in relevant embeddings ([Journal of Complexity 85 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jco.2024.101884)). This generic prior work is the main novelty warning: the current proof is likely a direct response-map instantiation of standard continuous-width machinery, rather than a new obstruction principle.

**Costs and evidence accounting.** The lower bound charges only q real response coordinates and assumes ideal access to arbitrary smooth controls; it imposes no cap on control trace norms, oscillation, source energy, or choice/calibration. It charges no acquisition time, PDE-forward-solve cost, hardware dynamic range, readout bits, detector noise, or noise calibration. These omissions make it a best-case information barrier, not a physical budget estimate. A real scalar pairing requires boundary excitation and a compatible flux/energy readout; the exact number of sessions and cost per pairing depend on the apparatus and are unknown. No noisy-data stability or bit complexity was derived. This investigation used no numerical experiment or formal verification; per-result research time, tool/compute totals, and the OpenAI Math result's task-level generation cost are unavailable. The OpenAI Math README's aggregate usage summary cannot be attributed to result 365 individually.
