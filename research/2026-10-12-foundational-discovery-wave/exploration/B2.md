# Stage I B2 — capital and obstruction audit

**Snapshot:** 2026-10-12. One North Star survives; the fractional-PDE lead is rejected as a standalone novelty claim. This is a research direction and an internally derived diagnostic, not a theorem or originality claim.

## North Star: simultaneous forward and inverse Sobolev approximation in three dimensions

1. **North Star.** Close the three-dimensional bi-Sobolev approximation gap by proving that a Sobolev homeomorphism and its Sobolev inverse can be approximated by the *same* smooth diffeomorphisms, strongly in both directions. The first tractable target is the endpoint $p=q=1$, with a cube and identity near its boundary; the wider target allows arbitrary bounded domains and finite exponents $p,q\ge1$.

2. **Exact question.** For an orientation-preserving homeomorphism $f:\Omega\to\Lambda$,
   \[
   f\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb R^3),\qquad f^{-1}\in W^{1,1}(\Lambda;\mathbb R^3),
   \]
   do there exist smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms $f_j:\Omega\to\Lambda$ with
   \[
   \|f_j-f\|_{W^{1,1}(\Omega)}+\|f_j^{-1}-f^{-1}\|_{W^{1,1}(\Lambda)}\longrightarrow0?
   \]
   The exact pilot version is $\Omega=\Lambda=(0,1)^3$, with $f=\mathrm{id}$ near the boundary. This is the forward-and-inverse norm problem (the bi-Sobolev problem), not the one-sided Ball–Evans theorem.

3. **Why it matters.** Forward Sobolev density alone does not supply smooth approximants admissible for energies that charge both stretching and collapse. The inverse derivative measures the cost of undoing the deformation; it is directly relevant to two-sided distortion control and nonlinear-elasticity energies. Hencl’s 2025 overview states this motivation and identifies simultaneous approximation as a separate open problem. The OpenAI Math family 368 manuscript likewise says its forward convergence does not give inverse Sobolev convergence or control singular determinant penalties.

4. **Obstruction.** For a smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $g$, change of variables gives
   \[
   \int_\Lambda |Dg^{-1}(y)|\,dy
   =\int_\Omega |(Dg(x))^{-1}|\,J_g(x)\,dx
   =\int_\Omega |\operatorname{cof}Dg(x)|\,dx.
   \]
   Thus at the endpoint $q=1$, the reverse Sobolev cost is a cofactor (second-minor) cost. Strong $W^{1,1}$ convergence of $g_j\to f$ controls $Dg_j$ in $L^1$, but by itself does not control these quadratic minors. A source-only approximation can also retain substantial derivative energy on a set whose image is null. Meanwhile, the natural vertexwise PL interpolation can fail injectivity because neighboring image simplices overlap. The recent PL-to-diffeomorphism theorem closes the smoothing step in dimensions 3 and 4, but does not construct the paired PL approximation from an arbitrary bi-Sobolev input.

   **Derived diagnostic, not a counterexample to the stated question.** Let $E\subset(0,1)$ be a fat Cantor set, so it is nowhere dense and $|E|>0$, and set
   \[
   w(t)=c\,\operatorname{dist}(t,E),\quad c^{-1}=\int_0^1\operatorname{dist}(t,E)\,dt,\quad
   \phi(t)=\int_0^t w(s)\,ds.
   \]
   The bounded derivative $w$ vanishes on $E$ and is positive on the dense open complement. Every nontrivial interval meets that complement in positive measure, so $\phi$ is a strictly increasing absolutely continuous homeomorphism of $[0,1]$. The one-dimensional area formula gives $|\phi(E)|=\int_E w=0$. For
   \[
   F(x,y,z)=(x,y,\phi(z)),\qquad DF=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,w(z)),
   \]
   we have $F\in W^{1,p}$ for every finite $p$, while $\operatorname{rank}DF=2$ on $E\times(0,1)^2$ and
   \[
   \int_{E\times(0,1)^2}|DF|_F^p\,dx=2^{p/2}|E|>0,\qquad
   |F(E\times(0,1)^2)|=|\phi(E)|=0.
   \]
   Yet $F^{-1}\notin W^{1,1}$: its third coordinate is $\phi^{-1}$, and if that were one-dimensional $W^{1,1}$, absolute continuity would give the Lusin $N$ property, contradicting $\phi^{-1}(\phi(E))=E$ with a null input and positive-measure output. Slicing transfers this contradiction to $F^{-1}$ on the cube. This pins down what the missing inverse hypothesis must rule out; it does **not** refute two-sided approximation because $F$ lies outside the bi-$W^{1,1}$ class.

5. **Missing capability.** Build a common source/target carrier that simultaneously controls $Dg$ on the reference domain and $\operatorname{cof}Dg$ (equivalently $Dg^{-1}$ after change of variables) on the image, while preserving global injectivity and the exact target. A construction that estimates only source energy, or invokes change of variables for the rough limit without proving its hypotheses, leaves the reverse cost unaccounted for. The useful new representation is a paired cell ledger: source derivative mass, target inverse-derivative mass, and the interface cost of matching their cells.

6. **First substantive attack.** Work first on the fixed cube with $f=\mathrm{id}$ near its boundary. Try to build a locally finite PL homeomorphism $P_k$ onto the same cube with
   \[
   \|P_k-f\|_{W^{1,1}}+\|P_k^{-1}-f^{-1}\|_{W^{1,1}}\to0.
   \]
   Use source cells where $Df$ is well approximated and target cells where $Df^{-1}$ is well approximated; explicitly charge the mismatch/interfaces instead of triangulating source vertices alone. The fat-Cantor shear above is the negative control: any proposed estimate that bounds target cost from source $L^1$-gradient alone must fail on it. Campbell–D’Onofrio–Vítek (2026), Theorem A, then supplies the dimension-3 smoothing of a locally finite PL bi-Sobolev map for every finite $p,q$, including $p=q=1$. The new burden is therefore isolated to the paired Sobolev-to-PL step. Stop if the paired mesh estimate requires an unproved Lusin $N$/change-of-variables assertion or if a low-energy interface cannot preserve injectivity.

8. **Decisive next lemma.** Prove or disprove the paired-PL lemma above for a bi-$W^{1,1}$ homeomorphism of the cube fixed near its boundary. A proof plus Theorem A of Campbell–D’Onofrio–Vítek yields the pilot smooth-diffeomorphism theorem immediately; a counterexample disproves the pilot. ## Screened-out lead: fractional fundamental-solution atom

N415’s global-origin hypothesis makes the simplest punctured-domain extension fail: $K_s(x)=\kappa|x|^{s-n}$ has $L_sK_s=\delta_0$, is locally integrable for $s>0$, and satisfies the weighted tail bound because the radial integrals are $\int_0^1r^{s-1}dr$ and $\int_1^\infty r^{-n-1}dr$. It solves the homogeneous equation away from zero but carries an origin atom. This is a correct boundary diagnostic, not a surviving North Star: fractional Bôcher classification for nonnegative solutions on punctured balls is already established by Li–Liu–Wu–Xu, *Non-negative solutions to fractional Laplace equations with isolated singularity*, Adv. Math. 373 (2020), 107329 ([primary source, DOI 10.1016/j.aim.2020.107329](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2020.107329)). Any remaining candidate would have to isolate a materially sharper weighted-system interface than merely adding $K_s$.
