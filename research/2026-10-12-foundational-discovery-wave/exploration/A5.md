# A5 — Scalable prediction and verified PDE simulation
**Date:** 2026-10-12  
**Status:** two target candidates; one exact internal calculation; no novelty claim.

## Recommendation

The useful research wedge is an acquisition-aware theory of which finite observations suffice to predict unresolved nonlinear dynamics, paired with trajectory-specific certificates for 3D flow computations. The first target exposes a concrete failure of instantaneous coarse closure. The second has a known conditional certification theorem but no practical resource law. The calculation below is an elementary Fourier consequence of the Navier–Stokes nonlinearity, not a new theorem.

## Target 1 — What can finite-resolution flow measurements predict?

**North Star.** Reliable forecasts of fluid, atmospheric, oceanic, and combustion systems when the full fine-scale state is unavailable.

**Exact question.** For periodic 2D incompressible Navier–Stokes, fix viscosity \(\nu>0\), known forcing, kinetic-energy bound \(E\), and Fourier cutoff \(K\). Characterize the minimax error
\[
\mathcal E_K(T;E,\nu)=\inf_{\mathcal A}\;\sup_{\substack{u_0\text{ smooth, div-free}\\\|u_0\|_{L^2}\le E}}
\;\sup_{0\le t\le T}
\|P_K u(t)-\mathcal A(P_Ku_0,\nu,f)(t)\|_{L^2},
\]
where \(\mathcal A\) receives only the resolved initial modes and the supplied forcing. Seek matching bounds in \(K,T,\nu,E\), then determine how bounds change when the interface also supplies a finite noisy time history or additional acquired summary variables. This separates forecast accuracy from the stronger and usually implicit claim that current resolved modes form a closed state.

**Importance.** It would say how sensing resolution, viscosity, and history limit a forecast before choosing a closure model. Such a result can guide where to spend measurement and compute budgets, and can distinguish irreducible state uncertainty from numerical or model error.

**Obstruction.** The quadratic nonlinearity transfers information from unresolved mode pairs directly to resolved modes. A low-mode snapshot alone does not in general determine even the instantaneous low-mode derivative. A bound on energy alone permits arbitrarily fine smooth structure; adding a uniform Sobolev/enstrophy bound changes the question and must be charged as an input assumption.

**Original first investigation (exact calculation).** Write the 2D vorticity equation on \(\mathbb T^2\) as
\[
\partial_t\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=\nu\Delta\omega,\qquad
u_j=-i\,j^\perp\omega_j/|j|^2,
\]
where these subscripts denote Fourier coefficients and \(j^\perp=(-j_2,j_1)\).
Let \(P_K\) retain the square of Fourier modes \(|j|_\infty\le K\), with \(K\ge1\). For Fourier indices \(p,q\) with \(p+q=k\), the two ordered convolution terms give the contribution
\[
(p\times q)\bigl(|q|^{-2}-|p|^{-2}\bigr)\,\omega_p\omega_q
\]
to \(\dot\omega_k\). Let \(p=(N,0)\), \(q=(-N+1,1)\), and \(k=(1,1)\). For a cutoff that resolves \(k\) but not \(p,q\), use the smooth real-valued initial data with Fourier coefficients \(\omega_{\pm p}=a\), \(\omega_{\pm q}=\sigma a\), all others zero, where \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\). Both inputs have the same resolved initial state \(P_K\omega_0=0\), while
\[
\dot\omega_k(0)
=\sigma a^2 N\left(\frac1{(N-1)^2+1}-\frac1{N^2}\right).
\]
Choose \(N=K+2\) and \(a=N\). Then each velocity coefficient has size \(a/|p|\) or \(a/|q|=O(1)\), so the kinetic energy stays bounded as \(K\to\infty\), but the two low-mode derivatives are opposite and have magnitude
\[
c_K=\frac{2N(N-1)}{(N-1)^2+1}\longrightarrow 2.
\]
For example, the magnitudes are \(12/5\) at \(K=1\), \(30/13\) at \(K=4\), and \(90/41\) at \(K=8\). Any single deterministic rule evaluated at the shared resolved state must make derivative error at least \(c_K\) on one member of the pair. Thus there is no exact instantaneous Markov closure from \(P_Ku\) alone on this energy-bounded smooth class. This does **not** establish a positive lower bound on finite-horizon forecast error as \(K\to\infty\): viscosity, time horizon, output norm, and stronger regularity assumptions matter. The minimax target above remains open.

**Closest prior work and reformulation check.**

- Foias–Prodi introduced determining modes for 2D Navier–Stokes: two solutions whose sufficiently many low modes agree asymptotically become indistinguishable at high modes ([1967 paper](https://eudml.org/doc/107242)). This uses trajectory agreement, not a map from one instantaneous low-mode snapshot to its derivative.
- Foias, Jolly, Kravchenko, and Titi construct a determining form for low modes, but its state is a bounded continuous function of an auxiliary history variable. They explicitly describe it as an embedding in a larger phase space, rather than a dimension reduction ([2012 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.5134), especially its discussion of the determining form).
- Azouani–Olson–Titi prove convergence of continuous data assimilation under sufficient finite-resolution observations ([2014 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0997)). This continuously injects observations into a model and establishes asymptotic synchronization; it does not solve the one-snapshot forecast problem.
- Mori–Zwanzig gives an exact non-Markovian resolved equation with memory and unresolved forcing. Practical memory approximations are known, but the orthogonal dynamics/kernel calculation can remain as expensive as the full system; Gouasmi–Parish–Duraisamy study a snapshot-based approximation for Burgers and Kuramoto–Sivashinsky ([2017 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06277)). The present mode-pair calculation is consistent with this established closure picture.

**Prior-work cost ledger (as reported or inferable from the stated interfaces).**

| Route | Acquisition | Precision / preprocessing | Output and cost boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fourier calculation above | Four Fourier modes and \(K\), supplied exactly; constant-size input | Integer/rational arithmetic; no data cleaning or learned representation | One exact low-mode derivative coefficient; a constant number of rational operations on \(O(\log K)\)-bit integers. This is a counterexample, not a forecast algorithm. |
| Determining modes / data assimilation | Model, \(\nu\), forcing, interpolant, and a continuing stream of resolved observations are supplied. Theorems bound sufficient resolution using physical parameters; they do not meter sensor calibration or data transport. | Mode/interpolant construction and numerical integration; the theoretical results do not provide an end-to-end bit-precision or physical acquisition ledger. | Asymptotically synchronized state/trajectory; not an instantaneous closure or standalone forecast from a single snapshot. |
| Mori–Zwanzig memory closure | Full governing dynamics and a projection are specified; practical methods may require full-order snapshots or orthogonal-dynamics information. | Kernel estimation/truncation is additional preprocessing; nonlinear-kernel approximation is not generally certified. | Memory kernel and reduced predictor; full-order cost can remain in the kernel-generation stage. |

**Missing capability.** A representation that is actually acquired from a stated sensor interface and carries enough information about unresolved forcing/memory to improve finite-horizon prediction with a proof of error and a complete cost ledger. Avoid declaring victory from a good fit on a closed training ensemble.

**Decisive next step.** First prove upper and lower bounds for \(\mathcal E_K(T;E,\nu)\) on the stated class, beginning with the explicit pair above and a low-mode norm estimate over a nonzero time interval. Then freeze a finite-history interface and compare it against (i) the same resolved-mode Markov predictor, (ii) standard Galerkin closure, and (iii) a Mori–Zwanzig finite-memory baseline on held-out Fourier phases. Charge observations, history storage, precision, preprocessing, failed fits, and runtime. If the lower bound vanishes at the intended \((K,T,\epsilon)\) regime, do not sell the instantaneous-closure obstruction as a practical forecast barrier.

## Target 2 — Certify a computed 3D flow trajectory

**North Star.** A scientific simulation that can say, with a checkable proof, when its computed 3D fluid trajectory approximates a unique strong solution for a stated time horizon.

**Exact question.** Given a divergence-free 3D periodic initial field and forcing in an explicit computable representation (start with finite rational Fourier data), viscosity \(\nu>0\), finite horizon \(T\), and tolerance \(\epsilon\), build a machine-checkable algorithm that either (a) certifies a unique strong solution on \([0,T]\) and an \(\epsilon\)-accurate output path, or (b) returns UNKNOWN. Determine finite resource bounds in terms of the input representation, Sobolev norms, \(\nu,T,\epsilon\). Do not turn failure of a sufficient certificate into evidence of blow-up.

**Importance.** This creates a trustworthy boundary between a plausible numerical trajectory and a theorem about the exact PDE, with direct relevance to high-consequence flow simulation.

**Obstruction.** Global regularity/uniqueness for arbitrary smooth 3D Navier–Stokes data remains unresolved. Even for a finite-time trajectory, a mathematically terminating conditional algorithm is not yet a practical resource guarantee. Coarse observations add a separate input-reconstruction problem.

**Original first investigation (literature theorem boundary).** Chernyshenko, Constantin, Robinson, and Titi derive an a-posteriori condition from a Galerkin approximation that guarantees a strong solution for the specified exact data. Their convergence theorem shows that if a strong solution already exists through \(T\), sufficiently refined Galerkin approximants pass the test; they also extend the framework to fully discrete approximations ([2007 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0607181), §§5–8, particularly Theorem 8 and Corollary 9). This is a conditional finite-termination result for an individual regular trajectory, not a proof that every input is regular and not an efficiency bound. Separately, Sun–Zhong–Ziegler show uniform computability of local strong 2D Navier–Stokes solutions under a deliberately constructed Type-2 representation of divergence-free fields ([2020 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-41672-0_7)); this reinforces that “the input field” is a representation choice, not free information.

**Prior-work cost ledger.**

- **Acquisition:** The a-posteriori theorem takes the initial field and forcing as mathematical inputs. For finite Fourier data, their coefficients and an encoding of \(\nu,T\) are supplied. If the field comes from sensors, reconstruction, calibration, unresolved-tail bounds, and measurement uncertainty are additional unpaid steps in the cited theorem.
- **Precision and preprocessing:** Galerkin coefficients, residual/tail estimates, and time-integrated norms must be bounded accurately enough to satisfy the criterion. A production certificate needs outward-rounded arithmetic and certified quadrature; the paper proves existence/termination, but supplies no modern end-to-end bit, memory, or wall-clock bound in Reynolds number.
- **Output:** A pass certifies a strong solution through the selected finite horizon and bounds the approximate trajectory. A fail means only that this sufficient test did not pass.
- **Compute model:** For a 3D spectral discretization with \(M^3\) modes, a nonlinear pseudo-spectral step is conventionally \(O(M^3\log M)\) arithmetic before validated interval overhead; this is a solver model, not a complexity theorem in the cited paper. The theorem gives no bound on the required \(M\), number of time steps, precision, or certificate size from \((\nu,T,\|u_0\|,\|f\|)\) alone.

**Missing capability.** A fail-closed verifier whose proof object can be independently checked, whose numeric operations are rigorously enclosed, and whose complete cost is reported. A trajectory-specific certificate is attainable when the sufficient inequalities close; a uniform all-input guarantee would cross into the unresolved regularity problem.

**Decisive next step.** Reproduce the published scalar a-posteriori criterion for one finite-Fourier 3D benchmark with public initial data, forcing, and viscosity. Report mode counts, time steps, interval precision, residual and tail bounds, certificate size, and whether the check passes. Use a known exact shear flow only as a pipeline sanity case; it is too simple to establish high-Re capability. If the certificate does not close, retain UNKNOWN and identify which bound dominates before changing the criterion.

## Findings and claim boundary

1. Exact instantaneous closure from resolved modes fails on a concrete smooth energy-bounded Navier–Stokes class; this follows from the quadratic Fourier interaction and is not claimed novel.
2. The result does not settle finite-time forecast error or show that learned/history closures cannot work.
3. Determining modes/assimilation and Mori–Zwanzig are strong existing routes with different contracts: trajectory assimilation and memory-based reduction. Neither erases the input/acquisition or cost question.
4. Verified 3D finite-time computation is already possible conditionally for particular regular trajectories; practical complexity and broad coverage remain the engineering/mathematical gates.
