**Date:** 2026-10-12

## Exploration status

The strongest candidate is **interventional thermodynamic identifiability for partially observed transport networks**. The mathematical question is promising and experimentally addressable, but the field is mature. The toy calculation below independently reconstructs a known hidden-channel obstruction; it is not a novelty claim, a new general theorem, or evidence of an energy advantage. The open part is a sharp, model-class-robust account of what controlled coarse observations identify about work-producing transport.

Three directions were screened:

- **Survivor — hidden-channel work and transport under interventions.** A small exact model gives a strong obstruction and a controllable experimental interface. Candidate for a focused mathematical programme; novelty confidence is low pending deeper review.
- **Not selected — minimum-dissipation control for overdamped diffusions.** Chen, Georgiou, and Tannenbaum connect finite-time excess work to squared Wasserstein-2 distance divided by protocol duration for controlled quadratic potentials; Blaber and Sivak review exact and approximate solutions across control regimes. I found no specific unresolved physical interface in the initial pass. A new direction would need genuinely partial observations, non-equilibrium steady states, or actuator constraints.
- **Not selected — coarse-grained macroscopic fluctuation laws.** Falasco and Esposito derive a macroscopic stochastic thermodynamics and fluctuation theory for broad classes of Markov jump systems, including chemical networks and continuous-space limits. “Find a general nonequilibrium coarse-grained law” is too broad here; no narrower transport theorem with a sharper empirical discriminator emerged in this pass.

## Survivor: interventional identifiability of useful work in partially observed transport networks

### 1. North Star

Build a **set-valued thermodynamic system-identification theory** that takes the measurements an experiment can actually provide—coarse state sequences, dwell times, a controlled force or load, and selected resolved fluxes—and returns the sharp range of compatible transport, useful output work, and dissipation. It should say when the data support a robust control choice and when hidden mechanisms make such a choice unknowable.

The plausible interface is a force-clamped molecular motor or catalytic transport cycle. The same mathematics could apply to electrochemical or chemical transport networks, provided the controlled load and measured flux are physically conjugate.

### 2. Exact question

Let a finite continuous-time Markov network have microscopic state (X_t), observed macrostate (Y_t=\pi(X_t)), and externally controlled load (f). Hidden transitions carry mechanical increments (d_e) and reservoir affinities. Assume a known inverse temperature (\beta), local detailed balance, and an explicit finite model class (for example, at most (N_{\max}) hidden states, known bounds on allowed increments and rates, and specified load coupling).

Given finite records of Y_t and dwell times under a selected set of force protocols, determine the **sharp identified set** of compatible current-response curves J(f), output-power curves P(f)=fJ(f), and entropy-production rates. Characterize the conditions under which each quantity is unique or has finite upper and lower bounds, and which additional measurements or interventions shrink that set. Include finite-sample confidence regions and their acquisition cost.

This is not asking whether an optimizer can fit one guessed hidden network. It asks what every thermodynamically admissible hidden network consistent with the data permits.

### 3. Importance

A coarse trajectory fit can be accurate while its cycle currents and fuel-to-work conversion are wrong. A robust identified set would prevent a control policy from treating a convenient microscopic realization as established physics. It could also turn experimental design into a mathematical problem: choose a small sequence of load or chemical-potential perturbations that most reduces uncertainty in deliverable work.

The possible payoff is a dependable bridge from measurements to interventions in driven microscopic transport. The present evidence supports the importance of the inference problem, not a broad claim about power generation or a civilization-scale energy gain.

### 4. Current obstruction

Coarse-graining erases which parallel transition occurred. Those channel labels can carry opposite mechanical increments and different chemical affinities, even when their rates sum to the same observed transition rate. Passive macrostate trajectories then fail to identify the physical cycle current or its dissipation. More passive data can estimate the visible process arbitrarily precisely without resolving this structural ambiguity.

Waiting-time asymmetries, fluctuation relations, and uncertainty relations recover useful lower bounds or exact values under structural conditions. They do not automatically identify a load-response law over all microscopic models. A finite collection of controlled measurements also need not identify an unbounded hidden network unless its admissible size, coupling, and rate class are specified.

### 5. Missing capability

The missing capability is an **intervention-aware inverse theory with explicit model-class assumptions**:

1. Map partial state and dwell-time observations, together with controlled loads, to all compatible local-detailed-balance generators.
2. Optimize current, work, and dissipation over that compatible set, returning sharp bounds rather than a single fitted model.
3. Separate structural uncertainty from finite-sample uncertainty and report what extra observation—channel label, displacement, turnover count, or another load point—would remove each ambiguity.
4. State the computational scaling in hidden-state count and the physical inputs assumed, including force calibration, temperature, fuel chemical potential, and rate bounds.

The output should be an auditable identified set and an experiment-design recommendation. Whether this can be made computationally useful beyond small networks remains open.

### 6. First actual investigation: exact two-channel counterexample and a positive probe

Take two observed states (A,B) and two parallel microscopic channels. Channel 1 moves a mechanical coordinate by (+1) on (A\to B), and by (-1) on (B\to A). Channel 2 has the opposite increments. Let (0<\delta<1/2), and define the chemical affinity
\[
\mathcal A_\delta=\log\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}.
\]
At load (f\), assign rates as follows:

| Channel | (A\to B) rate | (B\to A) rate | (A\to B) displacement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | \((1-\delta)e^{-\beta f/2}\) | \(\delta e^{\beta f/2}\) | \(+1\) |
| 2 | \(\delta e^{\beta f/2}\) | \((1-\delta)e^{-\beta f/2}\) | \(-1\) |

Reverse transitions carry the negative of their forward displacement. The channel rate ratios are
\[
\log\frac{k_{1,A\to B}}{k_{1,B\to A}}=\mathcal A_\delta-\beta f,
\qquad
\log\frac{k_{2,A\to B}}{k_{2,B\to A}}=-\mathcal A_\delta+\beta f,
\]
so the two channels obey local detailed balance with opposite chemical affinities and opposite mechanical increments.

At zero load, each aggregate macro transition rate is
\[
k_{A B}(0)=k_{B A}(0)=(1-\delta)+\delta=1.
\]
Thus, for every (\delta), the full observed (A/B) state-path law is the same symmetric two-state continuous-time Markov chain. Its stationary distribution is (p_A=p_B=1/2), and its visible stationary current and visible entropy-production rate are zero.

The stationary channel currents, oriented from A to B, are
\[
j_1=\tfrac12[(1-\delta)e^{-\beta f/2}-\delta e^{\beta f/2}],
\qquad j_2=-j_1.
\]
Because their displacements are (+1) and (-1), the mechanical velocity is
\[
v_\delta(f)=j_1-j_2=(1-\delta)e^{-\beta f/2}-\delta e^{\beta f/2}.
\]
The stall load and steady entropy production are therefore
\[
f_{\rm stall}=\beta^{-1}\mathcal A_\delta,
\qquad
\dot S_{\rm tot}/k_B=v_\delta(f)(\mathcal A_\delta-\beta f).
\]
At zero load, (v_\delta(0)=1-2\delta) and (\dot S_{\rm tot}/k_B=(1-2\delta)\mathcal A_\delta). For example, (\delta=1/4) gives unloaded speed (1/2), stall load (\beta^{-1}\log 3), and entropy production (\tfrac12\log 3) per unit time in (k_B) units. (\delta=1/10) gives speed (4/5), stall load (\beta^{-1}\log 9), and entropy production (\tfrac45\log 9). As (\delta\to0), all passive visible path data remain fixed while the stall load and hidden entropy-production rate diverge.

For a motor running against a calibrated opposing force, useful output power is (P_{\rm out}(f)=f v_\delta(f)) wherever (v_\delta(f)>0). This counterexample does **not** apply if the mechanical displacement is already directly measured; then the transport current is observed. Its point is that the macrostate label and passive dwell times alone do not supply that flux.

There is also a useful conditional positive result. Under exactly this two-channel rate law, both visible rates at force (f) equal
\[
r_\delta(f)=(1-\delta)e^{-\beta f/2}+\delta e^{\beta f/2}.
\]
One calibrated nonzero force and a measured dwell rate identify δ:
\[
\delta=\frac{r_\delta(f)-e^{-\beta f/2}}{e^{\beta f/2}-e^{-\beta f/2}}.
\]
If N independent exponential dwell intervals are observed at that force, the rate estimate has asymptotic variance approximately r_δ(f)^2/N; the corresponding δ-estimate variance is approximately
\[
\frac{r_\delta(f)^2}{N\,[e^{\beta f/2}-e^{-\beta f/2}]^2}.
\]
This is model-conditional identifiability only. It supplies a concrete first experiment and a falsifiable prediction, but it does not show that one force probe identifies transport in an arbitrary hidden network.

**Status of this derivation:** independently checked algebra for a thermodynamically consistent two-channel model; known-type hidden-channel obstruction; no physical motor data fitted; no novelty or efficiency claim.

### 7. Prior work plus acquisition, precision, and output costs

The closest prior art substantially narrows any novelty claim:

- Esposito and Parrondo, [“Stochastic thermodynamics of hidden pumps,” *Physical Review E* 91, 052114 (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.91.052114), show that hidden reversible pumping can generate apparently Poissonian transitions between observed states and can produce finite current at zero dissipation in a timescale-separation limit. This is very close to the coarse-channel issue above.
- Kawaguchi and Nakayama, [“Fluctuation Theorem for Hidden Entropy Production,” *Physical Review E* 88, 022147 (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.88.022147), formalize entropy production lost under elimination of variables.
- van der Meer, Ertel, and Seifert, [*Physical Review X* 12, 031025 (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.12.031025), use transition-based waiting-time distributions to infer entropy and hidden-cycle properties, with exact recovery under structural conditions and lower bounds when hidden cycles remain.
- Ehrich, [“Tightest bound on hidden entropy production from partially observed dynamics,” *Journal of Statistical Mechanics* 2021, 083214](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08803), fits hidden Markov realizations to observable dynamics to obtain the tightest compatible lower bound for masked Markov networks.
- Pietzonka, Barato, and Seifert, [“Universal bound on the efficiency of molecular motors,” *Journal of Statistical Mechanics* 2016, 124004](https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-5468/2016/12/124004), use a thermodynamic uncertainty relation to bound motor efficiency from accessible current and dispersion without specifying the detailed motor mechanism.
- Nadal-Rosa and Manzano, [“Thermodynamic inference in molecular motors: a Martingale approach,” *Physical Review E* 112, 034114 (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/3htj-mzk3), already infer motor thermodynamic quantities including rotational work in an (F_1)-ATPase model from limited information. This is the closest work-extraction precedent found in the pass.
- Martínez et al., [“Inferring broken detailed balance in the absence of observable currents,” *Nature Communications* 10, 3542 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11051-w), infer irreversibility from time-series waiting statistics. Hartich and Godec later published a [comment](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52602-0), and Bisker et al. a [reply](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-52603-z), clarifying that the inference depends on the time-decimation and reversal convention. This exchange is a warning to specify the observation map precisely.
- The discarded control direction has strong established results: Chen, Georgiou, and Tannenbaum, [“Stochastic control and non-equilibrium thermodynamics: fundamental limits”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01271), give a Wasserstein-2 finite-time work bound for a specified controlled diffusion; Blaber and Sivak, [“Optimal control in stochastic thermodynamics” (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1088/2399-6528/acbf04), review the area.

**Acquisition and precision boundary.** In the toy model, more zero-load state observations cannot estimate (\delta) at any precision: it is structurally absent from their likelihood. Under the specified model, a calibrated force clamp at (f\ne0) and sufficiently resolved macrostate dwell times estimate (\delta) with the (N^{-1/2}) sampling scale shown above. That estimate is only as reliable as the known load-coupling and two-channel assumptions. To validate actual output rather than predict it from those assumptions, measure the conjugate displacement (Y_T) under calibrated force; the work is (W_{\rm out}=fY_T). An efficiency claim additionally needs chemical input or fuel-turnover measurements and reservoir chemical potentials. A practical assay therefore pays for force calibration, displacement and state time resolution, thermal calibration, sufficient independent steps, and—if efficiency is the target—fuel-flux measurement. No empirical acquisition cost or advantage has been measured in this exploration.

### 8. Decisive next step

First make the inverse problem finite and falsifiable: fix a small hidden-state and edge bound, known (\beta), allowed displacement set, calibrated force coupling, and specified finite-sample observation protocol. For that class, compute the sharp attainable interval of (J(f)), (P(f)), and (\dot S_{\rm tot}) after (i) passive macrostate records, (ii) one force-clamp intervention, and (iii) adding displacement or fuel-turnover observations. The two-channel model above is the exact unit test for any claimed identifiability theorem.

A decisive result would be either:

1. a constructive pair or family of admissible networks showing that the proposed measurement set still leaves output unbounded or non-unique, together with the minimal added sensor/probe that separates them; or
2. a proved sharp bound and an estimator with finite-sample coverage for a clearly declared network class, followed by a blinded synthetic check against equally informed hidden-network alternatives and a real single-molecule record.

Before any claim of novelty, reproduce the best existing hidden-entropy and martingale estimators on the same two-channel and hidden-pump examples, charge the physical load/displacement/fuel measurements separately from post-supplied-model computation, and verify the closest literature on load-controlled work inference. The expected immediate outcome is a better-defined boundary and experiment, not a claimed new law or energy gain.
