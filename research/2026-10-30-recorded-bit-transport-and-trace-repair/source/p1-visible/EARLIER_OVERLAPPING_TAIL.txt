The present bridge obtains a related cancellation directly on recorded binary traces using sampling callbacks. It does not establish the general idea of correlated likelihood-free inference as new.

7. What is contributed, and what remains a resource

Dynamic sampling and incremental inference already have strong predecessors. Feng, He, Sun and Yin use local optimal couplings and probability-based resampling in dynamically changing graphical models. Cusumano-Towner and colleagues translate execution traces and correct them using probability-based weights. These establish that reusing simulations after model changes is an existing research direction.

Bernoulli-factory research also already supplies nonlinear sampling operations from unknown probabilities, including polynomial-query constructions for exponential-weight choices. This ruled out treating generic exact selection from unknown coin means as the new contribution.

The construction developed here supplies a particular missing operation:

Condition a useful coupling on a sample that is already recorded, implement that conditional operation using fresh bits alone, and compose it into exact trace repair and a reversible bridge between models.

The locality theorem then turns that operation into a computational capability. The freshness lower bound identifies a different returned-law requirement that cannot generally receive the same savings.

The material conditions remain:

Structured local sampling access. The compiler must be able to query both versions at specified parent values.
A paid initial ensemble and graph representation.
A condition limiting propagation for the cheap-work guarantee. Correctness alone does not prevent a large repair.
Independent repair randomness across ensemble members.
Model edits independent of the cached simulation ensemble for the direct ensemble-law theorem. Choosing edits from the same stored samples can introduce selection bias. The posterior construction uses a separate balance correction; simple repair does not solve that problem.
Incremental access to the result. Full copying or full downstream recomputation can restore linear cost.

A learned-probability simulator has a different approximation contract and may become attractive after enough paid calibration. No claim here establishes superiority over every such method for a specified scientific loss.

Finally, the physical and earlier computational failures remain intact. The binary experiment theorem, exact coupling marginals, expected-accuracy scaling and early-stopping comparator are preserved. Ordinary simulation still won the earlier finite-accuracy stationary-average task. The passive implementation’s approximately 1.2 million expected calendar windows for one pair remains a serious limitation. The older bounded-sensor optimization execution still failed its zero-experiment comparison.

All new execution counts above are ideal source flips, not physical calendar windows. A full returned-transcript error bound can be propagated through a finitely capped computation, but it does not by itself control an unbounded estimator, an unbounded runtime, or a perturbed chain’s stationary law.

The result worth carrying forward is an executable way to maintain complete stochastic states through local model revisions under sample-only access, together with a quantitative separation between that operation and fresh experimentation. The large repair executions support the former; the posterior comparison and freshness bounds prevent extending the claim beyond what the mechanism actually supplies.
