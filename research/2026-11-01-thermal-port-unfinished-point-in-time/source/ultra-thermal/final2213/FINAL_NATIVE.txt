I developed a [finite-controller bridge](producer-workspace/THERMAL_PORT_THEOREM.txt) covering **both Pauli error types**, using the [3D thermal-code framework](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10943v1) and current reviewed Astra premises. It uses finite coherent controllers and one-site controller baths, with no controller-elimination limit. The key condition is whole-device Gibbs-reference balance.

The additional correlated-input theorem gives
\[
\mathrm{error}\le CnT\sqrt{M p_{\mathrm{bad}}},
\qquad \rho_{CB}\le M\tau_{CB}.
\]
It retains the **same bath** and allows logical/controller/bath/reference correlations. Since \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}\) falls stretched-exponentially with size, a sub-barrier budget \(\log M\) preserves a long storage interval.

Four control programs passed. In the five-qubit/28-level fixture at \(\beta=8,T=10\), logical error was **0.001518**, versus **0.000634** with the same device plus five ideal scheduled recoveries. [Costs, sources and reproduction](producer-workspace/COSTS_AND_SOURCES.txt) record that comparison.

I also proved a scoped [selector obstruction](producer-workspace/SELECTOR_OBSTRUCTION.txt): bilinear couplers cannot preserve this product reference for codes satisfying the stated conditions. Implementable selectors, generic nonequilibrium faults and practical advantage remain unresolved. Earlier results are unchanged.
