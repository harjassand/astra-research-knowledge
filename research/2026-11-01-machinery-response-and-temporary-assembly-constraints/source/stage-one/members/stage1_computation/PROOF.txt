FROM RARE-EVENT MACHINERY TO CHEMICAL INTERVENTION DESIGN
Stage-one constructive contender and discriminating failures
1 November 2026

DECISION

The consequential proposed use is to design experimentally realizable controls
that favor a desired chemical transition over competing outcomes, using acquired
path data rather than a full new simulation or experiment for every design.
Existing machinery genuinely supplies restartable rare-event paths, estimated
committors, path ensembles and, with the correct integrator records, exact
likelihood ratios for changed dynamics. This is much more than a proposed ML
benchmark. However, the generic prediction-from-paths capability already has a
strong conventional construction: Girsanov reweighting and pathwise sensitivity.

The new step would have to acquire a compact INTERVENTION-sufficient record under
actual sensing and actuation constraints. A native rate, free energy or learned
coarse reaction coordinate does not automatically supply that record. The tests
below give an exact failure and a complete ordinary alternative. This contender
should not receive the main sustained construction stage merely for composing
rare-event sampling with a predictor. It remains useful if another physical
operation actually supplies otherwise inaccessible control-response information.

1. WORKING MACHINERY INSPECTED BEFORE CHOOSING THE USE

Three independent source families were examined.

(a) WESTPA 2.0: weighted trajectory splitting/merging with restartable simulator
states. The current source's _split_walker divides the parent weight among
children; _merge_walkers sums the weights and randomly chooses the continued
history proportionally to parent weight. The actual simulator state and history
references are retained. This is not physical cloning of an unmeasured specimen.

(b) AIMMD/RFPS: two-way shooting from selected molecular configurations and
reweighting of path ensembles. The Zenodo release contains independent runs,
reference committors and generating code; its contemporary GitHub code carries
forward/backward path records, shooting density estimates, path types and
normalization. The inspected source includes finite-data cutoffs and density
estimation. These operations do not license treating a fitted committor as an
exact physical control field.

(c) Girsanov reweighting in OpenMM/Deeptime: a 2024 primary implementation adds
on-the-fly likelihood-factor calculation to the integrator. It needs the applied
force differences and Gaussian innovations at the integration resolution. The
primary paper expressly discusses integrator-dependent absolute continuity and
loss of path overlap when perturbations shift the reaction channels.

The exact fetched M_ABOBA helper was executed on finite Gaussian records. It
reproduced the density-ratio mean and shifted-noise observables within the stated
Monte Carlo uncertainty. This verifies a source operation, not a molecular
application. WESTPA and AIMMD source were inspected but not executed end to end.
No 14 GB molecular dataset was claimed to have been downloaded or simulated.

2. THE STRONG CONVENTIONAL CONSTRUCTION

Let a continuous-time Markov jump process have rates q_e(x), with common initial
law, and let a physically specified control change rates to q_e^u(x). For a fully
observed path up to a bounded stopping/horizon convention, its likelihood ratio is

 L_u = product_j [q_(e_j)^u(X_(t_j-))/q_(e_j)^0(X_(t_j-))]
       * exp[-integral sum_e(q_e^u(X_t)-q_e^0(X_t)) dt].

Require absolute continuity and the usual integrability for the chosen horizon.
Then E_u[F]=E_0[L_u F]. This is standard path likelihood reweighting, not a new
theorem claimed here. The diffusion analogue is precisely the operation of the
inspected Girsanov implementation.

For constant scalar control with q_e^u=q_e^0 exp(b_e u), if the couplings b_e take
only K known values b_g, define channel-group counts and integrated hazards

 N_g = number of jumps whose b_e=b_g,
 A_g = integral sum_(e:b_e=b_g) q_e^0(X_t) dt.

Then

 log L_u = u sum_g b_g N_g - sum_g[exp(b_g u)-1] A_g.

Thus a K-group intervention-specific record is exactly sufficient for all
constant u in the admitted family, even if the microscopic state is huge. This
is an ordinary exponential-family reduction. It identifies an actual positive
construction and its cost: N_g, A_g and the physical couplings must be acquired.
Knowing only jump counts does not generally supply the hidden integrated hazards.
If each reaction has a different b_e, K can be the whole reaction catalogue.
If controls depend on time or feedback, their corresponding marked history must
be retained. Initial-law changes also require their likelihood ratio.

3. EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE: NATIVE OBSERVATIONS DO NOT IDENTIFY CONTROL RESPONSE

States are A,H1,H2,B,C. A is reactant; B,C are distinct stopped product outcomes.
The observation distinguishes A,H,B,C but merges H1 and H2. For an external
control u, set

 A -> H1: exp(u)/2,       H1 -> A: exp(u),
 A -> H2: exp(-u)/2,      H2 -> A: exp(-u).

Each intermediate also enters its product at rate 1. In model I, H1 produces B
and H2 produces C. In model II, the products are interchanged.

The A-H forward AND reverse rates receive the same factor. Their equilibrium
ratios therefore stay fixed: this is a barrier-control example, not an
unaccounted change in affinity on a cycle. The stopped-product description can
be embedded in a reversible process by adding reverse product rates; those
rates are irrelevant before the first product hit. A material realization of
this selective barrier control remains an additional requirement.

At u=0 the hidden-state permutation H1<->H2 maps model I into model II while
leaving the initial state and observation unchanged. Consequently their ENTIRE
observed native path laws coincide, not just their mean rates. Both have
P(B first)=1/2 and mean product first-passage time 3.

For model I, a renewal at A gives

 P_u(B first) = [a1/(r1+1)]/[a1/(r1+1)+a2/(r2+1)]
             = exp(u)/(1+exp(u)),

where a_i=r_i/2. The two terms in the denominator sum to 1/2 because r1*r2=1.
The same renewal equation gives

 E_u[tau_product] = [1+sum_i a_i/(r_i+1)]/[sum_i a_i/(r_i+1)] = 3.

Model II has the complementary product probability. At u=log(9), the intended
product yield is 0.9 in model I and 0.1 in model II, while mean time remains 3 in
both. No amount of additional UNCONTROLLED coarse observation can choose the
correct control sign. Control-response information is missing.

The strongest equally informed ordinary response is an intervention experiment.
Forty independent trials at that control, with exact product observation, identify
the model by majority with error about 1.03e-10. This finite calculation counts
40 whole reaction trials, with mean total path time 120 in model units; it does
not price preparation, actuator work, product readout or correlated runs. It
does show that a supplied gigantic native path corpus is not always the best
resource for design. An active conventional probe resolves the ambiguity.

The executable checks the generator equations, hidden-state marginal equality
and exact probabilities. The proof by permutation establishes the stronger
whole-observed-path equality.

4. COMPLETE LOCAL CONTROL INFORMATION AND ITS LIMIT

For a differentiable finite control vector theta, let S be the native path
score, S=gradient_theta log L_theta at zero. Let Y indicate the desired product.
Under differentiability/integrability conditions,

 g = gradient_theta P_theta(Y=1)|0 = E_0[Y S],
 I = E_0[S S^T],     E_0[S]=0.

I is the path Fisher information matrix. This is established machinery, with
primary precedents in biochemical-network sensitivity analysis. For a small
control perturbation with theta^T I theta<=2D,

 max theta^T g = sqrt(2D g^T I^+ g),
 theta proportional to I^+ g,

with g in the range of I. Proof: whiten the quadratic form and apply
Cauchy-Schwarz. Projection of Y-EY onto the score span also gives

 0 <= g^T I^+g/[p(1-p)] <= 1.

This fraction quantifies which outcome variation is locally accessible through
the supplied physical controls. It is not obtained from a scalar committor alone,
and D is path relative entropy to second order, NOT automatically dissipated work.

For the model in Section 3 at u=0, expected occupation times before absorption
are (2,1/2,1/2) for A,H1,H2. The score is

 S=N_AH1+N_H1A-N_AH2-N_H2A-T_H1+T_H2.

Its Fisher information is 3; g=1/4; the accessible fraction is 1/12. The
100,000-path simulation estimates the gradient and information using actual
full simulator records. It does not supply H identity or hazards to a coarse
experimental observer. Obtaining those records, or replacing them with an
equally informative intervention protocol, is the central missing physical step.

5. A SECOND FAILURE: PATH OVERLAP IS A RESOURCE

Let P be the native path law and Q the desired controlled law. Let p=P(B),
q=Q(B), and L=dQ/dP. For ordinary unbiased importance sampling of an event,
Z=1_B L. Rao-Blackwell conditioning on the event yields

 E_P[Z|1_B]=1_B q/p,
 Var_P(Z)>=q^2(1-p)/p.

Thus an iid native average needs N>=(1-p)/(p epsilon^2) to achieve relative
standard error epsilon even at this optimistic lower-variance limit.

Self-normalized importance sampling is a stronger ordinary competitor and is
included in the test. It can trade bias for lower variance; the preceding
UNBIASED variance bound does not apply to it. Its collapsed-event asymptotic
relative variance is (1-q)^2/[N p(1-p)]. However both forms of weighted empirical
event measurement return zero when their sample contains no event, with
probability (1-p)^N. An asymptotic error bar is misleading when Np is order one.

Illustration: p=1e-5, q=0.9, epsilon=0.1. The optimistic unbiased requirement is
about 10 million native paths. With 100,000 paths, the probability of no event is
36.8 percent. Even obtaining one event with 95 percent probability requires
299,572 native paths. Direct samples from Q require only 12 runs for the stated
relative STANDARD-ERROR calculation (not a high-confidence guarantee).

The target-event likelihoods used for this extremal calculation are analytic
Rao-Blackwell quantities, not an estimator that already knows the answer q.
The point is a variance/support limitation on a specified reuse scheme.

These bounds do NOT cover adaptive splitting, bridging ensembles, learned
structural models, new target-dynamics simulations or independent analytic
information. WESTPA and RFPS are legitimate ways to change the acquisition
process, not methods disproved by the iid argument. If their simulator and
couplings are available, the comparator must receive them too.

Data processing also gives D(Q||P)>=d_Bernoulli(q||p), which is 10.03655 nats in
the example. This is an information displacement, not a claimed minimum fuel
cost without a physical thermodynamic model.

6. STAGE-ONE JUDGMENT

Full success of actuator-constrained molecular intervention design would be
consequential for selective chemistry and materials control. The available
machinery can supply rate/path calculations for a specified microscopic model;
it does not by itself provide experimentally accessible intervention sufficiency.

The evidence changes the choice early: generic reweighting, committor learning
or a small score-based design algorithm is already supplied by strong ordinary
methods. A future construction would have to acquire physical control-response
information cheaply, exploit a demonstrated local intervention that changes the
sufficiency requirement, or overcome path support through an earned operation.
This run has not constructed that final service. It therefore recommends keeping
the exact counterexample, source operations and finite comparator as capital,
and concentrating sustained invention on a stronger surviving physical contender.
