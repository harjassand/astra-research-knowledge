FINITE THERMAL COVARIANCE VERSUS POSITIVE ACTIVE RESPONSE
Independent mathematical comparison for the finite-electrode branch
1 November 2026

DECISION

The covariance identity is useful and can avoid additional actuation when thermal
motion is well resolved. It does not by itself create a general advantage over
ordinary active identification. A four-read, positive on/off protocol already
estimates the same scalar response on one unreset trajectory, canceling unknown
constant and linearly changing drift. A finite likelihood calculation exhibits
a regime where this ordinary competitor is much more informative than hundreds
of passive reads. Its price is real forward displacement and mechanical work.

The other decisive obstruction is reachability: even exact knowledge of the
boundary response kernel does not turn PSD voltage covariance into signed
control. The two-electrode example below cannot drive the mean conductance down.
Any claimed programmable response needs an earned reverse mechanism, autonomous
relaxation, or an explicitly restricted attainable target set.

1. WHAT THE THERMAL IDENTITY REALLY ASSUMES

Let g(q) be independent components of the measured conductance matrix. If using
symmetric vectorization, use an orthonormal convention (sqrt(2) on off-diagonal
components) so the Euclidean product agrees with tr(G C). Let J=dg/dq and M be
the positive mechanical mobility. A supplied physical force law is

 f_control = kappa J^T c,        c=vec(C), C positive semidefinite.

For an overdamped thermal Itô model with diffusion 2 theta M, theta=k_B T_bath,
the output equation has conditional local form

 dg = [b(q) + kappa K(q)c]dt + noise,
 d[g,g]_t = 2 theta K(q_t)dt,     K=J M J^T.

The drift b contains autonomous forces, thermal mobility drift and the Itô
curvature of g. The quadratic-variation identity follows directly from Itô's
formula. Global stationarity is not required for this instantaneous identity.
However, an arbitrary finite-lag covariance is not quadratic variation. A
finite record needs the observer transfer function, sampling bandwidth, drift
model, state excursion and any additional nonthermal noise.

The usual frequency-domain equilibrium FDT is an additional, different route;
its equilibrium and bandwidth conditions cannot be omitted. Neither route
allows an unknown active noise source to be reclassified as known thermal noise.

2. EXACT FINITE OBSERVATION EXPERIMENT, WITH NO LOCAL RESETS

Use the scalar affine special case (already sufficient for a counterexample):

 dg = [b(t) + kappa K c(t)]dt + sqrt(2 theta K) dW,
 y_i = g(ih) + eta_i,  eta_i iid N(0,R), i=0,...,n.

K>0 is constant on the acquired path, theta is known, and c_i>=0 is constant
over interval i. Every estimator uses one contiguous trajectory. Unknown
constant or affine-in-time b is fitted or removed, not supplied by a reset.
The detector model here is deliberately explicit; it is not a claimed actual
conductance detector or a free observation operation.

For observed increments d_i=y_i-y_(i-1),

 mean(d)=h b + kappa K h c,
 Gamma=2 theta K h I + R L,

where L has diagonal 2 and adjacent off-diagonal -1. The shared localization
errors create correlations; treating increments as independent overcounts
information. This is the same exact Gaussian machinery developed in established
single-particle tracking work, not a new statistical theory.

Let a=2 theta h. The eigenvalues of L are

 lambda_j=2-2cos(pi*j/(n+1)), j=1,...,n,
 s_j=a K+R lambda_j.

With known R, the covariance Fisher information is exactly

 I_cov=(a^2/2) sum_j 1/s_j^2.

If R is also an unknown interior parameter, the efficient covariance information
is I_KK - I_KR^2/I_RR, where

 I_KR=(a/2)sum_j lambda_j/s_j^2,
 I_RR=(1/2)sum_j lambda_j^2/s_j^2.

No regular interior CRLB is claimed at the boundary R=0 when R is unknown.

For unknown constant drift, the additional information carried by the active
mean is

 I_mean=kappa^2 h^2 [c^T Gamma^-1 c
              -(c^T Gamma^-1 1)^2/(1^T Gamma^-1 1)].

For polynomial drift, replace 1 by its full sampled design matrix and project
in the Gamma^-1 inner product. Mean and covariance scores are orthogonal in the
Gaussian experiment, so total information is I_cov+I_mean. The executable uses
the full correlated-increment likelihood and includes unknown affine drift.

For constant positive c, I_mean=0 with unknown constant drift. Positive bias
then moves the material and consumes power without resolving response from
autonomous drift. Statistical subtraction of a baseline does not cancel its
physical force. Reversing voltage polarity leaves C and its force unchanged.

3. CONSTRUCTIVE ORDINARY COMPETITOR: FOUR READS, POSITIVE ON/OFF CONTROL

Apply c=0 on [0,T/4), c=cmax on [T/4,3T/4), and c=0 on [3T/4,T].
Read y at times 0,T/4,3T/4,T. Define

 K_hat=2 [y(0)-2y(T/4)+2y(3T/4)-y(T)]/(kappa cmax T).

For any unknown b(t)=b0+b1*t this is an unbiased estimate of K. The control is
everywhere nonnegative. There is no reverse force or return to the initial
state. The signed quantities are only arithmetic weights on observations.

Its exact variance is

 Var(K_hat)=8 theta K/(kappa^2 cmax^2 T)
             +40 R/(kappa^2 cmax^2 T^2).

The first term is thermal motion over the four contiguous quarters. The second
comes from detector coefficients (1,-2,2,-1), whose squares sum to 10, with the
outer estimator factor 4. A midpoint read is unnecessary.

This protocol's mean controlled displacement is

 Delta_act=kappa K cmax T/2,

and the induced viscous work contribution is

 W_act=kappa^2 K cmax^2 T/2.

Actual electrode Joule loss, reading power and autonomous-force work must be
added. No claim equates W_act with complete device energy.

The protocol also admits a finite drift-error statement. If |b''(t)|<=B2, the
Peano kernel of the contrast is everywhere nonpositive, with integral -T^3/32.
Therefore

 |bias(K_hat)|<=B2 T^2/(16 kappa cmax).

Thus taking longer records decreases statistical error but increases sensitivity
to unmodeled drift curvature and increases irreversible actuation displacement.
Arbitrary unknown drift can always absorb an arbitrary active mean response;
some temporal/state regularity must be earned by either method.

4. ACTIVE INFORMATION HAS A WORK AND DISPLACEMENT PRICE

In the complete noiseless continuous record, with unknown constant b,

 K^2 I_mean = [kappa^2 K integral c(t)^2 dt
             -kappa^2 K (integral c(t)dt)^2/T]/(2 theta).

The first term is W_act/(2 theta). The second is the information lost because
the constant component of the drive is confounded with autonomous drift. If
b=0, W_act is the expected actuator work in this affine overdamped model. If
autonomous forces exist, it is only the positive induced viscous contribution;
the complete actuator-work expression has additional cross terms.

Noisy measurements are a parameter-independent channel from the complete
record, so they cannot increase this active mean information. If 0<=c<=cmax,
write U=integral c dt and Delta_act=kappa K U. Then

 K^2 I_mean <= kappa cmax Delta_act/(2 theta)
               -Delta_act^2/(2 theta K T)
            <= kappa cmax Delta_act/(2 theta).

This is a bound on extra mean-response information, not a bound on total active
information: active records still carry thermal covariance information.
Without a peak/control-work bound, arbitrarily large ideal pulses defeat a
displacement-only comparison; those pulses consume unbounded work in the model.

5. NUMERICAL DISCRIMINATING TEST

Dimensionless affine test: K=1, kappa=1, theta=.002, R=.01, n=256, h=.02,
T=5.12. The positive protocol has cmax=.5. The exact finite information is

 passive covariance I_cov=2.62621,
 active additional mean I_mean=58.84272,
 passive relative CRLB=.61707,
 combined relative CRLB=.12755.

These are regular locally unbiased CRLBs, not finite confidence guarantees.
The finite likelihood test profiles unknown constant and linear drift and
includes both ordinary profile ML and restricted-likelihood estimates. Their
finite bias and boundary estimates are recorded rather than hidden. Six hundred
independent Monte Carlo repetitions each test one single unreset acquisition.
Results, including those finite-estimator details, are in the JSON artifact.

The much simpler four-read protocol has exact standard deviation .27117 under
the same parameters. Give the passive comparator the further oracle of exactly
known initial state and autonomous drift, calibrated R and every raw position
in its 257-read schedule. Its covariance information increases to 2.87061, with
regular locally unbiased CRLB .59022. The four-read active protocol still beats
this stronger passive bound while using fewer reads, by spending forward
actuation displacement 1.28 and induced
viscous work .64. Its active state range is larger; it is admissible only if the
linear-response region accommodates that motion. It is not a free improvement.
This comparison is not a lower bound over every alternative sampling schedule,
observer construction or biased estimator; it already refutes a general passive
information advantage under a specified, equally available observer.

For thermal noise well above the detector floor, passive covariance can already
be accurate with negligible extra actuation, and driving can be wasteful. In the
low-temperature/low-thermal-motion limit with fixed detector error, passive
covariance information vanishes while a finite active mean can remain visible.
The parameter grid in the code exhibits both regimes. There is no universal
winner independent of temperature, readout, control amplitude and allowable
state excursion.

6. NO INFINITE INFORMATION FROM FREE OBSERVER BANDWIDTH

With R=0, I_cov=n/(2K^2). Interpreting n->infinity at fixed duration as a physical
resource gain would supply an infinitely fast noiseless observer and an
overdamped model beyond its physical bandwidth.

Even allowing independent fixed-R reads at ever smaller h implicitly supplies
increasing detector resources. As a simple explicit fixed-resource model,
take R=S/h, where S is a fixed measurement noise spectral resource. At fixed
T=nh the covariance information tends to the finite limit

 I_cov -> 2 theta^2 sum_(j>=1)
          [2 theta K+S(pi*j/T)^2]^-2.

For theta=.01, K=1, S=.003, T=10, increasing n from 40 to 10240 gives relative
information from 3.193 to 2.978; the analytic limit is 2.97749. The point-read
kernel is an illustration. Actual finite exposure adds motion blur and needs its
proper observation kernel. The finite resource, not this particular kernel, is
the physical issue.

If conductance is read by ordinary applied voltage and measured current, a
schematic calibrated white-current-noise model gives

 Var(G_hat) = S_I/(p h),       p=V_rms^2,

up to the declared spectral convention. The same p contributes to the physical
PSD control covariance and force. Its heat is P=tr(G C_read). Thus changing
readout precision changes a force and power budget. Other observers, such as
passive equilibrium-noise measurements, may be possible, but must be constructed
and costed rather than silently substituted. The scalar simulation grants an
independent detector to BOTH competitors and does not claim to earn one.

7. LOCALITY WITHOUT RESETTING

Both methods need K approximately constant over the acquired region, or an
explicit nonstationary model. If |K(q)-K(q0)|<=L_K |q-q0| and the acquired path
stays in radius r, this produces response variation of order L_K r. A long
trajectory is not many samples of the original local K if it leaves that region.

For a zero-drift scalar thermal trajectory, the reflection principle gives

 P(sup_(0<=t<=T)|g(t)-g(0)|>=L)
 <=4[1-Phi(L/sqrt(2 theta K T))], clipped at 1.

This is a sufficient finite confinement check, not a resetting operation. An
active protocol must reserve additional range for Delta_act and autonomous
drift. If a controller traps or restores the specimen, its force law, readout,
information, work and effect on the acquired spectrum must be included.

8. EXACT PSD-CONE COUNTEREXAMPLE

For two electrodes let

 G(q)=q [[1,-1],[-1,1]], q>0,

with scalar positive mechanical mobility. For every voltage covariance C>=0,

 tr((dG/dq) C)=E[(V1-V2)^2]>=0.

Consequently the deterministic controlled velocity of q is nonnegative for
kappa>0. The boundary kernel K is nonzero and can even be known exactly, yet a
target requiring decreasing mean q cannot be produced by this actuator in the
zero-autonomous-drift affine model. Thermal excursions are not a deterministic
reverse actuator, finite-mean reset or stabilizer. This is a mean/local control
obstruction, not a claim that Brownian motion never visits lower q.

More generally, the available output velocity cone is

 V(q)= {kappa K(q) vec(C): C>=0}.

If K is invertible on the independent boundary-coordinate space, an infinitesimal
target velocity v is attainable exactly when mat(K^-1 v/kappa)>=0. Equivalently,
any separating functional w with mat(Kw)>=0 is nondecreasing under pure control.
Knowing a positive kernel does not remove this condition. Nonlinear paths might
change the cone; such a route needs its own construction and costs.

The PSD cone does linearly span the symmetric control space. Ordinary active
identification can therefore use rank-one controls e_i e_i^T and
(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T and recover signed matrix combinations arithmetically. This
is sufficient for identification, not signed actuation: all physical probes
have positive bias. For a known desired task, a strong competitor may need only
directional actions of K and can avoid learning the entire kernel.

9. EXACT FAILURE IF THE THERMAL SOURCE IS NOT EARNED

Suppose the actual output noise has diffusion theta K+N, with an unknown
additional positive active-noise contribution N. At theta=1, the scalar models
(K,N)=(1,1) and (2,0) have identical uncontrolled full path laws if their
autonomous drift and observation law coincide. Their response to the same
nonzero c differs by a factor two. Covariance cannot identify K without the
thermal-source condition; an ordinary active response probe can distinguish
these two models. This is not a counterexample to the thermal Itô identity; it
states exactly why the identity's physical premise matters.

10. IMPLICATION FOR THE ROOT CONSTRUCTION

The reusable asset is a direct link between a measured boundary fluctuation
kernel and a physically actuated boundary response, if its thermal and observer
conditions are earned. Its value may be the avoidance of additional probing
damage, or predicting many response directions from one record. That is an
existing FDT advantage, not automatically a new programmable-material principle.

A stronger surviving invention must show at least one of: an actual response
target reachable with its PSD controls; a physical reverse/relaxation mechanism
that removes the demonstrated cone obstruction; a sensor/control operation that
earns superior total resources under the locality bound; or a new capability
enabled by the composition beyond obtaining K. The finite positive active
construction should be included in that comparison.
