INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE FINITE RNA ASSEMBLY-GATE MODEL
1 November 2026

DECISION

No substantive balance or conservation bug was found in the root model's base
reaction network. The model provides a conditional finite-time kinetic example,
not evidence that a chemically realizable oligo has the required interactions.
Its positive result requires selective suppression of a wrong path, preserved
local folding, fast access/capture, native-state exclusion, and usable release.

Two findings strengthen and narrow the interpretation:

1. Broad release survives. A global exponentially distributed pulse time gives
expected native-time 8.25486 versus unassisted 4.85770. In the exact finite
mass-action model, early antidote with slow finite release also helps; release
need not be concentrated at an accurately observed folding-completion time.

2. A stronger static comparator also helps. Retuning guide binding affinity,
consistently with detailed balance, gives native-time 6.63826 with the same
guide dose 1.2 and no antidote. Thus temporary selective occupancy is the useful
mechanism. Timed antidote adds a conditional gain; it is not necessary for every
qualitative improvement in this illustrative model.

No root file was changed. All new calculations are in assembly_gate_audit.py
and assembly_gate_audit_results.json. The JSON records the root source hash.

1. DETAILED BALANCE AND CONSERVATION

For every RNA-only thermal edge i<->j, the root sets forward rate k and reverse
rate k exp(E_j-E_i). Thus q_ij/q_ji=exp[-(E_j-E_i)], as required by the stated
dimensionless state energies. Slowing an edge by the selective factor slows both
directions and does not add affinity.

The three bound states have E_RNAG=E_RNA-8. With standard free energies of free
G and A set to zero and of W set to -affinity, the chemical steps are

 RNA + G <-> RNAG:       Delta G = -8,
 RNAG + A <-> RNA + W:  Delta G = 8-affinity,
 G + A <-> W:           Delta G = -affinity.

Their equilibrium constants multiply consistently: exp(8)*exp(affinity-8)
=exp(affinity). Because all three bound RNA energies shift by the same amount,
cycles involving bound/unbound conformational edges also close. The native
state has no guide-binding edge; omitting an edge does not violate detailed
balance, though its physical inaccessibility is a substantive assumption.

The conserved physical quantities between antidote additions are

 RNA = U+C+M+N+UG+CG+MG,
 guide = UG+CG+MG+G+W,
 antidote = A+W.

SERVICE and RESETS are accumulated counters, not chemical species. An antidote
pulse changes the third total by the declared amount. The reset stress test
conserves RNA but is a separately supplied nonthermal transition and is excluded
from the detailed-balance statement.

For a positive ideal-mixture state x, define

 F(x)=sum_i x_i[log x_i-1+E_i],

including G,A,W with their chemical energies and excluding counters. Every
reversible reaction contributes -(v_forward-v_reverse)log(v_forward/v_reverse)
to dF/dt, which is nonpositive. The complete base network therefore has a
consistent closed-system thermodynamic interpretation between pulses.

Numerical audits over 100 random positive states found a largest conservation
residual 1.14e-13 and no positive free-energy derivative. An explicit family of
equilibrium concentrations, parameterized by RNA, G and A activities, made all
physical derivatives smaller than 6.7e-24. These checks supplement the algebra;
they are not empirical validation of the rates.

2. WHAT THE POSITIVE RESULT IS ACTUALLY GIVEN

The main timed protocol begins with one unit of RNA in U and 1.2 units of free
guide. Its initial capture hazard is 50*1.2=60, versus U->C rate1 and U->M rate3.
The single-initial-race capture probability is 60/(60+1+3)=.9375. Finite guide
depletion and later returns are handled by the ODE, but capture is still chosen
to be very fast relative to the competing processes.

While bound, local useful folding UG->CG remains at rate1. Wrong docking
UG->MG is reduced from3 to.006, a factor500. The associated reverse edge also
slows. The CG->MG leak remains.02. Thus the gate is not merely present; its
desired kinetic discrimination has already been supplied in the rate table.

Native RNA is inaccessible to the guide, so newly formed N cannot be rebound
and inhibited. This can be a useful physical design condition, but needs to be
established for the proposed sequence and assembly state.

At the default antidote pulse, A is added at2.4. The stated release coefficient50
gives a displacement hazard initially of order120 and subsequently at least60,
because A+W=2.4 while W<=1.2. Release is finite, but it is also much faster than
the unit folding rate. Its speed and access to a protein-coated intermediate
are not earned by the existence of purified RNA splint displacement.

The equal guide-binding free energy for U,C,M, selective slowing of only the
wrong transition, and preserved folding collectively specify a structural
hypothesis. The model does not construct bases, proteins or a steric geometry
that satisfy it. In particular, a site bound by an antisense guide may also be
needed for the supposedly preserved local folding; that compatibility needs a
molecular argument or measurement.

The pulse time is fixed and no internal-state feedback occurs during a run.
However, optimize_pulse obtains its timing using the full model and exact native
occupancy. A physical calibration protocol would require finite material,
assays, replicate time and parameter uncertainty. No guide preparation cost,
antidote synthesis cost, clock/mixing error or complete protein/rRNA preparation
is priced by this kinetic calculation.

The synchronized initial U population is shared by the displayed comparisons.
It is nevertheless a preparation condition. A continuous population of different
RNA ages would require a distributional or spatial-flow version of the protocol.

3. ONE MISLEADING FAILURE COMPARISON TO AVOID

The root's native_energy_shift=9 raises E_N from-12 to-3. This modifies the
unguided reaction network as well as the gated one. The matched unassisted case
has native-time2.13994 and final native fraction.16715. The corresponding timed
gate gives native-time4.33287 and final native fraction.30427.

Therefore it is incorrect to say this lesion erases the relative gate advantage
by comparing4.33287 against the STABLE unassisted4.85770. The lesion lowers
absolute service but still leaves a roughly2.02-fold native-time improvement in
its matched system. This is an interpretation/comparator issue, not a code bug.

4. STRONGER STATIC RESIDENCE-TIME COMPARATOR

The original static grid optimizes guide concentration while holding binding
energy at-8. Its unbinding rate50 exp(-8)=.01677 gives a mean isolated bound
lifetime about59.6, longer than the entire15-unit observation horizon. Such a
guide traps CG as well as protecting U.

Change only the common binding energy to-beta, shifting all bound-state energies
consistently and setting the dissociation rate to50 exp(-beta). All bound local
folding/selectivity and free-RNA rates are retained. RNA and finite guide are
conserved; no antidote, external pulse or irreversible helper is added.

At beta=2 and the SAME guide dose1.2:

 native-time = 6.61589,
 final native fraction = .58193,
 dissociation rate = 6.76676.

Optimizing beta at that fixed dose gives beta=1.79543:

 native-time = 6.63826,
 final native fraction = .57413,
 dissociation rate = 8.30284.

The illustrative timed gate still performs better (9.80764/.80708). But the
original statement 'best static guide does no better than nothing' applies only
to the fixed-affinity dosage grid. A weak, rapidly exchanging selective guide
already improves finite-time folding without timed antidote. It obtains many
short occupancy intervals, not one exact residence time.

This retuning is a conditional comparator. Changing an actual oligo's affinity
may also alter its on-rate, accessibility and kinetic selectivity. Independent
tunability is NOT demonstrated here. The same caution applies to choosing
release and selective-folding rates independently in the timed construction.

5. EXPONENTIAL RELEASE: THREE DISTINCT QUESTIONS

A. A random global antidote-pulse time

Let F(t) be native-time in the root's exact finite ODE when a single antidote
pulse occurs at t, and F_static the value with no pulse. If the pulse time has
exponential rate lambda, then over horizon H

 E[F] = integral_0^H lambda exp(-lambda t) F(t) dt
          +exp(-lambda H) F_static.

Numerical integration of the actual root model gives an optimum around

 lambda=.55040, mean pulse time1.81686,
 expected native-time8.25486.

This exceeds both unassisted4.85770 and the retuned static comparator6.63826,
while remaining below precisely timed9.80764. Qualitative benefit therefore
survives a broad global timing distribution. This is timer-jitter robustness;
it is not independent per-molecule release and does not provide an autonomous
chemical clock or the apparatus for adding antidote.

B. A per-molecule exponential observation/removal clock

For the reversible three-state bound RNA chain with generator Q_b and initial
UG, a state-independent exponential clock gives the exact release-state law

 p_release = e_UG^T lambda(lambda I-Q_b)^(-1).

Here the bound transitions include all reverse rates from the root's energies.
At lambda=.1 the release-state probabilities (UG,CG,MG) are approximately
(.12344,.73322,.14335); at lambda=.5 they are
(.35247,.61897,.02856). Thus exponential timing does not intrinsically prevent
release enriched in the useful locally ready state.

This resolvent identity assumes already captured RNA and a one-shot removal
clock. It does NOT earn their physical preparation or a non-rebinding removal
mechanism. An ordinary dissociated guide can rebind; declaring an exponential
off-clock to be permanent disappearance would silently add a helper.

C. Finite-antidote, broad individual release in the UNMODIFIED root model

The strongest useful check avoids a supplied permanent-removal clock by using
the actual finite G,A,W reactions. Optimize the finite second-order release
coefficient while putting antidote in at a fixed early time:

 antidote at t=0, release=.400712:
   native-time5.72857, final N=.50180;

 antidote at t=.1, release=.358616:
   native-time8.08665, final N=.69087.

The t=0 experiment genuinely includes competitive sequestration of free guide
before RNA capture, so it does not assume free prebinding. It gives a modest
18 percent improvement over unguided assembly, although it is weaker than the
retuned static guide. The t=.1 protocol leaves a finite early capture interval
and gives a larger improvement with no folding-completion pulse.

After antidote addition, conservation gives A in[1.2,2.4]. The first-exit hazard
of a bound guide, from unbinding plus displacement, is therefore bounded by

 50 exp(-8)+1.2*release <= hazard(t)
                         <=50 exp(-8)+2.4*release.

For t=0/release=.400712 these bounds are .49763 and.97848. Conditional survival
from a given binding event is bounded between the corresponding exponentials.
The actual hazard changes with finite antidote depletion and is not exactly
constant-rate exponential. Reverse duplex exchange remains finite in the ODE.

This is the strongest finite-model answer to the timing question: broad,
stochastic removal can retain the benefit, while finite capture, rebinding,
antidote depletion and competing free-guide sequestration remain explicit.
It still assumes the relevant microscopic release coefficient can be realized
at a protein-coated assembly intermediate.

6. WHAT THE SERVICE COMPARISON DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

The declared objective is integral N(t)dt over a fixed horizon. That is a useful
finite-time occupancy objective. It is not measured ribosome activity, protein
production or useful material throughput. N is a model state defined as native;
functional validation and translation substrates/fuel are outside the model.

The unguided network is reversible and eventually escapes M. The gate primarily
changes the finite-time path distribution; it does not prove a changed ultimate
native yield in the long-time free-RNA equilibrium. Finite deadlines can be
physically meaningful, but their source should be stated.

The reset comparator explicitly supplies M->U at rate1. It has selective access
to M and an irreversible nonthermal transition. Counting its transitions does
not establish its actual catalytic construction, chemical affinity, readout or
total energy. It is an ideal service comparator, as labeled, not evidence of
a complete thermodynamic superiority of the gate.

The correct surviving conclusion is conditional and useful: temporary selective
occupancy can redirect a reversible folding network before wrong docking,
and staged antidote can further improve a specified finite-time objective.
Exact release timing is not the core principle. The key unearned construction
is a real gate that retains useful local folding, suppresses the undesired
interaction, captures the relevant RNA state, releases through protein coating,
and leaves functional native machinery available with fewer total services.
