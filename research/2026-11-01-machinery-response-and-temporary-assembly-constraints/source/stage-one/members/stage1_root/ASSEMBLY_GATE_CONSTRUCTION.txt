REMOVABLE CONSTRAINTS FOR ASSEMBLING RNA MACHINES
Stage-one construction, 1 November 2026

STATUS

This is a proposed physical construction, an ordinary kinetic analysis, and
executable finite-resource mechanism tests. No new ribosome has been made.
No molecular release rate, assembly yield, or historical priority is claimed.
The purpose is to decide whether a specific missing physical operation merits
the sustained second stage. The abstract kinetic model is not evidence that
a particular oligonucleotide realizes the assumed rates.

1. CAPABILITY AND THE DISCOVERY THAT CHANGED THE QUESTION

The consequential capability is manufacture of complex, functional RNA-protein
machines from sequence-defined inputs and a fully enumerated set of services.
A renewable protein factory is the demanding application: active output must
replace its translation and assembly machinery while leaving useful production
capacity. An extract that supplies an unknown assembly proteome is an input to
be replaced, not a solved part of the factory.

The starting operations were found independently of this destination:

* Sheng et al. (2025), DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-61640-1, physically capture
  ribosome assembly intermediates with antisense oligonucleotides. Their data
  include both inhibiting probes and a facilitative DNA-RA15 result. The actual
  iSAT system supplies S150 extract, native-derived ribosomal proteins, RNA
  modification activities and assembly factors. Their antidote rescue is
  PREHYBRIDIZATION of probe and antidote, not release of an already bound probe.
* Smart et al. (2024), DOI 10.1093/nar/gkae143, use a DNA splint with exposed
  tails on purified ligated 16S rRNA, then remove the bound splint using a fully
  complementary competing DNA at 46 C under conditions intended to preserve
  RNA higher-order structure. This earns a related postbinding release operation
  on RNA, not its transfer to a protein-coated assembly intermediate.
* Huang and Karbstein (2021), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2101164118, identify a natural
  mechanism in yeast small-subunit assembly: block a junction's tertiary
  interaction partner while the junction can refold, rather than necessarily
  binding the misfolding junction itself. It motivates a geometric gate target;
  it does not establish a bacterial synthetic gate.

The candidate is therefore NOT generic antisense-assisted folding, which
already exists. It is controlled POSTBINDING removal of a deliberately delaying
constraint, so that a useful folding intermediate can progress using fewer
supplied assembly services. The substantial desired result is replacement of
an otherwise required assembly service at preserved active yield and fidelity,
not a modest increase in an extract-based reporter signal.

2. A CONCRETE PHYSICAL CYCLE

Let X denote a local RNA element that must first fold and P a partner whose
premature docking can trap a wrong X conformation. A gate strand G binds P;
an exposed terminal extension is reserved for the removal strand A.

  nascent RNA + G <-> gated RNA
  gated RNA -> locally prepared gated RNA
  prepared RNA:G + A <-> released prepared RNA + G:A
  released prepared RNA -> active RNA-protein assembly

All binding and release rates are finite. The reaction G+A <-> G:A also
quenches free guide so that it does not simply rebind. The finite guide and
antidote inventory is counted. No camera, per-molecule state classifier, reset,
or instantaneous intervention is assumed. A timed batch pulse is an available
macroscopic operation, but requires a finite preparation-time distribution.

The proposed first hardware is an ordinary synthetic DNA or RNA guide with an
exposed displacement extension and a full complementary antagonist. The strong
PNA/LNA traps in Sheng et al. do not automatically provide that release
interface. Changing backbone or appending an extension can change capture,
kinetic selectivity, and accessibility. These are hypotheses to measure.

Actual named starting sites are kept separate by role:

* RA20 targets H1. Its structural intermediates and affinity handle make it a
  useful test of postbinding accessibility and release, not a proved beneficial
  assembly gate. One class has already misdocked domains III/VI, so too long a
  hold can be harmful rather than productive.
* DNA-RA15, at a domain-II junction, is a positive chaperoning comparator.
  The published effect occurs with extract services present. Timed release must
  beat the best unmodified static-guide version and ordinary chaperone addition.
* RB0811, near the peptidyl-transfer centre, should not be chosen merely because
  it enriches late-looking structural classes. A mature-looking class is not a
  calibrated count of translationally competent particles.

Exact sequences were not recovered through the available approved content
routes; no sequence has been guessed. The supplementary-data endpoint, source
names and target sites are retained in ASO_GATE_INPUTS.json.

3. WHAT THE STRUCTURAL DATA REALLY SUPPLY

The current PNA_50S release is pinned to
e31ee0410bd2fde24fbb1a9b022d883c1796123c, Zenodo version 15421293.
The inspected mature contact matrix has 158 nodes and 622 off-diagonal edges.
H1 has deposited contact counts with H2 (12), H94 (22), H98 (30), H99 (10),
and uL13 (35). These locate a physically nontrivial docking constraint.

They do NOT supply a transition-rate matrix. The upstream dependency analysis
uses external density/occupancy inputs absent from the repository; the two
misdocked RA20-B-c classes are explicitly dropped from that particular graph.
Thus the graph must not be used as a successful-assembly controller or as proof
that its arrows are productive kinetic transitions. Identical structural
occupancy order is compatible with arbitrarily different passage times.

The mature contact graph also suggests a concrete change in where to place a
new gate. H1 directly contacts early/core elements and uL13, so blocking H1 is
not an obviously selective way to preserve useful local folding. Its partner
H98 has only four listed neighbors: H1 (30), H25 (2), H94 (20) and H99 (62),
and no direct protein neighbor at the deposited contact threshold. A candidate
therefore targets the H98-facing docking interface instead of simply making a
stronger RA20 blocker. This applies the partner-isolation principle to a
specific, independently inspected structural neighborhood.

That is a hypothesis, not a designed binding sequence: the contact counts do
not establish an accessible single-stranded target, distinguish a loop from a
buried helix surface, or show that H98-H94/H99 local folding survives the guide.
An oligonucleotide that disrupts that local fold could be worse than RA20.
Nucleotide-level access and this collateral-folding effect are the first
construction obligations before choosing a guide sequence.

4. EXACT SELECTIVITY TEST BEFORE AN ELABORATE MODEL

Consider an unresolved precursor U. Without a guide, it reaches a useful local
fold C at rate a and a long-lived wrong docking M at rate b. Assume here only
that these are competing exponential exits and that C can later complete.
The probability of choosing C is

  p0 = a/(a+b).

If a guide merely multiplies both rates by the same nonnegative s(t), then the
conditional exit identity is unchanged: its successful fraction is still p0.
Proof: the two cause-specific exit densities are a*s(t)*Survival(t) and
b*s(t)*Survival(t), so their integrated ratio is a:b. Indefinite suppression
can only leave additional material uncompleted. Merely holding RNA is not a
new assembly mechanism.

Now suppose a selective gate keeps useful folding at a_g>0 while wrong docking
occurs at b_g. Hold for time tau and then release. In the simplifying limit of
instantaneous release and stable C, the success probability after eventual
completion is

  p(tau) = p_g + (p0-p_g)*exp[-(a_g+b_g)*tau],
  p_g = a_g/(a_g+b_g).

For perfect wrong-path blocking b_g=0 and a_g=a this becomes

  p(tau) = 1 - [b/(a+b)] exp(-a*tau).

This elementary formula identifies the missing physical construction: a guide
must change the branching ratio, not only delay the entire process. It does
not provide capture, removal, final-state stability, or finite throughput.

If release after the pulse has exponential waiting time of rate rho, while the
gated exit dynamics remain the same, the eventual formula becomes

  p = p_g + (p0-p_g) exp[-(a_g+b_g)tau]
                     *rho/[rho+a_g+b_g].

Interpreting this as an engineering merit would be a mistake: as rho tends to
zero eventual success can look excellent while useful output over any finite
horizon tends to disappear. All selection calculations therefore count active
service over a finite horizon, not eventual absorption alone.

5. FINITE THERMODYNAMIC CONSTRUCTION

assembly_gate.py contains an explicitly unfitted mass-action model. Its seven
RNA states are U (unprepared), C (locally ready), M (misdocked), N (native), and
the three guide-bound versions UG, CG, MG. It also tracks free G, antidote A,
spent duplex W, integrated native occupancy, and explicitly supplied reset
events for an optimistic chaperone comparator.

In normalized concentration/time units the state energies in kBT are

  E(U,C,M,N,UG,CG,MG) = (0,-3,-4,-12,-8,-11,-12).

These numbers demonstrate a possible kinetic architecture, not ribosome
energetics. Every thermal edge i<->j has k_reverse=k_forward*exp(Ej-Ei).
All free/bound guide pairs have association equilibrium constant exp(8).
Guide-antidote duplex formation has equilibrium constant exp(22), and direct
displacement has equilibrium constant exp(14). The chemical cycle closes:

  exp(8)*exp(14) = exp(22).

Consequently guide binding, release and sequestration cannot quietly contain
an inconsistent energy source. The external antidote pulse is the stated
nonequilibrium resource. RNA, guide and antidote moieties are conserved between
pulses. Duplex stability is finite, reverse reactions are present, and native
RNA can unfold. No irreversible perfect lock is supplied.

The favorable example still supplies four UNMEASURED kinetic properties:

1. Guide access/capture outruns wrong folding. The initial binding hazard is
   60 in the example, versus total unbound precursor exit rate 4.
2. The guide suppresses the wrong U->M channel by a factor 0.002 while allowing
   U->C at rate 1. Thermodynamic consistency does not establish this selectivity.
3. A postbinding displacement reaction remains accessible with rate coefficient
   50 and a finite antidote stock.
4. The mature native state excludes the guide. The observed assembly-biased
   ASO effects motivate checking this; they do not give an exact universal rule.

The default calculation begins with one RNA unit, 1.2 guide units, and no
antidote. It adds 2.4 antidote units at the selected time. Each run follows one
ordinary batch history; no reset or internal-state feedback is used.

For the illustrative rates and a horizon of 15, optimized timed release gives
about 0.807 native fraction and 9.808 integrated native-time units, compared
with 0.431 and 4.858 without guide. A grid search over static-guide concentration
finds its best value at zero guide in this particular model. The timed protocol
consumes about 1.2 guide-antidote duplex units. These are not experimental yields,
and the numeric gain is not the reason to select the research question.

This static comparison is not the strongest possible guide design. An
independent constructor changed the binding free energy consistently from
-8 to -2 kBT, while retaining the same guide dose and assumed bound-state
selectivity. A static weak guide then gives native-time about 6.616 and native
fraction 0.582 with no antidote. It is therefore false that an actively timed
release is the only way to obtain a qualitative improvement. Affinity cannot
be assumed independently tunable from capture/selectivity in a real molecule,
but this is a valid stronger conditional comparator and changes the preferred
mechanism: first seek selective TEMPORARY occupancy; use an antidote only when
its extra controllability earns its additional material and process cost.

The discriminating tests are the failures:

* Prehybridized guide-antidote behaves like no guide. It cannot validate release
  of an existing RNA-bound gate.
* Nonspecific slowing of both folding routes gives no useful benefit.
* Slow removal makes the gate worse than no guide within the same horizon.
* Raising the native-state free energy reduces absolute service even after
  a good assembly path. All reverse rates change consistently with that energy.
  The matched unstable unassisted comparator has native-time about 2.140;
  the gated version has about 4.333. The relative gate benefit remains: this
  example must not be described as eliminating that relative benefit.
* A supplied perfectly selective resetting service outperforms the gate in the
  example. Its event count is reported, but its specificity and fuel cost are
  not earned by the calculation. A real RNA helicase or assembly factor is a
  serious comparator, not an implementation detail that can be omitted.

An independent audit also found that a broad finite release distribution can
retain a benefit: an early antidote pulse at time 0.1, with a slower finite
displacement coefficient near 0.36, gives native-time about 8.087. The timed
example therefore does not depend on a per-molecule folding-completion oracle
or require deterministic molecular release. This still presupposes capture,
selectivity and an accessible displacement pathway; it does not earn those
three properties from the structural snapshots.

No signal amplitude, transcript delay, or gate dose from these normalized runs
should be transferred directly into a laboratory protocol.

6. THE PERMANENT-CHEMISTRY OBSTRUCTION

After all gate interactions are removed, suppose the RNA assembly obeys an
ergodic thermal generator L with unique equilibrium pi. For any gate protocol
ending at tau, the later state law is p(tau) exp[(t-tau)L], which converges to pi.
A temporary guide can choose an initial population and avoid a kinetic trap;
it cannot change the final generator or equilibrium indefinitely.

This matters physically. Larsson et al. (2026), DOI 10.1093/nar/gkag800, observe
alternative conformations and reduced stability of hypomodified 23S RNA in
assembled ribosomes. Temporary assembly guides therefore cannot be claimed
to replace every modification enzyme. Those ribosomes were made in cells;
their existence does not establish defined assembly from unmodified transcripts.

Likewise, Green and Noller (1996), PMID 8849777, localized an important native
RNA segment by complementation of peptidyl-transferase activity. The roughly
80-nt region is not proof that a synthetic modified patch supports all steps
of full protein translation. The patch route is preserved as an alternative,
not silently installed as a solved renewal service.

7. FROM ASSEMBLY TO RENEWABLE MANUFACTURE

Let R be active ribosome concentration, delta its net loss rate after repair,
and J_R the newly assembled, functionally active ribosome flux. Maintenance
requires J_R >= delta R. Let vR be available residue-production capacity and
let component i require L_i residues per molecule, functional yield eta_i and
replacement flux ell_i. Then

  sum_i L_i*ell_i/eta_i + L_product*J_product/eta_product <= vR

is a necessary capacity inequality. Guide and antidote synthesis, RNA
transcription, chemical modifications, purification loss, ionic cycling and
energy regeneration add further resource inequalities, not free coefficients.

Successful repair of a finite native RNA scaffold reduces delta but does not
make J_R positive. With an irreparable residual loss delta0>0 and no scaffold
renewal, R(t)<=R(0)exp(-delta0*t) and total translated residues are bounded by
v*R(0)/delta0. This is why repairing old ribosomes or prolonging dialysis alone
does not fulfill the larger capability.

The existing June 2026 EngA/ObgE work, DOI 10.7554/eLife.109916.4, substantially
reduces the apparent assembly-helper burden, but uses native rRNA and TP70.
Its authors detect additional assembly factors in TP70 and explicitly do not
establish two-factor necessity/sufficiency. It is the strongest constructive
baseline to compare with, not a closed two-enzyme manufacturing service.

8. THE DECISIVE NEXT CONSTRUCTION

The stage-two question is:

Can a finite set of removable, sequence-specific constraints replace at least
one otherwise necessary assembly service in making a full-translation-active
RNA-protein machine from enumerated inputs, while preserving active yield,
fidelity and usable lifetime after the constraints are gone?

The first necessary result is postbinding release and functional recovery of
an already trapped assembly intermediate. The discriminating comparisons are
no guide, best static guide, prehybridized guide-antidote, guide followed by
antidote after actual binding, noncomplementary release strand, and the best
available defined chaperone/ionic/temperature route. Mature-ribosome controls
separate assembly effects from direct translation inhibition. A full protein
activity readout and measurement of newly made subunit material are needed;
GFP fluorescence or peptidyl transfer alone does not certify complete renewal.

The protected intermediate must actually become functionally competent after
release. If release fails, the model must not count it. If release succeeds
only in S150, the result establishes control over an extract-assisted pathway,
not replacement of the extract. If a known chaperone achieves the same result
with lower complete burden, use it and move the invention target to the next
unearned assembly operation.

A later cis-encoded guide and downstream antagonist could avoid external
per-molecule timing, but transcription order, folding delay, release kinetics,
and exact RNA-end processing are separate physical operations. This extension
is not part of the earned construction, nor counted as free autonomy.

9. WHY THIS MERITS CONCENTRATION, WITH A LIMIT

The wave/electrical branch produced a general response identity, a physical
scattering test, a useful sign correction, and strong observer/control limits.
But ordinary active control already earns its proposed local function; there
is no demonstrated total advantage. In contrast, the RNA branch now identifies
a specific missing physical operation at a consequential manufacturing
bottleneck and supplies related experimentally working components, rich
structural data, exact competing routes, and a falsifiable finite construction.

That is a stage-one selection reason. It is not a claim that a defined renewable
ribosome factory, general programmable matter, or a civilisation-changing
discovery has already been achieved.
