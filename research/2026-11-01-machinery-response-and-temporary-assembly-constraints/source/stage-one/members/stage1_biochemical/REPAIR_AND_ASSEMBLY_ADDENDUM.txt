REPAIR, ASSEMBLY AND THE SURVIVING OPENING
2026-11-01. This addendum supplements, and does not overwrite, the original
retained-catalyst construction, stoichiometric audit and useful failures.

1. Correction to premature retirement

An rRNA scaffold need not be completely rebuilt whenever a ribosomal protein
fails. Pulk et al. (2010), DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2958.2009.07002.x, demonstrated
replacement-based rescue of chemically damaged ribosomes. A fourfold excess of
LiCl-extracted split proteins restored poly(U) translation from 35% to 88.5%
of unmodified controls. The experiment deliberately damaged proteins, preserving
RNA. It establishes repair of that damage class, not repair of arbitrary aging,
complete full-length-protein production, or all ribosomal proteins. Exchange
uses pre-existing ribosomal structure, and replacement inventory and separation
are real costs. This is a meaningful maintenance route even when rRNA remains
finite. The experiment must not be represented as de novo chemically closed
ribosome renewal.
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1365-2958.2009.07002.x

Specific RNA break repair is also real. RtcB2-PrfH repair concerns a particular
damaged 16S geometry; it does not establish generic replacement of oxidized bases
or an indefinite RNA lifetime. An experimental patching or repair operation is
valuable without supporting the more general natural-life-cycle story sometimes
attached to it.

2. A costed repair bound without an ideal damage observer

Consider one retained ribosome, initially active. State A is active, D has a
repairable protein defect, I has irreparable RNA damage. Protein failure occurs
at rate lambda; irreversible RNA failure at rate delta from either A or D.
Treatments arrive at rate u for EVERY retained particle, including A and I.
Each treatment retains the particle with probability rho and repairs D, if
retained, with probability s. Let mu = rho*s*u and q = (1-rho)*u. No newly made
RNA scaffold enters. This is a model, not an experimentally measured reactor.

The equations for A and D are
 A' = -(lambda+delta+q) A + mu D,
 D' = lambda A -(mu+delta+q) D.
The irreparable retained pool satisfies I' = delta(A+D)-qI.
Thus A+D+I = exp(-q*t). Setting k=lambda+mu gives
 A(t) = exp(-(delta+q)t) [mu/k + (lambda/k) exp(-kt)].
For k=0 the continuous limit is understood.

Define phi(a,T)=(1-exp(-a*T))/a, with phi(0,T)=T. Cumulative active time is
 F(T) = (mu/k) phi(delta+q,T)
      + (lambda/k) phi(delta+q+k,T).
If a treatment costs c residue-equivalent units and active synthesis rate is v,
net residue-equivalent output by T is
 Q(T) = v F(T) - c*u*phi(q,T).
The treatment cost includes irreparable, unrecognized particles. It therefore
does not silently grant selective recycling, a damage detector or a perfect
shutdown rule. Batch diagnostics could reduce this waste, but their information
and separation costs would have to be added explicitly.

For q=0 and any positive delta,
 F(infinity)=(mu+delta)/[delta(lambda+mu+delta)] <= 1/delta.
With no repair F0(infinity)=1/(lambda+delta). Even perfect fast repair improves
active lifetime by at most 1+lambda/delta within this model. This can be a large
gain when protein failure dominates; there is no measured lambda/delta here.
With perfect retention and indefinite blind treatment, repair costs diverge
while active output remains finite. Treating only live cores until their failure
would remove that divergence by adding an unearned observer. With retention
loss, every treatment consumes some scaffold lifetime and must earn it back.

This makes the useful experimental question specific: does a real protein
replacement schedule increase full-protein output per total supplied protein,
RNA and separation work over the best uninterrupted reactor? It does not make
indefinite life the only useful endpoint. The 12.5-day dialysis result has not
established ribosome aging as the dominant bottleneck; membrane fouling or other
losses may dominate. Source: DOI 10.1021/acssynbio.4c00618.

3. Passive exchange already has a strong conventional comparator

For one exchangeable site and equal binding affinities, adding m fresh protein
copies to one initially old occupied site gives new fraction m/(1+m) at complete
equilibrium. A single well-mixed dose needs m=p/(1-p) for replacement fraction p.
In k ideal wash/refill stages with the same m per stage, old fraction is
(1+m)^(-k). Total fresh cost is k[(1-p)^(-1/k)-1], tending to -log(1-p).
Ordinary continuous diafiltration therefore already improves finite-dose
efficiency. This calculation omits kinetic differences, RNA loss, wash cost and
nonexchangeable proteins, so it is a conventional lower bound, not an invention.
It prevents a repeated-repair protocol from claiming an advantage merely by
forgetting its strongest replenishment-and-purge competitor.

4. Strongest informed assembly comparator

Sato et al. (2026) reconstitute functional ribosomes under translation-compatible
conditions using EngA and ObgE. They assay full-length sfGFP and active DHFR,
and estimate approximately 60% reconstitution from fluorescence rates. These
are substantially stronger endpoints than peptidyl-transferase or poly(Phe)
activity. However, both RNA and TP70 originate from native ribosomes. Their
mass spectrometry detects other biogenesis factors, including RsfS, and the
authors explicitly deny that two-factor necessity and sufficiency are proved.
Use it as a serious comparator; do not call it a chemically closed service.
Source: https://elifesciences.org/articles/109916
DOI 10.7554/eLife.109916.4, version of record 18 June 2026.

Green and Noller (1996), PMID 8849777, identify a small modified native 23S
segment whose addition rescues peptidyl-transferase activity of reconstituted
particles. This motivates a chemical patch but does not establish full-protein
translation from synthetic bulk RNA plus one patch. Later work on unmodified
Thermus RNA and synthetic atomic substitutions also needs its actual endpoint
kept distinct. The 2026 modification study shows alternative PTC structures and
catalytic effects: transient folding guidance cannot simply replace chemical
modifications that stabilize the functioning state after assembly.

5. Consequence for this stage

Repair is preserved as a realistic competitor and a finite-lifetime capability,
not selected as a new central invention. The stronger construction question is
whether an externally specified, finite-consumption nucleic-acid gate can keep
an identified docking partner unavailable during local folding, then actually
release from an RNA/protein intermediate and recover productive assembly. Its
advantage must survive the optimized conventional factor/ionic/temperature route
and the native modifications and proteins still required. The gate is a testable
physical missing operation, not a claim that a complete translation factory has
already been closed.
