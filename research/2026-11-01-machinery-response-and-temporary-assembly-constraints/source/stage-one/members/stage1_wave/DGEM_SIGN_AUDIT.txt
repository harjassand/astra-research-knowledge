Apparent sign inconsistency
--------------------------
DGEM SI Note2 says it uses the immittance convention of Ref.14 and defines i=Yu.
Ref.14 Eqs.5-6 and following text specify current positive into the region:

  u=sqrt(2)(s_plus+s_minus), i=sqrt(2)(s_plus-s_minus).

Its Eq.14 is

  F dot delta q = -j/(4 omega) (u* delta i + i* delta u).

At fixed u, insert Y=j omega C for a lossless capacitor with positive input
capacitance C. The result is +u* delta C u/4, the ordinary fixed-voltage force.
Equivalently, for B=omega C it is +u* delta B u/(4 omega).

DGEM SI Eqs.S20 and S43 instead use the negative of that expression. The main
Eq.2 and SI Eq.S48 carry that sign into the conductance-force relation. An outward
current convention would reverse the sign, but it was not explicitly adopted in
the inspected text; rather, the cited convention is inward. The safest reuse is
to state the physical terminal convention, rederive its coefficient, and verify
the sign on a simple known force. Do not reuse the printed sign unexamined.

Independent conduction derivation
--------------------------------
Define G_in by input currents i_in=G_in u, so the averaged dissipated power is

  P_heat = (1/2) u* G_in u >= 0

for peak complex phasor voltages. In a real, isotropic conduction problem with
all relevant boundary voltages fixed, define auxiliary permittivity sigma/omega
and zero auxiliary conductivity. The auxiliary capacitance is C_aux=G_in/omega.
Fixed-voltage virtual work therefore gives

  F_aux dot delta q = (1/4) u* delta C_aux u.

For an inclusion enclosed by a surface in a homogeneous exterior fluid, physical
and auxiliary electric fields are identical and the Maxwell stresses scale by
epsilon_m/(sigma_m/omega). Consequently,

  F_phys dot delta q = +epsilon_m/(4 sigma_m) u* delta G_in u.

The plus sign assumes currents into the region and peak phasors. RMS voltages
change the factor from 1/4 to 1/2. An outward-current G_out=-G_in reverses it.
An empirical circuit measurement must state these conventions. The exact
low-frequency limit also requires omega epsilon << sigma in every phase, fixed
material parameters, and no extra electrokinetic body force or unmodeled moving
boundary acting through the measured state.

Completeness and finite-frequency qualifications
------------------------------------------------
The preprint's measurement matrix often sets diagonal entries to zero because
their noise is high. That surrogate is not the full passive admittance. If all
terminals are accounted for and there is no unmeasured leakage path, Kirchhoff
conservation and common-mode invariance give G_in 1=0, so each diagonal is the
negative sum of its off-diagonal row. This reconstructs complete G but creates
correlated noise. Grounded boundary electrodes remain part of the circuit.

The reported proxy parameters at f=100kHz are epsilon_rm=78, sigma_m=.03 S/m,
epsilon_rp=3.7, sigma_p=1e-4 S/m. Taking epsilon_0=8.8541878128e-12 F/m gives
omega epsilon_m/sigma_m=.01446 and omega epsilon_p/sigma_p=.20584. Thus the
particle is not deeply in the asymptotic conduction limit. The reported numerical
agreement should be treated as finite-frequency evidence in that example.

No full-matrix thermal-covariance calibration without coordinate guide-stars was
found in the inspected DGEM main text or supplement. This is a scoped search
result, not a proof of novelty. The experiment uses optical position tracking.

Independent sphere stress calculation and executable check
---------------------------------------------------------
Run: python producer-workspace/small_inclusion_sign.py
Recorded output: small_inclusion_sign_results.json.

Place a sphere of radius a and conductivity sigma_p in homogeneous sigma_m.
Take incident harmonic potential

  phi_inc = -E z - (g/2)(z^2-x^2).

The exact exterior induced potential contains l=1 and l=2 components:

  K1 = (sigma_p-sigma_m)/(sigma_p+2 sigma_m),
  K2 = 2(sigma_p-sigma_m)/(2 sigma_p+3 sigma_m),
  phi_sc = K1 a^3 E z/r^3 + (K2 a^5 g/2)(z^2-x^2)/r^5.

Numerically integrate the physical exterior Maxwell traction

  epsilon_m/4 [2 E_field (E_field dot n) - |E_field|^2 n]

on enclosing spheres of radii 1.1a, 1.7a and 3a, using 80 Gauss-Legendre nodes
and 160 azimuthal angles. The force is

  F_z = 2 pi epsilon_m a^3 K1 E g.

The leading positive-input Dirichlet-to-Neumann perturbation is

  u^T Delta G_in u = 4 pi sigma_m a^3 K1 |E_background(q)|^2
                    + position-independent quadrupole contribution.

Its derivative with respect to z is 8 pi sigma_m a^3 K1 E g, giving exactly
the displayed force with +epsilon_m/(4 sigma_m). The printed minus predicts
the opposite direction. This is an independent continuum stress calculation,
not a test that simply reuses the asserted conductance-force law as its force.

For sigma_m=1, epsilon_m=2, a=.1, E=1, g=.3:
  sigma_p=.1: F_z=-.0016156762218461794;
  sigma_p=10: F_z=+.002827433388230814.
Maximum relative difference between stress integration and the positive-input
identity over both cases and all three integration surfaces is 5.74e-13.

This calculation is the isolated sphere in a prescribed polynomial incident
field. Port perturbations refer to the dilute-inclusion limit of a large fixed
Dirichlet domain; it is not a complete finite-electrode-device simulation.

Additional bookkeeping qualification
------------------------------------
The reported 16 by 16 measured active block has four other electrodes held at
ground. Row-sum reconstruction of diagonals applies to the complete 20-terminal
matrix, with currents/couplings to those grounded ports included, not to the
active subblock alone. The positive-input force identity equally applies to
the fixed-ground active block when all other applied boundary voltages are zero.
