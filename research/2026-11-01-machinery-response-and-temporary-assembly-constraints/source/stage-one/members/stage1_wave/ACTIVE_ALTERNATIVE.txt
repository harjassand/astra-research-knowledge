CONVENTIONAL TERMINAL-ONLY RESPONSE PROGRAMMING: CONSTRUCTION AND LIMITS
1 November 2026. Follow-up to the wave-machinery investigation.

Question
--------
Can moving inclusions be taught a desired complete terminal conductance G*,
using measured terminal conductance and realizable electrode voltages, without
images, particle coordinates, a material model, or supplied mobility?

Result
------
A conventional active system-identification controller already supplies local
descent of an arbitrary differentiable electrical objective whenever an improving
realizable voltage exists and the response stays sufficiently stationary during
identification. It need not recover coordinates or the full mobility-weighted
response map. An ideal dilute-inclusion executable confirms this construction.
This is not a proof of arbitrary response programmability, global convergence,
or a finite-noise experimental implementation. Arbitrary complete responses are
excluded for many inclusion systems by their response-manifold dimension alone.

1. Task-specific active construction
-----------------------------------
Let q denote hidden material coordinates; let G(q) be the observed symmetric
positive-input terminal conductance, in n independent voltage coordinates.
Suppose the local mean mechanical drift is quadratic in the applied voltage:

  dq/dt = b(q) + B_q(uu^T),

where B_q is an unknown linear map from symmetric voltage covariances to
configuration velocities. Fixed-voltage dielectrophoresis in the conduction
limit has this form. Allow also voltage-independent thermal diffusion. Let

  L(G) = (1/2)||G-G*||_F^2.

Its conditional infinitesimal mean rate has the form

  E[dL/dt | q,u] = b_L(q) + u^T A_L(q)u,

for an unknown symmetric n by n matrix A_L. All baseline drift and the Ito
correction are in b_L. This follows simply by composing the observable L with
the quadratic drift; it does not require identifying B, q, mobility, or force.

Subtract a zero-drive baseline measured at a comparable local state. With e_i
the voltage-mode basis, write ell(u) for the resulting conditional rate. Then

  (A_L)_ii = ell(e_i),
  (A_L)_ij = ell((e_i+e_j)/sqrt(2))
             - (ell(e_i)+ell(e_j))/2.

Thus p=n(n+1)/2 noiseless local pulse directions identify A_L exactly. If the
available voltages satisfy ||u|| <= V and include signed/phase-controlled modes,
the minimum eigenvector gives the steepest immediate decrease and the minimum
rate is V^2 lambda_min(A_L). If lambda_min >= 0, zero drive minimizes the local
control contribution. For box, phase, individual-electrode power or heating
constraints, solve the actual constrained quadratic problem; the eigenvector
shortcut is not generally realizable. Baseline drift may itself still decrease
or increase L and must not be confused with the control contribution.

The same probes can identify the whole output velocity map, since each output
component is a quadratic form in u. That is useful for multiobjective control,
but costs more measurements and is unnecessary for a single scalar objective.
Classic simultaneous-perturbation or extremum-seeking methods can further trade
more iterations and variance for fewer directional queries. The p-probe scheme
is a transparent exact local construction, not a universal query lower bound.

2. Contract the electrical observation to the task
------------------------------------------------
At an anchor response G0, set W=G0-G*. For short local calibration pulses hold
W fixed and observe

  Phi_W(G) = tr(WG).

At the anchor its first derivative equals that of L. With
W=sum_k lambda_k v_k v_k^T, Phi_W is a signed DIGITAL combination of at most n
measurements v_k^T G v_k. Negative weights do not require negative dissipation.
Thus an ideal implementation can use one full G acquisition at each anchor,
followed by at most n scalar terminal-power measurements per local pulse, rather
than scanning the full matrix after every pulse. If only a restricted set of
voltage patterns can be measured, reconstruct Phi_W from their spanning family;
the n-pattern eigenbasis shortcut then need not be available.

Measurement voltages also act on the inclusions. They must be weak/brief enough,
or their known applied contribution included in the identification experiment.
Finite scan duration, sequentially changing G, instrument noise, electrode
polarization and readout power cannot be discarded. This construction earns no
noninvasive measurement oracle. The executable below deliberately idealizes
instantaneous noiseless readout so that its result isolates algorithmic access.

3. Finite-pulse and noise costs
------------------------------
Let y=L or the contracted Phi_W. A short-pulse estimator is

  ell_hat = [y(t+tau)-y(t)]/tau - baseline_hat.

If each independent endpoint has variance sigma_y^2 and the physical observable
has local increment variance 2 D_y tau, before baseline subtraction

  Var(ell_hat) approximately 2 sigma_y^2/tau^2 + 2 D_y/tau.

An independent baseline-rate estimate adds its variance. Averaging R comparable
trials divides variance by R; it does not eliminate calibration-state bias.
Finite-difference bias is O(tau) under smooth bounded dynamics. A full sequential
pulse sweep also changes the local coefficients: a Lipschitz bound L_A gives
coefficient bias <= L_A max_k ||q_k-q_anchor||, besides pulse bias. Brownian
excursions make repeated experiments at the same hidden state imperfect even
without applied drift. Shorter pulses decrease drift bias but amplify instrument
noise as tau^-2; stronger pulses improve rate signal but increase heating and
unwanted structural displacement. These are costs shared with any response
identification route, not resolved by describing it as model-free.

4. Independent obstruction: the reachable response manifold
---------------------------------------------------------
For a complete N-terminal reciprocal conduction response with no unobserved
leakage, G is symmetric and G 1=0. Its ambient dimension is

  p = N(N-1)/2.

For K rigid spheres translating in d spatial dimensions, the configuration
dimension is r=dK. Away from collisions or changes of topology the response
map q -> G is smooth. If r<p, its image has p-dimensional measure zero (locally
by the rank theorem; globally under the usual smooth/countable-domain conditions
by Sard's theorem). Therefore a generic target in the ambient response space
cannot be obtained by rearranging those spheres, independent of sensing,
feedback, or thermal calibration. For N=16 complete terminals, r>=120 demands
at least 40 freely translating spheres in 3D or 60 in 2D. This is only necessary:
correlations, symmetries, interactions and geometric confinement can reduce rank.

If instead n active electrodes are measured against additional fixed grounded
electrodes, the active block is an n by n symmetric matrix and has up to
n(n+1)/2 independent entries; row-sum zero does not apply to that subblock.
DGEM's reported 16 active electrodes plus four grounded electrodes belong to
this latter bookkeeping unless the full 20-terminal response is reconstructed.

In the dilute isotropic-inclusion approximation each perturbation is

  Delta G = alpha E(q)^T E(q),

of rank at most d, with alpha the signed polarization strength. For particles
more conducting than the homogeneous background, the exact Rayleigh monotonicity
also gives G(q)-G_empty positive semidefinite when material is pointwise more
conducting. This does not imply that differences between two rearrangements
are positive semidefinite. Accessible targets inherit additional geometry and
contrast restrictions beyond being abstractly passive conductance matrices.

For comparison, an internally programmable resistor graph exactly realizes any
admissible reciprocal passive terminal Laplacian G* with off-diagonals <=0:
put a resistor of conductance c_ij=-(G*)_ij between each pair of terminals.
This uses O(N^2) internally specified edge conductances. It is an exact existing
construction, but its local programming operations are not available inside the
passive-inclusion chamber. A boundary-only reconfigurable material would replace
that internal addressing burden, not invent electrical function learning.

5. Executable discrimination
----------------------------
Run: python producer-workspace/active_loss_identification.py
Results: active_loss_identification_results.json.

The hidden material state is a dilute cylindrical inclusion with three harmonic
Dirichlet voltage modes on a disk:

  E(q) = [[1,0,x],[0,1,-y]],
  G(q) = diag(pi,pi,pi/2) + .2 E(q)^T E(q).

Inside the plant only, unit mobility times the force coefficient gives

  dq/dt = .4 u_3 (u_1+u_3 x, -u_2+u_3 y).

The controller receives only measure() and pulse(u,duration) callbacks and G*.
It receives no q, force law, mobility, polarizability or temperature and performs
no resets. The target is the full matrix generated by hidden q*=(-.2,.3), from
initial q=(.5,-.4). Six sequential local probes identify A_L and a minimum
eigenvector pulse advances the task. Norm-bounded signed modes are assumed.

With probe duration 1e-4, this simple nonoptimized implementation reaches full
Frobenius response error 8.0724e-6 (loss 3.258e-11), from initial loss .040768,
in 360 cycles. It uses 2520 physical pulses and 4681 ideal matrix reads; the
integrated squared voltage is recorded. With probe duration 1e-3, its final
error is .0011784 after 500 cycles. Fixed control pulses and finite sequential
probe motion leave a bias/overshoot floor. These counts are not optimum active
costs, and the contracted-observable improvement is not implemented here.

An exact unreachable target changes only G_11 by +.1. Since G_11=pi+.2 at every
inclusion position, every reachable configuration has loss >=.005. This failure
cannot be repaired by a better estimator. Thus the toy supplies both a successful
conventional complete-response objective and a physical response restriction.

6. Existing primary constructions and what they actually earn
------------------------------------------------------------
Lefevre et al. (Lab Chip, 2024; DOI 10.1039/D4LC00622D) already use real-time
electrical impedance signatures, not camera control, to select DEP voltages
for single-particle track swapping, size sorting and sequence sorting. A camera
is used for validation. The controller uses a calibrated electrical position
signature and finite-element-informed voltage patterns, not arbitrary whole-G
learning. The full accepted manuscript was inspected.

Massey et al. (Scientific Reports 6, 32197, 2016; DOI 10.1038/srep32197) go closer
to the present objective: external configuration voltages restructure a carbon
nanotube/liquid-crystal material while a differential-evolution controller sees
electrical outputs. No particle-coordinate or microscopy input is required for
training. The demonstrated task is simple classification, with substantial
training time and material-history effects; it is not arbitrary response-matrix
programming or a proof of complete controllability. Nevertheless, the broad
claim 'boundary electrodes teach matter an electrical function without imaging'
is already demonstrated. The full primary article was inspected.

Dillavou et al. (PNAS 2024; DOI 10.1073/pnas.2319718121) demonstrate physical
learning using locally adaptive MOSFET resistor edges, local capacitor memory,
paired free/clamped networks and edge comparators. This is a strong complete
learning machine but supplies internal actuators and memory at each edge, plus a
twin physical network. Those are not free helpers for a passive inclusion device.
The primary paper and Zenodo data/code release 12623376 were inspected; its
15.8 MB data.zip was not downloaded or executed. No associated official GitHub
implementation was established by this scoped search.

Pilati et al. (Advanced Electronic Materials, 2024; DOI 10.1002/aelm.202400750)
measure multiterminal nanowire dynamics and voltage maps. Their conductance
matrix comprises pairwise effective two-terminal conductances with other
terminals floating, not the direct terminal admittance G_in. Their experiments
monitor plasticity/relaxation rather than program arbitrary whole responses.
The primary author manuscript was inspected. Sequential matrix measurements
also explicitly expose the finite acquisition-time issue.

Implication for selecting the opening
------------------------------------
Neither 'camera-free DEP control' nor 'electrical training of rearranging matter'
is an adequate novelty/capability headline. A thermal-covariance method may still
offer reusable calibration or a better finite-cost route in an inaccessible
geometry. It must be compared with task-contracted active identification and
with the actual response manifold. The next decisive construction should earn
a target response family that changes a physical capability, its reachability,
and a quantified improvement under the same electrode, noise, heating, scan
time and permissible material-excursion constraints.

Exact primary-source URLs and access scope are in TERMINAL_SOURCES.txt.

7. Stronger conditional obstruction: planar response inequalities
----------------------------------------------------------------
The dimension obstruction can disappear when enough particles are available.
Circular planarity provides an obstruction that survives arbitrarily many
internal degrees of freedom. In a circular-planar resistor network with positive
conductances and terminals 1,2,3,4 in cyclic order, Curtis--Ingerman--Morrow's
response characterization gives

  det G[{1,2};{4,3}] = G_14 G_23 - G_13 G_24 >= 0.

Their current convention is explicitly into the network. More generally the
signed circular minors characterize the entire realizable response set, and a
critical network's positive conductances give smooth coordinates on its response
cell. This is an existing constructive synthesis and inverse-response theorem,
but implementing its prescribed internal edge conductances remains a physical
operation to earn.

A concrete impossible target has conductance 1 between each opposite pair
(1,3) and (2,4), and epsilon between each neighboring pair on the cycle. Thus

  G* = [[1+2e,-e,-1,-e],[-e,1+2e,-e,-1],
        [-1,-e,1+2e,-e],[-e,-1,-e,1+2e]],  0<e<1.

It is symmetric, has zero row sums, nonpositive off-diagonals, and eigenvalues
0, 4e, 2+2e, 2+2e. It is therefore a perfectly ordinary connected passive
terminal response, realizable with six resistor edges when wire crossings can
be insulated or put on different layers. Its circular minor is e^2-1<0. No
rearrangement of any circular-planar passive network can realize it.

This obstruction is quantitatively robust. If every one of the four entries
G_14,G_23,G_13,G_24 were within delta<(1-e)/2 of its target, positivity of the
pair conductances would give

  G_14 G_23 <= (e+delta)^2 < (1-delta)^2 <= G_13 G_24,

contradicting planarity. For e=.1, at least one such terminal-response entry
must err by .45 or more, regardless of feedback or available particle count.

This is exact for circular-planar networks. Ingerman--Morrow also prove the
corresponding alternating-sign kernel inequalities for positive C^2 scalar
conductivity on a simply connected C^2 planar domain. A finite-electrode
continuum implementation requires matching its electrode/contact model or a
convergent circular-planar discretization. A physically thin but genuinely 3D
chamber is NOT automatically covered. Anisotropic, reactive, active or nonlinear
responses need their own governing conditions; do not export this obstruction
to them silently.
