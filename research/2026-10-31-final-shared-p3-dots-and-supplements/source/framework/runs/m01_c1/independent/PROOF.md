# Unrestricted output polarizations on a compatible resonant triangle

The polynomial is the fixed sharp-boundary member

\[
 P_0(z,w)=F(z,w)+C(z,w)+Q(z,w),\quad
 F=|w|^4+2|z|^2|w|^2,\quad C=\Re(\bar z w^2),\quad
 Q=3(|z|^2+|w|^2)/16.
\]

The global QC question remains open. The result below concerns actual trigonometric gradient fields. It removes all output-polarization restrictions for a right-isosceles resonant triangle, and, by a uniform strict margin, for an open neighborhood of its triangle shapes.

## 1. The theorem

Let (p_1,p_2,p_3\) be arbitrary real two-vectors, and let (\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3\) be arbitrary phases. Set

\[
 \phi(X)=p_1\sin(X_1+\theta_1)+p_2\sin(X_2+\theta_2)
                  +p_3\sin(X_1+X_2+\theta_3).
                                                               \tag{1}
\]

For every mean matrix (A_0\),

\[
 \langle P_0(A_0+D\phi)\rangle\ge P_0(A_0).                    \tag{2}
\]

All output polarizations, amplitudes, relative amplitudes and phases are covered. If all three (p_j\) are nonzero, the inequality is strict. The corresponding statement holds for right-isosceles resonant frequency triangles in any orientation and at any common frequency scale. The proper input/output rotation symmetry preserving P0, already recorded in the frozen theorem, transfers the result; its output action imposes no restriction because all output vectors here are free.

There is also an open neighborhood of the right-isosceles triangle shapes for which (2) holds for every choice of the output vectors. This follows from the uniform margin established below. No width for that neighborhood is claimed, and arbitrary triangles or arbitrary Fourier spectra remain unproved.

The field in (1) is a genuine map on the two-torus. Its gradient is

\[
 G=\sum_{j=1}^3 R_j\cos(k_j\cdot X+\theta_j),\qquad
 R_j=p_j\otimes k_j,
 \quad k_1=(1,0),\ k_2=(0,1),\ k_3=(1,1).
\]

No probability measure on freely chosen matrices is substituted for this gradient.

## 2. Exact compatible moments

For an output (p=(a,b)\) and frequency (k=(c,d)\), the conformal components of its actual rank-one matrix are

\[
 z=(ac+bd,bc-ad),\qquad w=(ac-bd,bc+ad).                    \tag{3}
\]

Write (z_j,w_j\) for these components of (R_j\). At zero phases, the only nonconstant moments through degree four are

\[
 \langle c_j^2\rangle=1/2,\quad
 \langle c_1c_2c_3\rangle=1/4,\quad
 \langle c_j^4\rangle=3/8,\quad
 \langle c_i^2c_j^2\rangle=1/4\quad(i\ne j).
                                                               \tag{4}
\]

Here (c_j=\cos(k_j\cdot X)\). There is no non-pairing relation of length four: the integer relation module of the three frequencies is generated by ((1,1,-1)\). Formula (4) is therefore exhaustive for these polynomial energies.

The receiver `triad_core.py` constructs directly from (3)-(4)

\[
 H=\langle F''(G)\rangle,\quad
 v=\langle DF(G)\rangle,\quad w_0=\langle DC(G)\rangle,
 \quad f=\langle F(G)\rangle,\ c=\langle C(G)\rangle,
 \quad q=\langle Q(G)\rangle,
\]

and the symmetric matrix

\[
 N=\begin{pmatrix}
 H&v&w_0\\v^T&2f&c\\w_0^T&c&2q
 \end{pmatrix}.                                             \tag{5}
\]

For instance, its upper blocks are

\[
 H_{zz}=2\sum_j|w_j|^2 I,\quad
 H_{zw}=4\sum_j z_jw_j^T,\quad
 H_{ww}=2\sum_j(|z_j|^2+|w_j|^2)I+4\sum_jw_jw_j^T.
\]

Its third-moment columns are

\[
 v_z=2\sum_{\{j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3\}} z_j(w_k\cdot w_l),
\]
\[
 v_w=2\sum_{\{j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3\}}w_j(z_k\cdot z_l+w_k\cdot w_l),
 \qquad c=\frac12\sum_{\{j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3\}}
                      \Re(\bar z_jw_kw_l),                 \tag{6}
\]

where each sum has three terms indexed by j, and k,l are its two complementary indices. The remaining quadratic and quartic moments are also reconstructed explicitly in the receiver.

Expanding the actual energy gives

\[
 \langle P_0(A_0+G)-P_0(A_0)\rangle
  =\tfrac12(A_0,1,1)^T N(A_0,1,1).                         \tag{7}
\]

Thus PSD of (5) proves the desired inequality. This is the supplied compatible moment construction, used within its actual gradient scope; it is not claimed as a new general reduction.

Two inherited facts supply its diagonal Schur signs. Homogeneous QC of F gives

\[
 a=2f-v^TH^{-1}v\ge0.
\]

Global ROC of P0, applied separately to the real Fourier modes of the second variation, gives

\[
 b=2q-w_0^TH^{-1}w_0\ge0.
\]

When at least two modes are nonzero, H is positive definite: each summand (F''(R_j)\) has a one-dimensional acoustic kernel, and distinct noncollinear input directions cannot produce the same kernel. Degenerate cases can alternatively be reached by continuity. It remains to prove

\[
 \det N=\det H\,(ab-r^2)\ge0,
 \qquad r=c-v^TH^{-1}w_0.                                 \tag{8}
\]

## 3. Separate the safe two-wave boundaries

Use six unrestricted real output coordinates

\[
 p_1=(s_0,s_1),\quad p_2=(s_2,s_3),\quad
 p_3=(s_4+s_5,-s_4+s_5).                                  \tag{9}
\]

The last change is invertible and restricts no polarization. The exact determinant reconstructed from (3)-(6) is a degree-fourteen polynomial D with 2,780 nonzero rational monomials. It has even multidegree in each output pair. Separate it as

\[
 D=D_{p_1=0}+D_{p_2=0}+D_{p_3=0}
       +\sum_{i+j+k=7\atop i,j,k\ge1}D_{2i,2j,2k}.         \tag{10}
\]

There are fifteen interior multidegrees. There are no one-wave monomials, so the three boundary polynomials in (10) do not double count any nonzero terms. This is checked coefficient by coefficient by the replay.

Each boundary polynomial is nonnegative by a separate exact argument. With one mode absent, (v=c=0\), so (5) has the independent entry 2f and the block

\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}H&w_0\\w_0^T&2q\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The latter is PSD by global ROC: for every background A,

\[
 A^THA+2w_0\cdot A+2q
       =\langle P_0''(A)[G,G]\rangle\ge0.
\]

The inequality follows by applying the rank-one Hessian inequality to each actual sine/cosine Fourier coefficient. Homogenizing this nonnegative quadratic is precisely PSD of B. Also (f\ge0\) since F is pointwise nonnegative. Hence each boundary determinant is (2f\det B\ge0\). This step uses ROC and actual Fourier compatibility, not the desired QC conclusion.

## 4. An exact positive certificate for every interior coefficient form

For each interior multidegree (d=(2i,2j,2k)\), denote the corresponding output form by (D_d\). It is homogeneous of degree d_l in pair l. Normalize each nonzero output pair by its component of largest absolute value. There are eight charts. In each chart one component in each pair is 1, the other is (t_l\in[-1,1]\), and the removed scale enters to an even power.

Consequently positivity of (D_d\) reduces to a polynomial on the compact cube ([-1,1]^3\). Set (t_l=2u_l-1\) and convert to the tensor Bernstein basis of degrees d. The exact conversion for a univariate power is

\[
 [u^j] \text{ to Bernstein index }i:
 \qquad u^j=\sum_{i=j}^d
 \frac{\binom{i}{j}}{\binom d j}\binom d i u^i(1-u)^{d-i}.
                                                               \tag{11}
\]

For direct conversion of (t^k=(2u-1)^k\), the receiver uses

\[
 B_{d;k,i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,i)}
       (-1)^{k-j}2^j\binom k j\frac{\binom i j}{\binom d j}.
\]

These are exact rational numbers. Bernstein basis functions are nonnegative and sum to one. Dyadic de Casteljau subdivision preserves the represented polynomial and partitions its domain exactly.

The certificate covers all fifteen forms and all eight output charts, for 120 root cubes and **904 final boxes**. Every Bernstein coefficient on every final box is strictly positive. The smallest coefficient anywhere is exactly **864**. The complete subdivision paths and exact minima are in `all_free_strict_bernstein_certificate.json`.

`verify_all_free.py` does not accept a list of passing evaluations. It:

1. reconstructs N and its entire determinant from the polynomial and actual gradient modes;
2. compares all 2,780 determinant coefficients with the retained coefficient list;
3. forms each of the 120 compact chart polynomials by exact arithmetic;
4. rebuilds every specified dyadic subdivision by rational de Casteljau steps;
5. checks a complete binary cover with no missing half-boxes or leaf descendants;
6. checks every Bernstein coefficient, not just the stored minimum.

All checks passed. This finite algebraic certificate proves a continuum statement in all six output variables.

There is a useful quantitative consequence. Put

\[
 U=s_0^2+s_1^2,\quad V=s_2^2+s_3^2,\quad W=s_4^2+s_5^2.
\]

In a dominant-coordinate chart the removed scale in each pair has magnitude at least its Euclidean norm divided by sqrt(2). Since the total degree is fourteen, the bound 864 implies

\[
 D_{2i,2j,2k}\ge \frac{864}{2^7}U^iV^jW^k
                       =\frac{27}{4}U^iV^jW^k.
\]

Combining with the nonnegative boundary terms gives

\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \det N\ge\frac{27}{4}
       \sum_{i+j+k=7\atop i,j,k\ge1} U^iV^jW^k.\quad}       \tag{12}
\]

In particular the determinant is strictly positive whenever all three output vectors are nonzero. Together with the inherited Schur diagonal signs, (8) proves PSD of N, and in this case positive definiteness. Formula (7) proves (2).

## 5. Arbitrary phases and nearby triangle shapes

For general phases, the only changing moments are v and c, both multiplied by

\[
 h=\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2-\theta_3).
\]

The Schur determinant becomes

\[
 2fb-h^2\{b\,v^TH^{-1}v+r^2\}.
\]

Because b is nonnegative and H is positive definite, it is minimized at (h^2=1\). The zero-phase certificate therefore controls every phase. Zero individual amplitudes follow by continuity, or by the separate two-wave argument.

The fifteen interior coefficient forms vary continuously with a noncollinear resonant frequency triangle. By (12), each is uniformly strictly positive on the product of the three unit output circles at the right-isosceles triangle. Compactness and finiteness therefore give an open neighborhood of triangle shapes in which all fifteen remain positive simultaneously. The two-wave boundary argument applies for every triangle. This proves the neighborhood assertion in Section 1. No explicit neighborhood radius has been calculated; the certified shape itself is exactly the right-isosceles triangle.

This result genuinely includes noncharacteristic polarizations and nonzero resonant correlations. For example, take zero phases and

\[
 p_1=(1,1),\quad p_2=(1,1),\quad p_3=(2,0).
\]

Exact reconstruction gives

\[
 c=-6,\quad a=5614/19,\quad b=233/76,\quad r=-80/19,
 \qquad ab-r^2=33749/38>0.
\]

None of these three output directions is prescribed by (U(k)=i\bar k^3\). The positive theorem does not merely recover the vanishing-third-moment class.

## 6. Replays, failed approaches and remaining scope

Run, with Python bytecode writes disabled:

    PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 verify_all_free.py
    PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 check_actual_all_free.py

The second replay independently constructs eight actual trigonometric gradients with arbitrary output vectors, phases and nonzero means, and checks the direct integral against (7). That numerical check is implementation QA. The exact determinant and Bernstein cover are the continuum proof.

The construction developed through two simpler exact positive results. With two characteristic axial polarizations and an arbitrary resonant output, its determinant grouped into 29 nonnegative binary forms and admitted a simple Young-inequality SOS certificate. With only the first axial polarization fixed, the positive-amplitude determinant remainder admitted 286 two-monomial squares and 203 monomial squares. Those working constructions and exact replays are retained.

A direct signed-monomial/diagonal-SOS attack on the fully free determinant failed: the determinant has 1,140 negative coefficients, all with some odd output exponents, and the greedy diagonal allocation exhausted its available terms. This was a failed sufficient certificate, not a negative gradient energy. Separating the two-wave boundaries and using compact output charts resolved it.

An attempted extension to every orthogonal aspect ratio also failed at the coefficient-certificate stage: the straightforward shifted coefficient test had 49 uncertified groups. No energy violation or negative determinant was proved. Those scripts and coefficient lists are retained as unfinished exploration, with no theorem claimed for unrestricted aspect ratios.

The central statement that P0 is QC for every gradient remains open. Arbitrary frequency triangles outside the neighborhood, interactions among more than three modes, and independently elliptical sine/cosine output polarizations at a single frequency are not covered. This is not a density theorem and does not classify the entire exceptional polynomial face.

The frozen characteristic-polarized theorem, its cusp/zero-gap facts, global ROC admission of P0, homogeneous QC of F and the general compatible moment reduction retain their supplied scope and credits. The new result is the unrestricted-output right-isosceles resonant certificate, its quantitative determinant bound, and the resulting neighborhood in triangle shape. No external historical-priority or independent-review claim is made.
