## The precise acquired object

For a feasible set F in {0,1}^m, take the feature vector of all squarefree Boolean monomials of degree at most D. The span `V_D(F)` of actual feasible feature vectors determines every identity in that language: its orthogonal complement is precisely the polynomials vanishing on F. A catalogue W of actual feasible points whose feature vectors span V is a complete test set; its minimum size is rank R_D(F). This is linear-algebraic completeness, not a representative probability sample. [N27–75.]

The graph-specific advance is acquisition without supplying this span. In characteristic two, the alternating Tutte matrix's Pfaffian has one distinct formal monomial per perfect matching. For a partial matching S,

`H_S(z)=z^S Pf(T_(G-V(S))(z)) = sum_(M contains S) z^M`.

Hence a Boolean polynomial query p has enumerator `H_p=sum_M p(M) z^M`. It is identically zero iff p vanishes on every matching. Its fingerprint degree is n for a 2n-vertex graph, independent of D. Distinct matching monomials prevent characteristic-two cancellation from disguising a violating matching. This is algebraic feasibility/identity information, not the integer number of matchings. [N77–134.]

A random field probe returns the vector H(alpha). Expanding each field coordinate in a binary basis gives rows inside the true Boolean feature span. Thus rank increases add valid knowledge of feasible features. If the current span is incomplete, a query annihilating that span but not all actual matchings gives a nonzero degree-n polynomial; an unchanged probe has probability at most n/q. Stopping after t consecutive misses, with `(L_D+1)(n/q)^t<=delta`, bounds incompleteness by delta over all stopping stages. [N136–159.]

This is the important compilation quantifier: on one successful acquisition event, **all** future in-language queries are correct, even if chosen adaptively from earlier answers. Confidence need not be union-bounded again over an unlimited sequence of those queries. The guarantee remains probabilistic; no new structural certificate of completeness is obtained merely by seeing t misses.

## Witnesses, absence, and support semantics

The aggregate learner stores field points associated with rank increases, not necessarily every actual matching. A nonidentity query has a stored nonzero evaluation. Matching self-reduction partitions the residual solutions by the edge at a vertex; a nonzero sum has a nonzero term, allowing deterministic edge selection and endpoint deletion until an original matching is recovered. The final matching and requested query value can be checked directly. The eager implementation pays to recover a full basis of actual witnesses; those witnesses support direct evaluation of later Boolean circuits without expanding them into monomials. [N161–179.]

These guarantees should remain separated:

- A returned, verified matching is a genuine positive witness.
- A zero identity/empty conditional branch inferred from the learned catalogue is valid on the acquisition-completeness event, with its stated delta. An incomplete catalogue can falsely report an identity or miss a branch; finite non-discovery is not a deterministic absence proof.
- Witnesses and identities do not supply probabilities, uniform samples, multiplicities or counts. Weighted counting below invokes more computation.

The raw arithmetic-circuit extension concerns the **variable supports of nonzero formal monomials of the computed polynomial after cancellation**. Zeroing selected variables and taking characteristic-two finite differences filters monomials containing S, enabling the same feature-span acquisition. Repeated powers remain different formal monomials; this is not the Boolean satisfying-assignment set of an arbitrary circuit and not its syntactic derivation set. A conservative formal degree bound is computed from circuit syntax, and the field must exceed that bound for the stated failure estimate. ## Conditioning and adaptive computation: the actual closure law

For conditions `g_i(x)=b_i`, their Boolean indicator is `C=product_i(1+g_i+b_i)`, of degree at most `s=sum_i deg(g_i)`. If W tests all degree-D identities on F, then its points satisfying C test all degree-(D-s) identities on the conditioned F, provided s<=D. A prospective conditioned identity p is tested by Cp on the original catalogue. The corresponding relation-space consumer explicitly computes the image under multiplication by C and pays actual intermediate polynomial widths. [N245–298.]

The theorem supplies new identities arising after conditioning. An already valid identity survives every restriction without consuming another budget; remaining **complete** for new query facts is what costs degree. Confusing these statements would exaggerate both unrestricted reuse and the limitation. [N300–304.]

A finite adaptive Boolean-query program has the same reachable transcripts on W and F if the sum of query degrees along every path is at most D. Path indicators have degree at most D; any actual reachable path must have a witness in W. This is feasibility/transcript coverage, not the distribution or frequency of those transcripts. Circuit syntax gives conservative query-degree bounds; a compact circuit does not automatically have low degree, and semantic cancellations can make the syntactic guard unnecessarily reject it. [N306–323.]

The counterexample is governing, not incidental: F={00,10,01,11} and W={00,10,01} agree on all affine identities, but after x1=1 the catalogue retains only 10 and misses the real branch 11. The degree-one budget has been spent. Independent four-cycle matching choices require `sum_(j<=D) binomial(r,j)` witnesses in this representation and all 2^r choices at D=r. That is a representation-size lower bound, not universal computational hardness. [N325–347.]

## Acquisition, search and exact-counting costs

For matchings, incident-edge monomials can be omitted. There are at most `L_D=sum_(j<=D) binomial(m,j)` features, so the construction is polynomial at fixed D with m^O(D) dependence, not fixed-parameter tractable merely because D is named a parameter. Safe probes use at most L_D Pfaffian minors; nonsingular evaluations permit inverse/complementary-minor speedups, with a direct exact fallback for singular cases. Up to `(L_D+1)t` probes, binary elimination, field arithmetic, feature creation and storage are charged. The prototype's GF(256) imposes n<256 or formal circuit degree<256; the theorem must choose larger fields when necessary. [N349–378.]

The demand-driven comparator is material: cache proved identities and counterexample witnesses, and call an algebraic teacher only when the new query is undecided. On the all-correct event, each expensive call increases one of two orthogonal dimensions, giving at most L_D teacher calls over the adaptive lifetime. This conventional counterexample-guided route may avoid buying the complete eager theory when few queries will arrive. It must receive the same graph, arithmetic, accumulated knowledge and confidence resources. A claim of benefit should confront it rather than only recount avoided enumeration. [N380–394.]

The signed counting consumer is a separate construction. Learned quadratic identities search for a polynomial agreeing with the Pfaffian crossing-sign function on matchings. A correction of alternating rank rho expands into at most 2^rho signed Pfaffians; weighted unranking then produces actual matchings under additional exact count calculations. Arbitrary edge forcing/forbidding can preserve a valid counting identity beyond the catalogue's new-query degree budget, but parity evidence adds its own expansion. Exact arithmetic is used; correctness of identities learned probabilistically remains conditional on successful compilation. [N396–445.]

On K6, a greedy correction cost 64 terms; searching the learned equivalence class found four after 21 candidates. K4,4 found four after 27.
