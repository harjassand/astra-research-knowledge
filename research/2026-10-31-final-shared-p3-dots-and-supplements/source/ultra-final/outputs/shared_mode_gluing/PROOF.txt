RESULTS AND SCOPE

There are two results.

(A) A general finite-dimensional capacity principle. For an ACTUAL
compatible gradient and a positive rank-one covariance quadrature, the
least Hessian cost of matching its entire cubic moment is explicit. It
minimizes every linear combination of cubic signals simultaneously.
After removing the four translation modes, the available resource is an
EIGHT by EIGHT positive matrix. Covariance exchanges change this matrix
while preserving the other blocks. For a fixed field, the gluing test is
a NINE by NINE linear matrix inequality in the covariance weights.
This gives a convex resource-allocation principle for shared modes.
It does not assert feasibility for every field or every ray catalogue.

(B) The preceding overlapping network is now controlled at ALL
FOUR independent real amplitudes. Let F=F_* be the acquired generic
non-aligned Hopf quartic, and let p be ANY globally rank-one convex
polynomial of degree at most four with leading cF, c>0. Write

 phi(x,y)=a e1 sin x + b e2 sin y
       +h(e1+e2) sin(x+y-delta1)
       +d(2e1+e2) sin(2x+y-delta1-delta2).                 (1)

For every real a,b,h,d, every phase delta1,delta2, every mean M, and
all common proper output rotations U,

 <p(M+U Dphi)>-p(M) >= (c/32)<|Dphi|^4>.                  (2)

The two cubic resonances share TWO modes. Their non-pairwise quartets
are included. This extends the preceding fixed-relative-amplitude
four-wave theorem; its earlier proof remains intact.

Any finite nine-adically separated collection of these blocks and the
previous arbitrary-amplitude three-wave blocks satisfies the common
bound (c/200)<|G|^4>, with independent amplitudes, phases and output
rotations per block. The finite number of modes is unbounded.

The test fields remain restricted. In particular they lie in rotated
symmetric matrix subspaces blockwise. Unrestricted overlapping networks
and QC of the full 28-dimensional exposed polynomial face are OPEN.
No classification of arbitrary gradients, laminate representation,
Morrey counterexample, historical priority or physical advantage is
asserted.

2. THE EXACT HESSIAN QUADRATURE IDENTITY

Let G be an actual periodic zero-mean compatible gradient. Define
 S=<vec G (vec G)^T>, f=<F(G)>.
Choose real rank-one R_i and positive w_i such that

                    sum_i w_i R_i R_i^T = S.               (4)

Let backgrounds A_i satisfy

 sum_i w_i grad C(R_i).A_i = <C(G)> for EVERY C in V.        (5)

For p=F+C+Q+affine, homogeneity gives exactly

 <p(G)>-p(0)=f-E+(1/2)sum_i w_i p''(A_i)[R_i,R_i],
 E=(1/2)sum_i w_i A_i^T H_i A_i, H_i=F''(R_i).             (6)

To see the identity directly,
 p''(A)[R,R]=A^T F''(R)A+2 grad C(R).A+2Q(R).
The quadratic term is matched by (4), and the cubic term by (5).
Global ROC makes every Hessian evaluation in (6) nonnegative.
Thus E<=f-gamma<|G|^4> proves a variational gap for EVERY admitted p.

The backgrounds are actual finite matrices. They are not a presumed
compatible field or a selector to be differentiated. Nor does (6)
represent the full law of G as a laminate. It uses the moments relevant
to this polynomial face.

Translation of the argument preserves ROC and the leading F. Hence the
same construction applies to p(M+A), proving the bound at all means.
Dividing p by its positive leading scale c proves the factor c.
The source proof establishes left SO(2) invariance of F, so the entire
construction can be output-rotated. Scaling G scales its positive
weights quadratically, backgrounds linearly and budget quartically.

3. CANONICAL COMPOSITION: THE LEAST COST FOR ALL SIGNALS

Choose any basis P_1,...,P_12 of V. For each catalogue ray define the
four by twelve matrix

 J_i=[grad P_1(R_i),...,grad P_12(R_i)],

and let ell=(<P_1(G)>,...,<P_12(G)>). By (3), all columns of J_i are in
range H_i. Define the positive capacity matrix

                 W(w)=sum_i w_i J_i^T H_i^+ J_i.           (7)

A superscript + denotes the Moore-Penrose inverse. An exact solution
H_i Y_i=J_i may replace H_i^+ J_i in every formula: the resulting W
and all costs are independent of the nullspace freedom.

There are backgrounds satisfying (5) if and only if ell is in range W.
When they exist, canonical backgrounds are

             A_i^*=H_i^+ J_i W^+ ell,                     (8)

and their exact minimum cost is

                     E_min=(1/2)ell^T W^+ ell.             (9)

Proof. Range W is the span of the images J_i^T, since
 z^T W z=sum_i w_i (J_i z)^T H_i^+(J_i z),
 and each J_i z lies in range H_i. Equation (8) matches ell. For any
other matching backgrounds A_i=A_i^*+D_i,

 sum_i w_i (A_i^*)^T H_i D_i
   =(W^+ell)^T sum_i w_i J_i^T D_i=0.

Consequently E=E_min+(1/2)sum_i w_i D_i^T H_i D_i.

More strongly, let the columns of L be any number of cubic signal
targets. The canonical profile H_i^+ J_i W^+ L minimizes their cost
GRAM MATRIX in Loewner order, simultaneously for every real combination
of signals. Every other matching profile has Gram

 (1/2)L^T W^+ L + (1/2)sum_i w_i D_i^T H_i D_i.             (10)

Thus shared modes have ONE covariance weight each. Cubic signals are
superposed before their quadratic cost is taken. Adding independent
local costs while spending a shared weight twice is invalid.

If a target lies outside range W, a null vector z of W gives an exact
obstruction: the admitted cubic sum_j z_j P_j has zero gradient at
EVERY active catalogue ray but nonzero actual cubic moment. That only
obstructs this catalogue. Adding covariance-exchange rays can remove it.
The exact example in capacity_example.json reproduces the three-ray
obstruction and its repair by a six-ray exchange.

If E_min exceeds the desired quartic budget, no backgrounds on these
fixed weights and rays can meet that budget. This also is a catalogue
obstruction, not a global QC failure or a globally admitted separation.

4. WHY ONLY AN EIGHT-DIMENSIONAL CAPACITY HAS TO CHANGE

Use the ordered basis (partial_1 F,...,partial_4 F,C_1,...,C_8).
For an actual G define

 H=<F''(G)>, v=<DF(G)>, c=(<C_alpha(G)>)_alpha,
 B=[<DC_1(G)>,...,<DC_8(G)>].

For EVERY covariance quadrature (4), its matrix (7) has blocks

 W(w)=[[H,B],[B^T,D(w)]],
 D(w)_{alpha,beta}
  =sum_i w_i grad C_alpha(R_i)^T H_i^+ grad C_beta(R_i).     (11)

Indeed grad(partial_j F)(R_i)=H_i e_j. Therefore its translation block
is sum_i w_i H_i=H and its mixed block is sum_i w_i DC_alpha(R_i)=B.
Both identities use only equality of the second moments. They are
verified independently against actual Laurent moments in the code.

Covariance exchanges preserve H and B. They can change ONLY D, hence
only the eight by eight Schur capacity

 Lambda(w)=D(w)-B^T H^+ B.

When v and the columns of B belong to range H, set

 a=2f-v^T H^+ v,             r=c-B^T H^+ v.                (12)

These are the earlier compatible correlation quotient and leading
budget, now in all eight nontranslation cubic coordinates. If ell is
in range W, then r is in range Lambda and generalized Schur elimination
gives

 E_min=(1/2)v^T H^+v+(1/2)r^T Lambda^+r,
 f-E_min=(1/2)(a-r^T Lambda^+r).                            (13)

The range conditions follow automatically when the full capacity system
matches ell. They also follow from the positive block and the leading
QC moment inequality in the appropriate actual-field setting. If H is
positive definite, no range qualification on H is needed.

For a fixed actual field and a fixed rank-one ray catalogue, weights
must satisfy (4) and w_i>=0. The condition for a gap gamma is exactly

           [[a-2gamma<|G|^4>, r^T],
            [r,                  Lambda(w)]] >= 0.         (14)

This is a NINE by NINE linear matrix inequality in w: H,B,v,c,f are
fixed actual moments and D(w) is linear in the weights. Equivalently,
without first removing translation modes, use the thirteen by thirteen
block [[2(f-gamma<|G|^4>),ell^T],[ell,W(w)]]. For zero weights restrict
to the active rays. Condition (14) includes its necessary range test.

Thus positive covariance and quartic cost have separate, explicit jobs:
(4) allocates the shared second moment once, while (14) determines whether
that allocation has enough capacity for ALL cubic correlations and the
actual quartic interference. This is a general compositional criterion,
not a claim that every actual field has a feasible allocation.

The connection with the earlier moment-Schur test is direct. If
C=sum z_alpha C_alpha is the normalized lower cubic, global ROC implies

 2<Q(G)> >= z^T D(w)z

for every positive quadrature, by the global acoustic pseudoinverse
inequality. Subtracting w_C^T H^+ w_C yields b>=z^T Lambda(w)z, while
its correlation is z.r. If (14) holds at gamma=0, matrix Cauchy-Schwarz
gives (z.r)^2<=a z^T Lambda z<=ab. This recovers the variational condition
for every lower cubic and quadratic simultaneously.

5. AN EXPLICIT RESOURCE-ALLOCATION LAW

If local source profiles z_ie are to be combined on catalogue ray i,
write z_i=sum_e z_ie. With ONE final positive weight w_i their cost is

                   (1/2w_i) z_i^T H_i z_i.                (15)

If positive allocations u_ie satisfy sum_e u_ie<=w_i, weighted
Cauchy-Schwarz gives

 (1/w_i)(sum_e z_ie)^T H_i(sum_e z_ie)
                     <=sum_e (1/u_ie) z_ie^T H_i z_ie.     (16)

This is a sufficient fractional allocation rule. It explains when local
costs can be added. When that rule is too expensive, canonical gluing
(7)--(10) retains the cross terms instead of discarding them.

The joint catalogue cost bound K<=D0 can also be written as a linear
matrix inequality in unweighted sources Z_i (columns z_ie), weights
w_i and D0:

 [[2D0, Z_1^T H_1, ..., Z_N^T H_N],
  [H_1 Z_1, w_1 H_1, ..., 0],
                    ...,
  [H_N Z_N, 0, ..., w_N H_N]] >=0.                         (17)

Generalized Schur elimination gives exactly
2D0>=sum_i Z_i^T H_i Z_i/w_i. Source matching and covariance matching
are linear too. No irrational square roots are needed for exact replay.

9. SEPARATED MIXED BLOCKS AND AN UNBOUNDED NUMBER OF MODES

The spatial directions remain e1,e2,(1,1),(2,1). The receiver checks
sixteen exact two by two matrices proving, for all output vectors u,v,

 F''(u tensor n_i)[v tensor n_j,v tensor n_j]
             >=(3/50)|u tensor n_i|^2 |v tensor n_j|^2.    (25)

Use any finite collection of blocks at spatial scales L_j=9^j and
scale each potential by 1/L_j. All mixed frequency relations of total
length at most four reduce to opposite pairs. A nonzero contribution
from the largest block has a coordinate of absolute value at least
L_j; all smaller contributions together have coordinates at most
8 L_(j-1)<L_j. Three internally cancelling large modes cannot leave
one nonzero smaller mode to cancel.

Consequently quadratic and cubic moments add. The only mixed quartic
terms are the pair terms. If s_i=<|G_i|^2>, their F contribution is
at least (3/100)s_i s_j by (25), while their radial fourth-moment
contribution is at most 6s_i s_j. Since 3/100=6/200, the local 1/32
bound and the preceding triad 1/200 bound combine into a uniform
1/200 bound for every finite mixed union. This is not a density
argument for all compatible gradients or arbitrary shared networks.
