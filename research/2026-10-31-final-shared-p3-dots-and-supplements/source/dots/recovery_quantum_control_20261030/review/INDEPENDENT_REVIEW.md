## Waiting obstruction

The descending alternating-layer path n_i=m-ceil(i/2), i=0,...,2m, is valid, nonnegative and reachable from flat initialization. Vertex 0's final production can occur immediately before this cut. Vertex 1 cannot consume until the chronological first-increment chain 2m,...,1 occurs. Under independent rate-lambda Poisson attempts, each next required vertex remains enabled once its predecessor has incremented; other events cannot disable it before its own required first increment. Strong memorylessness therefore gives exactly Erlang(2m,lambda) delay, mean 2m/lambda, and the stated Poisson-tail identity.

This disproves an all-state, size-independent active-wire waiting bound based solely on valid cuts and perfect local attempted service. It does not quantify the frequency of producing such a slope from flat initialization under a particular stochastic controller, and does not contradict the native attempted-service theorem.
