# Cooperative activation of any fixed order below the clearing order

1 November 2026. Solo mathematical derivation. The two-A logarithmic theorem is
preserved in the companion proofs. This construction removes its uniform T/U
premise by an actual low-A section correction. It does not turn the failed
minimum-three-A ratio into a true statement. No independent audit, accepted
Astra receipt, historical novelty or overall mission success is claimed.

## Exact theorem contract

Fix integers d>=2, m>s>=2, and q=m+1. On composition species A,X_1,...,X_(d-1),
take a finite strongly connected homogeneous degree-m graph, every complex of
which contains at least sA. Require mA and every sA+(m-s)X_i, each latter source
having a positive-rate A-increasing edge. All rates are fixed and strictly
positive. Gate every fast complex by the SAME qB catalyst and add

    0 -> A+B -> mA+qB -> 0.

The construction gives nonexplosion, classwise positive recurrence, and every
invariant exponential moment of N+B. It is not a general weakly reversible
recurrence theorem, a rate-uniform bound, or an efficient stationary-law solver.
Write kappa=m-s>=1, nu=m-1, and g_j(A)=(1+A)^(-j).

## 1. General-order local Green bounds

Fix N and A>=s; fast dynamics preserve this set. Kill at
k_D(A)_m+u+vA, where u,v lie in a bounded nonnegative box. T,U,K_D have the same
ACTUAL first-terminal meanings as in the two-A local proof.

An A-decreasing fast jump needs at least s+1 source A. When s<=A<=eta N, one
required reservoir source contributes a negative g_j drift of order
A^(s-j-1)N^kappa, while all adverse contributions are at most
C A^(s-j)N^(kappa-1). Choose eta small. On A>=eta N the possible fast drift is
O(N^(m-j-1)) and clearing contributes -cN^(m-j). Consequently, for each fixed
finite list of positive real j,

    R[A g_j] <= C_j N^(-kappa) g_j(A_in).             (G1)

Here A^(s-2)>=c was used to compare the low-A drift with A g_j. Apply the same
calculation to h(A)=(1+A)^(1-s). Its low-A drift is <=-cN^kappa, global absolute
fast drift is <=CN^kappa, and the high-A killing term is of order N^(kappa+1).
Stopped Dynkin therefore gives finite mean and

    T <= C N^(-kappa) h(A_in) <= C N^(-kappa).        (G2)

The killed inverse-power supersolutions from the two-A proof still apply:

    T<=C g_m(A_in),        U<=C g_nu(A_in),
    K_D g_j(A_after)<=C_j g_j(A_in),
    K_D g_m(A_after)<=C T.                          (G3)

For (G3), the marked target is the physical A_after=A-m. At low A, Q_N g_j is
negative; at high A, clearing dominates its O(1/N) relative fast correction.
For the time/occupation envelopes, killing g_m supplies a constant and killing
g_nu supplies A, with the finitely many A<m states paid by low-A drift.
No output with A<s is continued as an active fast state.

The compensator and Holder give U<=C T^((m-1)/m)(1+T)^(1/m)=o(1). Thus K_D1>=1/2
for large N and U>=c N^(-nu). Also

    K_D g_m <= C p_N K_D1,      p_N=N^(-kappa).       (G4)

As before, restore physical time by dividing T,U by (B)_q. The normalized
competitors are u=k_I/(B)_q and v=k_T B/(B)_q. Carried high-activation and
immigration kernels have g_j norms O(p_N) for j>0 by (G1). The actual output
law (G4), including rare phase selection before observing A, is retained.

## 2. Good inputs have a vanishing ACTUAL time/occupation ratio

For a sufficiently small fixed c>0, exp(-cA) is excessive for the killed fast
generator, uniformly in N. At A<=eta N the A-increasing s-source dominates
the adverse s+1-source contribution, as above. At A>=eta N the positive fast
contribution is at most Cc N^m exp(-cA); choose c small enough that clearing
k_D(A)_m exp(-cA) dominates it. The extra killing channels help.

Let sigma be depletion to A<A_in/2 before the terminal event. Stopped Dynkin
applied to this positive excessive function gives

    P(sigma<terminal) <= exp(-c A_in/2).             (G5)

Before depletion T_pre<=2U_pre/A_in. After it, the actual remaining mean is at
most Cp_N by (G2), at the actual entrance in the SAME conserved N shell.
Divide by U>=cN^(-nu). Uniformly over active inputs,

    T/U <= 2/A_in + C N^(s-1) exp(-c A_in/2).        (G6)

In particular the ratio tends to zero on A_in>K(r)=log^2(2+r) at the section
B=q, where N=r+m. This does not assert a vanishing ratio for fixed A_in=s;
the preserved three-A controls show why such an assertion would fail.

## 3. Geometry, physical seeding and unweighted repair

Use EXACTLY the H(B), r=N-H(B), Z=B-N-I and active section Q of the two-A global
proof. These count identities depend on m,q, not on s. For

    K_head=(m-1)+(d-1)(m-s-1),

inactivity at N>K_head is precisely A<s. At most s actual immigrations, or an
earlier activation from A>=1, seed activity. Activation then creates at least
m A. Mean seeding time and residual overshoot are bounded; no seed is free.

The high processor, carried low committor h_b<=C_b g_(m+1-b), and the refined
first-law estimate from the two-A proof use (G1)--(G4), not its logarithmic
ratio. They therefore remain valid with p(r)=r^(-kappa). Inactive outputs are
rare JOINT clearing-kernel mass; a down clearing followed by seeding can cancel
its residual decrement and is not given a false pointwise margin.

Fresh-low competing immigration is small by (G4). The round construction gives,
for theta sufficiently large and then r_0 sufficiently large, actual terminal
weight <=2 exp(theta r) and actual running exp(theta r)-cost <=C_theta exp(theta r).
The same holds for any specified fixed polynomial residual enlargement, with
all weights evaluated at actual outputs. Arbitrary observed low/inactive starts
pay bounded physical priming once. No noncompact target is assumed recurrent.

## 4. The additional carried repair interface is earned

We need full terminal g_m transport, not merely the unweighted repair. Here is
the relative estimate paying a rare low immigration AFTER a clearing. For any
fixed j>0, Holder and (G3)--(G4) give, at its fully specified stage entrance,

    K_D g_(j+1)
      <= (K_D g_m)^(1/m)
         (K_D g_(jm/(m-1)))^((m-1)/m)
      <= C_j p_N^(1/m) g_j(A_in).                  (G7)

The exponents add exactly: 1+j=j+1, while the carried INPUT exponent is j.
Only one additional fixed local exponent jm/(m-1) is needed for each requested
j; no infinite chain of full-repair exponent assumptions occurs. The same
argument pays an inactive or fixed-low-A output, since on that bounded output
set g_j<=C g_(j+1). This is an unnormalized positive-kernel inequality; it does
not condition on a sampled A and multiply unrelated marginals.

For clarity, choose repair rounds as follows. Start at a pointwise active high
input. Treat high activation, high immigration, inactive output and the FIRST
competitive low immigration as designated bad branches. Continue zero-immigration
ordinary clearing/low-activation steps inside the round until Q or a down
continuation. Their low phases strictly decrease, so only a fixed number occur.
If a down clearing lands low, include its zero-immigration activation before
continuing down; an immigration there is a rare bad branch under (G7). This
avoids assigning a pointwise margin after observing an inactive low output.

High bad kernels have relative g_j norm O(p_N), and the reset/low bad kernels
have relative norm O(p_N^(1/m)) by (G7). Every bad branch is physically primed
to a pointwise active input or Q; A only increases in this bounded priming, so
g_j does not increase. Ordinary down kernels have norm C_j exp(-theta); all
rare upward overshoots cost only a fixed C_theta. Choose theta so the former
is <1/4, then r_0 so the latter is <1/4. Positive Neumann iteration proves

    E[ w_t(r_return) g_j(A_return)]
       <= C_(j,t,theta) w_t(r_in) g_j(A_in),         (G8)
    w_t(r)=(1+r)^t exp(theta r),

for each required fixed real t, including negative t. The actual running
weighted costs satisfy the same interface: local Green forcing pays fast time,
and low/seeding waiting has the immigration floor. Ordinary neutral fast-to-low
steps are inside the round, not unit-mass continuation rows. No B cap, frozen
hidden pool or independent reset has been introduced.

Starting from an arbitrary observed low state uses its bounded initial priming,
so (G8) also supplies the first-clearing continuation at B=0. For the finite
set of j needed here, choose their exponents/constants, then theta, then r_0.
Threshold filters discard only small A and improve conditional inverse moments;
other A-dependent selections retain their unnormalized joint kernels.

## 5. Two complete section interfaces

Let P be the actual first-section-return kernel, killed at r<=r_0. A first
connector event at Q is followed by the physical repair above. Let T,U be the
actual first-stage physical integrals at that section input.

First, the coefficient-one first-clearing argument of the two-A proof and the
downward first-activation branch give, for every specified fixed t,

    P w_t - w_t <= -c_theta w_t U + C_theta w_t T.  (S1)

This uses the refined incoming law C K_D g_m<=CT at the first clearing, not a
worst-case later reset p_N. A first activation enters 2q-1; its first clearing
before immigration is down, with inactive/birth mass o(1). Its repaired weight
is <=[2exp(-theta)+o(1)C_theta]w_t. Same-level returns have coefficient EXACTLY
one also for w_t; no generic O(1/r) error is appended to the small drift.

Second, apply (G8) with j=m after the first event. A first clearing contributes
C K_D g_m<=CT. An immigration contributes at most its probability k_I T. For
activation use its actual compensator and A*g_m(A+nu)<=C. The first shifts in
r are bounded. Thus the full actual section law satisfies

    P[ w_t(r_next) g_m(A_next)] <= C_theta w_t(r) T. (S2)

This is a whole-return marked interface with actual output coordinates. It is
not an assertion about all observed reset states or a product of two means.

## 6. Repair the non-small low-A row with its actual Green

Set Bad={A<=K(r)} on active Q, Good its complement, and h_t=w_t r^(-kappa).
By (G2), T<=Cr^(-kappa), so on Bad (S1) gives P w_t-w_t<=C h_t. On Good,
(G6) gives P w_t-w_t<=-c w_t U after raising r_0.

At a Bad OUTPUT, (1+A)^m<=(1+K(r))^m. Consequently, pointwise at actual outputs,

    h_t 1_Bad <= epsilon(r_0) w_t g_m,
    epsilon(r_0)=sup_(r>r0) r^(-kappa)(1+K(r))^m ->0.

Apply (S2), without substituting input r for output r. On a Bad input,

    P_Bad h_t <= C epsilon w_t T <= C' epsilon h_t. (S3)

Choose r_0 so C'epsilon<1/2. The POSITIVE killed-kernel series constructs a
finite H_t on Bad with H_t=2C h_t+P_Bad H_t and H_t<=4C h_t. Extend H_t=0 on
Good and at threshold. This earns the bad Green; its small norm is not assumed.

On Bad, P(w_t+H_t)-(w_t+H_t)<=-C h_t. On Good, (S2) gives

    P H_t <= C epsilon w_t T=o(w_t U),

so the negative good margin survives. Since U>=c r^(-nu) and kappa<=nu, both
regions therefore have the unified corrected section drift

    P(w_t+H_t)-(w_t+H_t) <= -c w_t r^(-nu).        (S4)

For t=nu its right side is -c exp(theta r). The corrected section weight is
bounded by C(1+r)^nu exp(theta r). This is why failure of a uniform T/U ratio
does not defeat the recurrence construction: low-A inputs receive a state-
dependent potential correction, whose feedback cost was proved in (S3).
