The strongest result is now an inference theorem, supported by an exact accounting of the retained evidence

I chose estimating a system’s failure probability as the consumer. The estimator may retain dependence, learn the primitive source parameters, allocate observations adaptively, and use biased estimates. It need not generate independent simulated worlds.

Two linked results emerged:

The exchangeable-probe operation preserves a computable likelihood for the complete acquired experiment. Its adaptive decision history and unordered pair counts form an evidence record; together with the restored cache, they reconstruct every acquired source value without knowing the source probabilities.
There is an information bound for actual mean estimation that can be matched by a cache construction. For the preserved recursive reliability example, the cache is asymptotically minimax optimal under a uniform variance guarantee, even against biased estimators and adaptive parameter learning. Under ordinary absolute mean-squared error, its asymptotic worst-case source cost is within about 1.36 of the best possible.

The ordinary comparison also produced a substantial failure: on the fair six-level example, a conventional product estimator has 5.63 times smaller variance at exactly the same source budget. I retain that result and would use the ordinary estimator in that regime.
