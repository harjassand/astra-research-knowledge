The first large graph was too structured

My first 128-spin graph used fixed shifts and admitted a narrow geometric description. Ordinary transfer methods could exploit that structure. I retained it as a compilation test and replaced it for the acquisition demonstration.

The replacement graph is irregular and connected. I have not proved an expansion bound, a large treewidth or computational hardness for it.

The first endpoint file was incomplete

The first sampling execution left an incomplete output file. The consumer rejected it before calculating an interval. I preserved its bytes and failure record, added explicit flush/close checks, and repeated the fixed computation. The reported result uses the complete 4,096-endpoint record.

Strong alternatives retain their advantages

Exact diagonalization is preferable on the small examples. More generally, the relevant controls include:

conventional linked-cluster and Kirkwood–Thomas algorithms;
variational Monte Carlo with optimized guiding functions;
sparse eigensolvers;
matrix-product methods where the graph permits them;
local convex relaxations supplying energy lower bounds.

A recent primary treatment of local relaxation hierarchies also supplies pertinent lower-bound algorithms, with its stated assumptions and convergence scopes. Lin–Rivera Cardoso–Bondesan.

All these methods may use the same trial function and the certificate derived here. The theorem is available to the ordinary comparator too. I have not established a best-method separation.

The remaining gaps are consequential.

Novelty remains unresolved. The ingredients have substantial prior literature, and the exact assembled certificate needs a more exhaustive comparison.

Independent review remains outstanding. This was a solo construction with self-checks and separately assembled diagnostics, not an independent audit.

The contraction requirement is restrictive. Failure of the influence test proves neither non-mixing nor impossibility, but this certificate then supplies no conclusion.

A learned positive model has not yet earned an advantage here. The executed trial function was constructed analytically. The theorem accepts a learned function after the same global checks, but this run did not demonstrate that learning finds better useful certificates at lower total cost.

The scientific outputs have specific scope. An energy interval does not establish ground-state fidelity. A pressure interval does not automatically give relative estimates of individual rare-event probabilities. Unknown model error must be bounded separately and enters the final enclosure directly.

The strongest surviving consequence is therefore precise: a compact positive trial model can carry a locally checkable certificate for a global spectral computation, and one acquired vector of expectations can support later adaptive computations under that fixed model. The construction supplies this operation with explicit costs and an executed non-enumerative example; the case for a broader scientific advantage still has to be earned.
