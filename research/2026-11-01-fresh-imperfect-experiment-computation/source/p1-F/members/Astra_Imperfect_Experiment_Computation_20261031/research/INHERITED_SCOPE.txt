Fixed Hermitian operators A_j admit chosen full-vector actions. Their unknown
generated algebra has blocks M_r tensor I_m and dimension d=sum r^2. Closing
q Gaussian probes acquires dimension

    R=sum r min(m,qr) <= qd

using kR source actions. Learned central blocks reveal s=min(m,qr). If s<qr,
m=s is exact. Otherwise projected probe energy has Gamma shape

    qmr >= q^2 r^2.

The resulting central trace estimate has one relative-error event over the
entire fixed positive cone, including later adaptive expressions. Optional
conventional regular representation reduces retained dimension to min(R,d).
Rational finite-bit sources have a separate charged arithmetic result.

The specific saturation/estimation combination remains retained. Inverse-
epsilon estimation of one PSD trace and algebra decomposition have substantial
existing predecessors. None of these statements alone establishes numerical
robustness, novelty or a physical experiment.

Preserved failures and limits:
  * Small generic perturbations may destroy exact low-dimensional algebra.
  * Normalizing tiny closure residuals expanded one numerical model to full
    dimension. Dropping them may hide a genuine component.
  * Approximate closure cannot certify unrestricted rational queries:
    diag(0,eta) is close to zero, while division by eta resolves a finite
    difference.
  * Useful prediction does not by itself make arbitrary planning cheap.

The new task-triggered solver does not alter these statements. It targets full
vector solves and one convex design class, permits continuing source access,
uses an explicit accuracy/coercivity scale, and retains a per-task residual
certificate. It does not inherit the older compiler's all-positive-cone trace
event or claim to dominate every small exact algebra.

Complementarity example: a rank-n/2 projector has a two-dimensional generated
algebra but a large numerical image range. Conversely, a generically perturbed
low-rank action can have a large exact algebra while retaining a small useful
image width at a fixed task tolerance.
