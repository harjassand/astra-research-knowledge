## Result: a reusable solver with a rule for further experiments

**The strongest result is a constructive theorem for learning reliable computation from imperfect operator actions without recovering an exact algebra.** The learner builds its own numerical representation, uses it to solve later problems, and expands it when a computed error certificate is insufficient.

At a fixed accuracy scale, the **total number of these expansions can be bounded across an arbitrarily long sequence of adaptive tasks**. Small full-rank perturbations are allowed.

The comparison is more restrained: **the strongest ordinary method tested ties the construction on source-action count.** This investigation earns a robust computational mechanism and a sharper information limit. It does not yet establish a major breakthrough or superiority over strong retaining numerical methods.

## 1. The capability I chose

I chose **repeated reliable inverse computations and continuous design decisions for an unknown operator family**.

The computational task is to solve changing systems

\[
A(\theta)x=b,
\qquad
A(\theta)=\alpha I+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\theta_jB_j,
\]

where the \(B_j\) are fixed unknown Hermitian operators. Both the coefficients \(\theta\) and the right-hand side \(b\) may depend on everything learned previously.

The experiments return imperfect full-vector actions:

\[
\mathcal O_j(v,\delta)=B_jv+e,
\qquad
\|e\|\le \delta\|v\|.
\]

The errors may be biased, correlated, and history-dependent. The ability to obtain the requested tolerance is an actual experimental premise; repeated measurements do not automatically remove systematic error.

For the inverse tasks, the admitted family has a known positive lower bound

\[
A(\theta)\succeq \mu I,\qquad \mu>0.
\]

This can be guaranteed by the construction of the task. For example, starting with the original Hermitian sources, we can form

\[
A(\theta)=\alpha I+\sum_j\theta_j A_j^2,
\qquad \theta\in\Delta_k,\quad \alpha>0.
\]

Then \(\mu=\alpha\). Each imperfect action of \(A_j^2\) is implemented by **two actual actions of \(A_j\)**, with their propagated errors and preparation costs charged. No positive operator or inverse solver is supplied as an oracle.

Repeated systems of this kind are an established target of reduced-basis and Krylov-recycling methods. Those methods therefore provide serious competitors for this investigation. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

### The change in what is learned

The retained knowledge consists of three things:

1. A small space of acquired operator-action directions.
2. A constructed solver using those directions.
3. An error certificate that determines whether the retained information suffices for the current task.

The source remains available. A genuinely new task direction can require another experiment. This continuing-access contract is a deliberate part of the construction.

## 2. Why universal offline compilation can be much more expensive

A supporting theorem makes this distinction precise.

Consider the family

\[
A\in\{I\}\cup\{I+a uu^*:\|u\|=1\},
\qquad 0<a\le 1.
\]

Every matrix has:

- condition number at most two;
- generated algebra dimension at most two;
- an elementary inverse.

Nevertheless, under the actionwise error contract, an admissible oracle can conceal the useful direction \(u\). It can return the identity response \(v\) whenever

\[
|u^*v|\le \frac{\sigma}{a}\|v\|.
\]

For a randomly oriented hidden direction, an uninformed probe typically has overlap of order \(n^{-1/2}\). Until a probe exceeds the error threshold, the learner receives exactly the identity transcript.

The proof handles adaptive randomized acquisition while keeping the unknown source fixed before the learner’s random choices.

If a compiled artifact must subsequently solve **every right-hand side without further source access**, to relative error

\[
\varepsilon<\frac{a}{a+2},
\]

with failure probability at most \(\delta_{\mathrm f}<1/2\), then necessarily

\[
\boxed{
2\,\mathbb E\sum_t
\exp\!\left(-\frac{n\sigma_t^2}{2a^2}\right)
\ge 1-2\delta_{\mathrm f}.
}
\]

Consequently:

- At fixed nonzero tolerance, the required number of experiments can grow exponentially with ambient dimension.
- Under the explicitly declared precision price \(\sigma^{-2}\), expected acquisition cost is at least of order \(n/a^2\).
- Allowing computationally relevant amplitudes \(a\) of order \(\varepsilon\) gives a lower bound of order \(n/\varepsilon^2\).

**Good conditioning and a tiny exact algebra do not guarantee cheap experimental observability.**

Allowing an experiment after the task arrives changes the requirement. When \(a\) is known, the same family obeys

\[
A^{-1}b=\frac{(2+a)b-Ab}{1+a}.
\]

One action on the actual \(b\), at tolerance \(\varepsilon\), gives the requested relative accuracy. This elementary example is supporting evidence for the choice of interface; the general solver below does not receive this identity.

The distinction is scoped. The lower bound concerns worst-case actionwise bounded errors and uniform future **full-vector solves**. It does not automatically apply to independent isotropic noise, average prediction, or the inherited positive-cone **trace** theorem.

## 3. The positive construction

The learner retains an orthonormal matrix \(U\) of acquired directions and imperfect actions of each primitive on those directions.

For a new task, it forms the small trial space

\[
V=\operatorname{span}(U,b).
\]

The current direction of \(b\) outside the cache is prepared and measured directly. Its action is not obtained by dividing a small difference of noisy cached answers.

Let \(Y\) be the assembled action matrix for the current \(B(\theta)\), calibrated so that

\[
\|Y-B(\theta)V\|\le \sigma.
\]

The learner constructs and solves the small system

\[
H=\alpha I+\operatorname{sym}(V^*Y),
\qquad
Hz=V^*b,
\qquad
x=Vz.
\]

Because the true operator is \(\mu\)-coercive,

\[
\lambda_{\min}(H)\ge \mu-\sigma.
\]

Thus the useful solver is actually constructed. Its conditioning comes from the admitted task bound, rather than an assumed well-conditioned hidden basis.

### Every accepted answer has a residual certificate

Set

\[
\tau=\frac{\mu\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon},
\qquad
\sigma=\frac{\tau}{16}.
\]

The acceptance test is

\[
\boxed{
\|b-\alpha x-Yz\|+\sigma\|x\|
\le \tau\|x\|.
}
\]

The left-hand side bounds the true residual \(\|b-A(\theta)x\|\). Therefore

\[
\|x-A(\theta)^{-1}b\|
\le \frac{\tau}{\mu}\|x\|
\le \varepsilon\|A(\theta)^{-1}b\|.
\]

This conclusion remains valid when the task was selected adaptively from previous answers.

### A failed certificate supplies useful information

If the test fails, the computed normalized action

\[
\widehat f=\frac{Yz}{\|x\|}
\]

has distance from the retained space exceeding

\[
g=\tau-2\sigma.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\widehat f=B(\theta)v+e,
\qquad
v=\frac{x}{\|x\|},
\qquad
\|v\|=1,\quad \|e\|\le\sigma.
\]

The learner appends the normalized component of \(\widehat f\) outside \(U\).

This is the operational connection: **a failed computed solve produces a reliably separated direction that can improve later solves**. There is no need to normalize every nonzero closure residual, search over all possible future tasks, or obtain an exact solution snapshot.

The cached linear combination is generally correlated with earlier observations. Independence is unnecessary: its error follows from the acquired bound on the entire action matrix.

## 4. The lifetime acquisition theorem

Let \(N\) be the total number of cache expansions across the task stream.

Suppose the actual task-action images encountered during failed checks lie within distance \(\eta\) of some \(r\)-dimensional subspace, with

\[
\eta+\sigma\le \frac g2.
\]

The learner receives neither that subspace nor \(r\). They are complexity parameters describing the source and encountered tasks.

If \(\|B_j\|\le L\), the construction satisfies

\[
\boxed{
N\le
\min\!\left\{
n,\;
r\max\!\left[
4,\;
2+4\log_2\!\left(\frac{L+\sigma}{g}\right)
\right]
\right\}.
}
\]

This bound applies to every finite prefix, and therefore to an arbitrarily long adaptive sequence at the fixed accuracy scale.

A stronger, convenient sufficient condition is that all primitive ranges admit a common approximation:

\[
\max_j\|(I-P_S)B_j\|\le\eta,
\qquad \dim S=r.
\]

The operators may contain generic full-rank perturbations. Their exact generated algebra need not remain small.

### Why the bound holds

Every added raw image has:

- norm at most \(L+\sigma\);
- distance at least \(g\) from the preceding retained span;
- distance at most \(\eta+\sigma\) from the comparison subspace.

The first two properties force the volume of the accepted vectors to grow. The third limits that volume through their singular values. Combining the inequalities gives the displayed bound.

That determinant argument is established reduced-basis machinery. DeVore–Petrova–Wojtaszczyk provide the relevant lemma, and Binev and colleagues already analyze robustness to imperfect snapshots. The construction here connects that machinery to **actual noisy operator actions, computed failed solves, and charged cache maintenance**. It is not a claim to have invented greedy-width analysis. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### How this handles the earlier failures

Small residuals are handled through a task-dependent error budget. They are not declared exactly zero.

If a later task requests a smaller error or a smaller coercivity floor, previously negligible components can become relevant. The learner then acquires more information and pays for finer precision.

The result consequently does not certify unrestricted rational expressions. The accuracy required to resolve a small denominator remains an explicit resource.

Numerical image width is also complementary to algebra dimension: a rank-\(n/2\) projector has a two-dimensional algebra but a large image range. This construction does not uniformly supersede the earned exact compiler.

## 5. The total cost remains substantial—and explicit

For \(T\) completed tasks and \(N\) expansions, the specified doubling-and-refresh schedule uses at most

\[
\boxed{Q\le k(T+4N)}
\]

primitive vector actions.

This includes failed solves and recalibration. A task already contained in the retained span may require no new action. A fresh task direction generally requires one action per active primitive.

There is an important precision charge. To keep the **whole cached action matrix** within error \(\sigma\), the construction measures its columns at tolerances proportional to

\[
\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\text{cache capacity}}}.
\]

For the declared action price \(\delta^{-p}\), total precision work is bounded by

\[
O_p\!\left(
k\sigma^{-p}
\left[
(N+1)^{1+p/2}
+T(N+1)^{p/2}
\right]
\right).
\]

For \(p=2\), the main terms are \(N^2+TN\). Counting only the vector calls would conceal this cost.

Retained storage is \(O(knN)\) scalars. Execution includes full-vector projections, small dense solves, and writing \(n\)-component answers. The source-action bound does not make these operations free.

The proof models exact internal arithmetic with bounded experimental errors. The package gives a preparation-aware cutoff and a finite-arithmetic specification with explicit rounding budgets. The executed programs use ordinary double precision; they do not constitute a completed outward-rounded implementation or a new formal bit-complexity theorem.

## 6. A constructed downstream design algorithm

To address the prediction-versus-planning obstacle, I constructed a specific optimization consumer.

For unknown positive semidefinite \(B_j\), consider

\[
\min_{\theta\in\Delta_k}
F(\theta),
\qquad
F(\theta)=
c^\top\theta+
b^*\left(\alpha I+\sum_j\theta_jB_j\right)^{-1}b.
\]

This is a convex continuous allocation problem.

The acquired solver supplies \(\widetilde x\) and a residual bound \(\rho\). Cached actions supply approximations to \(B_j\widetilde x\). These produce explicit intervals

\[
g_j(\theta)\in[\widehat g_j-\delta_j,\widehat g_j+\delta_j]
\]

for the true gradient

\[
g_j(\theta)=c_j-x_*^*B_jx_*.
\]

The computed quantity

\[
\boxed{
U(\theta)=
\sum_j\theta_j(\widehat g_j+\delta_j)
-\min_j(\widehat g_j-\delta_j)
}
\]

then certifies

\[
F(\theta)-F^*\le U(\theta).
\]

The search algorithm is explicit: select the smallest estimated gradient coordinate and take a Frank–Wolfe step toward that simplex vertex. The package proves a finite stopping bound and states the source precision needed for it. Frank–Wolfe and its gap analysis are established components; the contribution here is the complete experimental path supplying their numerical inputs. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

A separate consumer prototype reached a requested design gap of \(0.01\) in **16 design iterations**, using **1,026 source actions**, including unsuccessful solver iterations. Its computed gap bound was \(0.0091553\); an independent reference calculation gave \(0.0090647\).

That prototype uses a fully charged noisy Richardson solver. It verifies the consumer interface, while an executed reuse advantage for this optimization application remains unestablished.

## 7. What the numerical comparisons actually show

The main fixture used \(n=160\), four dominant directions, a dense positive full-rank tail, correlated bounded noise, and 80 tasks. The requested relative solution error was \(2\%\).

| Method | Source actions | Retained rank | Precision proxy \(\sum\delta^{-2}\) | Maximum actual relative error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Acquired action cache | **92** | **4** | \(7.94\times10^9\) | \(0.168\%\) |
| Gaussian/SVD basis with the same residual checks | **92** | **4** | \(1.33\times10^{10}\) | \(0.642\%\) |
| Retained correction snapshots | 103 | 4 | \(8.87\times10^9\) | \(0.679\%\) |
| Restart for every task | 691 | — | \(4.20\times10^{10}\) | \(0.381\%\) |

**The strongest tested ordinary comparator ties the proposed construction on source actions and retained dimension.** The precision-proxy difference depends on the tested schedule. It is not a measured time, sample-count, or energy advantage.

Adaptive Nyström methods are another strong comparator: they already acquire useful preconditioners without receiving the effective dimension or useful basis in advance. The present tests do not exhaust that comparison. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Two additional tests resolve concrete concerns:

- **Changing noncommuting tasks:** two operators with independent dense perturbations, 40 mixtures chosen using previous answers, and commutator norm about \(1.495\). The learner used 104 primitive actions, retained four directions, and had maximum relative error \(0.916\%\), below the \(2\%\) target.
- **A previously negligible tail becoming relevant:** tightening accuracy from \(0.02\) to \(0.00025\) caused the cache to grow from 4 to 16 directions over 12 additional tasks. It paid 62 additional actions, including the initial precision refresh, and a much larger precision bill.

The second test is particularly useful: the construction spends new information when the task demands it. It does not turn an old approximation into an unsupported exact statement.

## 8. The competing routes and corrections are preserved

The collaborators developed two serious alternatives.

**Variable-precision spectral acquisition** produced a reusable inverse model for a promised dominant mode plus a small generic tail. Its acquisition cost separates discovering the hidden direction from refining it. However, the strongest conventional noisy-power comparison already attains the same polynomial scaling. An initially tempting polynomial advantage disappeared when that comparator was corrected. Acquiring a certificate that no additional relevant mode exists restores the harder ambient-dimension cost.

**Relative-energy and dissipative computation** produced a useful principle: certain relative generator errors can support arbitrarily long switched computations without model error growing proportionally to elapsed time. The autonomous core already appears in Veselić’s work. The remaining difficulty is economical acquisition of the required geometry; absolute-noise readings cannot certify an unknown zero mode for unrestricted future time. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

Both routes, their ordinary proofs, numerical checks, costs, and failures remain in the archive. The inherited exact-algebra statements and their limitations are preserved separately.

## Scientific conclusion

**Imperfect experiments can acquire a reusable computational method consisting of a numerical action space, an executable solver, and an uncertainty test that determines when further experiments are needed.** Under a numerical-width condition, the additional learning events have a finite lifetime bound, even when exact algebraic structure is destroyed by perturbations.

The lower bound explains why this continuing experimental strategy can be substantially less demanding than compiling answers for every possible future task in advance.

What remains unearned is a major advantage over the strongest existing methods that also retain knowledge. The completed construction, the observability bound, and the corrected comparisons sharpen that objective without claiming it has already been reached.
