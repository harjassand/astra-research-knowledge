## 2. The main theorem: local rate uncertainty can control every rare target

Let \(Q_0\) be the reference generator. Its stationary law \(\pi_0\), maximum exit-rate bound \(\Lambda\), and symmetric Poincaré gap \(g>0\) must be earned from its construction.

Let \(Q\) be the actual generator, with unknown stationary law \(\pi\). Native observations must establish
\[
(1-\eta)q_{0,xy}
\le q_{xy}
\le (1+\eta)q_{0,xy}
\]
on every supported transition.

The two processes must share the declared support. A known kinetic dependence range makes these global inequalities checkable through finitely many local rate cells.

For a nonempty proper target \(A\), define
\[
M_0=\mathbb E_{\pi_0(\cdot\mid A^c)}\tau_A,
\qquad
M=\mathbb E_{\pi(\cdot\mid A^c)}\tau_A.
\]

The change in initial stationary law is included in the theorem.

### First earn the unknown stationary weights

Let \(T_\infty\) bound the stationary-adjoint Poisson inverse on mean-zero bounded functions. Put
\[
a=2\eta\Lambda T_\infty.
\]
When \(a<\tfrac12\),
\[
u=\log\frac{1-a}{1-2a}
\]
certifies
\[
e^{-u}\pi_0(x)\le \pi(x)\le e^u\pi_0(x)
\quad\text{for every state }x.
\]

For the reference class used here—independent bit flips of rate at least \(c\), plus any additional uniform-preserving Markov generator—I derived the compact bound
\[
\boxed{
T_\infty
\le
\frac{\log(4(n-1))+2}{4c}.
}
\]

This follows from cube hypercontractivity, an invariant-Markov Trotter argument and one smoothing step. It does not require enumerating the \(2^n\) states. The underlying hypercontractivity is classical; the proof records the normalization and explains why the nonreversible added generator and its adjoint preserve the required norm bounds. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

A certified bound on the reference’s stationarity residual can improve \(u\). A separate semigroup comparison remains available when the small-perturbation condition above fails.

### Then compare the rare means

Define
\[
v=u-\log(1-\eta),\qquad
K=\sqrt{\frac{2\Lambda}{g}},
\]
and
\[
F=\exp\!\left(
2\,\operatorname{asinh}\!\left[K\sinh(v/2)\right]
\right).
\]

The general result is
\[
\boxed{
\frac{e^{-3u}}{F}
\le \frac{M}{M_0}
\le e^{3u}F.
}
\]

For a rare target, retaining the outside-mass normalization gives a sharper version. Write
\[
p_0=\pi_0(A),\quad b_0=1-p_0,
\]
\[
b_-=1-e^u p_0,\qquad b_+=1-e^{-u}p_0.
\]
If \(b_->0\), then
\[
\boxed{
\frac{e^{-2u}b_0}{b_+F}
\le \frac{M}{M_0}
\le
\frac{e^{2u}b_0F}{b_-}.
}
\]

These inequalities hold **simultaneously for every target** on the same acquired rate-confidence event. Their source-error multiplier contains no factor proportional to the rare mean or to \(1/p_0\).

The actual gap is also earned:
\[
\boxed{
g_{\rm actual}\ge(1-\eta)e^{-2u}g.
}
\]
That allows the earlier finite lifetime-law result to turn the mean interval into an all-deadline additive CDF interval.

“All targets” here describes the stability theorem. Efficient computation for a particular target still requires its reference mass and the relevant sampling or solving operations. The parity target supplies these explicitly.

## 3. The mechanism: compare stationary flows instead of accumulated paths

The representation change is the central construction.

A direct comparison of complete trajectories accumulates discrepancy during the entire long wait. Existing goal-oriented relative-entropy methods already address hitting-time model error, but a computable stopped-path instantiation can become infinite in a rare-event regime. I constructed such an example and also checked the stronger state-dependent compensator version. The broader framework permits other reductions and is not refuted by that example. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

The subsequent coupling audit solved that example’s cylinder case, so its entropy failure is preserved as an informative limitation, rather than evidence of a remaining capability gap.

The surviving theorem works through three finite objects.

### A. A stationary perturbation equation

Set \(E=Q-Q_0\) and \(h=\pi/\pi_0\). With adjoints in \(L^2(\pi_0)\),
\[
h-1=R_0^*E^*h,
\]
where \(R_0^*\) is the reference Poisson inverse on mean-zero inputs.

Reference stationary balance gives
\[
\|E^*\|_{\infty\to\infty}\le2\eta\Lambda.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\|h-1\|_\infty
\le a\bigl(1+\|h-1\|_\infty\bigr),
\]
which yields the stationary-density bound above.

Actual mixing is not assumed in order to prove its own certificate.

### B. Balanced stationary flows

Define
\[
D_0=\operatorname{diag}(\pi_0)(-Q_0),
\qquad
D=\operatorname{diag}(\pi)(-Q).
\]
Both matrices have zero row and column sums. Their off-diagonal magnitudes are stationary transition flows.

The rate and stationary-weight bounds imply a multiplicative flow bound. From it, the reference gap and an edgewise Cauchy–Schwarz inequality give
\[
\left|f^\top(D-D_0)h\right|
\le
2K\sinh(v/2)
\sqrt{(f^\top D_0f)(h^\top Dh)}.
\]

This step requires no supplied cycle decomposition of the actual currents.

Applying the estimate to normalized primal and adjoint hitting potentials gives a scalar quadratic inequality for reciprocal mean lifetimes. Solving it produces \(F\).

### C. The correct change of initial weights

At fixed balanced flow matrix \(D\), the stationary-outside mean has the exact form
\[
M(w,D)=
\frac{w_B^\top D_B^{-1}w_B}{w(B)},
\qquad B=A^c.
\]
The killed inverse is entrywise nonnegative. Pointwise weight bounds therefore control the numerator and the outside-mass denominator separately.

That denominator is essential: the actual and reference means start from different stationary-conditioned distributions.

Relative stationary perturbation theory already has strong general results, including analytically bounded reference constructions. I have not treated full-state matrix computation as a universal limitation of that literature. The result developed here is the explicit compact composition with acquired local rate uncertainty and the Astra rare-output machinery. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="6"}

## 4. Native observations supply the rate information

For a local transition cell \(k\), record
\[
N_k(t)=\text{number of its observed jumps},
\]
and
\[
E_k(t)=\int_0^t
1\{\text{its pre-transition context is present}\}\,ds.
\]

The compensator is \(q_kE_k(t)\). Freeze each counter when its exposure first reaches a predetermined quota \(R\). The exponential counting-process martingale then gives simultaneous confidence intervals.

One useful form is the Chernoff-inverted interval
\[
I_k=
\left\{
q\ge0:
N_k\log\frac{N_k}{qR}-N_k+qR
\le \log\frac{2H}{\alpha}
\right\},
\]
where \(H\) is the number of cells.

This does not require independent cells, a stationary initial state, resets, clamps, primitive labels or observations of failed proposals. The reference is admitted only when every interval fits its proposed relative rate box.

The time required to accumulate those exposures is a separate cost. I proved an arbitrary-start occupation bound using the tilted generator and the acquired gap. That prevents a common accounting error: treating local contexts as freely available independent samples.

## 6. The sharp limitation: local-looking dynamics are insufficient

I strengthened the acquisition obstruction to avoid relying on hidden states, invisible clocks or slow mixing.

Take a 48-bit cylinder inside a 64-bit cube. Compare:

1. Independent unit-rate flips.
2. The same process, with both directions of every edge crossing the target boundary doubled.

Both processes have:

- The same known uniform stationary law.
- A full gap at least \(2\).
- Only one-bit jumps.
- Rates between \(1\) and \(2\).
- Completely observable state histories.

Yet their mean lifetimes are approximately
\[
5.99465\times10^{12},
\qquad
3.06262\times10^{12},
\]
a ratio of **1.95736**.

After \(10^6\) observation units, their complete path laws differ in total variation by at most
\[
3.411\times10^{-7}.
\]
Their deadline CDFs can nevertheless differ by more than \(0.22\).

The changed rate depends on a rare large pattern. Thus a sparse equilibrium graph, fast mixing and one-bit jumps do not establish bounded **kinetic dependence**.

This gives a precise boundary for the positive theorem: **a finite native fit cannot certify the absence of every unmodeled rare gate.** The support and dependence-range promise must come from the source’s construction or other justified information.
