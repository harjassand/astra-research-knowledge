NATIVE RATE INFORMATION AND RARE LIFETIMES UNDER MODEL ERROR
Root construction, continued 1 November 2026

Objective retained
==================
Predict a rare coarse-target mean lifetime and its deadline probabilities from
acquired local information, without waiting for the target event. This note
removes an exact-model restriction of the previous construction. The native
process need not have the fitted Gibbs law, preserve a known measure, or have
a supplied stationary short-cycle decomposition. It must have a justified,
bounded kinetic dependence range and the same transition support as a usable
reference. Complete native state changes and calibrated timestamps are inputs.

This is a finite theorem and an executable mathematical construction, not a
physical validation or a claim of historical priority. Count/exposure learning,
hypercontractivity, Markov potential theory and matrix perturbation are ordinary
ingredients. The addition developed here is their explicit composition into a
compact, target-uniform relative lifetime certificate for a misspecified source.
The new interface is stronger than the previous clamped/marked calibration.

1. Reference and actual source
=============================
Use finite row generators Q0 and Q, with L0=-Q0 and L=-Q. Both are irreducible.
The reference Q0 is executable, has known stationary probability pi0, a paid
maximum exit bound Lambda, and a paid symmetric Poincare gap g>0:
  E0(f)=<f,L0 f>_pi0 >= g Var_pi0(f).
The actual stationary probability pi is NOT an input. Suppose that native rate
confidence intervals imply the simultaneous edge bounds
  (1-eta) q0_xy <= q_xy <= (1+eta) q0_xy,       x!=y,        (1)
where 0<eta<1, and that both sources have the same off-diagonal support.
The statement is deterministic on this acquisition event.

One also needs a reference relative mixing time r_beta, 0<beta<1, satisfying
  |P0(t)(x,y)/pi0(y)-1| <= beta       for all x,y, t>=r_beta. (2)
Section 5 earns r_beta from compact source structure. An oracle giving (2) is
not assumed in the explicit cube construction.

For every nonempty proper target A, B=A^c, define
  p0=pi0(A), b0=1-p0, p=pi(A), b=1-p,
  M0=E_pi0(.|B) tau_A under Q0,
  M =E_pi (. |B) tau_A under Q.
The two means use their respective stationary conditioned laws. No arbitrary
initial-state guarantee is silently substituted. The receiver only needs the
reference target mass and reference conditional sampler; it does not need a
sampler for the unknown actual stationary law.

Put
  u = 2 eta Lambda r_beta/(1-eta) - log(1-beta),
  v = u - log(1-eta),
  K = sqrt(2 Lambda/g),
  F = exp(2 asinh(K sinh(v/2))).                            (3)
The u in (3) may be replaced by ANY smaller valid pointwise stationary-law
bound. Section 10 supplies a substantially sharper compact resolvent bound,
and Section 11 improves the mean comparison for a reversible reference while
still admitting nonreversible actual sources. The numerical forecasts use
these improvements when their conditions hold.

THEOREM A. The following hold simultaneously for every target:
  exp(-u) pi0(x) <= pi(x) <= exp(u) pi0(x),                 (4)
  g_actual >= (1-eta) exp(-2u) g,                          (5)
  exp(-3u)/F <= M/M0 <= exp(3u) F.                         (6)
There is no multiplier M, 1/p or |Omega| in the mean sensitivity bound.
The reference mixing and Lambda/g constants can still be large; they are paid.

For a rare target, a sharper asymmetric version is useful. Let
  p_minus=exp(-u)p0, p_plus=min(1,exp(u)p0),
  b_minus=1-p_plus, b_plus=1-p_minus.
If b_minus>0, then
  exp(-2u) b0/(b_plus F) <= M/M0
                         <= exp(2u) b0 F/b_minus.         (7)
Thus the effective log factor for very rare A is approximately 2u+log F,
rather than 3u+log F. The exactly retained b factors prevent a normalization
error when the target is not very rare.

2. Acquiring the unknown stationary weights indirectly
=====================================================
Metzler matrices (nonnegative off-diagonal entries) have positive semigroups,
and entrywise order is preserved by their exponentials. From (1), including
the diagonal exit rates, one obtains
  (1-eta)Q0 - 2eta Lambda I <= Q <=
  (1+eta)Q0 + 2eta Lambda I.
Consequently, entrywise,
  exp(-2eta Lambda t) P0((1-eta)t) <= P(t) <=
  exp( 2eta Lambda t) P0((1+eta)t).                         (8)
Set t=r_beta/(1-eta), and multiply (8) on the left by the unknown stationary
row pi. Since pi P(t)=pi and every reference row in (2) lies between the same
multiples of pi0,
  exp(-2eta Lambda t)(1-beta) pi0 <= pi <=
  exp( 2eta Lambda t)(1+beta) pi0.
The symmetric logarithmic bound (4) follows from
log(1+beta)<=-log(1-beta). This step requires no actual mixing estimate and no
actual stationary distribution calculation, avoiding a circular certificate.

3. Comparing balanced flows without a short-cycle promise
========================================================
Let D0=diag(pi0)L0 and D=diag(pi)L. Their off-diagonal negatives are balanced
stationary flows F0_xy=pi0(x)q0_xy and F_xy=pi(x)q_xy. Both D matrices have
zero row and column sums. Equations (1),(4) imply
  exp(-v)F0_xy <= F_xy <= exp(v)F0_xy.                     (9)
Hence
  |F_xy-F0_xy| <= 2sinh(v/2) sqrt(F0_xy F_xy).             (10)

For real f,h, balance permits replacing f by f-pi0(f) in
f^T(D-D0)h. Edge Cauchy-Schwarz and the reference gap give
  |f^T(D-D0)h|
  <= 2sinh(v/2)
       sqrt(sum_xy F0_xy (f_x-pi0(f))^2)
       sqrt(sum_xy F_xy (h_x-h_y)^2)
  <= 2sinh(v/2) sqrt(2Lambda/g)
       sqrt( (f^T D0 f) (h^T D h) ).                      (11)
Here sums are over ordered distinct pairs. The second energy sum is exactly
2h^T D h because F is balanced. This estimate is not an assumption about an
unobserved decomposition of the actual currents. It follows from the reference
gap and the acquired edge bounds.

Introduce the auxiliary generator -diag(pi0)^(-1)D. It has stationary law pi0.
Let M_D be its mean for A. Set C0=1/(b0 M0), C_D=1/(b0 M_D).
The normalized primal and adjoint potentials used in the previous MAIN_PROOF
belong to the same affine space {h:h|A=1, pi0(h)=0}; their energies equal C0
or C_D. The mixed weak-equation identity is
  C_D-C0 = (h0*)^T(D-D0)h_D.                              (12)
To see it directly, pair the equation for h_D with h0*, and use that h_D-h0
vanishes on A and has pi0-mean zero. The adjoint equation for h0* eliminates
the corresponding D0 term. Applying (11) gives
  |C_D-C0| <= 2K sinh(v/2) sqrt(C0 C_D).
Writing z=sqrt(C_D/C0), the inequalities |z-1/z|<=2K sinh(v/2)
are equivalent to
  F^(-1) <= M_D/M0 <= F.                                 (13)
No extra small-error condition is needed for this step.

At fixed balanced D, for any positive stationary weight vector w with total
mass one, the stationary-conditioned mean is exactly
  M(w,D)= w_B^T D_B^(-1) w_B / w(B).                      (14)
The killed inverse D_B^(-1) is entrywise nonnegative. Since pi/pi0 lies between
exp(-u) and exp(u), (14) implies
  exp(-2u)b0/b <= M/M_D <= exp(2u)b0/b.                   (15)
Bounding b/b0 between exp(-u) and exp(u) proves (6); bounding it using p0
proves (7). This explicitly accounts for the change of initial law.

4. Gap and deadline transfer
============================
The actual symmetric energy satisfies
  E_actual(f) >= (1-eta)exp(-u) E0(f).
Also Var_pi(f)<=exp(u)Var_pi0(f), by minimizing over constants. These prove
(5). Thus an actual gap needed for the lifetime-law theorem is earned from
the same rate event, rather than assumed to justify that event.

Suppose the reference screened receiver has certified
  m0_lo <= M0 <= m0_hi.
Apply (6) or (7) to obtain m_lo<=M<=m_hi. Let
  g_lo=(1-eta)exp(-2u)g,
  delta_hi=1/[g_lo b_minus m_lo].
This requires b_minus>0. When delta_hi<=1/4, the previously proved lifetime-law theorem gives a fully
specified all-deadline certificate. Define
  D(z)=min(1, (4z/pi)log(1+1/(4z^2))+48z/pi),
  a=min(1, [D(delta_hi)+p_plus]/b_minus + p_plus).
Then for every d>=0,
  max(0,1-exp(-d/m_hi)-a)
     <= P_pi(.|B)(tau_A<=d)
     <= min(1,1-exp(-d/m_lo)+a).                           (16)
D is increasing on 0<z<=1/4, so using delta_hi is valid. The final p_plus
allows replacement of the exponential scale bM by M. This is an additive CDF
certificate, not relative accuracy for an extremely small early failure risk.
There is no union cost over deadlines. Monte Carlo uncertainty of a new mean
query still needs its own allocated error probability.

5. Paying for reference relative mixing
=======================================
For any finite reference, the symmetric gap gives L2(pi0) contraction for P0
and its adjoint. Applying it between point masses gives
  |P0(t)(x,y)/pi0(y)-1|
     <= exp(-gt)/sqrt(pi0(x)pi0(y))
     <= exp(-gt)/pi0_min.
Thus r_beta=log(1/(beta*pi0_min))/g always suffices. An explicitly known lower
bound on pi0_min may be used. This is sometimes useful but wasteful on a cube.

Sharper cube construction. Suppose pi0 is uniform on n>=2 bits and
  Q0=H+J,
where H independently flips each bit at rate c>0, and J itself is a Markov
generator preserving uniform measure. Arbitrary driven preserving channels
are allowed in J. The reference has g>=2c.

Classical two-point/cube hypercontractivity gives
  ||exp(tH)||_{Lp->Lq}<=1 whenever q-1<=(p-1)exp(4ct).
An invariant Markov semigroup exp(tJ) contracts every Lp norm by Jensen.
In the finite-dimensional Trotter product, advance the exponent after each H
step, and use the J contraction at that exponent. Taking the product limit
proves the same hypercontractivity for P0(t). It also holds for P0*(t), because
J* is another uniform-preserving Markov generator.

Take q=n and t0=log(n-1)/(4c). Uniform finite-space embedding gives
  ||P0(t0)||_{2->infinity} <= 2^(n/n)=2,
and adjoint duality gives ||P0(t0)||_{1->2}<=2. This includes n=2,t0=0.
Writing Pi0 for stationary projection and inserting an L2 contraction between
these two smoothing operators yields
  ||P0(2t0+t)-Pi0||_{1->infinity} <= 4 exp(-2ct).
The left norm, including for a signed kernel, is exactly
  max_xy |P0(2t0+t)(x,y)/pi0(y)-1|.
Consequently the fully paid choice
  r_beta = log(4(n-1)/beta)/(2c)                           (17)
suffices. The state space has 2^n elements, but this penalty is logarithmic in n.
No reversibility of J is required, and no general assertion that extra driving
improves a log-Sobolev constant is used: the actual Trotter contraction proves
the needed norm statement.

For (17), let a0=eta Lambda/[c(1-eta)]. The beta that minimizes the displayed
u is beta=a0/(1+a0); substitute it in (3). Other explicit beta values are
equally valid. The generic gap/pi_min formula has the same minimizer with
a0=2eta Lambda/[g(1-eta)]. Numerical optimization is not a proof requirement.

6. Native local observations earn (1)
=====================================
A kinetic architecture specifies a finite list of observable local transition
cells k. Each cell fixes at most d bits, gives a changed-bit mask, and has a
constant unknown native rate q_k whenever that context occurs. Every possible
off-diagonal transition belongs to one cell; overlapping hidden primitive
contributions are combined into their observable aggregate rate. Support and
dependence range are model promises, not conclusions from a finite record.

Record only actual state changes, their changed-bit masks, and timestamps.
Let N_k be the selected jump count and E_k its pre-jump occupation exposure.
The compensator is q_k E_k. The exponential counting-process martingale is
  exp(theta N_k-(exp(theta)-1)q_k E_k).
Freeze cell k when E_k first reaches a predetermined R, including a partial
holding interval if needed. For z=log(2H/alpha), an exact Chernoff-inverted
confidence interval is
  I_k={q>=0: N_k log[N_k/(qR)]-N_k+qR <= z},               (18)
with the usual continuous interpretation at N_k=0. Monotonicity of the two
Poisson tails and the martingale bound give coverage >=1-alpha jointly for
the H cells. No independence between cells, stationary initial law, hidden
channel mark or null proposal observation is assumed.

An equally valid simpler version uses a justified q_min>0. For 0<gamma<1,
  R >= 3 log(2H/alpha)/(q_min gamma^2)                     (19)
ensures |N_k/(R q_k)-1|<=gamma jointly. Invert this to intervals
  [N_k/(R(1+gamma)), N_k/(R(1-gamma))].

For any chosen tractable reference, first ensure q0_k is constant on every
predeclared kinetic cell. Refine the cells to include its dependence if needed;
alternatively verify the following inclusion against every reference rate
represented by that cell. Then verify interval inclusion
  I_k subset [(1-eta)q0_k,(1+eta)q0_k] for every k.         (20)
Then (1) holds on the same event. The reference may be selected from the data,
because the simultaneous intervals cover the actual cells before that choice.
It must still supply the proved stationarity, gap, mixing and target-query
operations. If (20) fails, this theorem gives no narrow reference certificate.

Exposure completion and observation cost are separate from coverage. In a
class passing a fixed admission margin, (4),(5) give local context masses at
least exp(-u) times their reference masses and a positive actual gap. Standard
finite occupation bounds then pay for reaching all quotas. Section 7 spells
out a simple explicit bound. No rare-event boundary is visited by design.

Important limitation: selected clean rates suffice for the EXACT Gibbs/cycle
family in the companion construction, but do not by themselves earn (1) for
this larger class. All aggregate local rate cells must be acquired to use this
general certificate. The small native run in passive/control is labeled as an
exact-family demonstration and is not misrepresented as a general admission.

7. A finite exposure bound and asymptotic resource consequence
============================================================
An explicit exponential occupation bound works from an arbitrary initial
state. Let G be any proved actual symmetric gap lower bound, let pi_min>=pmin,
and let a context C have stationary mass p_C>=p_star. Write a unit vector as
f=a*1+h with pi(h)=0. For s=G/2, the elementary inequality
  (a+h)^2 >= a^2/2-h^2
and the Poincare inequality give
  E_actual(f)+s*pi(1_C*f^2)
  >= (G-s)||h||^2 + (s*p_C/2)*a^2
  >= s*p_C/2.
Thus the tilted semigroup exp(t(Q-s*1_C)) has L2 norm at most
exp(-s*p_C*t/2). Feynman-Kac and Cauchy-Schwarz imply
  E_nu exp(-s E_C(T)) <= ||dnu/dpi||_2 exp(-s*p_C*T/2).
For a deterministic start, ||dnu/dpi||_2<=pmin^(-1/2). Chernoff then gives
  P(E_C(T)<p_C*T/4) <= pmin^(-1/2) exp(-G*p_C*T/8).
Consequently all H quotas complete by a deterministic horizon with probability
at least 1-alpha_occ if
  T >= max{4R/p_star,
           [8/(G*p_star)]*[log(H/alpha_occ)+0.5*log(1/pmin)]}. (21)
Use G=(1-eta)exp(-2u)g, p_star=exp(-u)*reference_context_floor,
and pmin=exp(-u)*pi0_min from Theorem A. No equilibrium sample or burn-in is
required. These are fixed source-class bounds: a reference box that the actual
generator does not satisfy earns no such uniform completion-time guarantee.
For completeness, the simpler stationary covariance bound also gives
  P(min_k E_k(T)<p_star*T/2)<=8H/(G*p_star*T).
Coverage (18) or (19) itself is valid before either occupation argument is used.

For the uniform cube reference, bounded-size contexts have reference masses
at least 2^-d. With c,g fixed, Lambda=O(n), H=O(n), fixed d, the improved
stationary estimate in Section 10 and (3) give
  log(F)+2u = O(eta n^(3/2) log(n)).
Thus eta on the order of epsilon/[n^(3/2)log(n)] suffices for a fixed
rare-target multiplicative tolerance. To actually admit a perturbed source,
its distance from the reference must leave room for statistical uncertainty;
estimating at the exact tolerance boundary need not ever certify admission.

For a fixed margin and a known constant lower local rate, (19) gives occupation
R=O(n^3 log^2(n) epsilon^-2 log(n/alpha)). The second term in (21) is
also an explicit polynomial. Native elapsed time is polynomial in n, accuracy
and failure probability. Expected observed native jump count adds a factor
O(n). If an explicit O(n)-rate uniformization implementation is supplied, its
proposal work has the same bound. The generator alone does NOT bound arbitrary
unobserved null clocks or total physical implementation work. None of these
terms contains 2^m for a cylinder fixing m
bits. At moderate sizes the constants can exceed the rare mean; polynomial
scaling alone is not a practical speed claim. The numerical budget script
reports this cost instead of equating computational simulation with physical
acquisition. Target counting/conditional sampling and screened simulation are
paid as in the previous receiver.

8. What the extension does and does not establish
================================================
The previous theorem required true and fitted weights and flows to share a
known short-cycle architecture. Here the native law may violate the reference
Gibbs measure and current decomposition. A native rate box, a compact relative
mixing bound and a mixed-flow energy comparison replace those requirements.

This does not infer kinetic locality from equilibrium locality. The companion
rare-boundary counterexample has a uniform product law and gap at least two,
yet changes a rare mean by nearly a factor of two while short complete histories
are statistically indistinguishable. Its rates depend on a large rare pattern.
An unmodeled rare gate therefore defeats a passive global guarantee even with
no hidden states. The known kinetic range/support premise is substantive.

Exact arrival timestamps and full state changes are an observation interface.
Finite bandwidth that merges distinct jumps requires another error model.
No preservation of native dynamics under clamping is required here. No future
source drift is covered. Current-state conditioned risk is a different task
from the stationary-conditioned mean and deadline law proved here.

10. Sharper stationary transfer by a compact resolvent bound
==========================================================
The single-time Metzler comparison is robust but pays an avoidable logarithm
of the error tolerance. Ordinary stationary perturbation theory gives a better
route when combined with the compact hypercontractive bounds already earned.
This refinement is credited to that established machinery; the proof here
makes every norm and source condition explicit.

Write E=Q-Q0 and take adjoints in L2(pi0). Let h=pi/pi0, and let
  R0* = integral_0^infinity (P0*(t)-Pi0) dt
on the mean-zero subspace. Define T_infinity as an upper bound on the induced
L-infinity norm of R0* RESTRICTED TO MEAN-ZERO INPUTS. The norm of P0*-Pi0 on
unrestricted inputs may exceed the restricted contraction used below.
Stationarity and E1=0 give
  L0*(h-1)=E*h,  pi0(E*f)=0 for every f,
  h-1=R0* E*h.                                             (22)
Reference stationary balance bounds the absolute row sums of E*:
  ||E*||_(infinity->infinity)
  <= max_x eta[lambda0(x)+sum_(y!=x)pi0(y)q0_yx/pi0(x)]
  <= 2eta Lambda.                                         (23)
Put a=2eta Lambda T_infinity and z=||h-1||_infinity. Then
  z<=a(1+z).
If a<1/2, this proves the alternative stationary bound
  u_res = log((1-a)/(1-2a)).                              (24)
Use the smaller of (24) and the original u in every subsequent theorem.

The same identity can exploit a certified stationarity residual. If native
rate intervals imply d>=||E*1||_infinity, then
  h-1=R0*E*1+R0*E*(h-1),
  z <= T_infinity*d/(1-a),                               (25)
provided a<1. When the right side is less than one, u=-log(1-z) is valid.
More generally any earned b>=||R0*E*1||_infinity may replace T_infinity*d.
The universal d=2eta Lambda recovers (24). If d=0 is known exactly, pi0 is
already stationary for Q and uniqueness gives pi=pi0 without a<1.

A residual of the fitted point estimate is insufficient. One must bound
  (E*1)(x)=sum_(y!=x)[pi0(y)/pi0(x)*(q_yx-q0_yx)
                                  -(q_xy-q0_xy)]
over the whole simultaneous rate box and all allowed contexts. Algebraically
identical terms may cancel before interval evaluation. Local triangle bounds
are a paid efficient option; sharper maxima of local-factor sums require an
explicit algorithm. No unobserved residual is supplied for free.

Earning T_infinity. The reference gap alone yields, for mean-zero f,
  ||P0*(t)f||_infinity
    <= min(1,pi0_min^(-1/2)exp(-gt))*||f||_infinity.
Integrating gives
  T_infinity <= [1+0.5log(1/pi0_min)]/g.                   (26)
For the driven uniform cube reference of Section 5, one smoothing step
suffices: ||P0*(t0)||_(2->infinity)<=2, t0=log(n-1)/(4c), and a bounded input
already has L2 norm at most its L-infinity norm. Therefore
  ||P0*(t)f||_infinity
    <= min(1,2exp[-2c(t-t0)])||f||_infinity
for mean-zero inputs (use the first bound before t0). Integration gives
  T_infinity <= [log(4(n-1))+2]/(4c).                     (27)
This one-smoothing constant was independently sharpened in the native audit.
Equations (24),(27) give u=O(eta*n*log n) for small eta, with no log(1/eta).
The updated polynomial acquisition scaling in Section 7 uses this bound.

Additional product-reference gain. Suppose Q0 independently flips bit i at
rate c_i, and the actual source has ONLY the same single-bit jump support,
with arbitrary state-dependent rates q_i(x) in [(1-eta)c_i,(1+eta)c_i]. Its
locality promise is still needed to acquire these rates without rare contexts.
Let delta_i(x)=q_i(x)-c_i. Then
  E*1=sum_i f_i,  f_i(x)=delta_i(x^i)-delta_i(x),
and f_i is antisymmetric in bit i. Product-noise contraction gives exactly
  ||P0*(t)f_i||_infinity <= exp(-2c_i t)||f_i||_infinity.
Since ||f_i||_infinity<=2eta c_i,
  ||R0*E*1||_infinity <= sum_i eta = n eta.                (28)
Thus the improved stationary allowance is
  z_prod=n eta/(1-a),  u_prod=-log(1-z_prod),              (29)
when a<1 and z_prod<1. This is a bound for arbitrary allowed local functions;
it does not assume the actual law is Gibbs, product, reversible, or supplied.
For equal c_i and a coordinate-cylinder target, the ordinary reference mean
can also be computed exactly by a Hamming-weight birth-death recurrence. That reference computation is not
offered as a new capability; transferring it to the nonreversible unknown
actual source is the remaining operation.

Stronger ordinary construction found subsequently. In this product-reference,
single-bit-support subclass, biased-bit stationary coupling actually yields
  (1-eta)^n <= pi(x)/pi0(x) <= (1+eta)^n for EVERY x,       (29a)
and hence u=-n*log(1-eta), without a resolvent smallness condition. To prove it,
first sandwich the native process between independent bits having up/down
rates c_i(1-eta),c_i(1+eta), and the opposite pair, using common clocks and
state-dependent thinning. This preserves coordinate order without requiring
native attractiveness. Take a stationary joint coupling. The all-zero event
is a down-set, so its probability is bracketed by ((1-eta)/2)^n and
((1+eta)/2)^n. Relabeling the bits by x->x XOR z leaves the same rate bounds
and proves the assertion for any point z. This improves (29), and is the
stationary certificate used in the final coded-target instantiation. The
resolvent bounds remain useful for the broader driven-reference class.

11. Reversible reference, nonreversible actual source
====================================================
When D0=diag(pi0)L0 is symmetric, the general mixed-flow comparison can be
sharpened. The symmetry requirement is on the reference alone. Keep the true
balanced D=diag(pi)L, and write
  S=(D+D^T)/2,  A_skew=(D-D^T)/2,
  kappa=K*(exp(v)-1),  K=sqrt(2Lambda/g).
Flow comparison gives exp(-v)D0<=S<=exp(v)D0 as quadratic forms.

To bound A_skew, use (11) in both orders and average. Since D0 is symmetric,
its skew part vanishes. Also E_D=E_S and E0<=exp(v)E_S, so
  |f^T A_skew h| <= kappa sqrt((f^T S f)(h^T S h)).        (30)
All forms annihilate constants; the centering used in (11) remains legitimate
even when f,h are killed functions extended by zero on A.

The killed matrix S_B is positive definite. Put
  J=S_B^(-1/2)(A_skew)_B S_B^(-1/2).
It is real skew-symmetric with ||J||<=kappa. For every real vector z,
  z^T(I+J)^(-1)z = z^T(I-J^2)^(-1)z,
so the quadratic form lies between ||z||^2/(1+kappa^2) and ||z||^2.
Apply this to z=S_B^(-1/2)(pi0)_B and use (14). The auxiliary means obey
  M_S/(1+kappa^2)<=M_D<=M_S,
  exp(-v)M0<=M_S<=exp(v)M0.
Combining with the weight comparison (15) proves, when b_minus>0,
  exp(-2u-v)*b0/[b_plus*(1+kappa^2)] <= M/M0
                                      <= exp(2u+v)*b0/b_minus. (31)
Intersect (31) with the general bounds if that is tighter. For all targets
one may replace b0/b by exp(+-u). The penalty from actual circulation is
quadratic in kappa, rather than first order, in this reversible-reference
construction. This is elementary skew-resolvent algebra, not a claim that
nonreversibility has been removed from the actual source.

Support matters: a reversible reference has reverse-closed support. The old
model with a one-way simultaneous pair-cycle jump cannot use this refinement
without a reference/source architecture that already includes the reverse
edge. It remains covered by the general driven-reference theorem.

The finite-error scaling must keep the quadratic term. With the general cube
bound u=O(eta*n*log n), the rare lower log loss is
O(eta*n*log n+eta^2*n^3*log^2 n), not merely its first-order term. For a product
reference, (29) replaces u by O(n*eta) while its contraction margin holds;
the loss is O(n*eta+n^3*eta^2). Thus the two-sided tolerance generally needs
eta=O(min{epsilon/n,sqrt(epsilon)/n^(3/2)}) together with the explicit a<1
and z_prod<1 conditions. No optimality of this sufficient scaling is claimed.

The matrix mechanism is established: Duncan, Lelievre and Pavliotis (2016),
Variance Reduction Using Nonreversible Langevin Samplers, Lemma 3 and
equations (29),(31), DOI 10.1007/s10955-016-1491-2,
https://www.ma.imperial.ac.uk/~pavl/nonreversible.pdf, give the analogous
symmetric/skew inverse identity. Here it is applied to killed balanced flows
and combined with acquired stationary-density uncertainty.

Together, (27)-(29) and (31) materially improve finite acquisition budgets
near a product reference. They retain the coarse target, unknown actual
stationary law, native unmarked acquisition, nonreversible source, and finite
deadline output. They specialize the reference class; the more general driven
reference theorem remains available with its explicitly larger constants.

12. Stronger ordinary baseline and choice of target
==================================================
During the continuation, an ordinary monotone coupling was found to solve the
product-reference CYLINDER subcase directly. If every actual bit-flip rate is
between c(1-eta) and c(1+eta), biased independent-bit processes can be coupled
below and above the actual process even if its rate functions are nonmonotone.
Stationary coupling orders hitting times of a coordinate down-set. Dividing
the resulting unconditional mean bounds by the correct outside masses gives
explicit cylinder mean bounds from biased birth-death chains. The full proof
and executed comparison are in passive/computation/CYLINDER_COUPLING_BASELINE.md.
That solved subcase is retained as a baseline, not offered as the new opening.
The same coupling also gives the target-independent pointwise stationary
bounds (29a), so it is deliberately retained in the stronger code construction.
Only its DIRECT ordering of hitting times is lost for the new target.

The stronger executed reference target in passive/code_target is a nontrivial
linear code in 80 bits, specified by 64 independent parity constraints. It has
2^16 configurations and mass 2^-64. It is not a coordinate cylinder or a
coordinate-order monotone set, even after bit complements. Its mass and
conditional sampler are exact. The independent reference dynamics do lump by
syndrome, so ordinary character sums and MacWilliams weight enumeration give
an exact reference mean; the short-excursion receiver is checked against it.
General local state-dependent actual rates do not lump by syndrome. The
target-uniform transfer above supplies the bridge without assuming a false
actual quotient process or waiting for a parity-target arrival.

This does not prove that every equally informed specialized algorithm fails.
It identifies precisely what the two demonstrated ordinary constructions
supply: biased-bit coupling solves the cylinder; coding theory solves the
reference. The present acquired-flow certificate transfers that reference
answer to the admissible nonreversible, non-Gibbs actual source.
