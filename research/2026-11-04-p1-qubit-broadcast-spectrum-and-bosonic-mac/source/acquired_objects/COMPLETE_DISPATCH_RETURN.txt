Let \(\mathcal P_\Gamma\) be the profile of a qubit channel
\(\Gamma:M_2\to M_2\), and define
\[

These are ordinary, unassisted quantum capacities, in qubits per channel use.

Thus **the unitary-qubit profile is the unique profile in this class that forces positive ordinary quantum capacity in every finite realization**. The upper endpoint \(Q(\Gamma)\) remains symbolic. This theorem determines the entire range compatible with the profile without supplying an algorithm to evaluate that endpoint.

### The exact output-dimension cost

## 3. The central difficulty and its resolution

The previous full-Choi construction had a genuine failure at singular support. Its vacuum/single-excitation Schur-complement condition excluded every rank-deficient Choi matrix. Replacing a full-space eigenvalue by a support eigenvalue would not repair that condition.

The successful construction changes the space in which the positive joint state is built.

### Step A: represent the Choi support by graphs

Write
\[
S=\operatorname{supp}J_\Gamma
\subset \mathbb C^2_B\otimes\mathbb C^2_R.
\]
Trace preservation gives full support on \(R\). Nonunitarity gives
\(\dim S\ge2\), because a rank-one Choi matrix of a qubit-to-qubit TP channel represents a unitary.

The first key lemma is that some Bob contraction
\[
(\langle b|\otimes I_R)|_S:S\longrightarrow R
\]
is onto.

The proof is elementary but decisive. If every contraction had rank at most one, the two-coordinate matrix pencil would have either a common image or a common covector. A common image contradicts full reference support. A common covector contradicts the injectivity of the inclusion of a support space of dimension at least two.

After choosing such a coordinate, \(S\) is spanned by graph subspaces
\[
G_A=\{\,|0\rangle v+|1\rangle Av:v\in R\,\}.
\]
A section supplies one graph; varying that section by kernel vectors supplies enough additional graphs to span all of \(S\).

### Step B: prolong the graphs symmetrically

For each graph define
\[
W_k(A)v
=
\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^k}|x\rangle\otimes A^{|x|}v.
\]

This vector is symmetric in its \(k\) Bob systems. Every one-Bob/reference slice belongs to \(S\): fixing the other bits at Hamming weight \(\ell\) leaves
\[
|0\rangle A^\ell v+|1\rangle A^{\ell+1}v.
\]

Let \(\mathcal K_k(S)\) be the symmetric space satisfying these support constraints, and introduce \(k\) ancillary qubits:
\[
\mathcal D_k
=
\operatorname{Sym}^k(\mathbb C^2_{\rm anc})
\otimes\mathcal K_k(S).
\]
Its dimension obeys
\[
\dim\mathcal D_k\le2(k+1)^2.
\]

Now contract output pairs \(2,\ldots,k\) against Bell bras. The resulting map
\[
C_k:\mathcal D_k\longrightarrow \mathbb C^2_{\rm anc}\otimes S
\]
is **onto**.

The all-zero ancillary vector produces the first ancillary component of each graph. The one-excitation Dicke vector produces the second component plus a term already obtainable from the first. Subtract that term, then use the fact that the graphs span \(S\).

This surjectivity is the operation that the old construction lacked.

## 4. Why this gives the entire capacity spectrum

The earlier greatest-representative theorem gives
\[
Q(E)\le Q(\Gamma)

throughout a profile with a qubit representative. If its normalized algebra is commutative, the greatest representative is measure-and-prepare and all capacities in the class are zero.

For a nonunitary \(\Gamma\), choose a positive \(s_0\) furnished by the two-receiver construction. Then
\[
Q(F_{s_0}\Gamma)=0,
\qquad
Q(F_1\Gamma)=Q(\Gamma),
\]
because \(F_1\) simply appends a maximally mixed ancillary qubit.

The path \(s\mapsto F_s\Gamma\), for \(s\in[s_0,1]\), stays inside the exact profile and has fixed output dimension four. Finite-dimensional quantum capacity is continuous in diamond norm, so it assumes every intermediate value. No monotonicity in \(s\) is needed. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

For the unitary profile, every faithful finite realization has positive coherent information. The earlier finite witness remains useful here: such a realization can be written

Meanwhile \(F_s\) tends to a pure replacer as \(s\downarrow0\). Thus the unitary-profile capacities approach zero while remaining positive, consistent with the established complementary-depolarizing phenomenon. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

### Rational descriptions require a separate statement

There are only countably many rational channel descriptions, so they cannot attain every real number in a nondegenerate interval.

For rational \(\Gamma\), rational filter parameters give dense capacity values along the continuous path. Therefore:

- For a noisy profile, the **closure** of the rational-realization spectrum is \([0,Q(\Gamma)]\), with both endpoints attained.
- For the unitary profile, that closure is \([0,1]\); zero is an unattained infimum and one is attained.

The intermediate-value argument establishes existence over arbitrary real/complex channel coefficients. It does not provide an effective inverse of the capacity function or a rational channel for every prescribed exact capacity.
