## 1. The scientific question now resolved

The question was:

> Can finite observations of an apparatus with unresolved long memory identify a bounded intervention that produces sustained useful work, while accounting for how that intervention changes subsequent observations?

For the specified reciprocal resistor–capacitor class, the answer is yes. The procedure acquires two passive moments and an actual response scale, then continuously operates a bounded controller. It returns a positive certificate when the acquired signal supports one; otherwise it returns `UNKNOWN`.

The representation changes the learning target. Recovering every hidden relaxation rate would require resolving features that may evolve on inaccessible timescales. The useful target here is the relation between **a finite physical action, its conjugate work observation, and the predictable part of the next such observation**.

This also changes the treatment of measurement backaction. The apparatus boundary includes its installed measurement loading. The construction does not infer an inaccessible “bare” law by subtracting an unknown detector disturbance. A separate extension handles a specified active detector-noise contribution by acquiring the response directly.

### A physical architecture fixes what “the same law” means

For the installed reciprocal RC network, Kirchhoff’s law gives

\[
C\,dv=-Gv\,dt+eI\,dt+\sqrt{2\theta G}\,dB,
\qquad
V=e^\top v,
\qquad
\theta=k_BT.
\]

Here \(C\) is positive definite on the dynamic subspace, \(G\) is symmetric positive semidefinite, and \(e\) identifies the controlled terminal pair. All fixed passive loading remains connected.

The autonomous circuit matrices and noise law stay fixed as the delivered current changes. The current enters through the additive term \(eI\). Feedback changes the state distribution, and the work calculation includes that change.

This is a structural circuit contract. A covariance fit alone does not establish it. Actual hardware would still need its linearity, loading, current tracking, timing, and noise behavior characterized over the operating range.

The finite passive-acquisition theorem also retains an explicit initial-energy resource:

\[
E_0\le e_0\theta.
\]

It permits an unknown nonthermal initial state within that bound. It does not infer the bound from an incomplete terminal record.

## 2. The decisive object is the work of a closed current cycle

Choose a cycle duration \(\tau\), with charge shape

\[
\phi(t)=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{\tau}\right),
\qquad
\psi(t)=\phi'(t)
=\frac{\pi}{\tau}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi t}{\tau}\right).
\]

During cycle \(k\), deliver current proportional to

\[
I(k\tau+t)=b_k\psi(t).
\]

Because \(\phi(0)=\phi(\tau)=0\), the pulse returns its delivered charge to its starting value. Both legs are part of the action and its work accounting.

The corresponding unforced work observable is

\[
F_k=\int_0^\tau \psi(t)V^0(k\tau+t)\,dt.
\]

The counterfactual \(V^0\) is used in the proof as a coupling with the same initial state and noise. It is never supplied to the learner, physically replayed, or treated as a measurement oracle.

For the thermal RC class, the stationary terminal covariance has the form

\[
C_V(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-a|t|}\,\mu(da),
\qquad \mu\ge0.
\]

The filter theorem applies to every such positive finite mixture, including arbitrarily many arbitrarily slow modes.

Let

\[
c_n=\operatorname{Cov}(F_0,F_n),
\qquad
S(\omega)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}c_ne^{-in\omega}.
\]

I derived the exact inequalities

\[
c_n\le0\quad(n\ge1),
\qquad
\sum_{n\ge1}|c_n|\le\frac{c_0}{3},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\frac{c_0}{3}\le S(0)\le S(\omega)\le S(\pi)\le\frac32c_0,
\qquad
S(\pi)\le3S(0).
}
\]

Consequently, every nondegenerate finite covariance matrix of these observations satisfies

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{cond}_2(K_N)\le3.}
\]

The bound has **no minimum positive relaxation rate and no mode-count dependence**. The factor three is sharp.

This is stronger than merely cancelling a static offset. Pointwise voltage increments have a telescoping direction at zero frequency. The finite work integral retains fluctuations generated within each cycle, which supply the strictly positive lower spectral bound. A genuinely frozen mode contributes exactly zero to the closed-cycle observable.

### Why an unknown nonthermal start is manageable

For a fixed initial state, write the filtered record as a deterministic mean plus a centered Gaussian process. Relative to the stationary reference, the initial boundary terms obey

\[
\|\text{filtered mean}\|_{\ell^2}^{\,2}
\le \frac{e_0c_0}{2},
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(\text{covariance deficit})\le\frac{c_0}{4}.
\]

These are finite bounds even when no uniform equilibration time exists.

They give confidence intervals from **one contiguous record**, using Gaussian quadratic-form concentration and an explicit initial-energy correction. The observations are never replaced by independent cohorts.

The absence of a rate floor does not make an arbitrarily weak useful signal cheap to detect. Acquisition cost still depends on signal strength, readout noise, initial energy, and confidence. A finite record that cannot resolve the useful anticorrelation produces `UNKNOWN`.

## 3. The response is acquired through actual interventions

The direct controller allows unknown fixed positive voltage and current-command gains. It also allows independent additive Gaussian readout noise with unknown variance.

In instrument units, the response to the closed waveform has a same-cell coefficient \(q>0\) and delayed coefficients \(P_j\) satisfying

\[
\sum_{j\ge1}|P_j|\le\frac23q,
\qquad
\|P\|_1\le\frac53q.
\]

These bounds follow from the circuit response and waveform. The value of \(q\) is acquired.

Apply a fresh batch of random signed pulses,

\[
b_k=a\varepsilon_k,
\qquad
\varepsilon_k\in\{-1,+1\}.
\]

The actual observed batch can be written

\[
Y=X+Pb,
\]

where \(X\) includes the retained state, all earlier control tails, native fluctuations, and readout noise. Its complete path can be arbitrary for this acquisition argument, provided it is independent of the fresh signs.

From the actual observations, calculate

\[
\widehat q=\frac{1}{Na^2}\sum_k b_kY_k,
\qquad
s_Y=\sqrt{\frac1N\sum_kY_k^2},
\qquad
t=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(4/\delta)}{N}}.
\]

The proved confidence interval is

\[
\boxed{
q_L=
\max\!\left\{0,
\frac{\widehat q-ts_Y/a}{1+\frac73t}
\right\},
\qquad
q_U=
\frac{\widehat q+ts_Y/a}{1-\frac73t}.
}
\]

The denominator must be positive.

This construction needs neither an equilibrium preparation nor an estimate of the hidden state. It uses fresh randomized actuation to distinguish the response from the retained trajectory.

The pulses are paid interventions. Their expected measured work cost is \(Na^2q\); realized probe work is charged in the executions. Their residual effect is carried into deployment rather than removed by a waiting period.

Unknown positive gains leave the sign of extracted work invariant. Conversion into joules still requires the ordinary current–voltage power calibration. The simulations reveal that conversion only after the controller has been frozen.

## 4. A small acquired controller has a proved positive sustained rate

\[

\qquad
\beta=-c_1\ge\beta_L>0,

where \(\nu\) includes readout-noise variance. Let the signed probes provide \(q\le q_U\).

The controller uses just the previous actual observation:

\[
\boxed{
b_k=g\,\operatorname{clip}(Y_{k-1},[-M_c,M_c]).
}
\]

Choose \(M_c\ge2\sqrt{\nu_U}\). With \(p_2=2\Phi(2)-1\), choose an encoded positive gain satisfying

\[
g\le
\min\!\left\{
\frac{3}{20q_U},
\frac{9\beta_Lp_2}{88q_U\nu_U},

The acquired lower bound on extracted measured work per cycle is

\[
\boxed{
L=g\beta_Lp_2-\frac{44}{9}q_U\nu_Ug^2

The physical reason is simple: the preceding work integral predicts an oppositely signed next integral. The controller exploits that anticorrelation, while the acquired response bound limits the quadratic cost of driving.

The proof includes the controller’s effect on future observations. It establishes a contraction, a unique stationary causal regime, and transfer of the long-run rate to the actual retained apparatus after acquisition. Initial-state effects and the finite probe history have vanishing average contributions.

The confidence statement concerns the returned **asymptotic rate certificate**. It does not promise positive work on each cycle or a particular finite deployment.

## 6. The stronger construction certifies performance against the best causal policy

The main constructive difficulty was implementing this from finite acquired information.

### The obstruction and its resolution

A finite response cutoff does **not** have a uniformly vanishing relative absolute tail. For any fixed cutoff \(L\), a sufficiently slow mode can leave tail mass close to \(c_0/3\). Simply assuming the remaining response becomes negligible would invalidate the controller.

The waveform supplies two different bounds for two different purposes.

First, the optimal filter has nonnegative coefficients and bounded total gain:

\[
\|H_*\|_1
\le3(\sqrt3-1)\frac{\theta}{c_0}.
\]

Together with the response bound, this gives a contraction margin even for the entire unresolved delayed response.

Second, the response tail does vanish in the quadratic norm relevant to performance:

\[
\sum_{n>L}c_n^2
\le\frac{c_0^2}{9(2L+1)}.
\]

Combining uniform stability with this quadratic approximation gives a finite convex synthesis problem, an executable saturated controller, and a computable gap to the global ideal optimum.

The hard amplitude limit is enforced by saturation. The resulting certificate includes its performance cost; it does not claim to solve the exact nonlinear optimum under a fixed amplitude limit.
