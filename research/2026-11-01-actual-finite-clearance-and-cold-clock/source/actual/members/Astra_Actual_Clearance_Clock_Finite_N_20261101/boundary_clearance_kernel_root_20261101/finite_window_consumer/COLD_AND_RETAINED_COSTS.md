# Cold versus retained computational costs

The new numerical fixture consumer performs exact rational arithmetic on two encoded populations, using an already acquired scalar enclosure. Its runtime and operation count do not include a simulation of the reaction history. The mathematical source proofs, initial data, and twelve rates are explicit rather than acquired from noisy experiments.

The earlier scalar acquisition ledger remains binding capital: three ordinary runs charged 92,474,955 rational operations and 1,061,126,991,451,094,784 rational schoolbook bit units in total. The final ordinary acquisition took about136.27 seconds in its recorded environment; this is an observation, not a wall-time guarantee. Its proof deliberately used a much larger total acquisition reserve of3*10^27 abstract bit steps, plus separate old receiver/regression reserves. Those reserves are not silently replaced by the measured subtotal here.

At N=10^25 the new actual macro-target proof implies at least2*10^21-1 literal reaction events on its successful event. The old conservative3*10^27 cold reserve is larger than that event lower bound. Therefore THIS ledger does not prove a full cold worst-case speedup at that fixture. Comparing a small retained numerical evaluation with a cold SSA path would be an unfair headline if scalar acquisition were omitted.

The supported result is narrower: once the source mathematics and scalar certificate are retained, an actual stochastic timing interval can be returned without enumerating the source firing history. An ordinary comparator may retain its own knowledge too. The known cheap deterministic/multiscale/LNA estimators remain important competitors, and their point predictions do not become certified merely by comparison with this package. Conversely, this certificate does not establish that no equally cheap ordinary certified route exists.

A sharper complete cold cost bound, a broad automatically acquired source family, and finite-N distribution sampling remain research questions. No new scalar solve is required merely to repackage the already accepted enclosure, and old unsuccessful computations or measured costs are not erased.
