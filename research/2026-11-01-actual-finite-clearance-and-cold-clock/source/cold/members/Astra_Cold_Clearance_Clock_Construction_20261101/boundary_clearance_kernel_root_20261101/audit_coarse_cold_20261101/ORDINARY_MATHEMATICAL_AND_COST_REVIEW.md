## Scalar construction: ordinary proof

Write F(s,e)=2s^2+3se^2+(9/8)e^4. The formal recurrence determines nonnegative p_n.
For every truncation P, F(s,P)-P' has zero matched low coefficients and nonnegative
remaining coefficients, so P is a subsolution. The simple barrier e=s works through
s=1/2 since F(s,s)<=121/128<1. Thus existence through this interval and the bounds
0<=P(s)<=e(s)<=s hold; in particular p_n<=2^n. No numerical explosion time is an
input to this argument.

On the rectangle 0<=s<=1/2, 0<=e<=1/4, F_e<=105/128. Let R=F(s,P)-P',
Rmax=R(1/2), and delta=Rmax/(1-105/256). Nonnegativity bounds R(s)<=Rmax.
For H=P+delta*s, F(s,H)-H'<=Rmax+(105/128)delta*s-delta<=0.
The exact H(1/2)<1/4 check therefore makes this a valid upper barrier on the entire
rectangle, not a circular assumption about the unknown solution. The lower endpoint
is P(1/2). After rounding H(1/2) up to the 80-bit coordinate a, e' >=1/2 for s>=1/2
implies the outward inverse start [1/2, 1/2+2(a-P(1/2))]. The independently rebuilt
startup values agree with the certificate exactly.

For g(e,s)=1/[2(s+3e^2/4)^2], both g_e and g_s are nonpositive in the admitted
positive domain. Hence inverse solutions are increasing, their differences cannot
increase, and the solution from a point center stays in
[center, center+h*g(a,center)]. The formal reciprocal-series recurrence computes
the Taylor coefficients of the inverse ODE. Recomputing the order-seven coefficient
on the full e/solution tube encloses the Lagrange remainder for the order-six
polynomial. Directed 80-bit interval arithmetic encloses every operation. Adding
the incoming radius is justified by inverse nonexpansivity; it is not an omitted
Gronwall factor. Integer midpoint asymmetry can enlarge width by one tick per step.
The independent implementation explicitly verifies total inverse width equals
startup width plus local widths plus midpoint padding (152 ticks in this run).
The constructor's actual endpoints also contain this padding.

The scale rule takes at most 128 steps below e=2 and at most 64 in each of the
three subsequent dyadic annuli. Thus 320 is a static upper bound; 315 is the observed
exact count. The final step is clipped at16. No rejected step proposal is retried.
The 512-step check is an additional refusal guard, not a hidden numerical search.

## Bounded arithmetic and work words

The cost theorem is for the specified deterministic multitape bit algorithm using
explicit schoolbook arithmetic, not for a Python interpreter or operating system.
The optional two `monotonic_ns` observations and output timing field are deleted
from that abstract algorithm; they do not enter any proof decision. The exact
reference implementation remains usable without giving those calls a bit-time claim.

The stored-result guards alone would not justify a work-word bound. The following
separate facts close that gap:

* D_n=8^n n! is a common denominator for p_n. Product factorial denominators divide
  the total-index factorial; the 1/(n+1) recurrence and 9/8 multiplier fit D_(n+1).
* For all residual indices through112, 8D_112 is a common denominator. A coefficient
  of P^4 has at most29^3 terms and magnitude below2^(112+15). The larger stated
  residual ceiling2^(112+21) covers all multipliers, derivative terms and partial
  sums. Since112!<128^112, reduced numerator/denominator lengths are below1280.
* Evaluation at1/2 adds at most112 denominator bits. Multiplication by256/151 in
  the barrier and the finite additions leave reduced startup values below1536 bits.
  This covers the unguarded coefficient and residual accumulation, not just values
  later passed through `guard_rational`. The ceil-grid product adds80 bits. The
  inverse-start sum with P(1/2) remains below2048. Naively computing powers in the
  different implementation respects the same common-denominator bound.
* The successful IV/Box endpoint representation is an 80-bit grid with tick
  magnitude below2^2048. General stored-rational operations therefore form raw
  products/sums of at most4097 bits. The special Fraction quantization path can
  temporarily have a4177-bit numerator but a denominator of at most160 bits.
  Its cross comparisons fit4257 bits. Operations before an endpoint guard rejects
  are still within the8192-bit work-word bound.
* Width differences and sums may exceed the2048-bit endpoint guard. The separate
  4096-bit auxiliary ledger covers a sum of at most320 widths, including midpoint
  padding. The common dyadic denominator prevents cumulative denominator growth.
* Tail expressions use the grid endpoints and fixed powers through7. Their actual
  successful values, all fixture expressions, and all fixed series/power checks
  fit the stated smaller1024-bit rational bound. One does not infer the universal
  work bound from the observed207-bit guarded maximum.

For a deliberately explicit independent arithmetic estimate, an ordinary rational
operation with raw words w<=4097 is covered by

    8*(w+1)^2*(2*w+20) < 4*8193^3.

The 2w+20 allowance includes Euclidean remainder divisions, raw products/sums,
normalizing quotients and fixed copying/sign work. Euclid requires at most2w+2

An explicit expansion of Box operations gives at most18 such elementary operations
