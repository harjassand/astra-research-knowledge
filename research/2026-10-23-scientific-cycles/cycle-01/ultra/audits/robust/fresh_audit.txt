FRESH INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE WEIGHTED TWO-FULL-TASK LEMMA
21 October 2026
Scope: the proposed finite-dimensional proof and its scalar-relaxation consequence.
Read: the task statement and the contemporaneous robust/RESULT.txt. No source,
novelty, generic additivity, physical-realizability or all-prefix theorem claim
is validated by this audit.

VERDICT

No flaw found in the lemma, its sharpness construction, the Schmidt-number
corollary, the total-variation lower bound, or the endpoint modification.
The supplied proof is complete once the Bell-vector and partial-trace
normalizations are fixed as below. Its limitations are stated correctly in
RESULT.txt. In particular, the endpoint modification is an abstract feasible
point of the specified enlarged relaxation, not an example of a quantum state.

1. NORMALIZATION AND THE FIXED-SPECTRUM STATEMENT

Each Aa and Ba has dimension q. Set
  |Phi_q> = q^(-1/2) sum_{j=0}^{q-1} |j>_Aa |j>_Ba,
and use its rank-one projector, of Schmidt rank q. The phrase "rank-q Bell
projector" must be read this way, rather than as a projector of operator rank q.
Let lambda be the nonnegative squared Schmidt coefficients, summing to one.
The coefficient matrix X is a q^2 by q^2 matrix, with rows (a1,a2), columns
(b1,b2), and ||X||_2^2 = sum lambda_j = 1.

For example, the coefficient of |a2>_A2 |b2>_B2 after contracting with
<Phi_q|_A1B1 is q^(-1/2) sum_j X_{j a2,j b2}. Therefore
  q p1 = ||Tr_1 X||_2^2,
and similarly q p2 = ||Tr_2 X||_2^2. The traces here are ordinary, unnormalized
matrix partial traces using the Bell bases to identify row and column factors.
There is no missing q or q^2. They are not partial traces of |psi><psi|.

The exact statement for rank(lambda) <= q is consequently
  max_X [q(alpha p1+beta p2)]
    = max(alpha,beta) (sum_j sqrt(lambda_j))^2 + min(alpha,beta),
where X ranges over matrices with those singular values and alpha,beta >= 0.
For arbitrary rank up to q^2, the same right side is still an upper bound;
the proposed proof does not claim that it is then the exact maximum.

2. THE POSITIVE POLAR REDUCTION IS VALID FOR THE WEIGHTED SUM

Put L=sqrt(XX*) and R=sqrt(X*X). With a unitary polar extension X=UR,
  [[L,X],[X*,R]] = [U;I] R [U*,I] >= 0.
The singular case causes no obstruction in finite square dimension. Apply
the two-by-two amplification of a completely positive map Phi. The resulting
block [[A,B],[B*,C]] is positive, so B=A^(1/2) K C^(1/2) for some contraction K.
This holds for singular A,C too, either by the usual support-factorization
argument or by the epsilon regularization given in RESULT.txt. Schatten
Holder and the ideal property give
  ||B||_2 <= ||A^(1/2)||_4 ||K||_infinity ||C^(1/2)||_4
          <= (||A||_2 ||C||_2)^(1/2).
For Phi=Tr_a, square this inequality to obtain
  ||Tr_a X||_2^2 <= ||Tr_a L||_2 ||Tr_a R||_2.
For F(Z)=alpha||Tr_1 Z||_2^2+beta||Tr_2 Z||_2^2, scalar Cauchy gives
  F(X) <= sqrt(F(L)F(R)) <= max(F(L),F(R)).
Thus at least one common positive modulus improves the total weighted score.
There is no unsupported inference that the same modulus improves each task
separately. Both moduli preserve all singular values and the Hilbert-Schmidt
norm. Restricting the search for this upper bound to positive Y is sound.

3. PURITY ARGUMENT AND WEIGHT ORDERING

For positive Y let t=Tr Y, sigma=Y/t, and u=Tr sigma^2=1/t^2. Here sigma is
an auxiliary density matrix on the two matrix-index factors. It is not the
original bipartite physical density matrix. Let
  a=Tr[(Tr_1 sigma)^2], b=Tr[(Tr_2 sigma)^2].
One has 0 <= a,b <= 1 and a+b <= 1+u. To check the latter directly, the two
copy swaps F1,F2 are commuting self-adjoint involutions. Hence
  (I-F1)(I-F2) >= 0,
and its expectation in sigma tensor sigma is 1-a-b+u. Positivity of the trace
of a product of positive operators suffices; sigma tensor sigma need not
commute with the swap product.

If alpha >= beta,
  alpha a + beta b
    = (alpha-beta)a + beta(a+b)
    <= alpha+beta u.
Multiplying by t^2 gives alpha t^2+beta. Since t^2=R(lambda) and
t^2 u=Tr Y^2=1, this is precisely the claimed upper bound. Swapping the
weights yields the other case. All zero-weight and zero-weight-pair cases
are included. Nonnegative weights are essential to this argument and to
the stated result; no claim about arbitrary signed weights is justified.

4. SHARPNESS AND SCHMIDT NUMBER

Suppose alpha >= beta and r=rank(lambda) <= q. The explicit state
  (sum_{j=0}^{r-1} sqrt(lambda_j)|j>_A1|j>_B1)
     tensor |0>_A2|0>_B2
has the prescribed global Schmidt spectrum and gives
  p1=R(lambda)/q, p2=1/q.
These are actual independent task states. Reverse the task labels if beta
is larger. This attains the upper bound, including ties and zero weights.

For rank at most M, Cauchy gives R(lambda) <= M, hence p1+p2 <=(M+1)/q.
For finite-dimensional states of Schmidt number at most M, averaging over a
pure-state decomposition proves the same inequality. No assumption that a
mixed state has one fixed Schmidt spectrum is needed. The stated meaningful
rank regime is 1 <= M <= q. The linear inequality remains mathematically
true outside that range but need not be the sharp useful bound there.

5. ROBUST SEPARATION: THE TV FACTOR IS CORRECT

The abstract target law on the two full-task events is
  P10=P01=M/q, P11=0, P00=1-2M/q.
It is a probability law only if 2M/q <= 1; RESULT.txt explicitly records this.
For a quantum law Q of Schmidt number at most M,
  Q(E1 union E2) <= Q(E1)+Q(E2) <= (M+1)/q.
The target union mass is 2M/q. Therefore, using the standard convention
TV(P,Q)=(1/2)||P-Q||_1=sup_E |P(E)-Q(E)|,
  TV(P,Q) >= (M-1)/q.
No factor of two should be inserted. The joint full-task Bell tests commute
because they act on disjoint physical tasks. If P,Q are instead full prefix
outcome laws, the union is still an event in that larger outcome space, so
the same lower bound applies by coarse graining. For two separately bounded
marginal errors, summing them instead gives the weaker per-marginal bound
e >=(M-1)/(2q), as stated in RESULT.txt.

This is a valid lower bound, not a proof of the exact minimum TV distance to
the quantum set. In particular it becomes zero at M=1 and is not asserted to
settle feasibility there. A bound that tends to zero cannot by itself exclude
all vanishing-error approximations. The trace-distance sentence in RESULT.txt
also uses only triangle inequality and measurement contractivity; it does not
assume that Schmidt number is stable under arbitrary small perturbations.

6. SCALAR FAMILY AND ENDPOINT MODIFICATION

For integer D>=2 and k>=1, set T=D^k-1, q=D^T, M=D^(T-k). The inequality
D^k>=k+1 ensures T-k>=0. Thus M is a positive integer <=q,
  M/q=D^(-k)=1/(T+1),
and the TV lower bound is exactly
  (M-1)/q=1/(T+1)-D^(-T).
Along k tending to infinity at fixed D, this is asymptotic to 1/T. The
statement that errors o(1/T) are excluded is correct; arbitrary vanishing
errors are not excluded by this calculation.

The old law gives mass 1/(T+1) to every point (J,T-J), J=0,...,T. Move only
the two endpoints as proposed. Under the coupling that keeps all other
atoms fixed, every new point is coordinatewise no larger than its old point.
For every pair of upper-tail thresholds (i,j), the indicator of
{K1>=i,K2>=j} can therefore only decrease. All previous rectangular upper-tail
bounds survive exactly, without needing their particular formulas.

No resulting point has either coordinate T, so both full-task probabilities
are zero. They satisfy every nonnegative weighted inequality from the new
lemma with the unchanged flat spectrum. The removed mass is exactly
2/(T+1), and all added mass lies on the disjoint layer K1+K2=T-1. Hence the
TV change is exactly 2/(T+1). At T=1 the two destinations coincide, but the
same calculation remains valid. Each of the two moved atoms loses one unit
of K1+K2, giving
  E_new[K1+K2]=T-2/(T+1).
The Schmidt spectrum and therefore H(lambda) are unchanged. For the scalar
score (ln D)E[K1+K2]-H(lambda), the new gap is exactly
  [k-2/(T+1)] ln D.
The asymptotic shorthand k ln D-O(1/T) is correct for fixed D; keeping the
explicit expression avoids silently treating ln D as a universal constant.

7. WHAT THIS AUDIT DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

The exact fixed-spectrum nonnegative weighted support value is established
by the displayed finite proof. The rank-M supporting inequality is sharp as
an inequality on the two marginal Bell probabilities. The TV estimate and
the failure of adding only these full-task upper constraints to the previous
rectangle relaxation both follow with the stated constants.

The endpoint modification does not establish quantum realizability or defeat
an actual quantum tensor-entropy witness. Constraints linking near-full and
other overlapping prefixes may exclude it. The nonnegative weighted support
function need not describe every feature of the nonconvex fixed-spectrum
pure-state probability set. There is no extension here to all-prefix entropy
charging, strong subadditivity of purity, general E_F additivity, E_C,
novelty, external validation, experiments, or practical advantage.

No correction to the proposed lemma or RESULT.txt is required by this audit.

8. SUPPLEMENT: JOINT-EVENT INEQUALITY FOR SCHMIDT NUMBER ONE

The proposed M=1 supplement is correct, including its sharp TV value for
the sole M=1 member of the scalar family. It supplies information absent
from the earlier sum-of-marginals bound, which is zero at M=1.

For a normalized rank-one coefficient matrix write X=uv*, with unit vectors
u,v in C^q tensor C^q. The physical vector is u tensor conjugate(v), which
includes every global Schmidt-rank-one vector. Let rho_u,a and rho_v,a be
the reduced states on the matrix-index factor a. Direct index contraction
gives
  ||Tr_a(uv*)||_2^2 = Tr(rho_u,a rho_v,a).
Here the marginal is on the factor that was traced out of uv*, not the
factor on which the resulting matrix acts. This convention matters for
distinct u and v, and the proposed formula has the correct convention.

Evaluate the same positive operator (I-F1)(I-F2) in the two-copy vector
u tensor v. Its expectation is
  1 - Tr(rho_u,1 rho_v,1) - Tr(rho_u,2 rho_v,2) + |<u|v>|^2 >= 0.
Since q p_a=||Tr_a X||_2^2 and the simultaneous Bell contraction gives
  q^2 p12=|Tr X|^2=|<u|v>|^2,
one obtains the valid joint-event inequality
  q(p1+p2)-q^2 p12 <= 1.
All probabilities in this expression are linear in the physical density
operator, so convexity extends it to every mixed state of Schmidt number
one. The proof allows u and v to be entangled between their own two local
task factors; no extra local-product assumption has entered the upper bound.

For integer D>=2 and k>=1, the condition M=D^(T-k)=1 is T=k, hence
D^k=k+1. The only solution is D=2,k=1: for k>=2, 2^k>k+1. Therefore
the scalar-family M=1 case has T=1 and q=2. In this case the inequality is
  p1+p2-2p12 <= 1/2.
The left side is exactly the probability of the exclusive-or event
E1 symmetric-difference E2. The abstract target has P10=P01=1/2, hence
exclusive-or probability one. It follows that every Schmidt-number-one
quantum outcome law has TV distance at least 1/2 from that target.

This last bound is the exact minimum. Take u=v=|0> tensor |0>, equivalently
the physical state |0>_A1|0>_A2|0>_B1|0>_B2. The two Bell tests act on
independent product task states and each succeeds with probability 1/2.
Consequently Q00=Q01=Q10=Q11=1/4, whose TV distance to the target is
(1/2)(1/4+1/4+1/4+1/4)=1/2. This is an honest separable sharpness example
for the two-event distance, not a realization of the abstract target law.
