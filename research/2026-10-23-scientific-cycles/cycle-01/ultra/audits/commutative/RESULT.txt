EXACT TENSOR WITNESS FOR RECURSIVELY LOCALLY CLASSICAL SQUARE-ROOT STATES
21 October 2026 | bounded structural contribution | internal proof candidate

Scope and notation
------------------
There are r task blocks H_a=(C^D)^{tensor N}; m=ln D. For a density matrix rho
on H_1 tensor ... tensor H_r, put

 f_{a,i}(rho)=D^{-i} Tr[(Tr_{a,first i} sqrt(rho))^2],  1<=i<=N,
 W(rho)=m sum_{a,i} f_{a,i}(rho).

All partial traces are unnormalized ordinary matrix partial traces. Thus this
is exactly the positive-matrix formulation supplied by the common handoff.
For the pure bipartite coefficient vector associated to X=sqrt(d rho), where
d=D^{Nr}, f_{a,i} is its actual JOINT Bell-prefix probability. No probability
factorization or scalar Schmidt-envelope relaxation is imposed.

Inherited one-task theorem, used with its proved scope:
For every density matrix sigma on one task block,
 S(sigma)>=m sum_i f_{a,i}(sigma)-C_D,
with C_D independent of N and, for example,
 C_D=min{2D-ln D, ln D/(1-D^{-1/2})^2}; D=2 also permits 2-ln2.
This follows directly from the original universal single-chain pure-state
witness applied to its positive coefficient vector. It is an ordinary
one-task assertion, not the disproved complete/conditional strengthening.

Definition of the exact class
-----------------------------
Let RLC(A) be the following recursively specified class of density matrices
on a finite nonempty set A of task blocks. Every density matrix on one task
belongs to RLC. A density matrix on at least two tasks belongs to RLC if there
is a task a in A, an orthonormal basis (e_j) of its WHOLE D^N dimensional
block, probabilities p_j, and rho_j in RLC(A\{a}) such that

 rho=sum_j p_j |e_j><e_j|_a tensor rho_j.                         (1)

The basis e_j may be internally entangled across the N sites of task a.
The subsequent eliminated task, its basis, and every later elimination
choice may depend on j. Only the one task being eliminated is classical
at each node. The final uneliminated task may have any density matrix.
Zero probability branches are ignored.

This class contains density matrices diagonal in a common product eigenbasis
across whole tasks, and is strictly wider. For two tasks it includes every
classical-quantum or quantum-classical density matrix. For three or more it
allows branch-dependent bases and orders. No common product eigenbasis or
common classical joint distribution is assumed in (1).

Theorem
-------
For every rho in RLC on r tasks,

 S(rho)>=W(rho)-r C_D.                                          (2)

More precisely, the proof supplies nonnegative explicit compatibility losses
L_v at the classical nodes v of any witnessing recursion tree. If P(v) is
the probability of reaching node v, then

 S(rho)>=W(rho)-r C_D+m sum_v P(v)L_v.                          (3)

These inequalities are exact, permit arbitrary correlations represented by
(1), and have the identical horizon-independent per-task constant C_D.

Local quantum compatibility lemma
--------------------------------
Suppose (1) holds, without imposing any condition on rho_j. Its positive
square root and its marginal on task a are

 sqrt(rho)=sum_j sqrt(p_j)|e_j><e_j| tensor sqrt(rho_j),
 rho_a=sum_j p_j |e_j><e_j|.

For every OTHER task b and every prefix i, orthogonality of the classical
projectors gives the exact identity

 f_{b,i}(rho)=sum_j p_j f_{b,i}(rho_j).                         (4)

For task a define R_{i,j}=Tr_{a,first i}|e_j><e_j| and

 K_{i,jk}=D^{-i}Tr(R_{i,j}R_{i,k}) >= 0,
 A_{jk}=Tr(sqrt(rho_j)sqrt(rho_k)) in [0,1].

Here nonnegativity of K uses positivity of the two reduced matrices, not
commutation. A is real and nonnegative by the same fact, and A<=1 follows
from Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz and Tr rho_j=Tr rho_k=1. Expansion gives

 f_{a,i}(rho)=sum_{j,k} sqrt(p_j p_k) K_{i,jk} A_{jk}
             <=sum_{j,k} sqrt(p_j p_k) K_{i,jk}
             =f_{a,i}(rho_a).                                 (5)

Thus a real Gram matrix of quantum affinities suppresses, rather than
artificially enlarges, this task's prefix overlaps. Equality need not hold.
Set

 L_a(rho)=sum_i sum_{j,k} sqrt(p_j p_k)K_{i,jk}(1-A_{jk}) >=0.

Equations (4)-(5) yield the exact total-score recursion

 W(rho)=m sum_i f_{a,i}(rho_a)-m L_a(rho)
                     +sum_j p_j W(rho_j).                    (6)

This is additional simultaneous quantum information absent from a scalar
spectrum-and-tail relaxation. In particular, choosing scalar tail data and
an entropy independently does not guarantee a positive affinity Gram
representation of (6). The lemma does not assert such a representation for
arbitrary rho; its block-diagonal premise is essential.

Complete induction proof of the theorem
---------------------------------------
The base r=1 is the inherited one-task theorem. For r>=2 let (1) be a
valid first node. The exact block entropy formula gives

 S(rho)=H(p)+sum_j p_j S(rho_j)
       =S(rho_a)+sum_j p_j S(rho_j).

Apply the one-task theorem to rho_a and the induction theorem to each rho_j:

 S(rho)>=m sum_i f_{a,i}(rho_a)-C_D
            +sum_j p_j[W(rho_j)-(r-1)C_D]
        =W(rho)-r C_D+m L_a(rho).

If the stronger induction statement includes descendant losses, averaging
them with p_j gives (3). Since exactly one task is removed at each level,
every branch pays exactly r copies of C_D regardless of bases or order.
This closes the induction without assuming E_F additivity, without a
complete conditional entropy estimate, and without postselecting a physical
preparation or deleting any retained register.

Common product eigenbasis: independent classical proof
-----------------------------------------------------
For a useful audit of the simplest subcase, suppose
 rho=sum_z p_z tensor_a |e_{a,z_a}><e_{a,z_a}|,
where each e_{a,z_a} is a fixed whole-task orthonormal basis. Hold z_{-a}
fixed and normalize its diagonal positive amplitude vector on task a.
Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonality of all other task projectors implies

 sum_i f_{a,i}(rho)=sum_{z_{-a}} p_{z_{-a}}
                          sum_i f_{a,i}(rho_{a|z_{-a}}).

The one-task theorem gives
 H(Z_a|Z_{-a})>=m sum_i f_{a,i}(rho)-C_D.
The elementary inequality
 sum_a H(Z_a|Z_{-a})<=H(Z_1,...,Z_r)
follows by fixing the order 1,...,r and using
 H(Z_a|Z_{-a})<=H(Z_a|Z_1,...,Z_{a-1}), then the chain rule.
Summing proves (2). This argument alone would not cover (1), because its
conditional bases need not be a common product eigenbasis. The compatibility
lemma and recursion prove that extension.

Strictness of the class extension
--------------------------------
For two qubit task blocks (D=2,N=1), consider

 rho=(1/3)|0><0| tensor |0><0|+(2/3)|1><1| tensor |+><+|.

It is of form (1), with pure conditional states, and obeys (2). It cannot
be diagonal in ANY common product basis. Its unequal nonzero eigenvalues
1/3 and 2/3 force the nonzero eigenvectors |0>tensor|0> and |1>tensor|+>
up to phases. A common basis on the second task cannot contain both the
nonorthogonal distinct vectors |0> and |+>. This example is only a class
separation check; N=1 is not an asymptotic witness advantage.

Precise remaining quantum ingredient
-------------------------------------
The exact reduction to X>=0 does not supply the block form (1). A positive
matrix can have spectral projectors entangled across task blocks, and even
fully separable density matrices can have nonzero discord in both directions.
For a generic rho, off-diagonal blocks |e_j><e_k| with j!=k invalidate (4),
the square-root formula, and the exact block entropy chain rule together.
Discarding those blocks generally changes both entropy and prefix scores.
No lossless dephasing reduction has been proved here.

For r=2 this theorem excludes all CQ/QC positive representatives from a
counterexample, so a violating positive representative must be nonclassical
in BOTH task directions. It need not be entangled across the tasks. For
larger r, every violation lies outside this recursive locally classical
class. The theorem does not prove that an arbitrary candidate admits an
RLC representative with the same spectrum and at least as much score.
Such a reduction would resolve the boundary, but is an unproved assertion,
not a consequence of positive-matrix reduction or spectral rearrangement.

Operational boundary
---------------------
This is a restricted-class tensor witness on positive pure coefficient
representatives. It does not by itself imply a universal E_C lower bound:
an arbitrary t-copy dilution output or its pure decomposition need not have
RLC positive representatives. The ordinary universal tensor witness remains
open. It also gives no efficient test or efficient conversion to this class.
The proof is finite matrix algebra; no experiment or numerical optimization
is claimed. Single-task constants and known single-chain witness are inherited.
The new contribution in this note is the explicit affinity-kernel compatibility
identity and exact recursive-class closure. Historical novelty is UNKNOWN.
