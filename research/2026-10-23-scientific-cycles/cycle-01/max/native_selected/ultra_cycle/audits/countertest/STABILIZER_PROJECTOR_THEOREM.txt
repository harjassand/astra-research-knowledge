SHARP TENSOR WITNESS ON STABILIZER-CODE PROJECTORS
Bounded counterchallenge / positive scientific contribution, 21 October 2026

STATUS AND SCOPE
This is a complete finite-dimensional proof for a structured class of ACTUAL
positive coefficient matrices. It is not the requested theorem for arbitrary
positive matrices. It adds a genuine simultaneous quantum-compatibility
constraint, rather than using the excluded scalar Schmidt-envelope relaxation.
The proof uses elementary stabilizer symplectic linear algebra; no claim of
historical novelty is made. No generic E_F additivity or conditional entropy
inequality is used. The conditional coefficient-one premise remains false.

1. DEFINITIONS

Let D be prime. There are n=rN D-dimensional qudits, divided into r ordered
tasks, N qudits each. Put m=ln D. Let G be an abelian Pauli stabilizer group
with no nontrivial scalar element, with s independent generators. Equivalently
its Pauli labels form an s-dimensional isotropic linear subspace of the
nondegenerate symplectic space F_D^(2n). Choose a character/eigenspace so that
P is its joint stabilizer-code projector. P has rank M=D^(n-s).

Define rho=P/M and X=sqrt(rho)=P/sqrt(M), so Tr(X*X)=1. For a task a and its
first i qudits A_(a,i), define the ACTUAL canonical-purification Bell score

  p_(a,i)=D^(-i) Tr[(Tr_(A_(a,i)) sqrt(rho))^2].

This is the joint first-i Bell-projector expectation of the normalized
coefficient vector sum_uv X_uv |u>_A |v>_B. Its bipartite entropy is
S(rho)=ln M=(n-s)m. All tasks can be correlated through G.

THEOREM (sharp restricted tensor witness).

  m sum_(a=1)^r sum_(i=1)^N p_(a,i) - S(rho)
      <= r m (1-D^(-N))/(D-1)
      <= r m/(D-1).

In particular K_D=m/(D-1), independent of N and r, is valid and optimal
uniformly over this class. The first displayed finite-N defect is also optimal.

2. EXACT PREFIX FORMULA

Let f_a(i) be the rank over F_D of the map restricting Pauli labels in G to
the first i qudits of task a. Set f_a(0)=0. This records BOTH X and Z labels
on the tested qudits. The subgroup with identity on that prefix has size
D^(s-f_a(i)).

The Pauli expansion is P=D^(-s) sum_(g in G) chi(g) W_g, with phases/character
chosen consistently. Partial trace over A of size i removes every term with
nonidentity label on A and multiplies the remaining terms by D^i. Distinct
remaining labels on the complement are orthogonal for Hilbert-Schmidt trace.
The number of those terms is D^(s-f_a(i)); their squared phases have modulus
one. Since P and its partial trace are Hermitian, this gives

  Tr[(Tr_A P)^2]
    = D^(2i-2s) D^(n-i) D^(s-f_a(i))
    = D^(n+i-s-f_a(i)).

Dividing by D^i M yields the exact physical score

  p_(a,i)=D^(-f_a(i)).                                      (1)

This formula is for the actual density matrix and its actual tensor factors;
it is not a freely specified probability/spectrum pair.

3. THE ADDITIONAL SIMULTANEOUS QUANTUM CONSTRAINT

Choose arbitrary prefix lengths i_a in {0,...,N}. The common kernel of
restricting G to their union has dimension at least

  s-sum_a f_a(i_a),

because the rank of the joint restriction is at most the sum of the separate
restriction ranks. Every vector in this kernel is supported on the h remaining
qudits, where h=n-sum_a i_a. The kernel is isotropic in the nondegenerate
2h-dimensional symplectic space of those remaining qudits. Any isotropic
subspace W there has dimension at most h: W is contained in W^perp and
dim W+dim W^perp=2h. Therefore

  s-sum_a f_a(i_a) <= n-sum_a i_a,
  sum_a [i_a-f_a(i_a)] <= n-s.                            (2)

Negative lower bounds on the common-kernel dimension cause no problem; the
inequality still follows from its actual nonnegative dimension.

Define the nonnegative integers

  g_a=max_(0<=i<=N) [i-f_a(i)].

Taking a maximizing prefix separately for every task in (2) is permitted,
because (2) holds for their common union. It gives

  sum_a g_a <= n-s.                                        (3)

Equation (3) is an actual compatibility constraint arising from Pauli
commutation and support. The excluded scalar envelopes do not supply it.

4. SUMMING THE PREFIXES

The definition of g_a gives f_a(i)>=i-g_a and trivially f_a(i)>=0. Thus

  sum_(i=1)^N D^(-f_a(i))
    <= g_a + sum_(j=1)^(N-g_a) D^(-j)
    = g_a + (1-D^(-(N-g_a)))/(D-1).

The last remainder is at most (1-D^(-N))/(D-1), because g_a>=0. Summing,
using (3), multiplying by m and subtracting (n-s)m proves the theorem.

Sharpness: take rho=|0><0|^(tensor n), a rank-one stabilizer projector.
Then s=n, f_a(i)=i, g_a=0, and

  S(rho)=0,
  Delta/r=m sum_(i=1)^N D^(-i)=m(1-D^(-N))/(D-1).

Taking N to infinity proves that the uniform K_D cannot be smaller than
m/(D-1). This product-state example is also a necessary lower bound on any
eventual fully general tensor theorem's constant.

5. EXTENSION TO PHYSICAL PURE STABILIZER STATES

The accepted positive-matrix reduction from the common packet states, in
unnormalized Hilbert-Schmidt conventions just as well, that for every complex
coefficient matrix C and the total nonnegative weighted prefix score F,

  F(C) <= sqrt(F(|C|) F(|C*|)).                             (4)

Both moduli have the same squared singular values and entropy as C. A pure
stabilizer state's reduced density matrix on either party is the normalized
projector onto a stabilizer code: this follows by expanding its pure-state
Pauli projector and retaining only stabilizers with identity on the traced
party. Its nonzero spectrum is flat. Transpose of a stabilizer-code projector
is another stabilizer-code projector (Pauli transpose only changes phases).
For the coefficient convention used here, C*C is a transpose of one reduced
state, which preserves this assertion. Consequently both moduli in (4) have
the form covered above with the same M and entropy. Applying the bound to
both and using (4) proves the same sharp witness for EVERY physical pure
stabilizer state on the bipartite task outputs, including entanglement within
each party and across tasks.

More generally this argument covers any flat-Schmidt pure state for which
BOTH local Schmidt-support projectors are stabilizer-code projectors. The
partial isometry between the two codes can be arbitrary: no assertion that
it is Clifford is required. The proof does not cover an arbitrary support
projector or arbitrary nonflat Schmidt spectrum.

6. RESTRICTED RESOURCE CONSEQUENCE AND ITS LIMIT

For finite stabilizer circuits, suppose all local pure ancillas are stabilizer
states, allowed quantum operations are Clifford gates and Pauli measurements,
and all classical branches are counted. Begin with Bell supply of rank D^k.
If some local quantum registers remain at the end, measure them in local
computational bases and retain every result. Each resulting finite-output
branch is a pure stabilizer state; averaging the branch outputs leaves every
external Bell-prefix expectation unchanged. Each branch's entanglement is at
most the supplied k m by Schmidt-rank monotonicity under local Kraus maps.
Averaging the restricted pure-state witness therefore yields

  k m >= m sum_(a,i) p_(a,i) - r m/(D-1).                   (5)

The same argument averages a genuinely preselected random Bell supply and
charges its expected resource, including every branch. This is a restriction
on the PREPARATION AND DEVICE OPERATIONS, not an E_F lower bound deduced merely
because a mixed target has some stabilizer decomposition. There is no claim
that an arbitrary pure-state decomposition minimizing E_F is stabilizer.

If the finite stabilizer protocol's output is globally epsilon-close in
half-trace distance to t independent copies of a fixed N-slot output, the
same effect bound as in the common packet adds at most t N m epsilon to (5).
Thus, at fixed N, stabilizer-only approximate iid formation protocols obey
the corresponding per-copy lower bound as epsilon tends to zero. This does
NOT establish the general iid E_C boundary, since general formation and
device operations need not preserve stabilizer branches. It is a justified
smaller capability, not a substitute for that open boundary.

absolute residual 1.333e-15. 144 additional combinatorial Clifford samples up

These are implementation checks of a proved theorem, not its proof.

(4,2),(2,3),(2,4), with flat or harmonic nonzero spectra, fixed seed270321,

This bounded test found no growing counterexample. It establishes no bound
for arbitrary positive matrices, does not optimize over their eigenbases,
and does not realize or retry the excluded scalar relaxation family.

Unsuccessful generalization: replacing the stabilizer support argument by a
generic statement about arbitrary rank-M projectors is not justified. The
integer linear restriction ranks and isotropic-kernel dimension bound are
essential to (2). A generic density matrix has no such Pauli subspace.
Nonuniform stabilizer-basis spectra are not covered either; positivity does
not by itself turn their prefix scores into the dimension ranks in (1).

Remaining precise gap: obtain a suitable replacement for (2) for general
positive matrices, allowing nonflat spectra and nonstabilizer eigenspaces,
or construct an actual family that defeats every r K_D bound. The sharp
stabilizer result identifies a substantial compatibility-constrained family
where the critical ordinary tensor witness does survive, while generic E_C
formation remains open.
