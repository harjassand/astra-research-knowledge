1. THE ACTUAL TARGET AND THE SHARED BASELINE

Write d=D^(Nr), tau=Tr/d, and tau(X*X)=1. With E_(a,i) the normalized
partial-trace conditional expectation on task a's first i sites, put
  p_(a,i)(X)=||E_(a,i)X||_(2,tau)^2,
  Delta(X)=(ln D) sum_(a,i) p_(a,i)(X)-S(XX*/d).
The target is sup_X Delta(X)<=r K_D, with one finite K_D independent of N,r.
This is equivalent to the stated ordinary entropy-energy inequality because
S(XX*/d)=ln d-Ent_tau(X*X) and ln d=Nr ln D.

The normalized matrix/Bell-vector identification, ordinary entropy-energy
formulation, conditional counterfamily, and conditional iid operational bridge
are shared stage-1 material. They are not new results of either candidate.
The distinction between ordinary and conditional estimates is substantive.
For the common positive matrix X=d0|Omega_d0><Omega_d0| on two task spaces,
where d0=D^N, the one-block conditional entropy is 2N ln D and its energy is
(ln D)(N-sum_i D^(-2i)). That forces an extensive conditional defect. In the
actual two-block ordinary problem, the entropy is still 2N ln D but the energy
includes both blocks; its defect is only 2(ln D)sum_i D^(-2i), which is bounded.
Thus neither continuation may count this family as a physical counterexample.
Both correctly retain that restriction.

The relevant original source comparison is the single sorted Schmidt-spectrum
bound for a Bell projection tensored with an arbitrary residual space. Its
extension to arbitrary subsets of the tensor factors by local permutation is
valid. It is used in B's scalar analysis below. No general E_F additivity is
used or accepted in this adjudication.

2. CANDIDATE A: THE POSITIVE-MATRIX REDUCTION IS CORRECT

Exact hypotheses: finite square input matrices; any finite collection of
2-positive linear maps into finite square matrix algebras; one consistent
normalized Hilbert-Schmidt trace within each map's output; nonnegative fixed
weights w_j. Complete positivity of the actual conditional expectations is
more than sufficient. Unitality and trace preservation are not needed for the
basic polar reduction. The input is square so its polar partial isometry can
be extended to a unitary U.

Let R=|X| and L=|X*|=URU*. The block identity is exactly
  [L X; X* R] = [U; I] R [U* I] >=0.
Applying Id_2 tensor Phi yields a positive block [A B; B* C], with
A=Phi(L), B=Phi(X), C=Phi(R). Positivity provides a contraction K satisfying
B=A^(1/2)KC^(1/2). For singular blocks, adding epsilon I to both diagonal
blocks preserves positivity; compactness of the finite-dimensional unit ball
of contractions and continuity of square roots justify passage to the limit.
There is no kernel inversion gap.

Unnormalized Schatten Holder and the ideal property give
  ||B||_HS <= ||A^(1/2)||_4 ||K C^(1/2)||_4
           <= ||A||_HS^(1/2) ||C||_HS^(1/2).
Squaring and dividing by the common output dimension gives exactly
  ||Phi(X)||_(2,tau)^2
       <= ||Phi(L)||_(2,tau) ||Phi(R)||_(2,tau).
The normalization factors match: both sides acquire the same inverse
output-dimension factor after squaring. The argument requires 2-positivity,
not merely positivity, and both candidates meet that hypothesis.

WEIGHTING ACROSS PREFIXES
For p_j(Z)=||E_j Z||_2^2, the pointwise result is
  p_j(X)<=sqrt(p_j(L)p_j(R)).
Multiplication by w_j>=0 and ordinary Cauchy-Schwarz give
  F_w(X)<=sqrt(F_w(L)F_w(R))<=(F_w(L)+F_w(R))/2
         <=max(F_w(L),F_w(R)).
One common choice between L and R therefore improves the entire weighted
objective. This does not claim that one choice improves every prefix. That
distinction is correctly stated in A and B and is exactly sufficient for the
target witness. Arbitrary signed weights are outside the proved hypothesis.

Both L and R have the same singular values and normalized squared norm as X.
They preserve the Schmidt spectrum and its entropy. Consequently the maximum
of the weighted witness at each fixed Schmidt spectrum equals its maximum on
the positive conjugacy orbit. Finite-dimensional compactness justifies maxima.
Taking the supremum over spectra proves the lossless equality of the unrestricted
and positive-matrix optimization problems. This is genuinely new versus the
frozen report and applies to every N,r,D without paying an additive constant.

A's additional corollaries are also correct:
* A strict finite-dimensional violation can be approximated by positive
  definite Y_epsilon=(Y+epsilon I)/||Y+epsilon I||_2. Continuity suffices at
  that fixed dimension. This perturbation does not preserve the original
  spectrum, nor does A claim it does. No uniform-in-dimension continuity is
  needed for this existence statement.
* Writing X=sqrt(d rho) gives, for k=D^i,
    ||E_(a,i)X||_2^2=(1/k) Tr[(Tr_(a,prefix i) sqrt(rho))^2].
  Indeed E X=(sqrt(d)/k) I_k tensor Tr_prefix sqrt(rho); its squared trace
  divided by d is the displayed expression. A includes the essential 1/k.
* The full target is therefore equivalent to
    S(rho)>=(ln D) sum_(a,i) D^(-i)
                  Tr[(Tr_(a,prefix i) sqrt(rho))^2]-r K_D
  for all density matrices on the task tensor product.

REMAINING GAP FOR A
Positivity removes the independent left/right polar factors but not the
quantum eigenbasis problem. The density matrix can have entangled eigenvectors
across task blocks. No product eigenbasis, diagonal reduction, separability,
entropy tensorization, or uniform K_D follows from the argument. The canonical
purification formulation is an exact reduction, not a solution. A correctly
states this boundary. Its finite numerical checks are not evidence for the
full conjecture and are not needed for the proof.

3. CANDIDATE B: DUPLICATE REDUCTION PLUS A VALID NEW RELAXATION OBSTRUCTION

B's polar proof is the same valid argument as A's, stopping at the averaged
bound before summing. It makes the same global choice of modulus, so it has no
prefix-by-prefix selection error. This shared lemma must be counted once in
any integration, not as two independent advances.

The additional joint-prefix lemma is valid. For multi-index i=(i_1,...,i_r),
the intersection of the prefix projections is a Bell projection of total
Schmidt dimension D^s, s=sum_a i_a, tensored with the residual identity. A
local factor permutation gives the original source overlap argument. With one
sorted global Schmidt spectrum lambda this yields simultaneously
  Pr(K_a>=i_a for all a)<=B_s(lambda),
  B_s(lambda)=D^(-s) sum_blocks (sum_(j in block) sqrt(lambda_j))^2.
Here each block has D^s consecutive entries and B_0=1. The commuting nested
projectors define genuine simultaneous spectral variables K_a in {0,...,N}.
Thus ordinary probability consistency, including all marginal constraints
obtained by zero indices, is correctly included in the stated relaxation.

EXACT AUDIT OF THE COUNTERFAMILY
Fix integer D>=2 and k>=1. Let
  T=D^k-1, N=T, r=2, L=T-k, M=D^L.
The elementary inequality D^k>=k+1 proves L>=0, so M is a positive integer.
The ambient Schmidt dimension D^(2T) is at least M. Lambda consists of M
entries 1/M followed by zeros; it is normalized, sorted and has H(lambda)=L ln D.

For s<=L, D^s divides M and each nonzero block is full. Direct block summation
gives B_s=1. For s>L, all nonzero entries fit in the first block, giving
B_s=M/D^s=D^(L-s). Hence the exact envelope is
  B_s=min(1,D^(L-s)), 0<=s<=2N.
This is not a crude rank-only substitution.

Let J be exactly uniform on {0,...,T} and put (K1,K2)=(J,T-J). Its T+1 atoms
each have mass 1/(T+1), sum to one, and include every branch. For all i,j in
{0,...,T}, direct counting gives
  Pr(K1>=i,K2>=j)=max(T-i-j+1,0)/(T+1).
If s=i+j<=L, this is at most one. If L<s<=T, set u=T-s, so 0<=u<k.
The envelope constraint is precisely
  (u+1)/D^k <= D^(u-k), equivalently u+1<=D^u.
This holds for every integer u>=0, D>=2. If s>T, the left side is zero.
These cases exhaust every rectangular tail, including both extreme marginals,
the zero-index normalization condition, and all full intersections.

Every atom has K1+K2=T. Therefore
  (ln D)E[K1+K2]-H(lambda)=(T-L)ln D=k ln D.
For fixed D and r=2 this tends to infinity. No finite 2K_D can follow from
this precise relaxation. Since the constructed data satisfy each inequality,
all nonnegative combinations of those inequalities also remain satisfied.
The conclusion is a rigorous obstruction to the named operation of separately
applying the exact Schmidt envelope to every intersection and then using only
the resulting scalar probability problem. It is new versus stage 1, which
mentioned this route without proving such an obstruction.

SCOPE OF "ALL CONSTRAINTS"
The counterfamily satisfies all constraints of the explicit rectangular-tail
relaxation and ordinary probability consistency. It does not satisfy all
constraints imposed by a physical pure state and its specified Schmidt
spectrum. Extra operator compatibility information, or spectral bounds on
other combinations of projectors, is outside that relaxation. B states this
limitation correctly. Its conclusion is not a refutation of the actual tensor
witness, nor of every possible spectral argument that adds such information.

4. ADJUDICATOR SUPPLEMENT: THE EXACT SCALAR FAMILY IS NOT PHYSICAL

This strengthens the realizability check but is not needed for accepting B's
relaxation obstruction. It was derived during this blinded audit.

Set q=D^T and retain M=D^(T-k)<=q. Suppose a pure state psi has uniform
Schmidt spectrum of rank M. Consider a full-task Bell projection P on a task
of local dimension q, leaving the other task as a residual space. If
  <psi|P|psi>=M/q,
then the residual task is a product pure state, factored from the tested task.

Proof of the saturation claim. The projection norm equals
  max_(||eta||=1) |<psi|Phi_q tensor eta>|^2.
Let t1 be eta's largest Schmidt root. The first M sorted Schmidt roots of
Phi_q tensor eta all equal t1/sqrt(q), since M<=q. The singular-value overlap
bound gives
  |<psi|Phi_q tensor eta>|^2 <= (M/q)t1^2 <= M/q.
Saturation forces t1=1; hence the maximizing eta is a product alpha tensor beta.
Let R be the local projection fixing the residual A and B vectors to alpha
and beta. The vector R psi has Schmidt rank at most M and norm at most one.
Its overlap with Phi_q tensor alpha tensor beta is at most
sqrt(M/q)||R psi||, by the elementary rank-M Bell-overlap bound. Since the
actual squared overlap is M/q, ||R psi||=1, so R psi=psi. This proves the claim
without assuming an equality characterization of the full trace inequality.

Now B's abstract data saturate BOTH extreme marginal constraints:
  Pr(K1>=T)=Pr(K2>=T)=D^(-k)=M/q.
Saturation for the first full-task projection makes task 2 locally pure and
factored. Saturation for the second makes task 1 locally pure and factored.
Together they force psi to be a product across all four local task factors,
and in particular to have global Schmidt rank one. This contradicts M>1.

The only M=1 case in this family is D=2,k=1,T=1. The same factorization forces
the two full-task success events to have joint probability equal to the
product of their marginal probabilities, q^(-2)>0. The abstract law instead
has Pr(K1>=T,K2>=T)=0 because T>=1 and K1+K2=T. This is also impossible.
Thus the exact probability/spectrum pair is physically incompatible for
every D,k in the family, independently of the truth of the tensor theorem.

This is an exact saturation argument only. No robust quantitative separation
for approximate versions of the family is proved, and no inference about the
full uniform witness follows. In particular, neither this incompatibility nor
B's abstract divergence is a physical counterexample or a proof of the target.

5. DUPLICATION, COMPLEMENTARITY, AND ACCEPTED INTEGRATION

The candidates overlap on one substantive new reduction: arbitrary complex
coefficients can be replaced by a positive modulus for the total nonnegative
weighted witness without changing the Schmidt spectrum. Count that once.
A presents useful explicit fixed-spectrum, positive-definite, and density-matrix
corollaries. These are valid consequences of the same lemma, not separate
resolutions of the tensor estimate. B includes the same reduction and adds
the independent scalar-relaxation obstruction proved above.

Integration is a strictly stronger accepted starting point than the frozen
report and than A alone: it combines the positive-matrix reduction with a
second rigorously blocked proof route. Relative to B, A mainly supplies clear
equivalent formulations and consequences already implied by B's reduction;
it does not add another logically independent breakthrough. The adjudicator's
saturation supplement additionally establishes exact nonrealizability of B's
abstract family. That supplement should be tracked separately from candidate
contributions.

The integrated next obligation remains precise: prove the positive-density
matrix inequality of section 2 with finite K_D independent of N,r, or construct
an actual physical family with Delta/r unbounded. A scalar-law family alone
cannot discharge the latter obligation. A positive-matrix proof would now
automatically handle arbitrary complex coefficients with the same constant.
No generic E_F additivity theorem, classical product eigenbasis, or conditional
coefficient-one inequality may be inserted to fill the remaining gap.

6. OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCES REMAIN CONDITIONAL

Both candidates correctly preserve the common iid implication. If the actual
uniform tensor witness were proved, convex-roof averaging would apply it to
the t-copy finite output. For fixed horizon N, the positive witness satisfies
0<=W_t<=tN ln D, so global half-trace preparation error epsilon_t contributes
at most tN(ln D)epsilon_t. Dividing by t and taking t to infinity at that fixed
N removes the error. Only afterward may N vary. The polar reduction adds no
error or constant to this implication.

Neither candidate presently establishes an unconditional general E_C law,
exact output from approximate initialization, a joint N/t/error limit, a
finite-time preparation efficiency claim, or an experimental advantage.
