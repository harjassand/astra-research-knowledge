RESULT: SHARP JOINT-KL COEFFICIENT FOR THE LAZY REVERSIBLE MIXTURE
Bounded trial participant; 21 October 2026. Independent owner review requested.
No historical-priority claim. Source evidence was read only.

1. Claim and scope

Let pi and q be equivalent probability measures on an arbitrary measurable
space, W=dpi/dq, and let G be independence Metropolis-Hastings with proposal
q and target pi. Let L be any fixed pi-reversible Markov kernel. Fix
0<alpha<1, put beta=1-alpha, H=alpha G+beta L, C=(I+H)/2, and initialize
at q. For K,J>0 assume D(pi||q)<=K and D(q||pi)<=J.

For the supremum T_(K,J,alpha)(epsilon) of the first integer time at which
TV(q C^t,pi)<=epsilon, over exactly this class, the argument below proves

  lim_(epsilon down to 0) epsilon log T_(K,J,alpha)(epsilon)
      = max{K,(1-alpha)J}.                                    (1)

In particular, the unresolved diagnostic K=1,J=4,alpha=1/2 has coefficient
2. The limit is concluded by matching a new upper bound to the existing
proved lower bound; it is not assumed. Alpha is fixed throughout. The
finite-time inequality below controls TV itself, with no initial L2
density assumption. Neither the initialization nor the mixture is changed.

The new ingredient is the bounded-observable, pointwise near-harmonicity
of a lazy chain, applied specifically on the proposal-overweight tail.
It retains the first-update competition. This is not a spectral-gap or
asymptotic-variance comparison.

2. New finite-time inequality

Fix B,R>e and M>1 such that s0=pi(W<=M)>0. Define

  D={W<1/B}, E={W>R}, S=(D union E)^c,
  b=q(D), a=pi(E), A0={W<=M},
  U=M(B+1)+R,
  Delta_t = sqrt[U/(alpha s0 (2t+1))],
  eta_t = 2^(-t) binomial(t,floor(t/2)).

Then, for every integer t>=0,

  TV(q C^t,pi)
    <= max{a,beta b} + 2 alpha b^2 + alpha a b/R
       + (1+alpha b) Delta_t + b eta_t.                       (2)

The central binomial term satisfies eta_t=O((t+1)^(-1/2)). It is explicit
and does not depend on pi,q,L. As usual a bound exceeding 1 is uninformative.

For a bound using only the declared entropy budgets, take

  M=exp(2K+1), s0>=1/2,
  a0=K/(log R-1), b0=J/(log B-1),
  Delta0_t=sqrt[2U/(alpha(2t+1))].

Replacing a,b,Delta_t by a0,b0,Delta0_t in (2) gives an explicit valid
upper bound; every term on its right is nondecreasing in a,b,Delta_t.
One can replace a0,b0 by min(1,a0),min(1,b0) if desired.

3. Proof of (2)

3.1 Uniform near-harmonicity requires no initial-density control.

Let f be any measurable function with 0<=f<=1, and put g=C^t f. Write
p_j=2^(-t) binomial(t,j) for 0<=j<=t and p_j=0 otherwise. Since
C=(I+H)/2,

  g-Hg = sum_(j=0)^(t+1) (p_j-p_(j-1)) H^j f.

The coefficients sum to zero. The binomial sequence is unimodal, so the
sum of its positive successive differences is max_j p_j=eta_t, and the
sum of the absolute values of its negative differences is also eta_t.
Every H^j f takes values in [0,1]. Consequently

  |g(x)-Hg(x)| <= eta_t                                      (3)

pointwise (or for any fixed versions outside a common null set). In
particular, this holds where dq/dpi is arbitrarily large. The standard
central-binomial estimate, or Stirling's formula, gives eta_t=O(t^-1/2).

3.2 On the low-W tail, a global update nearly redraws from q.

For 0<=g<=1, the exact IMH identity is

  Gg(x)-qg
   = integral q(dy) [1-min(1,W(y)/W(x))] [g(x)-g(y)].

For x in D, the integrand can be nonzero only if W(y)<W(x)<1/B,
which implies y in D. Thus

  |Gg(x)-qg| <= b,  x in D.                                 (4)

Combining (3), (4), and H=alpha G+beta L gives

  g(x) = alpha qg + beta Lg(x) + r(x),
  |r(x)| <= alpha b + eta_t,  x in D.                       (5)

This step is where the factor beta is retained. The global term is the
same scalar qg for every starting x in D.

3.3 Anchor control of the common scalar and the core.

Now take f=1_F for an arbitrary measurable test event F, and let

  m = pi(g 1_A0)/s0.

Then m in [0,1]. The exact IMH form and conditional Jensen give

  E_G(g,g)
    = (1/2) integral pi(dx) pi(dy)
         (g(x)-g(y))^2 / max(W(x),W(y))
    >= (s0/2) integral (g-m)^2/max(W,M) dpi.                 (6)

This restricts only the y-leg to A0; its factor 1/2 is retained.
Reversibility and laziness put the spectrum of C in [0,1]. The spectral
measure of 1_F-pi(F) has total mass at most 1/4, so

  E_C(C^t 1_F,C^t 1_F)
    <= 1/[4(2t+1)],

using (1-lambda)lambda^(2t)<=1/(2t+1). Since
E_C >= (alpha/2) E_G, (6) implies

  integral (g-m)^2/max(W,M) dpi
     <= 1/[alpha s0(2t+1)].                                (7)

The two integrals needed for weighted Cauchy-Schwarz satisfy

  I = integral_S max(W,M)(1/W-1)^2 dpi <= M(B+1)+R = U,     (8)
  V = integral_S max(W,M)/W^2 dpi <= MB+1 <= U.              (9)

For (8), on W<=M use
(1/W-1)^2 <= 1/W^2+1 and 1/W^2<=B/W; this gives at most M(B+1).
On W>M the integrand is W-2+1/W<=W<=R, giving at most R.
For (9), on W<=M use M/W^2<=MB/W, whose pi-integral is at most MB.
On W>M the integrand is 1/W, whose pi-integral is at most 1.

By (7)--(9),

  |integral_S (g-m)(dq-dpi)| <= Delta_t,                    (10)
  integral_S |g-m| dq <= Delta_t.                           (11)

Also q(E)<=a/R and |g-m|<=1. Therefore (11) gives

  |qg-m| <= b+a/R+Delta_t.                                 (12)

This bound is for the common scalar in (5). It is of order b, not order
one, at the final choice of thresholds, so its multiplication by the
low-tail mass only costs a second-order error.

3.4 Signed-tail accounting retains the maximum of the two budgets.

Since W<1 on D and W>1 on E, the signed measure q-pi is nonnegative on
D and nonpositive on E. Let

  u=q(D)-pi(D),   v=pi(E)-q(E).

Then 0<=u<=b and 0<=v<=a. From (5), (12), and 0<=Lg<=1,

  -beta m-theta <= g(x)-m <= beta(1-m)+theta,  x in D,
  theta=alpha(2b+a/R+Delta_t)+eta_t.                        (13)

On E, the elementary bounds are -m<=g-m<=1-m. It follows that the
sum of the two outside integrals is bounded above by

  beta u(1-m)+v m+u theta <= max{beta u,v}+u theta,

and bounded below by

  -beta u m-v(1-m)-u theta >= -max{beta u,v}-u theta.

Thus

  |integral_(D union E) (g-m)(dq-dpi)|
      <= max{beta b,a}+b theta.                            (14)

The full signed measure has mass zero, hence
(q-pi)g=integral (g-m)(dq-dpi). Add (10) and (14) and substitute theta.
Taking the supremum over F proves (2).

4. Uniform asymptotic upper coefficient

By the binary relative-entropy tail bounds in the supplied source,

  a <= K/(log R-1),  b <= J/(log B-1).

For completeness, the first follows by writing h=pi(W>R), z=q(W>R),
noting z<=h/R, applying binary data processing, and using
(1-h)log(1-h)>=-h. It yields K>=h(log R-1). Apply the same argument
to the reversed pair (q,pi) for b. Choosing M=exp(2K+1) gives
s0>=1/2 by the first bound.

Let B=R=r_t=t/(log t)^4 for sufficiently large integer t. Then

  a <= K/(log r_t-1), b <= J/(log r_t-1),
  Delta_t = O_(K,alpha)((log t)^(-2)),
  b eta_t = O_(J)((log t)^(-1)t^(-1/2)),
  2 alpha b^2 = O_(J,alpha)((log t)^(-2)),
  alpha a b/R = O_(K,J,alpha)(1/[r_t(log t)^2]).

All constants are independent of pi,q,L. Therefore (2) yields

  limsup_(t->infinity) log t *
    sup_(admissible pi,q,L) TV(q C^t,pi)
       <= c_* := max{K,beta J}.                            (15)

For any delta>0, the uniform envelope is eventually at most
(c_*+delta)/log t. Taking t=ceil(exp[(c_*+2delta)/epsilon]) makes
this no larger than epsilon for every sufficiently small epsilon.
Thus limsup epsilon log T(epsilon)<=c_*. This uses finite-time TV
and uniformity; it does not assume that a minimax limit exists.

5. Matching lower coefficient and precise relationship to source

The supplied JOINT_KL_FUNNEL_NOTE.md, Section 1, already proves

  liminf_(epsilon down to 0) epsilon log T(epsilon)
     >= max{K,beta J}.                                    (16)

Its K construction takes L=I and pi=(p,1-p),
q=(p exp(-K/p),1-p exp(-K/p)); the forward divergence is below K,
the reverse divergence tends to 0, and the lazy-mixture contraction
rate differs from pure IMH only by alpha/2. This yields coefficient K.

Its beta J construction, for any fixed 0<rho<1 and ell>1/(beta rho),
takes b=ell epsilon, p=epsilon^2, kappa=K/2,
s=b exp(-J/b), r=p exp(-kappa/p),
pi=(p,s,1-p-s), q=(r,b,1-b-r).
For sufficiently small epsilon, both declared divergences meet their
budgets. The reversible path local kernel has
L_BA=rho, L_BC=1-rho, L_AB=rho s/p,
L_CB=(1-rho)s/(1-p-s), with diagonal complements.
It injects d=beta rho b>epsilon into A at the first non-lazy update,
and its non-lazy escape probability from A is

  h=alpha(1-p)exp(-kappa/p)+beta rho (b/p) exp(-J/b).

Binomial subordination for C gives, at integer n,

  q C^n(A) >= [d/(1-h)] [(1-h/2)^n-2^(-n)].

For any 0<c<log(beta rho ell), choose n=floor(2c/h). The last lower
bound divided by epsilon tends to beta rho ell exp(-c)>1, while
pi(A)/epsilon=epsilon tends to 0. Consequently this time is insufficient
and liminf epsilon log T>=J/ell. Let ell decrease to 1/(beta rho),
then let rho increase to 1. Every individual construction uses fixed
rho<1 and fixed alpha. This proves beta J. This paragraph reproduces
only the logical lower-bound bridge; the new advance is (2).

Combining (15) and (16) proves (1), including J>K. No new counterfamily
is claimed, and no extension to deterministic products, adaptive
selection, varying alpha, or nonreversible L is claimed.

TV(q C^n,pi) is nonincreasing in n because C is pi-invariant and Markov.
This justifies passing from an insufficient time in the lower family to
the first-time definition of T; the upper proof provides one common
sufficient integer time for every member of the class.

6. Validation and limits

The proof uses only algebra, Markov contraction, the existing exact IMH
Dirichlet form, positive-contraction spectral smoothing, and binary
entropy data processing. Numerical checks accompany this result to
catch constants/sign errors; they are not proof of universal quantifiers.
See check_inequality.py and numerical_checks.json for the recorded checks,
and OBSERVABLE_LOG.txt for exact activities and observed costs.

Recorded numerical outcome: 180 reversible instances, 4,860 threshold-time
checks, 900 final bounds below 1, and no positive violation of any of the
six checked component/final inequalities. The script ran in about 0.222 s.

The critical review points are: (3) is an L-infinity bound from binomial
unimodality, not an L2 claim; (4) integrates rejection only inside D;
(12) multiplies its O(b) error by u<=b; (14) combines opposite signs on
D and E, retaining max{beta b,a}. Dropping any of these features would
revert to the older coefficient or a weaker sum.
