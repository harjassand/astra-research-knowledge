\begin{abstract}
For the previously constructed closed qutrit device on an infinite regular tree, the supplied unnormalized-transcript lemma closes the fixed-horizon classical-feedback question: capacity remains $3/2$ qubits per forward call. We incorporate the lemma with normal measurable instruments and the correct finite vertex-coordinate cutoff. Its invariant is the Schmidt number of the complete block Choi state, not the number of Kraus operators.

We then solve a different cost operation. Permit a receiver-controlled stopping time, count every branch's forward calls, and require a deterministic decoded channel. For every fixed normal bath initializer, the minimum expected number of calls needed for logical dimension $K$ and fixed entanglement fidelity at least $f>0$ is $(2f/3)\log_2K+o(\log K)$. With uniform half-diamond error at most $\varepsilon<1$, the coefficient is $2(1-\varepsilon)/3$. These conclusions hold for both maximally-mixed-input and worst-input mean cost. A finite stopped-subchannel bound supplies the converse without transporting an unbounded expectation through trace-distance approximation. A receiver-selected lottery using the exact Dyck code attains the bound and returns the entire bath at termination.

Reliable expected-use capacity remains $3/2$, while its fixed-length strong converse fails. The lottery and classical expected-length error law have established predecessors; this account proves the quantum closed-memory specialization and its coherent-normal-state scope. No external certification, historical priority, efficient circuit synthesis, or material-device claim is made.

We also resolve endpoint stability for a new device family. Replacing edge reversal by $V_s=\cos(s)U+i\sin(s)I$ preserves the same faithful product stationary state. A coherent-history truncation, applied directly to each adaptive strategy, proves a strict capacity deficit for $|\sin s|\le1/512$. An inner/outer code corrects correlated error amplitudes without resetting the bath and supplies the matching lower limit. Both unassisted and backward-classical capacities tend to $3/2$ as $s\to0$. Their values at fixed nonzero $s$ remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The same device and the two inherited primitives}\label{sec:tree}

Let $T$ be the countable rooted $3$-regular tree. The root has child ports $0,1,2$. Every nonroot vertex has parent port $0$ and child ports $1,2$. Let $E=\ell^2(V(T))$. When the edge $v$--$w$ has labels $a$ at $v$ and $a'$ at $w$, set
\begin{equation}
 U\ket{a,v}=\ket{a',w}.\label{eq:U}
\end{equation}
This directed-edge reversal is a permutation involution, hence $U=U^\dagger$ and $U^2=I$. One fixed $U$ is repeated in chronological order on fresh external qutrits and the same bath. Write $W_n=U_{nE}\cdots U_{1E}$ and let $\cN_n^\tau$ be its block channel after tracing only the final bath.

The reference is $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1/8,1/8)$. Put $\pi(o)=1/3$ and, along each downward edge at parent port $a$, put
\[
 \pi(w)=\pi(v)\frac{\eta_a}{3/4},\qquad
 \tau_\pi=\sum_v\pi(v)\ket v\bra v.
\]
The mass at depth $\ell\ge1$ is $(4/9)(1/3)^{\ell-1}$, so these masses and the root mass sum to one. Edgewise detailed balance gives
\begin{equation}
 U(\eta\otimes\tau_\pi)U^\dagger=\eta\otimes\tau_\pi.\label{eq:stationary}
\end{equation}
Thus the exact same normal faithful stationary device from the owner checkpoint is used. The capacity and cost statements also extend to every fixed normal density $\tau$; stationarity is not required for that extension.

Set $a=2\sqrt2$ and $c=\log_2a=3/2$. Define $M_n=\lfloor a^n\rfloor$, including $M_0=1$.

\begin{lemma}[Individual operator ranks]\label{lem:walk}
For initial and final vertices $v,w$, let $A_{w,v}=\bra wW_n\ket v$. Then
\[
 \rank A_{w,v}=\#\{\text{length-$n$ walks from $v$ to $w$}\}\le M_n.
\]
For a vertex-diagonal initializer, $\cN_n^\tau$ has a countable Kraus family with every individual rank at most $M_n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$A_{w,v}$ is a partial permutation: each input word determines one walk and output word, and two input words with the same endpoint cannot have the same output by reversibility of $W_n$. Its rank is its number of nonzero columns.

Let $P$ be simple random walk on the tree, acting on $\ell^2(V)$ with counting measure. Put $h(v)=2^{-|v|/2}$. At a nonroot vertex, $(Ph)(v)/h(v)=2\sqrt2/3$; at the root the ratio is $1/\sqrt2\le2\sqrt2/3$. For finitely supported $g$, apply on each edge
\[
 2|g(v)g(w)|\le\frac{h(w)}{h(v)}|g(v)|^2+
 \frac{h(v)}{h(w)}|g(w)|^2.
\]
Summation proves the weighted Schur bound $\norm P\le2\sqrt2/3$. Consequently the walk count is $3^nP^n(v,w)\le a^n$. It is an integer, giving $M_n$. For $\tau=\sum_v p_v\ket v\bra v$, take Kraus operators $\sqrt{p_v}A_{w,v}$. Normality ensures $\sum_v p_v=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Exact code with terminal bath return]\label{lem:dyck}
For each $m\ge1$ there is a $2m$-call quantum code of dimension
\begin{equation}
 D_m=C_m2^m,\qquad C_m=\frac1{m+1}\binom{2m}{m},\label{eq:dyckdim}
\end{equation}
on which $W_{2m}$ acts as an external isometry tensor $I_E$. It preserves arbitrary reference entanglement and returns the bath, including an inert purification, exactly at the terminal block boundary.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use words with $m$ symbols in $\{1,2\}$ (colored pushes), $m$ zeros (pops), and no prefix with more pops than pushes. A push enters a child and emits $0$. A pop closes an edge opened within the same word and emits its push color. No pop crosses above the starting vertex, so the root's exceptional port $0$ is never used as an unmatched pop. The final bath vertex is the starting vertex.

The output word $F(w)$ is independent of that vertex. Its zero positions identify pushes; matching parentheses then recovers every push color from its corresponding pop output. Thus $F$ is injective and
\[
 W_{2m}(\ket w\otimes\ket v)=\ket{F(w)}\otimes\ket v
\]
for every allowed word and vertex. Linear extension and continuity prove the asserted isometry identity on the entire bath Hilbert space. Catalan counting gives \eqref{eq:dyckdim}. Inverting $F$ on its span and completing the decoder elsewhere gives a CPTP decoder.
\end{proof}
The bath can change during the block. Exact terminal return is not all-use return. If $m(K)$ is the least $m$ with $D_m\ge K$, elementary Catalan bounds give
\begin{equation}
 n_K:=2m(K)=\frac{\log_2K}{c}+O(\log\log K).\label{eq:length}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $4^m/[(2m+1)(m+1)]\le C_m\le4^m$ gives $\log_2D_m=3m+O(\log m)$, and choosing $m$ larger than $\log_2K/3$ by a suitable multiple of $\log\log K$ suffices. A $K$-dimensional subspace of this code transmits $\mathbb C^K$ exactly.

\section{Accepted fixed-horizon classical-feedback theorem}\label{sec:feedback}

\subsection{The general unnormalized-transcript lemma}

Consider any causal $n$-slot device with finite ports $X_i,Y_i$ and independent initial internal memory. Let $\cN_n:X_1\cdots X_n\to Y_1\cdots Y_n$ be its block channel with its internal interactions in the original order. Suppose it has Kraus operators $C_z$ with $\rank C_z\le M$. The index $z$ need not have at most $M$ values.

Before slot $i$, Alice uses a controlled CPTP map
$\mathcal A_i^{t_{<i}}:A_{i-1}\to A_iX_i$.
After receiving $Y_i$, Bob applies an instrument
$\{\mathcal B_i^{t_i|t_{<i}}:B_{i-1}Y_i\to B_i\}_{t_i}$,
retains its outcome and sends the classical value to Alice. His final decoder produces a $K$-dimensional logical output. Local measurements and discarded local environments can be retained in local memories and traced at the end. Initially the laboratories have no shared entanglement. Shared randomness generated by Bob's initial message is permitted and included in the transcript.

\begin{theorem}[Frozen transcript, with physical branch weights]\label{thm:transcript}
For each discrete complete transcript $t$, the actual unnormalized logical branch is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_t=\mathcal D_t\circ\cN_n\circ\mathcal E_t,\label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E_t$ is a transcript-specific CPTP block encoder and $\mathcal D_t$ is Bob's selected CP branch map. The complete logical Choi state has Schmidt number at most $M$, and
\begin{equation}
 F_e(\Lambda)\le\min\{1,M/K\}.\label{eq:feedbackbound}
\end{equation}
The conclusion also holds for normal measurable instruments with standard Borel outcomes and well-defined operational limits of such protocols.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First take finitely many local stages and discrete transcripts. Fix $t$ and do not divide by its probability. All classical controls are now parameters. Alice's later operators act on her retained memory and future inputs, disjoint from earlier device interactions and Bob's operators. Move Alice's operators before the device, preserving their internal order. Bob's earlier operators act on his memory and completed outputs; they commute with later device interactions. Move Bob's operators after the device, again preserving their order. The device interactions remain in their original chronological order.

Commutation of fixed operators on disjoint tensor factors is valid for entangled states. Remote steering therefore causes no problem for this unnormalized identity. It would be invalid to normalize intermediate branches or replace every $\mathcal E_t$ by the same map. Tracing local private systems proves \eqref{eq:factor}, also with an arbitrary logical reference. No claim is made that Alice knew $t$ in advance or that $\mathcal E_t$ alone describes her physical conditioned state.

Writing Kraus operators for the local maps gives logical branch operators
\[
 B_{t,b,z,e}=D_{t,b}C_zE_{t,e},\qquad \rank B_{t,b,z,e}\le M.
\]
All branches of the original physical protocol form a trace-preserving channel, hence $\sum B^\dagger B=I_K$. This follows from chronological instrument completeness, not from summing the encoders. Input-dependent transcript probabilities are already included. The singular-value inequality
\[
 |\Tr B|^2\le\rank(B)\Tr(B^\dagger B)
\]
then gives $F_e=K^{-2}\sum|\Tr B|^2\le M/K$. Countable nonnegative sums are harmless on the finite logical space. Equivalently, each vectorized $B$ has Schmidt rank at most $M$, so their positive mixture has Schmidt number at most $M$.

For measurable outcomes, Bob's whole strategy on the finite product of $Y$ ports defines a normal CP instrument with quantum output $\mathbb C^K$ and transcript $t$. Its finite-dimensional Choi-valued measure has a positive matrix density relative to its finite scalar trace measure $\mu$. Let $\mathcal D_t$ denote the corresponding CP density. Measurable controls give a measurable $\mathcal E_t$, and the same frozen-operator identity yields
\[
 \Lambda=\int\mathcal D_t\circ\cN_n\circ\mathcal E_t\,d\mu(t).
\]
A CP density need not individually be trace-nonincreasing. Only the integrated physical protocol is normalized. For example, $2t\,\id$ on $[0,1]$ integrates to $\id$ with Lebesgue measure but increases trace for $t>1/2$. No pointwise normalization is used.

On the finite logical Choi space, states of Schmidt number at most $M$ form a closed convex set: normalized vectors of Schmidt rank at most $M$ form a compact set, as does their convex hull. Positive integrals and limits remain in the corresponding cone. This proves the same bound for measurable branches and well-defined operational limits. Pathological nonmeasurable controls or undefined infinite local procedures are outside the contract.
\end{proof}

Taking $M$ to be the minimum possible maximal individual Kraus rank identifies the invariant as the block Choi Schmidt number. The equivalence and its overlap inequality are established finite-dimensional facts \cite{TH,CK}. They are used here only on finite external block and logical spaces. Infinite bath dimension does not turn this into an infinite-dimensional channel equivalence; that equivalence can fail \cite{Shirokov}.

The same argument applies to a selected collection of branches as a CP subchannel. If its subnormalized Choi state is $\omega$, then
\begin{equation}
 \bra{\Phi_K}\omega\ket{\Phi_K}\le
 \min\{1,M/K\}\Tr\omega.\label{eq:subchannel}
\end{equation}
No branch probability is normalized away. This form is the new stopping proof's essential input.

\subsection{Application and coherent initializer correction}

For the actual stationary $\tau_\pi$, Lemma~\ref{lem:walk} and Theorem~\ref{thm:transcript} give
\begin{equation}
 F_{e,n}\le\min\{1,M_n/K_n\}\le
 \min\{1,2^{n(c-R_n)}\},\qquad R_n=\log_2K_n/n.\label{eq:fixed}
\end{equation}
The unchanged Dyck code matches, so backward classical feedback capacity is exactly $c=3/2$. The bound holds for every vertex-diagonal normal initializer and has exponential decay at every rate separated above $c$ by a positive constant. No theorem that classical feedback is useless for general channels follows: their complete block rank ceiling may be the full port dimension.

For an arbitrary fixed normal $\tau$, choose a \emph{finite set of vertex coordinates} $F$ and put
\begin{equation}
 P_F=\sum_{v\in F}\ket v\bra v,\quad r=|F|,\quad
 q=\Tr(P_F\tau)>0,\quad \tau_F=P_F\tau P_F/q.\label{eq:cutoff}
\end{equation}
Every vector in its support has at most $r$ vertex components, so each corresponding device Kraus operator is a sum of at most $r$ matrices $A_{w,v}$ and has rank at most $rM_n$. An arbitrary rank-$r$ projection does not justify this statement. Projecting a purification of $\tau$ and normalizing gives
\begin{equation}
 \D(\tau,\tau_F)\le\sqrt{1-q}.\label{eq:gentle}
\end{equation}
Any fixed finite-horizon protocol is CPTP as a function of its initial bath. Its entanglement-fidelity test is an effect, hence
\begin{equation}
 F_{e,n}(\tau)\le\min\{1,rM_n/K_n+\sqrt{1-q}\}.\label{eq:coherentfixed}
\end{equation}
The error is a half-trace probability bound; the associated full trace-norm bound is $2\sqrt{1-q}$. Fix $F$ before taking $n\to\infty$, then enlarge it. Normality supplies $q\to1$, giving the same capacity and strong-converse limit for every fixed normal initializer. No uniform exponential rate follows for arbitrary coherent tails.

\section{New operation: receiver stopping and expected cost}\label{sec:stopping}

Bob may stop at a random integer $N\ge0$, determined by his classical instrument transcript, and send a classical stop instruction. Require termination almost surely on every logical input and a well-defined CPTP decoded channel $\Lambda_K$. Count all actual forward qutrit calls on every branch. There is no free Alice-controlled timing, silence, omitted-slot, or termination side channel; sending information forward requires a counted qutrit call. Infinite local processing is admitted only when it defines the stated normal instrument. No finite upper bound on $N$ is assumed.

Let
\begin{equation}
 n_K^\circ=\E_{\tau,I/K}N,\qquad
 \bar n_K=\sup_{\rho\in\mathcal S(\mathbb C^K)}\E_{\tau,\rho}N.\label{eq:costs}
\end{equation}
We prove the converse for $n_K^\circ$, so it also holds for $\bar n_K$. This choice includes arbitrary references: the duration distribution depends on the logical marginal. The actual initializer $\tau$ is the same for every $K$. A horizon-dependent supply state is excluded.

\begin{lemma}[A bounded stopped event]\label{lem:stopped}
For a vertex-diagonal initializer and every integer $L\ge0$, put
$\alpha_L=\min\{1,M_L/K\}$. Then
\begin{align}
 F_e&\le\alpha_L\Prob(N\le L)+\Prob(N>L),\label{eq:tailsharp}\\
 n_K^\circ&\ge(L+1)[F_e-M_L/K]_+.\label{eq:meandiag}
\end{align}
For any fixed normal $\tau$ and any coordinate cutoff \eqref{eq:cutoff},
\begin{align}
 F_e(\tau)&\le rM_L/K+\sqrt{1-q}+\Prob_{\tau,I/K}(N>L),\label{eq:tailnormal}\\
 n_K^\circ(\tau)&\ge(L+1)[F_e(\tau)-\sqrt{1-q}-rM_L/K]_+.\label{eq:meannormal}
\end{align}
These bounds do not assume termination or finite expected cost under $\tau_F$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Lambda_{\le L}$ be the CP trace-nonincreasing subchannel formed by all original branches stopping by $L$. It can be realized within an $L$-call circuit for the proof. Once a branch has stopped, store its decoded output, emit fixed dummy inputs for the remaining calls, and ignore their outputs. Those extra nonselective operations act on the bath and dummy systems, so cannot change the stored decoded marginal, even when it is correlated with the bath. Retain the original stopping flag and select it at the end.

This padding is mathematical, not a charge to the real protocol. Its final bath need not equal the real final bath. The fixed-horizon theorem applies to the selected subchannel. For its subnormalized Choi state $\omega_{\le L}$, \eqref{eq:subchannel} gives
\[
 \bra{\Phi_K}\omega_{\le L}\ket{\Phi_K}\le
 \alpha_L\Tr\omega_{\le L}=\alpha_L\Prob(N\le L).
\]
The remaining positive substate has trace $\Prob(N>L)$ and overlap at most that trace. Almost-sure termination of the actual protocol identifies their sum with its decoded Choi state. This proves \eqref{eq:tailsharp}. Since $N\ge L+1$ on the long event, the weaker bound $F_e\le M_L/K+\Prob(N>L)$ gives \eqref{eq:meandiag}.

For coherent $\tau$, apply the protocol only up to call $L$ and record a failure flag if it is still running. The event ``stopped by $L$ and passed the maximally entangled-state test'' is one effect on the initial bath. Its probability changes by at most $\D(\tau,\tau_F)\le\sqrt{1-q}$. Under $\tau_F$ its probability is at most $rM_L/K$ by the finite vertex support bound. Bound the long contribution using its probability under the \emph{actual} state $\tau$. This proves \eqref{eq:tailnormal}, and the same elementary tail bound proves \eqref{eq:meannormal}.

No unbounded expected stopping time was compared between nearby bath states. Such a continuity claim would be invalid. Only one bounded effect is compared, so the trace-distance error occurs once. The proof remains valid if the original infinite protocol would fail to terminate under $\tau_F$, because its prefix through $L$ is a defined finite instrument with a still-running flag.
\end{proof}

For a diagonal bath, grouping by the actual stopping length also gives the useful refinement
\begin{equation}
 F_e\le\sum_{n\ge0}p_n\min\{1,M_n/K\},\qquad
 p_n=\Prob_{\tau,I/K}(N=n).\label{eq:mixturelengths}
\end{equation}
One cannot replace $N$ by $\E N$ inside the exponential; convexity goes in the wrong direction. The proof of the leading frontier uses the bounded event instead.

\begin{theorem}[Expected cost lower bound]\label{thm:meanlower}
For any sequence of admissible protocols on one fixed normal initializer with $K\to\infty$ and $\liminf F_e\ge f$, where $0\le f\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{K\to\infty}\frac{n_K^\circ}{\log_2K}\ge\frac f c.
 \label{eq:meanlower}
\end{equation}
The same holds for worst-input mean $\bar n_K$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix a finite vertex set $F$ with mass $q>0$ before taking any message-size limit. With $x=\log_2K$, choose for all sufficiently large $K$
\[
 L_K=\left\lfloor\frac{x-\log_2r-\sqrt{x}}{c}\right\rfloor.
\]
Then $rM_{L_K}/K\le2^{-\sqrt x}$ and $(L_K+1)/x\to1/c$. Equation~\eqref{eq:meannormal} yields
\[
 \liminf\frac{n_K^\circ}{x}\ge\frac{[f-\sqrt{1-q}]_+}{c}.
\]
An increasing sequence of finite vertex sets has $q\to1$ by normality. Take this cutoff limit afterward. This proves \eqref{eq:meanlower}. Since the bound at fixed $K,F$ is uniform over protocols, it also bounds infimal costs even if no minimizing protocol exists.
\end{proof}

\section{Matching construction and complete error}\label{sec:lottery}

Let $K\ge2$, and use the exact length $n_K=2m(K)$ code from Lemma~\ref{lem:dyck}. Before any forward call, Bob samples a local Bernoulli($p$) coin, retains it and sends its one-bit value backward. On the run branch, transmit with the exact code. On the stop branch, make zero forward calls and let Bob prepare $I_K/K$. Alice's unused logical input remains local and is included in the environment of the decoded map. Every outcome is retained and included. The resulting CPTP channel is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_p=p\,\id_K+(1-p)\cD_K,\qquad
 \cD_K(X)=\Tr(X)I_K/K.\label{eq:lottery}
\end{equation}
Its duration is $N=n_K$ with probability $p$ and $N=0$ otherwise, independently of the logical input. Thus both means in \eqref{eq:costs} equal $pn_K$. This is not successful-branch accounting: the abort's error remains in the channel. It uses one backward classical coin bit, no backward quantum system, and no preshared resource. Both branches return the entire initial bath and its inert purification exactly at termination. If one requires a nonzero number of forward calls, a fixed two-call push/pop loop on the abort branch adds only $O(1)$ expected cost and also returns the bath.

\begin{lemma}[Exact error of the lottery]\label{lem:lotteryerror}
For \eqref{eq:lottery},
\begin{equation}
 F_e=p+(1-p)K^{-2},\qquad
 \varepsilon_\diamond=(1-p)(1-K^{-2})=1-F_e.\label{eq:errors}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On a maximally entangled input the depolarizing branch yields $I_{K^2}/K^2$, with overlap $K^{-2}$. This proves the fidelity identity.

The completely depolarizing channel is the equal mixture of the $K^2$ discrete Weyl conjugations. Removing the identity Weyl gives a channel $\mathcal W$ such that $\cD_K=K^{-2}\id+(1-K^{-2})\mathcal W$. Thus
$\tfrac12\norm{\id-\cD_K}_\diamond\le1-K^{-2}$.
On $\Ph$, the nonidentity Weyls produce Bell states orthogonal to $\Ph$; they distinguish $\mathcal W$ from $\id$ perfectly. The upper bound is attained. Homogeneity proves the second identity in \eqref{eq:errors}. This is a uniform arbitrary-reference error statement, not merely an average-input fidelity calculation.
\end{proof}

Let $C_\tau^\circ(K,f)$ and $\overline C_\tau(K,f)$ be infimal maximally-mixed and worst-input expected costs, respectively, among the specified protocols with $F_e\ge f$. Similarly define costs $C_\tau^{\diamond,\circ}(K,\varepsilon)$ and $\overline C_\tau^\diamond(K,\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon_\diamond\le\varepsilon$.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp leading cost and error frontier]\label{thm:frontier}
For every fixed normal initializer $\tau$ and every fixed $0<f\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{C_\tau^\circ(K,f)}{\log_2K}
 =\lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{\overline C_\tau(K,f)}{\log_2K}
 =\frac f c=\frac{2f}{3}.\label{eq:frontierfe}
\end{equation}
For every fixed $0\le\varepsilon<1$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{C_\tau^{\diamond,\circ}(K,\varepsilon)}{\log_2K}
 =\lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{\overline C_\tau^\diamond(K,\varepsilon)}{\log_2K}
 =\frac{1-\varepsilon}{c}.\label{eq:frontierdiamond}
\end{equation}
If error is specified as a full diamond allowance $e\in[0,2)$, the coefficient is $(1-e/2)/c$. The upper bounds already satisfy exact terminal bath and purification return, while the lower bounds require no return condition.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:meanlower} proves the first lower bound. For sufficiently large $K$, take
\[
 p=\frac{f-K^{-2}}{1-K^{-2}}\in[0,1].
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:lotteryerror} gives exactly $F_e=f$, and \eqref{eq:length} gives $pn_K/\log_2K\to f/c$. This proves both infimal-cost limits. When $f\le K^{-2}$, the zero-call branch already meets that finite-$K$ target; it does not affect the fixed-$f>0$ limit.

For any channel with half-diamond error at most $\varepsilon$, the maximally entangled input followed by its acceptance test gives $F_e\ge1-\varepsilon$. Apply the first lower bound. The same lottery with
$p=1-\varepsilon/(1-K^{-2})$ for sufficiently large $K$ achieves precisely that half-diamond error and expected coefficient $(1-\varepsilon)/c$. Full/half conversion gives the final statement.
\end{proof}

\section{Consequences and limits}\label{sec:consequences}

\subsection{Reliable capacity and the strong converse are different}

When $F_e\to1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:meanlower} bounds rate by $c=3/2$; the exact Dyck code attains it. The same holds for vanishing complete diamond error. Thus expected-cost relaxation does not increase reliable capacity.

At a fixed expected rate $R>c$, choose $p=c/R$ in \eqref{eq:lottery}. Then
\[
 \frac{\log_2K}{\bar n_K}\longrightarrow R,
 \qquad F_e\longrightarrow\frac cR>0,
 \qquad\varepsilon_\diamond\longrightarrow1-\frac cR<1.
\]
Conversely, applying the finite-cutoff bound to a subsequence realizing a limiting fidelity proves
\begin{equation}
 \limsup F_e\le\min\{1,c/R\}
 \quad\text{when}\quad \log_2K/\bar n_K\to R>0.\label{eq:ratefrontier}
\end{equation}
The same conclusion holds with $n_K^\circ$. Hence the positive limiting fidelity $c/R$ above capacity is optimal. The fixed-horizon strong converse fails under expectation alone. This failure needs only a two-point duration distribution, even though the converse allows unbounded support.

Equivalently, at a fixed nonzero entanglement-infidelity or half-diamond allowance $\varepsilon<1$, the expected-use rate is $c/(1-\varepsilon)$. This is a changed cost and error contract. It neither contradicts \eqref{eq:fixed} nor grants a successful branch free forward uses.

\subsection{What a high-reliability stopping distribution must do}

For a diagonal initializer, if $F_e\ge1-\varepsilon$ and $\alpha_L<1$, \eqref{eq:tailsharp} implies
\[
 \Prob(N\le L)\le\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\alpha_L}.
\]
For a general fixed normal initializer, \eqref{eq:tailnormal} implies
\[
 \Prob(N\le L)\le1-F_e+\sqrt{1-q}+rM_L/K.
\]
Fixing $F$ and taking $L$ below $(1-\gamma)\log_2K/c$ for any fixed $\gamma>0$ shows that reliable transmission must spend at least approximately $\log_2K/c$ calls on all but a vanishing fraction of branches; then enlarge $F$. A hidden population of arbitrarily cheap reliable branches cannot evade the theorem.

If stopping lengths concentrate below a cutoff $L_K$ with $rM_{L_K}/K\to0$, the same bound restores a strong-converse limit. For example, at rate $R>c$, concentration $N/\bar n_K\to1$ in probability permits a cutoff strictly between $\bar n_K$ and $\log_2K/c$, yielding $F_e\to0$. The lottery fails that concentration condition. The distinction is caused by the admitted duration distribution, not by a new source of bath entanglement.

\subsection{Invalid arguments and outstanding limits}

The following operations would invalidate this account: normalizing selected transcript branches before summing; using one encoder independent of the complete transcript; assuming CP densities are individually trace-nonincreasing; identifying individual error rank with the number of Kraus operators; replacing vertex-coordinate cutoffs by arbitrary finite-rank projections; transporting an unbounded expectation through a trace-distance approximation; or charging only the successful calls. Mathematical padding does not preserve the padded bath and is not counted as physical cost.

The frontiers are leading asymptotic statements, not exact finite-$K$ optima. Arbitrary coherent normal tails give no uniform convergence rate or common exponential bound. The encoder and decoder are mathematically specified, without optimized gate, runtime, laboratory-memory or energy bounds. Backward classical communication and local processing are free in the declared rate denominator; the matching construction uses one initial coin bit, but the converse permits unlimited measurable feedback. The tree is an abstract infinite-space unitary, not a qualified material implementation. Exact terminal return of the attaining protocols is stronger than bath marginal return at that endpoint and weaker than all-use return.

\section{Next construction: a coherent stationary perturbation}\label{sec:coherent}

Having closed the expected-cost operation, return to the original fixed-$n$ feedback contract and change the physical unitary in a controlled way. Keep the same bath, reference and normal stationary initializer, and define
\begin{equation}
 V_s=\alpha U+i\beta I,\qquad \alpha=\cos s,\quad\beta=\sin s,
 \qquad 0\le s\le\pi/2.\label{eq:partialswap}
\end{equation}
$U=U^\dagger$ and $U^2=I$ imply that $V_s$ is unitary. Equation~\eqref{eq:stationary} means $[U,\eta\otimes\tau_\pi]=0$, so
\begin{equation}
 V_s(\eta\otimes\tau_\pi)V_s^\dagger=\eta\otimes\tau_\pi\quad\text{for every }s.\label{eq:partialstationary}
\end{equation}
Every parameter choice is one fixed device across all calls and horizons. The endpoint $s=0$ is the original tree with capacity $c$, and $s=\pi/2$ is the identity channel up to phase with capacity $\log_2 3$. This family therefore isolates coherent dynamics while retaining exact normal faithful stationarity.

Replacing \eqref{eq:partialswap} by the stochastic mixture of $U$ and $I$ would change the process. Instead retain interference among histories. For a fixed $n$-call protocol, a history is a subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ of the calls on which the expansion selects $I$ rather than $U$. Let $k=|S|$ be its number of no-move calls. Define
\begin{align}
 R_{n,h}&=\sum_{k=0}^{h}\binom nk3^kM_{n-k},\label{eq:Rhistory}\\
 T_{n,h}&=\sum_{k=h+1}^{n}\binom nk\alpha^{n-k}\beta^k.\label{eq:Thistory}
\end{align}
$T_{n,h}$ bounds a coherent operator-amplitude remainder. It is not a probability of $k$ stochastic stays, since $\alpha+\beta$ generally exceeds one.

\begin{lemma}[Adaptive coherent-history certificate]\label{lem:history}
For any admissible fixed-$n$ backward-classical protocol on $V_s$ with a vertex-diagonal initializer,
\begin{equation}
 F_{e,n}\le\min\left\{1,\frac{R_{n,h}}K(1+T_{n,h})^2+
                 (2+T_{n,h})T_{n,h}\right\}.\label{eq:historyfe}
\end{equation}
For a fixed normal coherent initializer, the same upper bound holds with $R_{n,h}$ replaced by $rR_{n,h}$ and one additional $\sqrt{1-q}$ term, using \eqref{eq:cutoff}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use a common unnormalized branch representation, which also handles continuous outcomes without introducing coherent feedback. Bob's collapsed instrument and its scalar transcript measure $\mu$ depend on his strategy, not on which device history is substituted. The frozen encoder $\mathcal E_t$ likewise depends on the prescribed controls and $t$, not on that history. Choose common measurable finite Kraus lists $E_{t,e},D_{t,b}$ for these finite-dimensional maps, for example by their Choi square roots with zero-padding.

For each replacement history $S$ and common index $\nu=(t,b,e,v,w)$, let
\[
 F_\nu(S)=\sqrt{p_v}\,D_{t,b}A^S_{w,v}E_{t,e}.
\]
Each $U/I$ replacement sequence is a legal fixed causal device, so applying the same classical protocol to it is trace preserving. Consequently $Z_S:\psi\mapsto(F_\nu(S)\psi)_\nu$ is an isometry into the same output and direct-integral environment space. Expansion of the original device gives exactly
\[
 F_\nu(V_s)=\sum_S\alpha^{n-|S|}(i\beta)^{|S|}F_\nu(S),
 \qquad Z=\sum_S\alpha^{n-|S|}(i\beta)^{|S|}Z_S.
\]
The physical full protocol also makes $Z$ an isometry. This representation is mathematical; it never recombines distinct classical transcripts as an accessible quantum resource. Let $Z_{\le h}$ be the sum of the low histories. Since each $Z_S$ has norm one, the triangle inequality gives
\[
 \norm{Z-Z_{\le h}}\le T_{n,h},\qquad \norm{Z_{\le h}}\le1+T_{n,h}.
\]
This estimate is obtained directly on the adaptive circuit. It does not assert that a block-channel diamond estimate automatically bounds an adaptive strategy.

For the rank statement, use one common set of classical transcript, bath and local-environment indices for the full expansion. Freeze a transcript $t$ without normalizing it, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:transcript}. Its encoder $E_t$ and decoder branch $D_t$ do not depend on which history term is expanded. For a history with $k$ stays, the block operation is the original $n-k$-interaction device on those ordered ports, tensor the identity on the $k$ idle qutrit ports. Its vertex-conditioned Kraus matrices have rank at most $3^kM_{n-k}$. Multiplication by $D_t$ and $E_t$ cannot increase rank.

Summing the low histories coherently at the \emph{same} environment indices gives each truncated logical Kraus operator rank at most $R_{n,h}$. Different classical transcripts are still mixed, not coherently accessible. The truncated logical map is CP but need not be trace preserving or trace-nonincreasing. Its subnormalized Choi state $\omega_{\le h}$ has trace at most $(1+T_{n,h})^2$, so its overlap with $\Ph$ is at most $R_{n,h}(1+T_{n,h})^2/K$ by the unnormalized rank inequality.

On a purification of the initial state and maximally entangled logical input, let the two resulting vectors be $\psi$ and $\chi$. Their norm difference is at most $T=T_{n,h}$, with $\norm\psi=1$ and $\norm\chi\le1+T$. Hence
\[
 \norm{\ket\psi\bra\psi-\ket\chi\bra\chi}_1
 \le(\norm\psi+\norm\chi)\norm{\psi-\chi}\le(2+T)T.
\]
After tracing environments the fidelity effect changes by at most this full trace bound. No half-distance improvement is assumed for these unequally normalized states. Combining the bounds proves \eqref{eq:historyfe}.

The common-index construction used the normal instrument's finite-dimensional Choi-valued measure, so applies directly to standard Borel outcomes. Its Kraus density may increase trace pointwise; each complete replacement protocol supplies the integrated isometry normalization. Well-defined operational limits also preserve the bound. No uncountable orthogonal outcome basis or normal dephasing channel on an arbitrary $B(L^2)$ is assumed.

Finally a state with support on $r$ vertex coordinates has each history Kraus operator expressed as a sum of at most $r$ vertex matrices, multiplying the rank certificate by $r$. The true, finite-$n$ protocol's fidelity under $\tau$ and $\tau_F$ differs by at most $\sqrt{1-q}$. Apply that bounded-effect comparison only to the true normalized protocol, not to an unbounded cost or a renormalized truncated history sum.
\end{proof}

Let $h_2(t)=-t\log_2t-(1-t)\log_2(1-t)$ for $t\in(0,1)$. For $0<\theta<1/2$ set
\begin{align}
 B(\theta)&=c+h_2(\theta)+\theta(\log_2 3-c),\label{eq:Btheta}\\
 G_s(\theta)&=h_2(\theta)+(1-\theta)\log_2\alpha+
                  \theta\log_2\beta.\label{eq:Gtheta}
\end{align}
Take $0<s<\pi/2$ so the logarithms are finite.

\begin{theorem}[Upper stability and a robust strict deficit]\label{thm:stability}
If $\theta>\beta/(\alpha+\beta)$ and $G_s(\theta)<0$, then the fixed-horizon backward-classical capacity on every fixed normal initializer satisfies
\begin{equation}
 Q_{\leftarrow}(V_s,\tau)\le\min\{\log_2 3,B(\theta)\}.\label{eq:Qperturb}
\end{equation}
For a vertex-diagonal initializer, rates strictly above $B(\theta)$ have exponentially vanishing entanglement fidelity. In particular,
\begin{align}
 0<\sin s\le1/512&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 Q_{\leftarrow}(V_s,\tau)\le B(1/100)<1.58165<\log_2 3,\label{eq:explicitstable}\\
 \limsup_{s\downarrow0}Q_{\leftarrow}(V_s,\tau)&\le3/2.\label{eq:uppersemi}
\end{align}
This theorem supplies the upper bound. The next subsection proves the matching endpoint lower limit.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $h=\lfloor\theta n\rfloor$. The entropy bound $\binom nk\le2^{nh_2(k/n)}$ and monotonicity on $0\le t\le\theta<1/2$ of
$c+h_2(t)+t(\log_2 3-c)$ give
\[
 R_{n,h}\le(n+1)2^{nB(\theta)}.
\]
Its derivative is $\log_2[3(1-t)/(at)]>0$ on that interval. The tail exponent $h_2(t)+(1-t)\log_2\alpha+t\log_2\beta$ is decreasing for $t>\beta/(\alpha+\beta)$. Because $k>\lfloor\theta n\rfloor$ implies $k/n>\theta$, each tail summand is at most $2^{nG_s(\theta)}$. Thus
\[
 T_{n,h}\le(n+1)2^{nG_s(\theta)}\longrightarrow0
\]
exponentially. Equation~\eqref{eq:historyfe} proves the rate converse and diagonal exponential statement. For coherent $\tau$, fix its vertex cutoff before $n\to\infty$, then enlarge it as before.

For $\theta=1/100$ and $\beta\le1/512$, the peak is below $\theta$ and
$G_s(\theta)\le h_2(1/100)-9/100<-0.0092$.
The exact inequalities
\[
 100^{2500}<2^{202}99^{2475},\qquad
 3^{200}<2^{317},\qquad 3^{10000}>2^{15849}
\]
certify respectively $h_2(1/100)<0.0808$ and $1.5849<\log_2 3<1.585$. These explicit integer comparisons are reproducible arithmetic certificates, not a finite sample offered as a universal proof. They imply
$B(1/100)<1.5+0.0808+0.00085=1.58165$.
Its more precise value is approximately $1.581642761$.

For the limit, choose $\theta=\sqrt\beta$. For small positive $\beta$, $\theta<1/2$ and exceeds the peak. Using $\alpha\le1$ and $h_2(\theta)\le\theta\log_2(e/\theta)$ gives
\[
 G_s(\theta)\le\theta\log_2(e\theta)<0\quad(\theta<1/e).
\]
Meanwhile $B(\theta)\to c$, proving \eqref{eq:uppersemi}.
\end{proof}

The coherent expansion alone does not prove that the original exact Dyck code still returns the bath for $s\ne0$, nor does a bound proportional to $n\norm{V_s-U}$ settle a communication rate. The following construction supplies the missing lower limit by applying that estimate only to a \emph{fixed inner block}, then correcting a growing number of such blocks jointly.

\subsection{Robust coding without an independent bath for each block}

We use the inherited exact coding lemma proved in the unchanged owner source: a channel on a $D$-dimensional system with at most $k$ Kraus operators admits an exact code of dimension at least $\lceil D/k^4\rceil$. Its proof uses a greedy diagonal compression of the error-product operator system, the affine Tverberg theorem, and the Knill--Laflamme conditions; the full proof and primary dependencies are in the preserved source \cite{Owner,KLV}. This is an existence theorem, not an efficient coding claim.

Apply it to the uniform mixture of Pauli errors of weight at most $t$ on $m$ qubits. The number of errors is
\[
 k_{m,t}=\sum_{j=0}^t\binom mj3^j.
\]
The resulting exact code corrects every operator in their linear span, including coherent errors with environment-valued coefficients. For $t/m\le\delta<1/2$,
\[
 k_{m,t}\le(m+1)2^{m[h_2(\delta)+\delta\log_2 3]}.
\]
Thus this deliberately loose inherited bound gives asymptotic qubit rate at least
\begin{equation}
 A(\delta)=1-4[h_2(\delta)+\delta\log_2 3].\label{eq:outerrate}
\end{equation}
Only $A(\delta)\to1$ as $\delta\to0$ is needed; optimizing a coding constant is not the operation here.

\begin{theorem}[Lower stability from a bath-independent exact block]\label{thm:lowerstable}
Fix a Dyck block length $L=2m$ and let $\ell=\lfloor\log_2(C_m2^m)\rfloor\ge1$. Let $\widetilde U$ be any fixed unitary on the same qutrit and bath space, and put
\[
 \epsilon=L\norm{\widetilde U-U}.
\]
For $\epsilon>0$, if $0<\delta<1/2$, $A(\delta)>0$,
$\delta>\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)$ and
\begin{equation}
 h_2(\delta)+\delta\log_2\epsilon<0,\label{eq:lowercondition}
\end{equation}
then ordinary unassisted capacity obeys, for every initial normal bath state,
\begin{equation}
 Q(\widetilde U,\tau)\ge\frac\ell L A(\delta).\label{eq:lowerbound}
\end{equation}
The constructed recovered channels have vanishing full diamond error uniformly over the initial bath. There is no reset between blocks and no claim of terminal bath return for $\widetilde U$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Restrict the exact Dyck code to $2^\ell$ dimensions. Choose an isometric inner decoder that maps its ideal output subspace to the logical $\ell$-qubit packet tensor one fixed local syndrome vector. It can be extended isometrically to the entire physical output by adding a sufficiently large \emph{external} decoder register $F$. With ideal $U$, the encoded, transmitted and decoded block is therefore the isometry
\[
 G_0\ket\psi_{PE}=\ket\psi_{PE}\otimes\ket0_F
\]
for every logical packet and bath vector, even when they are entangled. This uniform identity on $E$ is essential.

For the actual $\widetilde U$, the same operations define an isometry $G$ into the same output space. Telescoping the $L$ unitary interactions and composing the fixed encoding and decoder gives
$\norm{G-G_0}\le L\norm{\widetilde U-U}=\epsilon$.
Write $G=G_0+B$. Use $N$ consecutive inner blocks on the \emph{same} bath, with distinct external packets and decoder registers. Expand their product as a sum over bad-block subsets. A history with $j$ factors $B$ has norm at most $\epsilon^j$.

Because every other block equals the identity on both its packet and the bath, that history acts nontrivially only on its $j$ bad logical packets and on environmental registers. Corruption of the bath cannot spread to a later ideal packet: $G_0$ is the identity for every possible bath input, including one entangled with previous packets. Expanding its finite packet operators in a Pauli basis expresses the history as a linear combination of Pauli errors on those packets tensor bounded operators on $E$ and the decoder environments. No independence or probabilistic distribution of the errors is assumed.

Take $t=\lfloor\delta N\rfloor$. An outer code on $\ell N$ qubits correcting every Pauli error of weight at most $\ell t$ corrects all histories with at most $t$ bad packets. The inherited code bound supplies a logical dimension with
\[
 \log_2K_N\ge\ell N A(\delta)-4\log_2(\ell N+1).
\]
Let $Z_{\rm good}$ be the sum of these low-bad-block histories after outer encoding. The omitted remainder has operator norm at most
\[
 T_N'=\sum_{j>\lfloor\delta N\rfloor}\binom Nj\epsilon^j
 \le(N+1)2^{N[h_2(\delta)+\delta\log_2\epsilon]}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The inequality uses the decreasing binomial exponent above its peak $\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)$, and \eqref{eq:lowercondition} gives exponential decay.

Take an isometric Stinespring implementation of the outer recovery. The Knill--Laflamme conditions imply that for each correctable Pauli $P_a$,
\[
 \mathcal V_{\rm rec}P_a\mathcal V_{\rm enc}\ket\psi
 =\ket\psi\otimes\ket{s_a},
\]
with $s_a$ independent of $\psi$ (the syndrome vectors need not be orthogonal). Linearity proves the same factorization for every environment-valued correctable combination. At operator level, the recovered low-history sum is therefore $I_{\rm logical}\otimes Z$ for one bounded map $Z$ from $E$ into the final bath, decoder environments and recovery syndrome. The map $Z$ is independent of the logical input and need not be normalized.

Let $H$ be the actual recovered isometry on logical input tensor $E$. Then
\[
 \norm{H-I\otimes Z}\le T_N'.
\]
Testing on product vectors and using that $H$ is an isometry gives
$(1-T_N')\norm\xi\le\norm{Z\xi}\le(1+T_N')\norm\xi$ for every bath vector $\xi$.
For $T_N'<1$, the polar decomposition $Z=W|Z|$ consequently has an isometric polar part $W$ on all of $E$, and $\norm{Z-W}\le T_N'$. Therefore
\[
 \norm{H-I\otimes W}\le2T_N'.
\]
For two isometries, a norm difference $b$ bounds the induced channels' full diamond distance by $2b$, using the vector norm bound on purifications and contractivity under partial trace. Thus the recovered logical full diamond error is at most $4T_N'$; its half-diamond error is at most $2T_N'$. This operator estimate is uniform in the initial bath and its purification, without assigning a probability to the coherent history sum. Dividing $\log_2K_N$ by $NL$ counted forward calls proves \eqref{eq:lowerbound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Endpoint capacity continuity for the coherent family]\label{cor:continuity}
For every fixed normal initializer $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{s\downarrow0}Q(V_s,\tau)
 =\lim_{s\downarrow0}Q_{\leftarrow}(V_s,\tau)=3/2.\label{eq:continuity}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\norm{V_s-U}=2\sin(s/2)$. Fix $L$ first. Then $\epsilon=2L\sin(s/2)\to0$. Choose $\delta=\sqrt\epsilon$. For sufficiently small $s$, it exceeds $\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)$, $A(\delta)>0$, and
\[
 h_2(\delta)+\delta\log_2\epsilon
 \le\delta\log_2(e\delta)<0.
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:lowerstable} gives $\liminf_{s\downarrow0}Q(V_s,\tau)\ge\ell/L$. Now let the fixed inner block lengths tend to infinity; Lemma~\ref{lem:dyck} gives $\ell/L\to c$. Since $Q\le Q_{\leftarrow}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:stability}'s upper limit proves both equalities. The order is: choose a finite inner block, take the number of outer blocks to infinity for each fixed device, then consider $s\to0$, and finally enlarge the inner block. No device parameter or bath initializer is varied within a capacity code sequence for a fixed $s$.
\end{proof}

This lower argument is robust to any sufficiently small operator-norm perturbation on the same system-bath space, even if it does not preserve the reference state. The upper argument is specific to the coherent $U/I$ family. Their combination proves continuity only at the specified endpoint, not a general continuity theorem for all infinite-memory channels. The exact bath-return code is used as a mathematical ideal for error correction; actual perturbed blocks can change the bath. Their uniform recovered logical error does not certify bath or purification return.
