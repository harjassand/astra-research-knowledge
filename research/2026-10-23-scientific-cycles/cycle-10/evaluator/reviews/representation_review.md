## 3. Pro2: a real representational escape, with bounded significance

### The latest theorem

For each fixed `p>=2`, the native class consists of connected graphs whose actual maximal blocks are `K_(p,k)`, with `k>=p+1`; each cut vertex belongs to exactly two blocks and is on the larger bipartition side of each. Depth, branching and block sizes are unbounded. There are no bridges or isolated vertices in this class. The original matrix is arbitrary supported real symmetric with free diagonal, and every query row has Euclidean norm at most one. All original actions are observed with arbitrary absolute Frobenius noise. [P2a]

| Requirement | Worst-case coordinate action count | Principal qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| Exact identification | `p+1` | Inverse conditioning grows on long block chains |
| Uniformly stable recovery | `p+2` | Operator and Frobenius bound `sqrt(3+sqrt(5)) ||E||F` |
| Star-color recovery | `2p+1` | Stronger operator `sqrt(3/2)` and Frobenius `sqrt(2)` bounds |

The stable-coordinate lower also allows nonzero signed unequal one-coordinate row amplitudes of magnitude at most one. It does **not** cover dense real rows mixing coordinates. For `p=2`, `k in {3,4}` lies inside the original chordless, width-two, maximum-degree-four target. For larger `p`, the blocks are bipartite but generally not chordless. The full arbitrary-real four-versus-five problem on all degree-four partial two-trees remains open. [P2a–P2b]

### Why the new operation matters

The original marked-root plan revealed a genuine logical failure. A single `K_(2,4)` can supply its special safe state at any one designated middle vertex, but cannot supply it at two different middles simultaneously. Three such blocks in a path form a 16-vertex, 24-edge support with no star-four coloring. That disproves universal star-four gluing; it does not disprove stable four-action recovery. [P2a; P2c]

Pro2 then constructs a stable four-action inverse on that **same support**, and on an unbounded intrinsic block-tree class. Hubs use `p` colors. Middle vertices use two colors alternating with block depth. Each bichromatic component becomes a spider whose arms have length one or two. The bound follows from the two-variable inequality

\[
x^2+y^2\le\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}\bigl(x^2+(y-x)^2\bigr),
\]

summed over disjoint arms and followed by mean projection. The incidence inverse has a size-independent bound even with arbitrarily many arms. Disjoint observation cells across color-pair components and symmetric duplication supply the full Frobenius bound. This is a proof about the native observation operator, not an extrapolation from local rank or bounded diameter. [P2a]

The coordinate lower uses a central cut in a long chain: a mean-zero divergence with constant values on its two sides has a unit coefficient on the cut edge while its observation norm tends to zero. For unequal amplitudes, a diagonal witness and a congruence-transformed cut witness give a diverging lower bound. This separates exact invertibility from uniform stability within the stated representation. [P2a]

That distinction has a plausible broad use: “minimum action count for exact recovery” can be the wrong optimization target when an inverse must remain stable. The construction also shows why a convenient sufficient condition such as star coloring should not become the definition of the research problem.

### What ordinary methods already supply

Sparse-Hessian recovery through star coloring and acyclic-coloring substitution is established. The primary Hessian paper explicitly studies recovery algorithms, arithmetic complexity and numerical stability. Pro2 correctly acknowledges this. Allowing bichromatic trees, solving incidence systems or saving colors through substitution is not by itself a new principle. The bounded potentially new checkpoint is the exact/stable/star separation on this intrinsic class, with the particular absolute-noise theorem and original-action operation; priority of that exact theorem remains unestablished. [P2f; W3]

The actual native receiver accepts an edge list and discovers the blocks and parameter. It was used on the 16-vertex obstruction and a 27-vertex branching `p=3` graph. Rational full-action reconstructions and independent normal-equation checks support the implementation. They are not experimental evidence of an end-to-end application speedup. Preparation costs `O(n+m log m)` in the actual code; decoding costs `O(nq+m)` arithmetic, with bit complexity and acquisition still paid. The higher-action star control has better conditioning. A total-cost preference requires the real action price and accuracy requirement. [P2a; P2d–P2e]

The initial receiver failed through variable shadowing, and review found a potentially quadratic list-membership check. The failed source and unknown failed-run cost were retained. Those are concrete signs of useful implementation feedback, not evidence for a particular agent count or prompting intervention. The total recorded successful and diagnostic bodies amount to only about 0.1874 wall seconds; that is manifestly not total scientific work. [P2c; P2e]

### Consequence and drift assessment

This continuation remains scientifically defensible. It changed the feasible action/conditioning boundary on a native class and escaped an obstruction that invalidated the original plan. The next theta-block question could be consequential if it establishes a reusable compositional invariant for stability, resolves a broad intrinsic class, or exposes the next genuine obstruction. > A research operation can become substantially easier when an agent derives a smaller interface sufficient for the receiving observations and future contexts; when those observations change, verified distinguishing contexts can identify exactly what must be restored or refined.

| Pro3 rate refinement | A missing parameter is restored | The claimed rate has both a uniform upper and attainable native lower |

This is consistent with ordinary abstraction and refinement theory.
