## Decision
There is real, controlled evidence for oxygen-dependent nitrate formation in concentrated suspended droplets. Neither paper qualifies oxygen-only finishing of a millimolar plasma capture liquid with quantified total nitrogen retention, absence of NO regeneration, complete conversion, or a process energy/resource balance. This is an observation gap, NOT proof that oxygen-only chemistry cannot work. Do not credit nitrate appearance as 100% capture/retention. Do not transfer acid-fed HONO uptake rates into pre-captured nitrite conversion.

## Monroe 2025: actual operation and scope
Methods: medical nebulizer; individually optically trapped droplets, mean radius 3–4 micrometres; 532 nm/0.06 W trap and Raman probe; 20 C; 70–80% RH; inlet 0.3 L/min, exhaust 0.25 L/min; trap about 100 micrometres above coverslip, potentially transport-restricted boundary layer.

Direct nitrite-finishing subset: four droplets generated from 4 M sodium nitrite, pure oxygen (99.994%), nitrate appearance (8.0 +/- 5) x 10^-4 M/s. Reported modeled rate coefficient 0.76 +/- 0.2 M^-1 s^-1; ionic-strength-adjusted oxygen Henry coefficient 9.86 x 10^-4 M/atm. Authors infer nitrite remaining by subtracting nitrate formed from initial 4 M, expressly assuming stoichiometric equivalence and no concentration enhancement. This does not independently measure nitrogen retention. Three 4 M nitrite droplets under nitrogen (99.998%) showed no continuing nitrate formation beyond initial room-air introduction. No directly matched air rate/population for this nitrite subset is specified in the main text. Initial nitrite-droplet pH is not specified there. Authors classify their modeled oxygen penetration/kinetics as volume-limited, not surface-limited.

Separate HONO uptake subset: gas source is 0.3 M sodium nitrite plus sulfuric acid, 1:1 molar acid:nitrite. Source flow 0.02–0.06 L/min supplies nominal 46 +/- 13 to 140 +/- 37 ppmv HONO; reduced exposure 3–9 ppmv. This is new supplied HONO, not finishing an inventoried captured-nitrite droplet. Nitrate appears at 0.023 +/- 0.01 M/s in saturated (~6 M) NaCl (four droplets), and 0.024 +/- 0.01 M/s in 4 M bisulfate (three sodium, one ammonium). Sulfate-buffered droplets produce (3.7 +/- 1) x 10^-4 M/s (two 2.5 M MgSO4 + 1 M NaCl, one 4 M ammonium sulfate). HONO nitrogen-atmosphere controls show no apparent uptake/product. Modeled final pH of NaCl/bisulfate droplets is -0.38/-0.34. An initially near-neutral unbuffered seed is therefore NOT a neutral-liquid endpoint. Sulfate buffering suppresses the fast pathway. Strong acid/hygroscopic salts and downstream liquid restoration belong in any resource ledger.

The authors discuss competing nitrous-acid decomposition and escaping NO/NO2 in earlier literature, but their own experiment does not provide outlet NO/NO2/HONO measurements or total-N closure. Nitrate is the monitored quantitative species; nitrite has a weaker Raman mode. Baseline bulk Raman standards are not a same-condition kinetic bulk control. Their fast acidic reaction orders are interpretations of high/low gas-HONO uptake, not a validated dilute-liquid constitutive law.

Light: trapping and sensing share 532 nm illumination. Main text does not establish a same-droplet laser-off kinetic control. It states an OH-scavenger exclusion, but the accessible main text does not supply the scavenger identity/concentration/data; supporting material was not retrievable. Do not invent that control's detailed strength.

## Meroni 2026: concise extraction
0.5–1 M NaNO2 + 1 M NaClO4; pH 9 +/-0.2 (also pH 5 via H2SO4). Radii 5/3.5 micrometres, laser 532 nm at 30/16 mW, three repetitions; humidified air 100 sccm after trapping. Upstream/downstream RH 99–100%/92–95%. Air: 20% O2/80% N2; nitrogen suppresses but does not eliminate nitrate, air ~threefold higher at 45 min. KSCN cuts production ~50%. pH 5/9 curves similar. Bulk controls: identical solution/pH, 10 mL amber vials, IC, still/stirred, no nitrate at 100 min. Raman nitrate/perchlorate area ratio (1050/932 cm^-1), calibrated using 200–1000 mM nitrate + 1 M perchlorate. Perchlorate is described as stable/nonvolatile/nonreacting, but potentially radical-enhancing; no-perchlorate evidence is assigned to SI Figure S4. Nitrite peaks disappear qualitatively. No gas-N inventory is reported. Light-effect argument: weak absorption and smaller-droplet enhancement despite lower laser power; not a same-size light-dose control. Authors flag nebulization concentration and report asymptotic apparent nitrate fractions, not quantitative nitrogen closure.

## Internal-standard inference (our analysis, not an author claim)
With Q = I(NO3)/I(ClO4) = beta*n(NO3)/n(ClO4), conserved perchlorate and known initial feed ratio give retained nitrate fraction y = (Q/beta)/(n(NO2,0)/n(ClO4,0)). Common water loss cancels. Their nominal-1-M-perchlorate calibration could therefore already encode a molar-inventory ratio, rather than a physical concentration at the post-nebulization volume. The paper's evaporation upper-bound warning and the calibration description are not automatically consistent. Actual calibration coefficients, spectra-processing implementation, and an independent initial inventory are not available in the inspected text. Preserve this precise ambiguity rather than dismissing high nitrate yield simply because water evaporates. Perchlorate influencing chemistry does not itself imply perchlorate consumption; conversely the label 'inert' is not a measured inventory.

If a defensible nitrate fraction exceeds 1/3, pure 3 HNO2 -> HNO3 + 2 NO + H2O cannot explain all nitrate in a closed initial N inventory. For a two-path mixture of full-N-retaining oxidation and disproportionation, y = x+(1-x)/3 gives x_min=(3y-1)/2 if all input reacts; incomplete reaction only increases required oxidation fraction for fixed y. At y=0.843, this lower bound is 0.7645. This does not exclude concurrent NO production, gas loss, or recycled NO oxidation. Oxygen dependence supports oxygen involvement but does not independently establish a sole reaction path or zero emissions.

## Transfer and balance
Desired net stoichiometry: 2 NO2- + O2 -> 2 NO3-. Oxygen demand 0.5 mol O2/mol N = 1.1429 kg O2/kg N, if this is the exclusive route. Acidic disproportionation retains only one third as nitrate if gaseous NO escapes, with two thirds N requiring recapture/reoxidation.

Concentrating 1 mM feed to 0.5, 1, or 4 M without N loss would require nominal concentration factors 500, 1000, 4000, leaving respectively 0.2%, 0.1%, 0.025% of initial liquid volume. These are algebraic dilution/concentration illustrations, not an assertion evaporation is required or energetically optimized. Concentrating is not free, and use of 1 M perchlorate adds a foreign-ion inventory/recovery liability. No study tests mM plasma-liquid matrices with the measured droplet kinetics.

A fitted asymptote is not eventual completion. The 2026 k is the approach-to-plateau rate, not the full nitrite-disappearance rate. Never use t99=4.605/k as time to 99% of initial N converted: it means 99% of A. Same-condition bulk negative controls establish an effect at the test chemistry, not useful scale economics.
