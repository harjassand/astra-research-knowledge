2. THEOREM ACTUALLY REVIEWED

Let d>=2 and P=diag(p_i)>0, with sum_i p_i=1. The reference charge law is
fair, with color law P, and sigma_P=(|0><0|/2 direct-sum P/2)^tensor k.
For every prefix density zeta, including coherent and entangled densities,
the target is the weighted square of the finite deep-bulk coefficient

  H_zeta=Tr_F[(I tensor zeta)G_1]+2Q-kI.

Set A=2(1-Tr P^2). There is a unique positive threshold t satisfying
sum_i(1-t/p_i)_+=1. Write r_i=(1-t/p_i)_+, R_*=diag(r_i), and
B=sum_i p_i(1-r_i)^2. For k>=2 define

  C_k=sum_{n=0}^{floor((k-2)/2)}(k-1-2n)4^(-n) binom(2n,n).

The current control establishes

  min_zeta Tr(sigma_P H_zeta^2)
    = v_k(P)=A k(k-1)/8+(2B-A)C_k/4,

attained by zeta_*=sum_i r_i (|i><i|)^tensor k. The operation report's
s=1-Tr P^2 convention gives s k(k-1)/4+(B-s)C_k/2, the same expression.

3. LOAD-BEARING ANALYTICAL CHECKS

The native stack proof is an algebraic source-wire identity, not a probability
argument that requires uniform colors. Deleting matched push/pop factors and
undoing the prefix leaves three cases according to prefix height h and middle
deficit delta. They give an input/input swap, an input/bath swap, or two export
placements. Input-color permutations preserve P^tensor q.

The input/input contribution is Tr[P^tensor2(I-SWAP)^2]=A. The critical
input/bath contraction is I-R_m for the full marginal density R_m; the native
swap convention produces no transpose. For Hermitian R,

  Tr[P(I-R)^2]=sum_i p_i(1-R_ii)^2
              +sum_{i<j}(p_i+p_j)|R_ij|^2.

Pinching preserves positivity and trace and decreases this objective. The
strictly convex diagonal minimization gives the displayed water-filling
threshold. Thus arbitrary complex marginals and entangled competitors are
covered. At least two active colors are necessary. Threshold ties have zero
primal weight and must not be mistaken for positive dual slack.

The lower bounds are simultaneously attainable: the single repeated-color
mixture zeta_* has every marginal R_*, and its pure components cancel both
export placements before mixing. This is a genuine global-attainability
argument, not an assumption that independently optimal marginals are compatible.

Different endpoint/charge blocks are distinct matrix entries. For the retained
sectors the adjoint weighted norm agrees because the relevant permutations
preserve P^tensor q. Independent fair-walk prefix heights and middle deficits
therefore give

  v_k=1/2 sum_{i+r<=k-2}
       [A Pr(M_i>M_r)+B Pr(M_i=M_r)].

Triangle symmetry and the reflection identity for fair-walk maxima produce
C_k and the stated factors. The generating function for the collision sum is
1/[(1-z)sqrt(1-z^2)]. The closed central-binomial expression in the control
report follows from summing its coefficients. No finite-size extrapolation is
needed for the all-k formula.

4. DUAL, BOUNDARY CASES AND NONCOMMUTATION

Only the optimizer H_* is asserted to commute with sigma_P. Let
Delta=diag((t-p_i)_+) and T_k(X)=Tr_A[(X tensor I)G_1]. The critical cross
contraction is

  Tr[P(I-R_*)]I-P(I-R_*)=BI+Delta,

because P(I-R_*)=diag(min(p_i,t)) and B=Tr[P(I-R_*)]-t.
The other cases contribute AI or zero. Thus

  D_k=T_k(sigma_P H_*)=v_k I+sum_{m=0}^{k-2}w_{k,m}Delta^(m)>=v_k I,

with the explicitly given nonnegative walk weights. Every listed weight is
strictly positive: the adjacent-endpoint event after m zero ports contributes
2^(-m-1). Their sum is C_k/2. The final prefix coordinate is unused here.

The claimed kernel is therefore
span{|i>:p_i>=t}^tensor(k-1) tensor C^d, and complementarity holds for zeta_*.
The report correctly says support alone is not sufficient for optimality.
The exact quadratic identity

  Tr(sigma_P H_zeta^2)=v_k+2Tr[zeta(D_k-v_kI)]
                         +Tr[sigma_P(H_zeta-H_*)^2]

uses the commuting optimizer in its cross term; it does not assume commutation
for an arbitrary competitor. Strict positivity of sigma_P gives the stated
equality criterion H_zeta=H_*. At a general candidate the gradient instead
uses T_k((sigma_P H+H sigma_P)/2), as the current report explicitly records.

The dual is scalar exactly when min_i p_i>=t. Rank deficiency alone does not
force it to be nonscalar. The tie example P=(2/5,2/5,1/5), t=1/5,
r=(1/2,1/2,0) has zero dual slack and is consistent. These boundary distinctions
are material parts of the proof, not cosmetic qualifications.
