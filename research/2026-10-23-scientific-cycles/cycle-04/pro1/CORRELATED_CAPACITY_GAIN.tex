\begin{abstract}
The previously selected two-call input family resolves the live strict-gain
versus plateau question for the coherent recurrent tree. For every fixed
$0<\beta<1$, the unchanged device $V=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}U+i\beta I$ admits one
unassisted code sequence that, for every fixed normal bath initializer,
preserves arbitrary reference entanglement at rate at least
$3/2+\beta^4/1024$ qubits per counted forward call, with vanishing full-diamond
error. In particular the actual fixed $\beta=1/512$ and $\tau_\pi$ have capacity
strictly above $3/2$. The guaranteed increment exceeds $2^{-46}$; the explicit
input's entropy gives a rate increment approximately $1.8171\times10^{-14}$.

The mechanism is a temporal coherence that changes bulk bath drift linearly
in $\beta^2c$, while its input entropy cost is quadratic in $c$. An exact
calculation retaining the vacuum exchange produces a closed five-step depth
chain, with both exceptional boundary rows. A fixed high-entropy density has
strictly negative bulk drift. An exponential moment bound on the actual
infinite bath proves bounded bath and complementary entropy, without a reset
or alternative initializer. Exact spectral type spaces and the inherited joint
compression theorem convert this into deterministic arbitrary-reference codes.

This is a strict separation from the optimized single-site iid coherent-
information ceiling, not a contradiction of it. The full capacity, feedback
equality, leading small-angle gain and efficient realization remain open.
The result is an asymptotic foundational construction; its small drift gives
very large constants and no practical blocklength claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{The exact correlated block map}

Consider the fixed two-call density
\[
\begin{split}
 \varrho={}&x|00\rangle\langle00|
 +z\sum_{i,j=1}^2|ij\rangle\langle ij|\\
 &+\sum_{j=1}^2\{y(|j0\rangle\langle j0|+|0j\rangle\langle0j|)
 +c(|j0\rangle\langle0j|+|0j\rangle\langle j0|)\},
\end{split}\tag{5}
\]
where $x,z\ge0$, $y\ge|c|$, $c\in\mathbb R$, $x+4y+4z=1$.
Its eigenvalues are $x$, four copies of $z$, and two copies each of $y+c,y-c$.
Both marginals have zero probability $q=x+2y$ and equal color probabilities.
Let
\[
 \mathcal C(X)=\sum_jS_jXS_j^*,\quad
 \mathcal D(X)=\sum_jS_j^*XS_j,
\]
\[
 A(X)=a(RXR+\mathcal D(X))+bX-i\alpha\beta[R,X],\qquad
 B(X)=a\mathcal C(X)+2bX.
 \tag{6}
\]
The map $A$ preserves trace; $B$ multiplies it by two.

\begin{lemma}[Complete root-retaining bath map]\label{lem:map}
The normal CPTP bath map of the two ordered calls on input (5) is
\[
 \Phi=xA^2+y(AB+BA)+zB^2+c\Gamma,
 \tag{7}
\]
where composition is right-to-left and
\[
\begin{split}
 \Gamma(X)={}&ab\{2\mathcal C(X)+2\mathcal D(X)-6X+P_0X+XP_0\}\\
 &+i\alpha^3\beta\{\mathcal C([X,R])+2[R,X]
                  +RX(I-P_0)-(I-P_0)XR\}.
\end{split}\tag{8}
\]
Equation (8) is an identity on all trace-class operators; $\Gamma$ alone
need not be positive.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Writing $V_{oi}=\bra oV\ket i$, the ordered bath amplitude is
$K_{o_1o_2,i_1i_2}=V_{o_2i_2}V_{o_1i_1}$. The complete map is
\[
 \Phi(X)=\sum_{o,i,j}\varrho_{ij}K_{o,i}XK_{o,j}^*.
 \tag{9}
\]
The diagonal components immediately give the first three terms of (7).
For the two coherent inputs $j0$ and $0j$, the only common nonzero output
amplitudes are the following:
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 \text{output}&j0&0j\\\hline
 00&i\alpha\beta S_j&aS_jR+i\alpha\beta S_j\\
 0j&aI&i\alpha\beta R-bI\\
 j0&i\alpha\beta R-bI&aS_jS_j^*\\
 jj&i\alpha\beta S_j^*&i\alpha\beta S_j^*
\end{array}\tag{10}
\]
Multiply each left amplitude by $X$ and by the adjoint of its partner, add
the reversed pair, and sum over $j$. Relations (2) give (8), with all root
terms retained. This finite algebra proves the identity for arbitrary $X$.
Positivity and normalization of (5) and unitarity of $W_2$ prove normal
complete positivity and trace preservation of (9). Equivalently diagonalize
(5); its nine eigenvectors and nine output words give at most 81 Kraus
operators. Every such operator has depth bandwidth at most two.
\end{proof}

\section{A closed depth law with the exact boundary}

Put $e_\ell=1_{\{\ell\}}(L)/2^{\ell+1}$. Formula (6) and (8) give
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
 A(e_0)=e_0,&A(e_\ell)=ae_{\ell-1}+be_\ell\quad(\ell\ge1),\\
 B(e_\ell)=2ae_{\ell+1}+2be_\ell,&
 \Gamma(e_0)=4ab(e_1-e_0),\\
 &\Gamma(e_\ell)=ab(4e_{\ell+1}+2e_{\ell-1}-6e_\ell)\quad(\ell\ge1).
\end{array}\tag{11}
\]
Thus radial states remain radial. In particular this applies to (4).
The stronger observable identity is useful for coherent initializers.

\begin{lemma}[Exact depth Markov reduction]\label{lem:depth}
For every bounded function $f$ on $\mathbb N_0$, $\Phi^*f(L)=(Pf)(L)$,
where $P$ is the following stochastic kernel on the infinite half-line.
At depths $\ell\ge2$ its increment probabilities are
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 k&-2&-1&0&1&2\\\hline
 p_k&xa^2&2ab(x+2y+c)&b^2+4ya^2-6cab&4ab(y+2z+c)&4za^2.
\end{array}\tag{12}
\]
At depth one, the $-2$ transition is clamped to zero. At depth zero,
\[
 P(0,2)=p_2,\quad P(0,1)=p_1+2ya^2,\quad
 P(0,0)=1-p_1-2ya^2-p_2.
 \tag{13}
\]
Consequently this exact depth law holds for every normal bath initializer.
It is not a stochastic replacement for the full coherent port process.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the zero-input dual,
$A^*f(L)=bf(L)+af((L-1)_+)$: the commutator with $R$ vanishes on this
observable, and $Rf(L)R=f(0)P_0$ supplies the boundary. The unnormalized
color dual is $B^*f(L)=2bf(L)+2af(L+1)$.
In (8), the imaginary terms of the dual vanish on $f(L)$:
$\mathcal D(f(L))=2f(L+1)$ commutes with $R$, and
$(I-P_0)f(L)R=Rf(L)(I-P_0)=0$. Its real dual is
\[
 \Gamma^*f(L)=ab\{4f(L+1)+2f(L-1)(I-P_0)-6f(L)+2f(0)P_0\},
 \tag{14}
\]
where the value $f(-1)$ is immaterial. Substitution in (7) proves all rows.
The entries sum to one by $a+b=1$ and $x+4y+4z=1$.
Their positivity also follows from the channel, but can be checked directly.
Only the zero increment requires a useful rearrangement:
\[
 p_0=b^2(x+4z)+4y(a^2+b^2)-6cab
 \ge b^2(x+4z)+y[4(a-b)^2+2ab]\ge0.
\]
At the root,
$P(0,0)=x+y(2a^2+4b)+4zb^2-4cab\ge x+2ya^2+4yb^2+4zb^2$,
and $P(0,1)=2ay(1+b)+8zab+4cab\ge2a^2y+8zab$.
Iteration of the bounded observable identity and normal duality proves the
claim for every normal density. No bath measurement is performed.
\end{proof}

Let $P_1=\sum_jS_jP_0S_j^*$ and $\delta=1/2-q$.
The exact bounded depth increment is
\[
 \boxed{\Phi^*(L)-L=dI+2a[x+y(1+b)+bc]P_0+xa^2P_1,\qquad
 d=4a\delta+2ab c.}\tag{15}
\]
It follows either from the rows or from (9): each amplitude has grade
$k=N(i)-N(o)$, since the device conserves depth plus port color-number.
The coherent terms connect equal-charge inputs, so their grade is common.
The sum $\sum_{o,i,j}k\varrho_{ij}K_{o,j}^*K_{o,i}$ is bounded and gives
(15) on the finite-coordinate core and by extension. It does not subtract
infinite mean depths.

The bulk term $2ab c$ is the new effect. Negative temporal coherence lowers
drift linearly in $\beta^2|c|$. It is absent from the single-site iid map.

\section{A fixed density with entropy above three and negative drift}

For $0<b\le1$, fix
\[
 \delta=\frac{b^2}{512},\qquad c=-\frac b{128},\qquad q=\frac12-\delta,
 \qquad x=q^2,\quad y=\frac{q(1-q)}2,\quad z=\frac{(1-q)^2}4.
 \tag{16}
\]
These quantities do not depend on the horizon.

\begin{lemma}[Analytic entropy and drift certificate]\label{lem:seed}
The density (16) is strictly positive, with all nine eigenvalues in
$[1/16,1/4]$, and
\[
 S(\varrho)-3\ge\frac{569}{262144}b^2>\frac{b^2}{512}>0.
 \tag{17}
\]
For $0<b<1$ its exact bulk depth drift is
\[
 d=-\kappa,\qquad \kappa=\frac{(1-b)b^2}{128}>0.
 \tag{18}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Normalization is immediate. Since $\delta\le1/512$,
$y=1/8-\delta^2/2>1/9>|c|$. The eigenvalue bounds follow directly:
$z\ge1/16$, $x>1/16$, and $y-|c|\ge15/128-1/524288>1/16$;
all are at most $1/4$.
The diagonal of (5) is the product of its two marginals, so its entropy is
\[
 S(\varrho)=3+2\delta-2[1-h_2(q)]
       -4y\left[1-h_2\left(\frac{1+c/y}{2}\right)\right].
 \tag{19}
\]
For $|u|<1$, twice differentiating the binary entropy and integrating gives
\[
 \frac{u^2}{2\ln2}\le1-h_2((1+u)/2)
 \le\frac{u^2}{2\ln2(1-u^2)}.
 \tag{20}
\]
Using $\ln2\ge2/3$, $y\ge1/9$ and $|c|/y\le9/128$, the two losses in
(19) are at most $7\delta^2$ and $28c^2$, respectively. Thus
\[
 S(\varrho)-3\ge2\delta-28c^2-7\delta^2
 =\frac9{4096}b^2-\frac7{262144}b^4
 \ge\frac{569}{262144}b^2>\frac{b^2}{512}.
\]
The elementary bound on $\ln2$ follows from the first term of
$\ln[(1+t)/(1-t)]=2\sum_{k\ge0}t^{2k+1}/(2k+1)$ at $t=1/3$.
Substitution into (15) gives
$4\delta+2bc=b^2/128-b^2/64=-b^2/128$, proving (18).
\end{proof}

At the actual fixed $\beta=1/512$ one has
$b=2^{-18}$, $\delta=2^{-45}$, $c=-2^{-25}$, and
\[
 \kappa=\frac{262143}{2305843009213693952}
   =1.1368640404\ldots\times10^{-13}.
 \tag{21}
\]
The numerical diagnostic gives
$S(\varrho)=3.0000000000000363414892363876858\ldots$.
The proof of positivity is (17), not subtraction of floating-point entropies.

\begin{proposition}[The averaging density is separable across its two calls]
\label{prop:separable}
Let $\sigma=\operatorname{diag}(q,(1-q)/2,(1-q)/2)$ and
$u=\sqrt{b/64}=\beta/8$. For $j=1,2$ and
$\phi\in\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ define
\[
 \sigma_j^\pm(\phi)=\sigma\pm u
 (e^{i\phi}|j\rangle\langle0|+e^{-i\phi}|0\rangle\langle j|).
\]
These are densities and the candidate (16) satisfies
\[
 \varrho=\frac18\sum_{j,\phi}\sigma_j^+(\phi)\otimes\sigma_j^-(\phi).
 \tag{21a}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each two-dimensional nontrivial block is positive because
$u^2=b/64\le1/64<y=q(1-q)/2$; trace is one.
The four phases cancel all terms linear in $u$ and the terms with phase
$e^{\pm2i\phi}$. The surviving pair terms for each $j$ are
$-u^2(|j0\rangle\langle0j|+|0j\rangle\langle j0|)$.
Averaging the two colors gives coefficient $-u^2/2=-b/128=c$, with the
unchanged product diagonal. This proves the identity.
\end{proof}

The stabilizing average therefore needs temporal correlations and coherent
local components, but not an entangled density across the pair. This does
not assert that the eventual logical quantum code is separable across calls;
its isometric encoder is selected inside a large spectral type space.

\section{Infinite-bath tightness and bounded complementary information}

The drift in (18) is a bulk statement. The following step retains the exact
root and level-one behavior and proves a genuine all-time tail bound.

\begin{lemma}[Exponential moment on the actual infinite bath]\label{lem:moment}
Fix $0<b<1$ and (16). Set
\[
 t=\frac{\kappa}{32},\qquad
 \lambda=1-\frac{\kappa^2}{64},\qquad
 C_0=\frac{64e^{3t}}{\kappa^2}.
 \tag{22}
\]
For every initial bath density with finite $\Tr(e^{tL}\tau)$,
\[
 \Tr[e^{tL}\Phi^m(\tau)]
 \le\lambda^m\Tr(e^{tL}\tau)+C_0(1-\lambda^m).
 \tag{23}
\]
For every normal initializer supported in $P_{\le a}$, this implies
\[
 \Tr[(I-P_{\le h})\Phi^m(\tau)]
 \le(1+C_0)e^{-t(h-a)}\quad(h\ge a),
 \tag{24}
\]
uniformly in $m$ and in coherent initializers in that support.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a bulk increment $J\in[-2,2]$, Taylor's remainder gives, for $0<t\le1$,
\[
 \E e^{tJ}\le1-\kappa t+2e^{2t}t^2
 \le1-\kappa t+16t^2=1-\kappa^2/64=\lambda,
\]
where $e^2<8$ and $t=\kappa/32$ are used. Here
$0<\kappa\le1/128$, so $0<t<1$ and $0<\lambda<1$.
At either boundary level, finite propagation gives $L'\le3$.
Consequently the exact Markov operator satisfies
\[
 P(e^{t\ell})\le\lambda e^{t\ell}+e^{3t}1_{\{0,1\}}(\ell).
 \tag{25}
\]
The Markov identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:depth} extends from bounded to
nonnegative functions by monotone convergence. If the initial moment is
finite, each finite-step moment is finite since jumps are bounded. Iterating
(25) and summing the geometric series gives (23). Markov's tail inequality
and $\Tr(e^{tL}\tau)\le e^{ta}$ prove (24).
\end{proof}

For the actual initializer,
\[
 M_0=\Tr(e^{tL}\tau_\pi)=\frac{2/3}{1-e^t/3}<\infty,
 \qquad M_*:=\max(M_0,C_0).
 \tag{26}
\]
Thus $\sup_m\Tr(e^{tL}\tau_m)\le M_*$ and
$\sup_m\Tr(L\tau_m)\le\mu_*:=\ln(M_*)/t$ by Jensen's inequality.
These are statements about the actual initial trajectory. No different
normal invariant density is initialized or assumed reachable.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded bath entropy and the block coherent-information limit]
\label{prop:information}
For $\tau_m=\Phi^m(\tau_\pi)$ and (16),
\[
 \sup_m S(\tau_m)\le1+\mu_*+\log_2(e(\mu_*+1))<\infty.
 \tag{27}
\]
If $F$ purifies the fixed $\tau_\pi$, then $S(E_{2m}F)$ is uniformly bounded
on input $\varrho^{\otimes m}$, and
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{I_c(\varrho^{\otimes m},\mathcal N_{2m}^{\tau_\pi})}{2m}
 =\frac{S(\varrho)}2>\frac32+\frac{\beta^4}{1024}.
 \tag{28}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The actual bath remains radial by (11). Conditional on depth $\ell$ it is
uniform on $2^{\ell+1}$ vertices. Therefore
$S(\tau_m)=H(L_m)+1+\E L_m$. For an integer-valued nonnegative random variable
of mean $\mu$, comparison with the geometric distribution of that mean gives
$H\le(\mu+1)\log_2(\mu+1)-\mu\log_2\mu\le\log_2(e(\mu+1))$.
This also follows by relative entropy, with truncations if needed; it proves
finite entropy from the moment bound. Hence (27).

The unchanged purifier has $S(F)=S(\tau_\pi)$, so subadditivity bounds
$S(E_{2m}F)\le S(\tau_m)+S(\tau_\pi)$. Unitary invariance gives
$S(B^{2m}E_{2m})=mS(\varrho)+S(\tau_\pi)$, whence
$S(B^{2m})\ge mS(\varrho)+S(\tau_\pi)-S(\tau_m)$.
Consequently
\[
 mS(\varrho)-2S(\tau_m)\le I_c\le mS(\varrho).
\]
The upper bound is the entropy triangle inequality for the purifying logical
reference. Divide by $2m$, apply (27), then (17).
All output correlations are retained. This information limit is established
before, and independently of, the coding implication below.
\end{proof}

The finite exponential moment also makes the actual orbit uniformly tight
in vertex coordinates. If an invariant density is desired analytically, the
Cesaro means have a trace-norm convergent subsequence: truncate to a common
finite coordinate space and use finite-dimensional compactness, with the
tail controlled by $M_*e^{-th}$ from (23)--(26). Trace-norm contraction and the telescoping Cesaro
identity make any limit invariant. This existence statement is unnecessary
for either (27) or the code construction; no convergence or mixing rate to
an invariant state is asserted.

\section{Spectral type spaces and the whole physical block}

Let $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_9$ be the nine eigenvalues of the fixed density (16).
Use orthonormal eigenvectors of the \emph{two-call} port space; for the
coherent sectors they are $(|j0\rangle\pm|0j\rangle)/\sqrt2$.
For $m$ input pairs choose a type $n_1+\cdots+n_9=m$ of maximum probability
under these eigenvalues, and let $T_m$ be its projector,
\[
 D_m=\rank T_m=\frac{m!}{\prod_i n_i!},\qquad
 w_m=D_m\prod_i\ell_i^{n_i}.
 \tag{29}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact type domination without a channel-independence premise]
\label{lem:type}
The type above obeys
\[
 w_m\ge(m+1)^{-9},\qquad
 \frac{T_m}{D_m}\le\frac{\varrho^{\otimes m}}{w_m},\qquad
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log_2D_m}{m}=S(\varrho).
 \tag{30}
\]
For any normal bath initializer $\tau$, every positive final-bath test has
expectation on $T_m/D_m\otimes\tau$ at most $1/w_m$ times its expectation
on $\varrho^{\otimes m}\otimes\tau$, under the actual $W_{2m}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
There are at most $(m+1)^9$ types and their probabilities sum to one.
On one type the product density is exactly $w_m T_m/D_m$, proving the
first two statements. Put $p_i=n_i/m$. The elementary multinomial bound
$D_m\le2^{mH(p)}$ gives $w_m\le2^{-mD_2(p\Vert\ell)}$; thus
$D_2(p\Vert\ell)\le9\log_2(m+1)/m\to0$. For example the multinomial bound
follows since $D_m\prod_i p_i^{n_i}\le1$. Because all $\ell_i>0$ and the
simplex is compact, nonnegativity and the unique zero of relative entropy
imply $p\to\ell$. From (29),
\[
 \frac{\log_2D_m}{m}
 =-\sum_i p_i\log_2\ell_i+\frac{\log_2w_m}{m}\longrightarrow S(\varrho).
\]
Tensor the operator domination with $\tau$, apply the positive map induced
by the complete $W_{2m}$, and pair with the positive test. This argument
does not replace the same-bath physical channel by a product channel.
\end{proof}

The maximum-probability type is merely convenient. The supplementary entropy
contribution supplies an explicit rounded type with
$\log_2D_m\ge mS(\varrho)-36-9\log_2(m+1)$ for $m\ge32$ and
$w_m\ge(m+1)^{-9}e^{-144/m}$. Either version yields the same rate.

\section{Deterministic arbitrary-reference codes}

The inherited joint compression lemma is restated and proved in
Appendix~\ref{app:compression}; it is used on the actual isometry $W_{2m}$,
not on a memoryless replacement. It needs only an averaged final overflow
bound on a finite input subspace and on the uniform state of a finite initial
bath coordinate space. Its auxiliary normalization is never a physical
encoder. The conclusion is uniform over coherent initializers in that space.

For $m\ge1$, choose the fixed-channel scales
\[
\begin{split}
 a_m&=\lfloor\log_2(m+1)\rfloor,\qquad r_m=r_{a_m},\\
 h_m&=a_m+\left\lceil\frac{25\ln(m+1)+\ln(1+C_0)}{t}\right\rceil,
 \qquad d_m=r_{h_m},\qquad \epsilon_m=(m+1)^{-16}.
\end{split}\tag{31}
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:moment} on the auxiliary $P_{\le a_m}/r_m$ and the exact type
domination give mean final overflow on the input type space at most
\[
 w_m^{-1}(1+C_0)e^{-t(h_m-a_m)}\le(m+1)^9(m+1)^{-25}=\epsilon_m.
 \tag{32}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Strict capacity gain from two-call temporal coherence]
\label{thm:gain}
For each fixed $0<\beta<1$, there is one sequence of unassisted isometric
encoders and deterministic decoders, independent of the initializer, such
that for every fixed normal bath density $\tau$,
\[
 \liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{\log_2K_m}{2m}\ge\frac{S(\varrho)}2
 >\frac32+\frac{\beta^4}{1024},
 \tag{33}
\]
and the logical full-diamond error tends to zero. More explicitly the code
dimension can satisfy
\[
 K_m\ge\left\lceil\frac{\lfloor D_m/2\rfloor}{(r_md_m)^4}\right\rceil,
 \tag{34}
\]
and, for all sufficiently large $m$,
\[
 \norm{\Lambda_{m,\tau}-\id}_\diamond
 \le\min\left\{2,4e_m+2\sqrt{1-\Tr(P_{\le a_m}\tau)}\right\},
 \quad
 e_m=r_m\sqrt{2\epsilon_m}+(1-2\epsilon_m)^{-1/2}-1\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{35}
\]
In particular this holds for the one unchanged actual $\tau_\pi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the compression lemma to the input type space, initial bath subspace
$P_{\le a_m}E$, final projection $P_{\le h_m}$ and (32). It gives (34) and
full-diamond error at most $4e_m$ for every initializer in the initial subspace.
Here $r_m<2^{a_m+2}\le4(m+1)$, so
$r_m\sqrt{2\epsilon_m}\le4\sqrt2(m+1)^{-7}\to0$; the normalization term also
vanishes. Since $t,C_0$ are fixed independently of $m$, $h_m=O(\log m)$ and
$\log_2(r_md_m)=O(\log m)$. Equation (30) then proves (33).

For arbitrary fixed normal $\tau$ write $q_m=\Tr(P_{\le a_m}\tau)\to1$ and,
when $q_m>0$, $\widehat\tau_m=P_{\le a_m}\tau P_{\le a_m}/q_m$.
Projecting and normalizing a purification on its bath factor gives
$\frac12\norm{\tau-\widehat\tau_m}_1\le\sqrt{1-q_m}$.
The actual normalized physical encoded/decoded process is CPTP in its bath
input. Its logical channels for the two initializers therefore differ in full
diamond norm by at most $\norm{\tau-\widehat\tau_m}_1$; tensor an arbitrary
logical reference before applying trace-norm contraction. Add the finite-
support error to obtain (35). This comparison is made \emph{outside} type
domination, so no uncontrolled initial tail is multiplied by $1/w_m$.

The code operates at even length $2m$. An unused, data-independent last call
may be appended for odd lengths and its external output discarded. A later
local bath interaction cannot alter the marginal of the already emitted
outputs, so the same decoder works and the one additional call is counted.
Rounding the logical dimension to a power of two loses less than one qubit.
\end{proof}

The quantifier is
$\forall\beta\ \exists(C_m,D_m)\ \forall\tau\text{ fixed normal}$,
with pointwise convergence in $\tau$. It is not a uniform error limit over all
normal initializers at each $m$. A normal density may concentrate above any
particular finite $a_m$. The cutoffs, type average and normalizer are proof
comparisons; none changes the physical initializer or demands a bath operation.

At $\beta=0$ the inherited exact capacity remains $3/2$. At $\beta=1$ the
device is $iI$ with capacity $\log_2 3$; the drift proof is unnecessary there.
Combining (33) with the inherited coherent-history upper bound at the actual
$\beta=1/512$ gives
\[
 \boxed{\frac32+2^{-46}<Q(V,\tau_\pi)
 \le Q_{\leftarrow}(V,\tau_\pi)
 \le1.58164276090312\ldots<\log_2 3.}
 \tag{36}
\]
The strict first inequality uses $Q\ge S(\varrho)/2$ and the strict entropy
certificate. No equality of feedback and unassisted capacities follows.

\section{Failures and assumptions that explain the result}

\paragraph{The iid obstruction does not cover correlated pairs.}
Let $\rho_c=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/4,1/4)$ and
$S(\varrho)=3+\varepsilon$. Exact relative entropy gives
\[
 D_2(\varrho\Vert\rho_c^{\otimes2})=2\delta-\varepsilon.
 \tag{37}
\]
The coherence entropy cost is at least $2c^2/(y\ln2)$, so high entropy
constrains $c^2=O(\delta)$, not $c=O(\delta)$.
Taking product diagonal weights at $q=1/2-\delta$ and
$c=-\sqrt\delta/8$ gives
$S(\varrho)-3=(2-1/(4\ln2))\delta+O(\delta^2)>0$.
Thus a term linear in $\beta^2c$ cannot be bounded by an arbitrarily assumed
$O(\beta^2\delta)$ correction. The new negative bulk term is a surviving
coherent cancellation, not an error probability assigned to an individual
history. The former single-site insertion corrector remains valid on its
stated input class.

\paragraph{The opposite coherence has identical input entropy but leaks extensively.}
Keep every parameter in (16) except change $c$ to $+b/128$. The two eigenvalue
pairs are interchanged, so the input entropy is exactly the same. Its exact
bulk drift is now $3\kappa>0$, and both boundary corrections in (15) are
positive. From the actual finite-mean initializer,
$\E L_m-\E L_0\ge3\kappa m$.
Conservation of depth plus total port color-number and
$S(\omega)\le h_2(\omega_{00})+1-\omega_{00}\le2-\omega_{00}$ give
\[
 S(B^{2m})\le m(3+2\delta)-[\E L_m-\E L_0].
\]
This uses only subadditivity, with all output correlations retained.
Writing $S(\varrho)=3+\varepsilon$, unitary entropy conservation then gives
\[
 S(E_{2m})\ge S(\tau_\pi)+m(\varepsilon-2\delta+3\kappa),\qquad
 I_c\le m(3-\varepsilon+4\delta-6\kappa).
 \tag{38}
\]
For $0<\beta\le1/8$, $a\ge63/64$, hence $3\kappa>2\delta$ and
$6\kappa>4\delta$. Its bath entropy is extensive and its per-call
coherent-information upper limit is strictly below $3/2$. This same-device,
same-entropy counterexample shows that the signed temporal interference in
the exact map is essential; input entropy alone cannot supply the new theorem.

\paragraph{Dropping the root gives a false global contraction.}
At $\beta=0$ and the critical product point, (13) gives root probabilities
$(1/2,1/4,1/4)$ to depths $(0,1,2)$, with drift $3/4$ although bulk drift is
zero. Even under (18), a global inequality
$\Phi^*e^{tL}\le\lambda e^{tL}$ with $\lambda<1$ and no boundary term is
impossible: iteration on a finite-moment state would contradict $e^{tL}\ge I$
and trace preservation. The additive term in (25) is essential.

\paragraph{Input entropy, a dark vector or a finite cutoff is insufficient.}
The entropy calculation alone would leave the central long-time obligation
open. The genuine added evidence is (7)--(15), followed by the infinite-bath
moment bound (23). A finite reflecting cutoff always has finite-dimensional
stationary states and supplies neither. The pure-color example in Cycle3
shows that depth growth and information growth also cannot be interchanged.
No such surrogate is used in (27) or (32).

\paragraph{An arbitrary type or average-error bound cannot replace the interface.}
A type containing only a least-probable eigenvector has probability
$\ell_{\min}^m$, exponentially small. Its domination factor would destroy the
logarithmic cutoff argument. Equations (29)--(30) are load-bearing.
Likewise a projector onto one bad logical basis vector has uniform-input
failure $1/D$ and worst-case failure one. The inherited spectral expurgation
and joint positive-operator bound, including its $r^2$ factor, convert the
mean bound into a reference-uniform operation; they are not optional.

\paragraph{No new physical resource is hidden in the proof.}
The final bath may differ from its initializer and correlate with retained
external syndromes. Only the logical reference is decoupled to the stated
error. Every Kraus branch is included in the exact auxiliary code and its
approximation. There is no success-only credit, return claim, inaccessible
purifier control or fresh-bath reinterpretation.

\section{Scope, primary comparison and the chosen next question}

The generic low-environment coding mechanism is established: the error-space
and convex-partition construction appears in Knill--Laflamme--Viola
\cite{KLV}, and the perfect-memory criterion and coding theorem in
Giovannetti--Burgarth--Mancini \cite{GBM}, Definition 1 and Theorem 2.
Their exact subexponential environment premise does not hold automatically
for the complete infinite-tree channel. The inherited compression proof
supplies its approximate, input-restricted version with coherent initializer
control. Our new receiving use is the correlated-pair depth law and stable
spectral-type average, which meet that interface above three input bits per
pair. Source delivery alone would not have established this consequence.

Kretschmann--Werner's coding theorem for forgetful memory channels
\cite{KW} requires forgetfulness. Negative drift of one selected average is
not such a theorem about the whole quantum memory channel. No claim of
forgetfulness or independent repeated physical blocks is needed here.
The exponential-moment argument is an elementary established Lyapunov method;
the new model-specific verification is the exact coherent kernel, root rows,
entropy/drift tradeoff and its actual coding consequence. This bounded primary
comparison is not an exhaustive priority search or an external certification.

The principal scientific change is now definite: there is no $3/2$ capacity
plateau for this perturbation on any nonzero small-angle interval. The
single-site iid information optimum is strictly below an attainable rate of
the same physical channel. We have not determined the exact capacity, a
feedback separation, a terminal-return capacity or an efficient code compiler.
The explicit constants are intentionally conservative; optimizing their
additive terms is not the selected next investigation.

At $\beta=1/512$ the numerical input rate is
$1.50000000000001817074461819\ldots$ qubits per call. The tail parameter
$t=\kappa/32$ is about $3.55\times10^{-15}$, and $C_0$ is about
$4.95\times10^{27}$. Thus the logarithmic dimension-loss coefficient in
the existence proof is very large. The symbolic replay is cheap, but this
does not measure code compilation, finite communication performance or the
scientific discovery cost. No qualified material device, laboratory advantage
or useful finite blocklength has been established.

\paragraph{Chosen next scientific frontier.}
Determine the small-angle order of the correlated capacity gain. This
construction supplies a quartic lower increment in $\beta$. Is that order
forced for every fixed finite temporal block, or can longer coherent blocks
produce a quadratic increment or a stronger nonanalytic scale? The selected
next operation is a charge-preserving four-call perturbation around the
critical product input: derive its exact bulk depth functional and entropy
Hessian, including the vacuum terms, and decide whether it permits a lower
entropy cost per unit inward drift than the pair construction. A surviving
finite-block direction must still pass the same infinite-bath overflow and
arbitrary-reference coding obligations. A local entropy or drift calculation
alone will not count as an improved capacity theorem.

The original strict-gain question is answered within this model; the broader
mission continues at this new boundary. Protected separately owned programmes
and closed physical-method operations remain unchanged. Original R1 results
and their provenance stay in the immutable nested source chain.
