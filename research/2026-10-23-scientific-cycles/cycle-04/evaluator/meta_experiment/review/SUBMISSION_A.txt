claim with a full proof below, not owner acceptance or an external review.
The numerical calculations are diagnostics and do not carry the proof.

Main consequence at K=1, alpha=1/2, finite accuracy-policy d=1:

  1.08848276573897058... <= liminf epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
      <= limsup epsilon log T_policy(epsilon) <= 1.54221141973774511....

The lower endpoint is inherited, unchanged, from the supplied owner source.
The previous supplied upper endpoint was 3.31290271377331231....
The existence or value of the exact coefficient at these parameters remains

The prescribed candidate theta=(1/2)min(eta,1/2) is proved contractive for

1. MODEL, NORMS, AND RECEIVED SOURCE

Use exactly the common TASK.txt model: finite strictly positive pi,q;
D(pi||q)<=K; alpha in (0,1), beta=1-alpha; fixed symmetric stochastic Q;
the stated two-factor local acceptance L_eta; independence MH G with proposal
q; and C=(I+alpha G+beta L_eta)/2, starting from q. Eta,Q,alpha remain fixed
during every trajectory. No row of a killed matrix is normalized.

The supplied CONTINUATION_SEED.md and VANISHING_SCREEN_THEOREM.md, Sections
1--7, supply the entropy envelope, spectral anchor, and first-hit smoothing
architecture. The lower coefficient used here is exactly that in the supplied
reviews/vanishing_lower_proof.md and LOWER_ACCEPTANCE.md. The source binding,
failure record, numerical record, and experiment contract were also read.
This proof replaces the source's first-hit transport estimate, not its model.

For h in (0,1/2), put

  W_x=pi_x/q_x,  A={x: W_x<h},  B=A^c,
  delta=exp(-(K+H2(h))/(1-h)),  b=q(A),
  rho_h=1-alpha*delta/2,
  H2(h)=-h log h-(1-h)log(1-h).

The source binary-entropy argument gives q(B)>=delta, hence b<=1-delta.
For completeness: d=pi(A)<=h; binary KL gives
K>=-H2(h)-(1-h)log(1-b), proving the assertion.

For 0<theta<=1/2 set p=1/(1-theta), D_theta=diag(q_x^theta).
All matrix actions below are on ROW vectors. Thus the counting l^1 induced
norm is the maximum row sum, and the counting l^infinity induced norm is
the maximum column sum. For a signed measure mu,

  ||mu D_theta^(-1)||_p = ||mu/q||_{L^p(q)}.

Only nonnegative vectors are needed. Because our matrices are nonnegative,
the same induced norm is obtained on all signed vectors by domination with
the absolute value. If A is empty all killed statements are trivial.

2. A SCALAR LOCAL-TRANSPORT PENALTY

Define, for 0<c<1,

  g(c)=c(1-c)^((1-c)/c).

It is strictly increasing from 0 to 1. Indeed

  d/dc log g(c) = -log(1-c)/c^2 > 0.

An elementary maximization gives

  max_{0<=s<=1} (s^(1-c)-s) = g(c),

with maximum at s=(1-c)^(1/c). This scalar optimization is exact.

Suppose 0<theta<=c*eta. Let L_A=L_eta[A,A] and
L_theta=D_theta L_A D_theta^(-1). Then

  max_x sum_y (L_theta)_{xy} <= 1+g(c),                (L1)
  max_y sum_x (L_theta)_{xy} <= 2.                     (Linf)

Proof of (L1). Keep the ACTUAL diagonal L_xx, including all rejected local
proposals. For r=q_y/q_x<1, local acceptance is at most r^eta, so its positive
contribution relative to the untransformed row is at most

  Q_xy r^eta(r^(-theta)-1)
     =Q_xy(s^(1-theta/eta)-s) <= Q_xy g(c),  s=r^eta.

For r>=1 that change is nonpositive. The original killed row has mass at
most one, and sum_y Q_xy<=1. Discarding negative changes gives (L1).
This retains the accepted-outflow contribution in the diagonal; replacing
the diagonal by one before this step would lose this estimate.

Proof of (Linf). For x!=y and r=q_y/q_x,

  L_xy r^(-theta) <= Q_xy min(1,r^eta)r^(-theta) <= Q_xy,

because 0<theta<=eta. The diagonal is at most one, and Q is symmetric
stochastic, so the sum of the other entries in each column is at most one.

For any nonnegative matrix M with row sums <=R and column sums <=S,
Holder/Jensen proves the elementary bound

  ||M||_{p->p} <= R^(1/p) S^(1-1/p).                 (J)

Explicitly, (sum_x v_x M_xy)^p <= (sum_x M_xy)^(p-1)
sum_x v_x^p M_xy; sum over y and use both bounds. Therefore

  ||L_theta||_{p->p} <= (1+g(c))^(1-theta) 2^theta.    (L)

There is no support-size or minimum-probability constant in (L).

3. THE KILLED INDEPENDENCE STEP HAS A SMALL WEIGHTED-NORM COST

Let G_A=G[A,A]. Every proposal from x in A to y in B is accepted, since
W_y>=h>W_x. Consequently EVERY row of G_A has mass EXACTLY b=q(A):

  sum_{y in A} G_xy = b.                              (G1)

For each y in A, the column sum of the q-density operator is at most 2b:

  (1/q_y) sum_{x in A} q_x G_xy
    = G_yy + sum_{x in A,x!=y} q_x min(1,W_y/W_x)
    <= b + (b-q_y) <= 2b.                            (Ginf)

Here G_yy<=b follows already from (G1). Notice that this is a weighted
column bound, not an incorrect counting-column bound for G.

Apply the same Jensen proof in measure q. If f=mu/q and
P f(y)=sum_x(q_x/q_y)G_xy f(x), the weighted column bound gives

  sum_y q_y |P f(y)|^p
     <= (2b)^(p-1) sum_x q_x |f(x)|^p sum_y G_xy
     <= (2b)^(p-1)b sum_x q_x |f(x)|^p.

Equivalently,

  ||D_theta G_A D_theta^(-1)||_{p->p} <= b*2^theta.    (G)

This uses the actual killing in the independence step. In particular, its
cost tends to b, not to a separate norm-growth constant, as theta tends to
zero. It also accounts for all internal independence transitions.

4. THE UNIVERSAL CAPPED-EXPONENT CONTRACTION THEOREM

Choose h in (0,1/2) and c in (0,1) with

  g(c) < alpha*delta/beta.                            (C0)

Define

  H = 1-alpha*delta+beta*g(c) in (0,1),
  theta_bar = min(1/2, -log H/(2 log 2)),
  theta(eta) = min(c*eta, theta_bar),
  p=1/(1-theta),
  r=(1+sqrt(H))/2 < 1.

Then, for EVERY admissible finite instance and EVERY eta in (0,1],

  ||D_theta C[A,A] D_theta^(-1)||_{p->p} <= r < 1.     (C)

This is a full induced-norm statement about the actual killed C, not a
renormalized kernel, a substituted local chain, or only a selected vector.

Proof. Theta<=c*eta<=eta and theta<=1/2. By the triangle inequality, (G)
and (L), and b<=1-delta,

  ||D_theta C[A,A] D_theta^(-1)||
   <= 1/2 +(alpha/2)b 2^theta
             +(beta/2)(1+g(c))^(1-theta)2^theta
   <= 1/2 +(1/2)[alpha(1-delta)+beta(1+g(c))]2^theta
    = 1/2 +(H/2)2^theta.

The second inequality uses (1+g)^(1-theta)<=1+g. The choice of theta_bar
ensures 2^theta<=H^(-1/2), and (C) follows. All constants depend only on
K,alpha,h,c and are uniform in eta, support, pi,q,Q.

The cap is a proof device and does not alter eta or C during a run.

5. SUMMABLE FIRST-HIT NORMS, INCLUDING ALL LATER RETURNS

Let tau=inf{j>=0: X_j in B}, and let

  mu_j(F)=P(X_j in F, tau>j),
  nu_j(F)=P(X_j in F, tau=j).

Thus mu_0=q restricted to A, mu_j=mu_0 C[A,A]^j, and, for j>=1,
nu_j=mu_{j-1} C[A,B]. These are unnormalized submeasures.
The original global escape bound remains

  m_j:=mu_j(Omega)<=rho_h^j.                          (M)

The contraction (C) gives

  ||mu_j D_theta^(-1)||_p <= r^j b^(1-theta) <= r^j.  (K)

For a nonnegative row vector y supported in A, put mu=y D_theta. Holder
gives mu(A)<=b^theta||y||_p<=||y||_p. On the crossing block A to B,
there is no identity/diagonal term. The global off-diagonal bound G_xy<=q_y
gives a transformed output norm <=mu(A)*q(B)^(1-theta)<=||y||_p.
The local off-diagonal bound L_xy(q_x/q_y)^theta<=Q_xy gives a transformed
output norm <=||y||_p: extend by zero and use the l^p contraction of the
doubly stochastic Q, then restrict its output. Therefore

  ||D_A C[A,B] D_B^(-1)||_{p->p} <= (alpha+beta)/2=1/2.

Combining this with (K), and using ||nu_0/q||_{L^p(q)}=q(B)^(1-theta)<=1,
we obtain the uniform summability bound

  sum_{j>=0} ||nu_j/q||_{L^p(q)}
       <= S:=1+1/[2(1-r)].                           (S)

This is the new transport input needed by the source's smoothing argument.
No return after the first hit is removed: the next section propagates every
nu_j by the full, original C^(n-j).

6. COMPLETE FINITE RISK CERTIFICATE

For n>=2 let N=floor(n/2). For R>exp(1), define

  M=exp(2K+1),
  Jbar=2/[alpha(2(n-N)+1)],
  A_R=min(1,K/(log R-1)).

With h,c satisfying (C0), theta=min(c*eta,theta_bar), r and S above, the
following holds uniformly over the original finite class and eta in (0,1]:

 R_eta(n) <= A_R + rho_h^N
       + S[(A_R/R)^theta + ((M/h+1)Jbar)^theta]
       + sqrt((M+R)Jbar).                            (F)

The right side can be capped above by 1. Every term is retained.

Here are the smoothing details, so that the relation to the original risk
is explicit. The accepted source entropy envelope gives

  a_R:=pi(W>R)<=A_R,  q(W>R)<=a_R/R,
  s_0:=pi(W<=M)>=1/2.

The actual C is pi-reversible and lazy. For g=C^t 1_F and
z=pi(g 1_{W<=M})/s_0, its IMH-dominated Dirichlet form gives the source's
anchor estimate

  integral (g-z)^2/max(W,M) d pi <= 2/[alpha(2t+1)].  (A)

For clarity, this estimate is unchanged received mathematics: restricting
one leg of the independence-MH Dirichlet form to W<=M and applying Jensen
gives its anchor quadratic form; laziness places the spectrum in [0,1],
and (1-lambda)lambda^(2t)<=1/(2t+1), together with Var_pi(1_F)<=1/4,
gives (A). The local detailed-balance identity and all constants are those
in VANISHING_SCREEN_THEOREM.md, Section 5.

For each j<=N separately take t=n-j, g_j=C^(n-j)1_F, and its own z_j.
Then (A)<=Jbar. On h<=W<=R it implies

  integral (g_j-z_j)^2 d q <= (M/h+1)Jbar.

Let D_j=||nu_j/q||_{L^p(q)}. Since theta<=1/2 and |g_j-z_j|<=1,
Holder with dual exponent 1/theta gives

  integral_{h<=W<=R}|g_j-z_j|d nu_j
       <=D_j[(M/h+1)Jbar]^theta.

The nu_j tail on W>R costs at most D_j(a_R/R)^theta. Nu_j has no mass
on W<h at its first hit. In the stationary comparison we keep ALL W<=R;
weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and (A) give

  integral_{W<=R}|g_j-z_j|d pi <=sqrt((M+R)Jbar),

and the remaining pi tail costs a_R. The measure nu_j and its comparison
nu_j(Omega)*pi have equal mass, so each z_j cancels. Therefore

 |nu_j C^(n-j)(F)-nu_j(Omega)pi(F)|
 <=D_j[(a_R/R)^theta+((M/h+1)Jbar)^theta]
     +nu_j(Omega)[a_R+sqrt((M+R)Jbar)].

Finally use the exact path decomposition

  q C^n = sum_{j=0}^N nu_j C^(n-j) + sigma_n,
  sigma_n(Omega)=P(tau>N)<=rho_h^N.

The residual is propagated by the full actual chain as well; its comparison
with the same mass times pi has TV at most its mass. Sum, use (S) and
sum_j nu_j(Omega)<=1, replace a_R by A_R in monotone occurrences, and
take the supremum over F and over admissible instances. This proves (F).

7. SHARP-COEFFICIENT SCALE AND ACCURACY-POLICY CONSEQUENCES

Write a0=alpha*exp(-K)/beta, and define

  psi(K,alpha)=1                       if a0>=1,
  psi(K,alpha)=the unique c in (0,1)
        with g(c)=a0                  if 0<a0<1.

For every fixed c<psi, continuity as h decreases to zero permits a fixed
h>0 satisfying (C0). Thus all constants in (F) can be fixed independently
of eta and horizon, while theta=min(c*eta,theta_bar).

Take R=n/(log n)^4 for sufficiently large n. The same elementary estimates
as in the supplied source give

  A_R=K/[log n-4loglog n-1],
  sqrt((M+R)Jbar)=O((log n)^(-2)),
  log((M/h+1)Jbar)=-log n+O(1),
  log(A_R/R)=-log n+3loglog n+O(1).

Whenever eta remains above the fixed cap threshold theta_bar/c, transport
decays polynomially in n. Below that threshold theta=c*eta. Consequently

  liminf eta_n log n/loglog n > 1/psi(K,alpha)
        implies log n R_{eta_n}(n) -> K.              (HORIZON)

The matching K lower here is the source's unchanged eta-independent Q=I
family. The convergence criterion eta_n log n -> infinity also follows
again from (F); its necessity is unchanged received lower mathematics.

For the accuracy policy in the supplied source let

  d_epsilon=eta(epsilon)/[epsilon log(1/epsilon)] -> d in (0,infinity).

For a constant A>max(K,1/(d*psi)), choose fixed c<psi with A*d*c>1,
then fixed h and the constants above, and n=ceil(exp(A/epsilon)). Because
eta(epsilon)->0, eventually theta=c*eta(epsilon). In (F) the entropy term
is (K/A+o(1))*epsilon, while transport is

  exp[-c eta(epsilon) log n+o(eta(epsilon)log n)]
       =epsilon^(c*A*d+o(1))=o(epsilon).

All other terms are o(epsilon), and K/A<1 gives a strict margin. Thus the
uniform risk at this n is <=epsilon eventually. Each individual policy
kernel is fixed throughout its run; ordinary fixed-kernel TV monotonicity
then controls T_policy. Letting A decrease proves

  limsup epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
       <=max(K,1/[d*psi(K,alpha)]).                  (UPPER)

Combining with the SOURCE lower, whose chi is defined by g(chi)=2*a0 when
2*a0<1 and chi=1 otherwise, gives

 max(K,1/[d*chi]) <= liminf epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
      <=limsup epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
      <=max(K,1/[d*psi]).                            (FRONTIER)

This does not invert a possibly nonmonotone diagonal sequence R_eta_n(n).
It holds eta fixed separately for every requested accuracy.

There is also a matching-coefficient subregion: if alpha*exp(-K)>=beta,
then psi=chi=1, so for every finite d>0 the actual coefficient exists and is

  lim epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)=max(K,1/d).

This follows directly by matching the received lower with (UPPER). At the
requested K=1,alpha=1/2 that subregion does not apply.

9. SCOPE, FAILURES, AND UNRESOLVED OBLIGATIONS

The numerical endpoint is not a counterexample, a finite-search win, an
observed mixing time, a theorem about a replacement kernel, or the exact
minimax coefficient. It is the consequence of (F), a universal inequality
for the actual supplied chain, using the source's accepted anchor and lower.

An earlier line of attack retained a killed-local B and the exact affine
IMH reset. It produced the same asymptotic admissibility condition but more
complicated constants. The direct weighted-Jensen estimate (G) supersedes
that route in this deliverable. The initial coarse endpoint interpolation
alone suggested a smaller improvement, around a 2.5 upper coefficient; it
was not used after the stronger local row estimate was obtained.

The crude counting-column bound for the local step and the separate triangle
inequality may still lose substantial structure. No obstruction is proved
against improving them. The remaining gap between psi and the source's chi
is not identified as structural, and no sharpness claim follows.

The full prescribed c=1/2, eta in (0,1] norm probe remains unresolved outside
the explicit interval just proved. This gap does not invalidate (F) or its
finite-d consequence because the cap is allowed to be a fixed proof choice
and eta(epsilon)->0 in that regime.

There is no historical-novelty claim, practical-speedup claim, owner acceptance,
controlled causal-efficacy claim, or general amplification claim. Exact-real
mixing and actual target-query/precision implementation costs remain distinct.
No changes were made to the scientific mission, sampler, oracle contract,
initialization, Q symmetry, KL budget, or counted target access.
