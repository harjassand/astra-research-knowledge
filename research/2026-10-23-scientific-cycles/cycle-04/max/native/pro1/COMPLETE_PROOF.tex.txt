\begin{abstract}
The closed qutrit tree device retains a universal reliable capacity floor of
$3/2$ qubits per use. We resolve the proposed next independent-input
stabilization operation negatively, without claiming a capacity converse.
For every fixed qutrit input density of entropy greater than $3/2$ and
$0\le\sin s\le1/4$, the exact infinite bath escapes every sublinear-depth
coordinate cutoff exponentially, from every normal initializer. There is
no invariant normal bath state. A norm-convergent insertion resolvent
provides a bounded correction to depth; a cancellation in its commutator
prevents the near-critical resolvent norm from destroying the drift bound.
The root exchange is retained and controlled by a positive-square estimate.

For the actual initializer $\tau_\pi$, a separate entropy-flow argument
proves extensive bath entropy and excludes asymptotically lossless
subexponential-rank bath compression whenever $\sin s\le1/8$ and the fixed
independent input has entropy above $3/2$. This includes the original
$\sin s=1/512$ coherent seed. Exact charge conservation and subadditivity
retain all output correlations. The result closes that sufficient coding
mechanism. A further consequence is an exact asymptotic $3/2$ ceiling for
optimized coherent information over iid inputs, allowing the marginal
to depend on the horizon. The strict-gain question remains open: temporally correlated code inputs
and correction tolerating an extensive environment remain unexcluded.
We export the bounded-drift/finite-band positive-tilt operation with a
counterexample to dropping its propagation control, and a same-device
example showing why depth escape alone is not an information bound.
\end{abstract}

\section{Owned state and unchanged contract}

The complete Cycle2 owner archive is preserved unchanged. It proves, for
each fixed $s\in[0,\pi/2]$, existence of one unassisted encoder/decoder
sequence attaining rate at least $3/2$ for every fixed normal bath
initializer, with vanishing full-diamond error. Convergence is pointwise
in the initializer. No terminal bath return or efficient code compiler is
claimed \cite{Owner}. At $0<\sin s\le1/512$, its inherited feedback upper bound is
$1.58164276090312\ldots$. Neither that proof nor the earlier feedback trial
is repeated here.

The current fixed device is $V_s=\alpha U+i\beta I$, with
$\alpha=\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$, $\beta=\sin s$. The qutrit port, labelled
rooted 3-regular tree, faithful reference
$\eta=\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1/8,1/8)$, and stationary bath $\tau_\pi$
are unchanged. All forward calls, outcome branches and retained registers
count. No bath access, fresh bath, reset, preshared entanglement, backward
quantum wire or timing channel is used. Terminal bath return is not a
condition of the live perturbed-capacity question.

This paper studies the bath average produced by \emph{one fixed independent
and identically distributed port density} $\rho$:
\[
 \Phi_\rho(\tau)=\Tr_{\mathrm{port}}
   V_s(\rho\otimes\tau)V_s^\dagger,\qquad \tau_n=\Phi_\rho^n(\tau_0).
\]
This average can be used in a coding proof, but it does not describe every
correlated code input. Purifying registers of a physical preparation are
not free or ignored in any claimed communication protocol. No new
communication rate is asserted from this bath-marginal analysis.

All entropies and rates are in bits. State full trace distance is
$\|\sigma-\tau\|_1$; half-trace distance is half that quantity. The preserved
capacity result uses full diamond norm, twice its half-diamond convention.
The new escape probabilities are ordinary probabilities of final bath
projections, not logical errors or conditional-success credit.

\section{Exact two-seed representation and coherent bath channel}

Deleting the root--port-0 edge identifies the entire bath with
$E=\mathbb C^2_{\mathrm{seed}}\otimes\mathcal F(\mathbb C^2)$.
Let $S_a\ket{j,w}=\ket{j,aw}$, let $R$ exchange the two vacuum seeds and
vanish elsewhere, and let $P_0=R^2$. Then
\[
 S_a^\dagger S_b=\delta_{ab}I,\quad
 \sum_aS_aS_a^\dagger=I-P_0,\quad
 RS_a=S_a^\dagger R=0,
 \qquad
 U=\begin{pmatrix}R&S_1&S_2\\S_1^\dagger&0&0\\S_2^\dagger&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The depth $L\ket{j,w}=|w|\ket{j,w}$ has finite level multiplicity
$2^{\ell+1}$, and
\[
 P_{\le a}=1_{[0,a]}(L),\quad
 \rank P_{\le a}=2(2^{a+1}-1),\quad
 \tau_\pi=\frac{I_2}{2}\otimes
 \left(\frac23\sum_w6^{-|w|}\ket w\bra w\right).
 \tag{2}
\]
Its depth mean is $1/2$ and its entropy is
$S(\tau_\pi)=\log_2 3+\tfrac12\log_2 6<\infty$.

Write $q=\rho_{00}$. A change of basis within the two-dimensional color
sector diagonalizes its color block; phases of those basis vectors can make
the zero--color entries purely imaginary with nonnegative magnitudes. Thus,
in these coordinates,
\[
 \rho=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 q&ig_1&ig_2\\
 -ig_1&a_1&0\\
 -ig_2&0&a_2
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad g_j\ge0,\qquad a_1+a_2=1-q.
 \tag{3}
\]
The corresponding creation operators are the same unitary linear
combinations of $S_1,S_2$ as the port basis vectors. They still obey (1);
the depth, root exchange, $\tau_\pi$, and the sum
$\mathcal C(X)=\sum_aS_aXS_a^\dagger$ are unchanged. This is a representation
change, not an allowed physical control being added to the device.

For a real vector $v$, put $S_v=\sum_jv_jS_j$; then $\|S_v\|=\|v\|_2$.
Set $D_g=S_g^\dagger-S_g$. Direct expansion gives
\[
 \Phi_\rho(\tau)=\beta^2\tau+\alpha^2\Psi_\rho(\tau)
                  -i\alpha\beta[A_\rho,\tau],
 \quad
 A_\rho=qR+i(S_g^\dagger-S_g),
 \tag{4}
\]
where
\[
 \Psi_\rho(\tau)
 =q\left(R\tau R+\sum_jS_j^\dagger\tau S_j\right)
  +\sum_j a_jS_j\tau S_j^\dagger
  +iR\tau S_g^\dagger-iS_g\tau R.
 \tag{5}
\]
The dual, for bounded $X$, is
\[
\begin{split}
 \Phi_\rho^*(X)={}&\beta^2X+
 \alpha^2\left[qRXR+q\mathcal C(X)+
    \sum_j a_jS_j^\dagger XS_j
    +iS_g^\dagger XR-iRXS_g\right]\\
 &+i\alpha\beta q[R,X]-\alpha\beta[D_g,X].
\end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]
These formulas retain coherent cross terms. In particular the
coherent depth evolution is not the scalar reflected birth-and-death chain
used at the critical diagonal input.

On the finite-depth core, the depth increment extends to the bounded operator
\[
 \Phi_\rho^*(L)-L
 =\alpha^2(1-2q)I+\alpha^2qP_0
       -2\alpha\beta\Reop S_g.
 \tag{7}
\]
For an infinite-mean normal state it is a bounded-increment expectation,
equivalently a limit of uniformly bounded capped-depth increments.
It is not a subtraction of two infinite means.

\section{A bounded insertion corrector}\label{sec:corrector}

Suppose $q<1/2$, $a_1,a_2>0$, and $\alpha>0$. Put
\[
 r=\frac q{1-q}<1,\qquad f=(g_1/a_2,g_2/a_1),\qquad
 F_{k,f}=\mathcal C^k(S_f),\qquad
 B_f=\sum_{k=0}^\infty r^kF_{k,f}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The series converges in operator norm and
$\|B_f\|\le\|f\|/(1-r)$. The crossed denominators in $f$ are essential.

\begin{lemma}[Exact resolvent and commutator]
\label{lem:resolvent}
For the true infinite bath,
\[
\begin{split}
 (I-\Phi_\rho^*)B_f
   ={}&\alpha^2S_g+i\alpha\beta qS_fR
           -i\alpha^2(g\cdot f)R
           +\alpha\beta[D_g,B_f],\\
 [D_g,B_f]
   ={}&(g\cdot f)I+S_fS_g
           -(1-r)(B_fS_g^\dagger+S_gB_f),
\end{split}
 \tag{9}
\]
and $\|[D_g,B_f]\|\le4\|g\|\|f\|$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $k\ge1$ the root and root--color terms vanish on $F_{k,f}$, and (6) gives
\[
 \Phi_\rho^*F_{k,f}
 =\beta^2F_{k,f}+\alpha^2
       [qF_{k+1,f}+(1-q)F_{k-1,f}]
       -\alpha\beta[D_g,F_{k,f}].
\]
At $k=0$, the weighted color term is $S_{\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2)f}$;
the remaining root terms are
$i\alpha^2(g\cdot f)R-i\alpha\beta qS_fR$.
Summing the norm-convergent series cancels every coefficient at $k\ge1$,
using $(1-q)r=q$. Its surviving undriven coefficient is
$\alpha^2S_{((1-q)I-\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2))f}=\alpha^2S_g$.
This proves the first identity, including the root terms.

The recursion $B_f=S_f+r\mathcal C(B_f)$ and the Cuntz--Toeplitz
relations imply
\[
 S_g^\dagger B_f=(g\cdot f)I+rB_fS_g^\dagger,
 \qquad B_fS_g=S_fS_g+rS_gB_f.
\]
Their difference proves the second identity. Its norm is at most
$|g\cdot f|+\|g\|\|f\|+
2(1-r)\|B_f\|\|g\|\le4\|g\|\|f\|$.
The factor $1-r$ is the critical cancellation. Replacing this estimate by
$2\|D_g\|\|B_f\|$ would lose the useful scale near $q=1/2$.
\end{proof}

Define the bounded correction and corrected depth
\[
 C=-\frac{2\beta}{\alpha}\Reop B_f,\qquad
 H=L+C,\qquad
 M=\frac{2\beta\|f\|}{\alpha(1-r)}\ge\|C\|.
 \tag{10}
\]

\begin{proposition}[Root-retaining positive drift criterion]
\label{prop:drift}
The bounded increment of $H$ obeys
\[
 \Phi_\rho^*(H)-H\ge\nu I,\qquad
 \nu=\alpha^2(1-2q)-8\beta^2\|g\|\|f\|
                 -\frac{\beta^4q}{\alpha^2}\|f\|^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking the real part of (9) and substituting (7) cancels the linear
coherent drift:
\[
 \Phi_\rho^*(H)-H
 =\alpha^2(1-2q)I+\alpha^2qP_0
 +2\beta^2q\Reop(iS_fR)
 +2\beta^2\Reop[D_g,B_f].
 \tag{12}
\]
The purely imaginary multiple of the selfadjoint $R$ vanishes here,
not earlier. The final term is bounded below by
$-8\beta^2\|g\|\|f\|I$.

For the root term set $J=S_fR$. It maps the vacuum subspace into depth
one, and $J^\dagger J=\|f\|^2P_0$. If $u=P_0x$ and
$v=(I-P_0)x$, its quadratic form is bounded below by
\[
 \alpha^2q\|u\|^2-2\beta^2q\|f\|\|u\|\|v\|
 \ge-\frac{\beta^4q}{\alpha^2}\|f\|^2\|v\|^2.
\]
This is completion of a positive square. In our applications $q>0$.
The $q=0$ limit is also immediate if the displayed parameters are defined.
The bound proves (11). In particular the vacuum exchange has not been
discarded as an asymptotically rare event.
\end{proof}

\section{A spectral window covering every high-entropy qutrit input}

\begin{lemma}[Entropy implies the spectral window]
\label{lem:window}
If $S(\rho)>3/2$ bits for a qutrit density, then
\[
 \frac16 I<\rho<\frac12 I.
 \tag{13}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If its largest eigenvalue is $b\ge1/2$, the entropy is at most
$h_2(b)+(1-b)$, a decreasing function for $b\ge1/2$ whose value
at $1/2$ is $3/2$. If its smallest eigenvalue is $a\le1/6$,
maximizing the other two gives entropy at most $h_2(a)+(1-a)$.
This function is increasing for $a\le1/3$, and
$h_2(1/6)+5/6<3/2$. The last strict inequality is equivalent to
$6(12/5)^5<2^9$, i.e. $1\,492\,992<1\,600\,000$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Positive drift for the entire spectral-window class]
\label{thm:windowdrift}
Suppose (13) holds and $0\le\beta\le1/4$. Write
$\delta=1/2-q>0$. The corrector (10) is bounded and satisfies
\[
 \Phi_\rho^*(H)-H\ge\nu I,\qquad
 \nu\ge\delta\left(2\alpha^2-16\beta^2
                    -\frac{6\beta^4}{\alpha^2}\right)
 \ge\frac45\delta>0.
 \tag{14}
\]
If $0\le\beta\le1/8$, the same bound in particular gives
$\nu\ge3\delta/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The color eigenvalues obey $a_j>1/6$, so $\|f\|\le6\|g\|$.
Positivity of $I/2-\rho$ and its Schur complement give
\[
 \delta>g^\dagger D^{-1}g\ge3\|g\|^2,\qquad
 D=I/2-\operatorname{diag}(a_1,a_2),\quad 0<D<I/3.
 \tag{15}
\]
This includes $g=0$, since $\delta>0$.
Inserting $\|g\|^2\le\delta/3$, $\|f\|\le6\|g\|$, and $q<1/2$
into (11) proves the first bound in (14). As a function of
$x=\beta^2$, its bracket is
\[
 b(x)=2-18x-\frac{6x^2}{1-x},
\]
which decreases on $0\le x<1$. At $x=1/16$ it equals $17/20>4/5$;
at $x=1/64$ it is greater than $3/2$. The stated conservative bounds
follow, with non-strict intermediate inequalities at $\beta=0$.
\end{proof}

\section{A finite-band positive-tilt operation}\label{sec:tilt}

The following lemma isolates the additional obligation between positive mean
drift and a true escape estimate. It concerns a normal completely positive bath
channel. No classical trajectory representation or intermediate measurement is
assumed.

\begin{lemma}[Bounded corrected drift gives exponential coordinate escape]
\label{lem:tilt}
Let $\mathcal E=\bigoplus_{\ell\ge0}\mathcal E_\ell$, with each level finite
dimensional, and let $L=\sum_{\ell\ge0}\ell P_\ell$. Let $\Phi$ be a normal
trace-preserving channel with an isometric dilation
$W:\mathcal E\to\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal K$ of depth bandwidth one:
\[
 (P_j\otimes I)WP_\ell=0\quad\hbox{if }|j-\ell|>1.
\]
Suppose $C=C^\dagger$ is bounded, $\|C\|\le M$, and on the finite-depth core
\[
 K=\Phi^*(L)-L+\Phi^*(C)-C
\]
extends to a bounded operator satisfying $K\ge\nu I$, with $\nu>0$.
For any $t$ obeying
\[
 0<t\le1,\qquad tM\le\tfrac12,\qquad
 t(5+8M)\le\tfrac{\nu}{2},\qquad \nu t<3,
 \tag{T1}
\]
define
\[
 A_t=I-tC,\qquad X_t=e^{-tL/2}A_te^{-tL/2},
 \qquad \lambda=1-\nu t/3.
\]
Then $X_t$ is a positive, injective compact operator,
\[
 (1-tM)e^{-tL}\le X_t\le(1+tM)e^{-tL},
 \qquad \Phi^*(X_t)\le\lambda X_t,\quad 0<\lambda<1.
 \tag{T2}
\]
For every normal density $\tau$, every $n\ge0$, and every $h\ge0$,
\[
 \Tr[P_{\le h}\Phi^n(\tau)]
 \le \min\!\left\{1,\,
 \frac{1+tM}{1-tM}e^{th}\lambda^n\right\}
 \le \min\{1,\,3e^{th-\nu tn/3}\}.
 \tag{T3}
\]
In particular, for every $v<\nu/3$ the mass at depths at most $vn$
decays exponentially. There is no invariant normal state, and every normal
orbit converges to zero against compact bath observables. The constants may
depend on the fixed channel and on $C$, but the displayed estimate is uniform
over initial normal densities.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $W=W_{-1}+W_0+W_1$ in its grade components. Each has norm at most one:
it is the corresponding Fourier coefficient of
$(e^{iuL}\otimes I)We^{-iuL}$, equivalently the strong integral of contractions
against a scalar phase. The finite-band hypothesis makes the sum exact.
The weighted dilation has the bounded extension
\[
 W_t=(e^{-tL/2}\otimes I)We^{tL/2}
 =e^{t/2}W_{-1}+W_0+e^{-t/2}W_1.
\]
This identity initially means equality on finite-depth vectors; the finite
sum defines the bounded extension, without applying an unbounded conjugation
to an arbitrary vector.

Put $D_t=W_t-W$ and $D_1'=(W_{-1}-W_1)/2$. For $0\le t\le1$,
\[
 \|D_t\|\le2\sinh(t/2)\le2t,\qquad
 \|D_t-tD_1'\|\le2(\cosh(t/2)-1)\le t^2/2.
 \tag{T4}
\]
Both scalar inequalities follow from their nonnegative power series on
$0\le t/2\le1/2$. Consequently
\[
 \|W_t^\dagger W_t-I-t(W^\dagger D_1'+D_1'{}^\dagger W)\|
 \le5t^2.
 \tag{T5}
\]
The derivative is $W^\dagger D_1'+D_1'{}^\dagger W
=L-\Phi^*(L)$ on the core and hence as a bounded operator. Define the bounded
positive tilted map
\[
 \mathfrak T_t(B)=W_t^\dagger(B\otimes I)W_t.
\]
For bounded $C$,
\[
 \|\mathfrak T_t(C)-\Phi^*(C)\|
 \le M(2\|D_t\|+\|D_t\|^2)\le8Mt.
 \tag{T6}
\]
Combining (T5)--(T6) yields the actual operator Taylor bound
\[
 \mathfrak T_t(A_t)-A_t=-tK+\mathcal R_t,\qquad
 \|\mathcal R_t\|\le(5+8M)t^2.
 \tag{T7}
\]
Under (T1), this is at most $-\nu tI/2$. Since $A_t\le3I/2$,
\[
 \mathfrak T_t(A_t)\le A_t-\nu tI/2
 \le(1-\nu t/3)A_t.
\]
Conjugate this bounded inequality by $e^{-tL/2}$. The finite-band
factorization gives
$\Phi^*(X_t)=e^{-tL/2}\mathfrak T_t(A_t)e^{-tL/2}$, first as forms on the
core and then everywhere by boundedness. This proves (T2). In particular,
we have not exponentiated an operator inequality for $L+C$.

Iteration and normal duality give
$\Tr[X_t\Phi^n(\tau)]\le\lambda^n\Tr(X_t\tau)\le(1+tM)\lambda^n$.
Also $P_{\le h}\le e^{th}e^{-tL}$. The lower bound in (T2) therefore proves
(T3), including its uniformity over $\tau$.

Injectivity follows from $A_t\ge I/2$ and injectivity of $e^{-tL/2}$.
Compactness follows from finite-dimensional levels and the decay of
$e^{-t\ell/2}$. If a normal invariant density existed, pairing (T2) with it
would force its strictly positive $X_t$ expectation to vanish. For each
fixed $h$, (T3) gives $\Tr(P_{\le h}\Phi^n(\tau))\to0$. Every finite-rank
operator is approximable in norm by its finite-coordinate compression;
uniform trace norm one then extends this convergence to every compact
operator. This is mass escape, not trace-norm convergence to the zero
operator.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{What is being reused.}
A receiver of this operation must supply the actual normal channel, a finite
depth-bandwidth dilation, a bounded correction, and a positive operator lower
bound for its corrected drift. Mere growth of a mean, or an uncontrolled
boundary term in a truncated drift calculation, is not a substitute. For
bandwidth greater than one the same derivation changes the explicit Taylor
constants; that extension is not needed here. Infinite-dimensional normality
is used in taking expectations and distinguishing an escaped probability mass
from a normal invariant state.

\paragraph{Established method, specific new verification.}
Exponential position conjugation, positive superharmonic operators and
Lyapunov criteria have primary precedents in quantum random-walk and
birth-and-death theory. The point of this lemma is to provide the exact
finite-band, rooted coherent-channel interface needed here, including a
uniform second-order remainder and the full operator positivity. It does
not assert a new generic principle of exponential tilting.

\section{Escape theorem and the original coherent seed}

\begin{theorem}[Independent-input escape]\label{thm:escape}
For every one fixed qutrit density with $S(\rho)>3/2$ and every
$0\le\beta\le1/4$, the true bath channel $\Phi_\rho$ has no invariant
normal state. For every normal initializer and every sublinear depth
$h_n=o(n)$,
\[
 \Tr[P_{\le h_n}\Phi_\rho^n(\tau)]\longrightarrow0
\]
exponentially in $n$, with constants depending on the fixed input and
device, but not on the initializer. The same conclusion holds under
the spectral-window hypothesis (13), without mentioning entropy.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The channel has a finite Kraus dilation: purify the three-dimensional
input and retain its purifier and the outgoing port. Each bath coefficient
of the dilation has depth shift in $\{-1,0,1\}$ by (1), including the
depth-zero root exchange. Theorems \ref{thm:windowdrift} and
Lemma \ref{lem:tilt} therefore apply. All parameters are fixed before the
$n$ limit. No finite reflecting bath or changed initializer is used.
\end{proof}

For the original selected seed,
\[
 \beta=\frac1{512},\quad
 q=\frac12-\frac{3\beta^2}{32},\quad g_1=g=\frac\beta8,\quad
 g_2=0,\quad a_1=a_2=a=\frac{1-q}{2}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The Cycle2 entropy certificate is preserved unchanged.
For this seed, (11) becomes
\[
 \nu_{\mathrm{seed}}=\alpha^2(1-2q)
 -\frac{8\beta^2g^2}{a}
 -\frac{\beta^4g^2q}{\alpha^2a^2}
 =\frac{3626718083480614861275163}
 {5070590311629645407074262188032}
 >\frac{\beta^2}{8},
 \tag{17}
\]
and $M\le8/(3\alpha)<3$. These are exact algebraic inequalities,
not extrapolations from a finite bath simulation.

Taking the conservative drift $\nu_0=\beta^2/8$ and
$t=\beta^2/464$ satisfies (T1). Hence
\[
 \Tr[P_{\le h}\Phi_\rho^n(\tau)]
 \le\min\left\{1,\,
 3\exp\left(\frac{\beta^2h}{464}
               -\frac{\beta^4n}{11136}\right)\right\}.
 \tag{18}
\]
For $h=\beta^2n/48$ the bound is
$3e^{-\beta^4n/22272}$, clipped at one. The constants are conservative:
at the specified angle the exponential denominator is
$1\,530\,520\,185\,864\,192$. This is an asymptotic theorem, not a useful
simulation horizon or a practical performance estimate.

The initial first moment
$\Tr[\Phi_\rho(\tau_\pi)S_1]=-(5/6)\alpha\beta g$
already has the opposite sign from the proposed stabilizing current.
That observation motivated the correction but did not prove this theorem.
The norm-convergent corrector and positive tilt supply the missing
long-time control.

\section{Entropy growth: a stronger obstruction than coordinate escape}
\label{sec:entropy}

The actual initializer $\tau_\pi$ has finite entropy and finite mean depth.
This permits an additional argument that is unavailable from normality alone.
Define the port color-number operator
$N=\ket1\bra1+\ket2\bra2$. On the finite-depth core,
\[
 [U,I_{\mathrm{port}}\otimes L+N\otimes I_E]=0.
 \tag{E1}
\]
Each child move raises depth by one and lowers port color-number by one;
each parent move does the reverse; the seed exchange changes neither.
The identity term also commutes, so (E1) holds for $V_s$ and gives the
block conservation of $L+\sum_{j=1}^nN_j$.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy-flow lower bound]\label{prop:entropyflow}
Let $\tau_0$ have finite entropy and finite mean depth, and use a fixed
product port input $\rho^{\otimes n}$. Suppose a corrected depth
$H=L+C$, with bounded correction $\|C\|\le M$, has drift at least
$\nu I$ for $\Phi_\rho$.
Then, for all $n$,
\[
 S(\tau_n)\ge S(\tau_0)
       +n\,[S(\rho)-2+q+\nu]-2M.
 \tag{E2}
\]
No independence of the outgoing ports is assumed.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Finite forward propagation preserves finite mean depth. A finite
Stinespring environment also ensures finite bath entropy after each finite
number of calls; for this channel its dimension is at most nine, so
$S(\tau_{j+1})\le S(\tau_j)+\log_2 9$.
Consequently all entropy manipulations below involve finite numbers.

Let $\omega_j$ be the outgoing port marginal of the $j$th call and
$q_j'=\Tr(\omega_j\ket0\bra0)$. Conservation (E1) gives
\[
 q_j'-q=\Tr[L(\tau_j-\tau_{j-1})],
 \qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^nq_j'=nq+\Tr[L(\tau_n-\tau_0)].
 \tag{E3}
\]
The fresh input marginal at that call is $\rho$, independent of the
current bath, even though the bath is correlated with earlier outputs.
The conservation identity also follows directly on the entire block.

Pinching a qutrit into zero/color sectors and maximizing its color
entropy give
$S(\omega_j)\le h_2(q_j')+1-q_j'\le2-q_j'$.
Subadditivity of the entire output, with all correlations retained, yields
\[
 S(B_1\cdots B_n)\le
 n(2-q)-\Tr[L(\tau_n-\tau_0)].
 \tag{E4}
\]
The complete unitary output on ports and bath has entropy
$nS(\rho)+S(\tau_0)$. A second application of subadditivity and (E4) gives
\[
 S(\tau_n)\ge S(\tau_0)+n[S(\rho)-2+q]
                        +\Tr[L(\tau_n-\tau_0)].
 \tag{E5}
\]
Telescoping the bounded corrected drift gives
$\Tr[L(\tau_n-\tau_0)]\ge\nu n-2M$, proving (E2).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Extensive bath information for high-entropy iid inputs]
\label{thm:entropy}
Fix $0\le\beta\le1/8$, the actual initializer $\tau_0=\tau_\pi$, and
one qutrit input $\rho$ with $S(\rho)>3/2$. Put
$\delta=1/2-q>0$ and $\epsilon=S(\rho)-3/2>0$. Then
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{S(\Phi_\rho^n(\tau_\pi))}{n}
 \ge c_\rho,\qquad
 c_\rho=S(\rho)-\frac32-\delta+\nu
 \ge\epsilon+\frac{\delta}{2}>0,
 \tag{E6}
\]
where $\nu$ is the explicit bound (11), or another bound at least
$3\delta/2$ supplied by (14) in this interval.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The spectral-window theorem gives $\nu\ge3\delta/2$ in this interval.
Apply Proposition \ref{prop:entropyflow} and divide by $n$.
\end{proof}

For the original seed, the sharper (17) gives
$c_{\mathrm{seed}}=S(\rho)-3/2-3\beta^2/32+\nu_{\mathrm{seed}}
> \beta^2/32$.
The preserved exact entropy interval and rational drift give its numerical
value if desired; its positivity, not an optimized coefficient, is the
scientific point. No reservoir preparation is changed to obtain it.

\subsection{Why this also excludes non-coordinate subexponential compression}

An entropy lower bound alone can be misleading in infinite dimension.
Here finite propagation and the actual exponentially decaying initializer
supply the needed additional control.

\begin{theorem}[Any asymptotically lossless bath compression has exponential rank]
\label{thm:rank}
Under Theorem \ref{thm:entropy}, let $Q_n$ be any finite-rank bath
projections, not necessarily coordinate projections. If
$\Tr[Q_n\Phi_\rho^n(\tau_\pi)]\to1$, then
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2\rank Q_n}{n}\ge c_\rho>0.
 \tag{E7}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $a_n=\lceil\log_3(n+1)\rceil$. From (2), the initial mass at
depth greater than $a_n$ is exactly
$u_n=3^{-(a_n+1)}=O(1/n)$. The initial density is block diagonal, hence
is exactly a convex combination of its normalized finite-depth part
$\sigma_{0,n}$ and its normalized tail $\zeta_{0,n}$. Linearity gives
\[
 \tau_n=(1-u_n)\sigma_n+u_n\zeta_n,
 \qquad
 \sigma_n=\Phi_\rho^n(\sigma_{0,n}),\quad
 \zeta_n=\Phi_\rho^n(\zeta_{0,n}).
 \tag{E8}
\]
No cutoff is implemented on the physical bath.
The finite part is supported in $P_{\le a_n+n}$, whose dimension
$D_n=2(2^{a_n+n+1}-1)$ obeys $\log_2D_n=n+o(n)$.
The normalized initial tail has entropy $a_n+1+S(\tau_\pi)$:
depth is shifted by $a_n+1$, and the prefix introduces that many
uniform binary color bits. Thus
\[
 S(\zeta_n)\le a_n+1+S(\tau_\pi)+n\log_2 9.
\]
The entropy upper bound for a binary mixture in (E8) gives
\[
 S(\tau_n)\le h_2(u_n)+(1-u_n)S(\sigma_n)+u_nS(\zeta_n)
 \le S(\sigma_n)+o(n).
 \tag{E9}
\]

Write $k_n=\rank Q_n$ and let $\widetilde Q_n$ be the projection onto
the range of $P_{\le a_n+n}Q_n$. This is a subspace of the finite
support, of rank at most $k_n$. The compression
$P_{\le a_n+n}Q_nP_{\le a_n+n}$ is a contraction supported in that
range, so $\Tr(\widetilde Q_n\sigma_n)\ge\Tr(Q_n\sigma_n)$.
By (E8) the latter tends to one when $\Tr(Q_n\tau_n)\to1$.
Let $v_n=1-\Tr(\widetilde Q_n\sigma_n)\to0$.
Pinching $\sigma_n$ across this projection and maximizing each block
entropy yield
\[
 S(\sigma_n)\le h_2(v_n)+(1-v_n)\log_2 k_n
                        +v_n\log_2D_n
 \le1+\log_2 k_n+v_n\log_2D_n.
 \tag{E10}
\]
If $k_n>D_n$ the desired intermediate upper bound is immediate using
$S(\sigma_n)\le\log_2D_n\le\log_2k_n$; zero-size blocks are omitted.
Combining (E6), (E9) and (E10) proves (E7).
\end{proof}

For comparison, on any infinite countable Hilbert space the normal states
$\xi_n=(1-1/n)\ket0\bra0+(1/n)I_{d_n}/d_n$, where the second summand
has support orthogonal to $\ket0$ and $d_n=2^{n^2}$, satisfy
$S(\xi_n)=h_2(1/n)+n$. Yet the fixed rank-one projection onto
$\ket0$ captures mass $1-1/n\to1$. Entropy growth alone therefore
does not prove (E7). The finite propagation and controlled weighted tail
entropy established in (E8)--(E9) remove that loophole in the actual model.

Thus the original high-entropy iid seed cannot be rescued by replacing
its low-depth cutoff with a different asymptotically lossless
subexponential-rank bath projection. This is stronger than coordinate
escape, but still concerns the \emph{averaged product-input bath}.
It does not prove a lower bound on every code's environment information.

\section{The optimized iid coherent information is exactly \texorpdfstring{$3/2$}{3/2}}
\label{sec:iidinformation}

Let $\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi}$ be the complete $n$-call port channel with
the one fixed actual initializer $\tau_\pi$. No bath is replaced between
calls. For an input density $\omega$, write
\[
 I_c(\omega,\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi})
 =S(B^n)-S(E_nF),
\]
where $F$ purifies the initial bath. This is an analytic purification,
not an accessible resource or a return obligation. The finite entropy
$S(F)=S(\tau_\pi)$ ensures that all displayed entropies are finite for
the inputs used below.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform iid information ceiling and matching limit]
\label{thm:iidinformation}
For every fixed $0\le\beta\le1/8$ and every $n\ge1$,
\[
 \frac1n\max_{\rho\in\mathcal D(\mathbb C^3)}
 I_c(\rho^{\otimes n},\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi})
 \le \frac32+\left(\frac4n\right)^{2/3}.
 \tag{I1}
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\max_{\rho\in\mathcal D(\mathbb C^3)}
 I_c(\rho^{\otimes n},\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi})=\frac32.}
 \tag{I2}
\]
The maximization in (I1)--(I2) may choose a different density at each
horizon. Within each block the input remains a tensor power. For one
fixed density with $S(\rho)=3/2+\epsilon>3/2$ and
$\delta=1/2-\rho_{00}$, a sharper statement is
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac1n
 I_c(\rho^{\otimes n},\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi})
 \le\frac32-\epsilon-\delta<\frac32.
 \tag{I3}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
First take $S(\rho)>3/2$. Unitary invariance gives
$S(B^nE_n)=nS(\rho)+S(\tau_\pi)$; subadditivity and the entropy
triangle inequality then give
\[
 S(E_nF)\ge S(E_n)-S(F)
 \ge nS(\rho)-S(B^n).
\]
Thus $I_c\le2S(B^n)-nS(\rho)$. Combining (E4) with the corrected
drift and $\nu\ge3\delta/2$ yields
\[
 I_c(\rho^{\otimes n},\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi})
 \le n\left(\frac32-\epsilon-\delta\right)+4M.
 \tag{I4}
\]
For a fixed density $M<\infty$, proving (I3).

The dependence of $M$ on the density must be controlled before placing
the maximization inside the limit. From (8), (10) and (15),
\[
 1-r=\frac{2\delta}{1/2+\delta},\qquad
 M\le\frac{2\sqrt3\,\beta(1/2+\delta)}{\alpha\sqrt\delta}
 \le\frac{2\sqrt3\,\beta}{\alpha\sqrt\delta}
 \le\frac1{\sqrt\delta}.
 \tag{I5}
\]
The final inequality uses $\beta\le1/8$ and
$2\sqrt3\,\beta/\alpha\le2/\sqrt{21}<1$.
Also $I_c\le S(\rho^{\otimes n})=nS(\rho)$, by the entropy triangle
inequality with the purifying input reference. Pinching the input into
zero/color sectors gives $S(\rho)\le h_2(q)+1-q\le3/2+\delta$.
Together with (I4)--(I5),
\[
 \frac{I_c}{n}-\frac32
 \le\min\left\{\delta,\frac4{n\sqrt\delta}\right\}
 \le\left(\frac4n\right)^{2/3}.
 \tag{I6}
\]
The last scalar bound follows by splitting at
$\delta=(4/n)^{2/3}$. Inputs with $S(\rho)\le3/2$ satisfy
$I_c\le nS(\rho)\le3n/2$ directly. This proves the uniform upper bound.
The maximum exists since the input and output port spaces are finite
dimensional and coherent information is continuous in the input.

For the lower limit use the unchanged critical input
$\rho_c=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/4,1/4)$. Its exact depth law is inherited
from Cycle2. Here is the short entropy consequence, including the boundary.
Starting from $\tau_\pi$, the bath stays diagonal and scalar on each depth
level: the equal color weights preserve this property, and the two vacuum
weights remain equal. Hence $[R,\tau_j]=0$ and the coherent root term
vanishes for this particular trajectory. At depth at least one the lazy
reflected walk moves up and down with probabilities $\alpha^2/2$ each;
at zero it moves up with probability $\alpha^2/2$ and otherwise stays.
Its squared-depth increment is at most $\alpha^2$, and
$\Tr(L^2\tau_\pi)=1$. Consequently
\[
 m_n=\Tr(L\tau_n)\le\sqrt{1+\alpha^2n}.
\]
For a nonnegative integer-valued variable of mean $m$, the entropy is
at most that of the geometric law of mean $m$; relative entropy to that
law proves this directly. It is at most $\log_2(e(m+1))$.
Conditioned on depth $\ell$, the bath has $2^{\ell+1}$ equiprobable
vertices. Therefore
\[
 S(\tau_n)=H(L_n)+1+m_n
 \le1+m_n+\log_2(e(m_n+1))=o(n).
 \tag{I7}
\]
Finally, unitary invariance and subadditivity give
$S(B^n)\ge3n/2+S(\tau_\pi)-S(\tau_n)$, while
$S(E_nF)\le S(\tau_n)+S(\tau_\pi)$. Thus
\[
 I_c(\rho_c^{\otimes n},\mathcal N_n^{\tau_\pi})
 \ge\frac{3n}{2}-2S(\tau_n).
 \tag{I8}
\]
This proves the lower limit in (I2).
\end{proof}

This theorem is an information result for a restricted family of block
inputs. It is not a single-letter quantum-capacity formula for this memory
channel. Maximizing over arbitrary temporally correlated inputs is a
strictly stronger operation than the maximization in (I2), and feedback
protocols are not represented by these product averages. The inherited
capacity floor already supplies actual arbitrary-reference codes; (I2)
does not replace that coding proof or create a fresh-bath block model.
Even independent but nonidentically distributed products
$\rho_1\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_n$ lie outside the optimized class in
(I2). The horizon-dependent choice of one common marginal is a genuine
uniform strengthening over a fixed-marginal limit; it is not the same as
allowing a separate marginal at every call.

\section{Counterexamples and assumptions that cannot be weakened}

\subsection{Bounded positive drift without propagation control has no exponential tail}

Consider the classical reflected chain on $\mathbb N_0$,
$Y_{j+1}=\max(0,Y_j+X_{j+1})$, with independent increments
\[
 \Pr(X=1)=\frac34,\qquad
 \Pr(X=-2^k)=\frac3{16}\,4^{-k},\quad k\ge0.
 \tag{F1}
\]
The probabilities sum to one and $\mathbb EX=3/8$.
At every state $y$ the conditional depth drift lies in $[3/8,3/4]$,
since it is
$3/4-(3/16)\sum_k4^{-k}\min(y,2^k)$.
This is a normal classical channel, or a normal quantum channel on
diagonal densities, with bounded strictly positive mean increment.

Starting at zero, $Y_{n-1}\le n-1$. Any last negative jump of size at
least $n$ resets the state to zero. With $J=\lceil\log_2n\rceil$,
\[
 \Pr(Y_n=0)\ge\frac14\,4^{-J}\ge\frac1{16n^2}.
 \tag{F2}
\]
No exponential low-depth bound can hold. Indeed its positive exponential
tilt is unbounded: the squared tilted-dilation norm on a depth-$y$ basis
vector is at least $e^{ty}\Pr(X\le-y)$.
This shows why positive drift alone is insufficient. Finite bandwidth
is one sufficient propagation condition; suitable exponential jump-tail
conditions could replace it, but have not been assumed or proved here.

\subsection{Same-device ballistic depth with subexponential information}

For the actual fixed $V_s$, use the pure color input $\ket1\bra1$
on every call and keep the actual $\tau_\pi$. Since the two possible
port outputs are orthogonal, the exact reduced bath channel is
\[
 \Phi_1(\tau)=\alpha^2S_1\tau S_1^\dagger+\beta^2\tau.
\]
Thus, with $p_{n,k}=\binom nk\alpha^{2k}\beta^{2(n-k)}$,
\[
 \Phi_1^n(\tau_\pi)=
 \sum_{k=0}^n p_{n,k}S_1^k\tau_\pi S_1^{\dagger k}.
 \tag{F3}
\]
For $\alpha>0$, its depth grows ballistically: the number of pushes
has binomial law. Nevertheless,
\[
 S(\Phi_1^n(\tau_\pi))
 \le S(\tau_\pi)+H(p_{n,\cdot})
 \le S(\tau_\pi)+\log_2(n+1).
 \tag{F4}
\]
For an initial cutoff $P_{\le a}$, the span
$\sum_{k=0}^nS_1^kP_{\le a}E$ has dimension at most
$(n+1)\rank P_{\le a}$ and captures at least
$\Tr(P_{\le a}\tau_\pi)$ of (F3).
Taking $a=O(\log n)$ with $a\to\infty$ gives a polynomial-rank,
asymptotically lossless non-radial coordinate compression despite ballistic depth.
This uses the same bath and fixed angle, not a replacement physical model.
Its port input has zero entropy and carries no logical code by itself.
It demonstrates why the separate entropy-flow proof in Section
\ref{sec:entropy} is necessary before excluding alternative projections.

\subsection{Critical and stationary limits}

At $q=1/2$, $g=0$, the insertion resolvent with $r<1$ ceases to be
norm convergent and the already-proved critical depth is diffusive.
One cannot extend a strictly positive drift estimate through that
singularity by notation. At the original reference input
$q=3/4$, $g=0$, the exact normal $\tau_\pi$ remains stationary for
every angle. At $\alpha=0$ the device is a scalar identity and every
normal bath is invariant. These examples delimit the hypotheses;
they do not contradict the new high-entropy, small-angle theorem.

\section{What is closed and the chosen next operation}

The proposed fixed iid coherent seed does not stabilize the bath. More
strongly, every fixed iid qutrit density of entropy above $3/2$ has the
proved escape behavior in the stated interval, and for $\beta\le1/8$
cannot have asymptotically lossless subexponential-rank final-bath
compression from the actual $\tau_\pi$. This closes the intended
high-entropy iid extension of the Cycle2 compression construction.
The iid (tensor-power) coherent-information optimum itself is exactly
$3/2$ asymptotically, even with a horizon-dependent single-site marginal,
by Theorem \ref{thm:iidinformation}.

The original capacity question remains open. A reliable code can exploit
temporal correlations, a small exceptional subspace of a product-typical
space, or a recovery that corrects an extensive environment. Neither
entropy growth of this average nor failure of a sufficient low-environment
coding theorem is a capacity converse. The inherited bounds remain
$3/2\le Q\le Q_{\leftarrow}\le1.58164276090312\ldots$
at $0<\beta\le1/512$. No new bath-return statement, efficient code,
physical device or rate gain is supplied.

The next selected operation is a minimal temporally correlated block,
with the same fixed $\beta=1/512$ and actual initializer $\tau_\pi$.
One explicit two-call family is
\[
\begin{split}
 \varrho_{x,y,z,c}={}&x\ket{00}\bra{00}
  +z\sum_{a,b=1}^2\ket{ab}\bra{ab}\\
 &+\sum_{a=1}^2\left[
 y(\ket{a0}\bra{a0}+\ket{0a}\bra{0a})
 +c(\ket{a0}\bra{0a}+\ket{0a}\bra{a0})\right],
\end{split}
 \tag{N1}
\]
where $x,z\ge0$, $y\ge|c|$, and $x+4y+4z=1$.
Its eigenvalues are $x$, four copies of $z$, and two copies each
of $y+c$ and $y-c$. Its marginals are diagonal with zero probability
$q=x+2y$, but its temporal coherences are not products of single-port
coherences. At $(x,y,z,c)=(1/4,1/8,1/16,0)$ it equals
$\eta_c^{\otimes2}$ and has entropy three bits. Negative $c$ favors
the antisymmetric color--zero order implicated by the exact dark-pair
identity. All two-call outcomes still count.

The actual next obligation is to derive the exact two-call reduced bath
map and determine whether entropy above three bits per block can coexist
with subextensive complementary information, or whether the
corrected-drift/entropy argument extends to this correlated family.
The new single-site iid theorem does not answer that. The full
nine-dimensional input space is used; this is not a return to coding
solely inside the balanced Dyck space. Neither $s$ nor the initializer
will vary with the horizon. A positive entropy calculation or one
dark vector will not count as a communication result.

\section{Primary comparison and internal status}

Quantum birth-and-death theory supplies invariant-state and bounded
Lyapunov criteria, including coherence-corrected number observables
\cite{Buecher,BuecherEtAl}. Those scalar half-line models do not classify this
branching qutrit channel: it has two independent creation operators,
exponentially growing level multiplicity, and a vacuum seed exchange.
The coherent depth algebra is not a closed scalar Markov algebra.

Exponential position conjugation and analytic deformations have primary
precedents for unitary lattice dynamics \cite{CedzichEtAl} and open quantum
random walks \cite{CarbonePautrat}. The former's translation-invariant
lattice hypotheses and the latter's position-dephasing model do not
automatically imply our strict drift or tail estimate. The general
Lyapunov/tilting principle is established. The specific contribution here
is the exact insertion corrector, its nondivergent commutator bound,
the root completion, the qutrit spectral-window bridge, and their
consequences for this unchanged infinite bath.

The entropy argument uses established unitary invariance, subadditivity,
pinching and finite-dimensional entropy continuity. Its model-specific
input is the exact conserved charge combined with the corrected drift.
It does not assume independent outputs. No novel general entropy
inequality or general quantum capacity converse is claimed.

Distinct internal contributions checked the infinite-bath algebra,
the anisotropic/spectral bridge, the finite-band positive tilt, and the
entropy/compression consequence and the uniform iid information limit.
Exact normal-form replays check
finite identities with their remainder terms; they do not certify the
infinite norm series or all-time limits. The complete proofs carry those
obligations. Internal checking, historical originality, efficient
implementation and physical performance remain separate statuses.
