Q4 MATCHING DOMINATION FOR REAL TRACE-ZERO INVOLUTIONS
2026-10-22. Exact analytical result. No numerical evidence is used.

THEOREM.
Let S be a real symmetric orthogonal 4-by-4 matrix with trace S=0
(equivalently, two +1 and two -1 eigenvalues), and put B_ij=S_ij^2.
Then B dominates, in the real positive-semidefinite order, a convex
combination of the three perfect-matching permutation matrices.

This certifies the entire real trace-zero involution class. It does not
prove the assertion for general complex symmetric unitaries, or by itself
for real involutions with one or three negative eigenvalues.

1. EXACT QUATERNION PARAMETRIZATION.
For a=(a1,a2,a3), b=(b1,b2,b3) in R^3 define

L_a = [ 0  -a1 -a2 -a3 ]       R_b = [ 0  -b1 -b2 -b3 ]
      [ a1  0  -a3  a2 ]             [ b1  0   b3 -b2 ]
      [ a2  a3  0  -a1 ]             [ b2 -b3  0   b1 ]
      [ a3 -a2  a1  0  ]             [ b3  b2 -b1  0  ].

If ||a||=||b||=1, then L_a and R_b commute, are skew-symmetric,
and square to -I. Thus S=L_a R_b is symmetric orthogonal and trace zero.
Its exact entries are

[ -a1b1-a2b2-a3b3,  a2b3-a3b2,      -a1b3+a3b1,       a1b2-a2b1      ]
[ a2b3-a3b2,       -a1b1+a2b2+a3b3, -a1b2-a2b1,      -a1b3-a3b1      ]
[ -a1b3+a3b1,      -a1b2-a2b1,       a1b1-a2b2+a3b3, -a2b3-a3b2      ]
[ a1b2-a2b1,       -a1b3-a3b1,      -a2b3-a3b2,        a1b1+a2b2-a3b3 ].

Conversely every real symmetric orthogonal trace-zero S has this form.
Here is an algebraic justification, avoiding a parametrization assumption.
Let E_ij=L_ei R_ej for the three coordinate vectors e_i. The nine E_ij
are symmetric and trace zero, and

    tr(E_ij E_kl)=4 delta_ik delta_jl.

They therefore form a basis of the nine-dimensional real space of
symmetric trace-zero 4-by-4 matrices. Write S=sum_ij T_ij E_ij. Direct
quaternion multiplication gives

    S^2 = ||T||_F^2 I - 2 sum_ij cof(T)_ij E_ij.

The identity and all nine E_ij are linearly independent. Since S^2=I,
we get ||T||_F=1 and cof(T)=0. Hence T has rank one and can be written
T=ab^T with ||a||=||b||=1. This gives S=L_a R_b as claimed.

2. EXACT WALSH COMPRESSION.
Let Q have the following orthonormal columns:

    q1=(1,1,-1,-1)^T/2,
    q2=(1,-1,1,-1)^T/2,
    q3=(1,-1,-1,1)^T/2.

These span the orthogonal complement of the all-ones vector. Write

    p_i=a_i^2,    q_i=b_i^2,    v_i=a_i b_i,
    A=(Q^T B Q+I_3)/2.

(The scalar q_i here is unrelated to the displayed column labels.)
Then sum p_i=sum q_i=1, and expansion of the entries above gives

    (Q^T B Q)_ii = (2p_i-1)(2q_i-1),
    (Q^T B Q)_ij = 4 v_i v_j  for i!=j.

Consequently

    A = diag(1-p_i-q_i) + 2 vv^T.                         (1)

The matching matrices P_(12)(34), P_(13)(24), P_(14)(23)
restrict respectively to diag(1,-1,-1), diag(-1,1,-1),
diag(-1,-1,1) in this basis. Thus the desired conclusion is exactly
existence of m_i>=0, sum m_i=1, with

    A-diag(m_i) >=0.                                     (2)

3. CONSTRUCTING THE NONNEGATIVE WEIGHTS.
Put n_i=1-p_i-q_i. Then sum n_i=1.
If all n_i>=0, take m_i=n_i; equation (1) leaves 2vv^T>=0.

Otherwise precisely one n_i is negative: two strict inequalities
p_i+q_i>1 would contradict sum_i(p_i+q_i)=2. Relabel the three
coordinates so the negative one is n_1=-r, where r>0. Then

    n_2-r=p_3+q_3>=0,    n_3-r=p_2+q_2>=0.                (3)

Let w=|v_2|+|v_3|. If w>0 set

    x=r |v_2|/w,     y=r |v_3|/w.

If w=0 set x=y=r/2. In every case x,y>=0 and x+y=r.
Define

    m_1=0,    m_2=n_2-x,    m_3=n_3-y.

Equation (3) shows all weights are nonnegative, and their sum is one.
The remaining matrix is

    R=A-diag(m)=diag(-r,x,y)+2vv^T.                       (4)

4. VERIFYING POSITIVE SEMIDEFINITENESS, INCLUDING ZERO CASES.
Cauchy-Schwarz gives

    w^2 <= (p_2+p_3)(q_2+q_3)
         = (1-p_1)(1-q_1).

Since v_1^2=p_1q_1 and r=p_1+q_1-1,

    v_1^2-w^2 >= p_1q_1-(1-p_1)(1-q_1)=r.               (5)

In particular v_1^2>=r and the upper-left entry of R is

    h=2v_1^2-r >= r>0.

When w=0, v_2=v_3=0, and R=diag(h,x,y)>=0 directly.
When w>0, take the Schur complement of h in (4). It equals

    diag(x,y) - (2r/h) [v_2,v_3]^T [v_2,v_3].            (6)

An x or y is zero exactly when its corresponding v coordinate is zero.
Such a row and column of (6) vanish. On the remaining positive diagonal
coordinates, the rank-one subtraction criterion is

    (2r/h) (v_2^2/x + v_3^2/y) <=1,

omitting zero-coordinate terms. Our choice of x,y makes the parenthesis
w^2/r, so the left side is 2w^2/h. Equation (5) gives

    h-2w^2 = 2(v_1^2-w^2)-r >=r>0.

Thus (6) is positive semidefinite in every case. Therefore R>=0,
which proves (2), and hence the theorem. QED.

VERIFICATION SCOPE.
All claims above follow from the displayed exact identities and inequalities.
The entry and compression identities were also expanded with SymPy as an
algebraic cross-check; no floating-point search or SDP solver is needed.
The only claim is matching domination for the stated real trace-zero class.
