Q4 MATCHING DOMINATION: CERTIFIED BALANCED-DIAGONAL REGIME
2026-10-22. Exact analytical partial result; no numerical evidence is used.

The full symmetric-unitary statement is NOT proved here, and no counterexample
to it is claimed. Nothing here establishes a full entropy theorem.

THEOREM.
Let B be any real symmetric, entrywise nonnegative 4-by-4 matrix with every
row sum equal to 1. Put d_i=B_ii. If

    2 max_i d_i <= sum_i d_i,

then there are p_1,p_2,p_3 >= 0, with p_1+p_2+p_3=1, such that

    B - [p_1 P_(01)(23) + p_2 P_(02)(13) + p_3 P_(03)(12)] >= 0

as a real quadratic form. Thus the assertion holds for every symmetric
unitary S whose B_ij=|S_ij|^2 satisfies the displayed diagonal condition.
The theorem itself needs neither unitarity nor unistochasticity.

PROOF.
First construct nonnegative symmetric edge weights w_ij=w_ji, w_ii=0,
with prescribed weighted degrees sum_j w_ij=d_i. Here is an exact existence
construction. Let D=sum_i d_i. If D=0 take every weight zero. Otherwise,
partition the circle R/(D Z) into four consecutive half-open arcs I_i of
length d_i, and let T(t)=t+D/2 modulo D. Define

    w_ij = Lebesgue measure of {t in I_i : T(t) in I_j}.

The involution T preserves measure, so w_ij=w_ji. No I_i overlaps its
half-turn except on sets of measure zero, because d_i<=D/2. Hence w_ii=0.
The partition gives sum_j w_ij=d_i.

Let K be the weighted graph Laplacian:

    K_ii=d_i,       K_ij=-w_ij for i!=j.

Then K times the all-ones vector is zero, and

    x^T K x = sum_{i<j} w_ij (x_i-x_j)^2 >= 0.

Set M=B-K. It is real symmetric with zero diagonal, nonnegative off-diagonal
entries M_ij=B_ij+w_ij, and every row sum equal to 1.

Every symmetric 4-by-4 matrix with zero diagonal and unit row sums has equal
entries on complementary edges:

    M_01=M_23,    M_02=M_13,    M_03=M_12.

For example, subtracting the sum of rows 2 and 3 from that of rows 0 and 1
gives 2(M_01-M_23)=0; the other identities follow by relabelling. Therefore
take p_1=M_01, p_2=M_02, p_3=M_03. Nonnegativity and the row-0 sum give the
required convex weights, and B-M=K is positive semidefinite. QED.

EXACT REFORMULATION OF THE REMAINING CASE.
For any real symmetric stochastic B, existence of the desired mixture is
equivalent to existence of a real K satisfying

    K >= 0,    K 1=0,    K_ii=B_ii,    K_ij<=B_ij (i!=j).

The forward implication sets K=B-M. The reverse implication uses M=B-K
and the complementary-edge argument above. Consequently any failure of
the symmetric-unitary assertion must lie in the regime where exactly one
diagonal entry B_kk exceeds the sum of the other three. The graph-Laplacian
construction cannot cover that regime, because weighted degrees require
every d_i<=sum_{j!=i}d_j. A PSD K with some positive off-diagonal entries
may nevertheless work there.

WALSH / DIAGONAL-PACKING REFORMULATION.
Let Q have orthonormal columns

    (1,1,-1,-1)^T/2, (1,-1,1,-1)^T/2, (1,-1,-1,1)^T/2,

and let A=Q^T B Q. The three matching matrices restrict in this basis to
diag(1,-1,-1), diag(-1,1,-1), diag(-1,-1,1), respectively. Because B and
every matching have the same all-ones eigenvector, the desired condition
is exactly

    A+I_3-diag(t_1,t_2,t_3) >=0,
    t_i>=0,    t_1+t_2+t_3=2,

with p_i=t_i/2. This is an exact equivalence, not a proof of feasibility.

PHYSICAL BOUNDARY EXAMPLE OUTSIDE THE CERTIFIED REGIME.
Take S=diag(I_3-(2/3)J_3, 1), a real symmetric orthogonal matrix. Then

    B_ii=1/9 for i=0,1,2; B_33=1;
    B_ij=4/9 for distinct i,j in {0,1,2}; B_i3=0 for i<3.

The balanced-diagonal hypothesis fails. Nevertheless the uniform mixture
M=(J_4-I_4)/3 works exactly:

    B-M=(1/9) v v^T,    v=(1,1,1,-3)^T.

Thus a large diagonal entry alone is not an obstruction, and this family
must not be misreported as a counterexample. The full problem remains
open in this note.

EXACT ONE-PARAMETER EXTENSION OF THAT BOUNDARY.
For every 0<=t<=1, let

    v(t)=(sqrt((1-t)/3),sqrt((1-t)/3),sqrt((1-t)/3),sqrt(t))^T,
    S(t)=I_4-2v(t)v(t)^T,    B(t)_ij=|S(t)_ij|^2.

These are real symmetric orthogonal S(t), and the SAME uniform matching
mixture M=(J_4-I_4)/3 works throughout. Indeed B(t) has eigenvalue 1 on
the all-ones vector. On the two-dimensional subspace of vectors supported
on {0,1,2} with sum zero, its eigenvalue is (4t-1)/3. On the remaining
direction (1,1,1,-3)^T, its eigenvalue is 1-16t(1-t)/3. Since M has
eigenvalue -1/3 on the entire all-ones orthogonal complement, B(t)-M has
the exact eigenvalues

    0,    4t/3,    4t/3,    (4/3)(2t-1)^2.

All are nonnegative. At t=0 this recovers the preceding rank-one boundary.
