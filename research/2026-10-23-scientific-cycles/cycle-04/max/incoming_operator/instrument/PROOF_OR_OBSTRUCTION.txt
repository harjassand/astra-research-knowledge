1. RESULT AND ITS PRECISE SCOPE

Use the original finite positive-probability class, forward KL budget K,
fixed symmetric stochastic target-independent Q, fixed eta during a run,
G=independence MH with proposal q, the specified two-factor L_eta, and
C=(I+alpha G+beta L_eta)/2, beta=1-alpha. The chain starts from q.

Define, for 0<c<1,

    F(c)=c(1-c)^((1-c)/c),    r=alpha exp(-K)/beta.

Let c_*=1 when r>=1; when 0<r<1 let c_* be the unique c in (0,1)
such that F(c)=r. Put Lambda_new=1/c_*.

The proved upper consequence is

    liminf eta_n log n/loglog n > Lambda_new
        ==> log n R_{eta_n}(n) -> K.                 [U1]

For an accuracy policy fixed during each run, if
eta(epsilon)/(epsilon log(1/epsilon))->d in (0,infinity), then

    limsup epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
        <= max(K, Lambda_new/d).                    [U2]

The owner's accepted growing-path lower is unchanged. Thus its chi gives

    max(K,1/(d chi)) <= liminf epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
       <= limsup epsilon log T_policy(epsilon)
       <= max(K,1/(d c_*)).                         [U3]

At K=1, alpha=1/2 the defining equations give, numerically,

    c_* = 0.6484195274407001807033953511037583...
    Lambda_new = 1.5422114197377451105644075304312803...

The original d=1 interval therefore improves from the source interval
[1.0884827657...,3.3129027138...] to

    [1.0884827657...,1.5422114197...].

The endpoints do not match, and existence of a sharp coefficient at these
parameters is not proved. When r>=1, the accepted three-state lower and
this upper DO match, proving the exact finite-d coefficient max(K,1/d).
This follows because the source's chi=1 whenever 2r>=1, in particular
when r>=1. The existing d=0 and d=infinity conclusions are unchanged.

There is also a sharp obstruction for this particular sufficient method:
when r<1, no c>=c_* permits a uniform rho<1 bound on
||diag(q_A^(c eta)) C[A,A] diag(q_A^(-c eta))||_{p->p},
p=1/(1-c eta), over all admissible instances and all sufficiently small
eta>0, for ANY fixed admissible h in (0,1/2). For c>c_* there are strict violations; at
c=c_* ratios approach one. Conversely every c<c_* has the needed
uniform contraction for a sufficiently small fixed h and eta_0.
This is a structural obstruction for the declared one-step norm class,
not a lower bound on the actual mixing frontier or on every positive-
operator method. The source's path chi exceeds c_* whenever r<1, so
this norm class alone cannot close the remaining gap to that path bound.

The shared c=1/2,h=1/1000 probe is proved here for 0<eta<=1/10, with an
explicit rational contraction constant rho=4957/5000. Its stronger demand
for all eta in (0,1] remains unresolved by this submission. The small-eta
result is enough for [U1]-[U3]; survival of a finite search is not used.

2. A BASIC POSITIVE-MATRIX NORM INEQUALITY

For a nonnegative rectangular matrix M with maximum row sum R and maximum
column sum S, acting on ROW vectors, Jensen/Hölder gives

    ||M||_{p->p} <= R^(1/p) S^(1-1/p),  1<p<infinity. [N]

Indeed (sum_i v_i M_ij)^p <= S^(p-1) sum_i |v_i|^p M_ij,
and summing j supplies R. Absolute values reduce arbitrary v to v>=0.
Coordinate restrictions and entrywise domination of positive matrices
cannot increase this norm.

For theta=1-1/p, the change of variables v=mu diag(q^(-theta))
identifies counting l^p with the density norm:

    ||v||_p = ||mu/q||_{L^p(q)}.

Consequently the correct row-density operator is D C D^(-1), D=diag(q^theta).
This is the same transformation as in the task, with no reversal of D.

3. REJECTION-AWARE LOCAL BOUND

Take 0<c<1, 0<eta<=1/2, theta=c eta, p=1/(1-theta), and let
M=D L_eta D^(-1). For x!=y write t=q_y/q_x and let a_xy be the actual
two-stage acceptance. Then

    a_xy <= min(1,t^eta),
    M_xy=Q_xy a_xy t^(-theta) <= Q_xy.               [L1]

The last inequality holds separately for t>=1 and t<1. Thus every column
sum of M is <=2: its diagonal is <=1 and the off-diagonal Q column sum
is <=1. Crucially, its row sum keeps the actual rejection diagonal:

    sum_y M_xy
      =1+sum_{y!=x} Q_xy a_xy(t^(-theta)-1).

Terms with t>=1 are nonpositive. For t<1, with s=t^eta,

    a_xy(t^(-theta)-1) <= s^(1-c)-s <= F(c).        [L2]

The maximum is attained at s=(1-c)^(1/c); differentiation gives the
displayed F(c). Since the off-diagonal Q row sum is <=1,

    max row sum M <=1+F(c),  max column sum M <=2.

Applying [N] proves

    ||D L_eta D^(-1)||_{p->p}
        <= (1+F(c))^(1-theta) 2^theta.             [L3]

The same bound holds for the actual killed block L_eta[A,A], since it is
a coordinate restriction, with the original diagonal and lost mass.
The proof also bounds the capped version theta=c min(eta,1/2), using
theta/eta<=c and monotonicity of F, but only the small-eta case is needed.

4. THE GLOBAL KERNEL RETAINS ITS ACTUAL KILLING

Set A={pi/q<h}, B=A^c, b=q(A), with 0<h<1/2. All proposals from A to B
are accepted by G, because their target/proposal weight strictly increases.
If A is nonempty, let qhat=q_A/b and pihat=pi_A/pi(A). The exact identity is

    G[A,A]=b Ghat,                                  [G1]

where Ghat is independence MH on A with proposal qhat and target pihat.
This identity includes the diagonals: G_xx=b-sum_{y in A,y!=x} q_y acc_xy.
No conditional renormalization is applied to the chain. Ghat is only an
auxiliary factorization of the actual sub-Markov block.

For any independence-MH kernel P with proposal r_i, its forward density
map has L^1(r) norm <=1 and

    (rP)_j/r_j=P_jj+sum_{i!=j} r_i acc_ij <=2.

There is an elementary p-norm proof of the resulting bound. Set
k_j=(rP)_j/r_j<=2. Jensen in the incoming weights gives

    |((rf)P)_j/r_j|^p
      <= k_j^(p-1) sum_i (r_i P_ij/r_j)|f_i|^p.

Multiply by r_j and sum j. Stochasticity of P gives norm <=2^theta.
The scaling q_A=b qhat cancels from the conjugation, so [G1] yields

    ||D_A G[A,A] D_A^(-1)||_{p->p} <= b 2^theta.    [G2]

This explicit b factor is genuine global killing, not a substituted chain.

5. UNIFORM KILLED CONTRACTION FOR SMALL SCREENS

Use the owner's entropy consequence

    q(B)>=delta_h=exp(-(K+H2(h))/(1-h)).

For completeness, if d=pi(A), then d<=h, binary data processing and
H2(d)<=H2(h) imply
K>=-H2(h)-(1-h)log(1-b), proving the bound. Let

    B_h(c,theta)=1/2[1+alpha(1-delta_h)2^theta
                       +beta(1+F(c))^(1-theta)2^theta].

The triangle inequality, [L3], and [G2] prove the UNIVERSAL finite bound

    ||D_A C[A,A] D_A^(-1)||_{p->p} <= B_h(c,theta). [K1]

At theta=0 its continuous extension is

    B_h(c,0)=1-[alpha delta_h-beta F(c)]/2.

Whenever beta F(c)<alpha delta_h, continuity therefore permits a fixed
eta_0 in (0,1/2] with

    rho=B_h(c,c eta_0)<1.

Both summands involving theta in B_h increase with theta, because
1+F(c)<2. Thus [K1] is <=rho for every 0<eta<=eta_0. All constants are
independent of support size, q_min, pi_min, Q, eta, and horizon.
If A is empty, there is no killed component and the following estimates
hold immediately; this case does not require qhat.

As h decreases to zero, delta_h tends to exp(-K). The function F is
continuous and strictly increasing from 0 to 1, since

    d/dc log F(c)=-log(1-c)/c^2 >0.

Therefore every c<c_* permits the fixed choices h,eta_0,rho above.

Explicit shared-probe certificate: K=1, alpha=beta=1/2, h=1/1000,
c=1/2, eta_0=1/10. Here F(c)=1/4, theta<=1/20, delta_h>9/25,
2^(1/20)<26/25, and (5/4)^(1-theta)<=5/4. Consequently

    B_h(c,theta)
      < 1/2[1+(1/2)(16/25)(26/25)+(1/2)(5/4)(26/25)]
      =4957/5000 <1.                               [K2]

These auxiliary bounds are elementary, not decimal rounding certificates.
For delta_h>9/25: H2(1/1000)<.008, since log1000<7 and
-(1-h)log(1-h)<h. Hence (1+H2)/(1-h)<1.01. The elementary exponential
series bounds e<11/4 and exp(.01)<100/99 give exp(1.01)<25/9.
The bound log1000<7 follows from e>27/10 and (27/10)^7>1000.
Finally (26/25)^20>2 is an exact integer comparison. The replay checks
these rational/integer comparisons. A sharper, non-interval diagnostic
value of B_h(c,1/20) is .9843790011126334..., but [K2] is the explicit
certificate used here.

6. FIRST-HIT TRANSPORT: A COMPLETE LINK TO THE ORIGINAL FRONTIER

Let tau=inf{j>=0:X_j in B}, nu_j the unconditioned first-hit submeasure,
and define H=D_A C[A,B] D_B^(-1). The global cross block is exactly
the rank-one matrix q_A^theta (q_B^(1-theta))^T. Its row l^p norm is

    b^theta(1-b)^(1-theta) <=1.

The local cross block, after conjugation, is entrywise <=Q[A,B] by [L1].
Both row and column sums of this rectangular Q block are <=1, so [N]
gives norm <=1. The identity contributes no cross block. Hence

    ||H||_{p->p} <=(alpha+beta)/2=1/2.              [H1]

The exact killed evolution, rather than a conditional law, now gives

    nu_0=q_B,
    nu_j=q_A C[A,A]^(j-1) C[A,B]   (j>=1).

Since ||q_A D_A^(-1)||_p=b^(1-theta)<=1, [K1] and [H1] imply

    ||nu_0/q||_{L^p(q)}<=1,
    ||nu_j/q||_{L^p(q)}<=(1/2)rho^(j-1)   (j>=1),
    sum_{j>=0} ||nu_j/q||_{L^p(q)}<=S,
    S=1+1/[2(1-rho)].                              [H2]

This summability estimate replaces exactly the owner's separate mass/
moment interpolation. It does not alter any transitions after the hit.

To make the consequence fully reviewable, here is the resulting finite
certificate. Choose h,c,eta_0,rho as above and 0<eta<=eta_0. Put

    theta=c eta, M=exp(2K+1), N=floor(n/2), n>=2,
    Jbar=2/[alpha(2(n-N)+1)],
    A_R=min(1,K/(log R-1)), R>exp(1),
    rho_h=1-alpha delta_h/2.

Then, uniformly over the original class,

    R_eta(n) <= A_R+rho_h^N
       +S[(A_R/R)^theta+((M/h+1)Jbar)^theta]
       +sqrt((M+R)Jbar).                            [F]

Proof of the transfer from [H2] to [F]: the source's spectral-anchor
inequality applies to the unchanged reversible lazy C. For each j<=N,
t_j=n-j and g_j=C^{t_j}1_E have an anchor z_j in [0,1] satisfying
integral (g_j-z_j)^2/max(W,M) dpi <=Jbar. On h<=W<=R this gives
integral |g_j-z_j|^2 dq <=(M/h+1)Jbar. Since 1/theta>=2 and the test
is bounded by one, Holder bounds its nu_j integral by
||nu_j/q||_Lp times [((M/h+1)Jbar)^theta+(a_R/R)^theta].
The equal-mass comparison with m_j pi, m_j=nu_j(Omega), costs at most
m_j[a_R+sqrt((M+R)Jbar)], with a_R=pi(W>R). Each anchor cancels
separately. This includes the target's low-W region; no return is removed.
The exact first-hit decomposition is

    q C^n=sum_{j=0}^N nu_j C^{n-j}+sigma_n,
    mass(sigma_n)=P(tau>N)<=rho_h^N.

Its equal-mass stationary comparison costs at most that mass. Sum, use
[H2], sum m_j<=1, and the entropy envelope a_R<=A_R to obtain [F].
This repeats the owner's accepted smoothing step with a new proved
summability input; the spectral-anchor lemma and entropy envelope are
explicitly inherited, not claimed as newly derived.

For [U1], choose R=n/(log n)^4. Then A_R=(K+o(1))/log n,
sqrt((M+R)Jbar)=O((log n)^(-2)), and the logarithms of the two bases
raised to theta are -log n+O(loglog n). A strict threshold above 1/c_*
permits a fixed c<c_* with c times that threshold >1. The transport
terms are o(1/log n) for eta_n<=eta_0. On the complementary indices
eta_n>eta_0, the owner's Theorem A with any fixed admissible exponent
has a positive uniform lower exponent and supplies the same conclusion.
The eta-independent Q=I lower proves the matching K limit. No uniform
fixed-eta theorem is substituted at vanishing eta without its finite bound.

For [U2], take n=ceil(exp(A/epsilon)), A>K, and R=n/(log n)^4.
If A d>1/c_* choose a fixed c<c_* with c A d>1 and the associated
h,eta_0,rho. Since eta(epsilon)->0, [F] applies eventually. Its entropy
term is (K/A+o(1))epsilon, and transport terms are
epsilon^(c A d+o(1))=o(epsilon). All other terms are o(epsilon).
The strict margin K/A<1 gives the required crossing. Take A down to
max(K,1/(d c_*)). Kernel monotonicity is used only within the single
fixed-eta run for each error; no varying-kernel diagonal risk is inverted.

7. STRUCTURAL OBSTRUCTION FOR THE SAME NORM CLASS

Fix any h in (0,1/2), choose 0<delta<min(h,1), 0<b<1-exp(-K), and 0<s<1.
For eta tending to zero let r_eta=s^(1/eta). On states 0,1,* set

    q=(b, b r_eta, 1-b-b r_eta),
    pi=(delta b r_eta^2, delta b r_eta,
        1-delta b(r_eta+r_eta^2)).                  [O1]

Let Q swap 0 and 1 and fix *. It is symmetric, stochastic, fixed and
target independent. All probabilities are positive for sufficiently small
eta. W_0=delta r_eta^2, W_1=delta, and W_* tends to 1/(1-b)>1;
thus A={0,1}. Forward KL tends to -log(1-b)<K: both unsafe terms tend
to zero, and the safe term has this limit. Hence the family is admitted
with strict asymptotic slack. This is an actual three-state family.

Forward local acceptance is s, reverse acceptance is r_eta s.
Both global proposals from state 0 to 1 and * are accepted. Therefore
the actual killed transformed row, with theta=c eta, is

    T_00=1-alpha(1-b)/2-beta s/2,
    T_01=(alpha b r_eta+beta s)s^(-c)/2.            [O2]

Take the explicit nonnegative unit row vector v=(1,0). Since p tends to
one, its norm ratio tends to

    1-alpha(1-b)/2+beta[s^(1-c)-s]/2.               [O3]

For 0<c<1 choose s=(1-c)^(1/c), so the bracket is F(c). If r<1 and
c>c_*, one can fix b<1-exp(-K) sufficiently close to that endpoint
so beta F(c)>alpha(1-b). Then [O3]>1, and actual finite admitted
instances violate contraction for all sufficiently small eta.
At c=c_*, taking b up to the endpoint makes [O3] approach one. For
any proposed uniform rho<1, choose such a fixed b first and then eta
small, giving a ratio >rho. Thus no uniform rho<1 exists at the endpoint.
This conclusion holds for any fixed admissible cutoff h and any fixed eta_0>0.
For c>=1 the same exclusion follows directly from [O3] by taking s small:
the bracket tends to one when c=1 and diverges when c>1, while r<1.
One then takes eta small enough that c eta<1 so the displayed p is valid.

It follows that the largest small-screen exponent available from this
strict one-step q-power norm route is exactly c_*, in the supremum sense.
For r<1/2, the owner's chi satisfies F(chi)=2r>r, hence chi>c_*;
for 1/2<=r<1, chi=1>c_*. This explicitly identifies why this particular
route cannot attain the existing path lower endpoint. Other norms,
multi-step estimates, restricted reachable-vector estimates, and different
positive-operator constructions are not excluded.

This obstruction is not itself a mixing lower: state 1 remains unsafe and
does not have a horizon-long retention property. A temporary norm gain
does not imply asymptotic TV error. The original-frontier improvement is
instead supplied by the proved universal inequality [F].

8. AN EXACT FINITE VIOLATION AT c=4/5

The floating instrument suggested a strict c=.8 violation. An independent
rational construction avoids its rounding/admission limitations. In [O1]
take K=1, alpha=beta=1/2, h=1/1000,

    c=4/5, eta=1/100, theta=1/125, p=125/124,
    b=63/100, delta=1/2000, r_eta=2^(-500), s=1/32.

All q and pi entries, Q, C and the two-state T block are exact rational
numbers. The replay stores them as rational strings and checks positivity,
normalization, stochasticity, reversibility, A={0,1}, and all branch choices.
KL<1 has an elementary exact certificate: unsafe KL terms are negative,
and the safe term is at most log(1/q_*). Since r_eta<1/1000,

    q_*>36937/100000>24/65>exp(-1).

The last inequality uses e>65/24, the first five terms of its series.
Thus KL<1. For v=(1,0), [O2] gives

    T_00=2879/3200,
    T_01=1/8+4q_1 >1/8.

For 0<x<=1, x^p>=x-(p-1): this follows from
exp(t)>=1+t and x log x>=-1. Therefore

    ||vT||_p^p
      >= (2879/3200)^p+(1/8)^p
      >=3279/3200-2/124
      =1+849/99200 >1.

This proves a genuine finite violation of the c=.8 contraction claim,
without optimizing the vector, numerical root evaluation, or rounding
claims. It does not refute the c=.5 shared probe. The broader exclusion
of all c>=c_* is proved in Section 7, not inferred from this one example.
