TASK 3 — Identification when A may be rank deficient

Changed assumption: A no longer necessarily has full column rank. Therefore y need not determine B+T theta, and the stage 1 condition rank(CT)=q cannot be reused as a sufficient condition.

Define the observed block matrices

    D=[A; C],    E=[AT; 0],    H=[D E]=[[A,AT],[C,0]].

The combined observation is o=[y;z]=DB+E theta. The exact necessary and sufficient condition for unique identification of theta at every attainable observation is

    rank(H)-rank(D)=q.

Proof: Two parameter pairs produce the same observations exactly when their differences satisfy

    D delta_B + E delta_theta=0.

Thus theta is uniquely identified precisely when E v belongs to col(D) only for v=0. Equivalently, the map v -> E v modulo col(D) is injective. Its rank is

    dim(col(D)+col(E))-dim(col(D))=rank(H)-rank(D),

and its domain has dimension q. This proves both directions. If the condition fails, a nonzero v and some u satisfy Du+Ev=0; adding (u,v) to any parameter pair preserves both observations while changing theta.

For completeness, let P be the orthogonal projector onto col(D)'s orthogonal complement. The condition is equivalent to PE having full column rank. When it holds, reconstruction is

    theta=(E^T P E)^{-1}E^T P o.

This does not require unique reconstruction of B.

In the specified example,

    AT=[[2,0],[0,1]],    CT=I_2.

Nevertheless,

    y_1=B_1+B_2+2 theta_1,    y_2=B_3+theta_2,
    z_1=B_1,                 z_2=B_3.

These equations identify theta_2=y_2-z_2, but leave theta_1 confounded with B_2. Here rank(D)=3 and rank(H)=4, so the rank increment is 1, below q=2. Consequently full column rank of CT does not suffice.

An explicit indistinguishable pair is

    B=(0,0,0)^T,      theta=(0,0)^T,
    B'=(0,-2,0)^T,    theta'=(1,0)^T.

For the second pair, B'+T theta'=(1,-1,0)^T lies in ker(A), and CB'=0. Both pairs therefore give y=(0,0)^T and z=(0,0)^T.

TASK 4 — Fixed and data-dependent projectors

For a fixed orthogonal projector Q, Q^T Q=Q. Expanding r_1^T Q r_2 and using independence and zero means eliminates all error terms except the shared c term. Hence

    E[(Q r_1)^T(Q r_2)]
      =theta_1^T Q theta_2 + E[c^T Q c]
      =(Q theta_1)^T(Q theta_2)+tr(Q Sigma).

Thus subtracting tr(Q Sigma) is unbiased for the projected signal when Q is fixed.

Changed assumption: If Q is learned from c, it is dependent on the shared error. Moreover, the projected signal can itself be random. Conditional unbiasedness would mean

    E[S-tr(Q Sigma) | Q]=theta_1^T Q theta_2,

where S=(Q r_1)^T(Q r_2). Unconditional unbiasedness would mean the equality after taking expectations on both sides. Neither follows from the fixed-Q calculation.

Indeed, if Q is a measurable function of c, write mu_Q=E[c|Q] and M_Q=E[cc^T|Q]. Independence of e_1,e_2 from c gives

    E[S|Q]=theta_1^T Q theta_2
           +(theta_1+theta_2)^T Q mu_Q+tr(Q M_Q).

The conditional mean need not be zero, and M_Q need not equal Sigma. Merely treating a learned Q as fixed after conditioning does not justify using the unconditional covariance Sigma.

Explicit counterexample: Let c~N(0,I_2), with theta_1=theta_2=e_1=e_2=0, and define

    Q=I_2-cc^T/||c||^2

almost surely; its value at the probability-zero event c=0 can be chosen arbitrarily as an orthogonal projector. For c nonzero, Q is a rank-one orthogonal projector and Qc=0. Therefore

    S=0,    tr(Q Sigma)=tr(Q)=1,
    S-tr(Q Sigma)=-1,

whereas the projected signal is zero. Consequently

    E[S|Q]=0,                   E[S]=0,
    E[S-tr(Q Sigma)|Q]=-1,       E[S-tr(Q Sigma)]=-1.

The proposed correction fails conditionally and unconditionally.

To see the conditional distribution explicitly, write c=R u with R=||c|| and u its direction. Gaussian isotropy gives independence of R and u, E[R^2]=2, and sign symmetry. Since Q determines uu^T=I_2-Q, we have

    E[c|Q]=0,    E[cc^T|Q]=2(I_2-Q).

The actual conditional shared-error contribution is therefore tr(2Q(I_2-Q))=0, consistent with Qc=0. Substituting the unconditional Sigma=I_2 instead yields the erroneous correction 1.
