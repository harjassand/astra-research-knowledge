N33 Extended Permanence — Research assessment
1. Reconstructed mathematical result
I retrieved the archived original N33 manuscript, examined its proof through the absorption and switching arguments, and checked the associated internal reviews and later Astra records.

N33 considers a finite network of power-law reactions
\[
y_e\longrightarrow y'_e,\qquad
\dot x=\sum_e k_e(t)x^{y_e}(y'_e-y_e),
\]

where the actual exponent-arrow diagram is endotactic, every rate is measurable, and
\[
0<\kappa\le k_e(t)\le K<\infty.
\]

Its central claim is that every positive stoichiometric compatibility class contains one compact convex, forward-invariant absorbing set shared by all admissible rate histories. The class need not be bounded, and the time to enter the set may depend on the initial condition but not on the subsequent rates.

The proof has three essential components:
1. A finite family of affine functions whose active slopes identify the relevant asymptotic ordering of reaction monomials, even when the required directional tolerance is discontinuous.
2. A weighted essential-source comparison proving positive inward drift at the lowest relevant source tier. This is the central N33 departure from the weakly reversible proof.
3. Compact convex plateaux and a finite progression through concentration scales, producing a common absorber without assuming that successive plateaux are nested.
The underlying affine construction is attributed to OpenAI's October 5, 2026 family 149 manuscript, which states permanence for fixed-rate, weakly reversible networks.

N33 continuation — New spatial result
The next extension is a finite-molecule reaction–diffusion safety theorem. It strengthens the previous checkpoint by allowing many interacting compartments, different migration processes for different chemical species, arbitrary time-dependent transport, and feedback-dependent reaction rates.

For the three-species, six-reaction model examined previously, I derived the following bound:
\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\tau_Q\le T)
\le
6n e^{-V/20}
+\frac{2205}{44}nVT\,e^{-V/480}
}
\]

Here \(n\) is the number of compartments, \(V\) is the molecular volume-scaling parameter per compartment, and \(\tau_Q\) is the first time any compartment leaves the positive operating box \(Q=[2/5,5/2]^3\).

The bound holds under the specified reaction-rate interval \([1/2,2]\), integer \(V\ge100\), initial concentrations \((1,1,1)\) in every compartment, and balanced molecular transport with total migration rate at most \(1/4\) per species and compartment. The migration graph can change over time, and its topology need not be shared across species.

N33 Extended Permanence — Further research
Main finding: Stochastic capture from outside the safe region
The previous investigation established finite-molecule safety after a stochastic reaction system had been initialized inside a protected concentration region. The remaining question was whether N33 could also guarantee that a noisy system starting elsewhere would reach such a region.

I have derived a stronger, conditional result:

Stochastic absorption-to-metastability theorem. For a finite ordinary-endotactic chemical reaction network with integer complexes and reaction rates bounded above and away from zero, every compact positive initial concentration range admits a common stochastic capture deadline \(T_*\) and a protected polytope \(Q\) such that, for sufficiently large molecular volume \(V\),
\[
\Pr\left(
X^V(t)\in Q\quad
\text{for every }t\in[T_*,T_*+e^{aV}]
\right)
\ge 1-C(1+V)e^{-bV},
\]

for some positive constants \(a,b,C\) independent of the admissible reaction-rate history.

The derivation permits predictable, history-dependent reaction controls and does not require the molecular system to possess a stationary distribution. It is conditional on the established N33 deterministic geometry and standard stochastic counting-process estimates.

The key new mechanism is to smooth N33's finite-affine barrier. This produces a differentiable quantity with a positive stochastic generator drift outside the target region, avoiding the extremely pessimistic exponential-in-time error amplification of a direct stochastic-versus-ODE comparison.

This is a mathematical extension of the previous checkpoint, not a claim of historical priority or an experimentally validated technology.

Research decision
This is a worthwhile mathematical continuation of N33, but not yet a technological breakthrough. It provides a route from deterministic classwise permanence to high-probability molecular capture and long finite-horizon residence, including under history-dependent controls.

The decisive next step is an independent formal or adversarial proof review of the near-active-slope lemma, finite-volume generator bound and stopping-time composition. After that, the highest-value research question is whether these certificates can be constructed effectively for a genuinely coupled, non-weakly-reversible chemical network, with useful molecule-number and computational costs.

If that construction remains non-effective, the result should be retained as mathematical research capital rather than promoted as a demonstrated scientific capability.
