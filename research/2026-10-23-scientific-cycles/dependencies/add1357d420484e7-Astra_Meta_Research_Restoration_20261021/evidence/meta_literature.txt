PRIMARY EVIDENCE FOR ASTRA RESEARCH-SYSTEM METHODS
Research date: 21 October 2026. All sources below are author papers or official
research announcements. No source establishes Pro-versus-Ultra superiority,
autonomous scientific discovery rates for Astra, or exponential progress.

1. ARCHITECTURE MUST MATCH THE WORK
Kim et al., Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems, arXiv:2512.08296v3,
8 April 2026. https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3
Retrieved reference: turn118view0; limitations turn123view0.
The updated paper evaluates 260 configurations over six benchmarks. Relative
multi-agent changes span +80.8% on decomposable financial reasoning to -70% on
sequential planning. Capability saturation survives more robustness checks than
several coordination correlations. Coding/terminal subsets contain only 20 tasks,
with wide intervals; prompts were standardized rather than optimized per model.
Implication: neither maximal parallelism nor global centralization is supported.
Scientific construction with serial dependencies and independent evidence search
should not automatically have the same subagent topology. These benchmarks do
not test three autonomous scientists choosing distinct research questions.

2. LONG CONVERSATION IS NOT EQUIVALENT TO USEFUL CONTINUITY
Laban et al., LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation, arXiv:2505.06120.
https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06120v1
Retrieved references: turn121view0, turn123view1.
Across over 200,000 simulated conversations, gradual revelation of instructions
produced a reported average 39% degradation over six tasks. Recapping instructions
partially helped but did not recover fully specified performance. This compares
sharded task specification with complete instructions; it is NOT an experiment
comparing a warm scientific owner with a fresh agent given an exact state seed.
The implication is to test premise consolidation and correction explicitly,
not adopt a universal reset rule. A warm owner may retain essential derivations
that a compressed seed loses; a stale owner may retain abandoned premises.

3. KNOWLEDGE CAN CHANGE SUBSEQUENT ACTIONS, BUT MORE MEMORY IS NOT ALWAYS BETTER
Wang et al., Agent Workflow Memory, ICML 2025, PMLR 267:63897-63911.
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wang25bx.html
Full original preprint: https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429v1
Retrieved references: turn118view2, turn123view3, turn124view2.
Induced reusable workflows improved WebArena task success from a published
BrowserGym baseline of 23.5% to 35.5%; a matched accessibility-tree baseline was
15.0%. Cross-template tests also improved. Mind2Web's headline improvement is
step-wise success, not necessarily whole-task completion. Online memory induction
depends on an LM judging prior success. Appendix C's combined offline+online
memory did not dominate either alone. These are web actions, not scientific
theorems. For Astra, assess actual downstream use and selective applicability;
archive growth or more context alone supplies no measured benefit.

4. SELF-IMPROVEMENT NEEDS AN OPERATION THAT MODIFIES FUTURE SEARCH
Zhang et al., Darwin Godel Machine, arXiv:2505.22954v3.
https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954
Retrieved references: turn118view3, turn119view0.
Agent code changes with frozen foundation models improved reported SWE-bench
performance from 20% to 50% and Polyglot from 14.2% to 30.7%. The archive supports
branching from earlier variants; ablations remove self-improvement or open-ended
exploration. Estimated SWE-bench costs are about USD22,000 per DGM run versus
USD10,000 per ablation. This supports testing cumulative search-procedure changes,
but is not a cost-matched proof of indefinite or exponential improvement. Coding
ability aligns unusually well with the skill of modifying a coding agent.

5. A MORE DIRECT TEST OF IMPROVING THE ABILITY TO IMPROVE
Zhang et al., HyperAgents, arXiv:2603.19461.
https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19461v1
Retrieved references: turn121view2, turn122view1, turn122view2.
Evolved meta-agents were frozen and transferred to generating math-grading agents
under a fixed modification budget. Reported transfer imp@50 was 0.63 versus near
zero initially. The initial task score was zero partly because required formatting
and parsing were absent. After 200 iterations, transferred versus fresh systems
scored 0.64 versus 0.61, a nonsignificant difference. The appendix's stated total
token arithmetic is inconsistent: 33M + 0.506M*100 equals 83.6M, not 88.6M.
Useful experimental idea: test whether an acquired improvement procedure produces
better descendants on unseen tasks. This paper does not establish exponential
scientific progress or justify importing its large infrastructure into Astra.

6. SCIENTIFIC RESULTS CAN FEED BACK INTO THE RESOURCE BASE
Google DeepMind, AlphaEvolve announcement, 14 May 2025.
https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/
Retrieved references: turn121view1, turn123view2.
The authors report deployed scheduling gains recovering 0.7% of worldwide Google
compute and a 23% training-kernel speedup translating to 1% overall Gemini training
time reduction. Candidate programs are evaluated automatically and retained for
later search. These are producer-reported deployment outcomes with access to
large proprietary workloads; they are not independently reproduced Astra gains.
The transferable principle is that a discovered improvement should change the
actual resources or operations used by subsequent research. A favorable surrogate
score without adoption or whole-system effect does not close this feedback loop.

SYNTHESIS / AN UNCONVENTIONAL MECHANISM WORTH TESTING
The following is our inference and proposed experimental mechanism, not an
established result of the cited papers.

Compile scientific experience into conditional transformations, including their
failure boundaries, rather than accumulating instructions or result summaries.
An entry should say what operation can be performed, its assumptions, a compact
proof or executable witness, and an explicit nearby counterexample where it
cannot be used. This creates material that a scientist can compose while choosing
their own question. It may also prevent a locally valid exclusion from suppressing
an entire research direction, which matters in Astra's recent history.

The controlled intervention is not simply giving an agent more information.
Compare matched receivers given (a) chronological source evidence, (b) the same
facts compressed into a conventional summary, and (c) conditional operation plus
counterexample. Use held-out continuations that require a genuine new application
and adversarial assumption changes. Evaluate proof/result correctness, rejected
false transfers, total construction/retrieval/integration cost, and independent
downstream utility. Conceal expected answers and keep retrieval permissions equal.
Include seed-construction cost: a shorter receiving run can still lose overall.

A second-generation test is essential. Let receivers improve the operation from
one completed transfer, freeze that improvement, and test on new continuations.
Success only at the original case or after answer leakage rejects the compounding
hypothesis. A single round with few synthetic cases is a feasibility check, not
evidence of higher scientific discovery rate. Keep warm owners for continuity;
fresh receivers are a specific experimental treatment or independent challenge,
not a mandatory replacement for scientific ownership.

EVIDENCE GAPS
No reviewed paper establishes the optimal number of Astra subagents, the relative
ability of the user's Pro/Ultra/Max branches, or the value of their particular
reasoning traces. Those require local matched tests. Actual operational limits
must come from the tools exposed in the current environment, not a paper or
marketing claim. Available context contains visible messages, code and outputs;
private unexposed model reasoning is not available evidence.
