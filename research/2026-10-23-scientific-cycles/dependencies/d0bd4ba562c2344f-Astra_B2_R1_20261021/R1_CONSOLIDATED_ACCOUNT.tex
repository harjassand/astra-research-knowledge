\begin{abstract}
The supplied R1 arguments support three complementary conclusions. First,
trace-norm recurrence of a normal bipartite memory under a faithful product
reference input permits replacement by one normal separable initializer on
the same spaces, under the unchanged device, for every finite causal horizon.
The essential extra lemma proves tightness of relative-entropy
near-minimizers without a uniform bound on their resource values. Second,
for fixed \(J\), the infimum of intrinsic relative entropy of entanglement
needed to release \(J\) exact Bell pairs with complete-memory half-trace
return error \(\delta\) is
\[
 \frac{J\ln2}{2\delta}-\ln(1/\delta)+O_J(1).
\]
A universal first-failure converse and an independently reconstructed
unflagged mixed reservoir prove this for terminal and every-use return.
Third, a fixed finite graded reservoir rules out increasing the general
resource exponent above \(1/2\). These are internal analytic conclusions,
not external certification or established priority. The logarithmic saving
concerns intrinsic \(\ER\); the exhibited preparation has no demonstrated
corresponding saving in Bell pairs consumed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and error conventions}\label{sec:model}
The external input ports \(A,B\) and output ports \(A',B'\) are
finite dimensional. Retained memories \(C_A,C_B\) are separable Hilbert
spaces. Memory states are normal trace-class density operators on
\(C_A\otimes C_B\). Every shared register retained between calls belongs
to this memory. All branches of every instrument are included in the
average process; successful outcomes are not selected for resource accounting.

The device
\[
 \Lambda:AB C_A C_B\longrightarrow A'B'C_A C_B
\]
is fixed, normal, completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP), and has a
finite-round, finite-outcome LOCC implementation. No fresh entanglement is
introduced after initialization. Free local ancillas and classical communication
are allowed. A strictly positive product input
\(\eta=\eta_A\otimes\eta_B\) defines the memory channel
\[
 \Phi(\xi)=\tr_{A'B'}\Lambda(\eta\otimes\xi).
\]
The complete \(n\)-slot external causal process from \(\xi\) is \(P_n^\xi\).
This includes output correlations and responses to adaptive and
reference-entangled testers, not only the reference-input marginal states.

Let \(\mathcal F_n\) be the strategy-norm closure of finite-dimensional
causal LOCC processes with the same slot order. Normalize strategy distance
using full trace norms of final tester states. Every complete tester is a
CPTP experiment on the initializer, so
\begin{equation}
 \dd(P_n^\alpha,P_n^\beta)\leq\normone{\alpha-\beta}
 \quad\text{for every }n.                         \label{eq:contract}
\end{equation}
This estimate is uniform in the horizon.

\(\SEP\) is the trace-norm closed convex hull of normal product states.
All logarithms are natural and
\[
 \ER(\rho)=\inf_{\gamma\in\SEP}D(\rho\Vert\gamma)
\]
uses normal quantum relative entropy, possibly \(+\infty\).
This set includes separable states that need not have a finite or countable
product-state decomposition.

For any effect \(0\leq E\leq I\) and states \(\rho,\sigma\),
\[
 |\tr E(\rho-\sigma)|\leq\tfrac12\normone{\rho-\sigma}.
\]
Pinsker in nats reads \(D(p\Vert q)\geq\frac12\|p-q\|_1^2\).
These two factors of two must not be conflated. If the strategy distance is
renormalized to a half-trace distance, its constants must also be divided
accordingly. Entanglement in ebits equals \(\ER/\ln2\).

Trace-norm recurrence means
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{\substack{t\to\infty\\t\in\mathbb N}}
       \normone{\Phi^t(\tau)-\tau}=0.              \label{eq:recurrence}
\end{equation}
A small fixed positive error at one call, or at a bounded collection of calls,
does not imply this condition.

\section{Inherited machinery: tomography, resource drop and returns}
\subsection{Finite free tomography}
In a \(d\)-dimensional space use the rank-one projectors onto
\(\ket i\), \((\ket i\pm\ket j)/\sqrt2\), and
\((\ket i\pm i\ket j)/\sqrt2\), for \(i<j\).
There are \(d(2d-1)\) such projectors \(s\), their sum is
\((2d-1)I\), and they span the Hermitian operators. They give an
informationally complete POVM after division by \(2d-1\).
For a faithful state \(\eta\), the preparations
\[
 \frac{\eta^{1/2}s\eta^{1/2}}{\tr(\eta s)}
 \quad\text{with probability}\quad
 \frac{\tr(\eta s)}{2d-1}
\]
span the same space and average to \(\eta\).

At each slot prepare these ensembles independently on both local input
ports, keep the labels, apply \(\Lambda\), and measure the outputs with
local informationally complete POVMs. This is a finite free instrument.
Its transcript law is \(p_n^\xi\); forgetting all labels and outputs leaves
\(\Phi^n(\xi)\). Conditional memory states from separable initializers
remain separable.

Products of spanning preparations and output effects span the tensor
space of finite process Choi operators. Consequently equality of these
transcript probabilities is equality of the entire causal process.
Finite-dimensional norm equivalence gives a finite constant \(K_n\) such that
\begin{equation}
 \dd(P,Q)\leq K_n\|p_n(P)-p_n(Q)\|_1.             \label{eq:IC}
\end{equation}
No efficient tomography scheme, useful growth bound on \(K_n\), or uniform
inverse estimate in \(n\) is asserted.

\subsection{The finite resource-drop inequality}
Let a finite free instrument have conditional outputs \(p_y\rho_y\),
average memory channel \(\Psi\), and separable alternative outputs
\(q_y\gamma_y\). For \(\ER(\rho)<\infty\), choose
\(D(\rho\Vert\gamma)\leq\ER(\rho)+\zeta<\infty\).
Relative-entropy data processing, the classical-quantum direct-sum identity,
and convexity imply
\begin{align}
 \ER(\rho)+\zeta
 &\geq D(p\Vert q)+\sum_y p_y D(\rho_y\Vert\gamma_y) \nonumber\\
 &\geq D(p\Vert q)+\sum_y p_y\ER(\rho_y)
 \geq D(p\Vert q)+\ER(\Psi(\rho)).               \label{eq:drop}
\end{align}
All subtracted resource terms below are finite. This argument never
subtracts two infinite entropies.

\subsection{Why a separable initializer gives LOCC closure}
A normal separable initializer can be approximated in trace norm by a
finite mixture of product states with finite local supports. Such a
mixture has a finite LOCC preparation. Equation~\eqref{eq:contract}
controls its entire subsequent process error.

For a fixed finite protocol tree and finite horizon, truncate retained
memory after each local instrument. On a finite-dimensional input support,
each normal CP branch has a positive trace-class Choi operator. Choose
a finite-rank local output projection whose Choi tail is small, compress
to it, and reset the discarded local tail to a fixed local state. This is
a local CPTP postprocessing; it keeps every branch and branch trace.
Gentle compression gives an error tending to zero with the Choi tail
(a conservative bound is proportional to \(2\sqrt e+e\)).
On a finite input space, Choi trace-norm convergence implies diamond-norm
convergence, with a finite dimension factor.

Choose these truncations successively on the finite input supports produced
by earlier truncations. A hybrid argument sums their diamond-norm errors
over the finitely many nodes. Finite direct sums combine the finitely many
supports and history labels. Thus the approximating process is
finite-dimensional causal LOCC, and its strategy error tends to zero.
It follows that \(P_n^\gamma\in\mathcal F_n\) for every normal
\(\gamma\in\SEP\). This does not identify separable operations with LOCC.

\begin{theorem}[Finite separated-return bound]\label{thm:finite}
Fix a normal \(\tau\), a horizon \(n\), and a normal approximant \(\sigma\)
with \(\normone{\sigma-\tau}\leq\varepsilon\) and
\(R=\ER(\sigma)<\infty\). Suppose \(J\) integer times
\(t_1<\cdots<t_J\) satisfy
\[
 t_{j+1}\geq t_j+n,\qquad
 \normone{\Phi^{t_j}(\tau)-\tau}\leq\varepsilon.
\]
If \(d=\dd(P_n^\tau,\mathcal F_n)\), then
\begin{equation}
 d\leq 2\varepsilon+K_n\sqrt{2R/J}.             \label{eq:finite}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write \(\sigma_t=\Phi^t(\sigma)\) and \(r_t=\ER(\sigma_t)\).
The numbers \(r_t\) are finite, nonnegative and nonincreasing.
Contractivity gives \(\normone{\sigma_{t_j}-\tau}\leq2\varepsilon\).
Apply \eqref{eq:drop} to the \(n\)-slot tomography instrument at
\(\sigma_{t_j}\), using a separable near-minimizer. Its external process
belongs to \(\mathcal F_n\), so \eqref{eq:IC} and Pinsker give, after
letting its slack decrease to zero,
\[
 \frac{(d-2\varepsilon)_+^2}{2K_n^2}
       \leq r_{t_j}-r_{t_j+n}.
\]
The windows are disjoint, and the sum of these resource drops is at most
\(r_0=R\). This proves \eqref{eq:finite}.
\end{proof}
If \(d>2\varepsilon\), there are at most
\(2RK_n^2/(d-2\varepsilon)^2\) such separated returns. The resource is
that of the specified approximant, not necessarily \(\ER(\tau)\).

Every normal \(\tau\) has locally finite-support approximants with finite
\(\ER\): compress onto a sufficiently large local product projection and
normalize; a product maximally mixed comparator on that support gives
finite relative entropy. Under \eqref{eq:recurrence}, arbitrarily many
separated returns exist at each positive tolerance. Letting \(J\to\infty\)
and then \(\varepsilon\to0\) in \eqref{eq:finite} gives
\[
 P_n^\tau\in\mathcal F_n\quad\text{for every fixed finite }n,
\]
even when \(\ER(\tau)=\infty\).

\section{Exact initialization: preventing mass escape}\label{sec:exact}
Per-horizon closure alone does not supply one normal initializer. The
following lemma supplies the missing compactness for the specific
near-minimizers used in the proof.

\begin{lemma}[Tightness of separable near-minimizers]\label{lem:tight}
Suppose \(\rho_k\to\rho\) in trace norm, each \(\ER(\rho_k)<\infty\), and
normal \(\gamma_k\in\SEP\) obey
\[
 D(\rho_k\Vert\gamma_k)\leq\ER(\rho_k)+\zeta_k<\infty,
 \qquad \zeta_k\to0.
\]
Then \((\gamma_k)\) is relatively compact in trace norm. No uniform bound
on \(\ER(\rho_k)\) is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First fix one pair \(\rho,\gamma\) with the displayed near-optimality
condition and slack \(\zeta\). Choose finite-rank local projections \(P_A,P_B\)
and form all four product sectors
\[
 Q_{ab}=P_A^{(a)}\otimes P_B^{(b)},\quad
 P_A^{(0)}=P_A,\quad P_A^{(1)}=I-P_A,
 \qquad a,b\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Use analogous notation for Bob, index the four sectors by \(i\), and put
\[
 \Delta(X)=\sum_iQ_iXQ_i,\quad p_i=\tr Q_i\rho,\quad q_i=\tr Q_i\gamma.
\]
For \(p_i>0\), finiteness of \(D(\rho\Vert\gamma)\) implies \(q_i>0\).
Define \(\rho_i=Q_i\rho Q_i/p_i\) and
\(\gamma_i=Q_i\gamma Q_i/q_i\) there. Each \(\gamma_i\) is normal
separable because the filtering is local and continuous on the separable
cone. No countable product decomposition is needed.

Data processing and disintegration give
\begin{equation}
 D(\rho\Vert\gamma)\geq D(p\Vert q)+T,\qquad
 T=\sum_{i:p_i>0}p_iD(\rho_i\Vert\gamma_i)<\infty. \label{eq:T}
\end{equation}
Reweight those same blocks:
\[
 \widehat\gamma=\sum_{i:p_i>0}p_i\gamma_i\in\SEP.
\]
For any finite pinching and a block-diagonal state \(\omega\),
\begin{equation}
 D(\rho\Vert\omega)
   =D(\rho\Vert\Delta\rho)+D(\Delta\rho\Vert\omega). \label{eq:pinch}
\end{equation}
This identity is valid without finite von Neumann entropies. Write the
pinching as a finite phase twirl and apply Donald's identity to the
twirled ensemble; unitary invariance makes its terms equal.
The extended positive trace-class version of Donald's identity is stated
explicitly in \cite[Eq.~(2.19)]{Shirokov}. Both terms on the right are
nonnegative; no entropy difference is being defined.

Moreover \(D(\rho\Vert\Delta\rho)\leq H(p)\). To see this, purify \(\rho\),
project the purification into the four orthogonal sectors, and compare
the original pure state to their mixture. The relative entropy is exactly
\(H(p)\). Partial trace and data processing give the claim.
Thus \eqref{eq:pinch} with \(\omega=\widehat\gamma\) gives
\[
 D(\rho\Vert\widehat\gamma)=D(\rho\Vert\Delta\rho)+T<\infty.
\]
Combining admissibility of \(\widehat\gamma\), near-optimality, and
\eqref{eq:T}, and canceling the \emph{already finite} \(T\), yields
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{D(p\Vert q)\leq H(p)+\zeta.}             \label{eq:noescape}
\end{equation}

Set \(P=P_A\otimes P_B\) and \(u=1-\tr(P\rho)\). The other three
sectors have total mass \(u\), so \(H(p)\leq\hbin(u)+u\ln3\).
Classical Pinsker and the event-probability bound give
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 1-\tr(P\gamma)\leq
 u+\sqrt{\frac{\hbin(u)+u\ln3+\zeta}{2}}.}        \label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
The bound depends on a tail probability and a near-optimality slack;
it is independent of the entanglement value.

Take increasing finite-rank local exhaustions \(P_{A,m},P_{B,m}\) and
\(P_m=P_{A,m}\otimes P_{B,m}\). For fixed \(m\),
\(1-\tr(P_m\rho_k)\to1-\tr(P_m\rho)\); normality makes the latter tend
to zero as \(m\to\infty\). Equation~\eqref{eq:tail} therefore makes the
tails of \(\gamma_k\) uniformly small for all sufficiently large \(k\).
The finite initial exceptional collection consists of normal states,
whose tails can also be captured by enlarging \(m\). Hence
\begin{equation}
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_k[1-\tr(P_m\gamma_k)]=0.   \label{eq:tight}
\end{equation}
Gentle compression gives
\(\normone{\omega-P\omega P}\leq2\sqrt{1-\tr(P\omega)}\).
For fixed \(m\), the compressed operators \(P_m\gamma_kP_m\) lie in a
bounded finite-dimensional set, hence in a compact set after closure.
Equation~\eqref{eq:tight} uniformly approximates the sequence by such
compact sets. It is totally bounded in trace class, which is complete.
Any convergent subsequence has a positive, trace-one, normal limit, and
the trace-norm closedness of \(\SEP\) makes that limit separable.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Using \(P_A\otimes P_B\) and its full complement as if both were local
filters would be wrong. The complement is not a product projection.
All four product sectors are needed. Likewise weak-* compactness alone
would not prevent a trace-one sequence from losing mass. The quantitative
tail estimate is what closes that gap.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[One exact common initializer]\label{thm:exact}
In the model of Section~\ref{sec:model}, if a normal \(\tau\) satisfies
\eqref{eq:recurrence}, there is one normal
\(\gamma\in\SEP(C_A:C_B)\) such that
\begin{equation}
 P_n^\gamma=P_n^\tau\quad\text{for every finite }n\geq1              \label{eq:exact}
\end{equation}
under the same \(\Lambda\). This holds when \(\ER(\tau)=\infty\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose locally finite-support \(\sigma_k\) with
\(\normone{\sigma_k-\tau}\leq1/k\) and finite \(\ER\).
For fixed \(k\), \(r_{k,t}=\ER(\Phi^t(\sigma_k))\) is finite,
nonnegative and nonincreasing, so all sufficiently late \(t\) satisfy
\(r_{k,t}-r_{k,t+k}\leq1/k\). Choose such a late recurrence time \(t_k\)
with \(\normone{\Phi^{t_k}(\tau)-\tau}\leq1/k\).
Put \(\rho_k=\Phi^{t_k}(\sigma_k)\). Then
\[
 \normone{\rho_k-\tau}\leq2/k,\qquad
 \ER(\rho_k)-\ER(\Phi^k(\rho_k))\leq1/k.
\]
Choose a normal separable near-minimizer
\[
 D(\rho_k\Vert\gamma_k)\leq\ER(\rho_k)+1/k<\infty.
\]
For each \(n\leq k\), the resource-drop inequality for tomography gives
\(D(p_n^{\rho_k}\Vert p_n^{\gamma_k})\leq2/k\). Thus
\begin{equation}
 \dd(P_n^\tau,P_n^{\gamma_k})\leq
       \frac2k+\frac{2K_n}{\sqrt k},\qquad n\leq k.                 \label{eq:pointwise}
\end{equation}

Lemma~\ref{lem:tight} gives one subsequence
\(\gamma_{k_\ell}\to\gamma\) in trace norm, with \(\gamma\) a normal
separable state on the original spaces. This subsequence is selected once,
without reference to \(n\). For any fixed finite \(n\),
\eqref{eq:pointwise} and \eqref{eq:contract} give
\[
 \dd(P_n^\tau,P_n^\gamma)
 \leq \frac2{k_\ell}+\frac{2K_n}{\sqrt{k_\ell}}
       +\normone{\gamma_{k_\ell}-\gamma}\longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:exact} for every finite horizon.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A non-effective uniform statement]\label{cor:uniform}
The particular near-minimizer sequence above obeys
\[
 \sup_{n\geq1}\dd(P_n^{\gamma_k},P_n^\tau)\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise a subsequence has supremum error bounded below by a positive
constant. Relative compactness gives a further trace-norm convergent
subsequence, with limit \(\gamma_*\). The preceding proof makes
\(\gamma_*\) exact at every finite horizon. Equation~\eqref{eq:contract},
uniformly in \(n\), bounds that positive supremum by
\(\normone{\gamma_k-\gamma_*}\to0\), a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For finite \(\ER(\tau)\), recurrence and lower semicontinuity imply that
\(\ER(\Phi^t(\tau))\) is constant. The closest-separable-state theorem
of Lami--Shirokov \cite{LS} then provides an alternative shorter proof:
the zero drop in \eqref{eq:drop} makes a single minimizer's tomography
identical at every horizon. Their Theorem~5(c) concerns finite-resource
minimizer compactness; Proposition~18 and Remark~19 require convergence
of resource values to a finite limit. They do not directly settle the
divergent-resource sequence used here.

\textbf{Operational meaning.} The theorem replaces only the initializer.
The comparison pinchings and reweightings are proof objects, not new device
operations. It does not assert that \(\tau\) is separable, that \(\gamma\)
is stationary, or that \(\gamma\) has finite support, finite preparation
cost, or a computable description. A finite-support approximation to
\(\gamma\) alone also does not make the subsequent infinite-memory device
finite dimensional. Exact behavioral equivalence is stronger than the
earlier per-horizon closure result, but it is not an efficient simulator.

\section{Finite Bell delivery: a universal first-failure converse}
\label{sec:bell}
Let \(B=\proj{\Phi_+}\), \(\ket{\Phi_+}=(\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt2\).
The task is to deliver the exact joint state \(B^{\otimes J}\) on the first
\(J\) outputs. This pure output automatically factors from retained
memory and all other registers. Exact Bell marginals also force this
joint product; there is no exact-output correlation loophole.

Write \(a=J\ln2\), \(D=2^J\), and \(B_D=B^{\otimes J}\). Let \(T\) be
the \(J\)-call block with product reference inputs. Suppose
\[
 T(\tau)=B_D\otimes\tau',\qquad
 \tfrac12\normone{\tau'-\tau}\leq\delta.
\]
Only terminal return is assumed in the next theorem.

\begin{theorem}[First failure]\label{thm:first}
For every integer \(N\geq1\),
\begin{equation}
 \ER(\tau)\geq
 a\sum_{m=1}^N\pos{1-(m-1)\delta}-\ln N.          \label{eq:first}
\end{equation}
This applies to arbitrary normal memories in the stated model.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Define the CP trace-nonincreasing map
\[
 S(x)=\tr_O[(B_D\otimes I)T(x)(B_D\otimes I)].
\]
Exact output gives \(S(\tau)=\tau'\). Put \(s_m=\tr S^m(\tau)\).
The normal adjoint yields effects \(E_{m-1}=(S^*)^{m-1}(I)\).
Since \(\tau'-\tau\) has trace zero,
\[
 s_m-s_{m-1}=\tr E_{m-1}(\tau'-\tau)\geq-\delta.
\]
Together with \(s_1=1\), this gives
\(s_m\geq[1-(m-1)\delta]_+\).

Run the actual block \(N\) times, retaining every output, and at the end
test each block with the Bell projector. Let \(K\) be the number of initial
consecutive passes, capped at \(N\). Earlier output tests commute with
all later operations on memory, so
\(\Pr_\tau(K\geq m)=s_m\) and \(\mathbb E_\tau K=\sum_{m=1}^Ns_m\).

For any normal separable initializer \(\gamma\), the unconditioned full
output is separable across all Alice systems versus all Bob systems.
Its overlap with \(k\) Bell blocks is at most \(D^{-k}\):
the bound holds for product states by Cauchy--Schwarz and extends by
convexity and trace-norm closure. Thus the distribution \(r\) of \(K\)
from \(\gamma\) satisfies \(r_k\leq D^{-k}\).
For the distribution \(p\) from \(\tau\), relative-entropy data processing
therefore gives
\[
 D(\tau\Vert\gamma)\geq D(p\Vert r)
 \geq a\,\mathbb E_\tau K-H(p)
 \geq a\sum_{m=1}^N[1-(m-1)\delta]_+-\ln N.
\]
The last entropy bound uses \(K\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\) under \(\tau\);
\(r\) is allowed to have mass at zero. Infimize over \(\gamma\).
The argument remains valid if \(\ER(\tau)=+\infty\).
\end{proof}
The Bell measurements are analytical distinguishing measurements.
They need not be LOCC, and the proof never asserts separability of memory
after their global postselection. Only unconditioned alternative outputs
are required to be separable.

Choose \(N=\lceil1/\delta\rceil\), \(0<\delta\leq1\). The triangular sum
is at least \(1/(2\delta)+1/2\), giving
\begin{equation}
 \ER(\tau)\geq
 J\ln2\left(\frac1{2\delta}+\frac12\right)
       -\ln\lceil1/\delta\rceil.                 \label{eq:bell-lower}
\end{equation}
In the source's full-error convention \(\varepsilon=2\delta\), this is
\[
 \ER(\tau)\geq
 J\ln2\left(\frac1{\varepsilon}+\frac12\right)
       -\ln\lceil2/\varepsilon\rceil.
\]
Changing notation does not strengthen this result.

\subsection{Imperfect output and finite-resource approximants}
Let \(q=1-\tr[(B_D\otimes I)T(\tau)]\) be the \emph{whole-block}
Bell infidelity, allow arbitrary output-memory and output-output
correlations, and assume
\[
 \tfrac12\normone{\Phi^J(\tau)-\tau}\leq\delta,\qquad
 \normone{\sigma-\tau}\leq\xi,\qquad R=\ER(\sigma)<\infty.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
 R\geq a\sum_{m=1}^N
 \pos{1-q-\xi/2-(m-1)(\delta+q)}-\ln(N+1)
 \quad(N\geq1).                                 \label{eq:noisy}
\end{equation}
Indeed, \(F=\Phi^J-S\) is CP after tracing the two orthogonal output
measurement sectors; \(F(\tau)\geq0\) and \(\tr F(\tau)=q\).
For every effect \(E\),
\[
 \tr E(S(\tau)-\tau)
 =\tr E(\Phi^J(\tau)-\tau)-\tr E F(\tau)\geq-\delta-q.
\]
Thus \(s_1=1-q\) and
\(s_m\geq[1-q-(m-1)(\delta+q)]_+\).
Each success-prefix probability is one effect on the initial state,
so replacing \(\tau\) by \(\sigma\) changes it by at most \(\xi/2\),
independently of \(m\). Repeat the proof above; now \(K\) can equal zero,
so \(H(p)\leq\ln(N+1)\).

No decoupling estimate is used. If the output full trace error is at most
\(\nu\), then \(q\leq\nu/2\). Marginal error claims alone must be
converted to a complete-block claim before use. Initializer approximation
changes the survival intercept, whereas output failure changes its slope.
Equation~\eqref{eq:noisy} is valid but no matching joint optimum is proved.

\subsection{The inherited shifted flagged stock}
On \(\bigoplus_{k\geq0}(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes k}\) per party, let
\(\omega_k\) be \(k\) Bell pairs in matching length sectors.
The counted length label is locally orthogonal. A fixed local channel
releases the last qubit on positive sectors and returns \(\ket0\) on
vacuum. Two local Kraus branches separate positive length and vacuum;
no infinite-outcome measurement is required.

For \(L\geq1\), initialize
\(\tau_{J,L}^{\rm flag}=L^{-1}\sum_{k=J}^{J+L-1}\omega_k\).
With \(Z=\proj{00}\), the complete state after any number \(j\) of calls is
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_j^{\rm flag}=\frac1L\sum_{k=J}^{J+L-1}
 \left(B^{\otimes\min(k,j)}\otimes Z^{\otimes(j-k)_+}\right)_O
       \otimes\omega_{(k-j)_+}.                 \label{eq:flag-full}
\end{equation}
All depletion branches are included. For \(j\leq J\), outputs are exactly
\(B^{\otimes j}\), factor from memory, and
\[
 \normone{\tau_j^{\rm flag}-\tau_{J,L}^{\rm flag}}
      =2\min\{j/L,1\},\qquad
 \ER(\tau_{J,L}^{\rm flag})
      =\left(J+\frac{L-1}{2}\right)\ln2.
\]
The return formula also holds after depletion: the positive occupied
sectors shared by the shifted and initial stocks determine the overlap,
and all absorbed mass lies in vacuum, outside the initial support.
Local flags make the resource the average sector entanglement; measuring
the flags proves the lower bound, and block-diagonal separable comparators
prove the upper bound. More generally
\[
 \ER(\tau_j^{\rm flag})=\frac{\ln2}{L}
           \sum_{k=J}^{J+L-1}(k-j)_+.
\]
For useful calls it decreases by exactly one ebit per call.

Choosing \(L=\lceil J/\delta\rceil\) meets every-use return and gives the
leading \(J\ln2/(2\delta)\) term. The occupied invariant local support has
dimension \(2^{J+L}-1\). An explicit preparation consumes \(k\) Bell pairs
on branch \(k\): average \(J+(L-1)/2\), maximum \(J+L-1\).
These are counted, not declared free.

\section{The logarithmic law: an independently reconstructed upper bound}
\label{sec:mixed}
Define the separable padding cell
\[
 C=\tfrac12(\proj{00}+\proj{11})=\tfrac12(B_++B_-),\qquad B_+=B.
\]
Set \(M=J+L-1\), and on \(M\) cells define
\begin{equation}
 \omega_k^{(M)}=B^{\otimes k}\otimes C^{\otimes(M-k)},\qquad
 \tau_{J,L}=\frac1L\sum_{k=J}^{M}\omega_k^{(M)}. \label{eq:mix}
\end{equation}
There is no retained label identifying \(k\). The different terms are
mixed in the same physical registers. Retaining a locally readable shared
\(k\) label would change the resource accounting and erase the saving below.

\subsection{One fixed finite-outcome normal device}
Fix once and for all
\[
 C_A=C_B=\bigoplus_{M\geq1}(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes M}.
\]
The local sector label \(M\) is counted memory. For a bit \(c\in\{0,1\}\),
the direct-sum map
\[
 V_c\ket{M;x_1\cdots x_M}
    =\ket{x_1}_O\otimes\ket{M;x_2\cdots x_Mc}_C
\]
is an isometry: it preserves orthogonality within and between sectors.
Alice samples \(c\) fairly, sends it to Bob, and both apply \(V_c\).
The averaged memory-output channel is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda_*(\xi)=\tfrac12\sum_{c=0}^1
 (V_c^A\otimes V_c^B)\xi(V_c^A\otimes V_c^B)^*. \label{eq:fixed-shift}
\end{equation}
It is normal and CPTP, with one communication round and two outcomes.
The external input is ignored. No measurement of the infinitely many
sectors is made. Both initial \(M\) labels have the same deterministic
value, so they add no entanglement. Every initializer has finite support,
and the same channel works for all \(J,L\).

Operationally, each call releases the first cell, shifts the rest, and
appends \(C\). The newly shared classical bit supplies no entanglement;
the appended cell and any actually retained records are counted.
The preparation label \(k\) in \eqref{eq:mix} has been discarded before
initialization, not silently kept outside the memory boundary.

\subsection{Complete outputs and every-use return}
For \(0\leq j\leq J\),
\[
 \Omega_j=B^{\otimes j}\otimes\tau_j,\qquad
 \tau_j=\frac1L\sum_{k=J}^{M}\omega_{k-j}^{(M)}.
\]
Cancellation of overlapping terms in the two mixtures, followed by the
triangle inequality, gives
\begin{equation}
 \tfrac12\normone{\tau_j-\tau_{J,L}}\leq\min\{j/L,1\}.
                                                        \label{eq:all-return}
\end{equation}
Thus \(L=\lceil J/\delta\rceil\) guarantees return within \(\delta\)
after every useful call.

For any \(j\geq0\), including exhaustion, the entire state is
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_j=\frac1L\sum_{k=J}^{M}
 \left(B^{\otimes\min(k,j)}\otimes C^{\otimes(j-k)_+}\right)_O
       \otimes\omega_{(k-j)_+}^{(M)}.             \label{eq:mix-full}
\end{equation}
Exhausted outputs are \(C\). Correlations after depletion are retained in
this mixture; replacing it by independent one-output marginals would be
incorrect. Because inputs are ignored, the formula specifies the full
external replacement process against arbitrary adaptive/reference testers.

\subsection{Exact relative entropy and the bounded conditional uncertainty}
For a finite maximally correlated state
\(\rho=\sum_{x,y}a_{xy}\ket{xx}\bra{yy}\),
\begin{equation}
 \ER(\rho)=S(\rho_A)-S(\rho).                    \label{eq:MC}
\end{equation}
A direct proof suffices here. The computational diagonal is separable and
gives this upper bound. Every separable \(\sigma\) satisfies
\(\sigma\leq\sigma_A\otimes I_B\), by checking product states and convex
mixtures. Operator monotonicity of logarithm therefore gives
\[
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)\geq
 -S(\rho)-\tr\rho_A\ln\sigma_A
 =S(\rho_A)-S(\rho)+D(\rho_A\Vert\sigma_A).
\]
Regularize singular comparators and take the limit. This proves the lower
bound. The formula is established machinery \cite{ChenYang,Rains},
not a new additivity principle for arbitrary mixed states.
Local isometric embedding into the countable direct sum leaves \(\ER\)
unchanged, by applying a local recovery channel to the occupied sector.

In \eqref{eq:mix} all components commute in the product Bell-sign basis,
and the local marginal is \(I/2^M\).
Let \(K\) be uniform on \(\{J,\ldots,M\}\), and let \(Y\) be the Bell-sign
string: its first \(K\) entries are plus and the rest are fair independent
signs. Then \(S(\tau_{J,L})=H(Y)\), and the entropy chain rule gives
\[
 H(Y)=\ln L+(M-\mathbb E K)\ln2-H(K\mid Y).
\]
Consequently, with \(h_L=H(K\mid Y)\),
\begin{equation}
 \ER(\tau_{J,L})
  =\left(J+\frac{L-1}{2}\right)\ln2-\ln L+h_L.    \label{eq:mix-resource}
\end{equation}

To bound \(h_L\), let \(T_Y\) be the number of initial plus signs, set to
\(M\) for the all-plus string. For a string with \(T_Y=t\), its
likelihood given \(K=k\) is \(2^{-(M-k)}\) when \(J\leq k\leq t\)
and zero otherwise. Thus the posterior depends only on \(t\), and
\[
 \Pr(K=k\mid Y)=\frac{2^k}{\sum_{\ell=J}^t2^\ell}.
\]
The variable \(Z=t-K\) is a geometric law proportional to \(2^{-z}\),
truncated to \(0\leq z\leq t-J\). Its mean is at most one, and its
normalization is at most two. Therefore
\[
 H(K\mid Y=y)
 =\ln\sum_{z=0}^{t-J}2^{-z}+\ln2\,\mathbb E[Z\mid Y=y]
 \leq2\ln2,
\]
and
\begin{equation}
 0\leq h_L\leq2\ln2.                            \label{eq:hbound}
\end{equation}
Mixing without a retained stock label contributes almost \(\ln L\)
of joint entropy while leaving the local marginal unchanged. That is the
mathematical origin of the logarithmic term.

\begin{theorem}[Two-term exact-Bell law]\label{thm:log}
Let \(R_{\rm term}(J,\delta)\) and \(R_{\rm all}(J,\delta)\) be the infima
of initial intrinsic \(\ER\) among admitted devices and initializers that
deliver \(J\) exact Bell outputs with, respectively, terminal or every-use
complete-memory half-trace return error at most \(\delta\).
For fixed \(J\geq1\), both satisfy
\begin{equation}
 R_{\rm term}(J,\delta)
 =\frac{J\ln2}{2\delta}-\ln(1/\delta)+O_J(1),
 \quad
 R_{\rm all}(J,\delta)
 =\frac{J\ln2}{2\delta}-\ln(1/\delta)+O_J(1).      \label{eq:loglaw}
\end{equation}
The lower bound is universal. One specified fixed device
\eqref{eq:fixed-shift} realizes the upper asymptotic for the whole family.
The statement is not an upper bound for every arbitrarily prescribed device.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For \(0<\delta\leq1\), \eqref{eq:bell-lower} gives
\[
 R_{\rm term}\geq
 \frac{J\ln2}{2\delta}-\ln(1/\delta)
       +\frac{J\ln2}{2}-\ln2.
\]
Take \(L=\lceil J/\delta\rceil\) in \eqref{eq:mix}. Equations
\eqref{eq:all-return}, \eqref{eq:mix-resource} and \eqref{eq:hbound} give
\[
 R_{\rm all}\leq
 \frac{J\ln2}{2\delta}-\ln(1/\delta)
       +J\ln2+2\ln2-\ln J.
\]
Since \(R_{\rm term}\leq R_{\rm all}\), these two bounds prove the claim.
\end{proof}
The two infima need not agree at finite error. The proof says they have
the same two asymptotic terms. It is for fixed \(J\); it does not establish
uniform remainder estimates as \(J\) also grows. In full norm it reads
\(E_{R,\min}(J,\varepsilon)
=J\ln2/\varepsilon-\ln(1/\varepsilon)+O_J(1)\);
the extra \(-\ln2\) from conversion is absorbed in the bounded term.

\subsection{The complete preparation ledger}
One explicit preparation samples \(K\), supplies \(K\) Bell pairs and
\(M-K\) free \(C\) cells, and discards the \(K\) record. Its expected
Bell-pair consumption is \(J+(L-1)/2\), its maximum is \(M\), and its
intrinsic entanglement loss at discarding is \(\ln L-h_L\).
If the record remains usable, it belongs to memory and the flagged
resource equals the average \(K\ln2\). Classical-information loss in
entanglement accounting is already an established phenomenon \cite{HV}.

Thus \eqref{eq:loglaw} is a law for intrinsic \(\ER\), not a theorem
identifying exact one-shot Bell formation cost with \(\ER\). This
preparation demonstrates no logarithmic reduction in Bell pairs spent.
For \(j\leq J\), the resource decreases exactly as
\[
 \ER(\tau_j)=\ER(\tau_{J,L})-j\ln2.
\]
Every useful call consumes one ebit. At \(j=M\) the memory is \(C^{\otimes M}\);
it never recurs to the entangled initial state. Increasing occupied support
as \(\delta\to0\) is essential. A fixed finite local dimension could not
support the asymptotic divergence.

\section{Weak outputs and the sharp general resource exponent}
\label{sec:graded}
The Bell task has fixed strong pure outputs. Its sharper converse cannot
be promoted to a uniformly larger resource exponent for all output strengths.
The following finite example explains why.

For \(0\leq x\leq\pi/2\), put
\[
 \ket{p_x}=\cos(x/2)\ket{00}+\sin(x/2)\ket{11},\quad
 p_x=\proj{p_x},
\]
\[
 q_x=\tfrac12\bigl(\proj{00}+\proj{11}
   +\sin x(\ket{00}\bra{11}+\ket{11}\bra{00})\bigr).
\]
Their exact full trace distance to separable states is \(\sin x\).
Dephasing gives the upper bound. For the lower bound use
\[
 W=\ket{00}\bra{11}+\ket{11}\bra{00}
                -\proj{01}-\proj{10}.
\]
It has operator norm one and nonpositive expectation in every product
state, by \(2|uv|\leq|u|^2+|v|^2\), hence in every separable state.
Its expectation on either cell is \(\sin x\).

Fix positive integers \(J,L\), set \(M=J+L-1\), and let
\[
 f_i=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&1\leq i\leq J,\\
 1-r/L,&i=J+r,\quad1\leq r<L,\\
 0,&i>M.
 \end{cases}
\]
For \(r=p\) or \(q\), initialize the finite memory
\(\tau_r(\alpha)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^M r_{\alpha f_i}\).
The fixed channel releases the first cell, shifts and appends the separable
cell \(r_0\). For \(q_0=C\), this uses the same finite classical coin
operation as before; for \(p_0=\proj{00}\), local padding suffices.
The channel and finite memory dimension are fixed as \(\alpha\) varies.
For every \(j\), including exhaustion,
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_r(j)=
 \left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^j r_{\alpha f_i}\right)_O
 \otimes\left(\bigotimes_{i=1}^M r_{\alpha f_{i+j}}\right)_C.
                                                        \label{eq:graded-full}
\end{equation}
The first \(J\) outputs are exact independent copies of \(r_\alpha\);
they have no hidden output-memory or cross-output correlations.

The pure-state return formula and an elementary product bound give
\begin{align}
 \normone{\tau_{p,j}-\tau_p}
 &=2\sqrt{1-\prod_{i=1}^M
       \cos^2\!\left(\frac{\alpha(f_i-f_{i+j})}{2}\right)} \nonumber\\
 &\leq \alpha\|f-T^jf\|_{\ell^2}
 \leq \frac{\alpha j}{\sqrt L}.                 \label{eq:graded-return}
\end{align}
Here \(T\) is the forward shift of the zero-extended sequence.
The first difference \(f-Tf\) has \(L\) entries \(1/L\), so its
\(\ell^2\) norm is \(1/\sqrt L\). Telescoping and shift contractivity
give the last step.
With \(\sigma_x\) denoting the Pauli bit flip, the fixed bilateral twirl is
\[
 \mathcal T(\omega)=\tfrac12\bigl[\omega+
       (\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x)\omega(\sigma_x\otimes\sigma_x)\bigr].
\]
It maps \(p_x\) to \(q_x\) for every \(x\); contractivity therefore gives
the same return upper bound for the mixed family. In particular the
denominator in \eqref{eq:graded-return} is \(\sqrt L\), not \(L\).
The extracted transcript obscures this square root.

By the maximally correlated identity \eqref{eq:MC}, applied also to the
whole product state,
\begin{align*}
 R_q&=\sum_i\left[\ln2-
          \hbin\!\left(\frac{1+\sin(\alpha f_i)}2\right)\right]
             =\Theta(\alpha^2),\\
 R_p&=\sum_i\hbin\!\left(\sin^2(\alpha f_i/2)\right)
             =\Theta(\alpha^2\ln(1/\alpha))
\end{align*}
as \(\alpha\downarrow0\), with \(J,L\) fixed.
No general additivity of mixed-state \(\ER\) has been assumed.
The mixed stock can be prepared by twirling the pure stock, losing
\(R_p-R_q\); these entropic resources still must not be confused with
integer one-shot Bell formation costs.

\begin{proposition}[No uniformly larger resource power]
For every \(\beta>1/2\), an inequality
\[
 d\leq2\varepsilon+B_0(R/J)^\beta
\]
cannot hold with finite \(B_0\) uniform over initializers, even for one
fixed finite device and the one-slot horizon in the stated model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix \(L>4J^2\). The replacement-output process has full strategy distance
\(d=\sin\alpha\) to LOCC closure: a free input witnesses the distance to
\(\SEP\), and the closest separable replacement channel attains it.
Take \(\varepsilon=\alpha J/\sqrt L\). All positive times \(1,\ldots,J\)
are admitted returns by \eqref{eq:graded-return}, and the finite-resource
approximant can be the initializer itself. Then
\[
 d-2\varepsilon
   =\alpha(1-2J/\sqrt L)+O(\alpha^3)=\Theta(\alpha)>0.
\]
For the mixed stock \((R_q/J)^\beta=o(\alpha)\).
Even using the full pure resource,
\[
 (R_p/J)^\beta=O(\alpha^{2\beta}|\ln\alpha|^\beta)=o(\alpha).
\]
Letting \(\alpha\to0\) contradicts the proposed inequality.
\end{proof}
This proves sharpness of the resource exponent only. It does not give an
optimal joint dependence on horizon, output strength or return error.
Maintaining the displayed ratio as \(J\) grows requires \(L\) of order
\(J^2\). It does not challenge the recurrence theorem: every one of these
stocks eventually depletes.

\section{Scope, prior machinery and credible novelty boundary}
\label{sec:priority}
The bibliography and the separate literature review record primary
sources actually examined. The following boundaries are essential.

\textbf{Infinite-dimensional attainability.}
Lami--Shirokov \cite{LS} establishes attainability and lower
semicontinuity for \(\ER\), including normal infinite-dimensional
systems, through a weak-* closed free cone. It supports the inherited
finite-resource argument. The additional contribution reconstructed here
is tightness along near-minimizing sequences with possibly divergent
resource values. Its historical priority has not been established.
The extended relative-entropy identities used in its proof are inherited
from standard machinery, with the needed infinite-dimensional statement
explicit in \cite{Shirokov}.

\textbf{Batteries, mixed states and embezzlement.}
Broad entanglement stocks and batteries predate R1 \cite{Battery}.
Van Dam--Hayden embezzlement \cite{vDH} produces approximate target-plus-
catalyst states. Its fidelity and reduced-state estimates cannot be
substituted for complete bipartite-memory return and exact output.
The complete characterization in \cite{ZTG} is also sensitive to
the conversion metric and pure-state family under consideration.
These are scope distinctions, not claims that R1 dominates those tasks.
In particular the target-specific pure sine ladder in
\cite[Eq.~(9)]{LW} is prior art, not an additional R1 construction.

The nearby mixed-catalyst teleportation construction in \cite{Xing}
allows approximate outputs and uses purified-distance and dimension
criteria. It does not give the exact-output intrinsic-resource optimum here.
Fang--Liu \cite{FangLiu} preserves a catalyst marginal at every use while
allowing correlated approximate outputs and supplying fresh resource
inputs each round. That different source boundary explains its
compatibility with R1. The related-work review records the inspected
definitions and equations; neither comparison is a novelty certificate.

\textbf{Earlier resource lower bounds.}
Rubboli--Tomamichel \cite{RT} gives dimension-independent
Renyi-divergence continuity machinery and constraints on imperfect
catalysis. In the finite-dimensional exact-Bell setting its parameter
can be optimized toward one to obtain inverse-error growth already.
Writing \(a=J\ln2\) and \(s=1-\alpha\), the relevant application gives
\[
 \ER(\tau)\geq \frac1s
        \ln\frac{1-e^{-sa}}{\delta^{\,1-s}}.
\]
Taking \(s=e\delta/a\) at small \(\delta\) gives
\[
 \ER(\tau)\geq \frac{a}{e\delta}-\ln(1/\delta)+O_J(1).
\]
Thus the comparison coefficient \(2/e\), relative to the present leading
\(a/(2\delta)\), is supportable as this particular application of prior
machinery. It is not the literal main theorem of that paper, a claim
about the strongest prior bound anywhere, or a separately justified
infinite-dimensional extension. The first-failure proof above works
directly for normal memory and needs no such extension.

\textbf{Different local algebras.}
Perfect or universal embezzlement in commuting-operator or type-III
settings \cite{CLP,vLSWW} does not contradict these conclusions.
The normal type-I tensor-product memory assumption is a substantive
boundary. This report does not change local algebras, allow singular
states, or treat a growing family of approximate catalysts as recurrence
of one normal state under one fixed device.

\textbf{What can credibly be presented as R1's distinctive content.}
Subject to a fuller priority review, the candidates are the
same-device all-horizon exact-initializer result via the no-escape lemma;
the direct first-failure bound, including its complete-block
infidelity and approximant form; the matching two-term intrinsic
resource law with a correctly counted unflagged construction; and the
fixed-device weak-output exponent obstruction. They should be presented
as one linked account, with inherited ingredients clearly cited.
The inverse-error order alone, the maximally correlated resource formula,
and the general idea of entanglement batteries are not new claims here.

\section{Consequences and the next allocation decision}
The strongest qualitative conclusion is an exact operational exclusion:
under the stated recurrence condition, normal memory entanglement is
unnecessary for the device's entire externally observable causal behavior.
It may remain in internal degrees of freedom, but one separable
initializer reproduces the same behavior under the same device.
This also rules out recurrent exact Bell production from free inputs
even when the initial \(\ER\) is infinite; the numerical resource lower
bound alone would be vacuous in that case.

The quantitative result shows how finite consumption coexists with a small
return disturbance. For exact Bell delivery the intrinsic initial
resource must grow like \(J/(2\delta)\) ebits, with the determined
logarithmic correction in nats. The unflagged construction saturates these
two terms and pays explicitly for every produced Bell pair.
Small trace disturbance is therefore not evidence of negligible resource
consumption or indefinite reuse.

The graded construction explains the remaining distinction: weak nonlocal
outputs can have strength of order \(\alpha\) with entropic resource
of order \(\alpha^2\), preventing a universally larger power in the general
return theorem. It does not turn weak output into the fixed strong Bell task.

The genuinely unresolved operation in this packet is approximate
\emph{complete-stream} delivery with a preparation budget, finite occupied
memory, all-use return and explicit treatment of output correlations.
For example, one can define a joint optimum at specified whole-block
infidelity \(q\), return \(\delta\), and horizon \(J\). Bound
\eqref{eq:noisy} does not determine that optimum, and the intrinsic
\(\ER\) law does not determine operational formation cost.
These are unresolved by this assessment; no global open-problem or
priority claim is made about them.

\textbf{Allocation judgment.} No additional active foundational
investigation is justified by the current handoff alone. Preserve the
mathematical result and release the lane. Reopening would require a
specified quantum operation for which the unresolved noisy-output or
formation-cost question changes an accepted conclusion. Such a bounded
operation can be mathematically important without an industrial story.
No source recovery, relay, review coordination or integration task is
assigned to the user.
