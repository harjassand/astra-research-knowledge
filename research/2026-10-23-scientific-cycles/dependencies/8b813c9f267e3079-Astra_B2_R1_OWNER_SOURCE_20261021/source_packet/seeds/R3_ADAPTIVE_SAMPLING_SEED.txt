R3 — ADAPTIVE GAUSSIAN CONDITIONAL ACQUISITION
Self-contained constructive seed, 21 October 2026

STATUS AND PROVENANCE
The supplied cross-Astra report describes a fixed-target conditional sampler
and claims sharp asymptotic raw acquisition 1+r^2 per output. The full original
Gaussian proof and prototype were not available in this review; the transcript
is included in sources/05_cross_astra_discovery.md. Do not treat its reported
sharp sequential theorem as independently replayed here.

The adaptive remaining-information construction below was developed during
this allocation review. It is a route for the next investigator to prove or
correct, not a completed adaptive theorem. Its fixed-design matrix algebra is
closely related to classical Gaussian experiment comparison and is not claimed
as new. Historical novelty and practical advantage are UNKNOWN.

1. EXACT INPUT/OUTPUT PROBLEM
Receive iid raw pairs (X_i,Y_i) from one joint multivariate Gaussian law with
unknown mean and covariance. Let Y have dimension d and nonsingular covariance.
Write
 X_i = alpha + B Y_i + epsilon_i,
 epsilon_i iid N(0,Sigma_res), independent of all Y_i.
The algorithm does not know alpha, B, Sigma_res, or the marginal Y parameters.
State any extra residual nondegeneracy needed by a lower bound.

For a fixed requested target y, the desired output law is X conditional on Y=y.
For adaptive targets y_t chosen using prior released outputs and permitted
history, the desired law is the SEQUENTIAL KERNEL
 Law(Z_t | released history before t, y_t) = Law_theta(X | Y=y_t).
Adaptive outputs need not be unconditionally independent.

2. INHERITED FIXED-TARGET IDENTITY
Weights w chosen from Y only, satisfying
 sum_i w_i=1, sum_i w_i Y_i=y, sum_i w_i^2=1,
give Z=sum_i w_i X_i with exactly the desired conditional Gaussian law,
conditional on the acquired Y values. Orthogonal weight columns give independent
residuals for nonadaptive targets. Acquisition stopping may use Y, not unreleased
X residuals. This identity is prior machinery, not a novelty claim.

3. ADAPTIVE REMAINING-INFORMATION MATRIX
Define z(y)=(1,y^T)^T and let A_n have rows z(Y_i)^T. Suppose prior outputs used
orthonormal columns W, with A_n^T W=[z_1,...,z_(t-1)]. When acquiring a new raw
pair, append a zero row to every previously used weight column.

Set H=I-W W^T. The unused design matrix is
 B_(n,t)=A_n^T H A_n=A_n^T A_n-sum_(s<t) z_s z_s^T.
Acquiring a raw pair adds z(Y_i)z(Y_i)^T; releasing a target subtracts z_tz_t^T.

The new weight must satisfy
 A_n^T w_t=z_t, W^T w_t=0, ||w_t||_2=1.
If z_t is in range(B), its minimum squared norm under the first two constraints
is ell=z_t^T B^dagger z_t, with minimizer v=H A_n B^dagger z_t.
If ell<1 and there is an unused unit direction u orthogonal to both W and A_n,
then w_t=v+sqrt(1-ell)u meets all constraints. If ell=1, v already works.
Include range and null-dimension conditions; ell<=1 alone is not always enough.

The candidate online rule acquires raw pairs until these conditions hold,
constructs w_t, returns sum_i w_ti X_i, and updates W and B.

4. LOAD-BEARING FILTRATION
Decision history can contain acquired Y values, independent algorithmic
randomness, targets, and previously released projections Z_s. It must not
contain arbitrary unreleased raw X coordinates when choosing new targets,
stopping, or weight directions. Software may hold the raw X buffer but its
control logic must respect this information barrier.

The proposed proof uses adaptive Gaussian innovations: conditional on released
projections, the unused residual remains Gaussian in their orthogonal complement.
The investigator must prove this for the actual adaptive stopping/direction
rule. Pathwise orthogonality alone does not excuse residual-dependent selection.

This is not an indefinitely reusable learned generator conditional on a frozen
complete dataset. Given all raw X and Y, a deterministic weighted output is
fixed. The procedure consumes independent residual directions and data
information. New acquisitions replenish that resource.

5. CONCRETE RESOURCE-SHARING TARGET
For M=E[z(Y)z(Y)^T] and C_m=sum_(t=1)^m z_tz_t^T, examine whether acquisition
can be controlled by
 lambda_max(M^(-1/2) C_m M^(-1/2))
plus explicit finite dimension, probability-tail, and numerical overhead.
This is a candidate scale, not a proved uniform bound for arbitrary policies.

For standardized scalar Y and balanced targets +1 and -1, C_m=m I_2. This
suggests leading acquisition m, versus 2m for separately processed fixed-target
batches each paying 1+r^2=2. First verify the finite algorithm and dimension
overhead. This toy case is a diagnostic; a consequential workload is still needed.

6. NUMERICAL CONTRACT
Choose before claiming certification:
(a) ideal real samples with a refinable interval oracle; charge refinements; or
(b) fixed rounded observations of an underlying Gaussian; state resolution,
    covariance/conditioning bounds, mean/target range, and an error metric.
Exact real-valued Gaussian observations are not the same resource as rounded
recordings. Small floating constraint residuals are not exact-distribution proofs.
Total variation between a discrete floating output and a continuous Gaussian
is 1, so compare to a rounded target law or use an appropriate continuous metric.

Benchmark acquisition, arithmetic, memory, failures/inconclusive requests,
conditioning, target geometry, and output validity. Known-parameter oracle
sampling is a correctness reference, not a same-information competitor.
Compare with classical fixed-design transformations and regression/predictive
methods at matched error when approximate use is the actual application.
