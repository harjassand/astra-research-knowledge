1. The capability and the obstruction
The intended capability was to turn an inexpensive approximate molecular calculation into a trustworthy, narrow interval containing the ground-state electronic energy, without solving the full many-electron eigenproblem or assuming a known gap above its ground state.

This has a direct connection to molecular stability and reaction energetics: useful predictions often depend on differences between energies. A variational calculation supplies an upper bound, but excellent apparent convergence does not itself establish a sufficiently close lower bound.

I selected an approximately 1 millihartree-wide interval per energy as the methodological target. Two such intervals produce an approximately 2 millihartree-wide interval for their difference. This was a declared accuracy target for investigating the method, rather than a requirement recovered from a particular catalyst or industrial application. For context, the conventional chemical-accuracy scale is about 1 kcal/mol, or 1.6 millihartree; established selected configuration-interaction methods already approach this scale on substantial benchmark sets. 

There are also serious existing approaches to the lower-bound problem. Weighted sum-of-squares constructions and variational two-particle reduced-density-matrix methods provide established lower certificates; numerical verification techniques can rigorously account for errors in solving the associated optimization problems. The opportunity therefore had to be a cheaper, sufficiently tight certificate, beyond the mere existence of a lower bound. 

The particular obstruction investigated was the loss of sign information when an enormous omitted space is replaced by inexpensive absolute-value bounds. The proposed escape was to preserve the important signed interactions through an exact elimination step before applying those bounds.

6. Decision on further concentration
The investigation made three concrete things possible:

An executable signed certificate that substantially improves on direct scalar-comparison bounds in several molecular fixtures.
Exact energy intervals and a difference interval for two stored water Hamiltonians.
A proved limitation, including a connected interacting example with an exact selected ground state and an exponential retained-space requirement for the defined certificate class.
What still prevents the intended capability is equally concrete: enumeration of the omitted determinant space, loss of sign information in the inexpensive certificate, costly exact verification, and absence of a demonstrated advantage over ordinary methods.

Further concentration on this architecture is not justified by these results. In particular, more positive-weight tuning or a more accurate selected wavefunction does not address the proved obstruction. Reopening this direction would require an actual operation that certifies omitted correlations without enumerating them, together with favorable evidence against established alternatives. This batch did not supply that operation.
