# Scope of the arbitrary-series stubbed-parallel grammar

Contributor: `/root/cycle7_structural_boundary`, Cycle7, 22 October 2026.
This is a structural interface result. It is not a coloring lower bound.

## 1. The precise grammar and question

Let H be the class of two-terminal networks generated from paths of edge length
at least two by the following productions. All terminal vertices have degree
one, and different children are otherwise vertex-disjoint.

* P_b, b in {2,3}: identify the b left terminals at a and the b right terminals
  at z, then attach new outer terminal leaves s and t by edges sa and zt.
* S_l, l>=2: concatenate l children in series by identifying consecutive
  terminals. Both series parities are allowed here.

The question is whether this enlarged grammar, possibly after choosing two
attachment vertices and adding outer terminal leaves to a desired atom,
represents every chordless series-parallel atom of maximum degree four.
It does not. The obstruction below remains after arbitrary series parity is
allowed; it concerns the graph representation, independently of coloring.

No graph recognition algorithm is silently assumed. All claims below concern
exact membership in this specified grammar and actual graph supports.

## 2. Paired degree creation

**Lemma 1.** Every network generated by this grammar has an even number of
degree-three vertices and an even number of degree-four vertices.

Proof. A path has none of either. At S_l, each identified pair consists of two
degree-one terminals and becomes a vertex of degree two. No other degree changes.
At P_b, the old degree-one terminals cease to be distinct vertices, and their
left and right identifications have degree b+1 after attaching the outer stubs.
Thus P_2 creates exactly two degree-three vertices and P_3 exactly two degree-four
vertices. All earlier nonterminal vertices keep their degrees. New terminals
have degree one. Induction proves both parity statements. This argument is
independent of the lengths and parities of series productions. QED.

**Lemma 2.** If deleting the two terminal leaves s,t of a generated network
produces a two-connected graph A containing a cycle, its top production is P_b.
Writing u,v for the two neighbors of s,t, respectively, we have

    deg_A(u)=deg_A(v)=b in {2,3}.

Moreover A-{u,v} has exactly b connected components, and each attaches to u
and v by exactly one edge. The vertices u,v are nonadjacent. Finally, A has an
even number of degree-four vertices.

Proof. A path production cannot have a cyclic core. A top series production
has at least one join j. Each child has at least two edges between its distinct
terminals, so after removal of only s,t there are surviving vertices on both
sides of the join. Removing j disconnects these sides. This contradicts
two-connectivity of A. The top production is therefore P_b.

Its join vertices have degrees b+1 in the full network and b after the outer
leaves are removed. Children have degree-one terminals, hence distinct children
each contribute exactly one edge at each join. Removing the two terminal leaves
of a connected graph with distinct nonadjacent leaves leaves a nonempty connected
interior; this applies to each child. The child interiors are disjoint and have
no cross-edges, so they are exactly the b components after removing u,v. Each
child terminal distance is at least two, giving nonadjacency of u,v.

Every degree-four vertex in A is an internal degree-four vertex of a child,
because its new top join vertices have degree two or three. Each child has an
even number of degree-four vertices by Lemma 1, and its leaves are not counted.
Summing gives the final parity claim. QED.

This provides two different graph-only necessary certificates: a balanced
two-vertex separator with exact port loads, and even degree-four count in a
two-connected core. Passing these conditions is not asserted sufficient.

## 3. An unbounded family excluded by the grammar

For every integer L>=2 construct A_L as follows. There are three branch
vertices a,b,c. Join a to b by two internally vertex-disjoint paths of length
two, join a to c by two such paths, and join b to c by one path of length L.
All five paths have mutually disjoint interiors.

The graph has

    n=L+6,  m=L+8,
    deg(a)=4, deg(b)=deg(c)=3,

and every other vertex has degree two. In particular it has exactly one
degree-four vertex. A_2 is an explicit eight-vertex, ten-edge example.

**Theorem.** Each A_L is simple, triangle-free, chordless, two-connected,
series-parallel, and has maximum degree four and no clique cutset. Nevertheless
there is no choice of two vertices of A_L to which one can add the two outer
terminal leaves and obtain a network in the above grammar. Equivalently, A_L
is not a two-connected core of a generated network.

Proof of the structural assertions. Simplicity follows from the disjoint
nonempty interiors of the five paths. The graph is the subdivision of the
three-vertex multigraph with edge multiplicities ab=2, ac=2, bc=1. It also has
an explicit ordinary two-terminal series-parallel expression with terminals
a,c: put the two a-c paths in parallel with the series composition of the two
parallel a-b paths and the b-c path. Here an ordinary parallel child's terminal
degree need not equal one. This verifies the series-parallel claim directly.

An explicit width-two tree decomposition is also supplied. Use central bag
{a,b,c}; attach bags {a,b,u} for each a-b interior vertex and {a,c,u} for each
a-c interior vertex. If w_1,...,w_(L-1) are the b-c path interiors, attach the
chain of bags {b,c,w_1}, {c,w_1,w_2}, ..., {c,w_(L-2),w_(L-1)} to the central
bag. For L=2 this chain consists of its first bag alone. Every graph edge is
covered and the bags containing each vertex are connected, proving width<=2.

Every edge has a degree-two endpoint. In a simple cycle, a degree-two vertex
uses both its incident edges. Thus no edge can be a chord of a cycle. There
are no triangles: a cycle entirely between a,b or a,c has length four, while
a cycle through all three branch vertices has length L+4>=6.

Deleting an internal path vertex leaves its two remaining path segments
attached to their branch endpoints, while the other paths still connect all
three branch vertices. Deleting a branch vertex leaves the other two connected
by their existing path or paths, and the remnants of its incident paths attach
to those remaining vertices. Hence deleting any single vertex leaves a
connected graph, proving two-connectivity.

In a triangle-free two-connected chordless graph, a clique cutset could only
be an adjacent pair x,y. If such a pair separated two components, each component
would meet both x and y by two-connectivity; paths through two different
components would form a cycle for which xy is a chord. This is impossible.
Thus the graph has no clique cutset.

For exclusion, suppose adding the two outer leaves at some selected vertices
gave a generated network. Removing them recovers the two-connected cyclic
graph A_L. Lemma 2 therefore requires an even number of degree-four vertices
in A_L, contradicting its exact count one. The conclusion holds for every
selection of vertices and does not depend on a failed selected coloring. QED.

The missing interface is concrete. In the ordinary series-parallel expression,
the branch through b has terminal degree load two at a and one at c. The other
two parallel branches each have load one at each endpoint. The total loads
are four at a and three at c. A grammar insisting on unit terminal loads in
each parallel child cannot express this configuration. Coloring states alone
cannot remove that graph-representation restriction.

## 4. The smaller K_(2,4) exclusion and a useful inclusion

K_(2,4) is another excluded core, despite having an even number of degree-four
vertices. Its hubs have degree four, so cannot be the join vertices u,v in
Lemma 2, whose core degrees are at most three. Its only candidate pair consists
of two degree-two vertices. Removing any such pair leaves connected K_(2,2),
rather than the two components required at b=2. Hence the exact separator
condition gives a separate obstruction not detected by degree-four parity.

This does not exclude every four-path theta atom. In particular

    Theta(2,2,2,L),  L>=4,

is the core of

    P_2(path(L-2), P_3(path2,path2,path2)).

The top join vertices are the two neighbors of the original hubs on the long
path. They are nonadjacent because L>=4. One child is their path segment of
length L-2; the other is the three-short-path parallel construction with its
outer stubs. This exact formula also makes clear that choosing a representation's
outer terminals does not grant arbitrary prescribed colors to the original
degree-four hubs. The received equal-terminal transmission constraint remains
a genuine condition on those original vertices.

## 5. Consequence and limitations

An all-depth recovery theorem for the arbitrary-series grammar is a correct
enlargement of the owner's admitted class. It does not, by graph structure
alone, extend to all chordless degree-four partial two-tree atoms. The A_L
family proves this omission persists at arbitrarily large sizes, so excluding
a finite list of small atoms does not repair it.

These are representation obstructions. They do not prove that A_L is hard to
color, needs more than three/four queries, or defeats any real-valued design.
Indeed A_2 has a simple star-three coloring: color a,b,c by 0,1,2 and each
path-interior vertex by the third color missing at its endpoints. Every
bichromatic component is a star. This is not a historical novelty claim. The proofs are elementary
and self-contained; no external theorem is used in the exclusion.

No query or reduced-action operation is introduced here. A broader receiving
theorem must either accept non-unit and unequal terminal degree loads, provide
a different decomposition of these actual atoms, or state its grammar scope
explicitly. The operator-noise and row-energy contract is unchanged.
