CYCLE9 RECEIVER CHALLENGE — nested parity freshness and irreversible back-action
Status: complete elementary sublemmas; no universal Bell-loss lower bound.
Source: actual nu-tail queue, PROOF(1), unchanged current inaccessible clocks.
All logarithms natural. No extra entanglement or correlated access is supplied.

1. A useful nonadmissible benchmark

If a global observer were allowed nonlocal Bell-projector measurements, it
could measure {Phi_D,I-Phi_D} on sites 2,...,L sequentially from the left,
stop at the first rejection J, and retain c=max(1,J-1-g) pairs (take J=L+1
if no rejection). All true Bell sites pass without disturbance. Conditional
on the actual clock k, subsequent nu sites pass independently with probability
D^-2. Hence c>=max(1,k-g), and c>k implies at least g+1 successive tail passes,
whose probability is <=D^-2(g+1). Reset everything after c. A same-clock,
same-complete-transcript pure-prefix reference, with nstar=min(k,c), is at
joint half-trace distance at most D^-2(g+1), including all old memory.
This gives constant loss for fixed error under unrestricted global channels.
It is NOT an LOCC receiver: the missing Bell-projection operation is substantive.

For the exact Bell-projector instrument, feeding product |00> creates Phi_D
on its pass branch, of probability 1/D. Thus implementing that instrument
exactly by LOCC with an independent auxiliary requires at least (ln D)/D
initial E_F, by strong monotonicity. An exact returned independent catalyst
cannot implement it for free. On N independent product inputs the flagged
parallel instrument yields N(ln D)/D average E_F. This is a CHANNEL-UNIFORM
implementation obstruction. It does not prove a source-specific lower bound,
and does not charge the actual queue N times by assumption.

2. Alternative deterministic freshness: nested prefix sums

Consider local SUM projectors for h_j(x)=sum_(i=2)^j x_i mod D. Each threshold
j in {2,...,L} may be used at most once; the order and next threshold may be
chosen adaptively from all earlier local outcomes. Fix k for proof only.
In diagonal statistics write a_i uniform Z_D, b_i=a_i+e_i, where e_i=0 for
i<=k and e_i independent uniform for i>k. For any distinct queried thresholds:

  j<=k: the two sums agree with certainty;
  j>k: the next pair of local sums is uniform on Z_D^2, even conditional
       on the ENTIRE earlier transcript at other distinct thresholds.

Proof: selected prefix-sum rows are jointly independent over Z_D by taking
successive differences at their sorted thresholds; each new interval has
a coefficient-one pivot. Rows with j>k remain independent when restricted
to the tail coordinates k+1,...,L. The joint map from (a,e_tail) to all
Alice sums and all nontrivial difference sums is surjective, with every
fiber having the same size. A new right-of-k row therefore contributes TWO
fresh independent D-ary digits. A new left-of-k row contributes one common
uniform digit. The argument uses only addition/subtraction, so applies to
composite D. Adaptive conditioning selects a fixed earlier threshold set
and fixed earlier values, and does not change this fiber argument.

Thus disjoint colors are NOT the only valid freshness mechanism. Repeating
an already queried threshold remains invalid: its sums are fixed, not fresh.

3. Exact source-specific entanglement cost of these projectors

Fix a realized complete transcript T and k. Let S be the distinct nonempty
thresholds used. For k>=2 define

 r_k(T)= #{j in S: 2<=j<k} + 1_{there exists j in S with j>=k};

for k=1 put r_k=0. This is the number of independent restricted prefix rows
on coordinates 2,...,k; all thresholds >=k have IDENTICAL restrictions.
After the local SUM measurements, with BOTH parties' whole unmeasured
external arrays retained, the conditional state has exactly

                    E_F = (k-r_k(T)) ln D.                      (A)

To see this, keep the computational tail strings z_A,z_B as actual local
orthogonal flags in the density operator. For each compatible pair of flags,
the two measured sum syndromes restrict the originally coherent Bell prefix
to a common affine solution set. Its nested interval coefficient-one pivots
leave k-r_k free digits. The prefix is therefore locally equivalent to
Phi_D^(k-r_k), with all other prefix coordinates fixed, and the tail flags
are product computational states. This proves the upper bound in (A).
Measuring each party's tail flags is local and reveals the affine syndromes;
the same local interval decoder then extracts k-r_k Bells deterministically.
Strong monotonicity gives the matching lower bound. This proof conditions on
k only to evaluate resource: no physical receiver receives k.

The same calculation applies along any adaptive path because all relevant
projectors commute and fixing the transcript fixes the row set. Subsequent
LOCC cannot restore the r_k destroyed Bells on average on that conditional
source without another paid resource. At finite target error this statement
must not be silently promoted to a universal smoothed Bell-loss theorem.

Consequence: if all queried thresholds lie at or after a final safe cut c,
their restrictions to the retained c-prefix have rank at most one. Bell
back-action can therefore be MUCH smaller than the number of queries. The
right resource for these nested rows is restricted rank, not query count.
A scientifically stronger receiver could exploit this separation; no complete
such receiver is provided here.

4. Why a tempting quantum eraser does not recycle the loss

For one prefix Bell and one nu-tail pair, query the sum of their two digits
on each party. Conditional on outcomes s_A,s_B, the exact post-measurement
state is

 (1/D) sum_x |xx><xx|_prefix tensor
          |s_A-x,s_B-x><s_A-x,s_B-x|_tail.

This is separable across Alice/Bob. Measuring the tail in a Fourier basis,
forgetting/resetting the outcomes, or applying phase corrections cannot
recover the lost Bell by LOCC. The initial maximally mixed tail is not a
pure coherent eraser: its classical mixture already killed the prefix cross
terms. The general affine argument in section3 gives the analogous exact
one-Bell loss for one independent restricted row. Supplying a purification
of the mixed tail would change the actual interface and must not be assumed.

5. What these results do and do not decide

Nested-prefix queries supply an admissible fresh conditional law with no
indifference band, but not reusable individual queries. Straight noisy binary
search still puts logarithmically many distinct thresholds below k on typical
paths, so its restricted rank is logarithmic. Straight descending inspection
must distinguish accidental matching tail runs from the active prefix; stopping
after a fixed short run has an error accumulating with L. Neither observation
is a universal lower bound on all possible algorithms or quantum LOCC.

The unrestricted Bell-test benchmark shows that an error-only lower bound
cannot by itself establish logarithmic loss at FIXED error. The exact
back-action calculation identifies a potential route around query-count loss,
and proves why the proposed mixed-tail quantum-eraser shortcut fails. The
remaining question is a complete admissible receiver with small restricted
prefix rank, or a universal LOCC obstruction beyond this parity family.

Historical novelty: UNKNOWN. These elementary calculations do not claim a
new noisy-search algorithm or a new global Bell measurement.
