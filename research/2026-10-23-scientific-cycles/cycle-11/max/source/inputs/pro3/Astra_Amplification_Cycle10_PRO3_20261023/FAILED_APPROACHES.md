# Failed premises, retained limits, and decisions

## Native phase lower does not imply minimax oscillation

The exact native family has a nonconstant periodic amplitude for d>d_star.
The uniform endpoint upper lacks its phase penalty. Treating the family as a
maximizer would be an unsupported step. Decision: assert the exact all-class
limsup and lower bound on the liminf; leave the all-class trough question open.

## Scalar control is not a universal native graph representation

Convex allocation and outgoing-mixture optimization solve the stated killed
forward-control model. They do not prove a scalar progress coordinate, fixed
core hazard or correct q-weighted mass map for arbitrary reciprocal graphs.
Decision: the control theorem rules out improvement by those restricted
methods, while a native reduction or heterogeneous counterconstruction is
the actual next scientific step.

## Arbitrary ceiling at the joining point

At d=d_star the ceiling may leave an order-one excess count cost. Taking
floor depth and a small positive attenuation cancels that first derivative
and proves the matching amplitude. This is an analytic construction choice;
no failed numeric fixture was tuned to pass.

## Endpoint substitution without multiplier control

The older strict-c upper cannot be extended by writing c=c(d). The new proof
bounds the actual finite perturbation H-r=O(eta), the cutoff deficit and
N log Gamma=o(1). Both the positive part and the equality r=exp(d) are paid.
Decision: retain the finite endpoint estimates, never infer Gamma^N->1 from
Gamma->1 alone.

## Double spending the information budget

Adding the high-W tail amplitude K and maximal low-W transport mass would
overstate what one KL budget admits. The three-cell inequality couples them.
The final laws use a MAX; separate lower families are compared, not combined
as if their positive entropy expenditures were independent.

## Rare forward moves do not give a uniform forward hazard

The clipped one-crossing construction has acceptance of order 1/log n.
Any proof requiring a positive lower bound on its forward hazard would fail.
The valid full-law argument instead uses its uniformly positive core hazard
to bound expected pre-hit time, then evaluates the competing exits exactly.

## Precision alternative does not justify a new campaign

The bounded assessment found a useful contract distinction but primarily
elementary consequences of target-corrected acceptance and coherent target
perturbations. An arbitrary per-edge error allowance is not an IEEE arithmetic
model, and a stationary-bias bound is not a mixing upper. Decision: preserve
the exact note and two-state diagnostic; continue the native transport problem.
