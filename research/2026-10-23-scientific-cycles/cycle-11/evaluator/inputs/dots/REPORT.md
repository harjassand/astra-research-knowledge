## Independent finite photon accounting

Let C be initial nitrite molarity, R the droplet radius in cm, P the power delivered at the sample, b=epsilon*phi the specified effective coefficient, and e_gamma=NA*h*c/lambda the photon energy per mole. Do not identify b with absorption epsilon alone or with recovered-nitrate quantum yield.

For the source's centered Gaussian straight-pass propagation model, the intensity-weighted aqueous path is

ell_G = integral[-R,R] {1-exp[-2(R²-z²)/w(z)²]} dz <= 2R.

The ideal initial effective-event rate is bounded by

r <= (P/e_gamma) ln(10) b C (2R).

This bound does not require inferring optical thinness from small b. For a homogeneous path L, the actual event fraction phi*[1-exp(-ln(10)*epsilon*C*L)] is at most ln(10)*b*C*L. Unknown absorption and quantum yield separately still prevent an exact long-path design.

With initial inventory n0=C*(4*pi*R³/3)/1000, the ideal fractional rate is proportional to P/R², but absolute rate is proportional to P*R. Halving radius at fixed P and C quadruples fractional rate while HALVING initial molar throughput. This is the decisive accounting distinction, independently derived and numerically checked.

The strongest clean matched-inventory ordinary comparator has the SAME volume and concentration, length2R and cross-sectional areaV/(2R), captures the same narrow beam, and uses the same effective chemistry. It has the same ideal rate and photon economy. This is a fair model comparator, not a qualified installed reactor or proof that chemistry is identical at all interfaces. A longer cuvette has a different inventory, mixing and propagation contract; a 1cm path cannot be supplied for free to a fixed tiny Gaussian beam/volume.

## Necessary concentration condition, not a new kinetic trajectory

The new source supplies electron competition coefficients k2=4.1e9 M−1s−1 for nitrite and k3=9.7e9 M−1s−1 for nitrate, plus the declared oxygen pseudo-first-order sink a=5.26e6s−1. At those prescribed conditions, all unlisted electron sinks nonnegative give

F_O2 <= a / (a+k2*c_nitrite+k3*c_nitrate).

At1mM total nitrite+nitrate, this upper bound is at most one half at and above nitrate fraction0.207142857. To keep strict oxygen-majority capture even POSSIBLE through90% nitrate under these coefficients, total concentration must be below0.575492mM. Equality permits at most50%, not a strict majority. This is instantaneous gross capture; being the largest individual sink is different from capturing a majority. Passing that test does not prove dominance; other sinks only reduce the share. Oxygen depletion, activity corrections, changing mixture and unknown intermediates are outside this simple necessary condition.

This explains a concrete transfer risk: concentrated-droplet behavior cannot simply stand in for dilute oxygenated finishing, and product accumulation changes even the favorable electron-sink partition. It is not a prediction of nitrate yield, retention, reactor time or useful solar performance. The named1mM case is a declared fixture, not an actual captured-liquid measurement.
