ASTRA CYCLE11 BOUNDED CLASSICAL SEARCH LEMMA
23 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane

Scope. This is a classical receiving lemma for an EXACT fresh threshold
oracle. It is not an implementation of the quantum receiver and does not
replace the actual Cycle8 indifference-band oracle. Its purpose is to expose
what prior entropy can and cannot buy before the root integrates any physical
construction. The root owns that integration.

1. INPUT, COST AND ORACLE CONTRACT

The hidden inventory K has an ACTUAL KNOWN finite prior p on an ordered finite
support S. Atoms of zero probability are removed; p is not an oracle supplied
for free when it is unknown. The construction can use exact rational p, or a
separately specified exact alphabetic tree satisfying the depth bounds below.
Computing/representing that tree and reading the prior must be charged.

A raw call at integer threshold j returns YES certainly when j<=K. When j>K,
the conditional probability of YES is at most 1/D, D>=2, even conditioned on K
and the COMPLETE previous history. Thresholds outside the possible range are
synthetic. There is NO indifference band. Conditional bounds suffice; mutual
independence is unnecessary. The query budget counts every call, including
verification and null/synthetic slots if their physical schedule uses a fresh
resource. Four fresh calls make a logical call: YES iff all four say YES.
Then the wrong-YES conditional probability is alpha<=1/16.

For any 0<delta<1, put s=ceil(log_7(1/delta)). There is a stopping-time search
whose conditional identification error, for EVERY k in S, is at most delta/6,
and whose expected raw query count obeys

 E[Q | K=k] <=16(2 ell_k+s),
 E Q <=32 H_2(p)+64+16s,                                 (1)

where ell_k is a leaf depth satisfying ell_k<=log_2(1/p_k)+2.
Every output and stop is observed, not postselected away. The unbounded
stopping-time version is almost surely terminating with finite expectation.
A finite worst-case truncation gives a finite-round version with conditional
error <=2delta/3 and no larger expected query count; see Section 5. The maximum
possible query count of that truncation depends on the deepest leaf, not just
H(p). This distinction matters for the color implementation.

2. EXACT ALPHABETIC TREE

For atom k let c_k=sum_{i<k}p_i+p_k/2 and
l_k=ceil(log_2(1/p_k))+1. Take the first l_k binary digits of c_k, using the
half-open dyadic convention at dyadic endpoints. Its dyadic cell has length
2^-l_k<=p_k/2 and is contained in the probability interval for atom k.
Those cells are disjoint and in support order. Hence the words are prefix
free and alphabetic. Form their binary trie and suppress all unary internal
vertices. Its leaves in order are the atoms of S, their depths ell_k<=l_k,
and sum_k 2^-ell_k<=1. Each subtree is an interval of support atoms, and each
binary split is one threshold comparison. If S has one atom, return it with
zero queries and zero error.

Attach a path of m_k=ell_k+s edges to leaf k. Its endpoint e_k will be an
absorbing acceptance endpoint. Every vertex on this extra path carries the
same singleton inventory interval as its original leaf. This is a classical
verification path, not a sequence of declarations that the inventory is known.

3. WALK OPERATION

Start at the original tree root. For any current nonendpoint vertex whose
inventory interval is [a,b], make two fresh logical calls at a and b+1.
Membership passes iff the first says YES and the second says NO. If it fails,
move to the parent. At the root both memberships are synthetic/certainly
passing. If membership passes at a binary internal vertex, make a third fresh
logical call at the minimum inventory in the right subtree; move right on YES
and left on NO. If membership passes at a leaf or verification-path vertex,
advance one edge on that leaf's path. Stop on reaching any endpoint e_j and
output j. At most three logical = twelve raw calls are used per walk step.
The endpoints and singleton bounds use actual support values, so gaps in the
support do not become free comparisons or invented inventory values.

Fix the actual K=k. Let e_k be the true endpoint. At every vertex other than
e_k, the conditional probability of moving one edge TOWARDS e_k is >=7/8:

* On the true root-to-leaf path, lower membership is certainly YES. Upper
  membership can fail with probability <=alpha; the split can be wrong with
  probability <=alpha. The union bound gives >=1-2alpha>=7/8.
* A wrong subtree lies completely on one side of actual k. If k<a, the lower
  test detects nonmembership with probability >=1-alpha. If k>b, the upper
  test gives YES certainly and detects nonmembership with certainty. Thus
  movement to the parent, towards e_k, has probability >=1-alpha.
* On the true verification path, membership passes with probability >=1-alpha.

These statements continue to hold under adaptive, history-dependent raw noise
satisfying the conditional bound. No sorting of adaptive replies and no
independence after sorting are invoked.

4. COMPLETE STOPPING AND ERROR PROOF

Write p0=7/8, q0=1/8, a0=p0-q0=3/4 and lambda=q0/p0=1/7.
Before hitting an endpoint, distance to the true endpoint changes by -1 with
conditional probability at least p0 and by +1 with probability at most q0.
The initial distance is d_k=ell_k+m_k=2ell_k+s. If tau is the first endpoint
time, summing stopped conditional drifts up to n gives

 0<=E distance(X_{tau wedge n},e_k)
   <=d_k-a0 E(tau wedge n).

Monotone convergence gives E tau<=d_k/a0<infinity. In particular tau is almost
surely finite. Multiplying by twelve proves (1). This is the stopping-time
argument; an unsupported entropy-gain/expected-ratio shortcut is not used.

For error, fix any wrong endpoint e_j. Let v be the least common ancestor of
the ORIGINAL leaves k and j. Every route to e_j must take a wrong edge out of
v and then reach depth h=dist(v,e_j) before returning to v. Here
h>=m_j+1. For a one-dimensional distance excursion with conditional downward
(towards v) probability at least p0, the probability of reaching h from depth
one before returning to zero is at most lambda^(h-1). Proof: while at positive
depth d, lambda^-d is a nonnegative supermartingale, since
p0 lambda+q0/lambda=1, and increasing the downward probability decreases its
conditional multiplier. Optional stopping at depth 0 or h gives the claimed
bound (even the sharper (lambda^-1-1)/(lambda^-h-1) is available).

The expected number of visits to v before termination is at most 1/a0.
To see this, after any visit the next edge toward e_k is taken with probability
at least p0. From that neighbor, return to v requires an adverse excursion
against the same p0 drift and has probability at most lambda. Therefore every
visit has conditional probability at least p0(1-lambda)=a0 of being the last
visit. Other absorbing endpoints only reduce visits. The resulting geometric
tail gives the expectation bound. Each visit initiates the particular wrong
edge with probability at most q0. A union bound over all visits therefore gives

 Pr(output j |k)<=q0/a0 * lambda^(h-1)
                <=(1/6)lambda^m_j.

Sum over wrong leaves, use m_j=ell_j+s and lambda<=1/2, and apply Kraft:

 Pr(output !=k |k)<=(1/6)lambda^s sum_j lambda^ell_j
                   <=(1/6)lambda^s<=delta/6.              (2)

This proof prices both false candidate confirmation and the number of chances
to make a false confirmation. Merely repeating a leaf verification s times
without bounding revisits would not prove (2).

5. FINITE-ROUND TRUNCATION AND THE REMAINING PHYSICAL BOUNDARY

Let dmax=max_k(2ell_k+s) and truncate at
T=ceil(2dmax/(a0 delta)) walk steps, outputting any fixed supported value on
timeout. Markov's inequality and E[tau|k]<=dmax/a0 give conditional timeout
probability <=delta/2. Total identification error <=delta/6+delta/2=2delta/3.
The actual expected query count can only decrease under truncation, but the
worst-case raw counter is <=12T. Stops/timeouts are retained as outcomes.

THIS DOES NOT IMPLEMENT THE CYCLE8 ZERO-AID COLOR SEARCH. That physical oracle
only proves YES certainty for j<=K and wrong-YES <=1/D for j>K+Q, where Q is
the total number of colors allocated and each is used once. Between K and
K+Q it has no conditional guarantee. The present membership tests at K+1
therefore need not have any drift. In addition, if one allocates colors for
the whole finite query cap 12T, the indifference width depends on max ell_k
and 1/delta rather than solely on E ell_k. Treating a random expected number
of colors as a deterministic geometric band is invalid. The root must prove
a different physical interface or a band-aware search reduction before using
(1) as a Bell-loss bound.

6. ERROR-WEIGHTED LENGTH IS A SECOND, DISTINCT GAP

An expected search cost O(H(p)+log(1/delta)) does not imply a source deficit
O(H(p)+1) when a bad classification can damage a segment of length L. An
ordinary continuity argument still charges delta L. Setting delta=L^-2
removes that term but reintroduces log L. A prior-adaptive classical decision
tree alone has not avoided it.

Decisive admission family: p(K=1)=p(K=L)=1/2. Then H(p)=ln2 regardless of the
gap. After the guaranteed first Bell pair, its accessible marginal is exactly
one half Phi_d plus one half I_(d^2)/d^2 with d=D^(L-1). A statement for ALL
finite known priors must handle this growing-dimensional two-point family.
It cannot charge the spatial gap as prior entropy. Conversely, this family
is not a no-go theorem for pooled distillation: a collective protocol could
avoid a single-copy classification/continuity bottleneck. No claim that the
optimal entanglement deficit diverges here is made.

7. KNOWN-METHOD / NOVELTY BOUNDARY

Alphabetic entropy codes and verification random walks are established tools.
This lemma is an explicit conservative derivation under the particular
conditional one-sided threshold contract, not a claim of a new universal
noisy-search invention. Dereniowski, Lukasiewicz and Uznanski, STACS 2025,
give stronger noisy binary-search constants for independent noise and explicitly
identify proof issues involving sorted adaptive replies and expected stopping
times in earlier work. Our elementary proof avoids both issues but does not
claim their optimal constants or reduce quantum back-action to their model.

8. DECISIVE OBSTRUCTION TO THE ERROR-WEIGHTED-CLASSIFICATION BRIDGE

The following lower bound is internal and elementary. It is not a converse
for distillable entanglement, for arbitrary quantum instruments, or for a
receiver whose errors can be corrected collectively without identifying K.
It does show that replacing the uniform confidence cutoff by the actual
gap-weighted identification error does not rescue the elementary search route.

Give K=1 and K=L equal probability and put Delta=L-1. Consider even the IDEAL
exact fresh raw threshold oracle with wrong-YES probability EXACTLY 1/D on
low inventory at an informative threshold j in {2,...,L}. All such answers
are conditionally independent. On high inventory all informative answers are
YES certainly. Common synthetic/out-of-range answers reveal nothing. Allow
arbitrary independent internal coins for free, adaptive queries, a stopping
time Q, and an output hatK in {1,L}. Thus the lower bound allows strictly more
randomization than the default no-free-seed physical contract. Suppose

 Delta Pr(hatK!=K)<=C,                                   (3)

with C>0 independent of L. For Delta>4C set a0=1-2C/Delta>1/2. Then

 E[Q|K=L]>=a0 log_D(a0 Delta/(2C)),
 E Q >=(a0/2)log_D(a0 Delta/(2C))=Omega_D(log L).          (4)

If the left expectation is infinite the claim is automatic. Otherwise couple
the algorithm's whole internal coin stream R independently of K. On K=L,
the full transcript is determined by R, and all informative replies are YES.
Let A be the R-event that its declared output is L and write a=Pr_R(A).
Equation (3) implies a>=a0. For each R in A the low-inventory run produces
the same full high-inventory transcript with probability D^-m(R), where m is
the number of informative calls on that high run. Since m<=Q, the probability
of the low run wrongly declaring L is at least

 E_R[1_A D^-Q]=a E[D^-Q|A]>=a D^-E[Q|A].

The last step is Jensen for the CONVEX function D^-x. On the other hand (3)
implies that low-inventory error probability is <=2C/Delta. Hence
E[Q|A]>=log_D(a Delta/(2C)) and
E[Q|K=L]>=a log_D(a Delta/(2C)). For Delta>4C the function on the right is
increasing for a>=a0 and positive there, proving (4). This proof retains every
stopping and wrong-output branch. No maximum query cap is needed.

An equivalent obstruction holds if an estimator may output any real number
and E|hatK-K|<=C. Thresholding it at (L+1)/2 gives a binary identification
procedure whose gap-weighted error is <=2C by Markov, so the same argument
applies with C replaced by 2C, for Delta>8C.

Consequently a proposed arbitrary-known-prior O(H(p)+1) entanglement deficit
cannot follow merely by obtaining such an identification estimate at O(H(p)+1)
comparison cost and charging its spatially weighted mistakes. Here H(p)=ln2,
but that classical goal has a logarithmic cost. A successful pooled quantum
protocol must exploit a materially different actual-state/coding relation,
or establish a weaker error functional that truly bounds its entropy deficit.
