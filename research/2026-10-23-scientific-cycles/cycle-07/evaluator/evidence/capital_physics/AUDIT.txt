The new Ultra example is a direct warning against interpreting a broad theorem's
uncontrolled defect as the scientific resource itself. Before pursuing its
uniform control, ask whether admitted nuisance degrees of freedom can inflate
it at unchanged ordinary optimal cost. The clock repair also shows that one can
make a proxy favorable by adding access that was not part of the old operation.
That is a valid new architecture when priced, not an inference about hidden
memory. This discriminates proxy suitability from genuine capability.

The new Dots example shows the complementary danger of demanding unnecessary
microscopic identification when whole-device evidence already answers a scoped
capability question. Neither principle is universal: the missing microscopic
closure is still essential when transferring to a new unsampled construction.
Thus an operation's actual target and evidence boundary must be determined
before choosing a representation, proxy or verification burden.

These are retrospective diagnostic insights. A prospective meta-experiment
would need independent difficult receiving problems, strong unrestricted
scientific controls, and evidence that a learned distinction helps invent a
new method or answer a previously inaccessible question. Repackaging these
already solved examples as easy strategy tests would not show amplification.
