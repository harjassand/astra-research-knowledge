TWO PROSPECTIVE-DESIGN CLARIFICATIONS RECOMMENDED

A. Distinguish shared pre-existing capital from the operation under test.
Section5 says the conventional control should share facts, representations and
acquired capital. Correct for ordinary reuse and all pre-existing capital, but
read literally this could give the control the new treatment operation itself
without paying its acquisition cost. For a future causal test, freeze the common
starting assets and explicitly define the permitted increment. Either compare
operation access against no access while charging development and giving both
sides the same underlying scientific evidence, or give both access and compare
execution/planning. These estimate different effects. The executed tests already
handle this fairly; this is a future-design clarity issue, not a current failure.

B. A future constraint variation selected after freezing the operation is a good
anti-encoding stress test, but does not by itself identify general improvement.
Freeze the variation-generation/selection rule beforehand if a performance
comparison is intended. If selected adversarially after seeing the operation,
label the result a robustness challenge. Also explicitly state that no such
prospective trial is yet admitted; its scientific interface and strong control
still need to be established without duplicating an owner's active allocation.

Neither clarification requires a new experiment or an ownership change now.
The report's present evidence supports the narrower decision it makes: preserve
the scientific programmes, decline proxy-based centralized selection, and make
generative scientific operations a testable candidate rather than a doctrine.
