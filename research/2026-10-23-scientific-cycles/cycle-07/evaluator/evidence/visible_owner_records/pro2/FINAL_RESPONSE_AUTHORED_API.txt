### 1. The new all-size construction

The support is specified by a construction expression whose networks have two ordered terminals, each of degree one. Start with a two-edge path and permit these operations:

| Operation | Construction |
|---|---|
| Parallel composition | Take two or three disjoint child networks, identify their left terminals and their right terminals, and add a fresh terminal edge at each end. |
| Odd series composition | Concatenate any odd number of child networks, at least three, by identifying consecutive terminals. |

There is no bound on depth, branching, series length or differences between child sizes. These graphs are simple, triangle-free, chordless and series-parallel, with maximum degree four. The parallel productions contain chordless two-connected cores of unbounded size. The construction expression is supplied input; recognizing arbitrary graphs as members of this class is not assumed free.

The operation uses three coordinate directions:
\[
X_i=e_{c(i)},\qquad \|X_i\|_2=1.
\]

For every real symmetric supported matrix \(D\), including its arbitrary diagonal, and every action error \(E\),
\[
Y=DX+E
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\|\widehat D-D\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le 5\|E\|_F.}
\]

The guarantee uses the original absolute Frobenius noise convention and the complete original actions.

#### The invariant that makes composition stable

Give the terminals distinct colors \(A,B\), and let \(C\) be the third color.

- A base path receives \(A,C,B\).
- At a parallel node, the two internal join vertices receive \(B,C\); each child is colored with ordered terminal colors \(B,C\).
- At an odd series node, junction colors alternate \(A,B,A,B,\ldots,A,B\).

The coloring is proper and acyclic. More strongly, no bichromatic component connects a network’s two terminals.

For terminal colors \(A,B\), track four actual component sizes:

\[
\begin{aligned}
x&=\text{size of the }AB\text{ component at the left terminal},\\
u&=\text{size of the }AC\text{ component at the left terminal},\\
v&=\text{size of the }AB\text{ component at the right terminal},\\
y&=\text{size of the }BC\text{ component at the right terminal}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The base vector is \((1,2,1,2)\). A parallel production gives the exact updates
\[
u'=v'=1,\qquad
x'=2+\sum_i(u_i-1),\qquad
y'=2+\sum_i(v_i-1).
\]
Its other potentially enlarged components have sizes
\[
1+\sum_i(x_i-1),\qquad 1+\sum_i(y_i-1).
\]

An odd series production preserves the roles of the four boundary quantities. A newly joined bichromatic component meets at most one series junction, because no child transmits between its terminals.

Consequently, the simultaneous bounds
\[
u,v\le2,\qquad x,y\le5,\qquad
\text{every bichromatic component has at most }13\text{ vertices}
\]
close under both productions at every depth. Parallel composition resets the smaller boundary quantities, and the permitted series operation cannot exchange them with the larger quantities.

This is the all-depth argument. Finite examples are used to check the implementation, not to establish the induction. The complete argument is in :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}[PROOF.md](sandbox:/workspace/scratch/67a1ac9a405d/cycle6_pro2/PROOF.md).

#### Recovery and sharp query count

The decoder retains each diagonal observation directly. On each bichromatic tree, it signs the incidence observations, projects them to zero mean and recovers edge coefficients by subtree sums. This is the exact least-squares inverse.

With component order at most \(13\), each vertex belongs to at most two relevant color-pair components. The resulting row estimate gives
\[
C^2\le 1+2(13-1)=25.
\]
Symmetry then bounds operator error by the maximum absolute row sum.

Three is also the **sharp worst-case exact and stable query count for this family**. On a cycle with \(k\) vertices, restrict the unknown matrix to its \(k\) edges and \(k\) diagonal entries. These are \(2k\) independent unknowns. For a rank-two design, symmetry imposes a nontrivial constraint on the \(2k\) output coordinates through symmetry of \(X^\top DX\), so the action cannot be injective. Rank-one or rank-zero designs have an even smaller output dimension. This lower bound allows arbitrary real designs.

The proof also supplies a reusable module version. For separated leaf modules with component cap \(K_0\), boundary masses at most \(\beta\ge2\), and maximum parallel arity \(B\ge2\), set
\[
R=2+B(\beta-1),\qquad
K=\max\{K_0,\,1+B(R-1)\}.
\]
The same induction bounds all component sizes by \(K\). Structural assumptions on the leaf modules remain explicit. An included path-coloring construction permits arbitrary leaf-path lengths at least two, giving \(K\le31\) and error constant \(\sqrt{61}\) when \(B=3\).

### 3. The decisive failure: even series feeds growing mass back into branching

The natural two-child series extension uses a third-colored junction and exchanges the smaller and larger boundary roles. That destroys the positive invariant.

Let \(L\) be the base path and define
\[
Q_0=L,\qquad
Q_h=P_2\!\left(S_2(Q_{h-1},L),\,S_2(Q_{h-1},L)\right).
\]

Under the tested extension, the coloring remains proper, acyclic and terminal-separated. The supports remain actual triangle-free series-parallel graphs, now with maximum degree three. Nevertheless, a boundary component has size exactly \(2^h\), and a branch cut has
\[
\Gamma=2^{h-2}-2^{-h}.
\]

The materialized \(Q_8\) has **1,788 vertices and 2,042 edges**. Its relevant bichromatic tree has 256 vertices, split by an edge into parts of sizes 127 and 129. The saved symmetric witness has a unit edge coefficient and
\[
\|DX\|_F^2=\frac{256}{16383},
\qquad
\frac{\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}{\|DX\|_F^2}
\ge \frac{16383}{256}.
\]

This proves instability of the specified coloring strategy on its family. It does **not** exclude a better coloring of these graphs, a four-coordinate construction, or arbitrary real designs.

Two additional boundary results constrain the next construction:

- **Prescribed equal terminal colors can be impossible.** In \(K_{2,4}\), assigning the two degree-four terminals the same color leaves only three colors for four internal vertices; a repeated color creates a bichromatic cycle. A universal arbitrary-boundary extension invariant is therefore false.
- **A component-size cap alone does not support remote reinsertion.** For every \(K\ge2\), the saved colored-cycle construction has a removed degree-two vertex whose reinsertion, without recoloring, necessarily exceeds cap \(K\). The received remote-vertex lemma therefore needs a stronger maintained invariant.
