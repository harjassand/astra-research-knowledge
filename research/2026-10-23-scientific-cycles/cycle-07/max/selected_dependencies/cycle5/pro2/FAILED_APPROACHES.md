# Failures and limitations that govern continuation

## Stable local inverses do not give a stable global inverse

The new exact double-star family joins two individually stable stars by one
edge. With t leaves at each hub, the central coefficient1 and leaf coefficients
-1/(t+1) give action norm squared2/(t+1). The specified two-color design's
operator inverse diverges at least sqrt((t+1)/2), despite diameter3 and only
two star pieces. Any gluing argument must control cut balance or an equivalent
global noise mechanism. This is an unbounded-degree coordinate example; it is
not imported into the Delta4 all-design problem.

## Requiring Frobenius output can discard a valid operator operation

On the exact same two-coordinate arm design, an alternating ramp forces a
Frobenius-output constant of order L, whereas the operator constant is of
order sqrt L. The requested norm must remain operator norm. This does not
weaken the action error contract: its absolute Frobenius norm is unchanged.

## Arbitrary maximal recoloring is inadequate

The literature contributor's caterpillar has an alternating0/1 spine and a
color2 leaf at every spine vertex. All leaves form a maximal independent set
in the graph square, yet recoloring them3 leaves the entire bichromatic spine
intact. A square-independent set is useful only if it actually fragments all
old bichromatic trees. Radius-two domination alone does not ensure this.
This defeats the proposed automatic packing shortcut, not every targeted
selection or every four-color design.

## Structural reductions are not the missing coloring

Bounded tree-partition width and a low-degree elimination order can propagate
long bichromatic components through arbitrary depth. No global stability
conclusion follows without a size/cut invariant. Triangle components are not
all bounded: saturated squared paths are arbitrarily long. The new boundary
extension removes them correctly; it does not assume they are small.

The reduced graph still has arbitrarily deep square/long-cycle and mixed
separator structure. Finding its bounded-component four-coloring is unsolved.
A low-degree vertex away from a boundary, from the cited primary classification,
does not by itself prove a valid reinsertion state. No finite solver output
is substituted for the missing all-size closure.

The strip extension must be followed by the global decoder on the original
support and full actions. Original port action cells contain the inserted
strip coefficients and cannot be treated as a reduced-operator observation.

## Established subdivision methods already own easier special cases

Exact one-subdivision of a three-colorable graph has a classical three-star-
color operation. An unmodified subdivision-only four-query construction would
lose that comparison. A nearby outerplanar bipartite star-five obstruction
has maximum degree five, so it does not settle the owner's degree-four question.
