# Insufficient routes and limits affecting continuation

## Unweighted absorption loses a real coefficient

At theta=eta, the old unweighted boundary energy would give a low-set capture
coefficient b. In the global-dominant regime, actual global escape is stronger
than the local transport allowance. Keeping boundary weight w=max(1,r_b)
turns that excess into the smaller coefficient b/r_b. The three-state family
attains the latter. An unweighted upper is valid but not sharp in this regime.

## A singular finite-spacing lower is not a construction

The old interior optimum has spacing tending to infinity at r0=1. Substituting
that endpoint into its many-level proof would not establish uniform depth,
integer rounding, waiting or retention. The new lower uses three actual states
and proves exact finite transition formulas and a full-law sandwich instead.
This is not an unsuccessful numerical campaign: the old method was rejected
as an insufficient premise before a new proof was constructed.

## A strict excess-killing margin would omit equality

At r0=1, a finite low-weight cutoff can have r_b slightly below one. A proof
requiring a uniform positive r_b-1, or dividing by that margin, would miss
the requested boundary. The exact positive deficit and bounded max-weight
retain that case. N Delta, N eta and N q(E) are checked separately.

## The interior entropy-slack argument cannot be copied

In the interior, an allocation k<K supports c>c* and suppresses the transport
term at critical scale. Here c=1 is the endpoint: an argument using c>1
would have no established local transport premise. The global regime instead
retains the sum K-k+(beta/alpha)(exp(k)-1)exp(-s), then proves its finite
convex maximum on the interval allowed by the single budget.

## Relative small-b errors would be nonuniform

An expansion involving eta log b or dividing by b need not be uniform over
positive atoms and growing supports. Weighted AM-GM bounds the power by
b/r_b+eta additively. This is valid at b=0 and arbitrarily small positive b.

## Full-chain returns are not deleted

The absorbed matrix is only a first-hit counter. All original C propagation
after first hits stays in the finite TV upper. The lower's first-core
decomposition bounds every later excursion by TV contraction from the actual
core state. Its marked retained paths provide a lower, and a separate full-law
upper proves their leading amplitude; no norm witness is called a mixing lower.

## No failed execution or fitted asymptotic curve

The diagnostic passed first time. All finite inputs and checks were frozen
before execution. The three-state rational fixture uses an explicit ell_proxy
that is not log(m); it checks formulas and inequalities only. No numerical
convergence rate is inferred from it, no favorable parameter search occurred,
and no old diagnostic or tree campaign was rerun.

## The useful comparison is adverse

The same-instance ordinary IMH law is asymptotically better by a factor of
order (log n)^3. This is a scientific obstruction to interpreting the screened
mixture as generically faster. It is not a runtime lower: complete target
enumeration on three known states may be cheaper, and caching is admitted.
