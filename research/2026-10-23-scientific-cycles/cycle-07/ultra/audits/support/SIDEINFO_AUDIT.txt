3. ENTROPY CONSEQUENCE

For each fixed z the map k -> k-a_z is injective on its posterior support.
Thus H(K|Z)=H(G|Z). Classical conditioning and the geometric maximum-entropy
bound imply
 H(K|Z)=H(G|Z)<=H(G)<=g(EG),
 g(u)=(u+1)ln(u+1)-u ln u, g(0)=0.                       (3)
For completeness, if u>0, compare the law of G with the geometric distribution
q_n=(u+1)^(-1)[u/(u+1)]^n on nonnegative integers. Relative-entropy positivity
gives H(G)<=-sum_n p_n ln q_n=g(u). For u=0, G=0 almost surely. All actual
variables here have finite range. Since g is increasing, (2) yields
 I(K;Z)>=ln L-g((b+C_D+s)/m),                            (4)
provided t=(b+C_D+s)/m>=0. If t<0, the requested score is impossible by (2).
One may replace the right side of (4) by its maximum with zero.

Consequently bounded b and bounded s require I(K;Z)>=ln L-O_D(1). This is an
information requirement on the PHYSICALLY SUPPLIED classical extension. It is

informative. Exact support retains those small posterior weights.

The detailed parameter E G is operationally matched by a conventional receiver.

Prepare the remaining L-J output pairs locally as |00>. The selector and any

 b=m E[J-a_Z]=m E[K-a_Z]=m E G.                           (5)
