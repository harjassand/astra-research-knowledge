For A=[[a,b],[c,d]], write t=(a+d)/2, q=(a-d)/2,
s=(b+c)/2, r=(b-c)/2, and R=sqrt(q^2+s^2). Define

 K(A) = (R^2-t^2) 1_{t+R>0}.

At the boundary t=-R the two formulas agree, so K is continuous,
finite, positively 2-homogeneous, and has quadratic growth. Claim: K
is globally quasiconvex. This is the zero-boundary, unmellinized
halfspace projection generator, not the compact-K logarithmic generator.

DIRECT HALFSPACE PROJECTION PROOF.
Let H={p in R2:p2<=0}, J=[[0,-1],[1,0]], and split A into rows p,qrow.
For y=p-J qrow, the boundary envelope is
 F_H(A)=sup_{z2=0} z.y-|z|^2 = (a+d)^2/4=t^2.
Set L_H=F_H when b>0 and L_H=max(F_H,det A) when b<=0.
We prove L_H quasiconvex; then M_H=L_H-det is quasiconvex.

For a background with b>0, L_H(B)=F_H(B) and L_H>=F_H globally,
so ordinary convex Jensen for F_H gives the claim. Suppose b<=0.
If L_H(B)=F_H(B), use the same argument. Otherwise it suffices to
prove integral L_H(DU)>=det B for affine-periodic smooth U.

Project the periodic part of U1 in scalar L2 onto the closed convex
set of functions with (U1')_y<=0, with the same affine increments.
The one-direction isotonic projection lemma (independently proved in
work/ws01/POSITIVE_PARTS_ISOTONIC_PROOF.md) gives a Lipschitz U1',
with p'=DU1' in H; where U1' differs from U1 its gradient lies in
the boundary p2'=0. Where they coincide, p'=p almost everywhere.
The projection commutes with translations; the projection variational
inequality applied to opposite small translations in each spatial
direction gives
 integral p'.(p-p')>=0.
The common affine increments cause no extra term because p-p' has
zero mean. This also follows from firm nonexpansivity on slices for
the transverse derivative and saturation in the constrained derivative.

Pointwise, when p' is on the boundary, L_H>=F_H>=
 p'.(p-J qrow)-|p'|^2 = det(p',qrow)+p'.(p-p').
When p'=p is in H, L_H>=det A is the same bound. Integrating and
using the determinant null Lagrangian for (U1',U2) proves the result.
The case b=0 can also be obtained by continuity of the integrand and
the background. Standard periodic-to-zero-boundary passage applies.

The explicit formula is
 M_H(A)=(q^2+s^2-r^2) 1_{r+sqrt(q^2+s^2)>0}.
Indeed b=r+s. If r<=-R, b<=0 and the max branch gives zero;
if -R<r<R the quadratic is positive and both branches agree; if r>=R,
b>=0 and the boundary branch retains the negative quadratic.
The boundary values are zero. Left multiplication by -J rotates
the conformal coordinate r to t while preserving R, hence
 K(A)=M_H(-J A). This proves the claimed global quasiconvexity.

AN EXPLICIT TOUCHING CERTIFICATE FOR ALL SYMMETRIC F_p, p>1.
Use the definition of F_p in
work/ws04/three_hinges/SYMMETRIC_FAMILY_ELLIPTIC_QC_PROOF.md.
Put L=p+1, eta=(p-1)/L, D=4-2(p-1)^2 and
 W(t,q)=F_p(diag(t+q,t-q))-L(t^2-q^2).
Its exact sectors are:
 t>=0: W=L^2(|q|-eta t)_+^2;
 t=-z<0, |q|<=eta z: W=-D z^2+2L^2 q^2;
 t=-z<0, |q|>eta z: W=L^2(|q|+eta z)^2-4z^2.
For D<=0, W>=0 and the central cell already has a PC support.
For D>0 the following GLOBAL pointwise inequality holds for all t,q,v:

 W(t,q)+2L v^2 >= D (q^2+v^2-t^2)
                         1_{-t+sqrt(q^2+v^2)>0}.       (1)

Proof: D<=2L, D<L^2, D<=L^2(1-eta^2)=4p for p>1.
If t<0 the indicator is one. In the inner sector the difference is
 (2L^2-D)q^2+(2L-D)v^2>=0.
In the outer sector put |q|=eta z+w, w>=0. The difference is
 (2L^2-D)eta^2 z^2 + (4L^2-2D)eta z w
 +(L^2-D)w^2+(2L-D)v^2>=0.
If t>=0 and the indicator is zero, W>=0 suffices. If it is one,
and |q|<=eta t, the difference is
 D(t^2-q^2)+(2L-D)v^2>=0.
For |q|>eta t the difference, before its nonnegative v^2 term, is
 (L^2-D)q^2-2L^2 eta t |q|+(L^2 eta^2+D)t^2.
Its minimum over unrestricted |q| is
 D[L^2(1-eta^2)-D]t^2/(L^2-D)>=0.
Thus all sectors prove (1).

For a central-cell background B, t0>0 and |q0|<eta t0, with arbitrary

 Phi_B(A)=L det A+4L s0 s-2L s0^2+D K(-(A-S0)).
