0. ACTUAL INPUT AND SCOPE
Read the unchanged native Cycle2 PROOF.txt/CHECKPOINT.txt, the Cycle2 stopping
PROOF.txt and Cycle1 PROOF.txt. Inherit their normal LOCC model and corrected
complete-memory accounting. The ideal complete memory tau, acted on by a fixed
normal LOCC block, emits an EXACT Bell state Phi_D, D=d^J, and changes to tau_1
with half-trace distance T(tau_1,tau)<=delta. Every retained register and outcome
branch counts. Let m=ln D. For each finite horizon N, actual output joint-prefix
Bell probabilities satisfy p_i=Tr S^i(tau)>=[1-(i-1)delta]_+, and p_1=1.
Gamma_N is the full unconditioned normal LOCC output channel. None of the Bell
stopping measurements below is asserted LOCC or a physical resource operation.

The existing generic iid bound is E_C^op>=m sum_i p_i-ln Z_N,
 Z_N=1+(N-1)(1-D^-1).
The old proof required preparation error tending to zero. Here the same finite
observable operation proves a strong converse, an explicit finite-copy error
bound, a leading law in simultaneous copy/return limits, and a sharp distinction
between deterministic and preselected EXPECTED supplied Bell resources.
This does NOT improve the logarithmic generic formation gap. It is not another
optimization of the separable testing exponent or the old noisy-output task.

Preparation: a deterministic supplied Phi_M is mapped by normal LOCC, with
free local ancillas, to sigma_t on the COMPLETE target memory. Its error is the
GLOBAL quantity epsilon_t=T(sigma_t,tau^tensor t), never a per-copy error.
All formation outcomes are averaged. The prepared memory need not emit exact
Bell states: the exact-Bell premise is only about the ideal tau.

1. ONE FIXED-HORIZON FINITE-COPY TEST
The orthogonal stopping measurement on one chain is F_k=P_k-P_(k+1), k<N,
and F_N=P_N, with P_0=I, P_i=Phi_D^tensor i tensor I. Its random variable K is
in {0,...,N}; on the ideal target K>=1 a.s. and mu=E K=sum_i p_i.
For t chains keep their ENTIRE joint stopping record K_1,...,K_t.
A globally A:B separable output law q obeys for every rectangular tail
 q(K_a>=i_a for all a with i_a>0)<=D^(-sum_a i_a).             (1)
The intersection is a Bell projector of Schmidt dimension D^(sum i_a), with
residual identity. A product local state has overlap at most its reciprocal;
convexity gives (1). The comparator may be arbitrarily correlated across copies.

Put g(0)=0, g(k)=D^k for k>=1. Write
 g(K)=sum_(i=1)^N a_i 1[K>=i], a_1=D, a_i=D^i-D^(i-1)>0.
Expanding the PRODUCT of these sums and applying (1) gives
 E_q product_a g(K_a)<= (sum_i a_i D^-i)^t=Z_N^t.             (2)
For kappa>=1 define the bounded accept event
 A_kappa={K_a>=1 for EVERY a, sum_a K_a>=t kappa}.
The all-positive requirement is essential because g(0)=0. On this event the
product in (2) is at least exp(mt kappa), so
 q(A_kappa)<=exp[-t(m kappa-ln Z_N)].                        (3)
No conditional-moment, supermartingale or optional-time premise appears.

For ideal tau^tensor t the K_a are iid in [1,N]. For 1<=kappa<mu and N>1,
 Hoeffding: p^tensor t(A_kappa)>=1-beta_t,
 beta_t=exp[-2t(mu-kappa)^2/(N-1)^2].                        (4)
One may replace beta_t by the EXACT finite convolution lower-tail probability,
or any rigorously better bound, without changing the rest of the argument.

2. DETERMINISTIC SUPPLY: FINITE-COPY BOUND AND STRONG CONVERSE
Let zeta_M=M^-1 sum_(j=1)^M |jj><jj| on the supplied Bell registers. Exactly
 Phi_M<=M zeta_M.                                          (5)
Apply the SAME entire preparation-plus-Gamma_N^tensor t normal positive channel
to both sides. Its second output is globally separable. Measuring the stopping
record gives p'_t<=M q pointwise. Normal infinite intermediate memories cause no
problem: order preservation is applied to one map from a finite input to a
finite observed output. No infinite-dimensional entropy continuity is invoked.
From (3), prepared acceptance is at most M exp[-t(m kappa-ln Z_N)]. Since total
variation of the ideal/prepared stopped laws is at most epsilon_t, (4) yields
 epsilon_t>=1-beta_t-exp[ln M-t(m kappa-ln Z_N)].              (6)
The right side can of course be replaced by its maximum with zero.
This is valid for EVERY finite t,N, complete ideal memory, formation protocol,
and kappa in [1,mu). It includes correlations across all prepared copies.

Write R_t=(ln M)/t, C_N=m mu-ln Z_N. If R_t<=C_N-gamma for some gamma>0,
and kappa=mu-gamma/(2m)>=1, then
 epsilon_t>=1-exp[-t gamma^2/(2m^2(N-1)^2)]-exp[-t gamma/2].  (7)
If this kappa is below1, choose kappa=1; ideal acceptance is then exactly1,
not merely Hoeffding-bounded, and (6) applies with beta=0. In particular any
strict rate below C_N has preparation error tending to ONE exponentially.
For N=1, ideal output is Phi_(D^t), and directly
 epsilon_t>=1-M/D^t.                                      (8)
Use (8) rather than divide by N-1.

An explicit finite-size budget bound follows from (6). For any eta in (0,1),
put kappa=mu-(N-1)sqrt[ln(1/eta)/(2t)] if this is at least1. If epsilon_t+eta<1,
 ln M>=t C_N-m(N-1)sqrt[t ln(1/eta)/2]+ln(1-epsilon_t-eta).  (9)
Thus the rate lower bound survives any fixed global error less than1. It is not
necessary to assume epsilon_t->0 for the leading resource law.

3. CONSTANT-ERROR AND SIMULTANEOUS RETURN/COPY LEADING LAWS
For precision define C_det,epsilon^*(delta) as follows. For each admissible
complete memory tau and fixed normal device with the stated exact output and
return at most delta, take the infimum of limsup_t (ln M_t)/t over deterministic
Bell-supply protocols with limsup_t T(sigma_t,tau^tensor t)<=epsilon. Then infimize
this cost over those admissible memory/device pairs. The same definition using
preselected expected E ln M_r instead defines C_expected,epsilon^*(delta) in
section4. Here epsilon in[0,1) is fixed; epsilon=0 means error tends to zero, not
that each finite-t preparation must be exact. All lower bounds hold before the
infima; the explicit stock operation supplies their matched leading upper.

The inherited survival bound implies mu>=sum_i[1-(i-1)delta]_+.
For 0<delta<1 choose N=ceil(1/delta). Then mu>=1/(2delta)+1/2, hence
 C_N>=m/(2delta)+m/2-ln[1+(ceil(1/delta)-1)(1-D^-1)].         (10)
For EVERY prescribed global error tolerance epsilon<1, fixed-delta iid
formation at limsup error<=epsilon therefore has the same universal lower
bound (10). The ordinary inventory construction, with explicit typical-stock
formation and error tending to zero, gives upper cost m/(2delta)+O_(J,d)(1).
Consequently its leading optimum remains1/2 at ANY constant error below1.
This strengthens the former vanishing-error-only statement.

There is also a legitimate diagonal-limit theorem, derived from finite (6),
not by exchanging the previous proof's limits. Let t->infinity, delta_t->0
arbitrarily, and permit a DIFFERENT complete memory/device at each t. Let
 R_t<= (1-xi)m/(2delta_t), fixed xi in(0,1).
Take N_t=ceil(1/delta_t), kappa_t=(1-xi/2)/(2delta_t). Eventually kappa_t>=1,
 mu_t-kappa_t>=xi/(4delta_t), N_t-1<=1/delta_t,
 and ln Z_(N_t)<=xi m/(8delta_t).
Thus (6) proves the explicit uniform estimate
 epsilon_t>=1-exp[-xi^2 t/8]-exp[-xi m t/(8delta_t)].          (11)
In particular every uniformly subcritical normalized rate forces global error
to ONE even when the desired return becomes arbitrarily tight with copy count.
Each operation at each t still uses one fixed FINITE horizon before its test.
No unbounded observable or entropy-limit exchange has been introduced.

Matched upper, uniform in delta_t: the old inventory uses L=ceil(J/delta_t),
K uniform on J,...,J+L-1 and initial width J+L-1. It returns the COMPLETE
unflagged memory within delta_t after each j<=J and emits J exact ideal pairs.
For t copies use fixed Bell inventory
 B_t=ceil[t E K+(L-1)sqrt(t ln t/2)]
rank-d pairs. On the bounded-stock upper-tail event, supply each sampled stock;
on overflow, emit a separable complete-memory fallback. Erase all sampled labels.
The global error is at most1/t. Moreover
 delta_t (ln d) B_t/t <=m/2 +delta_t m
   +m sqrt[ln t/(2t)]+delta_t ln d/t,            (12)
using L-1<=J/delta_t and E K<=J+J/(2delta_t).
The ceiling and the full J-block cost are included. The RHS tends to m/2
for EVERY delta_t->0. Thus the simultaneous leading constant is matched.
No efficient implementation or practical initialization claim follows.

4. PRESELECTED EXPECTED SUPPLY: SHARP ERROR FACTOR, NOT A STRONG CONVERSE
This is an explicitly DIFFERENT resource definition. Before resource supply
sample a classical branch r, receive Phi_(M_r), run that branch's normal LOCC
formation, average ALL branches, and charge c_t=E ln M_r. Approximation is still
GLOBAL half-trace error of the averaged complete output. Any erased coin/label
must really be unavailable. This generalizes the exact preselected factory by
allowing iid approximate preparation; it is not the definition of E_C^op above.

For B=m kappa-ln Z_N>0, branchwise (5)-(6) give
 p'_t(A_kappa)<=E min{1,exp(ln M_r-tB)}.
A stronger distributional statement is available before averaging costs. Put
b=tB and let s>0. Since min{1,exp(L-b)}<=1[L>=b-s]+exp(-s), the same accept test
proves the supply QUANTILE bound
 Pr{ln M_r>=b-s}>=1-epsilon_t-beta_t-exp(-s).                (13q)
Thus almost every branch must itself have near-threshold supply when global
error is small; a cheap average is not being obtained by uncounted branches.
For the diagonal parameters of section3 and s=xi m t/(8delta_t),
b-s>=(1-xi)t m/(2delta_t). Consequently
 Pr{ln M_r>=(1-xi)t m/(2delta_t)}
 >=1-epsilon_t-exp(-xi^2 t/8)-exp(-xi m t/(8delta_t)).        (13d)
These quantile statements allow the independently chosen branch channels to
differ: the free accept bound is uniform branch by branch. They do not presume
that the selected resource is independent of the selected classical protocol.

For every l>=0 and b>0, convexity of exp on [0,b] gives the chord bound
 min{1,exp(l-b)}<=exp(-b)+l/b.                              (13)
For l>=b it follows from1<=l/b; for l<=b use the chord connecting0,b.
Therefore the FULL expected supplied resource obeys
 c_t/t>=B[1-epsilon_t-beta_t-exp(-tB)].                     (14)
The sharper chord with slope (1-exp(-tB))/(tB) divides the RHS of (14) by
1-exp(-tB); the simpler version suffices for every limit stated below.
For fixed N and limsup epsilon_t<=epsilon<1, first take t->infinity at fixed
kappa<mu and then kappa up to mu. It follows that
 liminf c_t/t >=(1-epsilon)(m mu-ln Z_N)                    (15)
whenever the RHS is positive. For N=1 the exact all-Bell test gives the same
statement with C_1=m. These results require preselection before supply: they do
not price a favorable posterior branch after an unknown entangled input.

A real counterconstruction rules out replacing (1-epsilon) by1 in (15).
Before supply toss a coin with probability q. On success supply the Bell state
for an ordinary good preparation; on failure supply rank1 and output a separable
fallback on the COMPLETE target memory. Genuinely erase the coin and all
communication records not retained by the target. No branch is postselected away.
The mean cost is q times the good cost, while global error is at most
1-q+q e_t, where e_t is the good branch's global preparation error. If only an
approximate good initializer is available, q_t=(1-epsilon)/(1-e_t) gives error
at most epsilon for all sufficiently large t with e_t<epsilon.

For the ACTUAL inventory upper, expected supply permits a simpler EXACT good
initializer: preselect K_1,...,K_t independently from the known uniform stock law,
receive Phi_(d^(sum K_a)), and prepare the entire tensor memory by the original
stock factory, genuinely erasing the labels. Its good averaged output is EXACTLY
tau_delta^tensor t and its expected cost is t[J+(L-1)/2]ln d. Thus choose
q=1-epsilon directly. For every finite t the full operation has global error
at most epsilon and expected cost exactly
 (1-epsilon)t[J+(L-1)/2]ln d.                              (15a)
No typical-stock tail, good-branch approximation, or hidden resource is needed
for this expected-supply upper. Combining (10),(15),(15a) gives the matched law
 lim_(delta->0) delta C_expected,epsilon^*(delta)/(J ln d)
       =(1-epsilon)/2.                                   (16)
Here epsilon in[0,1) is the asymptotic global-error tolerance; the upper actually
meets the same bound at EVERY finite t. At epsilon=0 it is the exact preselected
factory initializer, whereas a general lower-bound initializer may still only
have error tending to zero. The deterministic-supply law has coefficient1/2.

An especially transparent finite-state counterexample is target Phi_D^tensor t.
Use the good resource Phi_(D^t) with probability q and rank1 otherwise, outputting
chi_D^tensor t on the bad branch. The erased-coin output is
sigma=q Phi_(D^t)+(1-q)chi_D^tensor t, of exact half-trace error
 (1-q)(1-D^-t), expected supplied cost q t ln D.
Thus any fixed q in(0,1) has strictly subcritical expected rate while its error
stays below1-q; the deterministic strong converse cannot extend to that budget.
The one-block memory device that emits Phi_D and replaces its memory with chi_D
has full return delta=1-D^-1, so this is also an actual finite memory instance.

The same normalized expected law holds on arbitrary simultaneous delta_t->0,
t->infinity: use the kappa_t of section3 in (14), then let xi->0 after the bound.
The exact gamble upper (15a) has normalized cost at most
(1-epsilon)(m/2+delta_t m), so matches without using (12). This is a matched resource/error statement,
not evidence that physical noisy Bell outputs are usable. The ideal task still
has exact Bell output; only initialization has the prescribed global error.

5. FAILURE / COMPARATOR / TRANSPORT BOUNDARY
The operator-order step (5), bounded likelihood tests, Hoeffding, and deliberate
success/failure mixing are standard machinery. General one-shot dilution and
resource strong-converse techniques already exist; the new obligation here is
the finite correlated stopping test for this actual normal-memory model, its
complete error/resource accounting and the uniform diagonal limit. Historical
novelty is not established. This is not a solution to generic E_F additivity,
the critical common-Gram witness, or the possible logarithmic collective saving.
The exact Cycle2 inventory testing obstruction still limits this method to its
relative-entropy threshold. Another tuning cannot recover formation cost.

The test's essential quantum input is the true JOINT rectangular-tail bound,
not a product of single-copy marginal bounds. For D>=2, two copies, put Alice's
two output sites in a local Phi_D and Bob's two sites independently in a local
Phi_D. This is an A:B product state. Each cross-party Bell test separately has
probability D^-2 but BOTH have probability D^-2, not D^-4. Thus conditional
factorization of the tests is false even on a fully separable A:B comparator.
Nevertheless (1) is saturated at the joint two-site tail and the theorem is valid.
This preserves the original entanglement-swapping warning: no optional-time or
conditional supermartingale strengthening has been inferred.

Reusable proof operation: if a resource omega_L satisfies omega_L<=exp(L) zeta_L
with zeta_L free, and free normal channels have a bounded finite-record accept
test q(A)<=exp(-b), while the target accepts with probability>=1-beta, then
 deterministic L: epsilon>=1-beta-exp(L-b);
 preselected average L: E L>=b[1-epsilon-beta-exp(-b)].
The second statement assumes L>=0 and every branch separately meets the same
free-test bound. Source dimension/energy need not be controlled if the entire
resource-to-finite-record channel is normal and positive. This operation is
transportable only after proving its actual free-test probability bound; an
analogy with another branch's stopping or bath model is not itself such a proof.
