# Fixed coherent parameter: useful representation and targeted primary comparison

Reviewer: `/root/stopping_literature`. Date: 21 October 2026.

Scope: identify an actual mechanism useful for `V_s = cos(s) U + i sin(s) I` on the same qutrit and rooted degree-three tree. This note does not repeat the feedback or continuity literature, does not resolve fixed-parameter capacity, and does not claim historical priority. Two focused search rounds were followed by three primary-source reads. No outreach or external computation occurred.

## Outcome

The most useful finding is an **exact two-sector full-Fock-space representation of the actual device**, including its exceptional root edge and its specified stationary state. It places every bath coefficient of a finite block in the Toeplitz–Cuntz algebra generated by two creation operators. This gives a concrete symbolic representation of the coherent histories and a route to exact block error spaces. It is more directly matched to this device than the retrieved scattering-walk or Temperley–Lieb channel literature.

The representation below is a direct derivation for the present model. The primary operator-algebra source supplies the established terminology and normal forms. No cited theorem by itself gives the capacity of the resulting repeated-memory process.

## 1. Exact coordinate reduction: two rooted binary trees

Let `r` be the distinguished root and `r0` its child along port zero. Delete only the edge joining `r` and `r0`. The remaining graph has two connected components, each a rooted binary tree with child labels 1 and 2. Thus

`E = C^2 ⊗ F(C^2)`,

where `F(C^2) = direct_sum_{m>=0} (C^2)^{tensor m}` is the full, unsymmetrized Fock space. Label its word basis by binary words, written with the topmost stack symbol first. The two components give the `C^2` factor. The vacuum vector `|empty>` identifies their respective roots.

Define left creation operators `L_i |w> = |i w>` for `i=1,2`, and put

`S_i = I_2 ⊗ L_i`,

`P = I_2 ⊗ |empty><empty|`,

`B = sigma_x ⊗ |empty><empty|`.

Then

`S_i^* S_j = delta_ij I`,

`S_1 S_1^* + S_2 S_2^* = I - P`,

`B^2 = P`, `B S_i = 0`, `S_i^* B = 0`.

In the external port order `(0,1,2)`, the **actual** edge-reversal unitary is

```
U = [ B      S_1    S_2  ]
    [ S_1^*   0      0   ]
    [ S_2^*   0      0   ].
```

This displayed array is algebraic matrix notation, not a diagram. It has the following direct verification. Input port 1 or 2 pushes that child label and outputs zero. Input zero at a nonvacuum pops its top label and outputs it. At either of the two vacua, input zero crosses the deleted edge to the other vacuum and outputs zero. These are exactly the original rules. The relations above also verify `U=U^*` and `U^2=I` without a boundary approximation.

Diagonalize `sigma_x` on the first factor. The bath splits into two invariant Fock sectors, and the unitary becomes `U_+ direct_sum U_-`, with

```
U_± = [ ±P_empty    L_1    L_2  ]
      [  L_1^*       0      0   ]
      [  L_2^*       0      0   ].
```

For external `Z=diag(-1,1,1)`,

`U_- = - Z U_+ Z`,

`V_s^- = - Z V_{-s}^+ Z`.

These are exact unitary coordinate changes. They do not erase the boundary, condition on a bath measurement, or change the initializer.

## 2. The specified stationary state is especially simple

With `eta=diag(3/4,1/8,1/8)`, the downward factor on a child-1 or child-2 edge is `1/6`; the exceptional root–child-zero ratio is one. Hence in the preceding coordinates

`pi(j,w) = (1/3) 6^{-|w|}`, for `j in {0,1}`.

Consequently

`tau_pi = (I_2/2) ⊗ rho_F`,

`rho_F = (2/3) sum_w 6^{-|w|} |w><w|`.

Normalization follows from `sum_{m>=0} 2^m 6^{-m} = 3/2`. The Hadamard change diagonalizing the two sectors leaves `I_2/2` unchanged. Thus the specified block channel is exactly an equal mixture of the two Fock-sector block channels, each initialized with the same fixed `rho_F`. The sector is retained throughout the entire block. Replacing that by a new independent sign at each call would change the process.

For a general fixed normal initializer, the global unitary is still block diagonal in these sectors. Off-diagonal initial sector blocks do not contribute to the external reduced channel after tracing the final sector. Within-sector Fock states can remain arbitrary and coherent. This observation does not make them fresh between calls.

## 3. Toeplitz–Cuntz normal forms and what they supply

Neal J. Fowler, *States of Toeplitz–Cuntz Algebras*, Journal of Operator Theory 42 (1999), 121–144.

Primary source:

https://www.theta.ro/jot/archive/1999-042-001/1999-042-001-006.pdf

Section 1 describes the universal algebra of isometries with mutually orthogonal ranges, its dense span of words `v_mu v_nu^*`, and its faithful irreducible full-Fock representation. The defect `p=I-sum_i v_i v_i^*` generates the compact ideal; `L_mu P_empty L_nu^*` are the rank-one word matrix units. These are precisely the relations needed above. The Cuntz quotient sets the vacuum defect to zero; doing so here would remove a real device operation and is not justified.

Relevant primary passages read: pp. 124–125, Section 1, including the Fock representation and compact matrix units. Browser refs: `turn79view0`, `turn80view0`.

For each sign sector, write `alpha=cos(s)`, `beta=sin(s)`. The nonzero bath-valued matrix entries are

`(V_s^±)_{00} = i beta I ± alpha P_empty`,

`(V_s^±)_{0i} = alpha L_i`,

`(V_s^±)_{i0} = alpha L_i^*`,

`(V_s^±)_{ii} = i beta I`, for `i=1,2`.

The bath coefficient of external input/output words `a_1...a_n` and `b_1...b_n` is the ordered product

`(V_s^±)_{b_n,a_n} ... (V_s^±)_{b_1,a_1}`.

Therefore every finite-block coefficient is a finite polynomial in these creation/annihilation operators and the vacuum projection. Reduction uses `L_i^* L_j=delta_ij I` and `P_empty=I-L_1L_1^*-L_2L_2^*`. Boundary-supported terms have explicit word matrix units. Histories leading to the same bath operator must be summed coherently; they are not separate classical noise events.

One useful warning follows directly. Since the Fock algebra contains all word matrix units, its commutant on one Fock sector is scalar. On the original doubled bath, operators commuting with both creators and their adjoints must have the form `A ⊗ I_F`; commuting also with `B` requires `[A,sigma_x]=0`. Thus the full bath coefficient algebra has only the two-sector abelian commutant. There is no hidden noiseless quantum tensor factor of the bath obtainable just from this commutant. This does **not** rule out external multi-use codes or bound their rate.

## 4. Exact finite-block codes: noncommutative graph criterion

Runyao Duan, Simone Severini and Andreas Winter, *Zero-error communication via quantum channels, non-commutative graphs and a quantum Lovász theta function*, arXiv:1002.2514; journal version IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 59 (2013), 1164–1174.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1002.2514

Sections II–III use the operator system `S=span{E_i^* E_j}` of a finite-dimensional channel. A projection `P` supports an exactly correctable quantum code precisely when `P S P = C P`, the Knill–Laflamme condition. The paper's Lovász-type quantity bounds entanglement-assisted **zero-error** classical messages and has a semidefinite formulation. It is not a converse for approximate unassisted quantum capacity.

Read: operator-system construction and the quantum-independence criterion, particularly p. 6. Browser refs: `turn79view1`, `turn80view1`, `turn80view2`.

Application to this task requires the actual `n`-slot correlated channel with its original fixed initializer. The above Fock form is a way to generate its Kraus coefficient space exactly, without confusing coherent history expansion with a Kraus decomposition. Since the external `n`-slot domain and range are finite, its operator system is finite-dimensional even with infinitely many bath coordinates. One can then test proposed exact code subspaces algebraically. No claim that these tests will produce an optimal approximate code is made.

In particular, `S_n` need not equal `S_1^{tensor n}`. The paper's independent-product graph results cannot be applied by resetting the tree between slots or between test blocks. A finding that a small block has no nontrivial exact code would not close positive asymptotic approximate capacity.

### 7.1 The inherited coding theorem is already for correlated channels

Vittorio Giovannetti, Daniel Burgarth and Stefano Mancini, *Communication Through a Quantum Link*, Physical Review A 79, 012311 (2009), arXiv:0810.0170.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.0170

Definition 1 calls a causal sequence of channels perfect-memory when its exact dilation dimension `k_n` obeys `log(k_n)=o(n)`. Theorem 2 supplies zero-error quantum codes of dimension greater than `d^n/(k_n^4+k_n^2)` for sufficiently large `n`. It does not assume tensor powers, bath resets, or IID noise. Its finite-block proof uses only input dimension and the number of Kraus operators, so the same argument applies with an arbitrary finite input-subspace dimension `D_n` in place of `d^n`. Read Definition 1, Theorem 2 and its proof, pp. 2–3. Browser refs: `turn81view0`, `turn82view0`, `turn84view1`.

Emanuel Knill, Raymond Laflamme and Lorenza Viola, *Theory of Quantum Error Correction for General Noise*, Physical Review Letters 84, 2525 (2000), arXiv:quant-ph/9908066.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9908066

Theorems 3–4 give the underlying general code-existence argument: first find a large orthogonal family with vanishing off-diagonal matrix elements of an error operator space, then group diagonal expectation vectors using a convex-partition theorem. This is a general finite-dimensional algebraic argument, not an efficient decoder construction. Read the complete existence-of-large-codes passage. Browser refs: `turn81view1`, `turn82view1`.

Therefore the new mechanism, if the owner's proof closes, is the **critical-input restriction and uniform approximate environment compression for this fixed infinite reservoir**. Applying an inherited low-Kraus coding theorem after that step should not be presented as a new general coding theorem. The uncompressed device has not been shown to satisfy the exact perfect-memory definition on its whole qutrit input space.

### 7.2 Exact obligations at the compression interface

Here is a useful elementary formulation, derived in this review rather than attributed to the preceding papers. Let `V:H_in -> H_out ⊗ E` be an isometry, `dim(H_in)=D`, and let `P_E` have finite rank `k`. Put

`F = V^*(I ⊗ (I-P_E))V`, `a = Tr(F)/D`.

For `0<theta<1`, let `G` be the spectral subspace of `F` for eigenvalues at most `theta`. Since `F>=0`,

`dim(G) >= D(1-a/theta)`.

On this subspace, `T=(I ⊗ P_E)V|_G` has `A=T^*T>= (1-theta)I_G`. Define the comparison isometry

`V_tilde = T A^(-1/2)`.

It has environment dimension at most `k`, and

`||V|_G - V_tilde|| <= sqrt(theta)+1-sqrt(1-theta) <= 2sqrt(theta)`.

The first summand bounds the discarded orthogonal environment component; the second follows from the spectral calculus of `A`. The resulting channels differ by at most `4sqrt(theta)` in full diamond norm, including arbitrary reference entanglement. Thus `a=o(1)` and `log(k)=o(n)` suffice after taking, for example, `theta=sqrt(a)`. One then applies the inherited exact coding argument to the comparison channel and its decoder to the original channel. The compression and normalization are proof devices; no bath measurement, successful-outcome selection, physical bath filter or reset is authorized or required.

For the proposed critical type, the input dimension is

`D_n = binom(n,n/2) 2^(n/2)`,

and the critical product state assigns the type probability

`p_n = D_n 2^(-3n/2) = Theta(n^(-1/2))`.

The normalized type projector is bounded above by `eta_c^(tensor n)/p_n`. This is a valid operator domination, but it amplifies any estimated average overflow by `1/p_n`. For an arbitrary normal initializer with no tail-rate assumption, first compare the physical channel with a normalized finite vertex-coordinate cutoff initializer using their trace distance. Only then apply the type-domination estimate to that comparison channel. Dividing an uncontrolled original bath tail by `p_n` would create an unjustified convergence assertion. A cutoff increasing to the identity gives trace-norm convergence by normality; it does not impose a horizon-dependent physical initializer.

### 7.4 A fully bath-uniform block identity really can remove external memory

The owner also reported a candidate two-call encoded/decoded qubit erasure subchannel. This review does not certify the candidate's operator calculation or its erasure parameter. It does clarify the exact compositional condition needed before importing a memoryless erasure-code theorem.

Let a normal block channel be `W:T(A ⊗ E)->T(B ⊗ E)`. Suppose there is a channel `N:T(A)->T(B)` such that

`Tr_E W(rho_AE) = N(Tr_E rho_AE)`

for **every** joint normal input, including extension by arbitrary reference systems. Equivalently,

`W^*(X_B ⊗ I_E) = N^*(X_B) ⊗ I_E`.

Then repeated blocks have exactly the external channel `N^(tensor m)` on arbitrarily entangled block inputs, despite retaining the same bath. To prove this, test the final output against a product observable `X_1 ⊗ ... ⊗ X_m`. Pull `X_m ⊗ I_E` backwards through the final block using the displayed identity. It leaves `N^*(X_m)` on the final input and identity on the bath. That external factor commutes with all earlier block maps. Repeat backwards through the remaining blocks. The result is the tensor product of the `N^*(X_j)`, tensored with `I_E`. Product observables span the finite external operator space, so equality holds for all observables and all input states. Tensoring the identity by retained external references proves the corresponding equality as an external causal process, including interventions between blocks.

Checking the identity only at one fixed bath state is insufficient: bath correlations and altered later bath marginals would remain unaddressed. An identity for every bath density and every external product input can be extended by linearity and normality, but the full operator identity is the cleanest statement to prove. Any receiver-side auxiliary register discarded by the block decoder must be included in the definition of that decoder; this argument does not grant access to the physical bath.

The closest primary terminology is Tilo Eggeling, Dirk Schlingemann and Reinhard F. Werner, *Semicausal operations are semilocalizable*, Europhysics Letters 57, 782–788 (2002), arXiv:quant-ph/0104027.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0104027

Their Eq. (3) defines semicausality by precisely this Heisenberg invariant-subalgebra identity. Their principal factorization theorem uses finite-dimensional Stinespring arguments. The simple backward-peeling proof just given needs only the normal identity itself and finite external systems, so no extension of that factorization theorem to the infinite reservoir is being asserted. Read Section II, Eq. (3), and the assumptions in Section III. Browser refs: `turn84view0`, `turn85view0`.
