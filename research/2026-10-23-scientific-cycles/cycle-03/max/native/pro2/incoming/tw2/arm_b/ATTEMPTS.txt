FAN REDUCTION: FIRST INSUFFICIENT ESTIMATE

The cone over a path is a width-two graph. Projecting the leaf rows off the
apex row leaves two query coordinates. A lifted path matrix can cancel
every leaf action parallel to the apex. The naive remaining apex action
is -t^T BZ/r_0, however. Bounding this by ||t|| ||BZ||/r_0 can introduce a
factor of order sqrt(m), destroying the growing path operator lower bound.
Therefore a mere cone construction plus a norm estimate is not enough.

REPAIR THAT PRODUCED THE MAIN RESULT

The unwanted apex action has only TWO real coordinates. The path flow
depends linearly on c. Restrict c to the THREE lowest sine modes and take
a nonzero vector in the kernel of those two constraints. Every vector in
that space still has derivative energy of order m^(-2) times its squared
norm. Thus one can cancel the apex action exactly while retaining a slow
path flow. This is the load-bearing new step of the proof.

ROW-CAP ISSUE AND ITS REPAIR

Projected row norms can be arbitrarily small even when the original rows
are unit vectors. The path-flow action then contains inverse projected
norms, so an unqualified unit-norm path estimate would be invalid.

Instead, let delta be the smallest projected row norm. The slow-flow
witness gives a lower bound delta times a quantity of order sqrt(m).
A separate rank-one witness supported on an apex-leaf edge gives the
lower bound 1/delta. Taking the larger of the two yields an m^(1/4) lower
bound for every capped design. This does not reuse the invalid monotone
row-amplitude argument identified in the supplied source.

DEGENERACY AUDIT

If the apex row is zero, its diagonal is invisible. If adjacent projected
leaf rows are dependent, the three rows on their triangle are dependent,
and a nonzero rank-one matrix supported on that triangle is invisible.
Only after these cases are removed are determinants and projected row
norms divided by. Nonzero c yields nonzero B and nonzero D. The action of
the chosen nondegenerate witness is nonzero because a zero discrete
derivative with zero boundary values forces c=0.
