Independent check: cooled-product recycle quench
2026-10-21 UTC. Scope: the saved instantaneous-mixing calculation only.

BOTTOM LINE

The recycle algebra and external endpoint energy balance are consistent with
one kg fresh feed and one kg net 300 K gas product. There is no extra recoverable
energy credit from circulating product. Against the stated ideal once-through
reference with the SAME fresh-feed preheat cap, the three saved recycle cases
are slightly worse before pumping: net NOx is lower by 0.003917%, 0.007188%, and
0.014433% at 2700/2900/3100 K. This is a conditional ideal-reference comparison,
not proof that either quench device is physically realizable.

A finite-entrainment test is justified as a bounded diagnostic of chemistry,
mixing requirements, and the word "favorable". It is NOT justified as a search
for a free recycle heat benefit or as evidence of plant superiority. Instant
mixing has not been shown to upper-bound NOx yield. Finite mixing could in
principle create additional NOx before cooling, because the net NOx source is
not sign-definite. The parent's proposed single 2700 K, R=0.85 all-species
adiabatic-entrainment check addresses that precise unresolved point.

An additional independent necessary inventory bound is available without any
chemistry rerun: the prescribed 0.14 s downstream cooling residence requires
0.252/0.275/0.298 kg gas hold-up per kg/s of fresh feed. At 1 atm, the six-species
atom-constrained minimum cooler volume is 0.641/0.699/0.758 m3 per kg/s fresh.
These are lower bounds for the cooler alone, excluding the mixer, piping,
separator, pump and start-up/reset inventory. They exceed the specified heater
volume/flow of 0.1 m3 s/kg by factors 6.41/6.99/7.58. Matching the finite heater
alone does not match total plant volume or total gas inventory.

1. RECYCLE ACCOUNTING AND THE NECESSARY EXTERNAL ENERGY IDENTITY

Write r for returned kg per kg fresh, Yh for heater-out composition, Yp for the
final product composition, and h(T,Y) for specific enthalpy INCLUDING formation
enthalpy. Temperatures are Th, Tq=1700 K, and T0=300 K. The heater sees only fresh
feed; returned product joins downstream, not at the heater inlet.

Frozen, instantaneous, adiabatic mixing requires
  Ym = (Yh + r Yp)/(1+r)
  h(Th,Yh) + r h(T0,Yp) = (1+r) h(Tq,Ym).
Ideal-gas mixture enthalpy is linear in Y. Therefore the saved formula
  r = [h(Th,Yh)-h(Tq,Yh)]/[h(Tq,Yp)-h(T0,Yp)]
is exactly the required recycle ratio for that stipulated frozen mixer.
It does not double-count product or fresh feed.

Let F be the complete prescribed per-pass cooler chemistry map. The fixed
point is Yp = F(Ym). A downstream splitter sends one kg out and r kg back,
both with Yp. Hence net product is no(Yp), NOT (1+r) no(Yp). Equivalently,
  (1+r) no(Yp) - r no(Yp) = no(Yp).
The code uses this correct net definition. Endpoint mass-fraction sums agree
with unity to 1.6e-15; atom balances agree with fresh air to 1.3e-13 mol/kg.
Tiny negative radical values are numerical residue, not meaningful inventory.

Let QH+ and QH- be the heater's positive heating and removal, and Qpre the
internally recovered fresh-feed preheat. Let Qrej be all other rejected heat.
At closed steady state and zero mechanical work,
  QH+ - QH- = h(T0,Yp)-h(T0,Yfresh) + Qrej.
The r enthalpy terms cancel identically. Internal circulation cannot lower the
external requirement by being counted as a new feed, a new product, or a new
heat source. With mechanical work W and no export, the corresponding overall
identity includes +W on the external-input side and its ultimate destination.

Endpoint-only recomputation recovers the saved rejected heat to <=9.8e-9 J/kg.
The net chemical/300 K sensible energy increases are 93.715, 119.881, and
143.927 kJ/kg. Rejected heat is 1409.993, 1827.390, and 2353.730 kJ/kg.
These strong algebraic checks do NOT certify that the exchanger or prescribed
cooling can realize those endpoints.

2. NECESSARY CONDITION TO BEAT THE SAME-CAP ONCE-THROUGH REFERENCE

Let Q0 be the saved heater input shared with the once-through comparator,
n0 its final NOx mol/kg fresh, nR the recycle product, W the incremental
recycle work, and dQ the added heater input caused by lower attainable
fresh-feed preheat. In the stated energy-per-product accounting, superiority
requires
  nR/n0 > 1 + (W+dQ)/Q0.
This is necessary, not sufficient. If W=dQ=0, even an infinitesimal claimed
advantage requires nR>n0. All three saved cases fail that condition.

2700 K: nR=1.030562935; n0=1.030603304 mol/kg; Q0=1.503707825 MJ/kg.
2900 K: nR=1.318254037; n0=1.318348799 mol/kg; Q0=1.947271090 MJ/kg.
3100 K: nR=1.582604517; n0=1.582832975 mol/kg; Q0=2.497656689 MJ/kg.

For the illustrative 1% pressure-ratio increment, the ideal recycle pump alone
raises the recycle SEC penalty to 0.04961%, 0.04971%, and 0.05328%, respectively.
Those pressure ratios are NOT demonstrated losses. No geometry-independent
strictly positive pump-work lower bound follows from the current data. Also,
one joule of electricity and one joule of supplied heat are combined only in
the stated energy metric; this is not a full exergy/economic comparison.

The heat moved from Th down to 1700 K into the recycled stream is 1.2710,
1.5317, and 1.7947 MJ/kg fresh. Its eventual arrival at the cooler does NOT
allow extra credit after the fresh stream has already reached its 1700 K cap.
The remaining heat must be rejected or assigned an explicitly admitted useful
sink. A different useful sink would change the comparison and must be shared
fairly with the once-through reference.

3. PINCH AND PRESCRIBED-COOLING LIMITS

The current zero-K minimum sampled pinch occurs at a deliberately coincident
1700 K hot inlet / fresh outlet. With finite conductance, zero approach cannot
deliver the stipulated nonzero terminal heat flux. Under regular local heat
capacities, the conductance-area requirement contains integral dQ/(Th-Tc),
which diverges at that terminal endpoint. This is an infinite-area limit, not
an admitted finite heat exchanger. The construction already calls it such.

The sampled-gap check is sensible as a diagnostic, but it does not establish:
- a nonnegative temperature approach over the whole continuous path;
- monotone heat removal, rather than a local segment needing heat input;
- finite heat-transfer area or a physical mechanism for the specified 1e4 K/s;
- an independently verified once-through exchanger pinch;
- heat rejection to an available sink with its own approach and flow costs.

For the cooler, with beta=-dT/dt=1e4 K/s, the signed removed-heat rate per kg
fresh is
  dQ/dt = (1+r)[beta cp(T,Y) - sum_i h_i(T) dY_i/dt].
A simple passive hot-side composite curve requires this to be nonnegative,
or else heating segments must be included explicitly. The saved result.json
does not contain the hot composition/full cooling path, so this independent
check cannot certify that condition or continuum pinch without additional
saved trajectory data. No claim that the existing cooler actually violates it
is made. The reported external residual is an identity and cannot test it.

As a coarse total-heat diagnostic, reconstructed once-through available heat
using the recycle endpoint as a clearly labelled proxy exceeds fresh preheat
by 139/296/559 kJ/kg. Thus there is no obvious gross total-heat deficit in the
ordinary reference. This proxy is NOT an independent pinch certificate.

4. WHY INSTANT MIXING IS NOT A PROVED PRODUCT UPPER BOUND

In the supplied reversible mechanism, number the reactions as paper_model.py.
Summing NO and NO2 removes their interconversion reactions R6 and R7. The
chemical NOx source is proportional to signed net rates
  R3 + R4 - R5,
from N2+O <=> NO+N, O2+N <=> NO+O, and NO+M <=> N+O+M.
It has no universal sign. A state containing N and O2 and no initial NO has a
positive NOx-production route, for example. This is a structural counterexample
to a general monotone-destruction assertion, not a simulated counterexample
for the three saved hot states. Frozen instantaneous mixing eliminates both
formation and destruction during that interval. It is mechanically optimistic
but is not necessarily chemically optimal.

Consequently, the saved lack of energy headroom parks a superiority claim for
these ideal rows, but does not prove that every finite-mixing trajectory has
less NOx. The proposed one bounded finite-entrainment check is a legitimate
way to test the missing assumption while preserving previous numerical work.

For R=0.85, do not prescribe 1700 K as an independent outcome. Adiabatic mixing
and its chemistry must determine Tmix. Limit fresh preheat to min(Tmix,1700 K),
with any finite exchanger approach reducing it further. The cooler must start
at actual Tmix. Added heater input is
  dQ = h(1700,Yfresh) - h(Tpreheat,Yfresh)
relative to the current cap, assuming the same fresh composition and heater
chemistry. Close all species and the total enthalpy returned at 300 K.

For a finite entraining material element of mass m, correct adiabatic flux
bookkeeping is d(m h)/dt = h_recycle dm/dt, together with species entrainment
and reaction. Using constant m while adding recycle would lose enthalpy and
inventory. Any prescribed cooling heat after mixing is an external transfer,
not automatically recovered energy. Preserve signed trial-state treatment,
check accepted physical states, and report any integration failure as a
numerical failure rather than physical exclusion.

A useful stopping condition is one bounded mixing-time sensitivity with
closure and repeatable endpoint quantities, showing whether finite chemistry
moves NOx up or down and quantifying preheat, inventory, and energy penalties.
It must not expand into an unbounded geometry or kinetics search merely
because the result differs from the instantaneous row. A chemistry increase
alone is not an operational advantage: the necessary condition in section 2
still applies, and a stronger matched-capacity ordinary comparator remains
necessary for a future superiority claim.

5. INDEPENDENT INVENTORY/VOLUME LOWER BOUND

The prescribed cooler takes tau=(1700-300)/1e4 = 0.14 s per pass. Its mass flow
is (1+r) mdot_fresh. Thus the steady cooler mass inventory is exactly
  M_cool/mdot_fresh = (1+r) tau,
assuming every through-flow element follows that residence-time path. It is
not merely r tau and cannot be normalized by the larger loop flow while
calling it fresh-feed-specific inventory.

For the specified six species, every molecule satisfies
  1 >= (number of N atoms)/2 + (number of O atoms)/4.
For the conserved dry-air atom totals this implies total amount of gas
  n_total >= n_N_atoms/2 + n_O_atoms/4 = 31.021842 mol/kg.
The bound is attainable compositionally by N2/NO2 at this N-rich atom ratio;
there is no assertion it is chemically attainable on the present trajectory.
Ideal-gas volume at P=101325 Pa therefore obeys
  V_cool/mdot_fresh >= (1+r) [R_universal (31.021842 mol/kg)/P]
                       integral_0^tau T(t) dt.
The temperature integral is 140 K s, yielding the 0.641/0.699/0.758 m3 s/kg
bounds stated above. This is stronger than a molecular-weight-only bound and
needs no reaction solve or missing temperature/composition trajectory.

It is a necessary bound for the specified six-species, 1-atm, prescribed
cooling model, not for an arbitrary mechanism, pressure, cooling rate, or
physical device. The finite R=0.85 case with a different Tmix needs its own
updated tau and temperature integral, plus its finite mixer inventory.

A recycle fixed point closes species at steady state, not a complete physical
operation. Start-up gas, shutdown/purge, material temperature histories,
recirculation equipment, gas/solid contact, finite heat sink, pressure work,
and chemical/thermal reset remain unqualified. N2O and surface chemistry are
not admitted. There is no novelty or surface-admission claim in this check.
