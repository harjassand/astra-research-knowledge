No equation defect was found. No heater, mixer, cooler, or recycle trajectory

1. Exact variable-mass, constant-pressure derivation

Let x=t/tau, species masses mi, total m=sum mi, Y=mi/m, and rho=P/(Rmix T).
Let wi be the volumetric mass-production rate (kg/m3/s), R=0.85 the final
entrained mass per kg initial hot gas, and Yr the returned composition.
Constant entrainment per normalized time gives
  dmi/dx = R Yr,i + tau (m/rho) wi.
Mass conservation of chemistry implies dm/dx=R. The pressure work associated
with expansion is included by using total enthalpy H=sum mi hi(T), rather
than internal energy. Adiabatic entrainment at 300 K requires
  dH/dx = R hr,
  dH/dx = sum hi dmi/dx + m cp dT/dx.
Consequently
  dT/dx = [R(hr-sum hi Yr,i)-tau(m/rho) sum hi wi]/(m cp),
which is precisely the implemented expression. Reaction formation enthalpy
is present through hi. There is no additional reaction-heat term to add.

This is a perfectly homogeneous, constant-pressure, variable-mass material
model. Momentum/kinetic energy, inlet jets, entrainment hardware, spatial
segregation, pressure drop and wall contact are not resolved. A modeled bulk
exit temperature below 1700 K does not bound hot filaments or local wall
exposure in an actual quench.

4. Meaning of the finite sweep

The parent's saved R=0.85 cases give:
- 10 us: Tmix=1666.459 K, net NOx=1.03050330 mol/kg fresh,
  ideal heater heat=1.499454 MJ/mol NOx.
- 100 us: Tmix=1682.496 K, net NOx=1.02786599 mol/kg fresh,
  ideal heater heat=1.484016 MJ/mol NOx.
- 300 us: Tmix=1703.097 K, net NOx=1.02265390 mol/kg fresh.
- 500 us: Tmix=1713.228 K, net NOx=1.01811735 mol/kg fresh.
- 1 ms: Tmix=1722.423 K, net NOx=1.00870868 mol/kg fresh.
- 10 ms: Tmix=1728.619 K, net NOx=0.92959733 mol/kg fresh.

Only the first two listed points pass the modeled 1700 K exit-temperature
condition. They still do not admit surfaces or a finite plant. The 300-us and
slower rows cannot be treated as cold-wall <=1700 K constructions without an
additional qualified quench step. Their reported heat quotients are not such
an admission.

The 100-us and 300-us observations straddle 1700 K in this model. They are not
a proof of a unique crossing, a global maximum allowable mixing time, or a
physical mixing requirement. The tolerance comparison at 100 us (rtol2e-8 to
2e-9) changes Tmix by 3.14e-7 K and NOx by 2.83e-10 mol/kg, a narrow repeatability
check, not a formal global error bound.

The finite cases show no energy advantage over the ideal same-cap once-through
reference of 1.459056 MJ/mol. Over this small tested set, longer entrainment
also reduces product NOx. The nonmonotonic heat quotient arises because some
chemical heat release raises Tmix and permits more feed preheat; it is not
new net energy. Increasing Tmix ultimately crosses the imposed inlet cap.
The appropriate conclusion is a bounded diagnostic result and a parked
physical-superiority claim, not a universal no-go theorem or justification
for an unbounded parameter search.
