RANK-TWO POSITIVE-MATRIX OBSTRUCTION
Date: 2026-10-22 Australia/Brisbane
Owner: bounded analytical helper /root/cycle2_nonstabilizer_probe/rank_two_check

Question and scope
------------------
Can positive coefficient matrices of rank at most two, with arbitrary actual
global eigenvectors, produce a witness gap growing with the prefix horizon N?
Answer: no. A direct positive partial-trace estimate bounds their full score by
a geometric series, uniformly in N. This is a construction restriction, not a
proof of the unrestricted critical tensor witness. No product eigenbasis,
stabilizer structure, compatibility relaxation, or numerical search is used.

Normalization
-------------
There are r task blocks, each with N matrix factors of dimension D >= 2.
Let rho be a density matrix on their total matrix Hilbert space and A=sqrt(rho).
For S=(a,prefix i), define the actual Bell-prefix probability

 p_(a,i) = D^(-i) Tr[(Tr_S A)^2],       1 <= i <= N.

Thus A is positive, Tr(A^2)=1, and S(rho) is the entropy of the pure bipartite
coefficient vector. In the normalized-trace convention of the owner prompt,
rho=X^2/Tr(X^2) for X>=0; this is just a change of normalization.
All logarithms below are natural. Put m=ln D and

 R = (Tr sqrt(rho))^2 = exp(S_(1/2)(rho)).

R is the effective rank of order one half. It is <= rank(rho).

Lemma 1: geometric prefix bound
--------------------------------
For every task and prefix, including global eigenvectors entangled between
tasks,

 p_(a,i) <= min{1, R D^(-i)}.                         (1)

Proof. B=Tr_S A is positive and Tr B=Tr A. Its nonnegative eigenvalues obey
Tr B^2 <= (Tr B)^2=R, proving the second bound. To see the first bound without
assuming the coefficient-vector interpretation, normalized partial trace is an
orthogonal projection in Hilbert--Schmidt space: E_S(A)=I_S/D^i tensor Tr_S A,
so ||E_S A||_2^2=D^(-i)Tr B^2 <= ||A||_2^2=1. QED.

For arbitrary nonnegative prefix weights w_(a,i), the same proof gives

 sum_(a,i) w_(a,i) p_(a,i)
   <= sum_(a,i) w_(a,i) min{1,R D^(-i)}.              (2)

In particular

 m sum_(a,i) p_(a,i) - S(rho)
   <= r m R (1-D^(-N))/(D-1) - S(rho).              (3)

Rank two
--------
Suppose rank(rho)<=2. Write its nonzero eigenvalues as lambda,1-lambda,
allowing lambda=0 or 1. Their eigenvectors are arbitrary actual orthogonal
vectors on all r tasks; no assumption about their reductions is made. Then

 R=1+2 sqrt(lambda(1-lambda)) <=2,
 S(rho)=h_2(lambda).

Consequently

 m sum_(a,i) p_(a,i) - S(rho)
 <= [r m (1-D^(-N))/(D-1)] [1+2 sqrt(lambda(1-lambda))]
       - h_2(lambda)
 <= 2 r m/(D-1).                                    (4)

For the requested two-task case r=2, this is <=4 ln D/(D-1), independently of
N. It is <=4 ln 2 for every integer D>=2. Therefore no sequence of genuine
positive rank-one or rank-two matrices can have an unbounded two-task witness
defect, irrespective of how globally nonstabilizer its eigenvectors become.

The constant in (4) is only a safe bound; it is not asserted optimal. No claim
is made that it equals the previously proved single-chain C_D. Improving this
bounded constant does not answer the owner's collective-preparation frontier.

For rank one the exact maximum is elementary: each term is <=D^(-i), and a
pure rho that is product across all matrix factors attains every such bound.
Hence its maximum defect equals r m(1-D^(-N))/(D-1).

Lemma 2: effective-rank version
--------------------------------
Equation (1) also gives the more useful bound

 m sum_(a,i) p_(a,i)
 <= r [ln R + m/(D-1)],

and therefore

 m sum_(a,i) p_(a,i) - S(rho)
 <= r S_(1/2)(rho) - S(rho) + r m/(D-1).             (5)

Proof. Extending the positive sum to all i>=1, write R=D^k a where
k=floor(log_D R) and 1<=a<D. Then

 sum_(i>=1) min{1,R D^(-i)} = k + a/(D-1).

The convex function a m/(D-1)-ln a has equal endpoint values m/(D-1)
at a=1,D, so is at most m/(D-1) on [1,D]. Multiplying the preceding sum by m
proves the assertion. If k>=N the infinite-series bound remains valid. QED.

Consequences for an actual construction search
---------------------------------------------
* Any fixed spectral-rank class is excluded from producing divergence. More
  generally, bounded S_(1/2) suffices to exclude divergence for fixed r.
* A divergent positive physical family must have
  r S_(1/2)(rho_N)-S(rho_N) unbounded. This is a necessary condition only.
* Large Hilbert-space dimension and strongly entangled eigenvectors alone are
  insufficient. The square-root spectral mass must also spread over an
  unbounded number of eigenvectors. A low-rank nonstabilizer optimizer cannot
  test the actual divergent frontier.
* Equation (5) is a coarse scalar-envelope consequence. It does not exploit
  the common Gram dilation and cannot close the general tensor theorem. In
  particular its right side can grow for flat, growing-rank spectra when r>1.

Cost and status
---------------
This is a finite algebraic proof. No numerical computation or optimization was
performed; tool use only located the task paths and wrote this note. Historical
originality is not claimed. The direct trace and projection estimates are
standard. Internal correctness is limited to the explicit finite matrix model;
there is no physical preparation or experimental-access claim.

Receiving-use instruction
-------------------------
Use this result to reject rank<=2 (or any fixed-rank) positive constructions as
divergent candidates. Retain them only as admission checks or fixed-size
countertests. For a consequential physical countersequence, move to genuinely
growing effective rank; do not present failure of low-rank numerics as evidence
for the unrestricted theorem.
