HISTORICAL SCIENTIFIC-REASONING OBLIGATION — NOT A NEW MORREY PROGRAMME

Let r>=1. Let v_1,...,v_(r+1) be nonzero vectors spanning R^r. Let N have these vectors as columns, and assume ker N=span{k} for a vector k whose entries are all strictly positive. For t in R set t_-=max(-t,0). Define Phi(z)=sum_i (v_i·z)_-^2.

Determine whether there always exist theta_i>=0, sum_i theta_i=1, such that
    sum_i theta_i (v_i·z)^2 <= Phi(z) for every z in R^r.

Give a complete construction and proof, or a genuine counterexample. Cover arbitrary r, unequal coefficients already absorbed into v_i, zero theta entries, and a possibly singular matrix S=sum_i theta_i v_i v_i^T. A formula supported only by numerical cases is not a solution. If full resolution is not reached, state the exact strongest lemma obtained and the remaining gap.

The reason this matters is that, for an arbitrary common quadratic Q, such an S makes Q+S a convex combination of the local quadratics Q+(v_i·z)^2 and simultaneously a global lower bound on Q+Phi. That would connect local admissibility to a global certificate in a broader mathematical construction. No knowledge of that construction is needed for the stated problem.
