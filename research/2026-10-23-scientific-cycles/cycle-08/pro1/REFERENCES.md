# Closest primary results and comparison boundary

The two primary papers below were read at their theorem statements for this
checkpoint. They delimit the ordinary coding machinery and its hypotheses;
this is not a broad historical-priority audit.

## Perfect-memory communication

Vittorio Giovannetti, Daniel Burgarth and Stefano Mancini,
**Communication Through a Quantum Link**, Physical Review A **79**, 012311
(2009), [arXiv:0810.0170](https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.0170).
Primary full text: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.0170).

Theorem 2 proves large zero-error quantum codes for perfect-memory channels,
with a code-dimension lower bound in terms of the effective environment
dimension. Subexponential growth of that dimension permits the full
asymptotic input rate. This is the closest ordinary compression mechanism.

The present model has an infinite bath and does not satisfy that theorem
merely by being a unitary memory model. We must select an actual high-entropy
input, control overflow of its evolving bath, pass to spectral type subspaces,
normalize the auxiliary channel, and pay the polar repair. Those complete
steps are inherited and reproduced here. The new contribution of this
checkpoint is the exact endpoint response and the resulting admissible block
scale. No new general perfect-memory coding theorem is claimed.

## Forgetful quantum memory

Dennis Kretschmann and Reinhard F. Werner,
**Quantum Channels with Memory**, Physical Review A **72**, 062323 (2005),
[arXiv:quant-ph/0502106](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0502106).
Primary full text: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0502106).

Theorem 13 gives the quantum-capacity expression for forgetful channels under
its finite-dimensional memory framework. The requisite forgetfulness and
finite-memory hypotheses have not been established for the present evolving
infinite tree bath. Consequently the physical blocks cannot be treated as
independent fresh channels or placed directly under that theorem.

## Same-premise internal comparator

The strongest retained explicit ordinary construction in this programme is
the actual nonscalar four-call state of Cycle5, improving the Cycle4 pair
code. Its original entropy and drift proofs remain in the exact preserved
chain. The present source compares against that construction, not merely an
iid input or an obsolete diagonal control.

For beta<=1/8, its entropy-rate excess is less than beta^4/256. At beta=1/512,
the new cubic lower bound exceeds this analytic upper bound on the old
explicit construction. It does not establish an optimum over every four-call
input, a full capacity formula, or feedback equality.

## Originality and usefulness

This focused comparison does not establish historical novelty of the exact
model, the all-order response identity, or the resulting cubic rate floor.
Internal proof review is not external certification. The code is existential:
efficient compilation, useful finite blocklength and physical realization
remain separate questions. No source-author outreach or public disclosure
was performed.
