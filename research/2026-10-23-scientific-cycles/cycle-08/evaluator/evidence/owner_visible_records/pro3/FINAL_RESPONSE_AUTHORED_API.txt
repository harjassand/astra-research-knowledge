## 1. The enlarged-class theorem

The scientific contract retains finite strictly positive \(\pi,q\), the single population bound
\[
D(\pi\|q)\le K,
\]
fixed \(K>0\), fixed \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\beta=1-\alpha\), proposal initialization \(q\), and the actual lazy mixture with independence MH.

The extension allows **any fixed target-independent row-stochastic \(Q\) with reciprocal off-diagonal support**. It need not be symmetric, reversible, doubly stochastic, or degree bounded. Both forward and reverse proposal probabilities must be evaluable at their actual cost.

For \(Q_{xy}>0\), define
\[
r_q(x,y)=\frac{q_yQ_{yx}}{q_xQ_{xy}},
\qquad
r_\pi(x,y)=\frac{\pi_yQ_{yx}}{\pi_xQ_{xy}},
\]
and
\[
\bar L_\eta(x,y)
=
Q_{xy}\min(1,r_q^\eta)
       \min(1,r_\pi/r_q^\eta).
\]
Its actual rejection diagonal completes the kernel, and
\[
\bar C_\eta=\frac{I+\alpha G+\beta\bar L_\eta}{2}.
\]

Although \(Q\) may be nonreversible, the resulting target kernel remains \(\pi\)-reversible. All kernels and \(\eta\) stay fixed within a run.

Let
\[
\bar R_\eta(n)=
\sup_{\text{enlarged admitted class}}
\|q\bar C_\eta^n-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}},
\qquad
r_0=\frac{\alpha e^{-K}}{\beta}.
\]

The complete critical laws retain the previous constants.

### Interior regime: \(r_0<1\)

Let \(c_*\in(0,1)\) solve
\[
F(c_*)=r_0,
\qquad
F(c)=c(1-c)^{(1-c)/c}.
\]
For fixed real \(s\),
\[
\eta_n\log n=\frac{\log\log n}{c_*}+s+o(1)
\]
implies
\[
\boxed{
(\log n)\bar R_{\eta_n}(n)
\longrightarrow
\max\left\{
K,\ (1-e^{-K})e^{-c_*s}
\right\}.
}
\]

### Global-dominant regime: \(r_0\ge1\), including equality

If
\[
\eta_n\log n=\log\log n+s+o(1),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
(\log n)\bar R_{\eta_n}(n)

\max\left\{
K,\ \frac{\beta}{\alpha}(e^K-1)e^{-s}
\right\}.
}
\]

Thus the symmetry assumption can be removed without changing the critical scale or amplitude. This conclusion covers growing finite supports and arbitrary admissible proposal geometry. It does not extend to nonreversible **target** kernels, within-run adaptation, deterministic compositions, empirical KL estimates, or implementation errors.

## 2. The new operation that makes the extension work

The old counting-column argument does fail for nonsymmetric proposals. I preserved an exact finite witness: an absorbed conjugated local column has sum **\(5>2\)**. Row stochasticity alone cannot control an incoming counting column.

The replacement uses **reverse proposal probabilities as Jensen coefficients**, and then retains the accepted outflow subtraction supplied by the true rejection diagonal.

Set
\[
A=\{W<h\},\quad B=A^c,\quad b=q(A),\quad E\subseteq B,\quad e=q(E).
\]
The auxiliary \(P^E\) keeps the original transitions from \(A\) into \(A\cup E\), kills exits to \(B\setminus E\), and absorbs \(E\), without normalizing any killed row. It remains a proof counter, not an alternative sampler.

For
\[
0<\eta<1,\qquad
0<\theta\le\eta,\quad \theta\le\tfrac12,\qquad
p=\frac1{1-\theta},
\]
write \(D=\operatorname{diag}(q^\theta)\) and
\[
\mathcal E_w(v)=\sum_{x\in A}v_x^p+
w\sum_{x\in E}v_x^p,\qquad w\ge1.
\]

For an off-diagonal transition, put \(a_{xy}=\bar L_\eta(x,y)/Q_{xy}\). The powered coefficient in the new Jensen calculation is exactly
\[
\frac{(D\bar L_\eta^ED^{-1})_{xy}^{\,p}}
     {Q_{yx}^{\,p-1}}
=
Q_{xy}a_{xy}^{p}r_q(x,y)^{-\theta p}.
\]

The coefficients \(Q_{yx}\), viewed down an output column, sum along an outgoing row of \(Q\). This supplies the required support-independent control. The diagonal then subtracts the actual accepted outflow, reducing the remaining local obligation to
\[
H_w=
\sup_{0<z\le1}
\left(wz^{\,p(1-\theta/\eta)}-z\right).
\]

The resulting local inequality is
\[
\mathcal E_w(vD\bar L_\eta^ED^{-1})
\le
2^{p-1}
\left[
(1+H_w)\sum_Av_x^p+
w\sum_Ev_x^p
\right].
\]

This is the weighted-energy statement that the pointwise off-diagonal observation did not establish.

Combining it with the separate \(q\)-density Jensen calculation for global IMH gives
\[
\mathcal E_w(vDP^ED^{-1})
\le
\Gamma_{\theta,w}(e)\,\mathcal E_w(v),
\]
where
\[
\Gamma_{\theta,w}(e)
=
\frac12+
\frac{2^{p-1}}2
\left[
1+\bigl(\beta H_w-\alpha(1-b)\bigr)_+
+\alpha we
\right].
\]
Consequently, the first-hit mass from \(q1_A\), accumulated over all hit times through \(N\), is bounded by
\[
\boxed{
\left(\frac bw\right)^{1-\theta}
e^\theta
\Gamma_{\theta,w}(e)^{N(1-\theta)}.
}
\]

Two identities connect this new operation to the accepted critical proof:

- For \(w=1\) and \(\theta=c\eta\), \(0<c<1\),
  \[
  H_1=
  F\!\left(\frac{c(1-\eta)}{1-c\eta}\right)
  \le F(c).
  \]
- At \(\theta=\eta\), the endpoint scalar is exactly
  \[
  H_w=w.
  \]

The first preserves the interior argument. The second preserves the weighted absorbing-boundary argument, including the reciprocal coefficient and the equality case \(r_0=1\).

The remaining interfaces are included in the proof: original full-chain propagation after every hit, accumulated multiplier control, smoothing, unhit mass, and the single information budget. In particular,
\[
k+a\log R
\le
K+(h+a)(k+1)+h\log(1/h),
\]
where \(k=-\log(1-b)\) and \(a=\pi(W>R)\). This couples low-weight capture and the high-weight tail; the two mechanisms cannot independently spend \(K\).

Matching lower bounds transfer by exact inclusion of the unchanged symmetric subclass, including its native transitions and diagonals. No one-step norm extremizer is substituted for reachable retained mass.

## 3. A further structural result: matched powers are necessary

The extension also exposes a distinction among reversible factorizations. Consider
\[
S_{xy}
=
\left(\frac{q_y}{q_x}\right)^\eta
\left(\frac{Q_{yx}}{Q_{xy}}\right)^\kappa,
\]
with acceptance
\[
Q_{xy}\min(1,S_{xy})
       \min(1,r_\pi/S_{xy}).
\]

Every such factorization preserves detailed balance. Nevertheless, over unrestricted reciprocal proposals, **fixed powers have uniform convergence exactly when they match**.

For fixed \(0<\eta<1\) and finite \(\kappa\ge0\), let \(R_{\eta,\kappa}(n)\) denote the corresponding worst-case error under the same contract. Then
\[
\kappa=\eta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\log n)R_{\eta,\eta}(n)\longrightarrow K.
\]

For every fixed mismatch,
\[
\boxed{
\kappa\ne\eta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\liminf_{n\to\infty}R_{\eta,\kappa}(n)
\ge
\frac{\beta(1-e^{-K})}
     {\alpha e^{-K}+\beta}
>0.
}
\]

The displayed positive floor is a **lower bound**, not a claimed exact minimax floor for the mismatched class.

### The actual adverse constructions

Fix
\[
0<b<1-e^{-K},
\qquad
e=\frac b{n^4},\quad
p=\frac1n,\quad
t=\frac1{n^3}.
\]
The root, leaf and core blocks have proposal masses
\[
(b,e,1-b-e)
\]
and target masses
\[
(t,p,1-p-t).
\]

Let
\[
d=|\eta-\kappa|,
\qquad
m=\left\lceil n^{4\eta/d}\right\rceil.
\]

There are two geometries:

| Mismatch | Native proposal geometry |
|---|---|
| \(\eta>\kappa\) | \(m\) roots each propose the single leaf with probability one; the leaf proposes each root with probability \(1/m\). |
| \(\kappa>\eta\) | One root proposes each of \(m\) leaves with probability \(1/m\); each leaf proposes the root with probability one. |

The core proposes itself locally. These are reciprocal row-stochastic proposals, fixed within each run and chosen without target evaluations.

Computing **per-vertex** ratios gives, in both cases,
\[
r_q=n^{-4},\qquad r_\pi=n^2,
\qquad
S=n^{-4\eta}m^d.
\]
The ceiling choice ensures
\[
1\le S\le2^d.
\]
Thus, eventually, the actual local transition accepts forward with probability one and reverse with probability \(n^{-2}\).

Multiplicity does not add an information-budget charge:
\[
D(\pi\|q)
=
t\log\frac tb+
p\log\frac pe+
(1-p-t)\log\frac{1-p-t}{1-b-e}
\longrightarrow-\log(1-b)<K.
\]

The exact native quotient retains all same-block moves. In the one-to-many construction, retention concerns the **entire leaf block**, including global moves between different leaves. Treating one leaf’s diagonal as block retention would be incorrect.

A full-law sandwich, retaining every return after the first core hit, proves
\[
\|qC^n-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\longrightarrow
\frac{b\beta}{\alpha(1-b)+\beta}.
\]
Taking fixed \(b\uparrow1-e^{-K}\) after the limit gives the stated uniform-floor lower bound.

### What happens when the mismatch tends to zero?

The same ceiling argument remains valid for moving powers with bounded absolute mismatch. If
\[
\sup_n|\eta_n-\kappa_n|<\infty
\]
and the powers differ at infinitely many horizons, a positive lower bound survives along that mismatched subsequence. Uniform convergence over the unrestricted class therefore fails—even if the nonzero mismatch tends to zero.

This has an explicit resource boundary:
\[
\log m
\asymp
\frac{\eta\log n}{|\eta-\kappa|}.
\]
Small mismatch can require enormous support and long state indices. The result is not a fixed-graph instability theorem or a statement about ordinary floating-point roundoff.

For moving policies, eventual exact matching is only a **necessary** condition in this result. A matched screen can still vanish too quickly. Unbounded moving mismatch and an exact optimum for the mismatched-class floor remain outside this checkpoint.
