INDEPENDENT QUANTUM AUDIT — ADAPTIVE Z-PARITY AFFINE EXTRACTION
22 October 2026

Verdict: PASS with a necessary syndrome convention. The prefix syndromes
must be adjusted using the subsequently measured tail bits. Raw Alice and
Bob parity outcomes need not agree when a probe row extends beyond K.
Conditioning on the full classical transcript preserves uniform coherence
on the resulting affine fiber. The complete success block factors from
every remaining reference once the fine transcript is fixed.

MODEL AND FINE TRANSCRIPT

Fix a source inventory K in {1,...,L}. Its quantum output is

  omega_K=Phi_2^tensor K tensor nu^tensor(L-K),  nu=I_4/4.

Let an arbitrary remaining reference R include the terminal memory, the
classical K record, and any purification or other extension of the private
noise. Conditional on K, the first K Bell pairs are pure and hence factor
from that reference and all remaining registers in every extension.

Both parties perform the LUDERS projective measurement of the SAME binary
Z-parity row h_j in
round j. A row, the stopping rule, and the final cut c may depend on past
classical parity outcomes and public coins. Fix the complete public-coin
realization and fine parity transcript. Its path fixes q rows and a
q-by-L binary matrix H, together with Alice and Bob syndrome vectors
a,b in F_2^q. The rows have zero first column and are supported on the
declared probed coordinates. The transcript then fixes c, with 1<=c<=L.
Finally both parties measure all coordinates >c in the Z basis, retaining
their complete outcomes z_A,z_B in F_2^(L-c).

The LUDERS instrument matters, not merely its two projector-valued effects.
It has an explicit admissible implementation: prepare a local |0> ancilla,
CNOT every row-selected data qubit into it, measure only that ancilla in Z,
and retain its outcome. The data Kraus operators are precisely the two
parity projectors. Measuring every selected data qubit separately and
reporting only their parity has the same coarse POVM effects but destroys
within-fiber coherence, so it would not satisfy this lemma.

All outcome records are genuinely classical. No additional unrecorded
state-dependent measurement or quantum-coherent choice of row is admitted.

CORRECT AFFINE CONSTRAINT

Write H=[H_c | H_tail] according to the retained prefix and measured tail.
Define the adjusted syndromes

  s_A=a+H_tail z_A,       s_B=b+H_tail z_B,                  (1)

with all arithmetic in F_2. On any nonzero fine branch with c<=K,

  s_A=s_B=:s,
  A={x in F_2^c:H_c x=s} is nonempty.                       (2)

If r=rank(H_c), then |A|=2^(c-r), and the conditional prefix state is
exactly

  |Psi_(H_c,s)> = 2^(-(c-r)/2) sum_(x in A)|x>_A|x>_B.      (3)

It is pure and factors from every remaining reference, conditioned on
the fine transcript and success c<=K.

WHY THE RAW SYNDROME CONVENTION WOULD BE FALSE

Take L=2,K=1,c=1 and the row h=(0,1), which probes only private noise.
The outcomes a=0,b=1 have positive probability. The first pair remains
Phi_2, while H_c=0. Thus the raw equations H_c x=a and H_c x=b are neither
equal nor both consistent. After tail Z readout, z_A=0,z_B=1 on this
branch, and (1) correctly gives s_A=s_B=0. Any root statement using raw
syndromes must be corrected or must explicitly ensure every probe row is
supported entirely within the final cut.

PROOF OF (2)-(3), INCLUDING ADAPTIVITY AND REFERENCES

For a fixed fine adaptive transcript, its Kraus operator is the product
of the fixed-path diagonal parity projectors. All these projectors commute.
The fact that later projectors were selected from earlier outcomes does
not add a new constraint: after conditioning on the complete path, the
operator is precisely the product for that path. Public-coin probabilities
are scalar factors independent of x within the path.

The final local Z projectors on coordinates >c also commute with every
parity projector. Move them first for this branch calculation. Because
c<=K, any nonzero tail outcome satisfies

  z_A,i=z_B,i for c<i<=K.

After that tail outcome, the first c pairs are still Phi_2^tensor c and
factor from a conditional reference state rho_R. This uses only purity
of the original Bell prefix; it does not assume the private noise lacks
a purification. Projecting its measured registers onto fixed basis vectors
changes rho_R but cannot make it depend on the still unmeasured x.

The remaining parity projectors are exactly the diagonal prefix tests
H_c x=s_A on Alice and H_c x=s_B on Bob. Since the unmeasured Bell prefix
has the matched expansion 2^(-c/2) sum_x |x,x>, a nonzero result requires
s_A=s_B and a consistent system. These projectors keep every x in the
affine fiber with the same amplitude and phase. Normalization gives (3),
and the reference factor remains rho_R. Dependent rows merely reduce r;
they create no nonuniform weights.

The chosen cut c adds no further measurement on this fiber, because it is
a classical function of already fixed outcomes and coins. Therefore the
argument also covers adaptive stopping, repeated rows, and row supports
extending beyond the final cut.

For a source with exactly independent nu tails, the probability of a
compatible fixed fine branch given K, apart from its fixed public-coin
weight, is

  2^(c-r-K) 4^(-(L-K)).                                    (4)

There is no x-dependent factor. Formula (4) is only a cross-check; the
reference-factor argument above establishes the stronger quantum claim.

LOCAL AFFINE NORMAL FORM AND PROTECTED FIRST PAIR

Gaussian elimination computes a particular solution x_0 and an invertible
binary linear map that sends ker(H_c) to a coordinate subspace. Apply the
same affine basis permutation to Alice and Bob using local X and CNOT
gates. It maps (3) exactly to

  Phi_2^tensor(c-r) tensor |00><00|^tensor r.                (5)

One need not touch coordinate 1: H has zero first column, so
ker(H_c)=span(e_1) direct-sum a subspace of coordinates 2,...,c, and a
particular solution can be chosen with first coordinate zero. Run the
elimination on coordinates 2,...,c and keep the first Bell pair in place.
Thus r<=min(q,c-1), and c-r>=1. Measured tail registers can be reset to
zero by local X gates controlled on their retained Z outcomes.

The protected first Bell pair is in fact unchanged on failure branches
c>K as well: no probe row or later normalization gate need act on it,
and c>=1 prevents it from being tail-measured. The remaining success
conclusion (3)-(5) is not asserted on those failure branches.

COMPLETE CQ/REFERENCE STATEMENT

Let T be the full fine classical transcript, including public coins, both
parties' parity outcomes, the resulting cut and rows, both complete tail
Z records, and any normalization choices. Let G be the event c(T)<=K.
For each positive-probability t, let f(t)=c(t)-rank(H_c(t)). After the
declared local affine normalization and resets, the success block has the
exact unnormalized form

  sum_t p(t,G) |t><t|_T tensor
       [Phi_2^tensor f(t) tensor |00><00|^tensor(L-f(t))]
       tensor rho_(R|t,G).                                (6)

This holds even when success is a mathematical event defined using a
retained classical K register that the receiver never reads. For fixed t,
the pure output in (6) is independent of the actual K among successful
inventories. Any correlations with K, terminal memory, measurement copies,
or the private-noise purification are confined to rho_(R|t,G).

The full state additionally has the positive failure block

  sum_t p(t,G^c)|t><t|_T tensor zeta_(output,R|t,G^c).         (7)

It must be retained and charged. Averaging only (6) is postselection and
does not describe the complete protocol. If one defines a comparison state
that replaces the output in (7) by the same ideal fixed-t pure state while
retaining its reference marginal, complete half trace distance is at most
Pr(G^c); no independence on failure branches is implied.

SCOPE / REQUIRED ROOT WORDING

1. State the shifted-syndrome convention (1) explicitly unless all probe
   rows lie in the retained prefix.
2. Specify the coherent-ancilla/Luders parity instrument above; the parity
   POVM effects alone do not establish the postmeasurement pure state.
3. Rows must be common to both parties on each fine classical path.
   Arbitrary different local row matrices require intersecting two systems
   and a revised rank bound.
4. All transcript branches, tail readouts and their retained copies count.
   The lemma does not prove a small failure probability, a cheap classical
   search, a memory return bound, or an ordinary-stock supply saving.
5. Use (6)+(7), not an unconditional pure-state assertion obtained from
   the success calculation.
