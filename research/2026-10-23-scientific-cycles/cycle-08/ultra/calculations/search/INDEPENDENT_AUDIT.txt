1. FRACTIONAL MEDIAN: AGGREGATE WEIGHTS ARE SUFFICIENT

Let C be cumulative weight before atom h, let w be its weight, and put
alpha=(W/2-C)/w in [0,1]. A uniform density on the atom places exactly alpha*w
below x and (1-alpha)*w above x. The YES multiplier on this atom is
1-alpha/2, and the NO multiplier is (1+alpha)/2. Re-uniformizing afterward
changes the algorithmic measure but preserves these aggregate weights. It does
not need to preserve any posterior interpretation.

The total weight after either answer is W/2 + (W/2)/2 = 3W/4. Multiplying all
new weights by 4 gives exactly the displayed integer formulas. Both median-atom
new weights lie between 2w and 4w, so they remain positive. The query-rounding
rule alpha<=1/2 iff W-2C<=w is correct. Exact median endpoints are harmless:
if alpha is 0 or 1, the stated endpoint query and update remain valid.

Clarification recommended: the potential equations W_t=n(3/4)^t and
w_K>=2^(-G-E) use UNRESCALED weights. The stored integer weights are 4^t times
those weights and have total n*3^t. The algorithm and proof are equivalent after
this deterministic rescaling; their notation should state this explicitly.

2. GOOD-QUERY POTENTIAL

For the general good interval [K,K+b+1], a non-good left query has j<K and
median atom h<=j<K. A non-good right query has j>K+b+1 and h>=j-1>K. Thus the
target atom is not split, and lies entirely on the side favored by the correct
answer. Its multiplier is one on a correct answer and one half on a wrong
answer. On good queries its multiplier is at least one half.

For b>=1, [K,K+b] is already sufficient: a non-good right query has
h>=j-1>=K+b>=K+1. For b=0 the larger [K,K+1] is required and is supplied.
Therefore w_K>=2^(-G-E), and w_K<=W implies equation (1) with the stated sign.

Each logical query consumes four fresh raw sources. On a non-good right query,
conditional probability of all four erroneous YES results is at most D^-4;
non-good left errors have probability zero. This remains valid with adaptive
thresholds, arbitrary behavior inside the band, and additional previous local
outcomes in the conditioning history.

Writing I_t for the indicator of a non-good wrong logical answer, its
conditional mean is at most 1/16. Iterating the conditional exponential moment
bound gives domination by Binomial(T,1/16). Hoeffding hence gives
Pr(E>T/8)<=exp(-T/128). On E<=T/8, log_2 n<=T/32 and
log_2(4/3)>13/32 yield G>=T/4. The strict numerical inequality is elementary:
2^51>3^32. These conclusions do not assume that the good queries are known.

3. AT MOST NINE QUANTILE CANDIDATES

Ranks 1+floor(l*T/8), capped at T, for l=0,...,8 contain the two endpoints and
have successive gaps at most ceil(T/8). All queries in any fixed integer
interval occupy a contiguous set of sorted ranks. For T>=32, a cluster of at
least T/4 ranks is too large to fit strictly between successive selected ranks.
Thus at least one selected candidate lies in the good interval. Duplicate
candidate values or capped ranks can be removed without changing this fact.

4. VALIDATION AND THE ALL-CANDIDATE REQUIREMENT

In the b>=1 version, a good candidate j is validated at
min(L+1,j+b+1). Without capping, this is at least K+b+1, hence strictly outside
the arbitrary band. With capping it is the known synthetic NO. Its s fresh
validation outcomes therefore fail to contain any NO with probability at most
D^-s. Every NO, including the synthetic one, gives j_NO>K, so the maintained
U is always at least K. A successful good-candidate validation gives
U<=j+b<=K+2b; the cap only improves this bound.

The choice of a good candidate in this proof may depend on K as well as the
first-phase transcript. This is an analysis choice only: the receiver need not
know which candidate is good because it executes ALL validations. Conditioning
on fixed K and that transcript is legitimate, and intervening validations do
not spoil the next fresh-color conditional bound. There is no missing union
bound over the at most nine candidates. First-phase failure costs at most
epsilon/2, and failure of this selected good validation costs at most epsilon/2.
The version with general good interval [K,K+b+1], validation j+b+2 and final
K+2b+2 follows by the same calculation.

5. DETERMINISTIC COLORS: CONDITIONAL LAW AND FRESHNESS

Conditional on the queue inventory K, the actual output source is a tensor
product across physical positions. The computational-basis differences vanish
on Bell positions and are independent uniform F_D variables on nu positions.
Each raw query acts only on positions of its assigned color. Before that
color's first query, its entire source factor is untouched. Conditioning on
all earlier local parity outcomes and their adaptive thresholds acts only on
other color factors. It therefore leaves the new color's conditional state
unchanged. This is a quantum tensor-factor statement; it does not assume
undisturbed individual Bell states after previous parity measurements.

For physical j>K+Q, the interval (K,j] contains every cyclic color at least
once. Since the current color has never been used, a uniform tail difference
of that color enters the parity with nonzero coefficient one. Summing it with
other independent variables is exactly uniform in F_D. For j<=K the parity
difference is zero. These facts establish the oracle conditional on the full
previous classical transcript and fixed K. They do not require a public random
row seed or knowledge of K by the receiver.

The source-specific proof does rely on the prime-power field and the displayed
product maximally mixed tail. It does not automatically apply to correlated
nuisance tails, a different source decomposition, reused colors, or arbitrary
epsilon-close initializers. Those restrictions are correctly retained.

6. BUDGET AND COMPUTATIONAL CLAIMS

T logical queries use at most 4T raw queries. There are at most nine candidate
validations of s raw queries each. Hence at most Q=4T+9s colors are needed;
synthetic queries or early decisions can only reduce actual use. The protocol
should assign a never-used color to every actual raw call (skipping colors for
synthetic calls is also safe). No physical position belongs to two raw query
supports. Consequently the parity stage touches each position at most once per
party even though threshold choices are adaptive.

The displayed integer update has bit length O(T+log L). An O((L+1)T) integer-
operation implementation is consistent with a linear scan for each median and
full weight update; a bit-operation claim must additionally charge arithmetic
on those integers. The source text already distinguishes integer operations
from their bit length. Communication is O(Q log D) symbols/bits as stated and
has no uncharged random seed. Query indices can be recomputed from common
answers; an implementation retaining them literally must count them.

7. RECEIVING-OPERATION SPOT CHECK ONLY

No obvious conflict was found in the stated disjoint-row syndrome decoding:
all rows exclude coordinate one; after measuring i>c and subtracting each
party's own tail outcomes, good branches constrain the Bell prefix to the same
affine linear subspace. The fresh-color supports make rank at most Q and permit
linear-cost specialized decoding. Full joint-state/error/resource adjudication
remains with the parent root and is outside this bounded audit's PASS.

IMPLEMENTATION ADDENDUM

The subsequently supplied colored_search.py was inspected against the proof.
PASS for the receiver-control implementation inspected. The raw callback is
an explicit interface obligation; the code does not implement or experimentally
validate a quantum parity device.

A. The lazy interval tree is exact. Before a node is first split, every atom
in it has the same weight: creation is uniform, multiplication preserves
uniformity, and any partial update or point setting would first split the node.
Thus total/(hi-lo) in push() is an exact integer. On an already split node,
push() propagates the accumulated multiplicative lazy factor to both children
and clears it. All partial updates recompute the parent total. A point set
clears the leaf lazy factor; it does not lose any outstanding multiplier.

B. median() keeps the target as twice the residual median mass and accumulates
the exact left mass when moving right. The >= branch chooses the earlier atom
when the median is exactly an atom boundary. This is a valid alpha=1 endpoint
choice and is consistent with the proof's query rounding and integer update.
The update snapshots old_total, weight and cumulative before modifying any
range. The two side intervals exclude h, so the final point assignment is the
stated median-atom formula. root.total==3*old_total is checked every step.

C. The code's ranks min(T-1,floor(l*T/8)) are the zero-based equivalents of the
proof's ranks min(T,1+floor(l*T/8)). Taking a set of candidate values only removes
duplicates. The code validates ALL candidate values and always uses threshold
min(L+1,j+Q+1), matching the tighter b=Q search proof.

D. raw_count increments on every call including sentinels; each physical callback
therefore receives a distinct color in 1,...,Q. Consuming unused colors on
synthetic calls is conservative and does not spoil freshness. The initial U=L
and every raw NO update U=min(U,j-1) match the no-underestimate invariant.
The logical query evaluates all four raw calls (no short-circuit elision), so
the code's total is exactly 4T plus s times the number of candidate values.
ceil_log_ratio uses exact integer-power comparisons and the displayed parameter
choices agree with the proof.

E. Each lazy-tree range multiplication, point setting and median descent has
O(log(L+1)) tree operations; all arithmetic remains on O(T+log L)-bit integers.
Only paths reached by the bounded number of query updates need allocation.
The original linear-scan complexity bound is conservative for this code.
The recorded full raw transcript consumes the stated O(Q(log L+log D)) bits
(up to integer encoding overhead); it is not silently free retained memory.

A fixed-path implementation admission compared exact leaf weights and medians
against a flat integer representation for n in {1,2,3,9}, each along the same
32-answer pattern. All 128 transitions agreed, including atom-boundary cases;
every total equaled n*3^t. IMPLEMENTATION_AUDIT_CHECK.json records those results.
This bounded check does not estimate failure probabilities or establish the
physical receiving theorem. No large fixture or campaign was repeated.

DIMENSION-SCOPE CORRECTION / ADDENDUM

The owner proposed removing the prime-power restriction for this construction.
PASS: it is not needed. Work in the cyclic group Z_D for arbitrary integer D>=2.
Each raw query uses only coefficient-one sums of disjoint color coordinates.
A uniform nuisance digit with coefficient one makes that sum exactly uniform
modulo D. No nonprimitive coefficient can occur in this actual query family.

For the receiving step, each nonempty restricted row has a disjoint support S.
Pick a pivot p in S. The local coordinate permutation
  y_p = sum_(i in S) x_i mod D,  y_i=x_i for i!=p
is invertible for every D, with inverse
  x_p = y_p - sum_(i in S, i!=p) y_i mod D.
Thus a row syndrome fixes exactly its pivot digit. Subtracting each party's own
syndrome leaves |00> at that pivot and Bell pairs on the nonpivot coordinates
on every good branch. Empty rows impose no constraint there. The number of
Bell pairs is c minus the number of nonempty restricted rows, at least c-Q.
Coordinate one is in no row and can be fixed throughout.

Accordingly, the earlier prime-power qualification in section 5 is superseded
for this specifically disjoint all-one construction. It remains a sensible
warning for a different, unrestricted coefficient-matrix construction. The
original code's modulo-D fixture is consistent with the corrected all-D
search-control scope; it was never a simulation of quantum postmeasurement
states. No general Gaussian elimination over a composite-modulus field is
being invoked.
