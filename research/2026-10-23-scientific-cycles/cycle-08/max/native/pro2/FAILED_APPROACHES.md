## This turn: recoloring all series junctions with a fourth color

This was considered and rejected before execution. In the inherited Q_h
construction, the large left-boundary bichromatic component inside the first
child can grow without passing through the recolored remote series junction.
Thus merely recoloring those junctions does not establish the needed reset.
No new experiment or universal impossibility is attributed to this reasoning.

## Requiring a universally rainbow port is not realizable

A length-two path has a shared neighbor at its two terminals, so its four
terminal/neighbor slots cannot all receive distinct colors. More generally,
prescribing a convenient abstract state is not the same as realizing it.
The completed proof begins with actual path leaf states and retains only
guaranteed families. Equal-terminal colors are allowed where realized, with
their flags and collision restrictions fully paid.

## Exact state closure is evidence, not a sampled-depth argument

The first exact path2 closure reached 17 nonempty signature-set types after
four rounds and checked all 1411 transitions. This is a closed finite algebra,
so together with the exact port lemma it supports an all-depth statement. It
was subsequently compressed to two guarantee families and 92 explicit witnesses.
Both the larger construction and its independent check remain charged and
preserved; their costs were not replaced by the smaller final gate.

The two-family theorem was valid for path2 leaves. It did not cover arbitrary
odd path leaves. This scope was identified explicitly, not papered over by
adding fictitious vertices or weakening the original-support contract.

## Adding the true path3 family did not immediately close

`results/path3_extension_diagnostic.json` records the exact template failure.
In the old L/N notation plus the true path3 family O, P2(L,O) and P3(L,L,O)
outputs contained none of the proposed guarantee families. Their actual output
sets were nonempty (19 and 15 canonical states respectively). This was a
failure of the chosen invariant family, not a graph noncolorability proof.

The owner added a fourth guaranteed family selected from the actual reachable
output, and verified all enlarged transitions. The resulting A2/B4/C9/D15
certificate covers every path2/path3/S2/P2/P3 composition. This material
extension, rather than another numerical size sweep, resolves arbitrary paths.

## Three-color star closure reaches an actual empty state

`results/star_three_control.json` stops at the first reached empty state and
gives a real 17-vertex expression. This is different from a template failure:
the port algebra is exact for all star colorings and its reachable state set
is empty. An independent exhaustive enumeration confirms zero star colorings
among all 49,320 proper three-color assignments. A comment in the reused
prototype originally still said four colors; it was corrected to three after
the calculation without altering the palette, computation or saved output.

The resulting graph has star chromatic number four. It says nothing about a
different acyclic three-coloring's cut masses or an arbitrary real three-query
design. The broad three-versus-four stable question on this grammar is not
closed by this representation-specific negative.

## The new grammar is not the full chordless-atom target

The first scope witness was K2,4: its usable degree-two exterior-port pairs
do not supply the required balanced child-interior separator. The stronger
unbounded A_L family follows from an odd degree-four count in its cyclic core,
which our grammar cannot generate. The full proof is in research/grammar_scope.
This is a graph-representation obstruction that survives arbitrary series
parity and arbitrary path leaves. A_L may have excellent colorings; A_2 is
explicitly star-three. Merely calling it a series-parallel graph does not make
the degree-one-terminal production cover its unequal terminal loads.

This failure changes the next scientific question to higher/unequal port loads.
It does not falsify the new theorem or establish full-class four-query hardness.
