\begin{abstract}
For the closed qutrit device $V_s=\cos(s)U+i\sin(s)I$ on the countable labelled tree, we prove an unassisted quantum capacity lower bound of $3/2$ qubits per forward use for every fixed $0\le s\le\pi/2$. A single encoder/decoder sequence for each $s$ works for every fixed normal bath initializer, including coherent states of infinite entropy. No bath operation, resetting, postselection, feedback, or terminal return condition is used.

The new mechanism is critical-input environment compression. The exact two-seed Fock representation makes the bath depth under the data-averaging state $\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/4,1/4)$ a reflected lazy symmetric walk, even for coherent initial baths. A type space of dimension $\binom n{n/2}2^{n/2}$ has subexponentially compressible final bath. Expurgation, a joint positive-operator inequality, and a common auxiliary normalization turn this mean estimate into a code uniform over a growing initial bath subspace. The final finite-error-set coding theorem is inherited. Polar normalization gives an explicit full-diamond bound and a pointwise extension to every fixed normal initializer.

Thus capacity cannot fall below its permutation-endpoint value. For $0<\sin(s)\le1/512$, the established upper bound still gives $3/2\le Q\le Q_{\leftarrow}\le1.581642761\ldots<\log_2 3$. A strict gain or a plateau remains unresolved. We also export an exact two-call erasure reduction valid on joint logical--bath inputs, an exact coherent balanced-word filter, and a two-call zero-error obstruction. These are distinct operational statements, not solutions of the remaining asymptotic question.
\end{abstract}

\section{Contract, inherited state, and the scientific change}

The physical bath is $E=\ell^2(V(T_3))$. The root has child ports $0,1,2$; every nonroot vertex has parent port $0$ and child ports $1,2$. If an edge $v$--$w$ has labels $a$ at $v$ and $a'$ at $w$, then
\[
 U\ket{a,v}=\ket{a',w},\qquad U=U^\dagger,\quad U^2=I.
\]
For a fixed $s\in[0,\pi/2]$, put $\alpha=\cos s$, $\beta=\sin s$, and
\[
 V_s=\alpha U+i\beta I,\qquad W_{n,s}=V_{s,nE}\cdots V_{s,1E}.
\]
Each code sequence uses this one parameter and one normal trace-class initializer $\tau$, independent of the message and its reference. The same bath is used throughout all $n$ forward qutrit calls. Alice encodes before the block and Bob decodes its outputs; all local environments and outcomes are counted. No bath access, replenishment, internal reset, preshared entanglement, backward quantum wire, or forward timing channel is available. The new lower construction is unassisted and deterministic. The inherited upper bound permits normal measurable backward-classical feedback and counts every branch.

The faithful reference remains
\[
 \eta=\operatorname{diag}(3/4,1/8,1/8),\quad
 \pi(o)=1/3,\quad \pi(\mathrm{child})=\pi(\mathrm{parent})\eta_a/(3/4).
\]
Both $U$ and $V_s$ preserve $\eta\otimes\tau_\pi$ exactly. The different distribution used below is an auxiliary average over \emph{data inputs}, not a replacement reference or initializer.

Let $Q(V_s,\tau)$ and $Q_{\leftarrow}(V_s,\tau)$ denote reliable unassisted and backward-classical capacities in qubits per counted forward use. All logarithms below are base two except exponential tail bounds, where $\exp$ is natural. For logical channels our error is the \emph{full} diamond distance $\norm{\Lambda-\id}_\diamond$; its half-diamond version is half that value. State half-trace distance is $\norm{\rho-\sigma}_1/2$.

The unchanged owner archive proves $Q=Q_{\leftarrow}=3/2$ at $s=0$, the receiver-controlled expected-use law there, and endpoint continuity for this family. Its whole-bath Dyck identity and coding lemma are actual inherited proofs, not summary-only claims. The evaluator's frozen-transcript contribution has already been incorporated and is not re-audited here. At $s=\pi/2$, $V_s=iI$ gives $Q=\log_2 3$.

The former live possibilities were increase, plateau, or decrease at a fixed small nonzero $s$. The principal theorem below removes decrease. It does not prove an increase, a plateau, feedback equality at nonzero $s$, or a full capacity formula. No terminal bath return is imposed or concluded.

\section{An exact representation of the entire bath}\label{sec:fock}

Delete the edge joining the original root to its port-$0$ child. The remaining graph is two rooted binary trees. Write their two roots as seeds $j=0,1$, and label descendants by finite words $w$ over $\{1,2\}$. Thus
\[
 E\cong\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathcal F(\mathbb C^2),\qquad
 \mathcal F(\mathbb C^2)=\bigoplus_{\ell\ge0}(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes\ell}.
\]
Choose the stack convention $S_a\ket{j,w}=\ket{j,aw}$. Let $P_0$ project onto the two empty words and let $R$ exchange them and vanish elsewhere. Then
\begin{equation}
 S_a^\dagger S_b=\delta_{ab}I,\quad
 \sum_{a=1}^2S_aS_a^\dagger=I-P_0,\quad R=R^\dagger,\quad R^2=P_0,
 \quad RS_a=S_a^\dagger R=0.\label{eq:fockrelations}
\end{equation}
With output port indexing rows and input indexing columns,
\begin{equation}
 U=\begin{pmatrix}R&S_1&S_2\\S_1^\dagger&0&0\\S_2^\dagger&0&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 V_s=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha R+i\beta I&\alpha S_1&\alpha S_2\\
 \alpha S_1^\dagger&i\beta I&0\\\alpha S_2^\dagger&0&i\beta I\end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:matrix}
\end{equation}
These formulas include both exceptional vertices, rather than replacing the bath by a reflecting numerical cutoff. Squaring the first matrix using \eqref{eq:fockrelations} gives $I$.

The original stationary density becomes
\begin{equation}
 \tau_\pi=\frac{I_2}{2}\otimes
 \left(\frac23\sum_w6^{-|w|}\ket w\bra w\right).
 \label{eq:stationaryfock}
\end{equation}
Indeed each seed has weight $1/3$ and each binary descent divides weight by six. The sum is one because $\sum_\ell2^\ell6^{-\ell}=3/2$.

Let $L\ket{j,w}=|w|\ket{j,w}$ and
\begin{equation}
 P_a=1_{[0,a]}(L),\qquad r_a=\rank P_a=2(2^{a+1}-1).
 \label{eq:rankcutoff}
\end{equation}
These are finite \emph{vertex-coordinate} projections. The Fock depth differs from original-root graph distance on the second component; all subsequent depth statements refer to $L$.

\section{Critical data inputs give diffusive bath depth}\label{sec:depth}

For $0\le q\le1$, set $\eta_q=\operatorname{diag}(q,(1-q)/2,(1-q)/2)$ and let
\[
 \Phi_{s,q}(\rho)=\Tr_{\rm port}V_s(\eta_q\otimes\rho)V_s^\dagger.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact closed depth algebra]\label{lem:depth}
For every bounded function $f$ on $\mathbb N_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{s,q}^*(f(L))=\beta^2 f(L)+\alpha^2\big[(1-q)f(L+1)+q f((L-1)_+)\big].
 \label{eq:depthlaw}
\end{equation}
Consequently, on independent product data inputs $\eta_q^{\otimes n}$, the final depth marginal equals the classical reflected walk with independent increments $X$ of law
\[
 \Prob(X=1)=\alpha^2(1-q),\quad
 \Prob(X=-1)=\alpha^2q,\quad
 \Prob(X=0)=\beta^2,
 \qquad Y_{t+1}=\max(0,Y_t+X_{t+1}).
\]
This is an identity of the depth marginal for arbitrary coherent initial bath densities. It is not an equality of the full quantum device with a stochastic move/stay process.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Direct expansion of \eqref{eq:matrix} gives
\begin{align*}
 \Phi_{s,q}(\rho)={}&\alpha^2\left[q\left(R\rho R+\sum_a S_a^\dagger\rho S_a\right)
 +\frac{1-q}{2}\sum_a S_a\rho S_a^\dagger\right]+\beta^2\rho
 +i\alpha\beta q(\rho R-R\rho).
\end{align*}
Since $[R,f(L)]=0$, the coherent cross term vanishes only on this observable algebra. For nonvacuum depth $\ell$, $\sum_a S_a f(L)S_a^\dagger=f(\ell-1)$; on the vacuum $Rf(L)R=f(0)P_0$ supplies the held downward step. Also $S_a^\dagger f(L)S_a=f(L+1)$ for each $a$. This proves \eqref{eq:depthlaw} on every coordinate and hence as an operator identity. Iteration gives the Markov kernel on depth marginals, regardless of off-diagonal matrix elements of the initial density.
\end{proof}

Take the critical value $q=1/2$ and write $\eta_c=\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/4,1/4)$. The increments now have mean zero.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform overflow estimate]\label{lem:tail}
For every bath density supported in $P_aE$, every integer $n\ge1$, and $b>0$,
\begin{equation}
 \Tr[(I-P_{\lfloor a+b\rfloor})\Phi_{s,1/2}^n(\rho)]
 \le4\exp[-b^2/(8n)].\label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
The estimate is uniform in $s\in[0,\pi/2]$ and in coherent states in that support.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the reflected walk let $S_t=\sum_{j=1}^tX_j$ and $M_n=\max_{0\le t\le n}|S_t|$. The pathwise reflection formula, or induction on the Lindley recursion, gives
\[
 Y_n=Y_0+S_n-\min\{0,\min_{0\le t\le n}(Y_0+S_t)\}
 \le a+2M_n
\]
when $Y_0\le a$. For independent mean-zero $X_j\in[-1,1]$, Hoeffding's exponential bound $\E e^{\lambda X_j}\le e^{\lambda^2/2}$ and the maximal exponential-supermartingale inequality give
\[
 \Prob\{\max_{t\le n}S_t\ge u\}\le\inf_{\lambda>0}e^{-\lambda u+n\lambda^2/2}
 =e^{-u^2/(2n)}.
\]
The same holds for $-S_t$. Hence $\Prob(Y_n>a+b)\le2e^{-b^2/(8n)}$, which implies the stated convenient bound. Lemma~\ref{lem:depth} applies this classical estimate to every initial depth marginal. This use of probabilities follows an exact reduced observable identity; it assigns no classical probabilities to the full coherent histories.
\end{proof}

For even $n$ let $\mathcal H_n$ be the span of qutrit words with precisely $n/2$ zeros and $n/2$ colored symbols. Its projector is $T_n$ and its dimension is
\begin{equation}
 D_n=\binom n{n/2}2^{n/2},\qquad
 p_n=\Tr(\eta_c^{\otimes n}T_n)=2^{-n}\binom n{n/2}\ge\frac1{n+1}.
 \label{eq:type}
\end{equation}
Every basis word in this space has $\eta_c^{\otimes n}$ weight $2^{-3n/2}$, so
\begin{equation}
 \frac{T_n}{D_n}\le\frac{\eta_c^{\otimes n}}{p_n}.
 \label{eq:domination}
\end{equation}
Unlike the inherited Dyck space, this space imposes no nonnegative-prefix restriction. We will select a smaller error-correcting subspace, not assert that all these words return the bath.

\section{A reusable uniform bath-compression operation}\label{sec:compression}

The next lemma separates the new uniformity issue from the particular tree. Its projections and normalization are mathematical devices for choosing a code; the physical protocol implements only an isometric encoder, the given channel interactions, and a deterministic decoder.

\begin{lemma}[Inherited finite-error-set code]\label{lem:code}
A CPTP map from a $D$-dimensional input space to any finite output space, with at most $k$ Kraus operators, has an exact subspace quantum code of dimension at least $\lceil D/k^4\rceil$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We restate the inherited proof to make its dependency and the unequal-output-dimension scope explicit. Let $\mathcal S=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb C}\{A_i^\dagger A_j\}$, with $d=\dim\mathcal S\le k^2$. It is self-adjoint and contains $I$. Choose a Hermitian basis $I,H_2,\ldots,H_d$. Greedily choose orthonormal vectors $v_1,\ldots,v_N$ with zero cross matrix elements for every $H_j$: after $m$ choices, avoid the span of the at most $md$ vectors $H_jv_a$. This gives $N\ge\lceil D/d\rceil$.

Their expectation vectors for $H_2,\ldots,H_d$ lie in $\mathbb R^{d-1}$. The affine Tverberg theorem partitions any $(r-1)d+1$ points in that space into $r$ disjoint groups with intersecting convex hulls. Choose $r=\lceil N/d\rceil$. Square-root convex weights on each disjoint group form orthonormal code vectors with identical diagonal expectations and zero cross expectations. Thus $C^\dagger A_i^\dagger A_j C=\gamma_{ij}I_r$, the Knill--Laflamme conditions, and $r\ge\lceil D/d^2\rceil\ge\lceil D/k^4\rceil$.

Recovery follows directly: choose syndrome vectors with Gram matrix $\gamma$ and map $A_iC\ket\psi\mapsto\ket\psi\otimes\ket{\xi_i}$. The equality of Gram matrices makes this an isometry on the error-image span; extend it using an external syndrome space. Only input-space products were used, so input/output dimensions need not agree. This is the established greedy/convex-partition mechanism \cite{KLV,Tverberg}; subexponential environment applications are established in \cite{GBM}. No new general coding theorem is claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Joint control from averaged environment overflow]\label{lem:uniformcompression}
Let $W:A\otimes E_0\to B\otimes E$ be an isometry, $\dim A=D$, $\dim E_0=r<\infty$, and let $P$ be a rank-$d$ projection in $E$. Define
\[
 F=W^*(I_B\otimes(I-P))W,\qquad L_A=\frac1r\Tr_{E_0}F.
\]
Assume $D^{-1}\Tr L_A\le\delta$, where $0<\delta<1/2$. There is an isometric encoder $C:\mathbb C^K\to A$ and a deterministic decoder with
\begin{equation}
 K\ge\left\lceil\frac{\lfloor D/2\rfloor}{(rd)^4}\right\rceil
 \label{eq:Kcompress}
\end{equation}
such that the recovered logical channel for every initializer supported in $E_0$ has full diamond error at most $4e$, provided $e<1$, where
\begin{equation}
 e=r\sqrt{2\delta}+(1-2\delta)^{-1/2}-1.
 \label{eq:ecompress}
\end{equation}
More strongly, its recovered isometry on logical input tensor $E_0$ is within $2e$ of $I_{\rm logical}\otimes Z_0$ for an isometry $Z_0$. The same code works on arbitrary coherent superpositions in $E_0$ and preserves arbitrary reference entanglement.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $Q=1_{[0,2\delta]}(L_A)$. Markov's eigenvalue count gives $\dim QA\ge D/2$ and $QL_AQ\le2\delta Q$. For every positive operator $X$ on $A\otimes\mathbb C^r$,
\begin{equation}
 X\le r(\Tr_{\mathbb C^r}X)\otimes I_r.\label{eq:positiveblock}
\end{equation}
One proof is the pinching bound $X\le r\sum_v(I\otimes\ket v\bra v)X(I\otimes\ket v\bra v)$, obtained by Cauchy--Schwarz after writing $X=Y^*Y$. Each positive diagonal block is bounded by the sum of all diagonal blocks. Applying this to $F_Q=(Q\otimes I)F(Q\otimes I)$ gives
\[
 F_Q\le r^2(QL_AQ)\otimes I\le2r^2\delta I.
\]
Thus the discarded isometry amplitude is uniformly at most $r\sqrt{2\delta}$, including inputs entangled with $E_0$.

Choose bases $v$ of $E_0$ and $w$ of $PE$. The operators
\[
 B_{wv}=\frac1{\sqrt r}\bra w(I\otimes P)W\ket v\big|_{QA}
\]
obey $\sum B_{wv}^\dagger B_{wv}=I_{QA}-QL_AQ$. Define the positive \emph{auxiliary} input normalization
\[
 J=(I_{QA}-QL_AQ)^{-1/2},\qquad
 \norm{J-I}\le(1-2\delta)^{-1/2}-1.
\]
The $rd$ operators $B_{wv}J$ now define a CPTP map on $QA$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:code} to choose a code inclusion $C$ and recovery isometry $D_{m rec}$ satisfying
\[
 D_{\rm rec}B_{wv}JC\ket\psi=\ket\psi\otimes\ket{\xi_{wv}}
\]
simultaneously for every $v,w$. Consequently the normalized projected joint map factors exactly:
\begin{equation}
 (D_{\rm rec}\otimes I)(I\otimes P)W(JC\otimes I_{E_0})
 =I_{\rm logical}\otimes Z,\qquad
 Z\ket v=\sqrt r\sum_w\ket{\xi_{wv}}\otimes\ket w.
 \label{eq:jointKL}
\end{equation}
This step uses all initial as well as final bath indices; coding only a single pure bath input would not provide it.

The actual physical encoder is $C$, never $JC$. Its recovered joint isometry $H=(D_{\rm rec}\otimes I)W(C\otimes I)$ satisfies
\[
 \norm{H-I\otimes Z}\le r\sqrt{2\delta}+\norm{J-I}\le e.
\]
Because $H$ is isometric, $(1-e)\norm\xi\le\norm{Z\xi}\le(1+e)\norm\xi$ for every $\xi\in E_0$. If $e<1$, the polar decomposition $Z=Z_0|Z|$ has an isometric polar part and $\norm{Z-Z_0}\le e$. Hence $\norm{H-I\otimes Z_0}\le2e$. The full diamond distance between channels induced by two isometries differing by norm $b$ is at most $2b$: apply to a purified input, use the pure-state trace-distance bound, and trace the unused outputs. This proves the claimed $4e$, or $2e$ in half-diamond convention. The external recovery syndrome is retained in this proof; tracing it defines the logical marginal and is not a hidden internal bath reset.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the uniformity hypotheses matter}

The factor $r$ in \eqref{eq:positiveblock} cannot simply be suppressed. For the normalized maximally entangled vector $\Omega_r$ on $\mathbb C^r\otimes\mathbb C^r$, let $X=\ket{\Omega_r}\bra{\Omega_r}$. Then $\norm X=1$ while $(\Tr_E X)\otimes I=I/r$. A bath-averaged effect $(1/r)\Tr_E X=I/r^2$ can hide a unit-probability joint failure. This example explains the $r^2\delta$ scale needed to upgrade the mean estimate to a bath-uniform operation.

Likewise, an overflow probability averaged over logical inputs cannot be substituted for a diamond bound without expurgation. The projector onto one logical basis vector has uniform-input mean $1/D$ but fails with probability one on that basis vector. The spectral restriction $Q$ removes precisely this possible concentration.

Finally, the auxiliary projected map need not be trace preserving. Applying an exact CPTP coding theorem to it without $J$, or physically treating $J$ as a deterministic encoder, would be invalid. Its normalization is used only in a comparison construction, with the norm difference explicitly charged to $e$.

\section{The capacity floor and its stronger quantifier}\label{sec:floor}

For even $n$ choose
\begin{equation}
 a_n=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor,\qquad b_n=\lceil n^{3/4}\rceil,
 \qquad H_n=a_n+b_n,
 \quad r_n=r_{a_n},\quad d_n=r_{H_n},
 \quad \delta_n=\frac4{p_n}\exp[-b_n^2/(8n)].
 \label{eq:scales}
\end{equation}
Use the auxiliary uniform initial bath $P_{a_n}/r_n$ only in the proof of the average. Type domination \eqref{eq:domination}, positivity, and Lemma~\ref{lem:tail} show that the mean final overflow on $\mathcal H_n\otimes P_{a_n}E$ is at most $\delta_n$. Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:uniformcompression} applies to $W_{n,s}$ restricted to this domain and to final projection $P_{H_n}$.

\begin{theorem}[Universal-in-initializer capacity floor]\label{thm:floor}
For each fixed $s\in[0,\pi/2]$ there exists a sequence of unassisted isometric encoders $C_n$ and deterministic decoders, independent of the initializer $\tau$, with
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_2K_n}{n}\ge\frac32,
 \label{eq:floor-rate}
\end{equation}
such that for every one fixed normal bath density $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\Lambda_{n,s,\tau}-\id_{K_n}}_\diamond
 \le\min\left\{2,\ 4e_n+2\sqrt{1-q_n}\right\}\longrightarrow0,
 \quad q_n=\Tr(P_{a_n}\tau),
 \label{eq:floor-error}
\end{equation}
where $e_n=r_n\sqrt{2\delta_n}+(1-2\delta_n)^{-1/2}-1$ for all sufficiently large even $n$. In particular,
\begin{equation}
 Q(V_s,\tau)\ge3/2\qquad\text{for every fixed normal }\tau.
 \label{eq:capacityfloor}
\end{equation}
The half-diamond bound is $2e_n+\sqrt{1-q_n}$. No finite bath entropy or tail moment is required.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The preceding construction gives
\[
 K_n\ge\left\lceil\frac{\lfloor D_n/2\rfloor}{(r_nd_n)^4}\right\rceil.
\]
By \eqref{eq:type}, $\log_2D_n\ge3n/2-\log_2(n+1)$. Also
\[
 \log_2(r_nd_n)<a_n+H_n+4=2a_n+b_n+4=o(n).
\]
These inequalities prove \eqref{eq:floor-rate}; rounding the logical dimension down to a power of two loses less than one qubit.

The errors tend to zero because
\[
 r_n\sqrt{2\delta_n}
 \le 2^{a_n+2}\sqrt{8(n+1)}\exp[-b_n^2/(16n)]
 \le2^{a_n+2}\sqrt{8(n+1)}e^{-\sqrt n/16}\longrightarrow0.
\]
In particular $\delta_n\to0$, the normalizer term vanishes, and eventually $e_n<1$. The code and recovery depend on $s,n$, and the fixed coordinate cutoffs, but never on $\tau$.

For an arbitrary fixed normal density, $q_n\to1$ because $P_{a_n}\uparrow I$. Whenever $q_n>0$, let $\tau_n=P_{a_n}\tau P_{a_n}/q_n$. A purification projected and normalized in the bath factor proves
\[
 \tfrac12\norm{\tau-\tau_n}_1\le\sqrt{1-q_n}.
\]
The true physical encoded/decoded process is CPTP in its initial bath input, so its logical channels for $\tau$ and $\tau_n$ differ in full diamond norm by at most $\norm{\tau-\tau_n}_1\le2\sqrt{1-q_n}$. Add the bath-uniform finite-cutoff error $4e_n$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:uniformcompression}. This proves \eqref{eq:floor-error}. The bath cutoff comparison is applied to the normalized physical process, \emph{outside} the type-domination estimate. In particular no uncontrolled tail of $\tau$ is multiplied by $1/p_n$.

The proof uses the even subsequence. If codes are required at every integer blocklength, append one unused data-independent final input to an even code and discard that last external output. Later bath interactions do not change Bob's earlier output marginal, so the same decoded channel works, with one extra counted use and unchanged limiting rate.
\end{proof}

This theorem says $\forall s\,\exists(C_n,D_n)\,\forall\tau\text{ fixed normal}$ with error tending to zero. It does not say $\sup_\tau\norm{\Lambda_{n,s,\tau}-\id}_\diamond\to0$. For each finite $n$, normal states may place all their mass above $a_n$. The displayed estimate consequently gives no uniform convergence rate over arbitrary normal tails. The physical initializer never equals a horizon-dependent cutoff by instruction or preparation; that cutoff is solely a comparison in the proof.

The result is an existence theorem. It neither supplies an efficient circuit implementation nor a useful laboratory blocklength. The type input dimension is exponential, the auxiliary Kraus count is subexponential, and the greedy/Tverberg coding construction can require substantial unbounded local computation. No physical tree device, energy budget, or engineering advantage is claimed.

\begin{corollary}[The remaining small-angle interval]\label{cor:bracket}
For each fixed normal $\tau$ and $0<\sin s\le1/512$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac32\le Q(V_s,\tau)\le Q_{\leftarrow}(V_s,\tau)
 \le B(1/100)=1.58164276090312\ldots<\log_2 3,
 \label{eq:bracket}
\end{equation}
where the established bound is
$B(\theta)=3/2+h_2(\theta)+\theta(\log_2 3-3/2)$ under its inherited admissibility conditions.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Combine Theorem~\ref{thm:floor}, inclusion of unassisted protocols among feedback protocols, and the unchanged coherent-history upper theorem in the preserved owner source.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact two-call reduction and a transferable composition rule}\label{sec:erasure}

The following is a separate exact operation on the entire bath. It does not yield a rate above the floor: its input contains only one qubit per two counted forward calls.

\begin{proposition}[A bath-uniform qubit erasure reduction]\label{prop:erasure}
Encode a logical color $a\in\{1,2\}$ into the two qutrit inputs $\ket{a,0}$. There is a fixed local output decoder whose logical output is the qubit erasure channel with erasure probability
\[
 r=\alpha^2\beta^2.
\]
The equality holds on every joint logical--bath input, with arbitrary references. Repetition on the same bath therefore gives the exact product erasure process on the designated reduced logical outputs, including entangled block inputs. The complete retained syndrome process is not asserted to be memoryless.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the two physical interactions without dropping cross terms:
\begin{align}
 W_{2,s}(\ket{a,0}\otimes\psi)={}&
 \alpha^2\ket{0,a}\otimes\psi
 +\ket{a,0}\otimes(i\alpha\beta R-\beta^2I)\psi\nonumber\\
 &+i\alpha\beta\sum_{b=1}^2\ket{a,b}\otimes S_b^\dagger\psi
 +i\alpha\beta\ket{0,0}\otimes S_a\psi.
 \label{eq:two-slot}
\end{align}
The eight good output words are $\ket{0,a}$ and $\ket{a,b}$, with $a=1,2$ and $b=0,1,2$. Decode them to logical $\ket a$ and four orthogonal syndrome vectors $g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3$, respectively. Decode $\ket{0,0}$ to an erasure symbol $e$ and a fifth syndrome orthogonal to all good syndromes. This defines an isometry from the nine-dimensional physical output into the logical qutrit and a finite external syndrome.

The good joint map is $I_{\rm logical}\otimes G$, where
\[
 G=\alpha^2\ket{g_0}\otimes I
 +\ket{g_1}\otimes(i\alpha\beta R-\beta^2I)
 +i\alpha\beta\sum_{b=1}^2\ket{g_{b+1}}\otimes S_b^\dagger.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:fockrelations} gives
\[
 G^*G=(\alpha^4+\beta^4+\alpha^2\beta^2)I=(1-r)I.
\]
The erasure map is $i\alpha\beta\ket e\otimes T$, where $T(\ket a\otimes\psi)=S_a\psi$. Since $S_a^\dagger S_b=\delta_{ab}I$, $T$ is an isometry on logical tensor bath. Tracing the orthogonal syndrome removes good/erasure coherence. The good map preserves the logical/reference marginal for all joint states because $G/\sqrt{1-r}$ is a bath isometry. The bad map outputs $e$ and preserves the reference marginal after tracing logical and bath. Thus for every normal $\rho_{RAE}$,
\[
 \Tr_{E,\mathrm{synd}}\mathcal W(\rho_{RAE})
 =(\id_R\otimes\operatorname{Er}_r)(\Tr_E\rho_{RAE}).
\]
The endpoint $r=0$ follows directly by omitting the zero branch. Every outcome and syndrome remains part of the complete physical description; the equality specifies its reduced communication interface.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Repeated bath-uniform interfaces]\label{lem:compose}
Let a normal block operation $\mathcal W:AE\to BE$ (with explicitly retained or traced external local registers) satisfy
\[
 \Tr_E\mathcal W(\rho_{AE})=\mathcal N(\Tr_E\rho_{AE})
\]
for all normal joint inputs, completely with external references. Then repeated blocks on the same bath realize the memoryless external process of $\mathcal N$ on the designated interfaces. No bath reset is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The hypothesis is equivalent to the normal Heisenberg identity
\[
 \mathcal W^*(X_B\otimes I_E)=\mathcal N^*(X_B)\otimes I_E.
\]
Starting with the final block observable, pull it backward through the last operation; its bath factor remains identity. Repeat through the earlier operations. Tensoring each identity with the other reference and intervention registers proves the corresponding equality of all finite external process tests, including entangled inputs and causal local interventions. This is the elementary semicausal identity discussed in \cite{ESW}; no finite-dimensional factorization theorem is needed for this normal infinite-bath argument.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Failure when only one stationary marginal is tested}

Take a single classical bath bit in $I/2$ and let every carrier qubit undergo controlled-$Z$ from that same bath bit. The bath marginal remains $I/2$ after every use. Each isolated carrier sees the completely dephasing channel. Two uses, however, give
\[
 \rho\longmapsto\tfrac12\rho+\tfrac12(Z\otimes Z)\rho(Z\otimes Z),
\]
not two independent dephasings. For example $\ket{\Phi^+}$ remains pure under the former and loses its off-diagonal terms under the latter. This finite normal example shows why stationary bath return and a one-initializer marginal channel are insufficient for Lemma~\ref{lem:compose}. The hypothesis must be the complete bath-uniform interface identity, not a product-state test at one chosen density. It also explains why the retained syndromes in Proposition~\ref{prop:erasure} cannot silently be declared independent.
