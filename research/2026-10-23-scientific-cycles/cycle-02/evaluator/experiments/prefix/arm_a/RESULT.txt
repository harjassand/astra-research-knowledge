FULL IMPOSSIBILITY FOR UNIFORM NULL-FAMILY FRACTIONAL SAVINGS

Independent bounded trial, arm A. First clock: 2026-10-21 15:19:59 UTC.

STATUS AND EXACT CLAIM

Within the frozen candidate-only target-evaluation contract, no stopping family
with 1 <= T_epsilon <= 2 n_safe(epsilon) can satisfy both the stated uniform
post-prefix safety and any fixed fractional saving rho < 1 uniformly over the
null family pi=q. This is a full impossibility for the primary question, not
an impossibility for all instance-specific adaptation or richer target access.

The proof below handles the full public labelled q and Q, repeated identities,
private randomization, the actual current IMH state at stopping, and all
branches of the stopped law. Every counterexample is a positive finite
instance. Its support size is allowed to increase with epsilon, as the
protocol permits. No singular instance is used as an admitted target.

In fact, the lower bound does not depend on the prescribed exponential
post-prefix time. It applies to any finite deterministic subsequent time
schedule common to all instances. The time schedule must not depend on the
unknown target or on the particular public q,Q; the frozen endpoint has
exactly this common-time property.

1. THEOREM

Fix K>0 and alpha in (0,1), and let beta=1-alpha. Write

  b_K = 1-exp(-K), lambda_K = -log b_K,
  gamma = beta/(1-alpha b_K),
  n(epsilon) = max(1, ceil(log(gamma/epsilon)/lambda_K)+1).

Consider any family of admissible prefix stopping rules with
1 <= T_epsilon <= 2 n(epsilon), using the frozen oracle interface and
outputting the actual IMH current state. Let t_epsilon >= 1 be any finite
integer schedule, common to all instances at each epsilon. If for every
sufficiently small epsilon

  sup_admitted TV(Law(X_T) C_pi^t_epsilon, pi) <= epsilon,              (1)

then

  liminf_(epsilon -> 0)
    sup_(public q,Q) E_(pi=q)[T_epsilon]/n(epsilon) >= 1.              (2)

The supplied certificate on the null is the same fixed K, not K=0.
Scale invariance is respected; indeed the lower proof matches raw observed
values exactly, so it does not need to exploit that additional restriction.

For the primary constants K=1 and alpha=1/2, the same statement applies with
b_K=1-exp(-1), gamma=1/(1+exp(-1)). The frozen safety endpoint implies (1)
by fixing any one zeta>0, for example zeta=1, and setting
t_epsilon=ceil(exp((K+1)/epsilon)). Therefore (2) contradicts every proposed
uniform savings condition with limsup bounded above by rho<1.

The inherited deterministic comparator T=n(epsilon) has null cost ratio
exactly one and the required upper exponent K. Thus this obstruction matches
that comparator's leading candidate cost for the uniform-null criterion.
No new lower coefficient for the subsequent random-prefix mixing time is
claimed here.

2. A FULLY PUBLIC PAIRED SPACE WITH A HIDDEN TARGET ASSIGNMENT

Fix epsilon, its finite time t=t_epsilon, and a constant b with 0<b<b_K.
Let n=n(epsilon), M=2n, m=n^3, and k=floor(bm). For sufficiently small
epsilon, 1 <= M <= k < m. Put f=k/m. There are 2m known labelled states

  a_1,...,a_m, v_1,...,v_m.

Set

  v = 8(t+1)^2,
  p = epsilon^2/(1+log v),
  r = p/v.

We work at sufficiently small epsilon that p<1/4. In particular
0<r<=p/32, p+r<1, and r<1/2. Disclose the complete proposal table

  q(a_i)=r/m,       q(v_i)=(1-r)/m,

and the symmetric Markov proposal

  Q(a_i,v_i)=Q(v_i,a_i)=1,

with all other entries zero. Thus Q is simply a known disjoint union of
deterministic swaps. Its reducibility is permitted. Its relation to every
candidate label is fully public.

The null target has pi_0=q and unnormalized oracle weights identically one.
It carries certificate K. Let T_0 be this rule's stopping time on the null,
and let e_0=E_0 T_0, with all its private randomization included.

For each fixed subset H of {1,...,m} of size k, define a different target
on EXACTLY the same public space, q, Q, K, alpha, and epsilon:

  pi_H(a_i) = p/m                         for every i;
  pi_H(v_i) = r/m                         for i in H;
  pi_H(v_i) = (1-p-fr)/(m-k)              for i outside H.

All these probabilities are positive and sum to one. Define the low
entrance and associated trap sets

  B_H = {v_i : i in H},       A_H = {a_i : i in H}.

Their masses are

  q(B_H)=(1-r)f,   pi_H(B_H)=fr,   pi_H(A_H)=pf.

Let W_H=pi_H/q, set h=r/(1-r), and supply the unnormalized weight oracle

  w_tilde_H(x)=W_H(x)/h.

Then w_tilde_H(v_i)=1 at every low entrance i in H, exactly matching the
null oracle. The alternative normalizer is 1/h, positive and finite.
The algorithm is not told this normalizer. It receives no off-candidate
weight evaluations and no local probes during its prefix.

The choice of H is a proof device for a finite family of fixed targets.
It is not a changing target, a changing local proposal, or an adaptive
choice of the subsequent kernel during any run.

3. ALL ALTERNATIVES SATISFY THE SAME FORWARD-KL CERTIFICATE

The divergence is independent of which k labels are in H:

  D(pi_H||q)
    = p log(p/r) + fr log(r/(1-r))
      + (1-p-fr) log[(1-p-fr)/((1-r)(1-f))].                          (3)

The low-entrance term is nonpositive since r<1/2. The last term is at
most -log((1-r)(1-f)): write it as a log a - a log d with
a=1-p-fr in (0,1) and d=(1-r)(1-f) in (0,1). Therefore

  D(pi_H||q) <= p log v -log(1-r)-log(1-f)
             <= epsilon^2 -log(1-r)-log(1-f).                       (4)

As epsilon decreases, n and m increase, f tends to b, and
r <= epsilon^2/32 tends to zero. The right side tends to
-log(1-b)<K. Consequently every H is admitted for all sufficiently small
epsilon, with a threshold depending on b,K but not on the finite value
of t. This argument has a strict entropy margin and uses only positive
finite instances. No equality limit at b=b_K is treated as admitted.

4. EXACT STOPPED-HISTORY MATCHING WITH IDENTITIES AND REPEATS

Use the same iid candidate stream, acceptance uniforms, and independent
private randomization to couple the null and any one alternative H. If
all null candidates through T_0 lie in B_H, every observed target weight
is exactly one in both experiments. All ordinary IMH proposals are then
accepted, all current states, identities, q-values, and acceptance
uniforms match, and the entire observed transcript matches through the
stop. Since the stopping rule is a function of that history and the
identical public inputs, it stops at the same time in the alternative,
with its actual output in B_H.

Conversely, an alternative history that stops having observed only B_H
is the same null history. Thus, writing E_H for this all-low stopped
history event,

  P_H(E_H) = P_0(Y_1,...,Y_(T_0) all belong to B_H).                  (5)

This is equality of full histories, not just equality of weight
multisets. No independence of T_0 and the observations is asserted.

Now average H uniformly over all k-subsets, independently of the null
experiment. On a null stopped history containing only entrance states
v_i, let D be the number of distinct observed entrance labels. Repeated
identities are explicitly retained: 1<=D<=T_0<=M. Conditional on this
entire history, the probability that its distinct labels all lie in H is

  (k)_D/(m)_D = product_(j=0)^(D-1) (k-j)/(m-j),                    (6)

where falling factorials are used. This conditional calculation is valid
even for an arbitrary identity-dependent stopping rule, because H is
independent of the whole null experiment and all public inputs are the
same for every H.

Define

  a = (k-M+1)/m,      0<a<1.

Every factor in (6) is at least a, hence (6) is at least a^D and at least
a^(T_0). A history containing a rare state a_i contributes zero. The
probability of seeing any such state before stopping is at most Mr,
by considering all M counted iid candidates, including initialization.
Consequently

  average_H P_H(E_H)
     >= E_0[a^(T_0)] - Mr
     >= a^(e_0) - Mr.                                               (7)

The last step is Jensen's inequality for the convex function x -> a^x.
This treats optional stopping directly. In particular, it does not
replace a deterministic N by E[T] in an IMH law. The expectation appears
only after the exact full-history calculation (5)-(6).

Public q can distinguish entrances from rare states, and Q reveals all
pairs. Both facts are allowed in T_0. Neither reveals the unknown subset
H. The combinatorial average (6) applies to whatever labels, repeated
values, cumulative observed q-mass, and public-input computations the
rule uses. Support size m=n^3 is much larger than the prefix cap; no
unobserved-state query is being hidden inside the argument.

5. THE FIXED SUBSEQUENT MH KERNEL CAPTURES LOW ENTRANCES

For each fixed alternative H, use precisely the prescribed
L_H=MH(Q,pi_H), G_H=IMH(q,pi_H), and

  C_H=(I+alpha G_H+beta L_H)/2.

These kernels remain fixed for all t subsequent steps. At i in H,

  L_H(v_i,a_i)=1,        L_H(a_i,v_i)=r/p.

Indeed pi_H(a_i)/pi_H(v_i)=p/r>1. Normalization cancels in the ordinary
acceptance ratio, exactly as required by the oracle contract.

Every state outside B_H has normalized importance weight at least h,
the common weight of B_H. For traps this follows from W_H(a_i)=p/r>1>h.
For other entrances it follows from p+r<1:

  (1-p-fr)/((1-r)(1-f)) >= r/(1-r).

Thus every global proposal from B_H to its complement is accepted;
global proposals within B_H also accept. Set

  b_q=(1-r)f,       z=(1+alpha b_q)/2.

From any current state in B_H, a subsequent C_H step remains in B_H
with probability z and makes a local capture into its paired A_H state
with probability beta/2. Counting only paths that remain in B_H until
this first local capture gives probability z^(j-1) beta/2 for capture
at step j. Other paths can only add nonnegative probability to the event
used for the lower bound.

Once captured at a_i in A_H, the probability of leaving that exact
state in a global IMH transition is at most 1/W_H(a_i)=r/p. This follows
directly from

  sum_(y != a_i) q(y) min(1,W_H(y)/W_H(a_i)) <= 1/W_H(a_i).

Its local exit probability is also r/p. Hence the one-step C_H exit
probability is at most r/(2p). The probability of staying at this state
for any remaining at most t steps is at least 1-t r/(2p). With our
choice of r,

  t r/(2p)=t/[16(t+1)^2] <= 1/64.

It follows, uniformly over the initial x in B_H, that

  C_H^t(x,A_H)
     >= c_t
      := (1-t r/(2p)) * beta/(1-alpha b_q) * (1-z^t)
     >= (63/64)*(beta/2)
      =: c_0 = 63 beta/128 > 0.                                    (8)

The last inequality retains only the j=1 term of the capture sum and
uses t>=1. No unproved long-time approximation is used. The finite
trap parameter depends on the prescribed finite t, so the large time
does not invalidate the persistence estimate.

6. A FINITE ADAPTATION/SAFETY TRADEOFF FOR THE ACTUAL STOPPED LAW

Let mu_H be the actual law of the stopped IMH state in alternative H,
including every branch and all randomization. By (5), on E_H its state
is in B_H. Equation (8) and nonnegativity of every other branch give

  (mu_H C_H^t)(A_H) >= c_t P_H(E_H).

Since pi_H(A_H)=pf, total variation satisfies

  TV(mu_H C_H^t,pi_H) >= c_t P_H(E_H)-pf.

Averaging over H and applying (7) proves the explicit finite bound

  max_(|H|=k) TV(mu_H C_H^t,pi_H)
       >= c_t [a^(e_0)-Mr]_+ - pf
       >= c_0 (a^(e_0)-Mr) - pf.                                  (9)

Here [x]_+=max(x,0). This is a quantified tradeoff for every admitted
stopping rule on this public q,Q, not merely a heuristic false-stop
probability. The target test event A_H may depend on the chosen fixed
instance, as total variation permits. The probability on all remaining
prefix branches was not conditioned away or subtracted as a fictitious
independent error.

7. DERIVING THE FULL IMPOSSIBILITY

Suppose the uniform safety (1) holds. All targets H above are admitted
for small epsilon, so the left side of (9) is at most epsilon. Thus

  a^(e_0) <= Mr + (epsilon+pf)/c_0,

and, once its right side is below one,

  e_0 >= log(1/[Mr+(epsilon+pf)/c_0])/(-log a).                       (10)

We have M=2n=O(log(1/epsilon)), p<=epsilon^2, and r<=epsilon^2/32.
Uniformly over all finite t>=1, therefore,

  Mr+(epsilon+pf)/c_0 = epsilon/c_0 + O(n epsilon^2),
  log(1/[Mr+(epsilon+pf)/c_0]) = log(1/epsilon)+log c_0+o(1).

Also a=(floor(bn^3)-2n+1)/n^3 tends to b, and

  n(epsilon) = log(1/epsilon)/lambda_K + O(1).

For each fixed b in (0,b_K), (10) shows that the null on the fully
disclosed q,Q constructed above satisfies

  liminf_(epsilon -> 0) e_0/n(epsilon)
       >= lambda_K/(-log b).                                      (11)

The public q,Q in (11) may depend on epsilon and t, exactly as allowed
inside the supremum in the frozen null-savings definition. Thus that
same lower bound holds for the liminf of the supremum over public q,Q.
It holds for every fixed b<b_K. Taking b upward to b_K AFTER taking
this liminf yields (2), since lambda_K/(-log b) tends to one.

If a proposed family had uniform null savings with any rho<1, its
limsup supremum would be at most rho, contradicting (2). This covers
the entire prohibited range rho<1, not only a nonempty subrange.

8. SCOPE, SOURCES, AND LIMITATIONS

The proof uses the bounded candidate cap, a public finite paired
proposal whose size grows with epsilon, candidate-only target
evaluations, and the absence of prefix local probes. All actual target
weights, q values, and local proposals are positive or legitimate as
specified; local Q is symmetric and reducible, and global q has full
support. G makes the admitted finite instances irreducible. Each L_H
is the ordinary fixed Metropolis-Hastings kernel from the SAME public
Q; numerical L tables are not supplied before stopping.

The result does not rule out savings on specified q,Q subclasses,
one fixed finite support, particular easy instances, or contracts with
free off-candidate target evaluation, known normalizers, or prefix
local probes. It does not establish a practical wall-clock speedup,
historical priority, or external acceptance. It proves a mathematical
obstruction for the exact restricted operation in the trial.
