ACTUAL INVESTIGATION RECORD

1. Read the trial protocol and designated common continuation, theorem,
   comparator, and failure records. Did not inspect sibling trials,
   evaluator audits, or conversation histories. Did not rerun settled
   fixed-prefix or joint-KL calculations. No subagents or outside messages.

2. Initial route considered: reuse a low entrance/rare trap obstruction
   with a fixed labelled low set. This alone does not give a universal
   null-cost lower bound: the rule sees the exact q and Q, can depend on
   labels, and may place its longer stopping histories on that particular
   set. A naive anonymous-weight indistinguishability argument would be
   insufficient. This route was not promoted as a proof.

3. Replaced the fixed set with a hidden k-subset of a large, fully
   disclosed entrance population, paired by public symmetric Q with rare
   states. q and Q are identical for every hidden assignment and the
   null. The hidden subset changes only unseen target values. This removes
   the label-bias problem without suppressing any public information.

4. Derived the exact conditional subset probability (k)_D/(m)_D for
   each complete null stopped history, with D the number of distinct
   entrance labels. A cap bounds D, and rare-state observations cost at
   most M*r. Only after this exact history treatment did Jensen supply
   a lower bound in terms of E[T]. This specifically addresses the
   optional-stopping problem rather than assuming independent stopping.

5. First parameterization considered p=epsilon^2, rare proposal mass
   r=p/t^2 and still smaller low target mass, at the frozen exponential
   t. It would give entropy cost O(epsilon), and long trap residence,
   sufficient for the main endpoint. It was superseded, not disproved.

6. Simplified and strengthened the finite construction: let
   v=8(t+1)^2, p=epsilon^2/(1+log v), r=p/v; each low entrance has target
   probability r/m and each paired rare state target probability p/m.
   Both local and global trap exit probabilities are then bounded by
   r/p, while trap entropy is at most epsilon^2. This works uniformly
   for every finite common t>=1 and gives an elementary capture bound
   c_0=63*beta/128.

7. Checked the load-bearing conditions analytically: all probabilities
   positive, same K on null, exact raw oracle matching, public pair
   labels retained, no prefix L access, outside-low global acceptance,
   trap persistence for finite t, forward-KL strict margin, current-state
   output, and nonnegative contribution of all other prefix branches.
   Derived the explicit TV lower tradeoff and then the expected-null-cost
   bound. Letting b approach b_K only after the asymptotic lower bound
   closes every rho<1.

8. No numerical CPU calculation, simulation, install, paid resource, or
   web lookup was performed. The work is an analytic proof. The initial
   fixed-label gap and superseded parameterization are retained above;
   there were no failed numerical executions to report.
