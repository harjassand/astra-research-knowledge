3. Adjoint localized rotations, chosen route.
The square of a path has 3n-3 supported symmetric degrees of freedom, while
three query columns give 3n output coordinates. If the map is injective,
its adjoint has precisely a three-dimensional kernel. Multiplying a
skew-rotation field by a scalar tent makes its adjoint edge values depend
only on local differences of that tent. Four disjoint tents therefore
provide a four-dimensional space of uniformly low residuals. Intersecting
with the orthogonal complement of the three-dimensional kernel forces a
small nonzero singular value. This yields Frobenius amplification of order
r*n, hence operator amplification of order r*sqrt(n).

4. Row-cap issue and rejected shortcut.
Did not normalize rows and assert monotonicity: the source's exact
counterexample invalidates that step. Instead used the separate diagonal
lower bound 1/r, where r is the smallest row norm, and combined it with the
r*sqrt(n) bound by max(a,b)>=sqrt(ab). This gives n^(1/4) uniformly over all
row-capped dense X. Zero rows and noninjective maps are treated explicitly.

5. Key proof challenges checked.
(a) Small adjoint residuals could merely lie in the exact kernel: four
independent fields versus nullity three, followed by explicit intersection
with the kernel's orthogonal complement, eliminates this failure.
(b) Residuals from disjoint fields need not be disjoint: the proof pays a
factor two by the triangle inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz, without assuming
residual orthogonality.
(c) A Frobenius inverse obstruction alone is insufficient: the argument
explicitly loses sqrt(n) before making its operator claim.
(d) The adjoint requires the supported symmetric projection and a factor
1/2 on off-diagonal entries: exact rational checks verify both identities.
(e) Unit-row statements do not settle capped rows: the diagonal tradeoff
is included in the proof, with the correct quantifier order.
