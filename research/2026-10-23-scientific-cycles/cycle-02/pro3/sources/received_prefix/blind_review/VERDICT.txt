1. THE CONTRACT AND ITS QUANTIFIERS
----------------------------------

The common contract permits arbitrary finite support sizes varying with
epsilon, gives the entire labelled q and symmetric Q, restricts target
values to counted iid candidates during the prefix, withholds the
normalizer and numerical target-dependent local transition information,
and requires the actual stopped IMH current state. The candidate cap is
2 n_safe, including initialization, duplicates, and rejected candidates.
Subsequent evolution is exactly the fixed instance kernel
C_pi = (I + alpha G + beta L_pi)/2 for the prescribed deterministic number
of steps.

Both proofs preserve these features. Their finite supports have size
2 n_safe^3, so they do not improperly extrapolate a fixed-support argument
or deny the algorithm information about support completion. All numerical
q entries and every local pairing are public. The uncertain object is a
subset assignment encoded in unseen target values, not any public label,
proposal probability, or local pairing.

To disprove the conjunction in the contract, it is sufficient to use one
fixed zeta, here zeta=1, because the same proposed stopping family must
satisfy safety for every fixed zeta>0. The common safety threshold then
applies to all epsilon-dependent finite targets constructed in the proof.
Neither proof exchanges this uniform guarantee for a separate threshold
depending on each target.

The supremum over q,Q in the savings condition is essential and is used
correctly. The adverse public input may vary with epsilon. The alternative
target subset may depend on the stopping rule through an existence
argument, but it is a fixed target for each run, not a target chosen
adaptively after seeing that run's observations.

2. PROOF_X: ADMISSIBILITY, NORMALIZATION, AND ENTROPY
--------------------------------------------------

In proof_x Section 2, q assigns masses (1-r)/m to ordinary labels and
r/m to paired rare labels. The local proposal is the deterministic
symmetric swap within each pair and is independent of the hidden subset S.
For every k-subset S, the target masses sum to s+c+p=1, with all masses
strictly positive once the stated eventual parameter conditions hold.

The normalized importance weight of an ordinary label in S is
h=s/[b(1-r)], b=k/m. Consequently the raw oracle W_S/h has normalizer
1/h=b(1-r)/s and raw value exactly 1 on every matched ordinary label.
The normalizer calculation is correct. Raw values, not merely normalized
weight ratios or a multiset of observations, match the null oracle.

The exact KL calculation in Section 2.1 is correct:
D = kappa + s log(s/[b(1-r)])
    + c log(c/[(1-b)(1-r)]).
The rare contribution is p log(p/r)=kappa. Under the stated low-weight
condition the s term is nonpositive. For d=(1-b)(1-r) in (0,1),
c log(c/d) <= -log d follows from c log c <= 0 and 0<c<1.
Thus the displayed upper bound on D follows without omitting a positive
entropy contribution.

The explicit Section 3 choice stays inside the admitted class at every
sufficiently small positive epsilon. Indeed
eta=b_K-b lies in [1/m,2/m), and
kappa=(1/2)log(1+exp(K) eta)>0 is of order 1/m.
The relation -log(1-b)=K-2 kappa is exact. Since
-log(1-r)=o(kappa), the entropy bound is strictly below K eventually.
No zero-mass limit or singular target is used as an admitted instance.
The raw weights and normalizers can be very large but remain finite and
positive, which is permitted by the ideal-real oracle contract.

3. PROOF_X: FULL STOPPED HISTORIES AND THE EXPECTATION BOUND
--------------------------------------------------------

The coupling in Section 2.2 is valid for a completely label-sensitive
randomized stopping rule. On F_S, the entire null candidate prefix through
its own stop lies in the low ordinary set. At every observed candidate
the raw target weight is 1 in both instances. Ordinary IMH therefore
accepts every proposal, and the two runs have the same state, q values,
identities, uniforms, private randomization, and stopping decision through
that time. The actual output is Y_tau in the low set. This is a pathwise
argument for the actual stopped output, not a fixed-time law.

Averaging a uniformly random k-subset independently of the complete null
experiment is legitimate because the public q and Q do not depend on S.
Given a full null stopped history containing D distinct ordinary labels,
its exact inclusion probability is (k)_D/(m)_D. This retains repeats and
all label-dependent information. With D<=tau<=M and k>=M^2, the product
bound in (2.2) is correct and gives at least (1/2)b^tau.

The event of any rare candidate before the random stop is contained in
the event of a rare candidate among the first M iid draws, so its
probability is at most Mr without any independence assumption about tau.
Subtracting this event and applying Jensen to the exact scalar random
variable b^tau yields
average_S P_0(F_S) >= (1/2)(b^(E_0 tau)-Mr).
This is the correct use of an expectation in an adaptive argument.
It does not substitute E[T] into q G^(T-1), and it does not condition away
false-stop branches.

Since the averaging concerns full probabilities, some deterministic S
attains the bound. That selection need not depend on an individual
realized transcript. Knowledge of q, Q, cumulative seen q-mass, or the
candidate labels cannot invalidate this conditional averaging step.

4. PROOF_X: FIXED-KERNEL CAPTURE AND ASYMPTOTICS
---------------------------------------------

For a low ordinary label, the target ratio to its paired rare label is
bp/s>=1. A first subsequent local choice captures it with probability
beta/2. At the captured rare label, the global exit probability is at
most r/p, using sum_y q(y)W_S(y)=1; its local exit probability is s/(bp).
The mixture therefore has the exit upper bound v in (2.4). The assumed
acceptance conditions also imply v<=1/2, so the retention estimate is
well-defined. Retention at the same individual label is a valid sufficient
event even though the TV test set contains all rare labels.

The test set R has stationary mass p. Counting only captured-and-retained
paths and retaining the nonnegative contributions of all other prefix
branches gives (2.5) with the correct beta/4 factor. The subsequent kernel
is fixed throughout; no adaptive local proposal, extra prefix target
query, or stopping at a random number of global moves is used.

For p=epsilon^2, m=n_safe^3, and kappa of order 1/m,
kappa/epsilon tends to infinity. Hence kappa/p dominates (K+1)/epsilon:
t v -> 0 at t=ceil(exp((K+1)/epsilon)).
Also Mr=o(epsilon), p=o(epsilon), and the remaining positivity and
acceptance requirements hold eventually. The persistence conclusion
h_t>=1/2 and the necessary safety inequality
b^d <= Mr+8(epsilon+p)/beta are justified.

The last quantitative step is also correct. Since
lambda_b=lambda_K+O(1/m), with lambda_b>=lambda_K, and
log(1/epsilon)/m -> 0, (4.2) implies (4.3). The ceiling bound
n_safe <= log(1/epsilon)/lambda_K + 2 follows from 0<gamma<1.
Combining them produces exactly the claimed additive constant.
The null q,Q sequence is specified independently of the stopping rule.

Result for proof_x: the original submission proves its full theorem,
including the stronger additive-constant claim, within the contract.

5. PROOF_Y: ADMISSIBILITY AND THE STOPPED-HISTORY BOUND
----------------------------------------------------

For proof_y, write V=8(t+1)^2 to distinguish the scalar called v in the
submission from the state labels v_i. The choices
p=epsilon^2/(1+log V), r=p/V are positive and finite for every finite
integer t>=1. They give r<=p/32 and p+r<1 eventually. The target masses
p/m, r/m on the hidden low labels, and
(1-p-fr)/(m-k) elsewhere are positive and sum to one.

The low normalized importance weight is h=r/(1-r). Supplying W_H/h
makes the raw value exactly 1 on those low labels and gives normalizer
1/h. This is the same exact raw-value matching required by the contract.
The public q and symmetric swap proposal do not depend on H.

The KL expression (3) is correct. The low term is nonpositive, the
remaining entrance term is bounded by -log((1-r)(1-f)), and
p log V<=epsilon^2. For every fixed b<b_K, f=floor(bm)/m<=b and
r<=epsilon^2/32; hence the entropy constraint has a strict eventual
margin, uniformly over finite t. Taking b upward to b_K later does not
treat the limiting boundary instance as an admitted target.

The Section 4 coupling matches full histories in both directions on
the all-low stopped event, including states, uniforms, labels, repeats,
and private randomization. Conditional on an entire null stopped history,
the exact hidden-subset inclusion probability is again (k)_D/(m)_D.
For a=(k-M+1)/m, every factor is at least a, since the numerator is at
least k-M+1 and the denominator at most m. With 0<a<1 and D<=T_0,
this is at least a^(T_0). The rare-candidate union bound and Jensen
therefore give (7). The argument permits arbitrary use of known q and Q;
it does not introduce anonymous observations or hidden public data.

6. PROOF_Y: CAPTURE CONSTANT AND FINITE-TIME TRADEOFF
--------------------------------------------------

On a low entrance v_i, its paired trap has target ratio p/r>1.
At the paired trap the reverse local acceptance is r/p. These are
ordinary MH acceptances for the same fixed public Q.

The proof also correctly checks that every normalized importance weight
outside the low entrance set is at least the low weight h. For the other
entrances this is exactly the inequality equivalent to p+r<=1.
Thus all global proposals from the low set accept, and the probability
of remaining inside it for one C step is
z=(1+alpha(1-r)f)/2. The probability of a local capture on a step is
beta/2.

The paths staying in the low set until a first local capture at step j
are disjoint across j and have probability z^(j-1) beta/2. After capture,
the global exit bound is r/p and the local exit is r/p, so the mixture
exit probability is at most r/(2p). The stated retention loss is
t r/(2p)=t/[16(t+1)^2]<=1/64.
The geometric capture sum and retention bound therefore give (8).
In particular c_t>=63 beta/128 follows simply by retaining the first
capture term. No mixing approximation is needed.

The actual stopped law gives the target test set A_H mass at least
c_t P_H(E_H). Its stationary mass is pf. Averaging these genuine TV
lower bounds over H yields (9); the positive-part strengthening is
legitimate because the average event probability is nonnegative.
All other stopped branches contribute nonnegative mass to the test event
and have not been discarded from the law. No falsely independent
conditioning or change of the subsequent kernel occurs.

7. PROOF_Y: LIMIT ORDERS AND FULL FRACTIONAL IMPOSSIBILITY
-------------------------------------------------------

Safety and (9) imply (10). Uniformly over finite t>=1,
Mr+(epsilon+pf)/c_0 = epsilon/c_0 + O(n epsilon^2).
Thus its logarithm has the expansion stated in Section 7.
For each fixed b<b_K, a tends to b and
n_safe=log(1/epsilon)/lambda_K+O(1), yielding the lower ratio
lambda_K/(-log b).

The public construction can depend on t and epsilon because the time
schedule is fixed and common to all instances and the savings criterion
takes a supremum over q,Q at each epsilon. No circular choice of a
target-dependent running time is used. For every fixed b, the liminf of
this supremum is at least lambda_K/(-log b). Taking the supremum of these
lower bounds as b increases to b_K gives one. A threshold uniform in b
is not needed: b is held fixed before each liminf is taken.

Result for proof_y: the original submission proves its full theorem.
Its arbitrary-finite-common-schedule extension is justified by the
t-uniform entropy and error bounds. For a very short schedule the
safety premise may be infeasible; that does not invalidate this
conditional necessary-cost theorem.

8. SCOPE AND FINAL DISPOSITION
-----------------------------

Both proofs give an obstruction only for the exact candidate-only,
unknown-normalizer, bounded-prefix, public-q/public-Q operation and
uniform null-family criterion stated in the contract. They do not rule
out savings on a chosen easy subclass or one fixed small support, or
under off-candidate target queries, known normalizers, pre-prefix local
probes, or access to numerical L_pi tables. Both explicitly maintain
these limits.

The common comparator and its upper exponent are supplied by the
contract; no new random-prefix lower mixing coefficient is inferred.
The two proofs have different additional strengths: proof_x supplies
the explicit additive-constant bound at the required exponential
schedule; proof_y supplies the leading-fraction lower bound for any
finite common deterministic schedule. These distinctions do not change
the full endpoint credit for either proof.

No concrete mathematical gap or incorrect step was found in either
submitted argument. The conclusions rest on checked symbolic
inequalities and quantifiers, not length, plausibility, numerical
experiments, or any information about the anonymized arms.
