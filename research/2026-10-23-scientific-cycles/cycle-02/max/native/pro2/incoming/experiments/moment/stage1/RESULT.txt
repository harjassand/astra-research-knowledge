Exact atomic specification
--------------------------
Let a_1,...,a_64 and b_1,...,b_63>0 be the saved exact Jacobi coefficients.
Let J be their64-by64 symmetric Jacobi matrix. Define rational functions by
  R_1(t)=1/(t-a_1),
  R_j(t)=1/(t-a_j-b_(j-1)^2 R_(j-1)(t)),
  R(t)=R_64(t)=e_64^T(tI-J)^(-1)e_64.
For y=x or y=x+2^(-60), define the EXACT rational quantities
  v(y)=(S-y)/(R(S)-R(y)),
  d(y)=y-v(y)R(y).
Their numerator/denominator pairs are stored in the corresponding witness.
Form the65-by65 symmetric tridiagonal matrix
  T_y = [[J, sqrt(v(y))*e_64], [sqrt(v(y))*e_64^T, d(y)]].
The square root is the positive algebraic square root, not a rounded number.
Numerically, for y=x, v is about0.009047734544225033 and d is about0.52731271859.

The65 atoms t_1<...<t_65 are the roots of the monic recurrence
  q_0(t)=1, q_1(t)=t-a_1,
  q_j(t)=(t-a_j)q_(j-1)(t)-b_(j-1)^2 q_(j-2)(t), 2<=j<=64,
  q_65(t)=(t-d(y))q_64(t)-v(y)q_63(t).
Each root is identified unambiguously by its saved rational isolating interval;
t_2=y and t_65=S are exact rational nodes, not merely narrow intervals.
All remaining roots are algebraic. Define p_0=1 and recursively
  p_1(t)=(t-a_1)/b_1,
  p_j(t)=((t-a_j)p_(j-1)(t)-b_(j-1)p_(j-2)(t))/b_j, 2<=j<=63,
  p_64(t)=((t-a_64)p_63(t)-b_63 p_62(t))/sqrt(v(y)).
For EVERY atom set
  w_i(y)=1/(sum_(j=0)^64 p_j(t_i)^2),
  mu_y=sum_(i=1)^65 w_i(y) delta_(t_i).
This is a full exact atomic measure. Its definition is sufficient to evaluate
each atom and weight to arbitrary certified precision. Every w_i is strictly
positive and the sum is1, since these are the spectral weights of the real
irreducible symmetric T_y at e_1. The source of positivity is this theorem,
not a floating eigensolver. Approximate root/weight arrays are not the measure.
The nondecisive weight intervals use less root refinement and may be wide;
this affects neither their exact algebraic definition nor the CDF result.

Schur complement for S I-T_y is

and for S I+T_y it is

Consequently -S I<T_y<=S I, so the entire measure has the REQUIRED support,
without replacing the support by the original J node range. This uses exactly

This algebraic path argument includes the odd degree127 and all lower degrees;
it is not an approximate moment residual test. Therefore mu_y is a probability
measure with exactly the required moment functional L on polynomials of

Explicit polynomial dual certificates
-------------------------------------
All symbols in the following formulas are EXACT algebraic numbers specified
by maximum_witness.json, including r and z_3,...,z_64. Put h=x-r>0 and

  B(t) = ((S-t)/(S-r)) product_(i=3)^64 ((t-z_i)/(r-z_i))^2,
  A = 1/(S-r) + 2 sum_(i=3)^64 1/(z_i-r),
  T = 1/B(x),
  C = (T-1-A h)/h^2.

B is a polynomial of degree125. Define two polynomials of degree at most127:

  P_minus(t) = ((x-t)/h) B(t) [1+(A+1/h)(t-r)],
  P_plus(t)  = B(t) [1+A(t-r)+C(t-r)^2].

These are an explicit compact certificate format: products, sums and exact
algebraic root selectors. No coefficient rounding or numerical polynomial
positivity assumption is hidden in this representation. All denominators are
nonzero by the checked root ordering. The identities

  P_minus(r)=1, P_minus'(r)=0,
  P_minus(x)=0, P_minus(z_i)=0, P_minus(S)=0,
  P_plus(r)=1, P_plus'(r)=0,
  P_plus(x)=1, P_plus(z_i)=0, P_plus(S)=0

follow by substitution. Values at the interior z_i have double zeros.

Proof of the global inequalities
--------------------------------
For t<z_3, B(t)>0. Let g(t)=1/B(t). Its constant prefactor is positive and
it is a product of (S-t)^(-1) and (z_i-t)^(-2). Every derivative of each
factor is strictly positive on t<z_3; hence g',g'',g''' are strictly positive
there. We have g(r)=1 and g'(r)=A. The quadratic
  H_2(t)=1+A(t-r)+C(t-r)^2
is the Hermite interpolant to g at r (value and derivative) and x (value).
For any t<=x, the Hermite remainder has sign
  g(t)-H_2(t) = g'''(xi)/6 * (t-r)^2*(t-x) <=0,
where xi lies between the interpolation/evaluation points, all below z_3.
At repeated points use continuity. Multiplying by B(t)>0 proves
  P_plus(t)>=1 for -S<=t<=x.
Strict convexity of g gives C>0, and A>0. For x<t<=S, each factor in B(t)
is nonnegative, and H_2(t)>0 since t-r>0. Therefore P_plus(t)>=0 there.
Thus P_plus>=1_[-S,x] on the entire support.

For the lower polynomial define G(t)=((x-t)/h)B(t)>0 for t<x. Then
k(t)=1/G(t) is again a positive product of reciprocal powers of factors
c-t with c>=x, and k''>0 on t<x. Its tangent at r is
  1+(A+1/h)(t-r).
Strict convexity puts this tangent below k everywhere t<x, with equality
only at r. Multiplying by G(t)>0 yields P_minus(t)<=1 for t<x. For x<t<=S,
G(t)<=0 and the tangent factor is strictly positive, so P_minus(t)<=0.
At x the polynomial is0. Hence
  P_minus <= 1_[-S,x) <= 1_[-S,x] <= P_plus
pointwise on [-S,S]. This is a proved polynomial certificate, not a claim
that the classical theorem applies under unchecked density hypotheses.

Integrating against ANY feasible measure gives
  L(P_minus)<=mu([-S,x])<=L(P_plus).
Using the exactly feasible quadrature mu_x and its interpolation values,
  L(P_minus)=w_1(x)=l,
  L(P_plus)=w_1(x)+w_2(x)=u.
Thus mu_x attains the inclusive upper bound and the open lower bound.
Moreover,
  P_plus-P_minus = ((S-t)/(S-x)) * ((t-r)/(x-r))^2
                  * product_(i=3)^64 ((t-z_i)/(x-z_i))^2,
which is the nonnegative cardinal polynomial for the atom at x. Its
L-value is w_2(x)=u-l. This also certifies the largest possible mass at x.

Inclusive infimum and nonattainment
----------------------------------
For y>x sufficiently close to x, R(y), v(y), d(y) vary continuously and
v(y)>0, the strict left support Schur inequality persists, and the right
Schur complement remains exactly0. Irreducible eigenvalues and spectral
weights vary continuously. The first node remains below x; the second is
y>x; all later nodes remain above x. Hence
  mu_y([-S,x])=w_1(y) -> w_1(x)=l as y decreases to x.
This proves the inclusive infimum equals l. An entirely concrete member
of this family, y=x+2^(-60), is certified in near_infimum_witness.json.

It cannot attain l: the gap 1_[-S,x]-P_minus is strictly positive everywhere
except the64 nodes r,z_3,...,z_64,S. Equality in the integral would force any
minimizing measure to be supported on precisely this finite zero set.
But the polynomial
  Q(t)=(t-r)(t-S) product_(i=3)^64 (t-z_i)
has degree64, vanishes on that set, and Q(x)!=0. Feasibility would imply
  integral Q dmu = L(Q) = w_2(x)Q(x)!=0,
contradicting support on its zeros. Therefore there is no inclusive minimum.
Convex mixtures of mu_x with the above approximating measures attain every
value strictly between l and u, which proves the claimed range (l,u].
The inclusive maximum is unique: equality in its polynomial bound forces
support on the65 canonical nodes, and their weights are determined by the
moments through degree64 (invertibility of the distinct-node Vandermonde).

Classical context and scope
---------------------------
This is a concrete use of classical canonical quadrature, not a new extremal
moment theorem. A close reference is Shoni Gilboa and Ron Peled, "A differential
version of the Chebyshev-Markov-Stieltjes inequalities", arXiv:1407.2467v2,
2015, https://arxiv.org/html/1407.2467v2 . Section2 describes canonical
representations of degree2n-1; equations(70)-(73) and Lemma6.5 give the
Hermite polynomial majorant/minorant mechanism. Their introduction carefully
distinguishes the inclusive upper CDF and open lower CDF. The proof above is
self-contained for this discrete finite-input functional and does not import
the paper's regularity assumptions on a weight density.

The width0.34015 is smaller than the saved family-specific staircase floor,
yet remains substantial. It establishes ambiguity for THIS SINGLE probe's
retained127 moments under the specified support information. No compatible
common operator across the eight probes is constructed here. This is not a
universal lower bound for joint-probe inference, nor for the unweighted
empirical CDF of a common fixed original A. Original A, the source's backward
completion B, and these abstract representing measures remain distinct.
