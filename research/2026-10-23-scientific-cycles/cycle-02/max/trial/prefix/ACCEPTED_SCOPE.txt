EXACT ACCESS AND PERFORMANCE CONTRACT
Known positive KL certificate K (including on the easy null pi=q); public
labelled full-support q and public target-independent symmetric proposal Q;
unknown target normalizer; target values during the global IMH prefix only at
counted iid candidates; no unseen-weight queries, target-code inspection or
local probes. All identities, q-values, repetitions, acceptance uniforms and
private randomization remain available. T is a stopping time bounded by
1<=T<=2 n_safe(epsilon); the actual stopped IMH state is the output. The later
kernel is fixed per instance: C=(I+alpha G+(1-alpha)MH(Q,pi))/2.

The primary constants were K=1, alpha=1/2. Both arguments cover fixed K>0 and
0<alpha<1. Let b=1-exp(-K), lambda=-ln b,
gamma=(1-alpha)/(1-alpha*b), and
n_safe=max(1,ceil(ln(gamma/epsilon)/lambda)+1).
Uniform safety at the prescribed common subsequent time for every zeta>0 is
incompatible with any fixed fractional null-family saving rho<1. Precisely,
liminf_(epsilon->0) sup_(public q,Q) E_(pi=q)[T]/n_safe >=1.
The supremum permits support and public inputs to depend on epsilon. This is
not a lower bound on every easy instance or on one fixed finite support.

ARM A: ORDINARY RESEARCH
Accept the leading-fraction impossibility for ANY finite deterministic
subsequent-time schedule common to all instances. Positive paired finite
targets and a hidden subset share exactly the same public q,Q and all raw
values on matched candidate histories. Averaging over subsets after conditioning
on the entire stopped labelled transcript handles arbitrary identity-dependent
rules and repeated labels. Jensen is applied only to that exact expression.
The trap parameter depends on the prescribed finite time. A strict KL margin
is held first, then sent to zero after the asymptotic cost bound. The explicit
finite tradeoff in RESULT.txt is accepted with its constants and scope.

ARM B: EXPLICIT DEPENDENCY-BYPASS INSTRUCTION
Accept the stronger additive bound on an explicit public null sequence,
E_(pi=q)[T] >= n_safe-C_(K,alpha),
C_(K,alpha)=3+ln(9/(1-alpha))/lambda,
at t=ceil(exp((K+1)/epsilon)). This is one time required by uniform safety.
The hidden subset size approaches the KL boundary while retaining positive
entropy slack kappa of order 1/(ln(1/epsilon))^3. With p=epsilon^2, the escape
exponent kappa/p dominates the prescribed time exponent. Root checked the
falling-factorial coverage factor, normalization, finite positivity, KL slack,
local capture and subsequent retention, and the explicit ceiling/constant
conversion. No target depends on observations during an actual run.

WHY THESE PROOFS SURVIVE THE PUBLIC-INFORMATION CHECK
Each family fixes the complete labelled q and Q independently of the stopping
rule's realized history. The target assignment is hidden only where the oracle
contract already withholds target values. All paired labels and q masses are
known. The subset averaging conditions on the FULL null history before taking
expectations. The proofs do not require ignorance of known support or q, do not
set the supplied KL bound to zero on the null, and do not use a normalized
target oracle. They do not insert E[T] into a deterministic-prefix formula.

LIMITATIONS
Free off-candidate target queries, known target code/normalizer, prefix local
probes or transition tables, restricted public proposal classes, one fixed
finite support, or a changed fallback/cost criterion require new analysis.
Neither proof rules out useful instance-specific adaptation. Neither demonstrates
practical sampling speed or a complete end-to-end advantage over pure IMH.
The broader Pro3 sampling mission remains active.

RESEARCH-METHOD RESULT
Both arms meet the frozen primary endpoint. The treatment has a sharper additive
cost bound at the stated exponent; the ordinary arm covers any finite common
subsequent schedule. These additional scopes were not ranked in advance and do
not create a post-hoc winner. Both derive a finite stopped-history tradeoff.
Reported participant elapsed times were 590 seconds ordinary and 570 seconds
