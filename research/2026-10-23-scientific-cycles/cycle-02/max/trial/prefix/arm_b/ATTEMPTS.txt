1. Endpoint reversal during the initial reading/proof-search phase.
Exact endpoint: retain the common post-prefix TV guarantee at
ceil(exp((K+zeta)/epsilon)) for every zeta>0, while reducing the worst null
expected candidate count by a fixed fraction relative to n_safe, with the
candidate-only oracle, public q and Q, actual IMH output, and cap unchanged.
A stronger intermediate that might seem necessary is to prove that observed

of the deterministic residual floor. Neither is needed. The weakest useful
obstruction is an unconditional lower bound on the probability of stopping
in an unseen low-normalized-weight region, coupled to just one subsequent
local capture and long retention. A constant capture probability suffices.

2. Rejected direct reuse of the three-state family.
A three-state pair would expose its low-region label through public q and Q
and can have frequent support completion. Matching anonymous weights alone
would violate the contract. I did not assert a lower bound from that family.
This motivated a growing labelled support and randomization of the hidden
low subset, with q and Q fixed before choosing that subset.

3. Productive bypass.
Use m ordinary labels and m rare labels, with the public symmetric Q pairing
each ordinary label to its rare partner. Hide the low set among k ordinary
labels; all low-set raw oracle weights equal the null weight 1. The null and
hard instance share all public information. On any null stopped transcript
whose candidates lie in the hidden set, all states, uniforms, weights,
acceptances and stopping decisions coincide pathwise. Averaging over the
hidden subset gives a hypergeometric inclusion probability bounded below
by a constant times b^T, even for label-dependent stopping. Jensen gives
b^(E_null T). This is an actual stopped-history argument, not substitution
of E[T] into a deterministic prefix formula.

4. Initial full-impossibility route.
For any fixed b<1-exp(-K) sufficiently close to its upper endpoint, the
tradeoff implies E_null T >= log(1/epsilon)/(-log b)-O(1). Taking b toward
the endpoint rules out every fixed fractional saving. Public identities,
known q masses, duplicates, and arbitrary computations on Q are retained.

5. Strengthening while checking the proof.
Let m=n_safe^3 and k=floor((1-exp(-K))*m)-1. Then b=k/m approaches the
entropy endpoint with a strictly positive slack of order 1/m. Choose the
trap target mass p=epsilon^2 and entropy allocated to traps
kappa=(K+log(1-b))/2. The trap holding exponent kappa/p still dominates
(K+1)/epsilon because epsilon*n_safe^3 -> 0. This gives the stronger
necessary cost E_null T >= n_safe-O(1) on an explicit public null family.
All finite instances remain strictly positive and satisfy the KL bound;
no singular limiting instance is treated as admitted.
