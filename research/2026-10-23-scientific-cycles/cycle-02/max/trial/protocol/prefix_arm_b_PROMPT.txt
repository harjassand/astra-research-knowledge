2. RECOMMENDED PRIMARY QUESTION

Can a scale-invariant stopping rule safely save a NONVANISHING FRACTION of the deterministic candidate budget, uniformly on the easy null family pi=q, while preserving the uniform forward-KL post-prefix guarantee?

This is an explicit, restricted meaning of “adapt to easier pairs.” It asks whether observations can certify a useful reduction even when the proposal is actually perfect but the supplied certificate remains the same positive K. It does not require improvements on every easy pair, and an impossibility here would NOT rule out all instance-specific savings, known finite-support coverage, or richer target-query access.

For a bounded trial freeze the primary constants K=1 and alpha=1/2. A result for general fixed K>0 and alpha in (0,1) is welcome but not required. All logarithms are natural. Let beta=1-alpha and define

 b=1-exp(-K), lambda=-log b, gamma=beta/(1-alpha b),
 n_safe(epsilon)=max(1, ceil(log(gamma/epsilon)/lambda)+1).

The inherited deterministic rule uses exactly n_safe candidates, including initialization. Its prefix consists of n_safe-1 ordinary global IMH transitions. It satisfies the uniform post-prefix exponent K; this is the mandatory comparator, not an uncorrected hybrid.

3. SCIENTIFIC MODEL: PUBLIC INFORMATION AND ORACLES

State space. Use known labelled finite or countable spaces, with q(x)>0 on every state. The mathematical class includes arbitrarily large finite supports and countable full-support proposals. Do not restrict to one fixed finite support: full observed support would otherwise supply an exact-certification shortcut and change the adaptation question. A lower bound may use a finite subfamily with support sizes depending on epsilon, provided it says so.

Public q information. The participant receives the entire normalized proposal law q as a mathematical object, not merely anonymous samples. On a finite space it is the complete labelled probability table; on a countable space it is an explicitly specified probability mass function and exact iid sampler. Candidate identity, q(candidate), repeated identities, and cumulative q-mass of the observed set are all available. Deterministic computation on this information is unrestricted. No theorem may pretend these observations are unavailable.

Target information. An unknown positive function w_tilde(x) has finite positive Z=sum_x q(x) w_tilde(x), and pi(x)=q(x) w_tilde(x)/Z. Only the certified population inequality D(pi||q)<=K is supplied. K is an upper bound, not the actual divergence. The same fixed K=1 is supplied even under the easy null pi=q. The normalizer Z, normalized target probabilities, actual KL and any target-tail or unseen-mass certificate are unavailable.

Candidate/weight stream. At counted candidate i, draw Y_i iid from q and reveal (Y_i, q(Y_i), w_tilde(Y_i)). Weights are exact ideal-real values; repeated values may be cached. No free weight query at an unobserved state is permitted. No sample from pi, rejection-sampling oracle, normalizer oracle or target code that exposes unseen values is supplied. Every iid proposal draw is counted, including duplicates and rejected proposals. This is a candidate-only target-evaluation restriction, and must remain visible in every theorem statement.

Public local proposal. Supply a known, target-independent symmetric Markov proposal Q on the same labelled space. Q may be reducible, including identity; G will still provide the global component. The later local kernel is ordinary Metropolis-Hastings with proposal Q and target pi:
 for x!=y, L_pi(x,y)=Q(x,y) min(1, pi(y)/pi(x)),
 with diagonal completion. Its acceptance ratio can be evaluated using q(y)w_tilde(y)/(q(x)w_tilde(x)); Z cancels. Thus L_pi is fixed and pi-reversible within each scientific instance. Q is fully disclosed, including its relation to candidate labels. It is not silently treated as hidden.

L access during the prefix. The prefix rule may inspect and compute with q and Q, but it may not query L_pi(x,y), draw local transitions, or use local simulations that evaluate new target weights. During the prefix these would constitute additional target observations and a different operation. After stopping, L_pi is available through the specified MH implementation and all required ordinary transition evaluations. No target information obtained in the subsequent phase can be sent back to change the stopping decision.

Why this restriction is representative. It models a reusable initialization front end that observes proposals and their expensive importance weights before handing its state to a fixed local/global sampler. It retains public identities, q and the public local proposal, rather than reducing to an anonymous weight-only experiment. It does not model applications allowing arbitrary off-proposal target evaluations, local probes before initialization, or full inspection of a known target formula. Conclusions must not be exported to those richer access models.

4. ALLOWED PREFIX AND COUNTED OUTPUT

Initialize X_1=Y_1. For i>=2, update X_i by ordinary IMH: accept candidate Y_i with probability min(1,w_tilde(Y_i)/w_tilde(X_(i-1))), using independent uniforms U_i, otherwise retain X_(i-1). All candidate weights, rejection outcomes, identities and the private random seed are observable to the rule.

Choose an integer stopping time T_epsilon for the filtration of this full prefix history and the public inputs (q,Q,K,alpha,epsilon). The rule must be measurable and unchanged when all observed w_tilde values are multiplied by any common positive constant. It cannot look ahead at future candidates. It may stop after an accepted or rejected transition. The output is the ACTUAL current IMH state X_T; no separate resampling rule, selected best candidate or postselection is allowed.

Recommended bounded-fallback restriction: 1<=T_epsilon<=2 n_safe(epsilon) almost surely. This gives a well-defined finite operation and excludes rare enormous prefixes as an unpriced workaround. It is an additional restriction, not an inherited theorem. The deterministic comparator T=n_safe is allowed, so the design has a known admissible baseline. A proposed theorem should state if it uses the stronger cap T<=n_safe.

The primary resource is E[T_epsilon] counted candidates. Also report distinct weight evaluations if needed, but do not replace candidate cost by that number. The law mu_epsilon=Law(X_T) includes all branches, uniforms and stopping decisions. It is not obtained by replacing N by E[T] in qG^(N-1), nor by assuming independence between T and X_T.

5. ENDPOINT THAT MUST BE PRESERVED

Let G be the instance's ordinary independence-MH kernel and C_pi=(I+alpha G+beta L_pi)/2. Starting from X_T, run exactly t subsequent C_pi transitions. The kernel and mixture weight remain fixed throughout that phase. No adaptive local-kernel selection or stopping at an exact number of random global selections is permitted.

Uniform safety at sharp upper exponent means: for every fixed zeta>0, there is epsilon_0(K,alpha,zeta)>0, independent of q,w_tilde,Q, such that for every 0<epsilon<epsilon_0,

 sup_(admitted q,w_tilde,Q) TV(mu_epsilon C_pi^ceil(exp((K+zeta)/epsilon)), pi) <= epsilon.

The SAME stopping family must meet this for every zeta>0. A fully explicit finite-time certificate is stronger and useful, but asymptotic uniformity with a proved common threshold suffices for the primary theoretical endpoint. The trial asks to preserve the known sharp UPPER coefficient K; participants must not claim a new matching random-prefix lower coefficient without proving it.

Uniform easy-family savings means the existence of a fixed rho<1 such that

 limsup_(epsilon->0) sup_(public q,Q) E_(pi=q)[T_epsilon] / n_safe(epsilon) <= rho.

Under pi=q all weights are constant up to arbitrary common scale; the participant is not told that this equality holds. Taking the supremum over fully known q,Q is deliberate: it distinguishes general observation-based recognition of easy proposals from a special finite-support trick. The order allows the adverse q or Q to vary with epsilon. Claims for one fixed q, a restricted Q, or a specified easy subclass are narrower and must be labelled.

These two displayed conditions, the observable information above and the cap together are the frozen primary question. Do not strengthen safety only after one arm fails, weaken the savings requirement for one arm, or change the normalizer/candidate access contract after observing outputs.

6. USEFUL IMPOSSIBILITY AND CONSTRUCTION OUTCOMES

Full construction success: an explicit admissible stopping family, a proof of uniform safety for the actual stopped law, and a nonvanishing easy-family fractional saving as defined above. A fixed-N theorem or heuristic confidence flag is insufficient. A certificate may be an internal statistic, but the proof must account for all false-stop branches and optional-stopping bias without conditioning away error.

Full impossibility success: a theorem ruling out the conjunction of uniform safety and ANY fixed fractional saving rho<1 in this class. An explicit lower tradeoff covering only a nonempty range of rho, or a lower bound on early-stop probability with a quantified consequent TV obstruction, is a useful partial. State the quantified range and remaining gap rather than calling it a complete impossibility.

Other accepted substantive partials: a rigorously quantified adaptation/safety tradeoff for all admissible stopping rules; a new stopped-law representation strong enough to prove a nontrivial endpoint bound; or a construction with a precisely stated nontrivial easy subclass and the original safety guarantee. These do not receive full endpoint credit. Repeating F_N, deriving n_safe again, observing that mu_T may be biased, or merely identifying a gap in an attempted argument is not itself a new scientific outcome.

Non-results/invalid routes: silently give K=0 on the easy instance; infer certified KL from empirical weights; omit q labels/Q information from the experiment; allow a free target query or a free local-kernel probe; normalize the unknown target; change C after observations; substitute E[T] into a fixed-time formula; rely on a singular limiting instance as if admitted; or claim practical sampling speedup from a prefix-only theorem.

7. LOWER-BOUND INFORMATION MATCHING: WHAT MUST BE IDENTICAL

Any indistinguishability pair must have the SAME public labelled space, exact q, exact Q, certificate K, alpha, epsilon, target-oracle interface, and target values on the histories claimed to be indistinguishable. Candidate identities and known proposal masses are part of each transcript. Match the laws of the full stopped histories, including the IMH acceptance uniforms/states and private randomization; matching only a multiset of weights is insufficient.

The normalizers and target values at unseen states may differ; that is the scientific uncertainty the oracle contract permits. The derived L_pi can consequently differ between instances, but its public implementation Q+MH recipe must be identical, and the prefix has no permitted transition-query transcript to distinguish it. Each instance's L_pi remains fixed and reversible. If full numerical L tables or pre-prefix transition samples were disclosed instead, this matching argument would have to match them too: a common Q alone would then be insufficient, and the old target-dependent three-state L family cannot simply be reused.

Known q can expose support completion, unseen proposal mass, state-specific rarity, or a chosen hidden-set label. A lower proof must survive those facts. This is why public q and identities are explicitly included, why fixed finite-support easy examples cannot establish a uniform lower bound, and why restrictions on L access must be stated before scientific work begins.
