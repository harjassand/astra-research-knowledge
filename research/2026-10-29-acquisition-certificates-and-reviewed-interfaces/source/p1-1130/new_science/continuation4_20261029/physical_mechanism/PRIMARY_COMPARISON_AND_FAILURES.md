### Probability-flow and minimum-rejection constructions

Hidemaro Suwa and Synge Todo, *Markov Chain Monte Carlo Method without Detailed
Balance*, Physical Review Letters 105, 120603 (2010).
[Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.2262),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.105.120603).
Read the formulation and construction through equations (1)–(10), plus the
ergodicity caveat. They allocate stationary mass between candidate states and
minimize rejected mass. On a complete candidate graph their minimum normalized
rejection is max(0,2 max pi-1), precisely the complete-graph specialization of
our deficiency. This is a decisive predecessor: flow allocation and its complete
graph holding threshold cannot be presented as our invention. Their algorithm
directly chooses a software transition; the present supplier schedules an
unknown physical pair kernel. Its symmetric coefficients survive independent
unknown kinetic changes, while arbitrary nonreversible software flows do not.

[Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0202316),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.66.046701).

## Failed attacks and scope repairs during construction

1. **Merely adding state-dependent scan weights.** A common native invariant
   law does not make an arbitrary state-dependent scan correct. The weighted
   selected submeasure must itself be invariant under each selected operation.
   Pair-constant coefficients supply this property on the full hidden state.

2. **Claiming a no-go only for memoryless policies.** That would be a weak
   comparison: adaptive feedback could exploit history. The common-p witness
   gives an indicator-drift inequality for every causal policy and proves the
   same stationary duty lower bound even if p is known exactly.

3. **Equating equal-duration endpoints with a physical-time law.** Arbitrary
   endpoint kernels do not specify their intermediate paths. The all-time claim
   requires actual invariant semigroups, or the explicitly supplied finite-rate
   mode generator. Ordinary decision latency remains an implementation cost.

4. **Treating the mode generator as apparatus.** Known mathematical switching
   rates gamma q_j(Y) do not build a detector or its coupling. The source supplies
   a complete mathematical hybrid process only. Its finite-energy interpretation
   needs compatible active fibers and can fail at exact zero mode probabilities.

5. **Calling this a new memory architecture.** The code selector isolates logical
   basins and controls intentional stochastic activity, but it does not correct
   arbitrary uncontrolled background errors. Moreover, an available genuine R
   can simply be used continually. The honest consequence is the sharp resource
   law, not a claimed memory advantage.

6. **Ignoring convergence.** An optimum can disconnect the selected graph. A
   target-invariant law and an optimal activity fraction do not prepare that law.
   A positive-flow regularization increases duty and still needs a mixing theory.

7. **Treating the flow optimization as an invention.** Suwa–Todo and directed-loop
   equations are close predecessors; ordinary network flow is sufficient to
   obtain the optimum. The surviving candidate contribution is the physical
   hidden-state interface theorem, the arbitrary-history lower bound, and the
   exact finite-rate supplier with its limitations. This is a useful independent
   intermediate, not yet an established major foundational advance.
