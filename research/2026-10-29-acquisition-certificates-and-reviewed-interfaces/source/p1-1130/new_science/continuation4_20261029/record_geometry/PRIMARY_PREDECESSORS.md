# Primary predecessors inspected for the preparation question

29 October 2026. Searches were performed before selecting the obstruction.
The preserved Astra scientific-acceptance source and handoff were consulted.
No accepted result or N675 dependency was reopened. These source comparisons
do not establish priority for the present four-state theorem.

1. Laszlo Lovasz and Peter Winkler, *Exact Mixing in an Unknown Markov Chain*,
   Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 2, R15 (1995).
   <https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v2i1r15/pdf>
   Read the introduction, state-recognition interface, first-exit tree
   construction, and the accelerated stopping rule and proof in Sections 1–4.
   The method stops an unknown irreducible finite chain at its actual current
   state with the stationary law and finite expected cost. Its input includes
   recognition of all microscopic state identities. This supplies the strongest
   finite-state ordinary alternative here and prevents treating unknown
   transition probabilities alone as an exact-sampling obstruction.

2. Peter Morters and Istvan Redl, *Skorokhod embeddings for two-sided Markov
   chains*, arXiv:1407.4734; primary author PDF.
   <https://www.mi.uni-koeln.de/~moerters/papers/Shift-revision.pdf>
   Read the definitions, Theorems 1–4, examples and start of the allocation
   equivalence in Sections 1–2. The paper constructs stopping embeddings by
   balancing normalized local times and proves poor moment properties for
   nonnegative unbiased shifts. Its objective preserves a two-sided Markov
   path law after shifting, stronger than preparing a current finite hidden
   state. Consequently its theorem is a close predecessor for root's
   Palm/extra-head construction, but is not silently substituted for the
   separately proved four-state visible-only universality obstruction.

3. David Aldous and James Fill, *Reversible Markov Chains and Random Walks on
   Graphs*, Chapter 2, Section 2.2, especially Proposition 2.4 and its proof.
   <https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/users/aldous/RWG/Book_Ralph/Ch2.S2.html>
   Read the occupation-measure derivation. A finite stopped occupation
   measure obeys the stationary flow identity when its endpoints have the
   same law; the telescoping calculation gives the source-minus-target
   variant used in the scenery proof. This is established machinery. Our
   counterexample pairs its Poisson equation with hidden scenery coordinates.

4. Alexander Holroyd and Yuval Peres, *Extra heads and invariant allocations*,
   arXiv:math/0306402; Annals of Probability 33(1),31–52 (2005).
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0306402>
   The primary abstract and its account in Morters–Redl were read; the full
   PDF retrieval failed during this round. Shift couplings between stationary
   configurations and their Palm versions, and translation-invariant
   allocations, are established prior work. No uninspected detailed theorem
   from this paper is needed for the new elementary arguments.

The four-state proof uses an explicit rare-swap coupling, summable response
coefficients and optional sampling. Its infinite-mean conclusion does not
come from assuming the stronger two-sided-path objective of source 2. The
reversible scenery precision bound uses explicit mass-transport counting and
the stopped random-walk isometry, with no matching upper bound asserted.
