## Source/cost extension is paid, not borrowed from a bounded fixture

The earlier local currency proof stated a bounded-source hypothesis. The
current source is Gaussian-tailed. The SAME pointwise clock-drift inequality
does not depend on source support or partner moments. For any probability
g0 with A0=E_g0 Lambda(V0)<infinity, truncate/renormalize g0 to |v|<=R.
The earlier stopped-counter proof applies, even if this truncated g0 is not
the h-tilt of a mass1 source with the original energy; the termination proof
uses only its probability law, fixed a,c and the pointwise drift inequality.
For large R, its A0(R)<=2A0. Thus its finite-time expected full tree size has
a common bound exp[(2A0/K)(exp(Kt)-1)]-1.

Couple each truncated-source draw by rejecting raw g0 values outside R.
The expected total number of raw initial draws is its expected leaf count
divided by P_g0(|v|<=R), uniformly bounded for large R. By predictable exposure
of fresh draws, probability ANY discarded tail draw is at most that count
times P_g0(|v|>R), tending0. If none is discarded, its whole execution equals
the literal full-source execution. This proves a.s. termination for the full
source and a uniform finite expected count via convergence/Fatou. It does
not import a g_t sampler or an unacquired source speed cap. The original
generator/conservation identification then applies to g0=h f0/c.

For Gaussian f_delta all required source moments are finite. Returned squared
speed is at most the sum of squared source-leaf speeds. Exposing fresh leaf
draws predictably and applying the nonnegative stopped-sum identity therefore
also gives a finite g_t second moment on each compact time interval. This
justifies the old physical fourth-moment bound and its exponential-in-time
cost qualifier. No claim of an exponential LOWER bound is made.

For a=3,c=6, draw g0 by the known mixture(aM+|v|^2M)/6: plain standard3-normal
with probability1/2 or its energy-biased chi5 radial law with probability1/2.
Then reject with(1+delta A)/(1+|delta|). Its acceptance is exactly1/(1+|delta|)
because the hM-weighted angular perturbation integrates to0. The physical
source uses the same rejection on a plain Gaussian. All rejected source draws,
normal/exponential/angle calls, failed final physical rejections, null partner
events and their complete lineages are counted. A tiny source deviation does
not authorize replacing its sampler by M at an unpaid exactness target.

## Scope of the actual comparison

One predeclared128-output cohort at t=1 is run for the new continuous positive
consumer and this exact full-law lineage, with distinct fixed seeds and the
same source/moments/kernel. This is a comparison of two particular operations,
not a new source/time grid, statistical validation of a1e-12 law, or proof
against an optimal transient solver. The floating implementations retain
unvalidated arithmetic/PRNG errors; the target contract is mathematical and
ideal-real. Two prescribed native gain/loss paths exercise nonlinear vector
children separately and are explicitly not iid draws.

The ordinary Gaussian approximation is ALSO given the new explicit relaxation
bound. Nonlinear LVDSMC/control variates and conservative3D spectral solvers
receive the same complete input, invariants, bounded norm, Gaussian tails and
operator admission. They may be practically better for selected observables
or a validated tolerance. No full-cycle validated1e-12 transient implementation
of those methods is acquired here; its absence here is not a lower bound or
historical novelty claim. An equally informed ordinary route may adopt this
new bounded query itself; the theorem is a reusable operation, not an exclusive
advantage over all possible implementations of the same mathematics.

An important SAME-ACCURACY ordinary hybrid is also available: use the exact
lineage only for t<T_epsilon, and M afterward, where
T_epsilon=(400/nu0)log[D_eff Z_beta/(2epsilon)]_+. The paid bound then
qualifies all requested times and this hybrid ALREADY has finite uniform-time
cost at each fixed epsilon. The new consumer is not claimed to be the first
way to make such an abstract finite-time cap. Its specific stronger cost
contract has no T_epsilon-sized lineage and needs only ONE bounded query per
proposal once epsilon exceeds the displayed clipping floor. The old lineage
upper bound produces a very large epsilon-dependent hybrid guarantee; it is
not a lower bound, and sharper ordinary relaxation/spectral methods may win.
