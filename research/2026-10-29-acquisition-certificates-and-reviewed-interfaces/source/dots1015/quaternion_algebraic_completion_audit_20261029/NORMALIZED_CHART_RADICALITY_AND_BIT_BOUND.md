The ratios of universal coefficient derivatives give the unique common root in the q0=1 affine chart; the denominator stays nonzero locally. Hence q(a) is an analytic (indeed regular rational) function on S near a0, and H(a,q(a))=0 there.

Write the four gradient blocks at a0 as beta_i times cubic evaluation at q0. Let J be the 4×3 derivative of H with respect to the affine root coordinates. The coefficient evaluation map delta a→y=(delta H_i(q0)) is surjective: varying the q0³ coefficient independently in each block realizes any y in C⁴. The tangent condition dU(delta a)=0 is precisely beta·y=0. Every tangent vector to a smooth hypersurface is realized by a local analytic curve in it. Differentiating H(a,q(a))=0 therefore gives

    y + J dq = 0, for every y in beta-perpendicular.

The latter space has dimension3, so rank J=3. Its image is exactly beta-perpendicular. Thus the Jacobian of the four normalized equations in the four unknowns (q1,q2,q3,lambda) is invertible.

## Self-contained fixed-parameter bit-cost argument
It is insufficient to cite a bound on the degree of a FINAL Gröbner basis and silently conclude that an arbitrary implementation has bounded intermediate work. A particular degree-compatible Buchberger algorithm admits the following argument.

Start with m polynomials in n variables of maximum degree d, retain an append-only basis (no deletion of old generators during this argument), and add only fully reduced nonzero S-polynomial remainders. A final interreduction can be performed afterwards. After k additions, maximum degree is at most2^k d: S-polynomial formation at most doubles the current degree, and degree-compatible reduction cannot increase it. Each newly added leading monomial is not divisible by any prior leading monomial. At depth k only finitely many monomials of degree≤2^k d are possible. The resulting tree of possible leading-monomial sequences is finitely branching. Dickson's lemma forbids an infinite sequence in which no earlier monomial divides a later one; by König's lemma the entire tree has finite depth. Its bound depends only on n,m,d and is computable by exhaustive enumeration of this finite tree, however enormous.

Consequently the number of basis additions, degrees, monomials, critical pairs and reductions is bounded by a fixed function of n,m,d, independent of coefficient heights. Every reduction decreases the leading monomial among a finite bounded-degree set. Rational operations and exact zero tests are therefore bounded in number by a fixed constant when n,m,d are fixed. A fixed number of rational additions/multiplications/divisions yields intermediate bit lengths at most a constant multiple of the input bit length; elementary rational arithmetic then has polynomial bit cost. Subsequent fixed40-dimensional multiplication/Krylov operations and fixed-degree exact root refinement retain polynomial bit complexity. This is an existence/algorithmic complexity argument with immense constants, not an empirical bound for SymPy's F5 implementation or a paid physical latency schedule.

Primary corroboration: Weispfenning (1988), *Some bounds for the construction of Gröbner bases*, DOI10.1007/BFb0039192, publisher abstract https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/BFb0039192 explicitly states fixed-(n,m,d) computation bounds and polynomial coefficient-size complexity.
