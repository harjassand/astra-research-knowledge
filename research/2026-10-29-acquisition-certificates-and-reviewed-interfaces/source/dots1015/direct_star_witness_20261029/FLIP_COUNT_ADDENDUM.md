# Nonnegative count certificate, not full-information learning

In a neutral-scheduled protocol choose candidate frames before a fresh private role coin. With probability p_w use the direct star witness, selecting one of m edges uniformly; otherwise emit the mathematical count zero. Let F_t be one exactly when this role is chosen AND input/output swap signs differ. Conditional on the actual history AND current candidate, E[F_t]=p_w G_t/(2m), where G_t=sum_i delta_t(S_i). A pre-role neutral stop can set both quantities zero. Role-dependent aborts are not silently included.

Since F_t is binary, E[exp(−F_t)|history,candidate]=1−(1−exp(−1)) E[F_t]≤exp(−(1−exp(−1)) E[F_t]). The product of these conditional exponential factors is a nonnegative supermartingale (current candidate choices are explicit intermediate filtration stages). Therefore with probability at least 1−eta,

 sum_t G_t ≤ (2m/[p_w(1−exp(−1))]) * (sum_t F_t + log(1/eta)).

The inequality is a bound on actual candidate/history losses, not a counterfactual comparator or a future noise guarantee. It avoids a square-root-T additive range penalty because the measured event is nonnegative and vanishes at zero loss. It does not supply the 35 full-information coefficients; one must pay additional witness calls and candidate-dependent physical preparation/readout. A service or outer-code acceptance theorem needs its own fine-filtration/all-input CP-transfer composition. No such new protocol is claimed here.

Strong baseline: at fixed four-spin ports, ordinary matching or full-path logical validation can have better constants and more direct task relevance. This count construction is a conventional verification mechanism, not itself a new theory of learning or an experimental result.
