# Independent receiving review: public-key mixed encoding rank barrier
2026-10-29. ACCEPTED at the stated minimax, forward-memoryless-query scope.

The key step survives queries after payload receipt. Fix all public flags, including the payload flag j, and restrict to histories with no query flag j. All query channels on this branch involve only other keys. Thus the averaged j-key twirl can be performed immediately after payload encoding, before any decoder operation; it leaves I_R/k tensor a decoder state. Subsequent adaptive CP maps, even with postselection and coherent workspace, cannot restore entanglement with R. The no-hit flag event has input-independent probability u_n. Padding unused calls only makes this inaccessible event smaller and preserves the bound.

For deterministic output, overlap with Phi of I_R/k tensor any normalized logical output is exactly 1/k^2. Hit branches have overlap at most one. Averaging gives the claimed bound; at least one fixed tuple attains the corresponding infidelity lower bound, and testing the Bell projector gives the half-diamond lower bound. This is minimax, not a pointwise assertion for every decoder/key pair.

For exact universal heralding, every fixed-key accepted Choi operator is on the identity ray. Each positive flag subbranch must be on that ray too. Averaging keys on a no-hit subbranch gives a separable operator; a nonzero operator on the maximally entangled ray is not separable. The averaged no-hit success is therefore zero. The guaranteed success cannot exceed 1-u_n. This argument does not confuse postselected normalized fidelity with a joint-event bound.

The matching Bell-probe/Weyl-Bell measurement is physically available from ordinary forward calls and known operations. The identity/depolarizing mixture saturates half-diamond because the depolarizing channel is the uniform Weyl mixture: the entangled input lower bound and triangle upper bound both equal 1-1/k^2.
