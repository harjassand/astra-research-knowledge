7. What this changes—and what the comparison supports

The earlier results remain as supplied:

The ordinary controller used 72 segments, duration 35.19, with approximate joint error bound 0.01115 across its declared uncertainty box. Its numerical nodes lacked certified rounding enclosures, and its model acquisition was uncharged.
The tested older algebraic families retained logical-error witnesses at least 0.44646, 0.93803 and 0.97106.
The blind degree-five and degree-nine filters retained their costs of 23,552 and 154,533,888 amplitude-scaled segments per cycle, before further switching and repetitions.

The new 25-segment example is not a matched comparison against the old 72-segment controller: its knowledge and qualified drift regime differ. A numerical optimal controller receiving the same acquired information could also improve on these explicit constants.

The supported scientific consequence is nevertheless concrete:

For this native spin interface, learning a small set of state-independent response scalars permits an exact, short, input-uniform entangler. The action has the correct worst-case dependence on normalized chirality, and the remaining bath error can be bounded using local information.

The elementary rotation geometry belongs to established control theory. Related non-Abelian quantum signal-processing work also exploits noncommuting quantum control variables, particularly in oscillator–qubit architectures. That broader principle therefore cannot be claimed as new on the basis of this construction. The specific result developed here is the native reduction, sufficient calibration interface, resource bounds and finite forward-bath accounting. 
Phys. Rev. X

The next substantial obstacle is now experimentally efficient acquisition and tolerance of faster local bath dynamics. Increasing the filter degree is no longer the natural default for this calibrated regime.
