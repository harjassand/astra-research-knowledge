### Assumptions that remain substantive

The construction requires:

- **Static native coupling structure and repeatable sign control.** The coarse norm and chirality bounds are supplied promises whose physical qualification has not been acquired here.
- **Specified regularity of the memory dynamics.** The proof requires bounds on the finite-order action of that dynamics on the native couplings. It does not cover arbitrary unbounded environments without those conditions.
- **Sufficient timing and switching precision.** These resources can be made finite for each requested accuracy, but their required quality can become severe. The theorem does not supply a reliable waveform controller.
- **A fixed, classically prescribed program.** Different pulse choices can produce different memory evolutions. Their separate product references do not automatically implement coherent control across future logical choices.

Unknown data holding and control asymmetry are not silently included in the ideal symmetry premise. Their actual effects must be compensated by an additional construction or charged to the physical error.

## 3. The extension to large retained baths

The initial proof used a bound on the total memory Hamiltonian norm. I subsequently replaced that premise with bounds on its local action:
\[
h_r=\left\|\operatorname{ad}_{H_M}^{\,r}(A_j)\right\|.
\]

For an endpoint mediator attached to a spin chain with on-site and nearest-neighbor terms bounded by \(J\), the derivation gives
\[
h_r\le B_0(4J)^r r!,

independently of chain length.

Consequently, one compiled program can serve **arbitrarily large finite retained chains** satisfying these promises, with arbitrary initial chain states and reference correlations. Increasing bath size does not itself require more microscopic identification or history storage for this gate.

The extension also supplies a timing estimate:

The difference in total free-memory evolution is absorbed into the actual same-memory reference. No estimate involving the total bath-energy norm is needed.

## 6. A separate necessary bound

I also derived a lower bound applying to every fixed program serving a continuous unknown-gain interval.

Let
\[
\mathcal A=\int\|H_{\mathrm{control}}(t)\|\,dt.
\]
For a fixed positive uncertainty interval, achieving error \(\epsilon\) requires
\[
\mathcal A=\Omega\!\left(\log\frac1\epsilon\right).
\]

The argument uses a logical observable whose response is analytic in the unknown gain. Exact implementation throughout an interval would force the same response at zero coupling, where the entangler cannot act. A quantitative interpolation argument yields the logarithmic bound.

Thus exact uniform implementation at finite control action is excluded for this program class. The enormous overhead of the current construction is **not** shown necessary.

There is also an orientation boundary: one unconditioned program covering both mirror-related chirality signs has worst-case logical trace-distance error at least \(1/2\) for the \(\pi/4\) entangler. Knowing which orientation class is present matters; knowing its precise magnitude need not be part of the gate’s information requirements.
