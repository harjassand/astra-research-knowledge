## The strongest completed result is a sharp theorem for using the interface until it succeeds

**The joint-bath guarantee supports a broader conclusion than a bound on specified faulty transmissions: it gives a sharp, independent worst-case model for the combined waiting times and hidden faults of a sequence of completed resources.**

The physical bath can retain quantum information indefinitely. Its state can change after every rejection and acceptance. Nevertheless, under the stated uniform operator inequalities, every increasing function of the completed resources’ waiting times and fault indicators is bounded by a model with independent geometric waiting times and independent Bernoulli faults.

I developed that theorem, an explicit counterexample showing where retrospective selection defeats it, and two conventional coding consumers. One consumer gives a high-rate information-theoretic result; the other has a small, explicitly compiled decoder that I checked exhaustively.

**This is a new guarantee derived here for known preparation and coding methods. It does not establish a new hardware threshold or repair the previous noisy endpoint’s failure to reach 1%.** The contribution is the precise connection between the physical joint-bath interface and a complete, causally stopped resource-generation process.

---

## 1. The question I chose—and the conventional construction that sharpened it

I chose to investigate when a component with unknown retained memory can be used repeatedly at a calculable reliability and resource cost, without identifying or resetting that memory.

The serious conventional comparator is already powerful:

1. Prepare fresh Bell attempts.
2. Discard rejected attempts, including their references.
3. Retain accepted pairs.
4. Apply deterministic stabilizer distillation.
5. Introduce the payload afterward and teleport it.

Entanglement purification, its relationship to error correction, and the five-qubit code are established machinery. Matsumoto’s general stabilizer-to-distillation construction explicitly accommodates an arbitrary joint input state; its version without final postselection is a trace-preserving process. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9604024v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Indefinite bath memory also has substantial precedent in fault tolerance. Aliferis, Gottesman and Preskill allow noisy gates to share a bath that can retain information indefinitely, under their locality and noise-strength assumptions. Thus, “no bath reset” alone would be an inadequate novelty claim. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0504218v3?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

What needed construction here was the passage from P3’s **joint operator bounds per attempted block** to the statistics and joint quantum state of resources produced at **random completion times**.

That passage matters because successful preparation changes the clock. It also matters because conditioning on later success records can change the apparent quality of earlier resources.

## 2. The precise interface

Let \(E\) include every system that can retain quantum memory and influence later attempts. After the required private averaging, an accepted attempt has a genuine Kraus decomposition

\[
K_{\lambda,P}=P\otimes M_{\lambda,P},
\qquad P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}.
\]

Define the bath effects

\[
A=\sum_{\lambda,P}M_{\lambda,P}^{\dagger}M_{\lambda,P},
\qquad
B=\sum_{\lambda,P\ne I}M_{\lambda,P}^{\dagger}M_{\lambda,P}.
\]

Thus \(A\) is the acceptance effect and \(B\) is the accepted nonidentity-fault effect. The supplied interface gives

\[
A\succeq sI,\qquad B\preceq bI.
\]

For the useful regime \(0\le b<s\), put

\[
p=\frac bs.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{B\preceq pA.}
\]

This relative inequality is the key to changing from attempted blocks to completed resources.

The construction retains four essential assumptions:

- Every attempt begins with a fresh Bell preparation independent of the current bath. Rejection discards that attempted pair.
- Previously accepted resources are isolated from subsequent transport operations, or their storage has a separately justified error model.
- The Pauli decomposition holds jointly with all retained memory. A coherent Pauli expansion is insufficient.
- The operator inequalities hold at every allowed attempt, including after the actual preceding history.

For the P3 transport instance, the original restrictions remain: the common, fixed, state-independent transfer block; the actual six-permutation average; inaccessible discarded transport keys; fresh labelled sources; and the qualified timing and reuse word. The abstract theorem below does not independently establish those physical conditions.

There is also a sharper parameter when the effects themselves are available:

\[
p_*=\left\|A^{-1/2}BA^{-1/2}\right\|_\infty.
\]

It is the smallest coefficient satisfying \(B\preceq p_*A\), hence the exact uniform bound on the bad fraction of an individual accepted attempt. Here I use the supplied, more conservative \(b/s\). The capture counts do not acquire the full operators needed to evaluate \(p_*\).

## 3. A sharp theorem after first-success completion

For completed resource \(i\), define

\[
W_i=\text{number of attempts until its first acceptance},
\]

and

\[
F_i=\begin{cases}
1,&\text{its accepted Pauli is nonidentity},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The \(F_i\) are mathematical labels of the positive Kraus decomposition; they need not be observable.

### Theorem

For every finite number \(n\) of completed resources, there exists a coupling under which

\[
W_i\le G_i,\qquad F_i\le Z_i
\quad\text{for all }i\le n,
\]

where all the variables on the right are mutually independent and

\[
G_i\sim\operatorname{Geom}(s),
\qquad
Z_i\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p).
\]

The geometric distribution counts the successful attempt, so its support starts at \(1\).

Consequently, for every nonnegative increasing function \(f\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E f(W_1,F_1,\ldots,W_n,F_n)
\le
\mathbb E f(G_1,Z_1,\ldots,G_n,Z_n).
}
\]

The theorem is uniform over the incoming bath state and allows an external reference correlated with that bath.

### Proof

Fix the complete history before resource \(i\). It can include earlier virtual Pauli labels: conditioning on such a history still leaves a positive conditional bath state.

First, each attempted rejection has conditional probability at most \(1-s\). Therefore

\[
\Pr(W_i>t\mid\text{previous history})\le(1-s)^t.
\]

Next, consider any rejected history within the current resource slot. Its evolution gives a completely positive map on the bath. Pulling the inequality \(B\preceq pA\) backward through that map preserves the inequality.

Summing over all rejected histories of length \(t-1\) gives

\[
\Pr(F_i=1,W_i=t\mid\text{previous history})
\le
p\,\Pr(W_i=t\mid\text{previous history}).
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(F_i=1\mid W_i=t,\text{previous history})\le p.
}
\]

Now construct the coupling. Use one fresh uniform random variable to sample \(W_i\) by its conditional quantile and \(G_i\) by the geometric quantile. The waiting-time bound ensures \(W_i\le G_i\).

Use a second, independent uniform variable to sample \(F_i\) from its conditional probability given \(W_i\), and \(Z_i\) with probability \(p\). The last inequality ensures \(F_i\le Z_i\).

Finally, sample the remaining current transcript from its conditional distribution and proceed to the next slot. Those records may affect the future bath and controls; the same argument applies at the next slot.

This reproduces the actual classical waiting/fault law while the envelope variables remain independent.

### Why the result is sharp

A memoryless instrument already attains the envelope. At each attempt, choose

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{Outcome}&\text{Probability}\\ \hline
\text{Reject}&1-s\\
\text{Accept with identity}&s-b\\
\text{Accept with a fixed nonidentity Pauli}&b.
\end{array}
\]

Its completed waiting times are exactly geometric, and its accepted fault indicators are independent Bernoulli variables with parameter \(b/s\).

Therefore no uniformly smaller envelope for this class of increasing costs follows from \(s,b\) alone.

**The theorem is a classical stochastic comparison of specified records. It does not replace the physical quantum process by an independent channel.** Bath states, Pauli types, and detailed transcripts can remain strongly correlated.

## 4. What it buys immediately

Let

\[
N_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_i,\qquad
K_n=\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_i.
\]

The theorem gives

\[
\mathbb E N_n\le\frac ns,
\]

\[
\boxed{\Pr(K_n>t)\le\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)>t],}
\]

and the exact standard deadline comparison

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(N_n>L)\le\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(L,s)<n].
}
\]

There is also a joint generating-function bound. For \(z,w\ge1\), with \(z(1-s)<1\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[z^{N_n}w^{K_n}]
\le
\left[
\frac{sz}{1-(1-s)z}(1-p+pw)
\right]^n.
}
\]

This controls combined penalties for long waits and accumulated faults. Positive weighted holding costs can also be evaluated against the same independent envelope.

The essential gain is that **causal completion does not introduce an exponentially small global acceptance denominator**. Each resource is completed before the next is counted; eventual completion is trace preserving because \(s>0\).

These are bounds in attempted blocks. Converting them into wall-clock or energy bounds requires the actual qualified attempt schedule.

## 5. The decisive boundary: unusually fast batches can be unusually bad

I constructed a fixed three-state memory instrument that satisfies the scalar effect bounds yet makes fast-batch postselection disastrous.

The memory states are “start,” “clean,” and “fault recorded.”

On the first attempt:

- acceptance is certain;
- a fault occurs with probability \(b\);
- the memory records whether it occurred.

On later attempts:

- if a fault was recorded, acceptance is certain;
- otherwise, acceptance has probability \(s\);
- there are no further faults.

This is one fixed instrument acting on different memory states. Its effects can be written

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,s,1),
\qquad
B=\operatorname{diag}(b,0,0),
\]

so \(A\succeq sI\) and \(B\preceq bI\).

For a batch of \(n\) resources, let “fast” mean that every resource succeeded on its first attempt. Then

\[
\Pr(\text{fast})=b+(1-b)s^{n-1},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(F_1=1\mid\text{fast})
=
\frac{b}{b+(1-b)s^{n-1}}.
}
\]

With \(s=1/2\), \(b=0.01\), and \(n=16\),

\[
\Pr(F_1=1\mid\text{fast})\approx0.996988.
\]

The first resource’s unconditional fault probability is only \(1\%\), but it is faulty with probability over \(99.6\%\) among those unusually fast batches.

The example belongs to the abstract instrument class defined by the effects. I have not claimed that this particular instrument is realized by the passive transport medium.

This establishes the boundary of the new theorem: selecting for short waiting times is outside its increasing-cost guarantee. It also demonstrates why the guarantee cannot be asserted separately for every terminal transcript. The general danger of global postselection under correlated noise is established in the AGP analysis; the example here makes the issue explicit for readiness times under the present scalar bounds. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0703264v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A high-probability deadline remains usable. If its abort probability is at most \(a\), an unnormalized bad-output weight \(\epsilon\) gives conditional error at most

\[
\frac{\epsilon}{1-a}
\]

on completed runs. That denominator must remain in the calculation.

## 6. A high-rate consequence using conventional stabilizer distillation

The accepted joint Pauli structure gives the \(n\)-pair state

\[
\rho_{QE}
=
\sum_e \Phi_e\otimes\tau_E^e,
\qquad \tau_E^e\succeq0.
\]

Here \(\Phi_e\) is the Bell state carrying Pauli pattern \(e\). The bath operators may encode arbitrary correlations among the errors. Timing records can be included in \(E\).

After all pairs have been generated and isolated, choose a fresh random \([[n,k]]\) stabilizer code. Its seed can be public; it must be independent of the already generated error pattern, and the bath must no longer be able to alter those pairs.

This coding seed is separate from the private transport-permutation keys, which remain discarded.

Use the conventional deterministic distillation construction. For each syndrome, select a matching Pauli of weight at most \(t\), with a fixed tie rule. Define a fallback for every other syndrome. There is no further syndrome postselection.

Let

\[
V(n,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{t}3^j{n\choose j}.
\]

For a uniformly random stabilizer, a fixed nonzero Pauli lies in its normalizer with probability

\[
\frac{2^{n+k}-1}{4^n-1}.
\]

A collision union bound and the stopped-process fault tail therefore give

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_{\mathrm{dist}}
\le
\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)>t]
+
V(n,t)\frac{2^{n+k}-1}{4^n-1}.
}
\]

No independence among the actual Pauli errors is used. Random stabilizer coding and finite-block bounds for arbitrary Pauli noise are established methods; Ostrev’s treatment provides a particularly close modern comparator. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15202v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### The output is close jointly with the actual bath

Correctly decoded branches produce the same \(k\)-pair Bell state. If \(C\) includes the public code seed, syndromes, and timing records, the output decomposes as

\[
\rho_{QEC}
=
\Phi^{\otimes k}\otimes\sigma_{EC}^{\mathrm{good}}
+
\rho_{QEC}^{\mathrm{bad}},
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}\rho^{\mathrm{bad}}\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{dist}}.
\]

Writing \(\tau_{EC}=\operatorname{Tr}_Q\rho_{QEC}\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac12
\left\|
\rho_{QEC}
-
\Phi^{\otimes k}\otimes\tau_{EC}
\right\|_1
\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{dist}}.
}
\]

The comparison uses the **actual final bath-and-record marginal**. It includes the public seed as a register; it does not promise the same error for every particular seed or transcript.

A payload introduced afterward can consume the resource by teleportation. Contractivity transfers this joint resource bound through that operation. This does not recover a previously erased payload.

### Rate

With ideal local processing and protected storage, the construction gives the familiar hashing fraction

\[
R_{\mathrm{accepted}}
\ge
1-h_2(p)-p\log_2 3
\]

whenever the right side is positive. The corresponding asymptotic yield per attempted four-carrier block is at least

\[
s\,[1-h_2(p)-p\log_2 3].
\]

The result is an achievable rate, not an exact capacity or an optimality theorem for coding.

## 8. An explicit, efficiently decoded five-qubit consumer

Use the standard \([[5,1,3]]\) stabilizer generated by

\[
XZZXI,\quad IXZZX,\quad XIXZZ,\quad ZXIXZ.
\]

Its sixteen syndromes distinguish the identity and the fifteen single-qubit Pauli errors. Exact correction concerns the error set; it does not require independently generated errors. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9604034v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I synthesized a Clifford decoder \(U\). Alice applies \(U^*\), Bob applies \(U\); each measures four qubits, and Bob uses the syndrome difference to correct the remaining qubit. Applying the conjugated circuit at Alice is necessary to preserve the ideal Bell reference.

One direct, unoptimized implementation of a five-to-one step uses, across both endpoints:

\[
22\ \mathrm{CZ},
\qquad
\le70\ \text{single-qubit gates},
\qquad
8\ \text{binary measurements}.
\]

These counts include replacing a SWAP by three CNOTs and each CNOT by a CZ with Hadamards.

A block fails only if at least two input pairs are faulty, so

\[
p_{\ell+1}\le10p_\ell^2.
\]

For disjoint blocks, the same argument preserves the requisite subset bounds without assuming independence. Thus

\[
\boxed{
p_\ell\le\frac{(10p)^{2^\ell}}{10}.
}
\]

This is a conservative ideal-consumer bound, not a physical gate threshold.

### Finite costs for one output Bell pair

| Quantity | Supplied-balance branch | Generic branch |
|---|---:|---:|
| Concatenation levels | 2 | 3 |
| Accepted raw Bell pairs | 25 | 125 |
| Transport-induced output fault bound | \(5.85\times10^{-13}\) | \(1.86\times10^{-13}\) |
| Expected attempted blocks, upper bound | 33.087 | 239.926 |
| Expected native transmissions, upper bound | 132.35 | 959.71 |
| Attempt deadline used | 62 | 346 |
| Deadline-abort bound | \(8.11\times10^{-10}\) | \(8.40\times10^{-10}\) |
| Additional consumer CZs | 132 | 682 |
| Additional single-qubit gates, upper bound | 420 | 2,170 |
| Additional binary measurements | 48 | 248 |

The counts expose the cost rather than hiding it inside “apply error correction.” Using the handoff’s nominal \(39\)-CZ attempt schedule, the expected attempt-plus-consumer CZ totals are approximately **1,422** and **10,039**, before teleportation, holding protection, or fault-tolerant implementation overhead.

Those are operation counts for the idealized construction. Their errors have not been absorbed into the tiny transport-induced bounds.

### Holding remains consequential

Generating all inputs consecutively preserves the fixed transport word, but requires holding 25 or 125 accepted pairs before decoding. For a batch of \(n\) pairs, the expected accumulated holding exposure, measured in pair-attempts, is bounded by

\[
\frac{n(n-1)}{2s}.
\]

The extremal independent process attains this scale.

A recursive streaming schedule can reduce the consumer’s peak retained pairs to \(4\ell+1\)—nine or thirteen in these examples. That schedule needs qualified timing, isolation, and local-control concurrency or pause semantics before its lower memory count can be credited to this apparatus. I have not silently changed the acquired reuse word.

## 9. A different representation remains useful, but has not supplied the missing operation

I also constructed a passive-source alternative rather than assuming the original encoding circuit was indispensable.

For two carriers \(a,b\), define the normalized split doubly occupied orbital pair

\[
g_{ab}^{\dagger}
=
\frac12
(a_0^\dagger+b_0^\dagger)
(a_1^\dagger+b_1^\dagger).
\]

The four-particle Gaussian state

\[
|G\rangle=g_{ab}^{\dagger}g_{cd}^{\dagger}|\mathrm{vac}\rangle
\]

obeys

\[
\boxed{
P_{\text{one per carrier}}|G\rangle
=
\frac12|s_{ab}s_{cd}\rangle
=
\frac12|0_L\rangle.
}
\]

Thus projection produces a known singlet-code basis state with probability \(1/4\).

There is a further algebraic property:

\[
\langle1_L|c_i^\dagger c_j|G\rangle=0
\quad\text{for all }i,j.
\]

An inter-dimer quadratic changes the dimer particle totals and is rejected. An intra-dimer quadratic leaves the other dimer a singlet, so the total-spin-zero projection can only return the original product-singlet codeword. I checked all 64 quadratic operators.

This is a useful foothold for cheaper preparation of known invariant states. **It has not constructed a reference-entangled logical Bell resource, nor supplied the coherent projection.** Counting that projection as free would simply move the difficult operation.

The broader alternative of ending in destructive measurements remains credible: established collective-noise QKD schemes already use entangled-pair sources, single-particle rotations, and detectors. Fermionic linear-optics results also make clear that the permitted measurement primitive matters greatly; a coherent parity measurement is a distinct resource. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0306199?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

I retain this source construction as a separate opening. It has not replaced the complete Bell-resource route.

## 10. Physical limits and evidence scope

The actual hardware conclusion remains constrained.

**Endpoint accuracy is still missing from the new suppression figures.** The previous complete supplied-noise schedule remains at conditional Bell half-trace-distance at most \(0.013435\), with acceptance at least \(0.52645\), in the balance branch; the generic figures remain \(0.025\) and \(0.35804\). The construction above has not improved those complete-schedule guarantees.

A per-resource trace-distance bound also cannot automatically be inserted as a stochastic Pauli fault probability. Coherent endpoint errors require their own structured noise argument. With a separately established uniform joint instrument error \(\eta\), a finite capped protocol can at least use a hybrid contribution proportional to its number of calls; that is a different guarantee from the quadratic suppression above.

**Acquisition remains limited to what it measured under its model.** The capture counts do not establish common orientation, singular-value balance, endpoint instrument accuracy, indefinite stationarity, or new timing words. The balance promise remains separately supplied. The original fresh-source and parity-encoding qualifications remain necessary.

**The ordinary alternatives keep their strength.** The three-carrier noiseless subsystem still removes common orientation with fewer transmissions and less rejection. Learned single-carrier polar correction remains relevant where its orientation certificate is affordable. The Gaussian loading result still loses to direct drains in its earlier comparison. The stopping theorem would benefit any alternative that supplies the same operator interface; it gives no universal total-cost advantage to four-carrier transport.

The executed checks in this turn were:

- exact rational bounds for the displayed finite probabilities, using conservative parameter rounding;
- exhaustive normalizer counting over 315 small stabilizer subspaces and all 63 nonzero Pauli vectors;
- all \(4^5=1{,}024\) Pauli patterns for the compiled five-qubit decoder;
- full matrix checks of all 256 no-error/single-error conditional Bell-output branches;
- a noncommuting two-dimensional retained-bath instrument, checking stopped effects and the joint generating-function bound numerically;
- the 64 quadratic-operator checks for the passive known-state source.

These checks support the ordinary proofs; they do not substitute for them. I did not execute the enormous high-rate decoders or re-run the unavailable original 18-mode implementation.

The stopping coupling and random-code argument received a native internal mathematical cross-check. The explicit consumer and passive-source calculations are root-derived and root-checked. The eleven original proof/code files were unavailable here, so their accepted status and review scopes remain exactly as supplied. No external validation or priority claim has been established.

**The completed theoretical advance is that, for this operator-qualified interface, memory length and bath complexity disappear from a sharp class of causal reliability and resource bounds.** Detailed terminal conditioning remains outside that reduction, as the counterexample proves. Reliable endpoint operations and protected holding remain the unresolved requirements for turning the theorem into a stronger physical capability.
