The finite-device result has become more concrete: we have a proposed polynomial bound on how the slab thickness must grow with its cross-sectional area to exclude extra low-energy states. Two internal checks have examined the construction and its resource accounting. It also appears to give the complete free Weyl-particle spectrum, including multiplicities, along a growing-device sequence. This is stronger than the earlier particle-signal result, but the bound is extremely costly and the proof still needs external scrutiny. Most importantly, it is still an ungauged matter construction. We have not shown that it produces a chiral gauge theory, and that is where the next decisive work is focused.
