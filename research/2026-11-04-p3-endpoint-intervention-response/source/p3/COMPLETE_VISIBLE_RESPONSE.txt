## Completed result: microscopic intervention response from endpoint observations

**I constructed and executed a method that computes the effects of microscopic rate changes inside a stochastic simulator using only complete, restorable states and native endpoint observations.** It can select a finite rate change with a rigorous improvement certificate, and it can compute finite-strength counterfactual expectations.

The central discovery is an operational distinction: **endpoint observations can support signed microscopic calculations even when they cannot support exact positive sampling of individual native jumps.** Recovering a positive microscopic trajectory was an unnecessary requirement for the intervention question.

## 1. The question I chose

The operation is:

> Given an unchanged stochastic simulator whose rates and event history are unavailable, determine which specified microscopic rate modification improves a chosen future observable—and certify a finite improvement.

Let the native process be a continuous-time Markov chain with unknown generator \(Q\). Its exit rates obey a certified ceiling

\[
\sum_{y\ne x}q_{xy}\le R.
\]

A known bounded rule \(w(x,y)\), with \(|w|\le W\), specifies an intervention:

\[
q^\theta_{xy}=q_{xy}\bigl(1+\theta w(x,y)\bigr),
\qquad y\ne x,
\]

with the diagonal adjusted to preserve row sums. The native edge set can remain unknown.

For a bounded terminal observable \(f\), the first target is

\[
D_w=
\left.
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
\mathbb E_{x_0}[f(X_T^\theta)]
\right|_{\theta=0}.
\]

This asks for the information needed to make an intervention decision. It does not require identifying every microscopic rate.

### The interface that is actually earned

The construction needs:

- A complete Markov-state checkpoint.
- Retention and restoration of checkpoints already acquired.
- Native evolution for a requested duration, with fresh independent future randomness.
- Endpoint state equality and evaluation of \(f\) and \(w\).
- A certified global exit-rate ceiling \(R\).

I implemented this through **GillesPy2 1.8.3’s SSA solver in a separate process**. The learner sends a retained state, duration, and fresh seed, and receives endpoint copy numbers. Its interface exposes no rate evaluation, event log, derivative, or parameter-change operation. GillesPy2 supplies the relevant model, initialization, solver, and seed operations. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

For the executed autonomous, non-delayed reaction model, the complete copy-number state is sufficient. Restoring those numbers while keeping the reaction definitions and parameters unchanged preserves the conditional native law. Exponential waiting-time memorylessness justifies fresh continuation.

**This is a software construction.** It uses the ability to copy a simulator state. It makes no claim that an unknown physical specimen can be cloned, reset, or subjected to a rate-changing actuator without backaction.

## 2. Constructing the missing microscopic operation

Choose an independent exponential duration,

\[
\tau\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\mu).
\]

One native endpoint call samples the kernel

\[
P=\mathbb E[e^{\tau Q}]
=\mu(\mu I-Q)^{-1},
\]

so

\[
Q=\mu(I-P^{-1}).
\]

Resolvent-based generator inference is established machinery. The missing operation here is implementing \(P^{-1}\) from endpoint samples, without estimating or inverting a full matrix. Existing generator-estimation literature already uses the resolvent identity, so that identity is not the novelty claim. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### A signed branching inverse

Start from a signed checkpoint \((z,a)\), where \(a\in\{+1,-1\}\).

1. Advance it independently for \(\operatorname{Exp}(\mu)\), obtaining \(y\).
2. If \(y=z\), stop that branch.
3. Otherwise add
   \[
   \mu a(\delta_y-\delta_z)
   \]
   to a sparse signed row, and create children \((z,a)\) and \((y,-a)\).

Repeated state labels are aggregated. Every restart uses an initial or previously returned checkpoint.

The returned signed row \(\Gamma\) satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E\Gamma=Q(x,\cdot).}
\]

Its entries can be negative. Those signs cancel indirect transitions that would otherwise be mistaken for native edges.

The computation terminates with controlled work. A node branches only when its endpoint differs from its origin, and

\[
\Pr(\text{branch})
\le \frac{R}{\mu+R}.
\]

With the useful choice

\[
\mu=5R,
\]

that probability is at most \(1/6\). The tree is dominated by a subcritical binary branching process, with expected node count at most \(3/2\).

This supplies the microscopic generator action as an executable random calculation, including its work and moment bounds.

### Why the signs are essential

Consider two three-state chains:

- One has only the directed cycle \(0\to1\to2\to0\).
- The other also has a direct \(0\to2\) transition.

Both have positive endpoint probabilities between every pair of states at every positive duration. Consequently, any finite adaptive endpoint transcript has mutually absolutely continuous laws under the two models.

Suppose an almost-surely terminating algorithm returned a **nonnegative unbiased estimate** of the direct rate \(q_{02}\). It would have to return zero almost surely when that edge is absent. Finite-transcript absolute continuity would force the same behavior when the edge is present—a contradiction.

Therefore, under this general endpoint-only interface:

- A universal nonnegative unbiased direct-rate estimator is impossible.
- A universal exact positive sampler of native jumps or uniformized microscopic transitions is impossible.
- A signed unbiased generator action is possible.

This is a specific application of the support obstruction familiar from Bernoulli factories and nonnegative unbiased estimation. It does **not** prove that positive sampling of every perturbed *positive-time endpoint distribution* is impossible. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

## 3. From the signed row to an intervention response

Define

\[
(A_wh)(x)=
\sum_{y\ne x}q_{xy}w(x,y)\bigl(h(y)-h(x)\bigr).
\]

The response identity is

\[
D_w=
\int_0^T
\left(e^{uQ}A_we^{(T-u)Q}f\right)(x_0)\,du.
\]

The construction now supplies the previously unavailable \(A_w\) operation.

A response replicate proceeds as follows:

1. Draw \(U\) uniformly on \([0,T]\).
2. Evolve the initial checkpoint natively to \(x=X_U\).
3. Construct the signed row \(\Gamma\) at that checkpoint.
4. Run fresh native futures of duration \(T-U\) from the retained states with nonzero coefficients. Use one shared future from \(x\).
5. Return
   \[
   H_w=
   T\sum_{y\ne x}\Gamma_yw(x,y)(F_y-F_x),
   \]
   where \(F_y\) is the terminal observable from the future starting at \(y\).

The futures are independent of the inverse tree. This matters: reusing a resolver endpoint as a terminal future would generally introduce bias.

### The proved resource theorem

Write

\[
\Delta=\sup f-\inf f.
\]

For a finite or countable bounded-rate state space, the uncapped estimator satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb EH_w=D_w,
\qquad
\mathbb EH_w^2
\le 19R^2T^2W^2\Delta^2.
}
\]

Its expected resources are

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\text{endpoint calls}]
\le\frac{35}{12}\approx2.917,
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\text{total native evolution duration}]
\le\frac{17T}{24}+\frac{3}{10R}.
}
\]

These bounds include the initial prefix, all inverse-tree calls, and all terminal futures.

A fixed finite family \(w_1,\ldots,w_d\) can share the same tree and futures. The number of simulator calls does not acquire a factor of \(d\); evaluating and returning the \(d\) answers still costs arithmetic and storage. Simultaneous absolute-error bounds can be obtained with

\[
O\!\left(
R^2T^2W^2\Delta^2\,
\epsilon^{-2}\log(d/\delta)
\right)
\]

replicates.

The result has no explicit state-space-size or mixing-time factor. **The costs of \(R\), \(T\), complete checkpoints, and relative precision on rare events remain real.** In a large interacting system, the global rate ceiling can itself grow with system size.

## 4. Closing the operation: a finite intervention certificate

A derivative alone would leave the useful decision unfinished. I derived a finite-change remainder.

For every valid segment of generators \(Q+\theta A_w\), Markov contraction gives

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\mathbb E f(X_T^\theta)-\mathbb E f(X_T)
-\theta D_w
\right|
\le
\theta^2R^2W^2T^2\Delta.
}
\]

There is no exponential factor in this remainder.

If a statistically valid derivative interval has lower endpoint \(L>0\), set

\[
C=R^2W^2T^2\Delta,
\qquad
\theta=
\min\left\{\frac1{2W},\frac{L}{2C}\right\}.
\]

Then the selected finite intervention preserves every positive native edge and has guaranteed improvement

\[
\mathbb E f(X_T^\theta)-\mathbb E f(X_T)
\ge \theta L-C\theta^2
\ge \frac{\theta L}{2}>0.
\]

Simultaneous intervals allow selection among several candidates using the same acquisition batch.

## 5. Finite-strength counterfactuals are also constructed

I continued beyond the derivative to compute

\[
\mathbb E[f(X_T^v)]
\]

for a known bounded multiplier \(v(x,y)\), where

\[
q^v_{xy}=q_{xy}(1+v(x,y)),
\qquad |v|\le V,\quad v\ge-1.
\]

The unchanged native simulator remains the only evolution engine.

At virtual Poisson times of rate \(\kappa\), the algorithm constructs a signed intervention row \(S\) from \(\Gamma\), then applies a random representation of

\[
I+\frac{S}{\kappa}.
\]

Between virtual times it runs native endpoint evolution. Signed importance weights account for the virtual operations. A common proposal over retained states lets a finite list of candidate interventions share every native call.

The resulting terminal estimator obeys

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\widehat F_v
=
\left(e^{TQ_v}f\right)(x_0).
}
\]

The explicit second-moment bound is

\[
\mathbb E\widehat F_v^2
\le
\|f\|_\infty^2
\exp\!\left[
\left(
6RV+\frac{201R^2V^2}{2\kappa}
\right)T
\right].
\]

Expected endpoint calls are at most

\[
1+\frac52\kappa T.
\]

Thus finite-strength counterfactual expectations are computationally reachable, but the exponential variance bound can make strong or long interventions impractical. This is a signed observable computation; it does not produce positive trajectories of the modified microscopic process.

### Improving the first finite-strength construction

The initial implementation used a bare randomized Dyson expansion. I replaced it with the identity-containing representation above and compared both at the same virtual-clock rate.

On a 12-state nonreversible reference model, each engine evaluated three interventions using 100,000 shared replicates:

| Quantity | Bare Dyson | Identity-containing version |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean endpoint calls | 1.57789 | 2.15298 |

The second version used more actual calls because the first often terminated early at zero weight. Nevertheless, **variance multiplied by actual endpoint-call cost improved by approximately 1.7–2.1 times** across these examples. All three estimates agreed with the separate matrix calculations within one empirical standard error.

That is an executed improvement on this benchmark, not a universal efficiency theorem.

## 6. What this adds over the strongest applicable methods

The literature comparison changed the claims substantially.

- **Rate-free sensitivity already exists.** Cao–Wan explicitly avoid requiring an explicit generator, but their continuous-time algorithms use transition epochs and sojourn times. The distinction here is endpoint-only access. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}
- **Generator recovery from endpoints already exists.** Resolvent estimators and more recent stroboscopic reconstruction use observed propagator matrices and matrix inversion or logarithms. The contribution here is local signed computation without enumerating those matrices, with finite-work and moment bounds. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}
- **Efficient unbiased reaction-network sensitivity already exists.** Gupta–Khammash’s Poisson Path Algorithm uses reaction jumps, propensities, and propensity derivatives. The present construction obtains the derivative action without that access. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="6"}
- **Random inverse series already exist.** The Neumann–Ulam literature also makes clear that convergence of the deterministic series does not alone establish finite-variance Monte Carlo computation. The explicit branching representation and second-moment calculation are therefore material parts of this result. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}

I found no exact prior implementation of the complete local endpoint/checkpoint-only construction with these resource guarantees. **That search result does not establish historical priority.** The appropriate claim is a constructed synthesis with a precise access theorem.

### The strongest matched numerical baseline nearly ties

A fair alternative under the same endpoint interface truncates the inverse series after several levels.

For formation sensitivity in the reference model:

| Endpoint estimator | Systematic bias |
|---|---:|
| One-stage endpoint estimate | −0.0651336 |
| Three inverse levels | −0.0025205 |
| Eight inverse levels | −0.000001089 |
| Full uncapped inverse | 0 |

At the tested budget, the eight-level method was effectively tied with the full estimator. I am therefore **not claiming a universal speed advantage over a tuned finite inverse series**.

The completed capability is the endpoint-to-intervention construction. The uncapped version supplies exact unbiasedness and finite resource bounds; the capped version supplies a deterministic query ceiling with its bias explicitly accounted for.

## 7. Exact limits and preserved work

Several boundaries remain essential.

**Full state is required.** Restoring a visible projection while omitting hidden reaction ages, promoter states, pending events, or other memory can change the future law. The theorem cannot simply be applied to an arbitrary observed time series.

**The rate ceiling must be earned.** Here it follows from the engine’s parameter ceilings and conservation. It is not inferred from finite observations of an unrestricted unknown system.

**Indistinguishable channels remain indistinguishable.** If two mechanisms produce the same state transition, endpoint laws identify their aggregate rate. A selective intervention on one hidden mechanism requires additional distinguishing information.

**Multiplicative edits cannot invent absent reactions.** The finite-strength engine can modify or remove existing rates. It cannot predict a genuinely new reaction channel from baseline data alone.

**Physical control remains separate.** A real catalyst, field, or material modification may change more than the specified edge multipliers. Nothing in the software interface proves otherwise.

The earlier local-law reconstruction, retained-system acquisition, unknown-neighborhood hashing, noisy-reset construction, closed-cycle work observables, finite-data response acquisition, and sustained feedback results remain preserved. Integrity checks verified the 57 original local-law files, the 54 continuous-work manifest members, and all 54 members of the preserved hidden-memory archive.

I also retained two useful findings from this continuation:

- A hidden-volatility counterexample showing that well-conditioned two-point covariance does not guarantee concentration of fluctuation energy, together with a narrower constructive nonlinear excitation theorem.
- An adaptive short-circuit stationary sampler that can remain efficient when static ancestry bounds fail, with its relationship to established perfect-sampling methods stated cautiously.

The endpoint-to-decision question is now closed for the stated computational class. The central scaling problem exposed by the construction is **controlling signed variance for long horizons and large interacting systems**, where the global \(R^2T^2\) response scale and exponential finite-strength bound can overwhelm the benefit of avoiding full microscopic reconstruction.
