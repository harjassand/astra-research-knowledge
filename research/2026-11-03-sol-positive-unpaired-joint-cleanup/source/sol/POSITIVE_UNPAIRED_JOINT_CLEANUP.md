# A population-controlled joint cleanup and delayed drain

Author construction in the live boundary investigation. This earns a
complete physical stopped operation on a previously unadmitted unpaired
cone, including arbitrary bounded initial anchors and signed phase debts.
It is NOT a full six-species recurrence theorem. In particular unequal
unpaired scales, negative/mixed signs, and the unbounded-anchor receiving
seam remain separate obligations. This file does not declare the live
stage complete or any predecessor packet modified.

## 1. Contract and raw regime

Use the exact two C3 blocks and the conservative pair already specified
in GLOBAL_SERVICE_RECEIVER.md, with all ten raw constants strictly
positive and ordinary falling-factorial propensities. Fix H>=1 and L>=1.
Write

    r1=B-C, r2=E-F, q=C+F,
    n=min(r1,r2), m(0,1,2)=(0,2,4), m(a)=7 for a>=3,
    Z=15+M-m(A)-m(D).

There exist acquired constants eps>0 and n0, depending only on the raw
list,H,L, such that EVERY input satisfying

    0<=A,D<=H, n>=n0, max(r1,r2)<=L n, q<=eps n             (1)

has an actual stopping episode with the SAME globally defined receiver

    E[ sigma + J_nc(sigma) + Z(X_sigma)^2 ]<=Z(x)^2.        (2)

J_nc counts all eight genuine block channels, including both competing
modules. Omitting that optional count reward also proves the physical
contract. Core activity is not required. There is no bound on either C
or F separately independent of n; they may both diverge in any manner
allowed by (1), and are never frozen or forced through zero.

Constants are existential raw scalar bounds proved below. This is not an
acquired efficient numerical compiler. P3's transferred nonexplosion
result is used to identify all localized stops with actual physical paths.

## 2. Actual terminal/core service, uniformly over the cold population

First fix positive anchors a,d in a finite head 1..h, and fixed unpaired
counts r_i in [n/2,Ln]. Before the first terminal these are fixed physical
parameters. Put C=2j+o1,F=2(J-j)+o2, o_i in {0,1}. The genuine core is the
finite nearest-neighbour chain on j=0..J with outgoing factors

    U1(j)=(r1+2j+o1)_2(2j+o1)_2,
    U2(j)=(r2+2(J-j)+o2)_2(2(J-j)+o2)_2.

Its j->j-1 rate is kf*a*d*U1 and its j->j+1 rate is kb*a*d*U2.
Killing is the ACTUAL terminal rate

    alpha3*(a)_3*U1+beta3*(d)_3*U2.

Suppose J>=1 and at least one anchor is >=3. This kill is accessible from
every j, by finitely many genuine core edges. For the pure service stop T,

    max_j E_j T <= C_h,L / [(n+J)^2 J].                   (3)

Here is a direct acquired barrier. Take the capable port to be port1
(otherwise relabel). Let N=n+J. Uniform integer-factor bounds give
U1<=C(n+j)^2 j^2 and U2>=c(n+J)^2 J^2 when j<=delta J,
for a raw sufficiently small fixed delta>0; choose delta so the inward
core drift exceeds c N^2 J^2 there. The positive function

    t(j)=K/[N^2 J]*(2-j/J)

has (Q_core-kill)t<=-Kc on that region. For j>delta J,
U1>=c_delta N^2 J^2. The positive part of Q_core t is <=KC, while
-kill*t<=-K c_delta J. Thus for J>=J0(raw,h,L) the latter dominates,
and K can be fixed so (Q_core-kill)t<=-1 everywhere.

For 1<=J<J0 there are finitely many integer j and parities. After division
by n^2, all nonzero rates along a fixed core path to the capable port and
its terminal have positive lower bounds and finite upper bounds, uniformly
over r_i/n in [1/2,L], n>=n0. These bounds follow from the displayed
integer factors and compact intervals; they can be bounded rationally.
At most J0 core steps followed by a terminal give a fixed positive
absorption chance in each finite block and holding mean C/n^2. Repetition
proves (3), with enlarged C. No gap or invariant entrance law is used.

During this SAME pure service, every competing source0/1/2 is retained as
a possible interruption. Their total hazard is <=C_h,L(n+J)(J+1).
Consequently the full process, stopped at its FIRST terminal OR growth,
has

    E residence<=C_h,L/n^2,
    P(growth interrupt)<=C_h,L/n.                         (4)

Indeed its residence is no larger than pure service residence, and the
interruption probability is bounded by the genuine growth compensator
before pure absorption. All core trajectories and all terminal locations
are included. The output is the actual raw landing.

If one anchor is zero, core is disabled. Use only the active port's J_i;
its terminal holding mean is <=C/[(n+J_i)^2 J_i^2] and the same ratio
bound (4) holds. The inactive port contributes only its constant source0,
irrespective of its unbounded stranded cold inventory. If both anchors
are zero, no service is called.

## 3. Bounded joint cleanup without a clean-zero minorization

The following is an ACTUAL state-dependent stop, not replacement dynamics.
Call SERVICE whenever a terminal is accessible under pure core at the
current anchors: for both positive anchors this means max(A,D)>=3 and
floor(C/2)+floor(F/2)>=1; for one zero anchor it means the other port is
terminal-ready. In SERVICE retain every core path and stop at the first
terminal or ANY source0/1/2. A growth is a cleanup FAILURE with its actual
landing; a terminal continues cleanup.

Otherwise call a GROWTH stage. Terminals are disabled throughout its core
fiber. Run all core paths until the first genuine block event. A source1
or source2 continues cleanup. ANY source0 is a failure with its actual
landing. End successfully when A=D=0. No particular source1/source2 word,
terminal destination or zero-inventory event is prescribed.

There is a PATHWISE bound N_H on the number of continuing noncore events
before success, independent of all four hidden inventories. A convenient
raw integer choice N_H=100(H+1) bounds the count and the anchors throughout.

Proof of finiteness and the bound: with both anchors positive put

    g=A+D-(3/2)(C+F).

Every continuing source1/2 lowers g by1/2; each terminal and each core
step preserves it. Initially g<=2H. A continuing growth while an anchor
is already >=3 requires SERVICE to be off, so J=0 and C+F<=2. Hence just
before such a growth g>=1. Apart from a possible INITIAL service burst,
once g<1/2 no terminal at an anchor>=4 can occur: creating that anchor
would require such a growth, while service at fixed anchors cannot lower
g. An initial burst has at most 2H/3 terminals. After it, every terminal
at this small g is at anchor3 and makes an anchor zero. Before that happens there
are at most 4H+O(1) continuing growths: after g<1/2, at most three more
growths create a high anchor and a packet, forcing service. Terminals
themselves are bounded by the anchor balance, since each lowers the
anchor sum by3. While both anchors are positive this also bounds the
anchor sum by 6H+O(1).

After the first zero anchor, core is disabled and that anchor stays zero
until a source0 failure. For the remaining autonomous port, the same
g=a-(3/2)c argument applies. A high-anchor growth needs c<=1, so once g
is small the next terminal is at3 and reaches zero. At most 2a+O(1)
additional growths and the corresponding terminals suffice. These
explicit bounds are well below 100(H+1). Infinite core activity before a
stage exit is excluded by (3), or by the growth hazard lower bound below.

As long as n>=4N_H, continuing source1 reactions lower r_i by at most N_H,
so all stage inputs retain r_i>=n/2 and bounded comparable upper scales.
At every GROWTH stage with an active port, its source1 hazard is >=c n.
Thus that physical stage has mean <=C/n, with source0 failure probability
<=C/n. Formula (4) pays every SERVICE stage. Union and strong Markov
bounds, over at most N_H stages, prove

    P(cleanup failure)<=C_H,L/n,
    E cleanup time<=C_H,L/n,
    J_nc(cleanup)<=N_H+1 pathwise.                         (5)

On either success or failure the actual Z increment has absolute value
<=b_H, a fixed raw integer, and hidden counts move only through bounded
noncore changes and genuine mass-conserving core motion. On success both
anchors are zero, q has changed by at most b_H, and r_i by at most b_H.
This is a bounded COST, not a negative cleanup assertion.

## 4. Delayed drainage stops BEFORE a new joint debt accumulates

After successful cleanup, start a complete activation attempt at A=D=0.
Wait for the actual first source0 and let its port be i. Until the other
port's next source0, that peer anchor stays zero and core is ACTUALLY
disabled. All its other propensities are zero, rather than omitted.

Follow the selected port's actual source1 at anchor1, then EITHER source1
or source2 at anchor2, then its terminal at anchor3. A peer source0 at
any point, an own source0 at positive anchor, or an own growth before the
terminal is an INTERRUPTION and receives its ACTUAL endpoint immediately.
No continuation is demanded from that endpoint inside this attempt.

Each full successful activation returns A=D=0. If its anchor2 event was
source2 the physical state is unchanged; if source1, it deletes exactly
one B (or E) and nothing else. The latter is a GOOD deletion. During all
proper steps Z is constant, except this good event decreases Z by1.
Every possible interruption changes the current Z by at most3; all
low-phase interruptions have nonpositive change. These are direct raw
count identities with the globally defined m, including anchor4 landings.

For current r_i>=n/2 and q<=eps n+O_H(1), raw competing-channel divisions
give constants c,C>0 such that EACH attempt, from its actual input, has

    P(good)>=c/(q+1),
    P(interruption)<=C/n,
    E physical time<=C,
    J_nc<=4.                                              (6)

For the good lower bound choose, for example, first portI (probability
alpha0/(alpha0+beta0)), then I1, then I1. The first I1 beats all constant
inputs with probability >=1-C/n. At anchor2 the source1/source2 ratio is
alpha1/[alpha1+alpha2*C], with C<=q+1; constant inputs cost only C/n.
This actual path therefore has probability >=c/(q+1). For the total
interruption bound, both early holds have genuine nonzero hazard >=cn;
at anchor3 the terminal has B,C>=2, and division of each lower source by
its true terminal hazard gives C/n uniformly. A peer input is constant.
The same raw ratios bound physical holding means; the initial wait is
exactly 1/(alpha0+beta0). Every competing source is paid in (6).

q is EXACTLY unchanged at each successful return, even if the second
source is1. Only r_i is decreased by a good deletion. In particular no
neighboring output profile or stationary background is inserted.

## 5. A finite population guard and one common paid output

Fix a raw K large enough below. Execute at most

    L_drain=ceil[K(q+1)]

attempts, stopping at the FIRST interruption. Also stop at the first good
deletion making the total good count D>=floor(n/4); this guard is an actual
proper-phase endpoint and has already decreased Z by D. It receives the
same physical Z^2. All inputs before the guard retain r_i>=n/2, after
enlarging n0 for cleanup's finite losses.

By (6), the interruption probability is <=C L_drain/n and E D<=L_drain.
Markov's inequality gives P(guard)<=C L_drain/n as well. Thus, if eps is
small enough and n large enough that C L_drain/n<=1/4, the expected number
of started attempts is >=L_drain/2. Its conditional good lower bound in
(6) gives

    E D >= c K/2,
    E drain_time + E J_nc(drain)<=C L_drain.               (7)

One can count a good event occurring at the guard: no discarded terminal
law is needed. On every path Z_out=Z_entry-D+e where |e|<=3 and e=0
unless an interruption occurred.

Combine cleanup and drain. Let b=b_H+3. On cleanup failure set D=0; then
for EVERY actual endpoint

    Z_out=Z_in+B-D, |B|<=b, 0<=D<=n/4+1.

From (5),(7), E D>=cK/3 for n large. Since Z_in>=2n+1,
D<=Z_in/4 for n large and

    E[Z_out^2-Z_in^2]
       <=2b Z_in -(3/2)Z_in E D+2b^2.                   (8)

Choose K>=8(b+1)/c. Enlarging n0 makes (8)<=-2Z_in.
The total physical time and noncore count are <=C_H+C K(q+1) in
expectation. Choose eps still smaller so this is <=Z_in at all sufficiently
large n. The same finite sequence of choices also ensures the guard and
interruption bound used in (7). This proves (2), with slack. The finitely
many scalar thresholds are obtained from the explicit inequalities;
neither an unknown exit expectation nor a global boundary oracle is a
premise.

## 6. An important failed shortcut, retained

It is FALSE that a service-priority busy period is just a collection of
uninterrupted four-event activations. A packet can leave a phase2 port,
be consumed by its peer terminal, and strand the first port with C=0.
Its next source1 can enter anchor3 with only C=1, requiring later growth
and another terminal. For example start (0,n,1,0,n,0) and use

    I0,I1, II0,II1,II2, COREf,II3,
    I1,I2,I3, I1,I2,I3.

The final state is (0,n-3,0,0,n+2,2). All service-priority requirements
are respected. The hidden minority sum has increased by1 although total
population is unchanged. Therefore the cleanup in section3 is charged
its full bounded cost; it is not declared a per-activation deletion.
The later drain deliberately stops on overlap before repeating this debt.

The finite adversarial priority search in search_priority_cascade.py did
not find positive mass in its bounded max-source cases, but that is NOT
used as a theorem or needed in the argument above. Five searches touched
their artificial caps. No general N2<=T assertion is made.

## 7. Consequence and remaining synthesis

This constructs the actual missing population-controlled operation in the
stated cone: several evolving unpinned cold inventories, actual routing
and terminal landings, arbitrary initial finite anchor phases, and bounded
activation debt followed by paid delayed repayment. It uses Z^2 itself,
so its error landings are received by the same physical quadratic quantity
as the frozen slow-return operations. No factorial-energy substitution or
stronger activity moment is necessary for THIS cone.

The general principle exposed is: acquire a uniform physical service
bound against the TOTAL competing growth hazard, prove a raw finite
activation-work bound, and repay its bounded cost by enough acquired
deletion trials before an overlap can accumulate another debt. The service
bound and the work proof were earned here; they are NOT supplied as
universal consequences of weak reversibility.

The full six-species theorem still requires compatible operations for
negative/mixed and arbitrarily separated unpaired scales, all remaining
intermediate/high anchor states, and the high receiving seam. The broad
weak-reversibility destination is still more general. The new operation
is not advertised as that destination or as a practical numerical solver.

service barrier, finite cleanup rule and complete common output above are
