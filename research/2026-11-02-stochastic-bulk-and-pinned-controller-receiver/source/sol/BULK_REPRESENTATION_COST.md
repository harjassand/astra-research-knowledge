# Representation and whole-scope limits of the bulk theorem

This note supplements the fixed-width upgrade without changing the preserved initial derivation.

## Polynomial growth of the receiving function

All retained slopes are in [-1,0]^d, and the all-ones vector is in the plateau for sufficiently large R. Thus L_i(1)>=M_(1/R), and for x>=0 with max x_i=N,

    M_(1/R)-L_i(x) <= -r_i.(x-1) <= d N.

Consequently 0<=U_R(x)<=dN+theta log m. The weights R^p up to R=(2N)^(1/H) sum to O(N^(p/H)), and the remainder has the locally convergent exponential bound already proved. Therefore

    V(x) <= C(1+N)^(p/H+1).

The matching-scale argument gives a polynomial lower bound tending to infinity. These are graph/class/rate-dependent abstract constants, potentially enormous. This is a polynomial-growth Foster FUNCTION in the fixed-width bulk, not a polynomial-time algorithm or a stationary-tail theorem.

## Effective acquisition remains separate

The external finite affine-family theorem is an existence construction involving limiting activity sets and a finite subcover. Its public successor explicitly distinguishes this from an efficient numerical extraction of slopes or H. The present abstract receiver uses the whole finite family as supplied by that proved mathematical lemma; it does not solve that separate effective-extraction problem. No executable instance of that entire family was emitted by `softmin_checks.py`.

Once actual affine data and evaluation access to its offsets are available, labels active in [1,R]^d are a finite linear-programming question, and a common Hoffman constant is a finite matrix error-bound calculation. The scale tail supplies a finite approximation rule for a specified x and error. This conditional evaluation fact is not a claim that the source family or its constants were numerically acquired in this run.

Hoffman's original *On Approximate Solutions of Systems of Linear Inequalities*, J. Research NBS 49 (1952), 263--265, proves the matrix-dependent uniform error bound used here. The proof removes right-hand sides by translation at a nearest face and takes a finite minimum over row subsets. Thus varying offsets and R do not introduce an uncharged R-dependent constant in the bulk argument. Primary source: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/049/4/v49.n04.a05.pdf . Its web-extracted text was read; direct raw download returned 403 and was not bypassed.

## Why the two new operations are not a full proof by composition

For a conservative core with sole flow 0<->A, u^M alone already dissipates whenever A exceeds a fixed threshold. The hard one-port problem is therefore the low-A face. The arbitrary-linkage bulk theorem concerns the broader general weakly reversible generator and is useful in that broader recurrence problem. It must not be sold as removing the low-A face of the one-port problem by itself.

The pinned finite-controller theorem genuinely closes its own whole model, including all low-A states, but its single free nonport inventory and marked-output architecture are substantive. Its controller species have counts zero or one; they do not belong to the fixed-width high-count bulk of the first theorem. Consequently there is no claimed automatic bulk/boundary join between these two results.

For arbitrary coupled cores, neither current result bounds actual transport between unpinned inventory-changing interfaces. Supplying such a transport inequality, a stationary fiber, or a full boundary law as a premise would simply supply the remaining difficult operation. The general recurrence/capability target remains NOT_ACHIEVED.
