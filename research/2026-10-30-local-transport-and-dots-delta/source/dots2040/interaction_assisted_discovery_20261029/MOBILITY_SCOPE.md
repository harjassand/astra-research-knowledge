# Scope check: unequal tracer mobility is not a free global clock oracle

29 October 2026. Focused primary-source check and model derivation; no specimen
qualification, new parameter-family experiment, or novelty claim.

## Decision

**A real, qualified differential-mobility operation would change the information
available and is outside the same-clock contact no-go. But calibrating two
tracers' mobilities in a reference fluid, even with an Einstein relation, does
not warrant `Q_2=c Q_1` throughout an arbitrary hidden heterogeneous network.**
The latter is a strong bulk-law premise. No such premise has been acquired here.
Preserve the qualified no-go and stop the local-family campaign; do not present
an unequal-clock symbolic escape as a new acquired capability.

This is not a claim that uniform clock scaling is physically impossible. There
is a legitimate narrow continuum class in which it follows from a microscopic
law without addressing hidden sites. That class must be established independently
rather than inferred from the desired reconstruction.

## What would actually justify the scaling?

Consider two dilute overdamped tracers in the **same** domain, at the same bath
temperature, experiencing the same force field `F(x)`. Suppose their mobility
matrices are known to satisfy `M_s(x)=c_s M(x)` everywhere. In an isothermal Itô
model their backward generators are

    L_s f = [M_s F + k_B T div M_s] · grad f
            + k_B T M_s : Hess f = c_s L f.

Thus the entire one-particle law is a deterministic time change. If the same
physical coarse-graining genuinely yields a CTMC generator `Q`, the corresponding
exact time-changed process has generator `c_s Q`. This is a mathematical deduction
from the stipulated microscopic law, not something an Einstein relation alone
establishes. For constant scalar mobility the statement is especially simple.
A common nonconservative force is allowed in this model; global equilibrium is
not necessary, although the thermal-noise constitutive premise remains necessary.

A controlled homogeneous-solvent, effectively point-tracer landscape could
approximately realize this structure. Yet changing a probe's size, surface,
charge, shape or solvent coupling may also change accessible geometry, wall
hydrodynamics, adsorption and energy barriers. Same externally applied *field*
does not imply the same force on different tracers. At finite separation,
hydrodynamic cross-mobility can also violate the independent-generator-plus-only-
contact model. Contact geometry and reaction rates need their own qualification.
Boundary/free-solution calibration cannot certify those hidden spatial relations.
An arbitrary latent CTMC need not even possess a shared physical-position space.

## Why the old quotient no longer applies automatically

For a ring with rates `u,v`, particles with known internal multipliers `c1,c2`
have relative clockwise/counterclockwise rates

    a = c2*u + c1*v,   b = c1*u + c2*v.

Consequently `a−b=(c2−c1)(u−v)`. Unequal mobility can encode common circulation
in relative dynamics. This invalidates the **specific** claim that the relative
quotient depends only on `u+v`. It does not by itself prove that boundary/contact
records identify oriented circulation: reflection or further output equivalences
can remain. Nor does it establish general network reconstruction or scalability.

## What primary microrheology actually supplies

1. **Crocker et al., “Two-Point Microrheology of Inhomogeneous Soft Materials,”
   PRL 85, 888 (2000).** The experimental acquisition cross-correlates the thermal
   motion of embedded tracer pairs to recover bulk rheology despite problematic
   local tracer-medium coupling. That is genuine additional information, but it
   observes interior displacement trajectories, not only a boundary return/contact
   record. It does not certify globally proportional hidden hopping generators.
   Read scope: indexed primary abstract and paper introduction excerpt; fulltext
   endpoint returned an authorization error. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.85.888),
   [primary bibliographic abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10991424/).

2. **Levine and Lubensky, “One- and two-particle microrheology,” PRL 85, 1774
   (2000), and “The response function of a sphere in a viscoelastic two-fluid
   medium,” PRE 63, 041510 (2001).** Their explicit bead/medium calculations show
   that generalized Stokes–Einstein response has a restricted validity range,
   depending on bead and medium parameters. Local perturbations can make a
   single-bead response differ from the bulk response; separated-pair correlations
   remove some such sensitivity. This is evidence against transferring a scalar
   calibration universally across heterogeneous interiors, not a proof that no
   engineered proportional-mobility class can exist. Read scope: first paper and
   detailed second-paper setup/validity discussion.
   [Primary 2000 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0004103),
   [primary 2001 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0010027).

3. **Blickle et al., “The Einstein relation generalized to non-equilibrium,”
   PRL 98, 210601 (2007).** A driven colloid in a periodic potential tests the
   nonequilibrium correction to the relation between effective diffusion and
   differential mobility. Importantly, their microscopic Langevin model retains
   thermal noise tied to the bare friction even while the *effective measured*
   diffusion/mobility relation has a correction. One must therefore distinguish
   a justified microscopic scalar friction from a measured coarse transport
   coefficient. Read scope: full four-page primary preprint, especially Eqs. (2)–(5).
   [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.2283).

## Gate before any further positive claim

Require an actual specimen/interface that warrants common geometry and force,
globally proportional mobility (or a quantified deviation), matched coarse
states, controlled interaction physics and boundary-only detection. The evidence
must not be merely a fit of the same boundary data whose identifiability is at
issue. Full interior tracking would qualify some premises but would abandon the
stated acquisition advantage. Without independent bulk-law evidence, `cQ` is the
missing capability supplied as an assumption. No further family extension is
recommended in this branch.
