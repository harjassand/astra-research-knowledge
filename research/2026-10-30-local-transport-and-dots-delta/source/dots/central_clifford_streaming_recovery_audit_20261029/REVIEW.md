## 1. Algebra classification
Let P support K=sum Delta_a^2. For any vector z, <z,Kz>=sum ||Delta_a z||^2, so ker K is the common kernel and all Hermitian Delta_a have support P. In the finite-dimensional C*-algebra generated on P, each anticommutator belongs to the algebra and commutes with each generator; hence it is central. On an irreducible representation it is a scalar g_ab. For real v, (sum v_a D_a)^2=(v^T Gv)I, proving G positive semidefinite and annihilation of every null linear combination.

Orthogonally diagonalizing G leaves q<=3 normalized anticommuting Hermitian involutions. One involution generates a commutative algebra, so its irreducibles are one-dimensional, with sign +/-1. For two, conjugating to Z tensor I and X tensor I follows by identifying the equal-sized +/- eigenspaces interchanged by the second. A third must have the form Y tensor B with Hermitian B and B^2=I. B's eigenspaces produce invariant two-dimensional sectors. Thus every irrep is one- or two-dimensional. q=0 cannot actually occur on active P, although its harmless scalar classification is correct. In a two-dimensional irrep each original generator is traceless and is t_a dot sigma; in a scalar irrep it is x_a. Multiplicity is real physical occupancy: sum n_j m_j=rank K, not just the number of distinct Gram records. Opposite scalar x can share exactly the same Gram and remain distinguishable by the linear signed program, as required.
