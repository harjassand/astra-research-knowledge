1. THE GENERAL HESSIAN-QUADRATURE PRINCIPLE

For a compatible mean-zero gradient G, write

 S=<vec G (vec G)^T>, f=<F(G)>, c_C=<C(G)>.

Suppose there are positive weights w_i, ACTUAL rank-one matrices R_i,
and actual background matrices A_i such that

 sum w_i R_i R_i^T=S,
 sum w_i grad C(R_i) dot A_i=c_C for EVERY admitted cubic C. (3)

Set E=(1/2)sum w_i A_i^T F''(R_i)A_i. Then for every p=F+C+Q+affine
in the stated class the EXACT identity is

           <p(G)>-p(0)=f-E+(1/2)sum w_i p''(A_i)[R_i,R_i].  (4)

This follows from quartic polarization, cubic polarization and covariance
matching. If E<=f, all Hessian terms in (4) are nonnegative by ROC, so
the desired variational inequality follows. A strict budget f-E gives
a strict gap. The A_i are finitely many explicit matrices; they are not
an assumed pointwise correction selector or a claimed gradient field.
Compatibility belongs to G, which is constructed explicitly.

The principle needs only the cubic moments relevant to the acquired
leading ray. It need not reproduce a full laminate or prove that the
field's entire probability distribution is a laminate.

2. WHY USING ONLY THE FIELD'S THREE RAYS FAILS

Put R0=E11, R1=E22 and R2=(1,1;1,1). The gradient in (1) is

                 a R0 cos(x+theta1)+b R1 cos(y+theta2)
                               +d R2 cos(x+y+theta3).

The only cubic resonance is the triple of these three frequencies.
Writing kappa=cos(theta1+theta2-theta3), its cubic moment is
abd kappa times its unit-amplitude, zero-phase moment. Its covariance
and its quartic moment are independent of phase.

The map from twelve background entries, one matrix at each of the
three original rays, to cubic functionals on the twelve-dimensional
admitted cubic space has rank TEN. Adjoining the required compatible
cubic moment increases the rank to ELEVEN. Thus even unrestricted
backgrounds on the original three rays CANNOT satisfy (3).

This is a precise obstruction to that proposed gluing construction.
It is not a QC obstruction. The remedy is to change the rank-one
covariance decomposition while preserving the actual quadratic moment.

7. GLUING ARBITRARILY MANY SEPARATED RESONANT BLOCKS

Let G_j be independently output-rotated blocks of the form (1), at
frequency scales L_j=5^j with their potential amplitudes divided by L_j.
A mixed frequency relation of length <=4 has no nontrivial cancellation
across scales. Indeed its largest nonzero block contribution has a
component of size at least L_j, whereas all lower contributions together
have size <=4L_(j-1)<L_j. If the largest block cancels internally, a
three-term internal triad cannot leave one lower term to cancel; the
only mixed fourth-order possibility is an opposite pair in each of
two blocks.

It follows that cubic moments and quadratic moments add by block, and
the only mixed quartic contributions are

                       (1/2)Tr(<F''(G_i)> <G_j G_j^T>).

For spatial directions n_i,n_j in {e1,e2,e1+e2}, the receiver checks
NINE actual 2 by 2 rational matrices to prove

 F''(u tensor n_i)[v tensor n_j,v tensor n_j]
              >=(3/50)|u tensor n_i|^2 |v tensor n_j|^2     (13)

for arbitrary output vectors u,v. Left rotation invariance reduces
the first output vector to e1; each of the nine matrices minus
(3/50)I is strictly positive definite. Thus, writing s_j=<|G_j|^2>,
the mixed F contribution is >=(3/100)s_i s_j.

For the radial quartic |A|^4 its Hessian is bounded above, on any pair
of matrices, by 12 times the product of squared norms. Its mixed
contribution is therefore <=6s_i s_j. Since 3/100=6/200, adding all
single-block inequalities proves the SAME 1/200 bound for the total
gradient, independent of the finite number of blocks. The polynomial
affine terms integrate away and the argument-translation reasoning
again covers every mean.

This is a real closure property of the gluing mechanism. It does not
handle overlapping resonance networks, where additional cubic sharing
and non-pairwise fourth-order interactions need a new budget argument.
