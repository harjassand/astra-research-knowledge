I’m replacing the overlapping-Schur-block argument with a constructive dual: match the field’s quadratic and cubic moments using positive rank-one Hessian evaluations, then compare their quartic cost with the field’s actual energy. That would prove gluing without assuming a correction selector.

The older [Bandeira–Ornelas laminate construction](https://journalofconvexanalysis.com/articles/jca18002/jca18002.pdf?v=2) is a useful comparison: it shows why a three-wave candidate needs a constructive check against laminates, not just a moment-based argument.
