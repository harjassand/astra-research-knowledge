## Physical source class, actuation and consequential goal

Each reaction station receives two individually characterized modules; only one intended amine/thioester pair is chemically exposed. Correctly protected and separately purified termini are a *source qualification*, not derived from an arbitrary pool. ## Proposed physical operation, in correct causal order
A product R is retained by old DNA hybrid O, independently purified incoming fragment D is reversibly docked by distinct handle A; native NCL joins them through transient sulfur-linked T and then stable native backbone amide C. Keep original R—O intact.
1. Displace the donor docking hybrid D—A with an addressed DNA fuel strand; unjoined donor can exit by flow. Correctly joined donor remains attached through R. Count the flow, residual nonspecific adsorption and compartment cross-talk.
2. Apply an explicit thiol-exchange/chemical challenge intended to reverse any remaining S-thioester T but preserve the N-amide C. The challenge is *not* a non-destructive guarantee or a measured uniform rate; k_cut and k_amide need real worst-case qualification under retained chemical environments. Failure to clear T leads to a false topological completion.
3. After that challenge, allow the donor's separate encoded tag to bind receiver N. Only after this new connection is established, displace old R—O. For successful C, physical ownership follows R—C—D—N; for no covalent R—D bridge, R cannot reach N and remains anchored at O.
4. Remove/cap old released handles to prevent historical tag “echo”; one route is 3′ accessible ssDNA-tail digestion by Exonuclease I while active duplexes are protected. ExoI chemistry is established, but survival and substrate access on this molecule under NCL compatible conditions are UNACQUIRED; do not call tag clearing free.
5. A successful DNA-topology transfer is NOT itself a chemical amide or stereochemistry receipt. Under a *restricted terminal-group chemical grammar* and independently verified purified fragments it can be meaningful. Any wrong stable covalent path survives exactly the same topology test: it requires chemical structure-selective tests (LC-MS/MS and authentic wrong standards) or must be chemically precluded. DNA-only observation cannot solve this.

## Exact result I: full chemical competition, without assumed per-join selectivity
Start docked unreacted U. U→temporary S-linked T at rate a, U→inactive hydrolyzed H at h, U→permanent wrong W at e. T→U at b, T→native correct amide C at k, T→permanent wrong W at w. Define Q_U=a+h+e, Q_T=b+k+w, D=Q_U*Q_T-a*b. Exact first-state absorption:
P(C)=a*k/D, P(W)=(e*Q_T+a*w)/D, P(H)=h*Q_T/D, E(time to any absorption)=(Q_T+a)/D.
The **wrong/correct odds**
  P(W)/P(C) = w/k+(e/a)*(b+k+w)/k.
A tether's local concentration may raise a and thereby diminish the e/a contribution, but DOES NOT diminish intrinsic wrong-stable rate w/k. This is a particular independently rederived 2-state variant of earlier internally accepted Astra N1020 chemistry (do not claim novelty of the general obstruction).

For an irreversible per-join wrong+root-death/correct odds r, repeated donor attempts and N-1 irreversible joins have full correct-original-root probability at most (1+r)^(-(N-1)) under the declared identical independent stages. To achieve 99% for N=1024 requires r <= 0.99^(-1/1023)-1 = 9.8244e-6. A small LC-MS yield or DNA-only tag receipt is not enough to infer this.

## Exact result II: physically accessible topology witness (narrow)
Represent peptide covalent graph and ephemeral DNA hybridization graph separately. Following donor-dock displacement, wash, and thioester-specific challenge, assume all unjoined donor–parent and wall-mediated paths have actually been severed; suppose there are no alternative stable R→D bonds. Once the only way for peptide R to follow the new donor tag N is a stable R–D peptide bond, successful transfer requires that bond by graph connectivity. The reverse holds given new-tag capture and old-anchor release. Wrong stable isopeptides also satisfy R–D and are false positives unless the restricted chemical grammar excludes them. Mechanical ownership **cannot** provide molecular stereochemical identity.

Capture window: for donor-tag intramolecular effective molarity E and residual free-donor concentration c, kon, observing for time tau gives success probability >=1-exp(-kon E tau) and unwanted tag-binding <=kon*c*tau, only under independent constant-hazard binding and no adsorbed long-lived alternative modes. A population criterion of <=epsilon/N false tags and <=delta miss probability demands E/c >= (N/epsilon)*ln(1/delta). For illustrative E=0.735mM, c=1nM, N=1000, epsilon=delta=.01, kon=1e6/M/s, feasible tau is 6.27–10ms; c=10nM OR E reduced 100-fold destroys the window. These are *model* geometry values, not validated molecular rates.

## Exact result III: post-bond chemical challenge versus cumulative damage
Suppose at any joint checkpoint probability of remaining sulfur intermediate <=p_T, and each chemical challenge removes such intermediates with a physically warranted conditional hazard at least k_cut, while each completed correct amide has damage hazard <=k_dam. To make probability of any wrongly accepted intermediate <=epsilon_T, choose t>=ln(N*p_T/epsilon_T)/k_cut. Under these conditional hazard bounds, on a successful final product with B total prior-bond challenge exposures, survival against this damage mechanism is at least exp(-k_dam*t*B). A sequential N-fragment chain challenges the N(N-1)/2 prior bonds; an ideal balanced merge tree N=2^L exposes exactly B=N*L-(N-1) bonds across L synchronous levels (provided failed modules are discarded and chemistry is not repeated on accepted final ancestry). This changes cumulative handling-damage from O(N²) to O(N logN), a standard benefit of convergent synthesis now applied to a topology-filter architecture.

Illustration, NOT observed chemistry: p_T=.15, epsilon_T=.005, k_cut=.1/s, k_dam=5e-9/s, N=1024. Then t=103.3267s, serial B=523776 -> lower survival .7629, balanced tree B=9217 -> .99525. The tree has 56.827-fold fewer challenges. A separate expected synthesis-work recurrence for fully discarded failed modules, C(1)=c_leaf, C(2n)=[2C(n)+c_join]/q, yields C(N)=Theta(N^(log2(2/q))) for constant q in(0,1]. At *illustrative q=.87*, N=1024, unit leaf+join costs expected 7768.9, NOT a measured yield or an O(N) manufacture claim. Note the tree replaces failed modules and therefore **does not preserve one irreplaceable original root**; for that task the serial-root obstruction remains.

## Physical differentiation and rates still unpaid
This is *not* a one-pot universal molecular factory. There is no measured integrated conditional q, k_cut, k_dam, c, kon*E, wash/residual concentration, handle survival, or wrong stable amide detection. Source chemistry for CAPTN can involve 6M guanidinium, 100mM MPAA, acid/nitrite stages and hours of waiting, incompatible with assuming ExoI, DNA strands, or repeated protected peptide functionality survive everything together. Need stage-isolated buffer exchange and count solvent waste, protective-group chemistry, light cleavage, fragment storage, LC-MS/MS and correction of wrong structures. End chemoselectivity depends on substrate (Val S→N shift slow). Uniform kinetic bounds cannot come from finite negative assays against P1's hidden modes.

A conclusive 2-fragment proof-of-operation includes intact amide, isolated reversible S-thioester, hydrolyzed donor, wrong isopeptide standards, and no-donor controls, under the **same flow/chemical cycle**. Compare 2/4/8/16 fragments to established staged NCL and parallel SPPS on total correct product molecules per laboratory joule, per reagent and per time. If chemical assignment cannot distinguish stable W from C or DNA handles fail under the protection/ligation buffers, reject this apparatus architecture, not just the controller.
