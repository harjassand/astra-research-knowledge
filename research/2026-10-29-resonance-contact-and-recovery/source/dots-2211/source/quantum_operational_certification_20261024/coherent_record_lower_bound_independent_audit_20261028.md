## Arbitrary decoder, not just orientation fitting

The service state Psi is a full-Schmidt-rank purification of diag(1-p,p) in the physical |+>,|-> basis. Writing Psi=(I tensor F)Phi with F=diag(sqrt(2(1-p)),sqrt(2p)), the inverse filter has squared norm 1/(2p) for 0<p<1/2. For L=D Ad_Vtheta-id, the normalized Choi trace norm is therefore at most l_theta(D)/p. The standard qubit bound diamond(L)<=2||J_normalized(L)||1 gives the displayed half-diamond bound l/p. This uses no unitarity or efficiency of D. The relevant Choi inequality applies to the difference of channels here, and a two-dimensional reference suffices.

Unitary precomposition converts each distance to that between D and Ad_Vtheta_dagger. Triangle inequality then lower-bounds l_plus+l_minus by p times the half-diamond separation of the inverse unitary channels, namely p sin(2delta) for the stated angle range. Thus an arbitrary CPTP decoder, randomized or dissipative, cannot evade the two-world separation. Full Schmidt rank is load-bearing: p=0 would not control an arbitrary map from this one service state. For p>1/2 the smallest Schmidt coefficient would be 1-p; the source correctly restricts its positive-gap claim to 0<p<1/2.

## Adaptive ordinary data and constants

On any identical classical history, the two hypotheses use identical preparation and measurement operations. Purifying a fresh probe and the fixed source isometry gives overlap |Tr(rho exp(-i2delta Z))|>=cos(2delta). Monotonicity of root fidelity under partial trace and measurement gives the same lower bound for conditional classical laws. Fresh within-probe ancillas are included, but carrying an unmeasured coherent register to later probes is not.

For continuous outcomes the history-tree sums can equivalently be read as integrals against a common dominating measure. Inductively weighting each conditional Bhattacharyya coefficient by the square root of prefix probabilities proves BC>=c^N. TV<=sqrt(1-BC^2)<=sqrt(N) sin(2delta) follows without iid outcomes. Private classical random coins and padding an early-stop algorithm do not change the argument.

Integrating the pointwise decoder separation against min(P_plus,P_minus) gives the sum-risk bound exactly. With delta=1/(4sqrt(N)), N>=1, sin(2delta)<=1/(2sqrt(N)), so TV<=1/2. Also sin(2delta)>=1/(pi sqrt(N)), using sin x>=2x/pi on [0,pi/2]. Consequently max risk >=p/(4pi sqrt(N)) and a uniform epsilon guarantee requires N>=p^2/(16pi^2 epsilon^2). Constants and ranges are correct. The binary prior may depend on the proposed N; this is valid minimax reasoning, and the two angles may be disclosed to the learner.

## Metric boundary and attempted counterexample

Real and reference keep the SAME actual P_theta(y), but attach different service states conditioned on y. Block-diagonality makes half trace distance exactly the average conditional loss. No reference-calibration-law substitution occurs. A final controller with access to the relevant memory/record can realize the distinction; an operational interface that never reveals it is not thereby lower-bounded.

Random opposite decoder errors give a concrete failed extension: each retained-record branch has loss |sin delta|, but their record-discarded mean channel has loss sin^2 delta. Convexity points in the direction that prevents transferring the lower bound. The companion covariant retired-record construction must therefore be evaluated separately. Likewise coherent multi-query calibration, source-memory access, other comparators, and exploiting source correlations carried from training to service fall outside this proof.

At p=1/2 the reduced channel is Z dephasing, independent of theta, so BC=1 and a constant separated-angle obstruction follows. This is a distinct zero-excitation boundary, not a claim that positive-gap acquisition is impossible.
