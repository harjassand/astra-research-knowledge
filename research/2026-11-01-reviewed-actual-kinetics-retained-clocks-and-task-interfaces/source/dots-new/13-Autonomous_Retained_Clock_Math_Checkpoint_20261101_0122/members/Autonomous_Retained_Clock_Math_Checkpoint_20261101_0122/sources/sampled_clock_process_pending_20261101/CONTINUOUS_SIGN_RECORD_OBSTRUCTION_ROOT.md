# Continuous sign observation is not a finite-jump clock

Status: elementary root scope obstruction; independent review pending. It does not refute coarse sampled-path approximation or hysteretic basin-return processes.

For the admitted overdamped dynamics the y coordinate has nonzero constant Brownian noise and a locally bounded drift along every nonexplosive finite path. Start with y<0 and let tau0 be its first hit of 0. Conditional on tau0<T, strong Markov Brownian local oscillations dominate the locally bounded finite-variation drift: y takes both signs arbitrarily soon after tau0, almost surely. The same conclusion follows from the local law of the iterated logarithm for the Brownian increment, since the drift increment is O(s), whereas Brownian excursions have scale sqrt(s log log(1/s)). Thus the raw sign label has infinitely many sign changes in every sufficiently small right neighborhood of tau0. Choosing the value at y=0 cannot repair its lack of a right-hand sign limit at that hit.

A finite-rate two-state continuous-time Markov chain has finitely many jumps on a compact time interval, almost surely. Therefore on a path observation space rich enough to distinguish these oscillations, total variation between the actual continuous sign record and such a chain is at least Pr(tau0<T). This is not a vanishing error on horizons where a barrier crossing has order-one probability. The event can be expressed through alternating labels on rational observation times, avoiding dependence on a chosen value exactly at zero.

The obstruction remains even when all fixed finite coarse-grid label laws are well approximated. It is one reason the finite-resolution denominator and sampling specification must remain explicit. It does not prove that a last-visited basin label with hysteresis A={y<=-b}, B={y>=b} fails to converge or be approximated by a Markov clock; that is a different retained-state return/settling problem. Standard metastability distinctions must not be replaced by a claim that one-time exponentiality supplies an iid renewal instrument.
