## 2. The exact organising law

Let \(\mathcal A=\mathcal L^\dagger\) be a stationary Heisenberg Lindblad generator, with physical emission operators \(L_\ell\). Let \(O\) be a Hermitian unwanted coupling, and define

\[
O_j=\mathcal A^j O.
\]

If

\[
[L_\ell,O_j]=0
\qquad
\text{for every outlet }\ell\text{ and every }j<r,
\]

then its stationary fluctuation spectrum obeys

\[
\boxed{
S_O(\omega)=\frac{S_{O_r}(\omega)}{\omega^{2r}}
}
\]

at regular nonzero frequencies, with stationary or singular spectral components handled separately.

The assumptions are substantive: a stationary state must exist; the relevant operators must share appropriate domains and have finite moments; and additional regularity is needed to infer a high-frequency asymptotic. **No Gaussian approximation is used in this identity.**

Its physical meaning is that the response depends on how many stages of internal evolution are required before the unwanted interaction reaches an irreversible outlet. Under sufficient correlation smoothness and decay,

\[
S_{O,\mathrm{sym}}(\omega)
\sim
\frac{
\sum_\ell
\left\langle
[L_\ell,O_r]^\dagger[L_\ell,O_r]
\right\rangle
}{
\omega^{2r+2}
}.
\]

Thus a directly damped coordinate can have a \(1/\omega^2\) tail; an internal coordinate that first communicates through a lossy buffer can have a \(1/\omega^4\) tail.

The proof uses an exact stationary resolvent identity. Expanding the resolvent exposes successive commutators; the assumed commutations remove its first \(r\) contributions at every physical outlet. Checks on non-Gaussian spin models and passive mode chains reproduce the hierarchy.

This gives a useful change in what must be controlled: **large common nonlinear motion need not itself be logical damage. The relevant object is the interaction that distinguishes logical states and how that interaction reaches emission.** Related algebraic spectral tails in Lindblad systems are established research; publication-level novelty of the present formulation has not been established. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### The finite-duration qualification matters

The stationary law does not certify a whole operation.

I constructed a counterexample in which the stationary response falls as \(1/\omega^4\), but the complete finite-window response, including subsequent ringdown, falls only as \(1/\omega^2\). Switching boundaries launch a disturbance that later escapes.

An exact finite-time identity exposes these boundary terms, the waveform’s integrated moments, and contributions from changes in the control generator. It supports second-order nuisance-response analysis; it is not an all-orders instrument guarantee.

This distinction also survived an attempted improvement: alternating the orientation of complete control cycles cancels an additional scalar integral, yet **made the joint gate calculation worse**. That refinement was rejected and preserved as a failure.

The adopted interaction uses three storage modes \(a_j\), three lossy buffer modes \(b_j\), two-photon stabilisation and a three-mode coupling:

\[
R(t)=e^{-i\sum_j\theta_j(t)n_j},
\qquad
\sum_j\theta_j(t)=0,
\]

through

\[
\boxed{
H_{\mathrm{lab}}(t)
=
R(t)H_0R(t)^\dagger+
\sum_j\dot\theta_j(t)n_j .

The zero-sum condition keeps the desired three-mode interaction fixed. Lower-weight unwanted interactions acquire rapidly varying phases.
