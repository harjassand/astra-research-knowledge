Question selected after constructive comparison
----------------------------------------------
Can physical redundancy turn the dominant loss, phase and entangling-control
defects into located erasures, while keeping leakage contained through later
gates and through a faulty checking apparatus?

The primary construction uses the familiar two-mode binomial-type N=2 code
|0>=|11>, |1>=(|20>+|02>)/sqrt(2). It is deliberately compared with stronger
N=4 loss-correcting codes, current dual-rail hardware, randomized compiling,
measurement-only teleportation and stabilized-cat fault tolerance. No claim
of inventing binomial coding, Pauli twirling, leakage reduction, or a general
fault-tolerance theorem is made.

The organising calculation follows the code DURING an operation. Scalar
compression of each actual unwanted coupling is the useful condition; an
endpoint membership test alone does not establish it. Independent modal loss
is distinguished from correlated loss. The latter is a decisive counterexample
for the N=2 code.

We pursue three levels together:
 (i) exact code/control/noise identities and a circuit-length bound;
 (ii) an actual helper-based symmetry test, with a known logical byproduct;
 (iii) native always-on dispersive control, including its obstruction to the
       ideal test's loss-balance property.

The physical byproduct is tracked rather than actively undone. Removing the
unnecessary undo pulse removes a real unflagged control-error mechanism.
Number checking, helper preparation, destructive readout, waiting, and reset
remain operations. A successful finite calculation is not a fitted device or
a threshold for this assembled architecture.
