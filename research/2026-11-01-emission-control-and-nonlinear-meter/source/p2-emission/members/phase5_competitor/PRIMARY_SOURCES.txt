1. An entangling gate for dual-rail erasure qubits.
Nature 656, 47-53 (2026), published 5 August 2026.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10822-y
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441885/
Retrieved source refs: turn149search1, turn156view0.
The existing SWS gate uses a cavity-transmon swap, dispersive wait, and
return. It reports about 500 ns gates, about 0.5% erasures, and residual
Pauli errors below 0.1%, with strong phase bias. Its loss propagation is
an unknown conditional phase on the partner; delayed end-of-round checks
are explicitly considered. Echoed no-jump behavior is already part of
its experiments. None of those ideas should be labelled new here.

2. Kathleen Chang et al., Surface Code with Imperfect Erasure Checks.
PRX Quantum 6, 040355 (2025), 4 December 2025.
https://doi.org/10.1103/d1v7-nctj
https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00842
Retrieved source refs: turn149search0, turn149academia7.
It studies imperfect, less frequent erasure checks. The leaked-partner
noise structure matters: generic depolarizing propagation degrades the
effective distance, while a restricted physical model preserves the
erasure distance advantage. A new proposal must beat or qualify against
this full comparison, not assume all earlier work uses ideal sensors.

3. Dual-rail encoding with superconducting cavities.
PNAS (2023).
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10576063/
Retrieved source refs: turn151search4 and turn146search4.
An existing hardware-efficient universal operation proposal, including
state preparation and readout and first-order detectable dominant cavity
and transmon errors. It treats differential no-jump backaction and its
relation to postselected error metrics. The basic dual-rail architecture
is an established baseline, not this branch's contribution.

4. A mid-circuit erasure check on a dual-rail cavity qubit using the
joint-photon number-splitting regime of circuit QED.
npj Quantum Information (2024).
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41534-024-00944-4
Retrieved source refs: turn151search0, turn176search5.
It combines a beam splitter and fixed single-mode dispersive interaction
to distinguish joint vacuum and code states. It explicitly uses a swap
echo for unequal-loss no-jump effects. The reported false-negative rate
is finite. Its native always-on Hamiltonian must not be replaced silently
by independently switchable ideal Hamiltonian terms in a new proof.

5. Error-Detectable Bosonic Entangling Gates with a Noisy Ancilla.
PRX Quantum 4, 020354 (2023).
https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.020354
Retrieved source refs: turn176search0, turn176search1, turn176search7.
Established constructions use programmable beam splitting, dispersive
coupling, and a three-level ancilla to detect dominant ancilla decay and
dephasing during bosonic gates. The work develops error closure and
distinguishes it from stronger error transparency. Its appendices discuss
no-jump evolution separately; detecting jumps alone is not a full-channel
proof. This is the main apparatus comparator for the proposed query.

6. Michael et al., New Class of Quantum Error-Correcting Codes for a
Bosonic Mode. Physical Review X 6, 031006 (2016).
https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.6.031006
Retrieved source refs: turn171search0, turn171search9.
Contains the binomial-code framework and two-mode fixed-total-number
variants. The two-mode four-photon single-loss-correcting example is
(|04>+|40>)/sqrt(2), |22>. Our smaller two-photon space is used for loss
DETECTION/ERASURE and first-order no-jump compression, not a claim of
full internal correction of an unknown loss channel.

7. Albert et al., Performance and structure of single-mode bosonic codes.
Physical Review A 97 (2018), including multimode comparisons.
https://jianggroup.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2018-v_albert_performance_physreva.97.pdf
Retrieved source ref: turn171search6.
Discusses the Jordan-Schwinger representation and two-mode binomial
codes, including their fixed-total-excitation structure. This provides
the appropriate established encoding framework for the spin-1 example.

8. A Mathematical Structure for Amplitude-Mixing Error-Transparent Gates
for Binomial Codes. Quantum 9, 1890 (2025).
https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2025-10-21-1890/
https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08870
Retrieved source refs: turn149search3, turn149search6.
Shows that jump transparency and transparency including no-jump errors
can have different squeezing-order costs. This is a serious comparator
for any claim that an error-transparent gate preserves all protection.

9. High-On-Off-Ratio Beam-Splitter Interaction for Gates on Bosonically
Encoded Qubits. PRX Quantum 4, 020355 (2023).
https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.020355
Retrieved source ref: turn176search3.
Demonstrates engineered cavity beam splitting and discusses coupler
coherence and cSWAP control. It identifies a meaningful physical
resource path, while its stated neglected channels are not evidence
that they vanish in a new arbitrary-time-noise contract.
