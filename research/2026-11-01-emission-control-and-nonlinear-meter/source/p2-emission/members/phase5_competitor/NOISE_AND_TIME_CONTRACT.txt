NOISE, LOCATIONS, RESOURCES, AND COMPLETE ELAPSED TIME
31 October 2026

1. CONTRACT FOR THE EARNED MODEL STATEMENTS

The bosonic model retains photon-number sectors needed for losses. Gates
are generated by specified number-preserving beam-splitter and Kerr
Hamiltonians. Their ideal switching and pulse phases are resources.
Independent cold Markovian modal losses have operators sqrt(kappa_j(t)) a_j.
The rate functions may be unequal, unknown, and time dependent. Heating,
unmonitored dephasing, parasitic self-Kerr drift, undesired exchange,
coupler-induced correlated data faults, and faulty Hamiltonian controls
are not reassigned to loss; their probabilities/amplitudes are explicit
residual parameters r, delta_H, and c.

For the two-photon construction, a single data loss reduces total number
and can be identified by a subsequent total-number or parity check.
The symmetry check addresses same-number Q leakage, including first-order
no-jump amplitudes. Single two-photon losses between parity samples restore
even parity and are second order in the cold-loss model. Number-resolved
readout would distinguish them but is a different detector requirement.

For a g/f helper the dominant relaxation is f->e, and helper controls do
not intentionally take e back to g/f. Number-diagonal helper dephasing
commutes through the ideal C X C measurement. A direct f->g transition is
not protected and must have its own residual rate. In a dual-rail helper,
the analogous absorbing flag is vacuum, until explicit reinitialization.

2. FINITE DETECTORS

q_FN means a worst-case conditional probability of reporting acceptance
for an already present, calibrated detectable error. It must be calibrated
on the bad sectors, not inferred only from clean-code preservation.
f_FP means false rejection of clean data. Both are finite.

Under separate local-stochastic fault assumptions a data loss of probability
p followed by an independent detector miss q contributes p*q. This is
second order only in a joint scaling where q=O(p). At fixed q=0.01 it is
still linear in p, with coefficient 0.01. Do not say that a finite detector
automatically gives quadratic asymptotics. Repeated fresh checks can
reduce misses only under the corresponding conditional independence or
local-stochastic bound. A persistent common failure does not yield q^r.

A common physical event that both corrupts data and blinds the sensor has
probability c, not p*q. It must either be flagged at a later independent
check with a sufficiently enlarged support mask, or remain an O(c) term.

Helper initialization faults within the intended number/control manifold
are safe for clean data in the byproduct query. They affect discrimination.
Wrong helper number and f->e flags are read at the end; a single helper
loss plus a separate readout miss is the ordinary product term. This does
not excuse a reset device that silently refills a lost helper during the
same query. No refill is allowed before the flag is latched.

3. LATE FAULTS AND STREAMING

Any finite QND measurement has a last data interaction followed by readout,
reset, feedforward, and possibly routing. A loss after that interaction may
be unflagged at the nominal cell output even if the sensor is perfect.
For a balanced one-photon cell with total hazard Lambda(t), an ideal
instantaneous sample at t_s and a tail ending at T leaves unflagged loss

    exp[-integral_0^t_s Lambda] *
    (1-exp[-integral_t_s^T Lambda]).

This is first order in the tail exposure. A finite map can have a longer
blind interval than the instantaneous-sample idealization. The saved
illustrative 1.5 us cell gives 0.00480 late-output loss, even at q_FN=0.

A useful ongoing model assigns a pending loss to the next check. Before
a fresh check, allow at most a bounded number k of entangling partners.
When loss is flagged, erase the lost block and all partners whose state
could have depended on it since its last reliable sampling interval.
For a single loss with a clean next sensor and one interaction between
checks, this is a constant-size two-operand mask. A one-check common
blindness event needs a longer history mask. Delayed checks after a whole
surface-code round are established but depend on the particular leakage
propagation model; they cannot inherit an arbitrary two-operand claim.

For no-jump Q amplitudes in the two-photon code, gate endpoints preserve
P and Q. The accepted first-order amplitude can remain in Q pending the
next check. The full accepted quantum state is not quadratically close in
trace distance while that amplitude is present. Root's circuit proof must
show how a later check/terminal measurement either resolves it or makes it
contribute only at the claimed order. A terminal quantum output cannot be
declared certified merely because an earlier sensor reported acceptance.

4. NO PERFECT OUTER RECOVERY

An inner erasure label is information for a noisy outer recovery circuit.
It is not recovery of the lost unknown qubit. Reinitializing an erased
mode produces an arbitrary replacement state at a KNOWN location. Keep
that location flagged while subsequent noisy syndrome data determine the
logical Pauli frame; do not assume a perfect state reconstruction device.

The required outer interface is a circuit-level noise model containing
located clusters from data/helper losses, finite syndrome/readout errors,
residual Pauli/control faults, delayed locations, reset faults, and their
correlations. A claimed overhead comparison needs the same decoder,
failure target, full elapsed exposure, and factory cost on both sides.
No outer threshold or qubit-saving factor has been computed in this
competitor branch. Published imperfect-erasure surface-code analyses
already address important parts of this interface and are comparators.

5. ACTUAL LOGICAL RESOURCES FOR THE CANDIDATE

Data per inner logical qubit: two bosonic modes, exactly two photons in
the intended code, access to |0>, |1>, and |2> Fock levels in each mode.
Independent cold loss rates and higher-Fock calibration are needed.

Single-qubit operations: a beam splitter with controllable amplitude and
phase, and an intra-pair Kerr or an equivalent qualified phase gate.
Interblock operation: a cross-Kerr between one mode of each pair, with
pi phase, plus switching and routing. Replacing this with an ancilla gate
requires redoing the same complete no-jump/control audit.

Symmetry check: two controlled mode-parity interactions, one unconditional
data BS pulse, helper preparation, destructive helper basis conversion,
readout, reset/reload, classical processing, and routing. Track the known
logical X instead of applying an undo pulse. Possible helper resources:

  - one g/f/e qutrit plus a readout resonator;
  - two helper modes with one photon and number-resolved readout;
  - two helper modes with two photons, which is sufficient but unnecessary.

If only an always-on single-mode dispersive coupling is available, the
unconditional middle BS is not a free primitive. A switchable interaction
or a fully qualified native synthesis is an additional requirement.

Data total-number/parity check: an additional coherent pointer interaction
with total N, or two single-mode controlled parity interactions, followed
by another helper measurement. A separate helper permits overlap only if
the simultaneous Hamiltonians and their noise channels really commute.
Using a total-N coupling commutes with BS and Kerr; sequential local
couplings are not automatically equivalent during overlapping BS pulses.

6. ELAPSED TIME

The physically exposed duration is

    T_cell = T_prepare + T_route_in + T_gate + T_switch/ramp
           + T_number_map + T_symmetry_map
           + T_basis_read + T_readout + T_reset/reload
           + T_feedforward + T_route_out + T_wait_for_schedule.

Only justified overlap removes a term. A helper's coherence exposure may
be shorter than the data exposure, but the data remain live through all
readout and reload delays. The correct leading loss is the integral of
sum_j kappa_j(t)<n_j(t)> over that entire duration, including gate couplers
that temporarily hold an excitation.

The saved numerical examples are deliberately labelled illustrative:

  Dual-rail completion example: gate 0.50 us; detector map 0.30; readout
  0.45; reset 0.10; feedforward 0.05; routing 0.10. Total 1.50 us. Complete
  integrated hazard is 0.024; total loss probability is 0.023714. A 0.50 us
  Hamiltonian duration would undercount the exposure materially.

  Two-photon symmetry example: two Kerr maps of 0.50 us each; one BS of
  0.20 us; helper readout rotation 0.20; readout 0.45; helper reload 0.30;
  feedforward 0.05. Total 2.20 us, BEFORE data number check, initial data
  preparation, switching, routing, outer syndrome, and recovery.

  With one reused helper and two local parity maps for the number check,
  an analogous extra number check costs about 2.00 us in that illustrative
  schedule. Adding a 0.50 us computational gate gives 4.70 us before the
  omitted routing/switching/outer costs. This is a resource warning, not a
  proposed optimized schedule or a measured timing prediction.

The data carry twice the photons of a one-photon dual rail throughout
these intervals. The candidate may win a stronger deterministic noise
contract while losing the practical erasure/latency comparison. Published
randomized/echoed dual rails must be compared using their actual complete
cycle and the same required error metric before claiming an advantage.
