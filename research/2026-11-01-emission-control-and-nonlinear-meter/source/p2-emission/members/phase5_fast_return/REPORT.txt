# Fast return checkpoint at the existing weak meter operating point

## Result and scope

A calibrated stop can close the **classical means** without waiting 60/G for the gauge to become nearly vacuum. With the supplied weak operating point, a concrete example uses a declared 0.5/G feedforward latency followed by a 0.01001/G correction interval. The required physical number rotation stays within |Δ|≤10G, and the required resonant buffer drive is only 0.642G, below the specified 2G bound. This is not an instantaneous-displacement assumption.

The returned gauge is **not fresh**. An exact time-dependent classical-mean plus quadratic-Hessian calculation gives covariance eigenvalues between 0.41423 and 0.63118 (vacuum is 0.5). Within the stated Gaussian model, a retained covariance domain can support the next calibrated native measurement. The sensing/return map contracts differences in covariance by at most 2.1×10^-12 at the supplied 80.4248/G sensing time.

The example reduces approximate single-photon-loss exposure from 0.004204 to 0.002425 at κ1/κ2=10^-4. The extra pump-isolation switching speed, feedforward latency, finite switch ramps, parasitic control terms, and full cubic/non-Gaussian behavior have not been jointly certified. This is a useful finite-time construction and Gaussian checkpoint, not a combined device-level result.

## 1. Fixed-radius sensing setpoint

Let κ_b denote the actual net buffer amplitude-damping parameter. For a downward rate γ_down and the supplied hot-buffer Lindblad rate γ_up, κ_b=γ_down−γ_up. Define

    C = g3 κ_b/(4 g2²).

The supplied exact symmetric mean solution can be rewritten with fixed storage radius α0 as

    α_base = sqrt(α0²−C²),
    A_s = sqrt(α0²−C²/4) − i s C/2 = α0 exp(−i s θ),
    θ = asin[C/(2α0)],
    B_s = −s g3 A_s*/(2g2).

This requires C<α0. It uses a single parity-independent change in the buffer pump setpoint. It does not require any drive conditioned on the unknown individual signs z_j.

For α0=4, g2=.125G, g3=.005G, γ_down=10G, γ_up=.01G:

    κ_b=9.99G, C=.7992,
    α_base≈3.91935,
    A_+=3.97998993−.3996i,
    B_+=−.07959980−.007992i,
    θ=.10006692 rad.

Using γ=10 without the small thermal drift correction gives C=.8 and α_base=sqrt(15.36), as in the proposed starting point. Calibrating the measured net damping removes that small mean mismatch.

The exact classical equations used for the transient are

    dA/dt = −2i g2 A*B − i s g3 A*²,
    dB/dt = −κ_b B/2 − i g2(A²−α_base²).

Starting from A=4,B=0 and sensing for 80.42477193/G yields radius error below 6×10^-8 relative to the fixed-radius steady state. All startup exposure remains part of that sensing interval; no prepared parity-dependent buffer pointer is assumed.

## 2. Bounded stop protocol

At the predetermined end of sensing, switch off g3 and the pump inducing g2, and turn off the buffer stabilization drive. These are pump-isolation operations. The storage means then remain fixed, while each buffer decays with κ_b/2. This prevents the correction pulse from fighting the two-photon exchange interaction.

During a declared feedforward latency τ_l, let the buffer freely decay. Once the final parity estimate ŝ is available:

* Apply a physical number rotation on all three storage modes. For the correct record, its field phase is exp(+i s θ), returning z_j A_s to z_j α0. A detuning magnitude at most 10G implements this in θ/(10G)≈.01001/G.
* In parallel, apply a constant complex resonant drive ε to each isolated buffer. For initial mean B_l=B_s exp(−κ_b τ_l/2), the equation is db/dt=−κ_b b/2−iε. Exact zero mean after τ_p is obtained with

      ε = −i κ_b B_l/[2(exp(κ_b τ_p/2)−1)].

  Thus a finite drive bound ε_max requires

      τ_p ≥ (2/κ_b) log[1+κ_b |B_l|/(2 ε_max)].

* Restore the usual α0 stabilization pumps and g2 after both corrections. The declared next operation may then start with the retained gauge domain below; there is no claim that its gauge is vacuum.

With τ_l=.5/G and ε_max=2G, the buffer alone needs only .003265/G. Choosing τ_p=.01000669/G to accommodate the number rotation requires |ε|=.64158G. Numerical endpoint residuals are |B|≈4.4×10^-18 and ||A|−α0|≈5.8×10^-8.

The 0.5/G latency is an explicit example, not a measured controller capability. Without such latency the buffer pulse is slower but still finite: for |B|=.08 and ε_max=2G, its minimum is about .0365/G. If the actual controller latency is L/G, use the stated formula with τ_l=L/G and count the entire interval. Finite pump switch-off/on ramps must be included and the endpoint calibration recomputed for those waveforms. Their duration is not hidden in the .51001/G figure.

If the hardware cannot switch off the g2 interaction independently on this timescale, this particular simple correction pulse is not earned. A continuously coupled inverse-engineered pulse would be a different construction; none is assumed here. Pump-controlled two-photon interactions make isolation plausible, but the achieved switching bandwidth is a device resource.

## 3. Quantum covariance and what is retained

Use sign-aligned fluctuations a_j=z_j(A_s+d_j), b_j=B_s+e_j. Permutation symmetry separates one collective and two difference modes. At a general point on the exact mean transient, each corresponding fluctuation pair obeys

    d(d)/dt = −2i g2 A* e − i(2g2 B + r s g3 A*) d†,
    d(e)/dt = −2i g2 A d − κ_b e/2 + input noise,

where r=2 for the collective mode and r=−1 for either difference mode. The program integrates the resulting real 4×4 drift matrices, covariance equations, and fundamental matrices. Diffusion includes both γ_down and γ_up. The initial covariance is .501 I, as supplied.

During isolation and latency, the storage covariance remains; storage–buffer correlations decay and the buffer approaches its thermal covariance. Coherent displacement changes only the mean. Number rotation rotates the storage covariance. It does not unsqueeze or purify it.

The returned full Gaussian covariance spectrum lies in

    .41423 I ≲ V_return ≲ .63118 I.

The cycle's covariance-difference contraction factors are approximately 1.09×10^-13 for the collective mode and 2.07×10^-12 for each difference mode. Hence, **in this same calibrated Gaussian trajectory**, the declared covariance set

    .40 I ≤ V ≤ .65 I

is invariant with margin: replacing the initial .501 I by any covariance in this interval perturbs the computed return covariance by less than about 3.1×10^-13. The opposite parity has an orthogonally related covariance and the same spectral bounds. This supports repeated native sensing in this model without calling the gauge fresh.

This does not prove that every non-Gaussian state with these second moments is safe. A full retained-gauge domain for the cubic device needs higher-moment or finite-Fock control and a certificate that each other primitive in the computation accepts that domain. Reused or overlapping native checks require their actual coordinate changes and schedules; the contraction here is not a certificate for arbitrary intervening gates.

The calculation uses unconditional Gaussian covariance. It does not assume a free conditional-state reset, a quantum-limited gauge-state estimator, or feedback on an unknown logical state. The measured homodyne record can condition the gauge, but deterministic parity-only mean cancellation leaves that stochastic state inside the unconditional covariance budget rather than pretending to remove it. Readout likelihood, colored-output noise, and the parity-error target have not been recomputed here; the root finite-time readout calculation supplies that separate obligation.

## 4. Misassignment and control errors

A wrong parity record reverses the storage rotation and chooses the wrong buffer displacement. At the stated point, the resulting mean displacement relative to the correct storage well is approximately .7992 per cat. After the example latency and pulse, the residual buffer displacement is .01246 per buffer. This is a gauge outlier branch, not a successful reset.

Its probability must be charged to the full operation's fault budget, approximately the supplied 1.1×10^-7 readout misassignment target when that target applies to the actual control trajectory. It is not automatically heralded and is not assumed corrected on the next round. A conservative bound counts the entire branch as failure; a sharper bound needs the nonlinear recovery dynamics and decoder.

A 1% error in the return rotation's integrated angle leaves approximately .00400 coherent storage displacement per cat, or total sign-aligned quadrature-mean norm about .0098 across three cats. That is small compared with the retained covariance width, but it is a calibrated control-error term. This calculation is not a certificate for the separate 1% common detuning error during sensing, nor for the 10^-4 odd phase-tracking contamination. Those must be composed with the stop protocol rather than identified with this return-pulse estimate.

## 5. Full exposure arithmetic

At κ1/κ2=10^-4 and κ2=4g2²/γ_down=.00625G, κ1=6.25×10^-7G. Using the fixed n=16 radius for three cats:

    passive reference: 48(80.42477+60) = 6740.39 photon/G,
    fast example:      48(80.42477+.51001) = 3884.87 photon/G.

The associated at-least-one-loss probabilities are approximately .004204 and .002425. These estimates omit the very small fluctuation-occupation contribution and treat the transient radius as its target value; they do not replace full exposure integration. Switch ramps, actual decision latency, and any added guard interval increase the fast value by approximately 3×10^-5 per extra 1/G of time at this operating point.

The construction removes most of the passive return interval. It does not remove the roughly 80/G sensing exposure. Stronger g3 points may change that frontier, but none is used in this same-weak-meter checkpoint.

## Reproduction and remaining scope

Run `python phase5_fast_return/check_fast_return.py`. It writes `results.json` and completes in about one second in the available runtime. The script checks exact symmetric classical means and the time-dependent Gaussian Hessian; it contains no ideal gauge-reset map.

Still uncombined: finite pump ramps, sensing-control parasitics, exact photon-loss/filter trajectories, the nonlinear cubic finite-Fock calculation, non-Gaussian retained-state tails, and acceptance of the retained gauge by every later computational primitive. Those are explicit scope limits, not claims that the fast return has already passed them.
