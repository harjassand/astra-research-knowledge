1. The proposed mechanism—and the prior invention it must credit

The candidate uses small, flow-accessible copper seams between prepared metallic terminals.

During engagement, both working faces become cathodes. Copper grows across their small separation. After qualification, the liquid is removed and the resulting metal connection carries the load without power. After unloading, reverse operation dissolves the copper connection, returning copper to the working bath and counterelectrode. The receiver geometry remains available for another joint.

This mechanism has relevant direct prior art. Kim and colleagues demonstrated reversible copper bridging and selective etching on protected steel structures, including 200 stiffness cycles; their reported joining and separation times were approximately three and two-and-a-half hours. That supports the underlying operation, but not a fast structural receiver joint. 
Wiley Online Library

Strong electrodeposited joints also exist. Fukumoto and colleagues reported approximately 240 MPa shear strength after a long deposition on a prepared copper joint. The result does not supply rapid coalescence, repeated selective release or adhesion to the proposed stop layer. 
jstage.jst.go.jp

The proposed engineering advance would have to be the simultaneous integration of:

Narrow seams that can form and dissolve quickly.
Real compliant supports that distribute mixed loads.
An electrode arrangement that supplies current uniformly.
Removal of electrolyte before passive holding.
Recovery of chemical stock and local surface geometry.
An observable, sufficiently reliable acceptance operation.

Neither copper redox nor distributed compliant load sharing is new. Directional dry-adhesive systems already demonstrate the latter. 
PMC

The unestablished physical operation is rapid formation of a sound structural microseam followed by repeatable restoration of the same bondable geometry. Electrical continuity and equal reverse charge cannot establish it.
