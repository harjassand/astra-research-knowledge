Let f be the fraction of all returning carrier sites that remains oxidized after thermal oxygen regeneration AND is subsequently reduced by contact with productH2. Reaction 2NiOOH+H2 ->2Ni(OH)2 consumes fraction f/u of grossH2. This loss requires actual contact/kinetics; it is not inferred from an electrode potential alone. A trace-O2 analyzer does not measure this solid oxidizing inventory. Conversely, incomplete regeneration retained in a stationary cyclic state is not automatically an ever-growing product loss. Track incoming and outgoing states and the actual reduction path.
Returning free/dissolved O2 is a separate inventory: each mole removed by catalytic deoxygenation consumes two molesH2 and forms two moleswater. Its catalyst, exotherm, cooling, water removal and service are separate duties. The present utility allowance does not establish them.
Define nO as O2 carried into the H2-side cleanup and nV as unrecovered H2 in a disjoint vent or transferred headspace. Then

is acceptedH2/grossH2, before any additional rejects. This addition uses separately defined streams, not overlapping measured yields. If a reported net Faradaic/product yield already includes a loss, do not charge it again.
For carrier site count nC=M/M_Ni(OH)2, electrochemical charging gives nC*u/2=n grossH2. Thermal regeneration releases nC*(u-f)/4 O2; exportedO2 is that release minus nO. Thus acceptedH2 plus ventedH2 equals twice exportedO2. The executable cases close this stoichiometric invariant to floating-point precision. This is inventory accounting, not an observed reactor.

A concrete optional transfer hypothesis
One possible hardware sequence is: discharge a low-pressure oxygen separator; cool regenerated carrier against incoming charged carrier in an internal exchanger; condition its gas and redox state; admit it through a fully flooded pressure lock to the pressurized electrochemical chamber; remove charged carrier through a separate flooded lock; return it to the low-pressure hot regenerator. H2 andO2 gas circuits remain separately collected; all charged and discharged solids/liquids return to their starting inventories.
Use two dirty-fluid piston or diaphragm chambers with isolated clean-side hydraulic coupling, valved alternation and a physical accumulator/load path. A high-pressure outgoing chamber can deliver work to the incoming chamber rather than dissipating the entire pressure difference in a throttle. This is a mechanism hypothesis, not a new principle or an actual manufactured pressure exchanger. Product-side metal/electrical contact, transfer-valve wear, solids settling, caustic compatibility, seal leakage, dead volumes, redox mixing and pressure transients remain unconstructed. Shared hydraulics must not create a gas/redox cross-contamination path.
Keep the recuperator finite: charged and discharged streams may differ in pressure, temperature, gas content, reaction state and heat release. Actual slurry heat transfer, plugging, abrasion, approach temperature and stable chemistry determine area and service. A nominally fully flooded valve is not proof of zero entrained/dissolved gas.
A vacuum degasser on hot aqueous carrier cannot be modeled as dry-gas removal alone. At actual water vapor pressure p_v(T), evaporation, cooling, condensation and pressure recovery must be paid. No vapor-pressure coefficients, stripping law or zero-water-loss separator is supplied here.
A stationary-carrier construction can avoid this solids transfer, but then must construct its own gas isolation and thermal switching. Near-isothermal stationary electrolysis avoids the alternating hot-carrier reset entirely while paying its own terminal voltage, crossover, pressure and life. Shared pressure/heat vocabulary does not establish that one route is best.
