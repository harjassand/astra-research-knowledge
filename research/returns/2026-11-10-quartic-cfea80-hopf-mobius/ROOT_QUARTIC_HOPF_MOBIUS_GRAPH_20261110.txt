Diagonal Hopf coordinates with the compatibility graph paid explicitly
10 November 2026. Original quartic at k=1; the full all-k question is unchanged.

1. Exact supplied starting point.

On the unit torus let B be the complex-linear Beurling multiplier with symbol
 beta(xi)=(xi1-i xi2)/(xi1+i xi2), xi!=0, and zero mode zero.
For an actual periodic vector gradient, p=B h and q=h, where h has mean zero. Conversely every mean-zero h in L4 gives a W1,4 potential by the supplied inverse dbar Fourier construction. At k=1 the ORIGINAL density is

 J_1/2=Q(X,Y)=|X|^4+|Y|^4-|X|^2|Y|^2
                   +( |X|^2-|Y|^2 )Re(X conjugate(Y)),
 X=X0+B h, Y=Y0+h.

With H1=|X|^2-|Y|^2,H2=2Re(X conjugate(Y)),H3=2Im(conjugate(X)Y),

 Q=H^T M H, M=[[1,1/4,0],[1/4,1/4,0],[0,0,1/4]].

Ordinary diagonalization of M does not preserve the original gradient graph without a further argument. The calculation below supplies that argument, together with a quantitative operator bound. It does not give the desired Jensen inequality.

2. Diagonalization and the transformed graph.

Choose the unique angle theta in (0,pi/8) with tan(4theta)=2/3, and put c=cos(theta),s=sin(theta),t=s/c. Define

 U=c X+s Y, V=-s X+c Y.

Then (H1,H2) rotates through angle 2theta, H3 is unchanged, and

 Q=lambda_+ (|U|^2-|V|^2)^2
      +4lambda_- [Re(U conjugate(V))]^2
      +[Im(U conjugate(V))]^2,
 lambda_+=(5+sqrt(13))/8, lambda_-=(5-sqrt(13))/8.

For the perturbations,

 v:=V-V0=(c I-s B)h,
 U-U0=(c B+s I)h=S_t v,
 S_t=(B+t I)(I-t B)^(-1).

In particular it would be incorrect to keep U-U0=B(V-V0) after diagonalizing. That would change the actual compatible class.

The sharp supplied L4 bound is ||B||<=3. Here t<1/6, so the denominator is invertible on mean-zero L4 by a convergent Neumann series. To see the strict algebraic bound without a decimal, tan(4 arctan(1/6))=840/1081>2/3, and the relevant angle lies below pi/8. Thus

 ||(c I-s B)^(-1)||_(4->4) <= [c(1-3t)]^(-1).

Both constants U0,V0 are arbitrary because the mixing is invertible. Conversely any mean-zero v in L4 reconstructs h=(c I-sB)^(-1)v and hence a genuine periodic W1,4 potential. This proves both directions of the transformed graph, rather than merely a necessary multiplier relation.

3. The Mobius symbol is an affine, anisotropically conjugated Beurling symbol.

Set

 B_t=(B-t I)(I-t B)^(-1),
 a_t=(1+t^2)/(1-t^2), b_t=2t/(1-t^2).

Exact multiplication of the two numerators gives

 S_t=a_t B_t+b_t I, a_t^2-b_t^2=1.

The symbol of B_t is

 [(1-t)xi1-i(1+t)xi2]/[(1-t)xi1+i(1+t)xi2].

On the plane this is the ordinary Beurling multiplier conjugated by an invertible real diagonal source change. Its L4 operator norm is therefore <=3: the constant Jacobian factors in the two L4 norms cancel. The periodic bound on the ORIGINAL torus follows from the same finite-frequency cutoff/transference argument used in the supplied graph construction, applied to this smooth homogeneous symbol. No irrationally changed period lattice is identified with Z2.

Consequently

 ||S_t||_(L4(T2)->L4(T2)) <= 3 a_t+b_t,
 b_t=(sqrt(13)-3)/2,
 a_t=sqrt((13-3sqrt(13))/2).

This is a finite explicit bound smaller than the elementary resolvent estimate (3+t)/(1-3t). It is an upper bound, not a claimed sharp norm. The eigenvalue ratio lambda_+/lambda_- is greater than two, so the diagonalized density is not ordinarily convex. Norm control of S_t does not by itself control translated mixed fourth and third moments with the needed sign.

4. Exact equivalent integral question and computational meaning.

Write

 P(U,V)=lambda_+(|U|^2-|V|^2)^2
      +4lambda_-[Re(U conjugate(V))]^2+[Im(U conjugate(V))]^2.

At k=1 original quasiconvexity is EXACTLY

 integral P(U0+S_t v,V0+v)>=P(U0,V0)

for every two complex constants and every mean-zero complex v in L4(T2). The density has no H1H2 cross term in these coordinates, but its actual operator graph has changed. This is an alternative central formulation, not an enlargement to incompatible fields and not a positive theorem. A negative here would settle the original universal question negatively; a positive here alone would settle only k=1, not every k>1/3.

All scalar coefficients are explicit algebraic numbers. For finite Fourier data the denominator acts mode by mode and never increases the support. Exact algebraic arithmetic can therefore compute the finite Fourier field and its quartic moments; no PDE solve, optimization oracle, new installation or floating sign assumption is needed. The number and bit size of such operations depend on the supplied finite Fourier catalogue, and nothing here asserts a terminating search for all v. For general L4 data the Neumann inverse has tail at most (3t)^(N+1)/[c(1-3t)] times ||v||4; finite Fourier density and strong L4 continuity pass the identity. This is an analytic error estimate, not an unperformed numerical test.
