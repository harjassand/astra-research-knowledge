Let host relative compliance c=1/kframe+1/kbar, prior endpoint separation r0, jam stiffness kj/rest separation rj, and passive counterholder stiffness kl/rest separation rl. Initial internal force is T0=kj*(r0-rj), opposed by the counterholder. A capture error eps changes its retained rest separation to
rl=r0+T0/kl+eps.
Host equilibrium gives
r=r0-c*R;
R=kj*(r-rj)+kl*(r-rl)
 =-kl*eps/[1+c*(kj+kl)].

Thus kl*eps is NOT generally the settled outer residual; host compliance feeds back into it. Conversely, a small outer residual does not bound the peak internal force during capture.

3. Removing the unit does not eliminate its stored energy

After outer disconnection, a free two-spring cartridge equilibrates at
r_free=(kj*rj+kl*rl)/(kj+kl).
Its opposing internal forces persist. For the case above the captured internal force is2613.699N and stored energy6.92630J. This is a DIFFERENT introduced parameter case from the earlier.6J example, not a correction to that example.

The cartridge's internal energy reduction from the connected equilibrium to free equilibrium is R^2/[2*(kj+kl)]. Including host relaxation, the available outer-adjustment energy is
.5*R^2*[c+1/(kj+kl)].
Here that is.000515956J. It is much smaller than the energy still inside the removed unit. The unit needs positive retention and a paid rack deenergization/refurbishment operation. A small external motion or zero resultant must not be described as an unstressed component.

The instant-offset excitation energy is
(kl*eps)^2/[2*(1/c+kj+kl)].
For this case it is.0131827J. Both this energy and the actual work that establishes a retained counterload belong to the transition model.
