EQUILIBRIUM-SAFE INITIALIZATION: A DISTINCT PREPARED OPERATION
5 November 2026

This corollary removes the inverse starting-atom-mass factor in the rare branch.
It does not replace the all-working initial condition by implication.

Let pi be the exact constructed product distribution, B the failure set,
A=B^c, p=pi(B), and nu=pi(.|A). Assume p<=1/2. Freeze the operating ports,
draw the m independent Bernoulli labels digitally, prepare/read the complete
register, and reject a drawn failed configuration. Ideal label-level acceptance
is 1-p>=1/2, so at most two full-register preparations are needed in
expectation. Each preparation pays m writes, verification, the graph-failure
test and protected storage. The primitive preparation/readout contract and its
errors must be retained; a short TV mixing argument is not an exact substitute.

The mean is M_nu=M_pi/(1-p). In lazy uniformized updates, the classical spectral
bounds used by supplied P2 are

  1/lambda - 1/g <= M_pi <= 1/lambda.

Use k=ceil([log(1/xi)+2]/g) and the rare gate p<=xi/[2(k+1)]. Then
lambda/g<=xi, p<=xi, and the reverse no-return observable obeys

  (1-2xi)lambda <= D_k=p E[Z] <= (1+xi)lambda,
  E[Z]>=g(1-2xi).

Consequently

  (1-xi)/(1-p) <= lambda M_nu <= 1/(1-p),

and, for xi<=0.01, the conservative interval

  1-4xi <= M_nu D_k <= 1+4xi

holds. A confidence interval [D_L,D_U] therefore gives the physical generator
mean interval [(1-4xi)/(R D_U),(1+4xi)/(R D_L)]. The fixed clock factor is applied
as in the main note. The same conditional failure sampler and useful-trial
lower bound imply polynomial computation with no kappa^{-1} parameter in this
rare branch.

For acquisition uncertainty at exact pi, the killed-form comparison directly
gives

  Mhat_nu/(1+rho) <= Mtrue_nu <= Mhat_nu/(1-rho),

because M_nu=pi_A^T L^{-1}pi_A/pi(A) is a quadratic inverse expression.

SCOPE: This is a deliberate random safe initialization, not the all-working
state. Efficient rejection initialization was proved only when 1-p is bounded
below. The corollary does not claim efficient conditional-safe preparation in
the regime where safe configurations themselves are exponentially rare. The
main fixed-start theorem, with its kappa parameter and nonrare branch, remains
separate.

INITIAL PREPARATION ERROR, UNDER THE SAME STRONG QND INSTRUMENT CONTRACT

Let p_L>0 be a known lower bound on pi(B). A conservative choice for any
nonempty failure set is p_L=pi_min, without evaluating the rare probability.
The spectral estimate gives the physical-generator uniform mean upper bound

  V = 1/R + [1+0.5 log(1/pi_min)]/(gamma_min p_L).

If an actual prepared initial law can be coupled to the specified ideal safe
initial law with error delta_init, then the mean changes by at most delta_init V,
since every state's mean lies between zero and V. The ideal safe-start mean is
at least 1/R. Thus delta_init<=epsilon_init/(R V) is sufficient for relative
initialization error at most epsilon_init. This also covers mistakenly prepared
failed states, whose first hitting time is zero.

Under independent, stable, nondemolition verification, a union bound over m
written bits gives the required coupling error using

  O(log(m/delta_init)/(1-2e)^2)

checks per bit, plus write retries and protected storage. Although p_L may be
exponentially small, log(1/delta_init) is polynomial in the product probability
bit length, m, log(1/g), and log(1/epsilon_init). No lifetime-scale waiting is
needed. Charge these initial writes/checks once, before deployment. The actual
sensor assumptions are essential; ordinary additive readout quality does not
establish them.

This initialization comparison is made for the ideal reciprocal model. The
finite-factory ratewise comparison holds simultaneously for all starting states,
so its multiplicative mean interval can be applied afterwards. Allocate the
initialization and factory error budgets separately.
