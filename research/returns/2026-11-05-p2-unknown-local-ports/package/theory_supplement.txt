PROOF SUPPLEMENT: ACQUIRING LOCAL REVERSIBLE MECHANISMS AND COMPUTING A RARE FIRST-FAILURE MEAN
Date: 5 November 2026
Authoring role: independent mathematical collaborator in this research session

This is an internal proof-level supplement. It makes no historical-priority claim. It contains no deadline-CDF theorem. The short-path construction extends the supplied P2 theorem; the additional work is the treatment of correlated local reversible mechanisms, unobserved channels, fixed-start correction without attractiveness, and uncertainty propagation.

======================================================================
1. EXACT MODEL, ACCESS, AND PARAMETERS
======================================================================

There are m binary components x=(x_1,...,x_m). The operating equilibrium law is

  pi(x) = product_i p_i^{x_i}(1-p_i)^{1-x_i},  0<p_i<1.

For a thermal realization one may write p_i=(1+exp(h_i))^{-1}, where h_i is the dimensionless energy difference beta E_i. The graph topology and its failure predicate are known. The computational theorem below uses all-terminal network disconnection B; A=B^c is the safe set. The starting state x0 belongs to A.

The generator is Q=Q0+sum_alpha Q_alpha. The independent backbone is

  Q0 f(x) = sum_i gamma_i [ E_{Z_i~Bernoulli(p_i)} f(x_{-i},Z_i)-f(x) ],

with gamma_i>0. Thus a_i=gamma_i p_i and b_i=gamma_i(1-p_i). Each Q_alpha is a reversible, nonnegative-rate generator supported on a declared local block S_alpha. Its rates depend only on the state of that block; a required context belongs in the declared block. It may contain arbitrary transitions among its local states, including simultaneous failures that have never appeared in passive observations. Individual reversibility for pi_{S_alpha}, rather than merely an unverified aggregate detailed-balance statement, is a premise of the thermal-port construction.

Let k_alpha=|S_alpha|, k=max_alpha k_alpha, and let D be the largest number of blocks containing any one coordinate. Let J be the number of blocks. The declared reach is physical prior information from the architecture. No finite local experiment excludes arbitrary additional global or nonthermal channels.

The full-chain spectral gap is at least gamma_min=min_i gamma_i: the backbone has this exact gap, and adding reversible nonnegative Dirichlet forms cannot reduce it.

Choose a known R >= 2 sup_x[-Q(x,x)]. Then P=I+Q/R is reversible and positive semidefinite (lazy). Put g=gamma_min/R. The algorithm uses a known positive lower bound for g when the exact gap is larger. Local rate tables and finite upper bounds on port rates provide R without enumerating all states. One kernel update can be implemented using a finite local-event upper-bound list and rejection/uniformization; its cost is polynomial in m, J, and the local tables of at most 4^k entries per block.

Write kappa <= pi(x0). The efficient fixed-start theorem has polynomial dependence on kappa^{-1}. This is a substantive restriction. For all-working initialization, kappa=product_i(1-p_i); it can stay constant when the expected number of failed components is bounded while system failure is exponentially rare. No claim is made that kappa is automatically inverse-polynomial for every product law.

The physical experiment must prepare a local state, remove preparation fields from the degrees allowed to jump, hold only the declared contextual degrees, and observe native operating-parameter exits. Preparing a rare local state is an intervention; accelerating a transition by leaving an uncalibrated bias in place is not an allowed substitute. Reset time, energy, detector bandwidth, and preparation errors are real resources. If separate mechanism ports overlap, they must either be separately isolatable/marked or assigned an experimentally identifiable, nonovercounting local transition table.

======================================================================
2. ADDITIVE LOCAL RATE ACQUISITION, INCLUDING OBSERVED-ZERO CHANNELS
======================================================================

For each local state u of block alpha, repeatedly prepare u and observe native exits. After an exit, restore the block and context. Concatenate a fixed total L_alpha of at-risk dwell time in u. Under the declared exponential race model, the count for each destination v is Poisson of mean r_alpha(u,v) L_alpha. Equivalently, this is the usual constant-intensity counting-process likelihood; competing exits censor the dwell but do not bias the cause-specific exposure count.

For each unordered pair {u,v}, orient the fitted transition from the endpoint with smaller fitted pi_alpha weight. Measure that physical direction. Denote its true rate r and estimate rhat>=0. Detailed balance determines the opposite fitted direction. Additive error |rhat-r|<=delta_alpha suffices, including when no event has been observed.

Suppose the total exit rate in every prepared local state is bounded by rbar_alpha. A conservative explicit simultaneous bound follows from the Poisson Bernstein inequality

  P(|N-rL| >= sqrt(2 rL z)+(2/3)z) <= 2 exp(-z).

Let K be the total number of tested directed local entries, at most sum_alpha 4^{k_alpha}, and z=log(2K/alpha_acq). It suffices to take

  L_alpha >= max{ 8 rbar_alpha z / delta_alpha^2,
                  4 z / (3 delta_alpha) }.

Then all required additive errors are at most delta_alpha with probability at least 1-alpha_acq by a union bound. There are at most sum_alpha 2^{k_alpha} prepared local states. The aggregate at-risk exposure is the sum over those states of L_alpha. It contains no inverse global first-failure probability or inverse rare uphill rate.

If N=0, the exact one-sided upper confidence bound is r <= z/L. Thus an observed-zero mechanism is retained as a nonzero upper interval, not deleted by assertion. A deterministic exposure cap is available because cumulative at-risk time is fixed. Reset counts are random; a Poisson tail bound for total local exits supplies a high-probability reset count of order rbar_alpha L_alpha + sqrt(rbar_alpha L_alpha z)+z per prepared state. Add each reset's time and work to this count. A separate exposure record is needed if a state cannot be held/prepared with the stipulated fidelity.

Absolute detector or preparation bias must be added to delta_alpha; energy calibration bias must be added to the energy radii below. No theorem converts an unquantified instrument error into sampling uncertainty.

======================================================================
3. MAIN UNKNOWN-SUPPORT CERTIFICATE: LOCAL VARIANCE TENSORIZATION
======================================================================

First take the product law as exact. Write E_alpha for a local Dirichlet form; use the standard unordered-edge convention c_uv(f(u)-f(v))^2, equivalent to one-half the ordered sum. The absolute rate errors define a nonnegative reversible error generator on each block: for each unordered pair its conductance is pi_low |rhat-r|.

Because the measured direction starts from the lower stationary weight, its induced error in the other direction is multiplied by pi_low/pi_high <= 1. Therefore BOTH directed error rates are at most delta_alpha. Each local state has at most 2^{k_alpha}-1 other destinations. The maximum exit rate of the error generator is at most

  s_alpha = (2^{k_alpha}-1) delta_alpha.

A reversible generator with maximum exit rate s has largest eigenvalue of minus the generator at most 2s. It annihilates constants. Consequently, for every function on the block,

  |E_alpha,true(f,f)-E_alpha,fit(f,f)|
       <= 2 s_alpha Var_{pi_alpha}(f).

Product variance tensorization gives

  Var_{pi_alpha}(f)
       <= sum_{i in S_alpha} E_{pi_alpha} Var_i(f).

Apply this conditionally on all outside coordinates and average. Summing over blocks yields

  |E_true(f,f)-E_fit(f,f)| <= rho E0,fit(f,f)
                           <= rho E_fit(f,f),

where the sharp heterogeneous certificate used here is

  rho = max_i [ 2 sum_{alpha: i in S_alpha}
                    (2^{k_alpha}-1) delta_alpha / gamma_i ].

For uniform delta and block size at most k,

  rho <= 2 D (2^k-1) delta / gamma_min.

This is a simultaneous bound for every full-configuration test function f. It does not enumerate 2^m configurations. In particular, it applies to functions set to zero on B, giving killed Laplacian form bounds

  (1-rho)L_fit <= L_true <= (1+rho)L_fit.

It does NOT require the fitted and true additional generators to have the same support. A channel with fitted rate zero is covered by its additive error budget. An arbitrarily rare downhill channel is harmless only after its allowed aggregate Dirichlet contribution is small enough; the theorem does not discard it merely because it is rare.

The theorem also clarifies the role of the backbone. It supplies a physically known route of stochastic motion against which every possible bounded-block mechanism can be compared. Without a positive backbone or a different proved comparison structure, the same absolute rate errors need not imply relative control of a lifetime.

======================================================================
4. ALTERNATIVE CANONICAL-PATH UNKNOWN-SUPPORT BOUND
======================================================================

The following looser argument is useful as an independent construction and for variants where a block Poincare bound is replaced by explicit routes.

Let d_min=min_i max(a_i,b_i). For local endpoints u,v, first set every differing bit to its individually more probable value, then set any remaining differing bits to v. There are at most k flips. Every intermediate product weight is at least min(pi(u),pi(v)): the first phase monotonically increases weight from u; the second decreases weight toward v. A backbone edge on this path has conductance at least d_min min(pi(u),pi(v)).

By Cauchy--Schwarz along a path,

  min(pi(u),pi(v)) delta [f(u)-f(v)]^2
    <= (k delta/d_min) sum_{e in path} c_e [f(e+)-f(e-)]^2.

A fixed full-configuration backbone edge belongs to at most D 4^k such candidate routes: only blocks containing its changed coordinate can contribute, and each block has at most 4^k ordered endpoint pairs. Hence an alternative bound is

  |E_true-E_fit| <= rho_path E0,
  rho_path = k D 4^k delta/d_min.

The main certificate in Section 3 is smaller and should be used for the product-backbone setting.

======================================================================
5. ENERGY AND BACKBONE CALIBRATION UNCERTAINTY
======================================================================

Suppose fitted dimensionless energies hhat_i have simultaneous errors |hhat_i-h_i|<=epsilon_i. Let Delta=sum_i epsilon_i. Since both log p_i and log(1-p_i) have derivative of magnitude at most one with respect to h_i,

  exp(-Delta) <= pi_true(x)/pi_fit(x) <= exp(Delta)

for every full configuration x. This includes normalization; no partition-function oracle is hidden because the Hamiltonian is additive.

Suppose the independent refresh rates have log errors at most b=max_i |log(gamma_i,true/gamma_i,fit)|. The backbone edge conductance is

  gamma_i pi(x_{-i}) p_i(1-p_i).

The derivative of log[p_i(1-p_i)] with respect to h_i has magnitude at most one, so each true backbone conductance lies within exp(+-[Delta+b]) of its fitted value.

For a local channel orient using the fitted lower-weight endpoint. Let an auxiliary generator use the TRUE measured rate in this direction but the FITTED product law for its detailed-balance counterpart. Its conductance differs from the physical true conductance solely by the ratio pi_true(source)/pi_fit(source), within exp(+-Delta). Its difference from the fitted conductance is bounded by the additive statistical error and is covered by Section 3. The direction selected by a slightly inaccurate energy need not be exactly downhill physically; if the ordering is wrong, the energy gap is no larger than the local calibration error, so this does not introduce an uncontrolled exponentially rare experimental direction.

Writing L for the fitted full or killed conductance Laplacian and L' for the true one gives

  exp(-Delta-b)(1-rho) L <= L'
                         <= exp(Delta+b)(1+rho) L.

This follows by applying the energy envelope to each nonnegative local form, then the additive error form bound; the independently calibrated backbone is handled by its own envelope. Thus a symmetric operator log radius is

  eta = Delta+b-log(1-rho),  provided rho<1.

This form certificate handles unknown support. There is no claim that every microscopic transition rate has a small relative error.

======================================================================
6. GENERAL FIXED-START MEAN PERTURBATION LEMMA
======================================================================

Let pi be a normalized reversible law, Q an irreducible generator, B a nonempty failure set, A its complement, and x in A with pi(x)>=kappa. Assume a full-chain gap at least gamma and a lazy uniformization bound R, and set g=gamma/R. Let

  L = diag(pi_A)(-Q_A),  M_x=e_x^T L^{-1} pi_A.

Let another model have normalized law pi' within exp(+-Delta) pointwise and killed form

  exp(-eta)L <= L' <= exp(eta)L.

Set C=4/(sqrt(g) kappa) and u=C(exp(eta)-1). If u<1, then

  exp(-Delta)(1-u) M_x <= M'_x <= exp(Delta)(1+u) M_x.       [6.1]

PROOF.

The inverse of each killed Laplacian is entrywise nonnegative. Holding L' fixed, changing the positive right-hand side pi_A to pi'_A therefore changes e_x^T L'^{-1} pi_A by at most exp(+-Delta).

Loewner comparison implies the operator norm of

  L^{1/2}L'^{-1}L^{1/2}-I

is at most exp(eta)-1. Hence changing only L changes the bilinear expression by at most

  (exp(eta)-1) sqrt[ (L^{-1})xx * pi_A^T L^{-1} pi_A ].      [6.2]

We bound this square root divided by M_x. Let lambda be the principal killed eigenvalue in physical time. The next killed eigenvalue is at least gamma by the min--max principle. Let v_1 be its nonnegative L2(pi)-normalized eigenfunction extended by zero on B, and a=<1_A,v_1>_pi. Poincare gives

  1-a^2 <= d,  d=lambda/gamma,
  |v_1(x)-a| <= sqrt(d/kappa).

The exact eigen-expansion of the mean, and Cauchy--Schwarz on the other modes, yield

  |lambda M_x-1| <= d+(1+d)sqrt(d/kappa).                   [6.3]

Details: the principal amplitude a v_1(x) differs from 1 by at most d+sqrt(d/kappa). The other-mode coefficients of 1_A have total squared norm at most d. The evaluation coefficients at x have total squared norm at most 1/kappa. Since all other eigenvalues are at least gamma, their mean contribution has magnitude at most sqrt(d/kappa)/gamma. Multiplication by lambda supplies the final d sqrt(d/kappa) term.

Always (L^{-1})xx <=1/(lambda kappa) and pi_A^T L^{-1}pi_A<=1/lambda. If d<=kappa/16, [6.3] gives M_x>=1/(2lambda), so the relative condition factor in [6.2] is at most 2/sqrt(kappa). Otherwise lambda>=gamma kappa/16. Positivity of L^{-1} gives M_x>=pi(x)(L^{-1})xx, so the square of that condition factor is at most [pi_A^T L^{-1}pi_A]/[pi(x)M_x]. A safe-start failure cannot precede a first genuine jump, so M_x>=1/R. The squared factor is therefore at most R/(lambda kappa)<=16/(g kappa^2), and the factor is at most 4/(sqrt(g) kappa). As g,kappa<=1 this also dominates the rare bound, proving [6.1]. The sharper constant was obtained by the independent prior-art/limits collaborator after the initial proof.

This constant is deliberately conservative. It establishes polynomial acquisition precision for the full algorithm, including its nonrare branch. It replaces the sharp independent-component coefficient rather than claiming that coefficient survives correlated kinetics.

======================================================================
7. SHARPER PERTURBATION INTERVAL IN THE RARE REGIME
======================================================================

The uniform bound [6.1] may be too conservative for practical acquisition. A tighter interval follows from the same eigenfunction estimate.

Define psi(d,kappa)=d+(1+d)sqrt(d/kappa). Suppose a fitted upper bound dbar >= lambda_fit/gamma_fit is known, for example

  dbar = p_U/[g_fit(1-p_U)]

from a static upper bound p_U on pi_fit(B). Suppose full and killed form comparisons from Section 5 hold and pi errors have radius Delta. Rayleigh quotients imply

  lambda_true/lambda_fit in exp(+-[eta+Delta]),
  gamma_true >= exp(-eta-Delta) gamma_fit,
  pi_true(x) >= exp(-Delta) kappa.

Consequently put

  dbar_true = exp(2[eta+Delta]) dbar,
  kappa_true_lower = exp(-Delta) kappa,
  r_fit=psi(dbar,kappa),
  r_true=psi(dbar_true,kappa_true_lower).

If both r values are below one, then

  M_true/M_fit >= exp(-eta-Delta) (1-r_true)/(1+r_fit),
  M_true/M_fit <= exp( eta+Delta) (1+r_true)/(1-r_fit).        [7.1]

Unlike the crude bound [6.1], the leading uncertainty here is the Dirichlet-form radius itself. Small omitted downhill-rate upper bounds therefore yield useful mean intervals at modest local precision when p is sufficiently small. The initial-state corrections are explicitly retained. One must not silently replace a fixed-start mean by a quasi-stationary lifetime.

======================================================================
8. COMPLETE COMPUTATIONAL THEOREM FOR CORRELATED PRODUCT-INVARIANT DYNAMICS
======================================================================

Assume a connected undirected graph with m edges, product stationary edge law, the above local reversible generator and backbone, and a safe x0 with pi(x0)>=kappa. All rates/tables and probabilities are rational, or are supplied with certified rational intervals included in acquisition error. For any requested relative interval width 1+epsilon and confidence 1-alpha, the following computes the mean M_x0 with work polynomial in

  graph size, table/input bit length, 2^k, D, g^{-1},
  kappa^{-1}, epsilon^{-1}, and log(alpha^{-1}).

There is no factor proportional to M or to inverse stationary failure probability in the rare branch. The full theorem pays actual local preparation/exposure from Section 2. Exponentially large block size, extremely small g, or exponentially small kappa can make it expensive.

The product stationary law, despite correlated temporal dynamics, permits use of Karger's all-terminal unreliability FPRAS. The P2 conditional sampler for pi(.|B) still follows by sequential edge self-reduction: two counting calls per bit after contraction/deletion, with exact handling of forced-zero/one branches. At most 2m counting calls per draw achieve total-variation error zeta using per-call relative accuracy at most zeta/(16m) and failure probability at most zeta/(8m). Fresh randomization per draw keeps the output trials independent.

Take an internal 0<xi<=0.01, for example xi=epsilon/100. Set

  k_win=ceil([log(1/xi)+2]/g),
  p_star=min{ xi/[2(k_win+1)], kappa g xi^2/8 }.

Obtain a confidence interval [p_L,p_U] for p=pi(B) of endpoint ratio at most 1+xi/4. If p_U<=p_star, use the rare branch. Otherwise p_L>p_star/(1+xi/4), so the nonrare branch has an inverse-polynomial positive lower bound on p. Allocate confidence between this count, conditional draws/trials, direct simulation, and acquisition by a union bound.

======================================================================
9. RARE BRANCH: PROOF AND FINITE CONFIDENCE
======================================================================

Work in discrete uniformized updates. Let K=P_A, with eigenvalues 1-lambda_j ordered so lambda=lambda_1 is smallest. Its spectral weights are c_j=<1_A,v_j>_pi^2. Standard Rayleigh/min--max arguments give

  gp <= lambda <= p/(1-p),
  1-c_1 <= lambda/g,
  lambda_j >= g for j>=2,
  sum_j c_j=1-p.

The lower bound follows because every f=0 on B has mean-square at most (1-p)||f||^2, so its variance is at least p||f||^2. The upper bound tests f=1_A, whose boundary flow is at most p. The principal-weight bound is Poincare on v_1. All killed eigenvalues of K are nonnegative because P is lazy.

The dimensionless fixed-start mean M_*=R M_x0 satisfies [6.3] with d=lambda/g. Under the rare gate p<=kappa g xi^2/8 and p<=1/2,

  d <= 2p/g <= kappa xi^2/4,
  |lambda M_*-1| <= d+(1+d)sqrt(d/kappa) <= xi.

One trial samples Y_0 from pi(.|B), runs k_win+1 native P updates, and returns Z=1 only if Y_1,...,Y_{k_win+1} are all safe. Reversibility gives the exact identity

  p z = D_k = sum_j c_j lambda_j(1-lambda_j)^k,
  z=E Z, k=k_win.

The principal term is at least lambda(1-xi)^2 because lambda(k+1)<=xi and lambda/g<=xi. For the remaining terms, x(1-x)^k decreases on x>=g when k>=1/g. Their total is at most

  g(1-g)^k sum_{j>=2}c_j
     <= lambda(1-g)^k <= xi lambda.

Therefore

  (1-2xi)lambda <= D_k <= (1+xi)lambda,
  z >= g(1-2xi),
  1-3xi <= M_* D_k <= 1+3xi.                              [9.1]

Use conditional draws with total-variation error zeta<=xi g/32. This changes the Bernoulli mean by at most zeta. Each draw uses fresh sampler randomness, so the approximate Bernoulli trials are independent and identically distributed. A conservative finite count is

  N=ceil[4096 g^{-1} xi^{-2} log(8/alpha_trial)].

Apply multiplicative Chernoff with relative radius xi/16 to the approximate mean, then enlarge by zeta to cover the exact mean. Combining this z interval with the p interval gives D_k in [D_L,D_U] with endpoint ratio at most 1+xi on the allocated good event. With these deliberately generous constants the lower endpoint is positive on that event; a failed internal positivity check is charged to the computation-failure budget.

Return

  [ (1-3xi)/(R D_U), (1+3xi)/(R D_L) ].                    [9.2]

Its endpoint ratio is at most (1+xi)(1+3xi)/(1-3xi), less than 1+8xi for xi<=0.01. Thus xi=epsilon/100 leaves room for other interval contributions.

Ignoring the invoked counting subroutine, the branch uses

  O[g^{-2} xi^{-2} log(1/xi) log(1/alpha_trial)]

kernel updates. Each update has local-table simulation cost and a connectivity test, e.g. O(|V|+m) by graph traversal. There are at most 2m counting calls per conditional start at inverse-polynomial precision O(xi g/m). No failure-conditioned oracle is left unconstructed.

======================================================================
10. NONRARE BRANCH: COMPLETE FINITE-WORK TREATMENT
======================================================================

No attractiveness or maximal-starting-state assertion is used. Define

  pi_min=product_i min(p_i,1-p_i),
  U=2+[1+(1/2)log(1/pi_min)]/(g p_L).

For every safe starting state x,

  P_x(tau_B>n) = (K^n 1_A)(x)
       <= pi_min^{-1/2} exp(-g p n),

by the L2 operator norm and lambda>=gp. Splitting the tail sum where the right-hand side first falls below one proves max_x E_x tau_B<=U. The dependence on extremely small product-state probabilities is only log(1/pi_min), polynomial in rational input bit length.

The exact discrete second-moment identity is

  E_x tau_B^2
    =2 E_x sum_{j=0}^{tau_B-1} M_*(X_j)-E_x tau_B
    <=2 U E_x tau_B.

As a safe-start hitting time is at least one update, relative variance is at most 2U. Median-of-means with O(U xi^{-2} log(1/alpha)) total paths estimates the mean to relative order xi. For example, batches of ceil(32 U/t^2) paths have Chebyshev failure probability at most 1/16 for relative tolerance t; an odd number O(log(1/alpha)) of independent batches makes the median exponentially reliable.

For a fixed work budget, L0=ceil(2U) satisfies sup_x P_x(tau_B>L0)<=1/2 by Markov's inequality. The strong Markov property then gives

  sup_x P_x(tau_B>r L0)<=2^{-r}.

Cap each of N paths after L0 ceil(log_2(4N/alpha_cap)) updates and charge any cap violation to the allocated failure probability. This gives a finite polynomial operation budget. A confidence interval for the uncapped sample estimator is valid on the event that no cap was reached. Division by R converts the result to physical time exactly.

Because the branch condition implies p_L is an inverse-polynomial fraction of p_star, U and the entire branch cost are polynomial in the parameters declared in Section 8. This closes all intermediate and common-failure regimes.

======================================================================
11. COMPOSING ACQUISITION AND COMPUTATION
======================================================================

Construct the fitted reversible generator by retaining all nonnegative estimated oriented local rates and imposing detailed balance with the fitted product energies. A zero estimate retains its experimental upper error through rho. Preserve the independent positive backbone.

On the joint acquisition confidence event, Sections 3--5 certify pi and form errors. Compute a model-mean interval [L_fit,U_fit] using Sections 8--10. Expand by [6.1], or by the sharper [7.1] when its explicit checks pass. For example, if a valid ratio bracket is [c_minus,c_plus], output

  [ c_minus L_fit, c_plus U_fit ].

Select energy precision and additive rate precision so that the deterministic expansion uses its allotted part of the final log-width. For the general uniform lemma, choosing Delta=O(epsilon) and eta=O(epsilon sqrt(g) kappa) is sufficient. Since rho is linear in the local additive radii, this is a polynomial acquisition requirement, though potentially conservative. In the rare refinement, eta=O(epsilon) can suffice once the explicit initial-state corrections are smaller than the allotted width.

Sum the acquisition, static counting, conditional-trial, and direct-simulation failure probabilities to at most alpha. Rounding energies/rates to finite rational values contributes to the stated error radii. Rational sampling can be implemented with fair-bit rejection and a global bit-work cap assigned an additional failure budget. Very small p is represented with polynomially many bits; inverse p is never a rare-branch execution parameter.

======================================================================
12. WHAT THE RESULT DOES AND DOES NOT ADD
======================================================================

The supplied P2 proof already supplied Karger self-reduction, short return-avoidance flux, its success-probability lower bound, and independent-network full-mean computation. The extension here permits correlated local dynamics that preserve a product thermal law, acquires their otherwise rare forward routes by prepared reverse responses, controls observed-zero channels additively without assuming identical support, and supplies a full fixed-start and calibration theorem without attractiveness when pi(x0) has a usable lower bound.

The new useful consequence is conditional: for this physically specified architecture, absence of an observed local mechanism need not force inverse-lifetime exposure, because its unobserved rate interval is compared to a known local stochastic backbone. The small number being predicted is assembled from calibrated thermodynamic weights and observed/prepared local kinetics, not inferred from passive silence.

This is not a universal reliability certificate. The premises exclude unobserved state coordinates, ageing, nonthermal irreversible destruction, arbitrary global common causes, non-Markov memory, and violations of declared spatial reach. A global port can be included only by paying its scope and preparation cost. The theorem does not certify detailed balance or locality merely from short records. The physical construction must earn those restrictions from its actual hardware, couplings, and instrument bounds.

Bare conductance comparability is insufficient for an arbitrary fixed-start MFPT without structural control. A reversible path with shunts to failure at each vertex and a huge stationary mass at its remote endpoint can make the mean dominated by an exponentially sensitive harmonic probability of reaching that trap. This is why the gap and kappa conditions are explicit. Stationary or quasi-stationary lifetime comparison by itself does not settle the requested fixed-start mean.
