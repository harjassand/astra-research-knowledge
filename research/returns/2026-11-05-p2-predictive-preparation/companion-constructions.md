## Oscillatory acquisition with a supplied spectral sector

The [sectorial passive bridge](package/structural/sectorial_passive_bridge.md) treats positive mixtures of complex rates `-lambda+iomega` with `|omega|<=Q lambda`, known finite Q and bounded mass. It retains a known strongly monotone nonlinear visible law, finite trap/metrology, old-state force, finite release and actual numerical operation. No hidden dimension, rate floor/ceiling or finite rate moment is supplied, but the sector is substantive: zero damping permits only zero frequency.

Physical filtering adds a complex phase that must be included in the fitting/comparison contract. Completely monotone positivity cannot simply be reused for an oscillating signed response. The source proves sector-dependent derivative/tracking/initialization/comparison bounds and prices control/stability margins. [Saved checks](package/structural/sectorial_bound_checks.json) support their stated formulas; no complete large-dictionary physical experiment was executed. The main random-window route allows a different broader hidden LTI class by using superposition and private timing; it does not inherit this companion's nonlinear visible law.

## Dissipation alone gives a finite-horizon response modulus

The [noncoercive lemma](package/independent/STEP_RESPONSE_WITHOUT_COERCIVITY.md) retains strict velocity dissipation but drops the quadratic position-storage floor. For zero-state step response S on [0,L], it gives `||S'||_L2<=sqrt(L)/Gamma`, `0<=S(t)<=t/Gamma` and modulus `sqrt(L|t-s|)/Gamma`. Finite ramp/readout and interpolation therefore remain possible with explicitly horizon-dependent resolution, without a spectral gap or hidden dimension bound.

The permanent spring supplies a stronger horizon-independent modulus and physical position/reaction/ADC bounds; it is useful hardware rather than a necessary premise of this finite-horizon analytic lemma. Without it, range and reaction bounds must be acquired another way. Initialization, force accuracy, metrology and processing still have their own bills.
