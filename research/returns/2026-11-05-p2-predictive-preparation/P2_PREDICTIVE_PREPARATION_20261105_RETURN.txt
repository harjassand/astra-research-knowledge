## 3. The central theorem: a finite random clock prepares a predictable experiment

First put the plant in its passive off mode. For an initial state with \(\mathcal E(0)\le E\),

\[
\int_0^\infty v_{\mathrm{off}}(t)^2\,dt
\le \frac{E}{\Gamma}.

This does not imply that the hidden environment has relaxed. It bounds the total visible motion available to it.

Partition the common counterfactual off trajectory into \(N\) disjoint slots \(I_0,\ldots,I_{N-1}\), each of duration at most \(D\). Define the visible range within a slot by

\[
Z_j=\sup_{s,t\in I_j}
|x_{\mathrm{off}}(s)-x_{\mathrm{off}}(t)|.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz gives

\[
Z_j^2
\le D\int_{I_j}v_{\mathrm{off}}(t)^2\,dt.
\]

Choose \(J\) uniformly and privately, after the initial state is fixed. Then

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(Z_J>\delta)
\le
\frac{ED}{\Gamma N\delta^2}.
}
\]

Consequently, taking a power of two satisfying

\[
N\ge \frac{ED}{\Gamma\alpha\delta^2}
\]

makes the selected slot visibly quiet to tolerance \(\delta\), except with probability \(\alpha\).

The operation has a finite bill:

\[
\text{maximum occupancy}=ND,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\text{selected start}]=\frac{(N-1)D}{2},

and it requires exactly \(\log_2N\) private random bits. The slot includes sensor clearing, baseline acquisition, the full future experiment, computation latency, and clock guards.
