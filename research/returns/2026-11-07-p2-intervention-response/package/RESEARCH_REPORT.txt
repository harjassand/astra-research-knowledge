INFORMATION FOR PREDICTING AND CHOOSING INTERVENTIONS
Finite response laws, polynomial-record global design, and the boundary of transfer
7 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane

RESOLVED QUESTION
=================

For a strongly heterogeneous medium, can information about a declared family
of changes support certified predictions and globally good decisions without
first reconstructing its microscopic coefficient? Under which information
and cost contract is the answer constructive?

This report gives a complete affirmative construction for affine, relatively
bounded positive interventions in symmetric scalar quadratic energy systems.
It covers the accepted degenerate periodic conductivity class in every
dimension. It gives two different operations:

* A finite calibrated record determines a rational polynomial that predicts
  the entire effective tensor throughout the declared control box. For a
  fixed number of controls and a fixed relative margin, the number of settings
  is polynomial in the requested accuracy digits.
* A specified continuous energy-benefit-minus-price objective can be globally
  optimized without acquiring that entire response law. An explicit rational
  ellipsoid protocol constructs its own cuts from feasible scalar response
  readings. Both the number of readings and the subsequent bit computation
  are polynomial in the explicit control description and accuracy digits.

The cost of producing each reading remains separate and fully charged. A
coefficient-name adapter and an explicit finite quadratic-form reader are
constructed below. An unknown material is not thereby measured. A finite
specimen's boundary data do not automatically equal a periodic effective
coefficient, and no quantitative finite-size calibration is invented.

A complementary negative theorem shows that even complete boundary response
data for every setting of an existing actuator family can leave the effect
of a new spatial actuator undetermined. The predictive object is consequently
relative to a warranted intervention class. This is stronger than the earlier
passive-law counterexample, and remains an information theorem with a stated
data contract, not a general computational impossibility theorem.

These constructions use classical parametric analyticity, approximation,
convex optimization and conductivity gauge machinery. No historical-priority
or demonstrated practical-superiority claim is made. The unrestricted QUBO
question is unchanged and unresolved.

1. INPUT, OUTPUT, AND ACCESS CONTRACT
====================================

Let Y be the unit d-dimensional torus, d>=1, with volume one. A(x) is a
measurable real symmetric positive-definite d by d matrix almost everywhere.
The unknown is a scalar potential. Thus this is a scalar PDE with a symmetric
matrix coefficient; it is not a restriction to isotropic coefficients a(x)I.
Use the fixed physical units of the accepted predecessors:

  K_A(x)=max(1, ||A(x)||op, ||A(x)^(-1)||op),
  integral_Y exp(tau K_A) <= M,       tau>0.                 (1.1)

The moment parameters are supplied rational bounds. The accepted existence,
weighted density, primal-dual and periodic homogenization results apply at
their original scalar scope. No recurring reproof is needed here.

The declared control law is

  A_theta=A+sum_(j=1)^k theta_j D_j,
  D_j(x)>=0,  sum_j D_j(x)<=A(x),
  theta in [-r,r]^k, 0<r<1.                               (1.2)

All orders are quadratic-form orders almost everywhere. The D_j need not
commute, have finite rank, be smooth, or have nonzero coupling to a measured
loading. For example D_j=phi_j A, with phi_j>=0 and sum phi_j<=1, describes
fractional changes in assigned regions. Arbitrary positive relative fields
and overlapping regions satisfying the total bound are permitted.

Equation (1.2) is a substantive supplied or physically warranted model. A
finite successful fit cannot establish that an unknown source obeys it.
The microscopic fields may be unknown in the record-input version. In the
coefficient-input version, valid represented names for A and each D_j are
supplied and every request to those names is charged.

Write F(theta)=(A_theta)_*. Under a fixed imposed mean field e,

  m_e(theta)=e.F(theta)e
       = min_(g in G_A) integral (e+g).A_theta(e+g).          (1.3)

G_A is the accepted closed periodic gradient space. This is an imposed-field
energy, not a force-controlled compliance objective silently substituted by
conductance. We optimize m_e(theta)-price.theta when that is the specified
utility. Different nonlinear or reciprocal utilities require their own
conversion and derivative bounds.

There are two numerical prior bounds:

  U>=||A_*||op,       S>=m_e(0).                            (1.4)

They may be supplied or obtained from calibrated baseline readings. Always
U=M/tau and S=(M/tau)|e|^2 are safe rational choices, since tau K<=exp(tau K)
and the constant-field variational trial bounds A_*. A tighter acquired upper
bound can reduce the cost. No exact baseline tensor is presumed known.

A scalar response record at a rational setting t and rational loading e is
a rational midpoint y and radius delta with

  |y-m_e(t)|<=delta.                                      (1.5)

The radius includes all calibration, setting, loading, receiver, preparation,
reset, drift and conversion errors needed to justify this nominal statement.
No statistical independence is assumed. Let C_acq(t,e,delta) be the actual
cost of obtaining the record. The algorithms below charge every such term.
An asserted source with unknown cost is not a uniform efficient solver.

The whole-law output is a rational tensor polynomial and a uniform error
bound. The decision output is an explicitly represented feasible control,
its calibrated value interval, and a global regret bound. Neither operation
requires reconstructing the coefficient or identifying its internal state.

2. A COMMON ENERGY SPACE REMOVES SPATIAL CONDITIONING FROM THE CONTROL LAW
========================================================================

From (1.2),

  (1-r)A <= A_theta <= (1+r)A,
  K_(A_theta) <= K_A/(1-r),
  integral exp((1-r)tau K_(A_theta)) <= M.                  (2.1)

The moment rescaling is global and uses fixed units. It is not a pointwise
normalization. Relative comparability makes all these problems use the same
closed gradient space and equivalent energy norms.

Let H=L^2(A;Y;R^d), with inner product <u,v>_A=integral u.A v.
Let P be the orthogonal projection onto G_A. Let S0 map e to its baseline
minimizing field E0^e=e+g0. Then P S0=0 and S0* S0=A_*.

Define the bounded self-adjoint operators T_j=A^(-1)D_j on H. They satisfy
T_j>=0 and sum T_j<=I. For complex control z, put W(z)=sum z_j T_j.
Even when these operators do not commute,

  ||W(z)|| <= max_j |z_j|.                                (2.2)

To see this, set Vh=(T_j^(1/2)h)_j. Then ||V||<=1 and
W=V* diag(z_1 I,...,z_k I) V. This compression identity, rather than a
triangle inequality losing a factor k, gives the common polydisc.

For max |z_j|<1 the Schur-complement formula is

  F(z)=S0* [ I+W
       - W P (I_G+P W|_G)^(-1) P W ] S0.                  (2.3)

For real z this follows by minimizing in the gradient correction:
g=-(I_G+P W|_G)^(-1)P W S0e. The displayed expression extends holomorphically
to complex z by the norm-convergent Neumann series. The adjoint S0* is fixed;
there is no conjugation of the variable z in this analytic extension.
If q0=max |z_j|, then

  ||F(z)|| <= U(1+q0+q0^2/(1-q0)) = U/(1-q0).             (2.4)

P and the inverse in (2.3) are proof devices. The record-input algorithms do
not evaluate them or assume them as oracles.

For real controls, variational comparison gives

  (1-r)A_* <= F(theta) <= (1+r)A_*.                        (2.5)

Differentiating the coercive problem in this common energy space yields

  Dm_e(theta)[h] = <E_theta, W(h) E_theta>_A,
  D^2m_e(theta)[h,h]
    = -2 <P W(h)E_theta,
          (I_G+P W(theta)|_G)^(-1)P W(h)E_theta>_A.        (2.6)

In particular m_e is concave, as also follows directly by taking an infimum
of affine functions in (1.3). Since
||E_theta||_A^2<=m_e(theta)/(1-r)<=((1+r)/(1-r))S,

  |Dm_e[h]| <= L_m ||h||infinity,
  0<=-D^2m_e[h,h]<=H0 ||h||infinity^2,

  L_m=(1+r)S/(1-r),
  H0=2(1+r)S/(1-r)^2.                                   (2.7)

The tensor is Lipschitz with constant (1+r)U/(1-r) in the same control norm.
These constants depend on the relative actuation margin and the energy
scale, not on an absolute essential ellipticity constant or an unresolved
microscopic mesh.

The argument is an abstract quadratic-energy argument. It also applies to
an explicitly finite SPD quadratic form with imposed port coordinates and
free internal coordinates, or another Hilbert variational energy with the
same bounded positive form perturbations. It does not extend automatically
to nonlinear, hysteretic, time-dependent or nonvariational systems.

3. A FINITE RATIONAL RECORD FOR THE ENTIRE RESPONSE FAMILY
========================================================

Normalize y=theta/r. Choose a rational rho>1 such that

  chi=(rho+rho^(-1))/2,   r chi<1,
  B_rho=U/(1-r chi),    B0=(1+r)U,
  L_y=r(1+r)U/(1-r).                                    (3.1)

Equation (2.4) bounds F(ry) by B_rho on the product of the k Bernstein
ellipses of parameter rho. This uses analyticity in control coordinates;
the spatial coefficient may remain only measurable and degenerate.

Use n Gauss-Chebyshev nodes x_j=cos((2j+1)pi/(2n)) in each coordinate.
There are N_grid=n^k settings. If I_n denotes the tensor interpolation
operator of degree at most n-1 in each coordinate, then

  sup_y ||F(ry)-I_n F(ry)|| <= E_n,
  E_n=2^(k+1) k B_rho rho^(-n)/(1-rho^(-1))^k.             (3.2)

Proof. The Banach-valued Chebyshev coefficients have norm at most
2^k B_rho rho^(-|alpha|). On the Gauss grid, every one-dimensional
Chebyshev mode aliases to a signed bounded mode or to zero. Thus the norm
of the interpolant of a tensor mode is at most one. Modes having at least
one index >=n have summed coefficient norm at most
2^k B_rho k rho^(-n)/(1-rho^(-1))^k. Comparing their true and interpolated
contributions proves (3.2). This also supplies a direct derivation of the
bound rather than assuming a convergence rate from a fit.

The actual nodes q_j are rational, |q_j-x_j|<=delta, q_j in [-1,1]. At every
actual setting r(q_j1,...,q_jk), acquire a rational tensor midpoint with
operator error at most nu. A symmetric d by d tensor requires
d(d+1)/2 scalar readings: e_i and e_i+e_j. Individual scalar radii
nu/(2d) suffice. The off-diagonal error after polarization is at most
3nu/(4d); the maximum row sum bounds the operator error by nu.

Apply the finite cosine transform using the rational weights T_l(q_j),
then express the result in the ordinary Chebyshev basis T_l(y). All final
coefficients are rational. The exact uniform certificate is

  sup_y ||F(ry)-Fhat(y)||
    <= E_n+(2n)^k [nu+(L_y+B0 k n^2)delta].                (3.3)

Indeed |T_l|<=1 and |T_l'|<=l^2 on [-1,1]. A product of k weights changes
by at most k n^2 delta. The value change at a displaced node is at most
L_y delta. Each coefficient's total transform weight is at most 2^k, and
there are n^k coefficients. This proves (3.3), including correlated
record errors and rational-node errors.

For requested epsilon, choose n so E_n<=epsilon/3, and take

  nu=epsilon/[12(2n)^k],
  delta<=min(1/(100n^2),
             epsilon/[12(2n)^k(L_y+B0 k n^2)]).             (3.4)

The code chooses a dyadic delta below this bound. Its rational node generator
uses Machin's identity for pi, alternating arctangent intervals, a Taylor
remainder for cosine, and final dyadic rounding. No exact irrational input
or elementary-function oracle is present.

One uniform choice is rho=1+s, where s is the largest dyadic power <=sqrt(1-r).
It is found by rational comparisons s^2<=1-r. Then 1-r chi>=(1-r)/2.
Writing g=1-r, the required degree obeys the conservative scaling

  n=O(g^(-1/2)[1+log_+(U/epsilon)+log(k+1)
                         +(k+1)log(1/g)+k]).              (3.5)

For fixed k,r,U, N_grid=O((1+log(1/epsilon))^k). Tensor output and future
evaluation cost d^2 N_grid storage and O(d^2 k N_grid) rational operations.
Construction by separable transforms costs O(d^2 k n N_grid) operations.
There is no dimension-independent claim in k, and no optimality claim for
this conservative interpolation bound.

The record precision is O(log(1/epsilon)+k log n+log_+ U) bits apart from
explicit parameter magnitudes; node precision includes log k, log n and
log(1/g). For arbitrary rational record denominators, let B_tab be the total
table bit length and b the dyadic node precision. Transform intermediates
have a common-denominator bound of the form

  O(B_tab+k n(b+2)+k log(n+1)+log_+(B0+1)).                 (3.6)

This follows by taking the product of all input denominators, the common
dyadic node denominator to degree k(n-1), and the transform denominator n^k.
Using schoolbook integer arithmetic and rational gcd reduction gives a
polynomial bit cost in these displayed quantities. For example, if Z_grid
is one plus the full parameter/table bit length, d+k+n+N_grid+b, a
conservative implementation bound is O(Z_grid^18). This intentionally loose
bound includes rational elementary-function enclosures and all transforms;
the operation count and bit bound above expose the useful dependence.

Total cost is the sum of every acquisition cost plus this finite bit work.
The source may be much more expensive than the interpolation.

4. GLOBAL DESIGN WITH A POLYNOMIAL NUMBER OF RESPONSE RECORDS
============================================================

Let V=(v_1,...,v_q) be an explicitly listed rational vertex set contained
in [-r,r]^k. Let K=conv V. The decision problem is

  maximize f(theta)=m_e(theta)-ell.theta over theta in K.    (4.1)

The output theta_hat is feasible and satisfies f* - f(theta_hat)<=epsilon.
For the ordinary nonnegative budget simplex, q=k+1. An exponential vertex
enumeration is not hidden inside the input contract. If a different
polytope is supplied only by constraints, conversion to this representation
has not been supplied for free.

Put

  D=max_coordinate(max_i v_i-coordinate-min_i v_i-coordinate),
  C=H0 D^2,     L_f=L_m+||ell||_1.                         (4.2)

These bound curvature along every feasible vertex direction and the
objective's Lipschitz constant in physical control infinity norm.

Lift the problem to the standard m-dimensional simplex, m=q-1:

  Delta={x_i>=0, sum_(i=1)^m x_i<=1},
  theta(x)=v_q+sum_(i=1)^m x_i(v_i-v_q).                  (4.3)

This avoids an unprovided physical inradius or rank condition. Delta is
full-dimensional even when the listed physical vertices are redundant or
their convex hull is lower-dimensional. Its Euclidean outer radius about
zero is one; it contains a ball of radius 1/q^2 about its barycenter.

Use the internal accuracy eta=epsilon/2 and the following rational values:

  Cbar=max(C,eta), h=eta/(16 Cbar),
  nu=eta h/256=eta^2/(4096 Cbar),
  L=max(m D L_f,eta),
  rho_star=eta/(16 q^2 L),
  B=min{b>=0:2^(-b)<=rho_star},
  N_cut=4 m(m+1)(B+1).                                   (4.4)

q=1 needs one reading; q=2 uses interval bisection. Assume q>=3 for the
ellipsoid description below. These parameters are computed by rational
arithmetic and integer comparisons.

4.1 Construct every objective cut from feasible readings
-------------------------------------------------------

At a feasible center x, let theta=theta(x) and acquire objective records
of total nominal error at most nu at

  theta,      theta+h(v_i-theta), i=1,...,q.               (4.5)

All these settings are feasible, including when theta is on a face. There
is no gradient query or extrapolation outside K. Let s_i be the measured
forward secant, using the same baseline midpoint. If
d_i=Df(theta)[v_i-theta], concavity and curvature give

  s_i-2nu/h <= d_i <= s_i+2nu/h+C h/2.                    (4.6)

Consequently

  f* - f(theta) <= G_U=max_i s_i+2nu/h+C h/2.              (4.7)

This is already a finite global certificate. For the ellipsoid cut, put

  g_i=s_i-s_q, i=1,...,m.                                (4.8)

The true derivative in lifted coordinate i is d_i-d_q. The one-sided
secant biases lie in a common interval of length C h/2, so

  ||g-grad_x f||infinity <= 4nu/h+C h/2 <= 3eta/64.         (4.9)

For x,z in Delta, ||z-x||_1<=2. The error of the associated supporting
linear form is therefore at most 3eta/32. This constructs the approximate
separation information; no separation oracle is assumed.

If the ellipsoid center violates a simplex constraint, use its corresponding
central constraint cut and acquire no physical reading. Otherwise acquire
(4.5), update the best recorded objective midpoint, and use the central
cut g.(z-x)>=0. If (4.7) is at most eta, the protocol can stop early.

4.2 Quantize the actual actuators, not just the internal arithmetic
-----------------------------------------------------------------

The lifted weights lambda=(x_1,...,x_m,1-sum x_i) are nonnegative and sum
to one at every acquired center; probe weights are (1-h)lambda+h e_i.
Choose an integer b_act with

  a_loss=L_f D(q-1) 2^(-b_act) <= nu/4.                   (4.10)

Round weights 2,...,q down to multiples of 2^(-b_act), assigning all
discarded mass to weight 1. The new weights still lie in the simplex.
The control displacement has infinity norm at most D(q-1)2^(-b_act),
so the objective displacement is at most a_loss.

Acquire the scalar energy at this rounded feasible physical setting with
radius nu/4; subtract its exact known price. Round the objective midpoint
to a dyadic grid of spacing <=nu/2. Reading error, actuator rounding and
digital rounding together are at most 3nu/4, hence at most nu as required
in (4.6). The output is the actuated feasible control, rather than a
nominal state requiring arbitrary physical precision. Its regret is at
most the nominal certificate plus a_loss.

Thus actuator precision is O(log(1/epsilon)+log_+ Cbar+log_+ L_f+log q),
in addition to the explicit vertex encoding. Fine ellipsoid centers are
internal digital numbers; they are not uncharged analog settings.

4.3 A fully rational ellipsoid update
------------------------------------

Write E(c,H)={z:(z-c)^T H^(-1)(z-c)<=1}. Begin with c=0,H=I.
For a nonzero central-cut normal a, retaining a.(z-c)<=0, the classical
containing half-ellipsoid is

  u=H a, b=a.H a,
  c_e=c-u/[(m+1)sqrt(b)],
  H_e=m^2/(m^2-1) [H-2 u u^T/((m+1)b)].                  (4.11)

These standard geometric formulas and their volume decrease are credited
to the classical ellipsoid literature [4]. Here is the finite rounding
contract used by the implementation, stated to expose every precision cost.

Put beta=1/[64m(m+1)]. Compute a rational t satisfying
|t sqrt(b)-1|<=m beta, using rational bisection for sqrt(b) and taking the
reciprocal of its upper endpoint. Set c'=c-t u/(m+1), then round each
coordinate to a dyadic grid with

  m 2^(-b_c)<=beta 2^(-N_cut).                            (4.12)

The first center error has H_e^(-1) norm at most beta. Indeed the eigenvalue
of the normalized H_e in the cut direction is m^2/(m+1)^2, so this norm is
exactly |t sqrt(b)-1|/m. The second center error has norm at most beta as
well, using the lower eigenvalue bound below. Inflate H_e by (1+2beta)^2.
Round its entries symmetrically to a dyadic grid and add m 2^(-b_H) I,
where

  2m 2^(-b_H)<=beta 2^(-2N_cut).                          (4.13)

The resulting H_new dominates the inflated matrix and is at most
(1+beta) times it. Therefore the retained half-ellipsoid is contained in
E(c_new,H_new), despite all roundings.

Inductively, before the last update,

  lambda_min(H_t)>=2^(-2t),  lambda_max(H_t)<=2^t.         (4.14)

The lower bound follows from H_e>=(m/(m+1))^2 H>=H/4 and the outward
rounding; the upper bound follows because the full multiplicative inflation
is less than two. These bounds justify the precision allocations (4.12)
and (4.13) without calculating a numerical smallest eigenvalue.

The classical central-cut volume ratio is at most exp(-1/[2(m+1)]).
Our added factors contribute at most
2m beta+(m/2)beta=5/[128(m+1)] to its logarithm. Thus each rounded update
still has volume ratio at most

  exp(-1/[4(m+1)]).                                      (4.15)

No real square-root instruction, exact-center assumption, or uncounted
exponentially growing rational denominator appears in this implementation.
The center grid uses N_cut+O(log m) bits after the binary point, and the
shape grid uses 2N_cut+O(log m). Their numerator lengths are also polynomial:
the eigenvalue bounds and the finite sum of center displacements bound
all coordinate magnitudes by 2^O(N_cut).

4.4 Global termination proof, including noisy value ordering
-----------------------------------------------------------

The lifted objective is L-Lipschitz in Euclidean norm, since
||theta(x)-theta(z)||infinity<=D||x-z||_1<=D m||x-z||_2.
Let x* maximize it. Let bar{x} be the simplex barycenter, and put
t0=eta/(16L). The ball of radius rho_star=t0/q^2 about
(1-t0)x*+t0 bar{x} lies in Delta. Every point in this ball is within 3t0
of x*, so its objective is at least f*-3eta/16.

Suppose the nominal point whose recorded midpoint is largest were still
more than eta below f*. Every acquired center would then have value less
than f*-eta+2nu. The near-optimal ball would have value strictly larger
than every such center by more than 3eta/32. Concavity and (4.9) imply that
each measured central cut retains the entire ball. Constraint cuts retain
Delta and therefore the ball too. The zero approximate-gradient case cannot
occur under this supposition; alternatively (4.7) certifies that case.

After N_cut updates, however, (4.15) bounds the ellipsoid's volume by
exp(-m(B+1)) times the initial unit-ball volume. This is strictly smaller
than rho_star^m times that volume, since 2^(-B)<=rho_star and ln 2<1.
It cannot contain the ball. This contradiction proves nominal regret <=eta.
Returning the best actually actuated point adds at most a_loss<=nu/4,
and therefore gives regret strictly below epsilon. For m=1, interval
bisection provides an even stronger volume decrease; m=0 is immediate.

The protocol thus terminates with a valid global certificate even if an
early directional-gap test never succeeds. This is not a local optimum or
an empirical stopping rule.

4.5 Complete resource bound
---------------------------

There are at most N_cut iterations and (q+1)N_cut scalar readings. Since

  N_cut=O(q^2[1+log(q)+log_+(L/epsilon)]),                 (4.16)

the number of records is polynomial in q and the requested accuracy digits.
Each raw source record needs radius nu/4=eta^2/(16384 Cbar). Thus its
requested precision is O(log(1/epsilon)+log_+ Cbar) bits, not an exact real.
Actuator precision is given by (4.10); all vertices and their coefficient
bits remain part of the complete input.

Per iteration, matrix and constraint work uses O(q^2+qk) rational operations,
plus polynomial-time square-root bisection. After the explicit roundings,
integer lengths are O(N_cut+B_par+log(q+1)), where B_par is the total binary
parameter/vertex/loading/price description. Raw records are first read in
full and then rounded; their actual total bit length B_rec is also charged.
With Z=1+B_par+B_rec+N_cut+q+k+d, a conservative bound for the complete
digital protocol, including the saved transcript, is O(Z^10) bit operations.
The formulas above give sharper operation and precision information.

The total resource contract is

  sum_(all requested records) C_acq(t,e,nu/4) + O(Z^10).    (4.17)

Setting, reset, geometry, calibration, receiver and source-computation work
are inside the acquisition terms, rather than replaced by unit-cost calls.
If an input representation supplies polynomial-cost readings at these
precisions, the combined decision procedure is polynomial in its full input
and accuracy digits. For arbitrary represented measurable media the earlier
complete solver provides termination but not such a uniform cost guarantee.

5. TWO CONSTRUCTED NUMERICAL ACQUISITION PATHS
============================================

5.1 Valid coefficient names in the accepted continuum class
-----------------------------------------------------------

A name of a field supplies an explicitly expanded rational dyadic box field
whose L1 operator-norm error is <=2^(-s). At a requested rational theta,
choose s so

  (1+sum_j |theta_j|)2^(-s)<=2^(-n).

Read the names of A and all D_j to s, expand their common dyadic grid, and
form their rational linear combination. The triangle inequality certifies
the combined error. Every name, box, coefficient, common-grid expansion and
bit operation is paid. The true coefficient has the moment bound (2.1).

The unchanged accepted solver then constructs primal and dual rational
certificates. Request a field-energy gap small enough that half the final
energy interval is below the scalar reading radius. Its midpoint and half
width form (1.5). The inverse-coefficient data are obtained by the accepted
explicit filtering construction, not by a new inverse-data oracle.

The acquisition cost is the sum over all solver stages of the accepted
name-generation and expanded-data arithmetic bounds. In the predecessor's
notation it is of the form

  sum_stages [T_Atheta(n)+T_Atheta(s_n)+(1+Z_n)^16].        (5.1)

The T_Atheta terms now include all component-name requests and common-grid
work. The solver's termination index depends on the represented instance;
there is no new uniform polynomial bound in a succinct coefficient program.
The adapter represented_name_reader in intervention.py implements this path.

5.2 Explicit finite positive quadratic systems
----------------------------------------------

Alternatively supply a rational SPD matrix Q0, explicitly listed rational
PSD direction matrices Qj with sum Qj<=Q0, and a partition into imposed port
coordinates e and free internal coordinates u. Define

  m_e(theta)=min_u (e,u)^T[Q0+sum theta_j Qj](e,u).

At every rational control, solve the internal SPD linear system by exact
Gaussian elimination and evaluate the quadratic energy, then round it with
a rational enclosure. finite_quadratic_reader implements this operation.
All matrix dimensions and entries are explicit input. Classical determinant
bit bounds for rational linear algebra make each reading polynomial in the
matrix input length, control bits and output precision. This yields a full
polynomial-bit algorithm for the continuous design problem in this finite
class, with no source oracle left unconstructed.

It is a convex continuous design result. It does not optimize arbitrary
binary topologies, arbitrary nonlinear utilities, or QUBO energies.

6. CONSEQUENCES AND THE BOUNDARY OF TRANSFER
==========================================

6.1 Whole-law information and task information are different
------------------------------------------------------------

The whole-law record supports every later control query and every loading:
an operator error epsilon implies energy error epsilon |e|^2. It can be
reused without any further coefficient solve or source measurement for those
queries. Its tensor-grid size grows rapidly with the number of control axes.

For one fixed loading, price and explicit feasible polytope, Section 4 has a
polynomial record bound in that description and the accuracy digits. It
constructs the relevant decisions directly. A whole-response representation
is not necessary for every decision task. Changing the loading or objective
can require additional records; the task transcript does not purport to
answer every later question for free.

No lower bound matching the whole-law grid size is proved. In particular,
that grid's dimension dependence is not an all-algorithm obstruction.

6.2 Finite positive interventions need not be infinitesimal
----------------------------------------------------------

Suppose instead 0<=u_j<=R, sum D_j<=A, with a finite rational R>0. Recenter:

  A_c=A+(R/2)sum D_j,
  D'_j=((R+2)/2)D_j,
  u_j=R/2+((R+2)/2)theta_j,
  |theta_j|<=r=R/(R+2).

Then sum D'_j<=A_c. The center has the fixed-unit moment budget
(tau/(1+R/2),M). The preceding constructions apply with their displayed
margin costs. This is a finite control-coordinate change, not a pointwise
material renormalization. The whole-law interpolation degree pays roughly
sqrt(R) times logarithmic factors for large R; the decision procedure pays
the corresponding logarithmic precision and iteration terms. No uniform
limit as a constitutive or control margin disappears is asserted.

6.3 Propagating a genuine uncertain coefficient record
------------------------------------------------------

Suppose a known represented family B_theta and the true family A_theta both
have a common supplied fixed-unit moment budget (tau',M'), and the record
warrants sup_theta ||A_theta-B_theta||_1<=eta_c. The accepted information
theorem gives, for each alpha<tau',

  sup_theta ||(A_theta)_*-(B_theta)_*||
       <=delta_c=C_alpha[log(e+1/eta_c)]^(-alpha).           (6.1)

Running the paid numerical construction on B_theta therefore predicts the
true family with error at most epsilon+delta_c. A design with regret epsilon
for B_theta has regret at most epsilon+2|e|^2 delta_c for the true energy
objective with the same price. This is a robust deduction from a supplied
uniform coefficient uncertainty record. Finitely many successful response
observations do not establish that uniform coefficient condition.

This also preserves the distinction between fidelity digits and succinct
program length. The upper endpoint alpha=tau' is not used or resolved.

6.4 Even all responses to an existing actuator family need not transfer
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The classical conductivity pullback is

  f^*sigma=det(Df) Df^(-1) (sigma composed with f) Df^(-T).  (6.2)

For boundary-fixing finite bi-Lipschitz maps it preserves the full Dirichlet-
to-Neumann operator; for the periodic maps used below it also preserves the
effective tensor. These are the classical gauge identities credited in [5].
No singular ideal cloak is needed.

Use the accepted two-dimensional radial family from the effective-response
information theorem. Its map f_T is identity outside a disk U of radius
r0=R exp(-T0), and is a dilation on the smaller core B_T of radius
R exp(-T). Its pullback A_T=f_T^*I has A_T=I on the core and outside U,
and satisfies the same fixed moment budget (tau,M), uniformly in T.
Let

  eta_T=|B_T|=pi R^2 exp(-2T),
  v_T=|f_T(B_T)|
     =pi R^2 exp(-2T0) T0^tau T^(-tau)
       *exp{tau[(log T+1)/T-(log T0+1)/T0]}.               (6.3)

These accepted formulas and moment bounds are retained without reproof.

Choose any fixed existing positive actuator family D_j supported outside U,
with sum D_j<=I there. Since f_T is identity on that support,

  f_T^*(I+sum theta_j D_j)=A_T+sum theta_j D_j.             (6.4)

Therefore the two candidate media I and A_T have exactly identical full
boundary response operators at every existing control setting, and identical
periodic effective tensors there. This remains true for complete, exact,
infinite records of that family. It is not a finite-sampling limitation.

Now supply a new actuator D_new=I times the indicator of B_T, with amplitude
s>0. In the first medium, its effective tensor is at most
(1+s eta_T)I by the arithmetic trial bound. In the second medium, it is the
pullback of an inclusion of conductivity (1+s)I occupying image volume v_T.
The harmonic bound gives the lower tensor bound

  [1-s v_T/(1+s)]^(-1) I.

The two responses to this same specified new actuator are separated by at
least

  s v_T/(1+s-s v_T)-s eta_T.                              (6.5)

For every fixed s>0 this is positive for all sufficiently large T and has
leading order T^(-tau), whereas eta_T is exponentially small. One can take
0<s<1 to remain within a finite relative actuation range; larger finite
positive amplitudes are handled by recentering as above.

Every algorithm with only the identical existing-family records must issue
the same prediction for the two truths. On one truth its scalar error is at
least half the separation. Randomization cannot remove the corresponding
worst-case expected absolute-error bound. Unlimited internal computation or
perfect fitting of the existing family does not resolve this ambiguity.

More generally, a boundary-fixing gauge that preserves every available
actuator preserves all of that family's acquired boundary data. This is a
sufficient structural source of ambiguity, not a classification of all
possible nonuniqueness. New actuator information or a warranted coefficient
representation can distinguish the systems. The statement is for the
symmetric matrix-coefficient scalar class; it is not an isotropic uniqueness
counterexample.

APPENDIX A. THE RETAINED CONDITIONAL-GRADIENT PROTOCOL
====================================================

The simpler protocol is useful independently of the ellipsoid construction.
It uses the same C and L_f as (4.2), but Cbar=max(C,epsilon),
h=epsilon/(8Cbar), nu=epsilon h/64. At each feasible theta it acquires the
baseline and q feasible secants, so (4.6)-(4.7) still apply. If G_U<=epsilon
it returns that global certificate. Otherwise the vertex with largest
secant has certified directional gain

  d_lower=max_i s_i-2nu/h > 7epsilon/8.

Choose the largest dyadic gamma<=min(1,d_lower/Cbar). The concave-curvature
bound gives improvement at least epsilon^2/(8Cbar) before rounding. Maintain
simplex weights for theta, and round them within the simplex by the same
downward/remainder scheme, using enough bits that the objective loss is at
most epsilon^2/(32Cbar). Every nonterminal rounded update therefore improves
the true objective by at least epsilon^2/(16Cbar).

The entire objective oscillation is at most

  R_f=2rS+max_i ell.v_i-min_i ell.v_i.

There can be at most floor(16Cbar R_f/epsilon^2)+1 such updates. All control
weights have an a priori bounded dyadic precision, rather than denominator
growth proportional to the iteration count. Every step, reading and round
is retained in DESIGN_CERTIFICATE.json. This is the claimed polynomial
1/epsilon bound; the stronger accuracy-digit bound belongs to Section 4.
