1. THE GOVERNING SCIENTIFIC QUESTION AND ITS INPUT CONTRACT

Let the square torus have side ell and n=ell^2 explicitly counted sites.
For spins sigma_i in {-1,+1}, supply rational positive Boltzmann factors

  u=exp(-2 beta J),  v=exp(-2 beta h),  0<u<=1/4096,  u^2<v<1.

The unnormalized equilibrium weight is exactly

  w(sigma)=u^{number of disagreeing nearest-neighbor edges}
           v^{number of minus spins}.

One rate-one proposal clock belongs to EACH site. If S_i is the sum of its
four neighbors, the exact flip rate is

  c_i(sigma)=r_i/(1+r_i),  r_i=u^{sigma_i S_i} v^{sigma_i}.

All rates are rational, and the exponents needed in a local update are
bounded constants. This avoids giving a transcendental-rate oracle an
unpriced role. Write theta=-(log u)/2, phi=-(log v)/2, R=theta/phi=J/h.
The intended growing-nucleus regime has C R<=ell<=R^p for fixed constants
C and p>1. Particular prior results keep their own sufficiently-low-
temperature and sufficiently-large-C conditions. The record does not
extend a theorem at theta>=512 to theta near 4.

The preparation is a=all-minus. The actual target is

  B={sigma: sum_i sigma_i>=0}.

It is tested by a running magnetization count. Its exponentially many
members are not explicitly listed. Hitting a proposed critical droplet is
not a substitute for hitting B.

The requested enabling operation is, at every fixed admissible u, total
bit work polynomial in n, the remaining complete binary input length,
epsilon^{-1}, log(delta^{-1}), and the binary length of a query time, giving
with confidence at least 1-delta:

  (i) a relative-epsilon interval for T=E_a tau_B;
  (ii) an additive-epsilon CDF enclosure at the query time, preferably a
       reusable enclosure valid for every time.

All dependence on u must be explicit and paid. Polynomial work at fixed
temperature is not a claim of uniform polynomial work in the bit length
of an arbitrary temperature parameter. The defining challenge is to avoid
an exp(c R) cost at fixed accuracy as the critical droplet grows. No actual
measurement, equilibrium sampler, committor, contour law, or complex-time
kernel is supplied as an oracle.

The current polynomial-time algorithm for (i) has not been constructed.
This report therefore cannot deliver (ii) for the full two-dimensional

3. A MAXIMAL-START OCCUPATION CRITERION

Consider any finite continuous-time Markov chain, killed on B, with almost
surely finite hitting time. Put D=B^c and

  m(x)=E_x tau_B,  T=m(a)>0.

For this section assume m(x)<=T for every x in D. An attractive spin process
started from its least configuration and an increasing target satisfy a
stronger property: tau_B from a stochastically dominates the hitting time
from every other state. This follows from the ordinary common-update
monotone coupling. It includes the Ising process in Section 1.

Define W=tau_B/T, and let

  d=1-E_a[tau_B^2]/(2 T^2).

THEOREM 1.
For this maximal-mean start, d>=0 and

  d_Wasserstein(Law(W),Exp(1)) <= 2d,
  sup_{t>=0}|P_a(tau_B>tT)-exp(-t)| <= min(1,2 sqrt(d)).       (3.1)

The parameter has the exact physical-occupation identity

  d = (1/T^2) E_a integral_0^{tau_B} [T-m(X_s)] ds.           (3.2)

Thus it is the occupation-weighted loss of remaining mean, relative to T.
This identity includes physical holding times, returns, and postcritical
growth. It does not assume an exponential law.

Equivalently d=(1-Var(tau_B)/T^2)/2. The implication from this scalar
moment defect to a full distributional bound relies on (3.5); matching
two moments of an arbitrary distribution would not justify it.

For any sequence satisfying the maximal-mean premise, convergence of the
normalized hitting law to Exp(1) is equivalent to d->0. Quantitatively, if
the Kolmogorov distance is at most e in (0,1), then

  d <= e[(log(1/e))^2/2 + log(1/e) + 1].                     (3.3)

There are also useful uniform bounds:

  integral_t^infinity P_a(tau_B>s) ds <= T exp(-t/T),
  P_a(tau_B>t) <= min(1, exp(1-t/T)),
  E_a[tau_B^k] <= k! T^k  for positive integers k.            (3.4)

If the start is stochastically maximal, the survival function is additionally
submultiplicative: S(t+s)<=S(t)S(s).

PROOF.
The strong Markov property gives

  E_a[(tau_B-t)_+]
    = E_a[1_{tau_B>t} m(X_t)] <= T P_a(tau_B>t).             (3.5)

For W of mean one, its equilibrium residual-life transform W^e has survival

  P(W^e>t)=integral_t^infinity P(W>s) ds.

Equation (3.5) says W^e is stochastically dominated by W. Couple by quantiles.
Then E|W-W^e|=1-E[W^2]/2=d. The equilibrium-transform exponential Stein
bound gives d_W<=2 E|W-W^e| [1, Theorem 2.1]. For completeness, for a
1-Lipschitz test h use

  f(x)=E h(Z)-E h(x+Z),  Z~Exp(1).

It satisfies f(0)=0, f'-f=h-Eh(Z), and f' is 2-Lipschitz. Integration against
the equilibrium transform gives E f(W)=E f'(W^e), so the error is bounded
by 2 E|W-W^e|. This establishes the Wasserstein assertion.

If a distribution function differs by q from a distribution with density
bounded by one at one point, monotonicity produces a triangular region of
area at least q^2/2 under the absolute difference of the CDFs. The one-
dimensional Wasserstein distance is that integral. Hence q<=sqrt(2 d_W),
which proves (3.1). No smoothness of the hitting-time density is needed.

The standard Markov moment identity is

  E_a[tau_B^2]/2=E_a integral_0^{tau_B} m(X_s) ds.

Subtract it from T^2 to obtain (3.2). For (3.3), write

  d=integral_0^infinity t[exp(-t)-P(W>t)]dt.

On [0,A] the integrand is bounded above by te; on [A,infinity) discard
the nonnegative survival term. Thus d<=e A^2/2+(A+1)exp(-A), and choose
A=log(1/e).

Let R(t)=E[(W-t)_+]. It has R(0)=1 and R'(t)=-P(W>t)<=-R(t), whence
R(t)<=exp(-t). For t>=1, P(W>t)<=R(t-1), proving the tail assertion;
for t<1 the trivial bound applies. Stochastic dominance W^e<=W gives
E W^{k+1}/(k+1)<=E W^k, proving the factorial moment bound. Finally,
stochastic maximality and the Markov property give submultiplicativity.
QED.

Interpretation and limitation.
Theorem 1 is a finite, nonasymptotic criterion. It does not supply m or the
occupation measure as computational inputs. Those quantities in (3.2)
are proof objects until a model-specific construction acquires bounds.
The old claim that rarity by itself means an exponential waiting time is
not used. Section 6 gives a reversible counterexample to that claim.

4. THE EXPLICIT SOFT-SOURCE CERTIFICATE

Now assume reversibility with positive weights w, and stochastic maximality
of the initial state a. Let A be a nonempty core disjoint from B, and let a
rate-kappa clock run while the physical process is in A. Define

  v(x)=P_x(the core clock rings before hitting B),
  Z_A=sum_A w,
  C=kappa sum_A w(1-v),
  N=sum_D wv,
  Q=N/C,
  rho=C/Z_A,
  delta_A=sum_{D outside A} wv/N.

Do not confuse the potential v in this section with the microscopic field
factor v in Section 1; the software calls it vv internally. All sums here
are over physical configurations, and are not free evaluation instructions.

Suppose a valid bound D0 is supplied for

  T-E_{mu_A}m <= D0,  mu_A(x)=w(x)1_A/Z_A.

The accepted affine comparison is

  Q<=T<=(1+kappa D0)Q+D0,                                  (4.1)

for every kappa>0. Also, with N_A=sum_A wv,

  N_A=Z_A-C/kappa.                                         (4.2)

THEOREM 2.
If 0<rho<=U<kappa and delta_A<=eta, define s=U/kappa and

  d_* = min{1, eta+kappa D0
                  + U D0 (2-s)/(1-s)^2}.                  (4.3)

Then the normalized hitting time obeys (3.1) with d replaced by d_*.
If Q in [Q_-,Q_+] is also supplied, set

  T_- = max{Q_-, U^{-1}-kappa^{-1}},
  T_+ = (1+kappa D0)Q_+ + D0.

An additional valid defect upper bound is

  1-Q_-/T_+ + eta + D0/[(1-U/kappa)T_-].                    (4.4)

The minimum of (4.3), (4.4), and one can be used.

PROOF.
Let H=-L_D be the positive killed generator. The potential equation is

  H v=kappa 1_A(1-v).

Define the normalized source distribution

  nu(x)=kappa w(x)1_A(x)(1-v(x))/C.

Reversibility gives its exact Green occupation measure:

  E_nu integral_0^{tau_B} 1_{X_t=x} dt=w(x)v(x)/C.           (4.5)

Consequently E_nu tau_B=Q and

  E_nu tau_B^2=2 sum_D wv m/C.

Stochastic maximality implies E_a tau_B^2>=E_nu tau_B^2.
On A, use m=T-(T-m), 0<=v<=1 and
sum_A w(T-m)<=Z_A D0. Outside A use m>=0. It follows that

  d <= 1-(Q/T)(1-delta_A)+(Z_A/N)(Q/T)(D0/T).               (4.6)

Equation (4.1) implies 1-Q/T<=kappa D0+D0/T. Equation (4.2)
implies Z_A/N<=1/(1-s) and T>=Q>=1/U-1/kappa. Substitution
in (4.6) proves (4.3). Direct use of the interval for T and Q proves
(4.4). The Green identity is an identity, not access to a Green oracle.
QED.

An acquisition-compatible source rate.
Let the desired normalized-CDF error be e in (0,1]. When D0>0, choose

  kappa=e^2/(16 D0).

If the ACTUAL corresponding certificates establish

  U D0<=e^2/128,   delta_A<=e^2/16,

then U/kappa<=1/8 and d_*<=19 e^2/128, so the CDF error in (3.1)
is less than e. Its source waiting scale 1/kappa=16D0/e^2 is polynomial
whenever D0 is polynomially bounded. This avoids introducing an exponentially
slow core clock merely to optimize the algebra. U and delta_A can depend
on kappa and must be proved for the chosen value. If D0=0, (4.3) reduces
to d_*<=eta for any kappa>U.

This result uses N1722's weighted residence in a time-law calculation. A
certificate for a bounded-lobe subfamily contributes only to that part of

5. THE CONSTRUCTED DIGITAL OPERATION

Given valid rational input bounds Q_-,Q_+,D0,U,kappa,eta, residence_law.py
computes T_-,T_+,d_* and an upward rational enclosure Delta of
min(1,2 sqrt(d_*)). At every rational t>=0 it returns

  max{0,1-exp(-t/T_+)-Delta}
    <= P_a(tau_B<=t)
    <= min{1,1-exp(-t/T_-)+Delta}.                          (5.1)

The exponentials in (5.1) are evaluated by an explicit rational interval
algorithm. They are not unevaluated global bounds or dynamics oracles.
The entire record defines a uniform-time enclosure; it does not require
sampling a time grid extending to the rare mean.

If T_+/T_-<=r, the width before numerical rounding is at most

  2 Delta + log(r)/e,

because the largest derivative of exp(-t/T) with respect to log T is 1/e.
One can allocate the requested CDF accuracy between this mean uncertainty,
the shape error, and elementary arithmetic. For p>Delta and p<1-Delta,
the p-quantile is correspondingly enclosed between

  T_-[-log(1-p+Delta)] and T_+[-log(1-p-Delta)].              (5.2)

The program implements (5.1); formula (5.2) is a mathematical consequence,
not a separately executed quantile routine. Additive CDF control does not
claim uniformly relative control of a tiny survival tail or of an
instantaneous hazard rate.

Bit cost.
Let B be the total binary length of the rational certificate and query
time. Let b be the number of requested output precision bits, so the
output-size dependence on b is counted. Square roots are bounded using
an integer square root of a scaled rational numerator. For exp(-x), if
x>=b+1, the returned interval [0,2^{-b}] is valid since e>2. Otherwise
0<=x<b+1. The exact alternating Taylor polynomial and its signed remainder
give an interval of width at most 2^{-b}.

There are O((b+2)^2) Taylor steps using an elementary factorial bound:
for integer k>=8x^2 and k>=b+1, k!>=(k/2)^{k/2} makes
x^k/k!<=2^{-k}. The rational polynomial numerator/denominator lengths are
O(k(B+log k)), with polynomial overhead for the rational operations and
Euclidean reductions. With school arithmetic, the complete processor is
bounded by 2^40(B+b+2)^12 elementary bit operations using school arithmetic
and ordinary Euclidean reductions, a deliberately conservative bound.
Indeed the Taylor degree is at most 16(b+2)^2; each reduced numerator or
denominator and each unreduced arithmetic intermediate has O((B+b+2)^3)
bits. Even a cubic-cost implementation of rational reduction fits the
displayed bound with this slack.
In particular it is polynomial in the BINARY length of t, not t itself.
The code contains no calls to a matrix exponential, committor, sampler,
physical clock, or missing contour construction.

This is a conditional digital operation. Acquiring Q, U, D0 and eta is
additional work. If those records have a joint validity probability
1-delta, the entire reusable enclosure has that same probability; no
union bound over all query times is needed once the record is valid.

6. WHY A RARE CAPACITY AND A GOOD CORE ARE INSUFFICIENT

For rational e>0, consider the reversible birth-death chain on 0,1,2 with

  q01=e, q10=e^2, q12=e, q21=e^2,
  w=(1,e^{-1},e^{-2}).

Start at 0, take A={0}, B={2}. The process is ordered and 0 is the
stochastically maximal hitting-time start. The core deficit is exactly zero.
Direct solution of its two transient equations gives

  T=(2+e)/e,
  d=1/(2+e)^2,
  delta_A=1/(2+e),
  C_kappa=(kappa^{-1}+e^{-1}+1)^{-1}.

As e decreases to zero, capacity vanishes and the mean diverges, but
tau_B/T converges to a shape-two Erlang variable divided by two. At scaled
time, this follows directly by multiplying all transient rates by 1/e:
the backward rate tends to zero and the two forward rates tend to one.
The mean of this rescaled time tends to two. At normalized
time 1/2 its limiting survival is 2 exp(-1), whereas the exponential survival
is exp(-1/2). The gap is about 0.1292.

The two rare waiting stages survive in the normalized law. Perfect source
equilibration alone cannot remove the second stage. This family is a
reversible-chain obstruction, not an Ising embedding and not an assertion
that the two-dimensional Ising model has this law.

7. A COMPLETE ALL-RADIUS FAILURE OBJECT FOR LOCAL STATE COMPRESSION

One candidate central operation would close the kinetic equations on a
histogram of all spin neighborhoods up to some radius. That exact closure
fails even before rare-event approximation is considered.

THEOREM 3.
For every integer r>=1, and every integer repetition count j>=1, put

  m=2r+1,  ell=10mj.

On the ell-by-ell torus define two configurations constant along each
vertical column, with cyclic column words

  x: (+^m -^{3m} +^{3m} -^{3m}) repeated j times,
  y: (+^{2m} -^{3m} +^{2m} -^{3m}) repeated j times.

Their complete histograms of radius-r square spin patterns are identical.
Both have plus density 2/5, so they are outside the actual target M>=0.
Nevertheless those histograms do not have the same infinitesimal transition
law under the exact heat-bath process, at any finite positive u,v.

PROOF.
Every constant-sign run has length at least m. A word of m consecutive
columns therefore contains at most one sign boundary. Counts of the
nonconstant m-words depend only on the number and orientation of boundaries,
which agree. The all-plus m-word count is j(2m+2) in both configurations;
the all-minus count also agrees. Repeating the same word vertically proves
equality of every two-dimensional square-neighborhood count. Smaller
neighborhood statistics are marginals and agree as well.

Let F be the number of all-plus m-by-m windows. A plus-to-minus spin flip
destroys m times the number of all-plus horizontal m-windows containing
its column. In a plus strip of width m, that latter number equals one at
every column. In a strip of width at least 2m, it equals one only at the
two boundary columns.

The two configurations have the same number of plus boundary columns,
with three plus neighbors and one minus neighbor at each site. Their rates
cancel in the comparison. In x there are an additional (m-2)j interior
columns in the narrow strips whose flips decrease F by exactly m. Every
site there has four plus neighbors. With

  q4=u^4 v/(1+u^4 v),

the difference between the total physical rates for the event
Delta F=-m is exactly

  (m-2)j ell q4 >0.                                       (7.1)

Since the common local-pattern record includes F, equality of rates into
each compressed state would imply equality of the rates into the union
having F decreased by m. Equation (7.1) contradicts that requirement.
This proves failure of strong Markov lumpability.

There is also a finite-time separation. Let

  d_r=(m-2)j q4/ell=(m-2)q4/(10m),
  t_r=d_r/(2 ell^2).

Use the common rate-n proposal-clock representation, n=ell^2. The
probability of exactly one proposal is a exp(-a), a=n t_r=d_r/2.
On that event the probability difference for F_t=F_0-m is
exp(-a)t_r times the rate difference in (7.1). The entire possible
contribution from two or more proposals has absolute value at most a^2/2.
Since d_r<=1/10,

  TV(Law_x(F_{t_r}),Law_y(F_{t_r}))
     >= (d_r^2/2)exp(-d_r/2)-d_r^2/8
     >= d_r^2/4 >= q4^2/3600.                             (7.2)

Any predictor given only the common initial histogram must therefore have
worst-case TV error at least q4^2/7200 on this pair. QED.

The separation is independent of the repetition count, so a fixed radius
fails on arbitrarily large tori. At very low temperature the displayed
constant is correspondingly small; it is not a practical error estimate.

Scope.
This refutes an exact local-histogram Markov representation and supplies
explicit falsifying objects. It does not refute an approximation with
other observables, global geometry, memory, or different data. It does
not prove a lower bound for the all-minus first-passage computation.
These stripe configurations have not been shown to carry significant
critical current. Nor has weak lumpability under that one specific initial
law been disproved. All such stronger statements are excluded.

8. HIGH ACCURACY DOES NOT REQUIRE AN EXTRA MYSTERY ORACLE

This section records a conditional cost closure useful once the actual
kinetic inputs have been earned. It is a theorem about stated resources,
not a currently available two-dimensional algorithm.

Suppose a family of rational, locally evaluable chains has total rate at
most n, a stochastically maximal initial state, T>=1/n, and an explicit
upper bound

  T<=U_T<=A(n,R) exp(aR),

where A is polynomial and a is a fixed positive constant. Suppose further
that a polynomial-work acquisition constructs the mean and a VALID rational
full shape certificate d<=D_R<=A_1(n,R) exp(-bR), with fixed b>0 and
polynomial A_1.
All temperature dependencies of A,A_1,a,b must be included.

If 4D_R<=e^2, use Section 5 with shape tolerance e. This is an exact rational
comparison. If it does not hold, then

  exp(aR) < (4 A_1/e^2)^{a/b}.                             (8.1)

In that branch the allowed precision cost makes U_T itself polynomial
in n,R,e^{-1}. A deliberately slow direct finite simulation can then be
made a fully bounded-bit, high-confidence mean/CDF computation. Thus a
uniform all-accuracy closure need not assume that the exponential law is
exact at very small requested errors.

Here is a concrete bounded simulation construction for that branch.
Set

  H=U_T[1+ceil(log_2(64 n U_T/e))],
  S=ceil(256(nH+1)^3/e),  h=H/S.

Use the absorbing-target discrete kernel P_h=I+hL, with at most S updates
per sample. The finite difference in question is a stochastic kernel since
nh<=1. It is not silently substituted for the physical process: uniformization
couples it to the physical chain on [0,H] with error at most

  S(nh)^2=n^2 H^2/S.

The time-grid rounding adds at most nh to a CDF error and at most h to a
mean error. Formula (3.4) bounds the physical truncated-mean tail by
U_T exp(-H/U_T)<=e/(64n). Coupling and rounding bound the remaining mean
bias by e/(128n). The same calculation bounds the uniform CDF bias,
including the censoring at H, by 3e/128.

Take

  M=ceil(2048(nH+1)^2 e^{-2} log(8(S+1)/delta)).

Hoeffding's bounded-variable inequality and a union bound over the S+1
grid points give, with the allocated confidence, sample-mean error at
most e/(64n) and uniform empirical-CDF error at most e/16. The mean
interval with radius e/(8n) therefore contains T and has endpoint ratio
at most 1+e for 0<e<=1. A CDF band of radius e/8 contains the physical
CDF. All numerical constants here leave slack.

For finite-bit randomness, approximate each step's n+1 cumulative outcome
probabilities by dyadics with p bits, with

  p>=ceil(log_2(8MS(n+1)/delta)).

Use p independent fair bits per step. The joint law of every simulated
trajectory differs from its ideal rational-kernel law by at most delta/8.
All rates, thresholds and times have polynomial-size rational descriptions;
the number of steps is deterministically bounded by MS. One conservative
work expression is

  O(M S n poly(L+bits(H)+log S+log M+log(delta^{-1}))).

This can be a high polynomial in U_T, and is explicitly paid. In the
branch (8.1), it is nevertheless polynomial in the required precision
and explicit input size, with exponents depending on a/b. If (8.1) is
not available, this argument supplies no polynomial rare-event speedup.
This auxiliary simulation algorithm is specified and proved here; it was
not implemented or run in this return.

The essential unearned premise is still a valid full shape/residence
construction and mean acquisition for the actual two-dimensional system.
A lobe-only result does not establish the exponential full shape bound.

10. WHAT REMAINS CENTRAL

The full endpoint requires an actual representation and acquisition of the
critical kinetic law, including sufficient control of physical residence
outside the thermal core and through growth to the magnetization target.
For the presently considered contour-current route this means covering
significant unrestricted principal contours, rather than only the acquired
regular loop family or bounded-lobe sectors, and constructing their
normalization/transport with a polynomial relative cost.

Every-path voltage bounds are one sufficient possible method, not a
necessary condition for a solution. The new local-pattern obstruction
likewise does not make every meaningful representation impossible. A
different representation may still provide the central operation.

This return has not proved full critical-current coverage, a polynomial
capacity estimator for that ensemble, or a global exterior-residence
