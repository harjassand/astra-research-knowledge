1. FINITE-BLOCK OPTIMIZATION IS COMPLETELY EFFECTIVE FOR RATIONAL CHOI INPUTS

Let a=dim A, b=dim B, and let J be the promised normalized rational-complex
Choi matrix. Put

  A_n=a^n, B_n=b^n,
  c_n(N)=(1/n) max_rho I_c(rho,N^{tensor n}),
  F_m(N)=max_{1<=n<=m} c_n(N).

Then 0<=F_m<=log_2 min(a,b), F_m is increasing, and sup_m F_m=Q.
The lower bound follows by using a pure input, whose coherent information is
zero. The upper bound follows from -H(R|B)<=min{S(R),S(B)}.

To compute c_n no complement need be supplied or acquired. For a pure state
psi on R A^n, with dim R=A_n, compute

  omega_RB=(id_R tensor N^{tensor n})(|psi><psi|).

Purity of the full Stinespring output gives

  I_c(Tr_R |psi><psi|,N^{tensor n})=S(omega_B)-S(omega_RB).

R here is a mathematical purifying reference used to evaluate an entropy. It
is not transmitted as an extra operational resource.

A rational vector v!=0 gives the EXACT rational projector

  P_v = v v^*/(v^*v).

Thus rational-complex input/output matrices suffice throughout. An irrational
normalization square root does not appear in the projector. Such projectors
are dense among all pure states on R A^n and cover all needed input reductions.

1.1 A finite covering grid and its actual cost

For requested error e>0 choose

  C=a+2b+1,
  s=max{2,ceil(32 C/e)}, delta=1/s^2,
  M=2 A_n s^2.

Enumerate the nonzero Gaussian-integer vectors v of length A_n^2 whose real
and imaginary coordinates lie in [-M,M]. Dividing v by M before normalization
does not change its projector. Round any unit vector psi coordinatewise to
this grid. The Euclidean rounding error is <=sqrt(2 A_n^2)/(2M). Normalizing
the rounded vector changes it by at most that error again, so the trace
distance between the corresponding pure projectors is <=delta.

Both channels to B^n and R B^n are trace-norm contractions. The entropy
continuity bound gives a normalized objective difference at most

  delta(log_2 a+2 log_2 b)+2 h_2(delta)/n
  <= delta(a+2b)+8/(s n).

The last inequality uses the deliberately loose elementary bound
h_2(x)<=4 sqrt(x) for x<=1/2. Indeed -x ln x<=sqrt(x),
-(1-x)ln(1-x)<=x<=sqrt(x), and ln 2>=1/2.

Compute the coherent information at every grid point in an interval of width
ne/4. The maximum of the lower endpoints, divided by n, is a lower bound;
the maximum upper endpoint divided by n plus the covering error is an upper
bound. Their width is at most

  e/4+(a+2b)/s^2+8/(s n)<e.

The full number of grid vectors is

  U_n(e)=(2M+1)^{2 A_n^2}-1.

The algorithm is total at every fixed n. It is not efficient: even n=1 for a
qubit at useful precision makes this literal universal grid impractical.
The code deliberately refuses when an optional operational point guard is
exceeded; that refusal is never relabelled as a completed optimization.

1.2 Certified entropy without an eigenvalue oracle

For 0<=x<=1,

  -x ln x = sum_{k>=1} x(1-x)^k/k.

After L terms the remainder is nonnegative and at most 1/(L+1), since

  x sum_{k>L}(1-x)^k/k
  <= x(1-x)^{L+1}/((L+1)x)
  <=1/(L+1),

with x=0 treated by continuity. Hence for a D-dimensional density matrix rho,

  T_L=sum_{k=1}^L Tr[rho(I-rho)^k]/k

is rational and satisfies

  T_L/ln2 <= S(rho) <= [T_L+D/(L+1)]/ln2.

Certified rational bounds on ln2 come from

  ln2=2 sum_{j>=0} [1/((2j+1)3^{2j+1})],

whose tail after m terms is at most

  9/[4(2m+1)3^{2m+1}].

For an entropy interval of width w, take

  L=max{1,ceil(4D/w)-1}

and ln2-interval width at most w/(8D). Because T_L<=ln D<=D and
ln2>=1/2, the resulting interval has width <=w. No lower spectral gap of rho
is assumed. Singular states and exact zeros are allowed.

For a fixed pure input sample the largest entropy matrix has dimension
D=(ab)^n. The polynomial entropy calculation uses O(L D^3) exact Gaussian-
rational arithmetic operations in the naive implementation. The output-state
construction uses O(A_n^4 B_n^2) such operations. Memory is
O(A_n^4+(A_n B_n)^2). The general net multiplies the per-sample work by U_n(e).
For entropy width proportional to ne, L=O((ab)^n/(ne)). These counts do not
claim unit-cost rational arithmetic: with a common denominator of input bit
length p_0, powers through degree L require O(L(p_0+log D)+L log L) bit-length
bounds for numerators/denominators, before ordinary exact arithmetic costs.
The channel tensor entries have bit lengths linear in n times an input common
denominator bit length; the literal matrix itself has (ab)^{2n} entries.

The implementation is finite_block.py. It supports Gaussian rational channel
entries, correctly groups A^n B^n Choi indices, and computes all intervals
with Fraction arithmetic. The proof of the grid covering is essential; a
selected finite fixture is not evidence that the unrestricted maximum was run.

2. A SELF-CONTAINED UNIFORM MODULUS OF CAPACITY CONTINUITY

Let t=(1/2)||N-M||_diamond<=1/2. For any two states at trace distance t,

  |H(X|Y)_rho-H(X|Y)_sigma|
  <=2t log_2 dim X+g(t),

  g(t)=(1+t) h_2(t/(1+t)).

For completeness, write rho-sigma=t(tau_+-tau_-) using its positive and
negative parts. The common state

  omega=(rho+t tau_-)/(1+t)=(sigma+t tau_+)/(1+t)

has two binary convex decompositions. Conditional entropy of a mixture is
between the average conditional entropy and that average plus the binary
mixing entropy. This follows from 0<=I(X;label|Y)<=H(label). Subtract the two
decompositions and use -log dim X<=H(X|Y)<=log dim X. The displayed bound
follows. It is independent of dim Y.

Now fix psi_RA^n and replace the n uses of N by M one at a time. Successive
output states differ in trace distance by at most t. Their marginals on all
systems except the replaced B_i coincide, because both maps are trace
preserving. Consequently both S(B^n) and S(RB^n) change by a conditional
entropy involving only the small system B_i. Summing the n hybrid steps gives

  |I_c(psi,N^n)-I_c(psi,M^n)|/n
  <= mu_b(t):=4t log_2 b+2g(t).

Taking the maximum over psi, then the maximum over n<=m, or the supremum
over every n, preserves the same modulus. Thus every c_n, every F_m, and Q
share the explicit modulus mu_b, independent of n and all purifying reference
dimensions. For a wholly rational sufficient estimate one can use

  mu_b(t)<=4bt+10 sqrt(t)  (0<=t<=1/2).

This proof uses no strong converse and no assisted capacity theorem.

3. A FINITE RATIONAL CHANNEL NET WITH ALL VALIDITY COSTS EXPLICIT

Let D=ab and choose rational 0<d<=1. Put

  tau=d/8, H=ceil(32 a D^2/d).

Enumerate every Hermitian matrix K whose D^2 independent real coordinates
are multiples of 1/H in [-1,1]. Project it onto the TP affine slice by

  P(K)=K-[Tr_B K-I_A/a] tensor I_B/b.

Retain exactly those P(K) that are positive semidefinite. Every retained point
is an exactly valid normalized rational-complex Choi matrix. There are at most

  (2H+1)^{D^2}

candidates. Positivity is decidable by exact rational Schur complements,
including singular cases, without an approximate eigensolver. The code
rational_channel_net implements this enumeration and affine projection.

Why this covers boundary channels: take any normalized Choi J and move to

  J_tau=(1-tau)J+tau I_D/D.

Its minimum eigenvalue is at least tau/D. Coordinate rounding gives a grid K
with ||K-J_tau||_infinity<=D/H. For Hermitian E, the affine linear correction
satisfies

  ||E-(Tr_B E) tensor I_B/b||_infinity<=2||E||_infinity.

Hence P(K) is within 2D/H of J_tau and remains positive, because
2D/H<=tau/(2D). Moreover normalized-Choi diamond bounds give

  ||N_{P(K)}-N_{J_tau}||_diamond
  <=a ||P(K)-J_tau||_1<=2aD^2/H<=d/16.

The initial depolarizing mixture changes the channel by at most 2tau=d/4 in
diamond norm. Thus the retained candidate is within 5d/16<d in full diamond
norm. This is a constructive valid-channel net, not a promise that arbitrary
entry rounding preserves positivity or trace preservation. Each entry of a net
channel has bit length O(log H+log(ab)); no unknown witness dimension is used.

4. EXACT EQUIVALENCE TO A DIMENSION-AND-PRECISION BLOCK CUTOFF

The following statements are equivalent.

(A) A total algorithm approximates Q of every promised rational-complex Choi
    channel to fixed additive error 1/4.

(B) A total algorithm approximates Q of every such channel to any requested
    positive rational error.

(C) There is a total computable integer B(a,b,e) such that, for every channel
    of dimensions a,b,

      0<=Q(N)-F_{B(a,b,e)}(N)<=e.

(D) There is such a bound allowed to depend additionally on the rational
    channel description itself.

Proof A=>B: for k=ceil(1/(4e)), form N^{tensor k}, preserving the rational
normalized Choi convention. Apply A and divide the answer by k. The identity
Q(N^{tensor k})=kQ(N) follows from the regularized definition and Fekete's
lemma: the subsequence of block lengths divisible by k has the same limiting
normalized coherent information. This uses no additivity assertion for two
different channels. The amplification requires input/output dimensions a^k,b^k
and (ab)^{2k} explicit Choi entries, with entry bit lengths O(k) times a common
input denominator bit length. This cost cannot be charged as one succinct
oracle query on the original input.

Proof B=>C: it is enough to treat 0<e<=1. Choose a computable rational d>0
so mu_b(d)<=e/8; for example d=1/s^2 with
s=ceil(256(b+1)/e). Construct the finite rational channel net in Section 3.
The net is even closer than required for the half-diamond convention.

At every net channel M_i, use B to obtain q_i with
|q_i-Q(M_i)|<=e/8. Compute increasingly accurate certified lower bounds
l_{i,m} to F_m(M_i), with error tending to zero. Stop the i-th search when

  l_{i,m}>q_i-e/4.

It terminates, since q_i-e/4<=Q(M_i)-e/8 and F_m increases to Q(M_i).
Let B be the maximum of the finitely many stopping block lengths. At each
net point,

  Q(M_i)-F_B(M_i)<3e/8.

For any N, choose its nearby M_i. The common continuity modulus gives

  Q(N)-F_B(N)
  <=[Q(M_i)-F_B(M_i)]+2 mu_b(d)
  <3e/8+e/4<e.

This constructs the dimension-only cutoff from the assumed evaluator,
including acquisition of every net point and every finite optimization.

C=>D is immediate. D=>B follows by computing finitely many c_n up to the
promised block cutoff, each to adequate certified accuracy. Thus all four
statements are equivalent.

There is also a useful nonconstructive conclusion. At every FIXED a,b,e a
finite sufficient B(a,b,e) EXISTS. The compact channel space and the common
continuity modulus imply this directly: for every N choose a block with gap
<e/3, use continuity to obtain a neighborhood with gap <e, take a finite
subcover, and maximize the finitely many block lengths. Equivalently, apply
Dini's theorem to the increasing continuous F_m with continuous limit Q.

No EFFECTIVE rule for choosing that finite subcover was obtained. In
particular, this existence argument does not implement B. For any fixed pair
of dimensions and any one fixed accuracy, a finite cutoff can be hard-coded
nonuniformly; a single algorithm across variable dimensions requires a
computable sequence of those choices. This distinction is essential here.

5. WHAT I TRIED TO TURN INTO THE MISSING OPERATION, AND WHY IT STOPPED

5.1 Plateau detection plus compactness

Candidate: stop when F_m barely changes, perhaps at every point of a fine
channel net, using the explicit common continuity modulus above.

Failure: no observed finite plateau certifies that later increases are small.
Even a known common modulus and an effectively compact domain do not repair
this. Consider an enumeration of machines e=1,2,... and disjoint triangular
tents centered at x_e=2^{-e}, supported in intervals of radius
r_e=2^{-e-2}, of height r_e. Define f_s to include tent e only when e<=s and
machine e has halted by stage s. Then f_s is a computable, monotone sequence
of continuous piecewise-linear functions on [0,1]. Each f_s and its limit f
are 1-Lipschitz (supports are disjoint and every slope has magnitude <=1).
Nevertheless f(x_e) is either zero or r_e according to halting. A uniform
evaluator at precision r_e/4 would decide halting. The limit therefore has
no total arbitrary-precision evaluator, despite the explicit common modulus
and compactness. This is an abstract computability countermodel, not a
construction of channels having those capacities.

The contrary implication also fails: RE-hard positivity, by itself, does
not preclude approximation. Define v_e=2^{-T_e} if machine e halts at time
T_e, and zero otherwise. To approximate within e_tol, simulate through a
computable time T with 2^{-T}<=e_tol; output the exact value if halting has
occurred and otherwise output zero. Positivity still encodes halting. The
comparison directly exposes the missing computable positive-rate margin in
a sign-only ordinary-capacity reduction.

5.2 Generalized relative-entropy approximation

I examined the possibility of using the Fang-Fawzi-Fawzi finite-dimensional
regularized-divergence approximation theorems for an upper certificate. One
can write the block objective as

  max_{omega in A_n} min_{sigma in output(N^n)}
       D(omega || I_R tensor sigma),

where A_n is the image of id_R tensor N^n on inputs of reference dimension
a^n. The inner minimum is attained at omega_B and equals the coherent
information. This formula is correct but retains a MAXIMUM over the first
image set. The cited convex-set divergence theorem controls an INFIMUM over
both sets, subject to its tensor-polar assumptions. Replacing the outer max
by that infimum changes the answer even for an identity channel (the allowed
product-reference output gives zero, while Q(id_a)=log a).

Likewise, unrestricted stabilization of the channel-divergence pair is not
legitimate: an additional untouched reference can purify the formal R
independently of A, attaining log a regardless of N. For a channel that
replaces every input by a fixed pure state, the ordinary capacity is zero
while that independently purified-reference construction still gives log a.
Thus neither substitution is an ordinary upper approximation hierarchy.
I found no valid reformulation that removes this max/min or stabilization
obstruction. This is not a no-go theorem for every possible divergence route.

5.3 Strong converse and coding fidelity

The original Beigi-Tomamichel 2609.11771v1 supplies an exponential strong
converse above the regularized capacity. Its proof selects a rate strictly
between Q and the proposed communication rate before applying a weak
converse. A finite bad code or finite failed optimization is not thereby an
upper certificate for Q: an eventual good code may occur later. Even an
explicit decay exponent conditional on a rate gap does not supply the block
length at which near-optimal achievability must appear. I did not obtain a
computable upper cutoff from that source.

5.4 Ordinary resource transfer from the accepted complete-order defect

The actual N675 defect ranges over arbitrary finite untouched references
and arbitrary state pairs, without equal-reference-marginal restriction. Its
quantitative gap is accepted. N1677 supplies an assisted operational margin;
N1680 pays known finite factors and exposes a signed entropy-rate history
correction, without a proved lower bound sufficient for an ordinary margin.

I considered using a fixed-dimensional symmetric channel repeatedly to
transport the unknown reference, quantum expanders to turn it into a boundary
memory, and finite catalysts to remove startup. Transporting a D-dimensional
reference takes a number of ordinary calls growing with log D in the simple
block realization; dividing by that unknown number loses the computable
per-use gap. A mixer's contraction of its retained memory is not recovery of
the reference at Bob; coherent mixer labels themselves are distributed to
receiver and environment. A stationary marginal at Bob is not return of the
joint resource. The predecessor's pure-catalyst removal theorem already rules
out a free pure-entanglement workaround.

A more ambitious weak-coupling matrix-product input idea would make history
corrections higher order if the unperturbed emitted state were independent of
memory. But the noncommuting baseline that carries the accepted reference
witness does not have that property. Dropping its correlations makes the
second-order local signal a computable finite-input quantity and discards the
hard source. I did not establish the needed signed entropy-rate bound, or
another dimension-independent ordinary conversion. These failures apply to
the constructions considered, not to all possible reductions.
