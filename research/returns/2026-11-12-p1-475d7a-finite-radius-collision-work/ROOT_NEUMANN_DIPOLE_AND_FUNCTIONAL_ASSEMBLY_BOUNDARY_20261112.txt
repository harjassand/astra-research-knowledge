normalized near-contact cone angle and a nearby tightened-configuration

2. An exact two-body Neumann cancellation from a touching start

Consider a relative Brownian motion outside a radius R ball in R3, with
constant isotropic covariance and inward-to-the-allowed-domain normal
reflection on |x|=R. For a fixed vector g set
 u(x)=g.x,
 v_R(x)=(R^3/2)*(g.x)/|x|^3,
 F_R=u+v_R.
Both u and v_R are harmonic outside the origin. Writing x=r n,
 partial_r u=g.n,
 partial_r v_R=-(R^3/r^3)g.n.
Thus partial_r F_R=0 at r=R. Ito's formula has NO contact local-time
term for F_R, including an initial touching configuration.

Moreover |v_R(x)|<=R|g|/2 throughout the allowed domain. Localization
and integrability of the bounded-gradient martingale give
 |E[g.(X_t-X_0)]|=|E[v_R(X_0)-v_R(X_t)]|<=R|g|
for every t>=0. This is an exact signed-displacement bound, not a
bound on unsigned local time or TV of full position paths.

Its correction energy is volume order:
 integral_(|x|>R)|grad v_R|^2 dx=(2pi/3)R^3 |g|^2.
Integration by parts gives this directly from the inner boundary:
v_R=(R/2)g.n and the outward derivative for the exterior domain is
-partial_r v_R=g.n; integrate (R/2)(g.n)^2 over the sphere.
The outer boundary contribution vanishes.

Thus a reflecting obstacle has a dipole-type cancellation for a smooth
linear test. Treating it as an absorbing obstacle and charging only
capacity O(R) discards that cancellation. This suggests a different
representation of the signed boundary error in the backward proposal
observable; it is not a universal hard-ball mixing theorem.

3. Explicit limits of extending that cancellation

For a general smooth backward test one can subtract its local linear
part and cut off the dipole outside a fixed radius r0. The endpoint
amplitude is O(R ||grad u||); cutoff generator defects away from the
hole have O(R^3) coefficients with paid powers of r0^-1. Second-order
Taylor remainders require their actual Hessian bounds. No error term is
declared small merely because total local time was bounded.

For unequal constant anisotropic relative covariance, a linear change
of coordinates turns the exclusion sphere into an ellipsoid and the
boundary condition into the corresponding conormal Neumann condition.
That is an analytical cell problem, not a compiler PDE oracle. A
polynomially conditioned ellipsoidal corrector would still need its
estimate. Independently varying species matrices, container walls,
variable coefficients, chemical dimension changes and correlated
predictable covariance errors are not supplied by the explicit sphere
calculation.

Most importantly, summing independent pair dipoles leaves cross-contact
normal derivatives when three or more particles touch. Such a cluster
can be dense locally even if the TOTAL excluded volume is tiny. The
returned cone/core estimates pay its geometry and local clocks, but do
not make the pair-corrector interaction operator contractive. A coupled
cluster corrector, a multiscale resummation, direct observable averaging
or a true global form bound remains a new operation. Arbitrary repeated
mergers/splits cannot be counted by n-1.

The exact two-body calculation therefore supplies a concrete option
for the full same-endpoint question while preserving complete method
freedom. It does not prescribe a Poincare proof as the only admissible
route or make a claimed missing theorem available by naming it.

4. What fixed local accuracy can and cannot imply for assembly

There is a useful exact warning against equating perfect microscopic
yield with scalable function. For n independent two-level defect sites
at inverse temperature beta and defect energy Delta, let
 p=1/(1+exp(beta Delta)).
The probability of NO defect is (1-p)^n. Requiring it >=1-epsilon gives
 beta Delta >= -log[(1-epsilon)^(-1/n)-1],
which grows as log(n/epsilon). The same identity holds for independent
two-state birth/repair sites with p=birth/(birth+repair). Fixed per-site
parameters do not give fixed perfect-yield probability as n grows.

This is an EXACT independent-site counterfixture, not a lower bound for
all interacting materials or an impossibility of functional assembly.
A majority block with independent error p<1/2 has error at most
exp[-2m(1/2-p)^2] by the elementary exponential-moment bound, so
O(log(n/epsilon)) redundant sites per logical component suffice at this
abstract independent scope. Spatially correlated defects, spontaneous
nucleation, bond misidentification, physical connectivity and actual
mechanical/chemical function are not paid by that majority bound.

A stronger physical programme should therefore specify the function
that tolerates residual microscopic defects and construct local error
repair, rather than require zero defects for free or merely reduce
particle size. Useful-density autonomous assembly would need actual
bond/orientation states, local recognition, fuel/heat accounting and
control of nucleation/growth. The present compiler describes particle
counts, steric exclusion and local reaction marks; it does not by itself
encode a spatial bond graph, a neighborhood-recognition device or an
assembly objective.
