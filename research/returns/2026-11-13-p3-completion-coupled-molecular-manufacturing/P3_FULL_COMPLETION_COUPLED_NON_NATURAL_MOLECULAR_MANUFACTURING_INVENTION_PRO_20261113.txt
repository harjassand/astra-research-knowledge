Can an autonomous molecular manufacturing system make useful, sequence-defined non-natural oligomers by advancing only after the intended covalent extension has actually occurred, while rejecting or repairing failed extensions and renewing its machinery for another production cycle?

The physical capability sought is programmable construction of chemically diverse matter with local control over the order of bond formation. Software may prepare a program, operate common utilities and analyse products; it must not supply an unconstructed molecular detector, perform each selective coupling off-stage, or stand in for the physical construction. The aim is a new manufacturing operation that could eventually support materials we cannot conveniently make today. Arbitrary chemical universality, a DNA-routing demonstration, a program barcode, or a simulation with an assigned successful-reaction flag would not establish this operation.

Choose an actual, benign non-biological oligomer family and a plausible implementation. Use at least four distinguishable non-natural building blocks, with at least two chemical functionalities beyond merely different DNA letters. The final useful product should be an abiotic chain or material precursor rather than a protein, a translated biological product or DNA serving only as stored digital information. DNA, enzymes or other molecular components may serve as construction tools if their real roles, compatibility and replacement costs are justified. You may choose a completely different physical organisation from the suggestions below.

The first complete production challenge is a library of 16 specified products, each requiring at least 20 ordered covalent growth operations from its initiator. Include adjacent repeats, alternating segments, long runs and pairs with identical overall composition but different order. All 16 programs must run together in one shared reaction environment, possibly with reusable local compartments or supports whose fabrication, occupation and transport are included. A globally timed feed schedule is allowed. A separate robot-addressed reaction sequence or independently selected monomer feed for each program is not a supplied operation; if you introduce it, construct and pay for it and compare the resulting capability with ordinary parallel synthesis.

Starting from prepared but explicitly costed stock, seek at least 1 nmol of independently authenticated correct released product per program within 24 hours, at least 90% correct full sequences in each accepted product population before any sequence-selective salvage, and at least 50% initiator-to-correct-released-product molar yield for every program. State how sequence errors are distinguished from incomplete chains, isomers, wrong linkages and molecules with the right mass but wrong order. If your chemistry makes one of these error classes physically impossible, justify that claim. Separate actual isolated yield, measurement response and predicted molecular populations. Analyse the complete mixed library, including minority sequences; an average dominated by the easiest sequence is insufficient.

The decisive operation is coupling chemical completion to progression and usable output. A successful reagent encounter, spent activating group, opened hairpin, accumulated charge or elapsed dwell time is not automatically evidence that the intended product bond formed. Show the physical chain of causation from that bond to the next permitted construction state. Include competing hydrolysis, off-pathway attachment, missed extension, false progression, product inhibition, off-target program exchange, unavailable building block, permanently stalled sites and accumulated damaged machinery. An intentional capping or rejection pathway may be useful, but discarding every failed chain is not the same operation as retrying a recoverable extension. Quantify which errors are repaired, which are rejected and which remain hidden.

Use finite, physically implementable observations and control where the design needs them. No controller may see a hidden chemical-state label. Independent destructive structural analysis may establish what was made, but an assumed infinitely fast sequence assay cannot silently operate the construction cycle. A reported program sequence must be related to the actual released chemical sequence through a justified mechanism and measured or independently testable errors. State the observability limitations when two different chemical products produce the same available signal.

As a starting diagnostic, suppose a weak temporary bridge unbinds at rate a and rebinds at rate b. A single endpoint retention observation has probability b/(a+b) + a/(a+b) exp(-(a+b)t), so waiting longer can leave a false-retention floor. Finite sampling or physically separating an unbound intermediate can change that result, but sampling bandwidth, separation geometry, force, clearance, fuel and loss of good products then become real operations. In an introduced independent-hazard screen, a=1/s, b=100/s and a good-product loss rate of 0.00001/s require about 76.6 s to reduce bad retention to 0.001 when an absorbing separation rate of 10/s is available; with separation 1/s the required time is about 704.5 s and good-product survival is only about 0.993. These are hypothetical rates, not material data. A wrong strong covalent bridge can pass the same retention test as the desired one. A mechanical or chemical checkpoint therefore needs both actual chemical selectivity and a physically achieved signal; force discrimination alone is not sequence authentication. You are free to discard this representation.

A second optional opening is to make the newly formed product itself complete a temporary communication or force-bearing path, then allow progression only after the redundant temporary paths have been removed. This might connect a local reaction to the next selection without a separate global assay. It is only a hypothesis: hydrolysis can release the same leaving group as successful coupling, noncovalent bridges can rebind, a wrong covalent product may look strong, and dismantling the checkpoint may consume the program or damage the useful chain. Construct the actual chemical and geometrical cycle or replace it with a better idea. An ideal labelled reaction network without compatible molecular parts and an independently checkable route through its intermediates is not the physical construction.

Product release and renewal are part of the same question. Design ten successive complete production cycles on the same constructed platform, with retargeting across at least four different 16-product libraries. The last cycle should retain at least 90% of the first cycle's accepted output rate and sequence fidelity. Identify every structural, catalytic, recognition and program-bearing stock, its chemical state after use and its restoration or replacement operation. At least 95% of each claimed reusable critical stock must remain functional after ten cycles. Explicitly consumable program strands, adaptors, protecting groups, substrates, activators or chemical fuels are permitted, but their complete preparation and replacement belong in the denominator; calling freshly supplied machinery a reset does not make it renewable. Include released-product separation, waste clearance, contamination carryover, rejected chains and return of the machine to an actually specified ready state. Investigate the limiting operations at 100 cycles and 100 growth steps without assuming the short-chain result transfers.

Connect the library to one useful physical materials operation, chosen by you. Examples could include selective non-biological molecular recognition, an optical response, charge transport, controlled assembly or a material interface, but these are suggestions rather than a required field. At least one same-composition pair should produce a predicted or source-supported, independently measurable difference of at least twofold in the chosen physical response under the same useful conditions. Explain why that difference matters and compare with a competent ordinary material. A merely different mass spectrum or an assigned digital message is not the physical application. Do not assume that sequence definition necessarily supplies a valuable function.

Compare the whole accepted library and its physical use with the strongest relevant existing construction: competent automated solid-phase or flow synthesis, convergent or iterative growth, template-directed chemistry, molecularly encoded libraries and other applicable methods. Give the alternatives automation, capping, parallel vessels, purification and structural analysis where useful. A single liquid-handling robot can already operate many reaction vessels; counting 16 separate manual chemists is not a fair baseline. Ordinary capping can suppress propagation of deletion products, while post-run analysis can reconstruct short sequences; neither should be claimed as a new invention merely by renaming it. Conversely, an ordinary machine's ability to copy a proposed new primitive does not itself show that the primitive already exists.

Seek a consequential whole-operation advantage: at least a threefold reduction in turnaround or a 30% reduction in cost per accepted, structurally authenticated library at matched output, sequence quality and useful physical function. Also examine a plausible expansion to 1000 distinct programs, paying for program preparation, orthogonality, cross-talk, compartment or support manufacture, common-feed transport, readout and product resolution. Distinguish a real scalable advantage from moving externally addressed operations into an equally expensive stock of customised molecular parts. Do not assume these advantage targets are achievable; identify the missing operation or the strongest contrary construction when the evidence does not support them.

Use actual attainable conditions and components wherever available. Account for reagent and program synthesis, activation and purification, solvent and water, mixing and diffusion, concentrations, temperature and pressure control, sensing and readout, product handling, machine and support fabrication, energy and fuel free energy, replacement lives, maintenance, human preparation, rejected output and waste. Report both preparation and production resources, with a reference service calculation at USD 80/MWh electricity and USD 40/hour labour plus useful sensitivities. Source-derived operating points, introduced design values and unresolved joint compatibility must remain distinct. A table assigning favourable rates to incompatible chemistries does not construct their common operating window.
