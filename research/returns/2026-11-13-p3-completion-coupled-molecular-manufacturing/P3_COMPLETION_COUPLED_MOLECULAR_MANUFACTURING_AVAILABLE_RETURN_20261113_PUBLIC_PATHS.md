## Visible final

Result

A physically plausible bond-to-progression mechanism can be constructed from identifiable chemical parts. I did not obtain a complete design that can honestly claim your library, renewal and economic targets.

The strongest candidate I developed is a product-retained key that removes an instruction clip:

A building block carries a removable molecular key.
The intended extension attaches that key to the growing product.
Temporary docking interactions are dismantled, and unattached keys are physically removed.
A retained key removes the current instruction and exposes the next.
A failed coupling leaves the original chain available for another attempt.

This is a more concrete construction than a controller receiving an assigned “reaction succeeded” flag. However, three central operations remain unestablished: chemically selective retention, strictly local and single-use instruction advancement, and restoration under a common chemical operating window. The procurement calculation also gives a substantial cost disadvantage for this implementation.

1. What already exists—and what would actually be new

Several primary constructions substantially narrow the invention claim.

Niu, Hili and Liu demonstrated DNA-templated synthesis of non-natural polymers containing 16 consecutively coupled building blocks. Some blocks themselves contained multiple residues; their reported 90 β-amino-acid residues therefore do not represent 90 individual programmed extension operations. Meng and colleagues subsequently demonstrated an autonomous DNA hybridization cascade that organized oligomer synthesis and recorded the assembly program. These are substantive predecessors, not merely digital barcodes. 
pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
+1

There is also now direct evidence for a related control primitive. Liu, Liu and Deng’s 2026 construction uses DNA ligation to enable subsequent hairpin progression, including confined tracks, ten-station signal transmission and repeated fueling/reset. Its enabling bond is a DNA phosphodiester; it does not establish that formation of an intended peptoid amide controls progression. Nevertheless, it supplies real control machinery that a new proposal must credit. 
nature.com

Kassem’s molecular arm supplies a different genuine capability: positioning a tethered substrate changes stereochemical outcomes under sequential chemical inputs. It does not supply an autonomous completion checkpoint. 
pure.manchester.ac.uk

Finally, retaining a removable handle through successful incorporation is itself established. He, Wang and colleagues’ modified-Fmoc construction attaches a capture handle to an incorporated amino acid, captures the product and subsequently removes the protecting group. Its demonstrated purification recoveries were 75–83% per round. Repeating those recoveries twenty times would leave only approximately 0.32–2.41%, before other losses. It is useful physical evidence, but cannot be inherited as the requested high-yield factory. 
pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

The narrower proposed contribution is therefore:

Use a removable, product-retained handle to perform a local, one-use instruction transition after physical clearance of unsuccessful reagent packets, while preserving failed chemical ends for retry.

I have not established patent novelty, nor demonstrated that this complete operation works.

3.2 Preparation is not a generic conjugation step

The donor contains two carboxyl groups during its preparation: the HEPL attachment acid and the glycine coupling acid. They need an actual protection sequence. Activating both and assuming selective DNA attachment would be invalid.

A proposed route is:

Protect the HEPL carboxyl.
Form its benzylic carbamate with an N-substituted glycine ester.
Expose the HEPL carboxyl and attach amino-DNA.
Expose the glycine carboxyl under conditions that preserve the DNA and photocarbamate.
Purify and establish the intended connectivity independently.

The EDANS-derived C monomer is harder. Its secondary naphthylamine can be acylated; an activated carboxymethyl derivative can also potentially close into a six-membered ring. Installing C last prevents later backbone acylations, but does not solve this precursor problem.

An explicit proposed remedy is temporary protection of its primary amine, Boc protection of the aryl NH, selective removal of the temporary protection, nonreducing mono-carboxymethylation and HEPL-carbamate installation. Aryl Boc remains through incorporation and is removed after product/platform separation. The exact regioselectivity and yields remain unmeasured.

3.3 A physical instruction transition

Each instruction clip contains:

The currently available monomer selector.
A blocker covering the next selector.
A covalent attachment neck containing a nickase recognition region.

After successful coupling and clearance, the retained product key completes the recognition duplex. Nicking severs the clip. The same detached piece removes the old selector and the blocker over the next one.

That gives a physical memory of progress: a clip is missing. It is stronger than relying on a temporary hairpin fluorescence state that may disappear during deprotection.

Nb.BbvCI is an attainable candidate enzyme with a 37 °C reaction stage. Its presence does not establish rapid or selective cutting of the proposed branched structure. 
neb.com

Two problems are load-bearing:

The key must not become catalytic. Cutting the clip does not necessarily consume the key. A real nickase-beacon construction demonstrates that one target can cleave hundreds of probes. Here that behavior would cause false progression of neighboring chains. 
doi.org
Blocking a future neck must not activate it. A fully complementary DNA blocker can itself create the double-stranded enzyme substrate. A mismatched, abasic or otherwise qualified blocker is required.

The proposed key remains bound to a long segment of the detached clip until enzyme quenching and waste removal. That is a testable kinetic trap, not established single-use behavior.

4. The complete common-feed operation

The physical organization is sixteen permeable, identifiable support cartridges in one recirculating reaction environment. They receive the same reagent mixtures, wash solutions, temperature changes and light exposure. Cartridge identity preserves product provenance; it does not select the monomer feed.

For each global position:

Dock inactive packets. All programs encounter the same candidate packet mixture. Only the exposed local selector should retain its matching packet.
Wash away free packets.
Activate and couple using a common reagent feed.
Strip the temporary docking duplex.
Physically remove unincorporated packets into waste.
Expose the retained advancement key and cut its local instruction clip.
Retry up to three times before moving the common feed schedule forward.
Remove enzyme, deprotect successful extensions and clear spent material.

Successful chains remain protected throughout the retries at that position. Otherwise a successful chain could acquire extra monomers while its slower neighbors retry.

This is bounded asynchronous retry within a globally staged process. Permanently stalled chains are retained in the material account and, unless physically separated, enter the released population as incomplete products.
