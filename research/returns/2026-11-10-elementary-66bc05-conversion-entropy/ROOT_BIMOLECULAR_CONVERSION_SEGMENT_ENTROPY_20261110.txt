Bounded local activation and conversion-segment relative entropy
10 November 2026. Mathematical scope and complete derivation.

1. Setting and retained premises.

Fix a finite labelled reaction structure R, maximum original complex order D>=1, and a unique-parent prefix lift. Original species represent their unit vectors; an auxiliary represents a prefix c with 2<=|c|<=D. Every elementary binding/release preserves the nonnegative integer decoder. A conversion between representatives realizes the associated original edge. The decoder is identity on original species. Every fine species has decoded size between one and D. Thus, on a decoder fibre x with n=|x|, every fine population is at most n and the fibre is finite. This finiteness is a proof property, not an instruction to enumerate or truncate the physical network.

All labelled elementary rate constants are positive. Let m be the fixed number of labels and let a_- and a_+ be their minimum and maximum. Under relative perturbations <=1/2, replace these by a_-/2 and 3a_+/2 when a common bound is needed. Falling factorial propensities at enabled states are positive integers times a rate. Hence on a decoder fibre

 a(z) <= m a_+ (n+1)^2.

Null and parallel labels are counted separately. A conversion is distinguished by its label, even when its decoded change is zero. First-conversion stopping below includes those labels. Nothing assumes that the process has already equilibrated inside the fibre.

2. Constant-length local path to a conversion.

Suppose an original source y is enabled at x. In any fine encoding z of x, expose the at most |y| missing original atoms as follows. If a required free atom is missing, choose an occupied prefix containing that atom and release that ONE prefix completely. At most D-1 release steps are required. All exposed atoms remain free until this stage finishes. Each chosen prefix supplies at least one of the still missing atoms, so at most |y| such complete releases suffice. Then assemble y along its prefix tree, using at most D-1 binding steps, and fire the conversion.

The number of marked jumps on this chosen path is <=L_D:=(D+1)^2+2, independent of n, the class size, and how much unrelated material remains bound. Sources of order zero or one need no auxiliary assembly. Releasing every auxiliary molecule is unnecessary. If a different conversion occurs during an attempted path, the first-conversion objective has already been attained.

Put r_n=min(1,a_-/[m a_+(n+1)^2]). Conditional on no previous conversion, the probability of following the required next binding/release label, or attaining a conversion earlier, has the usual jump-chain lower bound. From every current fine state in the same fibre, a fresh path of length <=L_D exists. Consequently, if J is the number of ALL marked fine firings up to and including the first conversion,

 P(J>q L_D) <= (1-r_n^L_D)^q,
 E J <= L_D r_n^(-L_D),
 E J^h <= C_(D,h) r_n^(-h L_D), h fixed positive integer.

At every preconversion state at least one label is enabled, so the total rate is >=a_-. Conditional holding times can be coupled below independent unit exponentials divided by a_-. This gives, for the physical first-conversion time sigma,

 E sigma^h <= C_(D,h) a_-^(-h) r_n^(-h L_D).

The same argument may be phrased with a geometric sequence of blocks and Laplace transforms, so dependence of holding times on the jump chain causes no independence assumption. In particular sigma is almost surely finite. For compiled a_+ and a_-^-1 polynomial in W, these are explicit polynomial numerical bounds in W and (n+1). If no original edge is enabled, this lemma asserts nothing; singleton/no-conversion cases are treated separately. The statement does not itself compare an exit location to the original reaction chain or prove recurrence between fibres.

3. Absorbing the independent binding errors exactly.

For the unique-parent assembly forest, use the ACTUAL binding and release constants to define activities recursively. A first prefix formed from original species i,j has

 c'_P=(tilde k_bind/tilde k_release) lambda_i lambda_j;

a later prefix P formed from parent Q and original species i has

 c'_P=(tilde k_bind/tilde k_release)c'_Q lambda_i.

Repeated original species use the same mass-action falling-factorial convention as the nominal table. These definitions make every actual reversible binding pair exactly detailed balanced. If nominal c_P=theta lambda^(D P), and each binding coefficient has relative error <=delta, then, with rho=(1+delta)/(1-delta),

 rho^(-(D-1)) <= c'_P/(theta lambda^(D P)) <= rho^(D-1).

Keep every actual binding coefficient unchanged and choose a REFERENCE conversion coefficient so that its complex flux at activities c' is the supplied original complex-balanced flux k_r lambda^(y_r). This reference is complex balanced. An actual conversion coefficient divided by the reference one has the form (1+error_r) times the activity-ratio of its source representative, with the obvious order-zero/one convention. Therefore all these ratios lie in [1-eta,1+eta] for eta<=8D delta when delta<=1/(16D); enlarging the harmless fixed constant covers every source order. The reference and actual processes have identical binding/release rates. They differ only on zero/unary conversion labels. Reference moment bounds and the full stopped-path argument are separate from any claim about the stationary law of the actual process.

4. Entropy bound for the ENTIRE segment ending at the first conversion.

Start both processes from the SAME arbitrary fine state z in an enabled decoder fibre. Let b_e(w) be the reference conversion intensities and b=sum_e b_e. Actual intensities are r_e b_e with fixed r_e in [1-eta,1+eta], eta<=1/2. Internal binding/release intensities agree exactly. Stop at the first conversion sigma, retaining every binding/release mark, every holding time, the final conversion mark and the resulting fine state. The local path lemma proves sigma<infinity almost surely, with finite moments, under both laws. Before sigma the state lies in a finite fibre, so localization of the likelihood formula is legitimate and can be removed using these bounds.

Under the reference law, the counting compensator of the aggregate conversion clock gives

 E_ref integral_0^sigma b(Z_t) dt = 1.

This also follows from its unit exponential integrated-hazard representation. It does not assert that sigma is an ordinary exponential physical time, and b may vanish at some intermediate states.

The relative entropy of complete stopped marked-path laws satisfies

 KL(P_ref^segment || P_actual^segment)
  = E_ref integral_0^sigma sum_e b_e(Z_t)[r_e-1-log r_e] dt
  <= 2 eta^2.

Indeed, internal jump likelihoods cancel, the final conversion contributes log(1/r_e), and its expected mark contribution is converted to an integrated intensity by the compensator. On [1/2,3/2], r-1-log r <=2(r-1)^2. Pinsker then gives

 TV(P_ref^segment,P_actual^segment) <= eta.

The same TV bound holds after projecting to the next conversion's mark, fine state, decoded state or any measurable function of the whole segment. This bound is uniform in n, the number of binding/release events, their absolute speed, and the initial fine state. For the reverse entropy, E_actual integral b <=(1-eta)^-1 and the corresponding convex integrand gives the same conclusion up to a universal factor. No minimum stationary point weight, finite-tree exponent or fibre Poincare inequality enters this single-segment statement.

5. Polynomial rewards and what remains unresolved.

The local path proof pays ALL marked firings and physical time in a segment, with fixed-power moments in n and the compiled reciprocal rates. It also bounds any fixed polynomial reward accumulated until sigma using N<=n on the fibre. The entropy bound itself controls bounded observables only. For unbounded time/firing rewards, use those moment bounds and a truncation or Holder estimate and retain the resulting numerical powers; TV alone is not an absolute-moment estimate.

Repeated conversion segments define a Markov kernel on fine postconversion states together with occupation-time and firing rewards. Identical starting states admit segment-TV couplings with error <=eta per conversion. That does NOT imply a time-uniform coupling, invariant-law stability, positive recurrence, or a polynomially bounded stationary population. Errors can accumulate over indefinitely many conversions. A uniform row-TV perturbation bound without a recurrence/coercivity condition is insufficient. The reference event-stationary weighting by b and its return/occupation rewards still need their own mathematical control. The possibility b=0 on some fine states makes a naive division by b invalid there.

Thus this note constructs a local activation/cost operation and a conversion-only perturbation interface. It does not settle the robust compiler. It also does not remove the supplied ordinary-gap-zero example or the failure of an inequality weighted by ALL binding/release activity. Those failures are not universal obstructions to other elementary designs, nor do they refute this conversion-segment estimate.

The completed elementary return supplies nominal infinite-class stationary accuracy, polynomial mixing from the canonical encoding, uniform nominal moments, polynomial finite-horizon robustness and paid all-firing costs. Its near-L1 transfer uses q about the logarithm of the inverse canonical point mass, rather than an exponentially large L2 prefactor. Its exact-binding-error absorption is the reference construction used in Section3. Nominal static accuracy and structural elementary nonexplosion also follow from the earlier independent nominal-accuracy note; they are retained once, not counted as separate discoveries.
