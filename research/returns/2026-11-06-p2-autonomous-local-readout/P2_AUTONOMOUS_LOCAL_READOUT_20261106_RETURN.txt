## 2. Precise statement

During autonomous operation, its ideal dynamics have the form
\[
\mathcal L_0=\sum_{a=1}^{M}\mathcal L_a,
\qquad M\le m_0N,
\]
where every \(\mathcal L_a\) is a local Lindblad generator on a bounded support. The finite layout, program, and initial controller state may depend on \(T\).

All mutable timing, address, program, syndrome, buffer, and I/O records are included in \(D\). There is no uncounted input-bearing environment or noiseless output store.

Let \(P\subset D\) be the fixed compact output port. Grant an ideal local readout channel
\[
\mathcal R:P\longrightarrow O
\]
to an output qubit. The decoder may be chosen separately for each horizon.

At a deterministic public time \(t_*\ge T\), require half-diamond error at most
\[
\delta<\frac12
\]
from the identity channel on an unknown qubit. The accepted target is \(\delta=1/4\).

**Theorem.** If this guarantee holds for every permitted assignment of CPTP corruption maps to the physical burst marks, then
\[
N\ge cT
\]
for sufficiently large \(T\), with \(c>0\) independent of \(T\). Since all installed hardware is charged throughout the autonomous interval,

U_{\mathrm{all}}\ge cT^2.

The theorem also covers a prescribed one-qubit unitary output: compose the mathematical readout with the inverse unitary. A universal unknown-input processor must, in particular, support the identity-storage task.

The proof permits arbitrary initial encoding and known apparatus preparation. Thus acquisition difficulty or poor initialization cannot explain away the obstruction. Any additional physical cost or error in those operations only makes the original task harder.

## 3. The proof

There are two main ingredients: a finite budget for local quantum availability, and a limit on how sharply a noisy time-independent device can change its output at late times. The actual burst process supplies an admissible noise assignment under which both apply.

### 3.1 An exact assignment from the physical burst process

For each bounded local interaction support \(S_a\), choose a constant-volume region of burst centers and a fixed radius interval such that every selected burst covers \(S_a\).

Because the hardware has bounded density and bounded local term multiplicity, these center regions have bounded overlap. Color them with a fixed number of colors. A randomized CPTP fault assignment selects a color and replaces only the corresponding support when the physical mark belongs to its selected source region. Reserve another selection for the output port.

This produces exact replacement streams with rate
\[
q=c_{\mathrm{src}}\lambda>0,
\]
independent of \(T\), for each local support and for \(P\). Every unused physical mark acts as the identity.

This construction is a permitted adversary inside the original marked Poisson process. The finite random labels can equivalently be included as convex mixtures in the allowed CPTP maps. They do not increase the physical intensity or alter burst geometry.

The resulting averaged noise is
\[
q\sum_a(\mathcal D_{S_a}-\mathrm{id})
+
q(\mathcal D_P-\mathrm{id}),
\]
where \(\mathcal D_S\) completely depolarizes the specified support.

**This is not an inferred independent effective-noise law for the compiler.** A universal robustness guarantee must hold for this particular memoryless assignment, so it is sufficient for a converse.

Only bounded-radius bursts are used. The new timing obstruction does not depend on the Pareto tail.

### 3.2 Local quantum availability consumes conditional information

Prepare a Bell pair between the input and an inaccessible reference qubit \(R\). Write
\[
D=PB,
\]
where \(B\) is the rest of the device.

Under the selected adversary, the noisy generator can be written
\[
\mathcal G=\mathcal K+q(\mathcal D_P-\mathrm{id}),
\]
with \(\mathcal K\) another CPTP generator.

The total mutual information \(I(R:D)\) cannot increase under device dynamics. A complete replacement of \(P\) changes it from \(I(R:PB)\) to \(I(R:B)\). The difference is
\[
I(R:P\mid B).
\]

More precisely, convexity of mutual information at fixed \(R\) marginal, followed by a Trotter-product argument, gives
\[
q\int_0^\infty I(R:P\mid B)_{\rho(t)}\,dt
\le I(R:D)_{\rho(0)}
\le 2\ln2.
\tag{1}
\]

The finite-time version suffices for the theorem. Natural logarithms are used throughout.

This inequality already accounts for information in the rest of the apparatus. Recovery from \(B\), extra blank ancillas, and known controller states cannot replenish correlations with the inaccessible reference that have been irreversibly discarded.

Now define the locally decoded Bell fidelity
\[
F(t)=
\langle\Phi^+|
(\mathrm{id}_R\otimes\mathcal R)\rho_{RP}(t)
|\Phi^+\rangle.
\]

To relate fidelity to conditional information, I use the corrected Brandão–Christandl–Yard one-way-LOCC bound:
\[
I(R:P\mid B)
\ge
\frac18
\left\|\rho_{RP}-\mathrm{SEP}(R:P)\right\|_{\mathrm{LOCC}\to}^{\,2},
\tag{2}
\]
in natural-log units. This is imported mathematical machinery. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

The relevant measurement is elementary. Randomly choose one of the Pauli parity tests \(XX,-YY,ZZ\). Its acceptance effect is
\[
M=\frac12I+\frac16(XX-YY+ZZ)
=\frac13I+\frac23|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|.
\]

Every separable two-qubit state has Bell overlap at most \(1/2\). Consequently, after composing this measurement with the local decoder,
\[
\left\|\rho_{RP}-\mathrm{SEP}\right\|_{\mathrm{LOCC}\to}
\ge
\frac43\left[F(t)-\frac12\right]_+.
\]

Combining this with (1) and (2) yields
\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty
\left[F(t)-\frac12\right]_+^2\,dt
\le
\frac{9\ln2}{q}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This is the **local quantum-availability budget**. A once-supplied unknown qubit cannot remain available with a fixed positive entanglement-fidelity excess at the same noisy compact port for an arbitrarily long total duration.

The statement is quantum-specific. A classical bit can be copied into \(B\), so replacing a readable local copy need not destroy any conditional information. The same argument does not establish a classical linear-hardware requirement.

### 3.3 Time-independent jump dynamics cannot make arbitrarily narrow late peaks

Suppose a generator admits an exact representation
\[
\mathcal G=\Lambda(\Phi-\mathrm{id}),
\]
where \(\Phi\) is CPTP. Then
\[
e^{t\mathcal G}
=
e^{-\Lambda t}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
\frac{(\Lambda t)^k}{k!}\Phi^k.
\]

For any fixed output effect,
\[
F(t)=\mathbb E[f_K],
\qquad
K\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda t),
\qquad 0\le f_K\le1.
\]

The Poisson score has variance \(\Lambda/t\). Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives
\[
|F'(t)|\le\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\Lambda}{t}},
\]
and hence
\[
\boxed{
|F(t)-F(s)|
\le
\sqrt{\Lambda}\,(\sqrt t-\sqrt s),
\qquad 0\le s\le t.
}
\tag{4}
\]

No unbiased clock estimator, renewal assumption, or independence of logical clock cycles is required.

The significance is that correctness at one late instant forces some nearby local availability. The task need not promise a correct output throughout an interval; the noisy time-independent dynamics themselves impose the interval.

### 3.4 Coherent Hamiltonian clocks are included

A noiseless Hamiltonian generator generally does **not** admit a finite-rate representation \(\Lambda(\Phi-\mathrm{id})\). Applying (4) directly to it would be an invalid step.

The permitted local replacement noise resolves this issue.

Let \(\mathcal L\) be a Lindblad generator on a support of Hilbert-space dimension \(n\), and let
\[
j=\|\mathsf J(\mathcal L)\|_\infty
\]
for its normalized Choi operator. With complete depolarization \(\mathcal D\), define
\[
a=2(j+q)+\frac{2n^2j^2}{q}.
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L+q(\mathcal D-\mathrm{id})
=
a(\Psi-\mathrm{id})
}
\tag{5}
\]
for a CPTP map \(\Psi\).

The proof uses conditional complete positivity of a Lindblad generator. In the Choi decomposition into the maximally entangled direction and its orthogonal complement, depolarization supplies a strictly positive block on the complement. The displayed choice of \(a\) makes the Schur complement nonnegative. Trace preservation follows from trace annihilation by the generator.

Applying (5) to every local term gives an exact representation of the **same noisy evolution** with
\[
\Lambda
\le
m_0N
\left[
2(j_*+q)+\frac{2d_*^2j_*^2}{q}
\right]+q
=
C_\lambda N+q.
\tag{6}
\]

The global channel in this representation is an algebraic convex mixture of local channels. It is not a proposed global hardware operation.

Thus the argument includes coherent internal timing, coherent data processing, and dissipative clock repair. For pure channel-jump dynamics with bounded total rate, the simpler estimate
\[
\Lambda\le\gamma_*N+q
\]
suffices without regularizing the other terms.

### 3.5 Combining the two constraints

Half-diamond error at most \(\delta\), tested on a Bell input, implies
\[
F(t_*)\ge1-\delta=\frac12+g,
\qquad
g=\frac12-\delta>0.
\]

Equation (4) forces
\[
\left[F(s)-\frac12\right]_+
\ge
\left[g-\sqrt{\Lambda}(\sqrt{t_*}-\sqrt s)\right]_+.
\]

Integrating the square of the right-hand side over its positive interval gives, for sufficiently large \(t_*\),
\[
\int_0^{t_*}
\left[F(s)-\frac12\right]_+^2ds
\ge
\frac{g^3}{2}\sqrt{\frac{t_*}{\Lambda}}.
\]

The availability budget (3) bounds this above. Rearranging yields the explicit rate law
\[
\boxed{
\Lambda
\ge
\frac{q^2g^6}{324(\ln2)^2}\,t_*.
}
\tag{7}
\]

Since \(\Lambda\le C_\lambda N+q\), fixed positive \(\lambda\) and fixed \(g>0\) imply
\[
N=\Omega(t_*)=\Omega(T).
\]

At fixed local bounds, the displayed estimates give sufficient small-intensity prefactors \(N=\Omega(\lambda^2T)\) for pure jump dynamics and \(N=\Omega(\lambda^3T)\) for general local Lindblad dynamics. These intensity powers and constants are not claimed optimal. The programme fixes \(\lambda>0\) before choosing \(T\), which is the limit needed for the resource separation.

## 4. A stronger fidelity consequence

The argument also provides a quantitative failure statement. For the admissible adversary and sufficiently late \(t\),
\[
F(t)
\le
\min\!\left\{
1,\;
\frac12+
\left[
\frac{324(\ln2)^2\Lambda}{q^2t}
\right]^{1/6}
\right\}.
\tag{8}
\]

With \(\Lambda\le C_\lambda N+q\), this implies
\[
N=o(T)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
F(T)\le\frac12+o(1).
\]

The decoder may be optimized for each horizon. It only needs to remain fixed when comparing nearby times for that same device.

Thus sublinear hardware cannot reach the accepted Bell-fidelity target of at least \(3/4\) at the specified port and deadline. This conclusion does not assume that the controller deadlocks or that logical error correction fails. It grants every internal repair and computation mechanism expressible by the stated dynamics.

The obstruction is the combination of two facts:

- A locally accessible unknown quantum state cannot tolerate prolonged exposure to port resets without consuming its finite reference-correlation budget.
- A bounded noisy autonomous device cannot concentrate that accessibility arbitrarily sharply around a late predetermined time.

### A larger readout region changes the bound

\[
q_P\asymp\lambda s^{-r}.
\]

The same argument gives, in the nontrivial large-horizon regime, the necessary tradeoff

### Random completion is a genuinely different interface

The proof concerns fixed-time marginals. It cannot be evaluated at a random stopping time merely by replacing \(t_*\) with that stopping time.

A device that delivers immediately after a causally detected completion event may escape this particular bound. Such an operation must establish its own receiving mechanism, detector response, timing records, failure probabilities, and resource costs.

A completion flag followed by leaving the quantum state at the same raw port until a predetermined pickup time remains covered by the theorem.

The result also does not cover unbounded-dimensional oscillators or uncounted non-Markovian reservoirs carrying persistent information. Those would require different accounting and a different dynamical model.
