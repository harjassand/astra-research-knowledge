2. An inert-recycle tradeoff derivable without a new catalyst
Consider a steady idealized thermal loop with fresh H2:N2=3:1. Fresh inert/N2 molar ratio is a, no inert is otherwise separated, all ammonia is removed between passes, and a fraction p of the remaining gas is purged. Let c be single-pass N2 conversion, F fresh N2, N reactor-inlet N2 and I reactor-inlet inert. These equations follow from actual molar conservation:
 F=[c+p(1-c)]N; aF=pI.
If reactor-inlet inert mole fraction is y, set s=I/N=4y/(1-y). Then
 p=a*c/[s-a(1-c)],
 u=c/[c+p(1-c)]=1-a(1-c)/s
is useful hydrogen/nitrogen utilization, when0<p<=1. All this assumes stoichiometric reactants and complete inter-pass NH3 removal; side reactions, incomplete removal and active purification change the balances rather than disappear.
The illustrative a=.012 is a DECLARED inert-contamination scenario, not a measured air-separation product. Actual N2 supply composition, oxygen cleanup and any argon/inert rejection must be measured and costed. Saying99.9% nitrogen without the relevant impurity spec does not supply this interface.

3. Steady operation is not silently imposed on a finite trial
The exact transient inert inventory is
 I_final-I_initial=integral(fresh inert flow)-integral(purged inert flow).
A100-hour run could initially accumulate inert or consume a precharged gas inventory instead of reaching this steady state. That possibility is not excluded by the formulas above. Its finite buffer, changing partial pressures, startup charge, final purge/reset and repeated-run cost must be measured and paid. A large buffer cannot be counted as indefinitely purified recycle. Long-utilization production economics ultimately require a sustainable inventory/return path.

4. Thermodynamics and actual useful rate are separate
For ideal gas N2+3H2->2NH3, normalized inlet1N2+3H2+I inert and no incoming NH3, conversion c gives exit quotient
 Q=4*c^2*(4-2c+I)^2/[27*(1-c)^4*(P/1bar)^2].
A thermal reaction in the forward direction requires the actual equilibrium constant K(T) to support the composition; a catalyst changes rate rather than that equilibrium. Continuous product extraction changes the physical gas activity and introduces an absorption/separation operation. Aqueous10wt% recovery, water back-crossover and gas drying cannot be replaced with perfect NH3 removal.
K(T) is deliberately UNASSESSED here. The first requested NIST hydrogen thermochemistry page was inaccessible; no remembered heat-capacity coefficients or alternate provider were used to fill its governing passage. The executable evaluates Q and the standard-free-energy threshold that would support it, not an equilibrium prediction.
The actually read NH3 formation enthalpy gives.748625kWh/kg at298K. This is a reference-state diagnostic, NOT heat available from a250C reactor or a self-heating design. Actual operating heat, losses, recycle sensible heat and startup remain to be calculated with permitted complete inputs.
