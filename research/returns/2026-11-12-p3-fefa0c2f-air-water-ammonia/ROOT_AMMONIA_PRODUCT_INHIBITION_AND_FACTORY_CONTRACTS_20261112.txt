3. Product inhibition trades against circulation
The proposed reactor has a very dilute NH3 window, about.0152--.065bar, and33.64kWh/kg gross recycle sensible heat. Its98% recuperation leaves.6728kWh/kg, while95% leaves1.682. High effectiveness is an actual area/pressure-drop/manufacturing operation, not a freely supplied heat-recovery factor.
A representative extension of the producer's conditional law r proportional to[1+p_NH3/.001bar]^-1.1, holding other variables fixed, would raise catalyst mass by12.2856 when a representative product pressure moves from.04 to.4bar. The potential gas-flow reduction is only of orderten for a similarly rescaled dilute profile. This extension is OUTSIDE that model's stated working range, not measured kinetics or an all-catalyst restriction. It exposes why simply concentrating the same product-inhibited stream does not supply a solution.
In a simplified cost representation C(p)=A*p^n+B/p, product-inhibited catalyst inventory increases as p^n while dilute-stream circulation area decreases as1/p. The stationary p is[B/(nA)]^(1/(n+1)) for positive A,B,n. Real reversible kinetics, reactant depletion, gas contraction, pellet diffusion and finite recovery must replace this toy law. Changing material selectivity or operating conditions can change A,n or the entire representation.

4. Hot selective extraction also needs a real constituent
For constant gas permeances and near-zero H2/NH3 downstream partial pressures, keeping lost H2 below.02 times stoichiometric useful H2 requires J_H2/J_NH3<=.03. At an illustrative12.15bar H2 and.05bar NH3, this demands NH3/H2 permeance selectivity>=8100. A pressure-balanced receiving stream or actual H2 recovery changes the model; the number is not an intrinsic limit on every separator.
The ideal isothermal work to pump already-pure NH3 from.05bar to1bar at245C is only.2105kWh/kg. That favorable work does not supply selective capture from the H2/N2 mixture, a membrane or mechanical compressor. An aqueous receiver introduces water activity and possible back-crossover into a water-sensitive catalyst; actual tolerance, return and guarding must be paid.

5. Higher pressure is not automatically the dominant energy cost
For an ILLUSTRATIVE stoichiometric fresh N2/H2 stream initially at1bar, ideal isothermal compression at298.15K divided by an introduced.60 efficiency costs.40376kWh/kgNH3 to20bar,.62067 to100bar and.71409 to200bar. The20-to100bar increment is.21692kWh/kg.
This is a thermodynamic diagnostic, not an actual compressor map, pressure-vessel cost or a plant prediction. If H2 is already pressurized by a paid electrolyzer, do not pay the same work twice. A higher operating pressure can alter kinetics, equilibrium, recycle and recovery; real pressure equipment, cooling, sealing, heat losses and materials must be paid together. An arbitrary low-pressure or low-temperature badge is not itself a useful performance advantage.
