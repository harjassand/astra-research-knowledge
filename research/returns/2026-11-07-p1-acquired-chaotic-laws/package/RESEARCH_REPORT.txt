# Finite experiments, exact chaotic laws, and reusable network orbit certificates

Research return — 7 November 2026

## 1. The question selected and the completed endpoint

The selected question is whether incomplete, finitely acquired dynamical information can determine an entire future orbit structure, and remain useful when independently acquired nonlinear components are coupled later.

The completed operation is the following. For a specified open class of stationary maps on a known torus, paid one-step experiments with bounded adversarial errors produce a finite rational replacement map and a certificate. The certificate proves that every source compatible with the measurements and the supplied regularity bounds has exactly the same topological orbit structure. It determines topological entropy and the number of fixed points of every iterate, and it supports a finite-bit compiler for any subsequently supplied periodic-orbit address. The source-location error of that compiled orbit does not grow with its period.

The operation composes. Separately acquired components may be connected through a later specified periodic nonlinear coupling with a certified block Lipschitz budget. In a uniform margin regime, acquisition is additive over components, the correspondence error per component is independent of network size, entropy adds, and periodic-point counts multiply. A joint grid of the network state space is unnecessary.

The mathematical core is classical hyperbolic shadowing and structural stability, made explicit here as an acquisition-to-use construction. The winding acquisition, complete numerical uncertainty budget, finite represented surrogate, periodic-address algorithm, and modular deductions are proved below. This is not a claim to have invented structural stability or to have established priority over the computational dynamics literature. Exact scope matters: the operation is for declared closed discrete-time state coordinates with global regularity guards and a checked margin. It does not obtain those coordinates from passive ports, solve general chaotic inference, or identify a physical stationary measure.

The earlier hidden-phase theorem and every predecessor retain their accepted scopes. No held source or earlier unconfirmed ZIP was recovered or included in this package. No peer scientist, additional worker, physical apparatus, or new infrastructure was used.

## 2. Actual input, experiment, and output contracts

### 2.1 Source and observations

The source is a fixed map

    F : T^d -> T^d,       T^d = R^d / Z^d.

The torus coordinates and their normalization are supplied. These coordinates form a closed state: there is no additional unprepared hidden state affecting the next update. Stationarity is a source promise. A C^3 lift is assumed, with warranted Euclidean operator bounds

    ||DF|| <= B,   ||D^2 F|| <= M2,   ||D^3 F|| <= M3.

The notation denotes derivatives of a lift; derivatives are independent of the chosen lift. Multilinear operator norms are used. The homology matrix A is not supplied. Any lift has the form

    F~(x) = A x + b + g(x),

where A is an integer matrix and g is Z^d-periodic. Its values and derivatives are initially unknown. The positive algorithm below checks that A is symmetric, unimodular, and hyperbolic, and checks a quantitative small-residual-derivative condition. This is a sufficient class, not a sharp boundary for all structurally stable maps.

A native query prepares a chosen rational coordinate x and returns a finite report y(x) of one source update. Its warranted torus error is at most eta in Euclidean distance. Preparation error, read error, timing error and digital rounding must already be in eta. For example, justified bounds give

    eta = delta_read + B delta_prepare + V delta_time + delta_round,

where V is a separately warranted sensitivity to update timing if timing is relevant. There is no automatic access to derivatives, a transfer function, noiseless output, an invariant measure, or a long trajectory oracle.

Every query pays its actual preparation, update and read cost. If the hardware offers precision p with error eta(p), its time and energy schedules are inputs to the accounting. Repeated adversarial errors do not average away. A fixed positive noise instrument can certify discrete invariants when the margin permits. Arbitrarily small orbit-location errors require genuinely refinable eta, not more repeats of a fixed-noise report.

This channel is stronger than the preceding passive port channel. The preceding component library does not, by itself, supply closed torus state coordinates, arbitrary state preparation, or these derivative bounds. The new scientific destination deliberately makes that access change explicit.

### 2.2 Finite outputs

The acquired representation contains:

1. the exact integer matrix A and a finite spectral certificate;
2. a rational offset b and a finite periodic grid of residual values;
3. a fixed Freudenthal triangulation rule giving a rational piecewise affine map Fhat;
4. rational certificates for source derivative uncertainty, surrogate Lipschitz constant, source/surrogate uniform error, and a global orbit correspondence;
5. algorithms for exact all-period counts, entropy as a finite expression in logarithms of algebraic numbers, and later periodic addresses;
6. an admissible later coupling budget, with uncertainty and computation charged separately.

No exact representation of g is acquired. The compatible family remains infinite dimensional. The conjugacy to the unknown source is proved to exist with a quantitative bound; its exact function is not falsely reported as measured. The finite surrogate is evaluable, and the later-task outputs are certified enclosures valid for every compatible source.

## 3. Exact integer topology from noisy one-step queries

Let M be a loop subdivision satisfying

    B/M + 2 eta < 1/2.                                      (1)

For each coordinate j, query x=(l/M)e_j for l=0,...,M-1. Use the same stored report at x=0 as the endpoint l=M. Do not pay for a second noisy endpoint and then assume its error cancels.

For each successive pair, take the componentwise principal difference of their reported torus values in [-1/2,1/2). A true lifted increment has norm at most B/M. By (1), the principal difference equals that true lifted increment plus the difference of the two endpoint errors, with no integer alias. Summing the increments around the loop telescopes the errors exactly. The result is A e_j.

Thus finite positive-error observations acquire the exact integer matrix A. There are at most 1+d(M-1) distinct queries, with the origin shared. Every output increment and sum can be processed in exact rational/integer arithmetic. Each column norm is at most B, so the integer entries have O(log(2+B)) bits. Conditions on reported cycles are also checkable.

The integer conclusion is not obtained by treating small numerical residuals as zero. It follows because the globally warranted increment bound rules out every other winding. Without a calibration or regularity bound ruling out aliasing, the same inference would be invalid.

## 4. Laws already forced by the acquired homotopy class

These deductions do not require the later small-derivative certificate.

### 4.1 Fixed-point lower bounds for every period

Suppose A has no eigenvalue on the unit circle. For every m>=1, D_m=A^m-I is invertible. For any continuous F inducing A,

    F~^m(x)-x = D_m x + g_m(x),

with g_m continuous, periodic and bounded. For every k in Z^d the continuous map

    x -> D_m^(-1)(k-g_m(x))

maps a sufficiently large closed ball into itself. Brouwer's theorem gives a solution to F~^m(x)-x=k. If solutions associated with k and l coincide modulo Z^d, then k-l belongs to D_m Z^d. The quotient has |det D_m| elements. Therefore

    # Fix(F^m) >= |det(A^m-I)|,        for every m>=1.       (2)

This includes noninvertible continuous maps. The left side may be infinite. Lower bounds (2) must not be Möbius-inverted to claim lower bounds for least periods; that inference would be invalid.

### 4.2 Entropy and a homeomorphism factor

Manning's first-homology theorem gives, for a continuous map on a compact manifold,

    h_top(F) >= log rho(A).                                 (3)

Only the first-homology claim is used for arbitrary continuous maps. No unsupported full exterior-algebra entropy bound is imported.

For a homeomorphism inducing a hyperbolic unimodular A, the stronger bound h_top(F)>=h_top(A) has an explicit factor construction. Let P_s,P_u be the stable and unstable spectral projectors. For a bounded bilateral sequence r define

    (G r)_k = sum_(j>=0) A^j P_s r_(k-1-j)
              - sum_(j>=0) A^(-j-1) P_u r_(k+j).            (4)

This is the unique bounded solution of u_(k+1)-A u_k=r_k. For a lifted F-orbit Y, set v=G(g(Y)) and X=Y-v, absorbing any constant b into g for this paragraph. Then X_(k+1)=A X_k. The map h(y)=y-v_0 is continuous, has degree one, is onto, and satisfies h F=A h. Uniformly convergent series establish continuity. Integer deck transformations are respected because A and A^(-1) preserve Z^d. Entropy does not increase under a factor, so

    h_top(F) >= h_top(A) = sum_(|lambda|>1) log |lambda|.   (5)

The factor argument is classical in origin. It is included to specify its actual homeomorphism gate, rather than applying it to every continuous map.

### 4.3 A global control obstruction

An arbitrary continuous periodic kick v changes F to F_v(x)=F(x)+v(x) modulo Z^d without changing A. It therefore preserves (2) and (3), even if v is too large for the later conjugacy theorem. Since |det(A^m-I)| becomes larger than one, such a map cannot have a single fixed point attracting every initial state. This does not preclude local stabilization or attraction of almost every initial state while exceptional invariant sets remain.

If A is unimodular, any finite composition of such continuous updates has nonzero degree, hence is onto. It cannot map the entire torus into a proper reset region. The statement concerns whole-torus preparation with this intervention grammar. The independent state-preparation instrument used for acquisition is a different interface and may reset the source. Discontinuous resets, changed topology, and extra controller memory are not covered by the obstruction.

## 5. A computable global conjugacy certificate

The complete constructive positive class uses symmetric hyperbolic A in GL(d,Z). Symmetry makes the stable and unstable projectors orthogonal and keeps the computed constants transparent. Set

    s = max_(|lambda|<1) |lambda| < 1,
    u = min_(|lambda|>1) |lambda| > 1.

Both subspaces are nonempty for a nontrivial hyperbolic unimodular matrix. Compute rational strict upper bounds

    C > 1/(1-s) + 1/(u-1),
    J > ||A^(-1)||_2.                                      (6)

The integer characteristic polynomial, exact symmetry and determinant checks, and real-root isolation produce these bounds. They are computed information, not instance-dependent free advice. For symmetric A, hyperbolicity is equivalent to det(A-I)det(A+I) != 0, since all eigenvalues are real. Finite algebraic root isolation then bounds the gaps and the inverse norm. Small gaps may make C and J very large; that cost is retained.

From (4), ||G|| on bounded sequences of Euclidean vectors is at most C. Let

    F~(x)=A x+b+g(x),   Lip(g)<=ell,
    C ell <1,          J ell <1.                           (7)

The lift is a global homeomorphism: for any y, x=A^(-1)(y-b-g(x)) is a contraction. Unimodularity makes the descended torus map a homeomorphism as well.

Let F0(x)=A x+b and let X_k=F0^k(x) be a bilateral affine orbit. On bounded sequences solve

    u = G( g(X+u) ).                                       (8)

The right side contracts with factor C ell. Let H_F(x)=x+u_0. Shifting the sequence in (8) gives H_F F0=F H_F. Integer deck equivariance follows from periodicity and A^k Z^d=Z^d for all integer k. Uniform convergence of the fixed-point iterations gives continuity.

For surjectivity, take any true bilateral F-orbit Y, put v=G(g(Y)), and form the affine orbit X=Y-v. The same fixed-point uniqueness gives H_F(X_0)=Y_0. For injectivity, coincident H_F-images have coincident true lifted orbits after an integer adjustment. The corresponding affine orbits differ by a bounded bilateral sequence A^k z. Hyperbolicity forces z=0. Thus H_F is a homeomorphism and

    ||H_F-id||_infinity <= C ||g||_infinity.                 (9)

No small amplitude assumption on b or on an added constant is needed. Smallness is in the residual Lipschitz bound.

Now take a finite surrogate Fhat=Ax+b+ghat with Lip(ghat)<=ellhat, satisfying (7) with ellhat, and suppose ||g-ghat||<=delta. Compare their fixed points in (8), using the same affine orbit:

    ||H_F-H_Fhat|| <= C delta/(1-C ell).

Consequently

    K=H_F H_Fhat^(-1),      K Fhat=F K,
    ||K-id|| <= D := C delta/(1-C ell).                     (10)

All distances on the torus are understood through the indicated lifts and hence imply the usual torus distance bound. Equation (10) covers every integer time:

    dist(F^t(Kx), Fhat^t(x)) <= D,      t in Z.              (11)

This is a correspondence with a shifted initial condition. It is not a guarantee that F^t(x) stays near Fhat^t(x) for the same x. No precision argument is allowed to erase that distinction.

The proof works in any norm with the stated bounds for G and A^(-1). That observation will be used for component networks.

## 6. Acquiring every quantity in the certificate

Choose a periodic grid of side q>=4 with

    B/q+2 eta <1/2.

Obtain one report at every vertex. Let b be the report at zero and fix the true lift so it is within eta of that report at zero. For each directed grid edge in direction e_j, unwrap the reported increment by its principal difference and subtract A e_j/q. Integrate these residual increments along a grid spanning tree, starting at zero.

The resulting rational array ghat(v) satisfies

    ghat(v)=g(v)+e_v,     ||e_v||<=eta.                      (12)

Errors telescope; they are not multiplied by the tree path length. In particular g(0)=-e_0 because b was a reported value. All periodic edge cycles can be checked exactly, including cycles that wind around the grid.

For each vertex v and coordinate j form the central difference

    Dhat_j g(v) = [ghat(v+e_j/q)-ghat(v-e_j/q)]/(2/q).

The guarded two-forward Taylor operation already retained in N1018 gives each column error at most

    e_col = M3/(6 q^2) + eta q.                            (13)

The matrix operator error is at most sqrt(d) e_col. Let beta be a rational upper bound on the maximum operator norm of these grid matrices, computed with exact matrix inequalities or real algebraic arithmetic. The nearest vertex is at distance at most sqrt(d)/(2q). Therefore every compatible source obeys

    ell = beta + sqrt(d)(M3/(6q^2)+eta q)
               + M2 sqrt(d)/(2q)                          (14)

as an upper bound for sup ||DF-A||.

Interpolate ghat by the standard periodic Freudenthal triangulation of the grid. A cell is split using the order of its fractional coordinates; only its d+1 chain vertices are needed at evaluation time. The full d! simplex list need not be stored. Let I_q ghat be this continuous periodic piecewise affine interpolant, and define

    Fhat(x)=Ax+b+I_q ghat(x) modulo Z^d.

Taylor expansion at the evaluation point, followed by barycentric cancellation of the linear term, gives

    delta = eta + M2 d/(2 q^2)                             (15)

as a bound for ||F~-Fhat~||. On a simplex, each column of the affine derivative is a directional edge difference. All its evaluation points lie within sqrt(d)/q of any selected point of the simplex. The derivative perturbation from curvature has operator norm at most M2 d/q; the difference of two endpoint errors contributes at most 2 sqrt(d) eta q. Hence

    ellhat = ell + M2 d/q + 2 sqrt(d) eta q                (16)

is a global Lipschitz bound for the interpolated residual. The argument applies across simplex boundaries because the interpolant is continuous and has these bounds on each segment of a line.

Accept when

    C max(ell,ellhat)<1,      J max(ell,ellhat)<1.            (17)

Equations (10)-(17) then give a sound certificate for the entire compatible family, not just a fitted representative. Source uncertainty and numerical approximation uncertainty remain in the same delta and D.

The concrete programs use a rational Frobenius upper bound for beta, which is sufficient for their records. A complete acquisition algorithm uses an operator-norm upper estimate of arbitrarily small rational excess; otherwise a fixed Frobenius relaxation could fail to certify some members of the promised operator-norm class.

## 7. Completeness, stopping, and the actual acquisition cost

Let L_* be the true residual Lipschitz constant and M_*=max(C,J). Suppose

    rho := 1-M_* L_* >0.                                  (18)

This is the strict class under study. Soundness of a returned certificate does not require a known rho: the program checks (17) directly. A guaranteed finite a priori budget requires a lower bound on rho. Without one, there is no asserted uniform budget over sources arbitrarily close to the certification boundary.

If the operator-norm estimator has rational excess at most tau_norm, its returned ellhat satisfies

    ellhat <= L_* + sqrt(d) M3/(3q^2)
                   +4 sqrt(d) eta q
                   +M2(d+sqrt(d)/2)/q +tau_norm.            (19)

For example, make each of the four additional terms <=rho/(8M_*). Together with the unwrap requirements, sufficient choices are

    q^2 >= 8 M_* sqrt(d) M3/(3 rho),
    q >= 8 M_* M2(d+sqrt(d)/2)/rho,
    eta <= rho/(32 M_* sqrt(d) q),
    tau_norm <= rho/(8 M_*).

For desired source-orbit error epsilon, also impose

    q^2 >= 2 C M2 d/(epsilon rho),
    eta <= epsilon rho/(4 C).                              (20)

Then the checked margin is at least rho/2 and D<=epsilon. Zero M2 or M3 causes no difficulty: the corresponding requirement is absent. Irrational square roots in planning can be replaced by rational upper bounds. q may be rounded upward to a power of two.

A uniform adaptive version doubles q and requests eta<=q^(-3), with tau_norm tending to zero, until (17) and the requested D bound pass. It terminates for every source satisfying (18) when that refinement is genuinely available. The condition-dependent budget is bounded by the preceding display, within a constant geometric factor for the earlier grids. It is not claimed to terminate with a positive conclusion outside the class. A finite-budget run may truthfully return 'not certified'; this is not a proof of nonhyperbolicity.

A single level uses at most

    Q_native <= q^d + 1+d(M_loop-1),                        (21)

with overlapping reports reused. It returns at most d Q_native scalar reports, each with the supplied precision. Each input coordinate has O(log q+log M_loop) bits. The observation data size is O(d Q_native p) for p-bit reports. Storage for the surrogate is O(d q^d[p+log(dq(B+1))]) bits. The exponential dependence on component dimension is real.

The total physical acquisition cost, if a physical instrument is supplied, is the sum over these actual queries of its preparation, update, read and calibration costs. Nothing in (21) licenses a free reset, precision refinement, hidden state measurement, or instantaneous actuation. Our executions below are software experiments, so there is no claimed hardware energy or settling time.

### Digital preprocessing

Let H=ceil(log2(2+max|A_ij|)). The characteristic polynomial has degree d and coefficient bits O(d[H+log d]). Deterministic Sturm-Habicht/root-isolation methods give finite polynomial bit cost for this step; a conservative classical isolation bound is soft-O(d^6+d^4 h^2) in degree d and coefficient bits h, obtained by padding the coefficient-bit parameter to at least d when using a soft-O(d^4 h^2) statement. Multiplicities can also be computed. Subsequent interval bisection and sign evaluation at precision r use at most O(d^3 r) polynomial arithmetic operations on integers of O(d[h+r+log d]) bits. Thus even a deliberately loose schoolbook bound O(d^6 r[h+r+log d]^3), in addition to root isolation, accounts for the spectral refinement. No root or eigenvalue oracle is being assumed.

For the grid norms, an especially simple complete implementation bisects beta and tests beta^2 I-Dhat^T Dhat for positive semidefiniteness by all principal minors. It is not the fastest implementation, but gives an explicit finite bound. With r_norm bisections and W bounding report, grid, trial-bound and supplied-rational bit lengths, this costs

    O(q^d 2^d d^6 r_norm W^3)

bit operations under conservative exact integer/rational arithmetic accounting. A polynomial-time exact PSD procedure can replace the principal-minor enumeration. The executed Frobenius shortcut is cheaper and sound for its accepted records.

Grid unwrapping, differences, and rational certificate arithmetic are polynomial in the explicit grid size and bit length. Large B, small spectral or derivative gaps, and the requested location accuracy enter q, eta and therefore the native costs. The method is not claimed polynomial in dimension or in the binary length of inverse physical tolerances.

## 8. Exact all-period laws and later periodic witnesses

Since F0 is translation-conjugate to A, equations (7)-(10) give

    # Fix(F^m) = |det(A^m-I)|,        every m>=1,            (22)
    h_top(F) = sum_(|lambda|>1) log |lambda|.                (23)

Hyperbolic toral automorphisms are topologically mixing and have dense periodic points; these qualitative properties transfer by conjugacy. The count in (22) is for points fixed by the m-th iterate, including shorter periods. Exact least-period point counts are

    P_m = sum_(q divides m) mu(m/q) |det(A^q-I)|,

and the number of distinct least-period m orbits is P_m/m. This use of Möbius inversion is justified because (22) is an equality. Divisor enumeration or factorization work is charged.

### 8.1 A later address

Given an integer period m and a label k in Z^d/(A^m-I)Z^d, form

    s_m = sum_(j=0 to m-1) A^j b,
    x0 = (A^m-I)^(-1)(k-s_m).                              (24)

The affine orbit X_t=F0^t(x0) closes after m steps modulo Z^d. Different cosets label different points of Fix(F0^m). A supplied integer representative k is enough for one task; full Hermite/Smith normal form enumeration is optional and must pay its output cost.

On the m-periodic sequences solve

    u_(t+1)-A u_t = I_q ghat(X_t+u_t),    u_m=u_0.          (25)

The inverse G_m of the cyclic linear difference operator is the restriction of (4) to periodic sequences. Thus ||G_m||<=C independently of m. The fixed-point map in (25) contracts with tau=C ellhat. It gives one unique surrogate periodic point for each label; all surrogate periodic points are covered. Conjugacy preserves least period.

An exact rational way to evaluate G_m r is

    u0=(I-A^m)^(-1) sum_(j=0 to m-1) A^(m-1-j) r_j,
    u_(t+1)=A u_t+r_t.                                    (26)

Apparent unstable cancellations in (26) are handled by finite integer/rational arithmetic. They are not dismissed as constant-cost real arithmetic.

### 8.2 Finite precision and runtime

Round the correction to a fixed dyadic grid after each iteration. If per-iteration vector rounding is at most (1-tau) epsilon_alg/8, contraction controls its accumulated contribution. Starting at zero, O((1-tau)^(-1) log[(1+C||ghat||)/epsilon_alg]) iterations suffice. A rational a posteriori bound is

    ||u-u_*|| <= ||u-Phi(u)||/(1-tau).                     (27)

The resulting approximate orbit has, for every compatible true source, a corresponding exact source orbit at distance at most

    D + epsilon_alg,                                      (28)

uniformly over the orbit and its repetitions. The irreducible source uncertainty D and the controllable arithmetic error epsilon_alg are kept separate.

Let K be the input label bit length, p the surrogate coefficient bits, and H the A-entry bits. Rational base-orbit numerators and denominators have a conservative bound O(K+p+dm[H+log d]) bits. If r bits are used for the rounded corrections, a surrogate evaluation and cyclic solve have bit length polynomial in K+p+r+dm[H+log d]; fixed rounding prevents denominator growth from multiplying indefinitely across iterations. Precompute A^m and (I-A^m)^(-1) once. Each iteration then uses O(md^2+md log d) arithmetic/comparison operations for the grid surrogate and cyclic solve, on those finite-width integers/rationals. With schoolbook arithmetic, multiplying this operation count by a conservative cubic bound in intermediate bit length gives a complete bit-cost upper bound. The dependence on (1-tau)^(-1) and on every later coupling-evaluation cost is retained.

The cost is polynomial in the numeric period m, dimension, finite representation size and log(1/epsilon_alg), apart from the explicit margin factor. It is not asserted polynomial in log m for an expanded m-point output. Even the exact integer count (22) has O(dm[H+log d]) bits, and enumerating all its points can be exponentially large. Finding an unknown optimum among periodic addresses is not supplied by this address compiler.

## 9. Later nonlinear interventions and large component networks

### 9.1 A later stationary kick

For any periodic Lipschitz v, define F_v=F+v and Fhat_v=Fhat+v modulo Z^d. If

    C(max(ell,ellhat)+nu)<1,
    J(max(ell,ellhat)+nu)<1,       Lip(v)<=nu,               (29)

the entire construction remains valid, with

    D_v = C delta/[1-C(ell+nu)].                           (30)

The exact counts and entropy remain (22)-(23) for every subsequently selected v in this class. The implementation uses a finite rational periodic piecewise affine description for v, so its evaluation cost and coefficient heights are explicit. More general computable v require their actual evaluation/error contract; no free nonlinear oracle is inferred.

If an implemented stationary coupling v_true differs from a represented v_hat by at most kappa, and both meet their stated Lipschitz bounds, replace delta by delta+kappa and use the corresponding total derivative margins. Arbitrary time-varying disturbances do not define the same stationary map and do not inherit exact invariant counts merely from a C0 amplitude bound.

### 9.2 Modular theorem

Acquire components F_i:T^(d_i)->T^(d_i) independently. Their certificates supply A_i,b_i,ell_i,ellhat_i,delta_i,C_i,J_i. Consider the declared network update

    F_net,i(x) = F_i(x_i) + v_i(x) modulo Z^(d_i).          (31)

This is an explicit intervention grammar: the uncoupled component one-step laws remain stationary, and the added term depends on the current declared network state. A physical connection that changes the local laws in an unmodelled way does not satisfy (31).

Use the block norm ||x||_b=max_i ||x_i||_2. If

    ||v_i(x)-v_i(y)||_2 <= sum_j nu_ij ||x_j-y_j||_2,

then Lip_b(v)<=nu=max_i sum_j nu_ij. Let

    C=max_i C_i,  J=max_i J_i,
    ell=max_i ell_i,  ellhat=max_i ellhat_i,
    delta=max_i delta_i.

The block-diagonal Green operator has norm at most max_i C_i in this norm, and ||A^(-1)||_b<=J. Therefore (29) proves a network conjugacy and the per-component error bound

    max_i dist(F_net^t(Kx)_i,Fhat_net^t(x)_i)
        <= C delta/[1-C(ell+nu)],      every integer t.     (32)

No square-root-of-network-size factor is needed. This is a bound in the declared block norm; converting it to whole-network Euclidean distance can introduce sqrt(number of components).

For every m>=1,

    # Fix(F_net^m) = product_i |det(A_i^m-I)|,              (33)
    h_top(F_net) = sum_i h_top(A_i).                        (34)

The network can be nonlinear, nonreciprocal and coupled on any finite graph satisfying the block-row budget. These conclusions do not require monotone dynamics or convergence to an equilibrium.

Acquisition costs sum: Q_net=sum_i Q_i. With bounded component dimension and uniform precision, regularity and margin parameters, this is linear in the number of components. The finite representation is the list of component surrogates and the later coupling program. A periodic address is a list of local affine labels. Applying the block cyclic Green operators and evaluating the finite coupling program implements the network version of (25). Computational costs are polynomial in the explicit number of components, requested period, coupling description, arithmetic precision and the inverse margin. A dense or expensive coupling description pays its actual size and evaluation cost.

This is the main breadth deduction: a component-level finite experimental record can determine exact extensive chaotic laws and all-period orbit witnesses for an arbitrarily large later network in the stated weak-coupling regime. A joint sampling grid exponential in the total network dimension is avoided because the composition law is supplied and checked, not because unknown interactions were silently inferred.

## 10. Other consequences and their limits

### Long-run observable ranges

Conjugacy maps the whole set of Fhat-invariant probability measures bijectively onto the set of F-invariant measures. For any observable f with known Lipschitz constant L_f, matched measures have integrals differing by at most L_f D. Consequently the extrema of the integral over all invariant measures differ by at most L_f D. The same bound applies to extrema of finite-time orbit averages over all initial states, uniformly in the averaging horizon. These are stability statements. No efficient optimizer for an arbitrary observable or invariant measure is smuggled in.

### Pseudo-orbits

If a bounded-error numerical sequence satisfies Y_(k+1)=F~(Y_k)+r_k with ||r||<=zeta, the fixed-point equation

    u=G(g(Y+u)-g(Y)-r)

produces a true orbit within C zeta/(1-C ell). A finite sequence may be extended by exact source orbits before and after its error interval for the existence argument. This is shadowing with a changed initial condition and can depend on future errors. It is not a causal physical controller or a same-initial-condition forecast.

### Metric information is still unidentified

Even exact finitely many one-step point values do not fix local expansion rates. Start with a cat map on T^2 and any finite query set. Choose a smooth bump supported in a sufficiently small neighborhood of its fixed point at zero, with no other distinct query in that neighborhood. A perturbation epsilon chi(x) x_1 e_1 vanishes at zero and at every other query while changing the derivative at zero from A to A+epsilon E_11. With positive slack in B,M2,M3 and the conjugacy margin, choose epsilon small enough to preserve every supplied bound. The unstable eigenvalue at that fixed point changes, hence its Lyapunov exponent changes, while entropy and every count (22) remain unchanged.

This witness requires slack in the regularity guards; for example M2=0 would rule out such a bump. It preserves the correct logical distinction: the acquired record can determine a topological class without determining metric expansion rates or a physically selected invariant distribution. The wider smooth-rigidity literature also requires additional hypotheses to upgrade a topological conjugacy to a smooth one.

### What the domain excludes

The theorem is not for arbitrary turbulent flows, hidden-state port processes, arbitrary nonstationarity, or unrestricted nonlinear feedback. In particular, the time-t map of a globally defined smooth flow on the same torus is isotopic to the identity and induces A=I. It therefore is not an example of the hyperbolic homology class used here. Return maps, discrete updates, or a separately justified suspension construction are different matters; no physical realization bridge is asserted in this report.
