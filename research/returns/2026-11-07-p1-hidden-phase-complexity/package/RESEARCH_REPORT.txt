A complete finite-bit construction, phase census, and constrained-selection theorem

0. ENDPOINT, RESULT, AND CONTRACT

Question resolved here:
Can an exactly known nonlinear port law, together with its exact matched global
ordering laws and worst full-system linear-response certificates, still conceal
extensive, robust nonlinear phase structure and computationally hard phase
selection? Resolve this within one uniformly bounded, finite, rational,
connected, reciprocal, attractive cubic source class.

Answer: yes, by the explicit construction below. For every known hidden size n
and every k=1,...,n there are sources with precisely the same entire calibration
input/output map, yet exactly 3^k equilibria after the same gain change, of which
2^k are stable minima. The number of equilibria with Morse index j is
binomial(k,j) 2^(k-j). All are uniformly nondegenerate. The minima have a
dimension-independent local contraction margin, basin radius, and escape-energy
barrier. An additional subfamily embeds every finite graph and has 3^n
equilibria with the same census. Its one-fixed-mean global minimum recovers
minimum bisection at inverse-polynomial energy accuracy. The corresponding
rational threshold decision is NP-complete.

There is a constructive positive counterpart: given the rational internal
model and any ternary branch label, a uniform rational contraction/bisection
algorithm computes that individual equilibrium, with a certified error and
polynomial bit complexity. Its input includes the internal model. Port data
alone cannot supply that missing information. Listing every equilibrium has
an exponential output size; compact branchwise representation does not solve
constrained global selection.

This is a complete structural result for the stated class. It is not a general
classification of all nonlinear systems, a new physical memory writer, a
finite-temperature lifetime calculation, or a resolution of P versus NP.
The results established by earlier accepted work remain at their original
contracts. The previous ZIP and all held source payloads were not accessed,
recovered, copied, embedded, or retransferred for this return.

Observation contract:
  * One collocated root port; exact initial state zero.
  * Scalar root actuation and observation, with arbitrary locally bounded
    inputs over any finite duration. Arbitrary adaptive protocols are allowed.
  * Mathematical conclusions remain true if the observer is additionally

  * Exact input/output is a stronger information grant than a finite paid

1. COMMON RATIONAL SOURCE FAMILY

For n>=1 set
  p = ceil(sqrt(n)),  a = 1/(10p).
Let L be a symmetric weighted graph Laplacian on n hidden nodes:
  L 1 = 0,  L_ij <= 0 for i!=j,  L >= 0.
All examples below have ||L||_2 <= 3. Define

                 [ 4       -a 1^T ]
  K_L          = [                  ].
                 [ -a 1      I+L  ]

The calibration system is
  dX/dt = -K_L X - X^(componentwise cube) + e_0 u,
  y = X_0 = z,   X(0)=0,   X=(z,x).

Every physical coefficient is rational. For the partition and graph families
below, each coefficient requires O(log n) bits and the dense source requires
O(n^2 log n) bits. The off-diagonal graph of K_L is connected because each
hidden node connects to the root, even when L is disconnected. All couplings
are attractive and reciprocal.

Since a sqrt(n)<=1/10,
  lambda_min(K_L) >= 9/10,
  ||K_L||_2 <= 41/10.
These follow by splitting off the off-diagonal root/hidden coupling, whose
operator norm is a sqrt(n), from diag(4,I+L). Also
  a sqrt(n) >= 1/20.
Thus aggregate port coupling does not tend to zero. The construction does not
rely on an unknown dimension, an arbitrarily small total port coupling, an
unbounded operator norm, or a singular calibration source.

The subspace x=s 1 is invariant. Its exact equations are
  dz/dt = -4z + an s - z^3 + u,
  ds/dt = az - s - s^3.                                  (1)

They do not contain L. Uniqueness of the ODE solution proves that every allowed
source has the same entire root input/output map from zero. Coupling two
adaptive protocols using the same private random seed and read-noise history
shows that they also have the same transcript and output law. This is an
all-input/all-time identity, not a finite-order expansion or empirical fit.

An orthonormal representation uses V=(e_0,(0,1,...,1)/sqrt(n)):
  Q = [[4,-a sqrt(n)],[-a sqrt(n),1]],
  reduced cubic coefficients = (1,1/n).
The rational row-coordinate representation is
  R = [[4,-an],[-a,1]].
The identity K_L T = T R with T=(e_0,(0,1,...,1)) avoids unnecessary irrational
arithmetic. The normalized square root is an exactly represented algebraic
quantity, not a precision oracle.

N594/N595 and the accepted collective-law theorem are credited as capital:

2. THE COMMON POST-GAIN ENERGY AND A GLOBAL EQUILIBRIUM BOUND

At common gain mu=2 and zero root drive the energy is
  E_L(z,x) = z^2 + z^4/4
           + sum_i (x_i^4/4 - x_i^2/2)
           + (1/2) x^T L x - a z sum_i x_i.                (2)
The dynamics are dX/dt=-grad E_L. Its quartic leading part is positive, so E is
coercive. Its stationary equations are
  2z+z^3 = a sum_i x_i,
  x_i^3-x_i+(Lx)_i-az = 0.                                (3)

At any real equilibrium, put U=max_i |x_i|. If U=0 then z=0. Otherwise choose
a coordinate attaining U and multiply its equation by its sign. The graph
Laplacian term is nonnegative there. Consequently
  |z| <= anU/2,
  U^3-U <= a|z| <= a^2 n U/2.
It follows that
  U^2 <= 1+a^2 n/2 <= 201/200,
  U < 101/100,
  |az| < 101/20000.                                       (4)

These are bounds for every equilibrium, including saddles. They establish the
global part of the census proof; later contraction boxes do not omit distant
critical points.

Define three disjoint branch intervals
  I_-=[-11/10,-9/10],
  I_0=[-1/10,1/10],
  I_+=[9/10,11/10].
On either outer interval, h(t)=t^3-t is increasing with h'>=143/100.
On I_0 it is decreasing with |h'|>=97/100. Each interval contains a unique
inverse branch psi_sigma for all |h|<=11/400 and for a slightly larger
neighborhood. All three inverse branches have Lipschitz constant <=100/97.

Write z(x) for the unique real solution of 2z+z^3=a sum x. Its derivative as
a function of its scalar right side is at most 1/2.

3. WEAK ARBITRARY GRAPHS: A COMPLETE 3^n CRITICAL-POINT CENSUS

Assume L has maximum weighted degree D<=1/100. Then
  ||L||_infinity <= 2D <= 1/50,
  ||L||_2 <= 2D <= 1/50.

For a ternary label sigma in {-,0,+}^n define, on the product of its branch
intervals,
  [T_sigma(x)]_i = psi_sigma_i( a z(x) - (Lx)_i ).          (5)

Since max |x_i|<=11/10 on this box,
  |a z(x) - (Lx)_i|
      <= (a^2 n/2 + 2D) * 11/10
      <= 11/400.
Thus T maps its box into itself. Moreover
  Lip_infinity(T_sigma)
      <= (100/97) (a^2 n/2 + 2D)
      <= 5/194 < 1/32.                                    (6)

Banach contraction gives exactly one equilibrium for each ternary label.

Completeness:
At any equilibrium (4) and the degree bound give
  |x_i^3-x_i| <= 2DU+|az| < 101/4000.
For 1/10<=t<=9/10, t-t^3 >=99/1000>101/4000.
Hence every coordinate of every equilibrium belongs to one of I_-,I_0,I_+.
It is therefore a fixed point of one of the maps (5). No other real
equilibria exist.

Morse index:
The Hessian is
  H = [[2+3z^2, -a 1^T],
       [-a 1, diag(3x_i^2-1)+L]].                         (7)
At a branch equilibrium, each middle coordinate has diagonal curvature
at most -97/100, and each outer coordinate has curvature at least 143/100.
The root diagonal is at least 2. The off-diagonal root block plus L is an
operator-norm perturbation of size at most 1/10+1/50=3/25.
Weyl's eigenvalue inequalities therefore preserve the signs of all eigenvalues:
  negative eigenvalues <= -17/20,
  positive eigenvalues >= 131/100.
In particular every critical point is nondegenerate, and its Morse index is
exactly the number of middle labels.

Consequences:
  total equilibria        = 3^n;
  minima                  = 2^n;
  index-j equilibria      = binomial(n,j) 2^(n-j);
  distance of Hessian spectrum from zero >=17/20.

An index-j equilibrium has exactly j growing linear modes under negative
gradient flow. The root direction is always among the stable directions;
the largest possible index is n, not n+1.

4. ONE EXACT PORT LAW WITH EVERY CENSUS 3^k, k=1,...,n

Partition the n hidden nodes into k nonempty groups C_j of sizes m_j.
Let
  L_P = 3 blockdiag_j( I_mj - (1/mj) 11^T ).               (8)
This is a rational graph Laplacian with norm at most 3. Every such partition
has exactly the same calibration equations (1), quotient Q, node count,
matched global laws, and worst linear-response certificates.

First prove that every equilibrium is constant inside each group. In a
group write x=b 1+d with sum d=0. Project the hidden stationarity equation
onto the zero-sum subspace and take its inner product with d:
  0 = 2||d||^2 + sum_i (x_i-b)(x_i^3-b^3).
Every term in the sum is nonnegative because the cube is increasing.
Therefore d=0.

The remaining equations are
  2z+z^3 = a sum_j m_j b_j,
  b_j^3-b_j = az.                                         (9)
For any of the 3^k branch labels, the same inverse-branch construction has
contraction constant
  (100/97)(a^2 n/2) <=1/194 <1/32.
It maps the box into itself. Bound (4) and |az|<101/20000 ensure all
equilibria have such labels. There are exactly 3^k equilibria.

For the Hessian, the m_j-1 within-group directions have eigenvalue
3b_j^2-1+3 >=2. The remaining k+1 dimensional matrix has diagonal
(2+3z^2, 3b_1^2-1, ..., 3b_k^2-1)
and root coupling vector (-a sqrt(m_j))_j, whose norm is at most 1/10.
Its inertia is again exactly the number of middle branches, and its
eigenvalues stay at least 87/100 from zero. Thus the uniform bound 17/20
is valid for both families.

For every k=1,...,n, within the same observational equivalence class:
  total equilibria = 3^k;
  stable minima = 2^k;
  index-j equilibria = binomial(k,j) 2^(k-j).

At the origin the Morse index is k. This explicitly separates the quotient's
correct leading growth-rate certificate from a claim to recover the whole
unstable spectrum or its dimension.

These are realized values, not a claim that powers of two are the only
possible minimum counts for arbitrary L with norm<=3.

5. UNIFORM WELL MARGINS, BASINS, AND ENERGY BARRIERS

At an outer-branch equilibrium, |x_i|>49/50. Indeed the bound on
|x_i^3-x_i| is less than 101/4000, whereas
  49/50-(49/50)^3 >101/4000,
and t-t^3 decreases on [9/10,49/50]. In the partition case the still
smaller bound |b_j^3-b_j|<101/20000 suffices. Combining with (4),
  49/50 < |x_i| <101/100 at every minimum.                  (10)

On any entire outer sign strip
  C_s = { (z,x): z real, 9/10<=s_i x_i<=11/10 for all i },
the Hessian is at least (7/5) I. To see this, subtract (7/5)I from (7).
Its root entry is >=3/5 and its hidden block is >=(3/100)I+L.
The squared root-coupling norm is <=1/100, while
  (3/5)(3/100)=9/500>1/100.
The Schur complement is strictly positive. This argument uses L>=0 and
works for both families.

Every minimum is therefore strongly stable, with a full Euclidean ball
of radius
  r0 = 2/25
inside its sign strip. For gradient flow from that ball,
  ||X(t)-X_*|| <= exp(-7t/5) ||X(0)-X_*||.                 (11)
For an additive disturbance with full vector norm at most eta,
  ||X(t)-X_*||
      <= exp(-7t/5)||X(0)-X_*||
         +(5eta/7)(1-exp(-7t/5)),                         (12)
provided eta<=(7/5)r0 and the initial state is in the ball.
The comparison inequality proves the ball remains invariant.

This uses a bound on the total Euclidean disturbance, not a free
dimension-independent bound on every individual node's noise.

Any continuous path to another minimum must leave the original sign
strip. Its first exit is at distance at least r0 from X_*. Strong convexity
gives the escape-energy lower bound
  barrier >= (7/10) r0^2
           = 28/6250 >1/250.                             (13)

These are deterministic energy and local dynamical statements. A thermal
switching time additionally needs a stochastic forcing law, mobility, and
other physical parameters. No such lifetime is inferred. No basin-volume
fraction under a declared preparation distribution is inferred.

The numerous equilibria are robust wells and saddles, not approximate
stationary points, numerical near-degeneracies, or merely slow transients.

6. CERTIFIED COMPUTATION OF ANY SUPPLIED PHASE ADDRESS

Input:
  * a weak rational Laplacian of degree<=1/100 and a ternary label of
    length n; or a partition list and one label per group;
  * rational 0<epsilon<=1/100;
  * all source coefficients, labels and precision data in binary.

Output:
  a rational state with ||X_hat-X_*||_2<=epsilon and the exact Morse index
  of the named unique critical point. For explicit weak-graph input, all
  physical coordinates are returned. Binary group sizes permit a compact
  output (root, group amplitudes, multiplicities) representing that state.
  Expanding this compact output to n+1 coordinates is optional and charged
  separately. For a minimum, an exact rational residual/energy certificate
  is available as below.

Algorithm:
  1. Check the rational Laplacian, row-sum, sign and degree conditions, or
     validate the supplied partition. Compute p and a by integer arithmetic.
  2. Set R=11an/20 and delta=epsilon/[32(n+1)].
     Start each leaf/group at its label -1,0,+1.
  3. Let T=ceil(log2(1/delta)).
     Repeat T times:
       a. Bisect 2z+z^3=a sum_j m_j x_j on [-R,R] to absolute error delta.
       b. Form t_i=a z-(Lx)_i by rational arithmetic, with L=0 in the
          group-amplitude problem.
       c. Bisect x^3-x=t_i on the named branch interval to error delta.
     All comparisons are exact rational comparisons. The middle branch is
     decreasing; its bisection orientation is reversed.
  4. Recompute the root to error delta. Return group amplitudes and their
     multiplicities compactly, or expand them if explicit coordinates are
     requested and the corresponding output cost is charged.

One approximate map evaluation differs from the exact T_sigma by at most
2delta in maximum norm. Indeed root inversion has error delta, its effect
on a leaf inverse is at most (100/97)a delta, and leaf bisection adds delta.
All inverse evaluations remain in their warranted branch intervals.

With q=1/32, the final hidden maximum-norm error is at most
  e = (1/5) q^T + 2delta/(1-q) <3delta.
The root error is at most an e/2+delta. Hence a rational Euclidean-error
upper bound is
  n e + an e/2 + delta <4(n+1)delta <epsilon.               (14)
No real-number oracle, exact algebraic solver, or asymptotically unspecified
precision call appears in this construction.

Complete bit-cost bound:
Let B>=1 be the complete binary input length. Let d be the number of
branch coordinates: d=n for explicit weak-graph input, d=k for a compact
partition input. Define
  b = ceil(log2((R+2)/delta)) + 2,
  W = 64[B+b+ceil(log2(n+1))+10].
Each scalar bisection uses at most b steps. Products of input denominators
have total bit length at most B. Each new branch midpoint lies on the
fixed dyadic subdivision of a rational interval with denominator 10;
each root midpoint lies on a dyadic subdivision of [-R,R].
Consequently denominators do not accumulate across outer iterations.
All required numerators, denominators and intermediate comparison integers
can be kept within W bits using common-denominator arithmetic.

A conservative schoolbook integer-arithmetic implementation is bounded by
  10^6 (T+1)(d+1)^2(b+1)W^3 elementary bit operations,       (15)
with polynomial storage O((d+1)^2 W + (d+1)b).
The bound covers validation, denominator preparation, input reading,
scalar comparisons, all matrix products and the output. It is deliberately
loose. In asymptotic form it is polynomial in the full binary input length
and log(1/epsilon): d<=B, and log n is included in the input length even
when binary group sizes encode a large physical population. Dependence on
graph size and all auxiliary work is included. An explicit expanded output
costs an additional O(nW) bit operations and output bits. For a compact
partition input, n can be exponential in B, so that expansion is NOT
claimed to have polynomial bit cost in B. The reference implementation
uses Python exact Fractions, whose integer arithmetic also obeys a
polynomial bound, and defaults to compact output for partition input.

For a minimum and any rational X_hat in its outer sign strip:
  ||X_hat-X_*||_2 <= (5/7)||grad E(X_hat)||_2
                  <= (5/7)||grad E(X_hat)||_1,
  E(X_hat)-E(X_*) <= (5/14)||grad E(X_hat)||_2^2.           (16)
Both right sides can be certified using exact rational arithmetic; the
squared-gradient formula has no square-root issue.

The polynomial operation computes a supplied phase. Choosing the best
phase under an additional constraint is a different task. Explicitly
listing all 2^n minima or all 3^n critical points requires exponential
output. A compact input plus branch compiler is already an implicit
description of them, and does not provide an optimal-address oracle.

7. ONE FIXED MEAN EMBEDS MINIMUM BISECTION

Let n>=2 be even and G any unweighted simple graph on n vertices. Set
  lambda = 1/(64 n^4),   L=lambda L_G.
Its weighted maximum degree is <=lambda(n-1)<1/100, so every result about
weak graphs applies. The source is connected through the root, attractive,
reciprocal, uniformly bounded and rational with O(log n) coefficient bits.

Ask for the global minimum of (2) under the single constraint
  sum_i x_i=0.
The root's unique minimizer is then z=0. Adding the known constant n/4
gives the task
  V_*(G) = min_{sum x_i=0} V_G(x),
  V_G(x) = (1/4) sum_i (x_i^2-1)^2
           +(lambda/2) x^T L_G x.                         (17)

Let b(G) be the minimum number of crossing edges in a balanced +/-1
assignment. For any balanced s,
  V_G(s)=2lambda cut_G(s).
Let Delta be G's maximum degree. We prove the finite-parameter bound
  2lambda b(G)-4lambda^2 n Delta^2
       <= V_*(G) <=2lambda b(G).                          (18)

Upper bound:
A minimizing balanced cut is feasible; also b(G)<=nDelta/2, so
V_*<=lambda nDelta.

Rounding a continuous minimizer:
The on-site term gives
  sum_i(x_i^2-1)^2<=4lambda nDelta.
No coordinate vanishes, since that would cost at least 1/4, exceeding the
displayed upper bound. For s_i=sign x_i,
  ||x-s||_2<=2sqrt(lambda nDelta).
Because sum x_i=0,
  |sum s_i|<=2n sqrt(lambda Delta)<2.
The sum of an even number of signs is an even integer, so it is zero.
Thus the rounded configuration is balanced.

Lower bound:
Write d=x-s. For every coordinate,
  (x_i^2-1)^2=(|x_i|-1)^2(|x_i|+1)^2>=d_i^2.
Positive semidefiniteness of L_G then gives
  V_G(x) >= 2lambda cut_G(s)
          +lambda s^T L_G d +(1/4)||d||^2
         >= 2lambda cut_G(s)-lambda^2||L_Gs||^2
         >= 2lambda b(G)-4lambda^2 nDelta^2.
This proves (18).

Set D_E=4lambda^2 nDelta^2. Then
  D_E/lambda=4lambda nDelta^2<=1/(16n)<=1/32.
An absolute approximation V_hat to V_* with error at most lambda/4
recovers b(G) by rounding V_hat/(2lambda) to the nearest integer. The
required accuracy is 1/(256 n^4), with O(log n) accuracy bits. There is
no exponentially fine precision hidden in this reduction.

The sign of every exact continuous minimizer is an optimal bisection.
The same holds for any exactly feasible configuration with
  V_G(x)<=V_*+lambda/4.
For the latter, the on-site estimate still makes sign rounding balanced:
  |sum sign(x_i)|^2 <= n[4lambda nDelta+lambda] <4.
If its cut were at least b(G)+1, the lower bound above would exceed
2lambda b(G)+lambda/4, a contradiction.

NP-complete finite rational decision:
For an integer threshold h, consider
  V_*(G) <= (2h+1)lambda.                                 (19)
The gap in (18) makes (19) equivalent to b(G)<=h.
This belongs to NP because a balanced cut is a polynomial witness, and
it is NP-hard by the classical MaxCut reduction:

  Given G0 on v vertices, append v isolated vertices, obtaining H on 2v
  vertices. Every cut of G0 extends to a balanced cut of H. Let J be the
  complement of H. Every balanced bipartition has v^2 crossing pairs,
  hence
      b(J)=v^2-MaxCut(G0).

Constructing J and K_lambda L_J takes polynomial bit work and O(v^2 log v)
output bits. This classical balanced-cut route is credited to the graph
complexity literature; it is not claimed as a new combinatorial reduction.

Therefore a uniform deterministic polynomial-bit algorithm for these
constrained energies or witnesses at the stated accuracy would imply P=NP.
This implication is not a proof that such an algorithm cannot exist.

All the phase-separation graphs at a given n retain precisely the same
root calibration law. Thus the information obstruction survives even if
computation is unlimited. Conversely, supplying the full graph removes
that information obstruction but leaves the standard computational one.

8. SCIENTIFIC CONSEQUENCES AND WHAT THEY DO NOT IMPLY

A. Exact response agreement does not bound hidden phase complexity.
The root law, known n, homogeneous cubic coefficient, reciprocal signs,
uniform calibration conditioning, matched global order and worst response
are all the same. The landscape can nevertheless have 2 or 2^n stable
phases, and every 2^k count in between in the constructed partition family.
The critical-point census and instability dimension vary just as strongly.

B. The ambiguity is robust in the landscape itself.
The Hessian gap, stable-well contraction, basin ball and energy barrier are
uniform. The result does not require declaring nearly stationary numerics
to be separate phases or exploiting a vanishing local restoring force.
Exact observational agreement is supplied by an invariant subspace, not
by finite measurement resolution.

C. No encoding or solver can infer a task that varies within one exact
observational equivalence class.
A sound interval for the number of minima over this class must include
both 2 and 2^n. A randomized exact-count procedure has worst-case success
at most 1/n on the n indistinguishable partition alternatives. Two
alternatives already prevent simultaneous success greater than 1/2.
Finite paid noisy observations cannot improve on the stronger exact
input/output information grant.

D. Attractive interactions do not by themselves make every equilibrium
task easy.
The previous convex global-order law remains valid without the new mean
constraint. The fixed signed mean is different from a block power
constraint, and the power-coordinate convexity does not carry over.
Here this one constraint exposes a balanced graph-cut task at
inverse-polynomial energy accuracy.

E. A reusable phase representation and global phase selection are separate.
For the supplied weak model, every branch has a polynomial-bit
equilibrium compiler and a transparent index certificate. The entire
exponential landscape has a compact generative description. Its optimal
balanced address remains NP-complete to select under the stated input.
Neither implicit representation nor local stability supplies unknown
ground-state search.
All deterministic root-only trajectories from the declared zero preparation
remain in the synchronous subspace. The nonsynchronous minima are therefore
unreachable through that interface. Their number is a landscape census,
not an operationally accessible memory capacity.

F. Extra information must be named and charged.
Off-synchrony preparations, hidden-node measurements, heterogeneous
interventions or known hidden stochastic forcing might distinguish these
models. This report does not show that such richer channels are
insufficient, and does not acquire them at no cost.

G. Operational and physical claims keep their earlier contracts.
There is no new hardware actuation protocol, acquisition budget, supplied
equilibration oracle, thermal lifetime, or global basin-preparation
guarantee. Mathematical control bounds (11)-(12) start in the stated
well. The earlier completed writer and causal-model operations remain
available under their separate interface, noise, power and timing premises.

This completes the stated structural endpoint. It establishes a sharp
separation in task content, with explicit exponential phase censuses and a
finite-bit computational embedding. It does not assert that all scientific
inference is impossible or that a universal upper bound has been found for
the number of minima in every attractive system.
