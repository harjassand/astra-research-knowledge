FINITE EXPERIMENTS FOR UNLIMITED-HORIZON CAUSAL PROTOCOLS
Complete construction, proofs, information boundary and resource contract
7 November 2026

STATUS AND ENDPOINT

The resolved question is robust verification and synthesis of arbitrary
finite-state temporal protocols from an acquired, uniformly accurate causal
input/output representation. The source can have unobserved memory and an
infinite-dimensional realization. The delivered decision operation includes a
quantified robustness gap. It does not decide exact zero-margin properties of
arbitrary physical systems.

There are four distinct results here:

  A. A horizon-independent, two-sided trace inclusion for EVERY causal
     observation-feedback policy. It gives sound controllers, counterstrategies
     against robust guarantees, and an accuracy-epsilon estimate of optimal
     read-noise tolerance for any supplied finite-state temporal requirement.

  B. Complete finite acquisition of the needed representation for nonlinear
     fading-memory sources, with a matching worst-case reading/storage lower
     bound in retained memory length.

  C. A compressed, paid port-acquisition construction for the retained linear
     dissipative component class, including an actual first-order actuator,
     uncertain preparation, finite relaxation, finite sensor precision, and a
     positive interval for controller computation.

  D. An executed three-port, 256-hidden-state acquisition and repeated-service
     game. It is a safety-game execution, not an implementation of every parity
     synthesis problem covered by A.

Classical fading-memory approximation, finite-input-history abstractions,
robust abstraction, and parity-game determinacy are credited in SOURCES.txt.
The mathematical link and explicit resource accounting below are derived
here. Historical originality and physical deployment are not asserted.

1. THE INFORMATION INTERFACE

Let A be a supplied finite alphabet of command codes, with K=|A|. Each code has
a finite physical waveform and cost. Let Q:R^p -> O be the supplied, calibrated
finite-output reader/quantizer. Its cells C_o={z:Q(z)=o} must have a finite
representation allowing exact intersection with rational boxes. Rational
rectangular cells, including open/closed boundaries and saturation cells,
suffice. Quantizer threshold calibration is substantive. A known ADC followed
by a threshold decoder is one such Q; the composed cells, not rounded nominal
thresholds, must be used.

Acquisition additionally uses a warranted finite-precision numerical port
reader, with its own paid accuracy/cost contract. The ability to obtain
numerical readings is not inferred from the later coarse alphabet O alone.

The source is causal and deterministic conditional on its fixed parameters,
preparation and admitted disturbance history. At round k it produces y_k in
R^p from the command history. Its reader returns

                 o_k = Q(y_k + nu_k),  ||nu_k||_infinity <= r.       (1)

For the converse, every sequence in the product of these error boxes is
admitted. Errors can vary adversarially and depend on the history. There is no
independence distribution and no averaging premise. If only a constant bias or
a narrower correlated noise law is permitted, the sound upper abstraction
still applies, but the converse for the full-box law must not be imported.

Source uncertainty is a family of FIXED causal sources. It is never replaced
by a newly chosen source at each round. An additional time-varying actuator
error is permitted only when explicitly included in the source contract.

After a charged preparation, suppose acquisition supplies a rational function
fhat on input suffixes of length m and a rational epsilon >= 0 such that

  ||y_k - fhat(u_(k-m),...,u_(k-1))||_infinity <= epsilon             (2)

for every admitted source, preparation, disturbance history, time k and
bounded command sequence. The known preparation supplies the initial suffix;
missing commands are padded with the actually imposed preparation command.
Equation (2) is a uniform same-input certificate, not a training loss.

An arbitrary deterministic controller may use the entire prior observation,
command and external-request history. No finite controller-memory bound is
assumed in the impossibility conclusion. External requests from a finite
alphabet can be represented by additional environment moves.

The requirement is supplied as a deterministic parity automaton D, with q
states and d priorities, over the finite command/observation/request alphabet.
The smallest priority occurring infinitely often is required to be even.
Finite-state safety, bounded deadlines, eventual completion, repeated service,
and fairness-conditioned response are included. If another specification
language is supplied, its translation and possible state explosion are an
additional cost; this contract does not provide a free temporal-logic oracle.

2. THE TWO-SIDED CAUSAL INCLUSION

For rho >= 0 define the finite suffix channel G_rho as follows. Its state is
s in A^m. After command u, set s'=shift(s,u). The environment may choose any
o such that

             C_o intersects fhat(s') + [-rho,rho]^p.                (3)

The new state is s'. All intersections use the exact quantizer boundary
convention. A rational witness can be obtained for each nonempty rational
box/cell intersection. Products with the specification and request automata
give a finite turn-based game.

Theorem 1 (trace inclusion, every horizon and every policy).
For any fixed admissible source F, any causal policy pi, and r>=epsilon,

 Traces(G_(r-epsilon),pi) subset Traces(F,r,pi)
                        subset Traces(G_(r+epsilon),pi).           (4)

Both finite and infinite traces are covered. The source F on the middle is
fixed throughout the inclusion. The initial preparation and any fixed
admissible actuator disturbance can also be fixed before constructing the
left-hand realization.

Proof of the right inclusion. On a physical run, y_k differs from fhat(s_k)
by at most epsilon. Its actual prequantizer value y_k+nu_k therefore belongs
to the radius-(r+epsilon) cube around fhat(s_k). Its observation is an allowed
move of G_(r+epsilon). Inductively, the same observation history makes pi
issue exactly the same commands in the two descriptions. This proves the
claim for every prefix and hence for the complete infinite trace.

Proof of the left inclusion. Fix any abstract run under pi. At a round with
abstract symbol o choose a prequantizer value z in its witnessing cell and
radius-(r-epsilon) cube. Run the fixed physical source with the already
matched commands, and set nu=z-y at this round. By (2), ||nu||<=r. The reader
returns the same o; the controller therefore issues the same next command.
Repeat. This constructs one permitted noise history realizing the complete
abstract run on the same fixed source. No source parameter switches occur.

Numerical readout refinement. If the actual interface supplies a full finite
ADC code but the temporal automaton uses only its coarsened label, the
nominal game may merge codes with that label. At fixed (suffix, monitor
state, command), all these codes have the same successor suffix and monitor
state, and the same future allowed moves. The player knows that complete
nominal state. Positional determinacy therefore removes any advantage from
remembering the finer past codes: a winning history-dependent raw-code
strategy exists exactly when a winning strategy exists in the merged game.
Apply (4) first to the full ADC channel, then this finite-game quotient.
Thus the executed coarse-label game also supplies its noise-margin converse
against controllers using the full admitted 40-bit readings. Extra reads,
different sampling periods or additional command levels are different
interfaces and are not excluded by that converse.

The construction of nu is an existence proof within the full bounded-error
contract. It is not a physically implemented attacker with a free true-state
reader. The returned executable counterstrategy is the finite GAME
counterstrategy; its physical force is to refute a guarantee quantified over
every admitted bounded read-error sequence.

The proof does not compare two freely diverging feedback trajectories and
does not sum epsilon over rounds. It matches the controller's observations
exactly by spending an explicitly changed noise allowance. No closed-loop
contraction, monotone controller, finite prediction horizon, or equilibration
oracle is required. This is why repeated tasks and infinite traces are valid.

Corollary 1 (controller and impossibility certificates).

  * If the controller wins G_(r+epsilon), its finite implementation wins on
    every source compatible with (2) at read-error radius r.
  * If it loses G_(r-epsilon), no causal controller can guarantee the property
    at radius r on ANY such fixed source under the full-box reader law.
  * Otherwise the data/model certificate leaves a robustness band unresolved.

The last statement is not promoted to a negative result. For r<epsilon no
nonnegative-radius lower game is supplied; only the sound upper test applies.

Corollary 2 (a complete gap decision).
For r>=2epsilon, the procedure distinguishes the promised alternatives:

  YES: the physical source admits a winning policy at radius r+2epsilon;
  NO:  the physical source admits no winning policy at radius r-2epsilon.

For YES, G_(r+epsilon) is included in F at r+2epsilon; any physical winning
policy wins that game. For NO, F at r-2epsilon is included in G_(r-epsilon);
if that game had a winning policy it would contradict NO. Applying the
ordinary upper/lower procedure therefore returns the correct promised answer
and a controller or counterstrategy. Positive guarantees apply uniformly to
the entire compatible family. The promises themselves are not inferred from
an empirical fit.

3. OPTIMAL NOISE TOLERANCE AND COMPLETE FINITE COMPUTATION

For a fixed quantizer, action language, initial context and specification,
write W_F(r) for existence of a controller winning on F with all read errors
of radius r. This is monotone decreasing in r. Let r_F^* be its supremum,
clipped to a supplied interval [0,R]. A failed radius-zero problem is reported
separately; assigning it clipped tolerance zero does not certify feasibility.
Define rhat^* analogously for G_rho.

Theorem 2 (task-specific optimum from task-independent acquisition).

                       |r_F^* - rhat^*| <= epsilon.                (5)

At upper/lower clipping endpoints the interval is intersected with [0,R].
For families whose preparation/actuator disturbance is also universally
quantified, the same conclusion holds because the upper proof covers every
such history and the lower proof can fix any one of them.

Proof. Winning G_(r+epsilon) implies W_F(r). Conversely, W_F(r) implies
winning G_(r-epsilon) whenever the latter radius is nonnegative. The two
monotone winning sets therefore have suprema separated by at most epsilon.
No claim is made about inclusion of the critical endpoint; it is checked
separately in the finite model.

More generally, for any two causal descriptions F and G whose uniform
same-input output distance is at most epsilon, the optimal read-noise
tolerance of every fixed operational temporal task differs by at most
epsilon. Thus this capability functional is 1-Lipschitz in that causal
distance, regardless of horizon or policy-memory complexity. The constant
is sharp: constant scalar outputs c and c+epsilon, with a threshold reader
at theta<c and the requirement that every report be above threshold, have
optimal tolerances c-theta and c+epsilon-theta. This sharpness example
supports the general theorem; it is not the programme's executed endpoint.

For rectangular rational cells, each allowed observation set changes only
when rho crosses a rational distance between fhat(s) and a cell. Whether an
edge is present at equality depends on the cell boundary convention. There
are at most K^m|O| such threshold values, plus the interval endpoints. Compute
and sort them, solve at open-interval representatives and boundary points,
and use monotonicity/binary search to locate the exact nominal supremum. A
policy for any radius strictly below a winning side is then returned. For
general finite rational polyhedral cells, exact linear feasibility gives the
same finite list of rational entry distances and endpoint flags.

Here is an entirely explicit finite algorithm for each parity game. If there
are N_c=qK^m controller vertices, at most N_e=KN_c environment vertices,
N=N_c+N_e and E<=KN_c(1+|O|) edges (multiply for an explicit request interface):

  1. Enumerate all at most K^N_c positional controller strategies.
  2. For each, retain its selected action edges and all environment edges.
     Determine original-graph reachability from the initial vertex.
  3. For every odd priority j, restrict to vertices with priority at least j
     and compute positive-length reachability. Reject the policy if a vertex
     of priority j, reachable in the original graph, lies on a cycle in this
     restricted graph. Such a cycle is exactly a losing ultimately periodic
     continuation. If no such cycle exists, return this winning policy.
  4. If none wins, enumerate environment positional strategies, at most
     |O|^N_e, and apply the complementary even-priority test. Return a
     counterstrategy that wins from the initial vertex.

Existence of a positional winner for finite parity games is the classical
accepted determinacy theorem, including its formally checked versions. It
ensures termination of step 4 with a witness. No parity oracle is assumed.
Faster standard parity solvers may replace this deliberately elementary
implementation; no polynomial-in-all-inputs synthesis claim is made.

An explicit conservative bit-operation upper bound for this elementary
algorithm, including naive sequential indexing and Boolean transitive
closure, is

  10^4 [K^N_c + |O|^N_e] (d+1)(N+E+1)^6 (B+log2(N+E+2)+2)^2,      (6)

where B bounds the finite integers/rationals used in constructing the graph.
This loose bound allows scanning an entire finite array per indexed access;
it is not a unit-cost real-RAM bound. Storage can be bounded by the explicit
graph, N-by-N Boolean reachability arrays and current strategies. Game
construction costs at most O(K^m m p^2 B^2 + qK^(m+1)|O|pB^2) with schoolbook
arithmetic for the linear representation below. Enumerating and sorting the
noise thresholds adds their explicit finite rational comparison cost.

For the executed safety automaton the greatest fixed point is much cheaper.
Start with all nonfailure states. Delete a state if every legal command has
an allowed observation/request continuation outside the current set. This
terminates in at most the number of states. A remaining state's selected
command gives a universal safety certificate. Each removed state receives
its deletion rank; for each command a move to failure or a lower rank gives
an explicit finite counterstrategy. The code audits BOTH certificates over
all game states, not only the executed history.

The finite policy can be compiled to a finite-state transducer. Its retained
state consists of the last m command codes and the required monitor/request
state. All stored tables, source-dependent advice, arithmetic precision and
offline game work are charged. On a bit-tape model a linear scan of the
finite policy/transition table gives a fully finite per-round lookup bound;
an actual random-access processor may be faster but must meet its measured or
warranted worst-case deadline. There is no unpriced nonuniform lookup.

4. ACQUISITION FOR A GENERAL FADING-MEMORY SOURCE

Assume all permitted bounded preparations remain in a warranted invariant
source class and that any two histories with the same last m input symbols
have current outputs differing by at most omega(m) in infinity norm, where
the finite, effectively usable modulus omega(m) tends to zero. The property
is supplied or independently justified; a finite successful fit does not
establish it. Every word in A^m must be physically permitted during
acquisition. The source law and calibration remain stationary across
acquisition and deployment.

Theorem 3 (complete word acquisition without exact reset).
Run a linearized de Bruijn sequence of order m: a word of length K^m+m-1
containing each m-symbol word once as a consecutive window. After its first
m inputs, acquire one p-vector reading for each new window. Store that
reading as fhat(s), with total certified read/quantization error eta. Then

                       epsilon = omega(m)+eta                      (7)

satisfies (2) for every future task and command history in the class.

Proof. The acquisition and deployment histories with suffix s need not have
the same hidden state or older history. The warranted forgetting modulus
bounds the difference of their true outputs by omega(m). The stored reading
adds at most eta. This proves (7) uniformly over all suffixes and hence over
all histories. Before use, impose a known m-command prefix so that the suffix
is known; its state is not assumed to reset.

A de Bruijn word can be constructed by an Euler tour of the graph of
(m-1)-words and their one-symbol shifts. This finite graph has K^m edges.
Its construction, traversal, commands, readers, storage and initialization
are paid. There are K^m vector readings, pK^m scalar readings and
K^m+m-1 acquisition command periods, plus m preparation periods before use.
An explicit stored table requires O(pK^m B) bits. Fixed adversarial eta does
not decrease with repetitions. Arbitrary epsilon requires a genuinely
refinable channel and a usable modulus, with their actual costs.

This theorem is an input/output construction. No hidden state, Jacobian,
resettable microscopic checkpoint or known internal dimension is read.

5. WHY GENERAL NONLINEAR ACQUISITION CAN BE EXPENSIVE

The following lower bound is for a reusable representation that answers all
later one-step predictions, and therefore all later protocols. It is not a
lower bound for every individual selected task or for every compressed
structured source class.

Fix binary inputs, rho=1/2 and hidden state z in
X=product_(i=1)^m [0,rho^(i-1)]. Its dynamics are

              z_next=(u,rho z_1,...,rho z_(m-1)).                   (8)

They contract by rho in infinity norm under identical inputs and forget the
initial state exactly after m steps. For each binary word w of length m,
let c_w be its resulting state. Distinct c_w are separated by at least
s=rho^(m-1). Let g=s/2. For an arbitrary binary table b indexed by these 2^m
words, use the scalar readout

 f_b(z)= g max_(w:b_w=1) [1-2||z-c_w||_infinity/s]_+,              (9)

with maximum zero for an empty index set. The open supports are disjoint.
The map is globally 1-Lipschitz, bounded in [0,g], and f_b(c_w)=g b_w.
Every member has the same rho-contracting dynamics and the same supplied
fading modulus omega(k)=rho^k. The unknown table is entirely in the readout.

Theorem 4 (worst-case information lower bound).
For uniform prediction error epsilon<g/2, every deterministic acquisition
scheme using instantaneous scalar readings must, in the worst case, make
at least 2^m informative readings. If it retains no later source access,
its acquired representation needs at least 2^m bits in the worst case.

Proof. One point reading lies in at most one open tent support, so it can
depend on at most one unknown table bit. Run an adaptive algorithm on the
all-zero table. With fewer than 2^m informative readings there is an untouched
support. Changing only its bit leaves the entire adaptive transcript and all
chosen inputs unchanged, yet changes the later output at that word by g.
One answer cannot approximate both within epsilon<g/2. This remains true
with noiseless, arbitrary-precision readings and even if arbitrary real
inputs are allowed: disjoint supports still expose at most one bit per
instantaneous read. It is not a claim about other observation operators such
as arbitrary exact integrals of a whole trajectory.

There are 2^(2^m) possible tables. A retained representation answering every
word within epsilon<g/2 must distinguish every pair, so it has at least 2^m
bits. This is a pigeonhole argument, not a complexity assumption.

The unknown readout in this family contains 2^m independent bits. This is
not a lower bound for sources supplied with polynomial-size parametric or
circuit descriptions. It identifies what the stated regularity promises
alone fail to compress.

Choosing m with rho^(m-1)>=8epsilon gives g>=4epsilon. The reading cost is
exponential in memory length and in the number of accuracy bits for this
family. For rho=1/2 it is of order 1/epsilon, not exponential in 1/epsilon.
The table upper bound of Theorem 3 is therefore order-sharp in memory length.
Uniform forgetting and a Lipschitz readout do not alone earn the compressed
linear acquisition in the next section.

6. PAID COMPRESSED ACQUISITION FROM THE RETAINED COMPONENT CLASS

Let X be a real Hilbert space, possibly infinite dimensional. The hidden
component and the actual command actuator obey

    x'=-H x+B v,       y=B* x,
    tau v'=-v+c+e_a.                                             (10)

The fixed, stationary H is self-adjoint with H>=aI, a>0; B*B=I_p. Ports and
their normalization are supplied. For between-read consequences assume
ran B subset Dom H and ||HB||<=g_B, as warranted by the retained A0 and
coupling-moment bounds: g_B<=sqrt(A0^2+c0). There is no upper spectral cutoff
and no hidden dimension bound. The actuator time constant is fixed and
calibrated within 0<tau_min<=tau<=tau_max, with a tau_max<=1/2 for the formulas
in this section. Slower actuators can be handled by a supplied slower decay
envelope, but that additional case is not used in the execution.

Commands c(t) are held between boundaries kh, with bounded alphabet values
||c||_2<=U_2, ||c||_infinity<=U_infinity. The actuator error has a warranted
L2 bound zeta; DAC amplitude/calibration errors can be included in it.
Initial bounds ||x||<=R_x and ||v||<=V_0 are supplied. Finite input and time
calibration, not arbitrary boundary/point control, is the admitted interface.

Choose a positive computation window 0<ell<h. Output for round k is read at
kh-ell. The next command is installed at kh. The previous command remains
active during ell while the reader, controller and communications complete.
This requires a warranted combined worst-case deadline <=ell. The time is
paid; a mathematical lookup count is not a hardware deadline demonstration.

With zero preparation and zero e_a, the command-to-output kernel is

 K(t)=integral_0^t B*exp[-H(t-s)]B tau^(-1)exp[-s/tau] ds.        (11)

Its norm satisfies

  ||K(t)|| <= [exp(-at)-exp(-t/tau)]/(1-a tau) <= 2 exp(-at),
  integral_0^infinity ||K(t)||dt <= 1/a.                          (12)

These follow directly from the semigroup contraction and the scalar
actuator convolution. An initial (x_0,v_0) contributes at most
(||x_0||+2tau||v_0||)exp(-at). Actuator disturbance contributes at most
zeta/a to every output. All are dimension-independent norm bounds, not
assumptions that hidden fast modes were experimentally absent.

Define the sampled response matrices for the ACTUAL read phase:

 G_0 = integral_0^(h-ell) K(t)dt,
 G_j = integral_(jh-ell)^((j+1)h-ell) K(t)dt, j>=1.             (13)

Their tail satisfies

       sum_(j>=m) ||G_j|| <= (2/a) exp[-a(mh-ell)].              (14)

After finite preparation, y at the read phase is the convolution of these
matrices with previous held command codes plus the initial/actuator residuals.
No exact transfer function or individual pole list is supplied to acquisition.

The numerical reader is noninvasive at this model level, or its fixed loading
is already in H and any additional force backaction is included in zeta.
Unbounded or unmodeled measurement backaction does not satisfy this contract.

Acquire each column by an actual finite pulse. For column l:

  * command zero for a finite W_acq;
  * command A e_l for one full period h, then zero;
  * read at h-ell,2h-ell,...,mh-ell after that pulse begins.

There are p trials and pm vector readings, or p^2m scalar readings. The
source evolves continuously between trials; it is not reset. If R_z bounds
the initial augmented output residue at the start of a zero relaxation,
and each raw read has error <=eta_acq, each acquired matrix entry has error

 eta_G = [R_z exp(-aW_acq)+zeta_acq/a+eta_acq]/A + eta_store.     (15)

Here eta_store is any additional representation rounding. Native numerical
error, ADC precision, calibration and sampling-time error belong in eta_acq.
The pulse amplitude A and its actual production cost are explicit.

Before deployment, relax for W_use. Store the matrices as finite rationals
and set fhat(s)=sum_(j=0)^(m-1) Ghat_j u_(k-1-j). Then (2) holds with

 epsilon = R_z exp(-aW_use)
         + (2U_2/a) exp[-a(mh-ell)]
         + mp U_infinity eta_G
         + zeta_use/a + epsilon_clock + epsilon_eval.             (16)

Proof. The first term bounds the actual unknown preparation and residual
driver state. The second bounds the missing command-history tail by (14).
The third bounds every entrywise coefficient error, every retained lag, and
every allowed input, without assuming errors average or cancel. The fourth
bounds persistent actuator error through the kernel's L1 norm. Clock and
evaluation errors are added under their actual bounds. No fitted realization
needs to be assumed passive: the acquired finite convolution is used with
its proved uniform input/output residual.

For a target epsilon, the memory length can be selected as

 m >= (ell + a^(-1) log(8U_2/(a epsilon)))/h,                    (17)

with a ceiling and nonnegative logarithm. Choose W_use to make the first
term <=epsilon/4, eta_G<=epsilon/(4mpU_infinity), and
zeta_use/a<=epsilon/4, with separate allocations if clock errors are present.
The acquisition relaxation and raw precision in (15) must actually meet
that eta_G. Arbitrarily fine epsilon is unavailable when these native error
floors cannot be refined. Increasing m with fixed eta_G can make the third
term worse; the planner must evaluate the complete expression.

Acquisition duration is p(W_acq+mh); it includes all pulse, relaxation and
read phases. Storage is p^2m finite scalars, with O(p^2mB^2) ordinary
schoolbook processing after the readings. The logarithm in (17) does not
make physical time polynomial in the binary encoding of 1/a. No global
polynomial bound is claimed for source-service cost, noise refinement or
the subsequent finite game.

7. TIMING, PHYSICAL OUTPUTS, COMPOSITION AND RESOURCE ACCOUNTING

Timing. A fixed calibrated read phase is already included in (13). Bounded
read-time jitter j_r adds at most M_y j_r, where

  V=max(V_0,U_2+zeta), R=max(R_x,V/a), M_y=g_B R+V.                (18)

For command-boundary timing errors of absolute size j_c<h/2 about the
calibrated grid, each switch creates a mismatch of amplitude <=2U_2 over
length <=j_c. Using (12), a conservative uniform output error is

        4 U_2 j_c exp(2a j_c)/(1-exp(-ah)).                      (19)

The sum includes at most one mismatch interval per nominal boundary. A
currently early switch and a preceding late switch can shorten their
separation by 2j_c; the geometric series with this allowance gives (19).
A currently incomplete interval is bounded by the same most-recent term.
If a different clock model permits accumulated
drift rather than uniformly bounded boundary jitter, (19) is not its bound.
The instrument must supply a corresponding envelope or the model must be
reacquired using that calibrated time law. The execution uses exact rational
sample times, a positive ell, and zero additional jitter.

Physical outputs. The core parity property is about actual observations and
commands. For a physical set P, an observed cell o certifies y in P when

                       C_o + [-r,r]^p subset P.                 (20)

For a halfspace w*y<=c, its sufficient measured margin is r||w||_1, plus
the cell extent. For continuous-time safety, add an output-motion allowance
M_y Delta (Euclidean distance) from the relevant sample; if every adjacent
sample is certified, distance to the nearest sample is <=h/2 and M_y h/2
suffices. Acknowledging at the command boundary rather than the preceding
read requires the additional M_y ell allowance. Finite rational polyhedral
set inclusions are exactly computable; semialgebraic predicates may use the
retained real-algebraic algorithms with their full cost.

This is an explicit bridge from observed protocols to true sampled/continuous
conditions. It does not make arbitrary hidden-state predicates observable.
Exact physical state selection, continuous intersample avoidance without a
motion envelope, and arbitrary nonlinear analog couplings are not silently
included in an observed-word theorem.

Component composition. Independently acquired components satisfying (2)
can be connected through a common bounded, strictly causal, sampled digital
controller. Retain their individual input suffixes and take the product of
their games. In a block infinity norm the error is the maximum of individual
epsilons (or retain a separate box radius for each component). The inclusion
proof works component by component for every shared feedback history.
No stochastic independence, hidden-state identification or closed-loop
small-gain condition is required. The full component preparation, bandwidth,
read and supply costs add. Product-state/game costs can be exponential in
component count. Continuous physical interconnections require the earlier
passive assembly theorem or another justified model of the assembled source;
this digital-composition result alone does not cover them.

Actuation. The actual drive obeys ||v||<=V and
||v'||<=(V+U_2+zeta)/tau_min. Command changes, DAC updates, amplitudes, slew
capability, and processor/read deadlines are explicit resources. Stationary
coefficients and supplied norm/decay bounds remain substantive assumptions.

Supply. In the power-conjugate normalization,

 d(||x||^2/2)/dt = -<x,Hx> + y*v.

Thus |y*v|<=RV, and any finite operating time T has an absolute port-work
upper bound RVT. This accounts for source supply, not all hardware energy.
A warranted driver envelope such as P_driver<=P0+alpha||v||^2+
beta||v'||^2+eta_eff^(-1)|y*v| can be integrated explicitly. Add reader,
communication, clock, processor, acquisition and preparation energy from
their actual envelopes. No watts/joules conversion is asserted without its
calibration. Unlimited-horizon logical correctness has a finite cost for
each finite duration; infinite operation is not supplied with finite energy.

A lower work bound is also available for repeated genuine output events.
Suppose every acknowledged event has ||y(t_j)||>=c>0, consecutive read times
are separated by at least h, and c/M_y<=h. On the preceding interval of
length c/M_y, Lipschitz continuity gives
||y(t_j-s)||>=c-M_y s. These intervals do not overlap. Hence N events imply

 integral <x,Hx>dt >= N a c^3/(3M_y),
 integral y*v dt >= N a c^3/(3M_y) - ||x_start||^2/2.             (23)

This assumes the counted preceding intervals lie within the operating
period, as they do in the execution. An acknowledged coordinate of magnitude
c suffices because it lower-bounds ||y||. For a perpetually backlogged
protocol with at most D ticks between completions, the limiting average net
source supply is at least a c^3/(3M_y D h). This deduction uses the actual
output-event and deadline guarantees, not an assumed settling state.

Finite zero-command preparation gives
||x_start||<=R_z exp(-aW_use)+zeta_use/a under the same semigroup bound,
so the initial-energy deduction is explicitly priced without a hidden-state
read. This is a net source-work bound in the declared power-conjugate units;
driver, computation and reader energy remain additional physical costs.

Computation and access. The total cost is

8. EXACT CLASSIFICATION WITHOUT A MARGIN IS NOT AVAILABLE

Consider two scalar components B=1, H=1-delta and H=1+delta, with a common
calibrated first-order actuator, zero preparation and |c(t)|<=1. They meet
common positive decay and norm bounds for small delta>0. Their output
operators differ by at most

                |1/(1-delta)-1/(1+delta)|
                         =2delta/(1-delta^2)                    (21)

in bounded-input/bounded-output norm. To see this, the difference of the
two scalar impulse responses has one sign, its L1 norm is the displayed
static-gain difference, and the actuator kernel has L1 norm one.

If the fixed adversarial acquisition noise eta exceeds half (21), both
sources can produce the same entire adaptive record: at each read return
the midpoint of their two true outputs, then apply the same calibrated
quantizer. Matching earlier observations forces matching future commands.
This argument covers arbitrarily long adaptive acquisition, not only a
preselected list of probes.

Nevertheless, the source H=1-delta can reach y>1 in finite time under a
constant unit command, whereas H=1+delta cannot reach y>1 under ANY permitted
command from zero. The latter output is bounded by its static gain<1; the
former monotonically approaches a value>1. Thus the unmargined physical
reachability question is not determined by the admitted noisy records.

This is an information obstruction for this specified reader, not a
computational lower bound, a claim that all physical reachability is
undecidable, or a contradiction of the robust gap operation.

Likewise, (4) is not an infinite-horizon total-variation approximation
theorem. Distinct iid Bernoulli laws can differ arbitrarily little per step
while their infinite records are mutually singular by their limiting
frequencies. The finite-horizon, on-policy whole-path certificate in the
read N736 original retains its stated T-dependent bound and experimental
gates. No finite-horizon statistical validation is promoted here to a
universal infinite-time claim.

9. FINITE-ARITHMETIC NATIVE EXECUTION CERTIFICATE

The private software source reuses the retained component-A fixture:
256 distinct rational diagonal rates in [1,2], three normalized Hadamard
port columns +/-1/16, A<=2I and hidden coupling Gram<=I/4. These supplied
structural bounds are checked exactly in native.py. The learner reads only
the port record and declared priors. The first-order actuator has tau=1/8;
its state is not reset between experiments or before use.

All native states and coefficients are integer multiples of 2^-80. Every
exponential coefficient has a rational enclosure computed by the inherited
finite Taylor/range-reduction routine at 104-bit precision, and its rounded
coefficient error is checked <=u=2^-80. The periods used are W=30,
h-ell=2-1/4096 and ell=1/4096. No uncharged ODE solver is called.

For a rate lambda and a held drive d, an interval of duration T uses

 a_lambda=exp(-lambda T),
 b_lambda=(1-a_lambda)/lambda,
 g_lambda=(a_lambda-exp(-8T))/(8-lambda),
 x_next=a_lambda*x+b_lambda*B d+g_lambda*B(v-d),
 v_next=exp(-8T)v+(1-exp(-8T))d.                                 (22)

Every update is evaluated with integer products followed by one exact
round-to-nearest-even division. Products and maximum bit lengths are counted.
The source has actual time-varying actuator error of size 2^-35 per
coordinate, below the declared L2 bound 2^-32.

Uniform numerical bound. Let L=ell. Enclosures check
exp(-lambda T),exp(-8T)<=1-L/2, b_lambda<=1 and g_lambda<=1/6.
Actual source and actuator L2 norms stay below 2 under the declared commands;
approximations stay below 3 in each coordinate by the bounds below.
Coefficient error plus rounding in an actuator step is <=5.5u, so its
coordinate error is at most 11u/L<12u/L. In a source coordinate, the three
coefficient errors and final rounding sum to at most
(3+3/8+15/16+1/2)u=77u/16.
The transported actuator error is at most
(1-a_lambda)*(3/16)*12u/L, because g_lambda<=(1-a_lambda)/lambda.
Consequently source coordinate error is at most
(3/16)*12u/L+(77/8)u/L=95u/(8L)<16u/L.
The port signed sum has absolute coefficient sum 16, so its error is at
most 256u/L before the final 40-bit ADC. These are uniform bounds for every
number of updates, including the short computation window. They also
justify the assumed approximate coordinate caps by induction.

The ADC reports the nearest even 40-bit code. The threshold code 1/2 is
even, so its positive analogue cell begins at
theta_A=1/2-2^-41, inclusively; the negative cell ends at -theta_A,
inclusively; the middle cell is open. game.py uses THESE cells. The software
