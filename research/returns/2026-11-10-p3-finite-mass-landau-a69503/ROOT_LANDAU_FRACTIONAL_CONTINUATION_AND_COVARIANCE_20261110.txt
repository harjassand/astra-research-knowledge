Finite-mass Coulomb continuation below one full derivative, and a score-preserving heat covariance

Model and starting construction. Use the exact finite-mass, symmetric-coupling two-species Landau-Coulomb equation in P3's full question. All n_i=int f_i are positive and finite. Let P_tau^i=exp((tau/m_i)Delta), H(f)=int f log f and
 E_s(f)=s(1-s)sum_i int_0^infinity [H(f_i)-H(P_tau^i f_i)] tau^(-1-s) d tau.
The completed return constructs this functional, proves
 E_s <= (3(n_1+n_2)/2)^(1-s) I_m^s/s,
 E_s >= c_s sum_i m_i^(-s)||sqrt(f_i)||dotHs^2,
 c_s=2^s Gamma(2-s),
and derives a truncated-heat-time collision formula. No evolution bound or global Cauchy theorem is granted. The statements below identify a complete continuation interface and an exact algebraic representation; they do not close that evolution estimate.

1. A fixed E_(7/8) bound suffices for the FULL strong continuation.

Fix s=7/8 and p=3/(3-2s)=12/5. Let C_s be a fixed explicit Euclidean fractional Sobolev constant divided by c_s, so the accepted lower bound gives
 ||f_i||_(12/5) <= C_s m_i^(7/8) E_(7/8).
The claim is conditional on K=sup_(0<=t<T) E_(7/8)(f(t))<infinity on a finite maximal classical interval. It does not assume a Fisher bound.

For any nonnegative f in L1 intersect Lp, with n=int f and p>3/2, split the Coulomb potential at radius R:
 |||.|^-1*f||infinity <= A_p ||f||p R^beta+n/R,
 beta=2-3/p>0,
 A_p=(4pi/(3-p'))^(1/p'), p'=p/(p-1).
Optimizing R gives
 |||.|^-1*f||infinity <= C_p n^(beta/(beta+1))||f||p^(1/(beta+1)),
 C_p=(beta+1)beta^(-beta/(beta+1)) A_p^(1/(beta+1)).
For p=12/5, beta=3/4. Therefore
 |||.|^-1*f_i||infinity <= C_p C_s^(4/7) n_i^(3/7) m_i^(1/2) K^(4/7).
All constants retain the actual particle numbers; unit probability normalization is not silently used.

Define M_q=sum_i m_i int f_i <v>^q and phi_q=<v>^q, q>=2. Symmetrizing the actual divergence-form equation gives
 M_q'=(1/2)sum_ij c_ij int f_i(v)f_j(w) {
   a(v-w):[D2phi_q(v)/m_i+D2phi_q(w)/m_j]
   +(1/m_i+1/m_j)b(v-w).(grad phi_q(v)-grad phi_q(w)) } dv dw,
 a(z)=P_z/|z|, b(z)=-2z/|z|^3.
Convexity makes the second line nonpositive. Since tr a=2/|z|, the potential bound proves
 M_q' <= K_q K^(4/7) M_(q-2) <= K_q K^(4/7) M_q,
where K_q is explicit in q,C_p,C_s,m_i,c_ij,n_i. For example, using a uniform Hessian bound C_q<v>^(q-2), take
 K_q=2 C_q C_p C_s^(4/7) max_i [m_i^-2 sum_j c_ij n_j^(3/7) m_j^(1/2)].
For phi_q itself C_q=q(q-1) works. Smooth convex radial truncations, continued linearly beyond a large radius, permit the argument without assuming that the q-th moment has already propagated; a fixed larger C_q, such as 8q^2, bounds all truncation Hessians. Monotone limits give
 M_q(t)<=M_q(0) exp(K_q K^(4/7)t).

Use q=25. Holder with exponents 7/5 and 7/2 yields
 int f_i^2<v>^7
 <=(int f_i^(12/5))^(5/7)(int f_i<v>^(49/2))^(2/7)
 <=||f_i||_(12/5)^(12/7)(M_25/m_i)^(2/7).
Consequently the actual local-continuation norm satisfies
 Y_7(t):=sum_i int f_i^2<v>^7
 <= C_s^(12/7)(sum_i m_i^(17/14)) K^(12/7) M_25(0)^(2/7)
    *exp((2/7)K_25 K^(4/7)t).
Every initial datum in the full question has the needed finite M_25(0). Species masses, total physical energy and entropy give the remaining fixed local-theory constants. For f_i=n_i g_i, weight the local probability-density proof's i-th equation by n_i; the cross weights are n_i n_j c_ij and remain symmetric. The local weighted-L2 argument therefore keeps the physical equation and all n_i. It is not an appeal to an unweighted theorem with wrongly symmetrized coefficients.

Thus a finite bound for E_(7/8) on every finite maximal interval extends the solution by the same local strong theory. In particular,
 T_*<infinity implies sup_(t<T_*) E_(7/8)(f(t))=infinity.
This is a closed conditional criterion below Fisher order. An E_s bound with only s>1/2 gives p_s>3/2 but does not by THIS interpolation alone give the L2 continuation norm. For that direct argument s>3/4 is sufficient. More generally p_s=3/(3-2s), the required moment order is q>=14s/(4s-3), and
 Y_7<=C E_s^(3/(2s)) M_q^((4s-3)/(2s)).
The new bound is conditional, not a global regularity theorem. The primary's existing unconditional moment argument can also be used; the direct bound above does not require importing it as a new assumption.

2. Exact posterior covariance without the divergent weight average.

Fix one pair and rotation field from the return:
 F(v,w)=f_i(v)f_j(w), alpha=|v-w|^-3,
 L=b_k(v-w).grad_v/m_i-b_k(v-w).grad_w/m_j,
 q=L log F,
 P_tau=exp(tau(Delta_v/m_i+Delta_w/m_j)).
Write its Gaussian kernel as K_tau(y,x), x,y in R6. The source vector-field identity gives [L,P_tau]=0. For fixed tau>0 define
 p_tau(y)=P_tau F(y),
 mu_y(dx)=K_tau(y,x)F(x)dx/p_tau(y).
For smooth profiles for which the displayed integrals are finite, the return's collision term is exactly
 B_tau=int p_tau(y) [ E_mu(alpha q^2)-E_mu(alpha q)E_mu(q) ]dy
      =int p_tau(y) Cov_mu(q,alpha q)dy.
This uses int P_tau(alpha Fq^2)=int alpha Fq^2. At Coulomb relative coincidence, q=O(|v-w|), hence alpha Fq^2=O(|v-w|^-1) and alpha Fq=O(|v-w|^-2), both locally integrable. E_mu(alpha) itself generally diverges logarithmically and is never introduced.

With x,x' independent under mu_y, the elementary identity
 (q-q')(alpha q-alpha' q')
 =(sqrt(alpha)q-sqrt(alpha')q')^2-(sqrt(alpha)-sqrt(alpha'))^2 q q'
gives
 B_tau=(1/2)int p_tau E_(mu x mu)[(sqrt(alpha)q-sqrt(alpha')q')^2]dy
       -(1/2)int p_tau E_(mu x mu)[(sqrt(alpha)-sqrt(alpha'))^2 q q']dy.
The first term is nonnegative. The second is a precise radial-coefficient commutator, not a positive variance. These separate integrals retain the score factors: their absolute values are controlled, at fixed tau under the stated integrability, by moments of alpha q^2 and |alpha q| rather than the divergent alpha alone. Cauchy-Schwarz yields E|sqrt(alpha)q|<infinity from E(alpha q^2); the cross term with alpha uses E|alpha q| E|q|. No cancellation of separately infinite alpha averages is required.

For fixed tau this is equivalently a symmetric two-point kernel with weight
 F(x)F(x') int K_tau(y,x)K_tau(y,x')/p_tau(y)dy.
It is state-dependent, not a translation-invariant fractional Laplacian. A Hardy estimate for an unweighted heat kernel cannot simply be transplanted.

Generic covariance positivity is false even for bounded positive weights. On a two-point probability space with equal weights, take (alpha_1,alpha_2)=(1,4), (q_1,q_2)=(2,1). Then Cov(q,alpha q)=4-(3)(3/2)=-1/2. Here sqrt(alpha)q is constant, so the entire negative part is the radial commutator. This is an algebraic obstruction to a generic posterior Cauchy-Schwarz proof, NOT a negative certificate for physical product scores, the integrated functional or the Cauchy problem. Product-score structure and integration across heat times remain actual mathematical information.

3. What is still missing.

The return's full-range identity is formal until small/large auxiliary-heat cutoffs and physical-time differentiation are justified. The covariance calculation is valid on truncated auxiliary ranges with suitable profile integrability; it supplies no uniform endpoint domination. Neither the square decomposition nor the two-point example proves the necessary evolution bound. A controlled growth estimate for a continuation norm could suffice without monotonicity. The finite-mass Fisher-growth example rules out universal monotonicity of I_m and, after a justified s-up-to1 derivative limit for its smooth seed, monotonicity of every E_s sufficiently near1. It does not exclude an E_(7/8) growth bound, an alternative functional, or another global construction.
