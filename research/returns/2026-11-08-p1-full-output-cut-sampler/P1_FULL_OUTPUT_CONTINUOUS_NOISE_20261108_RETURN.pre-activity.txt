## 8. Two shortcuts fail for precise, limited reasons

### Additive means do not supply the required conditionals

For a sampled prefix \(z\), put
\[
q=P(z),\qquad a=P(zb).
\]
The next-bit conditional is \(a/q\). If \(a\) and \(q\) are each known only to additive error \(\eta<q/2\), the ratio error can be of order \(\eta/q\). A general guarantee of next-bit error \(O(\epsilon/n)\) therefore needs
\[
\eta=O(\epsilon q/n).
\]

Even uniform output has prefix probabilities \(q=2^{-j}\). Uniform sampling is easy, but this calculation shows why a generic call to a polynomial-in-\(\eta^{-1}\) additive estimator at every prefix incurs exponential work.

The accepted fixed-support predictor does not promise growing-support prefix projectors in the first place. Granting that stronger additive estimator would still leave the relative-precision problem.

The MPS construction avoids this particular issue by computing conditional probabilities from a positive representation. Its cost appears in constructing that representation.

### Constant physical time does not make the global output uniform

Consider the valid rational Hamiltonian
\[
H=J\sum_i Z_i
\]
and initial state \(|0\rangle\langle0|^{\otimes n}\). The state stays diagonal, and the output consists of independent bits with
\[
P(0)=\frac{1+b}{2},\qquad b=e^{-\gamma T}.
\]

For the event that the number of zeroes is at least \(n/2+nb/4\), Hoeffding’s inequality gives
\[
\|p_T-\mathrm{Unif}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
\ge 1-2e^{-nb^2/8}.
\]
For every fixed \(\gamma T\), this tends to one as \(n\) grows.

This family is easy to sample—it is a biased product distribution. It refutes the proposed global-uniform shortcut, not polynomial sampling.
