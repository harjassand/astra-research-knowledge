9. The smaller decisive physical experiment

The appropriate physical discriminator has two linked parts.

Full-size cartridge test

Manufacture one actual two-stage cartridge with the real pin, 4 mm lands, keeper, mechanical key and optical path.

Test:

Complete-boundary observation with imposed depth and alignment errors.
A 10 kN one-land static and controlled-energy screen.
Deliberate ±1.5 mm total catch offset.
Persistent closed and partly open A jams.
Keeper capture without reversing A.
Independent B isolation.
Power loss at the permitted transition states.
Pre/post contact profile, permanent set and component damage.

Measure force and displacement independently of the controller, with high-rate instrumentation for the catch. The 40 fps proof camera cannot resolve the dynamic event.

Planar complete-exchange test

Use three two-stage legs in a three-degree-of-freedom rig carrying 100 N and 20 Nm in several directions. Execute 48 complete exchanges: 24 candidate and 24 competent control, divided between the two joint families.

Both groups receive good fixtures, positive retention, metrology and rescue inventory. The control is not made artificially weak by denying it an independent escape.

Challenges include false engagement, partly open and persistent release jams, a seized final attachment, contaminated observation and four one-hour outage intervals. Every attempt remains in its assigned denominator.

The present unresolved receiver and strut root cannot be drawn as completed hardware and handed to that test. Their actual fabrication geometry must first be closed. Engineering conclusion

The strongest result from this work is the complete-boundary isolation test combined with an independent keeper and capture-before-recentring control. It directly addresses the ambiguity of a force reading or central gap measurement, and it avoids requiring a jammed release to reverse before the alternative route can operate.

The complete six-leg implementation fails several independent checks:

Its optical installation is not clear.
Two face-tile withdrawal paths collide.
The moment-strut root and withdrawal are incomplete.
The final seized receiver lacks a constructed independent escape.
Installed dynamics and optical uncertainty do not establish the narrow motion bound.
Machinery mass consumes most of the wrench envelope.
Complete conditional cost and human work miss the requested advantage.

Those are reasons to reject this complete architecture, while retaining the useful isolation primitive for a materially simpler construction. They are not evidence that economical robotic connection exchange is impossible.
