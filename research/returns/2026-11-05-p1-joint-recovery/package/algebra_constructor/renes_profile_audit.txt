AUDITED SHARP PETZ ENERGY BOUND FOR ALL PREFIX INSTRUMENTS
5 November 2026.  This improves a constant in the existing prefix spectral-profile theorem; the general profile/cap mechanism is already in weak_stream_memory.pdf.

Notation. The prefix bath A has dimension e=2^ell. V is its collision unitary with the source qubit. T(X)=Tr_S[V(I_S tensor X)V^dagger]/2. Set H=I-T T^*. At the cut, grant the receiver the source S. Refine every retained-memory instrument to individual Kraus branches K_x:A->Q_x, with dim Q_x<=m. This only makes recovery easier. Write A_x=sqrt(K_x^dagger K_x). Then A_x>=0, rank A_x<=m, and sum_x A_x^2=I_A.

1. Exact transpose-channel identity.
The source-plus-memory channel has maximally mixed average state I_S/(2e) tensor K_x K_x^dagger in branch x. Its transpose channel followed by encoding and compression is obtained by the local Lueders map L(X)=sum_x A_x X A_x, inverse V, and bath trace. Pauli twirling gives exactly

 F_T = sum_x ||T^*(A_x)||_{2,tracial}^2.

Therefore, with ordinary (unnormalized) Hilbert-Schmidt vectorization,

 Z=(1/e) sum_x |A_x>><<A_x|,
 Tr Z=1,
 Tr(H Z)=1-F_T.

2. Density cap.
For every operator B and support projector P_x of A_x,

 |Tr(B^dagger A_x)|^2
 = |Tr(P_x B^dagger A_x)|^2
 <= rank(A_x) Tr(A_x^2 B B^dagger).

Summing gives <B|Z|B><= (m/e) Tr(B B^dagger). Thus 0<=Z<=(m/e)I. Both partial traces of Z are I/e as well. The rank cap handles arbitrary classical branching; no bound on the number of outcomes is required.

3. Sharp qubit transpose fidelity bound, with self-contained proof.
For an arbitrary qudit-input channel N, write rho=(id tensor N)(Phi_d), tau=N(I/d), sigma=(I/d) tensor tau, and C=sigma^(-1/4) rho sigma^(-1/4). Inverses are restricted to support. Direct substitution of channel Kraus operators in the transpose recovery gives Tr C^2=d^2 F_T.

For an arbitrary decoder R, let E=(id tensor R^dagger)(Phi_d), F=Tr(E rho), and q=Tr(E sigma)=1/d^2. Here 0<=E<=I. Set G_1=sigma^(1/4) E sigma^(1/4), G_0=sigma^(1/4)(I-E)sigma^(1/4). Their 2x2 Hilbert-Schmidt Gram matrix is

 G = diag(q,1-q)-h [[1,-1],[-1,1]],
 h=Tr(G_1 G_0)>=0.

Indeed G_1+G_0=sigma^(1/2), and Tr[G_1(G_1+G_0)]=q. Hence G<=diag(q,1-q). Choosing a=F/q and b=(1-F)/(1-q), Hilbert-Schmidt Cauchy-Schwarz gives

 Tr C^2 >= F^2/q+(1-F)^2/(1-q).

Consequently

 F_T >= F^2 + (1-F)^2/(d^2-1).

This is the sharp Renes/pretty-good recovery inequality, proved here without relying on an unverified scope assumption. For d=2 and half-diamond recovery error <=epsilon, F>=1-epsilon. The RHS is increasing for F>=1/4, so for 0<=epsilon<=3/4,

 Tr(H Z)=1-F_T <= eta(epsilon):=2epsilon-(4/3)epsilon^2.

The source grant and Kraus refinement only increase achievable F, so the inequality applies to every original chronological receiver with maximum retained quantum dimension m at this cut.

4. Existing profile consequences with the sharper energy budget.
If e/m=L is integral, and lambda_1<=...<=lambda_(e^2) are eigenvalues of H, then

 (1/L) sum_(i=1)^L lambda_i <= eta(epsilon).

For general m use the fractional final eigenvalue in the usual capped-density minimization. For every beta>=0,

 log_2 m >= ell-log_2 Tr exp(-beta H)-beta eta(epsilon)/ln 2.

For a>eta(epsilon), let r(a)=rank 1_[0,a)(H); then

 m >= e [1-eta(epsilon)/a]/r(a).

If the kernel is one-dimensional and gamma=lambda_2, then

 m/e >= 1-eta(epsilon)/gamma.

5. Comparison ranges.
The sharper budget improves the Barnum-Knill budget 2epsilon-epsilon^2 throughout the indicated range. Compared with the older profile budget 3epsilon(1-epsilon), it is smaller exactly for 0<epsilon<3/5. The endpoint 3/4 is the monotonicity/substitution range, not the comparison crossover. In the small-error regime the leading improvement is 3epsilon -> 2epsilon.

Remaining endpoint limitation. These statements do not by themselves control the low-energy spectrum of H as ell grows. They do not prove a general o(N) memory obstruction or construct a small-memory receiver.
