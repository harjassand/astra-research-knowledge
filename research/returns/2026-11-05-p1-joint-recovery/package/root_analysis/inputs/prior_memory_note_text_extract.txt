                       tracial partial-SWAP stream
        Proved bounds, physically specified candidates, and the unresolved tradeoff

                                            5 November 2026


                                                  Abstract
         The source is fixed: one arbitrary qubit, possibly entangled with a reference, interacts once
     with each independent maximally mixed carrier through the same known weak partial SWAP.
     The source is discarded. No bath purifiers, preparation keys, or return link are available. The
     accepted all-output decoder acquires the payload at the quartic collision scale, but retains the
     entire stream.
         This return does not establish the requested optimal causal memory/processing tradeoff. Its
     principal completed result is a converse for every permitted causal receiver: successful payload
     recovery forces a bistochastic low-Schmidt-number Choi state to have small energy for an exact
     bath-scattering singular operator. This yields finite-error spectral-profile, mode-count, heat-trace,
     branch-tail, and expected-occupancy bounds. The needed large-prefix spectral estimate is not
     proved.
         Several concrete compression proposals are resolved at their actual scopes. Ordinary measured-
     label Schur compression has an asymptotic half-diamond error floor of one half. Classical
     consecutive-pair fusion loses information at the same fourth order at which it is acquired. A
     two-qubit folded reverse-decoder statistic violates causal distinguishability monotonicity. Even
     reverse-decoded blocks retaining both a dummy qubit and its locally prepared Bell purifier need
     blocks on the original quartic scale, despite arbitrary collective final decoding. Exact proofs,
     finite matrix checks, and adaptive-control diagnostics are supplied. None of these restricted
     obstructions is substituted for a general impossibility theorem.

6.1    Growing-spin receivers

The accompanying files specify a physical growing-spin receiver, not merely a formal tensor con-
traction. A qubit R and spin-J register are retained; each incoming carrier undergoes a covariant
control and a permitted Clebsch–Gordan branch instrument. The encoded quantum dimension is
O(N ), so the register uses O(log N ) qubits, with binary arithmetic workspace. This is a memory
bill for the instrument, not a recovery theorem.
For the tracial conditional family, the invariant joint Choi state of reference, source, receiver qubit
and spin-J has only 20 Hermitian-block parameters for J ≥ 3/2. The next carrier temporarily
raises this to 70. General covariant controls reduce to constant-size multiplicity blocks. The exact
immediate-loss-minimizing branch choice reduces to a three-variable quadratic optimization on the
unit sphere. Its executable implementation keeps the full realized classical history and the permitted
coherences.
That particular adaptive policy performs poorly. At θ = 0.1 after 5 000 collisions, three recorded
histories have acquired Gram coefficients approximately 0.000878, 0.000961, and 0.004202. Their
remaining source-traceless signal is already very small. This is a diagnostic of a specified local control
objective. It proves no impossibility for other adaptive policies, nontracial initialized workspace, or
a coherent multi-irrep receiver.
The source notes also examine a full finite rotor, retain its left/right multiplicity and inter-irrep
coherence, and derive an optimal final readout for the relevant finite spin family. No high-fidelity
rotor reader is claimed. The pure-singlet, nontracial variant was implemented with its actual
weighted Choi state. In the tested preservation policy the pure resource becomes lossless storage
of the first two outputs; a recorded trajectory at θ = 0.3, N = 600 ends with optimal conditional
final-readout entanglement fidelity 0.48206991236. This is one implemented policy and history, not a
bound on all pure initialized receivers or a statistically certified policy average. Coherent sparse-flag
variants remain unproved; a small excitation count alone is not a recovery theorem.


6.2    Established alternatives and why they do not finish the task

Bacon–Chuang–Harrow provide efficient Schur and Clebsch–Gordan circuitry, with accuracy-
dependent compilation cost [1]. The new requirement here is to prove that the discarded register is
irrelevant to the arbitrary payload. Equations (15) and the pair-loss theorem show concrete failures
of that premise.
Blume-Kohout–Croke–Zwolak give sequential coherent gathering for a finite set of pure states
and matrix product states of bounded Schmidt rank [2]. Their mixed-state extension exploits
permutation symmetry of identically prepared copies. Our bath average is maximally mixed, the
payload-bearing output is ordered and correlated, and its physical purifiers are unavailable. A
small matrix-product operator bond or a compact formula for three Pauli observables is not the
pure-subspace condition of that construction.
Homogenization and recovery-existence work already relates partial-SWAP reservoirs to correctabil-
ity [4]. The accepted warm package supplies the stronger explicit all-output operation (1). Neither
a correctability criterion nor source forgetting furnishes the required forward causal compression.
The integrable transfer-matrix construction [3] supplies useful spectral and representation machinery.
Its homogeneous Jordan blocks mean that eigenvalues cannot simply replace the singular spectrum
in (4). A pictured double row is also not a proof of an applicable reflection-equation solution; the
exact boundary must be checked.

For binary-encoded spin receivers, a compiled update of depth G at local clock rate ΩR needs

8    Evidence, preservation, and the exact remaining gap
The computational checks are finite matrix identities and policy diagnostics, not physical experiments.
The crossed-state closed form was checked in 100 finite cases with maximum residual 1.33 × 10−15 .
The purified-block weighted matrices agree with the formulas to 6.31 × 10−14 ; direct two-block
channels agree with the scalar recurrence to 2.36 × 10−14 . Pair-fusion identities were checked against
full matrices at N = 2, 4, 6 with residual at most 1.2 × 10−15 . The archive records numerical
parameters, scripts, and output receipts. None of these checks is presented as simulation of a
43 462-carrier quantum register.
The complete earlier finite-error payload-memory theorem, its short-record construction, prefix
and occupancy statements, noisy-emission correction, and all three companions are preserved
without modification in the included warm archive. The tracial acquisition package is also retained
unchanged. The programmed emitter and early-record decoder remain separate from the present
homogeneous weak source. Their resource and timing premises are not silently transferred.
There are two precise unresolved endpoints:
1. A positive solution must give a CPTP causal instrument on the actual once-through carriers,
   prove reference-uniform recovery at N = Θ(θ−4 log(1/ϵ)), and bound all quantum registers and
   their service backlog jointly in θ, ϵ, N .
2. A negative solution must evaluate a genuinely general converse, such as (9)–(14), strongly enough
   on this family to prove the required memory boundary, with a matching operation. A failing
   spin update or block decoder does not establish it.
This return supplies neither endpoint. It supplies a source-specific general converse and closes
several tempting but invalid reductions, with their proofs and execution costs exposed. The optimal
memory/processing tradeoff and the requested compressed arbitrary-input acquisition operation
remain unresolved.
