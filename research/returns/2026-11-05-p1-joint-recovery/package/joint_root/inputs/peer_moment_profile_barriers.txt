EXACT SECOND MOMENT, LARGE-PREFIX BAND, AND LIMITS OF TWO SHORTCUTS
Companion to source_granted_converse.txt; 5 November 2026

1. An exact second moment

Keep the notation D=2^N, A_N=T_N T_N^*, H_N=I-A_N, r=1-s^4.
Set
  u=c^(2N), v=r^N, w=[c^2(c^2-s^2)]^N,
  h=N c^(2N-1)s^3,
with the continuous endpoint convention.  Then exactly

  Tr(A_N)/D^2 = (1+3u^2)/4 =:a_N,

  Tr(A_N^2)/D^2
    =(12h^2+9u^2+4v^2-6v+5w^2+4)/16 =:b_N.

Hence

  delta_N:=Tr[A_N(I-A_N)]/D^2
    =(3u^2-12h^2-4v^2+6v-5w^2)/16 >=0.

PROOF OF THE SECOND IDENTITY.  Use an actual global two-pass channel only as
a mathematical construction.  Append a tracial source S, apply U^(N) to S+B,
append a tracial dummy R, and apply U^(N)* to R+B in reverse collision order.
The resulting channel on B is T_N^* T_N.  Purify S by Q and R by A, so the
environment Q,S,R,A at the end is the Choi state of the channel F_N from
initial S to final S,R,A when R,A started in a Bell state and B started
tracial.  For a channel, its Choi state and its complementary output on a
maximally mixed input have the same nonzero eigenvalues.  Also the squared
Hilbert--Schmidt norm of a superoperator is D^2 times its normalized Choi
purity.  Therefore

  Tr[(T_N T_N^*)^2]/D^2 = Tr[Choi(F_N)^2].

Folding the dummy R wire onto its Bell partner A gives exactly the global
crossed-statistic channel constructed and proved in
../online/crossed_statistic_obstruction.txt.  This use is global; it does not
turn the non-TP local folded update into a physical causal receiver.
Writing F_N(I/2)=sigma_N and F_N(sigma_a/2)=delta_a,N, SU(2) covariance and
Pauli orthogonality give

  Tr[Choi(F_N)^2]=(Tr sigma_N^2+3 Tr delta_z,N^2)/2.

The exact Pauli family in that source note has

  Tr sigma_N^2=(1+3u^2)/8,
  Tr delta_z,N^2=(12h^2+6u^2+4v^2-6v+5w^2+3)/24.

Substitution proves the displayed second-moment expression.  This uses no
assumption about independence of past output correlations and provides no
physical purifier access to the receiver.

FINITE CHECK.  moment_transfer.py contracts the independently derived replica
transfer matrix for k=1,2 and compares with a dense bath superoperator for
N=1,...,4 at theta=.1,.3,.6.  The largest small-N discrepancy is 2.4e-15.
The second-moment closed form also agrees with transfer contractions through
N=10000 within accumulated floating-point error (about 1.7e-12 at most in
the normalized moment).  These are checks of an exact proof, not numerical
asymptotic evidence.

2. An exact large-prefix empirical spectral statement

For fixed 0<theta<pi/2,

  a_N ->1/4,
  delta_N/r^N ->3/8.

Indeed c^4<r and [c^2(c^2-s^2)]^2<=c^4, so the u,w,h terms vanish faster
than r^N, even after the polynomial factor in h^2.

For every threshold 0<eta<1, let n_N(eta) count eigenvalues of H_N below eta.
Then the exact inequality

  n_N(eta)/D^2 >= [a_N-delta_N/eta]_+

follows because the A_N weight outside this subspace is at most
Tr(A_N H_N)/eta.  Similarly, the fraction of H_N eigenvalues above 1-eta
is at least [1-a_N-delta_N/eta]_+ (endpoint conventions do not affect the
conclusion).  The fraction of A_N eigenvalues in [eta,1-eta] is at most
delta_N/[eta(1-eta)].

Consequently the normalized empirical spectrum of H_N converges weakly to

  (1/4) point_mass_at_0 + (3/4) point_mass_at_1.

This is a result about normalized spectral counting on increasing spaces.
It does NOT assert a finite-N exact kernel of dimension D^2/4.  It does NOT
determine the width or low tail within the near-zero band.  Those details
are precisely what a sufficiently high-precision prefix converse needs.

Every fixed integer k obeys the exact bounds

  a_N-(k-1)delta_N <= Tr(A_N^k)/D^2 <= a_N,

because x-x^k <=(k-1)x(1-x) for 0<=x<=1.  Thus all fixed normalized moments
converge to 1/4.  Fixed-replica limits cannot settle low-energy counts on
the exponentially fine part of the band.

In the weak-action quartic window N theta^4 ->t>0, the fast u,w,h terms
vanish, v->exp(-t), and the normalized second moment tends to

  1/4-(3/8)exp(-t)+(1/4)exp(-2t).

Its minimum is 7/64 at exp(-t)=3/4.  Equality of this number with a random
matrix/free-projection second moment is only an equality of one moment.
No independence, freeness, or uniform finite-size tail control has been
established here.  A free-Jacobi law cannot be imported from two moments.
Even convergence of every fixed normalized moment would not provide the
counting precision needed by the memory theorem.  To force q>=c0 N from
the mode-count bound, one needs n_N(eta) of order at most 2^((1-c0)N),
whereas the ambient operator space has dimension 4^N.  An exceptional
spectral fraction tending to zero can still contain far more than 2^N
modes.  Thus a square-root-dimension or stronger finite-size tail bound,
not only a bulk limiting law, is necessary for that inference.

3. A precise obstruction to an overly strong local comparison

Consider the natural adjacent-permutation Dirichlet operator on bath
operator Hilbert space:

  L_N=sum_(i=1)^(N-1) [I-Ad_SWAP(i,i+1)].

In the local Pauli basis Ad_SWAP interchanges two four-dimensional sites.
Thus L_N is the SU(4) ferromagnetic permutation Hamiltonian, with a
polynomial-dimensional symmetric ground space.  Its normalized tracial
mean and variance are exactly

  mean(L_N)=3(N-1)/4,
  var(L_N)=15(N-1)/16.

For the variance, each site swap has mean 1/4 and square I; two distinct
adjacent swaps have joint normalized trace 1/16, whether their supports
overlap or not.  All cross covariances therefore vanish.

Suppose an operator comparison H_N >= gamma_N L_N holds.  Since 0<=A_N<=I,
Cauchy--Schwarz gives

  Tr(A_N L_N)/D^2
    >= a_N 3(N-1)/4 - sqrt[a_N 15(N-1)/16].

Multiplying the alleged comparison by A_N and tracing now forces

  gamma_N <= delta_N /
    {a_N 3(N-1)/4 - sqrt[a_N 15(N-1)/16]},

whenever the denominator is positive.  At fixed nonzero theta this is

  gamma_N <= (2+o(1)) r^N/N.

In particular, a comparison

  H_N >= c0 sin^4(theta) r^N L_N

with c0>0 independent of N cannot hold over all N: it fails when
N sin^4(theta) grows without bound.  This is an algebraic obstruction,
not an inference from finite numerical gaps.  It does not exclude a
comparison with an additional inverse polynomial in N, or a comparison
limited to a bounded N sin^4(theta) window.  No such useful weakened
comparison is proved in this return.

4. Why the obvious double-row integrability identification is incomplete

The bath channel is a vacuum matrix element of the local Liouville
monodromy M.  Thus

  T_N T_N^* = <0| M P0 M^* |0>,  P0=|0><0|,

where |0> is the normalized identity in source operator space.  This is
a double-row tensor network, but a diagram of this shape does not supply
an integrable reflection boundary.

Take the rational local R in Liouville space

  G(z)=Ad_[(z I+i SWAP)/sqrt(z^2+1)].

The constant boundary P0 does not solve the ordinary reflection equation

  G(z-w) P0_1 G(z+w) P0_2
      =P0_2 G(z+w) P0_1 G(z-w).

To see this, apply both sides to X tensor I.  If
a(z)=z^2/(1+z^2), b(z)=1/(1+z^2), the left side has an I tensor X
coefficient b(z+w)a(z-w), generically nonzero.  The right side ends in
P0_2 on the left and has zero such coefficient.  Thus the natural fixed
boundary fails the needed equation.

The same obstruction is visible without tensor diagrams at N=2.  On the
orthonormal vector sector
  Z_1, Z_2, (X_1Y_2-Y_1X_2)/sqrt(2),
the bath channel has matrix

  [[a,0,0], [b,a,d], [d,0,a]],
  a=c^2, b=s^4, d=-sqrt(2)c s^3.

These T matrices commute at different theta, but their products T T^*
do not.  For theta_1=pi/6, theta_2=pi/4, the (1,2) entry of the commutator
of those products is (11+10sqrt(3))/2048, which is nonzero.

This rules out importing a fixed-boundary commuting Sklyanin family by
inspection.  It does not rule out embedding a fixed-angle operator in a
different family with angle-dependent inhomogeneities or fused/crossed
boundary matrices.  Such an embedding needs an actual reflection-equation
solution and compatible boundary contractions; neither is supplied here.

Primary background:
  E. K. Sklyanin, Boundary conditions for integrable quantum systems,
  J. Phys. A 21, 2375 (1988), doi:10.1088/0305-4470/21/10/015.
  B. Vlaar, Boundary transfer matrices and boundary quantum KZ equations,
  J. Math. Phys. 56, 071705 (2015), arXiv:1408.3364.

5. Domain-wall and positive-loop shortcuts are not established

The moment network has cyclic replica contractions and Bell/identity
auxiliary boundaries.  These are not the fixed-arrow domain-wall boundaries
of the standard Izergin--Korepin determinant.  The rational scattering
weights are complex at the physical unitary parameters, and the Liouville
Pauli transfer has both signs.  A nontrivial orthogonal local transfer matrix
cannot be made entrywise nonnegative merely by a basis-sign gauge: an
entrywise nonnegative orthogonal matrix is a permutation.  Global moment
positivity is not a positive local Boltzmann-weight representation.

These observations do not prove that every alternative loop representation
fails.  They state the missing steps in the proposed shortcut: identify the
correct boundary partition function, control any change of basis and its
condition number, and prove a bound uniform when the replica number grows
with prefix length.  A fixed-replica calculation, an eigenvalue formula for
a nonnormal transfer matrix, or matching a random-matrix second moment does
not supply that bound.
