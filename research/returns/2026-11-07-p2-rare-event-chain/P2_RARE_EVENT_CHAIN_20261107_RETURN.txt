## 2. The exact kinetic model and its computable bounds

Set \(t_0=t_n=0\), and define
\[
a_i=
\frac{t_{i-1}(1-t_i^2)}
     {1-t_{i-1}^2t_i^2},
\qquad
b_i=
\frac{t_i(1-t_{i-1}^2)}
     {1-t_{i-1}^2t_i^2}.
\]

Spin \(i\) flips at rate
\[
c_i(\sigma)=
\frac{\lambda_i}{2}
\left[
1-\sigma_i
\left(a_i\sigma_{i-1}+b_i\sigma_{i+1}\right)
\right],
\]
with the absent endpoint terms omitted.

These are per-site clocks. The resulting time scale is part of the supplied model.

The stationary distribution is explicitly
\[
\pi(\sigma)=
2^{-n}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}
\left(1+t_i\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\right).
\]

Define
\[
p=2^{-n}\prod_i(1-t_i),\qquad C=p^{-1},
\]
and
\[
\Lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i,\qquad
G=2\Lambda,\qquad
g=\min_i\lambda_i(1-a_i-b_i).
\]

All these quantities are computable rationals. Moreover,
\[
1-a_i-b_i
=
\frac{(1-t_{i-1})(1-t_i)}
     {1+t_{i-1}t_i}>0,
\]
so \(g>0\).

The relevant bounds are:

\[
\pi(\sigma)\geq p,\qquad
\operatorname{gap}(-Q)\geq g,\qquad
\operatorname{spec}(-Q)\subseteq[0,G].
\]

For the chain killed on entering \(A\), a certified spectral lower bound is
\[
d=g\,\pi(A).
\]

Indeed, if a function vanishes on \(A\), then
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}(f)
\geq \pi(A)\|f\|_\pi^2,
\]
and the full-chain Poincaré inequality gives the killed-chain bound.

Consequently,
\[
\Pr_x(\tau_A>t)\leq C e^{-dt},
\]
\[
\mathbb E_x\tau_A\leq \frac{C}{d},
\qquad
\mathbb E_x\tau_A^2\leq \frac{2C}{d^2}.
\]

When \(x\notin A\), a hit requires a jump, giving
\[
\mathbb E_x\tau_A\geq \frac1\Lambda.
\]

These bounds can be numerically very large or small. Their **binary lengths** remain controlled by the complete rational input. That distinction is essential to the construction.

## 3. Compressing a transition probability to one matrix exponential and one Pfaffian

The first substantive operation is an evaluator for an individual transition probability
\[
P_z(x,y)=(e^{zQ})_{xy},
\qquad \Re z\geq0,
\]
including complex \(z\).

Define the \(n\times n\) tridiagonal matrix
\[
K_{ii}=\lambda_i,\qquad
K_{i,i-1}=-\lambda_i a_i,\qquad
K_{i,i+1}=-\lambda_i b_i,
\]
and let
\[
U(z)=e^{-zK}.
\]

For \(i<j\), define antisymmetric matrices by
\[
C^0_{ij}=x_i x_j,\qquad
C^{\rm eq}_{ij}=\prod_{r=i}^{j-1}t_r.
\]

The one-spin means and two-spin correlations are
\[
m(z)=U(z)x,
\]
\[
C(z)=C^{\rm eq}
      +U(z)\bigl(C^0-C^{\rm eq}\bigr)U(z)^\top.
\]

The upper entries of \(C(z)\) are the physical pair correlations. Its antisymmetric diagonal is zero; the coincident-spin boundary condition in the correlation hierarchy remains \(\sigma_i^2=1\).

The higher moments are Pfaffians:

- an even-spin moment is the Pfaffian of the corresponding pair-correlation submatrix;
- an odd-spin moment is obtained by adding a ghost row containing the one-spin means.

For the stated deterministic initial configuration, these identities follow directly from the moment hierarchy. At time zero they give the initial products. Differentiation reproduces the nearest-neighbor evolution and its adjacent-collision terms. Uniqueness of the finite linear hierarchy completes the argument.

The remaining issue is that a configuration probability ordinarily involves all \(2^n\) spin moments. A Pfaffian summation identity removes that sum.

Use a ghost index \(0\), the physical indices \(1,\ldots,n\), and one zero dummy index if needed to make the total order even. For a target pattern \(y\), set
\[
M_{0i}=m_i y_i,\qquad
M_{ij}=C_{ij}y_i y_j,
\]
and
\[
J_{ij}=(-1)^{i+j+1}\quad(i<j).
\]

Then
\[
\boxed{
P_z(x,y)=2^{-n}\operatorname{Pf}\bigl(J+M(z)\bigr).
}
\]

Expanding the Pfaffian gives every required moment with coefficient \(+1\), which is exactly the expansion of
\[
2^{-n}\mathbb E_x\prod_i(1+y_i\sigma_i).
\]

Thus a requested transition entry requires an \(n\times n\) exponential and a Pfaffian of order at most \(n+2\). The predictor never constructs the \(2^n\times2^n\) generator.

### Why this is also a bit algorithm

A compact formula alone would leave a precision problem. Here that problem has an explicit bound.

The positive weights

\frac{1-t_{i-1}^2t_i^2}
{\lambda_i(1-t_{i-1}^2)(1-t_i^2)}

symmetrize \(K\). Hence, throughout the right half-plane, a safe complex row-norm bound is
\[
\|U(z)\|\leq
C_U:=2n\frac{\max_iw_i}{\min_iw_i}.

For nonnegative real time, the sharper substochastic bound \(C_U=1\) applies.
