FROM COMPRESSED RELAXATION TO FIRST-PASSAGE LAWS
A certified polynomial-bit construction for inhomogeneous open Ising chains
Research return, 7 November 2026

STATUS AND SCIENTIFIC CONSEQUENCE

This return constructs a deterministic finite-precision first-passage operation.
For the microscopic class specified below it computes the first-hit CDF at any
explicit rational time, a relative enclosure for the mean first-hit time, and
additive enclosures for the probabilities of entering each listed target. A
second finite construction produces a rational record enclosing the entire CDF
uniformly on the positive time axis. All work, including kernel evaluation,
quadrature, inversion, precision selection and record construction, is charged.

The organizing result is a closure principle: a reversible process whose
individual complex-time transition entries have a certified compact evaluator
also has a certified compact first-passage operation for an explicitly listed
finite target set. Killing need not preserve the original correlation closure.
The target renewal matrix handles it. The Ising construction below supplies the
kernel evaluator; it is not assumed as an oracle in that application.

This permits prediction of some exponentially long waiting times in a system
with exponentially many configurations, without simulating the waiting time
or listing the configurations. It does not establish a general rare-event
solver, general two-dimensional nucleation algorithm, experimentally acquired
kinetic model, efficient global design procedure, or P=NP. Historical novelty
and civilisation-changing practical capability are not established by this
return. The result is a fully specified mathematical operation at its stated
scope; the larger ambition remains unresolved.

1. INPUT AND OUTPUT CONTRACT

Input explicitly lists n>=1 spins; rational bonds t_1,...,t_{n-1} in [0,1);
positive rational site clocks lambda_1,...,lambda_n; an initial pattern
x in {-1,+1}^n; and a nonempty list A of m target patterns. Duplicate targets
are removed. The parameters t_i specify tanh(beta J_i) themselves exactly as
rationals. Exact access to arbitrary real beta J_i, their hyperbolic tangents,
or experimentally inferred clocks is not supplied by this representation.

Put t_0=t_n=0 and

 a_i = t_{i-1}(1-t_i^2)/(1-t_{i-1}^2 t_i^2),
 b_i = t_i(1-t_{i-1}^2)/(1-t_{i-1}^2 t_i^2).

The continuous-time Markov chain flips spin i at rate

 c_i(sigma) = lambda_i/2 *
             [1-sigma_i(a_i sigma_{i-1}+b_i sigma_{i+1})].             (1)

Missing boundary terms are zero. Clocks are physical/model time parameters:
they are per-site clocks, not a normalized single global update clock.

For tau_A=inf{t>=0:X_t in A}, and requested precision b>=1, the algorithms
return rational enclosures for:

 (i) Pr_x(tau_A<=T), absolute radius <=2^{-b}, for any supplied positive
     binary-rational T;
 (ii) E_x tau_A, with radius <=2^{-b} E_x tau_A when x is outside A;
 (iii) Pr_x(X_{tau_A}=a), a in A, absolute radius <=2^{-b};
 (iv) the vector E_x[exp(-s tau_A) 1_{X_tau=a}] at any nonzero rational
      complex s with Re(s)>=-|Im(s)|, absolute sum of entry errors <=2^{-b}.

In (iv), the probabilistic expectation is literal for Re(s)>=0. Elsewhere
the output is the analytic continuation defined by the killed resolvent;
the expectation need not exist there. This distinction also applies to the
internal left-half-plane evaluations used for inversion.

If x is in A, tau_A=0 and all answers are immediate. At T=0 the CDF is zero
when x is outside A. No stationary starting distribution is silently substituted
for the specified initial pattern.

There is also an optional finite rational piecewise-polynomial record with
uniform absolute CDF error <=2^{-b} for every T>=0. Its evaluation produces
an enclosure; the polynomial centers themselves need not be monotone CDFs.
No sampler is asserted. Additive accuracy is not a uniform relative guarantee
for all tiny tail or splitting probabilities. Exact symbolic rational means
can have much longer representations; the mean guarantee is a certified
relative enclosure, not an exact rational-expression output contract.

Let L be the complete binary length of the rational model, explicit patterns,
and any query T or s, including numerators and denominators. Precision costs
are polynomial in L+b, counting b as the number of requested accuracy digits.
If b is written in binary, this is not a claim of polynomial cost in log b.
An exponentially large target list costs exponentially many input bits. A
magnetization threshold or cylinder event is not converted to a small list by
this theorem.

2. CLASSICAL CREDIT AND THE ROLE OF CURRENT ASTRA WORK

Glauber introduced the exactly tractable kinetic Ising model. The later
fermionic and generating-function literature includes Felderhof, Aliev, and
Mayer--Sollich. Mayer--Sollich explicitly solve correlation hierarchies; their
published article also describes earlier fermionic and Grassmann approaches.
The one-dimensional correlation mechanism is classical, not a new physical
premise claimed here. The particular open, inhomogeneous formulas used below
are proved directly, so their scope is not inferred from a homogeneous-ring
formula or an inaccessible paper.

The relaxation/first-passage renewal relation is classical. Hartich--Godec,
section 2.3, equation (13), give its single-target Laplace form and develop
the relation between relaxation and first-passage spectra. State reduction for
explicit finite generators is also classical; Sharpe--Wales provide modern
first-passage graph-transformation algorithms. Here the full generator has
2^n states and is not supplied or constructed by the predictor. The work is
the compressed kernel, finite target matrix, and fully calibrated bit-cost
composition; no historical-priority claim is made for that combination.

3. BASIC COMPUTABLE BOUNDS

The stationary law of (1) is

 pi(sigma)=2^{-n} product_{i=1}^{n-1}(1+t_i sigma_i sigma_{i+1}).       (2)

This follows either from the open-chain Gibbs factorization or by checking
detailed balance for every flip. It is positive and normalized (sum spins
successively from an endpoint). Define the explicit rational quantities

 p = 2^{-n} product_i(1-t_i),       C=1/p,
 Lambda=sum_i lambda_i,            G=2 Lambda,
 g=min_i lambda_i(1-a_i-b_i)>0.                                    (3)

Indeed a_i+b_i=(t_{i-1}+t_i)/(1+t_{i-1}t_i), and
1-a_i-b_i=(1-t_{i-1})(1-t_i)/(1+t_{i-1}t_i)>0.
All stationary probabilities are at least p. Every exit rate is <=Lambda.
All eigenvalues of -Q lie in [0,G], by reversibility and Gershgorin.
The spectral gap is at least g; two proofs are available:

* The ordered heat-bath coupling has disagreement probabilities satisfying
  d_i'=-lambda_i d_i+lambda_i a_i d_{i-1}+lambda_i b_i d_{i+1}.
  Their maximum decays at least as exp(-g t), giving total variation at most
  n exp(-g t). Reversibility then gives the stated gap.
* The moment hierarchy in section 4 is triangular by degree, with diagonal
  blocks equal to the exterior powers of K. Its nonzero eigenvalues are sums
  of nonempty subsets of eigenvalues of K. K is symmetrizable and its
  eigenvalues are >=g by Gershgorin.

For D=Omega\A, the killed generator H=-Q_D is positive self-adjoint in
l^2(pi|D). Extending f by zero on A gives

 Var_pi(f) >= pi(A) ||f||_pi^2,
 <f,H f> >= g pi(A) ||f||_pi^2.

Thus d=g pi(A)>0 is a certified killed gap, and

 S_x(t)=Pr_x(tau_A>t) <= C exp(-d t),
 E tau_A <= C/d,      E tau_A^2 <= 2C/d^2.                          (4)

When x is outside A, E tau_A >=1/Lambda: a hit requires at least one jump.
No lower bound on a spacing between individual relaxation eigenvalues is
needed. Numerically coincident eigenvalues create no pole-location subtask.

4. A POLYNOMIAL-DIMENSION TRANSITION KERNEL

Define the n by n tridiagonal matrix K by

 K_ii=lambda_i, K_{i,i-1}=-lambda_i a_i,
 K_{i,i+1}=-lambda_i b_i,
 U(z)=exp(-zK), Re(z)>=0.

For i<j let C0_ij=x_i x_j and Ceq_ij=product_{r=i}^{j-1}t_r, completing both
matrices antisymmetrically and putting zero on their diagonals. Then

 m(z)=U(z)x,
 Cpair(z)=Ceq+U(z)(C0-Ceq)U(z)^T.                                   (5)

The upper entries of Cpair are the two-spin correlations. The diagonal of
this antisymmetric matrix is zero; it must not be confused with the physical
coincident-spin correlation E sigma_i^2=1 used as a boundary condition.

To prove (5), apply the generator to sigma_i and sigma_i sigma_j. Away from
an adjacent collision the equations are -K on each coordinate. At j=i+1,
the source is lambda_i b_i+lambda_{i+1}a_{i+1}. The equilibrium Ceq solves
this affine equation. Subtracting it leaves precisely the exterior-square
evolution. Thus one n by n exponential suffices, rather than an exponential
of order n(n-1)/2 or 2^n.

For any ordered even set I, the moment E product_{i in I}sigma_i equals
Pf(Cpair_I). For an odd set, add a ghost index 0 with entries M_0i=m_i and
take that Pfaffian. Here is a direct preservation proof. Differentiating
the Pfaffian by multilinearity gives the sum of -K acting on each physical
index. The adjacent-pair forcing term gives the lower-order moment when
two neighboring indices coincide. These are exactly the moment hierarchy
equations, since sigma_i^2=1. For repeated neighboring indices the other
terms cancel in pairs and the forced entry 1 leaves the reduced Pfaffian.
At time zero every Pfaffian is the product of the specified initial spins:
the Pfaffian of the skew matrix with all upper entries 1 is 1. Uniqueness of
the finite linear ODE hierarchy proves the identities. Analytic continuation
proves them for complex z as well. Nearest-neighbor order and the stated
rates are essential to this argument.

To obtain a full configuration probability without summing all moments,
let d0 be the smallest even integer >=n+1. Use a ghost index 0, physical
indices 1,...,n, and a zero dummy index if necessary. For target y put

 M_0i=m_i y_i,    M_ij=Cpair_ij y_i y_j,
 M entries involving a dummy =0,
 J_ij=(-1)^{i+j+1} for i<j.

Then

                 P_z(x,y)=2^{-n} Pf(J+M(z)).                        (6)

Proof of the summation identity: the coefficient of Pf(M_S) in the principal-
minor expansion of Pf(J+M) is the permutation sign for (S,S^c) times
Pf(J_{S^c}). For |S|=2r these signs cancel. Thus every even principal minor
of M appears with coefficient +1. Subsets without the ghost are even-spin
moments; subsets with it are odd-spin moments; subsets containing the dummy
have zero M-Pfaffian. Their sum is E product_i(1+y_i sigma_i). Dividing by
2^n gives the pattern indicator probability. Equations (5)--(6) are an
actual finite evaluator, not an unevaluated partition function.

5. NORM CONTROL AND CERTIFIED ARITHMETIC FOR (6)

Exact positive symmetrizing weights are

 w_i=(1-t_{i-1}^2 t_i^2)/[lambda_i(1-t_{i-1}^2)(1-t_i^2)].

They obey w_i lambda_i b_i=t_i/(1-t_i^2)=w_{i+1}lambda_{i+1}a_{i+1}, including
zero bonds. Therefore K is self-adjoint in the weighted inner product.
For all Re(z)>=0 its exponential has complex row-l1 norm bounded by
CU=2n max(w_i)/min(w_i). For real nonnegative z the sharper bound CU=1 holds
because -K generates a substochastic semigroup. In either case the same
bound holds at every scaled time z/2^j.

The implementation stores a complex matrix as two integer matrices divided
by 2^P. Scale A=-zK by 2^{-s} so its row-l1 norm is <=1/8. Round its entries
to this dyadic format; when n2^{-P}<=1/8 the rounded norm is <=1/4.
Use the rounded Taylor recurrence, rounding after each matrix product.
The product with the scaled tridiagonal matrix takes O(n^2) arithmetic
operations per Taylor term. Choose N so

 2(1/4)^{N+1}/(N+1)! <=2^{-P}.

The initial exponential error is <=4n(N+2)2^{-P}. Sum integers exactly and
square s times, rounding at every square. If E<=1, the new error is
<= (2CU+1) E+n2^{-P}. Consequently the final error is at most

 (2 ceil(CU)+2)^s 4n(N+3)2^{-P}.                                   (7)

Increase P until (7) is <= the requested eta<=1. This is an explicit
termination test with only integer/rational arithmetic. Its precision is
polynomial even for large or highly oscillatory complex times; replacing it
by an exp(|z| ||K||) estimate would lose this property.

The pair error caused by an exponential error eta<=1 is bounded by
2n^2 eta(2CU+eta), and the mean error by eta. Thus every perturbed Pfaffian
entry differs by at most Aerr eta, where Aerr=1+2n^2(2CU+1). For real time
the true entries of J+M have magnitude <=2. For complex time a valid bound
is H=4+2n^2 CU^2+CU. If r=d0/2 and the entry error is <=1, multilinearity
gives the Pfaffian Lipschitz factor

       Lip=r(d0-1)!!(H+1)^{r-1}.                                  (8)

Choose eta<=min(1/Aerr,delta/(4 Lip Aerr)). The 2^{-n} normalization and the
explicit dyadic output rounding then leave final error <=delta. The large
value of (8) costs its logarithm in precision; it is not a hidden unit-cost
real-number operation.

All equilibrium pair entries share the denominator D0=product of the bond
denominators. With Uhat=V/2^P, the pair matrix in (5) has common denominator
2^{2P}D0. Form J+M over that denominator and compute its Pfaffian over Gaussian
integers by fraction-free pair elimination. With current pivot p and previous
pivot q (initially q=1), the remaining entries update as

 Aij <- (p Aij-Aki A(k+1)j+Akj A(k+1)i)/q.                           (9)

Simultaneous row/column swaps choose nonzero pivots, and their signs are
tracked. If the relevant remaining row is zero, its Pfaffian is zero. The
divisions in (9) are exact; divisibility is checked. Pfaffian minor identities
bound intermediate bit lengths polynomially. The final pivot, divided by
the common denominator to the r-th power, gives the exact rational midpoint.
No sign of an approximate square root of a determinant is guessed.

Scalar exponentials use the same proved scaling/Taylor/squaring procedure
with n=1. For Re(w)<=0 their complex-l1 bound is 2; for Re(w)<=1 use 4.
If Re(w)<=-k, an exact zero midpoint with error 2^{-k} is admissible. Integer
rounding also compresses intermediate centers and outward error radii to
dyadics. Every such rounding has an allocated error budget.

6. A TARGET RENEWAL MATRIX, NOT A KILLED 2^n-STATE SOLVE

Write R(s)=(sI-Q)^{-1}. For Re(s)>0, the strong Markov property at the first
hit gives, for each target a,

 R_xa(s)=sum_{b in A} h_b(s) R_ba(s),
 h_b(s)=E_x[e^{-s tau_A}1_{X_tau=b}].

Consequently

        h(s)=R_xA(s) R_AA(s)^{-1},
        F(s)=sum_b h_b(s),
        G_x(s)=(1-F(s))/s.                                        (10)

G_x is the Laplace transform of survival. Equations (10) continue
analytically off the negative real axis. Only (m+1)m entries of the original
unkilled resolvent and one m by m inverse are required. This remains true
even though the absorbing modification itself does not obey the Pfaffian
correlation evolution.

There is a useful uniform conditioning bound. Let S=D_pi^{1/2}R D_pi^{-1/2}.
For |arg s|<=3pi/4, rotate 1/(s+lambda), lambda in [0,G], by half the angular
sector. Its real part is at least

       cos(3pi/8)/(|s|+G) > 1/[4(|s|+G)].

Compression to A preserves that real-part bound. Hence

 ||S_AA^{-1}||_2 <=4(|s|+G).                                       (11)

Changing from the weighted norm and using the computable p gives a safe
complex-row-l1 inverse bound B=8m C(|s|_1+G). With sigma=max(|Re s|,|Im s|),
a safe row bound for h is H0=(4m C/sigma)B. For desired row error epsilon,
it suffices to evaluate each resolvent entry with error at most

 delta=min(1,1/(16mB),epsilon/[32mB(1+H0)]).                         (12)

The code also computes an actual a posteriori certificate. If Bhat is the
exact inverse of the rational midpoint matrix, put q=m delta ||Bhat||_infty
(using entrywise complex-l1 as an upper bound). For q<1,

 ||h-hhat||_1 <= m delta (1+||hhat||_1)||Bhat||_infty/(1-q).          (13)

Here the row-l1 error means the sum of complex moduli; the computed norm
bounds may use the larger sums of absolute real and imaginary parts.
Condition (12) ensures termination with polynomially many digits. A zero
midpoint pivot or unverified inverse is never silently accepted.

7. CONSTRUCTING R(s) WITHOUT ITS FULL SPECTRUM

For Im(s)>=0 and Re(s)>=-|Im(s)| put z=1-2i. Then
Re(sz)>=sigma=max(|Re s|,|Im s|)>0 and |Im(sz)|<=3sigma. By reversibility,

 |P_zr(x,y)-pi(y)| <= C exp(-g r),  r>=0.

An eigenwise integral, used for proof only, yields

 R_xy(s)=pi(y)/s+
          integral_0^infinity z exp(-szr)[P_zr(x,y)-pi(y)] dr.       (14)

Indeed each nonstationary eigenvalue lambda>=g contributes
integral_0^infinity z exp[-z(s+lambda)r]dr=1/(s+lambda).
The code never enumerates these eigenvalues. For Im(s)<0 use conjugation.
Subtracting pi analytically prevents the integration interval from extending
to 1/|s| merely because s is tiny; the centered integrand decays at least as
exp[-(sigma+g)r].

Left and right tails at l and R are bounded by

 3C l,                3C exp[-(sigma+g)R]/(sigma+g).                (15)

For a bank of queries with sigma>=sigma_min, choose dyadic l<=delta/(32C).
Choose integer k with 3C 2^{-k}/(sigma_min+g)<=delta/16, and successively
double l until R>=k/(sigma_min+g). The number of panels is logarithmic in
these scales. The inequality exp(-k)<=2^{-k} makes the tail certificate
rational and explicit.

On a geometric panel r in [a,2a], write r=a q. The Bernstein ellipse with
parameter rho=3/2 around [1,2] has

 Re(q)>=23/24,   |Im(q)|<=5/24.

Thus Re(zq)>=13/24 and Re(szq)>=sigma/3. The analytic centered integrand
in (14) has modulus <=3C throughout the ellipse. It therefore admits the
certified quadrature in section 8 with polynomial degree.

KernelRay constructs one reusable finite record of the actual P_zr entries
at all required rational nodes. Subsequent s queries compute only scalar
exponentials and weighted sums, then the target solve. Every record entry
was computed by sections 4--5 and all that computation is charged. There is
no physical-reading or relaxation oracle in this operation.

8. THE QUADRATURE IS FINITE AND CERTIFIED

For a function f analytic and bounded by Mf on the rho=3/2 ellipse around
[lo,hi], set h=(hi-lo)/2, M=h Mf, and g(v)=h f(center+h v) on [-1,1].
With N=p+1 Chebyshev-root nodes x_j=cos((2j+1)pi/(2N)), use weights

 w_j=(2/N)[1+2 sum_{2<=k<=p, k even} T_k(x_j)/(1-k^2)].              (16)

They exactly integrate the interpolation polynomial of degree p. A direct
Chebyshev-series bound gives integrated interpolation error at most
16M(2/3)^p. Approximate every x_j by a rational within eta, clamped to [-1,1],
and evaluate the same finite formula (16) at those rational nodes. The sum
of absolute weights is <=4. Also |T_k'|<=k^2 on this interval, and a radius
1/24 disk around each real point lies inside the ellipse. Cauchy's estimate
then bounds the perturbation from nodes and weights by

                 (128+4N)M eta.                                  (17)

If each value of g has certified error <=nu, the additional integral error
is <=4nu. Choosing the three contributions below assigned fractions of the
panel budget supplies the stopping test in analytic_quad/quadrature_plan.

For efficient finite arithmetic the code also rounds the recurrence used in
(16). With scale 2^P, its Chebyshev-value error is <=4^N 2^{-P}; the complete
weight error is <=8*4^N 2^{-P}. Choose P from this inequality, enforce error
<=1/(8N^2) per weight, and charge M times the sum of weight errors. The strict
margin in the elementary bound on the exact weights preserves the computed
absolute-weight sum <=4. This avoids exact high-degree rational intermediates
and adds only O(N) guard digits, all included in the cost.

Pi is enclosed using the rational alternating arctangent series and
pi=16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239). Cosine nodes are evaluated as real parts
of the certified exponential at imaginary arguments. The argument error,
exponential error and dyadic rounding are all included in eta. Formula (16)
uses only the three-term rational Chebyshev recurrence. The finite ray
record rounds weights with additional error 3C sum|delta w_j| explicitly
charged, and stores dyadic centers and outward-rounded radii. This avoids
unnecessary denominator growth without changing the proof.

9. A CONSTRUCTED CDF INVERSION

Let H=-Q_D be the killed generator. Its spectrum is in [d,G]. The scalar
survival transform is a matrix element of (s+H)^{-1}, with bound

 |G_x(s)| <= C/dist(s,-[d,G]).                                     (18)

For a positive query T put w=sT. The upper half of a positively oriented
Dunford contour goes from w=1 vertically to 1+i, left to -1+i, and then
out along w=(-1+i)r, r>=1. If I is the integral along this half contour of
exp(w)G_x(w/T) dw/T, then

                    S_x(T)=Im(I)/pi.                              (19)

The lower half is its reversed conjugate. Closing to the left proves (19)
by residues; all residues are exp(-lambda T), with no need to locate lambda.
In terms of the constructed F from (10), the integrand is

                 exp(w)(1-F(w/T)) dw/w.                           (20)

The three parametrizations in the code are

 w=1+iv, v in [0,1],          dw/dv=i;
 w=u+i, u in [-1,1],          orientation multiplier -1;
 w=(-1+i)r, r in [1,infty),   dw/dr=-1+i.

All actual nodes have Re(s)>=-|Im(s)|, so the calibrated ray and inverse
contracts apply. On the rho=3/2 ellipses for the finite segments, distance
from the negative real axis is bounded below by a fixed multiple of 1/T;
exp(Re w) is bounded by a fixed constant. A bound 16C is sufficient for
each finite-segment integrand. For a ray panel r in [a,2a], the corresponding
analytic integrand is bounded by 16C/a. The ray tail past R>=1 is <=2C exp(-R)
in the unnormalized half integral.

Choose k so 2C 2^{-k} is below its assigned error, and R the smallest power
of two >=k. Apply the explicit quadrature above to the two finite segments
and the logarithmically many ray panels. Determine every required F accuracy
from (20), choose the most stringent (12), and build one common KernelRay
record. Perform the target solves and scalar multiplications with their
recorded errors. Round each contour contribution with an allocated error.
Finally use the rational pi interval in (19), and clip the CDF enclosure to
[0,1]. This is a finite inversion algorithm, not an invocation of a numerical
Laplace-inversion oracle or a conjectured relaxation time.

10. MEAN AND ENTRANCE PROBABILITIES

For positive s, let T_s=(1-F(s))/s. Since 1-e^{-u}<=u and
u-(1-e^{-u})<=u^2/2 for u>=0, (4) gives

          0<=E tau_A-T_s<=s C/d^2.                                (21)

For requested relative epsilon use
s=epsilon d^2/(16C Lambda), and evaluate F to row error
epsilon s/(32 Lambda). Dividing the certified transform error by s and
adding the one-sided bias (21) gives the stated relative enclosure, because
E tau_A>=1/Lambda. The small rational s and the cancellation in 1-F(s) cost
extra digits explicitly; their logarithmic sizes are polynomial in the input.

For each entrance probability q_a=h_a(0),

             |q_a-h_a(s)|<=s E tau_A<=s C/d.                       (22)

Use s=epsilon d/(8C), evaluate the transform to error epsilon/8, and add
the bias. No mixing-start assumption is made.

A useful exact specialization makes the rare-event demonstration less
conservative. Let y be any pattern and start at -y. Global spin reversal
commutes with the zero-field reversible generator. In a joint eigenbasis,

 D_y(t)=P_t(y,y)-P_t(-y,y)

has a nonnegative spectral measure supported on [g,G], of total mass one.
Only odd spin-reversal modes remain. Hence D_y is completely monotone,
|D_y(z)|<=1 for Re z>=0, D_y(t)<=exp(-g t), and

 E_{-y} tau_y = [integral_0^infinity D_y(t)dt]/pi(y),
                 1/G <= integral D_y <=1/g.                       (23)

The mean identity follows by expanding the single-target renewal ratio at
s=0. For computation D_y is one Pfaffian with the ghost row of J replaced
by zero; the other ghost entries are the one-spin means. Linearity in that
row extracts twice the odd part without subtracting two close probabilities.
Integrate over geometric real panels with the same certified quadrature.
An absolute integral error epsilon/(4G) suffices for relative mean error
epsilon/4, even if pi(y) is exponentially small. The final division by the
exact rational pi(y) is charged. This is an optimization of the completed
generic mean construction, not its only solved case.

11. A FINITE RECORD FOR THE ENTIRE TIME LAW

This optional construction is implemented by compile_cdf, using the already
constructed CDF algorithm as a subroutine with every call counted.

Choose dyadic l<=epsilon/(32 Lambda). For t<=l, F_CDF(t)<=Lambda t. Choose
k with C2^{-k}<=epsilon/32 and a dyadic endpoint R>=k/d. For t>=R, the survival
bound (4) is <=epsilon/32. Cover [l,R] by geometric panels [a,2a].

On the rho=3/2 ellipse for each panel, the analytic CDF is bounded by C+1.
Choose p so 8(C+1)(2/3)^p<=epsilon/8. Use N=p+1 approximate Chebyshev nodes
with error eta<=epsilon/[128(C+1)(N^3+24N)]. Evaluate the CDF at every node
to error delta=epsilon/(64N). Construct its Chebyshev coefficients by the
finite discrete-cosine formulas, evaluating T_k at the rational nodes; round
each coefficient within epsilon/(128N).

The interpolation error is <=epsilon/8. The total coefficient perturbation
is bounded by

 2N delta+(48N+4N^3)(C+1)eta,

plus the stated rounding. These bounds sum to less than epsilon. Outside
the panel range use centers 0 and 1. The record is a finite list of rational
endpoints and coefficients; query evaluation is rational Chebyshev recurrence
and clipping. It supplies uniform certified CDF values, not an exact symbolic
expansion into all killed modes. Mean accuracy is separately supplied by
section 10 and is not inferred from uniform additive CDF accuracy alone.

12. EXPLICIT POLYNOMIAL BIT COST

The following is a deliberately loose worst-case theorem, not a performance
forecast. In a conventional bit model using integer arithmetic with schoolbook
multiplication/division and Euclidean gcd, all outputs above, including the
optional entire-time record, can be constructed within

                   2^240 (16+L+b)^60 bit operations.               (24)

The model uses only a fixed program; no instance advice, precomputed spectra,
uncounted acquisition, exponential-state preprocessing, or physical ground-
state operation appears. Formula (24) is far from an optimized bound.

Here are the size controls underlying an explicit bound, with B=16+L+b.
Because each rational is explicitly supplied, 1-t_i is either a positive
rational with denominator already charged or a boundary value zero that is
excluded. Products in p and pi(A), and the local formulas for g, have O(L)
bit lengths after rational reduction. More concretely, log_2 C, log_2(1/g),
log_2 G and log_2 CU are bounded by a fixed linear multiple of L, allowing
positive and negative logarithms as appropriate. The same applies to the
query's magnitude and inverse magnitude. Summing m explicitly listed target
probabilities uses their common product denominator and charged pattern bits.

Consequences of the explicit choices in the preceding sections are:

* Ray cutoffs, killed-time cutoffs and all tail exponents have O(B) binary
  logarithms. The geometric panel count and interpolation degrees are O(B).
  Each ray or whole-time record has O(B^2) nodes or coefficients.
* Equation (12) and every nested requested scalar/kernel tolerance require
  O(B) accuracy digits. The logarithm of (8) is O(B^2). Matrix-exponential
  precision (7) is O(B^2), with O(B) squarings and at most O(B^2) Taylor terms.
* Each transition batch computes an n by n exponential, at most m+1 pair
  matrices, and at most (m+1)m Pfaffians of order at most n+2. Every target
  inverse is only m by m. Here n,m<=L because they are explicitly listed.
* The entire-time construction makes O(B^2) CDF calls. Each CDF uses O(B^2)
  outer nodes and a single O(B^2)-node kernel record. Scalar exponentials,
  exact weighted sums and target inversions are all included.

For a direct conservative tally one may allow 2^60 B^16 rational/integer
arithmetic operations and 2^40 B^12 bits per intermediate integer. These
bounds include the uncompressed rational Machin series, the finite Chebyshev
weights, exact fraction-free Pfaffian minors, exact Gauss-Jordan minors,
and rational error expressions before their next dyadic compaction. Polynomial
degrees in the preceding bullets are smaller than these allowances: a rational
sum has at most the sum of its input denominator lengths, each determinant/
Pfaffian intermediate has at most its minor degree times its entry length,
and the only repeated squaring is rounded as in (7). No exponential-degree
polynomial is formed. Taking a cubic bit bound for elementary integer/gcd
work gives less than (24), including preprocessing and output writing.

The retained entire-time record itself has O(B^2) coefficients of O(B) bits,
with similarly bounded endpoints; a cruder explicit cap is 2^40 B^8 retained
bits. A query to that record costs polynomially in the record length and the
complete bit length of its supplied rational time. Its construction can be
much more expensive than its use, and that construction is not free.

13. THE GENERAL STRUCTURAL THEOREM

The Ising-specific work can be separated from a reusable closure statement.
Suppose a uniformly described finite irreducible reversible chain has:

 (a) explicitly queryable state labels and stationary weights for those labels;
 (b) supplied/proved rational p>0, g>0, G, Lambda with pi>=p, gap>=g,
     spectrum(-Q)<=G and maximum exit rate<=Lambda;
 (c) a constructed evaluator for P_z(x,y), Re z>=0, with certified absolute
     error and cost polynomial in its complete input and accuracy digits;
 (d) polynomial-bit descriptions of these bounds and of the evaluator.

Then sections 6--11 construct the finite-target first-passage outputs with
polynomial bit overhead and polynomially many actual evaluator calls. A
complex-time kernel oracle without its construction and cost would only give
a conditional theorem. Sections 4--5 discharge that requirement for (1).

The proof uses neither a polynomial number of relaxation modes nor an
eigenvalue-spacing promise. It does use reversibility for sectorial positivity
and analytic norm control, a small explicitly enumerated target boundary,
and a genuinely computable complex-time kernel. Fast real-time sampling or
an equilibrium effective law does not automatically furnish (c).

14. CONSEQUENCES AND BOUNDARIES

Rarity and computational obstruction separate in this class. At fixed
ferromagnetic bond strengths, some complete patterns have stationary weight
exponentially small in n. Equation (23) then gives exponentially long mean
waiting times even when g is bounded away from zero. The computation depends
on n and the accuracy digits, rather than the numerical mean waiting time.
The closed form does not arise from a high stationary probability of the
target or from a stationary initial condition.

An explicit extensive family makes the distinction quantitative. Take
n=3k+1, periodic bonds (3/4,4/5,2/3), periodic clocks (1,2/3,7/5,5/4),
an alternating target y, and initial state -y. Every coefficient has bounded
description length, g>=1/48, G<=14n/5, and

 pi(y)=1/(2*480^k),
 (5/(7n))*480^k <= E_{-y}tau_y <=96*480^k.                           (25)

The lower and upper bounds follow directly from (23), not a fitted barrier
or an assumed exponential waiting-time law. Each flip rate is at least 1/96,
so an event-by-event exact trajectory simulation has expected jump count at
least (n/96)E tau_y, which is exponential in n. This is a cost statement about
that simulation rule, not a lower bound on other prediction algorithms. The
constructed relative-mean evaluator is polynomial-bit for every member of
this explicitly encoded family. The executed n=16 test is k=5, with exact
target stationary probability 1/50,960,793,600,000.

Many-target competition is covered by the matrix identity (10), without
replacing the initial condition by an equilibrium flux approximation.
Inhomogeneous bonds and clocks remain individual explicit inputs. New supplied
coefficient choices can be evaluated, but learning those coefficients from
macroscopic data is a different input contract. A static energy law alone
does not identify the clocks: multiplying all lambda_i by alpha leaves (2)
unchanged and divides every nonzero hitting time by alpha.

Signed nearest-neighbor zero-field couplings on an open path can also be
included by an exact gauge change: choose eps_1=1 and
eps_{i+1}=eps_i sign(t_i), taking sign(0)=1, replace sigma_i by eps_i sigma_i,
and replace t_i by |t_i|. Transform every explicit pattern accordingly. This
adds linear preprocessing. It supplies no extension to frustrated higher-
dimensional graphs, external fields, or unrestricted rates.

An exact input with some t_i=1 is outside the finite-temperature irreducible
contract; the gap/mean arguments cannot silently be continued through that
boundary. A succinct set such as {magnetization>=0} may contain exponentially
many patterns and is outside the polynomial-list promise. Arbitrary nonlinear
observables, arbitrary correlated initial distributions, exact symbolic tail
expansions, and global continuous/discrete kinetic design are not obtained by
calling this routine. The uniform CDF record is an approximation enclosure,
not a universal compressed representation of the entire stochastic process.

The original unrestricted exact-QUBO question, the previously stated effective-
response endpoint at exactly power tau, and the two-dimensional physical
nucleation target remain unresolved. No smaller solved class is used as their
resolution. The accepted static interface transfer theorem remains a theorem
about static interfaces; this kinetic construction does not retrofit dynamic
information into those interfaces.

15. IMPLEMENTATION AND EVIDENCE

chain_passage.py implements the finite constructions. The predictor contains
no floating-point linear algebra or full-state generator. NumPy is used only
for arrays of exact Python integers. The validation program uses small full
generators separately as comparators; their cost and state enumeration belong
to validation, not the prediction algorithm.

Executed inputs, enclosure values, timings, maximum working precision, kernel
record sizes and comparison results are recorded in RESULTS.json and the
individual validation records. Those records distinguish executed tests from
the optional entire-time record construction, which is proved and implemented
but was not run to completion for a nontrivial chain in this session. This is
not presented as an empirical validation of every input or of the asymptotic
bit theorem. Correctness and complexity rest on the proof above.

Earlier direct rational implementations of the one-spin contour test, and a
competing-target mean run, were interrupted because they retained unnecessarily
large rational intermediates.
The final version explicitly charges dyadic compaction and uses the certified
scalar exponential for node cosines and bounded dyadic Chebyshev recurrences
for weights. These are computational improvements,
not new scientific evidence for the theorem. The final measured implementation
remains conservative; polynomial bit complexity does not imply a competitive
general-purpose numerical package.

16. RETAINED FAILED ROUTES

A small equilibrium operator perturbation need not yield dimension-free total
variation accuracy after a large quench. For independent fair-equilibrium spins
started all minus, compare unit clocks with half the clocks 1+rho and half
1-rho. At a fixed positive t the normalized magnetization means differ by
exp(-t)(cosh(rho t)-1)>0. Unnormalized means then separate by order n while
standard deviations are order sqrt(n), so the total variation distance tends
to one. This refutes that proposed uniform implication only, not all kinetic
approximations or algorithms.

For the two-dimensional contour route, closing a connected microscopic cluster
under two-neighbor filling can fill its bounding rectangle without increasing
bare energy. That observation alone does not preserve the entropy/weighted
current of all microscopic configurations and does not construct the full
physical transition-time algorithm. The stronger conclusion was not used.

A proposed side-trap network warning in WORKING_MATHEMATICS.txt was exploratory
and was not promoted to a proved Ising counterexample or a general lower bound.
The SAT heat-bath failure family and all earlier scopes remain in the preserved
predecessor archives. No unrestricted algorithmic lower bound is claimed.

PRECISION ACCOUNTING CLARIFICATION

The implementation statistic max_fixed_bits counts fractional bits in rounded
matrix exponentials. It does not count the largest exact integer operand. If
the integer skew matrix has entries bounded by 2^W and order d0=2r, each
fraction-free remaining entry is bounded by (d0-1)!!*2^{rW}. Update products
and the Gaussian exact-division cross-products therefore require at most a
constant multiple of rW+log_2((d0-1)!!) bits. The common denominator has
2P+log_2(D0) bits, and W also includes the proved correlation magnitude bound.
This arithmetic is included in section 12. For the executed real n=16 example,
P<=145, D0=60^5, d0=18 and entry magnitudes <=3 times the common denominator.
One may take W=322; a conservative bound on integer operands inside its
fraction-free Pfaffian calculation is 9,000 bits. This is a proved upper
bound, not an instrumented maximum, and not a claim about every other routine.
