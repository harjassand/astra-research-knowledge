## Completed response

Result

I developed a materially different candidate: a ground-mounted cradle with short opposing support contacts, removable contacts in the original joint, and exposed sections that can be cut to retrieve a jammed support.

Its useful change is to separate three operations that the previous architecture made unnecessarily dependent on one another:

Capture movement mechanically, without trying to establish a precise six-axis force balance.
Remove the original joint’s actual load-bearing contacts, without translating the supported member.
Establish final isolation after substantial physical withdrawal, with an independent cutting route when ordinary withdrawal jams.

This removes the long support legs, microscopic final optical corridors, structural keeper, second keeper and keeper-ear recovery sequence. Healthy exchanges consume no escape sections.

It does not yet satisfy the complete brief. The executed model identifies substantive remaining failures:

The strut’s catch bound becomes 1.025 mm when calculated target deformation is added to the raw sensor-error budget.
The conservative jam-release calculation reaches 3.548 kNm, exceeding the proposed 3.5 kNm internal strength screen.
Complete approach and retrieval trajectories remain unverified for several real tool and frame bodies.
The prospective human-work reduction is 7.69-fold, below tenfold. An automated competitor allowed to adopt the same mechanism reaches economic parity.

These are retained in the deliverables; they are not replaced with favorable assumptions.

1. The proposed mechanism

The central construction is an exposed, enumerable load boundary.

Every structural route from a temporary support to the member passes through one accessible neck. Every route from that retrievable support to the grounded cradle passes through a separate accessible foot. Guides, retention keys and housings stay on their respective sides of these boundaries.

The normal release is a reusable screw-and-shoe operation. The independent escape removes material from the exposed neck. It does not require the seized screw, guide or receiver to reverse.

The important simplification is the ownership of the intermediate body. A pod-owned cage captures the head after a neck cut. It has no attachment to the member root. Consequently, it does not introduce another structural grip whose release must succeed before isolation can be established.

For a seized final foot, the member-side path is already absent. A mechanically holding gantry grip then owns the pod while the independent foot neck is cut. The seized receiver and its deck can subsequently be removed as a package from the grounded side.

This is a specific engineering proposal, not a claim that shoring or cutting a seized connector is new. Temporary load transfer, replacement and removal already appear in US8997873B2; resettable rolling release mechanisms appear in US6450064B1. JAXA’s LSRD is also concrete reusable release hardware, but its stated 20-operation life and axial release specification do not supply this maintenance service.

Only the cartridges, bolts and pins bridge the joint. There is no broad mating face that remains stuck after the bolts are removed.

The M12 bolt heads face the fixed side. The M24 cartridges are threaded into the moving flange. This puts the bulky high-torque tool on the fixed side; the moving side needs only a compact tool for the unloaded cartridges.

The removal sequence is:

Capture each bolt head.
Back the bolt out far enough to dissipate its bounded preload while substantial threads remain engaged.
Remove the slack bolt.
Retract the bearing cartridge by 3 mm.
Withdraw both shear pins completely.
Collect the complete cartridge/bolt/pin kit.

The supported member need not translate to open the joint.

The exchanged manufactured components are the cartridges, bolts and pins. The welded flanges remain permanent service interfaces and are paid accordingly.

B. Moment-carrying strut with removable radial keys

The strut has:

An 80 mm square tongue.
An 180 mm outer / 86 mm clear socket.
Eight 60 × 25 mm bearing keys.
Two bearing stations 130 mm apart.
Two 16 mm axial retention pins.
3 mm radial clearance after key removal.
20 mm clearance behind the tongue tip.

The keys withdraw radially by 75 mm. The retaining pins have a specified 230 mm complete withdrawal route. Thus the joint can lose all original bearing contacts without pulling the member 100 mm axially.

The socket replaces the relevant portion of the fixed stub. It is not concealed inside an unwindowed outer tube.

Its 150 × 50 × 25 mm caps use two M14-10.9 bolts each. Moment resistance comes from the separated bearing stations and the socket structure, not from treating a pivoting clevis as a moment joint.

Fastener removal requires a real torque tool

Including thread and under-head friction makes a bare 50 Nm spindle inadequate.

The selected construction has 10:1 reduction, an introduced minimum efficiency of 80%, and a measured 300 Nm output limit. The torque calculation includes under-head friction. Torque is not treated as a perfect preload measurement.

For the face, residual thread torque transmitted to the moving member consumes its wrench budget. For the strut, the bolt and reaction shoe are both on the fixed socket; parasitic reactions on the tongue still require measurement.

The gearbox, reaction shoe and their full swept envelopes remain qualification work. Their price is included; their successful fit is not assumed.

Use several load levels and both signs. Include persistent and partly open jams, false engagement, seized final receiver, friction/bias variation and one-hour outages.

Measure independently:

Applied load.
Both pad reactions.
Replacement reaction and transient peaks.
Cage and foot reactions.
Relative displacement during every transfer.
Screw, lock and retention states.
Blade speed, force, feed, tension and runout.
Full wall energy.
All fragments, rejected hardware and returned stocks.
Wear, permanent set and before/after sensor bias.

The discriminating result is a replacement carrying the declared load after every temporary path and tool has left, followed by a complete reset.

A clean cut, lower trigger force, an open-looking gap or a surviving magnet-like pull test would not establish that result.

This experiment can establish or reject the local ownership, release and observation mechanism. It cannot establish the full six-axis frame, complete gantry routes, 500-cycle life, annual calibration interval or economic advantage.

Engineering decision

The operation worth retaining is removing the original contacts without moving the member, capturing motion with short mechanically retained supports, and making each jam escapable through one exposed section whose detached body already has an owner. Large final withdrawal makes absence evidence much more manageable than the previous microscopic optical construction.

The present candidate nevertheless remains an incomplete engineering construction, not an achieved economical invention. Its outstanding work is specific: installed capture uncertainty, intermediate outage dynamics, complete tool routes, primary and cage transient qualification, the non-bypassing test source, and the human-work ledger. The model and report preserve those limits rather than converting 500 conditional controller completions into 500 qualified services.
