## 6. Further deductions that follow directly

### Positive curvature close to one does not restore predictability

Consider
\[
\Sigma=\{q:V(q)=1/2\}.
\]

Strong convexity makes \(\Sigma\) a smooth, closed, strictly convex hypersurface. The encoded mechanical curves remain on it, have unit speed, and have acceleration normal to it. They are therefore geodesics of its induced metric.

The Hessian bound gives explicit bounds on every sectional curvature \(K\):
\[
\boxed{
\frac{(1-\eta)^3}{(1+\eta)^2}
\le K\le
\frac{(1+\eta)^3}{(1-\eta)^2}.
}
\]

Both endpoints tend to \(1\) as \(\eta\to0\).

Thus the same promised-limit prediction obstruction occurs for encoded geodesics on strictly convex hypersurfaces with positive curvature arbitrarily close to unit curvature.

This statement concerns the constructed invariant family. It supplies no assertion about typical geodesics, positive-volume sets of initial conditions, or transverse stability.

### Long-time averages inherit the obstruction

A bounded convergent function and its Cesàro mean have the same limit. Hence
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}
\frac1T\int_0^T O(q_w(t))\,dt
\]
is the same halting bit.

No ergodicity assumption or additional physical application is needed for this deduction.

### Finite-discount response is computable, while its limiting extrapolation is not uniformly computable

For rational \(\lambda>0\), define
\[
A_w(\lambda)
=
\lambda\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}O(q_w(t))\,dt.
\]

For each positive \(\lambda\), this quantity is computable. To obtain \(b\) bits of absolute accuracy, truncate at
\[
T=\frac{b+3}{\lambda}.
\]
The discarded tail is smaller than \(2^{-b-3}\); certified finite-time integration and quadrature handle the remaining interval.

Nevertheless,
\[
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}A_w(\lambda)
=
\lim_{t\to\infty}O(q_w(t))
\]
is the halting bit.

Consequently there is no uniform computable positive cutoff \(\lambda_0(w)\) guaranteeing an error below \(1/4\) for all smaller discounts. Such a cutoff, followed by a finite-discount evaluation, would decide halting.

This identifies precisely where a proposed universal inference procedure would fail: finite computations remain available, but a universally computable rule certifying their asymptotic sufficiency does not.
