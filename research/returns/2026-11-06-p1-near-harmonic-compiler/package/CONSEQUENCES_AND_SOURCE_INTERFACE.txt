WHAT THE COMPILER IMPLIES, AND THE EXACT SOURCE INTERFACE
6 November 2026

This document composes the compiler in THEOREM_AND_ALGORITHM.txt with a
published polynomial-ODE simulator and an explicit compactification/latch.
The compactification and latch are credited to the publicly retrieved
Astra N716 source, section 6, rather than claimed as a new idea. Each
interface needed by this composition is checked below. N716 is a
source-derived proof candidate; the argument below does not assume that
all claims in that packet or its surrounding research are accepted.

The resulting consequences concern exact, computably specified classical
differential equations and computable initial conditions. They are neither
a proposal for a laboratory computer nor a claim about physical noise.

1. IMPORTED PRIMARY THEOREM

Cardona, Miranda and Peralta-Salas, "Turing universality of the
incompressible Euler equations and a conjecture of Moore",
arXiv:2104.04356v1, Theorem 3.1, states a polynomial-ODE simulation theorem,
crediting Graca, Campagnolo and Buescu, "Computability with polynomial
differential equations", Advances in Applied Mathematics 40 (2008),
330-349.

The interface used here is:

  Given a fixed Turing machine, and error e=1/8, construct a polynomial
  nonautonomous ODE and fixed auxiliary initial data. For every integer
  encoding of a machine configuration, the first three coordinates of its
  solution remain within e of the j-th discrete configuration throughout
  each interval [j,j+1/2], for all j>=0.

Adding the time coordinate omega'=1 gives an autonomous polynomial system
x'=P(x). The cited primary paper explicitly performs this step. Its
Remark 3.4 reports a universal simulator with 17 autonomous coordinates
and polynomial degree 56. The halting state is made absorbing in its
definition of the discrete transition function.

This is an accepted constructive theorem used as a building block, not
an unconstructed claim that a generic ODE probably simulates a machine.
The present work has not expanded or executed a numerical table of the
universal polynomial coefficients. "Constructible" is used as an
effective coefficient/initialization interface; no all-rational or
polynomial-time coefficient-generation claim is inferred. Actual
coefficient-generation programs, and their costs, enter the computable
coefficient extension in Section 9 of the compiler.

For the existence/undecidability theorem, any fixed universal simulator
supplied by this interface suffices. The dimension 1852 below uses the
specific 17-coordinate bound reported by the source; without that
numerical bound the same proof gives a fixed finite, explicitly
constructible dimension.

2. EXPLICIT COMPACTIFICATION, WITH ITS TIME COST

Let q be the machine-state coordinate and q_h its integer halting label.
Add h with

  h'=4*P_q(x),      h(0)=4*(q(0)-q_h).

Thus h=4*(q-q_h) exactly along valid trajectories. For the reported
simulator the enlarged system has k=18 real coordinates. Choose an
integer delta>=max(1,deg P), for example delta=56.

For every real coordinate x_j put

  a_j=(x_j^2-1)/(1+x_j^2),       b_j=2*x_j/(1+x_j^2).

These lie on a unit circle. The inverse on finite x_j is
x_j=b_j/(1-a_j). Define

  D_c(a)=2^(-k*delta) * product_j (1-a_j)^delta,
  Pi_j(a,b)=D_c(a)*P_j(b_1/(1-a_1),...,b_k/(1-a_k)).

Each Pi_j is a polynomial: every exponent in a monomial of P is at most
delta. In a new time sigma use

  a_j'=b_j*(1-a_j)*Pi_j,
  b_j'=-a_j*(1-a_j)*Pi_j.

Also add the clock circle (c,s), initially (1,0), with

  c'=-2*pi*s*D_c,       s'=2*pi*c*D_c.

The circle tangency identities hold algebraically, so this is a polynomial
field on a compact product torus and is complete there. On valid encoded
trajectories it represents the original simulator with

  d tau/d sigma = D_c > 0,       D_c <= 1.

For every finite original time T, the original solution is bounded on
[0,T], so D_c has a positive minimum there. Hence

  sigma(T)=integral_0^T 1/D_c(a(tau)) d tau < infinity,
  sigma(T)>=T.

All finite source events therefore occur at finite compactified times, and
infinite source time is not compressed into finite time. There is no useful
uniform lower bound on D_c over all source configurations; its possible
smallness is a real runtime cost. The clock satisfies s=sin(2*pi*tau).

Expanding these formulas into monomials, and then into finite Fourier
coefficients on the product torus, is an explicit finite algebraic
computation. The number of expanded terms and their coefficient precision
are paid for; sparse notation is not counted as free full expansion.

3. A FIXED GAP AND A LATCH THAT ALWAYS CONVERGES

Use the polynomial raw output

  P_out=(1+s)^4*(1-a_h)/32
       =((1+s)/2)^4 / (1+16*(q-q_h)^2).

It lies in [0,1] on the product torus.

For a nonhalting input, on every monitored interval [j,j+1/2], the
integer state differs from q_h by at least one, so |q-q_h|>=7/8.
There P_out<=4/53. Between monitored intervals s<=0, so the clock
factor is at most 1/16. Consequently

  P_out<=4/53 at every time for every nonhalting input.

If the machine halts at step j, then at source time j+1/4 the clock
has s=1 and |q-q_h|<1/8, giving P_out>=4/5. That is a finite,
uniformly separated pulse. One such pulse suffices for what follows.

Call the compact field built so far Y0 and its variables xi. Choose a
computable rational M>=max(1, ||D P_out * Y0||_infinity). The absolute
sum of its polynomial coefficients on the unit cube is an effective
upper bound. For computable coefficients use certified rational upper
bounds, not a global maximization oracle.

Adjoin

  u'=60*M*(2-u)*(u*(u+1)+P_out),
  l'=u*l*(1-l),                   (u,l)(0)=(-1,1/32).

The rectangle [-1,2] x [0,1] is forward invariant. Uniqueness gives
0<l<1 at every finite time from the stated initial condition.

Nonhalting case: at u=-1/2,

  u'=150*M*(P_out-1/4)<0.

Hence u remains in [-1,-1/2], and
d/dsigma log(l/(1-l))=u<=-1/2. It follows that l decreases to zero.

Halting case: at the pulse time P_out>=4/5. Its derivative bound makes
P_out>=7/10>2/3 for the next interval of length 1/(10*M).
For -1<=u<=1 in that interval,

  u*(u+1)+P_out>=5/12,         u'>=25*M.

Thus u reaches 1 within at most 2/(25*M)<1/(10*M), unless it was
already there. The region u>=1 is forward invariant, and there
u'>=120*M*(2-u). In particular u tends to 2, while l tends to 1.

This proof depends on exact real dynamics: l stays positive even if it
has become extremely small during a long nonhalting prefix. It supplies
no lower bound on the tolerated absolute initialization or force error
for arbitrarily delayed halting.

Compactify u and l by the same two circle transformations, and multiply
the previous field and latch equations by

  E_c=2^(-6)*(1-a_u)^3*(1-a_l)^3.

After substituting u=b_u/(1-a_u), l=b_l/(1-a_l), all denominators
clear, since both scalar latch polynomials have degree at most three
in each new variable. Use the same tangent-circle formulas as above.
On the valid invariant rectangle,

  1/1000 <= E_c <= 1.

The new time therefore still tends to infinity with sigma and preserves
all finite triggering events and limiting values.

The resulting system has k+3=21 circles for the reported simulator.
Each circle's angular velocity is a polynomial in the circle coordinates:
if a'=b*(1-a)*Pi and b'=-a*(1-a)*Pi then angular velocity is
-(1-a)*Pi. Thus it is precisely a finite Fourier field on T^21,
which is an input to the mechanical compiler.

Use the single circle coordinate

  f=b_l=2*l/(1+l^2)

as observable. On every valid input it lies in [0,1] and converges.
For nonhalting inputs it is always at most 64/1025<1/8 and tends to 0.
For halting inputs it tends to 1 and is eventually at least 3/4.

4. A FIXED NEAR-HARMONIC NATURAL SYSTEM WITH UNDECIDABLE LIMITS

Fix the universal machine once. For any prescribed rational
0<eta<1, apply the compiler to the preceding field and readout.
It outputs a fixed potential V_eta and fixed homogeneous quadratic
position observable O. Only the encoded initial condition varies with
the finite input word w.

Since the base frequency set already contains all coordinate modes,
M=d(d+1)/2, and

  D=4*d*(d+1)+4=1852       for d=21.

This counts configuration coordinates; the mechanical phase space has
3704 coordinates. It is a derived dimension count, not a dimension
tested by the reference implementation.

For each w an algorithm generates a computable initial condition
z_w=(Q(x_w,0), ZQ(x_w,0)). The coefficient program, compiler and
observable are independent of w. Along the corresponding trajectory,

  0<=O(q_w(t))<=1,

  if U(w) never halts:
      O(q_w(t))<=1/8 for all t>=0 and its limit is 0;

  if U(w) halts:
      O(q_w(t)) is eventually >=3/4 and its limit is 1.

All encoded trajectories have the same energy 1, and the potential has
one equilibrium and a globally positive Hessian arbitrarily close to I.

Suppose an algorithm took the finite word w (or the generated
computable-initialization program) and approximated the promised
observable limit within 1/4 for every w. Thresholding its answer at 1/2
would decide whether U(w) halts. The halting theorem excludes this.
The same reduction excludes an algorithm deciding, on this promised
input family, the alternatives "always <=1/8" and "eventually >=3/4".

The negative result applies to the newly defined long-time prediction
operation. It is not a lower bound for SAT and makes no statement that
polynomial-time SAT is impossible.

There is also no uniform computable convergence-time function T(w)
guaranteeing |O(q_w(t))-lim O|<1/4 for all t>=T(w). If there were,
finite-time evaluation at a sufficiently accurate computable time would
decide halting. This remains true when convergence and a binary limiting
value are promised in advance.

The individual limits 0 and 1 are computable real numbers. It is their
dependence on the input w that is not uniformly computable; those
statements should not be conflated.

5. POSITIVE CURVATURE DOES NOT REMOVE THIS OBSTRUCTION

For a compiled potential let

  Sigma={q : V(q)=1/2}.

Because V equals the quadratic near zero and has Hessian between
(1-eta)I and (1+eta)I, this is a smooth closed strictly convex
hypersurface. On Sigma,

  (1+eta)^(-1/2) <= |q| <= (1-eta)^(-1/2).

The gradient magnitude lies between (1-eta)|q| and (1+eta)|q|.
The second fundamental form is the tangential Hessian of V divided
by |grad V|. With the convention giving a convex sphere positive
principal curvatures,

  (1-eta)^(3/2)/(1+eta)
      <= kappa_i <=
  (1+eta)^(3/2)/(1-eta).

The Euclidean Gauss equation then bounds every sectional curvature K:

  (1-eta)^3/(1+eta)^2
      <= K <=
  (1+eta)^3/(1-eta)^2.

These bounds tend to 1 as eta tends to zero.

Every encoded mechanical position curve remains on Sigma, has unit
speed, and has acceleration -grad V normal to Sigma. It is therefore
a unit-speed geodesic of Sigma's induced metric. The same quadratic
position readout has the preceding undecidable limiting behavior.

This establishes an explicit family of positively curved, nearly
unit-curvature geometries supporting these encoded prediction
obstructions. It does not assert that their typical geodesics have
universal behavior or that the invariant encoding is transversely stable.

6. TWO EXACT INFINITE-TIME CONSEQUENCES

Because the observable is bounded and converges, its Cesaro mean

  (1/T) integral_0^T O(q_w(t)) dt

has the same limiting bit. Thus a general algorithm for these promised
long-time averages would also decide halting. This deduction needs
no ergodicity assumption.

For rational lambda>0 define the discounted response

  A_w(lambda)
    =lambda*integral_0^infinity exp(-lambda*t)*O(q_w(t)) dt.

For each fixed positive rational lambda this is uniformly computable.
To obtain error 2^(-b), truncate at T=(b+3)/lambda; the discarded
tail is at most exp(-(b+3))<2^(-b-3). On the finite interval use
certified trajectory evaluation and ordinary quadrature. A bound on
the derivative of the quadratic O along the bounded energy shell
supplies a computable quadrature mesh. All finite-horizon integration
and coefficient-evaluation costs are paid.

Changing variables s=lambda*t, or splitting the integral at a
convergence time, proves

  lim_(lambda down to 0) A_w(lambda)=lim_(t to infinity) O(q_w(t)).

There cannot be a uniform computable positive cutoff lambda_0(w)
that guarantees error below 1/4 for all 0<lambda<lambda_0(w):
evaluating at a rational lambda below that cutoff would decide halting.
This cleanly separates computable finite-discount response from
noncomputable uniform prediction of its zero-discount limit.

7. TIME, PRECISION, ACCESS AND PHYSICAL SCOPE

For the compiler the mechanical time per unit source-torus time is
1/epsilon. Before this step, polynomial-ODE compactification has the
additional input-dependent delay integral 1/D_c, and the latch
compactification contributes a factor between 1 and 1000. None is
removed by the representation.

The mechanical vector field is globally Lipschitz with constant
L<=1+eta. If the initial-state error is delta0 and the vector-field
error is uniformly at most zeta over the relevant finite-time region,
Gronwall's inequality gives

  state_error(T)
     <= exp(L*T)*delta0 + zeta*(exp(L*T)-1)/L.

This is a sufficient worst-case accuracy requirement, not a necessary
lower bound for every trajectory. A separated output does not supply
a time-independent allowed input error. For very long computations,
small state and force errors may be demanded by this certificate.

Finite-horizon evaluation itself is constructive. The quadratic
coercivity bounds the exact energy shell; the globally Lipschitz
vector field supplies a modulus for Picard or stepwise Euler
approximation. Its local truncation error can be made small enough
that the same Gronwall estimate certifies the requested final error.
The resulting step count may grow exponentially with the numeric
time horizon and with requested accuracy for a naive integrator;
no polynomial bound in log T is asserted.

The full source access is: explicit finite Fourier data for the
compiler; a cited constructive polynomial-simulation theorem for
the universal-system corollary; actual approximation programs for
its finite list of computable coefficients; exact computable
initial-state names generated from finite words; and standard
integer/rational arithmetic. No coefficient or initial datum
contains precomputed halting answers.

The reference computation verifies the compiler core on small finite
examples. It does not execute a universal machine, manufacture a
mechanical force field, or validate a physical realization. These
consequences are broad mathematical deductions from the construction.
They supply no demonstrated practical prediction gain and no
established civilization-scale advance. A famous undecidability
consequence alone does not establish such an advance.
