Construction, proof, numerical representation, and resource contract

STATUS AND CREDIT

This document supplies a proof and a uniform algorithm for the finite-Fourier
input class stated below. It is a research derivation, not an externally
reviewed or proof-assistant-certified publication. The main geometric route
already appears in Astra packet N1226 and its provisional natural-well V2
source. No priority is claimed for that route, the clock extension, the
frozen readout block, or potential-well universality. The contribution of this
work is an explicit rational Fourier implementation of the local isometry
step, computable smallness and tube constants, a complete evaluation
algorithm, and a cost account. The source's cited implementation file was
unavailable at its indicated repository path; no implementation from that
file has been used.

The local fixed-point method is based on Gunther's isometric embedding
argument as explained by Deane Yang, arXiv:math/9807169. The product identities
and signs used here are derived below and checked by exact Fourier arithmetic.
Tao, arXiv:1707.02389, is prior work on universality of potential-well dynamics.
Zergaenge, arXiv:1712.02766, supplies relevant smooth-family background, but
the proof here does not call a smooth-isometry existence theorem as an
algorithm. See SOURCES_AND_PROVENANCE.txt for pinned source information.

The reference code implements the free frame, its inverse, the Fourier
fixed point, finite-precision approximation, and the global smallness
certificate. Sections 7 and 8 specify the additional algorithm for evaluating
the ambient potential. That full ambient evaluator has not been implemented
or benchmarked in the accompanying code. No universal-machine trajectory,
physical device, noisy initialization, or practical acceleration was tested.

1. EXACT INPUT AND OUTPUT CONTRACT

Write T^d = (R / 2*pi*Z)^d. An input consists of:

  * d >= 1, with all d components of the vector field explicitly listed;
  * a real finite Fourier vector field Y on T^d, with binary integer
    frequencies and rational sine/cosine coefficients;
  * a finite list of real finite Fourier scalar observables f_j in the same
    representation (the list may be empty);
  * a rational tolerance 0 < eta < 1.

Equivalently, coefficients may be Gaussian rationals with exact conjugate
symmetry. The complete encoding is counted. Frequencies are not treated as
unit-cost integers.

The output is a finite numerical program, together with its exact defining
formulas and certified constants. It specifies a positive rational dyadic
epsilon, an integer D, a C-infinity potential V on R^D, an embedding Q of
T^(d+1) into R^D, and homogeneous quadratic position observables O_j.
For Z = epsilon*Y + partial_theta, the output satisfies:

  (1) ||D^2 V(q) - I||_op <= eta/2 < eta, globally;
  (2) V(q) = |q|^2/2 outside the annulus 1/2 < |q| < 3/2;
  (3) iota(x,theta) = (Q(x,theta), ZQ(x,theta)) is an embedding into phase
      space invariant under q' = p, p' = -grad V(q);
  (4) on this invariant set the dynamics is exactly Z, |p|=1,
      V(q)=1/2, and total energy is 1;
  (5) O_j(Q(x,theta)) = f_j(x), exactly.

The program evaluates V and any specified finite derivative jet at a rational
point to absolute error at most 2^(-b), for an input precision b. It likewise
evaluates Q and its derivatives. No trajectory, minimization, isometry, or
halting oracle is supplied.

This is not a polynomial-time compiler in the complete input length. For
each fixed rational input and fixed derivative order, evaluation has a
polynomial bit bound in b and the query-point encoding length. The parameter
constants include a large grid and inverse conditioning margins. Section 8
exhibits their costs. Time in the source system runs epsilon times as fast as
mechanical time; the factor 1/epsilon is part of the contract.

A finite list of computable real coefficients is also admissible when each
coefficient is supplied by an actual certified approximation program. Its
execution cost is additional input-access cost; no polynomial bound is
inferred for those programs. Section 9 makes this extension precise.

2. AN EXPLICIT SPHERICAL FREE EMBEDDING

Let Aset contain e_i (1 <= i <= d), e_i+e_j (i<j), and every nonzero
frequency needed by a declared observable, identifying a frequency with its
negative. Put M = |Aset|, L = 1+2M, m=d+1, and

  kappa = 1/(M+1),
  a = (1-M*kappa^2)/(1+M*kappa^2),
  c = 2*kappa/(1+M*kappa^2).

Then a>0 and a^2+M*c^2=1, with rational a,c. Define

  Psi(x) = (a, (c*cos(alpha.x), c*sin(alpha.x))_(alpha in Aset)),
  Q0(x,theta) = (Psi(x)*cos(theta), Psi(x)*sin(theta)).

Thus |Q0|=1 and partial_theta^2 Q0 = -Q0. The anchor coordinate a recovers
the circle clock, and the e_i coordinates recover all x_i, so Q0 is an
embedding. Its induced metric is

  g0 = gM + dtheta^2,
  gM = c^2 * sum_(alpha in Aset) alpha alpha^T.

The matrix gM is positive definite and rational.

Freeness means linear independence of all m first and m(m+1)/2 distinct
second coordinate derivatives. It follows explicitly at x=theta=0:
the anchor makes Psi independent of its derivatives; the sine e_i
coordinates separate the first derivatives; cosine e_i separates the
diagonal second derivatives; cosine e_i+e_j separates the mixed second
derivatives. The Q0 derivative family splits into real-block vectors
(Psi_i, Psi_ij, -Psi) and imaginary-block vectors (Psi, Psi_i), which are
independent. Translation of x and theta acts by orthogonal matrices, proving
freeness at every point.

Let F be the matrix whose rows are these first and second derivatives of
Q0. Its Gram matrix F F^T is constant: every translation acts orthogonally
on the entire derivative family. Its entries are rational and can be
computed at zero. Exact rational elimination gives

  E = F^T (F F^T)^(-1).

E is a finite rational Fourier matrix. It solves

  Q0_i . E(a_i,b_ij) = a_i,
  Q0_ij . E(a_i,b_ij) = b_ij.

This is an explicit frame inverse, not a pointwise pseudoinverse oracle.

3. A METRIC FOR WHICH THE DESIRED FLOW IS GEODESIC

Fix s=3/5 and t=4/5. Define

  g_epsilon((v,b),(w,e))
     = b*e + gM(v-epsilon*b*Y, w-epsilon*e*Y).

It is positive definite for every epsilon and

  g_epsilon(Z, .) = dtheta,       g_epsilon(Z,Z)=1.

The metric dual of Z is closed and its length is constant. The Koszul
identity therefore gives nabla_Z Z = 0. In particular this is an exact
geodesic realization of the clock extension, without averaging.

Let h=(g_epsilon-g0)/t^2 = epsilon*h1 + epsilon^2*h2. The only nonzero
entries are

  h_(i,theta) = -epsilon*(gM*Y)_i/t^2,
  h_(theta,theta) = epsilon^2*(Y^T*gM*Y)/t^2.

We construct R=Q0+v inducing g0+h. Then

  Q=(s*Q0, t*R)

induces s^2*g0+t^2*(g0+h)=g_epsilon. Its dimension is

  D=4L=4+8M.

The unchanged first block guarantees injectivity and preserves exact
quadratic readouts even after the second block is corrected.

4. THE PERIODIC FOURIER FIXED POINT

Let A0=Delta-1 and G=A0^(-1) on T^m. On Fourier mode k,
G multiplies by -1/(1+|k|_2^2). For a vector-valued v define

  q_i(v) = G[(A0 v) . partial_i v],

  q_ij(v) = G[ 2 sum_k partial_ik v . partial_jk v
                  + partial_i v . partial_j v
                  - 2(A0 v) . partial_ij v ].

Product differentiation gives the exact identity

  partial_i v . partial_j v
       = partial_i q_j(v) + partial_j q_i(v) + q_ij(v).

For verification, apply Delta-1 to both sides. Applying it to the left
produces Delta(v_i).v_j + v_i.Delta(v_j)
+2 sum_k v_ik.v_jk - v_i.v_j. Applying it to the two derivatives on the
right produces Delta(v_i).v_j + v_i.Delta(v_j)
-2 v_i.v_j +2(A0v).v_ij. The displayed q_ij supplies exactly the difference.
The operator Delta-1 has no kernel, proving the identity.

Use the fixed-point equation

  v = E(0,-h/2) + E(-q_i(v), q_ij(v)/2)
    = v_lin + B(v,v).                                      (FP)

B denotes symmetric polarization of the displayed quadratic expression.
Differentiate Q0_i.v=-q_i and use
Q0_ij.v=-h_ij/2+q_ij/2. The linearized induced metric is

  Q0_i.v_j+Q0_j.v_i = h_ij - v_i.v_j.

Consequently (FP) implies
(Q0_i+v_i).(Q0_j+v_j)=g0_ij+h_ij, the exact metric equation.

For a Fourier scalar use the norm

  ||f||_(w,2) = sum_k (|Re f_k|+|Im f_k|)
                         w^(|k|_1) (1+|k|_1)^2,

and sum over components for vectors and the listed symmetric tensor
entries. We first use w=2. The corresponding order-zero norm is a Banach
algebra; the order-two norm is also an algebra because its weight is
submultiplicative. Moreover

  ||G f||_(w,2) <= (m+1)||f||_(w,0),

since (1+|k|_1)^2 <= (m+1)(1+|k|_2^2). Each derivative of order at most two,
and A0, maps the order-two norm to the order-zero norm with bound one.

Let E0>=1 be the largest column-sum order-two, weight-two norm of E.
Let ell=m(m+1)/2 and set

  B0 = max(1, E0*(m+1)*(m+ell*(2*m+3)/2)),
  H0 = ||h1||_(2,2)+||h2||_(2,2),
  A1 = max(1,E0*H0/2).

These are finite explicitly computable rational numbers. The product
estimates above give

  ||B(v,w)|| <= B0 ||v|| ||w||,
  ||v_lin|| <= A1*epsilon        (epsilon<=1).

Choose epsilon <= 1/(16*A1*B0). With

  Rstar=2*A1*epsilon,
  qstar=2*B0*Rstar=4*A1*B0*epsilon <= 1/4,

the right side of (FP) sends the closed Rstar ball into its 5 Rstar/8 ball
and is qstar-Lipschitz there. The Banach fixed-point theorem gives a unique
solution in that ball. All constants and the convergence modulus are
explicit. Its exponential Fourier weight proves real analyticity of Q
on the torus and bounds every derivative. No differentiability bootstrap
or nonconstructive choice of an embedding is needed.

For each r>=0,

  ||v||_(C^r) <= r!*2^r ||v||_(2,2).

Here C^r is the maximum of the sup norms of the Frechet derivatives through
order r; component-sum Fourier bounds dominate these Euclidean operator
norms. The inequality follows from
u^r <= r! exp(u/2) 2^r and log(2)>1/2.

5. FINITE PRECISION, NOT EVER-GROWING EXACT PICARD ARITHMETIC

Exact rational Picard iterations prove computability but can accumulate
large denominators. The reference evaluator truncates and rounds after each
step, with an explicit error budget.

Use the weaker norm w=3/2. The same contraction constant qstar is valid
between elements of the strong Rstar ball. Truncating a strong-norm-bounded
Fourier series to |k|_1<=K loses at most

  ||f||_(2,2)*(3/4)^(K+1)

in the weak norm. Rounding real and imaginary coefficients toward zero to
dyadic precision p does not increase the strong norm and preserves conjugate
symmetry. Its weak error is at most

  2*N*(2K+1)^m*(1+K)^2*2^K*2^(-p),

where N=2L is the number of components in v.

To approximate v in C^r within 2^(-b), put

  c_r = r!*3^r,
  bstar = b+ceil(log2(c_r))+8,
  K = 4*(bstar+4),
  p = bstar+K+ceil(log2(32*N*(2K+1)^m*(K+1)^2)).

The final epsilon choice also ensures Rstar<=1/16. Since (3/4)^4<1/2,
the truncation plus rounding error per step is less than 2^(-bstar)/8.
The iterates remain in the strong ball. If e_j is their error in the weak
norm, then

  e_(j+1) <= qstar*e_j + 2^(-bstar)/8.

Within bstar+9 iterations the fixed error budget and the decayed initial
error are both small enough for the requested accuracy. The implementation
uses the stronger a posteriori check

  ||v-w||_(3/2,2)
       <= ||w-v_lin-B(w,w)||_(3/2,2)/(1-qstar).

The residual is a finite rational Fourier expression, and is computed
exactly. Multiplying this certificate by c_r bounds the C^r error because
log(3/2)>1/3. The stated iteration limit therefore terminates.

A conservative cost bound can be obtained without sparse-arithmetic
assumptions. Let F0 bound the numeric l1 frequency of the finite data E
and v_lin, let beta0 bound their rational coefficient bit lengths, and put

  S = (4K+2F0+1)^m,
  P = p+beta0+m*ceil(log2(2K+F0+2))+1,
  J = bstar+10.

Every finite expression in one iteration has at most O((N+m+ell+1)^3 S)
scalar Fourier entries. Direct convolutions and exact fraction summation,
followed by dyadic rounding, give the deliberately loose bit upper bound

  T_Fourier =
    O( J*(N+m+ell+1)^10*(S+1)^10*(P+1)^4 ).             (COST-F)

For example, summing at most polynomially many products of input rationals
can be done by taking a product of their denominators; its bit length is
at most the sum of their bit lengths. This supplies a simple, inefficient
bound covered by (COST-F), and prevents a unit-cost rational-arithmetic
assumption. Each iteration starts from p-bit dyadics, so this bound does
not recur by squaring the previous iteration's denominators.

Point evaluation adds elementary sine/cosine or unit-circle-power
evaluation at the required argument precision and coefficient summation.
Their standard range-reduced series algorithms have polynomial bit cost.
Argument bit length, frequency bit length, number of evaluated modes,
derivative order and output size are included. Fixed input and r therefore
give a polynomial in requested accuracy b. The exponent and constant
depend on dimension and the complete finite input.

Computing the preprocessing data uses explicit Fourier multiplication,
exact rational Gram elimination, rational norm summation, and the formulas
below. In particular, producing 2^|k| in a weighted norm costs its actual
bit length. Since k was binary encoded, this alone can be exponential in
the input length. It is not omitted from preprocessing.

6. GLOBAL TUBE AND POTENTIAL, WITH AN EXPLICIT HESSIAN BOUND

Write Qbar0=(s*Q0,t*Q0) and deltaQ=(0,t*v). For j>=0 let T_j be the
coefficient-sum Fourier C^j bound of Qbar0. It is finite and computable.
Define

  D_j=2*A1*j!*2^j,       M_j=T_j+D_j.

Then ||deltaQ||_(C^j)<=D_j*epsilon, and ||Q||_(C^j)<=M_j for epsilon<=1.
The code uses only j<=4 for the Hessian certificate.

A global co-Lipschitz constant, with shortest angular distance on T^m, is

  mu = s / (2*((1+1/a)/c+1/a)).

Indeed, the two anchor coordinates recover the clock chord with inverse
bound 1/a. After removing that clock phase, the Psi chord is at most
(1+1/a) times the Q0 chord. The e_i pairs recover all x chords with factor
1/c. Angular distance is at most pi/2 times chord distance, and pi/2<2.
The frozen s*Q0 block gives

  |Q(z)-Q(z')| >= mu*dist_T(z,z').

In particular the least singular value of DQ is at least mu.

Set

  rho=min(1/8, mu^2/(8*(M_2+1))),    lambda=mu^2/2.

If n is normal to Q at z and |n|<2*rho, Taylor's theorem along a shortest
angular segment v gives, for any z',

  |Q(z)+n-Q(z')|^2-|n|^2
        >= (mu^2-|n|*M_2)*|v|^2 >= lambda*|v|^2.

Thus Q(z) is the unique global closest point to Q(z)+n. Conversely,
compactness supplies a closest point for any y within 2*rho of Q(T^m),
and the first-order condition makes the displacement normal. This proves
uniqueness and a genuine global tube, not just local immersion control.

Let x(y) be its footpoint coordinates, pi(y)=Q(x(y)), and n(y)=y-pi(y).
The footpoint equation is

  G(x,y)=DQ(x)^T (Q(x)-y)=0.

Its x derivative is at least lambda*I on the tube. Implicit differentiation
gives the following explicit bounds:

  X1=M_1/lambda,
  X2=((3*M_1*M_2+2*rho*M_3)*X1^2+2*M_2*X1)/lambda,
  P1=M_1*X1,
  P2=M_2*X1^2+M_1*X2,
  N1=1+P1.

Here X1,X2 bound the first and second derivatives of x, P1,P2 those of pi,
and N1,P2 those of n.

Because Q is isometric and Z is geodesic, A=Z^2 Q is normal to Q(T^m).
The value and gradient jets needed for the perturbing potential are

  a_epsilon(x)=1/2-|Q(x)|^2/2,
  b_epsilon(x)=-Z^2 Q(x)-Q(x).

They obey da_epsilon=b_epsilon.dQ. Let Y3 be a Fourier C^3 bound of Y.
Using the C^2 product bound ||fg||_(C^2)<=4||f||_(C^2)||g||_(C^2), set

  Ca=4*T_2*D_2+2*D_2^2,
  Cb=D_4+D_2+(8*Y3+32*Y3^2)*M_4.

Then ||a_epsilon||_(C^2)<=Ca*epsilon and
||b_epsilon||_(C^2)<=Cb*epsilon. For b, expand

  Z^2 Q+Q
    =(partial_theta^2+1)deltaQ
      +2*epsilon*Y(partial_theta Q)+epsilon^2*Y(YQ).

The two terms in Y(YQ) are (Y_i partial_i Y_j)partial_j Q and
Y_i Y_j partial_ij Q. Their C^2 product estimates explain the factors
8 and 32. No small-time approximation occurs.

Define theta0(u)=exp(-1/u) for u>0 and 0 for u<=0, and

  chi(t)=theta0(1-t)/(theta0(1-t)+theta0(t-1/4)).

Then chi=1 for t<=1/4, chi=0 for t>=1, and chi is C-infinity.
On the transition interval the denominator is at least exp(-8/3)>1/16.
The estimates |theta0'|<=1 and |theta0''|<=8 imply the safe bounds

  |chi'|<=1024,       |chi''|<=65536.

These bounds follow directly by the quotient rule (the more immediate
bounds 528 and 38016 also suffice). Define

  H0j=Ca+Cb*rho,
  H1j=Ca*X1+Cb*X1*rho+Cb*N1,
  H2j=(Ca+Cb*rho)*(X1^2+X2)+2*Cb*X1*N1+Cb*P2,
  S1=2*N1/rho,
  S2=2*(N1^2+rho*P2)/rho^2,
  C1=1024*S1,
  C2=65536*S1^2+1024*S2,
  CV=H2j+2*C1*H1j+C2*H0j.

For dist(y,Q(T^m))<2*rho define

  W(y)=chi(|n(y)|^2/rho^2) *
            [a_epsilon(x(y))+b_epsilon(x(y)).n(y)],

and define W=0 outside that tube. Flatness of chi at its support boundary
makes this a global C-infinity function. The displayed derivative bounds
and the product rule give

  ||D^2 W||_op <= CV*epsilon.

Choose a positive dyadic epsilon satisfying

  epsilon <= min(1, 1/(16*A1*B0), 1/(32*A1), eta/(2*CV)).    (EPS)

One can start at epsilon=1 and repeatedly halve until this rational test
passes. There is no existential choice in (EPS). All constants were
computed independently of epsilon using epsilon<=1.

Finally put V(y)=|y|^2/2+W(y). Its Hessian differs from I by at most eta/2.
Also ||deltaQ||_infinity<=Rstar<=1/16 and rho<=1/8, so the perturbation is
supported in 13/16<=|y|<=19/16, a subset of the claimed annulus.
On the embedded submanifold, W=a_epsilon and grad W=b_epsilon by jet
compatibility. Therefore V(Q)=1/2 and -grad V(Q)=Z^2 Q, as required.

7. EVALUATING THE POTENTIAL WITHOUT A PROJECTION ORACLE

This section is an algorithm, not an assumption of access to x(y).
Queries consist of a rational y, a nonnegative integer r, and b. The
integer/rational operations below include their operand precision.

If |y|^2>=4, return the exact quadratic potential and its jet: W=0 there.
Otherwise |y|<2.

First construct a finite angular grid. Define

  LH=3*M_1*M_2+(2*rho+M_1)*M_3,
  rN=min(1/100, mu^2/(16*(1+LH))),
  U=1+M_1^2+(2*rho+M_1)*M_2+lambda,
  h=(1/m)*min(1, rho/(128*(M_1+1)), rN*lambda/(16*U)),
  ngrid=ceil(7/h),                  Ggrid=ngrid^m,
  delta_f=lambda*rN^2/1024.

Use the grid angles 2*pi*j/ngrid in each coordinate. Pi and the elementary
trigonometric functions are evaluated with certified series algorithms.
Compute all grid distances and values
f(x)=|Q(x)-y|^2/2 to errors less than rho/128 and delta_f, respectively.
For example, Q-value error less than
min(rho/1024,delta_f/16,1/1024), followed by the elementary arithmetic
to adequate precision, suffices since |Q|<2 and |y|<2.

Let d_est be the least approximate grid distance. The mesh and error bounds
give |d_est-dist(y,Q(T^m))|<rho/32. If d_est>3*rho/2, the true distance
exceeds rho, so return W and all its derivatives as zero. If this branch
is not taken, the true distance is less than 49*rho/32<2*rho, where the
projection exists uniquely. Both possible classifications in the overlap
are valid because W is already identically zero there.

Choose a grid point with the least approximate f value and lift its angles
locally to R^m. Let x* be the true footpoint. The global tube inequality
gives

  f(x)-f(x*) >= lambda*dist_T(x,x*)^2/2.

At a grid point within angular distance m*h from x*, Taylor's theorem
and the Hessian upper bound U give
f(x)-f(x*) <= U*(m*h)^2/2. Approximate minimization adds at most
2*delta_f. With the displayed choices the selected seed is within rN/8
of x*. Its numerical angle representation can be made accurate enough to
remain within rN/4.

On the Euclidean rN ball around x*, the x Hessian of f has spectrum in
[lambda,U]. At x*, its lower bound is at least 3*mu^2/4. Moving by rN
changes it by at most LH*rN<=mu^2/16. Thus these lower and upper bounds
hold throughout the ball. The iteration

  x <- x - G(x,y)/U

contracts distance to x* by at most 1-lambda/U. It stays in that ball.
For target coordinate error 2^(-B), choose B additionally large enough
that 2^(-B)<=rN/16. Compute each step, including its rounding, with
vector error at most

  (lambda/U)*2^(-B-3).

Then ceil((U/lambda)*(B+3)) steps suffice, and rounding keeps the iterates
in the certified ball. Evaluation of G uses Q and DQ from Section 5.
This supplies a finite uniform precision algorithm for x(y).

Higher derivatives do not require finite-difference limits. At the exact
root let H=G_x. Differentiate G(x(y),y)=0. The first derivative is
x_y=-H^(-1)G_y. For a multi-index beta of order k>=2, formal
differentiation produces

  H*(partial^beta x) + R_beta = 0,

where R_beta involves only lower derivatives of x and derivatives of G.
Enumerate these terms by ordinary polynomial jet arithmetic and solve
partial^beta x=-H^(-1)R_beta. The inverse has norm at most 1/lambda.
Substitute these jets into the explicit expression for W.

Here is an entirely arithmetic way to choose the needed internal precision:
build these finite jet expressions through order r and their first x
derivatives. Bound every sum by sums of absolute bounds, every product by
products, and each matrix inverse by 1/lambda. For derivatives of an inverse
use D(H^(-1))=-H^(-1)(DH)H^(-1). Use the explicit Fourier derivative bounds
T_j+D_j through order r+3 and finite Fourier bounds of Y through order r+2.
This yields a positive rational bound A_r for the norms and x sensitivity
of every required expression. Multiplying it by the finite number of
arithmetic operations also bounds accumulated evaluation error. For
finite-precision matrix data use the safer inverse bound 2/lambda and
require the computed matrix error to be below lambda/2; this margin is
included in the precision overhead.

All cutoff derivative bounds needed by this procedure are computable
without taking suprema as an oracle. If
theta0^(k)(u)=exp(-1/u)*p_k(1/u) for u>0, then

  p_0(v)=1,        p_(k+1)(v)=v^2*(p_k(v)-p_k'(v)).

The bound sum_j |[v^j]p_k|*j! is valid, since v^j exp(-v)<=j!.
On the transition interval the reciprocal denominator is at most 16;
differentiating its product with the denominator recursively gives
rational bounds for all derivatives of chi.

For arguments near u=0, evaluation uses flatness bounds rather than deciding
an exact real sign. In particular
v^j exp(-v)<=2^j*j!*exp(-v/2). If an argument interval is sufficiently
close to zero, this inequality certifies that the desired derivatives are
smaller than the requested error. Otherwise the sign is separated and the
usual exponential-series evaluator applies. This covers the cutoff's
support boundaries as well.

Choose B=b+ceil(log2(16*(1+A_r))) with the geometric lower bound on B
already described; choose elementary arithmetic precision to make its
certified total error less than 2^(-b-2). At an approximate footpoint the
same formal jet expressions are well-defined, since the inverse margin
persists in the rN ball. Their x-sensitivity bound and the error in x
then prove the final absolute derivative error <=2^(-b).

This specifies every search, stopping condition and precision request.
Interval arithmetic can implement the same bounds. No exact comparison
of a noncomputable real or unknown convergence rate is used.

8. GLOBAL EVALUATION RESOURCE ACCOUNT

Let C_r be the integer precision overhead obtained by the explicit
expression-bound procedure in Section 7, enlarged to cover rN, lambda/U,
the finite query arithmetic and the number of jet entries. Let P0 be the
fixed grid precision specified there, and let Hquery be the complete
binary length of the rational query. Let T_Q(p,j,H) denote the bound
(COST-F) plus point evaluation through derivative order j and input length H.

A conservative cost bound for one ambient query is

  T_pre
  + O(Ggrid * [T_Q(P0,2,Hquery)+D*(P0+Hquery+1)^4])
  + O((1+(U/lambda)*(b+C_r+3)) *
       [T_Q(b+C_r,r+3,Hquery)
        +(D+m+r+1)^(6r+20)*(b+C_r+Hquery+1)^4]).

T_pre is the cost of the specified exact finite preprocessing algorithms
and expression generation. The formula counts the finite angular grid,
inverse margins, rational precision, all Fourier modes and derivative
output entries. It remains valid if the grid values are recomputed for
each query; caching is optional and its storage is Ggrid times the
stored vector precision. Dense storage for the Fourier evaluator is

The purpose of the deliberately large exponents is a transparent finite

10. READOUT, DYNAMICS, AND COMPLETENESS

Name the frozen coordinates associated to the anchor u0,v0 and the
alpha cosine and sine modes u_(alpha,c),v_(alpha,c) and
u_(alpha,s),v_(alpha,s). Then

  O_(cos alpha)(q)
     =(u0*u_(alpha,c)+v0*v_(alpha,c))/(s^2*a*c),
  O_(sin alpha)(q)
     =(u0*u_(alpha,s)+v0*v_(alpha,s))/(s^2*a*c).

They equal cos(alpha.x) and sin(alpha.x), respectively, on Q.
The constant function is (u0^2+v0^2)/(s^2*a^2). Linear combinations
give every declared observable as a homogeneous quadratic in positions.

Since V(Q)=1/2 and -grad V(Q)=Z^2 Q, the curve
(Q(z(t)),ZQ(z(t))) solves the mechanical equations whenever z'=Z.
Its speed is one by the metric identity, proving energy one. The first
coordinate Q already embeds the torus, so the phase-space map is an
embedding as well.

The global Hessian bound implies strong convexity, a globally Lipschitz
gradient, and the unique equilibrium at q=0 (the perturbation vanishes
near zero). The mechanical vector field (p,-grad V(q)) is globally
Lipschitz with constant at most 1+eta, hence has complete unique
solutions. The theorem concerns an explicitly encoded invariant subset.
It makes no full-flow conjugacy, positive-volume universality, transverse
stability, physical construction, polynomial-time SAT, or finite-noise
robustness claim.
