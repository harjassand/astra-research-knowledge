WHAT CAN ANY FINITE EXPERIMENT PROGRAMME DETERMINE?
Constructive experimental accuracy and a computability boundary
7 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane

RESOLVED QUESTION AND STATUS

The previous construction computes sharp scientific consequences of a
given history. The question resolved here is the next operational one:

  With a specified model family, task and fixed bounded-error observation
  channel, what is the best worst-case accuracy obtainable by any finite
  experiment programme? Can that accuracy be computed, experiments that
  approach it be constructed, and irreducible ambiguity be witnessed?

The answer has three parts, with distinct input contracts.

A. For the retained bounded-curvature potential family with a finitely
   presented convex calibration prior, positive native measurement error,
   and finite-horizon polynomial tasks, the infimum experimental error is
   exactly half an observational modulus. A terminating ordinary bit
   algorithm computes it to any requested tolerance, constructs a finite
   experiment programme achieving that tolerance, and constructs two
   actual models that no permitted programme can distinguish. The
   algorithm uses the accepted, fully charged THG/Euler/CAD construction.

B. If model coefficients are instead supplied by certified approximation
   programmes, no uniform algorithm computes that experimental accuracy,
   even to a fixed constant tolerance, for a family consisting of only
   two strongly convex quadratics with polynomial-time forward evaluators.
   Every coefficient in the reduction is rational. Its representation is
   a programme, not an explicit numerator/denominator. A direct halting
   reduction proves the distinction. A separate precision argument gives
   no computable uniform work bound over all learnable instances.

C. For a supplied finite experiment catalogue and budget, exact minimax
   experimental design is implemented and executed for an entire family
   of stationary monotone constitutive laws. Its certificate covers every
   allowed action and every feasible report. A two-query example has risk
   1/12 adaptively versus 5/48 for the best fixed schedule, with extremal
   compatible laws. The unlimited-finite-experiment floor for its prior
   and native noise is exactly 1/32.

These are complete results at the stated interfaces. The modulus/radius
of information viewpoint, convex mixing, minimax recursion, halting
undecidability, interpolation and exact real algebraic algorithms are
credited established machinery. No historical novelty, independent peer
review, formal verification of this composition, practical superiority,
physical result or civilisation-scale achievement is claimed. General
CAD was specified and costed, not implemented or executed. The finite
catalogue computation and bounded evaluator checks were executed.

The predecessor inference archive, SAT results and near-harmonic compiler
remain unchanged. Their mathematical claims and their limits are retained.
Source contracts and the minimal preservation record are in SOURCES.txt.


PART A. A COMPUTABLE OPTIMAL EXPERIMENTAL ACCURACY

A1. Full scientific and computational input

Use the retained model contract:

  z'(t) = A z(t) + B grad V(C z(t)) + u(t),  0 <= t <= T.

A, B, C, T, the finite piecewise-constant forcing u, its switch times,
observation times and scalar polynomial task H are explicitly rational.
The task S(V,z0) is H of the specified z(t), V(Cz(t)), grad V(Cz(t)).
Initial states z0 range over a nonempty compact rational semialgebraic
set Z with a supplied rational containing box. Z is independent of V.
Z need not be convex. The task can involve several observation times.

Let rational mu <= L and G >= 0 be supplied. The unknown stationary V
is defined on all of R^d, V(0)=0, |grad V(0)|_2 <= G. If mu < L,

  h(x) = V(x) - mu |x|^2/2

is convex and has (L-mu)-Lipschitz gradient. If mu=L, use the retained
quadratic-affine special case. Negative mu is allowed. Convexity of the
FAMILY below does not require each V to be convex.

At finitely many supplied rational calibration locations, the values and
gradients belong to a closed CONVEX rational semialgebraic set D. Closed
affine equalities/inequalities and interval data are the principal case.
If a general rational formula for D is supplied, its convexity can itself
be checked by real quantifier elimination:

  for all v,w in D and all t in [0,1], (1-t)v+t w belongs to D.

That cost is charged. The construction is promised only for this convex
calibration contract. The previous posterior inference algorithm retains
its larger, possibly nonconvex semialgebraic calibration scope.

Write F for the resulting potential family. It is convex: curvature,
anchor and calibration conditions are all preserved by convex mixing.
Empty F or Z is detected exactly by finite interpolation and real algebraic
feasibility; return 'inconsistent prior' or 'no admissible initial state'.
All statements below concern the nonempty case.

Supply a nonempty rational box Q in R^d, allowing zero-length sides, and
a rational native error delta > 0. The only new observation operation is:

  query a rational x in Q and a nonnegative integer p;
  receive y in (2^(-p) Z)^d with
  |y-grad V(x)|_infinity <= delta + 2^(-p)/2.             (A1)

This is a closed bounded-error channel. It can be realized by native
coordinate errors of size <=delta followed by nearest dyadic rounding,
with both tie choices allowed. The closed inequality (A1) is the exact
contract used here; no favorable rule at rounding ties is assumed.
Reports use a fixed-point format with p fractional bits, so p precision
bits per coordinate are charged even for a short numerical value.

Errors can be adversarial, dependent and chosen differently on different
queries. Repetition therefore does not reduce delta. Refining p removes
only quantization error. Neither arbitrary new nonlinear measurements nor
unlimited gain nor direct task/equilibrium measurements are supplied.

Supply rational epsilon > 0. Count the complete finite binary input,
calibration and regularity information, queries, coordinates, p, complete
reports, all preparation and processing, arithmetic, CAD, witness sizes
and evaluations. This construction specifies an instrument interface; it
does not acquire its physical validity or perform physical measurements.

A2. Accuracy to be computed

A programme is an ordinary deterministic classical algorithm using (A1),
with rational queries and a finite binary description. It must terminate
on every admissible model, initial state and allowed response history,
and return a scalar estimate. Its worst-case absolute error includes all
those choices. Let R_delta^* be the infimum of this error over finite
programmes, with all preprocessing included. The infimum need not be
attained by one finite programme.

Define

  d_Q(V,W) = sup_{x in Q} |grad V(x)-grad W(x)|_infinity,

  Omega(r) = sup { |S(V,z)-S(W,w)| : V,W in F, z,w in Z,
                                      d_Q(V,W) <= r }.  (A2)

THEOREM A.

  R_delta^* = Omega(2 delta)/2.                          (A3)

There is a uniform deterministic terminating bit algorithm returning
rational ell,U with

  ell <= R_delta^* <= U,        U-ell < epsilon,

a finite query programme whose error is <=U, and two explicitly evaluable
compatible potential/initial-state pairs with task difference >=2 ell
and d_Q <=2 delta. They can produce identical reports under EVERY
permitted adaptive query sequence. The programme achieving U uses a
fixed finite grid; the lower bound applies to all adaptive programmes.
This does not say adaptivity never reduces a finite query budget.

A3. Indistinguishable-model lower bound

Fix any pair in (A2) with r=2 delta. At every query x, give the programme
a coordinatewise rounding of

  (grad V(x)+grad W(x))/2

to the requested dyadic grid. Before rounding it is within delta of both
gradients; afterward it satisfies (A1) for both. Since the algorithm is
deterministic, identical reports induce identical next queries and the
same stopping/output decision. One output must have error at least half
the two task values' separation. Taking the supremum proves

  R_delta^* >= Omega(2 delta)/2.                         (A4)

This is the classical two-source/radius-of-information argument. It
already allows a variable number of adaptive observations and arbitrary
finite auxiliary computation. For the witnesses actually constructed
below, gradients at rational query locations are real algebraic numbers.
Common reports can therefore be generated by paid exact algebraic
evaluation and comparison; no noncomputable witness oracle is needed.

A4. An explicit task bound under mixing

Use the accepted uniform constants from the predecessor. Let

  K=max(|mu|,|L|),
  a >= ||A||_2, b >= ||B||_2, c >= ||C||_2,
  R0 >= sup_{z in Z}|z|_2, U0 >= sup_t |u(t)|_2,
  Lambda=a+b K c, D0=b G+U0,
  E=2^ceil(2 Lambda T), R=(R0+D0 T) E,
  X=c R, M=Lambda R+D0.

All chosen bounds are rational and computed from the actual input.
Every trajectory stays within R, and

  |grad V(x)| <= G+K|x|,
  |V(x)| <= G|x|+K|x|^2/2.

Let L_H be the retained coefficient-sum Lipschitz constant of H in the
sup norm on a known box containing every task argument. Explicitly, take
W>=1 bounding their absolute values, using R and the gradient/value bounds,
and for H(y)=sum_alpha c_alpha y^alpha use

  L_H=sum_alpha |c_alpha| |alpha| W^max(|alpha|-1,0).

Define

  Dg = 2(G+K X),                 Dv = 2 G X+K X^2,
  Zmix = b Dg T E,
  Cmix = L_H max{ Zmix,
                  K c Zmix+Dg,
                  (G+K X)c Zmix+Dv },
  Cstar = max(1,Cmix).                                    (A5)

For V_t=(1-t)V+tW and the SAME initial state z,

  |S(V_t,z)-S(V,z)| <= Cmix t <= Cstar t, 0<=t<=1.       (A6)

Proof. The mixed model remains in F. Comparing its trajectory with V's
trajectory gives a forcing discrepancy <=t b Dg and common spatial
Lipschitz constant Lambda. Gronwall gives a state discrepancy <=t Zmix.
At every task time, the gradient discrepancy is <=t(K c Zmix+Dg).
For values the discrepancy is <=t((G+K X)c Zmix+Dv). Apply L_H to the
largest scalar-argument discrepancy. This proves (A6), including T=0,
zero coupling and constant-task cases. Cstar avoids division by zero.

Known additional fixed task evaluation points can be included by replacing
X with a bound covering them and recomputing the same constants. No
unknown trajectory or stability constant is assumed.

A5. Convex repair of almost-indistinguishable models

Suppose d_Q(V,W) <= r+s for r>0 and s>=0. Set

  lambda=r/(r+s),        Vbar=(V+W)/2,
  V'=Vbar+lambda(V-W)/2,
  W'=Vbar-lambda(V-W)/2.                                (A7)

These are convex mixtures of the original two models, hence remain in F.
Their gradient difference is lambda times the original difference, so
d_Q(V',W')<=r. Keep the initial states z and w unchanged. Each model is
mixed by (1-lambda)/2. By (A6), their ordered task difference decreases
by at most

  Cstar(1-lambda) = Cstar s/(r+s).

Apply the same argument with reversed order if needed. Therefore

  Omega(r+s) <= Omega(r)+Cstar s/(r+s), r>0.             (A8)

This is an explicit, effective right-continuity bound at positive native
noise. It uses convexity of the model family, linearity of the gradient
observation in the model, and a uniform task bound under mixing. It does
not require linear dynamics, a linear task, convex individual potentials,
or convex Z. Joint prior constraints coupling V with z0 would need a
separate repair theorem; independence is substantive here.

A6. The finite construction

Put eta=epsilon/4 and rho=epsilon/4. Choose a dyadic q=2^(-p), p>=0, with

  q <= delta epsilon/(2 Cstar).

For example p=max(0,ceil(log2(2 Cstar/(delta epsilon)))); implement the
inequality with exact rational comparisons, not an approximate logarithm.

If K>0, let h=delta epsilon/(4 K Cstar), and form a rational Cartesian
grid Q_h covering Q to Euclidean distance <=h. Along a side of length
l_i>0 use ceil(d l_i/h) equal subdivisions and both endpoints; a
zero-length side has one point. A coordinate spacing <=h/d suffices.
If K=0 all gradients are constant; use one rational point of Q and put h=0.
Let M_q be the number of grid points. Then, for K>0,

  M_q <= product_i (ceil(d l_i/h)+1).                   (A9)

Define

  s=q+2 K h,          Drepair=Cstar s/(2 delta+s).

Then s<=delta epsilon/Cstar and Drepair<=epsilon/2.

Construct a finite paired inference problem. It contains two independent
potential tables with the same prior F, independent z,w in Z, and the
additional constraints, coordinatewise at every grid point x_j,

  |grad V(x_j)-grad W(x_j)| <= 2 delta+q.              (A10)

Maximize the ordered true task difference S(V,z)-S(W,w). Both orientations
are allowed, so this also bounds absolute differences. Compute a rational
upper bound B and explicit feasible witnesses such that

  S(V,z)-S(W,w) >= B-eta.                              (A11)

Here is the complete finite reduction implementing that step; it is not
an oracle request. Use the predecessor's Euler mesh and global THG
interpolation constraints separately in both tables, at all calibration,
anchor, grid and trajectory locations. Include (A10) as linear constraints
between the two gradient tables. Include the explicit bounded-state,
gradient and value boxes from the predecessor. Each feasible table extends
to one stationary global potential; the Euler updates then belong to that
potential. The product construction therefore preserves exact source
consistency in both blocks, including every query and every trajectory.
The value/gradient boxes use a radius covering CR, all calibration
locations and Q, not just the task trajectory points.

Let C_task be the predecessor's bound

  |S_true-S_Euler| <= C_task Delta,

for maximum Euler step Delta. It is explicitly

  C_E=Lambda M T E/2,
  C_obs=max(1,K c,(G+K c R)c),
  C_task=L_H C_obs C_E.

Choose Delta<=eta/(8 C_task) if C_task>0, including all listed switch
and observation times. Then the two-task error is <=eta/4. If C_task=0,
use its zero-error case. Exact real-algebraic optimization of the compact
finite paired problem supplies its maximum b_N and an optimizing table.
Isolate b_N in an interval [b_lo,b_hi] of width <=eta/4 and put

  B=b_hi+2 C_task Delta.

The two true tasks of the reconstructed witnesses differ by at least
B-3 eta/4, which implies (A11). Every true feasible pair contributes an
Euler table, so B is also a valid upper bound. The feasible set is nonempty
because V=W and z=w are allowed. Thus B>=0.

To see what (A10) means over ALL of Q, use the K-Lipschitz gradient of each
model. A nearby grid point gives

  d_Q(V,W) <= 2 delta+q+2 K h = 2 delta+s.              (A12)

Apply (A7) with r=2 delta to the witnesses from (A11). The repaired pair
lies in F, obeys d_Q<=2 delta, and its ordered task gap is at least

  B-eta-Drepair.                                      (A13)

The repair is an explicit rational convex combination of two finite
algebraic THG witnesses. The predecessor supplies their complete
evaluation algorithm; evaluate both and combine, paying the two costs.

Return

  ell=max(0,(B-eta-Drepair)/2),
  U=B/2+rho.                                          (A14)

The experiment programme is concrete:

  1. Query every x_j in Q_h at precision p.
  2. Append the reported coordinate boxes of radius delta+q/2 to the
     calibration history of the SAME unknown potential.
  3. Run the retained complete posterior inference algorithm with each
     outer endpoint within rho of the true posterior endpoint.
  4. Return the midpoint of that certified interval, and the interval.

Any pair in the resulting posterior obeys (A10). Its task diameter is
therefore <=B. The outer interval has width <=B+2 rho, giving error <=U.
For an inadmissible history the same algorithm reports inconsistency.

The repaired pair supplies the universal lower bound ell. Moreover

  U-ell <= (eta+Drepair)/2+rho <= 5 epsilon/8 < epsilon.

The inequality also holds when ell=0: its defining truncation implies
B<=eta+Drepair. This proves the algorithmic output contract. As epsilon
is arbitrary, (A4) and these near-optimal finite programmes prove (A3).
No compactness-only limit without a rate is used to claim computability.

A7. Costs, representations and degenerate cases

Every requested constant, power and mesh is actually generated. E has
ceil(2 Lambda T)+1 bits; this can already be large in binary input length.
For J time subintervals between listed events, the paired construction has

  N <= 8 T C_task/eta+J

Euler steps, with the zero-error special case handled directly. Let q_c
be the number of prior calibration points and P=N+M_q+q_c+2 the number
of potential-table points per block. A conservative variable bound is

  v <= 2[(N+1)n+P(d+1)]+O(1).

The number of polynomial constraints is

  S_alg=O(P^2+N n+M_q d+v)+2 S_input.

Let D_alg be the maximum of 2 and the actual input/task degrees, and tau
the maximum coefficient bit length after the constructed constraints
are expanded and denominators cleared. Standard complete CAD has a
conservative bit bound of the retained form

  (S_alg D_alg)^(2^O(v)) (tau+b_out+1)^O(1),             (A15)

where b_out includes the requested isolation accuracy and the computed
task magnitude. Use the actual chosen ordinary CAD implementation and
its fixed constants. Convexity validation of a general semialgebraic D
is another charged quantified problem. The online posterior CAD call
uses one table and its corresponding costs. Algebraic root isolation,
sample extraction, comparisons and witness representation are included.

There are M_q vector measurements and d M_q scalar reports. If R_Q is a
rational upper bound on |x| for x in Q, their bit lengths are bounded by

  O(p+log2(2+G+K R_Q+delta))

per scalar, with signs and integer parts counted. Query-coordinate bits
are the actual rational grid encodings. Generated grid size, report size,
and query preparation are charged, not just the number of vector calls.
The dyadic precision p is a numeric cost, not merely the length of its
binary exponent. Subsequent evaluation of the returned THG witnesses
pays the finite active-face/algebraic arithmetic procedure in the prior
construction; convex repair doubles, rather than hides, that evaluation.

The method is not polynomial in the complete input. The grid has the
usual dimension-dependent size; the temporal mesh can be exponential in
accuracy bits or encoded physical magnitudes; CAD can be doubly
exponential in its already-large variable count. There is no unit-cost
real oracle, uncharged preprocessing, nonuniform lookup or hidden solver.

The mu=L quadratic-affine case, T=0, K=0, repeated/degenerate grid points,
empty calibration history and zero task derivative are all covered by
the stated branches and the retained exact interpolation special cases.
The positive native-error assumption is used in (A8); the theorem does
not silently supply a zero-noise effective inverse modulus.

A8. Deductions that come directly from the operation

1. Experimental futility certificates. The repaired pair certifies that
   no allowed adaptive experiment programme, regardless of its finite
   computation or number of observations, can guarantee error below ell.
   At the same time the delivered programme guarantees U. This completes
   a prediction-specific experimental information assessment, without
   first identifying the entire constitutive law.

2. Instrument accuracy sensitivity. With the same fixed prior and query
   region, for tau_noise>=0, applying (A8) gives

   0 <= R^*_(delta+tau_noise)-R^*_delta
          <= Cstar tau_noise/[2(delta+tau_noise)].       (A16)

   Thus within this presentation and positive-error contract, a smaller
   native error has a controlled value for the task. Increasing the
   number of repetitions alone has no such promised effect.

3. Existing history is reusable. Appending compatible convex data shrinks
   F and its indistinguishable pairs. Both optimal experimental error and
   posterior uncertainty cannot increase. History is imposed on one law,
   not on unrelated laws at different query or trajectory points.

4. Several tasks. For a finite task vector with maximum-coordinate loss,
   the exact error floor is max_j Omega_j(2 delta)/2. Use the union of
   finitely many near-optimal grids and midpoint estimators. Every
   coordinate lower bound remains valid, and the upper bounds hold
   simultaneously under the deterministic error contract. All repeated
   computations and reports are paid. This statement concerns the
   maximum-coordinate loss, not an arbitrary norm's Chebyshev radius.

5. Finite interventions. For a supplied finite list of interventions
   sharing the same unknown V and admissible initial-state contract,
   apply the vector construction to their outcomes. If every outcome
   prediction has error <=r, choosing the smallest predicted outcome has
   regret <=2r relative to the best listed intervention for the actual
   source: insert the two prediction errors in the comparison. This is a
   constructed decision rule and explicit bound, not a claim that the
   prediction floor equals the optimal decision-regret floor. Actual
   intervention execution and validity remain external assumptions.

6. Reach and limits. The previous mapping to mechanical systems,
   overdamped flow and known networks remains valid. The result includes
   nonlinear tasks and potentially nonconvex energies. Known coupling,
   qualified global curvature/anchor bounds, independence of the initial
   family, and direct gradient access over Q remain real information.
   The construction measures exactly what that information buys; it
   does not infer it from finite unqualified measurements.


PART B. CHEAP FORWARD MODELS DO NOT MAKE LEARNABILITY COMPUTABLE

B1. A different, explicit input representation

The input is a finite binary-tape Turing-machine description e, including
its initial finite tape. It generates two candidate potentials

  V_0(x)=x^2/2,
  V_1^e(x)=x^2/2+a_e x,

  a_e=1/2+beta_e,
  beta_e=2^(-(t+2)) if e first halts at step t>=0,
          0 if e never halts.                           (B1)

Every a_e is rational and 1/2<=a_e<=3/4. Both potentials are analytic,
have Hessian identically 1, are normalized at 0, and have anchor gradient
bounded by 1. Each scalar gradient flow x'=-V_i'(x) contracts at rate 1
and has unique equilibrium

  S_0=0,             S_1=-a_e.                          (B2)

The task may equivalently be minus the gradient at zero. Thus the example
does not rely on an unconstructed infinite-time limit or a difficult
trajectory evaluator: the equilibrium is the algebraic identity (B2).

The only measurements are gradient queries at rational x in [-1,1], with
the same dyadic closed channel (A1), now at fixed delta=1/4. Q, curvature,
stability, noise and task stay fixed as e varies. The prior is exactly
the two listed models, not their convex hull.

B2. Uniform polynomial-bit-time certified forward evaluation

To approximate a_e to error 2^(-p), simulate e for at most p steps.
If it halts at t<=p, return the exact dyadic 1/2+2^(-(t+2)). Otherwise
return 1/2 with the certified interval

  [1/2, 1/2+2^(-(p+3))].                               (B3)

Any still-unseen first halt has t>=p+1, so (B3) is valid. The evaluation
does not decide whether the simulation will ever halt. A direct bounded
interpreter uses time polynomial in |e|+p and a polynomial number of bits.
The returned dyadic denominator has O(p) bits. Schoolbook arithmetic and
a simple tape/transition-table simulation give a uniform conservative
polynomial bound; no accelerated universal simulation is needed.

For a rational input x of bit length b, potential evaluation at error
2^(-p) needs coefficient precision p+O(b), since |x|<=2^O(b). Gradient
and equilibrium evaluation are simpler. A conservative explicit bound
for a direct deterministic multitape implementation, including input
validation, tape/table scans and schoolbook rational arithmetic, is

  O((|e|+b+p+1)^6) bit operations.                     (B3a)

For example, a simulated step scans at most |e|+p+O(b) stored tape/table
symbols with polynomial bookkeeping, and there are at most p+O(b) steps;
the rational outputs have O(b+p) bits. The deliberately loose sixth power
covers these scans, comparisons, products and gcd computations. The
generic evaluator code and its error proof are supplied; this is stronger
than an uneffective promise that each candidate is computable.

B3. Exact learnability value and the reduction

If e never halts, a_e=1/2=2 delta. At any query x, the two gradients
x and x+1/2 admit the common analog report x+1/4 and therefore a common
dyadic report at every requested precision. The same-transcript argument
forces worst-case equilibrium error at least 1/4. The fixed estimate
-1/4 attains it. Hence

  R^*(e)=1/4 when e does not halt.                      (B4)

If e halts at t, beta_e>0. A uniform procedure can simulate until that
halt, construct its exact rational coefficient, choose p=t+3 and query
x=0. Here q=2^(-p)=beta_e/2, and the two allowed report intervals have
separation

  a_e - 2(delta+q/2) = beta_e-q >0.

They are disjoint. The report identifies the source; output its exact
equilibrium from (B2). This finite procedure has zero error. Its
simulation time, p bits, arithmetic and output are all charged. Thus

  R^*(e)=0 when e halts.                               (B5)

No halting advice is supplied: the construction on halting instances
waits for the actual halt. It is not claimed to terminate on a nonhalting
instance as an accuracy-zero procedure.

THEOREM B.

There is no uniform algorithm which, for every description e in this
family, either

  (i) decides whether some finite permitted programme can guarantee
      equilibrium error <1/8, or
 (ii) approximates R^*(e) within absolute error 1/16.

Either algorithm would distinguish (B4) from (B5), deciding the halting
problem. For (ii), compare its answer with 1/8; the two allowed answer
intervals are separated. This proves a fixed-margin impossibility for
the optimal ACCURACY VALUE, not merely noncomputability of an optimizer.

This does not contradict Part A or the predecessor. The input presentation
has changed: a_e is supplied by its certified evaluator, not by an
explicit numerator and denominator, and the two-model prior is nonconvex.
If the rational value a_e is supplied explicitly, the comparison with
1/2 is computable. Its potentially long representation must then be part
of the input and cost. No claim excludes the previous finite rational
semialgebraic-input inference algorithm.

B4. A bit-cost barrier on the instances that ARE learnable

This stronger quantitative observation uses the fixed-point report
encoding and closed channel exactly as specified in A1. Consider a
programme of total worst-case bit work <=B. Then every requested p<=B,
and the positive denominator b of any rational query x=a/b has <=B bits.
The source-0 and source-1 allowed report intervals intersect in

  [ x+1/4+beta_e-q/2, x+1/4+q/2 ].                      (B6)

Let L0=x+1/4-q/2 and choose y to be the smallest multiple of q strictly
greater than L0. Then 0<y-L0<=q. Since all quantities are rational,
the denominator of y-L0 divides b*2^max(2,p+1); consequently the positive
difference is at least 2^(-(2B+3)), a conservative bound.

If beta_e<=2^(-(2B+3)), this y lies in (B6). Return it after each query.
The resulting transcript stays compatible with both models regardless
of the programme's adaptation or auxiliary calculations. Its output
therefore has error at least a_e/2>=1/4 on one source.

For a first halt at time t, beta_e=2^(-(t+2)). Thus any programme that
guarantees error <1/8 must have worst-case work

  B > (t-1)/2.                                        (B7)

This bound counts query-position precision as well as reported precision:
finely moving x can exploit quantizer boundaries and must not be free.
The physical acquisition work can be larger; the bound already follows
from the stipulated finite bit interface. A different rounding-tie or
compressed-precision channel would require a new cost argument.

There is no computable function f(|e|) bounding the required work of all
learnable instances. Otherwise every halting machine would halt before
2 f(|e|)+1 steps by (B7), giving a computable halting-time bound and hence
a decision algorithm. This conclusion counts all preparation; it cannot
be evaded by an instance-specific lookup or by preprocessing the answer.

B5. What the discontinuity means

For two offsets 0 and a, the bounded-error experimental floor is a/2
when a<=2 delta, and zero when a>2 delta and arbitrarily fine paid
quantization is available. A tiny positive separation beyond the native
noise threshold changes optimal source discrimination discontinuously.
The reduction encodes a halting event in that tiny separation.

For the convex hull of these same quadratics, theta in [0,a], the exact
floor instead is min(a,2 delta)/2. In (B1), a>=2 delta=1/2, so it is
always 1/4. The discrete-prior gain has disappeared. This directly
illustrates why the convex repair in Part A controls the information
floor. Convexity alone is not an assertion that every arbitrary programme
presentation is effective; Part A also supplies finite algebraic
feasibility, uniform task estimates and explicit encodings.

The reduction is proved for arbitrary input machines. The code checks
only specified finite halting examples and a loop whose unchanging state
proves its own nonhalting. Passing those tests is not the undecidability
proof, and no general nonhalting inference is made from finite simulation.


PART C. EXECUTED OPTIMAL DESIGN FOR A WHOLE CONSTITUTIVE FAMILY

C1. Contract and attained posterior consequences

The unknown stationary scalar law g has all secants in [m,L], with
rational 0<m<=L. Prior samples are finite rational intervals

  ell_i <= g(x_i) <= u_i.

At least one sample is supplied. A query action is a rational location x,
a nonnegative precision p, and a positive integer cost. A finite catalogue
and an integer total budget are part of the input. Native error delta>=0
is rational; reports obey the same channel with eta=delta+2^(-p)/2.
Repeated actions are allowed. They can return different legal reports.

The task is r(g)=g^(-1)(u), the unique equilibrium of x'=u-g(x).
Global m>0 implies strict monotonicity, surjectivity and contraction.
Equivalently, g is the derivative of a C^(1,1) potential with curvature
in [m,L]. It need not be analytic. The closed-class N1039 envelopes are
the accepted primary operational ingredient:

  g_-(x)=m x+max_i[ell_i-m x_i-(L-m)(x_i-x)_+],
  g_+(x)=m x+min_i[u_i-m x_i+(L-m)(x-x_i)_+].           (C1)

The history is feasible iff g_-(x_i)<=u_i for every datum. For a feasible
history, both envelopes are actual globally compatible laws, and every
compatible g lies pointwise between them. Inverting the individual cones
gives the EXACT attained equilibrium interval [r_lo,r_hi]:

  r_lo=max_i[x_i+(u-u_i)/(m if u<=u_i, else L)],
  r_hi=min_i[x_i+(u-ell_i)/(L if u<=ell_i, else m)].     (C2)

Indeed g_+ is a minimum of increasing cones, so its inverse at u is the
maximum of their individual roots; g_- gives the corresponding minimum.
The extremal sources are g_+ and g_-. The optimal zero-new-data scalar
estimator is the interval midpoint and its risk is (r_hi-r_lo)/2.

To evaluate actual witness potentials, integrate an envelope from 0 to x.
It is piecewise affine. Split at all sample locations and all intersections
of the finitely many lines of slopes m,L defining the cones, then use
exact trapezoidal integration on each piece. These are explicit finite
rational source models, not per-measurement choices of unrelated laws.

C2. Exact finite-budget recursion

At a history D and an action a=(x,p,c), put q=2^(-p) and eta=delta+q/2.
All possible report integers j satisfy

  ceil((g_-(x)-eta)/q) <= j <= floor((g_+(x)+eta)/q).

For each j, append [j q-eta,j q+eta] at x, merge repeated locations by
intersection, and keep it exactly when (C1) proves feasibility. This
enumerates every permitted branch and excludes impossible branches.

Let R_b(D) be optimal worst-case task error with remaining budget b.
Then

  R_b(D)=min{ (r_hi-r_lo)/2,
              min_{a:c_a<=b} max_{feasible y}
                   R_(b-c_a)(D plus (a,y)) }.          (C3)

This finite recursion terminates because c_a>=1. It includes stopping.
All quantities are rational; rational comparisons choose an optimal
action, and a terminal node returns its exact midpoint.

Proof of optimality. Any allowed deterministic policy must stop or choose
one catalogue action. Every feasible report is a possible response from
some member of the current posterior. Induct on remaining budget. The
maximum of the child optimal risks is a lower bound for that choice and
is attained by using optimal child policies, so minimization gives (C3).
It is legitimate for the adversary to select a branch at each stage:
after a complete finite history, (C1) provides one fixed global source
for all its data. The pair of endpoint laws at a worst stopping leaf
generates the same entire transcript and forces half its root separation
against any estimator. No source is actually switched along the path.

C4. Exact continuum floor for this anchored family

More generally, for g(0)=0, m>0, L>=m, u>0, and an observation region
containing [0,u/m], refinable quantization and native delta>=0 give

  R_infinity = min{ (u/m-u/L)/2, delta/m }.             (C4)

For L=m the family is a singleton and the formula is zero. For L>m,
all roots lie in [u/L,u/m]. If two observed laws differ by at most 2 delta,
evaluate their difference at one root and use the lower slope m to get
root separation <=2 delta/m. This proves the upper bound on their
observational task modulus.

It is attained. Put A0=min(2 delta,u(1-m/L)), c0=A0/(L-m), and define

  g(x)=m x,
  f(x)=m x+(L-m) min(max(x,0),c0).                     (C5)

Both are anchored and have secants in [m,L], and their force difference
is <=A0 on all of R. Their roots are u/m and (u-A0)/m. The latter is
>=c0 precisely because A0<=u(1-m/L), so their separation is A0/m.
This proves the matching lower bound with explicit global sources.

The upper bound is approached by a complete finite operation, including
when delta=0. Query a rational grid of covering radius h in [0,u/m] at
precision q. Every posterior pair then has force difference at most
2 delta+q+2 L h throughout that interval. Formula (C2) therefore gives
posterior root diameter at most

  min(u/m-u/L, (2 delta+q+2 L h)/m).

Return its exact midpoint. Choose q<=m epsilon and h<=m epsilon/(2 L)
to obtain error <=R_infinity+epsilon; the prior-width bound handles the
case where that is the smaller term in (C4). All grid queries, report
bits and exact inverse-envelope operations are finite and paid. This
also proves the zero-noise scalar case directly; it does not assert a
general zero-noise inverse theorem outside Part A.

In the fixture, A0=1/16 and c0=1/32. The roots 15/16 and 1 remain
indistinguishable through the native channel; their half-separation is
1/32. The finite two-query risk is larger because its budget, catalogue
and quantization are restricted. The executed policy did not reach the
unlimited-programme floor, and no such claim is made.

C5. Bit costs and what was implemented

Let A_cat be the catalogue size, K_b=floor(budget/min action cost), and
t0 the number of distinct prior sample locations. For action a, let
q_a=2^(-p_a) and let [l_a,u_a] be the prior force range from (C1). A bound
on the number of reports at any descendant is

  J_a=floor((u_a-l_a+2 delta)/q_a)+2,
  Jmax=max_a J_a.

An empty catalogue returns the prior midpoint directly, with no recursion;
in that case the expressions involving a minimum action cost are unused.

The full recursion has at most

  T_tree=sum_{k=0}^{K_b} (A_cat Jmax)^k

states before memoization. There are at most A_cat Jmax outgoing edges
per state. History length is at most t0+K_b. A direct feasibility check
takes O((t0+K_b)^2) rational operations. Normalization, envelopes, inverse
cones and all response enumeration costs are included. Each arithmetic
operand has polynomial bit length in the original rational data lengths,
the numeric maximum p_a, and the budget encoding; interval intersections
select endpoints rather than repeatedly creating algebraic numbers.
For an explicit loose bound, let I be the complete input length, b the
largest bit length of an input rational component, P=max_a p_a, t=t0+K_b,
and w=1+b+P+ceil(log2(2+budget))+ceil(log2(2+T_tree)). Intermediate rational
operands have O(b+P) bits: each datum is an original interval or one
reported dyadic plus/minus the fixed error; intersections only select
endpoints, and (C1)-(C2) use a bounded number of arithmetic operations
on them. Schoolbook arithmetic and even linear-time associative lookups
therefore give the conservative bound

  O(I+T_tree^2 (A_cat Jmax+1)(t+1)^2(w+1)^3)           (C6)

for construction/replay, including the possible memoization lookup costs
and certificate output. A straightforward unmemoized tree traversal
avoids those lookup costs. This is exponential exhaustive minimax
computation in general, not a polynomial optimal-design claim. Numeric
budget and precision, rather than only their short binary exponents,
enter the bound. The reference Python execution has the usual finite
memory and recursion resources; the mathematical recursion can be
implemented with an explicit stack for larger budgets.
