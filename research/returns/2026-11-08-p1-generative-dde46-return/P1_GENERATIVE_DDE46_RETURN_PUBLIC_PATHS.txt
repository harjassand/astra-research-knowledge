5. A separate consequence of exterior positivity

The accepted CPTP-exterior identity also yields a useful transport theorem for the general GKLS short-time class.

For product input and product readout, write the exact output law on a retained site set $W$ as

$$P_W(x_W)=
\sum_{\mathcal F\text{ disjoint in }W}
\prod_{X\in\mathcal F}f_X(x_X)
\prod_{j\in W\setminus\cup\mathcal F}\mu_j(x_j),
\tag{10}$$

where

$$\sum_{x_X}f_X(x_X)=0,
\qquad
a_X=\frac12\|f_X\|_1.$$

Every $P_W$ is positive because it is the output law of the channel retaining whole anchors supported in $W$. These laws are not assumed to be consistent marginals.

Fix $i$, and compare

$$p=P_V,\qquad q=\mu_i\otimes P_{V\setminus\{i\}}.$$

There exists one coupling $(U,V_{\mathrm{out}})$ satisfying simultaneously

$$\Pr[U_j\ne V_{\mathrm{out},j}]
\le\sum_{X\ni i,j}a_X,
\tag{11}$$

and

$$\mathbb E\,d_H(U,V_{\mathrm{out}})
\le\sum_{X\ni i}|X|a_X.
\tag{12}$$

The proof starts from

$$p-q=\sum_{X\ni i}f_X\otimes P_{V\setminus X}.$$

Move the negative part of each local $f_X$ to its positive part while retaining its exterior value. These are nonnegative labelled flows of total mass $a_X$. Remove cycles and decompose the flow into surplus-to-deficit paths. The resulting positive coupling obeys every coordinate bound at once.

For the accepted anchored window, let

$$q_\star=esh\nu T<\frac14.$$

A rooted connected-word count gives

$$\sum_{X\ni i}\|K_X\|_\diamond
\le-\frac{\log(1-q_\star)}s$$

and hence

$$\boxed{
\mathbb E\,d_H(U,V_{\mathrm{out}})
\le\frac{q_\star}{2(1-q_\star)}<\frac16.
}
\tag{13}$$

The same coupling has a geometric probability bound for changes outside a radius-$R$ ball.

This is stronger than a global distance estimate, but the expected number of changed coordinates is not an expected computation bound. The explicit flow contains extensive exterior point probabilities.

A three-variable example makes the distinction exact. Put

$$\mu=(1/2,1/2),\qquad v=(-1,1),$$

and use kernels

$$f_{12}=t\,\mu_1\otimes v_2,\qquad
f_{23}=t\,v_2\otimes v_3,
\qquad 0<t\le1/8.$$

All induced laws are positive and the kernel norms tend to zero with $t$. The deletion coupling rooted at site $1$ changes only sites $1,2$. Nevertheless, no stochastic update on $1,2$ that is independent of site $3$ realizes it.

Such an update would induce a binary stochastic matrix $A$ with

$$A\mu=\mu+tv,\qquad Av=v.$$

The second identity forces $A=I$, contradicting the first. A localized change may require exterior information to decide when it occurs.

This is an abstract limitation of that proposed implementation. It is not native sampling hardness.

6. What prevents the full conclusion

General GKLS records are different from Pauli histories

For a Kraus instrument $\{K_e\}$, the synchronized auxiliary map

$$z\longmapsto\bigoplus_e(K_e\otimes K_e)z$$

is contractive in the sum of replica projective norms:

$$\sum_e\|(K_e\otimes K_e)z\|_\pi\le\|z\|_\pi.
\tag{14}$$

For a simple tensor, this is Cauchy–Schwarz and
$\sum_eK_e^\dagger K_e=I$.

There is also a valid physical-time cancellation: a jump amplitude is $\sqrt{dt}L_j$, but its synchronized two-replica contribution is

$$dt\,L_j\otimes L_j.$$

With a controlled finite-dimensional GKLS gauge, the associated activity bound is computable and does not diverge as the numerical step shrinks.

These facts are useful, but they condition on the same complete instrument record. They do not sample the record’s state-dependent Born law, and they do not justify discarding arbitrary remote parts of it.

The distinction is already visible in one-site depolarization. A pure Kraus refinement of the evolution of $|0\rangle$ has only pure $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ branches. Their record-conditioned impurity is zero; forgetting the record gives a mixed state. This easy example refutes that inference, not sampling.

The unital sampler avoids the problem because its entire Pauli history is independently samplable before any quantum-state calculation. No corresponding operation has been constructed here for every allowed multi-qubit GKLS perturbation at fixed positive $\eta$.

Short-time sampling cannot simply be composed in time

The two-copy cancellation starts from the original product preparation. After a short slab, the quantum state need not be product. A sample of its measurement distribution does not specify that state or its response to later noncommuting dynamics.

Restarting the short-time sampler from the sampled bits would change the experiment. Reapplying the proof to the original preparation instead makes the connected-expansion parameter depend on the accumulated time. Neither operation supplies arbitrary-time coverage.

Scalar cluster sampling still requires the physical scalar representation

I constructed a paid local-polymer proposal under an explicit geometric scalar-activity bound. Capping polymer size at $O(\log(1/\delta))$, and charging evaluation and dyadic choices, gives worst-case polynomial bit work at that scope.

The current Monte Carlo source samples connected clusters to estimate $\log Z$; its convergence and normalization hypotheses must hold for the actual scalar model. It is not directly an output-configuration sampler. arxiv.org [https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03893v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com]

The channel norm bounds do not automatically establish those hypotheses under physical readout boundaries. A native two-qubit family shows that even incorporating on-site noise into the reference law does not make all relative corrections small.

Take rational $r>0$,

$$b=\frac{4r^2}{(1+r^2)^2},\qquad T=b/J,$$

and the rational pure preparations

$$|\psi_1\rangle=
\frac{(1-r^2)|0\rangle+2ir|1\rangle}{1+r^2},
\qquad
|\psi_2\rangle=
\frac{(1-r^2)|0\rangle+2r|1\rangle}{1+r^2}.$$

Use $H=JX_1X_2$, native depolarization and computational readout. Let $p$ be the actual law and $q$ the law with $H$ omitted but the same noise retained. As $r\to0$,

$$q(11)=(J+\gamma/2)^2T^2+o(T^2),$$

$$p(11)=(\gamma J+\gamma^2/4)T^2+o(T^2).$$

Therefore

$$\frac{p(11)-q(11)}{q(11)}
\longrightarrow
-\left(\frac{g}{g+1/2}\right)^2,
\tag{15}$$

although $\|p-q\|_1\le2JT\to0$. At large fixed $g$, this relative negative correction is close to $-1$. The pair is easy to sample; it obstructs the proposed uniform relative-activity estimate.

For rank-one projective readout, this requires $V$ to be monomial in the measurement basis. An arbitrary Clifford need not satisfy it.

Final audited boundary

The short-time unital sampler is complete. It covers the native model with $\eta_z=0$, arbitrary rational Hamiltonian schedules, arbitrary specified rational product preparations, arbitrary finite rational one-site POVMs, and worst-case polynomial bit work. It uses the accepted pure-history MPS reader and the two-copy localization argument below.

The full arbitrary-time, positive-nonunital-neighbourhood endpoint remains unresolved. No claim about general fixed $\eta>0$ or all physical times follows from this result.

1. Explicit sufficient time regime

Expand each local Hamiltonian term into at most $4^k$ Pauli terms. Each coefficient has magnitude at most $J$. Put

$$d'=4^k d.$$

After removing scalar identity terms, a term-conflict coloring uses

$$c\le kd'$$

colors: the conflict degree is at most $k(d'-1)$.

Choose fixed connected anchor balls satisfying

$$|B_i|\le s,\qquad
\sup_x|\{i:x\in B_i\}|\le h,\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(B_i)\le b,$$

with $b\ge1$. All these constants are computable from the fixed physical locality class.

Use the conservative threshold

$$\boxed{
JT\le K^{-1},\qquad K=24shd'c.
}
\tag{1}$$

Sequential color embedding has duration at most $cT$, and the doubled anchored generator norm is at most $2d'J$. Hence

$$q=2e\,shd'cJT<6shd'cJT\le\tfrac14.
\tag{2}$$

This threshold is independent of $n,\epsilon$, the schedule length, and the particular allowed Hamiltonian.

2. Two-copy localization: the precise norm statement

For the two replicas, use the projective Hilbert norm

$$\|\phi\otimes\chi\|_\pi=\|\phi\|\,\|\chi\|.$$

Unitary evolution $U\otimes U$ is an isometry in this norm. A local doubled weak gate obeys

$$\|U_j\otimes U_j-I\|_\pi
\le 2\|U_j-I\|.
\tag{3}$$

Technical precision: use this bound after arbitrary idle physical systems are added in both replicas. Equivalently, use the completely amplified projective norm. An unamplified local projective norm should not simply be assumed multiplicative under such extensions.

One can avoid that terminology altogether by defining

$$\alpha_X
=
\sum_{\substack{\text{connected gate words}\\
\text{declared union }X}}
\prod_{j\ {\rm in\ word}}2\|U_j-I\|.
\tag{4}$$

Then every amplified connected kernel $K_X$ has projective norm at most $\alpha_X$, and disjoint products are bounded by products of these majorants. The rooted tree count applies directly to (4).

Let $A,B,C$ partition the sites, with $B$ shielding fixed-size $A$ from $C$, and let

$$R=\operatorname{dist}(A,C).$$

For rank-one product POVM outcomes $b$ on $B$, write

$$\psi_b=M_bU\psi_0,\qquad p_b=\|\psi_b\|^2.$$

With

$$P_A^-=(I-\operatorname{Swap}_A)/2,$$

$$\|P_A^-\psi_b^{\otimes2}\|
=
p_b\sqrt{\frac{1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{A|b}^2}{2}}.
\tag{5}$$

The map

$$v\longmapsto\{P_A^-(M_b\otimes M_b)v\}_b$$

is a contraction into the $\ell_1$-sum of Hilbert norms. For simple tensors this is Cauchy–Schwarz and POVM completeness; the projective-norm extension is immediate.

Every connected doubled kernel commutes with swapping the replicas on its declared support. If no component of a family connects $A$ to $C$, identical outcomes on its $B$ sites turn component-swap symmetry into symmetry on its part of $A$. Thus $P_A^-$ annihilates that family.

Only an $A$-to-$C$ component survives. The accepted inclusion–exclusion resummation uses exterior restricted evolution $U_{\rm ext}\otimes U_{\rm ext}$, whose amplified projective norm is $1$. Therefore

$$\sum_b p_b
\sqrt{\frac{1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{A|b}^2}{2}}
\le e^{A_{AC}}-1,
\tag{6}$$

where

$$A_{AC}:=\sum_{\substack{X\cap A\ne\varnothing\\X\cap C\ne\varnothing}}\alpha_X
\le
\frac{|A|}{s}
\frac{q^{\lceil R/b\rceil}}{1-q}.
\tag{7}$$

Consequently the average conditional output error satisfies the conservative bound

$$\boxed{
\mathbb E_{b,c}\operatorname{TV}
\bigl(P_{A|b,c},P_{A|b}\bigr)
\le 2(e^{A_{AC}}-1).
}
\tag{8}$$

There is no overlap lower bound and no division by a rare outcome probability in this estimate.

The gate-by-gate framework and its use of averaged postmeasurement impurity are credited to arXiv:2407.21203, Section IV/Theorem 7. Its source theorem does not itself establish the physical-time localization (6)–(8). 

3. Complete generative algorithm

Native replacement noise has independent state-independent Pauli histories:

$$\gamma(E_i-I)
=
\frac{\gamma}{4}\sum_{P=X,Y,Z}
(P_i\,\cdot\,P_i-I).
\tag{9}$$

Sample a bounded-precision history, with its count capped using a paid Poisson tail. Conditional on the history, a toggling frame leaves a pure Hamiltonian evolution with the same locality and norm bounds. Final Pauli factors are absorbed into the POVMs.

Approximate this history by $M$ rational weak local gates. At each gate supported on $A$:

- Keep the current outcomes outside $A$.

- Choose a shielding region $B$ with
$$R=b\left\lceil\log_4(64M/\epsilon)\right\rceil .$$


- Construct a positive rational approximation $Q_{AB}$ to the post-gate joint law.

- Resample the outcomes on $A$ from the exact rational conditional $Q_{A|B=b}$.

The local unitary leaves the outside marginal unchanged.

For arbitrary positive joint laws $P,Q$,

$$\boxed{
\mathbb E_{b\sim P_B}
\operatorname{TV}(P_{A|b},Q_{A|b})
\le2\,\operatorname{TV}(P_{AB},Q_{AB}).
}
\tag{10}$$

This includes $Q_B(b)=0$, where the conditional may be chosen arbitrarily.

Thus $Q_{AB}$ only needs absolute joint accuracy $O(\epsilon/M)$. Tiny conditional probabilities do not impose a relative-accuracy cost.

A Lieb–Robinson buffer reduces this local joint law to a patch with

$$N_W=O_{\rm class}\!\left(\log^2(M/\epsilon)\right).$$

The accepted pure-history MPS compiler applies there. In a row ordering,

$$A_\pi(W)=O_{\rm class}\!\left(J\log(M/\epsilon)\right),$$

and its integrated crossing-angle cost is

$$\exp\!\left[
O_{\rm class}\!\left(JT\log(M/\epsilon)\right)
\right],
\tag{11}$$

which is polynomial under (1).

The resulting rational MPS, normalized by its exact rational squared norm, defines a positive joint law. Conditional weights are rational transfer-matrix contractions with the specified local effects. Their bit lengths are polynomial. The zero-denominator branch is covered by (10).

The one-step errors telescope by stochastic-kernel contraction:

$$\operatorname{TV}(W_j,P_j)
\le
\operatorname{TV}(W_{j-1},P_{j-1})
+
2(e^{A_{AC}}-1)
+
2\operatorname{TV}(P_{j,AB},Q_{j,AB}).$$

Choosing each local error $O(\epsilon/M)$ completes the unconditional full-output TV guarantee.

4. Exact rational input and readout reductions

Square roots are unnecessary in the effects.

For

$$M=\begin{pmatrix}a&z\\z^*&b\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,\qquad a>0,$$

let $v=(a,z^*)^{\mathsf T}$. Then

$$M=\frac{vv^\dagger}{a}
+
\left(b-\frac{|z|^2}{a}\right)|1\rangle\langle1|.
\tag{12}$$

Both summands are positive rational rank-one effects. If $a=0$, positivity forces $z=0$.

Applying (12) to every POVM effect gives an exact rational rank-one refinement; final coarse-graining recovers the original labels.

Applying it to each input density matrix gives an exact rational mixture of at most two rational pure density matrices. Only the classical draw of a rational mixture weight needs finite random-bit approximation. Rational unnormalized pure vectors suffice internally: common normalization factors cancel in density contractions and are paid by the MPS precision accounting.

Exactly unitary rational Cayley gates may be used:

$$U_\Delta=
(I-i\Delta H/2)(I+i\Delta H/2)^{-1}.$$

For a Pauli term their angle is $2\arctan(h\Delta/2)$, whose magnitude is at most $|h|\Delta$. This preserves the weak-gate and integrated-crossing-angle bounds without rounding-induced interaction strength.

5. Complete cost and time encoding

Rescale time by $J$ first. All loops depend on

$$\tau=JT,\qquad \gamma T=\tau/g,$$

rather than on numeric $T$.

The native Pauli count has mean $3n\gamma T/4$, so a cap

$$K_{\max}
=
O\!\left(n\gamma T+\log(1/\epsilon)\right)$$

has a paid exceptional probability. Under (1),

$$\gamma T\le\frac1{gK}.$$

A rational colored product formula gives

$$M\le
\operatorname{poly}_{\rm class}
\left(n,B,1+g^{-1},\epsilon^{-1}\right).$$

Every patch reader has work polynomial in $N_W$, the complete compiled schedule, and $M/\epsilon$, times (11). There are $M$ updates. Input reading, jump generation, time rounding, effect refinement, MPS arithmetic, rational division, output, and bounded random-bit sampling are all included.

The resulting worst-case bit bound is therefore

$$\boxed{
\operatorname{poly}_{\rm class}
\left(n,B,1+g^{-1},\epsilon^{-1}\right)
}$$

for the regime (1), with a computable degree inherited from the accepted finite-bit MPS compiler.

6. Exact unresolved boundary

For general GKLS perturbations, the synchronized lift

$$v\mapsto\bigoplus_a(K_a\otimes K_a)v$$

is contractive in $\ell_1$ of the replica projective norm, and its small-step deviation from null-record identity is $O(\Delta)$. This gives a legitimate physical-time component expansion for complete-record-conditioned averaged impurity.

It does not control the impurity after remote records are hidden, and it does not sample their state-dependent Born distribution. Those missing operations prevent extending the completed sampler to fixed positive general $\eta$.

Accordingly:

- Proved: full-output polynomial-bit sampling for the scheduled native unital class in the explicit short-time regime (1).

- Not proved: the arbitrary-time or fixed-strength general-nonunital sampler.

- Not inferred: a sampling-hardness obstruction from either remaining gap.

1. Replica cancellation and amplification

The cancellation is valid.

For a connected component $X$, every doubled local generator supported in $X$ commutes with the swap of the two $X$ registers. Its connected kernel $K_X$ therefore does also. The initial product replica vector is invariant under every such swap.

After applying the same rank-one measurement bra to both copies of $B$, a swap on $X\subseteq A\cup B$ becomes a swap on $A\cap X$. For a retained disjoint component family, the components intersecting $A$, together with uncovered sites of $A$, partition $A$. The resulting vector is invariant under their product, namely $\mathrm{Swap}_A$. This proves its annihilation by $P_A^-$.

The norm argument needs—and the revised text explicitly uses—the fully amplified word-norm majorants. An individual local term

$$-i(H_\alpha\otimes I+I\otimes H_\alpha)$$

has projective-norm operator bound $2\|H_\alpha\|$ even with arbitrary untouched spatial registers. Products of words consequently have the required product bounds. The restricted exterior is $U_{\rm restricted}\otimes U_{\rm restricted}$, with norm one. No unamplified-to-amplified norm equivalence is being assumed.

I also checked the rank-one measurement map. Its projective-to-$\ell^1$-Hilbert contraction follows directly from completeness and Cauchy–Schwarz. Partial swap is used only as a Hilbert-norm contraction, so no problematic projective-norm bound for partial swap is required.

2. Separation exponent and constants

With

$$m_R=\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{dist}(A,C)}{\ell_0}\right\rceil,$$

a connected order-$m$ union spanning $A,C$ necessarily has $m\ge m_R$. The rooted bound gives

$$A_{AC}
\le
\frac{|A|}{s}\sum_{m\ge m_R}\frac{q_H^m}{m}
\le
\frac{|A|q_H^{m_R}}{s(1-q_H)}.$$

Thus the stated bound is conservative. If $C$ is defined by a strict outside-radius condition, a floor-plus-one version can improve the exponent, but that improvement is unnecessary.

The conservative window gives $q_H\le e/12<1/4$. For $|A|\le s$, the displayed choice based on

$$m_R\ge\left\lceil\log_4(256M/\epsilon)\right\rceil$$

has adequate slack for the claimed per-update conditional-TV budget.

3. The conditional-TV step uses the right normalization

The pure-state argument establishes

$$\mathbb E_{b,c}\,
\mathrm{TV}\!\left(P(A\mid b,c),P(A\mid b)\right)
\le2\zeta_R.$$

This is an average under the actual joint law. It does not require a lower bound on the probability of $b$ or $(b,c)$.

The exact gate update must use the after-gate law, as the algorithm intends. If $P^-$ and $P^+$ are the laws before and after a gate on $A$, then

$$P^-_{A^c}=P^+_{A^c}.$$

Consequently, retaining the outside record and redrawing $A$ from $P^+(A\mid A^c)$ maps $P^-$ exactly to $P^+$. Replacing that conditional by the buffered approximation incurs the average error measured under $P^+_{A^c}$, precisely the measure appearing in the replica and joint-law bounds. Markov-kernel contraction then telescopes the errors without multiplying them by rare-prefix reciprocals.

Equation (6.7) is also correct, including when $Q_B(b)=0$:

$$\sum_bP_B(b)\,
\mathrm{TV}\!\left(P(A\mid b),Q(A\mid b)\right)
\le2\,\mathrm{TV}(P_{AB},Q_{AB}).$$

Assigning any conditional at a zero $Q_B$ does not invalidate the inequality.

4. Original schedule versus colored circuit time

Padding each segment’s coloring to the same fixed $c$ handles varying term lists. Every compiled Hamiltonian prefix has a pure-unitary realization of duration at most $cT$, with the same fixed locality bounds and the stated local-strength bound.

Independent on-site Pauli histories can be moved into a toggling frame. They change local Hamiltonian signs or output bases but do not introduce a propagation velocity proportional to their number. This is essential and is satisfied here.

For the MPS crossing parameter, summing the actual crossing rotation angles gives

$$\Chi=O_{\rm class}(JT\,R).$$

Coloring does not increase this sum by the number of numerical steps. Local Pauli gates contribute no crossing entanglement. The patch radius remains $O_{\rm class}(\log(M/\epsilon))$, including the second locality buffer and its patch-volume prefactor. Thus the accepted MPS dimension bound is polynomial in this short-time regime.

5. Finite-bit conditional contractions

The finite-bit claim is valid if exact contractions are implemented with ordinary common-denominator management or reduction.

For a rational MPS and rational local effects, a contraction is a finite sum of products of printed coefficients. A common denominator can be bounded by the product of the denominators of those coefficients, with the fixed bra/ket multiplicities. Its bit length is polynomial in the printed representation size. Summing the virtual-index paths adds at most a polynomial number of bits, even though the formal number of paths is exponential.

Therefore a nonzero $Q_B(b)$ has polynomial bit complexity even when its numerical value is exponentially small. Forming a conditional ratio costs polynomial bit work; it does not cost inverse probability.

The implementation need not enumerate the outcomes on the logarithmic-radius shield. It contracts the supplied observed record $b$. Only the outcomes on the current constant-size gate support are enumerated; their number is polynomial in the explicitly supplied POVM lists.

The exact rational LDL refinements, sampled product-mixture labels, bounded dyadic categorical draws, and positive rational MPS representation also avoid hidden postselection or successful-branch runtime assumptions.

Scope conclusion

Section 6 supplies a complete restricted operation:

- arbitrary rational schedules and specified product preparations;

- arbitrary finite rational single-site POVMs;

- native all-site depolarization;

- $\eta=0$;

- the explicit short-time window;

- unconditional TV accuracy and worst-case polynomial bit work.

The proof’s crucial resource is the ability to sample the entire Pauli history independently of the quantum state, after which every circuit prefix is pure and comes from the original product preparation. General GKLS records are state-dependent, and later slabs do not restart from a product state. Neither extension follows from this audit.
