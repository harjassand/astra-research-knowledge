Local observable comparison, summable mixing and spatial conormal media

1. A uniform-in-gap refinement of the completed homogeneous-wall argument

Let P_{s,t} be a nominal evolution preserving a probability pi. Suppose
 sup_x |P_{s,t}f(x)-pi f|<=2 b(t-s)||f||infinity,
where b is nonincreasing and integrable, and
 Lip(P_{s,s+a}f)<=G||f||infinity
for a in[ell/2,ell]. Suppose that, conditionally on EVERY full history,
an actual evolution over such a short interval can be compared with a
nominal evolution started at the same actual position so that
 E distance(Q_end,Y_end)<=e.
For a selected terminal contact observable suppose separately that its
short-interval expectation discrepancy is<=e_contact. These are local
hypotheses, not consequences of stationary marginals.

Partition a gap into equal lengths a in[ell/2,ell]. The exact telescoping
identity tests each local endpoint discrepancy against the remaining
nominal backward observable. For any term with at least two intervals
remaining, use smoothing on the FIRST of those intervals and mixing on
the rest. Subtracting the constant pi f before smoothing gives
 Lip(P_{t_j,t}f)<=2G b(t-t_j-a)||f||infinity.
The one-interval case costs G. Thus, without propagating a long strong
coupling,
 |E f(Q_t)-P_{0,t}f(Q_0)|
 <=e_contact+2G e[2+2 ell^-1 integral_0^infinity b(v)dv]
for |f|<=1. A slightly larger constant covers gaps shorter than2ell
by using the trivial unmixed envelope. For the completed spatially
homogeneous conormal model, e<=8delta sqrt(ell) and the contact term is
polynomial times delta^(2/9). Hence its perturbation error can be made
UNIFORM in the whole chemical-free gap, rather than proportional to
delta*g. This deduction uses the actual conditional short comparisons;
it is valid for history-adaptive perturbations and does not identify a
faulty stationary law. Complete marked-path accumulation still pays the
number of proposal events; this is not an infinite-horizon path-TV claim.

2. The genuine next transport operation

In an inhomogeneous medium the nominal species mobility is D_s(t,x).
The Ito drift div_x D_s is necessary if the intended diffusion generator
is div(D_s grad) with conormal walls. Omitting it changes the invariant
measure even in one dimension: dX=sqrt(2D(X))dW with ordinary reflection
has density proportional to1/D, whereas dX=D'(X)dt+sqrt(2D(X))dW has
uniform density. This is a model distinction, not a chosen control or
an observation of D.

For smooth symmetric uniformly elliptic D, the nominal form on H1(Omega)
is integral grad f.D grad g. It preserves uniform volume. Thus the exact
geometric local-reaction normalizer q and Palm balance from the completed
compiler are unchanged: mobility values do not enter chemical rates or
the required volume computation. This separates chemical balance from
the new stochastic boundary problem.

The common Poincare inequality still gives L2 mixing at rate c*d0.
Fixed-dimensional Nash/extension bounds for a convex domain in[-1,1]^3
with inball radius kappa have constants polynomial in kappa^-1. One can
see the geometric conditioning from the radial representation about c:
the radial function lies between kappa and2sqrt(3), and its Lipschitz
constant is bounded by C/kappa. A radial ball map and its inverse have
fixed polynomial Lipschitz constants. Pull back to the unit ball, use
its fixed extension, and push forward; Jacobian distortion and first
derivative distortion are fixed powers, not powers of the facet count.
This provides a finite-dimensional nominal Nash bound, not an oracle.

With coefficients between d0 I and I, the nominal L1-to-Linfinity norm
over a fixed positive interval is therefore bounded by a fixed power of
kappa^-1,d0^-1. Combining this initial smoothing with the Poincare
contraction gives an integrable nominal all-start mixing envelope with
polynomial constants. The forward and backward forms both preserve the
same measure; one need not assert that a time-ordered product of symmetric
operators is selfadjoint. A pointwise Feller/reflecting realization must
still be identified at corners before applying an all-start statement.

3. What the old metric proof does and does not transfer

Put z=x-y, M(t,x)=D(t,x)^-1 and E=z^T M(t,x)z. For a spatially constant
D the conormal boundary terms were separately nonpositive. With D(t,x)
they now contain controlled quadratic remainders. At an actual face of x,
 grad_x E .[-D(t,x)n]=-2z.n+O(P E),
where P is polynomial in the derivative bounds and d0^-1. The first term
is nonpositive by convexity. At a nominal face of y,
 grad_y E .[-D(t,y)n]=2z^T M(t,x)D(t,y)n
 =2z.n+O(P E),
again with a nonpositive leading term. The remainder is integrated against
the corresponding face clocks, not ordinary dt. Space derivatives of M
also produce Ito drift and quadratic-variation terms. A globally bounded
C3 envelope makes their algebraic constants polynomial. It does NOT by
itself bound exponential moments of the relevant wall clocks or supply
a short-interval all-corner perturbation theorem.

That is the new central gap: obtain a sufficiently quantitative local
comparison for every admissible correlated/history-adaptive realization,
or replace that approach by a weak-observable argument. Bounds derived
only for independent nominal particles do not prove it. Nor does ordinary
Girsanov compare path laws of different quadratic variations. Once an
appropriate local comparison and nominal smoothing are paid, the exact
telescoping estimate in1 pays long gaps without exp(P*g). Merely writing
the old constant-matrix inverse metric with x inserted is insufficient.

Likewise the old wall potential .5(x-c)^T D(t)^-1(x-c) used a matrix that
was independent of position. With D(t,x), its gradient has additional
terms; the negative wall coefficient cannot be assumed. The full actual
wall-length bound remains part of the compiler question, including
species changes, arbitrary boundary starts and redundant facets.

4. Governing primary contracts actually read

After completed local hold checks, ROOT read Jarni--Missaoui--Ouknine,
https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05807v1, Theorem2.1 and Theorem3.1/A1--A5.
They concern NORMAL reflection in time-dependent convex domains, a
uniformly interior path and the stated Lipschitz/integrability conditions.
The constant-in-space conormal transformation fits that representation;
a general spatial matrix is not removed by one linear coordinate map.
These results are not a polynomial all-corner oblique stability theorem.

ROOT also read Laasri--Mugnolo, exact
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mma.5978,
Theorem4.3, Section6.5 equations68--74/Lemma6.6 and Theorem6.11.
Their form, Nash/Gagliardo--Nirenberg and ellipticity assumptions supply
nominal evolution/heat-kernel interfaces, with H1 selecting Neumann
conditions. They do not supply the history-adaptive transport comparison
or actual-wall-work compiler. Constants and boundary realization still
have to be matched. No citation therein was followed to recover a held
source, and the previously inaccessible Tanaka DOI remains held.

The result in1 is an actual new summable-error deduction. The proposed
spatial-mobility compiler and the local boundary estimates in3 remain
unconstructed. No practical medium, energetic implementation or unknown
coefficient evaluation is assumed.
