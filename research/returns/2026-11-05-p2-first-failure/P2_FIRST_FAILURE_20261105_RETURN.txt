## 2. The theorem

Consider a connected undirected graph with at least two vertices and \(m\) edges. Parallel edges are allowed. Edge \(i\) switches independently according to

\[
\text{working}\xrightarrow{a_i}\text{failed},
\qquad
\text{failed}\xrightarrow{b_i}\text{working},
\qquad a_i,b_i>0.
\]

The rates are constant in time. Repairs continue until the first system failure.

Let \(B\) be the set of configurations whose working edges do not connect all vertices, and let

\[
\tau_B=\inf\{t\ge 0:X_t\in B\},
\qquad
M=\mathbb E_{\mathrm{all\ working}}\tau_B.
\]

Define

\[
\gamma_i=a_i+b_i,
\qquad
\Lambda=\sum_i\gamma_i,
\qquad
g=\frac{\min_i\gamma_i}{\Lambda}.
\]

### First-disconnection theorem

For positive rational input rates, the construction returns an interval \([L,U]\) satisfying

\[
\Pr\{L\le M\le U\}\ge 1-\delta,
\qquad
\frac UL\le 1+\varepsilon,
\]

with work polynomial in

\[
|V|,\quad m,\quad
\text{input bit length},\quad
g^{-1},\quad
\varepsilon^{-1},\quad
\log\delta^{-1}.
\]

Writing

\[
\kappa_\gamma
=\frac{\max_i(a_i+b_i)}{\min_i(a_i+b_i)},
\]

we have \(g^{-1}\le m\kappa_\gamma\). Thus the construction is fully polynomial when the refresh-rate contrast is polynomially bounded.

**There is no claim of polynomial dependence on \(\log\kappa_\gamma\).** An exponentially large refresh-rate contrast can make this algorithm expensive.

The predicted mean includes every repair, unsuccessful approach to failure, and physical holding time before the first disconnection. The construction does not replace that mean with a stationary failure probability or reciprocal failure frequency.

## 3. The operation that makes the construction possible

The useful operation is:

> **Generate a disconnected configuration from the correct conditional stationary distribution, run a short ordinary repair-and-failure trajectory, and count it only if it reconnects immediately and avoids disconnection for the remainder of the trajectory.**

Three things must be earned: the conditioned starting distribution, a sufficiently frequent useful outcome, and the connection to the all-working mean.

### 3.1 Obtain the rare starting distribution without waiting for failure

Uniformize the continuous-time process. At each discrete update:

1. Choose edge \(i\) with probability \(\gamma_i/\Lambda\).
2. Redraw it as failed with probability \(a_i/\gamma_i\).

Updates occur at total rate \(\Lambda\). If \(M_*\) is the expected number of updates to first failure, then exactly

\[
M=\frac{M_*}{\Lambda}.
\]

This is an exact representation, including updates that leave an edge unchanged.

Its stationary law \(\pi\) is a product distribution. Let

\[
p=\pi(B)
\]

be the stationary probability of disconnection.

Karger’s corrected all-terminal unreliability approximation algorithm supplies a relative estimate of \(p\), including when the independent edge probabilities differ. It is an essential existing ingredient. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

A conditional sampler for \(\pi(\cdot\mid B)\) follows by fixing edges sequentially. For each next edge, estimate the remaining disconnection probability under each of its two possible states. Choose between those states in proportion to their product-distribution probabilities multiplied by those conditional disconnection probabilities.

Forced working edges are contracted; forced failed edges are deleted. Consequently, each conditional query remains an all-terminal unreliability problem.

One sample uses at most \(2m\) static approximation calls. Their accuracy can be chosen so that the resulting sample has a specified total-variation error. The report accounts for both numerical error and failed randomized oracle calls.

This is a **computational preparation**. It does not require physically placing the network in a rare failed state.

### 3.2 Correct repeated crossings with a short experiment

Let \(P\) be the uniformized transition kernel. Start

\[
Y_0\sim\pi(\cdot\mid B),
\]

and simulate \(k+1\) updates. Define

\[
Z=
\mathbf 1\{Y_1,\ldots,Y_{k+1}\notin B\}.
\]

A successful trial leaves failure on its first update and stays safe thereafter.

Reversibility gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
p\,\mathbb E Z
=
\Pr_\pi(\tau_B=k+1).
}
\]

The right side is a first-entry probability from stationarity. The left side estimates it through a positive Bernoulli experiment. It does not subtract two nearly equal survival probabilities.

Reactive-flux corrections and spectral descriptions of first-passage behavior are established machinery. The contribution pursued here is their combination with an earned conditional sampler and explicit complexity and initialization bounds for this succinct network model. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### 3.3 Prove that the useful outcome is not exponentially rare

Let \(1-\lambda\) be the largest eigenvalue of the transition kernel killed upon entering \(B\). Product-chain structure gives spectral gap \(g\), and the proof establishes

\[
gp\le\lambda\le\frac{p}{1-p}.
\]

Let

\[
H_m=\sum_{j=1}^m\frac1j.
\]

For an internal accuracy parameter \(\xi\), take

\[
k=
\left\lceil
\frac{H_m+\log(1/\xi)+2}{g}
\right\rceil.
\]

In the rare branch, selected using a certified upper bound on \(p\), the spectral calculation gives

\[
(1-2\xi)\lambda
\le p\,\mathbb E Z
\le(1+\xi)\lambda
\]

and, crucially,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E Z\ge g(1-2\xi).}
\]

This lower bound is the reason the method avoids a lifetime-sized cost.

The stationary failure probability \(p\) can be exponentially small. The useful outcome in the conditioned short experiment still has inverse-polynomial probability when \(g^{-1}\) is polynomial.

Accordingly,

\[
O\!\left(
g^{-1}\xi^{-2}\log(1/\delta)
\right)
\]

trials suffice for relative estimation. Each trial has at most

\[
O\!\left(
g^{-1}\log(m/\xi)
\right)
\]

updates.

### 3.4 Account for the all-working initial state

A stationary first-passage estimate is insufficient on its own: the requested system starts with every component working.

Let \(C\) be the first update by which every component has been refreshed at least once. Conditional on the sequence of selected components, their last redraws are independent samples from the stationary component laws. Hence \(X_C\sim\pi\).

Monotone coupling and the Markov property then give

\[
M_\pi\le M_*\le M_\pi+\mathbb EC,
\qquad
\mathbb EC\le\frac{H_m}{g},
\]

where \(M_\pi\) is the stationary mean in update units. Combining this with the spectral representation yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac1\lambda-\frac1g
\le M_*
\le\frac1\lambda+\frac{H_m}{g}.
}
\]

The initialization correction is additive on the mixing scale. In the rare branch it is controlled relative to the lifetime.

The construction also closes the nonrare branch. If the rare gate fails, the certified lower bound on \(p\) is inverse polynomial. Ordinary simulation then has a polynomial mean bound; a second-moment bound and capped median-of-means estimator supply the required relative confidence interval.

There is no parameter region where the method merely assumes that a suitable plateau has appeared.

## 4. A second result: sharp propagation of local calibration error

The calibration theorem applies more broadly than network connectivity. It holds for **any nonempty monotone failure rule** on independent binary repairable components, provided the all-working state is safe.

Suppose every measured failure and repair rate has logarithmic error at most \(\eta\):

\[
\left|\log\frac{\widehat a_i}{a_i}\right|\le\eta,
\qquad
\left|\log\frac{\widehat b_i}{b_i}\right|\le\eta.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
e^{-(2m-1)\eta}M
\le\widehat M
\le e^{(2m-1)\eta}M.
}
\]

**The coefficient \(2m-1\) is optimal over this model class.**

There is also simultaneous deadline robustness. For

\[
F(t)=\Pr_{\mathrm{all\ working}}(\tau_B\le t),
\]

the same rate perturbation gives

\[
\boxed{
e^{-2m\eta}F(t)
\le\widehat F(t)
\le e^{2m\eta}F(t)
\qquad\text{for every }t>0.
}
\]

The deadline statement is a robustness theorem. It does not itself provide an efficient algorithm for computing the entire failure-time distribution.

### Why the component bound is significant

A single physical rate occurs in \(2^{m-1}\) configuration transitions. Applying a generic transition-by-transition bound can therefore introduce an exponential factor. The proof treats all transitions caused by one physical component together.

Represent component failures and repairs by independent Poisson marks. Fix component \(i\), and condition on all other trajectories and on its repair marks. Survival to time \(t\) then requires that its failure process avoid a determined collection of dangerous time intervals.

Thus

\[
S(t)=\mathbb E\!\left[\mathbf1_Ge^{-a_iL_t}\right],
\]

where \(S(t)=\Pr(\tau_B>t)\), and \(L_t\) is independent of \(a_i\).

Concavity of \(-x\log x\) gives

\[
-a_i\partial_{a_i}S(t)\le-S(t)\log S(t).
\]

Starting from all-working maximizes survival under monotone coupling, so

\[
S(t+s)\le S(t)S(s).
\]

This property has a classical maintained-system interpretation; the report also proves it directly for the present model. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

Integrating the entropy inequality produces

\[
-1\le\frac{\partial\log M}{\partial\log a_i}\le0,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\log M}{\partial\log b_i}\ge0.
\]

Scaling every rate by \(c\) divides the mean by \(c\). Therefore all logarithmic sensitivities sum to \(-1\), and

\[
\sum_i
\left|
\frac{\partial\log M}{\partial\log a_i}
\right|
+
\sum_i
\left|
\frac{\partial\log M}{\partial\log b_i}
\right|
\le2m-1.
\]

Integrating this inequality between the true and measured rates proves the finite perturbation theorem.

Generic single-transition first-passage sensitivity bounds and the scaling sum rule already appear in recent primary research. The additional argument here is the bound for an entire physical-component group. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Sharpness follows from \(m\) identical parallel edges. When failures are slow relative to repairs,

\[
M(a,b)\sim\frac{b^{m-1}}{m a^m}.
\]

Changing \(a\) to \(ae^{-\eta}\) and \(b\) to \(be^\eta\) changes the mean asymptotically by \(e^{(2m-1)\eta}\).

## 5. Acquisition, errors, and execution costs

The result separates **learning local rates** from **computing a rare global lifetime**. Their costs differ.

### Local measurements

For each local rate \(r\), observe \(N_{\rm cal}\) complete exponential dwell times and estimate

\[
\widehat r=\frac{N_{\rm cal}}{\sum_jY_j}.
\]

For \(0<\eta\le1\),

\[
\Pr\!\left(
\left|\log\frac{\widehat r}{r}\right|>\eta
\right)
\le2e^{-N_{\rm cal}\eta^2/3}.
\]

Therefore

\[
N_{\rm cal}
=
\left\lceil
3\eta^{-2}\log\frac{4m}{\delta_{\rm acq}}
\right\rceil
\]

suffices for simultaneous confidence across all \(2m\) rates.

With \(\eta=\Theta(\varepsilon/m)\), a sufficient total observation count is

\[
O\!\left(
m^3\varepsilon^{-2}\log(m/\delta_{\rm acq})
\right).
\]

Ordinary alternating working and failed periods supply these observations. Their expected aggregate component exposure is

\[
\boxed{
N_{\rm cal}\sum_{i=1}^m
\left(\frac1{a_i}+\frac1{b_i}\right).
}
\]

If every local rate is at least \(r_->0\), parallel monitoring gives high-probability elapsed observation time

\[
O\!\left(
\frac{N_{\rm cal}+\log(m/\delta)}{r_-}
\right).
\]

These expressions expose a material limitation: **a rare local transition remains expensive to learn by passive observation.** The computational theorem removes the cost of a globally rare combination of local events; it does not make an individually unobservable rate observable.

The measurement premise includes component-state access and usable event timing. Initial preparation, missed events, state misclassification, timestamp uncertainty, and readout cost must be charged in an experimental implementation. Their errors must enter the rate intervals. No physical measurements were performed in this run.

In the rare branch, the number of simulated updates is

## 6. Why existing failure-frequency methods do not already supply the answer

A strong existing result matters here: Heidarzadeh, Sprintson, and Singh already give polynomial approximation of stationary all-terminal failure frequency. Ignoring that work would exaggerate the gap. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

The relevant distinction is that stationary failure crossings can arrive in clusters. Their frequency need not equal the reciprocal of the first-failure mean from all-working.

A two-edge example demonstrates this without a large state space. Take parallel edges with

\[
a_1=a,\quad b_1=1,
\qquad
a_2=b_2=1.
\]

The stationary disconnection probability and failure-crossing frequency are

\[
p=\frac{a}{2(1+a)},
\qquad
\nu=\frac{a}{1+a}.
\]

The actual all-working mean is

\[
M=\frac{a^2+3a+4}{a(a+3)}.
\]

Consequently,

\[
M\nu\longrightarrow\frac43
\qquad(a\to0).
\]

The stationary failure probability becomes arbitrarily small, and the refresh-rate contrast stays bounded. Nevertheless, reciprocal failure frequency retains an order-one error.

The short no-return experiment supplies the correction for these clustered crossings.

8. What this establishes—and where the original mission remains open

Within the declared model, the construction establishes a useful separation:

A global failure can be exponentially rare while all local events occur on ordinary time scales.
Its mean first-arrival time can be estimated without enumerating configurations or waiting for that global event.
The required dynamic correction can be obtained from static conditional sampling and short ordinary trajectories.
Local rate uncertainty can be propagated with an optimal coefficient linear in the number of physical components.

The assumptions exclude common-cause jumps, load-dependent cascades, shared repair crews, non-Markov ageing, unknown topology, and hidden physical failure variables. The computational theorem also does not automatically extend to arbitrary failure predicates or arbitrary terminal-connectivity questions.
