4. A tempting affine-moment certificate collapses under translation averaging

The lift is useful compactness; its linear moment constraints alone do not
automatically pay the sharp energy. For example, consider a proposed sufficient
pointwise inequality on (4) of the form
  Q_0(A Fcal)>=phi(theta)+psi(y)+r f(theta,y)
                    +S(theta,y):Z+c h,
where S is symmetric, div div S=0 and c is constant. Its integral is controlled
by (2): the S term integrates to zero, the h term integrates to zero, the
coordinate marginals pay phi,psi, and r pays f against lambda. A sufficient
certificate would require that total known integral to be >=Q_0(A).

However, the left side and admissible state set are independent of theta,y.
Average the proposed pointwise inequality over base translations. It remains
valid and all coefficient functions become constants. By (1), its right side
is then an ordinary affine function of the entries of Fcal. Equality of the
known integral with Q_0(A) is forced by testing Fcal=I. Thus this whole affine
linear-moment certificate class reduces to an affine supporting inequality for
Q_0(A F) on det F=1,F11>=0. It does not exploit nonlinear spatial compatibility.

This is a limitation of THAT certificate class, not a proof that a certificate
cannot exist, and certainly not an original negative. A more powerful use of
the compact lift would need additional nonlinear information, a different
energy mechanism or actual compatible constructions. A negative law satisfying
only (2)--(4) would not be an admitted original counterexample.
