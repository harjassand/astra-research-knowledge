1. Exact theorem

Assumptions

Fix a finite commuting stabilizer Hamiltonian and a fixed, circuit-independent decoder $\mathcal D$. During HOLD, let

$$\Phi_t=e^{t\mathcal L}$$

be evolution under the actual stipulated Pauli-Davies bath.

The resolved bath jumps are the individual one-site Pauli couplings with their actual frequency projectors and detailed-balance rates. The argument uses the accepted exact syndrome/decoder reduction. It does not automatically cover arbitrary coherent mixtures of different bath couplings.

Let:

- $s$ denote the stabilizer syndrome;

- $Q$ be its actual classical Markov generator;

- $\pi(s)$ be its stationary Gibbs distribution;

- $h(s)$ be the total rate of physical jumps that induce a nonidentity dressed logical Pauli under the fixed decoder;

- $\lambda_N=\sum_s\pi(s)h(s)$ be the equilibrium logical-error hazard.

Here $h$ measures logical risk, rather than energy, defect count or total bath activity.

For a released physical state $\Omega$, let $\mu_\Omega(s)$ be its syndrome probabilities. Define

$$\delta_M(\mu\mid\pi)
=
\sum_s[\mu(s)-M\pi(s)]_+,
\qquad M\ge0.$$

At $M=1$,

$$\delta_1(\mu\mid\pi)=\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\pi).$$

Retention bound

For every physical release $\Omega$, every untouched reference $R$, and every $t\ge0$,

$$\boxed{
\left\|
(\mathcal D\Phi_t\otimes\operatorname{id}_R)(\Omega)
-
(\mathcal D\otimes\operatorname{id}_R)(\Omega)
\right\|_1
\le
2\delta_M(\mu_\Omega\mid\pi)
+
2Mt\lambda_N.
}$$

There is no product assumption between the logical state, syndrome and reference.

The operational channel version is as follows. Let $\mathcal W$ be an actual noisy writer or native gate operation, with desired logical channel $\mathcal U$. Suppose

$$\|\mathcal D\mathcal W-\mathcal U\|_\diamond\le e_W$$

and, uniformly over all logical inputs,

$$\delta_M(\mu_{\mathcal W(\rho)}\mid\pi)\le\delta.$$

Then

$$\boxed{
\|\mathcal D\Phi_t\mathcal W-\mathcal U\|_\diamond
\le
e_W+2\delta+2Mt\lambda_N.
}$$

These are full trace and diamond norms, without a factor $1/2$. The uniform input requirement is substantive: a release estimate proved only for one known state does not establish this guarantee for an unknown reference-entangled input.

2. Why the exceptional mass is paid only once

The proof uses the accepted complete-channel hazard inequality

$$\text{decoded drift}
\le
2\int_0^t [\mu e^{uQ}](h)\,du,$$

together with a positive decomposition.

After virtual syndrome pinching, write the state as positive, unnormalized logical/reference blocks $\Omega_s$. This pinching is an analytical step: under the accepted Pauli-Davies intertwining, it changes neither decoded endpoint.

For each block, set

$$a_s=\min\left\{1,\frac{M\pi(s)}{\mu(s)}\right\},$$

and split

$$\Omega_{\mathrm{good},s}=a_s\Omega_s,
\qquad
\Omega_{\mathrm{bad},s}=(1-a_s)\Omega_s.$$

The good marginal satisfies

$$\mu_{\mathrm{good}}\le M\pi.$$

Positivity of the actual Markov semigroup and stationarity of $\pi$ imply

$$\mu_{\mathrm{good}}e^{uQ}\le M\pi$$

for every later time. Its integrated logical hazard therefore costs at most $2Mt\lambda_N$.

The bad part has total trace $\delta_M$. Its initial and final decoded states are positive operators of that same trace, so their trace distance is at most $2\delta_M$, however long the HOLD lasts.

Adding the two bounds proves the theorem. The decomposition need not be a physical operation or a linear decomposition of the writer channel. It is a pointwise proof device, followed by the uniform supremum defining the diamond guarantee.

This credits established positive-operator splitting, Markov domination and contractivity. I am claiming this deduction within the present effort, not historical priority for those methods.

3. The consequential improvement

The simplest specialization is

$$\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\pi)\le\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\text{HOLD drift}\le2\delta+2t\lambda_N.}$$

The marginal error does not need to be reduced to order $\epsilon/T$ merely because the desired HOLD lasts $T$. Order-$\epsilon$ syndrome accuracy can suffice, provided the initial logical state is correct and the equilibrium hazard is sufficiently small.

The theorem also separates two conditions that had previously been easy to conflate:

- The syndrome marginal must satisfy the stated bound.

- The decoded logical state must already be correct.

A state can have the exact Gibbs syndrome distribution while its logical qubit is completely erased. Conversely, a logically correct release need not be close in full physical trace norm to a product thermal encoding.

For several native blocks, arbitrary initial entanglement is allowed. Separate marginal estimates give

$$\text{joint decoded drift}
\le
2\sum_j\delta_j
+
2\sum_j M_jt_j\lambda_{N,j}.$$

There is no requirement to dominate their joint syndrome distribution by a product Gibbs law.

4. A precise bridge through a safe transient

The theorem also gives an exact formulation of what a useful preparation-to-retention transition would need to accomplish.

Let a real release have syndrome law $\mu_0$ and immediate decoded error $e_0$. For an initial passive interval $t_0$, define

$$B(t_0)
=
\int_0^{t_0}[\mu_0e^{uQ}](h)\,du,$$

and

$$\delta
=
\delta_M(\mu_0e^{t_0Q}\mid\pi).$$

A subsequent HOLD of duration $t$ obeys

$$\boxed{
\text{total decoded error}
\le
e_0+2B(t_0)+2\delta+2Mt\lambda_N.
}$$

Thus a logically safe polynomial-time transient, followed by only fixed-accuracy syndrome convergence, would be sufficient. This is a weaker requirement than exponentially precise whole-state equilibration.

But neither of the necessary estimates has been acquired for the full noisy release class:

- the logical hazard accumulated during the transient;

The accepted Gibbs-$L_2$ relaxation inequality does not automatically remove a large initial density norm or prove logical safety while relaxation occurs.

5. A rigorous limitation: constant-density faults do not automatically satisfy the theorem

The main hypothesis is not harmless bookkeeping.

Consider independent spins with Gibbs syndrome law

$$\pi=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q)^{\otimes N},
\qquad 0<q<\tfrac12.$$

Apply independent $X$ faults of a fixed probability $p\in(0,\tfrac12)$. This is an allowed $p$-bounded local fault pulse. The resulting distribution is

$$\mu=\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q')^{\otimes N},
\qquad
q'=q+p(1-2q)>q.$$

Taking $a=(q+q')/2$, elementary Chernoff bounds give

$$\boxed{
\delta_M(\mu\mid\pi)
\ge
1-e^{-ND(a\|q')}
-
M e^{-ND(a\|q)},
}$$

where $D$ is binary relative entropy.

Consequently, whenever $\log M=o(N)$,

$$\delta_M(\mu\mid\pi)\longrightarrow1.$$

This is an extensive mismatch on typical states. Removing a tiny exceptional set cannot repair it.

This limits the global syndrome-comparison method; it does not obstruct the full processor. Such spins could be irrelevant spectators appended to a functioning memory. Their poor global Gibbs comparison would not destroy that memory’s logical protection. A useful reduced description must therefore justify which syndrome information matters through the actual dynamics, rather than simply discard inconvenient coordinates.
