# Value and limits

## Candid assessment

This clears a useful-theory-capital threshold, but not a breakthrough threshold.

The result is more than a positive numerical test or another restricted ansatz: it admits every compatible perturbation with sufficiently small original-gradient supremum at the original family's degenerate full-rank means, for every r>0. The proof pays the lack of an ordinary H1 gap with an anisotropic finite-exponent estimate. Independent review also reconstructs the exact acoustic degeneracy classification.

The reusable contact-line reserve broadens the analytic operation beyond globally positive quartics. Its concrete application to G at I is not conditional on a full cone classification.

## Increment over the strongest prior

N806 already supplied the algebraic Type-II structure, Hodge correction and mixed L2/L4 compensation. The increment is a different finite exponent choice exploiting small original-gradient amplitude, followed by absorption; the reserve addendum then uses the same control to pay a quartic transverse loss.

N880 already supplies arbitrary-amplitude quasiconvexity at singular means for original J_r, plus a background cone. The new contribution is the remaining full-rank degenerate contact at small amplitude. Neither claim subsumes all the other's quantifiers.

The ordinary strictly elliptic means use classical reasoning. They are not the new part.

A still newer primary preprint, arXiv:2609.28311v2, states global Burkholder quasiconvexity through a convex-gradient projection. Its displayed theorem and proof were read during packaging. No transfer to J_r was established and no independent audit of that preprint is included. Its existence further limits any claim of field-level novelty or superiority.

## How it can be used

- Rule out a negative Jensen gap of arbitrarily small gradient amplitude at any fixed mean of original J_r.
- Reuse the contact-line lemma when a new quartic has the explicit Type-II normal form and a positive contact-line value.
- Separate a genuine compatible-gradient estimate from a weaker determinant-balanced moment calculation.
- Focus future global work on finite-amplitude or moving-background effects rather than an unproved ordinary H1 gap.

An unevaluated radius does not furnish a numerical search threshold. No claim is made that all small-amplitude searches can be pruned by an implemented bound.

## Why it is not a global answer

At a fixed mean, arbitrary-amplitude quasiconvexity requires the sharp integrated discriminant

    (int C(Dphi))² <=4(int Q(Dphi))(int F(Dphi))

for all compatible tests. The current estimates do not prove the required sharp constant. A mean-dependent local radius also does not control a sequence whose mean moves while its perturbation shrinks.

A separate positive-cone exclusion and a separate heat/exact-form obstruction concern proof methods only. Neither is a negative integrated Jensen test.

## Review strength and remaining uncertainty

Three distinct source-bound analytic challenges found no mathematical blocker in their stated scopes. They are coordinated internal research reviews, not external peer review or proof-assistant certificates. The complete general N806 classification remains an imported hypothesis. Historical novelty was not established. There is no practical or experimental claim.

For these reasons the appropriate delivery is a carefully bounded reusable proof package, not a claim to solve Morrey's problem.
