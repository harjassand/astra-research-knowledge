## 3. The full generator cone is the correct one

I independently differentiated Q=a s^2+b s d+c d^2 in a rank-one direction. With a generic matrix A=[[x,y],[z,w]], the acoustic form is

    12a x^2 +6b xw +(4a+2c) w^2
      +4a(y^2+z^2)-2b yz.

Its two independent 2-by-2 blocks are positive semidefinite exactly under the candidate's conditions: a>0, |b|<=4a and c>=-2a+3b^2/(8a), or a=b=0 and c>=0. Proper left/right rotations reduce every nonzero rank-one direction to a positive multiple of E11. Thus the boundary decomposition with t=b/(4a) and a nonnegative det^2 remainder is correct, including the pure det^2 ray.

Invertible left/right transformations preserve quasiconvexity when it holds for the generator. For a test phi on a domain Omega, write x=Ry and use psi(y)=L phi(Ry). Its gradient is L Dphi(Ry) R and the Jacobian contributes |det R|; orientation reversal does not change the argument. Positive sums preserve the inequality, and coefficient convergence passes through each fixed bounded-gradient test integral. This checks the interpretation of the cone, conditional on the primary positive theorem.

## 4. Compactification really includes unbounded condition numbers

Normalize both L and R to Frobenius norm one. Homogeneity absorbs ||L||^4 ||R||^4 into a nonnegative W_t coefficient. Every transformed det^2 term becomes a nonnegative multiple of det(F)^2. The normalized parameter space, now including singular matrices, is compact.

For F=xy^T with unit circle x,y, the determinant is zero and the atom's value is |Lx|^4 |R^T y|^4. Its averaged value is at least 1/16: each factor has mean at least 1/4 because E|Lx|^2=1/2 and Jensen applies. The stated fourth-moment formula gives the same bound and remains valid for positive semidefinite, possibly singular L^T L.

Therefore Lambda(f_n) bounds the total positive W_t mass by 16 Lambda(f_n), even if some transformed polynomials take negative values elsewhere. The nonnegative det^2 coefficient contributes zero to Lambda. This is the essential coercive normalization; mere compactness of normalized transforms would not by itself control diverging weights.

At F=I, ||LR||_F<=1 and |det(LR)|<=1/4. Since |6t^2-2|<=4, the bound |W_t(LR)|<=1+1+1/4=9/4 is valid. Hence the separate nonnegative det^2 coefficient is bounded by |f_n(I)|+(9/4) times the already bounded mass. It cannot diverge while being concealed by cancellation.

A bounded sequence of finite positive measures on this compact parameter space has a weakly convergent subsequence. Quartic coefficients of the integrands are continuous and uniformly bounded there, so the limit measure gives an exact coefficientwise representation, together with a finite nonnegative det^2 coefficient. This justifies closure, including transforms with condition numbers tending to infinity. No lower singular-value bound is being smuggled into the statement.

## 5. Tangential acoustic rigidity

Let det A!=0, rank H=1, cof(A):H=0. Along this rank-one line, det(A+zH) is constant. Invertible L,R retain all three properties after transformation. Proper rotations put H'=LHR into sigma E11 with sigma>0. The correspondingly rotated A'=[[x,y],[z,w]] satisfies w=0 and yz!=0.

Quartic polarization is valid: D^2 W_t(A')[H',H']=D^2 W_t(H')[A',A']. The displayed E11 formula is correct. Restoring the implicit scale gives

    D^2 W_t(A')[H',H']
      =sigma^2 {12(x+t w)^2
          +4[(y-tz)^2+(1-t^2)z^2]}.

With w=0, z!=0 and |t|<1 this is strictly positive. The positive sigma^2 factor was implicit in the source's comparison with E11; it does not alter its conclusion.

At |t|=1, W_t is the square of a nonnegative quadratic form and is convex. If either normalized transform is singular, its determinant vanishes identically and the atom reduces to |LFR|^4, also convex. All limiting atoms remain rank-one convex, so their acoustic contributions are nonnegative. The det^2 ray contributes 2(cof(A):H)^2=0 at this contact.

Thus a zero acoustic value for the positive integral forces almost every participating atom to have zero acoustic value. Every such atom is among the convex cases just identified. Its integral is a convex homogeneous quartic C0, and the complete polynomial is C0+nu det^2 with nu>=0. Taking g(F,z)=C0(F)+nu z^2 proves polyconvexity. Positivity of the measure, full rank of A and the constant-determinant contact are all essential hypotheses.

## 6. Independent expansion of the original density

Set k=(2r+1)/3, so r=(3k-1)/2 and k>1/3. With B=(3k-1)/[8(k+1)] and F0=(5k+1)/[2(k+1)], direct substitution gives

    alpha=B, alpha+beta=B k^2,
    r-4alpha=4Bk, 4alpha+1=F0.

These prove the candidate's complete reparameterization without changing the density. At A=[[0,k],[1,0]], H=E11, det A=-k and cof(A):H=0. Along A+aH, u=1+a^2, v=k^2, j=-k. The coefficient of a^2 is 2Bk^2+2Bk^2-4Bk^2=0. The acoustic contact is exact for every r>0.

### 6.1 Why a polyconvex support must exist

For a proper convex extended-real g representing a finite polynomial on the determinant graph, g is finite everywhere. Indeed, the graph has convex hull R^5: choose R with det R=z-det A, and average (A+R,det(A+R)) and (A-R,det(A-R)). The average is (A,z). A finite convex function on all R^5 has a supporting affine functional at every point.

If (P,lambda) is a supporting subgradient at (A,det A), differentiability of J_r along the graph gives DJ_r(A)=P+lambda cof(A). Expanding the determinant then yields the necessary inequality

    J_r(A+X)-J_r(A)-DJ_r(A):X-lambda det X >=0

for all X. This is the correct support polynomial; no assumption that the representing convex function is smooth is required.

### 6.2 Unique multiplier and null direction

The Hessian of the support polynomial must be positive semidefinite. Its E11 diagonal entry is zero, so its E11 row must vanish. The a,d entry of the original Hessian is

    4Bk(1-k)-2F0 k = -3k(k+1)/2.

Since the a,d entry of the determinant Hessian is 1, this fixes lambda=-3k(k+1)/2. The b,c block then becomes

    [[(3k+1)^2/2, k(3k+1)],
     [k(3k+1),   2k^2]].

I checked the entries directly by restricting to a=d=0 and expanding

    J_r(b,c)=B b^4+4B b^3c+(2B+F0)b^2c^2
                -4Bk bc^3+Bk^2 c^4.

The corrected block has null vector (h,1), h=-2k/(3k+1). Therefore V=[[0,h],[1,0]] is a zero quadratic direction for the support polynomial.

### 6.3 The cubic term is nonzero for all r>0

Along b=k+t h, c=1+t, the cubic coefficient of J_r is

    [(3k-1)h^3+(9k+1)h^2+4kh]/2
      =2k^2(3k-1)(k+1)/(3k+1)^3.

This is strictly positive for every k>1/3. Subtracting the affine tangent and lambda det X changes no cubic coefficient. Constant, linear and quadratic terms vanish, leaving exactly

    P(tV)=C3 t^3+J_r(V)t^4.

For sufficiently small negative t it is negative, regardless of the sign or size of the finite quartic coefficient. This contradicts the support inequality and proves non-polyconvexity for every r>0. The r=0 boundary is deliberately excluded: C3 vanishes there and the original density reduces to det^2, which is polyconvex. No uniform lower bound as r tends to zero is claimed.

V is generally full rank. A negative support polynomial in this direction is not a compatible gradient oscillation and is not a quasiconvex Jensen gap. The proof must not be rephrased as a rank-one or quasiconvex counterexample.
