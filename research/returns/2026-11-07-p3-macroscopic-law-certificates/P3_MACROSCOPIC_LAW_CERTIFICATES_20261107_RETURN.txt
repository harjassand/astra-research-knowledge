## 3. The complete certificate theorem

Let \(\mathcal S(P)\) contain **all translation-invariant stationary states**, including coexistence and mixtures, and write
\[
\beta_P(O)=\max_{\omega\in\mathcal S(P)}\omega(O).
\]

The central result is
\[
\boxed{
\beta_P(O)<b
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists\,G,H_1,\ldots,H_d,\ m>0:
\quad
bI-O+G-PG+\sum_j(H_j-\tau_jH_j)\succeq mI .

\]

Every operator in this inequality has finite support.

The roles of the terms are concrete. \(G-PG\) accounts for a finite transient contribution. The spatial differences \(H_j-\tau_jH_j\) cancel when summed around a periodic system. The remaining positive operator proves the bound.

For complex-rational input data and rational \(b\), the certificate can be chosen complex-rational. Consequently, there is a finite-search procedure that eventually returns a rigorously checkable certificate **whenever the strict inequality is true**. Exact boundary equality is a different problem: a nonnegative certificate is sound there, but finite discovery is not promised.

### Why the converse holds

The converse is the central difficulty.

Take increasingly large windows. In each window, optimize the expectation of \(O\) over positive, normalized density matrices satisfying every translation-consistency and stationarity equation whose supports fit. These are finite SDPs; in the classical case they are rational LPs.

The resulting upper bounds decrease. Individual feasible local marginals need not extend to a genuine infinite state, so they are only outer approximations.

Suppose their limiting bound remained above the true stationary maximum. Compactness permits a subsequence whose marginals converge on every fixed region. The limits are mutually consistent and satisfy every fixed local stationarity and translation equation. They therefore define a genuine state in \(\mathcal S(P)\), contradicting the supposed gap.

Thus the hierarchy converges to the exact stationary maximum. If \(b\) is strictly larger, some finite window already proves it. Finite-dimensional duality converts that finite-window bound into the displayed operator certificate. The strict gap permits rational approximation while preserving positivity.

This proves completeness without assuming a full-rank stationary state, a spectral gap or an attained exact dual optimum.
