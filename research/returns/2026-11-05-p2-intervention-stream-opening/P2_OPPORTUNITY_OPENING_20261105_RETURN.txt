## 2. Constructed opening: compose the entire experiment in relative entropy

Here is the central calculation.

Let \(P\) be the actual law of \((p,z,R)\), and define the local replacement operation

\[
CP=G_\sigma\otimes P_{z,R}.
\]

Write

\[
J(P)=D(P\Vert CP).
\]

For any actual law \(P\) and comparison law \(Q\), the relative-entropy chain rule gives

\[
\boxed{
D(P\Vert CQ)
=
J(P)+D(P_{z,R}\Vert Q_{z,R}).
}
\]

Consequently,

\[
D(P\Vert CQ)\le J(P)+D(P\Vert Q).
\tag{1}
\]

This identity is useful precisely because the actual and ideal hidden marginals may differ.

Suppose both campaigns first undergo the same declared waiting policy. At the next trial, the ideal campaign inserts \(C\); both then undergo the same exact marking and subsequent physical experiment. Data processing and (1) give

\[
D(P_j\Vert Q_j)
\le
D(P_{j-1}\Vert Q_{j-1})+\mathcal J_j,
\]

where \(\mathcal J_j\) is the actual campaign’s average local replacement entropy at that trial.

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{
D(P_{\mathrm{campaign}}\Vert Q_{\mathrm{campaign}})
\le \sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal J_j.
}
\tag{2}
\]

This controls a genuine sequential ideal-intervention campaign. It is stronger than a bound on an average contrast evaluated along the actual history.

The endpoint includes all declared retained records and the current hidden state. It does not include a fictitiously retained, infinitely precise microscopic trajectory.

### Why this helps

The preserved one-shot reasoning can upper-bound every trial by a budget that grows with the accumulated records. Applying that bound repeatedly charges much of the same information repeatedly.

Equation (2) permits a different accounting: **charge the local entropy remaining when the trial is actually launched, then relate those charges to entropy dissipated across the campaign.**

## 3. Exponential waiting makes the entropy charges telescope

Consider one passive waiting period, conditional on the retained history. Let

\[
D(t)=D(\rho_{S_tR}\Vert\pi_S\otimes\rho_R),
\qquad B=D(0).
\]

The accepted theorem supplies

\[
-D'(t)\ge 2\gamma J(t).
\tag{3}
\]

Use an independent exponential trigger \(T\sim\operatorname{Exp}(r)\). Retaining its time in the mathematical comparison, the average pre-intervention entropy is

\[
B^-=\int_0^\infty r e^{-rt}D(t)\,dt.
\]

Integration by parts yields

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J
&=\int_0^\infty r e^{-rt}J(t)\,dt\\
&\le \frac r{2\gamma}
\int_0^\infty e^{-rt}[-D'(t)]\,dt\\
&=\frac r{2\gamma}(B-B^-).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
\]

The preparation defect is charged to the entropy lost during that wait.

Suppose the marking, retained reports and controlled experiment following round \(j\) inject at most \(c_j\) relative-entropy units:

\[
B_{j+1}\le B_j^-+c_j.
\tag{5}
\]

For example, the preserved ledger permits a justified control-action charge together with a finite-record charge,

\[
c_j\le A_j+b_j\log 2,
\]

provided the physical action, measurement effects and fixed or padded endpoint conditions actually satisfy that ledger.

Define

\[
\mathcal E_n=D_0+\sum_{j=1}^n c_j.
\]

Summing (4) and using (5) gives

\[
\sum_{j=1}^n\mathcal J_j
\le \frac{r\mathcal E_n}{2\gamma}.
\]

Combining with (2),

\[
\boxed{
D(P_{\mathrm{campaign}}\Vert Q_{\mathrm{campaign}})
\le \frac{r\mathcal E_n}{2\gamma},
}
\tag{6}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}(P_{\mathrm{campaign}},Q_{\mathrm{campaign}})
\le
\sqrt{\frac{r\mathcal E_n}{4\gamma}}.
}
\tag{7}
\]

These statements average over actual histories. They do not assert a uniform entropy or accuracy bound after every rare posterior history.

### Finite deadlines and heralded timeout

The construction does not need an unbounded clock.

Truncate each exponential wait at \(H\), reporting timeout as an explicit abort branch. Then

\[
B^-=
\int_0^H re^{-rt}D(t)\,dt
+e^{-rH}D(H),
\]

and the same integration-by-parts identity applies, with replacement performed only on a successful trigger.

Taking

\[
H=\frac{\log(n/\alpha)}r
\]

gives total abort probability at most \(\alpha\). Expected passive occupancy is at most \(n/r\), and maximum passive occupancy is at most \(nH\). These are unconditional guarantees with the abort branch retained.

The resulting sufficient expected waiting time is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E T_{\rm passive}
\le
\frac{n\mathcal E_n}
{4\gamma\epsilon_{\rm th}^{\,2}}.
}
\tag{8}
\]

If \(c_j\le c\),

\[
\mathbb E T_{\rm passive}
=
O\!\left(
\frac{nD_0+cn^2}{\gamma\epsilon_{\rm th}^{\,2}}

\tag{9}
\]

The maximum-time guarantee carries the additional \(\log(n/\alpha)\) factor.

## 5. An exact physical family saturates the one-shot entropy bound

The inverse-square accuracy dependence is not merely an artifact of an arbitrary integrable function \(J(t)\).

Consider the harmonic Langevin system

\[
dx_t=\frac{p_t}{M}\,dt,
\qquad
dp_t=-kx_t\,dt-\gamma p_t\,dt
+\sqrt{2\gamma\sigma^2}\,dW_t,
\qquad
\sigma^2=\frac M\beta.
\]

Choose \(0<\lambda\le\gamma/2\) and

\[
k=M\lambda(\gamma-\lambda).
\]

Let \(R=\pm1\) be a fair old reference bit. Conditional on \(R\), initialize the exact Gibbs covariance, displaced by the mean

\[
(x_0,p_0)=R(d,-M\lambda d),
\qquad
d^2=\frac{2D_0}{\beta M\lambda\gamma}.
\]

The covariance remains Gibbs. The mean is a slow eigenmode and decays as \(e^{-\lambda t}\). Direct calculation gives

\[
D(\rho_{S,R,t}\Vert\pi_S\otimes\rho_R)
=D_0e^{-2\lambda t},
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
J(t)=\frac{D_0\lambda}{\gamma}e^{-2\lambda t}.
}
\tag{12}
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty J(t)\,dt=\frac{D_0}{2\gamma},
}
\tag{13}
\]

exactly saturating the accepted integrated bound.

For any independent trigger with \(\mathbb ET\le\tau\), the exact replacement TV is

\[
\delta
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
2\Phi\!\left(
\frac12\sqrt{\frac{2D_0\lambda}{\gamma}}\,
e^{-\lambda T}
\right)-1
\right].
\]

For \(\tau\ge1/\gamma\), choose

\[
\lambda=
\min\left\{\frac1{2\tau},\frac{\gamma}{2D_0}\right\}.
\]

Using Jensen’s inequality and a lower bound on the Gaussian density,

\[
\boxed{
\delta\ge
0.2135\,
\min\left\{1,\sqrt{\frac{D_0}{\gamma\tau}}\right\}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

Thus the one-shot \(D_0/(\gamma\epsilon^2)\) waiting dependence is sharp up to constants **for independent timing and state-preserving marking that retains every trial**.

I checked the slow-eigenmode, invariant-covariance and entropy identities symbolically.

### The physical qualification matters

As \(\lambda\to0\), the spring becomes softer and both the thermal position width and preparation displacement grow as \(\lambda^{-1/2}\). The excess preparation energy remains \(D_0/\beta\).

This is a finite physical family with a growing range requirement. It is not a lower bound for an apparatus with a fixed minimum stiffness and fixed spatial range.

## 6. A harder opening: hide the slow force from local observation

The preceding example is not sufficient against general instruments. Its reference bit can reveal which displaced Gaussian is present, allowing calibrated rejection.

I therefore constructed a variant in which the relevant sign is an **unobserved conserved sector** \(Z=\pm1\):

\[
H_Z(x,p)
=
\frac{p^2}{2M}
+\frac k2(x-Zd)^2.
\tag{15}
\]

The equilibrium law still has the exact momentum factorization required by the accepted theorem. Conditional equilibrium positions are centered at \(Zd\).

Initialize

\[
x_0\mid Z\sim N\!\left(0,\frac1{\beta k}\right),
\qquad
p_0\mid Z\sim N(ZM\lambda d,\sigma^2),
\]

independently conditional on \(Z\), with the same \(k,d\) as above.

Now the initial position distribution is identical in the two sectors. The conditional means evolve as

\[
\mathbb E[x_t\mid Z]=Zd(1-e^{-\lambda t}),
\qquad
\mathbb E[p_t\mid Z]=ZM\lambda d\,e^{-\lambda t}.
\]

The same exact local entropy law (12) holds, now with \(Z\) included in the hidden state.

There is also an exact acquisition calculation. Let \(\mathsf P_+^t,\mathsf P_-^t\) denote the laws of the entire continuously observed local path \((x_s,p_s)_{0\le s\le t}\) in the two sectors. Their momentum drifts differ by \(2kd\). Girsanov’s formula gives

\[
\boxed{
D(\mathsf P_+^t\Vert\mathsf P_-^t)
=
\frac{4D_0\lambda}{\gamma}
+
2D_0\lambda
\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\gamma}\right)^2t.
}
\tag{16}
\]

Any finite noisy local readout has no more distinguishability than this complete-path observation.

This supplies a concrete mechanism for a difficult instance:

- the local momentum’s sector-dependent shift is \(O(\sqrt{\lambda})\);
- the hidden influence persists for \(O(1/\lambda)\);
- information about its sign arrives at rate \(O(\lambda)\).

The important unresolved step is exact:

> **Does delivering the required intervention force a protocol to acquire enough of this hidden information, or can a physical intervention bypass learning it?**

Equation (16) is an inference bound. **It is not automatically an intervention lower bound.** A successful direct construction could remove the unwanted dependence without identifying \(Z\), and the selected question must allow that possibility.

The conserved-sector idealization and the growing position range also remain explicit. A finite-barrier realization must pay for its barrier and bound sector changes over the execution horizon.

## 7. Existing methods give serious challenges to optimality

### Ordinary OU sampling already has nearly linear cost on an easy family

For stationary OU momentum sampled at interval \(\Delta\),

\[
p_{j+1}
=e^{-\gamma\Delta}p_j
+\sigma\sqrt{1-e^{-2\gamma\Delta}}\,\xi_j.
\]

For the sampled momentum record, its KL divergence from independent thermal samples is exactly

\[
D=
-\frac{n-1}{2}\log(1-e^{-2\gamma\Delta}).
\]

Choosing

\[
\Delta
=O\!\left(\frac1\gamma\log\frac n{\epsilon^2}\right)
\]

gives whole-record TV at most \(\epsilon\) in

\[
O\!\left(\frac n\gamma\log\frac n{\epsilon^2}\right)
\]

time. Marking that record inherits the bound by data processing.

This is a simple passive-record benchmark, not a theorem for every adaptive experiment with additional hidden records. It nevertheless shows that the quadratic entropy construction is not a per-apparatus necessity.

### Calibrated rejection defeats the easiest lower example

Suppose the conditional momentum law is known to be \(G_\sigma(p-m_R)\), with the sign \(R\) available.

On \(|p|\le L\), rejection can reweight it to a truncated \(G_\sigma\), using an envelope

\[
M_L=
\exp\!\left(
\frac{|m|L}{\sigma^2}
+\frac{m^2}{2\sigma^2}
\right).
\]

For symmetric shifts, success probabilities are equal for both signs. In the displaced-Gaussian fixture, this preserves the relevant hidden/reference marginal on acceptance.

For small \(m\), the acceptance bill can be far smaller than inverse-square waiting. A valid optimality result must therefore optimize rejection and its population effects. Requiring every at
