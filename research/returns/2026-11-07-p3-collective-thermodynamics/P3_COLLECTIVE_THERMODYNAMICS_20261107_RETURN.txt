## 7. Why the result does not extend to generic thermodynamics

The tractability boundary can be stated with a direct reduction.

Take an unweighted graph \(G=(V,E)\), with \(m\) edges. Give each vertex its own collective ensemble, and let \(z_i\) denote that ensemble’s averaged \(Z\). Define

\[
P_G(z)=\frac1{2m}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}(1+z_i z_j).
\]

This is a commuting quadratic interaction with a compact known description. It is invariant under permutations within each ensemble.

Because the polynomial is affine in each coordinate separately, its minimum over \([-1,1]^{|V|}\) occurs at a vertex. At \(z_i=\pm1\), a cut edge contributes zero and an uncut edge contributes \(1/m\). Therefore

\[
\boxed{
e_0(G)=1-\frac{\operatorname{MAXCUT}(G)}m.
}
\]

The identity holds at every ensemble size.

An additive ground-energy-density approximation finer than \(1/(3m)\) recovers the integer MAX-CUT value by rounding. At positive temperature,

\[
e_0-\frac{\log2}{\beta}\le F_\beta\le e_0.
\]

Taking, for example, \(\beta=16m\) and free-energy error \(1/(16m)\) also suffices.

Unweighted MAX-CUT is NP-complete under the unit-weight condition stated in the original published result. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397576900591?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Thus a **deterministic** predictor with polynomial cost uniformly in the number of distinguishable ensembles and inverse accuracy for this larger family would imply \(P=NP\). The coefficients can already be supplied, and the Hamiltonian can already be commuting. Neither learning access nor quantum execution is responsible for this reduction.

The positive construction uses a fixed-dimensional macroscopic description. The negative construction lets that dimension grow with the input. This identifies a substantive source of tractability while retaining phase transitions in the positive class.
