1. The proposed mechanism
Separate finding contacts from locating the fixture

The earlier approach asked the rough-surface contacts to do two difficult jobs simultaneously: find enough asperities through compliant motion, then become a stiff, accurately located fixture.

The new construction separates those jobs.

The closed preload path is:

wall asperities → compliant spine banks → floating carrier → central drawbar and spring → fixture frame → four compression/friction feet → wall.

The spines provide the tensile reaction needed to clamp the feet against the wall. The feet then carry the fixture’s shear and distribute its bending load. Their guided posts provide a short structural path to the fixture frame.

This remains a real anchor: the spine contacts are still what keep it attached to the wall. The feet cannot supply tensile attachment by themselves.

All radial bank drives and equalizers belong to the floating carrier. A guide, cable or actuator crossing to the fixture frame must not become an uncounted load-bearing bridge.

Microspines, opposed gripping, passive retention and compliant load sharing are established constructions. Jiang and colleagues’ opposed-grip work explicitly treats engagement, rebound and release. Radial microspine grippers and patterned/stamped spine manufacture also precede this design. The proposed combination is an engineering hypothesis about manufacture and service; I have not established patent novelty.

7. Why I did not select passive wet suction or a gel instead
Finite-water suction fails the restoration ledger in the screened construction

A pre-evacuated reservoir with a water-sealed wall could avoid powered holding. The difficulty is that porous masonry takes up the functional water.

Using published generic masonry parameters—not measurements of the chosen lots—the screen predicts roughly 23 mL uptake on concrete and 63 mL on clay over 24 hours for the proposed contact area. A finite-disk model gives larger values. 
web.mit.edu
+1

A 200 mL active water inventory permits only 10 mL total replacement under the 5% rule. One predicted hold exceeds that.

Water left in the wall is functional-stock consumption. Calling it cleaning does not close the balance. Pressure-only leakage testing is also inadequate because liquid absorption changes the gas volume and can mask air ingress.

This rejects that construction, not every possible passive-suction mechanism.

Osmocapillary gels remain an actual material opening

Published PHEMA/PEG-based osmocapillary adhesion supplies a real preparation route. Its short tests on relatively controlled substrates do not establish clean masonry release, 24-hour hold or 500-cycle restoration. 
arxiv.org

For a 550 N clamp over 70 cm², required mean traction is about 78.6 kPa before proof, roughness and aging. A functional PEG inventory of 2.8 mL would allow only 0.28 µL average loss per cycle over 500 cycles.

Nonvolatility does not establish zero transfer, no residue or no creep. The gel could replace the spine clamp only after that simultaneous operation is established.
