1. The full question and the achieved answer

<b>Question.</b> Can the protected thermal state in the actual three-dimensional BDL family be made an efficiently accessible, reusable quantum register, with a reference-preserving guarantee for a complete adaptive experiment and with acquisition, controls, useful output and reuse included?

<b>Answer under the declared contract: yes.</b> The accepted P1 compiler supplies the classical syndrome law and protected frame. This exchange resolves the named sampler-comparison gate and proves that one background supports a complete campaign. It also constructs the previously explicit coefficient-access step. This is a mathematical operating capability conditional on the Hamiltonian, bath and control access, not a claim that those physical premises have been manufactured.

Why the whole campaign is the right object

A useful memory must return an unknown state, permit the next state to be written, and survive choices made from earlier outputs. A collection of separate one-hold estimates can charge preparation repeatedly and can fail to control information in the record. The new theorem compares the entire experiment with an ideal logical experiment on the same autonomous syndrome trajectory. It remains valid when a classical controller observes syndromes and chooses holding intervals adaptively.

The theorem is broader than a single BDL block. It applies to any stabilizer memory with syndrome-autonomous Pauli jump rates and a computable protected frame. BDL supplies stretched-exponential suppression of its stationary logical-jump flux. A bank of blocks follows by summing exposure and control budgets. This is the substantive deduction from the general theorem.

2. The actual physical matroid is extensively nonregular

Theorem 1: an extensive minor and cover-defect boundary

Fix any allowed odd refinement parameter ℓ ≥ 5. At every sufficiently large even construction depth, the physical Z-check matroid M<sub>Z</sub> of the BDL random-embedding family has a minor

MATH: \bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_N}F_7^*,\qquad r_N\geq a_\ell N,\qquad a_\ell>0.

Here F<sub>7</sub> is the binary Fano matroid. Both the physical relation space and its dual syndrome space therefore have regular-cover defect at least a<sub>ℓ</sub>N. The refinement is fixed before N grows; its constants may be extremely severe.

The physical-coordinate bridge

For an old Z-check matrix h<sub>Z</sub> with m rows and n qubit columns, put A = h<sub>Z</sub><super>T</super>. The physical ground elements of M(A) are the actual Hamiltonian checks. Introduce only as an algebraic intermediate the augmented matroid N(A) = M([I<sub>m</sub> | A<super>T</super>]). Its identity columns are not new physical interactions.

In the actual RX replacement, rows are x-local edges and q-local vertices; columns are x-local faces, q-local edges and old-Z point copies. Its boundary blocks are D<sub>X</sub>, G<sub>1</sub>, D<sub>Q</sub> and P, as in the source construction [B1]. Contract the x-face check columns. Vanishing H<sub>1</sub>(Y<sub>x</sub>) makes quotienting by im D<sub>X</sub> faithfully represented by vertex boundary d<sub>X</sub>. The identities d<sub>X</sub>G<sub>1</sub> = G<sub>0</sub>D<sub>Q</sub> and G<sub>0</sub>P = 0 remove the redundant top block.

Contract the upper and lower subdivided stars in each Y<sub>q</sub>, leaving one bridge per old qubit. The remaining physical minor is the Lawrence double with block rows [A, 0, I<sub>n</sub>] and [0, A, I<sub>n</sub>]. Its dependencies are exactly

MATH: (u,\ u+r,\ Au),\qquad u\in\mathbb{F}_2^m,\quad r\in\ker A.

A dual representation restricted to the upper-check and bridge columns is [I<sub>m</sub> | A<super>T</super>]. Thus N(A)<super>*</super> is a minor of the physical RX matroid. This proof allows redundant old checks; no independent-check simplification changes the Hamiltonian.

Transport through refinement and the other sector

For every odd ℓ ≥ 3, contracting qubit columns along dual spanning trees merges the subdivided x-faces and boundary strips. Contract internal q-arm vertices, retain one identity per merged check coordinate, and remove parallel boundary columns. This gives exactly the ℓ = 3 augmented block.

Closed vertex-disjoint Tanner strips extract as a literal direct sum: contract unwanted identity columns, then delete unwanted qubit columns. No off-diagonal coupling remains. Under RZ, contracting internal Y<sub>z</sub> trees preserves the old augmented Z matroid as a minor. The physical Z matroid itself undergoes a series extension, since its relations are constant on each connected Y<sub>z</sub> vertex block.

The resulting four-step chain takes disjoint strips through RX, RZ, RX, RZ to disjoint physical F<sub>7</sub><super>*</super> minors. Previous RZ square layers supply Θ(N) candidate strips. Bounded incidence gives a bounded-degree conflict graph, so greedy packing retains Ω(N) disjoint copies. Each replacement has fixed size ratio at fixed ℓ.

The quantitative cover lemma

For cycle spaces C ⊆ D, deletion intersects and contraction projects; both preserve inclusion and cannot increase dim(D/C). Regularity survives both. On a minor ⊕ C<sub>i</sub> of r disjoint Fano or Fano-dual blocks, each regular restriction D ∩ F₂<sup>Eᵢ</sup> strictly contains C<sub>i</sub>. Choose one extra vector per block. Disjoint supports make these r vectors independent modulo C, so dim(D/C) ≥ r. Duality proves the syndrome-space statement.

Independent subdivision checks use a separately built grid/cylinder model at ℓ = 3, 5, 7, 9. They verify contraction ranks 16, 38, 68, 106 and the same seven images; the symbolic contraction proves all odd refinements.

4. The strongest named Gibbs-sampler comparison

A full quantum Gibbs sampler is a valid loading source. Run it before the unknown input arrives, measure its stabilizers, retain a classical syndrome and use the accepted loader. Logical randomization inside the auxiliary sampler is harmless at that stage. Its initialization error is paid once. The comparison below grants exactly that access.

Why a logarithmic regular cover really would have sufficed

For linear syndrome spaces L ⊆ R, codimension q, and positive zero-preferred weights w(s) = ∏ exp(−λᵢsᵢ), every binary Fourier coefficient of w is nonnegative. The character average of the q homogeneous constraints gives π<sub>R</sub>(L) ≥ 2<super>−q</super>. Thus rejection would need at most 2<sup>q+1</sup> expected approximate proposals if proposal TV error δ ≤ 2<sup>−q−1</sup>. Its conditional error is at most 2<sup>q</sup>δ.

Set δ = ε·2<sup>−q</sup> and cap attempts at ⌈2<sup>q+1</sup> log(1/ζ)⌉. Total sampling/abort error is at most ε + ζ. For logarithmic q, even inverse-accuracy polynomial samplers remain polynomial. Our obstruction rules out precisely that sufficient route.

<b>Scope.</b> No theorem here says actual CSW is torpid, that every high-codimension cover has tiny acceptance, or that every possible reduction to an enlarged regular system fails. The accepted efficient history sampler itself disproves a universal preparation-hardness interpretation.

5. The recent quantum-decoding route has a real obstruction

The full primary text of QD-HDQI [B4] defines the code to decode as C = ker B, where B contains the actual indexed Hamiltonian Pauli labels. It is not the code’s physical-error decoder. For P1’s projector convention Jᵢ(I−Sᵢ)/2, its pure-state channel overlap is

MATH: t_i=\tanh(\beta J_i/2),\qquad p_i=\frac{1-\operatorname{sech}(\beta J_i/2)}{2}.

The codeword prior is uniform over the complete relation code. Algorithm 2 succeeds with probability exactly 1−p<sub>fail</sub>; Theorem 3.1 needs its reciprocal polynomial. Theorem 4.1 separately averages its error over all Hamiltonian signs, which is not automatically a guarantee for the fixed frustration-free BDL sector.

Theorem 2: disjoint physical relations force exponential failure

The actual final BDL check code contains L ≥ b<sub>ℓ</sub>N disjoint nonzero relation vectors, each of weight at most a constant w<sub>ℓ</sub>. In RX, an old qubit with two Z neighbors gives a cycle e in its two-star graph Y<sub>q</sub>. Every adjacent Y<sub>x</sub> fills that cycle, so (f,e,0) is a physical check dependency. Previous RZ grids supply a positive density of such qubits; bounded overlap permits a linear disjoint packing. Final RZ repeats the coefficients across fixed-size check blocks and preserves disjointness.

Reveal the complementary codeword coordinate to a decoder. This can only help. It leaves L independent unbiased bits encoded in disjoint binary pure-state blocks. For block j, the two states have overlap at least t<sub>*</sub><sup>wℓ</sup>, where t<sub>*</sub> = tanh(βJ<sub>min</sub>/2). Helstrom discrimination gives its optimal success. The optimum probability of getting every independent bit right multiplies, including for collective measurements, by the tensor-product dual witness for the discrimination SDP.

MATH: P_{\rm succ}\leq\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{1-t_*^{2w_\ell}}}{2}\right)^L \leq \exp\left(-\frac{L\,t_*^{2w_\ell}}{4}\right)

At every fixed positive βJ<sub>min</sub>, this is exponentially small in N. Even quadratic amplitude amplification leaves exponential cost for this operation. The USD/classical rejection variant independently obeys

MATH: P_{\rm accept}\leq(1-t_*^{w_\ell})^L.

The sign issue is separate

In the positive frustration-free sector, the normalized high-temperature expansion contains all combinations of those disjoint relations, giving Z′<sub>β</sub> ≥ (1+t<sub>*</sub><sup>wℓ</sup>)<sup>L</sup>. It is exponentially far from the near-one quantity controlled by the all-sign average. Pauli conjugation preserves relation frustration and cannot turn that average into a fixed-sector result.

This obstruction targets complete codeword decoding, not Gibbs preparation. Even disjoint constant-size redundant stabilizer blocks can have hard full-codeword recovery while their Gibbs state is easy to prepare. The accepted history route avoids the unnecessary recovery task.

6. The loader and its useful endpoint

The accepted compiler supplies the full thermal syndrome

Accept the P1 history compiler and its actual-family instantiation [A1]. Valid syndromes correspond bijectively to histories with bounded-degree local constraints. Nonzero connected components have additive energy at least a times their size. With κ the fixed connected-object counting factor and x = κe<sup>−βa</sup> satisfying the accepted low-activity condition, a cutoff L has excluded probability at most Mx<sup>L+1</sup>/(1−x).

Choose L = O(log(M/ε<sub>alg</sub>)), enumerate K = poly(N,1/ε<sub>alg</sub>) retained polymers, and run the explicit random-scan heat bath. Its contraction factor is at most 1−3/(4K), so O(K log(M/ε<sub>alg</sub>)) steps suffice. The complete constant-size base validity relation remains included. There is no excluded-syndrome preparation floor.

An arbitrary unknown input arrives only after preparation

Let V₀ encode the ground logical subsystem, F<sub>s</sub> be the computed correction frame and V<sub>s</sub> = F<sub>s</sub><sup>†</sup>V₀. Sample s before the input arrives. When the unknown state ρ, possibly entangled with a remote reference, arrives, prepare the required ancillas, apply V₀ and then F<sub>s</sub><sup>†</sup>.

MATH: \mathcal{E}_p(\rho)=\sum_s p(s)V_s\rho V_s^\dagger,\qquad \frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{E}_p-\mathcal{E}_\pi\|_\diamond=\operatorname{TV}(p,\pi)

This is an exact orthogonal-sector identity. Any full Gibbs sampler can replace the classical source by stabilizer measurement before input arrival. Initialization does not demand exponentially precise sampling merely because the later hold is exponentially long.

Read, compute, and replace while keeping the background

At an access time, isolate the holding bath or include its effects in the access error. Measure the commuting checks to obtain s; on the stipulated block-diagonal state this does not disturb the logical/reference state. Apply F<sub>s</sub> and the inverse Clifford encoder. The logical state is now on designated ports.

Apply the requested logical instrument or swap the old state into an output port and a new unknown state into the logical port. Re-encode and apply F<sub>s</sub><sup>†</sup>. Every ideal branch returns exactly the same syndrome s. The original Gibbs background is retained. New thermal sampling and whole-device thermal reset are unnecessary after each use.

Known stabilizer encoders and inverse encoders have polynomial gate descriptions; actual local routing, protected ports, ancilla preparation, measurement, decoder evaluation, isolation and reset are charged. An arbitrary logical computation is allowed only with its own implementation cost. This construction does not turn logical gates into passive operations.

7. A decoder can sometimes be replaced by central projection

The full relative-decoding paper [B5] requires matched indexed magnitudes, equal anticommutation matrices, K<sub>A</sub> ⊆ K<sub>B</sub>, and consistent relation phases. It takes a canonical source thermofield double (TFD). Its degree certificate must fit below the certified relative-decoding radius. The supplied chain example has a fixed inverse-temperature window.

A necessary relation test is elementary but useful: if d<sub>A→B</sub> &gt; w, then every target relation of weight at most w lies in K<sub>A</sub>. Thus a large-radius BDL source must absorb the explicit local relations above. We have not assumed that these generate every BDL relation, and have not excluded all possible relative sources.

Theorem 3: exact central-sector transduction

Under the algebraic compatibility premises, let q = dim K<sub>B</sub> − dim K<sub>A</sub>. The missing relations become q independent central source Paulis. Their joint required eigenspace has projector P. In that sector, polynomial symplectic elimination gives a Clifford identification of the source’s indexed Pauli algebra with the target, up to idle factors.

MATH: p_\beta=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}(P e^{-\beta H_A})} {\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta H_A})}.

Project the physical half of the canonical source TFD by P. Because P commutes with H<sub>A</sub>, this also selects the conjugate reference sector. Apply the Clifford and its complex conjugate, remove fixed sector registers, and add/remove idle Bell pairs. Conditioned on success, the output is exactly the canonical target TFD for every β.

Expected independent-trial cost is 1/p<sub>β</sub>. Coherent source preparation, its inverse and reflections permit amplitude amplification with query cost O(p<sub>β</sub><sup>−1/2</sup>). A source error δ can amplify by O(δ/p<sub>β</sub>) on conditioning; a certified p₀ ≤ p<sub>β</sub> permits source accuracy O(p₀ε) and a finite trial cap O(p₀<sup>−1</sup>log(1/ε)).

Two constructive success bounds

For a positive commuting source and a frustration-free homogeneous target sector, Fourier positivity gives p<sub>β</sub> ≥ 2<sup>−q</sup> at every temperature. Thus q = O(log N) can remove the need for a relative-distance decoder. For a noncommuting source, a unitary sector flip changing the Hamiltonian by operator norm at most Δ gives p ≥ (1+e<sup>βΔ</sup>)<sup>−1</sup> by eigenvalue comparison of the two restricted blocks.

The projection method is a compatible alternative, not a general lower bound or a free sampler. It pays for source preparation, conditioning, algebraic compression, reference storage and all controls. The next page gives an explicit target family where it extends the sufficient temperature window.

8. An explicit noncommuting target at every fixed temperature

Place constant-size cells (q<sub>v</sub>,c<sub>v</sub>) on the vertices of an L × L grid. Let P be the row-snake Hamiltonian path and T the column-snake path. The source Hamiltonian, viewed along P, is a nearest-neighbor chain:

MATH: H_A=\sum_v(a_vX_{q_v}+b_vZ_{q_v}+d_vZ_{c_v} +e_vZ_{c_v}Z_{q_v})+\sum_{(u,v)\in P}f_{uv}Z_{q_u}Z_{q_v}

All coefficients are supplied, bounded in magnitude by J₀; the X and Z terms make the Hamiltonian noncommuting. The c-qubit Z observables are central. The one-dimensional canonical-TFD theorem [B7] applies at every fixed finite inverse temperature after grouping each pair into a constant-size cell.

Local target and exact operation

Put a target qubit R<sub>e</sub> at each edge midpoint of T and define B<sub>v</sub> = ∏<sub>e∋v</sub>Z<sub>Rₑ</sub>. Replace every Z<sub>cᵥ</sub> by B<sub>v</sub>, keeping the q terms and row-path interactions. Each B<sub>v</sub> has weight at most two, so interactions have weight at most three, bounded degree and bounded geometric range. With all terms present the interaction graph contains a square-grid minor.

The only new relation is ∏<sub>v</sub>B<sub>v</sub> = I. Measure the source central parity ∏<sub>v</sub>Z<sub>cᵥ</sub> and retain +1. If F is the edge-vertex incidence matrix of the tree T, even c strings have a unique representation c = F<super>T</super>r. Prefix-parity CNOTs implement this change of variables on the physical and reference copies, leaving one fixed parity bit to remove.

For every fixed finite β, this is a constant independent of system size. 9. The whole-campaign reusable-register theorem

Theorem 4: stationary logical flux controls all uses

In the protected frame V<sub>s</sub>, let an energy-resolved elementary Pauli jump a have syndrome rate q<sub>a</sub>(s), next syndrome s+d<sub>a</sub>, and logical Pauli ℓ<sub>a</sub>(s) = V<sub>s+dₐ</sub><sup>†</sup>P<sub>a</sub>V<sub>s</sub>. The jump rate depends only on s, and the no-jump evolution is scalar on the logical subsystem. Define

MATH: b(s)=\sum_a q_a(s)\,\mathbf{1}\{\ell_a(s)\not\propto I\}, \qquad \lambda_L=\sum_s\pi(s)b(s)

Initialize once with a classical syndrome p satisfying TV(p,π) ≤ ε<sub>init</sub>. The joint memory/reference state is block diagonal in syndrome; arbitrary logical/reference entanglement conditional on that classical syndrome is permitted.

Allow syndrome-preserving logical instruments, classical adaptation to all retained records, and passive holding intervals whose accumulated duration is at most T on every branch. Isolate the bath during active operations, or charge its implementation effects. Let the accumulated uniform control error be bounded by ∑ ε<sub>ctrl,j</sub> on every branch.

MATH: \operatorname{dist}(\text{actual campaign},\text{ideal campaign}) \leq\epsilon_{\rm init}+T\lambda_L+\sum_j\epsilon_{{\rm ctrl},j}

Distance is trace distance of the complete output state and retained classical record, uniformly over compatible strategies and arbitrary logical references. The ideal experiment has the same syndrome trajectory and logical instruments; passive holding acts as the logical identity.

Proof without stationarity at an adaptive stopping time

Fix a stationary autonomous labeled syndrome trajectory in accumulated passive time on the deterministic interval [0,T]. Ideal active instruments preserve s and pause that trajectory. Any compatible classically timed experiment traverses a stopped prefix, even when its choices depend on syndrome reports or logical outcomes.

Couple actual and ideal experiments to the same trajectory and instrument realizations. Until the first nonidentity logical jump they agree, including references and records. A bad jump in the campaign is contained in a bad jump anywhere in [0,T]. Stationarity at deterministic intrinsic times gives E N<sub>bad</sub>(T) = Tλ<sub>L</sub>. Markov’s inequality bounds the probability of any such jump. Couple p to π once; then apply a control-channel hybrid. This proves the theorem.

The ideal does not supply independent fresh syndrome reports. The result is unconditional on the retained record; rare postselection requires its usual acceptance-probability correction. Coherent quantum control of bath exposure and a TFD coherent across syndrome sectors are different interfaces.

10. BDL consequence, deployment and reuse

Stable syndromes have b(s) = 0. With O(N) elementary jump channels of rate at most Γ and the accepted unstable-syndrome estimate,

MATH: \lambda_L\leq C\Gamma N e^{-cN^\eta}.

Thus the complete campaign has stretched-exponentially long passive exposure at fixed storage error. Repeated accesses do not multiply ε<sub>init</sub>; they add their actual control error and execution costs. For a bank of blocks, sum CΓN<sub>b</sub>T<sub>b</sub>e<sup>−cNᵦη</sup> and the initialization/control budgets.

Concrete mission specification

Choose target failure ε, total passive exposure T, and a finite access plan or uniform branchwise control budget. Allocate ε among calibrated initial sampling, controls and passive storage. Choose N so CΓNT e<sup>−cNη</sup> fits the storage allocation. Asymptotically a suitable power of log(T/ε) suffices, allowing the additional log N factor. This is a size law for a fixed source family, not a small practical prefactor.

Acquire the valid design and calibrated thermal coefficients before the input arrives. Compile the sampler, frame, encoder and access circuits. Load once. At each use, measure s, decode to a protected port, perform the requested logical operation, and restore the same s. Release the final unknown state to the specified output interface. Reset/dispose of access ancillas and records using the declared resources.

Precision is paid at the active interface

If the mission uses Q imperfect primitive control locations, a sufficient composable allocation is per-location error O(ε<sub>ctrl</sub>/Q). Let t<sub>g</sub>(δ), E<sub>g</sub>(δ), and analogous preparation, readout and reset functions denote actual costs of attaining accuracy δ. The bill uses those functions, plus routing and isolation. Merely writing down δ does not prove a constant-rate fault-tolerant implementation.

An arbitrary number of active calls is therefore not free, and an exponential number of accurate calls need not have polynomial total cost. The benefit is long passive gaps with polynomial-size endpoint circuits under the supplied accuracy primitives.

Holding still consumes physical resources

MATH: V_{\rm spacetime}\ \gtrsim\ NT,\qquad E_{\rm hold}=\int_0^{T_{\rm wall}}P_{\rm maintenance}(t)\,dt.

A bath-connected device occupies space and maintenance resources throughout the hold. Refrigerator power, clock and record protection, active delays and protected output-port storage remain in the bill. The source’s random-embedding constants are extremely large; no engineering-scale device or optimized energy budget is claimed.

The memory theorem assumes the specified Markov semigroup for the duration. A new noncommuting Hamiltonian residual can accumulate coherently and is not converted into a harmless coefficient-calibration error. Those are material remaining hardware questions, not omitted terms in the proved interface.

11. Coherent preparation of the canonical thermal state

Theorem 5: the accepted polymer compiler supplies a true TFD

A classical sampler is not automatically an amplitude-preparation circuit. Here the sampler is explicit enough to construct one. Choose the polymer cutoff with omitted probability τ = O(ε²), enumerate K retained polymers, and scale their activities by u ∈ (0,1]:

MATH: \pi_u(\Gamma)=Z_u^{-1}u^{|\Gamma|}\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}w_\gamma.

The same influence bound applies at every u ≤ 1. The random-scan heat bath is reversible, positive and has spectral gap at least 3/(4K). Its at most K+1 transitions and their probabilities are explicitly computable from compatibility and uw<sub>γ</sub>/(1+uw<sub>γ</sub>), so the coherent transition isometry has polynomial cost.

An explicit annealing path

Take u₀K ≤ cε². Since Z<sub>u₀</sub> ≤ exp(u₀K), the empty configuration is O(ε) close to the first stationary amplitude state. Put θ = log u and R = log(1/u₀). If every configuration uses at most M polymers,

MATH: \left\|\partial_\theta|\sqrt{\pi_{e^\theta}}\rangle\right\| =\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(|\Gamma|)}\leq M/4.

Use S = O(M²R²/ε²) equal logarithmic steps. Ideal successive stationary projections fail with total probability at most M²R²/(16S). Quantum-walk phase filtering [B6] implements them with phase gap Ω(K<sup>−1/2</sup>) and per-step error O(ε/S). The standard data/zero-ancilla subspace is orthogonal to unwanted zero-phase modes. All filtering and reversible arithmetic costs are polynomial.
