## 4. The reusable-register theorem

The central operating result applies beyond the particular BDL construction.

Let \(V_s\) identify syndrome sector \(s\) with a common logical subsystem. For the specified energy-resolved Pauli bath, jump \(a\) has rate \(q_a(s)\), changes the syndrome to \(s+d_a\), and applies logical Pauli

\[
\ell_a(s)=V_{s+d_a}^{\dagger}P_aV_s.
\]

The syndrome process is autonomous: its jump rates depend on \(s\), not on the stored logical state. Define the stationary logical-error flux

\[
b(s)=\sum_a q_a(s)\mathbf1\{\ell_a(s)\not\propto I\},
\qquad
\lambda_L=\sum_s\pi(s)b(s).
\]

Global phases are harmless.

### Theorem

Initialize once with a classical syndrome distribution \(p\) satisfying

\[
\operatorname{TV}(p,\pi)\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}.
\]

Allow arbitrary logical-reference entanglement conditional on that classical syndrome. Permit syndrome-preserving logical instruments and classically adaptive holding intervals whose total duration is at most \(T\) on every branch.

Then the complete campaign differs from its ideal logical counterpart by at most

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}
+
T\lambda_L
+
\sum_j\epsilon_{\mathrm{ctrl},j}.
}
\]

The bound includes the retained records and final quantum outputs.

### Why adaptation does not break the proof

The proof does not claim stationarity at an observation-dependent stopping time.

Instead, fix one stationary, labeled syndrome trajectory on the deterministic **passive-time interval** \([0,T]\). Ideal active operations preserve the syndrome and pause that trajectory. An adaptive experiment traverses a stopped prefix of it.

Couple the actual and ideal logical experiments to the same trajectory. They agree until a nonidentity logical jump occurs. Any bad jump encountered during the campaign is contained in a bad jump somewhere in the full interval. Stationarity at deterministic intrinsic times gives

\[
\mathbb E_\pi N_{\mathrm{bad}}(T)=T\lambda_L.
\]

The probability of any such jump is at most its expected count. Initial coupling costs \(\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}\) once; a channel hybrid charges implementation errors.

For BDL, stable syndromes have zero logical-error flux and the accepted unstable-syndrome bound gives

\[
\lambda_L\le C\Gamma N e^{-cN^\eta}.
\]

That yields the campaign theorem stated at the beginning.

### The actual reusable interface

1. Isolate the holding bath, or include its effects in the implementation error.
2. Measure the commuting checks to obtain \(s\).
3. Apply \(F_s\) and invert the ground-state encoder.
4. Perform the requested logical instrument, or swap out the stored state and swap in a fresh unknown input.
5. Re-encode and apply \(F_s^\dagger\).

Every ideal branch restores **the same syndrome \(s\)**. On the stipulated syndrome-block-diagonal state, measuring the checks does not disturb the logical/reference state.

Thus a fresh Gibbs sample and whole-device thermal reset are unnecessary after each use.

There are three consequential qualifications. The guarantee is unconditional on retained records; rare postselection can amplify conditional error. Timing decisions are classical. A TFD coherent across syndrome sectors is a separate preparation capability, not an initial state automatically covered by this trajectory proof.

## 5. Acquisition and the complete physical bill

The thermal weights can be acquired without estimating the rare lifetime.

For known commuting Pauli supports, prepare a ground eigenstate \(\psi_0\), apply a Pauli frame \(R\), and use a one-site probe \(P\). If \(P\) anticommutes with checks in \(B(P)\), the gap is

\[
\Delta_P(R)=\sum_{a\in B(P)}J_a\chi_a(R),
\]

where \(R^\dagger S_aR=\chi_a(R)S_a\).

A Ramsey sequence—controlled \(P\), natural evolution, controlled \(P\)—measures this gap without controlled-Hamiltonian access. Uniform Pauli-character orthogonality gives

\[
\boxed{
J_a=\mathbb E_R[\chi_a(R)\Delta_P(R)].
}
\]

This remains valid when stabilizer relations constrain the attainable sign patterns. Exact duplicate terms are combined rather than falsely treated as separately identifiable.

At fixed overlap and coupling bounds, estimating every coefficient to \(O(\epsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}/m)\) has the conservative total shot bound

\[
O\!\left(
m^3\epsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}^{-2}\log(m/\alpha)
\right).
\]

Every shot includes its actual preparation, routing, interrogation, readout and reset. Timing and coherent-control bias are included. Temperature acquisition needs a supplied, priced thermometer; a bath-rate upper bound does not imply fast thermometer equilibration.

The overall operation retains the following costs:

| Stage | Required resources |
|---|---|
| Design and acquisition | Valid physical construction, known supports, coefficient calibration, temperature access, confidence and model validity |
| Initial preparation | Polymer computation, randomness, ancillas, encoder, frame, routing and initial error |
| Passive holding | Physical volume, bath access, maintenance, refrigeration and total exposure |
| Useful access | Syndrome metrology, decoding, protected ports, logical controls, re-encoding and latency |
| Reuse | Ancilla and controller reset, protected records, actual precision and energy |
| Coherent TFD option | Second physical copy, coherent arithmetic, filtering, quantum controls and its own error budget |

If the mission contains \(Q\) imperfect control locations, a sufficient allocation is error \(O(\epsilon_{\mathrm{ctrl}}/Q)\) per location. The cost of attaining that accuracy must be supplied by the control platform. This is not a constant-rate noisy-control threshold.

Similarly, long passive life does not mean cheap total operation:

\[
V_{\mathrm{spacetime}}\gtrsim NT,
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{hold}}=\int P_{\mathrm{maintenance}}(t)\,dt.
\]

The result gives efficient accessibility and a useful endpoint within the supplied model. It does not assign an invented numerical cost to fabricating that model, nor convert general noncommuting Hamiltonian error into harmless thermal-weight uncertainty.
