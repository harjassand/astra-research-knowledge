# Sources and standard mathematical inputs

These references support standard tools used in the manuscript. They do not establish or externally endorse the new reduction. The construction-specific compiler, Fourier representation, all-state lift, pressure comparison, recoloring, merger and odd-component cancellation are proved in the manuscript.

1. R. L. Dobrushin. “The Description of a Random Field by Means of Conditional Probabilities and Conditions of Its Regularity.” Theory of Probability and Its Applications 13(2) (1968), 197–224. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1137/1113026
   Role: uniqueness and comparison from a uniformly contracting single-site influence matrix. The manuscript derives its concrete six-neighbor auxiliary bound.

2. R. Kotecký and D. Preiss. “Cluster Expansion for Abstract Polymer Models.” Communications in Mathematical Physics 103 (1986), 491–498. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211762
   Role: absolute convergence of the hard-core polymer expansion. The manuscript verifies the incompatible-polymer sum with an exponential size margin.

3. H.-O. Georgii. Gibbs Measures and Phase Transitions. Second edition. De Gruyter, 2011. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110250329
   Role: Gibbs specifications, entropy and variational principles, including the full-shift Gibbs/equilibrium correspondence used for the exponentially summable induced spin interaction.

4. T. Meyerovitch. “Gibbs and Equilibrium Measures for Some Families of Subshifts.” arXiv:0903.1426, Section 3. https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.1426 ; full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.1426
   Role: an explicit statement of the Lanford–Ruelle equilibrium-to-Gibbs theorem for an arbitrary shift of finite type and a potential with d-summable variation. No irreducibility assumption is needed in this direction. The manuscript does not assume the converse for arbitrary hard-tile sectors.

5. R. G. Edwards and A. D. Sokal. “Generalization of the Fortuin–Kasteleyn–Swendsen–Wang Representation and Monte Carlo Algorithm.” Physical Review D 38 (1988), 2009–2012. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.38.2009
   Role: the standard joint spin/bond representation. The additional all-TI conditional domination argument is supplied in the manuscript rather than assumed from a finite-volume representation.

6. R. Peierls. “On Ising’s Model of Ferromagnetism.” Proceedings of the Cambridge Philosophical Society 32(3) (1936), 477–481. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100019174
   Role: the low-temperature contour method. The manuscript gives its own explicit nonhalting magnetization and high-density planar-hole estimates.
