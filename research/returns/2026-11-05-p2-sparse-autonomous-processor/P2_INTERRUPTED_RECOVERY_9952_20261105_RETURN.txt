## 1. The theorem and what it earns

Let spatial bursts occur in ambient dimension \(d\), with radius tail

\[
\Pr(R\ge u)=u^{-\alpha},\qquad u\ge1,
\]

and define

\[
r=\alpha-d.
\]

Fix \(d\ge2\), \(r>1\), and a sufficiently small **positive physical intensity \(\lambda\)** before choosing the requested storage horizon.

### Sparse autonomous memory theorem

Within the assumptions below, one uniform algorithm constructs finite, input-independent layouts that:

1. acquire an unknown qubit from a fixed compact raw input;
2. preserve its correlations with an arbitrary reference;
3. retain it through a requested physical time \(T\), while protecting all mutable controller records;
4. deliver it at a fixed local port at a declared time \(t_T=T(1+o(1))\);

with total reference-inclusive error at most \(1/4\), and resources

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
L(T)&\le T^{1/r+o(1)},\\
N(T)&\le T^{1/r+o(1)},\\
U_{\mathrm{all}}(T)&\le T^{1+1/r+o(1)}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

Here:

- \(L\) is whole-device diameter;
- \(N\) counts installed registers, including control, scratch, transport, and backward tracks;
- \(U_{\mathrm{all}}\) charges **every installed register on every physical tick**, including idle time.

For every competing device in the same static bounded-range register model, fixed nontrivial accuracy requires

\[
\boxed{
L(T)=\Omega(T^{1/r}),\qquad
N(T)=\Omega(T^{1/r}),\qquad
U_{\mathrm{all}}(T)=\Omega(T^{1+1/r}).
}
\]

Thus the **polynomial exponents match**.

### Improvement over the preceding construction

| Resource | Previous dense reusable device | New sparse device | Lower bound |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Diameter | \(T^{1/r+o(1)}\) | \(T^{1/r+o(1)}\) | \(\Omega(T^{1/r})\) |
| Installed registers | \(T^{2/r+o(1)}\) | **\(T^{1/r+o(1)}\)** | \(\Omega(T^{1/r})\) |
| All-register execution cost | \(T^{1+2/r+o(1)}\) | **\(T^{1+1/r+o(1)}\)** | \(\Omega(T^{1+1/r})\) |

For example, when \(r=2\), installed hardware improves from approximately \(T\) to approximately \(\sqrt T\), and charged execution cost improves from approximately \(T^2\) to approximately \(T^{3/2}\). These statements suppress subpolynomial factors and potentially large constants.

The scientific consequence is a resource law for a completed operation. The construction and obstruction agree on how installed hardware must grow with retention time, while retaining unknown-input acquisition, corruptible control, and local delivery.

## 2. Assumptions that make the theorem true

The scope is important.

**Static patterned local hardware.** The device is a finite graph with bounded-degree, bounded-range interactions, bounded local register dimension, and bounded spatial density. The construction uses a two-dimensional embedding. Its layout may depend on the horizon but is independent of the unknown input.

**A fixed synchronous local law.** Every tick applies the same constant-depth local channel. Static wiring, local gate laws, and the synchronous tick remain immutable premises. The theorem does not establish asynchronous clocks or translation invariance.

**All state-bearing records are corruptible.** Data, program, syndrome, vote, counter, loaded/done flags, and I/O registers receive the same burst model. Programmable operation can use a fixed interpreter with its program held in protected mutable registers.

**Noisy known-controller initialization.** Known controller words are written using actual noisy local product preparations. Their error and write costs are included. Their prescribed spatial region may grow with the horizon. The unknown input-dependent seed remains fixed.

**Instantaneous spatial bursts.** Poisson marks determine the damaged regions. Within a marked region, corruption may be an arbitrary quantum channel; fault values may be correlated and mediated by an environment. Independence is used only for disjoint sets of actual Poisson sources. Persistent damage with its own lifetime would require another analysis.

**Fixed nontrivial final accuracy.** Raw input and output contribute a fixed \(O(\lambda)\) error. The target is, for example, total error at most \(1/4\). Increasing the encoded apparatus does not make one fixed noisy raw output cell arbitrarily reliable.

The hardware saving also depends on **physically absent padding sites**. It does not assume that mutable “empty” cells in a filled lattice remain harmless. Bounding-box area and installed-register count are different resources.

## 3. The central construction: sparse execution without losing control

The hard step was to remove unused hardware while preserving the operational semantics of the protected circuit—including malformed inputs, arbitrary junk, and quantum correlations.

### A. Count live values, rather than the full padded rectangle

The accepted local compiler uses a fixed finite code and gadget library. At concatenation level \(m\), its spatial extent and operation duration have the form

\[
H=B^m,\qquad
\tau_m=A^m,\qquad
A=CB,
\]

where \(B\) is a padding parameter and \(C\) is independent of \(B\).

A dense implementation allocates registers throughout the full width, even where no useful value is present.

The new liveness lemma gives a constant \(Q_*\), independent of \(B\), such that

\[
\boxed{Q_m\le Q_*Q_{m-1},\qquad Q_m\le cQ_*^m.}
\]

Here \(Q_m\) counts simultaneously live physical registers in a level-\(m\) block.

The definition of “live” is deliberately conservative: retain a value until its **last syntactic read**, including every possible controlled-gate operand. Count all code coordinates, private votes, syndrome records, scratch, and controller state. A corrupted control value cannot make a deleted record suddenly necessary.

Why can \(Q_*\) be independent of the padding? A fixed gadget routes a fixed collection of child blocks through \(B\) positions. Increasing \(B\) lengthens the route. It does not require \(B\) simultaneous copies of the data or workspace.

Destinations are prepared or normalized immediately before use. At each hop, the source, current destination, and bounded workspace remain present. This preserves the duration \(A=CB\), after changing the fixed constant if necessary.

Strong normalization is essential: a destination may be arbitrarily damaged before use. The imported strong-rectangle framework supplies validity on arbitrary physical inputs, with syndrome and environmental information retained in the correctness argument. This is the role of AGP’s Section 10, Lemma 6, rather than an assumption that incoming scratch is clean. 

### B. Turn the live circuit into a fixed local rule

Create one hardware vertex for each live source-register occurrence at circuit phase \(\phi\). Join each value to its next live continuation.

These continuation edges form directed paths and cycles:

- persistent data and controller values cross the period boundary;
- fresh ancillas begin at preparation sites and end after their last use.

At every retained vertex \(v\), install two mutable registers, \(A_v\) and \(B_v\). Apply the same four local layers at every tick:

1. swap \(A_v\) with \(B_v\);
2. swap \(B_v\) with \(A_{\operatorname{succ}(v)}\);
3. reset \(A_v\) at open-path sources;
4. apply the fixed gates between equal-phase forward registers.

The second layer is a matching: each edge uses its source’s backward register and its destination’s forward register.

For an open path \(v_0,\ldots,v_\ell\), this gives the exact identities

\[
A'_{v_j}=A_{v_{j-1}}\quad(j>0),
\qquad
A'_{v_0}=|0\rangle,
\]

and

\[
B'_{v_j}=B_{v_{j+1}}\quad(j<\ell),
\qquad
B'_{v_\ell}=A_{v_\ell}.
\]

Useful states move forward. Discarded states move backward. Their only re-entry to the forward computation passes through an **actual noisy reset**.

This supplies the missing local operation: sparse paths can begin and end without treating disposal, fresh ancillas, or unused registers as reliable external services.

### C. Why unrelated states cannot enter the computation

At global tick \(t\), a forward register at circuit phase \(\phi\) belongs to temporal cohort

\[
t-\phi\pmod P,
\]

where \(P\) is the circuit period.

Continuation preserves that label, and every fixed gate joins equal-phase operands. Therefore its inputs always belong to the same cohort.

This is an identity of the channel’s tensor-factor routing. It permits arbitrary initial junk and entanglement in other cohorts. It does not require their states to be independent.

A fault that touches an active interaction operand is still charged. The argument never exempts transport partners or source resets from the fault model.

### D. Why the burst theorem survives the change

Retained vertices keep their original source coordinates and use the existing annular embedding. Continuations, gate edges, and the period seam remain bounded-range.

At each microphase, the selected cohort—including both operands of transport swaps—occupies the same bounded-thickness interaction region used by the accepted full adapter. Straightening that region preserves the homogeneous Poisson source law.

Deleting padding does not enlarge the sufficient bad-source events. One can extend the sparse circuit by arbitrary discarded padding and apply the strong source semantics to the retained operations.

That is the compatibility bridge. It transfers the **actual separated-source recurrence**, without postulating independent effective faults or independent junk.

## 4. Deriving the upper exponent and paying the operation costs

Let \(w\) include logical data, program, controller, and work width. Let \(b\) be logical kernel depth, and write

\[
\bar b=b+w+1,\qquad
\kappa=\log_B A,\qquad
\zeta=\log_BQ_*.
\]

The source period has duration

\[
P=O(\bar bH^\kappa).
\]

The number of retained live occurrences is at most

\[
cwQ_*^mP,
\]

so the sparse hardware bound becomes

\[
\boxed{
N=O(w\bar bH^{\kappa+\zeta}).
}
\]

For \(K\) periods, the inherited error bound is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta\le{}&
\eta_{\mathrm{in}}+\eta_{\mathrm{out}}
+C_{\mathrm{io}}\lambda\\
&+C_{\mathrm{init}}\lambda w_cH^{-r}\\
&+C_{\mathrm{bulk}}\lambda KG H^{\kappa-r}
+\epsilon_{\mathrm{synth}},
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(G=O(w\bar b)\). The terms pay for declared raw apparatus errors, acquisition/delivery, known-controller preparation, protected execution, and whole-computation gate synthesis.

For memory, the countdown and its circuit have polylogarithmic width and depth. Taking \(K=\lceil T/P\rceil\), the main accumulated error is controlled by choosing

\[
H^r\gtrsim \lambda T\,\operatorname{polylog}T.
\]

An additional inequality pays the rounded extra period. Each fixed padding family then gives

\[
N=\widetilde O\!\left(T^{(\kappa+\zeta)/r}\right).
\]

The limiting step preserves the fixed physical noise intensity. It uses the completed baseline-boost construction: finitely many baseline levels enter each larger-padding family’s threshold, while the actual ascent and descent retain a uniform interface budget. A computable slow choice of families absorbs those finite costs into \(T^{o(1)}\).

Since

\[
\kappa+\zeta
=
1+\frac{\log(CQ_*)}{\log B}
\longrightarrow1,
\]

the installed-register exponent tends to \(1/r\).

### Costs retained in this accounting

| Operation or resource | Charged cost |
|---|---|
| Layout description and enumeration | \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)\) |
| Installed local components and wire segments | \(O(N)\) |
| Known controller preparation | At most \(cw_cQ_*^m\) noisy writes, with declared distributed access |
| Acquisition and delivery | Actual noisy ascent/descent and their error budgets |
| Ancilla creation and disposal | Actual reset channels during execution |
| Waiting, transport, protection, and control | All \(N\) registers charged on every tick |
| Output | Actual constant-size local readout |

The parameter \(T\) is **physical retention time**. A protected kernel costs \(P\) physical ticks per iteration. This is not a claim to execute \(T\) logical gates in \(T\) physical ticks.

## 5. Why fewer static registers cannot suffice

The matching hardware obstruction is short and does not depend on the details of the compiler.

Let \(C\) be the input-reachable component in the graph of all possible ideal local interactions. If there are \(N\) registers and every edge has length at most \(a\), then

\[
\operatorname{radius}(C)
\le c\bigl(aN+\operatorname{diam}(\text{seed})+1\bigr)
=O(N).
\]

A suitable Poisson substream covers this whole component at rate

\[
\nu_C\ge c\lambda N^{d-\alpha}
=c\lambda N^{-r}.
\]

Choose an allowed fault assignment that resets the whole component on those marks and acts identically on all other marks. Erased information is discarded.

Now use a Bell-pair input, with one half retained as the external reference. Before the first reset, local trace-preserving operations cannot change the reference/exterior-apparatus marginal. Pre-existing entanglement among apparatus components does not help: the raw unknown input was independent of apparatus preparation, and no signalling carries it into a disconnected component.

After the reset, every accessible register is independent of the reference. No later operation can restore the input.

The reset probability by time \(T\) is

\[
p=1-e^{-\nu_CT}.
\]

On this branch the output’s Bell overlap is \(1/4\); on the complement it is at most one. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\delta\ge
\frac34\left(1-e^{-c\lambda TN^{-r}}\right).
}
\]

For \(\delta\le1/4\), this forces

\[
N=\Omega\!\left((\lambda T)^{1/r}\right).
\]

Applying the same argument to actual bounding radius gives the diameter bound. Charging installed-register time gives the execution bound.

All persistent input-bearing wires, buffers, accessible environments, and readout storage must be counted. An external cached output or an uncounted long-range carrier changes the operation and the lower-bound model.

## 6. A second foundational result: initialization has an information cost

The most useful accompanying deduction concerns a stronger initialization demand:

> From the same fixed finite prescribed seed, the device must work for every possible state of all its other mutable registers.

This is stronger than noisy preparation of known controller words. It cannot generally be obtained at the same hardware cost.

### Restart-capacity theorem

Consider a time-homogeneous device of Hilbert-space dimension \(D\). Suppose that, for either input bit \(x\), starting from

\[
\sigma_x\otimes\tau
\]

returns \(x\) after \(T\) ticks with error at most \(\delta<1/2\), for **every** outside state \(\tau\) independent of \(x\), under every allowed fault assignment.

Suppose a permitted seed-replacement event occurs independently with probability \(q>0\) per reset-compatible slot.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\log_2D\ge qT\,[1-h_2(\delta)].
}
\]

At \(\delta=1/4\), the coefficient \(1-h_2(\delta)\) is approximately \(0.1887\).

### Proof mechanism

Inject independent fair bits \(X_1,\ldots,X_T\) through allowed seed-replacement faults. Let \(E_i\) indicate whether the replacement occurred at slot \(i\).

Immediately after an injection, the outside state is independent of that fresh bit. The arbitrary-junk guarantee must therefore promise its recovery \(T\) ticks later.

Inspect the entire device at checkpoint \(T\). For every injected bit \(X_i\), a decoder can run the future averaged dynamics for another \(i\) ticks and perform the declared output operation. Thus the checkpoint state is a quantum random-access encoding of the past injections.

Give the event vector \(E\) to the mathematical lower-bound decoder. This supplies no reliable flags to the actual machine. Fano’s inequality and data processing give

\[
I(X_i:Q_T\mid E)\ge q[1-h_2(\delta)].
\]

Conditional independence of the \(X_i\), the entropy chain rule, and the dimension bound then give

\[
\log_2D
\ge I(X_1\cdots X_T:Q_T\mid E)
\ge qT[1-h_2(\delta)].
\]

Individual decoders suffice; simultaneous recovery is unnecessary. The established information-theoretic machinery is the quantum random-access bound of Nayak. The new part is the reduction from autonomous restart semantics to that encoding requirement. 

For a prescribed initialization region of radius \(s\), Pareto marks provide seed replacement at rate proportional to \(\lambda s^{-r}\). With bounded local dimension,

\[
\boxed{Ns^r\ge c\lambda T.}
\]

For fixed \(s\),

\[
N_{\text{arbitrary-junk}}=\Omega(T),
\]

whereas the prepared sparse construction has

\[
N_{\text{prepared}}=T^{1/r+o(1)}=o(T)
\qquad(r>1).
\]

This establishes a polynomial separation **throughout the strict-Pareto regime**.

There is an especially clean specialization for bounded-range bursts:

| Initialization contract | Hardware | All-register execution cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Noisy known-controller preparation and reuse | \(\operatorname{polylog}T\) | \(T\operatorname{polylog}T\) |
| Arbitrary-junk restart: lower bound | \(\Omega(T)\) | \(\Omega(T^2)\) |
| Accepted one-pass restart construction | \(T\operatorname{polylog}T\) | \(T^2\operatorname{polylog}T\) |

Those polynomial exponents match. The exact arbitrary-junk optimum for the full Pareto law remains unresolved.

This theorem does not exclude paid commissioning, a specified typical initial distribution, a growing prescribed region, or a persistent external input source. Each changes the initialization or information-access contract.
