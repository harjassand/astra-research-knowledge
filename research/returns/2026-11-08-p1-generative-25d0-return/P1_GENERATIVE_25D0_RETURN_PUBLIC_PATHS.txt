$$D_r(t):=p_{00}p_{11}-p_{01}p_{10}

\tag{24}$$

This statement retains $\gamma>0$. A concise proof is available:

- Give each edge its own $J_j$. Cutting any edge makes the two sides independent, so $D_r=0$.

- Conjugating $Z$ on one side of an edge changes only that $J_j$’s sign while preserving preparation, measurement and noise.

- Every nonzero Taylor monomial therefore contains $\prod_jJ_j^2$.

- At total degree $2r$, no factor of $\gamma$ can occur. The coefficient can be computed at $\gamma=0$, where it equals $\prod_jJ_j^2$.

An explicit remainder estimate makes the sign certified. With

$$\Lambda=2rJ+2(r+1)\gamma,$$

$$t\le
\min\!\left\{
\frac1{2\Lambda},

\tag{25}$$

$$D_r(t)\ge\tfrac12J^{2r}t^{2r}>0.
\tag{26}$$

Audit verdict

The proposed channel-polymer theorem is valid. I found no flaw in the inclusion–exclusion identity, its use of CPTP exterior evolution, the connected-word tail estimate, or the claimed polynomial-bit compilation of the logarithmic-size kernels.

This supplies a globally consistent approximation in diamond norm, avoiding the extensive absolute-mass factor that invalidated total-order truncation. It does not by itself give efficient prefix contraction or the full sampler.

1. Exact polymer identity

Let the anchored generators $\mathcal A_i(t)$ be genuine GKLS generators supported on fixed declared balls $B_i$. The declarations must remain fixed across the complete schedule.

For nonempty $X$, let $\mathcal K_X$ sum all Dyson words whose anchor-ball overlap graph is connected and whose declared union is exactly $X$. Absolute convergence of the finite-system Dyson expansion permits regrouping. Disjoint components commute and their ordered integrals combine by the shuffle identity. Consequently,

$$\Phi_V
=
\sum_{\mathcal F\ {\rm disjoint}}
\left(\bigotimes_{X\in\mathcal F}\mathcal K_X\right)
\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{V\setminus\cup\mathcal F}.
\tag{1}$$

Define $Q_L$ by retaining every family whose members all have $|X|\le L$.

For a union $U$, let $\Phi^{\setminus U}$ be the actual evolution generated by those entire anchored generators whose declared balls avoid $U$. It is CPTP on $V\setminus U$.

Then

$$Q_L-\Phi_V
=
\sum_{\substack{\varnothing\ne\mathcal B\ {\rm disjoint}\\
|X|>L\;\forall X\in\mathcal B}}
(-1)^{|\mathcal B|}
\left(\bigotimes_{X\in\mathcal B}\mathcal K_X\right)
\otimes\Phi^{\setminus\cup\mathcal B}.
\tag{2}$$

To verify (2), expand each exterior channel using (1). For any full polymer family $\mathcal F$, its coefficient on the right is

$$\sum_{\varnothing\ne\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal F_{\rm large}}
(-1)^{|\mathcal B|},$$

which equals $-1$ when $\mathcal F$ contains a large polymer and $0$ otherwise. Those are exactly the coefficients of $Q_L-\Phi_V$.

A necessary implementation detail is that deleting an anchored generator may also remove its native replacement term on an exterior site near the boundary. This causes no problem: the surviving generator is still a sum of GKLS generators and hence defines a CPTP evolution. One must not instead delete selected pieces while retaining an inconsistent kernel definition.

2. Global diamond error

Put

$$A_L=\sum_{|X|>L}\|\mathcal K_X\|_\diamond.$$

Every exterior channel in (2) has diamond norm $1$. Therefore

$$\begin{aligned}
\|Q_L-\Phi_V\|_\diamond
&\le
\sum_{\varnothing\ne\mathcal B\ {\rm disjoint,\ large}}
\prod_{X\in\mathcal B}\|\mathcal K_X\|_\diamond\\
&\le e^{A_L}-1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3}$$

The absence of an $e^{\Theta(n\nu T)}$ exterior factor here is justified by the exact CPTP resummation. It is not an assumed cancellation.

3. Connected-word count

Let each ball have at most $s$ sites, covering multiplicity at most $h$, and
$\|\mathcal A_i(t)\|_\diamond\le\nu$. Set

$$q=e\,s h\,\nu T.$$

For $r\ge2$, the number of connected ordered $r$-tuples of anchors is at most

$$n\,r^{r-2}(sh)^{r-1}.$$

Choose a labelled spanning tree, choose its root anchor, and extend each tree edge using at most $sh$ overlapping anchors. Repeated anchors are allowed and covered.

The time-ordered integral contributes $T^r/r!$. Thus the total norm contribution of connected order-$r$ words is bounded by

$$n\,\frac{r^{r-2}}{r!}(sh)^{r-1}(\nu T)^r
\le \frac ns q^r.
\tag{4}$$

The same loose bound holds separately for $r=1$.

Since a word of length $r$ has union size at most $sr$,

$$A_L
\le
\frac ns
\frac{q^{\lfloor L/s\rfloor+1}}{1-q}.
\tag{5}$$

At

$$\nu T\le\frac1{12sh},$$

we have $q\le e/12<1/4$, and

$$S_0:=\sum_{X\ne\varnothing}\|\mathcal K_X\|_\diamond
\le\frac ns\frac q{1-q}
\le\frac n{3s}.
\tag{6}$$

For $0<\xi\le1$, the proposed choice

$$L=s\left\lceil\log_4\frac{16n}{\xi}\right\rceil
\tag{7}$$

has ample slack: (5) gives $A_L\le\xi/(48s)$. Equations (3) and (7) therefore leave room for the stated numerical compilation error.

4. Kernel compiler

For $Y\subseteq X$, compute the finite-patch channel $\Phi_Y$ retaining exactly those anchors whose entire declared balls lie in $Y$. Define

$$A_S=\sum_{Y\subseteq S}
(-1)^{|S|-|Y|}
\Phi_Y\otimes\operatorname{Id}_{S\setminus Y}.
\tag{8}$$

The inclusion–exclusion in (8) extracts all Dyson words whose declared union is exactly $S$.

In the disjoint-subset tensor algebra,

$$A=\exp_*(K),\qquad K=\log_*(A).$$

This can be compiled in $\exp(O(m))$, rather than Bell-number time, on an $m$-site patch. Fix $i\in S$. Selecting the component containing $i$ gives

$$A_S=
\sum_{\substack{C\subseteq S\\i\in C}}
K_C\otimes A_{S\setminus C}.$$

Hence

$$K_S=A_S-
\sum_{\substack{C\subsetneq S\\i\in C}}
K_C\otimes A_{S\setminus C}.
\tag{9}$$

Subset dynamic programming evaluates (9) with $O(3^m)$ subset operations, each acting on matrices of exponential-in-$m$ size.

The finite patch evolution can be evaluated by rational Taylor or scaling-and-squaring methods. Because its total integrated generator norm is $O(m)$ in the stated short-time regime, obtaining $\beta$ accuracy bits costs

$$\exp(O_{\rm class}(m))\,
\operatorname{poly}(B,\beta).$$

The complete number of schedule segments is paid. No assumption of a fixed common frequency set is needed.

Möbius inversion and (9) may amplify internal numerical errors, but a crude bound needs only $O(m^2)$ additional precision bits. They do not force superexponential arithmetic work.

5. Exact trace preservation after numerical compilation

Each nonempty exact kernel is Hermiticity-preserving and trace-annihilating. Trace-annihilation follows directly from every nonempty Dyson word, or from (8)–(9).

For an approximate kernel, use

$$\widehat K_X
\leftarrow
\operatorname{HP}(\widehat K_X)
-
\mathcal R_X\circ\operatorname{HP}(\widehat K_X).
\tag{10}$$

This makes the kernel exactly Hermiticity-preserving and trace-annihilating, and increases its diamond error by at most a factor $2$.

Consequently the assembled $\widehat Q_L$ is exactly HP and TP, although it need not be CP.

If there are $M$ kernel maps and the pre-projection map error is at most

$$\delta=\frac{\xi\,2^{-2n}}{64M},$$

the total kernel error after (10) is at most

$$E=\frac{\xi\,2^{-2n}}{32}.$$

A telescoping bound over polymer families gives

$$\|\widehat Q_L-Q_L\|_\diamond
\le E\,e^{S_0+E}.
\tag{11}$$

Using (6), the chosen precision safely compensates this factor. Its bit length is $O(n+\log M+\log(1/\xi))$, hence polynomial.

If “coefficient error” refers to individual printed matrix entries rather than map diamond error, include the usual exponential-in-$m$ dimension conversion. This costs only $O(m)$ additional bits and does not change the result.

6. What this establishes for the generative problem

The compiler produces a polynomial-size, finite-rational, HP/TP channel description $\widehat Q_L$ with

$$\|\widehat Q_L-\Phi_V\|_\diamond\le\xi.$$

For every supplied input state and finite product POVM, its induced signed law is exactly normalized and is within $L^1$ distance $\xi$ of the true positive output law. Its total negative mass is therefore at most $\xi/2$.

That is a genuine global approximation theorem. It resums arbitrarily many disconnected small contributions and survives arbitrary preparation and POVM boundaries at the error-certification level.

The remaining task is computational: evaluating the necessary prefixes of this single signed law in polynomial bit work. The polynomial number of printed kernels does not make the 2D disjoint-family contraction polynomial automatically. The accepted signed-law sampling theorem can be applied once those contractions are supplied, but the present audit does not establish them.

Final response

The ordinary algorithmic-cooling route cannot supply arbitrarily pure, known-basis physical ancillas at arbitrarily weak nonunital strength and one fixed $g$. There is a native local-relaxation obstruction, stronger than merely observing that the Shtanko–Sharma threshold depends jointly on noise and fixed-point purity.

This does not exclude encoded or outcome-flagged resources, and it is not a negative resolution of sampling.

1. The relevant native bound

Let $A$ be any traceless one-qubit Hermitian contraction. The accepted local relaxation and perturbation-stability machinery gives computable functions/constants, depending only on $a,k,d,g$, such that

$$\boxed{
\left|\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[A\rho^\eta_T\right]\right|
\le f_g(\gamma T)+C_g\eta,
\qquad
f_g(s)\longrightarrow0.
}
\tag{1}$$

The bound is uniform in:

- the reservoir/system size;

- the location of the output qubit;

- the allowed piecewise Hamiltonian and perturbation schedules;

- the duration;

- the initial preparation.

In fact, the argument allows arbitrary initial states, which is stronger than the required product preparations.

For clarity about the scope, this bound is available directly from the native all-site replacement and locality; it does not assume a stationary reference for the perturbed dynamics.

Why the constants are finite at every fixed $g$

For the unperturbed evolution on a finite $N$-qubit patch,

$$\mathcal L_0(t)=-i[H(t),\cdot]+\gamma\sum_i(E_i-\mathrm{Id}),$$

relative entropy to the maximally mixed state obeys

$$D(\rho_t\Vert I/2^N)
\le e^{-\gamma t}D(\rho_0\Vert I/2^N)
\le N\log2\,e^{-\gamma t}.
\tag{2}$$

The Hamiltonian contributes zero to this entropy derivative. For replacement noise, the entropy-production bound follows from

$$\sum_iD(\rho\Vert E_i\rho)
\ge D(\rho\Vert I/2^N).$$

Truncate the backward evolution of $A$ to a Lieb–Robinson ball of radius $O_g(1+s)$, where $s=\gamma t$. Its area is $O_g((1+s)^2)$. Pinsker’s inequality applied to (2), together with the locality error, gives an operator-norm local-relaxation bound of the form

$$f_g(s)\le A_g(1+s)e^{-s/2}+A'_g e^{-c_gs},
\tag{3}$$

with computable class constants. Exact numerical choices are not important for the cooling conclusion; the integrable decay is.

For the perturbation, the appropriate Duhamel identity is

$$\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{Tr}A\bigl(\rho^\eta_T-\rho^0_T\bigr)\\
&\quad=
\gamma\int_0^T
\sum_z\eta_z(t)\,
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
\rho^\eta_t\,
\mathcal D_z(t)^*
\Phi_0(T,t)^*(A)
\right]dt .
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}$$

Every GKLS generator satisfies $\mathcal D_z^*(I)=0$. Near the backward light cone, local relaxation bounds its contribution; outside it, locality bounds it. Summing the two-dimensional shells produces a bound of the form

$$\sum_z
\left\|\mathcal D_z(t)^*
\Phi_0(T,t)^*(A)\right\|_\infty
\le
C'_g(1+s)^3e^{-c'_gs},
\qquad s=\gamma(T-t).
\tag{5}$$

Hence

$$C_g
=
\int_0^\infty C'_g(1+s)^3e^{-c'_gs}\,ds
<\infty
\tag{6}$$

is computable and independent of volume and duration. This establishes (1) with general local GKLS perturbations and arbitrary allowed schedules.

2. Consequence for a physical-ancilla refrigerator

For a one-qubit reduced state, (1) implies

$$\boxed{
\lambda_{\max}(\rho_i^\eta(T))
\le
\frac{1+f_g(\gamma T)+C_g\eta}{2}.
}
\tag{7}$$

Suppose a refrigerator is intended to supply a known-basis ancilla with error at most $p_{\rm anc}<1/2$, so its required polarization is

$$b_{\rm anc}=1-2p_{\rm anc}>0.$$

Choose

$$\eta<\frac{b_{\rm anc}}{4C_g}$$

and a sufficiently late time satisfying

$$f_g(\gamma T)<\frac{b_{\rm anc}}4.$$

Then

$$\lambda_{\max}(\rho_i^\eta(T))
<
\frac12+\frac{b_{\rm anc}}4
<
1-p_{\rm anc}.
\tag{8}$$

Increasing the reservoir size or allowing an $\eta$-dependent, arbitrarily large constant overhead does not evade this bound. It is uniform in both. Bounded-range coherent controls at fixed $g$ cannot collect weak polarization rapidly enough to produce an unconditional high-purity physical output at late global times.

Pure ancillas supplied at time zero remain legitimate initial resources. The bound concerns their availability later, after they have been exposed to the native noise. A stock initialized at time zero cannot simply be treated as a fresh stock when used.

For the simplest permitted biased-reset source,

$$\mathcal D=\tfrac12(\mathcal R_0-\mathrm{Id}),$$

the isolated equilibrium polarization is

$$z_*=\frac{\eta}{2+\eta}.$$

This makes the purity loss as $\eta\to0$ explicit, but (7) additionally controls arbitrary allowed interacting cooling circuits.

3. What remains possible—and therefore unproved

The deduction rules out a specific proposed interface:

At one fixed $g$, use arbitrarily weak local nonunitality and an arbitrarily large reservoir to replenish unconditionally near-pure physical ancillas in known bases at late times.

It does not rule out:

- logical ancillas encoded across many physical qubits whose individual marginals are nearly maximally mixed;

- conditional purity accompanied by a classical or quantum record;

- special nonadaptive computations that tolerate uncorrected frames and decode only at the final classical output;

- an $\eta=0$ robust two-dimensional sampling-hardness family.

Those alternatives would require a separate native construction. In particular, an ensemble of pure states with different recorded Pauli frames can have an unconditionally maximally mixed marginal, so (7) must not be advertised as ruling out every flagged factory.

The familiar refrigerator-based fault-tolerance theorem therefore cannot be extended to the present quantifiers merely by allowing a larger $\eta$-dependent constant overhead. At fixed $g$, sufficiently small $\eta$ encounters the genuine physical-output bound above. A complete hardness argument would have to bypass that physical-ancilla interface, rather than assume it.

No new source access or held-original recovery was needed for this check.
