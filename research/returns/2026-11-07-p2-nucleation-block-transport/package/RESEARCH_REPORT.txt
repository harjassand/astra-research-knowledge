MICROSCOPIC CONDITIONAL BLOCKS AND THE NUCLEATION BOTTLENECK
7 November 2026

STATUS

The governing two-dimensional nucleation predictor remains UNRESOLVED.
This return constructs an exact conditional microscopic operation, gives its
physical comparison and full arithmetic cost, and proves a quantitative
obstruction to using the resulting block dynamics as a direct rare-transition
simulator. It does not construct the significant global critical ensemble,
relative capacity, or global physical-residence certificate. The chosen full
endpoint is retained. This is not a claim of a completed broader breakthrough.

1. GOVERNING ENDPOINT AND THE SCIENTIFIC MOVE

The physical system is the ferromagnetic nearest-neighbor Ising model on the
periodic square lattice with n=ell^2 explicitly counted sites. Its rational
microscopic parameters are

  u=exp(-2 theta), v=exp(-2 phi), 0<u<=1/4096, u^2<v<1,
  theta=beta J, phi=beta h, R=theta/phi=log(u)/log(v).

For a spin configuration sigma, its unnormalized weight is

  w(sigma)=u^(number of disagreeing edges) v^(number of minus spins).

Each site has its own rate-one proposal clock. When site i is proposed, its
spin is resampled from the exact Gibbs conditional. The actual flip rate is
r_i/(1+r_i), where r_i=u^(sigma_i sum_{j~i}sigma_j) v^(sigma_i).
Initial state a is all minus. The actual target is B={sum_i sigma_i>=0},
which has a polynomial-time membership test and is not supplied as an
exponential list. All relative and physical-time statements use these clocks.

The growing-nucleus class retains CR<=ell<=R^p, p>1 fixed, at every declared
fixed admissible u, with all extra source-theorem conditions on temperature,
core cutoff and C. In particular, the positive-field core, regular eight-arc
loop construction, open-interface results and bounded-lobe repairs keep their
different hypotheses. Their temperature conditions are not interchangeable.

The desired output remains a confidence-certified relative mean interval and
an additive-accuracy CDF operation, with total work polynomial in the complete
explicit input, epsilon^-1, log(delta^-1), and binary query-time length. It
must avoid exp(c R) work at fixed accuracy. No part of the new strip calculation
replaces this target or makes the full lattice implicit.

The next operation investigated here is resampling a supplied region while
retaining EVERY microscopic spin configuration in that region. The order of
the vertices is also supplied. If only a small number of internal edges cross
every prefix of this order, both the conditional normalization and a physical
transport comparison can be constructed. This avoids assuming a single-valued
interface, but it does not acquire the distribution of the exterior or the
distribution of globally important transition channels.

The mathematical ingredients are classical transfer/dynamic programming and
canonical-path comparison. In particular, Berger--Kenyon--Mossel--Peres,
Proposition 1.1 and Section 2.1, already develop Ising relaxation bounds using
cutwidth. Diaconis--Saloff-Coste supply the general reversible comparison
framework. The exchange calculation also specializes the accepted averaged
two-copy transport in the governing Astra material. No historical-priority
claim is made for those ideas or for this implementation.

2. EXACT CONDITIONAL OPERATION

2.1 Input and what is returned

Let S have m explicitly listed vertices, ordered 1,...,m. Every internal edge
and every edge from S to a fixed exterior spin is listed. Internal degree plus
boundary-edge count is at most four. The activities u=p/q and v=r/s are reduced
positive rationals with u,v<=1. Exterior spins may have any pattern; their
induced local fields can have both signs.

Let c be the maximum number of INTERNAL edges crossing an ordering prefix.
Let f be the maximum number of processed vertices still adjacent to an
unprocessed vertex. Both are computed from the given edge list, and f<=c.
No minimum-cutwidth or optimal-ordering oracle is used.

The operation returns the exact conditional partition sum, a conditional
sampler with an explicit random-bit/error contract, and, if requested, all
magnetization-resolved partition coefficients. Consequently it also returns
the exact normalizer for any explicitly supplied rational table of umbrella
weights indexed by the plus-spin count. The latter is a static calculation;
no mixing theorem for an umbrella or Kawasaki process is asserted.

2.2 Integer normalization and recurrence

Assign every internal edge to its later endpoint, and every boundary edge to
its endpoint in S. When processing vertex k, let t_k be the number of assigned
edges and d_k the number that disagree with its proposed spin. The local integer
factor is

  a_k=p^d_k q^(t_k-d_k) r, if the spin is minus;
  a_k=p^d_k q^(t_k-d_k) s, if the spin is plus.

Let e be the number of internal and boundary edges, each counted once. The
common denominator is D=q^e s^m. The product of the local factors is exactly
D times the conditional Gibbs weight, with the exterior's constant omitted.
That omission is legitimate because the exterior is fixed, not averaged.

At each step retain the spins on the current frontier. For frontier state z,

  M_k(z)=sum_{b=0,1} a_k(z,b) M_{k+1}(next(z,b)),
  M_m(empty)=1.

Past neighbors of vertex k are all still in the frontier, so every factor is
known. A processed spin is removed only when it has no future neighbor.
The exact normalizer is M_0(empty)/D.

For the magnetization refinement, attach a formal factor X to a plus-spin
choice and retain the coefficient array of each message. The root polynomial
is sum_j Z_j X^j, where Z_j is the INTEGER weight of all states with j plus
spins. Every coefficient is nonnegative, and sum_j Z_j=M_0(empty).

This recurrence sums all configurations. It includes holes, islands, multiple
interfaces, overhangs and contacts inside S. It imposes no smoothness, contour
regularity, or monotonicity condition on the spins in S. Conversely, it says
nothing about which regions or exterior states carry the global critical
current in the original lattice.

2.3 Bit and random-bit costs

Because p<=q and r<=s, every integer configuration weight is at most D. Hence
all messages and coefficients fit within

  B_*=1+m+e bit_length(q)+m bit_length(s)

bits. This is a conservative bound; e<=4m. Intermediate arithmetic includes
these bit lengths. Exponentially small probabilities are represented by
polynomial-length integers, rather than estimated by waiting for an event.

The scalar recurrence has at most (m+1)2^f messages. The magnetization
recurrence has O(m^2 2^f) integer coefficient entries. With schoolbook integer
arithmetic, O(m^2 2^c B_*^2) bit work is a conservative arithmetic estimate,
apart from polynomial indexing and input parsing. For a complete binary
encoding of length I, including the region, ordering, boundary pins, activities
and any umbrella table, the entire construction and one b-bit-per-site sample
can be implemented within

  2^(c+20) (I+b+2)^10 bit operations.

This intentionally loose bound covers indexing, arithmetic, rational reduction,
and the extra coefficient table. It is exponential in the SUPPLIED c. A
polynomial claim requires c=O(log I) with its constant stated; it is not a
uniform polynomial algorithm for arbitrary regions. No full exterior boundary
law is hidden in the input. If the boundary is extracted from a full n-site
state, reading and storing that state are charged, and the appropriate local
messages are recomputed when the exterior changes.

At each step the two branch weights are known integers. The exact sampler
draws an integer uniformly below their sum by binary rejection, then selects
the corresponding branch. Each rejection trial succeeds with probability at
least 1/2. It has polynomial expected bit work. The probability that any of
the m stages requires more than k rejection trials is at most m 2^-k. This
statement is an expected-work/tail contract, not a fixed worst-case exact
random-bit bound.

The fixed-budget sampler instead uses b independent fair bits per site and
rounds the branch probability upward to a multiple of 2^-b. Its total-variation
error is at most m 2^-b by sequential coupling. Choosing
b=ceil(log2(m/eta)) gives error at most eta with a fixed mb-bit budget.
Conditioning on an exact plus count uses the appropriate coefficient in each
message. The count is then satisfied exactly; the same TV bound applies
WITHIN that conditional law even when the conditioning event is extremely
rare. It does not divide its accuracy by the event probability.

Code uses an explicit random-bit provider. Seeded Python random is used only
for reproducible implementation checks. It is not asserted to be a physical
source of ideal randomness. For N approximate block updates with per-update
TV error eta, path-law error is at most N eta. All exact-chain comparisons
below refer to the exact conditional law, with this correction added when the
finite-bit implementation is used. Conditioning a truncated exact sampler on
successful termination would not, without additional argument, preserve the
unbiased law; no such claim is made.

3. PHYSICAL RELAXATION AND TRANSPORT

3.1 Exchange identity

Let mu be the normalized conditional Gibbs law on S. For x,y and a prefix I,
form z by taking y on I and x off I, and form z' by the complementary exchange.
Every unary factor, including all exterior interactions and the positive
physical field, cancels in

  W(x)W(y) / [W(z)W(z')].

Every internal edge not crossing the prefix also cancels. For each crossing
edge, the difference in total disagreement count is between -2 and 2.
Therefore

  W(x)W(y) <= K W(z)W(z'),   K=u^(-2c).

This statement concerns all pairs x,y. It has no dependence on a worst-case
field magnitude and no restriction to a particular contour family.

3.2 Canonical-path bound with the actual clocks

Route x to y by changing the differing coordinates in the supplied order.
The path has length at most m. For a fixed directed edge z->z^i, the companion
configuration reconstructs the pair (x,y) injectively. Applying the exchange
bound before and after the crossing gives

  sum_{(x,y): path uses z->z^i} mu(x)mu(y)
     <= K min(mu(z),mu(z^i)).

The physical conditional heat-bath conductance of the undirected edge is

  g(z,z^i)=mu(z)mu(z^i)/(mu(z)+mu(z^i))
          >= (1/2)min(mu(z),mu(z^i)).

Use Var_mu f=(1/2)sum_{x,y}mu(x)mu(y)(f(x)-f(y))^2 and Cauchy--Schwarz along
each path. Counting both orientations yields

  Var_mu f <= Gamma_S E_S(f),   Gamma_S=2m u^(-2c).             (1)

Here E_S is the physical Dirichlet form with a rate-one clock at EACH site
of S. Thus the physical inverse spectral gap is at most Gamma_S. There is no
extra factor of m from a one-clock-for-the-whole-block convention.

This is a constructive specialization of classical cutwidth/canonical-path
machinery. It is not a newly claimed general Ising mixing result. The explicit
constant is useful for keeping this return's normalization checkable.

One can also lift an explicitly supplied block jump current to physical edges.
Split its current equally over the forward canonical path and the reverse of
the backward canonical path. Each path has length at most m. The same
congestion bound controls the lifted electrical energy by Gamma_S times the
block-current energy. Listing those paths costs O(m) per supplied jump.
They are electrical comparison paths, not physical sample trajectories. No
exponential global current table has been acquired by describing this lift.

3.3 Conditional target time

For any H in the block with mu(H)>=alpha>0, a function vanishing on H has
Var_mu f>=alpha ||f||_2^2. Thus the killed gap is at least alpha/Gamma_S.
The survival bound from a deterministic x is

  P_x(tau_H>t) <= min(1,mu(x)^(-1/2) exp[-alpha t/Gamma_S]).

Integration and mu(x)>=2^(-B_*) give

  E_x tau_H <= (Gamma_S/alpha)[1+(B_*/2)log 2].                (2)

For a count-defined H, alpha is computed exactly by the coefficient table.
An exponentially small alpha is PAID through alpha^-1 in this physical-time
bound. The exact conditional sampler does not imply a cheap physical hitting
time. Equation (2) also does not apply to a newly constrained dynamics on a
magnetization slice without a separate proof for that generator.

3.4 Long thin regions

A strip of length L and width w, periodic along its length, with its two
exterior rows fixed, has c<=2w+1 under a column ordering and frontier size
at most 2w. Hence it admits the exact operation above with 2^O(w) digital
cost and

  Gamma_S <= 2Lw u^(-4w-2).

For w<=d log2 n, fixed d and fixed u, both are polynomial in the explicitly
counted input. For example, u^(-2c)<=n^(2d' log2(1/u)) when c<=d'log2 n.
The exponent depends on temperature and may be large. There is no practical
performance guarantee for the physical comparison bound.

The region must be SUPPLIED with its actual exterior spins. The result does
not prove that a logarithmic-width region contains a typical critical contour,
or that a polynomial list of such regions covers all significant currents.

4. A GLOBAL COMPARISON THAT DOES NOT LOSE EXCEPTIONAL CONFIGURATIONS

Let a fixed, explicitly listed family of blocks S have nonnegative rational
clocks rho_S. Add exact conditional Gibbs resampling of each S to the original
single-site generator. The auxiliary dynamics is reversible for the full
original Gibbs law and is attractive. Its kinetics are different from the
physical kinetics.

Conditioning (1) on every possible exterior configuration gives

  E_phys(f) <= E_aug(f) <= Lambda E_phys(f),
  Lambda=1+max_i sum_{S containing i} rho_S Gamma_S.             (3)

This comparison holds on the ENTIRE spin space, not just a regular-interface
sector. Boundary fields were already included in (1). A selector that depends
arbitrarily on the current configuration is not covered: fixed blocks and
clocks are used so reversibility and the comparison are actually established.

The usual capacity and killed-operator comparisons follow. They do NOT imply
pointwise comparison of every mean hitting time or CDF. In particular,
operator inverse order controls quadratic forms, whereas a point-start mean
is a cross-vector expression. The probability argument below handles the
minimal initial state and increasing target directly.

5. PHYSICAL-CAPACITY BOUND TO A PROPOSAL-COUNT OBSTRUCTION

5.1 Keep the accepted capacity envelope, including its source-clock term

Let A=C_r be the accepted downward-closed thermal core, Z_A=sum_A w, and
C_kappa the physical soft capacity to the actual B. The core retains the
accepted conditions 1<=r<=R, r<ell/4 and its fixed augmented-component
convention. Define the quantities in the accepted bounded-residence source:

  P_*=ceil(4R)-8,
  q_c=8 exp(-3 theta/2),
  q_5=4 exp[-theta(2-sqrt(5)/2)],
  eta_r=6n q_5^(2r)/(1-q_5),
  z=4 exp(-5 theta/4),
  m_c=(2/3) sum_{P>=4} P z^P
     =(2/3) z^4(4-3z)/(1-z)^2,
  D_B=exp[-phi(ceil(n/2)-1-n m_c)].

Under the accepted capacity theorem's conditions, in particular eta_r<1,
write its bound as

  C_kappa/Z_A <= a_cap + b_cap kappa, for every kappa>0,       (4)
  a_cap=[(8n)^10 q_c^P_* /(1-q_c) + n D_B]/(1-eta_r),
  b_cap=D_B/(1-eta_r).

This is the previously accepted upper bound, not a new relative capacity
construction. Both the n term and the kappa term remain. The entropy
subtraction in its leading exponent remains 4 log 8. If r is selected to make
eta_r bounded away from one and C is sufficiently large under the source
conditions, a_cap and b_cap are polynomial factors times exponentially small
quantities in R. A sufficient additional condition for the displayed volume
term to be no larger on the leading exponential scale is

  theta C^2(1/2-m_c) > 6 theta-4 log 8.

This condition is IN ADDITION TO the original capacity theorem's hypotheses;
it does not relax any source assumption on C, theta or the geometry.

5.2 Rescale the source clock in the comparison

For any reversible dynamics with the same weights, soft capacity is

  C_kappa=min_{f|B=0} [E_w(f)+kappa sum_A w(1-f)^2].

The core can intersect B; its contribution there is simply the constant
source penalty. The accepted definition and identity remain valid.
Equation (3), with the source clock rescaled, gives the useful inequality

  C_kappa^aug <= Lambda C_(kappa/Lambda)^phys.                 (5)

Combining (4) and (5) gives

  C_kappa^aug/(kappa Z_A) <= Lambda a_cap/kappa + b_cap.       (6)

The b_cap term is not multiplied by Lambda. Using only a same-clock
comparison would unnecessarily lose this fact and obscure the clock-cost
calculation that follows.

Let q_kappa(x) be the probability of reaching B before a rate-kappa clock
running only while the chain is in A. Equivalently,

  q_kappa(x)=E_x exp[-kappa integral_0^tau_B 1_A(X_s) ds].

Attractiveness, A decreasing and B increasing imply that q_kappa is increasing.
Thus at the actual all-minus initial state a,

  q_kappa(a) <= E_mu_A q_kappa
             =C_kappa^aug/(kappa Z_A).

On {tau_B<=t}, the exponential inside the expectation is at least exp(-kappa t).
For t>0 choose kappa=1/t. This proves the FULL-SPACE inequality

  P_a^aug(tau_B<=t) <= min(1, e[Lambda a_cap t+b_cap]).        (7)

For the original physical chain, take Lambda=1. This is a genuine one-sided
CDF bound for the actual target and initialization. Around the unknown mean
it need not be accurate and can be vacuous. It is not the requested complete
additive-epsilon CDF operation.

5.3 Count update proposals, not a freely chosen clock speed

The total rate of proposals in the augmented process is

  R_prop=n+sum_S rho_S.

Let Gamma_max=max(1,max_S Gamma_S). Equation (3) gives
Lambda/R_prop<=Gamma_max. This holds even for exponentially large rational
auxiliary clock values; their encoding does not create free computational
updates.

Let H_N be the probability of hitting B within N proposals, N>=1. Uniformize
the augmented chain at R_prop. At time t=4N/R_prop, the independent Poisson
proposal count has mean 4N and has probability at least 1/2 of being >=N.
For example, Chebyshev bounds its lower-tail probability by 4/(9N)<1/2.
Consequently H_N<=2 P_a^aug(tau_B<=t). Using (7),

  H_N <= min(1,8e Gamma_max a_cap N+2e b_cap)
      <= min(1,24 Gamma_max a_cap N+6 b_cap).                 (8)

This is independent of arbitrary clock inflation. If b_cap<=1/24, observing
one transition with probability at least 1/2 requires

  N >= 1/(96 Gamma_max a_cap).

For fixed temperature and c=O(log n), Gamma_max is polynomial. Under (4)'s
growing-volume suppression, the required proposal count is exponential in R.
Repeated direct restarts from all minus obey the corresponding union bound.
For finite-bit updates add the accumulated path-law error, at most N eta.
This cannot be reinterpreted as physical transitions caused by rounding error.

5.4 Skipping null updates does not remove the R dependence

The physical parameter contract u^2<v<1 gives the uniform conditional bound

  d_0=u^6/(1+u^6)>0

for the probability of changing any proposed site. An exact conditional block
resampling also changes the current state with probability at least d_0:
select any one site in the block, condition on all the newly sampled other
spins, apply the same single-site bound, then average. Therefore the augmented
exit rate from every configuration is at least d_0 R_prop.

Let H'_J be the probability of hitting B within J state-changing updates.
For this time comparison, continue the unabsorbed chain after its first hit.
The time of the Jth such update is stochastically bounded by a sum of J
independent exponentials of rate d_0 R_prop. Its probability of exceeding
2[J+log(1/zeta)]/(d_0 R_prop) is at most zeta, by the exponential-moment
bound at half that rate. Equation (7) yields

  H'_J <= min(1,
     (2e Gamma_max a_cap/d_0)[J+log(1/zeta)] + e b_cap+zeta). (9)

At fixed u, d_0 is a fixed positive constant, explicitly paid. Thus exact
skipping of null proposals does not turn this direct simulation into a
polynomial witness operation as R grows. This conclusion still concerns the
stated simulation family, not arbitrary computational prediction.

5.5 Explicit microscopic failure family and scope

Fix an admissible integer b>=6, u=2^(-2b), and a sufficiently large fixed
integer D satisfying the retained volume/capacity conditions. For k increasing
set v_k=1-2^-k and ell_k=D 2^k. Then

  R_k=2b log 2/[-log(1-2^-k)]=Theta(2^k),
  n_k=D^2 4^k=Theta(R_k^2).

Choose D with D/(2b log 2)>C; for every fixed p>1, ell_k<=R_k^p for all
sufficiently large k. Explicitly list the n_k lattice sites; this input is
polynomial in R_k, rather than a binary declaration of an implicit huge
lattice. One explicit cutoff is r_k=b+4k+4 ceil(log2(D+1)); for all sufficiently
large k it lies in the accepted range and eta_r<1/2. Equation (8) then rules
out polynomially many direct
proposals as a constant-probability way to witness nucleation for any fixed
polynomially specified block family with logarithmic cutwidth, polynomial
Gamma_max, and any nonnegative rational clocks. Equation (9) gives the same
conclusion after null-update skipping.

The failed claim is that these exactly normalized conditional block updates
by themselves remove the exponential nucleation waiting problem in the full
microscopic model. This is not a lower bound for importance sampling,
splitting, nonreversible dynamics, biased ensembles, adaptive methods outside
the stated comparison, deterministic weighted computation, or all algorithms
that predict the mean and CDF. No general impossibility follows.

6. IMPLEMENTATION AND WHAT THE FINITE CHECKS ESTABLISH

block_transport.py implements the exact integer recurrence, magnetization
coefficients, supplied umbrella normalizers, both sampling contracts, and
rational comparison constants. Its final two processors evaluate (7) and (8)
with e<3 from already valid rational upper bounds on a_cap and b_cap. They do
not acquire an unspecified capacity or residence certificate.

validate_transport.py performs four bounded checks:

* On an eight-site periodic strip with mixed frozen exterior, all 256 states
  are enumerated. Exact normalizers, all count coefficients, and an explicit
  umbrella normalizer match. The complete finite-bit output distribution is
  evaluated exactly, including on the four-plus slice. The observed TV errors
  are 0.0030041845 and 0.0013960219, below the proved 8/1024 bound.

* All 65,536 ordered configuration pairs and all nine exchange positions are
  checked, giving 589,824 exact exchange checks. Directed canonical-path loads
  and the resulting energy comparison are checked as rational inequalities.
  This validates this finite implementation; the general proof is Section 3.

* On a six-site cylinder with fixed exterior, exact 22-state killed and soft
  linear systems compare physical dynamics to three two-site block updates.
  Detailed balance, capacity order, rescaled initial escape control and the
  source mean lower bound hold exactly. The augmented/physical capacity ratio
  is about 1.3487473. This is not a growing-volume torus calculation.

* A 384-site strip, length 128 and width 3, at u=1/4096, v=255/256 is represented
  by 24,168 scalar messages. The integer normalizer has 12,723 bits. A separate
  48-site strip has 63,102 coefficient entries and samples its exactly
  24-plus conditional law without waiting for that event, whose unconditioned
  probability is about 2.3783e-7. Fixed-bit TV budgets and exact counts are
  checked. These are frozen-exterior representation checks. They do not
  identify a critical arrival law or simulate actual nucleation.

Observed machine timings are recorded only for reproducibility. They do not
establish a practical rare-event speedup. The proof and the complete input
contract, not passing finite tests or representing 2^384 states, determine
the mathematical scope.

7. THE CENTRAL OPERATION STILL NEEDED

The global comparison and the capacity-based bound are valid across all
configurations, but neither constructs the globally normalized transition
ensemble. Exact sums for a supplied region condition on a known exterior;
critical exterior configurations, multiple crossings, long junctions, remote
contacts, and repeated principal-hole changes are not sampled or integrated
out by that conditioning operation.

Equation (8) rules out using the new block operation merely as an unbiased
fast-forward simulator. A weighted computation could still avoid waiting.
To earn the retained predictor, that computation must construct the relevant
physical capacity to relative accuracy, and the physical occupation/mean
information needed through the actual target. On the retained soft-source
route this means valid bounds for C_kappa/Z_A, Q_kappa=N_kappa/C_kappa, and
GLOBAL residence outside the core, with their acquisition and all error costs
polynomial. Equivalently, another representation may compute the physical
mean and law directly; every-increasing-path voltage control remains optional.

The existing regular eight-arc law is a useful acquired component. Its
normalizer cannot stand in for the sum over all significant unrestricted
critical channels without a coverage theorem and paid correction. The
bounded-lobe currents similarly keep their geometric hypotheses. A polynomial
local conditional computation is not that missing coverage theorem. Nor does
the one-sided CDF upper bound certify the CDF near its transition scale.

The accepted residence-to-law theorem and rational mean/CDF processor are
unchanged. Their NBUE, equilibrium-transform and Stein foundations retain
credit, including Pekoz--Rollin. The complete supplied-kinetics, finite-target-
list chain predictor is unchanged. Unrestricted exact QUBO and the previously
open effective-response endpoint retain their unresolved status.

The fixed-temperature weak-field literature supplies major metastability and
Wulff results, including Schonmann--Shlosman's infinite-volume logarithmic
relaxation scale. That theorem is not a finite-volume, relative-mean,
arbitrary-accuracy algorithm. Likewise, general-graph fixed-magnetization
hardness results do not establish hardness for this homogeneous square torus.
Neither statement is used to declare the retained endpoint impossible.
