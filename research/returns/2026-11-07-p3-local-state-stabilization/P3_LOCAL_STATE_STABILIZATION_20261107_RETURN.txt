## 2. The construction: turn a known circuit into a restoring local process

Let

\[
C=U_T\cdots U_1
\]

be a supplied circuit on \(q\) work qubits, starting from \(|0^q\rangle\), with each gate acting on at most two qubits. Some work qubits form the physical output \(S\); the rest contain workspace or a purification.

Write its output as \(\tau_C\). If the desired state is \(\tau\), the source must already carry a warranted error bound

\[
D(\tau_C,\tau)\le \eta_{\rm src},
\qquad
D(\rho,\sigma)=\frac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1.
\]

This input matters. The construction requires the complete preparation procedure, including any probability generation and classical control. A single sampled branch of a mixed-state preparation is insufficient. In particular, the predecessor’s sampled Schur circuit cannot be substituted for the complete all-sector preparation algorithm.

### A clock whose unfinished states have a controlled total weight

Add \(T\) clock qubits, with legal words

\[
|t\rangle=|1^t0^{T-t}\rangle,
\qquad 0\le t\le T.
\]

This is an explicit unary clock; it is not a free \((T+1)\)-level physical system.

For a chosen \(0<\delta\le 1/2\), set

\[
K=2^{\left\lceil\log_2[T(1-\delta)/\delta]\right\rceil},
\qquad
a=\frac1{K+T}.
\]

Let \(|\psi_t\rangle=U_t\cdots U_1|0^q\rangle\). The stationary state is

\[
\Omega
=
a\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
|\psi_t\rangle\!\langle\psi_t|
\otimes |t\rangle\!\langle t|
+
Ka\,|\psi_T\rangle\!\langle\psi_T|
\otimes |T\rangle\!\langle T|.
\]

The total probability of an unfinished computation is

\[
\delta_c=\frac{T}{K+T}\le\delta.
\]

Consequently, the physical stationary output has the form

\[
\Omega_S=(1-\delta_c)\tau_C+\delta_c\chi
\]

for a mixture \(\chi\) of intermediate output states. Therefore,

\[
\boxed{D(\Omega_S,\tau)\le \eta_{\rm src}+\delta_c.}
\]

This is an **unconditional physical-output guarantee**. It does not require measuring the clock and retaining only completed runs.

### Four kinds of local event

The fixed process randomly selects among four kinds of operation:

1. **Input reset.** When the legal clock is at zero, reset one work qubit to zero.
2. **Computation step.** Move the clock forward and apply \(U_t\), or move it backward and apply \(U_t^\dagger\). The last backward move occurs with probability \(1/K\), producing the desired terminal weight.
3. **Clock dephasing.** Remove clock coherences.
4. **Clock cleanup.** Replace a neighbouring clock pattern \(01\) by \(00\).

A computation event examines at most three clock qubits and applies a gate to at most two work qubits. Thus **each event acts on at most five retained qubits**. The other events use fewer.

The channel list is padded to a power of two, so selection uses a finite number of independent random bits. The terminal reverse probability is dyadic and also uses a bounded number of bits. There is no unbounded rejection routine hidden in the clock.

The adjoints are inverses of **known logical gates**. This construction supplies no inverse of an unknown source or physical apparatus.

### Why arbitrary controller states are covered

A clock construction is incomplete if it works only after somebody has prepared a legal clock. The cleanup operation resolves that problem.

For a classical clock word \(c\), define

\[
W(c)=
\sum_{i=1}^{T-1}(T-i)\,
\mathbf 1\{c_ic_{i+1}=01\}.
\]

This quantity is zero exactly on legal unary words. Computation steps preserve it. Every effective cleanup event reduces it by at least one. Since

\[
W(c)\le \frac{T(T-1)}2,
\]

the report obtains a uniform polynomial bound on entering the legal clock space. Once entered, it is never left by the ideal process.

Within that space, a history-frame change of representation removes the computation gates from the analysis. It leaves a classical clock walk and work-qubit resets at clock position zero. That change of representation is used only in the proof; no large history-unitary oracle is implemented.

After every input qubit has been reset at least once, the work register has forgotten its initial state. An explicit killed-clock estimate bounds the time required for this to happen.

The resulting theorem is stronger than convergence of the output marginal. For every finite untouched reference \(R\),

\[
\boxed{
D\!\left(
(\Phi^m\otimes{\rm id}_R)(\rho_{QR}),
\Omega\otimes\rho_R
\right)\le\epsilon
}
\]

after the certified restoration period, for **every** initial work–clock–reference state.

That includes invalid clocks, coherent clocks, incorrectly initialized work registers and correlations with previous outputs.

### The event bound

A sufficient event count is

\[
\boxed{
m(\epsilon)=
O\!\left(
(q+T)T^2\,\delta^{-1}
\log\frac{(q+1)T}{\delta\epsilon}

The package also supplies a fully explicit integer certificate. It uses exact rational arithmetic and bit-length comparisons, not numerical estimates of a spectral gap.

The distinction between the two accuracy parameters is important:

- \(\epsilon\) controls how closely the current joint state has approached that stationary state.

The construction is polynomial in \(1/\delta\), while its convergence dependence on \(1/\epsilon\) is logarithmic.

## 4. The obstruction: auxiliary controllers cannot erase the precision cost

The negative result concerns the **actual physical system**, even when the controller is arbitrarily large.

Let \(P\) project onto one physical phase and \(B\) onto a narrow boundary between phases. Suppose the Kraus operators satisfy

\[
[P,K_\alpha](I-B)=0.
\]

This says that an event cannot cross the phase boundary when starting outside the boundary region. For a magnetization cut, an event affecting at most \(k\) physical spins satisfies this condition with a boundary of width \(2k\). Arbitrary auxiliary registers may participate.

Let \(\sigma\) be a stationary joint state of physical system and auxiliaries, and write

\[
p=\operatorname{Tr}(P\sigma),
\qquad
b=\operatorname{Tr}(B\sigma),
\qquad
\sigma_P=\frac{P\sigma P}{p}.
\]

The report proves

\[
\boxed{
D\bigl(\Phi(\sigma_P),\sigma_P\bigr)
\le 2\sqrt{\frac bp}.
}
\]

The proof uses a Stinespring isometry and retains the coherences between the phase and auxiliary registers. It does not replace the joint state by a classical distribution.

Telescoping gives

\[
D\!\left(
\operatorname{Tr}_A\Phi^m(\sigma_P),\sigma_S
\right)
\ge
1-p-2m\sqrt{\frac bp}.
\]

Thus even physical-marginal mixing to error \(\epsilon_{\rm mix}\) requires

\[
\boxed{
m\ge
\frac{1-p-\epsilon_{\rm mix}}2
\sqrt{\frac pb}.
}
\]

If the joint stationary state is block diagonal across the phase cut, a stronger bound holds:

\[
m\ge
\frac{(1-p-\epsilon_{\rm mix})p}{b}.
\]

The stronger statement still allows quantum dynamics. Its extra premise concerns the stationary state’s phase coherences.

These results build on the established quantum bottleneck line, which already distinguishes slow local sampling from the possible efficiency of other preparation algorithms. The particular auxiliary-uniform inequalities used here are proved explicitly in the report. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.04300v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
