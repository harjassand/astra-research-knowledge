## 2. How the unknown Hamiltonian becomes experimentally accessible

The useful feature of the inherited reset instrument is that it exports two distinct resources.

For a local bin, its ideal flagged form is

\[
V|\psi\rangle
=
\sqrt{1-p}\,|\psi\rangle_S|\emptyset\rangle
+
\sqrt p\,|\Phi_2\rangle_{S,A}|\psi\rangle_B,
\qquad
p=1-e^{-r\Delta}.
\]

On a reset:

- \(B\) contains the preceding source state, including its reference correlations.
- \(A\) purifies the fresh maximally mixed state left at the source.

The receiver obtains this instrument from the actual coherent fields. No source SWAP or externally controlled source reset is granted.

The first output carries the original payload. The second makes subsequent dynamics experimentally accessible: correlations between \(A\) and later exports probe how the source evolves.

### A finite experiment that isolates one coefficient

For a candidate Pauli \(P\), choose a site \(i\) in its support and a single-site Pauli \(Q_i\) anticommuting with \(P_i\). Define the signed Hermitian Pauli

\[
R_P=iPQ_i.
\]

Use three successive windows:

1. During a front window of width \(w\), retain the last replacement purifier at every site in \(\operatorname{supp}P\).
2. Let the source evolve for duration \(\tau\), discarding the intervening output.
3. During a back window of width \(w\), retain the first export from site \(i\).

Measure \(R_P^T\) on the front purifiers and \(Q_i\) on the back export. The transpose includes the required sign for \(Y\)-Pauli factors. Return the signed product when all required resets occurred, and return zero otherwise:

\[
Y\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]

Let \(b=|\operatorname{supp}P|\), \(s=b+1\), and \(f_P(w,\tau)=\mathbb E Y\). With the source initially maximally mixed,

\[
\boxed{
\partial_w^{\,s}\partial_\tau f_P(0,0)
=
2s!\,r^s\theta_P.
}
\]

This identity is the main acquisition result.

At the lowest successful order in \(w\), exactly the required \(b\) front resets and one back reset occur. Any additional reset or Hamiltonian action inside those windows contributes another power of \(w\). Consequently,

\[
[w^s]f_P(w,\tau)
=
r^s2^{-n}
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
R_P e^{\tau\mathcal L_H^\dagger}(Q_i)
\right].
\]

Differentiating in \(\tau\) introduces \(i[H,Q_i]\). Pauli orthogonality removes every coefficient except \(\theta_P\); the known depolarizing contribution also vanishes in this correlation.

The derivative is **not assumed to be an available laboratory operation**. It is acquired through finite experiments.

### Finite interpolation, with failed attempts counted

The signed expectation is analytic, with coefficients bounded by

\[
f_P(w,\tau)=\sum_{u,v\ge0}c_{uv}w^u\tau^v,
\qquad
|c_{uv}|
\le
\frac{(2\Lambda)^u\Lambda^v}{u!\,v!}.

Its useful variance property is exact:

\[
\mathbb E Y^2=(1-e^{-rw})^s.
\]

Thus the failed reset events remain part of the acquired distribution. Their cost is included in the trial count; there is no free normalized postselection oracle.

For fixed \(k\), one explicit schedule gives

\[

The hidden logarithmic power is large. The report gives both a conservative explicit schedule and a stronger schedule using factorial Taylor remainders, calculated interpolation weights, and randomized experiment selection.

## 3. Two distinct ways to handle an uncontrolled starting state

These constructions have different premises and should remain separate.

### General fixed interaction order: pay for passive preparation

For any source/reference state,

\[
\left\|
(e^{t\mathcal L_H}\otimes\mathrm{id}_R)(\rho_{SR})
-
\frac{I}{2^n}\otimes\rho_R
\right\|_1
\le
2\sqrt{n\log2}\,e^{-rt/2}.
\]

The proof uses conditional relative entropy. Hamiltonian evolution preserves that entropy, while local replacement dissipates it; strong subadditivity gives the required contraction.

Before each capped calibration trial, a sufficient waiting time is

\[
t_{\mathrm{burn}}
=
\frac2r
\log\frac{2\sqrt{n\log2}\,N_{\mathrm{cal}}}
{\epsilon_{\mathrm{mix}}}.
\]

This makes the complete sequence close to trials starting from the required maximally mixed state. It is actual source exposure, and the original quantum archive must survive it.

### Bounded complete neighborhoods: remove the preparation wait

Let \(C_i\) contain every candidate interaction support touching \(i\). If \(|C_i|\) is bounded, reset all of \(C_i\) in the front window.

The same coefficient identity then holds for **every starting state**, including states correlated with the rest of the source and the archived payload. All terms contributing to \([H,Q_i]\) lie inside the reset neighborhood.

A further construction is needed because the starting state may change between trials. Fixed batches at different interpolation nodes would combine different functions and would not justify the desired cancellation.

Instead, choose the interpolation node freshly at random each trial. If the coefficient weights are \(a_j\), choose finite dyadic probabilities satisfying

\[
q_j\ge\frac{|a_j|}{2A},
\qquad
A=\sum_j|a_j|,
\]

and return

\[
Z=\frac{a_jY}{q_j}.
\]

Conditioned on all previous records, the expected value is the interpolation functional of one current-state function. Its bias remains uniformly controlled, and a martingale concentration bound supplies the confidence guarantee.

**Bounded complete neighborhoods are stronger than bounded interaction order.** A two-body Hamiltonian can have a large neighborhood. The exponent in this version depends on neighborhood size.

## 4. Why later calibration may be correlated with the stored quantum state

This is a general composition result, beyond the particular Hamiltonian estimator.

Let \(\sigma_z\) be the subnormalized archive/reference state conditional on later calibration record \(z\). Suppose every good record produces a decoder within diamond distance \(e\) of one fixed ideal decoder:

\[
\|\mathcal D_{\widehat H(z)}-\mathcal D_H\|_\diamond\le e.
\]

Suppose bad records have total probability at most \(\delta\), uniformly over the original input.

Then

\[
\left\|
\sum_z(\mathcal D_{\widehat H(z)}\otimes\mathrm{id})(\sigma_z)
-
(\mathcal D_H\otimes\mathrm{id})\!\left(\sum_z\sigma_z\right)
\right\|_1
\le e+2\delta.
\]

Each good positive branch contributes at most \(e\,\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_z\). Each bad branch contributes at most \(2\,\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_z\). Summing proves the claim.

Later operations on the source and its outputs leave the unconditioned archive marginal unchanged. They may change its conditional decomposition, which the argument explicitly allows.

For the common-layout inverse decoder,

\[
e
\le
2T_0\|H-\widehat H\|
\le
