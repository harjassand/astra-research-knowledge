INDEPENDENT SEPARATION / PHYSICAL KINETIC LEARNING ROUTE
Status at handoff to finite-LO work, 2026-11-05

This route is NOT a completed civilization-scale capability and is not selected
as the main result. The complete notes below distinguish a proved inequality,
an exploratory calculation, and missing constructive/prior-art work.

1. Initial question

Could a universal physical machine sort an arbitrary multicomponent mixture
using only species-dependent kinetic multipliers and shared thermodynamic
affinities, with an exact minimum complete dissipation law?

Governing Astra baseline N1620 has a sharp binary endpoint potential and an
ideal retained-history construction; N1626 extends selected binary consequences
to interacting machines. These are substantial existing machinery. Strong
external alternatives include programmable multispecies ratchets, classical
multicomponent separation-value theory, and finite-time separation optimization.

2. Exploratory ternary calculation; not a proved global theorem

For weak two-state common-force cells with species rates d_s and composition r_s,
means satisfy mu_dot_s=d_s(F-mu_s), and quadratic dissipation is
integral sum_s r_s d_s (F-mu_s)^2 dt. The infinite-time endpoint cost is a singular
LQR Riccati form mu^T K(r) mu. With D=diag(d), a_s=r_s d_s, A=sum a_s,

  D K + K D + diag(a) = (K d+a)(K d+a)^T/A,
  K 1 = 0.

For d=(1,2,4), a numerical CARE regularization by 1e-12*11^T and posterior
coordinates r=(x1,x2,1-x1-x2) gives the local candidate Hessian
H=2 E^T diag(1/r) K diag(1/r) E, E=((1,0),(0,1),(-1,-1)).
At r=(.2,.3,.5), numerical derivative differences were approximately
partial_2 H11-partial_1 H12 = -4608.73 and
partial_2 H12-partial_1 H22 = 155.36.
This suggests that the locally optimal ternary cost is not the Hessian of one
scalar separation potential. It is not a certified integrability obstruction,
and does not characterize the global optimum over physical machines.
The primary 2017 paper 'Separation potential for multicomponent mixtures:
State-of-the-art of the problem' already discusses failures of binary-style
multicomponent value functions. No novelty is claimed for that qualitative issue.

3. Completed scoped terminal-state Fisher information inequality

Consider a finite, possibly time-dependent, thermodynamically modeled Markov
machine, including every controller/recorder whose retained information is
claimed. Its parameter theta in R^p enters each reversible edge e only as the
same positive multiplier in both directions:
  k_xy(theta,t)=a_e(theta,t) k_xy^0(t),
  k_yx(theta,t)=a_e(theta,t) k_yx^0(t).
The common factors may depend on modeled controller state. The initial machine
law is theta independent, unless its initial Fisher information is explicitly
retained in the bound. Put g_e=grad_theta log a_e, z_x=grad_theta log p_x,
u_e=p_x k_xy, v_e=p_y k_yx, j_e=u_e-v_e, A_e=u_e+v_e, and
sigma_e=j_e log(u_e/v_e). Let F=sum_x p_x z_x z_x^T be TERMINAL-STATE Fisher
information. Direct differentiation, edge by edge, gives EXACTLY

  F_dot = sum_e [-A_e Delta z_e Delta z_e^T
                  +j_e(g_e Delta z_e^T+Delta z_e g_e^T)]
        = sum_e (j_e^2/A_e) g_e g_e^T
          -sum_e A_e(Delta z_e-j_e g_e/A_e)
                       (Delta z_e-j_e g_e/A_e)^T.

Since j^2/(u+v) <= (1/2)(u-v)log(u/v),

  F(T)-F(0) <=_PSD (1/2) integral_0^T sum_e sigma_e g_e g_e^T dt.

This is NOT a trajectory-Fisher bound from system dissipation alone. A symmetric
two-state equilibrium process with unknown common rate a has positive trajectory
Fisher information (aT for log a under the corresponding clock convention)
and zero system entropy production. An external free trajectory observer is
not included in the theorem. To claim its complete record as terminal machine
state, its physical dynamics and initial resources must satisfy and be charged
under the model above. Equilibrium dissipation cannot be inferred merely from
the statistical information in an externally supplied count stream.

For one log-rate parameter, g_e in {0,1}, F(0)=0 implies F(T)<=Sigma/2. With a
regular prior density pi and a physical terminal estimator, van Trees then gives
Bayes MSE >= 1/(J_pi+E_pi Sigma/2). This is conditional on the FULL physical
machine model; it is not a model-free scientific-learning energy law.

4. Locally sharp one-cell calculation, not a completed full-cycle theorem

At a nominal rate d, one two-state probe has mu_dot=D(F-mu), D=e^theta, and
u=partial_theta mu satisfies u_dot=d(F-mu)-d u at D=d. Choose a terminal
sensitivity epsilon, q=exp(-dT),

  mu(t)=2 epsilon [exp(-d(T-t))-q]/(1-q^2),
  F(t)=2 epsilon [2 exp(-d(T-t))-q]/(1-q^2).

Then u(T)=epsilon exactly. The quadratic dissipation is
2 epsilon^2/(1-exp(-2dT)), while terminal Fisher information is
epsilon^2/(1-mu(T)^2). With epsilon ->0 and dT ->infinity, their ratio tends
to 1/2. The exact binary entropy-production integrand is
D(F-mu)(atanh(F)-atanh(mu)); finite small-amplitude corrections are O(epsilon^4).

To turn this into a sharp all-precision, full-cycle estimation machine still
requires a fully budgeted physical repeated-record/reset/compression protocol,
an initial coarse-rate acquisition, finite controls/timing, memory leakage,
and proof that these overheads vanish relative to the target dissipation.
I considered sufficient-statistic binomial compression and reversible common
controls, but did NOT complete that construction. Nor was historical novelty
of the Fisher inequality settled. Do not promote it to an established new
universal metrology theorem.
