   for lambda/(2 r^*). Each exit trial is censored, with finite external
   deadline/bit grid, timestamp/tie bounds, fresh focal pins and all services.
3. P=I+Q/(2Nr^*) is implemented without fitting the operating clocks.
   Reversibility in the declared local heat-bath form earns P*=P.
4. Heavy-completion pin mass uses a <=N-flip path of measured full local log
   odds to replace the previously unknown terminal numerical weight ratio.
   This closes true Count/SampleB, rather than assuming static access.
5. Predictable conditional kernels, conditional accepted records and a
   conditional bounded-mean Chernoff lemma compose the entire operation.
   Shared calibration confidence is distinct from fresh per-record TV.
   Calibration sample counts retain log(1/delta), without a forced
   per-high-level-call delta/D static accuracy.

   true physical law, consuming fresh experiments on each run.

Hard scope and costs
  Known topology/local closure; faithful local copies; actual arbitrary local
  clamps; calibrated and reproducible additive fields; known r_cal>0;
  justified uniform 0<r_*<=r_i^u(s)<=r^*; certified pinned contraction and fixed
  failure-surprisal contrast; actual operating environment reproduced on
  copies and deployment; priced state pins; paid detector, response, clock
  precision and reference drift; finite controllers and classical memory.
  Uniform kinetic bounds at unvisited contexts are not inferred from the
  coarse energy measurements. An arbitrary field-dependent clock is not
  assumed continuous under field-setting error.
  Equilibrium-safe physical preparation requires safe mass >=1/2 at every
  prepared menu item in the inherited routine. Other preparation needs its
  own paid inverse-safe-mass bill. Live-system exposure during acquisition
  remains separate.
  No inverse global-failure probability or mean-lifetime trajectory factor.
  This is not an economical prototype claim, general passive model discovery,
  arbitrary nonreversible reversal, unlimited offline learned simulator, tiny
  subevent relative-error guarantee, or adaptive in-mission policy theorem.
