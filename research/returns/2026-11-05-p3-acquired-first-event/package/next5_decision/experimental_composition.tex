\section{Adaptive composition and the full acquired operation}

\begin{lemma}[Predictable physical-query substitution]\label{lem:adaptive}
Consider a finite randomized program issuing at most $D$ adaptive queries.
Suppose that, conditional on every possible preceding experimental
transcript, its actual response kernel at query $j$ differs from the
specified ideal response kernel by at most $\theta_j$ in TV. If the
predictable budgets satisfy $\sum_{j\le D}\theta_j\le a$ deterministically,
the complete actual transcript differs from the ideal transcript by at most
$a$ in TV. A bad-calibration event of ideal probability at most $\delta_0$
then has actual probability at most $\delta_0+a$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Couple the program's classical randomness identically. Until its first
unequal response, its requested inputs agree; couple that pair of kernels
maximally. The conditional probability of a first mismatch at query $j$
is at most $\theta_j$. A union bound gives the result. This is a statement
about conditional kernels, so no independence of actual cells or their
past states is required.
\end{proof}

Every nested routine above has deterministic caps. The conditional
concentration estimates and sample/call error budgets can therefore be
composed adaptively or flattened into a finite controller. The next two
facts retain logarithmic confidence dependence; coupling every high-level
calibration sample at error $\delta/D$ can needlessly force inverse-$\delta$
static accuracies.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional bounded-mean concentration]\label{lem:means}
Let $Y_j\in[0,1]$ and let their predictable means satisfy
$|\E(Y_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})-\mu|\le b$, where $\mu\ge\mu_0>0$.
For $0<\eta\le1/4$, if $b\le\eta\mu_0/16$ and
\[
 n\ge\frac{256}{\mu_0\eta^2}\log(8/\delta_0),
\]
then $\Prb(|n^{-1}\sum_jY_j-\mu|>\eta\mu/2)\le\delta_0$.
No independence or common actual predictable mean is needed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $\mu_-=\mu-b$ and $\mu_+=\mu+b$. Convexity on $[0,1]$ gives,
for any real $t$,
$\E(e^{tY_j}\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})
 \le1+(e^t-1)\E(Y_j\mid\mathcal F_{j-1})$.
For $t>0$ this is at most $\exp(\mu_+(e^t-1))$, and for $t<0$
at most $\exp(\mu_-(e^t-1))$. Iteration and exponential Markov give
the usual upper and lower multiplicative Chernoff bounds with $\mu_+$
and $\mu_-$, respectively. At relative deviation $\eta/4$, their
failure probabilities sum to at most
$2\exp[-n\mu_0\eta^2/96]$ since $\mu_-\ge\mu_0/2$.
The stated $n$ is more than sufficient. The deterministic mean bias $b$
plus those deviations is below $\eta\mu/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Conditional accepted records]\label{lem:accept}
Let an ideal single attempt output either rejection or an accepted mark,
with success probability $q\ge q_0>0$ and successful-mark law $\nu$.
At every attempt and every prior history suppose the actual combined
rejection/mark law is within TV $a\le q_0/2$ of that same ideal law.
Its success probability is at least $q_0/2$, and its conditional
successful-mark law is within $2a/q_0$ of $\nu$.
After a cap of $J$ attempts, returning any mark on exhaustion gives
overall mark TV at most $2a/q_0+e^{-q_0J/2}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The success probabilities differ by at most $a$. Subtract the normalized
accepted subprobabilities and add and subtract the actual subprobability
divided by the ideal $q$. The numerator difference costs at most $a/q$
and its normalization costs at most $a/q$. This bounds conditional TV
by $2a/q_0$. It is uniform over each pre-success history, hence also
over their mixture. Conditional failure probability at any attempt is at
most $1-q_0/2$, so exhaustion is at most $(1-q_0/2)^J$.
Neither assertion assumes independent physical attempts.
\end{proof}

Here is an explicit allocation for one intervention and one compiled
record generator, for $0<\eps,\delta\le1/4$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Allocate $\delta/4$ to coarse structural certification, balancing,
and any shared metrology confidence. The declared model/faithfulness
premises themselves are not statistical hypotheses certified by this event.
\item Run the accepted first-event compilation at output target $\eps/2$
and confidence failure $\delta/2$, using the true static interfaces just
constructed. Include every pin-mass and cut-enumeration confidence in
its requested interface budget. Use its deterministic finite controller
and clamp failed estimates to the same certified floors/ranges as the
accepted construction.
\item For the Palm calibration mean, use the accepted
$\xi=(\eps/2)/128$, $q_0=g/8$ and per-proposal TV budget
$\rho_{\rm prop}=\xi q_0/64$. Include finite acceptance rounding
so every observable's conditional mean bias is at most
$2\rho_{\rm prop}\le\xi q_0/16$. Use
$n_{\rm Palm}\ge256(q_0\xi^2)^{-1}\log(32/\delta)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:means} supplies the required relative Palm-mean confidence.
Count has the same conditional bounded-mean construction.
Each calibration proposal's accuracy depends on $\eps,g$ and its finite
local length, not on $\delta/n_{\rm Palm}$.
\item Let $D_1$ be the deterministic cap for one later record. Allocate
total substitution error $\eps/4$, for example
$\theta_1=\eps/[4\max(1,D_1)]$ per query. For a nested module, split its
allocated query error over its own finite calls. The requested error is
the smaller of its own proof's tolerance and the outer allocation.
\item Supply the requested initial law to TV $\kappa\le\eps/4$, or an
exact initial state. All finite-bit clock and record-rounding errors
already paid by the accepted sampler remain in its $\eps/2$ budget.
\end{enumerate}
All controller caps are computed before execution from the accepted
explicit schedule and the displayed static caps. To avoid a circular
precision definition, first expand only down to ideal Gibbs-update,
operating-update and balanced-coin requests. Their number is fixed by
the static sample counts and mixing sweep lengths. Replacing a request
by its finite physical factory changes its service cost, not that outer
query count. An outer tighter budget changes hold length or factory cap,
not the number of observations already specified by a mean estimator.
Module-wise allocation using Lemma~\ref{lem:accept} is a sharper valid
alternative to the per-record coupling: it gives the accepted conditional
path error $4\rho_{\rm prop}/q_0$ and cap tail directly.

\begin{theorem}[Experiment-fed true first-event generator]\label{thm:full}
Under the declared model and physical services of Section 2, with fixed
$c$, a valid pinned contraction margin $\alpha$, and the all-terminal
disconnection failure rule, the preceding finite controller constructs a
compiled generator with the following property. With probability at least
$1-\delta$ over its shared calibration, for every past experimental
transcript and every efficiently supplied initial law $\mu$, its next
record has conditional law within $\eps$ in total variation of the true
quantized first-event record from $\mu$ for $Q^u$. The ideal initial law is
the target of the supplied sampler; its preparation/draw error is charged
as above. The generator returns the event time, terminal configuration,
and requested preceding window jointly.

Its operation uses fresh faithful local experiments, finite bits and
bounded physical service times on every draw. It needs no fine table of
operating rates. The on-demand static implementation needs no fine
exported energy-factor table, while the fitted-static hybrid is also
valid. Its full classical and local-query counts are polynomial in
the graph/input description at fixed $c$ and either fixed degree or
the successful pairwise coarse certificate above,
$B_0+U$, $\alpha^{-1}$, $g^{-1}$, requested window length,
clock digits, inverse output accuracy, and log inverse confidence,
including the coarse certification refinement cap.
The field, pin, setting, response, timer, memory and propagation costs
are the actual charged service functions at the resulting tolerances.
There is no inverse global stationary-failure probability or mean-lifetime
trajectory factor.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For ideal physical response kernels, Lemmas~\ref{lem:pinned} and
\ref{lem:pinmass}, the exact overlap identities, and their finite
confidence/precision schedules supply Count and SampleB for the true
invariant law. Lemma~\ref{lem:walk} supplies true forward and reversed
$P=P^*$ updates. Equation~\eqref{eq:gap} supplies the mass floor and gap.
The hypotheses of the accepted first-event computation theorem therefore
hold for this same true $Q^u,\pi^u,B$.

The mathematical path, Palm and mixing arguments of that theorem do not
require rates to be rational. Rationality in its numerical formulation
allows finite evaluation of update probabilities. The physical finite
kernel implementations above replace that evaluation. Known bounds,
requested precisions and the controller are rational, and every returned
record remains finite. Unknown real parameters are not evaluated by a
hidden real-arithmetic oracle.

In compilation, the true-law Count confidence and
Lemma~\ref{lem:means} for each bounded Palm statistic retain total
calibration failure at most $\delta/2$ with the displayed conservative
sample counts. Their implementation bias is already included.
The shared structural event adds at most $\delta/4$, leaving slack.
Thus with probability at least $1-\delta$ the realized compiled parameters
have their true-law correctness property. Conditional on any such compiled
state and any later experimental transcript, Lemma~\ref{lem:adaptive}
bounds each record's additional physical substitution error by $\eps/4$.
The accepted record error is $\eps/2$, and the initial-law error is at
most $\eps/4$. These add to $\eps$. Uniformity of the physical conditional
kernels makes the per-record bound valid after reusing cells or after
any earlier record outputs. It does not assert unconditional independence
of the physical records.

The accepted controller's finite caps have no inverse global-rarity term.
The constructed static schedule has $v_{\rm graph}^{O(c)}$ cut complexity
and polynomial inverse requested accuracy, while a local operating update
uses $O(\log(1/\theta))$ censored exits. The static pin-mass identity never
waits to see the rare pin event. Substitution therefore inserts only the
displayed polynomial query counts and actual service functions; no hidden
$1/\pi^u(B)$ or $\E_\mu\tau$ is introduced.
\end{proof}

The confidence event is shared across all later draws from a compiled
generator. Fresh record errors are conditional distribution errors, not
additional claims that every physical trial succeeds with certainty.
If random metrology failures are allowed on fresh calls, their probabilities
must be included in the per-record budgets. An indefinite sequence of draws
does not receive a zero-failure guarantee without a summable allocation.
This is a true-law \emph{generative physical procedure}, not an unlimited
offline algorithm learned once and then run without further experiments.

\subsection{A quantitative physical ledger}

Let $M_{0,G},M_{0,P},M_{0,O}$ denote compilation caps for Gibbs updates,
operating updates and balanced log-odds estimates after expanding all
static routines, and $M_{1,G},M_{1,P},M_{1,O}$ the corresponding per-record
caps. They are obtained directly from the preceding displayed caps and
the accepted first-event schedule. For prescribed allocations
$\theta_{a,j}$ and log-odds accuracies/confidences $(h_{a,j},b_{a,j})$,
the exact cost ledger is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totalcost}
 \begin{split}
 C_a\le{}& C_{a,\rm classical}
 +\sum_{j=1}^{M_{a,G}} C_G(\theta_{a,j})
 +\sum_{j=1}^{M_{a,P}} C_P(\theta_{a,j})\\
 &+\sum_{j=1}^{M_{a,O}} C_{\rm odds}(h_{a,j},b_{a,j}),
 \qquad a=0,1 .
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Compilation additionally pays coarse acquisition and shared metrology.
All storage for configurations, cut lists, counters, finite clock words,
and the precursor window is the accepted polynomial classical memory;
storing the earlier entire long physical trajectory is unnecessary.
Apparatus size, available cells, propagation and scheduling determine the
wall-clock realization of the sum. The charged $C_{\rm timer}$ includes
reference-frequency calibration and drift sufficiently accurate over the
whole interval $[0,D]$ to meet \eqref{eq:timing}, not just a nominal digital
timestamp spacing.
A requested physical precursor duration $h_{\rm phys}$ requires
$H=\lceil R h_{\rm phys}\rceil$ in the normalized first-event controller,
so its bill includes the aggregate upper update rate $R=2Nr^*$.
The finite quantized time conversion uses that same calibrated time unit.

For scale, a single operating update at $\theta=2^{-20}$ and $\rho=2$
has the sufficient values $m=23$, $n=1656$, $\beta=\theta/(8n)$,
$D=19.5$ in the model time unit (or $20$ after upward whole-unit rounding), and
$\Delta\le\beta/(256\rho)$, approximately $1.41\times10^{-13}$.
It may require 1656 focal pins and $32292$ units of censored observation
time, or $33120$ with that upward rounding, before pin, settling, context
and timer costs. The sufficient deadline-uniform grid has 109 bits using
the rational bound $e<3$.
The precise finite constants are conservative sufficient costs.
They do not demonstrate an economical device or a universal lower bound.
At small true $\lambda$ this bill remains independent of $1/\lambda$,
but the required finite precision and pin services remain real.

\section{Safe preparation and a consequential finite-menu decision}

For a physical mission starting at $\nu_u=\pi^u(\cdot\mid A)$, use the
accepted repairable-chain preparation and capped safe inspections with its
proved error budget and actual operating exposure. The mission clock starts
only after the accepted safe inspection completes. A simulator initial
draw can instead use experimental Gibbs sampling followed by safe
rejection; with a certified safe mass $a_0$ its cost pays $a_0^{-1}$,
and a finite rejection cap and a safe fallback give the declared TV error.
For the inherited equilibrium-safe physical preparation, require
$\pi^u(A)\ge1/2$ for \emph{every} menu item to be prepared; this condition
is certified or supplied separately for each item. If a different
preparation is claimed with smaller safe mass, its exposure and rejection
cost must explicitly pay the appropriate inverse safe mass.
These are separate physical and computational initializations.

The intervention is chosen before observing the realized accepted initial
configuration. Preparation after the chosen intervention, its field ramp,
settling and faithful reproduction of the chosen operating environment are
priced. The state of the live system during potentially long acquisition
is not protected by this theorem; paid safe shutdown, a continuing
preparation protocol, or its actual exposure risk must be specified.

\begin{corollary}[Acquired bounded-loss intervention choice]\label{cor:decision}
Let $K=|\mathcal U|<\infty$, and let $\ell_u$ be a computable loss of the
returned record in $[0,1]$. It may combine time, terminal damage subtype,
precursor attributes and a known scaled intervention cost. Suppose each
compiled menu generator has uniform conditional record error at most
$\eps_s$, on a joint shared event of probability at least $1-\delta_{\rm cal}$.
For $a>0$, use
\[
 n_{\rm dec}\ge \frac{1}{2a^2}\log\frac{2K}{\delta_{\rm dec}}
\]
fresh generated records per menu element, and choose an element minimizing
its empirical mean loss. If each computed loss has absolute error at most
$\eps_{\rm eval}$, then, with probability at least
$1-\delta_{\rm cal}-\delta_{\rm dec}$,
\[
 L_{\widehat u}\le\min_{u\in\mathcal U} L_u+
                  2(a+\eps_s+\eps_{\rm eval}),
 \qquad L_u=\E_{\nu_u,Q^u}\ell_u .
\]
If actual deployment-preparation laws are within TV $\kappa_{\rm prep}$ of
the corresponding $\nu_u$, the bound relative to the best similarly
prepared menu mission adds $2\kappa_{\rm prep}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Condition on a good shared calibration state. For each record, its
conditional expected loss differs from $L_u$ by at most $\eps_s$.
Losses minus these conditional means form bounded martingale differences.
Conditional Hoeffding gives a two-sided deviation at most $a$ with failure
probability $2e^{-2n_{\rm dec}a^2}$. A union bound over the $K$ menu elements
and the loss-evaluation error bound imply that every empirical mean
differs from its true $L_u$ by at most $a+\eps_s+\eps_{\rm eval}$.
Comparing the empirical minimizer to a true minimizer gives twice that
error. A bounded loss changes by at most the TV error of its initial law;
apply this once to the selected and once to the comparator mission.
\end{proof}

The complete decision bill is $K$ menu compilations, $Kn_{\rm dec}$ fresh
record draws with \eqref{eq:totalcost}, plus actual chosen-menu deployment
and safe preparation. Context/environment sharing may lower that bill
only when physically justified. The result is additive regret for a
finite menu, not relative accuracy for a tiny conditional subtype,
unbounded lifetime loss, arbitrary adaptive policy optimization, or an
unobserved natural failure mechanism. For a tiny deadline risk, the
separate acquired relative whole-CDF construction may be the appropriate
decision interface.
