## 6.1 Signed quadratic generation

\[

\[
\frac{d}{d\alpha}
\left[\mathcal M^{-1}C_{Qz}\mathcal M b\right]
=
-2\mathcal M^{-1}
 C_{P[((Qz)\cdot\nabla)F]}\mathcal M b.                            \tag{6.6}
\]
Thus its defect is bounded by
\[
C|\alpha|\|z\|_{H^1}\|b\|_{H^1}
\quad\text{in }H^{-3/4}.                                        \tag{6.7}
\]

The Lorentz defect follows from
\[
\begin{aligned}
&L_F\mathcal B(b,b)
-\mathcal B(C_Fb,b)-\mathcal B(b,C_Fb)\\
&\quad=
\bigl[L_F\mathcal B(b,b)
-\mathcal B(L_Fb,b)-\mathcal B(b,L_Fb)\bigr]\\
&\qquad
+2\mathcal B(P[(b\cdot\nabla)F],b)
+2\mathcal B(b,P[(b\cdot\nabla)F]).
\end{aligned}                                                    \tag{6.8}
\]
The bracketed expression is the Euler quadratic commutator from the NS lowering. The apparent second derivatives cancel. This proves both the \(H^{-3/4}\) tame bound and the stronger \(L^2\) tame bound needed for energy estimates.

Finally,
\[
\|Q^{-1}\Delta Q-\Delta\|_{H^{s+2}\to H^s}
+
\|\mathcal M^{-1}\Delta\mathcal M-\Delta\|_{H^{s+2}\to H^s}
\le C_s|\alpha|.                                                 \tag{6.9}
\]

## 6.4 Quantitative lowering and stability

Let \(H_{\rm nom}\) bound the nominal carrier, force, required derivatives, inverse interval lengths and interval count. It is computed from their coefficient descriptions.

The carrier amplitude is fixed by a desired **average quadratic force**:
\[
A\le C\sqrt{H_{\rm nom}}.
\]
It does not grow when the cell width shrinks. Choose, for example,
\[
\delta_0=\frac{\omega}{C(1+A_{\max}+H_{\rm nom})^3},
\qquad
\ell\le\frac{\delta_0}{CH_{\rm nom}}.                             \tag{6.10}
\]
Then \(\alpha=O(A\ell)\), \(L_\zeta\theta\), the force residual, the signed-scale linear defects and the quadratic defects all meet the prescribed tolerance after fixed polynomial budgeting.

Choose \(\omega\) only after the nominal schedule is fixed. One may use the rational minimum of the coercivity, macro-gap, velocity-reset, endpoint and disturbance tolerances. A conservative implementation divides that minimum by
\[
C(1+H_{\rm nom})^4\Gamma^8.
\]
The thresholds themselves include
\[
\mu,\quad gT_b,\quad r_0,\quad
\frac{e\mu}{K_\star^4},\quad
\frac{q}{\mathcal J K_0},\quad \eta_0.                            \tag{6.11}
\]
All have polynomial reciprocals.

Equations (3.8), (4.1)–(4.8), and (5.12)–(5.14) then retain their strict margins. In particular, the lowered magnetic macros still contract.

Iterating this procedure \(D_*\) times has polynomial cost because \(D_*\) is fixed. The outer time intervals are preserved exactly by rational subdivision. Every fast carrier vanishes at cell endpoints, so no distributional impulse is introduced.

The resulting **physical** force uses only the twelve prescribed fields.

# 7. Steering to the variable velocity target

The joint eraser ends in a small pair \(H^1\) ball. It remains to extend the supplied NS target writer to that ball.

First truncate at
\[
N=\left\lceil\frac{16M}{\epsilon}\right\rceil.
\]
Then
\[
\|V-V_N\|_2\le M/N\le\epsilon/16.                                \tag{7.1}
\]
Canonicalize and coalesce retained modes exactly, and round them with an \(H^1\) error budget \(\epsilon/16\), preserving solenoidality using the rational projector
\[
P_k=I-\frac{k\otimes k}{|k|^2}.
\]
Call the result \(\widetilde V_N\). Thus
\[
\|V-\widetilde V_N\|_2\le\epsilon/8.                             \tag{7.2}
\]

Modes with huge binary wavevectors are discarded by direct integer comparisons with \(N^2\); nothing is enumerated up to those wavevectors.

Use the accepted Bessel-plane synthesis and depth-two quadratic certificates for \(\widetilde V_N\). Their spatial algebra is unchanged. The new point is the coupled estimate for each physical primitive.

## 7.1 Translation leaves

Let \(z\) be the known smooth velocity prefix of one leaf. Its reference is
\[
(u_{\rm ref},B_{\rm ref})=(w+v_{\rm ref},0),
\]
where
\[
(v_{\rm ref})_t+L_wv_{\rm ref}=f_{\rm seed},
\qquad v_{\rm ref}(0)=z.
\]
The large time derivative and carrier transport cancel in this equation. Its remaining momentum residual is
\[
\nu\Delta v_{\rm ref}-\mathcal B(v_{\rm ref},v_{\rm ref}),         \tag{7.3}
\]
with polynomial smooth-norm bounds independent of the leaf’s compressed duration.

The leaf’s shear/inverse word returns both homogeneous propagators exactly:
\[
Q_u(t_{\rm end})=I,\qquad Q_B(t_{\rm end})=I.                     \tag{7.4}
\]
The magnetic identity uses the same rounded shear fields on the forward and inverse legs.

Subtract the reference and conjugate the velocity and magnetic errors by \(Q_u,Q_B\). The pair energy satisfies
\[
E'+\mathscr P^{-1}
\bigl(\|\widetilde e\|_{H^2}^2+\|\widetilde b\|_{H^2}^2\bigr)
\le \mathscr P(E+E^3+1+\eta^2),                                 \tag{7.5}
\]
where \(\mathscr P\) is a fixed-degree polynomial in the known leaf envelopes and \(\mu^{-1}\).

For a leaf duration \(\ell\), its endpoint pair-error bound is therefore
\[
E_{\rm pair,out}^{1/2}
\le
(1+\mathscr P\sqrt\ell)E_{\rm pair,in}^{1/2}
+\mathscr P\sqrt\ell(1+\eta).                                    \tag{7.6}
\]

This is a small multiplicative estimate for the rough unknown error. It does not claim that short diffusion is uniformly \(H^1\)-close to the identity.

## 7.2 Quadratic holds

For a desired dose \(-\lambda\mathcal B(\xi,\xi)\), translate by \(\alpha\xi\), hold for time \(h\), and translate back, with
\[
h\alpha^2=\lambda.
\]
During the unforced hold, put \(w=\alpha\xi\) and define the known reference by
\[
(v_{\rm ref})_t+L_{\alpha\xi}v_{\rm ref}
=\nu\alpha\Delta\xi-\alpha^2\mathcal B(\xi,\xi),
\qquad v_{\rm ref}(0)=z.                                        \tag{7.7}
\]

The relevant transport parameter is
\[
h\alpha=\sqrt{\lambda h}.
\]
For a polynomially small \(h\), the reference has polynomial fixed-order norms, and
\[
\|v_{\rm ref}(h)-[z-\lambda\mathcal B(\xi,\xi)]\|_{H^1}
\le \sqrt h\,\mathscr P.                                        \tag{7.8}
\]
After subtracting this reference, the remaining momentum residual again has the form (7.3). Both conjugated diffusions are coercive because the integrated carrier impulse is small.

Thus a hold also has error and amplification budgets \(O(\sqrt h\,\mathscr P)\). The spurious quantity \(h\alpha^4\) never appears.

## 7.3 Uniform cost over variable catalogues

The ordering is:

1. construct the retained target and its depth-two certificates;
2. choose the hold budgets and amplitudes, recursing only through those two levels;
3. choose the Bessel directional accuracy and resulting leaf count;
4. choose leaf durations using that count.

Shrinking leaf durations does not change the previously chosen impulses or hold amplitudes.

Known smooth prefixes are bounded using the polynomial total certificate weight, not by iterating a power bound once per leaf. Allocate the implementation budgets so that their total amplification is at most two and their total additive pair error is at most \(\epsilon/4\).

Consequently, for entrance pair \(H^1\) norm at most \(2q\), the writer gives
\[
\|(u_{\rm out}-\widetilde V_N,B_{\rm out})\|_{\mathcal H^1}
\le4q+\epsilon/4.                                               \tag{7.9}
\]
Its duration can be made at most \(\tau/4\), with polynomial resources.

# 8. The complete schedule and all Leray–Hopf realizations

The compiler concatenates:

1. the lowered joint eraser;
2. an unforced waiting interval;
3. the MHD target writer, ending exactly at \(\tau\).

Both active stages have duration less than the allotted fractions of \(\tau\), so the waiting interval has nonnegative rational length.

For a pair \(H^1\) norm of size \(q\), the ordinary unforced MHD energy estimate gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\|(u,B)\|_{\mathcal H^1}^2
+c\mu\|(u,B)\|_{\mathcal H^2}^2
\le C\eta^2/\mu,
\]
after absorbing the quadratic terms. Because \(q\ll\mu\) and \(\eta\le c_*\mu q\), the pair stays within \(2q\) during the wait.

Choose the output disturbance tolerance
\[
\eta=\eta_0/16,
\]
and allocate rational force rounding and lowering residuals within the remaining margins used above.

Combining (7.2) and (7.9),
\[
\begin{aligned}

&\le

&\le4q+\epsilon/4+\epsilon/8\\

## Why this covers every energy-admissible realization

Fix any admitted initial pair and any actual admitted disturbance pair. Construct its local physical strong MHD solution. The estimates above apply to that solution and supply bounds permitting continuation through every interval:
\[
(u,B)\in L^\infty(0,\tau;H^1)
       \cap L^2(0,\tau;H^2).                                    \tag{8.2}

The physical carrier norms can be large, but they are finite and bounded by the compiled polynomial envelopes.

The initial difference and forcing difference are exactly zero.

No quantitative disturbance tolerance is extracted from that Gronwall exponential. Robustness was already established directly for each actual disturbed strong solution. Uniqueness only transfers that conclusion to all admitted weak realizations.

The strong solution is continuous in \(L^2\). Equality with every Leray–Hopf realization therefore gives (8.1) for the stipulated one-sided total-energy trace.

It remains to distinguish numerical bounds from representation bounds.

## 9.1 Numerical bounds depend only on \(R\)

The choices are triangular:
\[
(q,K_0,a)
\longrightarrow\Gamma
\longrightarrow T
\longrightarrow\mathcal J
\longrightarrow r
\longrightarrow r_0
\longrightarrow\beta
\longrightarrow h
\longrightarrow\sigma
\longrightarrow e
\longrightarrow\delta.
\]
Every reciprocal is bounded by a fixed polynomial in \(R\).

For example,
\[
\beta^{-1}
\le1000+10^6\Gamma^8\rho^{-1}T^{-1},

\[
h^{-1}
\le
1000T^{-1}
+10^6\Gamma^8\rho^{-1}T^{-2}
+\left(10^6\Gamma^8K_0^2r^{-1}\right)^8,
\]
and
\[
\delta^{-1}
\le
100(\beta h)^{-1}
+c_*^{-1}\mu^{-3}r^{-4}e^{-4}K_\star^{12}.
\]

A degree ledger makes this dependence explicit. If
\[
a\lesssim X^{A_0},\quad q^{-1}\lesssim X^{Q_0},\quad
K_0\lesssim X^{B_0},\quad\Gamma\lesssim X^\gamma,

one may use

\begin{aligned}

These bound the respective inverse parameters. Here one can take \(A_0=13\), \(Q_0=10\), \(B_0=1\).

Apply the accepted fixed erasing-word bounds to these envelopes, multiply by the polynomial macro and microcycle counts, compose the fixed lowering bound \(D_*\) times, and add the depth-two writer bound. This produces a polynomial numerical envelope

E_{\rm phys}(R)

for force peak and \(\eta^{-1}\).

Since \(\tau\le1\),

The only lowering of the enlarged eraser uses a **fixed** catalogue. Variable target synthesis uses the supplied fixed-depth writer. Neither exponent depends on the variable Fourier radius.

## 9.2 Rational heights are bounded separately

The implementation uses the following rules.

- Parameter formulas have fixed algebraic depth.
- Repeated words reuse stored parameters.
- Repeated knots are computed from integer indices and stored rational lengths.
- Target modes are coalesced before numerical coefficient envelopes are used.
- Tiny target coefficients are rounded with absolute error budgets; they are not inverted.
- Root, trigonometric, \(\pi\), and Bessel computations use controlled dyadic precision.
- Every expansion of a normalized pulse into an ordinary rational time polynomial is included in the bit ledger.

Thus a small numerical value with an enormous input denominator is not treated as a free real number. Reading and manipulating its representation is charged to \(L\).

The prescribed accuracies have polynomial reciprocals in \(R\). Elementary rational series or fixed-point algorithms for the needed transcendental values consequently have polynomial work in the numerical argument bounds and requested precision. The inverse Bessel bounds are those of the accepted construction.

All final coefficients have time degree at most three.

The retained target has \(O(N^3)\) possible modes, with \(N\le CR^2\). Huge input frequencies are tested and discarded using integer arithmetic on their binary encodings. Exact coalescing has bit complexity polynomial in \(L\). The promise on \(\|V\|_{H^1}\), rather than an oracle evaluation of that norm, supplies the numerical coefficient bounds.

These rules produce a separate polynomial envelope
\[
E_{\rm bit}(L+R)
\]
for operation count, operand sizes, memory, total output length, interval count and individual rational bit lengths.

## 9.3 A terminating definition of \(A,p\)

Here is the final fixed precomputation defining the constants.

1. Run the accepted fixed-family, word and saturation precomputation, retaining its certificates and costs.
2. Generate the fixed-order bounds of Sections 1–7 using the finite coefficient and inequality rules described in Section 1.
3. Propagate numerical majorants through the displayed parameter formulas and the fixed primitive resource bounds. For minima use a sum of reciprocal majorants; for ceilings use \(1+x\); for repetitions multiply by the proved repetition count.
4. Independently propagate bit-height and arithmetic-cost majorants using the implementation rules above. Conservative schoolbook arithmetic bounds suffice.
5. Expand the resulting nonnegative-coefficient polynomials \(E_{\rm phys}\) and \(E_{\rm bit}\).
6. Set
   \[
   p=\max\{1,\deg E_{\rm phys},\deg E_{\rm bit}\}.
   \]
   Choose an integer \(A\) exceeding their coefficient sums and the certified fixed precomputation costs, including the cost of this majorant calculation and of forming \(A,p\).

This is a finite arithmetic specification of actual constants. It does not ask for a PDE estimate, optimization result, spectral norm or unknown real number at run time. The fixed searches are exactly those supplied by the accepted NS machinery; their recorded costs are included.

The resulting compiler therefore satisfies
\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
\text{bit operations, memory bits, output bits, interval count,}\\
\text{degrees and individual rational bit lengths}
\le A(L+R)^p,
\\[2mm]
\eta^{-1},\quad

Together, (8.1) and (9.2) establish the requested common twelve-actuator MHD compiler for every promised target and every strictly positive pair of diffusivities. The magnetic equation receives no chosen force, and the final estimate concerns the total physical velocity and magnetic field.
