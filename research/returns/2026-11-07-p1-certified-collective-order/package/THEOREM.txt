CERTIFIED COLLECTIVE ORDER AND INVERSE DESIGN FROM ACQUIRED PORT MODELS
7 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane

STATUS AND QUESTION

This treatment resolves the following complete restricted question.
Given finitely acquired uncertain passive components, can one compute the
global ordered states of a subsequently specified reciprocal cubic network,
certify its ordering regime, and construct couplings for a prescribed signed
amplitude pattern, without identifying the components' hidden states?

For the attractive/balanced class defined below, the answer is affirmative.
The output consists of a finite algorithm, error and phase certificates,
coupling specifications, and explicit uncertainty/cost accounting. This is
not a claim that arbitrary initial states physically find a global minimum,
that a finite noisy record fixes an exact critical point, or that arbitrary
frustrated networks admit efficient global optimization. Sections 10-11
give exact failures of those extensions. No historical novelty, physical
deployment or practical superiority is inferred from the construction.

The acquired port replacement theorem is an accepted premise, preserved
byte-for-byte in the predecessor archive. Its governing source assumptions,
instrument contracts, costs and limitations are not re-proved or weakened.

1. INPUT, INFORMATION AND OPERATION CONTRACT

There are finitely many independent source components. On a real Hilbert
space, component j satisfies

    x_j' = -H_j x_j + B_j v_j,     y_j = B_j* x_j,
    H_j = H_j* >= a_j I > 0,       B_j* B_j = I,
    ran B_j subset Dom H_j.

The ports are fixed, finite, normalized and collocated. The source is globally
linear and stationary. The supplied priors include a_j, a bound on
A_j=B_j*H_jB_j and on ||(I-B_jB_j*)H_jB_j||. Hidden dimension may be infinite;
there is no global frequency cutoff or full-state query in this operation.
Unbounded boundary/point ports and unknown leading port normalization remain
outside the predecessor acquisition theorem.

Each paid acquisition delivers a rational positive-residue model

    Zhat_j(s) = s I + Ahat_j - sum_l R_jl/(s+lambda_jl)

and a certified E_j with

    sup_{omega in R} ||Z_j(i omega)-Zhat_j(i omega)|| <= E_j.       (1)

E_j includes instrument, actuation, calibration, clock, initial residual,
record quantization, inversion, fitting and spectral-coverage errors under
the warranted acquisition channel. Fixed adversarial noise does not average
away. Refining E_j requires a genuinely refinable source/instrument contract
and its charged costs, not additional arithmetic on an unchanged record.

The learner computes the rational static statistic

    Shat_j = Zhat_j(0) = Ahat_j - sum_l R_jl/lambda_jl.

For S_j=Z_j(0), S_j>=a_j I and ||S_j-Shat_j||<=E_j. Stack the independent
components into block diagonal H,B,S,Shat; let n>=1 be the total port count.
Then ||S-Shat||<=max_j E_j. This static statistic is extracted from the earned
causal model; it is not an assumed exact transfer function or a new experiment.

A later assembly specifies a constant reciprocal law

    v = K y - beta odot y^3 + h,     K=K^T, beta_i>0.             (2)

All products in the cubic term are componentwise. K need not be passive.
The task includes a known sign gauge Sigma=diag(sigma_i), sigma_i in {+1,-1}.
In q=Sigma y coordinates, write

    T = Sigma(S-K)Sigma,     f=Sigma h.

The positive algorithm requires

    T_ij <= 0 for i != j,
    the graph of strictly negative off-diagonals is connected,
    f >= 0.                                                       (3)

These are conditions on the effective static interaction, not merely on
the controller K. They must hold for every source/calibration realization
included in the reported guarantee. A robust entrywise upper matrix is one
finite sufficient check. No sign structure is inferred from fitting examples.

The mathematical operation accepts rational nominal T0,beta0,f0, certified
uncertainty bounds, and a rational desired numerical accuracy delta>0. It
returns an approximation to the positive global branch, rigorous ranges
for the true branch, robust phase conclusions when supported, and optionally
a direct rational inverse design for a requested target p>0 and sign gauge.
At zero field an ordered state has a global sign ambiguity, explicitly
represented as the pair {+q,-q}, or {+Sigma q,-Sigma q} in physical ports.

Coefficient calibration may be represented by a stationary symmetric error
Delta_K, ||Delta_K||<=kappa, per-entry errors |Delta_beta_i|<=b_i and
|Delta_f_i|<=e_i, with beta0_i-b_i>0 and f0_i-e_i>=0. Put
E=max_j E_j+kappa. A time-varying error, latency, asymmetric coupling,
unmodelled actuator state or measurement noise continually re-entering (2)
does not satisfy this autonomous gradient contract. Such a physical
implementation needs its own warranted extension. The present output is
the certified mathematical design and prediction, not a hidden claim to
have built or operated that controller.

No initialization is needed to define or digitally compute an equilibrium.
The dynamics theorem below permits every finite-norm initial state. The
earlier acquisition's finite relaxation and its charged residual remain
in E_j. Initialization affects which equilibrium is reached; it does not
change the equilibrium set. No exact physical reset is introduced.

2. EXACT REDUCTION OF THE COMPLETE EQUILIBRIUM LANDSCAPE

Decompose x=B y+xi, xi perpendicular to ran B. In these coordinates

    H = [[A,V*],[V,D]],     D>=a I,
    S = A-V*D^{-1}V = (B*H^{-1}B)^{-1}.                            (4)

Because ran B subset Dom H and has finite dimension, V is bounded; the
off-diagonal block is bounded and D is self-adjoint on the hidden space.
The formula is also valid for the associated closed quadratic forms.

For any C^2 potential U on the ports, the gradient flow

    x'=-Hx-B grad U(B*x)

has potential

    Ecal(x)=<x,Hx>/2+U(y)
           = W_U(y)+||D^{1/2}(xi+D^{-1}Vy)||^2/2,
    W_U(y)=y^T S y/2+U(y).                                       (5)

Consequently:

* Global minima correspond exactly to minima of W_U, with hidden coordinate
  xi=-D^{-1}Vy. There is no omitted metastable hidden equilibrium.
* Every critical point corresponds exactly to a critical point of W_U.
* At a critical point the number of negative Hessian directions, and the
  nullity, equal those of S+D^2 U(y). The hidden block is positive.
* Two sources with the same S have the same entire equilibrium landscape
  and the same instability indices for every such later port potential U.
  Their transient trajectories and settling rates may differ.

Proof: complete the square in (4). The bounded invertible triangular change
(y,xi) -> (y,xi+D^{-1}Vy) gives the stated quadratic-form congruence, so
negative index and nullity are preserved. Equilibria satisfy the hidden
equation D xi+V y=0, which has the unique solution in (5). Since H>=aI and
B is an isometry, minimizing <x,Hx> subject to B*x=y also gives S>=aI.

For (2), after the sign gauge, the effective potential is

    W(q) = q^T T q/2 + sum_i beta_i q_i^4/4 - f^T q.              (6)

Thus an acquired finite matrix is task-sufficient for the entire static
landscape of this family of assemblies, including nonconvex ones. The
all-frequency causal model is still needed for transient tasks; (5) does
not replace that model for arbitrary future operations.

The term 'ground state' in this report means a global minimum of (6).
It does not assert a finite-temperature Gibbs distribution, a calibrated
thermodynamic free energy or thermodynamic equilibrium of an active device.

3. COMPLETE DYNAMICAL CONSEQUENCE FOR THE CUBIC CLASS

For every finite-norm initial x(0), the autonomous system (2) has a unique
global mild solution; it is strong for t>0 and converges to one equilibrium.
This assertion holds even without (3), for every symmetric K and beta_i>0.
In particular there are no nonconstant periodic or recurrent attractors
in this autonomous class. It is not a theorem about arbitrary delayed,
nonreciprocal or forced networks.

Here are the details needed in infinite hidden dimension.

Let a=min a_j, k>=||K||, b=min beta_i>0, h0=max_i |h_i|. Since
|t|<=1+t^2,

    y^T K y - sum beta_i y_i^4 + h^T y
      <= n h0 + n(k+h0)^2/(4b) = C.

Hence d||x||^2/dt <= -2a||x||^2+2C. All trajectories are bounded, so
the locally Lipschitz finite-rank cubic perturbation has no finite blow-up.
The source generator -H is self-adjoint analytic. Because B and HB are
bounded finite-rank maps, variation of constants implies x(t) in Dom H
for t>0 and a continuous strong solution there.

After any fixed positive time, the trajectory is asymptotically precompact
in the graph norm of H. Indeed, both kernel maps

    t -> exp(-Ht)B,   t -> H exp(-Ht)B=exp(-Ht)HB

are norm-continuous and have integrable exponentially decaying bounds.
The response to a bounded input is the integral of these kernels times that
input. Truncate the tail, approximate each kernel uniformly on the compact
remaining interval by a step function, and observe that the approximate
responses lie in a fixed finite-dimensional bounded set. The approximation
error is uniform over bounded inputs. The homogeneous part tends to zero
in the same graph norm. This proves the claimed precompactness without
assuming a compact resolvent, finite hidden order or discrete spectrum.

For t>0, differentiating the potential gives

    d Ecal(x(t))/dt = -||x'(t)||^2.                               (7)

The potential is bounded below: the positive quartic bounds its negative
finite-dimensional terms, while the completed hidden square in (5) is
nonnegative. Energy is continuous on the graph-norm compact closure.
The omega-limit set is compact, connected, invariant, and has constant
energy. Applying (7) to its trajectories shows that it consists of equilibria.

There are only finitely many equilibria. The n polynomial equations

    beta_i q_i^3+(Tq)_i-f_i=0                                    (8)

have pairwise relatively prime leading monomials q_i^3 in any graded
monomial order. The elementary coprime-leading-monomial criterion makes
them a Groebner basis; the quotient has the 3^n monomials with individual
exponents 0,1,2 as a basis. There are at most 3^n complex roots, counted
with multiplicity, hence finitely many real equilibria. A connected
omega-limit set in this finite set is a singleton. This proves convergence.
The proof is not a root-enumeration algorithm and incurs no 3^n computation
in the positive operation below.

At a nondegenerate equilibrium, the full linearization is minus a
self-adjoint finite-rank perturbation of H. Its unstable dimension is
exactly the negative index of

    T+3 diag(beta_i q_i^2).                                      (9)

Thus hidden passive memory cannot create a Hopf bifurcation in this class.
Degenerate equilibria require the nonlinear potential; zero eigenvalues
are not counted as an exponential stability certificate.

4. GLOBAL ORDER, PHASE CLASSIFICATION AND HIDDEN CONVEXITY

Assume (3). Replacing q by |q| cannot increase (6). Write c_ij=-T_ij>=0
for i<j and set z_i=q_i^2 on the nonnegative orthant. Then

    Phi(z) = sum_i T_ii z_i/2 + sum_i beta_i z_i^2/4
             -sum_{i<j} c_ij sqrt(z_i z_j) -sum_i f_i sqrt(z_i),
    z>=0.                                                        (10)

The geometric mean and square root are concave on their stated domains.
Thus Phi is a closed strongly convex function, with strong convexity
modulus b/2, b=min beta_i. Its unique minimizer is the squared amplitude
of the positive global branch of (6). This is a known hidden-convexity
mechanism; the report does not claim to originate it.

Complete classification:

(a) f=0 and T>=0: the only global minimum is q=0, including T singular.
    The positive quartic excludes other zero-energy states.
(b) f=0 and T is not PSD: there is a unique q*>0 in the positive orthant;
    all global minima are exactly +q* and -q*.
(c) f>=0 and f is not identically zero: there is a unique global minimum
    q*>0. The field selects its orientation.

Proof: a symmetric irreducible Z-matrix has a strictly positive eigenvector
for its lowest eigenvalue (apply Perron-Frobenius after a large diagonal
shift). If that eigenvalue is negative, a small multiple gives W<0, so a
nonzero minimizer exists. If a nonnegative nonzero minimizer had a zero
coordinate adjacent to a positive one, its one-sided derivative there
would be negative. Connectedness therefore forces strict positivity.
The same argument applies for nonzero f>=0. Strict convexity in z gives
unique amplitudes. Equality in the absolute-value comparison forces a
common sign along every edge; a nonzero nonnegative field selects +.

Every strictly positive solution of (8) is this global minimum. This can
be checked without a local-versus-global optimizer assertion. For any p>0
with (Tp)_i+beta_i p_i^3=f_i, one has the exact identity, for every real q,

    W(q)-W(p)
      = sum_i beta_i (q_i^2-p_i^2)^2/4
        +sum_{i<j} c_ij p_i p_j (q_i/p_i-q_j/p_j)^2/2
        +sum_i f_i (q_i-p_i)^2/(2p_i).                            (11)

Expanding proves it. Every term is nonnegative. This is a compact global
certificate, not a statement based only on the Hessian at p.

For a zero-field family T(g)=D0-g C, g>=0, with D0 positive diagonal and C
symmetric, nonnegative, nonzero and connected, the exact nominal threshold is

    g_c = 1/lambda_max(D0^{-1/2} C D0^{-1/2}).                    (12)

If C=0 there is no finite threshold in this family. The general input need
not have this one-parameter form: the rational PSD
test on T resolves its nominal zero-field phase. At criticality the
amplitudes remain computable without dividing by a vanishing spectral gap.
For the executed row-stochastic symmetric C and D0=I, g_c=1 exactly.

5. A COMPLETE FINITE-BIT ALGORITHM, INCLUDING CONDITIONING DATA

Let T,beta,f be the rational nominal input satisfying (3). For n=1 the
same construction applies with no edges. Let m be the number of strictly
negative off-diagonal pairs, b=min beta_i, B=max_i sum_j |T_ij| and
H=sum_i |f_i|. A deliberately loose rational radius is

    R = 2+4n(B+H+1)/min(b,1).                                   (13)

Every minimizer has ||q||<R. In fact W(q)>=b||q||^4/(4n)-B||q||^2/2-H||q||,
and for ||q||>=R the right side is positive, whereas W(0)=0.

Use d=3n+m real variables z_i,t_i,v_i,u_ij. Minimize the linear objective

    L = sum_i T_ii z_i/2+sum_i beta_i t_i/4
          -sum_edges c_ij u_ij -sum_i f_i v_i                    (14)

subject to nonnegativity, sum_i z_i<=R^2, and the 2-by-2 PSD constraints

    [[t_i,z_i],[z_i,1]] >=0,
    [[z_i,u_ij],[u_ij,z_j]] >=0,
    [[z_i,v_i],[v_i,1]] >=0.                                    (15)

Also bound every variable above by U=4R^4+4. These are rotated
second-order-cone constraints; no general nonconvex subroutine is used.
For fixed z the optimum in (14) uses t_i=z_i^2 and, whenever the respective
coefficient is positive, u_ij=sqrt(z_i z_j), v_i=sqrt(z_i). Therefore (14)
has exactly the global value of (6). Bounds (13) and U exclude no optimum.

The finite-bit convex theorem's geometric hypotheses are supplied, not
assumed. The rational point

    z_i=1, t_i=2, u_ij=1/4, v_i=1/4

is strictly feasible. Its Euclidean ball of radius 1/64 is feasible:
the first PSD block has smallest eigenvalue (3-sqrt(5))/2>3/8, the other
blocks have margin 3/4, and each perturbation has Frobenius norm at most
twice the variable perturbation norm. Nonnegativity, U and the sum-z
constraint all have larger slack by (13). The feasible body lies within
distance d U of this point.

For a literal standard-form SDP, put all blocks in (15), the lower/upper
scalar boxes, and the scalar slack R^2-sum z_i on one block diagonal matrix
M(x). Its order is

    k = 10n+4m+1.

The affine map is injective, because the scalar lower boxes contain all
variables. For perturbations, ||delta x||<=||delta M||_F<=4d||delta x||.
Thus the known relative-affine inner/outer radii can be taken as

    r_SDP=1/64,     R_SDP=4d^2 U.                                (16)

Affine equations specifying M(x) are rational, and may be obtained by
rational Gaussian elimination; there are at most k(k+1)/2 independent
equations. This supplies every input to the accepted finite-bit SDP
algorithm of Grotschel-Lovasz-Schrijver/de Klerk-Vallentin, including the
known feasible center. Its intermediate arithmetic precision is bounded
by that theorem's rational rounding procedure. An unconstrained assertion
that 'an SDP is polynomial' would not suffice here.

For requested numerical L2 amplitude error delta in (0,1], choose

    eta = min(1, b delta^4/(64n)).                               (17)

Compute a rational feasible solution of (14)-(15) of objective at most
eta above optimum by that finite-bit algorithm. Strong convexity gives

    ||z-z*||_2 <= 2 sqrt(eta/b),
    ||sqrt(z)-sqrt(z*)||_2 <= (4n eta/b)^(1/4) <= delta/2.        (18)

Compute each square root by integer/rational bisection to absolute error
at most delta/(2n). Its contribution to L2 error is at most delta/2.
This returns a rational approximation with proved error <=delta.
At zero field the exact rational PSD test can return q=0 immediately.

Cost statement: let L_in be the COMPLETE binary input length, including
all retained-model coefficients, calibrated intervention data and bounds.
Computing Shat uses sum_j O(L_j r_j^2) rational operations for L_j residues.
Parsing and all bit lengths are charged. The conic part has d=3n+m variables,
matrix order k=10n+4m+1, at most k(k+1)/2 affine equations, coefficient
length polynomial in L_in+log n, and the explicit radius ratio (16).
The cited uniform algorithm's bit running time is polynomial in

    k, k(k+1)/2, L_SDP, log2(256 d^2 U), log2(1/eta),              (19)

where L_SDP is the fully formed rational SDP encoding length. Even a dense
standard encoding is polynomial in n and L_in. Substitution of (13)-(17)
therefore gives polynomial bit time in L_in+n+log(1/delta). Rational PSD
testing and square-root recovery are polynomial in the same quantities.
Equation (19) uses an accepted complete finite-bit algorithm, not a hidden
unit-cost solver oracle. No iteration count for a floating local solver is
substituted for it. This treatment does not select or claim an unproved
universal numerical exponent for a different interior-point implementation.

The acquired coefficients can themselves be long. The total operation is

    sum_j acquisition_cost_j(E_j, priors_j, instrument_j)
      + rational static reduction
      + the finite-bit cost in (19)
      + certificate/output encoding.                            (20)

The predecessor acquisition has expanded dimensions depending on inverse
accuracy; it is not polynomial in log(1/E_j). Its finite experiment time,
amplitudes, waveform/readout bandwidth, precision and preprocessing costs
are all retained in (20). A small digital cost here does not erase them.

6. SOURCE AND NUMERICAL ERRORS IN ONE CERTIFICATE

Let qbar>=0 have nominal global energy gap at most eta. Suppose
||T-T0||<=E, ||f-f0||<=e_f, max_i |beta_i-beta0_i|<=e_beta, and
beta_i>=b>0, with (3) valid for each realization. Let R bound the norm of
every actual minimizer, using (13) with enlarged coefficient bounds.
Define

    D(r)=E r^2/2+e_f r+e_beta r^4/4,
    Gamma=eta+D(||qbar||)+D(R).                                  (21)

Then

    W_actual(qbar)-min W_actual <= Gamma,
    ||qbar-q_actual||_2 <= (4n Gamma/b)^(1/4).                    (22)

Proof: compare nominal and actual potentials at qbar and at the actual
minimizer; insert the nominal minimum between them. This gives (21).
Apply strong convexity of the actual Phi and the square-root inequality
used in (18). This bound remains valid at a zero-field transition and
keeps the source uncertainty and numerical residual in the same budget.
It is conservative, and is not asserted to be a sharp posterior range.

For independent single-port components, with only static diagonal source
errors and all other coefficients fixed, (10) gives the sharper relation

    ||z(s)-z(s')||_2 <= ||s-s'||_2/b.                             (23)

It follows from strong monotonicity and the gradient change (s-s')/2.
Equation (23) is a diagonal-uncertainty statement; it is not silently
extended to multiport off-diagonal errors.

For a rational positive candidate q, a fully exact standalone certificate
used in the execution is available. Let z=q^2 and

    g_i=((Tq)_i+beta_i q_i^3-f_i)/(2q_i).

The quadratic minorant

    Phi(w)>=W(q)+g^T(w-z)+b||w-z||^2/4,   w>=0                  (24)

has rational minimum at w_i=max(0,z_i-2g_i/b). Subtracting this minimum
from W(q) gives an exact rational global gap, with no optimizer status
assumption. The simpler gap sum_i g_i^2/b also works. At q=0, f=0,
PSD(T) is the exact certificate. This is the executed checker; the general
conic algorithm (19) is proved and costed, not executed in this report.

7. ORDER INTERVALS AND PHASE UNCERTAINTY

Suppose an entrywise symmetric error matrix d>=0 satisfies
|T_ij-T0_ij|<=d_ij. Source block j permits d_ij=E_j within that block
and zero across different source blocks; coupling calibration adds its
warranted entrywise bound. Define

    Tplus=T0+d, Tminus=T0-d,
    beta_plus/minus=beta0 +/- b_error,
    f_plus/minus=f0 +/- e_error.

If Tplus and Tminus satisfy (3), beta_minus>0 and f_minus>=0, then

    q(Tplus,beta_plus,f_minus)
       <= q_actual <= q(Tminus,beta_minus,f_plus)                 (25)

componentwise, with q=0 allowed in the disordered zero-field regime.
These are outer-task extrema; the endpoint matrices need not themselves
be compatible with the original joint frequency record. No sharp posterior
claim is made. The elementwise enlargement can be much looser than the
operator-norm error, so it can contain zero even when the operator-norm
phase test proves every actual source ordered.

Proof of comparison: for two positive roots, suppose alpha=max_i q2_i/q1_i
exceeds one. At an attaining coordinate, off-diagonal nonpositivity gives
T2 q2 >= alpha T2 q1 in that coordinate. If T2>=T1, beta2>=beta1 and
f2<=f1, substitution of the first root yields

    (T2q2)_i+beta2_i q2_i^3-f2_i
       >= alpha f1_i-f2_i+(alpha^3-alpha)beta1_i q1_i^3 >0,

a contradiction. When the larger root would be zero, nonnegative
quadratic forms on the nonnegative orthant and the Z sign imply the PSD
case remains PSD in the ordering direction. This completes the zero case.

An exact rational sub/supersolution check supplies numerical enclosures:
if ell>0, u>0 and

    T ell+beta ell^3-f<=0,
    T u+beta u^3-f>=0,                                           (26)

then ell<=q*<=u. The same maximum-ratio argument proves (26).
The execution uses small rational multiples of a proposed positive root.
At a degenerate zero branch it uses PSD(T); the general algorithm does
not require an unprovided relative root-separation margin.

For zero field, ||T-T0||<=E gives the finite tests

    T0-E I >=0  => every compatible source has q*=0;
    T0+E I has a negative eigenvalue
                 => every compatible source has an ordered pair. (27)

For T0=I-gC, C symmetric nonnegative connected with row sum one,
g<=1-E implies the first conclusion; g>1+E implies the second.
Between them this certificate is unresolved. This does not prove that
both phases are compatible with a particular complete experimental record.
Unknown additive bias would destroy an exact zero-field symmetry; (27)
therefore explicitly requires a warranted zero-field law.

8. DIRECT INVERSE DESIGN OF AN ARBITRARY BALANCED AMPLITUDE PATTERN

Input a rational p>0, rational beta>0, rational f>=0, a symmetric connected
nonnegative edge matrix W with zero diagonal, and a sign gauge Sigma.
Set

    L_p=diag((Wp)_i/p_i)-W,
    T*=L_p+diag(f_i/p_i-beta_i p_i^2),
    K*=Shat-Sigma T* Sigma,     h*=Sigma f.                      (28)

Then p solves the nominal stationarity equations exactly. The exact
weighted-Laplacian identity

    q^T L_p q = sum_{i<j} W_ij p_i p_j(q_i/p_i-q_j/p_j)^2

and (11) prove the complete global claim:

* for f=0, the ground states are exactly +/-Sigma p;
* for f>=0 nonzero, the unique ground state is Sigma p;
* the Hessian at the selected amplitude is
  L_p+diag(f_i/p_i+2 beta_i p_i^2)>0.

No model fitting, nonconvex search or internal source identification is
needed in this inverse operation. It takes O(n^2) rational operations
after Shat has been formed, with the sizes of all p_i reciprocals, gains,
coefficients and output entries charged. It permits arbitrary prescribed
nonzero amplitudes and balanced sign patterns; it does not encode arbitrary
frustrated constraints or promise an exponential associative-memory capacity.

The design is robust by finite checks, rather than by declaring Shat exact.
Choose 0<theta<1, ell=(1-theta)p, u=(1+theta)p. If

    (T*+d)ell+(beta+b_error)ell^3-(f-e_error)<=0,
    (T*-d)u+(beta-b_error)u^3-(f+e_error)>=0,                    (29)

and the robust sign/positivity conditions of Section 7 hold, every true
ground state on the selected branch lies in [ell,u]. These are rational
inequalities with O(n^2) arithmetic cost. They include source uncertainty,
stationary controller calibration and a positive field calibration range.

A useful complete existence deduction has a real actuation cost. With
exact beta and f=0, let D_i=sum_j d_ij p_j. It suffices to select beta_i
such that, for alpha_l=1-theta and alpha_u=1+theta,

    beta_i p_i^3(1-alpha_l^2) >= D_i,
    beta_i p_i^3(alpha_u^2-1) >= D_i.                            (30)

Both follow directly from (29), after division by alpha_l or alpha_u.
Thus any requested relative accuracy can be guaranteed at fixed bounded
static uncertainty by a sufficiently strong onsite saturation and matching
active self-coupling, provided the controller contract permits those gains.
They scale as D_i/(theta p_i^3), and K's diagonal scales as beta_i p_i^2.
This trades actuation size for robustness; it is not information-free
precision or a free hardware improvement. A supplied gain limit can make
the requested certificate unavailable. Under calibrated beta/f uncertainty,
the more general finite inequalities (29), not (30), govern the output.

The operation constructs a global potential minimum, not a universal
physical basin-selection protocol. Section 10 exhibits why that distinction
cannot be omitted. For intended physical port-power units the ordinary
passivity identity also gives, at a stationary output y,

    y^T v=y^T S y>=a||y||^2.                                   (31)

The active ordered state is not advertised as requiring no supply. A
conversion of these normalized supply units to energy or monetary cost
requires the corresponding calibration; none is invented here.

9. EXECUTED MULTICOMPONENT OPERATION

Only the two previously acquired three-port model JSON files are used.
Their native sources had 256 hidden coordinates each; the new solver never
loads those sources or queries them. The retained component models each
have 21 states. The execution uses six coupled ports and makes zero new
source observations.

The source errors, rounded upward to 30 fractional bits, are
E_A=0.007822413928806782 and E_B=0.00805309321731329. A stationary symmetric
coupling calibration allowance kappa=1/4096 is added, giving
E_total=0.00829723384231329. Each cubic coefficient has calibration allowance
1/4096. The zero-field scan assumes the bias channel is exactly disabled.
The pattern task instead includes independent field errors <=1/8192,
around a positive nominal field 1/64, preserving its sign.

10. EXACT METASTABILITY AND SLOWING FAILURES

An attractive graph can possess stable nonglobal equilibria. Take two ports,
f=0, beta=1, diagonal T_ii=-mu and off-diagonal T_12=-c, with mu>2c>0.
Then the opposite-sign states

    +/-sqrt(mu-c)(1,-1)

are equilibria. Their Hessian eigenvalues are 2mu-4c and 2mu-2c, both
positive. Their energy is -(mu-c)^2/2. The global pair is

    +/-sqrt(mu+c)(1,1),    energy=-(mu+c)^2/2.

The strict gap is 2mu c. The executed exact member mu=5,c=1 has nonglobal
equilibrium (2,-2), Hessian eigenvalues 6 and 8, and energy -8, whereas the
global energy is -18. It has an open attracting basin. This falsifies the
claim that connected attraction or a Lyapunov function guarantees global
optimization by relaxation from every initial condition.

Even when no basin issue intervenes, physical convergence need not have
the digital algorithm's precision dependence. The critical one-port flow
q'=-q^3 has q(t)=q(0)/sqrt(1+2q(0)^2 t). Reaching amplitude delta from a
fixed nonzero initial state takes order delta^{-2} time. The mathematical
convergence theorem is therefore not a uniform fast physical solver.

11. FRUSTRATION: AN EXPLICIT NP-HARDNESS REDUCTION

The sign restriction is substantive even for perfectly known, memoryless,
finite components. Let a graph have nonnegative integer edge weights J_ij,
and define the Ising objective E_I(sigma)=sum_edges J_ij sigma_i sigma_j,
sigma_i in {+1,-1}. Its minimum determines weighted MAX CUT.

Consider the continuous quartic potential

    F(q)=beta/4 sum_i(q_i^2-1)^2+sum_edges J_ij q_i q_j.          (32)

It is realized, up to a constant, by H=I,B=I, K_ii=1+beta,
K_ij=-J_ij and cubic coefficient beta. This is within the reciprocal cubic
architecture, but general graphs with frustrated cycles are outside (3).

Let D_i=sum_j J_ij, dmax=max D_i, M=sum_i D_i^2. Round sigma_i=sign(q_i),
choosing either sign at zero, and put r_i=|q_i|-1. Since r_i>=-1,

    (q_i^2-1)^2=r_i^2(r_i+2)^2>=r_i^2.

The change in the coupling term under rounding is at most

    sum_i D_i |r_i|+(dmax/2)||r||^2.

For beta>=4dmax this proves

    F(q)>=E_I(sigma)+(beta/8)||r||^2-sqrt(M)||r||
        >=E_I(sigma)-2M/beta.                                  (33)

On the other hand min F<=min E_I by taking q=sigma. Choose the integer

    beta=32M+4dmax+1.

Its bit length is polynomial in the complete graph/weight encoding. Any
candidate with F(q)<=min F+1/8 then satisfies

    E_I(sign q)<=min E_I+1/8+2M/beta<min E_I+3/16.

Since the Ising values are integers, the rounded assignment is an exact
MAX CUT solution. Thus a uniform polynomial-bit algorithm for this general
frustrated extension, even at fixed additive accuracy 1/8, would imply P=NP.
This is an NP-hardness result and a conditional boundary, not a proof that
P differs from NP. All unrestricted SAT predecessors remain unresolved.

The finite eight-node control enumerates 256 sign assignments to check one
member and its rounding certificate. Its exhaustive cost is stated. That
control is not the proof of (33) or a proposed scalable optimization method.

12. CONSEQUENCES AND EXACT REACH

The earned operation has three layers of breadth.

First, the Schur statistic S classifies all equilibria and instability
indices for every smooth conservative finite-port attachment whose
potential is defined. Infinite internal memory does not add independent
static order parameters. This is an exact task equivalence, not internal
identification; no algorithm for arbitrary nonconvex U is implied.

Second, in the attractive/balanced cubic class, the full global ordered
branch is computable by a uniform finite-bit convex algorithm for arbitrary
network size, topology and heterogeneous positive saturation. It remains
certifiable across a phase transition, with real source/calibration
uncertainty. Stable mixed-sign states are distinguished from global minima.

Third, a complete direct formula constructs arbitrary prescribed nonzero
balanced amplitude patterns, with compact global certificates and finite
robustness inequalities. The executed six-port instance demonstrates a new
later design task using acquired records, not renewed hidden-state access.

The operation does not require a laboratory gate for these mathematical
consequences. Its real limits remain: supplied global source structure;
warranted noise and normalization; conservative instantaneous reciprocal
interventions; a certified effective sign class; controller gain/calibration
budgets; uncertainty near criticality; and physical basin/settling limits.
No universal physical optimizer, thermal material law, P-versus-NP
resolution, historical first, or civilisation-scale practical consequence
has been established. Those larger conclusions do not follow merely from
the names of the source methods or the scope of the ambition.
