Invent a regenerative air-to-CO2 concentrator with low water use and a competitive complete process.

Can a new, physically implementable separation mechanism turn ordinary humid air into a useful concentrated carbon-dioxide stream without the large regeneration, water-handling and equipment penalties that defeat many attractive sorbent calculations? Develop the complete useful operation, not just a new adsorption isotherm, an ideal humidity cycle or a favourable material-capacity number.

The design target is a modular device capturing at least 1 kg CO2 per day from air containing 425 ppm CO2 at atmospheric pressure, delivering at least 95-percent CO2 on a dry basis at 1 bar. It must operate over a 100-hour run with at least 50-percent single-pass CO2 removal in its admitted capture stream. Use ambient temperatures 20--30 degrees Celsius and relative humidity 20--40 percent as the declared input envelope. Do not assume that the inlet is oxygen-free, pre-dried, preconcentrated or supplied by a free laboratory gas factory. You may specify a realistic particulate filter and its charged replacement/pressure drop. If active conditioning is needed anywhere, its energy, equipment and water belong to the device.

The targets are at most 0.50 kWh of purchased energy per kg of delivered CO2, no externally supplied process heat above ambient, and at most 2 kg net water consumption per kg CO2. Count startup, desorption, every fan/pump/vacuum/compressor, controls, drying, cooling, purification and product collection over the complete run. Distinguish electrical work from ambient heat and the chemical free energy consumed by evaporation into unsaturated air. Ambient heat and natural humidity gradients are allowed physical inputs, but are not evidence of energy-free separation. Count initial liquid inventories, water quality and treatment, losses to air, entrainment, aerosols and contaminated discharge. Recirculation is not recovery unless the actual return path closes.

At a credible 1-tonne-per-day modular production scale, aim for a full capture-and-delivery cost at most US$100 per tonne at electricity US$0.05/kWh and make-up water US$2/m3. Include contactors, active material and its synthesis, replacement, blowers, switching, vacuum/pressure equipment, controls, structures, plumbing, labour, waste, maintenance, yield and financing. State the annual utilization, capital life and discount assumptions, and show how actual material/device lifetime enters the result. A desired sorbent lifetime or factory learning curve is not an existing asset. Cost the first 1-kg/day demonstrator separately. Product compression above 1 bar, transport, utilization and permanent storage are outside this output operation, but must be added before claiming a sequestration cost or a negative-emissions result.

You may use moisture swing, electrochemical swing, membranes, reactive transport, a new material/process architecture or a different separation principle. The mechanism is your choice. No perfect selective membrane, freely supplied ion-exchange capacity, exact reversible affinity, sacrificial regenerant, uncharged alkaline sink or unmanufactured nanostructure may replace a physical constituent. All active materials and fluids must have an attainable synthesis or procurement route, quantities, operating conditions and a consistent full chemical/material balance. A catalyst, salt or solvent consumed in each cycle is feedstock, even if named a mediator.

There is a real starting opening but it is incomplete. A moisture-driven laboratory/pilot construction has moved commercial quaternary-ammonium ion-exchange resins between ambient air and an alkaline liquid, thereby delivering captured carbon into that liquid. Its actual belt/mesh transport and rapid wet release are useful mechanisms. They do not supply pure gaseous CO2 or a free alkaline regeneration loop. Bulk retained water, drying/loading time and the productivity--water tradeoff remain important. The reported low-cost high-capacity low-water scenario is aspirational, not an achieved material. A separate vacuum/moisture process uses air evacuation, vapor stripping and condensation; its ambient heat input does not eliminate actual vapor-flow and compression work. You are free to develop a different opening rather than extend either representation.

A few useful physical contracts:
- For an ideal isothermal gas separator, with inlet mole fraction x, capture fraction r and mixing function g(x)=x log x+(1-x)log(1-x), the reversible work per captured mole of pure product at the same pressure is RT[(1-r*x)g(x(1-r)/(1-r*x))-g(x)]/(r*x). At 25 C, x=425 ppm and r=1/2, this is about 20.01 kJ/mol, or 126.3 kWh per tonne. It is a thermodynamic diagnostic, not an attainable process.
- That operation processes about 2616 m3 of inlet air per kg CO2 at 25 C. At an illustrative 50 Pa pressure drop and 60-percent fan efficiency, air movement alone costs about 60.6 kWh/tonne. Neither the pressure drop nor fan efficiency is supplied for a real contactor.
- If a moisture cycle changes water loading by Delta w kg water/kg dry material and captures Delta q mol CO2/kg material, its evaporated-water burden before actual recovery is Delta w/(0.04401 Delta q) kg/kg. Faster drying alone does not lower this ratio. Reducing thickness may accelerate diffusion while increasing support area, coating cost or gas-side pressure losses.
- Reversible evaporation into air of relative humidity phi releases at most RT log(1/phi) of chemical free energy per mole of water at the stated temperature. Humidity conditioning and water recovery must fit the same balance; they cannot be credited on only one side.

Construct the physical geometry, transport and switching/regeneration mechanism, active-material preparation, operating cycle, product collection and control. Work through measured or bounded equilibria, coupled water/CO2/O2 transport, heat and mass transfer, pressure drops, finite rates, leakage and cyclic degradation. Give an executable model that predicts complete-run production, water and energy and that exposes rather than hides uncertainty. Use actual cyclic working capacity, not merely dry equilibrium uptake. Address how changing humidity and temperature across the declared envelope changes the rate and the useful output. An excellent mass-transfer calculation cannot stand in for unconstructed selective chemistry, and a good isotherm cannot stand in for a contactor.

Compare the entire same-output operation against the strongest relevant current direct-air-capture approaches. Keep measured demonstrations, theoretical lower bounds, company targets and aspirational cost models distinct. Do not compare pure CO2 from one route with bicarbonate solution, a dilute greenhouse stream or unpressurized exhaust from another. If an incumbent already meets this full contract, an invention must gain a consequential new advantage rather than reproduce it.

Develop a concrete smaller decisive prototype for later physical execution, with an attainable build sequence, materials/components and access, measured carbon and water balances, ordinary controls, contamination checks, sensors and metering, numerical pass/fail conditions and total cost. It must distinguish genuinely regenerated capture from consumption of initial alkalinity or accumulated material. Design this experiment here; do not perform it. State what its success would and would not establish about the 1-kg/day device and production-scale cost.

Pursue the complete invention question. Discover and work through a mechanism or engineering design that could actually provide the operation, with all critical physical assumptions explicit. A proposal-only answer, a model with gifted material properties or a claim that one failed route obstructs every separator does not complete the problem.
