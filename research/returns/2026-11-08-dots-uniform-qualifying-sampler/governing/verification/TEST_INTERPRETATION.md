# What the clean checks certify

The release runner executes all 31 bundled programs in a disposable copy and records exit codes, timings and logs. A zero exit code means that program completed and its built-in assertions passed; it does not upgrade a numerical diagnostic into a proof.

The new construction is checked by:

- 12 geometric slicing instances and 24 small noncommuting Taylor examples;
- exact coefficient-tensor interpolation, including redundant bonds, cancelling 421–603-bit raw gauges and zero tensors;
- a three-qubit local-generator coefficient-MPO prototype with 25 coefficients, 384 integer-entry comparisons and 48 normalized scalar cases;
- 224 exact Y-containing warm-boundary Taylor identities, 112 Walsh attenuation checks and 31 prefix-energy identities;
- independent combined cutoff, geometry, padding and retiling checks.

The older dependency diagnostics include exhaustive first-discovery words, full-noise return histories, the deliberately failing literal multi-site target formula and its repair, exact edge-Möbius algebra, trace-normalized finite-patch bounds, coefficient regularity, product probes, membership identities and finite sampling diagnostics. The failed old formula is an expected counterexample, not a failed release check.

The prototype polynomial need not be a positive channel. Its reported half-l1 output difference is a scalar diagnostic, not permission to sample directly from it. General error, rank, bit-height and complexity bounds are supplied by the proofs and reviews, not inferred from these small tests.

Saved timing fields vary by machine. Floating-point results may vary slightly with numerical libraries. Exact identities, assertion outcomes and the declared error tolerances are the reproducible contracts. The full asymptotic sampler is not implemented in this package.
