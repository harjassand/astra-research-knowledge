## 1. The question I chose

Suppose an instrument reveals only a coarse label of a complex system—for example, a binary signal switching between \(+1\) and \(-1\).

There may be many microscopic mechanisms behind that signal. Some consume free energy; others operate at equilibrium. Inferring the mechanism can be difficult because adding hidden states creates more ways to explain the same observations.

I pursued this question:

> **Can a small, explicitly measurable feature of a recording impose a positive dissipation cost on every compatible hidden mechanism—even when all two-time statistics admit an equilibrium explanation and the recording has no visible arrow of time?**

The difficulty is stronger than identifying a driven model that fits the data. A fitted model’s dissipation might disappear when a different hidden mechanism is considered. The desired conclusion must survive arbitrarily many hidden states.

The answer established here is **yes, under the stated physical model**. Three observations per measurement block suffice to expose the obstruction. Many repeated blocks are needed to estimate it reliably; “three observations” does not mean three readings in total.

The consequence is an exact separation between three kinds of evidence:

| Evidence available | Result for the constructed system |
|---|---|
| Every one-time and two-time snapshot distribution | Exactly reproducible by an equilibrium two-state process |
| Forward-versus-backward asymmetry of the complete observed recording | Exactly zero |
| A conditional correlation involving three snapshots | Excludes every equilibrium hidden realization in the specified class and gives a positive rate bound |

This changes what can be inferred from a restricted observation channel. It does not solve microscopic reconstruction or determine the exact dissipation.

## 2. The organizing operation: compare the two sides of the present

The central operation is to compare a future record with the past record read outward from the present.

At equilibrium, conditional on the **complete microscopic present state**, those two records are independent and identically distributed. Forgetting part of the present state preserves a positivity constraint.

For a numerical observed signal \(Y_t\), this implies

\[
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_{-t},Y_t\mid Y_0=y)
=
\operatorname{Var}\!\left(
\mathbb E[Y_t\mid X_0]\mid Y_0=y
\right)
\ge 0,
\]

where \(X_0\) is the hidden microscopic state.

The equality follows in two steps. The past and future are conditionally independent given \(X_0\), so their conditional covariance vanishes. Equilibrium makes their conditional means equal, so the remaining covariance is a variance.

Consequently, **negative conditional covariance rules out equilibrium regardless of how many hidden states are introduced**.

This qualitative principle belongs to the established theory of reflection positivity; it is not a new principle discovered in this work. Reflection-positive Markov processes have a developed mathematical literature. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

The quantitative step developed here compares the conditional forward and reverse path laws directly. It converts a negative reflected correlation into a dissipation lower bound **linear in the size of the violation**.

That distinction matters near equilibrium. A bound quadratic in a small observable defect can become much weaker than one linear in the same defect.

## 3. The quantitative theorem

### Assumptions and physical meaning

Let \(X_t\) be a stationary, finite, irreducible continuous-time Markov chain, with transition-rate matrix \(Q=(q_{ij})\) and stationary probabilities \(\pi_i\). The observed signal is a deterministic, noninvasive label

\[
Y_t=h(X_t).
\]

Assume every microscopic state is **even under physical reversal**: reversing time does not require flipping an unobserved velocity or exchanging hidden states.

Define the entropy production rate, using natural logarithms and \(k_B=1\), by

\[
\sigma
=
\sum_{i<j}
(\pi_iq_{ij}-\pi_jq_{ji})
\log\frac{\pi_iq_{ij}}{\pi_jq_{ji}}.
\]

As mathematics, this is the stationary path irreversibility rate. Its identification with physical entropy production requires a thermodynamically consistent model, including local detailed balance. Under a justified steady isothermal interpretation, the associated dissipation power is \(k_BT\sigma\). This standard connection is also used in the primary literature on dissipation inference. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### Statement

Choose a half-window duration \(t>0\).

Apply the same bounded real score \(g\) to the future half-record and to the past read backward from the present:

\[
g_+=g((Y_s)_{0\le s\le t}),
\qquad
g_-=g((Y_{-s})_{0\le s\le t}).
\]

The score can use a single endpoint or several snapshots. Let

\[
R=\sup g-\inf g
\]

be its range, and choose a central weight \(0\le w(Y_0)\le1\). Define

\[
C=\mathbb E[w(Y_0)g_-g_+].
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sigma\ge \frac{8[-C]_+}{R^2t},
}
\]

where \([x]_+=\max(x,0)\).

The right-hand side involves observed quantities. It contains no hidden-state count, hidden transition rates, or hidden graph.

There is also a nonlinear refinement. With

\[
p=\mathbb E[w],\qquad
\eta=\frac{4(-C)}{pR^2},
\]

a negative \(C\) implies \(0<\eta\le1\), and

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sigma\ge
\frac{2p}{t}\sqrt{\eta}\,
\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\eta}.
}
\]

The linear bound follows from
\(\sqrt{\eta}\operatorname{artanh}\sqrt{\eta}\ge\eta\).

### Proof of the linear bound

The stationary reverse generator is

\[
Q^*=D_\pi^{-1}Q^{\mathsf T}D_\pi.
\]

Let \(P_i\) and \(P_i^*\) be future half-path laws started in hidden state \(i\), generated by \(Q\) and \(Q^*\). Write

\[
u_i=\mathbb E_{P_i}[g],
\qquad
v_i=\mathbb E_{P_i^*}[g].
\]

Conditional independence of the two halves gives

\[
C=\sum_i\pi_iw_i u_iv_i.
\]

Using

\[
uv=\frac{(u+v)^2-(u-v)^2}{4},
\]

we obtain

\[
C\ge
-\frac14\sum_i\pi_iw_i(u_i-v_i)^2.
\]

A bounded observable with range \(R\) has expectation difference at most \(R\) times total variation distance. Pinsker’s inequality therefore yields

\[
(u_i-v_i)^2
\le
\frac{R^2}{2}
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_i\Vert P_i^*).
\]

Finally, the stationary average of these conditional path divergences is exactly

\[
\sum_i\pi_iD_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_i\Vert P_i^*)=t\sigma.
\]

Because \(w_i\le1\),

\[
-C\le\frac{R^2t\sigma}{8}.
\]

This proves the bound. The hidden conditional laws are used only in the proof; evaluating the observable score does not require reconstructing them.

## 4. An exact system that defeats pair statistics and time-arrow tests

Consider the four-state generator

\[
Q=\frac14
\begin{pmatrix}
-3a+2\epsilon&a&a-2\epsilon&a\\
a&-3a-2\epsilon&a&a+2\epsilon\\
a&a+2\epsilon&-3a&a-2\epsilon\\
a-2\epsilon&a&a+2\epsilon&-3a
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
a>0,\quad 0<\epsilon<a/2.

Its off-diagonal rates are positive. Rows and columns sum to zero, so its stationary distribution is uniform.

Observe only

\begin{cases}
+1,&X\in\{1,2\},\\

For every lag \(t\),

\[

These are exactly the distributions of an equilibrium two-state telegraph process that flips at rate \(a/2\).

Since every function of a binary variable is affine in that variable, this agreement includes every two-time correlation of observed state functions.

### The complete observed process is time-reversal symmetric

Let \(\mathsf R\) exchange hidden states 3 and 4. The generator satisfies

\[
Q^{\mathsf T}=\mathsf RQ\mathsf R,

and the observed label is unchanged by that exchange.

\[

\bigl(P_{\rm observed}\Vert P_{\rm observed}^{\rm reversed}\bigr)=0

on every finite observation window.

This violates the equilibrium positivity condition.

The refinement gives

### Why four states matter

I also proved a minimality result for this particular separation.

With at most three hidden states, the mean-zero observable space has dimension at most two. An exactly single-exponential binary autocovariance forces the observed binary function to be an eigenfunction of either the forward generator or its stationary adjoint. A two-valued eigenfunction forces constant transition rates between the observation blocks, making the observed process Markov.

Thus **four hidden states are minimal for an exact telegraph-pair law combined with a non-Markov binary output**.

This is a restricted minimality theorem. Other forms of detectable hidden dissipation can occur with fewer states.

## 5. The representation extends beyond one scalar score

The same reasoning produces a matrix representation.

Let \(U\) and \(V\) denote future and reflected-past observation words, each taking \(k\) possible values. Form

\[
M_{ab}=\Pr(V=a,U=b),
\qquad
S=\frac{M+M^{\mathsf T}}2.
\]

If \(r=S\mathbf1\), define the centered matrix

\[
A=S-rr^{\mathsf T}.
\]

At equilibrium, \(A\) is positive semidefinite. Away from equilibrium, let

\[
\nu=\sum_{\lambda_j(A)<0}|\lambda_j(A)|.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
\sigma\ge\frac{4\nu}{t}.
}
\]

The proof decomposes the reflected moment matrix into a positive mixture term minus a positive semidefinite correction measuring the mismatch between conditional forward and backward laws.

This turns the obstruction into something that can be searched for spectrally: negative directions identify observable scores that violate equilibrium positivity.

There is a computational limitation. Full word tables have \(k^2\) entries, and \(k\) can grow exponentially with word length. Validating a chosen scalar score is inexpensive; discovering a useful score in a general high-dimensional recording may be expensive. The result does not make that search free.

## 6. Turning the theorem into a valid experiment

A population inequality is insufficient for inference. Consecutive triples are dependent, and replacing \(C\) by an empirical mean without controlling that dependence would not establish a confidence bound.

I completed two protocols with different preparation requirements.

### Protocol A: one stationary recording, with no mixing-time input

Fix an endpoint score \(g\), a weight \(w\), a lag \(t\), and the number of blocks \(n\) before inspecting the validation record.

Use consecutive blocks that share their boundary readings:

\[
Z_j=
w(Y_{(2j-1)t})
g(Y_{(2j-2)t})
g(Y_{2jt}),
\qquad
\overline Z=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n Z_j.
\]

If \(|g|\le G\), put \(B=G^2\). For **every stationary equilibrium hidden chain in the stated class**,

\[
\Pr(\overline Z\le-z)
\le
\exp\!\left[
-n\,\operatorname{kl}
\left(\frac{1+z/B}{2}\,\middle\|\,\frac12\right)
\right]
\le
\exp\!\left[-\frac{nz^2}{2B^2}\right].
\]

Here \(\operatorname{kl}\) is binary relative entropy.

This is a dependence-aware bound. It does not assume the blocks are independent, nor require a hidden-state count or a mixing-time estimate.

The proof uses a tilted transition operator for each two-step block. Under equilibrium, its expansion has positive semidefinite coefficient operators. Their signs yield an exponential-moment bound,

\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left(-\theta\sum_j Z_j\right)
\le\cosh(\theta B)^n,
\]

from which the tail inequality follows.

I also derived a conservative numerical rate confidence bound. If \(T=2t\) and \(q(z)\) denotes the first exponential tail bound above, then

\[
L_\delta(z)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{4}{nT}\operatorname{kl}(\delta\Vert q(z)),
& q(z)<\delta,\\[4pt]
0,&q(z)\ge\delta
\end{cases}
\]

satisfies

\[
\Pr\!\left(
L_\delta([-\overline Z]_+)>\sigma
\right)\le\delta.
\]

This follows by comparing the driven process with its additive reversibilization and applying data processing to the rejection event.

**Executed result.** For the four-state example with \(a=1\), \(\epsilon=0.4\), \(t=1\), a prespecified center \(c=0.37\), and \(750{,}000\) consecutive blocks:

\[
\overline Z=-0.01041134.
\]

The worst-case equilibrium tail probability is bounded by

\[
\boxed{9.7436\times10^{-6}}.
\]

The conservative 95% rate lower bound is

\[
\boxed{\sigma\ge1.0092\times10^{-6}}.
\]

The large difference between strong equilibrium rejection and a weak numerical rate bound is real. This procedure is much better at establishing that dissipation is positive than at estimating its magnitude.

Stationary initialization is essential. In this synthetic experiment, it was supplied by drawing the initial state from the known uniform stationary distribution.

### Protocol B: arbitrary initial state, with preparation and waiting charged

For a stronger rate certificate from an arbitrary initial state, assume a supplied uniform mixing envelope

\[
\sup_x\operatorname{TV}(P_s(x,\cdot),\pi)
\le e^{-\kappa s}.
\]

This is extra information about the apparatus. It cannot be inferred merely from the visible exponential autocorrelation.

Before each training or validation block, wait

\[
g_{\rm wait}
=
\kappa^{-1}\log\frac{8(N+M)}{\delta},
\]

where \(M\) and \(N\) are training and validation counts.

A sequential coupling argument then controls the difference from independent stationary blocks. Training selects a center, which is frozen before validation. A bounded-score confidence interval supplies the rate certificate.

For the constructed family,

\[
\kappa=a-2\epsilon
\]

is a valid envelope: the generator contains a uniform-refresh component of that rate.

I executed this procedure from hidden state 0, without resetting the apparatus between blocks.

|---|---:|---:|
| Training blocks | None; score fixed beforehand | 20,000 |
| Validation blocks | 750,000 | 750,000 |
| Binary readings | 1,500,001 | 2,290,000 |
| Modeled elapsed time | 1,500,000 | 73,242,880.5 |

The arbitrary-start protocol waited approximately \(93.15\) time units before each block. Its large waiting bill is included in the reported duration.

Both experiments are synthetic. The code samples exact CTMC transition kernels at the observation times. The durations above are modeled physical time, not CPU runtime. Detector energy, hardware implementation, and physical preparation of an actual apparatus have not been demonstrated.

## 7. What the observation cost permits—and prevents

The weak-driving regime has an unavoidable statistical difficulty.

For independent triples at fixed lag, let \(P_\epsilon\) be the constructed triple law and \(P_0\) the equilibrium telegraph triple law. Their chi-square divergence is exactly

\[
\chi^2(P_\epsilon\Vert P_0)
=
\frac{m^2}{(1-r^2)^2},
\qquad
m=r^2(\epsilon t)^2.
\]

At fixed \(a,t\), the distinguishing signal is of order \(\epsilon^2\), and this divergence is of order \(\epsilon^4\).

A binary decision with both error probabilities at most \(\delta<1/2\) therefore needs

\[
N\ge
\frac{(1-r^2)^2}{m^2}
(1-2\delta)\log\frac{1-\delta}{\delta}.
\]

Bounded-score validation attains the same order:

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
N=\Theta\!\left(\epsilon^{-4}\log(1/\delta)\right)
}
\]

for independent fixed-lag triples, with \(a,t\) fixed.

This lower bound concerns that observation model. It is not a lower bound for every possible continuous or adaptively timed experiment.

For the uninterrupted test, I separately proved that the same order is sufficient on the constructed family when the score is correctly centered. The false-positive guarantee remains independent of a mixing-time input; the power calculation uses properties of the specified alternative.

Centering has a smaller cost. The exact score mean is

\[
C(c)=\frac12\left[(c-r)^2-m\right].
\]

Thus an \(O(\epsilon)\) centering error suffices, requiring \(O(\epsilon^{-2})\) independent training samples at fixed \(a,t\).

I also completed a calibrated readout-error correction. Independent bit flips with known probability \(\nu<1/2\) can be inverted to obtain an unbiased score. The conservative sample penalty scales as \(O((1-2\nu)^{-6})\). That correction is valid with the preparation-and-validation protocol; its potentially negative central weight prevents applying the no-mixing proof unchanged.
