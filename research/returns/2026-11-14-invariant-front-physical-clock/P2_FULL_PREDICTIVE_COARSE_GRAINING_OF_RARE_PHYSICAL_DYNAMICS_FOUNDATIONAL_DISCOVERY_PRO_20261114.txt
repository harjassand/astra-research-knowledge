A predictive physics of slow change from fast local dynamics

Can you discover and construct a general way to replace the enormous microscopic dynamics of an interacting material by a much smaller predictive dynamics that retains its physical clock, including rare collective changes? The useful operation is to predict when a stored physical state will fail, a phase will nucleate or a collective switch will occur, and how that prediction changes with controllable local interactions, without simulating the entire waiting time or already being given the successful pathway. Pursue the full constructive scientific result. A formal projection identity, a proposed simulator or an example with freely supplied slow coordinates is not the requested operation.

The microscopic starting point is a continuous-time Markov process on a growing spatial lattice or comparably explicit local physical system. Its state space may be exponential in the number N of local degrees of freedom, but the input specifies only finite-range local interactions, transition rules, their physical time units and their numerical precision. Local rates and preparation are supplied as an explicit physical model; they are not experimental evidence that the model describes an actual material. Preparation must be locally describable and implementable in the model, such as a specified product state. The two macroscopic regions A and B must have efficiently computable membership. Do not obtain a free initial conditional equilibrium distribution, stationary density, committor, transition tube, critical droplet, conditional sampler, reduced generator or list of rare paths.

Discover the representation and the conditions under which the reduction can actually be constructed and used. It may have retained history, additional physical state variables, non-Markovian memory or a different mathematical form. What matters is preserving the observable dynamics and physical elapsed time with controlled resources. The result must identify a broad, physically meaningful interacting class and give a predictive construction for it. The class must include genuinely cooperative transitions in a growing spatial system; independent components, an exactly supplied one-dimensional kernel, a fixed list of microscopic target states or a fixed-size landscape with a taller barrier do not establish that scope.

A concrete demanding starting family is local heat-bath spin dynamics on two-dimensional square lattices whose two side lengths grow. Let sigma_i be +/-1 and E(sigma)=-J sum_<ij> sigma_i sigma_j-h sum_i sigma_i, with J>0, h>0, inverse temperature beta and a declared single-site attempt rate nu. The flip rate is nu/(1+exp(beta*Delta_i E)). Begin in the all-minus state and predict first passage to nonnegative magnetization in a low-temperature regime with growing critical length J/h and system size large enough for a collective transition. Boundary conditions and parameter domains must be explicit. Work with the actual finite-temperature generator, rather than substituting zero-temperature deterministic growth or an uncosted asymptotic prefactor. You may change this physical family if another provides a stronger consequential construction, while preserving the challenge of cooperative rare dynamics, an earned reduced representation and useful total cost. The question is not tied to a spin representation or to one algorithm.

For your chosen class, construct useful quantitative predictions for the mean first-passage time and for the timing law at queried physical horizons. A single fitted exponential is insufficient unless its regime and error are established. Give two-sided controlled errors that resolve the relevant small transition or survival probability, rather than an absolute error larger than that probability. State the accuracy domain, including a supplied minimum probability if needed. An equivalent transform or memory representation is useful only with the cost and error of obtaining the requested physical-time prediction. The physical clock includes time in discarded states, unsuccessful excursions, preparation and returns. Preserve the initial-law contract; a result for a convenient stationary or conditioned start does not silently answer the prepared-state question.

The central advance must be a constructive principle explaining when slow observable dynamics can be represented and acquired much more economically than either enumerating all microscopic configurations or following a typical rare waiting time. Expose total dependence on system size and geometry, interaction range, activation parameters, rate/input bit precision, requested error and probability range. Pay for choosing coordinates, discovering hidden states or memory, initialization, sampling, training, normalization, numerical precision, validation and repeated parameter changes. A small reduced solve after an exponentially expensive construction does not remove the obstacle. Neither does a polynomial theorem with an exponential supplied representation, a free conditional sampler or an inaccessible certificate. Identify the physical structure responsible for the gain, its limits, and how that structure is recognized or constructed from the allowed input. Do not assume that locality or metastability alone guarantees an efficient answer.

A limited diagnostic illustrates the missing clock operation. Consider transient states A,G,H and absorbing B, with A->G at rate a, G->A at b, G->B at c, G->H at u and H->G at v. The probability from G of reaching B before A is c/(b+c), independently of the hidden trap. Eliminating H at zero frequency preserves that probability and the process observed only outside H, but erases its residence time. The actual mean from A is (b+c)/(a*c)+1/c+u/(v*c). Its first-passage Laplace transform is

F_A(s)=a*c/((s+a)*(s*M(s)+b+c)-a*b), with M(s)=1+u/(s+v).

At a=10^-6, b=c=u=1 and v=10^-12, the actual mean is 1000002000001 in these introduced time units, while the reduced trace clock gives 2000001. Rescaling the two remaining exit rates to match the mean still gives an incorrect timing law. Keeping the one-pole memory recovers this toy exactly, but is simply classical state elimination and supplies no compression advantage over retaining H. All rates in this diagnostic are introduced, not measured. The substantive missing operation is acquiring a compact, predictive clock for a growing interacting physical system, rather than rederiving this identity or declaring each hidden microscopic state to be a new memory variable.

Strong existing constructions already include trajectory splitting and forward-flux methods, weighted ensembles, potential theory, milestoning and generalized master equations, Markov state models with memory/reweighting corrections, spectral methods and tensor representations of ensemble evolution. Give them the same microscopic model, preparation, desired observables, reuse and computational information. An acceleration relative only to brute-force waiting is insufficient when a competent existing method already attains it. A new theorem that makes an existing method predictively reliable over a substantially broader physical regime can be the central discovery; a new algorithm name is unnecessary. Count their actual setup and correction costs as well as yours.

One optional current primary starting point is Strahan and Gingrich, Sensitivity Analysis in the Face of Rare Events, arXiv:2605.09148v1 (9 May 2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09148v1. Its explicit short-fragment Markov-state and iterative reweighting construction is a relevant comparator, including the intermediate kinetic trap, lag-time tradeoff and data costs. The paper's worked examples do not by themselves supply a uniform complexity or error guarantee for growing spatial materials. Classical exact state elimination and projection/memory methods are prior constructions, not discoveries earned by renaming them.

Use mathematical construction, physical reasoning and bounded executable calculations where they materially establish the mechanism. Small exactly solvable generators can discriminate a false clock or error claim; substantial success requires a result that persists in a growing cooperative class and changes what can be predicted or designed. Numerical evidence and analytic claims should retain their actual scope. The input model, rate uncertainty and transfer to a real material must remain explicit. A device specification or a future experiment proposal does not establish this scientific operation. Look for unexpected physical variables, mechanisms and representations, and challenge the framing if the stronger scientific route requires it.
