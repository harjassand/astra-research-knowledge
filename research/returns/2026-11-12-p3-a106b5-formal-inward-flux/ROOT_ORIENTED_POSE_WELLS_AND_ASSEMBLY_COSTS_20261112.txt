2. A local pair interaction can carry a complete relative pose

Allow each fixed-radius spherical carrier a body frame R_i in SO(3). The
centres still have the same hard-ball domain. A pair's intrinsic coordinates
are z=R_i^T(Q_j-Q_i), T=R_i^T R_j. A fixed preferred relative pose has
|z_*|>2r_* and T_* in SO(3). On a small compact neighbourhood use
 U(z,T)=-E_*+k[|z-z_*|^2+||T-T_*||_F^2].
Multiply by a smooth cutoff equal to1 on a smaller neighbourhood and0
outside a fixed contact range. Extend it smoothly to zero elsewhere.
All constants and all derivative bounds are fixed independently of object
size and reliability. Exchange symmetry is obtained by defining
 U_ts(-T^Tz,T^T)=U_st(z,T).
Thus the energy is a genuine pair interaction, invariant under common
translations and proper rotations, with no external orientation reference.

Near the preferred pose write T=T_* exp([theta]_cross). Then
 ||T-T_*||_F^2=2|theta|^2+O(|theta|^4).
The well has a nondegenerate Hessian in all SIX relative translation/rotation
directions. Unlike a radial well, it has no unpenalized tangential orbit of
bond orientations. This constructs local geometric information in a fixed
physical interaction, not a supply of lattice sites, a rigid infinite bond,
an already protected contact or an assembly theorem.

Pair energies can be multiplied by conformation-dependent fixed patch
strengths. Changing a conformation has a physical energetic load involving
the contacts that actually exist. Reverse rates chosen by the symmetric
