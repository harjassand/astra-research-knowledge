## 1. What the new question changes

The predecessor can preserve additive rewards and **mean** resource consumption through marginal occupations and transition flows. That does not make independently chosen local commands respect a shared instantaneous limit.

For example, several controllers can each have acceptable average demand while requesting their expensive modes simultaneously. A physical capacity constraint can forbid that combination regardless of how rarely it occurs.

The new construction addresses this distinction directly. It:

1. Computes local reference policies using a mean-capacity relaxation.
2. Allows only a capacity-feasible subset of their requests to reach the plant.
3. Updates the internal references using actual plant observations.
4. Proves a bound on the performance lost through rejected requests.

The third step is essential. An independently simulated reference whose commands are blindly copied can give the wrong performance even when no capacity request is rejected.

## 2. Exact scope

The principal theorem uses \(n\) binary components,
\[
X_i\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Each component has two supplied primitive commands. Its flip intensity is
\[
a+\gamma u_i,\qquad
u_i\in\{0,1\},\qquad
\gamma=b-a,\qquad 0<a<b.
\]

The hard constraint is
\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_i(t)\le M\quad\text{at every time }t,}
\]
where \(M\) is an integer.

Thus at most \(M\) components may have their HIGH primitive enabled simultaneously. The baseline rate \(a\) remains active.

The important qualifications are:

| Input or premise | What the result requires |
|---|---|
| **Actuation** | The commanded rate has the same meaning in every component state and exterior configuration. |
| **Dynamics** | The supplied generator consists of single-component flips. Hidden interacting rates or common shocks are not included. |
| **Resource meaning** | HIGH consumes one unit of enabled capacity. This is a joint rate-and-cost premise, not an inferred energy law. |
| **Objective** | Bounded local state rewards and bounded local single-jump rewards. |
| **Model access** | Rational rate and reward tables are supplied. General apparatus acquisition is not supplied. |
| **Observation** | The digital version receives actual state snapshots at specified intervals. |
| **Command execution** | Issued admissible commands are realized by the supplied primitives. Arbitrary actuator faults are outside this guarantee. |

Spatial interaction is allowed in the reward terms and in the feedback policies. The theorem does not cover arbitrary uncontrolled neighbour-dependent transition rates.

The expected reward is represented through its predictable rate,
\[
r(x,u)=v(x)+\sum_i(a+\gamma u_i)w_i(x).
\]
This accommodates the predecessor’s state-integral and event-reward objectives. Additional known command charges can be included with their actual sensitivity to command suppression.

### The main bound

Partition the components into patches \(B\), with
\[
k_B=|B|,\qquad k=\max_B k_B.
\]

Retain reward terms wholly inside patches. Suppose the omitted crossing reward rate lies in
\[
[L_\partial,U_\partial],
\qquad D_\partial=U_\partial-L_\partial.
\]

For suitable explicitly computable sensitivity constants \(K_x,K_u\), define
\[
C=K_u+\frac{(b-a)K_x}{2a}.
\]

Here \(K_x\) bounds the reward-rate effect of one state disagreement, and \(K_u\) bounds the direct loss from suppressing one HIGH command.

The constructed controller satisfies
\[
\boxed{
0\le \frac{J^*-J^{\mathrm{delivered}}}{n}
\le
\frac{D_\partial}{n}
+
\frac{C}{4}\sqrt{\frac{k}{n}}.
}
\]

The benchmark \(J^*\) allows globally informed, adaptive control subject to the same hard capacity limit.

The first term is the predecessor’s spatial boundary cost. The second pays for converting independently generated local demand into a command vector that is feasible at every instant.

## 3. The central operation

### Start with a valid relaxation

Using the accepted marginal-flow construction, form a finite occupation LP for each patch and impose the shared mean constraint
\[
\sum_B\mathbb E[\text{HIGH requests in }B]\le M.
\]

Let the optimal total retained reward be \(V_{\mathrm{rel}}\).

The predecessor’s marginalization and joint rate-and-cost conditions give
\[
J^*\le V_{\mathrm{rel}}+U_\partial.
\]

This is initially only an upper bound. The new work is the conversion into a hard-capacity operation.

A finite rational decomposition expresses each patch occupation solution as a mixture of deterministic endpoint policies. Choose a policy label independently for each patch and initialize its **internal reference state** in that policy’s stationary distribution.

These are draws into controller memory. They do not require preparing the physical plant in equilibrium.

The resulting reference patches are independent of one another. Within a patch, the states and requests may be strongly correlated.

### Admit only feasible requests

Let \(c_i\in\{0,1\}\) be reference requests. Choose a subset \(d\le c\) with
\[
\sum_i d_i=\min\!\left(M,\sum_i c_i\right).
\]

The plant receives \(d\). The number of rejected requests is
\[
O=\sum_i(c_i-d_i)
  =\left(\sum_i c_i-M\right)_+.
\]

Every issued command vector satisfies the capacity limit. There is no probability of overload built into this mathematical selector.

### Couple the reference through observable plant events

Let \(X\) denote the plant and \(Z\) the internal reference. Their joint update can be implemented from observed plant flips, known rates and private random numbers.

For one coordinate, the joint transition rates are:

| Old plant/reference bits | Real-only flip | Both flip | Reference-only flip |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Equal | \(0\) | \(a+\gamma d_i\) | \(\gamma(c_i-d_i)\) |
| Different | \(a\) | \(\gamma d_i\) | \(a+\gamma(c_i-d_i)\) |

This table has the required marginals:

- The actual plant flips at rate \(a+\gamma d_i\).
- The reference flips at rate \(a+\gamma c_i\).

The controller does not need to see which hidden microscopic channel caused a plant flip. When the two bits differ, it can decide whether the reference follows an observed flip using a private coin with probability
\[
\frac{\gamma d_i}{a+\gamma d_i}.
\]

The reference’s projected generator depends only on its reference patch. Consequently its independent patch laws remain correct even though admission is globally coordinated.

### Charge disagreements directly

Let
\[
D_t=d_H(X_t,Z_t)
\]
be the number of coordinates where plant and reference disagree.

The joint generator gives
\[
\mathcal L D
=
\gamma\sum_i(c_i-d_i)
\left(1-2\mathbf 1_{\{X_i\ne Z_i\}}\right)
-2aD,
\]
and therefore
\[
\boxed{\mathcal L D\le \gamma O-2aD.}
\]

After integration,
\[
2a\int_0^T\mathbb E D_t\,dt
\le
\mathbb E D_0+
\gamma\int_0^T\mathbb E O_t\,dt.
\]

If the retained reward satisfies
\[
r_0(z,c)-r_0(x,d)
\le
K_xd_H(x,z)+K_u\sum_i(c_i-d_i),
\]
then
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E\int_0^T
[r_0(Z_t,c_t)-r_0(X_t,d_t)]\,dt
\le
\frac{K_x\mathbb E D_0}{2a}
+
C\int_0^T\mathbb E O_t\,dt.
}
\]

This is the useful mechanism: rejected requests have a bounded accumulated performance cost.

It avoids a whole-patch synchronization-time factor. The coefficient \(1/(2a)\) is nevertheless a real cost; it becomes large when the baseline rate is small.

## 4. Why capacity loss is subextensive

Let \(D_B\) be the reference demand from patch \(B\). At any fixed time,
\[
0\le D_B\le k_B.
\]

The patch demands are independent, and their total mean is at most \(M\). Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E O
&=\mathbb E\left(\sum_BD_B-M\right)_+\\
&\le \frac12
\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_BD_B\right)}\\
&\le \frac14\sqrt{\sum_Bk_B^2}\\
&\le \frac14\sqrt{nk}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Only independence **between patches** is used here. There is no assumption of temporal independence or independent bits inside a patch.

Combining this with the disagreement estimate yields the main theorem.

### Finite time, from every plant start

Let
\[
\Gamma_{\mathcal P}=\frac14\sqrt{\sum_Bk_B^2},
\]
and let \(S\) be the sum of the potential spans in the relaxation’s Bellman upper certificate.

Then
\[
\frac{\mathbb E R_T^{\mathrm{delivered}}}{T}
\ge
V_{\mathrm{rel}}+L_\partial
-C\Gamma_{\mathcal P}
-\frac{K_x\mathbb E D_0}{2aT},
\]
while
\[
\sup_\pi\frac{\mathbb E R_T^\pi}{T}
\le
V_{\mathrm{rel}}+U_\partial+\frac{S}{T}.
\]

Thus the finite-time intensive regret is at most
\[
\boxed{
\frac{D_\partial}{n}
+\frac{C\Gamma_{\mathcal P}}{n}
+\frac{S+K_x\mathbb E D_0/(2a)}{nT}.
}
\]

Since \(D_0\le n\), this covers arbitrary initial physical states. The local potential spans remain explicit transient costs.

No slack below \(M\) is needed for this particular constraint because rejecting a HIGH request always produces another legal action. This does **not** remove the feasibility-margin conditions for arbitrary resource sets.

## 5. Broader deduction: the hard-capacity performance curve

For translation-invariant finite-range data on regular geometric systems, define
\[
v(\rho)=
\lim_{n\to\infty}
\frac{J_n(\lfloor\rho n\rfloor)}{n},
\qquad 0\le\rho\le1.
\]

Here \(\rho\) is the available HIGH capacity per component.

The new construction proves that this limit exists and is:

- nondecreasing;
- concave;
- Lipschitz continuous;
- computable to specified additive accuracy.

To see why the limit is effective, let \(g_L(\rho)\) be the mean-capacity occupation-LP value per component in a finite patch of side \(L\). With a computable boundary constant \(C_0\), the proof gives, on compatible large tori,
\[
g_L(\rho)-\frac{C_0}{L}
-\frac C4\sqrt{\frac{L^d}{n}}
-\frac Cn
\le
\frac{J_n(\lfloor\rho n\rfloor)}{n}
\le
g_L(\rho)+\frac{C_0}{L}.
\]

Taking larger systems at fixed \(L\), and then comparing different patch sizes, gives
\[
|v(\rho)-g_L(\rho)|\le \frac{C_0}{L}.
\]

This is an actual finite-patch enclosure for the bulk value.

### An exact price representation

Let \(\psi(\lambda)\) be the optimal bulk reward when there is no hard cap but each enabled HIGH primitive is charged a mathematical price \(\lambda\) per unit time.

Then
\[
\boxed{
v(\rho)=
\min_{0\le\lambda\le C_{\mathrm{full}}}
\bigl[\psi(\lambda)+\lambda\rho\bigr].
}
\]

The constant \(C_{\mathrm{full}}\) is the disagreement-and-suppression coefficient computed for the full reward.

A resource price ordinarily supplies an upper bound on a hard-constrained optimum. Here the constructive admission argument establishes equality in the bulk limit.

The finite-patch LPs provide the price duality, and their boundary error is uniform in \(\lambda\), because the price term is local to each component. That allows the limit and the variational bound to be connected rigorously.

There is also a finite-system sensitivity bound:
\[
0\le J_n(M_2)-J_n(M_1)
\le C_{\mathrm{full}}(M_2-M_1),
\qquad M_1\le M_2.
\]

Its proof uses the same observable coupling, with an optimal \(M_2\)-capacity controller as a reference and at most \(M_2-M_1\) rejected requests.

**These conclusions concern optimal sustained performance.** They do not identify every invariant law, establish differentiability of the capacity curve, or determine rare-event probabilities and relaxation times. The “price” is a reward-unit multiplier; it is not an acquired physical energy price.

## 6. Online global coordination can also be bounded spatially

The global admission step can be replaced by separately capped execution regions.

Group several patches into each region \(G\). If their reference mean demand is \(\mu_G\), allocate
\[
M_G=\lfloor\mu_G\rfloor.
\]
Then
\[
\sum_GM_G\le M.
\]

Each region operates its own admission rule. The total additional loss is bounded by
\[
C\sum_G
\left[
\frac14\sqrt{\sum_{B\subset G}k_B^2}
+\mu_G-\lfloor\mu_G\rfloor
\right].
\]

The second term pays for integer allocation. Remaining whole capacity units can be distributed to reduce it.

For regions containing between \(s/2\) and \(s\) components, the intensive allowance is at most
\[
\frac C4\sqrt{\frac{2k}{s}}+\frac{2C}{s}.
\]

Combined with the predecessor’s geometric boundary estimate, this gives finite spatial reach at fixed accuracy. For regular nested partitions in dimension \(d\), one constructive choice has patch volume
\[
k=O(\varepsilon^{-d})
\]
and execution-region volume
\[
s=O(\varepsilon^{-(d+2)}).
\]

The resulting region diameter scales as
\[
O\!\left(\varepsilon^{-(1+2/d)}\right).
\]

The offline resource allocation still has to be computed. Physical communication across a region still has finite delay. Neither expense disappears through the locality theorem.

A further extension handles several supplied **fixed, nonnegative** resource weights per HIGH primitive. Sequentially deleting the largest-weight requests enforces every cap. Its constants depend on the actual resource densities and weight bounds. State-dependent costs, signed resources and mandatory-action equalities remain outside that argument.

## 7. Finite observation intervals and finite bits

The continuous-time proof alone would leave an ideal event interface. I therefore derived a controller that reads the plant at times
\[
0,\Delta,2\Delta,\ldots,J\Delta=T
\]
and holds commands between readings.

For a held flip rate \(q\),
\[
p_q=\frac{1-e^{-2q\Delta}}2
\]
is the probability that the observed bit changes over one interval.

At the next observation, the reference can be updated conditionally from the actual endpoint. For example, when the actual bit did not change:

- If the old plant and reference bits were equal, flip the reference with probability
  \[
  \frac{p_c-p_d}{1-p_d}.
  \]
- If they were unequal, flip it with probability
  \[
  \frac{p_c}{1-p_d}.
  \]

Here \(p_c\) and \(p_d\) correspond to the requested and admitted rates. The other cases have deterministic reference updates.

These probabilities give the correct reference marginal and minimize endpoint disagreement. Their denominators are at least \(1/2\), so the computation does not divide by a rare-event probability.

The same rejection-loss coefficient \(C\) continues to apply. The additional issue is that holding a policy between observations changes its performance and mean demand.

Finite patch Poisson equations give computable constants \(A_r,A_c\) bounding those changes by
\[
\Delta A_r,\qquad \Delta A_c.
\]

If \(|r(x,u)|\le nR_0\), and the total finite-horizon sampling error is at most \(\delta\), the full bound becomes
\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{intensive regret}\le {}&
\frac{D_\partial}{n}
+\frac{C\Gamma_{\mathcal P}}{n}\\
&+\frac{\Delta(A_r+CA_c)}{n}\\
&+\frac{S+K_x\mathbb E D_0/(2a)}{nT}
+2R_0\delta.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

Every term has a finite construction or certificate.

Using
\[
p=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{4nJ}{\delta}\right\rceil
\]
random bits per conditional draw, together with sufficiently accurate probability evaluation and initialization, gives the stated finite-horizon error allowance.

The required internal stationary distributions are computed from finite patch matrices. Their conditioning and precision are paid. This is a digital initialization procedure, not physical equilibration of the plant.

The issued capacity limit remains exact even when a sampling probability is rounded: the resulting request vector still passes through the admissible integer selector. Physical failures to obey a LOW command require a separate safety mechanism.
