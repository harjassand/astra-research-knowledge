An actual unforced coupled heat window for magnetic wavepackets
ROOT, 11 November 2026 Brisbane

The whole-scalar passive transvection in the available P2 helper return uses
two short ideal induction maps and a positive heat interval. Pure magnetic
heat is not an available control in coupled MHD: its Lorentz term accelerates
the fluid. This note pays that specific interval by an ACTUAL unforced MHD
evolution. It does not construct an arbitrary-Pi writer or assume that
constant-field generator spanning controls the existing magnetic memory.

1. FINITE PACKET INPUT AND UNIFORM COEFFICIENTS

Put kappa=2pi. The actual scalar nu(t),rho(t) lie in[mu,1], mu>0, and are
otherwise unknown. Measurability suffices for this interval. Let |H|<=M and
write B=H+b. Consider zero force, zero nominal disturbance and initial u=0,
b=b_L+b_H. The packet has integer carrier Q=q^4 in one coordinate:
 b_L+b_H=sum_(|n|<=4) A_n(x) exp(i kappa n Q x3).
Each slow A_n is a finite Fourier field of radius F, with the realness and
solenoidality conditions of the actual packet. Only n=0 contributes b_L.
For a fixed rational envelope E, independent of q, assume
 ||b_L||X^r<=E for r=0,1,2,
 ||b_L+b_H||X^r<=E q^(2+4r) for r=0,1,2.             (1)
Here ||v||X^r=sum_(k!=0)(1+|k|)^r |vhat(k)|, and the vector-pair norm is
the sum of Euclidean Fourier-pair norms. A finite-degree Taylor jet of the
whole-scalar gate satisfies (1) with computable E from fixed derivatives of
its smooth input matrix and scalar field. Its slow support radius is also
computable before q. Constants below are polynomial in E,F,M,mu^-1.

Take
 T=mu^-1 q^-7, Q>=32(F+1).                            (2)
For q above a computable polynomial threshold, the nominal coupled solution
exists throughout[0,T] and
 ||u(T)||H1+||B(T)-(H+b_L)||H1
 <=P[q^-4+mu^-1 q^-7+q^6 exp(-c q)].                 (3)
In particular this is a positive-time filter of the ACTUAL coupled system,
uniform in nu,rho,H, not a passive assignment of magnetic heat. Every bound
uses fixed derivative orders and finite arithmetic/Fourier envelopes.

2. THE EXACT COUPLED FOURIER PROPAGATOR

Include the constant-flux Alfven coupling in the linear part. At frequency k
the pair (uhat,bhat) obeys
 d/dt (uhat,bhat)=
 [-kappa^2|k|^2 diag(nu,rho)+i kappa(H.k) offdiag(1,1)](uhat,bhat).
The off-diagonal part is skew-Hermitian. Thus its evolution E_k(t,s) satisfies
 ||E_k(t,s)||<=exp[-kappa^2 mu|k|^2(t-s)]             (4)
for ALL coefficient histories, without commuting the time-dependent coupled matrices.
Consequently
 || |k| E_k(t,s)||<=C[mu(t-s)]^-1/2.                 (5)
No coefficient value or propagator is computed by the emitted programme.
The magnetic mean remains H and velocity mean zero.

3. SHORT-TIME WIENER CONSTRUCTION AND A FINITE TREE TAIL

All remaining MHD terms are quadratic divergences. Leray's multiplier has
norm<=1. Fourier convolution, (5) and |k|<=|l|+|k-l| give, for r=0,1,2,
 ||Bil(Z,W)||C_tX0<=C mu^-1/2 sqrt(T)||Z||C_tX0||W||C_tX0,
 ||Bil(Z,W)||C_tXr<=C_r mu^-1/2 sqrt(T)
    (||Z||C_tX0||W||C_tXr+||Z||C_tXr||W||C_tX0).    (6)
The finite constants include the two equations and their polarizations.
With R0=E q^2, the contraction parameter is
 eta0=C mu^-1/2 sqrt(T)R0<=P q^-3/2.                 (7)
Choose q so eta0<=1/64. Picard construction then gives a unique nominal
solution with ||Z||C_tXr<=P q^(2+4r), r=0,1,2.

Expand into homogeneous binary-tree terms in the mean-zero initial packet.
Catalan counting and (6) bound the degree-n term in X1 by
 C n R1(C eta0)^(n-1), R1=E q^6.
The sum of degrees>=9 is therefore bounded in H1 by P q^-6. This is a
FIXED eight-leaf expansion. It neither enumerates the input trajectory nor
requires derivatives of unknown nu,rho. The constant-flux linear coupling
is included in every tree edge through (4), so it is not an omitted residual.

4. FINITE-PACKET FREQUENCIES PAY EVERY LOW AND HIGH TREE

For degrees n<=8, every tree frequency has the form j Q e3+xi with
|xi|<=nF. Hence a nonzero carrier has |k|>=Q/2. Internal quadratic factors
have size<=C_n Q; an output with zero carrier has derivative size<=C_n(F+1).

If the OUTPUT carrier is nonzero, every time slice through the tree has at
least one active nonzero-carrier edge: the sum of the active carrier indices
equals the nonzero output index. The sum of the lengths of those edges is
therefore at least T. Multiplying their contraction factors (4) gives
exp(-c mu Q^2 T)=exp(-c q). Bounds on derivatives, amplitudes and time simplexes
then give X1 output<=P q^6 exp(-c q), for the fixed collection n<=8.
This pays high output created at an intermediate time, not only high initial
data. Frequencies that arise by cancellation are handled in the next case.

If the output carrier is zero but some leaf is high, there are at least two
high leaves. Select one high initial leaf. Its first parent-time integration
contains exp(-c mu Q^2 s), contributing at most C/(mu Q^2). Bound the other
n-2 time integrations by T^(n-2), the n-2 non-root derivatives by C_n Q,
and the low output derivative and H1 weight by C_n(F+1)^2. This gives
 low-tree H1<=P_n R0^n Q^(n-2)(mu Q^2)^-1 T^(n-2)
             <=P q^-4 [P q^-1]^(n-2).              (8)
Enlarging q makes the bracket bounded. Using the full initial norm R0 is a
safe overestimate even when some leaves are low. The selected integration
bound is valid on the ordered tree simplex by extending its other variables
to[0,T]; no independence is assumed.

Trees with ONLY low leaves have fixed slow frequency bounds and contribute
at most P T in total beyond the linear term. On those low frequencies the
linear evolution differs from the identity by at most P T, directly from
its finite matrix ODE and |H|<=M. Summing these cases and the fixed tree tail
proves (3). The mean mode is exact since every quadratic nonlinearity is a
divergence. No inverse heat or uncontrolled waiting interval is used.

5. ROUGH INPUT ERRORS AND DISTURBANCES

The nominal solution from (1) has

Here the X1,X2 bounds from (6) imply the supremum estimates. Crucially

Let initial joint errors have L2 norm e0 and H1 norm e1; let mean-zero
disturbances have joint L2 essential supremum eta. The coupled relative

 sup_t ||(u,B)-(u_ref,B_ref)||L2<=P(e0+eta T).         (10)
This also holds for energy-admissible weak realizations against the smooth
reference. It is not a comparison of three different H-dependent trajectories.

For the H1 perturbation put E0=||error||L2^2,E1=||error||H1^2. Differentiated
coupled energy, the same leading cancellation and ordinary integration by
parts give

 <=C L E1+C D2 sqrt(E0 E1)+C mu^-3 E1^3+C mu^-1 eta^2.
Terms with second reference derivatives use L2 error, not a new exponent

 C D2 sqrt(E0 E1)<=E1/T+C T D2^2 E0.                 (11)

The polynomial P includes the powers of mu^-1. Initial errors and eta are
