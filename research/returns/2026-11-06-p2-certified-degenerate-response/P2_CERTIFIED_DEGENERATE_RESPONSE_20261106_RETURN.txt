## 2. Why finite-energy solutions are uniquely determined

The key analytic fact is a consequence of the critical div–curl product machinery already used in the accepted predecessor.

Suppose \(E\) is a periodic gradient with mean zero, \(B\) is divergence free, and
\[
J=E\cdot B\ge0,\qquad
|E|^2+|B|^2\le(K+K^{-1})J,
\qquad J\in L^1.
\tag{5}
\]
Under (1), this pair must vanish.

To see why, put
\[
R=\frac{|E+B|+|E-B|}{2}.
\]
The distortion inequality gives \(R^2\le KJ\). Splitting according to whether
\[
K\le\frac2\tau\log(e+R)
\]
gives
\[
\frac{R^2}{\log(e+R)}
\le \frac2\tau J+e^{\tau K}.
\tag{6}
\]
Thus both fields belong to the critical \(L^2\log^{-1}L\) class.

At this regularity, ordinary formal integration by parts would leave a genuine gap. Sbordone’s critical product theorem supplies the needed conclusion: the nonnegative pointwise product represents the canonical distributional div–curl product. Its periodic integral is therefore zero when the gradient has mean zero. Consequently \(J=0\), and (5) forces \(E=B=0\). This use of the primary theorem is precisely its stated nonnegative-product scope. [numdam.org](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASNSP_1997_4_25_3-4_739_0.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

That vanishing statement proves both required density results.

### Density of gradients

Let \(X_A\) be the Hilbert space of mean-zero periodic gradients with finite \(A\)-energy. Let \(H\) be the closure of smooth periodic gradients.

For \(g\in X_A\), take its Hilbert-space projection onto \(H\), writing
\[
g=h+r.
\]
Orthogonality to smooth gradients gives
\[
\operatorname{div}(Ar)=0.
\]
The residual \(r\) is itself a mean-zero gradient. The pair
\[
E=r,\qquad B=Ar
\]
satisfies (5), since the eigenvalues of \(A\) lie between \(K^{-1}\) and \(K\). Therefore \(r=0\), proving \(H=X_A\).

This projection is an analytic proof device. The numerical algorithm does not call an infinite-dimensional projection.

### Density of conserved fluxes

Apply the corresponding argument in the space of divergence-free fields with finite \(A^{-1}\)-energy.

If \(r\) is orthogonal to all smooth divergence-free fields, then
\[
E=A^{-1}r
\]
has zero mean and zero curl: constant tests establish its mean, and elementary divergence-free derivative tests establish its curl. Thus \(E\) is a mean-zero gradient, while \(r=AE\) is divergence free. The same vanishing argument gives \(r=0\).

These statements concern the actual anisotropic matrix energy. They do not replace it by a stronger scalar-weight energy and assume that the admissible spaces remain equal. Weighted-density theory has substantial prior results, including Zhikov’s criterion and its development by Ambrosio, Pinamonti and Speight; the proof above supplies the exact gradient and flux statements needed here. [cvgmt.sns.it](https://cvgmt.sns.it/media/doc/paper/2456/Weighted%40Sobolev%40MMS.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Existence now follows by minimizing the strictly convex quadratic functional on \(e+X_A\). Its forcing term is continuous because
\[
\left|\int_Q F\cdot g\right|
\le
\left(\int_Q F\cdot A^{-1}F\right)^{1/2}\|g\|_A.
\]
The Euler equation gives (2). If two finite-energy weak solutions existed, their difference would give another pair of the vanishing type, proving uniqueness among all such solutions.

This is a new existence conclusion for the **stated periodic symmetric linear problem**. It does not change the earlier nonlinear regularity theorem into a general nonlinear existence theorem.

## 3. The finite certificate

Define the primal and dual functionals
\[
P(E)=\int_Q(E\cdot AE-2F\cdot E),
\]
\[
D(B)=2e\cdot\overline B-
\int_Q(B+F)\cdot A^{-1}(B+F),
\qquad \operatorname{div}B=0.
\]
For the exact solution \((E_*,B_*)\), the density results justify all required pairings and give
\[
\boxed{
P(\widetilde E)-D(\widetilde B)
=
\|\widetilde E-E_*\|_A^2+
\|\widetilde B-B_*\|_{A^{-1}}^2.
}
\tag{7}
\]

This is the classical Prager–Synge or hypercircle principle, with the low-regularity admissibility obligations discharged. Primal–dual bounds for numerical homogenization are established prior machinery; for example, Vondřejc, Zeman and Marek develop guaranteed bounds in the uniformly elliptic setting. I am not claiming to have originated that principle. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3614?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

What makes it a finite certificate here is that uncertain coefficient data can be paid for explicitly.

Suppose
\[
\|A-S\|_{L^1(\mathrm{op})}\le\eta,\qquad
\|A^{-1}-C\|_{L^1(\mathrm{op})}\le\zeta.
\]
For bounded rational admissible trials, compute
\[
U=
\int_Q(\widetilde E\cdot S\widetilde E-2F\cdot\widetilde E)
+\eta\|\widetilde E\|_\infty^2,
\]
\[
L=
2e\cdot\overline{\widetilde B}
-\int_Q(\widetilde B+F)\cdot C(\widetilde B+F)
-\zeta\|\widetilde B+F\|_\infty^2.
\tag{8}
\]
These are rational calculations for the chosen finite representation. They guarantee (4) whenever \(U-L\le\varepsilon^2\).

The certificate is valid for **every coefficient consistent with the supplied approximation and moment bounds**. It does not require identifying the remaining microscopic uncertainty exactly.

There is also an ordinary \(L^1\) error consequence. With
\[
\mu=\frac M\tau,
\qquad \int_Q K\le\mu,
\]
weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
\|\widetilde E-E_*\|_1^2+
\|\widetilde B-B_*\|_1^2
\le \mu(U-L).
\tag{9}
\]

## 4. Constructing \(A^{-1}\) data without an inverse oracle

A certificate involving \(A^{-1}\) would leave an access gap if inverse-coefficient data were merely assumed available. I removed that assumption.

For a rational symmetric matrix \(S\), rational \(R\ge1\), and integer \(k\ge1\), define
\[
T(S)=(I+R^2S^2)^{-1},
\]
\[
\boxed{
C_{R,k}(S)=R^2S\bigl[T(S)+T(S)^2+\cdots+T(S)^k\bigr].
}
\tag{10}
\]
The matrix being inverted satisfies
\[
I+R^2S^2\ge I
\]
even when \(S\) has negative eigenvalues. Thus this construction uses ordinary finite rational matrix operations.

For the true positive matrix \(A\), the geometric sum gives the exact identity
\[
A^{-1}-C_{R,k}(A)=A^{-1}T(A)^k.
\]
On \(\{K\le R\}\), its norm is at most \(R2^{-k}\). On the remaining set, it is at most \(K\), and the exponential moment implies
\[
\int_{\{K>R\}}K
\le M(R+\tau^{-1})e^{-\tau R}.
\]

A resolvent calculation also gives a perturbation bound valid for **noncommuting matrices**:
\[
\|C_{R,k}(X)-C_{R,k}(Y)\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le
\frac{k(k+3)}4R^2\|X-Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]
Combining these facts yields
\[
\boxed{
\|A^{-1}-C_{R,k}(S)\|_{L^1}
\le
\frac{k(k+3)}4R^2\eta
+R2^{-k}
+M(R+\tau^{-1})e^{-\tau R}.
}
\tag{11}
\]

Every term can be allocated using rational comparisons. To request inverse accuracy \(2^{-n}\):

1. Increase an integer \(r\), set \(R=\max\{1,2r/\tau\}\), and require
   \[
   M(R+\tau^{-1})2^{-2r}\le2^{-n-2}.
   \]
2. Choose \(k\) with \(R2^{-k}\le2^{-n-2}\).
3. Request coefficient precision \(s\) satisfying
   \[
   \frac{k(k+3)}4R^2\,2^{-s}\le2^{-n-1}.
   \]

Since \(e\ge2\), these rational inequalities imply the desired bound in (11). For fixed \(\tau,M\),
\[
r,k=O(n+\log n),\qquad s=n+O(\log n).
\]
The spatial table size and the coefficient program’s runtime remain separate, fully charged costs.

## 5. Why the algorithm terminates

The implementation uses compatible finite spaces on periodic dyadic simplicial grids:

- Trial gradients come from continuous periodic piecewise-affine scalar potentials.
- Trial conserved fluxes are constants plus divergences of antisymmetric piecewise-affine tensor potentials. Their divergence vanishes exactly, including across element faces.

At precision stage \(n\), the algorithm obtains coefficient and inverse-coefficient approximations, then visits grid levels
\[
0\le m\le\lfloor\log_2(n+1)\rfloor.
\]
For each level it assembles rational primal and dual quadratic forms. It checks their finite Gram matrices by exact \(LDL\) elimination, solves positive-definite systems exactly, and evaluates (8). A failed finite positive-definiteness check causes that approximation to be skipped at that stage.

The termination proof has two parts.

First, at any fixed grid level, the approximate Gram matrices converge to positive-definite exact Gram matrices. Eventually the check passes, and the rational finite solutions converge to the exact finite-space solutions.

Second, the unions of the primal and dual finite spaces are dense in the energy spaces proved above. Therefore some finite pair has arbitrarily small total error. Once that grid is visited with sufficient coefficient precision, the error margins in (8) become small enough for the stopping test to pass.

This proves halting for every valid input and every positive requested accuracy. Passing numerical tests is not used as evidence for that universal assertion.
