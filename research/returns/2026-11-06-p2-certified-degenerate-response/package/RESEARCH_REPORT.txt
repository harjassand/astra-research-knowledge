CERTIFIED RESPONSE AND HOMOGENIZATION WITHOUT A UNIFORM ELLIPTICITY GAP
Complete derivation, represented-input algorithm, and exact certificates
6 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane

0. QUESTION, RESULT, AND STATUS
==============================

The selected question is whether a periodic material whose scalar conduction
equation has unbounded and vanishing local coefficients nevertheless has a
unique response, an identifiable effective constitutive tensor, and complete
finite certificates computable from explicitly represented material data.

The answer proved here is affirmative for the following full coefficient
class: arbitrary measurable real symmetric positive-definite d by d matrices
on the unit torus, d>=2, for which the coefficient and its inverse in fixed
units have one supplied exponential moment. No continuity, Muckenhoupt
condition, finite Fourier support, uniform essential ellipticity gap, or
finite spatial scale is imposed. Periodicity, scalar linear conduction,
symmetry, the moment hypothesis, and the information representation remain
conditions of the result.

The construction supplies existence and uniqueness of the finite-energy
periodic weak solution; density of smooth admissible gradients AND fluxes;
an exact primal-dual error identity; a rational procedure that constructs
inverse-coefficient data rather than querying an inverse oracle; a uniform
deterministic algorithm that halts on every valid coefficient name; and the
periodic variational homogenization theorem identifying the computed tensor.

The termination theorem is not a polynomial-time theorem in a succinct
coefficient-program length or in the requested precision. The cost of the
coefficient program, its expanded spatial data, all rational arithmetic,
finite matrix solves, and the actual stopping stage are retained below.
Point samples alone do not furnish the input representation. A separate
explicit counting reduction exposes a P=NP consequence of a much stronger
succinct-input efficiency claim. It does not prove that claim impossible.

This is a completed mathematical/computational construction at this scope.
No historical priority or demonstrated civilisation-changing capability is
claimed. The effective-law question was unresolved in this programme; the
report does not assert that every constituent theorem was previously open
in the literature. Prager-Synge duality and existing numerical homogenization
are prior machinery, not new discoveries here. The decisive admission of
low-regularity products is credited to Sbordone [1]. Weighted density has
substantial prior literature, including [2].

The earlier sharp norm, scalar elliptic interval, nonlinear extension,
Fourier solver, unbounded-distortion logarithmic theorem and endpoint
families are unchanged. None is silently promoted to a theorem about coupled
systems, arbitrary boundaries, arbitrary nonlinear laws, or efficient
inversion of unencoded measurable inputs. QUBO and P versus NP remain open.

1. EXACT ANALYTIC AND DIGITAL CONTRACTS
======================================

Write Q=R^d/Z^d with volume one. Let A(x) be real symmetric positive definite
almost everywhere. In one fixed choice of units put

  K(x)=max{1, ||A(x)||op, ||A(x)^(-1)||op}.

Assume rational tau>0 and M>0 satisfy

  integral_Q exp(tau K) <= M.                         (1.1)

In particular K^(-1)I<=A<=KI. This is not a local condition-number assumption.
An x-dependent coefficient normalization changes the PDE and is not allowed.
Set mu=M/tau. Since exp(tau K)>=tau K,

  integral K <= mu;  integral K^r <= r! M/tau^r       (1.2)

for every positive integer r. The valid contract implies mu>=1.

Prescribe a rational mean field e in R^d and an explicitly supplied bounded
rational dyadic step vector F. The problem is

  E=e+grad u,   mean u=0,
  B=A E-F,     div B=0,                              (1.3)
  integral E.AE < infinity.

The main material-response case is F=0. F permits an additional distributional
source div F without adding a numerical oracle. The weak formulation tests
against smooth periodic scalar functions. A solution is a periodic potential
in W^(1,q) for every q<2, with its gradient understood distributionally.

DIGITAL INPUT. A finite program, together with its mathematical input promise,
returns for each integer s>=1 an explicitly expanded rational dyadic step
matrix S_s and a rational error eta_s<=2^(-s) such that

  integral ||A-S_s||op <= eta_s.                      (1.4)

Symmetrizing S_s preserves this bound. Each table lists every box on its
stated uniform dyadic grid, and d is explicit. Other finite representations
are admissible only after their expansion or integration cost is paid. The
program's runtime, output length, coefficient bit lengths and grid sizes are
part of the resource account. The program is not a unit-cost oracle.

The validity of the infinite Cauchy-name and moment promises is not decidable
by the finite checker in general. Certificates are conditional on this
supplied contract. In the infinite laminate below the promises are proved
directly, so the application needs no unverified numerical error estimate.

OUTPUT, for rational epsilon>0: finite rational compatible fields E_tilde,
B_tilde, a rational interval [L,U] containing the minimum of

  P(E)=integral (E.AE-2F.E),   mean E=e, curl E=0,     (1.5)

and the certificate

  ||E_tilde-E||_A^2 + ||B_tilde-B||_(A^-1)^2
      <= U-L <= epsilon^2.                           (1.6)

Here ||v||_A^2=integral v.Av. The physical flux approximation is B_tilde+F.
This is arbitrary-precision approximation to continuum fields and energy,
not an exact finite binary encoding of an infinite field or arbitrary real.
It also yields

  ||E_tilde-E||_1^2 + ||B_tilde-B||_1^2 <= mu epsilon^2.

2. THE POSITIVE PERIODIC PAIR THAT MUST VANISH
============================================

We use the following consequence of the critical div-curl product theorem
already used in the predecessor. If E is a periodic gradient of mean zero,
B is divergence free, J=E.B>=0 is integrable, and

  |E|^2+|B|^2 <= (K+K^(-1))J,

with (1.1), then E=B=0 almost everywhere.

Indeed, with R=(|E+B|+|E-B|)/2, the distortion inequality gives R^2<=KJ.
The elementary split K<=(2/tau)log(e+R) and its complement gives

  R^2/log(e+R) <= (2/tau)J+exp(tau K).

Thus both fields are in L^2 log^(-1)L. The nonnegative pointwise product is
the canonical distributional product by [1], Corollary 3.3 and the following
paragraph on printed page 745. This is the critical fact; formal integration
by parts of two arbitrary sub-L^2 fields would not suffice.

Both fields lie in L^q for all q<2, by weighted Holder and (1.2). Choose
q=2-1/(d+1). A mean-zero potential u lies in W^(1,q); its Sobolev exponent
q*=dq/(d-q) satisfies 1/q+1/q*<1. Hence uB is integrable. The canonical
product is div(uB), so its average on Q is zero. Therefore integral J=0.
The distortion inequality now gives E=B=0. This argument uses the existing
critical product machinery, not a new unproved distributional identity.

3. NO GAP BETWEEN SMOOTH AND FINITE-ENERGY ADMISSIBLE FIELDS
==========================================================

Let X_A be all distributional periodic gradients g of mean zero with
integral g.Ag finite. It is a closed Hilbert subspace of L^2(A):

  ||g||_1 <= (integral K)^(1/2)||g||_A

makes the curl and mean conditions closed under energy convergence. Let H
be the closure in X_A of smooth periodic gradients.

For g in X_A, project it orthogonally onto H, writing g=h+r. Projection is
used only in this analytic proof, never as an algorithmic primitive. Then
r is a mean-zero gradient, and orthogonality to smooth gradients implies
div(Ar)=0. Set E=r and B=Ar. The nonnegative product r.Ar is integrable,
and eigenvalue by eigenvalue

  |r|^2+|Ar|^2 <= (K+K^(-1)) r.Ar.

Section 2 forces r=0. Thus H=X_A.

There is an equally necessary dual statement. Let Y_(A^-1) be all periodic
divergence-free B with integral B.A^(-1)B finite, including nonzero means.
It is closed, by the same L^1 embedding. Project B onto the closure of smooth
divergence-free fields and call the residual r. Orthogonality gives
E=A^(-1)r orthogonal to every smooth divergence-free field. Constant tests
give mean E=0; tests of the form partial_j(phi)e_i-partial_i(phi)e_j give
curl E=0. Thus E is a mean-zero periodic gradient. With flux r=AE, Section 2
again gives r=0. Smooth divergence-free fields are dense in Y_(A^-1).

These two conclusions prevent a hidden admissible-space gap. They are
established for the anisotropic matrix A itself, without replacing its
energy by the larger scalar energy integral K|grad u|^2.

4. EXISTENCE, UNIQUENESS, AND THE EXACT CERTIFICATE
=================================================

The functional (1.5) is continuous and strictly convex on e+X_A. Its linear
term is bounded because

  |integral F.g| <= (integral F.A^(-1)F)^(1/2)||g||_A.

Hilbert-space minimization therefore gives a unique E*. Its Euler equation
gives B*=AE*-F in Y_(A^-1). Conversely, if two finite-energy weak solutions
exist, their difference r is a mean-zero gradient and Ar is divergence free.
Section 2 makes r=0. Thus uniqueness holds among all finite-energy periodic
weak solutions, not just those first constructed in a completion.

For an admissible gradient E and a divergence-free B define

  D(B)=2e.mean(B)-integral (B+F).A^(-1)(B+F).

The density results justify the weighted pairings with the exact fields.
For any finite-energy admissible trials,

  D(B) <= P(E*) <= P(E),
  P(E)-D(B)
    = ||E-E*||_A^2 + ||B-B*||_(A^-1)^2              (4.1)
    = integral |A^(1/2)E-A^(-1/2)(B+F)|^2.

To verify (4.1), the Euler equation cancels the cross term in
P(E)-P(E*). The dual cross term vanishes because pairing a gradient with
a divergence-free field gives the product of their means. At the solution
P(E*)=D(B*). Adding the two squared-error identities proves the assertion.

This is the classical Prager-Synge/hypercircle and primal-dual principle,
with all the needed low-regularity admissibility now justified. The principle
and its use in numerical homogenization are explicitly credited to [3,4].

For bounded rational trials suppose S approximates A with L1 error eta and
C approximates A^(-1) with L1 error zeta. Exact rational integration gives

  U=integral (E.S E-2F.E)+eta ||E||_infinity^2,
  L=2e.mean(B)-integral (B+F).C(B+F)
                         -zeta ||B+F||_infinity^2.  (4.2)

Then D(B)<=P(E*)<=P(E) and [L,U] encloses all three with the appropriate
inequality directions, so (1.6) holds whenever U-L<=epsilon^2.
Neither S nor C needs to be pointwise positive for (4.2) to be sound.

Crucially, (4.2) is also an uncertainty certificate for EVERY coefficient
consistent with the supplied approximation error and moment bound. It does
not require determining the unresolved microscopic coefficient exactly.

5. CONSTRUCTING THE INVERSE DATA BY RATIONAL OPERATIONS
======================================================

This step removes a potentially hidden inverse-coefficient input. For any
real symmetric matrix S, rational R>=1 and integer k>=1 put

  T(S)=(I+R^2 S^2)^(-1),
  C_(R,k)(S)=R^2 S [T(S)+...+T(S)^k].             (5.1)

All operations are rational matrix additions, multiplications and one finite
positive-definite matrix inversion per supplied box. I+R^2 S^2>=I even when
S has negative eigenvalues. No eigensolver or continuum inversion is used.

For the true positive matrix A, the geometric sum gives exactly

  A^(-1)-C_(R,k)(A)=A^(-1)T(A)^k.

On K<=R its operator norm is <=R 2^(-k). On K>R it is <=K. Since (1.1)
implies |{K>t}|<=M exp(-tau t), tail integration gives

  integral K 1_(K>R) <= M(R+1/tau)exp(-tau R).

The perturbation estimate is valid for noncommuting S and A. Let
F_R(X)=R^2 X(I+R^2X^2)^(-1). Resolvent identities at +/-i/R give

  ||F_R(X)-F_R(Y)||op <= R^2||X-Y||op,
  ||T(X)-T(Y)||op <= R||X-Y||op,
  ||F_R(X)||op<=R/2,  ||T(X)||op<=1.

Use C=F_R sum_(j=0)^(k-1) T^j and the telescoping identity for T(X)^j-T(Y)^j.
It follows that

  ||C_(R,k)(X)-C_(R,k)(Y)||op
      <= [k(k+3)/4] R^2 ||X-Y||op.

Therefore, for an L1 approximation S with error eta,

  ||A^(-1)-C_(R,k)(S)||_1
  <= [k(k+3)/4]R^2 eta + R 2^(-k)
                     +M(R+1/tau)exp(-tau R).       (5.2)

An entirely rational error allocation constructs an inverse name of accuracy
2^(-n). Increase an integer r until, with R=max{1,2r/tau},

  M(R+1/tau)2^(-2r)<=2^(-n-2).

The exponential tail is bounded by this number because e>=2 and tau R>=2r.
Choose k with R 2^(-k)<=2^(-n-2), and s with

  [k(k+3)/4]R^2 2^(-s)<=2^(-n-1).

Request S_s and apply (5.1) on its explicitly listed boxes. Equation (5.2)
proves the requested inverse error, with no assumption on a smallest global
eigenvalue. For fixed tau,M, r,k=O(n+log n) and s=n+O(log n). Dependence on
the sizes of tau and M, and all coefficient-program costs, remains counted.

6. A UNIFORM TERMINATING FINITE ALGORITHM
========================================

Use the periodic dyadic grid of side 2^(-m), each cube split into d!
Freudenthal simplices. Let V_m be continuous periodic piecewise affine
scalar functions, with one nodal value fixed to remove constants.
Admissible primal fields are e+grad V_m.

Admissible dual fields are constants plus div(Psi), where each entry of the
antisymmetric tensor Psi is in V_m. Mixed distributional derivatives commute,
so this field is exactly divergence free, including across simplex faces.
Select a linearly independent subset of these rational vector fields using
exact elimination. This is elementary vector-potential algebra, not a norm
claim about higher-degree differential forms.

Smooth gradients are uniformly approximated by gradients of such scalar
interpolants. Smooth divergence-free fields have a smooth antisymmetric
potential plus their mean on the torus; interpolating this potential gives
uniformly convergent dual fields. Combined with Section 3, the unions of
both finite spaces are dense in their respective energy spaces.

At stage n=1,2,...:

  (a) Obtain S_n, and construct C_n approximating A^(-1) to 2^(-n) by Section 5.
  (b) For every 0<=m<=floor(log2(n+1)), assemble the rational primal and dual
      quadratic forms, using S_n and C_n respectively.
  (c) Check positive definiteness of their Gram matrices by exact LDL. If
      a form fails, skip it at this stage; no global assertion follows.
  (d) Solve each positive-definite finite rational system exactly. Its
      minimizer/maximizer supplies an admissible pair of rational fields.
  (e) Evaluate (4.2), including both coefficient errors. If U-L<=epsilon^2,
      return the fields and the certificate. Otherwise continue.

For any fixed m the exact Gram matrices are positive definite. Their
approximations converge entrywise, so eventually (c) passes and the finite
solutions converge to the exact Galerkin solutions. Density makes the sum
of the exact primal and dual errors tend to zero as m increases. Choose
one finite m with total squared error less than epsilon^2/4. At sufficiently
large n this m is visited, its rational solution is close enough, and the
terms eta||E||_infinity^2 and zeta||B+F||_infinity^2 are small enough.
The stopping test must then pass. This proves termination on every valid
input, including when ess sup K=infinity. It is not a finite-test inference.

The executable exact_response.py implements this algorithm, including the
inverse-name construction, compatible finite spaces, exact matrix solves,
and certificate. An optional max_stages argument is an explicit local
resource cap; exhausting it reports no certificate, not a theorem of failure.

7. THE FULL RESOURCE ACCOUNT
============================

Let N* be the first stage that certifies. This is a finite, instance-dependent
stopping stage, not a supplied advice string. The algorithm discovers it.
No uniform polynomial upper bound on N* is claimed.

At stage n, let m_n=floor(log2(n+1)), and let ell_n be the maximum of m_n,
the source grid level, and the returned grid levels of S_n and S_(s_n).
The common mesh has

  J_n=d! 2^(d ell_n) simplices.

The primal dimension is <=2^(dm_n)-1; the dual dimension is at most
d+[d(d-1)/2]2^(dm_n). Grid expansion, basis arrays and dense Gram matrices
are actually formed in the reference implementation. There is no claim of
dimension-independent storage.

Let H_n bound the bit lengths of rational data AFTER the inverse filter,
including tau,M,F,e, mesh coordinates and filter parameters. Those lengths
are charged; they are not replaced by machine-precision scalars. For a
box matrix whose entries have B bits, clearing denominators in (5.1) uses
O(d^2 B+bitlength(R)) bits. Determinants and adjugates add a factor O(d),
and the k finite powers add a factor O(k). Ordinary rational elimination
or fraction-free elimination therefore computes each box filter in a
polynomial number of bit operations in d,k,B and bitlength(R).

A conservative explicit bookkeeping bound can be stated without pretending
to know N*. Let Z_n be the total binary length of all explicitly expanded
input tables, filtered tables, mesh coordinates, basis arrays, and input
scalars used across the levels of stage n, plus n+k_n+r_n+s_n+1. The added
integers are counted by value to charge the filter/parameter loop lengths,
not just their binary encodings. This is a finite computable quantity.
If T_A(s) is the
actual runtime of the supplied coefficient-name program at precision s,
the total reference-algorithm cost is bounded, up to a universal constant, by

  sum_(n=1)^N* [ T_A(n)+T_A(s_n)
                 + (1+Z_n)^16 ].                   (7.1)

Z_n includes the filtered matrix entries and the source precision needed to
produce them; constructing those entries is charged as polynomial work in
their precursor data. One may instead add that filtering polynomial as a
separate term. Repeated calls can be cached, reducing this upper bound.

For justification of the deliberately loose exponent 16: the number and
dimension of finite arrays are <=Z_n. A common denominator for all supplied
rationals has at most Z_n bits. Each integration entry is a bounded-degree
polynomial sum in those data, with polynomial bit length. Exact elimination
has polynomially many operations and determinant-controlled bit lengths.
Solving the systems, forming their fields, and recomputing the two quadratic
integrals also have polynomial bit cost. Clearing denominators explicitly
gives a bound below the displayed exponent even with schoolbook integer
arithmetic. This bound is polynomial in EXPANDED stage data, not in a short
program that describes an exponentially large table.

The input-name runtime can itself be arbitrarily large, and the theorem gives
no uniform spatial approximation rate. Equation (7.1) thus establishes paid
finite computation and certificate verification, not an efficient general
solver. A verifier need not replay the search; it checks the finite trials,
their exact constraint identities, rational integrals, and supplied error
contract. It does not decide arbitrary infinite program promises.

8. THE EFFECTIVE TENSOR AND ITS MACROSCOPIC MEANING
=================================================

Set F=0. Linearity and uniqueness make E_e and B_e linear in e. Define

  A_* e=mean B_e;   e.A_* e=min P(E).

The tensor A_* is symmetric positive definite. Cauchy-Schwarz and the trial
E=e give the elementary fixed-unit bounds

  (1/mu) I <= A_* <= mu I.                         (8.1)

Solve the d unit-mean loading problems. If each certified squared-error gap
is <=epsilon_tensor^2/(d mu), form the matrix whose columns are the rational
mean trial fluxes, then symmetrize it. Weighted Cauchy-Schwarz gives a
Frobenius, hence operator, error <=epsilon_tensor. Consequently

  Ahat-epsilon_tensor I <= A_* <= Ahat+epsilon_tensor I

is a certified Loewner enclosure. Only d solves are needed; their costs are
all charged. The earlier logarithmic theorem now applies to these existing
homogeneous cell solutions, giving the retained sharp concentration bounds
with cell energy <=mu|e|^2. No new nonlinear existence conclusion follows.

For completeness, A_* is also the variational homogenized tensor. Let Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and

  I_delta(u)=integral_Omega grad u.A(x/delta) grad u,

with zero trace and finite energy. Its domain can be taken either as the
smooth energy completion or the maximal finite-energy W^(1,q)_0 class, with
q=2-1/(d+1). The following argument proves the same Gamma limit in strong
L^2(Omega) for either choice:

  I_0(u)=integral_Omega grad u.A_* grad u,
  domain H^1_0(Omega).                             (8.2)

COERCIVITY. Holder with r=q/(2-q)=2d+1 gives
||grad u||_q^2 <= (integral K(x/delta)^r)^(1/r) I_delta(u).
Periodic cell covering and (1.2) bound the moment uniformly for delta<=1.
Since q>2d/(d+2), the Sobolev embedding into L^2 is compact. Thus bounded
energy sequences are precompact in L^2.

LOWER BOUND. First take smooth bounded periodic divergence-free trial fields
b_1,...,b_d, and a compactly supported smooth macroscopic vector p(x).
The flux b_delta(x)=sum_j p_j(x)b_j(x/delta) gives pointwise

  grad u.A_delta grad u
    >=2 grad u.b_delta-b_delta.A_delta^(-1)b_delta.

Its divergence contains only derivatives of p, since div_y b_j=0.
For u_delta converging strongly in L^2, integrate by parts and use periodic
averaging to pass the linear term to its limit. The quadratic term averages
because A^(-1) is integrable and b_j are bounded. Approximate the exact cell
fluxes B_(e_j) by such smooth b_j in the dual energy norm, using Section 3.
Their means converge to A_*e_j and their quadratic matrix converges to A_*.
It follows, for every p, that

  liminf I_delta(u_delta)
    >= integral [2 grad u.A_*p-p.A_*p].

Taking the supremum over p yields (8.2), including u in H^1_0. Membership
first follows from the bounded quadratic dual norm of the distributional
gradient; zero trace follows from weak W^(1,q)_0 convergence.

UPPER BOUND. For smooth compactly supported u and smooth bounded periodic
corrector trials phi_j, put

  u_delta=u+delta sum_j phi_j(x/delta) partial_j u.

Its gradient has leading term sum_j(e_j+grad phi_j)(x/delta)partial_j u.
The remaining term is O(delta) with bounded factors and therefore has
vanishing energy because A is integrable. Periodic averaging gives the
quadratic form of the trial energies. Approximate the exact cell correctors
in their energy norm, then diagonalize in the trial index and delta. Finally
approximate general u in H^1_0 by smooth compactly supported functions.
All recovery functions are in the smooth energy completion, which proves
the same upper bound for both domain choices.

This completes the variational homogenization proof. Adding a continuous
linear loading -2 integral f u with f in L^2 preserves Gamma convergence;
strict convexity and compactness give convergence of the variational
minimizers to the unique homogenized solution. This statement supplies no
finite-delta numerical error rate and no stochastic or nonperiodic law.

9. AN INFINITE, GENUINELY DEGENERATE EXECUTED EXAMPLE
===================================================

On Q in dimension two, let I_j=(2^(-j),2^(1-j)] for j>=1, and set

  A(x)=a(x_1) I,
  a=j on I_j for odd j; a=1/j on I_j for even j.

Both the essential upper bound of A and that of A^(-1) are infinite. This
scalar weight is not Muckenhoupt A2: small balls centered at an interface
between odd j and even j+1 have equal halves of values j and 1/(j+1), so the
product of their weight and reciprocal-weight averages grows quadratically
in j. Yet
K=j on I_j, and with tau=1/4,

  integral exp(K/4)=sum_(j>=1)2^(-j)exp(j/4)<2,

because exp(1/4)<4/3. Replacing the tail j>J by a=1 gives an L1 coefficient
error <=(J+3)2^(-J), proving an explicit valid input name. The expanded
uniform-grid name has exponentially many boxes; a specialized certificate
can instead integrate the J explicitly listed strips directly.

The exact effective tensor is

  diag( 1/[8/9+(log 3)/2], 10/9+(log(4/3))/2 ).     (9.1)

This formula is a reference identity, not a logarithm oracle in the
computation. For the first loading E*=(h/a,0), B*=(h,0), where
h=(integral 1/a)^(-1). For the second, E*=(0,1), B*=(0,a).

Let a_J equal a on the first J strips and one on the tail, and put
h_J=(integral 1/a_J)^(-1). Use the finite trials

  E_1=(h_J/a_J,0), B_1=(h_J,0),
  E_2=(0,1),      B_2=(0,a_J).

They are exactly compatible. Geometric-series sums and
1/j<=1/(J+1) on the tail give rational upper and lower energies. The total
tail mismatch is bounded by

  sum_(j>J) 2^(-j)(a_j+a_j^(-1)-2)
       <= [J+1/(J+1)]2^(-J).

At J=50 the saved rational certificate gives energy interval widths

  first loading:  2.147854856520813e-14,
  second loading: 4.442633624813764e-14.

The displayed interval endpoints (rounded, not the proof data) are

  first:  [0.6953159876786917, 0.6953159876787132],
  second: [1.2549521473369571, 1.2549521473370016].

All exact endpoints are rationals in UNBOUNDED_LAMINATE_CERTIFICATE.json.
Since d=2 and mu=M/tau=8, Section 8 also certifies operator error <=10^(-6)
for the tensor formed from the two mean trial fluxes. The saved laminate
certificate uses at most 227 bits in any numerator or denominator.

The universal grid implementation was separately exercised on a two-phase
cell, an off-diagonal coefficient with a nonzero source, and a noncommuting
coefficient perturbation in the inverse filter. The finite and forced cases
terminated at stages 4 and 3, respectively. verify_certificates.py checks
their saved fields directly, without replaying the search, as well as the
analytic infinite-series certificate. These tests validate those executions;
Sections 2-6 prove the universal assertions.

10. TWO ACCESS/COMPLEXITY OBJECTS THAT LIMIT THE CLAIM
=====================================================

POINT ACCESS. Any deterministic procedure making finitely many adaptive
point evaluations can receive A=I at every query. A coefficient equal to
2I away from the finite queried set gives the same transcript, satisfies the
same fixed moment bound tau=1/4,M=2, and has effective tensor 2I rather than I.
Thus point values alone cannot certify the general measurable response.
Even continuity without a supplied modulus does not repair finite-query
uniform certification: put smooth dips to I near the query points with total
support volume v<1/100, leaving 2I elsewhere. The harmonic lower bound is
2/(1+v) I, still separated from I. This covers all such finite deterministic
point-query procedures; it is not a lower bound against richer information.

SUCCINCT COUNTING. For a Boolean circuit C on n explicitly listed bits, put
d=n+1 and

  A_C(x)=[1+C(1_(x_2>=1/2),...,1_(x_(n+1)>=1/2))] I.

This is a compact explicit coefficient program with 1<=A_C<=2, and the same
fixed exponential moment bound. For mean field e_1, E=e_1 is exactly the
solution because the coefficient does not depend on x_1. Hence

  e_1.A_*e_1 = 1 + #SAT(C)/2^n.                    (10.1)

An algorithm polynomial in the circuit length and precision bits, returning
this energy with error <2^(-n-2), would recover the exact count by rounding
2^n(value-1). It would in particular establish P=NP. This is a conditional
counting-hardness theorem for a SUCCINCT encoding, not an unconditional
complexity separation and not a hardness claim for an already expanded
2^n-entry table. The present algorithm pays that expansion. The solution
gradient in this family is already known: integration itself carries the
counting task. No physical ground-state or relaxation operation is involved.

Both objects are preserved in FAILURE_OBJECTS.json with their failed claims
and exact scopes. Neither resolves unrestricted exact QUBO.
