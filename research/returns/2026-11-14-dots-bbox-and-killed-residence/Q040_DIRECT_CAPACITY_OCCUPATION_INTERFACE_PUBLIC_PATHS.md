# A direct capacity/path occupation inequality and its limitation

Let h(x)=P_x(τ_a<τ_B), c_xy=w_xq_xy, and C=cap(a,B). The equilibrium current c_xy(h_x−h_y), oriented down h, is acyclic and has total source C. Every positive edge current is at most C: take a level set of h between its endpoint values, whose outward total current equals C. Consequently any physical path γ from x to B gives
h(x)≤C Σ_{e∈γ}1/c_e.

Thus the normalized numerator integrand is bounded by
[w(x)/w(a)]h(x)≤min(w(x)/w(a), [C/w(a)]w(x)R_γ).
This is exact and accommodates arbitrary topology when γ is valid and stops at B. It could support an initial-occupation estimate without a uniform mean-duration theorem.

However, if the path keeps thermal exterior Y fixed, its resistance contains exp(E(Y)), which cancels the factor exp(−E(Y)) in w(x). Therefore the capacity-based branch cannot simply be summed with an unchanged Gibbs exterior factor. One must control the actual conditional current allocation, or bound the capped minimum with a legitimate thermal entropy/fractional-moment calculation. Counting all companions without weights loses the crucial entropy control. No completed macroscopic-sector estimate is claimed from this inequality.
