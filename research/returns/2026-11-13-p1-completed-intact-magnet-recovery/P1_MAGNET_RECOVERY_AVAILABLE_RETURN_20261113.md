2. The proposed mechanism: remove the pocket progressively while transferring support

The useful physical idea is to change how the joint is loaded.

An axial push against a fully bonded bar can mobilize a large fraction of the whole joint. The proposed machine instead removes the original steel pocket in thin axial increments, supporting each magnet while the release front advances.

The actual sequence

Receive and identify the complete rotor at 25°C.
Weigh it, record serial/revision and condition, inspect accessible ends, and account for all admitted magnet inventory. Remove shaft and end hardware with a supported fixture. Unknown caulks, welds or encapsulation require paid access work.

Pick one lamination at the rim.
Twelve compliant jaws engage a single 0.28 mm plate. Optical thickness verification stops multiple-sheet pickup. Initial slow displacement allows the plate to bend and initiate release at its interfaces.

Support the exposed magnet ends individually.
Twenty-four split end-stop pins support the 24 axial columns. Each has an individual force limit and measurement. A low total head force cannot establish a safe force on a jammed individual magnet.

Unthread the released plate into a collector.
The plate moves axially over the exposed bars. This is a real sliding operation; the model does not pretend that a closed pocket can simply move radially away.

Transfer support before emptying the collector.
After 30 plates, stripping pauses. A separate radial comb seats on unused strips of the magnet end faces. Its support is verified; the pins retract; the filled collector moves aside; the pins return through the empty collector; only then does the comb withdraw.

Repeat from the opposite end.
The remaining central steel collar supports both axial sets of magnets.

Close the final removal operation under broad support.
The reference construction leaves a 13.26 mm collar. Broad body grippers support the exposed magnets while wire EDM cuts steel-only contours. A three-side cut, grip transfer, and fourth-side cut prevent a permanent gripper from obstructing the wire path. The resulting short steel sleeves still require supported opening and removal.

The potentially new contribution is the specific release-and-support relay through repeated plate collection and final collar removal. Peeling, lamination separation and magnet reuse already have prior art. US7285890B2 discusses removable retention and breaking adhesive attachment by peeling; JP7798226B1 describes heating, resin embrittlement, deformation of laminated steel and magnet separation. I am not claiming patent novelty.

Engineering conclusion
