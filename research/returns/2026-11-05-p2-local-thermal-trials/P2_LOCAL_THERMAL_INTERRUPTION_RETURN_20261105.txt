## 2. The precise theorem

### Physical premises

Assume a classical Hamiltonian system with one constant-mass kinetic coordinate:

\[
H(p,z)=\frac{p^2}{2M}+V(z).
\]

Its Gibbs probability law therefore factors as

\[
\pi(dp,dz)=G_\sigma(dp)\,\pi_z(dz),
\qquad
\sigma^2=\frac{M}{\beta},
\]

where \(G_\sigma\) is the centered Gaussian momentum law and \(\beta^{-1}=k_BT\).

There is an **actual fresh local Langevin thermostat**:

\[
dx=\frac pM\,dt,
\qquad
dp=F(S)\,dt-\gamma p\,dt
+\sqrt{2\gamma\sigma^2}\,dW_t,
\qquad \gamma>0.
\]

The conservative dynamics preserves the Gibbs law. Additional invariant dissipative channels are permissible. The hidden interactions can be nonlinear, and no hidden dimension, spectral gap, or slowest relaxation rate needs to be known.

The kinetic factorization matters. Arbitrary momentum-dependent interactions are not covered.

Let \(R\) contain all previously retained classical information. The supplied preparation resource is

\[
\boxed{
D(\rho_{SR}\Vert\pi\otimes\rho_R)\le D_0<\infty.
}
\]

This quantity includes both physical nonequilibrium and shared information:

\[
D(\rho_{SR}\Vert\pi\otimes\rho_R)
=
D(\rho_S\Vert\pi)+I(S:R).
\]

A marginal free-energy bound alone would omit the second term. An exact reference copy of continuous microscopic coordinates can make this resource infinite.

### Local replacement result

Let \(C\rho\) denote the mathematical operation that keeps the joint \((z,R)\) marginal and replaces \(p\) by an independent draw from \(G_\sigma\). Define

\[
J(t)=D(\rho_{SR,t}\Vert C\rho_{SR,t}).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^\infty J(t)\,dt
\le \frac{D_0}{2\gamma}.
}
\]

If the private trigger time \(T\) is independent of the source and has a probability density bounded by \(C_g\), then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}
\left(
\mathcal L(T,p_T,z_T,R),
G_\sigma\otimes\mathcal L(T,z_T,R)
\right)
\le
\sqrt{\frac{C_gD_0}{4\gamma}}.
}
\]

Here

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=\frac12\|P-Q\|_1.
\]

The actual time is included in the comparison. This is an average guarantee over the private trigger and old history; it is not uniform over every exceptional realized time or rare posterior.

### Why the theorem holds

The thermostat gives the entropy-dissipation inequality

\[
-\frac{d}{dt}D(\rho_{SR,t}\Vert\pi\rho_R)
\ge \gamma\sigma^2 I_p(t),
\]

where \(I_p\) is relative Fisher information in the momentum coordinate.

Conditional Gaussian logarithmic Sobolev gives

\[
J(t)\le \frac{\sigma^2}{2}I_p(t).
\]

Integrating proves the first result. Averaging against the trigger density and applying Pinsker’s inequality proves the second.

These are established mathematical ingredients. Conditional momentum entropy in nonlinear stochastic Hamiltonian systems is particularly close to Vladimirov’s work; velocity-only entropy dissipation and local equilibria also have substantial earlier machinery in Desvillettes–Villani. The contribution constructed here is the finite physical timing, reference, measurement, and intervention operation built from them.

\[
\boxed{
J(t)
=
D(\rho_{p,t}\Vert G_\sigma)
+
I(p_t:z_t,R).
}
\]

Thus local dissipation bounds the time-integrated sum of **nonthermal momentum shape and hidden confounding**. It need not make the entire environment approach equilibrium.

## 4. The clock and measurement are constructed operations

### Private clock

A calibrated Poisson arrival clock, stopped at a finite timeout \(H\), has

\[
\Pr(\text{timeout})=e^{-rH}=\alpha_c
\]

and, conditional on arrival,

\[
C_g=\frac{r}{1-\alpha_c}.
\]

For thermal error allocation \(\delta_{\rm th}\), sufficient settings are

\[
r=
\frac{4\gamma(1-\alpha_c)\delta_{\rm th}^2}{D_0},
\]

\[
\boxed{
H=
\frac{D_0\log(1/\alpha_c)}
{4\gamma(1-\alpha_c)\delta_{\rm th}^2}.
}
\]

Expected clock occupancy, including timeout, is \((1-\alpha_c)/r\).

An actual independent arrival detector can supply the continuous trigger without storing an infinitely precise timestamp. Finite independent latency preserves the density bound. Detector errors, privacy, standby power, watchdog operation, and coupling to the specimen remain paid contracts.

A finite list of digital point times does **not** automatically inherit this theorem. The proof controls a time integral, which can concentrate at those points. Timeout produces an abort branch, not an experiment launched at a timeout atom.

### Finite momentum estimate

The main implementation uses two finite position windows, of width \(b\) and separation \(\ell\):

\[
\widehat p=\frac{M(Y_1-Y_0)}{\ell}.
\]

The noiseless expression is a positive weighted average of actual momentum over the window. The logical intervention time is its **end**, rather than an assumed frozen state at its beginning.

Given local moment and sensor bounds, a sufficient error is

\[
e_p\le
B_*(\ell+b)
+\sqrt{2\gamma\sigma^2(\ell+b)}
+\frac{2Me_x}{\ell}
+M\sqrt{\frac{2\nu_x}{b\ell^2}}.
\]

Here \(B_*\) bounds the local drift in mean square, \(e_x\) includes finite position-readout errors, and \(\nu_x\) is the declared white detector-noise density. Overflow is charged separately. The arm error is at most \(L_a e_p\), where \(L_a\) is the assignment probability’s Lipschitz constant.

### Explicit pointer and backaction

For each window, use a fresh canonical pointer \((Q,P)\) and a smooth finite interaction

\[
H_{\rm int}(t)
=
-Gw(t)\chi_x(x)\chi_P(P),
\]

with

\[
w(t)=\frac{1-\cos(2\pi t/b)}b.
\]

The saturations \(\chi_x,\chi_P\) equal the identity on declared finite ranges and have bounded derivatives. They make interaction energy and force finite.

On the good range event, the detached pointer has the exact signal

\[
Q(b)
=
Q_0+\frac{bP_0}{m_d}
-G\int_0^b w(t)x(t)\,dt.
\]

Its measurement backaction acts only in the thermostatted momentum direction. For two pointers with momentum variance \(v_P\),

\[
J_u:=\mathbb E\int u^2dt
\le \frac{3G^2v_P}{b}.
\]

Girsanov and Pinsker then give the full hidden/reference path bound

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm back}
\le
\sqrt{\frac{J_u}{8\gamma\sigma^2}}.
}
\]

The implementation also has explicit finite peak and slew requirements:

\[
|u|_{\max}\le \frac{2GP_{\rm cap}}b,
\qquad
|\dot g|_{\max}\le \frac{2\pi G}{b^2},
\]

and switching work per window is bounded by

\[
\frac{4GX_{\rm cap}P_{\rm cap}}b.
\]

The pointer initialization is separately constructed using a known isolated harmonic Langevin reset, with a finite convergence time from a bounded initial second moment. Trap release, readout, refrigeration, reset, and receiver protection are additional physical costs.

**Imprecision and backaction cannot be chosen independently.** The pointer covariance obeys their joint tradeoff, and a quantum pointer must satisfy \(v_Qv_P\ge\hbar^2/4\). Fixed hardware therefore has an error floor. This classical theorem does not imply arbitrary accuracy through quantum or finite-bandwidth limits.

## 6. A proved separation from global equilibration

Consider two coupled rotors with

independently of the hidden barrier \(B\).

Start with the Gibbs ensemble conditioned on one hidden well and shift \(p\) by \(a\sigma R\), where \(R\) is a retained fair sign. Then

independently of \(B\).
