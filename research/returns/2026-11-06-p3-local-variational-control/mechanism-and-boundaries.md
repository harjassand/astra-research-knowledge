# Marginal flow, finite-time control and the geometric boundary

These are the [complete return's reported results](P3_LOCAL_VARIATIONAL_CONTROL_20261106_RETURN.txt), without a new Capital scientific check.

There are `n` finite-state components, each with at most `q` states; each jump changes one component. The benchmark can observe the whole state, keep arbitrary history, randomize and coordinate globally. For every allowed component transition it can select any intensity in a common box `a≤u_iy≤b`, where `0<a<b<∞`, and every selection must remain attainable as neighbouring states change. A generalization uses a common compact convex outgoing-rate set. Preserving physical actuation costs requires a **jointly attainable rate-and-cost set**; bounds on rates alone do not bound energy.

The objective is expected additive reward per component per unit time: bounded local state rewards plus bounded local one-jump rewards. Rational model tables or computable enclosures with counted precision cost are supplied. Acquisition of an arbitrary unknown apparatus is not part of this theorem.

Given a stationary global occupation `μ` and balanced transition flux `f`, marginalize both over the exterior of a block `B`. Exterior jumps cancel from the balance equations; `a μ_B(z)≤f_B(z,i,y)≤b μ_B(z)` survives summation. The local command `u^B_iy(z)=f_B(z,i,y)/μ_B(z)` therefore realizes the marginal occupation and interior flux. Independent blocks reproduce all retained interior additive rewards, even when the original global process has correlated blocks. They do not reproduce its full trajectory law or arbitrary distant correlations. The optimal global occupation is a proof object, not a compiler input: the compiler solves finite block problems directly.

If omitted crossing rewards have predictable-rate bounds `L∂≤r∂≤U∂`, write `D∂=U∂−L∂` and let `λ_B` be the block retained optimum. The source establishes

`Σ_B λ_B+L∂ ≤ Jblock ≤ J* ≤ Σ_B λ_B+U∂`, hence `0≤(J*−Jblock)/n≤D∂/n`.

This premise fails if an uncontrolled component flips at rates 0.1/0.9 depending on its neighbour but no actuator can implement the marginal 0.5 independently. That is a failed premise of this construction, not a general interacting-control impossibility. For scalar rewards the Bellman expression is affine in each rate, so endpoint primitive commands suffice for an optimal block policy. Exact vector-performance preservation retains the convex attainability requirements.

For all initial states/distributions and every `T>0`, finite Bellman upper/lower residuals give global upper and delivered-policy lower bounds with explicit `Σ span(h_B^±)/(nT)` transients. The benchmark includes history-dependent controllers. Pointwise residual integration supplies the bound without estimating a global mixing time or preparing equilibrium. Potential spans can be large; they are paid transient costs. Implementation intensity error is weighted by `|w_iy+H(x^{i,y})−H(x)|` and integrated, rather than divided by an unknown global spectral gap. Source-reported martingale/adaptive-stopping extensions have allocated failure budgets; they do not establish first-catastrophe protection.

For bounded-incidence finite-range rewards on a fixed-dimensional lattice, side-`L` blocks have `D∂/n≤C/L`. A block contains `k=L^d=O(ε^(−d))` sites; its occupation LP has `O(k q^(k+1))` variables/constraints. The conservative compilation bound is `n poly(k q^(k+1),B)` for input bit length `B`. It is linear in `n` at fixed accuracy, **exponential in accuracy-dependent block volume**. Translation-invariant rational data/common rate box permit finite-LP enclosures of a limiting intensive bulk optimum on expanding boxes/tori. This does not compute every infinite-volume stationary law, phase structure or relaxation time.

Vector additive performance/resource sets can be approximated. Mean constraints need boundary/implementation allowances, with a supplied feasibility margin or explicit certificate near a boundary; a margin `σ` can cost error divided by `σ`. **Hard instantaneous shared budgets are outside this transfer.** Global-state fidelity, arbitrary path correlations and first catastrophes are also outside the additive-performance theorem.

For general bounded-degree graphs, fixed constants `a,b,ε₀>0` retain NP-hard additive-`ε₀` optimal-intensive-value certification. The MaxCut reward reduction bounds the source-model optimum between `M(G)−2p|E|` and `M(G)` with fixed bit error `p=a/(a+b)`; sufficiently small fixed `p` preserves the cubic-MaxCut approximation gap. Alimonti–Kann supplies the discrete hardness prior, while preservation under this control model is the reported deduction. Positive noise alone is not a general tractability theorem. This does not resolve P versus NP or claim that writing an unevaluated policy is hard.

The return credits factored LP/constraint elimination and Bellman methods to Guestrin–Koller–Parr–Venkataraman, influence/stability local-policy guarantees to Faizal–Ozdaglar–Wainwright, average-reward/ergodicity learning guarantees to Qu–Lin–Wierman–Li, and identical-arm/shared-action-budget fluid control to Goldsztajn–Avrachenkov. Their assumptions differ; neither universal dominance nor a matched practical speedup is established. Baker's geometric decomposition is credited as precedent without importing an unread planar theorem.
