## 2. Exact assumptions

Consider \(n\) components with finite state spaces \(S_i\), each containing at most \(q\) states. The complete state is

\[
x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in\prod_i S_i.
\]

A transition changes one component at a time. Write \(x^{i,y}\) for the state obtained by replacing \(x_i\) with \(y\).

### The substantive control assumption

For every component transition, the controller can select its intensity within

\[
a\le u_{iy}(t)\le b,
\qquad 0<a<b<\infty.
\]

**Every such choice must actually be admissible, including when the exterior state changes.** Merely knowing that an uncontrolled system’s rates lie between \(a\) and \(b\) does not satisfy this assumption.

The ideal benchmark may observe the complete state, retain its entire history, randomize and coordinate globally. Thus the comparison is against a stronger information and control interface than the delivered noisy local controller receives.

The mathematical extension replaces the box with an appropriate common compact convex set of attainable outgoing rates. If physical actuation costs must also be preserved, the relevant object is the **jointly attainable rate-and-cost set**. A rate constraint alone does not establish an energy constraint.

### The objective assumption

The physical performance record has the form

\[
R_T
=
\int_0^T v(X_t)\,dt
+
\sum_{\substack{\text{jumps }(i,y)\\t\le T}}
w_{iy}(X_{t-}),
\]

where \(v\) is a sum of bounded local state terms, and each jump reward \(w_{iy}\) has a bounded local support containing the jumping component.

Examples within the model include:

- a reward for completing a job;
- a cost per transition;
- a penalty for neighbouring components being occupied simultaneously;
- additive operating costs;
- several such records considered together.

The principal objective is expected reward per component per unit time,

\[
\frac{\mathbb E R_T}{nT}.
\]

The model tables must be supplied with rational entries or computable enclosures whose precision costs are counted. **General acquisition of an arbitrary unknown local apparatus is not supplied by this theorem.** The completed acquisition construction below is narrower.

## 3. The central operation: implement the marginal flow

The crucial step is an explicit construction.

Let \(\mu(x)\) be a stationary occupation law under an admissible global controller, and let \(f(x,i,y)\) be its stationary transition flux. They satisfy flow balance and

\[
a\mu(x)\le f(x,i,y)\le b\mu(x).
\]

For a block \(B\), define the marginal occupation and interior flux by

\[
\mu_B(z)=\sum_{x:x_B=z}\mu(x),
\qquad
f_B(z,i,y)=\sum_{x:x_B=z}f(x,i,y).
\]

Summing the global balance equations over all exterior states cancels every transition outside \(B\). The remaining equations are exactly the block’s balance equations. The rate bounds also survive summation:

\[
a\mu_B(z)\le f_B(z,i,y)\le b\mu_B(z).
\]

Therefore the block controller

\[
\boxed{
u^B_{iy}(z)=\frac{f_B(z,i,y)}{\mu_B(z)}
}
\]

is admissible and has precisely the required marginal stationary occupation and flux.

Running these block controllers independently reproduces, simultaneously, every retained interior state reward and every retained interior one-jump reward.

**The original process need not have independent blocks.** The construction preserves the statistics needed by the additive objective. It does not preserve the complete trajectory law or arbitrary correlations between distant blocks.

### The global optimality bound

Partition the network into blocks. Retain only reward terms whose full support lies inside one block.

Let the predictable reward rate of the omitted, crossing terms satisfy

\[
L_\partial\le r_\partial(x,u)\le U_\partial,
\qquad
D_\partial=U_\partial-L_\partial.
\]

Let \(\lambda_B\) be the optimal retained reward rate for block \(B\). Let \(J^*\) denote the best global long-run total reward rate, allowing all admissible adaptive controllers, and let \(J^{\mathrm{block}}\) be the rate achieved by the product of optimal block controllers.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_B\lambda_B+L_\partial
\;\le\;
J^{\mathrm{block}}
\;\le\;
J^*
\;\le\;
\sum_B\lambda_B+U_\partial
}
\]

and consequently

\[
\boxed{
0\le \frac{J^*-J^{\mathrm{block}}}{n}
\le \frac{D_\partial}{n}.
}
\]

This is the main theorem: **the value of global coordination is bounded by the reward variation across the chosen spatial interfaces.**

The optimal global occupation law appears only in the proof. The compiler does not need to acquire it. It solves the finite block problems directly.

### Why the control assumption cannot be weakened to rate bounds

Suppose a component has no actuator and flips at rate \(0.1\) when its neighbour is zero and \(0.9\) when its neighbour is one. Marginalization might produce an effective rate \(0.5\), but there is no command that implements \(0.5\) independently of that neighbour.

That example identifies a failed premise of this construction. It is not a general impossibility theorem for controlling interacting systems.

A useful simplification is available for the scalar objective: because the Bellman expression is affine in each rate, an optimal block policy can select only the endpoints \(a\) and \(b\). Thus **two correctly realized primitive commands suffice for the scalar controller**, even though the convex rate set is used in the marginal-flow proof and as a stronger benchmark. Exact preservation of general performance vectors retains its convexity requirements.

## 4. Finite-time guarantees, without equilibrium preparation

The result does not require the system to begin in stationarity.

For each block, a finite potential \(h_B\) supplies a checkable Bellman inequality. Let \(U_B\) be a valid upper residual for every admissible action, and let \(L_B\) be a valid lower residual for the delivered block policy. With separate upper and lower potentials, define

\[
S_\pm=\sum_B\operatorname{span}(h_B^\pm).
\]

Then, from **every initial state or distribution**, for every \(T>0\),

\[
\sup_\pi\frac{\mathbb E_\pi R_T}{nT}
\le
\frac{\sum_B U_B+U_\partial}{n}
+\frac{S_+}{nT},
\]

while

\[
\frac{\mathbb E_{\mathrm{block}}R_T}{nT}
\ge
\frac{\sum_B L_B+L_\partial}{n}
-\frac{S_-}{nT}.
\]

The upper bound includes controllers that retain the complete history.

The proof integrates the pointwise inequality for \(r+\mathcal L H\), where \(H=\sum_B h_B\), and bounds the endpoint term \(H(X_T)-H(X_0)\). **A local potential span replaces the need to estimate global relaxation.** The span is a real transient cost and can be large.

The same argument gives a direct implementation-error allowance. If the actual intensity differs from the certified one by \(\delta_{iy}(t)\), the residual error is bounded by

\[
\sum_{i,y}
|\delta_{iy}(t)|
\left|
w_{iy}(X_t)+H(X_t^{i,y})-H(X_t)
\right|.
\]

This makes calibration errors, stale observations and incorrect command selections enter through their accumulated effect on the task. There is no division by an unknown global spectral gap.

The report also supplies martingale bounds for retained additive records and adaptive stopping, with explicit failure-budget allocation. Those extensions do not turn the result into a first-catastrophe guarantee.

## 5. Broad deductions

### Finite patches approximate arbitrarily large geometric systems

For bounded-range rewards of bounded incidence on a fixed-dimensional lattice, blocks of side length \(L\) can be chosen so that

\[
\frac{D_\partial}{n}\le \frac{C}{L}.
\]

A block contains at most \(k=L^d\) components. To achieve an intensive error \(\varepsilon\), it therefore suffices to take \(L\) of order \(C/\varepsilon\).

The block occupation LP has \(O(kq^{k+1})\) variables and constraints. With local input bit length \(B\), a conservative compilation bound is

\[
n\,\operatorname{poly}\!\left(kq^{k+1},B\right),
\qquad
k=O(\varepsilon^{-d}).
\]

This is linear in system size at fixed accuracy. **Its accuracy dependence remains exponential in the block volume.** The theorem does not make that expense practically harmless.

### The optimal bulk performance is computable

For translation-invariant rational finite-range data and a fixed common rate box, the intensive optimal values on expanding lattice boxes or tori converge.

A finite block LP gives an explicit enclosure of the limiting value. Consequently, one can compute the bulk optimum to additive accuracy without representing the eventual global state space.

This concerns the optimal intensive performance. It does not imply that every infinite-volume stationary law, phase structure or relaxation time is computable.

### Several useful outputs and mean resources can be handled together

The marginal construction preserves every interior coordinate of a finite vector of additive records. It therefore approximates the attainable performance/resource set, rather than only one scalar reward.

Mean resource constraints can be enforced with conservative boundary and implementation allowances. Exact feasibility near a constraint boundary needs a supplied feasibility margin or another explicit certificate. A margin \(\sigma\) can lead to an additional loss proportional to the approximation error divided by \(\sigma\).

**Hard instantaneous shared budgets remain a different control problem.**

### Fixed positive stochastic noise does not remove general computational hardness

The report proves that there are fixed constants \(0<a<b\) and \(\varepsilon_0>0\) for which additive-\(\varepsilon_0\) certification of the optimal intensive value is NP-hard on general bounded-degree graphs.

The construction uses the MaxCut state reward. If \(M(G)\) is the maximum cut, every controller satisfies \(J^*(G)\le M(G)\). A policy biased toward a maximum-cut assignment has independent stationary bit error

\[
p=\frac{a}{a+b},
\]

giving

\[
M(G)-2p|E|
\le J^*(G)\le M(G).
\]

Choosing a sufficiently small **fixed** positive \(p\) preserves the constant approximation gap from cubic MaxCut. The underlying cubic-graph hardness is due to Alimonti and Kann; preservation of that gap under the stated stochastic control model is the deduction in this report. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

This is a hardness result for certified optimal values. It does not resolve P versus NP, classify every graph family, or prove that merely printing an unevaluated controller is hard.
