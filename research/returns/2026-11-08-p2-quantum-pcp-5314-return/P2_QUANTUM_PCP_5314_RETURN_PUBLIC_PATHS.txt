2. Construction and proof

The construction combines two facts:


- Neighboring qubits can approximately agree after prescribed rotations, giving small energy.
- Exact agreement around sufficiently many cycles imposes incompatible rotation axes, even on entangled states.

The distinction between approximate agreement and exact satisfaction persists with fixed local matrices.


2.1 Two fixed rational projectors

Let $X,Y$ be Pauli matrices, and define
$U=\frac{I-2iX}{\sqrt5},
\qquad
V=\frac{I-2iY}{\sqrt5}.$
For $s\in\{U,V\}$, put
$h_s
=
(I\otimes s)|\Psi^-\rangle\langle\Psi^-|(I\otimes s^\dagger),
\qquad
|\Psi^-\rangle=\frac{|01\rangle-|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}.$
Equivalently,
$h_s=\frac{I-(s^\dagger\otimes s)\operatorname{SWAP}}2.$
These are rank-one projectors. They have the explicit descriptions
$h_U=\frac1{10}v_Uv_U^\dagger,
\qquad
v_U=(-2i,1,-1,2i)^T,$
and
$h_V=\frac1{10}v_Vv_V^\dagger,
\qquad
v_V=(-2,1,-1,-2)^T.$
Consequently, the square roots in $U,V$ disappear from the projector matrices. Every real and imaginary entry has a fixed rational description.

Their Bloch-sphere rotations are
$R_U=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&-3/5&-4/5\\
0&4/5&-3/5
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R_V=
\begin{pmatrix}
-3/5&0&4/5\\
0&1&0\\
-4/5&0&-3/5
\end{pmatrix}.$
For pure qubit states with unit Bloch vectors $r_i,r_j$,
$\operatorname{Tr}\!\left[h_s(\rho_i\otimes\rho_j)\right]
=
\frac{1-r_j\cdot R_s r_i}{4}
=
\frac{\|r_j-R_s r_i\|^2}{8}.
\tag{1}$
Thus approximately following the rotations gives low energy.

An oriented edge $i\to j$ places $h_s$ on the ordered pair $(i,j)$. If supports must be listed in increasing order, reversed edges use the swapped matrix, equivalently the inverse twist. This accounts for the alphabet of at most four matrices.


2.2 The rotations freely generate a group

The required freeness has a short arithmetic proof, so no unread group-theory source is needed.

Consider the integral quaternion letters
$1+2\mathbf i,\quad 1-2\mathbf i,\quad
1+2\mathbf j,\quad 1-2\mathbf j,$
each of norm $5$. Represent the quaternion generators over $\mathbb F_5$ by
$\mathbf i\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf j\longmapsto
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\4&0\end{pmatrix}.$
These square to $-I$ and anticommute. The four letter matrices have rank one and four distinct image lines. The kernel of each letter matrix is exactly the image of its inverse-letter matrix.

It follows that a product associated with a freely reduced word is nonzero modulo five: adjacent rank-one factors annihilate precisely when the letters are inverses.

Suppose a nonempty reduced word of length $L$ represented the identity rotation. Its integral quaternion numerator would be a scalar $a$, with
$a^2=5^L.$
For odd $L$, this is impossible for integral $a$. For even positive $L$,
$a=\pm5^{L/2},$
whose matrix vanishes modulo five, contradicting the nonzero-product calculation.

Therefore $R_U,R_V$ generate a free subgroup of $SO(3)$. In particular, noncommuting free words have noncommuting rotation images.


2.3 A finite approximate action of the rotations

Partition the unit sphere into $K$ equal intervals of its height coordinate and $K$ equal longitude intervals. This gives $N=K^2$ cells $C_i$, each of normalized area $1/N$.

A cell has diameter at most
$\frac2{\sqrt K}+\frac{2\pi+2}{K}.$
Choose a rational unit vector $r_i$ within $1/K$ of each cell center. Rational stereographic coordinates give an effective construction with $O(\log K)$-bit coordinates. Every point of $C_i$ is then within
$\delta=\frac{12}{\sqrt K}$
of $r_i$.

For each $s\in\{U,V\}$, form a bipartite graph between two copies of the cells, including $i\to j$ whenever
$\|R_s r_i-r_j\|^2
\le16\delta^2
=\frac{2304}{K}.
\tag{2}$
This is an exact rational comparison.

If $R_s(C_i)$ intersects $C_j$, the representative distance is at most $2\delta$, so that intersection is represented by an edge in this graph.

The graph satisfies Hall’s condition. For any left-cell set $I$, the union of its rotated cells has area $|I|/N$. Its neighboring right cells cover that union, up to null boundaries, and each has area $1/N$. Hence there are at least $|I|$ neighbors.

A deterministic perfect matching therefore gives a permutation $\pi_s$ satisfying
$\|R_s r_i-r_{\pi_s(i)}\|\le4\delta.
\tag{3}$
Introduce the two labeled edges
$i\to\pi_U(i),\qquad i\to\pi_V(i)$
at every vertex. Initially there are $2N$ edges, and each vertex has at most four incidences.


2.4 Removing loops and parallel edges

Let $\mathcal W$ be the 16 nonempty reduced words of length one or two in $U^{\pm1},V^{\pm1}$.

$\|R_w r_i-r_i\|\le8\delta.

If $i$ is bad for $w$, every $x\in C_i$ satisfies

For rotation axis $a_w$,
$\|R_wx-x\|

The relevant points lie in two polar caps of total normalized area at most
$\left(\frac{10\delta}{\kappa_w}\right)^2
\le\frac{5625}{K}.$
Equal cell areas therefore bound the number of bad vertices by
$|B|\le\frac{90000N}{K}.$
Delete every edge incident to a bad vertex. Since each vertex has at most four incidences,
$m_K

\tag{5}$
The remaining graph is simple. A loop would give an approximate fixed point of a generator; two edges with the same unordered endpoints would give an approximate fixed point of a reduced word of length two. The triangle inequality bounds the relevant displacement by $8\delta$, so an endpoint would have been deleted.

The retained graph has maximum degree four and at most one incoming and one outgoing edge of each label at each vertex.


2.5 Exact satisfaction against arbitrary entangled states

This is the essential soundness step.

Take an edge subset $F$ with a nonzero common kernel. Consider a connected component on $q$ sites, allowing an arbitrary external reference.

Choose a spanning tree and local unitaries $W_i$ obtained by transporting labels from its root. Under this local basis change, an edge $i\to j$ labeled $s$ has twist
$A_e=W_j^\dagger sW_i.$
Every tree twist becomes identity. Its constraint becomes
$\frac{I-\operatorname{SWAP}_{ij}}2.$
Exact satisfaction therefore makes the state invariant under every tree-edge transposition. These transpositions generate all permutations of the component, so its state lies in
$\operatorname{Sym}^q(\mathbb C^2)
\otimes\mathcal H_{\mathrm{reference}}.
\tag{6}$
Now consider a non-tree edge. Its twist is the holonomy around a fundamental cycle.

The labeled graph immerses into the two-generator rose: a nonbacktracking graph path has freely reduced labels, because adjacent inverse labels would force the same edge to be traversed backward. Thus the map on fundamental groups is injective. Projective freeness implies that the cycle holonomy $A_e$ is nonscalar.

Inside the symmetric subspace, its constraint says
$(A_e^\dagger)_i(A_e)_j|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle.$
By symmetry, the same equation holds for every pair of component sites. If $|a_+\rangle,|a_-\rangle$ are the two eigenvectors of $A_e$, the equation excludes unequal eigenlabels. Hence the component must lie in
$\mathcal G_A
=
\operatorname{span}\{
|a_+\rangle^{\otimes q},
|a_-\rangle^{\otimes q}
\},
\tag{7}$
tensored with the reference.

If the component has two independent cycles, their holonomies do not commute projectively. Their eigenaxes are therefore distinct.

For $q\ge3$, the two corresponding spaces in (7) have zero intersection. To verify this directly, write the second eigenbasis as
$|b_+\rangle=\alpha|a_+\rangle+\beta|a_-\rangle,
\qquad
|b_-\rangle=-\bar\beta|a_+\rangle+\bar\alpha|a_-\rangle,$
where $\alpha\beta\ne0$.

If a combination
$A|b_+\rangle^{\otimes q}
+
B|b_-\rangle^{\otimes q}$
belongs to $\mathcal G_A$, its Dicke coefficients of weights one and two vanish. After factoring nonzero powers, the coefficient matrix is
$\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha&-\bar\beta\\
\beta&\bar\alpha
\end{pmatrix},$
whose determinant is
$|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1.$
Thus $A=B=0$. The argument works equally when $A,B$ are vectors in a reference space.

A simple graph with at least two independent cycles has at least four vertices, so the $q\ge3$ condition always applies.

Therefore every exactly satisfiable component is a tree or unicyclic. Every exactly satisfiable edge subset is a pseudoforest, with at most $N$ edges.

For any density matrix $\rho$, let
$F_\rho=\{e:\operatorname{Tr}(h_e\rho)=0\}.$
Positivity forces the support of $\rho$ into the common kernel of these terms. Consequently,
$|F_\rho|\le N,$
and
$\frac{|\{e:\operatorname{Tr}(h_e\rho)>0\}|}{m_K}
\ge
1-\frac{N}{m_K}
\ge\frac13.
\tag{8}$
This proves the combinatorial bound against every entangled state.

\frac{I+r_i\cdot\sigma}{2}.$

3. Precision, construction costs, and the real-matrix version

The geometric construction does not assume an oracle for cell intersections. Intersections are used only to prove Hall’s condition. The algorithm itself uses the rational distance test (2).

The paid operations are:


- Construct $N$ rational unit vectors with $O(\log N)$-bit coordinates.
- Perform $O(N^2)$ exact rational comparisons for the two matching graphs.
- Find two perfect matchings deterministically.
- Perform 16 fixed-word rotation tests per vertex.
- Delete edges and print every local matrix and support address.

The elementary function evaluations needed for cell centers require only $O(\log N)$ bits. Certified fixed-point series, square-root bisection, and rational stereographic projection suffice with $\operatorname{polylog}N$ work per representative.

A straightforward augmenting-path matching algorithm gives
$O(N^3\operatorname{polylog}N)$

$O_s=

\operatorname{Re}s&-\operatorname{Im}s\\

5. Two other attempted interfaces and their exact limits


State-preserving localization loses normalized strength

Consider the long OR projector
$P_r=I-|0^r\rangle\langle0^r|.$
Suppose a proposed replacement is
$K=\frac1m\sum_j k_j,\qquad 0\le k_j\le I,$
with locality at most $k$, allowing arbitrary ancillary sites. Let an extension $\sigma$ of $|0^r\rangle$ have energy at most $a$.

If $d_i$ counts terms touching input site $i$, then
$\sum_i d_i\le km.$
Flipping site $i$ changes only those $d_i$ expectations, each by at most one. Thus some one-bit-flipped extension obeys
$\operatorname{Tr}(KX_i\sigma X_i)\le a+\frac{k}{r},$
although its original OR energy is one.

For a depth-$D$ encoding with gate arity $g$, the same backward-lightcone argument gives $kg^D/r$.

This obstructs shallow, state-preserving localization of these tests. It does not obstruct an arbitrary compiler, which may reorganize the proof and need not preserve those input states or their complete energy function.


Fixed postselection primitives can amplify tiny errors

Let $s=1/2$, and define
$H_N=\frac1N\sum_i|1\rangle\langle1|_i,
\qquad
A_N=(s|0\rangle\langle0|+|1\rangle\langle1|)^{\otimes N}.$

$\rho=(1-q)|0^N\rangle\langle0^N|

The input energy is $q$. The filter succeeds with probability $q(2-q)$, and conditioned on success the energy becomes
$\frac1{2-q}\ge\frac12.$
Therefore a fixed rational filter alphabet gives no generic stability guarantee for conditioned energy.

This does not disprove a specially balanced postselection analysis. It identifies the necessary kind of control: errors must be bounded relative to the actual success weights. Small survival probability also does not automatically mean exponential description cost; its logarithm may be polynomial.
