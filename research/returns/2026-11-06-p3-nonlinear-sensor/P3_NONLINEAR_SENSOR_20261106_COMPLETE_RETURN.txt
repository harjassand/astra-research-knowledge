Existence comes from the true pullback of the ordinary affine connection. For uniqueness, the difference of two solutions is a symmetric bilinear tensor whose associated endomorphisms preserve every native line. The same block algebra used above forces that tensor to vanish on every block of dimension at least two.

Thus the construction uses finite-dimensional linear algebra at each point.

### Integrate the resulting connection

Along a path \(\gamma\), solve

\[
\dot E
=
E\sum_k \Gamma_k(\gamma)\dot\gamma_k,
\qquad
\dot z=E\dot\gamma,
\]

starting from \(E=I\), \(z=0\) at a chosen base point.

Under the source promise, the connection is flat. The observed domain is diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^n\), so the integration is path-independent and yields

\[
H=A g^{-1}+b.
\]

The state-combination formula then follows.

For noisy estimates, the fitted connection need not be flat. The finite construction therefore uses specified integration paths and controls their error against the true chart. It does not treat a small fitting residual as a flatness certificate, or assert that an approximately transported frame is automatically the derivative of its approximate chart.

### Precision is carried through to the operation

Writing the linear system as \(A\gamma=c\), suppose

\[
\sigma_{\min}(A)\ge s,\qquad
\|\gamma\|\le G_0.
\]

If the measured coefficients satisfy

\[
\|\widehat A-A\|\le\eta_A\le s/2,
\qquad
\|\widehat c-c\|\le\eta_c,
\]

then

\[
\|\widehat\gamma-\gamma\|
\le
\frac{2}{s}\bigl(\eta_c+G_0\eta_A\bigr).
\]

The conditioning margin can be certified from a measured matrix with a perturbation allowance. It need not be supplied by an exact spectral oracle.

Connection error is then propagated through the integration. For connection error \(\eta\), path length at most \(D\), and connection bound \(G\),

\[
\|\widehat H-H\|
\le
D^2\eta e^{(G+\eta)D},
\]

before numerical-integration and rounding errors.

Finally, if the chart error is uniformly at most \(\epsilon\), \(H^{-1}\) is \(L_H\)-Lipschitz, and the numerical inverse has residual at most \(\eta_{\rm inv}\), the operation error is

\[
\boxed{
\|\widehat y-\mathcal S_w(y_1,\ldots,y_k)\|
\le
L_H
\left[
\left(1+\sum_i|w_i|\right)\epsilon+\eta_{\rm inv}
\right].
}
\]

For convex combinations, the chart contribution is \(2L_H\epsilon\), independently of the number of states. For transferring a displacement, it is \(4L_H\epsilon\).

A single uniform chart-confidence event therefore supports adaptive reuse throughout the certified domain.

## 4. Broad deductions from the constructed operation

### Physical arithmetic becomes identifiable

The reconstructed chart determines physical midpoints, weighted averages, displacement transfer and parallelogram completion, even though the sensor is nonlinear and its absolute physical calibration is unknown.

This is an operation-level invariant. An arbitrary linear basis ambiguity can remain while all these outputs are fixed.

### Entire classes of intervention laws become identifiable

There is also an exact-law corollary.

Consider any finite adaptive protocol that alternates:

1. native Markov evolution; and
2. specified physical affine state updates.

The waiting times, combination weights and reference observed states may depend on the retained record. The observed process law identifies the native transition kernels, and the construction identifies every state-update map. Composing those kernels and maps identifies the joint law of the complete protocol record.

This corollary assumes the same native dynamics resume after the prescribed state update. It does not establish that an actuator can perform the update, or that changing the state leaves an unmodelled environment unchanged.

It also does not turn the finite chart construction into an efficient sampler of arbitrary future trajectories. Acquisition and computation of future transition laws remain separate costs.

### Some apparent nonlinearities become scientifically decidable

Within the rigid class, the remaining coordinate changes are affine. Affine linearity and polynomial degree of the latent drift consequently become invariant properties of the observed law.

A nonlinear sensor can no longer arbitrarily convert a physically affine drift into an inequivalent nonlinear explanation within the declared source class.

This does not identify a labelled interaction graph or an actuator address: a general linear basis change can alter those objects.

### Minimal perturbation geometry can be counted

Starting from \(n\) coordinate directions, one additional direction involving every coordinate removes all nonlinear calibration freedoms.

If an additional direction may involve at most \(k\) coordinates, \(2\le k\le n\), then at least

\[
\left\lceil\frac nk\right\rceil
\]

such directions are necessary to cover every coordinate, and that number is attainable by groups of sizes between two and \(k\), with overlap when needed.

This is a count of perturbation mechanisms. Engineering those mechanisms, maintaining their rates and observing them are separate physical costs.

## 5. Finite noisy acquisition: an operation with explicit costs

The finite construction uses a narrower source class than the exact theorem: a finite-rate piecewise-deterministic process, without a Brownian component.

Its quantitative promises include:

- a bound on between-jump velocity and total jump rate;
- a lower intensity for small jumps in each direction;
- separation and regularity of the observed direction fields;
- stationary probability lower bounds for the required spatial neighborhoods;
- quantitative mixing and initialization bounds;
- a calibrated finite-accuracy reading model, including clocks and simultaneous access.

These are substantive source and apparatus conditions. Full stationary support alone does not establish them, and the construction does not universally certify them from a short record.

### Multiple events are tolerated

The acquisition procedure uses packets containing two readings separated by a short interval. It retains suitable small increments near a spatial grid point.

A genuine single jump lies near one of the local direction lines. A packet with several jumps is treated as a possible outlier. The procedure chooses the packet interval so that each genuine direction contributes substantially more samples than the total outlier budget.

A maximum-occupancy clustering rule then recovers the directions without knowing the event labels. It repeatedly selects a small projective ball containing the most retained increments, records its center, and removes a slightly larger ball.

The proof guarantees that each selected cluster captures one complete genuine direction cluster and cannot consume another. It avoids the much more expensive requirement that no multiple-event interval occur anywhere in the campaign.

### The entire path to an answer is finite

The report carries this through:

- dependent-trajectory acquisition using paid separation times and warm-up;
- neighboring direction matching;
- finite-difference derivative estimation;
- interval linear algebra;
- finite-bit integration;
- a finite inverse search with a residual criterion.

The inverse search may be expensive. Its existence is not replaced by an ideal inverse oracle.

For fixed dimension and fixed positive source and conditioning constants, one conservative choice gives a packet count of the form

\[
N
=
O\!\left(
\eta^{-(2n+6)}
\log\frac{K(m+1)}{\delta}
\right),
\]

where \(\eta\) is the required connection-input accuracy and \(K\) is the spatial grid size. The complete formula retains the coverage, rate, conditioning and integration constants.

Those constants matter greatly. An explicit illustrative two-dimensional parameter choice in the report gives approximately

\[
\boxed{N\approx2.78\times10^{23}\text{ sampling packets}}
\]

before readout averaging.

These are illustrative inputs to the bound, not certified constants of the numerical example. They show that the general construction establishes finite attainability without establishing a useful engineering design.

### Metrology and elapsed time remain paid

Independent, mean-zero electronics noise can be reduced by repeated readings when a noninvasive repeated-reading model is available. Unknown fixed bias cannot be removed that way. ADC resolution, saturation, calibration residuals, timing precision and the probability of jumps during reading bursts all remain in the budget.

The physical duration includes warm-up and gaps between packets:

\[
T\le W+N(\Delta+G_{\rm gap})
+\text{reading-burst allowances}.
\]

It is not merely \(N\Delta\).

The report also retains raw reading counts, computation, storage, bandwidth, calibration, reset and an energy ledger. It supplies no invented joule estimate and no transfer of the earlier quantum fault-tolerance guarantees to this apparatus.

## 8. Sharp failures delimit the conclusion

### Coordinate jumps alone leave physical midpoints ambiguous

With only \(e_1,e_2\) directions, the transformation

\[
F(x_1,x_2)=(x_1+x_1^3,x_2)
\]

preserves the declared source class after transforming the amplitude laws and sensor.

The complete observed process can remain unchanged, while the physical midpoint assigned to observed endpoints \((0,0)\) and \((2,0)\) changes from \((1,0)\) to

\[
(\phi^{-1}(5),0),\qquad \phi(t)=t+t^3,
\]

whose first coordinate exceeds \(1.5\).

Adding arbitrarily small \((1,1)\)-direction jumps rules out this transformation: their transformed tangent direction depends on the state.

### Fixed lattice-sized kicks are insufficient

For integer kicks,

\[
F_i(x_i)=x_i+\varepsilon\sin(2\pi x_i),
\qquad 0<\varepsilon<\frac1{2\pi},
\]

commutes with every integer translation.

Thus even coordinate kicks plus a common integer kick can leave a nonlinear calibration ambiguity. Arbitrarily small perturbations—or a separately earned replacement rigidity condition—are substantive.

### Rare informative directions impose an unavoidable waiting cost

Two sources can be coupled to agree until the first event from an additional informative direction of rate \(\lambda\). For the specified coupled starting campaign,

\[
\|P_T-\widetilde P_T\|_{\rm TV}
\le1-e^{-\lambda T}.
\]

Uniform binary error at most \(\delta\) therefore requires

\[
T\ge
\frac1\lambda\log\frac1{2\delta}.
\]

This reuses the accepted first-event coupling machinery. It is not a new universal acquisition lower bound, and it does not grant a free stationary prehistory under a modified source.
