Twelve fundamental shear directions: algebraic generation and the remaining uniform physical interface

On the unit three-torus put kappa=2pi and B(u,v)=P[(u.grad)v]. Start with all twelve real fields e_i cos(kappa x_j),e_i sin(kappa x_j), i!=j. The algebra below concerns generated spatial directions, not an implemented common-force controller.

For pure divergence-free complex modes a exp(i kappa k.x), b exp(i kappa l.x), their symmetric quadratic interaction at k+l, after dividing by the common i kappa factor, is
 V_(k,l)(a,b)=P_(k+l)[(a.l)b+(b.k)a].
Trigonometric phase combinations isolate either sum or difference frequencies in the real setting. Each individual pure shear has B(F,F)=0; hence the signs of a cross interaction can be generated from the two quadratic expressions B(F+G,F+G) and B(F-G,F-G). No unbounded optimization or frequency search is needed for the finite examples below. Fixed kappa factors have ordinary effective enclosures when rational physical controls are later requested.

First shell to complete second shell. For distinct i,j,l, use k=e_j,l=e_l,a=e_i,b=e_j. Then V=e_i. Thus both sine and cosine at e_j+e_l have their out-of-plane polarization. All sign versions are obtained in the same way.

From k=e1+e2 with a=e3, and l=e3, choose b=e1 and then b=e2. The generated vectors at K=(1,1,1) are P_K(e1+e3) and P_K(e2+e3). Their difference is e1-e2; their sum has nonzero e3 component after projection, so they span the full K-perp plane. Every generated direction has zero self-interaction because it is still a pure mode.

Now combine the available K=(1,1,1), a=e1-e2, with l=-e3, b=e1. The interaction is e1-e2 at K-e3=e1+e2. This supplies the missing in-plane polarization there. Coordinate permutations and phase/sign changes give the complete transverse planes at every signed two-coordinate diagonal.

Unequal-length step. If k,l are nonparallel, both transverse planes are available, and |k|!=|l|, the interactions span (k+l)-perp. One polarization perpendicular to span(k,l) follows by taking a=k cross l and b in l-perp with b.k nonzero. For an independent in-plane one, take a,b in span(k,l), with a.k=b.l=0. The projected in-plane coefficient is a nonzero multiple of |k|^2-|l|^2. This is verified directly in the two-dimensional span by substituting perpendicular vectors and projecting along k+l. Thus the two generated polarizations are independent.

Every fixed Fourier wavevector is obtained by finitely many such steps. The complete diagonal (1,1,0) can be incremented repeatedly by e1 to obtain (N,1,0), then by -e2 to obtain (N,0,0) for every N>=2; throughout, the parent non-unit vector is nonparallel to the unit increment and has unequal length. Sign and coordinate permutations give the axes. Mixed vectors can be constructed by growing their nonzero coordinates from a signed two-coordinate diagonal, adding a third coordinate while another remains nonzero. At every such increment the parent has length at least sqrt(2) and is nonparallel to the chosen unit vector. Finite rational linear algebra computes polarizations.
