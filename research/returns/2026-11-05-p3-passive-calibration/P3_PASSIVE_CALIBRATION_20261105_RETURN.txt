### Detector deadtime and timestamp precision are separate resources

A deletion-only detector with deadtime \(d_0\) can be supported by splitting each arm into \(L\) labelled rails. Comparing the **unclipped** moments first gives the mean-bias bound

\[
(b+1)\frac{Rd_0}{L}\left(1+\frac{d_0}{w}\right).
\]

This avoids multiplying the detector error by the large clipped estimator amplitude. The literal construction uses \(6nL\) counters and pays splitter loss, routing, propagation, recovery, readout, and aggregation.

Timestamp uncertainty has a different cost. For bounded endpoint errors \(\Delta t\), or the stated source-independent jitter model, the normalized moment bias is bounded by

\[
2(b+1)\frac{\Delta t}{w}
\left(1+\frac{2\Delta t}{w}\right)^b.
\]

The report includes the combined deadtime-and-timing bound.

**More counters do not remove a fixed timestamp-jitter floor.** A direct clock implementation pays frequency of order \(1/\Delta t\), sufficient timestamp bits for the full experiment, and an actual detector latency/bandwidth contract meeting that tolerance. A general decoder for a fixed nonflat response filter has not been constructed here.

### Illumination, calibration, memory, and processing

The detected oscillator flux while illuminated is

\[
\frac{3n\kappa r}{4}.
\]

Leaving it on through forgetting pays that flux over the full acquisition period. Gating instead pays actual ramp duration, slew, and ramp photons. Incident illumination divides the detected allocation by the actual oscillator-path efficiency.

Gain and phase errors receive explicit moment-bias budgets. Receiver pilots can calibrate the receiver at their injection plane; they do not certify upstream loss, source axes, or missing interactions.

Records can be streamed using classical counters and running sums. Per-block estimator work is \(O(kM)\); the inverse adds the connected-set computations and linear programs. **Calibration needs no quantum memory.**

The construction combines three operations.

Boundary-conditioned short paths. Static failure sampling places the process at a rare boundary without waiting for a naturally occurring global failure. An inverse-crossing-multiplicity estimator corrects the forced crossing.
