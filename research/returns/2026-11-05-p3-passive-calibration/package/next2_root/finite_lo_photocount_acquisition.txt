PREMISES

static local H, known state-independent vacuum loss of efficiency kappa>0.
Balanced detector arms have equal calibrated efficiencies. Phase, LO

No claim that observed counts certify the completeness of this source model.

1. FINITE LO EXACT MOMENTS
For one isolated mode b, mix with an independent coherent LO a of real
amplitude sqrt(mu). A balanced splitter yields D=N_plus-N_minus=b^dagger a+
a^dagger b. Therefore E_LO[D/sqrt(mu)]=X=b+b^dagger and
E_LO[D^2/mu]=X^2+n/mu. For disjoint modes the LO tensor product gives the
product of these operator moments even for entangled source outputs.
Vacuum loss gives the same original cross moments after dividing each
observed D by sqrt(mu*kappa). No infinite-LO approximation occurs.

Direct window counting does not isolate/store a temporal mode. CW LO has
a coherent displacement only in the matched uniform mode of each window;
its other temporal modes are vacuum. The full window count difference has
the exact second-moment identity E_LO[D^2/mu]=X^2+N/mu, where N is TOTAL
signal photons in that window (not just the matched mode population).
Because X^2<=4n+2 and n<=N, E_LO[D^2/mu]<=2+(4+1/mu)N.

For Weyl ports L^dagger L=r/4 I, disjoint original photon counts are
independent Poisson for every source/reference input: all marked rates are
constant and normalized jump updates are unitary. Vacuum loss thins them.
For s disjoint selected windows of widths w_j,
  Y=product_j D_j/sqrt(mu_j*kappa_j),
  E Y^2 <= product_j {[2+(4+1/mu_j)kappa_j*r*w_j/4]/kappa_j}.
At w_j=1/r and mu_j=1, V_s=[(2+5*kappa/4)/kappa]^s suffices; at kappa=1,
V_s=(13/4)^s. Bound is uniform in H, source state, and references.

2. FINITE COUNTER RANGE
Total counts in both detector arms are Poisson with mean
q_j=mu_j+kappa_j*r*w_j/4. Splitters conserve total number; signal and LO
incident totals are independent Poisson. Distinct window totals are independent.
Return zero if any selected total exceeds K. Then
  |E Y_clipped-E Y|<=sqrt(V*s*Pr(Poisson(q)>K)), q=max_j q_j.
Choose K>=2e q and 2^(-K)<=zeta^2/(64 V s), yielding bias<=zeta/8.
|Y_clipped|<=Ymax=K^s/product_j sqrt(mu_j*kappa_j), and E Y_clipped^2<=V.
A conservative conditional Bernstein/Freedman count is
  N>=128*(V/zeta^2+Ymax/zeta)*log(2M/delta)
per timing group. Constants must align with final inverse-map error budgets.
The antisymmetric statistic (forward minus reverse)/2 has the same V and
Ymax bounds, provided clipping treats the union of its selected modes and
the tail union factor is increased to at most 2s.

3. SHARED ACQUISITION AND PAID BURN-IN
All 3n port outputs may be counted concurrently. One shared front/back
record supplies products for every candidate; O(M) product arithmetic is
paid. A single common timing schedule suffices for adjacent windows.
Statistical dependence among coefficient estimates costs a union log M,
not separate source trials for each coefficient.
The source effect for a normalized product has operator norm<=product sqrt(rw_j),
thus <=1 when rw_j=1: each Jordan insertion has norm<=1 and evolutions are
CPTP; ordered integration volumes sum to full product volume. The difference
of forward and reverse effects divided by two also has norm<=1.
Conditional mean bias after passive waiting B is bounded by
2sqrt(n ln2) exp(-rB/2), using the completed complete forgetting theorem.
Taking B=(2/r)log(16sqrt(n ln2)/zeta) makes this <=zeta/8.
Martingale concentration handles all previous records; whole-record total
variation O(epsilon/N) is unnecessary for statistical calibration.

4. REAL DETECTOR SERVICE AND TIMING
Combined two-arm count intensity is exactly R=mu/w+kappa*r/4. For fixed
deadtime d0, probability of two arrivals within d0 in a window is <=R^2 w d0.
Passive equal splitting into L labelled detector rails reduces same-rail
pair probability to <=R^2 w d0/L. Sum counts from both arms over all rails.
This preserves the ideal statistic, paying hardware, routing and propagation.
For continuous illumination include the preceding deadtime interval too:
the safe bound is R^2*(w+d0)*d0/L, paying a pre-window arrival that makes
the detector temporarily blind. The clipped-estimator bias is at most
2Ymax*(2s)*Rmax^2*(wmax+d0)*d0/L. Choose L for its response-bias budget.
This is polynomial in inverse response accuracy with bounded local detector
response; it is not an optimized architecture or a free detector primitive.

If each window endpoint has actual timing/latency error<=dt, photon counts
in moved strips have total probability <=4s Rmax dt (double if two distinct
timing-error mechanisms are budgeted). Pay 2Ymax times this probability.
Finite timing precision is required; an exact fixed-response detector model
could alternatively enter the response map only after a new inverse proof.
Phase, gain and loss uncertainty receive a uniform scalar response-bias
allowance, with an s-times local effect telescoping bound at small errors.
Unknown drift/correlated backgrounds are not inferred away.

Direct unmixed counts with calibrated efficiency can estimate r, since the
rate is kappa*r/4 independently of H. Poisson confidence pays exposure and
inverse precision. Unknown r and unknown kappa cannot be separated by these
counts alone. Complete Pauli-port axes and pure input model remain premises.

5. SCOPE
This acquires classical scalar response statistics for H calibration. It
does not acquire the original arbitrary quantum payload through a classical
record. Deferred recovery still pays its separate coherent archive and
protected memory. Fixed detection loss is allowed in calibration and does
not weaken the previously proved same-source first-ingress loss converse.

Counter clipping is local to each coefficient's selected windows. Overflow
in an unrelated port does not discard the block. Thus the bias union factor
is 2s while all M coefficient estimates receive simultaneous confidence.
