COMPLETED INDEPENDENT RESULT
Uniform first-failure CDF sensitivity under Dirichlet-form uncertainty

Status: completed mathematical derivation, preserved 2026-11-05.
Authoring context: independent companion to the selected quantum mission.

1. WHAT HAS BEEN COMPLETED

For two finite lazy reversible Markov chains with the same invariant law,
the same failure set, and the deliberately prepared equilibrium-conditioned
safe initial law, a multiplicative comparison of their killed Dirichlet
forms gives the SAME multiplicative comparison of the entire first-failure
CDF, at every integer deadline simultaneously. Noncommuting killed
operators are allowed. The corresponding continuous-time result holds
for reversible generators without a laziness restriction.

In particular, if a simultaneous calibration event supplies

  |E_true(f,f) - E_fit(f,f)| <= rho E_fit(f,f),   0 <= rho < 1,

for every function vanishing on the failure set, then

  (1-rho) F_fit(n) <= F_true(n) <= F_fit(n)/(1-rho)       (1)

for every n >= 1. The continuous-time counterpart holds for every t > 0.
When both true and fitted forms contain the SAME positive backbone E0
and |E_true-E_fit|<=rho E0, the sharper bound is

  F_fit(n)/(1+rho) <= F_true(n) <= (1+rho)F_fit(n).    (1b)

Here rho>=0 is allowed, without a rho<1 condition. This is the governing
bound for the accepted shared-backbone P2 construction. It follows from
the MUTUAL form comparison proved in Section 3.2 below; (1) remains a
valid general bound when only fit-relative control is available.

There is no multiplication by the deadline, mean lifetime, inverse
failure probability, number of queried deadlines, or number of states.
This is a relative bound on failure probability, not a relative bound on
the small surviving tail at very late times.

There is also a separate, proved transfer theorem for a finite physical
implementation which may be nonreversible and history-dependent. If its
initial total-variation error is delta and its conditional per-update
total-variation error is at most v, then

  |F_actual(n) / F_ideal(n) - 1| <= (delta+v)/(g p)      (2)

simultaneously for every n >= 1. Here g is a certified lower bound on the
ideal chain's spectral gap and p is its stationary failure mass. This
second statement explicitly exposes the absolute precision demanded of
the operating device. It does not convert an unmodelled catastrophe into
a harmless error. Proofs, scope, and the physical cost interpretation follow.

2. ABSTRACT OPERATOR THEOREM

Let H be a finite-dimensional real or complex Hilbert space. Let K0,K1
be positive definite self-adjoint operators. In the discrete-time case
also assume K0 <= I and K1 <= I. Let eta >= 0 satisfy the form inequalities

  exp(-eta) K0 <= K1 <= exp(eta) K0.                    (3)

For a fixed nonzero v in H, define

  h_i(n) = <v, [I-(I-K_i)^n] v>,       n=1,2,...,
  h_i(t) = <v, [I-exp(-t K_i)] v>,     t>0.

Discrete-time conclusion, requiring the contraction assumptions:

  exp(-eta) h_0(n) <= h_1(n) <= exp(eta) h_0(n)         (4)

for all integers n>=1. Continuous-time conclusion, with no upper bound
on K0 or K1:

  exp(-eta) h_0(t) <= h_1(t) <= exp(eta) h_0(t)         (5)

for all real t>0. The same statement holds as an operator inequality for
the displayed functions of K, since v is arbitrary.

The constant eta is not merely a first-order estimate. The exponent is
exact. As a uniform constant for the discrete theorem it cannot be
improved: at n=1 and K1=exp(eta)K0 (with sufficiently small K0), the upper
bound is attained. The continuous theorem approaches this equality as
t tends to zero. This does not assert optimal bounds for any particular
chain or deadline.

2.1 Proof: an admissible multiplicative path

Put

  C = K0^(-1/2) K1 K0^(-1/2),
  K_s = K0^(1/2) C^s K0^(1/2),     0<=s<=1.

Every eigenvalue of C is in [exp(-eta),exp(eta)]. Therefore

  -eta K_s <= dK_s/ds <= eta K_s.                      (6)

This follows by applying |log c|<=eta to the spectral calculus for C.
The path is positive definite, joins K0 to K1, and need not itself be a
Markov killed generator. It only has to satisfy the operator properties
used in the proof.

For the discrete case, the scalar weighted arithmetic-geometric
inequality c^s <= (1-s)+s c gives

  K_s <= (1-s) K0+s K1 <= I.                          (7)

Thus P_s=I-K_s is a positive semidefinite contraction throughout the path.

Inequality (6) also gives the following cross-term bound for any x,y:

  |<x, (dK_s/ds) y>|
      <= eta ||K_s^(1/2)x|| ||K_s^(1/2)y||.           (8)

Indeed, K_s^(-1/2)(dK_s/ds)K_s^(-1/2) is self-adjoint with operator norm
at most eta. No commutation between K0 and K1 is assumed.

2.2 Proof: discrete energy identity

Write h_s(n)=<v,[I-P_s^n]v>. Product differentiation gives

  d h_s(n)/ds
    = sum_{u=0}^{n-1}
        <P_s^(n-1-u)v, (dK_s/ds) P_s^u v>.

Let a_u=||K_s^(1/2)P_s^u v||. By (8), Cauchy-Schwarz and the arithmetic-
geometric mean inequality,

  |d h_s(n)/ds|
    <= eta sum_{u=0}^{n-1} a_u a_{n-1-u}
    <= eta sum_{u=0}^{n-1} a_u^2.                     (9)

Because K_s and P_s commute, the last sum is

  <v, K_s sum_{u=0}^{n-1} P_s^(2u) v>.

For each eigenvalue q in [0,1] of P_s,

  (1-q) sum_{u=0}^{n-1} q^(2u)
    = (1-q^(2n))/(1+q)
    <= 1-q^n.                                        (10)

The inequality is equivalent to q^n<=q and is valid for every n>=1.
The endpoint q=1 is interpreted by continuity (it is unnecessary under
the present positive-definiteness hypothesis). Applying (10) spectrally
to (9) yields

  |d h_s(n)/ds| <= eta h_s(n).

Since h_s(n)>0, integration of the logarithmic derivative over s in
[0,1] proves (4). This is one deterministic inequality for every n, so
no union bound over deadlines is involved.

2.3 Proof: continuous energy identity

Write h_s(t)=<v,[I-exp(-tK_s)]v>. Duhamel differentiation gives

  d h_s(t)/ds
    = integral_0^t
        <exp(-(t-u)K_s)v, (dK_s/ds) exp(-uK_s)v> du.

Let a(u)=||K_s^(1/2)exp(-uK_s)v||. Then

  |d h_s(t)/ds|
    <= eta integral_0^t a(u)a(t-u) du
    <= eta integral_0^t a(u)^2 du
    = (eta/2) <v,[I-exp(-2tK_s)]v>
    <= eta <v,[I-exp(-tK_s)]v>.                       (11)

The last step is the scalar inequality 1-exp(-2x)<=2(1-exp(-x).
Again integrate the logarithmic derivative to obtain (5).

3. FIRST-FAILURE INTERPRETATION AND CALIBRATION CONSEQUENCE

3.1 Exact premises

Let X be a finite state space, let pi(x)>0 be a fixed known probability
law, and let B be a nonempty proper subset designated as failure. Put
A=X\B, p=pi(B), a=pi(A)=1-p. The failure rule is supplied, not learned
from a short period with no failures.

For discrete time, P is an irreducible pi-reversible stochastic matrix
whose spectrum in l^2(pi) lies in [0,1]. The usual sufficient condition
is P(x,x)>=1/2 for every x. Let P_A be its principal restriction to A
and let K=I_A-P_A. Then 0<K<=I_A. Start with

  nu(x)=pi(x)/a, x in A,
  tau_B=inf{n>=1 : X_n in B}.

Writing v=1_A as a vector in l^2(A,pi), the survival and failure laws are

  S(n)=Pr_nu(tau_B>n)=<v,P_A^n v>/a,
  F(n)=Pr_nu(tau_B<=n)=<v,[I-P_A^n]v>/a.              (12)

For continuous time, replace P by an irreducible pi-reversible Markov
generator Q, let K=-Q_A, and start from the same nu. Then

  F(t)=<v,[I-exp(-tK)]v>/a.                           (13)

All comparisons in Sections 2-3 require the SAME pi, A, v and a. An
arbitrary deterministic safe initial state does not usually produce
this quadratic form. The theorem makes no corresponding relative CDF
claim for arbitrary fixed starts. Likewise, an uncertainty certificate
for pi itself is not automatically covered: it changes the Hilbert
space weights, initial distribution, and operator representation.

3.2 From unknown local support to the whole curve

The accepted P2 unknown-local-ports theorem supplies, under its declared
product invariant law, reciprocal bounded-reach ports and operating
refresh backbone, a simultaneous local-calibration event on which

  |E_true(f,f)-E_fit(f,f)| <= rho E_backbone(f,f)
                            <= rho E_fit(f,f).       (14)

Its input includes positive upper intervals for unobserved admitted
channels. No common positive support is needed in (14). Apply (14) to
functions zero on B to obtain

  (1-rho) K_fit <= K_true <= (1+rho) K_fit.

For rho<1, take eta=-log(1-rho). Since
1+rho<=1/(1-rho), hypothesis (3) holds. Equations (4)-(5), divided by the
common a, prove (1).

There is a sharper consequence when, as in the accepted operating-
backbone construction, BOTH E_true and E_fit contain E_backbone as a
positive summand. Then

  E_true <= E_fit+rho E_backbone <= (1+rho)E_fit,
  E_fit <= E_true+rho E_backbone <= (1+rho)E_true.

Consequently

  K_fit/(1+rho) <= K_true <= (1+rho)K_fit.

Taking eta=log(1+rho) proves (1b), with no need for rho<1. This sharper
mutual comparison was identified by the coordinator during handoff and
is the recommended constant for the actual shared-backbone premise.

This improves the outcome available from the same
calibration event: it certifies relative uncertainty in every deadline's
failure probability, not only in a mean or integrated risk.

For clocked dynamics both fitted and true endpoint kernels must meet
the discrete contraction premise at the SAME physical tick duration.
A fitted reversible generator with reconstructed rows is not by itself
a stochastic lazy endpoint kernel. Row constraints and a declared actual
null-tick operation can supply that premise. Adding null ticks changes
the physical clock and that resource must be paid. The algebraic
surrogate Q=(P-I)/T preserves the discrete chain's mean hitting time
after multiplying by T, but does NOT preserve its first-failure CDF.
No such substitution is used in this result.

If a computation provides a simultaneous interval [L(n),U(n)] for the
fitted CDF, calibration produces the simultaneous true-model interval

  [(1-rho)L(n), min{1,U(n)/(1-rho)}].                 (15)

Its endpoint ratio is at most the fitted endpoint ratio times
(1-rho)^(-2). Under the stronger shared-backbone premise, replace (15)
by [L(n)/(1+rho), min{1,(1+rho)U(n)}], whose width multiplier is
(1+rho)^2. The confidence level is the probability of the single
calibration/computation event, not a probability allocated separately
to infinitely many deadlines.

3.3 Actual acquisition cost represented by the inherited certificate

For completeness, the accepted local-calibration resource is not an
unpriced complete model. For port alpha with at most k bits, prepare
each local input and execute the actual isolated operating collision.
Infer each reciprocal pair from the lower stationary-weight endpoint.
All writes, readout, ancillary preparation, isolation and service times
are paid. With absolute probability accuracy h, the accepted fixed-shot
count scales as

  O(number_of_ports * 2^k * h^(-2)
       * log(number_of_ports * 4^k / alpha_acq)).

Bias and systematic errors enlarge h. Zero counts retain a positive
upper band. The accepted form radius is

  rho = max_i [2 sum_{alpha containing i} r_alpha / gamma_i],
  r_alpha <= (2^|S_alpha|-1) delta_alpha,

where delta_alpha is an absolute local rate/probability radius in the
same clock units and gamma_i is the operating refresh coefficient.
Consequently small gamma_i, large incidence degree, large k and demanding
rho all have explicit costs. Neither the product invariant law, bounded
port reach, fresh-memory assumption nor active operating backbone is
inferred from silence. The original P2 source cited below governs these
premises and their acquisition, not this file.

4. FINITE IMPLEMENTATION ERRORS: A SEPARATE PROVED TRANSFER

The form theorem requires reversibility and a common invariant law.
The following argument does not pretend that an approximate factory
preserves either property exactly.

4.1 Assumptions and claim

Let the ideal P satisfy the discrete premises above. Let g>0 be a proved
lower bound on its full spectral gap. The actual process may depend on
its entire past. Require that at EVERY admitted history h ending at x,

  TV(Law(actual next state | h), P(x,.)) <= v.         (16)

This is a conditional error contract, not a bound on stationary average
error and not merely a collection of one-bit marginal errors. Let the
actual initial law differ from nu by TV at most delta. Failure is the
same declared state predicate. For every n>=1,

  |F_actual(n)-F(n)|
    <= delta+v E_nu[min(n,tau_B)]
    <= delta+v F(n)/(g p),                            (17)

and since F(n)>=g p, inequality (2) follows. If actual initialization
can put mass directly in B, that counts as an initial failure.

4.2 Proof of the killed gap and the truncated-lifetime inequality

Extend a function f on A by zero on B. Cauchy-Schwarz gives

  |<f,1>_pi|^2 <= a ||f||_pi^2,
  Var_pi(f) >= p ||f||_pi^2.

The full-chain Poincare inequality therefore gives

  <f,Kf>_pi >= g Var_pi(f) >= g p ||f||_pi^2.

Thus the smallest killed eigenvalue satisfies lambda_1(K)>=g p.
Let {lambda_i} be the killed eigenvalues and let

  alpha_i = |<v,e_i>|^2/a,
  alpha_i>=0, sum_i alpha_i=1.

Since P_A is positive semidefinite,

  S(n)=sum_i alpha_i (1-lambda_i)^n,
  F(n)=sum_i alpha_i [1-(1-lambda_i)^n].               (18)

It follows that

  E[min(n,tau_B)]
    =sum_{j=0}^{n-1} S(j)
    =sum_i alpha_i [1-(1-lambda_i)^n]/lambda_i
    <= F(n)/(g p).                                   (19)

Also F(n)>=F(1)=sum_i alpha_i lambda_i>=g p for n>=1.

4.3 Proof by stopped coupling

Couple the initial states so that they disagree with probability at
most delta. While they agree and remain safe, couple the next actual
update to an ideal P update maximally. By (16), conditional discrepancy
probability is at most v at each such step. The ideal marginal remains
the Markov chain P even though the joint construction remembers the
actual history.

If no discrepancy occurs before the ideal failure or deadline n, both
first-failure indicators agree. A union bound over the steps before
that stopping time gives

  Pr(indicators disagree)
    <= delta+v sum_{j=0}^{n-1} Pr_nu(tau_B>j).

This proves the first inequality of (17); (19) proves the second. One
does not use an n v bound and then try to absorb a lifetime factor.

4.4 Precision, necessity, and combination with calibration

If p_L is a certified positive lower bound for p, choosing

  delta <= zeta_init g p_L,
  v     <= zeta_fac  g p_L

gives relative error at most zeta=zeta_init+zeta_fac for every n>=1.
Combining with (15) yields

  [(1-zeta)(1-rho)L(n),
   min{1,(1+zeta)U(n)/(1-rho)}]                       (20)

when zeta<1. Its endpoint ratio is at most

  [U(n)/L(n)] * (1+zeta)/(1-zeta) * (1-rho)^(-2).

With the shared-backbone premise, the sharper combined interval is

  [(1-zeta)L(n)/(1+rho),
   min{1,(1+zeta)(1+rho)U(n)}],

whose width multiplier is [(1+zeta)/(1-zeta)](1+rho)^2.

The order 1/(g p) in the required physical precision is necessary in
general. Consider a two-state heat-bath chain
P=(1-g)I+g Pi with stationary failure mass p. Starting from its single
safe state, F(1)=g p. Adding failure probability v in one update changes
F(1) by v, exactly giving relative change v/(g p). An initialization
failure atom delta likewise has relative effect of order delta/(g p)
when g p is small.

Under the accepted strong verified-write model, an absolute error of
order epsilon g p_L may be obtained with a number of repeated checks
logarithmic in 1/p_L, 1/g and 1/epsilon. That statement relies on its
stable basis, conditionally independent measurement-error bound below
1/2, nonbackacting checks, write success floor, protected storage,
memory removal and padded finite clock. The tests, retries and padded
service time are physical costs. A systematic error floor or an omitted
failure mechanism need not obey this improvement. No short passive
record establishes (16) for all unadmitted mechanisms.

For a genuine continuous-time chain with gap gamma and a coupling
discrepancy intensity at most v, the same stopped-coupling argument,
with exact initialization, gives

  |F_actual(t)-F(t)| <= v integral_0^t S(u)du
                         <= v F(t)/(gamma p).

A nonzero unrestricted initial TV error cannot give a uniform relative
claim as t approaches zero: it may put an atom delta directly in B while
the ideal CDF tends to zero. The clocked theorem intentionally begins
at n=1. A continuous all-positive-time statement needs exact safe
initialization or an appropriate multiplicative density guarantee.

5. STRONGEST PRIOR COMPARISON ACTUALLY ESTABLISHED

The central capability completed here is an all-deadline RELATIVE
sensitivity certificate for the equilibrium-safe first-failure CDF,
derived directly from common-invariant killed-form uncertainty. The
operator proof is elementary once the geometric interpolation and
energy identity are used. No claim of first invention is established
by the literature search performed in this session.

(a) Aldous and Brown's reversible rare-event approximation, and the
Aldous-Fill treatment, already provide exponential approximation from
relaxation-time information. A standard bound is additive, of order
relaxation time divided by mean hitting time. Such an additive bound
does not by itself give relative accuracy when the deadline failure
probability is much smaller than that error. The completed theorem
here compares two uncertain killed operators multiplicatively at
every deadline; it is not a claim to have discovered spectral mixtures
or exponential rare-event approximation.
