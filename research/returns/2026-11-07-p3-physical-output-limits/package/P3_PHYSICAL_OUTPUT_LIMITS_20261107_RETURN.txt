P3 — PHYSICAL OUTPUT UNDER CONTINUING NOISE
An auxiliary-uniform control limit, delivery/yield law, and matched
target-preserving rate scaling
7 November 2026

PROGRAMME

Repeatable many-body physical-state control, with information, initialization,
calibration, reset, storage, noise, native operation and continuing resources
paid. That endpoint is retained. This return resolves a structural question
about its achievable operating regimes; it does not announce completion of
the whole programme or replace its physical output with a decoded output.

RESULT IN ONE PARAGRAPH

A fixed nonzero local noise rate and a fixed bound on control strength per
output site cannot support scalable, high-fidelity, unconditional pure
physical outputs in the class proved here. The statement is uniform over
arbitrary auxiliary systems, arbitrary initial correlations, time-dependent
controllers and compatible measurement feedback. It already holds for a
product target under independent bit flips, and also holds for bounded-degree
graph states under physical on-site phase flips. In the general controller
class the certified fidelity ceiling is O(1/N); it governs restoration from
arbitrary initialization, continuing storage and delivery after positive
latency. Postselection at a scheduled receipt can recover quality only at a
bounded yield. For controllers that preserve the target subspace, a stronger
bound gives a necessary return rate Theta(N lambda/epsilon); established
independent reset/stabilizer pumping matches that scaling under its explicit
ideal-pump interface. General coherent-control optimal scaling is not settled
by that matching result. No physical apparatus or new native primitive was
acquired.

1. THE QUESTION AND WHY IT MATTERS

The accepted sequence already provides complete logical preparation,
physical-output restoration under supplied local dynamics, noise completion
for stated target classes, native feasibility conditions and source-driven
sampling without precise rate identification in an interior native regime.
Those operations are retained, with their calibration anchors, full source
procedures, sector restrictions, residual budgets and holding costs. They are
not re-proved here.

The question selected here is:

  Can an arbitrary controller, including an arbitrarily large auxiliary
  apparatus, turn bounded strength per physical output site and a fixed
  positive local noise rate into a scalable threshold for complete pure-state
  output, restoration and delivery?

“Threshold” here means that a fixed positive noise level is compatible with
a prescribed small global error for arbitrarily large N, while the stated
per-site bounds remain independent of N. This is a stronger output contract
than correct decoded logical information, accurate selected observables,
nonzero entanglement, a useful noisy resource state, or small error density.
The question has a negative answer under the interfaces below. This is not
merely a failure of the previous reset embedding, one history-clock design,
one variational controller or one way of learning a noise rate.

The opening came from combining established fidelity-rate bounds for noisy
control [1,2] with a local return-capacity calculation that survives arbitrary
ancillary dimension. The consequence reaches genuine entangled physical
targets: bounded-degree graph states include the cluster-state resources of
measurement-based computation [4]. Nothing in the result rules out useful
fault-tolerant processing of noisy cluster states; the object bounded here is
overlap with the complete specified pure state on the physical output ports.

The central construction is the locality-to-capacity proof in Section 3.
Its output is a computable finite-N differential inequality. The rest of the
return derives preparation, storage, delivery, yield and resource statements
from that one inequality, and checks its finite algebra and sharpness.

2. ACTUAL MODEL AND RESOURCE CONTRACT

2.1 Output and reference

Let W consist of N qubits. Let A contain every auxiliary, controller, record,
entropy sink included in the model, pre-supplied resource, and any untouched
reference. A can have any finite dimension, growing arbitrarily with N and
with the duration of the finite experiment under consideration. The theorem
does not place a useful size, preparation, computation or reset-cost bound on
A. Granting such resources makes the negative result stronger; it is not a
claim that they can be obtained free of charge for a positive construction.

First take the work target to be |0^N>. Define

  P = |0^N><0^N|_W tensor I_A,       Q = I-P,
  F(t) = Tr[P rho_WA(t)].                                      (1)

F is overlap fidelity with a pure target, without a square root. It concerns
the unconditional physical work output. It is not fidelity of a selected
trajectory, and it does not demand that the auxiliary state be pure.

For any work/reference target |0^N><0^N| tensor rho_R,

  D(rho_WR, |0^N><0^N| tensor rho_R) >= 1-F,                    (2)

where D is half the trace norm. The projective test {P,I-P}, followed if
needed by tracing the reference, proves (2). Thus an upper bound on F is a
necessary obstruction to the complete output/reference contract. It is not
merely a bound on one inconsequential observable.

2.2 Irreducible noise and joint control

The first noise model is a genuinely additive, stable component

  N(rho) = sum_i lambda_i D[X_i](rho),
  D[L](rho) = L rho L^dagger - {L^dagger L,rho}/2,
  Lambda = sum_i lambda_i > 0,      mu = max_i lambda_i.        (3)

All rates have units of inverse time. Independent Markov increments, and
the continued validity of this component while control is applied, are
physical premises. The theorem does not infer them from metadata, a fit to
some populations or a formal splitting with negative dissipative rates.

The joint control can be any well-defined finite-dimensional GKSL process

  K_t = -i[sum_S H_S(t), .] + sum_{S,alpha} D[L_S,alpha(t)],   (4)

where a term touches only work sites in S but may act on arbitrary auxiliaries.
Terms acting on A alone have exactly zero action on P and may be arbitrarily
strong. Arbitrary initial W-A-reference correlations are allowed. No
independence assumption about controller ancillas is used.

The important bounds concern operations that actually touch the work. A
convenient sufficient contract is

  ||H_S(t)|| <= h_S,
  sum_alpha ||L_S,alpha(t)||^2 <= d_S,                        (5)

with supplied, valid upper bounds for every allowed control choice. Define
r_S=d_S and b_S=h_S+d_S/2. Tighter target-frame bounds r_S,b_S can be used
when separately warranted; their precise meaning is in Section 3.

Set

  R = max_i sum_{S contains i} r_S,
  V = max_i sum_{S contains i} b_S,
  A_b = sum_{S nonempty} b_S,       B^2 = A_b V.              (6)

A fixed local native menu, bounded arity and bounded incidence with bounded
rates normally supply constants R,V and A_b=O(N). Bounded arity is not needed
by the proof: even larger-support controls are allowed if they satisfy the
same incidence bounds. A large-support operation does not become a free
instantaneous output swap merely because it has a short classical name.

These are instantaneous strength caps, not only long-time average power or
integrated activity. Short pulses whose peak strength grows with N can escape
these hypotheses while paying increasing bandwidth and peak capacity. This
return does not prove the same impossibility from an average-only budget.

The H/L decomposition is not a representation-independent price of a
Lindbladian. It is a supplied physical decomposition that gives sufficient
bounds. Alternatively, if each local generator K_S has a warranted diamond
norm bound g_S, one may take r_S=b_S=g_S. This follows from the dual operator
norm bound on K_S^dagger(P). The resulting constants can be less sharp but
avoid asserting uniqueness of a Lindblad representation. Computing or
acquiring such a bound is not performed by the certificate emitter.

The controller may use time variation, fresh classical randomness and
measurement records. A precise feedback extension permits

  d rho_c = (N+K_t)(rho_c) dt + dM_t,

with nonanticipating K_t satisfying the same bounds and with a zero-mean
martingale increment. Averaging and concavity of sqrt(F(1-F)) give the same
inequality for unconditional F. This includes appropriate continuous
measurement or jump-record feedback with bounded subsequent physical action.
A zero-time work correction correlated with a noise jump changes the
effective noise operator. It is not an extra bounded term in (4). Likewise,
an ideal instantaneous final reset, swap, nonlocal measurement-and-correction
or output redefinition is not supplied by this model. Such operations must
have their own warranted native law and be charged during their action.

The core deterministic proof requires no feedback assumptions at all: it
already permits arbitrary joint Markovian auxiliary controllers. Unbounded
operators in an infinite-dimensional bath are not covered by a bare appeal
to auxiliary dimension independence. Each finite-horizon model must be
well-defined and satisfy the stated work-coupling bounds.

3. THE CENTRAL PROOF: LOCAL RETURN CAPACITY WITH ARBITRARY AUXILIARIES

3.1 The general projection inequality

For any state rho, positivity and Schatten Cauchy-Schwarz give

  ||P rho Q||_1 <= sqrt(Tr[P rho] Tr[Q rho])
               = sqrt(F(1-F)).                               (7)

Suppose a Hermitian adjoint generator action L^dagger(P) obeys

  P L^dagger(P) P <= -Lambda P,
  Q L^dagger(P) Q <= a Q,
  ||P L^dagger(P) Q|| <= B.                                 (8)

Taking its expectation in rho and using (7) yields

  dF/dt <= -Lambda F + a(1-F) + 2B sqrt(F(1-F)).              (9)

This is the familiar fidelity-rate strategy in a target-subspace form. The
novel work needed for this programme is to earn a and B for an arbitrary
auxiliary controller without replacing physical-output fidelity by fidelity
of a pure joint target, or using a global operator norm proportional to N
where only its square root is warranted.

3.2 Exact noise blocks

Let P_i be the projector onto the work string with its sole error at i,
tensored with I_A. Since X_i P X_i=P_i,

  N^dagger(P) = sum_i lambda_i P_i - Lambda P.               (10)

The P_i are mutually orthogonal. Consequently the P block is -Lambda P,
the Q block is bounded by mu Q, and the off-diagonal block vanishes. This
step retains extensive leakage Lambda and bounded return mu. Bounding the
whole noise generator first would obscure the distinction.

The same algebra works whenever the supplied noise has the adjoint action
-Lambda P plus a positive orthogonal-return operator of norm at most mu and
zero off-diagonal block. Independent excitation and relaxation channels, for
example, give Lambda equal to the sum of excitation rates and mu bounded by
the largest relaxation rate. Additional local noise can be included among
the terms whose return and off-diagonal strengths are bounded. Its adverse
P-block contribution need not be discarded if it is known.

3.3 Support projectors

For a nonempty work support S define

  E_S = (I_S tensor |0_{S^c}><0_{S^c}| - |0^N><0^N|)
        tensor I_A.                                        (11)

E_S projects onto nonzero work errors wholly contained in S. A term confined
to S cannot restore an error outside S to the product target. In particular,

  P L_S Q = P L_S E_S.                                     (12)

For any nonnegative weights w_S,

  ||sum_S w_S E_S|| = max_i sum_{S contains i} w_S.           (13)

Proof: on a computational-basis error set T nonempty, the eigenvalue is
sum_{S contains T} w_S. It is bounded by the incidence at any i in T. A
singleton error at a site with maximum incidence attains the upper bound.
Tensoring by arbitrary I_A changes no eigenvalue or norm. This is the
auxiliary-uniform step that converts locality into a global return bound.

3.4 Dissipative return and coherent transfer

For any GKSL controller,

  P K^dagger(P) P = -sum_alpha P L_alpha^dagger Q L_alpha P
                  <= 0,
  Q K^dagger(P) Q = sum_alpha Q L_alpha^dagger P L_alpha Q.

For terms on S, the latter positive operator is at most d_S E_S. Thus

  Q K^dagger(P) Q <= R Q.                                  (14)

For a local term its off-diagonal block is

  B_S = P K_S^dagger(P) Q
      = -i P H_S Q
        + (1/2) sum_alpha P L_alpha^dagger (P-Q) L_alpha Q.  (15)

It satisfies B_S=B_S E_S and ||B_S||<=h_S+d_S/2=b_S. More
generally, r_S can be any valid bound on the local Q block and b_S any valid
bound on (15), taken in the actual target frame and uniformly over auxiliaries.
For example, a target-preserving lowering pump has b_S=0, a sharper statement
than the generic bound d_S/2.

For any vector v, weighted Cauchy-Schwarz and (13) give

  ||sum_S B_S v||^2
    <= (sum_S b_S) sum_{S:b_S>0} ||B_S v||^2/b_S
    <= A_b sum_S b_S ||E_S v||^2
    <= A_b V ||v||^2.                                     (16)

This bound does not assume that ancillary records, resource states or
controller actions on different sites are independent. It remains valid
when every work coupling accesses the same auxiliary subsystem.

Combining (10), (14) and (16) proves the main theorem:

  F' <= g(F) := -Lambda F + (mu+R)(1-F)
                         + 2 sqrt(A_b V F(1-F)).            (17)

This proof covers complex matrices. The delivered finite rational audits
also include complex entries; their finite sizes are not the scope of the
theorem.

3.5 What is sharp

The instantaneous envelope is attainable with genuine one-site terms. Set
H=j sum_i X_i, add lowering pumps of rate kappa, and use uniform bit-flip
noise lambda. Here R=kappa, B=j sqrt(N). At the pure state

  |phi_f> = sqrt(f)|0^N> + i sqrt(1-f)|W_N>,
  |W_N> = N^(-1/2) sum_i |single error i>,

the derivative equals

  -N lambda f + (lambda+kappa)(1-f)
                     + 2j sqrt(N f(1-f)).                  (18)

Thus neither a constant in place of the sqrt(N) coherent transfer nor a
smaller pointwise fidelity-only envelope follows from these inputs. This
does NOT prove that the same family can maintain a state saturating the
stationary ceiling: its dynamics also creates population and coherences
outside the two-dimensional span used in (18).

4. PREPARATION, STORAGE, LATENCY, ACCEPTANCE AND RESIDUAL ERROR

Write a=mu+R and B=sqrt(A_b V). The unique root in (0,1) of g is

  y_* = (B + sqrt(B^2+Lambda a))/Lambda,
  F_* = y_*^2/(1+y_*^2).                                  (19)

For B=0 this reduces to F_*=a/(Lambda+a). The trivial zero-return,
zero-coupling limit has F_*=0.

4.1 Unconditional restoration and asymptotic control

The function g is concave, g(0)=a and g(1)=-Lambda. It is strictly negative
above its final root. Scalar comparison gives

  F(t) <= max(F(0),F_*),       limsup_{t->infinity} F(t)<=F_*.
                                                               (20)

The second statement holds also for time-varying controllers under uniform
bounds. If F(0)<=F_*, the ceiling cannot be crossed upwards. A compiler that
promises restoration from every work/auxiliary initialization must cover
initial work states of zero fidelity; it therefore cannot deliver a larger
unconditional fidelity at any prescribed time under these same caps.

The theorem does not need to assume an existing stationary state, an
ergodicity gap, or convergence of the controller itself. Stationary output
is just one consequence. A perfectly initialized work state is allowed to
start above the ceiling; the next two bounds quantify its loss of quality.

4.2 Explicit finite-time bound

For any 0<theta<1, Young's inequality yields

  2B sqrt(F(1-F)) <= theta Lambda F
                    + B^2/(theta Lambda)(1-F).

Define

  C_theta = a+B^2/(theta Lambda),
  gamma_theta=(1-theta)Lambda+C_theta,
  f_theta=C_theta/gamma_theta.

Then

  F(t) <= f_theta + (F(0)-f_theta) exp(-gamma_theta t).       (21)

For B=0 use the exact linear expression gamma=Lambda+a and f=a/(Lambda+a).
For an arbitrary initial state, replace F(0) by 1. The finite compiler emits
an exact rational upper bound on the exponential; it does not label an
ordinary binary64 evaluation as a certificate.

When Lambda is Theta(N), a=O(1) and B^2=O(N), theta=1/2 gives
C_theta=O(1), gamma_theta=Theta(N), and

  F(h) <= exp(-Theta(N)h)+O(1/N).                            (22)

Thus any fixed positive delivery or holding delay loses high pure-state
fidelity, even if the register started perfectly and the ancillary resources
were ideal. The constants, rather than big-O notation, are given by (21).

4.3 Maximum uninterrupted high-quality interval

Let f>F_*. Since g is decreasing on [f,1], during an uninterrupted interval
with F>=f it satisfies F'<=g(f)<0. Its duration is at most

  (1-f) / [Lambda f-a(1-f)-2B sqrt(f(1-f))].                (23)

For f=1-epsilon and fixed strength, this is asymptotically
epsilon/[N lambda(1-epsilon)]. It is a maximum continuous quality interval,
not a claim that every noise realization experiences a fault at that time.
The emitter certifies the denominator's positivity by exact squaring and
uses an outward rational square-root bound.

4.4 Readiness events and postselection at a scheduled receipt

Suppose a readiness event occurs at a stopping time and the controller is
then committed to receipt after a fixed h>0. Condition on its complete past.
Assume the same Markov noise law and control bounds remain valid throughout
that interval. The conditional initial state can be completely arbitrary;
using F(0)<=1 in (21) still gives the delivery bound B_del(h).

If a final classical record, or an auxiliary measurement, selects an accepted
subensemble of probability p with conditional fidelity at least f_acc, then

  p f_acc <= F_unconditional(h) <= B_del(h),
  p <= min(1,B_del(h)/f_acc).                               (24)

This uses positivity of the rejected branch. The selector must not secretly
apply an unpriced operation that changes the physical work state at receipt.
Any such operation belongs inside the charged delivery interval and its law.

For repeated non-overlapping trials with the same conditional guarantee,
p_max bounds each trial's success probability even after arbitrary earlier
history. A first-success protocol consequently has expected attempt count
at least 1/p_max. For sequential attempts with interval h, the expected time
spent in these delivery intervals is at least h/p_max. Failed attempts,
readiness acquisition, reset and additional waiting add their actual costs.
Parallel attempts use additional ports and hardware; they do not evade
their per-port rate and holding obligations.

A zero-delay event-time output is a genuinely different contract. A monitored
classical bit-flip trajectory can, for example, hit an exactly known all-zero
configuration at a random time while its fixed-time ensemble has low target
fidelity. Equations (20)-(24) do not assert a universal waiting-time bound for
every stopping-time/postselection protocol with zero delivery latency. A
later conditioning event cannot be silently identified with an unconditional
output or with positive-time storage. This boundary is essential.

4.5 Repeated outputs and a common holding window

If R_out N-qubit registers must all be present in their specified product
or bounded-degree graph targets during a final window, treat their union as
the work register of size N R_out. The same theorem applies if the noise and
per-site bounds hold on every held register. The controller may correlate
all registers and use every earlier record. One may not multiply independent
success bounds without an independence premise; the joint theorem needs none.
An independent pure-output contract is at least as demanding as the resulting
joint pure-target test. Outputs moved into a separate, better protected store
have changed their physical noise and resource contract; that store must be
supplied and costed.

4.6 Unknown residual dynamics

Suppose the actual drift differs from the modeled one by Delta_t and

  |Tr[P Delta_t(rho)]| <= eta

for all allowed joint states. A sufficient condition is
one-half ||Delta_t||_diamond<=eta. Then add +eta to (17), or equivalently use

  Lambda_eff=Lambda-eta,       a_eff=a+eta,                  (25)

when Lambda_eff>0. All preceding comparison formulas then apply. This is a
separate worst-case generator budget. It is not a universal hardware
threshold, and a large total residual budget can make the conclusion weak.
If an additional noise contribution is itself a supplied local GKSL term,
its more informative local blocks can instead be included directly.

5. GENUINELY ENTANGLED OUTPUTS WITHOUT AN ASSUMED PHYSICAL U FRAME

Let G be a supplied simple graph of maximum degree Delta. Define

  U_G = (product_edges CZ) H^tensor N,
  |G> = U_G |0^N>.

Now let the actual physical noise be sum_i lambda_i D[Z_i]. Since CZ gates
commute with Z_i and H Z H=X,

  U_G^dagger Z_i U_G=X_i.                                  (26)

Conjugation is used only as a mathematical representation of the proof. It
does not require executing U_G, its inverse, or a dressed actuator family.
Physical on-site phase flips really do become the independent errors in (3).

A control term originally supported on work set S is supported, in this
representation, on S plus its graph neighbours. Norms are unchanged. With
D=Delta+1, physical incidence bounds R_0,V_0 give

  R <= D R_0,     V <= D V_0,
  A_b unchanged.                                           (27)

In particular A_b<=N V_0, so B^2<=D N V_0^2, not D^2 N V_0^2.
The coefficient accounting for the sum of local strengths is not multiplied
again by the support expansion. More detailed supplied support lists can
give tighter constants; the exact emitter performs that expansion directly.

For uniform lambda and fixed R_0,V_0, put

  C_G = [sqrt(D)V_0 + sqrt(D V_0^2+lambda(lambda+D R_0))]
        /lambda.

Then the stationary/preparation barrier is

  F_* <= C_G^2/(N+C_G^2).                                  (28)

For product targets D=1 and the graph notation may be omitted. This covers
the full bounded-degree graph-state family, including square-lattice cluster
states. An on-site unitary change of target and noise axes is equally
permitted when it represents the actual physical operators.

For a general bounded-depth non-Clifford target, this return does not infer
that ordinary physical on-site noise becomes independent bit flips. A
different warranted transformed noise law or separate block estimate is
needed. Nor does a star-graph description of a GHZ state have bounded
degree independent of N. These restrictions prevent an unsupported extension
to every entangled pure state or every noise channel.

6. RESOURCE SCALING AND THE TARGET-PRESERVING CLASS

6.1 General controllers: a necessary strength increase

For a product target, suppose a common per-site local-generator bound J
supplies R<=J and V<=J. Then A_b<=NJ. To support fidelity 1-epsilon at the
barrier or asymptotically, it is necessary that

  J >= lambda [N(1-epsilon)-epsilon]
             /[epsilon+2 sqrt(N epsilon(1-epsilon))].      (29)

If the numerator is negative the bound is replaced by zero. At large N it
scales as (lambda/2)sqrt[N(1-epsilon)/epsilon]. For a bounded-degree graph
target, the denominator becomes D epsilon +
2 sqrt(D N epsilon(1-epsilon)). More directly, distinct R,V,A_b bounds can
be inserted into (17) without reducing them to a common J.

Equation (29) is a necessary condition. It is not an economical coherent
controller or a claim that sqrt(N/epsilon) is sufficient. The instantaneous
sharpness in Section 3.5 explains why a stronger universal result cannot be
obtained solely by tightening this scalar derivative inequality. Maintaining
the required state trajectory could require more resources.

6.2 Target-preserving controllers

There is a stronger, useful class-wide result. Suppose every allowed
controller preserves the work target subspace when no irreducible noise is
applied, for EVERY auxiliary state. Equivalently, its channels take states
supported on P to states supported on P. This is stronger than existence of
one joint stationary target with one special initialized controller state.

For a target-preserving channel Phi, the effect Phi^dagger(P) is bounded by
I and has P block equal to P. Positivity of I-Phi^dagger(P) forces its
off-diagonal block to vanish. Differentiating the semigroup at zero gives

  P K^dagger(P) P=0,       P K^dagger(P) Q=0,
  K^dagger(P)=Q K^dagger(P) Q >= 0.                         (30)

The local return bound R still applies, so

  F' <= -N lambda F+(lambda+R)(1-F),
  F_* <= (lambda+R)/(N lambda+lambda+R).                    (31)

Thus fidelity 1-epsilon requires

  R >= lambda[N(1-epsilon)/epsilon-1].                     (32)

The same linear conclusion holds whenever the actual controller off-diagonal
block is zero, even if it adds further leakage and is not target-preserving.
That observation is useful for the accepted noisy native menu below.

6.3 Matching scaling by established ideal pumps

As a mathematical comparator, assume native one-site lowering pumps
kappa D[|0><1|_i] in addition to lambda D[X_i]. This is an explicit ideal
pump law, not a primitive supplied by the earlier noisy-actuator family.
Its known independent-reset dynamics has stationary excited population

  p = lambda/(kappa+2lambda),
  F_stationary=(1-p)^N.                                   (33)

The global trace distance from |0^N> is exactly 1-F_stationary. Since
1-(1-p)^N<=Np, kappa=N lambda/epsilon is sufficient for stationary trace
error at most epsilon. Together with (32), this establishes

  optimal necessary/sufficient order within the target-preserving
  return-rate contract: R=Theta(N lambda/epsilon).          (34)

It also matches the leading coefficient in the joint large-N, high-accuracy
regime. It does not identify the exact finite-N optimum over all such
controllers. The exact required independent-pump rate, when positive, is
lambda[1/(1-(1-epsilon)^(1/N))-2].

For graph states the corresponding established stabilizer pump is

  L_i = sqrt(kappa) Z_i (I-K_i)/2,
  K_i = X_i product_{j neighbour i} Z_j.                   (35)

It equals sqrt(kappa) U_G |0><1|_i U_G^dagger, has operator norm
sqrt(kappa) and support at most Delta+1. A supplied physical implementation
of (35) is a substantive native-operation assumption. The proof's use of
U_G in Section 5 did not supply it. Known dissipative state-engineering and
stabilizer-pumping machinery is credited [3]; this is a sharpness and cost
comparison, not a new claim of having built those actuators.

6.4 Initialization, complete error and continuing cost of the comparator

The accepted local noisy-actuator contraction, specialized to the ideal
pump with zero actuator flip fraction, gives complete half-diamond error
at most 2N exp(-kappa t/2) from its full product stationary state. Unitary
conjugation gives the same bound for (35). This is imported at its full
physical-output/reference contract, not re-proved or inferred from a
single initial-state simulation.

For a supplied upper bound lambda on the independent noise rates, choose

  kappa=2N lambda/epsilon,
  m=ceil(log_2(4N/epsilon)),
  t_prep=2m/kappa.                                        (36)

Stationary trace error is at most epsilon/2 and transient complete error
is at most 2N 2^(-m)<=epsilon/2. This supplies an unconditional preparation
comparison from arbitrary initialization under the explicit native law.

Its charged mathematical rates are

  N pumps;
  total peak jump-norm-squared rate N kappa;
  work-site incidence at most (Delta+1) kappa for graph pumps;
  integrated preparation rate N kappa t_prep = 2Nm;
  integrated holding rate over duration h = N kappa h.       (37)

The preparation time shrinks as 1/kappa. Its modest integrated preparation
rate does not make its peak strength, bandwidth, cooling/reset capacity,
ancillary implementation or Markov approximation free. At fixed h and
epsilon, holding activity grows quadratically with N in this comparator.
Logical synthesis of a pump does not establish the corresponding continuous
native law at an arbitrarily large kappa. All physical gate, reset, control,
source-acquisition, calibration and residual-error costs must be added if a
device is proposed. No such physical claim is made in this return.

No universal energy, heat or thermodynamic-work price is inferred from a
Lindblad coefficient. Equations (37) count rate capacity and its time integral;
actual jump counts, entropy export, standby costs and physical work are
different quantities. A supplied implementation and pricing law are needed
to turn those rates into complete physical costs.

The prior warranted actuator law includes extra noise proportional to its
activity and has a positive population floor. Increasing its command rate
does not silently convert it into (35). Its accepted interior mixed-state
operation remains valid and useful, but is a different resource contract.

7. CONSEQUENCES FOR THE ACCEPTED MENU AND COLD MIXED TARGETS

7.1 The existing declared native regime

The earlier source-driven construction used the declared intervals

  0.9<=lambda_i<=1.1,      0.08<=e_i<=0.12,      0<=a_i<=5,
  L_i(a_i)=lambda_i D[X_i]+a_i(D[|0><1|_i]+e_i D[X_i]),    (38)

in its warranted common frame. These were conditional/synthetic model
inputs, not a newly acquired apparatus. No new numerical identification is
needed to apply this return's bound to them.

The controller part has zero off-diagonal target block, return at most
a_i(1+e_i)<=5.6, and nonpositive additional target leakage. Keeping only
the lower irreducible leakage gives

  F' <= -0.9 N F+6.7(1-F),
  F_* <= 67/(9N+67).                                      (39)

This holds for time-dependent or compatible feedback choices within that
menu; independent or identically selected commands are not required. It
does not include additional unbudgeted physical operations. At N=10^6,
the ceiling is about 7.4443890e-6 for the pure target in that common frame.

The accepted source-driven sampler targets a specified faithful mixed state,
not this pure state. Equation (39) leaves its complete mixed-state output,
fresh repeated-output independence and paid source costs intact. It explains
why removing precise rate identification does not by itself remove the
physical port's purity boundary. Extra side information about (38), even
perfect information, cannot violate the rate inequality.

7.2 Targets close to a pure graph state

Let sigma_N be any desired mixed work state with overlap f_sigma with the
pure target used in this theorem. If the actual work state is within trace
distance epsilon of sigma_N, then

  F_actual >= f_sigma-epsilon.                             (40)

Therefore a stationary or restored output below (19) cannot meet this
mixed-state target if f_sigma>F_*+epsilon. This is a consequence for
high-purity mixed targets, without falsely declaring all faithful states
uncontrollable.

For the graph Hamiltonian H_G=-sum_i K_i in units where each term has
coefficient one, the canonical state has

  <G|rho_beta|G> = [1/(1+exp(-2beta))]^N.                   (41)

Maintaining a fixed near-unit value of (41) requires beta of order
(1/2)log N. The independent-pump realization in (33) has
kappa=lambda(exp(2beta)-1), and hence pays order N rate for such a global
purity requirement. At a fixed finite beta the target instead has extensive
thermal excitations and can remain an exactly specified mixed-state target
under bounded rates. The difference is in the requested physical state,
not in whether the word “thermal” appears in its description.

These equations do not extend to arbitrary noncommuting Gibbs preparation,
an inexpensive physical Gibbs oracle, economical Hamiltonian acquisition,
or generic polynomial thermodynamics. All earlier finite-N thermal and
collective-system contracts remain unchanged.

8.2 Delivered certificate emitter

physical_output_bounds.py is a standard-library exact-rational emitter. Its
inputs are finite work supports and warranted return/off-diagonal bounds,
or supplied Hamiltonian/jump norm bounds; noise-rate bounds; an optional
graph; a requested precision; and latency/error specifications. It expands
graph supports, computes incidences, emits (17), brackets (19) by exact sign
comparisons, and certifies exclusions, dwell times and accepted probabilities.

For a proposed f, define u=Lambda f-a(1-f). Strict exclusion is equivalent to

  u>0 and u^2>4 B^2 f(1-f).                               (42)

No square root is needed for this decision. Root bracketing uses rational
bisection. For displayed drift and dwell intervals, integer square roots
give outward dyadic bounds. For x>=0, the emitted finite-time bound uses

  exp(-x) <= 1/[sum_{j=0}^{m_exp} x^j/j!].                 (43)

Equation (43) is an exact upper bound, not a statement that its error is a
particular number of bits. The order is explicit. Binary64 values are used
only in the diagnostic simulation and the plotted display.

Given the support list, the incidence calculation is linear in its expanded
length, plus the output-register size. For a bounded-degree graph expansion
cost is at most a factor of Delta+1. A B_bit-step root bracket needs B_bit
exact comparisons. Bit work is polynomial in the encoded rational input
length and requested bracket length; denominator sizes and integer arithmetic
are real digital costs. Uniform symbolic bounds can be evaluated without
enumerating N terms. The compiler never enumerates a 2^N work Hilbert space.

This economy belongs to the certificate, not to acquisition of its inputs.
Exact native matrices, simultaneous operation laws, support/routing validity,
calibration and sufficiently strong uniform norm bounds need their own
information and physical costs. Computing generic norms from a fully expanded
large controller can be expensive. No hidden efficient physical oracle is
called by the program.

9. WHAT THIS RESOLVES, AND WHAT THE PROGRAMME MUST STILL EARN

The selected full question has a definite negative answer: a bounded-strength
per-site threshold cannot provide universal high-accuracy pure physical
outputs under the stated continuing local noise, even after granting ideal
auxiliary resources and arbitrary compatible controllers. Product targets
already refute such a universal capability; the graph-state extension shows
that the same obstruction directly reaches an extensive family of entangled
outputs. It governs finite restoration and delivery, not only exact
stationarity. In the target-preserving class the necessary resource scaling
is also matched, to order, by an explicit established pumping construction.

Several deductions materially constrain subsequent scientific claims:

  Information is not the whole missing resource. Perfect knowledge of the
  model, unlimited classical computation and pre-supplied auxiliary states
  leave the physical-output bound intact. Source-driven exact sampling can
  remove an identification task without removing local purity leakage.

  Increasing controller size alone is insufficient. The bound is uniform
  in auxiliary dimension, including correlated memories and ideal resources.
  The work-coupling strength, actual noise component, latency or output
  contract has to change to evade its hypotheses.

  Holding is part of preparation. A terminal logical circuit description or
  a perfect state at one instant does not establish a finite-time physical
  handoff. Equation (23) gives a paid delivery deadline; equation (24) gives
  the corresponding scheduled acceptance cost.

  Encoded information and complete pure physical output are different tasks.
  Correctable physical errors can preserve a decoded logical state while
  taking the physical register outside its specified pure target. Nothing
  here contradicts threshold theorems with their own encoded access,
  decoder, locality, noise, reset and output contracts.

  Faithful mixed-state control remains a legitimate full-state capability.
  The accepted construction really prepares its specified mixed state; it
  does not owe fidelity to a different pure state. Conversely, silently
  replacing a requested pure state by a noisy thermal or encoded state would
  change the endpoint and is not counted as solving it.

This return does not prove a fundamental impossibility for every physical
noise model, a no-go for all event-time heralding, or an optimal resource
frontier for arbitrary coherent controllers. It supplies a broad obstruction
and a matched structural subclass, not a claim of P=NP, an economical generic
quantum apparatus or a universal constant hardware threshold. The earlier
five-local compiler, finite-time restoration and source/native constructions
remain accepted at their original contracts.

The unfinished programme therefore retains its actual endpoint. A future
positive claim of scalable near-pure physical delivery must explicitly earn
an escape from these hypotheses: for example a genuinely reduced output-noise
law, appropriate growing native capacity, or a warranted protected physical
delivery interface. Those are operations to construct and cost, not names
for unacquired resources. This structural result closes the bounded-capacity
route within its proven noise class; it does not pretend that the entire
programme has been completed by adopting an easier output.
