PHYSICAL DESIGN CONTRACTS FOR PARALLEL OPTICAL FABRICATION AND NET HEAT CONVERSION
ROOT,12November2026Brisbane. Engineering calculations and scoped primary inputs. No physical experiment or device performance is asserted.

1. Optical attenuation is a finished-material constraint.
For a homogeneous nonmagnetic medium n+i*kappa, a plane wave's intensity ratio over L is exp(-4*pi*kappa*L/lambda). A homogeneous1dB absorption budget over100um at650nm therefore requires kappa<=lambda*ln(10)/(40*pi*L)=1.1910203481790702e-4. This is a diagnostic, not a guided-mode theorem. Actual modes require dispersive modal energy, group velocity and material overlap; roughness, bends, finite confinement, couplers and structural disorder add losses. A final manufactured composite cannot inherit a lossless bulk refractive index merely from its constituents.

If N optical locations are critical and each has marginal bad-event probability at most p, then the probability of any bad event is at most N*p, with no independence assumption. N=500,p=2e-4 permits an upper bound0.1. This neither establishes those marginals nor says every structural defect is fatal. A correlated twin plane can make the relevant event and critical geometry entirely different. Conversely, error-tolerant photonic geometry may avoid demanding atomically perfect placement. Actual optical performance and the actual fabrication distribution must be connected.

The manufactured useful output includes both mode couplers. At a3dB end-to-end budget, independently allocating0.5dB to each port leaves2dB for propagation/bends/disorder. That is a design allocation, not a measured component. A low-loss isolated bend is not an end-to-end route.

2. The complete heat boundary changes the optical target.
At a steady chosen physical boundary, define Pel as gross generated electricity, Qcell as cell heat, Prefl as cell-reflected optical power, r as its energy-weighted fraction actually returned to and absorbed by the hot reservoir before loss, Qother as other net hot-reservoir losses, and Paux as converter auxiliary electricity. Terms must be disjoint; an internal reflected ray cannot be counted twice. Then
 Qhot=Pel+Qcell+(1-r)*Prefl+Qother,
 Pnet=Pel-Paux.
For eta_cell=Pel/(Pel+Qcell), a=Paux/Pel, u=Prefl/Pel and e=Qother/Pel:
 eta_net=(1-a)/(1/eta_cell+(1-r)*u+e).
The necessary scalar return threshold for target eta is
 r>=1-[(1-a)/eta-1/eta_cell-e]/u  (u>0).
If this exceeds1, those inputs cannot meet the target. These identities assume a consistent steady control volume; full spectral/angular transport, illumination-dependent cell performance and thermal feedback determine their actual terms.

With eta_cell=.35 and positive a/e, .35 net is unavailable. High cell efficiency or a high single-bounce reflectance alone cannot close this balance.

For a discretized energy-conserving optical transport model with internal-state transition matrix K>=0 and terminal hot-absorption probabilities h>=0, the eventual hot return vector satisfies r=h+K*r. When rho(K)<1, r=(I-K)^-1*h. Cold absorption/escape terminal probabilities complete each row sum. Specular angle, wavelength and polarization can be separate states. This is a way to compute repeated returns, not a supplied real reflector or an assumption that a finite discretization resolves the optics. Energy conservation and angular/spectral convergence must be checked on the actual architecture.

3. Power density is a cost interface.
For purchased active-area cost cA dollars/cm2, gross output density pA W/cm2 and auxiliary fraction a, cell-only capital cost has floor
 Ccell_per_net_kW=1000*cA/[pA*(1-a)].
At hypothetical cA=1,pA=1,a=.03, the floor is1030.93USD/netkW, already above500 before packaging/cooling. At pA=3.78 it is272.73USD/netkW, but that density cannot be transplanted from a hotter measured cell into1400C. These are conditional thresholds, not price quotations. Wafer reuse, epitaxy, metallization, assembly yield, interconnects and balance of plant determine actual cost.

Likewise, if charging electricity costs2cents/kWh, a35%-efficient converter gives at least5.7142857cents/kWhe input-electricity cost even with perfect charging and storage. Actual thermal retention, startup, cycling, capex, replacements and financing add. Heat-market duties and grid-electricity storage are different useful operations and require separate comparators.

4. Remaining physical operations.
Optical manufacture still needs an actual route encoding, material conversion retaining that route, realistic disorder-to-optical performance, yield and competitive complete process. Net heat conversion still needs a cavity/active material/electrical/cooling/containment design that meets the coupled operating point and tolerances. Neither is constructed by the scalar calculations here. Small prototype designs must discriminate the proposed mechanism and strongest failure mode; simulation or a cost spreadsheet does not establish an operational invention. No physical execution, procurement or new access is performed by this note.
