## 3. Why ordinary whole-history reweighting fails

### 3.1 A direct finite-confidence bound for the mean estimator

Consider the ordinary unbiased estimator
\[
Y=\tau L_\tau,\qquad
\widehat T_N=\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N Y_j
\]
from \(N\) independent proposal histories.

For any \(s>0\),
\[
\mathbb E_Q[Y\mathbf1_{\{\tau\le s\}}]
=
\mathbb E_P[\tau\mathbf1_{\{\tau\le s\}}]
\le s.
\]

If \(\widehat T_N\ge T/2\), then either at least one proposal history lasted longer than \(s\), or the average contribution of the shorter histories reached \(T/2\). A union bound and Markov’s inequality give
\[
Q\!\left(\widehat T_N\ge\frac T2\right)
\le
N\,2^{-\lfloor s/2\rfloor}+\frac{2s}{T}.
\]

Set \(s=T/8\). Then
\[
Q\!\left(\widehat T_N\ge\frac T2\right)
\le
N\,2^{-\lfloor T/16\rfloor}+\frac14.
\]

Therefore, to achieve relative error at most \(1/2\) with success probability at least \(3/4\), this estimator necessarily requires
\[
\boxed{
N\ge \frac12\,2^{\lfloor T/16\rfloor}.
}
\]

This is a success-probability argument. It does not infer a confidence lower bound merely from a large variance.

It also grants exact likelihood weights and exact clocks. Additional numerical precision cannot remove the bound.

### 3.2 The corresponding survival/CDF obstruction

The ordinary weighted survival estimator is
\[
\widehat S_N(t)
=
\frac1N\sum_{j=1}^N
L_t^{(j)}\mathbf1_{\{\tau_j>t\}}.
\]

When none of the proposal histories survives until \(t\), this estimator is zero. But
\[
Q(\text{at least one of \(N\) histories survives to \(t\)})
\le N\,2^{-\lfloor t/2\rfloor}.
\]

If the physical survival probability satisfies \(S_P(t)\ge1/2\), additive error below \(1/4\) with success probability at least \(3/4\) requires
\[
\boxed{
N\ge\frac34\,2^{\lfloor t/2\rfloor}.
}
\]

This also applies to the CDF estimate \(1-\widehat S_N(t)\).

There are relevant times with short binary encodings. The accepted maximality of the all-minus start gives \(m(x)=\mathbb E_x\tau\le T\). Hence

### 3.3 A broader work–second-moment inequality

\boxed{
