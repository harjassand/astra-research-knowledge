# Targeted prior-art scope check, 5 November 2026

## What was checked

The target was not generic perfect sampling or known AKLT spin-correlation decay. It was a uniform all-length contraction/perfect-sampling certificate for the positive routed closed-loop gas of the UNDECORATED square spin-2 AKLT model, with its 3/5 contact weights and arbitrary activity suppression.

Targeted searches for combinations of AKLT, square lattice, routed loops, Dobrushin, strong spatial mixing, loss networks, perfect simulation and perfect sampling did not locate an earlier theorem with this exact scope. This is a limited literature check, not proof of novelty and not permission to advertise a first result.

## Closest model-specific primary sources

Martyn, Kato and Lucia, Physical Review B 102, 035121 (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10327. This supplies the square-AKLT boundary loop representation and numerical boundary evidence. Appendix B uses plaquette-based random sampling and truncation of configuration sectors; it does not give an exact finite-termination Gibbs sampler. The paper itself proposes more selective Monte Carlo sampling as a possible improvement. This is not the strongest practical baseline by itself: modern matched MCMC must still be compared.

Jackson, Nachtergaele and Young, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12184, published 2026. This treats the same edge-simple routed-trail convention and square-lattice contact structure. Its stated square-lattice LTQO theorem requires at least one decoration. It distinguishes known spin-spin correlation decay from the still-open undecorated square ground-state uniqueness question. Our CLASSICAL loop-gas uniqueness result does not resolve that quantum uniqueness question.

Kastoryano, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27993, 2026. Its operator boundary-response theorem and honeycomb implementation are the relevant quantum benchmark. Our closed-loop certificate does not replace its noncommutative response hypothesis.

## Established general algorithms, not new inventions

Fernandez, Ferrari and Garcia, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9806131 and https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9911162, establish the loss-network/ancestor approach. The present work must be described as a model-specific quantitative certificate and constructive loop proposal, with the soft-mark refinement spelled out, not as inventing perfect sampling or ancestor clans.

Feng, Guo and Yin, “Perfect sampling from spatial mixing,” Random Structures & Algorithms 61 (2022), https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.21079, gives general perfect-sampling results from strong spatial mixing. Its bounded-degree spin-system framework does not directly supply our loop certificate or a useful all-length loop-birth oracle. Applying it to a local vertex encoding would require proving the relevant pinning/mixing hypotheses and charging all conversion costs.

## Appropriate claim boundary

A defensible provisional description is: an explicit independently checked all-loop contraction certificate for this square-AKLT classical representation, coupled to a constructive perfect-sampling implementation whose full cost and exposure invariant must be audited separately.

Do not claim a new general sampling paradigm, established practical superiority over tuned MCMC, a proved square-AKLT quantum gap, or a new MBQC universality theorem. A comprehensive citation/priority review would still be needed before a publication-level novelty claim.
