UNRESTRICTED EXACT QUBO: CONTINUATION AFTER THE FIRST SAT TRANSFER
6 November 2026

STATUS

The unrestricted question is unresolved. This continuation does not construct
a uniform deterministic polynomial-bit algorithm for arbitrary signed-integer
QUBO and does not prove a general lower bound against all such algorithms.
The accepted earlier representation, envelope and certificate results keep
their original scopes. Their proofs are not repeated here.

The current work concerns the requested output directly: an exact minimum
and one minimising assignment. It does not assume that a successful algorithm
must represent all energies or generate a probability distribution.

The results obtained here are: (1) an explicit polynomial-coefficient family
with exponentially large coordinate-branching proof trees under a specified
SDP bound; (2) a short integer certificate that directly solves that same
family; (3) a six-bit counterexample to completeness of the resulting
unconditional quadratic certificate language, accompanied by a one-variable
conditional certificate. These are mathematical results at the stated scopes,
not a resolution of unrestricted QUBO. Historical novelty is not claimed.

2. DIRECT BRANCHING ATTEMPT AND AN EXPLICIT FAILURE FAMILY

2.1 Model being tested

Consider a binary coordinate-branching proof tree. A node fixes some input
variables. Its lower bound is the optimum of the first Boolean moment SDP,
strengthened by every two-variable probability inequality. For its remaining
variables, the relaxation has formal means mu_i and moments Y_ij, with

  [1  mu^T; mu  Y] positive semidefinite,   Y_ii=mu_i,
  0<=mu_i<=1,
  Y_ij>=0, mu_i-Y_ij>=0, mu_j-Y_ij>=0,
  1-mu_i-mu_j+Y_ij>=0.

Fixed variables have their corresponding exact moments. The quadratic
objective is evaluated linearly on these moments. These locally feasible
moments are not assumed to arise from an actual joint distribution.

Give the method an assignment that happens to be optimal and its value as
incumbent at the start. Its optimality is not furnished as a proved premise;
it is treated as a feasible incumbent requiring certification. A node may
terminate when the relaxation lower bound, including
integer ceiling, reaches that incumbent; a complete assignment may also be a
leaf. Otherwise it branches on an individual free bit. Arbitrary adaptive
variable selection and node order are allowed. Additional cuts, aggregate
branching, alternative preprocessing certificates, proof-DAG sharing, and
unrestricted terminal rules are not part of this model.

Providing an exact relaxation bound without charging its evaluation makes
this a stronger restricted proof model. It is used solely for a lower bound
on proof-tree size, not as an oracle in a proposed algorithm.

2.2 The family

Let n=2m+1>=3 be odd, and set

  S=sum_{i=1}^n x_i,  W=n(n+1)/2,  A=W+1,
  E_n(x)=A(2S-n)^2+sum_{i=1}^n i*x_i.

The expanded signed-integer coefficients are

  c=A*n^2,
  a_i=4A(1-n)+i,
  b_ij=8A for every i<j.

For n>=3, their magnitudes are at most A*n^2<=n^4. Every coefficient therefore
has O(log n) bits. The complete indexed input has Theta(n^2 log n) bits.
There are no auxiliary variables. Even the gcd of all nonconstant
coefficients is one, since consecutive linear coefficients differ by one.
This last fact is not a hardness assertion; the family remains easy.

The unique optimum and assignment are

  E*=A+m(m+1)/2,
  x*=(1,...,1,0,...,0), with m leading ones and m+1 trailing zeros.

To see this, (2S-n) is odd. Its smallest squared value is one, attained at
S=m or S=m+1. All other cardinalities have squared value at least nine.
Their extra penalty 8A exceeds the entire possible linear-weight variation
W. At a fixed cardinality, selecting the lowest weights is uniquely optimal.
Cardinality m has smaller weight than cardinality m+1. Section 3 gives a
second proof that also supplies a compact certificate.

2.3 A feasible fractional moment assignment at every mandatory node

At a node, let p be the number of fixed ones, q the number of fixed zeros,
f=n-p-q the number of free variables, and r=n/2-p. Whenever p<m and q<m,

  f>=3, r>=3/2, f-r>=3/2.

Give every free variable the same formal mean and every distinct free pair
the same formal second moment:

  mu=r/f,
  nu=r(r-1)/(f(f-1)).

The four probabilities for any pair are

  p11=nu,
  p10=p01=r(f-r)/(f(f-1)),
  p00=(f-r)(f-r-1)/(f(f-1)).

They are all nonnegative and sum to one. The Schur complement of the leading
one in the moment matrix is the covariance matrix with diagonal mu-mu^2 and
off-diagonal nu-mu^2. Its eigenvalue along the all-ones vector is zero; on the
orthogonal subspace it is

  mu-nu=r(f-r)/(f(f-1))>0.

Thus the full moment matrix is positive semidefinite. Extending it with fixed
variables preserves feasibility. The formal mean of S is n/2 and its formal
variance is zero. Hence the relaxed expectation of A(2S-n)^2 is zero.
The remaining linear objective lies between zero and W. Therefore the node
relaxation optimum beta satisfies

  beta<=W=A-1<E*.

Since W is an integer, ceil(beta)<=W as well. The supplied optimal incumbent
cannot prune this node. The relaxation is feasible, and no integral optimum
of this relaxation can attain beta<E*, so those alternative standard
termination conditions do not help either.

The moment numerators and denominators have O(log n) bits. No high precision
or tiny numerical margin is responsible for the failure.

2.4 Counting mandatory branches

The tree must keep branching until at least m ones or at least m zeros have
been fixed. Count the nodes where a root path first reaches p=m or q=m.
Let T(p,q) be the number of such first-boundary descendants. Then

  T(p,q)=T(p+1,q)+T(p,q+1) when p,q<m,
  T(m,q)=T(p,m)=1.

The recurrence is independent of which free variable is selected. Counting
paths with their last step reaching p=m, and then the symmetric q=m case,
gives

  T(0,0)=2*sum_{j=0}^{m-1} binomial(m+j-1,j)
        =2*binomial(2m-1,m)
        =Theta(2^n/sqrt(n)).

Every such frontier node is a distinct node of the proof tree, whether or not
it requires further branching. This is superpolynomial in the complete
input length Theta(n^2 log n). It rules out the attempted universal
polynomial bound for this specified proof-tree method.

It does not rule out a general classical algorithm. The proof model must not
be silently enlarged to include other pruning rules. There is extensive
prior work on restricted branch-and-bound lower bounds; see [2], [3], [4].
The proof above is supplied independently for this QUBO and this SDP bound.

3. AN EXPLICIT POLYNOMIAL INTEGER CERTIFICATE FOR THE SAME FAMILY

Set t=S-m. The following is an exact identity on the Boolean cube:

  4(E_n-E*) = (16A-n)*t(t-1) + n*t(t+1)
            + 2*sum_{i=1}^m (n-2i)*(1-x_i)
            + 2*sum_{i=m+1}^n (2i-n)*x_i.                 (1)

Both t(t-1) and t(t+1) are nonnegative because t is an integer. All displayed
coefficients are positive, and all the literal terms are nonnegative.
Consequently E_n>=E*. At x*, every term is zero. Conversely, equality forces
x_i=1 for i<=m and x_i=0 for i>=m+1, proving uniqueness.

For a short derivation, write H=sum_i i*x_i and H*=m(m+1)/2. The literal sum
without its outer factor two is

  G=sum_{i<=m}(n-2i)(1-x_i)+sum_{i>m}(2i-n)x_i
   =2(H-H*)-n*t.

Also E_n-E*=4A*t(t-1)+(H-H*). Substitution proves (1).

This is a direct minimum certificate. It bypasses the large coordinate
branching tree. It uses O(n) terms, two aggregate affine forms with O(n)
entries, and O(log n)-bit integer coefficients. It is constructible uniformly
from n. No search over real precision is involved.

A sequential coefficient comparison verifies the identity using O(n^2)
small-integer operations. Schoolbook multiplication has cost O(log^2 n) on
these coefficients. A deliberately conservative Turing-bit bound allowing
sequential-table lookup overhead is

  O(L+n^4 log^2(n+2))

for recognition of an input in this particular ordered family, construction
of the displayed assignment and value, and verification of (1). The output
assignment has n bits and the value has O(log n) bits. This family algorithm
is not asserted to recognise or solve arbitrary QUBO.

The elementary integer products in (1) are related to established
hypermetric correlation inequalities; see [5]. They should not be presented
as a new general principle whose discovery resolves integer optimisation.

4. TESTING COMPLETENESS OF UNCONDITIONAL QUADRATIC CERTIFICATES

The positive certificate suggests the following candidate language for a
quadratic g=E-E(x*). Allow finite nonnegative linear combinations of:

  (a) squares of affine forms;
  (b) ell(x)*(ell(x)-1), where ell is an integer-coefficient affine form;
  (c) any function of at most two original bits that is nonnegative on its
      Boolean domain.

Polynomials are identified modulo x_i^2=x_i. Integer shifts are included in
ell, so (ell-r)(ell-r-1) and t(t+1) are covered. Arbitrary real affine-square
coefficients and real nonnegative multipliers can be allowed for the
impossibility statement; imposing finite rational encodings only weakens
this language. Coordinate permutations and complements do not circumvent
the argument. Conditional multipliers, auxiliary extensions and other
certificate languages are not included.

The claim that every nonnegative quadratic has a certificate in this
language is false, even if there is no restriction on length or coefficient
magnitude.

4.1 The explicit six-bit quadratic

For x=(x1,...,x6), define

  F(x)=3*x5+x6+sum_{1<=i<j<=4} x_i*x_j
       -2*x5*(x1+x2+x3+x4)-x6*(x1+x3)+x5*x6.           (2)

This object comes from the established Grishukhin Gr7 cut inequality,
printed explicitly in Avis and Ito [6]. In their inequality, set the seventh
cut bit to zero, use delta_ij=x_i+x_j-2*x_i*x_j, and take minus one half of
the left side. The result is (2). The object is therefore not claimed as a
new facet discovery. The independent algebra and rank witness below make
its use in this certificate test directly checkable.

4.2 A direct nonnegativity proof with one case split

Let S4=x1+x2+x3+x4. When x5=0, set U=x1+x3-x6. Then

  F|_{x5=0} = sum_{1<=i<j<=4, {i,j}!={1,3}} x_i*x_j
              + (1/2)*U(U-1).                         (3)

When x5=1,

  F|_{x5=1} = (1/2)*(S4-2)*(S4-3)
              + x6*(1-x1) + x6*(1-x3).               (4)

Every summand is nonnegative on Boolean inputs. The aggregate factors use
consecutive integers. This proves F>=0. Since F(0,...,0)=0, its exact minimum
is zero and the all-zero vector is a minimizer.

Equations (3) and (4) also show why the certificate obstruction below is
limited: a single split on x5 succeeds. Multiplying (3) by (1-x5), (4) by
x5, and adding yields a conditional certificate; the cubic coefficients
cancel. Such conditional multipliers are outside the proposed unconditional
quadratic language.

4.3 Exact zero-set rank witness

The space of multilinear polynomials of degree at most two on six variables
has dimension 1+6+15=22. Order its features as

  1, x1, x2, x3, x4, x5, x6,
  x1x2, x1x3, x1x4, x1x5, x1x6,
  x2x3, x2x4, x2x5, x2x6,
  x3x4, x3x5, x3x6, x4x5, x4x6, x5x6.

The zero assignments of F, in this bit order, are exactly

  000000 000100 001000 001001 001110 010000 010110
  011010 011110 100000 100001 100110 101001 101010
  101011 101110 101111 110010 110110 111010 111011.

Evaluate the 22 features at these 21 points to obtain a 21-by-22 integer
matrix M. The square minor obtained by deleting the final x5x6 column has
determinant -1 in the listed row order. Thus rank(M)=21. The coefficient
vector of F lies in its nullspace, which consequently consists exactly of
scalar multiples of that vector.

GRISHUKHIN_WITNESS.json contains every entry of M, its feature order, its
coefficient vector and the determinant witness. The audit uses exact rational
Gaussian elimination. For this fixed finite object, an exact nonzero
determinant is a complete rank certificate, not an empirical extrapolation.
The analytic nonnegativity proof is (3)-(4).

4.4 Why no allowed unconditional decomposition exists

Suppose F were a sum of nonnegative allowed generators with nonnegative
multipliers. Every nonzero weighted summand must vanish at all 21 zeros of
F, because their sum is zero there and no summand is negative. The rank
witness forces each such degree-two summand to be a scalar multiple of F.
Since it is nonnegative and nonzero, that scalar is positive.

A square of an affine form and an integer-affine consecutive product both
have off-diagonal coefficients of the form 2*v_i*v_j, up to an overall
nonzero multiplier. But in F,

  b12=1, b16=-1, b26=0.

The first two nonzero products force v1,v2,v6 all to be nonzero, which forces
the third product to be nonzero. Thus no such generator is a positive
multiple of F. A function of at most two bits also cannot be a nonzero
multiple of F, which depends on more than two variables. Boolean identities
vanish identically on the cube and cannot change this conclusion.

No nonzero allowed summand exists, giving the contradiction. This rules out
every finite conic decomposition in the specified language, not merely the
ones tried by a search procedure. It does not rule out other languages,
conditional certificates or any unrestricted algorithm.

5. THE GENERAL CONSTRUCTION OBLIGATION THAT REMAINS

An optimum-only certificate route must do more than give certificates for
selected instances. For every input it must construct an actual minimizing
assignment and enough evidence of its optimality within one fixed polynomial
in complete input length. A compact certificate is useful only when its
uniform construction and verification costs are both accounted for.

The branch construction is sound, but the family in Section 2 disproves its
proposed universal polynomial tree bound. The direct certificate in Section
3 avoids that obstruction, but Section 4 disproves completeness of the
unconditional generator cone. A conditional proof tree with the same leaf
generators is complete if all variables are eventually fixed: each leaf is
then a constant. Its evident general construction can still have 2^n leaves.
No polynomial upper bound on the necessary total tree size, encoded leaf
coefficients, or search cost has been established here.

These are separate obligations. A polynomial-size certificate existence
theorem by itself does not provide a polynomial-time method to find one.
Moreover, a sound, complete, polynomially bounded and polynomial-time
verifiable system for arbitrary quadratic nonnegativity would imply
NP=coNP, using the accepted NP-completeness machinery for the complementary
threshold problem. This is a conditional consequence, not an impossibility
proof. A uniform polynomial-time procedure that constructs such certificates
whenever valid, with a sound verifier and a known polynomial timeout, would
already decide the nonnegativity question in P and imply P=NP. Therefore
certificate generation cannot be hidden in preprocessing or advice.

The requested output itself has no exponential-size obstruction. If
B=|c|+sum_i |a_i|+sum_{i<j}|b_ij|, then every energy lies in [-B,B]. The binary
length of B is polynomial in L; the assignment has n<=L bits. Expensive full
representations and slow samplers are therefore not requirements imposed on
an arbitrary optimum-returning algorithm.

The unrestricted target remains exactly the user's target. This continuation
has not proved that such a polynomial algorithm exists, and has not proved
that it cannot exist. The current authoritative Clay listing still marks
P versus NP as unsolved [1]. That status is context, not a proof that further
research must fail.

establish an asymptotic runtime bound. The fixed six-bit determinant, in
contrast, is an exact finite witness for the finite rank statement.
