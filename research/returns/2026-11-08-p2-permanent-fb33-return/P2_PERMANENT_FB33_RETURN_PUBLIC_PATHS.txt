1. Exact endpoint ambiguity despite knowing every smaller matching coefficient

For every even $n\ge4$, let $P_n$ be the cyclic permutation matrix and define

$$A_n=I_n+P_n,
\qquad
B_n=(I_{n/2}+P_{n/2})\oplus(I_{n/2}+P_{n/2}).$$

These are explicit zero–one matrices. Their bipartite graphs are respectively one cycle $C_{2n}$, and two disjoint cycles $C_n$.

They satisfy

$$\operatorname{per}(A_n)=2,
\qquad
\operatorname{per}(B_n)=4.$$

Write

$$Z_A(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}m_k(A)z^k,$$

where $m_k(A)$ is the number of size-$k$ matchings in the corresponding bipartite graph. Then the stronger identity

$$\boxed{Z_{B_n}(z)=Z_{A_n}(z)+2z^n}$$

holds exactly.

Consequently,

$$\boxed{m_k(A_n)=m_k(B_n)\quad\text{for every }0\le k<n.}$$

This includes the entire collection of subtop matching coefficients, not merely a fixed-order Taylor expansion. In particular, both near-perfect counts are exactly

$$m_{n-1}(A_n)=m_{n-1}(B_n)=n^2.$$

Proof

For a cycle of length $L$, its matching partition function is

$$Z_{C_L}(z)=\lambda_+^L+\lambda_-^L,
\qquad
\lambda_\pm=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4z}}2.$$

This follows from the two-state transfer matrix

$$\begin{pmatrix}1&z\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

Because $\lambda_+\lambda_-=-z$ and $n$ is even,

$$\begin{aligned}
Z_{B_n}(z)
&=(\lambda_+^n+\lambda_-^n)^2\\
&=\lambda_+^{2n}+\lambda_-^{2n}+2(\lambda_+\lambda_-)^n\\
&=Z_{A_n}(z)+2z^n.
\end{aligned}$$

The same construction remains doubly stochastic after replacing both matrices by half their values. This simply replaces $z$ by $z/2$, and the permanent ratio remains exactly two.

2. Quantitative conditioning of bounded-fugacity scalar records

Set

$$t(z)=\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\frac1{2\sqrt z}\right).$$

Then

$$Z_{A_n}(z)=2z^n\cosh(2nt(z)),$$

and therefore

$$\boxed{
\frac{Z_{B_n}(z)-Z_{A_n}(z)}{Z_{A_n}(z)}
=
\operatorname{sech}(2nt(z)).
}$$

For every fixed upper fugacity bound $\Lambda>0$, define

$$d_n(\Lambda)=
\operatorname{sech}\!\left(
2n\operatorname{arsinh}\frac1{2\sqrt\Lambda}
\right).$$

For all $0<z\le\Lambda$,

$$1\le\frac{Z_{B_n}(z)}{Z_{A_n}(z)}
\le1+d_n(\Lambda).$$

At fixed $\Lambda$,

$$d_n(\Lambda)=\exp(-\Theta_\Lambda(n)).$$

This gives a precise enclosure statement. If a partition-function instrument is required only to return a certified multiplicative interval of width $1+\eta$, with

$$\eta\ge d_n(\Lambda),$$

then for every rational query $0<z\le\Lambda$, the same rational interval

$$[\,Z_{A_n}(z),Z_{B_n}(z)\,]$$

is a valid answer for both matrices. This remains true for an adaptive sequence of such queries: the common transcript can be supplied at every step.

By contrast, any single interval containing both permanents $2$ and $4$ has multiplicative width at least two. It therefore cannot provide the desired permanent enclosure for any $\epsilon<1$.

Precise scope

This is an obstruction to recovering the endpoint from:

- every strictly lower matching coefficient; or

- the indicated bounded-fugacity, limited-relative-precision scalar records.

It is not an obstruction to arbitrary algorithms given the explicit matrix. These particular matrices are easy: their cycle components can be identified after reading the input, and the permanent computed directly. The construction does not rule out richer matrix-dependent processing, high-fugacity evaluation, or sufficiently precise partition evaluation.

The information precision needed here is only $O(n)$ binary digits at fixed fugacity. Thus even the exponential smallness of the relative distinction does not itself establish an exponential bit-work lower bound. It becomes costly for an evaluator whose certified work depends polynomially on the inverse relative tolerance; an algorithm with a different dependence is not excluded by this example.

3. High-fugacity endpoint extraction has an explicit cost threshold on this family

Suppose one tries the positive, cancellation-free upper approximation

$$\widehat p(z)=\frac{Z_{A_n}(z)}{z^n}.$$

Since $\operatorname{per}(A_n)=2$,

$$\frac{\widehat p(z)}{\operatorname{per}(A_n)}
=
\cosh\!\left(
2n\operatorname{arsinh}\frac1{2\sqrt z}
\right).$$

Thus its relative error is at most $\epsilon$ exactly when

$$\boxed{
z\ge
\frac{1}{
4\sinh^2\!\left(
\operatorname{arcosh}(1+\epsilon)/(2n)
\right)
}.
}$$

This threshold is $\Theta(n^2/\epsilon)$ for $0<\epsilon\le1$, with leading form $n^2/(2\epsilon)$ in the small-$\epsilon$ regime.

Accordingly, this simplest positive endpoint extraction cannot remain in a fixed-fugacity regime, even on cycles. That is compatible with the accepted KLM tradeoff reported by the other agent; it does not establish optimality of that tradeoff or a lower bound for other extraction mechanisms.

Representation and exact arithmetic

For a rational query $z=p/q$, both endpoints of the common record are rational, with bit lengths

$$O\bigl(n(\operatorname{bits}(p)+\operatorname{bits}(q)+1)\bigr).$$

The transfer recurrence constructs them using polynomially many bit operations. There is no hidden exact-real oracle in the family.

When matrices are supplied as full dense arrays, reading them costs $\Omega(n^2)$ bits; this is paid. The family’s simplicity does not depend on treating a sparse description as the user’s complete input.

4. Doubly stochastic normalization does not guarantee benign near-perfect ratios

I also investigated whether pruning edges outside every perfect matching, followed by matrix scaling, could force a polynomial bound on

$$\frac{m_{n-1}(A)}{\operatorname{per}(A)}.$$

Such a bound would be a useful root-gap or endpoint-susceptibility input. It cannot be assumed.

Ian Wanless’s primary abstract for “The Holens–Doković conjecture on permanents fails” explicitly states that the proposed polynomial bound on the ratio of near-perfect to perfect matchings fails even for regular bipartite graphs. Scaling a regular biadjacency matrix by its degree gives a doubly stochastic matrix, so double stochasticity alone does not provide the desired polynomial control.

Final central-opening check

I found no completed route through real-rootedness, Stieltjes approximation, or rapidly mixing chains. The missing operation can be stated precisely, and none of the statements below is a general impossibility theorem.

1. Real-rooted matching polynomials do not stably determine the permanent from their lower coefficients

Let

$$Z_G(z)=\sum_j m_j(G)z^j$$

be the matching generating polynomial. For an even cycle,

$$Z_{C_\ell}(z)=\alpha^\ell+\beta^\ell,
\qquad
\alpha,\beta=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1+4z}}2.$$

For $k\ge2$, compare the bipartite graphs

$$G=C_{4k},
\qquad
H=C_{2k}\sqcup C_{2k}.$$

They have the same number of vertices and degree two, and

$$Z_H(z)=Z_G(z)+2z^{2k}.$$

Consequently every coefficient below the leading coefficient agrees exactly, while the numbers of perfect matchings are respectively $2$ and $4$.

For fixed $z>0$, their relative partition-function difference is

$$\frac{Z_H(z)-Z_G(z)}{Z_G(z)}
=\frac{2t^{2k}}{1+t^{4k}},
\qquad
t=\frac{\sqrt{1+4z}-1}{\sqrt{1+4z}+1}\in(0,1).$$

It is exponentially small in $k$. Constant-relative distinguishability through these evaluations requires $z=\Omega(k^2)$, or exponentially fine accuracy at bounded $z$.

Thus real-rootedness and low-order information—even all lower coefficients—do not themselves provide stable recovery of the leading coefficient. These graphs are easy to count directly; the example proves no general counting lower bound.

2. A precise sufficient partition-function operation remains unconstructed

Let $A$ be the supplied matrix, and let $Z_A(z)$ count all partial bipartite matchings with edge weights from $A$. Its leading coefficient is $\operatorname{per}(A)$.

After the exact zero test, use the common-denominator height argument from the earlier report. Each coefficient-to-leading-coefficient ratio satisfies the elementary bound

$$\frac{m_j(A)}{m_n(A)}
\le 2^{B_A} n^{2n}
=:C.$$

Choose an explicitly represented integer fugacity

$$z\ge \max\{2,\; 8C/\epsilon\}.$$

Its bit length is only

$$O(B_A+n\log n+\log \epsilon^{-1}).$$

Then

$$1\le
\frac{Z_A(z)}{z^n\operatorname{per}(A)}
=1+\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac{m_{n-r}(A)}{m_n(A)}z^{-r}
\le1+\frac{C}{z-1}
\le1+\epsilon/4.$$

Therefore a deterministic relative evaluator for $Z_A(z)$, with bit work polynomial in $n,B_A,\log z,\epsilon^{-1}$, would immediately give the requested permanent FPTAS.

Specifically, if it returns

$$L_Z\le Z_A(z)\le U_Z\le(1+\epsilon/4)L_Z,$$

then

$$L=\frac{L_Z}{z^n(1+\epsilon/4)},
\qquad
U=\frac{U_Z}{z^n}$$

satisfy the requested enclosure, since

$$\frac UL\le(1+\epsilon/4)^2\le1+\epsilon.$$

All scaling and endpoint bit lengths are polynomial.

The missing premise is precisely this uniform large-fugacity deterministic relative evaluation with polynomial dependence on the fugacity’s bit length. A bounded-activity evaluator, or one polynomial in the numerical fugacity, does not supply it.

Rational/Stieltjes approximations can reduce the number of transform queries needed to recover a logarithmic coefficient. They do not construct those high-fugacity transform evaluations. Treating $Z'/Z$ or its Stieltjes transform as an oracle would leave the central operation unpaid.

3. Rapid mixing does not, by itself, construct a deterministic evaluator

Consider the chain on $\{0,1\}^r$ with

$$P(x,y)=2^{-r}.$$

It mixes exactly in one step. For a supplied Boolean circuit $f$, the stationary expectation is

$$2^{-r}\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^r} f(y).$$

A deterministic polynomial-time constant-additive evaluator for arbitrary such $f$ would solve circuit acceptance-probability approximation, including its usual promise-BPP threshold problem.

This is a direct demonstration that a mixing-time or spectral-gap theorem alone does not give deterministic expectation evaluation. It is not a reduction from arbitrary circuits to the permanent.

For the actual permanent chain, one still needs either:

- a proved deterministic integration/PRG operation for its specific estimator, or

- a special algebraic evaluator that avoids enumeration of its exponential state space.

Neither operation has been constructed here.

Result of this final check: the real-rooted and mixing representations identify legitimate sufficient operations, but do not currently earn them at the required uniform bit cost. The unrestricted deterministic FPTAS remains unresolved in this branch.
