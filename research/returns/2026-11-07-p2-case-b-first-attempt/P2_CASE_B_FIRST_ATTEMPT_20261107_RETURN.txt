## 2. Why there is a local prediction horizon

The construction combines entropy production with spatial locality. The entropy and locality ingredients are classical primary machinery; their composition here provides the explicit predictor.

### 2.1 Entropy contraction survives the Hamiltonian

Consider any finite restriction containing \(m\) qubits, with any retained Hamiltonian. Let
\[
\omega_m=I/2^m.
\]
For the replacement-depolarizing convention in the question,
\[
\mathcal L=-i[H,\cdot]+\gamma\sum_i(E_i-\mathrm{id}),
\]
one has the conservative bound
\[
D(\rho_t\Vert\omega_m)
\le e^{-\gamma t}D(\rho_0\Vert\omega_m)
\le m\log 2\,e^{-\gamma t}.
\tag{2}
\]

The entropy-production input is the quantum Shearer inequality used by Müller-Hermes–França–Wolf in their treatment of tensor products of depolarizing channels. Their corrected argument, rather than the earlier erroneous proof discussed in that paper, is the governing source. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.07021v1?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The extension to the present Hamiltonian is direct. Hamiltonian evolution contributes zero to the entropy derivative. Concavity of entropy gives
\[
\frac{d}{dt}S(\rho_t)
\ge
\gamma\sum_i
\left[\log2+S((\rho_t)_{-i})-S(\rho_t)\right].
\]
Using
\[
\sum_iS((\rho_t)_{-i})\ge(m-1)S(\rho_t)
\]
makes the right side at least
\[
\gamma\bigl(m\log2-S(\rho_t)\bigr).
\]
Integration proves (2). Regularization covers initially rank-deficient states.

Pinsker’s inequality then yields
\[
\|\rho_t-\omega_m\|_1
\le \sqrt{2m\log2}\,e^{-\gamma t/2}.
\tag{3}
\]

This estimate holds for every initial state. Applying it to the whole \(n\)-qubit system would introduce a \(\log n\) relaxation time. For the fixed-support prediction task, locality removes that dependence.
