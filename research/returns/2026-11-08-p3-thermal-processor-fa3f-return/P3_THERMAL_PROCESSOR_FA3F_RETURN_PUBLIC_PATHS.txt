2. An exact reduction for the actual HOLD channel

The useful object is the decoded logical channel, rather than closeness of the entire physical state to a chosen encoding.

Consider a frustration-free commuting stabilizer Hamiltonian
$H=-\sum_a J_aS_a$
on $N$ qubits. Fix a ground-code isometry $V$, independent of circuit gate choices. For every valid syndrome $s$, fix a Pauli representative $F_s$ and write
$V_s=F_sV.$
The associated mathematical decoder is
$\mathcal D(\rho)
 =\sum_s V_s^\dagger\Pi_s\rho\Pi_sV_s.$
For a one-site Pauli $P_i$, let $u_i$ be its syndrome increment. There is a logical Pauli $\ell_i(s)$, up to phase, such that
$P_iV_s=V_{s+u_i}\ell_i(s).$
A physical jump therefore has two effects: it moves the syndrome and may change the decoded logical information.

For the allowed Davies model with separate Pauli couplings, denote the transition rate by $q_i(s)$. Define the logical-error hazard
$h(s)=\sum_i q_i(s)\,
   \mathbf 1\!\left[\ell_i(s)\not\sim I\right].$

Why this remains valid with coherent faults and arbitrary references

Let the initial physical state be entangled with an untouched reference $R$. In the decoder frame, write its diagonal syndrome blocks as $\rho_s^{LR}$.

Under this Pauli-Davies evolution, off-diagonal syndrome blocks never feed diagonal syndrome blocks. Consequently, ignoring those coherences when calculating a subsequently decoded output is exact. This does not introduce a physical syndrome measurement or a noiseless twirl.

The masses
$\mu_t(s)=\operatorname{Tr}\rho_s^{LR}(t)$
obey a closed classical syndrome Markov chain, even when the logical system, syndrome and reference are correlated. The decoded state satisfies
$\frac{d\rho_{LR}}{dt}
 =
 \sum_{s,i}q_i(s)
 \left[
 \ell_i(s)\rho_s^{LR}\ell_i(s)^\dagger-\rho_s^{LR}
 \right].$
Since each block is positive,
$\left\|
(\mathcal D\Phi_t\otimes\mathrm{id}_R)(\rho)
-(\mathcal D\otimes\mathrm{id}_R)(\rho)
\right\|_1
\le
2\int_0^t\sum_s\mu_u(s)h(s)\,du. \tag{1}$
Now let $\mathcal W$ be the actual noisy writer, including its encoded-input interface. Set
$\delta_{\mathrm W}
=\|\mathcal D\mathcal W-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond.$
Equation (1) gives
$\boxed{
\|\mathcal D\Phi_t\mathcal W-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond
\le
\delta_{\mathrm W}
+
2\sup_{\rho_L}
\int_0^t\sum_s\mu_u^\rho(s)h(s)\,du .
} \tag{2}$
The reference is already included in this statement. No independence between logical information and syndrome is assumed.

If $B=\{s:h(s)>0\}$, then
$h(s)\le 3N\gamma_{\max}\mathbf 1_B(s).$
Thus a bound of the form
$\sup_{\rho_L,u}\mu_u^\rho(B)
\le A e^{-cN^\alpha} \tag{3}$
would establish
$\|\mathcal D\Phi_t\mathcal W-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond
\le
\delta_{\mathrm W}
+6N\gamma_{\max}A\,t\,e^{-cN^\alpha}. \tag{4}$
Equation (2) is established; equation (3) is not established for the physical writer. An adequate integrated bound in (2) would also suffice. This identifies the missing dynamical quantity without demanding global Gibbs domination.

A static Gibbs bound without independent checks

Let $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m$ be the valid syndrome space and
$\pi(s)=\frac{1}{Z}\mathbf 1_{s\in C}\prod_j r_j^{s_j},
\qquad 0\le r_j\le1.$
Then, for every subset $A$ of checks,
$\boxed{
\Pr_\pi[A\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(s)]
\le \prod_{j\in A}r_j .
} \tag{5}$
The subset $A$ need not itself be a valid syndrome.

The proof handles dependent and redundant checks. Fixing the coordinates in $A$ to one leaves an affine coset of the corresponding zero-coordinate subspace. The product weight has nonnegative Walsh coefficients:
$\widehat f(u)
=\prod_j\bigl(1+(-1)^{u_j}r_j\bigr)\ge0.$
Its sum over an affine coset cannot exceed its sum over the zero coset. That zero-coset contribution is included in the partition function, giving (5).


An exact component representation

The next step is more than applying a generic sampling theorem.

I enlarged the decoder-history graph so that the complete variable scope of every finite local rule forms a clique. This includes intermediate cleanup, copying and equality constraints, cochain equations, marking rules and the entire fixed-size bottom decoder. Lookup ties are fixed deterministically, with zero input producing zero correction.

Each rule then encounters active variables from at most one connected component. Therefore:


- removing an active component preserves every local rule;
- combining nonadjacent valid components preserves every local rule;
- retained cochain equations prove that each isolated component has a valid top syndrome;
- different components have disjoint top-syndrome supports.

This proves exact removal and recombination even though the local correction lookups can be nonlinear.

For a component $\gamma$, let $K_\gamma$ be its history support and $\tau_\gamma$ its top syndrome. The alternating contraction of the decoder levels and their fixed local fanout give a computable constant $a_0$ such that
$|K_\gamma|\le a_0|\tau_\gamma|.$
Consequently, for uniform violation energy, the syndrome distribution is exactly a hard-core polymer gas:

\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}z_\gamma,
