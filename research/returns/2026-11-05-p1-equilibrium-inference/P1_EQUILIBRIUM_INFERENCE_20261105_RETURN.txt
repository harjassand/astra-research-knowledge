## 1. The question I chose

**Can we determine a physical system’s eventual response from brief experiments—and precisely what must replace the waiting?**

A useful affirmative answer could change how slowly relaxing systems are characterized. A useful negative answer must withstand active experimental design, feedback, nonlinear inference, and unlimited computation. Merely showing that one extrapolation algorithm is unstable would not settle the question.

I chose a structured physical class in which both directions can be established. Consider an unknown reciprocal passive relaxation system

\[
\dot x=-Ax+bu,\qquad y=b^\top x,
\]

with

\[
A=A^\top,\qquad
\mu I\preceq A\preceq\Lambda I,\qquad
\|b\|_2=1.
\]

The hidden dimension, eigenvalues, and observable mode weights are unknown. The positive lower rate \(\mu\), upper rate \(\Lambda\), and coupling normalization are supplied information.

Its impulse response has the representation

\[
k_\nu(t)=\int_\mu^\Lambda e^{-\lambda t}\,d\nu(\lambda),
\]

where \(\nu\) is a probability measure. This representation is consequential: physical positivity is retained, while the unknown system becomes an unknown distribution of relaxation rates.

The target is one dimensionless static response,

\[
S(\nu)
=\mu b^\top A^{-1}b
=\int_\mu^\Lambda \frac{\mu}{\lambda}\,d\nu(\lambda).
\]

It is the eventual response to a sustained input, with a normalization that places it in \([1/\kappa,1]\), where \(\kappa=\Lambda/\mu\). Recovering the entire hidden system is unnecessary for this target.

Each trial begins at zero, lasts at most \(T\), and supplies the noisy scalar observation

\[
dY_i(t)=(k_\nu*u_i)(t)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_i(t).
\]

The controller may choose inputs using all previous observations, including feedback within a trial. Computation and classical memory are unrestricted. The resource optimized is total **squared-drive effort**,

\[
E=\sum_i\int u_i(t)^2\,dt,
\qquad
\mathcal B=\frac{E}{\sigma^2\mu^2},
\]

with an almost-sure effort cap.

Two distinctions matter throughout:

- Squared-drive effort is not automatically thermodynamic work. The latter is the port integral \(\int uy\,dt\).
- \(T\) is the maximum age of an observed transient. It is not total laboratory time after repetitions, preparation, and resets.

The theorem concerns this declared interface. It does not cover every system that could be described as passive.

## 2. The central result

Let \(\mathcal B_\star\) be the minimum worst-case normalized effort needed to estimate \(S\) to absolute error \(\varepsilon\), with success probability at least \(3/4\), uniformly over the model class.

Define

\[
s=\mu T,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\Lambda}{\mu},\qquad
L=\log\frac1\varepsilon.
\]

For

\[
0<s\leq\frac1{64},\qquad
16\leq\kappa\leq\infty,\qquad
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac1{16},
\]

the derived law is

\[
\boxed{
\log(1+\mathcal B_\star)
=
\Theta\!\left[
\sqrt{\kappa}\,L
\log\!\left(1+\frac{L}{s\sqrt{\kappa}}\right)
\right].
}
\]

For an unbounded upper-rate class, the expression is interpreted by its limit:

\[
\boxed{
\log(1+\mathcal B_\star)
=
\Theta\!\left(\frac{L^2}{s}\right).
}
\]

Here \(\Theta\) means universal upper and lower multiplicative constants **in the logarithm of effort**. It does not mean that the optimal effort itself has been determined to a constant factor.

The important consequence is the crossover:

| Regime | Scale of \(\log(1+\mathcal B_\star)\) | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| \(s\sqrt{\kappa}\ll L\) | \(\sqrt{\kappa}L\log\!\bigl(L/(s\sqrt{\kappa})\bigr)\) | At fixed rate range and accuracy, shortening the observation window costs a polynomial in \(1/T\). |
| \(s\sqrt{\kappa}\gtrsim L\) | \(L^2/s\) | A sufficiently broad rate class permits an exponential inverse-horizon cost. |

This resolves a potentially misleading blanket statement about early inference. An exponential cost in \(1/T\) is not the uniform answer for a fixed relaxation-rate range. In the hard family producing that exponential regime, the allowed fastest rate and hidden system order grow as the deadline shrinks.

The lower bound permits every causal controller in the stated model. The upper bound uses an ordinary, explicit experiment with finite input slew and finitely integrated observations.

## 3. The operation that attains the law

The attaining experiment is simple to describe:

1. Raise the input monotonically from zero to a plateau \(U\) during the first half of the trial.
2. Hold it at \(U\) during the second half.
3. Integrate the output over finitely many equal bins.
4. Take a particular signed weighted sum of those bin averages.
5. Repeat independent trials sufficiently many times to control sensor noise.

A peak limit \(U_{\max}\) and slew limit \(V_{\max}\) are respected by choosing

\[
U\leq \min\{U_{\max},V_{\max}T/2\}.
\]

No impulse, instantaneous jump, hidden-state access, or fitted spectrum is required.

### Why this works

After the ramp has ended at \(t_r=T/2\), the deterministic output can be written exactly as

\[
y(t)=
U\int \lambda^{-1}
\left[1-a_r(\lambda)e^{-\lambda(t-t_r)}\right]d\nu(\lambda),
\]

where

\[
0\leq a_r(\lambda)\leq1.
\]

The precise monotone ramp shape need not be reconstructed: it enters through a bounded factor.

Let the remaining observation duration be \(H=T/2\). Divide it into \(m+1\) bins of width \(h=H/(m+1)\), and set

\[
a=e^{-\Lambda h},\qquad b=e^{-\mu h}.
\]

A decay \(e^{-\lambda t}\) becomes a geometric sequence in

\[
z=e^{-\lambda h}\in[a,b].
\]

We therefore construct the polynomial

\[
R_m(z)=
\frac{
T_m\!\left(\frac{2z-a-b}{b-a}\right)
}{
T_m\!\left(\frac{2-a-b}{b-a}\right)
}
=\sum_{j=0}^{m}w_jz^j,
\]

where \(T_m\) is a Chebyshev polynomial.

The two useful properties are:

\[
R_m(1)=1
\]

and a small uniform magnitude on the entire permitted interval \([a,b]\).

The first preserves the constant static contribution. The second suppresses every admissible transient simultaneously.

Using the integrated bin averages \(\bar Y_j\), return

\[
\widehat S=\frac{\mu}{U}\sum_{j=0}^{m}w_j\bar Y_j.
\]

The exact bias identity is

\[
\mathbb E\widehat S-S
=
-\int
\frac{\mu}{\lambda}
a_r(\lambda)
\frac{1-e^{-\lambda h}}{\lambda h}
R_m(e^{-\lambda h})\,d\nu(\lambda).
\]

Every factor preceding \(R_m\) lies between zero and one. Consequently,

\[
|\mathbb E\widehat S-S|
\leq
\frac{1}{
T_m\!\left(\frac{2-a-b}{b-a}\right)
}.
\]

That is a uniform finite certificate over the unknown spectrum.

### Where the cost appears

The cancellation requires large positive and negative weights. Their statistical and systematic costs are explicit:

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\widehat S)
=
\frac{\mu^2\sigma^2}{U^2h}
\sum_jw_j^2,
\]

and

\[
\mathcal A_m:=\sum_j|w_j|
=
\frac{
T_m\!\left(\frac{2+a+b}{b-a}\right)
}{
T_m\!\left(\frac{2-a-b}{b-a}\right)
}.
\]

If each measured bin has an arbitrary deterministic error of magnitude at most \(\delta\), its contribution to the final error is bounded by

\[
\frac{\mu}{U}\mathcal A_m\delta.
\]

Thus the same operation that removes the unknown transients amplifies uncertainty. Repetition reduces independent random noise; it does not remove arbitrary systematic error.

A degree of order

\[
m=
O\!\left(
\min\left\{\sqrt{\kappa}L,\frac{L^2}{s}\right\}
\right)
\]

suffices for the required bias. Bounding the coefficient growth then gives the upper half of the resource law.

### The exact compiler

The implemented compiler avoids relying on floating-point exponential endpoints. For rational input parameters, it uses the enclosing interval

\[
a=\max\{0,1-\Lambda h\},
\qquad
b=\frac1{1+\mu h},
\]

because

\[
1-x\leq e^{-x}\leq \frac1{1+x}.
\]

The weights, bias bound, amplification, squared weight norm, and sufficient repetition count are then computed with exact rational arithmetic. This enclosure can increase finite costs, but preserves the asymptotic law.

The compiler produces a certified experimental prescription under its declared model. It does not itself perform the experiment or certify the hardware assumptions.

## 4. Why feedback and more powerful inference cannot evade the bound

The converse constructs two **positive, physically realizable spectra** whose early responses are extremely close but whose static responses differ substantially.

The starting point is the classical best polynomial approximation error for the reciprocal function:

\[
E_m(K)
=
\inf_{\deg p\leq m}
\left\|\frac1x-p(x)\right\|_{\infty,[1,K]}
=
\frac{K-1}{2K}
\left(\frac{\sqrt K-1}{\sqrt K+1}\right)^m.
\]

This formula is established approximation theory; it is not a discovery of this programme. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

At the alternating extrema of the minimax error, interpolation gives a signed measure that annihilates every polynomial of degree at most \(m\). Splitting its positive and negative parts produces two probability measures \(\nu_+\) and \(\nu_-\) with:

\[
\int x^j\,d\nu_+
=
\int x^j\,d\nu_-,
\qquad j=0,\ldots,m,
\]

but

\[
S(\nu_+)-S(\nu_-)=2E_m(K).
\]

Only \(m+2\) support points in total are needed.

A small example illustrates the mechanism:

\[
\nu_+=0.8\delta_1+0.2\delta_{16},
\qquad
\nu_-=\delta_4.
\]

They have the same mass and first moment, yet

\[
S(\nu_+)=0.8125,
\qquad
S(\nu_-)=0.25.
\]

Higher-order versions match more early-time derivatives while preserving a large difference in eventual response.

Moment matching removes the first terms of the exponential series. Uniformly over the entire observed interval,

\[
\eta:=
\sup_{0\leq t\leq s}|k_+(t)-k_-(t)|
\leq
2\left[\frac{es(K-1)}{m+1}\right]^{m+1}.
\]

Choosing the support range \(K\) and moment order \(m\) jointly gives

\[
\eta\leq e^{-c\Phi},
\]

while keeping the two targets more than \(4\varepsilon\) apart.

The step from indistinguishable traces to **all adaptive experiments** is essential. For any input,

\[
\|(\Delta k)*u\|_{L^2[0,T]}
\leq T\eta\|u\|_2.
\]

On the same observed history, a causal algorithm selects the same input under either hypothesis. Gaussian change of measure therefore gives

\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_+\|P_-)
\leq
\frac{\eta^2T^2E}{2\sigma^2}
=
\frac{\eta^2s^2\mathcal B}{2}.
\]

Any estimator accurate under both models would distinguish them. Binary testing requires a nonzero amount of information, so \(\mathcal B\) must be exponentially large in \(\Phi\).

This argument covers feedback, nonlinear estimators, adaptive trial allocation, and full system-identification algorithms. The limitation is the information available through the permitted experiment.

## 6. The physical requirements that remain attached to the result

The mathematical result earns no exemption from preparation, control, noise, or reuse costs.

### Access and prior calibration

The experiment uses one input port and one scalar output. It requires certified rate bounds and normalization. The theorem does not explain how to acquire those bounds from arbitrary unknown hardware.

A positive finite spectrum has a deterministic circuit realization: a series connection of parallel RC branches with

\[
C_j=\frac1{p_j},
\qquad
R_j=\frac{p_j}{\lambda_j}
\]

realizes the impedance

\[
\sum_j\frac{p_j}{z+\lambda_j}.
\]

All components are finite and positive. That establishes realizability within the deterministic model. It does not establish that a laboratory can manufacture the finely tuned hard examples to the required precision.

### Preparation and reuse

The main inference model grants zero-state trials. A physical implementation must supply them or account for residual state.

For an initial-state norm bounded by \(B\), passive waiting for \(W\) leaves a residual at most \(Be^{-\mu W}\). Its effect on the estimator is bounded by

\[
\frac{\mu}{U}\mathcal A_m B e^{-\mu W}.
\]

To allocate preparation error \(\varepsilon_p\), it suffices to wait

\[
W\geq
\frac1\mu
\left[
\log\frac{\mu\mathcal A_mB}{U\varepsilon_p}
\right]_+.
\]

This wait can greatly exceed the short interrogation horizon. Observing a long reset trace also supplies older-response information and changes the interface.

Serial repetitions pay every interrogation and reset. Parallel repetitions pay for identically prepared specimens and measurement channels. The report also derives a separate randomized-sign upper construction for bounded unknown initial states; it does not use that extension to claim the same lower theorem for unrestricted continuous reuse.

### Control, computation, and protection from error

Finite peak and slew limits are compatible with the upper construction by reducing the amplitude and increasing repetitions. Detector bandwidth is a separate constraint: the experiment needs \(m+1\) distinguishable integration bins in \(T/2\).

The Chebyshev recurrence takes \(O(m^2)\) arithmetic operations to construct all coefficients. Actual computation must also pay for growing numerator and denominator lengths and for cancellation precision.

Calibration drift, saturation, clock error, nonlinear response, and arbitrary bin-dependent bias must be bounded. Large \(\mathcal A_m\) makes these requirements severe. A common constant offset behaves differently because the weights sum to one, but that does not protect against general systematic error.

### Thermal fluctuations

The matching theorem assumes parameter-independent additive sensor noise.

I also derived an attainable upper extension for the specified stochastic plant

\[
dx=(-Ax+bu)\,dt+\sqrt{2\Theta A}\,dB_t,
\]

starting from zero, with thermal noise independent of sensor noise. Its output covariance is

\[
\Theta\left[k_\nu(|t-v|)-k_\nu(t+v)\right],
\]

so the estimator gains variance at most

\[
\left(\frac{\mu}{U}\right)^2\Theta\mathcal A_m^2.
\]

Independent repetitions can pay that variance. The added normalized effort includes the explicit factor

\[
O\!\left(
\frac{\Theta T}{\sigma^2}
\mathcal A_m^2\varepsilon^{-2}\log\frac1\alpha
\right).
\]

At a fixed thermal-to-sensor noise ratio \(\Theta T/\sigma^2\), this leaves the upper exponent unchanged.

**The adaptive lower theorem does not automatically extend to that thermal interface.** Parameter-dependent fluctuations can carry information about the unknown system. Different equilibrium fluctuation models can even encode the desired static response directly in a variance. Preparation and the fluctuation law must therefore be specified before making a physical capability claim.

## 7. Comparison with the strongest relevant existing approaches

The attaining construction belongs to ordinary optimal extrapolation. The result does not establish superiority over it.

Brown and Grabovsky’s work on completely monotone extrapolation is especially close prior research. It already gives a precise instability analysis despite positivity.
