# Methane concentration and two isotopes: an executed inverse with a weak source–sink contrast

This retained observational route asks whether concentration and two isotope observables sharpen attribution of methane growth to sources versus hydroxyl removal. It supplies positive reconstructions, an algebraically identifiable fixed-signature inverse and public-ensemble covariance/GLS calculations, but **does not earn decisive attribution or a new atmospheric mechanism**. Read the [initial construction](package/next_science/observation/INITIAL_CONSTRUCTION.txt), [mixing/fractionation repair](package/next_science/observation/TRANSFER_CHALLENGE_AND_REPAIR.txt) and [main report](package/next_science/research_report.txt).

Different published percentages refer to different windows, denominators and definitions and are not treated as contradictory measurements. A well-mixed isotope contrast cancels hydroxyl only under its mixing/fractionation premises. Spatially selective removal adds a term the simple cancellation does not control. Fixed source signatures make the two-isotope inverse algebraically solvable; unknown signature evolution and correlated observational uncertainty are separate questions.

Using the supplied authors' Monte Carlo ensembles, a favorable GLS design gives an OH-related uncertainty of roughly **14.8–16.1 Tg/year** for a compared difference of about **14.3 Tg/year**. This is a statistically weak contrast, not a confirmed new source/sink separation. [Covariance check](package/next_science/observation/dual_isotope_covariance_check.json), [GLS design check](package/next_science/observation/dual_isotope_GLS_design_check.json), [inverse record](package/next_science/observation/dual_isotope_inverse.json). The displayed uncertainties inherit the given model, signatures, ensemble and window choices; they are not a general atmospheric identifiability theorem.

Six unchanged archived global-isotope ensemble workbooks and selected published source spreadsheets are included, along with original NOAA snapshot headers, extracted covariance tables, programs and URL/hash manifests. The authors' full upstream isotope/mass-balance pipelines were **not rerun** and their large upstream archives are not bundled. These are computations from selected archived outputs, not new measurements or revised author reconstructions. NOAA records are dated snapshots that can be revised, not claims about today's live series.
