CONSTRUCTIVE PEER CHALLENGE: FINITE GEOMETRY COMPLETION FOR RARE RATES
Derived after independent question construction, in collaboration with the
transfer constructor. This is an explicit mathematical operation, not a
novelty claim. First proof checkpoint before nearest FEM literature checking.

SETTING
=======
Omega=[0,ell] x product_{j=2}^d T_{L_j}. All transverse directions are periodic.
The two flat end faces have Dirichlet values0 and1. Conductivity a(x)>0 is
scalar, with log a K-Lipschitz in Euclidean distance on the cylinder:
|log a(x)-log a(y)|<=K distance(x,y). For reversible overdamped isotropic
diffusion, a is proportional to exp(-beta V), with diffusion prefactor included
consistently. This proof does NOT cover arbitrary nonreversible drift, an
unknown diffusion tensor, or general unacquired basin geometry.

C=inf_{u in H1; u|left=0,u|right=1} E_a(u),
E_a(u)=integral_Omega a |grad u|^2.
The unit-current Thomson problem is R=inf_J R_a(J)=1/C, where
R_a(J)=integral_Omega |J|^2/a, div J=0 in distributions, J is transverse-
periodic, and the net cross-sectional first-coordinate flux is1. This permits
nonuniform and signed local normal flux at the end faces. Exact optimizers
are q and J*=a grad q/C (orientation left to right).

Use a rectangular mesh with spacings h_j=L_j/n_j (h_1=ell/n_1), h=max h_j,
and h_1<=ell/4. Let V_h be conforming Q1 functions with these boundary values.
Let W_h be rectangular RT0 currents satisfying exact cell divergence0 and net
cross-sectional flux1. Define finite optima
 U_h(a)=min_{V_h} E_a(u), R_h(a)=min_{W_h} R_a(J).
They always give 1/R_h(a)<=C<=U_h(a).

CLAIM
=====
Put r=(3/2)*sqrt(sum_j h_j^2), s=ell/(ell-2h_1). Then

 U_h(a)<=s exp(K(h_1+r)) C,
 R_h(a)<=exp(K r)/C,
 U_h(a)R_h(a)<=s exp(K(h_1+2r)).

In particular,
 log[U_h R_h]<=h[K(1+3 sqrt(d))+4/ell].

This is a relative capacity certificate. No exponentially small absolute
committor residual, supplied exact committor, global H2 regularity constant,
or saddle/Morse hypothesis occurs. The averaging of q,J below is only an
existence proof: the actual algorithm minimizes the finite quadratic forms
using its acquired coefficients.

ONE-DIMENSIONAL AVERAGING KERNELS
===============================
Let B_h(z)=1/h on [-h/2,h/2], zero elsewhere. On a grid cell [mh,(m+1)h]
with t=(x-mh)/h define

 K0(x,y)=(1-t) B_h(y-mh)+t B_h(y-(m+1)h),
 K1(x,y)=(1/h) integral_0^h B_h(y-mh-s) ds.

Each kernel is nonnegative. For each x, its integral over y is1. For each y,
its integral over all x is1: for K0, global hat weights have integral h and
the centered boxes tile; for K1, the translates of a triangular B-spline sum
to1 after multiplication by cell length h. Both are supported on |x-y|<=3h/2.
The same statements hold on periodized grids.

Tensor products of these kernels are therefore positive, row- and column-
normalized local averaging operators. For any scalar f and positive scalar a,
Jensen and the log-Lipschitz bound imply

 integral a(x) [integral K(x,y) f(y) dy]^2 dx
 <= exp(K r) integral a(y) f(y)^2 dy,

if the kernel support has distance<=r. Replacing a by1/a gives the identical
inequality. Restricting the x integral to a subset only reduces the column
mass, so the inequality also holds for a zero-extended input f.

PRIMAL CONSTRUCTION
===================
Compress the exact q into [h_1,ell-h_1]. Define q_b=0 to the left of h_1,
q_b=1 to the right of ell-h_1, and
 q_b(x_1,x')=q(s(x_1-h_1),x')
in between. Extend by constants outside the cylinder in coordinate1.
The inverse coordinate map moves points by at most h_1. Under change of
variables, its first derivative is multiplied by s and transverse derivatives
by1, while the volume factor is1/s. Consequently

 E_a(q_b)<=s exp(K h_1) E_a(q)=s exp(K h_1) C.

At each mesh vertex z set u_z to the average of q_b over the centered box
product_j[z_j-h_j/2,z_j+h_j/2], then interpolate by Q1. The buffers make
u_z exactly0/1 on the end faces. For component j, the derivative of this Q1
interpolant is a tensor-product averaging of partial_j q_b: K1 in coordinate
j and K0 in every other coordinate. The finite-difference identity is
(q_b(z+h_j e_j+r)-q_b(z+r))/h_j
=integral_0^1 partial_j q_b(z+t h_j e_j+r) dt.
Thus each component obeys the preceding Jensen inequality with radius r.
Sum the components, obtaining E_a(u_h)<=exp(Kr) E_a(q_b), as claimed.

DUAL CONSTRUCTION AND BOUNDARY CHECK
===================================
Reflect J across x_1=0 and x_1=ell with J_1 even and every J_j, j>1, odd.
Repeat to make a field on the doubled torus T_{2ell} x product T_{L_j}.
Reflect a evenly. The extended field remains divergence-free in distributions:
the interior divergence changes sign under reflection, and the normal trace
is continuous so no surface delta source appears. Tangential jumps do not
create divergence deltas. The net first-coordinate flux is1 at almost every
cross-section; this follows from div J=0 and transverse periodicity.
The extended weighted energy is exactly twice the original energy.

Convolve this extended J with the centered rectangular box of spacings h_j.
Then apply the standard RT0 face-average interpolant: component j is linear
in x_j between its two face means and constant in the transverse cell
coordinates. Face means use the same convolved field on neighboring cells,
so normal continuity holds. Gauss' theorem shows its cell divergence is
exactly the averaged divergence, namely0. Its cross-sectional flux is1.

For component j, the resulting map of the original J_j has tensor kernel K0
in coordinate j and K1 in each other coordinate. These are the same positive
double-stochastic factors with roles exchanged. Apply Jensen with weight1/a
on the doubled torus to get energy<=exp(Kr) times exact current energy.
The even/odd symmetries survive both centered convolution and the grid-aligned
RT0 interpolation. Thus energy restricted to Omega is exactly half of the
doubled energy, with no factor2 loss. This proves the dual estimate.

FINITE ACQUIRED COEFFICIENTS
===========================
Suppose a piecewise-constant positive rational a_hat is acquired with a true
pointwise enclosure exp(-eta) a_hat<=a<=exp(eta) a_hat. The finite matrices
for Uhat=U_h(a_hat) and Rhat=R_h(a_hat) can be integrated exactly: Q1 gradient
products and RT0 component products are polynomials on rectangular cells.
Then the actual continuous capacity is certified in

 [exp(-eta)/Rhat, exp(eta) Uhat].

By comparing each discrete optimum with the true-coefficient optimum, a
conservative ratio bound is

 exp(4 eta) s exp(K(h_1+2r)).

The eta budget includes physical acquisition error, variation within each
cell if cell-center values are used, and rational rounding of exponential
weights. The target probability/escape-rate mass denominator must be bounded
separately using the same potential enclosure; it is not free.

FINITE ALGEBRA AND RESOURCES
===========================
There are O(product_j n_j) Q1 unknowns and O(d product_j n_j) RT0 degrees of
freedom and constraints. For fixed d and fixed geometric aspect ratios,
choosing h<=epsilon/[K(1+3 sqrt(d))+4/ell] and eta=O(epsilon) gives a 1+O(epsilon)
relative bracket with O(((K+ell^-1)/epsilon)^d) cells. This is exponential in
dimension, and that cost must remain visible.

Use rational domain coordinates and positive rational conductivity weights.
For a dimensionless potential span H and relative coefficient rounding eta,
dyadic exponent/mantissa weights can be represented by ordinary rationals
with O(H+log(1/eta)) bit length, plus geometry bits. Exact fraction-free linear
algebra solves the finite SPD/KKT systems after redundant constraints are
removed. Hadamard determinant bounds give polynomial bit lengths in matrix
dimension and input bit length. Thus an exact dense solver has a polynomial
bit-operation bound in cell count and H, without waiting exp(H) for a rare
transition. No nearly-linear solver claim is required. Unknown coefficient
or domain metrology, acquisition time, holding control and all sensor errors
belong in the transfer constructor's separate physical resource theorem.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT ESTABLISH
===========================
No global high-dimensional efficiency; no nonlinear reaction-coordinate
discovery; no arbitrary-basin geometry oracle; no nonequilibrium capacity
formula; no universal MFPT from an arbitrary initial condition. It supplies
the missing finite spatial solve for the specified fully acquired reversible
strip/cylinder problem. General torus slab pairs create two parallel strips;
their certified capacities can be added, so a second path cannot be silently
discarded. General disconnected geometries require their own decomposition
or geometric acquisition.
