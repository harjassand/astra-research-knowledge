PROJECTED FLUX FLATNESS IS NOT A RELATIVE RATE CERTIFICATE
Root derivation, 2026-11-04; comparator challenge, not a novelty claim.

Consider a reversible continuous-time Markov chain with symmetric edge
conductances on A--c--B and A--d--B. Conductances are respectively
1, epsilon, epsilon, 1, with epsilon>0. A common stationary-mass
normalization rescales all following capacities but not any ratio.

The exact committor has q(A)=0, q(B)=1,
q(c)=epsilon/(1+epsilon), q(d)=1/(1+epsilon).
Each branch is a pair of resistances in series, so total capacity is
    C = 2 epsilon/(1+epsilon).

The trial coordinate u(A)=0, u(c)=u(d)=1/2, u(B)=1 satisfies the
boundary conditions exactly. Its oriented cut flux across every level
t in (0,1/2) and every level t in (1/2,1) is
    Phi_u(t) = (1+epsilon)/2.
Thus its projected flux is perfectly flat, and its Dirichlet energy is
    E(u) = (1+epsilon)/2.
Nevertheless,
    E(u)/C = (1+epsilon)^2/(4 epsilon),
which is unbounded as epsilon -> 0.

The problem is local nonconservation: the flux divergence at c is
(1-epsilon)/2 and at d is its negative. Projection puts c and d at the
same level and hides both defects. A scalar level-flux profile cannot
replace pointwise/nodewise conservation of a dual flow.

An exactly conserved unit flow sends 1/2 along each complete branch.
Its resistance energy is
    R(j)= (1/2)(1+1/epsilon),
so the Thomson lower bound 1/R(j) equals C. A genuinely conserved flow
therefore detects the missing information that the projected plateau
does not. This requires knowing or acquiring the small conductances.

Access warning: finite equilibrium configurations of the uniform chain
do not identify epsilon. If only stationary samples are available, all
epsilon give the same law. Dynamical information or a known generator
must be supplied/acquired. Time-resolved passive data and actively reset
local trajectories have different costs and cannot be silently exchanged.

Existing comparator: Petersen, Lichtinger, Covino, arXiv:2608.02536v1,
Supplement section 'Where the estimate fails' explicitly recognizes
that flux flatness is necessary but insufficient. Their empirical
plateau and nested-refinement diagnostics are not attributed here as a
claimed rigorous two-sided relative guarantee. The explicit example
sharpens that known limitation; it does not refute their scoped theorem.
