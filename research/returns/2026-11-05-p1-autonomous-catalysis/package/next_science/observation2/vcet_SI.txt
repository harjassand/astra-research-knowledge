 1   Experimental Section

 2   Chemicals

 3        All chemicals were purchased from Aldrich and TCI Shanghai as well as used without further

 4   purification (Guaranteed reagent). Comercial PtCo/C (20 wt.%, atom rate = 1:1) and Pt/C (40 wt.%)

 5   catalyst were purchased from Premetek and Johnson Matthey as benchmark. High-purity N2, O2,

 6   H2, 5% H2/N2, were purchased from Nanjing Tianze Gas CO, LTD.

 7   Preparation of tert-butylsulfonylcalix[4]arene

 8        The synthesis of tert-butylthiacalix[4]arene was achieved by dissolving 2.15 mol tert-

 9   butylphenol, 4.312 mol sulfur (S₈), and 1.107 mol NaOH in 40 mL dimethoxyethane by ultrasonic.

10   Under nitrogen protection, the reaction solution was then slowly heated to 230 oC and maintained

11   at this temperature for 3 h. After cooling the reaction to approximately 60 oC, 70 mL anhydrous

12   ether, 18 mL toluene, and 70 mL 4 M sulfuric acid were added. The solution was stirred for 2 h,

13   followed by filtration and recrystallization to yield a white solid. In the next step, tert-

14   butylsulfonylcalix[4]arene was obtained by oxidizing 1.38 mmol tert-butylthiacalix[4]arene with 20

15   mmol NaBO3·4H2O in 30 mL of chloroform and 50 mL of acetic acid at 50 oC for 24 h. After the

16   reaction, 50 mL of saturated NaCl and 20 mL of chloroform were added for extraction. The organic

17   phase was subjected to rotary evaporator to afford the tert-butylsulfonylcalix[4]arene as a white

18   powder.

19   Preparation of tBuC[4]A-PtCo/C catalyst

20        50 mg of PtCo/C (Premetek) was uniformly dispersed in 8 mL DMF, and then 2 mL DMF and

21   triethylamine solution containing 100 mg/mL tert-butylsulfonylcalix[4]arene was added. The

22   mixture was stirred for 12 hours, filtered, and washed with DMF and methanol, followed by drying

23   to obtain the tBuC[4]A-PtCo/C catalyst. As comparison samples, tert-butylphenol was modified on

24   the surface of PtCo/C using a similar method, replacing tert-butylsulfonylcalix[4]arene with tert-

25   butylphenol. The use of commercial PtCo/C as the starting material exclude batch to batch errors in

26   small amounts of synthesis in the laboratory.

27   Physical Characterization

28        DRIFT spectra were collected on a Thermo Fisher Nicolet iS5 spectrometer equipped with a

29   diffuse reflectance accessory. Powder samples were loaded directly into the sample cup. Spectra

                                                     S2
 1   were recorded at room temperature in air with the following parameters: resolution = 4 cm-1, number

 2   of scans = 32, mirror velocity = 0.4747, aperture = 80.00, and gain = 1.0. Background correction

 3   was performed using the OMNIC software.

 4          XPS measurements were performed on a Thermo Scientific K-Alpha spectrometer using a

 5   monochromatic Al Kα source (1486.6 eV). The analysis chamber pressure was below 5.0 × 10-7

 6   mBar, and charge compensation was achieved using a dual-beam (electrons/Ar+ ions) flood gun.

 7   Survey scans used a pass energy of 50 eV and step size of 1.0 eV; high-resolution scans used a pass

 8   energy of 20 eV and step size of 0.1 eV. All binding energies were referenced to the C 1s peak at

 9   284.8 eV. Data fitting employed a Shirley background and Voigt (mixed Gaussian-Lorentzian) peak

10   profiles.

11          HRTEM images and EDS analyses were obtained using an FEI Talos microscope operated at

12   200 kV (Ceta CCD camera, electron dose ~5 nA). Samples were prepared by dispersing the catalyst

13   powder in ethanol via ultrasonication for 15 min, followed by drop-casting the suspension onto a

14   copper grid and drying in air. Post-ADT samples for TEM analysis were recovered by carefully

15   scraping the catalyst layer from the electrode and re-dispersing it in ethanol using the same

16   procedure.

17          NMR spectra were recorded on a Bruker 600 MHz spectrometer. Samples were dissolved in

18   CDCl3. Spectra were acquired with 16 scans, a relaxation delay of 1 s, and at 298 K. For the post-

19   stability analysis, tBuC[4]A was recovered from the test solution by extraction with

20   dichloromethane, drying over anhydrous Na2SO4, and recrystallization from toluene prior to

21   measurement.

22          Co K-edge and Pt L3-edge X-ray absorption spectra were collected at the BL14W1 beamline

23   of the Shanghai Synchrotron Radiation Facility (SSRF) in transmission mode using a Si(111)

24   double-crystal monochromator. Homogeneous powder samples were pressed into thin pellets. Data

25   processing (energy calibration, background subtraction, normalization, EXAFS extraction) was

26   performed using the Demeter software package. EXAFS oscillations were k3-weighted and Fourier-

27   transformed. Detailed fitting parameters are provided in Tables S1 and S2. Wavelet transform

28   analysis was conducted using the HAMA Fortran code with a Morlet mother wavelet (σ = 1, κ =

29   10).

10   Electrochemical Characterization

11        The electrochemical tests were performed using a three-electrode system on an electrochemical

12   workstation (CHI 760e). The working electrode was prepared by dispersing 5 mg catalyst powder

13   in a mixture of isopropanol and 5 wt% Nafion (v / v = 1.97 / 0.03) under ultrasonic agitation for 40

14   min in ice-water bath. Subsequently, the ink was transferred onto a glassy carbon electrode (φ = 5.6

15   mm, Pt loading ≈ 12 μgPt cm-2) and dried at room temperature. Graphite electrode and Hg/HgCl2

16   electrode were used as the counter and reference electrode in 0.1 M HClO4 solution, respectively

17   (The calibration of Hg/HgCl2 electrode was carried out with Pt foil as working electrode in H2-

18   saturated electrolyte). Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) was conducted at the potential of 0.6 - 1.0

19   V (vs. RHE) with a scan rate of 20 mV s-1 and iR-95% correction, in O2-saturated 0.1 M HClO4

20   electrolyte.

21        The electrochemical active surface area (ECSA) was obtained by integrating the hydrogen

22   underpotential deposition peak at the potential of 0.05 - 0.35 V (vs. RHE) in the N2-saturated 0.1 M

12   PEMFCs Test

13        Membrane electrode assembly was prepared with carbon paper and proton exchange

14   membrane (Gore M765.08), without any treatment, coated by a catalyst, through hot pressing at 140

15   oC for 2 min. Among them, 'catalyst ink' was composed of catalyst powder, isopropanol, and Nafion


16   solutions, as well as are sonicated until no obvious powder deposits are observed at the bottom of

17   the cup before spraying. 'Catalyst ink' was sprayed onto proton exchange membrane through

18   ultrasonic atomization and compressed air gas guidance. Commercial PtCo/C or tBu[4]-PtCo/C was

19   used as cathodic catalysts (Pt loading ≈ 0.1 mg cm-2) as well as commercial Pt/C (40 wt.%) on

20   anode. The cathode is fed with high-purity oxygen with 100% R.H., 150 kPa, and the flow rate is

21   controlled by a mass flow meter (AST10-DL) at 500 sccm. Polarization curve and 30k-cycle ADT

22   (3 s for 0.95 V and 3 s for 0.6 V) were measured using an electrochemical workstation (CS2020B)

23   and current amplifier. Except for the calculation of mass activity, there is no IR correction for the

24   output power and activation energy of PEMFC.

25   Theoretical calculation

26        DFT calculations: DFT calculations are performed via the Vienna ab initio simulation

27   package (VASP)1, 2. Ion-electron interactions are described by projection-augmented waves (PAWs).

28   The exchange-related functional is represented by the Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) functional

29   under the Generalized Gradient Approximation (GGA)3. The kinetic energy cut-off for

                                                      S5
 1   geometrically optimized was set to 500 eV, and a (3 × 3 × 1) k-point was used for the sampling of

 2   the Brillouin zone. The structure was optimized until the energy and force reached the convergence

 3   thresholds of 10-5 eV and 0.03 eV/Å, respectively. The dispersion correction of DFT-D3 was

 4   introduced into the whole calculation process. The Pt1Co1 alloy was cleaved to exposed the 111

 5   surface, and tBuC[4]A was modified at the Co site according to the synchrotron radiation results.

 6   The vacuum layer thickness was set to 20 Å to fully avoid interactions caused by periodic mirroring.

 7        MD simulations: The entire molecular dynamics simulations were performed using the

 8   GPUMD software package4. Our simulation cells consist of 6893 atoms. To achieve the best balance

 9   between accuracy and cost of massively parallel computing, we used neuroevolutionary machine

10   learning potentials formulated by the method referenced to Fan et al5. The training dataset was

11   derived from ab initio molecular dynamics simulations and fine-tuned using parameters from the

12   recently published General Machine Learning Potential NEP896.The generation was set to 50000

13   until the loss function converges, and the Ziegler-Biersack-Littmark(ZBL) potential, which includes

14   an additional switching function that ramps the energy, force, and curvature smoothly to zero

15   between an inner and outer cutoff was introduced when the distance is less than 2 Å to correct the

16   errors in the force field when atomic distances are very close. The potential energy due to a pair of

17   atoms at a distance rij is given by:
