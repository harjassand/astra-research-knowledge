EXACT PULSE-TO-TURNOVER ACCOUNTING
Observation constructor, 2026-11-04

1. The problem with counting accelerated elementary jumps

A pulse that advances OH reduction can leave the next adsorption or
protonation as the bottleneck. Its response can include later depletion
and refractory time. A positive branching kernel is therefore not an
automatic consequence of a faster elementary rate. The following
identities account for complete chemical cycles before bounding gain.
They are applications of standard occupation and Poisson-equation
machinery; no foundational novelty is asserted from the identities alone.

2. Exact irreversible-cycle identity

Consider a cyclic catalyst with n chemical states. In the cold reference,
state i advances to i+1 at rate k_i>0. The complete turnover current is
J0=(sum_i 1/k_i)^(-1). During operation let the conditional rate be
k_i u_i(t), where u_i can depend on the entire history, including other
catalysts' reaction events. Assume finite long-time means. If p_i is
the time occupancy of state i and J the common cycle current, counting
flow across each edge gives

  J = k_i p_i + k_i <(u_i-1) 1_{X=i}>.

Sum after dividing by k_i and use sum p_i=1. The exact result is

  J/J0 = 1 + sum_i <(u_i-1) 1_{X=i}>.                 (1)

There is no independence, Poisson-arrival, rapid-mixing or additional-
offspring assumption in (1). Finite-time versions have only bounded
edge-count/state-occupancy boundary corrections divided by run time.

3. A whole-cycle upper bound from finite pulse exposure

Suppose a pulse emitted by a donor event has nonnegative multiplier
envelopes f_i(s), s>=0, with u_i(t)-1 bounded by the sum of received
envelopes. Define I=integral_0^infinity max_i f_i(s) ds. Using max_i
inside the integral avoids charging the same gate once for each chemical
step: the catalyst occupies only one chemical state at a time.

If pulses arrive at mean rate nu, (1) implies

  J/J0 <= 1 + nu I.                                  (2)

If nu<=mJ because at most m donor pulses are received per complete
target turnover, then, whenever m J0 I<1,

  J/J0 <= 1/(1-m J0 I).                              (3)

This is an upper bound, not a branching-process equality. It already
allows the most favorable phase alignment and ignores detrimental
effects of the pulse. Adding other chemical bottlenecks cannot evade
it by substituting a high elementary k_i for the measured whole-cycle J0.

For heterogeneous interacting catalysts, let B_ab be the sum of envelope
integrals received by catalyst a per complete donor-b turnover. Then
componentwise J <= J0 + diag(J0) B J. If the nonnegative matrix
diag(J0)B has spectral radius below one, the certified vector upper bound
is J <= [I-diag(J0)B]^(-1) J0. This matrix inverse bounds whole currents;
its entries are not assumed to be true offspring numbers.

4. Conditions that matter physically

The reference and modified system must share the same chemical states,
sites and cold rates, apart from the stated pulse modulation. Static
ligand/electronic changes, changed active-site normalization and changed
mass transport are separate mechanisms and cannot be hidden in u_i.
The elementary cycle in this section is irreversible; full reversible
networks need the corrected-reward construction below.

The pulse budget nu<=mJ is a productive-event budget. Arbitrarily many
reversible adsorption/desorption events are NOT bounded by the net ORR
current. Their gross rates, captured energy and reverse energy exchange
must be measured or obtained from a thermodynamically closed model.
The envelope condition allows reset/saturating pulses and arbitrary
temporal correlations. It need not hold for unbounded nonlinear coherent
accumulation of pulse energies. Such a mechanism requires its own
amplitude distribution, energy accounting and lifetime measurements.

5. Exact corrected-current identity for reversible networks

Let the cold reference be an ergodic finite continuous-time Markov chain
with generator Q0, rates k0_ij, and signed chemical reward d_ij on a jump.
Here each i->j label denotes a reaction channel; parallel channels with
different chemical rewards must remain separate in reward sums even
though their rates add in Q0.
For an ORR current, a cycle-completion edge has forward reward +1 and
reverse reward -1. Define j0(i)=sum_j k0_ij d_ij and J0=pi0 dot j0.
Choose a solution h of the Poisson equation

  Q0 h = J0 - j0.                                    (4)

An additive constant in h is immaterial. For arbitrary adapted actual
rates k_ij(t), write delta k_ij(t)=k_ij(t)-k0_ij. New edges are allowed.
The counting-process martingale identity gives

  E[C_T+h(X_T)-h(X_0)]
    = J0 T + E integral_0^T sum_j delta k_Xj
                       [d_Xj+h(j)-h(X)] dt.           (5)

Divide by T. For finite h the boundary term vanishes, giving the exact
long-time net-current response

  J-J0 = <sum_j delta k_Xj [d_Xj+h(j)-h(X)]>.          (6)

This identity retains negative response and delayed depletion. It does
not identify an accelerated elementary reaction with an extra turnover.
On edges with k0_ij>0 one can write delta k_ij=k0_ij(u_ij-1).
For positive multiplier-increase envelopes f_ij(s), one safe pulse upper bound
is A=integral max_i sum_j k0_ij f_ij(s)
                         [d_ij+h(j)-h(i)]_+ ds,
and J<=J0+nu A. Reverse edges and gate changes must also be included
whenever their rates change. If some rates decrease, apply the signed
version of (6); dropping those terms without checking signs is invalid.
For an added edge with k0_ij=0, use its absolute delta-k envelope in A
instead of the product k0_ij f_ij; multiplicative perturbations alone
would omit the new chemical/gate channel.

For an irreversible cycle, equation (4) makes
  k_i[d_i+h(i+1)-h(i)]=J0
on every forward edge. Thus (6) reduces exactly to (1). A fully reversible
physical catalyst can be checked with (6), using its acquired generator;
one cannot import (3) unchanged while ignoring backward gross activity.

The Poisson identity is established Markov reward/sensitivity machinery.
Its role here is to repair the central causal accounting error: the
quantity charged to a chemical pulse must be its complete-cycle current
response, including its future effect on state occupancy.

6. Continuous decay, finite lifetime and executed resource requirements

For a continuously decaying barrier reduction
  DeltaE(t)=DeltaE0 exp(-t/tau), c=DeltaE0/(kBT),
the extra multiplier exposure is exactly
  I_exp = tau [Ei(c)-EulerGamma-log(c)].              (7)
This is not tau times the peak rate ratio. At T=298 K, DeltaE0=.192 eV,
tau=41 ns, c=7.47673651 and exp(c)=1766.46651,
  I_exp=11.5311414 microseconds.
If instead the barrier is maximally reduced for a random lifetime with
mean tau, a rectangular-amplitude upper bound is
  I_rect=tau[exp(c)-1]=72.3841269 microseconds.         (8)
This also follows from a global amplitude cap and energy-area bound via
the convex chord inequality exp(beta*x)-1 <=
  [exp(beta*DeltaE0)-1] x/DeltaE0, 0<=x<=DeltaE0.
It does not follow from knowing one simulated excitation's energy alone.

Equation (2) requires nu >= (G-1)/I. For G=3.3 this is at least
199,459.874 received pulses/s for (7), or 31,774.922/s for (8), even with
perfect chemical-state alignment. No elementary attempt frequency enters
these complete-cycle lower requirements. The hot and cold rates must,
however, share the prefactor used to turn a barrier change into u_i.

Example: J0=4 complete cycles/s and m=100 productive donor pulses per
target turnover imply G<=1.00463383 or G<=1.02981696. To allow G=3.3,
the same assumptions require m>=15,110.60 or >=2,407.19; alternatively,
tau>=6.1953 microseconds or >=0.98695 microseconds. At fixed tau and m,
required barrier reductions are .33757 eV or .27367 eV. These conditions
are necessary, not sufficient: finite chemical-phase occupancy lowers
the attainable response. The full parameter grid, including J0=.2,1,4/s
and the site-normalization alternative, is pulse_resource_bounds.json.

The source reports an elementary barrier pair .375 -> .183 eV and a
41 ns storage annotation, but its accessible SI does not establish their
operating-potential reference or the actual pulse waveform. Therefore
these calculations are conditional challenges, not an exclusion of the
authors' mechanism. Nor is m=100 a measured geometric limit. The 1.6 nm
source distance is used to calculate ballistic propagation time; it is
not evidence that all coupling beyond 1.6 nm vanishes.

7. Exact full-cycle finite-switching construction

Use six balanced forward states: empty, O2*, OOH*, O*, OH*, H2O*.
The chemostatted proton/electron/water stoichiometry yields
  O2 + 4H+ + 4e- -> 2H2O
per complete cycle. This benchmark is explicitly irreversible/high-
affinity; the independent closed-network construction retains reverses.
At chemical state i, gate g in {0,1} has generator G with up rate a and
down rate b. Di is diagonal with the two chemical exit rates. A reaction
can change the gate, with rate matrix Ri whose row sums equal Di.
The exact phase-type renewal matrices and waiting-time vectors are
  Hi=(Di-G)^(-1) Ri, ti=(Di-G)^(-1) 1.                (9)
Let rho H0...H5=rho. Then the complete current is
  J^(-1)=sum_i rho H0...H_(i-1) ti.                  (10)
This retains the conditional gate distribution at arrival in each state;
replacing it with a universal mean duty factor is generally incorrect.

full_cycle_renewal.py executes (9)-(10) at 60-digit precision, checks every
Hi is stochastic and checks stationary cycle phase. For a baseline
J0=1/s with 75% of cold waiting time at OH*, all other five waits equal,
the reported barrier ratio and a fixed-height/exponential-lifetime gate:
  own adsorption-triggered pulse gain: 1 + 3.99e-23;
  perfectly aligned pulse on OH* entry: 1.000072382;
  independent 100 pulses/s, no thermal gate: 1.00541846;
  independent 10,000 pulses/s, no thermal gate: 1.45949743;
  always-excited limit: 3.99321830.
The own-site pulse result comes from three intervening chemical waits;
it does not rebut the source's specifically adjacent-site mechanism.

For a hypothetical thermal two-state free-energy gap q=.126 eV,
a=b exp(-q/kBT)=180,427.70/s and b=24,390,243.90/s. Ordinary thermal
gating then gives complete-cycle gain 3.29257654. Adding 100 chemical
pulses/s gives 3.29287384, a relative increment 9.03e-5. This is a
concrete existing-physics alternative with complete-cycle accounting,
not a causal fit. The source's .126 eV single-point energy increase
DOES NOT establish a two-state thermal free-energy gap. Mode degeneracy,
conditional state energies, bath coupling and transition-state energies
remain needed to apply this hypothetical alternative to the material.

8. Continuous-amplitude own-cycle delay, without a two-state replacement

If S is the sum of the three chemical waits between adsorption and OH*,
each independent exponential with rates k_j, the expected residual extra
hazard on OH* arrival is exactly
  E[A(S)]=k_OH tau sum_(n>=1) c^n/(n n!)
                        product_j k_j/(k_j+n/tau).   (11)
For fixed S and z=c exp(-S/tau), the exact target survival is
  exp{-k_OH t-k_OH tau[F(z)-F(z exp(-t/tau))]},
where F(z)=Ei(z)-EulerGamma-log(z). Integration gives its mean wait.
Equation (11) and its product upper bound are executed in the resource
script. With J0=1/s, OH waiting fraction .75, the expected extra hazard
is 3.41e-25; its ratio to a perfectly coincident fresh pulse is 2.22e-20.
This very small example is a declared kinetic scenario, not measured
ORR intermediate dwell times. The general full-cycle bounds do not need
these scenario-specific dwell times or this self-pulse delay assumption.

9. A reversible counterexample and the exact remaining observable

The preceding simple-cycle inequalities are NOT universal for reversible
microkinetics, even when the total reaction affinity is large. The
independent invention constructor supplied a particularly transparent
two-state E/I counterexample with two parallel channels. Precursor
E->I has rate c=1000/s and I->E rate d=10^6/s. The product channel I->E
has rate a=1046.465912/s and reverse E->I rate
  b=(ca/d) exp[-1.2eV/(kBT)]=5.3127e-21/s.
Thus the net cycle pays total affinity 1.2 eV at 298 K. Its exact current
  J0=(ca-db)/(a+b+c+d)=1.04432873/s,
while symmetric product-barrier scaling (a,b)->r(a,b) gives
  J(r)=r(ca-db)/[r(a+b)+c+d].                        (12)
Its product-barrier degree of rate control is
  (c+d)/(a+b+c+d)=.99895567,
despite J0/a=.000997958. Repeated precursor recrossing makes the reactive
state rare. Interpreting J0/a as a waiting fraction would be wrong here.

For this baseline, choose h(E)=0, h(I)=(a+b)/(a+b+c+d). The corrected
product reward is w=1-h(I)=.99895567. A prescribed pulse multiplying
both product-channel rates by g(t) has exact net additional turnover
response, INCLUDING all later return to the cold steady state,
  R=integral [g(t)-1] w [(a+b)p_I(t)-b] dt,
where
  dp_I/dt=[c+g(t)b](1-p_I)-[d+g(t)a]p_I.             (13)
This is an exact two-dimensional integration, not a positive-offspring
approximation. The rectangle also has an analytic exponential solution.

reversible_impulse_response.py executes (13). With the same .192 eV
and 41 ns parameters, an unconditioned stationary pulse yields
  R_exp=1.19581509e-5 and R_rect=7.27620853e-5 turnovers.
A pulse conditioned on arrival at reactive I yields
  R_exp=.0118794449 and R_rect=.0714169118 turnovers.
The roughly 10^3-fold difference arises solely from chemical readiness;
the waveform and microscopic rate multiplier are unchanged. A lasting
3.3-fold current gain cannot be inferred from this isolated-pulse result:
finite pulse rates change the arrival state distribution and require a
full joint process. Nor has the energetic cost of the prescribed pulse
or readiness conditioning been supplied in this toy example.

The response demonstrates exactly what must be acquired for a physical
autonomous interpretation: the joint law of donor events, gate phase,
target chemical state and subsequent signed productive response. Mean
pulse frequency and lifetime alone do not determine it. The event-
conditioned response can be computed from the full reaction/gate
generator with (4)-(6), retaining reverse capture and relaxation edges.
No fabricated equilibrium free energy or unmeasured event synchronization
can replace those rates. A conditional probability relative to this
nonequilibrium steady state gives a relative-entropy information measure;
kBT times that measure is not by itself an unconditional work-cost bound.
