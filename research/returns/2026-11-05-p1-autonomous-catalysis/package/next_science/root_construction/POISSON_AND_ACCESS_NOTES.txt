ROOT CONSTRUCTION NOTES — 2026-11-04
Working derivations. These are not a completed new physical capability.

1. Exact target and goal-oriented certificate
Let rho>0, A=-div(D rho grad) with zero Dirichlet data at target B and
reflecting/periodic remaining boundary. Let f=rho 1_A0 and g=rho on the
non-target domain, so physical mean from Gibbs conditioned on A0 is
S/Z_A0, S=<f,A^-1 g> in unweighted volume pairing. The initial set A0 is
not an absorbing set in this Poisson problem.

Let u=A^-1 f, v=A^-1 g. Choose conforming p,q and feasible fluxes
-div(j_f)=f, -div(j_g)=g, with zero reflecting normal flux. Define
E_f=int |j_f-D rho grad p|^2/(D rho), and similarly E_g. Integration by
parts gives energy errors ||u-p||_a^2<=E_f, ||v-q||_a^2<=E_g. Exactly
S-[<f,q>+<g,p>-a(p,q)]=a(u-p,v-q), hence the error is bounded by
sqrt(E_f E_g). This is standard goal-oriented elliptic error machinery,
not a newly discovered duality. It directly certifies the requested mean,
unlike capacity divided by basin mass.

2. A conservative perturbation bound, with its condition number exposed
Suppose exp(-eta)rho<=rho_tilde<=exp(eta)rho, common D. For fixed sources,
Loewner comparison of A and A_tilde and polarization/Cauchy give
|<f,(A_tilde^-1-A^-1)g>| <= (exp(eta)-1) sqrt(Q_f Q_g),
Q_f=<f,A^-1 f>, Q_g=<g,A^-1 g>. The positive Green kernel then accounts
for the changing positive sources f_tilde=rho_tilde 1_A0,g_tilde=rho_tilde:

exp(-2eta)[S-(exp(eta)-1)sqrt(Q_f Q_g)] <= S_tilde
 <= exp(2eta)[S+(exp(eta)-1)sqrt(Q_f Q_g)].

The lower endpoint is replaced by zero if necessary. Dividing the changing
normalizer gives ratios exp(+/-3eta)[1 +/- (exp(eta)-1)chi],
chi=sqrt(Q_f Q_g)/S. This is sufficient, not asserted sharp for physical
potential perturbations. chi can be large; its largeness alone does not
prove that local potential perturbations or experiments attain the bound.
No universal capacity-robustness claim for all initial means follows.

3. Exact first variation with respect to dimensionless potential
For rho_s=rho exp(-s w), sources f_s=rho_s 1_A0,g_s=rho_s, and fixed D,
dS/ds at0 = int w[D rho grad(u).grad(v)-f v-g u].
For T=S/Z_A0, the derivative of log T is int w K, where
K=[D rho grad(u).grad(v)-f v-g u]/S +f/Z_A0.
Integral K=0, as required by invariance under a constant potential shift.
This is a standard adjoint sensitivity calculation. It suggests task-weighted
force acquisition, but neither novelty nor an end-to-end optimal design
algorithm has been established here. The L1 norm of K is a local potential
condition number; the energy angle chi can be a loose bound.

4. Possible polynomial spatial approximation route
For a flat cylinder Omega=(0,L1)xT^(d-1), zero Dirichlet on the two x1
faces, periodic transverse boundaries, L=D Delta-grad(V).grad, Hess(V)>=-J I,
weighted Bochner/Reilly integration includes a weighted boundary term:
int (L u)^2 rho = D^2 int |Hess u|^2 rho
                     +D int Hess(V)(grad u,grad u)rho
                     +D^2 int_boundary (H-(partial_n V)/D)(partial_n u)^2 rho.
Flatness sets H=0 but DOES NOT remove partial_n V. The first draft of this
lead incorrectly dropped that term; the one-dimensional integration by
parts exposes the error. If partial_n V<=0 on the absorbing faces, then
for -L u=F the favorable bound does follow:
D^2 int |Hess u|^2 rho <= int F^2 rho + D J int |grad u|^2 rho.
For arbitrary bounded normal force, a local weighted trace inequality may
absorb the adverse boundary term into half the Hessian term at the cost
O(M^2/D^2+M/(DL1)) times the gradient energy. That extension is only a lead.
Local weighted interpolation on cells with h M/D=O(1) might therefore avoid
a global exp(osc(V)/D) ellipticity factor. A complete argument still needs
precise interpolation constants, source-support bounds on the relative
energy error, feasible source-flux approximation, rational quadrature and
an end-to-end termination/resource bound. This note is not that theorem.

5. Finite control and sensor issue at a torus cut locus
For torus shortest lifts v(X-z), the implication
dist(X,Y)<=sigma => |v(X-z)-v(Y-z)|<=sigma is false at the cut locus.
One coordinate near +L/2 and the other near -L/2 gives a lift difference
near L while the torus distance is arbitrarily small. Thus a global
radial-control loss bound using Euclidean lift error cannot be assumed.
An elementary safe one-step bound chooses the observed shortest lift w and
a lift e of X-Y with |e|<=sigma. It compares V(X_next)<=|w+e+increment|^2
to V(X)=dist(X,z)^2. The initial discrepancy is at most O(R sigma+sigma^2),
R=L sqrt(d)/2, creating an O(R sigma/Delta) term in the average-distance
budget. A better bound requires a separate argument at the cut locus.

6. Periodic force balance instead of differentiating noisy positions
Set theta=2pi/L, phi_j(x)=sin(theta(x_j-z_j))/theta, h_j=cos(theta(x_j-z_j)).
Ito gives
phi_j(X_T)-phi_j(X_0)-int h_j u_j dt+D theta int sin(theta(X_j-z_j))dt
 =int h_j b_j(X_t)dt+M_T, with <M>_T<=2D T.
If the paid localization guarantees average distance<=r and theta r<1,
then A_T=int h_j dt >=(1-theta r)T. Divide the displayed statistic by A_T.
Its drift bias is <=Jr/(1-theta r), martingale error is bounded by
sqrt(4D log(2d/alpha)/T)/(1-theta r) simultaneously with a union bound.
This follows because |int h_j(b_j(X)-b_j(z))|<=J int dist(X,z).
Endpoint bounded measurement error costs2sigma/T; integrand errors involve
theta sigma times known control and D theta, rather than sigma/Delta.
Sample-and-hold quadrature, denominator error, actuator calibration, timing,
unknown D calibration and the localization controller still require payment.
This is force balance/umbrella integration, not a new physical principle.

7. Relevant real measurements acquired after the transfer question
Bryan/Basak/Bechhoefer/Presse, iScience25(9),104731 (2022),
DOI10.1016/j.isci.2022.104731, PMC9400092. Public data DOI10.5281/zenodo.6680673.
The paper used 100 barrier-initialized real traces, 5ms and500 samples each,
to infer an8.6kT barrier against a10kT design potential. The authors explicitly
say the design potential may differ from the realized potential because of
calibration/drift, and the top-of-barrier error bar is artificially low.
Therefore exp(1.4)~4.06 is only an Arrhenius sensitivity illustration; it is
NOT an observed factor4 kinetic prediction failure. Two-detector calibration
found nonzero mean difference0.28+/-0.02nm due to nonlinear calibration.
These known systematics must be retained in any reuse of the data.
