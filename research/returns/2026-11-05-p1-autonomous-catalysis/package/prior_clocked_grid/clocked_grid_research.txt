ENERGY SCHEDULING FOR CLOCK-DRIVEN GRID DISTURBANCES
Research return, 5 November 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)
=================================================

OUTCOME

This work starts with measured one-minute disturbances in electricity grids
and a documented incomplete firmware mitigation. It provides a complete
conditional mathematical scheduling construction, exact rational software
certificates, public-data reanalysis, and explicit limits. It does not
establish a new physical source mechanism, historical priority, a deployed
UPS controller, or a civilization-changing breakthrough.

The question is: when diagnostic tasks are indivisible and must retain their
service cadence, what physical disturbance can timing control remove, and
what timing, calibration, controllability and execution resources are
indispensable? The answer is proved for recurring known waveform classes,
with a complementary arbitrary-waveform certificate for selected harmonics.

The observational anchor preceded Astra retrieval. Hartmann et al., Nature
Energy, published 30 October 2026, identify minute-locked disturbances in 12
grids. Their local UPS investigation traces a self-test-related signal to the
110-kV connection and includes clock and firmware interventions. They already
discuss random scheduling and timing remedies. We use the final paper rather
than mixing its claims with the materially different October 2025 preprint.

EXISTING EXPLANATION AND SYSTEMATICS

The source authors already establish the local clocked UPS mechanism.
Resonance, control changes, effective inertia, changing demand, other clocked
devices, and measurement resolution affect the grid response. Local UPS
attribution does not establish an exclusive worldwide UPS origin. Some
unaffected-campus measurements have higher noise, so absence of a visible
line is not proof that no small source exists.

The before-update trace contains seven absent timestamps and nine additional
missing values in one channel. The extraction preserves timestamps and
missing observations. Raw MODBUS DC currents require a 0.1 conversion, but
the ampere-labelled Figure 7 columns must not be blanket-rescaled. The
three-second building averages do not resolve subsecond pulse shapes or
certify millisecond dispatch. These limitations remain in every replay.

An elementary lattice calculation explains why irregular gaps can preserve
coherence. If tasks remain at times nT+phi with stationary ergodic energy
marks Q_n of mean e, their coefficient at k/T is

    (e/T) H(k/T) exp(-2*pi*i*k*phi/T).

Randomly skipping tasks while compensating their energy leaves this line
unchanged when the kernel shape is fixed, and can add broadband noise. A
changed pulse duration changes H and must be included. The published trace
does not establish an iid marking model; this is a conditional explanation,
not a fit to three events. See lattice_randomization.txt.

CONSTRUCTION AND EXACT THEOREM

In each class c, device i performs a causal signed waveform e_i h_c once
every T, where e_i>0 and integral |h_c|=1. The full diagnostic and recharge
response at the grid-facing interface belongs in h_c. Assign

    phi_i=T(sum_{j before i} e_j + e_i/2)/E_c,
    E_c=sum_{i in c} e_i.

For the weighted event train minus its uniform mean, a primitive F_c jumps
from -e_i/2 to +e_i/2 at event i and decreases linearly between events. Its
oscillation is exactly e_max,c. Every phase schedule has a jump at least
e_max,c, so the schedule is optimal for this cumulative event-energy metric.

If every event may move independently by at most delta, then on a sufficiently
long schedule the exact one-class minimax law is

    inf_phases sup_errors osc(F)=e_max+2(E/T)delta.

The upper bound follows by counting events between t-delta and t+delta.
Matching extremes follow by advancing events near one extremum and delaying
events near a distant extremum. The exact lower bound is not asserted for
one fixed offset per device or a short observation horizon.

Convolution with a signed unit-L1 event kernel preserves the centred bound.
For fixed repeated waveform errors r_i with integral|r_i|<=eta_i,

    B=sum_c[e_max,c+2 E_c delta_c/T]
      +sum_i[(1+2 delta_i/T)eta_i]

bounds the oscillation of a primitive of actual mean-removed power. The
factor multiplying eta_i accounts for timing error acting on that residual.
Arbitrary per-cycle changes require an additional cumulative-energy contract;
an unsupported stationarity assumption cannot supply an all-time guarantee.

For a supplied stable causal response kernel g of bounded variation,

    peak incremental frequency <= (B/2)(|g(0+)|+TV(g)).

For M*f_dot+D*f=-u, M,D>0, this is B/M. Initial response and uncontrolled
disturbances remain separate. The paper's static power-frequency slope does
not identify this dynamic kernel. Different injection locations need
compatible effective-response classes or a network model. Energy bounds
alone do not control instantaneous power or RoCoF. For BV kernels there is
also a power bound, with complete proof in theorem_energy.txt.

EXECUTION AND RESOURCE BILL

The scheduler changes task timing and needs manufacturer-supported control
that preserves diagnostic validity. It acquires no new high-bandwidth pulse
actuator. Calibration must cover the complete AC response, operating state,
recharge, temperature, clock drift, and firmware state. This software cannot
substitute for those physical measurements or access rights.

Midpoint construction takes O(N) arithmetic. Each endpoint retains its phase,
period, schedule version and executed-event state; no per-cycle central
message is needed. The supplied compiler uses exact rational arithmetic,
rounds to actual timer ticks, and verifies primitive extrema at event
boundaries without temporal sampling.

Commissioning advances tests with shorter permitted intervals until the new
phase is reached. The compiler takes the old nominal phase explicitly and
checks minimum recovery and maximum deadline with 2*delta guards. Nominal
work advances by less than one period, bringing at most one nominal extra
execution into the transition window. Timing and waveform uncertainty need
their own transient reserve. The balanced steady bound is not asserted
throughout migration. If spacing is immutably T and no early or late test is
allowed, no phase migration is possible.

Phase and event state must survive reboot. A burst replay of missed tasks
defeats balancing. Mandatory immediate safety tests after a simultaneous
restart form a separate load. Membership changes require recompilation or
a dropout budget. The mean power demand is not removed by scheduling.

With Q=T/q timer slots, midpoint rounding gives event span at most e_max+E/Q
before other errors; some slot carries at least E/Q energy. Fixed timing
precision therefore does not guarantee fixed absolute accuracy as a fleet
grows on the same grid. Independent systematic waveform errors also add in
the worst case. Actual smoothing can reduce slot aliases, but must be
measured rather than inferred from an unresolved sampled waveform.
Shared errors can be less harmful: a constant common time offset only shifts
the whole schedule, and a common positive energy gain within a class simply
rescales its bound. The independent adversarial error floor is not universal
over all correlation structures or over grids whose physical response scales.

NUMERICAL EXECUTIONS

Synthetic single-class instances used 16, 64, 256, 1024 and 4096 devices with
integer weights from 5 to 100 J, T=60 seconds and a 1 ms timer. At 4096
devices, event-energy spans were: aligned 213849 J; one independent-random
draw 6335.321 J; weighted midpoint 103.3783 J. These are mathematical
instances, not UPS measurements, random-phase percentiles, or observed grid
improvements. The ideal exact bound is 100 J; timer rounding is charged.
Signed-kernel and illustrative first-order replays satisfy the bounds.

For a fabricated population of 200 replicated measured templates, K=3 and
20 allowed phases, the harmonic constructor leaves six fractional devices
before deterministic rounding. Final band RMS is 133.684 W, compared with
an independent-random root-mean-square reference of 1338.191 W. Full sampled
RMS is still 741.459 W. This verifies the implementation and the distinction
between a selected-band and whole-waveform claim. Replicas are not observed
devices or independent experimental units. The current demonstration code's
support scans give O((NM)^2+NM(2K)^3) arithmetic; an active support list can
remove the quadratic scan overhead. No exact bit-complexity claim is made.

ALTERNATIVES AND NOVELTY

The source paper already proposes desynchronization and reports an update.
Independent random fixed phases give expected squared harmonic amplitude
sum_i |c_i|^2 and may already be operationally adequate. Asymmetric converter
interleaving, distributed DESYNC, weighted fair queuing, network-calculus
jitter bounds, and Shapley-Folkman load aggregation are established. We do
not claim their discovery or an exclusive ability to mitigate this problem.

The combined resource law, compiler, whole-response bound and data checks
are a specialized construction. Priority of the exact combination remains
unestablished. The observed failure does not imply new microscopic physics.

# Energy-weighted scheduling of periodic diagnostic loads

## Status and purpose

This note proves a conditional scheduling capability motivated by the one-minute power disturbances measured by Hartmann et al., Nature Energy (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41560-026-02144-0. Their work already identifies a clocked UPS diagnostic mechanism locally, traces its propagation, proposes timing remedies, and reports a partial firmware remedy. The present contribution is an explicit mathematical construction and its implementation conditions. It is not a claim of novel mathematical priority, a newly discovered source mechanism, or completed deployment on a physical fleet.

The central quantity is cumulative excess energy, not merely one Fourier coefficient. A schedule can keep that quantity at the scale of the largest diagnostic event in each waveform class. A stable grid response of bounded variation then gives a bound on the entire frequency waveform. Clock error, waveform mismatch, commissioning, and mean-load changes must be paid separately.

## 1. Model and conventions

All nominal tests have period T > 0. Partition devices into C classes. For device i in class c, the nominal single-event power signature is e_i h_c(t), where e_i > 0 and h_c is a real, possibly signed, integrable function with integral |h_c| = 1. Physical event kernels are causal. Thus e_i has energy units. If h_c is nonnegative and integrates to one, e_i is net event energy; otherwise it is an absolute-energy scale. The complete local diagnostic waveform, including any recharge tail, belongs in h_c.

Write E_c = sum_{i in c} e_i, A_c = E_c/T, and e_max,c = max_{i in c} e_i. A device launches at nominal times nT + phi_i. Its actual launch is nT + phi_i + xi_i,n, with |xi_i,n| <= delta_i <= delta_c. Every intended event occurs exactly once. Timing changes do not change the local diagnostic operation, and operation remains valid at the permitted phases. These are physical interface assumptions requiring manufacturer confirmation.

Allow a fixed per-device waveform residual r_i, so each actual event is e_i h_c + r_i, with integral |r_i| <= eta_i. Fixed means the same residual repeats; it may have either sign and a nonzero integral. Per-cycle changing residuals require a different error assumption, described below.

The proofs first concern a bi-infinite or established schedule. Finite startup and unknown initial grid state are handled separately. All derivatives of cumulative functions can be read as distributional derivatives, allowing impulses and jumps.

## 2. Exact nominal construction

Choose any order within class c. Define S_i = sum_{j preceding i} e_j and assign

    phi_i = T (S_i + e_i/2) / E_c.

For this section suppress c. Let mu = sum_{i,n} e_i delta_(nT+phi_i). A periodic primitive of mu - A is, for 0 <= t < T,

    F(t) = sum_{phi_i <= t} e_i - A t.

Immediately before event i, F = -e_i/2; immediately afterward, F = +e_i/2. Between events F decreases linearly. Consequently

    ||F||_infinity <= e_max/2,
    osc(F) := sup F - inf F = e_max.

This is exactly optimal for cumulative event-energy oscillation. For any phase assignment, F has a positive jump of at least e_max, so osc(F) >= e_max. Coincident events only enlarge the jump. Weighted midpoints attain the lower bound without searching an optimization problem. Ordering the devices differently does not change this guarantee.

This optimality concerns the unsmoothed event-energy primitive of one class. It does not establish optimal frequency excursion for a particular grid, optimal joint cancellation between classes, or feasibility when phases are restricted.

## 3. Exact minimax effect of per-event timing error

For any nominal periodic positive-weight event train, enumerate its event times s_j and weights w_j. Define the finite local crossing sum

    J(t) = sum_j w_j [1{s_j + xi_j <= t} - 1{s_j <= t}].

Only events within delta of t contribute when |xi_j| <= delta. If F_0 is a nominal primitive, F_xi = F_0 + J is an actual primitive with the same subtracted mean A. Positivity gives the cumulative sandwich

    F_0(t-delta) - A delta <= F_xi(t)
                              <= F_0(t+delta) + A delta.

Thus osc(F_xi) <= osc(F_0) + 2 A delta. The upper bound is exact as a worst case over arbitrary bounded per-event errors on an unrestricted long schedule. Choose two distant times t_plus and t_minus such that F_0(t_plus+delta) approaches its supremum and F_0(t_minus-delta) approaches its infimum. Their radius-delta windows can be disjoint by periodicity. Advance all events relevant to t_plus by delta and delay all events relevant to t_minus by delta. Both sandwich extremes are then attained or approached. Therefore

    sup_{|xi_j| <= delta} osc(F_xi) = osc(F_0) + 2 A delta,

and consequently

    inf_phases sup_errors osc(F_xi) = e_max + 2 A delta.

For midpoint phases the chosen primitive already satisfies

    ||F_xi||_infinity <= C_c := e_max,c/2 + A_c delta_c.

Reordering across devices causes no point-process difficulty. The matching lower bound is not asserted for short finite horizons, fixed per-device offsets, or extra execution constraints that exclude the constructed errors. One device with a static offset is a counterexample to that extension: its oscillation remains e_i. A constant common clock offset translates a balanced schedule harmlessly; a time-varying common offset can temporarily change event density.

## 4. Signed waveforms and full-spectrum propagation

The nominal-shape power ripple in class c is h_c * (mu_c - A_c). A primitive is U_c = h_c * F_c. Because integral |h_c| = 1,

    ||U_c||_infinity <= C_c.

No nonnegativity, bandwidth cutoff, or minimum pulse width of h_c is needed for this cumulative bound. With several classes, U = sum_c U_c satisfies ||U||_infinity <= sum_c C_c. This controls all harmonics together; it does not discard unbalanced high-frequency terms.

The number of genuinely different classes matters. If every device needs its own class, sum_c e_max,c becomes sum_i e_i, losing the useful population scaling. Approximate grouping must retain its waveform-error bill.

## 5. Correct fixed-waveform mismatch bound

For a single device, its unit-weight nominal event train has a primitive of oscillation one and centred norm 1/2 after subtracting 1/T. Bounded per-event displacement gives a primitive G_i with

    ||G_i||_infinity <= 1/2 + delta_i/T.

The residual power train, after subtracting its own mean (integral r_i)/T, has primitive r_i * G_i. Hence

    ||r_i * G_i||_infinity <= (1/2 + delta_i/T) eta_i.

The complete mean-removed disturbance therefore has a primitive of norm at most B/2, where

    B = sum_c [e_max,c + 2 E_c delta_c/T]
        + sum_i [(1 + 2 delta_i/T) eta_i].

The factor multiplying eta_i is essential under variable event timing. Simply adding sum_i eta_i to the span bill is not justified in that setting. Fixed event-energy calibration error can be absorbed into r_i: if the only discrepancy is a_i h_c, then eta_i = |a_i|.

The removed mean is the actual mean

    P_bar = [sum_i e_i integral h_c(i) + sum_i integral r_i] / T.

Scheduling does not remove this power demand. Relative to a nominal baseline, the residual DC change is sum_i integral r_i / T and must be supplied or separately included in frequency error.

If r_i,n changes every cycle, a bound on each integral |r_i,n| alone does not imply the preceding finite cumulative bill: persistent mean bias can accumulate indefinitely. A sufficient replacement is an explicit bound osc(W) <= B_extra for a primitive W of the unmodelled, appropriately mean-removed power. Add B_extra to B. Otherwise use an appropriate direct power or stochastic bound, not this theorem.

## 6. General grid kernel and first-order specialization

Assume a common stable causal linear response g from the modelled aggregate power disturbance to the frequency observable. Require g to be an ordinary integrable bounded-variation function, finite g(0+), and g(t) -> 0. Define

    Gamma_g = |g(0+)| + TV_(0,infinity)(g).

For U' = u, integration by parts gives

    (g*u)(t) = g(0+) U(t) + integral_(0,infinity) U(t-s) dg(s).

It follows that

    ||Delta f||_infinity <= (B/2) Gamma_g.

Adding a constant to U leaves the identity unchanged. Signed or oscillatory g is allowed, but resonance can enlarge its total variation. A Dirac feedthrough is excluded and would require an additional instantaneous-power bound.

For M df/dt + D f = -u, with M,D > 0,

    g(t) = -(1/M) exp(-Dt/M),
    Gamma_g = 2/M,
    ||Delta f||_infinity <= B/M.

M has units W s/Hz. A DC modelling error d adds equilibrium shift -d/D; a grid-control response may differ. RoCoF is not bounded by event energy alone because df/dt contains instantaneous u/M. The guarantee is the incremental contribution of the controlled fleet, not total frequency in the presence of arbitrary external disturbances.

Different injection locations generally have different transfer kernels. A scalar common g requires an appropriate coherent-grid approximation or an independently bounded kernel mismatch. A full network needs effective-kernel classes, error terms, or multichannel scheduling. The empirical paper's static power-frequency characteristic does not identify Gamma_g. Nonlinear operating limits also require separate validation.

## 7. Finite power and waveform bandwidth

If each h_c, extended by zero outside its physical support, is of bounded variation on the real line, differentiation under convolution yields

    ||u_model||_infinity <= sum_c C_c TV_R(h_c).

This counts endpoint jumps. For example, a rectangular normalized pulse of width w has TV_R(h)=2/w. If each r_i is also BV, add

    sum_i (1/2 + delta_i/T) TV_R(r_i).

An L1 error eta_i alone does not bound instantaneous residual power: its energy can be concentrated into a very narrow spike. Finite slew, converter limits, voltage constraints, and diagnostic validity remain device-level requirements. The schedule changes event timing, not the event waveform, so it requires no new high-bandwidth pulse actuator.

## 8. Service contract and constructive commissioning

Let physical successive launch gaps for device i be required to lie in [T_min,i, T_max,i]. T_min must include pulse duration and recovery time. For independent bounded launch errors, a sufficient steady-state guard is

    T_min,i + 2 delta_i <= T <= T_max,i - 2 delta_i.

Nominal T=60 s with a hard actual maximum of 60 s and arbitrary nonzero jitter is not certified by this guard. One must reduce the nominal interval or enforce a dependent deadline controller and recheck its timing bound.

An actual bridge from old phase a_i to target phase b_i is possible when L_i = T - T_min,i - 2 delta_i > 0. Let d_i = (a_i-b_i) mod T in [0,T). For d_i>0 choose m_i = ceil(d_i/L_i). Following a completed old-schedule test, use m_i nominal intervals of length T-d_i/m_i, then resume nominal period T. The total advance is d_i, giving phase b_i. Every bridge interval respects the same guarded gap limits; the first bridge interval is referenced to the stored old nominal time, with the previous launch error included.

This uses the identical diagnostic operation. It temporarily accelerates tests and advances less than one period of work per device. If exact spacing T is mandatory, migration is impossible without changing that contract. No scientific argument can assume away this service limitation.

The steady balanced bound is not automatically a bound throughout migration. Relative to the old event stream, a device advancing by less than T can advance at most one event's cumulative work at any instant. A conservative transition reserve must therefore account for the old disturbance and up to the total event energy of the migrating fleet, plus timing and waveform errors. The coordinator must check that transient envelope or commission before energization; the tighter steady certificate begins only after the bridge and inherited waveform tails are accounted for.

For the first-order grid, a residual state error x at a settled handover time contributes |x| exp[-D(t-t_h)/M]. Add this to B/M. For a higher-order plant, one frequency sample does not determine hidden governor states: use a state-error envelope or a justified decay bound. Infinite waveform tails require a quantified tail bound rather than an assumed finite clearing time.

## 9. Calibration, timers, and persistence

Commissioning needs the actual input-power signature at the grid-facing interface, not battery current alone. Record waveform families, absolute event energies, allowed phase windows, test gap limits, timing jitter, and operating-state variation. These measurements produce e_i, h_c, eta_i, and delta_i. Repeatability across SOC, temperature, load, and firmware state is part of the claimed uncertainty envelope. The mathematical note does not supply those missing physical measurements.

Computing midpoints requires O(N) arithmetic, O(N) stored phase assignments, and no repeated optimization. Each device stores its phase, period, schedule version, persistent identity, and executed-event sequence state; runtime work per test is constant. Use monotonic execution deadlines and idempotent event IDs. Clock corrections must preserve offsets and avoid duplicate execution or a catch-up burst. An atomic version transition must not silently substitute UTC-minute boundaries for commissioned phases.

If Q clock slots exist per period, some slot carries at least E/Q energy. Thus worst-case cumulative norm is at least max(e_max,E/Q)/2 for narrow events. Nearest-slot rounding of the midpoint schedule gives at most e_max/2 + E/(2Q), before other errors. Timer resolution q=T/Q therefore has an explicit price; phase memory needs at least ceil(log2 Q) bits. Slot aliases can leave higher-frequency comb components even when the minute fundamental is suppressed, which the cumulative bound already includes.

Differential clock error can be budgeted as initial synchronization error plus drift-rate bound times resynchronization interval plus dispatch jitter. Fixed precision does not give a fleet-independent absolute guarantee as E grows on a fixed grid. A common static offset is harmless. Anonymous deterministic devices with identical reset state and common inputs cannot break symmetry; stored unequal assignments or independent randomness are indispensable. A deterministic hash alone has no worst-case guarantee over arbitrary selected fleets.

Calibration uncertainty also has a sharp floor. If Fourier coefficients have independent adversarial uncertainty disks |Delta c_i|<=rho_i, every phase schedule has worst-case coefficient magnitude |sum_i c_hat_i exp(-ik phi_i)| + sum_i rho_i. Rotation cannot shrink the disks. Do not claim cancellation of systematic uncertainty without a justified correlation model or aggregate calibration.

## 10. Failures and interpretation

Missing tests, repeated tests, unbounded clock steps, corrupted phases, mean-load changes, and prolonged outages invalidate particular premises. Bound their additional cumulative disturbance explicitly and add it; otherwise suspend the certificate. Permanent device removal also changes mean power. Arbitrary corruption of a fixed positive fraction of the fleet cannot retain a fleet-independent absolute guarantee without detection, rebalancing, or reserve resources.

Persistent phases permit ordinary reboot recovery only if policy permits waiting for a scheduled test. If every device must test immediately after a simultaneous restart, that burst cannot be removed by stored offsets while preserving the immediate-test requirement. A powered-off device cannot meet a continuous diagnostic deadline. These are interface and resource constraints, not implementation details to omit.

Random fixed independent phases remain a strong benchmark: for harmonic k their aggregate has expected squared amplitude sum_i |c_i,k|^2. For a sufficiently large fleet this may already meet operational needs. Other alternatives include asymmetric interleaving, stronger primary frequency control, and local energy buffering. The proposed advantage is a deterministic cumulative guarantee with simple timer-only deployment under its stated class structure, not exclusive access to mitigation.

Irregular task intervals do not imply incoherent physical forcing
================================================================

This elementary signal-theory calculation explains a mitigation failure
already reported by Hartmann et al. (2026). Neither the calculation nor the
physical mechanism is claimed as historically new.

Let launches remain confined to nT+phi. Let Q_n be stationary ergodic
nonnegative energy marks with mean e. All tasks share a real integrable
kernel h with H(f)=integral h(t) exp(-2*pi*i*f*t) dt. Form

    p(t)=sum_n Q_n h(t-nT-phi).

After removing boundary tails, the long-record complex coefficient at k/T is

    C_k=(e/T) H(k/T) exp(-2*pi*i*k*phi/T).

At f=k/T every lattice factor exp(-2*pi*i*k*n) is one. The coefficient over
L periods equals H(k/T) exp(-2*pi*i*k*phi/T) times sum(Q_n)/(LT), plus a
vanishing boundary term. Ergodicity gives the displayed limit. Thus variance
in inter-event gaps, or skipping many cycles, does not itself remove a line.
A spectral zero of H, phase diversification, or mean energy reduction is
needed to remove that line in this model.

For iid Q_n of variance sigma_Q^2, the time-averaged two-sided PSD is

    S_p(f)= (sigma_Q^2/T)|H(f)|^2
          + sum_{k in Z} (e^2/T^2)|H(k/T)|^2 delta(f-k/T).

Here f is cycles/second and R_p(tau)=integral S_p(f) exp(2*pi*i*f*tau) df.
The cyclostationary process is averaged over one clock period. Correlated
marks can change the continuous term and add further structure.

If tests are retained independently with probability r and energy e/r,
Q_n=(e/r) B_n with B_n Bernoulli(r), then sigma_Q^2=e^2(1-r)/r. Mean work per
period remains e. With a fixed kernel the line is unchanged and broadband
noise increases as r falls. If compensation lengthens the pulse, the new
H_r must be used. The line may then change. This does not claim that the
published firmware update left harmonic amplitude unchanged. Random skipping
also cannot meet a finite hard maximum inter-test interval almost surely.

The public after-update trace has starts at elapsed 86,145,506 s, or 26,25,26 s
modulo 60. It confirms phase concentration at one-second sample resolution,
but does not establish iid marks, ergodicity or an asymptotic PSD. Supplementary
Note 9 already documents phase locking, longer pulses and partial mitigation.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-026-02144-0, Supplementary Note 9.
Data version: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21997337.

DETERMINISTIC HARMONIC SCHEDULING OF CLOCKED POWER ROUTINES
Independent mathematical construction, error audit, and prior-art boundary
Prepared for /root on 2026-11-05. Internal scratch note, not an empirical validation.

1. EMPIRICAL TARGET AND WHAT IS ALREADY EXPLAINED

Hartmann et al., One-minute frequency disturbances from unintended
synchronization in cyber–physical power systems, Nature Energy, published
30 October 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41560-026-02144-0, identify clock-synchronized
software routines in UPS/battery systems as a source of minute-periodic
power peaks. Their evidence includes propagation across voltage levels and
a manufacturer software update. Random scheduling, adaptive offsets, and
event-driven control are already proposed by the empirical paper. The
causal mechanism itself is not our discovery.

The specific mathematical question here is: given measured, heterogeneous
periodic power signatures and an allowed start-time interface, can one
assign one fixed start phase per device so that selected aggregate Fourier
components are bounded by a few individual-device contributions rather
than by the number of devices? What calibration, timing, and deadline
conditions make that statement physically meaningful?

This note supplies a finite constructive answer conditional on that
interface. It does NOT prove that all UPS firmware exposes the required
interface, certify the hardware measurements, or demonstrate a field
deployment. It also does NOT provide a population-independent guarantee
under fixed arbitrary errors at every device.

2. MODEL AND FOURIER CONVENTION

T is the routine period (60 s in the motivating observation), and
omega = 2*pi/T. Device i has a T-periodic real incremental power signature

  p_i(t) = c_i0 + 2 Re sum_{k>=1} c_ik exp(i*k*omega*t).

The nominal signature is translated by the chosen offset tau_i; it is not
amplitude-modulated, shortened, interrupted, or removed. Each required
routine runs once per period after deployment. The required assumption is
that changing its start phase leaves its signature unchanged within a
specified error budget. State-dependent changes, local controller
interactions, and replenishment/recharge effects must be included in that
budget, not treated as zero by definition.

For K selected harmonics,

  S_k = sum_i c_ik exp(-i*k*omega*tau_i),  1 <= k <= K.

The retained-band RMS power is sqrt(2 sum_k |S_k|^2). Its peak magnitude is
at most 2 sum_k |S_k|. These factors of two follow from the complex Fourier
coefficient convention and must not be dropped.

Take M > K allowed phases tau_m = m*T/M, m=0,...,M-1. More generally the
devices can have different allowed phase sets, but uniform feasibility
then requires a separate check.

Choose nonnegative weights w_k, for example w_k=1 for source-power RMS or
w_k=|H(k*omega)|^2 for a known stable linear frequency response. Define a
real d=2K dimensional vector

  v_im = (sqrt(w_1) Re[c_i1 exp(-2*pi*i*m/M)],
          sqrt(w_1) Im[c_i1 exp(-2*pi*i*m/M)], ... ).

The letter i in exp denotes the imaginary unit, while i indexing a device
is conventional; implementations should use unambiguous variable names.

Every phase vector for device i has the same Euclidean norm

  B_i^2 = sum_{k=1}^K w_k |c_ik|^2.

Because M>K, (1/M) sum_m v_im = 0 for every device independently, regardless
of its waveform, harmonic phases, or amplitude. This is the only property
that makes the initial zero target feasible without further optimization.

3. EXACT EXISTENCE AND CONSTRUCTION THEOREM

Theorem. Under the model in section 2, one phase m_i can be assigned to
each device so that

  sum_k w_k |S_k|^2 <= sum_{i in F} B_i^2 <= sum_largest_min(N,2K) B_i^2,

where F is a constructively obtained set containing at most 2K devices.
Consequently,

  ||S||_w <= sqrt(2K) max_i B_i,

and the retained-band RMS source power is <= 2 sqrt(K) max_i B_i when
w_k=1. For equal bounds |c_ik| <= C this is at most 2*K*C in RMS power.
The first inequality can be sharpened by replacing B_i^2 with the actual
rounding variance B_i^2 - ||b_i||^2, defined below.

This is an application of Shapley–Folkman/Starr convexification plus
conditional-expectation rounding. Neither ingredient is claimed new.

Proof, first stage (fractional representation).
Start with x_im=1/M. It obeys

  x_im >= 0; sum_m x_im=1 for every i; sum_im x_im v_im=0.

An extreme point of this compact feasible polytope has no more than N+d
positive entries, since there are N simplex equalities and at most d
independent coupling equalities. Every device has at least one positive
entry, and every fractional device has at least two. Thus at most d
devices remain fractional. Rank deficiency only improves this bound.

A direct elimination algorithm provides the same conclusion without
treating a generic LP solver as a black box:

  (a) While more than d devices have two or more positive weights, select
      d+1 of them and two positive options a_i,b_i in each selected row.
  (b) The d+1 vectors v_i,a_i-v_i,b_i are linearly dependent in R^d. Find a
      nonzero null vector alpha.
  (c) Change x_i,a_i by t*alpha_i and x_i,b_i by -t*alpha_i. All simplex
      sums and all aggregate harmonics are preserved.
  (d) Move t to a nonzero endpoint of the interval preserving
      nonnegativity. At least one previously positive weight becomes
      zero. No new nonzero weight is introduced.

There are at most N*(M-1) eliminations. Stop when at most d rows are
fractional. Pure rows already specify their final physical phases.

Proof, second stage (deterministic rounding).
For fractional row i define b_i=sum_m x_im v_im. If independently selecting
Y_i=v_im with probability x_im, then E Y_i=b_i and

  E ||sum_i (Y_i-b_i)||^2
    = sum_{i in F} (B_i^2-||b_i||^2).

The pure-row contributions plus sum_i b_i are zero, so the expression on
the left is exactly the squared norm of the final aggregate. At most d
variance terms remain.

Randomness is not required in implementation. Let s be the sum of
already-fixed rounding errors. For the next fractional device select the
positive-weight phase m minimizing ||s+v_im-b_i||^2. Its value cannot exceed
the average over x_im. The conditional expectation of the final squared
norm therefore never increases. After every row is fixed, the claimed
deterministic bound holds.

The actual vector norm or per-harmonic residual obtained by the algorithm
should be reported rather than merely the conservative worst-case bound.

4. FINITE PRECISION IMPLEMENTATION

The algebra above is exact. It must not be quietly interpreted as infinite
precision physical timing or exact measured Fourier coefficients.

One explicit numerical construction uses a rational mesh h. Round every
coordinate of each v_im to the nearest multiple of h, obtaining r_im, and
then center each device's vectors:

  vtilde_im = r_im - (1/M) sum_l r_il.

The rational vectors have exactly zero uniform average. Since the true
uniform average is zero, every coordinate differs from the true one by
at most h; hence ||vtilde_im-v_im|| <= sqrt(d)*h.

Apply the exact rational fractional construction and rounding to vtilde.
The real-vector aggregate differs from the rational aggregate by at most
N*sqrt(d)*h, and the maximum vector norm has increased by at most
sqrt(d)*h. A fully explicit bound is therefore

  ||S||_w <= sqrt(d)*(B_max+sqrt(d)*h) + N*sqrt(d)*h.

Choose h to put this last numerical term below the allocated numerical
error. Required coefficient precision grows only logarithmically with N
for a fixed absolute numerical budget. This is a computational precision
cost; it does not remove the potentially much larger physical calibration
or timing cost.

The straightforward elimination procedure costs O(N*M*d^3) arithmetic
operations with dense nullspace calculations; conditional rounding costs
O(d^2*M). For fixed K,M this is linear in N in arithmetic-operation count.
Do not advertise the same bound as an exact bit-complexity theorem for a
naive rational implementation. Standard rational LP algorithms can find a
basic feasible point in polynomial bit complexity. In practical floating
point implementations, recompute the final sums using interval or
otherwise bounded-error arithmetic and certify the result directly.

Memory is O(N*K) measured complex coefficients plus up to O(N*M)
fractional weights. Each endpoint stores one phase index (ceil(log2 M)
bits), period and scheduler state. Offline calibration and assignment need
communication; routine execution subsequently needs no per-period
communication under fixed membership and valid clocks.

5. WHAT IS IMPROVED OVER RANDOM PHASES

Independent uniform phases give

  E sum_k w_k |S_k|^2 = sum_i B_i^2.

Thus random static assignment has typical retained-band residual of order
sqrt(N)*B for comparable devices. The deterministic construction bounds
the same quantity by sqrt(2K)*B under arbitrary measured waveform
heterogeneity. This is a genuine mathematical difference in the stated
model, but not evidence of a field performance improvement.

A fixed randomly assigned fleet is still exactly T-periodic. Its ensemble
mean harmonics vanish; the realized fleet generally retains nonzero
lines. Randomizing every period spreads energy into sidebands and has a
different deadline/timing analysis; it is not interchangeable with the
static comparison above.

For identical common-template waveforms, ordinary equally spaced
interleaving already cancels the first M-1 harmonics exactly when group
counts are equal. Even unequal positive amplitudes have a stronger simple
whole-waveform construction. If p_i(t)=a_i p(t), A=sum_i a_i, place phases
at weighted quantile midpoints:

  tau_i/T = (sum_{j<i} a_j + a_i/2)/A.

The weighted CDF differs from uniform by at most a_max/(2A). Integration
by parts gives

  sup_t |sum_i a_i p(t-tau_i) - A*pbar|
    <= (a_max/2)*TV_circle(p).

For a unit-height rectangular pulse TV_circle(p)=2, so the whole-waveform
error is <=a_max. This is classical discrepancy/quadrature reasoning.
It is the relevant comparator in the common-template case. The harmonic
construction earns waveform heterogeneity and multiple outputs, not an
advantage over optimal interleaving when all waveforms are identical.

6. PHYSICAL ERRORS, INCLUDING THE POPULATION-SCALING OBSTRUCTION

Let chat_ik be the calibrated coefficients used for scheduling. At run
time suppose |c_ik-chat_ik| <= e_ik. Write actual timing as the intended
tau_i plus a common clock offset plus relative offset eta_i, where
|eta_i|<=delta_i. Let D be missing devices. A common offset merely rotates
all contributions in harmonic k and does not affect its magnitude.

For the remaining devices the actual harmonic satisfies

  |Sactual_k| <= |Shat_k| + E_k,

  E_k = sum_{i not in D} e_ik
      + sum_{i not in D} |chat_ik| min(2,k*omega*delta_i)
      + sum_{i in D} |chat_ik|.

The timing term can be sharpened to
2 sin(min(k*omega*delta_i/2,pi/2)) in place of min(2,k*omega*delta_i).
Unmodeled newly arriving devices require an additional sum of their
harmonic amplitude bounds. Common multiplicative calibration gains in a
harmonic preserve cancellation; the error budget concerns relative
mismatch. Missing q devices cost at most q*C_k if |chat_ik|<=C_k.

The retained-band RMS bound is then

  sqrt(2 sum_k (|Shat_k|+E_k)^2),

or the corresponding weighted expression for known transfer gains.

Clock error between resynchronizations separated by R seconds can be
budgeted as initial relative synchronization error + relative drift rate
times R + timer quantization/execution jitter. A hardware claim needs
actual bounds for these terms. The common-mode part is harmless to
cancellation. Independent or correlated differential errors are not.

Critical limitation: fixed arbitrary per-device errors destroy an
absolute N-independent guarantee. Arbitrary complex coefficient errors of
radius e can align after phase shifts and produce N*e residual. Even real
amplitude errors in [-e,e] have worst-case residual at least (2/pi)*N*e
for equal-amplitude sources (with e smaller than the nominal amplitude).

There is an equally direct timing lower bound. Let the nominal scheduled
fundamental contributions a_i all have magnitude C and sum_i a_i=0. Give
each device a timing error eta_i=-sigma_i*delta, sigma_i in {-1,+1}.
Then

  Sactual_1 = i*sin(omega*delta) sum_i sigma_i*a_i.

Averaging the absolute projections of a_i over a direction in the complex
plane gives (2/pi)*sum_i |a_i|. Therefore some choice of signs obeys

  |Sactual_1| >= (2/pi)*N*C*|sin(omega*delta)|.

This is exact, not merely a first-order approximation. Thus bounded
absolute residual for arbitrary independent device errors requires
precision that improves with population, an independently justified
aggregate-error bound, a shared timing/calibration structure, or reserve
control that pays for the aggregate error. A fixed per-device timing
tolerance alone is insufficient. Stochastic errors can give sqrt(N)
scaling under stated independence assumptions but cannot be substituted
for an adversarial guarantee.

If jitter changes from cycle to cycle, the output has sidebands and the
static Fourier formulas alone do not certify all spectral energy. One can
apply them to per-cycle Fourier projections only with an explicit window
convention and no uncontrolled edge spillover. For a shifted BV waveform,
the global L1 translation error is <= |eta|*TV(p); for a smooth waveform,
the L2 translation error is <= |eta|*||p'||_2. Nonsmooth square edges have
L2 translation error proportional to sqrt(|eta|), so a derivative-based
bound must not be applied to them without smoothing.

7. DEADLINES, ALLOWED OFFSETS, AND DEPLOYMENT

The full-period phase freedom is an operational requirement, not a free
mathematical trick. With device-dependent allowed phase sets Phi_i, zero
is feasible exactly when

  0 belongs to sum_i conv{v_i(phi): phi in Phi_i}.

This is a finite LP feasibility question after discretizing allowed
phases. If feasible, the same <=2K fractional-device proof applies. If it
is infeasible, a separating vector lambda supplies a lower bound:

  delta = sum_i min_{phi in Phi_i} lambda dot v_i(phi) > 0
  implies every physical assignment has norm >= delta/||lambda||.

Example: all original fundamental coefficients have the same phase and
positive magnitudes, and every allowable start lies in the same window of
width W<T/2. Rotating to the window midpoint yields

  |S_1| >= cos(pi*W/T) sum_i |c_i1|.

So tightly synchronized deadlines can enforce a macroscopic coherent
disturbance; randomization cannot overcome this geometry either.

If no zero target is feasible, choose any feasible aggregate target y and
apply the construction while preserving y. Conditional-expectation
rounding gives

  ||S||^2 <= ||y||^2 + sum_{i in F} (B_i^2-||b_i||^2).

Selecting y as the closest point of the convexified aggregate set gives a
useful best-relaxation-plus-rounding certificate, with numerical tolerance
explicitly added.

Changing an existing schedule must not silently violate its maximum
inter-test interval. A naive delay can create a gap approaching 2T.
Instead advance successive test times: use intervals T-a_j, with
0<=a_j<=T-h_min, where h_min is a required minimum recovery/refractory
interval. Sum the advances to the required cyclic phase advance, less
than T. This takes ceil(advance/(T-h_min)) transition intervals if h_min<T
and preserves both h_min and maximum gap T. If h_min=T, no phase change
is possible without an extra test or a late test; additional operational
permission/slack is indispensable.

Stagger the commissioning transitions themselves or certify their
temporary aggregate power. Store the offset persistently and run the
routine using a monotonic scheduling clock. Do not shift the system wall
clock. Restoring a device at its stored phase avoids accidental common
catch-up bursts. A device outage can invalidate its own test deadline;
this construction cannot guarantee tests while the device is unavailable.
The advance-only transition can bring at most one additional execution
per device into a finite commissioning window compared with the original
schedule, because the total advance is less than one period. Its energy,
recovery interval, and aggregate transient must still be paid. Equal
steady-state work per period does not make commissioning power free.

8. CALIBRATION, HIGHER HARMONICS, AND GRID RESPONSE

The method needs per-device or per-controllable-group signatures, not just
aggregate grid frequency. If measurement error in one period is bounded
pointwise by e_i(t), coefficient error is bounded by
(1/T) integral e_i(t) dt. Statistical averaging needs an actual dependence
and noise model; samples from a single correlated trace are not independent
replicates by default.

For uniform time samples with spacing Delta, if the waveform is smooth
with |p_i'|<=L_i and |p_i|<=P_i, a left-Riemann coefficient estimate has
quadrature error <= (L_i+k*omega*P_i)*Delta/2. For discontinuous pulses use
a bounded-variation quadrature bound instead; do not invent a finite
pointwise derivative. Sampling, meter calibration, and state variation
must all enter e_ik.

Suppressing the first K harmonics is NOT a whole-waveform peak guarantee.
The harmonics k>=K+1 can remain coherent and may scale with N. A bound on
the actual frequency response requires a separately controlled tail, for
example

  output peak tail <= 2 sum_{k>K} sum_i |H(k*omega)*c_ik|,

when that sum converges. Fourier coefficients of a BV pulse decay only as
1/k, so absolute summability of source coefficients is not automatic.
Additional transfer-function decay or a whole-waveform construction is
needed. RMS-tail bounds also require a measured or proved tail budget.

The source cancellation theorem itself does not require knowledge of grid
inertia. A frequency-response claim does require a valid transfer model
or a certified measured gain. It is not a nonlinear grid-stability proof.
If device locations have different transfer paths to monitored sites,
stack the complex coefficients H_lik*c_ik for L monitored outputs. The
dimension becomes 2*K*L, the fractional-device bound becomes 2*K*L, and
all response-model uncertainty must be included. Aggregate source-power
cancellation at one boundary alone does not certify feeder voltage or
every local flow.

the construction mechanics on one generated instance; it is not evidence

1.58e-13; actual residual was 5.472818121 in single-device coefficient
units, above the proved lower bound 5.333235856. This illustrates why the
ideal cancellation certificate cannot omit differential timing error.
