1. EXACT TWO-LEVEL EQUIVALENCE

The collision event must count all incoming photons over a rail's entire
FINITE active source-time window, including startup, the field-coupling guard,
and any local readout interval during which the main coupling remains nonzero.
On the complement, every rail's joint field and memories have total excitation
at most one. All capture couplings and the local XY readout conserve excitation
number. Therefore lowering operators and their adjoints have exactly the same
reachable matrix elements in the qubit and oscillator descriptions. This is
an exact joint-isometry equality, not a reduced-state or mean-field claim.

Nonzero delays do not change the equality. Each node is a causal unitary on its
own initialized memory and the arriving field. Retarded envelopes implement the
same sequence in source-time coordinates. Earlier and later field slices are
still genuine propagating systems; none is assumed stored without hardware.

The good-sector statement is preserved under arbitrary input-reference and
inter-rail entanglement. The splitter collision estimate is an input-operator
bound because the marked Weyl-jump probabilities are scalar on the source.
Thus the 4sqrt(p_bad) complete-channel comparison is valid and unconditional.

2. REQUIRED ORDER OF THE COMPLETE CHANNEL COMPARISON

The Poisson photon-number cap is exact for the ACTUAL source output. The
normalized at-most-one-jump-per-site/bin comparison is conditioned on event G.
Its photon-cap tail would be at most p_num/Pr(G), not automatically p_num.
The following hybrid order retains the existing constants without that factor:

  (a) If readout has an infinite actuator tail, first truncate that physical
      readout interaction after a finite final time, charging its bounded
      Hamiltonian tail. This is explained in section 5 below.
  (b) Replace the actual finite-window qubit cascade by the oscillator
      comparison using the uniform rail-collision bound.
  (c) Charge the occupation cutoff and compare extracted W modes with the
      exact physical-time bin modes on the ACTUAL source output. The uniform
      Poisson cap is available here, including startup and guard photons.
  (d) Only then apply the full-dilation initial-edge, Hamiltonian-splitting,
      and normalized-G comparisons through exact-bin extraction and the
      fixed extended decoder. These are now ordinary postprocessing
      contractions of the already proved source-isometry bounds.

Every processing channel is defined on overflow, multi-photon and collision
sectors, even when its desired interpretation is used only in a comparison
subspace. Thus this ordering introduces no postselection or undefined action.
Alternatively one may retain the opposite order and explicitly pay the factor
1/Pr(G), but doing so is unnecessary.

The W-versus-bin principal-angle argument is sound. Initial finite memory vacuum
coordinates make the complete captured modes orthonormal, although their field
pieces alone are not. The Gram matrix is the correct compression of the
target-bin projector. Its polar factor W=P_N E(E^dagger P_N E)^(-1/2) is a
physically stored orthonormal family. A nearby one-particle unitary is an
analysis comparison only; it is not a required operation on unavailable field.

3. PROPAGATION, TIMING, AND BANDWIDTH SCOPE

A logical balanced splitter tree has depth log_2 P. At fixed hardware density
this is not automatically O(log P) physical latency. The physical note's safe
planar construction has root-to-leaf path O(aP), rail path O(aN), and total wire
O(ma[P log P+PN]) for m original ports. Longest in-module propagation is
O(a(P+N)/v), plus fixed component delays and external delivery distance.

The processor note had an old resource line O(N tau_link+log P tau_split).
Its author agreed to replace that line by actual link delays and the safe
fixed-density bound. No gate algorithm changes are needed.

Unknown small path-delay errors can be represented as shifts of the local
scheduled envelope in retarded coordinates, and charged by the envelope slew.
For the retained-memory/source channel, freely relabeling a discarded output
environment is also allowed in a Stinespring comparison. This does NOT prove
a uniform fixed-coordinate time-shift bound on arbitrary unlimited-bandwidth
output field states. The latter is neither true nor needed for the target
retained channel; wording should distinguish them.

Static co-propagating directional couplers with bounded local one-particle
coefficient give the claimed splitter in the declared dispersionless model.
They do not establish a microscopic all-frequency material model. A bounded
local linewidth or controller pole is distinct from a fixed aggregate hard
optical passband. Increasing rails/cells increases the total collective
response and is paid hardware. The new same_weyl_hard_bandwidth_audit.txt gives
a direct obstruction if the aggregate inaccessible frequency subspace is fixed.

4. EXPLICIT POLYNOMIAL MAJORANT: FIXED-DURATION LOCAL READOUT

This removes any circular appeal to monotone doubling when the main capture
coupling is generated by the three-pole controller in the physical note.

Use its notation:
  B: fixed capture-controller pole;
  g0: maximum capture amplitude;
  t1: end of useful source-time interval;
  tau_rd: fixed duration of the compact smooth local readout pulse;
  G: capture-tail guard before readout;
  A=t1+G+tau_rd: full active source-time window;
  m: number of physical source ports, individual rate nu, total R=m nu;
  K: number of useful time bins, width Delta;
  alpha: a positive lower bound on kappa on the useful interval;
  K1: upper bound on |kappa'| there;
  eps_mode, eps_rail, eps_tail: error allocations;
  p_num: photon-cutoff tail allocation.

Define dimensionless z=1+BA. The useful-window alpha and K1 do not depend on G.
The entire post-turnoff coupling area is bounded, so a uniform Laguerre-domain
bound is

  X0=g0^2[t1+33/(16B)].

Choose the photon cap Q(A)=ceil((exp(1)-1)RA+log(1/p_num)), increased to at
least one. With

  q0=1+(exp(1)-1)R/B+log(1/p_num),

one has Q(A)<=q0 z. Set z0=16q0^2/eps_mode^2. The Gram deficit choice
zeta=eps_mode^2/(16Q(A)^2) satisfies zeta^(-1)<=z0 z^2.

Use the conservative physical-note mode bound and round upward. It gives

  M(A)<=M0 z^4,

  M0=1+K+256X0 K^2 z0^2/(alpha^2 Delta^2)
             +4X0 K K1^2 z0/alpha^4+2X0 K z0.                 (4.1)

The power-of-two rail choice satisfies

  P(A)<=P0 z^2,
  P0=2+16m(nu/B)^2/eps_rail^2.                               (4.2)

Therefore the number of capture nodes D=mPM obeys

  D(A)<=D0 z^6,        D0=mP0M0.                             (4.3)

Let F(g0,L) be the explicit passive readout perturbation function in section
11 of the physical note:

  F(g0,L)=g0 L+2sqrt(L)(1+g0^2L)
                         +(L/sqrt(2))(2g0+g0^3L).

Set

  a=1+B(t1+tau_rd),
  C=8F(g0,tau_rd)g0[(R/B)a+1/2]/eps_tail,
  c=a+4log(max{exp(1),CM0}).

The required guard inequality is implied by

  z>=c+16log z.                                               (4.4)

An explicit solution is

  z_star=2c+512,
  A_star=(z_star-1)/B,
  G_star=A_star-t1-tau_rd.                                    (4.5)

Indeed log z<=sqrt(z) for z>=1, and z=2c+2(16)^2 satisfies
z>=c+16sqrt(z). Thus it satisfies (4.4). Provision Q,M,P using A_star and use
G_star. Then the guard formula holds for the actually provisioned M(A_star).
There is no unresolved recursive definition. Every displayed coefficient is
polynomial in the supplied finite-accuracy parameters and fixed-response
hardware inverses; logarithms only improve this statement.

5. BOTH CAPTURE AND READOUT WITH FIXED CONTROLLER POLES

If the local readout itself has an exponential actuator tail, it must not be
treated as finished at its command-end time. Here is a complete explicit
closure of that optional stronger operational model.

Let the readout remaining area after command-end plus w obey

  a_tail(w)<=C_rd exp(-B_rd w/2),

where B_rd is fixed. A three-pole response to a bounded rectangular readout
setpoint Omega_* of fixed duration tau_cmd, with Omega_* tau_cmd=pi/2,
has total known area pi/2 and permits C_rd=4Omega_*/B_rd. Its amplitude,
slew and controller poles are fixed.

Use the same z=1+BA and majorants Q<=q0z, M<=M0z^4, D<=D0z^6, now with A
including the finite readout-tail wait w. Put

  K_rd=max{exp(1),4C_rd D0/eps_rd},
  w(z)=(2/B_rd)[log K_rd+6log z].                             (5.1)

Then 4D(A)a_tail(w(z))<=eps_rd. The factor four pays two distinct effects:
first compare the ACTUAL qubit architecture with readout Hamiltonian zero
after the finite final time, costing <=2D a_tail by bounded-Hamiltonian
Duhamel, independent of arbitrarily many future field photons; separately,
the reference readout is short of its ideal pi/2 pulse area by at most a_tail,
costing another <=2D a_tail. Only after this physical tail truncation does one
apply the finite-window rail collision argument. Counting an infinite tail's
incoming photons instead would incorrectly make collisions certain.

The main field coupling remains nonzero during the ENTIRE finite readout
interval L=tau_cmd+w(z). Its perturbation bound must use F(g0,L), not the
constant F(g0,tau_cmd). Define

  Lbar=tau_cmd+(2/B_rd)[log K_rd+6],
  Fbar=F(g0,Lbar).

Since log z<=z and z>=1, L<=Lbar z. Every power of L in F is at most two, so

  F(g0,L)<=Fbar z^2.

Using sqrt(RA+(RA)^2)<=RA+1/2<=(R/B+1/2)z, the main coupling-tail error is
at most

  4M0 Fbar g0(R/B+1/2) z^7 exp(-BG/2).

Therefore choose

  K_tail=max{exp(1),4M0 Fbar g0(R/B+1/2)/eps_tail},
  G(z)=(2/B)[log K_tail+7log z].                             (5.2)

Finally put

  a=1+B(t1+tau_cmd),
  c=a+2log K_tail+(2B/B_rd)log K_rd,
  d=14+12B/B_rd,
  z_star=2c+2d^2,
  A_star=(z_star-1)/B.                                       (5.3)

Again z_star>=c+dlog z_star. Hence

  t1+tau_cmd+G(z_star)+w(z_star)<=A_star.

Provision Q,M,P from the upper window A_star, use the explicit guards (5.1)
and (5.2), and compute the actual finite capture kernels for those schedules.
The count and mode majorants remain valid even when the actual total duration
is shorter than A_star. Both controller-pole branches are thus constructive
with polynomial hardware and finite time; no monotone-search termination
assumption remains.
