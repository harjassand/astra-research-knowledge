DILUTED TWO-LEVEL ABSORBER CASCADES, FINITE RISE/FALL RESPONSE,
RETARDED PROPAGATION, AND COHERENT LOCAL READOUT

Date: 2026-11-04
Revision: planar fixed-density wire/delay costs and the passive readout
perturbation proof clarified in this note only.
Status: constructive interface theorem within the specified calibrated

SCOPE

This note earns a physical field-to-stationary-register operation. It does not
assume that the incident field has already been buffered, discretized, counted,
or converted into qubits. The actual incident Weyl ports enter a specified
passive splitter network followed by specified two-level absorbers. All
receivers start independently of the unknown source. Their local coupling
operators, local Hilbert-space dimensions, reset rates, readout strengths and
control response times can be bounded independently of n.

Accuracy is instead paid with more rails, more absorbers, longer guards,
preparation/calibration time, stationary memory, and subsequent quantum
processing. The output-only reverse-reset theorem and the source-specific
digital decoder are separate composable results. A fixed nonzero uncontrolled
loss or error floor in the uncoded capture hardware is not silently repaired
by later quantum error correction.

The rail-dilution construction was independently supplied by the constructive
collaborator working on strict local norms. The bounded digital-processing
branch supplies the subsequent occupation-register decoder. This note gives
the physical input, freezing, leading-edge, delay, preparation and readout
proofs, plus explicit calibration and actuator-tail budgets.

1. PHYSICAL INPUT AND PARAMETERS

The source has n q-level sites and the actual calibrated pure-vacuum Weyl
ports

  L_(i,mu) = (sqrt(r)/q) W_(i,mu),  mu=1,...,q^2-1.

The ports themselves, with their coherent phases and labels, are delivered.
The reduced generator alone would not supply this interface. Let

  m = n(q^2-1)                  number of original physical ports,
  nu = r/q^2                   photon intensity per original port,
  R = m nu = nr(1-q^(-2))       total physical photon intensity.

The source Hamiltonian may be continuous, interacting and time dependent as
in the main theorem. It does not change these counting intensities because
each L_(i,mu)^dagger L_(i,mu)=nu I. Conditional physical jump histories are
products of normalized Weyl unitaries and source propagators. Therefore,
for every source/reference input, photon counts in a window of duration A
have the same Poisson distribution as prescribed classical constant-rate
clocks. No actual counting measurement is made by this receiver.

Let t0>0 be a small leading interval omitted from the target recovery window.
The useful window is [t0,t1], where t1=t0+T and T is the main theorem's
required recovery horizon. The receiver nevertheless interacts with the
physical field from source time zero. The initial emitted output need not
have been vacuum before zero.

Each original physical port is coherently split into P equal rails. Each rail
has M two-level capture nodes. Each capture node has an adjacent initially
empty stationary storage qubit. Denote

  D = m P M                    number of capture nodes.

There are D capture qubits and D storage qubits, before optional protected
memory and digital-processing overhead.

2. STATIC COHERENT DILUTION: FIXED LOCAL DIMENSION

Choose P to be a power of two if desired. A fixed balanced binary tree of
50:50 passive beam splitters implements

  b_in^dagger(f) -> P^(-1/2) sum_(p=1)^P b_p^dagger(f),

with the other inputs in actual vacuum. The tree uses P-1 beam splitters per
original port and has LOGICAL depth log_2 P. This is not a claim of O(log P)
physical radius or delay at fixed hardware density. Section 5 gives a safe
planar layout with root-to-leaf path length O(aP) and total tree wire length
O(aP log P), where a is a fixed component/spacing scale.

The splitter tree needs no time-dependent switch or measurement. Known phases
and actual path delays are incorporated into the calibrated rail coordinates.
Equal path lengths are convenient but not necessary when the individual
delays and phases are calibrated.

For any m_ph-photon state in one original port, with arbitrary temporal
correlations, the probability that some rail contains at least two photons
is at most

  m_ph(m_ph-1)/(2P).

Reason: in first quantization the splitter applies the same path vector
P^(-1/2) sum_p |p> to every photon. Each unordered photon pair has probability
1/P to share a rail. The union bound applies independently of the temporal
wavefunction or entanglement with the source/reference.

For Poisson intensity nu and a window of duration A,

  p_bad <= m (nu A)^2/(2P)
        = n(q^2-1) r^2 A^2/(2 q^4 P)
        <= n r^2 A^2/(2P).                                  (2.1)

On the complement, every entire rail contains zero or one photon over the
whole active window. A chain of initially ground-state two-level absorbers
then has EXACTLY the same dynamics as a chain of bosonic linear absorbers:
there can never be two excitations in any one absorber. This is an exact
single-excitation statement, not an uncontrolled low-intensity replacement.

Embed the qubit model in the oscillator Fock space. Its unitary agrees with
the oscillator unitary on the good incoming sector. On the bad sector the
difference of two isometries has norm at most two. Thus the source-to-receiver
channel replacement error is at most

  4 sqrt(p_bad).                                             (2.2)

This is uniform for arbitrary source/reference inputs. It is not a
postselection protocol. A sufficient choice for rail error eta_rail is

  P >= 8 m nu^2 A^2 / eta_rail^2,                            (2.3)

rounded up to a power of two. The actual device remains a fixed-size
(two-level) device even on the bad sector; that sector is simply included in
the approximation bound.

For the compact finite switch-off model below, A may be the freeze time t2.
For the exponential-tail model, A must include the subsequent local readout
interval during which the capture nodes still have a residual field coupling.

3. ACTUAL LOCAL CAPTURE MODULE

Each capture node is a two-level system with

  L_j(s)=g(s) sigma_j^-,

in a frame rotating at the fixed carrier frequency. The controlled coupling
satisfies

  ||L_j(s)||=|g(s)| <= g0,
  ||dL_j/ds||=|g'(s)| <= S0,

where g0 and S0 can be fixed constants. The principal construction has fixed
local Hilbert dimension and does not hide a sqrt(occupation) growth inside an
oscillator lowering operator.

The nodes are connected by actual one-way, calibrated, coherent feedforward
paths. The local field coupling and the local storage-qubit coupling are the
specified physical operations. No effective dense many-node Hamiltonian is
assumed as a primitive.

For the moment work in a common retarded source-time coordinate s. On the
single-excitation rail sector, write a_j for the equivalent oscillator
annihilation coordinate. A chain of M nodes with the same envelope obeys

  da_j = -g(s)^2 a_j ds/2
         -g(s)^2 sum_(k<j) a_k ds - g(s) dB_in(s).

Let

  kappa(s)=g(s)^2,
  u(s)=integral_0^s kappa(v) dv,
  U=u(t2),
  x(s)=U-u(s),
  A0=-I/2-J_lower,

where J_lower has ones strictly below its diagonal. The exact solution is

  a(t2)=exp(A0 U) a(0)
        -integral_0^t2 g(s) exp(A0 x(s)) 1 dB_in(s).           (3.1)

A direct induction, or the Laguerre differential recurrence, gives

  [exp(A0 x)1]_j=exp(-x/2) L_(j-1)(x).

Hence the actual stored field kernels are

  f_j(s)=-g(s) exp[-x(s)/2] L_(j-1)(x(s)).                   (3.2)

This is a derived field-to-stationary-mode operation, not an assumed
transducer. It is valid for a varying common envelope, including finite
turn-on and switch-off intervals.

4. INITIAL VACUUM COORDINATES: NO INFINITE PREHISTORY

The functions exp(-x/2)L_j(x) form an orthonormal basis on x in [0,infinity).
However, the physical integral in (3.1) only ranges over source times
[0,t2], corresponding to x in [0,U]. The missing part of the canonical mode
is supplied by the FINITE M-mode initial vacuum vector a(0).

Indeed

  A0+A0^dagger=-11^dagger,

so

  integral_0^U exp(A0 x)11^dagger exp(A0^dagger x) dx
     = I-exp(A0 U)exp(A0^dagger U).                          (4.1)

Thus (3.1) preserves the canonical commutators in the oscillator comparison,
with the remaining covariance supplied exactly by the initially empty modes.
Equivalently one may extend the x-domain beyond U using a mathematical
vacuum. This is a representation of the finite initial vacuum coordinates,
not an infinite preparation time, an incident rising-exponential photon
extending to minus infinity, or access to earlier source output.

In the physical two-level implementation all capture nodes start in their
ground state. The oscillator comparison is used only on the good rail sector.
The finite preparation protocol is specified in Section 9.

5. NONZERO PROPAGATION DELAYS AND WHAT IS STORED

Let d_(p,j) be the calibrated total delay from the original source port to
node j of rail p, including the splitter paths and preceding interstage
links. Program the physical node envelope as

  g_(p,j)(t)=g(t-d_(p,j)),

with known phase compensations. The capture node starts its local interaction
when its source-time-zero field slice arrives, and it freezes at physical
time t2+d_(p,j) in the compact-switch model.

The delayed network has exactly the same stored-mode map as (3.1), up to the
known phases. To see this without assuming zero-delay coupling, regard each
node as a unitary on its own initial mode and the passing field window.
Gates at different nodes acting on different temporal field slices commute.
The actual causal network can therefore be topologically reordered into the
spatial composition U_M(g)...U_1(g). A pure propagation delay only relabels
the field time and adds its known phase. This gives the same cascade transfer
kernels as the retarded-coordinate calculation.

Each node is isolated after its own prescribed local freeze time. At the
common physical time t2+d_max, all stored coordinates coexist and are
ordinary commuting local registers. Any free carrier phases accumulated
between local freeze times and this common time are known and can be included
in the digital mode-conversion circuit.

By t2+d_max every source-time slice at or before t2 has either entered a
stationary register or left the last node. No unprocessed field between
nodes is asserted to be stored at no cost. The paths have real length and
real transit time. In general d_max is computed from the ACTUAL calibrated
path lengths, group velocities and fixed component delays; logical tree
depth does not determine it.

A safe fixed-density planar construction is explicit. Take P=2^L leaves at
pitch 2a. Put the nodes at each logical level in a separate column, columns
spaced a apart, and center each parent on the vertical interval of its
descendant leaves. Connect parent to child by a vertical-then-horizontal
Manhattan path inside that subtree's interval. Subtree intervals at a given
level are disjoint, so the tree is planar. Constant-size bends and couplers
only change fixed factors. Every root-to-leaf path has the same vertical
length a(P-1) and horizontal length a log_2 P. The sum of vertical wire
lengths is aP log_2 P and the horizontal sum is O(aP). Thus the safe costs per
original port are

  equal fan-out path length:  O(aP),
  total splitter-tree wire: O(aP log P),
  total P-rail wire:         O(aPM),
  longest rail length:       O(aM).

The P length-M absorber rails continue from the leaves at fixed pitch, so
they have a planar layout as well. For fixed component dimensions and fixed
propagation speed v (including fixed component transit delays in the
constants), the in-module delay is O(a(P+M)/v), not O(log P/v). Any external
source-to-module delivery distance must be added separately. Across m
original ports the total internal wire length is O(ma[P log P+PM]); the
original-port modules themselves also need an explicit overall placement.

Early stationary registers must remain coherent for the actual delay or be
encoded into protected memory immediately after their local readout. No
zero-delay limit, fixed-dimensional exponential packing, or uncharged path
equalization is used.

A source-time slice before zero sees every receiver coupling equal to zero.
Thus an already-running source is allowed: its older output passes through
without preparing or entangling the receivers. Source vacuum INPUT ports do
not imply a vacuum OUTPUT prehistory, and this construction does not use that
incorrect implication.

6. COMPACT FIXED-RISE-TIME ENVELOPE AND THE LEADING EDGE

A simple exact input-output model uses g0=sqrt(kappa_c) and fixed positive
rise/fall durations tau_on,tau_off. Assume t1>=tau_on, enlarging T by a
constant if needed. Set

  g(s)=0                                      for s<=0,
  g(s)=g0 sin^2(pi s/(2 tau_on))                for 0<s<tau_on,
  g(s)=g0                                     for tau_on<=s<=t1,
  g(s)=g0 cos^2(pi(s-t1)/(2 tau_off))           for t1<s<t2,
  g(s)=0                                      for s>=t2,
  t2=t1+tau_off.

This envelope is continuously differentiable. It has

  |g|<=g0,
  |g'|<=pi g0/[2 min(tau_on,tau_off)],

independent of n, M, P and accuracy. There is no physical modulation at the
small digital bin width used later by the decoder. The pulse shape and its
feature time are fixed; only the array size and the later processing grow.

The two ramp areas are 3 kappa_c tau_on/8 and 3 kappa_c tau_off/8. Thus

  U=kappa_c[t1-5 tau_on/8+3 tau_off/8].

The whole switch-off interval is included in the exact kernels (3.2). It is
not discarded as a supposedly negligible interval with O(R tau_off) photon
probability. Its photons are ordinary extra input modes, separated from the
useful prefix by the later mode extraction.

At the leading edge the coupling begins at zero while the receiver is
independently initialized. Retain only the useful output window [t0,t1].
On this interval

  kappa_min >= kappa_c min{1,(t0/tau_on)^4},
  max|kappa'| <= 2 pi kappa_c/tau_on.                          (6.1)

For inverse-polynomial t0, these are polynomially controlled bounds.

The source marginal over [0,t0], after tracing the early output, is

  E_early=exp(-R t0) U_H + [1-exp(-R t0)] N,

where U_H is the known Hamiltonian propagator and N is a channel. Therefore

  ||E_early-U_H||_diamond <=2[1-exp(-R t0)]<=2R t0.            (6.2)

Recovering the source state at t0 and then applying U_H^dagger on the
receiver gives an input-recovery error bounded by (6.2), in addition to the
later acquisition/decoding errors. A conservative full-dilation bookkeeping
bound is 2sqrt(2R t0). The choice

  t0=eta_edge^2/(8R)

is safe for that stronger bookkeeping too. The physically early photons need
not be detected or thrown away at an abrupt hardware boundary: the later
mode extraction selects modes supported on [t0,t1] and discards the rest.

7. EXPLICIT WARPED-BIN LAGUERRE APPROXIMATION

This section closes the finite rise-time regularity issue. It does not replace
the source-output approximation or digital decoder proved in the other notes.

Partition [t0,t1] into K bins of length Delta=T/K. A normalized physical-time
bin mode is f(t)=Delta^(-1/2)1_bin(t). In the x coordinate its normalized
function is

  h(x)=f(t(x))/sqrt(kappa(t(x))).

Let X=U, kappa_min be the minimum on the useful interval, and
K1=max|kappa'| there. Smooth each indicator linearly inside its two edges,
using physical-time width w<=Delta/2. If h_s is the transformed smoothed
function, then

  ||h-h_s||_2^2 <= 2w/(3Delta),
  ||h_s'||_2^2 <= 4/(kappa_min^2 Delta w)
                    +K1^2/(2kappa_min^4).                    (7.1)

For the derivative, use dx=-kappa(t)dt and

  dh_s/dx = -[f_s'/kappa^(3/2)
                 -f_s kappa'/(2kappa^(5/2))].

The Laguerre functions phi_j=exp(-x/2)L_j(x) diagonalize

  A f=-(x f')'+x f/4,
  A phi_j=(j+1/2)phi_j.

For a smoothed function supported in [0,X], its quadratic form is at most
X(||h_s'||_2^2+||h_s||_2^2/4). Projection onto the first M Laguerre functions
therefore gives

  ||(I-P_M)h||_2^2
    <=4w/(3Delta)
       +[8X/(kappa_min^2 Delta w)
          +X K1^2/kappa_min^4+X/2]/(M+1/2).                 (7.2)

The sum of these K mode tails bounds the bin Gram deficit. For target
summed deficit zeta_G, a conservative explicit choice is

  w=Delta zeta_G/(8K),

  M >= 256 X K^2/(kappa_min^2 Delta^2 zeta_G^2)
       +4 X K K1^2/(kappa_min^4 zeta_G)
       +2 X K/zeta_G.                                      (7.3)

All terms are polynomial in the required finite-accuracy parameters because
(6.1) is inverse-polynomial after the small initial omission. No increase of
local coupling strength, local dimension or rise/fall bandwidth is used.
The exponents are conservative and large; this is an existence/resource
construction, not a useful device-economics estimate.

8. PHYSICAL READOUT: A LOCAL COHERENT SWAP, NOT A NEW FIELD BUFFER

Give each absorber an adjacent storage qubit initially in |0>. Once the
main capture coupling is zero, apply

  H_rd(s)=Omega(s)(sigma_capture^+ sigma_store^-
                     +sigma_capture^- sigma_store^+).

Its operator norm is |Omega(s)| on the entire two-qubit Hilbert space. A pulse
with integral Omega ds=pi/2 swaps the vacuum/one-excitation state into the
storage qubit, up to a known phase. This preserves every source/reference
correlation. The capture qubit is returned to ground on the ideal good rail
sector, and the storage qubit is physically separate from the incident field.

One fixed bounded pulse is

  Omega(s)=Omega_* sin^2(pi s/tau_rd),  0<=s<=tau_rd,
  tau_rd=pi/Omega_*,

zero outside that interval, with Omega_* chosen no larger than the specified
strength bound and the square root of the specified slew bound. Its peak is
Omega_*, its maximum slew is Omega_*^2, and it has the required pulse area.
Its time scale is independent of n. The known excitation phase can be
absorbed into the subsequent digital decoder.

This is an actual local readout operation. It does not release the unknown
state into another flying mode and then assume a second perfect absorber.
The resulting stationary qubits are the input to the separately constructed
bounded-rate quantum processor. Alternatively the capture qubits themselves
can be processed after isolation, but an adjacent storage qubit gives a
clean physical separation from the continually incident source field.

9. INDEPENDENT FINITE PREPARATION

Before each node's first interaction, keep its main coupling zero and reset
its capture and storage qubits through isolated cold ports

  L_prep=sqrt(gamma_p) sigma^-,   gamma_p<=gamma_0.

The preparation ports are distinct from the incident signal paths. No
preparation radiation is injected into a downstream signal rail.

For Q=2D qubits, independent amplitude damping for time tau_p has a uniform
reset-channel error bounded by

  2sqrt(2Q) exp(-gamma_p tau_p/2).

This follows from the amplitude-damping Stinespring overlap with pure reset,
and remains valid with arbitrary external references. Thus it suffices to use

  tau_p >= gamma_p^(-1) log(8Q/eta_init^2).                   (9.1)

Fixed-duration ramp guards can be included before the main interaction.
Preparation of node j can be scheduled before its own retarded start time,
so long physical propagation delays do not demand instantaneous local reset.
All controls used in preparation have bounded local amplitudes and response
rates. The total number of prepared qubits, cold-port uses and heat-disposal
operations grows with Q; those are hardware resources.

If an actual preparation actuator has an exponential off-tail, turn it off
early enough that its integrated residual coupling during data storage fits
the explicit local-noise budget. Waiting O(log(Q/eta))/B_p at a fixed response
pole B_p suffices. A fixed finite-temperature reset error or unmodeled drift
is not assumed to vanish merely by naming an ideal ground state.

10. WHAT 'BOUNDED BANDWIDTH' MEANS

The compact envelope of Section 6 earns a bounded amplitude, bounded slew,
and fixed local feature/rise time. It does not have compact Fourier support.
A nonzero strictly Fourier-bandlimited function cannot vanish on an open
half-line and then turn on, nor can it turn off exactly at a finite time.
Thus hard spectral support and exact finite-time switching must not both be
claimed.

A stronger operational fixed-response model is available. A stable three-pole
actuator with fixed pole rate B can produce, from a bounded setpoint pulse,

  F3(x)=1-exp(-x)(1+x+x^2/2),

  g(s)=g0[F3(Bs)-1_(s>=t1) F3(B(s-t1))],   s>=0,
  g(s)=0,                                   s<0.             (10.1)

Its poles, peak and slew are independent of n. The actual quantum coupling
is this known smooth response, not an instantaneous command switch. Extra
fixed command-rise filtering may be included and only changes fixed powers
in the leading-edge estimates.

Before turnoff, for 0<t0<=1/B,

  kappa_min >= g0^2 exp(-2)(B t0)^6/36,                      (10.2)

because F3(x)=integral_0^x exp(-v)v^2/2 dv. Also max|kappa'|
can be bounded by 2g0^2 B. The generalized estimate (7.3) therefore still has
polynomial cost.

For s=t1+u after turnoff,

  |g(s)| <= g0 exp(-Bu)[1+Bu+(Bu)^2/2]
          <=2g0 exp(-Bu/2).                                 (10.3)

Moreover

  integral_(t1)^infinity |g(s)|^2 ds
      <=33g0^2/(16B).                                       (10.4)

The effective x-domain length stays O(g0^2[t1+B^(-1)]), independent of how
long a tail guard is used. The next section gives a finite source-channel
error bound for reading out while this residual coupling remains nonzero.
This is a fixed-response bandwidth statement, not a hard Fourier cutoff.

11. FINITE RESPONSE TAIL: A COMPLETE READOUT ERROR BOUND

A useful exact perturbation estimate applies to one passive module, including
its local coherent readout Hamiltonian. Suppose two field-coupling waveforms
g,h on a duration L obey

  |g|,|h|<=G,  ||g-h||_infinity<=delta_g,

and the same passive internal Hamiltonian is used in both. Their complete
one-particle input-output unitaries satisfy

  ||U_g-U_h|| <= F(G,L) delta_g,

  F(G,L)=G L+2sqrt(L)(1+G^2 L)
                    +(L/sqrt(2))(2G+G^3 L).                 (11.1)

Proof, including the actual absorber-to-store readout: on the vacuum plus
one-excitation sector, the local XY exchange acts as a Hermitian two-by-two
one-particle Hamiltonian h_rd(t). Write the coupling row and internal drift as

  C_g(t)=(g(t),0),
  A_g(t)=-i h_rd(t)-C_g(t)^dagger C_g(t)/2.

The same h_rd(t) is used in the actual and reference readout. Let Phi_g(t,s)
be the internal propagator generated by A_g. Since

  A_g+A_g^dagger=-C_g^dagger C_g <= 0,

  d||Phi_g(t,s)v||^2/dt = -||C_g(t)Phi_g(t,s)v||^2 <= 0,

we have ||Phi_g(t,s)||<=1 even while the readout Hamiltonian is active. The
Hamiltonian need not be small and need not commute with the coupling. No
exp(||h_rd||L) loss is necessary. For the two coupling waveforms g,h,

  ||A_g-A_h||<=G delta_g,
  ||Phi_g(t,s)-Phi_h(t,s)||<=G delta_g(t-s),

by Duhamel and contraction of both propagators. The common readout
Hamiltonian cancels from A_g-A_h.

The complete one-particle input-output unitary has four blocks: Phi_g(L,0);
the field-to-internal kernel -Phi_g(L,s)C_g(s)^dagger; the internal-to-field
kernel C_g(t)Phi_g(t,0); and the field block I minus the Volterra kernel
C_g(t)Phi_g(t,s)C_g(s)^dagger for s<t. Their difference norms are bounded by

  internal block:      G L delta_g,
  each cross block:    sqrt(L)(1+G^2 L) delta_g,
  field block:         (L/sqrt(2))(2G+G^3 L) delta_g.

For the last block use the Hilbert-Schmidt bound on the time triangle, whose
area is L^2/2. Adding the four blocks proves exactly (11.1). It is valid for
the local absorber-plus-store readout used here, with the SAME function F.

The hypothesis is passive, excitation-preserving internal evolution. An
active drive that creates excitations would require a different argument;
none is used for this coherent readout. The physical two-qubit evolution
agrees with this one-particle module on the good rail sector, and the
separate collision bound handles its complement.

Consider the actual three-pole capture response (10.1). At

  t2=t1+G_guard

begin the local bounded readout pulse of duration tau_rd. Compare it with a
reference module that has the identical capture dynamics until t2 and has
zero main-field coupling during readout. The only difference has supremum

  delta_g <=2g0 exp(-B G_guard/2).

The common prefix can be factored out, so in (11.1) take L=tau_rd rather than
the entire writing interval. M stages multiply this one-particle error by
at most M. Different rails and ports are direct sums and do not add another
one-particle factor of P or m.

For a passive one-particle unitary difference delta_U, second quantization
obeys ||Gamma(U)-Gamma(V)||<=k delta_U on the k-photon sector. The source's
full photon count through

  A=t1+G_guard+tau_rd

is Poisson(RA). Orthogonal photon sectors consequently give the complete
source-channel bound

  eta_tail_actual
    <=2 M F(g0,tau_rd) delta_g sqrt(RA+(RA)^2).               (11.2)

It is uniform for arbitrary source/reference inputs. The same extension to
physical two-level absorbers is valid on the good rail sector, and the
collision error (2.2) is separately charged using this enlarged A.

For an explicit guard, put

  A0=R(t1+tau_rd+B^(-1))+1/2,
  F0=F(g0,tau_rd).

Using sqrt(x+x^2)<=x+1/2 and (1+x)exp(-x/4)<=2 for x>=0, a sufficient choice is

  G_guard=(4/B) log max{e, 8 M F0 g0 A0/eta_tail}.            (11.3)

Thus the actuator pole B remains fixed; accuracy costs a logarithmic waiting
guard. Formula (11.3) has already resolved the guard's appearance inside the
Poisson second moment.

After the local readout pulse ends, the storage qubit has no interaction with
the incident field. A remaining capture-coupler tail can interact with the
old absorber indefinitely without changing the stored register's marginal
state. There is no need to assume the source stops emitting.

If the readout Hamiltonian itself has an exponential actuator tail, let its
remaining pulse area after a chosen final time be a_tail. Its entire future
effect on one storage pair is at most 2a_tail in diamond norm, independent of
future source traffic, because the exchange Hamiltonian has norm at most its
coefficient. Design the total known pulse area to be pi/2, and wait until the
uncompleted area and the remaining possible disturbance together obey

  4D a_tail <= eta_readout_tail.

A fixed response pole reaches this condition in O(log(D/eta_readout_tail))
additional time. Include that finite time in A and in the tail guard budget.
There is no exact hard-bandlimited finite-time freeze assumption here either.

12. CALIBRATION, TIMING AND LOCAL NOISE BILL

For compact switching, let A be the full local writing/readout duration and
mu2=RA+(RA)^2. The same one-particle proof gives a conservative waveform
calibration error bound

  eta_wave <=2 M F(g0,A) delta_g sqrt(mu2),                  (12.1)

in addition to the rail-collision budget. A retarded-clock error delta_t
changes the coupling by at most S0 delta_t, so it can be included through

  delta_g <=delta_amplitude+S0 delta_t.

If every splitter layer has one-particle unitary error at most delta_S, its
contribution is at most

  2 log_2(P) delta_S sqrt(mu2).

Likewise known path phases can be compensated, while phase-calibration
errors add through the path depth. A pure delay error affects the scheduled
envelope in retarded coordinates; it is not necessary to bound a time-shift
operator uniformly over arbitrary unlimited-bandwidth field inputs.

The required amplitude, phase and timing accuracies are inverse-polynomial
for a polynomial-size design. Their specification needs polynomially many
bits. Fixed feature time does not mean fixed timing/phase precision.
Calibration/stability resources must be paid; for a stationary noisy
calibration experiment, more bounded-intensity probes and more time can
improve statistical precision without increasing coupling strengths or
response bandwidth. A fixed uncontrolled drift floor is not covered by
this statement.

The links and splitter outputs must be actual coherent calibrated paths.
Propagation delay and total wire length are paid in Section 5. In particular,
propagation loss and stability requirements must be charged against actual
line length, not merely the number of abstract graph edges. Loss, dispersion
outside the calibrated model, uncontrolled dephasing, and thermal excitation
are separate errors.
For instance, a conservative finite-dimensional local-generator budget is

  eta_uncontrolled <= sum_devices integral 2||H_error(t)||dt
                       +sum_noise_ports integral 2||C_error(t)||^2dt,

when C_error represents an added inaccessible Lindblad coupling. Gate/channel
error budgets may instead be summed by ordinary complete-channel telescoping.
These are sufficient conservative budgets; some passive loss bounds can be
sharper because few rails are excited.

Storage protection after local readout may use a separately specified standard
fault-tolerant architecture. The uncoded input-capture section itself still
needs its uncontrolled-loss budget to be small. A fixed loss before the
unknown quantum information has been encoded is not automatically repaired
by downstream quantum error correction.

A common exact spectral blind spot in all proposed couplers cannot be repaired
by increasing the number of identical devices: a photon mode wholly in that
blind subspace never enters memory. Thus this theorem specifies actual
Markov input-output couplers and calibrated transport for the source modes;
it is not a claim about arbitrary devices sharing a reduced decay rate or an
unspecified finite passband.

13. PHYSICAL RESOURCE SUMMARY

For given M,P and a source-time window A:

  Signal hardware:
    m(P-1) static splitter components;
    m P M two-level capture nodes;
    m P M adjacent storage qubits;
    specified one-way calibrated links with total count O(m P M+mP);
    safe planar internal wire length O(ma[P log P+PM]) at fixed pitch a.
    External delivery paths and the placement of the m modules are additional
    specified geometry, not zero-cost connections.

  Local quantum coupling strengths:
    capture ||L||<=g0, fixed;
    capture slew <=S0, fixed;
    readout Hamiltonian norm <=Omega_*, fixed;
    readout slew <=Omega_*^2, fixed;
    preparation rate <=gamma_0, fixed.

  Local control response:
    fixed compact-ramp feature times, or the specified fixed three-pole
    actuator rate B with an explicit finite-error tail guard.

  Initialization:
    O(log(D/eta_init))/gamma_p preparation time in parallel;
    O(D) cold-port/reset uses and isolated auxiliary resources.

  Source-to-last-node transit:
    parameterize d_max by actual calibrated path lengths and velocities;
    a safe fixed-density planar module has equal fan-out paths O(aP),
    length-M rails O(aM), and delay O(a(P+M)/v);
    external source-to-module delivery delay is added separately;
    logical splitter depth log_2 P is not a physical-radius/delay bound.
    This is real latency, not an assumed zero-delay global interaction.

  Readout:
    one bounded local exchange pulse per capture node;
    constant duration for the compact pulse model;
    logarithmic extra guard if actual readout tails must be bounded.

  Protected storage:
    O(D) stationary qubits before the digital decoder;
    early readouts wait for the rest of the array and for processing;
    memory errors during that lifetime require an explicit budget or QEC.

  Control/calibration precision:
    inverse-polynomial coefficient, phase and timing errors sufficient;
    no shrinking waveform feature time or increasing local response pole.

  Total parallel drive/reset resources:
    grow with D; bounded per-device strength does not mean bounded total power,
    wiring, cold-bath capacity, or total hardware.

The root source-output approximation and the constructive quantum processor
supply polynomial M and polynomial decoding cost. Equations (2.3), (6.1),
(7.3), and (11.3) show that the additional rail, initialization, switching,
propagation and readout requirements are also polynomial. Logarithmic guard
feedback can be handled by integer parameter enlargement; no exponential
Hilbert-space decoder or unbounded local quantum control is introduced.

14. ORDINARY CONSTRUCTIONS AND WHAT IS BEING CLAIMED

A single passive cavity perfectly writes specially matched temporal modes,
not every arbitrary finite-front wavepacket. Linear-network zero-dynamics
memory theory already characterizes matched absorption and the separation
between writing, storage and reading. The present derivation uses that
ordinary input-output machinery; it does not claim the first passive quantum
memory or the first switched storage mode.

The extra operation earned here is an approximation to a specified set of
arbitrary finite-window modes of an actual continuously emitted quantum field,
with independent receiver initialization, a paid leading edge, finite local
response, and bounded local Hilbert dimension. Rail dilution extends the
single-excitation linear dynamics to the required multiphoton source channel
with a uniform error bound. The full source-specific computational consequence
requires composition with the separate Gram/source-output and digital-decoder
proofs.

A standard direct source-register SWAP is faster when clean coherent source
access is granted. That access is absent in the output-only task. Conversely,
a conventional shaped-photon link can use a much smaller matched receiver
when the sender controls the waveform. The present source emission is the
actual fixed Weyl-port dilation, so matched-waveform source control is not
silently granted.

A permanently coupled finite stable passive receiver eventually re-emits its
excitations. It is not a stationary memory merely because it temporarily
delays a photon. Here the finite switch/readout operation is explicit and its
latency is paid. Nor is a field buffer assumed: the field-to-register map
is equation (3.1) implemented by the specified physical chain.

16. CONCLUSION OF THE CONSTRUCTION

Within the stated calibrated pure-port, passive-scattering and local-control
model, there is a real continuous-field-to-stationary-qubit interface with
bounded local quantum coupling norms, fixed local dimension, and fixed local
rise/response time. It starts independently of the unknown source, includes
the leading edge, includes finite switch-off or actuator tails, accommodates
nonzero physical propagation delays, and has a bounded-strength physical
readout path. Polynomial hardware and latency replace the diverging peak
controls of a single small chronological collector.

The scientific claim is this conditional constructive interface and its
composability. It is not a claim of useful device economics, fixed per-node
noise tolerance before encoding, recovery from arbitrary inaccessible baths,
or a new source operation hidden inside receiver hardware.
