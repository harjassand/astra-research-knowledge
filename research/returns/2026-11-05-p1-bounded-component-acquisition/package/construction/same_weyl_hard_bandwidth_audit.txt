FIXED AGGREGATE HARD-BAND ACCESS: ACTUAL WEYL-SOURCE CONVERSE
2026-11-04, independent audit of root's construction

This addendum extends the collection-loss audit to a fixed inaccessible
frequency complement of the SAME actual Weyl source. It is separate from the
Lorentzian amplitude-emission example, whose stronger bandwidth dependence
belongs to a different source. All prior notes are unchanged.

1. MODEL AND GENEROUS ACCESS

Take a qubit source with H=0 and calibrated coherent vacuum ports

  L_x=sqrt(r)X/2, L_y=sqrt(r)Y/2, L_z=sqrt(r)Z/2.

The source channel is depolarizing at rate r, and its initial state is an
arbitrary unknown qubit, potentially entangled with a reference. Consider
emission over a finite interval [0,T]. Extend each output field to all real
times with independent vacuum outside [0,T]. Grant the receiver EVERY Fourier
mode with |omega|<=B, with arbitrary noncausal access and arbitrary processing.
The orthogonal Fourier complement and the source remaining at T are
inaccessible.

This is a deliberately generous information-theoretic access model. It does
not assert a physically causal exact brick-wall filter. A causal receiver
whose accessible one-particle subspace is contained in this band has no more
information and obeys the same converse. Finite T gives finite photon counts;
no infinite-time steady-radiation Fock state is used.

2. LOST MEAN FIELD

For source input |+Z> or |-Z>, the Z output mean is plus/minus

  m_T(t)=(sqrt(r)/2) exp(-rt)1_[0,T](t).

Use the Fourier convention 1/sqrt(2pi). Then

  |m_T_tilde(omega)|^2
    =r |1-exp(-(r+i omega)T)|^2/[8pi(r^2+omega^2)].             (1)

Let P_out project onto |omega|>B and define a normalized inaccessible mode

  f=P_out m_T/||P_out m_T||,
  B_f=integral f(t)^* b_Z,out(t)dt,
  mu^2=||P_out m_T||^2.

When mu=0 the stated bound is trivial. Otherwise this mode has canonical
commutator [B_f,B_f^dagger]=1 and means plus/minus mu.

If T>=1/r, then for every omega,

  |1-exp(-(r+i omega)T)|^2 >=(1-exp(-1))^2.

Integrating (1) over |omega|>B gives

  mu^2 >= [(1-exp(-1))^2/8] h_B,
  h_B=1-(2/pi)arctan(B/r).                                   (2)

The factor 1/8 is the full squared norm of the infinite-time mean function;
it is not the norm of a one-photon emission wavepacket.

3. UNIFORM SECOND MOMENT AND DISTINGUISHABILITY

Within [0,T], the normally ordered correlation is

  <b_Z,out^dagger(t)b_Z,out(s)>=(r/4)exp(-r|t-s|).              (3)

It follows by source regression: the adjoint depolarizing semigroup sends
Z to exp(-ru)Z and Z^2=I. The kernel exp(-r|t-s|) on [0,T] has norm at most
2/r by the Schur test. Although the lost Fourier mode f need not be supported
in [0,T], its restriction there has norm at most one. Outside the interval
the field is the appended independent vacuum. Therefore

  N_f=<B_f^dagger B_f><=1/2.

For Q_f=(B_f+B_f^dagger)/sqrt(2), Cauchy-Schwarz implies

  <Q_f^2><=N_f+1/2+sqrt[N_f(N_f+1)]<2.

Its two input-dependent means differ by 2sqrt(2)mu. Applying Cauchy-Schwarz
to the two quadrature probability measures, with dominating measure their
sum, gives

  ||p_plus-p_minus||_1 >= 2mu^2.

Hence the inaccessible environment states distinguish the initial Z inputs
by trace distance D>=2mu^2. For any deterministic Bell recovery with
infidelity e, Choi Uhlmann followed by reference-Z measurement gives
D<=4sqrt(e). Thus

  e>=mu^4/4
   >=c_B h_B^2,
  c_B=(1-exp(-1))^4/256.                                     (4)

For T<1/r, the inaccessible source alone has Z-input trace distance
2exp(-rT)>2/exp(1). It gives e>exp(-2)/4, stronger than (4). Therefore
(4) holds uniformly over every finite deterministic acquisition time T.

4. COLLECTIVE AND HERALDED CONSEQUENCES

For n independent sources, their Bell-output Choi states are products. The
optimal deterministic Bell-recovery SDP tensorizes exactly:

  d F_opt(rho)=min{TrY: rho<=I_R tensor Y, Y>=0}.

Primal and dual tensor-product feasibility prove multiplicativity. The result
extends to the continuous field by finite-rank approximation of its trace-class
Choi state. Thus even an arbitrary collective decoder satisfies

  F_Bell,opt(n,T)<=[1-c_B h_B^2]^n.                           (5)

Any heralded success map can be completed to a deterministic decoder with
nonnegative failure contribution. Therefore

  s F_Bell,conditional<=[1-c_B h_B^2]^n.                      (6)

This does not assume input-independent success. Fixed nonvanishing Bell-input
success cannot preserve high conditional fidelity for a fixed finite B as
n grows.

The deterministic recovered channel has unhalved diamond error at least

  2{1-[1-c_B h_B^2]^n}.                                      (7)

If this error is <=epsilon in (0,1), then, using (1-x)^n<=exp(-nx),

  h_B<=sqrt[-log(1-epsilon/2)/(n c_B)]=:y.

When y<1 this is equivalently the necessary bound

  B>=r cot(pi y/2).                                          (8)

For high accuracy and large n, h_B=(2/pi)arctan(r/B) is asymptotic to
2r/(pi B). Hence

  B=Omega(r sqrt(n/epsilon)).                                (9)

The constant in (4) is deliberately unoptimized. The full consequence is the
failure of fixed aggregate hard-band access for full unknown-input transfer,
not a claim of zero coded or assisted capacity.

5. RELATION TO THE POSITIVE RECEIVER AND ORDINARY COMPARISONS

This converse concerns a permanently inaccessible Fourier subspace. Fixed
linewidth and fixed control-response rate of each individual receiver cell do
not imply such an aggregate cutoff. A growing passive network may increase its
collective acceptance while keeping local parameters fixed; its hardware and
transport costs must be paid.

The positive bounded-qubit construction therefore remains compatible with
(9): it grows the total array rather than retaining a fixed aggregate hard
passband. Its dispersionless coherent-port assumption should be stated, not
interpreted as a microscopic finite-material-bandwidth theorem.

For ordinary amplitude-emission, the companion exact calculation instead
gives B=Omega(n gamma/epsilon). That result must remain identified with its
own source and spectral envelope. Equation (9) is derived directly for the
actual H=0 Weyl source used in the acquisition theorem.
