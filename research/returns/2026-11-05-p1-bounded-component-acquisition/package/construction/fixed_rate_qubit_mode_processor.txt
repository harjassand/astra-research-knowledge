EXECUTIVE RESULT

The strict two-level rail-capture architecture can be followed by a polynomial
quantum processor with fixed local dimension, bounded local Hamiltonian norm,
and bounded local slew. No ideal field-to-qubit transducer is assumed. The
physical captured systems are already qubits. The operation is:

  1. append zero qubits, interpreting each captured 0/1 excitation as a binary
     occupation register;
  2. digitally invert the known passive spatial splitter;
  3. digitally rotate captured Laguerre modes into approximate temporal-bin modes;
  4. read the existing occupation least-significant bits as the bin qubits;
  5. apply an explicit fixed-dimensional Kraus-environment isometry;
  6. copy reset flags coherently to unused ancillas and execute the fixed full
     reverse-bin circuit controlled on them, then trace auxiliary registers.
     No physical flag readout is needed (section 13).

Fine virtual time bins do not require fine physical time switching. They are
synthesized from stored modes AFTER capture, using more qubits, gates and time.
No exponentially large global Fock or source matrix is synthesized. A single
abstract two-mode gate has a polynomial-size occupation matrix and is compiled
into ordinary bounded few-qubit gates.

The construction also closes two potential loopholes. The one-photon-per-site/
bin comparison is an unconditional channel approximation, not postselected
success. A no-prehistory receiver startup can omit an arbitrarily short initial
interval, explicitly charge its error, and undo the known initial Hamiltonian
at the end. It does not pretend that the source's initial unknown state waited
without evolving.

1. PHYSICAL PREMISES, NOTATION, AND THE CAPTURE OUTPUT

There are n source sites, fixed local dimension q, and known local Hamiltonian
H(t)=sum_X h_X(t), |X|<=k. Define

  beta = sup_t max_i sum_(X containing i)(|X|-1)osc(h_X(t)),
  chi  = sup_t max_i sum_(X containing i)osc(h_X(t)).

The source has specified calibrated coherent depolarizing output ports. For
matrix-unit ports L_ab=sqrt(r/q)|a><b|, their physical intensity per site is rho=r.
For nonidentity Weyl ports L_mu=sqrt(r)W_mu/q, mu!=0, it is rho=r(1-q^-2).
Both give the same reduced generator r(R_tau-id), tau=I/q. Let F be the total
number of original ports, F=O(n) for fixed q, and R=n rho.

Each original port is coherently split into A equal rails by a static balanced
binary tree, A a power of two. Every additional input is vacuum. The tree has
A-1 degree-two passive components and depth log_2 A. All rail path lengths are
equalized or their known delays are incorporated in retarded clocks. No photon
number measurement is made before capture.

Each rail feeds N two-level absorbers, initially |0>, coupled causally to its
chiral field with L_j(t)=g(t-d_j) sigma_j^-. The field propagates only downstream.
The common envelope g has fixed amplitude and derivative bounds. It starts at
zero, uses a fixed smooth turn-on, and is smoothly returned to zero after the
chosen collection interval. Stage j freezes at its corresponding retarded time.
The interface_theory branch derives this finite-propagation capture operation.
For a compact bounded-slew envelope, freezing is exact. For a fixed-pole envelope
with decaying tails, its finite-guard perturbation is explicitly charged as
epsilon_front_tail, using that branch's finite-system generator bound. An optional
protected-qubit exchange is explicitly implemented in section 14. No ideal
field-to-register transducer is assumed.

On the sector with at most one incoming photon over a rail's entire active
window, the two-level chain is EXACTLY the passive oscillator chain: every
sigma^- has the same action as an annihilator on vacuum and single excitation.
If the active source window ends at T_f, the probability of any rail containing
two or more photons has the uniform bound

  p_rail <= n r^2 T_f^2/(2A).                                 (1)

For Weyl ports this follows directly by Poisson thinning. More generally,
conditional on m photons in one original port, equal splitting gives expected
same-rail unordered pairs m(m-1)/(2A); summing physical ports at one site is
bounded by the Poisson total site's factorial moment (r T_f)^2. This gives the
same uniform bound for matrix-unit ports.

The qubit and oscillator capture isometries coincide on the good rail sector.
Their difference on an arbitrary input is therefore at most 2 sqrt(p_rail) in
vector norm, giving channel error <=4 sqrt(p_rail). No rail-collision test or
restart is physically performed. This is a comparison bound for the actual
unconditional qubit architecture.

All finite internal capture Hilbert spaces have dimension two, and

  ||L_j(t)||=|g(t-d_j)|,
  ||dot L_j(t)||=|dot g(t-d_j)|.

These upper bounds are fixed as n and accuracy change. More rails and absorbers
are paid hardware. Static passive field scattering is part of the specified
coherent port interface; this does not assert a bounded microscopic white-field
Hamiltonian outside the input-output model.

2. STARTUP, AN EXPLICIT ORIGINAL-INPUT TARGET, AND THE PHOTON CAP

Let t0>0 be a short omitted initial interval and let the retained source-output
window be I=[t0,t0+T]. Let T_f=t0+T+tau_off include the capture-off guard. Take,
for example,

  g(t)=sqrt(kappa_c) sin^2(pi t/(2 tau_on)), 0<=t<=tau_on,

followed by its constant plateau and a fixed smooth off-ramp. The source starts
at time zero with the unknown payload; all receiver absorbers also start locally
in |0> with coupling zero. No operating prehistory is needed.

The initial no-emission branch has source propagator
exp(-R t0/2) U_0, where U_0 is the exact Hamiltonian unitary over [0,t0]. Its
probability is input independent. Comparing the full initial source+field
isometry to U_0 with an empty initial field gives channel error

  epsilon_edge <=2 sqrt(1-exp(-R t0))<=2 sqrt(R t0).           (2)

Thus t0=epsilon_edge^2/(8R) is a safe choice. A sharper reduced-source error
2R t0 is also available when ideal early-field discarding is used, but (2)
already suffices. The decoder recovers the source state at time t0 and then
applies U_0^dagger on the receiver to target the ORIGINAL time-zero payload.
This initial Hamiltonian inverse is a paid bounded-gate operation. It is not
silently omitted or imposed on the physical source.

For later mode comparison, the total physical count through T_f is Poisson
with mean mu=R T_f. A sufficient finite cutoff is

  Q=ceil((e-1)mu+log(1/p_num)),

for which Prob(N_ph>Q)<=p_num, by the exponential moment at exponent one.
All processing unitaries below conserve photon number in their ideal occupation
representation. A conservative channel error allocation 4 sqrt(p_num) handles
the finite occupation cutoff. The implementation never postselects on this cap;
its actions on overflow sectors are defined arbitrarily as part of finite
unitary/channel extensions.

3. A NORMALIZED COMPARISON CHANNEL WITH FLAT BIN MODES

Partition I into L equal bins, Delta=T/L. The prior full-history comparison,
valid for time-dependent H, bounds the actual source+field dilation by the model
with noise alone in each bin followed by that bin's exact Hamiltonian unitary:

  epsilon_split <=2 chi Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2).                (3)

Hamiltonian pieces preserve their original time labels/order when moved only
within their bins. Later source evolution through the off-ramp can be appended
to both compared channels without enlarging the error.

In this split model, let G be the event that every SITE/bin has at most one
physical jump. Counts have independent Poisson laws of mean s=rho Delta, so

  Prob(G)=[exp(-s)(1+s)]^(nL),
  Prob(G^c)<=nL s^2/2=n rho^2 T Delta/2.                       (4)

The probabilities are input independent, because the coherent jump mark at a
site is an isometry and total local intensity is rho I. Projecting onto G and
normalizing only defines an isometric comparison channel. Its overlap with the
full split isometry is sqrt(Prob(G)) times the identity; their complete-channel
diamond distance is at most

  epsilon_collision <=2 sqrt(Prob(G^c))
                     <=2 sqrt(n rho^2 T Delta/2).            (5)

This is not an implemented postselection. The actual decoder runs on all inputs
and all records. Subsequent CPTP operations contract this comparison error.

Within G the photon waveform in a physical port is exactly constant on its bin:
there is no Hamiltonian inside the split noise interval and no-jump attenuation
is scalar. The desired orthonormal one-particle modes are

  e_j(t)=Delta^(-1/2)1_[t0+jDelta,t0+(j+1)Delta](t),
  j=0,...,L-1.

There is at most one photon across all ports at a site/bin. Its exact normalized
local Kraus family is

  K_0=I/sqrt(1+s),
  K_mu=sqrt(Delta)L_mu/sqrt(1+s).                              (6)

For matrix-unit ports this is exactly D_p=(1-p)id+pR_tau with
p=rDelta/(1+rDelta). For Weyl ports it is exactly D_p with

  p=rDelta/(1+rho Delta), rho=r(1-q^-2).                       (7)

Assume rDelta<=1. Then p<=1 and the effective reset rate obeys

  r_eff=-log(1-p)/Delta
       >=log(1+rDelta)/Delta>=r-r^2 Delta/2.                   (8)

If kappa=r-beta>0 and Delta<=kappa/r^2, then r_eff-beta>=kappa/2. The prior
reverse-decoder theorem applies with conditional input at time t0 and ideal error

  epsilon_reverse<=2 sqrt(n exp[-(r_eff-beta)T]).              (9)

For the diagonal decoder the failure bound is instead 2n exp(-r_eff T), with no
condition comparing r and beta.

4. THE FROZEN MODE COORDINATES AND A FINITE GRAM CERTIFICATE

In the ideal oscillator comparison, the common-envelope cascade stores warped
Laguerre modes. Set kappa(t)=g(t)^2 and

  x(t)=integral_t^(T_f) kappa(s) ds,
  ell_m(x)=exp(-x/2)L_m(x).

The field part of the mth frozen coordinate has kernel

  f_m(t)=sqrt(kappa(t)) ell_m(x(t)), m=0,...,N-1.

With a finite zero-coupling startup, these field kernels alone need not be
orthonormal on [0,T_f]. Include the initially empty cavity coordinates in the
one-particle dilation. The complete frozen coordinates are orthonormal. An
equivalent mathematical representation extends the Laguerre x-domain beyond
x(0) using vacuum modes. This only represents the INITIAL VACUUM ancillas; it
does not assume any physical operating prehistory.

Let E embed the desired e_j into that complete one-particle input space, let
P_N project onto the N frozen coordinates, and calculate

  B_(m,j)=<f_m,e_j>,
  G=B^dagger B=E^dagger P_N E.

If ||I-G||<=zeta<1, the family

  W=P_N E G^(-1/2)                                             (10)

is orthonormal and physically available inside the N stored modes. All matrices
are at most N-by-L or L-by-L, with condition number <=1/(1-zeta).

A principal-angle decomposition supplies a one-particle unitary V such that
VE=W and

  ||V-I||=sqrt(2-2sqrt(lambda_min(G)))<=2sqrt(zeta).

This unitary is ONLY an analysis comparison; it need not be applied to the
unavailable field. On the <=Q-photon sector,

  ||Gamma(V)-I||<=Q||V-I||<=2Q sqrt(zeta).

Therefore extracting W rather than the exact E modes costs at most

  epsilon_modes<=4Q sqrt(zeta)                                (11)

in complete-channel diamond norm. This includes any original reference,
uncaptured modes, initial vacuum ancillas, and guard-interval photons. The
one-particle map is block diagonal across original ports, so its norm is the
maximum per-port norm; Q already counts photons across all ports.

The Gram certificate has an explicit polynomial size bound even during startup.
Let

  kappa_min=min_(t in I) kappa(t)>0,
  D_kappa=max_(t in I)|kappa'(t)|,
  d_min=Delta kappa_min,
  X=max_(t in I) x(t).

The warped bin is h_j(x)=1/[sqrt(Delta)sqrt(kappa(t(x)))] on its interval and zero
elsewhere. It has norm one, sup|h_j|^2<=1/d_min and

  integral |h_j'(x)|^2 dx<=D_kappa^2/(4 kappa_min^4)

away from its endpoint jumps. Multiply it by an interior cutoff with linear
ramps of width w<=d_min/2. For the smoothed function u_j,

  ||h_j-u_j||^2<=2w/(3d_min),
  integral|u_j'|^2<=4/(d_min w)+D_kappa^2/(2 kappa_min^4),
  ||u_j||^2<=1.

The Laguerre functions diagonalize A=-(x f')'+x f/4 with eigenvalues m+1/2.
The spectral energy bound and doubled triangle inequality yield

  ||(I-P_N)h_j||^2
    <=4w/(3d_min)
      +[8X/(d_min w)+X D_kappa^2/kappa_min^4+X/2]/(N+1/2).   (12)

Since I-G is positive, its operator norm is at most the sum of these L tails.
Choose w=d_min zeta/(8L), and

  N>=128 X L^2/(d_min^2 zeta^2)
        +2 X L D_kappa^2/(kappa_min^4 zeta)+X L/zeta,
  N>=L.                                                       (13)

The trace bound is <=zeta/6+zeta/2<zeta. For the fixed sin-squared amplitude
turn-on, kappa_min is proportional to t0^4 near zero, hence inverse-polynomial
when t0 is inverse-polynomial. For a three-pole step-up, g(t) instead starts
proportional to t^3, so kappa_min scales as t0^6; it is still inverse-polynomial
and the same general bound (13) applies. Thus N is finite polynomial. A constant-envelope
prefix permits the sharper previously derived bound with 64 replacing 128 and
no derivative term, but is not needed for no-prehistory startup.

Laguerre overlaps and Gram factors can be computed to certified precision using
polynomial arithmetic/bit complexity. The prescribed sine ramp and constant
pieces are efficiently describable; arbitrary uncomputable control functions
are not being supplied free.

5. PHYSICAL QUBITS TO OCCUPATION REGISTERS: NO TRANSDUCER

Let b=ceil(log_2(Q+1)). For each frozen physical absorber qubit, append b-1 fresh
zero qubits and designate the absorber as the least-significant bit. This is the
literal isometry

  alpha|0>+beta|1> -> alpha|0...00>+beta|0...01>,

including arbitrary entanglement. No unknown state is copied, measured or
transduced. The resulting b-qubit register can represent occupation 0,...,Q,
with harmless additional padded basis states. On the good rail sector its joint
state is exactly the encoded frozen-oscillator state. On other sectors the
actual finite operation is still well defined, and the error was charged in (1).

At most F A N b physical qubits are required for this fully parallel register
layout, plus polynomial reusable work ancillas. These are actual finite local
systems, each of dimension two; Q is register length rather than local level
count. The initial encoding step is merely allocation of zero ancillas.

6. DIGITAL INVERSE SPATIAL SPLITTING AND TEMPORAL MODE ROTATION

Every rail has the SAME capture envelope and Laguerre kernels in retarded source
time. In the ideal oscillator comparison, spatial splitting acts on the rail
index and capture acts on the temporal index, so they commute. Therefore invert
the original balanced splitter tree on the occupation registers separately for
each Laguerre index m. It uses

  G_spatial=F N(A-1)

abstract passive two-mode gates. This restores one N-mode array per original
port and leaves the other A-1 combinations in vacuum in the comparison model.
Those auxiliary registers may be discarded. This avoids an unnecessary
O((A N)^2) arbitrary mode transformation.

For each remaining original-port array, complete the orthonormal W columns in
(10) to an N-by-N unitary. Adjacent-row Givens elimination decomposes it into
O(N^2) neighboring two-mode rotations and phases. The actual digital processor
implements this transformation on the occupation registers. Its first L output
registers then represent the W_j modes. Thus

  G_temporal=O(F N^2),
  G_modes=O(F[N A+N^2]).                                      (14)

Only N-by-N single-particle matrices are calculated. The Fock-space register
contains the unknown quantum amplitudes; it is not stored as a classical vector.

An abstract passive two-mode gate is computable and implementable with polynomial
bounded-qubit-gate cost. On a pair of occupation registers, define it to agree
with the true beam-splitter unitary whenever the two occupations sum to s<=Q,
and to be identity on sectors s>Q and on unused padding states. In the s sector,
the Hamiltonian matrix has size s+1 and off-diagonal entries

  sqrt((m+1)(s-m)),   m=0,...,s-1,

with the known complex phase of the splitter. It is the finite spin-s/2 rotation
matrix. Its matrix exponential can be calculated in polynomial time. The whole
pair unitary has dimension at most

  d_pair=2^(2b)<4(Q+1)^2.

A generic QR decomposition into two-level rotations uses O(d_pair^2)=O(Q^4)
rotations. A rotation between two b-bit-pair basis strings is implemented by a
Gray-code sequence of controlled bit gates and a controlled single-qubit
rotation, with O(b^2) bounded few-qubit gates and O(b) reusable ancillas. Hence
a sufficient quantum cost per abstract two-mode gate is

  O(Q^4 log^2(Q+1) polylog(1/eta)),                            (15)

for operator-norm precision eta. The classical dense calculation is polynomial,
e.g. O(Q^6) arithmetic operations plus precision factors. This is deliberately
conservative; sparse Hamiltonian simulation or specialized rotation arithmetic
can substantially improve it, but is not required for the polynomial theorem.

On total photon number <=Q, every occupation sum at the boundaries between
abstract passive mode gates is <=Q. Thus each finite-gate extension agrees EXACTLY
with the untruncated bosonic gate before numerical compilation. The physical
qubit subcircuits need not conserve the VIRTUAL photon number during their
internal steps; their local norm/slew bounds hold on their entire qubit spaces.
Photon number is conserved by the completed ideal abstract gates in the comparison.
No intermediate single-photon assumption is needed when undoing rail splitting:
virtual photons may coalesce into one occupation register. The digital encoding
handles them without an unbounded local physical interaction.

Choose per-abstract-gate operator error eta<=epsilon_digital/(2G_modes). Channel
telescoping bounds the total by epsilon_digital. If the QR implementation is
further compiled into a fixed finite universal set, allocate each elementary
rotation its corresponding fraction of eta; its sequence overhead is only
polylogarithmic in G_modes, Q and 1/epsilon_digital. The stated cost is for a
finite explicit circuit, not an existence proof of arbitrary global recovery.

All physical elementary gates act on a constant number of actual qubits. Choose
a fixed universal gate set whose pulse Hamiltonians have norm <=Omega_0 and
slew <=B_0, with a fixed gate duration tau_gate sufficient for those bounds.
Executing more gates or inserting waits never raises these local maxima. Dense
connectivity is not supplied for free: on a connected bounded-degree register
network of V qubits, even a crude routing bound of O(V) SWAPs per nonlocal gate
is polynomial. A more suitable tree/chain layout follows the splitter and modal
Givens circuits and is cheaper. Gates can be scheduled so each physical qubit
participates in at most one active local interaction at a time.

7. THE EXISTING BIN BITS ARE THE FINITE FIELD REGISTERS

In the normalized good-history comparison from section 3, each desired temporal
mode has occupation zero or one, and a site/bin has at most one photon across
its fixed number of physical ports. After the digital mode conversion, its
occupation-register high bits are therefore zero. Keep the least-significant
bits and discard the others. This transfers no unknown state: the relevant
qubits ALREADY carry its amplitudes. Discarding high bits is a CPTP operation;
any actual deviations contract the already established channel-error bounds.

A site/bin thus has the coherent one-hot basis |vac>, |mu> for its port label,
not a classical photon-count outcome. The vacuum/one-photon coherence must be
preserved until the following conversion. Measuring the physical photon event
first would give the wrong instrument for Weyl ports.

8. EXPLICIT LOCAL KRAUS-ENVIRONMENT CONVERSION TO RESET/EXPORT

The protected local register carries the Kraus family (6), whose channel is
exactly D_p. A constant-size unitary, with fresh constant-size ancillas if needed,
converts its environment to

  |psi> -> sqrt(1-p)|psi>|empty>
             +sqrt(p)|Phi_q>_(S A)|psi>_B.                   (16)

For matrix-unit ports this only requires relabelling the one-hot mark as the
pair (a,b): (6) is already the Kraus family of (16).

For Weyl ports an explicit isometry is useful. Let c0=1-p+p/q^2 and use the
input environment basis |0>, |mu!=0>. Set

  V_env|0>
    =sqrt((1-p)/c0)|empty>
      +sqrt(p)/(q sqrt(c0)) |Phi_q>_(AB),

  V_env|mu>
    =q^(-1/2) sum_(a,b) [W_mu]_(ab)^* |a,b>_(AB), mu!=0.     (17)

The columns are orthonormal by Weyl trace orthogonality. Expanding a matrix unit
in the Weyl basis verifies that the output Kraus operators are
sqrt(1-p)I and sqrt(p/q)|a><b|. Thus (17) is an exact finite construction of (16),
not an inference about an unknown bath from its reduced dynamics.

The target environment has dimension q^2+1, constant because q is fixed. Extend
the isometry to a unitary on a constant number of physical qubits. Ordinary
bounded-gate synthesis implements it to any required precision, with a
polylogarithmic gate-count overhead. Small p changes angles or their gate
program, not the upper strength or slew of the physical gates.

The mathematical record may be viewed as measured only AFTER (17). The primary
implementation instead copies flags coherently and uses section 13. Ideal flags
are independent Bernoulli(p). The measured variant retains B and discards A
(or measures A for the diagonal decoder). Approximate physical flag probabilities
need not be exactly input independent:
the entire instrument is compared in diamond norm and is followed by the same
record-controlled CPTP decoder. No erroneous conditional renormalization is used.

9. THE PRIOR DECODER AT A FIXED PHYSICAL GATE RATE

GENERAL INTERACTING CASE.
Prepare an n-qudit receiver dummy in I/q^n using local Bell preparation and
tracing one half. In each flag branch, run the known Hamiltonian/reset history
backward on the dummy and the retained B registers. Section 13 implements all
branches coherently in one fixed program. Hamiltonian
simulation and reverse SWAPs all act on receiver hardware. The source is never
pulsed or accessed.

For the OPTIONAL measured-history optimization, take static
H=sum_(a=1)^m_H h_a and B_H=sum_a||h_a||. Combining consecutive bins without reset
flags gives at most M_reset+1 Hamiltonian segments. The primary fixed program
pays the full-L bound (19). A conservative
product-formula circuit has

  O(m_H[M_reset+1+B_H^2 T^2/epsilon_H])

known fixed-arity local exponentials, plus M_reset receiver SWAPs, before local
unitary synthesis and routing. Retaining every bin boundary instead is still
polynomial. Efficient time-dependent simulation requires the stated efficient
Hamiltonian-description and regularity promise.

A tiny SIMULATED time interval never demands a fast PHYSICAL pulse. Implement
its small-angle gate with a lower-amplitude fixed-duration smooth pulse, or
compile it into the fixed bounded-rate universal gate set. Pausing the simulated
Hamiltonian while executing a SWAP is harmless offline. Accuracy costs gate
count and wall-clock time. Finally apply U_0^dagger from section 2 to recover the
original time-zero payload, rather than only its evolved time-t0 state.

DIAGONAL CASE.
Measure each reset A in the product basis, retain the first B per site, and
accumulate the known phase table F_X on each Hamiltonian term. Apply one final
fixed-arity diagonal gate exp(iF_X) per term. The ideal error is bounded by
2n exp(-r_eff T); no r>beta condition is needed. The initial U_0^dagger can be
included as an additional known diagonal phase. All interaction strengths remain
charged in numerical integration, circuit length and capture-mode resources.

Memory may be provisioned conservatively for every site/bin, O(nL) constant-size
registers. Alternatively process bin registers one at a time, discard empty/A
systems, and retain only the O(nrT) expected B exports, with a finite tail cap.
The physical capture/register array itself is larger and remains explicitly
charged. No compact global q^n Stinespring receiver is invoked.

10. PARAMETERS, COMPLETE ERROR BUDGET, AND COSTS

For r>beta let kappa=r-beta. One sufficient generic choice is

  T>=2[log n+2log(2/epsilon_reverse)]/kappa,

  Delta<=min{1/r, kappa/r^2,
       epsilon_split/[2chi sqrt(RT+(RT)^2)],
       epsilon_collision^2/[2 n rho^2 T]}.

Omit the split constraint when chi=0; round L=T/Delta upward. Choose t0 by (2)
and fixed startup/off durations, giving T_f=t0+T+tau_off. Take a power-of-two A
large enough that 4sqrt(nr^2 T_f^2/(2A))<=epsilon_rail. Choose p_num and Q from
section 2 with 4sqrt(p_num)<=epsilon_number. Set

  zeta<=epsilon_modes^2/(16Q^2),

and N by (13). Assign finite gate/algorithm precision so that digital mode
conversion, local reset conversion, Hamiltonian correction and U_0^dagger
contribute at most epsilon_processing. Then

  ||N_actual-id||_diamond
   <=epsilon_edge +epsilon_rail +epsilon_number +epsilon_front_tail
      +epsilon_split +epsilon_collision +epsilon_modes
      +epsilon_reverse +epsilon_processing
      +epsilon_memory +epsilon_calibration.                 (18)

PROOF ORDER FOR THE UNCONDITIONAL NUMBER TAIL. First compare qubit capture with
oscillator capture on the ACTUAL source, using the uniform rail bound. Jointly
compare finite occupation processing/extraction of W with exact extraction of E,
also on the ACTUAL source. On <=Q photons their isometry error is at most
2Q sqrt(zeta), and on its complement at most 2. This joint step therefore costs
4Q sqrt(zeta)+4sqrt(p_num) in channel norm. Exact-bin extraction is extended as a
CPTP map on arbitrary Fock inputs, agreeing with the specified 0/1 action. Only
then contract the edge/split/normalized-G comparison through that exact-bin
extraction and the fixed decoder. No Poisson tail under the normalized G model
is assumed, and no factor 1/Prob(G) is omitted.

All comparisons preserve arbitrary reference entanglement. This is a deterministic
CPTP transfer of the original unknown payload. No postselected repeat, new input,
or source-access pulse is hidden in the construction.

Every displayed parameter is polynomial in n, inverse error, inverse positive
margin kappa, 1/r and the supplied local strengths/regularity bounds, for fixed
q,k and fixed hardware control maxima. The startup lower coupling kappa_min can
be very small, but is inverse-polynomial; its cost appears openly in (13).

A conservative complete receiver bill is:

  - F(A-1) fixed degree-two passive splitters, with balanced depth log_2 A;
  - F A N two-level capture systems, each with the same bounded field coupling
    envelope and fixed local control-response timescale;
  - actual fixed-density planar geometry: longest path O(a(A+N)/v) at spacing
    a and propagation speed v, total wire O(Fa[A log A+AN]), plus component
    delays; staggered clocks use actual path lengths. Logarithmic splitter
    graph depth alone does not imply logarithmic physical delivery latency;
  - F A N ceil(log_2(Q+1)) occupation-register qubits at peak, plus reusable
    work ancillas and the ordinary logical decoder registers;
  - O(F[N A+N^2]) abstract two-mode transformations;
  - a conservative O(F[N A+N^2] Q^4 log^2(Q+1)
       polylog(F[N A+N^2]Q/epsilon_processing)) elementary bounded-qubit gates
    for those transformations, before routing;
  - O(nL) constant-dimensional reset-conversion circuits, full quantum
    record/export registers and untouched flag-copy ancillas;
  - O(m_H[L+C_H T^2/epsilon_sim]+nL) local gates for the primary fixed
    reverse-bin program, before universal synthesis and routing;
  - the prior polynomial reverse-decoder circuit, or m_H diagonal correction
    gates with polynomial classical phase-table processing;
  - polynomial-size numerical Gram/QR computations and gate programs;
  - fresh pure ancillas, coherent flag copies, discard/reset operations and
    their entropy removal; no flag readout is needed for the primary program.

Each elementary physical gate has fixed norm <=Omega_0 and slew <=B_0. Section
14 adds O(B^(-1)log(G/epsilon_actuator)) guard per primitive for the primary
fixed-response implementation. Wall time is the routed primitive count times
that guarded duration. Total apparatus power, volume
and stored energy may grow; a local bound does not imply a fixed total budget.
These are conservative polynomial bounds, not useful device economics.

11. LIMITS, ORDINARY BASELINES, AND WHAT WAS EARNED

The oscillator/linear-optics language is only a calculational model. The actual
capture memory in the primary architecture consists of qubits, with bounded
sigma^- couplings. Its later processing uses bounded qubit gates on binary
occupation registers. Thus it avoids the false claim that an untruncated
annihilation operator has bounded norm, and avoids assuming a finite-dimensional
ladder directly coupled to a continuum as a new unearned component.

The construction proves fixed amplitude, fixed slew, and use of fixed smooth
pulse families at a bounded gate rate. Exactly hard-bandlimited controls cannot
also vanish exactly outside a finite time interval. If the intended hardware
model imposes a strict spectral cutoff, approximate switching tails must be
separately bounded and charged. Do not silently identify these two meanings of
bandwidth. The Markov continuum itself also remains an explicitly stated model.

Lossless coherent delivery and protected receiver operation remain substantive
premises. A fixed inaccessible erasure before an unknown state is encoded cannot
be repaired by later coding: for a d-dimensional Bell input, an erased branch has
fidelity at most 1/d^2, giving infidelity at least p(1-1/d^2) for erasure probability
p. A later fault-tolerant processor cannot retroactively remove that loss.
Propagation/cavity loss, idle-memory noise, gate errors and calibration drift
must meet the allocated full-channel bounds in (18), or an independently earned
fault-tolerant FRONT INTERFACE must be supplied. Photon-number truncation and rail
dilution are not automatically error-correcting codes for inaccessible loss.

The strongest simple same-port baseline at H=0 is ordinary first-reset collection.
A direct source-side parallel SWAP can be stronger when that additional source
access is permitted. Dense recovery over the full captured Fock space is not
needed here: the processing uses ordinary passive unitary decompositions and
local Kraus conversion to expose the polynomial reset decoder. The primitives
are established; the construction earns their end-to-end use with an initially
arbitrary source and bounded local capture/control resources.

13. PRIMARY FIXED-PROGRAM OPTION: NO MEASUREMENT OR READOUT

A physical flag measurement is unnecessary. This section removes record-dependent
classical scheduling from the general reverse decoder and supplies the robust
primary implementation of sections 8-10.

After local environment conversion, each site/bin has an explicit flag qubit F_ij
and fixed-size A_ij,B_ij registers. The empty branch has F=0 and fixed dummy values
for A,B; the reset branch has F=1 and its reset dilation. Prepare a fresh copy
C_ij in |0>, apply CNOT from F_ij to C_ij, and leave every C untouched. This
coherently copies a computational label, not an arbitrary unknown quantum state.

Prepare the maximally mixed n-qudit receiver dummy D locally. Execute the SAME
full program on every run:

  for j=L-1,...,0:
      apply U_j^dagger, the inverse source-Hamiltonian bin unitary, to D;
      for every site i:
          apply SWAP(D_i,B_ij), controlled on F_ij=1.

Each controlled SWAP is a fixed-size receiver gate. For F=0 it is identity, so
unused B registers need no special mixed-state preparation. Finally apply the
known U_0^dagger from the startup interval, keep D as payload, and ignore/trace
F,C,A and consumed B. The Bell partners used to prepare D can also be kept and
traced only at the end. Everything before the final partial trace can therefore
be unitary with locally initialized pure ancillas.

EXACT EQUIVALENCE.
For any acquired state, including arbitrary reference correlations, write

  sigma=sum_(gamma,gamma') |gamma><gamma'|_F tensor sigma_(gamma,gamma').

Copying the flags adds |gamma><gamma'|_C to each block. The ideal reverse circuit
is V=sum_gamma |gamma><gamma|_F tensor V_gamma, with V_gamma precisely the
fixed-record inverse on D,B. Tracing C kills every gamma!=gamma' block. The
payload is consequently

  sum_gamma Tr_(A,B) V_gamma
      [tau_D tensor sigma_(gamma,gamma)] V_gamma^dagger.

This is exactly the channel obtained by measuring flags, using the resulting
record's decoder, and forgetting the record. The equality holds for EVERY sigma,
not only the ideal input-independent record distribution. Thus it remains exact
CPTP postprocessing for the actual approximate interface and preserves all the
previous complete-channel error bounds. No physical readout or feedforward is
required. The original flags alone could also be traced, but keeping untouched
copies makes the deferred-measurement argument explicit.

FIXED PROGRAM COST.
Pay O(nL) flag-copy qubits and O(nL) complete A,B/export registers. The primary
program does not claim the sparse measured-history speedup in section 9. For
static H=sum_(a=1)^m_H h_a let C_H bound the first-order product-formula
coefficient, e.g. a safe constant times sum_(a<b)||[h_a,h_b]||; the cruder
C_H=(sum_a||h_a||)^2 is sufficient. Using

  s=max{1,ceil(C_H T^2/(L epsilon_sim))}

microsteps per bin gives deterministic local-gate count

  O(m_H[L+C_H T^2/epsilon_sim]+nL),                           (19)

before fixed-dimensional synthesis and routing. The nL term includes controlled
SWAPs and flag copies. For differentiable time dependence, include a bound on
sum_a||dot h_a|| in the first-order coefficient, or use the promised efficient
time-dependent simulator. Known discontinuities add deterministic segment
boundaries. U_0^dagger is simulated to its allocated error as well.

The measured-record variant remains a valid optional optimization. Equation (19)
is the conservative primary result: one fixed program and no measurement,
feedforward, or record-dependent classical runtime assumption.

14. FIXED THREE-POLE ACTUATORS: FINITE ERROR WITHOUT INSTANTANEOUS OFF

The following implements the fixed universal gates with a specified constant
response bandwidth. It strengthens a bounded-slew assertion without pretending
that a hard Fourier cutoff and finite pulse support are compatible.

Use a fixed finite set of local Hermitian generators G_a, each supported on at
most two physical qubits and normalized ||G_a||<=1. On a bounded-degree layout,
only a constant number D_act of actuator channels is incident on any qubit.
Universal primitives are a fixed finite collection of rotations exp(-i theta G_a),
with |theta|<=theta_max. Accuracy is obtained by longer sequences of these same
primitives.

Each actual amplitude x_a is produced by THREE identical stable poles of fixed
rate B>0:

  dot z_1=B(u_a-z_1),
  dot z_2=B(z_1-z_2),
  dot x_a=B(z_2-x_a),

with locally initialized zero actuator states. The physical Hamiltonian is
H_act=sum_a x_a G_a. Its impulse response is

  h_B(t)=(B^3 t^2/2)exp(-Bt)1_(t>=0),
  integral h_B(t)dt=1.

For |u_a|<=U, positivity and unit DC gain give |x_a|<=U. The state equations give
|dot x_a|<=2BU. Incident norm and slew are therefore <=D_act U and <=2D_act BU,
independently of system size or accuracy. Choose U under the desired fixed
bounds. B and the number of incident hardware channels never increase.

A primitive command is a rectangle of signed amplitude U and duration
 tau=|theta|/U, giving signed area theta. The finite primitive angle set has fixed
core durations. The ACTUAL pulse is its filtered output: it starts smoothly and
has a nonzero decaying tail. No instantaneous output OFF is assumed. A fixed
smooth command edge is also allowed, with its known area and fixed-response tail
charged instead of the rectangle; constants change but scaling does not.

Let g be a guard after the command ends. The absolute remaining pulse area obeys

  a_tail(g)<=|theta| S_2(Bg),
  S_2(x)=exp(-x)(1+x+x^2/2).                                 (20)

This follows by convolving the rectangle with the impulse-response survival
function. A second useful bound, obtained by domination by a fully charged
step-off response, is

  a_tail(g)<=(U/B) exp(-Bg)[3+2Bg+(Bg)^2/2]
           <=(4U/B)exp(-Bg/2).                              (21)

Thus even a known upper bound on the actuator's internal charged state is enough
to bound its finite guard error. All quantities have their physical units:
a_tail is a rotation angle, U is a rate, and B is a response rate.

An explicit qubit-to-qubit protected readout, if the capture architecture uses
one, is covered by the same construction. Couple capture qubit a to a fresh
protected qubit b through G_ex=sigma_a^- sigma_b^+ + sigma_a^+ sigma_b^-.
Its norm is one. A total area pi/2 maps

  alpha|00>+beta|10> -> alpha|00>-i beta|01>.

A known phase correction on b completes the transfer. The unknown state and
all its reference entanglement are preserved. This is an explicitly generated
bounded exchange, not an assumed transducer. For D such transfers, incomplete
area plus all future exchange tails contribute at most 4D a_tail(g) in channel
norm. The source/field perturbation from any residual CAPTURE coupling is a
separate bound from the interface construction; it is not erased by calling
this local exchange a readout. After transfer, the protected b qubit can serve
as the occupation least-significant bit in section 5.

FINITE WHOLE-PROGRAM ERROR, INCLUDING NONCOMMUTING TAILS.
Let G be the total primitive-pulse count AFTER all synthesis and routing. Execute
one command-plus-guard slot per pulse, sequentially. The resulting finite time is

  T_program=sum_(j=1)^G (tau_j+g).

Compare the actual Hamiltonian with one that truncates each FILTERED pulse at
the end of that pulse's guard slot. The truncated pulses have disjoint supports.
Their angles differ from the desired angles by at most a_tail,j. Product
telescoping bounds their unitary error by sum_j a_tail,j. The integral of the
norm of all omitted old-pulse tails is at most the same sum. Duhamel's bound gives

  ||U_actual(t)-U_target||<=2sum_j a_tail,j,
  ||Ad_U_actual(t)-Ad_U_target||_diamond
       <=4sum_j a_tail,j
       <=4G theta_max S_2(Bg),       for ALL t>=T_program.    (22)

Noncommuting overlaps are included by the norm integral. No commutation or
instantaneous OFF assumption is used. Reusing an actuator is included because
its ODEs are linear and individual command responses superpose. Bounded inputs
and bounded physical degree preserve the local norm and slew bounds. The proof
is uniform over unknown quantum data and all reference systems.

Using S_2(x)<=8exp(-x/2), the explicit safe guard

  g=(2/B)log(max{e,32G theta_max/epsilon_actuator})             (23)

makes (22) at most epsilon_actuator. Bound (21) gives the same logarithmic scaling.
Consequently

  T_program=O(G[tau_max+B^(-1)log(G/epsilon_actuator)]).        (24)

No response pole, local amplitude, or local slew grows with accuracy. Primitive
command areas may remain fixed; only the guard and gate program grow. Command-
area calibration errors delta_j add at most 2sum_j|delta_j| in channel error and
must meet their assigned calibration budget. Compilation does not remove them.

Uniformity of (22) for ALL later times means the output already has the promised
accuracy at finite time T_program, and all subsequent residual tails are included.
An exact final OFF switch, detector, or infinite wait is unnecessary. Ancillas
may remain unused, or later bounded operations can be analyzed with the same
tail perturbation bound. The coherent decoder needs no flag readout at all.

For the primary implementation allocate epsilon_actuator and any explicit
protected-readout exchange error within epsilon_processing of (18). Charge
residual SOURCE/FIELD capture coupling separately as epsilon_front_tail; it is
zero for the ideal compact-envelope variant and bounded by the capture-interface
finite-guard estimate for fixed-pole actuators. Apply (24) to the routed
primitive count, and charge the increased memory-exposure time. The filter has
fixed poles and 1/omega^3 high-frequency rolloff. It is NOT a hard Fourier cutoff.

A fixed-duration compact smooth pulse with fixed norm/slew and known area is a
simpler abstract alternative sufficient for the bounded-slew version. The
three-pole construction is the robust primary option for a claim about fixed
physical actuator response bandwidth, because its noncompact output tails have
been explicitly bounded.

15. CONDITIONAL OFFLINE COMPONENT CALIBRATION AND ITS COST

The operational theorem may simply take factory-calibrated receiver/control/link
coefficients, with the stated error and drift bounds, as its model interface.
It does not automatically acquire an unknown source Hamiltonian or identify an
arbitrary unknown optical network. The following CONDITIONAL finite calibration
statement prices a suitable offline calibration station when one is provided.

Assume each of M_comp components has a known fixed-dimensional parameter model
 theta in a known bounded box in R^d, with d constant. A known fixed finite set
of S probe protocols gives Bernoulli outcome probabilities p_s(theta). Each
protocol is implemented by bounded-rate preparations, interactions, and
measurements, with known duration tau_probe or a polynomial upper bound. State
preparation/measurement errors must either be independently calibrated to their
assigned tolerance or INCLUDED in a jointly identifiable finite parameter model.
They cannot be declared perfect merely because they are called calibration.

Assume explicitly that a known branch/trust region satisfies

  ||p(theta)-p(theta')||_infinity
       >=sigma_cal ||theta-theta'||_infinity,                (25)

with sigma_cal>0 known, and that forward probabilities and their Lipschitz upper
bound L_forward can be computed to specified precision in polynomial time.
A validated local inverse plus a known trust region can replace (25). A positive
Jacobian singular value at one point alone does not certify global injectivity,
resolve phase-wrap branches, or remove preparation/measurement gauge freedoms.
Identifiability is a substantive premise, not automatic for these components.

For desired coefficient error delta_theta and total calibration-failure
probability alpha, let a be a sufficiently small constant multiple of
 sigma_cal delta_theta. Repeating each setting

  m=O(sigma_cal^(-2) delta_theta^(-2)
        log(2M_comp S/alpha))                                 (26)

times makes every empirical probability accurate to a with probability at least
1-alpha, by the bounded Bernoulli concentration bound and a union bound. Fit the
finite model to those probabilities. A conservative grid mesh
O(a/L_forward), followed by residual minimization, suffices. Its grid size is

  O((parameter_range L_forward/(sigma_cal delta_theta))^d),   (27)

which is polynomial because d is fixed. Forward numerical errors and fitting
residuals are allocated small fractions of a. The inverse bound (25) then gives
||theta_hat-theta||<=delta_theta. Classical forward evaluation and fitting are
also explicitly polynomial resources; no exponentially large source matrix is
learned or fitted.

The calibration bill is M_comp S m probe repetitions, at most
M_comp S m tau_probe serial wall time plus preparation/reset and fixed-response
guards, the corresponding known probe qubits/fields and measurements, and the
finite grid/numerical work in (27). Parallel calibration can reduce elapsed time
but does not remove the total number of trials or probe energy. If delta_theta,
sigma_cal and alpha are inverse-polynomial and tau_probe is polynomial, this
bill is polynomial. The operational coefficient-error budget determines
 delta_theta; calibration does not improve an actual irreducible component loss.

Between calibration and use, coefficient drift and model discrepancy must remain
inside the remaining assigned tolerance. Fixed-pole tail formulas use a
certified LOWER bound on B and certified upper bounds on amplitudes/gains.
Unmodelled cross-couplings, unknown drift or a nonidentifiable measurement model
invalidate an automatic self-calibration claim. Without a suitable probe/SPAM
and identifiability premise, the honest scope is the factory-calibrated model.

Offline calibration occurs before the arbitrary payload is supplied. If its
failure event has probability <=alpha independent of that payload, the average
unconditional transfer-channel error is at most the conditional good-calibration
bound plus 2alpha. Thus a confidence allocation can also be included directly
in the final diamond-error budget; it need not be hidden as an unquantified
experimental-confidence statement. No claim of unknown-H learning or of curing
fixed pre-encoding loss follows from this calibration lemma.
