FIRST-INGRESS LOSS AND HARD-BANDWIDTH CONVERSES
Research note, 4 November 2026
Agent: independent_opening, including an independent audit of root's lossy-Weyl proof

PURPOSE AND STATUS

The companion bounded_qubit_receiver_compiler.txt earns finite-dimensional
bounded-strength causal capture in an ideal coherent-port model. This note
establishes why that result cannot silently be extended to a fixed inaccessible
fraction of the initially unencoded signal.

There are two distinct theorems:

A. ACTUAL DEPOLARIZING/Weyl PORTS. For independent qubit sources with H=0 and
   physical L_mu=sqrt(r) sigma_mu/2, a fixed collection efficiency eta<1 imposes
   a nonzero one-site optimal Bell-infidelity floor at every acquisition time.
   The optimal collective Bell fidelity for n initially unencoded qubits is at
   most [1-c_0(1-eta)^2]^n, c_0=(1-exp(-2))^2/256. The same expression bounds
   success times conditional Bell fidelity for any heralded decoder. This is
   root's construction, independently checked here in full detail.

B. ORDINARY AMPLITUDE EMISSION. When a qubit is emitted into vacuum plus one
   photon and an unavoidable fraction 1-eta of that photon mode is inaccessible,
   the exact optimal collective deterministic Bell fidelity is
   [(1+sqrt(eta))^2/4]^n. A hard frequency window for a Lorentzian spontaneous-
   emission mode gives an explicit necessary aggregate-bandwidth scaling.

Theorems A and B concern recovery of an already unknown unencoded input.
They are not statements that a lossy link has zero quantum or assisted capacity.
They do not forbid encoding before the lossy interface, preparing entanglement
offline, changing the physical collection interface, or reducing loss as
resources grow. They prohibit assuming that arbitrary later hardware or error
correction automatically replaces a missing first-interface resource.

Conventions: ||rho-sigma||_1 and channel diamond distance are unhalved. Bell
fidelity F means the overlap with the normalized target Bell state, not its
square root. Bell infidelity is e=1-F.

PART I. TWO GENERAL LEMMAS

1. ENVIRONMENT DISTINGUISHABILITY BOUNDS BELL RECOVERY

Let V:A->BE be an isometric channel for a qubit A. B is everything the receiver
can access; E is inaccessible. Let rho_E^0 and rho_E^1 be its environment states
for computational basis inputs |0>,|1>. Set

  D=||rho_E^0-rho_E^1||_1.

For any deterministic recovery D_rec:B->A', let its Bell-input fidelity be
1-e. Purify the recovery into A'F. Apply V and this purification to one half
of |Phi>_RA=(|00>+|11>)/sqrt(2), obtaining a pure state |Psi>_RA'FE.
Uhlmann's theorem supplies a pure |chi>_FE such that

  || |Psi><Psi| - |Phi><Phi|_RA' tensor |chi><chi|_FE ||_1
       <=2sqrt(e).                                             (1)

Trace A'F and measure R in the computational basis. On the actual state, both
outcomes have probability 1/2 and their conditional E states are rho_E^0 and
rho_E^1. On the comparison state, both have the same E state sigma_E. Trace-norm
contractivity and block diagonality give

  (||rho_E^0-sigma_E||_1+||rho_E^1-sigma_E||_1)/2 <=2sqrt(e).

The triangle inequality therefore implies

  D <=4sqrt(e),       e >= D^2/16.                            (2)

One may replace E by any environment subsystem or any measurement on it. This
lemma includes arbitrarily complicated receivers, fresh independent ancillas,
quantum memories, collective processing within B, and error correction.

2. EXACT MULTIPLICATIVITY OF OPTIMAL DETERMINISTIC BELL RECOVERY

For any bipartite state rho_RB with dim R=d, define

  F_opt(rho)=max over CPTP D:B->A' of
             <Phi_d|(id_R tensor D)(rho)|Phi_d>.

The semidefinite program is

  d F_opt(rho)=min TrY
      subject to Y>=0 and rho<=I_R tensor Y.                   (3)

Its dual is

  max Tr(rho X)
      subject to X>=0 and Tr_R X<=I_B.                         (4)

The correspondence with recovery maps follows from
X=d(id tensor D^dagger)(|Phi_d><Phi_d|). CPTP maps give Tr_R X=I_B.
Conversely an inequality-feasible X can be completed to equality with a positive
operator; because rho>=0 this never reduces its objective. The Choi
correspondence then gives a recovery. Standard finite-dimensional SDP duality
equates (3) and (4).

For two independent states rho and sigma, tensor products of feasible Y's in
(3) are feasible for rho tensor sigma. Tensor products of feasible X's in (4)
are also feasible. These two inequalities prove equality:

  F_opt(rho tensor sigma)=F_opt(rho)F_opt(sigma).              (5)

This is not an assumption that a collective decoder acts separately. It proves
that a collective decoder cannot improve this particular full-input Bell
fidelity beyond the product of the one-copy optima.

Continuous field output B may be infinite-dimensional. Its finite-time Choi
state is trace class. Project its B marginal onto a finite-rank subspace with
arbitrarily small tail and normalize the projected state. The resulting state
converges in trace norm. F_opt is trace-norm continuous: every recovery pulls
the Bell projector back to an effect between 0 and I, so changing rho by delta
changes F_opt by at most ||delta||_1. The finite-dimensional identity (5)
therefore passes to the limit. It applies to the finite-time continuous fields
below without assuming a finite temporal-mode model.

For any trace-nonincreasing success map D_s, let s be its Bell-input success
probability and F_s its conditional Bell fidelity. Complete D_s to a CPTP map
by preparing an arbitrary output state on failure. The deterministic Bell
overlap is at least the success contribution sF_s. Thus

  sF_s <= F_opt(rho).                                         (6)

Success may depend on the input; only its value on the stated Bell input is
used. This extension needs no conditional version of the Uhlmann argument.

The SDP/recovery identity is established primary theory: R. Koenig, R. Renner,
and C. Schaffner, The Operational Meaning of Min- and Max-Entropy, IEEE
Transactions on Information Theory 55, 4337-4347 (2009),
https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.1338,
https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2009.2025545.
The tensor-product and heralded bounds above have been included explicitly.

PART II. THE ACTUAL LOSSY-WEYL-SOURCE THEOREM

3. PHYSICAL MODEL

One source qubit has H=0 and independent actual coherent vacuum ports

  L_x=sqrt(r)X/2, L_y=sqrt(r)Y/2, L_z=sqrt(r)Z/2.              (7)

Its reduced channel obeys

  d rho/dt = (r/4) sum_{mu=x,y,z}(sigma_mu rho sigma_mu-rho)
           = r(I Tr(rho)/2-rho).

In particular the Bloch vector decays as exp(-rt). Split each actual outgoing
port with a passive loss beamsplitter of transmissivity eta. The receiver gets
the transmitted ports. The lost ports and the source remaining at time T are
inaccessible. The lost coupling operator for the Z port is

  L_lost,Z=sqrt((1-eta)r) Z/2.                                (8)

This is actual collection loss in the physical dilation. It is not a
relabeling of the Lindblad generator as a different hidden-noise mechanism.
Any causal output-only receiver is included in an arbitrary final operation on
the collected fields; a chiral receiver does not change the source dynamics.

4. AN EARLY LOST MODE RECORDS THE INITIAL Z BIT

First suppose T>=1/r. In the lost Z field choose the normalized temporal mode

  f(t)=sqrt[2r/(1-exp(-2))] exp(-rt),  0<=t<=1/r,
  f(t)=0 otherwise,
  B=integral f(t)^* b_lost,Z,out(t) dt.                       (9)

The output field has canonical commutator [B,B^dagger]=1. For source inputs
|+Z> and |-Z>, input-output theory gives

  <b_lost,Z,out(t)>_plus/minus
      =plus/minus sqrt((1-eta)r) exp(-rt)/2.

Consequently

  <B>_plus/minus=plus/minus mu,
  mu^2=(1-eta)(1-exp(-2))/8.                                  (10)

This first moment already certifies information in an inaccessible subsystem.
To turn it into a trace-distance lower bound without a bounded observable, a
uniform second-moment bound is needed.

For t>=s the normally ordered output correlation, evaluated by a single
vacuum collision at s followed by source evolution, is

  <b_lost^dagger(t)b_lost(s)>
    =Tr[L_lost^dagger exp((t-s)L_generator)(L_lost rho(s))]
    =((1-eta)r/4) exp[-r(t-s)].                               (11)

The last equality uses exp(t L_generator^dagger)(Z)=exp(-rt)Z and Z^2=I.
For t<s take the conjugate. Equation (11) is independent of the initial source
state. The integral kernel exp(-r|t-s|) on [0,1/r] has operator norm <=2/r by
the Schur test. Normalization of f therefore gives

  N_B:=<B^dagger B><=(1-eta)/2<=1/2.                          (12)

Use the normalized quadrature Q=(B+B^dagger)/sqrt(2). It has means
plus/minus sqrt(2)mu. Cauchy-Schwarz gives

  |<B^2>| <= sqrt[N_B(N_B+1)],
  <Q^2> <= N_B+1/2+sqrt[N_B(N_B+1)] <2.                      (13)

The loose bound 2 is enough. It is essential to specify this quadrature
normalization; B+B^dagger by itself would not satisfy the same bound.

Let p_plus and p_minus be the quadrature outcome measures, and let
D_Q=||p_plus-p_minus||_1 denote their unhalved total-variation norm. Put
lambda=p_plus+p_minus and w=(p_plus-p_minus)/lambda. Then |w|<=1, so

  |<Q>_plus-<Q>_minus|^2
    =|integral x w(x)d lambda|^2
    <=(integral x^2 d lambda)(integral w(x)^2 d lambda)
    <=(<Q^2>_plus+<Q^2>_minus)D_Q
    <=4D_Q.

By (10), the left side is 8mu^2. Measurement contractivity gives

  ||rho_lost^plus-rho_lost^minus||_1 >= D_Q >=2mu^2
      =(1-eta)(1-exp(-2))/4.                                (14)

Later source dynamics and receiver processing cannot remove this already
emitted lost-field marginal. Combining (14) and the general lemma (2) proves

  e >= c_0(1-eta)^2,
  c_0=(1-exp(-2))^2/256,                                    (15)

for every recovery at every T>=1/r.

5. SHORT ACQUISITION TIMES AND A UNIFORM BOUND

If T<1/r, the inaccessible remaining source alone distinguishes the two Z
inputs with trace distance

  ||rho_S^plus(T)-rho_S^minus(T)||_1=2exp(-rT)>2/exp(1).

Equation (2) gives

  e > exp(-2)/4.

This is stronger than (15), because c_0<exp(-2)/4 and (1-eta)^2<=1.
Thus (15) is valid uniformly for all deterministic acquisition times T>=0.
No optimization of the constant is attempted; its positive, time-uniform
dependence on the physical lost fraction supplies the architecture boundary.

6. ARBITRARILY MANY UNKNOWN QUBITS AND COLLECTIVE DECODING

Take n independent copies of (7), each with the same collection eta and no
source control. The input may be an arbitrary n-qubit state entangled with a
reference. Evaluate the channel on n Bell pairs. Before decoding, its Choi
state is a tensor product of the single-site Choi states. Equations (5) and
(15) give

  F_Bell,opt(n,T) <= [1-c_0(1-eta)^2]^n                       (16)

for every T, even with an arbitrary joint decoder across all collected fields,
unlimited initially independent protected ancillas, and any later computation.
Different deterministic local acquisition durations satisfy the analogous
product bound. A bounded-time causal stopping protocol is included by granting
the decoder the entire field up to its latest possible stopping time.

For heralded recovery, equation (6) yields

  s F_Bell,conditional <= [1-c_0(1-eta)^2]^n.                 (17)

Hence any fixed nonvanishing Bell-input success probability forces conditional
Bell fidelity to zero as n grows when eta<1 is fixed. High conditional fidelity
can remain compatible with exponentially small success; no stronger success
tradeoff is asserted.

The full-input diamond error of any deterministic recovered channel is at
least

  2{1-[1-c_0(1-eta)^2]^n},                                  (18)

because a Bell-projector measurement distinguishes its output from the target
Bell state by at least twice its Bell infidelity.

For small requested diamond error epsilon, (18) gives the necessary condition

  n c_0(1-eta)^2 <= -log(1-epsilon/2),

using (1-x)^n<=exp(-nx). Therefore collection loss must at least decrease as
O(sqrt(epsilon/n)) for this family if one wants full arbitrary-input accuracy.
This is a necessary scaling, not an achievability claim or an optimized loss
threshold. Later error correction alone cannot change the initial inaccessible
field in (14).

PART III. EXACT ORDINARY AMPLITUDE-EMISSION COMPARISON

7. INACCESSIBLE MODE FRACTION IS EXACTLY AMPLITUDE DAMPING

Consider an ordinary source that coherently emits a qubit as

  |0> -> |vac>,             |1> -> |1_f>,

where f is a normalized one-photon wavepacket. Let the receiver access only a
one-particle subspace A_field, with squared projection eta. Orthogonal lost
modes E_field then give the isometry

  |0> -> |0>_A|0>_E,
  |1> -> sqrt(eta)|1>_A|0>_E+sqrt(1-eta)|0>_A|1>_E.           (19)

Tracing E is precisely the qubit amplitude-damping channel AD_eta with

  K_0=diag(1,sqrt(eta)),
  K_1=sqrt(1-eta)|0><1|.

The receiver is generously granted all retained modes, arbitrary later gates,
and unlimited independent ancillary systems. Any causal implementation is a
special case of this access model.

8. EXACT SHARP BELL-RECOVERY FIDELITY

The normalized Bell-output state in basis R0A0,R0A1,R1A0,R1A1 is

  rho_eta=(1/2) [[1,0,0,sqrt(eta)],
                [0,0,0,0],
                [0,0,1-eta,0],
                [sqrt(eta),0,0,eta]].

Define the positive operator on A

  Y_eta=(1/2)diag(1+sqrt(eta), eta+sqrt(eta)).                  (20)

Then rho_eta<=I_R tensor Y_eta. Indeed, their difference has nonnegative
middle diagonal entries and corner block

  (sqrt(eta)/2) [[1,-1],[-1,1]],

which is positive. For any CPTP decoder D,

  <Phi|(id tensor D)(rho_eta)|Phi>
    <=<Phi|I tensor D(Y_eta)|Phi>
    =Tr(Y_eta)/2
    =(1+sqrt(eta))^2/4=:f(eta).                              (21)

The identity decoder attains (21). Thus the strongest possible ordinary
deterministic correction, including arbitrary ancillas, is no better than
retaining the received single-rail qubit in this Bell-fidelity metric.

Tensoring the positive inequality in (20) gives, for any global n-qubit decoder,

  F_Bell,opt(AD_eta^tensor n)=f(eta)^n.                       (22)

The product identity decoder attains the upper bound. This elementary witness
is a concrete instance of the general SDP product rule, and independently
checks its normalization.

Any heralded decoder also satisfies

  s F_conditional<=f(eta)^n.                                 (23)

Therefore deterministic full-channel diamond error is at least

  2[1-f(eta)^n].                                             (24)

At any fixed eta<1, the exact optimal Bell fidelity tends to zero and this
diamond lower bound tends to its maximal value 2.

9. FIXED HARD SPECTRAL ACCESS FOR AN ORDINARY MARKOV EMITTER

An initially unknown two-level source with ordinary amplitude-emission
coupling sqrt(gamma) sigma_minus emits, at infinite time, the normalized mode

  f(t)=sqrt(gamma)exp(-gamma t/2)1_{t>=0}.

In a rotating frame with Fourier convention 1/sqrt(2pi), its spectral density
is

  |f_tilde(omega)|^2=(gamma/(2pi))/(omega^2+(gamma/2)^2).       (25)

Suppose only frequencies |omega|<=B are ever accessible, and the complementary
spectral modes are irreversibly discarded. Granting the receiver every in-band
mode for all time is stronger than any causal finite-time receiver. Its
available fraction is

  eta_B=(2/pi)arctan(2B/gamma).                              (26)

Thus (22)-(24) apply with eta=eta_B. For epsilon in (0,1), a necessary condition
for unhalved diamond error <=epsilon on n arbitrary unknown qubits is

  eta_B >= [2(1-epsilon/2)^(1/(2n))-1]^2=:eta_req,
  B >= (gamma/2)tan(pi eta_req/2).                            (27)

In the small-error, large-bandwidth regime,

  1-eta_B = gamma/(pi B)+O((gamma/B)^3),
  1-f(eta_B)=(1-eta_B)/2+O((1-eta_B)^2).

Consequently the necessary aggregate accepted half-bandwidth scales as

  B=Omega(n gamma/epsilon),                                 (28)

with leading inversion B approximately n gamma/(pi epsilon) when the
asymptotic expansions apply. This conclusion is not about a single resonator's
linewidth. Many fixed-linewidth cells may improve collective acceptance with
hardware. It is specifically about a permanently inaccessible frequency
subspace of fixed width.

Equation (26) belongs to the amplitude-emission example. It is not the loss
law of the depolarizing Weyl output. Part II establishes that source's loss
boundary directly in its actual dilation.

PART IV. PHYSICAL AND ARCHITECTURAL CONSEQUENCE

10. WHAT THE CONVERSES DECIDE

The positive bounded-qubit receiver uses more hardware to reduce coherent
approximation and saturation errors at fixed local strength. The present
converses show why three apparently similar statements must remain distinct:

  - Fixed local coupling/linewidth with growing collective hardware can be
    compatible with arbitrarily accurate coherent acquisition.
  - A fixed aggregate inaccessible spectral subspace can impose a full-input
    accuracy floor even when later memory and decoding are ideal.
  - Fixed physical collection loss in the actual Weyl source already gives
    an exponential many-input fidelity ceiling, independent of receiver size.

Passive spatial dilution is not a loss-correcting encoding: the logical vacuum
and a distributed single excitation become indistinguishable when that
excitation is lost. Extra splitter stages and longer paths therefore cannot
be treated as free if each has fixed nonzero loss. Similarly, an early material
T1 event while the unencoded amplitude is being loaded is outside the ideal
coherent receiver proof. A later fault-tolerant processor does not retroactively
restore the subsystem that left through an inaccessible port.

To evade these bounds one must change a resource that appears in the actual
interface: collect the missing modes, decrease physical ingress loss with
hardware, encode before the loss, supply protected initial payload access, or
change/control the emitter. Each can be a valid architecture, but each must be
charged and compared with the strongest ordinary construction under the same
access. Offline entanglement purification and teleporting a later payload are
different operations and are not contradicted here.

Theorems A and B are resource converses, not zero-capacity theorems. In
particular, coding over deliberately prepared inputs can exploit redundancy
that is absent when n arbitrary unknown qubits must already cross n interfaces
without upstream encoding. The fidelity product rule concerns preservation of
that entire n-qubit input dimension, not transmission of a smaller encoded
logical space.
