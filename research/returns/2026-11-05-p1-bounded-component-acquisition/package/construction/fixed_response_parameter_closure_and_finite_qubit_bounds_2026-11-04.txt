MAIN CONCLUSIONS

1. The proposed finite-qubit capture-tail and calibration bounds are correct
   uniformly in diamond norm for the retained source/absorber/store systems,
   with the ongoing field traced. They require no photon cutoff or linear
   oscillator approximation.
2. All readout-actuator tails can be removed from the infinite-horizon
   difficulty by bounded-Hamiltonian Duhamel BEFORE applying a finite-window
   rail/photon comparison.
3. The stated switching majorant C z^11 exp[-(z-a)/4] is correct.
4. The unshifted z=2c+2*44^2 construction is safe, but its guard contains a
   term proportional to t1. A shifted explicit construction given below
   preserves a logarithmic guard even when the source horizon is polynomial.
5. A certified monotone doubling algorithm terminates with the same bound.

1. NOTATION AND THE RETAINED-CHANNEL SCOPE

Let

  F = n(q^2-1)                      number of original physical ports,
  ell = sqrt(r)/q,
  R = F ell^2,
  P = number of equal coherent rails per original port,
  N = number of two-level absorbers per rail,
  D = F P N                         number of absorber/store pairs.

Each original source coupling has norm ell. After the exact calibrated
coherent fanout, the source coupling on one rail is S=L_source/sqrt(P),
with norm ell/sqrt(P). Known phases may be absorbed into the coupling
operators. Write c_j=g_j sigma_j^- for the absorbers on that rail, and
C=sum_j c_j.

For a one-way cascade the finite-system generator contains

  D[S+C],
  H_source--collector = Im(C^dagger S),
  H_collector--collector = sum_(j>k) Im(c_j^dagger c_k),

in addition to common source and local-control Hamiltonians. Here
D[A](rho)=A rho A^dagger-{A^dagger A,rho}/2 and
Im(X)=(X-X^dagger)/(2i). There is one such field term per rail.
The source Hamiltonian is counted once globally, not once per rail.

These are algebraic generators for the equivalent feedforward cascade.
They do not grant a physical all-to-all Hamiltonian. The actual implementation
uses the finite-delay paths and retarded local schedules in the companion
physical note. The one-way circuit can be topologically reordered into the
retarded-time cascade. Once the readout qubits are isolated, this yields the
same final retained-register channel. No feedback to the source is allowed
in this equivalence.

All bounds below are complete-channel bounds for retained finite systems,
including arbitrary inert external references. The future output field is
traced. They are NOT bounds on the outgoing-field Stinespring dilation itself.
That distinction matters: a small spontaneous-emission probability can have
an amplitude-sized effect on a joint field dilation while causing only a
probability-sized error on the reduced finite system.

2. UNIFORM FINITE-QUBIT TAIL BOUND

Suppose |g_j(t)|<=delta on a readout interval, and compare with the same
source/readout program but zero collector couplings. For one rail,

  ||C||<=N delta.

Expanding the dissipator gives

  ||D[S+C]-D[S]-D[C]||_diamond <=4||S||||C||,
  ||D[C]||_diamond <=2||C||^2.

The source--collector Hamiltonian contributes at most 2||S||||C|| to the
commutator-superoperator norm. There are N(N-1)/2 collector--collector pairs;
their Hamiltonian contribution is at most N(N-1)delta^2. Hence the one-rail
generator difference is at most

  6 ell N delta/sqrt(P) +3N^2 delta^2.

Summing F original ports and P rails yields

  ||L_actual-L_zero||_diamond
      <=6F ell N sqrt(P) delta+3F P N^2 delta^2.              (2.1)

For time-dependent delta(t), integrate the right-hand side. For a constant
upper bound delta on duration L_rd,

  epsilon_capture_tail
      <=L_rd[6F ell N sqrt(P) delta+3FPN^2 delta^2].          (2.2)

Proof of integration: the Duhamel formula for the difference of two
propagators has CPTP maps on both sides of the generator difference.
Their diamond norm is one, so the integral of the generator-norm bound
is sufficient. There is no exponential of the common Hamiltonian norm.

The common XY readout cancels exactly. In fact this generator comparison
does not require that a common Hamiltonian be passive; passivity is needed
elsewhere for the mode identification, not for (2.1). The bound is uniform
on all finite-qubit states, including sectors with many excitations.

3. UNIFORM CALIBRATION BOUND

Let the nominal absorber coefficients have magnitude at most g0, and let
the coefficient error on each absorber be at most delta<=g0. The actual
coefficients are therefore bounded by 2g0. The source couplings and the
local readout Hamiltonians are common in this comparison.

For one rail, put J=S+C and J'=S+C'. Then

  ||J||<=ell/sqrt(P)+Ng0,
  ||J'||<=ell/sqrt(P)+2Ng0,
  ||J'-J||<=N delta.

The general dissipator difference bound

  ||D[A]-D[B]||_diamond
        <=2(||A||+||B||)||A-B||

gives a contribution at most

  4 ell N delta/sqrt(P)+6N^2 g0 delta.

The source--collector Hamiltonian adds
2 ell N delta/sqrt(P). For each collector pair,

  ||c_j'^dagger c_k'-c_j^dagger c_k||
       <=delta*2g0+g0*delta=3g0 delta.

After summing its commutator bound over pairs, the collector--collector
Hamiltonian adds at most 3N^2 g0 delta. Therefore

  ||L_actual-L_nominal||_diamond
      <=[6F ell N sqrt(P)+9F P N^2 g0]delta.                 (3.1)

Over duration A,

  epsilon_calibration
      <=A[6F ell N sqrt(P)+9FPN^2 g0]delta.                 (3.2)

This is again uniform without a photon cutoff. It applies to coefficient
calibration, including phase errors or clock errors that have already been
converted to coefficient errors. Source-coupling errors, fanout imperfections,
and readout-Hamiltonian errors require their own additional terms. A source
reduced-generator match alone does not fix the coherent S operators used here.

4. THE ACTUAL THREE-POLE CONTROLS

Let F3(x)=0 for x<0 and

  F3(x)=1-exp(-x)(1+x+x^2/2),  x>=0.

Use a fixed response pole B>0 and fixed peak coefficients g0,Omega0>0.
The capture command is on until t1:

  g(t)=g0[F3(Bt)-F3(B(t-t1))].

Define the guard G>=0 and the readout command duration

  tau0=pi/(2Omega0),
  t2=t1+G,
  t3=t2+tau0+G,
  A=t3=t1+tau0+2G.

The readout coefficient is

  Omega(t)=Omega0[F3(B(t-t2))-F3(B(t-t2-tau0))].

Because the controller has unit DC gain,

  integral_(-infinity)^infinity Omega(t)dt=Omega0*tau0=pi/2.

The peak couplings and their response poles are fixed. The local exchange
Hamiltonian has norm |Omega(t)|. No exact Fourier cutoff or exact finite-time
actuator stop is claimed.

At all t>=t2,

  |g(t)|<=delta=2g0 exp(-BG/2).                               (4.1)

The remaining readout pulse area after t3 obeys

  a_tail=integral_(t3)^infinity |Omega(t)|dt
      <=(Omega0/B) exp(-BG)[3+2BG+(BG)^2/2]
      <=(4Omega0/B) exp(-BG/2).                             (4.2)

For (4.2), the maximum of
[3+2x+x^2/2]exp(-x/2) occurs at x=sqrt(2) and is at most four;
exp(u)>=1+u proves that final numerical inequality. The bound (4.1) follows
from the corresponding three-pole response tail.

5. CORRECT PROOF ORDER FOR THE INFINITE READOUT TAIL

An infinite actual tail must not be included in a literal Poisson
single-photon-per-rail window. Doing that would eventually make collisions
certain. The complete finite-qubit estimates permit a different order.

First compare the ACTUAL architecture with one in which each actual readout
Hamiltonian is set to zero after its local retarded time t3. This is a
mathematical comparison, not an assumed instantaneous hardware switch.
Bounded-Hamiltonian Duhamel gives

  epsilon_future_readout<=2D a_tail.

This is uniform regardless of arbitrarily many future source photons and
regardless of the absorber's future state. It follows directly from the
integral of the norm of the omitted local Hamiltonians.

In this finite-window comparison architecture, the storage qubits are
isolated after t3. Their physical delayed network has the retained-register
channel of the retarded cascade. Compare the capture couplings during
[t2,t3] with zero, keeping the same finite readout Hamiltonian in both.
Equation (2.2) gives

  epsilon_capture_tail
    <=(tau0+G)[6F ell N sqrt(P) delta+3FPN^2 delta^2].         (5.1)

The reference capture nodes are now isolated from the incident field during
readout. By t3, the local exchange has accumulated angle pi/2-a_tail.
Comparing this with the completed ideal SWAP costs at most

  epsilon_missing_swap<=2D a_tail.

Thus all switching/readout tails are bounded by

  epsilon_switch
    <=(tau0+G)[6F ell N sqrt(P) delta+3FPN^2 delta^2]
      +4D a_tail.                                          (5.2)

Only AFTER these uniform finite-qubit comparisons does one use the ideal
rail-good-sector and finite-photon comparisons. The conservative counting
window is the finite A=t3. There is no infinite-time collision event in the
argument. The retained-channel scope of (2.1) is sufficient for the physical
output registers and their subsequent computation.

An optional sharper variant integrates (2.1) to infinity. With (4.1)'s
exponential envelope shifted to the actual time,

  epsilon_capture_tail
     <=(24F ell g0/B)N sqrt(P) exp(-BG/2)
       +(12F g0^2/B)P N^2 exp(-BG).

This bounds any finite readout interval and eliminates its duration from the
main-tail estimate. It is not needed for the explicit closure below.

6. GUARD-INDEPENDENT INPUT DATA AND POLYNOMIAL MAJORANTS

The useful source horizon T, omitted initial interval t0, t1=t0+T, number of
physical-time bins J and bin width Delta=T/J are selected before the guard.
Their source-forgetting, source-splitting, bin-collision and leading-edge
budgets are unchanged.

For the three-pole envelope, use the guard-INDEPENDENT area upper bound

  X_* = g0^2[t1+33/(16B)].                                  (6.1)

Use any certified guard-independent positive lower bound kappa_min on the
useful interval [t0,t1], and a bound D_kappa on |kappa'| there. For example,

  kappa_min >=g0^2 exp(-2) min{Bt0,1}^6/36,
  D_kappa<=2g0^2 B.

These depend on the startup and useful interval, not on the length of the
later guard. They are inverse-polynomial/polynomial in the original inputs.

Introduce

  z=1+BA,
  a=1+B(t1+tau0),

so

  z=a+2BG,   z>=a>=1.                                      (6.2)

For a number-tail probability p_num in (0,1), take

  Q=ceil[(e-1)RA+log(1/p_num)].

Then

  Q<=q0 z,
  q0=1+(e-1)R/B+log(1/p_num).                              (6.3)

For a mode error epsilon_modes in (0,1], set

  zeta=epsilon_modes^2/(16Q^2).

Write a conservative Gram prescription as

  N=ceil max{J, a4*zeta^(-2)+a2*zeta^(-1)},                 (6.4)

where one sufficient choice from the physical note is

  a4=256 X_* J^2/(kappa_min^2 Delta^2),
  a2=4 X_* J D_kappa^2/kappa_min^4+2X_*J.

The slightly smaller coefficients in the digital-processing note also work.
Define

  b0=16q0^2/epsilon_modes^2,
  M0=1+J+a4 b0^2+a2 b0.

Then, including all ceiling operations,

  N<=M0 z^4.                                                (6.5)

For the rail error epsilon_rail, put

  cP=8F ell^4/epsilon_rail^2,
  P=nextpow2(max{1,cP A^2}),
  P0=2 max{1,cP/B^2}.

Thus

  P<=P0 z^2.                                                (6.6)

The conservative source-independent numerator 8nr^2/epsilon_rail^2 may be
used instead of cP; the proof is unchanged. Define

  D0=F P0 M0,

so D=FPN<=D0 z^6. Every coefficient q0,M0,P0,D0 is fixed before G and is a
fixed-degree polynomial upper bound in the supplied input/resource data
(with logarithms such as log(1/p_num) also explicitly specified).

7. AUDIT OF THE SWITCHING MAJORANT

Let

  v=exp[-(z-a)/4]=exp(-BG/2).

Then delta<=2g0 v and a_tail<=4Omega0 v/B. Also

  tau0+G<=A<z/B.

The three terms in (5.2) are consequently bounded by

  linear capture:    (12F ell g0 M0 sqrt(P0)/B) z^6 v,
  quadratic capture:(12F P0 M0^2 g0^2/B) z^11 v^2,
  readout remainder:(16D0 Omega0/B) z^6 v.

Since z>=1 and v<=1, their sum is at most

  epsilon_switch<=C z^11 exp[-(z-a)/4],                      (7.1)

  C=12F ell g0 M0 sqrt(P0)/B
       +12F P0 M0^2 g0^2/B+16D0 Omega0/B.                   (7.2)

These are exactly the proposed constants. The factor z^11 openly includes
the accuracy-dependent duration tau0+G of the actual readout interval.
The old constant-duration F_mod estimate must not be used unchanged when
a new readout-tail waiting interval has been added; (7.1) avoids that issue.

8. CLOSED EXPLICIT LOGARITHMIC GUARD

The proposed unshifted choice

  c=a+4log max{e,C/eta_switch},
  z_old=2c+2*44^2,
  G_old=(z_old-a)/(2B)

is SAFE: log z<=sqrt(z), followed by Young's inequality, verifies
z_old>=c+44log z_old. However, G_old contains a/(2B), hence a term
proportional to t1. It is logarithmic only when t1 itself is logarithmic,
as in the fixed-positive-gap regime. It remains polynomial in general,
but is not the strongest guard claim for an inverse-polynomial source gap.

A shifted explicit choice removes that unnecessary term. Set

  b=4log max{e, C a^11/eta_switch},
  y_*=2b+2*44^2,
  z_*=a+y_*,
  G_*=y_*/(2B).                                             (8.1)

This is a closed formula using only the guard-independent constants.
There is no remaining parameter self-reference.

Proof: for a>=1 and y>=0,

  (a+y)^11<=a^11(1+y)^11,
  log(1+y)<=sqrt(y).

The second inequality follows by integrating
1/(1+t)<=1/(2sqrt(t)), which is equivalent to 1+t>=2sqrt(t).
Young's inequality gives

  44sqrt(y)<=y/2+44^2/2.

At y=y_*, these inequalities imply

  y_*>=b+44log(1+y_*).

Therefore

  C(a+y_*)^11 exp(-y_*/4)
      <=C a^11 exp(-b/4)
      <=eta_switch.                                        (8.2)

In particular,

  G_*=O(B^(-1)[1+log_+(C/eta_switch)+log a]).                  (8.3)

Here log_+(u)=max{0,log u}. Because C and a are fixed-degree polynomial
upper bounds in the original input/resource parameters, (8.3) is O(B^(-1) log polynomial(input)), even
when t1 is polynomial because the source mixing margin is small. Local
response poles, coupling strengths and local Hilbert dimensions remain fixed.

9. MONOTONE DOUBLING ALGORITHM

A direct evaluation of (8.1), rounded upward, is sufficient. The following
monotone integer algorithm is an alternative that can use certified scalar
arithmetic and has a finite termination bound.

  1. Compute a,q0,M0,P0,D0,C from the guard-independent data.
  2. Set y=64.
  3. Evaluate the upper bound

       log C+11log(a+y)-y/4.

     If it is at most log(eta_switch), stop. Otherwise set y=2y
     and return to step 3.
  4. Set G=y/(2B), A=t1+tau0+2G, then compute the actual integer
     Q, the actual zeta, and the actual N,P using Section 6.

One may recompute Q,N,P at each iteration if desired; they are nondecreasing
as y grows. It is unnecessary because the fixed majorants already cover them.
The stopping certificate is monotone for y>=64:

  d/dy [log C+11log(a+y)-y/4]
       =11/(a+y)-1/4<0.

The explicit y_* from (8.1) is at least 2*44^2, and guarantees the certificate.
Therefore doubling stops by the first dyadic y>=y_*, with returned y<2y_*.
The number of iterations is at most

  1+ceil(log_2(max{1,y_*/64})).

Certified upper bounds for logarithms prevent false acceptance; alternatively
use the explicit guard with a small positive numerical slack. This is a
finite constructive design algorithm, not an appeal to an unspecified fixed
point or an unproved asymptotic existence statement.

10. COMPLETE FINITENESS AND REMAINING ERROR ACCOUNTS

At the returned guard, z=a+O(log polynomial(input)), so

  Q<=q0 z,
  N<=M0 z^4,
  P<=P0 z^2,
  D<=D0 z^6

are all polynomial. Source time bins and source Hamiltonian simulation costs
were fixed earlier. Digital processing remains the specified fixed-degree
polynomial in these quantities. The safe planar layout's actual path length,
propagation latency, and protected-storage lifetime are also polynomial.

Calibration precision can now be selected from (3.2), and readout-control
errors can be charged by their bounded-Hamiltonian integrals. These choices
occur AFTER the final dimensions/time are known and create no new guard loop.
Preparation actuator tails are removed sufficiently early before local input
arrival; their guards cost preparation time and do not enlarge the source
photon-count window. Memory error/QEC and actual propagation loss remain the
explicit independent budgets of the physical note.

All retained-channel tail comparisons precede the finite-window rail and
number-sector comparisons. This ordering is essential when the actual
actuators have infinite exponentially decaying tails. It also establishes why
arbitrarily many photons emitted at later times do not invalidate the proof.

No claim about proximity of an uncollected future field dilation is needed.
The completed target is the stored register channel, with arbitrary external
reference entanglement, followed by the already constructed decoder.
