# Separate operations: continuum completion, clock assignment and relative preparation

These companions to [direct mean prevention](README.md) have different objectives or stronger access assumptions. They remain independently scoped in the original package.

## Continuum desired-completion minimization

The [continuum report](package/next6_direct_decision/continuum_completion.pdf), [proof source](package/next6_direct_decision/continuum_completion.tex) and [status](package/next6_direct_decision/STATUS.txt) assume an additional certified reciprocal kinetic multiplier response c. The objective is f(c)=pi(B) R M(c). Convexity, gradient homogeneity and curvature support a finite-confidence continuum optimizer with an observable gap, using finite scalar short reverse/no-return oracles and a nonrare fallback.

This operation minimizes a desired completion time. It is not a general maximization theorem for catastrophe prevention. It assumes an equilibrium-conditioned safe start, static operating access, and paid finite slew/deployment error. Unbounded-mean abort effects are treated separately. The response law is an actuator premise, not something inferred for arbitrary field changes.

A multiplier bounded by C improves mean completion time by at most C; actual completion waiting can remain exponential. The [sufficient schedule](package/next6_direct_decision/sufficient_schedule.json) evaluates an N=128 bound of roughly 10^61.65 potential updates. That is neither an executed optimization nor a lower bound. Convex Markov design, potential arguments and Frank-Wolfe methods are credited; no economical prototype or priority claim is established.

## Assigning transferable clock modules

The [sorting intervention proof](package/next6_decision_theory/sorting_intervention.tex) addresses n! assignments under narrower independent-component and transferable clock-module premises. It supplies exact and acquired near-optimal assignments, dimension-independent parameter sensitivity, and finite holds in the actual contexts.

It does not solve arbitrary interacting control menus. Ordinary rearrangement comparisons remain relevant; this theorem does not establish stochastic ordering of every safe-start catastrophe distribution or a full CDF promise. See the supplied [decision-theory status](package/next6_decision_theory/STATUS.txt).

## Stronger relative-density equilibrium preparation

The [relative-density proof](package/next6_preparation/relative_density_preparation.tex) and [status](package/next6_preparation/STATUS.txt) retain a stronger preparation service for tasks that need approximate equilibrium, rather than the main mean-only construction's uniform safe-start bound.

Premises include a supplied positive conditional floor b, certified spectral influence norm at most 1-m, an actual reciprocal clock backbone r_min, model closure, the actual post-ramp generator, repairability and an exogenous timer. The entropy constant is

```
C_LS = 4 / (b m^2 r_min).
T = (C_LS/2) log(max(1, log(1/pi_floor)))
    + gap^-1 log(e^2/epsilon),     pi_floor = b^N.
```

The result gives uniform pointwise density within 1+/-epsilon from every initial law. At fixed margins this is order log N native wall time and order N log N summed component exposure. The spectral norm premise is stronger than a mere row-margin condition; one cannot substitute the latter silently. The example b=0.025, m=0.5 and r_min=0.2 gives C_LS=3200.

The [inspection interface](package/next6_preparation/inspection_interface.md) distinguishes finite actual noisy inspection from perfect inspection and explicitly pays retry costs. Without the stronger acquired influence norm, the gap-only fallback retains its log(1/pi_min) cost. This relative-equilibrium branch is useful for broader forecast tasks; the [main assay/deployment page](assay-and-commissioning.md) obtains its mean-only guarantee directly from all-safe-start mean bounds, without inferring an unbounded mean from total variation.
