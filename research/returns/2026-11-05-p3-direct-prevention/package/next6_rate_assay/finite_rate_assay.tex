\begin{abstract}
We construct the arithmetic mean of unknown heterogeneous local operating
escape rates without recovering their full table. Four first-exit waits
at one randomly chosen requested context give a classical inverse-gamma
rate statistic with finite variance. Explicit clipping, censoring,
first-event timing, finite arithmetic and conditional service errors
yield a bounded physical trial whose second moment remains independent
of the requested accuracy. A conditional median-of-means proof gives
quadratic precision cost even for repeated cells, without assuming
independent hardware errors. The complete dwell bill includes discarded
censored trials. A two-menu rare-target family gives a matching
quadratic total-exposure lower bound even with full local trajectories,
adaptive calibrated fields and free parameter-independent resets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Executed synthetic service and scaling record}
The supplied program runs the finite direct-mean protocol at
$\eta=1/8$, $\lambda_-=1/2$, $\lambda_+=6/5$, total $K=6$ menu assays
and $\delta=0.05$. A rational upper count based on an integer
$\log_2$ ceiling gives 49,152 trials per menu, at most 196,608 pin--release
observations, $D=22$, and $\Delta=1.05964\cdot10^{-5}$ model-time units.
The clipping threshold is $a=5/12$, the output cap is $B=36/5$, and
all controls and arithmetic tolerances are recorded exactly as fractions.

The synthetic service includes a persistent detector latency
$\Delta/4$, an actual censor deadline $D+\Delta/2$, a dyadic timestamp
grid no larger than $\Delta/8$, output rounding, sequential early censor stops, and all
discarded active dwell. Thus it executes the finite observations used
by the proof; it is not a computation from an exposed kinetic table.
Reciprocal quantization and final interval endpoints use exact integer
and rational arithmetic; decimal interval displays are rounded outward.
Hidden arrays define the simulator and are inspected only by the
separate audit. Contexts are heterogeneous and indexed by a uniform
coordinate request. The two menus have different actual operating
clocks. The assumed copy fidelity, source model and bound calibration
are not themselves acquired or tested by this synthetic execution.

The relative confidence intervals are disjoint within every menu pair
and select menu 1 for smaller mean escape rate. All observed relative
errors are below $0.54\%$. This observed error is not a replacement for
the $12.5\%$ theorem guarantee. One censored trial in the largest menu-1
run, including its preceding successful waits, is present in the
discarded-dwell ledger. No global first-event law or stationary rarity
was computed in this local audit; the global decision bridge is a
separate theorem.

These time units are defined by the supplied local rates. If the rates
are interpreted in inverse seconds, an active dwell of $3.4\cdot10^5$
units is approximately four days on one serial channel, before pins,
clamps, settling, detector/timer service, calibration, finite slew, safe
mission preparation and deployment. No numerical price for those
unprovided services is invented. The timing bound is sufficient, not a
universal lower bound, and the statistical constants are conservative.
The earlier $\kappa^2$ median-of-means schedule and its larger execution
are retained as a valid development record; the final proof improves
the conditional second-moment bound to $4\lambda_+\mu$ and supplies
both the sharper median version and the direct-mean option.

High-precision integration checks the clipping loss and exact discarded
dwell formulas at four rates. Separate algebra checks the likelihood
upper bound and two-menu mean ratio at four accuracies. These are
mathematical and synthetic service diagnostics, not a physical apparatus
experiment.
\section*{Primary sources and attribution}
The inverse-gamma unbiased exponential-rate identity is standard; an
author-written derivation is Eduardo Garc\'ia-Portugu\'es and Isabel
Molina, \emph{Statistical Inference}, Chapter 3, Example 3.4,
\url{https://egarpor.github.io/SI-UC3M/point.html}.
The proof here is included in full rather than imported as a new
estimator. The confidence lower bound uses the same change-of-measure
principle as \emph{On the Complexity of Best-Arm Identification in
Multi-Armed Bandit Models}, E. Kaufmann, O. Capp\'e, and A. Garivier,
JMLR 17(1):1--42 (2016),
\url{https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume17/kaufman16a/kaufman16a.pdf}.
The controlled continuous-time likelihood bound is derived explicitly
for the present two-menu family; an iid bandit formula is not silently
transported across physical access models.
The direct-mean confidence argument is the classical martingale
Bernstein/Freedman method, here proved with the needed constants; see
D. A. Freedman, \emph{On Tail Probabilities for Martingales},
Annals of Probability 3(1):100--118 (1975), DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.1214/aop/1176996452}{10.1214/aop/1176996452}.

The operating-context, faithful-copy, calibrated-field and priced-pin
premises come from the unchanged acquired first-event package,
especially its \texttt{experimental\_operating.tex} source and the
preserved active acquisition construction. This new assay uses their
actual first-exit observation access, but does not execute the earlier
uniformized operating-coin factory or infer a clock at an unmeasured
operating field.
