1. Exact finite-LO and broadband moments

For one signal window, mix the field with an independent coherent LO of total
detected mean mu and constant phase. The integrated balanced count difference
D obeys, after tracing out the LO,

  E_LO[D/sqrt(mu)] = X_matched,
  E_LO[D^2/mu] = X_matched^2 + N_total/mu.

N_total counts ALL signal temporal modes in that window. Replacing it by the
matched-mode occupation would be incorrect for direct broadband counting.
The identity follows by writing the LO as its coherent displacement plus vacuum;
the square of the remaining signal-vacuum exchange term gives N_total. Products
factor through the independent LO spaces for disjoint port/time windows even
when the signal fields are entangled. Equal calibrated arm loss can be commuted
to signal/LO attenuation; known signal efficiency kappa is then normalized out.
No infinite-LO or stored-temporal-mode operation is used.

2. Matched LO is the preferred finite physical schedule

Set q=r w and mu=kappa q/4. After the calibrated loss, the two detected jump
operators of one Pauli port can be written

  J_+ = sqrt(kappa r/2) P_+,
  J_- = -sqrt(kappa r/2) P_-,   P_+/-=(I+/-sigma)/2.

Thus their total intensity is the scalar R=kappa r/2. Their signed jump map is
R J_sigma, where J_sigma(rho)=(sigma rho+rho sigma)/2. Let m=Rw=kappa q/2.
The normalized count D/m has exactly the source-normalized quadrature mean.
For each forward or reverse product, all s=b+1 physical ports are distinct:
the two measured axes at the shared source site anticommute and hence name
different ports. Its ideal total clocks T_j are independent Poisson(m),
conditionally on every prior source/reference/classical history. Since |D_j|<=T_j,

  E[product_j (D_j/m)^2] <= (1+1/m)^s
                          = (1+2/(kappa q))^s.

The odd difference divided by two has the same bound, without assuming its
forward and reverse products are independent. Empty windows are observations.
There is no postselection or hidden success-probability denominator.

The detected LO flux is kappa r/4 per port, independent of window width.
CW operation through the entire paid burn-in and acquisition avoids an ideal
instantaneous LO switch. It pays 3n kappa r T_wall/4 detected LO photons; actual
incident flux also pays detector attenuation. Gating instead requires actual
ramp durations, slew, service time and ramp photons. These are alternative
physical schedules, not free pulse edges.

3. Deadtime with a finite detector array

After balanced mixing, fan out each arm passively into L labelled equal rails.
Rail labels are fresh uniform marks of the incident total Poisson process;
arm signs may depend on the source, but the rail marks do not. Each detector
has a specified deadtime d0 and recovers after at most d0 in the absence of
another incident event. Nonparalyzable and ordinary paralyzable service both
admit the following upper bound. No afterpulses, feedback to the source, or
uncalibrated state-dependent efficiency are silently included.

For a window [a,a+w], let C count same-rail ordered incident pairs separated
by at most d0, including a predecessor in [a-d0,a]. A conservative bound is

  E C <= R^2 (w+d0)d0/L.

The number of missed photons, and therefore the absolute error in the signed
count, is at most C. Burn-in/guard time at least d0 removes arbitrary initial
detector blindness; CW illumination still requires the predecessor interval.

Compare UNCLIPPED products first. Product telescoping gives

 |product D_j - product D'_j| <= sum_j C_j product_{l!=j} T_l.

Each C_j depends only on port j's total clock and rail marks, and is independent
of the other selected ports' total clocks. Dividing by m^s therefore gives

  mean deadtime bias <= s R d0 (1+d0/w)/L.

Apply this separately to forward and reverse products; their odd average has
the same bound. The union of both products contains some repeated ports in
different windows, but this does not invalidate the separate-product argument.
No clipped bound K^s multiplies this mean bias. This is the essential improvement
over bounding the probability that an entire record differs.

Observed totals are at most incident totals, so the same Poisson moment and
overflow bounds remain valid after missed detections. Clip actual observations
AFTER this unclipped comparison. A local overflow flag must be available; a
silent saturating counter is not enough to implement the specified estimator.

4. Timing/latency: a direct normalized-mean bound

For a forward product the normalized expectation is

  integral C(t_1,...,t_s) product_j a_j(t_j) dt_1...dt_s,
  a_j=1_{I_j}/w,   |C(t_1,...,t_s)|<=1.

The last inequality follows from the Jordan-insertion norm bound and CPTP
propagation. It holds for arbitrary source states, all orderings of the distinct
port times, and every allowed H. No derivative of C, Hamiltonian frequency bound,
or clipping magnitude is required.

If each window is shifted by a fixed clock error of magnitude at most dt,
then ||a'_j-a_j||_1<=2dt/w and both kernels have L1 norm one. Telescoping gives

  absolute normalized mean bias <= 2s dt/w.

The same bound holds for stationary independent timestamp jitter with
E|jitter|<=dt, by convolution and averaging. More generally one may condition
on any source-independent collection of bounded clock offsets. Correlation
between those offsets is harmless. Signal-dependent, adversarial electronic
jitter is NOT covered by this kernel argument.

If the two endpoints vary independently, the actual width can also change.
With each endpoint error at most dt and normalization by the nominal w, a safe
bound is

  2s(dt/w)(1+2dt/w)^(s-1).

Use the same inequality for the odd statistic by averaging the two errors.
For an allocated response bias delta_t and dt<<w, it is sufficient to take
dt<=delta_t w/(4s), with harmless constant slack for endpoint variation.
Because the response accuracy for coefficient eta is proportional to w eta,
this gives ordinary timing precision of order eta*w^2, rather than a
K^s-amplified requirement.

Keep a clock guard so the earliest shifted window follows the paid burn-in.
For bounded jitter combined with deadtime, enlarging the photon interval to
w+2dt gives a safe combined deadtime expression

  [s R d0/L] [1+(2dt+d0)/w] (1+2dt/w)^(s-1).

Clipping bias is allocated separately. Stationary time jitter preserves the
mean Poisson window count; independently shifted endpoints can instead use
the conservative Poisson mean R(w+2dt) in moment/cap bounds.

5. Finite clipping, concentration, and calibration scope

For ideal/observed totals dominated by Poisson(m), choose K from the Poisson
tail. A coefficient's odd statistic uses at most 2s selected windows; local
overflow clipping costs at most sqrt(2s V Pr[Poisson(m)>K]), where
V=(1+1/m)^s. Its absolute clipped magnitude is at most (K/m)^s. Thus a fixed
Freedman/Bernstein schedule with terms V/delta^2 and (K/m)^s/delta is valid,
after including mean biases from burn-in, deadtime, timing and calibration.
Unrelated-port overflows do not discard the coefficient's observation.

Known coherent receiver pilots with nonzero calibrated visibility can determine
relative gain and phase: four pilot phases isolate the sine/cosine interference
terms from bounded-mean Poisson counts. Ordinary Poisson confidence then costs
O(tolerance^-2 log(n/failure)) pilot photons/exposure at fixed visibility and
bounded known efficiencies. This prices receiver calibration at its actual
injection plane. It does NOT certify loss upstream of that plane, source Pauli
axes, vacuum purity, missing ports, or completeness/stationarity of H.

A phase error phi changes the normalized insertion from J_sigma to
(exp(-i phi)sigma rho+exp(i phi)rho sigma)/2; its map difference is at most
|phi| in operator norm. Local gain/phase errors therefore telescope into an
O(s times local error) mean-bias budget. Balanced arm efficiencies are a stated
model condition; arbitrary imbalance/backgrounds are not covered by the exact
Poisson-clock formulas without a separate calibrated model or domination bound.

6. Spectral scope: a real remaining interface premise

The proof supplies finite LO energy, finite count ranges, finite timing tolerances,
and finite deadtime arrays. It ASSUMES the calibrated broadband count response
on the measured output modes. Deadtime control does not itself prove that a
microscopic detector with a fixed hard optical passband implements that POVM.
A nonflat spectral filter changes both the measured temporal-mode kernel and
the count law; its effect must be separately bounded or included in a new
response inverse. No fixed hard-passband completion has been constructed here.
The supplied L proportional to Pauli ports are not ordinary spontaneous-emission
ports merely because their intensities agree.

7. Focused inverse/connected-set check

The damping-aware Dyson bound in OUTPUT_ONLY_CLASSICAL_CALIBRATION.md is valid.
For a nonzero Pauli path, each pre-insertion waiting integral contributes
1/[r wt(A)] after extending its interval to infinity and dropping final damping.
The summed outgoing absolute commutator coefficient is at most 2J wt(A), so
each nonnegative transition column sums to at most alpha=2J/r. Identity columns
have zero Hamiltonian transitions and must be treated as zero, not divided by
zero. Hence one propagator's l-th term has coefficient-l1 norm at most alpha^l.
There are b inter-measurement gaps, giving binom(l+b-1,b-1) alpha^l. Stationary
trace reversal and self-adjoint Jordan insertions cancel every even order.

The finite-time alternative also checks: current support is at most
b+(k-1)l, and the total available time is at most 2w. The ordered simplex gives
(b/a)_l (4aJw)^l/l!, a=max(1,k-1). Telescoping one Hamiltonian difference yields
the stated remainder Lipschitz bounds. The common small-window choice gives
the advertised contraction at every positive r, with its weak-port signal cost.

For connected-set evaluation, inclusion-exclusion isolates Dyson words whose
DISTINCT interaction set is exactly S. A component disconnected from the measured
root cancels because stationary identity and local depolarization factor across
components. Omitting sets of size>L therefore omits only orders>L, including
all repetitions correctly. Root-connected sets can be enumerated in at most
(4kd)^l choices; each has support at most b+(k-1)l. The stated numerical
precision allocation pays inclusion-exclusion cancellation. This establishes
polynomial dependence on inverse accuracy at FIXED k,d; if d grows with n,
the same enumeration alone does not establish that polynomial claim.
