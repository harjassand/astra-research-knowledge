TIMING KERNELS AND THE BOUNDARY OF THE DETECTOR CONSTRUCTION
Root derivation, 5 November 2026

Use the preferred matched-LO observation of the main mathematical note.
Every unnormalized signed click insertion is R J_A, R=kappa*r/2, with
J_A(X)=(AX+XA)/2. Dividing a window count by m=Rw gives a normalized
integration kernel f(t)=1_[a,a+w](t)/w. The stationary ordered regression
kernel for any selected distinct-port product has absolute value at most 1:
all Jordan insertions and adjoint channels are contractions, and the initial
maximally mixed trace is a norm-one functional. This remains true when small
window shifts reorder two selected event times; define the kernel on the
whole time cube by the corresponding ordered regression in each simplex.

Suppose each actual window endpoint differs by at most tau. Its kernel g
obeys ||g-f||_1<=2 tau/w and ||g||_1<=1+2 tau/w. For a product of s factors,
tensor telescoping gives the exact mean-difference bound

  2 s (tau/w) (1+2 tau/w)^(s-1).

The same bound applies to the forward/reverse odd average. For tau<=w/(8s)
it is at most 4s tau/w. Therefore tau<=delta*w/[128(k+1)] pays delta/32
of normalized mean bias. Apply this comparison before finite-counter
clipping, then use the already stated clipping budget separately.

Pad the source discard interval so the full promised burn duration precedes
the earliest possibly accepted true event. Constant calibrated propagation
latency is translated in the time labels; residual uncertainty obeys the
same endpoint allowance. The actual path length and propagation delay are
paid. Keep the LO on over every possible counted window and its timing
uncertainty, or charge its actual envelope's L1 difference and ramp photons.

The argument also applies after averaging over timing offsets/jitter that
are independent of the source/count marks and obey the stated bound.
It does not apply to arbitrary outcome-dependent timing errors. Total counts
in a moved window are dominated by a Poisson count over width w+2tau. Across
the distinct ports used in each product, total-count independence gives a
second-moment enlargement at most (1+2tau/w)^(2s). This is below a fixed
factor in the allotted regime and fits the conservative statistical budget.

For simultaneous timestamp error and deadtime, enlarge the actual incident
window to w+2tau in the same-rail pair argument. Its normalized mean bound is

  [s R d0/L] [1+(2tau+d0)/w] (1+2tau/w)^(s-1).

Use this expression to choose the integer rail count when both errors are
present; the separate displayed ideal-timestamp expression is its tau=0
case. The correction is negligible at the illustrative numerical tolerance,
but is part of the finite implementation rather than an omitted cross term.

PHYSICAL LIMIT: finite detector deadtime and finite timestamp precision are
different resources. The rail construction fixes the first under its
deletion-only model. It does not create precise timestamps from a detector
with a fixed unknown latency distribution or fixed jitter floor. A direct
digital timestamp clock needs period at most the allotted tolerance, clock
frequency of order 1/tau, and finite bits of order log(T_total/tau). The
detector's actual latency uncertainty and usable bandwidth must satisfy the
same contract. These growing demands remain part of the resource bill.
A general exactly calibrated response-filter inverse could be a different
construction; it is not presumed or proved by the ideal-window argument.

The current protocol therefore does not claim fixed aggregate optical
bandwidth, fixed full detector hardware at arbitrary accuracy, or a
microscopic finite-band realization of the assumed Markov Pauli fields.
Its calibration has finite classical outputs and no quantum storage, while
its actual source ports and timing interface remain substantive resources.
