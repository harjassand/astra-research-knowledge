## 1. What the decision establishes

Consider \(N\) components, each either working or failed. Catastrophe occurs at the single configuration

\[
b=(1,\ldots,1),
\]

where every component has failed. This includes a redundant network of parallel components, with interactions between their failure and recovery dynamics.

There is a finite menu of feasible interventions. The objective is to choose an intervention whose **mean time to the first catastrophe** is within a specified multiplicative tolerance of the best menu item.

The interventions may change different local clocks in different contexts. One intervention can be faster in some configurations and slower in others, so a simple ordering of generators need not decide which is best.

The central restriction is that the interventions preserve the same equilibrium law. They change reciprocal kinetics: at component \(i\), with neighboring configuration \(s\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
0\longrightarrow1 &: \quad r_i^u(s)\,q_i(s),\\
1\longrightarrow0 &: \quad r_i^u(s)\,[1-q_i(s)].
\end{aligned}
\]

Here \(u\) is the intervention. The unknown clock \(r_i^u(s)\) changes with the intervention, while the unknown conditional failure probability \(q_i(s)\) is common to all interventions.

Thus the intervention changes how quickly the system moves through configurations while preserving its stationary distribution. This is a substantive physical restriction. **Slowing repair alone is not covered:** that would generally change the equilibrium law as well.

The result chooses among the available interventions; it does not manufacture an actuator with these properties.

### Exact operating assumptions

The principal family has:

- A known interaction graph of bounded degree and a declared local Gibbs model.
- Strictly positive local rates and a uniform conditional reliability bound
  \[
  q_i(s)\le \bar q<\frac12.
  \]
- Actual operating-clock bounds
  \[
  0<r_-\le r_i^u(s)\le r_+<\infty.
  \]
- A certified spectral-gap lower bound \(\gamma>0\), obtainable here from a local Dobrushin certificate and the operating refresh backbone.
- Faithful local experimental copies, with paid neighborhood settings, pins, operating controls, reads and timing.
- A finite intervention menu whose members genuinely share the same invariant law.
- Repairable operation during commissioning, including after visits to the failed configuration.

The coefficients and stationary weights need not be numerically known. **The model’s closure, faithful-copy property and common-invariant intervention mechanics remain premises.** Similar measured probabilities cannot establish exact common invariance against arbitrary alternatives.

## 2. The mathematical mechanism: the expensive unknown scale cancels

Let

\[
p=\pi(b)
\]

be the unknown stationary probability of catastrophe, and let

\[
M_u=\mathbb E_{\pi(\cdot\mid b^c),Q^u}\tau_b
\]

be the equilibrium-safe mean first-event time.

Define the total rate of leaving the failed configuration under intervention \(u\),

\[
\Lambda_u
=
\sum_{i=1}^N
r_i^u(\mathbf 1)\,[1-q_i(\mathbf 1)].
\]

The exact representation is

\[
\boxed{
M_u=\frac{B_u}{\Lambda_u\,p(1-p)}.
}
\]

The factor \(B_u\) corrects for returns to the failed configuration. It cannot simply be discarded.

The representation comes from classical Green-function and hitting-time identities. Those identities, and their connection to reversible Dirichlet forms and capacities, are established machinery. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

The completed construction supplies a **finite, computable envelope**

\[
\boxed{\ell\le B_u\le U}
\]

uniformly over every intervention, using only the coarse local bounds.

Since \(p\) is exactly common to all interventions, it cancels from every ratio:

\[
\frac{M_u}{M_v}
=
\frac{B_u}{B_v}\frac{\Lambda_v}{\Lambda_u}.
\]

Consequently, if local experiments estimate the average boundary escape rate

\[
\mu_u=\frac{\Lambda_u}{N}
\]

to relative error \(\eta\), choosing the smallest estimate gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac{M_{\widehat u}}{\max_u M_u}
\ge
\frac{\ell}{U}\frac{1-\eta}{1+\eta}.
}
\]

This is the bridge from local experimental evidence to the consequential global decision.

### Why the correction is controlled

Write \(w(x)\) for the number of working components. Near catastrophe, recovery is strongly favored because many failed components can recover, while only the few working components can fail.

A superharmonic comparison bounds the probability of returning to catastrophe before reaching \(k\) working components. Define

\[
C=\frac{r_+\bar q}{r_-(1-\bar q)},\qquad
\rho=\frac{Ck}{N-k},
\]

and, when \(\rho<1\),

\[
z=\frac{C}{(N-1)(1-\rho)}.
\]

The return probability is at most \(z\). This argument works configuration by configuration; **it does not assume that the number of working components is itself a Markov chain**.

With

\[
a=\frac{\bar q}{1-\bar q},\qquad
g=\frac{\gamma}{Nr_+},\qquad
\bar p=\bar q^N,
\]

one explicit certificate is

\[
\ell=(1-\bar p)^2,
\]

\[
U=
\frac{1}{1-z}
+
La^k
+
\frac{a^{k/2}e^{-gL}}{g}.
\]

The integers \(k,L\) are chosen and checked using finite arithmetic. At fixed local margins, one can use

\[
k=O(\log N),\qquad L=O(N\log N),
\]

with \(z=O(1/N)\) and small remainder terms.

**Neither \(L\) nor the corresponding mathematical chain is simulated in this branch.** They are used to certify the correction.

For smaller systems or finer accuracy where this correction is too large, the report retains a stopped-return construction and a finite polynomial fallback. Those operations are more expensive, but they still avoid an inverse-global-rarity simulation cost.

## 3. The actual local experiment

There are two completed measurement realizations.

### Binary finite holds

Choose a component uniformly. On its faithful local copy:

1. Set its declared neighbors to the failed context.
2. Pin the focal component to failed.
3. Apply the actual requested intervention.
4. Release it for a finite hold.
5. Read whether it has recovered.

Only a bounded local neighborhood is prepared. The full installation is never prepared globally failed for this measurement.

The response gives a bounded interval for \(\mu_u\), with explicit allowances for hold-time uncertainty, pin errors, read errors, context errors and finite random-coordinate selection. The preferred timing bound allows the hold duration to depend on previous trials, provided it is exogenous to the current focal trajectory. A separate, weaker bound covers timing responsive to the current trajectory.

This realization needs only endpoint reads.

### Grouped first-exit waits

A second realization improves the accuracy dependence of the number of resets and reads.

At one randomly chosen component, collect four first-exit waits under the same requested operating context:

\[
T_1,\ldots,T_4,\qquad
S=T_1+\cdots+T_4.
\]

For a local escape rate \(\lambda_i\),

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[\frac3S\right]=\lambda_i,
\qquad
\mathbb E\!\left[\left(\frac3S\right)^2\right]
=\frac32\lambda_i^2.
\]

This is the classical unbiased exponential-rate estimator, not a new estimator invented here. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

The construction groups waits at one component before averaging across components. Pooling heterogeneous waits and then inverting their overall mean would estimate the wrong quantity.

The physical implementation adds:

- A finite censor deadline for every wait.
- Clipping to bound the reported statistic.
- Finite timestamp and deadline errors.
- Finite numerical rounding.
- A conditional allowance for incorrect settings, pins, reads and missed events.
- The full exposure of censored and discarded trials.

The proof includes the probability that timer error changes a censoring decision. A Lipschitz bound on the recorded sum alone would miss that error.

Suppose

\[
\lambda_-\le\lambda_i^u\le\lambda_+,\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\lambda_+}{\lambda_-}.
\]

A sufficient number of groups per intervention is

\[
\boxed{
n=
\left\lceil
40\kappa\eta^{-2}\log\frac{2K}{\delta}
\right\rceil.
}
\]

With probability at least \(1-\delta\), conditional on the common calibration event, all \(K\) estimated means have relative error at most \(\eta\).

This confidence statement permits dependent reused cells through uniform conditional service guarantees. Repeating a persistently miscalibrated experiment does not create independent confidence.

The expected active dwell is at most approximately \(4/\lambda_-\) per group; the report gives a deterministic finite cap and the exact discarded-dwell accounting. The sufficient timestamp tolerance scales as

\[
\Delta
=
\Theta\!\left(
\eta^{5/3}\frac{\lambda_-}{\lambda_+^2}
\right).
\]

It depends on local rates and desired decision accuracy, rather than the global catastrophe probability or the number of components. Its physical service price remains part of the bill.

## 4. The stronger initialization result

The initial formulation still paid for equilibrium-safe preparation. The completed argument shows that this is unnecessary for the mean-only decision in the certified boundary regime.

There are explicit constants \(A_*,B_*\), computed from the same local certificate, such that

\[
\boxed{
A_*M_u
\le
\mathbb E_{x,Q^u}\tau_b
\le
B_*M_u
\quad\text{for every safe state }x\ne b.
}
\]

These constants approach one when the return correction and finite remainders are small.

The lower bound follows because every safe state either already has \(k\) working components or reaches that region before catastrophe with probability at least \(1-z\). The upper bound follows from a uniform Green-function remainder estimate.

Therefore, for **any actual initial law** \(\nu_u\) with

\[
\nu_u(b)\le f,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
(1-f)A_*M_u
\le
\mathbb E_{\nu_u,Q^u}\tau_b
\le
B_*M_u.
}
\]

An initially failed mission receives lifetime zero. This is a direct bound on an unbounded mean; it does not infer mean stability from total-variation closeness.

### A bounded operating wait supplies the required initial law

Set

\[
a_{\mathrm w}=r_-(1-\bar q),
\qquad
c_{\mathrm w}=r_+\bar q.
\]

The actual process can be dominated by independent comparison bits that recover at rate \(a_{\mathrm w}\) and fail at rate \(c_{\mathrm w}\), initially all failed. This graphical comparison works even when the actual interactions are not attractive.

It gives

\[
\Pr(X_t=b)\le q_{\mathrm{dom}}(t)^N,
\]

where

\[
q_{\mathrm{dom}}(t)
=
\frac{c_{\mathrm w}}{a_{\mathrm w}+c_{\mathrm w}}
+
\frac{a_{\mathrm w}}{a_{\mathrm w}+c_{\mathrm w}}
e^{-(a_{\mathrm w}+c_{\mathrm w})t}.
\]

The comparison clocks are a proof device. They are not additional physical controls.

Actual clocks have uncertainty. If deployment begins at any time in a certified window \([D_-,D_+]\), define

\[
q=q_{\mathrm{dom}}(D_-),\qquad
\Delta_D=D_+-D_-.
\]

Bounding both the chance of already being failed and the expected number of entries into failure during that window gives

\[
\boxed{
f\le
\chi_D
=
\min\!\left\{
1,\,
q^N+Nc_{\mathrm w}\Delta_Dq^{N-1}
\right\}.
}
\]

This covers the entire window, including bounded adaptive terminal timing.

The physical operation is consequently simple: **install and settle the selected intervention, let the repairable system operate for the certified finite interval, then begin the mission**. This branch needs no inspection, rejection or equilibrium sampler.

It does not guarantee that the particular specimen is certainly safe. It supplies the explicit initial-failure probability required by the mean guarantee.

Combining measurement, the global comparison and initialization yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\mathcal M_{\widehat u}}
{\max_u\mathcal M_u}
\ge
(1-\chi_D)
\frac{A_*}{B_*}
\frac{\ell}{U}
\frac{1-\eta}{1+\eta},
}
\]

where \(\mathcal M_u\) is the mean under the actual finite preparation. Every factor is checked against the requested tolerance before issuing the decision.

At fixed margins, this warm-up has bounded whole-system duration, but it still costs \(N\) times that duration in summed component exposure. Installation, propagation, slew, settling and timer calibration remain additional costs.

A finite one-bit inspection alternative and the stronger relative-equilibrium preparation are retained separately.

## 5. What advantage is actually proved?

### A separation from absolute prediction at the same access

The report proves a lower bound for absolute relative lifetime prediction even in a much simpler independent-component subclass, with known fast clocks.

The forecaster is granted:

- Arbitrary calibrated local fields.
- Perfect clocks.
- Complete continuous observation of released local cells.
- Adaptive experiment selection and stopping.
- Parallel cells, with their exposures summed.
- Free parameter-independent pins in the relaxed information bill.

For an estimator satisfying

\[
\Pr\!\left(
|\log\widehat M-\log M|\le\epsilon
\right)\ge1-\delta
\]

uniformly over the allowed models, the expected local information bill obeys

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E W
\ge
\frac{N^2}{512e\,\epsilon^2}
\operatorname{kl}(1-\delta,\delta).
}
\]

The proof uses two nearby models whose local logits differ by order \(\epsilon/N\). Their absolute mean lifetimes differ by a fixed relative amount, while each local observation supplies only order \(\epsilon^2/N^2\) distinguishing information.

The likelihood and sequential change-of-measure techniques are classical; the continuous-time access calculation and the lifetime sensitivity are derived explicitly for this family. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

The direct decision avoids acquiring that sensitive common scale. With fixed degree, menu size, margins and accuracy:

| Task | Established local-information scaling |
|---|---:|
| Coarse acquisition for the direct decision | \(\widetilde O(N)\) |
| Boundary-rate decision after those certificates | Independent of \(N\) in its number of local trials |
| Mean-only commissioning | \(O(N)\) summed component exposure at fixed service bounds |
| Uniform absolute relative lifetime prediction | \(\Omega(N^2)\) local evidence |

These statements retain the numerical dependence on local rates, margins, precision and confidence. Hardware services can introduce further costs.

There is also a separate lower bound of order

\[
\eta^{-2}\log(1/\delta)
\]

on local exposure for an appropriately separated two-menu decision. The grouped-wait assay matches that accuracy–confidence dependence at fixed rate contrast. I am not claiming optimal constants, contrast dependence, timer cost or actuator cost.

### Comparison with the strongest ordinary methods

The comparison is not against brute-force waiting for catastrophe.

Capacity methods, exact product-system formulas, likelihood estimation and direct boundary-flux reasoning are strong established alternatives. In particular, **Loper, Zhou and Geman already use target-local capacities and local simulations to compute suitable rare first-passage probabilities**, with explicit flatness-error bounds. Their treatment also explains why those calculations do not provide an overall first-passage time scale. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

The present construction uses common invariance to cancel that missing scale, then supplies the finite correction, acquired operating statistic, uncertainty and initialization interfaces needed for a relative lifetime decision.

An ordinary method exploiting the same cancellation can match the direct approach. **The lower bound applies to requiring an absolute forecast before deciding, not to every specialist direct-decision method.** I have not established a historical-priority claim.
