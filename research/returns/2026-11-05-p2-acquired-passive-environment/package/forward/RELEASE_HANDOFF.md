# Finite release and physical delivery of the untrapped query force

## Outcome and explicit added resources

The proposed finite-release correction is valid. One distinction is essential: the aging constant must bound the hidden y-relative-to-reference state at the **start of the final hold**, while the release excursion needs a bound at the **start of release**. A common global G can be used when it bounds both, as in the root fixture. A bound supplied only at release does not alone justify the earlier-age denominator H-b.

Untrapped deployment uses two declared actuator capabilities beyond a fixed-stiffness moving trap: a nonnegative stiffness k(t) with finite slew that reaches zero, and an independently movable support of the known local viscous element. Moving that support must leave the hidden modes and V in their original laboratory frame. These operations are not inferred from moving the trap. Keeping fixed k and absorbing its quadratic potential into V is valid but predicts a different physical law.

## 1. Times, signs, and initial envelopes

Use the backward convention

x'=-V'(x)+integral(y_lambda-x)dnu+f_support-k(t)(x-q_H),
y_lambda'=lambda(x-y_lambda).

The known local drag is normalized to one. The measure is positive, has actual mass b0<=B, and has no rate floor, ceiling, or order bound. V is mu-strongly convex.

During the final hold [0,H], |x-x_ref|<=delta. Put d_lambda=y_lambda(0)-x_ref and assume integral|d_lambda|dnu<=G_hold. A finite positive normalized readout supported on [H-b,H], b<H, returns c within eta_g of the actual signed memory force m_y=integral(y-x)dnu. This is the negative of the forward acquisition residual f-V'(x)-x', which estimates integral(x-y)dnu.

Release lasts [H,H+tau]. Deployment time zero is H+tau and the prediction horizon T starts there. Assume integral|y_lambda(H)-x_ref|dnu<=G_release. A global invariant envelope supplies G_hold=G_release=G. If only G_hold is known, the automatically safe release envelope is G_release<=G_hold+Bdelta.

## 2. A finite trap program with no center jump

Let S be a smooth monotone step from zero to one, with zero endpoint first derivatives and sup|S'|=C1. Quintic smoothstep gives C1=15/8; the septic in the forward theorem gives C1=35/16. At release time s in [0,tau], use

k_rel(s)=k[1-S(s/tau)].

It remains nonnegative, reaches zero continuously, and has |k_rel'|<=C1 k/tau. Keep the center at its previous fixed held value q_H.

Let a0=k(q_H-x_ref), alpha=k_rel/k, and

D_ref=max(|V'(x_ref)|,|a0-V'(x_ref)|).

Then |alpha a0-V'(x_ref)|<=D_ref. Both q_H=x_ref and the known held feedforward q_H=x_ref+V'(x_ref)/k give D_ref=|V'(x_ref)|. Thus the proof covers the held center without silently moving it during release. A known center/feedforward force error is added to D_ref.

## 3. Release excursion bound

For e(s)=x(H+s)-x_ref and v_lambda(s)=y_lambda(H+s)-x_ref,

v_lambda(s)=exp(-lambda s)v_lambda(0)
 +lambda integral_0^s exp[-lambda(s-r)]e(r)dr.

Suppose |f_rel|<=F. At a first crossing of the increasing barrier r(s)=delta+C s, every preceding |e| is at most r(s). The freely decaying hidden term is bounded by G_release. The driven hidden term is at most [b0-K(s)]r(s); it cancels the adverse part of -b0 e at that extremum. Strong convexity and nonnegative trap stiffness add inward forces. The same argument applies to the negative barrier. Taking C=G_release+F+D_ref yields

|e(s)|<=delta+[G_release+F+D_ref]s,

and therefore

**delta_rel=delta+[G_release+F+D_ref]tau.**

With a common global G and either center convention above, this is the requested delta+[G+F+|V'(x_ref)|]tau. No hidden derivative, rate ceiling, or reset is used. The stiffness derivative does not enter this excursion comparison, although its physical slew is paid separately.

## 4. Whole hold-plus-release old-force certificate

On [0,H+tau], the observed deviation is bounded by delta_rel. Integrating the hidden equation once over this entire interval gives

y_lambda(H+tau)-x_ref=exp[-lambda(H+tau)]d_lambda
 +lambda integral_0^(H+tau) exp[-lambda(H+tau-s)]e(s)ds.

The integral term is bounded by delta_rel[1-exp(-lambda(H+tau))]. Hence the future force relative to the unchanged reference,

g_rel(u)=integral exp(-lambda u)[y_lambda(H+tau)-x_ref]dnu,

satisfies |g_rel(u)-g0(H+tau+u)|<=Bdelta_rel, where g0(t)=integral exp(-lambda t)d_lambda dnu. This one term already covers the hidden offset generated by both hold and release; adding another Bdelta hold term here would double count it.

During the measured hold, |m_y(s)-g0(s)|<=2Bdelta. For t>0,

|g0'(t)|<=G_hold/(e t),

because lambda exp(-lambda t)<=1/(e t), without any rate moment. For s in [H-b,H] and u in [0,T],

|g0(H+tau+u)-g0(s)|
 <=(G_hold/e) log[(H+tau+T)/(H-b)]
 <=G_hold(b+tau+T)/[e(H-b)].

Any positive normalized weighting on the stated readout support obeys this bound. Thus

**gamma_rel=eta_g+2Bdelta+Bdelta_rel+G_hold(b+tau+T)/[e(H-b)]**

satisfies |g_rel(u)-c|<=gamma_rel uniformly on the post-release horizon. With a common global G, this is exactly the proposed formula. As tau tends to zero it recovers the earlier eta_g+3Bdelta+G(b+T)/[e(H-b)] certificate. Frozen modes persist but have zero age derivative.

The compiler must retain its reference initialization xhat(0)=x_ref. Changing the reference to the actual post-release position changes the old force by K(u)[x(H+tau)-x_ref] and requires another Bdelta_rel allowance. That is a different initialization convention.

## 5. Real additive drive from a movable local viscous support

Let r(t) be the position of an independently driven support for the local viscous element. Its physical force is Gamma[r'-x']. Force balance reads

Gamma x'+V'(x)+integral(x-y)dnu+k_rel(x-q_H)=Gamma r'.

After normalization of the known Gamma to one, the additive term is f=r'. Prescribing support velocity therefore supplies the drive without measuring x' or applying instantaneous force feedback. The support couples only through this local viscous element; it must not advect the hidden bath or translate V. That selective coupling is an explicit source/actuator premise.

The query must be available by release time if f(0) is nonzero. During release prescribe r'(H+s)=S(s/tau)f(0), starting from rest. Then |r'|<=F and |r''|<=C1 F/tau. At release completion r'=f(0), so delivery joins the query continuously. Afterward set r'(H+tau+u)=f(u). A continuous piecewise-Lipschitz query with |f|<=F and stated global slew L_f gives support acceleration bounded almost everywhere by L_f. Total support travel is at most F(tau+T).

The actual dashpot force is Gamma(r'-x'), not just Gamma r'. Its force and work are bounded in section 7. Fixed-k moving-center control instead produces kq-kx, which does not provide this untrapped force operation for free.

## 6. Finite query implementation and calibration

An arbitrary computable real function is not automatically an exact finite hardware program. A query may supply a finite certified piecewise program, or it can be compiled into a continuous piecewise-linear f_tilde with a paid error epsilon_drive.

For known L_f, take a finite time mesh of spacing Delta_f and certified value intervals of width epsilon_val. Choose rational knot values in these intervals and [-F,F], subject to |v_(i+1)-v_i|<=L_f Delta_i. This finite rational linear feasibility problem is nonempty because the true samples are feasible. Linear interpolation preserves the force and slew caps and has

||f-f_tilde||inf<=epsilon_val+L_f Delta_f/2.

Its integral is a finite piecewise-quadratic support path. Ramp to f_tilde(0) during release. The compiler can predict that delivered program directly, or add the displayed approximation error to its residual for the intended f.

If normalized support velocity has calibrated error at most epsilon_support, use F_actual=F+epsilon_support in the release and physical force/travel bounds, and add epsilon_support to the deployment forcing residual. If Gamma normalization has residual uncertainty, its induced drag/drive mismatch must also be bounded on the proved speed/force envelopes and included in the residual; it is not inferred from the unknown bath. The clean theorem uses known calibrated Gamma=1 as requested.

This is preprogrammed open-loop delivery. A feedback query whose initial force is unavailable before release needs its own compatible startup construction and is not silently supplied by this handoff.

## 7. Complete force, slew, range, work, and power bill

Write R=delta_rel and use the actual calibrated drive envelope F_actual (equal to F in the exact normalized model). During release,

integral|y_lambda(H+s)-x_ref|dnu<=G_release+BR,
|integral(y_lambda-x)dnu|<=G_release+2BR.

Define V_abs(R)=max(|V'(x_ref-R)|,|V'(x_ref+R)|). A safe actual position-speed bound is

W_rel=F_actual+V_abs(R)+G_release+2BR+|a0|+kR.

The following bounds are finite and explicit:

- Trap force: |a0|+kR.
- Trap-force slew: C1(|a0|+kR)/tau+k W_rel.
- Stiffness slew: C1 k/tau.
- Actual local-support reaction force: Gamma(F_actual+W_rel).
- Support speed: F_actual; commanded release acceleration: C1 F/tau, plus its stated calibration allowance.

Absolute work associated with the monotone stiffness change is at most (k/2)[R+|q_H-x_ref|]^2. Its peak parameter-power magnitude is at most C1 k[R+|q_H-x_ref|]^2/(2tau). Under the harmonic energy convention, decreasing k extracts trap energy; the ability to perform that finite-rate control remains a paid resource.

For deployment, put G_after=G_release+BR and

X_dep=max(R,[G_after+F_actual+|V'(x_ref)|]/mu).

The same first-exit argument proves |x-x_ref|<=X_dep. Therefore

W_dep=F_actual+V_abs(X_dep)+G_after+2B X_dep

bounds actual deployment speed. With W=max(W_rel,W_dep), support reaction force is at most Gamma(F_actual+W), total travel at most F_actual(tau+T), and absolute support work at most Gamma(F_actual+W)F_actual(tau+T).

A short tau can require large finite stiffness slew, support acceleration, force slew, and power. Those costs must be displayed rather than described as an instantaneous release.

## 8. Post-release prediction certificate

At deployment time zero, k=0, |x(0)-x_ref|<=delta_rel, and ||g_rel-c||inf<=gamma_rel. The source is the intended untrapped law. If zeta is the acquired kernel bound, D_dec the decoded predictor velocity bound, rho_num its execution residual, and epsilon_drive the total physical drive approximation/calibration allowance, then

**sup_(0<=u<=T)|x(u)-xhat(u)|
 <=max{delta_rel,[zeta D_dec+gamma_rel+rho_num+epsilon_drive]/mu}.**

The kernel does not change during release, so it need not be reacquired. Its acquired horizon remains T measured after release. The hidden-force age and the actual physical duration explicitly include tau. The query-dependent startup is part of the allowed preparation. The same acquisition/readout confidence event supports all release/query branches obeying these deterministic envelopes; replaying a saved numerical state does not provide a physical reset.

## 9. Meter-history scope correction

An optional positive causal force-meter filter must be bounded using a force cap valid over its entire contributing history. In the earlier delay estimate, use F_history (including centering/release peaks if the filter retains them), not merely F_hold, unless finite support or a certified cleared sensor state excludes the earlier high-force interval. Likewise, its frontend linear range must cover the admitted physical force history. The root report's use of F_global and an explicit frontend-range premise is correct. For the primitive of directly calibrated finite-bin averages, no additional sensor-history filter is being assumed.

## Status

The release lemma and physical force-delivery construction are complete under the explicitly added stiffness and viscous-support resources. No new unrelated test or literature claim is introduced. All formulas also apply to larger tau selected to meet slower hardware limits.
