Invent an economical electricity-driven process that turns actual iron ore into useful iron without depending on sales of chlorine or another large chemical coproduct.

Can a new reactor, separation or reagent-return mechanism make primary iron production from ore economical with clean electricity? Develop the complete ore-to-metal operation, including the physical manufacture and collection of the iron. Choose the strongest chemistry and architecture; aqueous electrolysis is an opening, not a required method.

Use a real, obtainable hematite-bearing ore with a measured dry assay of 55--65 wt% Fe and at least 5 wt% combined silica and alumina. Choose and characterize one actual feed rather than claiming tolerance to every ore. Pay crushing, classification, beneficiation, washing, drying and any conversion into a more reactive precursor. Purchased high-purity oxide or a prepared ferrous salt must include the actual route from the specified ore. Target at least 90% recovery of feed iron into a product containing at least 97 wt% total Fe, at least 95% metallization, and no more than 0.05 wt% each sulfur and phosphorus. Define an actual usable product form and its electric-arc-furnace acceptance limits. Finished steelmaking is a separate operation.

Develop an integrated nominal 1 kg/day construction and a complete 100-hour material/energy run model. Separately develop a 100,000-tonne/year nameplate manufacturing plant at 90% utilization. At that plant target no more than 4 MWh of purchased energy per tonne of accepted metallic iron and US$450/t complete production cost with electricity US$40/MWh; also report US$80/MWh. Include heat, cooling, gas and vacuum handling, purification, separation, auxiliaries, actual feed and reagent preparation, equipment, yield, maintenance, replacement, financing, labor, waste and decommissioning. Use a 20-year financial horizon and 8% real discount rate without granting a 20-year material life. Give the small construction's actual energy and first-build cost separately; industrial thermal sharing cannot erase laboratory overhead.

Choose physical temperature and pressure. If hydrogen, carbon, acid, alkali, chloride, a metal mediator or another reductant is used, construct and pay its supply or regeneration and every resulting stream. Electricity-derived hydrogen must include water treatment and complete production energy. Account for support/current-collector consumption and all metallic iron initially present. A chemical feedstock may be consumed when its full production, transport and waste costs are included; naming it a catalyst does not close its inventory.

The base plant must work without a buyer for chlorine, caustic or another large coproduct. Construct its return, internal use or paid disposal. Report a separately bounded coproduct-sale scenario only with realistic volume, quality, purification, transport and market limits; do not use unlimited chemical credits to establish scalable iron economics. Oxygen also receives no automatic revenue.

A useful experimental starting point is Noble, Moutarlier and Kempler, ChemRxiv v2, DOI10.26434/chemrxiv-2023-d22xj-v2:
https://www.cambridge.org/engage/api-gateway/coe/assets/orp/resource/item/64c7e2099ed5166e93cc7195/original/electrochemical-chlor-iron-process-for-iron-production-from-iron-oxide-and-seawater.pdf
Read it as existing work, not the new invention. Its externally heated 80 C small flow cell used concentrated alkaline oxide slurry, acidic brine, a chlor-alkali membrane and a chlorine-evolving anode. Four-hour operation was about 3.2 V with 84% iron current efficiency. Low-purity feed sharply reduced selectivity. It does not supply this ore plant, reagent return or long-term operation.

Some conditional screens expose a constructive opening:
- Reduction from Fe(III) requires about 1439.78 Ah/kg Fe. The cited 3.2 V/84% point corresponds to about 5.485 kWh/kg of cell work before heating and auxiliaries. A 4 kWh/kg whole-plant budget at 90% current efficiency permits less than 2.500 V mean cell voltage before other work. A different counter reaction, transport state or reactor may change the operation; a nominal voltage reduction alone does not supply it.
- The chlor-iron reaction makes approximately 2.149 kg NaOH and 1.905 kg chlorine per kg Fe. Returning those streams to salt, water and oxygen is a new physical operation. The overall desired oxide-to-metal process can be written Fe2O3 -> 2Fe + 1.5O2 only when all supporting states actually close.
- A one-pass silica leach consumes about 133 kg NaOH per 100 kg SiO2 through Na2SiO3 formation. Removing gangue is not free merely because alkali occurs elsewhere in the process. Construct separation and regeneration or pay fresh reagent and waste.

Work through an actual feed route, reactor geometry, transport/current distribution, product morphology and harvesting, residue handling, startup and repeated operation. Build an executable complete-process model with credible coupled parameters, uncertainty and aging. Keep measured behavior, extrapolation and new material hypotheses distinct. The useful product, recovery, energy and cost must occur at the same operating point.

Compare against strong hydrogen direct reduction, existing electrolytic ironmaking and conventional ore-to-iron production at the same product quality, electricity/carbon boundary, scale and utilization. Include ore upgrading and hydrogen manufacture in the comparison. Credit known processes. Identify the new useful operation and the whole-system advantage it could create.

Design a smaller decisive experiment for later physical execution, with obtainable materials, actual preparation, full cost and measurements that distinguish sustained ore reduction from consumed electrode metal, initial reduced inventory or unclosed reagents. Do the mechanism/design work here; a list of future experiments alone does not answer the invention question. An unsuccessful route is not a negative for all ironmaking.
