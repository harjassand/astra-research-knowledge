## 1. The precise missing step in the claw-state approach

For an idealized trapdoor claw pair \(f_{k,0},f_{k,1}\), let \(x_0(y),x_1(y)\) be the two branch preimages of a valid image \(y\). An honest quantum prover can produce a state of the form

\[
\frac{|0,J(x_0(y))\rangle+|1,J(x_1(y))\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Measuring its remaining registers in the Hadamard basis gives a classical pair \((c,d)\) satisfying

\[
c=d\cdot\bigl(J(x_0(y))\oplus J(x_1(y))\bigr).
\]

The verifier can check this using its trapdoor, together with the function family’s validity and good-\(d\) conditions. In a noisy construction, the preparation and checking discrepancies must also be charged.

The established LWE adaptive-hardcore machinery constrains an algorithm that supplies **a valid preimage together with the equation**. It does not prohibit the honest quantum algorithm from supplying only the image and equation. The original interactive construction obtains the classical contradiction by asking a later challenge and rewinding the classical prover. Its “single-round test” includes that later challenge, so it is not the one-challenge/one-response protocol requested here. [ieee-focs.org](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2018-Papers/pdfs/59f320.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The fixed code of \(E_A\) could depend on the fixed code of \(A\); no security-parameter-dependent advice would be allowed.

Here is the conditional soundness calculation. Suppose \(A\) passes the valid equation test with probability \(p\), extraction fails with probability at most \(\eta\), and the applicable hard-core game bounds valid-preimage-and-equation success by \(1/2+\nu\). Running \(A\) and then \(E_A\) gives

## 2. A private verifier cannot certify an internal distinction absent from the reply distribution

This gives an exact test for a proposed replacement based on coherent or witness-oblivious sampling.

Let \((k,t)\) have the generator’s joint distribution. Let \(Q_k\) and \(C_k\) denote quantum and classical conditional distributions of the sole response. Neither prover receives \(t\). Then, for **any** verifier, even an unbounded one,

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\Pr[V(k,t,Q_k)=1]-\Pr[V(k,t,C_k)=1]
\right|
\le
\mathbb E_k\,\operatorname{TV}(Q_k,C_k).
}
\tag{1}
\]

To prove this, define

\[
h_k(a)=\mathbb E_{t\mid k}\Pr[V(k,t,a)=1].
\]

This is a function taking values in \([0,1]\). Its expectation differs under \(Q_k\) and \(C_k\) by at most their total variation distance. Averaging over \(k\) proves (1), including arbitrary correlation between the challenge and the private verification data.

Consequently, your completeness and soundness requirements imply, for every fixed uniform classical sampler \(C\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E_k\,\operatorname{TV}(Q_k,C_k)
\ge \frac14-\operatorname{negl}(\lambda).
}
\tag{2}
\]

This matters for quantum oblivious LWE sampling. Debris-Alazard, Fallahpour and Stehlé construct quantum samplers whose internal witness-access properties differ from ordinary classical sampling while producing the intended LWE instance distribution. That internal distinction is a substantive result, but it is not itself a classical-output verification gap. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.03807v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

For example, if the sole response is an LWE sample \(b\), a classical prover can sample \(s,e\) and return

\[
b=As+e.
\]

If the quantum sampler has the same conditional output law, the private verifier cannot distinguish them. More quantitatively, suppose the quantum law is within average conditional total variation \(\zeta\) of ordinary LWE sampling for uniform \(A\), and the generator’s \(A\)-marginal is \(\eta\)-close to uniform. The acceptance difference is at most

\[
\zeta+\eta.

The trapdoor does not change this conclusion. A successful protocol must make coherence visible in an additional property of the response distribution.

Even granting an injective outer map does not repair this inference. Let \(f_k\) be one-way, and include a freshly generated hard image

\[
y=f_k(x^\star)

\[
A(k,z,y)=F_z(y)

produces an image of \(F_z\circ f_k\). An extractor guaranteed to return \(x\) satisfying

### Inner composition can remove every claw

\[
f_0(u,s)=(h_0(u),s),
\qquad
f_1(u,s)=(h_1(u),s\oplus1).
\]

A claw of the original pair gives a claw of this pair with opposite \(s\)-bits. Finding a claw of the padded pair also finds one for the original pair.

For any EOWF \(F_z:D\to X\), append a fixed zero:

\[
\widetilde F_z(x)=(F_z(x),0).
\]

This preserves the EOWF’s ordinary one-wayness and extraction properties. Yet

\[
(f_0\circ\widetilde F_z)(x)=(h_0(F_z(x)),0),

whereas

\[
(f_1\circ\widetilde F_z)(x)=(h_1(F_z(x)),1).

Their images are disjoint. Measuring the output identifies the branch, so the quantum procedure produces no claw superposition.

This example preserves the ordinary component properties being discussed. It does not silently assert preservation of every additional adaptive-hardcore property. Its conclusion is exact: **generic inner composition need not preserve the central coherent operation.**

## 4. An extractable preimage record can destroy the required interference

The compatibility problem also has a quantitative form.

Let \(z_0\ne z_1\in\{0,1\}^{m}\), and consider

\[
|\Psi\rangle=
\frac{|z_0\rangle|\eta_0\rangle+
      |z_1\rangle|\eta_1\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\]

where the auxiliary states are normalized. Apply Hadamards to the first register and measure \(D\), leaving or discarding the auxiliary register. Direct expansion gives

\[
\Pr[D=d]
=
2^{-m}\left[
1+(-1)^{d\cdot(z_0\oplus z_1)}
\operatorname{Re}\langle\eta_1|\eta_0\rangle
\right].
\]

Exactly half the \(d\)’s have each parity. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Pr[D\cdot(z_0\oplus z_1)=0]
=
\frac{1+\operatorname{Re}\langle\eta_1|\eta_0\rangle}{2}.
}
\tag{3}
\]

For the claw-state protocol, take \(z_b=(b,J(x_b))\).

A distinct deterministic classical record of each preimage makes the two record states orthogonal. Equation (3) then gives correct-parity probability exactly \(1/2\). Computational hiding of the record does not alter this Hilbert-space orthogonality.

There is also a quantitative tradeoff. If the two pure record states can be distinguished with error at most \(\xi\le1/2\), diagonalizing their density-matrix difference gives

\[
|\langle\eta_1|\eta_0\rangle|
\le 2\sqrt{\xi(1-\xi)}.
\]

Thus

\[
\Pr[\text{correct parity}]
\le \frac12+\sqrt{\xi(1-\xi)}.
\tag{4}
\]

A nearly reliable which-branch record leaves nearly random parity in this implementation.

Even equality of the exposed record marginals is insufficient. For example,

\[
|\eta_b\rangle=
\frac{|0,b\rangle+|1,1\oplus b\rangle}{\sqrt2}
\]

has the same second-register marginal for both \(b\), but

\[
\langle\eta_1|\eta_0\rangle=0.
\]

The retained garbage contains the distinguishing correlation.

Computing and then uncomputing all distinguishing records restores coherence, but leaves no such record in the transmitted reply. A successful proof-carrying construction must explicitly handle this issue; classical witness indistinguishability alone does not establish the needed coherent implementation.

## 7. The finite-gate and precision requirement can be met conditionally

This part has a positive construction.

Suppose a valid ideal prover has:

- at most \(G(\lambda)\) constant-arity gates;
- a uniform circuit-description algorithm;
- a certified procedure evaluating every local target matrix to \(s\) bits in \(E(\lambda,s)\) bit operations;
- computational-basis ancillas.

Fix an algebraic, inverse-closed finite universal gate set \(\mathcal A\). Kuperberg’s finite-precision synthesis theorem permits exponent \(2\): an \(s\)-bit local approximation has expanded length \(O_{\mathcal A}(s^2)\) and compilation work \(\widetilde O_{\mathcal A}(s^2)\), with the target supplied to \(s+O_{\mathcal A}(1)\) bits. Its computational model charges precision and word expansion. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.13158v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Set

\[
s=\left\lceil\log_2\frac{G}{\eta}\right\rceil+c_{\mathcal A}.
\]

Compile each gate to operator-norm error at most \(\eta/G\). Telescoping gives

\[
\|U-\widetilde U\|
\le \sum_{j=1}^{G}\|U_j-\widetilde U_j\|
\le\eta.
\]

The resulting classical-output total variation, and therefore any acceptance-probability change, is at most \(\eta\). Sequential channel hybrids give the corresponding statement for adaptive computational-basis measurements with a uniform worst-case gate bound.

The costs are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{quantum gates}
&=O_{\mathcal A}(Gs^2),\\
\text{classical compilation}
&\le
G\,E(\lambda,s+O_{\mathcal A}(1))
+\widetilde O_{\mathcal A}(Gs^2)
+\text{fixed preprocessing}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For \(\eta=2^{-\lambda}\) and \(G\le\lambda^d\),

\[
s=\lambda+O(d\log\lambda),
\]

so the expanded gate count is polynomial:

\[
O_{\mathcal A}\!\left(
\lambda^d(\lambda+d\log\lambda)^2
\right).
\]

The fixed gate-set preprocessing is charged as a fixed algorithmic constant, not security-parameter-dependent advice. Input loading, output measurement, circuit-description generation and adaptive classical control retain their actual costs. Controlled gates must be compiled with their conditional phases intact.

This changes only the honest implementation. The classical generator and verifier can remain unchanged, so an already-proved classical soundness bound remains unchanged. **It cannot establish that soundness bound.** Moreover, it requires a paid gate-matrix evaluator; arbitrary unspecified one-qubit rotations do not supply one.
