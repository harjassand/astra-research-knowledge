1. An exact amount identity within the specified species model
Let N denote total ammonia amount NH4+NH3, C denote dissolved inorganic-carbon amount CO2*+HCO3+CO3, and S the amount of strong-ion excess: non-ammonium, non-hydrogen cation equivalents minus non-carbonate, non-hydroxide anion equivalents. Let
A = n(HCO3)+2n(CO3)+n(NH3)+n(OH)-n(H)
be total alkalinity amount in the carbonate/ammonia/water model.
Electroneutrality gives
S = A - N.
Therefore, when R moles nitrogen leave the feed as NEUTRAL NH3, with neutral CO2 exchange and no change in nonvolatile-ion inventory,
A_final = A_initial - R.
More generally,
A_final = A_initial - R + DeltaS,
where DeltaS must have an actual strong-reagent, nonvolatile-ion-transfer or explicitly paid outlet path. Changes in liquid volume must be handled with amounts, not by reusing concentration differences at inconsistent volumes.

This identity does not require a rate law or the ideal pKa values used for numerical examples. It does require this stated accounting/species boundary. Other weak acid systems have to enter the definition and balance; ammonia oxidation/reduction, ionic ammonium export, acid or salt carryover and separate liquid outlets require their corresponding paths. It is NOT a universal bound on all nitrogen-recovery architectures.

Consequently, a locally reversible pH or proton-shuttle cycle cannot by itself make global equivalent deficits disappear while all its media and other feed ions return unchanged. A stoichiometric acid trap located in a separate product loop does not remove unassigned nonvolatile feed counterions. Ion transport or a different chemical organization may solve the problem, but it must be constructed and paid.

2. A return-pH feasibility screen
Use introduced ideal25C pKaNH4=9.25,pKaCO2=6.35,pKaHCO3=10.33,Kw=1e-14. Let fN(pH) be the uncharged NH3 fraction, and qC(pH) the mean negative charge per inorganic-carbon molecule. Then
A/V = fN*N/V + qC*C/V + Kw/[H] - [H].
For C>=0 and returnpH>=6.5, A_final has the lower bound
fN(6.5)*N_final + (Kw/H_6.5-H_6.5)*V_final.
Thus the minimum extra strong equivalents compatible with this return-pH boundary are
max(0, R + A_final,min - A_initial).
This is a necessary condition for a restricted no-other-ion-transfer path. It does not supply an electrode, a membrane, a gas exchanger, a finite current, an energy requirement or the optimal amount of reagent for a real feed.

4. A decisive measurement implication
The current full operation already requires actualN/carbonate/charge and final-state closure. A useful experimental discriminator is simultaneous measurement of feed/effluent alkalinity, TAN, dissolved inorganic carbon, all important nonvolatile ions, every product/waste volume and the integrated CO2/NH3 gas flux. A process that claims low net base use and unchanged other-ion inventory should satisfy A_final=A_initial-R with the appropriate volume and weak-acid corrections. A measured discrepancy identifies missing chemical or ionic inventory; it does not establish which instrument or mechanism is at fault.

Compare repeated steady/reset cycles, not only initial fastNH3 release. A finite resin, redoxmedium or alkaline surface can initially supply the required equivalents; its initial and final states must close across useful repeated operation. LocalpH and recoveredN flux should be measured together with global equivalents and carbon transfer. No extra routine reproof gate, fixed source quota or prescribed mechanism is created.
