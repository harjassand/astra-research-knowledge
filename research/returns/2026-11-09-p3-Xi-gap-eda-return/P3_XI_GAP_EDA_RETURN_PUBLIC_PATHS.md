## 1\. An exact multiscale finite-cut criterion

Let \(a\in\Gamma\)\. Choose finitely many nonzero vectors

\[
v_1,\ldots,v_k\in\mathbb N_0^d
\]

such that \(a+v_\ell\in\Gamma\) for every \(\ell\), and positive integers \(s_1,\ldots,s_k\)\.

Define

\[
\xi(n)=a+\sum_{\ell=1}^k n^{s_\ell}v_\ell.
\]

Choose a finite nonempty set of distinct offsets \(H\subset\mathbb Z^d\) such that \(a+h\in\Gamma\) for every \(h\in H\), and put

\[
D_n=\{\xi(n)+h:h\in H\}.
\]

All these states belong to \(\Gamma\)\. To see this, take paths \(a\leadsto a+v_\ell\) and \(a\leadsto a+h\)\. Since each \(v_\ell\geq0\), translating and repeating the first paths produces \(\xi(n)\), and translating the second paths produces \(\xi(n)+h\)\.

Define the coordinate growth degrees

\[
p_i=
\begin{cases}
\max\{s_\ell:(v_\ell)_i>0\},&\text{if some }(v_\ell)_i>0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

For \(h\in H\), put

\[
t(h)=\sum_i p_i h_i,
\qquad t_*=\min_{h\in H}t(h).
\]

Call a reaction \(r\) *eventually enabled at offset \(h\)* if

\[
a_i+h_i\geq(y_r)_i
\quad\text{for every }i\text{ with }p_i=0.
\]

These are exactly the reactions enabled at \(\xi(n)+h\) for all sufficiently large \(n\)\.

Let \(\partial H\) be the finite collection of pairs \((h,r)\) satisfying eventual enabling and

\[
h+z_r-y_r\notin H.

\[
\Delta=
t_*+
\max_{(h,r)\in\partial H}
\left(
\sum_i p_i(y_r)_i-t(h)
\right).
\tag{1}
\]

If \(\partial H\) is empty, take \(\Delta=-\infty\)\.

## 3\. Why this does not yet give a total classifier

The finite-cut condition detects a mechanism in which a group of high-population states has progressively smaller outgoing conductance relative to its stationary mass.

The preceding calculation also shows something stronger: whether a *fixed* motif satisfies \(\Delta<0\) depends only on integer growth orders, reaction source complexes, boundary enabling and the chosen offsets. Its truth does not depend on the positive numerical rate constants or concentration values, provided the required complex-balanced class remains the same.

That makes it an effectively testable structural obstruction to a positive gap.

However, the desired decision procedure requires a converse or a separate exhaustive positivity theory.

For example, establishing

\[
\lambda=0
\Longrightarrow
\text{some finite polynomially pumpable motif satisfies }\Delta<0

would turn the present result into a substantially stronger classification tool. **I have not proved this implication.** In particular, an arbitrary sequence of low-energy functions need not, by any result established here, be replaceable with indicators of fixed-size translated patterns.

A zero-gap sequence might involve supports whose shape or cardinality grows with the population scale. Excluding that possibility, or incorporating it into a finite decision procedure, requires a new argument.
