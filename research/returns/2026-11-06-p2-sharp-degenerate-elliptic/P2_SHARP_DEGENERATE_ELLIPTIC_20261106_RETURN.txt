## 1. The exact theorem

\operatorname{curl}E=0,

\operatorname{div}B=0

Define the stored-energy density

\[
J=E\cdot B

and the combined field amplitude

\[
|\mathcal F|=\sqrt{|E|^2+|B|^2}.
\]

Assume finite local energy,

\[
J\ge0,
\qquad
J\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),
\]

and the distortion inequality

\[
|E|^2+|B|^2
\le
\left(K+\frac1K\right)E\cdot B,
\qquad K(x)\ge1.
\tag{1}
\]

There is **no finite upper bound on \(K\)**. Instead, assume

\[
e^{\tau K}\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

for some \(\tau>0\).

Then, for every \(0<\beta<\tau\),

\[
\boxed{
J\log^\beta(e+J)\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

\tag{2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal F|^2\log^{\beta-1}(e+|\mathcal F|)
\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega).

\tag{3}

## 3. The proof, including the finite-energy issue

### Start with the actual finite-energy class

Set

\[
R=\frac{|E+B|+|E-B|}{2}.
\]

Elementary identities give

\[
R^2\le|\mathcal F|^2\le2R^2,
\]

and distortion condition (1) is equivalent to

\[
R^2\le KJ.
\tag{4}
\]

First obtain the preliminary integrability needed for legitimate integration by parts. Split the domain into

\[
K\le\frac2\tau\log(e+R)
\]

and its complement.

On the first set, (4) gives

\[
\frac{R^2}{\log(e+R)}
\le\frac2\tau J.
\]

On the complement,

\[
(e+R)^2<e^{\tau K},
\]

so

\[
\frac{R^2}{\log(e+R)}
\le e^{\tau K}.
\]

Therefore both fields belong locally to

\[
L^2\log^{-1}L.
\tag{5}
\]

This step uses only the given finite energy and exponential moment.

For fields in the critical class (5), Sbordone’s div–curl product result identifies a nonnegative pointwise product with the weak distributional product. Thus \(J\) carries no additional, unaccounted singular energy measure. Conversely, starting with that critical logarithmic class and nonnegative product already gives local integrability of \(J\). The finite-energy formulation therefore also covers the minimal logarithmic starting class of the original problem. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

This avoids assuming \(L^2\) regularity in order to prove \(L^2\) regularity.

### Localize while preserving the differential constraints

Work on concentric balls, with a smooth cutoff \(\chi\) equal to one on the inner ball.

Choose

\[
q=2-\frac1{d+1}.
\]

The fields belong to \(L^q\). On a ball, the potential operators give

\[
E=\nabla u,
\qquad
B_i=\sum_j\partial_j\Psi_{ij},
\qquad
\Psi_{ij}=-\Psi_{ji},
\]

with \(u,\Psi\in W^{1,q}\). Sobolev embedding places these potentials in \(L^{q^*}\), where \(q^*>2\).

Define compactly supported fields

\[
a=\nabla(\chi u)=\chi E+g_a,
\]

\[
b_i=\sum_j\partial_j(\chi\Psi_{ij})
=\chi B_i+(g_b)_i.
\]

They satisfy

\[
\operatorname{curl}a=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{div}b=0
\]

exactly. The cutoff errors belong to \(L^{q^*}\).

Write

\[
D=a\cdot b=J_0+C_0,
\qquad
J_0=\chi^2J\ge0.
\]

The cross-term error \(C_0\) belongs to some \(L^{1+\delta}\), because

\[
\frac1q+\frac1{q^*}
=\frac2q-\frac1d<1.
\]

The critical product identity just established now justifies

\[
\int D=0.
\tag{6}
\]

Let

\[
r=\frac{|a+b|+|a-b|}{2}.
\]

For any \(\eta>0\),

\[
r^2
\le
(1+\eta)KJ_0+C_\eta|g|^2.
\tag{7}
\]

The errors have enough additional integrability to absorb any fixed logarithmic power. On the inner ball, \(\chi=1\), the errors vanish, and the localized pair equals the original pair.
