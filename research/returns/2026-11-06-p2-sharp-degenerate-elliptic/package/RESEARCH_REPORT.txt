SHARP LOCAL REGULARITY WITH UNBOUNDED ELLIPTIC DISTORTION
6 November 2026 — source-grounded mathematical derivation

QUESTION, STATUS, AND ATTRIBUTION
================================

The completed uniform-ellipticity and real-vector reflection results are retained
without reproof. The next full question selected is the sharp local regularity
threshold for finite-energy scalar fields and fluxes when the normalized
coefficient and its inverse can be unbounded, but their distortion has an
exponential moment.

The result below proves the strict, dimension-independent threshold posed in
Iwaniec-Sbordone's Conjecture 1.1 [5], at its div-curl/scalar scope, using the
retained arbitrary-dimensional L_t inequality from the preceding construction.
The critical endpoint fails by an explicit family. The planar version and
radial extremal methodology have substantial prior history [6]. No claim of
historical priority is made for this derivation. The primary paper [2] receives
credit for its planar theorem and announced higher-dimensional programme.

The new operation is a damped logarithmic integral of the retained calibration.
Its damping makes every integral legitimate at finite energy, before the
desired regularity is known. Localization is justified through the critical
weak div-curl product theorem [3] and regularized Sobolev potentials [4].

The result is an analytic regularity theorem for existing weak solutions. It
does not construct all such solutions, evaluate an arbitrary measurable input,
or expand the finite Fourier solver's prior access and cost contract. The
unrestricted exact-QUBO question remains unresolved.

1. EXACT INPUT AND OUTPUT OF THE MATHEMATICAL THEOREM
====================================================

Let d>=2 and Omega be an open subset of R^d. Let E,B be locally integrable
real vector fields satisfying

  curl E=0,       div B=0

in distributions. Put

  J=E.B,
  alpha=|E+B|/2,       b=|E-B|/2,
  R=alpha+b,
  |F|=(|E|^2+|B|^2)^(1/2).

The letter b here is a nonnegative scalar, not the vector field B. We have

  J=alpha^2-b^2,       R^2 <= |F|^2 <= 2R^2.

Assume:

  (i)  J>=0 and J is locally integrable (finite local energy);
  (ii) K:Omega->[1,infinity) is measurable and finite almost everywhere;
  (iii) R^2<=KJ almost everywhere;
  (iv) exp(tau K) is locally integrable for some tau>0.

The distortion inequality is equivalently

  |E|^2+|B|^2 <= (K+K^(-1)) E.B.                 (1.1)

THEOREM. For every 0<beta<tau,

  J log^beta(e+J) is locally integrable,          (1.2)
  |F|^2 log^(beta-1)(e+|F|) is locally integrable. (1.3)

The threshold beta=tau cannot be included in general. It fails for every
tau>0 and every dimension d>=2, even for an explicitly given symmetric
coefficient field and continuous scalar solution.

Equivalently, in the notation L^2 log^a L, the sharp sufficient range is

  -1<a<tau-1.

These statements determine the full sharp logarithmic exponent threshold.
They do not assert a dimension-independent constant in every localized norm
estimate. The exponent threshold is dimension-independent; localization
constants may depend on d.

2. QUANTITATIVE NORMALIZATION
=============================

For concentric balls B_r and B_(2r) compactly contained in Omega, put

  q=2-1/(d+1),       m=q/(2-q)=2d+1,
  E0=average_(B_(2r)) J,
  M0=average_(B_(2r)) exp(tau K).

If E0=0 then both fields vanish almost everywhere on B_(2r). Otherwise use

  A0^2 = [2m/(e tau)] E0 M0^(1/m).              (2.1)

Hölder's inequality and |F|^2<=2KJ give

  (average |F|^q)^(2/q)
    <= 2 E0 (average K^m)^(1/m)
    <= [2m/(e tau)] E0 M0^(1/m)=A0^2,           (2.2)

since x^m <= [m/(e tau)]^m exp(tau x) for x>=0.

The proof below gives a finite constant C(d,beta,tau) such that

  average_(B_r) [
    (J/A0^2) log^beta(e+J/A0^2)
    + (|F|/A0)^2 log^(beta-1)(e+|F|/A0)
  ] <= C(d,beta,tau)(1+M0).                     (2.3)

The proof specifies the scalar parameter choices, including their potentially
large thresholds. Fixed-ball Sobolev-potential constants enter C. Formula
(2.3) is a quantitative analytic estimate; this report does not claim an
implemented numerical routine for that full constant or for arbitrary
measurable inputs. The energy and exponential moments in (2.1) are mathematical
hypotheses or supplied bounds, not quantities secretly computed by an oracle.

3. THE RETAINED CALIBRATION AND A LOW-REGULARITY EXTENSION
========================================================

For arbitrary vectors a,b in R^d define

  r=(|a+b|+|a-b|)/2,       D=a.b,
  L_t(a,b)=D                     if r<=t,
           t|a+b|-t^2            if r>t.

The accepted preceding construction [1], Section 2, proves that for a
periodic gradient plus its mean and a periodic divergence-free field,

  average L_t(a,b) >= L_t(average a,average b).  (3.1)

That result is used as capital. It is stronger information than a bare
L^p operator-norm bound; its scalar projection origin is credited to [2].

In particular, for compactly supported smooth gradient and divergence-free
fields on R^d,

  integral L_t(a,b)>=0.                        (3.2)

One can place the support in a periodic cube and rescale. Both means vanish.

For each fixed t, L_t is globally Lipschitz as a function of (a,b), with
Lipschitz constant at most a fixed multiple of t. Inside r<=t, both |a| and
|b| are at most t; outside it the other expression has the same gradient
bound. The two expressions agree on the interface r=t. Hence (3.2) passes
by convolution to compactly supported L^1 div-curl fields. This extension
does not require either field to be in L^2.

4. WHY THE FINITE-ENERGY STARTING CLASS IS SUFFICIENT
===================================================

First obtain the exact critical preliminary integrability, without assuming
the conclusion. Split into

  K <= (2/tau) log(e+R)

and its complement. On the first set, R^2<=KJ gives

  R^2/log(e+R) <= (2/tau)J.

On the complement, (e+R)^2<exp(tau K), so

  R^2/log(e+R) <= exp(tau K).

Thus E and B belong locally to L^2 log^(-1)L. In particular they belong to
every local L^q with q<2.

The critical product fact used now is [3], Corollary 3.3 and the following
paragraph on page 745: for a div-curl pair in the conjugate L^p log^(-1)L
classes, the weak div-curl product has a canonical definition; when the
pointwise product is nonnegative, it is locally integrable and represents
that weak product. We use p=2. Consequently the given nonnegative J is the
distributional div-curl product, with no missing singular energy measure.

Conversely, starting with the critical L^2 log^(-1)L class and nonnegative
pointwise product already gives local integrability of J by that theorem.
Thus the finite-energy formulation here also covers the minimal logarithmic
starting class in the original conjecture; it is not an extra L^2 assumption.

This identity is essential. Mere formal integration by parts for two
sub-L^2 fields would not justify the proof.

5. LOCALIZATION WITH CONTROLLED ERRORS
=====================================

Rescale B_(2r) to a fixed ball and divide both fields by A0, so their L^q
norms, with q from Section 2, are bounded by a dimension-dependent constant.
All subsequent constants are for these normalized fields.

On a ball, standard regularized Poincare potential operators [4] supply

  E=grad u,
  B_i=sum_j partial_j Psi_ij,       Psi_ij=-Psi_ji,

with u and Psi in W^(1,q) and controlled norms after fixing their gauges.
Sobolev embedding gives u,Psi in L^(q*), where q*=dq/(d-q)>2.

Choose a fixed smooth cutoff chi supported inside the outer ball and equal
to one on the inner ball. Define compactly supported fields

  a=grad(chi u)=chi E+g_a,
  b_i=sum_j partial_j(chi Psi_ij)=chi B_i+(g_b)_i.

They satisfy curl a=0 and div b=0 exactly. Write g=(g_a,g_b). Then

  g is in L^(q*),
  D=a.b=J0+C0,       J0=chi^2 J>=0,
  C0=chi E.g_b+chi B.g_a+g_a.g_b.

Since q>2d/(d+1),

  1/q+1/q*=2/q-1/d<1.

It follows that C0 belongs to some L^(1+delta), with delta>0 and a controlled
norm. Also |g|^2 belongs to such a space.

The weak-product identity from Section 4, the product rule, and integrability
of these cross terms identify D with the actual distributional product of
a and b. Therefore

  integral D=0.                                (5.1)

For example, this follows by testing the distribution
div[(chi u)b] against a function equal to one on its compact support. The
potential times the flux is locally integrable by the same Sobolev exponents.

The norm r(a,b) satisfies, for any eta>0,

  r^2 <= (1+eta)chi^2 R^2+C_eta|g|^2
      <= (1+eta)KJ0+C_eta|g|^2.                 (5.2)

Finally, r is in L^s for every s<2. For each fixed power v>=0, the quantities

  integral |C0| log^v(e+r),
  integral |g|^2 log^v(e+r)

are finite with controlled bounds, by Hölder and the strictly super-L^1
error exponents. These facts pay for all cutoff errors below.

6. THE NEW DAMPED LOGARITHMIC OPERATION
======================================

Fix beta>0. For epsilon>0, and t>=e, use the positive weight

  w_epsilon(t)=t^(-1-epsilon)(log t)^(beta-1).

For r>e define

  A_epsilon(r)=integral_e^r (1-t/r)w_epsilon(t) dt,
  B_epsilon(r)=integral_e^r t(r-t)w_epsilon(t) dt,

and set both to zero for r<=e. The letter B_epsilon denotes a scalar
function, not the flux.

The algebraic identity

  D-L_t=(r-t)(D/r-t),       t<r,

gives

  I_epsilon(a,b)
    :=integral_e^infinity w_epsilon(t)[D-L_t(a,b)]dt
     =D A_epsilon(r)-B_epsilon(r).               (6.1)

For each fixed epsilon>0, Fubini is legitimate: A_epsilon is bounded, D is
integrable, and B_epsilon(r) is bounded by a constant times
1+r^(2-epsilon/2). The latter is integrable by Section 5. Equations (3.2)
and (5.1) therefore imply

  integral I_epsilon(a,b)<=0.                  (6.2)

Damping is retained until a uniform bound has been obtained. Taking an
undamped logarithmic moment at the start would presume the desired result.

7. SHARP SCALAR COEFFICIENTS, UNIFORM IN THE DAMPING
==================================================

For any beta>0 and 0<eta<=1/8 there are R0>e and epsilon0>0 such that for
R>=R0 and 0<epsilon<=epsilon0,

  A_epsilon(R) >= ((1-eta)/beta) R^(-epsilon)(log R)^beta,      (7.1)
  B_epsilon(R) <= ((1+eta)/2) R^(2-epsilon)(log R)^(beta-1).    (7.2)

Also 0<=A_epsilon(R)<=(log R)^beta/beta for R>=e.

Here are effective choices and proofs. Let

  epsilon0=eta/16,
  delta0=eta/[16(1+|beta-1|)],
  L0=max{2, (4/eta)^(1/beta), 4 max(beta,1)/eta,
            (4/delta0) log[64/(eta delta0)]},
  R0=exp(L0).

For A_epsilon, use t^(-epsilon)>=R^(-epsilon) to obtain

  A_epsilon(R)>=R^(-epsilon){[(log R)^beta-1]/beta
                              -(1/R)integral_e^R(log t)^(beta-1)dt}.

If beta>=1, the last average is at most (log R)^(beta-1). If 0<beta<1,
split at sqrt(R), obtaining at most R^(-1/2)+2(log R)^(beta-1).
The choices of L0 then give (7.1).

For B_epsilon, substitute t=Rv. If beta>=1, directly bound the logarithmic
factor by (log R)^(beta-1) and use

  integral_0^1 (1-v)v^(-epsilon)dv
    =1/[(1-epsilon)(2-epsilon)] <= (1+eta/4)/2.

For 0<beta<1, split at t=R^(1-delta0). Above the split the logarithmic factor
is at most (1+eta/4)(log R)^(beta-1). Below it, its contribution divided by
R^(2-epsilon)(log R)^(beta-1) is at most

  2 exp(-delta0 L/2)L^(1-beta)
    <= (8/delta0) exp(-delta0 L/4) <= eta/8,

where L=log R>=L0. The high contribution is at most (1+eta/4)^2/2. Their sum
is at most (1+eta)/2. This proves (7.2).

The leading constants 1/beta and 1/2 are the reason the final threshold is
exactly beta<tau. They are not replaced by dimension-dependent estimates.

8. ABSORPTION AND THE LIMIT
===========================

Fix 0<beta<tau. Choose

  gamma=(beta+tau)/2,
  eta=(gamma-beta)/(8gamma),
  a_*=(1-eta)/beta,       b_*=(1+eta)/2.

In particular eta<1/8. These choices ensure

  c_*:=a_*-2b_*(1+eta)/gamma
       >=(gamma-beta)/(2beta gamma)>0.          (8.1)

Use R0 and epsilon0 from Section 7. For the localized pair define

  X_epsilon=integral_(r>=R0) J0 r^(-epsilon)(log r)^beta,
  Y_epsilon=integral_(r>=R0) r^(2-epsilon)(log r)^(beta-1).

The signed error C0 and the region r<R0 are controlled by Section 5 and
the uniform upper bound on A_epsilon. Thus (6.2), (7.1), and (7.2) give

  a_* X_epsilon <= b_* Y_epsilon+C.            (8.2)

Split the Y_epsilon integral according to whether

  K <= (2/gamma) log r.                        (8.3)

On this set, (5.2) gives

  r^(2-epsilon)(log r)^(beta-1)
    <= [2(1+eta)/gamma]J0 r^(-epsilon)(log r)^beta
       +C_eta |g|^2 r^(-epsilon)(log r)^(beta-1).

The last term is uniformly integrable. On the complement of (8.3),

  r^2 < exp(gamma K).

Hence, with v=max(beta-1,0),

  r^(2-epsilon)(log r)^(beta-1)
    <= exp(gamma K) max{1,(gamma K/2)^v}
    <= C(beta,gamma,tau) exp(tau K).

The gap tau-gamma>0 absorbs the polynomial factor. Consequently,

  Y_epsilon <= [2(1+eta)/gamma]X_epsilon+C(1+M0).   (8.4)

Combine (8.1), (8.2), and (8.4). Both X_epsilon and Y_epsilon are bounded
independently of epsilon as epsilon decreases to zero. On r>=R0>1 their
integrands increase monotonically in this limit. Monotone convergence gives

  integral J0(log r)^beta<infinity,
  integral r^2(log r)^(beta-1)<infinity

on that region. On the inner ball, chi=1 and g=0, so r=R and J0=J. The
bounded-r region adds a finite amount. Since J<=R^2 and |F| is comparable
to R, these are exactly (1.2)-(1.3). The normalized error bounds also give
(2.3). This completes the proof for arbitrary d>=2 and every finite-energy
field satisfying the stated distortion and moment assumptions.

9. EXPLICIT ENDPOINT FAMILY
===========================

For any tau>0 choose T>max(e,tau+1) and work in the disk 0<r<exp(-T).
Let

  t=log(1/r),
  rho(r)=t^(-tau/2)(log t)^(-1/2),
  h(t)=[tau+1/log t]/(2t),
  K(t)=1/h(t)=2t/[tau+1/log t].

Then rho'(r)=h(t)rho(r)/r and 0<h<1. Put

  u=rho(r)cos(theta),       v=rho(r)sin(theta),
  E=grad u,       B=-Jrot grad v,
  Jrot=[[0,-1],[1,0]],
  A=K e_r tensor e_r+K^(-1)e_theta tensor e_theta.

The fields satisfy B=AE and div B=0 in distributions, including at the
origin. Both scalar potentials tend to zero there and have integrable weak
derivatives. Assign arbitrary finite values at the single point r=0.

Direct calculation gives

  J=E.B=h rho^2/r^2,
  |F|^2=(1+h^2)rho^2/r^2,
  R=rho/r,
  R^2=KJ.

The energy is explicitly finite:

  integral_disk J dx = pi rho(exp(-T))^2
                     = pi T^(-tau)(log T)^(-1).       (9.1)

The critical exponential moment is also finite. Exactly,

  integral_disk exp(tau K) dx
    =2pi integral_T^infinity exp[-2t/(tau log t+1)]dt. (9.2)

Since log t<=sqrt(t) for t>=1, let c=2/(tau+1) to bound (9.2) by

  4pi exp(-c sqrt(T))[sqrt(T)/c+1/c^2].               (9.3)

But log R~t and log J~2t. Therefore each of

  integral |F|^2 log^(tau-1)(e+|F|),
  integral J log^tau(e+J)

has a tail comparable to integral_T^infinity dt/[t log t], which diverges.
This is an exact obstruction at beta=tau, with exp(tau K) integrable, not
merely a failure at some smaller exponential rate.

Extend u,v and these fields independently of d-2 extra coordinates on a
cylinder, with A acting as the identity on those coordinates. The same
properties and divergence hold in every d>=2. This is a scalar PDE with
explicit symmetric positive-definite coefficients, not an abstract sequence
of unrelated measurable functions.

In particular, tau=1 gives a medium with exp(K) integrable and finite energy
whose gradient and flux are not in ordinary L^2 locally. Taking T=16 makes
the moment upper bound 20pi exp(-4) and the energy pi/[16 log(16)].

The radial logarithmic construction is consistent with the planar extremal
method in [6]. The all-dimensional obstruction is obtained by the explicit
embedding above; historical novelty for the family is not claimed.

10. EVEN ALL EXPONENTIAL MOMENTS DO NOT FORCE A POWER GAIN
=========================================================

A second exact family prevents an overstatement of the theorem. Let t=log(1/r)
and, for t>T>1, choose

  rho(r)=exp(-2sqrt(t)),       h(t)=1/sqrt(t),       K(t)=sqrt(t).

Use the same formulas for u,v,E,B,A. For every finite sigma>0,

  integral exp(sigma K) dx
    =2pi integral_T^infinity exp(-2t+sigma sqrt(t))dt < infinity.

All finite logarithmic moments of the quadratic field amplitude are finite.
Nevertheless, for every p>2,

  integral |F|^p dx
    is bounded below by a positive multiple of
    integral_T^infinity exp[(p-2)t-2p sqrt(t)]dt
    =infinity.

Thus an assumption of all exponential distortion moments still does not
provide a universal L^(2+epsilon) improvement for any epsilon>0. The
logarithmic scale in the theorem is essential.

11. ELLIPTIC AND NONLINEAR CONSEQUENCES
======================================

For a measurable symmetric positive-definite coefficient field satisfying

  K(x)^(-1)I <= A(x) <= K(x)I,                    (11.1)

every existing finite-energy weak solution of div(A grad u)=0 has E=grad u,
B=A grad u and satisfies the theorem. The equivalence of (11.1) with the
quadratic distortion bound follows eigenvalue by eigenvalue from

  1+a^2 <= (K+K^(-1))a,       K^(-1)<=a<=K.

One may choose a fixed global reference scale for A before applying (11.1).
An x-dependent rescaling cannot silently be performed: it changes the
divergence equation. In particular the local condition number alone is not
the hypothesis. The coefficient and its inverse are controlled in fixed
units.

The same conclusion holds for an existing finite-energy scalar solution
of div Acal(x,grad u)=0 whenever its constitutive law satisfies

  |z|^2+|Acal(x,z)|^2
    <=(K(x)+K(x)^(-1)) z.Acal(x,z).

No differentiability or uniform Hessian bound on that nonlinear law is
needed for this regularity deduction. Existence and uniqueness of solutions
for every such law are separate questions and are not asserted here.

The sharp rule has immediate consequences:

* tau>1 guarantees local square integrability of both gradient and flux.
  tau=1 does not guarantee it, as Section 9 shows.
* tau>2 guarantees their local L^2 log L integrability.
* For any beta<tau, the energy density is in L log^beta L. Consequently
  energy cannot accumulate in arbitrary amounts on sets of vanishing volume
  within a family having the uniform local bounds of Section 2.

For example, if on a compact region

  integral J log^beta(e+J/A0^2) <= C A0^2,

then for every measurable set S of volume v<1 and T0>0,

  integral_S J <= T0 v + C A0^2/log^beta(e+T0/A0^2).

Choosing T0=A0^2 v^(-1/2) gives an explicit modulus tending to zero. This
is a genuine concentration bound for the energy. For the field squared,
the analogous uniform-integrability conclusion requires a positive log
power, available when tau>1. No identification of a limiting homogenized
coefficient, physical failure threshold, or experimental performance follows
without additional evidence.

12. INPUT/ACCESS/RESOURCE CONTRACT AND PRESERVATION
==================================================

The output of this work is a proved sharp analytic implication, quantitative
normalization, and exact counterexample families. Its inputs are mathematical
fields, distributional constraints, the distortion inequality, finite energy,
and an exponential-moment hypothesis. It does not obtain these inputs by
querying a black box or by solving an unspecified PDE.

No finite-bit algorithm for arbitrary measurable coefficients is claimed.
The only implemented solver in the predecessor still requires its explicit
Fourier encoding and checked contraction certificate; all dimension,
frequency and inverse-gap costs remain. The new logarithmic theorem does
not remove those costs, construct a missing discretization rate, or imply
an efficient algorithm where the uniform contraction gap vanishes.

The auxiliary proof thresholds are explicitly in Section 7. They can be
very large when beta approaches tau or zero. The theorem's sharpness concerns
the exponent range, not a numerically optimal prefactor or practical solver.
