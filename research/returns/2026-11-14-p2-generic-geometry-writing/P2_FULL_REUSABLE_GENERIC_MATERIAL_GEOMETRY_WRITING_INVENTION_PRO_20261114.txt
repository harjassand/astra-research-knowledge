Can one physically write, use and repeatedly rewrite load-bearing geometry into the SAME generic material stock, using a common external writer, so that the material becomes a useful freely retained structure after the writer and temporary supports withdraw?

I want a new reusable material-to-structure operation. The central operation is to set the actual stress-free geometry, or an equally effective persistent physical state, locally in existing stock and then obtain structural stiffness and load capacity. Changing a drawing, computing a desired shape, moving a permanently attached robot, or manufacturing a shape-specific replacement does not supply it. The programmable class consists of ten consecutive 50 mm zones whose two bending-curvature components in the transported material frame can each be chosen from {-2, 0, +2} per metre, with no commanded twist. The structure must then sustain its own complete weight and an additional tip force in any direction with magnitude up to 10 N for 24 hours without supplied holding power, with no more than 10 mm additional load-induced displacement or 2 degrees additional end rotation, and no progressive instability. These are separate shape and loaded-function requirements; do not hide initial error inside a later displacement measurement.

The material may be unloaded and independently retained before rewriting; support, load transfer and its subsequent withdrawal are paid operations.
