P3 — NOISY THERMAL CONTROL
Positive repair of interacting thermal states, including the actuators
7 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane

PROGRAMME: Repeatable many-body physical-state control with information,
initialization, calibration, reset, storage, native operation, noise and
continuing resources paid. The programme remains unfinished.

QUESTION RESOLVED HERE

For finite-temperature, noncommuting one-dimensional spin systems, can local
modeled noise be absorbed by a positive controller without paying the global
Gibbs condition number or losing reference-complete rapid restoration? Can
the same construction include modeled noise generated by its own actuators,
and identify precisely when the resulting positive repair loop has finite
rates and fits supplied capacities?

Answer: yes, at the explicit generator and simultaneous-native-law contracts
below. The construction covers every bounded finite-range chain or cycle at
each fixed positive temperature. It is not restricted to commuting Hamiltonians,
translation-invariant systems, product targets or bounded-depth faithful targets.
It yields full physical Gibbs output, including decoupling from arbitrary
untouched references. A finite compiler uses local windows and positive jump
maps, rather than enumerating the full many-body Hilbert space. Modeled actuator
noise closes through a nonnegative linear system. Its reachable gain criterion,
least balancing rates and capacity test are exact.

This is a structural mathematical resolution, not a claim that an economical
apparatus has been acquired. The conservative dense-window compiler can be
extremely costly. The actual native response law, an operational lower bound
on the mixing constant, physical realization of the emitted interactions,
calibration precision, reset, randomness and continuing holding resources must
be supplied and priced. A logical channel description does not install it.

The new consequence is the combination of noncommuting Gibbs locality,
complete entropy contraction, and positive closure of actuator-generated
noise. KMS completion, CKG sampling, complete MLSI, Araki locality, relative-
entropy data processing, positive-system small-gain analysis and finite
channel synthesis are credited established machinery. No priority claim is made.

1. WHY THIS QUESTION, AND WHAT WAS ALREADY AVAILABLE

The accepted physical-output limit excludes a constant-strength whole-state
purity threshold for its continuing local noise and product/bounded-degree
graph targets. It does not exclude faithful thermal targets. Earlier accepted
noise completion supplied a positive partner for an arbitrary modeled jump,
but the economical locality and restoration results concerned commuting
Gibbs states or bounded-depth faithful targets. The unresolved possibility
was a genuinely interacting noncommuting thermal class.

Current primary work supplied a stronger opening than an adjacent refinement
of the purity bound. Gao, Guo and Wang [1], Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2,
give a size-independent complete modified log-Sobolev inequality for CKG
samplers on bounded finite-range one-dimensional chains and cycles at fixed
positive temperature. The retained reference is arbitrary. This supersedes,
for the present purpose, the ordinary rapid-mixing route in Chen–Ding [2].
The complete bound is used, not rederived or claimed as new.

Selective reading also covered the distinct new Rouze–Franca [3] and Kim [4]
originals. They support the broader current Gibbs-sampling context. The
coherent filter identity from [3] is useful below. Its block dynamics is not
silently substituted for the CKG generator governed by [1]. The held earlier
Nature original was not recovered through these different papers.

2. INPUTS, OUTPUT AND NORMALIZATION

Work consists of N sites, local dimension d, on a path or cycle. A supplied
Hamiltonian has the form

 H = sum_X h_X,    h_X = h_X*,
 diam(X) <= r0,    sup_i sum_(X containing i) ||h_X|| <= J.

Fix beta>0, d, r0 and J independently of N. Units are declared with hbar=k_B=1;
physical rate and energy calibrations remain separate supplied information.
The target is

 sigma = exp(-beta H) / Tr exp(-beta H).

The supplied local matrices may be complex and spatially nonuniform. They need
not commute. Finite rational/finite-precision inputs describe a nominal model;
uncertainty in that model has the separate cost and error contract in Section 9.

For a jump F, write D[F](rho)=F rho F* - {F*F,rho}/2. Modeled noise types are

 D_i = D[F_i] - i[h_i, .],

where F_i and h_i are supplied on intervals of bounded length s0. A bounded
number of jumps on one support can instead be grouped into a noise type. The
normalization ||F_i||<=1 transfers strengths into nonnegative coefficients.
There are m=O(N) types in the bounded-density regime. Known coherent drifts
are included and paid; cancellation of h_i is an actual Hamiltonian command.
An additional drift commuting with sigma can also be retained as stationary.

The ideal base sampler is the normalized Gaussian CKG generator S of [1],
with unit-rate site terms. On qubits its nonidentity proposals are X/2,Y/2,Z/2.
Write alpha>0 for a justified CMLSI lower bound in this normalization. It depends
on beta,d,r0,J, not N or reference dimension. Multiplying S by an actual native
rate u>0 multiplies the certified entropy rate by u. This is not a free change
of physical clock units.

The exact target output contract is the replacement channel R_sigma:

 (R_sigma tensor id_R)(rho_WR) = sigma tensor rho_R.

The operational error convention is half the diamond norm. This simultaneously
bounds every joint input and every untouched finite reference R. It concerns
the physical work spins, not a logical state available only after decoding.
No claim is made about preserving arbitrary unknown information in W: the
operation intentionally prepares a fixed state and removes its input memory.

3. POSITIVE NOISE COMPLETION AND COMPLETE RESTORATION

For each F_i define the accepted KMS partner and coherent correction

 J_i = exp(-beta H/2) F_i* exp(beta H/2),
 K_i = F_i*F_i + J_i*J_i,
 G_i = (i/2) tanh[(beta/4) ad_H](K_i),
 C_i = D[J_i] - i[G_i-h_i, .].                              (1)

The accepted paired KMS construction, credited to the detailed-balance
machinery in Ding–Li–Lin [5], gives

 P_i := D_i + C_i,                P_i(sigma)=0.             (2)

Each C_i is a valid GKSL generator: its dissipative coefficient is nonnegative,
and its coherent term is Hermitian. This construction adds a physical repair
generator; it does not subtract the environmental dissipator. The partner
formula is accepted capital and is not reproved here.

THEOREM 1 — Complete contraction survives stationary additions.

Let S satisfy complete entropy decay at rate 2 alpha for a faithful sigma.
For any nonnegative coefficients x_i, set

 L = u S + sum_i x_i P_i.                                  (3)

Then, for every joint state rho_WR,

 D((exp(tL) tensor id_R)(rho_WR) || sigma tensor rho_R)
     <= exp(-2u alpha t) D(rho_WR || sigma tensor rho_R).    (4)

No commutation between S and the P_i is required, and the modeled coefficients
x_i need not be small. They still consume their real rates and other resources.

Proof. Each exp(tP_i) tensor id_R fixes sigma tensor rho_R. Relative-entropy
data processing makes its entropy-production contribution nonnegative.
Entropy production is linear in the generator at a fixed input. The uS term
already contributes at least 2u alpha D; every additional term contributes
at least zero. Integration gives (4). Singular inputs and reference marginals
follow by restriction to the reference support and continuity. This uses the
complete source inequality, not an ordinary inequality with an unearned
amplification to references. QED.

Let

 A_N = max{1, sqrt[N(log d + beta J)]}.

The initial joint relative entropy is at most 2N(log d+beta J): use
-S(W|R)<=N log d, ||H||<=NJ and log Z<=N log d+beta NJ.
Pinsker's inequality applied after (4) therefore gives

 1/2 ||exp(tL)-R_sigma||_diamond
     <= h_N(t) := min{1, A_N exp(-u alpha t)}.              (5)

Thus t = (u alpha)^(-1) log(A_N/epsilon) suffices in the exact model.
The logarithmic N dependence in (5) is inherited from [1]. The new point here
is its survival under the explicitly constructed ambient-plus-repair law,
including the actuator loop developed next.

4. REPAIRING THE NOISE OF THE REPAIRS

The simultaneous native law is an explicit input. At commands u and x>=0,
assume the warranted effective generator is

 L_nat(u,x) = u S + sum_i [b_i(u)+(E x)_i] D_i
                     + sum_i x_i C_i,                    (6)
 b(u) = b_bg + u e0 >= 0,    E_ij >= 0.

Here b_bg is ambient noise, e0 is additional modeled noise caused by running
the base sampler, and E_ij is the additional noise of type i caused by one
unit of repair actuator j. All effects are simultaneous. This law must cover
the actual compiler, coherent controls, resets and routing when their errors
are being treated as modeled noise; uncovered errors go into Section 8.
One may not infer (6) from isolated actuator calibrations or from gate-level
simulation of the ideal generators. Arbitrary hysteresis, memoryful reservoirs
or state-dependent coefficients outside the declared GKSL law are not inputs.

Choose rates satisfying the positive balance equation

                    x = b(u) + E x.                       (7)

Then (6) is exactly (3). This pays every modeled repair-induced noise term,
including further noise caused by repairing the first set of actuator errors.
It is a solved self-consistency equation, not an unending runtime sequence.

THEOREM 2 — Exact finite-rate and capacity criterion for this repair law.

Draw a directed edge j->i whenever E_ij>0. Let R be the vertices reachable
from the support of b. Let E_R be the corresponding principal submatrix.
A finite nonnegative solution of (7) exists if and only if

                         spectral_radius(E_R) < 1,        (8)

with the empty reachable set interpreted as feasible. When feasible, the
componentwise least solution is

 x_R = (I-E_R)^(-1) b_R = sum_(k>=0) E_R^k b_R,
 x_(outside R)=0.                                         (9)

Consequently, supplied componentwise capacities x_i<=cap_i are feasible
within the balancing construction exactly when this least vector fits them.
It also minimizes every nonnegative linear price vector over balancing
solutions. This optimum concerns (7), not all coherent or dissipative control.

Proof. Every nonnegative solution dominates each finite Neumann partial sum.
If (8) holds, those sums converge to (9), which solves the full equation
because no edge exits the reachable set. For necessity, decompose the
reachable graph into strongly connected components. If a reachable component
has Perron root >=1, its balance equation receives a nonzero nonnegative
input from b or from earlier reachable components. Multiplying by its strictly
positive left Perron vector would give

 (1-rho) w^T x_C = w^T b'_C > 0,

which is impossible for x_C>=0 and rho>=1. Minimality, the capacity criterion
and nonnegative-price optimality follow from domination of the partial sums.
QED. This is classical positive-system/Perron–Frobenius machinery applied to
the native thermal-repair law; the general control lineage is credited [6].

There is an exact rational certificate avoiding numerical eigenvalue tests.
For nonempty R compute w=(I-E_R)^(-1)1. The condition that this inverse exists
and every w_i>0 is equivalent to (8): E_R w=w-1<w supplies a positive weighted
contraction. Exact Gaussian elimination, reachability and rational inequalities
are polynomial in m and input bit length. They do not require a quantum-state
oracle. The executable gain_certificate.py implements this test.

Useful rate regimes are explicit. If max_i sum_j E_ij<=g<1 and b_i<=b_max,
then x_i<=b_max/(1-g). A weighted inequality E w<=g w gives the corresponding
weighted bound. The gap 1-g is an actual feasibility margin. The scalar law
x=b/(1-g) shows how rates diverge as the margin closes. This is a threshold
for the specified modeled repair loop, not a hardware error threshold.

Example with nearest-neighbor actuator cross-noise:

 E_ii=1/8, E_(i,i+1)=E_(i+1,i)=1/16, all other entries zero.

Every row sum is at most 1/4. For b_i<=1/2 the sufficient bound is x_i<=2/3,
independent of chain length. For the three-type record b=(3/10,2/5,1/2), the
exact minimal solution is

 x=(1292/3395, 256/485, 2068/3395).

The exact positive certificate is w=(120/97,128/97,120/97), with weighted gain
31/128. All three unit rate caps pass. At prices (2,3,4), the exact minimum
balancing price is 16232/3395. These are declared mathematical coefficients,
not acquired device observations.

Failure of (8) is deliberately not promoted to general impossibility. For a
qubit target diag(p,1-p), choose the down and up noises with background rates
p and 1-p. Their total drift at that target vanishes. Even with E=2I, for
which positive componentwise balance is impossible, x=0 already leaves the
target stationary when added to the base sampler. Noise-type drifts can cancel
without individual completion. The supplied-menu stationarity problem and
all-controller optimality remain different questions.

5. THE LOCALITY BRIDGE: NO GLOBAL GIBBS CONDITION-NUMBER COST

The naive estimate ||J_i||<=||sigma^(1/2)|| ||sigma^(-1/2)|| ||F_i|| is generally
exponential in N. It is unnecessary for bounded-support noise in one dimension.
The following explicit bounds show why, and specify a finite compiler.

For a seed interval S of length at most s0, let B_l be its enlargement by r0*l
sites on both sides, intersected with the chain/cycle. Write H_l=H_(B_l).
Set

 s=beta/2, v=4r0(1+J), a=v*s*exp(v*s),
 M0=exp(2J*s0*s), M=M0 exp(a), w_l=s0+2r0*l.

The complex-time shell estimate underlying Araki locality, in the explicit
form used in [1, Appendix C], gives for ||F||<=1

 J_l=exp(-sH_l) F* exp(sH_l),
 ||J_l-J_(l-1)|| <= M0 a^l/l!  (l>=1),
 ||J_l||,||J_full|| <= M,
 ||J_full-J_R|| <= j_R := M0 sum_(l>R) a^l/l!.             (10)

For a seed interval rather than a singleton, the initial shell has norm at
most exp(2J*s0*s); each added layer still contains at most 2r0 sites. The same
Duhamel induction therefore gives (10). All constants are independent of N.
The factorial tail is summable and explicitly computable. These constants can
grow very badly with beta, J and s0; fixed temperature is substantive.

Define K_l=F*F+J_l*J_l. Then ||K_l||<=1+M^2 and

 ||K_l-K_(l-1)|| <= 2M M0 a^l/l!.                          (11)

For any Hermitian A define G_H(A)=(i/2)tanh[(beta/4)ad_H](A). The real-time
filter identity of [3, Lemma 3.3] is

 G_H(A) = (1/beta) integral_0^infinity
       [exp(itH) A exp(-itH)-exp(-itH) A exp(itH)]
                      / sinh(2pi t/beta) dt,
 ||G_H(A)|| <= beta ||[H,A]||/8.                          (12)

Thus G_H is local on exponentially decaying operator shells even though a
dimension-independent norm bound on the unrestricted multiplier is unavailable.
For an operator supported on B_R, approximating H by H_(B_(R+q)) gives, for
q>=1, the explicit estimate

 ||[G_H-G_(H_(B_(R+q)))](A)|| <= ||A|| B_beta(q),

 B_beta(q)= q exp(-q/2)/(2e pi)
           +(2/pi) log coth[pi q/(2e v beta)].             (13)

Proof of (13). Real-time shell differences for a fixed interval seed obey
||(tau_H^t-tau_(H_(B_(R+q)))^t)(A)||
 <= ||A|| sum_(l>q) (v|t|)^l/l!.
For 0<=t<=T=q/(2e v), the tail is at most v t exp(-q/2).
Insert both positive and negative times into (12), use
1/[beta sinh(2pi t/beta)]<=1/(2pi t), and integrate to T.
For t>T use the norm bound 2||A|| on each evolution difference and integrate
the remaining hyperbolic kernel exactly. The two terms are (13). This proof
also covers restricted Hamiltonians and ends of a chain. QED.

The finite repair uses the actual positive jump J_R and the Hermitian matrix

 G_Rq = G_(H_(B_(R+q)))(K_R),
 C_i^(R,q)=D[J_R]-i[G_Rq-h_i,.].                           (14)

It is GKSL for every finite radius. We have not truncated a signed generator
expansion and then assumed the result stays completely positive.

Since ||[H,A_l]||<=2J w_l ||A_l|| for a shell on B_l, (11)-(13) give

 ||G_full-G_Rq|| <= g_Rq,
 g_Rq = (beta J M M0/2) sum_(l>R) w_l a^l/l!
                              +(1+M^2) B_beta(q).          (15)

The elementary dissipator perturbation estimate then yields

 1/2 ||C_i-C_i^(R,q)||_diamond <= 2M j_R + g_Rq.            (16)

This estimate includes arbitrary references. The same shell sum starting at
l=1, together with ||K_0||<=1+M0^2, bounds ||G_full|| independently of N.
For example a valid bound is

 (beta J/4)[s0(1+M0^2)+2M M0 sum_(l>=1) w_l a^l/l!].

Therefore completion of each bounded-strength local noise has bounded ideal
quasilocal norm at fixed model parameters. The exponential global Gibbs
condition number never enters the computation.

6. FINITE POSITIVE COMPILATION OF THE BASE SAMPLER

The base sampler also requires compilation and is not an acquired Gibbs oracle.
Use its local Hamiltonian restriction H_(B_R) in the Gaussian CKG filter, then
compute its coherent term in the larger window B_(R+q), exactly as in (14).
All data come from supplied microscopic interaction terms.

Here is a finite positive form of the frequency integral. In an energy basis
of a local window, let u_ab=beta(E_a-E_b), and let A be an onsite proposal.
The completely positive jump map has the Gram matrix, indexed by (a,b),(c,d),

 Q_(ab,cd) = A_ab conjugate(A_cd) k(u_ab,u_cd),
 k(u,v)=2^(-1/2) exp[-1/2-(u^2+v^2)/4+(u+v-2)^2/16].     (17)

This follows by integrating the product of the two Gaussian Fourier amplitudes
against exp[-(beta omega+1)^2/2]. In particular, it retains cross-frequency
coherences; replacing it by a diagonal Davies/secular approximation would be
a different sampler and would not inherit the used theorem automatically.
Q is positive semidefinite because it is an integral of rank-one positive
matrices. Sum Q over the fixed onsite proposal list, factor it, and reshape
the factors into at most D^2 jumps for a D-dimensional local window. Form
K_R=sum_j L_j*L_j and G_Rq=G_(H_(B_(R+q)))(K_R).

For completeness an explicit truncation estimate is available. Put
a_p=sum_a ||A_a||^2, a constant at fixed d, and

 E_R = (v beta/sqrt(pi)) exp(-R/2)
                         +2 erfc[R/(2e v beta)].           (18)

Splitting the Gaussian real-time filter at R/(2e v), using the shell bound
as in Section 5, gives a jump-amplitude error bounded by its zero-frequency
norm times E_R. Since the squared zero-frequency norm times integral gamma
equals one, the jump-map difference and its K difference each have diamond/
operator bound 2a_p E_R. Also ||K_R||<=a_p. Define delta_l=2a_p E_l. The
same shell-commutator argument gives the following half-diamond generator
error for one site of the base sampler:

 e_S(R,q) = 2a_p E_R
       +(beta J/4) sum_(l>R)(1+2r0*l)(delta_l+delta_(l-1))
       +a_p B_beta(q).                                    (19)

The shell sum converges exponentially. For qubits with X/2,Y/2,Z/2,
a_p=3/4. The binary64 resource evaluator uses (16) and (19), with explicit
tail sums or dominating geometric sums. Those floating-point evaluations
are diagnostics; directed rounding or rational series enclosures are needed
for executable numerical certificates.

Fix beta,d,r0,J,s0 and a bounded repair-gain margin. Choosing R,q logarithmic
in N/eta makes the whole-generator truncation error <=eta. Window size is

 w=O(log(N/eta)),     D=d^w,

with constants depending on the fixed physical parameters. Dense construction
of (17) and its factorization uses polynomially many operations in D, hence
polynomially many in N/eta. One conservative implementation is O(N D^6)
matrix arithmetic and O(D^4) working memory per local block, plus the repair
matrices and the classical native-law solve. Dense storage is an explicit
algorithm, not a promise that the polynomial has a useful exponent.

Finite-bit compilation does not require stable eigenvectors or a promised
spectral gap between local energy levels. Matrix exponentials have convergent
norm-controlled series. The Gaussian completely positive map can be formed
with positive quadrature and certified tails, or a certified approximation
to (17) followed by controlled positive projection. The coherent filter can
be integrated with its removable small-time singularity and certified tail.
Thus a finite rational matrix/channel description can be produced to any
positive tolerance with the stated finite-dimensional polynomial work.
The mathematical construction and the delivered binary64 matrix emitter are
different products. The latter does not implement interval arithmetic,
finite-alphabet gate synthesis or a physical controller.

Compilation must not hide a native-work factor. Although ||K_R||<=a_p bounds
the maximal CP jump intensity, a dense factorization only ensures
sum_j ||L_j||^2 <= Tr K_R <= D a_p. Its individual jump list can have D^2
entries. A native primitive implementing the whole CP map may have a better
price, but only if supplied. O(N) ideal CP intensity is not automatically
O(N) gate, reset, communication or control activity. Raw support incidence of
the truncated interactions grows with w, and the terms are not fixed-k local.

7. COMPOSING THE FINITE COMPILER WITH THE NATIVE LAW

For a requested tolerance, first form the finite matrices S^(R,q) and
C_i^(R,q). Obtain or use the actual simultaneous law for that menu. The
matrix E can depend on the chosen radii and physical realization. Check its
reachable gain condition and capacities for that actual menu; do not reuse
a gain certificate for a different implementation.

Choose x by (9) using its modeled law. Relative to the exact generator (3),
the finite native construction has half-diamond generator error at most

 eta_comp <= u sum_sites e_S(R,q)
                    +sum_i x_i [2M_i j_(i,R)+g_(i,Rq)],   (20)

plus separately bounded matrix arithmetic, synthesis, timing and other
implementation errors. The equal noise coefficients b+Ex=x remain exact at
the coefficient level. Imperfect stationarity from truncating C_i and S is
already included in (20); it is not silently assumed away.

There is no circular free oracle here. The controller matrices are computed
from local supplied data; the coefficient balance is solved exactly for the
warranted menu; the remaining physical realization and source costs are
explicit inputs. However, this is not a theorem that a generic unknown
laboratory can supply that menu economically. If its law, gain margin,
capacities or required precision are unavailable, the physical operating
claim has not been earned, even though the mathematical generator exists.

The source mixing constant deserves the same distinction. The theorem [1]
defines a positive model-dependent rate; its proof gives alpha=1/(4 C_s A)
with the factorization and local-production constants. It is not a measured
apparatus rate. The class-level conclusion (5) follows from that theorem.
A numerical operating schedule requires a justified lower bound on alpha in
the actual native normalization. This return does not extract numerical
values of those proof constants for an apparatus. The resource scenarios
explicitly label alpha=0.1 as a supplied illustrative parameter. They are
not a calibrated end-to-end commissioning certificate or an efficient oracle
for unknown mixing constants.

8. PERSISTENT RESIDUAL ERROR, HOLDING AND REPEATED OUTPUT

Let L be the exact balanced generator and let the actually implemented,
possibly time-dependent, trace-preserving GKSL evolution have generator
L+Delta_t. Assume the warranted residual bound

             sup_t 1/2 ||Delta_t||_diamond <= eta.         (21)

This is a global bound on the modeled discrepancy. It includes anything not
already matched in (6): truncation, source drift, calibration errors, idle
storage, resets, random selection, coherent-control error and crosstalk.

THEOREM 3 — Complete restoration and unlimited active holding with a floor.

Put H_N=(1+log A_N)/(u alpha). For all initial joint states and all t>=0,

 1/2 ||(T_actual(t,0) tensor id_R)(rho)-sigma tensor rho_R||_1
       <= h_N(t)+2 eta integral_0^t h_N(s) ds
       <= A_N exp(-u alpha t)+2 eta H_N.                 (22)

Proof. Duhamel's identity compares the actual evolution with exp(tL).
Every Delta_t has zero trace, so R_sigma Delta_t=0. Therefore the exact
propagator following an error can be replaced by exp(sL)-R_sigma. Its diamond
norm is bounded by 2h_N(s), and all actual intervening propagators are CPTP.
The integral difference bound is 2 eta integral h_N. Equation (5) completes
the proof. Integrating the minimum defining h_N gives H_N. The argument
holds with every finite reference and does not assume the perturbation is
reversible, stationary, or itself a GKSL generator. Only the total physical
evolution must be CPTP. QED.

If 2 eta H_N < epsilon, a sufficient preparation time is

 t_prep = (u alpha)^(-1)
           log[A_N/(epsilon-2 eta H_N)].                  (23)

Continuing the same warranted active controller then maintains the bound
for all later times. Accuracy does not degrade linearly with the holding
duration in this active model; the rate and material/energy supply continue
for the entire holding duration. Switching the controller off, moving the
output to an unmodeled device, or using it under an unmodeled interaction
requires a separate holding/delivery contract.

For local unknown residuals with half-diamond bounds delta_i, the sufficient
global eta is sum_i delta_i. Consequently the displayed whole-state guarantee
typically needs local precision of order epsilon/[N log N] at fixed model
parameters. This is a sufficient budget, not a proved optimal necessity and
not a nonzero size-independent hardware threshold. Exact modeled noise at a
fixed rate and unknown residual error have different roles.

Repeated output uses the complete, not merely marginal, guarantee. Treat all
earlier outputs, the recorded history and any untouched memory as reference
for the next preparation. Sequential replacement errors telescope: K fresh
preparations with conditional complete errors epsilon_j have joint error at
most sum_j epsilon_j from sigma^(tensor K), before additional holding errors.
Arbitrary correlations in initial work registers are allowed. Fresh/reset
implementation resources and the warranted Markov channel law are essential;
a hidden environment retaining correlations is not made independent by a
single-system stationary-state calculation. Simultaneously retained outputs
require their own continuing controllers or other separately justified
storage resources. The rate, source and holding cost scales accordingly.

9. INFORMATION, CALIBRATION AND FULL RESOURCE PRICES

The Hamiltonian and noise description are supplied inputs at their actual
acquisition cost. A model with O(N) local terms has a compact representation;
this is not evidence that its coefficients or native law are cheaply learned.
No hardware or calibrated source was supplied or acquired in this return.
Earlier exact source-driven sampling retains its own calibrated-anchor and
matched-law promises; it does not supply these new matrices E, F_i or h_X.

For a nominal Hamiltonian Hhat, the accepted sharp Gibbs perturbation bound
from the operational thermal-state construction is

 1/2 ||sigma_H-sigma_Hhat||_1
          <= tanh[beta osc(H-Hhat)/4].                    (24)

It applies to complex noncommuting finite Hamiltonians. The prior proof is
not repeated. A sufficient estimate uses osc(H-Hhat)<=2 sum_X ||h_X-hhat_X||.
Thus a global-state specification can demand per-term precision shrinking
with N even when the representation has only O(N) terms. Confidence failure
probabilities remain separate and add to operational failure budgets.

If commands x were computed from nominal bhat,Ehat, while the true coefficients
are b,E, then the unmatched noise vector is exactly

             r=(b-bhat)+(E-Ehat)x.                        (25)

Its half-diamond generator contribution is at most
sum_i |r_i| ||D_i||_diamond/2, plus changes in the generator matrices themselves.
This can be used in (21). If the true and nominal row gains are bounded by
g<1, the sensitivity of the balancing solution additionally obeys

 ||x_true-xhat||_infinity
 <= [||b-bhat||_infinity+||E-Ehat||_infinity ||xhat||_infinity]/(1-g).

Since ||xhat||<=||bhat||/(1-g), calibration sensitivity can contain a second
inverse-margin factor. Declaring a positive gain margin does not make
calibration precision free.

For rational m-type law data, the exact solve has polynomial bit work; a dense
E costs m^2 entries to supply and inspect. O(N) acquisition or storage of E
requires a warranted sparse/local representation. Dense positivity checks or
an empirical fit are not substitutes for the simultaneous-law premise.

Let c_0 be the actual resource-price vector for one unit of the base sampler
and c_i the price vector for one unit of repair i. Components may count reset
events, gate activity, routing, random bits, power or another separately
warranted resource. Unlike units must not be added without declared prices.
For constant commands, preparation time t and active holding time h, the
continuing part of the resource ledger is

       (t+h)[u c_0 + sum_i x_i c_i].                      (26)

Add source/model acquisition, calibration, compiler work, installation and
delivery costs. The coefficients in (26) are physical resource prices if
they were physically justified; mathematical Lindblad rates alone are not
joules or heat. If an individual jump emitter has a D-dependent overhead,
its c_i or c_0 must include it. No comparison that drops that overhead is made.

There is a simple optional price calculation within this fixed linear law.
For a scalar nonnegative resource price and a fully subcritical relevant E,
x(u)=(I-E)^(-1)(b_bg+u e0). Hence total instantaneous price is p0+u p1.
With exact dynamics, set a=alpha^(-1) log(A_N/epsilon), so t=a/u. The full
preparation-plus-holding price is

      p0 a/u + p1 a + p0 h + p1 u h.                     (27)

For p0,p1,h>0 the unconstrained minimizer is sqrt(p0 a/(p1 h)), clipped to
the actually feasible rate interval. Thus faster restoration trades against
continuing holding price. This calculation assumes the stated linear prices
and native law; it is not a thermodynamic work optimum or an optimum over
general coherent controllers.

10. WHAT THE DELIVERED CALCULATIONS ESTABLISH

The exact rational gain executable passes six independent scope checks:
subcritical balance and price; unreachable unstable modes; a reachable critical
mode; a reachable supercritical mode; capacity failure; and a stationary target
outside the componentwise-balancing regime. These verify the algebraic
certificate and its distinction from all-control feasibility.

The numerical quantum instance has N=3, beta=0.65 and

 H=0.37 Z0Z1 +0.29 X1Y2 +0.23 X0 -0.17 Z1 +0.19 Y2.

It is a complex noncommuting Hamiltonian. The three noise jumps are down_0,
X_1 and (X_2+0.3i Z_2)/1.3. The full Gaussian cross-frequency Gram construction,
KMS completions and the rational gain law above are evaluated in binary64.
The base stationarity residual is about 7.03e-15; the actual noisy controlled
generator's stationarity residual is about 1.72e-14 in the reported matrix
norm. The two independently assembled generators agree to roundoff.

A faithful joint input with an entangled retained qubit reference was evolved
at ten declared times. Its final joint trace distance at t=30 is about 1.81e-9.
An independent entropy-drift check confirms nonpositive joint relative-entropy
drift for the stationary repair addition at that input. These computations
are finite diagnostics, not proofs of a diamond norm supremum, CMLSI constant,
scalable performance, or physical execution. Theorems 1-3 carry the universal
claims. The saved NPZ contains the actual emitted finite matrices.

The resource scenarios make a limitation quantitative. They set beta=0.2,
J=1, d=2, epsilon=0.01, row gain <=0.25, and an explicitly illustrative supplied
alpha=0.1. At N=10^6 the conservative balanced radii are 61 for noise repair
and 58 for the sampler, giving a maximum sufficient window of 245 spins.
The generic dense D^6 operation-count scale for that window is approximately
10^442.5. This is an upper-bound implementation cost, not a complexity lower
bound. It decisively does not demonstrate practical commissioning. The formal
polynomial improvement over enumerating 2^N dimensions does not remove this
problem. The logarithmic generator mixing time is not an inexpensive digital
compilation or native installation time.

Five additional six-site operator calculations check the jump and coherent
localization bounds on a noncommuting chain. Four independent numerical
Gaussian integrals agree with the closed cross-frequency kernel. The retained
reference input has a negative partial-transpose eigenvalue, explicitly
checking that the diagnostic input is entangled. These remain numerical
checks of the algebra and bounds, not substitutes for their proofs.

The finite-bit theorem, exact rational coefficient solver, binary64 matrix

11. SCIENTIFIC CONSEQUENCES AND PROGRAMME STATUS

A. Noncommutation is not itself a structural barrier to modeled-noise
absorption or complete rapid thermal restoration in this entire 1D class.
The source sampler supplies complete contraction; local modular partners
remove the global Gibbs condition-number penalty from noise completion.

B. The output is a whole canonical physical state, including its interacting
correlations. Every measurement event changes in probability by at most the
certified trace distance; a bounded observable changes in expectation by at
most twice that distance times its norm. This is stronger than matching a
mean response or obtaining a free-energy-density certificate.

C. Native error can sometimes be included as an exactly closed positive
operating regime. The computed repair rates and capacities include the noise
that the repairs themselves induce. The reachable gain condition is exact
for this balancing mechanism and is accompanied by a counterexample to
mistaking its failure for failure of all control.

D. Exact modeled noise need not generate a stationary accuracy floor here.
Unknown residual discrepancy does generate the explicit sufficient floor
in (22). Arbitrary initial work/reference correlation is allowed, while a
physical controller's resets, bath memory and law calibration remain real
resources. No universal hardware threshold is established.

E. Active holding can prevent secular accumulation of bounded residual error,
but incurs continuous rates and supply. Independent repeated outputs require
complete replacement guarantees and fresh implementation resources; marginal
equilibrium measurements alone would not justify that conclusion.

F. Fixed positive temperature and one-dimensional bounded finite range are
substantive. Nothing here proves all-dimensional low-temperature Gibbs
preparation, exact hard-window stabilization, ground-state control, generic
polynomial thermodynamics, P=NP, or cheap acquisition of an unknown apparatus.
If beta grows with N, the locality constants, mixing constant, native gain
margin and compilation cost must all be re-evaluated. Even a single two-level
thermal partner can have squared norm exp(beta gap). The earlier physical-
output purity limit and its finite yield/delivery consequences remain intact.

The selected structural question is closed by the explicit generator law,
finite positive compilation, exact repair-loop criterion, complete restoration
proof and full error/cost transfer. The programme of economical repeatable
physical many-body state control remains open. Its unresolved part is not
hidden inside the word 'polynomial': actual inexpensive native realization,
commissioning and numerical operating certificates have not been supplied.
No restriction of class, access or output in this report is being used to
declare that larger capability complete.
