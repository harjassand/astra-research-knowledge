1. EXACT SEPARATION COST OVER Z/(2^p)

1.1 The kernel

For a nonzero integer t, define

             [ 1   1-t ]
  K_t     =  [         ].
             [ 0    t  ]

Rows are indexed by y in {0,1}, columns by x in {0,1}. This is the Boolean
interpolation kernel l_y(t x), with l_0(z)=1-z and l_1(z)=z. For b wires set

  K(y,x) = product_{i=1}^b K_{t_i}(y_i,x_i).

Work over R=Z/(2^p), p>=1, and write delta_i=v_2(t_i). The integers t_i may
have arbitrary odd parts and signs. Define

  r(p;delta) = #{ S subset [b] : sum_{i in S} delta_i < p }.             (1)

The empty subset is included. If t_i is zero modulo 2^p, its valuation can
be capped at p for this formula. Unit entries, delta_i=0, are also allowed.

1.2 Theorem

The smallest r for which there are arbitrary functions f_j,g_j with values
in R satisfying

  K(y,x) = sum_{j=1}^r f_j(y) g_j(x)                                  (2)

for every y,x is exactly the number in (1).

For a common positive valuation delta, this becomes

  r = sum_{j=0}^{min(b,ceil(p/delta)-1)} binom(b,j).                     (3)

In particular, t_i=2 and p=b+1 give r=2^b. An even coupling does not imply
small exact separated dimension at a precision linear in the number of
wires.

1.3 Proof, including the finite-ring issue

The matrix Q_t=[[1,t-1],[0,1]] is invertible over R and satisfies

  K_t Q_t = diag(1,t).

Taking tensor products gives an invertible column change from K to a
diagonal matrix whose entry for subset S is product_{i in S} t_i. Removing
odd units by invertible diagonal rescaling leaves entries
2^{sum_{i in S} delta_i}. Such an entry is nonzero in R precisely when its
exponent is below p. This proves an upper bound of (1) by writing each
nonzero diagonal entry as one separated term and undoing the changes.

Ordinary rank over the residue field is insufficient for the lower bound:
for example, diag(2,2) is zero modulo two but need not factor through a
one-dimensional R-module. Instead use the image module.

For D=diag(2^{a_1},...,2^{a_r},0,...) with all a_i<p,

  im(D) = direct_sum_{i=1}^r 2^{a_i}R,
  dim_{F_2}(im(D)/2 im(D)) = r.

Therefore im(D) requires at least r generators over R.

Every submodule of R^k can be generated by at most k elements. One elementary
proof is by induction: its projection to the first coordinate is an ideal
(2^a), generated by one element; subtracting multiples of a chosen lift of
that generator leaves a submodule in the remaining k-1 coordinates.

If D factors as U V through R^k, im(V) is such a submodule of R^k and has at
most k generators. Its image under U, im(D), consequently has at most k
generators. Thus k>=r. A representation (2) gives exactly such a
factorization. This proves the lower bound over R, without pretending that
R is a field or that even pivots are invertible.

1.4 Scope

The theorem concerns exact separated representations of the full boundary
kernel. It does not require a long explicit table as input: K is given by b
two-by-two matrices. But it does not prove that evaluating a contraction of
K with compact structured boundary factors requires r operations. For
example, product boundary factors give a product of b scalar contractions
and are easy despite the large separated dimension. Nonlinear summaries,
factor-specific algorithms and other representations are not excluded.

2. A POSITIVE CNF FAMILY REALIZING THE SAME SEPARATION COST

Set G_t=[[1,1],[1,1+t]]. It is equivalent to diag(1,t) by invertible integer
row and column operations: with A=[[1,0],[1,1]] and B=[[1,1],[0,1]],

  G_t = A diag(1,t) B.

Consequently tensor products of G_t have exactly the separation cost (1).
For t=2^delta with delta>=1, G_t is realized by an ordinary CNF conditional
count, with no signed or weighted clauses.

For each boundary pair x_i,y_i introduce a selector s_i and delta_i bits
a_{i,1},...,a_{i,delta_i}. Use the clauses

  (!s_i OR x_i), (!s_i OR y_i),
  (s_i OR !a_{i,j}) for 1<=j<=delta_i.                                 (4)

If (x_i,y_i) is not (1,1), s_i must be zero and all a bits must be zero:
there is one extension. If (x_i,y_i)=(1,1), the s_i=0 extension contributes
one and s_i=1 leaves all delta_i a bits free, contributing 2^{delta_i}.
Thus the two-boundary conditional-count matrix is G_{2^{delta_i}}.
Disjoint gadgets give its tensor product.

For delta_i=1, there are 4b variables and 3b binary clauses, with complete
indexed binary length O(b log(b+1)). At precision p=b+1, the conditional-count
matrix requires exactly 2^b separated terms.

These formulas are always satisfiable, and their disjoint structure makes
their counts easy. Their role is to falsify a universal polynomial-size
separated representation claim, not to establish difficult SAT instances or
P!=NP. Their simple quadratic clause-violation energies are also available
from their explicit binary clauses if a later coordinator chooses to reduce
the objects to another formulation.

3. AN EXACT GLOBAL ALGORITHM, WITH THE REMAINING COST EXPOSED

3.1 Input and identity

Let E wires carry paired bits y_i,x_i. A-side vertex tensors A_v use a
partition of the y ports, and B-side tensors B_w use a partition of the x
ports. Each local tensor is explicitly tabulated. Scalar vertices are
allowed. The desired contraction modulo 2^p is

  Z = sum_{x,y} product_v A_v(y_v) product_w B_w(x_w)
               product_{i=1}^E K_{t_i}(y_i,x_i).                     (5)

The kernel is zero unless supp(y) is contained in supp(x). When it is not
zero, its value is

  product_{i:y_i=1} t_i * product_{i:y_i=0,x_i=1}(1-t_i).

For each B-side tensor define its weighted superset transform

  Btilde_w(y_w) = sum_{x_w superset y_w}
                    B_w(x_w) product_{i in x_w minus y_w}(1-t_i).     (6)

Then the whole contraction is exactly

  Z = sum_{y: sum_i delta_i y_i < p}
          product_{i:y_i=1} t_i
          product_v A_v(y_v) product_w Btilde_w(y_w)    mod 2^p.       (7)

Terms not retained in (7) vanish individually modulo 2^p. This truncation
does not appeal to cancellations between different states.

3.2 Algorithm for the local transform

On the explicit table of B_w, process one port i at a time. For every local
mask y with bit i zero, perform

  table[y] <- table[y] + (1-t_i) table[y union {i}]  mod 2^p.

After all its ports are processed, the table is (6). A degree-d table needs
d 2^{d-1} modular additions/multiplications. This is polynomial in its
explicit input length. A succinct high-arity evaluator is a different input
model and is not silently treated as an explicit table.

3.3 Global enumeration, precision and cost

Enumerate the subsets of wires with weight below p. A depth-first include/
exclude search prunes an inclusion when it reaches the budget. Every valid
partial choice has its all-zero continuation, so enumeration uses polynomial
delay and space, with O((E+1)r) visited prefixes, where r is (1).

For each retained y, evaluate the product in (7) by table lookups and modular
multiplication. No conditional counting oracle is used.

All residues have p bits. Schoolbook arithmetic gives polynomial bit cost
per addition, multiplication and lookup. If I is the full explicit input
length, a conservative bound is

  O((I+1)^3(p+1)^2 + r (E+1)^2 (p+1)^2)                              (8)

bit operations, with polynomial space in I+E+p. This loose bound includes
parsing, table transforms, subset indices and arithmetic. It is not a
polynomial bound in the input alone when r is superpolynomial, or when p is
an exponentially large requested precision encoded in binary. The actual
p bits of work are charged.

The bound is useful when r is small. It does not settle SAT because the
precision and couplings in a general SAT-preserving input need not make r
small. The next exact construction demonstrates this directly.

4. A UNIFORM SAT-PRESERVING WEAK-WIRE CONSTRUCTION

4.1 A constant-size identity

Define the fixed integer matrices

  K = [[1,-1],[0,2]],
  J = [[2,1],[0,1]],
  H = J J^T = [[5,1],[1,1]].

They satisfy

  K J = 2 I,
  K H K^T = 4 I.                                                     (9)

Both K and H have rank one after reduction modulo two. Nevertheless their
composition carries an exact equality constraint, multiplied by four.

4.2 Constructing the network from every CNF

Start with any finite CNF F. Normalize variable names while preserving a
map to the original input. There is no satisfiability promise.

Represent every clause of length at most three by its Boolean satisfaction
table. For a longer clause, use a chain of deterministic two-input OR gates
and require its output to be true. A gate has three ports and its table is
one exactly when its output is the OR of its signed inputs. For each original
assignment there is a unique assignment to these gate-output signals. An
empty clause is a zero scalar. The empty conjunction is the empty product.

Whenever a logical signal has d occurrences, connect its ports through an
equality tree. For d=1 use the unary table (1,1); for d=2 use binary equality;
for d>=3 use a tree of trivalent equality vertices. Given the signal bit,
all internal equality-tree bits are uniquely determined. Explicitly declared
unused input bits contribute a factor two each; for the ordinary occurring-
variable convention there are no such factors.

This gives an ordinary tensor network with maximum degree three, Boolean
gate tables, and contraction S(F), the exact satisfying-assignment count.
The assertion is counting-preserving because all auxiliary values are
unique for an original assignment.

Replace each ordinary wire by the two-K, one-H gadget (9). If the original
network has m wires, the new graph has E=2m weak wires and

  Z_weak = 4^m S(F) = 2^E S(F).                                      (10)

All tensors and couplings have bounded arity and constant-size integer
entries. The weak wires have K as their coupling matrix; alternatively,
each K can be represented as a degree-two tensor between ordinary identity
wires. That conversion is also linear in the graph size and bipartite after
placing old vertices and H vertices on one side and K vertices on the other.

4.3 Full size and precision accounting

Let N be the complete binary input length and l the number of literal
occurrences. The OR construction uses at most 3l ports. Equality-tree
internal edges add at most that many again, so the original wire count is
at most 6l and E<=12l<=12N. The graph descriptions, port indices and variable
map use O(N log(N+1)) bits. All local tables have at most eight entries.
Straightforward normalization and construction take, conservatively,
O((N+1)^3) bit operations.

If F has n explicitly considered input variables, use

  p = E+n+1.                                                         (11)

For occurring variables n<=N, this is at most 13N+1 working precision bits.
Since 0<=S(F)<=2^n, the residue of Z_weak modulo 2^p, divided by 2^E, recovers
S(F) exactly. Recovery is an ordinary shift on O(N) bits. There is no
unaccounted precision, exponential advice or preprocessing in this reduction.

A uniform polynomial-bit exact contraction procedure for all these inputs
would therefore decide unrestricted SAT, and standard self-reduction would
recover an assignment. Such a contraction procedure is not constructed here.
Equation (10) is a reduction showing what the proposed global operation must
handle, not an oracle supplied to solve it.

4.4 Applying the actual global algorithm to the gadget

Let U=[[1,-1],[0,1]], so K=diag(1,2) U. The local transform (6) acts on H as

  U H U^T = diag(4,1).                                                (12)

After this transform, inconsistent endpoint bits are forbidden. If both are
zero, the transformed H supplies a factor four; if both are one, the two
wire weights supply that factor four. Thus (7) restores four times the
original equality constraint on every old wire.

The precision (11) is greater than E, while delta_i=1 on all E weak wires.
Consequently all 2^E boundary subsets satisfy the elementary budget test.
The local weighted transform is exact and polynomial, but it returns a
scaled copy of the original counting problem. This is the concrete reason
that the algorithm in Section 3 does not become a polynomial SAT solver.

More selective enumeration may remove many states, or a different algorithm
may exploit the resulting constraints. Nothing here gives a lower bound for
all such algorithms. Likewise, this reduction does not assert that the
particular pinned source's deterministic layout selects these exact gadgets
at one of its evaluation nodes. It tests a broader compression rule based
only on divisibility, local rank and polynomial precision.

5. DETERMINANTS: THE VALID CASE AND AN EXACT HIGHER-MODE FAILURE

5.1 What the accepted identity actually computes

Borchardt's identity for an ordinary Cauchy matrix C_ij=1/(a_i-b_j) says

  permanent(C) = det(C entrywise squared)/det(C),

when the denominators and determinant are nonzero. It is a primary-literature
identity, not a universal determinant formula for arbitrary matrices.

There is a useful confluent limit for repeated SIMPLE Cauchy columns. Let
beta_j>=0, sum_j beta_j=L, with distinct row nodes a_i, distinct active tile
nodes b_j and no coincidences a_i=b_j. For columns indexed by (j,r),
0<=r<beta_j, form

  D_{i,(j,r)} = (a_i-b_j)^(-r-1),
  N_{i,(j,r)} = (r+1)(a_i-b_j)^(-r-2).

Then

  [Z^beta] product_{i=1}^L sum_j Z_j/(a_i-b_j)
      = det(N)/(det(D) product_j beta_j!).                            (13)

To derive this, coalesce ordinary column nodes in Borchardt's identity.
The shared within-cluster Vandermonde factors cancel between numerator and
denominator. Their leading Taylor columns are exactly D and N. The limit
of the permanent has repeated simple columns, and each target monomial is
counted product_j beta_j! times by column permutations.

For explicitly specified rational nodes, exact determinants and factorials
in (13) have polynomial bit cost in L and their binary input length. Powers
have exponent at most L, their coordinate bit lengths are polynomial, and
fraction-free determinant arithmetic keeps intermediate lengths polynomial.
Modulo powers of two, the valuation lost in the denominator must still be
paid; the exact rational identity is not permission to divide a nonunit
at unchanged precision.

Formula (13) handles full-degree coefficients built only from zero-order
Cauchy modes, including repeated mode exponents. It does not say that the
permanent of the confluent matrix D itself is det(D squared)/det(D).
Higher-mode columns in the source have precisely that different role.

5.2 The attempted extension and its failure

The source's cross-group modes at a tile have coefficients proportional to

  (T-y0+x)^(-1) (2^{i+1}/(T-y0+x))^b.

Different mode orders b give different powers of the denominator. To test
whether the simple determinant quotient still computes their coefficient,
take an even integer a>=4, set L=2^a, and use the source grid

  S={-L,...,L-1}, C(T)=product_{q in S}(T-q).

Choose node t=1, local coordinate zeta=1, alpha=2L, so T=2L+1. Use tile
y0=0, valuation level i=0, row slots 0,2,4, and mode orders 0,1,2. Put

  g=C(T)/C'(0),
  M_{r,b}=g 2^b/(T+2r)^{b+1},  r,b in {0,1,2}.                       (14)

These are entries of the source's actual higher-mode coefficient formula.
The coefficient of Z_0 Z_1 Z_2 in the product of these three row factors is
permanent(M). Adding arbitrary constant terms to those row factors does
not change this full-degree coefficient.

The proposed quotient is well-defined over Q, but it is wrong:

  det(M entrywise squared)/det(M) - permanent(M)
      = 16 g^3 / [T^2 (T+2)^2 (T+4)^2].                             (15)

Proof: set z_r=1/(T+2r). Factoring the common row and column scales reduces
the matrix to V_{r,b}=z_r^{b+1}. Vandermonde evaluation gives

  det(V squared)/det(V) = (z_0 z_1 z_2)
                         (z_0+z_1)(z_0+z_2)(z_1+z_2).

On expanding, this exceeds permanent(V) by 2(z_0 z_1 z_2)^2. The product of
the scales in (14) is 8g^3, proving (15).

5.3 The discrepancy survives the required finite precision

The complete residue grid shows v_2(g)=a+1: after substituting T=1+2L,
one grid factor has valuation a+1, and the other factors retain the
derivative grid's valuations. The denominators in (15) are odd. Hence

  v_2(discrepancy)=3a+7.

At precision p=3a+8, the discrepancy is nonzero. In fact the correct
permanent is 2^{p-1} modulo 2^p, while the proposed quotient is zero.
For the retained a=4 instance,

  L=16, T=33, p=20,
  permanent(M) mod 2^20 = 524288,
  proposed quotient mod 2^20 = 0.

The a=6 and a=8 instances give an explicit continuing family. The test code
computes g both from the source product and from an independent binomial
expression, and checks equality. Thus the failure is not caused by replacing
the source's coefficient formula with an arbitrary matrix.

The source's node precision is p_t=p+8hD_t+8M(a+1). For an active call with
M>=1 this exceeds 3a+8. Therefore the source's prescribed precision does not
automatically discard the discrepancy. The three-row local formula is being
tested here; this does not assert that every deterministic layout contains
this chosen three-row configuration.

The assertion being falsified is this particular extension of Borchardt's
quotient to higher-mode matrices. Borchardt's theorem itself remains valid.
No conclusion excluding all determinant identities or all polynomial
algorithms follows. The local example also does not by itself prove that an
entire run of the source algorithm returns a wrong result; it falsifies the
proposed replacement operation before inserting it into that algorithm.
