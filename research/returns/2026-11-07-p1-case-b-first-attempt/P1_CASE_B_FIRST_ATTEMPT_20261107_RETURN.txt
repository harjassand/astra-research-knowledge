## 2. A uniform entropy bound for the actual generator

Write
\[
E_i(\rho)=\frac{I_i}{2}\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_i\rho.
\]
The noise convention remains exactly
\[
\mathcal L(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]+\gamma\sum_i(E_i(\rho)-\rho).
\]
Thus a Pauli string of weight \(r\) is damped by the noise generator at rate \(\gamma r\). Equivalently, the coefficient of each one-site Pauli dissipator is \(\gamma/4\).

Consider any finite \(m\)-qubit region, retaining all its on-site noise and any Hamiltonian terms internal to it. Let
\[
\omega_m=I/2^m.
\]
For **every initial state**, including correlated or pure states,
\[
\boxed{
D(\rho_t\Vert\omega_m)
\le e^{-\gamma t}D(\rho_0\Vert\omega_m)
\le m\log 2\,e^{-\gamma t}.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Here is a proof with a conservative decay rate; no optimal log-Sobolev constant is needed.

For a full-rank state, the Hamiltonian makes no contribution to the entropy derivative because \([\rho,\log\rho]=0\). Also,
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\bigl[(\rho-E_i\rho)\log(E_i\rho)\bigr]=0.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}D(\rho_t\Vert\omega_m)
&=-\gamma\sum_i
\left[D(\rho_t\Vert E_i\rho_t)+D(E_i\rho_t\Vert\rho_t)\right]\\
&\le-\gamma\sum_iD(\rho_t\Vert E_i\rho_t).
\end{aligned}
\]
But
\[
D(\rho\Vert E_i\rho)
=\log 2+S(\rho_{\bar i})-S(\rho),
\]
and strong subadditivity gives
\[
\sum_i S(\rho_{\bar i})\ge(m-1)S(\rho).
\]
The sum of the relative entropies therefore bounds \(D(\rho\Vert\omega_m)\) from below. Grönwall’s inequality proves (1); regularization covers initially singular states.

Quantum Pinsker then yields
\[
\boxed{
\|\rho_t-\omega_m\|_1
\le\sqrt{2m\log 2}\,e^{-\gamma t/2}.
}
\tag{2}
\]

This conclusion survives arbitrary allowed noncommuting Hamiltonian terms. It uses the actual unital noise and does not assume refreshed ancillas or an average over preparations.

The global bound alone would have a \(\sqrt n\) prefactor. Spatial locality lets us replace that dependence by the size of a region around the measured observable.

## 5. A sharper valid reduction using actual noisy evolution

The preceding algorithm uses a dense finite-region representation. I investigated a route that could avoid that cost.

For an \(m\)-qubit operator, define the normalized Schatten norm
\[
\|X\|_{p,\tau}=
\left(2^{-m}\operatorname{Tr}|X|^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]
King’s depolarizing hypercontractivity theorem gives contraction between suitable normalized Schatten norms. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.8412?utm_source=chatgpt.com) Interleaving that inequality with Hamiltonian conjugations—which preserve every Schatten norm—and taking the product-formula limit gives, for the actual continuous dynamics,
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi_s(X)\|_{q,\tau}\le\|X\|_{p,\tau}
\quad\text{if}\quad
q-1\le e^{2\gamma s}(p-1).
}
\tag{17}
\]

This does not require the Hamiltonian and noise to commute. To see the interleaving explicitly, in an \(N\)-step approximation use
\[
p_j-1=e^{2\gamma sj/N}(p-1).
\]
Each noise layer contracts from \(p_{j-1}\) to \(p_j\), while the intervening unitary is an isometry at \(p_j\). The finite-dimensional limit proves (17).

Taking \(p=2\) and comparing the Schatten \(q\)-norm with operator norm yields
\[
\boxed{
\|\Phi_s(X)\|_\infty
\le
2^{m/(1+e^{2\gamma s})}\|X\|_{2,\tau}.
}
\tag{18}
\]
Thus, for
\[
s=\frac{\lceil\log_2 m\rceil}{2\gamma},
\]
the prefactor is at most two.

Now suppose \(T\ge s\), and construct a sparse approximation
\[
\widetilde B=\sum_{j=1}^{K}b_jP_j,
\qquad
\|\Phi_{T-s}(O)-\widetilde B\|_{2,\tau}\le\eta.
\]
The observable-level approximation from the frozen Angrisani–Puig–Teng–Holmes construction is relevant here. Its local damping parameters must be mapped correctly: allocating the on-site noise among at most \(d\) incident Hamiltonian blocks gives a minimum block rate \(\gamma/d\), not automatically \(\gamma\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40174v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Equation (18) then proves, for **every specified input state**,
\[
\boxed{

\right|
\le2\eta.
}
\tag{19}
\]

This is a genuine worst-case norm bridge. It uses a portion of the evolution already present in the query; it cannot append extra noise or extra time for free.

Those Pauli strings can have support growing with requested accuracy and can be spread across the retained region. The state is still specified, and the buffer time grows with the region size.

## 6. Why the new circuit theorem does not directly finish the construction

For the October circuit result, the stated two-dimensional exponent depends as \(O(p^{-3})\), where \(p\) is the depolarizing probability per circuit layer. Its sufficient depth scales as \(O(p^{-1}\log p^{-1})\) at small \(p\). [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.00548v2?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

A product-formula interval of duration \(h\) in B has
\[
p_h=1-e^{-\gamma h}\sim\gamma h.
\]
Refining the discretization therefore sends \(p_h\) to zero. Direct substitution into that circuit bound gives an exponent
\[
O((\gamma h)^{-3}),
\]
which is not a fixed exponent when \(h\) must shrink with accuracy.

Likewise, up to fixed coloring factors, the sufficient circuit depth corresponds to a physical-time threshold of order
\[
\gamma^{-1}\log\frac1{\gamma h}.
\]
A fixed positive continuous ratio \(\gamma/J\) does not make the per-layer circuit probability fixed.

There is another issue for the smoothing route: discrete circuits have exact finite-depth lightcones, whereas continuous local evolution has small tails outside a physical lightcone. At requested error \(e^{-L}\), retaining only a radius \(O(\log L)\) because the buffer time is \(O(\log L)\) is not justified by the ordinary locality bound. The required precision must also enter the radius or the contraction method.

These observations invalidate the **direct transfer argument**. They do not prove that a uniform continuous-time adaptation, a method exploiting near-identity gates, or a different representation is impossible.
