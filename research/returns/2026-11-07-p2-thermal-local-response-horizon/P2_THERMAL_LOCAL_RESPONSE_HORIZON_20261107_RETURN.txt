## 2. The central operation: realizing a thermal spectral law locally

Let \(J\) be a real symmetric Jacobi matrix with diagonal \(d_i\), positive neighboring entries \(b_i\), and spectrum in \([\Omega/2,\Omega]\). Construct

\[
H=
\sum_{i=1}^{N}d_i\frac{I-Z_i}{2}
+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}b_i
\left(X_iX_{i+1}+Y_iY_{i+1}\right).
\]

This is an open chain with onsite terms and nearest-neighbor two-body interactions. Its observable is \(A=X_1\).

The classical free-fermion representation turns \(H\) into the second quantization of \(J\). If \(\nu\) is the endpoint spectral measure of \(J\), the actual normalized Gibbs correlator is

\[

=
\frac{\operatorname{Tr}
\left(e^{-(\beta-z)H}A e^{-zH}A\right)}
{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}}
=
\int
\frac{e^{-z\omega}+e^{-(\beta-z)\omega}}
{1+e^{-\beta\omega}}
\,d\nu(\omega).
\]

The requested real-time response is

\[
R_\nu(t)
=
-\operatorname{Im}C_\nu(it)
=
\int
\tanh(\beta\omega/2)\sin(t\omega)\,d\nu(\omega).
\]

This equals \(\frac{i}{2}\langle[A(t),A]\rangle_\beta\), fixing the sign and normalization.

The useful operation starts with any probability measure \(\mu\) on the frequency band and defines

\[
W_\mu=1+\int e^{-\beta\omega}\,d\mu(\omega),
\qquad
d\nu(\omega)
=
\frac{1+e^{-\beta\omega}}{W_\mu}\,d\mu(\omega).
\]

Substitution gives exactly

\[
C_\nu(\tau)
=
\frac{C_\mu(\tau)+C_\mu(\beta-\tau)}
{1+C_\mu(\beta)},
\]

and

\[
R_\nu(t)
=
\frac{\int(1-e^{-\beta\omega})\sin(t\omega)\,d\mu(\omega)}

\]

For finitely many positive weights, orthogonalizing the monomials constructs a Jacobi matrix with endpoint measure \(\nu\). Consequently, the normalized thermal law is realized by the local spin chain above.

The thermal weighting is substantive. Constructing the Jacobi matrix directly from \(\mu\) generally produces a different Gibbs correlator. The archive retains an explicit two-frequency counterexample to that shortcut.
