ATTAINABLE CERTIFIED INFERENCE FOR NONLINEAR NETWORKS
Complete operation, proofs, rational evaluation and full resource contract
6 November 2026

1. RESOLVED OPERATION

Input: a finite cooperative network, finitely many interval observations
of each unknown local law, supplied global secant-slope bounds, known
constant forcing, and a box of possible initial states. Output: arbitrarily
sharp certified coordinate and nonnegative-linear-task ranges at any
specified finite time, over ALL laws consistent with the observations.
The output includes two actual stationary source models attaining the
true endpoints, and rational numerical certificates for evaluating them.

If a rationally decidable comparison-matrix condition holds, the operation
also returns arbitrarily sharp equilibrium ranges, a unique-equilibrium
proof for every compatible law, and certified all-future task bounds.
The condition and its contraction rate are computed from input matrices
and slope bounds, rather than supplied by an unconstructed stability
oracle. Failure of this test is not a general instability conclusion.

This operation is implemented in network.py and infer.py. The package
contains executable inputs, outputs and exact replay checks. Its results
are consequences of the supplied mathematical model. No physical data
were acquired, and no claim of novelty, laboratory validation, priority
or civilisation-scale advantage is made.

2. INPUT AND INFORMATION CONTRACT

Let n>=1 and let A be a rational n by n matrix with A_ij>=0 when i!=j.
Directed cycles and bidirectional loops are allowed. The equations are

    x_i' = sum_j A_ij x_j - g_i(x_i) + u_i,              (N)

where u is a known rational vector. The unknown laws are stationary.
For each i, give rational m_i<=L_i and nonempty finite data
(xi_ij,ell_ij,u_ij), with ell_ij<=u_ij. The allowed laws are exactly the
globally defined continuous functions satisfying

    m_i <= [g_i(y)-g_i(x)]/(y-x) <= L_i  for all x<y,
    ell_ij <= g_i(xi_ij) <= u_ij.                         (S)

Each family is independent of the others: their joint uncertainty is the
Cartesian product, not an unstated correlated or common-law constraint.
Each local potential integral_0^x g_i(s) ds is C^(1,1); the extremal laws
below need not be analytic or differentiable at every point.

Supply an initial box [a,b], a rational horizon T>=0 and tolerance eta>0.
The implemented task is every coordinate, plus a supplied rational
nonnegative linear readout w.x (default: the arithmetic mean). eta is
an endpoint-sharpness tolerance for both the coordinates and that readout.
Other monotone tasks follow as stated below, with their evaluation costs.

The declared global bounds, direct constitutive observations, matrix,
forcing and initial box are scientific premises. Finite observations do
not establish the global slope bounds. Boundary-only network observations
are not silently converted to direct local-law samples. Unknown couplings,
leaks, common-law correlations, or changing laws require a different
explicit information contract. Physical acquisition and qualification
costs are not hidden in the inference algorithm.

The basic finite-time operation covers every well-formed input satisfying
this model contract and reports inconsistent observations exactly. No
stability condition is needed for finite T. The equilibrium operation has
the additional comparison-matrix condition in section 6, checked by code.

3. ALL COMPATIBLE LAWS HAVE TWO ATTAINED ENVELOPES

For one law, omit i and put D=L-m. Define

 g_lower(x) = m x + max_j[ell_j-m xi_j-D(xi_j-x)_+],
 g_upper(x) = m x + min_j[u_j-m xi_j+D(x-xi_j)_+].         (E)

These are finite piecewise-affine functions, directly evaluable by rational
arithmetic. The formulas are the compatible-law envelope construction
used in accepted Astra N1039, with its source conditions retained. They
are elementary closed-class optimal recovery, not a new instrument or
a claim of a new interpolation theorem.

Feasibility is equivalent to g_lower(xi_j)<=u_j for every j. To see this,
subtract m x from every law. The resulting function must be nondecreasing
and D-Lipschitz. Each lower cone in (E) is nondecreasing and D-Lipschitz;
so is their maximum. At xi_j it is at least ell_j-m xi_j. If the indicated
upper checks pass, g_lower itself is a legal law. Conversely, every legal
law must lie above every lower cone and below every upper cone, giving

    g_lower(x) <= g(x) <= g_upper(x) for all real x.      (E2)

The minimum of the upper cones is also nondecreasing and D-Lipschitz
after removing m x. It is below each upper datum at its own site, and
above g_lower, so it also satisfies every lower datum. Thus BOTH envelopes
are actual legal laws. They attain the pointwise infimum/supremum at ALL
points simultaneously. For m=L, these are simply two parallel affine
functions, and the same feasibility proof applies.

This endpoint attainment is for the declared closed secant class. It is
not transferred to an analytic class with exact-data constraints. N1039's
positive-slack analytic approximation qualifications remain in force.

Construction and feasibility need O(sum_i k_i^2) rational operations for
k_i observations per law in this reference implementation. Evaluating
all envelopes at a state needs O(sum_i k_i) operations. Data are not
sampled afresh at each prediction or time step.

4. TWO STATIONARY MODELS ATTAIN EVERY ORDERED DYNAMIC ENDPOINT

Define x_minus as the solution of (N) using every g_i_upper and initial
state a. Define x_plus using every g_i_lower and initial state b. Then

    x_minus(t) <= x(t) <= x_plus(t) componentwise         (O)

for EVERY legal model and EVERY initial state in [a,b], for all t>=0.
Both bounds are attained by the specified two stationary models.

Proof: compare x with x_minus. Writing z=x-x_minus, each same-law divided
difference yields a measurable d_i(t) in [m_i,L_i]. Then

 z' = [A-diag d_i(t)]z + g_upper(x_minus)-g(x_minus).

The forcing term is componentwise nonnegative. The matrix is Metzler
(nonnegative off-diagonal), so its time-varying linear flow is positive.
Variation of constants and z(0)>=0 give z(t)>=0. A proof of this positivity
uses any scalar shift making the matrix entrywise nonnegative on a finite
time interval and the nonnegative Picard series; bounded slopes ensure
the needed uniform shift. Repeat for the upper solution. Global Lipschitz
bounds give unique complete finite-time solutions, so no crossing or
existence assumption is omitted. This is the standard cooperative-system
comparison mechanism, compatible with the classical results of
Angeli--Sontag; the divided-difference proof also covers the envelope kinks.

The SAME lower/upper model works for every component and every time.
There is no per-step switching between incompatible laws. Consequently
the extrema of any coordinatewise nondecreasing functional of finitely
many state observations occur at these two histories. A nonnegative sum,
average, maximum, or a polynomial certified to be nondecreasing on the
relevant state box is included. A numerical task evaluator must have a
supplied or computed continuity modulus, whose cost is paid. The CLI
implements nonnegative linear readouts without that extra premise.

Mixed-sign tasks need not attain their extrema at these two models.
Neither does a correlated law family necessarily contain both envelope
tuples. Those statements are not claimed. The general finite potential
construction can address compatible explicitly represented correlations
and arbitrary polynomial tasks, at its much larger stated cost.

5. A RATIONAL NUMERICAL CERTIFICATE FOR FINITE TIME

For each of the two explicit models set

 Lambda=max_i(sum_j |A_ij|+max(|m_i|,|L_i|)),
 gamma=max_i(sum_j A_ij-m_i).                            (C)

Lambda is a global sup-norm Lipschitz bound. gamma is a logarithmic-norm
upper bound: for two states, the divided-difference matrix is
A-diag d_i, whose infinity matrix measure is at most gamma. In particular,
for a continuous approximate path y with residual r=y'-F(y),

    D^+ |x-y|_infinity <= gamma |x-y|_infinity+|r|_infinity.

This is the classical logarithmic-norm error inequality, not a new
stability theorem. It is derived here directly from the largest absolute
coordinate and the row-sum bound, so the certificate does not rely on an
unverified numerical eigenvalue.

On a segment of length h, start at a rational stored state y and use the
linear path y+s F(y). Its residual has norm <=Lambda |F(y)| s. Suppose
the next stored state y_new differs from y+hF(y) by a jump J. If gamma h<1,
put q=1/(1-gamma h) and kappa=max(1,q). Since exp(gamma h)<=q, a sufficient
new error radius is

    R_new >= q R_old + kappa Lambda |F(y)| h^2/2 + J.     (R)

All quantities in (R) are rational. A checker need not trust a floating
solver or the producer's rounding scheme. It recalculates F(y), the jump,
and this inequality for every segment. It checks the initial radius,
time mesh, state dimensions and final reported bound. The supplied
checker uses explicit exceptions, so its checks remain active under
python -O. Certificate validity still depends on ordinary execution of
the checker and on the mathematical proof; it is not a Lean proof object.

The producer uses N equal steps h=T/N, with N>=2 max(gamma,0)T. Set

    Bgrowth=2^ceil(4 max(gamma,0)T),
    p=max(24,ceil_log2(32(N+1)Bgrowth/tol)).              (P)

Here tol is the desired radius, capped at 1 if larger. Store states by
rounding toward zero to multiples of 2^-p, and round every radius upward
to that grid. Exact Fraction arithmetic computes the expressions between
resets. No growing exact Euler denominator is carried through the entire
trajectory. A stored trace is part of the output and its size is charged.

The code starts at a dyadic N>=max(8,2 max(gamma,0)T), checks the final
radius, and doubles N until the requested tolerance is met. Failed runs
are included in its recorded operation counts.

Termination and cost are explicit. Let D0=|F(0)|, R0=|x0| and

    Rbar=(R0+D0 T+1) 2^ceil(2 Lambda T),
    Mbar=Lambda Rbar+D0.

The sum of rounding perturbations in any run is at most N 2^-p<=1.
Discrete Gronwall gives |y_j|<=Rbar and |F(y_j)|<=Mbar. For gamma>0 and
gamma h<=1/2, q^N<=exp(2gamma T)<=Bgrowth and kappa<=2. For gamma<=0,
q<=1 and kappa=1. Summing (R), with the upward rounding costs, yields

    R_N <= Bgrowth[(2N+1)2^-p+Lambda Mbar T^2/N].         (R2)

Therefore any dyadic N at least

    max(8,2 max(gamma,0)T,8 Bgrowth Lambda Mbar T^2/tol)

suffices. The doubling process terminates, with total steps in failed and
successful runs less than twice the final N. Zero horizon and constant
vector fields cause no singular case; their local defect term vanishes.

Let E be the number of stored matrix entries and Kobs=sum k_i. Each step
uses O(E+Kobs+n) rational operations; envelope construction adds the
quadratic data-feasibility cost above. Stored traces use O(nN) rationals.
If Binput is the complete input bit length, a conservative bound for the
operand bits in a step is polynomial in Binput plus

    p+log(N+1)+log(1+Rbar)+log(1+T).

With schoolbook integer arithmetic and Euclidean gcd, polynomial bit
arithmetic per such operation suffices. This bounds full precision cost,
not just the number of real operations. In particular N may scale as
1/tol and exponentially with numeric time/conditioning parameters. This
first-order transient method is NOT polynomial in accuracy bits.

The input eta is an endpoint-sharpness tolerance. Since a numerical
interval [y-R,y+R] can be 2R from the exact endpoint on one side, infer.py
uses tol=eta/[2 max(max_i v_i,sum_i w_i v_i)], where v_i are any positive
coordinate scaling weights (all 1 for its transient mode). Thus every
reported coordinate and the supplied nonnegative readout has an outer
bound at most eta beyond its true endpoint. The factor two is charged.

6. CONSTRUCTED CONTRACTION, EQUILIBRIA AND ALL-FUTURE INFERENCE

First try gamma<0 in (C), with alpha=-gamma. If it fails, form

    M=A-diag(m_i)

and solve (-M)v=1 exactly over the rationals. If the system is nonsingular
and v>0 componentwise, change coordinates x_i=v_i y_i. Then

 Atilde_ij=A_ij v_j/v_i, gtilde_i(y)=g_i(v_i y)/v_i,
 utilde_i=u_i/v_i.

The slope intervals are unchanged; each sample becomes
(xi_ij/v_i,ell_ij/v_i,u_ij/v_i). This is exactly another input of the same
type, not an approximate model. Its row logarithmic-norm bound is

    gamma_tilde=max_i (Mv)_i/v_i=max_i(-1/v_i)<0.

The positive rational vector is a directly checkable certificate. For a
Metzler M, this test succeeds iff M is Hurwitz: a positive v with Mv<0
implies contraction in the weighted infinity norm; conversely, if M is
Hurwitz, exp(Mt)>=0 and v=integral_0^infinity exp(Mt)1 dt=(-M)^-1 1>0.
The integral is used in the equivalence proof, not in the algorithm.
The algorithm is rational Gaussian elimination plus sign checks, with
ordinary polynomial matrix-arithmetic bit cost. This is the established
positive-system/M-matrix criterion, not a new spectral theorem.

This is a complete test of that comparison matrix condition. It is NOT
a complete test of all possible stability mechanisms for the uncertain
nonlinear family. When it fails, the CLI reports the missing condition
for equilibrium mode; the finite-time operation remains available.

In contracting coordinates, set alpha=-gamma>0 and

    h=1/[1+max_i(L_i-A_ii)_+], q=1-alpha h.

For two points, the difference of G(x)=x+hF(x) is a nonnegative matrix
times their difference. Its row sums are at most q, with 0<=q<1. Thus G
is a global contraction of all R^n. Banach's theorem supplies a unique
fixed point x_star, which is precisely a unique equilibrium of F. It
also gives the exact residual certificate

    |x-x_star|_infinity <= |F(x)|_infinity/alpha.         (Q)

The code iterates G with dyadic rounding and stops only when (Q) is
within the requested radius tol. This is a complete evaluator, not just
a verifier waiting for an unspecified approximate equilibrium.

For precision and termination, let Lambda be (C), d0=|F(0)| and choose

 p=max(24,ceil_log2[32 max(1,Lambda)/(alpha^2 h tol)]),
 J=max(1,ceil_log2[16(1+Lambda)(1+d0)/(alpha^2 tol)]),
 Klimit=ceil[J/(alpha h)]+4.                             (Q2)

Starting at zero, after k rounded iterations the state error is at most

    q^k d0/alpha + 2^-p/(alpha h).

Multiplication by Lambda/alpha bounds the residual radius. At Klimit,
the second term contributes <=tol/32; q^k<=exp(-alpha h k), together
with the definition of J (including the case the logarithm argument is
below one), makes the first contribution <=tol/16. Thus the code's
finite iteration budget suffices for every input in this contract.

For fixed conditioning and input magnitudes, this is polynomial in the
requested accuracy bits. The numerical condition 1/(alpha h), coordinate
scaling ratios, d0, all input bits and exact arithmetic are explicitly
paid; it is not uniformly polynomial in binary input length if alpha
is exponentially small. Each iteration has the same O(E+Kobs+n) local
arithmetic cost as a transient step. A final equilibrium certificate
needs only O(n) state/residual entries, rather than the iteration trace.

Run this operation for the upper-force and lower-force envelope models.
Comparison (O) with any common initial state and passage to the limit
shows that their two equilibria attain the componentwise extrema over
all compatible stationary laws. All coordinates and nonnegative linear
tasks are attained simultaneously. Thus arbitrarily accurate equilibrium
inference is completed by two explicit computations.

Every source also satisfies

    |x(t)-x_star|_v <= exp(-alpha t)|x(0)-x_star|_v,
    |x|_v=max_i |x_i|/v_i.                              (TAIL)

Using certified outer equilibrium bounds [l_i,u_i] and the initial box,
compute

    Rtail=max_i max(|a_i-u_i|,|b_i-l_i|)/v_i.

For all t>=t0 every legal solution then lies between
l_i-v_i Rtail exp(-alpha t0) and u_i+v_i Rtail exp(-alpha t0).
The rational bound exp(-s)<=2^-floor(s) for s>=0 makes this an immediately
checkable all-future task certificate. This consequence does not require
the general infinite-time predictor ruled out by earlier mechanical
examples: contraction has actually been constructed for this class.

Known piecewise-constant forcings can be treated by using the same radius
recurrence on a mesh containing the switches, carrying the radius across
each switch. Order comparison and endpoint attainment still hold. The
present CLI takes constant forcing; the general mathematical method in
GENERAL_THEOREM.txt includes arbitrary supplied finite switch schedules.

7. EXECUTED SCIENTIFIC EXAMPLES

All inputs below are synthetic mathematical model/data contracts. All
display decimals are illustrative; exact rational endpoints and full
numerical certificates are in the JSON files.

Scalar family. Let x'=-g(x), x(0)=1, g(0)=0,g(1)=2, slopes in [1,3].
On [0,1], g_upper=min(3x,x+1) and g_lower=max(x,3x-1). Solving their two
piecewise-linear equations gives the exact sharp x(1) interval

    [(32/27)exp(-3), 2^(-1/3)exp(-1)].

The lower state reaches 1/2 at log(4/3) and then decays at rate 3; the
upper state reaches 1/2 at log(4)/3 and then decays at rate 1. This derives
the formulas, rather than inferring sharpness from the numerical test.
The exact rational integration outputs the outer interval

    [3928299/67108864,39208919/134217728]
    approximately [0.0585362166,0.2921292111].

Each endpoint model used 2048 final steps, with radii respectively
97881/268435456 and 75827/268435456. The law ambiguity is far larger than
the numerical uncertainty; increasing arithmetic precision cannot erase it.

Coupled periodic grids. For side length s=4 or 8, use n=s^2 nodes with
periodic nearest-neighbor coupling. Each diagonal A_ii=-1/4 and each of
the four neighbors has coefficient 1/16. Supply slopes [1/2,3/2] at every
node. Let r_i=3/4+(i mod 4)/16. The three data intervals are

    g_i(0)=0,
    g_i(1/2) in [r_i/2-1/64,r_i/2+1/64],
    g_i(1) in [r_i-1/32,r_i+1/32].

Set u_i=1/4+(i mod 3)/32. These data admit the linear law g_i(x)=r_i x
but also infinitely many nonlinear laws. Coupling has actual graph loops;
no known-tree flow formula is used. The computed constants are

    Lambda=2, gamma=-1/2, alpha=1/2.

For n=16, initial box endpoints are
3/4+(i mod 4)/32 +/- 1/128. At T=1 the certified outer mean interval is

    [129554107/268435456,39308767/67108864]
    approximately [0.4826266579,0.5857462734].

The lower endpoint took 256 final steps, 504 including failed refinements;

All-future consequence. Give the same initial-box formula to the 64-node
system. The certified maximum distance from an initial state to its own

    Rtail=1562237019334665965/2305843009213693952
         approximately 0.6775123081.

For EVERY compatible law tuple, every allowed initial state, and every

    mean x(t) <= 16922514148419692345/36893488147419103232

Weighted test. With A=[[-2,10],[0,-2]] and slopes [1,3], the unweighted

Controls. Replacing a last-step radius by zero is rejected. Removing a

not based on a successful floating-point solve.

8. WHAT THE CONSTRUCTION ENABLES AND WHAT IT COSTS

The delivered operation takes partial local-law knowledge to sharp
network-level forecasts, equilibria and threshold conclusions, without
choosing a uniquely identified constitutive law. Its two source models
also witness how much prediction uncertainty remains. The same retained
calibration information supports further tasks without rerunning an
experiment. Data acquisition, noise coverage and stationarity remain
explicit premises.

The mathematics directly covers finite thermal models with known
diffusion and uncertain local heat-loss laws; finite resistor/capacitor
models with known linear coupling and uncertain shunt characteristics;
and cooperative nonlinear feedback models of the displayed form. These
are mappings to explicitly supplied finite equations, not assertions
that an arbitrary continuum model, real device or application has been
validated. Discretization error, uncertain couplings and physical noise
must be supplied as additional information if those claims are desired.

The operation is built from established optimal-recovery envelopes,
cooperative comparison, positive-system contraction and validated
residual computation. It supplies a complete constructive consequence
at a broad stated model interface. It does not establish the larger
civilisation-changing ambition merely by naming that ambition.
