CERTIFIED CONSEQUENCES OF AN UNKNOWN POTENTIAL
Complete finite-horizon inference and robust-design construction
6 November 2026

STATUS AND QUESTION

The resolved question in this document is the following.

Given finitely described calibration information about one stationary
potential, supplied global lower/upper curvature bounds, a finite time
horizon, and a specified dynamical experiment, can one compute arbitrarily
sharp bounds on a scientific task over EVERY compatible potential, and
construct actual compatible models approaching both endpoints?

Yes, under the explicit contract below. The proof gives a terminating
ordinary deterministic algorithm. It also gives approximately optimal
robust interventions from a compact polynomially described intervention
family. General computation is expensive. It is not polynomial-time SAT,
unrestricted scientific inference, an algorithm for all infinite-time
limits, or a proof of practical/civilisation-scale advantage.

The smooth interpolation theorem and its conjugate construction are
accepted results of Taylor, Hendrickx and Glineur (THG). Exact real-algebraic
computation is an accepted algorithmic ingredient, with its costs paid.
The contribution here is the specified composition, uniform approximation
argument, witness extraction, resource accounting and reference software.
No historical priority or independent/formal verification is claimed.

Implementation boundary: algebraic_problem.py emits and checks finite
quadratic problems. interpolation.py evaluates rational interpolants. A
general CAD optimizer was NOT implemented or run in this package. The
cooperative-network operation in COOPERATIVE_INFERENCE.txt IS implemented
end to end, including exact numerical certificate replay.

1. COMPLETE INPUT CONTRACT

All rational inputs use finite binary numerator/denominator encodings.
All dimensions, polynomial terms/exponents, matrices, constraints, times,
and output tolerances are part of the charged input.

Let n,d >= 1. Supply rational matrices A (n by n), B (n by d), C (d by n).
The system is

    z'(t) = A z(t) + B grad V(C z(t)) + u(t).

Here u is a specified rational piecewise-constant function on [0,T], T is
a nonnegative rational, and all break times are supplied. Initial states
belong to a closed bounded semialgebraic set Z, given by a finite formula
using AND/OR of weak polynomial inequalities/equalities, together with an
explicit rational containing box. Empty Z is allowed and is detected.

Supply rational mu <= L and G >= 0. The unknown V is one function on ALL
of R^d, with V(0)=0, |grad V(0)|_2 <= G. For mu < L define beta=L-mu and

    h(x) = V(x) - mu |x|^2/2.

Require h to be convex, continuously differentiable, with beta-Lipschitz
gradient. Equivalently, V is C^(1,1) with curvature between mu and L in
the usual almost-everywhere/convex-shift sense. Negative mu is permitted.
For mu=L, the class is V(x)=mu|x|^2/2+a.x with |a|<=G.

Supply finitely many rational calibration locations. Their values and
gradients obey a finite closed semialgebraic constraint D. This permits
interval errors, exact observations, and specified correlations among
observations. D may be infeasible. No unlisted observation is available.
The potential and initial-state uncertainty are independent unless their
joint closed constraints are explicitly included in the finite problem;
the robust-design result below uses a design-independent product family.

Supply finitely many rational observation times in [0,T], and a rational
polynomial H in any of the corresponding z(t), V(Cz(t)), grad V(Cz(t)).
The task is the scalar H. A vector of finitely many tasks is handled by
repeating this operation and paying the repetitions. Supply epsilon>0.

The global curvature and anchor bounds are substantive information. They
are not certified for an unknown physical system by finitely many local
measurements. No statistical noise model, measurement acquisition,
hardware actuation, or model validity is obtained at zero cost.

2. OUTPUT CONTRACT

Either report that the compatible model/initial-state set is empty, or
return rational numbers ell,u and two finitely described compatible
potentials V_minus,V_plus and compatible initial states such that

    ell <= H(V,z0) <= u                 for every compatible pair,
    H(V_minus,z0_minus) <= ell+epsilon,
    H(V_plus,z0_plus) >= u-epsilon.

Thus both numerical endpoints are epsilon-sharp. The entire interval
need not be narrow: a large interval can be the exact consequence of
insufficient model information. The witnesses are actual stationary
C^(1,1) potentials, not sequences of unrelated per-step forces.

Witness coefficients and initial states may be real algebraic numbers,
returned as exact polynomial/root-isolation encodings. Rational input
does not imply rational optimal witnesses. They have complete finite
evaluation algorithms; neither a real-number oracle nor infinite advice
is supplied. The rational implementation is an explicitly smaller case.

3. EXACT FINITE INTERPOLATION

For any finite triples (x_i,g_i,f_i), the required potential exists iff
every ordered pair satisfies

 f_i-f_j-g_j.(x_i-x_j)-mu|x_i-x_j|^2/2
       >= |g_i-g_j-mu(x_i-x_j)|^2/(2 beta).                 (I)

This is THG's smooth convex interpolation theorem applied to h. The
extension to negative mu in this formulation is simply the stated
convex shift, not an assertion that THG assumed negative strong convexity.

For completeness, the imported constructive witness is explicit. Put

 s_i=g_i-mu x_i,  h_i=f_i-mu|x_i|^2/2,
 a_i=x_i-s_i/beta, b_i=|s_i|^2/(2 beta)-h_i,
 h_star(s)=|s|^2/(2 beta)+max_i(a_i.s+b_i),
 V(x)=sup_s[x.s-h_star(s)]+mu|x|^2/2.                     (W)

Inequality (I), with its indices reversed, makes the i-th quadratic attain
the maximum at s_i. Its gradient there is x_i, so x_i belongs to the
subdifferential of h_star at s_i. Strong convexity of h_star makes its
conjugate differentiable with beta-Lipschitz gradient. Fenchel equality
gives exactly V(x_i)=f_i and grad V(x_i)=g_i. The maximum of finitely many
quadratics is finite and strongly convex, so (W) defines V globally.

At a query x the unique optimizer s satisfies

    x-s/beta in conv{a_i : a_i.s+b_i is maximal}.

By finite-dimensional convex geometry, an affinely independent active
subset of size <=d+1 suffices. For each candidate subset, solve

    beta (a_i.a_j) lambda_j + nu = beta a_i.x+b_i,
    sum lambda_j=1.

Reject singular systems, negative weights, or positive-weight indices
whose affine height is not globally maximal. A valid subset must occur.
Then s=beta(x-sum lambda_i a_i), and (W) gives value and gradient.
This is an actual finite evaluation algorithm. For rational data/query,
all of these calculations are rational. For algebraic data they are
ordinary algebraic-number arithmetic and comparisons. At arbitrary
computable query points, use rational approximations and the explicit
Lipschitz bounds below; the requested precision and input-access cost are
charged.

Face enumeration tries at most sum_{k=1}^{min(M,d+1)} binomial(M,k)
supports for M data points. Each support uses a system of order k+1,
finite vector products and M height comparisons. This cost is not hidden
behind a QP oracle. Algebraic witness degrees and isolating-bit costs are
part of the real-algebraic computation cost.

For fixed rational x_i,g_i with unknown unconstrained values, (I) is a
system f_i>=f_j+w_ij. Bellman-Ford with reversed sign either supplies
values or a positive directed cycle. This is the cyclic compatibility
observation already stated in THG Remark 3, not a new interpolation
theorem. O(M^3) relaxations after O(M^2 d) arithmetic build the graph.

4. A UNIFORM DYNAMICAL AND NUMERICAL BOUND

Put K=max(|mu|,|L|). Every V in the class has K-Lipschitz gradient.
One direct verification uses the beta-cocoercivity of grad h:
grad h(x)-grad h(y) lies in the ball with center beta(x-y)/2 and
radius beta|x-y|/2. Adding mu(x-y) bounds the resulting norm by
max(|mu|,|L|)|x-y|. Therefore

    |grad V(x)| <= G+K|x|,
    |V(x)| <= G|x|+K|x|^2/2.                             (B1)

Choose rational upper bounds a,b,c on the Euclidean operator norms of
A,B,C (the sums of absolute matrix entries suffice), R0 on |z0|, and U
on |u(t)|. These are computed from the input, including its containing
boxes. Define the following rational/integer quantities:

    Lambda = a+b K c,       D0 = b G+U,
    E = 2^ceil(2 Lambda T), R = (R0+D0 T) E,
    M = Lambda R+D0,        C_E = Lambda M T E/2.          (B2)

Because 2^(2x)>=exp(x) for x>=0, E>=exp(Lambda T).
Gronwall gives |z(t)|<=R and |z'(t)|<=M. The vector field is globally
Lipschitz, so every initial condition has a unique complete finite-time
solution. No bounded-trajectory promise or ODE-solving oracle is needed.

Take an Euler mesh including every forcing switch and observation time,
with maximum step hmax. Its states obey the same bound R: apply the
discrete inequality |z_{k+1}|<=(1+Lambda h_k)|z_k|+D0 h_k and
product(1+Lambda h_k)<=exp(Lambda T). The one-step truncation error is
at most Lambda M h_k^2/2. Propagation gives, at EVERY mesh node,

    |z(t_k)-z_k| <= C_E hmax.                            (B3)

Time switches cause no extra truncation term because the mesh contains
them. If T=0 or C_E=0, use the direct zero-error construction.

For task coordinates, the same state error e changes grad V(Cz) by at
most K c e and V(Cz) by at most (G+K c R)c e. Let

    C_obs=max(1,K c,(G+K c R)c).

Choose rational W>=1 bounding every scalar argument of H, using (B1),
R and the observation definition. If H(y)=sum_alpha c_alpha y^alpha,

    L_H=sum_alpha |c_alpha| |alpha| W^max(|alpha|-1,0)

is a Lipschitz constant for H in the sup norm on that box. This sum
already counts multiple observations and coordinates. Consequently

    |H_true-H_Euler| <= C_task hmax,
    C_task=L_H C_obs C_E.                                (B4)

All constants are explicit computable rational expressions. Their large
numeric sizes, integer powers, and resulting bit lengths are charged.

5. THE FINITE ALGEBRAIC PROBLEM

If C_task>0 choose hmax<=epsilon/(4 C_task). Partition each interval
between listed switches/observations into ceil(length/hmax) equal steps.
If C_task=0 any such finite mesh suffices. Let N be its step count.

Introduce variables z_k and a value/gradient pair (f_k,g_k) at C z_k,
including the final node. Include calibration pairs and the anchor
(0,grad V(0),0). Impose:

  (a) the initial-set and calibration constraints;
  (b) |grad V(0)|^2<=G^2;
  (c) every Euler equation z_{k+1}=z_k+h_k(Az_k+B g_k+u_k);
  (d) inequality (I) for EVERY ordered pair of all these points.

The variables remain in the actual physical coordinate dimension d.
Replacing them by an unrestricted Gram matrix would require additional
rank reasoning and is not part of this algorithm. All constraints (b)--(d)
have degree at most two. Only input calibration/initial constraints and
the task may have higher degree.

Add explicit redundant bounds to make compactness transparent. If X is
a rational upper bound on max(cR, norms of calibration locations,0), add
|z_k|<=R, |g_i|<=G+KX, and |f_i|<=G X+KX^2/2. Squared-norm bounds and
coordinate boxes are polynomial. They remove no legal table by (B1)--(B3).

Every genuine model supplies a feasible Euler table. Conversely, every
feasible table extends by (W) to ONE genuine model, and uniqueness of
Euler updates makes the recorded states exactly that model's Euler
trajectory. Thus feasibility is exact, and the feasible tables form a
closed bounded semialgebraic set. Model inconsistency is detected exactly.
An empty initial set is reported as no admissible experiment, not as a
statement about physical law impossibility.

Use a complete real-algebraic algorithm, for example sign-invariant
cylindrical algebraic decomposition with algebraic sample points, to
find the exact minimum a_N and maximum b_N of H on this compact set,
and exact algebraic optimizing tables. Standard quantifier elimination
describes the attainable objective values; extrema are its boundary
points and the optimizer fibers supply the tables. This includes the
cost of detecting empty sets, comparisons, root isolation and witnesses.

Isolate a_N and b_N to rational intervals of width <=epsilon/4. Write
a_lo for the lower endpoint of the minimum's isolation interval and
b_hi for the upper endpoint of the maximum's interval. Let

    delta=C_task hmax <= epsilon/4,
    ell=a_lo-delta,  u=b_hi+delta.

For every true model, (B4) gives ell<=H_true<=u. The two optimizing
tables extend to genuine potentials by (W). Their true tasks are at most
epsilon/4+2 delta<=3epsilon/4 from the corresponding outer endpoints.
This proves the complete output contract, including attainable witness
models. It does not infer a universal result from finite testing.

Degenerate cases are explicit. For mu=L substitute the quadratic-affine
form and its unknown bounded vector a; skip the divided expression (I).
For mu>L reject the contract. For T=0 use a single state and no Euler
equations. Repeated locations or coincident trajectory/calibration nodes
are allowed: (I) forces equal gradients and values there. Empty
calibration data are allowed because the anchor and gradient bound remain.

6. COMPLETE RESOURCE ACCOUNTING

Let q be the calibration count, J the number of positive intervals cut
out by observation/switch times. When C_task>0,

    N <= 4 T C_task/epsilon + J.

The potential table has M=q+N+2 points. There are

    v=(N+1)n+M(d+1)+O(1)

real variables (this overcounts fixed anchor entries) and

    S=O(M^2+N n+v)+S_input

polynomial constraints. Let D=max(2, input polynomial degrees, degree H),
and let tau be the bit length of the integer coefficients after clearing
rational denominators in the ACTUALLY CONSTRUCTED problem. Each constraint
can be expanded explicitly using polynomial arithmetic; mesh construction,
matrix products, the M^2 d pair computations, and all generated
coefficients are charged. Binary-encoded high degree still incurs its
actual numeric-degree and power/bit costs.

A standard conservative CAD bound has the form

    (S D)^(2^O(v)) (tau+b+1)^O(1) bit operations,

where b is the requested objective-isolation precision after the explicit
task bound is included. The fixed constants depend on the selected
ordinary CAD implementation, not on an instance-specific oracle. Output
algebraic degrees/coefficient sizes and exact witness extraction have
the corresponding finite algebraic bounds. This is an accepted
real-algebraic algorithm, not an uncharged feasibility oracle. Subsequent
potential evaluation pays the active-face/algebraic arithmetic costs in
section 3 and any requested numerical evaluation precision.

The exponent ceil(2 Lambda T) has that many output bits when E is formed.
N can be exponential in epsilon's requested accuracy bits and in
binary-encoded physical magnitudes. CAD can then be doubly exponential
in this already-large N. There is no polynomial complete-input claim,
no free real arithmetic, and no uncharged preprocessing, advice or
nonuniform lookup. The mathematical resolution is total computability
and sharp attainable inference under the stated contract.

7. ROBUST INTERVENTION DESIGN IS A CONSTRUCTED CONSEQUENCE

Extend the input with a nonempty compact rational semialgebraic design
set Theta in R^p and a containing rational box. Replace each prescribed
forcing value by a supplied polynomial u_k(theta); allow H also to be a
polynomial in theta. The calibration/model family and initial set are
fixed independently of theta. Bounds on u and H over the containing box
are computed by coefficient sums, so (B2)--(B4) hold UNIFORMLY in theta.

Let P_N(theta,w) be the finite table constraints. Define

    G_N(theta)=max_{w:P_N(theta,w)} H_N(theta,w),
    Q_N=min_{theta in Theta} G_N(theta).

Quantifier elimination computes the least q satisfying

    exists theta in Theta, for every w,
        [ P_N(theta,w) implies H_N(theta,w)<=q ].          (D1)

It also supplies an exact algebraic minimizing theta_N and a maximizing
table at theta_N. The extrema exist. For fixed models, Euler trajectories
depend continuously on theta with uniform bounds, hence G_N is continuous
on compact Theta; alternatively the finite feasible fibers plus this
uniform modulus establish the same fact. The actual dynamics has the
same uniform dependence. Nonemptiness of the model family was checked
first, so (D1) is not a vacuous adversarial constraint.

Write G(theta)=sup_{V,z0} H_true(theta,V,z0) and Q=min_Theta G(theta).
Then |G-G_N|<=delta uniformly, whence

    |Q-Q_N|<=delta,
    G(theta_N)<=Q+2delta.                                (D2)

Choose delta<=epsilon/4 and isolate Q_N as above. This outputs an
epsilon-optimal intervention, a certified bound on its worst compatible
outcome, and an explicit compatible near-worst-case model. It is a
mathematical robust-design operation, not an application bridge left to
an unspecified future optimizer. It is also not a claim that a physical
intervention has been authorized, executed or verified.

The quantified problem has p additional variables and the degrees and
coefficients of u(theta), Theta and H. They enter S,D,v,tau in section 6;
quantifier alternation is handled and charged by the chosen CAD method.
Exact algebraic controls have finite encodings. If an application requires
rational controls, it must supply a rationally approximable feasible
design interface or interior margin; this is not inferred for a design
set consisting of isolated irrational points.

8. FURTHER SCIENTIFIC CONSEQUENCES

Task identification without full model identification. If the interval is
narrow, the task is accurately determined even if many potentials remain.
The midpoint has worst-case absolute error <=(u-ell)/2. The two witness
models prove that ANY estimator based only on the same information has
worst-case error at least max(0,u-ell-2epsilon)/2. Thus the operation also
approximates the optimal information-limited prediction accuracy.

Model falsification. Empty finite compatibility constraints prove that no
member of the declared model class explains the data. This conclusion is
conditional on the data/error and curvature contracts; it does not
identify which premise failed in a physical experiment.

Certified task inequalities. If u<c, H<c for every compatible model. If
ell>c, H>c for every compatible model. For a strict positive gap, refining
epsilon eventually certifies the correct side whenever the whole family
lies on that side. Equality or a family spanning c need not be classified
as a strict universal inequality. Returning a sharp interval remains a
complete operation in those cases.

Reusable information. Added compatible calibration constraints shrink
the feasible family, so exact task minima increase and maxima decrease.
Previously computed outer intervals may be intersected with new certified
ones. A single simultaneous data-coverage event E implies that all later
conditional model conclusions are valid on E, even if tasks are chosen
after seeing the history. This is a pathwise implication, not a new
confidence theorem. Independent physical/model validity and simultaneous
coverage must be supplied; marginal per-reading coverage is insufficient.

Reach. The equation includes natural mechanics with z=(q,p),
A=[[0,I],[0,-D]], B=[[0],[-I]], C=[I,0]; overdamped gradient flow; and
known linear networks driven by unknown gradient constitutive laws.
Finite-horizon tasks may be nonlinear and may involve nonconvex V when
mu<0. No convergence assumption is used for this finite-time theorem.

9. RETAINED FAILURE OBJECT

At points (0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1), set
g(x)=(x_1-x_2,x_1+x_2), mu=0,L=2. Every two-point sector test satisfies

    (g_i-g_j).(x_i-x_j) = |x_i-x_j|^2 = |g_i-g_j|^2/2.

Nevertheless the interpolation weights w_ij around the oriented square
sum to 2>0. Summing the required value inequalities would assert 0>=2.
Thus no compatible potential exists. Pairwise sector constraints after
discarding values do not enforce a single potential. The implementation
finds a positive three-edge cycle of weight 1 as another exact witness.
This falsifies that relaxation, not unrestricted inference or SAT.
