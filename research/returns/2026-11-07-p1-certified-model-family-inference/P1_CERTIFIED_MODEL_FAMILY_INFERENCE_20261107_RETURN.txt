### A finite table can represent a genuine global law

Suppose a finite table contains positions \(x_i\), gradients \(g_i\) and values \(f_i\). Put \(\beta=L-\mu\). Such a table admits a potential in the declared class exactly when every ordered pair satisfies
\[
f_i-f_j-g_j^\mathsf T(x_i-x_j)
-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_i-x_j\|^2
\ge
\frac{\|g_i-g_j-\mu(x_i-x_j)\|^2}{2\beta}.
\tag{1}
\]

This is the smooth convex interpolation theorem of Taylor, Hendrickx and Glineur, applied after the stated quadratic shift. Their constructive interpolation and cyclic-compatibility results receive the credit for this ingredient. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.05666?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The global witness is explicit. Define
\[
s_i=g_i-\mu x_i,\qquad
h_i=f_i-\frac{\mu}{2}\|x_i\|^2,
\]
and
\[

\max_i\left[
x_i^\mathsf Ts-h_i+\frac{\|s-s_i\|^2}{2\beta}
\right].
\]
Then
\[
V(x)=\sup_s\{x^\mathsf Ts-h^*(s)\}
+\frac{\mu}{2}\|x\|^2
\tag{2}
\]
interpolates the entire table.

Equation (2) also has a finite evaluation algorithm. After separating its common quadratic term, the remaining function is a maximum of affine functions. An optimizer is supported by an affinely independent active subset of at most \(d+1\) entries. Enumerating those subsets, solving the corresponding linear systems and checking the active inequalities evaluates the potential and gradient. For rational tables and rational query points, this computation is exact rational arithmetic. Its combinatorial cost is included.
