# Long-distance autonomous FIFO: research result and unresolved endpoint

**I cannot establish the full theorem or a genuine obstruction for the unrestricted model.** I can establish an explicit long-distance, continuously operating FIFO transport and latency theorem, together with a quantitative demonstration of why its unprotected payloads fail under admissible faults.

The distinction is important: the construction below addresses physical distance, autonomous service, traffic and finite-state ordering. It does **not** establish the required noisy joint bad-label law.

## 1\. A long-distance, counter-free transport theorem

For each of the \(L^2\) ports, construct a physical exclusion conveyor with sites

\[
(i,j,z),\qquad z=0,\ldots,L.
\]

Each site has alphabet \(\{\varnothing,0,1\}\)\. Use the following time-independent local CTMC rules\:

- An independent unit-rate source arrival places its bit at \(z=0\) if empty; otherwise that record is deleted.
- Each bond \(z\to z+1\) has an independent rate-\(\mu\) proposal clock. An occupied site moves its bit to an empty successor when that clock fires.
- A rate-\(\mu\) clock at \(z=L\) emits an occupied bit and empties the final site.

There are \(L^2(L+1)\) sites, constant local states, nearest-neighbour interactions, no pointers, and no externally supplied clock. Records physically cross distance \(L\)\. They cannot overtake one another, and the source is never backpressured.

### Theorem: a uniform exponential latency tail in the fault-free conveyor

Let \(D\) denote the time from admission to output of any particular admitted record. Set \(n=L+1\)\. Conditional on the entire history at admission,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\left(D>\frac{2n^2}{\mu}\,\middle|\,\mathcal F_{\rm admission}\right)
\leq e^{-(1-\log2)n^2}.
}
\]

This bound holds uniformly over all input histories and causal payload choices.

**Proof.** At admission, at most \(n-1\) particles precede the tagged particle. Later arrivals cannot overtake it.

Imagine clearing these predecessors from right to left, then clearing the tagged particle. Each particle requires at most \(n\) further forward moves, including its output transition. Therefore at most \(n^2\) designated successful moves suffice.

At every designated move, the next proposal of the required bond has an exponential waiting time of rate \(\mu\)\. Other legitimate forward moves can only advance this clearance schedule. The strong Markov property consequently supplies the stochastic domination

\[
D\preceq\sum_{k=1}^{n^2}E_k,
\qquad E_k\overset{\rm iid}{\sim}\operatorname{Exp}(\mu).
\]

For \(\theta=\mu/2\),

\[
\mathbb E e^{\theta\sum E_k}=2^{n^2}.
\]

The exponential Markov inequality now yields the stated result.

For the whole fabric, define \(G_{\rm transport}\) to be the event that every admitted record arriving by \(T\) completes within

\[
\ell_L=\frac{2(L+1)^2}{\mu}.
\]

Using the conditional estimate and the expected number \(L^2T\) of source arrivals,

\[
\Pr(G_{\rm transport}^{c})
\leq L^2T\,e^{-(1-\log2)(L+1)^2}
\leq C T e^{-cL^2}
\]

for fixed positive constants \(c,C\)\.

Thus, **physical distance \(L\), live unit-rate Poisson traffic, finite occupancy, preserved order and polynomial-latency completion are simultaneously achievable without a global clock**, in the absence of state faults.

The missing requirements remain error-corrected payload transport, sufficiently rare admission losses with the prescribed joint law, and containment of faults in the moving data and its control states.

### Continuing resource costs

The conveyor has \(nL^2\) nominal hop/output proposal processes of rate \(\mu\), plus \(L^2\) source streams. Counting disabled proposals as work, its fault-free proposal count satisfies

\[
W_T\sim\operatorname{Poisson}\bigl(L^2(1+\mu n)T\bigr).
\]

Writing \(m_T=L^2(1+\mu n)T\),

\[
\Pr(W_T\geq m_T+s)
\leq
\exp\left(-\frac{s^2}{2(m_T+s/3)}\right).
\]

The continuing proposal work is therefore \(O_\mu(L^3T)\), with an explicit concentration bound. Initialization is the empty product state and takes \(O(L^3)\) preparation operations.

These are costs of the baseline, not of a fault-tolerant implementation.

## 2\. A rigorous failure theorem for unprotected long-distance payloads

Choose the following admissible physical fault process: every conveyor site has an independent rate-\(\epsilon\) Poisson fault clock. At each proposal, flip the payload bit if occupied; otherwise do nothing.

These are legitimate bounded local classical state maps. They do not change particle positions, so the conveyor still preserves the physical order of admitted particles.

Let \(S\) be the total residence time of an admitted record before emission. The record requires \(n=L+1\) forward transitions. Consequently,

\[
S\succeq\operatorname{Gamma}(n,\mu).
\]

Conditional on its physical trajectory, the number of bit flips is Poisson with mean \(\epsilon S\)\. The probability of an odd number of flips is therefore

\[
\Pr(\mathrm{wrong}\mid S)
=\frac{1-e^{-2\epsilon S}}2.
\]

Taking expectations gives

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(\mathrm{wrong}\mid\mathrm{admitted})
\geq
\frac12\left[
1-\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu+2\epsilon}\right)^{L+1}
\right].
}
\]

For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\) and every fixed finite \(\mu\),

\[
\liminf_{L\to\infty}
\Pr(\mathrm{wrong}\mid\mathrm{admitted})
\geq\frac12.
\]

Thus simply transmitting a bit through a clean FIFO particle lineage does not maintain its information.

### This also defeats arbitrary semantic rematching in this conveyor

The preceding calculation concerns the natural particle-to-particle matching. The user's contract permits a more flexible mathematical partial matching, so that calculation by itself would not prove failure.

A stronger argument uses random independent fair input bits, which are an admissible causal payload strategy.

Conditional on all arrival and motion trajectories, distinct particles accumulate independent bit-flip parities. If \(X\) is the input bit word and \(Y\) the output word by time \(T\), the conditional joint distribution is close to one in which \(Y\) consists of independent fair bits unrelated to \(X\)\.

Indeed, put

\[
\lambda=\frac{\mu}{\mu+2\epsilon}<1.
\]

Coupling each emitted bit to a fresh independent fair bit, then summing over admitted arrivals, gives the total-variation estimate

\[
\left\|
\mathcal L(X,Y,\mathcal H)
-\mathcal L(X,U,\mathcal H)
\right\|_{\rm TV}
\leq \frac{T}{2}\lambda^{L+1},
\]

where \(\mathcal H\) is the occupancy/motion history and \(U\) is an independent fair output word of the same random length. The estimate uses the conditional exponential-clock bound above and the fact that the expected number of arrivals at one port before \(T\) is \(T\)\.

For every polynomial \(T(L)\), this distance tends to zero.

Suppose, for contradiction, that this particular conveyor satisfied the full semantic law for some \(q<1/100\), some polynomial latency bound, and some permitted rare event \(G\)\. Choose a sufficiently large polynomial horizon \(T(L)\), and let \(N=(1-o(1))T\) be a deterministic number of first input labels which, with probability tending to one, all arrive before \(T-\ell_L\)\.

The demanded inclusion law implies that, with \(r=\lceil0.05N\rceil\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Pr\bigl(
|\mathrm{Bad}\cap\{1,\ldots,N\}|\geq r,\ G
\bigr)
&\leq \binom Nr q^r\\
&\leq \exp(-0.031N+o(N)).
\end{aligned}
\]

The rare-event bound also makes \(\Pr(G^c)\to0\) on a polynomial horizon.

Hence the contract would require, with probability tending to one, an order-preserving match of at least \(95\%\) of the first \(N\) input bits with the output word.

But the output word has length \(M=(1+o(1))N\) at most, and is asymptotically independent of those input bits. For two independent uniform binary words,

\[
\Pr\left(\operatorname{LCS}(X,U)\geq k\right)
\leq
\binom Nk\binom Mk2^{-k}.
\]

At \(k=\lceil0.95N\rceil\), the right-hand side decreases exponentially because

\[
2h(0.05)-0.95\log2<0,
\]

where \(h\) is binary entropy in natural logarithms.

This contradicts the demanded high-probability semantic matching.

**Conclusion of this theorem\:** the unencoded exclusion-conveyor family fails the full joint labelled-index contract even when arbitrary order-preserving semantic rematching is permitted. This is a genuine impossibility for that specified architecture and fault process, **not** an impossibility for the unrestricted three-dimensional model.

## 3\. A sufficient mathematical criterion for the missing joint fault law

A useful way to isolate the main remaining problem is through a bounded-overlap witness theorem.

### Witness-cover theorem

Let \(\{Z_v:v\in V\}\) be primitive physical defect indicators satisfying

\[
\Pr(Z_v=1\ \forall v\in H)\leq\eta^{|H|}
\]

for every finite deterministic \(H\subset V\)\.

Suppose that, on a proposed good event \(G\), every bad record label \(j\) has at least one defective witness in a predetermined set \(W_j\subset V\), with

\[
|W_j|\leq D,
\]

and suppose each primitive witness \(v\) belongs to at most \(K\) of the sets \(W_j\)\.

Then, for every deterministic finite label set \(F\),

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(F\subseteq\mathrm{Bad},G)
\leq\left(D\eta^{1/K}\right)^{|F|}.
}
\]

**Proof.** Assign one defective witness \(v_j\in W_j\) to each \(j\in F\)\. There are at most \(D^{|F|}\) assignments. Because a primitive witness occurs in at most \(K\) witness sets, every assignment contains at least \(|F|/K\) distinct witnesses. Apply the joint primitive-defect bound and take a union bound over assignments.

In particular, if

\[
\eta\leq(q/D)^K,
\]

the required \(q^{|F|}\) inequality follows.

This theorem is exact, including joint selections containing input and output labels. It does not assume independence of the resulting bad labels.

### What the physical construction must supply

The difficulty is obtaining \(D,K\) independent of \(L\) from an actual moving, continuously reused record system.

A single physical fault that can disturb an unbounded number of later records would destroy the bounded-overlap premise. Likewise, a synchronized bank that loses alignment and then misinterprets every later record does not possess a bounded witness cover.

The primitive-defect domination also has to be proved for actual Poisson-clock events. It cannot be inferred merely by declaring that separate logical records have independent faults: their service intervals, buffer occupancy and clock histories can overlap.

There is also a strict quantitative constraint on the exceptional event. For any deterministic \(F_L\),

\[
\Pr(F_L\subseteq\mathrm{Bad})
\leq q^{|F_L|}+CTe^{-cL^a}.
\]

Thus, on polynomial horizons, an upset that spoils a predetermined growing collection of labels with probability bounded away from zero cannot simply be classified as a rare catastrophe. Its probability must genuinely decrease at the required scale.

This explains why a source-index pointer, phase register or rolling acknowledgement state needs an actual repair and resynchronization law, rather than merely a low single-step error probability.

## 5\. Precise remaining research obligation

The transport theorem and the witness-cover theorem isolate two fundamentally different tasks.

**Reliable propagation.** A long-distance physical channel must continually regenerate the information carried by each source record. If an architecture uses \(L\) successive protected stages, a simple sufficient per-stage target is a logical record-error probability of order \(q/L\)\. Achieving that bound requires error correction whose controllers, local synchronization and decoding are themselves faultable. The spatial and temporal redundancy must be implemented at constant microscopic rates without reducing the offered throughput.

**Reliable alignment and retirement.** The same channel must prevent a local error from creating an indefinite offset between the input and output event sequences. Recoverable deletions and insertions must have bounded or exponentially tailed causal damage; persistent alignment failures must fit the much smaller \(CTe^{-cL^a}\) exceptional-event budget. Its proof must cover simultaneous streaming traffic, reused finite storage, output framing and adapted fault maps.

The first task is not implied by a clean FIFO service theorem. The second is not implied by a conventional block-code error probability.

An implementation establishing both, with independent physical-clock witnesses and a polynomial resource compiler, would complete the requested positive theorem. No such implementation has been established in this analysis.
