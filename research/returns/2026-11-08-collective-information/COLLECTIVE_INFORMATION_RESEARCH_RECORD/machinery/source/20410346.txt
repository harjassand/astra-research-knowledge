Abstract
We propose a coordinate-independent quantitative framework for the instantaneous error
induced by coarse-graining high-dimensional Markov generators. Within the Mori-Zwanzig
projection formalism, we isolate the reduction error through the commutation defect ∆ π =
LP−PL 0, which coincides with the initial orthogonal forcing termQLP. We introduce a
spectral energy functional associated with the fiber transfer semigroup and prove rigorously
that it controls the short-time growth of the memory kernel, under explicit spectral gap
and domain regularity assumptions. Unlike discrete closure hierarchies or moment-based
truncations, our approach provides a stable quantitative characterization of model reduction
error in terms of operator norms and spectral energy distribution. We further introduce a
spectral entropy functional that quantifies the effective dispersion of the defect across fiber
modes.

6 Interpretation
The spectral energyE π[Φ] quantifies the magnitude of instantaneous non-Markovian forcing,
while the spectral entropyS π[Φ] quantifies its dispersion across fiber relaxation modes. To-
gether, they characterize the structural complexity of model reduction error in a coordinate-
independent manner.
Theorem 1 establishes that the initial activation of memory is controlled entirely by the
spectral energy of the commutation defect. Systems with smallE π[Φ] generate weak memory
kernels; systems where the defect is concentrated in few fiber modes (lowS π[Φ]) have memory
dominated by those modes.
7 Conclusion
We have introduced a quantitative framework for non-Markovian reduction error based on the
commutation defect in the Mori-Zwanzig formalism. The main result (Theorem 1) establishes
rigorously, under explicit spectral gap and analytic semigroup assumptions, that the memory
kernel is controlled by the spectral energy of the defect, with constants uniform inϵon the nat-
ural local timescalet∼ϵ. By replacing discrete closure classifications with spectral energy and
entropy functionals, we obtain a stable and coordinate-independent characterization of mem-
ory generation in multiscale systems. A complete uniform-in-ϵtheory for all times, requiring
homogenization methods and Poisson equation resolutions, is left for future work.
