1. Source and operational question

Let X be a real Hilbert space of arbitrary finite or infinite dimension.
H is a self-adjoint operator with H>=a I, a>0. B:R^r -> X is an isometry,
range B is contained in Dom H. Source dynamics are
    x'=-H x+B v,  y=B* x.
Only v and y are accessible. No state vectors, H-actions, eigenmodes,
source dimension, transfer function, or source realization are supplied
to the learner. The installed collocation, units B*B=I, stationarity,
self-adjointness and the following quantitative bounds are supplied:
    A=B*HB, ||A||<=A0,
    V=(I-BB*)HB, ||V||^2<=c0,
where a,A0,c0 are finite rational data. Unknown initial state has a known
energy-norm bound R0. These promises are not established by a finite fit.

No upper bound on the whole unbounded H is assumed. A0 and c0 are port
regularity/coupling bounds. For a bounded symmetric source with spectrum
in [a,b], A0=b and c0=b^2 are sufficient (sharper bounds can be supplied).
The earlier whole-state-action theorem remains unchanged and is credited.

Goal: finitely many calibrated, bounded-amplitude, finite-duration port
input/output experiments acquire a finite causal model, whose same error
certificate covers every subsequent qualified monotone nonlinear port
attachment, extra damped dynamic states, modular interconnection and
model-family inference task, at the actual paid input/error/cost interface.

THE INPUT/OUTPUT CONTRACT. The algorithm receives rational r,a,A0,c0,R0
and an error target, together with a qualified finite experimental channel.
The channel accepts a finite rational port-waveform/preparation request
and returns finitely many rational coordinates with warranted absolute
error bounds. Its allowed amplitude, frequency, time, calibration,
initialization and precision capabilities, and their costs, are part of
that interface. The algorithm issues only requests within it. Arbitrarily
small targets require genuinely refinable channel precision, not a fixed
noise oracle. An instrument cost C_chan for each actual request is added
in full to digital cost; no polynomial bound on an unspecified physical
instrument, or on computing an arbitrary infinite-dimensional source,
is asserted. When a required request is unavailable, this particular
uniform accuracy guarantee is unavailable; a supplied weaker target or
the directly checked certificate (1) can still be used when feasible.

Its output is rational Ahat,R_j,lambda_j, an exact rational error E and
the causal equations below. The source is not identified internally.
The meaning of the output is a simultaneous theorem about every source
compatible with the class, the instrument contract and the acquired
record. The later attachment is not fixed when the record is acquired.

2. Exact port object and useful norm

On X=range B plus its orthogonal complement, H has blocks [A,V*;V,D].
The finite off-diagonal block is bounded, so subtraction of that bounded
self-adjoint block establishes that D is self-adjoint and D>=a I on
its natural domain. Eliminating zero-initial hidden states gives
    Z(s)=G(s)^(-1)=s I+Q(s),
    G(s)=B*(s I+H)^(-1)B,
    Q(s)=A-V*(s I+D)^(-1)V,
    K(t)=V*exp(-Dt)V.
The kernel has a positive matrix-valued spectral measure Sigma supported
on [a,infinity), with Sigma(total)=V*V<=c0 I. In particular
    ||K(t)||<=c0 exp(-at),  ||Q(iw)||<=C=A0+c0/a,
    ||G(iw)^(-1)||<=w+C  for w>=0.
These are mathematical consequences of the source class, not acquired
transfer values. The construction below acquires finite time-domain data.

For two frequencies w,v>=0, the resolvent identity gives
 ||Q(iw)-Q(iv)|| <= c0 |w-v|/[sqrt(a^2+w^2)sqrt(a^2+v^2)].
The same bound with M instead of c0 holds for any retained model
    Qhat(s)=Ahat-sum_j R_j/(s+lambda_j),
    R_j>=0, lambda_j>=a, sum tr R_j<=M.

Use the rational frequency grid
    t_i=i/N, w_i=2a t_i/(1-t_i^2), i=0,...,N-1.
Writing w=a tan(theta), theta=2 atan(t), proves that every nonnegative
frequency, including the limit at infinity, is within resolvent distance
2/(aN) of one grid point. Therefore, if finite data Ztilde_i satisfy
    ||Ztilde_i-Z(iw_i)||<=eta_i,
then a completely checked candidate has the global certificate
    E=max_i(eta_i+||Qhat(iw_i)-(Ztilde_i-iw_i I)||)
          +2(c0+sum tr R_j)/(aN).                         (1)
Thus sup_w ||Z(iw)-Zhat(iw)||<=E. Negative frequencies follow by
conjugation. Plancherel gives the induced L2 causal operator bound on
every finite horizon. The common derivative term cancels; the remaining
impedance difference is an instantaneous matrix plus an L1 kernel.

This norm is chosen for later feedback. An arbitrary small open-loop
transfer error does not give the same all-intervention statement; see
the explicit fast-weak-mode obstruction below.

3. Acquiring each frequency value by finite port experiments

For each port e_j and frequency w, perform two real experiments with
nominal inputs cos(w(t-T)) e_j and sin(w(t-T)) e_j, from t=0 to T.
Their combined ideal response at T is
    G(iw)e_j - B*exp(-(H+iw)T)(H+iw)^(-1)B e_j.
The sign convention is chosen so cos+i sin=exp(iw(t-T)). Hence the
transient error is at most exp(-aT)/a. The actual finite response, not
an exact G oracle, is the measured quantity.

Let Rstar=max(R0,2/a). Since every delivered input has norm at most 2,
the semigroup estimate preserves ||x||<=Rstar throughout a continuous
sequence of trials and waits. No between-trial reset is needed. Waiting
W under zero input therefore gives at most Rstar exp(-aW) at the start
of EACH trial. Its additional output error is bounded by:
  initialization: Rstar exp(-aW) (conservative at readout);
  delivered-input mismatch: nu/a if ||v_actual-v_nominal||_infinity<=nu;
  timing: L_y tau_clock;
  sensor noise, offset/scale calibration, quantization and numerical
  forward-evaluation errors at their actual certified absolute bounds.
For nu<=1 a sufficient timing constant is
  L_y=(A0+c0+1)(Rstar+2/a)+2,
since ||HB||<=A0+sqrt(c0)<=A0+c0+1. A waveform is continued through the
readout uncertainty window. If a separate instrument has a bounded-reset
capability instead, its cost and actual bound must be stated. The
continuous experiment sequence here uses relaxation alone.

Let gamma_i bound the SUM of these contributions and the finite-time
transient for each real scalar reported coordinate. Then complex matrix
Gtilde_i, assembled from the 2r trials, obeys
    ||Gtilde_i-G(iw_i)||<=2r gamma_i.
With m_i=w_i+C, exact rational complex inversion has error
    eta_i = m_i^2 (2r gamma_i)/(1-m_i 2r gamma_i),       (2)
provided the denominator is positive. Every matrix inversion and its
bit growth is paid; singular reports are not silently accepted.

For target 0<epsilon<=a/4 set
    M=r c0+1, tau=epsilon/(16r),
    N>=max(2,ceil(8(c0+M)/(a epsilon))),
    gamma_i<=tau/[64r(m_i+1)^2].
This gives eta_i<tau/8 with ample margin. Allocate the gamma_i budget
among the displayed actual errors. T and W are finite logarithmic
waiting times; choose rational upper bounds using exp(-x)<=2^(-x)
for x>=0 instead of assuming an exact logarithm operation.

An actual zero-order-hold digital waveform implementation is available:
use step length h<=nu/(4 max(w,1)), amplitude rounding <=nu/4,
frequency-setting error <=nu/(4(T+tau_clock)), and phase error<=nu/4.
The derivative of sine/cosine bounds the delivered-input error by nu.
It costs at least the explicitly planned number of actuator updates
ceil((T+tau_clock)/h), with every amplitude value computed at its paid
precision. A qualified analogue generator is a different installed
interface; its bandwidth, calibration and cost are not erased.

The mathematical physical experiment uses only accessible port inputs
and outputs. The accompanying execution is a certified finite-dimensional
SOFTWARE realization of these time-domain experiments. Its finite source
encoding, private native calculations, preparation, forward arithmetic
and quantization are charged separately. No physical trial is claimed.

With fixed positive native noise or a fixed calibration floor, a request
whose gamma_i budget is smaller cannot be promised. Repetitions do not
reduce arbitrary bounded adversarial bias. Refinable precision must be
an actual qualified capability. All source-class and calibration bounds
remain supplied rather than inferred from fit quality.

4. Guaranteed finite model construction, without an order promise

The unknown positive kernel measure can be approximated uniformly in
the ENTIRE imaginary axis by quantizing inverse rates z=1/lambda in
[0,1/a]. Use grid z_j=j/(aL), j=0,...,L. Move every spectral value to
its lower grid point. z_0=0 means its contribution is dropped; all other
poles are lambda_j=aL/j. Since
    f_w(z)=z/(1+iw z), |f'_w(z)|<=1,
the impedance approximation error is <=c0/(aL). This includes arbitrarily
fast hidden modes; there is no uncharged spectral cutoff oracle.

Choose L>=max(1,ceil(16c0/(a tau))) and
    mu=min(1/(4rL), a tau/(16L)).
Existential quadrature weights have total trace <=r c0. Add mu I to
each weight. This introduces error <=L mu/a<=tau/16 and leaves total
trace <=r c0+1/4<M. They satisfy R_j>=mu I. Together with (2), this
constructs an interior feasible model with sample component errors
strictly below tau/4.

The learner does NOT use those unknown weights. It solves the following
finite rational semidefinite problem from its acquired data:
    Ahat symmetric, |Ahat_uv|<=A0+1;
    R_j>=mu I/2, sum tr R_j<=M;
    abs(real/imag entry of Qhat(iw_i)-Qtilde_i)<=t;
    minimize t, 0<=t<=Tmax.
All coefficients 1/(lambda_j+iw_i) and all observations are rational
complex numbers. A finite list of real rational linear inequalities
expresses the fitting constraints. The number of scalar unknowns is
    d=(L+1)r(r+1)/2+1.

There is a KNOWN strictly feasible starting point: Ahat=0, R_j=mu I,
and t=t0 greater than max absolute real/imag data entry+Lmu/a+2;
take Tmax=2t0+2. A rational ball of radius
 min(mu/(4r),1/(4rL),1/2,1/[4(2+L/a)])
around that point lies inside all inequalities in these coordinates.
The body also lies in an explicit ball of radius
 d(A0+1+M+Tmax+1). These are actual conditioning data.

The classical rational ellipsoid/SDP weak-optimization algorithm, with
objective accuracy tau/4, returns a rational feasible model with t<tau.
This applies its well-bounded/interior hypotheses rather than treating

The inequalities hold for every such locally Lipschitz F. The COMPLETE

7. Structural obstruction: small open-loop error can fail under new feedback

At each of two ports compare the scalar transfer functions
    G0(s)=1/(s+1),
    Ge(s)=(1-e)/(s+1)+e/(s+1/e), 0<e<1.
They have normalized high-frequency mass 1, source dissipation a=1 and
port first moment A_e=2-e<=2. Their impulse responses differ in L1 by
e(1-e)<=e. Consequently every acquisition history whose delivered total
port effort has sup norm <=U has output difference <=e U, regardless
of duration. Positive adversarial readout errors can conceal this from
all such finite adaptive acquisition histories.

Their hidden coupling variance is e(1-e)(1/e-1)^2, which diverges as
e tends to zero. Thus this family omits precisely the common coupling budget
used by the positive construction while retaining a=1 and A<=2.

Now add the monotone skew interconnection J=[[0,w],[-w,0]], w=1/e,
and apply the unit rotating input. At frequency w the nominal closed-loop
gain is 1, whereas the alternative tends to 1/(3/2+i/2) as e->0.
Their limiting output difference has magnitude 1/sqrt(5), despite the
vanishing open-loop discrepancy. Both complete systems remain dissipative
with margin 1. Their startup transients decay exponentially, so a fixed
finite horizon gives a constant separating task for sufficiently small e.
The larger later reactive forces, frequency and setting precision are
real intervention costs, not free resources in this obstruction.

For rational source coefficients use e=(2k/(k^2+1))^2, and the rational
normalized port direction ((k^2-1)/(k^2+1),2k/(k^2+1)) on the two modes.

9. Calibration, leading port mass, and implementation scope

B*B=I is exact structural information in this source class. It makes
the leading term of Z equal to sI, which cancels when comparing sources.
This is not an exact physical calibration inferred from the experiments.
Finite actuation/sensor/clock errors DURING the acquired trials are
separate bounded deviations from those defined model ports, included
in gamma. If the leading port mass itself is uncertain, that is a
different source class and it cannot silently be charged as a bounded
impedance error across all frequencies.

A short additional obstruction shows the distinction. At two ports
compare G_1(s)=I/(s+1) with G_m(s)=m I/(s+1), m=1+delta>1. These can
be realized with H=I and B=sqrt(m) I, so the latter omits exactly the
known-normalization premise; its bulk has no hidden memory. The open-loop
impulse difference has L1 norm delta. Add J=[[0,w],[-w,0]] with w=1/delta
and use the rotating unit input. The complex gains are respectively 1
and m/(1-i). As delta tends to zero, their distance tends to 1/sqrt(2).
Thus even small unknown leading port scale is consequential for the
unrestricted future-skew family. One must know that structural mass,
retain its uncertainty with a correspondingly wider task bound, or bound
the later intervention frequencies/gains. The main theorem uses the
first explicitly stated option; it does not infer it from finite tests.

For actual later hardware, supplied forcing and feedback implementation
errors are also paid. If their combined effect in the model equations
has a warranted L2 norm delta_impl, replace the computed-path quantity
Delta by R_num+E||zbar||_2+delta_impl. The same energy proof gives the
pointwise and L2 certificate. Gain/clock errors cannot be assumed to stay
small uniformly over arbitrarily large future gains. Polynomial field
and energy bounds also give a finite bound on the required later port
force; a physical intervention must fit its real actuator range. The
mathematical reuse operation predicts the specified model equations and
