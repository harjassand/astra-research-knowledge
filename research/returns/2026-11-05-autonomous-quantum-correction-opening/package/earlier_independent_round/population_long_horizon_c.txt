RESEARCHER C: EXPONENTIALLY LONG FINITE-ENSEMBLE REGULATION
5 November 2026. Constructive mathematical extension, jointly suggested with
the principal and independently checked. Priority unconfirmed; no efficiency
or demonstrated physical capability is claimed.

1. Interface and deterministic constants

Use the exact plant q^u_ij=q_ij exp[(u_i-u_j)/2] on a strongly connected finite
directed graph, unknown q with known qmin,qmax, positive target rho and gain k.
All n labels and potential actuators are resolved. Let r0=min rho_i, and take
A,R0,B,m,eps,cW,CW,lambda from population_control_addendum_c.txt Sections 1--2.
Let u* denote the unknown target-stationary optimum. The strict function is
  x=p-rho, y=u-u*,
  W=F(u)-F(u*)+(k/2)D(p||rho)+eps x.y.
Whenever F<=A on the segment to u*,
  cW(||x||^2+||y||^2)<=W<=CW(||x||^2+||y||^2),
  L_det W<=-lambda W.
The optimum is used for analysis, never by the controller.

There are M identical particles, with rates Q^{chi(u)} conditional on the shared
controller. Their empirical fractions P^M drive u'=k(P^M/rho-1). The smooth
component clamp chi is 1-Lipschitz, identity on [-B-1,B+1], and bounded by B+2.
This counts M plants, n-1 controller variables, all particle-state sensing and
continuous ideal precision. It is active feedback throughout storage, not
passive memory or sampling of an unknown baseline law.

2. A local basin where the original strict constants remain valid

For target tolerance delta>0 define
  ru=min(1/2, (1/2)log(A/R0)),
  r=min(r0/8,ru/4,delta/sqrt(n)), h=cW r^2.
A>R0 since k>0 and n>=2, so ru,r,h are strictly positive and computable.
On the broad neighborhood D={||x||_2<=4r, ||y||_2<=4r}, p_i>=r0/2 and
  F(u*+y)<=F(u*)exp(||y||_infinity)<=R0 exp(ru)<A.
The clamp is the identity there. The original W estimates therefore hold on D
and on its needed line segments, rather than assuming a noisy path preserves
the full deterministic Lyapunov sublevel.

Before W reaches h, the quadratic lower bound gives ||x||,||y||<r. If
M>=sqrt(2)/r, an empirical jump has norm sqrt(2)/M<=r, so its endpoint and
the whole Taylor segment stay inside 2r, well within D. A continuous exit from
D would first cross W=h. Thus the stopped process at tau=inf{t:W>=h} has valid
interior estimates including jump overshoot. While t<tau,
  ||P^M_t-rho||_1<=sqrt(n)r<=delta.
No stationary distribution of the noisy closed loop is assumed.

3. Generator estimates and an explicit stopped exponential bound

In D each row out-rate is <=Q0=A/r0, hence total particle event rate is <=M Q0.
For a data jump v=(e_j-e_i)/M, Taylor's theorem gives
  Delta W=grad_p W.v+R, 0<=R<=k/(r0 M^2),
since Hess_pp W=(k/2)diag(1/p_i) and the segment has p_i>=r0/2.
The tangential probability gradient obeys
  ||grad_p W||_tangent<=G sqrt(W/cW), G=k/r0+eps.
The irrelevant common k/2 in the entropy gradient drops out of v. This uses
||log(p/rho)||<=2||p-rho||/r0 on D. Consequently
  G_M W<=-lambda W+C0/M, C0=kQ0/r0.

Let
  K=sqrt(2)G/sqrt(cW), D0=K sqrt(h)+k/r0,
  theta=min{1/D0,lambda cW/[4e Q0 G^2]},
  b=theta C0+theta^2 e Q0 k^2/r0^2,
  Cstar=b exp(2b/lambda).
All are known, finite and positive. With V=exp(theta M W),

  G_M V/V <=-(theta lambda/2) M W+b.                       (L1)

To verify the exponential remainder: M|Delta W|<=D0, and
  sum_rates (Delta W)^2
    <=4Q0 G^2 W/(cW M)+2Q0 k^2/(r0^2 M^3).
Use e^z-1-z<=z^2 exp(|z|)/2 and theta D0<=1. The W term in the remainder is
<=theta lambda M W/2 by the choice of theta; the remaining term is <=the
second term defining b. The first term in b is the Taylor source theta C0.

If MW>=2b/(theta lambda), the right side of (L1) is nonpositive. Otherwise
V<=exp(2b/lambda). Therefore G_M V<=Cstar before tau, uniformly in M. Dynkin's
formula for the stopped process gives
  P(tau<=T)<=exp[-theta M(h-W0)]+Cstar T exp(-theta M h).
For an initial state with W0<=h/4, this becomes
  P(tau<=T)<=(1+Cstar T)exp(-3theta M h/4).                (L2)

For rigor, extend W smoothly off the interior neighborhood before applying
Dynkin; the stopped pre-jump and post-jump points stay inside D. Finite bounded
rates and the bounded-controller construction below make all stopped tests
integrable. Overshoot only increases V at exit and helps the probability bound.
This is a direct exponential-martingale estimate, not an imported small-noise
metastability theorem with unverified hypotheses.

4. Computable finite burn-in, with logarithmic confidence cost

Start all M particles in state1, u0=0, matching the ideal p0=e1. Put
  U0=CW(2+nB^2), s0=sqrt(h/(4CW)),
  T0=max{0,(2/lambda)log[2sqrt(2U0/cW)/s0]}.
The ideal combined error ||p-rho||_2+||u-u*||_2 is <=s0/2 at T0. Let
  Qbar=(n-1)qmax exp(B+2), L=2Qbar+k/r0,
  zeta=(s0/2)exp(-L T0).
The clamped empirical tracking estimate from the addendum says that an empirical
vector martingale bound sup_(t<=T0)||mathcal M_t||<=zeta ensures combined
tracking error <=s0/2 at T0. The resulting state has W<=h/4.

Each coordinate of mathcal M has jumps bounded by 1/M and predictable quadratic
variation <=Qbar T0/M. Its exponential martingale gives the two-sided maximal
Bernstein bound
  P(sup_(t<=T0)|mathcal M_i(t)|>=a)
    <=2 exp[-M a^2/(2(Qbar T0+a/3))].
This follows from e^z-1-z<=z^2/[2(1-|z|/3)] for |z|<3 and optional stopping;
the finite rates make the exponential compensator well defined. Union over n
coordinates, with a=zeta/sqrt(n), gives
  P(sup||mathcal M||_2>zeta)
    <=2n exp[-M zeta^2/(2(nQbar T0+sqrt(n)zeta/3))].

For a desired total failure alpha in (0,1), it is sufficient to take
  Mburn=[2(nQbar T0+sqrt(n)zeta/3)/zeta^2]log(4n/alpha).
Then P(W(T0)>h/4)<=alpha/2. These constants can be enormous, but T0 and their
dependence on model/accuracy are explicit and independent of storage horizon T.

5. A genuinely bounded controller state on every path

Bounding only the actuator output could hide an unbounded virtual integrator.
Remove this by multiplying u' by a smooth scalar cutoff eta(||u||^2), equal to
one on the ball of radius
  Rmem=1+max{sqrt(n)(B+1),k sqrt(n)T0/r0}
and zero outside radius Rmem+1, with values in [0,1]. This preserves rho.u=0.
The outer ball is invariant because the vector field vanishes at its boundary.
During burn-in, even the worst path has ||u_t||<=k sqrt(n)T0/r0, so the cutoff
never activates. In the local basin, ||u||<=sqrt(n)B+r<Rmem, so it also never
activates there. Both preceding arguments are unchanged, while controller
memory, actuator range and slew rate are finite on EVERY path for all times.

This is bounded continuous memory; a finite-bit precision theorem is a separate
implementation requirement. The number of particle-state transitions and their
sensing work continues to grow with storage time. No passive-memory or zero-
power statement follows from a bounded controller state.

6. Long-horizon theorem and exact resource dependence

Given alpha,delta,T, choose integer M at least the maximum of
  sqrt(2)/r,
  Mburn,
  [4/(3theta h)]log[2(1+Cstar T)/alpha].
The strong Markov property at T0 and (L2) then prove
  P(sup_(T0<=t<=T0+T)||P^M_t-rho||_1>delta)<=alpha.
For fixed n,rho,qmin,qmax,k,delta this is M=O(log((1+T)/alpha)), with a known
potentially huge coefficient and fixed burn-in. Conversely, for a sufficiently
large fixed M, a horizon exp(cM) is protected with error exp(-c'M), apart from
the separately specified burn-in confidence. Taking burn-in confidence itself
exponentially small in M is allowed by the Bernstein bound.

This improves the earlier exp(2LT) replica bound by using deterministic strict
stability after burn-in. It does not improve the exact actuator, state-resolution
or hardware-precision standard, and it is not evidence of practical advantage.
The principal independently obtained a boxed variant with the same conclusion;
researcher C hostile-checked its generator constants. The population mechanism's
exact priority remains unresolved. Bernstein, strict Lyapunov and small-noise
stability techniques are established mathematics, not new general principles.
