1. QUESTION, DECISIVE OPERATION, AND RESULT

Question: Can an unknown finite driven stochastic network be programmed to spend
any prescribed positive fraction of time in each state, using only state
observations and state-potential actuation, without identifying its transition
rates or waiting for equilibrium after every control adjustment?

The constructive answer below is yes for a precisely stated, strong interface:
all states are distinguishable; every state has an independently controllable
potential; and rates respond by the symmetric potential tilt

  q^u_ij = q_ij exp[(u_i-u_j)/2],       i != j.                 (1)

The base rates q_ij may be unknown and nonreversible. Strong connectivity is
required. This is an exact actuation law, not a consequence of arbitrary physical
energy control. In particular it keeps geometric mean rates on a bidirectional
edge fixed; an Arrhenius apparatus changing only source energies need not obey it.

Two constructive theorems emerged.

A. For an evolving population distribution p, the integral controller

  p' = p Q^u,
  u'_i = k[p_i/rho_i-1],       k>0, rho_i>0, sum_i rho_i=1,    (2)

converges globally to p=rho and to the unique potential u* making rho stationary.
No detailed balance, model knowledge, small gain, or time-scale separation is
needed. The decisive missing operation was a JOINT Lyapunov function controlling
both the plant distribution and its integral controller.

B. A SINGLE observed trajectory admits the controller

  du_i/dt = k[1{X_t=i}/rho_i-1]                                (3)

with the same controlled jump rates (1). The resulting finite-state/continuous-
memory Markov process has the exact invariant law

  Psi_k(i,du) = rho_i Z_k^-1 exp[-2 F_rho(u)/k] du,             (4)
  F_rho(u) = sum_{i != j} rho_i q_ij exp[(u_i-u_j)/2].

Here du is Lebesgue measure on H={u:rho.u=0}. Thus the observed-state marginal is
EXACTLY rho at finite constant gain, while the controller carries the unknown
kinetic information. The process is ergodic in its invariant law. From any
initial law with a density in u, its time occupations converge almost surely to
rho. An all-deterministic-start Harris theorem and quantitative burn-in theorem
are not asserted here.

The endpoint u* is the already-known Donsker-Varadhan optimal control. After
cross-audit, the single-trajectory architecture was identified exactly with
factorized Event Chain Monte Carlo after a clock change (Section 11). Broad
novelty claims for that architecture are retired. The remaining candidate
operations are the nonadiabatic population Lyapunov, its bounded-hardware and
exponential extension in population_control_addendum_c.txt, and the recorded
cost identity. Priority for these particular consequences remains unresolved.

2. FORMAL ASSUMPTIONS AND COST INTERFACE

The state space S={1,...,n} is finite, n>=2. A known directed support E is strongly
connected. Base off-diagonal rates are fixed finite numbers q_ij>0 for (i,j) in E
and zero otherwise. The diagonal of Q^u is minus the row sum of off-diagonal
rates. The target rho is supplied explicitly and is strictly positive.

The controller knows rho and k, not q. For (2), it observes instantaneous
population fractions p_i. For (3), it observes the present individual state
without delay and continuously accumulates residence times. The actuator realizes
(1) on the existing edges; it does not add edges.

There are n-1 effective real control variables. The natural gauge rho.u=0 is
preserved by both controllers. Adding a constant to all u does not affect rates.
On bidirectional supports every finite cycle affinity is preserved because the
potential differences telescope around a cycle.

The exact controller permits unbounded potential amplitudes and ideal continuous
precision. It uses one integrator per independent state occupation. Applied to an
N-body state space with n exponentially large, this is an exponentially large
interface. No local-many-body implementation or small-port hidden-state control
is implied.

Path-relative entropy per unit time is the specified mathematical control cost.
It is not identified with total apparatus power: sensing, memory, actuator work,
and their thermodynamic implementation require additional physical assumptions.

3. THE CONVEX POTENTIAL AND THE KNOWN OPTIMAL ENDPOINT

For every q above, F=F_rho is smooth, invariant under constant shifts, and strictly
convex modulo constants. Its Hessian obeys

  v^T Hess F(u) v
    = (1/4) sum_{i != j} rho_i q^u_ij (v_i-v_j)^2.             (5)

Its nullspace consists exactly of constant vectors by connectivity.

F is coercive on H. Let M=max_i u_i, m=min_i u_i. A directed simple path from an
M-vertex to an m-vertex has at most n-1 edges, and one edge has drop at least
(M-m)/(n-1). Therefore, writing c=min_{(i,j) in E}rho_i q_ij>0,

  F(u) >= c exp[(M-m)/(2(n-1))].                              (6)

On rho.u=0, min u<=0<=max u and M-m>=||u||_infinity. Thus F has compact sublevels
on H and a unique minimizer u* there. Direct differentiation gives

  partial_i F(u) = -(rho Q^u)_i/2.                            (7)

The constrained critical point on H is also an unconstrained critical point
modulo constants: if grad F is proportional to rho, its coordinate sum is zero,
so that proportionality constant vanishes. Hence rho Q^{u*}=0.

Known minimum-control result, also proved here to fix cost conventions:
for any generator A supported on E with rho A=0, define

  C_rho(A||Q)
    = sum_{i != j}rho_i[a_ij log(a_ij/q_ij)-a_ij+q_ij].

Let A*=Q^{u*}. Since log(a*_ij/q_ij)=(u*_i-u*_j)/2,

  C_rho(A||Q)-C_rho(A*||Q)
    = sum_{i != j}rho_i[a_ij log(a_ij/a*_ij)-a_ij+a*_ij]
    >= 0.                                                   (8)

The extra linear term vanishes by stationarity of both generators. Equality
forces A=A*. In particular

  I_Q(rho) = min_{rho A=0} C_rho(A||Q) = F(0)-F(u*).           (9)

This is established Donsker-Varadhan/Doob-transform machinery. It is not claimed
as an original theorem. Conservative potential control already attains the
optimum over all finite-cost generators having invariant rho.

4. GLOBAL NONADIABATIC POPULATION FEEDBACK

THEOREM 1. Under the assumptions in Section 2, every solution of (2), with p(0)
a probability vector and finite u(0) in H, exists for all t>=0 and converges to
(rho,u*).

For positive p define r_i=p_i/rho_i and D(p||rho)=sum_i p_i log r_i. Set

  V(p,u) = F(u)+(k/2)D(p||rho).

The feedback gives, using (7),

  dF/dt = -(k/2) sum_{i != j}rho_i q^u_ij(r_j-r_i),
  (k/2)dD/dt
    = (k/2) sum_{i != j}rho_i r_i q^u_ij log(r_j/r_i).

Consequently

  dV/dt = -(k/2) sum_{i != j}rho_i q^u_ij
             [r_i log(r_i/r_j)-r_i+r_j] <= 0.                (10)

Each bracket is a nonnegative scalar relative-entropy divergence. Equality holds
only when r_i=r_j on every directed edge, hence p=rho. F coercivity bounds u;
p remains in the simplex. These facts give global existence and a compact
positively invariant sublevel.

For completeness, an initial boundary p causes no obstruction. The derivative
u'_i is bounded by k max(1,1/rho_i-1). On every finite time interval, controlled
edge rates are bounded and preserve a strongly connected support, so all p_i(t)
become strictly positive for t>0. On the compact u sublevel their rates have
uniform positive lower bounds on E. Applying a fixed finite-time transition
minorization gives a positive lower bound for p_i after a fixed initial time.
LaSalle can therefore be applied in a compact interior region.

The largest invariant subset of {p=rho} is the single point (rho,u*): there u'=0,
and p can remain rho only if rho Q^u=0. Strict convexity identifies u*. This
proves global convergence for every k>0.

This is not an adiabatic statement about pi(u). The plant can be far from its
current stationary law while the controller moves; the joint Lyapunov function
handles that lag exactly.

5. SINGLE-TRAJECTORY CONTROLLER AND EXACT INVARIANT LAW

The process Z_t=(X_t,u_t) has generator

  L f(i,u) = k(e_i/rho_i-1).grad_u f(i,u)
                + sum_j q^u_ij[f(j,u)-f(i,u)].               (11)

Its vector fields are constant and tangent to H. They have bounded norm. Thus u
is bounded on each finite time interval, so the jump rates are bounded on that
interval; the process is nonexplosive. The exact holding-time formula below also
makes the construction explicit.

By (6), exp[-2F/k] is integrable on H and has moments of every order. Let
phi(u)=Z_k^-1 exp[-2F(u)/k]. The forward jump term for densities rho_i phi is

  phi(u)(rho Q^u)_i.

The transport contribution is

  -rho_i k(e_i/rho_i-1).grad phi
      = 2phi partial_i F
      = -phi(rho Q^u)_i.                                   (12)

The coordinate sum of grad F is zero, so the constant-vector part cancels. Hence
the two contributions cancel exactly. For compactly supported C^1 tests this is
an integration-by-parts proof of invariance. Smooth positive coefficients,
nonexplosion, and the characteristic/jump construction give uniqueness of the
corresponding forward evolution, so the normalizable density (4) is genuinely
invariant, not merely a formal candidate.

6. ERGODICITY AND INITIALIZATION: PRECISE CLAIM

The following argument establishes the stationary ergodic law and the almost-
sure occupation theorem for absolutely continuous initial controller laws.
It deliberately avoids an unverified stronger claim for every individual point.

The velocities v_i=k(e_i/rho_i-1) affinely span H, and zero lies in the relative
interior of their convex hull since sum_i rho_i v_i=0. Given u,u' and start/end
states, choose a directed walk that visits every state and ends at the desired
state. For sufficiently large prescribed duration T, u'-u can be expressed as a
positive combination of the velocities with total coefficient T. Assign these
positive dwell times among visits of the walk.

The map from dwell-time variables, subject to their sum T, to the final u has
rank n-1. The probability density of every such finite allowed jump sequence and
positive dwell times is strictly positive. Thus its image has an absolutely
continuous component with positive density near u'. Varying T over sufficiently
large integers proves irreducibility with respect to counting measure on S times
Lebesgue measure on H. The same construction is uniform on compact input/output
sets and provides local smoothing/minorization.

Standard irreducible Markov-process invariant-measure theory therefore makes
Psi_k unique and ergodic in its stationary law. One can alternatively identify
ergodicity by observing that a nontrivial invariant set would contradict the
positive transition component between positive-measure parts of H.

If an initial law nu has a density in u (with arbitrary state distribution),
then nu is absolutely continuous with respect to Psi_k, because phi>0 everywhere
and every rho_i>0. Its entire path law is absolutely continuous with respect to
the stationary path law, with density dnu/dPsi_k evaluated at Z_0. Birkhoff's
almost-sure time-average conclusion therefore transfers to nu:

  (1/T) integral_0^T 1{X_t=i}dt -> rho_i, a.s.                (13)

For example, draw any Gaussian on H at initialization and start X at a fixed
state. Neither sampling the unknown invariant controller density nor learning q
is needed for this asymptotic conclusion. Quantitative burn-in and a complete
all-deterministic-start Harris proof remain separate tasks.

7. EXACT EVENT SIMULATION AND OCCUPATION DISCREPANCY

While X=i, all outgoing rates share the same exponential time multiplier:

  a_ij=q_ij exp[(u_i-u_j)/2],
  A_i=sum_j a_ij,       b_i=k/(2rho_i),
  q_ij(t+s)=a_ij exp(b_i s).

For E~Exp(1), the next holding time and jump destination are

  tau = log(1+b_i E/A_i)/b_i,
  P(next=j)=a_ij/A_i.                                      (14)

Thus simulations need no time discretization or numerical event-root solve.
The simulator knows q as its plant model; the physical controller still uses
only the observed state and residence times.

Integrating (3) gives the pathwise exact identity

  hatrho_i(T)-rho_i
     = [rho_i/(kT)] [u_i(T)-u_i(0)].                         (15)

In stationarity, every u_i has finite variance, and therefore

  Var(hatrho_i(T))
     <= [4rho_i^2 Var_Psi(u_i)]/(k^2 T^2).                  (16)

This suppresses zero-frequency occupation fluctuations through feedback. It is
not an estimator for an unknown uncontrolled distribution: rho was given and the
controller changes the process. It is also not a proven end-to-end sample
complexity advantage, because (16) assumes stationary initialization and does not
account for burn-in or hardware costs.

8. EXACT STATIONARY PATH-KL COST AND NEAR-OPTIMALITY

The controlled X path is generally non-Markovian after forgetting u. This cost
RETAINS the controller initialization/record. The reference has base Q jump rates,
the SAME memory-update rule and initial joint law. Conditional on the record,
the jump-intensity likelihood ratio applies. If u(0) is hidden and marginalized
out, X-only KL could be smaller; equality of those rates is not assumed.
Under (4), X and u are independent, and the recorded relative-entropy rate is

  J_k = E_phi sum_{i != j}rho_i
          [q^u_ij log(q^u_ij/q_ij)-q^u_ij+q_ij].

The logarithmic term equals u.grad F. Integration by parts on the d=n-1
dimensional H gives

  E_phi[(u-u*).grad F] = kd/2,
  E_phi[grad_H F]=0.

All boundary terms vanish by the superexponential tails. Hence

  J_k = F(0)-E_phi F + k(n-1)/2.                            (17)

Convexity gives

  0 <= E_phi[F-F(u*)]
        <= E_phi[(u-u*).grad F] = k(n-1)/2.

Combining with the known optimum (9),

  0 <= J_k-I_Q(rho) <= k(n-1)/2.                            (18)

Thus the single-trajectory feedback has exact target occupation at finite gain
and approaches the known minimum path-KL cost as k decreases. This cost does NOT
include a physical implementation of measurement and memory. No claim of minimum
total energy, heat or power follows from (18) alone.

9. RANGE AND LIMITS

Known bounds q_min<=q_ij<=q_max on edges and rho_min>0 give, with
R_max=(n-1)q_max and c=rho_min q_min,

  osc(u*)<=2(n-1)log(R_max/c).                               (19)

Put d=n-1, a=1/[2(n-1)sqrt(n)], b=2c/k. Coercivity implies
F(u)>=c exp(a||u||_2). On the unit ball of H, F<=e R_max, so radial integration
and Z_k>=v_d exp(-2e R_max/k) give

  P_Psi(||u||_infinity>R)
    <= min{1, d exp(2e R_max/k)
         integral_R^infinity s^(d-1)exp[-b exp(a s)]ds}.       (20)

Extra radial powers bound moments and the constant in (16). This is computable
given the rate bounds; exp(a s)>=exp(a R)[1+a(s-R)] gives an explicit tail sum.
At controller range B, individual rates may be q_max exp(B), and controller speed
may be k/rho_min. The stationary tail bound is not a clipping-error or burn-in
theorem.

Unbounded range is essential to this EXACT finite-gain stationary law, even for
one fixed Q: phi has positive mass beyond every finite amplitude cap. Stationary
ergodicity gives an asymptotically positive time fraction beyond each such cap,
and the path-law absolute-continuity argument transfers this to AC starts. No
fixed saturation limit realizes the exact infinite-run law (4). A modified
bounded controller requires a separate theorem. This does not preclude bounded
POPULATION control, for which the Lyapunov sublevel is invariant; see the
addendum.

Counterexamples/limits: non-strongly-connected supports cannot transfer mass
between closed classes; zero rho_i requires infinite potentials; bounded controls
restrict target odds (two states have rho_1/rho_2=(b/a)exp(u_2-u_1)). Coarse hidden
labels sharing one potential cannot generally be separately regulated.

Equilibrium reweighting fails under drive: a 3-cycle with clockwise rates 4 and
counterclockwise rates 1 has uniform base stationary law, but for rho=(1,4,9)/14,
u_i=-log rho_i gives rho Q^u=(3,-12,9)/14, not zero.

The target rho is GIVEN; controlled variance suppression estimates no unknown
baseline distribution. All n resolved labels/potentials are counted. Source-only
Arrhenius changes, arbitrary load splitting, delay, and quantization are outside
the exact actuator proof. No total physical-power or engine conclusion is drawn.
Subsequent addenda prove bounded population actuation, explicit exponential
settling, and bounded shared-replica regulation over exponentially long finite
horizons after a computable burn-in. Finite-bit precision, bandwidth, delay and
an independently realized physical actuator remain implementation requirements.
