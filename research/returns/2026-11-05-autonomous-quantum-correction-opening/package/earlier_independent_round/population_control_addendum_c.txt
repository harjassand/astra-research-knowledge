1. Exact interface and invariant box

E is a strongly connected directed graph on n>=2 resolved states. Its UNKNOWN
positive rates obey known bounds 0<q_min<=q_ij<=q_max on E, and vanish off E.
Given a positive probability vector rho and k>0, use
  p'=pQ^u, u'_i=k(p_i/rho_i-1), q^u_ij=q_ij exp[(u_i-u_j)/2].
The sensor supplies the exact instantaneous distribution p. Initialize u0=0
and arbitrary p0. The gauge rho.u=0 is preserved. This is a deterministic
population plant, not automatically a finite noisy empirical ensemble.

Define r0=min rho_i, R0=(n-1)q_max, c=r0 q_min, and
  F(u)=sum_(i,j in E)rho_i q^u_ij,
  A=R0+(k/2)log(1/r0), B=2(n-1)log(A/c).
The joint Lyapunov V=F+(k/2)D(p||rho) satisfies
  V'=-(k/2)sum rho_i q^u_ij
       [r_i log(r_i/r_j)-r_i+r_j]<=0, r_i=p_i/rho_i.
This identity is proved in researcher_c.txt Section 4. Since V0<=A and a
directed max-to-min path gives F>=c exp[osc(u)/(2(n-1))], osc(u)<=B always.
The gauge then implies |u_i|<=B. The unique optimum u*, with rho Q^{u*}=0,
has the same bound since F(u*)<=F(0). Segments between u and u* stay in the box.

Choose a smooth scalar actuator clamp equal to identity on [-B-1,B+1] and
bounded in [-B-2,B+2]. Up to a first exit from the identity region the clamped
system equals the original; the invariant bound forbids that exit. Thus bounded
hardware realizes the EXACT population trajectory and p(t)->rho for all k>0.
Reachable edge rates are <=q_max exp(B), and the slew rate obeys
|u'_i|<=k max(1,1/rho_i-1). For a known bound |u_i(0)|<=B_init, replace R0 in A
by R0 exp(B_init). The initialization and exact response law are assumptions;
generic source-energy actuation, noisy sensing and finite precision are not
silently covered. No anti-windup assertion is needed outside the reachable box.

2. Explicit global exponential convergence

All norms below are Euclidean. Set x=p-rho, y=u-u*. Then
  grad F=-(rho Q^u)/2,
  x'=-2 grad F+xQ^u, y'=k diag(rho)^(-1)x.
Rows in the master equation are identified with coordinate vectors in dots.
Safe uniform constants on reachable points and their segments to u* are
  m=r0 q_min exp(-B/2)/[4n(n-1)], M=A/2,
  C=2A/r0, a=k r0 m.
They give Hess_H F>=mI, Hess F<=MI, ||Q^u||_2<=C, and V'<=-a||x||^2.

Proof of the constants: for v in H={rho.v=0}, ||v||^2<=n osc(v)^2. A directed
max-to-min path gives sum_E(v_i-v_j)^2>=||v||^2/[n(n-1)]. Apply this to
Hess F[v,v]=(1/4)sum rho_i q^u_ij(v_i-v_j)^2 using q^u_ij>=q_min exp(-B/2).
Convexity keeps F<=A on these segments. Since trace Hess F=F/2, its largest
eigenvalue is <=A/2, giving M. F<=A bounds every row out-rate by A/r0 and
every column incoming sum by A/r0, so absolute row/column sums are <=2A/r0.
This gives C. The rho-weighted activity is bounded by A. The actual p-weighted
activity and each actual edge rate are bounded by A/r0. Finally
D(ri||rj)>=r0(ri-rj)^2/2 for 0<=ri,rj<=1/r0. Use the path bound on r-1 in H
and ||r-1||>=||x|| in V' to obtain a. Boundary p is handled at t>0, where all
components become positive, followed by continuity at t=0.

Put E0=F-F(u*)+(k/2)D(p||rho), and choose
  eps=min{m/2,k/4,a/[2(k/r0+C^2/(4m))]}, W=E0+eps x.y.
Taylor, Pinsker, and D(p||rho)<=sum_i x_i^2/rho_i imply
  c_W(||x||^2+||y||^2)<=W<=C_W(||x||^2+||y||^2),
  c_W=min(m/4,k/8), C_W=max((M+eps)/2,k/(2r0)+eps/2).
The decisive strictification is
  (x.y)'=k sum_i x_i^2/rho_i-2y.grad F+y.(xQ^u)
     <=[k/r0+C^2/(4m)]||x||^2-m||y||^2,
using y.grad F>=m||y||^2 and Young's inequality. Therefore
  W'<=-(a/2)||x||^2-eps m||y||^2<=-lambda W,
  lambda=min(a/2,eps m)/C_W>0.
For all t>=0,
  ||p(t)-rho||^2+||u(t)-u*||^2 <= W0 exp(-lambda t)/c_W.
Since ||p0-rho||^2<=2 and ||u*||^2<=nB^2, W0<=C_W(2+nB^2), giving
  ||p(t)-rho||_1<=sqrt[8n C_W(2+nB^2)/k] exp(-lambda t/2).
A uniform computable settling time for accuracy delta is the positive part of
(2/lambda)log(sqrt[8n C_W(2+nB^2)/k]/delta). All design constants are independent
of the unknown q. They can be extremely poor in n, rate contrast and gain;
there is no established efficient practical advantage.

3. Impossibility without rate information; resource boundary

No fixed potential-difference cap works uniformly over unbounded unknown rate
ratios, even with arbitrary time-dependent feedback. For n=2 write d=u1-u2,
q12=a,q21=b and assume d(t)<=D. Then
  p1'>=b exp(-D/2)(1-p1)-a exp(D/2)p1.
Scalar comparison gives liminf p1>=b/[b+a exp(D)]. As b/a grows, this approaches
one and forbids a universal half-half target. The unavoidable target error is
at least the positive part of b/[b+a exp(D)]-rho1. The static optimum requires
d*=log[b rho2/(a rho1)]. A uniform settling time also needs an absolute rate
scale: multiplying all q by a tiny number defeats any fixed-time transfer at
fixed actuator amplitude from a concentrated population.

The exact population sensor is a serious resource. Replacing p by the empirical
fraction of a finite ensemble creates martingale noise and a different closed
loop. The deterministic invariant-box proof does not bound that controller
forever. For the single-path ECMC law, every finite cap is exceeded infinitely
often almost surely. Approximation over a finite horizon, or a saturated noisy
closed-loop stability theorem, must be proved separately. No partition oracle,
small-port many-body sampler, or free total-power advantage is implied.

5. A finite-ensemble finite-horizon implementation bound

A standard martingale/Gronwall operation removes the exact distribution sensor
for one certified finite-time approximate target. It gives a very poor but
finite resource bound, not an infinite-run noisy saturation theorem.

Use M particles whose conditional individual jump generator is Q^{chi(u)},
where chi is the componentwise 1-Lipschitz smooth clamp from Section 1. The shared
controller observes their empirical fractions P^M and uses
  u'=k(P^M/rho-1).
Initialize every particle in state1, u0=0. This matches the ideal p0=e1 exactly.
Let the fixed horizon T be chosen by Section 2 so that ||p(T)-rho||_1<=delta/2.
Write R=B+2, Qbar=(n-1)q_max exp(R), L=2Qbar+k/r0.
All individual total jump rates are <=Qbar, independently of controller range.

The empirical process has decomposition
  P^M_t=P^M_0+integral_0^t P^M_s Q^{chi(u^M_s)} ds+mathcal M_t.
Each jump changes the empirical vector by a vector of squared norm 2/M^2;
total event rate is <=M Qbar. The square-integrable martingale therefore obeys
  E||mathcal M_T||^2<=2Qbar T/M,
  E sup_(s<=T)||mathcal M_s||^2<=8Qbar T/M
by the Hilbert-space Doob inequality.

The generator has Euclidean norm <=2Qbar. Its difference at two clamp inputs is
bounded by 2Qbar times the Euclidean input difference: apply the mean-value
theorem to each rate and the absolute row/column sum bound. Consequently, in
the error norm ||P^M-p||_2+||u^M-u||_2, the deterministic drift has Lipschitz
constant <=L. Since chi(u)=u along the ideal path, Gronwall gives
  sup_(s<=T)[||P^M_s-p_s||_2+||u^M_s-u_s||_2]
     <=exp(LT) sup_(s<=T)||mathcal M_s||_2.
Thus the probability that this error exceeds eta is at most
  8Qbar T exp(2LT)/(M eta^2).
Taking eta=delta/(2sqrt(n)) and
  M>=32n Qbar T exp(2LT)/(alpha delta^2)
proves P(||P^M_T-rho||_1>delta)<=alpha. The initial fractions can also be any
exactly supplied empirical p0; an initial approximation error is amplified by
at most exp(LT).

All actuator outputs are bounded by R, virtual memory obeys
|u_i(t)|<=kT/r0 over this specified run, and only finite integer population
counts are sensed. This supplies a finite-amplitude, finite-ensemble, finite-
horizon theorem without knowing q. Continuous real precision and the exact
multiplicative response law are still explicit idealizations.

If an implementation produces additional combined drift error of norm at most
zeta, the same proof adds zeta T exp(LT) to the right side. This is a precise
finite-horizon approximation contract, not an assertion that a given physical
sensor/actuator satisfies it. No uniform long-time approximation is inferred.

6. Population control reaches the known optimal stationary path-KL cost

For the deterministic population controller, the controlled single-particle
law has prescribed time-dependent rates Q^{u(t)} and marginal p(t). Its path KL
against base Q with the same initial law is the integral of
  C(p,u)=sum_(i,j in E)p_i[q^u_ij log(q^u_ij/q_ij)-q^u_ij+q_ij].
The established Donsker--Varadhan stationary optimum at target rho is
I_Q(rho)=F(0)-F(u*). The controller converges to its optimizing generator without
knowing q, for every k>0. This does not make the endpoint optimum itself new.

An explicit transient bound follows from Section 2. Put
  G=(B+1)A/r0+R0,
  Kcost=(sqrt(n)G+AB)sqrt[W0/c_W].
Every row cost is bounded by G since row activity is <=A/r0 and the absolute
log tilt is <=B. Thus changing p to rho changes C by <=G||p-rho||_1.
For the u derivative, differentiating a log(a/q)-a gives a log(a/q) times
the derivative of log(a/q). Along [u*,u], its rho-average is bounded by AB
in Euclidean norm, because F<=A and |(v_i-v_j)/2|<=||v||_2. Therefore
  |C(p(t),u(t))-I_Q(rho)|<=Kcost exp(-lambda t/2),
  |KL(path_[0,T])/T-I_Q(rho)|<=2Kcost/(lambda T).
Use W0<=C_W(2+nB^2) for a q-independent constant. Finite-time averages can lie
below the stationary optimum while the target has not yet been established;
the claim is convergence to the known optimum, not a pointwise lower bound.
This mathematical cost omits physical sensing, memory and actuator power.

7. Finite-ensemble target and cost accuracy together

The bounded-clamp construction also has a finite resource contract for target
and average mathematical cost at once. Retain the FULL M-particle trajectory
record and shared controller; compare with M base-Q particles and the same
memory rule. The jump-intensity likelihood formula gives joint path KL divided
by M equal to the integral of E C(P^M_t,chi(u^M_t)). No independence after
feedback or equality for a hidden single-particle marginal is assumed.

On the globally clamped range R define
  G_R=Qbar(R+1)+R0, Lcost=sqrt(n)G_R+Qbar R.
Each instantaneous cost lies in [0,G_R]. A mean-value bound gives Lipschitz
constant <=Lcost in the combined error norm used in Section 5. If the uniform
tracking event has probability >=1-alpha0 and error <=eta, expected cost error
is <=Lcost eta+2G_R alpha0 at every time up to T.

Given target error delta, average-cost error epsilon and failure budget alpha,
choose T so the ideal population target is within delta/2 and its average cost
is within epsilon/2 of I_Q(rho), using Sections 2 and 6. Then take
  eta<=min(delta/(2sqrt(n)),epsilon/(4Lcost)),
  alpha0<=min(alpha,epsilon/(8G_R)),
  M>=8Qbar T exp(2LT)/(alpha0 eta^2).
This gives target error <=delta with probability >=1-alpha, and average FULL
recorded path-KL per particle within epsilon of the known optimum. All bounds
are computable without q, but M and time can be prohibitive. This is finite-
horizon approximate optimal regulation under the exact actuator, not an
efficient unknown-network sampler or an infinite-run finite-range theorem.
