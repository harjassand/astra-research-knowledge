RESEARCHER B — THERMAL LOCALIZATION OF A CLASSICAL CODE MEMORY
5 November 2026. Independent opening; subsequent hostile audit and incumbent comparison incorporated.

STATUS

The targeted capability was a passive, geometrically local, fixed-temperature classical memory protecting many logical bits. A constructive transfer operation survives the current internal audit: use logarithmically wide zero-field Ising ribbons, contract their ports exactly, and transfer a data-marginal bottleneck to the actual local physical dynamics. This is more than an energy-barrier argument. It is not a quantum-memory result, a demonstrated device, a novelty/priority finding, or a perturbatively stable memory.

The resulting conditional theorem uses an incumbent expander-code family. Its improved three-dimensional layout has N=O(n^(3/2) log^2 n) occupied physical binary spins, k=Omega(n) logical bits, and log(memory time)=Omega(n), with important initialization, symmetry, and readout assumptions. The enclosing spatial volume is O(n^(3/2) log^3 n); empty space is accounted for separately. Growing interaction strength is replaced by geometric redundancy. The basic wire contraction, LDPC-memory mechanism, and optimal three-dimensional graph routing are established predecessors. The October 2026 perturbatively stable 2D memory is a stronger incumbent on robustness. No major capability or practical resource advantage over the full incumbent set has been established.

The decisive unsolved operation is stability of these long thermal wires under arbitrary small local fields and crosstalk. Simple paired or symmetrically encoded width blocks do not neutralize an arbitrary perturbation: an adversary couples directly to their local order parameter, or produces an extensive bias after the proposed cancellation. A different gauge-protected information carrier would be needed. That operation has not been constructed here.

1. INPUT AND OPERATIONAL TARGET

Take binary linear codes C_n=ker H_n with n data bits and the following fixed constants:

  k_n>=r n; check weight <=q; bit degree <=d;
  |H_n e|>=kappa |e| whenever 0<|e|<=gamma n;
  a decoder corrects every error of weight <=a_dec n.

All constants are independent of n; q,d are finite. Select a>0 smaller than gamma, gamma/2, and a_dec, and put ell=floor(a n). Reducing a further causes no problem.

These are incumbent coding ingredients. A left-d-regular Tanner graph with |N(S)|>=(1-epsilon)d|S| for |S|<=gamma n has at least (1-2epsilon)d|S| unique neighboring checks: if U counts neighbors of multiplicity one, d|S|>=U+2(|N(S)|-U). Every unique neighbor is violated, proving confinement with kappa=(1-2epsilon)d. The rate is at least 1-m/n. Suitable expander codes have efficient bounded-distance decoders; see Sipser–Spielman, IEEE TIT 42 (1996), 1710–1722, https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/PAPERS/expandersIT.pdf . Explicit constant-degree lossless-expander constructions are an independent input, e.g. Capalbo–Reingold–Vadhan–Wigderson (2002), https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~zdvir/expanders/crvw01.pdf . No new expander construction is claimed.

The output stores an arbitrary codeword c in n designated data spins. During storage only a stationary local single-spin thermal Markov dynamics operates. Reading observes the n data spins and applies the incumbent decoder. No ongoing syndrome measurement, feedback, or refreshing is included.

Writing is an explicit external resource: data are prepared at c and hidden spins are prepared in the full Gibbs distribution conditional on those data. Approximate preparation with total variation error epsilon_prep adds epsilon_prep to the failure bound. A polynomial-time transfer-matrix sampling procedure is described below; autonomous local writing and arbitrary pure hidden-spin initialization are not proved.

2. EXACT WIRE AND CHECK CONTRACTION

A zero-field finite Ising wire has two terminal spins s,t and arbitrary internal spins y, with only ferromagnetic pair couplings. Spin reversal implies that its terminal partition function is

  Z_e(s,t)=A_e(1+c_e s t),

where A_e>0 and c_e is the terminal correlation in the free zero-field wire. This follows immediately by writing its two equal-spin and two opposite-spin partition sums as A_e(1+c_e) and A_e(1-c_e). It is an exact finite identity, not a low-temperature approximation.

For each check a, introduce one port t_e per incidence e=(i,a). Wires connect data s_i to these ports. Put a check factor exp(J_c product_e t_e), with fixed J_c>0. Summing all its ports gives exactly

  sum_t exp(J_c product t_e) product_e(1+c_e s_i t_e)
   =2^{q_a} cosh(J_c)[1+tanh(J_c)(product_e c_e)(product_i s_i)].

To prove this, write exp(J_c P)=cosh(J_c)(1+tanh(J_c)P) and sum the Fourier monomials in the port variables. Only the empty monomial and the monomial using every port survive. Consequently the data marginal is precisely

  pi_D(s) proportional to exp(sum_a K_a product_{i in a}s_i),
  K_a=atanh[tanh(J_c) product_{e in a}c_e].

The entire hidden partition function has been integrated. There is no assumption that a typical microscopic wire is defect-free.

For a target K_0>0, choose a fixed integer J_c>K_0 and a fixed c_min<1 satisfying

  tanh(J_c)c_min^q >= tanh(K_0).

If every wire has c_e>=c_min, then every K_a>=K_0. The target accuracy 1-c_min is a fixed constant, not an accuracy shrinking with n.

This contraction is established Ising-wire methodology. Qiu–Zoller–Li, PRX Quantum 1, 020311 (2020), Sec. III.2, derives an exact thermal wire construction with wire strength growing logarithmically with length: https://arxiv.org/html/2008.00006 . The candidate operation here trades that growing strength for logarithmic ribbon width and then proves a memory statement for physical local dynamics. It does not claim the contraction itself as new.

3. WHY THIN WIRES FAIL, AND A FULL RIBBON BOUND

For a length-L 1D Ising chain with fixed exponent coupling j, c_e=(tanh j)^L. Thus a bounded-strength thin-wire subdivision loses its intended check interaction at fixed nonzero temperature. Ground-state equivalence alone cannot repair this.

Use instead the zero-field rectangle R=[0,L]x[0,2w], with free boundary, nearest-neighbor exponent coupling j, endpoints u=(0,w), v=(L,w), L>=2w and w>=4. Attach each external terminal to its corresponding endpoint by one additional bond of coupling j. These two terminal bonds are genuine constant-degree bottlenecks. Their contribution is included exactly:

  c_e=(tanh j)^2 <sigma_u sigma_v>_R.

In particular, width alone cannot make the port correlation tend to one at fixed j. We choose a sufficiently large but fixed j to make its nonzero endpoint error floor smaller than the fixed target tolerance.

Set q_f=1-tanh j and z=4q_f<1. Define

  E_end(j)=1000 sum_{m>=1}(m+1)^2 z^m
          =1000 z(4-3z+z^2)/(1-z)^3,
  E_long(j,L,w)=1000(L+w+1) z^{floor(w/2)}/(1-z).

RIBBON LEMMA.

  c_e >= 1-2q_f-E_end(j)-E_long(j,L,w).

PROOF. The Edwards–Sokal expansion with bond parameter p=1-exp(-2j) gives <sigma_u sigma_v> as the random-cluster probability that u and v are connected. For q_cluster=2, every conditional bond-open probability is at least p/[p+2(1-p)]=tanh j. Sequential conditioning couples the bond set above independent Bernoulli bonds with this parameter. It therefore suffices to bound the Bernoulli disconnection probability.

Split the exterior dual face into the upper and lower boundary arcs from u to v. Failure of a primal u–v connection supplies a simple closed-bond dual crosscut joining those arcs. Using the ordinary dual with one undivided exterior vertex would obscure the following necessary geometry.

If the crosscut's two ends are on the same left or right side, they straddle the corresponding terminal. A length-m path then begins within O(m) boundary distance of that terminal. A safe upper bound for all such rooted paths is 1000(m+1)^2 4^m. Otherwise its endpoints lie on different sides, or one is on top/bottom, and its length is at least w-O(1). With w>=4 and L>=2w, using the weaker cutoff floor(w/2) is safe. There are at most O(L+w) boundary roots, and at most 4^m path choices from a root; 1000(L+w+1)4^m is a conservative count. A simple length-m closed path has probability q_f^m. Union bounds give E_end+E_long. The two extra terminal bonds multiply the correlation by (tanh j)^2 >=1-2q_f. This proves the lemma. The constants intentionally overcount and are not performance estimates.

The Fortuin–Kasteleyn/Edwards–Sokal identities are standard; the argument above supplies the part needed here rather than assuming a macroscopic ordered phase automatically transmits between point ports. The primary coupling paper is Edwards–Sokal, Phys. Rev. D 38 (1988), 2009–2012, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.38.2009.

For epsilon=1-c_min, choose a fixed INTEGER j so that 2q_f+E_end(j)<epsilon/2. Then choose w so that E_long<epsilon/2. Since the longest embedded wire will have L<=C sqrt(n) w, such a w exists with w=O(log(n+2)). It can be found effectively by increasing even w until the displayed finite inequality holds, using C sqrt(n) w as the length upper bound. The layout below already makes every wire at least 2w long. All couplings remain fixed as n grows.

4. GEOMETRY AND ALL PHYSICAL RESOURCES

PRIMARY ROUTING INPUT. Eades–Symvonis–Whitesides, Discrete Applied Mathematics 103 (2000), 55–87, Theorem 2, gives an intersection-free orthogonal integer-grid drawing of every v-vertex graph of maximum degree six. With s=ceil(sqrt(v)), each edge has at most seven bends and length at most 16s-10, and the box dimensions are (3s+2) by 5s by (8s-3). Author-hosted primary text: https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~sue/papers/eades-sym-whi.pdf . Its drawing definition forbids crossings and overlaps of edge interiors. These path-length and bend bounds supply the control absent from a bare scalar-thickness statement. No new routing theorem is claimed.

RIBBON LAYOUT LEMMA. Fix Delta. Every v-vertex graph of maximum degree Delta has the following realization for each integer w>=4. Every original edge has its own abstract rectangular ribbon [0,L_e]x[-w,w], with 2w<=L_e<=C_Delta sqrt(v) w. Its two middle endpoint spins are at the corresponding original vertex clusters. The union of ribbons has a binary-spin embedding in the cubic integer lattice with fixed finite interaction range, fixed density, and fixed local degree, all independent of v,w. It uses O_Delta(v^(3/2)w^2) spins inside a box of volume O_Delta(v^(3/2)w^3). The degree bound Delta is fixed.

PROOF, INCLUDING PORTS AND FRAMING. Replace each original degree-D vertex by a rooted binary tree with D leaves, maximum degree three, and at most 2D+1 vertices. Attach each original incidence to its own leaf. The resulting graph has v'=O_Delta(v) vertices and maximum degree three, so the primary theorem applies. This is only a routing construction. No helper-tree vertex, equality constraint, or additional check is placed in the Hamiltonian.

For an original incidence e=(i,a), take the drawn helper-tree path from root i to its leaf, the drawn incidence edge, and the drawn helper-tree path from its other leaf to root a. This is a simple curve using O_Delta(1) drawn edges. Its length is O_Delta(sqrt(v)) and its number of bends is O_Delta(1). At most Delta such curves use any helper-tree edge. Distinct unit axis-aligned grid segments that do not meet have distance at least one: if parallel, an orthogonal coordinate differs by an integer; if perpendicular, either the third coordinate differs by an integer or an endpoint is at integer distance at least one. Proper grid drawing also prevents a nonincident route from passing through a helper vertex. Thus every unit-size box meets O_Delta(1) curve pieces, counting shared helper segments with their original-incidence multiplicities.

Scale all coordinates by Cw for a sufficiently large fixed C, for example 1000 before further Delta-dependent microscopic packing. Every nonzero straight segment is now at least 1000w long. Smooth every right-angle bend inside its 40w neighborhood with a circular arc of radius 20w. Nonconsecutive portions of any one original curve remain separated by a multiple of w. Different curves can coincide on shared helper segments or meet near helper vertices, but their multiplicity is bounded by a constant depending only on Delta. Their spins stay distinct: no equality or interaction is introduced at an overlap.

Give each smoothed curve c(t) a continuous unit normal n(t), using arc length t. On each circular arc parallel-transport the normal, with curvature at most 1/(20w). On a long straight piece rotate the normal over a subinterval of length at least 100w to the desired normal for the next bend. A rotation through at most pi suffices; choose its derivative at most 1/(10w), stationary near its ends. This explicitly frames the interval as a surface strip.

Map the rectangle by F(t,k)=c(t)+k n(t), |k|<=w. On a bend the longitudinal derivative has norm between 19/20 and 21/20. On a twisting straight piece its projection along the straight axis is one and its norm is at most two. The transverse derivative has norm one, orthogonal to the longitudinal derivative. A circular strip has a uniformly bounded inverse in radial/angular coordinates; a twisting straight strip has t recovered by projection onto its straight axis. These give uniformly bounded local grid density and edge lengths. Reparameterize to integer longitudinal length L_e=ceil(length(c)); the derivative changes by a fixed factor only. The abstract spin graph remains EXACTLY the original rectangular grid. Bends, twists, and overlaps with independent ribbons therefore leave its Ising partition function unchanged.

Rounding grid images to integer cells leaves at most M_Delta=O_Delta(1) spins per cell: only a bounded number of strip pieces meet a cell, and each has the displayed bounded inverse density. Assign those spins distinct locations in a fixed-size microscopic cubic array and scale cell coordinates by that array's pitch. Every spin is then a distinct binary variable at an integer-lattice site. Interaction ranges increase by a fixed factor only. Independent labels at coinciding ribbons are a counted constant spatial overhead, rather than an uncounted growing alphabet.

Every incident ribbon's central endpoint F(0,0) is at its original root, and likewise at its other end. The data spin, its finitely many attached ribbon endpoints, and all q check-port spins therefore occupy the fixed microscopic root array. The q-body check has fixed-radius support. A ribbon's entire transverse endpoint line can extend a distance w and overlap other incident ribbons with the bounded multiplicity already counted. No check ports spread a distance w apart, and no thermally unproved tapered channel is used.

There are O(v) original edges, O_Delta(sqrt(v)w) longitudinal sites per ribbon, and 2w+1 spins per cross-section. The occupied-spin count is O_Delta(v^(3/2)w^2). The scaled routing box has side lengths O_Delta(sqrt(v)w), giving enclosing volume O_Delta(v^(3/2)w^3). Smoothing, root arrays, and transverse extent change these only by constant factors. This proves the lemma.

Apply it to the Tanner graph. Remove empty checks; then m<=dn and v=n+m=O(n). Thus

  N=O(n^(3/2)w^2)=O(n^(3/2)log^2 n) occupied binary spins,
  V_box=O(n^(3/2)w^3)=O(n^(3/2)log^3 n) enclosing lattice volume.

If every site of the box must contain a physical spin, fill unused sites with uncoupled binary spins and count O(n^(3/2)log^3 n) spins. They do not influence any data marginal. The layout is three-dimensional and has fixed finite interaction range; it is not a binary nearest-neighbor-only claim. A simpler crossbar gives the earlier O(n^2 log^2 n) slab count, but is not used for this improved exponent.

At beta=1 take physical bond energy j(1-s s') and check energy J_c(1-product t_e). With fixed integer j,J_c, elementary term energies belong to {0,2j,2J_c}; all Boltzmann weights are positive. Combining terms on coincident supports still gives one fixed finite set because density and degree are bounded. Checks retain bounded body weight q. Their ports occur within a fixed-radius physical neighborhood. The layout is spatially programmed and is NOT translation invariant. Decoupling crossing coordinates is exact in the model and is a physical assumption, not demonstrated hardware.

5. ACTUAL DYNAMICAL LIFETIME, NOT AN ENERGY-BARRIER INFERENCE

Select fixed K_0 so that

  2K_0 kappa a > H_2(a),
  H_2(a)=-a log a-(1-a)log(1-a).

The exact data marginal in Section 2 has all K_a>=K_0. For any codeword c and error e with |e|<=gamma n,

  pi_D(c+e)/pi_D(c)
   =exp[-2 sum_{a violated by e}K_a]
   <=exp(-2K_0 kappa |e|).

Let B_c contain every physical configuration whose DATA spins lie within Hamming radius ell of c, regardless of hidden spins. Let S_c be the data shell at exactly ell. Only data-spin flips can leave B_c. For any continuous-time single-spin dynamics with stationary full Gibbs distribution pi and flip rate per data spin at most r_max,

  Q(B_c,B_c^c)<=n r_max pi(S_c).

Initialize nu_c=pi(. | data=c). If J_t counts exits from B_c, stationarity gives E_pi J_t=t Q(B_c,B_c^c). Path-measure domination by 1/pi_D(c) then yields

  P_nu_c(first exit from B_c by time t)
   <=t n r_max pi(S_c)/pi_D(c)
   <=t n r_max binom(n,ell) exp(-2K_0 kappa ell).

The proof does not require hidden degrees to evolve faster than data degrees, or a Markov approximation to the data marginal. It also does not require reversibility once stationarity and the rate bound hold. Local Glauber heat-bath dynamics supplies an explicit such process. Since ell/n approaches a, the bound is <=t n r_max exp(-c n) for some fixed c>0 and all sufficiently large n.

Until exit, bounded-distance readout returns c. Therefore the whole k>=r n-bit message is recovered with failure probability at most epsilon_prep+t n r_max exp(-c n). In particular, memory time at any fixed target error is exp(Omega(n)), and all logical bits have this joint guarantee. Independent per-logical-bit failure bounds are not being multiplied without justification.

The exact same conclusion holds at every fixed beta>=1 by ferromagnetic monotonicity of the wire correlations and monotonicity of tanh(beta J_c). Writing cost and constants can depend on that fixed beta.

The stationary-flux argument is established Markov-chain methodology, also explicitly used by the October 2026 incumbent. The contribution of the present calculation is that the set B_c is defined by an EXACT contracted data marginal and an explicitly preparable conditional distribution, so the hidden wire entropy has not been discarded.

6. INITIALIZATION, READOUT, AND COST LIMITATIONS

With all data clamped to c, hidden degrees factor across checks: each check gadget consists of at most q ribbons joined by one port parity factor. Sum its 2^q port assignments, then sample each ribbon conditional on its two endpoints. A longitudinal transfer matrix has dimension 2^(2w+1)=n^O(1). Forward/backward sampling therefore computes a conditional hidden sample to total variation epsilon_prep in time polynomial in n and log(1/epsilon_prep), with a polynomial whose exponent depends on the fixed width coefficient and couplings. Exponentially small epsilon_prep requires only O(n) precision bits, still polynomial. Encoding c can use the incumbent encoder, or polynomial-time linear algebra. Readout uses n physical observations plus the code decoder.

This is an algorithmic preparation description, not an autonomous local writing protocol. The theorem does not cover starting every hidden spin in a chosen ground-state assignment. Comparing that point mass to equilibrium can cost exp(O(N)), destroying the useful bound. Nor does a stationary Gibbs marginal specify a physical bath: single-spin, stationary, bounded-rate dynamics is an explicit assumption. Ideal data-spin readout is assumed; measurement noise has not been included. No stochastic spectral gap or fast total mixing is asserted.

The rate in occupied physical spins is at least Omega(1/(sqrt(n) log^2 n)), rather than a positive constant. In terms of occupied spins the guaranteed tradeoff is

  k=Omega(N^(2/3)/(log N)^(4/3)),
  log t_mem=Omega(N^(2/3)/(log N)^(4/3)).

Counting the entire enclosing volume V instead gives

  k, log t_mem=Omega(V^(2/3)/(log V)^2).

These follow directly from N<=C n^(3/2)log^2 n, V<=C n^(3/2)log^3 n and n<=O(N),O(V); padding to an exact asymptotic size is unnecessary. Couplings j,J_c and check body weight q are fixed but can be large. No optimized constants, laboratory materials, fabrication tolerance, wall-clock lifetime, or practical advantage are known.

7. MATCHED INCUMBENTS AND WHETHER ANY CAPABILITY IS NEW

Hong–Guo–Lucas already prove constant-rate classical LDPC passive memory with nonlocal connectivity: https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10599v3 . Our localization spends a superlinear spatial overhead and loses physical constant rate. Lin–Wills–Hsieh, Theorem 1.4, already obtain in D=3 geometrically local classical codes with k=Omega(N^(2/3)), distance Omega(N), energy barrier Omega(N^(2/3)), and soundness Omega(N^(-1/3)): https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16104v2 . Those are not a fixed-temperature retention theorem; their graph-subdivision embedding Theorem 6.1 is explicitly labeled Upcoming. Our layout instead imports the complete primary ESW routing theorem and proves its ribbon thickening. No improvement in their zero-temperature capacity/barrier exponent is claimed. Here logarithms buy a fixed-temperature channel bound and physical dynamical retention for the stated initialization. Whether the same composition already appears elsewhere remains open.

Qiu–Zoller–Li already give thermal port contraction and O(n^2) bounded-degree three-dimensional layouts, spending log n coupling strength rather than log-width redundancy. ESW and its 1990s VLSI predecessors already give the O(n^(3/2)) routing exponent. Neither the exact contraction nor the graph-drawing exponent is new here. The limited constructive gain over this note's earlier crossbar is their fully accounted combination with finite-temperature ribbons and the physical stationary-flux bound.

The strongest robustness comparator is Balasubramanian–Davydova–Lin–Thorngren, arXiv:2610.22548v1: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22548v1 . Theorem 1 proves bounded-local 2D thermal memory stable under small arbitrary local Hamiltonian perturbations, with thermal-sector encoding. Section 1.1.3's multibit outline gives log lifetime=N^(alpha(1-theta)); K~N^theta is inferred from the independent early gauge-generator counts, rather than stated as an exact tradeoff in Theorem 1. Equation (4.63) explicitly counts 3ell-3 Tanner edges per replaced edge. Bounded degrees imply N_(2n)=Theta(((3ell-3)^2)^n); Theorem 7's 2^n witness scale gives alpha=log 2/[2 log(3ell-3)]. At ell=7 this is about .120, and theta=1/2 gives about .060. Our three-dimensional zero-field powers are (2/3,2/3) up to logs. Dimensions, robustness, and initialization differ materially, so this is not overall superiority or a full independent validation of that preprint.

In a matched exactly symmetric, exactly decoupled model, the candidate gives an explicit bounded-strength thermal localization route with favorable lifetime/capacity exponents. The underlying components are standard, and a search did not establish absence of the same composition in prior work. No generic novelty is claimed. Under the more meaningful requirement of arbitrary small fabrication fields/crosstalk, the October incumbent supplies a capability our construction does not. A civilization-scale or practical breakthrough has not been demonstrated.

As simpler baselines, independent 2D Ising patches with the same number of stored bits and sites have a weaker usual interface-barrier scaling; full 3D Ising cubes improve it but still spend volume per bit. That comparison is a mathematical scaling benchmark only. The gauge-protected comparator above is the more consequential boundary.

8. DECISIVE REPAIR ATTEMPT: ARBITRARY SMALL WIRE FIELDS

The exact contraction assumes every wire has global spin-flip symmetry. A general field makes Z_e(s,t)=A exp(h_L s+h_R t+K_e s t), introducing endpoint biases as well as changing K_e. Positive-field Ising clustering is an obstruction to treating a long equilibrated wire as a symmetry-neutral channel; no uniform finite-rectangle bias-stability theorem is supplied here. Increasing width alone does not cancel a bulk bias, and our proof gives no n-independent tolerance to arbitrary wire fields or crossing couplings.

Try two antiferromagnetically paired rails, encoding a sign by (+,-) versus (-,+). A uniform identical field cancels in the ideal subspace, but arbitrary rail fields h_1,h_2 contribute (h_1-h_2)s. The adversary chooses h_1=epsilon,h_2=-epsilon and produces a linear bias at every longitudinal position. Finite pairing strength also permits violations, so exact cancellation outside the ideal subspace would be unjustified. Adding finitely many rails merely permits the adversary to choose each local field aligned with the difference of the two code patterns; its total energy splitting grows with the number of distinguishing sites.

More generally, if two deterministic width-block patterns differ on m sites, fields of magnitude epsilon produce energy splitting 2epsilon m by choosing the sign at each differing site. A symmetric random encoding can cancel selected low-order moments, but cancellation of ALL arbitrary local perturbations requires equality of their local marginals, not just a zero mean. Independent random gauge masks with fixed finite local penalties do not provide that equality automatically: if the encoded bit appears in a bounded-support observable, an allowed perturbation can couple to it. If the bit is only a nonlocal parity, a local, thermally reliable port must be rebuilt and proved; the original ribbon theorem no longer applies.

There is a more promising parity-mask repair, which can be tested exactly. A block with ideal weight exp(J s product_i sigma_i), after summing masks in arbitrary one-site fields h_i, has a logical bias proportional to tanh(J) product_i tanh(h_i), exponentially small in block width. But its interaction has growing body weight and growing geometric support. Replace it by bounded-weight constraints J t_(i-1) sigma_i t_i on a chain, with t_0=+ and t_w=s. Then the zero-field readout correlation is exactly

  E[s product_i sigma_i]=(tanh J)^w,

because the constraint signs are independent after a change of variables. Thus the parity readout tends to a fair coin at fixed J. Integrating the masks in fields gives effective chain factors 1+tanh(J)tanh(h_i)t_(i-1)t_i and an endpoint bias (tanh J)^w product_i tanh(h_i). The masking works for these selected fields, but reliable transmission/readout has been lost. Restoring constant readout correlation in this chain requires J of order log w, hard constraints, or a further fault-tolerant/gauge construction. Fields on the auxiliary parity spins and a constant-size physical port must also be included in any repair. Five finite widths were checked by exact rational enumeration.

Thus the simple deterministic-block and bounded-strength parity-chain repairs fail constructively. This is not a theorem forbidding gauge-protected classical memories—the October incumbent demonstrates that they exist. It isolates the missing operation: a neutral nonlocal carrier whose local port/readout and thermal confinement survive arbitrary local bias at fixed bounded strength. No such compiler was obtained here. The route should not be advertised as robust physical memory until that operation is supplied.

9. CROSS-AUDIT OF RESEARCHER A'S FINITE POISSON CERTIFICATE

From the supplied statement, A's core certificate is valid under an autonomous unital local quantum Markov semigroup: ||A+L(B)-aI||<=r implies time-averaged expectation error <=r+2||B||/T. It remains valid under remote driving only when all actual generator terms touching supp(B) remain fixed, or their action on B is explicitly bounded. Finite-collar implementation is therefore meaningful.

If ALL stationary infinite states agree on A, weak-star compactness of time-averaged states gives uniform Cesaro convergence on A. Integrating B_T=int_0^T(1-t/T)P_t(A-aI)dt gives the approximate Poisson solution; graph-norm Lieb–Robinson truncation is the required extra hypothesis for a finite certificate. Local block-Pauli bounds imply a diamond-close replacement channel after discarding the random-time register, with a dimension factor such as sum_P epsilon_P <=(d^2-1)epsilon.

This prepares a randomized-time finite block, not a fixed-time globally prepared many-body state or a fast sampler. Completeness supplies no recursive uniform certificate size or time bound in hard families. Agreement of all stationary dynamical states is a separate premise from all-Gibbs uniqueness; identifying stationary states with DLR states requires an additional theorem. Its robustness to genuinely remote driving is operationally broader than our exact-symmetry memory theorem; our theorem instead addresses long retention after a specifically supplied initialization. Neither result alone implies the other.
