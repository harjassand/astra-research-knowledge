PRINCIPAL INDEPENDENT INITIAL QUESTION — 5 November 2026

Chosen before exchanging competitors: lift scalar positive-loop locality to operator-relative boundary control, to certify a 2D quantum parent-Hamiltonian gap. Concrete target: square-lattice spin-2 AKLT. Recent primary comparator Kastoryano arXiv:2609.27993v1, Theorem 1 and Corollary 3.1, has a common-response criterion and a hexagonal application. It is not automatically applicable to square geometry.

Constructed local cap: for p(n)=product_j(1-a_j dot n), |a_j|<=1, A=int p and M=int n p, |M|<=m A/(m+2). Proof: R=Pi_sym tensor_j(I-a_j dot sigma) Pi_sym is positive on spin j=m/2; coherent resolution gives A=TrR/(m+1), M=Tr(RJ)/[(m+1)(j+1)], and |Tr(RJ)|<=j TrR. This is known Berezin/coherent-state machinery, not a novelty claim.

For a degree-d AKLT core with one effective leaf, r=(d-1)/(d+1) bounds its affine classical polarization. The effective leaf spin (d-1)/2 gives another r in positive coherent quantization. Thus the local relative comparison has epsilon=r^2 and two-endpoint amplification (1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon)=(d^2+1)/(2d): 5/3 for d=3, 17/8 for d=4, conditional on the correct effective regional geometry.

Missing operation: a uniform rooted OPERATOR response bound for square crossings and soft 3/5 contacts. A scalar closed-loop bound is insufficient. The density diag(1,eps) and X=sigma_x give relative norm eps^(-1/2) despite ordinary norm one. No square gap or new preparation capability is proved. Short boundary paths may require a nonperturbative one-dimensional reference resummation. This opening should lose to a complete stronger competitor until its global operator bound is supplied.
