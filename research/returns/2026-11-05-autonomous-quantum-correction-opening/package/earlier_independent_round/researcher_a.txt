1. OPERATION AND INCUMBENT BOUNDARY

Question: Can specified local quantum dynamics provide a finite protocol for
predicting/preparing its local stationary state, from an unknown global input,
without a supplied mixing rate or spectral gap?

Answer: yes for RANDOMIZED-TIME local preparation, with a finite matrix
certificate simultaneously giving spatial support, observation time and model
error tolerance. Search local Hermitian B and rational a,r with

  ||A+L(B)-aI||<r.                                             (1)

Every initial state obeys

  |T^-1 integral_0^T rho_t(A)dt-a|<=r+2||B||/T.                 (2)

Local generator drift of per-site cb-norm eta adds eta|K|||B||, K=supp(B).
Arbitrary time-dependent driving disjoint from K and arbitrary finite-device
boundaries outside its interaction collar are allowed. Soundness needs no
light-cone relation between device diameter and observation time.

If ALL infinite stationary states agree on A, certificates exist at every
precision. Uniqueness of the full stationary state is sufficient. For a complete
Pauli basis of a queried block, discarding a uniform random stopping time gives
a local replacement channel close in DIAMOND norm, hence also erases correlation
with an untouched external reference. A finite rational time grid suffices.

The static optimization in (1) is already the dual of local stationary-state
SDPs. Poisson functions, auxiliary-function duality and uniform Cesaro convergence
are established machinery. The earned synthesis is their complete finite-local
operationalization with time, boundaries, calibration errors and reference-system
guarantees. Historical novelty of this synthesis is unestablished. No new general
SDP paradigm or practical speedup is claimed.

2. FORMAL INPUT

The quasilocal algebra is the norm completion of finite tensor matrix algebras
on Z^d, with site dimension q<infinity. The Heisenberg generator is

  L=sum_Z ell_Z,
  ell_Z(C)=i[H_Z,C]+sum_alpha[V_Zalpha^* C V_Zalpha
                                 -{V_Zalpha^*V_Zalpha,C}/2].   (3)

Assume supplied finite range a>=1, a uniform finite bound on term incidence per
site and cb-norm J, and uniformly computable local matrices. A finite list of
exact rational/algebraic matrices and translates is a clean finite input.
Translation invariance is unnecessary if an effective local list/bounds is given.
These hypotheses give a strongly continuous UCP semigroup T_t; local C are in
its generator domain with L(C) the finite sum (3). Primary matches are in §9.

Let S_L be the set of ALL stationary states. It is nonempty and weak-star
compact: average an arbitrary orbit and take a compact limit. The prediction
promise for A is only omega(A)=a_* for every omega in S_L. Agreement of only TI
stationary states is insufficient for this proof. Uniqueness of Gibbs states
does not silently identify the stationary set of an arbitrary physical sampler.

3. FINITE CERTIFICATE SOUNDNESS

Set R=A+LB-aI and ||R||<=r. Let an actual time-dependent finite-device generator
L'_t generate UCP maps S_{0,t}, with sup_t||(L'_t-L)B||<=m. The propagator identity
dS_{0,t}(B)/dt=S_{0,t}(L'_t B) gives the OPERATOR identity

  T^-1 integral_0^T S_{0,t}(A-aI)dt
   =-[S_{0,T}(B)-B]/T
      +T^-1 integral_0^T S_{0,t}[R+(L'_t-L)B]dt.

Contraction therefore gives

  ||T^-1 integral_0^T S_{0,t}(A)dt-aI||<=r+2||B||/T+m.          (4)

Taking any initial state proves (2), uniformly over correlated global inputs.
The 2||B|| can be replaced by osc(B), by shifting B by a scalar. For a stationary
state the endpoint term vanishes.

If L'_t-L=sum_Z Delta ell_Z(t), with
  sup_t sup_x sum_{Z contains x}||Delta ell_Z(t)||_cb<=eta,
terms disjoint from K annihilate B, so m<=eta|K|||B||. The cb-norm controls tensor
extensions; an unamplified local superoperator norm alone would not suffice.
Hamiltonian error E contributes at most 2||E||. Replacing one jump V by V+E
changes its dissipator by cb-norm at most 4||V||||E||+2||E||^2.

Let H(K) be K union all interaction supports meeting K. Any finite device
containing H(K), retaining the specified terms meeting K, has the SAME L(B).
Additional well-defined Lindblad driving outside K, even time dependent and
arbitrarily strong, does not affect (4). This is an exact generator identity,
not an extrapolated finite-size estimate.

The conclusion is about averaged EXPECTATIONS, equivalently a randomly stopped
channel. It is not a pathwise bound on a continuously measured quantum trajectory.

4. COMPLETENESS: THE DECISIVE LOCALITY STEP

THEOREM. For local Hermitian A and real a_* the following are equivalent:
 (i) every stationary state gives A expectation a_*;
 (ii) ||M_T(A)-a_*I||->0, M_T=T^-1 integral_0^T T_t dt;
 (iii) inf_{B local Hermitian}||A+LB-a_*I||=0.

Proof (i)->(ii). Otherwise choose T_n->infinity and states rho_n whose averaged
A expectation stays epsilon away. The averaged states bar rho_n have a weak-star
convergent subsequence. For fixed s,C,
 |bar rho_n(T_s C)-bar rho_n(C)|<=2s||C||/T_n.
The limit is stationary, a contradiction. For Hermitian operators the norm is
the supremum of absolute expectation over all states. This compact-state Cesaro
argument is standard; it is not claimed as a new ergodic theorem.

Proof (ii)->(iii) WITHOUT an assumed local-core theorem. Fix T. In a finite box
V_R let L_R retain terms wholly within it, T_t^R=e^{tL_R}, and define

 B_R=integral_0^T(1-t/T)T_t^R(A)dt.
 L_R(B_R)=M_T^R(A)-A,              ||B_R||<=T||A||/2.           (5)

The second identity follows by integration by parts. Thus

 A+L(B_R)=M_T^R(A)+(L-L_R)(B_R).                              (6)

For fixed T, Barthel–Kliesch Theorem 2 gives M_T^R(A)->M_T(A) in norm. Only terms
crossing the boundary contribute to (L-L_R)B_R. Their Theorem 1, applied to each
crossing ell_Z as a local Liouvillian probe, gives
 ||ell_Z T_t^R(A)||<=C_A||ell_Z||_cb||A||exp(vT-dist(Z,supp A)/a),
uniformly for 0<=t<=T. There are O(R^{d-1}) crossing terms, at distance R-O(1).
Consequently

 ||(L-L_R)B_R||<=C'_A T J(1+R)^{d-1}exp(vT-R/a)->0.           (7)

The constants have effective upper bounds from the input. First choose T using
(ii), then R using (6)-(7). This proves (iii) without applying an unbounded
generator to an uncontrolled norm limit. Finally (iii)->(i) follows by applying
a stationary state: omega(LB)=0 for every local B. QED.

5. ALGORITHM AND DUALITY

Enumerate rational Hermitian B on finite boxes and rational a, and dovetail
strict tests ||A+LB-aI||<epsilon. The theorem and rational density guarantee
termination under all-stationary agreement. Every found certificate is valid
regardless of that promise. Algebraic inputs allow exact finite positive-
definiteness checks; effective real inputs allow rational matrix enclosures and
strict rational margins. Correct effective coefficient names are an assumption,
not a noncomputable oracle or a self-verifying program.

One may instead enumerate integer T, radii R and finite approximations of (5).
No uniqueness proof, spectral gap or mixing modulus must be supplied.

More generally, the same proof establishes
 sup_{omega stationary}omega(A)=inf_{B local}lambda_max(A+LB),
 inf_{omega stationary}omega(A)=sup_{B local}lambda_min(A+LB).   (8)
For the first identity, nearly maximizing averaged states have stationary compact
limits; (6)-(7) then approximate the optimum. The second follows by negating A.

At fixed K, (8) is exactly the dual of a density-matrix SDP on H(K), imposing
stationarity for every B on K. Its dual is strictly feasible by B=0 and a large
scalar upper bound. A finite-time/drift version is the ordinary SDP

 minimize u+(2/T+eta|K|)b,
 subject to A+LB<=uI,             -bI<=B<=bI.                 (9)

It immediately bounds the actual randomized-time expectation. Lower bounds use
-A. This is a useful regularization of an incumbent dual, not a new SDP method.

6. COMPLETE-REFERENCE LOCAL PREPARATION AND FINITE CLOCKS

For a block of k qubits let D=2^k and P_1,...,P_m be its nontrivial Pauli basis,
m=D^2-1. Suppose ||P_j+LB_j-a_jI||<=r_j. For a physical target tau with Pauli
coefficients t_j, the averaged local channel Phi_T obeys

 ||Phi_T^*(P_j)-t_jI||<=e_j,
 e_j=r_j+|a_j-t_j|+2||B_j||/T+eta|K_j|||B_j||.
 ||Phi_T-Replacement_tau||_diamond<=sum_j e_j.                 (10)

Proof of the diamond bound: for any ancilla and any trace-class X, expand the
output difference as D^-1 sum_j P_j tensor R_j. Its identity coefficient is zero
by trace preservation. Trace-norm duality gives ||R_j||_1<=e_j||X||_1, because its
dual pairs X with (Phi_T-Replacement_tau)^*(P_j) tensor a norm-one ancilla matrix.
Since ||P_j||_1=D, the triangle inequality proves (10), uniformly in ancilla size.
This proof does not assume the input or reference is uncorrelated or classical.

If all stationary states have the same block marginal tau, then |a_j-t_j|<=r_j.
Choose residuals small, then finite T large and eta>0 small. The certificate search
is complete for arbitrarily accurate randomized-time local replacement.

The classical target is computable: H=(I+sum_j a_jP_j)/D is within sum_j r_j of
tau in trace norm. Normalize H's positive part to obtain a density matrix sigma;
its distance to tau is <=2sum_j r_j. Rounded numerical output adds a controlled
error. No pure/full-rank assumption is needed. Finite qudits use a fixed operator
basis with an explicit basis constant in place of the Pauli sum.

The stopping-time record must be DISCARDED; conditioning on a known time can
retain input information. This is not deterministic-time mixing.

An ideal continuously uniform real is unnecessary. If T=N Delta, stop at a
uniform integer grid point 0,Delta,...,(N-1)Delta. A uniform local bound
||L'_t(P_j)||<=G_j gives Riemann-average error <=G_j Delta/2. Add
(Delta/2)sum_j G_j to (10). These G_j involve only terms meeting the query;
arbitrary remote driving causes no hidden system-size factor. The integer can be
sampled with finite expected random-bit cost. Independent repetitions permit
ordinary bounded-outcome statistical estimates. No single-trajectory quantum
concentration theorem is asserted.

7. EXACT RATIONAL INTERACTING EXAMPLE

On a qubit chain take bulk H=sum_x X_x+J sum_x Z_xZ_{x+1}, J=1/256, with local
jumps V_x=2|0><1|_x. Only terms meeting the central site zero must be fixed.
Any finite chain containing {-1,0,1} is allowed, with arbitrary additional
time-dependent Lindblad driving disjoint from zero. Set

 tau=(I-(2/3)Y+(2/3)Z)/2=[[5/6,i/3],[-i/3,1/6]], det(tau)=1/36>0,
 B_X=X/2,        B_Y=Y/3-Z/6,       B_Z=(Y+Z)/6,
 a_X=0,          a_Y=-2/3,          a_Z=2/3.

Exact computation gives R_j=P_j+LB_j-a_jI:
 R_X=-J Y_0(Z_-1+Z_1),              ||R_X||=2J,
 R_Y=(2J/3) X_0(Z_-1+Z_1),         ||R_Y||=4J/3,
 R_Z=(J/3) X_0(Z_-1+Z_1),          ||R_Z||=2J/3,
 sum_j||R_j||=1/64,
 sum_j||B_j||=1/2+(sqrt(5)+sqrt(2))/6<9/8.                  (11)

At T=1000 and eta<=1/1000,
 ||Phi_T-Replacement_tau||_diamond
   <1/64+(2/1000+1/1000)(9/8)=19/1000.                     (12)
For spacing Delta=1/10000, N=10000000 grid points, the local derivative bound
sum_j G_j<17 includes this eta. The discrete protocol has diamond error below
 19/1000+17/20000=397/20000<1/50.                            (13)

Thus a finite randomized-time protocol prepares this local mixed state to <2%
diamond error for all unknown inputs/references, admitted boundaries/driving and
calibration errors. No uniqueness theorem or numerical time extrapolation is
needed for this example. Exact symbolic checks in certificate_demo_a.py verify
the residual identities/eigenvalues, positivity, norms and rational bounds;
certificate_demo_a_results.json records all checks passing.

This is a soundness illustration, not a surprising physical regime: strong
damping and weak interaction are standard favorable conditions, and the incumbent
local SDPs can derive these same static constraints.

8. COUNTEREXAMPLE TESTS, COSTS AND LIMITS

Observable agreement does not imply instantaneous relaxation: for a closed
qubit H=Z/2, A=X, B=-Y gives A+LB=0, but initial |+> has X mean cos(t). The exact
checker verifies the identity. This tests the useful weaker observable-agreement
premise, not a statement about globally unique finite Lindblad steady states.

For one qubit damping at rate lambda>0, time-averaged excited population from
|1> is (1-e^-lambda T)/(lambda T). Random-time reset costs order 1/(lambda epsilon);
known-rate instantaneous reset costs order lambda^-1 log(1/epsilon). Averaging
can be slower when good mixing constants are already known.

For stationary states disagreeing on A by g, every certificate has r>=g/2.
Small finite-tolerance certificates can still be useful without deciding global
uniqueness. Unknown/nonlocal terms meeting K need an explicit action-on-B bound;
calling them remote does not make them harmless. Gibbs uniqueness/clustering is
not silently promoted to a dynamical uniqueness or rapid-mixing hypothesis.

B on N sites has q^(2N) real parameters; residual PSD tests have dimension
q^|H(K)| and bit costs depending on coefficient precision. Dense matrix work and
the incumbent SDP have the same exponential-in-volume scale at matched level.
Exhaustive certificate enumeration is a completeness algorithm, not a practical
solver. A k-qubit block uses 4^k-1 scalar certificates. Physical time and calibration
tolerance follow from the displayed B norms. No general polynomial bound on
support, norm, search time, observation time or eta follows from uniqueness.

PROVED: finite soundness, local-drift/boundary/time-dependent transfer, complete
local Poisson approximation, promise-terminating computation, reference-safe
replacement, finite clocks and the rational <1/50 example.

ESTABLISHED MACHINERY REUSED: stationary SDP duality, auxiliary/Poisson functions,
compact-state Cesaro convergence and dissipative Lieb–Robinson bounds.

CONDITIONAL PRACTICAL VALUE: a moderate-support/moderate-norm certificate in a
model lacking a useful mixing theorem would earn a usable new prediction or
preparation guarantee. No difficult incumbent-beating instance was demonstrated.
Quantitative certificate growth and implementation of the calibrated dissipators
are the decisive missing operations. Exact sampling, instantaneous mixing,
efficient Gibbs sampling, phase preservation, and quantum advantage remain open
or outside scope. This is not a new undecidability corollary.

12. DECISIVE FOLLOW-UP: FAST LOCAL RESET AND SLOW GLOBAL MEMORY

Can witnesses have constant support/norm with a genuinely coupled arbitrarily
slow spectator? Yes, but the exact positive example is also solved more strongly
by matched LOCAL mixing. The useful further result is a sharp obstruction when
the slow sector is locally distinguishable. No incumbent-beating engine emerged.

12.1 Exact positive example

For two qubits A,B, gamma>0,J!=0, take
 L_delta(C)=i[JZ_AZ_B,C]
    +(gamma/4)sum_{P=X,Y,Z}(P_A C P_A-C)
    +delta(X_B C X_B-C), delta>=0.                           (19)
The ZZ interaction is entangling and its Hamiltonian evolution does not commute
with central depolarization on the full algebra. Relevant local subspaces close:
 L X_A=-gamma X_A-2JY_AZ_B,
 L(Y_AZ_B)=2JX_A-(gamma+2delta)Y_AZ_B,
 L Y_A=-gamma Y_A+2JX_AZ_B,
 L(X_AZ_B)=-2JY_A-(gamma+2delta)X_AZ_B,
 L Z_A=-gamma Z_A,               L Z_B=-2delta Z_B.          (20)

With D=gamma(gamma+2delta)+4J^2, zero-residual witnesses are
 B_X=[(gamma+2delta)X_A-2JY_AZ_B]/D,
 B_Y=[(gamma+2delta)Y_A+2JX_AZ_B]/D,
 B_Z=Z_A/gamma.                                             (21)
Their supports have at most two qubits and norms <=1/gamma uniformly in delta,J.
For X,Y, use anticommutation and
 D^2-gamma^2[(gamma+2delta)^2+4J^2]
   =4J^2(gamma^2+4gamma delta+4J^2)>=0.
The averaged local-reset error is <=6/(gamma T), with drift <=5eta/gamma.

But the same invariant subspaces give a stronger matched mixing result. Write
T_t(X_A)=a(t)X_A+b(t)Y_AZ_B; its norm is sqrt(a^2+b^2), and (20) gives
d(a^2+b^2)/dt<=-2gamma(a^2+b^2). Likewise for Y, and Z is immediate. Thus the
DETERMINISTIC-TIME local channel has diamond error <=3exp(-gamma t) from I_A/2.

The global mode Z_B decays at exactly 2delta. At delta=0 it is conserved and
I_A/2 tensor every Z_B-diagonal state is stationary. For delta>0 the stationary
state is uniquely I_AB/4: the Hilbert–Schmidt Dirichlet identity forces a
stationary density to commute with all A Paulis and X_B. It is I_A tensor sigma;
Hamiltonian stationarity then forces [Z_B,sigma]=0, hence sigma scalar. The
global gap is <=2delta, tending to zero, while local reset remains fast.
Diagonal spectator networks commuting with Z_B can be appended without changing
(20). This earns local/global separation but no advantage over local mixing.

12.2 General resource obstruction

Let L0 have stationary states omega_+,omega_- whose expectations of Hermitian A
differ by g>0. For L_delta=L0+delta D, any certificate
||A+L_delta B-aI||<=r obeys, by applying omega_+-omega_-,
 g<=2r+2delta||D(B)||,
 ||D(B)||>=(g/2-r)/delta.                                    (22)
If ||D||_cb<=c this forces ||B||>=(g/2-r)/(c delta). With only per-site drift
bounds it forces |supp B|||B||=Omega(1/delta). This constrains ALL local B, of
any support, not a chosen ansatz or finite SDP level.

12.3 A sharp exact interacting obstruction

Take
 L_delta(C)=i[X_A+Z_AZ_B,C]+D_{2|0><1|_A}(C)
                        +delta(X_B C X_B-C),                (23)
where D_V(C)=V^*CV-{V^*V,C}/2. At delta=0 the two stationary states are
 omega_+=tau_+ tensor |+Z><+Z|,
 omega_-=tau_- tensor |-Z><-Z|,
 tau_+/-=[I +/- (2/5)X -(2/5)Y +(4/5)Z]/2.
Their X_A means are +/-2/5, so g=4/5. All sixteen Pauli stationarity equations
are checked exactly. For delta>0 the stationary density is unique and has X_A
mean zero. The determinant of the generator's 15-by-15 nonidentity Pauli block is
 -32768delta(delta+1)^2(3delta+5)^2(delta^2+4delta+5),          (24)
nonzero for every delta>0. Finite Lindblad stationary-state existence plus this
rank computation proves uniqueness; the exact stationary equations give X_A=0.
Z_B again has eigenvalue -2delta, so global relaxation is arbitrarily slow.

Since ||Ad_XB-I||_cb<=2, ANY residual-r certificate for X_A satisfies
 ||B||>=(2/5-r)/(2delta),                 r<2/5.             (25)
The order AND leading coefficient at r=0 are sharp. Set Q=delta^2+4delta+5.
The two-qubit Hermitian operator
 B_delta=Q^-1[(1/delta)Z_B+(delta^2+3delta+3)X_A/2
                    -(delta+2)Y_AZ_B/2+Z_AZ_B/2]            (26)
obeys X_A+L_delta B_delta=0. Since delta B_delta->Z_B/5 in norm,
||B_delta||=1/(5delta)+O(1), matching (25)'s leading coefficient.
The lower bound applies even if arbitrarily many extra sites are allowed in B:
the perturbation still acts only on this one spectator, with cb-norm <=2delta.

There is a physical, not merely computational, obstruction. Duhamel and
contraction give ||T_t^delta(A)-T_t^0(A)||<=2delta t||A||. From omega_+, X_A's
expectation stays >=2/5-2delta t. Reaching error <=1/5 from the stationary zero
therefore needs t>=1/(10delta). For uniform time averages the same argument gives
T>=1/(5delta). Actual local reset is slow; a fast witness here would be false.

local_reset_a.py verifies the fast witnesses, global slow mode, conditional
stationary states, determinant, zero stationary X_A, and (26)'s leading term.
local_reset_a_results.json records every exact symbolic check passing.

Conclusion for this lane: complete finite operational certification was earned,
but the decisive matched test found no advantage beyond stationary SDP/local-
mixing incumbents. It did produce a sharp support-independent slow-sector
obstruction: in the small-perturbation setting of (22), bounded-norm accurate
certificates require the query to be insensitive to the limiting stationary
sectors at the requested accuracy.
A practical advance requires a difficult model with a usable witness where known
local-mixing methods do not already give a stronger guarantee.

13. FINAL INDEPENDENT CHECK OF C'S DETERMINISTIC POPULATION EXTENSION

This is a cross-audit after independence was released, not an independently
originated construction. It concerns the exact master equation p'=pQ^u with
u'=k(p/rho-1), not the empirical distribution of finitely many random particles.
Assume n>=2, a known strongly connected directed support, 0<qmin<=qij<=qmax on it,
rho_i>0, sum rho_i=1, u(0)=0, and arbitrary probability p(0). Edge rates qij
need not be known individually and need not obey detailed balance. They must
respond exactly as qij^u=qij exp[(ui-uj)/2]. The population p is sensed exactly.

Put F=sum rho_i qij^u, E=F-F*+(k/2)D(p||rho), x=p-rho, y=u-u*, with the gauge
rho.u=0. Direct differentiation gives the Euclidean identities
 grad F=-(rho Q^u)/2,
 x'=-2grad F+xQ^u,             y'=k D_rho^-1 x,
 E'=-(k/2)sum rho_i qij^u [ri log(ri/rj)-ri+rj], ri=pi/rho_i.
The formulas hold for t>0; strong connectivity gives p_i(t)>0, and continuity
handles a boundary initial p. On 0<=ri,rj<=1/rhomin, the scalar bracket is at
least (rhomin/2)(ri-rj)^2. This is the Bregman bound for s log s, whose second
derivative is 1/s>=rhomin on that interval.

Let A=(n-1)qmax+(k/2)log(1/rhomin). Since E+F* decreases and F(0)<=
(n-1)qmax, F(u(t))<=A. Each directed edge satisfies
 ui-uj<=2log[A/(rhomin qmin)].
A directed path of at most n-1 edges from a maximum to a minimum therefore
gives osc(u)<=B=2(n-1)log[A/(rhomin qmin)]. The gauge implies |ui|<=B.
The same bounds hold at u*, and on their connecting segment by convexity.
A smooth actuator clamp equal to the identity on [-B-1,B+1] never activates:
the first-exit argument uses the unmodified bound strictly inside its identity
region. This is a bounded actuator theorem for this deterministic plant.

On that box valid constants are
 m=rhomin qmin exp(-B)/[4n(n-1)],
 C=2(n-1)qmax exp(B),              a=k rhomin m.
The Hessian identity is Hess F=(1/4)sum rho_i qij^u (ei-ej)(ei-ej)^T.
For v with rho.v=0, a directed max-to-min path gives sum_E(vi-vj)^2 >=
||v||^2/[n(n-1)]; hence Hess F>=m on this gauge space. The row and column
absolute sums of Q^u are <=C, so its Euclidean operator norm is <=C.
Since D_rho^-1 x lies in the same gauge space, the dissipation gives E'<=-a||x||^2.
There is no missing projection or metric factor in this step.

For W=E+epsilon x.y,
 (x.y)'=k sum xi^2/rho_i-2y.grad F+y.(xQ^u).
Strong convexity and Young's inequality give
 W'<=-[a-epsilon(k/rhomin+C^2/(4m))]||x||^2-epsilon m||y||^2.
Any positive epsilon at most min{m/2,k/4,a/[2(k/rhomin+C^2/(4m))]} yields
 W'<=-(a/2)||x||^2-epsilon m||y||^2,
 W>=(k/8)||x||^2+(m/4)||y||^2.
A useful upper comparison is
 W<=Ux||x||^2+Uy||y||^2,
 Ux=k/(2rhomin)+epsilon/2,        Uy=A/4+epsilon/2.
Indeed D(p||rho)<=sum xi^2/rho_i, and trace(Hess F)=F/2<=A/2 along the segment.
Thus W'<=-lambda W with lambda=min{a/(2Ux),epsilon m/Uy}>0. This proves the
claimed global exponential convergence for every fixed gain, with conservative
explicit constants. I found no sign, gauge, or coercivity error.

Scope: an N-particle empirical distribution has jumps and a martingale term;
it does not obey p'=pQ^u pathwise. The preceding finite clamp proof does not
give an all-time finite-N guarantee, a noisy/delayed/finite-precision sensing
theorem, or the bounded single-trajectory ECMC process. Those are new problems.
