# Independent first-principles question (checkpoint)

Question: Can a stable scientific subsystem coupled to arbitrarily many unresolved dissipative modes be predicted at the level of its experimentally accessible response and equilibrium fluctuations by an observable-scale passive memory model, with certified finite-time error and explicit acquisition/execution cost?

Representation to retain: the unresolved bath is a positive spectral measure / memory kernel, including its slow spectral mass. Current Markov closures discard this memory; unconstrained fitting can destroy passivity and long-time stability.

Hardest operation: acquire enough low-frequency bath information to distinguish a genuinely absent slow mode from a weakly coupled, extremely slow mode. A theorem that hides this in exact moments, an equilibrium oracle, or a spectral-gap assumption would not answer the question.

I am first deriving the mechanistic and information-theoretic limits before nearest-literature comparison. Candidate useful endpoint is a certified response simulator, not microscopic trajectory reconstruction. Whether this is sufficiently new/substantial will depend on a strong comparator challenge.
