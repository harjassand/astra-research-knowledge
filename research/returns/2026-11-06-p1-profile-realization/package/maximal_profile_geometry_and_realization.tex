\begin{abstract}
A complete maximal-information profile records a channel's maximal $f$-divergence on every faithful input pair with every finite reference. Prior Astra result N1698 identifies equality of such profiles with equality of ordered normalized moments and supplies a rational greatest-channel compiler. This manuscript supplies three further constructions at that stated scope.

First, an unequal pair of rational channels admits an exactly compiled, well-conditioned separable state pair that separates the channels in one common orientation for every nonaffine operator-convex $f$, with gap at least $f''(1)\mu/2$ for an explicit positive rational $\mu$. Second, a finite family has a computable affine profile dimension $p-1$ and a panel of exactly $p-1$ scalar probes that is simultaneously sufficient for every such $f$; the same rational affine relations apply to all reference sizes and input pairs. Third, a signed affine combination of complete profiles is physically realizable precisely when its moment functional is positive and its null space is a two-sided ideal. Equivalently, its density in the generated finite $C^*$-algebra has a central support projection. The criterion produces either a rational CPTP channel or a finite negative-norm or null-ideal certificate in polynomial dense-input bit complexity. An explicit qubit family exhibits a positive, nonphysical boundary profile and separates reference-free information from complete information.

These are finite mathematical constructions. They do not determine ordinary quantum capacity, supply a free divergence or optimization oracle, prove practical scalability, or achieve the programme's civilisation-level consequence anchor. The research programme remains open. Exact regression checks accompany the manuscript; they are not independent proof verification or a novelty determination.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions and rational coordinates}\label{sec:setup}

Let $\Phi_j:\M_a\to\M_{b_j}$, $1\le j\le k$, be completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) linear maps. Their input-major Choi matrices are explicit over $\Q(i)$. Set
\[
 \Omega_j=\Phi_j(I_a),\qquad e_j=\rank\Omega_j,
 \qquad T_j(X)=\Omega_j^{-1/2}\Phi_j(X)\Omega_j^{-1/2},
 \qquad \tau_j(Z)=\frac1a\Tr(\Omega_j Z).
\]
All inverses involving $\Omega_j$ are taken on its support. Thus $T_j$ is unital completely positive into $\M_{e_j}$, and $\tau_j$ is a faithful state, usually \emph{not} a trace. Define
\[
 T(X)=\bigoplus_j T_j(X),\quad
 \Alg=C^*(T(\M_a))\subseteq\bigoplus_j\M_{e_j},\quad
 D=\dim_{\C}\Alg,\quad N=\sum_j e_j^2.
\]
Then $D\le N$. The coordinate maps from $\Alg$ to the $j$th summand are denoted $\pi_j$.

Choose a rational Hermitian traceless basis $H_1,\ldots,H_q$ of the input, $q=a^2-1$, with $\norm{H_h}\le1$: diagonal differences and the real and imaginary off-diagonal matrix units suffice. The unit together with these letters generates the input matrix algebra as a vector space. For a word $w=h_1\cdots h_n$, write
\[
 W_{w,j}=T_j(H_{h_1})\cdots T_j(H_{h_n}),\qquad
 m_j(w)=\tau_j(W_{w,j}).
\]
The unit moment is $1$ and the one-letter moments are $\Tr(H_h)/a=0$ for every channel. All nontrivial differences therefore begin at length at least two.

\subsection{Complete profiles and effective supports}

For faithful positive matrices $S,P$, define the maximal $f$-divergence
\[
 \Dhat_f(S\Vert P)=\Tr\left[P^{1/2}
 f(P^{-1/2}SP^{-1/2})P^{1/2}\right].
\]
Here $f:(0,\infty)\to\R$ is operator convex. The complete profile $\Pi_f(\Phi)$ is the function of \emph{all} finite $r$ and faithful density pairs $\sigma,\rho\in\M_a\otimes\M_r$ given by
\[
 \Pi_f(\Phi)(\sigma,\rho)=
 \Dhat_f((\Phi\otimes\id_r)(\sigma)\Vert(\Phi\otimes\id_r)(\rho)).
\]
If $\rho\ge\epsilon I$, then its output is at least $\epsilon\Omega\otimes I_r$, so every such output is faithful on the fixed effective support. No finite value of $f(0+)$ is required.

Write the noncommutative perspective as
\[
 \mathcal P_f(S,P)=P^{1/2}f(P^{-1/2}SP^{-1/2})P^{1/2}.
\]
Perspective congruence gives the useful identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perspective}
 \Pi_f(\Phi_j)(\sigma,\rho)
 =a(\tau_j\otimes\Tr_r)
 \mathcal P_f((T_j\otimes\id_r)(\sigma),(T_j\otimes\id_r)(\rho)).
\end{equation}
The expression inside the functional belongs to $\Alg\otimes\M_r$ when evaluated jointly. The second tensor factor carries the ordinary, unnormalized matrix trace.

\begin{remark}[A reference-system correction that matters]
For a replacer $\Phi(X)=\Tr(X)\omega$, the complete profile is
$\Dhat_f(\sigma_R\Vert\rho_R)$, not the constant $f(1)$ in general. It equals $f(1)$ for a common reference marginal, or without a reference. When $a=1$, all channels have this same reference-only complete profile.
\end{remark}

These are maximal/RLD profiles. They are not measured/SLD divergences, Umegaki relative entropy, capacities, or a channel divergence defined by optimizing over different inputs for different channels.

\subsection{Square-root-free word closure}

For each word use raw coordinates
\[
 B_{w,j}=\Omega_j^{1/2}W_{w,j}\Omega_j^{1/2}.
\]
The unit is $\Omega_j$, a letter is $\Phi_j(H_h)$, and multiplication is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rawproduct}
 B_{uv,j}=B_{u,j}\Omega_j^+B_{v,j},\qquad
 m_j(w)=\frac1a\Tr B_{w,j}.
\end{equation}
Here $\Omega_j^+$ is rational. For example, if $P_j$ is the rational orthogonal support projector,
\[
 \Omega_j^+=(\Omega_j+I-P_j)^{-1}P_j.
\]
A rational column basis $C$ for $\ran\Omega_j$ gives $P_j=C(C^*C)^{-1}C^*$.

Starting with the unit, retain an independent word whenever left multiplication by a generator leaves the current span. At most $D$ words are retained and at most $qD$ transitions need be considered. The retained words have length at most $D-1$. Indeed, if the span of words of length at most $n$ equals that at length at most $n+1$, it is already invariant under every generator. This is a closure computation, not enumeration of all $q^{D}$ words. Rational independence tests and ordered traces give exact moment relations.

N1698 supplies the equality implication: unequal complete profiles yield a retained word with unequal moments. The elementary argument above explains the short-word and rational computational interface used in the additional constructions.

\section{A universal separable inequality witness}\label{sec:witness}

\begin{theorem}[One physical witness for every nonaffine maximal $f$]\label{thm:witness}
Given two explicit rational CPTP maps with a common input, polynomial dense-input bit computation either decides their complete maximal profiles equal, or returns rational faithful separable states $\sigma,\rho$ on $\C^a\otimes\C^r$, an orientation $(E,F)$ of the two maps, and a rational $\mu>0$, such that
\begin{align*}
 &r\le e_E^2+e_F^2,\\
 &\frac{I}{2ar}\preceq\rho,\sigma\preceq\frac{3I}{2ar},\\
 &\Tr_R\rho=\Tr_R\sigma=I_a/a,\\
 &\Pi_f(E)(\sigma,\rho)-\Pi_f(F)(\sigma,\rho)
 \ge \frac{f''(1)}2\,\mu
\end{align*}
for every nonaffine operator-convex $f:(0,\infty)\to\R$. The states have an explicit rational product-state mixture with $O(D)$ terms. Their reference marginals are also common. Their bit lengths, and $\log(1/\mu)$, are polynomially bounded in the dense input length.
\end{theorem}

The equality branch uses the governing finite moment criterion. The standalone witness checker verifies the unequal branch. The supplied pairwise equality output is only status metadata; it is not advertised as a separately checkable equality certificate. The finite-family checker below reconstructs word closure for that purpose.

\subsection{Local RLD information controls every maximal \texorpdfstring{$f$}{f}}

For $S=P+\Delta$ of the same trace as $P$, put
\[
 \chi(S,P)=\Tr(\Delta P^{-1}\Delta).
\]
An operator-convex $f$ has the representation~\cite{HiaiMosonyi}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loewner}
 f(x)=f(1)+f'(1)(x-1)+c(x-1)^2
   +\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{(x-1)^2}{x+s}\,d\lambda(s),
\end{equation}
where $c\ge0$, $\lambda$ is positive, and
\[
 a_f:=\frac{f''(1)}2=c+\int\frac{d\lambda(s)}{1+s}<\infty.
\]
Nonaffinity is equivalent to $a_f>0$. This representation and its use on a compact positive spectral interval accommodate $f(0+)=+\infty$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:sandwich}
If $-\eta P\preceq\Delta\preceq\eta P$, $0\le\eta<1$, and $\Tr P=\Tr S=1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
 \frac{a_f}{1+\eta}\chi(S,P)
 \le\Dhat_f(S\Vert P)-f(1)
 \le\frac{a_f}{1-\eta}\chi(S,P).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The nonlinear perspective associated with~\eqref{eq:loewner} is
\[
 c\Delta P^{-1}\Delta+
 \int\Delta(S+sP)^{-1}\Delta\,d\lambda(s).
\]
The affine term has trace $f(1)$. Since
$(1+s-\eta)P\preceq S+sP\preceq(1+s+\eta)P$, inverse order bounds the integrand between the corresponding scalar multiples of $\Delta P^{-1}\Delta$. Also
$1+s\pm\eta$ lie between $(1\pm\eta)(1+s)$ in the needed directions. Taking traces proves the claim; the quadratic term obeys the same looser bounds.
\end{proof}

Complete positivity transports the relative-order bound from an input pair to each output pair. Consequently an exactly evaluated quadratic contrast can be converted into a witness for all $f$ with constants that do not require calling an $f$-evaluator.

\subsection{A short word becomes a physical path probe}

Let $w=A H_1\cdots H_m B$ be a distinguishing word, with endpoints $A,B$ in the Hermitian traceless basis. For an original word of length $n\ge3$, take $m=n-2$. For a word $AB$ of length two, insert the middle letter $I_a$ and take $m=1$. In both cases set $r=m+2=\max\{3,n\}$. Use reference vertices $0,\ldots,m$ and one spare vertex $s$.

For a map $T_j$, define $L_j$ and $Y_{j,\pm}$ by applying $T_j$ to the input factor of
\begin{align}
 L&=\sum_{h=1}^m H_h\otimes
 (|h-1\rangle\langle h|+|h\rangle\langle h-1|),\label{eq:L}\\
 Y_\pm&=A\otimes(|s\rangle\langle0|+|0\rangle\langle s|)
 \ \pm B\otimes(\zeta|s\rangle\langle m|+\bar\zeta|m\rangle\langle s|),\label{eq:Y}
\end{align}
where $\zeta\in\{1,i\}$. Unital positivity gives $\norm{T_j(H_h)}\le1$, whence
$\norm{L_j}\le2$ and $\norm{Y_{j,\pm}}\le\sqrt2$. The same bounds hold before applying a channel.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:states}
 \rho=\frac{I-tL}{ar},\qquad
 \sigma_\pm=\rho+\frac{uY_\pm}{ar},\qquad
 g_{j,\pm}=\frac1r(\tau_j\otimes\Tr_r)
  Y_{j,\pm}(I-tL_j)^{-1}Y_{j,\pm}.
\end{equation}
Then $\chi(\Phi_{j,R}\sigma_\pm,\Phi_{j,R}\rho)=u^2g_{j,\pm}$. All $g$ values are rational and can be computed from raw output matrices without normalizing square roots.

Let $R_j=(I-tL_j)^{-1}$. Reference-block multiplication gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contrast}
 g_{j,+}-g_{j,-}
 =\frac4r\Re\left[\bar\zeta\,
 \tau_j(T_j(A)(R_j)_{0m}T_j(B))\right].
\end{equation}
The two cross terms that survive the reference trace occupy the spare--spare block and are adjoints of one another. This is the reason for~\eqref{eq:contrast}; no cyclic permutation under the nontracial $\tau_j$ is used.

The unique length-$m$ path from $0$ to $m$ yields
\[
 (R_j)_{0m}=t^m T_j(H_1)\cdots T_j(H_m)+\mathcal R_j,
 \qquad
 \norm{\mathcal R_j}\le\frac{(2t)^{m+1}}{1-2t}.
\]
Write $\delta=m_E(w)-m_F(w)$, choose $\zeta$ so that
$d=\abs{\Re(\bar\zeta\delta)}=\max\{|\Re\delta|,|\Im\delta|\}>0$, and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witness-t}
 t=\frac{d}{2^{m+4}}.
\end{equation}
Since $d\le2$, $t\le1/16$. The ratio of the two-channel remainder to the leading magnitude is at most
\[
 \frac{2(2t)^{m+1}}{(1-2t)dt^m}
 =\frac1{4(1-2t)}\le\frac27<\frac12.
\]
Thus the contrast of the two sign contrasts is nonzero. At least one sign, with an orientation of $E,F$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Delta}
 \Delta_g:=g_E-g_F\ge\frac{dt^m}{r}>0.
\end{equation}
The compiler computes both signs exactly and chooses one. A temporary $u=1/8$ may be used to evaluate quadratic divergences; dividing by $u^2$ recovers the same $g$.

\subsection{Uniform orientation, margins, and separability}

Put $S_g=g_E+g_F\ge\Delta_g$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final-u}
 u=\frac{\Delta_g}{16S_g},\qquad
 \eta=2u\le\frac18,\qquad
 \mu=\frac{u^2\Delta_g}{2}.
\end{equation}
The input pair obeys
\[
 \norm{\rho^{-1/2}(\sigma-\rho)\rho^{-1/2}}
 \le\frac{\sqrt2 u}{1-2t}\le2u=\eta.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich} now gives
\begin{align*}
 \Pi_f(E)-\Pi_f(F)
 &\ge a_fu^2\left(\Delta_g-\frac{\eta S_g}{1-\eta}\right),\\
 \frac{\eta S_g}{1-\eta}
 &=\frac{\Delta_g}{8-\Delta_g/S_g}\le\frac{\Delta_g}{7}.
\end{align*}
This is stronger than $a_f\mu$. The norm bounds also give the displayed state-conditioning bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}.

For explicit separability, let $\omega=I_a/a\otimes I_r/r$. If $H,K$ are traceless Hermitian contractions, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sep}
 Q_{\epsilon}(H,K)=\frac12\left[
 \frac{I+H}{a}\otimes\frac{I+\epsilon K}{r}
 +\frac{I-H}{a}\otimes\frac{I-\epsilon K}{r}\right]
 =\omega+\frac{\epsilon H\otimes K}{ar},\quad\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}.
\end{equation}
These are rational product-state mixtures. Each path edge and each of the two endpoint edges contributes such a term. The inserted identity letter, when present, uses the single product state $I_a/a\otimes(I_r+\epsilon K)/r$. The total absolute coefficient weight is
\[
 mt+2u\le\frac{m}{2^{m+3}}+\frac18\le\frac3{16}.
\]
Use the remaining nonnegative weight on $\omega$. This supplies a concrete mixture, rather than a separability test. Tracing out the reference gives $I_a/a$. Endpoints are traceless, so the two states also share their reference marginal; it is $I_r/r$ except for the inserted identity case.

All retained word lengths, matrix sizes, inversions, and rational trace operations are polynomially bounded. A nonzero rational $d$ of polynomial height can be exponentially small, but its encoding is still polynomial. Equations~\eqref{eq:witness-t}--\eqref{eq:final-u} retain this property, including the bound on $\log(1/\mu)$. This completes Theorem~\ref{thm:witness}.

\section{Exact affine geometry and a minimal common probe panel}\label{sec:panel}

\begin{theorem}[Signed-linear moment criterion]\label{thm:linear}
Let real $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ satisfy $\sum_jc_j=0$. The following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sum_jc_j\Pi_f(\Phi_j)=0$ on every faithful input pair and every finite reference, for one fixed nonaffine operator-convex $f$;
\item the same identity holds for every operator-convex $f$;
\item $\sum_jc_jm_j(w)=0$ for every ordered word;
\item $\sum_jc_j\tau_j\circ\pi_j$ vanishes on one retained word basis of $\Alg$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The last two conditions are equivalent by finite word closure. If the functional vanishes on $\Alg$, its extension by the reference trace vanishes on the perspective in~\eqref{eq:perspective}, proving the second condition.

For the other direction, fix the $f$ in the first condition. For any faithful $\rho$ and trace-zero Hermitian $H$, the coefficient of $\epsilon^2$ in the profile at $(\rho+\epsilon H,\rho)$ is $a_f$ times the RLD quadratic form. This follows either by analytic functional calculus near the identity or from~\eqref{eq:loewner}. Since $a_f>0$, the asserted profile identity gives a signed identity for every such quadratic form.

Insert the path probes~\eqref{eq:L}--\eqref{eq:states}, subtract the two signs, and vary the sufficiently small real parameter $t$. The coefficient of $t^m$ in~\eqref{eq:contrast} extracts the real part of any ordered moment of length at least two. Using $\zeta=i$ extracts the imaginary part. Length two uses the identity insertion. The zero- and one-letter conditions already follow from the sum constraint and trace preservation. Thus every signed ordered moment vanishes.
\end{proof}

This argument extends the equality mechanism to signed linear relations; pairwise equality alone would not justify that extension.

\subsection{The affine rank}

Make a real row matrix from the constant row $(1,\ldots,1)$ and the real and imaginary moment rows of a retained word basis. Let its rank be $p$ and kernel be $K_{\rm rel}\subset\R^k$. Then
\[
 \dim\operatorname{aff}\{\Pi_f(\Phi_1),\ldots,\Pi_f(\Phi_k)\}=p-1,
 \qquad\dim K_{\rm rel}=k-p.
\]
These dimensions and the complete space of affine identities do not depend on the chosen nonaffine $f$. They include reference systems and all faithful input pairs, not just a selected finite sample.

\begin{theorem}[An optimal fixed scalar panel]\label{thm:panel}
For a rational family, polynomial dense-input bit computation returns $p-1$ rational separable faithful probe pairs, each with reference dimension at most $N$, such that for every nonaffine operator-convex $f$ the corresponding $p-1$ scalar profile values determine every profile in the entire affine span. The panel is common to all such $f$. It also returns $p$ affinely independent family members and rational coefficients expressing every family member in terms of them; these same coefficients work on every faithful query, every finite reference, and every operator-convex $f$.

The number $p-1$ is minimal for a fixed scalar-evaluation panel that is injective on the entire affine span, or on the relative interior of its physical mixture simplex. This is not a lower bound for identifying one member of a finite labelled list.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A uniform construction without adaptive height feedback}

If $p=1$, return an empty probe panel, any one family member as the affine basis, and coefficient $1$ for every family member. All complete profiles then coincide. No minor perturbation or maximum over probe rows is needed. In the rest of this subsection assume $p>1$.

Retain $p$ independent real moment rows, with the first the constant row, to form $Z_0\in\Q^{p\times k}$. Select $p$ columns giving a nonsingular minor $V_0$. Put
\[
 K_0=\norm{V_0^{-1}}_{\infty},\qquad
 M=\max\left\{1,\ n-2:\ n\text{ is a selected nonconstant word length}\right\},
\]
and choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:panel-t}
 t=\frac1{16\max\{1,K_0p\}\,2^{M+1}}.
\end{equation}
For every nonconstant row, use its phase ($1$ or $i$) and its path probe. The scaled sign difference $r(g_+-g_-)/(4t^m)$ approximates that moment row. Each entry error is at most
\[
 \varepsilon_t=\frac{2^{M+1}t}{1-2t}.
\]
Our choice makes $K_0p\varepsilon_t<1$. Thus the $p$ selected columns of the matrix of scaled sign differences and the constant row remain nonsingular. This is a fixed exact perturbation estimate from the \emph{moment} minor, whose height was already bounded.

Collect the constant row and all individual quadratic-probe rows $g_+,g_-$. Their rank is at least $p$, by the preceding sign differences. It is at most $p$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}, since every row annihilates $K_{\rm rel}$. Select the constant row and $p-1$ independent individual rows to form $G\in\Q^{p\times k}$. Choose a nonsingular column minor $V=G_J$, and put
\begin{align*}
 K&=\norm{V^{-1}}_{\infty},\qquad
 R_* =\max_{\text{selected probe rows }i}\sum_{j\in J} g_{ij},\\
 u&=\min\left\{\frac1{16},\frac1{16K\max\{1,R_*\}}\right\},\qquad\eta=2u.
\end{align*}
For any fixed nonaffine $f$, normalize a probe row by replacing its entry with
\[
 \frac{\Pi_f(\Phi_j)(\sigma_i,\rho_i)-f(1)}{a_fu^2}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sandwich} bounds the difference from $g_{ij}$ in absolute value by $\eta g_{ij}/(1-\eta)$. Hence the perturbation $E_J$ of $V$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:panelmargin}
 \norm{V^{-1}E_J}_{\infty}\le\frac{K\eta R_*}{1-\eta}\le\frac17<1.
\end{equation}
Every normalized $f$-panel has rank $p$, and its kernel contains $K_{\rm rel}$. Equality of dimensions makes that kernel exactly $K_{\rm rel}$, for every $f$ simultaneously. The states are separable by the same mixture construction as before; here $mt+2u\le3/16$ again.

Finally define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
 \beta=V^{-1}G\in\Q^{p\times k}.
\end{equation}
The constant row makes every column of $\beta$ sum to one. Its columns on $J$ are the standard basis. The difference between the $j$th coordinate vector and its $J$-supported $\beta$ column belongs to $K_{\rm rel}$. Therefore
\[
 \Pi_f(\Phi_j)=\sum_{\ell=1}^p\beta_{\ell j}\Pi_f(\Phi_{J_\ell})
\]
as an identity of complete profiles, not just an interpolation identity on the selected panel.

Scalar evaluation is affine on a profile affine span of dimension $p-1$. Fewer than $p-1$ scalar evaluations have a nonzero kernel direction, which also produces distinct sufficiently close physical mixtures in an affinely independent simplex. This proves the stated minimality.

\begin{remark}[A retained failed route]
An earlier adaptive prototype shrank $t$ after estimating inverse-minor heights of the resulting probe matrix. That made a polynomial bit bound circular: shrinking $t$ itself changes those heights. It is preserved in the packet as \texttt{adaptive\_panel\_prototype.py}, but it is not the governing construction or proof. Equations~\eqref{eq:panel-t} and~\eqref{eq:panelmargin} repair this by fixing a moment basis first and perturbing its known minor, then choosing $u$ only once from the exact quadratic panel.
\end{remark}

No $f$-evaluator is used to compile the panel. Evaluating the panel for a general $f$, or measuring its outputs on a device, remains a separate paid operation. For the quadratic choice $f(x)=(x-1)^2$, every panel entry is rational and exactly evaluable from the supplied matrices. Signed entries of $\beta$ are not physical mixing probabilities. The next section resolves that distinction.
\section{Which affine profiles have a physical realization?}\label{sec:realization}

Fix real coefficients $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ with $\sum_jc_j=1$, and define the unital linear functional
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ell}
 \ell_c(X)=\sum_{j=1}^k c_j\tau_j(\pi_jX),\qquad X\in\Alg.
\end{equation}
Here $\pi_j$ is the projection to the $j$th channel block. The proposed profile is the signed affine expression $\sum_jc_j\Pi_f(\Phi_j)$. A physical realization means a completely positive trace-preserving map on the same input algebra, with an arbitrary finite output dimension, whose \emph{complete} profile equals that expression. Negative $c_j$ are allowed in the specification; they are not branch probabilities.

\begin{theorem}[Exact physical realization criterion]\label{thm:realization}
The following conditions are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Some finite-output channel $\Gamma$ realizes $\sum_jc_j\Pi_f(\Phi_j)$, for one fixed nonaffine operator-convex $f$, on every faithful input pair and every finite reference.
\item A single such channel realizes the expression for every operator-convex $f$.
\item The functional $\ell_c$ is positive, and its zero-norm subspace
\begin{equation}\label{eq:null}
 \Nul_c=\{X\in\Alg:\ell_c(X^*X)=0\}
\end{equation}
is a two-sided ideal of $\Alg$.
\item Write $\ell_c(X)=\Tr(Z_cX)/a$, with $Z_c\in\Alg$ the density with respect to the ordinary matrix trace in the given faithful representation. Then $Z_c\ge0$ and its support projection $p_c$ is central in $\Alg$.
\end{enumerate}
If the original channels and $c$ have rational real and imaginary parts, there is a deterministic polynomial algorithm in their total dense binary input length. It returns either a rational CPTP realization, or one of the following exact certificates excluding \emph{every} finite-output realization:
\[
 \ell_c(P^*P)<0,
 \quad\text{or}\quad
 \ell_c(P^*P)=0<\ell_c((PT(H))^*(PT(H))).
\]
Here $P$ is an explicitly given rational complex linear combination of retained words, and $H$ is one of the fixed Hermitian input generators. The algorithm does not evaluate $f$.
\end{theorem}

The two conditions in item 3 have different jobs. Positivity makes $\ell_c$ a state. The ideal condition makes that state faithful after passing to a quotient that retains a well-defined labelled algebra of channel observables. An ordinary GNS representation exists for every positive functional; that fact alone does not provide a physical profile realization, because its distinguished vector state need not be faithful on the represented algebra.

\subsection{Why every physical realization forces a quotient}

Suppose $\Gamma$ satisfies item 1. Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} to the combined family $(\Phi_1,\ldots,\Phi_k,\Gamma)$ with coefficients $(c_1,\ldots,c_k,-1)$. The resulting identity says that the normalized ordered moments of $\Gamma$ equal $\ell_c$ on every word.

Start with noncommutative $*$-polynomials in the Hermitian input letters. If a polynomial $P$ evaluates to zero in the original joint algebra $\Alg$, then
\[
 0=\ell_c(P^*P)=\tau_\Gamma(P_\Gamma^*P_\Gamma).
\]
The state $\tau_\Gamma$ is faithful on the effective output support: its density is $\Gamma(I_a)/a$, which is strictly positive on that support. Therefore $P_\Gamma=0$. Evaluation at the normalized generators of $\Gamma$ consequently descends to a unital surjective $*$-homomorphism
\[
 \pi:\Alg\longrightarrow\Alg_\Gamma,\qquad
 \pi(T(X))=T_\Gamma(X),\qquad \ell_c=\tau_\Gamma\circ\pi.
\]
Positivity follows, and faithfulness gives
\[
 \Nul_c=\ker\pi.
\]
This kernel is a two-sided ideal. This proves necessity even when the putative output dimension is unbounded in advance and the channel entries are not rational.

\subsection{The density and the central-support condition}

The ordinary trace restricted to a finite-dimensional $*$-algebra is a faithful inner product, so $Z_c$ exists and is unique. Positivity of $\ell_c$ is equivalent to $Z_c\ge0$. For example, a negative spectral projection of a Hermitian $Z_c\in\Alg$ belongs to $\Alg$ and would contradict positivity. For a positive $Z_c$, let $p_c$ be its support. Then
\[
 \ell_c(X^*X)=\frac1a\norm{XZ_c^{1/2}}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
 \qquad
 \Nul_c=\{X:Xp_c=0\}=\Alg(1-p_c).
\]
This is always a left ideal. It is a right ideal exactly when $(1-p_c)\Alg p_c=0$. Taking adjoints then gives $p_c\Alg(1-p_c)=0$, which is equivalent to centrality of $p_c$. Conversely, centrality immediately makes $\Alg(1-p_c)$ two-sided.

In a decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:blockdensity}
 \Alg\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda}(M_{n_\lambda}\otimes I_{m_\lambda}),
 \qquad Z_c\simeq\bigoplus_\lambda(z_\lambda\otimes I_{m_\lambda}),
\end{equation}
the criterion has a simple form: every $z_\lambda$ must be either positive definite or identically zero. A nonzero density that loses rank \emph{inside} a simple matrix block fails the criterion. A whole block may be discarded.

\subsection{The channel construction}

When the criterion holds, retain the central support $p_c$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:algebraic-realizer}
 \Gamma_c(X)=Z_c^{1/2}T(X)Z_c^{1/2}
 \quad\text{on }p_c\mathcal H.
\end{equation}
This is completely positive. Its trace is
\[
 \Tr\Gamma_c(X)=\Tr(Z_cT(X))
 =a\ell_c(T(X))
 =\sum_jc_j\Tr X=\Tr X.
\]
Its output on the identity is $Z_c$, and its normalized map is $X\mapsto p_cT(X)p_c$. Since $p_c$ is central, this is the labelled quotient of $T$ by $\Nul_c$. Its normalized state is $Y\mapsto\Tr(Z_cY)/a$, which is faithful on that quotient. Its ordered moments are exactly $\ell_c$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:linear} proves all the profile identities in item 2.

This proves the abstract equivalence. The square roots in~\eqref{eq:algebraic-realizer} need not be rational. The following compilation gives a rational channel without assuming an exact radical or a polynomial-time integer factorization operation.

\subsection{Finite certificates for positivity and the ideal condition}

Let $W_1,\ldots,W_D$ be a retained complex word basis of $\Alg$. Form the Hermitian Gram matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:positivegram}
 F_{ij}=\ell_c(W_i^*W_j).
\end{equation}
Then $F\ge0$ if and only if $\ell_c$ is positive on all of $\Alg$. A negative rational quadratic-form witness for $F$ gives the first obstruction in Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}. Exact Hermitian elimination supplies such a witness, including the case of a zero diagonal with a nonzero off-diagonal entry.

If $F\ge0$, a rational basis of $\ker F$ gives a basis of $\Nul_c$. Test each basis element after right multiplication by each of the $q=a^2-1$ Hermitian input generators. A positive resulting squared norm gives the second obstruction. If every test is zero, right invariance under all generators, their products, and complex linear combinations makes $\Nul_c$ a right ideal. It is already a left ideal by positivity, so the criterion holds.

All basis words have length at most $D-1$. A negative-square certificate can therefore be written using words of length at most $D-1$, and its square has degree at most $2D-2$. A null-ideal obstruction uses a right product of degree at most $D$, and a squared expression of degree at most $2D$. At most $qD$ right-multiplication tests are required. These are finite algebraic certificates, not a search through possible target output dimensions.

\subsection{Compilation in raw rational coordinates}

Use the direct-sum notation
\[
 \overline\Omega=\bigoplus_j\Omega_j,\qquad
 \overline\Phi(X)=\bigoplus_j\Phi_j(X),\qquad
 C_c=\bigoplus_jc_jI.
\]
The map $\overline\Phi$ is CP and has trace $k\Tr X$; it is not a channel when $k>1$. The corresponding channel is the uniform flagged source $\Theta=\overline\Phi/k$.

On effective supports, write the raw retained basis as
\[
 B_i=\overline\Omega^{1/2}W_i\overline\Omega^{1/2}.
\]
The raw closure constructs these $B_i$ directly, using only rational products and pseudoinverses. No square root is used to compute them. The two Gram matrices needed by the compiler and the projection target are
\begin{align}
 F_{uv}&=\frac1a\sum_{j=1}^k c_j\Tr(B_{u,j}^*\Omega_j^+B_{v,j}),\label{eq:Fraw}\\
 H_{uv}&=\Tr(\overline\Omega^+B_u^*\overline\Omega^+B_v),\qquad
 v_u=\Tr(B_u^*C_c).\label{eq:Hraw}
\end{align}
The matrix $H$ is nonsingular because the $W_i$ are a basis. Solve $H\alpha=v$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rationalK}
 B_c=\sum_i\alpha_iB_i,\qquad
 K_c=\overline\Omega^+B_c\overline\Omega^+.
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $\Ealg$ is the ordinary-trace conditional expectation onto $\Alg$, then
\[
 Z_c=\Ealg(\overline\Omega C_c),\qquad
 B_c=\overline\Omega^{1/2}Z_c\overline\Omega^{1/2},\qquad
 K_c=\overline\Omega^{-1/2}Z_c\overline\Omega^{-1/2}
\]
on support. The normal equations~\eqref{eq:Hraw} are exactly the orthogonal-projection equations for this $Z_c$. Thus $K_c$ is rational and positive semidefinite whenever the positivity test succeeds.

Factor $K_c=C^*C$ with a rectangular rational complex $C$, as explained below, and emit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-realizer}
 \widetilde\Gamma_c(X)=C\overline\Phi(X)C^*.
\end{equation}
It is rational, CP, and TP. To verify its normalized structure, put $M=C\overline\Omega^{1/2}$. Then $M^*M=Z_c$. Its polar decomposition is $M=UZ_c^{1/2}$ with $U^*U=p_c$. Equation~\eqref{eq:rational-realizer} is an isometric embedding of~\eqref{eq:algebraic-realizer}; therefore it has the required complete profile. Pseudoinverses and the factorization can operate in the original output spaces, retaining zero-support coordinates harmlessly.

\paragraph{Rational Gram factorization.}
Exact Hermitian $LDL^*$ elimination reduces the factorization to positive rational pivots $d=n/m$. Write the integer $nm$ in binary. An even power of two is one square; an odd power of two is the sum of two equal squares. After division by $m^2$, this gives
\[
 d=\sum_\nu r_\nu^2,\qquad r_\nu\in\Q,
\]
with at most twice the bit length of $nm$ summands and polynomial bit length. Expanding each pivot row accordingly gives $C^*C=K_c$. This elementary rectangular construction is prior N1698 compiler capital; it is used here for a different signed-functional realization problem. It does not promise the smallest rational output dimension.

\subsection{A paid heralded implementation from the supplied family}

The rational channel construction also specifies an exact success operation on the uniform flagged source $\Theta(X)=\bigoplus_j\Phi_j(X)/k$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:herald}
 p=\frac1{k\Tr K_c},\qquad
 \mathcal S(Y)=pk\,CYC^*.
\end{equation}
Because $K_c\ge0$ and $K_c\le(\Tr K_c)I$, this is trace-nonincreasing. Its failure effect is the rational positive matrix $I-pkK_c$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:success}
 \mathcal S\circ\Theta=p\widetilde\Gamma_c.
\end{equation}
The identity holds as a CP-map identity, including on entangled reference systems. The success probability is the same $p$ for every input. Trace preservation of $\widetilde\Gamma_c$ implies $k\Tr K_c\ge1$, so $0<p\le1$.

For a fully rational instrument, express the positive scalar $pk$ as a sum of rational squares $\sum_\nu s_\nu^2$ and use success Kraus operators $s_\nu C$. These give \emph{the same} output channel in~\eqref{eq:success}. Factoring an arbitrary replacement Gram matrix without this output check could produce a different isometric encoding. Factor the failure effect to obtain a failure map to a fixed orthogonal flag.

For this particular filter, the largest permitted success scaling is
\[
 p_{\mathrm{filter}}=\frac1{k\norm{K_c}_{\mathrm{op}}}.
\]
The rational $p$ in~\eqref{eq:herald} is a certified lower choice. No optimality over all channel-conversion protocols is asserted. For $n$ adaptive target calls, the all-success branch has probability $p^n$ and is exactly the target branch. Full-block attempts with known resettable inputs have expected cost $n/p^n$ source calls if every attempt consumes $n$ calls. Failure can destroy an unknown input; this calculation does not authorize retrying unknown quantum data. The factor $k$ is essential because the available flagged \emph{channel} is $\overline\Phi/k$.

\subsection{Consequences for the physical region and minimum dimension}

Within the finite affine functional space generated by the supplied family, the realizable region is convex. If two positive densities have central support projections, a strictly positive weighted sum has the join of those supports, again central. Its closure is precisely the positive affine slice. To see density, mix any positive $\ell_c$ with
\[
 \tau_0=\frac1k\sum_j\tau_j\circ\pi_j.
\]
The state $\tau_0$ is faithful on $\Alg$. Hence $(1-\epsilon)\ell_c+\epsilon\tau_0$ is faithful and realizable for every $\epsilon>0$, and tends to $\ell_c$.

The physical region is thus the union, over central projections, of the positive densities that are strictly positive on that central support and zero elsewhere, intersected with the specified affine slice. It need not be closed. These closure statements concern finite moment coordinates, equivalently a fixed complete-profile panel, or pointwise convergence at each fixed faithful query. They do not assert uniform convergence over all reference sizes and all nearly singular input states. If $\Alg$ is commutative, every support projection is central: positivity alone suffices, and the region is closed. A noncommutative algebra permits the obstruction but does not force every affine slice to exhibit it.

The quotient $\Alg/\Nul_c$ also fixes the smallest possible complex output dimension. In~\eqref{eq:blockdensity}, let the active indices have $z_\lambda>0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:min-dimension}
 b_{\min}=\sum_{\lambda\,\mathrm{active}}n_\lambda.
\end{equation}
Necessity follows because every realization gives a faithful representation of this quotient, and a faithful representation must contain each active simple summand. Sufficiency follows by absorbing multiplicity into $h_\lambda=m_\lambda z_\lambda$ and using
\[
 X\longmapsto\bigoplus_{\lambda\,\mathrm{active}}
 h_\lambda^{1/2}T_\lambda(X)h_\lambda^{1/2}.
\]
This is the minimum faithful-representation argument and greatest-channel structure already supplied by corrected N1698, now applied to the newly determined quotient. The rational compiler above can have a larger output. Equation~\eqref{eq:min-dimension} is not an additional polynomial guarantee for a minimum-dimensional rational encoding.

\begin{corollary}[Finite physical interpolation with an explicit promise]\label{cor:interpolation}
Suppose a target complete profile is promised to lie in the affine span of a supplied rational family, and its exact values on the quadratic panel of Theorem~\ref{thm:panel} are supplied as rational numbers. Then polynomial dense-input computation either produces a rational physical channel with that entire profile, or produces a finite obstruction proving that no finite-output channel has that profile.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The constant row and the panel invert the affine coordinate system. Solve for rational coefficients $c$ with $\sum c_j=1$, then apply Theorem~\ref{thm:realization}. Redundant coefficient choices give the same functional by Theorem~\ref{thm:linear}, so the answer is independent of that choice.
\end{proof}
The affine-span promise and the exact rational values are part of the input contract. A finite panel cannot certify that an arbitrary unknown device lies in the promised span, and noisy measured values are not exact rational profile data.

\section{An explicit rational qubit family and a nonphysical positive boundary}\label{sec:example}

The distinction between a positive functional and a physically realizable complete profile occurs already in a two-dimensional simple algebra. The example also shows that reference-free tests can miss an entire affine degree of freedom.

Write $\sigma_x,\sigma_y,\sigma_z$ for the Pauli matrices and fix $\kappa=1/4$. Define the unital, trace-preserving CP map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qubitT}
 T(X)=\frac{\Tr X}{2}I_2+
       \frac\kappa2\Tr(X\sigma_x)\sigma_x+
       \frac\kappa2\Tr(X\sigma_y)\sigma_y.
\end{equation}
Thus $T(\sigma_x)=\kappa\sigma_x$, $T(\sigma_y)=\kappa\sigma_y$, and $T(\sigma_z)=0$. Complete positivity follows, for example, from its Choi matrix: the only nontrivial $2\times2$ principal block has eigenvalues $1/2\pm\kappa$. The algebra generated by its range is $M_2$.

For $-1<s<1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qubitfamily}
 D_s=\begin{pmatrix}(1+s)/2&0\\0&(1-s)/2\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Gamma_s(X)=2D_s^{1/2}T(X)D_s^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
This is a channel because $\Tr(D_sT(X))=\Tr X/2$. In input-major Choi order its matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qubitch}
 J_{\Gamma_s}=\begin{pmatrix}
 d_0&0&0&\kappa\sqrt{1-s^2}\\
 0&d_1&0&0\\
 0&0&d_0&0\\
 \kappa\sqrt{1-s^2}&0&0&d_1
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad d_0=\frac{1+s}{2},\quad d_1=\frac{1-s}{2}.
\end{equation}
The relevant determinant is $(1-s^2)(1/4-\kappa^2)>0$, and the partial trace is $I_2$. Its normalized map is exactly $T$, and its normalized state is
\[
 \tau_s(W)=\Tr(D_sW).
\]
Choose the two rational channels $\Gamma_0$ and $\Gamma_{3/5}$ as the supplied family. Their off-diagonal Choi entries use the rational numbers $1$ and $4/5$. The affine functional
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qubitweights}
 \ell_s=\left(1-\frac{5s}{3}\right)\tau_0+
              \frac{5s}{3}\tau_{3/5}
       =\Tr(D_s\,\cdot)
\end{equation}
is positive exactly for $-1\le s\le1$, and has a central support projection in $M_2$ exactly for $-1<s<1$. Consequently the realizable portion of this affine complete-profile line is the \emph{open} interval
\[
 -1<s<1,
\]
while its positive closure is $[-1,1]$. The endpoints are positive formal profiles with no finite-output physical realization. This assertion quantifies over every candidate channel, not merely the displayed parametrization.

\subsection{A signed combination that is physically attainable}

At $s=4/5$, the coefficients are $(-1/3,4/3)$ and $\sqrt{1-s^2}=3/5$. Therefore the rational qubit channel $\Gamma_{4/5}$ satisfies, for every nonaffine operator-convex $f$, every reference, and every faithful query,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:qubitrelation}
 \Pi_{\Gamma_{4/5},f}
 =-\frac13\Pi_{\Gamma_0,f}+\frac43\Pi_{\Gamma_{3/5},f}.
\end{equation}
This is a relation between complete profiles, not an instruction to subtract physical channels or sample a negative probability. The compiler produces a physical representative and a separate success operation. On this input its automatically emitted rational representative has output dimension $14$, effective output rank $4$, and certified success probability $8/29$ from the uniform flagged source. The explicit channel in~\eqref{eq:qubitfamily} shows that the minimum complex output dimension is $2$. The larger compiler output is a cost of the chosen rational factorization, not a necessity theorem.

\subsection{A positive endpoint with a finite obstruction}

At $s=1$, the coefficients are $(-2/3,5/3)$ and the functional is the vector state $\ell_1(W)=\langle0|W|0\rangle$. Let
\[
 P=|1\rangle\langle1|=
 \frac12\left(I+i\kappa^{-2}T(\sigma_x)T(\sigma_y)\right).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundaryobstruction}
 \ell_1(P^*P)=0,
 \qquad
 \ell_1\big((PT(\sigma_x))^*(PT(\sigma_x))\big)=\kappa^2>0.
\end{equation}
The first equality forces $P$ to vanish in any faithful physical realization. Multiplying its image by $T_\Gamma(\sigma_x)$ would still give zero, contradicting the second equality. This is the null-ideal obstruction without a spectral decomposition or an unknown-output search. The included compiler emits a differently scaled word certificate with positive product norm $1/64$.

For $s=6/5$, positivity already fails: $\ell_{6/5}(P^*P)=-1/10$. The corresponding regression uses another negative polynomial square, with value $-11/6400$. The two obstruction types are mathematically distinct and are tested separately.

\subsection{Why the limiting channel does not realize the limiting profile}

As $s\uparrow1$, the channels $\Gamma_s$ converge to the pure-output replacer
\[
 \Gamma_1(X)=(\Tr X)|0\rangle\langle0|.
\]
This limiting channel has a smaller effective support. For the faithful input states
\[
 \rho=I_2/2,\qquad \sigma=(I_2+h\sigma_x)/2,\qquad 0<h<1,
\]
one computes for every $-1<s<1$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundaryjump}
 \Dhat_f(\Gamma_s(\sigma)\Vert\Gamma_s(\rho))
 =\frac12\left[f(1+h\kappa)+f(1-h\kappa)\right].
\end{equation}
At the endpoint channel $\Gamma_1$, the value is $f(1)$. For the quadratic generator $f(x)=(x-1)^2$ and $h=1/2$, the values are $1/64$ and $0$, respectively. Thus convergence of channels through a loss of output rank need not commute with taking these maximal-information profiles. This concrete discontinuity is part of the example; no claim to the first discovery of singular-boundary discontinuities is made.

There is also a fully rational sequence approaching the endpoint. Set
\[
 s=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad
 \sqrt{1-s^2}=\frac{2t}{1+t^2},\qquad t>0\text{ rational},
\]
and let $t$ tend to zero. Every channel in the sequence has rational Choi entries, yet the positive limit of its affine profile coordinates is not physically realizable. For any fixed faithful query the formal profile values have a finite affine limit. There is no uniform assertion over input states whose eigenvalues also approach zero.

\subsection{An entire degree of freedom invisible without a reference}

More is true than~\eqref{eq:boundaryjump}: all the channels $\Gamma_s$, $-1<s<1$, have the same \emph{reference-free} maximal-$f$ profile for every $f$ in the stated class. To verify this, let $R=T(\rho)$ and $S=T(\sigma)$ for arbitrary faithful qubit states. Both lie in $\spanR\{I,\sigma_x,\sigma_y\}$ and have trace $1$. The positive $2\times2$ matrix $R^{-1/2}SR^{-1/2}$ has at most two eigenvalues, so there are scalars $\alpha,\beta$, depending on $R,S,f$ but not on $s$, such that
\[
 f(R^{-1/2}SR^{-1/2})=\alpha R^{-1/2}SR^{-1/2}+\beta I.
\]
The perspective is therefore $\alpha S+\beta R$. Since $\Tr(D_sR)=\Tr(D_sS)=1/2$, the divergence is $\alpha+\beta$, independent of $s$.

Complete profiles nevertheless distinguish $s$, because the ordered moment
\[
 \tau_s\big(T(\sigma_x)T(\sigma_y)\big)=i\kappa^2s
\]
does. Theorem~\ref{thm:witness} turns this imaginary second moment into an explicit reference-assisted, separable, faithful input pair. A reference is essential for this distinction even though entanglement of the two input states is unnecessary. The state on the normalized algebra is not tracial, so discarding imaginary ordered moments would lose real information.


\section{Interfaces and complete cost accounting}\label{sec:costs}

\subsection{What is supplied and what is returned}

The algorithms take \emph{explicit dense rational Choi matrices}, with a common input dimension. Their dimensions, the bit lengths of their entries, and (for realization) the rational affine coefficients are part of the input size. The code uses $\Q(i)$ arithmetic and rejects floating-point entries, nonpositive Choi matrices, and trace-preservation failures. Input-major Choi ordering is recorded in the packet README.

The three interfaces are distinct:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inequality witness.} Given two channels, decide profile equality by prior finite closure. On inequality return the word, rational faithful separable input states, their explicit product-state mixtures, the orientation, and a positive rational $\mu$. The guarantee is simultaneous over the entire stated $f$ class. No call to an $f$ evaluator occurs in the compiler.
\item \textbf{Affine geometry.} Given a finite family, return its dimension, a basis of every affine relation, independent family representatives, rational reconstruction coefficients, and a common panel of $p-1$ physical input pairs. For the quadratic generator, every panel value is rational and directly computable. For a general $f$, numerical evaluation, accuracy and conditioning of the resulting coordinate inversion are additional tasks.
\item \textbf{Physical realization.} Given a finite family and rational $c$ with $\sum_jc_j=1$, return a rational CPTP Choi matrix, a rectangular rational factor of its filter Gram matrix, and finite moment verification data, or return a finite polynomial obstruction. A NO certificate excludes \emph{all} finite output dimensions. A YES certificate does not promise the smallest rational output dimension, a short gate sequence, or a practically efficient instrument.
\end{enumerate}

These inputs are not experimental black boxes, compact quantum circuits, implicit optimization polytopes, arbitrary functions given by names, or samples from an unknown device. Translating any of those representations into this interface requires its own cost and validity argument. A general operator-convex $f$ need not have a supplied computable evaluator; the uniform theorem does not manufacture one.

\subsection{Why the dense-input bit bounds are polynomial}

Write $q=a^2-1$, $N=\sum_je_j^2$, and $D=\dim_\C\Alg\le N$. Closure retains at most $D$ independent words, of length at most $D-1$, and tests at most $qD$ generator extensions after the unit. Raw multiplication uses rational matrices and the support pseudoinverses. A common-denominator bound for words of length at most $D$ gives polynomial bit length: multiplication adds entry heights, and matrix summation adds only a logarithmic factor per multiplication. Rank tests and linear solves can use fraction-free exact elimination, so determinants and their minors give polynomial output-height bounds. Thus the field-operation counts and the required integer bit lengths are both polynomial in the dense input.

The affine construction uses fixed minors of already bounded-height matrices. Its two perturbation parameters are explicit rational reciprocals; the proof never repeatedly halves a parameter while re-estimating a height that itself depends on that parameter. This is why the unsuccessful adaptive prototype is not used for the complexity claim.

For realization, forming the $D\times D$ moment and Hilbert--Schmidt Gram matrices takes polynomially many exact matrix operations. Positive-semidefiniteness, kernels, the density solve, and at most $qD$ right-generator null tests are polynomial exact linear algebra. A single $D\times D$ solve has cubic arithmetic complexity using ordinary elimination; this is \emph{not} a cubic bound for the full channel algorithm, which also contains matrix products, closure tests, and rational bit arithmetic.

If $R=\sum_jb_j$ and $H$ bounds the bit heights of rational positive pivots in the filter factorization, the binary sum-of-squares construction uses $O(RH)$ output rows. It uses no integer-factorization oracle and no irrational square roots. The emitted dense Choi matrix therefore has polynomial length. A rational Kraus description can also be obtained by applying the same factorization method to that Choi matrix. None of this asserts efficient exact synthesis over a prescribed finite universal gate alphabet.

\subsection{Compilation is separate from observation and control}

The rational numbers $t,u,\mu$ and the heralding probability can be exponentially small in their bit-description lengths. Faithfulness and bounded condition number of the probe states do not make the output difference large. There is no polynomial sample-complexity conclusion hidden in the polynomial compiler.

For a direct operational illustration, compare the identity channel with
\[
 \Psi_\varepsilon=(1-\varepsilon)\id+\varepsilon\mathcal R,
 \qquad \varepsilon=2^{-L},
\]
where $\mathcal R$ is a fixed rational replacer. Their half diamond distance is at most $\varepsilon$. Replacing one use at a time in an arbitrary adaptive $n$-use protocol bounds the final half trace distance by $n\varepsilon$. A constant distinguishing advantage can consequently require $\Omega(2^L)$ uses even though the channel description and the exact witness have polynomial bit length. This follows from the usual hybrid/contractivity argument and does not assume a particular measurement or a particular divergence estimator.

Likewise, the heralded realization described in Section~\ref{sec:realization} pays for the uniform flag channel, the filter, its success record, and failure handling. For $n$ uses of the success branch the specified construction has all-success probability $p^n$. Restarting a complete known-input block costs $n/p^n$ expected channel uses. This restart statement is unavailable for an unknown quantum input that cannot be recreated after failure. Directly synthesizing the emitted channel may be preferable; its actual control cost is a separate implementation question. The probability quoted here is the certified probability for the supplied filter, not an optimum over every possible implementation.

\subsection{Equally informed alternatives and non-claims}

An alternative given the same dense rational input may use matrix-algebra decomposition, exact linear algebra, semidefinite methods where their hypotheses apply, or a greatest-representative compiler from N1698. The present result does not establish a runtime advantage over all such alternatives. Its specific enabling operation is the complete YES/NO realization criterion together with physical probes and finite certificates; the additional obstruction is closure of zero-norm relations under multiplication.

The algorithms are polynomial in $a,b_j$ and entry heights. Since $a=2^{n_A}$ for $n_A$ input qubits, dense-input polynomial time can already be exponential in the number of qubits. No NP-hard optimization interface, polynomial ordinary-capacity evaluation, or general polynomial quantum-circuit compilation is provided. N1690's evaluator/capacity separation remains operative: a complete maximal-information profile does not in general determine ordinary quantum capacity. N675's differing input/interface and reference-cutoff obstructions are not contradicted by an algorithm on the explicit finite moment representation used here.

\subsection{What the accompanying computation actually verifies}

The packet contains three compilers, three certificate checkers, exact rational fixtures, and regression scripts. The checkers replay certificates from emitted matrices and states, including positivity, trace preservation, product-mixture decomposition, moment relations, and the relevant sign or null-ideal condition. They share the exact-arithmetic support module and some construction utilities. They are therefore useful reproducibility checks, not independent implementations or formal proof verification.

The recorded suites contain the following cases:

The longer path tests the path construction and is not claimed to be a shortest closure witness.

\end{itemize}

All 17 recorded cases pass their specified checks. In the positive nonphysical boundary fixture the checked null norm is $0$ and the checked right-product norm is $1/64$. \subsection{Useful failures retained}

Three failed shortcuts shaped the construction and remain part of the record.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Adaptive panel height feedback.} Repeatedly shrinking a probe parameter while bounding a minor that depends on that parameter did not establish a polynomial height bound. The file \texttt{adaptive\_panel\_prototype.py} is retained as a deprecated prototype. The fixed-moment-minor construction in Section~\ref{sec:panel} replaces that argument.
\item \textbf{Positivity as physical realization.} A positive moment Gram matrix alone can yield a nonfaithful cyclic state whose zero-norm relations do not survive multiplication. The boundary example proves the failure exactly. The two-sided-ideal test repairs the operation without borrowing a peer's unconstructed realization map.
\item \textbf{Reference-free reconstruction.} The qubit example has an entire parameter invisible to every reference-free maximal-$f$ query. The complete representation must retain ordered complex moments and reference-assisted probes. Dropping imaginary moments or substituting a tracial weight would erase the distinction.
\end{enumerate}

Earlier useful failures and accepted constructions remain preserved at their original scopes. In particular, this packet does not revisit or extend N675/N557, the prior rounding result, the universal extension, or the previously paid recovery and capacity-source work.

\subsection{Consequences earned and the larger unresolved objective}

The earned deductions are exact: finite affine dimension and relations; a minimum fixed scalar panel over the affine span; a common all-$f$ physical inequality witness; a finite rational physical-realization compiler or obstruction; the positive-cone/physical-subset distinction; closure by faithful mixtures; the minimum complex output dimension at the algebraic scope; and the explicit qubit separation between reference-free and complete profiles. These deductions are mutually compatible because they use the same generated algebra, its faithful source weights, and exact rational input.

They do not by themselves deliver the programme's required civilisation-changing capability. A route to that consequence must supply an additional operation with a materially stronger interface or cost guarantee, or a broad explanatory result that genuinely reaches the consequence anchor. Ordinary quantum capacity, practical device-scale inference, and a polynomial solution of arbitrary NP problems remain unresolved here. It would be scientifically incorrect to present this finite construction as a completed substitute for that larger objective.

The immediate scientific organization remains independent work: P2's live quantum-processor question and P3's independent construction are preserved. Publication, if separately authorized, does not constitute verification.
