\boxed{F\text{ is reachable}\iff s\ge d(N),}
\]

and, whenever it is reachable,

\[
\boxed{T_{\min}(F)=\frac{s+d(N)}{\Lambda}.}
\]

Singular matrices and matrices with negative determinant cannot be reached in finite time with these bounded controls.

### The actual least-noisy fastest programme

The shortest shape is generated by a rotating symmetric strain. The compiler determines its initial orientation and rotation rate from \(N\).

Then:

1. **Perform the shape change at maximum rank-one restoring strength**, for duration
   \[
   T_{\mathrm{shape}}=\frac{2d(N)}{\Lambda}.
   \]
   During this stage, the two eigenvalues of \(A(t)\) are exactly \(0\) and \(\Lambda\).

2. **Apply maximal isotropic relaxation last**, using \(A=\Lambda I\), for duration
   \[
   T_{\mathrm{cool}}=\frac{s-d(N)}{\Lambda}.
   \]

The durations add to \(T_{\min}\). The resulting mean map is exactly \(F\).

If the shape stage has mean matrix \(F_g\) and added covariance \(Q_g\), put

\[
b=e^{-(s-d(N))},
\qquad
m=\frac D\Lambda.
\]

The complete endpoint is

\[
F=bF_g,
\qquad
\boxed{Q_*=b^2Q_g+m(1-b^2)I.}
\]

The code calculates \(Q_g\) using a constant-matrix exponential in the rotating frame. Thus the procedure produces the force schedule, duration, covariance and confidence radius directly from the model inputs.

This is a completed mathematical assembly operation. Its physical implementation remains conditional on having the declared force controls and bath model.

## 3. A fully explicit case: rotate every unknown input

For a requested attenuated rotation

\[
F=e^{-s}R_\theta,
\qquad -\pi\le\theta\le\pi,
\]

define

\[
\boxed{L_\theta=\sqrt{\theta^2+2\pi|\theta|}.}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{s\ge L_\theta}
\]

is necessary and sufficient, and

\[
\boxed{T_{\min}=\frac{s+L_\theta}{\Lambda}.}
\]

The deterministic geometric content here is an application of established \(SL(2)\) geometry, not a newly discovered geometry.

### Explicit force schedule

For \(\theta\ne0\), set

\[
T_r=\frac{2L_\theta}{\Lambda},
\qquad
\omega=
\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\theta)(\pi+|\theta|)}{T_r},
\qquad
S=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Apply

\[
\boxed{
A_r(t)=\frac{\Lambda}{2}
\left[I+R_{\omega t}S R_{\omega t}^{\top}\right],
\quad 0\le t\le T_r.
}
\]

This produces exactly

\[
F_r=e^{-L_\theta}R_\theta.
\]

Follow it with \(A=\Lambda I\) for \((s-L_\theta)/\Lambda\).

The construction uses only restoring forces. There is no instantaneous rotational force term. Rotation emerges from their ordered composition—a phenomenon already treated directly in positive-matrix factorisation and gradient-flow holonomy. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

### Exact thermal error

Write

\[
\rho=\frac{L_\theta}{\pi+|\theta|},
\qquad
f=1-e^{-2L_\theta}.
\]

At the least-contracted boundary \(s=L_\theta\), the added-noise eigenvalues are

\[
\boxed{
q_\pm=
\frac{2D}{\Lambda}f
\left(1+\rho^2\pm\rho\sqrt{1+\rho^2}\right).
}
\]

For surplus contraction \(\delta=s-L_\theta\), they become

\[
q_{\pm,*}
=
e^{-2\delta}q_\pm
+\frac D\Lambda(1-e^{-2\delta}).
\]

The entire radial error law is therefore

\[
\|\eta_*\|^2
\overset d=
q_{+,*}Z_1^2+q_{-,*}Z_2^2,
\]

where \(Z_1,Z_2\) are independent standard normal variables.

## 4. The central proof: why the ordering is globally optimal

The essential additional operation is an exact comparison of noise under composition.

### First, the duration lower bound

Decompose

\[
A=aI+B,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}B=0,
\qquad
k=\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\]

The allowed eigenvalues imply

\[
a\ge k,
\qquad
a+k\le\Lambda.
\]

For any programme reaching \(F\),

\[
s=\int a\,dt,
\qquad
K=\int k\,dt\ge d(N).
\]

Consequently,

\[
s\ge d(N),
\qquad
\Lambda T\ge s+d(N).
\]

The explicit programme attains both bounds.

### Second, a positive quantity that composes

Let \(\Phi(T,t)\) denote the deterministic transition from \(t\) to \(T\). Added thermal covariance is

\[
Q=2D\int_0^T\Phi(T,t)\Phi(T,t)^\top\,dt.
\]

Define

\[
E=Q-\frac D\Lambda(I-FF^\top).
\]

Direct integration gives

\[
\boxed{
E=
\frac{2D}{\Lambda}
\int_0^T
\Phi(T,t)(\Lambda I-A(t))\Phi(T,t)^\top\,dt
\succeq0.
}
\]

For successive operations,

\[
\boxed{E_{21}=E_2+F_2E_1F_2^\top.}
\]

This is ordinary Gaussian evolution algebra used as an ordering tool; I do not claim the algebra itself is new.

A maximal isotropic relaxation has zero \(E\). Move such a relaxation, with contraction factor \(b\), from **before** an arbitrary admissible operation to **after** it. The mean map and total duration stay the same, while the covariance decreases by

\[
\boxed{(1-b^2)E\succeq0.}
\]

That is an executable transformation of a control programme with a proved endpoint benefit.

### Third, it applies to every fastest programme

Every duration minimiser must traverse a shortest shape and saturate the rate constraint. Its controls decompose into maximal rank-one shape evolution and isotropic relaxation. Moving the latter to the end proves the covariance ordering.

Multiple shortest shapes require care. Their optimal covariances can differ by an orthogonal conjugation. There is generally **an orbit of minimal covariances**, rather than one covariance matrix below all the others.

Nevertheless, orthogonal conjugation preserves radial error. Adding independent centred Gaussian noise decreases the probability of lying inside every centred ball. This gives simultaneous optimality for all radial thresholds.

The same conclusion survives independently preselected random programmes implementing the same \(F\). For rotation targets, randomising two optimal orientations can make the covariance isotropic, but the resulting error law is a **Gaussian mixture**, not an isotropic Gaussian.

## 5. The sharp finite-stage resource law

The cleanest companion theorem prices a restriction that continuous-control descriptions often leave implicit: **how many constant-force stages are available?**

Suppose at most \(N\) constant symmetric controls may be used, with ideal instantaneous switches, to implement

\[
e^{-s}R_\theta,
\qquad 0<|\theta|\le\pi.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
s_N(\theta)=
N\operatorname{arcosh}
\left[
\frac{\cos(\pi/N)}
{\cos((\pi+|\theta|)/N)}
\right].
}
\]

Then the exact conditions are

\[
\boxed{
N>2+\frac{2|\theta|}{\pi},
\qquad
s\ge s_N(\theta).
}
\]

Whenever these hold,

\[
\boxed{T_N=\frac{s+s_N(\theta)}{\Lambda}.}
\]

The same construction also minimises the worst condition number among the positive factors:

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{opt}}
=
\exp\!\left(\frac{2s_N(\theta)}{N}\right).
}
\]

### Matching construction

Set

\[
\ell=\frac{s_N}{N},
\qquad
\delta=\operatorname{sgn}(\theta)\frac{\pi+|\theta|}{N},
\]

and choose

\[
H_j=
R_{j\delta}
\begin{pmatrix}e^{-\ell}&0\\0&e^\ell\end{pmatrix}
R_{-j\delta},
\qquad j=0,\ldots,N-1.
\]

Their product is exactly \(R_\theta\). Multiplying each by \(e^{-s/N}\) gives the desired physical contractions; each is realised by a constant admissible \(A_j\) for the specified duration.

### Why this is a global bound

Normalised positive factors generate a closed hyperbolic polygon:

- Each factor contributes side length \(2k_j\).
- Its signed area encodes twice the rotation angle, modulo \(4\pi\).
- Hyperbolic polygon isoperimetry bounds the least perimeter enclosing the required area.
- A convexification argument covers self-intersections and extra winding.

The polygon inequality is classical mathematics, explicitly attributed in the record. 

For five factors producing a \(180^\circ\) rotation,

\[
s_5=8.08460833755943,
\qquad
\kappa_{\max}^{\mathrm{opt}}
=25.3770021071485.
\]

The recent factorisation paper already gives minimum factor counts and an illustrative five-factor construction with condition number about \(30\). The distinction here is a proved optimal formula over the whole feasible \((N,\theta)\) family. Its example is not being treated as a lower bound against its method; the established method can use this optimal construction too. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

The lower bound also applies to the **local response** of nonlinear \(C^2\) convex potentials held fixed per stage when they share the same stationary minimum and obey the same Hessian cap there. It does not automatically cover moving minima or trajectories sampling spatially varying Hessians.

## 6. A stronger claim failed—and the failure became a theorem

The fastest, least-noisy programme need not minimise noise when additional duration is allowed.

The distinction is exact.

### At the least possible contraction

For \(s=L_\theta\), every admissible shape must already be shortest. Slowing it down or pausing only adds positive covariance.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
s=L_\theta:
\text{ the fastest programme is radially optimal across all durations.}
}
\]

### With any surplus contraction

For every nonzero principal \(\theta\) and every \(s>L_\theta\), I constructed an admissible perturbation preserving the **exact same target matrix** such that

\[
\Delta\operatorname{tr}Q=-c\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2),
\qquad c>0,
\]

while

\[
\Delta T=O(\varepsilon^2),
\qquad
\Delta W_{\mathrm{net}}=O(\varepsilon^2).
\]

The construction deforms the shape loop while preserving its holonomy. Its length changes only to second order, but the timing and orientation of thermal-noise propagation change to first order. The proof establishes the sign analytically for every nonzero rotation angle.

For example, at \(\theta=\pi/2\), \(s=L_\theta+0.05\), and \(D=\Lambda=1\):

| Quantity | Fastest, least-noisy programme | Explicit slightly slower programme |
|---|---:|---:|
| Duration | 7.074814731 | 7.076363501 |
| Added mean-square error \(\operatorname{tr}Q\) | 5.815416499 | 5.783020919 |
| Net expected work/\(k_BT_{\mathrm{bath}}\) | 7.024814731 | 7.027912270 |

About **0.022% more time** reduces mean-square error by about **0.557%**.

A broader deduction follows. For any finite nonnegative \(\lambda,\mu\) and positive \(\alpha\),

\[
J=\alpha\,\operatorname{tr}Q+\lambda T+\mu W_{\mathrm{net}},
\]

the fastest programme is not a minimiser when \(s>L_\theta\). A sufficiently small perturbation improves \(J\).

This does **not** solve the whole slower-time optimisation problem, and it does not prove that every individual radial tail improves. The fastest point remains Pareto efficient: the improvement costs additional time and work.

## 7. Work, preparation and physical limitations

### The work that is actually accounted for

Assume the apparatus begins and ends at \(A=\Lambda I\), and count the changes into and out of the rotating trap. For the constructed optimal programme,

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E W_{\mathrm{net}}=2k_BT_{\mathrm{bath}}\,d(N).}
\]

For a rotation, this is \(2k_BT_{\mathrm{bath}}L_\theta\).

The identity includes endpoint potential changes and holds for any initial distribution with finite second moment. It follows because the constructed controls satisfy \(A^2=\Lambda A\).

It is **net expected potential-modulation work**. It credits negative work recovered during parts of the operation. It does not price gross energy supply, an energy-recovery mechanism, peak power, actuator losses or work fluctuations.

### Input preparation cannot be hidden in that small number

The amplitude loss makes the initial signal resource expensive.

For an unknown input of radius \(R\), requiring error at most a fraction \(\epsilon\) of the desired output radius with probability \(p\) gives the necessary stored-energy bound

\[
\boxed{
\frac{E_0}{k_BT_{\mathrm{bath}}}
\ge
\frac{(e^{2L_\theta}-1)\log[1/(1-p)]}{\epsilon^2},
}
\]

where \(E_0=\gamma\Lambda R^2/2\) is the energy stored in the initial reference trap.

For the fastest \(90^\circ\) boundary operation, the exact error law requires approximately

\[
E_0\ge 1.16\times10^6\,k_BT_{\mathrm{bath}}
\]

to obtain a 95% probability of error below 10% of the desired output radius.

That is a requirement on stored input energy, not a claim that preparing the unknown input costs exactly that much work. It exposes why the net modulation work alone would be a misleading operating-cost account.

### Bandwidth is a separate resource

During the continuous rotation stage,

\[
\|\dot A\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=
\frac{\Lambda^2(\pi+|\theta|)}{2L_\theta}.
\]

This diverges as \(\theta\to0\). Endpoint changes are ideal instantaneous switches. Consequently, the theorem is an amplitude-cap result, not a finite-bandwidth apparatus optimum.

### Actual experiments challenge the physical scope

Controlled Brownian relaxation is experimentally real. Martínez and colleagues implemented a \(0.5\) ms equilibration protocol for micrometre silica particles in water. Their methods specify finite switching and acquisition rates, repeated-cycle statistics and calibration errors. This supports the ordinary experimental setting, but does not demonstrate the rotating rank-one force family or our unknown-input operation. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="6"}

There is also direct evidence against using the memoryless model indiscriminately at high speed. Franosch and colleagues measured roughly 50-second records at 1 MHz and observed hydrodynamic-memory resonances and negative position correlations. In one plotted acetone case, fitted fluid and trap times were \(2.4\,\mu\mathrm{s}\) and \(19.2\,\mu\mathrm{s}\). Those observations invalidate a simple white-noise exponential-relaxation description in that regime. I acquired the paper and its reported conditions, not the original raw time records. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}
