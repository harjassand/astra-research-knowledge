BOUNDED GRADIENT CONTROL: COMPLETE MEAN MAPS AND MINIMUM-TIME THERMAL ERROR
Internal research construction, 5 November 2026

STATUS AND FAILED NOVELTY CLAIM

The initial proposed primitive was accumulated shape holonomy: a sequence of
instantaneously symmetric restoring forces can have a rotating total response.
The root agent's literature challenge found this is established mathematics,
including SL(2,R) sub-Riemannian geometry and a recent direct treatment of
positive-definite factorization via gradient-flow and optimal-transport
holonomy. Therefore neither holonomy nor the normalized distance is claimed as
new. The deterministic attenuation/time inequalities below translate that
existing geometry to a particular bounded positive-control model.

The further deduction is a complete thermal-error ordering among all
minimum-time realizations of a fixed linear map. It uses an explicit Gaussian
composition inequality, not an unknown local constitutive input. Novelty of
this deduction remains subject to the root's primary-literature audit.

No physical experiment was performed. No material, apparatus or delivered
physical capability is claimed. All numerical cases are synthetic checks of
the stated controlled linear model. The failure of a stronger, unrestricted
time/noise-optimality claim is preserved separately.

1. MODEL AND ACTUATION RESOURCES

Mean dynamics: x_dot = -A(t)x, with real symmetric 2x2 A(t) satisfying
0 <= A(t) <= Lambda I in Loewner order, Lambda>0. The control is a prescribed
open-loop function of time. The full fundamental matrix M(t), not only one
selected input trajectory, is the controlled object. M(0)=I.

Thermal version: dX=-A(t)X dt + sqrt(2D)dW, D>0. The bath is isotropic and
memoryless; feedback based on the realized noise is not in the control class.
An independently chosen random open-loop program is discussed explicitly.
For drag gamma, U(x,t)=gamma x^T A(t)x/2 and D=k_B T_bath/gamma.

The eigenvalue cap is a drift-rate cap (inverse time), not a bound on
parameter slew, actuator power, laboratory energy, or all operating costs.
Endpoint changes of A are ideal quenches; their expected energy work is
counted when work is discussed, but finite actuator bandwidth is not solved.
Initial state preparation and later readout/storage are not supplied.

2. THE EXACT ALL-TARGET MEAN-MAP FRONTIER

Let a=tr(A)/2, S=A-aI, k=||S||op. Thus S is symmetric traceless with eigenvalues
+/-k. Positivity and the cap give 0<=k<=a and a+k<=Lambda. Define
s(t)=integral a, K(t)=integral k. Liouville gives det M=e^(-2s).

Normalize Y=e^s M. Then Y in SL(2,R) and Y_dot=-S Y. Equip the horizontal
directions (symmetric traceless matrices) with norm ||S||op. Their control
length is K. Let d(I,N) denote this established sub-Riemannian distance.

For a requested real 2x2 M_f with det M_f>0, put
 s=-log(det M_f)/2; N=e^s M_f.
It is reachable in finite time if and only if s>=d(I,N).
Under the cap, its exact minimum time is
 T_min = (s+d(I,N))/Lambda.

Necessity: any admissible path has K>=d, s>=K and s+K<=Lambda T.
Sufficiency: traverse a distance-minimizing horizontal path at constant speed
k=d/T and choose a=s/T. The eigenvalues a+/-k lie in [0,Lambda] exactly when
s>=d and T>=(s+d)/Lambda. In 2D the minimizing path is explicitly computable
below. Singular maps and negative determinant maps are not reachable in
finite time with bounded A. Scalar contraction is the d=0 case.

3. USABLE FORMULA FOR THE DISTANCE AND CONTROL

For N in SL2 define complex numbers
 u=(N11+N22)/2 + i(N21-N12)/2,
 v=(N11-N22)/2 + i(N12+N21)/2.
Then |u|^2-|v|^2=1. Put q=|v|, r=asinh(q), theta=arg(u) in [-pi,pi].
Orthogonal conjugation changes the phase of v but leaves q and theta fixed.

For q=0, N is a rotation. If theta=0, d=0. Otherwise
 d=sqrt(theta^2+2pi|theta|), w=sign(theta)(pi+|theta|).

For q>0 set h=q-atan(q).
If theta=0: d=r and w=0.
If 0<|theta|<=h: uniquely solve z in [0,r] from
 |theta|=z sqrt(q^2-sinh(z)^2)/sinh(z)
           -atan2(sqrt(q^2-sinh(z)^2),cosh(z)).
Then d=q z/sinh(z),
     w=sign(theta) z sqrt(q^2-sinh(z)^2)/sinh(z).
The z=0 limits are d=q and |w|=q.
If |theta|>h: uniquely solve delta in (0,pi) from
 |theta|=delta sqrt(q^2+sin(delta)^2)/sin(delta)
           -atan2(sqrt(q^2+sin(delta)^2),cos(delta)).
Then d=q delta/sin(delta),
     w=sign(theta) delta sqrt(q^2+sin(delta)^2)/sin(delta).

The hyperbolic branch decreases from h to 0 as z increases from 0 to r.
Writing t=sinh z and b=sqrt(q^2-t^2), its derivative is
 q^2(t-z cosh z)/(b t^2)<0.
The elliptic branch increases from h to infinity on (0,pi). Writing
t=sin delta and b=sqrt(q^2+t^2), its derivative is
 q^2(t-delta cos delta)/(b t^2)>0.
Thus only a monotone scalar root is required. The corresponding d increases
strictly with the absolute unwrapped theta.

For v nonzero choose alpha=(arg(v)-w-pi)/2; for pure rotations alpha is free.
Let E=diag(1,-1), J=[[0,-1],[1,0]], and R_phi=exp(phi J). For any admissible
duration T, set a=s/T, k=d/T, omega=w/T and
 A(t)=a I+k R_(alpha+omega t) E R_(-alpha-omega t).
Its response is
 M(t)=e^(-at) R_(alpha+omega t) exp[-(kE+omega J)t] R_(-alpha).
At T this is the requested map. At T_min the largest drift eigenvalue equals
Lambda. This construction requires no unknown specimen future or endpoint fit.

Why these are global minimizers rather than only stationary candidates:
The horizontal distribution on SL2 is contact, since the bracket of the two
symmetric traceless directions is the missing skew direction. A nonconstant
abnormal extremal is impossible: an abnormal covector annihilates both
horizontal directions, and preservation of these annihilations along a
nonzero control forces it also to annihilate their bracket. The covector
would be zero. The normal maximum principle makes the horizontal direction
rotate at constant rate; this gives exactly the displayed exponential family.

In its elliptic regime let delta=sqrt(w^2-d^2). A segment with delta=pi ends
at a rotation independently of alpha. Distinct alpha therefore give equal
length paths to the same point with distinct terminal directions. A path
continuing beyond this first meeting cannot minimize: replace its initial
segment by another such path and retain the tail. The equal-length broken
path would also have to minimize, contradicting the smooth normal extremal
equations. Thus a minimizing non-rotation endpoint has delta<pi.

Within this range the scalar formulas list all candidates. Their costs grow
with absolute unwrapped theta, so among theta+2pi m the principal absolute
angle minimizes. At theta=pi both signs tie. At pure rotations the first
meeting delta=pi gives the distance above; later meetings cost more.
Existence follows from bounded-speed compactness: matrix and inverse norms
are bounded by exp(length), and normalized arclength paths have compact
subsequences. These are standard controls-geometric arguments; the global
geometry itself is prior art, not a novelty claim here.

4. ROTATIONS: AREA, WINDING AND AN ELEMENTARY GLOBAL BOUND

Let N=R_theta, principal |theta|<=pi. P=Y^T Y lies in the determinant-one
positive symmetric matrices, identified with the hyperbolic plane of curvature
-1, with metric ds_H^2=tr[(P^-1 dP)^2]/2. Its speed is 2k and its loop length
is 2K. The connection of the horizontal lift has curvature minus one half of
hyperbolic area. Hence signed area A_signed satisfies
 A_signed/2 = theta + 2pi m for some integer m.

This does not assume a simple loop. For a piecewise smooth loop, resolve its
crossings into oriented Jordan loops. Hyperbolic isoperimetry for each gives
length_j>=sqrt(area_j^2+4pi|area_j|). Summing, the triangle inequality and
subadditivity of sqrt(x^2+4pi x) for x>=0 give the same bound with the total
algebraic area. Approximation extends it to finite-length loops. Therefore
 (2K)^2 >= A_signed^2+4pi|A_signed|
           >=4theta^2+8pi|theta|.

Thus K>=L(theta)=sqrt(theta^2+2pi|theta|), including arbitrary winding and
self-intersections. A circle attains equality. The explicit rotating control
above has w=sign(theta)(pi+|theta|), d=L and
(dE+wJ)^2=-pi^2 I, so its endpoint is exactly R_theta. The shape loop's
closure does not imply that its physical trap axes are initially/finally the
same; endpoint quenches below account for that distinction.

5. THE THERMAL CHANNEL AND A COMPOSING POSITIVE EXCESS

For a prescribed program, the full endpoint is
 X_T=M X_0+eta, eta~N(0,Q), independent of X_0,
 Q=2D integral_0^T Phi(T,t)Phi(T,t)^T dt.
This statement holds for every input distribution independent of the bath;
only actuator-work expectations require a finite second moment.

Put c=D/Lambda. Differentiating Phi(T,t)Phi(T,t)^T with respect to its lower
endpoint gives 2Phi A Phi^T. Hence
 I-MM^T=2 integral Phi A Phi^T <=(Lambda/D)Q,
and the excess matrix
 E_noise=Q-c(I-MM^T)
is positive semidefinite. For channels applied successively,
 M=M2 M1, Q=Q2+M2 Q1 M2^T,
 E_noise=E2+M2 E1 M2^T.

A maximal isotropic relaxation has M_b=bI and Q_b=c(1-b^2)I, so its excess
is zero. Moving this interval from BEFORE an admissible (F,Q) to AFTER it
preserves the mean map and total duration, and reduces covariance by
 (1-b^2)[Q-c(I-FF^T)] >=0.

6. COMPLETE NOISE ORDERING AT THE MINIMUM TIME

Every time-optimal mean realization has K=d and a+k=Lambda almost everywhere.
Its shape curve must be a shortest horizontal curve. Write S=k S_hat, where
S_hat has eigenvalues +/-1. Then its instantaneous drift decomposes as
 A=alpha[(Lambda/2)(I+S_hat)]+(1-alpha)Lambda I,
 alpha=2k/Lambda in [0,1].

Trotter time-splitting approximates this Gaussian evolution by alternating
maximal-speed rank-one shape steps and maximal isotropic steps. The total
shape time is 2d/Lambda and the total isotropic time is (s-d)/Lambda.
Repeatedly moving isotropic steps to the end decreases covariance by the
preceding inequality. The reordered rank-one steps preserve the original
ordered shape path. Taking the bounded-coefficient splitting limit gives:

For each minimizing shape, the least endpoint covariance among all its
minimum-time schedules is achieved by traversing that shape at maximum speed
FIRST, then doing all surplus isotropic cooling LAST.

If (M_g,Q_g) is the fastest rank-one shape stage, M_g=e^(-d)N, put b=e^(-(s-d)).
The exact optimum is
 M_f=b M_g,
 Q_star=b^2 Q_g+c(1-b^2)I.

Different minimizing shapes must be handled, not silently fixed. Away from
the cut locus the shape is unique. For rotations its initial orientation is
free, giving orthogonally conjugate Q_star. At negative symmetric targets the
two shortest shapes are related by a reflection that fixes the target, again
giving conjugate covariances. Thus all shortest shapes have the same noise
eigenvalues and the same radial error law.

For a deterministic program its Q dominates one such rotated Q_star. There
is generally no SINGLE Loewner-smallest matrix across all free orientations.
Sorted eigenvalues, trace and determinant attain their sharp deterministic
minima at the shape-first/cool-last construction.

More strongly, Anderson's Gaussian translation inequality implies that for
each centered Euclidean ball, adding independent Gaussian noise decreases
the probability inside it. Since rotations/reflections preserve the ball,
Q_star simultaneously minimizes EVERY radial error tail among all
minimum-time programs. This is a distribution-level endpoint guarantee,
not only a mean-square or diffraction-type score.

Independent random mixtures of programs with the same branchwise mean map
cannot improve this radial law: condition on the program and average the
same bound. Randomization can, however, improve a coordinate-dependent
variance criterion FOR SCALAR-ATTENUATED ROTATION TARGETS ONLY. For those
targets a fair coin between orientations differing by pi/2 preserves the
target and gives unconditional covariance (tr Q_star/2)I, while retaining
the same radial law. Its noise is a mixture of two Gaussians, not an isotropic
Gaussian. This pi/2 construction does not extend to generic target matrices:
orthogonal conjugation would generally change the requested map.

7. EXPLICIT ROTATION NOISE AND ERROR QUANTILES

Let L=sqrt(theta^2+2pi|theta|), r=L/(pi+|theta|), f=1-exp(-2L). At minimum
attenuation s=L the fastest shape has a=k=Lambda/2 and omega=w Lambda/(2L).
In the rotating frame the drift is B=aI+kE+omega J. Since exp(-BT)=-e^(-L)I,
the added covariance is
 Q_g=(2D/Lambda)f R_(alpha+w)
       [[1, sign(theta) r],[sign(theta) r,1+2r^2]] R_(alpha+w)^T.
The eigenvalues are
 (2D/Lambda)f [1+r^2 +/- r sqrt(1+r^2)].
For surplus attenuation s-L, apply the scalar Q_star formula above.

The radius distribution is specified exactly by
 ||eta||^2 = lambda_1 Z_1^2+lambda_2 Z_2^2,
where Z_1,Z_2 are independent standard normals. Its tail at radius R is
 (2/pi) integral_0^(pi/2)
 exp[-R^2/(2(lambda_1 cos^2(phi)+lambda_2 sin^2(phi)))] dphi.
Every confidence radius is therefore a monotone one-dimensional quadrature
and root, supplied in the executable. No observed endpoint is used to set it.

8. NET ACTUATOR WORK WITH COUNTED ENDPOINT QUENCHES

Assume the control before and after the operation is A=Lambda I. Count the
expected work of switching into/out of the rank-one stage. Let
S(t)=E[X_t X_t^T], including a nonzero mean if present. Ito's formula gives
 W=gamma integral tr[(A^2-Lambda A)S] dt
     +gamma D integral (2Lambda-tr A)dt.

For projector controls A=Lambda P, A^2=Lambda A. The rank-one geometric stage
lasts 2d/Lambda; the rank-two cooling stage contributes zero. Therefore
 W_net=2 gamma D d = 2 k_B T_bath d.

This equality is independent of the input second moment, provided it is
finite. The code independently sums initial quench work, continuous rotating
trap work, and final quench work to verify it. It is a NET thermodynamic work
of the specified ideal control, not a minimum-work theorem, hardware energy
bill, or claim that initial state preparation is free. Work recovery during
negative-work parts and ideal parameter quenches are declared resources.

9. LIMITATIONS THAT CHANGE THE SCIENTIFIC CLAIM

* The normalized geometry and proposed holonomy primitive are prior art.
* The thermal ordering is optimal among minimum-time programs. It is NOT
  asserted optimal among programs allowed additional time. A deformed-loop
  counterexample in the same exact model decreases trace noise at slightly
  longer time; this failed strengthening is recorded separately.
* There is no feedback, anisotropic bath, inertia, nonquadratic confining
  potential, finite slew bound, unknown drift calibration, or apparatus
  manufacturing model in the theorem.
* At the sharp rotation frontier, the required finite within-stage slew is
  ||A_dot||op=Lambda^2(pi+|theta|)/(2L), which diverges as theta tends to zero.
  Endpoint switches are ideal. Thus Lambda alone is not a full actuator cost.
* A rotated vector is attenuated by e^(-s). There is no free output amplifier.
* The result describes an endpoint channel. It does not supply indefinite
  storage, readout, preparation of the input, or a physical demonstration.
* Numerical calculations check formulas; they are not evidence that nature
  violates an older description or that the ideal resource class is available.

NUMERICAL DEFECT AND REPAIR (PRESERVED)

The first floating compiler solved directly for delta near pi, then divided
by sin(delta). An independent audit found about 0.01 normalized endpoint
error at q=1e-12, theta=0.7, despite a well-scaled target matrix. This was a
real numerical defect; the exact analytic theorem was unaffected.

The repair uses epsilon=pi-delta=qz and t=sin(qz)/q=z sinc(qz). It computes
d=(pi-qz)/t, w=d sqrt(1+t^2), and solves
w-atan2(q sqrt(1+t^2),-cos(qz))=|theta| in z. This resolves the small scale
without subtracting nearly equal floating numbers. The old q<1e-14 rotation
substitution was removed. near_rotation_regression.py records the executed
q=1e-2 through 1e-14 regressions, both angle signs, pi, and small angles.
These are floating residual checks, not a certified all-scale compiler.
