GEOMETRIC WORK ORDER UNDER ASSEMBLY
Internal construction, 2026-11-05. No literature search and no physical experiment.

REPRESENTATIONAL INTERVENTION

An effective mechanical body is often represented by a boundary stiffness or
another fixed-order Taylor expansion of boundary energy. Replace that truncated
jet by the first nonzero order of minimal boundary work, together with the exact
geometric relations which compose it. The primitive is the valuation

  ord_0 U = smallest p with U(x)=C*x^p+o(x^p), C>0,

with ord_0 U=infinity for a genuinely zero-energy mechanism. Valuation is not a
new mathematical concept. The question is whether assembly obeys a useful law
which is invisible to any fixed-order stiffness representation.

EXPLICIT ORDINARY-MECHANICAL CONSTRUCTION

Use a planar staircase of ideal frictionless one-dimensional sliders. Let
e_i alternate between the coordinate unit vectors. Put O_0=(0,0) and
O_(i+1)=O_i-l*e_(i+1), with l>0. Slider i is P_i=O_i+q_i*e_i.
Adjacent sliders are joined by an axial element of unstressed length l.
The actual element length is

  r_i = sqrt(q_i^2 + (l-q_(i+1))^2).

There are n such elements, n+1 sliders, and one final ordinary linear spring
joining the last slider to q_n=0. The imposed input is q_0=x. No nonstandard
force law is assumed. In units l=1, all reference coordinates are integers of
magnitude at most n, all rest lengths are 1, and all stiffnesses can be 1.
An explicit coordinate list uses O(n log n) bits; the repeated design rule
uses O(log n) bits plus the integer n. The assembly has O(n) components and
overall linear span O(n*l). The linear guide constraints are declared physical
resources, not silently obtained from the distance constraints.

First use rigid links, r_i=l. In the local branch containing q=0,

  q_(i+1)=F(q_i),  F(q)=l-sqrt(l^2-q^2)
                         =q^2/[l+sqrt(l^2-q^2)].

For |x|<l this gives the complete mechanism q_i=F^i(x), and final-spring energy

  U_n(x)=(k/2)*[F^n(x)]^2.

F(q)=q^2/(2l)+O(q^4), so induction gives

  F^n(x)=x^(2^n)/(2l)^(2^n-1) * [1+O(x^2)]

for each fixed n. Consequently

  U_n(x) ~ (k/2) x^(2^(n+1))/(2l)^(2^(n+1)-2),
  U_n'(x) ~ k*2^n x^(2^(n+1)-1)/(2l)^(2^(n+1)-2).

The work order doubles at every geometric toggle. U_n(x)>0 for x!=0 near zero,
yet every derivative through order 2^(n+1)-1 vanishes at zero. Removing the
terminal spring gives an exact zero-energy mechanism with the identical
boundary energy jet through all those orders.

SHARP QUADRATIC-CONSTRAINT BOUND

Suppose n polynomial constraints f_1,...,f_n of degree at most 2 in n+1 real
coordinates have Jacobian rank n at a reference configuration. The local
configuration set is then a regular real analytic curve and also a regular
complex analytic curve after complexification. Choose a linear input coordinate
x whose tangent derivative is nonzero. Let y be a linear output displacement,
vanishing at the reference point, which is not identically zero on this local
curve. Then

  ord_x y <= 2^n.

Proof: the constraints together with y=0 have an isolated zero on the smooth
local complex curve. Its local intersection multiplicity equals ord_x y. The
local Bezout bound for isolated intersections gives multiplicity at most
deg(y)*product_i deg(f_i) <= 2^n. Other global components are allowed; it is the
local isolated intersection that matters. A terminal spring therefore has
finite work order at most 2^(n+1). The staircase construction attains equality.

Consequently the boundary-energy jet through order 2^(n+1) is sufficient to
distinguish a genuinely zero-energy local motion from an isolated minimum in
this rigid, regular, n-quadratic-constraint family. No lower uniform derivative
order suffices, by the explicit staircase. This is a sharp complexity-in-order
statement, not a numerical condition number or a measurement protocol.

This uses the classical local Bezout theorem as a mathematical input, not as
a newly proved theorem. The bound applies to rigid constraints and linear
observables, after eliminating the declared affine guide constraints. It is
not a general bound on relaxed energies of arbitrary elastic assemblies.
Regular parameter changes preserve the order. Singular parameter changes or
nonlinear output observables require their own degree accounting.

FINITE AXIAL STIFFNESS THEOREM

The explicit construction does not require perfectly rigid links. Give element
i a positive finite axial stiffness K_i and the ordinary Hookean energy

  W_i(q_i,q_(i+1))=(K_i/2)*(r_i-l)^2.

Let k>0 be the terminal-spring stiffness, and define the relaxed boundary
energy by minimizing over the internal slider coordinates:

  U_n(x)= min_(q_1,...,q_n) [sum_(i=0)^(n-1) W_i(q_i,q_(i+1)) + (k/2)q_n^2].

In a neighborhood of zero this minimizer is unique and analytic. Its work
order is exactly 2^(n+1), and

  U_n(x) ~ (k_eff/2) x^(2^(n+1))/(2l)^(2^(n+1)-2),
  k_eff=(1/K_(n-1)+1/k)^(-1).

Thus finite stiffness of all the earlier elements affects higher-order terms,
but does not change this leading exponent or coefficient.

Proof: at the reference configuration the Hessian in internal coordinates is
the positive diagonal matrix diag(K_0,...,K_(n-2),K_(n-1)+k). The analytic
implicit function theorem gives a unique analytic stationary branch and a
strict local minimum for small x.

Eliminate the chain backwards. Suppose a tail has relaxed energy
T(r)=C*r^p+o(r^p), with even integer p>=2 and C>0. The next elimination is

  S(q)=min_r { (K/2)*[sqrt(q^2+(l-r)^2)-l]^2 + T(r) }.

The first energy is exactly zero at r=F(q)=q^2/(2l)+O(q^4), and its second
derivative in r tends to K. If p=2 and T(r)=(k/2)r^2+O(r^4), minimizing the
leading quadratic in r yields

  S(q) ~ [K*k/(2*(K+k))]*q^4/(2l)^2.

If p>2, stationarity and the positive r-Hessian imply
r(q)=F(q)+O(F(q)^(p-1)). The elastic penalty is then O(F(q)^(2p-2)), which is
higher order than T(F(q)). Hence

  S(q) ~ C * q^(2p)/(2l)^p.

Starting with the terminal quadratic gives exponent four and the series
stiffness k_eff at the last element. Every earlier elimination doubles the
exponent and multiplies the coefficient by (2l)^(-p). The formula follows.

The stationary branch is the global minimum for sufficiently small x. Bounded
total energy bounds q_n via the terminal spring and bounds every element
length, hence successively bounds all slider coordinates. Every sequence of
global minimizers as x->0 has a convergent subsequence with limiting energy
zero. Zero energy forces q_n=0; r_(n-1)=l then forces q_(n-1)=0, and induction
forces all coordinates zero. Thus global minimizers approach the origin,
where the analytic minimum is unique.

EXACT CONSEQUENCE FOR FIXED-ORDER EFFECTIVE DESCRIPTIONS

For every finite derivative order d, choose n with 2^(n+1)>d. Two assemblies
of n axial elements and n+1 sliders, differing only by the terminal spring,
have the same zero boundary-energy d-jet. One has a true zero-energy motion,
and the other has an isolated stable minimum. Therefore no uniform finite
derivative order distinguishes the behaviors over this family. The number
of elements grows logarithmically in the invisible derivative order.

This is an exact theorem, not a numerical pattern or a measured effect.
The passive construction uses ordinary positive stiffnesses and explicit
geometry. It does not require a fictional material law or active feedback.

ANGULAR IMPERFECTIONS AND THEIR COST

Keep the same construction but let delta_i=e_i dot e_(i+1), rather than zero.
The length becomes

  r_i^2=q_i^2+(l-q_(i+1))^2+2*delta_i*q_i*(l-q_(i+1)).

For a rigid link the local relation is exactly

  F_delta(q)=l+delta*q-sqrt(l^2-(1-delta^2)*q^2)
            =delta*q+(1-delta^2)*q^2/(2l)+O(q^4).

If every delta_i is nonzero, the terminal displacement has a linear term
(product_i delta_i)*x. Generic angular errors therefore restore ordinary
quadratic boundary energy. For finite axial stiffnesses the exact linearized
boundary stiffness is

  k_lin=(product_(i=0)^(n-1) delta_i)^2 /
        [1/k + sum_(j=0)^(n-1) (product_(i=j+1)^(n-1) delta_i)^2/K_j].

Proof: put e_j=q_(j+1)-delta_j*q_j. Then q_n=A*x+sum_j B_j*e_j, with
A=product delta_i and B_j=product_(i=j+1)^(n-1) delta_i. Minimize the positive
quadratic 1/2 sum K_j e_j^2+k/2(A*x+sum B_j e_j)^2. A one-dimensional Schur
complement gives the formula. Empty products are 1.

For equal small errors delta, k_lin is asymptotic to k_eff*delta^(2n).
The stiffness can be very small but is generically nonzero.

For the rigid ideal chain let q_i=F^i(x). The exact first-order relative output
sensitivity at zero angular errors is

  d(log q_n)/d(delta_i) = (q_i/q_(i+1)) *
    product_(j=i+1)^(n-1) [1+l/sqrt(l^2-q_j^2)].

At the last stage this is q_(n-1)/q_n ~ 2l/q_(n-1). To keep a small fixed
relative error in output while preserving the ideal nonlinear regime, the
last-stage angular error must be of order at most epsilon*q_(n-1)/(2l).
For fixed n and x/l tending to zero,

  q_(n-1)/(2l) ~ (x/(2l))^(2^(n-1)),

This gives the leading small-amplitude precision estimate

  b approximately 2^(n-1)*log2(2l/|x|) + log2(1/epsilon).

That fixed-n asymptotic must not be mistaken for an additive-O(1) estimate
uniform in n. For a uniform size bound, set r=|x|/l<1 and d=1+sqrt(1-r^2).
Monotonicity and r_i^2/2 <= F(l*r_i)/l <= r_i^2/d imply the exact inequalities

  (r/2)^(2^i) <= q_i/(2l) <= (d/2)*(r/d)^(2^i).

Hence, up to O(1) factors from the local sensitivity convention, the last-stage
precision bit count is between

  2^(n-1)*log2(d/r)+log2(2/d)+log2(1/epsilon)
and
  2^(n-1)*log2(2/r)+log2(1/epsilon).

It is exponential in n at any fixed 0<r<1. This is a declared precision cost,
not an experimentally measured machining requirement. The exact high order is not
robust under arbitrary geometric perturbations. Dimensional offsets, preload,
friction, guide compliance and thermal fluctuations are further costs absent
from this ideal model.

PHYSICAL AND SCIENTIFIC LIMITS

Frictionless sliders, fixed geometry, zero prestress and quasistatic relaxation
are assumptions. No physical prototype, loading time, useful delivered output,
thermal stability, acquisition apparatus or manufacturing process was built.
The model construction is complete; a useful physical metamaterial capability
is not established. Exponential order does not mean a free exponential
engineering gain, because the relevant force/work and geometric tolerances
also become extremely small.

INCUMBENT AND NOVELTY AUDIT FROM MEMORY ONLY

Higher-order infinitesimal rigidity, prestress stability, singular mechanisms,
normal forms, geometric stiffness and Lojasiewicz exponents already describe
the relevant mathematical terrain. Ordinary nonlinear finite-element or exact
multibody mechanics supplied the same springs and geometry calculates this
response. Partial minimization of energy is standard. Valuation and its
multiplicative behavior under composition are standard algebraic tools.

The sharp quadratic-constraint contact-order bound uses classical Bezout.
The explicit finite-stiffness staircase theorem is a completed deduction,
but I cannot identify it responsibly as a new organizing principle or as a
result previously inaccessible to an equally informed strong incumbent.
It is a constructive challenge to fixed-order effective descriptions, with
priority and scientific value still requiring primary-literature challenge.
It does not by itself satisfy the user's full breakthrough target.

PRESERVED UNSUCCESSFUL STEPS

1. A bare polynomial squaring chain q_(i+1)=q_i^2 was considered first. It did
   not by itself earn a mechanical realization. It was replaced by the actual
   slider/bar geometry above, whose exact relation is circular rather than
   an invented polynomial force law.
2. Perfectly rigid links initially appeared necessary. Backwards minimization
   showed that finite positive Hookean axial stiffnesses preserve the work
   order, with an explicitly changed leading coefficient.
3. A naive extrapolation of the fixed-n small-x formula would have claimed an
   additive-O(1) tolerance estimate uniformly in n. The Taylor errors themselves
   amplify under iteration. The explicit uniform bounds above replace that
   incorrect uniform claim.
4. The finite-jet obstruction does not defeat an equally informed nonlinear
   mechanical model. The proposed primitive's discovery value remains unearned
   unless the primary-literature challenge finds a substantive new consequence.
