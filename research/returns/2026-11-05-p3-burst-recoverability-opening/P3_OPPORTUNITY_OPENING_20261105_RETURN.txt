## Selected question: the sharp boundary for recoverable information under spatial burst damage

**I select the following full question:**

> **When faults can corrupt arbitrarily large spatial regions, is \(\alpha>d+1\) the exact low-intensity boundary for preserving and retrieving initially finite information using local operations, when burst radii satisfy \(\Pr(R>r)=r^{-\alpha}\)? Can an explicit operation attain that boundary with finite commissioning, corruptible control and decoding state, bounded local resources, and a physically specified readout?**

The first concrete construction target is two spatial dimensions, where the proposed boundary is **\(\alpha=3\)**.

The opening establishes the impossibility side and derives the scaling that an attaining construction would need to match. **The recovery construction is not yet proved.** That is the central operation I would take into the question-level exchange.

A complete resolution would give a consequential classification: it would identify when local redundancy and repair can preserve any initially finite information, and when every such controller must eventually forget—even though the average local disturbance rate is finite. The positive result would have to return useful information. The negative result already constrains every subsequent computation or decision that must distinguish its original inputs.

This selection was made independently of the other researchers’ new tournament proposals. I investigated alternative openings, compared them with primary research, and retained their derivations and rejection reasons.

## 1. The operational target

The task is to retain an unknown bit \(B\), initially equally likely to be zero or one, and return an estimate at a requested finite time. A fixed nontrivial guarantee, such as

\[
\Pr(\widehat B_T\ne B)\le \frac14
\qquad\text{for every finite requested horizon }T,
\]

is enough to establish the foundational distinction.

The quantifiers matter. Fix the burst law and a sufficiently small **positive intensity** first. The initial input-dependent seed may depend on those parameters and the desired fixed accuracy, but **its spatial extent cannot grow with the requested horizon**. One uniform construction algorithm may produce different finite layouts and schedules for different horizons, with their hardware and execution costs charged.

That formulation allows ordinary engineering choices without supplying the information being protected. It also avoids making autonomous fabrication or translation-invariant self-organization an unnecessary requirement.

The model has four substantive premises.

**Complete information support.** At the end of commissioning, every accessible record that can depend on \(B\) lies within

\[
C_0=[-r_0,r_0]^d.
\]

This includes backups, keys, controller records, retained outputs and any external copy that can assist recovery. Everything outside has a joint law independent of \(B\). No later source reinjects the answer.

**Local operation.** All usable input information propagates at speed at most \(v\). Local state, degree, hardware density and update rate are bounded independently of the horizon. Finite-range, bounded-depth local circuit layers provide an exact mathematical model of this condition.

**Spatial burst faults.** Burst centers form a homogeneous Poisson process of intensity \(\lambda\,dz\,dt\). A burst with radius \(R\) affects

\[
z+[-R,R]^d.
\]

For the impossibility theorem, it resets every accessible mutable state in that cube to an input-independent state. This is one fault allowed within the stronger arbitrary-corruption class that the positive construction should tolerate. A known reversible flip would require a different argument.

**A useful endpoint.** Readout must use a specified finite local procedure. Signal collection, decoding, faults during waiting, output storage and metrology count. If storage lasts \(T\) and reading takes additional time, the guarantee must cover the actual delivery time.

Immutable local gate laws and a bounded-rate input-independent schedule remain declared mathematical premises. All mutable decoding and control records are exposed to faults. A physical platform claim would additionally have to earn those gate and scheduling abstractions. The present result is a foundational opening within this contract.

## 2. Established opening: a burst can erase every possible copy

The decisive observation is architectural.

At time \(t\), every possible difference between an execution starting with \(B=0\) and one starting with \(B=1\) lies within

\[
C_t=[-(r_0+vt),\,r_0+vt]^d.
\]

A burst covering this entire cube destroys every accessible record of the distinction.

For a radius-\(R\) burst, the set of centers that cover \(C_t\) has volume

\[
\bigl[2(R-r_0-vt)_+\bigr]^d.
\]

Consequently, the number of complete-cover bursts by time \(T\) is Poisson with mean

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda(T)
=
\lambda 2^d
\int_0^T
\mathbb E\!\left[(R-r_0-vt)_+^d\right]dt.
}
\]

Integrating first for a fixed radius gives the exact identity

\[
\Lambda(T)
=
\frac{\lambda 2^d}{v(d+1)}
\mathbb E\!\left[
(R-r_0)_+^{d+1}
-
(R-r_0-vT)_+^{d+1}
\right].
\]

This is the **expectation of the difference**. It remains meaningful when the two separate higher moments are infinite. The assumption \(\mathbb E R^d<\infty\) makes the finite-\(T\) expression finite.

### The information-loss theorem

Let \(P_b^T\) denote the law of all accessible state at time \(T\), conditional on \(B=b\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}(P_0^T,P_1^T)
\le e^{-\Lambda(T)}.
}
\]

Hence even an ideal global decoder has binary error

\[
\boxed{
P_e(T)\ge \frac{1-e^{-\Lambda(T)}}2.
}
\]

The proof couples the two input worlds using the same outside initial state, randomness, burst field and future controller randomness. Their differences remain inside \(C_t\). At the first complete-cover reset, all differences disappear. The worlds then evolve identically. The probability that this has not happened is \(e^{-\Lambda(T)}\); the coupling inequality and binary testing identity give the bounds.

This proof permits arbitrary adaptive repair, coding, replication and computation within the information-propagation contract. It also grants an unrealistically powerful final decoder, strengthening the impossibility statement.

The same argument bounds retained information:

\[
I(B;X_T)\le e^{-\Lambda(T)}H(B).
\]

Revealing whether a complete-cover reset occurred cannot decrease mutual information. Conditional on such a reset, the final state contains no information about the input.

Taking \(T\to\infty\),

\[
\Lambda(\infty)
=
\frac{\lambda 2^d}{v(d+1)}
\mathbb E(R-r_0)_+^{d+1}.
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E R^d<\infty,\qquad
\mathbb E R^{d+1}=\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
P_e(T)\longrightarrow \frac12
}
\]

for every finite seed and every admissible local controller.

This applies uniformly to the entire family of horizon-specific devices described above. More input-independent hardware, different wiring or a different schedule cannot change the initial support constraint or the propagation bound.

**The missing converse is substantive:** finite \(\mathbb E R^{d+1}\) removes this particular obstruction. It does not establish recovery.

## 4. The constructive opening and the operation still missing

There is an established local mechanism with the right isolated-event timescale: the two-dimensional north-east-center Toom rule,

\[

=
\operatorname{maj}
\{x_t(z),x_t(z+e_1),x_t(z+e_2)\}.
\]

On an otherwise correct uniform background, an error set inside an \(a\times b\) rectangle disappears within \(a+b-1\) synchronous rounds and never leaves the rectangle. To see this, monotonicity reduces the problem to a completely erroneous rectangle. Its surviving sites at round \(t\) satisfy

\[
(a-1-x)+(b-1-y)\ge t.
\]

Induction proves the formula; complement symmetry handles either stored bit. An isolated radius-\(R\) corruption therefore has repair time \(O(R)\), using constant-size local state and local updates. Toom’s stability theory and modern contour methods are existing machinery available for this programme. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

### Why isolated repair does not compose automatically

Take two initial error sets,

\[
A=\{(1,0)\},\qquad B=\{(0,1)\}.
\]

Each singleton disappears after one round. Together they produce an error at the origin:

\[
F(A)=F(B)=\varnothing,
\qquad
F(A\cup B)=\{(0,0)\}.
\]

A diagonal of \(m+1\) such singleton errors generates successive diagonals and survives \(m+1\) rounds.

This exact example defeats a tempting shortcut: assigning every fault its own independent repair region and taking their union does not contain the collective error evolution. Interactions can prolong damage.

There is a second preparation obstacle. Toom’s rule also erodes a finite island of the desired bit in the opposite blank background. Its stability around an infinite prepared phase does not supply the finite commissioned input required here.

The constructive target must therefore preserve the **meaning of the input** while expanding, repairing and decoding it. The repair metadata and decoding registers belong to the fault model.

This counts bursts centered outside the device as well. Their occurrence probability is at most \(\mu_L\).
