\[

\tag{11}

=
\frac{3m\gamma}{4}
+\frac4\gamma\sum_{|Q|=1}h_Q^2.

\tag{12}

Equivalently,

### Proof

Any representation (10) gives
\[
\mathrm{id}+\mathcal L/\Lambda
=
\sum_j\frac{\lambda_j}{\Lambda}\Psi_j,
\]
which must be CPTP. Conversely, if this Euler map is CPTP, it supplies a single channel event at rate \(\Lambda\).

Use the orthonormal Pauli–Choi vectors
\[
|P\rangle\rangle=(P\otimes I)|\Omega\rangle.
\]
The normalized Choi matrix of the Euler map is
\[
\begin{aligned}
J(\mathrm{id}+\mathcal L/\Lambda)
={}&
\left(1-\frac{3m\gamma}{4\Lambda}\right)
|I\rangle\rangle\langle\langle I|\\
&+\frac{\gamma}{4\Lambda}
\sum_{|Q|=1}|Q\rangle\rangle\langle\langle Q|\\
&-\frac i\Lambda
\sum_{P\ne I}h_P
\left(
|P\rangle\rangle\langle\langle I|
-
|I\rangle\rangle\langle\langle P|
\right).
\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
\]

If a coefficient \(h_P\ne0\) has weight at least two, the principal minor on \(I,P\) is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
1-\dfrac{3m\gamma}{4\Lambda}& ih_P/\Lambda\\[3pt]
-ih_P/\Lambda&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is
\[
-\frac{h_P^2}{\Lambda^2}<0.
\tag{14}
\]
Complete positivity fails for every finite \(\Lambda\).

When all terms have weight one, the remaining Choi matrix has a positive diagonal lower block. Its Schur complement is
\[
1-\frac{3m\gamma}{4\Lambda}
-\frac4{\gamma\Lambda}\sum_{|Q|=1}h_Q^2.
\]
Nonnegativity is exactly (12).

The construction can then be local: for each site choose
\[

+\frac4\gamma\sum_{\alpha=X,Y,Z}h_{i,\alpha}^2,
\qquad
\Psi_i=\mathrm{id}+\mathcal L_i/\Lambda_i.
\]
These are one-qubit CPTP channels and their generators sum to \(\mathcal L\).
