### The local operation: regularization followed by computation

Encode one logical bit in five spatially separated information registers. Call an encoding *regular* when at least four agree. Workspace can be arbitrary.

A three-pass round operates as follows. Each pass resets its work registers, copies the relevant input replicas into message lanes, routes them locally, majority-decodes them, computes the desired fixed-fanin Boolean function, and broadcasts the result to five holding positions. Separate holding tracks preserve the three pass results. Each output information register takes their local majority.

The spatial replica separations and temporal gaps exceed a fixed fault-island width \(w\).

Starting from regular inputs, one fault island can introduce at most one additional wrong spatial replica and spoil at most one whole temporal pass. The other passes agree, and all but at most one output replica receive the correct result.

The crucial addition is **one normalizing round before the logical round**:

- If the island hits normalization, the subsequent logical round is clean. It sees fixed input words and writes a perfect repetition of their decoded result.
- If the island hits the logical round, normalization was clean and supplied perfect repetitions.
- An island in a separating gap or final commit affects at most one replica.

This gives two deterministic properties:

\[
\textbf{R:}\quad
\text{a nice macrostep produces a regular output from arbitrary input;}
\]
\[
\textbf{C:}\quad
\text{a nice macrostep correctly updates regular encoded inputs.}
\]

All input information is frozen during the passes, and output commits are synchronized globally. Fixed tracks and spatial coloring implement the routing with bounded local gates.
