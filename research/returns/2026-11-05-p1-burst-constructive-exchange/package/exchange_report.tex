\textbf{Selected completed target.}
For instantaneous Poisson cube bursts in $d$ dimensions with
$\Prb(R>r)=r^{-\alpha}$, determine which tail exponents permit a fixed finite input-dependent seed to retain a classical bit for every requested finite horizon, using bounded local state, speed and density, a uniform finite circuit, and a paid local readout. Immutable gate laws and an input-independent schedule are granted as in P3's final refinement.

\textbf{Result.}
At sufficiently small positive intensity, the operation is possible precisely when $\alpha>d+1$. For $\alpha\le d+1$, the accepted complete-cone erasure theorem forbids it at every positive intensity. The construction here supplies the positive direction, with output error at most $1/4$ uniformly in the requested horizon.

\textbf{Quantitative operation.}
For a family-dependent exponent $1<z<\alpha-d$, the mutable line uses $O(T^{1/z})$ cells, delivery takes $O(T)$ time, and local work is $O(T^{1+1/z})$. The exponent approaches the necessary extent exponent $1/(\alpha-d)$ from above. Constants and the admitted intensity can be severe.

\textbf{P2 response.}
Finite noisy position measurements and additive force synthesize a momentum replacement independently of a hidden constant force and old reference records in nominal time $1.2/\gamma$, without learning the force. A more expensive route directly prepares the original sine-biased arms in $1.1/\gamma$. Uniform interval certificates cover the entire soft-spring range. Preparation, calibration, cooling, switching and reuse remain material costs.

\textbf{P1 retained and strengthened.}
The accepted thermal-loading compiler remains at scope. An extensive Fano-minor certificate for the actual BDL check matrices excludes both logarithmic graphic/cographic defect guarantees and the regular-matroid sampler route. It does not prove slow mixing or sampling hardness.

\textbf{Status.}
This is a mathematical construction with reproducible finite certificates, not a physically built device or an established historical-priority claim. Complete proof notes, independent audits and code accompany this report. The selected theorem concerns strict Pareto tails and the granted circuit primitives; it does not claim autonomous control or the general finite-$(d+1)$-moment endpoint.

\section{The exact theorem}
Fix $d\ge1$. Centers and times form a Poisson process of intensity $\lambda\,dz\,dt$. Independent radii obey $\Prb(R>r)=r^{-\alpha}$ for $r\ge1$, with $\alpha>d$. A burst instantaneously corrupts all mutable registers in $z+[-R,R]^d$, with arbitrary input-dependent values. The finite $d$th moment makes the local hit rate finite.

Hardware has bounded density, degree, local state, arity, range, rate and information speed. A fixed Boolean basis supplies COPY, reset and local computation. Workspace, messages, outputs and mutable control records remain corruptible. Immutable wiring, laws and an input-independent schedule may depend on $T$, as granted by P3's final refinement; one uniform algorithm produces them, and description/preparation costs count. Their reliability is a premise.\cite{p3}

Every input-dependent variable starts inside a fixed finite seed of radius $r_0$, independent of $T$. Horizon-dependent input-independent hardware contains no copy of the bit. A finite local decoder delivers it at $S(T)\ge T$, and every fault exposure through delivery counts.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp tail exponent at low intensity]\label{thm:main}
If $d<\alpha\le d+1$, every positive intensity prevents a horizon-uniform output error at most $1/4$ from a fixed finite input seed. If $\alpha>d+1$, a uniform finite circuit family achieves that operation for all sufficiently small positive intensities.

For fixed integers $B,U$ satisfying
\[
 U\ge c_0B,\qquad z=\log U/\log B\in(1,\alpha-d),
\]
the active binary line has $O(T^{1/z}+1)$ cells, total delivery takes $O(T+1)$ time, and local work is $O(T^{1+1/z}+1)$. Constants and the admitted intensity depend on the chosen family.
\end{theorem}
This is a low-intensity tail classification, not an exact critical-intensity calculation or a theorem for all non-Pareto laws with finite $\E R^{d+1}$.

\section{Necessity}
The accepted P3 coupling confines the two input worlds' difference to a cone of radius $r_0+vt$. A burst covering it can erase all input-dependent registers to common values. With
\[
 \Lambda(S)=\lambda2^d\int_0^S\E(R-r_0-vt)_+^d\,dt,
\]
the complete outputs obey
\[
 \TV(P_0^S,P_1^S)\le e^{-\Lambda(S)},\quad
 P_{\rm error}\ge\tfrac12(1-e^{-\Lambda(S)}).
\]
This holds uniformly over the permitted horizon-specific schedules.\cite{p3}
For $a\ge1$, Pareto integration gives
\[
 \E(R-a)_+^d=\frac{d!}{\prod_{j=1}^d(\alpha-j)}a^{d-\alpha}.
\]
Thus $\Lambda(S)$ diverges exactly for $\alpha\le d+1$. At equality, for $r_0\ge1$,
\[
 \Lambda(S)=\frac{\lambda2^d}{v}\log\frac{r_0+vS}{r_0}.
\]
A finite error rate at each cell therefore does not suffice. A fixed-extent device also has ceiling $T\lesssim L^{\alpha-d}/\lambda$ at fixed accuracy. Our construction approaches this extent exponent; it does not prove a universal cell-count optimum for sparse or moving architectures.

\section{The central operation and the existing comparator}
Gacs supplies separated spatial replicas and repeated temporal computation.\cite{gacs} The closer comparator is the hardwired Tsirelson/exRec machinery of Balasubramanian--Davydova--Lake, which handles arbitrary bad-gadget outputs and incoming damage, and explicitly discusses repeated correction.\cite{bdlake} These are credited existing principles.

Its $p$-bounded theorem does not directly apply to Pareto bursts: one event corrupts many adjacent cells with polynomial probability, violating a bound $p^m$ for a fault set of size $m$. The fixed Tsirelson space/time factors $3,6$ also do not automatically approach the causal exponent. The needed bridge is a bounded-island circuit with adjustable block length $B$ and time $O(B)$.

\subsection{Two rounds, six passes}
Five separated Info bits encode one bit. A regular word has at least four agreeing copies; workspace may be arbitrary. A nice fault set fits within one spatial interval and one temporal interval of fixed width $w$.

A three-pass round freezes Info. Each pass resets Work, copies neighboring input replicas into fixed message lanes, routes locally, majority-decodes, computes the desired Boolean function, and broadcasts to five Hold positions. Three Hold tracks and gaps longer than $w$ separate the passes. Each output Info takes its local three-Hold majority. All spatial colors finish a pass before the next starts; all outputs commit only after the layer is ready.

With regular inputs, one old wrong replica and one new spatial corruption still leave three correct copies. One island spoils at most one entire pass and corrupts all retained Holds at at most one output position. Other positions receive two correct Holds. Output is regular and logically correct.

Prepend one normalizing three-pass round. If the island hits normalization, the subsequent clean logical round produces a perfect repetition from the resulting fixed words. If it hits the logical round, normalization supplied perfect inputs. Gap/commit faults affect one replica. We obtain:
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item[R.] Nice macrosteps regularize arbitrary incoming registers.
\item[C.] Nice macrosteps correctly update regular input encodings.
\end{description}

Let $I_t(i)$ mark irregular Info and $\Bad(i,t)$ a non-nice macrostep window. Then
\[
 I_{t+1}(i)\le1_{\Bad(i,t)},\qquad
 E'\subseteq{\cal P}(E):=\Bad(E)\cup{\cal N}^{+}\Bad(E).
\]
Here $\mathcal N^{+}$ advances one step and expands by the fixed input neighborhood. This is a monotone, value-independent support bound for every raw fault field. A damaged encoding cannot leave an indefinitely propagating invalid structure.

Fixed lanes, coloring and nearest-neighbor COPY/SWAP give $O(B)$ time. Flatten tracks into adjacent binary sites; Info moves only within a fixed microcell, while long routes carry copies. Recursively compile every binary site, including workspace. The alphabet stays fixed. Padding uses explicit identity/reset gates, themselves protected at the next level. Hence $L_k=B^k$ and $\tau_k=U^k$.

Choose $U=\lceil B^{z_*}\rceil$ with $1<z_*<\alpha-d$ and sufficiently large $B$. The exact exponent $z=\log U/\log B$ meets the strict inequalities. Full routing and boundary details are in the formal compiler supplement.
\section{Probability under the actual burst law}
Restrict the circuit to a line. Integrating transverse burst centers gives the marked intensity
\[
 \lambda(2R)^{d-1}\,dx\,dt\,F(dR).
\]
The line sees a radius law tilted by $R^{d-1}$, with effective exponent $\alpha-d+1$. Using the original one-dimensional law would give the wrong threshold.

Define $E_{k+1}={\cal P}(E_k)$. A specified level-$k$ test depends on raw faults in a local spacetime box. Including the preceding-period and neighbor guard, its half-spans satisfy
\[
 \rho_k\le aL_k+\rho_{k-1},\qquad
 \eta_k\le b\tau_k+\eta_{k-1}
\]
for fixed $a,b$. Their ratios to $L_k,\tau_k$ are uniformly bounded by geometric sums. Choose $w$ larger than twice those bounds and a radius guard, then choose $B$ larger again. A non-nice parent contains two child tests sufficiently separated in space or time.

Let $p_k$ bound any specified level-$k$ support-test probability. First charge every burst with $R\ge cL_k$ intersecting an enlarged parent region. Its mean count is at most
\[
 \lambda\tau_{k+1}
 \E[(A L_{k+1}+2R)(2R)^{d-1}1_{\{R\ge cL_k\}}]
 \le D\lambda\theta^{k+1},\quad \theta=UB^{d-\alpha}.
\]
After removing those marks, two separated child tests have disjoint primitive Poisson influence regions. Spatial separation uses the radius cutoff; temporal separation uses instantaneous bursts. The small-radius fields are independent. Monotonicity bounds each test probability by $p_k$, without conditioning on successful data history.

There are at most $C(B,U)$ candidate pairs, hence
\[
 p_{k+1}\le Cp_k^2+D\lambda\theta^{k+1}.
\]
The square follows from disjoint primitive regions, not multiplied marginal estimates. The base bound is $p_0\le C_0\lambda$. For
\[
 A_0\ge\max(C_0,2D),\qquad \lambda\le\frac{\theta}{2CA_0},
\]
induction gives
\[
 \boxed{p_k\le A_0\lambda\theta^k.}
\]
Each term consumes at most half the next bound. The scale errors are summable when $1<z<\alpha-d$, possible precisely for $\alpha>d+1$.

\section{Finite seed, growth and local delivery}
A large initially perfect repetition code would evade the central question. Instead start from a fixed finite seed and, if needed, a fixed writer. Its exposure costs at most $C_{\rm prep}\lambda$ plus its declared entry error.

At level $k$, an ordinary local child circuit copies the current logical bit into five Info positions of a level-$(k+1)$ block. It includes reset, transport, waiting identities and guards. At most $M(B)=O(B^2)$ child spacetime locations suffice, a fixed family constant. Its failure is at most $M(B)p_k$ by a union bound.

Unused hardware may start arbitrarily. A constant reset puts zero into every clean Hold pass regardless of incoming Info, so a nice reset produces regular zero. Warm scale 1, then scale 2, and so on. Lower failures stay in the support envelope; one does not demand that every microscopic reset succeed. Parallel warmup costs $O(\sum_{j\le k}\tau_j)=O(\tau_k)$ while the retained source executes a fixed number of protected level-$k$ identities.

The destination is fresh and disjoint from the source; reset masks never erase the source. Successive adjacent intervals occupy only a geometric sum $O(L_K)$. Deterministic phase padding aligns all child macro-boundaries, with preceding-period guards retained.

The precise adapter identities are
\[
 C^{k+1}(f)=C^k(C(f)),\quad
 \mathrm{up}=C^k(\mathrm{Enc}_B),\quad
 \mathrm{down}=C^k(\mathrm{Dec}_B).
\]
They use $O(B)$ child depth and $O(B^2)$ child spacetime. The lower-level ports and decoders do not change across an adapter; only the outer grouping changes.

When its finitely many coarse locations work, an upward conversion produces the exact next-level projected encoding. Lower physical residues remain in the support field and guards. Downward conversion locally collects five child values, votes, and retains one child encoding, with the same error bound. Iteration reaches a fixed finite terminal circuit. No global decoder, external backup or early protected transcript is used.

Given $T$, choose $K$ with $\tau_K\ge T$ and $\tau_K=O(T+1)$. Ascend, hold as needed, then descend. Delivery takes $O(T+1)$, including possible $O(T)$ readout delay; the bit remains exposed until delivery. For fixed starting level $k_0$, total error is at most
\[
 \delta_{\rm prep}
 +2M(B)A_0\lambda\frac{\theta^{k_0}}{1-\theta}
 +C_{\rm hold}A_0\lambda\theta^K
 +\delta_{\rm terminal}.
\]
Choose fixed writer/terminal budgets and sufficiently small positive intensity to make this at most $1/4$, uniformly in $T$. This completes Theorem \ref{thm:main}.
