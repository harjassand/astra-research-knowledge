\[

\sum_{S\subseteq J}

-\mu\!\left(\bigcup_{i\in S}E_i\right)

A useful upper bound is

\[
\boxed{
\Pr\!\left\{\mathsf N(E_i)\ge1\ \forall i\in J\right\}

\[

b_{\{1\}}b_{\{2\}}+b_{\{1,2\}}.

## 3. A precise failure of the marginal waiting-time argument

I worked out an exact example because it identifies a gap in the proposed continuous-time threshold transfer.

Suppose \(k\) operations all wait for one common service completion time

\[
S\sim\operatorname{Exp}(v).
\]

Each operation has its own independent rate-\(q\) damaging source, also independent of \(S\). There are no spatial burst correlations in this example.

Each marginal failure probability is

\[
\Pr\{\text{operation }i\text{ is hit before }S\}
=
\frac{q}{q+v}.
\]

Nevertheless, putting \(a=q/v\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_k
&=
\Pr\{\text{every operation is hit before }S\}\\
&=
\mathbb E(1-e^{-qS})^k\\
&=
\frac{\Gamma(1+1/a)\Gamma(k+1)}
{\Gamma(k+1+1/a)}\\
&=
\frac{k!a^k}{\prod_{j=1}^k(1+ja)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For fixed \(a>0\),

\[
P_k\sim \Gamma(1+1/a)\,k^{-1/a}.
\]

Thus the joint failure probability is polynomial in \(k\), despite the constant small marginal. No fixed \(p<1\) gives \(P_k\le p^k\) for all \(k\).

For example, with \(q/v=0.05\) and \(k=100\),

\[
P_{100}\approx 3.39\times10^{-23},
\]

whereas multiplying the marginals would give approximately

\[
\left(\frac{0.05}{1.05}\right)^{100}
\approx 6.00\times10^{-133}.
\]

This is a counterexample to an inference, not an autonomous-computation impossibility result. In particular, it does not revive the naive marching-rule deadlock argument. It shows why a finite mean repair time, an exponential completion tail, or an \(O(q/v)\) marginal hazard cannot by itself establish the joint noise condition needed for recursive fault tolerance.
