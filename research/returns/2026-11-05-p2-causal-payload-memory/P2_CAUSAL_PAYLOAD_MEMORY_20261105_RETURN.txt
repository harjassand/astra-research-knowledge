A sufficient composable error allocation is

\[

\[

\[
O\!\left(\zeta^{-2}\log(1/\delta)\right)

## 5. What this adds to the strongest applicable work

The general phenomenon of late information forcing quantum memory is established. The contribution here needs a narrower comparison.

**Ballester, Wehner, and Winter** already showed that predicting one Boolean answer after late basis information can require retaining the entire input for perfect performance. Their discussion following that construction raises the near-perfect case. The bare “one answer can require full memory” phenomenon is not new. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

**Bluhm, Rauber, and Wolf** developed measurement-relative compression with unrestricted classical side records, exact incompressibility, and small-error stability for fixed observable families. Their compactness argument does not supply the dimension-uniform numerical threshold used here, and their principal common-decompressor formulation preserves specified observables for all input states. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

**Ioannou and collaborators** provide the closer operational framework: common quantum/classical compression followed by setting-dependent measurements, with low-rank channel characterisations and substantive quantitative bounds. Their analytic bound for all isotropically noisy rank-one projective measurements uses the entire unitary-symmetric measurement family. That premise does not follow from this construction’s finite binary-probe family and average score. Their result therefore does not directly supply the gap-dependent implication proved here. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

The early measurement-and-correction mechanism belongs to established **environment-assisted correction**. An equally informed early receiver can use that approach; no advantage over it is claimed. The present operation supplies the local realisation and proves what delaying its basis record costs. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

The completed increment is the combination of:

- an explicit finite-error bound from a **finite family’s commutator gap and average binary-probe score**;
- applicability to selector-dependent decoders recovering one arbitrary payload qubit;
- every-prefix and expected retained-qubit-time bounds;
- an explicit short-record source and exact once-through local receiver;
- physical preparation, timing, precision, and emission-error accounting.

This is a scoped derivation and construction, not an exhaustive historical-priority claim.

5. What this adds to the strongest applicable work

The general phenomenon of late information forcing quantum memory is established. The contribution here needs a narrower comparison.

Ballester, Wehner, and Winter already showed that predicting one Boolean answer after late basis information can require retaining the entire input for perfect performance. Their discussion following that construction raises the near-perfect case. The bare “one answer can require full memory” phenomenon is not new. 
arxiv.org

Bluhm, Rauber, and Wolf developed measurement-relative compression with unrestricted classical side records, exact incompressibility, and small-error stability for fixed observable families. Their compactness argument does not supply the dimension-uniform numerical threshold used here, and their principal common-decompressor formulation preserves specified observables for all input states. 
arxiv.org

Ioannou and collaborators provide the closer operational framework: common quantum/classical compression followed by setting-dependent measurements, with low-rank channel characterisations and substantive quantitative bounds. Their analytic bound for all isotropically noisy rank-one projective measurements uses the entire unitary-symmetric measurement family. That premise does not follow from this construction’s finite binary-probe family and average score. Their result therefore does not directly supply the gap-dependent implication proved here. 
arxiv.org

The early measurement-and-correction mechanism belongs to established environment-assisted correction. An equally informed early receiver can use that approach; no advantage over it is claimed. The present operation supplies the local realisation and proves what delaying its basis record costs. 
arxiv.org

The completed increment is the combination of:

an explicit finite-error bound from a finite family’s commutator gap and average binary-probe score;
applicability to selector-dependent decoders recovering one arbitrary payload qubit;
every-prefix and expected retained-qubit-time bounds;
an explicit short-record source and exact once-through local receiver;
physical preparation, timing, precision, and emission-error accounting.

This is a scoped derivation and construction, not an exhaustive historical-priority claim.
