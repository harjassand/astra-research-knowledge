FINITE THERMAL PULSES IDENTIFY A HIDDEN LINEAR DIFFUSION
An independent constructive companion, 5 November 2026

Status and scientific claim
===========================

This note completes a conditional physical identification operation. In a
certified finite-dimensional, observable, all-even Ornstein--Uhlenbeck model,
one scalar thermometer-like intervention that multiplies ALL native thermal
noise while preserving drift lets one scalar position sensor identify the
entire diffusion and drift up to similarity, and hence the native stationary
entropy-production rate. The intervention is a finite smooth heat pulse,
with bounded amplitude and slew. Randomized hot/control cycles work on one
continuously operating specimen: no hidden-state resets, independent
trajectories, or relaxation between trials are required. The construction
includes finite observation bins, finite-sample bounds, conditioning,
anti-alias and dimension premises, precision costs, and calibration errors.

The physical access is substantive: a certified common temperature actuator
must reach every noise contribution of the declared model and leave its
mobility and deterministic forces unchanged to the paid tolerance. The result
does not identify arbitrary omitted baths or modes, controller dissipation,
underdamped entropy production, or extract useful work. It is not a historical
priority claim. Classical covariance identification and stochastic realization
already provide much of the algebra once informative interventions exist.

The actual new operation relative to the warm same-port impossibility is
precise. That impossibility supplies different native entropy productions
with exactly the same stationary sensor process and all same-port conjugate
force-control transcripts. A finite, physically different noise intervention
separates every diffusion in an observable declared OU model. A raised-cosine
pulse has an invertible covariance transfer for every Hurwitz drift, and
random assignment isolates it without preparing a hidden initial state.

1. Physical model and the quantity being acquired
================================================

The native model is

  dX = A X dt + sqrt(2D) dW,            Y = C X,

where X has n real coordinates, all even under time reversal, A is Hurwitz,
D is strictly positive definite, and C is a scalar row. The native stationary
covariance Sigma is the unique positive solution of

  A Sigma + Sigma A^T = -2D.                                  (1)

This can describe an overdamped linear Brownian network with constant mobility
M, deterministic linear force L X, A=M L, and D=k_B T_0 M. The force need not
be conservative. Uniformly varying the bath temperature while holding M and L
fixed changes D and leaves A fixed. This is a model of a real thermodynamic
resource, not an assertion that a macroscopic thermostat automatically has
uniform reach, unlimited speed, or temperature-independent mobility.

The available added port supplies a known scalar u(t)>=0:

  dX = A X dt + sqrt(2[1+u(t)]D) dW.                           (2)

Its implementation contract includes all of the following:

* all native diffusion in the n-dimensional model scales by 1+u(t);
* drift, sensor coupling, and mechanical mobility stay fixed to stated errors;
* bath memory and spatial equilibration errors are below the model error budget;
* a known finite n bounds all modeled degrees of freedom contributing to the
  target entropy production; no arbitrary weak extra mode is excluded by data;
* the native pair (C,A) is observable, with its numerical conditioning paid;
* when A is unknown, a native frequency bound excludes sampling aliases;
* an initial fourth-moment bound, or a physically prepared stationary native
  distribution, bounds statistical exposure costs.

Native stationary entropy production, in units k_B per unit time, is

  Q = A Sigma + D = (A Sigma - Sigma A^T)/2,
  sigma = Tr[ Q^T D^{-1} Q Sigma^{-1} ] >= 0.                 (3)

This follows by integrating j^T D^{-1}j/p for the stationary Gaussian density
and current j=(A+D Sigma^{-1})X p. Equivalently,

  sigma = Tr[A^T D^{-1} A Sigma] + Tr A.

The positive expression (3) avoids cancellation near equilibrium. In the
single-bath overdamped physical model it gives the modeled system's native
steady dissipated power divided by k_B T_0. Thermostat losses and the internal
cost of the force-generating controller are outside that target unless their
degrees of freedom are explicitly added to the model.

For any nonsingular T, the replacement

  A'=T A T^{-1}, D'=T D T^T, Sigma'=T Sigma T^T

leaves (3) unchanged. Therefore reconstruction up to similarity is sufficient;
one need not attach physical names or sensors to every hidden coordinate.

2. A finite pulse with no blind covariance direction
====================================================

Choose pulse duration h>0 and temperature amplitude eta>0. The hot pulse is

  u(s) = eta sin^2(pi s/h),       0 <= s <= h,                 (4)

and u=0 outside this interval. The control cycle leaves u=0 throughout the
same interval. The pulse returns to native temperature and has zero slope at
both ends. Its peak is eta and maximum absolute slew is eta*pi/h. Hardware
limits eta_max and v_max therefore allow

  eta <= min(eta_max, v_max*h/pi).

Let L_A act on matrices by L_A(Z)=A Z+Z A^T and put omega=2*pi/h. For any
common initial distribution, the difference between hot and control state
covariances at pulse end is

  S = T_A(D)
    = 2 eta int_0^h sin^2(pi s/h) exp(A s) D exp(A^T s) ds.   (5)

Time reversal s -> h-s within the symmetric pulse gives this expression from
the usual covariance evolution. It does not require the initial covariance
to be stationary, known, or experimentally repeatable. The initial mean is
the same under the two assignments and is propagated by the same drift.

THEOREM 1: The linear map T_A on real symmetric matrices is invertible for
every real Hurwitz A and every h,eta>0. In fact

  T_A = eta omega^2 [exp(h L_A)-I]
                     [L_A (L_A^2+omega^2 I)]^{-1}.           (6)

Proof. For Re z<0, direct integration gives

  int_0^h exp(zs) sin^2(pi s/h) ds
    = (omega^2/2) (exp(zh)-1) / [z(z^2+omega^2)].

Every eigenvalue of L_A has negative real part. None of z, z^2+omega^2, or
exp(hz)-1 vanishes there. Analytic functional calculus gives (6), including
defective/nonnormal A, and the spectral mapping theorem gives invertibility.
Both T_A and its inverse preserve the real symmetric subspace. Also S>0
whenever D>0, directly from (5). This completes the proof.

The chosen pulse shape matters. A general finite nonnegative waveform need
not have a Laplace transform without zeros in the left half-plane; a proof
for arbitrary pulse shapes is not asserted. The raised-cosine profile gives
one explicit zero-free transfer, at finite amplitude, duration and slew.

The inversion does not need a dense n^2-by-n^2 solve. Set F_h=exp(Ah), and
solve the Stein equation

  U - F_h U F_h^T = S.

Then

  D = -[ L_A^3(U) + omega^2 L_A(U) ]/(eta omega^2).            (7)

Stable F_h makes the Stein solve unique. Standard Schur-based Stein solving,
matrix exponentiation, and the three applications of L_A use O(n^3)
arithmetic and O(n^2) memory at fixed numerical precision. Certifying a
condition number by explicit dense superoperators can cost O(n^6) arithmetic
and O(n^4) memory; that optional general-purpose certificate is not hidden.

At short h, T_A(D)=eta h D+O(eta h^2 ||A|| ||D||). A bounded slew implies
eta=O(h), so the weak-pulse signal is O(h^2), not free instantaneous access.
At very large h, (6) gives T_A approximately -eta omega^2 L_A^{-3}, so its
size can fall as h^{-2}; a very slow pulse cools its early covariance signal
before the measurement begins. Conditioning must be optimized against the
actual hardware, rather than declaring that arbitrarily short/long pulses
solve the acquisition problem.

3. Converting one scalar output into full covariance information
================================================================

After pulse end, take a finite stack of observations Z=(Y(t_0),...,Y(t_{L-1}))^T
and define

  O = [ C exp(A t_0); ...; C exp(A t_{L-1}) ].

All future native process noise contributes the same cross-time covariance in
hot and control cycles. Thus the hot/control raw second-moment difference is

  V = O S O^T.                                               (8)

Raw moments suffice; there is no need to estimate a tiny difference between
two separately estimated means. Additive sensor noise independent of the
source innovations and state, and with the same conditional law in both
assignments, cancels in expectation, although it still increases statistical
variance. Equal sensor-noise marginals alone are insufficient if their
cross-correlations with the heated source change.

If A,C are known and O has full column rank,

  S=O^dagger V (O^dagger)^T,       D=T_A^{-1}(S).              (9)

The error amplification is explicit:

  ||Dhat-D||_F <= ||Vhat-V||_F /
                  [ s_min(T_A) s_min(O)^2 ].                 (10)

Observability is necessary for unrestricted recovery of D. If v belongs to
the unobservable invariant subspace, the added diffusion vv^T is never
transmitted to the output under the unchanging drift, even while heated.
No inference can distinguish that direction. With an unobserved driven block
one can also vary native entropy production while preserving all accessible
laws. The theorem does not turn an unobservable object into an observable one.

Finite detector bins
--------------------

Infinitely short samples or derivatives are unnecessary. For a detector
reporting a bin average of width b<=Delta,

  Y_j = b^{-1} int_{j Delta}^{j Delta+b} C X(t) dt,

the initial-state contribution is C_b exp(A j Delta) X(0), with

  C_b = C f_b(A),       f_b(A)=(exp(Ab)-I)/(bA).

For Hurwitz A, f_b(A) is invertible and commutes with A. Consequently
observability of (C_b,A) is equivalent to that of (C,A). Future process noise
inside overlapping time histories, including detector integration intervals,
still cancels in (8). The same proof therefore applies with C_b. The
measurement cycle lasts h+(L-1)Delta+b plus actual controller latency.

A known finite detector impulse response can be included in the same way.
Unknown sensor dynamics cannot be silently counted as thermodynamic state
coordinates. A transfer-function zero on a native mode can destroy reach.

4. Unknown drift and sensor gain: acquisition up to similarity
==============================================================

Assume the certified order is n and the scalar pair (C,A) is observable. Use
n+1 equally spaced post-pulse readings, at j Delta for j=0,...,n, or the finite
bins above. Put F=exp(A Delta) and

  O_n=[C_b; C_b F; ...; C_b F^{n-1}].

A native anti-alias condition

  |Im lambda(A)| < pi/Delta                                  (11)

together with continuous-time observability ensures discrete observability
and invertibility of O_n. Distinct A eigenvalues cannot coalesce under the
exponential within that strip; the derivative of the exponential is nonzero,
so Jordan information is retained.

Let H0 be the upper-left n-by-n block of V, and H1 its rows 1,...,n and columns
0,...,n-1. Equation (8) gives

  H0=O_n S O_n^T > 0,
  H1=O_n F S O_n^T.

THEOREM 2: The following finite reconstruction identifies the native drift and
diffusion up to similarity, and exactly identifies (3):

  F'=H1 H0^{-1},
  A'=Delta^{-1} Log(F'),
  S'=H0,
  D'=T_{A'}^{-1}(S'),
  A' Sigma'+Sigma' A'^T=-2D',
  sigma=Tr[(A'Sigma'+D')^T D'^{-1}(A'Sigma'+D') Sigma'^{-1}]. (12)

Here Log is the principal matrix logarithm. Indeed F'=O_n F O_n^{-1}; (11)
makes A'=O_n A O_n^{-1}. Covariance transfer commutes with similarity, so
D'=O_n D O_n^T and Sigma'=O_n Sigma O_n^T. Similarity invariance proves the
last equality. No values of the hidden physical A,D,C are supplied to (12).
An unknown constant scalar sensor gain is absorbed into the similarity.

This is a shifted-covariance realization calculation, not a new substitute
for classical stochastic realization theory. An equivalent numerical version
whitens H0=LL^T and uses L^{-1}H1 L^{-T}; then the pulse covariance becomes I.
Whitening can improve representation conditioning but does not remove the
acquisition cost of a small eigenvalue of H0.

The frequency bound is a genuine physical premise. Fixed-rate observations
cannot choose a matrix-logarithm branch without it. Exact noiseless rank can
reveal a minimal order, but finite noisy data cannot exclude an extra mode
with arbitrarily small sensor coupling. A known n (or a bound with a declared
singular-value gap) remains necessary for a finite certificate of completeness.

5. One continuously running experiment, with no hidden resets
=============================================================

Cycle k begins immediately after the previous recording interval ends. Draw
R_k independently and uniformly from {-1,+1}, independently of all past
state, noise and sensor data. If R_k=+1 apply (4); if R_k=-1 leave the bath at
native temperature for the same duration. Record Z_k and proceed to the next
cycle. No relaxation to a native stationary state is requested between cycles.

Let F_{k-1} include the entire physical past and hidden state at cycle start.
It is a proof filtration, not an experimentally accessible record. The two
assignments propagate that starting state identically in mean, and differ
only by the new covariance S. Therefore

  E[2 R_k Z_k Z_k^T | F_{k-1}] = V.                          (13)

Hence

  Vhat_M = M^{-1} sum_{k=1}^M 2 R_k Z_k Z_k^T               (14)

is unbiased. Its centered summands are martingale differences even though
successive Z_k can be correlated through all preceding pulses. Independent
replicas and state re-preparation are not part of the operation.

THEOREM 3 (finite exposure): If E||Z_k||^4<=B4 uniformly, then

  E||Vhat_M-V||_F^2 <= 4 B4/M,
  Pr(||Vhat_M-V||_F>delta) <= 4 B4/(M delta^2).               (15)

Proof. The Frobenius norm squared of ZZ^T is ||Z||^4. Conditional centering
cannot increase the mean square. Distinct martingale differences are
orthogonal in L2. Summing their variances and applying Markov's inequality
proves (15). No mixing-time factor is needed because the randomized contrast
has a fixed conditional mean. Native slow dynamics still affect observability,
moment bounds, pulse transfer, and the length of each recorded cycle.

For a native stationary zero-mean Gaussian initial state, condition on the
entire independent pulse coin sequence. The state remains zero-mean Gaussian
and its covariance is bounded above by (1+eta)Sigma. If each measured scalar
has variance at most v_max (including sensor noise), then a length-L stack
has trace covariance at most B2=L v_max and

  B4 <= 3 B2^2,
  M >= 12 B2^2/(alpha delta^2)                               (16)

suffices for failure probability alpha. The bound is deliberately simple and
conservative; optimal Gaussian likelihood or weighted covariance estimators
can be much more efficient. This note does not claim optimal confidence
dependence. A nonstationary initial state with a certified fourth moment also
works, using its propagated B4 bound. Obtaining that initial bound or paying
an initial relaxation exposure is part of preparation, not a free inference.

For known A,C, target Frobenius error epsilon_D in D is guaranteed by

  M >= 12 B2^2 /
       [alpha epsilon_D^2 s_min(T_A)^2 s_min(O)^4].           (17)

With weak observability or diffusion transfer this can be very expensive.
There is no uniform dimension-only polynomial bound. The result is finite and
polynomial in the explicitly displayed inverse condition values, inverse
accuracy, and inverse confidence parameter.

6. A complete finite confidence procedure for unknown A
=======================================================

Here is an explicit deterministic propagation of a moment ball
||Vhat-V||_F<=delta. Add deterministic model/quantization biases to delta, and
allocate confidence to any probabilistic calibration bounds separately. All
matrix norms without a subscript below are spectral norms; vectorized-matrix
operator norms act on the Frobenius space.

Form H0hat,H1hat from Vhat and symmetrize H0hat. If

  h0hat=lambda_min(H0hat)>delta,

set Fhat=H1hat H0hat^{-1}. The true F' lies in the ball

  ||Fhat-F'|| <= r_F
    = delta(1+||Fhat||)/(h0hat-delta).                        (18)

This follows from (F'-Fhat)H0=(H1-H1hat)+Fhat(H0hat-H0).

For an explicit matrix-logarithm certificate define

  g(t)=||(Fhat+t I)^{-1}||,       t>=0.

If Fhat has no eigenvalue on the nonpositive real axis and

  r_F sup_{t>=0} g(t) < 1,

the whole ball avoids the branch cut. The resolvent representation of the
principal logarithm and the resolvent identity give

  ||Ahat-A'|| <= r_A
   = (r_F/Delta) int_0^infinity g(t)^2/[1-r_F g(t)] dt,        (19)

where Ahat=Log(Fhat)/Delta. This is a finite, computable conditioning cost.
On a finite t interval it can be bounded by validated numerical matrix norms;
for t>||Fhat||, g(t)<=1/(t-||Fhat||) supplies an explicit integrable tail.
Nonnormality and a nearly aliased spectrum can make this cost large. A point
estimate of a matrix logarithm without a branch/conditioning certificate is
not a confidence guarantee.

Require Ahat Hurwitz; otherwise collect more data. Let T_hat be the pulse
operator for Ahat and put M_A=||Ahat||+r_A. Duhamel's formula gives the explicit
operator perturbation bound

  ||T_{A'}-T_hat|| <= b_T
   = 4 eta r_A int_0^h s sin^2(pi s/h) exp(2 M_A s) ds
  <= 2 eta r_A h^2 exp(2 M_A h).                             (20)

The last bound can be loose; direct resolvent/semigroup bounds can replace
it. Let tau_hat=s_min(T_hat), Dhat=T_hat^{-1}(H0hat). If tau_hat>b_T,

  ||Dhat-D'||_F <= r_D
   = [delta+b_T ||Dhat||_F]/(tau_hat-b_T).                    (21)

Solve Ahat Sigmahat+Sigmahat Ahat^T=-2Dhat. Let
ell_hat=s_min(L_Ahat). If ell_hat>2r_A,

  ||Sigmahat-Sigma'||_F <= r_Sigma
   = [2r_D+2r_A||Sigmahat||_F]/(ell_hat-2r_A).                (22)

Require

  d_* = lambda_min(Dhat)-r_D > 0,
  s_* = lambda_min(Sigmahat)-r_Sigma > 0.

Put Qhat=Ahat Sigmahat+Dhat and

  r_Q = r_A||Sigmahat||_F + (||Ahat||+r_A)r_Sigma + r_D,
  q_* = ||Qhat||_F+r_Q.

Then direct telescoping of the four matrix factors in (3), using
||D^{-1}-Dhat^{-1}||<=r_D/d_*^2 and its Sigma analogue, proves

 |sigmahat-sigma| <= r_sigma
   = 2q_* r_Q/(d_* s_*)
     +q_*^2 [r_D/(d_*^2 s_*)+r_Sigma/(d_* s_*^2)].           (23)

Return [max(0,sigmahat-r_sigma), sigmahat+r_sigma]. If any gate fails, the
current data do not certify the target precision. For every fixed model with
the strict premises and positive conditioning margins, sufficiently small
delta passes all gates and gives any fixed positive absolute error target.
Relative precision near sigma=0 or arbitrarily singular D is not free.

The observation budget can be fixed in advance. For an adaptive stopping
certificate, use stages M_j=2^j M_0 and assign alpha_j=6alpha/[pi^2(j+1)^2]
to the moment bound at stage j. A union bound makes all stage certificates
simultaneously valid with probability at least 1-alpha. Stopping at the first
passing stage is then valid. Reusing a fixed-time 95% radius after arbitrary
repeated attempts would not supply that guarantee. Calibration and clipping
confidence must likewise be allocated across stages when necessary.

This supplies a finite algorithm, rather than assuming all inverse maps are
well conditioned. It also exposes an unattractive practical fact: simple
worst-case bounds can demand far more samples than an accurate point estimate.

7. Physical, numerical and initialization costs
===============================================

Acquisition and storage
-----------------------

For L=n+1 observations, M cycles require M independent random assignment bits,
ML scalar readings and physical time

  T_obs = M [h+n Delta+b+latency].                            (24)

Here b=0 denotes the ideal point-sample calculation; physical finite-bin
execution uses b>0. There is no intercycle state-preparation time. Initial
native stationarity, if used for the Gaussian moment bound, is a separate
preparation. A known fourth-moment bound removes that preparation requirement.
The temperature actuator must actually return to baseline by pulse end and
the bins must finish before the next cycle begins.

Accumulating (14) uses O(L^2) classical memory and O(M L^2) arithmetic; each
cycle need retain only its L scalar readings. Off-line reconstruction uses
O(n^3) ordinary dense linear algebra through (7) and (12). Precision and
condition certification can increase bit work; no exact real arithmetic is
claimed as a physical primitive.

Heating and reach
------------------

The bath volume, thermal diffusion time, relaxation time, controller routing,
and heat capacity belong to the actuator bill. For a bath with approximately
constant heat capacity C_bath, one hot pulse changes its stored thermal energy
by C_bath T_0 eta at the peak and rejects that amount on the return, before
heat leaks, source work and controller losses. The expected number of hot
pulses is M/2. These are separate thermodynamic costs of diagnosis; the target
sigma is the native device's rate at u=0. The theorem gives no universal
numerical energy cost without a physical bath/controller specification.

A single knob can have global physical reach. Describing it as scalar does
not make that reach local, cheap, self-calibrating or independent of n. A
hidden active-noise source that does not scale with bath temperature violates
(2). In that case the pulse identifies the heated contribution, and inserting
it as if it were the entire D into (3) is unjustified. The old unknown-support
and hidden-catastrophe limitations are not bypassed.

Calibration and model error
----------------------------

If the realized hot drift is A+E_A(t) and its diffusion is
(1+u(t))D+E_D(t), the error in its raw second moment P satisfies, relative to
the ideal process, a variation-of-constants bound

 ||P_actual(h)-P_ideal(h)||_F
 <= int_0^h ||exp(A(h-s))||^2
      [2||E_A(s)|| ||P_actual(s)||_F+2||E_D(s)||_F] ds.        (25)

The shared initial raw second moment is held fixed in this comparison. The
resulting post-pulse moment error is at most ||O||^2 times (25). Waveform error
du contributes E_D=du D. Sensor temperature dependence and control-associated
pickup add their own heat-tagged covariance errors and must be calibrated.

Equation (25) is conditional on the actual initial state if applied within a
cycle. If only an expectation bound on ||P_actual|| is supplied, it bounds
the expected accumulated bias, not its realized value deterministically. A
conservative confidence implementation allocates alpha_sys and uses Markov's
inequality to multiply that expected bias bound by 1/alpha_sys. Alternatively
a certified pathwise or conditional bias budget can be added directly.
Statistical martingale fluctuations around the actual conditional mean are
still bounded by the appropriate actual fourth moment. There is no silent
replacement of a mean calibration error by a pathwise guarantee.

If observation rows have deterministic error E_O, then

 ||(O+E_O)S(O+E_O)^T - O S O^T||_F
 <= (2||O|| ||E_O||+||E_O||^2)||S||_F.                       (26)

Timestamp, finite waveform, sensor-kernel and calibration precision can be
charged this way, with their actual physical bounds. A calibration error
larger than the final statistical target creates an error floor; collecting
more cycles does not remove it.

Digitization and numerical precision
------------------------------------

For zero-mean Gaussian mixtures with per-reading variance <=v_max, clipping
the digitizer to +/-B gives, by a union bound over ML readings,

  Pr(any clipping) <= 2ML exp[-B^2/(2v_max)].

Choose B=sqrt[2v_max log(2ML/alpha_clip)]. If the per-reading quantization
error is at most q on this range, the induced error in (14) is at most

  4 L B q + 2 L q^2                                         (27)

in Frobenius norm on the no-clipping event. This follows from the difference
of two rank-one outer products and the factor 2 in (14). It fixes a finite
number of ADC bits, O(log(B/q)), for a chosen moment tolerance. Mean offsets
require a corresponding range allowance; assignment-independent offsets
still cancel in the ideal contrast.

Matrix inversions, logarithms, exponentials and Stein/Lyapunov solves must
likewise be computed to certified residual tolerances below their allocated
error radii. Formulae (18)--(23) show where those errors enter. The included
Python execution uses double precision and is not such an interval-certified
high-confidence implementation.

8. Executed examples and their actual scope
===========================================

The reproducible program heat_pulse_demo.py writes heat_pulse_execution.json.
It performs two distinct executions.

A. The warm three-coordinate force-indistinguishable family is

  A=-[[2,-1,0],[-1,2,-1],[0,-1,2]], C=[1,0,0],
  D_t=[[1,0,0],[0,1,t],[0,t,1-4t]],
  Sigma_t=(-A)^{-1}-2t e3 e3^T,
  sigma(t)=4t^2/(1-4t-t^2), 0<=t<sqrt(5)-2.

Its stationary output and all same-port conjugate-force transcripts coincide,
as established in the warm companion. For h=eta=1 and Delta=0.5, the exact
heat-tagged covariance and (12) recover, in floating point,

  t=0:   sigma approximately 3.8e-26 (true 0),
  t=0.1: sigma 0.0677966101695 (true 0.0677966101695),
  t=0.2: sigma 1.0000000000006 (true 1).

The formula (5)--(6) is compared with independent numerical quadrature.
No finite-sample accuracy for this family is claimed. Its observability
matrix has condition number about 45.65, and H0's smallest eigenvalue at
t=0.2 is about 1.13e-5. A practical arbitrary-parameter reconstruction is
therefore expensive. This is an actual cost, not a nearly free resolution of
the hidden-dissipation ambiguity.

B. A more observable two-coordinate example has

  A=[[-1,-3],[3,-1]], D=diag(1,2), C=[1,0],
  h=0.5, eta=1, Delta=pi/6.

The program executes one million consecutive randomized cycles on ONE OU
trajectory, with no between-cycle resets. Exact Gaussian transition
covariances are used for both the smooth pulse and native intervals; Euler
time-discretization error is absent. It records three million scalar readings
over 1,547,197.5512 units of simulated physical time. The maximum temperature
slew is 2*pi per time unit. Hidden A,D are used by the simulator, not supplied
to the reconstruction (12).

The true native entropy rate is 20.25. Population reconstruction recovers it
to floating-point precision. The one-path empirical reconstruction returns
21.07724758; its tagged-moment Frobenius error is 0.01014736. The conservative
95% moment radius from (16) is 0.13477982. That loose radius is NOT claimed to
certify the displayed empirical entropy error. For comparison, (17) budgets
208,600,580 cycles to certify D error 0.1 in this example when A,C are known,
using the stated conservative inequality. This budget was calculated, not
executed. Full unknown-A confidence additionally pays (18)--(23).

The numerical work establishes algebraic identities and exercises a real
correlated one-path sampling schedule in simulation. It is not experimental
hardware, a calibrated thermal controller, or evidence that a million-cycle
experiment would be economical on a specified physical platform.

9. Strongest applicable alternatives and exact incremental consequence
=======================================================================

The useful comparison has three levels.

First, at the old physical interface, no estimator can work on the warm
family: the accessible probability law is identical for different sigma.
More computation, better likelihood methods, longer passive observation, or
arbitrarily sophisticated same-port mechanical feedback do not change that
fact. The thermal port changes the statistical experiment itself and makes
its covariance map injective. This is the precise new capability.

Second, once this same additional physical port is supplied, established
methods are strong competitors. General correlation/noise-covariance
identification, LTV measurement-difference methods, and stochastic
realization can use such informative covariance changes. This note proves no
computational or sample-complexity dominance over those equally informed
methods. They may provide much better practical estimators than (14). The
specific contribution here is an explicit finite pulse with zero-free
Lyapunov transfer, an observability-level sufficiency proof, a randomized
contrast that requires no hidden re-preparation, and a thermodynamic/output
interface with its physical and numerical costs shown.

Third, resolving this restricted model does not establish unrestricted total
dissipation inference. Incomplete bath reach, unobservable modes, hidden
order, unknown time-reversal parities, non-Markov noise, and uncalibrated
temperature-dependent drift can all invalidate the desired consequence.
Those remain supplied physical premises or separately paid errors.

Relevant primary machinery, read during construction:

* Kost, Dunik, Puncochar and Straka, "Unobservable Systems: No Problem for
  Noise Identification", IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control (2026),
  arXiv:2505.23983v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23983v2 . Their model supplies
  known bounded state, input, noise-shaping and measurement matrices and
  estimates unknown noise covariance parameters by correlation measurement
  differences. Structured noise parameters can be identifiable even when the
  state is not. Their result does not assert that arbitrary diffusion on a

  represented as known time-dependent noise shaping.

* Kim, Bienkowski, Zhang and Pattipati, "A Single-Pass Noise Covariance
  Estimation Algorithm in Nonswitching Multiple-Model Adaptive Kalman Filters
