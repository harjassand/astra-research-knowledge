AN EXPLICIT LOGARITHMIC-HEADER, CONSTANT-GAP STREAM
Finite-field Pauli implementation of late_selector_theorem.txt
5 November 2026

1. Result and convention
========================

Use half diamond distance d_diamond(A,B)=||A-B||_diamond/2. A quantum memory
has dimension at most m IN EACH classical branch; arbitrary classical records
are allowed. Every coherent register, protected storage block, and delay line
crossing the specified cut counts toward that quantum dimension.

For every n>=1, this construction encodes one arbitrary qubit, possibly
entangled with an external reference, into n maximally mixed gauge qubits
followed by one flag qubit. The original source qubit is explicitly discarded
after a final SWAP into the emitted flag. There is no coherent return link,
accessible bath purifier, or gauge preparation label.

The selector is an encoding-program header of 2k+1=O(log n) classical bits.
If it is available before the gauge stream, the receiver recovers the entire
input/reference channel exactly with constant coherent buffering, O(n) local
quantum operations, O(n log^2 n) elementary classical work, and O(log n)
classical working state. If it arrives only after all n+1 outputs, every
receiver with average half-diamond recovery error epsilon<=1/2 obeys

  m / 2^{n+1} >= 1 - 10 epsilon(1-epsilon).                   (1)

For epsilon=0.01 the right-hand side is 0.901. Therefore a memory consisting
of whole qubits must have capacity at least n+1 at that cut: a smaller
maximum capacity across branches is impossible. Individual branches may
retain fewer; the expected-occupancy bound is separate below.
If the cut is after the n gauge outputs but before
the flag and header, the corresponding bound is

  m_gauge / 2^n >= 1 - 10 epsilon(1-epsilon),                 (2)

and at one-percent error capacity for at least n gauge-memory qubits is necessary.

More generally, after ANY prefix of ell emitted qubits and before the header,
the retained quantum dimension must satisfy

  m_ell / 2^ell >= 1 - 10 epsilon(1-epsilon).                 (2a)

To prove this, grant the receiver the whole untouched suffix of dimension
2^{n+1-ell}. Its actual prefix compression tensored with the identity on that
suffix is a common compression of the complete B system. Its dimension is
m_ell 2^{n+1-ell}; applying (1) proves (2a). Granting the whole suffix and
arbitrary subsequent processing can only help the receiver. The reduction
uses the one-way architecture: future source gates never return to an already
emitted carrier. At one-percent error, the maximum whole-qubit memory capacity
over classical branches must therefore be at least ell after that prefix.
This does not assert that every individual branch retains ell qubits.

There is also a stronger accounting result for variable branch capacities.
Set r_epsilon=1-10 epsilon(1-epsilon). Under a maximally mixed payload (and
therefore under the input marginal of a Bell-pair experiment), let q_y be the
number of retained quantum-memory qubits in classical branch y after a prefix
of ell carriers, and let p_y be that branch's actual probability. Then

  E_y[2^{min(q_y,ell)-ell}] >= r_epsilon,

  E_y q_y >= max{0, ell (r_epsilon-2^{-ell})/(1-2^{-ell})}.   (2b)

To verify the branch accounting, write C_y for a branch of the compression,
sigma_y=C_y(I/N), and p_y=Tr sigma_y. The common Petz witness restricted to
branch y is trace preserving on supp sigma_y. Its composite Kraus operators
K_{yab} therefore satisfy sum_{ab} Tr K_{yab}^dagger K_{yab}=N p_y, while their
rank is at most min(m_y,N). Consequently the witness entanglement fidelity is
at most sum_y p_y min(m_y,N)/N. Applying this to prefix compression tensored
with the granted suffix gives the first line of (2b). The convex-chord bound

  2^{min(q,ell)-ell}
    <= 2^{-ell}+(1-2^{-ell})q/ell,       q>=0,

gives the second. Continuous classical outcomes use the same inequality
integrated over outcomes; it does not depend on a finite alphabet. The
reference-entangled input has encoded output marginal I/N for EVERY selector,
so these probabilities are those of the actual experiment, not a separate
artificial ensemble. This expected-memory bound avoids summing unrelated
worst-case branches when accounting for retained qubit-time below.

The statements concern this explicit programmed stream. They do not prove a
memory obstruction for the homogeneous weak partial-SWAP reader, nor supply
its unresolved small-memory decoder. The emitter uses finite strong controlled
Pauli operations and one final full SWAP; these are paid resources.

2. A complete finite small-bias construction
===========================================

Put r=2n. Choose

  k=ceil(log_2(max(2,2(r-1)))),     q=2^k.

Thus q>=2(r-1), q=O(n), and k=O(log n). Fix a monic irreducible polynomial
f of degree k over F_2, giving F_q=F_2[t]/(f). The field construction and its
cost are specified in Section 7; it is not a supplied uncomputed oracle.

Select a,b independently uniformly from F_q. Define the r-bit string

  x_i(a,b)=Tr_{F_q/F_2}(a b^i),       i=0,...,r-1,            (3)

where b^0=1 including b=0. Strings are sampled with multiplicity from the
q^2 seed pairs. Distinct seeds may give the same string; that causes no
problem and must not be silently replaced by a uniform distribution over
distinct strings.

LEMMA 1. For every nonzero w in F_2^r,

  0 <= E_{a,b} (-1)^{sum_i w_i x_i(a,b)}
    = Pr_b[ sum_i w_i b^i=0 ]
    <= (r-1)/q <= 1/2.                                     (4)

Proof. Let p_w(z)=sum_i w_i z^i, a nonzero polynomial over F_q of degree at
most r-1. For a fixed b, the parity in (4) is Tr(a p_w(b)). If p_w(b)=0 it
is identically zero. If p_w(b)!=0, multiplication by p_w(b) permutes F_q.
The field trace is a nonzero F_2-linear functional, so exactly half the a's
give trace zero and half give trace one. Averaging over a gives the indicator
of p_w(b)=0. A nonzero polynomial of degree at most r-1 has at most r-1
roots, proving (4).

For completeness, the trace is nonzero without any unproved normal-basis
assumption. Its polynomial z+z^2+...+z^{2^{k-1}} is nonzero and has degree
less than q, so it cannot vanish at all q field elements. Its values satisfy
Tr(z)^2=Tr(z), hence lie in F_2. Its nonzero linearity gives the balanced
character property used above.

This is standard small-bias machinery, specialized to a very simple parameter
choice. The field-character/root argument is not claimed as a new result.

3. From classical characters to a Pauli contraction
===================================================

Interleave the label bits so that on gauge qubit ell=0,...,n-1,

  u_ell=x_{2ell},       v_ell=x_{2ell+1},
  P_ell=i^{u_ell v_ell} X^{u_ell} Z^{v_ell} in {I,X,Y,Z},
  P_{a,b}=tensor_ell P_ell.                                 (5)

The phase convention makes every P_{a,b} Hermitian and unitary. Each program
is a depth-one product of single-qubit Paulis. Define the gauge channel

  Phi(A)=q^{-2} sum_{a,b} P_{a,b} A P_{a,b}.

For a Hermitian Pauli basis element P_y, commutation gives

  Phi(P_y)=lambda_y P_y,
  lambda_y=E_{a,b}(-1)^{[y,x(a,b)]},                          (6)

where [.,.] is the nondegenerate binary symplectic form. A nonzero y defines
a nonzero linear character of x. Consequently

  lambda_0=1,           0<=lambda_y<=1/2 for y!=0.            (7)

These eigenvalue statements hold on the complete complex operator space,
since the Pauli basis is orthogonal for the Hilbert--Schmidt inner product.
In particular ||Phi(A)||_2<=||A||_2/2 for every traceless complex A.

Only this Hilbert--Schmidt contraction is asserted. Constant bias does not
make Phi an approximate private quantum channel in trace or diamond norm
with an n-independent error. No encryption claim is used in the argument.

4. A Z anchor closes the flag commutant, with exact kappa=4/5
=============================================================

Let D=2^n and N=2D. On flag F and gauge G, use the following selector family:

  H_*=Z_F tensor I_D,                 p_*=1/5;
  H_{a,b}=X_F tensor P_{a,b},         p_{a,b}=4/(5q^2).        (8)

Every H is a balanced Hermitian involution: H^2=I, Tr H=0, with equal
+/- eigenspace dimensions D. The extra Z anchor is essential. Without it,
X_F tensor I_D would commute with every X_F tensor P_{a,b}, and no positive
commutator gap could hold.

LEMMA 2. For every complex N-by-N matrix K,

  sum_j p_j ||[K,H_j]||_2^2
    >= (4/5) ||K-(Tr K/N)I||_2^2.                           (9)

The constant 4/5 is exact for this family.

Proof. Consider the self-adjoint averaging map

  Psi(K)=(1/5)H_* K H_*+(4/5) E_{a,b} H_{a,b}K H_{a,b}.

The tensor Pauli operators sigma_F tensor P_y diagonalize it. For a gauge
eigenvalue lambda_y from (6), its four eigenvalues, in the order
sigma_F=I,X,Y,Z, are

  1/5+(4/5)lambda_y,
 -1/5+(4/5)lambda_y,
 -1/5-(4/5)lambda_y,
  1/5-(4/5)lambda_y.                                       (10)

At y=0, these are 1,3/5,-1,-3/5. At y!=0, (7) shows that the largest is
at most 3/5. Thus Psi has largest eigenvalue at most 3/5 on the traceless
subspace. For involutions,

  sum_j p_j ||[K,H_j]||_2^2
    =2||K||_2^2-2 Re Tr[K^dagger Psi(K)].

Removing the scalar part proves (9). Equality is attained by K=X_F tensor I,
whose Psi eigenvalue is 3/5. This proves exactness.

Notice the eigenvalue -1 in (10). The lifted map Psi is not an absolute-
contraction quantum expander on its entire traceless space. What is proved,
and what late_selector_theorem.txt requires, is the one-sided commutator
(Poincare) gap. The gauge map Phi does have the absolute contraction in (7).

5. A once-through emitter with an actual discarded source
=========================================================

The source receives an arbitrary qubit S, potentially entangled with a
reference R that remains untouched. It has access to independent fresh
tracial gauge carriers A_1,...,A_n, each I/2, and a final fresh tracial flag
carrier E. The encoding program j is chosen independently of S,R.

For the anchor j=*, apply no encoding gates to S or gauge. Emit the gauge
carriers in their fixed order, then SWAP S with E and emit E. Discard S.

For j=(a,b):

1. Apply a Hadamard H to S.
2. On carrier A_ell, apply controlled-on-zero P_ell,
   |0><0|_S tensor P_ell + |1><1|_S tensor I, then release A_ell.
3. After all n gauge carriers, SWAP S into E, emit E, and discard S.

Emission times, carrier labels and routing are fixed independently of j.
Gate slots are padded for identities and for the anchor branch. Otherwise
timing or routing could itself disclose the supposedly late program. Any
additional accessible side channel revealing j changes the interface and is
outside the lower-bound premise.

The emitter implements the fixed channel E_j independently of receiver
actions. Arbitrary receiver-internal adaptive measurements and classical
feedback are allowed. A return/control channel that changes future emitter
operations, discloses the program, or revisits emitted carriers is additional
physical access outside this interface and this lower-bound statement.

The controlled product is exactly controlled_0(P_{a,b}). Hence, writing F for
the emitted flag, the output channel is

  E_j(rho)=V_j(rho tensor I_D/D)V_j^dagger,
  V_*=I,
  V_{a,b}=controlled_0(P_{a,b})(H_F tensor I_D).              (11)

The placement of F last in time is a physical stream order; the tensor
notation lists it first only for matrix readability. Equation (11) gives
H_j=V_j(Z_F tensor I_D)V_j^dagger exactly as required by the main theorem.

After the final SWAP, the discarded original source is I/2 and independent
of F,G,R. The receiver is not given that source, any bath purifier, or the
basis labels in a hypothetical preparation of I_D/D. The header j specifies
the applied encoding program and is an explicitly timed resource, not a
purification record.

The source retains only its one quantum payload qubit between carriers and
uses at most two quantum qubits simultaneously for a gate. Its classical
program state is O(log n), as detailed below. This is constant QUANTUM source
memory; it is not a claim that the full classical controller has constant
dimension independent of n.

All gates are one- or two-qubit operations. The common source flag makes
encoding serial even though the gauge Pauli P_{a,b} is depth one. There are
at most n controlled-Pauli gates, one Hadamard, and one SWAP. A bounded norm
J Hamiltonian proportional to a Hermitian involution implements the
corresponding gate, up to global phase, in time pi/(2J). Standard compiled
gate sets give another fixed constant per block. These finite strong
encounters are allowed here; a weak-action encounter restriction would be
an additional premise and is not supplied by this construction.

6. Early-header decoder and its arbitrary-reference proof
==========================================================

When j=* is known before the gauges, discard them and output the final flag.
When j=(a,b) is known, generate the local P_ell from the seed. On arrival of
each gauge qubit, measure it in the eigenbasis of P_ell; for P_ell=I no
measurement is needed and its eigenvalue is +1. Multiply the measured signs
into a single classical parity s in {+1,-1}. Discard each measured carrier.
When the final flag arrives, apply Z^{(1-s)/2}, then H, and output it.

Proof for an arbitrary input/reference state rho_FR. Let

  rho_tilde=(H_F tensor I_R)rho_FR(H_F tensor I_R).

For any collection of gauge eigenprojectors with product eigenvalue s, the
probability of the outcome is its gauge rank divided by D, independent of
rho_FR. The normalized conditional flag/reference state is

  (diag(s,1)_F tensor I_R) rho_tilde
       (diag(s,1)_F tensor I_R)^dagger.                      (12)

For s=-1, diag(s,1) differs from Z only by a global phase. The stated
correction therefore returns rho_FR exactly for every outcome. It follows
that the unconditional decoded channel is the identity including every
reference; the half-diamond error is zero in the ideal operation.

The gauge measurements can actually occur as each carrier arrives. A
measurement on an already emitted carrier commutes with all later source
gates acting on S and new carriers. Deferring those measurements in the proof
does not grant the receiver storage or a coherent return channel.

The receiver needs a constant-size calibrated local measurement apparatus,
one parity bit, the O(log n) seed-generator state, and the final flag while
applying its correction. Its measurement apparatus and any meter reset or
reference-preparation resources are supplied hardware and must be counted
in a physical implementation. They are not obtained from unknown tracial
preparation labels.

If the header is late, retaining every output and then doing these same
local measurements is an exact O(n)-operation decoder. It is the appropriate
equally informed processing baseline; an expensive generic unitary inverse
or Petz implementation is unnecessary for this particular family. What late
metadata forces is quantum retention before the measurement bases are known.

7. Finite classical construction, header and online costs
=========================================================

Irreducible polynomial acquisition
----------------------------------

A finite deterministic setup algorithm enumerates monic degree-k polynomials
with constant coefficient 1. There are at most 2^{k-1} candidates. For each
candidate f compute v_i=t^{2^i} modulo f by repeated modular squaring. Check

  gcd(f,v_i-t)=1 for i=1,...,floor(k/2), and v_k=t mod f.

These tests are necessary and sufficient for irreducibility: a reducible
polynomial has an irreducible factor of degree at most k/2, detected by one
of the gcd tests, while an irreducible degree-k polynomial divides
t^{2^k}-t and none of the smaller-degree factor tests. Irreducible polynomials
exist at every positive degree, so enumeration terminates. The k=1 case is
handled by the same modular tests.

With naive binary polynomial arithmetic a conservative bound is O(q k^4)
bit operations and O(k) working bits for this one-time search. Sharper
finite-field algorithms exist but are unnecessary to establish finiteness.
Both parties can deterministically obtain the same first polynomial, or one
party can transmit its k+1 coefficients once and the other verify it. This
shared setup is separate from the per-payload 2k+1-bit header.

Trace mask and streaming program
--------------------------------

Precompute the k-bit mask tau_l=Tr(t^l) for l=0,...,k-1. Direct repeated
squaring costs O(k^4) bit operations. Thereafter Tr(z) is just the parity of
the masked k-bit representation of z, costing O(k) elementary bit work.

Maintain z=a. Emit the next label bit Tr(z), then replace z by bz in F_q.
Two successive bits give (u_ell,v_ell) for the next local Pauli. Interleaving
the X and Z labels in (5) avoids storing an n-bit half-program. A schoolbook
field multiplication costs O(k^2) bit work, so the full program costs
O(n k^2)=O(n log^2 n) bit operations. The field polynomial, trace mask, a,b,z,
branch label and counter occupy O(k+log n)=O(log n) classical working bits.

The seed is exactly 2k uniform bits. The header can be fixed length 2k+1,
using one anchor/Pauli bit and the seed; the seed is ignored for an anchor.
The branch probabilities 1/5 and 4/5 may be treated as a calibrated classical
random source. From fair bits alone, rejection sampling a uniform number in
{0,...,4} costs 4.8 expected bits for the branch. For bounded runtime, cap
the number of three-bit attempts at L; the probability of truncation is
(3/8)^L. Charging that total-variation difference to the channel error makes
the complete finite-time implementation explicit. Classical header errors
must likewise be bounded or corrected with their actual transmission costs.

Online quantum operations and time
----------------------------------

There are n+1 fresh tracial carrier preparations, at most n+2 source gates,
at most n local gauge measurements, and at most two flag correction gates.
The source protects its arbitrary quantum input throughout encoding. The
early receiver has only constant coherent buffering, but pays the local
meter, classical parity and seed state. The late receiver pays at least the
logical memory in (1) or (2), and any code/block overhead needed to protect it.

At a fixed serial classical bit-operation rate, O(n log^2 n) program work
also enters wall time. One may precompute the entire program and stream it
at O(n) quantum gate time, but that uses O(n) additional classical program
storage and does not erase its computation cost. A delay line is a quantum
memory and is not excluded from the late-receiver bill.

For equally spaced carriers separated by tau, define

  L_ell=max{0, ell(r_epsilon-2^{-ell})/(1-2^{-ell})}.

The actual expected retained qubit-time, under a Bell-input marginal, is at
least

  tau sum_{ell=1}^n L_ell + t_hdr L_{n+1},                  (13)

where t_hdr is a post-output interval during which NO selector information
has reached the receiver, such as idle latency or an atomic packet held
unavailable until its endpoint. Apply (2b) at every instant between arrivals
and integrate the expected occupancy. If seed/header bits arrive sequentially,
the original unconditional bound need not persist after the first informative
bit. Subsequent compression depends on the conditional selector ensemble;
one must not charge the entire serial header duration at L_{n+1} without a
separate conditional argument.
This handles branch-dependent compression, releases, and allocations without
assuming that all pointwise worst-case branches lie on one execution path.
For fixed positive r_epsilon, sum L_ell=r_epsilon n(n+1)/2-O(1), so (13) is
Omega(n^2 tau) before protection overhead. At one-percent error r_epsilon is
0.901. A receiver with a uniform reserved memory capacity additionally needs
at least ell whole qubits during the interval after prefix ell, but that
capacity statement should not be confused with every branch's occupancy.
Early delivery sends the same number of classical
header bits before the stream. If the payload already exists during that
wait, its source protection time must include the wait. If it is introduced
after header preparation, no such extra quantum wait is needed.

8. Precision, calibration and error accounting
==============================================

All ideal guarantees above are exact channel statements. For finite hardware,
use half-diamond errors throughout. If G=O(n) implemented source/decoder
locations each deviate by at most nu from their ideal channel, telescoping
gives total error at most G nu. Additional independent tracial-preparation
errors sum under the same bound: if each fresh carrier is within trace
distance zeta of I/2, the product preparation contributes at most (n+1)zeta.
Correlated preparation errors require a global preparation bound; matching
the local marginals alone is insufficient.

Thus a simple sufficient condition for total online error eta is
nu,zeta=O(eta/n), with separate allocations for the classical header, parity
record, meter, source storage, routing and final output. A fixed unprotected
per-site measurement error does not yield a constant-error parity decoder
as n grows. One may instead use a declared protection scheme, paying its
physical qubits, gates, resets, measurement and classical processing costs.

An experimentally acquired gate guarantee is not cost-free. For a constant
set of unknown fixed-size gate/measurement channels used by a common source
port and local receiver, ordinary process tomography with calibrated known
reference preparations gives a conservative finite setup route of
O(n^2 eta^{-2} log(1/alpha_cal)) samples for nu=Theta(eta/n), up to constants
from fixed Hilbert-space dimension. This calibration can be amortized over
many payloads, but it is not included in the O(n) ONLINE quantum-operation
claim. Spatially distinct or drifting components can require a larger bill.
The construction does not establish a fault-tolerant hardware realization.

For a late-receiver lower bound with imperfect preparation, suppose the
physical encoder is within average half-diamond eta_enc of the declared ideal
E_j, and the receiver's actual average recovery error is epsilon_phys. Then
the ideal-encoder composition with that same receiver has error at most
epsilon=epsilon_phys+eta_enc. Insert that epsilon into (1). Calibration,
program-distribution and modeling error therefore consume the bound's error
budget rather than disappear from the premise.

The ideal point epsilon=0.01 refers to that TOTAL comparison error. A physical
claim of one-percent recovery plus an uncharged one-percent preparation error
would use epsilon=0.02, not 0.01.

9. Numerical execution and the n=1000 point
===========================================

small_bias_checks.py executes exact integer finite-field arithmetic. It
enumerates every seed and every binary character for n=1,2,3,4,5,8, verifying
nonnegative nontrivial biases, the root-count bound, and the <=1/2 contraction.
It also verifies the complete early-decoder Choi channel for every one of the
64 three-gauge-qubit tensor Pauli programs, explicitly summing the tracial
mixture and measurement outcomes. The largest identity-Choi Frobenius error
was 5.56e-16 in floating point. These are mathematical execution checks, not
physical-device experiments or independent evidence replacing the proof.

For n=1000:

  r=2000, q=4096, k=12, header length=25 bits,
  f(t)=t^12+t^3+1 (binary polynomial 0x1009),
  (r-1)/q=1999/4096=0.488037109375,
  kappa=4/5,
  epsilon=0.01 => m/N>=0.901.

The field polynomial was obtained after testing five candidates. The source
and early receiver can generate the complete 1000-site program using the
24-bit seed, 12-bit field arithmetic and a small counter; they need not store
the full string. One program with seed a=17,b=31 was generated exactly and
contained 262 identities, 232 X, 253 Z and 253 Y factors. The program has
738 nonidentity controlled-Pauli source gates, plus Hadamard and final SWAP;
the guaranteed worst case remains n+2 gates. This counts abstract local gates,
not a hardware-specific decomposition, protection or calibration budget.

The point requires whole-qubit capacity 1001 after all outputs before the
header, or 1000 at the gauge-only cut before the flag/header, if the receiver
is restricted to whole-qubit memory and the total error is at most one
percent. A 1000-qubit density matrix, 1000-qubit noisy factory and physical
memory were not simulated. The finite-field program and analytic bounds were
executed; their scope is recorded in small_bias_execution.json.

10. Strongest prior machinery and the actual increment
======================================================

Small-bias spaces and their use for Pauli mixing are established tools:

* Alon, Goldreich, Hastad and Peralta, "Simple Constructions of Almost k-wise
  Independent Random Variables", Random Structures & Algorithms 3,289--304
  (1992), DOI 10.1002/rsa.3240030308. Author-hosted primary text:
  https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/PSX/aghp.pdf .

* Ambainis and Smith, "Small Pseudo-Random Families of Matrices: Derandomizing
  Approximate Quantum Encryption" (2004), arXiv:quant-ph/0404075:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0404075 . They connect small-bias classical
  distributions to Pauli matrix families. This note uses that connection for
  a commutator gap and memory theorem, not a new encryption primitive.

* Dickinson and Nayak, "Approximate Randomization of Quantum States With
  Fewer Bits of Key" (2006), arXiv:quant-ph/0611033, author-hosted full text:
  https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anayak/papers/DickinsonN06.pdf . Appendix B
  supplies the finite-field polynomial-root small-bias argument and its
  classical generation costs; Appendix A spells out the Pauli-character
  eigenvalues. Our trace pairing is an equivalent linear-character form.

Postmeasurement-information memory obstructions are also established:

* Ballester, Wehner and Winter, "State Discrimination With Post-Measurement
  Information", IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 54,4183--4198 (2008),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0608014 . Theorem 6.6 already constructs
  three bases for which exact recovery of one Boolean target requires keeping
  all input qubits despite unlimited classical records. Its subsequent
  discussion leaves near-perfect performance for that construction open.

* Bluhm, Rauber and Wolf, "Quantum Compression Relative to a Set of
  Measurements", Annales Henri Poincare 19,1891--1937 (2018),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.04898 . Theorem 5.1 proves small-error stability
  by compactness, and Lemma 5.2 controls arbitrary classical side information
  through the Schmidt number of the composite channel. Their primary
  formulation has a common decompressor and all-input observable preservation.

The incremental result here is the quantitative, dimension-independent
payload-memory bound from the common Petz witness, together with an explicit
local stream that realizes it at percent-level error using O(log n) metadata.
The early decoder reconstructs the full arbitrary input/reference, so the
positive side is a coherent payload operation rather than Boolean prediction.
The lower bound still follows from a Boolean probe, which makes the older
postmeasurement-information literature directly relevant. No broad historical
priority claim follows from this targeted comparison.
