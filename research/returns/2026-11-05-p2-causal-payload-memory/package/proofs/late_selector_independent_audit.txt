INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF THE LATE-SELECTOR PAYLOAD MEMORY THEOREM
5 November 2026

Audited result: late_selector_theorem.txt, with its explicit implementation in
small_bias_instantiation.txt. This audit changes no warm result or thermal
companion. All diamond distances below use ||.||_diamond/2.

VERDICT
=======

The common-Petz argument is sound even though the physical late decoder may
depend on the selector. Unlimited classical side information is permitted.
The coefficient 8 epsilon(1-epsilon)/kappa is correct under the stated
average half-diamond recovery assumption. The explicit small-bias family,
including its Z-flag anchor, has exact kappa=4/5 and therefore gives the
dimension fraction 1-10 epsilon(1-epsilon).

One original implementation proposal needed a substantive repair: a family
consisting only of H_j=X_flag tensor U_j cannot have positive commutator gap,
because X_flag tensor I commutes with the whole family. Adding the Z-flag
anchor is essential, not a cosmetic change. The final small-bias construction
includes it with weight 1/5.

The main claim is a uniform memory-capacity bound. Branch-dependent retained
qubit-time requires the weighted refinement below; summing maxima over
unrelated classical branches would not by itself be valid.

1. COMMON PETZ WITNESS AND THE HALF-DIAMOND CONSTANT
===================================================

Let N=2D, E_j(rho)=V_j(rho tensor I_D/D)V_j^dagger, and
H_j=V_j(Z tensor I_D)V_j^dagger. Let C be the common pre-header compression.
Write sigma=C(I/N), x_j=C(H_j)/N, and t_j=||x_j||_1. The two encoded basis
states are (I+/-H_j)/N, and their compressed versions are sigma+/-x_j.

If the average decoded channel sum_j p_j D_j C E_j has half-diamond distance
at most epsilon from identity, measuring its output in the computational
basis guesses a uniformly chosen input bit with probability at least
1-epsilon. Even a selector-dependent decoder cannot improve the optimum
binary discrimination probability of sigma+/-x_j, namely (1+t_j)/2.
Therefore sum_j p_j t_j >= 1-2epsilon. This step uses no common decoder.

The single analytic map

  R(X)=C^*(sigma^{-1/2} X sigma^{-1/2})/N

is completely positive and trace preserving on supp sigma; extend it
arbitrarily and trace preservingly on the orthogonal output support. Since
sigma+/-x_j>=0, every x_j has support inside supp sigma. For T=R C,

  q_j=Tr[H_j T(H_j)]/N
     =Tr[x_j sigma^{-1/2} x_j sigma^{-1/2}] >= t_j^2.

One direct verification of the last inequality sets A=sign(x_j),
B=sigma^{1/4} A sigma^{1/4}, and Y=sigma^{-1/4}x_j sigma^{-1/4}.
Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz gives t_j^2 <= ||B||_2^2 ||Y||_2^2.
Diagonalizing sigma and using sqrt(s_i s_l)<=(s_i+s_l)/2 and A^2<=I gives
||B||_2^2<=Tr sigma=1. Also ||Y||_2^2=q_j.

For any Kraus representation T(X)=sum_a K_a X K_a^dagger,

  sum_a ||[K_a,H_j]||_2^2 = 2N-2Tr[H_j T(H_j)].

The assumed gap and sum_a Tr K_a^dagger K_a=N then imply

  F_e(T) >= 1-(2/kappa)(1-sum_j p_j q_j)
         >= 1-(8/kappa)epsilon(1-epsilon).

Here epsilon<=1/2 justifies squaring the nonnegative lower bound 1-2epsilon.
The map R is an analytic witness of preserved information; the construction
does not require the receiver to compile or implement it.

2. ARBITRARY CLASSICAL BRANCHES AND VARIABLE MEMORY
==================================================

For a discrete branch y, let C_y have quantum output dimension m_y,
sigma_y=C_y(I/N), and p_y=Tr sigma_y. The corresponding R_y is trace
preserving on supp sigma_y. Composite Kraus operators K_{yab} factor through
that branch and satisfy

  rank K_{yab} <= min(m_y,N),
  sum_ab Tr K_{yab}^dagger K_{yab} = N p_y.

The trace/nuclear-norm inequality |Tr K|^2<=rank(K) Tr K^dagger K therefore
gives

  F_e(R C) <= sum_y p_y min(m_y,N)/N.

If every branch has m_y<=m, this proves the original m/N lower bound.
For arbitrary continuous outcomes, use the same integral argument or the
normalized branch Choi states. Each branch Choi state has Schmidt number at
most min(m_y,N), whose maximally entangled overlap is at most min(m_y,N)/N.
This property survives convex mixtures and limits. No finite classical
alphabet is assumed. For uniform branch dimension m, the finite-dimensional
Choi state also admits a finite decomposition by Caratheodory, as used in
Bluhm--Rauber--Wolf Lemma 5.2; that is prior machinery, not a new claim.

For a prefix of ell qubits, grant the receiver the untouched suffix of
dimension 2^{n+1-ell}. Then m_y is replaced by m_y 2^{n+1-ell}, while
N=2^{n+1}. Setting m_y=2^{q_y} yields

  E 2^{min(q_y,ell)-ell} >= r,
  E q_y >= max{0, ell(r-2^{-ell})/(1-2^{-ell})},

where r=1-8epsilon(1-epsilon)/kappa. The second inequality follows because
the exponential on [0,ell] lies below its endpoint chord; the same chord
remains an upper bound for q>=ell.

For Bell input the entire encoded output marginal is I/N for every selector,
so the p_y in this argument are the actual receiver-branch probabilities.
Apply this inequality at each instant before any selector information arrives
and integrate. At equally spaced carrier times with spacing tau and a final
delay t_hdr during which NO selector information has yet arrived, expected
retained qubit-time is at least

  tau sum_{ell=1}^n max{0,ell(r-2^{-ell})/(1-2^{-ell})}
  + t_hdr max{0,(n+1)(r-2^{-(n+1)})/(1-2^{-(n+1)})}.

For fixed r>0 this is r tau n(n+1)/2-O(tau)+r(n+1)t_hdr-o(t_hdr), before
memory-protection overhead. It is a bound on expected physical execution
occupancy, not a sum of unrelated maximal branch capacities. The extra delay
term covers idle latency or an atomic packet unavailable until its endpoint.
For a sequentially received header, the original unconditional gap cannot
automatically be used after the first informative bit: conditional selector
ensembles may permit compression. The entire serial header duration is not
covered without a separate conditional argument.

3. PHYSICAL AND STATISTICAL SCOPE
================================

The fixed emitter channel is independent of receiver actions. Receiver-
internal adaptive measurements and classical feedback are covered. Access
that modifies the future emitter, revisits released carriers, reveals its
program through timing or routing, or supplies bath purifiers changes the
interface. The construction pads every emission/gate slot independently of j
and explicitly assumes no other program side channel is provided.

The source has one retained quantum qubit and O(log n) classical controller
state; calling its entire physical state space constant-dimensional would
be inaccurate. Its source-controlled local gates are serial, even though
each gauge Pauli program is depth one. It uses strong finite controlled-Pauli
encounters and a final full SWAP. Thus it is not an obstruction specific to
the unresolved weak partial-SWAP receiver.

At early header the actual once-through local-measurement decoder returns
the arbitrary input/reference, not just the two basis states used in the
lower-bound proof. The late-header baseline retains all outputs and then
uses the same O(n) local decoder. The separation concerns retained memory
and time, not a gratuitously expensive generic late decoder.

At n=1000 the exact construction has q=4096, k=12, a 25-bit per-payload
program header, and kappa=4/5. At total comparison error epsilon=0.01,
m/N>=0.901. A uniform whole-qubit memory therefore cannot save even one
qubit at any prefix, although variable branches may retain less and are
governed by the expectation bound above. One-time field construction,
O(n log^2 n) classical generation, source storage, local reference preparation,
meter resets, routing, precision, calibration and quantum protection remain
part of the execution bill.

4. PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY
====================

Ballester, Wehner and Winter, "State Discrimination With Post-Measurement
Information", IEEE TIT 54,4183--4198 (2008), Theorem 6.6, already establishes
that exact prediction of a balanced Boolean function in suitable three bases
can require keeping the entire input, despite unlimited classical records.
Its following discussion leaves near-perfect performance for that particular
construction open. Do not claim the first one-bit/full-memory obstruction.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0608014 .

Bluhm, Rauber and Wolf, "Quantum Compression Relative to a Set of Measurements",
Annales Henri Poincare 19,1891--1937 (2018), Theorem 5.1, proves robust
incompressibility by compactness for each fixed observable set. Lemma 5.2
handles arbitrary classical side information via Schmidt number. Their
principal common-decompressor/all-input-observable formulation is stronger
than the present ensemble-specific, selector-dependent-decoder premise.
Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.04898 .

Alon, Goldreich, Hastad and Peralta (1992) supplies the finite-field small-bias
machinery, and Ambainis--Smith (2004) and Dickinson--Nayak (2006) connect
small-bias characters to Pauli mixing. These ingredients are established.
In particular Dickinson--Nayak Appendix B gives a polynomial-root character
construction equivalent to the trace-pairing form used here, while Appendix A
gives the Pauli eigenvalues. Primary texts:
https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/PSX/aghp.pdf
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0404075
https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anayak/papers/DickinsonN06.pdf

The supported increment is the explicit dimension-independent quantitative
bound via one common Petz witness for differently decoded payload ensembles,
plus its logarithmic-header, constant-gap local stream implementation and
the matched arbitrary-reference early decoder. This targeted audit is not
an exhaustive historical priority determination.
