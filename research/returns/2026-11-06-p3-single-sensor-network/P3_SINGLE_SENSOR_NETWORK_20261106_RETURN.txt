## 2. Why the spatial information survives in one time series

The mechanism is temporal structure.

A disturbance in coordinate \(j\) produces the scalar impulse profile

\[
h_j(t)=e_1^\top e^{At}e_j\,1_{\{t\geq0\}}.
\]

The observer receives an overlapping superposition of translated, randomly weighted copies of these profiles, together with Gaussian noise.

The proof does **not** assume that the observer has already separated those copies.

The governing prior input is Kabluchko and Stoev’s uniqueness theorem for minimal spectral representations of infinitely divisible processes. It relates two minimal representations of the same process law by a measure-space isomorphism. Three properties then recover the physical structure.

### Causality fixes the time origin

For a contribution beginning at time \(s\), the response is zero before \(s\). After \(s\), it is analytic and cannot vanish throughout an interval unless it vanishes identically.

Therefore a nonzero contribution’s full temporal profile identifies its onset. The usual ambiguity of shifting a source and compensating by shifting its filter is removed by this causal analytic form.

This is an identifiability argument. The finite procedure below never estimates the onset of an individual event.

### Observability separates the coordinate profiles

Suppose

\[
\sum_j b_jh_j(t)=0
\]

for all positive \(t\). Taking the first \(n\) right derivatives at zero gives

\[
O_Ab=0.
\]

Since \(O_A\) is invertible, \(b=0\). The coordinate profiles are linearly independent; in particular, two different coordinates cannot have proportional profiles.

A source point—its onset, coordinate and nonzero amplitude—therefore maps injectively to its rational-time response sequence. This supplies the minimality needed by the representation theorem.

### The recovered profile directions determine the drift

Equality of output laws forces a matching of the profile directions, with one scale factor per coordinate. In matrix form,

\[
e_1^\top e^{\widetilde A t}
=
e_1^\top e^{At}T^{-1}.
\]

The first \(n+1\) right derivatives imply

\[
O_{\widetilde A}=O_AT^{-1},
\qquad
O_{\widetilde A}\widetilde A=O_AAT^{-1}.
\]

Invertibility yields

\[
\widetilde A=TAT^{-1}.
\]

Again, derivatives appear in the proof of uniqueness. They are not supplied as measurement oracles.

### Reciprocity removes the unknown amplitude scales

If both \(A\) and \(TAT^{-1}\) are symmetric,

\[
(T^\top T)A=A(T^\top T).
\]

Because \(T\) is monomial, \(T^\top T\) is diagonal. Writing its entries as \(b_i>0\), the commutation relation gives

\[
(b_i-b_j)A_{ij}=0.

Every nonzero edge forces its endpoint scales to agree. Observability of a symmetric network implies connectivity, so all scales agree. Their common factor cancels in the similarity transform.

Positive couplings then force the remaining coordinate signs to agree along every edge. A common sign also cancels.

This is the main strengthening: **reciprocal interaction structure supplies the relative amplitude calibration that would otherwise be missing.**
