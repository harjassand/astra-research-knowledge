1. SETUP AND RATE DICTIONARY

There are n fixed-q sites. The known source Hamiltonian is

  H(t) = sum_X h_X(t).

Actual calibrated pure-vacuum output ports are

  L_(i,mu) = (sqrt(r)/q) W_(i,mu),  mu = 1,...,q^2-1,

where the nonidentity Weyl operators are unitary and traceless. The reduced
single-site noise is r(R_(I/q)-id). Define

  c = r(1-q^(-2)),
  R = n c,
  chi = max_i sup_t sum_(X containing i) osc(h_X(t)).

The physical Weyl-field jump rate is c per site and R in total. The synthetic
reset probability of a noise-only interval of duration Delta is instead

  p = 1-exp(-r Delta).

These are different instruments and different rates. The global-history
splitting proof uses physical Weyl-field histories. The queue proof uses the
synthetic reset flags obtained after coherent acquisition and a finite local
environment isometry. Neither proof assumes a physical photon detector.

Take a fixed source-time horizon T and N bins, with Delta=T/N.

2. GLOBAL-HISTORY SPLITTING: PROOF AND FINITE N

The physical total intensity is state independent:

  sum_(i,mu) L_(i,mu)^dagger L_(i,mu) = R I.

The full source-field isometry therefore admits a common orthogonal-history
representation

  V |psi> = integral sqrt(p(h)) U_h |psi> tensor |h> dh.

Here h retains the actual jump times, sites and Weyl marks. Conditional on h,
U_h is a product of source propagators and normalized local Weyl unitaries.
The count m(h) is Poisson(RT), independently of the initial state and any
external reference.

Centering each h_X(t) at its spectral midpoint gives

  ||[H(t),W_(i,mu)]|| <= chi.

Duhamel's formula consequently gives

  ||U(t,s) W_(i,mu) - W_(i,mu) U(t,s)|| <= chi (t-s).

Let U_tilde_h be the split product obtained by moving the Hamiltonian evolution
within each bin to the bin's end, retaining the same jump marks and times in the
environment. Each jump crosses at most Delta of Hamiltonian evolution. A
conditional-history telescoping bound is

  ||U_h-U_tilde_h|| <= chi Delta m(h).

Crucially, square and integrate this bound over the FULL orthogonal history
before applying a channel bound. For every normalized input, including any
reference,

  ||(V-V_tilde)|psi>||^2
    = integral p(h) ||(U_h-U_tilde_h)|psi>||^2 dh
    <= chi^2 Delta^2 E[m^2]
    = chi^2 Delta^2 [RT+(RT)^2].

Thus, using the full diamond norm convention,

  ||V_channel-V_tilde_channel||_diamond
    <= 2 chi Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2).                         (2.1)

There is no requirement R Delta <= 1. There is no triangle inequality over
individual bin errors in (2.1). A sufficient integer choice is

  N = max{1, ceil(2 chi T sqrt(RT+(RT)^2)/eta_split)},
  Delta = T/N.                                             (2.2)

If chi=0, the splitting error is zero and N=1 suffices for this step. For
RT >= 1, (2.2) gives

  N = O(1+chi R T^2/eta_split),
  nN = O(n+n^2 chi r T^2/eta_split).

The common histories in this proof need not be measured, time resolved, or
retained by the actual receiver. Any identical downstream causal receiver,
including an adaptive capped-memory receiver, is a channel on the output and
cannot increase (2.1).

The argument permits time-dependent H(t) and needs no derivative bound on H
for this splitting inequality. Efficient description, calibration, and inverse
simulation of H(t) are separate decoder requirements.

An independent global-norm fallback uses ||H J-J H|| <= 2 Lambda, where
Lambda=sup_t||H(t)||. One must not assert chi <= 2 Lambda for an arbitrary
chosen local decomposition, because decomposition terms may cancel.

3. TWO FIXED CASCADED BANKS PER SITE

Put two local q^2-level collector banks A and B sequentially on the one-way
output paths of each site. Each bank acts on all q^2-1 local Weyl ports.

  A handles even source-time bins.
  B handles odd source-time bins.

For each bank, shift its active window by the known propagation time from the
source to that bank. The inactive bank has main-field coupling C_mu=0, so a
field packet passes that bank unchanged. Each source-time packet encounters
exactly the bank assigned to its bin. The two banks may be simultaneously
active in wall-clock time on different packets; this is harmless when the
windows are assigned in retarded source time.

During each inactive interval of length Delta, a bank performs:

  1. The finite endpoint collector completion and reset/export isometry.
  2. Readout of the synthetic reset flag.
  3. On reset, transfer of the old-state B qudit into its queue.
  4. Reinitialization of the collector and its endpoint workspace into the
     specified local pure/Bell state for the next active interval.

All main-field couplings remain zero throughout these operations. Refresh
must use isolated ancillas, ordinary local readout/feed-forward, or auxiliary
reset ports. Emitting reset radiation into the same main traveling-field ports
would contaminate the other bank's assigned bin and is not this construction.

A bank is required to be ready before its next active interval. No
instantaneous endpoint gate, measurement, queue insertion or preparation is
assumed.

4. TWO QUEUES: SIMPLE FINITE LOCAL ROUTING

Give each bank its own adjacent queue of m_cap q-level cells. This uses
2 m_cap queue cells per site and hence retains O(n m_cap+n) protected-register
space.

Separate queues avoid two otherwise necessary scheduling details:

  * Propagation delays can make the banks' endpoint-processing windows overlap.
  * Wall-clock export order from different banks can differ from source-bin
    order.

Within each bank, exports arrive in source-bin order. A nearest-neighbour stack
is sufficient. If k cells are occupied, shift those k cells one place and SWAP
the new B qudit into the front cell. This uses at most k+1 adjacent SWAPs. Its
cost is O(m_cap) on every accepted branch. The inverse decoder consumes each
bank's queue in reverse order, selecting the appropriate bank from the stored
source-bin timestamp/parity.

A single shared queue is also possible, but needs timestamp-aware insertion
or distinct logical subqueues, and a constant-factor allowance for potentially
simultaneous endpoint processing. The separate-queue implementation avoids
these assumptions.

When a queue overflows, mark the branch as overflow and discard excess exports.
At completion, an overflow branch can output a fixed state. No immediate
global communication, source feedback, or physical termination of the source
is needed to implement the fixed-horizon capped channel.

5. FINITE READOUT, GATE, AND ISOLATED-REFRESH COSTS

Let eta_loc be the complete local error allocation. Let g_q(eta_loc,m_cap)
include the fixed-q endpoint circuit, finite readout, queue movement, ancillary
handling, and Bell reinitialization. At fixed q, a conservative finite
compilation has

  g_q(eta_loc,m_cap) = poly(m_cap, log(1/eta_loc)).

Individual queue/cycling gates can be allocated error
O(eta_loc/(m_cap+1)) so their sum fits the local budget. This is in addition to
the already specified collector-waveform calibration and interpolation budget.

The physical deadline is

  tau_endpoint + tau_queue + tau_readout + tau_refresh <= Delta.     (5.1)

If Omega_g denotes the clock rate of this FULL operation set, the sufficient
scaling is

  Omega_g >= constant * g_q(eta_loc,m_cap)/Delta.                    (5.2)

If Omega_g means only unitary gate speed, it does not by itself guarantee
(5.1); finite readout and refresh latencies/rates must also be specified.
Classical local feed-forward required by queue management is included in the
bank deadline. Separate bank queues permit independent local processing.

One explicit isolated refresh model is amplitude damping of a fixed-dimensional
receiver register to |0>. Use independent vacuum reset ports

  L_j^reset = sqrt(kappa) |0><j|,  j != 0.

For duration tau_refresh, a Stinespring comparison with pure reset gives

  ||E_refresh-R_|0>||_diamond
       <= 2 sqrt(2) exp(-kappa tau_refresh/2).                     (5.3)

For example, the excited component has residual amplitude sqrt(u),
u=exp(-kappa tau_refresh); the isometry difference has squared norm at most
2u. This proves (5.3) uniformly with external references.

It suffices to choose

  kappa >= (2/tau_refresh) log(2 sqrt(2)/eta_refresh).              (5.4)

Taking tau_refresh to be a fixed fraction of Delta gives a polynomial reset
rate and finite coupling amplitude sqrt(kappa). The register dimension and
number of auxiliary reset ports are constant at fixed q. A fixed-dimensional
Bell preparation follows the reset. Any finite detector/readout error is
also included in eta_loc.

This explicit refresh is an additional receiver-side resource. It supplies no
source access. A standard finite measurement-and-feed-forward reset is an
alternative when its latency and accuracy satisfy (5.1). If the available
hardware cannot meet this deadline, the preserved all-banks construction
remains valid and uses O(nN) receiver registers.

The local collector's analog controls are unchanged by recycling. For example,
using the previously derived conservative allocation,

  peak ||C_mu|| <= 16 sqrt(r)/eta_loc,
  waveform segments per use = O_q(1+r^2 Delta^2 eta_loc^(-5)),
  slew = O_q(sqrt(r)/(eta_loc Delta)+r^(3/2)/eta_loc^4).

These costs, as well as the gate/readout/refresh clock, must be paid. Recycling
reduces register space; it does not replace nN local collector calls by the
much smaller number of successful exports.

6. IDEAL OVERFLOW TAIL

For the exact acquired split instrument, a site/bin has reset probability

  p = 1-exp(-r Delta).

This probability is independent of the source state, arbitrary references,
and previous flags. Thus the total site count is

  M_i ~ Binomial(N,p),     Np <= rT.

The exponential moment satisfies

  E[exp(M_i)]
    = (1+p(e-1))^N
    <= exp((e-1)Np)
    <= exp((e-1)rT).

Consequently

  Pr(M_i > m_cap) <= exp((e-1)rT-m_cap).

Choose

  m_cap = ceil((e-1)rT + log(n/zeta)).                            (6.1)

A union bound gives

  Pr(exists i: M_i > m_cap) <= zeta.                              (6.2)

Here e in (6.1) is Euler's number, not the collector's guide-regularization
parameter. The rate is r, not the physical photon rate c.

With two queues of capacity m_cap at each site, the event that either queue
at site i overflows is contained in {M_i > m_cap}. Therefore the same n-site
union bound applies; there is no need to replace n by 2n in (6.1).

The ideal flag distribution is payload independent. This also makes the
ideal tail bound uniform for arbitrarily reference-entangled payloads.

7. ACTUAL FLAGS, UNCONDITIONAL ERROR, AND HERALDED CLAIMS

Set

  B_calls = nN,
  eta_loc = eta_collect/B_calls,
  nu_front = eta_split + eta_collect.

The local allocation eta_loc must include the finite collector, endpoint
instrument, cycling/queue gates, and preparation/readout errors. Complete
channel telescoping remains valid with the stored queues as references and
with adaptive local classical control.

The actual physical flags need not be independent or exactly binomial. Their
full flagged instrument is nu_front-close to the ideal split instrument.
Therefore

  p_overflow_actual <= zeta + nu_front/2.                         (7.1)

The factor 1/2 uses the full diamond-norm convention and the ordinary
trace-distance bound for an event probability. The continuous-to-split error
must be included here; eta_collect alone is insufficient for an assertion
about the actual continuous source.

To compare the actual capped recovery with the ideal uncapped decoder, first
replace the actual front end by the ideal front end while keeping the cap,
then remove the cap. The first error is at most nu_front. The second is
supported on an ideal event of probability at most zeta, and replacing its
output changes a channel by at most 2zeta in full diamond norm. Hence

  ||R_actual,cap-R_ideal,uncapped||_diamond
       <= eta_split+eta_collect+2zeta.                            (7.2)

Combining with the weighted-operator recovery theorem gives, for example,

  ||R_actual,cap-id||_diamond
       <= 2 sqrt(n exp(-(r-beta)T))
          + eta_split + eta_collect + 2zeta + eta_decode.         (7.3)

A channel-level average error bound does not preserve an exact conditional
accuracy assertion on every arbitrarily rare measured branch. For a heralded
accepted protocol, compare the trace-nonincreasing accepted instruments and
normalize using a certified lower bound on the actual acceptance probability.
The fixed-horizon unconditional guarantee (7.3) needs no such normalization.

8. REGISTER SPACE, CLASSICAL RECORDS, AND RUNTIME

The recycled construction needs

  O_q(n [rT+log(n/zeta)] + n)

protected receiver qudits. This includes two queues per site, two banks per
site, and constant ancillary workspace. The reverse decoder's n-qudit dummy
source fits the same asymptotic scaling. These are protected-register costs;
the calibrated traveling-field transport and any explicitly required path
delays remain part of the original physical interface.

On a non-overflow branch, record reset timestamps rather than all no-reset
flags. With two queues this takes O(n m_cap log N) classical bits, up to fixed
factors. Extra resets after overflow can be discarded because that branch is
already marked for replacement output.

The inverse history can combine consecutive no-reset Hamiltonian kicks exactly
into the known propagator on the longer interval. It need not retain a dense
quantum history merely because the acquisition clock used N bins.

If r-beta is bounded away from zero at fixed local scales, the main theorem's
T=O(log(n/epsilon)) gives O(n log(n/epsilon)) protected-register space, with
polynomial receiver control and gate/readout/reset throughput. This is not a
claim that fixed-bandwidth or fixed-error hardware automatically realizes the
asymptotic family. Memory protection over the full acquisition, transport, and
decoding lifetime is a separately paid requirement.

9. STOPPING-TIME LATENCY CORRECTION

The main fixed-horizon theorem is unaffected by stopping-time issues. A
mathematical prefix certificate and a real-time stopping capability are
separate statements.

Suppose the ideal acquired record has a computable certificate v, and let
J be the first finite-bin prefix that meets the certificate. Its source-time
label is tau_grid=J Delta. This identifies which already emitted registers
are sufficient for the certified decoder. It does NOT assert that those
registers and flags have reached the receiver, that the certificate has been
computed, or that decoding has completed at wall-clock time tau_grid.

In particular, the all-banks construction may process its flags only after
collecting the prescribed full horizon. It can then select an earlier
certified prefix and decode using that prefix. Later source-only evolution
cannot change the marginal state of the already emitted prefix. Nevertheless,
this retrospective selection does not shorten the actual collection duration
or establish a real-time stopping latency.

For an online two-bank variant, define an explicit causal lag L_acq that
bounds the time from the end of any source-time prefix until:

  * its relevant field segments have reached their assigned banks;
  * the banks' endpoint, flag-readout and required export-storage operations
    have completed;
  * needed classical flag information has reached the certificate controller;
  * a certified classical comparison update has finished; and
  * any stop/disable messages and required pipeline completion have finished.

Known optical delays alone do not supply this bound. The classical certificate
processor and communication must keep pace at the chosen finite bin rate, or
the resulting processing backlog must be added to L_acq. An n-dimensional
certificate being polynomial-time computable does not make its computation
instantaneous. Certified upper rounding/interval bounds must be used when
required by the certificate proof.

Only with such a paid causal schedule can one claim a completed-recovery bound
of the form

  t_complete <= tau_grid + L_acq + t_decode,

and the analogous expectation bound. A final endpoint/flush interval and
propagation delay cannot be omitted. The per-bank deadline (5.1) gives a
finite local endpoint bound, but does not by itself pay remote classical
communication or the global certificate calculation.

For a fixed cutoff T_cap, assigning all queues using the full T_cap in (6.1)
is a safe way to cover pipeline overshoot and branches on which no early stop
is reached. Alternatively, a stopping-based smaller buffer would require a
separate bound that includes the exports acquired during the causal lag.
This addendum makes no such smaller-buffer claim.

The ideal continuous reset process may have a Poisson-compensator identity
E[number of exports through tau]=nr E[tau] for a suitable stopping time tau.
That identity concerns the ideal retained prefix. It is not a count of all
physical collector operations and does not include late pipeline work.
For the finite-bin ideal instrument, bounded stopping at J instead gives

  E[number of retained exports] = n p E[J] <= nr E[tau_grid],

with p=1-exp(-r Delta), by the stopped Bernoulli martingale. The equality with
nr E[tau] is not exact for the finite-bin synthetic flags.

Any physical stopping claim must also preserve the distinction in Section 7:
a finite unconditional acquisition error controls the aggregate flagged
instrument, not the conditional accuracy of every rare stopping record.

10. AUDIT CONCLUSION

The full-history estimate (2.1) is valid for the actual pure Weyl-field
Stinespring dilation and improves the required number of bins. The two-bank
schedule is valid with fixed one-way cascaded paths and finite paid recycling.
Two separate capped queues provide a simple local implementation with
O(n[rT+log(n/zeta)]) protected-register space. Equations (7.1)-(7.3) are the
appropriate actual-versus-ideal overflow and unconditional error accounting.

The smaller-space variant relies on the stated readout, isolated refresh and
control-throughput resources. The preserved all-banks implementation remains
a valid fallback without that real-time recycling premise. A retrospective
stopping certificate alone never establishes reduced physical collection or
completed-recovery latency.
