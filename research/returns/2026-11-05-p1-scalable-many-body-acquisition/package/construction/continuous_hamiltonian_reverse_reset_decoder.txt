STATUS AND MAIN CLAIM

This note supplies an explicit quantum decoder, not merely a mixing estimate or a classical simulation. Its input is the actually collected quantum output of local reset/export ports. Its output approximates an arbitrary initial n-qudit state, including its entanglement with an arbitrary reference. The receiver never accesses the surviving source. The sufficient condition is that the local reset rate exceeds an explicitly computed interaction-growth rate.

The decoder is the infinite-temperature transpose/Petz channel of a PARTICULAR mixed-bath export channel. Its implementation is unusually simple: retain the exported old-source qudits, initialize a fresh maximally mixed n-qudit register, run the known source Hamiltonian backwards on that register, reverse every reset by a SWAP with its retained export, and discard the consumed auxiliary registers. No inverse of an exponentially large density matrix, amplitude amplification, rare Bell projection, or dense q^n-dimensional unitary synthesis is used.

The central estimate is

  || R_T - id ||_diamond <= 2 sqrt(n exp[-(r-beta) T]),                  (0.1)

where

  beta = sup_t max_i sum_{X containing i} (|X|-1) osc(h_X(t)),
  osc(h) = lambda_max(h)-lambda_min(h).

Thus a positive gap kappa=r-beta gives collection time

  T >= [log n + 2 log(2/epsilon)]/kappa.                              (0.2)

The mean number of retained exported qudits is at most nrT. A finite implementation also pays a polynomial number of local port-collection operations, finite receiver control strengths, known-local-Hamiltonian simulation, classical record processing, memory protection, and calibration precision. A full coherent-dilation bin approximation is proved below; a reduced-generator approximation alone would be insufficient.

The ideal reset-register theorem and its reference-preserving error proof are complete. The finite bosonic-port implementation uses the inherited, actually acquired LOCAL constant-q receiver theorem in N1732. Its specialized waveforms, finite ramps, interpolation program, coefficient precision, and complete acquisition-memory count are given in Section 7.4 and the companion interface audit. No claim is made that arbitrary inaccessible baths, finite-efficiency collection, or an unverified reduced Lindbladian satisfy the port premise. Historical priority of this synthesis is not established.

1. SOURCE AND ACCESS MODEL

There are n source sites of dimension q. The initial state rho_SR is arbitrary; R is an inaccessible reference of arbitrary dimension. There is no preparatory encoding or prior entanglement involving the unknown payload. Let d=q^n.

The source Hamiltonian is known and local:

  H(t) = sum_X h_X(t),  |X| <= k.

Each h_X is Hermitian. For scalable compilation, q and k are fixed, the number of terms and their finite descriptions are polynomial in n, and their time dependence is efficiently computable to the requested accuracy. Static arbitrary local Hamiltonians are included. Smooth time dependence with polynomially bounded derivative is one sufficient computational promise. A black-box arbitrary real function is not automatically an efficient input description.

The ideal acquired reset/export instrument at site i has an independent rate-r Poisson clock. At a clock event it implements

  rho_(S_i rest) -> (I/q)_(S_i) tensor rho_(B rest),

where B is an actually delivered receiver qudit. The time/site flag is available. In a pure dilation an extra mark A purifies the fresh source state; the theorem may discard A. Keeping B quantum is essential. The flag probability is independent of the payload and all reference correlations.

Equivalently, conditioned on its event record gamma, the source and exports implement a known unitary U_gamma on S and m fresh bath qudits B_1,...,B_m, each initially I/q. Between events, U_gamma applies the continuous source Hamiltonian. Each event is a SWAP between source site i and the next fresh bath qudit. After its SWAP that bath qudit never reinteracts with the source; it is exported. This is a mathematical representation of an acquired output instrument, not authorization to perform source SWAP controls.

The receiver is allowed protected quantum memory, known local gates on that memory, and the actual coherent source output. It is not allowed to operate on the surviving source. The delivered output is one-way: receiver coupling must not feed an unaccounted field back into the source. Pure-vacuum source ports must be calibrated at the dilation level and delivered coherently without unaccounted loss. The existence of a reduced depolarizing generator does not establish those resources.

Time-dependent or unequal reset rates can be handled by a pointwise positive Lyapunov margin. The equal, constant rate is used to keep the statement clear.

3. THE DECODER AS AN EXPLICIT QUANTUM CIRCUIT

For an event record gamma with m events, let b=q^m and tau_B=I_B/b. Its normalized export channel is

  C_gamma(X) = Tr_S U_gamma (X tensor tau_B) U_gamma^dagger.            (3.1)

Initialize a new n-qudit receiver register S' in tau_S=I_S/d. Define

  D_gamma(Y) = Tr_B U_gamma^dagger (tau_S tensor Y) U_gamma.            (3.2)

Operationally:

  1. Keep every exported B qudit and its time/site flag.
  2. At the chosen collection endpoint initialize S' in I/q^n.
  3. Run H backwards on S' over the last inter-event interval.
  4. SWAP the indicated S' site with the last retained export.
  5. Discard the consumed export register.
  6. Repeat steps 3-5 in reverse event order, and finally reverse the first interval.
  7. Return S'.

Every operation is on receiver hardware. U_gamma has an efficiently known local decomposition: source Hamiltonian segments plus elementary SWAPs. Only the latter SWAPs are implemented by the decoder, on its own memory. A fresh maximally mixed receiver can be prepared by local randomization or by preparing and discarding halves of local maximally entangled pairs.

Equation (3.2) is CPTP. Since C_gamma(I_d/d)=I_b/b, its Petz map at I_d/d is

  D_gamma = (b/d) C_gamma^dagger.                                    (3.3)

The large scalar in this algebraic formula does not create a postselection cost: (3.2) is already a deterministic physical implementation. Maximal mixedness of the fresh bath and of the dummy receiver makes the adjoint implementable by the inverse of the known local unitary.

The whole decoded channel is

  R_T = E_gamma [D_gamma C_gamma],                                  (3.4)

with the payload-independent physical event distribution. This is an average over an observed classical record, not an ensemble of cloned inputs. One copy of the unknown input is used.

4. EXACT PROJECTION IDENTITY

Use normalized Hilbert-Schmidt inner products, <A,B>_S=Tr(A^dagger B)/d and analogously on S tensor B. Let

  J_gamma(X) = U_gamma (X tensor I_B) U_gamma^dagger.

J_gamma is an isometry between operator Hilbert spaces. Let P_0 denote conditional expectation onto operators that are identity on the entire source:

  P_0(O) = I_S tensor Tr_S(O)/d.

P_0 is an orthogonal projection. Direct substitution into (3.1)-(3.2) gives

  D_gamma C_gamma = J_gamma^dagger P_0 J_gamma.                        (4.1)

Consequently, as Hermitian operators ON OPERATOR HILBERT SPACE,

  0 <= D_gamma C_gamma <= id,

and, for every complex input operator X,

  <X,(id-D_gamma C_gamma)X>
    = ||(id-P_0) J_gamma(X)||_2^2.                                  (4.2)

These are Hilbert-space order statements, not an assertion that id-D_gamma C_gamma is a completely positive physical map.

For each source site i, let P_i be conditional expectation onto its identity operator and Q_i=id-P_i. They commute as operator-space projections. Define the source-support observable

  W = sum_i Q_i.

Since P_0=product_i P_i,

  id-P_0 <= W <= n id.                                               (4.3)

Therefore decoding loss is controlled by the amount of operator support still residing on source sites, while arbitrary support on already exported bath qudits is harmless.

5. CONTINUOUS HAMILTONIAN SUPPORT-GROWTH BOUND

Let O(t) denote an input operator X, tensored with identity on all fresh bath qudits and evolved by the conditional source-plus-bath unitary. Its normalized Hilbert-Schmidt norm is constant.

A reset SWAP at i transfers every nontrivial operator factor on source site i to its newly exported bath qudit. The fresh source factor is identity. Therefore the reset sets

  <O,Q_i O> -> 0,

while all <O,Q_j O>, j!=i, remain unchanged. Averaging the independent clocks gives exact drift -r <O,W O> from resets.

Now fix a Hamiltonian term h_X on a set of s=|X| sites. On operator Hilbert space define the Hermitian operator

  K_X = ad(h_X),  ad(h_X)(A)=[h_X,A].

Its operator norm is osc(h_X). Let

  W_X = sum_(i in X) Q_i,
  P_X = id - product_(i in X) P_i.

K_X annihilates the local identity sector and preserves its orthogonal complement:

  K_X P_X = P_X K_X = K_X.

On that complement, the spectrum of W_X lies in {1,...,s}. Therefore

  || W_X - (s+1) P_X/2 || <= (s-1)/2,

and

  || [K_X,W_X] || <= (s-1) osc(h_X).                                 (5.1)

The commutator is supported on P_X. Thus

  i[K_X,W_X] <= (s-1) osc(h_X) P_X
              <= (s-1) osc(h_X) W_X.                               (5.2)

One-site Hamiltonians have s-1=0: they rotate local operators but do not spread their support.

Let c_X(t)=(|X|-1)osc(h_X(t)) and assume the uniform bound

  beta >= sup_t max_i sum_(X containing i) c_X(t).

Adding (5.2) over terms gives the deterministic Hamiltonian drift inequality

  d/dt <O,W O> <= beta <O,W O>.                                     (5.3)

After averaging resets,

  d/dt E <O,W O> <= -(r-beta) E <O,W O>.

For kappa=r-beta>0,

  E <O(T),W O(T)> <= n exp(-kappa T) ||X||_2^2.                       (5.4)

Combining (4.2)-(4.3) with (5.4) yields the uniform operator-space inequality

  0 <= id-R_T <= delta_T id,
  delta_T = min(1,n exp(-kappa T)).                                  (5.5)

This statement holds for every input operator, not only for a Pauli average or the maximally mixed input. That uniformity supplies the reference-preserving conclusion.

An equivalent finite-dimensional analytical description is useful for checking the proof. Regard each source-site operator space as a q^2-level system with identity vector |0>. Conditional Hamiltonian motion is generated by ad(H); a reset exports that operator-space site and replaces it with |0>. The averaged operator-space density matrix evolves under pure local reset channels. For time-independent H, if L_op is that generator, then

  R_T = exp(T L_op^dagger)(|0...0><0...0|).

The bound follows from L_op^dagger(W)<=-kappa W. This auxiliary process is only a proof tool. The actual decoder does not construct or simulate its q^(2n)-dimensional matrices.

6. DIMENSION-FREE REFERENCE AND FULL-RECORD GUARANTEE

Hilbert-space positivity and order in (5.5) tensor with the identity on the operator Hilbert space of any reference R. For any normalized pure joint state Psi_SR,

  Tr[ Psi (R_T tensor id_R)(Psi) ]
    = 1 - <Psi,[(id-R_T) tensor id_R](Psi)>_(unnormalized)
    >= 1-delta_T.

Fuchs-van de Graaf for a pure target implies

  ||(R_T tensor id_R)(Psi)-Psi||_1 <= 2 sqrt(delta_T).

Maximizing over pure inputs and references gives (0.1). There is no factor q^n. A Choi-state average estimate followed by a generic diamond conversion would lose this fact and is unnecessary.

The same bound can retain the entire classical event record. For each fixed pure Psi, define f_gamma(Psi)=Tr[Psi (D_gamma C_gamma tensor id)(Psi)]. The averaged operator inequality gives E_gamma[1-f_gamma(Psi)]<=delta_T uniformly in Psi. This does NOT require averaging per-record operator norms. Each conditional output obeys the pure-target trace-distance bound, and the classical-quantum output trace norm is the probability-weighted sum of its orthogonal record blocks. Therefore, by concavity of square root,

  E_gamma ||(D_gamma C_gamma tensor id)(Psi)-Psi||_1
       <= 2 sqrt(delta_T).

Hence the joint output instrument is close to an independent event record tensored with the original payload. Discarding or processing the record afterwards cannot increase the error.

This fixed-time statement is unconditional. It does not herald or retry a destroyed input. It applies to the single originally supplied state.

7. FINITE ACQUISITION FROM THE ACTUAL CONTINUOUS OUTPUT

The Boolean active-set analysis in causal_reset_decoder.txt does not survive infinitesimal Hamiltonian gates: a tiny entangling gate can have a full Boolean support. The present weighted estimate has finite growth exp(beta Delta), so it survives a convergent dilation discretization.

7.1 Full coherent-dilation bin comparison

Write Lambda=sup_t ||H(t)||. In this subsection R denotes the total PHYSICAL coarse jump rate of the actual nonidentity-Weyl ports,

  R = n c,  c=r(1-q^(-2)).

The conservative upper bound R<=nr can be used in every bin-size formula. This physical photon rate is distinct from the effective reset rate r used in the recovery estimate and p=1-exp(-r Delta). A local decomposition supplies the sharper quantity

  chi = sup_t max_i sum_(X containing i) osc(h_X(t)),

which includes one-site terms. Split [0,T] into N equal bins of width Delta=T/N.

Compare the actual source-and-output isometry in each bin with a fictitious model consisting of noise-only local depolarizing evolution throughout that bin, followed by the exact time-ordered Hamiltonian unitary for that bin. The Hamiltonian kick is a comparison model; it is NOT an extra action on the physical source.

Use the ACTUAL nonidentity-Weyl output dilation for this comparison; no coherent reference port is added. Its no-jump damping is scalar because total intensity is R I. Coarse time/site histories therefore have payload-independent Poisson weights. At a physical jump of site i, preserve the internal port label coherently and use the normalized local isometry

  J_i = (1/sqrt(c)) sum_(mu!=0) L_(i,mu) tensor |mu>.

This J_i is generally not a reset isometry. The following comparison only needs its locality and isometry property, which it has because sum L^dagger L=c I. Every Hamiltonian term disjoint from i commutes through J_i. Centering a touching Hermitian term around the midpoint of its spectral interval gives

  || H_out(t) J_i - J_i H_in(t) ||
       <= sum_(X containing i) osc(h_X(t)) <= chi.

Duhamel's identity therefore gives

  || U(t,s) J_i - J_i U(t,s) || <= chi |t-s|.

For a history with m physical jumps in a bin, move all Hamiltonian evolution to the end, preserving the time order of Hamiltonian segments. Each local jump isometry crosses total Hamiltonian duration at most Delta, so

  ||V_history-W_history|| <= chi Delta m.                            (7.1)

The stronger bound keeps the entire history coherent before estimating the error. Let m_total be the total number of physical events in [0,T]. Telescoping only the within-bin changes gives

  ||V_full_history-W_full_history|| <= chi Delta m_total.

Actual and split models have the same orthogonal full time/site history sectors and the same payload-independent Poisson weights. Since m_total is Poisson with mean RT, squaring and integrating gives

  ||V_continuous-W_split||
       <= chi Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2).                                (7.2)

An isometry error a gives channel diamond error at most 2a. Consequently

  epsilon_split <= 2 chi Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2).                       (7.3)

This global-history estimate avoids a triangle inequality over bins. It requires no restriction R Delta<=1; the noise-only local collector treats all event numbers within a bin. A sufficient integer count is

  N >= max(1,ceil[2 chi T sqrt(RT+(RT)^2)/epsilon_split]).             (7.4)

For comparison, estimating one bin at a time and then adding its errors gives the weaker but independently valid bound

  epsilon_split <= 2 chi T sqrt(R Delta+(R Delta)^2).

The independent global-norm bound uses 2Lambda per local-isometry crossing. It yields 4 Lambda Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2) with the global-history argument, or 4 Lambda T sqrt(R Delta+(R Delta)^2) with per-bin telescoping. No inequality between decomposition-defined chi and 2Lambda is assumed: cancellation between local terms can invalidate such an inequality.

These are FULL source-plus-output bounds, stronger than a reduced-source Trotter estimate, and they remain valid after any common receiver processing. They pay the noncommuting continuous dynamics in the actual coherent output. They do not identify channels with different unacquired environments merely because their reduced generators agree.

8. STORAGE, INVERSE SIMULATION, AND COMPLETE COST ACCOUNTING

8.1 Quantum and classical storage

For the exact continuous reset-register model, M is Poisson with mean mu=nrT. For the finite-bin comparison, M is binomial(nN,p), with mean mu=nNp<=nrT. In either case,

  E exp(M) <= exp((e-1)mu).

Thus the fixed cap

  M_max = ceil((e-1)mu + log(1/zeta))

has overflow probability at most zeta. Replacing overflow output by an arbitrary state adds at most 2zeta to the full channel error. The receiver need not store unbounded memory.

The logical decoder storage requirement is n+M_max q-dimensional registers. The fully finite conservative interface additionally provisions O(nN) constant-dimensional collector banks as in Section 7.4. Each consumed export can be discarded during reverse processing. At fixed positive margin kappa and fixed target error, expected retained payload exports are O(n log n); this does not replace the O(nN) complete acquisition-memory bill. The ideal record can be stored as O(M_max log(nN)) classical bits in the binned construction: absent-reset bins need not all remain in memory. Reading and processing all local bin flags still costs O(nN).

8.2 Inverse Hamiltonian simulation

For static H=sum_(a=1)^L h_a, let

  C_H=sum_(a<b)||[h_a,h_b]||.

A first-order product formula over subintervals of maximal duration s has total unitary error bounded by a constant times C_H T s. Insert exact record-dependent SWAPs at the appropriate boundaries. Choosing s=O(epsilon_sim/(C_H T)) suffices. The number of local Hamiltonian gates is bounded by

  O( L [ C_H T^2/epsilon_sim + M_max + 1 ] ).                         (8.1)

The M_max term allows every reset boundary to be an additional simulation cut. In the binned model one can combine consecutive Hamiltonian bins with no reset, so a large N does not by itself force N separate digital inverse simulations. A cruder O(LN) decomposition is also polynomial.

For fixed k,q and bounded interaction degree and strength, L=O(n) and C_H=O(n) in fixed physical units, making (8.1) polynomial. If H(t) is time dependent, a bound on sum_a||dot h_a|| gives a standard additional first-order freezing error; efficiently accessible higher-order or modern simulation methods can replace this conservative construction. One-site fields do not enter beta, but their simulation and control costs are still paid.

Each fixed-q^k gate is synthesized to error O(epsilon_gate/G), where G is the actual finite gate count. Universal-gate synthesis has its usual precision-dependent overhead. Routing between a site register and stored export registers must also be included: arbitrary local-memory wiring can be supplied explicitly, or conservative nearest-neighbour SWAP routing gives polynomial additional gates. No exponential-dimensional gate is assumed primitive.

8.3 Time resolution and register capture in the ideal event model

If event times are only known to resolution sigma, and there are at most M_max events, a crude telescoping bound gives decoder channel error at most 4 M_max Lambda sigma. An inverse-polynomial sigma therefore suffices. The bin-collector construction already fixes a finite time grid and does not require exact analog event-time storage.

8.4 Physical control, loss, and calibration

Total interface work is nN local bin uses, not merely the mean O(nrT) successful exports. Local collector peak control, classical clock precision, pure ancilla resets, port switching/delay, and quantum-memory protection are resources. The explicit N1732 specialization in Section 7.4 determines their polynomial dependence on Delta and eta. If a hardware instance has a fixed smaller bandwidth or peak coupling, the asymptotic family does not automatically fit that instance.

Each receiver operation with error nu contributes at most nu by telescoping. The sum of gate, capture, loss, and storage errors must fit the stated budget. Fixed physical error per operation with no error correction is not sufficient as n grows. Fault-tolerant implementation may be used under a separately specified standard threshold and architecture; it is not hidden in an ideal memory assumption.

The model assumes known calibrated H and ports. For example, an integrated Hamiltonian mismatch integral ||H-H_hat|| dt contributes a corresponding unitary/channel error and must be bounded. Unknown many-body calibration is a separate acquisition problem. Access to the reduced generator alone supplies neither coherent field collection nor the required calibration.

Any additional inaccessible dephasing or loss channel needs its own error/resource accounting and can invalidate high-fidelity full-input recovery. The earlier acquisition converse remains applicable when its inaccessible-noise and noncommuting-control hypotheses hold. Here the actual accessible depolarizing reset/export operation supplies a different paid interface.

9. OPTIONAL OBSERVABLE WEIGHTED STOPPING CERTIFICATE

The sharper fixed-time theorem uses beta above. A slightly more conservative growth matrix yields a computable certificate for individual observed records.

For a norm-one propagated operator O, define x_i=||Q_i O||_2^2. For a term h_X with |X|>=2, the commutator with the projection Q_i satisfies

  ||[ad(h_X),Q_i]|| <= osc(h_X),  i in X.

It is supported on P_X. Hence

  |d x_i/dt| <= sum_(X containing i, |X|>=2)
                    osc(h_X) sum_(j in X) x_j.                      (9.1)

For one-site terms the derivative is zero. At a reset of site i, x_i becomes zero and other x_j are unchanged.

Define a nonnegative classical comparison vector v with v_i(0)=1 and

  v_i' = sum_(X containing i, |X|>=2)
                 c_X sum_(j in X) v_j,                             (9.2)

where c_X is a certified time-uniform upper bound on osc(h_X(t)). At each observed reset set v_i=0. Comparison for a positive linear system gives

  x_i(t) <= v_i(t) ||X_initial||_2^2

uniformly for every input operator, even if it has reference factors. One may safely clamp v_i to 1 because x_i/||X_initial||^2<=1; this prevents numerical overflow and can only improve the bound.

For the actual record gamma at time t,

  0 <= id-D_gamma C_gamma <= min(1,sum_i v_i(t)) id.                  (9.3)

Thus every record with sum_i v_i<=a has conditional decoder diamond error at most 2sqrt(a). This certificate depends only on the known Hamiltonian bounds and observed reset flags. It does not estimate an unknown quantum observable or measure the payload.

Let

  beta_cert=max_j sum_(X containing j, |X|>=2) |X| c_X,
  kappa_cert=r-beta_cert.

If kappa_cert>0, then E sum_i v_i(t)<=n exp(-kappa_cert t). Let tau be the first time the sum is at most a. Since tau>t implies the sum at t exceeds a,

  Pr(tau>t) <= min(1,(n/a)exp[-kappa_cert t]),
  E tau <= [log(n/a)+1]/kappa_cert.                                  (9.4)

Each accepted stopping record has error at most 2sqrt(a). A cutoff at

  T_cap=log(n/(a zeta))/kappa_cert

fails with probability at most zeta. The success flag remains independent of the payload in the ideal acquired model. Counting all failure probability, a capped decoder has unconditional error at most 2sqrt(a)+2zeta, before compilation errors. The mean number of exports before stopping is nr E tau by the Poisson compensator.

In the finite-bin construction, evolve v through the known Hamiltonian comparison over a bin and apply the observed reset mask in the matching order. The expectation contraction is exp[-kappa_cert Delta]. Certified upper rounding and interval arithmetic preserve the certificate; ordinary unverified floating-point underestimation does not. A sparse n-dimensional positive linear ODE, or conservative bounds on its exponential, can be processed in polynomial time. No q^(2n)-dimensional quantum-state calculation is required.

The all-bank physical construction may defer flag processing until collection ends. It can then identify an earlier certified prefix and decode from that prefix, since later source-only evolution cannot change already emitted registers. It does not thereby shorten the actual collection duration. Real-time stopping gains require a finite causally available endpoint/flag-processing schedule and its latency cost; the main fixed-time theorem does not need that extra schedule.

The uniform per-record assertion applies to the ideal acquired instrument. A finite collector error specified only in unconditional diamond norm controls the full instrument, but does not preserve the same conditional bound on every arbitrarily rare branch. For a physical capped protocol, aggregate accepted-map errors must be divided by the actual success probability when reporting conditional accuracy. The fixed-time unconditional theorem avoids this normalization issue.

10. WHY THE CONSTRUCTION WORKS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT PROVE

Operational corollary: under this acquired interface, every subsequent G-gate quantum experiment on the initial register can be performed output-only by composing its circuit with the decoder. Complete-channel contractivity bounds the additional error by epsilon, including external references, adaptive measurement records, and quantum outputs when all branches are retained. Its cost is G plus the stated polynomial acquisition overhead. Rare postselection requires its usual success normalization. This supplies efficient access to the initial quantum register; it does not supply a new payload-loading operation or an unearned communication-throughput claim.

A maximally mixed replacement source is generally not enough to invert an arbitrary open-system dilation. For example, a maximally mixed classical key controlling Pauli randomization can leave the source reduced state completely mixed and its bath alone independent of the input; the information resides in source-bath correlations. Mere source forgetting or unitality therefore does not prove output recoverability after discarding bath purifiers.

The additional result here is that, under sufficiently strong local reset/export, EVERY initial operator loses its residual source support at a uniform rate. This makes inverse dynamics with a dummy source close to true recovery. It is a bound on the specifically implemented decoder, not merely existence from information-disturbance duality.

The noise threshold r>beta is sufficient and generally conservative. Below it the proof does not establish a no-go or zero capacity. Keeping the A purifiers, using another decoder, exploiting extra symmetries, or accepting another task may help. The record certificate can sometimes succeed for individual histories outside its simple average threshold.

The inverse receiver uses polynomial, generally nonlocal memory processing. This note does not prove a constant-size chronological memory receiver, a constant-depth decoder, or useful device economics at fixed physical error and bandwidth. Expected retained memory is O(n log(n/epsilon)); total finite interface work can have a much larger polynomial prefactor because the explicit binning proof is conservative.

11. STRONG ORDINARY COMPARATORS AND PRIOR-ART BOUNDARY

(a) Direct parallel source SWAP. If the user can coherently SWAP every source site into protected memory, that is the simpler ordinary construction. This theorem grants no such source access. The actual source Hamiltonian continues throughout collection. All deliberately implemented inversion SWAPs occur inside the receiver. No advantage over direct source control is claimed.

(b) Same ports, no interactions. When H contains only one-site terms, beta=0 and reversing those rotations plus collecting old-site exports solves the ordinary coupon-collector problem. After the rotations are undone, the decoded channel is the tensor product of n maps [(1-exp(-rT))id+exp(-rT)R_(I/q)]. Its exact diamond error is

  2 {1-[1-(1-q^(-2))exp(-rT)]^n},

by expressing the channel as a Weyl mixture and testing a maximally entangled input. Thus this ordinary reset instrument already achieves logarithmic collection time, with error of order n exp(-rT). The interacting result preserves logarithmic time under a paid local interaction margin, although the general square-root bound is more conservative. No optimality against every possible coherent field decoder is asserted.

(c) Same ports, read only classical Pauli jump labels. This yields known unitary error records but does not deliver the already unknown source state. The coherent local mark conversion is a different measurement/interface operation on the actual output. Measuring a,b separately destroys the exported quantum payload.

(d) Generic transpose/Petz recovery. Petz recovery, time reversal, and inverse-unitary implementations for suitable mixed environments are established tools. The new derivation here is the operator-support contraction and its composition with a finite acquired local interface, yielding a uniform full-input guarantee with explicit scalable implementation. No claim is made to invent the Petz channel or operator-size methods.

(e) General N1732 receiver. It handles any known finite-dimensional pure-port Markov source and approximates the canonical Stinespring channel. Applied globally to d=q^n it has dense costs in d. Here it is invoked only on independent constant-q noise-only bins after a FULL coherent-dilation comparison. Global payload recovery is performed by the structured reverse circuit. This is the step that removes dense exponential synthesis for this source class.

(f) High-noise classical simulation. Being able to predict local observables or sample classical outputs does not produce the actual arbitrary quantum state or preserve an external reference. The decoder above does both; it requires quantum memory and quantum inverse dynamics.

(g) Offline entanglement generation and later teleportation. That is a useful different architecture, but it requires introduction of the payload after the entanglement resource or a source-side Bell operation. It does not replace acquisition of the already present arbitrary input under the same output-only access restriction.

The accompanying script verify_reverse_reset.py performs finite exact matrix checks for n=2 qubits, H=XX+0.37 ZI+0.23 IZ, and per-site reset rate r=6. The one interacting term has osc(XX)=2, so beta=2 and kappa=4.

It independently constructs:
  - a two-reset trajectory as a unitary on source and two maximally mixed bath qubits;
  - its export channel C and physical inverse-unitary decoder D;
  - the corresponding operator-space pure-reset evolution;
  - the exact continuous averaged recovery matrix from the auxiliary generator;
  - its physical Choi matrix and the Lyapunov operator inequality.

  direct trajectory versus operator-space identity operator-norm error:
      5.88e-16;
  physical decoder versus scaled Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint error:
      2.55e-16;

      0 within numerical precision;
