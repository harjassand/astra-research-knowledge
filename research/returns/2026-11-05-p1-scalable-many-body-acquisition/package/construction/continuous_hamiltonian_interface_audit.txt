1. EXACT CHANNEL AND NORMALIZATION AUDIT

There are n sites of local dimension q, d=q^n. An independent input-independent
reset at site i exports its old state into B and leaves a fresh maximally mixed
state tau=I/q at i. The pure event isometry is

  V_i |psi> = |Phi_q>_(S_i A) |psi>_B.

The receiver obtains the coarse time/site flag and B coherently and may discard A.
For a fixed record gamma with m events, set b=q^m. The actual conditional channel
has a unitary representation on source plus fresh maximally mixed bath registers:

  C_gamma(rho) = Tr_S U_gamma (rho tensor I_B/b) U_gamma^dagger.

The physical decoder prepares an n-qudit dummy source I_S/d, applies U_gamma^dagger on the dummy and all retained B registers, and traces the bath:

  D_gamma(sigma) = Tr_B U_gamma^dagger (I_S/d tensor sigma) U_gamma.

Both are CPTP. All inverse operations act on receiver hardware. The unitary
representation of the reset is a statement about the acquired instrument; it
does not grant source-side SWAP access.

For normalized Hilbert-Schmidt inner products Tr(X^dagger Y)/d and Tr(E^dagger F)/b,
define J(X)=X tensor I_B and E_S(Y)=Tr_S(Y)/d. Then

  F_gamma = E_S Ad_(U_gamma) J,
  C_gamma = (d/b) F_gamma,
  D_gamma = (b/d) F_gamma^dagger,
  R_gamma := D_gamma C_gamma = F_gamma^dagger F_gamma.

Here dagger on F is the adjoint between the two normalized operator Hilbert
spaces. This distinction matters: naively identifying C^dagger with the physical
decoder loses the b/d factor. The factors cancel in R.

Let P(Y)=I_S tensor Tr_S(Y)/d. This is the orthogonal projection onto operators
with identity on every source site. Unitary invariance and the isometry J give

  0 <= R_gamma <= id  [operator-Hilbert-space order],
  <X,(id-R_gamma)X>
      = ||(id-P) U_gamma(X tensor I_B) U_gamma^dagger||_2^2.

The inequality is NOT a claim that id-R_gamma is a completely positive physical
map. It is a positive Hermitian matrix on the Hilbert space of operators.

2. CONTINUOUS LOCAL SUPPORT BOUND

For each source site let E_i be normalized conditional expectation onto its
identity factor, Q_i=id-E_i, and W=sum_i Q_i. The Q_i are commuting orthogonal
projections and id-P <= W <= n id.

For O=U_gamma(X tensor I_B)U_gamma^dagger, write w_i=<O,Q_i O> and w=sum_i w_i.
The normalized Hilbert-Schmidt norm of O remains ||X||_2. A reset SWAP at i replaces
the source-site operator factor by a fresh identity factor. Consequently w_i is
set EXACTLY to zero, and every w_j for j!=i is unchanged, even when O contains
arbitrary source/bath correlations. Independent reset clocks give expected drift
-r E w.

Write the known local Hamiltonian H(t)=sum_X h_X(t), |X|<=k. On operator Hilbert
space K_X=ad(h_X) is Hermitian and ||K_X||=osc(h_X), with osc(h)=lambda_max-
lambda_min. Let P_X=id-product_(i in X) E_i, the complement of local identity.
K_X=P_X K_X P_X. On this subspace W_X=sum_(i in X)Q_i has spectrum 1,...,|X|.
Subtracting its midpoint therefore gives

  ||[K_X,W_X]|| <= (|X|-1) osc(h_X).

The commutator is supported on P_X, so as a quadratic-form inequality

  i[K_X,W_X] <= (|X|-1)osc(h_X) P_X
              <= (|X|-1)osc(h_X) sum_(i in X)Q_i.

This includes complex, non-Hermitian input X. No positivity assumption about X
has entered. One-site Hamiltonians contribute exactly zero.

Define

  beta = sup_t max_i sum_(X containing i)(|X|-1)osc(h_X(t)),
  kappa = r-beta > 0.

It follows that E w(T) <= n exp(-kappa T)||X||_2^2, uniformly over X. For
Rbar_T=E_gamma R_gamma,

  0 <= id-Rbar_T <= delta_T id,
  delta_T = min(1,n exp(-kappa T)).

This proves the main Lyapunov claim. The expectation is over an input-independent
record. Arbitrary feedback controlled by that record would require rechecking
locality and the pointwise beta bound; no unrestricted feedback claim is needed.

3. REFERENCE-PRESERVING ERROR AUDIT

The preceding positive operator-space inequality tensors with the identity on
the operator Hilbert space of an arbitrary reference. Multiplying the normalized
inner product by the source/reference dimension, for any joint pure density Psi,

  Tr[Psi (Rbar_T tensor id)(Psi)] >= 1-delta_T.

Because the output is a density matrix, pure-state fidelity and trace distance
imply

  ||Rbar_T-id||_diamond <= 2 sqrt(delta_T).

There is NO factor q^n. The proof controls all operator directions and is stronger
than an average Choi-state fidelity statement. It is incorrect to insert a generic
Choi-to-diamond dimension factor at this step.

The whole classical record can be retained: conditional squared overlaps have
average infidelity at most delta_T, and concavity of square root yields the same
bound on the record-labelled output instrument versus an independent record
and perfect payload. This also justifies average-error use of finite memory caps.

For ideal decoder error epsilon_0, it suffices to take

  T >= [log n+2 log(2/epsilon_0)]/kappa.

The result concerns the original arbitrary input. There is no heralded restart or
new payload supplied after a resource has been prepared.

4. COHERENT DILATION SPLITTING AUDIT

Revised to the stronger complete-history comparison on 2026-11-04. The earlier
per-bin triangle bound was valid but unnecessarily lost a square root of the
bin width.

Assume actual local calibrated vacuum ports L_(i,ab)=sqrt(r/q)|a><b|_i, all
coherently delivered. Their total jump intensity is R I with R=nr. Actual
nonidentity Weyl depolarizing ports L_(i,mu)=sqrt(r)W_mu/q, mu!=0, are also
included; their physical total intensity is R_phys=nr(1-q^-2). More generally,
the following comparison uses any specified local isometric jump marks with
state-independent total jump intensity R I.

Partition time into L bins, Delta=T/L. Compare the actual joint source+field
isometry with the fictitious model having noise-only depolarization during every
bin followed by the exact time-ordered Hamiltonian unitary for that bin. This
fictitious kick is only an analysis model; the actual source is not pulsed.

Set

  chi = sup_t max_i sum_(X containing i) osc(h_X(t)).

Let J_i denote the normalized local coherent jump-mark isometry. For matrix-unit
ports it is exactly the reset isometry V_i. Terms disjoint from i intertwine
through J_i. Center each remaining Hermitian h_X at its spectral midpoint. Since
J_i is an isometry, the triangle inequality proves

  ||H_out(t)J_i - J_i H_in(t)|| <= chi.

Duhamel's identity then bounds commuting a Hamiltonian segment of duration s
through J_i by chi*s. Fix ONE COMPLETE event history gamma over [0,T], with
m_gamma total events. Move Hamiltonian pieces only inside their original bins,
preserving the chronological order of all Hamiltonian pieces. Each jump crosses
at most Delta total Hamiltonian time. Isometries and unitaries have norm one, so

  ||V_gamma-W_gamma|| <= chi Delta m_gamma.

This estimate includes the no-event history, whose discrepancy is exactly zero.
It is uniform over all input vectors, including a reference, and involves the
coherent mark space rather than a measurement of each jump's matrix index.

The full arrival-time/site histories are orthogonal sectors in the output Fock
space. Their weights are input independent because the total intensity is R I.
Writing the full dilation as their direct integral gives

  ||V-W||^2 <= integral p(d gamma) ||V_gamma-W_gamma||^2
             <= chi^2 Delta^2 E[m_gamma^2].

Here m_gamma is Poisson(RT), so E[m_gamma^2]=RT+(RT)^2. The resulting FULL joint
source+field channel bound is

  epsilon_split <= 2 chi Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2).                 [improved]

One may cap the right side at 2. The time variation of H does not spoil this
argument: the H pieces preserve their original time labels and order when moved;
no time-independent-H replacement is being made.

Thus a sufficient integer number of global bins is

  L >= ceil(2 chi T sqrt(RT+(RT)^2)/epsilon_split),   L>=1.

For RT>=1 this is O(1+chi R T^2/epsilon_split). The number of LOCAL collector
uses is nL, not L. There is no separate requirement R Delta<=1 for this global
bound. Locality can alternatively be dropped by bounding the whole intertwining
commutator by 2 sup_t||H(t)||, giving the independent valid bound
4 sup_t||H(t)|| Delta sqrt(RT+(RT)^2). No inequality between chi and
2 sup_t||H(t)|| is assumed for a particular local decomposition.

For the actual nonidentity Weyl ports, perform this comparison in their physical
Fock space using R=R_phys. The noise-only local channel is nevertheless exactly
D_p=(1-p)id+pR_tau with p=1-exp(-r Delta). N1732 can acquire its canonical local
environment directly; a subsequent finite-dimensional environment isometry gives
the empty/reset dilation used below. Its coarse reset flags are not physical
photon counts. No explicit identity-port augmentation is required on this route.

Alternatively, one may append the source-independent coherent identity port
sqrt(r)I/q at the receiver and then mix the q^2 local ports into the matrix-unit
representation. That route pays flux r/q^2 per site. Appending the SAME pure
reference and applying the SAME port unitary after the physical comparison
preserves the improved bound with R_phys. Do not replace R_phys by a smaller
count rate unless the actual calibrated dilation supports it. Knowing only a
reduced depolarizing generator does not acquire any of these coherent ports.

This is a bound on the FULL coherent dilation. A reduced-source Trotter bound
would not justify applying an output decoder. A common receiver postprocessing
contracts this bound, which is exactly why the local collectors can be run on
the real interacting continuous output.

5. LOCAL COLLECTOR AND FINITE RESOURCE BILL

The fictitious local noise bin has channel

  D_p=(1-p)id+p R_tau, p=1-exp(-r Delta).

Its convenient nonminimal dilation is

  |psi> -> sqrt(1-p)|psi>|empty>
           +sqrt(p)|Phi_q>_(S A)|psi>_B.

The empty-versus-reset measurement does not measure a or b separately. Coarse
flags are independent Bernoulli(p), and B is retained quantum. Converting the
canonical q^2-dimensional environment into this q^2+1-dimensional nonminimal
representation is a fixed-dimensional isometry with a local workspace. It is not
an equality of environments inferred only from reduced dynamics: the canonical
channel is first acquired by the inherited receiver, including the source and
all references, before this local isometry is applied.

Invoke N1732 on local source dimension q in each site/bin. There are N=nL local
collector uses. If each full collector/instrument error is eta, their total is
at most N eta. The same operations on the actual field differ by epsilon_split.
The ideal digital support bound has one-bin multiplier exp(beta Delta) times
exp(-r Delta), so it gives the same n exp(-kappa T) bound uniformly as Delta->0.

The actual measured record need not be exactly input independent after these
approximations. All comparisons must be made at the full instrument/channel
level; do not assert exact record probabilities for the physical approximate
implementation and then condition or reweight for free.

A conservative total budget is

  epsilon_total <= 2 sqrt(n exp(-kappa T)) + epsilon_split
                   +N eta +epsilon_inverse +epsilon_memory
                   +epsilon_calibration +2 Pr(overflow).

Take T=O(log(n/epsilon)/kappa), L=poly(n,T,chi,1/epsilon), eta=Theta(epsilon/N).
For fixed local dimension and arity and polynomial numerical input promises,
all calls below have polynomial cost in n, 1/epsilon, 1/kappa and the charged
local strengths. This is a polynomial upper bound, not a favorable polynomial.

5.1 Collector controls from the governing source

N1732's source return gives a faithful unital guide floor mu=alpha/q^2, with
regularization alpha=Theta(eta^2) sufficient for guide error O(eta). If g=||G||,
l_j=||L_j||, s=sum_j l_j^2, and b0=2g+s, it states

  ||C_j|| <= l_j mu^(-1/2),       ||H_M|| <= g mu^(-1/2),
  ||dot C_j|| <= l_j b0 mu^(-3/2),
  ||dot H_M|| <= g b0 mu^(-3/2),
  N_segments >= 2 b0(g+5s)Delta^2/(zeta mu^2).

For fixed q and local depolarization, g,s,b0=O_q(r). Choose zeta=Theta(eta):

  peak port coupling: O_q(sqrt(r)/eta),
  peak receiver Hamiltonian: O_q(r/eta),
  pre-ramp port slew: O_q(r^(3/2)/eta^3),
  pre-ramp Hamiltonian slew: O_q(r^2/eta^3),
  segments per local bin: O_q(1+(r Delta)^2/eta^5).

The local dimension is fixed, so dense local root/Sylvester work is a fixed-
dimension operation repeated polynomially. The same analytic depolarizing
waveform can be compiled once and reused across sites/bins. Total executed
control pieces are N times the per-bin count; waveform reuse does not remove
physical execution cost.

Ramps and inter-bin processing are real additional costs. A sufficient simple
ramp duration is at most min(Delta/4,c eta^3/r): the joint source+collector
Lindblad generator norm is O_q(r eta^-2), so modifying it for that duration costs
O(eta). The resulting ramp slew is at most the peak divided by this duration,
still polynomial. This is a conservative sufficient estimate. Two alternating
collector banks per site allow one bank to receive while the other exports,
measures, and reinitializes. Their constant-dimensional gates must complete
within Delta; control rate O(poly(log(1/eta))/Delta) or equivalent extra banks
is charged. No fixed laboratory bandwidth is thereby certified adequate.

These are finite Markov-model control bounds. As in N1732, they do not imply a
bounded microscopic field Hamiltonian or a verified device beyond its calibrated
bandwidth. A physically fixed power/bandwidth ceiling must be substituted into
these inequalities before any device claim.

5.2 Memory, clock and inverse gates

For the split model M is binomial(N,p), E M=Np<=nrT=:mu0. A convenient cap is
ceil((e-1)mu0+log(1/zeta)); exponential Markov bounds overflow by zeta. Quantum
storage is n+M_cap qudits plus O(n) collector workspace. Expected retained exports
are O(nrT). Classical record storage is O(M_cap log(nL)) bits, while all N coarse
flags still have to be processed. The exact continuous ideal model uses Poisson
M and satisfies the same cap.

For static H=sum_(a=1)^m h_a, let B0=sum_a||h_a||. A conservative first-order
product formula of total simulation time T, subdividing additionally at reset
locations, uses

  O(m[M_cap+1+B0^2 T^2/epsilon_inverse])

local exponential gates, plus M_cap inverse SWAPs. Fixed q,k permit standard
fixed-dimensional synthesis with a logarithmic precision overhead. Local
commutator sums can sharpen B0^2; the crude bound already proves polynomial cost.
Efficient time-dependent simulation requires an efficiently computable promised
H(t), e.g. piecewise smooth with polynomial variation. An arbitrary real-valued
function is not an efficient program.

Local storage error must be budgeted over the actual dwell time and number of
stored registers. Fault tolerance, if used, has its ordinary encoding, gates,
ancillas and time overhead. The theorem does not supply ideal memory for free.
Calibration must be sufficient to meet the acquired full-channel interface error
budget. It is a premise/paid characterization requirement, not established by a
claim to have fitted a reduced Lindbladian.

6. RECORD-WISE COMPUTABLE CERTIFICATE

For a specific observed ideal record, the following n real numbers upper bound
all operator directions simultaneously. Set v_i(0)=1. Between resets integrate

  v_i' = sum_(X containing i, |X|>=2) osc(h_X(t)) sum_(j in X) v_j.

At reset i set v_i=0. All coefficients are nonnegative. For the ideal split model,
use this differential evolution during each Hamiltonian kick and then set v_i=0
at its flagged reset sites in the appropriate chronological order.

Proof: for Q_i a projection, ||[K_X,Q_i]||<=||K_X||=osc(h_X). The commutator is
supported on P_X, hence its quadratic form is bounded by osc(h_X) sum_(j in X)w_j.
One-site terms commute with Q_i. Thus w_i' <= sum_X osc(h_X)sum_j w_j. Initially
w_i<=||X||_2^2, and the exact reset sets w_i to zero. Positive linear-system
comparison on every interval, followed by the shared reset maps, proves

  w_i(t) <= v_i(t)||X||_2^2.

Consequently, for every record gamma,

  ||R_gamma-id||_diamond <= 2 sqrt(min(1,sum_i v_i(T))).

This gives an explicit quality certificate without constructing the exponentially
large recovery matrix. It can support record-dependent stopping, but the mean
rate condition for THIS coarse certificate is different: define

  a = sup_t max_j sum_(X containing j, |X|>=2)|X| osc(h_X(t)).

Then E sum_i v_i <= n exp(-(r-a)T) only when r>a. The main theorem's sharper r>beta
bound does not automatically imply fast convergence of this particular record
certificate. Do not conflate these two sufficient conditions. Observing a small
certificate remains sound at any rates; failure to certify is not failure of
recovery. Validated numerical integration can maintain conservative upper bounds.

7. EXACT DIAGONAL-HAMILTONIAN SUPPLEMENT: NO RESET-TO-INTERACTION THRESHOLD

Assume every h_X(t) is diagonal in one known fixed product basis and efficiently
integrable. At each reset measure its purifier A in that basis. The result a is
uniform and independent of the payload; it prepares the new source site in known
basis state |a>. Retain B only at the FIRST reset of each site. Later exports from
that site are known basis states and can be discarded.

Let tau_i be the first reset time of i, and let a_i(t) be its most recently observed
reset label after tau_i. For an initial product-basis string x, define

  z_i(t;x)=x_i for t<tau_i, and z_i(t;x)=a_i(t) for t>=tau_i.

Conditioned on the complete classical record, the unknown initial amplitudes
are multiplied only by the phase

  exp[-i f_gamma(x)],
  f_gamma(x)=integral_0^T sum_X h_X(t; z_X(t;x)) dt.

After every site has reset once, those amplitudes reside in the first-export
registers B_i, possibly entangled with any original reference. The final source
and every discarded later export are basis states specified by the record and
carry no x dependence. Apply the diagonal unitary exp[+i f_gamma] to the retained
B_i. Recovery is EXACT for that record.

A local term contributes a phase on at most k retained logical qudits, with all
already-reset source labels inserted as known classical values. Break its
integral at reset events touching that term. For bounded local degree the total
number of phase intervals is O(m+degree*M). Their phase tables can be accumulated
classically, one q^|X|-entry table per Hamiltonian term X. All correction phases
commute, so the quantum circuit needs only ONE known <=k-local diagonal gate per
term, not one gate per phase interval. The classical integral/accumulation cost
is O(q^k(m+degree*M)), plus ordinary finite synthesis and integration accuracy. This is an actual quantum operation on B_i, not a classical
simulation of amplitudes. It never measures x_i.

At deterministic collection time T the only ideal failure is an unreset site:

  Pr(failure)=1-(1-exp(-rT))^n <= n exp(-rT).

Flag failure and output any state, giving diamond error <=2 n exp(-rT). Thus
T=log(2n/epsilon)/r suffices at EVERY r>0, independently of interaction magnitude.
The receiver can alternatively wait until all first resets; the expected time
is H_n/r. Retained quantum payload memory is n qudits, plus collector workspaces.
Classical processing grows with all observed resets. Streaming phase-table
accumulation avoids retaining the full record; the final quantum phase gate
count is the number of Hamiltonian terms, with fixed-dimensional synthesis.

For finite actual continuous vacuum ports, use the SAME coherent-dilation
splitting/local-collector construction above, then the exact diagonal circuit
algorithm on the split record. The bin width pays chi and the desired accuracy.
No r>beta condition is needed, but large interaction strengths and small r can
increase collection-control and phase precision costs. This does not assert that
finite bandwidth can handle unbounded J/r at no cost.

8. COMPARISON, LIMITS, AND CONSEQUENCE

The acquired operation is the missing element: a known n-qudit initial state,
possibly maximally entangled with an external reference, is transferred using
only emitted outputs, protected receiver storage and a compiled inverse local
circuit. Generic N1732 applied globally has dense dependence on d=q^n. This
construction invokes it only at fixed local dimension and processes the global
payload by a polynomial-size quantum circuit. It establishes efficient recovery
for the stated source class rather than approximation of every environmental
observable or of the entire global canonical Stinespring channel in a compact
memory. Keeping all successful exports costs O(n log(n/epsilon)) on average
at fixed rates and positive gap.

Ordinary comparisons:
- If direct source-side SWAP controls are allowed, parallel SWAP transfer is
  simpler and faster. It has different access and can dominate this scheme.
- With the same reset/export ports and H=0, ordinary first-reset collection
  already gives coupon-collector logarithmic time. The theorem extends this
  structured quantum transfer to noncommuting interacting continuous dynamics.
- General full-bath transpose recovery and information-disturbance theorems are
  established, but their abstract existence does not compile an exponential
  density matrix or an arbitrary global environment operation efficiently.
- Rapid reduced-source relaxation alone is insufficient for the B-only decoder:
  discarded purifier A registers and final source can retain joint information.
  The operator-support proof is the extra recovery statement.
- The condition r>beta is sufficient and not a no-capacity threshold; the exact
  diagonal-H supplement demonstrates this explicitly.
- Hidden Z noise, lost ports, thermal unpurified baths and uncharged source access
  do not satisfy the premise. No earlier bare-source acquisition converse has
  been bypassed by relabelling its inaccessible noise as accessible reset output.
