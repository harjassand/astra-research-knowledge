### The proposed reusable object

A local constitutive operation would have the form

\[
\mathcal R_{\alpha|\beta}
:
\left(
T,\,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol\mu},\,
z_{\alpha|\beta}
\right)
\longmapsto
\left(
\mathbf J,\,
\text{phase-creation and dissolution rates},\,
\dot z_{\alpha|\beta}
\right),
\]

where \(z_{\alpha|\beta}\) is a physically specified interface state: composition, relevant geometry and whatever additional local structure proves necessary.

The important output is \(\dot z_{\alpha|\beta}\). A reaction changes the medium through which subsequent reactions proceed. A reusable law must therefore predict the consequences of its own operation for later reactions.

For a bulk assembly, local interfaces share material inventories and chemical-potential fields. Their laws cannot be treated as independent reaction scores. At minimum, assembly must preserve elemental balance and nonnegative phase amounts:

\[
\dot{\mathbf n}=S\mathbf r,
\qquad
AS=0,
\qquad
\mathbf n\ge 0,
\]

where \(A\) maps phase amounts to elemental totals.

These balance equations are established machinery. They do not supply the unknown constitutive physics. Likewise, a near-equilibrium Onsager relation supplies a disciplined starting point, while nucleation and strongly driven transformations may require additional physics.

**The possible invention is a sufficiently complete, measurable and reusable interface state, together with a composition rule that retains predictive accuracy as that state changes.**

There are two constraints that keep this from becoming vacuous:

1. The required state cannot simply be the complete microscopic trajectory of the new specimen. Its inputs must be practically measurable or calculable.
2. Calibration and computation must cost less, over the intended uses, than repeatedly solving the entire new synthesis problem experimentally.

If pairwise interfaces are insufficient, the evidence may require junction states, longer-range supply constraints or other physical context. That possibility remains open. Expanding the state is justified only when it produces successful transfer at an acceptable cost.

## 5. The large public collection is useful, but it does not prove the proposed closure

I also acquired and audited the **Precursor Genome** ledger. It contains **1,035 distinct unordered pairs of 46 precursors**, exactly all pair choices, together with 1,351 diffraction scans and 1,950 candidate refinements. The original release provides experimental metadata and links to the underlying measurements. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

The actual ledger makes several boundaries clear:

- **981** samples contain recorded actual temperature profiles.
- **54** lack hold temperature or time.
- The recorded holds include **750 one-hour treatments and 231 four-hour treatments**.
- There are **no duplicate unordered-pair entries**.
- Rescans and alternative refinements are not independent repeated syntheses.
- Different pairs received different temperatures.

This is valuable empirical coverage of pair outcomes. It is not a collection of 1,035 kinetic trajectories, a systematic geometry intervention, or a test of composing pair laws into higher-order mixtures.

That distinction matters for the selection. The data can support candidate inventories and endpoint challenges, but they cannot independently determine the proposed physical-time operator.

I also rejected an apparent anomaly in the transport data after examining its original uncertainty. A reconstructed mean Onsager matrix was indefinite. The archived coefficient uncertainties, however, were larger than the plotted error for the problematic term. An explicit physically admissible matrix lay within all the componentwise archived one-standard-deviation intervals. That is not a joint confidence statement, but it removes the basis for treating the mean-matrix inconsistency as evidence for new physics.

The selection therefore rests on an unmet constructive capability, not on dataset size or a dramatic-looking inconsistency.

## 6. What the complete result would earn

The prospective consequence is **reliable conversion of material specifications into physical preparation procedures**.

A successful construction would accept a target phase and quality requirements, then predict a procedure’s intermediate phases, useful endpoint and timing under stated preparation conditions. The same independently determined local knowledge would remain useful when assembled into a new recipe.

That would extend substantially beyond another fitted example in three ways.

**It would transfer interventions.** A changed ramp, hold, atmosphere or particle distribution would become a physical input whose effect is predicted, rather than a reason to refit an unexplained rate factor.

**It would transfer assemblies.** Local knowledge acquired in one setting would help construct a new precursor combination or chemical family. The central result would be demonstrated reuse, not an improved score on a random endpoint split.

**It would deliver material.** The endpoint would include accepted product quality and quantity. A diffraction score alone would not establish usable composition, microstructure or function.

This could make computational materials discovery more consequential by reducing the repeated experimental search needed to realize candidate materials. It could also make useful nonequilibrium endpoints more deliberately accessible. The present work does not quantify a global energy saving or demonstrate a new functional material.

The complete resource boundary must include:

| Resource | What must be counted |
|---|---|
| Precursors | Their production, purity, cost and embodied preparation effort |
| Powder preparation | Milling, drying, size/contact characterization and variability |
| Processing | Actual furnace energy, losses, atmosphere control, holds and cooling |
| Recovery | Separation, waste and delivered mass meeting specifications |
| Scientific inputs | Atomistic calculations, interface calibration, measurements and uncertainty |
| Reuse | Whether those acquisition costs are amortized across genuinely new procedures |

Even the microscopic input stage is substantial. The 2026 study’s stated nine-composition, five-temperature, five-replicate, 5 ns protocol corresponds arithmetically to **225 trajectories and 1.125 microseconds of aggregate molecular-dynamics time**, alongside specialized potential preparation. That is not a GPU-hour or monetary estimate, but it prevents treating the interface library as a free resource.

A full solution must earn its advantage against the complete cost of the best existing route.
