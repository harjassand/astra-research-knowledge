# Redox and electronic coupling in solid-state synthesis: prior-art challenge

## Decision

Adding electrons, holes, redox-dependent defects, or electrochemical potentials to a reaction-interphase transport model is not by itself a new scientific organizing principle. It is a standard extension of coupled irreversible thermodynamics, with Wagner oxidation theory and ambipolar transport as direct physical precedents. Oxygen-pressure-dependent spinel formation was experimentally investigated in 1959. Surface exchange as a separate rate-limiting boundary process was quantitatively incorporated and tested in mixed-conducting oxides by 1994.

The scope limitation of the 2026 kinetic-selectivity paper (DOI 10.1038/s41563-026-02596-5) therefore identifies an application regime it does not cover; it does not establish that the governing transport operation is undiscovered. A transferable planner that predicts absolute physical time and phase selection across redox-changing synthesis protocols could still be a consequential capability. This audit has not constructed or validated that capability. It should not be presented as an achieved discovery merely because the latest comparator explicitly excludes variable oxidation states.

## What the recent comparator already contains

The parent research branch acquired and read the full text of the 2026 Nature Materials study at [DOI 10.1038/s41563-026-02596-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41563-026-02596-5). Its local text is `opportunity_root/kinetic_selectivity.txt`. It uses full Onsager transport matrices in liquid-like product-interphase analogues and couples its kinetic selectivity score to ReactCA. Its published construction fixes a representative particle radius of 0.1 micrometre and uses a score scale of 5 × 10^13 to place onset above barium-carbonate decomposition. These are consequential modeling resources; a universal, calibrated physical clock is not thereby established. The selected Ba–Ti–O example keeps oxidation states fixed, enabling approximation of electrochemical potential by bulk chemical potential. That approximation is explicitly restricted in the source.

The strongest immediate extension is to retain electrochemical potentials and solve the induced electric field jointly with electronic and ionic transport. The next section shows why that operation itself is elementary constrained Onsager theory, rather than a new transport law.

## Exact elimination of the electric field under zero net current

Choose a consistent material reference frame. Let \(J\) be the vector of species fluxes, including mobile electronic carriers as needed, let \(z\) be their signed charge numbers, and let \(L=L^T\succeq0\) be the local isothermal phenomenological transport matrix. In an appropriate common convention,

\[
J=-L(\nabla\mu+Fz\nabla\phi).
\]

Here \(\mu\) contains chemical potentials and \(\phi\) is electric potential. Under the local zero-current condition \(z^T J=0\), if \(z^TLz>0\),

\[
F\nabla\phi=-\frac{z^TL\nabla\mu}{z^TLz}.
\]

Substitution gives

\[
J=-L_{\rm constrained}\nabla\mu,
\qquad
L_{\rm constrained}=L-\frac{Lzz^TL}{z^TLz}.
\]

This matrix is symmetric. It satisfies \(z^TL_{\rm constrained}=0\). For every vector \(x\), Cauchy–Schwarz in the semidefinite inner product generated by \(L\) gives

\[
x^TL_{\rm constrained}x=x^TLx-\frac{(x^TLz)^2}{z^TLz}\ge0.
\]

Thus eliminating the self-consistent electric field preserves nonnegative entropy production while restricting transport to electrically allowed fluxes. If \(z^TLz=0\), positive semidefiniteness implies \(Lz=0\), so the zero-current constraint is already satisfied and the division is unnecessary.

With several linear flux constraints \(AJ=0\), the corresponding compatible projection is the usual Schur complement

\[
L-LA^T(ALA^T)^\dagger AL,
\]

subject to the standard compatibility of the imposed constraints. This is an algebraic deduction supplied here for clarity, not a novelty claim. In the elementary model with separate ionic and electronic channels, the familiar effective conductivity factor becomes

\[
\sigma_{\rm amb}=\frac{\sigma_{\rm ion}\sigma_{\rm e}}{\sigma_{\rm ion}+\sigma_{\rm e}}.
\]

Stoichiometric charge factors and thermodynamic derivatives are still needed to translate this factor into an oxygen or product-formation rate. It is not itself a universal diffusivity. If one channel is much less conductive than the other, it limits the coupled neutral transport. This already supplies the proposed redox/electronic bottleneck mechanism.

The constraint used here assumes the applicable local electroneutral, zero-net-current regime. It does not cover arbitrary transient charge accumulation, space-charge layers, or externally imposed currents without modification. Those regimes require conservation equations, electrostatics, and boundary conditions. Adding those established ingredients does not resolve the predictive parameter problem automatically.

## Primary evidence establishing prior explanation

### Wagner's oxidation theory

Carl Wagner, *Beitrag zur Theorie des Anlaufvorgangs*, Zeitschrift für Physikalische Chemie 21B (1933), 25–41. [DOI 10.1515/zpch-1933-2105](https://doi.org/10.1515/zpch-1933-2105).

The original publication metadata and DOI were checked, but the original full article was not acquired. The mechanism is independently set out in the full 1994 primary paper below: chemically driven oxygen transport is mediated by simultaneous ionic and electronic motion. This audit therefore does not purport to have replicated or translated the full 1933 derivation.

### Direct precedent in solid-state spinel synthesis

Yoshio Iida, *Formation of NiAl2O4 Spinel by Solid Reaction*, Journal of the Japan Society of Powder and Powder Metallurgy 6(2) (1959), 55–68. [DOI 10.2497/jjspm.6.55](https://doi.org/10.2497/jjspm.6.55), [publisher abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspm1947/6/2/6_2_55/_article/-char/en).

The experiment considered starting-powder preparation, particle size, impurities, and atmosphere. It reported slower spinel formation at lower oxygen partial pressure and interpreted the dependence in connection with the spinel's electrical conductivity and activation energy. This is already a direct experimental link between electronic/defect transport, oxygen environment, and solid-state product formation. Only the primary publisher abstract was inspected; no raw kinetics curve was acquired or independently fitted. It is a prior-mechanism check, not an additional quantitative reproduction.

### Bulk transport and surface exchange were already separated and tested

H. J. M. Bouwmeester, H. Kruidhof, A. J. Burggraaf, *Importance of the surface exchange kinetics as rate limiting step in oxygen permeation through mixed-conducting oxides*, Solid State Ionics 72 (1994), 185–194. [DOI 10.1016/0167-2738(94)90145-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2738(94)90145-7), [primary PDF](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/6511980/Bouwmeester94importance.pdf).

The primary PDF was read through the web tool. A separate local download was denied, so there is no claimed local PDF copy. The paper derives chemically driven oxygen flux from concurrent anion/electron transport, then includes finite surface exchange. It identifies a crossover length separating bulk and surface control; in a stated limiting case this scales as diffusivity divided by surface-exchange coefficient. Oxygen-permeation results were compared with independent isotope-exchange measurements. This means that a new synthesis proposal must account for both mechanisms without treating their mere coexistence as a discovery.

## What would have to be achieved to exceed this baseline

The following are discriminating requirements, not a proposed completed project:

1. Predict phase identities, induction times, and phase fractions on an absolute time axis for genuinely held-out thermal schedules, atmosphere histories, and precursor geometries. A re-scaled simulation step or known-product filter does not supply that result.
2. Obtain the relevant transport matrices, defect/redox equilibria, and interface/surface-exchange rates from independently measured or calculated quantities. Explicitly price their simulation, characterization, and preparation costs. Re-fitting a separate clock for every reaction cannot support a universal capability claim.
3. Compare with an equally informed reaction-diffusion model using standard constrained Onsager/Wagner transport, phase equilibria, and surface kinetics, as well as the recent kinetic-selectivity/ReactCA method. The comparison must control both parameter information and experimental feedback. Beating a thermodynamic score alone does not beat the strongest applicable explanation.
4. Test whether a reduced representation actually transfers across protocols and chemistries. A convenient graph, new score, or abstract transport projection acquires scientific value only if it replaces an otherwise necessary family of measurements or permits a reproducible new construction.

The remaining issue is not whether redox can influence synthesis: it can, and established theory explains why. The hard unresolved operation would be a quantitatively transferable preparation-to-product calculation with a useful and complete cost model. No evidence acquired in this audit proves that adding electronic coupling alone provides that operation.

## Overall research choice

The dense-electrolyte branch was rejected after a concrete matched experimental comparison and a stronger primary prediction benchmark; see `electrolyte_opportunity_audit.md`. The redox synthesis extension is also rejected as a standalone claim to a new physical principle. It may be a necessary ingredient of the parent's physical-time synthesis planner, but its absence from one current paper should not be mistaken for a new foundational opening.
