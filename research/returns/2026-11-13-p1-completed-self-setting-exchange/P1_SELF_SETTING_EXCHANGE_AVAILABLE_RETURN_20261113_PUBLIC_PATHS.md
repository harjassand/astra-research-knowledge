1. The useful mechanism: establish contact after locking, then service failures from outside

The previous catch arrangement depended on establishing a very small open gap relative to a deforming workpiece. This candidate changes that operation.

While the original joint still holds the member, each opposed support pair advances until its compliant crowns touch the actual workpiece tracks. The crowns then move onto internal mechanical stops. The screws are positively locked, drive torque is removed, and every contact is checked again.

The proposed acceptance evidence for one jaw consists of three different observations:

A front force measurement: contact force is measured immediately behind the crown, ahead of the guide and internal stop.
An internal displacement comparison: the crown is compared with its actual captive stop.
Positive lock evidence: the screw dog is engaged, followed by another force and stop reading.

These observations address different ambiguities. A rear thrust sensor can register force because an internal guide has seized while the crown remains clear of the workpiece. Moving the force sensor ahead of that friction path removes that particular false indication. An internal stop reading alone can still be fooled by a damaged or contaminated datum. Neither observation is sufficient by itself.

Each jaw contains:

A 40 mm diameter crown with a 250 mm spherical radius.

What this changes—and what it leaves unresolved

The useful change is that the setting operation references an internal loaded datum after the actual locks have engaged. It avoids correcting a remote optical target for a calculated workpiece deformation.

It still requires:

Known crown and track condition over the actual contact region.
An installed front-force error bound.
Measured lock-induced movement.
A correct model of opposed-jaw preload.
Controlled behavior when one jaw unloads and contact opens.
Temperature and wear limits between setting and release.

The audit found that the present model does not yet establish all of these. In particular, it assigns shared structural compliance independently to opposed jaws, making their common preload loop artificially compliant. Consequently, the model’s successful setting window is not a demonstrated property of the manufactured pair.

A jammed support is removed as a whole cartridge

There are two independent withdrawal levels.

Fine-drive seizure. The healthy opposing jaw first withdraws. A separate macro axis moves the complete pair while the failed fine drive remains stationary.

Common guide or macro seizure. Two captive M12×1 keeper screws, positioned outside the failed mechanism, capture the entire jaw before its six external mounting bolts are removed. These keepers restrain outward load as well as apply separating force.

That distinction matters: ordinary push-off screws would not control a cartridge being driven outward by stored load.

The proposed operation is:

Engage both keeper bearings and establish measured capture.
Remove the external mounting preload incrementally.
Meter whole-cartridge motion with bounded skew.
Establish approximately 3 mm separation and check residual force.
Transfer only the cartridge’s mass to the gantry.
Withdraw the complete failed unit by 170 mm.

Each keeper is screened for the whole 12 kN load rather than assuming equal sharing. The proposed skew limit is 25 µm. Installed backlash, keeper engagement, load sharing and sudden release remain unqualified.

If a keeper itself is the single persistent fault, the inner fine and macro mechanisms remain available for unloading, followed by whole-pod quarantine. Two independent persistent mechanical jams are outside this recovery envelope.

A seized final foot has a separate physical route

The foot receiver uses an open-through T-slot and retaining pins. If normal withdrawal fails after the replacement carries the load:

A gripper first owns the unloaded pod.
A saw cuts its sole exposed attachment throat.
The moving stub shifts in one direction to clear the retained fragment.
The pod then withdraws through the open slot.

The selected geometry separates the retained fragment and moving stub into disjoint intervals. The old member-neck saw route was rejected because the enlarged jaw obstructed it.

Healthy exchanges consume no escape necks. The proposed final-foot escape consumes a neck, blade and capture liner.

However, the current campaign implements this operation as a timed cut followed by a state change. It does not resolve the complete cut, blade seizure, frame-strain release, fragment capture and withdrawal mechanics. Charging 2,000 seconds of saw time does not establish that operation.

Recovery of the recovery tool

The separate dock has its own spindle, indexed pallet, positive forks and capture pins. The robot’s root tool coupling sits inside an externally removable mount whose fasteners and media connections are accessible to the dock.

A seized inner coupling can therefore remain seized while the dock removes the complete coupling package.

The resulting worst-case handled package is 32.14 kg, requiring the selected 40 kg gantry. This correction is included in the mass and cost model.

Commercial tool changers provide useful retained-tool behavior, but retention during air loss is not equivalent to preserving position. ATI explicitly distinguishes fail-safe retention from positional accuracy after pressure loss. That limitation is retained here.

4. Power loss, final isolation and restoration
Power loss

Structural retention is intended to use positive mechanical dogs, rather than maintained motor torque or pneumatic pressure. Tool handling has a finite air inventory: a proposed 2 L receiver at 8 barg, regulated to 5.5 bar, with a specified leakage gate.

At 20 standard cm³/min leakage, a one-hour outage consumes 1.2 normal litres. The modeled usable pressure interval contains approximately 5 normal litres. Valve behavior, actual leakage and temperature still require measurement.

More seriously, the current campaign invokes outages only at a protected stage, assigns the locks engaged, and advances the clock by one hour. It does not establish:

Coast before dog engagement.
Asynchronous lock engagement.
An outage during keeper transfer.
One-hour thermal movement and creep.
All partly open support configurations.

Existing safety-clamp hardware illustrates why release travel and unloading cannot be ignored: SITEMA’s spring-base arrangement requires unloading and finite base motion. It does not supply a complete submillimetre six-axis catch.

Final absence of temporary paths

The candidate retains the large-clearance principle.

All temporary material must park beyond a complete observed corridor: shoes, keys, guides, gauge tongues, saw frames, grips, cables, hoses, ties and debris. The selected nominal parking clearance is 35 mm. With introduced allowances of 1 mm installed observation error and 2 mm possible closure, the remaining clearance would be approximately 32 mm; the selected acceptance threshold is 20 mm.

For opaque bodies, disjoint complete projections can provide sufficient separation evidence. That requires all potentially bridging material to be included and the observations to remain valid after the final wrench, tool and ownership changes.

The present implementation does not yet provide that evidence. Its observation is generated from a parking-state flag. Consequently, it cannot detect every cable, tool or foreign bridge that could remain.

Final absence is therefore an explicit failed acceptance gate.

Restoration uses finite replacement stock

The dock is intended to clean, compare, proof and either restore or quarantine a cartridge. It can install a verified spare while a damaged mechanism remains quarantined.

The proposed reference sequence compares travel at 0, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mm, reverses direction, remasters at the actual stop and performs scheduled force checks. A failed datum or force channel causes a complete-part replacement.

This does not claim autonomous repair of every seized thread. It is restoration of the working system using finite, paid stock.

The independent audit found decisive gaps

1. The setting model is too compliant in the wrong mode. Shared pair/frame compliance is independently applied to both jaws. This can create a favorable preload-setting window that the actual closed pair does not possess.

2. Some lock operations bypass the real mechanism. Certain setting and pin-dither actions directly change drive position or assign dog engagement. They do not pass through the complete screw torque, backlash and discrete-lock process.

3. Initial uncertainty is sampled but not fully propagated. The model starts with 1–1.5 mm and 2–3° acquisition uncertainty, then samples a favorable docking residual. It does not propagate that residual into every actual contact normal, lever arm and swept route.

4. False engagement is insufficiently represented. The injected false-seat case is an early insertion obstruction. It does not establish detection of a full-depth but unlocked replacement, missing key or partly engaged thread.

5. Some material transitions remain commands. Pin stiffness disappears at a withdrawal threshold; keeper capture and foot cutting are incomplete mechanical transitions; final isolation derives from parking flags.

6. The 500 episodes do not preserve continuous mechanical history. A separate ledger propagates serial duty and stock consumption, but each mechanical episode begins from a fresh model. It does not propagate accumulated wear, permanent set, debris or prior damage through 500 exchanges.

These are reasons the strict acceptance count is zero even before considering the load apparatus.
