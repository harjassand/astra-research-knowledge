STABILITY AND INFORMATION LIMITS OF EFFECTIVE RESPONSE
A complete leading-exponent theorem for the accepted periodic scalar class
7 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane (6 November UTC)

RESULT AND SCOPE
===============

The full question resolved here is the uniform information sensitivity of
effective response throughout the accepted exponentially controlled class:
does a small integral error in a material determine a small error in its
effective tensor, and what is the exact leading accuracy exponent?

For every dimension d>=2, fixed tau>0, and fixed M>exp(tau), define the class
C(tau,M) of arbitrary measurable, real symmetric positive-definite d by d
coefficients on the unit torus Q by

  K_A=max{1,||A||op,||A^(-1)||op},
  integral_Q exp(tau K_A)<=M.

Analytic statements allow real tau,M. The digital construction takes supplied
rational positive tau,M and rational target error, as in the accepted contract.
All coefficients and their inverses are measured in the same fixed units.
There is no pointwise renormalization, essential ellipticity gap, minimum
feature size, continuity, sparsity, or distribution assumption. This is a
scalar linear PDE with a matrix conductivity, not a coupled vector-valued
system. The period and the volume-one convention are part of the contract.

Let A_* be the accepted variationally homogenized tensor. Put

  omega(eta)=sup{||A_*-C_*||op:
                 A,C in C(tau,M), integral ||A-C||op<=eta}.

The main result is

  lim_(eta down to 0) log(omega(eta))/log(log(1/eta)) = -tau.       (R1)

More explicitly, for sufficiently small eta,

  c(tau,M)[log(1/eta)]^(-tau) <= omega(eta)
    <= C(tau,M,alpha)[log(e+1/eta)]^(-alpha)                     (R2)

for every 0<alpha<tau. The constants can be chosen independently of d.
The upper constants are computable from the stated bounds. The lower
bound comes from an explicit family of finite, individually uniformly
elliptic coefficients with one common exponential-moment budget.

Thus the exact leading logarithmic exponent is tau. No uniform Holder
law C eta^a, for any a>0, is possible. No logarithmic power greater than
tau is possible. This report does NOT assert the upper endpoint
omega(eta)=O(log(1/eta)^(-tau)); (R1) determines the exponent and leaves
possible subpower factors at that endpoint unspecified.

The central operation is a geometric interpolation of positive matrices.
It turns changes in the coefficient into logarithmic relative changes
weighted by the actual energy density. The accepted concentration theorem
then controls these changes uniformly. The second operation, a radial
change of coordinates, constructs the matching exponent obstruction.

The resulting information theorem applies to all algorithms receiving
only a specified uncertain coefficient record. It does not give a runtime
lower bound against every explicitly known material formula. A finite
coarse-material construction and its costs are supplied below, so neither
the positive bound nor its use hides an averaging, inverse, or PDE oracle.

Another full negative conclusion follows from the same family: even
complete passive boundary response does not determine the response after
a prescribed localized additive material intervention in this anisotropic
scalar class. This is an explicit consequence of the classical conductivity
coordinate gauge, credited below.

These are mathematical deductions, not a historical-priority claim or a
claim of demonstrated civilisation-scale capability. The earlier scalar
regularity, existence, density, homogenization, Fourier and certificate
results retain their exact scopes. Unrestricted exact QUBO and P versus NP
remain unresolved.

1. ACCEPTED CAPITAL AND PRECISE OUTPUT
=====================================

For a prescribed constant e in R^d, write

  m_A(e)=e.A_*e
        =min integral (e+grad u).A(e+grad u).

The minimum is over periodic distributional gradients with finite A-energy.
The accepted construction supplies the unique minimizing field E_A^e,
the divergence-free flux B_A^e=A E_A^e, smooth density in the actual primal
and dual energy spaces, and

  m_A(e)=sup_(div B=0) [2 e.mean(B)-integral B.A^(-1)B].          (1.1)

Its primal-dual certificate algorithm computes arbitrarily accurate
enclosures from a valid coefficient name. It already constructs the
inverse data with its full cost. That algorithm is used, not replaced
by a black-box oracle.

Set mu=M/tau. The variational bounds give

  mu^(-1) I <= A_* <= mu I.                                    (1.2)

The accepted sharp logarithmic theorem and the global periodic calibration
give the following quantitative specialization for unit e:

  ||J_A^e||_(Phi_beta) <= C_E(tau,M,beta),  0<beta<tau,
  J_A^e=E_A^e.A E_A^e.                                        (1.3)

Here the Luxemburg norm uses the complementary Young functions

  Phi_beta(s)=integral_0^s [log(1+r)]^beta dr,
  Psi_beta(t)=integral_0^t [exp(r^(1/beta))-1] dr.               (1.4)

Consequently integral fg<=2||f||_(Phi_beta)||g||_(Psi_beta).
Appendix A gives an explicit, dimension-independent choice of C_E by a
global specialization of the already accepted damped calibration. No
local Sobolev or unspecified geometric constants enter that choice.

The requested new output is a tensor error bound, a uniform precision law,
and a finite way to use that law. No representation of the entire energy
landscape or microscopic solution is required to state or apply the bound.

2. THE LOGARITHMIC MATRIX DISTANCE
=================================

For two positive-definite matrices define

  rho(A,C)=||log(A^(-1/2) C A^(-1/2))||op.                       (2.1)

This is the Thompson distance, equivalently the least r>=0 for which

  exp(-r) A <= C <= exp(r) A.                                 (2.2)

The pointwise distance is symmetric and obeys the triangle inequality.
Monotonicity and the triangle inequality of the Luxemburg norm therefore
make ||rho(A,C)||_(Psi_beta) a genuine metric on coefficients modulo
almost-everywhere equality. It is finite throughout the moment class.
It remains valid when the matrices do not commute. In particular, the
difference log C-log A is not substituted for the matrix logarithm in
(2.1). The underlying positive-matrix mean and multiplicative metric are
classical; see Ando-Li-Mathias [4].

Define pointwise

  A_t=A^(1/2) (A^(-1/2) C A^(-1/2))^t A^(1/2),  0<=t<=1.      (2.3)

Let S=A^(-1/2) C A^(-1/2). The Loewner-Heinz inequality, applied to
K_C^(-1) A^(-1)<=S<=K_C A^(-1), gives

  K_(A_t) <= K_A^(1-t) K_C^t
           <= (1-t)K_A+tK_C.

Therefore

  integral exp(tau K_(A_t))<=M                               (2.4)

when both endpoints are in C(tau,M). The full interpolation remains in
the same fixed-unit moment class. This is the step that avoids paying
an uncontrolled ratio of the endpoint conductivities.

For every real vector z,

  |z.(dA_t/dt)z| <= rho(A,C) z.A_t z,                         (2.5)

because S^t commutes with log S and
-||log S|| S^t <= S^t log S <= ||log S|| S^t.

THEOREM 1 (geometric response stability).
For every 0<beta<tau,

  ||A_*-C_*||op <=
      2 C_E(tau,M,beta) ||rho(A,C)||_(Psi_beta).               (2.6)

The coefficient distance on the right is an integral Orlicz distance;
it need not be essentially bounded.

Proof first for bounded, uniformly elliptic endpoints. The minimizer
along (2.3) depends continuously on t in H^1. Comparing neighboring
minimizers, or differentiating the strictly convex variational problem,
gives

  d m_(A_t)(e)/dt=integral E_t.(dA_t/dt)E_t.

The derivative of the field contributes zero by its Euler equation.
Equations (2.5), (1.3), and Orlicz Holder give

  |d m_(A_t)(e)/dt|
     <=integral rho(A,C) J_t
     <=2 C_E ||rho(A,C)||_(Psi_beta)

for every unit e. Integrating in t and taking the supremum of the absolute
quadratic form proves (2.6). The path is an analytic proof device; the
algorithm never evaluates a continuum of intermediate cell solutions.

For general endpoints, clamp every eigenvalue to [1/L,L], L>=1, to obtain
A_L,C_L. The moments do not increase. Both coefficients and their inverses
converge in L^1 to the originals, by domination by their integrable K's.
Equation (1.1) and the accepted primal and dual smooth density imply

  (A_L)_* -> A_*,  (C_L)_* -> C_*.

Indeed every fixed bounded smooth primal trial gives the limsup inequality,
and every bounded smooth dual trial gives the liminf inequality. Density
then identifies the two limits.

Put K0=max(K_A,K_C). Pointwise convergence also gives rho(A_L,C_L)->rho(A,C),
and each of these distances is bounded by 2 log K0. Moreover

  integral exp(tau K0)<=2M.

For every finite p,c>0, exp(c(log K0)^p) is integrable, since its growth is
eventually slower than exp(tau K0). Thus 2 log K0 belongs to the Orlicz
heart of every Psi_beta. Dominated convergence at every positive Luxemburg
scale proves norm convergence of the distances. Passing to the limit proves
(2.6) for the full class. This argument uses no unproved differentiation
claim in a changing degenerate energy space.

A useful limiting comparison is immediate: if rho(A,C)<=r everywhere,
then exp(-r)A_*<=C_*<=exp(r)A_*. Equation (2.6) admits unbounded, spatially
distributed relative errors by using the concentration estimate.

3. FROM ABSOLUTE COEFFICIENT ERROR TO RELATIVE ERROR
==================================================

Let delta(x)=||A(x)-C(x)||op and eta=integral delta. Pointwise order gives

  rho(A,C) <= log(1+K0 delta),    rho(A,C)<=2 log K0.            (3.1)

For example C<=A+delta I<=(1+K_A delta)A; the reverse comparison supplies
the other side of (2.2).

Here is an explicit Orlicz estimate. For beta>0 put

  D_beta=4*8^beta.

For L=log(1/eta) sufficiently large depending only on beta,tau,M,

  ||rho(A,C)||_(Psi_beta)
       <= D_beta log L / L^beta.                             (3.2)

Proof. Put R=L/(2tau) and a=D_beta log L/L^beta. The good set is

  G={K0<=R, delta<=exp(-L/2)}.

On G, rho<=R exp(-L/2). Its complement S satisfies

  |S| <= (1+2M) exp(-L/2).                                   (3.3)

On S intersect {K0<=L^2}, rho<=4 log L. Hence z=rho/a obeys

  z<= (L/8)^beta,   z^(1/beta)<=L/8.

The elementary bound Psi_beta(z)<=z exp(z^(1/beta)) yields a contribution
at most (1+2M)L^beta exp(-3L/8).

For K0>L^2, assume 2 log L>=1/beta. The function
(log K)^(1/beta)/K is then decreasing for K>=L^2. It follows that

  (rho/a)^(1/beta) / K0 <= 1/(8L).

If L>=(beta+1)/(4tau), then
(beta+1)(rho/a)^(1/beta)<=tau K0/2. Since
z exp(z^(1/beta))<=exp((beta+1)z^(1/beta)), this tail contributes at most

  integral_(K0>L^2) exp(tau K0/2)
    <=2M exp(-tau L^2/2).                                    (3.4)

Finally, on G,
z<=L^(beta+1)exp(-L/2)/(2tau D_beta log L), so this contribution tends to
zero. The three parts have total Luxemburg modular at most one for
large enough L, proving (3.2).

An explicit effective threshold L0 is obtained as follows. Start with a
dyadic integer strictly larger than

  max{e, 2(beta+1), 8beta/3, exp(1/(2beta)),
       1/(2tau), (beta+1)/(4tau)}.

Double it until each of these strictly verified inequalities holds:

  L0^(beta+1) exp(-L0/2)/(2tau D_beta) < 1/(4e),
  (1+2M)L0^beta exp(-3L0/8) < 1/4,
  2M exp(-tau L0^2/2) < 1/4.                                 (3.5)

They remain true for larger L by the starting thresholds. Interval
evaluation of elementary functions verifies each strict test; the loop
terminates. Thus no unknown asymptotic constant is being used as an oracle.

THEOREM 2 (uniform L^1 response law).
For every 0<alpha<tau,

  ||A_*-C_*||op <=
    C_alpha [log(e+1/||A-C||_1)]^(-alpha),                    (3.6)

with the right side interpreted as zero when A=C almost everywhere.

One explicit choice takes beta=(alpha+tau)/2, L0 from (3.5), and

  C_alpha=max{
     2mu(L0+1)^alpha,
     2^(alpha+1) C_E(tau,M,beta) D_beta/[e(beta-alpha)]
  }.                                                        (3.7)

For eta<=exp(-L0), combine (2.6), (3.2), and
log L/L^(beta-alpha)<=1/[e(beta-alpha)].
Also log(e+1/eta)<=2L. For larger eta use
||A_*-C_*||<=2mu and log(e+1/eta)<=L0+1.
This proves (3.6) on the whole range.

4. EXPLICIT FAMILY AT THE CRITICAL LOGARITHMIC EXPONENT
======================================================

Fix tau>0 and M>exp(tau). Choose an integer T0>=max{4,2tau} and a dyadic
R in (0,1/4] small enough that

  exp(tau)+pi R^2(18/T0+1) < M.                               (4.1)

This is an effective selection for rational tau,M with the strict slack.
Work in a disk centered at (1/2,1/2) in the two-dimensional unit torus.
Write r=|x-center|, t=log(R/r), and r0=R exp(-T0).

For t>=T0 define

  h(t)=(tau/2)[1/t+(log t)/t^2],
  K(t)=1/h(t)=2t^2/[tau(t+log t)],                            (4.2)

  rho(t)=r0 (t/T0)^(-tau/2)
          exp{(tau/2)[(log t+1)/t-(log T0+1)/T0]}.            (4.3)

Here 0<h<1 and d log rho/dt=-h. For T>=T0 put r_T=R exp(-T).
Define f_T to be identity outside r0, the radial map with image radius
rho(log(R/r)) on r_T<r<r0, and the linear dilation rho(T)/r_T inside
the core r<=r_T. Extend it periodically. Each f_T is a finite bi-Lipschitz
homeomorphism, although its constants are not uniform in T.

Define A_T^(1) by I outside the annulus and inside the core, and by

  A_T^(1)=K(t) e_r tensor e_r+K(t)^(-1) e_theta tensor e_theta

on the annulus. Define A_T^(2) by changing only the core to 2I.

These are explicit measurable, individually uniformly elliptic
conductivities. Their coefficients differ by precisely I on the core:

  eta_T=||A_T^(2)-A_T^(1)||_1=pi R^2 exp(-2T).                 (4.4)

UNIFORM MOMENT BUDGET. The exact identity

  tau K(t)-2t
    =-2 log t+2(log t)^2/(t+log t)

and (log t)^2/t<=4/e^2 imply

  exp(tau K(t)-2t)<=9/t^2.

The annular moment is at most 18pi R^2/T0. The outside region contributes
at most exp(tau), and the core at most
pi R^2 exp(-2T0+2tau)<=pi R^2. Thus (4.1) puts BOTH families inside
the same C(tau,M), uniformly in T. No coefficient normalization is used.

EXACT EFFECTIVE RESPONSE. The classical change-of-variables law is

  A_f(x)=det(Df(x)) Df(x)^(-1) sigma(f(x)) Df(x)^(-T).         (4.5)

The transformation identity and boundary-fixing gauge are credited to [5].
We use only nonsingular, finite bi-Lipschitz maps in each member of the
family; a singular ideal cloak is not a premise.

If U(x)=V(f_T(x)), change of variables gives

  integral grad U.A_f grad U
    =integral grad V.sigma grad V.

Because f_T(x)-x is periodic, composition maps affine-plus-periodic
potentials with mean gradient e bijectively onto themselves. Thus effective
tensors are invariant under this pullback.

On the annulus Df_T has radial and tangential eigenvalues h rho/r and
rho/r. Applying (4.5) to sigma=I gives exactly A_T^(1). In two dimensions
a scalar dilation pulls a scalar conductivity back to the same scalar
conductivity. Therefore

  (A_T^(1))_*=I.

The second material is the pullback of sigma_T=2I on a disk of image
radius rho(T), and I elsewhere. Its image volume is

  v_T=pi R^2 exp(-2T0) T0^tau T^(-tau)
      exp{tau[(log T+1)/T-(log T0+1)/T0]}.                    (4.6)

The elementary arithmetic and harmonic variational bounds give

  [1/(1-v_T/2)] I <= (A_T^(2))_* <= (1+v_T)I.                 (4.7)

In particular

  ||(A_T^(2))_*-(A_T^(1))_*||op
      >=v_T/(2-v_T)>=v_T/2>=c0 T^(-tau),                    (4.8)

where

  c0=(pi/2)R^2 exp(-2T0) T0^tau
      exp[-tau(log T0+1)/T0] >0.

For d>2 use diag(A_T^(i)(x1,x2), I_(d-2)), independent of the remaining
coordinates. Averaging trial potentials over those coordinates shows that
the effective tensor has the identical block form. The moment and L^1
distance are unchanged. We do not apply a two-dimensional determinant
formula incorrectly as a d-dimensional pullback.

Equations (4.4) and (4.8) prove the lower bound in (R2) for every small
eta: choose the real T satisfying eta=pi R^2 exp(-2T). The bounds also
hold along an integer sequence of T's, which suffices for all impossibility
and accuracy-exponent conclusions.

They rule out every uniform Holder estimate and every logarithmic power
greater than tau. Combined with (3.6), they give (R1). If M=exp(tau), the
class consists only of A=I almost everywhere and omega is zero; if
M<exp(tau), the class is empty. The nontrivial conclusion explicitly
requires M>exp(tau).

5. INFORMATION PRECISION: ALL ALGORITHMS UNDER A STATED RECORD
=============================================================

Consider a record consisting of a known represented coefficient B, a
radius eta, and the promise

  A in C(tau,M), ||A-B||_1<=eta,

where the true A is otherwise unspecified. Such a record is different from
a Cauchy name permitting arbitrarily accurate queries of the true A.
Computation may use the record for arbitrarily long; it is not permitted to
invent additional measurements of A.

Take B_T=(A_T^(1)+A_T^(2))/2. It equals 3I/2 in the core and the common
coefficient elsewhere. Convexity of K followed by convexity of exp shows
that B_T has the same moment bound. Both possible truths A_T^(1), A_T^(2)
are consistent with the identical record and radius eta_T/2.

Their responses in the e1 loading differ by at least v_T/2. Every
deterministic answer therefore has error at least v_T/4 on one admissible
truth. The same lower bound holds for worst-case expected absolute error
of randomized answers by the triangle inequality. For error tolerance
smaller than half the response separation, no randomized procedure can
have success probability greater than one half for both truths.

This obstruction holds with unlimited internal computation. It concerns
a pair of consistent physical coefficients, not failure of one solver,
relaxation, representation, or training procedure.

The center itself has an explicit computable name: for finite T its formula
is piecewise elementary, uniformly bounded and elliptic, and its circular
interfaces have explicitly controlled measure. Subdivide rational dyadic
boxes; bound interface boxes by their area times a known coefficient bound;
use interval evaluation on other boxes. Refining and tightening intervals
gives a valid L^1 name. Every subdivision and evaluation is paid work.
A rational step center with a slightly enlarged radius gives the same
exponent, if a finite table is required.

Conversely, if the center B is itself in a fixed moment class, the accepted
algorithm can compute B_* to any specified tolerance. Theorem 2 bounds the
additional uncertainty in A_*. Thus the upper law is computationally
attainable from a valid represented center, with the coefficient-name and
solver costs retained.

If eta=2^(-b), worst-case response uncertainty has exact leading order

  b^(-tau+o(1)).                                               (5.1)

To guarantee response error epsilon, the required coefficient fidelity b
has leading exponent

  b=epsilon^(-1/tau+o(1)).

Writing epsilon=2^(-p), this becomes

  b=2^(p/tau+o(p)).                                           (5.2)

Precisely, the lower family requires b>=c epsilon^(-1/tau).
For every alpha<tau, a valid-center upper procedure uses
b<=C_alpha' epsilon^(-1/alpha) for sufficiently small epsilon.
These two statements imply the exponent in (5.2); no endpoint constant
has been smuggled into the inversion.

A single uniform precision selector can attain this leading exponent:
at requested accuracy 2^(-p), evaluate the proved sufficient bounds for
alpha_k=tau k/(k+1), 1<=k<=p, and choose the smallest sufficient fidelity.
Every fixed k is eventually included, so the limsup of log_2(b)/p is at
most 1/alpha_k for every k, hence at most 1/tau. The record lower bound
gives the matching liminf. All parameter evaluations are charged; this
selector is not asserted to be computationally efficient.

Here b counts absolute-accuracy digits, NOT necessarily bits in a succinct
formula. An analytic formula can encode fine structure compactly, and
knowledge of which formula is the true coefficient removes the ambiguity.
Only an access convention that charges at least b operations for a
b-digit request or explicit b-bit mantissa turns this precision requirement
into an exponential-in-p operation bound. This report proves no universal
runtime lower bound for the earlier represented-input solver or for all
succinct coefficient programs.

6. A FINITE COARSE MATERIAL, WITH NO AVERAGING OR INVERSE ORACLE
==============================================================

The positive response law can be used from the accepted coefficient-name
contract. A finite program returns, on an integer precision request m,
a fully expanded rational uniform-dyadic step table S_m with

  ||A-S_m||_1<=2^(-m).

The program, d, every grid cell, all coefficient bits, its running time,
and the validity of its error and moment promises remain part of the input.

Fix eta=2^(-m). Symmetrize S_m. Let its grid partition be P, with N cells
of common volume v=1/N. Denote the mathematical cell means of A by Abar_j.
These means guide the proof but are NOT queried as an oracle.

Since inverse is operator convex, K is convex on positive matrices.
Consequently exp(tau K) is convex and

  integral exp(tau K_Abar)<=M,
  K_Abar_j <= (1/tau)log(M/v),
  ||A-Abar||_1 <= 2eta.                                      (6.1)

The last inequality follows by comparing both A and its conditional mean
with S_m, which is constant on P.

Compute a rational upper bound

  R>=max{1,(1/tau)log(M/v)}.

Choose a positive dyadic delta so small that

  delta<=min{eta, 1/(2R), 1/[8tau R^2 exp(2tau R)]}.           (6.2)

Use outward elementary-function bounds in this choice. Request S_s with

  2^(-s)<=v delta.

On each cell of P, compute the exact rational mean of S_s, using a common
dyadic refinement. Call the resulting rational step matrix B_j. Then

  ||B_j-Abar_j||op<=delta.

Thus B is positive definite, with

  (2R)^(-1)I<=B<=2RI,   ||A-B||_1<=3eta.                       (6.3)

The inverse identity bounds
||B^(-1)-Abar^(-1)||<=2R^2 delta; in particular
|K_B-K_Abar|<=4R^2 delta. Both K's are at most 2R.
The mean-value theorem and (6.2) therefore give

  integral exp(tau K_B) <= M+1/2 <= M+1.                     (6.4)

We have constructed an explicitly finite, uniformly elliptic rational
material close to A, while retaining a uniform moment budget. Its exact
pointwise inverse is computable by rational elimination; the earlier
inverse-name procedure is also available. No true cell mean of A and no
inverse oracle was used.

To compute A_* with operator error at most epsilon, use C_alpha from
Theorem 2 with moment budget M+1, choose m with

  m log 2-log 3 >= (2C_alpha/epsilon)^(1/alpha),               (6.5)

construct B as above, and run the accepted finite certificate algorithm
on B to operator tolerance epsilon/2. Its rational output H satisfies

  ||H-A_*||op<=epsilon.

The tensor enclosure H-epsilon I<=A_*<=H+epsilon I is then valid.
The construction is uniform and halts on every valid input name.
It introduces a priori coefficient fidelity into the earlier complete
certificate procedure, not a claim of uniform efficiency.

FULL COST ACCOUNT. Let T_A(q) be the actual running time of the supplied
name at q. If the two returned grids have levels h,h_s, their common
expanded grid has N_f=2^(d max(h,h_s)) cells. Let B_in bound numerator and
denominator bit lengths in the supplied tables. A conservative mean-entry
bit bound is

  Z=O(N_f[B_in+d max(h,h_s)+1]).

With schoolbook exact rational arithmetic, symmetrization and all cell
averages cost O(d^2 N_f Z^2), in addition to reading the tables. Element-
function interval computations for the constants, R, delta, m and s
are charged by their actual finite running time T_parameters. These are
finite rational algorithms: reduce logarithms to 2 atanh(z) with |z|<=1/3
and use a geometric tail; reduce exponentials by halving to |x|<=1/2,
use Taylor bounds, then square with outward rounding; rational powers use
integer powers and root bisection. Every iteration, integer exponent and
intermediate bit length is counted. If W_i is the expanded data length
at parameter step i plus its loop counters by value, a deliberately loose
bit bound is T_parameters<=C sum_i(1+W_i)^16. Strict verified slack in the
choices, or a larger outward bound, avoids any equality-decision oracle.
The complete wrapper cost is bounded by

  T_parameters+T_A(m)+T_A(s)+O(d^2 N_f Z^2)+T_cert(B,epsilon/2). (6.6)

For clarity, T_cert is the existing explicit finite algorithm, not an
operation assumed to cost one unit. Its already accepted conservative
bound is the sum, over each required cell solve and all stages n through
the discovered stopping stage N_*, of

  T_B(n)+T_B(s_n)+(1+Z_n)^16.                                 (6.7)

Here Z_n is the total expanded binary length of the tables, filtered
inverse data, common simplex mesh, basis arrays and scalars at that stage,
plus the loop counters by value. The common mesh has
d! 2^(d ell_n) simplices; primal and dual dimensions and exact matrix
elimination are included. For finite B, T_B includes copying its explicit
table. Computing the whole tensor requires the previously specified
finite collection of cell solves with their error allocations.

There is no uniform polynomial bound on these expanded sizes, parameter
values, source runtimes or stopping stages in a succinct program length.
The current proof adds no such bound. A dimension-independent analytic
constant does not remove the dimension dependence of grid expansion.

Conditional means alone also do not provide a spatial approximation rate.
The known residual ||A-S_m||_1 is indispensable. A coarse image with no
certified residual does not satisfy this input contract.

7. EFFECTIVE RESPONSE IS NOT CLOSED UNDER LOCAL INTERVENTIONS
=============================================================

The family in Section 4 proves more than bad conditioning.

Let the two baseline materials be the uniform coefficient I and A_T^(1).
Both have effective tensor I. Since f_T is identity near the cell boundary,
their complete Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators on the square are also
identical, by (4.5). Thus even ideal, complete passive boundary measurements
cannot distinguish these two baselines.

Apply the SAME specified physical intervention to either baseline:

  add I on the disk r<r_T, and add zero elsewhere.

Both baselines already equal I there. For the uniform baseline, the
post-intervention coefficient is 1+chi_core, so its effective tensor is
at most (1+eta_T)I. For the transformed baseline it is A_T^(2), whose
effective tensor is at least (1-v_T/2)^(-1)I.

Their post-intervention responses are separated by at least

  v_T/(2-v_T)-eta_T,

which is positive for all sufficiently large T and is comparable to
T^(-tau). Meanwhile eta_T is exponentially small in T.

It follows that no operation using only the passive effective tensor,
or even the complete passive boundary operator, together with the
coordinate-labelled intervention can in general predict the post-change
response uniquely in this class. Unlimited computation cannot repair
identical input data. Additional structure that resolves the coordinate
gauge, or actual intervention information, is needed.

This is an organizing limitation on using a passive constitutive law as
a complete state for reconfiguration. It is not an impossibility theorem
for controlled intervention experiments, richer input representations,
or a known full coefficient. Conductivity transformation invariance has
longstanding primary precedent [5]; the explicit moment-controlled
amplification law here is derived in Section 4.

8. MACROSCOPIC CONSEQUENCES AND LIMITS
=====================================

The error bounds control all constant loadings simultaneously:

  |m_A(e)-m_C(e)|<=||A_*-C_*||op |e|^2.

They also control the effective resistivity:

  ||A_*^(-1)-C_*^(-1)||op<=mu^2 ||A_*-C_*||op.

For homogeneous Dirichlet macroscopic problems with the same forcing
f in H^(-1)(Omega), where that norm is dual to ||grad(.)||_2, let u,v solve
the constant-tensor equations with A_*,C_* respectively. Testing the
difference equation gives

  ||grad(u-v)||_2
    <=mu^2 ||A_*-C_*||op ||f||_(H^(-1)).                      (8.1)

Thus the coefficient-fidelity theorem propagates to macroscopic fields,
dissipation, and effective inverse laws. It is not merely an estimate
for a particular scalar entry of the tensor.

The upper law is uniform over all microstructure scales in the cell;
no geometric resolution scale was assumed. The lower family shows why
average coefficient accuracy is nevertheless an intrinsically weak
resource in a strongly heterogeneous medium.

These results do not compare microscopic solutions in a common unweighted
energy norm, supply a uniform finite-period homogenization rate, remove
the valid-name promises, handle arbitrary nonperiodic media or nonlinear
systems, or establish polynomial bit complexity in succinct data.
The earlier existence, uniqueness and homogenization conclusions remain
those of the accepted symmetric scalar periodic class.

APPENDIX A. COMPUTABLE GLOBAL CONCENTRATION CONSTANT
===================================================

This is a quantitative specialization of the accepted damped logarithmic
calibration, not a new assumption or a recurring proof of its primary
div-curl machinery.

Let |e|=1, mu=M/tau, a0=1+mu. Divide both E and B by a0, and put
j=J/a0^2 and r=R_field/a0, where
R_field=(|E+B|+|E-B|)/2. Then

  r^2<=Kj, j<=r^2, integral j<=1/4,

and the corresponding mean pair has R_field/a0<=1. The accepted global
periodic L_t inequality therefore has mean value integral j for every
t>=e. For the damped weights of the predecessor, it gives

  integral [j A_eps(r)-B_eps(r)]<=0.

No localization errors occur. Set

  gamma=(beta+tau)/2,
  theta=(gamma-beta)/(8gamma),
  delta0=theta/[16(1+|beta-1|)],

  ell0=max{2,(4/theta)^(1/beta),4max(beta,1)/theta,
               (4/delta0)log[64/(theta delta0)]},
  R0=exp(ell0),

  a=(1-theta)/beta, b=(1+theta)/2, c=a-2b/gamma>0,
  C0=R0^2 ell0^beta, v=max(beta-1,0),

  H=1 if v=0, otherwise
    max{1,[gamma v/(2e(tau-gamma))]^v}.

The previously proved scalar A_eps,B_eps bounds yield, on r>=R0,

  a X_eps<=b Y_eps+C0,
  Y_eps<=(2/gamma)X_eps+HM.

The latter splits at K=(2/gamma)log r. On its complement,
r^2 log^(beta-1)r<=H exp(tau K). Thus

  integral_(r>=R0) j log^beta r <= (C0+bHM)/c.

Using log(e+j)<=3log r on r>=R0 and integral j<=1/4 elsewhere, put

  C_J=3^beta(C0+bHM)/c
      +(1/4)[log(e+R0^2)]^beta,
  C_E=a0^2 max{1,C_J}.                                       (A1)

Then integral Phi_beta(j)<=C_J, and convex scaling proves (1.3).
All constants are dimension independent. Elementary interval algorithms
can replace every real constant in algorithmic uses by a rational upper
bound and c by a positive rational lower bound. Strict slack ensures
termination. The constants are very large and are not presented as
practical engineering prefactors.
