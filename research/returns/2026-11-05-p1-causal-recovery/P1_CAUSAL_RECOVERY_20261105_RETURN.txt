\frac12\operatorname{Tr}_S

Use normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norms and define
\[
\gamma_n
=
1-\left\|T_n\big|_{\mathbf1^\perp}\right\|_{2\to2}^{\,2}.

For

### Translation into physical memory

Suppose the receiver attains error \(\epsilon\), and every classical branch at the prefix has quantum dimension at most \(m\). Define
\[
e_\epsilon=2\epsilon-\frac43\epsilon^2.

The general receiver theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
\frac{m}{2^n}\ge1-\frac{e_\epsilon}{g_n}.

Whenever \(e_\epsilon<g_n\), its retained-qubit requirement therefore satisfies
\[
\boxed{
q_n\ge
n+\log_2\!\left(1-\frac{e_\epsilon}{g_n}\right).
}
\]

These formulas expose the dependence on angle, prefix length and error. They do not assume a particular receiver architecture or a bounded number of classical outcomes.

\epsilon\ge \frac{g_{q+1}}4.

Revealing the refinement only helps the receiver. Set
\[
A_x=\sqrt{K_x^\dagger K_x}.
\]
Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}A_x\le m,
\qquad
\sum_xA_x^2=I.
\]

This representation includes measurements, pure ancillas, discarded work registers and arbitrary classical histories.

### Step 2: turn successful recovery into a constrained quantum state

Let \(D=2^n\), and form the normalized Choi witness
\[
Z=\frac1D\sum_x
|A_x\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle A_x|.
\]

It obeys
\[
\operatorname{Tr}Z=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}_1Z=\operatorname{Tr}_2Z=I/D,
\qquad
0\le Z\le\frac mD I.
\]

The last inequality is the memory constraint. It remains valid with arbitrarily many classical outcomes.

For
\[
H_n=I-T_nT_n^*,
\]
the logical qubit’s Petz recovery has the exact identity
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(H_nZ)=1-F_{\mathrm P}.
\]

The sharp qubit recovery inequality is
\[
F_{\mathrm P}
\ge
F^2+\frac{(1-F)^2}{3},
\]
where \(F\) is the entanglement fidelity of any recovery. Half-diamond error at most \(\epsilon\) gives \(F\ge1-\epsilon\), hence
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(H_nZ)
\le
2\epsilon-\frac43\epsilon^2.
\]
The recovery inequality itself is established machinery due to Renes; the proof package also derives it directly to check its applicability here. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

This sharpens the earlier Astra spectral-profile coefficient \(3\epsilon(1-\epsilon)\) throughout the small-error range under consideration.

The intuitive consequence is useful. The identity-operator vector is maximally entangled across the bath’s input/output split. A low-memory witness cannot place more than \(m/D\) of its weight there. If every other direction has a positive loss \(\gamma_n\), very accurate recovery forces \(m\) close to \(D\).

### Step 3: evaluate the previously missing singular defect

The collision circuit can be written using the rational monodromy
\[
M_n(z)
=
(zI+iP_{S,n})\cdots(zI+iP_{S,1}),
\]
where \(P_{S,j}\) swaps the source and carrier \(j\).

Define
\[
\mathscr C(A)
=
(\sigma_y\otimes I)A^{t_S}(\sigma_y\otimes I).
\]
The local swap identity gives
\[
\boxed{
\mathscr C[M_n(z-i)]
=
(z^2+1)^nM_n(z)^{-1}.
}
\]

This is a mathematical crossing identity. Neither partial transpose nor inverse filtering is being offered as a physical receiver operation.

Now take a unit-norm traceless bath operator \(X\), put \(Y=T_n(X)\), and let
\[
\delta^2=1-\|T_n(X)\|_2^2.
\]
Small \(\delta\) means the circuit almost carries a bath-only operator to another bath-only operator. It produces a small *intertwining defect*
\[
E(z)
=
(I\otimes Y)M_n(z)-M_n(z)(I\otimes X).
\]

A critical point is that \(X\) and \(Y\) need not initially be equal. Treating the condition as simple commutation would leave a gap in the proof. Crossing alternates their roles; two shifts return to the original pair.

The norm amplification telescopes along the shifted parameters. Interpolation at \(n+1\) even shifts controls both:

- the leading coefficient, which is \(I\otimes(Y-X)\);
- the value at \(z=0\), where \(M_n(0)\) becomes a cyclic permutation of all \(n+1\) sites.

Thus small source leakage forces \(I\otimes X\) to almost commute with that cycle. Since it is exactly identity on the source site, cycling it around forces it to be close to identity on every site. A variance inequality for the commuting single-site trace projections then gives the explicit \(g_n\).

That is the operation which was missing from the prior general converse: a quantitative estimate on the actual homogeneous channel, valid for arbitrary prefix length.

### Why ordinary eigenvalues were insufficient

The bath channel also connects to the folded transfer matrices studied by Claeys, Herzog-Arbeitman and Lamacraft. Their work provides commuting transfer families and analyzes homogeneous Jordan structure. The recovery bound, however, requires singular-value loss. For a nonnormal operator, its ordinary eigenvalue gap does not control its largest nontrivial singular value. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

One constructor obtained an exponential bound for a fixed-point defect \(\|X-T_nX\|_2\). That did **not** close the problem: a contraction can have a large fixed-point defect and still preserve a vector’s norm exactly. The archive records this failed implication and a concrete counterexample, so it is not silently used in the main theorem.

## 4. Further exact results earned during the attempt

### General one- and two-qubit error floors

The general instrument theorem can be combined with sharper exact calculations at small prefixes.

For two carriers,
\[
\gamma_2=2\sin^4\theta-\sin^8\theta
\qquad
\text{when }\tan^4\theta\le\frac13.
\]
Every receiver retaining at most one qubit after that prefix therefore has a positive error floor whose weak-angle expansion begins
\[
\frac12\theta^4+O(\theta^6).
\]

For three carriers, exact symbolic calculation gives
\[
\gamma_3
=
\frac{7-\sqrt{33}}2\,\theta^4+O(\theta^6).
\]
Consequently every receiver retaining at most two qubits after the third carrier has an error-floor expansion beginning
\[
\boxed{
\frac{7-\sqrt{33}}8\,\theta^4
\approx0.15693\,\theta^4.
}
\]

These statements include arbitrary instruments and unlimited classical histories. They exclude arbitrarily accurate fixed-memory recovery at a fixed sufficiently weak angle. Their floors also tend to zero with the angle, so they do not establish a large error at every fixed practical tolerance.

### An exact carrier-loss law

I also derived and audited an exact extension for carriers lost **before receiver processing**.

Let
\[
b=\sin^4\theta,\qquad d=\cos^4\theta,
\]
and initialize
\[
a_0=0,\qquad q_0=1.
\]
For any specified retained subset, the exact recurrence is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{retained carrier:}\quad&
a_j=a_{j-1}+bq_{j-1},
& q_j&=(1-b)q_{j-1},\\
\text{lost carrier:}\quad&
a_j=a_{j-1},
& q_j&=dq_{j-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]

A physical reverse decoder achieves
\[
\epsilon_{\mathrm{Petz}}=\frac34(1-a_N),
\]
while every decoder of all retained outputs obeys
\[
\epsilon_{\mathrm{opt}}
\ge
\frac34(1-\sqrt{a_N}).
\]

For independent flagged loss with probability \(\ell\), the infinite-count acquired coefficient is
\[
\boxed{
\bar a_\infty
=
\frac{(1-\ell)\sin^4\theta}
{(1-\ell)\sin^4\theta+\ell(1-\cos^4\theta)}.
}
\]

This exposes a mismatch of scales. Acquisition contributes at fourth order in the weak angle, while losing a carrier damages the still-encoded signal at second order. In particular,
\[
\ell=\kappa\theta^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bar a_\infty\longrightarrow\frac1{1+2\kappa}.
\]

The necessary and sufficient-order loss tolerance for high-accuracy recovery is therefore
\[
\boxed{\ell=\Theta(\epsilon\theta^2).}
\]

At \(\theta=0.1\), the exact formulas give:

| Lost-carrier probability | Achieved error at \(N=43{,}462\) | Lower bound on any decoder at that count |
|---:|---:|---:|
| \(0\) | \(0.00999962\) | \(0.00501659\) |
| \(10^{-5}\) | \(0.0113890\) | \(0.00571628\) |
| \(10^{-4}\) | \(0.0236813\) | \(0.0119356\) |
| \(10^{-3}\) | \(0.128466\) | \(0.0672476\) |

With loss probability \(10^{-3}\), even infinitely many encounters leave the arbitrary-decoder lower bound at approximately \(0.0653091\). Error \(0.01\) is therefore impossible in that independently erased stream.

There is also a matching-order intact-subset result. With at most \(m\) untouched retained carriers, the Gram/Petz coefficient is maximized by keeping the first \(m\):
\[
a_N\le1-(1-\sin^4\theta)^m.
\]
Together with the decoder bounds, this forces
\[
m=\Theta\!\left(\theta^{-4}\log(1/\epsilon)\right)
\]
within that class.

**These erasure results are companion theorems, not substitutes for the general compression question.** A receiver can coherently process a carrier before discarding it; erasing that carrier beforehand is a different operation.

### A concrete physical candidate was ruled out

The operational constructor completed the rotated inverse tensor
\[
V=cI-2is\Phi
\]
to a valid four-outcome instrument:
\[
K_k=\frac12V(P_k\otimes I),
\qquad
\sum_kK_k^\dagger K_k=I.
\]

This was a real candidate, not just an unnormalized tensor contraction. But after its first processed carrier is discarded, distinguishability of opposite source inputs has already decreased. Even granting the actual source and allowing unlimited later memory cannot undo that loss. The resulting error floor begins at
\[
\frac12\theta^2-\frac{11}{12}\theta^4+O(\theta^6).
\]

That failure illustrates why a small operator description is insufficient. A low MPO bond does not automatically supply a trace-preserving causal compression preserving the quantum payload. Existing tensor-network compression theorems have operational state-space or purification premises that must actually be satisfied. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}
