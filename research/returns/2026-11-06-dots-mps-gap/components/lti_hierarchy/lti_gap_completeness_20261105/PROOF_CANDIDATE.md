# Candidate proof: exact LTI-gap convergence for proper injective-MPS parent chains

Date: 2026-11-05. Internal research draft, not a verified theorem or publication claim.

## Status and precise scope

This is a provisional proof candidate, not a publication or established theorem claim. Three internal independent audits have examined the nearest-neighbor unrestricted theorem below, including its ideal stitching algebra and proof components, without identifying a counterexample or remaining proof gap within the reviewed scope. This is internal review, not external verification or a novelty determination. The elementary strict-product-parent special case is separately proved below. No counterexample to the broader question was found.

**Exact assumptions of the restricted candidate.**

1. The site dimension d and MPS bond dimension D are finite. The nonzero nearest-neighbor interaction h>=0 is translation invariant, frustration free, and has norm M.
2. There is a fixed injective/normal MPS tensor A with primitive canonical transfer map and faithful invariant density.
3. There is a finite L_0 such that, for every open interval I with |I|>=L_0, ker H_I is exactly the MPS boundary space S_|I| generated by A. This is the **proper-parent** hypothesis, not merely inclusion of the MPS in the kernel.
4. The usual proper-parent consequences used explicitly are a uniform open-chain gap g>0 for sufficiently large intervals and a unique periodic ground state for sufficiently large lengths. Equivalently these consequences can be listed as additional explicit hypotheses if their standard MPS-parent theorem is not taken as background.

The interaction need not be projector-valued. The assertion concerns the unrestricted nearest-neighbor LTI SDP described below.

**Not established here.** Merely assuming a unique injective-MPS ground state on the infinite chain does not imply the proper-parent finite-ground-space hypothesis. PVBS already has a unique product bulk ground state but persistent extra open-chain zero modes. Thus this draft does not settle that literally broader promise without an additional argument.

Under the proper-parent assumptions, the argument claims

    gamma_n >= Delta_n - O(sqrt(log(n)/n)),

where gamma_n is exactly the **unrestricted** Rai LTI hierarchy in Eq. (17), restricted to the valid nearest-neighbor levels n>=3, and Delta_n is the n-site periodic gap. Combined with gamma_n <= Delta_N for n>=3 and N >= 2n, this would imply existence of the periodic gap limit and gamma_n -> Delta. Here Delta is defined solely as lim_n Delta_n. The draft does not separately identify that limit with a GNS/bulk-Hamiltonian spectral gap. The constant is model-dependent; no optimized rate is claimed.

**Hierarchy scope and separate extension.** The theorem proved in this document uses unrestricted Hermitian Y. The separate [projected-Y extension](PROJECTED_VARIANT_EXTENSION.md) addresses the Appendix D/certifiable formulation under its own stated assumptions and argument; it has been reviewed by the first internal auditor only, not the second or third. It is not an automatic consequence of restricting Y in this proof, and no claim is made here for arbitrary restricted ansatzes. The auxiliary ground-space compression mu below remains a proof device and does not add a support restriction to the Eq. (17) dual feasible set.

## 1. Definitions and the exact dual moments

Use the nearest-neighbor indexing of Rai et al. Eq. (17), with n>=3 throughout the certified hierarchy. On n sites put m=n-1 and h_i=h on sites i,i+1. A dual feasible rho satisfies

    rho >= 0; Tr_1 rho = Tr_n rho; Tr(rho h_1)=1.

Its objective is

    v = Tr rho [h_1^2 + sum_{d=1}^{m-1} {h_1,h_{1+d}}].

The minimum n=3 is essential for validity: it retains the adjacent anticommutator, which need not be positive. At n=2 the same formal SDP omits that term and is not generally a lower bound on the periodic gap, so n=2 is not a level of the certified hierarchy used here. An exact counterexample already lies inside the proper-parent class: for the spin-1 AKLT projector interaction, the formal value is gamma_2=1, whereas a four-site periodic eigenvector has energy 1/3, so Delta_4<=1/3 despite 4>=2*2. The exact witness is supplied by check_minimum_level.py / minimum_level_regression.json and independently by ../lti_gap_fresh_resource_audit_20261105/check_level_two.py / level_two_counterexample.json. For n>=3 every discarded interaction product has disjoint support and is positive.

Set a=Tr rho h_1^2 and b_d=Tr rho {h_1,h_{1+d}}. Local translation invariance implies every translate fitting in the interval has the same corresponding moment. In particular Tr rho h_i=1, Tr rho h_i^2=a. Positivity gives

    0 <= a <= M,
    b_1 >= -2a >= -2M,
    b_d >= 0 for d >= 2,
    v=a+sum_d b_d.

For H_o=sum_{i=1}^m h_i,

    Tr rho H_o = m,
    T_o := Tr rho H_o^2
         = m a + sum_{d=1}^{m-1}(m-d)b_d
         = m v - sum_d d b_d
         <= m v + 2M.                                      (1)

Optional independent moment check (not needed for the final contiguous-core estimate): for any subset S of r bonds and B_S=sum_{i in S}h_i, let c_d count unordered pairs in S at separation d. Since 0<=c_d<=r,

    Tr rho B_S^2 = r a + sum_d c_d b_d
                <= r v + 2Mr.                            (2)

Proof of (2): if b_1>=0, all b_d are nonnegative and the bound r v suffices. If b_1<0, use c_1 b_1<=0 and sum_{d>=2}c_d b_d<=r sum_{d>=2}b_d, giving r(v-b_1)<=r v+2Mr.

At every valid level n>=3, the optimal gamma_n is at most 2M. Indeed its primal is a certified lower bound to Delta_N for N>=2n (and we may choose N sufficiently large for the proper-parent conclusions). A nonzero single-site local excitation A|Omega_N> orthogonalized to the unique periodic ground state has energy at most 2M, because only the two overlapping interaction terms act on it. Thus Delta_N<=2M. Work with a nearly optimal dual v<=2M+eta and let eta->0 at the end.

There is no unresolved duality assumption: rho_0=I/Tr(h_1) is strictly positive, LTI, and energy-normalized (h is nonzero). The primal is feasible at delta=-M: for the minimal valid nearest-neighbor level n=3, the local generator can be chosen as (h_1+h_2)^2+(M h_1-h_1^2), which is positive and differs from g_3(-M) by the coboundary h_2^2-h_1^2. Larger levels add positive disjoint terms. Equation (1) bounds the dual objective below by -2M/m. Thus standard SDP Slater duality identifies the primal and dual optima. No dual attainment is assumed; use an approximating sequence.

## 2. Exact removal of the noncompact ground-state mass

Let P_n project onto ker H_o and Q_n=I-P_n. Define

    mu=Q_n rho Q_n.

Every h_i annihilates P_n, on both sides, and so does every h_i h_j. Consequently every moment used in (1)-(2) and Tr(mu h_i)=1 is preserved **exactly**. It does not matter that mu need no longer satisfy the full LTI marginal constraint.

The uniform open gap H_o>=g Q_n gives

    Tr mu <= m/g.                                         (3)

This is the crucial facial reduction for the present stitching argument. Adding arbitrary open-ground mass to rho makes Tr rho unbounded, but that mass and all ground/excited coherences can be discarded without changing any energy moment in the proof. The projection maneuver itself is not claimed novel; related ground-space projections occur in existing hierarchy analyses. The candidate contribution is its combination with controlled MPS boundary repair to prove exact convergence.

## 3. Canonical MPS block maps and their polar isometries

Choose a canonical gauge with transfer map E(X)=sum_s A^s X A^{s*} unital and faithful invariant density sigma for E*. Let

    Gamma_L |a,b> = sum_{s_1,...,s_L}(A^{s_1}...A^{s_L})_{ab}|s_1...s_L>.

For L beyond the injectivity length, Gamma_L is injective and has range S_L. Its Gram matrix G_L=Gamma_L*Gamma_L converges exponentially to a positive product matrix. Write G_infinity=I tensor R, where R>0 is DEFINED by this actual Gram limit. For Gamma_L |a,b> with coefficients (A_word)_{a,b} and E(X)=sum A X A*, the actual limit is R=sigma, NOT sigma transpose: G_L[(a,b),(c,d)]=E^L(|d><b|)[c,a] tends to delta[a,c] sigma[b,d]. Other vectorizations can introduce a transpose. All formulas below use R by definition, avoiding a convention-dependent substitution. Choose 0<theta<1 and C such that

    ||G_L-G_infinity|| <= C theta^L.

This remains valid when the transfer matrix has nontrivial Jordan blocks, by choosing theta strictly above its nontrivial spectral radius. Define

    S=G_infinity^(1/2),
    V_L=Gamma_L G_L^(-1/2),
    D_L^0=S Gamma_L^+,
    W_L^0=Gamma_L S^(-1).

V_L is an exact isometry onto S_L. On S_L,

    ||V_L* - D_L^0|| <= C_1 theta^L,
    ||V_L - W_L^0|| <= C_1 theta^L.                         (4)

All relevant maps and pseudoinverses have uniformly bounded norms for large L. D_L^0 Gamma_L=S exactly; W_L^0 has range S_L. The ideal maps need not be isometric or trace preserving. They are only comparison maps.

## 4. An explicit CPTP periodic boundary repair

Take disjoint end buffers L=[1,ell] and R=[n-ell+1,n], leaving the middle unchanged. Assume n>2ell+2 and ell is above all parent/injectivity thresholds.

Decode each buffer into its two virtual factors with the CPTP map

    D_ell(X)=V_ell* X V_ell + Tr[(I-P_ell)X] tau,

where tau is any density matrix on the two virtual factors and P_ell=V_ell V_ell*. With a reference system, the second summand is the usual trace-and-prepare channel, not a scalar replacement of the reference.

The left buffer has virtual factors (outer_L, inner_L); the right buffer has (inner_R, outer_R). Discard outer_L and outer_R, preserve the two inner factors and all their reference entanglement, order the retained factors as (inner_R,inner_L), and encode them via V_{2ell}. Place its physical output in the cyclic order R followed by L. This defines a CPTP map Phi supported only on the two end buffers. It is allowed to act jointly on these buffers; the proof constructs a state and does not assert a geometrically local physical implementation.

An alternative **joint-failure completion**, matching full_operation.py, uses the success projector P_0=P_ell^L tensor P_ell^R on both end buffers and Q_0=I-P_0. On the successful branch, apply the same two-buffer decode, discard outer virtuals, and joined V_{2ell} encode. On the failure branch, trace BOTH end buffers after projection Q_0 X Q_0 and prepare any fixed normalized vector chi in the joined S_{2ell}:

    Phi_failure(X)=|chi><chi|_joined
                    tensor Tr_buffers[Q_0 X Q_0],

with tensor factors placed back into their physical positions and the reference/core preserved. The successful branch has trace Tr(P_0 X), and the failure branch has trace Tr(Q_0 X), so the sum is CPTP. In particular the failure branch uses Q_0 X Q_0, not an unprojected reference marginal. Partial tracing the buffers removes success/failure cross terms, so the combined map leaves the core marginal unchanged. Both completion choices agree on the code used in the ideal-algebra estimate and obey all bounds below.

Every output is exactly in S_{2ell} on the joined buffer region R followed by L. Thus all h terms internal to that joined region, including the periodic seam h_{n,1}, annihilate every output.

Let P_L^+=P_{S_{ell+1}} on sites [1,ell+1] and P_R^+=P_{S_{ell+1}} on [n-ell,n], and set P=P_L^+ P_R^+. These projectors have disjoint support. On ran(P), the complement/reset branches of both decoders vanish exactly.

### 4.1 The algebraic ideal stitching identity (main point to audit)

Replace the two exact decoders by D_ell^0 and the joined encoder by W_{2ell}^0. Keep the discarded outer factors as an environment rather than tracing them; call the resulting comparison map K_0. Let K be the corresponding actual Stinespring map on ran(P). Equation (4) and bounded operator norms give

    ||(K-K_0)P|| <= C_2 theta^ell.                         (5)

The image of K_0 P lies in S_{2ell+2} on the cyclic region

    untouched right inner neighbor, R buffer, L buffer,
    untouched left inner neighbor,

tensored with the environment and the rest of the core. Here is the algebraic reason, not just a clustering assertion:

- On the original left window, Gamma_{ell+1} has coefficients
      (A^{word_L} A^{s_L})_{alpha,b}
      =sum_x (A^{word_L})_{alpha,x} A^{s_L}_{x,b}.
  Ideal decoding maps Gamma_ell to S=I_outer_L tensor sqrt(R)_inner_L. The outer factor alpha is free, while the retained inner factor gets sqrt(R).
- On the original right window, the coefficients are
      (A^{s_R} A^{word_R})_{c,beta}
      =sum_y A^{s_R}_{c,y}(A^{word_R})_{y,beta}.
  Ideal decoding leaves I on inner_R=y, and puts sqrt(R) only on the free outer_R factor.
- The ideal joined encoder is Gamma_{2ell}(I_inner_R tensor sqrt(R)_inner_L)^(-1), so it cancels exactly the retained sqrt(R) from the left window.
- The remaining physical coefficients are therefore proportional to
      [A^{s_R} A^{word_R} A^{word_L} A^{s_L}]_{c,b},
  with alpha and the transformed beta carried to the environment.

More explicitly, a spanning original code vector with arbitrary reference coefficients is

    sum (A_p A_s)_{a,c} (A_t A_q)_{d,b}
        X_{a,c,d,b,ref} |p,s,t,q,ref>,

where p is the left buffer, q the right buffer, s the untouched left inner neighbor, and t the untouched right inner neighbor. Ideal decoding leaves retained factors f (right-buffer left endpoint) and e-prime (left-buffer right endpoint), with factors A_t[d,f], sqrt(R)[e-prime,e] A_s[e,c], and environment factors a and sqrt(R)[b-prime,b]. Encoding with Gamma_{2ell}(I tensor R)^(-1/2) in physical order r,l contracts sqrt(R)^(-1)[z,e-prime] sqrt(R)[e-prime,e] to delta[z,e]. The physical amplitude becomes exactly

    (A_t A_r A_l A_s)_{d,c}
       sqrt(R)_{b-prime,b} X_{a,c,d,b,ref},

with a,b-prime in the environment. This is an MPS on the cyclic physical order core_R, R-buffer, L-buffer, core_L, for every environment/reference coefficient.

This holds for arbitrary linear combinations and arbitrary entanglement with the core, so it is an operator identity on ran(P), not merely a statement about a selected MPS state.

Let H_b be the sum of all periodic interaction terms outside the unchanged middle. The identity gives

    H_b K_0 P=0.                                          (6)

Both K and K_0 have their joined-buffer output in S_{2ell}, so H_b acting on either image reduces to the two buffer/core seam terms. Their sum has norm at most 2M. Equations (5)-(6) imply

    P Phi*(H_b^2) P <= 4M^2 C_2^2 theta^(2ell) P.          (7)

Furthermore, for every input (including reset branches), only those two seam terms act on the output; hence

    ||Phi*(H_b^2)|| <= 4M^2.                              (8)

### 4.2 Positive-block domination and energetic repair cost

For every F>=0, a projector P, and PFP<=epsilon P,

    F <= 2||F||(I-P) + 2epsilon P.

This follows directly from positivity/Cauchy-Schwarz on the P/(I-P) blocks. Apply it to F=Phi*(H_b^2), using (7)-(8):

    F <= 8M^2(I-P) + 8M^2 C_2^2 theta^(2ell) I.

Proper-parent ground spaces and the uniform open gap give

    I-P <= (I-P_L^+)+(I-P_R^+)
          <= (H_L^+ + H_R^+)/g,

where each H^+ is an ell-bond open Hamiltonian. Since all local energy moments of mu equal one,

    Tr Phi(mu) H_b^2 <= K_{n,ell},
    K_{n,ell} := 16M^2 ell/g
                 + 8M^2 C_2^2 m theta^(2ell)/g.            (9)

An analogous bound holds for Phi*(H_b). It is not needed below because H_b>=0 and the unchanged middle provides the denominator lower bound.

## 5. Turn the repaired marginal into a periodic excitation witness

Let H_c be the sum of the contiguous untouched middle bonds, and put m_c=m-2ell. Its support is disjoint from the two buffers on which Phi acts. Ground-space compression preserved all moments, so for every nearly optimal dual witness

    Tr mu H_c = m_c,
    Tr mu H_c^2
      = m_c a + sum_{d=1}^{m_c-1}(m_c-d)b_d
      <= m_c v + 2M.                                     (10)

Indeed, subtracting the displayed expression from m_c v gives sum_{d<m_c} d b_d + m_c sum_{d>=m_c}b_d. All summands are nonnegative except possibly the b_1 contribution, whose coefficient is one and whose value is at least -2M. The empty-sum case m_c=1 obeys the same bound. This direct contiguous-core estimate avoids any subtraction or triangle bound involving removed edge bonds.

Set nu=Phi(mu). Because Phi leaves the middle unchanged, its H_c and H_c^2 moments equal those of mu. The n-site periodic Hamiltonian is H_n^per=H_c+H_b. Positivity and the weighted Hilbert-Schmidt triangle inequality give

    Tr nu H_n^per >= m_c,
    sqrt(Tr nu (H_n^per)^2)
      <= sqrt(m_c v+2M)+sqrt(K_{n,ell}).                   (11)

The exact finite periodic spectral inequality (H_n^per)^2>=Delta_n H_n^per applies to the positive unnormalized nu. Combining with (11),

    Delta_n m_c
      <= [sqrt(m_c v+2M)+sqrt(K_{n,ell})]^2.

The constants in K_{n,ell} do not depend on the dual witness. Taking any sequence of dual objectives v decreasing to gamma_n therefore yields

    Delta_n m_c
      <= [sqrt(m_c gamma_n+2M)+sqrt(K_{n,ell})]^2.         (12)

No dual optimizer or limiting state nu is required. Nor does nu need to be orthogonal to the periodic ground state.

Choose ell=ceil(log(m)/(-2 log(theta))) plus a fixed threshold. Then m theta^(2ell)<=1, K_{n,ell}=O(ell), ell=O(log n), and m_c is positive and asymptotic to n. Since gamma_n<=2M, rearrangement of (12) yields the explicit comparison

    Delta_n-gamma_n
      <= [2M+2 sqrt((2M m_c+2M)K_{n,ell})+K_{n,ell}]/m_c,

and hence

    gamma_n >= Delta_n - O(sqrt(log(n)/n)).               (13)

The implicit constants depend on M,g,the MPS Gram convergence constants, and injectivity thresholds.

Finally the valid-level sequence (gamma_n)_{n>=3} is nondecreasing and bounded. In the monotonicity step n to n+1, the newly retained separation is n-1>=2 and hence has disjoint support. Its limit L satisfies L<=liminf_N Delta_N because gamma_n<=Delta_N for every n>=3 and N>=2n. Equation (13) gives limsup_n Delta_n<=L. Thus the periodic finite-size gaps have a limit Delta=L, and gamma_n converges to that same limit. This argument proves only this periodic-limit statement; it does not separately prove equality with any GNS spectral-gap definition.

## 5A. Finite-range extension note (not part of the primary claim)

For a range-k interaction with k>=2, define an explicit natural variant on n sites by m=n-k+1, h_i supported on i,...,i+k-1, and objective v=Tr rho[h_1^2+sum_{d=1}^{m-1}{h_1,h_{1+d}}]. Its valid levels must satisfy n>=2k-1. Indeed the largest retained separation is n-k, and all potentially nonpositive overlapping terms at separations 1,...,k-1 must be retained. Under n>=2k-1, discarded separations are at least k and have disjoint support; the monotonicity step adds separation n-k+1>=k. Smaller n do not generally define certified lower bounds. This explicit definition and minimum-support condition, rather than a potentially ambiguous printed general-range index, are essential. Then b_d>=0 for d>=k, and the same moment argument gives

    Tr rho H_o^2 <= m v + M k(k-1),
    Tr rho B_S^2 <= r[v+2M(k-1)].

Use end buffers of ell physical sites, with defect projectors on ell+k-1 sites including k-1 untouched core neighbors. The untouched core has m-2ell terms. Encoding into the joined S_{2ell} leaves only 2(k-1) crossing interaction terms active, so ||Phi*(H_b^2)||<=4(k-1)^2 M^2. In the ideal stitching algebra, s and t are words of length k-1 instead of single symbols. With uniform open gaps and the same Gram estimates, these substitutions indicate the same O(sqrt(log(n)/n)) convergence argument. This extension is not used in the primary theorem. The second internal audit explicitly did not review this finite-range note, so the nearest-neighbor theorem's two-audit status does not apply to it.

Alternatively, a finite-range proper parent can be blocked into a nearest-neighbor interaction while preserving the global Hamiltonian on compatible periodic sizes. The primary theorem then concerns the hierarchy of that blocked interaction. It does not by itself identify levels or optima of an unblocked hierarchy with a different truncation convention.

## 6. Elementary strict-product-parent proof, independent of MPS stitching

Assume ker h=span{|00>} and h>=c(I-|00><00|), c>0. The bulk vacuum is unique and M=||h||. Starting directly with a dual rho_n, pad it with two vacuum sites to form a state on the periodic n+2-site chain. H_o still has moments in (1). Let V consist of the two new boundary bonds. The remaining outside-vacuum bond annihilates the padded state.

For the vacuum-compressed left boundary B_L acting on site 1,

    0<=B_L<=M n_1<=(M/c)h_1,
    Tr rho B_L<=M/c,
    Tr rho B_L^2<=M^2/c.

The right boundary is analogous. For disjoint distant h_j (j>=3),

    Tr rho {h_j,B_L}<=(M/c)b_{j-1}.

At the direct boundary bond, {h_1,B_L}<=h_1^2+B_L^2, whose expectation is <=M+M^2/c. The single adjacent remaining bond commutes with B_L and contributes at most 2M^2/c. Since sum_{d>=2}b_d<=v+M and v<=2M, adding both boundaries gives the conservative bound

    Tr {H_o,V} <= 2M+12M^2/c.

Also V^2<=2(h_L^2+h_R^2), so Tr V^2<=4M^2/c. The padded periodic energy is at least m. Thus

    Delta_{n+2} m <= m v+4M+16M^2/c.

Taking the dual infimum gives

    gamma_n >= Delta_{n+2} -(4M+16M^2/c)/(n-1).

This proves exact convergence in this strict D=1 parent subclass, with an O(1/n) comparison to finite periodic gaps. It also extends, with changed constants, whenever some fixed open block has unique product-vacuum kernel, since that block Hamiltonian dominates endpoint occupation.
