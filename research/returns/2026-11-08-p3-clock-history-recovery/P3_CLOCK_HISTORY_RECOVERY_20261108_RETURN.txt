## 2. Exact criterion for recovering an erased execution history

Here is the precise family of states for which I obtained a complete answer.

Let \(E:\mathcal H_L\rightarrow\mathcal H_D\) be an isometric data encoding. Suppose that after a specified damage or repair event, history \(h\) leaves the accessible state

\[
\mathcal F_h(\rho)
=
E W_h\rho W_h^\dagger E^\dagger\otimes \sigma_h,
\]

where:

- \(W_h\) is the logical operation performed along that history;
- \(\sigma_h\) is the surviving record state, independent of the unknown input;
- every additional accessible record is included in \(\sigma_h\) or in the encoded data.

We seek one CPTP recovery \(\mathcal R\) satisfying

\[
\mathcal R\circ\mathcal F_h=\operatorname{id}_L
\qquad\text{for every }h.
\]

A common desired final unitary can be factored out of this test. The recovery is allowed arbitrary complexity and knowledge of the programmed circuit. Thus failure of this test cannot be repaired merely by choosing a more powerful decoder.

### Recovery criterion

**A common exact recovery exists if and only if, for every pair \(h,k\), either**

\[
W_h=e^{i\theta}W_k,
\]

**or the supports of \(\sigma_h\) and \(\sigma_k\) are orthogonal.**

This is an application of the established Knill–Laflamme recovery conditions, credited here as primary machinery. [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9604034?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

To see it directly, choose positive probabilities \(p_h\), diagonalize

\[
\sigma_h=\sum_a\lambda_{h,a}|s_{h,a}\rangle\langle s_{h,a}|,
\]

and consider the channel \(\sum_h p_h\mathcal F_h\). Its Kraus operators can be written

\[
A_{h,a}
=
\sqrt{p_h\lambda_{h,a}}\,
\bigl(EW_h\otimes |s_{h,a}\rangle\bigr).
\]

Their cross products are

\[
A_{h,a}^{\dagger}A_{k,b}
=
\sqrt{p_hp_k\lambda_{h,a}\lambda_{k,b}}\,
\langle s_{h,a}|s_{k,b}\rangle
W_h^\dagger W_k.
\]

Recoverability requires these operators to be scalar multiples of the identity on the logical input. Whenever \(W_h^\dagger W_k\) is nonscalar, every corresponding record overlap must vanish.

Conversely, orthogonal record supports let a recovery distinguish the logical-operation class and apply its inverse.

### What this means for clock repair

If repair replaces all surviving records by the same state,

\[
\sigma_h=\sigma\quad\text{for all }h,
\]

then exact recovery is possible only when all the histories induce the same logical channel.

For example, suppose the only remaining possibilities are

\[
\rho
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
X_L\rho X_L,
\]

with identical accessible control records. Both are valid encoded states. A data-code syndrome can be trivial in both cases. A recovery that preserves every unknown logical state cannot decide whether to undo \(X_L\).

The scope matters. This criterion assumes the displayed family with a common data encoding and input-independent record states. A processor can use a different joint encoding of data and causal information, retain additional syndromes, or suppress the damaging histories sufficiently strongly. The theorem does not exclude those possibilities.

\[

| History state | Next state | Transition rate |
|---|---|---:|

\[
\frac{1}{2(\gamma+\lambda)}

\begin{pmatrix}b_r\\b_d\end{pmatrix}

\begin{pmatrix}
-\gamma&\lambda\\

\begin{pmatrix}b_r\\b_d\end{pmatrix},

\[
\boxed{

\frac{1-|b_r(t)|-|b_d(t)|}{2}.
