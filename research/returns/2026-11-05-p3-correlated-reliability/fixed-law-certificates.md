# Fixed-law auxiliary certificates: known mechanism and explicit limits

This [independent retained route](package/independent_routes/next3_transfer/INDEPENDENT_OPENING.txt) gives a mathematically sound certificate and useful examples, while explicitly recognizing that established auxiliary-function theory already provides its central mechanism. It was not selected as a new capability and does not have a useful uniform degree/runtime or new physical implementation theorem.

For a polynomial ODE `x_dot=F(x,theta)` on a known compact forward-invariant set, with **time-constant** theta in a known compact parameter set, a verified inequality `f+F.grad_x V<=b` gives long-time average at most b and finite-time bound `b+osc(V)/T`. No theta derivative appears because the epistemic law is fixed. Existing auxiliary-function strong duality on the augmented flow `(x_dot,theta_dot)=(F,0)` supplies the worst-trajectory endpoint under its compact-flow/smooth-extension premises. It includes exceptional unstable trajectories, not just a selected physical invariant distribution.

Rational polynomial enumeration plus exact real quantifier elimination is a finite semidecision search: a strict feasible upper bound is eventually found under the stated approximation premises. There is no supplied effective degree, coefficient or useful runtime bound. Bounded-degree floating SOS feasibility alone is not a verified certificate; a complete hierarchy requires its extra positivity assumptions. Finite noisy endpoint data can form a simultaneous parameter confidence set only with supplied preparation, speed/Lipschitz, sensor and quantization errors; short difference quotients can cost inverse-fourth-power accuracy sampling. Exact certificate search does not repair a wide compatible-model range.

Two exact examples clarify the boundary. On the unit circle with rotation rate `theta=+-1`, each fixed-law average of x is zero; the parameter-dependent auxiliary `V=-theta y` certifies it. Requiring one parameter-independent V instead admits a switched-law interpretation and an upper bound no better than one. Keeping uncertainty frozen is a real modeling distinction, not a new auxiliary-function principle.

For `x_dot=theta(1-x^2)`, initial x=0, the infinite-time x average jumps from minus one to plus one as theta crosses zero. Finite noisy derivative queries need order `sigma^2/a^2` observations to separate `theta=+-a`; no sample budget uniform over arbitrarily small a gives a narrow infinite-time answer. This is a finite-information obstruction, not an unconditional computational runtime lower bound or a claim that all robust physical classes fail.
