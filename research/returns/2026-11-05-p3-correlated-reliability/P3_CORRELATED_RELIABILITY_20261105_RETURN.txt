## 2. The theorem covers dependence that materially changes the answer

A small exact family shows why the stronger condition is consequential.

Take two interacting failure bits with weights

\[
W(0,0)=1,\qquad
W(1,0)=W(0,1)=t^2,\qquad
W(1,1)=t^3,
\]

where \(t=2^{-b}\).

Their conditional failure probabilities range between

\[
q^-=\frac{t^2}{1+t^2},
\qquad
q^+=\frac{t}{1+t}.
\]

They satisfy

\[
q^-\ge(q^+)^2,
\]

so \(c=2\) works for every \(b\). Yet their conditional odds vary by

\[
\frac1t=2^b.
\]

Their Dobrushin influence is below \(t\), so the sampling margin remains bounded away from zero.

Now make these two bits the two parallel edges of a network. Disconnection requires both edges to fail. With

\[
Z=1+2t^2+t^3,
\]

the true probability is

\[
p=\frac{t^3}{Z}.
\]

The independent model with **exactly the same one-edge marginals** assigns probability

\[
p_{\rm ind}
=
\left[\frac{t^2(1+t)}{Z}\right]^2.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{p}{p_{\rm ind}}
=
\frac{Z}{t(1+t)^2}
\asymp 2^b.
}
\]

Matching the component marginals therefore leaves an exponentially wrong system-failure probability.

The class also extends beyond independent pairs and bounded interaction degree. Let \(I\) be an arbitrary interaction graph of maximum degree \(D\ge1\), and define

\[
W(x)
=
2^{-2Db\sum_i x_i+b\sum_{ij\in E(I)}x_ix_j},
\qquad b\ge1.
\]

Valid conditional bounds are

\[
q^-=\frac1{1+2^{2Db}},
\qquad
q^+=\frac1{1+2^{Db}}.
\]

Again,

\[
q^-\ge(q^+)^2,
\]

while each Dobrushin row sum is at most

\[
\frac{D}{1+2^{Db}}\le\frac12.
\]

Thus **fixed \(c=2\) and a fixed sampling margin suffice even as interaction degree and conditional-odds distortion grow**. The factor descriptions, conditional evaluations, and actual degree are still paid. The physical network graph and the interaction graph need not coincide.

This example establishes a scope change: the method can retain dependence in the rare configurations that govern failure, rather than approximating their joint probabilities by products.

## 3. The hardest missing operation: acquire rare-event access without observing rare events

The construction combines established machinery with explicit interfaces and error bounds. The individual sampling-to-counting, cut-contraction, and inverse-multiplicity principles are credited to their existing sources.

### A. Construct a finite sampler under every pinning

A pinning fixes selected coordinates and samples the remaining variables from the corresponding conditional Gibbs law.

Under the stated row-sum influence bound, pinning only removes influences and restricts the configurations over which their maxima are taken. The same margin \(\alpha\) therefore applies to every pinned system.

A fixed-order heat-bath sweep contracts the maximum coordinate disagreement probability by at least \(1-\alpha\). After sufficiently many sweeps, the output is within total variation \(\eta\) of the desired pinned law, using

\[
O\!\left(
N\alpha^{-1}\log(N/\eta)
\right)
\]

local updates.

The implementation rounds conditional probabilities to dyadic values, pays the accumulated rounding error, and uses a fixed finite number of random bits. It never violates a pinning. This realizes the required operation rather than treating equilibrium sampling as an unexplained oracle. Dobrushin-style rapid mixing is established machinery; the report gives the particular finite implementation and keeps the row orientation explicit. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

These pinnings are computations on a declared model. They do not assume that a physical apparatus can be forced into rare configurations for free.

### B. Estimate a rare pinned probability by following likely branches

This was the decisive representation change.

For a pinning \(D\), define its restricted partition sum

\[
Z_D=\sum_{x\models D}W(x).
\]

Directly estimating \(\pi(D)\) by counting how often an unconditioned sample satisfies \(D\) would reintroduce inverse rarity.

Instead, estimate the next conditional bit marginal only to **additive** precision, then choose the empirically more probable bit. Repeat until a complete state \(x^\star\) has been selected.

Every estimated selected-branch probability is at least \(1/2\). On the simultaneous confidence event, its true probability remains above a fixed constant. Therefore the product of these branch probabilities can be estimated relatively without resolving a rare Bernoulli outcome.

The chain rule gives

\[
\pi(x^\star\mid D)
=
\prod_i h_i
=
\frac{W(x^\star)}{Z_D}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\widehat Z_D
=
\frac{W(x^\star)}
{\prod_i\widehat h_i}.
}
\]

The weight \(W(x^\star)\) may be extremely small, but it is evaluated from the supplied rational factors. Small numerical value is paid through bit length.

For log-relative tolerance \(u\), the explicit construction uses

\[
O\!\left(
N^3u^{-2}\log(N/\beta)
\right)
\]

pinned draws. Adaptively chosen prefixes are handled by conditional concentration and a union bound. Constants introduced by pinning remain in the absolute weight \(W\).

This is a weighted realization of the established Jerrum–Valiant–Vazirani sampling-to-counting principle. The new use here is to supply correlated cut probabilities within the full reliability construction. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### C. Construct a finite list of relevant failure cuts

Every disconnection contains a failed graph cut.

Assign each edge a positive rational weight \(w_e\) satisfying

The report gives a closed rational construction, so tiny weights do not require an unpriced logarithm computation.

This step uses conditional bounds, not independence.

Let \(\lambda\) be the minimum weighted cut. Then

Karger’s contraction argument controls the number of cuts near \(\lambda\). For integer \(a\), contracting until \(2a\) vertices remain and then choosing a random partition outputs any designated cut of weight at most \(a\lambda\) with probability at least \(v^{-2a}\). ### D. Correct overlapping cuts and sample conditional failure

For retained cut events \(E_1,\ldots,E_J\), let

\[
d(x)=\#\{j:x\in E_j\}.
\]

Choose a cut with probability proportional to its acquired mass, then draw from its pinned Gibbs law. With exact masses, the resulting proposal density is proportional to

\[
\pi(x)d(x).

- Averaging \(1/d(x)\) estimates the union probability.
- Accepting a proposal with probability \(1/d(x)\) produces the conditional law on that union.

Degree and factor representation. The general theorem pays explicit factor evaluation and requires certified conditional and influence bounds. The executed implementation enumerated local neighborhoods; its maximum neighborhood degree was three, with 46 conditional-table entries.
Witness enumeration and memory. Cut lists, cut-membership scans, rational weights, sampler state, CDF grids, and Poisson tables all contribute to the bill. The conservative graph exponent is substantial.
Dynamics. A small numerical gap remains expensive. The required reversible backbone must actually belong to the modeled dynamics.
Initialization. The CDF target is the exact equilibrium-conditioned safe start. Physical preparation and finite conditional-update errors retain their separate costs.
Model completeness. Declared latent variables can be included. Undeclared common causes, external catastrophes, aging, hidden memory, and arbitrary nonreversible dynamics are not inferred away.

The theorem therefore changes what can be computed from an explicit interacting model. It does not certify that a quiet physical system has no omitted failure mechanism.

8. Independent routes and preservation

Each independent route initially owned a full scientific question.

Independent question	Actual outcome
Can finite passive current observations universally certify a transport coefficient without a supplied relaxation bound?	An exact obstruction was derived using a reversible passive OU family. A weak, arbitrarily slow hidden mode can make the transport coefficient arbitrarily large while finite observed path laws remain close. A conditional tail bracket survives when an external spectral floor is supplied.
Can finite local data yield fixed-law long-time bounds for chaotic dynamics without learning an invariant measure?	The auxiliary-function construction was derived, but its central capability is already supplied by established duality and polynomial-certification theory. It was preserved without claiming a new breakthrough.
Can an unequilibrated passive bath be acquired and simulated through a nonlinear observed coordinate without identifying microscopic modes?	A passivity-based trajectory-error theorem was completed. The finite-slew acquisition and full physical execution endpoint remained unresolved; existing Astra retained-device acquisition machinery was credited.

Those results, failed openings, and unresolved operations are retained in the package.

The earlier quantum and effusion constructions are also intact: the complete classical-feedback all-strength theorem; the distinct sharper global-budget result with its corrected scope; the matched parity/GHZ resource law; finite-slew and calibration constructions; output-only calibration and first-input recovery; the bounded-neighborhood no-burn-in result; and the independent effusion operation. The effusion proof retains its thin-shell geometry, actual force/clearing interface, and remaining exposure bill.

The new general principle earned here is a sufficient one: rare interacting failure can be computationally accessible when failure witnesses have controlled geometry, their probabilities are tied to that geometry, and their conditional laws can be sampled efficiently from known finite weights. For undirected all-terminal failure, the witness enumeration and every required probability operation have now been constructed. Physical model acquisition, economical large-scale execution, and broader initialization or dynamical classes remain separate scientific endpoints.
