PROOF AUDIT — MAIN ACCESS THEOREM

1. Acquired channel, not an assumed initial noise layer.
   Dephasing acts simultaneously with allowed control from time zero. For each
   nonempty unresolved set R, remove only Hamiltonian terms touching R. The
   remaining propagator is CPTP and factors the exact decay exp(-2 lambda |R| t).
   A Duhamel insertion removes at least one unresolved site. Induction therefore
   terminates after finitely many set reductions. Fully controlled dynamics on
   already resolved sites remain inside a CPTP contraction; an unsigned Dyson
   expansion over arbitrarily long acquisition time is not used.

2. Full retained instruments are constrained.
   Covariance of the averaged source channel is insufficient. A projective X
   measurement has an averaged source channel commuting with Ad_Z but its
   retained outcome reveals X. Giving that outcome free invalidates the bound.
   The required covariance applies to every retained quantum output, classical
   outcome, controller and record. Noncommuting readout must be paid.

3. Norm convention and strength decomposition.
   D[Z](rho)=Z rho Z-rho, so transverse decay is 2 lambda per site.
   Each source-touching Hamiltonian term is charged h_a=2 ||H_a||. The sum of
   h_a over terms touching each source site is at most Omega. A total bound on
   ||[H,Z_i]|| alone does not imply this decomposition or its summed budget.

4. Measured feedback versus coherent control.
   Classical records form a direct-sum operator algebra with trace norm equal
   to the sum of block norms. Charge the terms in each actual record branch,
   multiply by that block's norm, then sum. A sum of maxima over mutually
   exclusive branches would be an incorrect argument. Coherent control instead
   obeys the global Hamiltonian decomposition and norm budget.

5. Collective access to fresh copies.
   Hybridize the initial copies one at a time. For each hybrid difference, the
   other copies and the receiver form the remaining system; the theorem acts
   on the designated copy's transverse source sites. Their physical per-site
   budgets and retained-instrument constraints remain necessary. This covers
   joint ingestion, cross-copy processing, adaptive measurements and flags.
   Failed trials consume copies; the argument does not grant free repetition
   of a measurement on the same unknown source input.

6. Scope of the purity lower bound.
   GHZ+ and its dephased mixture have purity 1 and 1/2, and their difference
   has trace norm 1 in the full transverse sector. Any universal estimator of
   additive accuracy strictly below 1/4 must distinguish them. This is a
   worst-case result, not hardness for every state or every scientific property.

7. Explicit controls and their costs.
   The positive meters assume H(t)=omega(t) P tensor Y/2 as an actual control.
   The triangle has duration 1/lambda, peak Omega/2, and slew Omega lambda.
   It is not strictly band-limited. Independent local meters yield standard
   shadow statistics with explicit contrast, calibration and precision costs.
   An n-body P term is a distinct resource, not a free compilation from k-local
   gates. No microscopic implementation is asserted for that term.

8. Tightness and open boundary.
   Ordinary independent block meters attain the one-copy weak-control power
   n/k. The general sample lower bound and elementary sampling upper bound
   differ by a factor two in their exponential exponents. Omega=2 lambda is
   a boundary of this proof, not an identified physical threshold. An all-fixed-
   strength-ratio exponential task lower bound remains open in this return.

9. Evidence.
   Independent constructive audit and analytic derivation support the stated
   proof. Floating-point finite-model checks passed, including strong allowed
   commuting backgrounds and an entangled receiver. These checks are not a
   theorem prover, a diamond-norm SDP, or an exhaustive control optimization.
