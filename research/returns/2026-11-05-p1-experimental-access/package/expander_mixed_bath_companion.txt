EXPANDER RIGIDITY, MIXED-BATH RECEPTION, AND LOW-PURITY MEMORY CERTIFICATION
Independent constructive companion to Astra P1's experimental-access hierarchy
Prepared 5 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane

STATUS AND SCOPE

This note records a completed mathematical derivation developed in the independent branch. It imports established explicit quantum-expander constructions and standard quantum capacity coding theorems, but the mixed-probe conversion, memory bound and mixed collision-source composition below are derived explicitly. No historical-priority claim, laboratory demonstration, independently executed formal proof, or practical device advantage is asserted. The main P1 branch selected a different result; this construction is preserved as a companion rather than presented as a solution to every physical-reception problem.

The central conclusion is a separation between small source dimension and the quantum memory required to preserve an entire mixed physical output experiment. A constant-dimensional, time-programmed source interacting with a maximally mixed incoming collision stream can produce an output for which almost all emitted qubits must be retained. Its channel to that output can nevertheless be entanglement breaking and have a constant-dimensional input. This does not contradict the pure-port collection theorem: unavailable bath purifiers and mixed incoming modes change its premises.

The positive counterpart is an explicit trusted test for large coherent memory dimension, entanglement fidelity, and—under memoryless repeated-use assumptions—quantum capacity, using a constant number of mixed input states. Each state is prepared from one biased qubit and a maximally mixed register. The relevant preparation/measurement circuits and their total accuracy are paid. Constant sample complexity in system dimension does not mean constant total physical resources.

1. RIGIDITY ASSUMPTION AND NOTATION

Let the target input and output Hilbert spaces have dimension N. Let H_1,...,H_r be traceless Hermitian involutions:

  H_j = H_j^dagger, H_j^2 = I_N, Tr H_j = 0.

Let p_j > 0, sum_j p_j = 1. Assume a known commutator spectral gap kappa > 0:

  sum_j p_j ||[K,H_j]||_2^2
    >= kappa ||K - (Tr K/N) I_N||_2^2                     (1)

for every complex N-by-N matrix K. The norm here is the unnormalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm. This is stronger than merely saying that the H_j have scalar commutant: it supplies a dimension-uniform quantitative rigidity constant.

Choose a known bias 0 < a <= 1 and define

  tau_{j,+} = (I_N + a H_j)/N,
  tau_{j,-} = (I_N - a H_j)/N.                            (2)

Both are density matrices. For a<1 they are full rank. Each balanced pair averages to I_N/N.

For a channel T:N->N define its score

  S(T) = (1/2) sum_j p_j [Tr H_j T(tau_{j,+})
                         - Tr H_j T(tau_{j,-})]
       = (a/N) sum_j p_j Tr[H_j T(H_j)].                  (3)

A physical trial chooses j with probability p_j and a fair sign b in {+1,-1}, prepares tau_{j,b}, applies the device, measures H_j with outcome h in {+1,-1}, and records Y=b h. Then E Y=S(T). The identity channel has S=a.

The channel is the same for every hidden preparation setting. Supplying the classical label (j,b) to the device invalidates the memory interpretation: it could discard the specimen and reprepare the advertised state. All quantum systems crossing the storage cut must count, including entanglement halves, unobserved quantum records, and external delay lines.

2. EXACT RIGIDITY-TO-FIDELITY THEOREM

Let T(X)=sum_alpha K_alpha X K_alpha^dagger be CPTP. Its entanglement fidelity against the identity on the maximally mixed input is

  F_e(T) = (1/N^2) sum_alpha |Tr K_alpha|^2.

For each Hermitian involution H, trace preservation gives the exact identity

  sum_alpha ||[K_alpha,H]||_2^2
    = 2N - 2 Re Tr[H T(H)].                              (4)

No unitality assumption is required. To check it directly, expand the square, use H^2=I, cyclicity of trace, and sum_alpha K_alpha^dagger K_alpha=I.

Apply (1) to every K_alpha and sum. Since

  sum_alpha ||K_alpha - (Tr K_alpha/N)I||_2^2
    = N [1-F_e(T)],

and the weighted sum of (4) equals 2N[1-S(T)/a], one obtains

  F_e(T) >= 1 - (2/kappa)[1-S(T)/a].                      (5)

Bounds below zero can be replaced by zero. The identity (4) also shows S(T)<=a.

An equivalent preservation formulation is useful for blind compression. If

  max_{j,b} ||T(tau_{j,b}) - tau_{j,b}||_1 <= epsilon,

then S(T)>=a-epsilon, and therefore

  F_e(T) >= 1 - 2 epsilon/(a kappa).                      (6)

Here epsilon uses the full trace norm, without the conventional factor 1/2.

3. ROBUST BLIND-COMPRESSION AND QUANTUM-MEMORY LOWER BOUND

Suppose T=D C factors through quantum memory of dimension at most m. Arbitrarily large classical records are allowed, provided the quantum dimension in every classical branch is at most m. Any composite Kraus operator has rank at most m. The elementary inequality

  |Tr K|^2 <= rank(K) Tr K^dagger K

then gives

  F_e(T) <= m/N.                                         (7)

Equivalently, its Choi state has Schmidt number at most m, so its overlap with the N-dimensional maximally entangled state is at most m/N.

Combining (5) and (7),

  m >= N {1 - (2/kappa)[1-S(T)/a]}.                       (8)

Under the full trace-norm preservation condition,

  m >= N [1 - 2 epsilon/(a kappa)].                       (9)

Consequently, a fixed finite family of states can require almost the entire ambient quantum dimension for robust single-specimen blind compression. A small number of possible preparations, a small input alphabet, a small Holevo-information upper bound, or zero quantum capacity of the preparation channel does not itself bound this blind quantum-memory cost.

The distinction is operational. A recipient who is told the preparation label can regenerate the state from classical memory. A recipient given only one unknown specimen generally cannot do so. A receiver interested in one preselected measurement may measure immediately and retain its classical result; (8) concerns preserving the joint ability to implement the later quantum output experiment through one common reconstruction channel.

4. HERALDING DOES NOT REMOVE THE DIMENSION WITNESS

Let Q be a completely positive trace-nonincreasing success map. Define

  s = Tr Q(I_N)/N,
  S(Q) = (a/N) sum_j p_j Tr[H_j Q(H_j)],
  F_e(Q) = (1/N^2) sum_alpha |Tr K_alpha|^2.

Operationally, all trials are counted. A failed trial contributes zero to the signed score and zero to the success indicator. The balanced input ensemble has average I_N/N, so s is exactly its average success probability, even when success depends on the input state.

The trace-decreasing counterpart of (4) is

  sum_alpha ||[K_alpha,H]||_2^2
    = 2 N s - 2 Re Tr[H Q(H)].

It yields

  F_e(Q) >= s - (2/kappa)[s-S(Q)/a].                      (10)

If every successful branch passes through at most m quantum dimensions, then

  F_e(Q) <= s m/N.

For s>0,

  m >= N {1 - (2/kappa)[1-S(Q)/(a s)]}.                   (11)

This statement does not assume uniform success on all input states. It does require all setting probabilities and all failures to be recorded. It does not establish the ordinary quantum capacity of a heralded device from its conditional score alone: small success probability and input-dependent normalisation must still be accounted for in any communication protocol.

5. EXPLICIT H_j FROM ANY KNOWN QUANTUM EXPANDER

Let U_1,...,U_k act on dimension D, and suppose their average conjugation map satisfies

  ||(1/k) sum_j U_j B U_j^dagger||_2 <= (1-gamma)||B||_2

for all traceless B, with 0<gamma<=1. Set N=2D and introduce a flag qubit f. Define

  H_X = X_f tensor I_D,
  H_Z = Z_f tensor I_D,
  H_j = |0><1|_f tensor U_j + |1><0|_f tensor U_j^dagger.

These are balanced Hermitian involutions. Use weights

  p_X=p_Z=1/4; p_j=1/(2k).

A sufficient explicit gap is

  kappa = gamma/5.                                       (12)

Proof of (12): write K=I_f tensor B+R, where Tr_f R=0, and remove the scalar part from B when necessary. The X,Z contributions obey

  (1/4)(||[K,H_X]||_2^2+||[K,H_Z]||_2^2) >= ||R||_2^2.

For P=I_f tensor B with Tr B=0, the original expander and its adjoint give

  sum_j ||[P,H_j]||_2^2 >= 2 k gamma ||P||_2^2.

Also ||[R,H_j]||_2<=2||R||_2. Apply

  ||A+B||_2^2 >= (1/5)||A||_2^2 - (1/4)||B||_2^2

to each commutator. The H_j contribution with weights 1/(2k) is at least

  (gamma/5)||P||_2^2 - (1/2)||R||_2^2.

Adding X,Z gives at least (gamma/5)||P||_2^2+(1/2)||R||_2^2, which proves (12) by orthogonality.

This proof works for non-Hermitian U_j and arbitrary complex K. It is not restricted to Hermitian error operators.

6. PREPARATION AND MEASUREMENT FROM ONE BIASED QUBIT

Every probe (2) for the lifted family is unitarily equivalent to

  omega_a = [(I_f+a Z_f)/2] tensor I_D/D.

For H_j define

  V_j = (|0><0|_f tensor U_j + |1><1|_f tensor I_D)
        (Hadamard_f tensor I_D).

Then V_j (Z_f tensor I_D) V_j^dagger=H_j. Thus tau_{j,+}=V_j omega_a V_j^dagger, and tau_{j,-} is obtained by flipping the flag before applying V_j. For H_X use a flag Hadamard; for H_Z use the identity. Measurement of H_j is implemented by V_j^dagger followed by a Z measurement of the flag.

No initially entangled reference is retained during the test. The tested register uses one qubit of non-maximal mixedness, independent of D. For a=1 it uses one pure qubit; its probes have rank N/2. For a<1 all probes are full rank. The controller, calibrated gates, state preparation and readout are separate resources: this is not a claim that fault-tolerant laboratory control needs only one clean qubit.

A standard explicit choice is the Gross–Eisert quantum Margulis expander: k=8 for admissible odd D, with contraction at most 5 sqrt(2)/8. The conservative lifted gap is then

  kappa = [1-5 sqrt(2)/8]/5 approximately 0.0232233.

There are 2(k+2)=20 states and 10 observables. Their algorithms give efficient circuits in log D; a desired finite total implementation error must also be included in gate synthesis. This construction is suitable for the efficient-circuit theorem but does not by itself give a constant-buffer streaming source. Do not replace poly(log D) circuit cost by constant physical control cost.

7. FINITE-DATA CERTIFICATE AND ERROR ACCOUNTING

For independent repetitions of a fixed channel, Y is in [-1,1]. If M trials yield mean S_hat, a conservative lower confidence bound is

  S >= S_hat - sqrt[2 log(1/delta)/M]

with probability at least 1-delta (a two-sided version replaces log(1/delta) by log(2/delta)). If the trusted preparation and measurement have a certified combined additive score bias at most nu, subtract nu as well. Put

  S_low = S_hat - sqrt[2 log(1/delta)/M] - nu.

Substitution into (5) and (8) gives explicit fidelity and memory certificates. For fixed a,kappa and desired infidelity eta, the general conservative sample cost is O((a kappa eta)^(-2) log(1/delta)), independent of N. Each trial still prepares, routes, stores, and measures a log N-qubit register.

When a=1, ideal trials never fail the signed test. Let q=(1-S)/2 be the probability that Y=-1. Equation (5) becomes

  1-F_e(T) <= 4q/kappa.

If there are zero test failures, the exact one-sided binomial bound is q<=1-delta^(1/M)<=log(1/delta)/M. Therefore

  M >= 4 log(1/delta)/(kappa eta)

suffices in this zero-failure regime to certify F_e>=1-eta. The clean-qubit resource changes the statistical regime; a<1 has an intrinsic baseline failure probability (1-a)/2. Variance-aware bounds interpolate between these cases, but the exact zero-failure claim above is only for a=1 and appropriately corrected trusted errors.

For heralding, estimate the unconditional S and average success s from the same complete trial log, and use conservative confidence bounds for their ratio in (11). A simple Hoeffding bound may cost order 1/s^2; variance-aware/binomial treatment can improve the dependence in favourable regimes. The success cost is not absent.

If the preparation/measurement circuit has g elementary gates and each has worst-case diamond error <=p, a simple telescoping bound gives nu=O(gp) plus state-preparation and readout errors. Thus a fixed physical gate error does not guarantee an asymptotically useful certificate as g grows. One must obtain a sufficiently accurate finite program, use suitable fault-tolerant control, or acquire a justified tighter implementation error model. The derivation assumes the supplied total nu is valid; it does not manufacture laboratory calibration from a gate description.

8. CAPACITY CONSEQUENCE FOR AN ORDINARY MEMORYLESS CHANNEL

For a trace-preserving N->N channel T with F_e(T)>=1-eta and 0<=eta<=1/2,

  Q(T) >= log_2 N - h_2(eta) - eta log_2(N^2-1),           (13)

where Q denotes the unassisted quantum capacity per use, and h_2 is binary entropy. The right side may be replaced by zero if negative.

Proof: let J=(id tensor T)(Phi_N), so J_A=I/N. The coherent information is I_c(J)=-S(A|B). Conditional entropy is concave, so I_c is convex. Isotropic U* tensor U twirling preserves overlap F with Phi_N, and every unitary conjugate has the same I_c; hence the original I_c is at least that of the twirled state. The latter has maximally mixed output and entropy h_2(1-F)+(1-F)log_2(N^2-1). This gives the right side of (13) at the maximally mixed input; the standard Lloyd–Shor–Devetak coding theorem makes coherent information an achievable asymptotic rate. The displayed upper-error substitution is monotone in the stated range.

This is a mathematical capacity consequence, not a throughput or finite-hardware advantage claim. It assumes repeated memoryless uses of the same channel, adequate encoding/decoding and buffering, and does not certify every future drifted implementation. It also does not give a dimension-independent diamond-error bound. A channel can corrupt a small subspace severely while retaining high entanglement fidelity, so universal worst-input acquisition must not be inferred from (13).

9. CONSTANT-SOURCE MIXED COLLISION-STREAM SEPARATION

For a strict chronological example, use the explicit 2026 depth-1 expander of Anshu, Balasubramanian, Haferkamp, Harrow and Tan, arXiv:2609.01605v1, Theorem 2.2. For n=3s qubits it gives 16 explicitly specified generators, each a depth-1 product of Pauli or CNOT gates, with interaction range at most four on a periodic one-dimensional chain. Its proved uniform Hilbert–Schmidt gap is

  gamma >= 1/(2*10^8).

The lift above has 18 Hermitian involutions, 36 probes, and the conservative uniform kappa>=10^-9. This constant is mathematically positive but extremely poor for useful experimental economics. A larger empirically observed finite-size gap cannot replace the proved uniform bound without another valid certificate.

Construct a source holding a 36-level label register L, one biased flag f, and a fixed-size buffer. The label specifies a probe (j,b). Every incoming data qubit is maximally mixed I/2; its external purifier is unavailable. The source first rotates/flips f as specified, then implements the flag-controlled circuit U_j while fresh data qubits stream through.

Because each U_j is one layer of disjoint bounded-range gates, its controlled version can be executed with a bounded sliding window. Hold the first four data qubits until the end to handle periodic wraparound gates, and use a constant sliding buffer for the remaining sites. Emit each completed site, emit the held boundary sites, and finally emit f using a SWAP with a fresh maximally mixed output bin. A fixed output permutation is used for every label; it has no effect on the compression lower bound. A loose allocation of sixteen buffered/flag qubits is ample, so total source dimension is bounded by 36*2^16, independent of n. This is deliberately a loose constant, not an optimised device design.

The controlled one-/two-data-qubit gates are fixed-arity operations because the label register and flag have fixed dimension. They admit finite bounded-Hamiltonian implementations; serial gate count and duration are O(n) at fixed gate strength, plus the finite terminal release. The classical control program has O(n) instructions and needs an external clock or programmed horizon. There is no hidden exponentially dense bath unitary in this construction.

For a source label state |j,b>, the complete emitted data-plus-flag state is exactly tau_{j,b}, on N=2^(n+1) dimensions. For a general input density matrix rho on L, tracing the retained source label gives

  B_n(rho)=sum_{j,b} rho_{(j,b),(j,b)} tau_{j,b}.

Thus B_n is entanglement breaking, with a fixed input dimension 36. Nevertheless any channel C_n compressing the emitted output into a final local register, with reconstruction D_n such that

  ||D_n C_n B_n - B_n||_diamond <= epsilon,

must in particular preserve every tau_{j,b}, and therefore requires

  dim(M_n) >= 2^(n+1) [1-2 epsilon/(a kappa)].             (14)

Unlimited classical side memory does not change this bound. For epsilon<=a kappa/4, at least n quantum bits of retained memory are necessary. The ordinary construction—retain every emitted qubit—uses n+1 qubits and is optimal to an additive constant in this regime.

This is a theorem for known finite time-programmed collision sources with mixed incoming bins. It is NOT a theorem already proved for an immutable time-homogeneous thermal Lindblad source or a fixed microscopic optical port. It does not establish any useful rate or noise tolerance for the numerical gap above. It demonstrates why a general fixed-source-dimension causal collector cannot be inferred merely by replacing pure incoming modes with arbitrary mixed ones.

When incoming bath bins are pure, a finite-dimensional sequential source has a uniformly bounded output support across the source/reference cut; known sequential compression can exploit that structure. The same controlled circuit with a pure bath does not inherit the mixed-state lower bound. The difference is not an increase in information capacity: the mixed output B_n is entanglement breaking. It is an increase in the quantum memory needed to preserve the complete blind output experiment.

10. COMPARISON WITH THE STRONGEST RELEVANT ORDINARY CONSTRUCTIONS

(a) Koashi–Imoto: exact and asymptotic mixed-state/hybrid compression already separates classical, quantum and redundant parts. Therefore 'a mixed ensemble may be irreducible' is not a new principle. Equations (8)-(14) supply a quantitative, dimension-uniform finite-error expander certificate and a concrete bounded-source streaming composition.

(b) Aharonov et al. 2014: quantum expanders were already used to test large maximally entangled states with constant communication. The gap/commutator argument is established machinery. The claimed new work here is its explicit conversion into blind mixed-state memory bounds and one-biased-qubit preparation/measurement, not the invention of expander rigidity or entanglement testing.

(c) Hofmann 2005: two complementary pure-state bases already give efficient process-fidelity bounds without an entangled reference. Random sampling can have dimension-independent trial count. This companion does not claim the first efficient quantum-channel certificate. Its possible resource improvement is constant input purity and a constant number of exact mixed-state preparations, with trusted entangling-control costs and weaker conservative constants charged.

(d) Reich–Gualdi–Koch 2013: a dimension-independent minimal set can algebraically characterise a target unitary, but their paper explicitly explains why the minimal mixed-state set does not provide useful general fidelity bounds. The positive expander gap is the quantitative feature that prevents the finite-error rigidity constant here from silently shrinking with dimension.

(e) Gross–Eisert 2008 supplies an explicit efficiently implementable constant-degree expander with a good analytic gap, suitable for the low-purity test. Anshu et al. 2026 supply the local depth-1 structure needed by the constant-buffer source counterexample. These are imported results and are not Astra discoveries.

(f) Direct storage is the strongest simple comparator for the obstruction: keeping n+1 output qubits saturates the lower bound to a constant additive number of qubits. The theorem identifies a genuine obstacle to universal compression; it does not offer an artificial 'improvement' over direct storage.

11. GOVERNING ASTRA CONTEXT

The current packets N1732 and N1733 were read through the GitHub connector. N1732 preserves finite causal collection of the complete canonical Stinespring channel for a known finite-dimensional Markov source with actual calibrated pure-vacuum ports, coherent lossless delivery, and paid receiver controls. It explicitly distinguishes d^2 receiver levels from efficient scaling in log d. N1733 is a whole-finite-bath theorem for an extreme CPTP source channel and is not automatically causal or a theorem for arbitrary thermal inputs.

The construction in this note respects those boundaries. Its source channel is not required to be extreme. Its incoming collision data are mixed. It neither invalidates the pure-port theorem nor acquires a coherent physical port from a reduced generator. The source/receiver split and all unavailable purifier assumptions are part of the operational model.

FINAL BOUNDARIES

The theorem and witness are conditional on the exact control and no-side-channel model stated above. The note does not prove constant-noise, fault-tolerant implementation, a laboratory calibration procedure from raw device data, a stationary thermal-Markov no-go, historical novelty, or a civilisation-changing technology already realised. It does provide a complete finite-error mathematical mechanism, its strongest ordinary comparator, a positive operational certificate, and an explicit reason that fixed source dimension and quantum capacity fail to determine universal mixed-output acquisition memory.
