## Post-update phase concentration and the conditional lattice explanation

In the source workbook's literal **After Update / Current 3 [A]** column, the supplied detector takes the start of each contiguous run above half that channel's maximum. It finds starts at 86, 145 and 506 seconds, giving phases 26, 25 and 26 modulo 60 and circular concentration `0.9987819015`. This descriptive short-trace calculation retains minute-phase locking already documented by the paper. It is not a population estimate, identification of every diagnostic event or a new causal experiment.

The supplement names the updated physical unit block 1, while the changed workbook column is labelled Current 3; that mapping remains unresolved and no physical-block identity is invented. Seven before-update timestamps and nine further observations in one channel are absent. The [extracted timestamp-preserving table](package/data/ups_fig7f_measured_current.csv) keeps those gaps; dropping them and applying an equally spaced FFT would be invalid. A 0.1 unit conversion for raw MODBUS files is not applied again to ampere-labelled workbook columns.

The separate [lattice calculation](package/lattice_randomization.txt) explains a possibility, not a fitted three-event model. For stationary ergodic event marks Q_n at times `nT+phi`, mean e and fixed kernel h, the line coefficient at `k/T` is `(e/T)H(k/T)exp(-2pi i k phi/T)`. Randomly omitting tasks while compensating their energy can preserve that line and add broadband noise. A changed pulse duration changes H and must be included. The measured trace does not establish iid marks or prove that this conditional model uniquely explains the update.

## Three-building profiles and attainable fundamental floor

The supplied Figure 6 workbooks contain 16 weekly-labelled profiles from each of three aggregate building meters: 48 profiles, 960 power values, 20 samples per profile at three-second spacing. Values are watts, not kilowatts. Labels run through 7 July, but the documented measurement acquisition ends 3 July 2024; the final label does not extend observed coverage.

For the sampled profiles, use `c_k=(1/L)sum_j p_j exp(-2pi i k j/L)`, so sinusoid peak amplitude is `2|c_k|`. In every week, one fundamental magnitude exceeds the other two combined. Even generously permitting arbitrary independent shifts of all three aggregate profiles, the best summed fundamental is therefore `max(2max_i|c_i|-sum_i|c_i|,0)`: recorded floors are 83.2045–99.1962 W in complex magnitude, or 166.4091–198.3924 W in sinusoidal peak amplitude. These are sampled-profile results, not uncertainty-qualified bounds for unmeasured continuous power. [Extracted profiles](package/data/building_measured_minute_waveforms.csv), [fundamental bounds](package/measured_fundamental_bounds.csv).

Exhaustive 20-slot weekly optimization reduces first-three-harmonic RMS by 3.2–23.8%, but two band optima increase absolute sampled peaks. Freezing the earliest week's relative offsets `[0,12,45]` seconds worsens band RMS in ten of the following fifteen weeks, median ratio `1.047742`, worst `1.140773`. This is retrospective out-of-week counterfactual replay, not a preregistered prospective test or independent experiment. It rejects an unqualified permanent-benefit claim for that frozen-profile calibration. It does not test an implemented absolute-task scheduler or recalibrating controller.

The transformers aggregate many loads; independently translating whole building traces is a generous counterfactual, not measured access to their constituent tasks. Three-second averages do not resolve subsecond signatures, millisecond execution or all spectral tails. A firmware deployment still needs individual or controllable-group waveform acquisition and authorized cadence-preserving control.

## Irish comparison, existing alternatives and attribution limits

The published monthly Irish waveform half-ranges recomputed in the source are 1.779898 mHz for December 2016 and 7.012353 mHz for December 2021, ratio about 3.940. Half-range, first-sample drop and fundamental amplitude are different observables. The underlying 2016 one-second raw data are not public; the supplied public artifact is the published monthly waveform. Different years and acquisition systems do not make this a controlled UPS-only causal effect. [Extracted public waveforms](package/data/ireland_measured_minute_waveforms.csv).

The authors' local UPS mechanism, clock intervention and firmware investigation are prior empirical findings. They do not establish an exclusive worldwide UPS explanation. Resonance, inertia, regulation, other clocked devices, demand changes, noise and resolution remain alternatives or modifiers. The evidence audit also reports substantial existing regulation-associated reductions in weekly summary amplitudes; these are descriptive comparisons, not isolated causal effects. More exposure at one noisy site cannot be represented as proof that no small disturbance exists.

The supplied scheduling compiler and synthetic figures acquire no new physical actuator or calibrated grid response. The conditional mathematical problem is resolved at its stated access/error/deadline scope; a deployed device and civilisation-scale practical advantage are not established.
