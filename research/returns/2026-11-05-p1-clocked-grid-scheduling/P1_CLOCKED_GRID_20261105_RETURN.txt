\frac{e}{T}H(k/T)

## 5. The complete construction for recurring waveform classes

Consider one class of devices with a common period \(T\). Device \(i\) performs a task with grid-facing power waveform

\[
e_i h(t),
\qquad e_i>0,
\qquad \int_{\mathbb R}|h(t)|\,dt=1.
\]

Here \(e_i\) is the waveform’s **absolute energy**, measured in joules. The kernel \(h\) may be signed, so the model can include a discharge-and-recharge signature with small or zero net energy. The complete response belongs in the kernel.

Define

\[
E=\sum_i e_i,\qquad
e_{\max}=\max_i e_i.
\]

Choose any ordering of the devices and assign

\[
\boxed{
\phi_i=
\frac{T}{E}
\left(
\sum_{j<i}e_j+\frac{e_i}{2}
\right).
}
\]

Each device then executes at \(nT+\phi_i\).

This places each event at the midpoint of its share of the period, with the share proportional to event energy.

### Why it is exactly optimal for cumulative imbalance

Let the weighted event train be

\[
\mu(t)=\sum_{i,n}e_i\delta(t-nT-\phi_i),
\]

and let \(F\) be a primitive of its deviation from uniform delivery:

\[
F'=\mu-E/T.
\]

Between events, \(F\) decreases linearly at rate \(E/T\). At event \(i\), it jumps upward by \(e_i\).

The assigned phases make the values immediately before and after that event

\[
F(\phi_i^-)=-e_i/2,\qquad
F(\phi_i^+)=+e_i/2,
\]

after choosing the additive constant appropriately. Therefore,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{osc}F=e_{\max}.}
\]

Every possible schedule contains a jump of at least \(e_{\max}\). Its oscillation cannot be smaller. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\text{phase schedules}}\operatorname{osc}F=e_{\max}.
}
\]

This is an exact optimum for the stated cumulative-energy metric. It does not assert that this schedule minimizes every possible plant’s peak response.

For a small example,

\[
(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)=(10,20,5,15)\ \mathrm J,
\qquad T=60\ \mathrm s,
\]

the phases are

\[
(6,24,39,51)\ \mathrm s,
\]

and the optimal event-energy span is \(20\ \mathrm J\).

### The exact timing-error cost

Now allow each individual execution to move by at most \(\delta\):

\[
t_{i,n}=nT+\phi_i+\xi_{i,n},
\qquad |\xi_{i,n}|\le\delta.
\]

Every event still occurs once and retains its energy.

A counting argument gives

\[
F_0(t-\delta)-\frac{E}{T}\delta
\le
F_\xi(t)
\le
F_0(t+\delta)+\frac{E}{T}\delta.
\]

On a sufficiently long horizon, the two extremes can be attained by advancing events near one extremum and delaying events near a distant extremum. Hence,

\[
\boxed{
\inf_{\phi}\sup_{|\xi_{i,n}|\le\delta}
\operatorname{osc}F_\xi
=
e_{\max}+2\frac{E}{T}\delta.
}
\]

This identifies the scaling constraint directly:

- Ideal indivisibility costs the **largest event**.
- Arbitrarily varying bounded timing errors cost **total fleet energy multiplied by timing uncertainty**.

The exact minimax statement concerns arbitrary per-event bounded errors. It is not the sharp law for one fixed offset per device. A constant common offset merely translates the entire schedule.

## 6. From event timing to the full physical waveform

The event-energy theorem is useful because it can be propagated through physical response kernels.

For several waveform classes \(c\), let each have a unit-\(L^1\) kernel \(h_c\), total event energy \(E_c\), maximum event energy \(e_{\max,c}\), and timing envelope \(\delta_c\).

Let a device’s actual repeated waveform differ from its model by \(r_i\), with

\[
\|r_i\|_1\le\eta_i.
\]

A primitive of the **actual mean-removed aggregate power** then has oscillation bounded by

\[
\boxed{
B=
\sum_c
\left(
e_{\max,c}+2\frac{E_c}{T}\delta_c
\right)
+
\sum_i
\left(1+\frac{2\delta_i}{T}\right)\eta_i.
}
\]

The proof uses convolution and the triangle inequality. Convolution with a signed unit-\(L^1\) kernel preserves the centered bound. The residual term includes timing uncertainty acting on the residual waveform itself.

Three limits are important.

First, the number of waveform classes matters. Giving every device its own class replaces the useful sum of a few class maxima with approximately the fleet’s total event energy.

Second, the residual model above is a **fixed repeated error**. Arbitrary errors that change every cycle need an additional cumulative-error contract. A bound on each cycle’s error alone does not prevent cumulative bias from growing indefinitely.

Third, this is an energy-imbalance bound. It does not by itself bound instantaneous power or rate of change of frequency. A very narrow pulse can have small energy and a large peak. Stronger waveform regularity is needed for those quantities.

### A frequency-response guarantee

Suppose the incremental frequency response to aggregate power is described by a **supplied, calibrated, stable causal linear response** \(g\), with finite total variation. Integration by parts gives

\[
\boxed{
\|\Delta f_{\mathrm{fleet}}\|_\infty
\le
\frac{B}{2}
\left(
|g(0^+)|+
\operatorname{TV}_{(0,\infty)}g
\right).
}
\]

This accommodates signed and resonant responses: resonance increases the response’s total variation and therefore the bound.

For the illustrative first-order model

\[
M\dot f+D f=-u,\qquad M,D>0,
\]

the result becomes

\[
\boxed{\|\Delta f_{\mathrm{fleet}}\|_\infty\le B/M.}
\]

Here \(M\) has units \(\mathrm{W\,s/Hz}\).

This bounds the fleet’s incremental contribution under the stated model. Initial conditions, uncontrolled disturbances and other sources remain separate. Spatially different injections require appropriate effective-response classes or a network model.

The paper’s static power–frequency relationship does not identify the required dynamic response \(g\). I therefore have not converted the scheduling calculation into an invented millihertz guarantee for a real grid.

I selected a documented failure of timing control in electricity systems: periodic diagnostic loads can remain synchronized to the clock even after their execution intervals become irregular. The observational starting point is a recent study of minute-periodic disturbances in twelve electricity grids, with a local investigation tracing UPS battery diagnostics through the electrical network to a 110-kV connection. The authors already identify the local mechanism and discuss timing remedies. That explanation is the starting evidence here. 
Nature Energy

The strongest completed result is an exact scheduling law for indivisible recurring tasks, a proof of its optimality for cumulative energy imbalance, and a quantitative account of how timing errors, waveform uncertainty and grid dynamics limit the benefit. I implemented an exact-arithmetic schedule compiler and tested the construction alongside a reanalysis of the public measurements.

The public data also supplied an important negative result: a seemingly successful phase adjustment, optimized on the first week, worsened the selected disturbance measure in ten of the following fifteen weeks. This is a retrospective calculation, not a field intervention, but it prevents an unjustified claim that one calibration is enough.

The completed work is available here:

clocked_grid_research.txt — full scientific account, proofs, empirical analysis, limitations and provenance.
clocked_grid_research.zip — reproducibility package containing the source workbooks, extraction and analysis code, scheduling compiler, certificates and figures.

The mathematical capability is established under explicit assumptions. A commissioned fleet, historical priority for the combined construction, and a civilisation-changing practical outcome remain unestablished.

1. What the observations actually establish

Hartmann and colleagues’ final Nature Energy paper, published on 30 October 2026, reports minute-periodic patterns across twelve grids. Its local investigation is unusually valuable because it goes beyond finding a spectral peak:

It measures battery diagnostic currents and associated AC-side power fluctuations.
It follows the disturbance through transformers toward the grid connection.
It obtains manufacturer confirmation of the self-tests.
It examines changes in clock configuration and firmware.
It reports a firmware modification that provides only partial mitigation. 
Nature Energy

This provides a real causal opening: digitally coordinated equipment can introduce coherent disturbances into a much larger physical system.

It does not establish that UPS diagnostics explain every minute-periodic disturbance worldwide. Other clocked loads can contribute. Grid inertia, governor dynamics, resonance, demand composition and control rules determine how a particular power disturbance appears in frequency. A clocked source and a resonant response can coexist. The final paper discusses these distinctions; overlooking them would manufacture an anomaly that the evidence does not require.

Systematic effects that matter

Several details materially constrain interpretation:

The before-update trace has missing timestamps and additional missing current values. I preserved those gaps.
Raw MODBUS currents require a scale conversion; the figure workbook already labelled in amperes must not receive that conversion again.
The building profiles have three-second resolution. They cannot reveal subsecond pulse shapes or validate millisecond dispatch.
DC battery current alone does not specify the complete grid-facing waveform. Conversion losses, recharge and control responses belong in the electrical disturbance.
The source does not supply uncertainty bounds sufficient to turn the sampled profiles into certified continuous-time waveforms.

Those limitations are carried through the calculations.

2. The scientific question

The evidence led to this question before Astra was consulted:

Can a heterogeneous fleet perform every indivisible recurring diagnostic within its valid service cadence while keeping its aggregate physical disturbance bounded as the fleet grows—and exactly what timing precision, calibration, controllability and execution resources does that require?

This is sharper than “would randomization help?” It asks for a complete capability:

Every required task still executes.
Its physical waveform remains an admissible diagnostic.
Heterogeneous task sizes are accounted for.
Timer resolution, clock errors and waveform errors are charged.
Installation, reboot and schedule migration are handled.
A bound on the scheduled load is connected to a bound on the physical system’s response.

The useful organizing variable turns out to be cumulative energy imbalance: how far the fleet’s delivered event energy gets ahead of or behind a uniform schedule.

That variable connects the indivisibility of a diagnostic task to a physical response bound.

Three limits are important.

Calibration

Calibration must cover the full AC response, including recharge and its dependence on operating state, temperature, loading and firmware.

Arbitrary independent systematic errors add in the worst case. More devices do not automatically average them away.

Correlation structure can help. A constant common clock offset only shifts the schedule. A common positive gain within a waveform class rescales the class bound once. The adverse independent-error calculation should not be misrepresented as a universal law for every error model.

Astra was consulted after the empirical question had been formulated, at

I read the relevant packets and their actual governing sources. Two lessons were useful:

Neither source supplies an automatic theorem for this fleet. In particular, reciprocal thermal-network assumptions do not transfer to an arbitrary electricity grid. Immutable governing-source URLs and hashes are included in the report.

The general understanding gained is precise: the control opportunity is governed by the largest indivisible task, while imperfect execution reintroduces dependence on total fleet scale. A fleet can therefore have a bounded ideal scheduling disturbance and still lose that advantage through coarse timing, unmodelled waveform changes, restricted control access or startup behavior.

This resolves a substantial conditional control problem and provides executable machinery for it. The remaining physical question is whether a manufacturer-authorized fleet, calibrated at its actual AC interface and operated across relevant conditions, satisfies the assumptions tightly enough to obtain a useful grid-frequency guarantee. The public observations do not supply that commissioning evidence, so the completed mathematical result should not be promoted into a demonstrated infrastructure breakthrough.
