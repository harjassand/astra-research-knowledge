EVIDENCE AUDIT: CLOCKED POWER DISTURBANCES

Scope and provenance

This audit uses the final Hartmann et al. Nature Energy article (30 October 2026), its published supplement, and its numerical source workbooks. The versioned public data deposit is Zenodo 21997337, concept record 17366692. Source files and SHA-256 hashes are recorded in ../grid_observations_data/data_manifest.json. Figure 3 was downloaded directly from the publisher; Figures 5–7 were extracted from the versioned source-data ZIP by byte range, with member CRC verification. These are published measurements and authors’ processed summaries, not experiments performed in this run.

Units, dates, and the accessible operation

Visual inspection of the published Figure 6 confirms its vertical axis is Power (W), not kW. Its three panels are Building 3/PQ023, Building 1/PQ007, and Building 4/PQ051. The source workbook contains 16 weekly-labelled profiles per meter, hence 48 profiles and 960 numeric power samples. Each profile has 20 points at seconds 0,3,...,57. Labels run from 24 March through 7 July 2024. Supplementary Note 7 states the actual acquisition period MP1 was 24 March 00:00:00 through 3 July 23:59:57; therefore the last weekly label must not be represented as measurements continuing through 7 July. The first and last labelled weeks may be partial.

These are averaged, aggregate transformer-level waveforms, not independently characterized single-device pulse responses. Arbitrarily translating every building waveform, independently controlling all three meters, or creating many exact copies is ONLY a generous replay model. Neither the source data nor our calculations establish that those operations are physically available. Actual documented intervention is narrower: Building 3 EMS clock settings were changed, and a manufacturer updated one battery block. A field implementation of a proposed phase scheduler still requires an authorized firmware interface, maintenance-deadline compliance, individual waveform calibration, and checks for feedback, state dependence, and network interactions. Replay does not earn those requirements.

The half-range ratio is approximately 3.940. The paper’s shorthand description of a roughly 3-to-12 mHz dip must not be substituted for half-range, first-sample drop, or fundamental amplitude. Underlying 2016 Irish one-second raw data are not public: they were supplied under an EirGrid agreement. The published monthly waveform is public. Different acquisition systems and years also prevent treating this comparison as a controlled UPS-only causal estimate.

Firmware trace and retained phase concentration

Figure 7 panel f contains nominal 0–600-second before/after windows. Using contiguous samples above half that channel’s maximum as a transparent detector of large current events:

After Update / Current 3 [A]        15.05          86,145,506                  26,25,26
After Update / Current 1 [A]        12.40          26+60k, k=0,...,9            all 26

For the three large events in the first row, the circular start-phase resultant is |mean exp(2*pi*i*t/60)| = 0.998781902. This confirms the specific short trace retains nearly identical minute phase despite irregular gaps. It is not a statistical estimate from a large fleet, not an identification of every diagnostic event, and not new empirical confirmation beyond the published evidence. The sampling interval limits onset resolution.

There are seven missing before-update timestamps: 64,198,351,412,518,528,578 seconds. Current 1 additionally lacks values at 91,98,105,113,281,294,404,525,536 seconds. Thus its finite count is 585, compared with 594 observed timestamps; the other before channel has 594 finite values. Both after channels have 601 values. Dropping gaps and applying an ordinary equally spaced FFT would be invalid. The extraction preserves gaps and performs no imputation.

Supplementary Note 9 says tests become irregular, last longer, and retain approximately the same consumed energy overall; it explicitly notes residual minute-phase locking and possible resonant excitation by broadband forcing. It identifies the updated physical unit as battery block 1, whereas the visibly changed workbook trace is labelled Current 3. This mapping remains unresolved. Keep literal source labels. The deposit warns that original UPS_DC_#.csv MODBUS values require a factor 0.1 for amperes; that instruction must not be blindly applied again to columns already labelled [A].

Other accounts and limits

Supplementary Notes 2–3 consider hydropower resonance and operational correlations; Notes 5 and 12 retain frequency-control and inertia changes. They support amplification of external forcing, not unique attribution of all global signals to UPSs. Note 7 specifies 3-second campus sampling and instrument accuracy at the fundamental electrical frequency; this does not certify millisecond task timing or unmeasured high-frequency waveform content. Note 9 reports approximately 5–7 seconds/day phase movement with NTP enabled. The source’s post-transition daily modal indices generally advance two 3-second bins/day, consistent with that observation.

Existing regulation-based mitigation is quantitatively substantial. From Figure 7e, the median of 52 weekly summary amplitudes falls from 6.038933 mHz (Scandinavia 2022) to 3.797932 (2025), and from 6.906457 (Australia 2019) to 3.925854 (2022). These are descriptive comparisons of published weekly summaries, not isolated causal effects. Any new source-scheduling claim must acknowledge this successful existing alternative.
