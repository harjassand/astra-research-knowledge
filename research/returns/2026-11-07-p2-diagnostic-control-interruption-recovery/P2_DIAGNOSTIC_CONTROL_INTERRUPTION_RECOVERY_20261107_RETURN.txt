Here \(x_+=\max(x,0)\). Both acceptance probabilities are rational numbers in \([0,1]\).

### Why the error is exponentially small

The hot acceptance probability per trial is
\[
A_N=\mathbb E(r-\overline Y_c)_+
=A+e_c,
\]
where
\[
e_c=\mathbb E(\overline Y_c-r)_+\ge0.
\]
Since the true mean lies at least \(d_c\) below \(r\),
\[
e_c
\le \Pr(\overline Y_c>r)
\le e^{-2N_c d_c^2}.
\]
Similarly,
\[
B_N=B+e_h,
\qquad
0\le e_h\le e^{-2N_h d_h^2}.
\]
These are the standard bounded-sample concentration estimates of [Hoeffding, 1963](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830).

In particular, the trial success probability remains at least \(g\). The hot-label bias of the uncapped finite-sample race obeys
\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|\frac{A_N}{A_N+B_N}-\frac{A}{A+B}\right|
&=
\frac{|Be_c-Ae_h|}
{(A+B)(A+B+e_c+e_h)}\\
&\le\frac{e_c+e_h}{g}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Choose
\[
N_c=
\left\lceil
\frac{\log(2/(g\beta))}{2d_c^2}
\right\rceil,
\qquad
N_h=
\left\lceil
\frac{\log(2/(g\beta))}{2d_h^2}
\right\rceil.
\]
Then this label bias is at most \(\beta\).

The sample averages are used inside an acceptance probability whose expectation is linear apart from the rare clipping event. **They are not used to fit the rates to accuracy \(\epsilon/n\).** This is why the required sample count depends logarithmically on inverse preparation error.

The controller always returns a command; no postselection is used.
