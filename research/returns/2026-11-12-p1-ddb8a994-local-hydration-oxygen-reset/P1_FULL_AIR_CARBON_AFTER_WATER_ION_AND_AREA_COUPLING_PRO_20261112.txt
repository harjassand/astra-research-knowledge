1. A covering that passes atmospheric CO2 into a wet reactive interface is different from an internal ion-conducting separator exposed to concentrated product. For constant gas permeances Kc and Kw, a perfect CO2 sink, dilute plug flow and 50 percent capture, the log-mean CO2 driving pressure is P*x/[2*ln(2)], about 31.06 Pa at 425 ppm and 1 atm. The evaporative mass ratio is (Mw/Mc)*(Kw/Kc)*Delta_pw/p_logmean. If the internal water activity were 0.9, limiting evaporation to 1 kg/kg would require CO2/water-vapour permeance ratios of about 15.4 at 20 C/40 percent RH, 25.1 at 25 C/30 percent RH and 39.2 at 30 C/20 percent RH. At the illustrative 0.19 mol/m2/h CO2 flux, the same favorable screen needs about 5080 GPU CO2 permeance. Finite interfacial CO2, support/gas-film resistance, defects and real humidity profiles still need payment. Liquid-water rejection or contact angle supplies no such vapor selectivity. Lower water activity is a possible change of physical chemistry, not a supplied conductivity or free desiccant.

2. For a hypothetical homogeneous inner separator, let a=Pc*Delta_p for molecular CO2 backdiffusion, L its thickness, sigma its actual through-plane conductivity, J NET carbon flux and eta useful charge efficiency. If the actual chemistry transfers z electron equivalents per gross forward CO2, j=(zF/eta)*(J+a/L), and its ohmic work per NET mole is
(zF/eta)^2*[J*L+2a+a^2/(J*L)]/sigma.
It is at least 4(zF/eta)^2*a/sigma within this model, with equality at L=a/J, where backdiffusion equals NET output. That equality is not a total-energy optimum. A thinner layer trades resistance against back-leakage; a multilayer or different chemistry may change the model. The relevant transport properties must coexist in the actual carbonated, humid, biased working material.

3. In an illustrative two-electron pump, perfect useful charge would already be 1218 Ah/kg, or 50.75 A average for 1 kg/day. The 0.50 kWh/kg limit permits 0.4105 V effective full-cycle energy per useful equivalent charge before auxiliaries. Processing air through an illustrative 50 Pa pressure drop at 60 percent fan efficiency uses about 0.0606 kWh/kg and leaves 0.3608 V on that reference-charge convention. An alternating battery process must instead integrate its actual terminal power, both cycle legs and real energy recovery; net signed charge alone is insufficient. Establish the electron/ion/water stoichiometry rather than assuming z=2.

These are unquoted thresholds, not actual costs or lifetimes. Higher flux reduces those area terms but changes current, voltage, water transport, airflow and degradation.
