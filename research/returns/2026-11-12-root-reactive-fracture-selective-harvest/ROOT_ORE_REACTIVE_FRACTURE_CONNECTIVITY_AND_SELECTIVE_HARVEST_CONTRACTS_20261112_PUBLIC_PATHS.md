3. Hypothesis: conversion-created contact plus selective release

An alternative physical operation is a shallow, pressure-contacted ore layer against a perforated, conducting product-iron support. Cathodic metal growth may create bridges and fracture paths within dense oxide. A subsequent partial pressure release and forward liquid pulse might remove liberated gangue while connected metal remains attached. Recontact could restore otherwise isolated oxide fragments; final metal and any support transferred to product require a separately paid harvest/seed return. This is a testable integrated mechanism hypothesis, not an available material or an achieved device.

The competing effects are central. Fracture exposes oxide but can destroy electrical contact. Confinement can preserve contact while closing liquid paths or trapping gangue. A metallic bridge can retain desired metal and also bind an unacceptable composite. A flush can remove gangue and simultaneously export fine Fe or unreduced oxide. Changing current, pressure, liquid flux and residence independently is not a constructed joint operating point. Initial seed corrosion is not ore conversion. Merely naming a moving bed, magnet or reactive fracture does not construct this operation.

An inventory model must track at least solid FeIII/II/0, dissolved FeIII/II, attached and detached metal, each gangue/P/S stream, reduced seed, gas and electrolyte/reagent states. For Fe species define reduction equivalents R=sum_i(3-z_i)n_i. Metallic Fe contributes3n0; FeII contributes n2. With explicitly inventoried starting states, no uncounted oxygen return/other redox and measured hydrogen, cathodic electron balance is Q/F=DeltaR+2nH2. Increased reduced-oxide capacity alone is not produced metal. Corrosion-generated hydrogen, collector/seed changes, other electroactive elements and reverse reactions must be assigned their actual terms rather than hidden in an apparent current efficiency.
