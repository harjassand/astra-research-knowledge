1. THE COMPLETE QUESTION AND ITS INPUT CONTRACT

Can finitely many certified linear-action experiments acquire a finite
representation sufficient for all later zero-initial-state predictions
and monotone interventions through fixed ports of an incompletely known
dissipative medium, with accuracy and actual costs specified in advance?

Answer: yes, to a specified nonzero operator error, under the following
contract. Exact reuse has a different information requirement described
in Section 11. This is a complete result for this source/task class.

Source: a stationary real n by n matrix H satisfies

  (H+H^T)/2 >= a I,       ||H||_2 <= b,       0 < a <= b.

The positive rational a and b are supplied global certificates. A faster
branch is available if H is also promised symmetric. The ports P select
r known coordinate positions, 1 <= r <= n, fixed before acquisition.
Reorder coordinates into port y and hidden h, writing

  H = [ A  U ; V  D ].

No sparsity, low rank or small minimal realization is assumed. Subblocks
have norm at most b, and D has symmetric part at least a I.

Information: a query is a rational n-vector q with ||q||_2 <= 1 and a
precision request p. The report is an n-vector of dyadic rationals with
certified Euclidean error at most eta from Hq. All n returned coordinates
are counted. Acquisition can excite and read the entire medium; this is
not an acquisition theorem from port-only physical measurements.

Finite realization: for a rational source matrix, a supplied finite
instrument programme evaluates every row dot product exactly and rounds
each coordinate to the nearest 2^(-p). Consequently eta can be chosen
as n*2^(-p-1), conservatively bounding sqrt(n)*2^(-p-1). Its matrix input,
coefficient bits, exact arithmetic, output bits and work are charged.
The acquisition routine receives only the reports, not those entries.
An alternative finite action programme can be used only with its actual
proved accuracy and paid cost interface. A unit-cost solver oracle is
not part of the result. N997 supplies the retained precedent for this
kind of checked software accuracy/cost interface.

The structural source promises are not learned physical laws. With a
fixed native Euclidean error delta, substitute delta plus quantization
error for eta throughout. The resulting certificate remains valid when
its inequalities hold, but arbitrary accuracy is no longer guaranteed.
This refinable software interface differs from the predecessor theorem
with a fixed positive native noise floor. A coordinatewise native error
must first be converted to a Euclidean bound; it is not the same number.

Later tasks: after acquisition, add any finite number q of known states
w and choose

  y' = -A y - U h - F_y(y,w) + u_y,
  h' = -V y - D h,
  w' = -D_c w - F_w(y,w) + u_w.                    (1)

Here D_c is a positive rational diagonal matrix with minimum entry d>0.
When q=0, omit it. Put z=(y,w). F is locally Lipschitz, F(0)=0 and

  (z-z')^T [F(z)-F(z')] >= 0                      (2)

for every pair z,z'. A broad finite input syntax is F=Jz+grad psi(z),
where J is a rational skew matrix and psi is a globally convex rational
polynomial with grad psi(0)=0. General polynomial monotonicity is a
decidable real-algebraic condition, at the paid cost of that decision.
The executed syntax uses nonnegative sums of rank-one quadratic and
quartic potentials, so monotonicity follows directly from the syntax.

The inputs are finite rational piecewise-constant functions, the horizon
is any positive rational T, and all physical and retained-memory states
start at zero. Reset and the zero hidden state are substantive premises.
An unknown hidden initial state supplies an additional unknown forcing
term and is not silently covered. The later readouts concern y and w,
not the original hidden coordinates or the artificial memory states.

The mathematical error result extends to locally square-integrable
inputs and locally Lipschitz monotone F. A terminating numerical
algorithm needs the finite polynomial data or another supplied effective
local evaluation/modulus contract. It is not inferred from abstract
continuity alone.

2. WHAT INFORMATION IS SUFFICIENT FOR THE WHOLE TASK FAMILY?

Elimination of the zero-initial hidden state gives

  h(t) = -int_0^t exp(-D(t-s)) V y(s) ds,
  Z_H y = y' + A y - K*y,
  K(t) = U exp(-Dt) V.                             (3)

Consequently (1) is equivalently

  Z_H y + F_y(z) = u_y,
  w' + D_c w + F_w(z) = u_w.                       (4)

The port memory, rather than a single equilibrium response, is the
relevant source information. The exact linear port transfer matrix is

  G_H(s) = P^T (s I + H)^(-1) P,
  G_H(s)^(-1) = s I + A - U(s I + D)^(-1)V.         (5)

For this class, equality of G_H is equivalent to equality of A and K,
and to equality of every later response in (1). To prove the forward
direction, expand the inverse transfer at large s to recover A and the
Laplace transform of K, then use uniqueness of that transform. Equation
(4) and uniqueness of the later ODE give identical attached responses.
Conversely, take q=0, F=0 and all finite piecewise-constant inputs: their
responses determine the impulse kernel and therefore G_H. Rational
step inputs and rational sample times suffice by continuity/analyticity.

This is an exact task equivalence relation, not a free kernel oracle.
Hidden orthogonal changes of coordinates preserve it, so identification
of all physical matrix entries is unnecessary even for an exact task
representation. Its degree can nevertheless grow with n. Section 3
constructs and pays for a finite approximation directly from actions.

For every absolutely continuous port path y with y(0)=0, reconstruct h
as in (3). Integration by parts gives the crucial dissipation identity

  int y^T Z_H y
    = (||y(T)||^2 + ||h(T)||^2)/2
      + int (y,h)^T H (y,h)
    >= ||y(T)||^2/2 + a ||y||_(2,T)^2.             (6)

The integrations are from 0 to T. This uses the symmetric part of H,
not reciprocity. It will control all later monotone attachments at once.

3. FINITE MEMORY EXPANSION WITH AN ALL-HORIZON ERROR

Choose rational c>0 and 0<=rho<1 so R=I-D/c has norm at most rho.
Two explicit choices are

  symmetric: c=(a+b)/2,       rho=(b-a)/(b+a);
  general:   c=b^2/a,         rho=1-a^2/(2b^2).    (7)

For the general choice,
  ||I-D/c||^2 <= 1-2a/c+b^2/c^2 = 1-a^2/b^2,
and sqrt(1-t)<=1-t/2 proves (7). If a=b, the source constraints force
H=aI by equality in Cauchy-Schwarz; no experiment is needed.

Define M_j=U R^j V. Then

  K(t) = exp(-ct) sum_(j>=0) (ct)^j/j! M_j,
  ||M_j|| <= b^2 rho^j.                            (8)

Every nonnegative scalar basis kernel exp(-ct)(ct)^j/j! has integral
1/c. If Ahat and Mhat_0,...,Mhat_(k-1) are retained, define Khat by the
truncated series and Zhat=d/dt+Ahat-Khat*. A rigorous operator error is

  E = ||A-Ahat||
      + b^2 rho^k/[c(1-rho)]
      + (1/c) sum_(j<k) ||M_j-Mhat_j||.             (9)

Young's convolution inequality, extending paths by zero when needed,
proves for every horizon

  ||(Z_H-Zhat)y||_(2,T) <= E ||y||_(2,T).           (10)

This estimates the difference of impedances, including its sign. No
finite frequency sampling, fitted decay law, orthogonal-dynamics oracle,
or limiting experimental convergence is invoked.

Finite realization: for j=0,...,k-1 retain r-vector filters

  s_0'=-c s_0+y,
  s_j'=-c s_j+c s_(j-1)  (j>=1),
  yhat'=-Ahat yhat+sum Mhat_j s_j-F_y(yhat,what)+u_y,
  what'=-D_c what-F_w(yhat,what)+u_w.              (11)

The base representation has r(k+1) states and r^2(k+1) scalar matrix
coefficients, before a later q-state attachment. It is causal and can
be executed without a new source action. The filters are auxiliary
states, not purported physical hidden-state reconstructions.

4. ACQUIRE THE COEFFICIENTS WITH FINITE PRECISION

The following construction is exactly the one in memory.py.

(i) Query each unit port coordinate. The port portions give A columns,
and the hidden portions give V columns. Divide the latter by b and
round internally to the same dyadic precision p, obtaining What_0.

(ii) For j=0,...,k-1, and for each of the r columns of What_j, query
q=(0,What_j/2). Twice b times its port report is the corresponding
column of Mhat_j. The next hidden column is

  What_(j+1) = round_p(What_j-2*hidden_report/c).   (12)

(iii) Symmetrize the estimates only in the symmetric branch. For a
general dissipative source they must remain nonsymmetric.

Here is a simultaneous finite-error proof. Exact W_j=R^j V/b has each
column norm at most 1. Let eta bound both native report error and the
Euclidean internal rounding error. Then

  e_0 <= eta/b+eta,
  e_(j+1) <= rho e_j+(2/c+1)eta,
  e_j <= C_w eta,
  C_w = 1+1/b + (1+2/c)/(1-rho).                   (13)

Choose C_w eta<=1/2. Inductively each queried column divided by 2 has
norm at most 3/4; no illicit large query is needed. Each moment column
has error at most b^2 C_w eta+2b eta. Bounding spectral norms by r
times the largest column bound, including any symmetrization, gives

  ||A-Ahat|| <= r eta,
  ||M_j-Mhat_j|| <= r b eta(b C_w+2).              (14)

Let target=min(requested_epsilon,a/4). Take the first integer k with

  b^2 rho^k/[c(1-rho)] <= target/2,

unless r=n, in which case the hidden kernel is absent and k=0. Put

  C_data = r + (k r b/c)(b C_w+2).

Choose the first nonnegative p satisfying

  eta=n*2^(-p-1) <= min(1/(2C_w),target/(2C_data)). (15)

Equations (9),(13),(14) now give E<=target<=a/4. This finite rational
loop is the full acquisition plan, including its error budget. It uses
r(k+1) source actions and returns n r(k+1) scalar reports. All rounding
and input normalization are included. The last filter-update columns
are computed even though no subsequent moment uses them; the executed
work includes that implementation choice.

With kappa=b/a and a nontrivial hidden block,

  symmetric k = O(kappa log(2+b^2/(a target))),
  general   k = O(kappa^2 log(2+b^2/(a target))).   (16)

An explicit bound follows by replacing rho^k with exp(-(1-rho)k).
The algorithm itself compares rational powers, so it needs no real-log
oracle. No optimal approximation order or comparison advantage over
rational Krylov/balanced truncation is asserted.

5. ONE CERTIFICATE FOR EVERY LATER MONOTONE ATTACHMENT

Let

  alpha=min(a,d),       alphahat=min(a-E,d),

using alpha=a, alphahat=a-E when q=0. Because E<a, alphahat>0.
From (6),(10) the emulator satisfies

  int y^T Zhat y >= ||y(T)||^2/2+(a-E)||y||_2^2.

Together with (2), its equation yields

  ||zhat||_(2,T) <= ||u||_(2,T)/alphahat,          (17)
  sup_(t<=T)||zhat(t)|| <= ||u||_(2,T)/sqrt(2 alphahat).

These estimates also give global existence. Local existence/uniqueness
holds for the finite polynomial ODE. Before any possible finite escape,
the observed states are bounded by (17); the stable filter cascade is
then bounded. Continuation excludes finite escape. The true system has
the analogous stronger bound with alpha.

Subtract the two equations and write e=z-zhat. Apply (6) to the port
error, add the attachment energy, and use monotonicity of F:

  ||e(T)||^2/2 + alpha ||e||_(2,T)^2
    <= E ||e||_(2,T) ||yhat||_(2,T).              (18)

Therefore

  ||z-zhat||_(2,T) <= E ||u||_(2,T)/(alpha alphahat),
  sup_(t<=T)||z(t)-zhat(t)||
    <= E ||u||_(2,T)/(sqrt(2alpha) alphahat).       (19)

The endpoint estimate maximizes R v-alpha v^2 at v=R/(2alpha), with
R=E||yhat||_2. It is not an unproved small-gain assertion.

The same acquired representation and the same E cover all the allowed
later nonlinearities, couplings, added states, input programmes and
horizons. Stronger nonlinear gain does not appear in (19); it can still
make numerical evaluation much more costly. For fixed input energy the
bound is horizon-independent. For a constant-amplitude input continued
for time T, that energy grows as sqrt(T); do not call its bound constant.

The certificate is deterministic and simultaneous over every compatible
source. Selecting a later task adaptively using earlier emulator results
does not invalidate it. Each actual evaluation still incurs its full
computational cost. No ability to choose new physical ports, measure
hidden states, or apply active nonmonotone feedback is implied.

6. CERTIFY AN ACTUAL NUMERICAL PATH, NOT JUST AN EXACT ODE

Let a continuous proposed trajectory (zbar,sbar_0,...,sbar_(k-1)) start
at zero and be piecewise polynomial on the input intervals. Its ordinary
observed residual in (11) is r_obs. Its filter residuals are

  r_0=sbar_0'+c sbar_0-ybar,
  r_j=sbar_j'+c sbar_j-c sbar_(j-1).

Reconstruct exact filters driven by ybar and subtract. The first stable
filter has L2 gain 1/c and each succeeding cascade stage has gain 1.
Consequently an effective observed residual bound is

  R_num = ||r_obs||_2
          +(1/c) sum_(j<k) ||Mhat_j|| sum_(i<=j)||r_i||_2.  (20)

Now reconstruct the true hidden source response driven by ybar. The
remaining defect in the exact source equation has norm at most

  Delta = R_num + E ||ybar||_2
        <= R_num + E ||zbar||_2.                  (21)

Apply the same energy argument directly between the exact source and
this proposed path. It gives

  sup_t ||z(t)-zbar(t)|| <= Delta/sqrt(2alpha),
  ||z-zbar||_2 <= Delta/alpha.                    (22)

Thus the final trajectory certificate includes source ambiguity AND
numerical integration error. It does not rely on the nominal solver's
order, an estimated truncation constant, or agreement with a reference.

Executed checker: ordinary floating RK4 proposes nodes only. Nodes are
rounded to dyadic rationals. Exact rational field values at both ends
define a cubic Hermite segment. For each polynomial residual on its
unit interval, the sum of absolute coefficient values bounds its sup
norm. Summing h times squared coordinate bounds gives a rational L2
bound. Square roots are rounded upwards with integer isqrt. The exact
coefficient residuals have degree at most nine for the executed cubic
loads. All coefficients, segments, input switches and initial values
are retained. The checker also binds the path to the requested load
and input programme. A poor proposal merely gives a poor certificate.

The separately computed full-source trajectories are validation only.
They have their own exact residual certificates and are never inputs

7. A COMPLETE LATER EVALUATION ALGORITHM AND ITS COST

The executed RK4 proposal is not asserted to terminate successfully at
every possible polynomial input. A complete fallback exists as follows.
This subsection specifies it mathematically; the general fallback and
general CAD optimizer have NOT been executed as software here.

Given finite piecewise-constant rational u, compute a rational upper
bound B on ||u||_2 and a rational R_z>B/sqrt(2alphahat). Formula (17)
bounds every observed coordinate by R_z. Each memory filter has sup
norm at most R_z/c. Choose a rational coordinate box K containing these
bounds with positive slack. The dimension is m=r(k+1)+q.

For the explicitly represented polynomial field on each input interval,
coefficient sums bound a rational vector-field norm M>=1 and a rational
Euclidean Lipschitz constant L>=1 on K. For example, bound each monomial
and derivative by its coefficient magnitude times powers of the box
radius and take dimension factors rather than uncomputed spectral norms.

Use projected Euler, with dyadic rounding before final coordinatewise
projection back into K. If every step has length at most h, there are
N steps, and each rounding vector has norm at most gamma, then at the
knots

  error <= exp(LT) [LM T h/2 + N gamma].           (23)

Projection is nonexpansive and the true solution stays in K. The local
defect bound is LM h_i^2/2 on each constant-input interval. Iterating
e_(i+1)<=(1+Lh_i)e_i+LMh_i^2/2+gamma proves (23).

For an explicit rational exponential bound, take
  D_exp=2^ceil(2LT) >= exp(LT).
For required numerical error nu>0 choose
  h <= min(1, nu/[4 D_exp LM max(T,1)], nu/[8M]),
split every original interval into ceil(length/h) equal rational steps,
and choose m*2^(-P-1) <= nu/[4 D_exp N].
This gives knot error below nu/2. Linear interpolation adds at most
2Mh, so yields a uniform error below nu. Every loop terminates and every
bit is finite. It is often very expensive; no polynomial-time promise
in binary T, stiffness, polynomial degree or 1/nu is claimed.

If the field has s explicit monomials and maximum total degree D_F,
one dense evaluation takes a conservative O(m^2+s*m*D_F) rational
operations (sparse syntax can be cheaper). Resetting to P-bit dyadics
keeps intermediate lengths bounded by a polynomial in the coefficient
length, D_F, P, log m, log of the box radius and the rational step length.
Using schoolbook arithmetic, a conservative cubic bit cost per such
rational operation is sufficient. Multiply by N, include coefficient
bound construction, the size of D_exp, all earlier rejected proposals,
and output storage. The finite algorithm is complete, not unit-cost.

A later polynomial readout at finitely many rational times is evaluated
using a derivative/coefficient bound on the certified state box. Multiply
the state enclosure by its Lipschitz bound and add its own arithmetic
rounding. Integral readouts add their time/quadrature costs explicitly.
After a fixed acquisition, one can refine the emulator computation to
any numerical precision, but its source-ambiguity term E remains.

8.2 Equilibria of subsequently attached nonlinear systems.

For a constant forcing f, the exact static port impedance is

  S=A-U D^(-1)V,
  Shat=Ahat-(1/c)sum Mhat_j,   ||S-Shat||<=E.       (24)

To prove strong monotonicity of S, put h=-D^(-1)Vy. Then H(y,h)=(Sy,0)
and y^TSy=(y,h)^TH(y,h)>=a||y||^2. Hence the later equilibrium maps
diag(S,D_c)z+F(z) and diag(Shat,D_c)z+F(z) are strongly monotone with
margins alpha and alphahat. They have unique solutions. Their error is

  ||z_* - zhat_*|| <= E ||f||/(alpha alphahat).     (25)

There is an explicit complete numerical equilibrium solver. Use a box
of radius greater than ||f||/alphahat, compute a local Lipschitz bound
L_g on it, and iterate projection of

  z - (alphahat/L_g^2) [ghat(z)-f].

Its contraction factor is at most
sqrt(1-alphahat^2/L_g^2)<=1-alphahat^2/(2L_g^2). At its unique fixed
point the variational inequality with comparison point zero gives
||z||<=||f||/alphahat, so the point lies in the box interior and solves
the original equation. Round with a paid error below the desired
accuracy times 1 minus the contraction factor. The computed residual
divided by alphahat independently bounds the distance to the emulator
equilibrium. All coefficient, precision and iteration costs are counted.
This general equilibrium algorithm is proved here, not executed here.

9. ALL INFORMATION AND BIT COSTS

Let B0 bound the binary lengths of the rational acquisition parameters.
Planning uses O(k) rational tail updates on O((k+1)B0) bits and O(p)
dyadic precision comparisons. A deliberately loose schoolbook bit bound
is O((k+p+1)^4 (B0+log(n+r)+1)^4). There is no hidden real operation.

Let B1 include B0, p, log n and the integer-part bits of b and c and
their reciprocals. Apart from the instrument, acquisition, normalization,
norm checks, rounding and coefficient assembly take a conservative
O((n r(k+1)+r^2(k+1)+1) B1^4) bit operations. Intermediate learner
states are rounded each iteration, preventing growth proportional to
the full history length. Planning powers are counted separately.

If each rational source entry has numerator and denominator of at most
B_H bits, exact dot products on dyadic inputs have O(n B_H+p+log n)
intermediate bits: denominators may accumulate across a row. A dense
action has a conservative bit cost

  O(n^2 (n B_H+p+log n+1)^3).

Read/parse the complete source encoding and its n by n indexing too.
A sparse action uses its actual nonzero count, with the corresponding
row-length denominator bound. The experiment cost is the sum over all
r(k+1) actions, never one unit per call. More general supplied action
programmes contribute their declared complete cost C_source(q,p).

Query and response traffic is O(n r(k+1)(p+B0+log n+1)) bits. Working
learner storage can use O(nr) rounded numbers plus retained moments.
The full replayable audit log has n-dependent size and is retained.
The compact online coefficient data require
O(r^2(k+1)(p+B0+log n+1)) bits plus metadata and port indices. Thus the
count of online states/coefficients, not their entire lifetime information
cost or bit length, is independent of n at fixed a,b,r,target.

The numerical dependence on kappa=b/a is substantive. In discretized
PDEs or large networks, a or b may change with n; no discretization-
independent advantage follows without uniform bounds. Conversely, the
construction does not require the unknown source itself to be low rank.

At later runtime exploit the filter structure: the linear work per field
evaluation is O(k r^2+k r+q^2+qr) rather than a dense n-state update,
before adding the stated polynomial-load work. Storing a dense execution
matrix, as the small reference code does, has its actual larger cost.
Later q, polynomial degree, coefficient bits, horizon, forcing magnitude,
precision and stiffness all remain part of the input and work contract.

For the executed quadratic/quartic syntax, each exact residual interval
uses two rational field evaluations plus polynomial arithmetic of degree
at most nine. There are 128 such intervals for each retained and each
full-source path, hence 256 exact endpoint field evaluations per path in
addition to the 512 floating proposal evaluations. Polynomial coefficient
formation, rational squaring/summation, upward square roots, transcript
replay and the complete saved paths are also charged. For general degree
D_F the polynomial residual degree is at most 3D_F. Naive polynomial
arithmetic gives a finite polynomial number of rational operations in
the explicit monomial count, state count, interval count and D_F; the
bits of ALL intermediate coefficients and accumulated rational sums
must multiply that work. They are not fixed-precision real operations.

Source-aware incumbents may inspect the complete source entries or
exploit its structure if those are available to them. An ordinary
equally informed retaining method can implement this same construction.
No superiority is claimed from relabeling a conventional method, from
granting only one method a source-code interface, or from hiding the
acquisition transcript. This retains the N997/N1018 accounting lessons.

11. THE EXACT-REUSE INFORMATION BOUNDARY

The following lower bound is scoped to an ACTION interface with unknown
rational coefficient heights. It is not a bit-complexity lower bound
for all algorithms or a claim about a known bounded rational alphabet.

Theorem. In the class of all rational symmetric n by n matrices with
a I < H < b I, for a<b, no deterministic procedure using fewer than n
exact full-vector rational-input actions in every case can guarantee an
exact representation of every future response at even one fixed port.
No bound on the numerator/denominator heights is supplied to the learner.
In the exact-action interface, n basis actions suffice to recover H and
thereby supply an exact finite equation representation of those responses.
This upper statement does not promise rational outputs for transcendental
trajectory values; it promises their defining equations and a complete
certified approximation algorithm.

Proof. Run the proposed procedure on a rational symmetric irreducible
tridiagonal H0 strictly inside the spectral interval, with port e_0.
For example take diagonal (a+b)/2 and offdiagonal (b-a)/8. Its spectral
slack is at least (b-a)/4. The vectors H0^j e_0, j=0,...,n-1, are a
basis, since their first new coordinate j is a nonzero product of
offdiagonals. Thus e_0 is cyclic.

Let q_1,...,q_k be the actual queries, with k<n. Exact rational linear
algebra produces a nonzero rational v perpendicular to all of them.
Choose rational tau>0 with tau||v||^2 smaller than the spectral slack.
Both

  H_plus=H0+tau vv^T,   H_minus=H0-tau vv^T

are admissible, and their actions agree with H0 on every query. By
determinism, adaptive queries and the final transcript remain identical.

Set h(s)=v^T(sI+H0)^(-1)e_0 and g(s)=v^T(sI+H0)^(-1)v. Cyclicity
implies h is not identically zero. The rank-one resolvent identity gives

  G_plus(s)=G0(s)-tau h(s)^2/(1+tau g(s)),
  G_minus(s)=G0(s)+tau h(s)^2/(1-tau g(s)).         (27)

For positive s the denominators are nonzero; the functions differ.
Therefore no common exact representation can answer every later port
task for both sources. This completes the lower bound.

A finite explicit future witness can also be extracted. Find the first
j with Delta_m=e_0^T(H_plus^j-H_minus^j)e_0 nonzero. There is one with
j<=2n-1, either by the rational transfer numerator degree or by expanding
(27) at infinity. For a unit step, the leading response difference is
(-1)^j Delta_m t^(j+1)/(j+1)!. At positive rational time

  t <= min(1,1/b, |Delta_m|(j+2)/(12 b^(j+1))),

the remaining power-series tail is less than half its magnitude: use
|e_0^T(H_plus^l-H_minus^l)e_0|<=2b^l and exp(bt)<3. Thus a finite
unit-energy-bounded task distinguishes the sources. Computing v, tau,
the moments and this rational time is finite rational arithmetic with
the query/coefficient lengths included. The gap can be extremely small;
the proof does not impose a comparable lower bound for fixed accuracy.

Crucial height qualification. Suppose instead that H_ij=m_ij/L with
known L and integers |m_ij|<=M. Choose Q>2M+1 and query q_j=Q^(-j),
j=1,...,n. Its norm is less than 1. Each exact report, multiplied by
L Q^n, encodes one complete row in signed base Q. Rounding finer than
1/(2 L Q^n) also permits recovery. One call can therefore identify the
matrix at this strengthened prior/precision interface. Its query/report
bits are of order n^2 log Q and its requested precision is of order
n log Q+log L, before source arithmetic. This is why the n-action lower
bound must not be promoted into a universal bit or coefficient-prior
lower bound. The approximate construction counts these distinctions.
