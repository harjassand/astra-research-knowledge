This introduces **no probability distribution over Pauli faults**. The original Poisson geometry remains fixed. The basis histories are deterministic tests on its supports.

\[

## 2. A direct extension for sharp classical measurement outputs

For classical outputs there is a second sufficient criterion, which can avoid requiring a retained logical quantum output in the certificate.

Let the ideal target be the projective measurement \(\{\Pi_y\}\) on the full logical input space:

\[
\Pi_y\Pi_z=\delta_{yz}\Pi_y,
\qquad
\sum_y\Pi_y=I.
\]

Its reference-inclusive measurement channel is

\[
\mathcal M_\Pi(\rho_{LR})
=
\sum_y |y\rangle\!\langle y|
\otimes
\operatorname{Tr}_L
\!\left[
(\Pi_y\otimes I_R)\rho_{LR}
\right].
\tag{5}
\]

Fix one output measurement instrument for all fault histories. In a common dilation, let \(Q_y\) be its output-label projectors. Suppose every basis history satisfies

\[
V^\dagger W_{\mathbf a}^\dagger Q_yW_{\mathbf a}V
=
\Pi_y.
\tag{6}
\]

Then arbitrary shared-environment CPTP faults on the same supports also give exactly (5).

The key elementary fact is

\[
W^\dagger QW=\Pi,
\qquad
W^\dagger W=I,
\qquad
Q^2=Q,\quad \Pi^2=\Pi
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
QW=W\Pi.
\tag{7}
\]

To prove it, first take a vector in the kernel of \(\Pi\). Its image under \(W\) has zero squared norm in the \(Q\) subspace. Then take a vector in the range of \(\Pi\). Its image has zero squared norm in the complementary subspace. These two cases give (7).

Applying this to each basis history gives

\[
Q_yW_{\mathbf a}V=W_{\mathbf a}V\Pi_y.
\]

The identity survives the coherent sum (1):

\[
(Q_y\otimes I_E)W_{\mathrm{actual}}V
=
W_{\mathrm{actual}}V(\Pi_y\otimes I_E).
\tag{8}
\]

Since the actual dilated evolution is an isometry,

\[
(W_{\mathrm{actual}}V)^\dagger
(Q_y\otimes I_E)
(W_{\mathrm{actual}}V)
=
\Pi_y\otimes I_E.
\tag{9}
\]

Thus the outcome effects—and therefore their correlations with an arbitrary reference—are correct.

## 3. Why a general classical output needs more care

The projectivity condition above is substantive. Passing every individual Pauli test for a general measurement channel is insufficient.

Consider an unknown qubit \(Q\) and an ancilla \(A\) initialized in \(|0\rangle\). Use the joint projective measurement

\[
F_0=\frac{I+X_A\otimes Z_Q}{2},
\qquad
F_1=\frac{I-X_A\otimes Z_Q}{2}.
\tag{10}
\]

On the logical input \(Q\), its induced effects are

\[
\langle0|F_0|0\rangle_A
=
\langle0|F_1|0\rangle_A
=
\frac{I_Q}{2}.
\]

The intended measurement channel therefore produces an independent fair bit.

Now allow one fault on \(A\) immediately before the measurement.

Every Pauli \(P\in\{I,X,Y,Z\}\) sends \(|0\rangle\) to a computational-basis state up to phase. Each has zero \(X\) expectation. Hence

\[
\langle0|P^\dagger F_yP|0\rangle_A
=
\frac{I_Q}{2}
\qquad
\text{for every Pauli }P.
\tag{11}
\]

Every Pauli fault preserves the **entire logical measurement channel**, not merely its value on a few test inputs.

But the coherent unitary fault \(H_A\) sends \(|0\rangle\) to \(|+\rangle\), giving

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle0|H_A^\dagger F_0H_A|0\rangle_A
&=\frac{I_Q+Z_Q}{2},\\
\langle0|H_A^\dagger F_1H_A|0\rangle_A
&=\frac{I_Q-Z_Q}{2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

The same output instrument now measures \(Z_Q\).

For a Bell-entangled input \(QR\), the Pauli cases produce

\[
\rho^{\mathrm{ideal}}_{YR}
=
\frac{I_Y}{2}\otimes\frac{I_R}{2},
\]

whereas the Hadamard fault produces

\[
\rho^H_{YR}
=
\frac{|00\rangle\!\langle00|+|11\rangle\!\langle11|}{2}.
\]

Their trace distance is exactly

\[
\frac12
\left\|
\rho^H_{YR}-\rho^{\mathrm{ideal}}_{YR}
\right\|_1
=
\frac12.
\tag{13}
\]

Both output-bit marginals are uniform. A check of the output distribution alone would miss the failure.

I verified these identities using exact rational matrix arithmetic. This is a counterexample to a proposed reduction step, **not** an obstruction to classical-output quantum computation. Projective targets and full quantum-channel certificates provide the positive extensions above; other general POVMs may admit extensions under stronger cross-term conditions.
