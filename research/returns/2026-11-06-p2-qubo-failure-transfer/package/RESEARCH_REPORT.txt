0. WHAT THIS RETURN DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

The requested uniform deterministic classical algorithm, polynomial in the
complete binary input length, has not been constructed. No general lower
bound excluding every such algorithm has been proved. Thus neither P=NP nor
P!=NP follows from this work.

The work below establishes scoped representation obstructions and an exact
relaxation counterexample. The strongest two are:

* A bounded-coefficient quadratic on a square grid requires a superpolynomial
  number of explicit min-plus separator states at every balanced variable
  split, even for a whole-function approximation with error less than 1/2.
* A cardinality-square quadratic requires exponentially many components in a
  pointwise minimum of submodular functions, even when every component chooses
  its own fixed coordinate complements and error less than 1/2 is allowed.

These are not general computational lower bounds. In fact the displayed
families have easy global minima. This explicitly separates the cost of an
attempted representation from the cost of the optimisation problem itself.

Proofs are given below, with exact finite diagnostics supplied separately.

The star identity is standard pseudo-Boolean machinery; the min-plus rank

1. THE FULL BIT MODEL

The input is

  E(x) = c + sum_i a_i x_i + sum_(i<j) b_ij x_i x_j,
  x in {0,1}^n,

with explicitly listed variables and signed integer coefficients encoded in
binary. Let L be the complete input length. In particular n<=L. Coefficient
positions, signs, lengths, and any supplied zero entries count toward L.

Set

  C = |c| + sum_i |a_i| + sum_(i<j) |b_ij|.

Every assignment energy is an integer in [-C,C]. If the input is nonempty,
the bit length of C is at most the largest input coefficient bit length plus
ceil(log2(number of supplied coefficients))+1, hence O(L). Computing C,
evaluating an assignment, comparing energies, and writing an n-bit answer
therefore require polynomial bit cost. Large numeric coefficient magnitudes
do not themselves force an exponentially long output.

A direct exact baseline enumerates every n-bit assignment, evaluates E with
integer addition, and retains the first minimizer in a fixed order. It is
correct for every input, including n=0. A conservative direct bit-operation
bound is O(2^n L^2): there are at most L supplied terms; locating an assignment
bit and adding an O(L)-bit coefficient each cost at most O(L) in a direct
implementation. Preprocessing takes O(L^2), and working storage is polynomial.
This is exponential, not the requested polynomial bound.

The Python reference solver in verify_research.py is intentionally this exact
exponential baseline. It is a fixture verifier, not a claimed breakthrough.

The equivalence with P versus NP is prior machinery. The threshold problem
"does some x have E(x)<=K?" is in NP, with a polynomial-bit verifier. Standard
polynomial reductions establish its NP-hardness. Conversely, if P=NP, a
polynomial threshold decider plus O(log(C+1)) threshold queries and n prefix
queries produces the exact value and an assignment. Restrictions only add
explicit input coefficients, so their bit lengths remain polynomial. Thus
the output requirement does not create a different complexity question.

Lucas [S1] supplies standard Ising/NP encodings; no reduction is counted here
as a new solution. The current Clay statement [S2] still lists P versus NP as
unsolved. That status is background, not a substitute for the attempts below.

2. EXACT ELIMINATION: ONE BIT CAN CREATE EXPONENTIALLY MANY MONOMIALS

Writing the last-bit dependence as

  E(x,y) = E0(x) + y*(a + sum_i b_i x_i)

gives the exact identity

  min_y E(x,y) = E0(x) + min(0, a + sum_i b_i x_i).

All coefficients in this step can be handled with polynomially many bits.
The attempted closure under short explicit multilinear polynomials fails.

For k>=2, take

  E_k(x,y) = y*(1 - sum_(i=1)^k x_i).

Only k+1 coefficients are nonzero, and all are +1 or -1. Eliminating y gives

  F_k(x) = min(0,1-sum_i x_i)
         = 1-sum_i x_i - product_i(1-x_i).

Proof of the second equality: at the all-zero assignment the right side is
zero. At every other assignment the product vanishes and 1-sum_i x_i<=0.
Expanding the product yields

  F_k(x) = sum_(S subset [k], |S|>=2) (-1)^(|S|+1) product_(i in S) x_i.

The multilinear representation is unique. Consequently all 2^k-k-1 terms
are unavoidable in that representation, and its degree is k. The sparse
input has O(k log k) bits with explicit indices, or polynomially many bits
under a dense encoding. This is a superpolynomial expansion after one bit.

The identity is closely the complemented negative-monomial quadratization
in Boros--Gruber [S3, equations (5)--(7)], not a novelty claim.

Scope: this refutes polynomial closure of the EXPLICIT monomial list. The
factored product above is short, and the star optimum is simply 1-k. It does
not refute short arithmetic circuits, other elimination orders, or general
algorithms. Introducing an auxiliary bit to quadratize the function can
reintroduce the bit just eliminated; a separate progress bound is needed.

3. A GENERAL MIN-PLUS COLLISION LEMMA

Consider a finite matrix H that is represented as

  H(i,j) = min_(1<=s<=r) [alpha_s(i) + beta_s(j)].                 (3.1)

The smallest possible r is its min-plus factorization (Barvinok) rank; see
Develin--Santos--Sturmfels [S4]. This representation describes a finite list
of separator states with independent costs on either side of the separator.
The factors may be arbitrary real-valued functions; no short formula for
them is presumed in the lower bound.

Suppose M has m distinguished row/column pairs, relabelled (i,i), such that

  M(i,j)+M(j,i)-M(i,i)-M(j,j) >= 2       for every i!=j.          (3.2)

If |H(i,j)-M(i,j)|<=epsilon<1/2 everywhere, then every representation (3.1)
has r>=m.

Proof. Perturbing the four entries changes the left side of (3.2) by at most
4 epsilon, so H(i,i)+H(j,j)<H(i,j)+H(j,i). Choose a state attaining each
distinguished diagonal entry. If one state s attained both i and j, then

  H(i,i)+H(j,j)
   = alpha_s(i)+beta_s(i)+alpha_s(j)+beta_s(j)
   = alpha_s(i)+beta_s(j)+alpha_s(j)+beta_s(i)
   >= H(i,j)+H(j,i),

a contradiction. All m distinguished entries need different states. QED.

This is an explicit elementary application of standard min-plus rank ideas.
It proves a number-of-states bound, even before coefficient precision or
the cost of constructing the factors is charged.

3A. RANK-ONE QUADRATICS WITH FULL MIN-PLUS RANK AT EVERY SPLIT

For n>=2 and an integer 0<=T<2^n, let

  w_i=2^(i-1),
  E_(n,T)(x)=(sum_i w_i*x_i-T)^2.

Its explicitly expanded QUBO has

  c=T^2,
  a_i=w_i^2-2*T*w_i,
  b_ij=2*w_i*w_j.

There are O(n^2) coefficients of O(n) bits each, hence total input length
O(n^3), including explicit variables and indices. The symmetric continuous
quadratic matrix ww^T has ordinary rank one.

For ANY partition A|B, set u=sum_(i in A) w_i*x_i and
v=sum_(i in B) w_i*x_i. All 2^|A| possible u values are distinct, as are all
2^|B| possible v values. Put m=2^min(|A|,|B|), select m values on each side,
and order them as

  u_1<...<u_m,       v_1>...>v_m.

For M(i,j)=(u_i+v_j-T)^2 and i<j,

  M(i,j)+M(j,i)-M(i,i)-M(j,j)
      = 2*(u_j-u_i)*(v_i-v_j) >= 2.                              (3.3)

The collision lemma proves r>=2^min(|A|,|B|), both for exact representation
and for uniform error epsilon<1/2. In the exact case this is also an upper
bound: one state per row of the smaller matrix dimension suffices, with a
large finite penalty on the other rows. Transpose if needed. Thus exact
min-plus rank equals 2^min(|A|,|B|).

Every variable ordering has a balanced middle split, requiring at least
2^floor(n/2) explicit states. Changing the ordering cannot remove this bound.
The energy matrix across a split nevertheless has ordinary real rank at
most three, since it is a sum of a row term, a column term, and 2*u*v.

Scope and countercheck: the optimum is zero, attained uniquely at the binary
expansion of T, which is directly computable. This is therefore explicitly
NOT a lower bound on optimising even this family. The original square is
already a short arithmetic expression. What fails is replacing that cross
interaction by polynomially many separated min-plus states.

4. THE SAME REPRESENTATION FAILURE WITH BOUNDED COEFFICIENTS

Let k be positive and even, G the ordinary k-by-k square grid with nearest-
neighbour edges, N=k^2, and v0 a fixed corner. Define

  E_G(x)=x_v0 + sum_(uv in edges(G)) (x_u-x_v)^2.                (4.1)

On binary inputs this is a QUBO with c=0,

  a_v=degree_G(v)+1_(v=v0),       b_uv=-2 on grid edges,

and all other pair coefficients zero. In particular |a_v|<=5 and |b_uv|<=2.
A sparse explicitly indexed input has O(N log N) bits; a dense encoding is
also polynomial in N. The graph is connected, so the unique global minimum
is zero at the all-zero assignment: each squared difference must vanish,
then the anchored bit fixes the common value to zero.

Claim. At EVERY balanced partition A|B of the N variables, any representation

  H(x_A,x_B)=min_(s<=r) [f_s(x_A)+g_s(x_B)]

with |H-E_G|<=epsilon<1/2 everywhere has

  r >= 2^ceil(k/32).                                           (4.2)

This is superpolynomial in the polynomial-size input and does not rely on
large coefficients.

Proof, step 1: every balanced grid cut has at least k crossing edges.
Let a_i be the number of A vertices in row i. Let q be the number of rows
with 0<a_i<k. Horizontal crossing edges contribute at least q, and vertical
crossing edges contribute at least sum_i |a_(i+1)-a_i|.
If all rows are mixed, q=k. If both a full and an empty row occur, the total
variation is at least k. If full rows but no empty rows occur, at most k/2
rows are full because |A|=k^2/2; hence q>=k/2. Some row has a_i<=k/2, so the
variation is at least k/2. The case of empty rows but no full rows follows
by exchanging A and B. These cases exhaust the possibilities.

Step 2: the bipartite crossing graph has maximum degree at most four. Select
a crossing edge uv, then delete every remaining crossing edge incident to
any vertex in the crossing-graph neighbourhood of u or v. At most
2*4^2=32 edges are deleted per selected edge. Repeating produces an induced
crossing matching of size m>=ceil(k/32). "Induced" here concerns the
BIPARTITE crossing graph; edges within A or within B are permitted.

Step 3: keep only the 2m matched endpoint bits free and set all other bits
to zero. Write the two matched-endpoint vectors as a,b in {0,1}^m. The
restricted energy is

  d_H(a,b) + F(a) + G(b),                                     (4.3)

where d_H is Hamming distance. Other edges and the anchor contribute only
row or column functions. The induced matching ensures there are no other
cross terms between the free endpoint sets.

Subtract F and G from the represented matrix; this preserves factorization
width and uniform error. The resulting exact kernel d_H has 2^m diagonal
entries zero, and for a!=b its two crossed entries sum to 2*d_H(a,b)>=2.
The collision lemma now gives r>=2^m, proving (4.2). QED.

Scope: the bound applies to a finite separated-state representation of the
WHOLE energy table. It is not a lower bound for every dynamic program or
symbolic representation. Equation (4.1) is submodular and easy to minimise.
Ordinary graph-cut algorithms are not excluded; this is precisely why the
representation obstruction cannot be promoted to P!=NP. Bucket-elimination
width costs in [S5] are relevant background, not a general complexity proof.

5. EXPONENTIALLY MANY SUBMODULAR COMPONENTS, INCLUDING BIT COMPLEMENTS

This route tried to keep each consumer easy by writing the objective as a
pointwise minimum of tractable submodular components. If a polynomial number
of such components could be built and optimised with polynomial bit cost,
minimising their pointwise minimum would require only their individual
minima. The following family refutes this universal representation claim.

Let k=floor(n/2) and

  E_n(x)=(sum_i x_i-k)^2
        =k^2+(1-2*k)*sum_i x_i+2*sum_(i<j) x_i*x_j.             (5.1)

This is an explicit O(n^2 log n)-bit input under a simple indexed encoding.
There are no hidden variables in the input. Its minimum is zero at every
assignment of Hamming weight k and can be obtained directly.

Definition. A component g is submodular after fixed coordinate complements
if, for some fixed mask t in {0,1}^n, h(y)=g(y XOR t) satisfies

  h(u)+h(v) >= h(u AND v)+h(u OR v)                             (5.2)

for all u,v. Each component may use a different mask.

Theorem. Suppose

  H(x)=min_(1<=s<=r) g_s(x),
  |H(x)-E_n(x)|<=epsilon<1/2   for every binary x,

and every g_s is submodular after its own fixed coordinate complements.
Then

  r >= ceil[ binomial(n,k) / 2^floor(n/2) ]
    >= 2^ceil(n/2)/(n+1).                                     (5.3)

If complements are not allowed, the stronger bound r>=binomial(n,k) holds.
The functions may be of arbitrary degree. Their evaluation costs are not
assumed small, making this a representation bound before those costs are
even added.

Proof. Let X_k be the middle Hamming layer. For component s, let A_s consist
of the points of X_k at which it attains the minimum H. These sets cover
X_k. Since g_s>=H pointwise, all components satisfy g_s>=E_n-epsilon, while
at their active points they satisfy g_s<=E_n+epsilon.

Fix one component. Let P be its uncomplemented coordinates and Q its
complemented coordinates. Suppose two distinct active points x,y agree on
Q. Their differences lie in P, and both have the same Hamming weight k.
Their meet and join in the component's oriented Boolean lattice have
original Hamming weights k-d and k+d for some integer d>=1. Therefore

  E_n(meet)+E_n(join)=E_n(x)+E_n(y)+2*d^2.

Submodularity and the error bounds imply

  E_n(x)+E_n(y)+2*epsilon
    >= g_s(x)+g_s(y)
    >= g_s(meet)+g_s(join)
    >= E_n(x)+E_n(y)+2*d^2-2*epsilon.

Thus 4*epsilon>=2*d^2>=2, contradicting epsilon<1/2. Projection onto Q is
therefore injective on A_s. If x,y instead agree on P, complementing all
their differing coordinates exchanges meet with join, and the same two
Hamming weights and argument apply. Projection onto P is also injective.
Consequently

  |A_s| <= min(2^|P|,2^|Q|) <= 2^floor(n/2).

Covering binomial(n,k) middle-layer points proves the first inequality in
(5.3). The middle binomial coefficient is at least 2^n/(n+1), proving the
second. Without complements Q is empty, so each A_s contains at most one
point. QED.

Auxiliary-variable extension. If a component is written as

  g_s(x)=min_y G_s(x,y)

with G_s jointly submodular after fixed complements of its x and y bits,
then g_s is submodular after the induced x complements. Choose minimising
auxiliary assignments for two inputs and apply joint submodularity to their
meet and join; the corresponding partial minima can only be smaller. Thus
(5.3) also applies to these graph-cut-type auxiliary representations,
regardless of the number of auxiliary bits. Partial minimisation preserving
submodularity is standard; see [S3].

Why the precision threshold is meaningful. If E has integer values and an
explicitly minimised H approximates E uniformly within epsilon<1/2, then
a minimizer z of H has E(z)<=min E+2*epsilon<min E+1, and hence is an exact
minimizer of E. The theorem therefore rules out this potentially sufficient
approximate-envelope route as well as the exact envelope route.

Scope: this is not a lower bound for every algorithm that calls min-cut or
submodular minimisation. It does not exclude arbitrary optimisation-
preserving reductions, different tractable component families, or implicit
optimisation over an exponentially large component index. The latter would
need its own paid algorithm. The easy optimum of (5.1) is retained.

6. A THREE-BIT INSTANCE WITH A RATIONAL PSD AND PAIR-MARGINAL GAP

Take

  E(x)=-31*x1-30*x2-28*x3+32*(x1*x2+x1*x3+x2*x3).              (6.1)

The eight exact values are

  x1 x2 x3    E
   0  0  0     0
   1  0  0   -31
   0  1  0   -30
   1  1  0   -29
   0  0  1   -28
   1  0  1   -27
   0  1  1   -26
   1  1  1     7

The unique optimum is -31 at (1,0,0). The coefficients have gcd one, so
merely dividing out a common coefficient scale does not remove the example.

Consider the first Boolean moment SDP with moment matrix indexed by
(1,x1,x2,x3), constraints M_00=1 and M_ii=M_0i, and M positive semidefinite.
The following is a feasible rational moment matrix:

  M = [[1,   1/2, 1/2, 1/2],
       [1/2, 1/2, 1/8, 1/8],
       [1/2, 1/8, 1/2, 1/8],
       [1/2, 1/8, 1/8, 1/2]].

Its Schur complement at the upper-left 1 is

  (3/8)*I_3-(1/8)*J_3,

which is PSD: for any real v, 3*sum_i v_i^2-(sum_i v_i)^2>=0.
Its eigenvalues are 0,3/8,3/8. Thus no floating-point PSD assertion is needed.

The associated functional L has L(x_i)=1/2 and L(x_i*x_j)=1/8 for i!=j.
It evaluates the objective to

  L(E)=(-31-30-28)/2+32*3/8=-65/2=-32.5.                     (6.2)

For each distinct pair, the local distribution on (00,01,10,11) is

  (1/8,3/8,3/8,1/8).

All entries are nonnegative and these distributions share the same single-
bit marginals. Hence ALL inequalities valid for an individual pair are
satisfied, not only the usual McCormick inequalities. Adding such pairwise
inequalities to this SDP does not invalidate this witness.

Since this is a minimisation relaxation, its optimum is at most -32.5.
Its ceiling is therefore at most -32, still strictly below the true optimum
-31. Solving this relaxation more accurately cannot make integer rounding
certify the exact answer.

Equivalent certificate statement. A representation

  E-ell = sum_j p_j(x)^2 + sum_i lambda_i*(x_i^2-x_i),

with affine p_j and real lambda_i would imply ell<=-32.5 after applying L.
The same conclusion holds if nonnegative functions of at most two bits are
added to the right side, because the displayed pair distributions give them
nonnegative expectation. Thus this certificate class cannot certify -31,
or even a lower bound strictly above -32 sufficient for integer rounding.

Scope: higher-order certificates, additional global inequalities, other
relaxations, and unrestricted algorithms are not excluded. Eight explicit
energy evaluations already solve this instance.

8. WHAT REMAINS OPEN

The independent routes examined here were:

* Exact bit elimination with an explicit monomial representation.
* Exact/near-exact finite min-plus separation, including low ordinary rank
  and bounded-coefficient attempts to control the number of states.
* Exact/near-exact pointwise envelopes of submodular consumers, including
  independently chosen coordinate complements and submodular auxiliaries.
* Degree-two PSD and pairwise-marginal lower certificates followed by integer
  rounding.

Their precise universal claims fail as above. A general algorithm need not
use any of these representations. Conversely, leaving a minimum over an
exponential state or component set unevaluated does not supply an algorithm.
No proof in this return closes the remaining unrestricted operation.

To meet the original positive contract, one still needs a uniform exact
constructor with a proved polynomial bound on ALL computation, coefficient
and intermediate precision, preprocessing, auxiliary variables, and witness
recovery. To meet the negative contract, one still needs a lower bound that
covers every uniform deterministic classical algorithm. Neither has been
earned here. The original full question is retained, not replaced by any of
the easy families or representation questions above.
