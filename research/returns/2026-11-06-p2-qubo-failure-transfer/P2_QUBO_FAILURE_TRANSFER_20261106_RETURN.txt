\[
\min_{y\in\{0,1\}}E(x,y)
=
E_0(x)+\min\left(0,a+\sum_i b_i x_i\right).
\]

The arithmetic in one step is manageable. The first issue is whether the resulting function remains short in the representation used for subsequent steps.

### An explicit failure family

For \(k\ge2\), take

\[
E_k(x,y)=y\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_i\right).
\]

This is a star-shaped quadratic with just \(k+1\) nonzero coefficients, all equal to \(1\) or \(-1\). Eliminating \(y\) gives

\[
F_k(x)=\min\left(0,1-\sum_i x_i\right).
\]

On the Boolean cube,

\[
F_k(x)
=
1-\sum_i x_i-\prod_i(1-x_i).
\]

At the all-zero assignment this expression is zero. At every other assignment the product vanishes, leaving \(1-\sum_i x_i\le0\).

Expanding the product therefore gives

\[
F_k(x)=
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq[k]\\|S|\ge2}}
(-1)^{|S|+1}\prod_{i\in S}x_i.

The multilinear representation is unique, so it contains exactly

\[
\boxed{2^k-k-1}

nonzero monomials, and its degree is \(k\).

Thus **one exact elimination step can turn a polynomial-size quadratic input into an exponentially long explicit monomial list**.

## 3. A stronger obstruction: exponentially many separated minimisation states

Consider representations of the form

\[
H(x_A,x_B)
=
\min_{1\le s\le r}
\left[f_s(x_A)+g_s(x_B)\right].
\]

The index \(s\) is a separator state: once it is selected, the two groups of variables contribute independently.

For an explicitly indexed matrix, the smallest possible \(r\) is its **min-plus factorisation rank**, also called Barvinok rank. This is an established notion in tropical linear algebra. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

The attempted algorithmic advantage would be to keep \(r\) polynomial, so that the remaining minimisations could be performed through a manageable list of states.

### A collision lemma

Suppose a matrix \(M\) has \(m\) distinguished row–column pairs, relabelled \((i,i)\), satisfying

\[
M_{ij}+M_{ji}-M_{ii}-M_{jj}\ge2
\qquad(i\ne j).
\]

Suppose also that

\[
|H_{ij}-M_{ij}|\le\varepsilon<\frac12.
\]

Then every representation

\[
H_{ij}=\min_{s\le r}(\alpha_{s,i}+\beta_{s,j})
\]

requires \(r\ge m\).

To see this, the error bound preserves

\[
H_{ii}+H_{jj}<H_{ij}+H_{ji}.
\]

Choose a state attaining each distinguished diagonal entry. If the same state attained both \(H_{ii}\) and \(H_{jj}\), separability would imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{ii}+H_{jj}
&=\alpha_{s,i}+\beta_{s,i}
 +\alpha_{s,j}+\beta_{s,j}\\
&=\alpha_{s,i}+\beta_{s,j}
 +\alpha_{s,j}+\beta_{s,i}\\
&\ge H_{ij}+H_{ji},
\end{aligned}
\]

a contradiction. Each distinguished entry needs its own state.

This argument allows arbitrary real-valued factors. The lower bound arises before charging the cost of constructing or evaluating them.

### A rank-one quadratic with maximum separation rank

Take

\[
E_{n,T}(x)=
\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}2^{i-1}x_i-T\right)^2,
\qquad 0\le T<2^n.
\]

Its expanded coefficients are

\[
c=T^2,\qquad
a_i=2^{2i-2}-2T\,2^{i-1},\qquad
b_{ij}=2^{i+j-1}.
\]

There are \(O(n^2)\) coefficients, each with \(O(n)\) bits. The complete input therefore has polynomial length, \(O(n^3)\).

For any partition \(A\mid B\), write

\[
u=\sum_{i\in A}2^{i-1}x_i,
\qquad
v=\sum_{i\in B}2^{i-1}x_i.
\]

Each side has distinct subset sums. Select

\[
m=2^{\min(|A|,|B|)}
\]

values on each side, ordered as

\[
u_1<\cdots<u_m,
\qquad
v_1>\cdots>v_m.
\]

For \(M_{ij}=(u_i+v_j-T)^2\),

\[
M_{ij}+M_{ji}-M_{ii}-M_{jj}
=
2(u_j-u_i)(v_i-v_j)\ge2
\quad(i<j).
\]

The collision lemma proves

\[
\boxed{r\ge2^{\min(|A|,|B|)}}.
\]

This holds for exact representation and for uniform additive error below \(1/2\). For exact representation the bound is sharp: one state per row or column of the smaller dimension suffices.

Every variable ordering has a balanced middle split. Hence every ordering requires at least

\[
\boxed{2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}
\]

explicit separator states at that split.

The continuous quadratic matrix here has ordinary rank one. The matrix of energies across a split has ordinary real rank at most three. Neither rank controls this min-plus representation cost.

**The decisive limitation:** the global minimum of this family is simply zero, attained at the binary expansion of \(T\). It is easy to compute. The result rules out the proposed separated-state representation as a universal compact representation; it does not rule out efficient optimisation.

## 4. The separation obstruction survives bounded coefficients

The preceding example uses coefficients whose bit lengths grow with \(n\). I checked whether bounding their magnitudes removes the obstruction. It does not.

Let \(G\) be the ordinary \(k\times k\) square grid, with even \(k\), and let \(v_0\) be a fixed corner. Define

\[
E_G(x)
=
x_{v_0}
+
\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}(x_u-x_v)^2.
\]

On binary inputs this becomes

\[
E_G(x)
=
\sum_v
\bigl(\deg_G(v)+\mathbf 1_{\{v=v_0\}}\bigr)x_v
-
2\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}x_ux_v.
\]

Thus all coefficients are bounded by an absolute constant; in particular,

\[
|a_v|\le5,\qquad |b_{uv}|\le2.
\]

There are \(N=k^2\) explicitly listed variables, and a sparse indexed encoding has \(O(N\log N)\) bits.

### Quantified bound

At **every balanced partition** of the variables, any representation

\[
H(x_A,x_B)=\min_{s\le r}[f_s(x_A)+g_s(x_B)]
\]

satisfying

\[
|H(x)-E_G(x)|<\frac12
\]

uniformly must have

\[
\boxed{r\ge2^{\lceil k/32\rceil}}.
\]

This is superpolynomial in the polynomial-size input.

The proof has three parts.

**First, every balanced grid cut has at least \(k\) crossing edges.** Let \(a_i\) be the number of vertices on one side of the cut in row \(i\), and let \(q\) be the number of partially occupied rows. Horizontal crossing edges contribute at least \(q\); vertical crossing edges contribute at least

\[
\sum_i |a_{i+1}-a_i|.
\]

If every row is partial, \(q=k\). If a full and an empty row both occur, the variation is at least \(k\). In the remaining cases, balance forces at least \(k/2\) partial rows and variation at least \(k/2\).

**Second, those crossing edges contain a sufficiently large induced matching.** The crossing graph has maximum degree four. Greedily selecting an edge and removing crossing edges incident to the neighbours of either endpoint removes at most \(32\) edges per selection. This produces an induced crossing matching of size

\[
m\ge\lceil k/32\rceil.
\]

**Third, restrict all other bits to zero.** On the matched endpoints, the energy has the form

\[
d_H(a,b)+F(a)+G(b),
\]

where \(d_H\) is Hamming distance. Subtracting the row and column terms leaves a matrix with \(2^m\) zero diagonal entries and

\[
d_H(a,b)+d_H(b,a)\ge2
\qquad(a\ne b).
\]

The collision lemma gives the result.

Again, the family is easy to optimise: its unique minimum is zero at the all-zero assignment. The squares force every bit to agree, and the corner term selects zero. Its negative pair coefficients also place it within standard submodular minimisation machinery. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

This supplies an explicit warning against equating large representation width with intrinsic computational hardness.

## 5. Exponentially many submodular components—even with independently complemented bits

The next attempt was to retain an easy optimiser for each component. If an arbitrary objective could be written as the pointwise minimum of polynomially many tractable functions,

\[
H(x)=\min_{s\le r}g_s(x),
\]

then

\[
\min_x H(x)=\min_{s\le r}\min_x g_s(x).
\]

That would be a genuine algorithmic route if construction, component minimisation, and \(r\) all had polynomial bit cost.

The following theorem defeats a broad version using submodular components.

### The family

Let \(k=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\), and take

\[
E_n(x)=\left(\sum_i x_i-k\right)^2.
\]

Its expanded form is

\[
E_n(x)
=
k^2+(1-2k)\sum_i x_i
+
2\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j.
\]

This is an explicit polynomial-size integer QUBO.

A function \(g\) is *submodular after fixed coordinate complements* if there is a fixed bit mask \(t\) such that

\[
h(y)=g(y\oplus t)
\]

satisfies

\[
h(u)+h(v)\ge h(u\wedge v)+h(u\vee v).
\]

Each component is allowed its own mask.

### The theorem

Suppose

\[
H(x)=\min_{1\le s\le r}g_s(x),
\qquad
|H(x)-E_n(x)|\le\varepsilon<\frac12
\]

for every binary assignment, with each \(g_s\) submodular after its own fixed coordinate complements. Then

\[
\boxed{
r\ge
\left\lceil
\frac{\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}
     {2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}
\right\rceil
}
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
r\ge\frac{2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}{n+1}.
}
\]

If coordinate complements are not allowed, the stronger bound is

\[
\boxed{r\ge\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}}.
\]

### Proof

Consider the middle Hamming layer

\[
X_k=\left\{x:\sum_i x_i=k\right\}.
\]

For each component \(s\), let \(A_s\) be the points of \(X_k\) at which it attains the minimum \(H\). These sets cover \(X_k\).

Every component satisfies

\[
g_s(x)\ge H(x)\ge E_n(x)-\varepsilon.
\]

At its active points it also satisfies

\[
g_s(x)\le E_n(x)+\varepsilon.
\]

Fix a component. Let \(P\) be its uncomplemented coordinates and \(Q\) its complemented coordinates.

Suppose two distinct active points \(x,y\) agree on \(Q\). They differ only within \(P\), and they have equal Hamming weight. Their meet and join in the component’s oriented Boolean lattice therefore have original Hamming weights

\[
k-d,\qquad k+d
\]

for an integer \(d\ge1\). Consequently,

\[
E_n(\mathrm{meet})+E_n(\mathrm{join})
=
E_n(x)+E_n(y)+2d^2.
\]

Submodularity and the approximation bounds imply

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_n(x)+E_n(y)+2\varepsilon
&\ge g_s(x)+g_s(y)\\
&\ge g_s(\mathrm{meet})+g_s(\mathrm{join})\\
&\ge E_n(x)+E_n(y)+2d^2-2\varepsilon.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus \(4\varepsilon\ge2d^2\ge2\), contradicting \(\varepsilon<1/2\).

Projection onto \(Q\) must therefore be injective on \(A_s\). The same argument with \(P\) fixed shows that projection onto \(P\) is injective too. Hence

\[
|A_s|
\le
\min(2^{|P|},2^{|Q|})
\le2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.
\]

Covering the \(\binom nk\) middle-layer points gives the first bound. Using

\[
\binom n{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\ge\frac{2^n}{n+1}
\]

gives the second. Without complements, \(Q\) is empty, so each component can be active at at most one middle-layer point.

### Auxiliary variables do not remove this particular obstruction

If a component is represented as

\[
g_s(x)=\min_y G_s(x,y)
\]

and \(G_s\) is jointly submodular after fixed complements, then \(g_s\) remains submodular after the corresponding complements of the original variables. Partial minimisation preserves submodularity. Thus the lower bound also covers such auxiliary-variable representations. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

The \(1/2\) threshold matters operationally. If an integer-valued energy is approximated uniformly by \(H\) within \(\varepsilon<1/2\), a minimiser \(z\) of \(H\) satisfies

\[
E(z)\le\min E+2\varepsilon<\min E+1,
\]

so \(z\) is an exact minimiser of \(E\).

**Scope.** This excludes polynomial-size pointwise envelopes of these components, including sufficiently accurate approximate envelopes. It does not exclude every algorithm that uses min-cut or submodular minimisation as a subroutine.

The displayed family itself has minimum zero at any assignment of Hamming weight \(k\).
