## 1. An inexpensive bounded filter exposes a scalar decay

Receive the checked one-particle certificate

    P>=I,      A^dagger P+PA<=-gamma P,

and the proved metric occupation/activity bounds in `METRIC_OCCUPATION_AND_ACTIVITY.md`. Select the actual global cutoff K>=1 from that theorem. Let pmax>=||P|| be a certified finite scalar bound and define

    Z=I+P/(K pmax),
    kappa=gamma/(2K pmax),
    S=Gamma(Z^(1/2)),
    Atilde=Z^(1/2) A Z^(-1/2),
    C=Atilde+(kappa/2)I.                                  (1)

Because A+A^dagger<=0,

    A^dagger Z+ZA
       <=-gamma P/(K pmax)<=-kappa Z.

The second inequality uses P>=I and Z<=2I. Thus C+C^dagger<=0. It need not be diagonal or normal. Its forward second-quantized exponential is an explicitly acquired contraction, by the same finite-matrix Julia block construction as stage two.

On N<=K,

    ||S||<sqrt(e)<2,          ||S^(-1)||<=1.               (2)

This is the reason for using Z close to I rather than directly filtering by P. Direct use of Gamma(P^(1/2)) could cost ||P||^(K/2), which would destroy the inverse-accuracy claim. The bounded filter pays a weaker decay kappa, polynomially in K and pmax, while retaining constant filter normalization.

The filters are actual finite circuits. Diagonalize the small positive matrix Z to an absolute error allocated to K; approximate Jacobi/Givens arithmetic requires no eigenvector-gap promise. Apply its second-quantized unitary basis rotation, compute the sum of binary occupations times log of the corresponding eigenvalues, and rotate a success flag with amplitudes

    exp[(sum_i n_i log z_i)/2]/2        for S/2,
    exp[-(sum_i n_i log z_i)/2]        for S^(-1).

Undo the arithmetic and basis rotation. Eigenvalues lie in [1,1+1/K], and sum occupations is at most K. Both amplitudes are valid, and the first has a fixed margin below one. Invalid total-number states have an explicit unitary extension. All arithmetic, rotations, inverses and precision are paid. No observable Gamma(P) is applied by the algorithm.

## 4. A new weighted clock removes the sqrt(t) convolution penalty

Let B0(t)=Gamma(exp(At)) exp(-iH_S t), and retain the physical loss definitions

    f(t)^2=||E^dagger exp(At)||_F^2,
    J(t)=integral_0^t f(s)^2 ds<=M.

Equation (1) also proves

    ||exp(At)||^2<=2 exp(-kappa t).

Define two explicitly acquired cumulative masses

    Jw(t)=integral_0^t exp(kappa s/2)f(s)^2 ds<=3M,
    Iw(t)=integral_0^t exp(-kappa s/2)ds<=2/kappa.         (8)

The first bound follows by integration by parts. With u(s)=tr[F(s)^dagger F(s)], f^2=-u' and u(s)<=2M exp(-kappa s),

    Jw(t)=M-exp(kappa t/2)u(t)
          +(kappa/2)integral_0^t exp(kappa s/2)u(s)ds
          <=3M.

The physical first jump obeys the exact forward convolution

    Jcal K0(t)=Jcal B0(t)
       -i integral_0^t Jcal B0(s)V K0(t-s)ds.             (9)

For this convolution use a row time density `exp(kappa s/2)f(s)^2/Jw(t)` and column density `exp(-kappa s/2)/Iw(t)` on the SAME time-index basis. Their product's square root is `f(s)/sqrt[Jw Iw]`. SELECT uses

    [Jcal B0(s)/(sqrt(K)f(s))]

    d=2a sqrt(K+1).                                     (10)

The overlap is the convolution divided by `4d sqrt(K Jw Iw)`. Known attenuations using (8) convert this into normalization

This replaces the old sqrt(t) normalization by an inverse-decay scale that is paid in the activity-sized window count. The physical jump direction and color in (10) are exactly the acquired original-loss blocks, not the filtered loss matrix or an assumed common-loss stack.

With beta0(t)=sqrt(K)f(t), choose

## 5. Acquiring Jw from a finite matrix without a growing exponential

Set Aw=A+(kappa/4)I. Then the weighted integrand in (8) is `||E^dagger exp(Aw s)||_F^2`. The Z-metric certificate gives an exponential bound for this shifted semigroup; its Euclidean norm is at most sqrt(2). Compute the finite Lyapunov matrix

    Aw^dagger X+X Aw=-EE^dagger,
    X=integral_0^infinity exp(Aw^dagger s)EE^dagger exp(Aw s)ds.

Then

    Jw(t)=tr[X-exp(Aw t)^dagger X exp(Aw t)],
    X>=0,  tr X<=3M.                                   (14)

The inverse Lyapunov operator is bounded, up to finite-dimension norm conversions, by O(1/kappa): integrate the exponentially decaying shifted semigroup. Its finite bit conditioning is therefore explicitly controlled by kappa, R and matrix size. Gaussian elimination on the M^2-dimensional classical linear system, with finite precision and a certified absolute error, acquires X in polynomial time in these bit lengths. This is not a large Fock-space solve.

Scaling/squaring computes exp(Aw t) with O(log(1+Rt)) small-matrix products. The exact intermediate norms are uniformly bounded by sqrt(2), so the required guard bits remain polynomial logarithmic; no growing exp(+kappa t/2) is separately formed. The right CDF Iw is stable elementary expm1 arithmetic.

## 6. Activity, deterministic scheduling and actual system action

The metric theorem supplies

    error_K<=2aT sqrt(K+1)exp(mu/2)2^(-K/2),
    E exp(theta C)<=exp[Lambda T(exp(theta)-1)],
    Jmax=O(Lambda T+log(1/epsilon)).                     (15)

Take a dyadic maximum duration within a factor two of

    min{kappa/[256a^2(K+1)], T/(p+Jmax+3)}.              (16)

The a=0 branch emits nothing from vacuum and uses ordinary system evolution, so it does not divide by a. The T=0 branch applies the prescribed zero-time instruments. At positive a,T, the deterministic number of first-event calls obeys

    S_calls=O(a^2 K(K+1)pmax T/gamma
                   +Lambda T+p+log(1/epsilon)+1),
    S_calls*tau=O(T).                                  (17)

Furthermore a_P a_inv>=a^2, so the first extensive term is at most a polynomial in K,pmax times Lambda T. This is near-linear **activity**, rather than a surviving aT window penalty. In the rapid limit a proportional to sqrt(gamma), with Lambda fixed, the schedule does not regain a sqrt(rate) loop.

The bridge candidate-immigration mean is at most `G_rate*tau/2=O(1/(K+1))`. The actual acquired scalar source therefore has candidate cap `Hcap=O(log(S_calls/epsilon)+log(K+1)+1)` and virtual word cap `Vcap=O(K+Hcap)`, with no rate-sized loop. The first stage-three metric theorem alone improved horizon dependence but still left windows of size 1/(a sqrt(K)). That would lose rapid-limit activity scaling when a grows as sqrt(rate).
