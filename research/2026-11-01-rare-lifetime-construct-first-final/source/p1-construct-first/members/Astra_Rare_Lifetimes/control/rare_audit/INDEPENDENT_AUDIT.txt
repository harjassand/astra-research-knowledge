INDEPENDENT AUDIT: NONREVERSIBLE SCREENED HITTING AND SHORT-CYCLE RATE STABILITY
1 November 2026

VERDICT

The two stated mathematical inequalities are correct under the finite-state
contracts below. I independently derived them and checked 240 screened-hitting
instances and 120 rate-perturbation instances in exact Fraction arithmetic.
The audit identified an algorithmic precision caveat: relative accuracy of the
screened exit rate does not always give relative accuracy of R_s because its
definition subtracts 1/s. A sufficient rare-target condition or an alternate
estimator is needed. Several interface qualifications remain essential.

FILES

exact_audit.py: independent rational generator construction, stationary-law
checks, linear solves and inequalities; no dependency on root implementation.
exact_audit_results.json: actual output from running that script.

1. CONVENTIONS AND REQUIRED INPUTS

Q is a finite irreducible continuous-time generator acting on column functions
by (Qf)(x)=sum_y q_xy f(y); its rows sum to zero. pi is its unique positive
stationary law. L=-Q. Inner products are weighted by pi. Adjoints always mean
the pi-weighted adjoint. Let H=(L+L*)/2 and assume a supplied/earned g>0 with

   <f,Hf> >= g ||f||_pi^2 whenever E_pi f=0.

This is the gap of the additive symmetric part, not merely a real-part bound on
eigenvalues of a nonnormal generator. A target A is nonempty and proper; write
p=pi(A), b=1-p, B=A^c. The promised output is

   M=E_(pi conditioned on B) tau_A.

The formula concerns this mean. It does not by itself identify a full lifetime
distribution, a worst-start mean, or a rare-event probability over a deadline.

2. FORWARD-EXIT FLUX IS CORRECT EVEN WITHOUT REVERSIBILITY

For s>0 let u=E_x exp(-s tau_A), so u=1 on A and

   (sI-Q_B)u_B=q_BA 1.

Equivalently, (L+s)u=0 on B. On A,

   (Lu)(x)=sum_(y in B) q_xy [1-u(y)].

Stationarity gives <1,Lu>=0, hence

   sum_A pi(x)(Lu)(x)=s sum_B pi(y)u(y).

Therefore the stated FORWARD exit quantity

   r_s=s+E_(pi|A) sum_(y in B) q_xy[1-u(y)]

satisfies

   p r_s=s C,   C=E_pi u.

No time-reversal, detailed balance or backward exit rates are required. Since
p<C<1 for a nontrivial finite irreducible chain and finite s>0,

   R_s=1/(p r_s)-1/s=(1-C)/(s C)>0.

The companion identity useful for an alternate estimator is

   M_s=E_(pi|B) min(tau_A,E_s),  E_s~Exp(s) independent,
   R_s=b M_s/(1-s b M_s),

because 1-u=s E min(tau_A,E_s) pointwise on B.

3. EXACT COMPRESSED-RESOLVENT REPRESENTATION

Let V be the subspace of mean-zero functions that are constant on A, Pi_V its
orthogonal projector, and

   z=1_A/p-1 in V,   C0=Pi_V L|V.

Use C0 here to avoid confusing this operator with the scalar C=E_pi u above.
Its symmetric part is at least gI. Define

   w_s=(u/(E_pi u)-1)/s.

Then w_s belongs to V, w_s|A=R_s, and (L+s)w_s=-1 on B.
For every v in V, stationarity and the zero mean of w_s imply

   <v,(L+s)w_s>=v|A=<v,z>.

Consequently

   w_s=(C0+sI)^(-1)z,
   R_s=<z,(C0+sI)^(-1)z>.

At s=0, let t=E_x tau_A, t=0 on A, L_B t_B=1_B. The function

   w_0=E_pi t-t

belongs to V and solves C0 w_0=z. Its A value is E_pi t=b M, so

   R_0=<z,C0^(-1)z>=b M.

Every inverse here is on the finite indicated space. None is an inverse of the
singular full generator.

4. TWO-SIDED NONREVERSIBLE SCREENING BOUND

Write C0=H0+K0 with H0 symmetric positive definite, H0>=gI, and K0 skew.
For H_s=H0+sI,

   Sym[(C0+sI)^(-1)]
      = [H_s+K0* H_s^(-1)K0]^(-1).

This follows by multiplying the inverse quadratic form; it is valid for a
nonnormal C0. Put kappa=1+s/g. Then

   H0 <= H_s <= kappa H0,
   kappa^(-1)H0^(-1) <= H_s^(-1) <= H0^(-1).

If S_s=H_s+K0*H_s^(-1)K0, these inequalities give

   kappa^(-1) S_0 <= S_s <= kappa S_0.

Invert, evaluate at z, and use R_0=bM. The result is exactly

   R_s/[b(1+s/g)] <= M <= (1+s/g)R_s/b.

The two-sided form matters. R_s need not decrease with s. For a unit-rate
directed six-cycle, A={0}, s=1/10, exact arithmetic gives

   p=1/6, b=5/6, M=3,
   R_s=649150/257187 > bM=5/2.

A reversible-style assertion R_s<=bM would therefore be false.

5. SHORT PERMUTATION CYCLES GIVE THE CLAIMED SECTOR CONSTANT

Now assume the actual generator has the separately supplied structure

   L=sum_a lambda_a (I-P_a), lambda_a>0,

where each P_a is a known permutation, all of whose cycles have length at most
ell>=2. Uniform pi is stationary. If the resulting chain is irreducible, it is
the unique stationary law. No estimation of pi is then needed, although target
mass and conditional target sampling can still be difficult.

For K_a=I-P_a and H_a=(K_a+K_a*)/2, a cycle eigenvalue exp(i theta) gives

   |1-exp(i theta)|/[1-cos(theta)]=1/|sin(theta/2)|
       <= csc(pi/ell)

on the nonzero range. Thus, for all f,h, including vectors extended by zero
from B,

   |<f,K_a h>| <= c_ell sqrt(<f,H_a f><h,H_a h>),
   c_ell=csc(pi/ell).

Cauchy--Schwarz across a proves the corresponding bound for an arbitrary
relative rate perturbation E=Lhat-L:

   |<f,E h>| <= epsilon c_ell
                        sqrt(<f,Hf><h,Hh>)

whenever |lambdahat_a/lambda_a-1|<=epsilon<1 for EVERY a. Also

   Hhat >= (1-epsilon) H.

These statements compress to B unchanged. The inverse on B exists because A
is nonempty and both generators remain irreducible.

6. RATE ROBUSTNESS PROOF

Set v=1_B and use Dirichlet restrictions in this paragraph. Write

   a=<v,L_B^(-1)v>=bM,
   ahat=<v,Lhat_B^(-1)v>=b Mhat.

The two b values coincide because both generators have the same uniform pi.
By the resolvent identity,

   ahat-a=-<L_B^(-*)v,E_B Lhat_B^(-1)v>.

The H-energy of the first argument equals a. The H-energy of the second is at
most ahat/(1-epsilon). Hence

   |ahat-a| <= d sqrt(a ahat),
   d=epsilon c_ell/sqrt(1-epsilon).

With t=sqrt(ahat/a), this is |t^2-1|<=d t. Solving the quadratic gives

   exp(-2 asinh(d/2)) <= Mhat/M <= exp(2 asinh(d/2)).

This is the claimed formula. On one event bounding every primitive rate, it
holds for EVERY nonempty proper target simultaneously; adaptive later target
selection does not require an extra statistical union bound. For reversible
libraries the estimate is conservative compared with direct Loewner bounds.

The short-cycle factor cannot simply be discarded. A twelve-cycle with equal
unit forward/backward rates, changed to 6/5 and 4/5 respectively, gives

   Mhat/M=11369157/15082067 ~= 0.7538195527

for A={0}. This violates the same formula with c_ell incorrectly set to one.
The forward shift is a cycle of length twelve, so it is outside an ell=2 or
ell=3 primitive contract even though each state transition looks spatially
local. Primitive cycle length, rather than graphical edge length, is what the
proof uses.

7. ALGORITHM PRECISION CAVEAT AND A SUFFICIENT REPAIR

Directly computing R_s from a relatively estimated r_s entails cancellation:

   |d log R_s/d log r_s|=1/(1-C),  C=p r_s/s.

This becomes arbitrarily large when C approaches one. It would be incorrect to
assert the same relative accuracy for r_s and R_s for arbitrary targets merely
because p is small.

Let Lambda be a valid total marked-clock rate, including null primitive calls.
Then r_s<=s+Lambda. The checkable rare-target condition

   p <= s/[2(s+Lambda)]

therefore ensures C<=1/2. More generally any established upper bound C<=c<1
allows a controlled conversion with its explicit 1/(1-c) sensitivity. Finite
error formulas, not just a derivative, should be used in the final confidence
interval transformation.

If the rare-target condition fails, 1/p<=2(1+Lambda/s). Ordinary direct
hitting-time simulation is then already polynomial in Lambda/g at the usual
choice s proportional to epsilon*g. Indeed functions vanishing on A obey

   <f,Hf> >= g p ||f||_pi^2,

because (E_pi f)^2<=b||f||_pi^2. The killed semigroup is an L2 contraction with
decay exp(-gp t), yielding

   Pr_(pi|B)(tau_A>t)<=exp(-gp t), M<=1/(gp).

Its second moment is at most 2/(gp)^2. With the first holding-time lower bound
M>=1/Lambda, ordinary averaging/median-of-means gives a fully paid polynomial
fallback. Its exact constants and restarting costs should be included if used;
this audit establishes the needed bounds, not a missing implementation.

An alternative is the bulk-start screened-time estimator

   R_s=b M_s/(1-s b M_s).

When C>1/2 this transform is stable. Independent killing gives
E[T^2]<=2E[T]/s for T=min(tau_A,E_s), while M_s>=1/(Lambda+s). Both target-A
and target-B initializers and the counts of short restarts must be charged.

8. DATA AND OPERATION GATES THAT MUST NOT DISAPPEAR

(a) Marked Poisson primitive clocks, including null firings, make every global
rate observable without visiting a rare enabling state. Observing only the
state path does not provide those counts. The stronger instrument is a real
resource. Counting null events is not a harmless change of notation.

(b) The primitive maps P_a must be actually supplied, known and executable at
a stated cost. Learning their rates does not learn unknown action maps on an
exponentially large state space. If actions require fresh native access, later
simulation is not a source-free computational service.

(c) Uniform pi follows mathematically from the permutation library, but p=|A|/N
and an exact uniform sample from A do not follow efficiently from a membership
test. Rejection can cost 1/p; counting can be intractable. An explicit target
enumerator, structured event sampler or separately acquired target service is
needed. For a coordinate pattern in a product uniform state space these
operations are ordinary and cheap; for arbitrary implicit targets they are not.

(d) The gap g must be supplied or earned. Rate estimates can preserve a known
structural gap: Hhat>=(1-epsilon)H implies ghat>=(1-epsilon)g. This does not by
itself acquire a gap for an unknown kinetic architecture.

(e) Calibrating all rates requires observation time set by the smallest rate
one insists on estimating relatively. Null-clock visibility removes state
rarity, not the waiting time for a physically rare primitive clock. A claimed
logarithmic-state-space result must retain the number of clocks and rate scale.

(f) Uniformization plus an independent kill category is a valid simulation of
the learned generator. Each trajectory has expected number of primitive-clock
events at most Lambda/s; null events and the initial boundary choice count.
For fixed-time or high-confidence computational budgets, geometric tails or
explicit truncation error must be paid, not replaced by an expected-cost claim.

(g) A physical system may have nonuniform equilibrium, state-dependent attempt
rates, hidden controllers, or unobserved null events. Such a system is outside
the present short permutation-cycle acquisition model until an actual reduction
and its costs are supplied.

9. EXECUTED EVIDENCE

240 exact tests used nonuniform rational stationary laws with added positive
reset flow (certified gap lower bound g=nu) and independent directed cycle
currents. They tested the forward flux identity, the screened resolvent scalar
identity and both sides of the theorem for varied proper targets and s/g.

120 exact tests used connected permutation libraries with maximum cycle length
2, 3, 4 or 6, rational positive rates and simultaneous rational relative rate
changes bounded by 1/5. The exponential/asinh comparison was checked through
the algebraically equivalent exact rational inequality

   (Mhat-M)^2 <= [epsilon^2 c_ell^2/(1-epsilon)] M Mhat,

where c_ell^2 is rational for those four cycle lengths. All tests passed.
The finite checks supplement the full proofs above; they neither establish
historical novelty nor qualify a physical device or its observation interface.
