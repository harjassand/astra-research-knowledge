1. DEFINITIONS, INCLUDING THE TWO DIFFERENT C SYMBOLS

Retain Q, L=-Q, pi, A, p=pi(A), b=1-p, M=E_(pi|B)tau_A and g>0 from the mean
audit. V is the pi-mean-zero subspace of functions constant on A, z=1_A/p-1,
and K=Pi_V L|V. Its symmetric part H is at least gI. Define

   R_s=<z,(K+sI)^(-1)z>,
   c_s=1/R_s, c_0=1/(bM), delta=c_0/g.

The lowercase c_s is a scalar inverse resolvent, not the compressed generator
K. At imaginary s all inverses exist: K+i omega is strictly accretive, and
Re R_(i omega)>0 because z is nonzero. This justifies reciprocation.

The initial distribution for the unconditional statement is pi. Its hitting
time is zero on A and has an atom p at zero. Its mean is bM. Define

   Y=tau_A/(bM),
   phi_Y(t)=E_pi exp(-itY).

Using a minus sign in the characteristic function is consistent with the
Laplace convention s=i omega and has no effect on the smoothing inequality.

2. THE COMPLEX DISK INEQUALITY

The resolvent identity gives

   R_(i omega)-R_0
     =-i omega <K^(-*)z,(K+i omega)^(-1)z>.

The squared norms of these two vectors are bounded respectively by R_0/g and
Re R_(i omega)/g. To see this, their H-energies are exactly R_0 and
Re R_(i omega), while H>=gI. Thus

   |R_(i omega)-R_0|^2
       <= (omega/g)^2 R_0 Re R_(i omega).

Put w=c_(i omega)/c_0=R_0/R_(i omega). Divide by |R_(i omega)|^2 to obtain

   |w-1|^2 <= (omega/g)^2 Re w.

This argument does not use normality or detailed balance. It is an exact
resolvent-energy inequality for a real finite accretive compression.

3. CONSEQUENCES WITHIN THE FREQUENCY WINDOW

Write a=|omega|/g. The disk can be written

   (Re w-1-a^2/2)^2+(Im w)^2 <= a^2+a^4/4.

For 0<=a<=1/2 it gives

   |w-1| <= a sqrt(1+a^2/4)+a^2/2 <= 2a,
   Re w >= 1+a^2/2-a sqrt(1+a^2/4) >= 1/2.

Now omega=t c_0, so a=delta |t|. Assume delta<=1/4 and
|t|<=1/(2delta). Then |Im w|<=2delta|t|, giving

   |w+it|^2 >= 1/4+(1-2delta)^2 t^2
               >= (1+t^2)/4.

The scalar identity from the mean proof analytically extends to imaginary
frequency:

   E_pi exp(-s tau_A)=1/[1+sR_s]=c_s/(c_s+s).

It follows either by the finite killed linear system or by the compressed
resolvent derivation. Therefore

   phi_Y(t)=w/(w+it).

Subtract the mean-one exponential characteristic function 1/(1+it):

   |phi_Y(t)-(1+it)^(-1)|
      = |t| |w-1|/[|w+it| sqrt(1+t^2)]
      <= 4delta t^2/(1+t^2).

This verifies the proposed characteristic bound with the stated window and
constants.

4. CDF SMOOTHING AND CONDITIONAL INITIALIZATION

Assuming the conservative Esseen inequality for a comparison distribution with
density bounded by one,

   D_CDF <= (1/pi) integral_(-T)^T |phi_Y(t)-phi_Exp(t)|/|t| dt
               +24/(pi T),

insert T=1/(2delta). Direct integration gives exactly

   D(delta)=min{1,
       (4delta/pi) log[1+1/(4delta^2)]+48delta/pi}.

The exponential comparison CDF is globally Lipschitz with constant one, so its
corner at zero and the atom in the hitting-time law do not defeat the density
condition. The computation agent owns the independent primary-source check of
the smoothing constant; this audit verifies the substitution and arithmetic.

For every x>=0, F_pi(x)=p+bF_B(x). Consequently

   |F_B(x)-F_Exp(x)| <= [D(delta)+p]/b.

The same time scale bM is used in both F_B and F_Exp here. The conditional mean
is M, not bM. Relabeling this as an exponential with mean M without paying the
scale change would be incorrect.

A useful consistency fact is

   R_0 <= ||z||_pi^2/g = b/(pg),
   hence delta >= p/b.

Thus delta<=1/4 automatically implies p<=1/5. The atom at zero is compatible
with the small-delta premise; it cannot be held large while taking delta to
zero.

If one specifically wants the conditional comparison exponential to have mean
M, the exact CDF distance between exponentials of means bM and M is

   p b^(b/p),

obtained by maximizing exp(-x)-exp(-x/b). This can be added to the displayed
conditional bound. No such change is required for the root's stated bM scale.

5. SIMULTANEOUS DEADLINE ENVELOPE FROM AN ACQUIRED MEAN INTERVAL

Suppose one actual confidence event gives 0<Mlo<=M<=Mhi, p is known, and g_lo
is a genuine lower bound on the true additive-symmetric gap. Put

   delta_max=1/[g_lo b Mlo].

If delta_max<=1/4, D(delta) is nondecreasing for delta>0, so the same event gives
the common error E=min(1,[D(delta_max)+p]/b) and, for EVERY physical deadline
T>=0,

   max{0,1-exp[-T/(b Mhi)]-E}
       <= Pr_(pi|B)(tau_A<=T)
       <= min{1,1-exp[-T/(b Mlo)]+E}.

These inequalities include all later adaptively chosen deadlines on the same
event. There is no additional statistical union bound over T. A rate-learning
confidence event can supply the mean interval via the separately proved
short-cycle stability theorem. The generator gap and initialization remain
those of the TRUE process, not an unqualified fitted process.

This is an ADDITIVE CDF guarantee. If the relevant failure probability is much
smaller than E, it does not give relative accuracy for that early rare tail.
The theorem is also not a worst-start or arbitrary prepared-state lifetime
theorem; its initial law is pi or pi conditioned on B. Acquiring/preparing that
law is a separate operational requirement.
