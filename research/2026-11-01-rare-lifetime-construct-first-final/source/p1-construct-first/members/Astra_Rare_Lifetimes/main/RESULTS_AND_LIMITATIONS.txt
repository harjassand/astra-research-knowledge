Small-system ordinary benchmark
================================
The root directly solved killed Poisson systems on19 finite cases. This is
the correct ordinary method at those sizes and was not declared inferior.
One64-state fast-recrossing fixture has true mean0.6989965392201121. A
flux-only shortcut gives0.10271460014673514, only0.1469457921 of the correct
answer. Its screened estimate0.6951598084179247 lies within the proved interval
[0.6877119731842238,0.7026883027819464]. This is a failure of discarding return
information, not of strong conventional potential theory.

Failures preserved because they restrict the claim
==================================================
1. Coarse indicator substitution. The pinned N1213 heatbath counterexample
   disproves a singleton Green identity applied to a coarse set. Its small
   killed solve is exact and easy. The present mechanism keeps boundary return
   information explicitly rather than assuming the target can be one label.

2. One-sided nonreversible screening. A directed6-cycle with rate1, target
   one vertex and s=1/10 has exact mean3, yet R_s/(1-p)>3. Reversible monotonicity
   cannot be used. The proved nonreversible interval has two sides.

3. Rarity without separation. A directed128-cycle has target mass1/128 but
   delta=1/(g b M) about13.074 and CDF discrepancy about0.14834. The lifetime
   approximation requires fast relaxation relative to the mean, not small p
   alone. Several moderate-delta CDF certificates are vacuous and retained.

4. Long cycles. Changing clockwise/anticlockwise rates on an n-cycle from
   (1,1) to(1+eta,1-eta) preserves uniform stationarity. Mean singleton hitting
   times change from n(n+1)/12 to asymptotically n/(4eta). Thus fixed relative
   rate accuracy plus a shared stationary distribution does not give a
   dimension-independent lifetime sensitivity bound. Local cycle geometry
   or another earned form bound is necessary.

5. Complex cancellation. At target mass10^-7, solving for a transform and
   subtracting1-m_s caused a spurious imaginary capacity error about6.6e-7.
   The ordinary centered bordered-system solve removed it. Source precision
   and stable numerical representation are separate obligations.

6. Unknown target access. For a uniformly hidden singleton A={z} in2^n
   strings, even supplying p=2^-n leaves a membership-only algorithm with
   success at most(k+1)/2^n after k queries and one output. Conditional
   initialization cannot be earned in polynomial time from that interface.
   The executed cylinder grammar has an actual transfer-matrix construction.

7. Intervention modularity. Two devices can share the same Gibbs law and
   every measured clamped rate, but multiply all FREE rates by different
   constants. All calibration observations agree while free lifetimes differ
   inversely by that constant. Therefore native-kernel invariance under local
   clamps is a genuine architectural/physical condition. Gibbs detailed
   balance alone does not imply it.

8. Thermal visibility. A rank-one spectral difference with normalized
   Hilbert--Schmidt norm Delta/sqrt(N) changes relative partition function by
   about1/2 and energy by more thanDelta/6 at beta*Delta=log(N). The pinned
   nonlinear masking service can hide that direction. For one fixed linear
   operator perturbation, conventional filtering/range finding recovers it;
   the included N=16384 run used1568 vector actions. The lower bound is not
   incorrectly extended to that stronger service.

9. Quantum entropy. The Hilbert--Schmidt relaxation ingredient does not
   directly give arbitrary-state entropy contraction. Nonuniform diagonal
   full-rank states have zero initial entropy production in the stated
   Hamiltonian-plus-diagonal-dephasing dynamics. Delayed entropy contraction
   remains a separate unproved inequality, not a completed capability.

Limits that remain part of every claim
======================================
The means concern stationary initial laws conditioned outside the target.
Arbitrary preparations, worst-state lifetimes and active feedback protocols
are not covered. The CDF guarantee is additive; relative errors for extremely
early rare failure probabilities can remain uncontrolled. The physical time
to a rare event has not been shortened: a numerical prediction is computed.

Known local channel architecture, calibrated absolute clocks, no hidden
unmodelled channels, and their continuation after calibration are source
assumptions. The learned Gibbs extension acquires factors and cycle rates,
not an arbitrary unknown graph, thermodynamic mechanism or quantum device.

The simulations have expected work bounds. Unbounded geometric stopping is
actually used; maximum observed counts are reported. A hard runtime cap would
need an explicit additional failure allowance. Each initial boundary proposal,
null event and conditional initialization is charged. In the Gibbs experiment
background clocks are distinguished from selected observed counters.

The finite confidence statements assume the ideal sampling model. Seeded
numerical samples are not a theorem about a deterministic PRNG's independence.
Reported seconds are computation timings; synthetic aggregation of trillions
of counts is not a physical observation campaign.

The exact historical novelty of the inequalities is unestablished. Existing
rare-event, nonreversible-capacity and graphical-model theory is strong. No
civilization-changing physical discovery, universal predictor or superiority
over all equally informed conventional algorithms is claimed. The package
preserves the strongest result actually earned and the evidence needed to
challenge, reproduce or extend it.
