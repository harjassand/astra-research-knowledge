FROM THE MEAN TO EVERY DEADLINE
An explicit additive approximation for finite nonreversible chains
Companion to MAIN_PROOF.txt, developed 1 November 2026 Australia/Brisbane

Status and predecessors
=======================
The theorem below is independently reconstructed in two included audits. It
uses established resolvent/potential theory and a classical smoothing theorem.
Exponential approximation for hitting times is established prior work, including
Aldous--Brown in reversible chains and Mathieu (1997) for non-symmetric
processes. This document does not establish historical originality. Its role
is to earn the precise deadline guarantee used with the acquired-rate receiver.

1. Setting and theorem
======================
Use the definitions of MAIN_PROOF.txt: finite irreducible row generator Q,
L=-Q, invariant pi, A nonempty and proper, p=pi(A), b=1-p,
M=E_{pi(.|B)} tau_A, mu=E_pi tau_A=bM. The symmetric Poincare gap is at
least g>0. Reversibility and a sector bound are not assumed in this theorem.
Let
   C_0=1/mu,     delta=C_0/g=1/(g b M).
For 0<delta<=1/4, define
   D(delta)=min{1,
       (4 delta/pi) log(1+1/(4 delta^2)) + 48 delta/pi}.       (L1)
Here the pi in denominators is the numerical circle constant; pi as a
probability measure always has an argument or appears in a subscript.

THEOREM 3 (stationary lifetime law).
   sup_y |P_pi(tau_A/mu<=y)-F_Exp(1)(y)| <= D(delta).          (L2)
In particular, for every t>=0,
   |P_{pi(.|B)}(tau_A<=t) - [1-exp(-t/(bM))]|
      <= min{1,(D(delta)+p)/b}.                             (L3)
This is a uniform ADDITIVE probability bound. It does not yield relative
accuracy for arbitrarily tiny early-deadline probabilities. The initial law
is specified; worst-state or arbitrary prepared-memory lifetimes are not covered.

2. Complex potential identity
==============================
Use the inner product <f,h>_pi=sum_x pi(x) conjugate(f(x)) h(x).
For s=i omega, omega!=0, define
   u_s(x)=E_x exp(-s tau_A),   m_s=pi(u_s),
   h_s=(u_s-m_s)/(1-m_s),     C_s=s m_s/(1-m_s).
From B, a finite-chain hitting law is an absolutely continuous mixture of
positive waiting times. Since b>0, |m_s|<1 for omega!=0, so normalization is
valid. At omega=0 use the continuous limits h_0 and C_0 from MAIN_PROOF.txt.
The finite killed resolvent is nonsingular on the imaginary axis.

As before, pi(h_s)=0, h_s=1 on A, and (L+s)h_s=-C_s on B. Stationarity and
the boundary normalization imply the COMPLEX identity
   <h_s,(L+s)h_s>_pi=C_s.
Let h_0* be the real normalized adjoint potential. Testing differences that
vanish on A and have mean zero gives
   C_s-C_0=s <h_0*,h_s>_pi.                                (L4)
These formulas are valid with the stated sesquilinear convention; no
positivity or reversibility of complex potentials is used.

The symmetric gap yields
   Re C_s >= g ||h_s||_pi^2,
   ||h_0*||_pi^2 <= C_0/g.
Write z=C_s/C_0 and q=omega/g. By Cauchy--Schwarz, (L4) gives
   |z-1|^2 <= q^2 Re z.                                   (L5)
In particular Re z>0. If |q|<=1/2, elementary geometry of this disk yields
   |z-1|<=2|q|,     Re z>=1/2.                            (L6)
For completeness, writing a=|z-1|, Re z<=1+a gives
a^2<=q^2(1+a); its positive root is at most 2|q| in this range. The least
possible real part allowed by (L5) is
1+q^2/2-|q|sqrt(1+q^2/4), which exceeds 1/2 at |q|<=1/2.

3. Transform control
=====================
Put x=omega/C_0; then q=delta x. For |x|<=1/(2delta), (L6) applies. Since
delta<=1/4,
   |Im z|<=2delta |x|<=|x|/2,
   |z+ix|>=sqrt((1/2)^2+(|x|/2)^2)
          =(1/2)sqrt(1+x^2).
The transform of Y=tau_A/mu from stationary pi is
   E exp(-ixY)=m_{i C_0 x}=z/(z+ix).
The Exp(1) transform in the same sign convention is 1/(1+ix). Hence
   |E exp(-ixY)-(1+ix)^-1|
     = |ix(z-1)/[(z+ix)(1+ix)]|
     <= 4delta x^2/(1+x^2)                               (L7)
throughout |x|<=1/(2delta). Using the positive characteristic-function
convention simply conjugates both transforms and changes no estimate.

4. Converting transforms to a CDF
=================================
Use the classical smoothing inequality in Flajolet--Sedgewick, Analytic
Combinatorics, Theorem IX.5 (citing Feller vol. II, p.538): for a comparison
CDF G with density bounded by m,
   ||F-G||_infinity
      <= (1/pi) integral_{-T}^{T}|(phi(x)-gamma(x))/x| dx
         +24m/(pi T).
Author-hosted source: https://algo.inria.fr/flajolet/Publications/book.pdf
Only this standard theorem, not a new Fourier inversion theorem, is used here.
The Exp(1) density is bounded by 1. Its corner at zero is handled by
convolution with a smooth approximate identity preserving that bound and
then taking a uniform limit. An atom in F, including mass p at zero, is allowed.

With T=1/(2delta), (L7) makes the integral term
   (4delta/pi) log(1+T^2),
and the smoothing remainder is 48delta/pi. This proves (L2).

Since P_pi(tau_A<=t)=p+b P_{pi(.|B)}(tau_A<=t) for t>=0, subtracting the
same exponential CDF and dividing by b gives (L3).

The conditional law has mean M, while the exponential in (L3) has mean bM.
If a mean-M comparison is desired, the exact uniform discrepancy between
these two exponential CDFs is p b^(b/p)<=p. Adding that quantity to (L3) is
valid. We retain bM in the executable deadline certificate to avoid this
unnecessary additional approximation.

The condition is about relaxation separation, not rarity alone. Indeed the
compressed mean-zero resolvent or the killed-semigroup bound gives
   bM<=b/(gp), hence delta>=p/b.
A directed 128-cycle has p=1/128 but delta about 13.074; the independent
audit measures CDF discrepancy about 0.14834. It fails the separation
hypothesis and is preserved as a useful counterexample to a stronger claim.

5. Composition with a learned mean interval
===========================================
Suppose a simultaneous confidence event gives M in [M_lo,M_hi], and a genuine
structural or independently acquired lower bound g_lo on the TRUE gap is known.
Put delta_max=1/(g_lo b M_lo). If delta_max<=1/4, set
   E=min{1,(D(delta_max)+p)/b}.
The expression inside the minimum in (L1) increases on (0,1/4]: its derivative
before multiplication by 4/pi is
   log(1+1/(4delta^2))-2/(1+4delta^2)+12 > 0.
Thus the SAME confidence event gives, for EVERY t>=0,
   max{0,1-exp(-t/(b M_hi))-E}
      <= P_{pi(.|B)}(tau_A<=t)
      <= min{1,1-exp(-t/(b M_lo))+E}.                      (L8)
There is no further union bound over a deadline chosen after the model and
mean interval are inspected. A new target Monte Carlo estimate still needs
its own properly allocated failure budget.

6. Executed certificate and arithmetic
======================================
The retained 64-bit experiment has known single-flip rate floor 1/2, so the
TRUE gap is at least 1 without using the hidden true fitted error. Each
target has p=2^-48 and qualified M_lo>2.73e12. Thus delta_max<4e-13.
At delta_u=4e-13, pi>3 and
   log(1+1/(4delta_u^2))<60
imply D(delta_u)<96delta_u=3.84e-11. The executable checks the logarithm
inequality through a positive rational Taylor lower bound on exp(60).
The conditional law error is therefore less than the conservatively
chosen rational E=10^-10 for all three targets.

deadline_certificate.py evaluates (L8) at t=10^12 using exact Fraction
arithmetic. For exp(-x), it bounds exp(x) by a positive Taylor sum and its
geometric remainder, then reciprocates. Printed decimal endpoints are
directed outward. Results are in deadline_results.json. The joint confidence
remains at least 0.998527301001297196 under the declared independent sampling
model. No additional source observations or simulations are used for this
deadline-law upgrade.

7. Numerical implementation warning and comparison
===================================================
The independent computation audit found a real numerical failure when solving
for u_s and then subtracting 1-m_s for a very rare target. A stable ordinary
repair solves the centered bordered system directly:
   [L_B+sI, 1; pi_B^T, 0] [h_B; C_s] = [-L_BA 1; -p].
The audit preserves both the failure and successful centered implementation.
This is a numerical conditioning repair, not a new access primitive.

For reversible systems, Aldous--Brown already gives the sharper uniform
unconditional error delta. The O(delta log(1/delta)) bound here is useful as
an explicit finite nonreversible certificate and in the acquired-model
composition. No assertion is made that it is the sharpest known possible
bound for every subclass, or that exponential lifetime laws themselves are
a new discovery.
