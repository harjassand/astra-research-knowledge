LEARNING RARE LIFETIMES FROM LOCAL CLOCKS
Finite-error computation for driven Markov systems
Research developed 1 November 2026 (Australia/Brisbane)

Scope and status
===============
This is a self-contained mathematical construction and reproducible synthetic
calculation. It is not an experimentally validated device, an algorithm for
arbitrary unknown dynamics, or an established historical-priority claim.
Independent mathematical reconstructions and exact finite checks are included.
The positive result uses established Markov potential theory, sector estimates,
Poisson clocks and Monte Carlo. The proposed addition is the complete chain from
acquired local rate uncertainty, through uniform rare-target stability, to a
finite-error short-excursion receiver and later decisions.

Prior Astra research is bound to repository harjassand/astra-research-knowledge,
revision 51ce47ce39f976517d60e2f87bbe280b651f6056. Its 00_START_HERE.txt was the
first corpus file read. The decisive inherited obstruction is N1213's failure
of replacing a singleton return identity by a coarse-target indicator. The new
proof below is independent of any unavailable attachment from that record.
Source paths, conditions, comparisons and useful alternative failures are in
SOURCES_AND_COMPARISONS.txt and the independent notes in this package.

1. Intended consequence and exact contract
=========================================
Question: Can finite, ordinary-state calibration support reliable later
prediction of rare first-passage times in a driven stochastic system, without
waiting for those passages, preserving its entire long trajectory, or assuming
a precomputed committor? We construct such a service for the following class.

The source has a finite state space Omega and known executable maps P_a. Each
P_a is a permutation preserving the same known probability pi; uniform pi is
the principal explicit implementation. Every cycle of every P_a has length at
most ell >= 2. Independent Poisson clocks of unknown positive rates lambda_a
apply these maps. A clock mark remains observable even if its map fixes the
current state. This last requirement is an observation resource, not a
consequence of observing state changes.

On functions, with row-generator convention,
   Q f(x) = sum_a lambda_a [ f(P_a x) - f(x) ],    L = -Q.
The maps and positive support are structural input. A known lower rate bound
or another valid rate-acquisition stopping guarantee is required. Irreducibility
is assumed or earned by an explicit independently flipping component.

For a target A, let B=Omega\A, p=pi(A), b=pi(B), with 0<p<1, and
   tau_A = inf{t>=0 : X_t in A},
   M_A = E_{pi(.|B)} tau_A.
This initial distribution is part of the output contract. A worst-state mean,
an arbitrary preparation, and a driven feedback trajectory are different tasks.

Later target queries must supply an efficient membership test, the actual mass
p and a sampler for pi(.|A). For some fallback procedures pi(.|B) is also used.
Membership alone does not supply counting or conditional sampling. In the
explicit n-bit model, A is a cylinder fixing m specified bits to a pattern:
p=2^-m, and sampling the remaining bits is exact and costs O(n) bits/work.
The target contains 2^(n-m) configurations and is not a singleton.

A lower bound g>0 on the Poincare gap of the SYMMETRIC PART is required:
   E_L(f):=Re <f,Lf>_pi >= g ||f||_pi^2 whenever pi(f)=0.        (1)
The gap is not the minimum real part of the nonnormal spectrum. For independent
single-bit flips of rates at least c, (1) holds with g=2c. Adding any of the
pi-preserving cycle primitives adds a nonnegative symmetric energy, so keeps
this bound. This proves a gap for the implemented architecture without spectral
analysis of its exponential state space.

The retained source model consists of the known maps and acquired rates. All
future simulation and numerical operations are paid. There is no later physical
source use in the explicit receiver, conditional on source stationarity and the
same primitive mechanism continuing to define the target task.

2. What strong conventional machinery already provides
======================================================
For a supplied finite generator, the ordinary killed Poisson solve
   L_B t_B = 1_B,  t_A=0,
returns the exact desired mean <1_B,t>_pi/b. It is the correct comparator on
small systems and wins there. It requires a representation of the killed
problem; a full matrix has exponential dimension in the n-bit example.

Singleton return-Green identities already allow short-return calculations.
They do not extend by simply inserting a coarse indicator. The pinned N1213
two-spin example has a full gap >=1/2, yet that substitution overestimates the
true coarse mean by a factor tending to 10/9 as the event becomes rare.

Flux corrections for recrossing are also established: Aldous's Poisson clumping
construction uses stationary outward flux and a short-term no-return factor,
including coarse targets and nonreversible examples. Soft capacities of
Bianchi, Gaudilliere and Milanesi provide an established exponential killing
construction in reversible potential theory. Gaudilliere-Landim and Huang-Mao
provide nonreversible capacity/mean variational principles. Local rare-target
simulation is not a newly invented idea here.

The remaining work addressed here is an explicit finite-error receiver for a
learned nonreversible source, including a global rate-uncertainty theorem that
does not multiply calibration error by the enormous passage horizon. An
ordinary potential-theory or kinetic simulation implementation may adopt these
ingredients. No fastest-algorithm or historical-originality claim follows.

3. A finite-error screened mean theorem, without reversibility
============================================================
Assume a finite irreducible Q with stationary pi and (1). This section does
not require a permutation decomposition or a sector bound for L.

For s>0, define
   u_s(x) = E_x exp(-s tau_A),
so u_s=1 on A and (L+s)u_s=0 on B. Let E_s be an independent Exp(s) clock,
and kappa_s(y)=P_y(E_s<tau_A)=1-u_s(y) for y in B. Define the boundary quantity
   r_s = s + E_{x~pi(.|A)} sum_{y in B} q_xy kappa_s(y),       (2)
   R_s = 1/(p r_s) - 1/s.                                  (3)

THEOREM 1.
   R_s/[b(1+s/g)] <= M_A <= (1+s/g) R_s/b.                  (4)
For reversible Q, the stronger one-sided bracket is
   R_s/b <= M_A <= (1+s/g) R_s/b.                           (5)
The reversible one-sided claim is false for general nonreversible Q. The exact
directed-cycle counterexamples in the package verify the need for both sides.

Proof of the flux identity. Stationarity gives pi(Lu_s)=0. On B, Lu_s=-s u_s;
on A, Lu_s(x)=sum_{y in B}q_xy[1-u_s(y)]. Therefore
   p r_s = s pi(u_s).                                     (6)
This uses the actual FORWARD exit rates. No adjoint simulator is required.

Proof of (4). Let t=L_B^-1 1_B extended by zero on A, and put
   mu=pi(t)=bM_A,   C_0=1/mu,   h_0=1-t/mu.
Then pi(h_0)=0, h_0=1 on A, and Lh_0=-C_0 on B. Directly,
   <h_0,Lh_0>_pi = C_0.                                   (7)
For the stationary adjoint L*, its killed mean is the same scalar mu, because
<1_B,L_B^-1 1_B>=<1_B,(L_B*)^-1 1_B>. Its corresponding potential h_0* also
has mean zero, equals one on A, and has energy C_0.

Let m_s=pi(u_s),
   h_s=(u_s-m_s)/(1-m_s),
   C_s=s m_s/(1-m_s)=1/R_s.
Then h_s has mean zero, equals one on A, and (L+s)h_s=-C_s on B. Consequently
   <h_s,(L+s)h_s>_pi=C_s.                                 (8)
Every difference between these normalized potentials belongs to
   W={w : w|A=0, pi(w)=0}.
For every such w, <w,(L+s)h_s>=0 and <h_0*,Lw>=0. Pairing the equations yields
the exact, nonreversible identity
   C_s-C_0 = s <h_0*,h_s>_pi.                              (9)
By (1), (7), and (8),
   ||h_0*||^2 <= C_0/g,   ||h_s||^2 <= C_s/(g+s).
Thus, writing x=C_s/C_0,
   |x-1| <= s sqrt(x/[g(g+s)]).
The two positive roots are g/(g+s) and (g+s)/g. Invert to obtain (4).
In the reversible case, C_s is a minimum of E(h)+s||h||^2 over the same affine
constraint set, so C_s>=C_0 and (5) follows. End proof.

This is a finite theorem for each s, not an unquantified limiting statement.
The screening horizon has expected duration <=1/s. Choosing s proportional to
the requested relative accuracy times g makes the bias small independently
of M_A. All primitive calls made before termination remain charged.

4. Local calibration error controls every later rare target
==========================================================
We next compare true and fitted generators with the SAME invariant pi and the
SAME maps. Put
   L=sum_a lambda_a(I-P_a), Lhat=sum_a lambdahat_a(I-P_a),
and suppose |lambdahat_a/lambda_a-1|<=eta<1 for every a. Set
   K=csc(pi/ell),  d=eta K/sqrt(1-eta),
   rho=2 asinh(d/2),   F=exp(rho).

THEOREM 2. Simultaneously for every nonempty proper A,
   F^-1 <= Mhat_A/M_A <= F.                               (10)
There is no factor involving p, M_A, |Omega|, or the number of primitives.
The theorem concerns a relative mean error, not a trajectory-distance bound.

Proof of the primitive bound. A permutation is unitary in L2(pi) because it
preserves pi. On each length-k cycle its nonconstant Fourier eigenvalues are
exp(2 pi i j/k). For each nonzero mode,
   |1-exp(i theta)|/(1-cos theta)=1/|sin(theta/2)|<=K.
Cauchy-Schwarz in this simultaneous eigenbasis gives
   |<f,(I-P_a)v>| <= K sqrt(E_a(f)E_a(v)).                 (11)
Fixed points contribute zero. Summing over a and applying Cauchy-Schwarz again,
   |<f,(Lhat-L)v>| <= eta K sqrt(E_L(f)E_L(v)),            (12)
   (1-eta)E_L <= E_Lhat <= (1+eta)E_L.                    (13)

Let C=1/[bM_A], Chat=1/[bMhat_A], and use the normalized primal and adjoint
potentials from Section 3. The same affine weak equations give
   Chat-C = <h_0*,(Lhat-L) hhat_0>_pi.                    (14)
Since E_L(h_0*)=C and E_L(hhat_0)<=Chat/(1-eta),
   |Chat-C| <= d sqrt(C Chat).
Solve the quadratic inequality in sqrt(Chat/C). Its endpoint ratios are
exp(+-2 asinh(d/2)); inversion gives (10). End proof.

Alternative proof: use killed resolvents, t=L_B^-1 1 and t*=(L_B*)^-1 1.
The first resolvent identity gives the same geometric-mean relative bound for
mu=bM directly. This independent derivation is in the review notes.

With log-rate error delta=max_a|log(lambdahat_a/lambda_a)|, interpolation gives
the useful equivalent bound
   |log(Mhat_A/M_A)| <= 2 asinh(K sinh(delta/2)).           (15)
For two-cycles K=1 this reduces exactly to delta, the usual reversible
conductance comparison. Cycle decompositions and sector comparisons are
established ingredients; the full proposed empirical composition is distinct.

Uniformity is literal. The rate event implies (12) for all functions, so
targets may be selected after inspecting the acquired model. There is no
union bound over the collection of target sets. Each subsequent Monte Carlo
computation has its own failure budget, however; unlimited simulations do not
share one finite sampling budget without an appropriate allocation.

Necessity of geometric control. On a uniform n-cycle, change clockwise and
anticlockwise rates from (1,1) to (1+eta,1-eta). The stationary distribution is
unchanged and both primitive rates change only relatively by eta. The mean
hit on one site from stationary outside is n(n+1)/12 at eta=0 and asymptotic
to n/(4eta) for fixed positive eta. The ratio diverges with n. These primitive
cycles have length n; a dimension-independent constant does not follow from
stationarity and relative rates alone. This is the standard diffusive-to-drift
phenomenon, retained as a counterexample to dropping the sector requirement.

5. Acquisition from actual observable clocks
============================================
Suppose r known primitives have independent marked Poisson clocks, their
positive rates obey lambda_a>=lambda_min>0, and observation time T is calibrated.
Record all N_a(T), INCLUDING marks that do not change the visible state. Set
lambdahat_a=N_a(T)/T. For 0<eta<=1, ordinary Poisson Chernoff bounds give
   P(exists a: |lambdahat_a/lambda_a-1|>eta)
      <= 2r exp(-lambda_min T eta^2/3).                   (16)
Thus T >= 3 log(2r/alpha)/(lambda_min eta^2) suffices.

If clocks run concurrently, the elapsed source observation time is T, not rT.
The expected number of acquired marks is T sum_a lambda_a. Every marked event
and the timing/label readout are resources. The proof does not require a rare
target preparation or a stationary initial state, because the clock laws are
state independent by hypothesis. A model inferred only from visible jumps
would have a different, generally occupancy-dependent acquisition problem.

The known maps cannot be learned over exponentially many configurations from
these scalar counts. Their validity, the absence of hidden channels, the shared
stationary measure and the ability to resolve marks are structural conditions.
Finite success on ordinary configurations does not certify that structural
contract against arbitrary alternatives. Unknown zero rates are not silently
included in the positive-support theorem.

In the synthetic execution, r=127, lambda_min=1/2, eta=1/50, T=200000. The
source-count union bound is below 0.000411378. Counts are SIMULATED Poisson
observations; this experiment demonstrates arithmetic and algorithm execution,
not that a physical source supplies marked clocks. The actual fitted error in
the synthetic truth was 0.0071875, but the certificate uses 0.02.

6. A positive finite-bit computational receiver
==============================================
Work with the fitted generator. Let Lambda=sum_a lambdahat_a, and let R be the
sum of rates of primitives capable of leaving A. Omitting primitives that
provably cannot leave the cylinder is an exact optimization, not a statistical
assumption. A general implementation may simply use R=Lambda.

One boundary Bernoulli trial:
 (i) Draw x~pi(.|A).
 (ii) Select a candidate leaving primitive with probability lambdahat_a/R.
 (iii) Apply it. If y=P_a x is still in A, return zero.
 (iv) From y simulate the fitted chain until either A is hit (return zero) or
      an independent Exp(s) clock rings (return one).

If theta is the success probability, then
   r_s=s+R theta.                                         (17)
This trial obtains the no-return factor; it does not assume one. It also
includes the correct boundary flux weighting without a rejection factor 1/p.

Finite-bit implementation. When fitted rates and s are rational, combine all
primitive marks and the killing clock into one rational categorical draw with
weights (lambdahat_1,...,lambdahat_r,s). If the kill category appears, terminate.
Otherwise apply the chosen known map, including null moves, and test A. No
exponential real-valued waiting time is needed for this Bernoulli output. Exact
integer rejection sampling implements the rational categories from fair bits.
The subsequent excursion has expected categories at most (Lambda+s)/s; include
one additional initial boundary proposal per trial. Its expected physical-
equivalent excursion duration is at most 1/s. Map execution, membership, conditional
initialization, and category bit costs all remain part of the bill.

Let theta_L<=theta<=theta_U be any sound interval, e.g. a bounded Bernoulli
Hoeffding interval. Define r_L=s+R theta_L and r_U=s+R theta_U. A directly
evaluated bracket for the fitted mean is
   [ (1/r_U-p/s)/(p b (1+s/g)),
     (1/r_L-p/s)(1+s/g)/(p b) ].                           (18)
If a lower endpoint is negative it supplies only zero; the interval must be
refined or a fallback used before claiming a relative answer. Divide the lower
endpoint by F and multiply the upper by F to include true-source uncertainty.

Cancellation qualification. Put C=p r_s/s=pi(u_s). Relative error in r_s is
amplified by 1/(1-C) in R_s. Precisely, if rhat=(1+e)r_s,
   Rhat/R_s = 1 - e/[(1+e)(1-C)].                         (19)
A checkable sufficient condition for C<=1/2 is
   p <= s/[2(s+R)].                                      (20)
All large executed targets satisfy this by many orders of magnitude.

Rarity-independent sample cost. Every mean-zero h with h|A=1 has
||h||^2>=p/b. Consequently C_s>= (g+s)p/b, and using Section 3 gives
   r_s-s >= s g b/(s+g p),
   theta >= s g b/[R(s+g p)].                            (21)
For p<=s/g, theta>=g b/(2R). Multiplicative Bernoulli concentration therefore
needs O(R log(1/alpha)/(g b xi^2)) boundary trials for relative factor xi.
In the stable branch (20), this also controls the relative mean. Taking
s=O(epsilon g) and xi=O(epsilon) gives expected marked simulation work
   O( R Lambda log(1/alpha)/(g^2 b epsilon^3) ),           (22)
apart from known map, initialization and finite-bit factors. There is no 1/p.
This is a sufficient polynomial bound, not an optimality claim. On actual
fixtures theta can be far larger than its sufficient lower bound.

Fallback for targets outside (20). Use the conservative R=Lambda test. If
p>s/[2(s+Lambda)], then 1/p<2(1+Lambda/s), polynomial in the other parameters.
For f supported on B, Var_pi(f)>=p||f||^2, so the killed semigroup contracts
in L2 at rate >=gp. Therefore
   P_{pi(.|B)}(tau_A>t)<=exp(-gp t),
   E tau_A<=1/(gp),  E tau_A^2<=2/(gp)^2.                 (23)
Also M_A>=1/Lambda, since hitting from B requires a state transition and the
total clock rate is Lambda. Ordinary truncated direct simulation with this
tail and lower mean bound gives a polynomial relative-error fallback. Uniform
sampling from B is paid: for a cylinder with p<=1/2, rejection from the uniform
whole space has expected at most two draws; an exact excluded-pattern sampler
is another implementation. No inverse exponentially small p enters this branch.

An alternative uses two short screened receivers: bulk Bernoulli trials from
pi(.|B), with theta_B=P(E_s<tau_A), give
   R_s/b = theta_B/[s(1-b theta_B)].                      (24)
This branch is stable when C=1-b theta_B is bounded below. A constant-accuracy
pilot can select between the complementary stable boundary/bulk branches.
This optional improvement was not used in the reported large run.

Expected versus worst-case work. The categorical count has a geometric tail.
A deterministic per-trial cap can be set using
P(N_events>k)<=(Lambda/(Lambda+s))^k. A run exceeding the cap must be flagged or
its failure allowance included. An unreported deterministic cap would bias the
Bernoulli service. The retained executable runs to actual stopping and reports
every event, including null events; (22) is an expected-work guarantee.

7. Concrete n-bit architecture and executed inputs
=================================================
There are n single-bit flip permutations and n-1 neighboring two-bit cycles.
On each adjacent pair the forward cycle is
   00 -> 01 -> 11 -> 00, with 10 fixed,
or its inverse, alternating along the chain. The global map is a product of
identical local three-cycles over all outside configurations. Thus ell=3 even
though |Omega|=2^n. Positive individual flips make the chain irreducible.
Uniform pi is invariant under every primitive and under any positive reweighting.

Unknown source rates used to generate synthetic observations are listed in
large_results.json. They are spatially inhomogeneous; all lie above 1/2. The
fitted model retains the actual counts divided by T. The symmetric gap of the
fitted chain is at least 2 min_i lambdahat_flip_i =1.4908. Screening uses
s=g/20=0.07454. Its expected horizon is at most 13.4156157768 model-time units.

Three cylinder queries each fix 48 of the 64 bits and leave 16 free. Their
mass is exactly 2^-48. Each uses 30000 boundary trials. The final Bernoulli
radius is 3/250; its individual failure allowance is below 0.000353774.
The source event plus all three query events have joint failure below
0.001472699, under the declared independent sampling model.

The program applies 126339512 excursion categories and 90000 initial boundary
proposals, totaling 126429512 proposal calls across the three queries. It
observed 25389896 synthetic calibration marks. Total wall time was
about 7.77 seconds in this environment including compilation, small dense
controls, synthetic calibration generation and all three calculations. The
physical acquisition model still charges T=200000 time units and every mark;
generating Poisson counts in software does not remove this physical cost.

All final reported mean intervals are re-evaluated with exact Fraction
arithmetic and decimal endpoints rounded outward in qualified_intervals.json.
For eta=1/50 and K<=7/6, the conservative rational source factor 300/293 is
used, rather than rounding the sharper transcendental factor. Screening factor
21/20 and radius 3/250 are rational. Exponential tail allowances are upper
bounded using positive rational Taylor lower bounds for exp.

The random source is a seeded numerical PRNG. The mathematical confidence
theorem is for independent ideal random samples. The implementation is a
reproducible numerical realization of that model, not a proof of physical
randomness or a formal verification of the standard-library sampler.

8. Later control and selection
==============================
Suppose a paid actuator can multiply primitive a's rate by a known nonnegative
w_a(theta), keeping the same maps and invariant pi. If the admitted designs
preserve an earned positive gap, the rate event still implies the same form
comparison for every theta. Thus (10) holds jointly over all targets and designs.

For an actually optimized finite design menu, if approximate computed values
obey Mtilde(theta)/Mtrue(theta) in [1/G,G] simultaneously, choosing a maximizer
of Mtilde guarantees
   Mtrue(chosen) >= max_theta Mtrue(theta)/G^2.            (25)
This elementary decision consequence requires the menu search, all target
simulations and their failure budgets to be paid. It is not a generic efficient
planning theorem over exponentially many designs. Known actuator multipliers
are not learned by the clock-count experiment unless their calibration is
separately performed. The execution here computes three later targets, not a
large controller optimization benchmark.

9. Boundaries and strongest surviving meaning
=============================================
The result does not identify a hidden mechanism from arbitrary passive data.
It does not solve slow mixing, arbitrary Gibbs counting, arbitrary conditional
sampling, unknown primitives, unknown hidden channels, unmarked null dynamics,
or large-cycle sensitivity. It is a finite-state continuous-time stochastic
model, not a quantum error-correction device or a general biomolecular simulator.

For this qualified class, the complete theorem removes an actual exponential
waiting burden from rare coarse-target computation while retaining finite
calibration precision. It does not remove the rare event's physical waiting
time. It computes a prediction of that time from the learned mechanism.

Ordinary methods remain part of the strongest construction. Potential theory,
Poisson clumping, local simulation, matrix solves, and concentration are credited.
An equally informed implementation can adopt the global uncertainty theorem and
finite screening bracket. No experimental benefit or universal best-method
speed advantage has been established. The package supplies a candidate general
tractability principle with proofs and executable evidence; its broad usefulness
and precise historical novelty need further assessment.

The supplementary LIFETIME_LAW.txt supplies an independently reconstructed
deadline-law theorem beyond the mean. Its executed deadline_certificate.py
and exact rational endpoints are included. The separate UNKNOWN_GIBBS_PROOF.txt
extends the acquisition contract to unknown stationary weights on a binary
path; the gibbs directory contains its executed 80-bit construction. It reuses
the pinned Astra N1213 local-clamp acquisition ingredient and replaces the
supplied stationary-law/target-initialization interface by an explicit learned
rational path model. Neither extension changes the limitations above on
arbitrary hidden mechanisms or physical validation.
