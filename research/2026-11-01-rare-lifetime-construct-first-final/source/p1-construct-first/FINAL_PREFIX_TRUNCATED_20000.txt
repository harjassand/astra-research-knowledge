## 2. The central mechanism: control weights and stationary circulation

Let \(Q\) be a finite continuous-time Markov generator and \(L=-Q\). Write its stationary distribution as
\[
\pi(x)=\frac{w(x)}{\sum_y w(y)},
\]
using positive, unnormalized weights \(w\).

The stationary directed flow along an edge is
\[
w(x)q_{xy}.
\]
These flows balance at every state. Suppose they have a specified decomposition into positive directed cycles, each of length at most \(\ell\). This condition is earned explicitly in the constructed models: heatbath edges give two-cycles, and the driven components give three-cycles.

Let the acquired model have weights \(\widehat w\) and cycle-flow coefficients \(\widehat c_j\). Suppose a common calibration event establishes
\[
e^{-u}w(x)\le \widehat w(x)\le e^u w(x)
\]
for every state, and
\[
e^{-v}c_j\le \widehat c_j\le e^v c_j
\]
for every cycle coefficient.

For a target \(A\), define
\[
M_A=\mathbb E_{\pi(\cdot\mid A^c)}\tau_A.
\]
The fitted mean \(\widehat M_A\) uses the fitted process and **its own** stationary distribution conditioned outside \(A\).

### The stability theorem

With
\[
K=\csc\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\ell}\right),
\]
I obtained
\[
\boxed{
\frac{e^{-3u}}{F(v,K)}
\le
\frac{\widehat M_A}{M_A}
\le
e^{3u}F(v,K),
}
\]
where
\[
F(v,K)=
\exp\!\left[
2\operatorname{asinh}\!\left(K\sinh(v/2)\right)
\right]
\le e^{Kv}.
\]

In particular,
\[
\boxed{
\left|\log\frac{\widehat M_A}{M_A}\right|
\le 3u+Kv.
}
\]

The bound holds **simultaneously for every nonempty proper target**. It contains no target probability, rare waiting horizon, or state-space dimension. It also needs **no relaxation-gap assumption**. A gap is needed later for efficient computation, which is a separate obligation.

This does not make acquisition independent of system size. In the learned interacting model, \(u\) is a sum of local errors. Keeping \(u\) bounded requires increasingly accurate local calibration, and that cost is charged.

### Why the theorem holds

There are two distinct steps.

First, hold the weights fixed and compare the circulating flows. A cycle’s Fourier modes give the form bound
\[
|B_j(f,h)|
\le
K\sqrt{E_j(f)E_j(h)}.
\]
The coefficient error satisfies
\[
|\widehat c_j-c_j|
\le
2\sinh(v/2)\sqrt{c_j\widehat c_j}.
\]

Pairing the true adjoint hitting potential with the fitted forward potential gives an exact capacity sensitivity identity. Combining it with the cycle bound yields
\[
|\widehat C-C|
\le
2K\sinh(v/2)\sqrt{C\widehat C}.
\]
Solving this quadratic inequality gives the factor \(F\).

Second, change the stationary weights while keeping the balanced flow fixed. Set
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(w)L,\qquad B=A^c.
\]
The killed matrix \(D_B\) has an entrywise nonnegative inverse, and
\[
M_A=
\frac{w_B^{\mathsf T}D_B^{-1}w_B}
{\sum_{x\in B}w(x)}.
\]
The two weight factors in the numerator contribute at most \(e^{2u}\); the denominator contributes another \(e^u\). This gives the \(e^{3u}\) comparison and correctly includes the change in the initial stationary law.

The resulting interpretation is useful: **a rare kinetic observable can be stable under acquired uncertainty even when reproducing an entire trajectory over that lifetime would be an unreasonable accuracy requirement.**

The underlying cycle, sector, and nonreversible capacity machinery has established predecessors. The contribution here is the explicit comparison, including changing weights, and its complete acquisition-to-consequence composition. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

### Why the cycle condition is substantive

Relative rate accuracy and stationarity alone are insufficient.

On a uniform \(n\)-cycle, change clockwise and anticlockwise rates from \((1,1)\) to \((1+\eta,1-\eta)\). The stationary distribution stays uniform. Yet the mean time to one site changes from
\[
\frac{n(n+1)}{12}
\]
to approximately
\[
\frac{n}{4\eta}
\]
for fixed positive \(\eta\) and large \(n\).

The ratio grows with \(n\). These primitive cycles have length \(n\), so they do not satisfy a uniform short-cycle bound. This counterexample is preserved in the research record.

## 3. Computing the mean through a short boundary experiment

The stability theorem transfers a computed answer between models. It does not itself compute that answer.

For this step, assume an earned lower bound \(g>0\) on the gap of the **additive symmetric part**:
\[
\operatorname{Re}\langle f,Lf\rangle_\pi
\ge g\|f\|_\pi^2
\quad\text{when }\pi(f)=0.
\]
For a nonnormal generator, a bound on the real parts of eigenvalues is not a substitute.

Let
\[
p=\pi(A),\qquad b=1-p.
\]
Choose a killing rate \(s>0\). Starting from a state \(y\notin A\), let
\[
\kappa_s(y)=
\Pr_y\{\operatorname{Exp}(s)<\tau_A\}.
\]

Define the screened boundary quantity
\[
r_s=
s+
\mathbb E_{x\sim\pi(\cdot\mid A)}
\sum_{y\notin A}q_{xy}\kappa_s(y),
\]
and
\[
R_s=\frac{1}{p r_s}-\frac1s.
\]

### The finite nonreversible screening theorem

I derived
\[
\boxed{
\frac{R_s}{b(1+s/g)}
\le M_A
\le
\frac{(1+s/g)R_s}{b}.
}
\]

This holds for finite nonreversible chains without a sector bound. Choosing \(s\) proportional to the desired relative accuracy times \(g\) gives a short expected excursion horizon \(1/s\), independent of the rare mean.

The two-sided interval is necessary. A directed-cycle counterexample shows that the reversible monotonicity assertion \(R_s/b\le M_A\) can fail.

The proof normalizes the hitting potential and its adjoint. If \(C_0=1/(bM_A)\) and \(C_s=1/R_s\), their weak equations give
\[
C_s-C_0=s\langle h_0^*,h_s\rangle_\pi.
\]
The symmetric-gap energy estimates bound the two potential norms. The resulting scalar quadratic inequality gives precisely the factor \(1+s/g\).

### The actual experiment

The boundary quantity is obtained by the following positive simulation:

1. Draw a state from the fitted stationary law conditioned on \(A\).
2. Select a candidate outgoing channel with the appropriate rate weighting.
3. Apply its transition or acceptance rule. A null proposal or a move remaining inside \(A\) produces zero.
4. After an exit, simulate until the process returns to \(A\), producing zero, or the independent killing clock rings, producing one.

If the success probability is \(\theta\) and the proposal-rate sum is \(R\), then
\[
r_s=s+R\theta.
\]

The calculation therefore estimates an ordinary Bernoulli probability. It **obtains** the recrossing correction; it does not assume that every exit starts a new independent rare-event episode.

With rational fitted rates, exponential waiting-time draws are unnecessary. Uniformization combines the transition proposals and killing into rational categorical choices. Integer rejection implements those choices, including null events. The initial boundary proposal, subsequent proposals, and conditional initialization are all counted.

There is a numerical qualification: \(R_s\) subtracts \(1/s\), so relative accuracy in \(r_s\) can be amplified by cancellation. I derived and checked a sufficient stable regime,
\[
p\le \frac{s}{2(s+R)}.
\]
All large executed targets satisfy it comfortably. The package also gives a polynomial direct-simulation fallback outside that regime and an alternative complementary estimator.

For the stable rare-target regime, a sufficient expected simulation-work bound is
\[
O\!\left(
\frac{R\Lambda}{g^2b\,\varepsilon^3}
\log\frac1\alpha
\right),
\]
apart from the explicitly paid map, membership, initialization, and finite-bit costs. Here \(\Lambda\) is the total proposal rate. There is no \(1/p\) factor.

Exponential killing, soft capacities, and local rare-event simulation are established ideas. The particular finite nonreversible bracket and its acquired-model composition are what this work develops explicitly. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

## 4. Extending the result to deadline probabilities

A mean alone does not determine the probability of failure before a deadline. I therefore developed a separate lifetime-law argument.

Set
\[
\mu=bM_A,\qquad
\delta=\frac{1}{g\mu}.
\]
For \(\delta\le 1/4\), define
\[
D(\delta)=
\min\left\{
1,\,
\frac{4\delta}{\pi}
\log\!\left(1+\frac{1}{4\delta^2}\right)
+\frac{48\delta}{\pi}
\right\}.
\]

The result is
\[
\boxed{
\sup_{t\ge0}
\left|
\Pr_{\pi(\cdot\mid A^c)}(\tau_A\le t)
-\left(1-e^{-t/(bM_A)}\right)
\right|
\le
\min\left\{1,\frac{D(\delta)+p}{b}\right\}.
}
\]

The scale \(bM_A\) is intentional: it is the unconditional stationary mean, including the probability of starting inside \(A\). The conditional mean is \(M_A\).

The new derivation continues the normalized potential to imaginary killing rates. Its key estimate is a complex disk constraint,
\[
|z-1|^2\le(\omega/g)^2\operatorname{Re}z,
\]
for the normalized complex capacity \(z\). This controls the hitting-time characteristic function. A classical smoothing inequality then gives the displayed CDF bound. The smoothing theorem and constants are credited to Flajolet–Sedgewick’s statement of the standard result. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Exponential approximation for hitting times is also established prior science, including non-symmetric processes in Mathieu’s work. For reversible systems, Aldous–Brown theory already gives a sharper error bound than the general nonreversible bound above. I do not claim to have discovered exponential lifetime laws or improved the reversible result. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

Once a mean interval is established, this theorem gives an envelope at **every deadline simultaneously**. In the unknown-stationary-law experiment, I also propagate the acquired uncertainty in \(p\), rather than silently using the fitted mass as the true one.

Two limits matter:

- The guarantee is **additive probability error**. It need not provide useful relative accuracy for an extremely small early-deadline probability.
- Rarity alone is insufficient. A retained directed 128-cycle has \(p=1/128\), but its separation parameter is approximately \(13.074\), outside the theorem’s regime; its measured CDF discrepancy is about \(0.14834\).

## 5. Earning the stationary law and rare-event initialization

The first execution used a known uniform stationary law. I then strengthened the construction to avoid supplying the stationary weights and target initialization as unexplained inputs.

The stronger model is a binary path with unknown rationally fitted factors:
\[
w(x)=
\prod_i a_i^{x_i}
\prod_i b_i^{x_ix_{i+1}},
\qquad
a_i,b_i\in[0.9,1.1].
\]

It has unit-rate heatbath updates and driven cycles on adjacent pairs. If \(v_i\) is the product of factors touching a pair, its directed cycle has rate
\[
q_i(x,P_ix)=\frac{\alpha_i}{v_i(x)},
\qquad \alpha_i\in[0.5,1].
\]
For fixed outside bits, its stationary circulation is exactly
\[
\alpha_i w_{\mathrm{outside}},
\]
constant around the three-cycle. This proves stationarity despite broken detailed balance.

### Local information actually acquired

Under the stated local intervention interface, hold a site and its neighbors while observing identified native candidate transitions. The heatbath odds are
\[
r_i(l,r)=a_i b_{i-1}^{\,l}b_i^{\,r}.
\]

Two neighbor settings identify the factors:

- Neighbors zero give \(a_i\).
- Right neighbor one and left neighbor zero give \(a_ib_i\); dividing the two raw ratios gives \(b_i\).

At pair state \(00\), its touching weight is one, so the directed candidate rate identifies \(\alpha_i\).

The estimates come from finite Poisson counts. I fit one compatible global Gibbs model, clip to the supplied parameter ranges, and quantize the factors rationally. This avoids treating independently fitted, potentially incompatible conditional tables as a joint law.

### The physical assumption that remains

The local clamps must leave the native update kernels and clock laws unchanged.

That condition cannot be deduced from Gibbs equilibrium alone. Two devices can agree on every clamped experiment and share the same Gibbs distribution, yet multiply all free-running rates by different constants. Their free lifetimes then differ by the inverse constants.

The mathematical architecture supplies the needed invariance by construction. An external physical system would need evidence for it.

Efficient target initialization also remains representation-dependent. Even under a known uniform law, a hidden singleton accessible only through membership queries takes exponentially many queries to identify with constant success. The executed cylinder targets avoid that obstruction through the actual transfer calculation.
