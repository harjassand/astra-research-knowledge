MAIN THEOREM

Let H be a real homogeneous quartic on Sym(2), with Hessian operator
bound ||H''(S)||<=C|S|^2 in Frobenius norm. Suppose its ACTUAL Hessian
variational gap has the quantified strong bound

 <H(S+D^2 psi)>-H(S)
 >=c[|S|^2<|D^2 psi|^2>+<|D^2 psi|^4>]                    (1)

for EVERY symmetric mean S and EVERY actual periodic scalar potential,
with c>0. Smooth tests suffice; quartic approximation extends (1) to
W^(2,4) potentials.

Set

 B2=2C^2/c+3C/2,
 B4=108C^4/c^3+9C^2/(4c)+3C/2,
 K=32, Bz=B4 K^4.

For any nu>0, the polynomial

       P(A)=H(sym A)+nu(b-c)^2+mu|A|^6                     (2)

is GLOBALLY quasiconvex whenever

       mu*nu >= max(256 B2^2, 1024 Bz^2).                 (3)

This is a full variational theorem for ALL actual vector-gradient
perturbations and ALL affine means. It is not an outer moment bound,
a finite field theorem, a tangent-only result or an assumption that
rank-one convexity implies (1). Its proof uses an actual Hodge
decomposition and explicitly controlled correction fields.

The enormous coefficient bound is an existence bound, with no asserted

1. ACTUAL HODGE DECOMPOSITION AND A SELF-CONTAINED L4 BOUND

Let G=Dphi be any smooth mean-zero actual periodic vector gradient,
and z=G12-G21. Write J=(0,-1;1,0), and define

            E=-J Hess Delta^(-1) z, X=G-E.                (4)

E is an actual gradient. It has exactly the prescribed curl z. Thus
X has zero curl as well as the gradient row compatibility, and is an
ACTUAL scalar Hessian D^2 psi, up to an irrelevant constant in its
potential. All these fields have mean zero. Put Y=sym E; then

 sym G=X+Y,
 ||Y||_2<=||E||_2=||z||_2,
 ||Y||_4<=||E||_4<=K||z||_4, K=32.                       (5)

The L2 equality follows frequency by frequency from the multiplier
k tensor k/|k|^2 and the orthogonality of J. Here is a quantitative
L4 proof that does not assume the conjectured sharp Beurling norm.

For a real mean-zero trigonometric polynomial f, the directional
Hilbert transform T has the Fourier identity

           2T(f Tf)=(Tf)^2-f^2.

The exceptional directions orthogonal to a nonzero Fourier frequency
have measure zero and may be omitted. L2 boundedness, Holder and the
triangle inequality give

 ||Tf||_4^2<=||f||_4^2+2||f||_4||Tf||_4,
                 ||T||_(4->4)<=1+sqrt(2).

A Riesz transform is the rotational average of these transforms:
the probability-circle integral of theta_j sign(theta.k) is
(2/pi) k_j/|k|. The factor pi/2 and the average |theta_j|=2/pi cancel
in the norm bound. Each Riesz transform has the same L4 upper bound.
Each Hess Delta^(-1) entry is a product of two Riesz transforms.
Summing its four component bounds gives

 ||Hess Delta^(-1)z||_4<=4(1+sqrt(2))^2||z||_4
                                      <25||z||_4<32||z||_4.

These Fourier and norm arguments first apply to trigonometric
polynomials; density gives the L4 statement. No unknown sharp norm
constant or unproved two-dimensional Morrey premise is used.

2. EXTENDING THE CONSTRAINED GAP WITH A QUANTIFIED ERROR

Let M be ANY mean matrix and S=sym M. Taylor's formula, the Hessian
bound and |S+X+tY|^2<=3(|S|^2+|X|^2+|Y|^2) give an error bounded by

 2C |S|^2 |X||Y|+2C |X|^3 |Y|
 +(3C/2)[|S|^2 |Y|^2+|X|^2 |Y|^2+|Y|^4].                 (6)

The constant DH(S):Y integrates to zero. Apply Young's inequality:

 2C |S|^2 |X||Y|
   <=(c/2)|S|^2|X|^2+(2C^2/c)|S|^2|Y|^2,

 2C |X|^3 |Y|<= (c/4)|X|^4+(108C^4/c^3)|Y|^4,

 (3C/2)|X|^2|Y|^2<= (c/4)|X|^4+(9C^2/(4c))|Y|^4.

Absorb the two positive X terms using (1), retaining one half of
its positive bound. Drop that remaining positive part. With (5),

 Delta_H(S,sym G)
 >=-B2 |M|^2<z^2>-Bz<z^4>.                              (7)

This estimate uses the actual constrained QC inequality once, on the
actual Hessian X. It does not apply that inequality to arbitrary
symmetric matrix laws or assume a pointwise compatibility selector.
