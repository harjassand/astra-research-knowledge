THEOREM

Let H be ANY real homogeneous quartic on Sym(2). Use Frobenius norms.
Suppose H is strictly convex along its rank-one directions in the
quantitative sense

 H''(X)[R,R]>=c |X|^2 |R|^2                                (1)

for every symmetric X and every symmetric rank-one R, with c>0.
Choose C such that ||H''(X)||<=C|X|^2. Set

                  D=C+2C^2/c.

For ANY nu>0 and mu>=CD/(6nu), the full 2 by 2 polynomial

 P(A)=H(sym A)+nu(b-c)^2+mu|A|^6                            (2)

is GLOBALLY rank-one convex, at every background and in every rank-one
direction. This is not merely a conic admission or a local statement.
The data include three exact quartics with positive 9 by 9 ROC Grams,
and their explicit globally admitted extensions (2).

If H has a strict actual scalar-Hessian QC failure, the SAME extension
has a strict actual 2 by 2 gradient QC failure at an explicitly small

No such negative field has been found in the present searches.

1. GLOBAL PROOF, INCLUDING THE NORMAL DIRECTIONS

Let P_E be orthogonal projection to E=Sym(2), and L(A)=b-c. For a
rank-one direction R=u tensor xi with |xi|=1, define

 R0=(u.xi) xi tensor xi, W=P_E R-R0.

Then R0 is symmetric rank one (or zero), and

               |W|<=|L(R)|, |P_E R|<=|R|.                 (3)

Indeed the perpendicular component of u supplies W, whose Frobenius
norm is |L(R)|/sqrt(2). This is an exact compatible rank-one
decomposition of a DIRECTION, not a proposed pointwise covariance map.

Put X=P_E A. By (1), the Hessian norm bound and Young's inequality,

 H''(X)[P_E R,P_E R]
 >=c|X|^2|R0|^2-2C|X|^2|R0||W|-C|X|^2|W|^2
 >=(c/2)|X|^2|R0|^2-D|X|^2 L(R)^2.                       (4)

The quadratic term in (2) contributes 2nu L(R)^2. Therefore the
quartic plus quadratic is ROC whenever |A|^2<=nu/D; the sixth-degree
term is convex and cannot spoil that local region.

For |A|^2>=nu/D use instead

 H''(X)[P_E R,P_E R]>=-C|A|^2|R|^2,
 (|A|^6)''[R,R]>=6|A|^4|R|^2.

The choice of mu makes the latter dominate the former throughout
this entire exterior. The normal quadratic remains nonnegative.
The local ball and exterior cover every A. This proves GLOBAL ROC
without sampled angles, bounded-background assumptions or a cutoff.
