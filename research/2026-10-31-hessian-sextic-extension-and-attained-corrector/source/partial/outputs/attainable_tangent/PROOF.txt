UNIVERSAL THEOREM

Let F be ANY real homogeneous quartic on 2 by 2 matrices. Let L(A)=ell:A
be a linear form whose 2 by 2 coefficient matrix ell is invertible. Let
m be another linear form, lambda a real scalar and nu>0. Set

 C=L^2 m+lambda det(A)L,
 p=F+C+nu L^2,
 q=2L m+lambda det(A),
 R=F-q^2/(4nu).                                             (1)

Fix ANY affine mean M and ANY smooth mean-zero actual periodic gradient
G=Dphi with L(G)=0 pointwise. Among ALL smooth mean-zero ACTUAL gradient
correctors H, the fourth-order energy coefficient of

          A_epsilon=epsilon M+epsilon G+epsilon^2 H         (2)

has an EXACT minimum, and that minimum is ATTAINED:

 inf_H lim_(epsilon->0) epsilon^-4
       [<p(A_epsilon)>-p(epsilon M)]
                  =<R(M+G)>-R(M).                         (3)

The infimum is over genuine compatible correctors, not free matrix laws,
outer moment matrices or unattainable covariance kernels. Its minimizer
is given by a single explicit Poisson solve below. Thus a strict negative
constrained test for R yields a concrete finite-epsilon actual violation
for p. If p is independently admitted globally ROC, this is a genuine
ROC/QC separation. No such admitted negative has been found here.

This is a universal LOCAL fourth-order variational principle. It is not
an assertion about the limit of infima over unbounded epsilon-dependent
correctors, an unrestricted Gamma-limit, full QC of p, sharp QC/ROC
threshold equality, or full acoustic-capacity attainment.

1. EXACT TAYLOR COEFFICIENT AND THE ACTUAL NULL IDENTITIES

Write l=L(M), z=L(H). On the affine constraint L(M+G)=l define

 Psi=2l m(G)+lambda[cof(M):G+det G]
                        =q(M+G)-q(M).                      (4)

Mean G=mean H=0, and the determinant null-Lagrangian identities give

 <det G>=0, <cof(G):H>=0, <Psi>=0.                         (5)

The middle identity follows by polarizing <det(G+tH)>=0 for actual
mean-zero periodic gradients. It is not valid for arbitrary free laws.

Expand the full polynomial p in (2), subtract its affine value and its
linear tangent (whose integral is zero). The epsilon^2 coefficient is
zero because L(G)=0. The epsilon^3 coefficient is lambda*l*<det G>=0.
The exact epsilon^4 coefficient is

       Delta_F(M,G)+<z Psi>+nu<z^2>,                       (6)

where Delta_F=<F(M+G)>-F(M). Pointwise an additional term
lambda*l*cof(G):H occurs; its integral vanishes by (5).

2. THE MINIMIZER IS A GENUINE GRADIENT

L(Deta)=div(ell^T eta). Since ell is invertible, EVERY smooth mean-zero
scalar z has the explicit gradient right inverse

 eta_z=ell^(-T) grad Delta^(-1) z,
 H_z=Deta_z=ell^(-T) Hess Delta^(-1) z,
                         L(H_z)=z.                        (7)

This is an actual smooth periodic vector potential. On a nonzero
Fourier frequency k its gradient coefficient is

                 H_z(k)=z(k) ell^(-T)k tensor k/|k|^2.     (8)

Complete the scalar square in (6). The unique scalar minimizer is

                       z*=-Psi/(2nu),                     (9)

which is smooth and has mean zero by (5). Equation (7) supplies an
attaining compatible H*. Any additional L-free gradient corrector
has the same coefficient. The value is

                    Delta_F-<Psi^2>/(4nu).                (10)

Because q has the null-mean property <q(M+G)>=q(M) on this constraint,

 <Psi^2>=<q(M+G)^2>-q(M)^2,

so (10) is exactly the right side of (3). This proves the theorem.
For a rough L4 constrained field, Psi is L2; the right-inverse minimizer
need not be L4. Smooth approximation gives the same infimum and strict
negative transfer, but literal smooth/L4 attainment is not asserted
for every rough field. The stated smooth theorem is fully attained.

4. WHAT GLOBAL ROC IMPLIES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

If p in (1) is globally ROC, then R is rank-one convex along every
rank-one direction R0 with L(R0)=0. Indeed, choose a rank-one curve
R_t through R0 with prescribed derivative of L(R_t). Such a curve
exists because ell is invertible, so L and det meet transversely at
every nonzero rank-one R0 on the constraint. Expanding p'' at small
backgrounds and minimizing the free transverse scalar gives

 F''(R0)-P(R0)P(R0)^T/(2nu)>=0,
 P(R0)=2m(R0)ell+lambda cof(R0).                           (11)

For L(R0)=det R0=0, Dq(A):R0=A:P(R0), while q''[R0,R0]=0.
Quartic polarization therefore identifies (11) with
R''(A)[R0,R0]>=0 at every A.

The difference between these two implications is real: conic ROC
does not supply the actual constrained variational inequality by
definition. No outer QC moment bound is being substituted for it.
