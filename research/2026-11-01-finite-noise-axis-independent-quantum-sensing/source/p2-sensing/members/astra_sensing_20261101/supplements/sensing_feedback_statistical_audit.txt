Statistical audit of syndrome-dependent native-noise feedback
1 November 2026; independent complementary construction

Scope. The parent construction now uses a finite physical meter with ongoing
sensor noise, coherent removal of logical information from the meter, and a
feedback choice between two scalar native noise rates. This note audits the
information claim and preserves an exactly solved simpler instantaneous weak-
parity instrument as a reference. It does not claim a device demonstration or
historical novelty.

1. Pathwise statement and the correct achievable information

Take H=omega Z_P/2 and jumps A_theta=cos(theta)X_P+sin(theta)Y_P, with fixed but
unknown theta. Encode |0_L>=|00>, |1_L>=|11>. No physical correction flips are
applied. Let S(t)=+1 in the even sector and -1 in the odd sector, with S(0)=+1,
and A_T=integral_0^T S(t)dt. All logical-safe measurement/control amplitudes
must be scalar in the logical bit conditional on a syndrome path.

For every path ending in the even sector, the product of unknown A_theta
jumps has no remaining theta dependence. Its logical unitary is
exp(-i omega A_T Z_L/2), up to a known convention on the sign of A_T.
This follows from A_theta^2=I and A_theta exp(-i x Z/2) A_theta=exp(+i x Z/2).
The assertion holds for an arbitrary logical state and reference system.

Unobserved paths are still mixed. Define
  p_e(T)=P(S(T)=+1),
  M(T)=E[A_T 1{S(T)=+1}].
If an ideal final measurement resolves even Y_L and odd erasure, its actually
achieved Fisher information at omega=0 is
  I_omega(0)=M(T)^2/p_e(T).
It is NOT E[A_T^2 1_even], since the unknown residence time is not revealed.

The parent now avoids perfect final parity altogether. The observable
  O=(X_P Y_A + Y_P X_A)/2
equals Y_L in the even sector and zero in the odd sector. Choose either product
Pauli measurement with probability 1/2, and keep only its product outcome z=+/-1.
Then P(z)= [1+z f_T(omega)]/2, where
  f_T(omega)=E[1_even sin(omega A_T)]
up to an overall known sign. This entire binary distribution is theta-independent.
At omega=0, f_T=0 and f_T'=M(T), so the achieved classical FI is exactly M(T)^2.
When p_e -> pi_e and M(T)/T -> pi_e v>0, this yields
  I_omega(0)/T^2 -> pi_e^2 v^2.
No quantum Fisher information attainability assumption is needed.

2. An actual weak-signal discrimination guarantee

The local FI is not alone an arbitrary-frequency estimator. A finite operational
consequence nevertheless follows without any central-limit approximation.
Since |A_T|<=T and |sin x-x|<=|x|^3/6,
  |f_T(omega)-omega M(T)| <= |omega|^3 T^3/6.
Suppose the explicit finite-time transfer calculation certifies M(T)/T>=c0>0.
For omega=h/T, h>0, and h^2<=3c0,
  f_T(h/T)>=h c0/2=:d.
Under omega=0 the binary product measurement has mean zero. Under omega=h/T
it has mean at least d. For N independent runs, classify by whether the sample
mean is above d/2. Hoeffding's inequality gives each error probability at most
exp(-N d^2/8). Thus
  N >= 32 log(2/alpha)/(h^2 c0^2)
suffices for total binary-decision error at most alpha. N is independent of T.
This establishes operational resolution of a weak signal proportional to 1/T,
uniformly in the unknown azimuth theta, at total exposure NT=O(T) for fixed
alpha,h,c0. It does not establish global estimation on a fixed frequency interval.

3. Why global frequency claims require another construction

At a fixed nonzero omega, unobserved A_T fluctuations typically dephase the
mixture on a time scale proportional to 1/omega^2. The quadratic local FI at
the null does not show uniform Heisenberg precision over a fixed interval.
A plausible extension is multiscale adaptive physical Z detuning to keep the
residual omega-omega_hat in O(1/T). This needs an actual controllable reference
Hamiltonian, and its effect on the bath rates/orientation must be checked.
Changing only the final readout phase does not remove dephasing caused by the
unknown residence time. The parent result should state this global limitation.

3A. Constructive global extension with a physical counterdrive

The missing construction CAN be supplied if a known physical control Hamiltonian
-c Z_P/2 is available throughout each experiment. It must shift the signal from
omega to delta=omega-c while preserving the admitted bath/meter model, or keep
its parameters inside a certified box. This is a substantive extra operation,
not merely a change in the final measurement phase.

Assume a coarse certificate M(T)/T>=c0>0 for all T>=T0 at allowed integral
cycle durations. Choose b>0 with b^2<=c0. For |delta|T<=b,
  f_T'(delta)=E[1_even A_T cos(delta A_T)]
      >=M(T)-delta^2 T^3/2 >=c0 T/2.
The inequality uses |1-cos x|<=x^2/2 and |A_T|<=T; it does not assume A_T>=0.
Also f_T(0)=0. Full response calibration is unnecessary.

Here is an explicit interval-refinement algorithm. Suppose the current certified
frequency bracket has center c and radius R=b/T. Apply the counterdrive -cZ/2
and perform N independent T-duration binary product-measurement experiments.
Write zbar for their sample mean and epsilon=c0 b/8. On the confidence event
|zbar-f_T(delta)|<=epsilon, update as follows:
  zbar>=epsilon: retain [c,c+R].
  zbar<=-epsilon: retain [c-R,c].
  otherwise: retain [c-R/2,c+R/2].
The first two updates follow from monotonicity and f_T(0)=0. In the middle case,
|f_T(delta)|<=2epsilon implies
|delta|<=4epsilon/(c0 T)=R/2, so this update is also valid.
All three updates halve the bracket radius. Double T for the next stage. The
new bracket again has radius b/T and remains inside the scaled monotonic region.

Hoeffding's inequality gives a per-stage failure probability at most alpha_k if
  N_k >= [128/(c0^2 b^2)] log(2/alpha_k).
Let T_k=2^k T0 for k=0,...,K and allocate
  alpha_k=(alpha/2)2^{-(K-k+1)}.
Their sum is less than alpha/2. Allow initial coarse-bracket failure alpha/2.
At the final stage, with probability at least 1-alpha, the output midpoint has
absolute error at most b/(2T_K).

The total sensor/control exposure is
  sum_k N_k T_k
  <= [128/(c0^2 b^2)]T_K[2 log(4/alpha)+4 log2]+2T_K,
using ceiling bounds and the geometric series. Preparation and final readout
overhead t_over per shot adds O(t_over[(K+1)log(1/alpha)+(K+1)^2]); it is lower
order in T_K at fixed hardware constants. Thus this is an actual high-confidence
1/total-time frequency resolution result in the admitted model, not only a QFI
claim. The constants can be large and have not been optimized.

3B. Coarse gain certificate for the parent's finite physical meter

For the parent physical-meter formulas, suppose fixed admitted parameter bounds
are available:
  gamma_min<=gamma_l<gamma_h<=gamma_max,
  gamma_h-gamma_l>=g_gap>0,
  lambda tau in [a,pi/2], gamma_j tau<=G,
  eta in [eta_min,eta_max] with eta_min>0 and eta_max<1,
  d in [d_min,d_max] with d_min>0,
  Delta in [Delta_min,Delta_max], Delta_min>0.
These bounds must hold for all counterdrives used by the global algorithm.

The parent detector correlation is
  s_j=lambda exp(-gamma_j tau)
      sinh(tau sqrt(gamma_j^2-lambda^2))/sqrt(gamma_j^2-lambda^2),
with the analytic sine continuation when the square root is imaginary.
For lambda tau<=pi/2, sinh(x)/x>=1 in the real case and sin(x)/x>=2/pi in
the imaginary case. Hence s_j>=s_min=(2a/pi)exp(-G)>0. Also
  b_j=exp(-2gamma_j d)>=b_min=exp(-2gamma_max d_max).
Each s_j and b_j belongs to [0,1]. The denominator of the parent's D formula
therefore lies in [1,3], implying
  D>=D_min=(2/3)eta_min s_min b_min.
With q_min=(1-exp(-2gamma_min Delta_min))/2,
  v>=v_min=D_min q_min^2 g_gap/(Delta_max gamma_max^2)>0,
and pi_even>=1/2. Thus the asymptotic gain c_infty=pi_even v is at least v_min/2.

The controller has four classical states: actual parity and selected scalar
rate. The imperfect classical meter label has each probability at least
epsilon_min=(1-eta_max)/2, conditional on any active-interval path. During the
dead interval, either endpoint parity has probability at least
q_dead=(1-exp(-2gamma_min d_min))/2. Thus every transition-matrix entry is at
least epsilon_min q_dead. Its Doeblin contraction coefficient is at most 1-rho,
  rho=4 epsilon_min q_dead>0.

Let P be this row transition matrix, R its signed-exposure reward kernel, and
e the endpoint-even indicator. For any initial distribution p0,
  M_m=p0 sum_{k=0}^{m-1}P^k R P^{m-1-k}e,
and ||R||_infinity<=Delta. Doeblin contraction bounds each summand's difference
from (pi R 1)(pi e) by 2Delta(1-rho)^k+Delta(1-rho)^{m-1-k}. Summing gives
  |M(T)/T-c_infty|<=3Delta/(rho T).
Consequently the coarse choices
  c0=v_min/4,
  T0>=12Delta_max/(rho v_min)
suffice uniformly over the admitted box. These are conservative analytic
constants. They prove that no frequency-accuracy-level response calibration is
required; sufficiently informative fixed coarse bounds are enough.

3C. One explicit initial coarse-frequency protocol

A bounded initial prior |omega|<=Omega is needed. A target-independent coarse
bracket can be acquired at fixed gamma using ordinary short-time process
tomography. In the sensor Bloch x-y plane, the generator is
  B=[[gamma(cos2theta-1), gamma sin2theta-omega],
     [gamma sin2theta+omega, -gamma(cos2theta+1)]].
Its antisymmetric component satisfies omega=(B_21-B_12)/2, independently of
theta. Prepare +x and +y in separate runs and measure x and y after a small t_c.
This estimates M=exp(t_c B). Choose t_c<=1/[4(Omega+2gamma_max)], so
||M-I||<=exp(1/4)-1<0.285. If ||Mhat-M||<=epsilon_M<=1/8, then both lie in
||.-I||<1/2 and the matrix logarithm series yields
  ||log Mhat-log M||<=2epsilon_M.
Therefore the inferred omega has error at most 2epsilon_M/t_c.
Choose epsilon_M<=b t_c/(2T0) to obtain an initial radius b/T0. Four scalar
bounded-observable means estimate the entries, with Hoeffding sample count
O(epsilon_M^-2 log(1/alpha)) for a certified operator-norm error. This cost
depends on the fixed hardware/gain constants and the original frequency range,
not the final target accuracy. It uses ordinary accurate state preparation and
Pauli measurement, assumptions also needed for the protected protocol.

4. Exact weak-instrument reference model

At the beginning of each interval Delta, measure parity weakly with outcome
y=+/-1, using
  K_y=sqrt((1+y eta)/2)P_e+sqrt((1-y eta)/2)P_o,
  eta=1-2epsilon>0.
Choose noise rate gamma_l after y=+, gamma_h after y=-. Define
  q_j=(1-exp(-2 gamma_j Delta))/2, u_j=q_j/gamma_j,
  a=(1-epsilon)q_l+epsilon q_h,
  b=(1-epsilon)q_h+epsilon q_l.
The grid-time syndrome chain has row transition matrix
  P=[[1-a,a],[b,1-b]],
stationary pi_e=b/(q_l+q_h), and lambda=1-q_l-q_h in (0,1).

The expected signed time in one interval conditional on the initial sector is
  r_e=(1-epsilon)u_l+epsilon u_h,
  r_o=-[epsilon u_l+(1-epsilon)u_h].
The stationary mean signed exposure per unit time is exactly
  v=(1-2epsilon)q_l q_h(1/gamma_l-1/gamma_h)
       /[Delta(q_l+q_h)].
It is positive whenever gamma_h>gamma_l>0 and eta>0.

A stronger identity supplies exact finite-time first moments. For a constant-
rate symmetric telegraph interval, the joint reward kernel
E[A_interval 1{S_end=s'} | S_start=s] is diag(u_j,-u_j); its offdiagonal entries
vanish by time-reversal symmetry within the interval. Consequently, putting
R=diag(r_e,r_o), for T=mDelta,
  p_e(T)=e_e^T P^m e_e,
  M(T)=e_e^T sum_{k=0}^{m-1} P^k R P^{m-1-k} e_e.
In particular M(T)=pi_e v T+O(1). A closed form is
  M=m pi_e (pi_e r_e+pi_o r_o)
    +2 pi_e pi_o(r_e-r_o)(1-lambda^m)/(1-lambda)
    +m pi_o(pi_o r_e+pi_e r_o)lambda^{m-1}.
For equal rates, v=0 and M(T)=(1-exp(-2gamma T))/(2gamma); the supposed
T^2 advantage disappears, as it must.

This weak-instrument reference does not itself earn finite readout duration.
The parent's finite-meter extension is necessary to charge jumps during meter
interaction, coherent erasure, readout, and reset.

5. Alternative imperfect final parity statistic (not needed by final parent)

A constant-strength final parity detector with contrast eta_f>0 and a measurement
z of Y_P X_A also suffices. If r is the noisy parity report, define
  W=z(1+r/eta_f)/2.
Then E W=f_T(omega) exactly, since E[(1+r/eta_f)/2|e]=1 and its odd-sector
conditional mean is zero. At omega=0,
  Var W=p_e(T)+(eta_f^{-2}-1)/4,
independent of theta. The local method-of-moments information is therefore
  M(T)^2/[p_e(T)+(eta_f^{-2}-1)/4].
This is a statistical readout construction; it is not a physical postselection
or purification of an unknown quantum output.

6. New primary comparison sources

Anton Halaski and Christiane P. Koch, 'Obstacles to continuous quantum error
correction via parity measurements', Phys. Rev. Applied 26,034074,
30 September 2026, https://doi.org/10.1103/8bkm-b48b .
Their obstruction concerns continuous circuit-QED parity measurements that
replace a three-body coupling with sums of two-body couplings, causing backaction
in logical or error sectors. The parent retained-meter/coherent-CZ-erasure
construction changes the operation set and is not a refutation of that scope.
Close predecessors cited there include feedback-assisted parity measurement
(Tornberg and Johansson 2010) and undoing measurement-induced dephasing
(Kockum, Tornberg and Johansson 2012). Historical novelty is unestablished.

Hyukgun Kwon, Uwe R. Fischer, Seung-Woo Lee and Liang Jiang,
'Restoring Heisenberg scaling in time via autonomous quantum error correction',
npj Quantum Information 12,103 (2026), arXiv:2504.13168v3,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13168 .
Their stated sufficient condition assumes noise jump operators commute with the
signal Hamiltonian, plus a constrained linear equation; a finite engineered/native
dissipation ratio yields an error O(kappa T/R^c). Our jumps anticommute with Z
and we construct a local weak-signal task rather than claim their theorem fails.
