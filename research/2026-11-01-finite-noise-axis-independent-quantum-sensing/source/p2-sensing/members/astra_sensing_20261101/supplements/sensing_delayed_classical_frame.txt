# Optional controller variant: fast noisy feedback and delayed accurate phase records

Independent audit of the root's variant, 1 November 2026.

## Claim and scope

The finite two-body meter construction does not require the coherent CZ erasure gate if the true computational-basis meter outcome can instead be recovered reliably as classical information. Use the first imperfect report immediately for scalar-rate feedback, but postpone the commuting logical Z frame until the final classical sensing outcome is processed. Repeated QND reports can reduce the frame error with logarithmic detector work and logarithmic concurrent hardware, under an explicitly stated stable-pointer model.

The operation being removed is the coherent ancilla-meter CZ. The essential replacement resource is a stable, repeatedly readable classical pointer or an independently justified classical protection scheme. No absolute hardware superiority is claimed.

## Exact delayed-frame identity

For meter input |0>, each actual sensor jump history gives meter amplitudes cos(Phi) and -i z sin(Phi). Conditional on true Z-meter outcome m in {0,1}, its logical action has the form

    scalar(history,m) times Z_a^m.

Z_a commutes with the signal Hamiltonian, every sensor jump J_theta, all later diagonal sensor/ancilla-meter couplings, and scalar noise-rate switching. Therefore after M cycles the entire frame is

    F = m_1 xor ... xor m_M,
    logical frame = Z_a^F.

The final logical-Y-type observable A=(X_sY_a+Y_sX_a)/2 anticommutes with Z_a. One can perform the final local product measurement immediately at the end of sensing, store its raw classical outcome W, and later report (-1)^F W. The sensing qubits need not retain coherence while the detector pipeline drains.

In particular, a wrong FAST report does not create a wrong quantum correction gate. It merely changes the next scalar-rate setting. The final frame uses the separately decoded slow record.

## No fictitious instantaneous projective readout is required

After the active interaction the meter is decoupled from the data. Suppose all subsequent meter observations are QND in Z and the meter is eventually traced out. Then the observable data-plus-classical instrument is unchanged by inserting an unrecorded Z dephasing of that decoupled meter. It is therefore valid to represent its true computational pointer value m as a latent classical variable, even if an individual physical QND readout is weak and does not fully collapse the meter in one step.

The classical observation model must still be physically earned. The explicit model used below gives conditionally independent binary reports of this latent m with error epsilon<1/2. Arbitrary correlated detector errors or pointer flips are not included in that model.

## Majority decoding and uniform channel error

For each meter collect an odd number L of independent reports

    R_k = m xor E_k,  E_k iid Bernoulli(epsilon).

Use R_1 immediately for feedback; include it in the final majority if desired. Set eta=1-2epsilon. Hoeffding's inequality gives

    Pr(majority != m | m) <= exp(-L eta^2/2).

To decode B pointer bits with total error probability at most alpha, choose odd L satisfying

    L >= 2 log(B/alpha)/eta^2.

Here B=M for known initial meter state 0, or B=2M if both initial and final meter bits are decoded. The first report's correlation with the eventual majority does not invalidate the bound. Conditioning on the state before that meter's observations fixes its latent m; a union bound works across adaptively generated future sensor histories.

On the event that every pointer bit is decoded correctly, the delayed-frame output is exactly the corresponding correctly framed protocol with the same fast-feedback record. With uniformly bounded failure probability alpha, the resulting full instrument differs from that ideal by diamond norm at most 2 alpha, i.e. half-diamond error at most alpha. This holds with arbitrary logical-reference entanglement because each history's quantum action differs only by the commuting known-or-misdecoded Pauli frame.

For the specific final sensing observable, let Y be the full signed signal exposure and E denote final even parity. The reported mean is

    g_actual(omega) = E[(-1)^(Fhat xor F) 1_E sin(omega Y)]

up to a fixed sign convention. The exact theta-independence survives even an erroneous frame: the odd-sector contribution to A still vanishes. Since |Y|<=T,

    |g_actual(omega)-g_ideal(omega)| <= 2 alpha,
    |g'_actual(0)-g'_ideal(0)| <= 2 alpha T.

Thus if the ideal asymptotic slope is pi_e v T, any fixed alpha<pi_e v/2 retains a positive quadratic local information coefficient, bounded asymptotically below by (pi_e v-2 alpha)^2. This stronger statement uses the concrete trajectory model, rather than inferring Fisher information continuity from diamond distance alone.

## Throughput, latency, and actual physical work

Let each QND report occupy detector time t_r and let active sensing cycles have fixed duration Delta. The first report's actual latency is included in Delta and uses the OLD rate, exactly as in the finite-controller kernel. Later reports operate on decoupled meters in parallel with new sensing cycles.

With B=M pointer bits, detector work is M L reports. A pipeline requires at least on the order of ceil(L t_r/Delta) concurrent report lanes/stored meters, plus the active meter and fixed buffers. If t_r is comparable to Delta, this is O(L)=O(log(M/alpha)/eta^2) hardware. Startup and final reporting latency are O(L t_r). The useful sensing duration remains M Delta; full wall-clock delivery includes the additive pipeline latency.

If initial meter values also require L observations, detector work is approximately 2 M L and the pipeline capacity is adjusted accordingly. Initial-readout latency can be paid before preparing the sensing state. Final pipeline drain can occur after the sensing qubits are measured.

One serial detector lane with no additional storage or parallelism cannot retain fixed Delta while doing L reads per cycle. Its growing latency must be charged, and the earlier fixed-Delta coefficient cannot be reused unchanged.

## Initially mixed computational-basis meters

If the meter starts in a classical mixture of |b> with b in {0,1}, the conditional logical frame is

    Z_a^(m xor b).

The feedback should use the transition bit m xor b, because its probability is the same rotation information used by the original meter kernel. The initial b can be majority decoded before activation; its error is included in the B=2M union bound. No assumption on the mixture weight is needed.

If only one noisy initial report is used for fast feedback, independent initial and final BSC errors combine to epsilon_eff=2 epsilon(1-epsilon), with contrast eta_eff=(1-2epsilon)^2; subsequent accurate records can still restore the final frame. A general coherent unknown initial meter state is not covered by this classical-mixture argument. It requires actual preparation/dephasing or a separate analysis.

## The crucial remaining physical condition

The repeated reports must concern a stable pointer value. A meter population flip during the O(L t_r) repeated-readout interval invalidates the iid-copy model. Simply using longer majority decoding does not suppress that error; it may make it worse.

One must therefore either:

* justify the stable-pointer approximation over the stated finite pipeline retention time and add its failure probability to alpha;
* supply a proved classical protection/latching mechanism with an explicit failure bound and cost; or
* replace the decoder and theorem by a physical hidden-Markov pointer model and show that its initial-bit error can meet the required target.

The last route does not automatically offer arbitrarily small error at fixed hardware rates: information lost before the first reliable observation can create a genuine floor.

Active meter X/Y faults during the sensor-meter interaction can already produce unobserved logical Z errors. They are not corrected by accurate later classical records unless the fault history is separately revealed. Reference-ancilla noise, native-noise axis changes under rate modulation, and final data readout imperfections also retain their existing budgets.

Under its explicit classical-pointer model, this variant removes an exact coherent CZ requirement and exchanges it for quantified classical redundancy. Whether that is a favorable physical trade depends on the platform.
