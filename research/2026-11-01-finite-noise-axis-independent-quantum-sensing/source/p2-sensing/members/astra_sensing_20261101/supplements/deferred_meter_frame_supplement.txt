Deferred classical readout of the meter's logical Pauli frame
1 November 2026

Claim audited. In the finite-meter construction, the coherent CZ from logical
ancilla A to meter M may be omitted, if the true final meter Z outcome can be
decoded later with a sufficiently small total classical error. The first noisy
readout can still drive the native-rate feedback immediately. Accurate live
logical-frame information is unnecessary.

1. Exact frame identity

For a fixed sensor-jump path and logical Z_A label z=+/-1, the active meter
state before erasure is
  cos(Phi)|0>_M - i z sin(Phi)|1>_M,
where Phi is path dependent but independent of z. A projective QND Z_M outcome
m=0 or 1 therefore induces, on the logical state,
  scalar(path,m) Z_A^m.
The scalar may carry a path-dependent global phase but no logical-label
dependence. This is precisely the frame removed by the earlier coherent CZ.

Z_A commutes with the signal Hamiltonian omega Z_S/2, the native sensor jumps
cos(theta)X_S+sin(theta)Y_S, and all later meter interactions of the form
g_S Z_S X_M + g_A Z_A X_M. It also commutes with later meter measurements.
Thus after M meter outcomes the only change from the coherently erased
construction is Z_A^B, B=sum_i m_i modulo 2. All meter outcome probabilities,
including joint correlations with syndrome paths and the first noisy live
report, remain identical. The parent's finite controller transfer matrix and
its drift formula are unchanged.

The final sensing observable
  O=(X_S Y_A+Y_S X_A)/2
anticommutes with Z_A. Its sampled product outcome z_final must therefore be
multiplied by (-1)^B. No physical recovery pulse is needed.

2. Delayed readout and its resource cost

Assume each measured meter remains in its true classical Z eigenstate while
it is read repeatedly. Given the bit, the L reports have independent error
probabilities bounded by (1-eta_r)/2, with eta_r>0. A majority decoder obeys
  P(m_hat!=m) <= exp(-L eta_r^2/2).
For at most M meter uses and a total frame-error budget alpha_frame, it suffices
to take an odd L with
  L >= (2/eta_r^2)log(M/alpha_frame).
The union bound gives P(any decoded bit wrong)<=alpha_frame. This remains valid
under adaptive sensor control because the conditional per-bit bound is uniform
over the history. Requiring every bit correct is sufficient, though not optimal
for decoding only their parity.

The first report can be used for immediate native-rate feedback and included
among the L later majority reports. Correlation between that report and the
subsequent sensor history does not invalidate the conditional decoding bound.

If each read takes t_read and sensor cycles take Delta, a pipeline needs
O(1+ceil(L t_read/Delta)) retained meters/readout lanes to sustain a fixed cycle
rate. Total read work is O(M L). A nonadaptive run has a final latency O(L t_read)
in addition to its sensor-cycle time. The adaptive frequency-refinement protocol
has K stage boundaries at which delayed frames are needed before choosing the
next counterdrive. It therefore incurs O(K L t_read) flushing latency. With
K=O(log M), this is O(log^2 M) at fixed hardware/readout constants, in addition
to the leading sensor time and ordinary preparation overhead.

3. Correct statistical accounting

Do NOT apply Hoeffding after conditioning on all frame decoders having succeeded;
such conditioning can alter the outcome distribution. Instead couple the
observed protocol to the same physical protocol whose final signs use the true
meter bits. Until the first decoding failure, the corrected outcomes and all
adaptive choices coincide. The ideal true-frame protocol has the proved binary
distribution and its stated concentration bounds. Consequently
  P(final estimation failure)
     <=alpha_stat+alpha_frame+alpha_initial,
with the chosen budgets. No independence between the frame-failure event and
the statistical-failure event is required.

4. Conditions that cannot be omitted

The repeated-read model requires a stable classical meter bit and independent
or otherwise concentration-controlled readout noise. A quantum meter that can
undergo unknown X/Y flips while its bit is retained does not automatically meet
this assumption. At a fixed uncorrected mutation rate, repeating reads may have
an error floor rather than exponentially improving confidence. Such mutations
must be bounded in the total failure budget or prevented by an explicitly
protected classical storage/readout mechanism.

The old meters must be decoupled from sensor and ancilla during delayed reads,
so that their readout, reset and storage cannot apply untracked logical errors.
Native sensor noise continues throughout the charged cycle and waiting times.
Active-meter faults, cross-talk, initial meter preparation errors, generic
ancilla errors, and inaccurate counterdrives are not solved by delayed Pauli
frames. The result trades an exact entangling eraser for classical redundancy,
hardware/latency overhead, and a specified stable-bit service.

The Pauli-frame idea and repeated classical decoding are conventional tools.
The substantive role here is their compatibility with the pathwise unknown-
orientation cancellation and the finite noisy live controller.
