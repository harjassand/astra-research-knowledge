## The opening I chose

**Can a quantum sensor retain the benefit of long coherent measurements without needing an increasingly precise calibration of the noise it is correcting?**

I developed a concrete answer for a restricted but useful physical model: **let errors return the sensor to its original encoding, and control how long it spends between those returns.** The unknown error rotation cancels along completed return paths. A finite, imperfect observer biases the residence times so that a useful signal survives.

The strongest result now includes an explicit physical interaction model, an exact treatment of continuing sensor noise, a complete frequency-estimation procedure, and a refinement that removes repeated quantum correction gates by delaying accurate classical bookkeeping.

**Its status is a conditional architecture and theorem, with independent mathematical and executable checks. Historical novelty, laboratory feasibility, and practical superiority remain open.**

## 1. Why this was the opening worth pursuing

I first examined three consequential capabilities: trustworthy quantum simulation beyond classical computation, quantum transfer through noisy intermediaries, and sensing under uncertain noise.

The strongest existing work eliminated several tempting formulations. Recent bounded-error quantum simulation already combines Hamiltonian and Lindbladian learning with quantified prediction errors. Adaptive and interference-based transduction already correct substantial classes of imperfect coherent transfer. Simply proposing those capabilities again would not identify a new opening. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

Quantum sensing exposed a sharper failure.

The established theory of error-corrected sensing is powerful: under the Hamiltonian-not-in-Lindblad-span condition, suitable error correction can preserve the signal while suppressing noise. Its standard construction grants protected ancillas and sufficiently fast, accurate control. Recent subsystem work substantially reduces the ancilla and syndrome burden for specified noise models. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

But **a recovery operation can preserve coherence while creating an unknown signal bias**. That is a more serious problem than merely shortening the sensor’s lifetime. It can make a well-protected sensor confidently report the wrong frequency. Existing research on bias in error-corrected sensing makes this an essential part of the comparison. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

The investigation therefore concentrated on removing a particular environmental calibration requirement, while charging the actual observation and control needed to do so.

## 2. The first result: a precise obstruction to “just learn the noise”

Consider a sensing qubit with

\[
H_\omega=\frac{\omega Z}{2},
\]

where \(\omega\) is the frequency or field component we want to measure.

Its noise consists of random flips around an unknown transverse axis:

\[
L_\theta=\sqrt{\gamma}\,J_\theta,
\qquad
J_\theta=\cos\theta\,X+\sin\theta\,Y.
\]

The angle \(\theta\) is fixed during an experiment but unknown.

For every known value of \(\theta\), conventional error-corrected sensing can protect this model. The problem appears when the correction uses a guessed axis.

### Small decoherence can conceal a large measurement bias

If the recovery-axis error is \(\delta\), averaging the random error count gives a coherence factor

\[
\exp\!\left[-i\omega T+\gamma T\left(e^{2i\delta}-1\right)\right].
\]

Its two leading effects are different:

\[
\text{dephasing rate}\approx 2\gamma\delta^2,
\qquad
\text{false frequency}\approx 2\gamma\delta.
\]

Thus a quadratic estimate of residual decoherence does not establish accurate sensing. The bias is first order in the calibration error.

Keeping the error record helps, but does not by itself remove the difficulty. With standard parity correction, a record containing \(N\) flips produces the phase

\[
\phi=\omega T-2\theta N.
\]

Since \(N\) is approximately \(\gamma T\), the effects of \(\omega\) and \(\theta\) become nearly indistinguishable over long runs.

### A bound covering arbitrary adaptive control

We derived an exact finite-collision version of this obstruction and its continuous Markov limit. At one fixed noise rate,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\omega)\ge \frac{\gamma}{T}}
\]

for locally unbiased estimation in the unknown-\(\theta\) model, even allowing protected ancillas, arbitrary adaptive processing, and perfect event flags.

The proof follows a parameter path along which a change in frequency is accompanied by a change in noise direction. Along that path, the coherent contributions to information accumulation cancel. The remaining information grows only linearly with time. The general channel-bound and multiparameter machinery has strong established predecessors; the archive distinguishes that machinery from the particular construction and application developed here. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Crucially, the conventional strategy is already strong: ordinary parity correction with the complete record asymptotically attains this bound’s coefficient. The obstacle survives an informed comparator.

**This tells us what must change: the physical experiment needs another way to distinguish signal accumulation from noise-induced phase accumulation.**

## 3. The central mechanism: let the unknown errors cancel themselves

The useful identities are

\[
J_\theta^2=I,
\qquad
J_\theta e^{-ixZ/2}J_\theta=e^{+ixZ/2}.
\]

Two occurrences of the same unknown flip cancel. Between them, the sign of signal accumulation reverses.

Encode one logical qubit into a sensor \(S\) and a protected reference qubit \(A\):

\[
|0_L\rangle=|00\rangle,
\qquad
|1_L\rangle=|11\rangle.
\]

A sensor flip takes the state from even parity to odd parity. Another returns it.

Let

\[
r(t)=
\begin{cases}
+1,&\text{even parity},\\
-1,&\text{odd parity},
\end{cases}
\qquad
A_T=\int_0^T r(t)\,dt.
\]

For **every trajectory that returns to even parity**, the final logical action is

\[
\boxed{
U_{\mathrm{return}}
=
\exp\!\left(-\frac{i\omega A_T Z_L}{2}\right),
}
\]

with no dependence on the unknown \(\theta\).

This is an operator identity. It holds for an unknown input entangled with an untouched external reference. When the trajectory is unobserved, the output is the corresponding mixture of signal rotations; we do not replace that mixture by a known unitary.

### Why returning is insufficient at a fixed rate

At equal forward and backward rates, the sensor spends approximately equal times in the two parity sectors. The useful exposure \(A_T\) grows through fluctuations rather than through a sustained drift.

We proved a stronger statement for perfectly observed errors: for any integrable stopping rule that stops at an even event count, at one fixed rate,

\[
\mathbb E[A_\tau^2]=\frac{\mathbb E[\tau]}{\gamma}.
\]

Clever stopping alone cannot recover quadratic time scaling.

### The additional physical operation

Allow two strictly positive scalar rates,

\[
0<\gamma_\ell<\gamma_h,
\]

while preserving the **same unknown flip operator** \(J_\theta\).

Use the lower rate when the system appears to be in even parity and the higher rate when it appears to be in odd parity. This encourages long useful intervals and shorter reversed intervals.

With ideal event observation, the resulting mean signed exposure per unit time is

\[
v_{\mathrm{ideal}}
=
\frac{\gamma_h-\gamma_\ell}
{\gamma_h+\gamma_\ell}.
\]

The noise remains present. The control changes its timing.

This is a real physical resource requirement. It calls for scalar control of one shared coupling operator. Adding a separately oriented noise source, or changing the noise axis along with its rate, does not satisfy it.

## 4. Earning the observer: finite interactions while noise continues

An ideal parity detector would hide the main implementation difficulty. I therefore developed the actual meter interaction.

During a finite interval \(\tau\), couple a fresh meter qubit \(M\) through

\[
H_{\mathrm{active}}
=
\frac{\omega Z_S}{2}
+
g_s Z_SX_M
+
g_a Z_AX_M.
\]

Both controller interactions involve only two qubits. Sensor noise continues throughout.

For any particular sensor history, let \(z=\pm1\) label the logical computational state and put

\[
\Phi=g_a\tau+g_s\int_{\mathrm{active}}r(t)\,dt.
\]

The meter ends in

\[
\cos\Phi\,|0\rangle_M
-i z\sin\Phi\,|1\rangle_M.
\]

This expression is exact even when the sensor flips during the interaction.

It exposes the danger: the meter temporarily carries information about the logical state. Reading or discarding it incorrectly can damage that state.

That danger is directly relevant to current primary research. A September 2026 analysis identifies logical backaction in continuously operated parity measurements built from two-body couplings. Its transient-meter problem cannot be dismissed by simply making the interaction stronger. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

### First implementation: erase the logical information coherently

A controlled-\(Z\) between \(A\) and \(M\), performed before classical readout, removes the factor \(z\):

\[
\cos\Phi\,|0\rangle_M-i\sin\Phi\,|1\rangle_M.
\]

The meter can then be read incorrectly without choosing an incorrect logical correction. A bad report changes the feedback decision.

The erasure gate takes finite time. Sensor evolution during that gate, readout, reset and feedback delay is included in a dead period \(d\). Every cycle lasts

\[
\Delta=\tau+d.
\]

We obtained an exact four-state transfer matrix for the controller, covering all hidden jump counts and times. It does not assume a bounded number of faults or treat the active measurement interval as noiseless.

This construction uses a retained meter and a scheduled erasure operation. Those operations change the conditions of the continuous-parity obstruction; the result is not a refutation of that paper.

## 5. A stronger refinement: accurate information can arrive later

The coherent erasure gate creates a new burden: a systematic error in that gate can itself accumulate into a false signal.

I therefore constructed a second implementation that removes the repeated erasure gate.

Without it, true meter outcome \(m\) contributes only a known logical Pauli factor,

\[
Z_A^m.
\]

That factor commutes with the signal Hamiltonian, native sensor errors, and all subsequent meter interactions. It can be tracked and corrected later.

This permits two different information timescales:

- **Immediate, imperfect reports** choose the next noise rate.
- **Later, more reliable records** determine the sign correction applied to the final sensing result.

The sensor need not wait for the accurate record.

### The record must actually be acquired

Each old meter is decoupled from the sensing system and repeatedly read through a quantum nondemolition measurement of \(Z_M\). It is eventually discarded.

A useful Astra result applies here under its actual conditions: tracing a retired meter after a known QND readout word removes its cross-eigenspace terms. Consequently, the data-and-record statistics admit an exact latent classical meter value, even if the first physical read was weak. **That identity does not reveal the value to the controller.** Repeated observations still have to acquire it.

For conditionally independent report errors with contrast \(\eta_r>0\), majority decoding obeys

\[
\Pr(\widehat m\ne m)
\le e^{-L\eta_r^2/2}.
\]

For \(M\) meter uses, choosing

\[
L\ge
\frac{2}{\eta_r^2}
\log\frac{M}{\alpha_{\mathrm{frame}}}
\]

makes the probability of any erroneous decoded frame at most \(\alpha_{\mathrm{frame}}\).

This exchanges quantum-gate accuracy for explicit classical resources:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\text{total read work}
&=O\!\left(M\log\frac{M}{\alpha_{\mathrm{frame}}}\right),\\
\text{concurrent meter/readout capacity}
&=O\!\left(\log\frac{M}{\alpha_{\mathrm{frame}}}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

For example, with \(10^4\) meter uses, 10% independent report errors, and a 1% total frame-error budget, the simple bound permits **45 reads per meter**. At the illustrative timing in the calculation, that means about **24 concurrent meters/readout lanes and 450,000 total reports**.

The physical condition is equally explicit: the meter’s \(Z\) populations must remain stable during those repeated reads, or be protected by a separately justified storage mechanism. Repetition alone does not establish that stability.

Correcting measurement-induced phase backaction from a sufficiently complete record is established physics. The candidate contribution here is its integration with returning error trajectories, finite noisy observation, and a paid acquisition pipeline. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

## 6. What the sensor actually measures

A perfect final parity measurement is unnecessary for the sensing task.

At the end, randomly choose one of two local Pauli-product measurements:

\[
X_SY_A
\quad\text{or}\quad
Y_SX_A.
\]

Their averaged observable is

\[
O=\frac{X_SY_A+Y_SX_A}{2}.
\]

It acts as logical \(Y\) in the even sector and vanishes in the odd sector. With the appropriate delayed sign correction, the binary outcome has mean

\[
\boxed{
f_T(\omega)
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{even}}\sin(\omega A_T)
\right].
}
\]

This entire distribution is independent of the static unknown noise angle.

Every attempted run is included. Odd endpoints contribute to the measurement statistics; they are not silently discarded at zero cost.

Define

\[
M(T)=\mathbb E[A_T\mathbf 1_{\mathrm{even}}].
\]

The finite controller produces

\[
M(T)=\pi_e vT+O(1),
\]

where \(\pi_e\) is its stationary even-parity probability and \(v>0\) its actual signed-exposure rate.

At the controlled working point, the **achieved classical Fisher information** is

\[
\boxed{
I(0,T)=M(T)^2
=
\pi_e^2v^2T^2+O(T).
}
\]

This comes from an explicit binary measurement. It does not rely on assuming that an abstract optimal quantum measurement is available.

It also uses the correct information quantity. Since the full residence history is not observed, we use the square of the accessible mean exposure, rather than crediting the larger \(\mathbb E[A_T^2]\).

## 7. Beyond a local sensitivity calculation

A large Fisher information at one frequency is insufficient to establish a complete sensor. At a fixed nonzero detuning, unobserved residence-time fluctuations can dephase the response.

I completed the additional statistical construction.

It requires an actual compensating field,

\[
H_{\mathrm{control}}=-\frac{cZ_S}{2},
\]

which changes the residual frequency to \(\omega-c\) while preserving the admitted noise and meter model. Merely changing the final measurement phase does not do this.

Suppose coarse bounds establish

\[
\frac{M(T)}{T}\ge c_0>0
\qquad(T\ge T_0).
\]

The response is then monotone in a sufficiently small interval of width proportional to \(1/T\). A simple procedure repeatedly:

1. Centers the physical reference field on the current frequency interval.
2. Runs a bounded number of binary sensing experiments.
3. Keeps the left, right or middle half of the interval according to the sample mean.
4. Doubles the interrogation time.

The proof uses only a coarse positive gain bound. It does not require precise knowledge of the noise angle or a precisely calibrated response curve.

With an appropriate allocation of failure probabilities, it achieves frequency error

\[
|\widehat\omega-\omega|
=
O(1/T_{\mathrm{final}})
\]

using total sensing time

\[
O\!\left(T_{\mathrm{final}}\log(1/\alpha)\right)
\]

for confidence \(1-\alpha\).

The initial coarse interval is also paid for through ordinary short-time sensor tomography. The antisymmetric part of the sensor’s Bloch generator identifies \(\omega\) independently of \(\theta\).

For the delayed-record implementation, frame-decoding failures are included in the same overall confidence budget. Its extra detector work is logarithmic per meter, and its stage-by-stage pipeline flush adds a logarithmic-squared latency term. The sensing-time statement therefore comes with explicit auxiliary costs.

## 9. What the strongest conventional competitor can already do

This comparison materially limits the claim.

**Once two noise rates are available, ordinary parity QEC with retained counts and joint estimation of frequency and noise angle can also recover local quadratic information.**

Therefore, “rate modulation enables Heisenberg scaling” is not sufficient grounds for declaring a new capability.

| Route | What it already supplies | What the present construction changes |
|---|---|---|
| Known-axis HNLS QEC | Quadratic information under suitable protected-ancilla and control assumptions | Removes the need to know the static noise axis |
| Two-rate QEC with joint inference | Local quadratic information with the same rate-control resource | Eliminates nuisance fitting and sensor recovery flips in the explicit construction |
| Subsystem sensing | Reduced ancilla and syndrome requirements for specified error structures | Supplies a finite observation model for this unknown-axis setting |
| Autonomous sensing protection | Protection through engineered dissipation under stated conditions | Uses transverse returning errors and scalar-rate feedback |
| Fault-tolerant sensing | Longer useful lifetimes under specified controller-noise models | Does not yet match that fault-tolerance guarantee |

The subsystem, autonomous and fault-tolerant comparators are substantive current research, not hypothetical baselines. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="6"}

The surviving contribution is narrower and more concrete:

> **An explicit finite interaction-and-observation architecture that removes a static environmental nuisance, retains a useful signal, and supplies a complete statistical consumer with charged observation costs.**

Whether that architecture beats the best conventional implementation on an actual platform remains unresolved.
