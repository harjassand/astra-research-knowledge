# Exact-arithmetic audit and unresolved adaptive gate

A genuinely rigorous finite-bath baseline now exists in `exact_inertia_audit.py` and its JSON output. It certifies the specified U=4, N=2 midpoint physical electron Hamiltonian, not the 600-node Gauss bath or continuum. The full dimension is 64; all seven spin sectors (20,15,15,6,6,1,1) are included. No Lanczos or floating-point diagonalization enters the proof.

The only irrational matrix coefficient is t=sqrt(1/pi). Machin's identity and rational alternating-series remainders enclose pi; integer square-root inequalities enclose t in an interval of width 10^-16. The rational surrogate differs from the exact physical Hamiltonian in operator norm by at most 4*10^-16, because there are four hopping operators, each of norm one. Exact Fraction LDL elimination computes inertia, with rational cutoff shifts when a zero pivot occurs. Rational bisection plus Weyl's inequality gives ordered eigenvalue enclosures and certifies completeness. Printed decimal intervals are illustrative; the exact rational intervals in JSON are the proof data.

Result: a ground doublet, next excitation in approximately [0.627287240589,0.627287573675], fourth ordered level above 1.2065846. The count below the true ground plus 0.8 is exactly three, using counts at both enclosing ground-shifted cutoffs. Measured elapsed time is in JSON (about 2–3 seconds here). This is slower than the prior ordinary floating-point small-matrix runs and is a different bath size. It establishes neither a competitive runtime nor convergence to the continuum.

## How to certify the adaptive q and count, without circularity

For every retained-sector effective Hamiltonian, choose a proposed rational lower bound b and prove H_eff-b I positive definite by interval LDL with all pivots positive, or exact rational LDL after enclosing the physical matrix by a surrogate and a norm radius r. In the latter case prove H_eff,surrogate-(b+r) I positive definite. Do this in every sector, not only the candidate ground sector. Then q=b+s is a certified tail threshold. An accurate approximate ground vector may propose b but cannot validate it. Gershgorin lower bounds are valid alternatives, but need not pass the gate.

For H_K and H_K-W/(q-t), enclose each matrix and prove the inertia at both t-r and t+r agrees. Include every symmetry sector. Exact rational LDL has a valid zero-pivot failure mode; a production implementation should use symmetric 1x1/2x2 pivoting or change the test cutoff within the requested isolation interval. Unpivoted floating-point LDL with apparently nonzero pivots is not sufficient. Sparse factorization can generate candidate factors, but every residual and inverse/conditioning bound must be outward enclosed before asserting an inertia.

The large 16-mode calculation has a largest sector of 48620. A dense rational factorization there is not a plausible competitive baseline. A dense double matrix alone needs about 18.9 GB, before factors or interval overhead. Exact arithmetic also suffers numerator/denominator growth. The small exact audit does not pay this bill or furnish a lower q for that calculation.

## Continuum enclosure that is sufficient, but not yet implemented

Use raw source-polynomial functions F, not floating-point QR vectors as exact continuum functions. Enclose their Gram matrix B=integral F*F, first moment J=integral F*hF, second moment K=integral F*h²F, and resolvent moment R_s=integral F*(h-s)^-1 F. Also enclose source overlaps. Integrals are over the explicitly weighted normal band. Validate B positive definite and an interval orthonormalization X with X* B X=I. Then A=X* J X, C=X* K X-A², and A_eff=sI+(X* R_s X)^-1 are enclosed continuum objects. Source containment is algebraic because raw F includes the source functions.

A simple certified integrator partitions the band into rational cells, evaluates every elementary integrand with interval square roots/divisions, multiplies its interval range by cell width, and sums outward. It converges for s<1 because denominators are separated from zero. Adaptive or validated high-order quadrature is likely necessary for cost, especially s=0.999. Interval enclosure of these finite matrices, propagated into Fock operator norm radii, is sufficient; no direct operator-norm approximation of the infinite many-body Hamiltonian is claimed. For one-particle perturbation E on m retained modes, ||dGamma(E)|| <= m||E|| is a safe conservative budget. Source-coefficient errors require a separate CAR norm budget.

Neither a quadrature-order comparison nor a Gauss rule alone is such an enclosure. In particular the moment orthonormalization may be ill-conditioned, magnifying small entry errors. No positive numerical continuum certificate, certified q for the adaptive benchmark, or complete 16-mode inertia has been produced by this audit. Those remain explicit blockers rather than assumed successes.

## New rank-eight rational-source calculation supersedes the resource blocker

A later calculation uses only four spatial retained bath modes (eight spin modes), built from parity sources and one pole at 0.5. `verified_moments.py` supplies continuum interval enclosures and `verified_sector_spectrum.py` validates all eleven many-body sectors, largest dimension 252. This changes the practical conclusion above: the large 48620-dimensional calculation is unnecessary for this parameter point.

The spectrum validator computes complete approximate orthogonal eigenbases and then independently bounds their residuals and orthogonality defects. If QᵀQ−I has norm at most η<1, the polar unitary U satisfies ||Q−U||≤η/(1+sqrt(1−η)). Hence every ordered eigenvalue is within ||HQ−QD||+(||H||+||D||)||Q−U|| of the proposed diagonal spectrum. Frobenius norms bound spectral norms, and standard IEEE754 gamma_n bounds enclose matrix-product rounding. Additional norm radii cover continuum input intervals, CAR assembly and symmetrization. It does not assume that the eigensolver found the right eigenvalues. Its numerical assumptions are explicit: nearest binary64 operations, conventional dot-product error bounds, and no overflow/underflow. It is a numerical-analysis certificate, not machine-checked formal verification.

Current result: q≥−1.185275736582, excitation enclosed in outward-rounded [0.53508161286,0.53580779182], width below 0.000727. Complete inertia counts agree at three throughout the possible cutoffs corresponding to the true ground plus 0.8. Eigenvalue error budgets are below 4.6e−9. A controlled one-thread run took 0.29 seconds after moment integration; another run with uncontrolled thread settings took 10.17 seconds, illustrating why the timing is not a solver comparison.

Final continuum certification is conditional on that independent audit accepting the stated quadrature enclosure.
