## 1. A computable bound that applies before choosing an encoder

Consider a graph of \(n\) physical qubits, including returning quantum ancillas. Every qubit has a guaranteed additive, unmonitored independent dephasing component
\[
\gamma_v(t)(\Delta_v-\mathrm{id}),\qquad \gamma_v(t)\ge\gamma&gt;0,
\]
with noise continuing during gates, waiting and cooling contacts.

Let:

- \(j_v\) bound local entropy removal at site \(v\), in bits per unit time;
- \(b_e\) bound entropy transfer through physical connection \(e\);
- \(s_0\) be the entropy of the independent auxiliary source before a unital encoding.

For a two-qubit Hamiltonian term of norm at most \(g_e\), the established small incremental entangling theorem supplies the conservative allowance
\[
b_e=\frac{18g_e}{\hbar}.
\]
Arbitrary spectator entanglement is included in that bound. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

For an entire \(\ell\)-qubit output with **full diamond error** \(\delta&lt;1\), define
\[
\eta=h_2(\delta/2)+\frac{\delta}{2}\log_2(2^{2\ell}-1),
\qquad q=\ell-\eta&gt;0.
\]
Here \(q\) is the guaranteed recoverable coherent information.

The graph quantity is
\[
\mathcal M(G)=
\min_{A\subseteq V}
\left[
|V\setminus A|
+\frac{\sum_{v\in A}j_v+\sum_{e\in\partial A}b_e}{2\gamma}
\right].
\]

I derived the finite resource law
\[
\boxed{
q\le
\frac{n-s_0+2\gamma T\,\mathcal M(G)}
     {1+2\gamma T}.
}
\]

Replacing \(s_0\) by zero grants the strongest possible initial purity allowance. That weaker bound also covers an already encoded source, without requiring a many-body entropy estimate. For any target \(q&gt;\mathcal M(G)\), the theorem gives
\[
\boxed{
T\le
\frac{n-s_0-q}{2\gamma\,[q-\mathcal M(G)]}.
}
\]

These are necessary bounds, with the source, whole-register accuracy and physical duration retained. The ordinary proof is in :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}[GRAPH_THEOREM.md](producer-workspace/GRAPH_THEOREM.md).

### Why the proof works

The useful quantity for a cut \(A\mid O\) is
\[
W_A=S(RA\mid O),
\]
where \(R\) is the untouched logical reference and \(O\) contains outside quantum memory and the retained classical history.

Complete dephasing of \(A\) leaves only the outside quantum memory able to carry quantum information. The inherited complete entropy-production inequality therefore forces \(W_A\) to grow at rate at least
\[
2\gamma\,[q-|O_{\mathrm{quantum}}|]_+.
\]

An outside-only CPTP operation—including an arbitrarily powerful reset—cannot decrease \(S(RA\mid O)\). Only charged interior entropy removal and crossing operations can offset that growth. The final output requirement supplies the endpoint bound
\[
W_A(T)\le |A|-q.
\]

This avoids assigning the logical information to a particular location. Moving it outside is a legitimate option, and the outside-memory term pays for it.

### The graph calculation is implemented

An ordinary source–sink network computes \(\mathcal M(G)\):

| Network connection | Capacity |
|---|---:|
| Source to physical vertex \(v\) | \(1\) |
| Physical vertex \(v\) to sink | \(j_v/(2\gamma)\) |
| Each direction of physical edge \(e\) | \(b_e/(2\gamma)\) |

The implementation uses exact rational arithmetic and emits a flow–cut certificate. Its verifier checks capacities, conservation, cut equality, the cut decomposition and the derived finite physical bounds. This requires polynomial work in the supplied graph and coefficient precision; no many-body state or encoder search is represented.

The abstract flow certifies the bound’s optimization. Constructing a quantum protocol that approaches it remains a separate problem.

## 2. The physical consequence: reset speed cannot replace access

Suppose only \(m\) sites provide entropy removal, the interaction graph has degree at most \(z\), and each coupling has norm at most \(g\). Even granting unlimited reset strength at those sites,
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M(G)\le
m\left(1+\frac{9zg}{\gamma\hbar}\right).
}
\]

At fixed local control-to-noise ratio, increasing reset speed at the same few locations cannot indefinitely preserve an extensive unknown quantum register.

For a \(d\)-dimensional cube whose **actual quantum entropy outlets** are confined to a boundary collar of fixed thickness,
\[
\mathcal M(G)=O(L^{d-1}).
\]
Consequently:

- For durations at least of order \(L/\gamma\), recoverable width is at most surface order.
- At a fixed positive logical rate \(q/n\), the lifetime stays \(O(1/\gamma)\) as the processor grows, even with a volume-order pure starting bank.

The physical qualification is essential: **a refrigerator attached at the surface does not establish boundary-only quantum entropy removal**. Interior amplitude damping, readout fields, lossy modes and local record storage are interior resources. If the bulk has service at most \(j_{\mathrm{bulk}}\) per site, the same theorem adds its explicit contribution
\[
\frac{j_{\mathrm{bulk}}}{2\gamma}L^d.
\]
The result concerns the actual microscopic interfaces. It does not infer a heat-flow restriction from the refrigerator’s position.

The prefactors also retain \(g/(\hbar\gamma)\). With very fast gates, the asymptotic surface restriction may become informative only at large sizes; no practical hardware crossover has been acquired.

## 4. The independent construction adds a stronger test for a specified code

The complementary attack produced more than a second version of the universal bound.

For a memory that returns to a stated stabilizer code before each fresh, independent Pauli-noise interval, the noise generates a mandatory syndrome-entropy load. This remains true with arbitrary correlations between logical data and retained quantum helpers.

For errors inside a physical cut,
\[
S_A=B_AE_A\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_2,
\]
and
\[
\boxed{
H(S_A)\ge
\max_{J\text{ a column basis of }B_A}
\sum_{i\in J}h_2(p_i).
}
\]
At equal error probability, this becomes
\[
H(S_A)\ge \operatorname{rank}(B_A)\,h_2(p).
\]

Greedy binary elimination acquires the bound for a supplied cut. It can remain informative even when outside quantum storage is large enough that the universal factor \([q-h]_+\) vanishes.

Code-dependent correction entropy has established ancestry; the addition here is the explicit helper-stable cyclic budget and its localization to the physical cut. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

The assumptions matter. The interval must begin in the actual declared code, and the noise must factor across the cut. Correlated stabilizer noise and interleaved code rotations give executed counterexamples to dropping those conditions. On the same interval, the syndrome demand and universal demand are combined by taking their **maximum**, avoiding double counting.
