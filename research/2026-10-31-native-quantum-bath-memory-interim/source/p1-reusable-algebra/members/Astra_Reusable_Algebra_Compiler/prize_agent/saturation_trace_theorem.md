# Uniform trace acquisition from a generated matrix algebra

Internal complementary proof note, 31 October 2026.

## 1. Scope and originality boundary

The object of study is a fixed, unknown, finite-dimensional unital complex matrix *-algebra generated by Hermitian operators available through exact matrix-vector calls. The aim is to acquire a reusable finite representation that answers arbitrary later noncommutative spectral and positive-trace questions, even when those questions depend on the acquired representation and the random probes.

The following are conventional ingredients, not new discoveries: finite-dimensional *-algebra structure; cyclic modules/GNS representations; invariant-subspace spin-up and generalized Krylov closure; simultaneous block diagonalization; preservation of spectrum and positivity by faithful *-representations; Gaussian quadratic-form concentration; and variance reduction by symmetry averaging. A potentially distinct scoped result is the operational combination of unknown-algebra acquisition, exact multiplicity recovery when a sampled module saturates, tracial averaging otherwise, and a single simultaneous guarantee over its whole positive cone. Novelty of that combination remains unestablished.

## 2. Fixed setting and information interface

Let A_1,...,A_p be fixed Hermitian n by n complex matrices. The learner receives the ambient dimension n and can submit any v in C^n to an oracle returning A_i v. It receives neither a decomposition nor an algebra basis. Exact arithmetic and exact zero/rank decisions are used in this theorem. Generic Gaussian sampling is mathematical, rather than a finite-bit implementation guarantee.

Let A be the unital complex *-algebra they generate. For proof only, choose a unitary decomposition

    C^n = direct_sum_alpha (C^{r_alpha} tensor C^{m_alpha}),
    A = direct_sum_alpha (M_{r_alpha}(C) tensor I_{m_alpha}).

There are b simple components. These r_alpha,m_alpha and their unitary coordinates are hidden from the acquisition algorithm. Write D=sum r_alpha^2 for the abstract algebra dimension and R=sum r_alpha for its smallest faithful representation size.

Draw q independent standard circular complex Gaussian vectors g_1,...,g_q, with E|g_j[k]|^2=1, and let

    K_q = span{ B g_l : B in A, 1 <= l <= q }.

Compute an orthonormal basis Q of K_q by repeatedly applying every generator to each retained basis vector, retaining only linearly independent residuals. Include the identity action by starting from the seeds. Once all generator actions preserve the span, closure is exact. There are at most p k_q generator calls on retained basis vectors, where k_q=dim K_q. Initial seed processing and orthogonalization are separately charged. Dense arithmetic for the naive implementation is O(n q^2 + p n k_q^2), in addition to oracle work; storage is O(n k_q + p k_q^2). A dense oracle can itself cost O(n^2) per action. No poly(log n) claim follows.

## 3. Exact module multiplicity theorem

**Theorem 1.** With probability one,

    k_q = sum_alpha r_alpha s_alpha,
    s_alpha = min(m_alpha, q r_alpha).

Restriction to K_q is a faithful unital *-representation of A. It is unitarily equivalent to

    direct_sum_alpha (M_{r_alpha}(C) tensor I_{s_alpha}).

Consequently, exact algebra decomposition of the small reduced generators Q* A_i Q recovers r_alpha and s_alpha, with blocks identified up to permutation and unitary equivalence. Whenever s_alpha < q r_alpha, one has m_alpha=s_alpha exactly. At s_alpha=q r_alpha, only m_alpha>=q r_alpha follows; equality must remain in the unresolved case.

**Proof.** In the hidden alpha block identify g_l with an r_alpha by m_alpha coefficient matrix G_{alpha,l}. Left action of B_alpha replaces this matrix by B_alpha G_{alpha,l}. The module generated by all q vectors consists of all r_alpha by m_alpha matrices whose rows lie in the sum of the row spaces of G_{alpha,l}. The stacked q r_alpha by m_alpha matrix has generic rank min(m_alpha,q r_alpha), because a suitable maximal minor is a nonzero polynomial in independent continuous variables. This proves the dimension and the reduced multiplicity. Every simple block occurs with positive multiplicity, so the kernel of the restricted representation, a two-sided ideal, contains no simple summand. It is zero. Hermitian generators ensure the invariant module is reducing, so restriction preserves adjoints. The multiplicity inference follows directly from the minimum formula. QED.

Faithfulness preserves every polynomial identity, invertibility, spectra as sets, singular values as sets, operator norm, and positivity. It preserves noncommutative rational expressions wherever the original inverses exist. It does not preserve ambient eigenvalue multiplicities or ambient trace without extra weights. Arbitrary ambient vectors and matrices outside A are not automatically supported.

## 4. Why direct Gaussian quadratic forms are unnecessarily restrictive

For one seed, g*Bg on an alpha block equals Tr(B_alpha G_alpha G_alpha*). Uniform relative trace approximation over every B_alpha>=0 therefore demands G_alpha G_alpha* approximately m_alpha I in Loewner order. This entails a Wishart conditioning requirement on m_alpha relative to r_alpha and epsilon. It also fails deterministically when m_alpha<r_alpha because the empirical covariance is rank deficient.

After the algebra is acquired, the within-block anisotropy can be removed. Define the central block energy

    W_alpha = sum_{l=1}^q ||P_alpha g_l||^2,

where P_alpha is the ambient central projection. It is computable from the reduced central projection E_alpha and reduced seed coordinates c_l=Q*g_l:

    W_alpha = sum_l ||E_alpha c_l||^2.

The ordinary trace on the irreducible algebra block is intrinsic. One may obtain an irreducible block B_alpha by standard decomposition of the reduced algebra, or use Tr_K(E_alpha pi(B))/s_alpha. Thus no ambient central projector need be explicitly materialized.

## 5. Saturation-or-concentration estimator

Define acquired weights

    mhat_alpha = s_alpha                         if s_alpha < q r_alpha,
                 W_alpha/(q r_alpha)             otherwise.

For every B in A define the reusable linear functional

    That(B) = sum_alpha mhat_alpha Tr(B_alpha).

Equivalently,

    That(B) = sum_alpha (mhat_alpha/s_alpha)
                         Tr_K(E_alpha pi(B)).

The first branch uses algebraically exact multiplicities. The second branch replaces the empirical state by a trace on each simple component; it is the algebraic form of symmetry averaging/Rao–Blackwellization.

**Theorem 2.** For fixed q>=1 and 0<epsilon<=1,

    Pr[exists B in A, B>=0:
          |That(B)-Tr_n(B)| > epsilon Tr_n(B)]
      <= 2 b exp(-epsilon^2 q^2/4).

Hence q=ceil(2 sqrt(log(2b/delta))/epsilon) suffices for probability at least 1-delta, with no lower bound on any unknown m_alpha. A choice using b<=n is available before acquisition. The theorem remains valid for an unbounded, adaptively chosen sequence of positive elements of this fixed algebra. It incurs no union bound over future tasks.

**Proof.** The deterministic hidden central projections have rank d_alpha=r_alpha m_alpha. Circular Gaussian invariance gives independent block entries, hence W_alpha has Gamma(shape N_alpha=q r_alpha m_alpha, scale 1) distribution. For X~Gamma(N,1), the moment-generating function is (1-t)^(-N), t<1. Chernoff optimization gives

    Pr[X >= (1+epsilon)N]
      <= exp[-N(epsilon-log(1+epsilon))],
    Pr[X <= (1-epsilon)N]
      <= exp[-N(-epsilon-log(1-epsilon))].

For 0<epsilon<=1, the first rate is at least epsilon^2/4 and the second at least epsilon^2/2 (the epsilon=1 lower-tail event is null). Thus the two-sided error is at most 2 exp(-N epsilon^2/4).

Resolved components are exact. Every unresolved component has m_alpha>=q r_alpha and hence N_alpha>=q^2 r_alpha^2>=q^2. A union bound over the b fixed simple components gives, with the stated probability, (1-epsilon)m_alpha<=mhat_alpha<=(1+epsilon)m_alpha simultaneously. For any positive B, every Tr(B_alpha) is nonnegative. Multiply each weight inequality by that number and sum. This proves the inequality for the full positive cone at once, including every data-dependent later selection from it. QED.

The q count is a number of full Gaussian seed vectors, not the number of oracle calls. Closure costs p k_q actions, with k_q=sum r_alpha min(m_alpha,q r_alpha)<=min(n,qD), plus the reduced decomposition work. A claim of O(q) total oracle work for arbitrary algebras would be false.

For q=1, the tracial estimator without the exact branch already requires only r_alpha m_alpha >= O(epsilon^(-2) log(b/delta)) on unknown blocks, rather than m_alpha >= O(r_alpha epsilon^(-2) log(b/delta)). The saturation branch eliminates the need for that promise by increasing q.

## 6. Explicit observed-data certificates

Fix q independently of its samples. For t>0 define

    lower_shape(W,t) =
      (max(0, sqrt(max(W-t/2,0))-sqrt(t/2)))^2,
    upper_shape(W,t) =
      (sqrt(W+t/2)+sqrt(t/2))^2.

For X~Gamma(N,1), standard Chernoff consequences are

    Pr[X > N + sqrt(2Nt) + t] <= exp(-t),
    Pr[X < N - sqrt(2Nt)] <= exp(-t).

These also follow directly by applying the elementary chi-square tail calculation to 2X. Solving the two quadratic inequalities in sqrt(N) gives

    lower_shape(X,t) <= N <= upper_shape(X,t)

outside an event of probability at most 2 exp(-t). The lower endpoint is zero when X<=t.

Choose t=log(2b/delta), or conservatively log(2n/delta). On each unresolved block form the integer interval

    L_alpha = max(q r_alpha,
                  ceil(lower_shape(W_alpha,t)/(q r_alpha))),
    U_alpha = min(floor(n/r_alpha),
                  floor(upper_shape(W_alpha,t)/(q r_alpha))).

Resolved blocks use L_alpha=U_alpha=s_alpha. On one event of probability at least 1-delta every true m_alpha belongs to its interval. Empty intervals mean the concentration event or numerical/algebraic assumptions failed; the algorithm must not silently repair them into an asserted certificate.

For all B>=0 in the fixed algebra, simultaneously,

    sum_alpha L_alpha Tr(B_alpha)
       <= Tr_n(B)
       <= sum_alpha U_alpha Tr(B_alpha).

These are confidence bounds valid uniformly after observing the data. They are not a Bayesian posterior. One may additionally intersect with the known ambient-size condition sum r_alpha m_alpha=n. For a future B, optimizing over the continuous relaxation of this box plus one equality gives sound tighter trace bounds in O(b log b) via a fractional-knapsack allocation using slopes Tr(B_alpha)/r_alpha. Exact integer optimization is not being claimed to have that cost.

If q is chosen adaptively, a fixed-q confidence statement is insufficient. A simple anytime construction allocates delta_j=6delta/(pi^2(j+1)^2) to predetermined stages q_j, and uses t_j=log(2b/delta_j). A union bound gives validity at every stage and any stopping time. Reuse of nested independent Gaussian samples is allowed. Data-dependent choice of future operators inside the fixed algebra is allowed; changing the algebra based on the same probes is not covered.

### 6.1 Gamma-deviance inversion and a reusable real confidence set

A sharper computational alternative inverts the exact Chernoff exponent

    D(w,nu) = w - nu - nu log(w/nu).

For fixed true nu>0 and W~Gamma(nu,1), the event D(W,nu)>t is the union of a lower and an upper tail, each bounded by exp(-t). For fixed w>0, D(w,nu) is convex in nu, has its unique minimum zero at nu=w, derivative log(nu/w), and limiting value w at nu=0. Its sublevel set is an interval [ell(w,t),u(w,t)]; the lower endpoint is zero if w<=t, otherwise a scalar root below w, and the upper endpoint is a scalar root above w. Both roots are found by bracketing and bisection.

Use ambient block sizes n_alpha=r_alpha m_alpha, not multiplicities, as coordinates. The shape is nu_alpha=q_alpha n_alpha. A resolved block has exact n_alpha=r_alpha s_alpha. An unresolved one has n_alpha>=q_alpha r_alpha^2 and the gamma interval

    ell(W_alpha,t)/q_alpha <= n_alpha <= u(W_alpha,t)/q_alpha.

Intersect all these intervals with sum n_alpha=n. On the simultaneous event, this real polytope contains the true sizes, so it supplies valid bounds for every future Hermitian operator, including operators selected after inspecting the confidence set. If a future irreducible block is X_alpha, its trace slope is c_alpha=Tr(X_alpha)/r_alpha and its ambient trace is sum c_alpha n_alpha. Slopes may have either sign. The exact real-polytope extrema are obtained in O(b log b): start at all lower bounds, then distribute the remaining total size to blocks in increasing slope order for the minimum and decreasing order for the maximum. This is fractional knapsack, and does not claim exact optimization over integer multiplicities.

Known total size can be strictly stronger than the probabilistic intervals. If only one block remains unresolved, its size is n minus all resolved sizes, exactly, regardless of its random energy. If b=1 this applies immediately. The code prioritizes this exact deduction before consulting its Gaussian interval.

The module `trace_confidence.py` implements this real confidence polytope; `check_trace_confidence.py` verifies the greedy method against independent general linear programming, checks deviance inversion, and illustrates indefinite cancellation, a data-dependent selection of the most uncertain block, known-total tightening, and deterministic exact recovery with one unresolved block. Double precision computations are a numerical implementation rather than a formal interval-arithmetic certificate.

## 7. Consequences that do not require new ambient queries

For every later Hermitian H in A, exp(H)>=0, so its partition function is approximated relatively by the same acquired functional. This holds simultaneously over all adaptive choices of coefficients, noncommutative expressions, and temperatures. The log-partition error is at most -log(1-epsilon). Finite representation work to form exp(pi(H)) must still be charged.

For an arbitrary Hermitian B, positive-cone control implies

    |That(B)-Tr(B)| <= epsilon Tr(|B|),

not a relative bound in |Tr(B)|. Cancellation can make the latter impossible. For rational expressions, denominators require invertibility and numerical evaluation requires a conditioning budget. Exact positivity of the representation does not make downstream nonconvex optimization or planning easy. Trace constraints in an SDP can need robustness margins; positive-objective approximation alone is not a universal feasibility theorem.

## 8. A lower bound already inside a two-dimensional algebra

This lower bound is a corollary of existing work, not an original lower-bound theorem.

Swartworth and Woodruff, *Optimal Eigenvalue Approximation via Sketching*, arXiv:2304.09281v1, Theorem 23 in Appendix A, prove that for sufficiently large r and n>=C r^2, distinguishing a uniformly random rank-r real orthogonal projector from a random rank-(r+2) projector with success at least 3/4 requires at least r/10 adaptive matrix-vector queries. Primary PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09281 .

Apply an acquired positive-trace service to the single generator P. Its unital algebra is span{I,P}, so its abstract dimension is only two. A (1+-epsilon) estimate of Tr(P) distinguishes ranks r and r+2 whenever

    (1+epsilon) r < (1-epsilon)(r+2),

equivalently epsilon(r+1)<1. Taking r=floor(1/(2epsilon)) for small epsilon gives r=Theta(1/epsilon) and establishes an Omega(1/epsilon) worst-case oracle requirement. Complex-vector access changes the lower bound by at most a factor two, since one complex query to a real P can be simulated by queries on its real and imaginary parts. This shows that the inverse-epsilon dependence cannot generally disappear even when D=2. It does not prove the optimal dependence on D, p, b, failure probability, or numerical precision.

## 9. Strong comparisons and what has not been earned

- **MeatAxe spin-up:** The official SharedMeatAxe documentation already computes generator-invariant closures from seeds and restricts the generators to that subspace. This is a direct algorithmic antecedent to acquisition. https://users.fmi.uni-jena.de/~king/SharedMeatAxe/prog_zsp.html .
- **Automatic simultaneous block diagonalization:** Maehara and Murota give an algorithm from matrix generators with no algebraic structure supplied in advance. Thus 'hidden structure is acquired rather than given' is not sufficient novelty. Their method uses spectral decompositions of generic algebra elements. https://www.keisu.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/data/2008/METR08-26.pdf .
- **Regular *-representation:** De Klerk, Pasechnik and Schrijver explicitly reduce large symmetric SDPs via a smaller *-isomorphic algebra representation. Exact PSD preservation of a learned algebra is an inherited principle. https://homepages.cwi.nl/~lex/files/symm.pdf .
- **Hutch++:** Existing PSD trace estimation already achieves inverse-epsilon rather than inverse-epsilon-squared matvec dependence for an individual target, using a low-rank variance-reduction stage. The present q dependence is not a first such improvement. The different target is a simultaneously valid reusable tracial functional over an acquired noncommutative algebra. https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09649 .
- **Stochastic Lanczos quadrature:** Spectrum sketches can be retained and reused across many scalar functions of one matrix. Any comparison that reruns Lanczos from scratch for each commuting task is too weak. Chen, Trogdon and Ubaru, ICML 2021, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/chen21s.html .
- **Uniform scalar-function guarantees:** Chen, Trogdon and Ubaru's *Randomized matrix-free quadrature: unified and uniform bounds for stochastic Lanczos quadrature and the kernel polynomial method* (arXiv 2022, revised December 2024; SIAM J. Scientific Computing 2025) explicitly proves simultaneous accuracy over bounded analytic scalar functions. Thus reusable uniform spectral trace queries are established, not a novelty of the present proposal. https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01941 .
- **Reused parameter-dependent trace estimation:** Matti, He, Kressner and Lam, *Stochastic Trace Estimation for Parameter-Dependent Matrices Applied to Spectral Density Approximation* (arXiv 2025, revised February 2026; SIAM 2026), analyze fixed randomization for Girard-Hutchinson, Nystrom and Nystrom++ and reuse matvecs through Chebyshev approximation. Their main error criterion is integrated L1 over the parameter interval, rather than relative control of every positive algebra element. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18626 and https://doi.org/10.1137/25M1737857 .
- **Uniform stochastic trace optimization:** Saibaba and Ipsen, *Stochastic Trace Optimization of Parameter Dependent Matrices Based on Statistical Learning Theory* (August 2025), analyze reused Rademacher trace probes over compact, possibly nonlinear matrix families using epsilon nets and generic chaining. A claim that adaptation to a reusable trace estimate is itself new would therefore be too broad. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05764 .
- **BOLT and XTrace:** Recent block/variance-reduced trace methods should be considered in practical numerical comparisons; a theoretical exact-closure construction is not automatically faster. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12289 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.07825 .

Current search found no exact match to the whole saturation-or-concentration universal-algebra statement. That is not evidence of historical novelty. The construction does not prove a practical advantage over a retaining spin-up/decomposition implementation equipped with the same trace estimator; such an implementation is essentially a conventional implementation of the construction.

## 10. Adaptive generator admission: review of the parent's separate extension

Suppose a basis B_1,...,B_D has an injective stacked witness map B -> (B h_j)_j. A proposed operator C is either inconsistent with that old witness span, proving it independent, or has unique fitted coefficients c_i. If R=C-sum c_iB_i is nonzero but agrees on all old probes, a fresh independent finite-grid vector h detects R h != 0 with probability at least 1-1/|S|. This follows by conditioning on all but one input coordinate corresponding to a nonzero coefficient in any nonzero row of R. The candidate must be fixed before h is sampled. On detection, adjoining C and h preserves witness injectivity: R vanishes on all old probes but not on h.

Thus successful discoveries strictly increase operator span dimension. Persistent closure over each essential-generator/basis pair gives at most p_*D_* tested product candidates, plus admission tests. This is an incremental algebra-basis closure and polynomial-identity-testing analysis; no search over failed candidate coefficients is hidden because the old witness fit is unique. The arithmetic bit size and full vector action cost remain.

A positive Gram form sum_j (B_i h_j)* (B_k h_j) makes the faithful regular representation into a *-representation, using left multiplication. This is conventional algebra representation theory. Crucially, the trace concentration theorem above is for a fixed algebra independent of the Gaussian trace probes. Adaptive enlargement informed by those probes requires fresh trace sampling after each fixed admission stage or a separate valid model-selection bound. Relation verification with fresh finite-grid probes remains valid under adaptive candidates.
