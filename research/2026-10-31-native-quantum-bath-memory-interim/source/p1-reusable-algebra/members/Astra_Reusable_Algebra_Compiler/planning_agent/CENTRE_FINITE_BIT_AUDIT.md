# Scoped audit: centre-only extraction and finite rational seeds

## Verdict

The centre-only refinement is valid. A full Wedderburn basis is unnecessary for the trace theorem. Exact algebraic ranks and traces obtained from central projectors recover the required simple dimensions and represented multiplicities. The refinement replaces a larger decomposition subroutine with explicit algebra closure, linear systems, a separating central element, and algebraic-number operations. Their costs remain charged.

The finite-grid seed version is also valid with a union bound combining algebraic rank failure and unconditional Hanson-Wright concentration. Do not condition the concentration statement on the successful-rank event. Source noise remains outside this exact-input theorem.

## 1. Rational coordinate model

Assume the fixed generators have entries in Q(i), or that the exact matrix-vector oracle returns that field with its bit cost charged. Retain unnormalised independent word vectors in V. Let

    G=V*V,
    A_j V=V B_j.

Then G is positive definite, B_j has rational-complex entries, and

    B_j^dagger_G := G^(-1) B_j* G = B_j.

The coordinate representation is a *-algebra with this G-adjoint. No Cholesky square root is necessary.

Acquire an exact linear basis E_1,...,E_d of the **entire compressed algebra** by closure under multiplication by the B_j. The subsequent dimension formula uses this full basis, not only the original generators.

Find the centre by solving [Z,B_j]=0 subject to Z in span(E_l). The centre has complex dimension b. A rational G-selfadjoint basis of its real selfadjoint part can be constructed from

    (C_l+C_l^dagger_G)/2,
    (C_l-C_l^dagger_G)/(2i)

for a rational-complex centre basis C_l, retaining b independent candidates. These candidates span the centre over C and the selfadjoint part over R.

## 2. A separating central element

Choose a random rational/integer linear combination Z of the b G-selfadjoint centre basis elements. In a complex Wedderburn decomposition it acts as scalar lambda_alpha on each simple isotypic component.

For each distinct pair alpha,beta, equality lambda_alpha=lambda_beta is one nonzero linear equation in the chosen coefficients. If coefficients are independent and uniform from a set T, collision probability is at most b(b-1)/(2|T|). Choosing |T|>=b(b-1), for example, gives success probability at least one half for b>=2. The b=1 case is immediate.

The algorithm need not trust that probability: compute the minimal polynomial f of Z exactly and accept only if deg(f)=b. Since Z is G-selfadjoint and central, its minimal polynomial is square-free and has as many roots as the distinct central scalar values. Therefore deg(f)=b certifies separation. Independent retries form a Las Vegas procedure with constant expected attempts under the stated choice; failed attempts must be counted. A prescribed high-probability time cap can allocate a logarithmic retry budget.

Although Z has entries in Q(i), its minimal polynomial lies in Q[t]: G-selfadjointness makes every eigenvalue real, and conjugating the monic minimal polynomial gives another minimal polynomial of the same roots. Its coefficients are therefore both in Q(i) and real, hence rational.

## 3. Projectors without a splitting field

For each real root lambda_alpha of f, define in F_alpha=Q(i,lambda_alpha)

    q_alpha(t)=f(t)/(t-lambda_alpha),
    P_alpha=q_alpha(Z)/f'(lambda_alpha).

This is polynomial division in F_alpha[t], not an inverse of the singular matrix Z-lambda_alpha I. Because f is square-free, f'(lambda_alpha) is nonzero. Evaluating the scalar polynomial at each eigenvalue shows P_alpha equals one on the alpha component and zero on every other component. Thus

    P_alpha^2=P_alpha,
    P_alpha^dagger_G=P_alpha.

Only the field generated by one root is needed for each block. Its degree is at most 2b over Q. Rational polynomial factorization and real-root isolation provide a finite exact representation (irreducible polynomial plus isolating interval). Algebraic operations and zero/sign decisions must be charged; they are not floating-point rank tests.

## 4. Recover dimensions and one-copy trace

Compute

    d_alpha = dim_(F_alpha) span{P_alpha E_l : 1<=l<=d},
    R_alpha = rank_(F_alpha)(P_alpha).

Matrix rank is unchanged on extending F_alpha to C. Over C the first image is M_(r_alpha)(C) tensor I_(s_alpha), an algebra of dimension r_alpha^2, and the projector carrier dimension is r_alpha s_alpha. Consequently

    r_alpha = integer_sqrt(d_alpha),
    s_alpha = R_alpha/r_alpha.

No assumption that the simple factor splits as M_r(F_alpha) is needed. Its dimension still becomes r^2 after scalar extension, and exact matrix rank is invariant under extension.

For any acquired expression Xhat,

    tr_one_copy(X_alpha) = Tr(P_alpha Xhat)/s_alpha.

For a seed with rational coordinate c_j satisfying g_j=V c_j, the projected ambient energy is

    ||P_alpha^ambient g_j||^2
      = c_j* G P_alpha c_j.

The identity follows from G-selfadjointness and idempotence. Thus every required quantity is available without an irreducible basis.

Per-block algebraic outputs can be retained separately, so a common splitting field is unnecessary. If an exact scalar sum across blocks is requested for a rational-complex future expression, conjugate roots of an irreducible factor have the same r_alpha,s_alpha and classification; their contributions can be combined with a number-field trace. This gives rational-complex output without constructing the compositum of all root fields. Alternatively, retaining the finite sum as an expression and using certified numerical evaluation keeps the representation finite, while separately charging any requested output accuracy.

This field-cost statement is cleanest for future expression circuits with Q(i) scalar coefficients. The uniform PSD probability theorem permits arbitrary adaptively chosen complex coefficients as a semantic matter, but a later task that explicitly combines algebraic constants from several unrelated fields must pay for its own scalar representation and arithmetic. That downstream field cost is not removed by the per-root acquisition construction.

## 5. Finite rational random seeds

Let every standard-coordinate entry of each seed be independently uniform on

    S={-M,-M+1,...,M},   M>=1.

These are real integer vectors, which are allowed even for a complex algebra. Their mean is zero and variance is the rational number

    sigma^2=M(M+1)/3.

Use the raw integer vectors in all exact computations. The unresolved-block estimator is

    mhat_alpha = w_alpha/(q r_alpha sigma^2).

Normalizing a seed by sigma is needed only in the probability proof; no irrational normalization is performed in the implementation.

### Algebraic rank event

For each block, choose one nonzero maximal minor of the stacked reshaped seed matrix. Its size is s_alpha^*=min(m_alpha,q r_alpha). The product of these minors is a nonzero polynomial in the qN real input coordinates, with complex coefficients and total degree

    sum_alpha s_alpha^* <= sum_alpha m_alpha <= N.

Restriction of a nonzero complex polynomial to real variables does not make it identically zero. The polynomial grid zero bound therefore gives

    P(any non-maximal block rank) <= N/|S|.

Taking |S|>=2N/delta allocates at most delta/2 to this failure. The fixed algebra must precede the random draw.

### Concentration event

Let z=g/sigma only for analysis. Its coordinates are independent, centred, variance one, and uniformly bounded by sqrt(3), so their subgaussian norm is bounded by an absolute constant.

For a fixed complex orthogonal central projector P with rank n=m r, real z obeys

    z*Pz = z^T Re(P) z.

The real symmetric matrix Re(P) is PSD with operator norm at most one, trace n, and Frobenius norm squared at most n. Apply Hanson-Wright to the q-fold block diagonal matrix diag(Re(P),...,Re(P)). Its operator norm is at most one, Frobenius norm squared at most qn, and expected quadratic form qn. For 0<epsilon<1 this yields, for a universal c>0,

    P(|w/(sigma^2 qn)-1|>epsilon)
       <= 2 exp(-c epsilon^2 qn).

For unresolved blocks on the maximal-rank event, n=m r>=q r^2>=q, so the exponent is at least c epsilon^2 q^2. A union bound over b fixed canonical blocks gives total concentration failure at most

    2b exp(-c epsilon^2 q^2).

Thus q>=C sqrt(log(4b/delta))/epsilon for a suitable universal C gives concentration failure at most delta/2. Combining with rank failure gives the full probability guarantee at least 1-delta. Unlike the Gaussian proof, this statement does not claim an explicit small numerical constant from the generic Hanson-Wright theorem.

Primary source for the concentration inequality: Rudelson and Vershynin, *Hanson-Wright inequality and sub-gaussian concentration*, Electronic Communications in Probability 18(82), 2013; https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2872 ; https://www.math.uci.edu/~rvershyn/papers/rv-Hanson-Wright.pdf .

## 6. Data-dependence checks

* Do not condition Hanson-Wright on successful ranks; use the intersection of two unconditional events and a union bound.
* The learned compressed central projector is the restriction of a fixed ambient projector. Computing it from seed-dependent coordinates does not change the statistic c*GPc=||P^ambient g||^2.
* A separating Z may be randomized after acquisition. Its eigenvalues and labels can depend on the acquired coordinates, but its primitive central projectors still select the same fixed canonical ambient blocks, up to permutation.
* For the cleanest fixed-q theorem use a known bound B>=b (B=N always works). If a pilot learns b and then determines q, account for pilot rank failure. On the good event the selected q equals a deterministic function of the fixed true b; comparison with the corresponding fixed-q experiment avoids an invalid conditional-independence claim.
* The Gaussian explicit constant in the earlier audit has been corrected: use exp(-nu epsilon^2/4), not exp(-nu epsilon^2/3), uniformly over 0<epsilon<1.

## 7. Cost and remaining limits

Full algebra basis acquisition from the compressed matrices has additional matrix-product and rank-elimination costs. Centre extraction is another linear system. Computing central projectors, d_alpha, and R_alpha uses algebraic-number operations and root isolation. This is an explicit finite procedure; it is not included in the kD generator-matvec count.

With rational generators of bounded bit length, polynomial word depths and exact linear algebra give finite bit-length bounds. Any claimed polynomial bit-complexity bound should state its input model, oracle return sizes, algebraic-field representation, and requested output precision. The construction avoids the potentially huge full splitting field; it does not make algebraic elimination or high-precision downstream expression evaluation free.

Exact algebraic equality decides whether a mode exists, even if numerically tiny. This repairs continuous randomness and irrational-basis assumptions; it does not repair noisy source access, floating-point rank classification, or unrestricted ill-conditioned rational expressions.
