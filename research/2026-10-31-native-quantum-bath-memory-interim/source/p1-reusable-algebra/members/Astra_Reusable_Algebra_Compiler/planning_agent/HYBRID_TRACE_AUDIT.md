# Independent audit of the hybrid exact/estimated multiplicity theorem

## Audit conclusion

The proposed theorem is valid in the exact matrix-vector / exact-rank / exact-decomposition model. I found no dependency error from learning the central projectors using the same random seeds. Its correctness follows from three facts: joint cyclic multiplicity is min(m,q r), unsaturated multiplicities are exact, and unresolved blocks necessarily have enough Gaussian degrees of freedom for a 1/epsilon seed bound.

The significant remaining qualifications are acquisition/decomposition cost, numerical rank visibility, and comparison with existing matrix-* algebra reduction and Hutch++-type rank-aware trace estimation. This audit does not establish historical novelty or an advantage over the strongest retaining implementation.

## Complete statement

Let a fixed unital complex matrix *-algebra A, generated by k fixed Hermitian N-by-N matrices, act as

    A ≅ direct_sum_(alpha=1)^b (M_(r_alpha)(C) tensor I_(m_alpha)).

Let d=sum r_alpha^2. Draw q independent standard complex Gaussian vectors g_1,...,g_q in C^N, with E|g_(j,l)|^2=1. Acquire

    K_q = sum_(j=1)^q A g_j.

Let its exact compressed algebra be decomposed into inequivalent simple factors, with irrep dimension r_alpha and represented multiplicity s_alpha. Almost surely,

    s_alpha = min(m_alpha, q r_alpha),
    D_q = dim K_q = sum r_alpha s_alpha <= min(N,q d).

The compressed algebra is faithful almost surely.

Define a block as resolved if s_alpha<q r_alpha, and unresolved otherwise. For a resolved block set mhat_alpha=s_alpha. For an unresolved block set

    w_alpha = sum_(j=1)^q ||P_alpha g_j||^2,
    mhat_alpha = w_alpha/(q r_alpha),

where P_alpha is the canonical ambient isotypic central projector. Its restriction is learned by the compressed decomposition, so w_alpha is computable from the seed coordinates in K_q.

For X=direct_sum (X_alpha tensor I_(m_alpha)) in A, define

    T_hat(X) = sum_alpha mhat_alpha tr(X_alpha).

For 0<epsilon<1, 0<delta<1, choose

    q >= ceil( 2 sqrt(log(2b/delta)) / epsilon ).

Then, with probability at least 1-delta, simultaneously for every PSD X in A,

    (1-epsilon) Tr(X) <= T_hat(X) <= (1+epsilon) Tr(X).

The event is uniform, so X may be selected adaptively using every seed, all learned coordinates, previous estimates, and arbitrarily many earlier computations. The algebra itself must be fixed before the seeds, as in the faithful-cyclic theorem.

## Proof of cyclic multiplicity

For a fixed alpha, reshape g_j's component as G_(alpha,j) in C^(r_alpha x m_alpha). The cyclic subspace for g_j consists of matrices X G_(alpha,j). The joint cyclic subspace is the set of matrices whose rows lie in the span of the rows of all q matrices G_(alpha,j). A qr-by-m Gaussian matrix has rank min(qr,m) almost surely. Therefore s=min(m,qr), and its carrier dimension is r s. The central projections separate the blocks, so their dimensions add.

## Proof of exact inference and the concentration rate

If s<qr, then min(m,qr)<qr forces m=s. Thus exact multiplicity inference is logically valid without a guessed upper bound on m.

If s=qr, then m>=qr. Unitary invariance of the standard complex Gaussian shows that

    w_alpha ~ Gamma(nu_alpha,1),
    nu_alpha = q m_alpha r_alpha >= q^2 r_alpha^2 >= q^2.

For Y~Gamma(nu,1) and 0<epsilon<1, Chernoff bounds give

    P(Y >= (1+epsilon)nu)
      <= exp[-nu(epsilon-log(1+epsilon))]
      <= exp[-nu epsilon^2/4],

    P(Y <= (1-epsilon)nu)
      <= exp[-nu(-epsilon-log(1-epsilon))]
      <= exp[-nu epsilon^2/2].

Consequently

    P(|mhat/m-1|>epsilon) <= 2 exp(-q^2 epsilon^2/4).

A union bound over at most b unresolved blocks gives failure probability <=2b exp(-q^2 epsilon^2/4)<=delta. Resolved blocks have no estimation error.

**Correction preserved:** an earlier audit draft used epsilon^2/3 for the upper Gamma tail. That inequality fails near epsilon=1: 1-log(2)<1/3. The safe epsilon^2/4 bound above is valid throughout 0<epsilon<1 and supersedes the earlier constant.

On the resulting event, every PSD X has tr(X_alpha)>=0. Therefore

    |T_hat(X)-Tr(X)|
      <= sum_unresolved |mhat_alpha-m_alpha| tr(X_alpha)
      <= epsilon Tr(X).

This deterministic last step supplies all-future adaptivity without a union bound over future tasks.

For a Hermitian X with indefinite sign, the same event gives the additive bound

    |T_hat(X)-Tr(X)| <= epsilon Tr(|X|).

It does not give relative error to |Tr(X)| when positive and negative contributions cancel.

## Why same-seed learned projectors do not invalidate concentration

Each ambient isotypic central projector P_alpha is determined by the fixed algebra, independently of all seeds. The acquired projector is merely its restriction to K_q. Because every g_j lies in K_q,

    ||Pbar_alpha Q* g_j|| = ||P_alpha g_j||.

The learned basis and labels may depend on the seed, but the numerical statistic is exactly the fixed-projector quadratic statistic. Labels may be permuted; a union event over all canonical blocks is permutation-invariant. The classification s<qr versus s=qr is also deterministic given the fixed (m,r,q), outside the zero-probability rank-deficient event.

## Unknown number of blocks and adaptive q

The formula can use a known upper bound B>=b, for example B=N, with log(2B/delta). Alternatively, one exact Gaussian pilot cyclic compression already exposes all simple factors almost surely and therefore reveals b. Computing q from that correctly recovered b equals computing it from a deterministic property of the fixed algebra; this by itself does not introduce data-dependent optional stopping.

Choosing q from observed fluctuation sizes, stopping when empirical confidence intervals look short, or refining different blocks adaptively needs an anytime confidence argument or a fresh independent validation batch. The fixed-q proof does not silently justify those procedures.

## Acquisition and computation costs that must remain explicit

Queue-based joint cyclic closure takes kD_q matrix-vector calls once an independent basis of the q seeds has been formed. Its conservative maintained-factorization arithmetic cost is O(k N D_q^2 + k D_q^3), plus generator service cost and O(Nq) seed generation/read cost. It stores O(ND_q+kD_q^2) numbers. These are exact-arithmetic counts.

The hybrid estimator additionally requires extraction of simple-factor dimensions and represented multiplicities. A full Wedderburn decomposition with equivalent irreducible copies grouped is one sufficient route, but is **not necessary**. The later centre-only refinement in CENTRE_FINITE_BIT_AUDIT.md computes a full compressed algebra basis, its centre, separating central projectors P_alpha, d_alpha=dim span(P_alpha E_l)=r_alpha^2, and rank(P_alpha)=r_alpha s_alpha. This recovers the required dimensions and one-copy traces without any irreducible basis. Its matrix products, linear systems, exact algebraic ranks, and root isolation are additional to closure cost. A collection of arbitrary finest irreducible blocks without equivalence grouping would not by itself identify s_alpha.

After acquisition, evaluation of a future expression still incurs its circuit length, small-matrix arithmetic, inversions, matrix exponentials, and conditioning costs. The oracle guarantee does not accelerate an exponentially long input expression or make a nonconvex coefficient optimization easy.

The exact rank test s<qr is the key information-bearing step. Near-rank-deficiency is not automatically distinguishable with finite precision. The theorem therefore needs either exact-field input or a separately earned quantitative numerical condition before operational performance claims are made.

## Safe downstream implications

* **Adaptive partition functions:** for every Hermitian H in the fixed algebra, including H chosen after acquisition, exp(H) is PSD and its trace is estimated with relative error epsilon. The log partition function error is at most -log(1-epsilon).
* **Positive trace objectives on a fixed feasible domain:** if feasibility is defined by exact affine matrix-pencil PSD constraints in the algebra, faithful compression preserves feasibility. If the objective X(c) is PSD, the optimal value of T_hat(X(c)) is within the same relative factors of the optimal value of Tr(X(c)), for either minimization or maximization when the values are finite and nonnegative.
* **Indefinite objectives:** only an additive trace-norm error is automatic.
* **Trace equality constraints:** constraints Tr(B_i X)=b_i are not preserved exactly with estimated multiplicities. A general SDP with these constraints needs robust feasibility margins, exact trace recovery, or a separate perturbation analysis.

## Strong retained baseline and novelty assessment

Hutch++ already obtains 1/epsilon matrix-vector complexity for one PSD trace by computing a low-rank component exactly and estimating a residual. Primary source: Meyer, Musco, Musco, and Woodruff, *Hutch++: Optimal Stochastic Trace Estimation*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09649 and https://www.chrismusco.com/hutchplusplus.pdf . Therefore the seed exponent alone is not a new capability.

The candidate's distinction is that one acquired fixed-algebra representation supports a uniform cone-wide trace functional for arbitrarily many adaptive future expressions, while low multiplicities are learned exactly and large multiplicities supply concentration. This should be compared with a retaining matrix-* algebra learner equipped with batched/rank-aware trace estimation of its central projectors, which may reproduce the construction. This audit has not shown an asymptotic separation from that strong comparator.

The algebraic operation implicit in the estimator is tracialization inside each simple factor: directional random-state fluctuations are replaced by the block's scalar estimated multiplicity. Faithful spectral compression and learning blocks from generators are established; the scope for a fresh result is the fully charged uniform adaptive acquisition theorem and any consequence that established retained methods do not already achieve.
