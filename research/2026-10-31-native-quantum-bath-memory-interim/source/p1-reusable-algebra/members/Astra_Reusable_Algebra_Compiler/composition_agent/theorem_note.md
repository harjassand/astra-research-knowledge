# Cyclic acquisition of a faithful finite operator algebra

This is a mathematical mechanism and a rigorous cost accounting, not a claim of novelty over matrix-algebra algorithms, invariant-subspace methods, or simultaneous block diagonalization. The implementation in `cyclic_algebra.py` is only a floating-point proof of mechanism.

## 1. Access model and the acquired object

Let A_1,...,A_s be Hermitian N-by-N matrices. The permitted expensive primitive is an exact matrix-vector product v -> A_i v; its actual cost is charged. Let A be the unital complex *-algebra generated by the A_i. In the finite-dimensional structure theorem there is an unknown unitary U such that

    U* A U = direct_sum_alpha (I_{m_alpha} tensor M_{r_alpha}(C)),
    N = sum_alpha m_alpha r_alpha,
    d = dim_C A = sum_alpha r_alpha^2.

The learner is not given U, r_alpha, m_alpha, d, a multiplication table, or an invariant subspace. It forms the cyclic subspace K_g = span{w(A)g : w a word, including the empty word}. Its output is compressed generators B_i on K_g, together with the inherited inner product if a nonorthonormal exact basis was used.

The resulting representation supports future noncommutative programs using addition, scalar multiplication, multiplication, adjoints, and inverses. It supports spectra as sets, operator norms, and positivity. It does NOT automatically preserve eigenvalue multiplicities, traces, determinants, arbitrary N-vector outputs, or arbitrary new bilinear forms.

## 2. Exact cyclic theorem

**Theorem.** Let g be a standard circular complex Gaussian vector, and let Q have orthonormal columns spanning K_g. With probability one:

1. R := dim K_g = sum_alpha r_alpha min(m_alpha,r_alpha) <= min(N,d).
2. The map pi(X)=Q*XQ from A to M_R(C) is an injective, unital *-homomorphism.
3. For every noncommutative rational expression F that is defined at A_1,...,A_s, F(B_1,...,B_s)=pi(F(A_1,...,A_s)), where B_i=Q*A_iQ. Conversely, every intermediate inverse is defined on one side exactly when it is defined on the other.
4. For every such X=F(A), the spectral sets and singular-value sets agree with those of pi(X). Consequently ||X||_2=||pi(X)||_2, and X is Hermitian positive semidefinite exactly when pi(X) is.

All these conclusions hold simultaneously for future expressions, including ones selected adaptively after seeing the learned representation. No union bound over future tasks is involved.

**Proof.** Write the alpha component of U*g as the vectorization of an r_alpha-by-m_alpha matrix G_alpha. In that component, the cyclic space is {vec(XG_alpha): X in M_{r_alpha}(C)}. If t_alpha=rank G_alpha, its dimension is r_alpha t_alpha, and the restriction of the algebra to it is unitarily equivalent to t_alpha copies of its r_alpha-dimensional defining representation. Independent Gaussian entries give t_alpha=min(r_alpha,m_alpha) almost surely. Every t_alpha is positive, so no simple component is missing; the restriction is faithful. K_g is invariant under each Hermitian generator, hence reducing, and restriction is therefore a unital *-homomorphism. Each original block X_alpha occurs m_alpha times, while its restriction occurs t_alpha times. Spectral and singular-value sets, positivity, and operator norms are therefore identical. Invertibility is identical, and restrictions preserve inverses; induction over the expression DAG proves the rational statement. QED.

The argument also works with real Gaussian probes for real symmetric generators. Circular complex Gaussian probes are preferable for the unitary-invariant Wishart trace statement below. A real-vector oracle can execute a complex probe using two real products.

## 3. Acquisition algorithm and full arithmetic costs

Start with a nonzero g and maintain an independent column list V. For every column v_j that enters V, query A_i v_j for every i. Test whether that image lies in the current span. If it does not, append it. If it does, retain its coordinate relation. Continue until every appended column has been processed for every generator. Existing relations remain valid when the basis grows: pad their coordinates with zeros.

The algorithm does not assume d in advance. If it terminates with R columns, it has made exactly sR matrix-vector products. Of these candidate images, precisely R-1 enlarged the initial one-dimensional span; the other sR-(R-1) calls were dependent images, including all closure checks. There is no uncharged separate closure oracle. All generators mapping all basis columns into the span certify invariance exactly.

With standard incremental elimination or reorthogonalization, additional arithmetic is O(s N R^2), and working storage is O(NR+sR^2) if image coordinates are recorded as they arrive. If all raw images are instead kept, storage becomes O(sNR). The expensive primitive cost is exactly sum_{i,j} cost(A_i v_j); a uniform bound C_mv gives O(sR C_mv+s N R^2). These costs include reading and writing N-vector data: there is no claim of poly(log N) acquisition.

After learning, Q can be discarded for spectral-only tasks. Stored data are s matrices of size R-by-R, costing O(sR^2) scalars. Retain starting coordinates if fixed-probe quadratic readouts are desired. A future circuit with L dense multiplication/inversion nodes costs O(L R^3) arithmetic by ordinary dense algorithms, plus its scalar input/output cost. Eigenvalues, logarithms, and other analytic evaluations require their ordinary precision-dependent numerical work; exact transcendental answers are not claimed at unit cost. Powers represented by repeated-squaring DAGs are evaluated in the DAG size, but output bit length and conditioning must still be charged.

### Rational exact arithmetic

For rational symmetric input matrices, do not pretend to sample exact real Gaussians. Instead choose each g_j independently from a finite integer set S of size M. Fix an R_max-by-R_max nonzero minor of the matrix of a spanning list of vectors w(A)g, where R_max=sum r_alpha min(m_alpha,r_alpha). This determinant is a nonzero polynomial of degree R_max in the coordinates of g. Schwartz-Zippel gives probability at most R_max/M <= N/M that the maximal cyclic dimension is missed. Choosing M >= N/delta gives the theorem's generic dimension and faithfulness with probability at least 1-delta. A weaker faithfulness-only bound is k/M, where k is the number of simple blocks: missing a block imposes at least one nonzero linear equation on g.

Keep the raw word-vector columns V rational and use exact elimination. Orthogonalization with square roots is unnecessary. The rational compressed matrices are uniquely defined by A_i V=V B_i. Keep H=V*V. They satisfy B_i*H=HB_i; the correct involution on the compressed algebra is X^sharp=H^{-1}X*H. Operator norms are measured in H, and positivity of a compressed H-self-adjoint B is tested by HB >= 0. Equivalently, orthonormalize using an algebraic square root of H if desired. Using the ordinary Euclidean norm of nonorthogonal B would be an error.

This is an arithmetic-operation result, not an unconditional bit-complexity shortcut. If B_R bounds the entry bit lengths of all queried word vectors through length R, pivot determinants and relation coefficients have bit lengths O(R(B_R+log R)) by determinant bounds. Fraction-free elimination gives a polynomial bit implementation at those lengths. The matrix-vector oracle's bit costs must be included at the actual growing vector precision. For explicitly supplied rational matrices, word length is at most R-1 for basis columns and at most R for closure images, so B_R and total intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in R, N, and the input encoding size. A numerical threshold test is not this exact implementation.

## 4. Trace theorem: uniform, and separate from faithfulness

For q independent standard Gaussian probes g_1,...,g_q define tau_hat(X)=(1/q) sum_l g_l*Xg_l. In the hidden decomposition, reshape probe components into r_alpha-by-m_alpha matrices G_{alpha,l}. Then

    tau_hat(X) = sum_alpha tr(X_alpha W_alpha),
    W_alpha = (1/q) sum_l G_{alpha,l}G_{alpha,l}*,
    tr(X) = sum_alpha m_alpha tr(X_alpha).

If (1-epsilon)m_alpha I <= W_alpha <= (1+epsilon)m_alpha I for every alpha, then simultaneously for every positive X in A,

    (1-epsilon) tr X <= tau_hat(X) <= (1+epsilon) tr X.

For every Hermitian X in A, |tau_hat(X)-tr X| <= epsilon tr|X|. The same absolute inequality holds for general X by operator/nuclear-norm duality. This is uniform over the entire algebra, so adaptive future program choice is allowed.

To make the probability explicit, put t=sqrt(2 log(2k/delta)). The usual Gaussian singular-value tail bounds imply, simultaneously for every alpha, that W_alpha/m_alpha has eigenvalues between (1-a_alpha)_+^2 and (1+a_alpha)^2, where

    a_alpha = (sqrt(r_alpha)+t)/sqrt(q m_alpha),

with probability at least 1-delta. A convenient sufficient condition for 0<epsilon<=1 is

    q m_alpha >= 9 (sqrt(r_alpha)+sqrt(2 log(2k/delta)))^2 / epsilon^2

for every alpha. These bounds use real or complex Gaussian singular-value concentration with conservative constants. The event directly yields the uniform Loewner inequalities; no covering of future programs is needed.

For q=1 and large multiplicities, one retained probe computes a uniformly valid trace functional after cyclic compilation. For q>1, closing all probes under the generators gives

    R_q = sum_alpha r_alpha min(m_alpha, q r_alpha) <= min(N,q d)

almost surely. With compressed starting-coordinate matrix Z=Q*[g_1 ... g_q], every future trace estimate is tr(Z*F(B)Z)/q. Costs of all q probes and the larger closure must be charged. Alternatively, one can learn a faithful algebra once and acquire a trace functional separately; that requires additional implementation and accounting.

For positive definite X with spectrum in [a,b], log(X) is generally not positive. The safe conclusion is additive:

    |tau_hat(log X)-log det X| <= epsilon tr|log X|
      <= epsilon N max(|log a|,|log b|).

This controls normalized free energy. It does not imply a small relative error in det X.

## 5. Exact traces in the single-simple-block case

If A is simple, A is unitarily equivalent to I_m tensor M_r(C). Its normalized trace is unique. The cyclic restriction contains t=min(m,r) equivalent copies, so

    tr_N F(A) / N = tr_R F(B) / R

exactly, with R=rt. In particular, for a positive definite F(A),

    log det_N F(A) = (N/R) log det_R F(B).

This requires verifying that the learned faithful algebra has one simple block (equivalently, a one-dimensional center), and using faithfulness. It is false for a general direct sum unless the relative original multiplicities are known or learned. A generic probe identifies which blocks exist, not their original multiplicities.

## 6. Full-rank noncommuting example and numerical check

Choose two generic symmetric 3-by-3 matrices C_1,C_2, normalized to operator norm one, and a random 120-by-120 orthogonal U. Set A_i=U(I_40 tensor C_i)U^T. The generators and most positive combinations have full rank; they do not commute. Their algebra is generically a hidden copy of M_3(R), of dimension nine. There is no nine-dimensional output range approximating these full-rank operators on all inputs.

The supplied demonstration found cyclic dimension nine with exactly 18 matrix-vector products. Its maximum closure residual was 3.802e-14. For 17 previously unqueried theta in [-0.8,0.8], it evaluated normalized log determinants of

    I+A_1^2+A_2^2+theta(A_1A_2+A_2A_1)

using only the nine-dimensional compressed operators. The maximum difference from dense 120-dimensional evaluation was 5.551e-16. The norm of the unseen noncommuting program (A_1 A_2)^12 agreed to 8.160e-15. This is an illustration, not a benchmark, a robustness theorem, or a numerical certificate.

## 7. Necessary failures and limitations

* Generic dense Hermitian generators usually generate all of M_N: d=N^2 and R=N. No compression follows from the theorem.
* Repeated algebraic structure is a promise of usefulness, not a claim about generic scientific problems. The algorithm can discover that no useful small closure exists, but its cost then grows accordingly.
* A deterministic starting vector can miss an entire block. A random finite probe has an explicit nonzero failure probability. Exact closure of the observed space alone does not certify that the representation is faithful without this randomization argument or another witness.
* Non-Hermitian generators without adjoint access invalidate the reducing-subspace argument. The repair is to include adjoints among the generators and charge their products; if adjoints are unavailable, this theorem does not apply.
* Approximate closure does not justify unlimited compositional extrapolation. If ||(I-QQ*)A_iQ||<=eta and all generators are contractions, the action error for a word of length L is at most L eta ||g||. A short repeated-squaring DAG may represent exponentially large L. Resolvents require explicit spectral gaps; long-time dynamics require explicit horizon-dependent error control.
* Example: A=[[1-eta/2,eta/2],[eta/2,1-eta/2]], Q=e_1. The closure residual is eta/2, while at T approximately 2/eta, e_1^T A^T e_1 approximately (1+exp(-2))/2 and (Q^TAQ)^T approximately exp(-1), differing by approximately 0.20. An arbitrarily small residual therefore permits an order-one error at sufficiently long horizons.
* For arbitrary new input vectors, reconstructing original-coordinate action requires further information and at least the output cost. A small faithful algebra does not identify the dense hidden U or grant cheap arbitrary N-vector actions.

## 8. Competitive status

The mechanism is an ordinary randomized invariant-subspace/algebra compiler. It is not a new physical resource, not a new fundamental class of learnability, and not established as superior to standard matrix-algebra software. Prior art includes Murota--Kanno--Kojima--Kojima (2010), Maehara--Murota's error-controlled block diagonalization, and Zhang--Motter (SIAM Review 2020), whose algorithm explicitly explores common invariant subspaces under matrix multiplication. Weighted-automata/Hankel realization is also a direct competitor when only one scalar observable is needed.

The sharper question worth testing is whether matrix-free acquisition of a faithful computational algebra plus a certified trace functional produces an end-to-end break-even improvement for many varied noncommutative spectral programs. The theorem supplies a valid mechanism and clear falsifiers; novelty and practical advantage require a literature comparison and real workloads.
