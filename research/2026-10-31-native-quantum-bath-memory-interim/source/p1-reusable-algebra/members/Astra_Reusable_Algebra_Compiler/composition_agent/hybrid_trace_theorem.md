# Hybrid exact-and-estimated multiplicities

This supplements `theorem_note.md`. The mechanism was strengthened jointly with the parent agent. It is a theorem about a classical matrix-algebra compiler, not a novelty claim.

## Statement

Let A be the unital complex *-algebra generated by Hermitian N-by-N operators. Write its unknown representation as direct_sum_alpha I_{m_alpha} tensor M_{r_alpha}(C), with b simple components and d=sum r_alpha^2. Draw q independent circular complex Gaussian probes, E[g g*]=I. Close their joint span under all generators. Almost surely, the compressed representation is faithful and its alpha component has multiplicity

    s_alpha = min(m_alpha, q r_alpha).

Infer the center and its primitive projectors P_alpha from the compressed generators. If R_alpha=rank P_alpha and d_alpha=dim(P_alpha A), then

    r_alpha=sqrt(d_alpha),    s_alpha=R_alpha/r_alpha.

No alignment of the individual equivalent irreducible copies is needed. Let Z=Q*[g_1 ... g_q] be the compressed probe coordinates and w_alpha=||P_alpha Z||_F^2. Set

    mhat_alpha = s_alpha                       if s_alpha < q r_alpha,
                 w_alpha/(q r_alpha)           otherwise.

The first branch is exact: an unsaturated multiplicity cannot have been truncated. For every future positive X in the algebra, estimate

    T_hat(X) = sum_alpha (mhat_alpha/s_alpha) tr(P_alpha pi(X)).

With probability at least 1-delta, simultaneously for all positive X,

    (1-epsilon) tr X <= T_hat(X) <= (1+epsilon) tr X,

provided 0<epsilon<=1 and

    q >= 2 sqrt(log(2b/delta)) / epsilon

for circular complex Gaussians. There is no assumption on the unknown multiplicities. The same event implies |T_hat(X)-tr X|<=epsilon tr|X| for every Hermitian X. The compiler's exact spectral/norm/positivity guarantee is independent of this probabilistic trace guarantee.

## Ordinary proof of the statistical part

The original central projection E_alpha is fixed by the target algebra, even though its representation P_alpha is discovered using the probes. Reducing invariance and the inclusion of all probes in the cyclic space give

    w_alpha = sum_l ||E_alpha g_l||^2.

Thus the data-dependent-looking compressed formula is exactly a quadratic form in a fixed original projector. For circular complex Gaussians, w_alpha has a Gamma distribution with shape nu_alpha=q m_alpha r_alpha and scale one. The standard moment generating function is (1-t)^(-nu_alpha). Chernoff's bound gives

    P(w >= (1+epsilon)nu) <= exp[-nu(epsilon-log(1+epsilon))],
    P(w <= (1-epsilon)nu) <= exp[-nu(-epsilon-log(1-epsilon))].

For epsilon in (0,1], epsilon-log(1+epsilon)>=epsilon^2/4, and -epsilon-log(1-epsilon)>=epsilon^2/2. Hence

    P(|w/nu-1|>epsilon) <= 2 exp(-nu epsilon^2/4).

On every unresolved component, m_alpha>=q r_alpha, so nu_alpha>=q^2 r_alpha^2>=q^2. A union bound over at most b components gives the stated choice of q. The resolved components have zero multiplicity error. For a positive X, all numbers tr X_alpha are nonnegative, so componentwise multiplicative accuracy implies the uniform positive-cone guarantee by summation. For Hermitian X, split its positive and negative parts within the algebra.

Do not replace the upper-tail constant 4 by 3 over all epsilon<=1: epsilon-log(1+epsilon)>=epsilon^2/3 is false near epsilon=1.

For real Gaussians in a real orthogonal direct sum of full real matrix algebras, w is chi-square with nu degrees of freedom, equivalently Gamma(nu/2,2). The conservative explicit exponent is -nu epsilon^2/8, so q>=sqrt(8 log(2b/delta))/epsilon suffices. The numerical demonstration uses this real convention. The asymptotic q order is unchanged.

## Why tracialization matters

The raw probe functional is generally not tracial: it need not assign equal values to XY and YX. Averaging it over unitary conjugations inside the learned algebra replaces each block's empirical covariance by its scalar average, retaining only its total mass w_alpha. This produces the positive tracial functional

    X -> sum_alpha [w_alpha/(q r_alpha)] tr X_alpha.

For Gaussian probes this is the conditional expectation of the raw trace estimator given the block norms, and is a conventional variance-reduction step. The additional unsaturated-block rule replaces certain random masses by exactly inferred multiplicities. Both operations are implemented using the learned algebra and center; no integration or repeated target queries are necessary after compilation.

## Dimension constraint and integer improvements

The known ambient dimension supplies sum_alpha m_alpha r_alpha=N. If exactly one block remains unresolved after exact inference of the others, this determines the remaining multiplicity exactly. More generally, confidence intervals can be intersected with this equality, integrality, and the lower bounds m_alpha>=q r_alpha on unresolved blocks. This improvement must be credited explicitly; a demonstration with only one unresolved block should not present its remaining stochastic error as unavoidable.

## Finite random bits and exact rational arithmetic

For real rational input operators, choose each raw probe coordinate independently and uniformly from {-M,...,M}. Its exact variance is sigma^2=M(M+1)/3. Keep the integer probes and use

    mhat_alpha = w_alpha/(q sigma^2 r_alpha)

on unresolved components. No irrational normalization is needed in stored data or the formula.

Let R_q=sum r_alpha min(m_alpha,q r_alpha). Fix a nonzero R_q-by-R_q minor of a joint word-probe matrix for generic probes. Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial of total degree R_q in all Nq integer coordinates. Schwartz-Zippel gives generic-rank failure probability at most R_q/(2M+1)<=N/(2M+1). Therefore the exact multiplicity inference remains valid except with this explicitly bounded probability.

The normalized coordinate h/sigma is centered, isotropic, bounded in absolute value by sqrt(3), and hence has universally bounded subgaussian norm. Apply Hanson-Wright to the block-diagonal projector diag(E_alpha,...,E_alpha) across q probes. Its operator norm is one and squared Frobenius norm is q m_alpha r_alpha. For epsilon<=1 the resulting tail is

    P(|w_alpha/(sigma^2 q m_alpha r_alpha)-1|>epsilon)
       <= 2 exp[-c epsilon^2 q m_alpha r_alpha]

for an absolute constant c>0. Consequently q>=C sqrt(log(2b/delta))/epsilon suffices for the same uniform trace guarantee, with unspecified absolute C. The total failure probability is the sum of the finite-grid rank failure and the concentration failure. Do not condition the Hanson-Wright tail on successful closure.

For complex central projectors with real integer probes, h*Eh=h^T Re(E)h; Re(E) is real positive semidefinite with operator norm at most one, trace equal to rank(E), and squared Frobenius norm at most rank(E), so the same bound applies. Alternatively, use independent integer real and imaginary parts and account for their doubled variance.

This separates three distinct claims: an exact finite-bit rational algebra algorithm, a mathematical Gaussian statement with explicit constants, and a floating-point implementation that requires a numerical rank policy. The first does not need exact Gaussian samples, and the third is not automatically certified by either of the first two.
