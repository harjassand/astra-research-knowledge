ACQUIRING REUSABLE SPECTRAL COMPUTATION AND ITS TRACE WEIGHTS
Astra learning/computation branch — 31 October 2026

STATUS

This is an ordinary constructive research note. The main exact-arithmetic
construction has received complementary mathematical reviews. A finite-grid
extension, a centre-only reconstruction, and a cumulative admission construction
are given separately so their interfaces and costs remain visible. Exact rational
and floating-point programs accompany the note. Floating-point experiments do
not certify exact rank, robustness, novelty, or a practical speed advantage.

The central candidate contribution is the combined acquisition theorem:
unknown matrix-algebra acquisition, exact recovery of exhausted multiplicities,
tracial averaging of the remaining multiplicities, and one confidence event for
the entire positive cone of all future expressions. The algebraic ingredients,
Krylov closure, symmetry reduction, and inverse-epsilon single-trace estimation
are established mathematics. Historical novelty of this complete combination
and superiority over a strong retaining algebra learner remain unestablished.

1. SCIENTIFIC QUESTION AND CAPABILITY

Can a computational system acquire operations that support many later, previously
unspecified numerical calculations, instead of relearning each answer? The target
chosen here is a reusable spectral and trace service for a fixed family of large,
possibly dense, full-rank, noncommuting operators. Later questions can contain
new coefficients, sums, products, adjoints, inverses where defined, spectral
functions, and positive matrix pencils. They may be chosen after seeing the
acquired representation and its random probes.

This is a numerical computation interface: a submitted vector v returns A_j v.
It is stronger than passive observations or the scalar-return experimental
interface in Astra N1515/N1521. No physical mechanism for implementing arbitrary
vector queries is inferred from those sources. Every comparator receives the
same vector interface, and every original action is charged.

The valuable regime is small generated algebra rather than small matrix rank.
For example, A_j=U [direct_sum_alpha (B_j,alpha tensor I_malpha)] U*, with hidden
dense U and small noncommuting blocks B_j,alpha, can have rank N for every j.
Ordinary low-rank output-range compression does not explain its tractability.
Established matrix-algebra methods do; this work uses those methods and extends
the all-future trace contract. No arbitrary planning or NP-hard optimization
oracle is obtained.

2. SOURCE AND COST MODEL

Let A_1,...,A_k be fixed Hermitian matrices in M_N(C). Their entries or hidden
block coordinates are not supplied. The learner knows N and can request exact
A_j v for chosen full vectors v. All matrices are fixed independently of the
random trace-acquisition probes. The unital complex *-algebra they generate is
denoted A.

For proof only, finite-dimensional *-algebra structure gives a unitary form

 A = direct_sum_(alpha=1)^b [M_(r_alpha)(C) tensor I_(m_alpha)],
 N = sum_alpha r_alpha m_alpha,
 d = dim_C A = sum_alpha r_alpha^2.                         (2.1)

The learner receives none of U,b,d,r_alpha,m_alpha or their decomposition.
Different alpha denote inequivalent simple components; equivalent copies are
already grouped into m_alpha. The order of tensor factors is immaterial.

The first theorem uses an ideal exact real/complex model, including exact
rank decisions and real-root operations, and standard circular complex Gaussian
seeds, E|g_i|^2=1. This is not a finite-bit algorithm for arbitrary real oracles.
Section 10 gives a finite-randomness algorithm for rational Hermitian sources
with exact rational vector actions and charged algebraic-number operations.
An ordinary floating-point action oracle is a different model. Exact closure
is not certified by a small numerical residual.

The account separates: source matvecs; reading/writing N-dimensional vectors;
orthogonalization and algebra acquisition; exact/algebraic arithmetic and bit
length; decomposition of the learned centre; stored representation; and later
program evaluation. A seed count is not a total matvec count.

3. ACQUIRE THE JOINT CYCLIC MODULE

Draw q independent standard complex Gaussian vectors g_1,...,g_q. Set

 K_q = span{w(A) g_l : w a finite word, 1<=l<=q}.

An executable acquisition needs no enumeration of all words. Start with an
independent basis of the seed span. For every retained basis direction, apply
each generator once. Append an action if it is outside the current span; retain
its coefficients otherwise. Process every appended direction in the same way.
Stop when all generator actions on the retained basis have been processed.

If R=dim K_q, this uses exactly kR generator matvecs in the version that processes
every retained direction, including all dependent closure checks. It uses at
most N independent directions. With an orthonormal basis Q, store C_j=Q*A_jQ and
c_l=Q*g_l. In rational arithmetic retain original word vectors V, store
G=V*V>0, and solve A_jV=VC_j. Then G C_j=C_j*G; this avoids irrational
normalization. All future adjoints and norms in these coordinates use G.

THEOREM 3.1 (acquired multiplicity). Almost surely,

 s_alpha = min(m_alpha,q r_alpha),
 R = sum_alpha r_alpha s_alpha <= min(N,q d),              (3.1)

and restriction pi:A -> End(K_q) is a faithful unital *-representation with
block form direct_sum_alpha [M_ralpha(C) tensor I_salpha].

PROOF. In block alpha identify each seed with an r_alpha by m_alpha coefficient
matrix. Arbitrary left matrix action produces arbitrary row combinations.
The sum of the row spaces of q seeds is the row space of a q r_alpha by m_alpha
Gaussian matrix, whose rank is min(q r_alpha,m_alpha) almost surely. Since A
contains its canonical central projections, different simple components can
be separated by elements of A; their module contributions form a direct sum.
This proves (3.1). Every component has s_alpha>=1, hence restriction has zero
kernel. Each A_j is Hermitian, so the invariant K_q is reducing and restriction
preserves the adjoint. QED.

COROLLARY 3.2 (exact computational service). On that event, substitution
A_j -> C_j preserves every algebra identity, invertibility, spectrum AS A SET,
operator norm, and positive-semidefinite order. It preserves every well-defined
noncommutative rational expression. The same statement holds for matrices over
A, because the amplified representation is also a faithful *-representation.

PROOF. In the displayed decomposition restriction changes only positive copy
counts m_alpha to s_alpha. The same irreducible matrices occur. The conclusions
follow block by block, including after amplification. QED.

This does not preserve eigenvalue multiplicities or the ambient trace. Nor does
it supply arbitrary ambient solution vectors: returning N coordinates still
costs at least their output size. It supplies the specified operator-algebra
and scalar/matrix-query service.

4. ACQUIRE THE CENTRE WITHOUT SUPPLIED BLOCKS

All following work uses only acquired small matrices. Close their matrix-word
span to get a basis B_1,...,B_d of pi(A). Since pi is faithful, this is the true
abstract algebra dimension. Each accepted basis word is stored as a parent word
followed by one generator; rejected words are charged linear-dependence checks.

Solve the linear commutator equations [Z,C_j]=0 with Z in span{B_i}. Their
solution is the centre Z(pi(A)), of complex dimension b. Construct a self-adjoint
real basis Z_0,...,Z_(b-1); in rational coordinates the adjoint is
Z^dagger=G^-1 Z* G. Hermitian and skew-Hermitian parts give such a basis.

One can choose a separating central element finitely and explicitly. Try

 Z(t)=sum_(j=0)^(b-1) t^j Z_j, t=0,1,2,... .

On any pair of distinct simple components its central values differ by a nonzero
polynomial of degree <=b-1, because the centre basis separates those components.
There are b(b-1)/2 pairs. Hence among the first

 1 + (b-1)b(b-1)/2

integer values some Z(t) has b distinct eigenvalues. Check this by the degree of
its square-free minimal polynomial; self-adjointness makes it diagonalizable.
This finite polynomial-size search is charged. A generic random centre element
is a convenient numerical proposal, but not a substitute for checking it.

Let f be the minimal polynomial of a successful Z. Its degree is b. For each
real root lambda_alpha, compute the projector

 E_alpha = [ f(x)/(x-lambda_alpha) ](Z) / f'(lambda_alpha). (4.1)

In the exact rational case the roots are algebraic over Q, and this calculation
lives separately in Q(i,lambda_alpha). It does not require constructing a common
splitting field of all roots. General sources use the ideal root operations
specified in section 2; their cost is not treated as ordinary field arithmetic.
Compute

 R_alpha = rank E_alpha,
 d_alpha = dim_C span{E_alpha B_i : 1<=i<=d},
 r_alpha = sqrt(d_alpha),  s_alpha=R_alpha/r_alpha.         (4.2)

These are integers because the ideal is M_ralpha and its module has s_alpha
copies. Rank over the exact coefficient field equals complex rank. Thus no
choice or alignment of a single irreducible copy is necessary. A centre-only
decomposition suffices. For X in A,

 tr(X_alpha) = Tr_(K_q)(E_alpha pi(X))/s_alpha.             (4.3)

The E_alpha are learned restrictions of the FIXED ambient canonical central
projectors P_alpha. In particular, because all seeds lie in K_q,

 W_alpha=sum_l ||E_alpha c_l||^2=sum_l ||P_alpha g_l||^2.   (4.4)

If rational coordinates are used, the norm on the left is the G-norm.
No ambient P_alpha needs to be queried or materialized for (4.4).

5. THE MULTIPLICITY CONSTRUCTION

If s_alpha<q r_alpha, equation (3.1) reveals the true multiplicity exactly:

 m_alpha=s_alpha.                                        (5.1)

Equality s_alpha=q r_alpha DOES NOT certify m_alpha=q r_alpha. It means only
m_alpha>=q r_alpha. Preserve equality in the unresolved branch.

For every block define

 mhat_alpha = s_alpha,                  if s_alpha<q r_alpha,
              W_alpha/(q r_alpha),     otherwise.        (5.2)

Store the positive tracial functional

 That(X)=sum_alpha (mhat_alpha/s_alpha)
                        Tr_(K_q)(E_alpha pi(X)).          (5.3)

Equivalently That(X)=sum mhat_alpha tr(X_alpha). This removes the anisotropic
within-block empirical state: it retains only a central weight and uses the
intrinsic matrix trace inside each factor. One may precompute the central
weight matrix sum (mhat_alpha/s_alpha) E_alpha for quick evaluation.

THEOREM 5.1 (one event for all future positive traces). For fixed q>=1,
0<epsilon<1 and the complex Gaussian convention above,

 Pr[ exists X in A, X>=0:
      |That(X)-Tr_N X|>epsilon Tr_N X ]
      <= 2b exp(-epsilon^2 q^2/4).                       (5.4)

Consequently

 q=ceil(2 sqrt(log(2b/delta))/epsilon)                    (5.5)

suffices for failure probability delta. One may use b<=N before learning b.
Alternatively a first seed learns b almost surely; since correct b is a fixed
property of the source, choosing q from it does not select favourable random
trace energies. No unknown multiplicity lower bound is assumed.

PROOF. W_alpha has Gamma(shape nu_alpha=q m_alpha r_alpha,scale1) distribution:
it is the sum of squared magnitudes of q m_alpha r_alpha independent standard
complex Gaussian entries in a fixed unitary block. For W~Gamma(nu,1), Chernoff
optimization using E exp(tW)=(1-t)^(-nu) gives

 Pr[W>=(1+epsilon)nu] <= exp[-nu(epsilon-log(1+epsilon))],
 Pr[W<=(1-epsilon)nu] <= exp[-nu(-epsilon-log(1-epsilon))].

For 0<epsilon<1, the two exponents are respectively at least
nu epsilon^2/4 and nu epsilon^2/2. Thus the two-sided probability is at most
2 exp(-nu epsilon^2/4). The tempting denominator3 in the upper exponent is
not valid throughout this interval; near epsilon=1 it is false. The corrected
constant4 is used throughout the main statement.

Resolved blocks have zero multiplicity error. On every unresolved block,

 nu_alpha=q m_alpha r_alpha >= q^2 r_alpha^2 >=q^2.

Union-bound only over the b fixed simple components. Outside the resulting
event, |mhat_alpha-m_alpha|<=epsilon m_alpha for every alpha. For ANY positive
X, each tr(X_alpha)>=0, hence

 |That(X)-Tr X|
 <=sum |mhat_alpha-m_alpha| tr(X_alpha)
 <=epsilon sum m_alpha tr(X_alpha)=epsilon Tr X.

This deterministic implication holds over the whole positive cone at once.
It includes operators chosen after observing all acquisition data. QED.

The learned coordinates and their labels depend on the probes, but the ambient
P_alpha in (4.4) are fixed. Neither that dependence nor a permutation of block
labels invalidates the probability calculation. The resolved/unresolved status
also depends only on the true dimensions and q outside the null rank event.

For REAL Gaussian probes in real orthogonal sums of real full matrix blocks,
W_alpha is chi-square with q m_alpha r_alpha degrees of freedom. The safe
corresponding constants are 2b exp(-epsilon^2 q^2/8) and
q>=sqrt(8 log(2b/delta))/epsilon. The numerical real fixtures use this convention,
not the circular-complex constant. A general complex algebra with integer-real
probes is covered instead by section10's quadratic-form bound.

6. THE KNOWN AMBIENT DIMENSION AND A RETAINED CONFIDENCE SET

The known identity sum r_alpha m_alpha=N should be used. If b=1, m=N/r is
already EXACT once the algebra factor size is acquired. If all but one m_alpha
are resolved, the last is exact from that identity. The trace challenge is
distribution of ambient dimension among central components.

A useful stronger output is a joint confidence set, allowing task-specific
bounds rather than committing to one estimate. Put n_alpha=r_alpha m_alpha.
For fixed q define D(w,nu)=w-nu-nu log(w/nu), w,nu>0. The same Gamma Chernoff
calculation proves

 Pr[D(W,nu)>t]<=2e^(-t).                                 (6.1)

For observed w>0 the sublevel set D(w,nu)<=t is an interval, since its second
derivative with respect to nu is 1/nu. Its limit as nu decreases to zero is w.
When t>=w the lower endpoint is zero; otherwise invert the lower positive
boundary. Invert the upper positive boundary in either case to get an interval
for n_alpha=nu/q. Intersect with n_alpha>=q r_alpha^2 on unresolved
blocks; use the exact n_alpha on resolved blocks. With t=log(2b/delta), all
true block dimensions belong to their intervals on one event of probability
at least1-delta. Keep the continuous polytope

 C={ n : lower_alpha<=n_alpha<=upper_alpha,
             sum n_alpha=N }.                            (6.2)

Using a continuous relaxation is sound; requiring n_alpha/r_alpha to be integer
can tighten it but is not assumed to have a cheap general optimizer. Empty C
means rejection, not permission to silently alter the confidence set.

For any Hermitian X define a_alpha=Tr(E_alpha pi(X))/R_alpha. Then

 min_(n in C) sum n_alpha a_alpha <=Tr X
      <=max_(n in C) sum n_alpha a_alpha.                 (6.3)

These bounds are valid simultaneously for all future X, including indefinite
ones and adaptively chosen ones. They can be computed in O(b log b): start at
the lower bounds and allocate the remaining mass N-sum lower to capacities
upper-lower in increasing (minimum) or decreasing (maximum) order of slopes.
This is a confidence set, not a Bayesian posterior. Finite numerical inversion
must use outward bounds if a numerical certificate is asserted.

For indefinite X the simpler point estimator satisfies only

 |That(X)-Tr X|<=epsilon Tr|X|.                           (6.4)

Relative error in |Tr X| is false under cancellation. This also qualifies
log-determinant claims: logdet M=Tr log M has an error controlled by
epsilon Tr|log M|, unless log M is positive or further structure is supplied.

Repeated acquisition stages may retain work. For predetermined q_j use a
summable error allocation, for example delta_j=delta/[j(j+1)], j>=1.
The union event permits stopping based on the acquired intervals. A fixed-q
confidence formula cannot simply be reused after energy-dependent stopping.

7. WHAT THE ACQUIRED OBJECT ACTUALLY ENABLES

For every future noncommutative rational program of length L with valid inverses,
evaluate it using the learned C_j. Dense evaluation uses O(LR^3) arithmetic
as a conservative bound, followed by O(bR^2) trace contractions or O(R^3)
spectral work. It uses NO new original generator calls. Bit lengths, function
approximation, inverse conditioning, and requested output accuracy are still
charged. Actual matrix-valued outputs have their ordinary output-size cost.

Optional regular-representation recompression removes an avoidable dependence
on q from the retained dimension. Let B_1,...,B_d be the already acquired algebra
basis, and let e be the coordinate vector of its identity. Compute the matrices
L_j of left multiplication by C_j in that basis. With the physical adjoint
dagger (ordinary adjoint in orthonormal coordinates, or G^(-1)B*G otherwise), put

 H_il = Tr_(K_q)(B_i^dagger B_l),
 f_i = That(B_i).

H is positive definite, since the trace is a faithful positive functional on
the acquired concrete algebra. The identity H L_j=L_j* H follows from
Tr(B_i^dagger C_j B_l)=Tr((C_j B_i)^dagger B_l). The left representation is
faithful because L_X e gives the coefficient vector of X itself. Consequently
the same identities, inverse, spectral and positivity contracts hold in this
d-dimensional model, using metric H. For a future expression X,

 That(X)=f^T L_X e,                                      (7.0)

where the transpose is intentional: the trace functional is complex-linear.
Retain each central normalized trace as a coefficient vector as well if the
joint confidence intervals are wanted. This requires no original matvecs.
Structure constants are obtained during the paid small-algebra closure; forming
the metric costs O(d^2 R^2) in orthonormal coordinates and conservatively
O(d R^3+d^2 R^2) with rational metric coordinates, plus exact bit costs.

Choose the smaller of the R-dimensional cyclic and d-dimensional regular models.
Writing D=min(R,d), ordinary future evaluation is therefore O(L D^3) arithmetic,
with metric conditioning and requested precision still charged. This is the
established regular-representation construction, not a separate novelty claim.

Example: a future design supplies positive semidefinite H_1,...,H_t in A as
programs, and lambda>0. For u in the simplex define

 M(u)=lambda I+sum_i u_i H_i,
 F(u)=Tr M(u)^(-1).

F is a convex A-optimal design objective. Its whole feasible family is handled
by the acquired representation. If ordinary optimization finds uhat with
That(M(uhat)^(-1))<=inf_u That(M(u)^(-1))+xi, then

 F(uhat) <= [(1+epsilon)/(1-epsilon)] inf_u F(u)
                                      +xi/(1-epsilon).   (7.1)

PROOF. Apply (5.4) to every positive inverse and chain the lower bound at uhat,
the optimization inequality, and the upper bound at an optimal or limiting u.
QED. This is objective transfer for a specified convex task, not an efficient
planner for arbitrary learned dynamics. The ordinary optimizer's work and
conditioning remain in its cost.

Affine matrix-pencil positivity is preserved exactly. Trace equality constraints
such as Tr(B_i X)=b_i are NOT exact when multiplicities are estimated; a general
SDP feasibility claim needs exact weights or an explicit robustness argument.

For any Hermitian H in A, exp(H)>=0 gives a simultaneous relative partition-trace
bound and |log That(exp H)-log Tr exp H|<=-log(1-epsilon). Computing the exponential
in the learned representation remains a paid numerical operation. No physical
equilibrium preparation or quantum-memory capability is inferred.

8. COMPLETE ACQUISITION COST

Let R=R_q. Exact joint closure costs kR original matvecs, not just q, and includes
dependent closure calls. A conservative dense arithmetic count, exclusive of
the oracle's own implementation, is

 O(Nq^2 + k N R^2 + k R^3),                              (8.1)

with O(NR+Nq+kR^2) working storage. A dense original action can itself cost N^2.
If R=N, the exact full representation gives the trace directly and there is no
reason to keep a stochastic multiplicity estimate.

The small-matrix algebra basis has d<=R^2 elements. Breadth-first matrix-word
closure uses <=kd matrix candidates, with O(kdR^3+kR^2d^2) arithmetic using
ordinary multiplication and incremental elimination. Centre equations, all
unsuccessful separating-Z trials, minimal-polynomial construction, exact root
isolation, projector evaluation and ranks (4.2) are additional polynomial-size
linear/algebraic computations. Denote their measured or exact bit cost C_center;
do not erase it by calling a decomposition 'available'. The explicit procedure
in section4 constructs it instead of receiving the useful block geometry.

After acquisition the ambient Q/V can be discarded for the specified scalar
service. Retain the k small generators, centre projectors or weight matrix,
trace confidence data, and exact metric if nonorthonormal. Storage is polynomial
in R,k,b, not N; a simple representation uses O((k+b)R^2+Rq) numbers.
With optional section7 recompression, retain O(kd^2+d^2+bd) numbers instead:
regular generators, their positive metric, identity coordinates, and central
trace coefficient vectors. Select the smaller model after paying its construction.

Combining R<=qd and (5.5) gives an upper bound of

 O(k d sqrt(log(2b/delta))/epsilon)

original matvecs, plus all vector, centre, and arithmetic work above. This is a
scoped parameter bound, not sublinear reading of N-dimensional vectors and not
an optimal dependence on d. Generic matrices can generate d=N^2 and force R=N.
There is no useful universal compression guarantee for arbitrary matrices.

9. ACCURACY COST CANNOT DISAPPEAR EVEN AT ALGEBRA DIMENSION TWO

This lower bound is a COROLLARY OF EXISTING PRIMARY WORK, not a new lower-bound
discovery. Swartworth and Woodruff, Optimal Eigenvalue Approximation via Sketching,
arXiv:2304.09281v1, Appendix A Theorem23, show that for large enough a and
N>=C a^2, distinguishing a random real orthogonal projector of rank a from one
of rank a+2 with success3/4 needs at least a/10 adaptive matrix-vector queries.

The unital algebra generated by such a projector P is span{I,P}, dimension2.
A relative-epsilon positive-trace service distinguishes the ranks when

 (1+epsilon)a < (1-epsilon)(a+2),
 equivalently epsilon(a+1)<1.

Taking a=floor(1/(2epsilon)) for small epsilon yields Omega(1/epsilon) original
queries. Complex queries against a real projector can be simulated with two
real queries. Thus the inverse-epsilon dependence is unavoidable in the present
oracle family, even with a two-dimensional algebra. For fixed b and failure
probability the central theorem matches that accuracy dependence. This does
not prove optimal d,k,delta,precision or wall-time dependence.

10. FINITE RANDOMNESS AND EXACT RATIONAL SOURCES

This is a separate corollary, not a claim that floating-point Gaussian scripts
implement exact real arithmetic. Suppose A_j are Hermitian over Q(i) and the
oracle returns exact rational actions. Draw each seed coordinate independently
uniformly from {-M,...,M}; its variance is sigma^2=M(M+1)/3. No square-root
normalization is required. Divide observed energy by q sigma^2 when estimating
block dimension, or by q sigma^2 r_alpha for multiplicity.

The maximum-rank joint word matrix has a nonzero R_q by R_q minor whose entries
are linear in the seed coordinates. Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial of
degree R_q<=N. A real restriction of a nonzero complex polynomial remains a
nonzero polynomial (or one of its real/imaginary parts does). The grid bound
therefore gives failure probability <=N/(2M+1) for the exact rank formula and
faithfulness. Choose the grid large enough to allocate the desired rank budget.

The normalized real coordinates h_i/sigma are independent, mean zero, variance1,
and bounded by sqrt3. For a fixed complex Hermitian projection P, h*Ph equals
h^T Re(P)h; the latter is a real quadratic form with operator norm<=1,
Frobenius norm squared<=rank P, and trace=rank P. Hanson--Wright concentration
applied to q independent copies gives, for a universal c>0 and epsilon<=1,

 Pr[ |W/(q sigma^2)-n_alpha|>epsilon n_alpha ]
       <=2 exp(-c q n_alpha epsilon^2).                  (10.1)

On unresolved blocks n_alpha>=q r_alpha^2. Thus
q=C epsilon^-1 sqrt(log(2b/delta_stat)), for a sufficiently large universal C,
has the same order as the Gaussian seed bound. Union-bound statistical and
rank failures. Do not condition the quadratic-form concentration on successful
rank discovery; both events are controlled unconditionally.

Retain rational word bases and their rational Gram matrices. Section4's centre
construction uses rational linear algebra followed by real algebraic root
isolation and exact calculations in Q(i,lambda_alpha). All of these are finite
operations. The cost includes coefficient heights, extension degrees, exact
rank decisions, and later expression bit growth. The grid seed precision is
O(log(N/delta_rank)) bits per coordinate. The unspecified universal
Hanson--Wright constant is not replaced by an unproved numerical constant in
the supplied executable.

For rational-source future expressions with coefficients in Q(i), each central
calculation can stay in Q(i,lambda_alpha), of degree at most 2b over Q. Additional
algebraic coefficients in future queries have their own field and bit costs;
arbitrary real query constants do not inherit this finite-bit guarantee.

This finite-bit corollary removes the need for infinitely precise random seeds.
It does NOT remove the need for an exact rational source or turn a noisy source
into one. The exact rational program in the package checks acquisition and
intertwining on a small dense conjugated noncommuting model; it is not a
large-scale exact algebraic centre-decomposition implementation.

11. CUMULATIVE ADMISSION OF NEW GENERATORS

The trace theorem is for a fixed source algebra. A new generator chosen after
seeing old probes can be invisible: if u is orthogonal to their joint cyclic
space, the new projector uu* kills every old seed while adding spectrum1.
Therefore arbitrary adaptive enlargement needs fresh challenges and fresh trace
acquisition. This section supplies a separate exact relation-learning mechanism.
Newly admitted generators are Hermitian. More generally, adjoint actions must
also be available, separately admitted and charged, so that the closed operator
algebra is a *-algebra.

Retain an operator-word basis B_1,...,B_D and witness vectors h_1,...,h_l such
that E(B)=(Bh_1,...,Bh_l) is injective on their span. Retain their actual word
programs and evaluated witness columns. For a proposed operator C fixed before
the next fresh challenge, fit unique coefficients c_i to all old witness actions.
If no fit exists, C is truly independent. If a fit exists, form the executable
residual R=C-sum c_iB_i and evaluate R h on one fresh grid vector h.

If R is a nonzero matrix, condition on all but one coordinate of h in a nonzero
row: at most one value of that coordinate makes the row vanish. Therefore

 Pr[R h=0 | all previous history and fixed C] <=1/|grid|. (11.1)

A nonzero residual proves a new operator direction; append C and this witness.
The witness map stays injective because R vanishes on the old witnesses but not
on the new one. If the old fit was inconsistent, the old witness map already
separates the extension. Accepted dependencies have a false-positive probability
controlled by (11.1). Summable fresh-check budgets cover adaptively chosen inputs.

Keep only genuinely new generators as essential generators, and close every
essential-generator/basis-word product once. On the joint correct-relation event,
all independent discoveries strictly grow the actual final algebra. For final
dimension D_* and k_* essential generators, at most k_*D_* product candidates
are tested over the lifetime, plus the T external admission requests. This is
a bound on candidates, NOT on their full-vector execution: evaluating a residual
pays the new operator and every necessary retained word on a fresh witness.
Word programs, witness recomputation, exact coefficient solving and bit growth
are all charged. There are no free relation or equivalence queries.

The positive Gram matrix G_ij=sum_l <B_i h_l,B_j h_l> is nonsingular. Left
multiplication in the acquired algebra, with this metric, is a faithful
*-representation of dimension D_*. It supports the same exact spectral
operations. This combines conventional algebra closure, fresh-vector identity
testing and regular representation; a broad new-learning claim is not made.

For a changed algebra, old trace concentration cannot be carried over merely
because relation admission succeeded. Freeze that enlarged algebra, use fresh
trace probes and allocate an epoch confidence budget. There is no proved bound
here making every arbitrary adaptive enlargement free.

12. PRESERVED FAILURES AND LIMITS

* A small predictive model does not itself make planning tractable. The included
  four-state strictly positive doubly stochastic construction has common uniform
  stationarity and exact known dynamics, yet encodes NP-hard exact OPEN-LOOP
  planning. Its hard signal is exponentially small; it makes no fixed-accuracy
  hardness claim and does not concern observed-state feedback MDP planning.
* One Gaussian quadratic form retains within-block anisotropy. Uniform PSD trace
  reuse requires a Loewner covariance bound if that raw estimator is used. The
  learned tracial functional removes that unnecessary condition.
* Equality s=q r is unresolved. Treating it as exact multiplicity is an error.
* Three-block numerical examples with only one remaining unknown multiplicity
  can be solved exactly by the known N. The main nontrivial numerical comparison
  uses two remaining unknown large components and retains the earlier example.
* Near-invariant numerical vectors can spuriously grow a tiny algebra to the
  full ambient dimension if small roundoff residuals are normalized repeatedly.
  This occurred in the four-block test and is preserved. Batched frontier SVD
  repaired that fixture; it is not a general numerical robustness theorem.
* Conversely, discarding small residuals can hide a genuine operator component.
  Approximate closure does not justify arbitrary rational queries: A=diag(0,eta)
  is close to zero, while x/eta or an inverse near eta separates them at order1.
* Indefinite cancellation, changing generators after calibration, exact trace
  constraints, output-size costs and expensive centre discovery retain the
  qualifications stated above.

cb87e72296824ae2a677ccd337025d02d20cbb0a, starting with 00_START_HERE.txt and
AGENTS.md and browsing by learning/computation capability. Astra Research was
not exposed as a callable tool in this session; current GitHub supplied the
source route. N1515 and N1521 governing proofs were fetched and read. Their
common-stationary scalar experiments, geometric/conditioning bills and ordinary
retaining-learner wins are preserved. Their physical access is not promoted to
the vector interface here. N1130 and N1223 packets supplied additional warnings
about qualified conditioning families and paying all acquisition candidates.

The following competitors receive explicit credit:
  - invariant-subspace spin-up and noncommutative Krylov acquisition;
  - automatic matrix-* algebra decomposition from generators (Murota/Maehara
    and Zhang/Motter), without a useful decomposition supplied;
  - faithful algebra/regular-representation SDP reduction
    (de Klerk/Pasechnik/Schrijver);
  - Hutch++, optimal sketching, XTrace and block trace estimators;
  - retained stochastic Lanczos quadrature, including uniform guarantees over
    bounded analytic function families (Chen/Trogdon/Ubaru);
  - parameter-dependent trace approximation with reused probes and Chebyshev
    interpolation (Matti/He/Kressner/Lam), and simultaneous stochastic trace
    bounds for compact parameter families (Saibaba/Ipsen);
  - the direct random-projector accuracy lower bound of Swartworth/Woodruff.

The inverse-epsilon rate is already known for an individual PSD trace. Spectral
compression and hidden algebra discovery are also known. The potential addition
is the all-future, whole-positive-cone acquisition theorem with unknown
multiplicities resolved by saturation or estimated through the centre, together
with its actual closure/decomposition bill and finite-bit qualification.

A retaining conventional algebra learner equipped with the same central
multiplicity method can implement this construction. No separation from that
competitor has been proved. The numerical comparisons diagnose the mechanism
and its failures; they do not establish an overall speedup over strong methods.

This is a completed scoped computational construction and a meaningful candidate
for further originality/practical analysis. It does not yet warrant a claim of a
civilisation-changing result, a general theory of intelligence, or a new
unrestricted tractability class. The next consequential missing theorem is a
quantitative acquisition guarantee for approximately structured numerical
sources, with stable finite precision and strong retained comparators included.
The exact mechanism above is preserved as a proved resource, not replaced by
that open problem.

References and exact source locators are in ../SOURCES.txt. Executed checks,
numerical results and corrections are retained in the accompanying directories.
