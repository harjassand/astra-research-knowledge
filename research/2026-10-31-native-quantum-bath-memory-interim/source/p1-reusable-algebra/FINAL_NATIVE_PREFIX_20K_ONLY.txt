# Acquiring reusable computation from hidden operator structure

I constructed a mechanism that acquires a reusable spectral and trace computation service from a fixed collection of large, noncommuting operators. Its strongest result is **one confidence guarantee covering every future positive operator expression in the acquired algebra—including expressions chosen after inspecting the learned model.**

The central idea is to combine two ways of learning how often each irreducible component occurs:

- When the acquired subspace exhausts a component’s multiplicity, recover that multiplicity exactly.
- When it does not, the unresolved component necessarily contains enough independent probe coordinates to estimate its multiplicity accurately.

That combination gives an acquisition bound proportional to \(1/\varepsilon\), followed by computation on a retained representation with no further calls to the original operators. A conventional regular representation can then remove an avoidable dependence of the retained dimension on the number of acquisition probes.

**This is a completed, scoped construction with proofs and executable checks. Historical novelty of the combined theorem and superiority over a strong retaining algebra learner remain unestablished. It does not yet justify a civilisation-changing claim.** The consequential unresolved issue is acquiring the same capability reliably when the structure is approximate and observations have finite precision.

## 1. The scientific prize

The capability I targeted is **acquisition of reusable operations for questions that were unspecified during learning**.

For a large computational system, those later questions might involve different combinations of its operators, new coefficients, products in different orders, inverses, spectral functions, or optimization objectives. A useful learner should acquire enough structure that these later computations depend primarily on the system’s compositional complexity, rather than repeatedly returning to its full ambient representation.

The concrete question became:

> Can a learner discover a compact noncommutative operator model, learn the trace information that spectral compression loses, and provide a uniform guarantee for arbitrary later computations within that model?

The trace qualification matters. A compressed representation can preserve spectra and positivity while changing eigenvalue multiplicities. It can therefore answer whether an operator is positive or invertible, yet still give the wrong trace, partition sum, or inverse-trace objective. Learning the operation algebra alone does not settle that problem.

The opening is **small generated algebra**, which can occur even when every source matrix is dense and full rank. For example, hidden coordinates may conceal repeated small matrix components:

\[
A_j
=
U\left[
\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{b}
\left(B_{j,\alpha}\otimes I_{m_\alpha}\right)
\right]U^*.
\]

The matrices \(B_{j,\alpha}\) can be noncommuting. A low-rank approximation of each \(A_j\) need not explain the resulting tractability.

This structural regime is established territory: matrix algebra decomposition already discovers useful block structure without receiving it in advance, and regular representations already support smaller semidefinite computations. Those are ingredients of the construction and strong comparators, rather than discoveries attributed to this branch. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

## 2. The acquired capability and its theorem

Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\) be fixed Hermitian \(N\times N\) matrices. Their generated unital complex \(*\)-algebra has the abstract form

\[
\mathcal A
\simeq
\bigoplus_{\alpha=1}^{b}
\left(M_{r_\alpha}(\mathbb C)\otimes I_{m_\alpha}\right),
\]

with

\[
N=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha m_\alpha,
\qquad
d=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal A=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha^2.
\]

Here \(r_\alpha\) is the size of an irreducible matrix component, and \(m_\alpha\) is its multiplicity in the ambient system.

**The learner receives none of the block coordinates, component sizes, multiplicities, or algebra dimension.**

For \(q\) independent standard circular complex Gaussian probes, the construction acquires a faithful representation of dimension

\[
R_q
=
\sum_\alpha r_\alpha\min(m_\alpha,q r_\alpha)
\leq \min(N,qd).
\]

The specified closure algorithm uses exactly

\[
kR_q
\]

original matrix-vector actions, including dependent closure checks.

It then constructs a positive tracial functional \(\widehat T\) satisfying, for \(0<\varepsilon<1\),

\[
\Pr\!\left[
\exists X\in\mathcal A,\ X\succeq0:
\left|\widehat T(X)-\operatorname{Tr}X\right|
>
\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}X
\right]
\leq
2b\exp\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^2q^2}{4}\right).
\]

Consequently,

\[
q=
\left\lceil
\frac{2\sqrt{\log(2b/\delta)}}{\varepsilon}
\right\rceil
\]

suffices for failure probability at most \(\delta\). Before learning \(b\), one can use \(b\leq N\). Alternatively, an initial Gaussian seed learns the fixed value of \(b\) almost surely.

The resulting original-query upper bound is

\[
O\!\left(
\frac{kd\sqrt{\log(2b/\delta)}}{\varepsilon}
\right),
\]

with acquisition arithmetic, vector handling, centre reconstruction, and precision costs additional.

The retained model provides:

| Later computation within the fixed algebra | Contract |
|---|---|
| Algebra identities and well-defined rational expressions | Preserved exactly in the exact model |
| Spectrum as a set, invertibility, operator norm | Preserved |
| Positivity, including matrix-valued amplification | Preserved |
| Trace of every positive expression | Simultaneous relative error at most \(\varepsilon\) |
| Trace of an indefinite expression | An absolute bound or a retained confidence interval |
| Arbitrary ambient output vectors | Ordinary output-size costs remain |

The guarantee ranges over the entire positive cone. It therefore needs **no additional failure budget for later query selection**, provided the source algebra remains fixed.

## 3. The mechanism

### Acquire the space reached by composing the sources

Starting from probes \(g_1,\ldots,g_q\), form

\[
K_q
=
\operatorname{span}
\{w(A)g_\ell:
w\text{ is a finite operator word},\ 1\leq\ell\leq q\}.
\]

This does not require enumerating all words. Maintain an independent vector basis; apply every generator once to each retained direction; append a result only when it adds an independent direction. Stop after processing all generator actions on all retained directions.

To see what this learns, consider one hidden component \(M_r\otimes I_m\). A probe can be viewed as an \(r\times m\) coefficient matrix. Arbitrary left matrix action spans its row space. With \(q\) probes, the combined row space has dimension

\[
s=\min(m,qr)
\]

almost surely. Thus the acquired component is \(M_r\otimes I_s\).

Every simple component is represented, so restriction to \(K_q\) is faithful. Because the generators are Hermitian, this invariant subspace is reducing: restriction also preserves the adjoint. That gives the spectral and positivity contracts.

This part uses conventional invariant-subspace and finite algebra machinery. Its role here is to provide an explicitly acquired representation and a useful formula for what information about multiplicity survives.

### Learn the centre without receiving the decomposition

After closure, all further structural work uses the acquired matrices.

The construction:

1. Closes their matrix-word span to obtain an algebra basis.
2. Solves the commutation equations to obtain the centre.
3. Finds a selfadjoint central element that separates the simple components.
4. Constructs its central spectral projectors.
5. Infers component sizes from dimensions.

If \(E_\alpha\) is an acquired central projector, then

\[
\dim(E_\alpha\mathcal A)=r_\alpha^2,
\qquad
\operatorname{rank}E_\alpha=r_\alpha s_\alpha.
\]

These identities reveal both \(r_\alpha\) and the retained multiplicity \(s_\alpha\). There is no need to align an individual irreducible copy.

The separating central element is also constructive. For a selfadjoint centre basis \(Z_0,\ldots,Z_{b-1}\), search

\[
Z(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}t^jZ_j.
\]

Only finitely many \(t\) fail to separate two central characters. The proof gives a finite search bound and charges unsuccessful trials, polynomial construction, root operations, and projector ranks. It does not assume that a useful decomposition is supplied.

### Recover a multiplicity exactly or estimate its central weight

The formula

\[
s_\alpha=\min(m_\alpha,qr_\alpha)
\]

creates the decisive split.

If

\[
s_\alpha<qr_\alpha,
\]

then the learner knows

\[
m_\alpha=s_\alpha
\]

exactly.

If instead

\[
s_\alpha=qr_\alpha,
\]

then the multiplicity remains unresolved: only \(m_\alpha\geq qr_\alpha\) is established. Equality must not be treated as exact recovery.

For an unresolved component, retain its total projected probe energy

\[
W_\alpha
=
\sum_{\ell=1}^q
\|E_\alpha c_\ell\|^2,
\]

where \(c_\ell\) is the probe in acquired coordinates. Although those coordinates were learned from the probes, this quantity equals

\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^q\|P_\alpha g_\ell\|^2
\]

for the fixed ambient central projector \(P_\alpha\).

Set

\[
\widehat m_\alpha=
\begin{cases}
s_\alpha, & s_\alpha<qr_\alpha,\\[4pt]
W_\alpha/(qr_\alpha), & s_\alpha=qr_\alpha.
\end{cases}
\]

Then define

\[
\widehat T(X)
=
\sum_\alpha
\frac{\widehat m_\alpha}{s_\alpha}
\operatorname{Tr}_{K_q}\!\left(E_\alpha\pi(X)\right).
\]

This uses the intrinsic matrix trace within each component and learns only the central multiplicity weights. It removes the within-component directional anisotropy of a raw probe estimator.

### Why the error bound contains \(q^2\)

For circular complex Gaussian probes,

\[
W_\alpha\sim
\operatorname{Gamma}(q\,m_\alpha r_\alpha,1).
\]

An unresolved component satisfies \(m_\alpha\geq qr_\alpha\). Its Gamma shape therefore obeys

\[
q\,m_\alpha r_\alpha
\geq q^2r_\alpha^2
\geq q^2.
\]

The components with too little averaging have already become exact. Every remaining component has enough coordinates for the \(q^2\) concentration exponent.

A Chernoff bound gives

\[
\Pr\!\left(
|\widehat m_\alpha-m_\alpha|>\varepsilon m_\alpha
\right)
\leq
2e^{-\varepsilon^2q^2/4}.
\]

Union-bound over the \(b\) components. On the resulting event, every \(X\succeq0\) satisfies

\[
\begin{aligned}
|\widehat T(X)-\operatorname{Tr}X|
&\leq
\sum_\alpha
|\widehat m_\alpha-m_\alpha|
\,\operatorname{tr}(X_\alpha)\\
&\leq
\varepsilon
\sum_\alpha m_\alpha\operatorname{tr}(X_\alpha)\\
&=
\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}X.
\end{aligned}
\]

The final step is deterministic and holds over the whole cone simultaneously. That is what permits adaptive future queries.

## 4. Retaining the computation efficiently

Keeping the acquired \(R_q\)-dimensional matrices is sufficient, but can be unnecessarily expensive.

Once the algebra basis \(B_1,\ldots,B_d\) is known, construct the matrices \(L_j\) for left multiplication by each generator. With the physical adjoint \(\dagger\), define

\[
H_{i\ell}
=
\operatorname{Tr}_{K_q}(B_i^\dagger B_\ell).
\]

This is positive definite. Left multiplication is a faithful \(*\)-representation in the \(H\) metric.

Let \(e\) be the coordinates of the identity, and store

\[
f_i=\widehat T(B_i).
\]

For a future expression \(X\),

\[
\widehat T(X)=f^T L_Xe.
\]

The transpose is intentional: the trace functional is complex-linear. Central normalized trace functionals can be transferred in the same way.

The learner can therefore retain the smaller of the cyclic and regular models:

\[
D=\min(R_q,d).
\]

A length-\(L\) expression then has the conservative dense arithmetic bound

\[
O(LD^3),
\]

with no new original matrix-vector calls. Forming the regular representation, its metric, and its functional is charged during acquisition. This is established regular-representation mathematics applied to complete the mechanism. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

In exact rational coordinates, ordinary transpose is insufficient: the physical adjoint is \(G^{-1}B^*G\), where \(G\) is the acquired Gram metric. The supplied exact recompression check uses this nonorthonormal case, reduces dimension eight to five, and preserves a later rational-expression trace of \(10/3\).

### The complete cost remains material

Before recompression, a conservative closure arithmetic bound is

\[
O(Nq^2+kNR_q^2+kR_q^3),
\]

in addition to the oracle’s own work. A dense source action can itself cost \(N^2\). Algebra-basis construction and centre extraction add their own costs.

This is not a universal compression result. Generic matrices can generate the full algebra \(M_N\), with \(d=N^2\), and closure can reach \(R_q=N\).

The exact theorem also distinguishes two computational models:

- An ideal real/complex model with exact ranks and charged root operations.
- A finite-bit corollary for exact rational Hermitian sources, bounded integer probes, and charged algebraic-number operations.

The finite-bit corollary combines a polynomial rank-failure bound with bounded-subgaussian quadratic-form concentration. It avoids assuming infinitely precise random seeds. It does not turn noisy numerical actions into exact rational actions. Hanson–Wright concentration supplies the relevant quadratic-form tool. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

## 5. Consequences beyond estimating one trace

### Reusable design objectives

Suppose a later task supplies positive operators \(H_i\in\mathcal A\) as programs and asks to optimize

\[
F(u)
=
\operatorname{Tr}
\left(\lambda I+\sum_i u_iH_i\right)^{-1},
\qquad
u\in\Delta,\quad\lambda>0.
\]

This is a convex inverse-trace design objective. The retained event covers every feasible \(u\), including choices made by an optimizer after observing the model.

If conventional optimization finds \(\widehat u\) within \(\xi\) of the retained objective’s optimum, then

\[
F(\widehat u)
\leq
\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\inf_uF(u)
+
\frac{\xi}{1-\varepsilon}.
\]

The acquisition cost is shared across later design problems constructed from the fixed algebra. The optimizer’s computation and conditioning remain paid.

### Partition traces and positivity

For any later Hermitian \(H\in\mathcal A\), \(e^H\) is positive, so the same event gives relative accuracy for \(\operatorname{Tr}e^H\), and

\[
\left|
\log\widehat T(e^H)-\log\operatorname{Tr}e^H
\right|
\leq-\log(1-\varepsilon).
\]

Affine matrix-pencil positivity is preserved by the faithful representation. Exact trace equality constraints are a separate matter: estimated multiplicities do not justify claiming exact semidefinite feasibility.

This is a computational capability. It does not imply physical equilibrium preparation or another quantum-memory programme.

### Retained uncertainty for signed questions

The known identity

\[
\sum_\alpha r_\alpha m_\alpha=N
\]

should be used. If only one multiplicity remains unknown, it is determined exactly.

More generally, the construction retains a joint confidence set for the ambient component dimensions \(n_\alpha=r_\alpha m_\alpha\):

\[
\mathcal C
=
\left\{
n:
\ell_\alpha\leq n_\alpha\leq u_\alpha,\quad
\sum_\alpha n_\alpha=N
\right\}.
\]

Every later Hermitian trace becomes a linear functional of \(n\). Its minimum and maximum over this set can be computed in \(O(b\log b)\) by sorting component slopes and allocating the remaining dimension within the bounds.

These intervals are valid simultaneously for signed and adaptively selected queries. They avoid an invalid relative-error promise under cancellation. For an indefinite \(X\), the simpler point estimator guarantees only

\[
|\widehat T(X)-\operatorname{Tr}X|
\leq\varepsilon\operatorname{Tr}|X|.
\]

## 6. The accuracy cost is unavoidable

The inverse-\(\varepsilon\) dependence is not merely an artifact of this construction.

Swartworth and Woodruff prove that, under their stated ambient-dimension conditions, distinguishing random orthogonal projectors of rank \(a\) and \(a+2\) requires \(\Omega(a)\) adaptive matrix-vector queries at constant success probability. The relevant result is Appendix A, Theorem 23 of *Optimal Eigenvalue Approximation via Sketching*. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

A projector generates an algebra of dimension only two:

\[
\mathcal A=\operatorname{span}\{I,P\}.
\]

Relative trace accuracy distinguishes the two ranks whenever

\[
(1+\varepsilon)a<(1-\varepsilon)(a+2),
\]

equivalently \(\varepsilon(a+1)<1\). Taking \(a\) proportional to \(1/\varepsilon\) yields

\[
\Omega(1/\varepsilon)
\]

original queries, even at algebra dimension two.

This is an inherited lower-bound corollary. It shows that compact acquired structure cannot make trace calibration free. For fixed component count and confidence, the construction matches that accuracy dependence; it does not establish optimal dependence on algebra dimension, generator count, precision, or wall time.
