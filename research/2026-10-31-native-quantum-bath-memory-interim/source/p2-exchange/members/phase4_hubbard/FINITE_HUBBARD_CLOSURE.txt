FINITE HUBBARD IMPLEMENTATION OF THE COMPOSITE SINGLET FILTER
2026-10-31. Physical-model closure and calibration mechanism.

Principal result
================

The composite six-spin filter has a compatible implementation in one
finite-U, single-orbital Hubbard model. The entire five-pulse evolution
can be propagated with all charge sectors retained. Its accuracy is not
obtained merely by choosing U/t large: timing from J=4t^2/U is inadequate
for the long composite sequence at moderate U/t. Calibrating the pulses
to the exact isolated-pair exchange removes the leading relevant
fourth-order correction. The remaining fourth-order term is an exchange
between the three rest spins that commutes with the filter.

At U/J_nom=50, t/U=1/(2 sqrt(50)), exact pair-phase calibration and smooth
40/U ramps, the computed five-pulse interaction lasts 1370.3431/U. The
maximum code rejection probability is 6.3411e-6; the maximum false
acceptance probability on spin leakage is 2.2445e-6. Charge excursion is
retained throughout; the final worst charge-anomaly probability is
2.4415e-6. These are population/effect diagnostics, not a diamond-error
certificate or experimental device fidelity. The accepted map on the
code is exactly scalar by physical permutation symmetry, independently
of the numerical precision of those probabilities.

The same U and hopping ceiling also support helper-singlet preparation,
spin-to-charge conversion, and isolated pair controls. Their full
durations are included below. A calibrated charge sensor remains a
specified resource with its own duration and instrument error. A
continuum or semiconductor device with all these parameters, higher
orbital suppression and long-time spin coherence has not been fitted.

1. The complete finite-charge model
==================================

There are four data sites 0,1,2,3 and two helper sites a=4,b=5. At zero
bias use the repulsive, spin-independent Hamiltonian

 H = U sum_i n_i,up n_i,down
     - sum_<ij>,sigma t_ij(s)(c_i,sigma^dagger c_j,sigma+h.c.).       (1)

During a contact, the ON edges are (0,a),(1,a),(2,a),(3,b), all with the
same hopping. During a helper phase only edge (a,b) is ON. Other edges
are OFF. The three-leaf star and the disjoint pair thus use the same U,
hopping envelope and calibrated clock. No reservoir is connected during
the coherent filter. Charge preparation and control calibration replace
the arbitrary-source requirement for this new construction.

The initial state has one particle per site and helpers a,b in a singlet.
The accepted event means all six sites again have one particle and the
helpers are in their singlet. Its Kraus operator on the four data spins is

 K = <S_ab| P_single U_full P_single |S_ab>.                      (2)

P_single in the final event is operationally meaningful. It requires a
spin-blind charge check, or else charge anomalies must be treated as an
unmonitored physical-model error. This note uses a paid charge check;
the program does not covertly discard charge leakage between pulses.

The numerical propagation retains the complete N_up=N_down=3 sector,
with 400 Fock states. Six singly occupied data-input basis states at
data S_z=0 are propagated as columns. SU(2) invariance means that this
sector contains a representative of every data-spin irrep and all its
multiplicities, so the accepted-effect norms are the full spin-input
norms. There is no truncation of intermediate doubly occupied states.

2. Exact accepted-code protection at finite U
============================================

Hamiltonian (1), the initial helper singlet, the final helper-singlet
projection, and the all-sites-single projection commute with permutations
of data sites 0,1,2. They also commute with global spin rotations. Hence
K commutes with data SU(2) and this S3 action. The two-dimensional J=0
data code carries the irreducible two-dimensional representation of S3.
Schur's lemma therefore gives

                       K P_code = alpha P_code                 (3)

at every U and for every common star/pair pulse shape. This protects the
accepted logical vector, rather than just its total-spin label. It does
not guarantee |alpha|=1 or that leakage cannot be accepted. Those two
questions require the complete pulse calculation.

A spin-blind final charge sensor has identical fine operations on a fixed
charge configuration. On the successful all-single configuration it
therefore does not resolve the logical states. The subsequent helper
singlet readout also acts on a one-dimensional helper state; each fine
readout branch multiplies (3) by a scalar. Real spin-orbit terms,
hyperfine gradients, spin-sensitive sensor scattering or nonuniform
star couplings break the premises and belong in the physical error
budget, not in a relabeling of the environment.

3. Why isolated-pair calibration works unusually well here
==========================================================

At half filling let P project onto one particle per site, Q=I-P and
R=Q/H_U with H_U=U sum_i n_i,up n_i,down. Write V for hopping. Since
PVP=0, a Hermitian low-energy expansion has

 H^(2) = -P V R V P,
 H^(4) = -P V R V R V R V P
         + {P V R V P, P V R^2 V P}/2.                          (4)

For the three-leaf star, direct evaluation gives

 H_star,eff = (4t^2/U-16t^4/U^3)
                 sum_i=0..2 (s_i.s_a-1/4)
              +(4t^4/U^3) sum_0<=i<j<=2 (s_i.s_j-1/4)
              + O(t^6/U^5).                                    (5)

The formula is checked as an exact matrix identity in the six-dimensional
m=0 spin space, using the full 36-state four-site Hubbard resolvent in
(4). Its entries are dyadic rationals; the reported zero residual is
exact in binary arithmetic, not a fitted small difference. The disjoint
pair has the usual exchange coefficient

 J_pair(t)=(sqrt(U^2+16t^2)-U)/2
          =4t^2/U-16t^4/U^3+O(t^6/U^5).                         (6)

Thus the star and pair agree through fourth order after a single physical
pair calibration. The extra leaf term in (5) is

 (2t^4/U^3) [R_rest^2-15/4],

where R_rest=s_0+s_1+s_2. It commutes with the star contact and every
helper phase, and is scalar on the code, where R_rest=1/2. It can change
phases of leakage sectors but cannot change the filter's discrimination
through this order. Positive-time implementation of the inverse may
therefore differ by a commuting rest-spin phase without spoiling the
filter. This is why calibrating J_pair is materially better than simply
quoting a large Hubbard gap.

The sixth-order star multiplet gaps are not exactly those of one pair.
For example at U/J_nom=50, the symmetric-to-standard star gap ratio is
1.9995544, not 2, and J_pair/J_standard=1.0001265, not 1. The exact
spin-model period and polynomial cancellation do not hold at finite U.
The full five-pulse calculation includes that discrepancy. The static
expansion is an explanation, not a replacement for the propagated
charge dynamics or a claim that all finite-time errors have the same
asymptotic power.

This use of a strong-coupling Hubbard expansion is standard. The added
mechanism is the compatibility of its calibrated pair coefficient and
commuting leaf correction with this specific composite filter.

4. Pulses, timing and numerical result
======================================

Set hbar=1 and U=1 for the following dimensionless numbers. Define
J_nom=4t^2/U, phi=acos(1/3). The chronological sequence is

  contact pi;
  helper phase 2pi-phi;
  contact 3pi;
  helper phase phi;
  contact pi.

Every pulse turns its hopping on as t sin^2(pi s/2), 0<=s<=1, remains
at t for a plateau, and turns it off with the reversed envelope. Each
ramp takes tau_r. The plateau is chosen so that

                 integral J_pair(t(s)) ds = target angle.       (7)

Equation (7) is used for all five pulses. It is a classical calibration
of a known interaction, not an assumption of a time-independent
unprepared quantum gain. All intermediate charge amplitudes remain
coherent when the next pulse is applied.

The isolated-pair Hubbard convention is J(s_i.s_j-1/4), so known scalar
phases differ from the bare s_i.s_j convention in the spin theorem. The
ideal accepted coefficient in this convention is +i rather than -i;
this is a physically irrelevant overall phase.

The calibration counterexample at U/J_nom=20 is useful:

                                    code rejection  false acceptance
  abrupt pulses, nominal J timing        0.54686          0.22177
  10/U ramps, nominal J timing            0.19785          0.35157
  10/U ramps, J_pair calibration          0.0025711        0.00007805

Smooth charge closure alone does not cure accumulated spin-phase error.
At U/J_nom=50, 10/U ramps with nominal timing still give 4.03% code
rejection and 7.67% false acceptance. This preserves a decisive failure
of the naive projected-spin timing at the very same U and hopping.

Selected same-model operating point:

  U/J_nom                           50
  t/U                               0.0707106781
  ramp duration                     40/U per turn-on or turn-off
  chronological pulse durations     210.0043/U, 307.4098/U,
                                    530.3259/U, 112.5988/U,
                                    210.0043/U
  total five-pulse duration          1370.3430837/U
  maximum code rejection            6.3411479e-6
  final code charge anomaly         2.4414668e-6
  worst final charge anomaly         2.4414668e-6
  worst false acceptance             2.2445046e-6
  accepted code coefficient          -0.11598753+0.99324748 i
  accepted code scalar residual      1.71e-12

The complete contact/phase Hamiltonian has 20 low spin states in the
400-state m=0 sector. On sampled amplitudes 0,.25,.5,.75,1 of both pulse
types, the minimum separation from this band to the next charge level
is 0.79203 U. This sampled spectrum is a model check; the full propagated
evolution, rather than the sampled gap alone, establishes the reported
finite-duration probabilities.

Ramps use a fourth-order two-exponential commutator-free propagator;
plateaus use full Hamiltonian eigendecomposition. Refining each selected
ramp from 80 to 160 steps changes K by 8.48e-8 in operator norm, the
code rejection by 4.22e-10, charge probability by 2.77e-10, and false
acceptance by 2.51e-11. Norm preservation is 5.17e-13.

Do not read these probabilities as a full instrument accuracy of a few
parts per million. Here sqrt(p_miss)=0.0014982 and sqrt(q_charge)=
0.0015625. On an arbitrary coherent superposition of code and leakage,
accepted off-diagonal blocks can cost the miss amplitude. Complete
rejection records and coherent charge leakage can introduce similar
square-root terms in a universal norm comparison. The point verifies a
physical interaction model and identifies its error effects; it has not
proved a device-level diamond bound or a fault-tolerance threshold.

5. Same-parameter helper preparation and complete read conversion
===============================================================

Use the same U and t ceiling for sites a,b, with potentials +delta/2 and
-delta/2. In the singlet basis (D_L,S_11,D_R),

 H_S(delta,t) = [[U+delta, -sqrt(2)t, 0],
                [-sqrt(2)t, 0, -sqrt(2)t],
                [0, -sqrt(2)t, U-delta]].                       (8)

All three triplets have the same charge (1,1) and H_T=0 in this
spin-independent one-orbital model. The read conversion uses:

  a 40/U hopping ramp at delta=0;
  a linear detuning sweep 0 -> 1.5U over 400/U at fixed t;
  a 40/U hopping ramp to zero at delta=1.5U.

The duration is 480/U. Exact three-state propagation gives

  Pr(D_L | S) = 6.02077e-8,
  Pr(S_11 | S) = 2.43724e-5,
  Pr(D_R | S) = 0.9999755674.

Either doubly occupied endpoint is a singlet signal. The singlet false
triplet probability is therefore 2.43724e-5. Every triplet remains (1,1),
with identical fine dynamics, in the stated model. End the hopping before
charge sensing. Sensor fields then couple to fixed charges; no useful ON
exchange is required during the charge integration. Data-helper edges
remain OFF throughout the conversion and read.

For preparation, first load D_R, the unique two-fermion spin singlet in
one orbital, at delta=1.5U. Reverse the above three stages. The resulting
S_11 probability is 0.9999755674. A spin-blind charge check of (1,1)
heralds a pure helper singlet because the entire preparation remains in
the total-spin-zero sector. A failed charge check is reset and retried
before the helper pair touches unknown data. Without that herald, the
2.44326e-5 preparation probability cannot simply be inserted as a
coherent-operation error; its amplitude must be accounted for.

At zero hopping the two-site addition energies for delta=1.5U are
(-.75U,.25U,.75U,1.75U). A reservoir chemical potential mu=.5U lies
between the second and third addition energies, with U/4 margins.
The two-particle ground state is D_R. A cold, spin-independent loading
reservoir and verified initial empty helper sites therefore have an
explicit charge-admission window. The finite loading rate and thermal
tails are still resources; no numerical reservoir rate is invented here.
For an admitted ideal unidirectional two-spin loading model the inherited
bound p_not_full<=2 exp(-kappa_load t_load) may be used. Otherwise loading
has to be qualified with the actual reservoir spectrum and rates.

If higher orbitals are admitted, the final bias must remain below the
lowest triplet-compatible doubly occupied excitation. A familiar
kinematic condition is U<delta_final<U+Delta_orb, with the actual
interaction and exchange shifts included. This condition prevents a
simple Pauli-blockade failure; by itself it is not a bound on virtual
higher-orbital errors. Their matrix elements and complete-duration
effects need separate qualification. No claim that Delta_orb>U alone
provides arbitrary physical accuracy is made.

The numerical read-conversion gap between the two lowest singlet
eigenvalues is at least 0.19975 U along the fixed-t sweep. The preparation
and read-conversion calculations use the same U,t as the selected filter.
The charge sensor is characterized by its complete instrument error
delta_Q and actual duration t_Q, including integration and settling.
Those quantities are not obtained from this Hubbard calculation.

6. Local controls and the simultaneous-gate counterexample
==========================================================

Ordinary sequential encoded controls are available in this same model.
For an isolated zero-bias pair, S_11 couples only to D_+=(D_L+D_R)/sqrt(2)
with the two-state Hamiltonian [[0,-2t],[-2t,U]]. Let
Omega=sqrt(U^2+16t^2). A calibrated rectangular pulse with

 tau=2pi m/Omega

returns all charge exactly and gives

                 R_theta=exp(i theta P_S),
                 theta=pi m(1-U/Omega).                        (9)

Given a desired positive theta, choose integer m large enough that
theta/(pi m)<1, and then

  t=(U/4) sqrt([1-theta/(pi m)]^(-2)-1).

m can always be chosen so t does not exceed the same hopping ceiling.
The program verifies pi/4, pi/2, pi and both helper phase angles. Pair
exchange on different rest edges supplies the established noncommuting
logical axes of the four-spin singlet code. Equation (9) is an exact
finite-Hubbard control construction; physical switching bandwidth and
other orbitals remain part of an embedding, and these rectangular pair
pulses are not silently substituted into the smooth five-pulse results.

Exact commutation of rest-spin Heisenberg gates with the contact does
not imply commutation of their Hubbard hoppings. As a concrete check,
an always-ON rest hopping (0,1) was included during all five finite-U
pulses, corresponding to an independently calibrated isolated-pair
exchange J_g=.2 J_nom. At U/J_nom=50 and 10/U filter ramps it gives

  code rejection                    0.00485169,
  code final charge probability     0.00472285,
  worst false acceptance            0.000210785,
  accepted-code operator difference
    from alpha U_rest P_code         0.00108079.

The extra rest edge is switched on/off abruptly in this deliberate
test, so its charge transient is included. Its hoppings also add paths
which invalidate the exact S3 argument. This is a counterexample to
promoting spin-model commutation into exact physical concurrent gates,
not a lower bound on smoother or jointly optimized controls. The
strictly qualified fallback here is sequential ordinary local gates
and the S3-symmetric detector. No exact concurrent Hubbard logical gate
is claimed.

7. Timing, isolation and remaining admission
============================================

A conservative serial cycle consists of:

  empty/reset and load the helper pair                 t_load/reset;
  separate loaded D_R into a singlet                  480/U;
  verify helper charge                               t_Q,prep;
  apply the complete composite filter                1370.3431/U;
  verify all six charges                             t_Q,end;
  convert helper S/T to charge                        480/U;
  read helper charge                                 t_Q,ST;
  dispose/reload the measured helper pair as needed   t_disposal.

Thus the specified coherent/control time is 2330.3431/U, plus the actual
loading, reset, sensing, routing and classical-latency times. The small
preparation failure probability permits retry while data are isolated;
an expected retry time is not a fixed-time guarantee. If retries are
capped, the final failure probability and actual branch duration both
belong in the instrument. Prepared helpers may be pipelined, but that
changes resource accounting rather than making preparation free.

The filter graph can be fixed: three data sites surround a, data site 3
couples to b, and the helper a-b edge supplies the phase pulses. No
physical movement is required for this one module. Any routing used by
a larger architecture, or the additional connectivity of an entangling
gate, has its own time and controls. A flagged data block is not repaired
by resetting the helpers. It must be handled by a specified outer-code
erasure/leakage recovery or by an appropriate restart of a known-state
task, with that cost included.

In the ideal pulse model OFF hoppings vanish. A conservative physical
allowance for residual tunneling is the time integral of its operator
norm; for four residual spin-independent edges with amplitude at most
t_off this is at most 8 t_off T_off. Sensor backaction which can supply
charge-excitation energy cannot be ignored merely because the static
Hubbard gap is large. The prior local energy-transfer theorem can be
used when its commutator and source-domain hypotheses are established;
this new calculation does not impose an arbitrary-source contract.

If the sum of spin-breaking terms has norm at most beta during the
complete cycle, its unitary comparison allowance is beta T_total, with
the actual complete time above. Calibration error, finite sensor error,
the loading reservoir, higher orbital matrix elements, undesired
inter-site Coulomb or direct exchange terms, and any additional
continuum modes require their own admission in a realized device.
Cold charge occupation, relative barrier control and a maintained
detector are deliberately used resources. They are not consequences
of leaving an auxiliary spin unpolarized.

This is one compatible finite-charge interaction model with loading,
read conversion and controls, rather than separate optimistic
component parameters. It is not yet a demonstrated microscopic device
window, an advantage over equally calibrated exchange hardware, or a
universal fault-tolerant processor.
