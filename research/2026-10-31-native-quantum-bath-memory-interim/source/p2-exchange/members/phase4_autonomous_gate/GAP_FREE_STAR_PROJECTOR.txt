1. SPINS, GEOMETRY, AND THE SINGLE QUERY

Data spins are i and r=1,2,3, all spin 1/2. Let

    R = s_1+s_2+s_3,
    J_D = R+s_i.

Two helpers a,b start in a singlet |s_ab>. Choose the four simultaneous exchange
edges

    H_star = J [ R dot s_a + s_i dot s_b ]
           = J [ sum_{r=1}^3 s_r dot s_a + s_i dot s_b ],   J>0.   (1)

The three star edges have the same calibrated strength as the separate i-b edge.
During these queries the helper a-b edge and unwanted data edges are off, apart
from explicitly allowed gates on the three r spins. The contact graph is a
three-leaf star plus a disjoint edge. No ferromagnetic exchange is required.

Define

    U = exp[-i pi H_star/J],
    K = <s_ab| U |s_ab>.

The three r spins have either R=1/2, with multiplicity two, or R=3/2, with
multiplicity one. U is identity on the R=1/2 multiplicity, which becomes the
logical qubit whenever J_D=0.

In the R=1/2 sector, R behaves as a spin 1/2. Since the two terms in (1) commute,

    U = (-i) SWAP_{R,a} SWAP_{i,b}.                        (2)

This swaps the singlet/triplet state of the effective pair (R,i) with the helper
pair, while preserving the logical multiplicity. A data singlet and helper
singlet return to themselves up to -i. A data triplet produces a helper triplet,
and hence has zero accepted amplitude.

In the R=3/2 sector, R dot s_a has eigenvalues 3/4 and -5/4. At time pi/J their
phases coincide, so the first factor of U is exp(-3 pi i/4) times identity. The
remaining spin-1/2 exchange is a SWAP, giving

    U = - SWAP_{i,b}  on R=3/2.                            (3)

Contracting a SWAP with a singlet helper yields

    <s_ab| SWAP_{i,b} |s_ab> = I_i/2.

Let P0,P1,P2 be the data total-spin projectors. Let D_i be the rank-three part of
P1 with R=3/2, and B_i=P1-D_i. The R=3/2 sector is D_i+P2. Combining (2),(3),

    K = -i P0 - (D_i+P2)/2.                               (4)

Thus the nonzero singular values are exactly one on the desired code and one
half on the undesired R=3/2 sector. The six-dimensional B_i sector already has
zero accepted amplitude. These singular values are fixed by angular-momentum
addition and the physical exchange query, rather than supplied as a black-box
measurement premise.

Repeated single queries with fresh helpers would suppress all leaked amplitudes
by 2^{-N}. The next construction removes this repeated-probe overhead exactly by
keeping one helper pair coherent and using its singlet phase as a control.

2. THREE QUERIES TURN THE CONTRACTION INTO AN EXACT PROJECTOR

On the full data-plus-helper Hilbert space let

    P = I_data tensor |s_ab><s_ab|,
    R(phi) = I + [exp(i phi)-1] P,
    phi = arccos(1/3).

Use the composite unitary

    C = U R(phi) U^dagger R(-phi) U.                      (5)

Chronological order is right to left. Keeping the complete helper Hilbert space,
expand the two phase operators. Sandwiched between P, the accepted operator is

    <s|C|s>
       = [exp(i phi)+exp(-i phi)-1] K
           + [exp(i phi)-1][exp(-i phi)-1] K K^dagger K
       = K (4 K^dagger K-I)/3.                           (6)

The equality uses only unitarity of U; it does not assume that all intermediate
helper histories have been measured or become classical. Evaluating the cubic
on the singular values {1,1/2,0} from (4) gives

    K_C := <s|C|s> = -i P0.                              (7)

Consequently code input is accepted with probability one and every state in P1
or P2 is rejected with probability one. There are no ideal-model false erasures.
The earlier finite-gap construction's error floor and logarithmic helper count
have disappeared because the capability and resource choice changed.

This is a physical application of the established ideas of phase-controlled
amplitude amplification and singular-value transformation. Those ideas are not
claimed as new. The constructive point is the explicitly realizable exchange
query, its finite representation spectrum, and this exact five-segment spin
instrument, including concurrent control and its fault restrictions.

3. ALL PULSES USE ANTIFERROMAGNETIC EXCHANGE

For the complete six-spin star,

    exp[-4 pi i H_star/J] = I.

Therefore U^dagger is implemented by the same positive star Hamiltonian for
3 pi/J. It is not necessary to reverse the sign of J.

The helper exchange satisfies

    exp[-i theta s_a dot s_b]
      = exp(-i theta/4) [ I+(exp(i theta)-1) P ].          (8)

Thus a helper-only exchange pulse of area theta implements R(theta) up to a
global phase. Positive areas phi and 2 pi-phi supply R(phi) and R(-phi).

One physical schedule, in chronological order, is:

    star area pi;
    helper exchange area 2 pi-phi;
    star area 3 pi;
    helper exchange area phi;
    star area pi;
    final helper singlet/triplet measurement.

For constant star strength J and helper exchange strength J_h the interaction
duration is

    T_int = 5 pi/J + 2 pi/J_h.                           (9)

This counts all coherent query and phase durations. A star segment involves four
simultaneous physical exchange edges. There are fourteen edge-pulse instances
if each edge in each segment is counted, not merely five pairwise gates.

The SAME helper pair stays coherent through all five segments. Measuring,
discarding, cooling, dephasing between singlet and triplet, or resetting the
helper between queries invalidates (6). It would replace coherent polynomial
cancellation by a different, weaker instrument.

4. COMPLETE INSTRUMENT, REJECTION, AND RESET

Choose any orthonormal helper triplet basis |t_m>, m=-1,0,+1, and define

    K_m = <t_m| C |s>.

Unitarity and (7) imply exactly

    sum_m K_m^dagger K_m = I-P0,
    K_m P0 = 0.                                         (10)

The final helper instrument is therefore

    accepted: rho -> P0 rho P0;
    rejected fine outcome m: rho -> K_m rho K_m^dagger.  (11)

Every fine triplet history is retained in (11). The rejected branch need not be
a Lüders projection onto I-P0 and need not preserve a leaked logical state.
It is only the accepted operation that is exactly undisturbing on the code.
An operational erasure gadget must append a maintained classical rejection flag
and either discard/reload the data block or replace its output by an abstract
erasure symbol. The root's no-recovery result in the earlier note explains why
arbitrary unknown local errors cannot instead be claimed repaired by this flag.

The helper detector may resolve or destroy triplet magnetic components. It must
classify singlet versus the complete triplet space reliably. Triplet-preserving
coherent detection is not required; relaxation that changes a triplet into a
singlet before recording the flag is a detection fault and must be budgeted.

This verifier supplies the previously missing physical all-J singlet check. It
can follow a multi-block exchange gate even when local J=2 states occur. Root's
global-spin selection argument then rejects any single local vector error that
occurred during an otherwise ideal preceding exchange computation.

5. UNIVERSAL LOCAL GATES RUN CONCURRENTLY, INCLUDING FINE HISTORIES

Any exchange h_jk=s_j dot s_k among the three r spins commutes with R as a vector,
with H_star, and with both helper-only phase operations. Consequently a general
time-dependent logical control

    H_L(t)=sum_{j<k; j,k in r} J_jk(t) h_jk

commutes pointwise with every filtering generator, even when H_L(t) does not
commute with H_L(t') at another time. Hence the total propagator factors as

    U_total = U_L C,
    U_L = time-ordered exp[-i integral H_L(t)dt].          (12)

For EVERY final fine helper outcome,

    K_h^{with gates}=U_L K_h^{filter}.                  (13)

On accepted input this is the desired logical operation U_L P0. For rejected
input the same physical U_L is present in every fine branch; no averaging over
emission histories or source histories is used to get the factorization.

These controls generate the full logical SU(2). In the standard four-spin basis

    |0_L>=|s_12 s_34>,
    |1_L>=(|t+_12 t-_34>+|t-_12 t+_34>-|t0_12 t0_34>)/sqrt(3),

with contacted spin i=1, two untouched-three exchanges restrict to

    h_34 = -I/4-Z_L/2,
    h_23 = -I/4+Z_L/4+sqrt(3) X_L/4.                   (14)

Their axes are not parallel, and positive exchange pulses generate arbitrary
single-qubit unitaries. Thus the verifier does not freeze the logical qubit
while it operates. It supplies the same local gate in every helper history.

The controls in (12) are calibrated scalar physical controls. Replacing them by
arbitrary noncommuting source-valued controls would require checking their source
commutators; commutation of the spin operators alone would not establish (12).

6. SINGLE ACTIVE-COUPLER FAULTS: AN EXACT, SCOPED PROTECTION

Consider an otherwise ideal composite, with one instantaneous error insertion
on one of the four active physical star edges. Let V_e=s_r dot s_a for a star
edge, or V_e=s_i dot s_b for the separate edge. At every time t in any star
segment,

    P0 <s| C_after(t) V_e C_before(t) |s> P0 = 0.        (15)

Proof. On code input, every ideal full star query U or U^dagger returns the
data/helper singlet pair state to itself up to phase. Helper-phase segments act
only by a scalar on that state. Backward evolution from the final code state
therefore equals forward evolution at t, up to the known final phase. Within
R=1/2, a physical constituent spin has the form

    P_{R=1/2} s_r P_{R=1/2} = A_r(logical) tensor R.

The coefficient A_r can be a nontrivial logical operator. Nevertheless R dot s_a
commutes with the ideal partial-SWAP evolution within a star segment, and its
expectation in the initial two singlets is zero. The same is true of s_i dot s_b.
Terms changing R cannot return to the code through one insertion because the
remaining ideal controls conserve R. This proves (15). Concurrent gates (12)
rotate A_r but do not change the zero spin expectation, so the statement survives.

Because a two-spin exchange has only two eigenvalues, an arbitrary-angle single
exchange error exp(-i theta V_e) is a I+b V_e. It follows that its accepted
operation on the logical code is exactly scalar identity (or the concurrent
intended U_L). More explicitly,

    a(theta)=exp(i theta/4)[cos(theta/2)-(i/2)sin(theta/2)],
    p_pass = |a(theta)|^2 = 1-(3/4)sin^2(theta/2).         (16)

The remaining probability becomes a flag. Such a scalar exchange error preserves
total spin zero; helper singlet output therefore forces data back to J_D=0.
There is no accepted leakage in this particular single scalar-error case.

This also holds for every fine error Kraus operator in span{I,V_e}, including
operator-valued coefficients on a source. Merely requiring that the averaged
error channel be SU(2)-covariant is insufficient. Its fine operations could be
vectors or higher-rank tensors, which are a different fault class.

A continuously wrong relative star coupling supplies arbitrarily many Dyson
insertions. It is not one instantaneous exchange error and need not be exactly
transparent. Its accepted logical error begins at second order. If an additive
Hamiltonian perturbation consists only of active-star-edge terms and

    eta = integral ||delta H(t)|| dt,
    r = exp(eta)-1-eta,

then the first-order accepted code term vanishes by (15), and the remaining
accepted Kraus deviation from the ideal phase is at most r. After actual final
helper measurement and discarding the flag branch as an erasure, a conservative
half-diamond allowance to the ideal code identity is 2r+r^2. This quadratic bound
does not apply to a coherently retained, unmeasured helper, whose first-order
orthogonal amplitude remains accessible.

Equal-amplitude waveform/timing errors preserve logical identity structurally
for a single query, but they spoil the exact composite leakage discrimination
unless pulse areas are correct. Arbitrary nonuniform data exchanges are worse:
they act logically and are not detected. This distinction remains after the
star improvement. Intra-data h_jk faults can give a traceless logical term of
norm 1/2 at first order, as the program explicitly verifies.

7. MAGNETIC FAULTS DURING THE VERIFIER: A NECESSARY BOUNDARY

A local spin-vector operator at any point changes global data-plus-helper spin
from zero to one. Subsequent scalar exchange cannot return it to global spin
zero. Therefore it cannot produce an accepted J_D=0 logical error. However a
fault DURING this verifier is not the same as a pre-existing fault followed by
the complete verifier. A late vector fault may survive in the data as unflagged
J_D=1 leakage.

There is an exact counterexample. Insert Pauli Z_i on the singled-out data spin
with time r/J left in the final star segment. The accepted output on code input,
up to the overall composite phase, is

    K_fault P0 = cos^2(r/2) Z_i P0.                      (17)

It is entirely in J_D=1. The unflagged-leakage probability is

    p_miss = cos^4(r/2).                                (18)

To derive (17), move the error to the end through the last remaining i-b partial
SWAP. The conjugated Z_i contains cos^2(r/2) Z_i, a helper-spin term and a term
linear in a helper Pauli operator. The latter two vanish on singlet contraction.

For r=pi the miss probability is zero. For r=pi/2 it is 1/4; for r=0.1 it is
0.9950104; for r=0.01 it is 0.9999500; and at the endpoint it is one. Hence this
finite apparatus cannot promise perfect detection of every fault occurring up
to its final instant. It does promise that a lone vector fault does not become
a hidden logical rotation inside P0. An outer leakage-safe gadget or subsequent
verification is necessary before promoting that statement to fault tolerance.

8. LOADING, ISOLATION, IRREVERSIBILITY, AND FULL DURATION

The maintained resources are explicit:
  * four singly occupied data orbitals carrying the logical singlet multiplicity;
  * one prepared two-electron singlet helper, not an arbitrary mixed helper;
  * a three-edge star with matched exchange strengths, a separate matched edge,
    and a controllable helper-helper exchange;
  * isolation from unwanted edges, spin-orbit anisotropy, additional orbitals and
    reservoir exchange throughout the coherent sequence;
  * coherence of the SAME helper pair through U, U^dagger and both singlet phases;
  * final complete singlet/triplet discrimination, classical recording, and a
    discard/reload route for flagged blocks.

The irreversible outlet is final measurement and reset/reload. It is not active
between the coherent queries. A continuously operating reset bath would change
the construction and is not silently admitted.

For serial operation the elapsed time is

    T_total = T_load + T_attach + T_int + T_switch/ramp
                + T_detach + T_read + T_flag/reset/reload.   (19)

If rejection is handed to an outer code without immediate reload, the invalid
block must remain marked and isolated until replacement. Claims of a code cycle
must charge for that replacement and any interaction partners exposed before it.
Helper preparation failure, incorrect electron count, orbital excitation,
off-state hopping, detector misclassification, and decoherence throughout (19)
are actual-history channel errors; a preparation cost cannot be removed by
calling the logical gauge spin unpolarized.

No single fitted quantum-dot parameter regime has been established here. The
construction replaces the inherited weak Coulomb carrier with ordinary exchange
and a small coherent helper resource, yielding a stronger exact operation. It
does not itself establish a system-level performance advantage over calibrated
exchange processors, dissipative bosonic hardware, or trapped-ion correction.

9. STRONG COMPARATORS AND PRIOR-ART CREDIT

The general resources are established: exchange-only universal computation,
four-spin singlet encoding, prepared singlet ancillary spins, and exchange
leakage-reduction units. Langrock and DiVincenzo (2020) give a three-spin
reset-if-leaked unit using two ancillary spins and as few as fourteen sequential
nearest-neighbor exchange interactions. Our five segments contain fourteen
edge-pulse instances; their pulse count therefore cannot be cited as an automatic
advantage. The tasks, encoding, simultaneous geometry, and access to concurrent
control differ and need a common complete-duration hardware comparison.

Heinz, Sharma and Kattemolle (2026) develop four-spin singlet-only erasure
detection with flagged XXXX/ZZZZ circuits and auxiliary single-spin control.
Their construction treats circuit-level faults. Siegel and Benjamin (2026)
develop singlet-triplet leakage conversion with transfer/reinitialization and
outer erasure-aware decoding. Neither generic erasure conversion nor the need
for leakage management is an unclaimed novelty.

The physical addition earned here is the explicit collective-spin swap query,
its representation-determined singular spectrum, and exact coherent conversion
to a nondisturbing singlet-code verifier with simultaneous local exchange gates.
The phase polynomial is a special case of established amplitude-amplification
and singular-value-transformation methods; Hoyer (2000) and Gilyen et al. (2018)
provide appropriate general credit.

Autonomous bosonic and ion error correction repair specified errors and can
retain the logical qubit. This apparatus flags and discards an unknown erroneous
block. Its utility is therefore as a physical operation for the root's selected
error-detecting exchange framework, not as a stronger universal replacement for
all dissipative or calibrated-control approaches.
