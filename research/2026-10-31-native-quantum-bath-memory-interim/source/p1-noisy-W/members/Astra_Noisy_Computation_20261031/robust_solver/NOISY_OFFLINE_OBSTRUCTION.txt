NOISY OFFLINE ACQUISITION CAN REQUIRE EXPONENTIALLY MANY ACTIONS
A fixed-source, bounded-error obstruction and its constructive implication
31 October 2026 — ordinary proof, no historical-priority claim

1. PURPOSE

This result separates two objectives that coincide too easily in exact models:
acquiring a uniform offline service before the future task is supplied, and
using a future task to reveal the next useful experiment. It is not a general
impossibility result for imperfect experiments. It applies to a specific,
explicit noise geometry: query-dependent, norm-bounded action error. It does
not cover unbiased independent noise or a single fixed operator perturbation.
The fixed-accuracy theorem does not make greater precision free: sections 7A
and 7B separately prove lower and upper precision-work bounds when action
accuracy can be increased at an explicitly charged cost.

The example is well conditioned and has exact generated algebra dimension two.
Its obstruction therefore is not the destruction of exact algebra under generic
perturbations, an unstable inverse, or large algebra dimension. It is the small
correlation between an undiscovered direction and any task-blind query.

The initial proof is over the real field. Section 4A gives the complete complex
Hermitian extension, including arbitrary complex adaptive query algorithms.
Every stated lower bound retains its displayed conservative constants in both
fields. Section 7B treats both real and complex random probes explicitly.

2. SOURCE AND ORACLE MODEL

Fix N>=3, a known unit vector e in R^N, and known numbers lambda,beta>0.
Let u be any unit vector in e-perp. Define

 A_0 = lambda I + beta e e^T,
 A_u = lambda I + beta(e e^T + u u^T).

Both sources are symmetric positive definite. Both have spectrum
{lambda,lambda+beta}; both have condition number 1+beta/lambda; both generate
the two-dimensional unital algebra spanned by I and their nontrivial spectral
projector. In particular lambda=beta=1 gives condition number two.

An experiment may request any vector v and returns y with

 ||y-A v||_2 <= eta ||v||_2.                              (2.1)

The algorithm is randomized and adaptive. It may process all coordinates of
every returned vector and use unlimited local arithmetic. It makes at most q
queries before producing its retained offline computation. The source is fixed
before its random coins. The experiment error is allowed to depend on v but
must obey (2.1) at every v, including vectors not actually queried.

The lower bound grants the learner lambda,beta,e and the entire source class.
It therefore does not arise from withholding those descriptions.

3. A CONTINUOUS, FIXED MASKING ORACLE

For a fixed u, define t=u^T v, a=eta ||v||, and

 O_u(v) = A_0 v + u sign(t) max(beta |t|-a,0).             (3.1)

Set sign(0)=0. This map is fixed before the algorithm runs, continuous, odd,
and positively homogeneous. It is not a linear map. It needs no history or
knowledge of the algorithm's random seed.

The error relative to the fixed linear source A_u is

 O_u(v)-A_u v = -u sign(t) min(beta |t|,eta ||v||),

so (2.1) holds globally. Whenever

 beta |u^T v| <= eta ||v||,                              (3.2)

the response is exactly A_0 v. The baseline source uses the exact oracle
O_0(v)=A_0 v. Thus a large set of query directions gives identical transcripts.

4. SPHERICAL CAP LEMMA WITH AN EXPLICIT CONSTANT

Let u be uniform on the unit sphere in an m-dimensional Euclidean space.
For every unit a and x>=0,

 Pr{|u^T a|>x} <= 2 exp(-m x^2/2).                       (4.1)

PROOF. Symmetry gives zero odd moments, and the exact even moments satisfy

 E[(u^T a)^(2p)]
 = (2p-1)!!/[m(m+2)...(m+2p-2)]
 <= (2p-1)!!/m^p.

Consequently E exp(s u^T a)<=exp(s^2/(2m)). Chernoff optimization for the
positive and negative tails gives (4.1). The p=0 term is one. QED.

For an arbitrary v in R^N and u uniform on the sphere in e-perp, projection
of v into e-perp has norm at most ||v||. Therefore

 Pr{beta |u^T v|>eta ||v||}
 <=2 exp(-(N-1)eta^2/(2 beta^2)).                        (4.2)

4A. COMPLEX HERMITIAN SOURCES AND ARBITRARY COMPLEX QUERIES

Let e be a known unit vector in C^N, and let u be uniform on the complex unit
sphere of e-perp, of complex dimension m=N-1>=2. Replace transposes by conjugate
transposes throughout the source definitions:

 A_0=lambda I+beta ee*,
 A_u=lambda I+beta(ee*+uu*).

These are Hermitian positive definite and have the same spectrum, condition
number, algebra dimension and trace distinction as the real sources. The
algorithm may choose arbitrary complex vectors, process complex observations,
and use arbitrary randomized adaptive complex arithmetic.

For z=u* v define the radial mask

 O_u(v)=A_0v+beta u z max(1-eta||v||/(beta|z|),0),         (4A.1)

where the added term is defined to be zero when z=0. Equivalently the added
term is u (z/|z|) max(beta|z|-eta||v||,0). The map is globally continuous,
fixed before the algorithm's coins, and homogeneous under every complex scalar:
O_u(cv)=c O_u(v). Its error norm is

 ||O_u(v)-A_uv||=min(beta|u* v|,eta||v||)<=eta||v||.

Moreover v*O_u(v) is real, so even this necessary Hermitian-consistency check
cannot reject the masked observations. If beta|u*v|<=eta||v||, the response
is exactly A_0v, just as in the real coupling construction.

For every unit a in C^m and 0<=s<=1, the complex spherical cap has exact tail

 Pr{|u*a|>s}=(1-s^2)^(m-1).                              (4A.2)

To see this, generate u by normalizing m independent circular complex Gaussian
coordinates. Their squared magnitudes are independent exponential variables;
the first squared magnitude divided by their sum has Beta(1,m-1) density.
Unitary invariance then gives (4A.2) for every a. For s>1 the probability is
zero. Hence for all s>=0,

 Pr{|u*a|>s}<=exp(-(m-1)s^2)<=2exp(-m s^2/2),            (4A.3)

because m>=2. Projecting an arbitrary complex v onto e-perp only reduces its
norm, so the complex escape probability obeys exactly the conservative bound
(4.2), with |u*v| replacing |u^T v|. In fact the first exponential in (4A.3)
gives a stronger complex constant; no later statement needs that improvement.

The adaptive transcript coupling below conditions on the entire algorithm
seed, including any complex randomization. Its baseline query sequence is
independent of fixed random u. Thus the argument applies unchanged to arbitrary
complex query algorithms, not merely to real algorithms embedded in C^N.

5. ADAPTIVE TRANSCRIPT COUPLING

Fix the algorithm's random seed r. Run it against the baseline oracle O_0.
The resulting query sequence v_1(r),...,v_q(r) is fixed independently of u;
pad a shorter execution with zero queries. If none violates (3.2), or its
complex counterpart beta|u*v|<=eta||v||, induction
shows that the A_u/O_u execution has exactly the same sequence of responses,
queries, stopping decision, and retained computation as the baseline run.

Union-bound (4.2) over that baseline query sequence, then average over r:

 Pr_(u,r){transcripts differ}
 <= p_q := min{1,2q exp(-(N-1)eta^2/(2 beta^2))}.         (5.1)

This is not a source chosen after seeing the probes. For every u, the source
A_u and its oracle O_u are fixed independently of the algorithm coins. The
random-u mixture is used only to establish that at least one fixed u is hard.

6. POSITIVE-TRACE SERVICE LOWER BOUND

Consider the fixed, positive polynomial query

 X(A)=(A-lambda I)/beta.

It is a projector on every source in the class. Its trace is one for A_0 and
two for A_u. This avoids a relative-error-at-zero issue. With lambda=beta=1,
the expression involves no small division, and its operator norm is one.

THEOREM (REAL OR COMPLEX). Suppose an acquisition algorithm returns a source-free
answer to this query with relative error epsilon<1/3, with probability at least1-delta for
every source above and every globally admissible oracle of form (2.1), where
delta<1/2. Then, for 0<eta<beta,

 q >= [(1-2delta)/2] exp((N-1)eta^2/(2 beta^2)).           (6.1)

PROOF. The two allowed answer intervals [1-epsilon,1+epsilon] and
[2(1-epsilon),2(1+epsilon)] are disjoint. Let G_0 be the event that the baseline
run answers in the first interval. Its probability is at least1-delta. On G_0
and identical transcripts, the spike run uses the same answer and therefore
fails. Thus average spike failure is at least1-delta-p_q. Uniform success
requires average failure at mostdelta, so p_q>=1-2delta, yielding (6.1).
Because the average is over fixed u, some fixed u has at least that failure
probability if the bound is violated. QED.

If eta>=beta, every query can be masked and no finite q suffices under this
success requirement. The exponential lower bound is useful for noise that does
not shrink as beta/sqrt(N); if eta is much smaller than that scale, its numerical
value is weak and no exponential obstruction is asserted.

The theorem applies a fortiori to the all-future-positive-cone service of the
exact compiler, because its fixed single positive polynomial query is included.
It does not say that every noisy trace is hard. In particular normalized traces
may be insensitive to a single rank-one component at a coarse accuracy scale.

7. UNIFORM INVERSE-ACTION COROLLARY

Let F denote any retained offline solver, not necessarily linear, that promises

 sup_(b!=0) ||F(b)-A^(-1)b||/||b|| <= a                  (7.1)

on one probability event. The two inverse operators differ by

 A_u^(-1)-A_0^(-1)
 = -[beta/(lambda(lambda+beta))] u u^T,

with uu* in the complex case. All norms and guarantees below use the relevant
real or complex Euclidean space.

If a<beta/[2 lambda(lambda+beta)], baseline and spike cannot both satisfy (7.1)
for the same retained F: evaluate at b=u and apply the triangle inequality.
The coupling proof therefore gives the same lower bound (6.1).

For the conventional relative solution guarantee

 sup_(b!=0) ||F(b)-A^(-1)b||/||A^(-1)b|| <= epsilon,

the two balls at b=u are disjoint whenever epsilon<beta/(2lambda+beta).
Thus lambda=beta=1 gives the SAME epsilon<1/3 condition as the trace theorem.
The offline lower bound then concerns precisely the relative inverse/RHS task,
not only recovery of an explicit matrix or a necessarily linear solver.

This does not obstruct a cheap preconditioner for this particular family: the
identity already gives condition number two when lambda=beta=1. It obstructs
accurate, uniform, source-free inverse computation. Future online correction
can be substantially cheaper than uniform offline identification.

7A. BUYING ACTION ACCURACY DOES NOT REMOVE ITS INFORMATION BILL

Suppose a query additionally requests a positive error tolerance eta_t, and a
globally admissible experiment may return O_u(v,eta_t) using (3.1), or (4A.1)
in the complex Hermitian case, with that tolerance. The algorithm may choose
eta_t adaptively. The source and the function (v,eta)->O_u(v,eta) are still fixed
before its random coins.

Along the baseline transcript both v_t and eta_t depend only on those coins.
The proof gives the necessary condition

 1-2delta <= 2 E sum_t exp(-a eta_t^2),
 a=(N-1)/(2 beta^2).                                    (7A.1)

Now assign precision work eta_t^(-p) per action, p>0, and suppose the total
precision work is at most B on every execution. Maximizing eta^p exp(-a eta^2)
over positive eta gives (p/(2ae))^(p/2), so

 exp(-a eta^2) <= [p/(2ae)]^(p/2) eta^(-p).

Substitution in (7A.1) proves

 B >= [(1-2delta)/2] [e(N-1)/(p beta^2)]^(p/2).           (7A.2)

The same inequality holds for arbitrary complex adaptive query algorithms:
section 4A supplies the same cap bound conditional on every baseline query
and its chosen tolerance. The real and complex precision-work statements do
not assume that eta_t is fixed, chosen nonadaptively, or independent of the
algorithm's random coins.

An additional multiplicative price constant multiplies this lower bound.
For the price p=2, the necessary precision work grows linearly with N. This
does not assert a universal physical price law: it spells out the implication
under the stated calibrated bounded-error access model. In particular it must
not be advertised as a lower bound for unbiased independent stochastic noise;
that smaller oracle class needs a separate argument.

7B. A MATCHING PRECISION-WORK UPPER BOUND FOR DETECTION

The calibration exponent in (7A.2) is attainable for detecting baseline versus
spike in this source class. This upper bound does not itself reconstruct u,
produce an offline inverse-action service, or implement every future trace.
It only answers whether the positive projector trace is one or two; after
detection that single trace is known exactly under the promised source class.

Put m=N-1. Draw q independent unit vectors v uniformly on the sphere in e-perp,
for example by normalizing independent real Gaussian vectors in the real case
or independent circular complex Gaussian vectors in the complex case.
Request action accuracy

 eta = beta/(4 sqrt(m)).                                 (7B.1)

After each action y, test whether ||y-A_0 v||>eta. Declare a spike as soon as
this happens; otherwise declare the baseline after all q queries. All vector
generation, norm computation, full-vector readout and precision work are paid.

The baseline never gives a false positive, because every admissible error has
norm at most eta. For the spike, irrespective of the error direction or history,

 ||y-A_0 v|| >= beta |u^T v|-eta.

Thus beta |u^T v|>2eta forces detection. The fixed-source random variable
Z=m(u^T v)^2 has E Z=1 and E Z^2=3m/(m+2)<=3. Paley--Zygmund gives

 Pr{Z>=1/2} >= (1-1/2)^2/(E Z^2) >=1/12.

For complex probes replace |u^T v| by |u*v| and take Z=m|u*v|^2. Then E Z=1
and E Z^2=2m/(m+1)<=2, giving the stronger probability lower bound 1/8.
Consequently the common lower bound 1/12, query count and work bound below
are valid in both fields against every allowed, potentially history-dependent
error strategy. This upper bound uses fresh independent probes; the lower
bound permits arbitrary adaptive queries.

On this event beta |u^T v|>=beta/sqrt(2m)>2eta, so detection is forced with
probability at least1/12 per independent query. This holds conditionally on
every previous history, for every fixed u and every allowed error strategy.
Consequently

 Pr{miss spike} <= (11/12)^q <= exp(-q/12).

Taking q=ceil(12 log(1/delta)) achieves failure at most delta. Under the price
eta^(-p), its total precision work is

 B <= ceil(12 log(1/delta)) (16m/beta^2)^(p/2).            (7B.2)

In addition to this precision-work charge, the detector uses q full-vector
source actions, O(Nq) ordinary real or complex vector arithmetic, and O(N)
streaming storage. Formula (7B.2) is a precision-work bound, not a claim that
vector readout is free or a finite-bit implementation theorem.

Together, (7A.2) and (7B.2) establish a matching Theta(N^(p/2)) dependence at
fixed beta,p,delta for this detection task. The upper bound has a logarithmic
confidence factor; no claim that the delta dependence is sharp is made.
It uses the calibrated bounded-error oracle, with accuracy paid per action,
and requires no stochastic or unbiased behavior from the experiment errors.

An offline source-free inverse service is a stronger task. The lower bound
applies to it, but the upper bound (7B.2) is asserted only for detection and the
one promised projector-trace query. Its probes do not establish a representation
accurate for arbitrary later right-hand sides. The conventional online inverse
formula in section8 remains a separate one-action-per-demand construction.

8. A DEMAND VECTOR CHANGES THE INFORMATION GEOMETRY

If the later task supplies b=u, querying it immediately gives a signal of norm
beta, instead of a typical task-blind signal of norm beta/sqrt(N). Under (3.1),
O_u(u)-A_0u=(beta-eta)u for eta<beta. More generally any allowed action error
leaves ||y-A_0u||>=beta-eta. A norm test separates baseline from spike if
beta>2eta, because baseline errors have norm at mosteta.

Thus the same bounded error that permits exponentially costly task-blind
acquisition does not prevent a single task-revealed experiment from exposing the
relevant direction. This motivates a learned solver with paid demand-specific
checks and cumulative information retention. It is not itself a proof that the
total number of such corrections is small for arbitrary task sequences.

An especially strong ordinary comparator exists on this separator family.
Its known two-point spectrum implies the exact identity

 A^(-1) = [(2lambda+beta)I-A]/[lambda(lambda+beta)].

For each supplied b, one noisy action y therefore gives
xhat=[(2lambda+beta)b-y]/[lambda(lambda+beta)], with

 ||xhat-A^(-1)b||/||A^(-1)b|| <= eta/lambda.

This establishes the offline-versus-demand information separation using a
conventional one-action solver, with no newly learned representation required.
It would be wrong to present this family as evidence that a new cumulative
learner outperforms the strongest ordinary method. Its role is the information
boundary. A broader affine family with unknown spectral structure is needed
to test the substantive cumulative construction.

9. RELATED PRIMARY SOURCES AND SCOPE

Hardt and Price, The Noisy Power Method: A Meta Algorithm with Applications
(2014), Corollary1.1 and discussion immediately following it:
https://web.mit.edu/~ecprice/www/papers/npm.pdf
Their sufficient condition separately controls noise projected onto the target
subspace at the initially small random correlation scale. This is consistent
with the masking mechanism. Their result is an algorithmic convergence theorem,
not the unrestricted adaptive-query lower bound proved above.

Aguié, Even and Massoulié, Improved Analysis of the Accelerated Noisy Power
Method with Applications to Decentralized PCA (ICML2026), arXiv:2602.03682v2:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03682
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03682
Their optimality results concern accelerated power iterations and noise
conditions. Do not advertise ordinary noisy-power convergence as a new result.

Frangella, Tropp and Udell, Randomized Nyström Preconditioning (SIMAX2023):
https://tropp.caltech.edu/papers/FTU23-Randomized-Nystrom-SIMAX.pdf
Existing acquisition from matvecs, effective-dimension bounds, and adaptive
rank selection make a plain Nyström preconditioner an established comparator.

Hegde and Cockayne, Learning to Solve Related Linear Systems (2025):
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v271/hegde25a.html
Prior information reuse for related systems is already explicit in the field.

Dereziński, Epperly and Meyer, The matrix-vector complexity of Ax=b (COLT2026):
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v336/derezinski26a.html
Their exact-query lower bounds concern general linear systems. They should not
be silently substituted for an SPD result or the bounded-noise model above.

The spherical-cap argument is elementary and closely related to standard noisy
query and random-initialization obstructions. No exhaustive novelty search or
historical-priority claim has been completed. The useful contribution to this
investigation is a fully specified obstruction, with fixed-source quantifiers,
that forces the acquired computational object and the permitted recourse to be
chosen together.

10. STRONG PREDECESSORS FOR THE POSITIVE DIRECTION

Dunagan and Harvey, Iteratively Constructing Preconditioners via the Conjugate
Gradient Method (STOC2007):
https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~nickhar/papers/CG/DunaganHarvey-CG.pdf
Already converts slow solver progress into preconditioner improvements and
reuses the resulting preconditioner for multiple related right-hand sides.

Monteiro, O'Neal and Nemirovski, A New Conjugate Gradient Algorithm Incorporating
Adaptive Ellipsoid Preconditioning (2004):
https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2004/10/973.pdf
An earlier adaptive-preconditioning construction.

Chu, Gao, Ye and Udell, Provable and Practical Online Learning Rate Adaptation
with Hypergradient Descent (ICML2025):
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/chu25a.html
Online-learning methods already explain adaptive optimization and preconditioning.

Wenzhi Gao, Online Learning to Precondition I (author exposition,16March2026):
https://web.stanford.edu/~gwz/blogs/3/index.html
Explicitly discusses progress-or-learn potentials and credits the earlier
constructions. Its illustrative implementations assume access to an optimality
gap; that qualification should be retained when comparing oracle interfaces.

Consequently, the positive branch should not claim novelty for learning from
solver failures, low-rank matrix updates, or a nonnegative potential argument
alone. A more specific bounded-noise, approximate-comparator, arbitrary-future-
family service contract may be a candidate addition, pending attribution work.
