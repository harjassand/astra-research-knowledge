CONVENTIONAL SECANT LEARNING COMPARISON
31 October 2026

Scientific conclusion
=====================

The robust acquisition contract does not uniquely belong to the halfspace
update. Ordinary symmetric least-change secant learning (PSB), equipped with
the same fresh validation, spectral clipping, individual-source information
reuse, and thresholded updates, also has a finite lifetime correction bound
against an unknown operator-norm approximate comparator. On the fixed compact
experiment it slightly beats the halfspace rule in source actions. BFGS also
matches or beats the halfspace rule empirically, though the Frobenius potential
used here is not monotone for BFGS and no noisy BFGS lifetime bound is asserted.

The distinction to preserve is therefore the information and service contract:
imperfect experiments can support reliable, request-adaptive computation with
finite cumulative correction work even when source-free uniform acquisition
is information-theoretically expensive. The particular update is an established
numerical ingredient. It should not be presented as the foundational discovery.


1. Exact comparison protocol
============================

Code: robust_solver/compare_conventional.py
Data: robust_solver/conventional_comparison_results.json
Imported root prototype: robust_computation/demand_solver.py (not modified).
Imported fixed fixture: robust_computation/check_demand_solver.py (not modified).

Each method receives the same 120 fixed pairs (theta,b), generated once from
seed 32171. The root's separate adaptive modification of every seventh right
hand side is intentionally absent from this comparison. There are three
Hermitian positive-definite source operators on N=192 coordinates, scalar
initializations alpha=(1,1.1,.9), and known spectral interval [.85,2.6]. The
source matrices have a common rank-five core, different noncommuting core
matrices with several distinct eigenvalues, and different generic diagonal
perturbations of norm at most .002. The actual generated algebra need not
remain small. The learner does not receive the core, the perturbation, their
basis, or the exact solver.

The callback is the same fixed, continuous, homogeneous but nonlinear masking
oracle for all methods. Its action error is at most eta=.003 times the input
norm. The acceptance threshold is tau=.060. Scoring uses the separate exact
Woodbury solution and the hidden comparator only after each learned answer;
none of that information enters an algorithmic decision.

All methods freshly validate every returned vector. The accepted certificate
is (a+eta)/(mu-a-eta), where a=||y-A_model v|| for the learned models and
a=||A_observed x-b||/||x|| for ordinary CG. Every reported true relative error
was below its reported certificate. All experiments are floating-point checks,
not interval-certified executions of the real-arithmetic theorem.

The aggregate halfspace prototype discards the individual y_j after forming
sum theta_j y_j. Individual halfspace, PSB, and BFGS retain all y_j already paid
for by that mixed action. After a failing mixed check, every generator whose
individual defect exceeds tau is updated once. A failing convex mixture must
contain at least one such generator. Accepted mixed checks need not trigger
any individual model update.

In directed variants, two additional probes follow the residual direction of
the generator with largest halfspace gain. Those probes cost one original
source action each. For that generator the update uses the best of its initial
and directed observations. Other individually failing generators retain their
initial observation. No matrix is updated until probe selection is complete.
This is identical for the individual halfspace, PSB and BFGS variants.

Fresh CG starts each task from the known scalar-initialization solution and
uses the ordinary CG recurrence with scalar preconditioning. Recursive
residuals only decide when to request a fresh check; they never certify a
returned answer. If a recursively small residual fails its fresh check, CG
restarts from the freshly observed residual. The implementation uses no hidden
core information and retains no directions between tasks. In this experiment
there were no residual restarts or positive-curvature failures. No convergence
theorem for arbitrary nonlinear calibrated oracles is claimed for CG.

All 120 tasks returned a certified answer in each row. "Rounds" counts failing

Every positive theta_j means a mixed action costs three source actions.

The maximum actual relative errors range from .0388 (CG) to .0547 (individual
halfspace); maximum reported certificates range from .0772 to .0798. Each method
aims at the same acceptance threshold, not exactly the same final true error.

The halfspace rule therefore does not win this comparison. Ordinary PSB with
two directed probes uses five fewer source actions than individual halfspace
with the same policy, and stores five fewer directions. Without directed
probes, BFGS has the smallest source count among retained models. No practical
superiority of one update should be inferred from this small one-seed fixture.

Reported wall times include truth scoring and unequal potential audit costs.
They are deliberately not used to claim speed. A full-vector source action
already costs at least the vector output size. Retained coordinates, repeated
small dense solves, eigendecompositions for clipping, and growing bases are
real additional costs. The compact storage demonstration does not eliminate
them. The ordinary secant competitors share the same storage implementation.


3. Standard secant formulas used
================================

For a unit vector v and a cleaned Hermitian-compatible observation y, set
r=y-Bv, a=||r||, d=Re(v* r). Cleaning removes i Im(v* y) v, the component
impossible for an exact Hermitian source. It cannot increase the observation
error. Both v* y and d are then real.

The symmetric least-change secant update (PSB) is

  Delta = r v* + v r* - d v v*,
  B_new = clip_[mu,L](B+Delta).

Before clipping, (B+Delta)v=y. This is the minimum-Frobenius-norm Hermitian
correction satisfying that secant equation. The BFGS update is

  B_new = clip_[mu,L](B - (Bv)(Bv)*/(v* Bv) + yy*/(v* y)).

Its denominators are positive here: B>=mu I and a calibrated source A>=mu I
with eta<mu imply v*Bv>=mu and v*y>=mu-eta. There were no skipped curvature
pairs. The update rule is conventional; gating and validation provide the
experiment's service contract.

The clip is Frobenius projection onto {M=M*: mu I<=M<=L I}. Because scalar
initialization lies inside that set, each update plus clipping changes only
the current retained span enlarged by v and r. Both formulas thus admit the
same growing shared-basis implementation as the halfspace update.


4. Lifetime theorem for thresholded individual PSB
=================================================

Let unknown fixed sources A_j satisfy mu I<=A_j<=L I. Each unit-vector source
observation y_j may depend on all prior history and have error at most eta.
Let theta range over the simplex. Let C_j be ANY fixed Hermitian comparators
in [mu I,L I] with ||A_j-C_j||op<=zeta. The algorithm is not given C_j or zeta.

Initialize B_j=alpha_j I. For every failing mixed validation, select at least
one observed individual pair whose defect a_j exceeds tau and perform its
clipped PSB update. Any number of other individually failing observations may
also be updated, including directed observations. Never apply the theorem to
a stale observation after its own B_j has changed without recomputing its
residual. In the implementation each selected generator is updated once per
round, so every selected pair remains current.

Assume tau>4(eta+zeta). Then the total number M of individual matrix updates,
over any finite or infinite adaptively chosen task sequence, satisfies

  M <= sum_j ||C_j-alpha_j I||F^2 /
       [tau (tau-4(eta+zeta))].

Consequently the total number of failing mixed validations is at most M.
For p directed individual probes per failing mixed check, all k generators
queried at each validation, and T nonzero completed tasks, source work is at
most kT+(k+p)M. More precisely kT+(k+p)F is the implemented source count when
there are F failing mixed validations. No source-free uniform model accuracy
is asserted.

Proof. Write D=B-C and e=y-Cv, so ||e||<=eta+zeta and Dv=e-r. For the PSB
correction above,

  ||Delta||F^2 = 2a^2-d^2,

  ||B-C||F^2-||B+Delta-C||F^2
      = 2a^2-d^2 - 2 Re <e,2r-dv>.

Since |d|<=a and ||2r-dv||^2=4a^2-3d^2<=4a^2, this decrease is at least

  a^2-4(eta+zeta)a.

Projection onto the spectral interval cannot increase Frobenius distance to
the admissible C. Therefore every individually gated update decreases the
nonnegative product-space potential sum_j||B_j-C_j||F^2 by more than
tau(tau-4(eta+zeta)). Telescoping proves the claim. A mixed defect is the norm
of a convex combination of individual defects; if all individual defects were
at most tau, the mixed defect would also be at most tau. Thus each failing
mixed check pays for at least one gated update. QED.

This simple constant is not optimal. For example, from the exact decrease,
when a>=3(eta+zeta) the worst d gives the sharper lower bound
a(a-2(eta+zeta)). The conservative theorem is sufficient for the comparison.
It should not be confused with a new update or a claim that all ordinary
quasi-Newton methods automatically have this contract.

In the fixture eta+zeta=.005, tau=.060, so the conservative condition holds.
Every individual PSB update's hidden-comparator decrease was checked against
the stated lower bound. The minimum surplus above that bound was .00430
without directed probes and .00617 with probes. The corresponding halfspace
bound was also checked. This is evidence for the code's implementation of the
proof, not the proof itself.


5. Qualifications about BFGS and prior art
==========================================

BFGS decreases the displayed Frobenius potential on many updates but can
increase it: the smallest observed decreases were -.0522 without directed
probes and -.0300 with probes. This defeats a naive transfer of the PSB proof
to BFGS. Other potentials may apply; none has been established here for this
full unknown-comparator, calibrated-error, adaptive-family contract.

Learning preconditioners after failed progress and retaining them for multiple
right hand sides already has strong predecessors. In particular, Dunagan and
Harvey's 2007 STOC paper is a direct comparator, including reusable rank-one
updates and amortized multiple-right-hand-side analysis. This must remain in
the main novelty assessment.

Primary sources supporting attribution and comparison:

J. E. Dennis Jr. and R. B. Schnabel, "Least Change Secant Updates for
Quasi-Newton Methods," SIAM Review 21(4), 1979.
https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/1021091

R. Fletcher, "A new low rank quasi-Newton update scheme for nonlinear
programming," research manuscript, 2005; displays the conventional BFGS
formula and its positive-definiteness condition.
https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2005/08/1192.pdf

J. Dunagan and N. J. A. Harvey, "Iteratively Constructing Preconditioners via
the Conjugate Gradient Method," STOC 2007, especially sections 6 and 7.
https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~nickhar/papers/CG/DunaganHarvey-CG.pdf

Noise-robust quasi-Newton methods are themselves an established research line;
for example "Secant Penalized BFGS: A Noise Robust Quasi-Newton Method Via
Penalizing The Secant Condition," arXiv:2010.01275. This comparison did not
implement all such methods and does not establish state-of-the-art status.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01275


6. Reproduction
===============

From the shared project directory run:

  OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python robust_solver/compare_conventional.py

The script prints one summary per method and writes detailed records and
potential audits to conventional_comparison_results.json. There is no external
data dependency and no use of a learned representation supplied by an oracle.
