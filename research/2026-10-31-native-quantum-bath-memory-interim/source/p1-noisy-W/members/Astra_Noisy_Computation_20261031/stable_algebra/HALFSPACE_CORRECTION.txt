SUPPORTING-HALFSPACE CORRECTION FOR NOISY OPERATOR EXPERIMENTS
31 October 2026

This simpler correction has the same worst-case margin guarantee as the exact
observation-ball projection, although it can move less toward that full set.
It avoids scalar root finding. It is a conventional Hilbert-space projection
component, not claimed as a new update rule.

Let v be a unit vector, B_j Hermitian models, and theta real mixture weights.
Observe y and define r=y-B_theta v, a=||r||. Choose a known resolution budget
omega. All comparator tuples under consideration satisfy

  ||C_theta v-y||<=omega.

This follows, for example, from per-experiment error eta, operator-norm
comparator departure zeta<=zeta_budget, and omega=eta+zeta_budget for simplex
weights. The comparator itself is never supplied to the learner.

For a>omega set

  G_j=theta_j sym(r v*),  sym(Z)=(Z+Z*)/2,
  h=a(a-omega),
  Delta_j = h G_j / sum_l ||G_l||_F^2.

Using the real Frobenius inner product on Hermitian matrices,

 <G,C-B>
 =Re(r* (C_theta-B_theta)v)
 >=a^2-a omega=h.

Thus every compatible comparator lies in the closed halfspace
<G,X-B>>=h. B+Delta is its orthogonal projection, and

 sum_j ||B_j+Delta_j-C_j||_F^2
 <=sum_j ||B_j-C_j||_F^2 - h^2/sum_j||G_j||_F^2.

Writing s=sum theta_j^2, the exact gradient norm for real data is

 sum_j||G_j||_F^2 = s*(a^2+(v^T r)^2)/2.

For complex data the exact expression is s*(a^2+Re((v*r)^2))/2, equivalently
s*((Re(v*r))^2+||r-(v*r)v||^2/2), and is still <=s*a^2. Since r may have an imaginary
component parallel to v that no Hermitian action can fit, compatibility and
a>omega ensure G is nonzero. The safe bound is always

 sum_j||G_j||_F^2 <=s*a^2.

Consequently the potential drop is at least (a-omega)^2/s. For simplex weights
s<=1. Clipping each updated model's spectrum to [mu,L] is another convex
projection and cannot increase the potential against comparator matrices in
that interval. Every update is supported on span{v,r} and has rank at most two.

No secant equation is asserted after clipping. The proof uses only the
potential decrease, compatibility of the comparator, and the spectral bounds.

The exact numerical fixture uses real data, two source matrices, nonuniform
simplex weights, a tight observation halfspace, and active spectral clipping.
It verifies all inequalities with Fraction arithmetic.
