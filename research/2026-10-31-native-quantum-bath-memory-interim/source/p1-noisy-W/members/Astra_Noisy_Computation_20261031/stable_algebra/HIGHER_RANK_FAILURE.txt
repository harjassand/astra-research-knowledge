A COHERENT-NOISE FAILURE OF NAIVE BLOCK NYSTROM
31 October 2026

Purpose. This explicit example explains why the rank-one amplification theorem
cannot simply be repeated or promoted to a rank-r, O(eta)-error theorem without
additional experiments or a conditioning/noise bill.

Let U have r orthonormal columns, let h be a unit vector perpendicular to U,
and let the true positive semidefinite defect be C=UU*. A well-conditioned full
source can be A=I+C. Query the r columns u_i of U. The experimental oracle may
return defect actions

  z_i=u_i+eta h.

Each query error has norm exactly eta, so these are legal observations under
the per-action bounded adversarial model. Their common direction is allowed.
Set Z=[z_i]. Since U*Z=I_r, the standard block Nystrom estimate is

  B=Z(U*Z)^(-1)Z*=ZZ*.

Writing g=U1/sqrt(r), the nonzero error block B-C on span{g,h} is

  [[0,eta sqrt(r)],[eta sqrt(r),r eta^2]].

Consequently

 ||B-C|| = [r eta^2+sqrt(r^2 eta^4+4r eta^2)]/2 >=eta sqrt(r).

Thus the sampled core is perfectly conditioned and the true defect is exactly
rank r, yet r individually accurate action measurements do not imply an
O(eta) operator approximation independent of r. This failure is not blamed on
the pseudoinverse or generic algebra instability.

The data error matrix has norm eta sqrt(r). Treating it as a single fixed
operator perturbation of norm eta would be an invalid change of noise model.
Indeed, on g, a subsequent experiment still has error at most eta; the learned
model's error on that direction is much larger. A task or a corrective probe
can expose the mismatch. The parent's validation-and-correction construction
retains that ability, whereas unqualified offline replacement does not.

This example does not prove that all active rank-r acquisition needs sqrt(r)
noise amplification. It refutes that conclusion for the displayed block
construction and identifies what a stronger algorithm must repair.
