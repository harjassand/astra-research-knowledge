## 1. The leverage: design an experiment whose unresolved dynamics matter less

The consequence sought is substantial: a learned model that can substitute for repeated **feedback experiments**, with justified probabilities for their complete observed records. That requires more than fitting an uncontrolled trajectory or predicting a mean response. A later controller changes its actions in response to its observations, and its entire resulting law must remain trustworthy.

The opening is to design the physical action and observation together.

For an unknown reciprocal overdamped thermal system, the port response is a positive mixture of exponential relaxation modes. There may be arbitrarily many modes, arbitrarily slow positive rates, and frozen modes. I use a completed-cell current pulse and a measurement weighted by that same pulse:

\[
\psi(t)=\pi\bigl[\sin(2\pi t)-2\sin(4\pi t)\bigr],
\qquad 0\le t\le1.
\]

Its current and accumulated charge vanish at the cell boundaries, and

\[
\int_0^1 t^k\psi(t)\,dt=0,
\qquad k=0,1,2.
\]

These cancellations suppress the response of unresolved slow modes. They change the physical experiment: slow information and forcing directions become weak in the chosen interface.

### A sharp classification

Let \(m\) be the first nonzero moment order of a nonzero real matched pulse. I derived the following classification over **all positive mixtures of relaxation modes**:

| Pulse cancellation | Worst-case relative absolute response tail after \(L\) lags |
|---|---|
| \(m=0\): nonzero net integral | Infinite supremum for every fixed \(L\) |
| \(m=1\): only the net integral vanishes | Approaches a positive floor |
| \(m\ge2\) | Decays sharply as \(L^{-(2m-2)}\) |
| Selected pulse, \(m=3\) | Explicit, asymptotically sharp \(L^{-4}\) bound |

For the selected pulse, with \(h_0=q>0\),

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\sum_{j>L}|h_j|}{q}
\le
\frac{144}{e^4(2\pi)^4}\frac1{L^4}
\approx
\frac{0.001692252214}{L^4}.
}
\]

There is no hidden mode count or positive lower relaxation-rate bound in this inequality. A single mode with dimensionless rate approximately \(4/L\) supplies the matching asymptotic lower bound.

The mechanism behind the exponent is precise. At a small relaxation rate \(a\), the same-cell dissipative response scales as \(a\). The two matched pulse transforms contribute \(a^{2m}\), while summing the delayed geometric response contributes \(1/a\). The normalized tail therefore scales as \(a^{2m-2}\); maximizing against \(e^{-aL}\) gives the stated power law.

The public retained-feedback precursor’s pulse falls into the \(m=1\) case, with floor \(2/3\). Its specialised work guarantees can still hold through their own stability and performance arguments. The new pulse changes the admissible experiment and does not establish an improvement in that earlier work optimum. ## 2. The construction earns the steps between memory suppression and a usable simulator

A short response tail alone does not establish a model valid under feedback. The completed construction resolves five additional difficulties.

**Acquiring the noise law from correlated, nonstationary observations.**  
A passive record from the retained apparatus supplies a covariance estimate with a uniform spectral error bound. The calculation includes the unknown initial-state mean and covariance deficit. It does not assume independent cells or silently restore stationarity.

**Acquiring response through actual interventions.**  
A separate block of fresh, precommitted random signs estimates the impulse response. This permits fixed unknown positive input and output gains and independent Gaussian white detector noise of unknown variance. Response is not inferred from an incorrectly calibrated fluctuation–dissipation conversion.

**Conditioning on the calibration that actually occurred.**  
Deployment on the same apparatus shares noise history with acquisition. I bounded the conditional future residual law after a finite guard interval, and separately paid for the surviving response to the calibration probes. The guard observations are withheld from the later controller.

Finite encoding adds a further complication: the future law conditional on encoded calibration is generally a mixture, not Gaussian. Conditioning first on the latent initial state and full analog history, then applying Markov’s inequality and convexity of total variation, resolves that step without observing the latent state.

**Transferring the learned model to arbitrary bounded causal feedback.**  
The controller may be discontinuous, nonlinear, depend on its complete available history, and be selected after calibration. Its current action must precede the current completed observation.

The proof uses a triangular transformation between noise and output histories. After whitening with the true Gaussian covariance, the response discrepancy is predictable from earlier innovations, so the likelihood cross term vanishes. With acquired impulse error \(\epsilon_h\), covariance floor \(\kappa\), and relative covariance error \(t_K<1\), the response-and-covariance contribution is

\[
\frac{B\epsilon_h}{2}\sqrt{\frac{H}{\kappa}}
+
t_K\sqrt{\frac{H}{8(1-t_K)}}.
\]

The retained-noise and earlier-probe terms are added explicitly. The calibration good event supports every admitted later policy; it is not a separate fit for each tested controller.

**Executing a finite numerical model.**  
A finite-support sampler cannot approximate an unencoded continuous Gaussian law in total variation. The operational target is therefore the same finite observation encoding on the plant and simulator.

I implemented exact rational coefficient and action arithmetic, interval Cholesky factorization, finite uniform-bit midpoints, certified inverse-normal brackets, and exact quantization. A first-encoding-disagreement argument controls numerical error even when the controller switches discontinuously.

## 5. Failures that determined the scope

### Passive oscillators break the fixed-pulse extension

For a thermal oscillator whose damping approaches zero, the filtered covariance retains a term proportional to

\[
\left|\int_0^1\psi(t)e^{i\Omega t}\,dt\right|^2\cos(\Omega j).
\]

A fixed pulse suppresses this only at frequencies it notches. A nonzero compact pulse cannot notch every possible frequency in an interval. Thus passivity alone does not supply the positive result: the real, nonnegative relaxation spectrum is doing essential work.

The exact-cell oscillator calculation confirms the failure numerically. This changed the proposed physical class rather than being absorbed into an optimistic approximation.

### Interacting mean closure does not close the full observed process

A separate construction proves that an \(n\)-site equilibrium exclusion path with positive exchange rates and one informative endpoint detector has stationary output Hankel rank exactly \(2^n\), at every fixed positive sampling interval. The proof uses the established finite-state observability framework and native exclusion dynamics. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

Consequently, exact linear predictive representations and exact finite-state hidden Markov realizations can require the full exponential dimension even though unconditional mean equations close.

This does **not** establish an exponential constant-accuracy lower bound. The numerical witnesses become severely ill-conditioned. It also does not exclude nonlinear compression or a compact program simulating the microscopic system.

### Imperfect cancellation has an explicit cost

First-moment leakage restores a nonzero memory floor; net-charge leakage destroys the uniform normalized bound over arbitrarily slow modes. I derived a limited matched harmonic-leakage extension with explicit error floors and modified initial-state bounds. Those tolerances are supplied calibration conditions, not measured hardware performance, and are not silently included in the ideal-pulse execution.
