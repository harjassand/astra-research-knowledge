## Label identity and terminal reward

If an ordinary type-i particle restores the protected D token, move its ordinary identity to the old token. Exactly one ordinary identity changes type, from i to the old token's actual terminal type. Physical counts are unchanged. If the old token itself restores D, no ordinary identity moves. This requires that ordinary particles did not evolve during the deleted excursion; otherwise the one-exchange accounting is insufficient.

Use the actual FIRST terminal event, including other terminal exits, as the killing time. The terminal ordinary-restoration counter, including its mark at that time, has expectation equal to its stopped intensity integral. With frozen y_i this is y_i E integral h_i. A nonnegative supersolution -Q_y psi_i>=h_i bounds the integral by psi_i: localized Dynkin gives the inequality up to bounded stopping, and nonnegativity/Fatou removes localization. The entrance sum therefore bounds the entire trace exchange intensity by b_i y_i. This is a terminal probability statement, not independence of competing exit times. At y_i=0 the exchange is absent and no division by y_i is needed.

The two examples correctly distinguish reward from time: with ordinary intensity beta y_i, the entrance-weighted reward is bounded by beta/mu; with ordinary intensity beta s y_i, time cost remains bounded but the exchange rate at y_i=1 grows linearly in s. Thus time-only ingress cannot replace this certificate.

## Exact trace and forcing

Deleting completed excursions creates their actual exit kernel in the trace, provided the state includes all persistent phase metadata and terminal outputs are retained. Source-uniform assignment yields per-label hazard at most b_i. Predictable thinning of independent potential residual clocks realizes the state-dependent kernel. No physical reaction is switched off; the forcing argument uses the event of no residual rings in an exact realization. Baseline progress must still admit its previously required splitting in this same trace clock. Finite localized rates and almost-sure excursion termination do not independently prove global nonexplosion.

For uphill-only charging, use marked rewards when needed. If terminal marks l from transient phase p produce different old-token post-types d_l, the correct reward is

    h_i^up(p,y) = sum_l h_{i,l}(p,y) 1{r(d_l)>r(i)}.

A single pre-jump phase indicator is valid only when those outputs share the indicated type. This explicit marked interpretation is binding and implements the note's actual-post-jump convention. Summing all marked uphill exits controls their full trace rate; rank-nonincreasing exits may remain adapted unbounded excess. At rank-zero target a, every genuine departure is uphill. With no residual rings and at least L progress rings, every label is therefore at a, exactly as in the retained rank forcing argument.

## Finite phase-scaled acquisition

For transient rational Q0 and nonnegative hbar, t=(-Q0)^(-1)hbar is nonnegative. With s>0 fixed during the excursion and Q_y=s Q0-diag(k), psi=t/s gives

    -Q_y psi = hbar + diag(k)t/s >= h.

The aggregate entrance domination by s ell yields sum lambda psi<=ell dot t. Additional killing, even arbitrarily large downhill restoration, strengthens this inequality. The displayed two-state example satisfies -Q0(4/5,7/10)=(1,2), and ell dot t=23/10 exactly.

This is a finite certificate once raw factorization and uniform inequalities have actually been supplied and checked. Matrix inversion alone does not verify them. In particular, h_i^up must be uniformly bounded by the chosen hbar, while every terminal rate remains in the diagonal killing. A population-dependent inverse in the more general rule requires genuine uniform verification, not finitely many samples.
