## Forcing

When initial Y>=n/2, a selected persistent working cohort has uniformly bounded adverse participation for the whole horizon by monotonicity of Y. The reviewed product theorem therefore supplies the stronger nT^L scale, which implies the weaker claimed scale for T<=1 after constants are reduced.

Otherwise at least n/2 initial identities are source labels. Genuine disjoint entry subrates admit independent rate-gamma potential clocks for those identities. A clock ring by the midpoint ensures that identity is already working, even if an extra entry occurred first. This proves the binomial domination, not independence of the realized worker population. It requires irreversible source membership and the explicitly genuine allocation; one shared conversion event cannot be credited repeatedly.

On first-half success, choose an integer cohort M of order nT from actual midpoint workers. Their membership persists and the denominator 1+Y cannot decrease. Their adverse participation is at most B/T throughout the remaining horizon, even when source-entry events cause adverse collateral resets. All such participation was required to be counted. The product damping exp(-constant b_T T) is consequently bounded below uniformly; the Poisson rank-progress factor contributes T^L. Conditional application of the genuine progress generator gives threshold and exponent of order nT^(L+1). No successful future-rank or survival conditioning occurs. For small nT the prefactor K can cover the bounded exponent, including integer-cohort issues.

## Exact dilution mechanism

The obstruction has fixed n, irreversible source entries, and exactly one existing worker reset uniformly per entry. Its per-worker adverse participation gamma C/Y is at most 2 gamma C/(1+Y) for Y>=1; at Y=0 no existing worker is reset. Ordinary progress is separately allocated and batch size is one, so it meets the productive-pool contract.

Suppress progress to obtain Z, the old targets never reset. Conditional on final working size y, a specified j-tuple survives every entry with product over r=a,...,y-1 of (1-j/r). Telescoping gives (a-1)_j/(y-1)_j. Multiplying by (a)_j proves (D). This is an exact correlated-survival factorial moment, not a binomial approximation.

At T=a^2/n, expected new workers are of order a^2, and a binomial lower-tail estimate makes E Z_T uniformly bounded. The rare low-worker event contributes at most a times an exponentially small probability. Actual working progress before T has probability at most q E integral Y_t dt=O(a^3/n+a^4/n), using an un-killed extension. It tends to zero for epsilon<1/4.

For the kill, pay any progress event separately. Until such progress, actual targets equal Z. On t<=a/n, the factorial count is at most a^m. On the remaining interval, conditional factorial moments and Y_t>=c nt outside an exp(-c'nt) event bound the expected hazard by C a^(2m)/(nt)^m plus its exponentially small exception. Integration gives C a^(m+1)/n for m>1, and C a^2 log(a)/n for m=1. Fixed m is smaller than a for all sufficiently large n, so the factorial formulas apply. The stated epsilon restriction makes these costs vanish. Actual stopped kill compensation thus bounds early killing by o(1); no survival-conditioned factorial law is assumed.

A fixed K with P(Z_T<=K)>=3/4 exists by the uniform first moment. Subtracting progress and kill probabilities leaves probability at least one half of A_T<=K while alive. Therefore the alive inverse-profile expectation stays bounded away from zero, while f_s(A_0) tends to zero for every fixed s>0. This disproves precisely the stated uniform incoming A-only bound. It is not a counterexample to earlier uniform-adverse-rate contracts, since gamma C/Y can grow with n here.

## Carried quantity and boundary

At an entry with current Y and Z, expected old targets afterward are Z(1-1/Y). Since the new working size is Y+1, expected Z_after times Y equals Z(Y-1). Thus Z(Y-1) is an exact martingale for entry/reset dynamics; replacing Z by A gives the same entry calculation, while ordinary progress raises A(Y-1). This identity alone gives no favorable inverse-profile generator inequality: nonlinear jump corrections must be computed, not discarded by a mean-value identity.
