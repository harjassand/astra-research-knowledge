The advance is now a route to thermal-state access

I pursued the stronger opening: can the acquired representation support the constraints needed to construct a quantum thermal state, while keeping the interacting computation explicit and fully paid?

The answer is yes for the promised class, with three distinct results:

A proved inner solver: the bulk and fast temporal coordinates admit an explicit Gaussian/path Gibbs update with a convergence bound independent of the temporal mesh. A finite annealed-importance certificate computes their normalizer without assuming equilibrium at every intermediate step.
A completed classical computation: the full high-frequency method now runs on an acquired, connected, frustrated input. It computes the physical log normalizer to certified error below 
0.03
0.03, including acquisition, temporal approximation, quadrature, sampling and numerical costs.
A proved state-preparation consequence: the construction survives exact spin constraints at selected imaginary-time slices. Those constrained normalizers supply an established quantum Gibbs-state preparation method. I also proved a sharp precision bound for that interface.

The general state-preparation algorithm remains a theorem; no quantum circuit was executed. The native classical implementation covers the recorded rank-one model with a connected cycle bulk and one retained static coordinate. Direct spectral computation wins decisively on that small input. The consequential result is the broader capability and its constructive cost, rather than a performance claim for the fixture.
