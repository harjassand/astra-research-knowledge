\title{Acquiring hidden algebraic response types\\
\large Exact counting and optimal synthesis from manufactured summaries}
\author{Astra P3 research}

\begin{abstract}
We construct an exact representation from a raw dense Boolean polynomial.
Its higher derivatives reveal primitive coordinate blocks; a metric on
quadratic block responses then acquires unknown local changes of basis and
repeated response types. The output is a symmetric matrix of rank at most
$R$, an explicitly retained residual with at most
$15\beta^2(2E+n)$ scalar edges, and local nonlinear terms. Here $\beta$
is the largest acquired block, $R$ is the total dimension of the hidden
response types, and $E$ is the sum of the ranks of the hidden off-block
errors. Neither the blocks, bases, types, errors nor type profile are
supplied. Acquisition costs $2^{O(\beta^2)}\poly(L+n)$ time and polynomial
space; for fixed block sizes it is polynomial.

Combined with the earlier local-phase extension of the pinned OpenAI
counting manuscript, this gives exact counting in
$\poly(L+n)2^{o(n)}$ time when
$\beta=n^{o(1)}$, $R=o(n)$, and $\log(2+E/n)=o(\log n)$.
It removes the earlier rank-times-error condition for this structured
family, while preserving the original independent input count.
The acquired coordinate classes also yield shared $\Z/4\Z$ summaries.
Their exact labelled fiber populations permit optimal Boolean prediction
within this acquired feature class, with a certified uniform-input error
count, at the same subexponential scale.

Acquisition, identities and restricted failure theorems are unconditional.
The subexponential counting and synthesis bounds depend on the governing
manuscript. Classical centroid, clustering, co-clustering, sum-rank and
Gaussian ingredients are credited. No historical first-priority,
generic-circuit, polynomial-time SAT, or practical speed claim is made.
Executed programs validate acquisition and exact reconstruction with
exhaustive finite leaf counters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Input and precise guarantees}
\label{sec:results}
All matrix ranks are over $\F_2$. For an alternating matrix $B$
(symmetric with zero diagonal), put
\[
q_B(x)=\sum_{i<j}B_{ij}x_ix_j,\qquad
e(B)=|\{(i,j):i<j,\ B_{ij}=1\}|.
\]
The initial input is a degree-at-most-three Boolean polynomial $p$ in
explicit algebraic normal form (ANF), on $n$ independent bits. The bit
description length $L$ includes every coefficient and index; it may
contain $\Theta(n^3)$ terms. The targets are
\[
Z(p)=\sum_{x\in\F_2^n}(-1)^{p(x)},\qquad
N_\tau(p)=\frac{2^n+(-1)^\tau Z(p)}2.
\]
Let $T(u,v,w)=D_uD_vD_wp(0)$ be its third additive derivative.
We first require that $T$ have zero radical. Its primitive direct-sum
blocks are acquired in polynomial time below. Write their dimensions
as $b_1,\ldots,b_N$, with $\sum_a b_a=n$ and
$\beta=\max_a b_a$. The useful promise concerns the quadratic
interactions in \emph{some unknown bases} of these blocks:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
B_{aj}=L_aK_{\tau(a),\tau(j)}L_j^\top+S_{aj}\quad(a\ne j).
\end{equation}
Here $L_a\in\GL_{b_a}(\F_2)$, a type can be shared only by blocks
of the same dimension, and $K$ is an arbitrary symmetric matrix
indexed by type-coordinate pairs. If $c_d$ types have dimension $d$,
put
\[
R_*=\sum_d d c_d,\qquad E=\sum_{a<j}\rank S_{aj}.
\]
The within-block quadratic terms are arbitrary. The compiler is
given none of $\tau,L_a,K,S$, or the profile $(c_d)$.
Independent block basis changes preserve $E$. If the hidden error
has $M$ scalar edges, $E\le M$; if it occupies $M_B$ block pairs,
$E\le\beta M_B$. Arbitrary affine disguises of the original inputs
are allowed: their effects on lower-degree terms are charged below.

\begin{theorem}[Matrix acquisition]\label{thm:matrix}
Given a raw alternating matrix $B$, a partition into blocks of sizes
$b_a$, and an integer budget $0\le R\le n$, a deterministic algorithm using
$2^{O(\beta^2)}\poly(n)$ time and polynomial space returns a verified
block-diagonal invertible $G$, a partition of scalar coordinates into
$d\le R$ nonempty classes, a symmetric $d$ by $d$ matrix $H$, and an
alternating residual $S'$ such that
\[
GBG^\top=\off(C)+S',\qquad C=UHU^\top,\qquad \rank C\le d\le R.
\]
The columns of $U$ are the class indicators; each row has one $1$.
A budget too small for one type of each occurring block dimension
is reported infeasible. If \eqref{eq:model} holds with $R_*\le R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
e(S')\le15\beta^2(2E+n).
\end{equation}
No coordinates are deleted. Recovering the planted witness is unnecessary.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Raw polynomial counting]\label{thm:raw}
Assume a raw cubic $p$ has radical-free third derivative and satisfies
\eqref{eq:model} with $R_*\le R$.
Acquire its primitive blocks, then apply Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}.
The algorithm constructs an invertible map $x=Ay$ and the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identity}
p(Ay)=q_{\off(C)}(y)+q_{S'}(y)+f(y),
\end{equation}
where every monomial of $f$ is inside an acquired block and has degree
at most three. Its nonconstant monomial count is $m_0=O(n\beta^2)$.
Conditional on the local-phase theorem in Section~\ref{sec:count},
the exact counting bit time is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total}
\poly(L+n)\left(2^{O(\beta^2)}
+2^{R+F(n,m_0+15\beta^2(2E+n)+O(n))}\right),
\end{equation}
with polynomial space. It is $\poly(L+n)2^{o(n)}$ whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regime}
\beta=n^{o(1)},\qquad R=o(n),\qquad
\log(2+E/n)=o(\log n).
\end{equation}
The exponent uses the original $n$ independent inputs.
\end{theorem}

This does not cover arbitrary dense cubics. The favorable intrinsic
structure is restricted, but its representation is acquired and checked.
Failed tests or unfavorable parameters permit exhaustive fallback.
All budgets $R\le n$ can be inspected with polynomial overhead.
Choose a certificate by an explicit guarded counting bound or dovetail
its exact counter with the others. Sequentially exhausting a bad
exponential case before trying a good one would not justify this bound.

\section{Acquiring the nonlinear blocks}
\label{sec:centroid}
Centroid decomposition is established multilinear-form theory
\cite{huang}; its use against hidden block-diagonal cubic descriptions
also appears in \cite[Appendix A]{blaser}. We give the binary
construction to make characteristic and costs explicit.
Compute
\[
\mathcal A=\{P:T(Pu,v,w)=T(u,Pv,w)=T(u,v,Pw)
\text{ for all }u,v,w\}.
\]
Basis-vector tests give $2n^3$ linear equations in $n^2$ binary
unknowns. For $P,Q\in\mathcal A$, slot transfers show
\[
T(PQu,v,w)=T(Qu,Pv,w)=T(u,Pv,Qw)
=T(Pu,v,Qw)=T(QPu,v,w).
\]
The zero radical implies $PQ=QP$. Transfers also give closure
under multiplication and the identity lies in $\mathcal A$.
Thus $P\mapsto P^2+P$ is linear on this commutative binary algebra.
Its kernel contains exactly all idempotents, obtained by another
linear system without assuming semisimplicity.

Starting with $I$, split each current projector $P$ into its nonzero
pieces $PE$ and $P(I+E)$ for every basis idempotent $E$. The
pieces commute and are orthogonal; at most $n$ survive. A further
idempotent is a linear combination of the processed basis and acts
on every final piece as zero or identity, so no further split exists.
Moreover
\[
T(Ex,(I+E)y,z)=T(x,E(I+E)y,z)=0.
\]
Conversely, a direct-sum projection satisfies the centroid equations.
The resulting images are therefore the intrinsic primitive blocks,
up to permutation. Their chosen bases give an invertible column
matrix $V$. Naive elimination costs, for example, $O(n^7)$ binary
operations and polynomial space.

The compiler substitutes into the \emph{full ANF} $p(Vu)$.
Transforming only the quadratic coefficient matrix is incorrect:
Boolean substitution into a cubic can create quadratic and unary
terms. In the acquired coordinates these corrections stay within
blocks. The change between any hidden and acquired block bases is
block-diagonal up to permutation, so \eqref{eq:model} and $E$
remain valid, with arbitrary new diagonal blocks. Affine translations
also create only local lower-degree terms from the local cubic
parts, and unary terms from the quadratic part.

If $T$ has a radical, the earlier Gaussian-radical compiler can
return an exact smaller core. The present theorem then applies only
if that core has the stated properties. Eliminating a large radical
is not assumed to preserve the response types.

\section{An invariant metric and acquisition of the type profile}
\label{sec:metric}
For two $d$ by $n$ row blocks, divided into $d$ by $b_j$ pieces, put
\[
\rho_d(X,Y)=\sum_j\rank(X_j-Y_j),\qquad
\bar\rho_d([X],[Y])=\min_{Q\in\GL_d(\F_2)}\rho_d(QX,Y).
\]
Rank subadditivity makes $\rho_d$ a metric. Left invertible
multiplication is an isometry, so $\bar\rho_d$ is a metric on
orbits, or a pseudometric on raw row blocks. Symmetry uses $Q^{-1}$
and the triangle inequality composes two gauges.
Independent right invertible changes on the column blocks are
also isometries. These are elementary sum-rank properties
\cite{sumrank}; scalar Hamming distance would not suffice before
the unknown bases are aligned.

Complete the ideal matrix from \eqref{eq:model} on its diagonal
blocks using the same symmetric kernel, obtaining $D$.
Each arbitrary diagonal-block discrepancy has rank at most $b_a$.
Consequently the total directed row-block error satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T}
T_0:=\sum_{a,j}\rank(B_{aj}-D_{aj})\le2E+n.
\end{equation}
After left normalization, ideal row blocks of a common type are
identical. Let $T_d$ be the error contribution of dimension $d$.
In each hidden type choose the observed row closest to its ideal
center. If the errors are $\epsilon_a$, distance to this observed
center is at most $\epsilon_a+\min\epsilon_a$. Summing proves
that the optimum with $c_d$ \emph{observed} centers costs at most
$2T_d$.

For every occurring dimension $d$ and every $c=1,\ldots,N_d$,
run exact one-swap metric $c$-median local search. Its established
factor-five guarantee \cite{arya} gives
\[
\kappa_d(c_d)\le10T_d
\]
for the promised budget. Distances are integral and at most $n$,
so objectives are at most $N_dn$; each strict improvement lowers
this by at least one. Thus exact local search terminates after
polynomially many swaps. Zero-distance duplicates and discarded
empty facilities do not invalidate the guarantee.

The hidden profile is selected by a polynomial budget DP:
\[
\min\sum_d\kappa_d(c_d)\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\sum_d d c_d\le R,\quad 1\le c_d\le N_d.
\]
Only occurring dimensions enter. The promised profile is feasible,
so the chosen solution obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
\kappa\le10\sum_dT_d\le10(2E+n).
\end{equation}
The DP needs neither $E$ nor monotonicity of the acquired costs.
It has polynomially many integer states, transitions and backpointers.

All orbit distances and minimizing gauges are computed by streaming
fewer than $2^{d^2}$ binary matrices and testing invertibility.
Only the best gauge for each pair is stored; their total bits are
at most $\sum_d d^2N_d^2\le n^2$, apart from indexing.
For uniform blocks a coarse distance cost is $O(2^{d^2}n^3)$;
unequal blocks and all budget searches still cost
$2^{O(\beta^2)}\poly(n)$ time and polynomial space.
Materializing the whole group for growing $\beta$ would violate
the space claim and is not required.

\section{Simultaneous alignment and exact symmetric rounding}
\label{sec:round}
Let $s(a)$ be row block $a$'s selected observed center and choose
$G_aB_a$ closest to $B_{s(a)}$. Set $G=\diag(G_a)$, $B'=GBG^\top$,
and define the generally nonsymmetric model
\[
X_{aj}=B_{s(a),j}G_j^\top.
\]
It repeats the \emph{raw} center row followed by the common right
transform. Using the center row of $B'$ instead would incorrectly
insert that center's own left gauge. Right invariance gives
\[
\sum_{a,j}\rank(B'_{aj}-X_{aj})=\kappa,\qquad
A_0:=\ham(B',X)\le\beta^2\kappa.
\]
The last bound holds because each nonzero rectangular block has
at most $\beta^2$ nonzero entries and rank at least one.
Scalar coordinates of the same acquired block type and the same
position form one class. There are $d\le R$ such classes, and
the rows of $X$ are constant within them.

\begin{lemma}[Symmetric rounding]\label{lem:round}
If $B'$ is symmetric and $X$ is constant on each row class of a
partition also used for columns, majority-rounding $B'$ on each
class-pair block, with consistent zero ties, gives a symmetric
$C=UHU^\top$ with
\[
\ham(B',C)\le3\ham(B',X).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $P(X)$ replace each row's entries on every column class by their
majority. It is a best Hamming projection onto column-class-constant
matrices and remains row-class constant. Since $X^\top$ is
column-class constant,
\[
\ham(X,P(X))\le\ham(X,X^\top)
\le\ham(X,B')+\ham(B',X^\top)=2\ham(B',X).
\]
Thus $\ham(B',P(X))\le3\ham(B',X)$. Majority projection of $B'$
does at least as well as this possibly nonsymmetric candidate.
Symmetry and consistent ties give a symmetric majority table $H$;
the class indicator matrix $U$ yields $C=UHU^\top$.
\end{proof}

This principle has close co-clustering ancestry
\cite[Theorem 3.6]{cocluster}. Its role here is an exact interface
with the acquired finite-field gauges.
Retain $C$'s free diagonal: zeroing it can destroy low rank.
The residual $S'=B'+\off(C)$ is alternating, and off-diagonal
errors count twice in Hamming distance. Hence
\[
e(S')\le\tfrac32 A_0\le15\beta^2(2E+n).
\]
Direct checks of symmetry, rank and every coefficient certify the
identity regardless of whether the hidden witness exists. This
proves Theorem~\ref{thm:matrix}.

The final coordinates satisfy $u=G^\top y$, $x=VG^\top y$.
Recompute $p(VG^\top y)$ and subtract $q_{B'}$.
Every cubic-induced quadratic correction stays within a block,
and substitution into a quadratic creates only an additional
unary term beyond its congruent polar matrix. Thus the difference
is block-local and has $O(n\beta^2)$ monomials, proving
\eqref{eq:identity}.

\section{Counting and full reconstruction costs}
\label{sec:count}
The earlier Astra P3 local-phase theorem, conditional on
Proposition 3.4, Lemma 4.7 and Appendix B of the pinned source
\cite{governing}, counts $m$ explicit fixed-locality phases of fixed
order $2^b$ in $\poly(L+n)2^{F(n,m)}$ bit time and polynomial
space. It has $F(n,m)=o(n)$ whenever
$\log(2+m/n)=o(\log n)$. In its guarded small-parameter regime,
\[
h=\left\lceil\log_2(2+km/n)\right\rceil+O_{k,b}(\log\log n),
\quad a=h/\log_2 n,\quad
F=O_{k,b}\!\left(n\sqrt a[\log_2(2/a)]^{3/2}\right).
\]
Outside the guards it uses exhaustive enumeration.
Its filtered phase products use the algebra
\[
\Q(\zeta)[V]/(V^{2^{b-1}(n+2)}).
\]
They have retained input degree
$O_{k,b}(n)$ independently of $m$. The source manufactures
replacement forms and summaries. Exact reconstruction precedes
the final coefficient-sum map modulo $2^{n+2}$; that map is not
a ring homomorphism obtained by substituting $V=1$ into the
truncated ring. The full earlier phase proof and audits accompany
the source archive.

For the acquired $C=UHU^\top$, define
\[
Q_H(z)=\sum_aH_{aa}z_a+2\sum_{a<b}H_{ab}z_az_b\pmod4.
\]
Because each row of $U$ is a unit vector, its exact refinement is
\[
(-1)^{q_{\off(C)}(y)}
=i^{Q_H(U^\top y)}\prod_j i^{-C_{jj}y_j}.
\]
For general overlapping column codes an extra unary sign is
required; it vanishes for these indicator classes.
Walsh expansion yields
\[
i^{Q_H(z)}=2^{-d}\sum_{\alpha\in\F_2^d}
\Gamma_H(\alpha)(-1)^{\alpha^\top z},\qquad
\Gamma_H(\alpha)=\sum_z i^{Q_H(z)}(-1)^{\alpha^\top z}.
\]
Exact quadratic elimination computes each Gaussian integer
$\Gamma_H(\alpha)$ in polynomial time and $O(d)$ bits.
Thus each of at most $2^d$ characters changes only unary phases.
The inner counter sees $m_0+e(S')+O(n)$ factors of locality at
most three and phase order four.

Stream the characters. Reconstruct each inner answer exactly in
$\Z[i]$ before multiplying by $\Gamma_H$ and dividing the final
numerator by $2^d$. Numerators use $O(n+d)$ bits; the final count
is integral in $[-2^n,2^n]$. There is no loss of precision before
division. This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:raw}.

\subsection{Raw programs and original outputs}
For an XOR/AND straight-line program, propagate exact Boolean ANFs,
reducing $x_i^2=x_i$ after every instruction. An intermediate
degree-at-most-three function has $O(n^3)$ terms; multiplying two
such functions uses at most $O(n^6)$ term pairs. Reject this
front end or use a separate fallback if the reduced result exceeds
degree three. Otherwise the intermediate-degree property is
certified, with conservative cost $O(Sn^6)$ coefficient operations
for $S$ instructions and polynomial storage.
Long affine computations and shared cubic computations therefore
introduce no internal-state counting variables. This does not
recognize every low-degree final output through arbitrary
high-degree intermediate states.

Restricting acquired coordinates preserves the local foreground
and the rank bound. Padding to the original $n$ variables maintains
the uniform counting estimate with an exact known multiplicity.
Conditional counts consequently produce witnesses and uniform
satisfying samples, mapped back by $x=Ay$. Self-reduction itself
is established; the new scope is the acquired family.

\section{Universal fibers and optimal Boolean synthesis}
\label{sec:synthesis}
For the acquired scalar coordinate classes $A_1,\ldots,A_d$, set
\[
s_a(y)=\sum_{j\in A_a}y_j\pmod4.
\]
These summaries are linear over $\Z/4\Z$ on the acquired Boolean
coordinates. In the original inputs, first compute the retained
invertible binary coordinate change; they are not claimed to be
$\Z/4\Z$-linear directly in the original bits.
Since $\binom w2\bmod2$ depends only on $w\bmod4$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:summaryquad}
q_{\off(C)}(y)=\sum_aH_{aa}\binom{s_a(y)}2+
\sum_{a<b}H_{ab}(s_a(y)\bmod2)(s_b(y)\bmod2)\pmod2.
\end{equation}
Denote this sign by $h_H(s)$ and put
$F_0(y)=(-1)^{f(y)+q_{S'}(y)}$, including its constant.
The signed local fiber population satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fiber}
W(s):=\sum_{y:s(y)=s}F_0(y)
=4^{-d}\sum_{\chi\in(\Z/4\Z)^d}i^{-\chi\cdot s}T_\chi,
\quad
T_\chi=\sum_yF_0(y)i^{\sum_a\chi_a s_a(y)}.
\end{equation}
Character orthogonality proves this identity. Every $T_\chi$
has the same local factors and only additional unary fourth-root
phases. It is an exactly reconstructible family shared by all
fibers and by any subsequent Boolean function of the summaries.

\begin{theorem}[Exact synthesis within acquired summaries]\label{thm:synth}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:raw} and
\eqref{eq:regime}, conditional on the local-phase theorem,
the exact numbers of zeros and ones of the original $p$ can be
streamed for every summary fiber. One can output a Boolean
predictor minimizing uniform-input mistakes among \emph{all
Boolean functions of these acquired summaries}. The bit time is
\[
\poly(L+n)\left(2^{O(\beta^2)}
+2^{4R+F(n,m_0+e(S')+O(n))}\right),
\]
with polynomial working space and at most $4^R$ output rows.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Compute $a_j(t)=\sum_{w\equiv t\ (4)}\binom{|A_j|}{w}$ by
four-state dynamic programming. The unlabelled fiber size is
$N(s)=\prod_j a_j(s_j)$, because the classes partition all
coordinates. Its original-label imbalance is $h_H(s)W(s)$.
Consequently
\[
N_0(s)=\frac{N(s)+h_H(s)W(s)}2,\qquad
N_1(s)=\frac{N(s)-h_H(s)W(s)}2.
\]
Predict one exactly when $N_1(s)>N_0(s)$, choosing zero on
ties and empty fibers. The independent fiber optima give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:risk}
\operatorname{Err}_{\min}
=\sum_s\min(N_0(s),N_1(s))
=\frac{2^n-\sum_s|W(s)|}{2}.
\end{equation}
There are $4^d$ fibers and $4^d$ characters for each. Recompute
the $T_\chi$ as necessary and output each fiber immediately.
This gives the time and polynomial working space. Each inner
answer is reconstructed exactly before division by $4^d$;
the numerator has $O(n+2d)$ bits. Populations and error totals
have $O(n)$ bits.
\end{proof}

Here $d$ is the number of indicator classes, not merely $\rank C$.
Compacting to an overlapping column basis loses the product
population formula. The possible $4^R$-row output is explicitly
subexponential, not asserted polynomial.
Any specified Boolean program $h$ on the $2d$ bit encodings of
the summaries can also be evaluated against these fibers:
\[
\sum_yF_0(y)(-1)^{h(s(y))}
=\sum_s W(s)(-1)^{h(s)}.
\]
Its description and evaluation cost are charged, without counting
its intermediate states as independent inputs. The theorem is
neither globally optimal compression, unrestricted synthesis,
nor sample-based statistical learning.

\section{Beyond cubics, and substantive boundaries}
\label{sec:bounds}
For every fixed degree $k\ge3$, the top $k$-linear additive
derivative admits the same centroid construction when radical-free:
there are $n^2$ unknowns and $O_k(n^k)$ equations.
After full ANF substitution, explicitly require every monomial of
degree $3,\ldots,k$ to lie within one primitive block.
Top-degree structure alone does not imply this; crossed
lower-degree nonlinear terms must reject this route.
If the test passes, the foreground contains
$O_k(n\beta^{k-1})$ monomials of fixed locality $k$.
All matrix, Fourier, and fiber arguments remain valid, giving
the same regime \eqref{eq:regime}, with polynomial cost in
the explicit raw ANF.

\subsection{Prior machinery and an expanded guarantee}
The complete interface is the contribution examined here:
higher derivatives acquire blocks, rank distance handles
unknown local bases, clustering and the budget DP acquire types,
rounding preserves the exact residual, and the phase theorem
handles that residual without an easy interaction graph.
Centroid methods \cite{huang,blaser}, local search \cite{arya},
co-clustering \cite{cocluster} and sum-rank geometry \cite{sumrank}
are prior ingredients. Joint clustering and gauge synchronization
also has substantial prior work; inspected recovery theorems
\cite{sync} use stochastic models rather than this arbitrary
symmetric finite-field kernel with adversarial error.
A focused search did not locate the complete compiler, which
does not establish historical first priority.

The earlier generic symmetric-rank compiler guarantees subexponential
time for rank $r=o(n)$ and $M$ scalar errors when $rM=o(n^2)$.
The present promise is more structured and removes that product
condition. For example, three-dimensional blocks with
$c\asymp n/(3\log n)$, a full-rank type kernel, and errors
$S\sim G(n,\log n/n)$ have
$R\asymp n/\log n$ and $E=\Theta(n\log n)$.
They meet the present regime, while $RM=\Theta(n^2)$ is outside
the older sufficient bound. This is not a proof excluding every
alternative old certificate.

Nor is this a small-width promise. Lee--Lee--Oum
\cite[Theorem 1.1(ii)]{rankwidth} gives this error graph
rank-width $n/3-o(n)$ with high probability. Adding a rank-$R$
symmetric completion changes every cut rank by at most $R$,
so the background still has rank-width $n/3-o(n)$.
Rank-width simulation \cite{colnet} and active Clifford-frame
simulation \cite{clifft} are relevant competitors. The comparison
is between guarantee parameters, not every rewrite or hybrid method.

\subsection{Adaptive affine Gaussian leaves still require exponentially many pieces}
For $n=3t$, let the cubic part be
$\sum_{j=1}^t x_{3j-2}x_{3j-1}x_{3j}$.
Its third tensor is a direct sum of $t$ volume tensors.
Each has slice rank three, and slice rank is additive on direct
sums \cite{gowers}, so its slice rank is $n$.
If $T|_{W^3}=0$ for a codimension-$c$ subspace, a basis adapted
to $W$ gives slice rank at most $3c$: every nonzero term uses
a complement coordinate in some slot. Thus $\dim W\le2n/3$.
Every affine leaf where the polynomial becomes quadratic has
such a direction space, so any affine quadratic-leaf cover
of all inputs needs at least $2^{n/3}$ leaves, including adaptive
ones. Quadratic perturbations and affine disguises preserve this
barrier. It does not apply to signed decompositions or all algorithms.
For the phase state $2^{-n/2}\sum_x(-1)^{p(x)}|x\rangle$,
the same radical-free tensor implies full Pauli stabilizer nullity:
a stabilizing shift would have affine derivative and hence lie
in the radical.

\subsection{Small global rank does not imply few response types}
The new compiler must remain alongside the previous one.
Index three-coordinate blocks by all three-dimensional subspaces
$W\le\F_2^r$, choose row bases $U_W$, stack them into $U$ and
put $D=UU^\top$. Then $\rank D=r=O(\log n)$, with
$n=3s$ and \(s=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{r}{3}_{2}\).
Distinct block row orbits have sum-rank distance at least
\[
\delta=\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{r}{3}_{2}-\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{r-1}{3}_{2}.
\]
Any alignment leaves a nonzero row of $GU_A-U_B$.
Only \(\genfrac{[}{]}{0pt}{}{r-1}{3}_{2}\) column subspaces lie in its kernel,
and every other column block contributes a rank unit.
Pairwise triangle inequalities within each approximation class
therefore force total directed rank error at least
$(s-c)\delta/2$ for an approximation with at most $c$ orbits.
Arbitrary diagonal blocks can absorb at most $n$ directed rank
units. At least
\[
M'\ge (s-c)\delta/4-n/2
\]
off-block scalar errors remain. This is $\Omega(n^2)$ for
$c=o(s)$, despite logarithmic original rank and zero original
error. A volume cubic per three-coordinate block retains
precisely the relevant primitive nonlinear blocks.
This is a representation boundary, not counting hardness.

\section{A sharp failure of finite-field Gaussian reconstruction}
\label{sec:failure}
The general arbitrary dense quadratic background with near-linear
local nonlinear perturbations remains open in this work.
Replacing rational interpolation by finite-field additive
phases does preserve Gaussian algebra, but loses a required
correlation:

\begin{theorem}[Joint-jet threshold]
Let $F=\F_{2^e}$, $p_c(T)=\sum_{j=0}^D c_jT^j$, $D\ge1$, and
$\psi(a)=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\F_2}(a)}$.
An observation may be any complex function of Hasse jets at
distinct nonzero nodes, with total jet length at most $D$.
No linear combination of such observations, however many
node sets occur, equals $\psi(p_c(0))$ for all $c$.
Total joint length $D+1$ is sufficient and is the exact threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For one observation with jet lengths $J_t$, the polynomial
$\prod_t(T-t)^{J_t}$ lies in its jet kernel, has degree at most
$D$, and is nonzero at zero. A scalar multiple has trace one
there. Translation by its coefficient vector preserves the
observation while reversing the target sign, so its correlation
with the target over all coefficients is zero. Any linear
combination also has zero correlation, whereas the target's
correlation with itself is $|F|^{D+1}$. Conversely Hermite
interpolation with total length $D+1$ determines the polynomial.
\end{proof}

The populations $\operatorname{Tr}(c_0)=0$ and $1$ thus have
identical full histograms for every allowed joint observation
but opposite target sums. This is an observation-model result,
not a lower bound on algorithms inspecting the raw description
in other ways.

Other preserved failures narrow the same frontier.
Groupwise Fourier dualization sends an invertible quadratic
polar matrix $B$ to $B^{-1}$ and preserves every cross-cut rank.
Gaussian moments do not justify a sublinear perturbation cutoff:
independent triples give $H(V)=(4+2V)^{n/3}$, whose every
nonempty omitted tail changes the exact-count residue.
For an $a$ by $a$ polynomial matrix $C(T)$ of entry degree
at most $R$, nonsingular at zero, determinant multiplicities give
\[
\sum_t(a-\rank C(t))\le aR
\]
at distinct nodes. Thus lowering a fixed full-rank cut at
every node loses the small degree-to-node ratio needed by
the source construction. None is a generic algorithmic lower bound.

\section{Execution and limitations}
The finite programs execute acquisition and exact identities;
their local-phase leaves are exhaustive, not the asymptotic
replacement engine. No practical speed comparison is claimed.
Seven root raw-cubic cases through $n=24$ passed $14{,}912$
pointwise identities and all original/hidden/acquired counts;
six also ran Gaussian--Walsh reconstruction. The $24$-variable
raw input had $979$ cubic terms; acquisition returned rank $5$,
$75$ residual edges, $29$ foreground terms and signed sum $-3328$.
The heterogeneous compiler checked its DP against finite profile
enumeration and reconstructed raw cases with blocks $(5,3,3)$
and $(3,3,6)$, including $6144$ pointwise checks.

Independent actual-code audits tested $27$ orbit models, $3645$
metric triangles, $693$ two-sided gauge isometries, $27$ exact
medoid optima and $50{,}248$ majority-rounding cases.
Further heterogeneous tests checked $12$ unknown-profile models,
all four feasible budgets on another model, $14$ malformed inputs,
and two independently generated raw cases with $6144$ pointwise
identities and exact reconstructed sums $-80$ and $64$.
Five degree-$4,5,6$ fixtures passed $9728$ pointwise identities.
The three quartic fixtures ran the full orbit and Gaussian
reconstruction; degree-five and degree-six cases checked
centroid/locality and exact count preservation only.

The synthesis program compiled $2091$ intermediate states on
nine independent inputs, certified degree at most three,
and acquired rank $2$ with ten residual edges. Its exact
zero count was $260$. Across three classifiers, $192$
labelled fibers and $4672$ original-input labels matched
independent exhaustive counts. For the nine-input program,
the best function of its three acquired mod-four summaries
makes exactly $190$ errors among $512$ inputs.
This is optimality within that feature class, not accuracy
on an external task. The streaming fiber iterator stores
neither a character table nor output rows; finite validation
wrappers optionally collect those small tables.

The strict low-rank boundary executed $21{,}840$ gauge checks.
Its largest certificate has $4185$ variables, global rank $6$
and $1395$ primitive blocks; at most $139$ response orbits
force more than $387{,}267$ off-block errors.
The joint-jet failure executed $189$ deficient joint-histogram
equalities and three injectivity checks at the exact threshold.
