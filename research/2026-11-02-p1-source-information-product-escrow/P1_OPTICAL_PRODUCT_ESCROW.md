## Distribution-free inventory theorem
Let chamber Omega=A×[0,l] have arbitrary nonnegative concentrations c_m(u,z), c_j(u,z) in mol/m3. At specified wavelengths assume measured Beer–Lambert attenuation per ray
    tau(u)=∫[k_m(u,z)c_m(u,z)+Σ_j k_j(u,z)c_j(u,z)] dz,
with k_m<=k_m_plus and 0<=k_j<=k_other_plus (units m2/mol); all unknown absorbing interferents together contribute no more than N_other_plus moles. The calibrated spatial illumination density w(u)>=0 satisfies ∫_A w du=1, w(u)<=wmax. A full-aperture absorptance observation has Y=∫w(u)(1-exp(-tau(u)))du+e with |e|<=e_det.

For every possible 3D spatial distribution, including arbitrarily rare highly segregated modes,
    N_CH4 >= L(Y) := max(0, [(Y-e_det)/wmax - k_other_plus*N_other_plus]/k_m_plus).  (1)
**Proof:** 1-exp(-tau)<=tau for tau>=0, hence Y-e <= wmax[k_m_plus N_CH4+k_other_plus N_other_plus]. Since Y-e_det<=Y-e, rearrange. No mixing, hidden rank, path length extrapolation, or rare-mode sampling assumed. The source-side claim is ONLY about product actually in the sealed optical chamber.

If independently bounded w>=wmin, k_m>=k_m_minus, and every ray's optical depth tau<=tau_max, define alpha=(1-exp(-tau_max))/tau_max (alpha=1 at tau_max=0). Because 1-exp(-tau)>=alpha tau,
    N_CH4 <= (Y+e_det)/(alpha*wmin*k_m_minus).                 (2)
The geometry/pressure and CH4/interferent spectral envelopes need independent physical qualification. A spot optical ray does NOT supply this theorem: a rare concentrated gas patch can lie wholly outside it. The area integral is a necessary information operation, not a statistical estimator of the future catalyst mode.

For M independent calibrated photon detection trials, a two-sided Hoeffding contribution to e_det is sqrt(log(2/delta)/(2M)); systematic illumination, responsivity, window, species, and detector correlation errors must be ADDED and physically justified. This is NOT a programmable noise-port service or a guarantee against unmeasured interferents. An unknown high-absorption species INVALIDATES the single-band contract, calling for multispectral or GC analysis.

## Origin and cumulative charge — conservation instead of a hidden-state model
Let M_b be measured bound (1) of actual CH4 moles in each collected batch; let B_blank be the upper bound of preexisting CH4 in the entire source/feed/cells and B_nonCO2 the maximum moles of methane-carbon from all non-inorganic feed carbon (catholyte organic additives, deposits, replacements, inlet impurities, lines) across the WHOLE ACCOUNTED CAMPAIGN. Atom conservation gives
    N_carbon_from_intended_inorganic_CO2_in_collected_CH4 >= max(0, Σ_b M_b-B_blank-B_nonCO2). (3)
The source's pyridine stabilizer itself contributes carbon: nominal 20:1 ligand/Cu at 3.6 mg/L Cu and 500mL catholyte contains ~2.83 mmol pyridine-carbon atoms (5 atoms per ligand). This can overwhelm a 5min CH4 lot. An isotope tracer or long-horizon feed-carbon accounting is necessary; do NOT assert same-batch carbon provenance from CH4 optical absorption alone. Source headspace lag requires campaign-wide gas and cathodic charge accounting, or an independently qualified hold-up kernel. A rate expressed as intrinsic electrochemical FE needs further ruling out of chemical donors, leakage and co-fed carbon.
