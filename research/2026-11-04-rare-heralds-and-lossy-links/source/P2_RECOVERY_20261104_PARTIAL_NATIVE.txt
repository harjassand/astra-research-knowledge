\]

These probabilities sum to one because \(g=b+Qg\). The initial vertex has weight \(V_{iu}g_{(u,0)}\). Since \(\rho(Q)<1\), absorption occurs almost surely.

**Third, add independent even closed walks.** For each even length \(\ell\ge2\), the number of loops is Poisson with mean

\[
\lambda_\ell=\frac{\operatorname{tr}C^\ell}{\ell}.
\tag{4}
\]

The identity behind this component is

\[
\log G_{\mathrm{loops}}(Z)
=
\sum_{\substack{\ell\ge2\\\ell\ \mathrm{even}}}
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(ZC)^\ell-\operatorname{tr}C^\ell}{\ell}.
\]

For a chosen length, select root \(j\) with probability proportional to \((C^\ell)_{jj}\). With \(t\) steps remaining, a bridge transition is

\[
P(u\to v)
=
\frac{C_{uv}(C^{t-1})_{vj}}{(C^t)_{uj}}.
\tag{5}
\]

Again, closure is acquired directly. Counting every visit from the open and closed walks gives the sampled photon configuration.

## 4. The hidden infinite cost is bounded independently of herald rarity

An infinite walk expansion is not yet an efficient algorithm. The decisive bound comes from the conditional quantum state itself.

After heralding,

\[
|\psi_h\rangle\propto P_h(a^\dagger)|G_C\rangle,
\qquad \deg P_h\le H.
\]

Let \(U_C\) prepare \(|G_C\rangle\). Undoing this squeezing transforms each creation operator into a linear combination of creation and annihilation operators. Therefore

\[
U_C^\dagger|\psi_h\rangle
\]

is supported on photon numbers at most \(H\).

Writing \(\rho=\|C\|\), the quadrature-energy inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[N_R\mid h]+\frac r2
\le
\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}
\left(H+\frac r2\right).
}
\tag{6}
\]

There is no division by \(P_B(h)\).

A convenient computable bound is

\[
M=4(1+\mu_C)\left(H+\frac r2\right),
\qquad
\mu_C=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[C^2(I-C^2)^{-1}\right],
\]

because \((1+\rho)/(1-\rho)\le4(1+\mu_C)\).

Choose

\[
L=\left\lceil\frac{4M}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil.
\]

Omit loops longer than \(L\), cap the total number of visits at \(L\), and return a fixed fallback on overflow. Coupling to the exact process shows that these changes together cost at most

\[
P(N_R>L\mid h)\le\frac{M}{L+1}\le\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
\]

in total variation. This pays for the entire photon tail, including all open walks.

### Finite computation and random bits

The remaining operations also have finite budgets.

Matching self-reduction uses at most \(H^2\) approximate counting calls on weighted graphs with at most \(2H\) ports. Per-call relative error \(O(\varepsilon/H)\), and a summable failure allocation, control the resulting matching distribution.

Rational weights can be compiled into unweighted graphs with polynomial bit overhead. A two-terminal gadget has completion counts \(1,W,0,0\): neither terminal, both terminals, or exactly one terminal included. Parallel composition adds \(W\); a series construction multiplies it. Binary expansion realizes an integer \(W\) with \(O(\log W)\) vertices. Clearing denominators therefore makes the unweighted FPRAS sufficient.

For loops of length at most \(L\),

\[
\Lambda=\sum_{\substack{2\le\ell\le L\\\ell\ \mathrm{even}}}
\frac{\operatorname{tr}C^\ell}{\ell}
\le\frac{\mu_C}{2}.
\]

An ideal Poisson oracle is unnecessary: approximate \(\operatorname{Poisson}(\Lambda)\) by
\(\operatorname{Bin}(J,\Lambda/J)\), taking

\[
J\ge \Lambda+\frac{8\Lambda^2}{\varepsilon}+1.
\]

The standard coupling bound is at most \(2\Lambda^2/J\). All subsequent categorical choices have rational probabilities and a bounded number of draws; sufficiently many random bits make their accumulated error another prescribed fraction of \(\varepsilon\).

Thus the sampler has a **worst-case finite program**, polynomial in the stated quantities. Its dependence is on the actual photon and energy values, not merely their logarithms.

## 5. Acquisition: learn the accepted state without collecting accepted copies

Ordinary Gaussian tomography is already known to be efficient. The additional issue here is whether its errors remain controlled after an exceptionally rare conditioning event. Generic trace-distance continuity would introduce a reciprocal success probability. Positivity supplies a stronger route. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

For a heterodyne sample \(\alpha=x+iy\),

\[
\Sigma:=2\mathbb E[xx^T]=(I-B)^{-1},
\qquad
B=I-\Sigma^{-1}.
\tag{7}
\]

Under the gap promise,

\[
\frac12I\preceq\Sigma\preceq\frac1g I.
\]

Gaussian concentration and stable inversion therefore recover a rational, support-preserving \(\widehat B\) with entrywise error at most \(\beta\), using

\[
K_{\rm cal}
=
O\!\left(
\frac{m+\log(1/\delta_{\rm cal})}{g^2\beta^2}
\right)
\tag{8}
\]

unconditioned preparations. Projection onto the promised support, clipping and a spectral check ensure that the returned model is physical.

### Why conditioning remains stable

For remaining photon pattern \(n\), with \(N=\sum_i n_i\), define the unnormalized weight

\[
W_B(h,n)=
\frac{\operatorname{haf}(B_{h,n})^2}{h!\,n!}.
\]

It is a nonnegative homogeneous polynomial of degree \(H+N\) in the entries of \(B\). Consequently, for \(N\le L\),

\[
\boxed{
e^{-\alpha}W_B(h,n)
\le W_{\widehat B}(h,n)
\le e^\alpha W_B(h,n),
\qquad
\alpha=\frac{2(H+L)\beta}{b_{\min}}.
}
\tag{9}
\]

This holds before normalization, including for arbitrarily rare \(h\).

Both models have a common conditional mean bound

\[
\mu_*=\frac4g\left(H+\frac m2\right).
\]

If \(L\ge\mu_*/t_0\), their conditional tail probabilities are at most \(t_0\). Equation (9) then gives

\[
\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
P_{\widehat B}(\cdot\mid h),P_B(\cdot\mid h)
\right)
\le2t_0+\tanh\alpha.
\tag{10}
\]

The source normalization is also controlled:

\[
\left|
\log\frac{P_{\widehat B}(h)}{P_B(h)}
\right|
\le
\frac{2m\beta}{g}
+\alpha-\log(1-t_0).
\tag{11}
\]

For example, take

\[
t_0=\frac{\varepsilon}{8},\qquad
L=\left\lceil\frac{8\mu_*}{\varepsilon}\right\rceil,
\]

and

\[
\beta=
\min\left\{
\frac{g}{4m},
\frac{b_{\min}}2,
\frac{\varepsilon b_{\min}}{8(H+L)},
\frac{\varepsilon g}{8m}
\right\}.
\]

Then the conditional TV error is at most \(\varepsilon/2\), and the herald probability has logarithmic relative error at most \(3\varepsilon/4\), on the calibration success event. A sufficient sample bound is

\[
\boxed{
K_{\rm cal}
=
O\!\left(
\frac{
\bigl(m+\log(1/\delta_{\rm cal})\bigr)(H+m)^2
}{
g^4b_{\min}^2\varepsilon^4
}
\right).
}
\tag{12}
\]

Finite detector resolution suffices. Gaussian tails bound the recorded coordinates by
\(R=O(\sqrt{g^{-1}\log(K_{\rm cal}m/\delta_{\rm cal})})\); rounding at resolution \(O(\beta/[m(R+1)])\) fits the covariance error budget. Dense covariance formation costs \(O(K_{\rm cal}m^2)\) arithmetic operations.

Known pure loss of efficiency \(\kappa>0\) in the **calibration detector** can also be included:

\[
\Sigma_{\rm obs}=(1-\kappa)I+\kappa\Sigma.
\]

Undoing it increases the sufficient sample bound by \(\kappa^{-2}\). This statement concerns calibration copies, not loss in the heralded resource experiment.

### The guarantee concerns the pure state, too

All conditional Fock amplitudes are nonnegative in the promised phase convention:

\[
|\psi_{B,h}\rangle
=
\sum_n\sqrt{P_B(n\mid h)}\,|n\rangle.
\]

Hence conditional TV error \(\eta\) implies

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\left\|
|\psi_{B,h}\rangle\langle\psi_{B,h}|
-
|\psi_{\widehat B,h}\rangle\langle\psi_{\widehat B,h}|
\right\|_1
\le\sqrt{2\eta}.
}
\tag{13}
\]

Requesting a sufficiently small polynomial accuracy therefore learns a compact classical description of the **actual pure accepted state**, in trace distance, without obtaining accepted copies.

### A necessary limitation on acquisition

The lower bound on positive couplings cannot generally be removed. Consider

\[
B_{\theta,v}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&\theta v_1&\theta v_2\\
\theta v_1&0&0\\
\theta v_2&0&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \|v\|=1,\quad v>0.
\]

Heralding one photon in the first mode produces exactly

\[
v_1|10\rangle+v_2|01\rangle,
\]

with probability \((1-\theta^2)\theta^2\). Two fixed, distinct choices of \(v\) give substantially different accepted states, while their upstream overlap is \(1-\Theta(\theta^2)\). Even an unrestricted quantum measurement needs \(\Omega(\theta^{-2})\) preparations to distinguish them.

Thus the theorem removes an additional exponential conditioning penalty when elementary couplings are controlled. It does not make arbitrarily faint unknown signals free to learn.

## 6. Relative fidelity is also computable

For two supplied positive source models \(B_j=(A_j,V_j;V_j^T,C_j)\), with a common remaining-mode basis, define

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_1&=A_1+V_1C_2(I-C_1C_2)^{-1}V_1^T,\\
D_2&=A_2+V_2C_1(I-C_2C_1)^{-1}V_2^T,\\
E_{12}&=V_1(I-C_2C_1)^{-1}V_2^T.
\end{aligned}
\]

Repeat their respective herald labels in

\[
K_{12}=
\begin{pmatrix}
D_1&E_{12}\\
E_{12}^T&D_2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Gaussian overlap gives the normalized squared fidelity

\[
\boxed{
F_{12}
=
\frac{
\sqrt{\det(I-C_1^2)\det(I-C_2^2)}
}{
\det(I-C_1C_2)
}
\frac{
\operatorname{haf}(K_{12})^2
}{
\operatorname{haf}(K_{11})\operatorname{haf}(K_{22})
}.
}
\tag{14}
\]

All three hafnians are positive matching sums. Their sizes are \(H_1+H_2\), \(2H_1\) and \(2H_2\). This supplies relative fidelity accuracy in polynomial bit time, without inverse fidelity or inverse herald probability. Zero overlap is detected exactly.

This is relative accuracy in \(F\). It does not imply relative accuracy in an exponentially small infidelity \(1-F\), and source descriptions learned to finite trace accuracy give corresponding additive fidelity uncertainty.

## 7. Strongest alternatives, executed checks, and limits

The closest existing methods substantially constrain the novelty claim.

**Quesada–Arrazola already provide sequential conditional Gaussian boson sampling.** Their exact algorithm costs exponentially in generated photon number. Their nonnegative-kernel discussion uses random hafnian estimators with a weaker approximation statement than a uniform relative FPRAS. The new construction keeps the matching dimension at \(2H\), independently of generated occupation. 

**Anand and colleagues already give polynomial sampling for the collision-free graph subset distribution**
\[
P(S)\propto c^{2|S|}\operatorname{PM}(G[S])^2.
\]
Their doubled-graph construction has a polynomial near-perfect/perfect matching ratio. Arbitrary specified heralds remove the slack edges used by that argument; removing all such edges leaves two copies of a general matching problem. Their theorem therefore does not itself provide the arbitrary rare-herald guarantee developed here. Some special cases of my construction are bipartite and already accessible through permanent approximation. 

**Gagatsos–Guha already supply analytic heralded-state, yield and fidelity formulas.** I am not claiming those observables or Gaussian integral methods as inventions. The result here is their constructive positive matching-and-walk realization, the herald-independent energy argument, and the acquisition guarantee after rare conditioning. Historical priority for the full composition has not been established. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="8"}
