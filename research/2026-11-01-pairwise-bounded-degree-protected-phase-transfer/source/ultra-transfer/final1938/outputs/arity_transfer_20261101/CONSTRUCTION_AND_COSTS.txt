What the new consequence actually says
=======================================
Conditional on the preserved Tanner density/phase candidate, the transfer
theorem supplies a static quantum-memory GENERATOR with a uniform finite
primitive alphabet:

  one-qubit fields;
  two-qubit P tensor X couplings, P in {X,Y,Z};
  XXX couplings on controller qubits only;
  one-qubit lowering loss.

Every qubit meets at most THREE coupling terms, every coupling has arity
<=3, and the ordinary interaction-graph degree is <=3. Original DATA
qubits themselves need only pairwise couplings, at most one per Pauli
type. All controller rates, fields and couplings are finite and independent
of system size and storage time. They need not be comparable in size.

The direct positive certificate includes all mediator states, finite
response, physical local faults, and the ordinary data output decoder.
All k=Omega(n) encoded coordinates, both CSS sectors, arbitrary initial
controller/logical/bath/reference correlations and a shared finite bath
are retained. There is no supplied controller ground state, physical
frame-changing circuit, global recovery clock, or hidden timed reset.

The contribution is a protected-memory certificate transfer, with an
explicit bounded-primitive consequence. A globally accurate simulation
of the table Hamiltonian or of every many-body observable is not proved.

The price: the table becomes a large network
============================================
Let c=Delta^2, R_0=c+2c^2 and h=R_0+c. The original branch-controller
Hamiltonian has at most (8/c)4^h n Pauli monomials. The conservative bound
uses an arbitrary h-qubit Hermitian Pauli expansion; actual sparsity
could improve it, but has not been acquired for a useful Tanner family.

For one weight-s monomial, balanced arity reduction uses s-3 mediators
and s-2 final three-body couplers. It takes <=ceil(log_2(s-2)) stages.
For m resulting nontrivial couplings, port isolation adds <=3m qubits.
Degree reduction adds <=9m further qubits and takes at most twice the
logarithm of the old endpoint degree in additional stages. These are
actual private mediator vertices, one-body fields, losses, and listed
couplers, rather than a declaration that a large table is one operation.

The deliberately loose total is

 N_phys <=[9+13h (8/c)4^h] n.                                      (C1)

It is linear in n after the code constants are fixed, but exponentially
large in a support parameter which was already enormous. Rates, distance,
logical-error exponent and thresholds measured per TOTAL physical qubit
therefore have extremely poor constants. No compact table, practical
block, useful threshold, or small hardware overhead has been found.

The uniform coupling-degree bound is independent of that table size;
the number and coefficients of all the low-degree modules still carry
its information. Offline code acquisition, local truth-table construction,
Pauli decomposition, calibration specification and exact parameter choice
may require astronomical computation/storage. None is claimed delivered
for a concrete useful Tanner block. Generic decomposition is constructive
mathematically and executable on finite examples, not economically efficient.

Finite scales and noise precision
=================================
At a subdivision stage the target hP is generated by

 Omega n_a +sqrt(|h|Omega/2)(A-sign(h)B)X_a,

with one-body loss rate Gamma, Omega=epsilon^-2. The fork uses the same
form with K=(P-aX_b-bX_c)/sqrt(2) and the PAID counterterm a b X_bX_c.
All scalar shifts can be dropped; no nonlocal counterterm is hidden.

TRANSFER_THEOREM.txt (13)-(17) chooses, in order: the new excitation
weight lambda, a small allowed source eta, a finite loss Gamma large
enough to absorb excited-controller blocks, then a sufficiently large
detuning Omega so loss dressing and all remaining local errors fit the
source margin. At later stages the previous actual strengths/rates enter
the constants. Thus scale hierarchies may grow extremely badly with the
number of stages. No polynomial bound in h or realistic bandwidth is claimed.

Fault strength is sum of ACTUAL local-generator diamond norms <=delta n,
including all data and controller hardware. For N_phys=nu n, ordinary
bounded-incidence per-device faults of strength f require a bound of order
nu f<=delta, with the actual incidence multiplier included. In particular:
  detuning error DeltaOmega costs <=2|DeltaOmega|;
  Pauli coupler error Deltag costs <=2|Deltag|;
  sqrt(Gamma)(sigma^-+E) instead of sqrt(Gamma)sigma^- costs
       <=Gamma(4||E||+2||E||^2);
  upward loss-basis excitation rate gamma_up costs <=2gamma_up per qubit.
Every channel and term is counted. Relative frequency/coupler precision
can be tiny because the required nominal numbers are large. An error
tolerance per original table entry is not a budget for the whole network.

For local thermal-gain models, sum_i 2nu_i gamma_up,i must fit delta,
including old controllers. Using gamma_up,i/Gamma_i=exp(-hbar omega_i/k_B T)
translates this to a conditional temperature requirement. It is not a
microscopic derivation of a bath for the full interacting Hamiltonian.
The modeled one-body loss, its bandwidth, parasitic couplings, heat removal
and pump stability must be physically qualified for the intended duration.

Response, reset activity, energy, and fault spread
==================================================
No controller is treated as instantaneous. Its strong detuning and finite
loss are part of the actual joint generator. The proof bounds occupied
and coherent mediator sectors, rather than assuming their disappearance.
Miscalibration and mixed data/controller/bath faults are charged on the
physical side before the analytical frame. Its finite local cones give
an explicit, n-independent fault-spread allowance Q_final. This allowance
can be huge and increases the minimum block size needed for 2r+Q_final<d.

For controller j, let M_j be the sum of absolute strengths of all physical
Pauli terms which contain X or Y on j, including any transverse one-body
field. Fields commuting with n_j do not enter M_j. Its Hamiltonian
excitation current is off-diagonal in j
and bounded by Young's inequality as

 i[H,n_j] <=(Gamma_j/2)n_j+(2M_j^2/Gamma_j)I.

Total trace preservation, locality and sum||V_a||<=delta n imply
||V^*(sum_j n_j)||<=2qdelta n, even for signed individual terms. Hence
the nominal integrated lowering activity obeys

 sum_j Gamma_j integral_0^T <n_j>dt
 <=2sum_j<n_j(0)>+[4sum_j M_j^2/Gamma_j+4qdelta n]T.                (C2)

There is also the ceiling T sum_j Gamma_j. For identifiable physical
lowering channels this counts expected emitted reset quanta. Under signed
jump-calibration differences it is a nominal activity metric until the
actual unraveling is specified. Worst-case initial excitations are paid
by the first term; their purity is not free. At most three couplings per
qubit makes M_j explicitly finite, but not necessarily small.

The device requires ongoing entropy removal and reservoirs/pumps for the
WHOLE storage interval. A finite controller register does not imply a
closed device with finite one-time energy/entropy capacity. If a qualified
device supplies an energy cost per reset and idle power, (C2) and the
physical-qubit count allow those costs to be charged. Those values and
thermodynamic/microscopic consistency are not acquired here. Bare gap
energy per emission alone does not determine wall-plug work for these
noncommuting, nonequilibrium interactions. No passive Gibbs-memory or
free refrigeration claim follows.

The final graph has bounded degree but remains long-range when embedded
at fixed density in ordinary low-dimensional space. Three-body arity and
three-neighbor graph degree do not bound wire lengths, propagation times,
or bus errors. No planar, 2D/3D geometric, or finite-speed networking
implementation has been supplied. Physical latency cannot be folded
into an uncharged constant as n grows in such an embedding.

Useful failures that determined the framework
==============================================
1. Overlapping subdivision gadgets have NONZERO order-one cross-controller
Hamiltonians. Their ground block is zero; their full operator is not small.
Discarding them produces an unjustified transfer. Our damping certificate
absorbs them. The exact two-gadget control checks this distinction.
2. Conjugated damping has a first-order Gamma epsilon correction. Dropping
it also loses the claim. Its zero-ground block and Schur charge appear
explicitly in (13). Making a detuning large is insufficient on its own.
3. A fork needs its a b X_bX_c counterterm. The exact algebra control
fails the desired ground Hamiltonian if that term is omitted.
4. Repeating a fork directly increases degree at old fork vertices.
Fresh subdivisions before every pairing round prevent that accumulation.
The structural control checks the resulting degree at EVERY vertex.
5. The generic argument presently stops at THREE-body Hamiltonians.
A tempting third-order three-to-two gadget has a conditional old-system
term of order epsilon^-2 C n_a. Bounding that term with the current
small-lambda excitation moment forces Gamma lambda to that scale,
while transformed-loss source includes Gamma epsilon^2. The present
hierarchy then cannot make the source arbitrarily small by decreasing
epsilon. This is a failure of this certificate/transfer argument, not a
two-body impossibility theorem. A different conditional/erasure moment
or a different controller may help, but no two-body-only phase is claimed.

two noncommuting weight-four target terms, and one-body loss. The physical
