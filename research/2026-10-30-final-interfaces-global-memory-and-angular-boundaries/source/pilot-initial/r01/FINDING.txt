WHEN ACOUSTIC-ZERO LIFTING IS COMPLETE, AND WHY HEAT CORRECTIONS ARE NOT
30 October 2026

The consequential question is whether the acquired angular machinery can
turn rank-one information into a complete quasiconvexity mechanism. This
tranche establishes a restricted completeness theorem and two precise
limits. It does not settle planar Morrey or the unresolved curved arc.

The main limit is already inside the supplied 2x2 quartic family: an
explicit polyconvex quartic admits NO global exact-form certificate for
ordinary heat flow, regardless of the degree or representation of the
primitive. Changing to any constant-coefficient uniformly parabolic
linear flow cannot repair this particular example. Ordinary polyconvexity
proves its quasiconvexity immediately.

The constructive theorem identifies where the acoustic-zero lift itself
is complete. At an isolated matrix zero with quadratic nondegeneracy, a
strict lift exists exactly when the tangent Schur biquadratic form has a
positive definite Gram matrix in the canonical first-order variables.
This is automatic if either the parameter dimension or corank is at most
two. Thus, under the stated hypotheses, the two-dimensional canonical
matrix problem in the supplied case has no further local lifting
obstruction. Acquiring a closed correction and proving positivity away
from the zeros remain separate obligations.

The attempted extension to arbitrary dimensions fails. An explicit
strictly elliptic 3x3 quadratic symbol has no analytic factorization with
a strictly positive central lift of any finite size. Its associated
quadratic gradient energy is nevertheless quasiconvex by Fourier analysis.
The exact SOS/polyconvex transition of this one-parameter example is
computed below. This is a Choi-type obstruction, not a new Morrey example.


1. AN EXACT-FORM OBSTRUCTION IN THE ORIGINAL 2x2 FAMILY

Write T=|A|^2 and q=det A. Consider

                 F(A)=F_(0,2048)(A)=T^2+8188 q^2.             (1)

This is polyconvex: (A,z) -> |A|^4+8188 z^2 is convex. Hence it is
quasiconvex. This proof uses the literal coefficients in (1), without any
angular primitive or certificate acquisition.

Use normalized averages on the flat torus [0,2 pi]^2. Put

 X=x+sin y,
 v_h,1=x+(1-h) sin y,
 v_h,2=y+[1-h(1+cos^2 y)] sin X-h sin y cos X.                (2)

This is a smooth affine map with a periodic displacement. It can be
derived from the area-preserving composition of shears

                 w=(x+sin y, y+sin(x+sin y))

by setting v_h=w+h Delta(w-(x,y)). The parameter h specifies an initial
map; it is NOT the actual time along the subsequent heat flow.

Starting at v_h, let v_h,t=(x,y)+exp(t Delta)(v_h-(x,y)). Exact integration
gives, at actual time t=0,

 d/dt <|Dv_h,t|^4>
 =-(2304559 h^4-3189560 h^3+2786384 h^2-953024 h+144768)/4096,

 d/dt <(det Dv_h,t)^2>
 =-h(955 h^3+576 h^2+544 h-64)/128.                           (3)

For h=1/10 these derivatives are respectively

       -743703359/40960000,            577/256000.

Therefore (1) has the strictly positive heat derivative

       d/dt <F(Dv_h,t)>|_(t=0)=12212801/40960000 > 0.          (4)

More generally the SAME map excludes ordinary heat dissipation for
F_(0,r) whenever

                    r > 744072639/369280.

This bound is a sufficient witness range, not the exact boundary of the
heat-dissipating cone. No conclusion about the two accepted anisotropic
boundary seeds follows from this witness.

The executable derivation in heat_obstruction.py uses

 S=sin y, C=cos y, J=sin X, K=cos X,
 partial_x=K partial_J-J partial_K,
 partial_y=C partial_S-S partial_C+C partial_x.

These commuting physical differential operators act on polynomials.
The change x -> X is a translation for each fixed y and preserves torus
measure. Odd circle moments vanish, and the even moments are exactly

 <sin^(2a) cos^(2b)>=(2a)!(2b)!/[4^(a+b) a! b! (a+b)!].

Thus (3)-(4) are exact rational calculations, not quadrature. The program
also reconstructs the Hessian density directly and verifies independently

 <sum_j F''(Dv_h)[partial_j Dv_h,partial_j Dv_h]>
                         =-12212801/40960000 < 0.             (5)

Indeed the second derivative of q^2 in direction B is
2(cof A:B)^2+4q det B; this convention is used explicitly in that check.

Suppose any global exact two-form Omega=d alpha made

 sum_j F''(A)[B_j,B_j]+Omega_A(B_1,B_2) >= 0

for every A and compatible Hessian pair B_1,B_2. Assume enough regularity
to pull back the form and use Stokes, as holds for the supplied acquired
primitives. The periodic gradient map A(x,y)=Dv_h(x,y) pulls back Omega
to an exact form on the torus, so its integral is zero. Integrating the
pointwise inequality contradicts (5). Therefore NO such primitive exists.
This excludes polynomial primitives of every degree and all regular
angular P/T profiles, not merely the supplied ten-parameter ansatz.

The example does not contradict the Bruno-Pasqualotto invariant quartic
classification. Their Appendix A explicitly proves the boundary part by
heat flow and adds the nonnegative det^2 part by polyconvexity. The
supplied proof makes the same distinction. The present calculation gives
a literal finite witness that the distinction cannot be removed by
enlarging the correction ansatz.

Constant linear diffusion does not repair (1).

Let L be a real, constant-coefficient second-order linear operator on
two-component maps,

 (Lu)_alpha=sum_(beta,i,j) a_(alpha,beta,i,j) partial_i partial_j u_beta,

with uniformly positive symmetric principal symbol. If its energy
derivative were nonpositive for EVERY smooth affine-plus-periodic map,
the same would hold for every conjugate of L under signed permutations
of the output and spatial coordinates: (1) is invariant under independent
orthogonal output and domain changes, and these permutations preserve the
torus. The energy derivative is linear in the generator L. Average all
these conjugates. Sign changes eliminate off-diagonal coefficients, and
coordinate swaps equalize the diagonal ones. The averaged operator is

           c Delta I,  c=(sum_(alpha,i) a_(alpha,alpha,i,i))/4 > 0.

It would therefore dissipate F for every initial map. Equation (4)
contradicts this. Consequently no such uniformly parabolic constant
linear diffusion universally dissipates (1). A different nonlinear flow,
a static decomposition such as polyconvexity, or another mechanism is
required; merely changing constant diffusion coefficients is insufficient.


2. A CONSTRUCTIVE LOCAL COMPLETENESS THEOREM FOR THE LIFT

Let y in R^d. After a constant invertible congruence, let a symmetric
polynomial matrix be partitioned as

                C(y)=[ A(y)  B(y)  ],
                     [ B(y)^T H(y) ]

where A is k by k, H is r by r, and

 A(0)=0, DA(0)=0, B(0)=0, H0=H(0)>0.                         (6)

Here k is the corank. For r=0 omit H and B. These finite jet conditions
follow if C is positive semidefinite in a full neighborhood, has corank k
at zero, and its kernel has been placed in the first k coordinates. The
theorem below can instead take (6) as directly checked input; local
positivity need not be assumed.

Let A2 and B1 be the homogeneous degree-two and degree-one Taylor parts.
Define the tangent Schur matrix and its biquadratic form by

 S2(y)=A2(y)-B1(y) H0^(-1) B1(y)^T,
                       q(y,z)=z^T S2(y) z, z in R^k.         (7)

Set E(y)=y tensor I_k, a dk by k matrix, and

                       D(y)=diag(E(y),I_r).

Theorem. There is a symmetric polynomial M(y) such that

                C(y)=D(y)^T M(y) D(y),  M(0)>0              (8)

if and only if there is a positive definite dk by dk Gram matrix P with

                       S2(y)=E(y)^T P E(y).                 (9)

In particular, (8) implies C>=0 on some full neighborhood of zero. The
strict positivity is in the lift and retains the actual kernel of C.

Proof and acquisition formula.

Define k by k blocks G_ij and k by r blocks R_i by exact polynomial
integrals in an auxiliary scalar t:

 G_ij(y)=integral_0^1 (1-t) partial_i partial_j A(t y) dt,
 R_i(y)=integral_0^1 partial_i B(t y) dt.

Let G be the dk by dk block matrix and R the stacked dk by r matrix.
Taylor's integral formula gives, identically as polynomials,

                    A=E^T G E,      B=E^T R.                (10)

Both operations divide monomial coefficients by positive integers; no
functional or pointwise selection oracle is used. Set G0=G(0), R0=R(0).
Then P0=G0-R0 H0^(-1) R0^T is a possibly indefinite Gram matrix of S2.
For any P satisfying (9), the constant symmetric matrix N=P-P0 obeys
E^T N E=0. Thus

                   M=[ G+N  R ]
                     [ R^T  H ]

still satisfies (8), and its Schur complement at zero is P>0. Since
H0>0, M(0)>0, and positivity persists by continuity. This proves
sufficiency and constructs the actual polynomial identity.

Conversely, partition a canonical M into these same block sizes.
Equation (8) fixes the lower block as H. Its off-diagonal block R has
B1=E^T R(0), and its upper block G has A2=E^T G(0) E. The positive
definite Schur complement of M(0) is exactly a Gram P as in (9).
This proves necessity. No positivity oracle or missing division is used.

Corollary: automatic lifting in low dimensions.

Assume the finite jets (6) and quadratic nondegeneracy

                 q(y,z)>=c |y|^2 |z|^2, c>0.               (11)

If min(d,k)<=2, (8) always exists. For k=1, (9) is simply the positive
definite scalar Hessian matrix, so the assertion is elementary and
self-contained. For a factor of dimension two, apply Calderon's
classical theorem that every nonnegative 2-by-n biquadratic form is a
sum of squares of bilinear forms. Subtract (c/2)|y|^2|z|^2 from q,
obtain a positive semidefinite Gram by that theorem, then add (c/2)I.
The resulting Gram is strictly positive and (8) follows constructively.

With rational C and a rational zero/kernel basis, all Taylor data are
rational. The Gram constraint is a rational affine linear system. If it
has a strictly positive real solution, rational solutions are dense in
that affine space, so a rational positive solution exists. Exact rational
enumeration plus principal-minor testing will find one under this promise.
This is a halting statement, not a practical acquisition complexity bound.
Algebraic zeros can instead be handled with specified real algebraic
number arithmetic; they are not rationalized for free.

Finite-cover consequence.

Suppose a polynomial C on a compact rational box is positive definite
except at finitely many interior algebraic points, with the finite jets
(6) and strict Gram condition (9) at each point. Each point has a local
polynomial lift with M>0. Away from these neighborhoods C has a positive
margin. On sufficiently small rational boxes, the tensor Bernstein
coefficient matrices of the appropriate M or C are positive definite:
as the box shrinks each coefficient converges to the matrix value.
Compactness gives a finite cover. Exact real algebraic arithmetic can
verify its coefficients; when the acquired lifts have rational
coefficients, the usual rational receiver suffices. The theorem does not
claim rational grouped-zero lifts always cost the same as algebraic ones,
or that all zeros or their kernels can be acquired efficiently.

Comparison with ordinary Schur analysis.

The strongest ordinary local competitor is exactly the Schur complement
A-B H^(-1)B^T. It proves the same neighborhood positivity from (11).
The lift is an explicit polynomial compilation compatible with Bernstein
verification, not a stronger positivity theorem or a demonstrated speedup.
In low dimensions it removes a local representation uncertainty; it does
not supply the globally closed correction or determine its coefficients.

Connection to the supplied seed.

The B1 receiver was replayed directly: its actual 104-term primitive,
exterior derivative, symmetries, polynomial factorization, 129-box cover,
6,321 positive coefficient matrices, and strict non-PC cuts passed their
literal exact checks. This is an internal replay, not independent external
review. The accepted scope of B0 and of the local arc is retained.

For B1, the zero coordinates (w,g) are simple: the exact discriminant of
g is 198319028988377799684, and resultant_d(g,w(0,d)) is 18800896000000.
Since det d(w,g)/d(b,d)=2b g'(d), neither factor can vanish at a common
zero. The supplied strictly positive local M then gives a positive
quadratic Schur form in (w,g), hence also in local (b,d) coordinates.
The local theorem therefore explains why an isolated nondegenerate
corank-one acoustic zero can be resolved without a special miracle.
It does NOT infer that all rank-one-convex quartics have this closed
primitive, this zero geometry, or positivity away from the zeros.

An additional exact example in lift_criterion.py acquires a 4x4 lift for
a 3x3 matrix with corank one. Its identity holds as polynomials; all nine
tensor Bernstein coefficient matrices are strictly positive on
[-1/2,1/2]^2. Its tangent Schur Gram is diag(1,3). Replacing its scalar
Schur function by x^4+y^4 makes the canonical central lift singular.
Thus the quadratic nondegeneracy hypothesis has substantive content.


3. THE HIGHER-DIMENSIONAL FAILURE AND ITS EXACT TRANSITION

For x in R^3 define

 C0(x)=[ x1^2+2x3^2   -x1x2       -x1x3      ],
       [ -x1x2        x2^2+2x1^2 -x2x3      ],
       [ -x1x3        -x2x3       x3^2+2x2^2 ],

                    C_e(x)=C0(x)+e |x|^2 I3, e>=0.           (12)

All principal minors of C0 are nonnegative polynomials in squares; its
determinant is

 4(x1^4 x2^2+x2^4 x3^2+x3^4 x1^2+x1^2 x2^2 x3^2).

Therefore C0>=0 and C_e>=e |x|^2 I. For e>0 the origin is an isolated,
quadratically nondegenerate corank-three zero. The same positivity that
suffices for corank one is now insufficient for lifting.

Theorem. The biquadratic q_e(x,z)=z^T C_e(x) z is an SOS of bilinear
forms if and only if

                    e >= e_*=(2 sqrt(7)-5)/3.               (13)

Its canonical Gram can be positive definite if and only if e>e_*.

Necessity without any Gram symmetry assumption.

If q_e=sum_l (sum_(i,j) V_ij,l x_i z_j)^2, regard V_ij as vectors.
Write d_i=V_ii and o_ij=V_ij. Coefficient comparison gives

 ||d_i||^2=1+e,
 d_i dot d_j+o_ij dot o_ji=-1, i<j.

For each opposite off-diagonal pair, its two squared norms are 2+e and
e. Hence o_ij dot o_ji >=-sqrt(e(2+e)), and

 0<=||d1+d2+d3||^2
           =-3+3e-2 sum_(i<j) o_ij dot o_ji
           <=-3+3e+6 sqrt(e(2+e)).                           (14)

For 0<=e<1 this requires 3e^2+10e-1>=0, giving (13). For e>=1 the
same lower threshold is automatic. At e=e_* equality in (14) forces
d1+d2+d3=0, so no Gram on all nine monomials can be positive definite.

Sufficiency and the exact convex-minor decomposition.

On the three diagonal monomials choose the Gram with diagonal 1+e and
off-diagonal -a. On each opposite pair choose the Gram

                       [ 2+e  -b ],
                       [ -b    e ].

Set a=(1+e)/2 and b=(1-e)/2. The diagonal block is
(1+e)(3I-11^T)/2>=0. Each pair block is PSD exactly when
(3e^2+10e-1)/4>=0. The cross coefficient is correct since a+b=1.
This gives an SOS for every e>=e_*. Adding (e-e_*) times the identity
Gram to a boundary Gram gives a positive definite Gram for e>e_*.
At e=1/10 an entirely rational strictly positive Gram is obtained with
a=109/200, b=91/200; the code verifies its full exact identity.

In particular at e=1/16,

 (1-e)^2-4e(2+e)=93/256>0,

so q_e is strictly positive on nonzero products but is NOT a bilinear
SOS. Suppose any finite analytic factorization C_e=D^T M D existed
near zero with M(0)>0. Since C_e(0)=0, necessarily D(0)=0. Its degree-two
Taylor part would then be D1(x)^T M(0) D1(x), making q_e a sum of squares
of bilinear forms. This contradiction excludes EVERY finite analytic
D and strictly positive central M, not just the canonical tensor lift.
An analytic invertible congruence cannot fix the obstruction: its leading
term only makes an invertible change of the z variables.

The associated ordinary gradient energy.

For a real 3x3 matrix A define the quadratic form

 G_e(A)=sum_i Aii^2-2 sum_(i<j) Aii Ajj
                     +2(A13^2+A21^2+A32^2)+e |A|^2.        (15)

On A=z tensor x it equals q_e(x,z). Fourier/Plancherel therefore gives,
for every compactly supported smooth phi and every constant M,

 integral [G_e(M+Dphi)-G_e(M)]
     =integral G_e(Dphi)>=e integral |Dphi|^2.

Thus all e>=0 are quasiconvex, and e>0 gives a quadratic strong modulus.
No polynomial lift is needed. This is the strongest ordinary approach
with the same information and operations for this quadratic problem.

For completeness, (15) is polyconvex exactly for e>=e_*. The constructed
Gram gives a convex quadratic H_e on the nine matrix entries and

 G_e(A)=H_e(A)-2b sum_(i<j)(Aii Ajj-Aij Aji),                 (16)

so sufficiency is explicit. Conversely, a convex minor lift has a support
at the minor vector of zero. That point is interior to the convex hull of
the minor graph: any linear functional nonnegative on the graph has zero
linear coefficients (test small signed matrices), zero 2-minor
coefficients (test matrices with two nonzero entries in distinct rows and
columns), and zero determinant coefficient (test signed diagonal matrices).
A support therefore implies G_e(A)>=lambda:minor_2(A)+mu det A.
Scaling A with both signs forces mu=0. Subtracting the quadratic null
Lagrangian leaves a globally nonnegative quadratic H, hence a sum of
squares of linear matrix forms. On rank-one matrices the minors vanish,
which would give a bilinear SOS for q_e. Necessity follows from (13).

This comparison separates three different facts: the energy is
quasiconvex; its acoustic symbol is pointwise positive away from zero;
and that symbol may still have no strictly positive analytic lift. It
does not identify (12) with the special compatible-Hessian density of a
planar quartic. Extending a failure from general polynomial matrices to
that special class would require a further construction.


4. WHAT REMAINS AND HOW TO REPLAY

The failed universal claim was that positivity plus acoustic-zero
factorization should be enough in arbitrary families. The repaired
mechanism uses the tangent Schur Gram and the exact Taylor construction.
Its low-dimensional completeness is conditional on acquired finite jets
and quadratic nondegeneracy. The higher-dimensional counterexample shows
that those hypotheses cannot simply be discarded.

The further obstruction (1)-(5) shows why a full quasiconvexity
classification cannot be reduced to finding richer exact corrections for
one constant linear flow. The source's hybrid use of ordinary
polyconvexity is structurally necessary for at least some members of the
family. This is a method obstruction, not a failure of quasiconvexity.

The central unresolved obligation for the consequential planar problem
is still to acquire a legitimate closed correction or a different
global mechanism for the unclassified rank-one-convex quartics, and to
establish positivity on the whole matrix space. The local theorem does
not provide that correction. The heat witness lies far above the curved
boundary and leaves the accepted non-polyconvex seeds and arc untouched.
No physical capability or historical-priority claim is made.

Both new verifiers use exact rational polynomial arithmetic and SymPy:

 python3 -B heat_obstruction.py
 python3 -B lift_criterion.py

They create only HEAT_EXACT.txt and EXACT_RESULTS.txt in this directory.
No numerical solver, equipment, new primitive-selection oracle, or
production source modification is used. The finite tests support the
displayed identities and witnesses; the proofs above carry the general
quantifiers. No general acquisition or computational advantage is claimed.

Theorems of Calderon and Choi are classical inputs. The argument here
combines them with elementary Schur/Taylor algebra, a computed exact
transition, and a literal periodic heat witness. Related matrix local-
global and Hessian positivity theorems already exist; novelty of the
present formulation is NOT established by this tranche.
