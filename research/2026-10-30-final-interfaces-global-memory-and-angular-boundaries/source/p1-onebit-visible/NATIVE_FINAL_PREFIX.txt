## 1. The question I chose

The new question is:

> **Can one persistent specimen supply a reusable experimental interface whose complete observable behavior approximates a fresh-specimen interface, without identifying or resetting its hidden state?**

This is a broader target than convergence of one optimizer. If the interface works, an experimenter can choose a new command after seeing each result, stop according to the data, or begin a different task. The guarantee should survive those choices.

The reference interface is deliberately modest. For a query \(u\), let its unknown, fixed incremental response be

\[
x=f_\theta(u),\qquad |x|\le U.
\]

For the original linear class, this could be one selected scalar component of \(Au\). The desired reference response is a bit \(D\in\{-1,+1\}\) satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D=+1\mid u)=\frac12+\frac{f_\theta(u)}{4U}.
}
\]

The unknown response is encoded in a probability. Repeated bits provide information about it, and a downstream algorithm can adapt its queries.

The construction concerns **that binary interface**. It does not provide a fresh physical state, a full analog measurement, or an arbitrary vector of simultaneous measurements.

## 2. The physical operation

### The retained physical assumptions

For one active interrogation, the measured scalar field has the form

\[
y=h+s\,x,
\]

where:

- \(h\) is the old field generated by the specimen’s retained state;
- \(x=f_\theta(u)\) is the fixed incremental response to the chosen command;
- \(s\in\{-1,+1\}\) is a fresh fair sign, drawn after the command is chosen.

The old field may depend on every previous command, sign and measurement. It must be determined before the current fresh sign. The fixed additive-response decomposition remains a substantive assumption.

I use a new energy symbol, \(J\), for the energy cap of **one active interrogation window**. This avoids silently identifying it with the handoff’s energy cap \(S\) per forward/adjoint pair.

Let \(E_i\) denote stored energy before calendar window \(i\), and let \(e_i\) bound the squared scalar old field:

\[
h_i^2\le e_i.
\]

The required passive balance is

\[
\mathbb E[E_{i+1}\mid\mathcal H_i]
\le E_i-e_i+Jp_i,
\]

where \(\mathcal H_i\) includes the complete history and hidden physical state before the current activity draw, and \(p_i\) is its fresh activity probability.

This balance follows, for example, from a passive linear scattering system: averaging over the current fresh sign removes the cross term between the old field and the new response.

### The readout

Choose a comparator threshold range

\[
R=2U.
\]

For an active query:

1. Choose the command \(u\) from the previously returned record.
2. Draw a fresh private sign \(s\), and apply \(su\).
3. Draw a fresh independent threshold \(T\), uniformly distributed on \([-R,R]\).
4. Obtain the comparator bit
   \[
   C=\operatorname{sgn}(h+sx-T).
   \]
5. Return
   \[
   \boxed{D=sC.}
   \]

The downstream algorithm receives \(D\), together with the permitted clock information. It does not receive the private sign, threshold, raw comparator result or hidden field.

The comparator is an actual bounded-output measurement operation. No precise analog value is first read and then rounded in software.

Random-threshold one-bit sensing, including affine outcome probabilities, has established primary precedents. Wang, Ishwar and Saligrama explicitly construct comparators whose binary outcome probability is affine in the analog input. Random chopping for offset compensation also has a long history. Those ingredients are existing machinery; the result developed here concerns their conditional-history behavior, passive-memory error and composition across adaptive experiments. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

## 3. Why one bit changes the result

Define

\[
m_R(z)=\operatorname{clip}(z/R,-1,1).
\]

A uniform threshold gives

\[
\mathbb E[C\mid y]=m_R(y).
\]

Averaging over the fresh sign therefore gives

\[
\mathbb E[D\mid h,x]
=
\frac{m_R(h+x)-m_R(h-x)}{2}.
\]

Whenever both possible fields lie inside the comparator’s linear probability range,

\[
|h|+|x|\le R,
\]

this becomes

\[
\mathbb E[D\mid h,x]
=
\frac{(h+x)-(h-x)}{2R}
=
\frac{x}{R}.
\]

Since \(D\) has only two possible values,

\[
\boxed{
\Pr(D=+1\mid h,x)
=
\frac12+\frac{x}{2R}.
}
\]

The entire returned-bit distribution is independent of the hidden old field.

For \(R=2U\), this equality holds whenever \(|h|\le U\), uniformly over every allowed query with \(|x|\le U\).

That is the central mechanism: **choose an observation alphabet whose complete law is determined by the quantity that fresh randomization can cancel correctly.**

### Clipping is retained in the analysis

The controller cannot observe whether this condition held. It does not discard suspect bits.

Let \(\Delta_R(h,x)\) be the total-variation distance between the actual returned-bit law and the fresh reference law. For binary distributions, this is simply the difference between their probabilities of \(+1\).

Writing \(a=|x|\le U\), direct calculation gives

\[
\Delta_R(h,x)
=
\frac{1}{4R}
\min\!\left\{2a,\; (|h|-R+a)_+\right\}.
\]

This describes an attenuation of the signal encoded in the bit. A sufficiently large old field makes the returned bit fair; it does not require an overflow report or a fabricated replacement measurement.

At \(R=2U\),

\[
\Delta_{2U}(h,x)
\le
\frac{(|h|-U)_+}{8U}
\le
\boxed{\frac{h^2}{32U^2}}.
\]

The final inequality follows from

\[
(t-2)^2\ge0.
\]

The constant is sharp for this pointwise quadratic bound: equality occurs at \(|x|=U\), \(|h|=2U\).

The important consequence is that a potentially large, unobserved clipping event has a charge against **old-field energy**, rather than requiring the controller to recognize the event.

## 4. The whole-experiment theorem

Let \(P^\pi_N\) be the distribution of the record returned during the first \(N\) calendar windows when a controller \(\pi\) uses the persistent specimen.

Let \(Q^\pi_N\) be the corresponding reference experiment with:

- the same randomized activity clock;
- the same controller;
- the same permitted command set;
- a fresh binary response with probability
  \[
  \frac12+\frac{f_\theta(u)}{4U}
  \]
  at each active query.

The controller can choose its next command from its previous returned bits and clock information.

### Theorem

Under the stated response, randomness, sensor and passive-energy conditions,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}(P^\pi_N,Q^\pi_N)
\le
\min\!\left\{
1,\,
\frac{
p_1E_0+
J\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb E[p_i^2]
}{
32U^2
}
\right\}.
}
\]

This holds uniformly over the permitted adaptive controllers.

Deterministic nonincreasing \(p_i\) are sufficient. The same argument also permits predictable activity probabilities that are nonincreasing along every realized history.

### Proof

There are two parts.

First, the energy argument. Multiply the conditional balance by the predictable \(p_i\), take expectations, and sum:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb E[p_i e_i]
\le
\mathbb E\!\left[
\sum_{i=1}^N p_i(E_i-E_{i+1})
\right]
+
J\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb E[p_i^2].
\]

Pathwise summation by parts gives

\[
\sum_{i=1}^N p_i(E_i-E_{i+1})
=
p_1E_1-p_NE_{N+1}
+
\sum_{i=2}^N(p_i-p_{i-1})E_i
\le p_1E_0.
\]

Fresh activity gives

\[
\mathbb E[B_i e_i]=\mathbb E[p_i e_i].
\]

Consequently,

\[
\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb E[B_i h_i^2]
\le
p_1E_0+
J\sum_{i=1}^N\mathbb E[p_i^2].
\]

Second, compose the observation kernels. Conditional on any returned history, the actual next-bit law is a mixture over possible hidden states. Convexity of total variation and the one-bit bound give a conditional discrepancy at most

\[
\mathbb E\!\left[
\left.
\frac{B_i h_i^2}{32U^2}
\right|
\text{returned history}
\right].
\]

Replacing successive observation kernels and summing their discrepancies bounds the distance between the complete record laws. Substitution of the energy inequality proves the theorem.

No mixing time or last-warning time enters the proof.

### What the guarantee transfers

For any event determined by the returned experiment,

\[
\left|\Pr_P(\mathcal A)-\Pr_Q(\mathcal A)\right|
\le \varepsilon
\]

whenever the displayed bound is at most \(\varepsilon\).

Thus a reference procedure with failure probability \(\alpha\) has failure probability at most \(\alpha+\varepsilon\) on the persistent specimen. The event can include a data-dependent stopping rule or a sequence of subsequent tasks.

The quantifier matters: **every permitted controller has this guarantee**. It does not assert one physical event on which every counterfactual controller simultaneously receives correct answers.

This is a comparison of observable experiments, a well-established statistical framework. The construction supplies a particular physical reduction into that framework. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

## 5. The time, precision and operating costs

### A finite service of \(K\) queries

Use constant activity probability \(q\) until \(K\) active queries have occurred, then stop.

The expected number of calendar windows is

\[
\mathbb E[\tau_K]=\frac Kq.
\]

The stopped weighted-energy calculation gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}(P^\pi_K,Q^\pi_K)
\le
\frac{q(E_0+JK)}{32U^2}.
}
\]

Therefore, to allocate \(\varepsilon\) to memory effects, it is sufficient to choose

\[
q=
\min\!\left\{
1,\,
\frac{32U^2\varepsilon}{E_0+JK}
\right\}.
\]

The resulting expected duration is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\tau_K]
=
K\max\!\left\{
1,\,
\frac{E_0+JK}{32U^2\varepsilon}
\right\}.
}
\]

For large \(K\), this construction has quadratic calendar cost.

That is an upper bound for this implementation. **It is not a lower bound on all controllers or all ways to simulate the reference interface.**

A finite deadline is also available. For \(Nq>K\),

\[
\Pr(\tau_K>N)
=
\Pr(\operatorname{Binomial}(N,q)<K)
\le
\exp\!\left[
-\frac{(Nq-K)^2}{2Nq}
\right].
\]

This deadline includes an explicitly budgeted timeout probability. It does not identify a time after which the specimen has become clean.

### Finite threshold settings

A continuously uniform threshold is an ideal sensor model. I also analyzed a finite implementation.

Use \(M\) equally spaced midpoint thresholds on \([-R,R]\), selected uniformly. Its cumulative distribution differs from the continuous uniform distribution by at most

\[
\frac{1}{2M}.
\]

Consequently, a \(K\)-query experiment has an additional total-variation allowance of

\[
\boxed{\frac{K}{2M}.}
\]

This cost is retained below. One-bit output does not imply one-bit instrument complexity: the finite example uses **12 bits of threshold selection**, plus the actuator sign and activity-clock randomness.

### An indefinite-use corollary, with its actual scope

For the ideal continuous-threshold model, choose

\[
p_i=c\,i^{-a},
\qquad \frac12<a<1.
\]

Then

\[
\sum_i p_i=\infty,
\qquad
\sum_i p_i^2=c^2\zeta(2a)<\infty.
\]

There are infinitely many active queries almost surely, while the total distance between the entire returned process and its fresh reference is bounded by

\[
\boxed{
\frac{cE_0+Jc^2\zeta(2a)}{32U^2}.
}
\]

The same finite allowance covers the whole infinite record, rather than being charged again for each later task.

This corollary still requires continued valid operation. It also does **not** automatically apply to a fixed finite threshold grid: the \(K/(2M)\) allowance cannot be omitted. Increasing precision with summable allowances, or a separately justified continuous sensor law, would be needed for that extension.

For a finite \(K\), ordinary passivity supplies a finite input-amplitude rating requirement. With at most \(K\) injections,

\[
|y_i|\le \sqrt{E_0+JK}
\]

in the normalized scalar model. The comparator’s threshold range and its safe input range are different resources.

### A passive specimen that supplies the operation

Consider a lossless delay line. At each window, its arriving stored amplitude \(z\) interacts with the input \(v\) through

\[
\begin{pmatrix}

\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}

\end{pmatrix}

\qquad

Use commands \(v=s\), giving

Thus \(h=tz\), \(x=r\), \(U=0.6\), and the input-energy cap is \(J=1\). Assume initial stored energy at most \(E_0=16\). The controller need not know the delay-line length.

## 7. Failure tests that delimit the result

Several tempting extensions fail.

### Revealing the private sign breaks the claimed interface

Inside the linear threshold range,

\[
\mathbb E[D\mid s,h,x]
=
\frac{x+sh}{R}.
\]

The bit alone can have the correct law while the pair \((s,D)\) reveals memory dependence.

An exact check with \(h=0.2\), \(x=0.4\), \(R=1.2\) gives the correct marginal bit law, but a total-variation discrepancy of \(1/12\) when the sign is included.

The theorem therefore concerns the specified returned record. It does not cover the complete device log.

### Reusing a sign breaks the cancellation

If an old field contains the preceding sign and that sign is reused, it is no longer fresh relative to the physical history.

For example, with \(h=s/2\), \(x=1/2\), \(R=2\), reusing \(s\) gives returned-bit mean \(1/2\). A fresh sign gives the correct mean \(1/4\).

The source handoff’s chronology requirement survives.

### Several output bits under one shared sign can expose memory

Even with independent threshold dithers and no clipping, two returned bits under one shared sign satisfy

\[
\mathbb E[D_1D_2]
=
\frac{x_1x_2+h_1h_2}{R^2}.
\]

Each marginal can be correct while the joint law is wrong.

For \(x_1=x_2=0\) and \(h_1=h_2=R/2\), the joint discrepancy from two independent fair bits is \(1/8\).

A vector response therefore does not come free. The present construction supplies one scalar bit per independently signed interrogation.
