# Infinitely many bounded observations with finitely many large-memory encounters

## 1. Consequence and supplier

A persistent passive source need not reset, mix or have a known decay time for a finite-range instrument to obtain indefinitely many useful measurements. Fresh sparse activity keys give a weighted energy bound. If their probabilities have infinite sum but finite sum of squares, infinitely many active experiments occur but only finitely many guard encounters occur, almost surely.

This does not make every subsequent conditional gradient bias exactly zero: the last observed flag is a future-dependent random time. Ordinary ADC noise and calibration costs remain. The result concerns actual guard/saturation encounters and a summable conditional-bias budget, not a microscopic reset. Its finite-total-encounter conclusion can cost more calendar time than useful accuracy requires.

Retain the corrected complete supplier of `BOUNDED_TASK_LEARNING_WITH_PERSISTENT_MEMORY.md`. Before round i's fresh key,

\[
y_i=\beta_i+B_i\sigma_i A u_i,
\quad z_i=\chi_{0,i}+B_i\sigma_iLu_i+
 B_i\kappa\tau_iA^*\widehat r_i,
\quad\|A\|,\|L\|\le1.                             \tag{1}
\]

Commands obey ||u_i||<=U and ||kappa rhat_i||<=V; put S=U^2+V^2. Inactive rounds execute both actual zero-input windows with the same loading and timing. Every ADC has its calibrated clipping mean conditional on the complete pre-read physical/instrument history and current signs/commands, using fresh independent dither. Its fixed rail and absolute quantization interval are actual instruments. The marginal-mean Delta*tau counterexample remains binding.

Let E_i be stored energy and e_i=||beta_i||^2+||chi_{0,i}||^2. Sign-averaged passivity gives

\[
e_i\le E_i-E[E_{i+1}\mid H_i]+S p_i.                \tag{2}
\]

Here p_i is deterministic and B_i is fresh before e_i can depend on it. A warranted E_1<=E_0 may hold conditional on a completed retained old record. The actual active guard statistic satisfies

\[
0\le F_i\le C_F,\qquad F_i\le K_F e_i
\quad\text{on active rounds},                     \tag{3}
\]

with the stronger fixed rail margins and constants from that source. No clipped observation is removed, and no internal coordinate is read.

## 2. Weighted physical occupation

For every deterministic nonnegative nonincreasing sequence q_i,

\[
\sum_{i=1}^N q_i E[e_i]
\le q_1E_0+S\sum_{i=1}^Nq_i p_i.                  \tag{4}
\]

Indeed, multiplying (2) by q_i makes the energy terms telescope to

\[
q_1E[E_1]-q_NE[E_{N+1}]
 +\sum_{i=2}^N(q_i-q_{i-1})E[E_i]\le q_1E_0.
\]

Taking q_i=p_i for a nonincreasing activity schedule and using fresh B_i yields

\[
\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^NE[B_i e_i]
\le p_1E_0+S\sum_{i=1}^Np_i^2.}                  \tag{5}
\]

The squared activity probabilities are the added physical leverage. Excitation injects energy with probability p_i; an independently selected probe encounters returning energy with a further duty factor. The proof uses actual storage and chronology, not independent futures copied from one state.

## 3. Infinite observations, finite guard encounters

Suppose sum_i p_i=infinity and sum_i p_i^2<infinity. Fresh keys produce infinitely many active rounds almost surely. Equations (3)--(5) give

\[
E\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}B_iF_i
\le K_F\left(p_1E_0+S\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}p_i^2\right)<\infty.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]

The nonnegative sum is finite almost surely. Every positive flag statistic is at least min(U,r_max)>0, so only finitely many active guard encounters occur. Every actual clipped coefficient triggers a guard; hence only finitely many active ADC clips occur. Hidden dark coordinates may remain frozen, and observable modes may have arbitrarily long memory.

This is not a deterministic deadline, a known last-flag time, a posterior guarantee on a selected rare record, or a uniform bound on all native fields. External waveform and front-end ratings still need the packet or another actual supplier. No necessity of random probing against every conventional waiting/control alternative is claimed.

## 4. The active experiment is the correct learning unit

Let t_k be the calendar index of the kth active pair. Reveal its realized waiting history and current physical state, and B_{t_k}=1, before fresh phase signs and ADC dither. Return

\[
g_k^{\rm act}=\lambda u_k+\tau_k\widehat z_k/\kappa. \tag{7}
\]

There is no inverse-duty importance weight. The fixed task gradient depends on A and u, not on a uniform sample of hidden states. The current state remains a nuisance, with clipping paid by the flags; waiting selection does not create a fresh or uniform initial law.

Conditionally on the pre-sign active history,

\[
E[g_k^{\rm act}]=\nabla F(u_k)+h_k,
\qquad\|h_k\|\le E[F_k\mid\text{active history}].   \tag{8}
\]

The active signal norm is at most sqrt(d)R_z/kappa. An ordinary optimizer updates only on active pairs. Idle windows consume time and evolve the source but do not artificially shrink the numerical iterate through a regularizer-only update.

Use predeclared uniform averages of the K active inputs and gradients. The quadratic affine-gradient identity is pathwise. Both guard and observed-square certificates apply in the active index with p=1 and w=1:

\[
D_K=\frac{\sum_{k\le K}F_k+C_F\log(1/\delta_B)}{1-e^{-1}},
\qquad b_K=R_z/\kappa.                             \tag{9}
\]

The empirical-variance proof supplies H_K^obs from actual squares of tau_k zhat_k/kappa. Hence

\[
\|\bar g_K-\nabla F(\bar u_K)\|
\le (H_K^{\rm obs}+D_K)/K.                        \tag{10}
\]

Confidence is allocated at predetermined active-count checkpoints and converted to optimum distance with the projected fixed-point residual. These checkpoints have random calendar times, which is legitimate because the inference filtration counts completed active experiments. Waiting times, signs, commands and costs remain in the record. No arbitrary outcome-selected reweighting is licensed.

The bounded-sensor noise continues even after the last actual guard. The inferred conditional bias need not literally vanish after that future-dependent time. Equations (8)--(10) avoid that invalid inference.

## 5. Convergence for faster schedules, including p_i proportional to i^(-1/3)

Finite total flags are stronger than needed. Let p_i be deterministic nonincreasing and comparable to i^{-a}, 0<a<1. Write P_i=sum_{t<=i}p_t and K_{i-1}=sum_{t<i}B_t. For active index k use

\[
u_{k+1}=\Pi_D\{u_k-2g_k^{\rm act}/[\lambda(k+1)]\}. \tag{11}
\]

The active gradient is uniformly bounded. To prove convergence it remains to check summability of the step-weighted clipping bias, despite the unknown waiting states.

Take q_i=p_i/(1+P_{i-1}) in (4). It is nonincreasing, and

\[
\sum_iq_i p_i=\sum_i\frac{p_i^2}{1+P_{i-1}}<\infty, \tag{12}
\]

because its power-law tail has order i^{-1-a}. Thus sum_i q_i E[e_i]<infinity.

Fresh Bernoulli concentration gives
Pr{K_{i-1}<P_{i-1}/2}<=exp(-P_{i-1}/8). On its complement,
1/(K_{i-1}+1)<=2/(1+P_{i-1}). The conditional active bias is bounded both by K_b e_i, where K_b is the clipping-bias constant (8) of the bounded-source proof, and by the fixed constant

\[
H_{\max}=\sqrt d R_z/\kappa+U+\|b\|.
\]

Freshness of B_i therefore yields

\[
\sum_i E\frac{B_i\|h_i\|}{K_{i-1}+1}
\le 2K_b\sum_iq_iE[e_i]
 +H_{\max}\sum_i p_i e^{-P_{i-1}/8}<\infty.        \tag{13}
\]

The second sum is finite by comparison with the integral in the cumulative variable P. Equation (13) is exactly step-weighted bias summability in the active index. The squared-step noise sum is finite because active gradients are bounded. The standard projected-distance almost-supermartingale argument now gives u_k->u_* almost surely. The uniform active average also converges.

The same proof with F_i in place of ||h_i|| gives sum_k F_k/(k+1)<infinity almost surely. Since F_k>=0, this implies K^{-1}sum_{k<=K}F_k->0: the normalized tail is bounded by its arbitrarily small harmonic-weighted tail. Consequently D_K/K tends to zero at dyadic active checkpoints. The observed-square noise radius also tends to zero because the reports are bounded. Thus the measured optimum-distance certificate eventually reaches each positive threshold under indefinite valid operation.

This proves completion for a=1/3 without asserting finite total flags. It is not an operation through an arbitrary finite device lifetime, a uniform deterministic calendar deadline, or a certificate based on raw hidden fields.

## 6. Choose the precision objective before the duty schedule

These are expected active counts and expected guard-budget orders, not complete finite-confidence wall-time bounds. Dimensions, rails, lambda, confidence, initial energy and implementation costs remain.

| Duty p_i comparable to i^(-a) | Expected active pairs by N | Expected total guard debit bound | Consequence |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| a=1/3 | N^(2/3) | O(E_0+N^(1/3)) | Average clipping debit and sampling error have nominal order N^(-1/3) |
| a=1/2 | N^(1/2) | O(E_0+log N) | Few large-field encounters relative to observations; sampling error dominates |
| 1/2<a<1 | N^(1-a) | O(E_0+1) | Finite total guards almost surely, with slower calendar acquisition |

The first row suits useful finite accuracy; finite total flags are not compulsory. A rapidly relaxing or differently calibrated device can favor another conventional schedule.

There is a real cost to the stronger scheduling premise. If sum p_i^2<infinity then P_N=o(sqrt N). Split P_N at J and bound its tail by sqrt(N sum_{i>J}p_i^2); let first N and then J tend to infinity. This sufficient finite-encounter route therefore provides fewer than order sqrt(N) expected experiments. It is not a lower bound on every physical controller.

Every zero-input window is charged. Native memory can persist, but those windows still provide real opportunities for energy to escape. A stochastic occupation argument replaces a known relaxation envelope; elapsed time is not free.

A new task selected from a completed record may start on the same source using its current warranted energy debit. Even with clipped readouts, E_start<=E_0+sum of all actual incident energies is conservative. Ignoring unmeasured outgoing energy is safe; actuator-energy calibration remains necessary. The original E_0 is not reused by declaration, and an unlimited series of new tasks does not inherit one finite-total-flag budget for free.

The complete conditional ADC channel is essential. A fixed mean defect adds the declared gradient floor; finite reports do not create unlimited analogue calibration precision. Forward coefficient preparation, references, timing and computation must meet the tolerance. Operator drift and outcome-dependent loading are not old additive fields.

Ongoing material-energy injection adds sum_i p_i E[Q_i] to (5). A positive constant injection rate with sum p_i=infinity need not have finite weighted total, so finite encounters are not established for that interface. A known bath model or another operation could support useful accuracy, but it is outside this proof. Apparatus work retains its actual costs.

The inference concerns the fixed incremental quadratic task and its recorded receiver. It neither prepares an exact hidden state nor cancels every raw warm field. Passive wave variables, reciprocal adjoints, stochastic ILC, binary-sensor identification and dither have strong primary predecessors already recorded. Ordinary algorithms are legitimate consumers; external priority and practical speedup remain unestablished.

## 8. Failure, matched alternative and review boundary

The first bounded calendar-weighted run used 262,144 pairs, including 514,658 zero-input windows. Its audited optimum error was about 0.00253, but its best distance certificate was 1.833, larger than the feasible-set diameter 1.4. All saturation was retained. This prompted active indexing and uniform active averages; the population answer was not declared certified at its realized error.

The ordinary active-pair implementation then used the identical source, target, pulse caps, ADCs, calendar budget, activity/sign sequence and ordered dither stream. Its distance certificate was 0.79078, while its audited error was about 0.00206. Both used 4,815 active pairs and 524,288 native windows; all four actual saturated scalar reports were retained. Incident energies were about 395.21 and 391.99. The certificate is narrower, but the following zero-probe baseline shows that neither bounded execution has demonstrated useful certified optimization at this budget. The incumbent's realized mean-gradient error was smaller, so universal dominance is not claimed.

For this fixture, lambda=0.5, ||b||=0.4 and U=0.7. The singular-value formula for the unconstrained ridge optimum gives

\[
\|u_*\|=\|(A^*A+\lambda I)^{-1}A^*b\|
\le \frac{\|b\|}{2\sqrt\lambda}=0.2828427125.       \tag{14}
\]

Indeed s/(s^2+lambda)<=1/(2 sqrt(lambda)) for every s>=0. The bound is below U, so the unconstrained optimum is feasible and is also the ball-constrained optimum for every permitted contraction A. Returning u=0 with zero physical calls therefore certifies distance 0.282843, which is stronger than both executed bounded certificates. A comparison only with the diameter 1.4 was inadequate. The original execution data, all calls and pins remain unchanged; no fixture was enlarged or retuned to conceal this failure. The asymptotic completion and weighted-energy results are separate from useful finite-budget performance.
