# The remaining bounded-coordinate issue is real, even at deficiency zero

For the single-linkage cycle

    0 → B → mA+B → 0,

all rates1 and any fixed integer m≥1, the full process is complex balanced at c_A=c_B=1 and has a product-Poisson stationary law. Nevertheless every fractional-factorial candidate mu_alpha=(A!B!)^(−alpha), 0<alpha<1, has positive adjoint eventually along B=0,A=n.

At (n,0) the outgoing rate is1 (0→B). Incoming mA+B→0 from(n+m,1) contributes

    [(n+m)!/n!]^(1−alpha).

The other incoming reactions are unavailable at B=0. Consequently

    (L*mu_alpha)(n,0)/mu_alpha(n,0)
      =[(n+m)!/n!]^(1−alpha)−1 >0.

The physical class contains an unbounded subsequence of these states: repeated B→mA+B reactions create arbitrary multiples of m while B=1, followed by mA+B→0. Thus the positive ray is not excluded by a conserved-population argument.

This does not challenge recurrence or the bulk theorem. It proves that bounded-coordinate corrections are necessary even in the easiest globally recurrent case. A strip correction must suppress phase1 relative to phase0 by a polynomial n^(−m(1−alpha)) or stronger. Such a polynomial correction is compatible with factorial summability, but its outgoing interface flux and the common choice of alpha/strip size must still be controlled rather than assumed.
