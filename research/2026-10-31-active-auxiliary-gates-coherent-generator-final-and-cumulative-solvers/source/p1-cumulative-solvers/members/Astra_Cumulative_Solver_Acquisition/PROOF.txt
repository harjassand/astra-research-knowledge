ACQUIRE A REUSABLE PRECONDITIONING SPACE FROM FAILED CERTIFICATES
31 October 2026. Ordinary finite-dimensional construction and proofs.

STATUS

This is a derivation assembled for the present investigation. Nyström
approximation, randomized norm estimates, preconditioning, subspace recycling,
and determinant potential arguments are established methods. Historical
priority for the particular changing-operator statement below has not been
established. The purpose is a complete, paid learning operation with a useful
downstream effect, not a claim to have invented preconditioning.

1. QUESTION AND SOURCE CONTRACT

Can a sequence of difficult linear equations teach an algorithm useful
computational directions, so later equations require only a small number of
applications of their actual operator, without receiving a useful coarse
space, rank, or spectral envelope in advance?

The explicit source is a finite-dimensional matrix-vector service. The ambient
dimension n and its ordinary coordinates are known. A task supplies a real
symmetric positive-semidefinite operator A, a shift lambda >= mu > 0, and a
right-hand side b. The actual equation is

    K x = b,               K = lambda I + A.

The learner may choose vectors v and pay for A v. Its stored directions may
be used as later query inputs. This is a stronger observation operation than
the earlier bounded scalar endpoint-return service. It is not derived from
that service, and it is not an inverse/solution oracle. The learner must still
solve the equation. Arbitrary operator applications are nevertheless a real
input assumption, not an acquired consequence.

For a completely finite exact implementation, all source matrices, task
parameters, and right-hand sides can be rational, and the source returns
exact rational products. Rational arithmetic and returned bit lengths are
paid. The mathematical norm statements also apply over the reals. A floating
implementation needs additional rounding/error enclosures; binary64 output
alone is not the exact certificate specified here.

The task operators can depend on all earlier outputs. They must be fixed
before fresh random probes for their current certificate are drawn. Subsequent
right-hand sides can be arbitrary and adaptive after a certificate is issued.

The learner retains a growing subspace S. It starts with S = {0}. No useful
directions, rank, eigenvectors, preconditioner, or common envelope are supplied.

2. THE NYSTROM RESIDUAL AS AN ACQUIRABLE OBJECT

Let V have independent columns spanning S. Orthogonality is convenient but not
needed to define the approximation. With

    Y = A V,               H = V^T A V,

define

    N_S(A) = Y H^dagger Y^T,
    R_S(A) = A - N_S(A).

Here H^dagger is the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse. It is not an oracle. The
implementation below replaces it by an ordinary inverse on a maximal positive
principal submatrix of H.

LEMMA 1 (residual identities). For every A >= 0:

    0 <= R_S(A) <= A,
    R_S(A) V = 0,
    z^T R_S(A) z = min_c (z - Vc)^T A (z - Vc).       (1)

Proof. Put F = A^(1/2) V. Then N_S(A) = A^(1/2) P_F A^(1/2),
where P_F is the orthogonal projection onto range(F). Consequently
R_S(A) = A^(1/2)(I-P_F)A^(1/2). The three claims follow. The square
root is used in this proof only; the algorithm never queries or computes it.

Two consequences are essential.

(a) If S is enlarged, R_S(A) decreases in positive-semidefinite order.

(b) If 0 <= A <= C, then R_S(A) <= R_S(C), because the minimized
quadratic in (1) is no larger for A than for C. This is the shorted-operator
monotonicity needed for a changing family, not a claim that Nyström errors
add linearly under changing operators.

To apply E = R_S(A), compute

    E z = A z - Y H^dagger (Y^T z).

This costs one actual A application plus the low-dimensional arithmetic, after
Y and the small inverse have been constructed. No inverse of A is queried.

3. A FINITE TEST THAT EITHER ACCEPTS OR ACQUIRES A NEW DIRECTION

Let

    q = max(1, ceil(log_4(2n))).

The integer can be selected by repeated multiplication: choose the least
positive q for which 4^q >= 2n. At global certificate stage s, allocate

    delta_s = delta/[s(s+1)],

and choose an integer r_s with

    (11/12)^r_s <= delta_s.                            (2)

The count can also be found by rational multiplication, without trusting a
floating logarithm. Draw r_s independent Rademacher vectors g_j, whose n
coordinates independently equal +1 or -1 with probability 1/2. Form

    v_j = E^q g_j,
    M = max_j ||v_j||_2^2.

The exact acceptance test is

    2 M <= (mu/2)^(2q).                               (3)

If (3) holds, accept the current preconditioner. Otherwise choose a maximizer
v_j and append that nonzero vector to S. The append can be rescaled by a
nonzero rational scalar without changing S. Resume the test with fresh random
probes and the next global stage index.

A more economical implementation processes probes sequentially. Reject at
the FIRST j for which 2||v_j||^2>(mu/2)^(2q), and append that witness v_j.
Accept only after all r_s probes pass. This is the same acceptance event,
and the rejection proof requires only a threshold crossing, not a maximizer.
Record both planned r_s and actually used ell_s<=r_s probes. Unused probes
are not charged. The same modification applies to the optional alpha test
below, using threshold alpha(mu/2)^(2q). The original full-batch version is
a valid but potentially wasteful implementation.

There is no inconclusive third outcome. Every failed test adds an independent
direction with a quantitative residual witness. This is what makes the entire
acquisition path chargeable.

LEMMA 2 (probabilistic soundness). Conditional on all previous history and on
the current A, with probability at least 1-delta_s, acceptance implies

    ||E||_2 <= mu/2.                                  (4)

Proof. Let u be a unit eigenvector for the largest eigenvalue a = ||E||,
and let Z = u^T g for a Rademacher vector. Then E Z^2 = 1 and E Z^4 <= 3.
The Paley-Zygmund inequality, proved here by Cauchy-Schwarz, gives

    Pr(Z^2 >= 1/2) >= 1/12.

Indeed, for X=Z^2, EX=1 and EX^2<=3. On {X<1/2}, E[X 1_{X<1/2}]<=1/2;
hence E[X 1_{X>=1/2}]>=1/2. Cauchy-Schwarz then gives
1/4 <= EX^2 Pr(X>=1/2) <= 3 Pr(X>=1/2).

If |u^Tg| >= 1/sqrt(2), then ||E^q g|| >= a^q/sqrt(2).
For at least one of r_s independent probes this happens except with
probability (11/12)^r_s. Therefore

    U := (2 M)^(1/(2q)) >= a

on that event. Test (3) is exactly U<=mu/2. A union bound over the global
stage allocation gives probability at most delta that any issued certificate
is false, even for adaptively selected tasks and spaces. No subsequent union
bound over right-hand sides or requested solution accuracies is needed.

LEMMA 3 (every rejection is a useful new direction). Whenever (3) fails,
let v attain M and define x=v/||v|| for analysis. Then

    x perpendicular to S,       ||x||=1,
    x^T R_S(A) x > mu/4.                              (5)

This implication holds deterministically, for every realized set of probes;
it does not require the confidence event in Lemma 2.

Proof. Since E V=0 and E is symmetric, range(E) is perpendicular to S.
Because q>=1, v=E^qg is in that range. Rejection implies v is nonzero.
Write the spectral decomposition of E, and let

    m_k = g^T E^k g / ||g||^2.

These are moments of a nonnegative random variable under the spectral measure
of g. Holder's inequality gives

    m_(2q+1) >= m_(2q)^((2q+1)/(2q)).

Thus

    x^T E x = m_(2q+1)/m_(2q)
              >= m_(2q)^(1/(2q))
              = (||E^qg||/||g||)^(1/q).

Every Rademacher g has ||g||^2=n. Since rejection means
2||v||^2 > (mu/2)^(2q),

    x^T E x > (mu/2)/(2n)^(1/(2q)) >= mu/4,

where the last inequality follows from 4^q>=2n. This proves (5).

Operationally the learner stores a rational multiple of v; it never needs the
irrational normalized x. Because each new v is exactly perpendicular to the
old space, all retained raw vectors are mutually orthogonal in exact
arithmetic. One can rescale each by a power of two so its squared norm lies
in [1/4,1]. This gives a well-conditioned Euclidean frame without receiving
a useful basis in advance. It does not guarantee that V^T A V is numerically
well conditioned for a later different operator.

OPTIONAL CONSTANT IMPROVEMENT. The same proof works for any fixed rational
alpha in (0,1). Choose q as the least positive integer with 4^q>=n/alpha,
and r_s so

    [1-(1-alpha)^2/3]^r_s <= delta_s.

The exact acceptance comparison becomes

    max_j ||E^q g_j||^2 <= alpha (mu/2)^(2q).

Paley-Zygmund gives Pr(|u^Tg|^2>=alpha)>=(1-alpha)^2/3; the sound upper
estimate is U=(max_j||E^qg_j||^2/alpha)^(1/(2q)). Rejection still yields
x^TEx>mu/[2(n/alpha)^(1/(2q))]>=mu/4. The parameter alpha=1/2 is the
simple version above. The choice alpha=1/16 gives success probability at
least 75/256 per probe, so the miss probability is at most (181/256)^r_s,
at the price of a slightly larger q. These different choices have different
literal application counts and should be benchmarked with those costs.

For either version, if ||E||<=mu/4, acceptance is guaranteed deterministically,
because ||E^qg||^2<=n||E||^(2q) and 4^q>=n/alpha. Thus the two thresholds
leave no unresolved gap requiring unlimited additional sampling.

4. CUMULATIVE ACQUISITION BOUND FOR CHANGING OPERATORS

Let A_1,A_2,... be any sequence of task operators. Suppose C>=0 is any
matrix satisfying C>=A_t for every task under consideration. The learner
does not know C and does not use it. This is a retrospective comparison
quantity. An envelope over a future task domain yields a uniform bound for
all sequences from that domain. An envelope over just the encountered tasks
gives a bound for that finite sequence.

THEOREM 4 (hindsight determinant bound). Set tau=mu/4. The total number J
of acquired directions over all tasks is bounded, for every random path, by

    J <= min{ n, log det(I+C/tau)/log 2 }.
      = min{ n, sum_i log_2(1+4 lambda_i(C)/mu) }.       (6)

If rank(C)=k, also J<=k, since all acquired directions belong to range(C).
No exact low-rank promise is needed for (6).

Proof. Let Q be an orthonormal basis for the current acquired space, used
only in the proof. Let x be a normalized newly acquired direction. Lemma 3
and monotonicity from Lemma 1 give

    x^T R_Q(C)x >= x^T R_Q(A_t)x > tau,
    x perpendicular to range(Q).

Define the potential

    Phi(Q) = log det(I+Q^T C Q/tau),       Phi(empty)=0.

By the block determinant formula,

 exp(Phi([Q,x])-Phi(Q))
   = 1 + [x^T C x
          - x^T C Q (tau I+Q^T C Q)^(-1) Q^T C x]/tau.

For positive-semidefinite C, Q^T C x lies in range(Q^T C Q). On that
range, (tau I+Q^T C Q)^(-1) <= (Q^T C Q)^dagger. Therefore the bracket
is at least x^T R_Q(C)x>tau. Every append increases Phi by more than
log 2. On the other hand, eigenvalue interlacing, or extending Q to an
orthogonal basis and using the determinant Schur complement, gives

    Phi(Q) <= log det(I+C/tau).

Summing proves (6). Each append is Euclidean-independent, giving J<=n.
If A_t<=C, then ker C is contained in ker A_t, so range(A_t) is contained
in range(C). Every new direction is in range(R_Q(A_t)), which is contained
in range(A_t); hence J<=rank(C).

The bound remains true even on a confidence failure path: Lemma 3 and the
determinant accounting are deterministic. An incorrect acceptance can
compromise a solve, but it cannot create an infinite chain of unproductive
refinements. For a finite number T of task operators there are exactly T
accepted terminal stages and J rejected stages, hence T+J stages in total.
Each new operator finishes after at most n additions even without any useful
envelope. There is no assumption of a favorable unobserved rank or gap in
the stopping mechanism.

The bound can be useful without an exactly finite shared span. For any k,

 J log 2 <= sum_(i<=k) log(1+4 lambda_i(C)/mu)
             + (4/mu) sum_(i>k) lambda_i(C).           (6a)

This follows from log(1+x)<=x on the tail. For example, if a retrospective
envelope obeys lambda_i(C)<=c i^(-p) with p>1, then

    J <= [pi/(log 2 sin(pi/p))] (4c/mu)^(1/p).         (6b)

To check the constant, use monotonicity to dominate the sum of
log(1+a i^(-p)) by the integral from 0 to infinity. Substituting
x=a^(1/p)y and integrating by parts gives

    integral_0^infinity log(1+a x^(-p)) dx
      = pi a^(1/p)/sin(pi/p).

These are yield/complexity statements under a favorable envelope, not facts
inferred by the learner or additional acceptance premises. The algorithm's
certificate remains the paid test (3).

For a sequence of finite discretizations with the SAME constants c,p and mu,
(6b) is a pathwise bound on acquired-direction count uniform in discretization
dimension. This is a prospective continuum consequence, not an implemented
infinite-dimensional sampler. The Rademacher probe, n-coordinate output,
q=O(log n) power count, and O(nJ) vector storage remain finite-dimensional and
are charged. Identity-covariance random signs are not a square-integrable
Hilbert-space probe in infinite dimension; an actual continuum observation
operation would require an additional construction and its own costs.

5. THE DOWNSTREAM CAPABILITY: ARBITRARY ACCURACY VIA REAL CORRECTION

For a task accepted on the joint good event, put

    N = N_S(A),        P = lambda I + N,        K=lambda I+A.

By (4), E=A-N lies between 0 and (mu/2)I. Since lambda>=mu,

    P <= K <= (3/2) P.                                (7)

The stored preconditioner is an apply operation for P^(-1). The actual
operator K is still used to calculate residuals. Starting at x_0=0,

    x_(k+1) = x_k + P^(-1)(b-Kx_k).                   (8)

This explicit Richardson iteration is enough; an established preconditioned
conjugate-gradient solver is a stronger standard implementation.

THEOREM 5 (all right-hand sides). For every b, including b chosen after the
certificate and after earlier solves, iteration (8) satisfies

    ||x_k-K^(-1)b||_K <= 2^(-k) ||K^(-1)b||_K.         (9)

Thus ceil(log_2(1/epsilon)) actual residual correction iterations suffice
for relative energy-norm error epsilon, with no new model precision
requirement. The acquired residual tolerance is mu/2, independent of the
requested final epsilon.

Proof. K^(1/2)P^(-1)K^(1/2) is symmetric with spectrum in [1,3/2] by (7).
In K-energy coordinates, the iteration error operator is its complement
to the identity, so its norm is at most 1/2. Repeated application proves (9).

A computable stopping certificate is available too. For r=b-Kx,

    r^T K^(-1) r <= r^T P^(-1) r,
    b^T P^(-1) b <= (3/2)b^T K^(-1)b.

Consequently, the rational test

    r^T P^(-1)r <= (2/3)epsilon^2 b^T P^(-1)b          (10)

implies ||x-K^(-1)b||_K <= epsilon ||K^(-1)b||_K.
If b=0, return zero. This is a residual test on the actual current operator.

The number of iterations needed to meet (10) differs from (9) by at most a
constant, because (7) bounds both quadratic-form ratios. The stronger PCG
bound applies with preconditioned condition number at most 3/2.

To bound work on bad-confidence paths too, cap this residual-tested solve at

    k_max = ceil(log_2(3/(2 epsilon)))

updates for 0<epsilon<1, and return a charged failure if test (10) has not
passed. The cap can be selected using exact powers of two. On the joint good
event it always suffices: (7) and (9) imply

 (3/2) [r_k^T P^(-1)r_k]/[b^T P^(-1)b]
       <= (9/4) 4^(-k).

A false model certificate therefore cannot cause an unbounded correction
loop. The concrete implementations may choose a larger explicit cap and pay
for any work done before reporting failure. This does not turn a failed
confidence event into a successful solve; joint certificate soundness is
still the probabilistic statement established in Lemma 2.

This is the useful computational mechanism. Knowledge makes future residual
correction contract quickly for every right-hand side. It does not need to
memorize every requested answer or predict the final answer to epsilon from
one fixed approximate operator alone. It also does not abolish the cost of
actual operator applications or make noisy residual access exact.

6. FINITE AFFINE LIBRARY AND WHAT MUST BE CACHED

Suppose the operator source exposes m fixed PSD primitives B_1,...,B_m, and
tasks specify

    A(theta) = sum_(j=1)^m theta_j B_j,      theta_j>=0.

The unknown B_j are accessible by paid products B_j v. For each acquired
raw vector v, obtain and retain every B_j v. This costs exactly m primitive
products per new direction, or mJ in total. At any later theta the matrix

    Y(theta)=A(theta)V=sum_j theta_j (B_jV)

is assembled from the cache with no new source queries. Likewise H(theta)
is formed from cached small matrices V^T B_j V. Its inversion and the
construction of P(theta)^(-1) are still paid numerical work.

The certificate powers and actual residual correction are not free cache
lookups: their argument vectors are generally new. Each application of
A(theta) to such a vector uses m primitive products if the interface exposes
only the B_j services. If a combined A(theta) service is available and
charged as one operation, that is a different accounting unit. State both
units rather than silently calling m physical operations one.

If 0<=theta_j<=u_j on a stated domain, then

    C = sum_j u_j B_j

is one valid analysis envelope. It need not be the sharpest one and need not
be known to the learner. For a finite sequence one may use any smaller C
that dominates exactly the encountered A(theta). The theorem does not infer
that such a favorable C exists from PSD structure alone.

When the parameter box 0<=theta_j<=u_j is actually given, there is a stronger
offline/online version. The operator C=sum_j u_j B_j is itself accessible by
paid primitive products. Run acquisition on C until it is accepted. Cache
all B_j v for every new direction v. On the single global confidence event,

    R_S(A(theta)) <= R_S(C) <= (mu/2) I

for EVERY theta in that box, by Lemma 1. All future tasks in the domain are
covered without new randomized spectral tests. Their Nyström approximations
and inverse preconditioners are assembled from the cached primitive actions;
small-matrix work and actual residual corrections are still paid. This gives
one accepted terminal acquisition stage, J rejected stages, and the same
determinant bound with this accessible C. The parameter box and primitive
PSD source are supplied structure, while the useful subspace is acquired.

It is not generally valid to replace this envelope test by separate primitive
tests. The shorted operator is concave in A. For an exact two-dimensional
counterexample, let A=diag(1,0), B=diag(0,1), and let S be the span of (1,1).
Then N_S(A)=A and N_S(B)=B, so each primitive residual is zero. But A+B=I,
N_S(I) is the orthogonal projection onto S, and R_S(I) has eigenvalues 0,1.
Thus individually exact Nyström models do not imply that the task-specific
Nyström model of their mixture is exact. One must use the common envelope
certificate, test each new combined task directly, or change the stored
model. Summing individually stored PSD approximations is valid, but it can
have rank as large as mJ and is not the rank-J construction proved here.

7. EXACT IMPLEMENTATION WITHOUT A PSEUDOINVERSE OR EIGENVALUE ORACLE

Given rational V and exact Y=AV, H=V^TY is rational PSD. Find a maximal
positive definite principal submatrix H_II using exact pivoted LDL^T
elimination. For a PSD matrix a zero remaining diagonal pivot implies its
whole remaining row and column vanish, so this finite elimination identifies
the rank without a numerical threshold. The selected columns A^(1/2)V_I
span the same space as A^(1/2)V. Therefore

    N = Y_I H_II^(-1) Y_I^T                           (11)

equals the full pseudoinverse formula. Ordinary rational elimination computes
H_II^(-1). If the rank is zero, N=0.

For h=rank(H), the inverse preconditioner can be applied using

 P^(-1)z
   = lambda^(-1)z
     - lambda^(-1)Y_I
       (lambda H_II+Y_I^T Y_I)^(-1)(Y_I^T z).          (12)

This follows by the Woodbury identity. The small matrix in (12) is positive
definite. Its factorization is paid and retained for that task. The algorithm
does not invert an unknown n by n matrix as a premise.

All acceptance and stopping tests (2), (3), and (10) are rational comparisons.
The power vectors, raw retained vectors, and cached primitive products are
rational. No exact real-valued random source, eigenvector oracle, normalized
hidden-state preparation, or square-root operation is required.

Exact arithmetic is a finite existence construction, not a promise of small
bit complexity. A projected Gram can have extremely small nonzero pivots for
a new operator even when the Euclidean retained vectors are well conditioned.
Fraction numerators and denominators can grow through powers and elimination.
The determinant update bound controls number of directions, not their bit
lengths. A full implementation must record operand/output bit lengths, exact
elimination work, and memory. There is no rank-only polynomial bit-cost claim
here. A practical numerical implementation can use stabilized Nyström methods
and validated residual arithmetic, but that requires its own explicit
rounding analysis and must be distinguished from this rational theorem.

8. COST LEDGER

For T new task operators, J new directions, and stage probe counts r_s:

  * Exactly T+J certificate stages occur.
  * Sequential certificate powers use q sum_(s=1)^(T+J) ell_s
    combined-operator products, where ell_s<=r_s is the actual probe count.
    The original full-batch version has ell_s=r_s.
  * In the affine library, permanent primitive cache construction uses mJ
    primitive products.
  * Every combined-operator product costs m primitive products if no combined
    source exists; any known zeros among coefficients can be omitted and
    counted literally.
  * A solve with the explicit Richardson algorithm costs O(log(1/epsilon))
    additional combined-operator products and preconditioner applications,
    including any final residual verification. Repeated b for an identical
    certified operator reuses its certificate without new acquisition tests.
  * All rejected stages are in the sum. No failed acquisition bill disappears.

Since q=O(log n) and r_s=O(log(s/delta)), this gives

    O((T+J) log n log((T+J)/delta))

combined-operator applications for certification. This conservative finite
Rademacher certificate can be expensive. The theorem does not claim it beats
adaptive Nyström in practical constants.

Sequential rejection can substantially reduce that actual bill. If the
current ||E||>mu/2, each independent probe crosses the threshold with
probability at least (1-alpha)^2/3, so its expected number of used probes is
at most 3/(1-alpha)^2 (before the finite cap). This assertion applies to a
fixed stage whose true residual exceeds mu/2. It is not an unconditional
claim that every rejected stage has that expected cost: stages with true
norm in (mu/4,mu/2] can also reject, and their probabilities can differ.
All accepted stages use their full planned r_s probes. Actual used counts,
not this expectation, should determine every experimental comparison.

The arithmetic outside source applications is also paid. At dimension d<=J,
an E application costs O(nd+d^2) rational arithmetic after retaining Y and an
inverse/factor of H. A dense small factorization costs O(d^3) arithmetic.
Building a dense small Gram from n-vectors costs O(nd^2). In the affine
library, forming Y(theta) costs O(mnd), forming H(theta) from cached small
primitive Grams costs O(md^2), and forming Y(theta)^T Y(theta) costs
O(nd^2) per new task. Applying (12) costs O(nd+d^2).

Permanent cache storage is O(mnJ + mJ^2 + nJ) rational numbers. A current
task additionally needs O(nJ+J^2) work storage. Processing probes one at a
time avoids O(n r_s) working storage. Storing all past task-specific factors
would add its own cost; the theorem does not require that. Raw confidence,
query, and failure ledgers add storage proportional to the recorded work.
Bit-operation costs depend on actual operand lengths; each rational number
is not a unit-cost machine scalar.

Any comparison with dense Cholesky, sparse direct solvers, multigrid, recycled
Krylov, adaptive randomized Nyström, or a retaining operator model must give
those methods the same source interface and caching rights. The acquisition
bound is a capability theorem and does not prove universal practical
superiority over those methods.

9. WHAT IS AND IS NOT EARNED

Earned within the explicit service: useful directions are generated by real
matrix applications; unsuccessful certificates become quantitative witnesses;
their lifetime count is bounded by a determinant of any common retrospective
envelope; a constant-quality acquired object makes later arbitrary-accuracy
solves fast in number of real residual correction steps; all RHS for an
accepted operator are covered, including adaptively selected ones.

Not earned: a new general learning theory, discovery of unknown ambient
coordinates, cheap conversion from scalar endpoint reports, noisy physical
residual evaluation at arbitrary accuracy, useful low-dimensional envelopes
for every operator family, cheap bit complexity, or practical dominance over
established retaining solvers. The construction is intentionally different
from pure model prediction, and its stronger source operation is explicit.
