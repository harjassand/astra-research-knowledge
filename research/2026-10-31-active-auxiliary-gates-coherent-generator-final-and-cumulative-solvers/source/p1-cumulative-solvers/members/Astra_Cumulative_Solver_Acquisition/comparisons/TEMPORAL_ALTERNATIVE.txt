TEMPORAL ACQUISITION CANDIDATE -- 31 OCTOBER 2026
Companion exploration; not selected as a novelty claim.

Question: can a learned mechanism make later exponentially rare temporal
queries and policies accessible without waiting to observe the rare event?

1. Source and learning operation

Finite, FULLY OBSERVABLE states S, finite actions A. The learner knows the
state/action alphabets but not transition probabilities. A paid query chooses
(s,a), prepares exactly that observable state and returns ONE next-state draw.
This is a simulator/generative-model interface, or a physical preparation
interface only where physically implemented. Endpoint-return reports alone
do not supply it. Every preparation and transition is paid.

Assume every nonzero transition is at least p_min>0. Exact topology known with
unknown positive probabilities is an alternative, permitting adaptive relative
interval stopping with costs depending on the actual smallest probability.
Without topology or a nonzero-probability floor one cannot finitely distinguish
an impossible edge from an arbitrarily rare edge, so uniform relative answers
for all later temporal events are impossible after bounded observations.

Let d=|S|, a=|A|, H>=1. Choose a path-ratio tolerance eta in (0,1/2]. Query each
of the d*a rows N times, retain actual counts, and set P_hat=count/N. Taking

 N >= [3/(p_min eta^2)] log(2 d^2 a / delta)

ensures simultaneously for every edge:

 (1-eta) P(s'|s,a) <= P_hat(s'|s,a) <= (1+eta) P(s'|s,a).

This is the ordinary multiplicative Chernoff bound plus union bound. True zeros
have count zero exactly; true positives are observed on this event. Conditional
independent row draws suffice. No useful low dimensional basis was supplied.
The price is the whole explicit state space and its source access.

2. Uniform later temporal transfer

For any history-dependent randomized policy pi (including one selected after
seeing the entire acquisition record), any start state, and any measurable
nonnegative function F of trajectories of at most H steps,

 (1-eta)^H E_P^pi F <= E_Phat^pi F <= (1+eta)^H E_P^pi F.       (T1)

Proof: for a fixed policy, every path mass is an initial-state factor, the
identical product of policy factors, and H transition factors. The transition
likelihood ratio lies in the interval in (T1). Sum nonnegative path terms.
Padding shorter trajectories by a deterministic terminal self-loop gives the
same bound. The one simultaneous edge event proves this for ALL policies and
F, hence adaptive future selection costs no further union bound. Signed F
requires separate treatment of cancellation and is excluded.

For eta<=1/2, exp(-2H eta) <= (1-eta)^H and (1+eta)^H<=exp(H eta).
Let rho=2H eta. Then every event probability, including exponentially small
ones, is within exp(+-rho) multiplicatively. Probability zero is preserved.
No reciprocal GLOBAL event probability occurs in the acquisition bound.
The local p_min, horizon, state count, preparations and precision remain.

3. Constructing the downstream planner

A later temporal task supplies a DFA with q states describing accepted
observable trajectories. Form product states (s,z), update z by the DFA upon
each observed symbol, and run finite-horizon backward dynamic programming.
The learner must read the DFA: q is charged. The recurrence is

 V_t(s,z)=max_a sum_s' P_hat(s'|s,a) V_(t+1)(s',delta(z,s')).

Terminal V_H is the acceptance indicator. It constructs an optimal learned-
model policy with at most O(H q d^2 a) rational arithmetic operations, or
O(H q E) for E actual nonzero controlled edges. Memory can be O(qd) values
plus O(Hqd) selected actions. All transition estimates are rational counts;
exact rational execution is finite but not unit-cost. Bit lengths can grow
linearly in H log N before reductions. Fixed point log/scaled computations
need explicit rounding reserves for extremely small values.

Let pi_hat maximize learned-model success and pi_star maximize true success.
The uniform transfer gives

 P^pi_hat(F) >= [(1-eta)/(1+eta)]^H max_pi P^pi(F).              (T2)

Proof: apply the upper transfer to pi_hat, its learned optimality, then the
lower transfer to pi_star. Thus the learner acquires policies achieving a
relative fraction of optimum even when the optimum is exponentially rare.
This does not make rare success common in the actual system: if the physical
maximum success probability is tiny, execution still succeeds rarely.
It makes reliable analysis/choice accessible; it is not a success amplifier.

4. Complete rough bill and meaningful gain

Preparations and transitions: d*a*N. Each preparation can have actual cost
c(s,a), in which case sum_(s,a) N c(s,a), plus observation and bookkeeping.
No original-equilibrium preparation or arbitrary physical reset is free.
Storage: counts for O(E) edges plus support metadata and confidence ledger.
Acquisition computation: count accumulation O(d*a*N), unless an aggregate
binomial simulator is explicitly identified as only a simulation shortcut.
Every later DFA incurs the dynamic programming and numerical cost above.
Horizon increase requires checking the old path-ratio tolerance and buying
more local observations when necessary. Existing counts can be reused with
confidence sequences or a stage-union budget, not repeated fixed-CI peeking.

For a chain of H unknown moderate local success probabilities, global success
can be 2^(-H) while p_min is constant and d=O(H). The acquisition bill above is
polynomial O(H^3 epsilon^-2 log(H/delta)), whereas direct Bernoulli endpoint
relative estimation requires Omega(2^H epsilon^-2) samples. This is a valid
separation in observation operations and reusable representation. It is NOT a
new separation from strong model-based or splitting learners with equal access.
Our conservative all-path maximum bound is also not statistically optimal:
independent product estimates often need O(H^2 epsilon^-2) total samples for
one product by variance-sensitive allocation. Direct tables may still dominate
when there are few short, moderate-probability queries.

5. Why this is not yet the main research choice

The source already makes the observable Markov state representation accessible.
The theorem pays d, so it does not overcome state-space explosion or discover a
small hidden model. Ordinary maximum-likelihood transition learning followed
by model checking/DP has exactly the same benefits and can retain all data.
The question becomes much harder, and more novel, when the local observable
state or useful coarse state must itself be discovered, or when reset costs
make local querying rare. Those steps are not constructed here.

Relevant primary works:
- Global PAC Bounds for Learning Discrete Time Markov Chains (2020),
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7363184/
  Gives reusable global temporal guarantees, topology/probability-floor issues.
- Ashok, Kretinsky, Weininger (2019), PAC Statistical Model Checking for Markov
  Decision Processes and Stochastic Games,
  https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-25540-4_29
  Model-based exploration, confidence allocation and reachability without a
  supplied mixing-time bound. Strong mandatory comparator.
- Cerou, Delyon, Guyader, Rousset (2019), On the Asymptotic Normality of Adaptive
  Multilevel Splitting, https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.08494
  Adaptive splitting already attacks rare events given reaction-coordinate and
  trajectory cloning operations; naive endpoint sampling is too weak a baseline.
- Awad, Glynn, Rubinstein (2013), Zero-Variance Importance Sampling Estimators
  for Markov Process Expectations,
  https://web.stanford.edu/~glynn/papers/2013/AwadGRubinstein13.html
  Approximate zero-variance estimators and Lyapunov-based MSE bounds.
- Munos (2006), Geometric Variance Reduction in Markov Chains: Application to
  Value Function and Gradient Estimation,
  https://jmlr.org/papers/v7/munos06a.html
  Acquired approximations can reduce later Monte Carlo variance geometrically
  until a representation-error threshold. Worth considering as inherited
  mechanism, not claiming residual-based variance reduction as new.

6. Useful Doob-transform obstruction and identity

For finite absorbing chain with failure A and success B, take candidate h>0
on transient states, h=0 on A, h=1 on B. Define Q(x,y)=P(x,y)h(y)/(Ph)(x), where
the denominator is positive. For a Q path reaching B at time tau,

 W = h(x0) product_(t<tau) [(Ph)(X_t)/h(X_t)].                 (D1)

This is the likelihood-ratio identity and E_Q W=P_x0(hit B before A), provided
Q reaches B almost surely and all successful P paths are represented. A bound
on average-state L2(h-h_true) does NOT imply bounded relative variance of W.
Tiny relative errors in regions important under successful paths can dominate.
Uniform residual |log[(Ph)/h]|<=r and deterministic tau<=H give
W/h(x0) in [exp(-rH),exp(rH)], hence relative second moment at most exp(4rH).
For unbounded tau one needs an exponential moment under Q (or a Lyapunov
certificate), not simply finite mean tau. Producing h, computing Ph, sampling
Q and exact likelihood weighting each require source operations; endpoint
reports alone supply none automatically. This is the acquisition burden.
