EXACT PHOTON-PAIR RECOVERY IN THE SPLIT OPTICAL MANIFOLD
2026-10-31. Companion to RECYCLED_OPTICAL_THEOREM_AND_OBSTRUCTION.txt.

Result and scope

At a fixed real polarization, the complete spin-one optical model has an
exact source-independent logical recovery algebra even when Delta1!=Delta2.
An x/y photon transfers the logical qubit from atom to field; the next x/y
photon creates an atom/field entangled pair. A joint Bell measurement of those
two photons and Pauli recovery yields identity on the logical qubit for
each fully retained timing/source history. This is not a moving gate and
not a completed physical optical memory. It requires coherent collection,
polarization-preserving arrival/storage, a full joint two-photon analyzer,
Pauli control or a tracked Pauli frame, and a timed stopping procedure.

The precise outlet is L_l=sqrt(Gamma)D_l^dagger tensor I_E. Its source
independence, including recoil and collection-mode effects, remains a
physical qualification. Photon loss, an analyzer which cannot implement the
needed Bell POVM, and a source-dependent polarization-distinguishing outlet
are not silently included. Source dynamics H_E(t) and common gain A(t) may
otherwise be arbitrary noncommuting operators on the admitted domain.

1. Exact reachable space

Fix epsilon=z and define the code carrier C of dimension six by

  |j,g>=|g,j>, |j,r>=|z,j>, |j,t>=|j,z>, j=x,y.

Thus C=C^2_logical tensor C^3_optical. Define the bright space B of dimension
three by

  |b>=|g,z>, |r_B>=|z,z>, |q_B>=(|x,x>+|y,y>)/sqrt(2).

C plus B is exactly invariant under the full Hamiltonian and every emission
starting from ground spin states. The other three excited vectors are not
entered. Put Delta_bar=(Delta1+Delta2)/2 and delta=Delta2-Delta1. On C,

  H_C=I_logical tensor h_C,

  h_C=I_3 tensor H_E
      +Delta_bar(|r><r|+|t><t|) tensor I
      +(delta/2)(|r><t|+|t><r|) tensor I
      +sqrt(3)(|r><g| tensor A+|g><r| tensor A^dagger).

On B,

  h_B=I_3 tensor H_E
      +(Delta2/3)[[0,0,0],[0,2,-sqrt(2)],[0,-sqrt(2),1]] tensor I
      +sqrt(3)(|r_B><b| tensor A+|b><r_B| tensor A^dagger).

In particular, the bright return chain depends on Delta2, not Delta1.
These identities follow directly from P0=|vec I><vec I|/3,
P1=(I-F)/2, P2=(I+F)/2-P0. They do not eliminate an excited manifold.

2. Exact fine jump identities

The z jump preserves each sector:

  L_z|_C=sqrt(Gamma)I_logical tensor |g><r| tensor I_E,
  L_z|_B=sqrt(Gamma)|b><r_B| tensor I_E.

For j=x,y the same emitted channel has two opposite transitions:

  L_j=sqrt(Gamma)|b><j,t| tensor I_E
      +sqrt(Gamma/2)|j,g><q_B| tensor I_E.

Starting in C, the first x/y photon is necessarily C->B. The second is B->C,
the third C->B, and so forth. Every z photon stays in the current sector.
The no-photon propagator is block diagonal in C plus B because
sum_l L_l^dagger L_l=Gamma I_excited and the Hamiltonian is block diagonal.

The x/y label of an odd photon therefore carries the preexisting logical
qubit; the x/y label of the next even photon is entangled with the returned
atom. Measuring the odd photon in the fixed Cartesian basis destroys that
coherence. Coherently retaining its x/y polarization preserves it.

3. Pairwise instrument identity, with arbitrary source backaction

Retain all times, all z emissions, and all source evolution before and
between one x/y pair. Leave the two x/y polarization labels unmeasured.
For each such auxiliary history h, the four Cartesian pair Kraus operators
have the form

  K_{k j,h}=|k><j|_logical tensor M_h, j,k=x,y.

M_h maps the incoming optical/source state to a state with optical auxiliary
g. It contains every noncommuting common-source propagator, every safe z
jump, and both optical transfer amplitudes. Crucially M_h is THE SAME
operator for every j and k; no source state or averaged susceptibility has
been substituted. This remains true when the initial logical state is
entangled with a reference or correlated with the retained source.

Proof: the C propagators and z jumps are I_logical tensor source/optical
operators. The first x/y jump selects the t auxiliary and removes j from
the atom into the photon, leaving the same |b> for every j. All subsequent
B dynamics is independent of j. The next x/y jump selects q_B and creates
|k,g> with an identical coefficient for either k. Multiplying the physical
operators gives the formula above, including all recoil-free source factors
in their actual time order. Safe z photons after the pair preserve the same
factorization. This is a trajectory identity, not a rare-emission estimate.

A Bell-basis unitary mixing of the four photon pair labels gives

  K_{mu,h}=(1/sqrt(2))sigma_mu tensor M_h,
  sigma_mu in {I,X,Y,Z},

up to known irrelevant phase conventions. Pauli correction makes

  K_corrected_{mu,h}=(1/sqrt(2))I_logical tensor M_h.

Thus each corrected fine history acts identically on every logical vector.
No optical eigenvalue degeneracy and no knowledge of H_E or A dynamics is
required. Concatenating completed pairs preserves this property on their
actual histories. A stored Pauli frame may replace immediate correction
while the fixed-axis logical dynamics remains the identity.

This is stronger than merely observing that a reduced qubit channel happens
to be random unitary: the Bell analyzer can be fixed independently of the
unknown source operators M_h. It remains conditional on supplying the
coherent field operation. It does not establish compatibility with a moving
polarization path, arbitrary logical gates, or a microscopic collection
apparatus.

The independent full-matrix program exact_photon_pair_recovery.py constructs

tensor M_h/sqrt(2) to errors below 4.2e-19. Internal closure and Hamiltonian

the operator proof; they are not a source-state average or an experiment.

4. Bright-return duration can be bounded on one admitted source domain

For Delta2=20 Gamma and A/Gamma=I+B_E with ||B_E||<=1/4, the rational
recycling certificate already constructed applies directly to h_B, even
when Delta1 differs. Every x/y photon exits B. All z recycling is retained.
For any actual source state and common H_E,

  Pr(no B->C return by time t) <= (9/2)exp[-(11 Gamma/180)t].

For Hermitian gain in the same ball the exponent improves to 2 Gamma/15.
Thus a return deadline t_B=(180/(11 Gamma))log[(9/2)/epsilon_B] makes the
complex-gain failure probability at most epsilon_B. This is a sufficient,
conservative certificate, not an optimized practical pumping rate. The gain
must be maintained in the stated ball during that return interval. Its
validity on actual histories makes source reset between pairs unnecessary.

No analogous statement about how rarely a C->B transfer occurs is required
for this algebra. Such transfers may be frequent. Their photon-pair storage,
Bell operations, and actual delay must be charged to the memory protocol.

5. Finite stopping: what a completed pair does and does not provide

A completed x/y pair places the atom in C ground. A later detector signal,
control latency, or finite ramp means the common drive may already have
re-excited it. After the drive is removed and optical settling finishes, an
unpaired x/y photon leaves the atom in bright ground and the logical qubit
in that photon. Waiting with the drive off does not return bright ground
to code. One must retain the photon, repump, obtain the next return photon,
perform the pair operation, and try stopping again.

There is a uniform probability bound on this extra exposure which does not
require a source frequency or derivative bound. Suppose a pair has just
returned to C ground at time zero. Let tau include signal/control latency
and the subsequent drive-off ramp. Let the actual common drive satisfy
||A_on(t)||<=a |u(t)| and define

  Q(t)=sqrt(3)a integral_0^t |u(s)| ds.

The no-photon excited amplitude is at most Q(t): in the interaction picture,
the excited propagator has norm at most exp[-Gamma(t-s)/2], while the
ground-to-excited coupling norm is at most sqrt(3)a|u(s)|. This holds for an
arbitrary purified initial source state. Consequently

  Pr(any photon during the ramp OR a residual excitation afterward)
    <= Gamma integral_0^tau Q(t)^2 dt+Q(tau)^2
    <= (1+Gamma tau)Q(tau)^2.

Here the first term is the exact first-photon probability bound; ALL later
histories after that first photon are included in that event. The second
term is the disjoint no-photon residual-excitation event. The bound therefore
covers a new x/y pair or an odd ending without assuming such events rare in
the preceding memory interval. It is an upper bound; z photons within this
event are harmless and make it conservative.

For latency ell at full amplitude followed by a linear ramp of duration r,
the sharper expression is

  r_stop <= 3a^2[(ell+r/2)^2
            +Gamma(ell^3/3+ell^2 r+(2/3)ell r^2+(2/15)r^3)].

The bound does not grant zero latency or an unprepared optical analyzer.
It specifies the control area and elapsed time that have to be supplied.
After drive-off, p_excited(tau+s)=exp(-Gamma s)p_excited(tau) exactly in this
model. A finite settling deadline leaves that explicit population tail; a
claim about a coherent output instrument must also handle its amplitude.

If a physical apparatus provides polarization-preserving photon arrival
and pair heralds with the stated latency, maintains the source domain, and
can distinguish/retry odd endings without losing the stored qubit, the
uniform r_stop<1 bound supports geometric retry estimates on actual
histories. Those apparatus capabilities have NOT been constructed by the
algebra. In particular an ordinary destructive x/y photon count cannot
serve as a qubit-preserving arrival flag. Full Bell measurement, photon
storage exposure, detector inefficiency, heralding, and finite control
durations must appear in a physical closure calculation.

Conclusion

Actual fine splitting does not by itself destroy all protected quantum
structure: in the admitted recoil-free source-independent outlet model,
logical information alternates between atom and outgoing photon, and a
completed photon pair has an exact common-source recovery identity. Passive
atomic protection, correction from a fixed classical record, and correction
through a coherent photon-pair measurement are different capabilities.
The theorem earns the last identity and a usable return-rate calculation;
it does not supply a complete optical processor or a moving physical gate.
