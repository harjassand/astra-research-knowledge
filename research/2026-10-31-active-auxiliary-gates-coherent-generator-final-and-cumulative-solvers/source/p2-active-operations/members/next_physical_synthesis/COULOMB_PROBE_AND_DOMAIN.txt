AN EXPLICIT CHARGE OUTLET FOR THE ACTIVE-SOURCE HUBBARD INSTRUMENT
Working derivation, 31 October 2026. Root synthesis; not a qualified device.

1. Purpose and the resource boundary

The data comprise two electrons in two loaded spatial orbitals. Their spins
need not be polarized. A retained barrier source E is arbitrary, can be
correlated with the data/reference, and is never reset. Ordinary spin-independent
tunnelling has a Hermitian amplitude G_E with g I <= G_E <= h I. Source dynamics
can fail to commute with G_E. A charge readout is needed after each short tunnel
pulse; resolving BOTH doubly occupied states is essential to reliable return.

Here is a bounded microscopic-mode detector, using density-density Coulomb
coupling, single-particle helper controls, and particle outlets. It avoids
postulating a singlet projector as a detector coupling. Its fresh probe ORBITAL
state, calibrated controls, separate reservoirs and port discrimination are
explicit resources. This does not assert that every auxiliary is unprepared.

The underlying joint mode Hamiltonian contains only one-body hopping,
spin-independent density interactions, source-modulated data hopping, and
helper-to-lead tunnelling. No data pair-star or native spin gate is assumed.
Continuum, orbital and spin-breaking corrections are addressed in section 7.

2. Density interaction gives a charge phase without a spin coupling

Let q=n_L in {0,1,2}, n_R=2-q. A probe electron occupies one of two auxiliary
spatial modes, p=0,1, with n_p1=n and n_p0=1-n. Probe spin is a spectator and
may be mixed. Assume no particle exchange between the data and probe wells.
The diagonal density part of the direct electrostatic interaction is

 H_C = sum_(i=L,R; a=0,1) V_ia n_i n_pa
     = c + a q + b n + chi q n,

 c=2 V_R0, a=V_L0-V_R0, b=2(V_R1-V_R0),
 chi=V_L1-V_L0-V_R1+V_R0.

The helper bias cancels b. The term a q is a data charge energy and cannot
distinguish spin vectors. During a charge read q is conserved. Source-only
and q-dependent source Hamiltonians commute with this scalar charge phase,
provided they do not act on the probe; their strengths and speeds need not be
known. Probe-source cross-coupling is allowed as a bounded correction below.

Nonzero chi is not a fine equality condition. For data sites (-a0/2,0),
(a0/2,0), probe sites (-a0/2,b0),(a0/2,b0), point-charge Coulomb constant C0:

 chi=2 C0 [1/sqrt(a0^2+b0^2)-1/b0] != 0, for a0,b0>0.

For localized finite orbitals replace these V's by their actual direct
Coulomb integrals. An open neighborhood of this geometry still has chi != 0.
At large b0, |chi| ~ C0 a0^2/b0^3. This is a direct, spin-independent coupling,
not an effective spin interaction obtained by eliminating optical levels.

The SAME microscopic Coulomb expansion also contains transition-density
matrix elements. In particular, an occupied probe can modify data hopping
through integral w_L(x)w_R(x) Phi_probe(x) dx. It would be inconsistent to
retain this term for a barrier source while omitting it for the detector.
During readout it belongs in the total residual data hopping h_off; during
an active pulse it must be included in the common Hermitian gain bounds on
the whole allowed probe-occupation domain. Data-dependent helper hopping
is likewise included in the bounded one-probe calibration error nu.
Simultaneous data/probe transition-density terms and particle-exchange terms
need their own bounds. The diagonal density model is exact only when these
specified terms vanish; the physical construction always debits them.

3. One prepared probe and its entire emission history

Prepare the probe orbital |+>=(|0>+|1>)/sqrt(2), close data hopping, hold
H=chi q n for t_phi=pi/(2|chi|), then apply a calibrated Hadamard to the probe.
Ignoring a harmless sign change of the phase, its two port amplitudes are

 k_0(q)=(1+exp(-i pi q/2))/2,
 k_1(q)=(1-exp(-i pi q/2))/2.

The port-0 probabilities are 1, 1/2, 0 for q=0,1,2, respectively.
The Hadamard is a one-particle SU(2) operation from probe hopping and relative
onsite energy. For example H=(X+Z)/sqrt(2) up to a global phase is generated
by (Omega/2)(X+Z)/sqrt(2) for duration pi/Omega. Its charge-dependent Coulomb
detuning during this finite pulse is an error at most 2|chi| pi/Omega in
operator norm after known scalar compensation; finite errors are tolerated
below. Preparation has the same explicit control obligation. One can instead
calibrate a composite pulse over the three detunings. We do not assume a
zero-duration helper rotation or a switchable electrostatic interaction.

After the helper rotation, close its intermode hopping and open two separate
escape ports into empty outgoing reservoirs. The Markov mode model has

 L_j=sqrt(kappa_j) |vac><j|, j=0,1;  kappa_j >= kappa_min>0.

This is orbital notation. For a spinful electron the physical fine jumps are
L_(j,sigma)=sqrt(kappa_j)|vac><j,sigma|, with the outgoing spin retained by
the reservoir. Summing over sigma gives the stated orbital effect. No
polarized probe spin or erasure of an unknown DATA spin is being assumed.

These jumps are the identity on data spin. Charge-dependent diagonal energies
during escape change only phases. The first emitted electron identifies its
port; there is at most one electron. Every emission time is retained in the
fine instrument, and all no-count evolution is included. An observation
window t_out has no-emission probability <= exp(-kappa_min t_out), on every
data/source state with the probe initially OCCUPIED. Vacuum does not emit;
load failures are included in nu. No equality of the two decay rates is needed.

In the wide-band empty-reservoir model these L_j follow from ordinary
probe-to-lead tunnelling. Finite reservoir bandwidth, energy dependence,
collection inefficiency and port misidentification are qualifications of the
outlet, not properties of an arbitrary warm source. A missed emission does
not act on data spin, but must be counted as an erasure or label error.

Before each probe is reloaded, open both escape ports for an emptying interval
t_empty. This bounds a residual occupied state from the preceding test by
exp(-kappa_min t_empty), uniformly on the complete input domain; a failed
earlier count is never silently treated as a successful reset. Reloading a
probe into mode 0 also uses a reservoir and occupancy restriction.
For an empty/singly-occupied probe domain, an injection channel
J_in=sqrt(kappa_in)|0><vac| has loading failure <= exp(-kappa_in t_in) FROM
VACUUM. It does not reset an occupied mode 1. Compulsory emptying followed
by injection has target-orbital population deficit bounded uniformly by
p_reset<=exp(-kappa_min t_empty)+exp(-kappa_in t_in).
For a possibly coherent empty/single-probe input entangled with E, a
population failure bound p_reset is charged conservatively as
2 sqrt(p_reset)+2 p_reset in the complete preparation error nu. We do not
equate population reset with channel reset. This changes only logarithmic
emptying/loading times. Probe spin remains a spectator; the prepared object
is the orbital state, with its source/outgoing-spin channel retained.
Finite charging energy, extra probe orbitals, thermal backflow and imperfect
occupancy blockade must be qualified. An initial loaded probe is not free.
One may use fresh spatial probe slots instead; the same loading resources
and number of electrons are then charged without a reusable-reset claim.

4. Imperfect controls and quantum source noise need not accumulate as a
   coherent logical error

Let the actual complete ONE-probe binary test differ in half-diamond norm
from the ideal test by at most nu on each fixed-q input, uniformly over E and
all actual prior records. It is enough to bound each finite control error,
probe preparation error, outlet error, and any unwanted probe-source
Hamiltonian by Duhamel on its actual duration. In particular a perturbation
R(t), ||R(t)||<=r(t), costs <=integral r(t) dt independently of H_E.
Finite-time loading/escape erasures can be assigned any binary value and
included in nu. Their time and particles are still paid.

The important structural condition is exact q conservation during readout
and spin blindness. We do NOT telescope m copies of this fixed nu as a
logical error: these imperfections merely change the probabilities of the
classical charge test, and all q-sector fine operations still have the same
logical multiplicity action. For any actual history h the conditional
probability p_h of port 0 therefore obeys

 q=0: p_h >= 1-nu;
 q=1: 1/2-nu <= p_h <= 1/2+nu;
 q=2: p_h <= nu.

No independence or classical stochastic replacement of E is assumed.
For nu<1/4, repeat m fresh probes and let F be the port-0 frequency. Classify
q=0 if F>3/4, q=2 if F<1/4, and q=1 otherwise. With a=1/4-nu>0, conditional
Hoeffding/Azuma (bounded-increment supermartingale) gives, in every sector,

 p_meter <= 2 exp(-2 m a^2).

This is an operator effect bound, since it holds for every source/reference
input. It remains true under history-dependent quantum source backaction.
For perfectly calibrated probes the faster classifier declares q=0 on an
all-zero record, q=2 on an all-one record, and q=1 otherwise; its largest
label error is 2^(1-m). We do not use that fragile zero-probability classifier
when nu is nonzero.

Coarse-graining the record gives an approximate complete charge instrument.
The standard sector-instrument completion bound p+sqrt(p), or its conservative
multi-outcome version 2 sqrt(p)+2p, bounds its error from an ideal q projection
with actual source channels on each q sector. The conservative version is
used in the combined cost estimates. The proof inserts the correct q label
in the fine Stinespring dilation, bounds the wrong-label part by sqrt(p),
and completes the omitted sector maps by a positive trace deficit. Fine
records may steer pre-existing data-source correlations; the assertion is
not that each conditional reduced data state stays unchanged.

On q=1, a residual SU(2)-invariant exchange can distinguish singlet from
triplet. It is handled by retaining the corresponding block evolution in
the ideal q-preserving map, rather than claiming identity on all of q=1.
It cannot distinguish triplet orientations. The eventual S/T instrument
still has the required identity on each logical S/T multiplicity.

5. Joint compatibility with the data pulse

The probe is empty between tests. The Coulomb interaction then vanishes
because every n_pa=0; no physical switch of the Coulomb force is needed.
For a finite loading/escape error, the probe may still be occupied. Include
the bounded worst-case residual probe Coulomb detunings in d in the data
pulse lemma AND include its transition-density hopping in the full g,h
gain bounds. For example a Hermitian extra hopping B_probe with norm<=beta
replaces g by g-beta and h by h+beta, with g-beta>0. Alternatively charge a
complete reset-channel failure in the protocol error. A population bound
alone does not justify treating the residual probe as absent.
Since q is a bounded number and the probe is at most singly occupied, this
correction is bounded even when E evolves arbitrarily quickly. It is not an
unbounded receiver quadrature disguised as a bounded detuning.

The admitted joint Hamiltonian thus uses the same data carrier domain for
(i) spin-independent source-modulated hopping, (ii) scalar charge-energy
controls, (iii) capacitive charge probing, and (iv) helper-only irreversible
escape and loading. All fine terms are spin blind. The Coulomb sensor does
not need the barrier source to be initialized or to commute with H_E.

Symmetry here means invariance under rotations of the DATA spins alone.
Joint rotational invariance of data plus a spinful source is insufficient:
a Heisenberg coupling to that source can disturb the data triplet. Other
live data spins are reference systems for the theorem, not parts of E on
which an arbitrary source channel is allowed to act. Inactive edges and
data/probe exchange must be isolated or charged over the full elapsed time.

6. Durations, attempts and what is actually improved

Let one probe cost
 t_probe=t_empty+t_in+t_prepare+t_phi+t_H+t_out+t_control,
with every term finite. One charge read costs m t_probe. A capture and return
protocol with at most N attempts in each phase has at most 2N charge reads,
2Nm consumed probe electrons, and duration

 T_pair <= 2N [t_switch+tau+m t_probe].

For the robust classifier, fixed nu<1/4 and desired per-charge-instrument
error delta require m=O(log(1/delta))/(1/4-nu)^2. With N=O(log(1/epsilon))/r,
delta=O(epsilon/N), the pair-instrument time is

 O( r^(-1) log(1/epsilon) [tau+t_switch+
       t_probe log(N/epsilon)/(1/4-nu)^2] ).

This is a logarithm-squared accuracy cost at fixed admitted parameters, not
a claim of a fast device. If per-probe preparation/outlet errors are fixed
below the contrast margin, no further increase of their precision with m
is necessary. Residual DATA hopping during readout, spin-breaking terms,
or leakage outside the admitted orbitals are different: they can accumulate
with actual time and must appear separately in the final error budget.

7. A microscopic-domain rule and a finite-gap bound

The exact two-data-orbital theorem does not make all higher orbitals warm.
Loaded orbital/charge domains and a maintained reservoir are paid. An active
source with unconstrained Bohr frequencies can bridge an arbitrarily large
orbital or optical detuning; the optical sideband counterexample in
../next_recycling_optics/ shows why a gap alone is not a qualification.

Here is a sufficient bound for an explicitly static microscopic segment.
Write H=H_0+V, H_0=H_low direct-sum H_high, with off-diagonal V only,
||V||<=v, and ordered spectral separation
 inf spec(H_high)-sup spec(H_low)>=Delta>0.
Include the full retained source and all occupied probe modes in these
blocks. Thus the separation is a JOINT energy separation, after source
bandwidth and control energies have been subtracted, not a bare orbital gap.
For v<=Delta/4 there is an anti-Hermitian off-diagonal S with
 [S,H_0]=-V, ||S||<=v/Delta (Sylvester integral). Then

 e^S H e^(-S)=H_0+R,
 R=sum_(k>=1) k/(k+1)! ad_S^k V,
 ||R|| <= v(exp(2||S||)-1) <= 4 v^2/Delta.

Unitary Duhamel and ||e^S-I||<=||S|| imply

 ||e^(-iHt)-e^(-iH_0t)|| <= 2v/Delta+4v^2 t/Delta.

The effective diagonal correction could instead be retained/calibrated to
improve this conservative bound; it is not silently discarded. For K
static segments, telescope their individual bounds. For finite ramps whose
spectral regularity has not been established, the safe Duhamel cost is
integral ||V_off(t)|| dt. Arbitrarily rapid time dependence is not covered
 by the static-segment spectral argument. Source eigenvectors, state and
exact frequencies need not be learned; a coarse joint spectral enclosure
and physically specified controls suffice.

This bounded static Hamiltonian lemma does not by itself qualify the
loading/escape Markov model or an unbounded electronic lead. Reservoir
bandwidth, the occupied/empty Fermi windows across all admitted source
transitions, and the corresponding reduced-channel errors remain separate
physical qualifications. A bare orbital gap cannot substitute for them.

A spin-breaking Hamiltonian remainder of norm b(t) contributes at most
integral b(t)dt to the full half-diamond error. Residual inactive-edge hopping
of norm h_off(t), probe/data exchange, thermal loading errors, and non-Markov
outlet corrections have analogous explicit costs or dedicated bounds.
Spin-independent orbital corrections preserve triplet orientations by SU(2)
symmetry, but can still create false singlet labels through extra orbitals;
they cannot be ignored merely because total spin is conserved.

For exponentially localized wells, probe/data exchange decreases with
wavefunction overlap whereas the direct capacitive signal is algebraic in
separation. This supplies a constructive geometrical way to suppress that
particular unwanted process relative to the meter signal. Numerical
parameters for an actual device, all other noise rates, and a complete
fault-tolerant architecture have not been qualified here.

8. Provenance and comparison boundary

The Coulomb decomposition, Ramsey probabilities, martingale bound and static
joint-gap estimate are derived above. Capacitive charge sensing, stochastic
charge detection and Pauli blockade are established mechanisms; we claim no
priority for them. The contribution sought here is their explicit compatible
composition with a uniform active-quantum-source capture/return instrument.
