Astra research continuation, 31 October 2026

Selected result: a complete singlet/triplet measurement-and-return mechanism

Status: derived and checked in an explicit orbital-mode interaction model.
This is not an experimental qualification, a fault-tolerance threshold, a

1. THE QUESTION I CHOSE

Can the physical algebra of ordinary interactions enforce a useful quantum
instrument even when its tunnelling amplitude is a noncommuting quantum
operator whose state and time dependence are unknown?

I selected a nondestructive two-spin singlet/triplet instrument as the
consumer. Complete success is consequential because this single pair
instrument, a supplied mixed-spin ensemble, classical feedback and pair
selection already suffice for universal quantum computation [R1]. The
universality theorem is established prior work. The present task is earning
its physical instrument, return and resource interface under the active-source
contract, rather than assuming an ideal spin projector or a pair-star gate.

There is a useful obstruction behind this choice. On a two-electron,
two-orbital system, all spin-independent controls act identically on the three
triplet orientations. That protects them, but also forbids a nontrivial gate
within that single triplet. Changing which PAIR is measured in a larger
register supplies noncommuting operations on the register's representation
multiplicities. This changes the required physical control bank.

2. ONE HAMILTONIAN SUPPLIES THE COMMON COUPLING

The data occupy two spin-degenerate spatial orbitals L and R. Ordinary hopping
has the exact second-quantized form

 H_hop=sum_sigma(c_Lsigma^dagger c_Rsigma+h.c.) tensor G_E,
 g I <= G_E <= h I, g>0.

Commonness across spin channels follows from a spin-independent potential.
It is not an equality independently imposed on several optical transitions.
After retaining ALL six states of the two-electron sector, the three
triplets are dark to hopping, while the singlet charge sector is

  |S_11>, |D_L>, |D_R>,

with equal matrix elements sqrt(2)G_E from S_11 to either doubled state.
On-site Coulomb terms and differential bias supply the two charge detunings.
Bounded source-dependent differential shifts are retained, with norm <=d.
The common source Hamiltonian H_E(t) need not commute with G_E and no norm or
derivative of H_E enters the finite-orbital pulse theorem.

The common source term can itself be traced to a Coulomb potential. Projecting
the potential Phi_E(x) of a charge source into real data orbitals gives

 B_E=integral w_L(x)w_R(x) Phi_E(x) dx,

which multiplies BOTH spin hoppings. Source hopping makes this Hermitian
operator noncommute with H_E. A known bare hopping amplitude t0>||B_E||
supplies positivity.
Mirror-even source geometry cancels the differential diagonal shift at a
symmetric nominal setting; actual biased settings use the bounded d instead.

Complete orbital closure matters here too: orthogonality gives
integral w_L w_R=0, so a spatially constant source potential supplies no
useful modulation. The companion Hubbard note constructs nonzero modulation
from the curvature of an actual Coulomb potential using symmetrically
orthogonalized Gaussian wells. Two source positions have distinct gains.
Source/data exchange, other orbitals, and all OFF-state hopping still require
their stated bounds; they are not hidden in the common source term.

3. A SHORT-PULSE PROGRESS THEOREM, WITH NO SOURCE RESET

Tune the desired doubled state D_j into scalar resonance with S_11. Keep the
other doubled state, including its possibly large known scalar energy, in
the exact Hamiltonian. In the common-source interaction picture, the selected
hopping block is sqrt(2)G_I(t), still >=sqrt(2)g I. The unused edge acquires a
scalar phase. The remaining interaction has norm <=M=2h+d.

The time-ordered unitary satisfies

 U_I(tau)=I-i integral V_I(t)dt+R_2,
 ||R_2|| <= M^2 tau^2/2.

The remainder follows by iterating Duhamel once and using unitarity. Hence
both forward and backward selected charge blocks have least singular value

 sigma_min >= sqrt(2)g tau-M^2 tau^2/2.

Choose

 tau=g/M^2,
 r=(sqrt(2)-1/2)^2(g/M)^4.                              (1)

Each selected transition then has probability >=r on EVERY source state,
including states conditioned on all previous results. Arbitrarily rapid
quantum evolution does not remove this lower bound. Finite ramps use the
same proof with time integrals of the gain and norm envelopes.

The rate is deliberately conservative. At g=h,d=0, r>=0.0522. A controller
with additional Hamiltonian information can use longer or better pulses.
This theorem is not an assertion that the repeated protocol is time-optimal.

4. THE OUTLET IS AN EXPLICIT CHARGE METER

During readout close the data barrier and load a separate one-electron probe
into a superposition of two probe orbitals. Let q=n_L in {0,1,2}, and let
n_p be one probe-site occupation. Direct Coulomb density interactions reduce
algebraically to

 H_C=constant+a q+b n_p+chi q n_p,
 chi=V_L1-V_L0-V_R1+V_R0.

An ordinary helper bias compensates b. The a q term is harmless to spin.
For two parallel pairs of sites, chi is nonzero on an open set of geometries;
no exact equality of several microscopic optical matrix elements is required.

Prepare the probe orbital |+>, expose it for pi/(2|chi|), apply its calibrated
Hadamard, then let it escape through separate counted ports. The probability
of port 0 is respectively

                 q=0       q=1       q=2
                  1        1/2        0.

The fine escape operator acts on the probe, not on the data spin. Every
emission time and outgoing probe spin can be retained. Emptying before
loading, finite helper rotations, injection, escape, reservoir freshness,
port collection, and finite control switching are all included in the cost.
The probe orbital IS prepared; the data barrier source is not.

A fixed bounded error nu<1/4 in each complete one-probe test is tolerable.
This can include unknown probe/source interactions bounded by a Duhamel
integral, finite helper rotations and reset/collection failures. Uniformly
on every actual record, port-0 probabilities lie in

 [1-nu,1], [1/2-nu,1/2+nu], [0,nu]

for the three charges. These bounds hold on conditional quantum source states;
there is no assumption that detector trials are statistically independent.
After m probes, thresholds 1/4 and 3/4 on the port-0 frequency give

 p_Q <= 2 exp[-2m(1/4-nu)^2].                          (2)

A bounded-increment martingale proof establishes this uniform label bound.
Because charge is conserved and all operations are identical inside the
required spin multiplicities, it gives a complete charge instrument with
conservative error delta_Q <=2 sqrt(p_Q)+2p_Q, including source and record.
Fixed contrast errors reduce the exponent; they do not create an m nu
logical floor. Charge-changing errors are different and are charged below.

The same Coulomb expansion also creates small transition-density terms.
Probe-induced data hopping is included in OFF-state leakage during readout,
and in the active g,h bounds if a failed reset leaves the probe occupied.
Data-dependent helper hopping is included in the one-probe control error.
Particle exchange with the probe/source is a separate spin-breaking error.
The construction does not obtain a common Coulomb gain by retaining one
off-diagonal matrix element while silently dropping all analogous terms.

5. CAPTURE AND RETURN, INCLUDING THE CHARGE TRAP

Start with one electron on each data site.

CAPTURE: pulse the selected tunnel edge, close the barrier, read all three
charges, and repeat until a doubled charge is seen or N_c trials have elapsed.
Any doubled charge certifies singlet. Triplets never leave (1,1), and every
fine source/probe operation is the identity on their three orientations.
For an initial singlet, the probability of missing it is

 p_miss <=(1-r)^N_c.

RETURN: if the observed charge is D_j, bring THAT doubled state into scalar
resonance, pulse and read again. Repeat using the latest charge label until
(1,1) is confirmed, or N_r trials have elapsed. Then

 p_return <=(1-r)^N_r.

No data electron is discarded in this cycle. A timed-out return is explicitly
flagged. The singlet has rank one, so a separately paid verified singlet
reload can also replace a failed return without discarding additional unknown
spin information. It is never a free recovery operation.

Why full charge measurement is necessary: at symmetric detuning the state
(D_L-D_R)/sqrt(2) is dark to hopping. A detector can create components there.
A continuous return calculation that keeps only S_11 and (D_L+D_R)/sqrt(2)
can therefore miss trapped population. Resolving D_L and D_R between pulses
removes the coherence responsible for this obstruction and yields (1) on
every return trial.

With M_Q=N_c+N_r, the complete pair instrument obeys

 epsilon_ST <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss+p_return
               +M_Q delta_Q+eta_phys.                 (3)

Error means half the diamond norm, including a reference, retained source,
classical outputs, and final data charge. The ideal comparison retains the
actual outcome-dependent source channels. Fine records can steer pre-existing
data/source correlations; no claim is made that each conditional data marginal
is unchanged. After coarse S/T readout the logical instrument is the desired
Lueders instrument. All other live data are reference systems, not arbitrary
source degrees of freedom on which uncontrolled channels may act.

6. LOADING AND ROUTING ARE ACTUAL OPERATIONS

Offline, isolate one spatial orbital and empty it, then inject from an
unpolarized reservoir through J_sigma=sqrt(kappa_in)c_sigma^dagger. On the
empty/singly/doubly occupied orbital domain, each spin hole 1-n_sigma obeys
exact exponential decay. Thus the probability of not reaching double
occupation is <=2 exp(-kappa_in t), even with arbitrary charge-diagonal source
dynamics. Double occupation of this single orbital is a unique spin singlet.
The already proved RETURN protocol separates it. Tracing out one member gives
an independent maximally mixed spin; keeping both supplies a singlet link.
Preparation error and all emptying/injection/verification/return time count.
The reservoir chemical-potential and temperature window must cover intended
addition transitions including the admitted source spectrum while excluding
extra orbitals and backflow. This is a maintained entropy outlet, not a
property of an arbitrary warm source.

Routing can also be compiled without assuming a calibrated exchange SWAP.
For an unknown spin 1 and an adjacent singlet on 2,3, measure pair 1,2. A
singlet result transfers the unknown spin to site 3 without a Pauli correction.
After a triplet, measure pair 2,3 and retry pair 1,2. The identity

 P_12 P_23 P_12=(1/4)P_12

gives exact transfer on every successful full record, even for a reference-
entangled input. Failure after K target attempts is

 (3/4)(5/8)^(K-1),

using at most 2K-1 S/T calls; the expected number is 5. Actual singlet links,
isolated neighbor edges, storage space and physical pair-call times are paid.
This gives a route to pair access, not free long-range connectivity.

For the established STP consumer, the companion resource calculation also
implements growth of a maximally mixed symmetric N-spin block. It proves a
finite cap of O[N^3 log(1/delta) log(N/delta)] pair calls for that stage.
Relative-axis preparation, entangled resource production and the remainder
of the computation still add calls. A fully polarized symmetric input cannot
generate singlets by these measurements: arbitrary dirty spin input is not
equivalent to a mixed-spin supply. No fault-tolerance threshold is inferred.

7. WALL TIME AND THE PHYSICAL DOMAIN

Let t_probe include probe emptying, loading, preparation, phase exposure,
rotation, escape, readout and controls. A complete pair call takes at most

 T_ST=M_Q[tau+t_switch+m t_probe]+t_final_recovery.      (4)

Tunnelling exposure is at most M_Q tau. Every idle qubit is exposed for the
FULL (4), which is often readout dominated. At fixed contrast and gain bounds,
(1)-(3) yield a logarithm-squared accuracy cost. For a finite program of L
pair calls, including loading/routing/resource calls, set the per-call error
to O(epsilon/L) and sum the complete channel errors on all admitted histories.

The executable conservative budget makes the size of these constants visible.
At g=h=1,d=0, probe-test error nu=0.05, and target epsilon=0.001, one sufficient
allocation uses 336 capture attempts, 168 return attempts, and 389 probes per
charge read: at most 196,056 prepared probe uses, plus loading and routing.
Its statistical error bound is 0.00049267, leaving the remaining allowance
for other physical errors. This is a worst-case proof allocation, not an
optimized requirement or device prediction. It expressly prevents reading
the asymptotic logarithms as a practical speed claim.

The full error budget includes residual hopping during readout and on
inactive edges, data/source or data/probe exchange, spin-orbit coupling,
field gradients, discarded orbitals, thermal loading errors and outlet
bandwidth errors. A proved Hamiltonian remainder contributes its actual
norm integral. Fixed spin errors can overwhelm the statistical improvement
as time grows; equation (4) is not optional in a useful operating claim.

The finite-orbital theorem allows arbitrary common H_E. A microscopic
projection cannot inherit that freedom without additional evidence. A source
Bohr frequency can bridge a discarded orbital or optical gap. For a static
segment whose FULL low/high source-plus-carrier spectra have ordered gap
Delta and off-block coupling norm v<=Delta/4, the companion derivation gives

 ||U(t)-U_0(t)|| <=2v/Delta+4v^2 t/Delta.

This follows from a bounded Schrieffer-Wolff generator and unitary Duhamel.
It is a sufficient admission test, not a proof that a particular device meets
it. Time-dependent ramps and unbounded leads need their separate bounds.
The source can be unprepared inside a coarse admitted spectral window without
its eigenstates or detailed dynamics being learned.

8. TWO COMPLEMENTARY RESULTS RETAINED

A. Full optical recycling is now proved in an exact-degeneracy window.
For the complete 12-level spin-one optical model with equal complement
detunings 20 linewidths, a rational adjoint-Poisson witness proves attraction
including every return photon. Its rate is at least 2/15 linewidth for
Hermitian gain ||G-I||<=1/4, and 11/180 for the full complex ball
||A-I||<=1/4. This closes the missing contraction and yields a moving-subsystem
O(1/T) channel bound there. A recorded instrument bound additionally uses a
co-rotating polarization analyzer. It does not qualify an actually split atom.

For split complements, a positive bounded gain coupled to a resonant quantum
source defeats large-bare-detuning protection. Keeping the actual splitting
delta fixed yields the exact harmful first-photon response

 B=delta^2(delta^2+2u^2+Gamma^2)/
   [2delta^4+(4u^2+3Gamma^2)delta^2+(2u^2+Gamma^2)^2],
 u=sqrt(3) eta g0.

At Gamma=g0=1, eta=1/4, delta=2, B=1376/3129. Natural optical return gives
Bell-input trace error 3B/4=0.3298178, independent of increasing bare detuning
in the stated resonant limit. Recovery using the already measured Cartesian
polarization record can lower this to B/2=0.2198786. These are NOT lower bounds
against coherently capturing and jointly measuring the emitted quantum field.
The optical note explicitly separates that larger recovery resource. This is
a source-domain obstruction and a scoped instrument failure, not a universal
impossibility theorem for warm auxiliaries.

In fact, the recovery comparison is constructive. Optimizing a coherent
measurement of just the first photon reduces its best Bell-input error to
[1-sqrt(1-B^2)]/2, which is 0.0509416 in that example. With coherent access
to both the harmful photon and the eventual return photon, a Bell-basis
measurement mixes the four rank-one history maps |k><j| into known Pauli
maps; Pauli feedback gives exact recovery in the limiting model. Coherent
photon collection, storage, a two-photon analyzer and feedback are physical
resources, and earlier Cartesian counts cannot be undone. The larger
resource therefore cannot be ruled out by the fixed-record bound [R9].

Following that correction produced an exact strengthening beyond the resonant
limit. At a fixed real polarization, the FULL split optical model closes on
a six-dimensional carrier C=logical qubit tensor three optical states and a
three-dimensional bright space B. Its C Hamiltonian and all z-polarized
emissions are logical identities. An x/y photon alternates C->B and B->C.
For every complete pair of such photons, with all intervening times and z
recycling retained, the exact four history maps are

  K_(k,j,h)=|k><j| tensor M_h, k,j in {x,y},

with the SAME operator M_h for every k,j, even for arbitrary noncommuting
common A_E(t),H_E(t). A fixed Bell analyzer and Pauli recovery turn them into
I tensor M_h/sqrt(2). This is a genuine static recovery identity in the split
manifold, not an optical-degeneracy assumption. At Delta2=20 linewidths its
bright-return delay reuses the proved complex-gain recycling rate.
The companion STATIC_SPLIT_PHOTON_PAIR_MEMORY.txt also bounds finite stopping
latency/ramp exposure by (1+Gamma tau)Q(tau)^2, with
Q(t)=sqrt(3)a integral_0^t |u(s)|ds. An odd ending leaves the logical qubit
in a stored photon and requires a paid repump/re-pair cycle. This theorem
earns an operator identity and a return rate; coherent collection, arrival
heralds, storage, the full Bell analyzer and endpoint control remain missing
physical operations, and a moving gate has not been constructed.

B. Absorbing transport can change the gate's accuracy law.
For a prepared helper g,e,f, the excitation M tensor |e><g| tensor A_E and
data-independent absorbing e->f decay factor exactly through a gauge when M
is unitary. Each completed emission implements M and retains the full logical
space at the outlet. The inherited contraction gives error <=C^2e^(-2lambda T)
for the field-traced or photon-counted channel, with reset time also paid.
M=(Z_A+iZ_B)/sqrt(2) is an entangler and decomposes into two data/helper
quadrature couplings sharing A_E. This is an explicit circuit-Hamiltonian
opening; a microscopic common-modulation coupler is still unearned. Such
dissipation-driven gates and exponential suppression have strong prior art
[R4,R5]. This result is preserved as organising capital, not substituted for
the physically more explicit Hubbard construction.

9. EVIDENCE AND COMPARISON

The executable checks use NumPy/SciPy and, for the exact optical witness,
Python Fraction rational arithmetic. They check the following concrete risks:

* Full fermionic reduction: residual 4.44e-16.
* 84 constant/switched-source pulse cases, source frequencies up to 5000
  in the chosen units and unused scalar charge gap 1000: smallest checked
  transition probability 0.00319356, above the proved 0.00133726 bound.
* Complete capture/return CP history tails and exact loading adjoint identities.
* Forced-teleportation projector algebra and expected five-call cost.
* Coulomb probe effects on ALL source states through effect eigenvalues,
  and full correlated record probabilities without resetting the source.
* Exact rational optical recycling witness and full 24-state source/atom
  Lindblad evolution, retaining every physical decay channel and return.
* Absorbing gate gauge factorization, including a counterexample where
  logical-blind event probabilities nevertheless leave logical purity 0.82.

Floating-point checks establish their tested cases; they do not prove an
universal inequality, certify experimental performance, or establish novelty.

Strong conventional alternatives receive the same information and resources:

Calibrated exchange: universal exchange-only control has already been
demonstrated with unpolarized gauge spins [R2]. It can be substantially faster
when pulse dynamics can be characterized. Unprepared auxiliary state alone
is not the distinction claimed here.

Pauli-blockade detection: established devices already combine spin-to-charge
conversion, charge sensors, electrostatic control and loading [R3,R6]. The
required task here is the full triplet-preserving instrument plus return.
Some physical blockade regimes relax T0 while preserving T+ and T-, and
therefore realize parity readout rather than the present instrument [R3].

Dissipative and holonomic control: reservoir-driven holonomies, dissipative
computing, discrete jump gates and experimental Zeno gates are serious prior
alternatives [R4,R5,R7,R8]. They can reuse the same source qualifications and
stability proofs. Failure of the old naive coherent route does not settle
their comparison. No claim that this construction defeats an equally
informed optimizer is made.

The selected advance is a complete compatible operation at the stated
Hamiltonian level, with a source-uniform guarantee and a paid physical
cycle. The next decisive physical test is a simultaneous bound on ON gain,
OFF hopping, orbital/source transition spectrum, data-only spin breaking,
and detector contrast over the actual duration. Those quantities determine
whether the proved capability has a useful device operating point.
