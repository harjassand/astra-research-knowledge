HUBBARD CAPTURE, CHARGE MEASUREMENT, AND RETURN WITH AN ACTIVE BARRIER SOURCE
Research contribution for Astra; 2026-10-31. Proofs below are new derivations in this work, not claims of priority. Hardware and STP computational universality are established prior art. This note does not claim a demonstrated device window or fault-tolerant architecture.

1. Consequence and change of consumer

The relevant physical consequence need not be a continuously moving dark-space gate. A complete, nondestructive two-spin singlet/triplet (STP) instrument, including return of the singlet branch to two singly occupied sites, is already a universal computational primitive when combined with supplied maximally mixed spins, classical feed-forward, and pair selection. Rudolph and Virmani proved this universality. Their polynomial construction is not an efficient fault-tolerant architecture; its state preparation and routing costs must be counted.

Here ordinary spin-independent hopping supplies the common quantum coupling by fermionic algebra. Full charge measurement supplies the irreversible outlet. Classical electrostatic detuning and barrier controls select edges and resonance. The source of the hopping can remain in an arbitrary state and evolve noncommutatively throughout. No local spin rotations, calibrated exchange pulse area, or postulated two-register holonomic star are used in the construction.

The essential correction is to retain BOTH doubly occupied charge states. A symmetric three-level Hubbard problem has a dark charge combination. A naive capture-and-continuous-return construction can trap that combination. Complete charge readout between bounded tunnelling pulses removes this obstruction, and yields a uniform success probability on every actual history.

2. Actual two-site Hamiltonian and its exact single-orbital reduction

There are two electrons in two spin-degenerate orbitals L and R. For sigma=up,down, the ordinary second-quantized Hamiltonian is

 H = I_electron x H_E(t)
   + sum_i epsilon_i n_i + sum_i U_i n_i,up n_i,down + V n_L n_R
   + sum_sigma [c_R,sigma^dagger c_L,sigma + c_L,sigma^dagger c_R,sigma] x G(t)
   + charge-diagonal source shifts.

G is Hermitian and g I <= G(t) <= h I, g>0. The common amplitude on the two spin channels follows from spin-independent tunnelling, not an independent equality assumed for two optical legs. The source may be entangled with data, reference systems, other edge sources, and previous measurement records. No bound on dG/dt or ||H_E|| enters the theorem below. A physical low-orbital reduction has its own bandwidth and loading requirements in Section 9.

A minimal explicit source is a barrier two-level defect:

 G(v)=t_c(v) I + t_z(v) Z_E,  H_E=Omega X_E + zeta Z_E,
 t_c(v)>|t_z(v)| on the active setting.

The defect may start anywhere in its full two-dimensional Hilbert space; Omega need not be small compared with the hopping or pulse rate. Spin-independent source shifts of charge configurations are allowed as bounded residual detunings. The interdot barrier control v needs an ON range with the above g,h and an OFF range with a bounded small residual hopping. It need not multiply G by an exactly known scalar over its entire sweep.

Choose the two-electron basis

 |T+>=|up,up>, |T0>=(|up,down>+|down,up>)/sqrt(2), |T->=|down,down>,
 |S>=(|up,down>-|down,up>)/sqrt(2),
 |D_L>=c_L,up^dagger c_L,down^dagger|vac>,
 |D_R>=c_R,up^dagger c_R,down^dagger|vac>.

A fixed convention for fermionic signs gives

 H_triplet = I_3 x H_E'(t),

 H_singlet = I_3 x H_E'(t)
  + |D_L><D_L| x [Delta_L I+B_L(t)]
  + |D_R><D_R| x [Delta_R I+B_R(t)]
  + sqrt(2)(|S><D_L|+|S><D_R|+h.c.) x G(t).

The common (1,1) source shift was absorbed into H_E'. A sign change of one D basis vector changes nothing. The residual diagonal operator has norm <=d. The EXACT triplet Hamiltonian is scalar on every triplet vector, which is stronger than conserving the triplet label.

With epsilon=epsilon_L-epsilon_R and C_i=U_i-V,

 Delta_L=C_L+epsilon, Delta_R=C_R-epsilon.

Setting epsilon=C_R makes D_R resonant with S and leaves Delta_L=C_L+C_R. Setting epsilon=-C_L makes D_L resonant and leaves Delta_R=C_L+C_R. No simultaneous resonance is demanded. The unused charge state's arbitrarily large known scalar energy is retained exactly; it is not adiabatically eliminated. The relative electrostatic control and calibration of C_i are maintained resources.

2A. Deriving the common source operator from ordinary electrostatics

The model above can be obtained from a projected electrostatic interaction instead of assuming separate identical tunnelling amplitudes. Let real localized data orbitals obey w_R(x,y,z)=w_L(-x,y,z), and let the quantum source create the operator-valued scalar potential

 Phi_E(r)=integral K(r,r') rho_E(r') dr',

where K is the ordinary Coulomb kernel and rho_E is a source charge-density operator on its full admitted finite orbital/Fock space. Expanding the DATA density in these two orbitals gives

 H_DE = D_L,E n_L + D_R,E n_R
       + B_E sum_sigma(c_L,sigma^dagger c_R,sigma+c_R,sigma^dagger c_L,sigma),

 D_i,E=integral |w_i(r)|^2 Phi_E(r)dr,
 B_E=integral w_L(r)w_R(r) Phi_E(r)dr.

B_E is Hermitian and the same for both spin channels because an electric scalar potential has no data-spin index. This is the desired common QUANTUM amplitude from an actual ordinary two-body interaction. It is not an equality imposed between independently driven optical matrix elements.

Choose source orbitals centered on the perpendicular bisector of the data pair, each even under x->-x. Their diagonal densities AND off-diagonal density matrix elements are even. Hence Phi_E is mirror-even as an OPERATOR, not just in a particular source state. It follows that D_L,E=D_R,E=D_E. With exactly two data electrons this term is 2D_E, a common source Hamiltonian, and can be absorbed into H_E'. Small departures from this geometry supply the explicitly bounded differential shifts B_L,B_R; exact parity is a simple construction, not an uncharged assumption of infinite tolerance.

There is a necessary cancellation: orthogonality gives integral w_L w_R=0, so a SPATIALLY CONSTANT source potential produces no hopping modulation at all. A nonzero useful matrix element must come from spatial variation. An explicit example shows that the even source construction survives this cancellation. Take normalized real Gaussian orbitals phi_+(x),phi_-(x), of width sigma centered at +/-a, with overlap s=exp(-a^2/sigma^2), and symmetrically orthogonalize them to obtain localized w_L,w_R. For the even Coulomb potential F_y(x)=C/sqrt(x^2+y^2), let A(y) be its average in either displaced Gaussian density and B0(y)=integral phi_+ phi_- F_y. Then

 B(y)=integral w_L w_R F_y = [B0(y)-s A(y)]/(1-s^2)
     = s[<F_y>_central Gaussian-<F_y>_displaced Gaussian]/(1-s^2)>0.

The inequality follows because a symmetric decreasing Coulomb potential convolved with a symmetric Gaussian has its maximum at the origin. For y large compared with a,sigma,

 B(y)=s C a^2/[2(1-s^2)y^3]+O(y^-5).

The cancelled monopole would have scaled as 1/y; the surviving curvature is 1/y^3. Two different source positions y yield different B(y). Thus source motion can genuinely modulate the hopping while the common diagonal source shift remains equal at the symmetric nominal geometry. This elementary example proves a nonzero projected matrix element, not a particular double-well spectrum or device parameter window.

For example, the source may have two orbitals at distinct positions on this perpendicular bisector. Its internal hopping H_E contains Omega(a_1^dagger a_2+h.c.), while its two charge distributions give different contributions to B_E. Thus [H_E,B_E] is ordinarily nonzero. None of its occupations or coherences need to be prepared. A finite set of source orbitals with bounded occupancies has a finite Fock domain, so one can bound b=||B_E|| over that ENTIRE domain, including empty, singly occupied and multiply occupied sectors if all are admitted. The known bare real hopping t0 can then be chosen with t0>b, giving

 G=t0 I+B_E,   g=t0-b>0,   h=t0+b.

Here a fixed site-phase convention has made the bare hopping positive; the same convention also changes the sign of every source-assisted coefficient. The positive B(y) in the illustrative Gaussian convention proves nonvanishing only. It does not assume that a repulsive source increases the magnitude of the physical bare hopping. The norm-dominance condition is insensitive to this sign convention.

The source hopping can be arbitrarily fast relative to the data transition rate in the pulse proof. A complete microscopic device still has the joint orbital/source bandwidth qualification discussed in Section 9; high source Bohr frequencies cannot be ignored when discarding other orbitals or deriving a reservoir window.

An antisymmetric external potential gives opposite diagonal shifts on the nominal w_L,w_R while its off-diagonal integral vanishes by parity, providing a relative detuning control at this projected mode level. An even barrier potential changes the common hopping and common onsite energy. Real relative-detuning controls also change the actual localized orbitals: mirror cancellation of the diagonal source term must NOT be assumed at both resonant operating points. The resulting bounded differential terms belong in d, whereas the common spin-independent hopping survives without mirror symmetry. Real devices have finite orbital changes and ramps; their couplings to discarded orbitals must be bounded rather than treated as cost-free controls.

The OFF setting needs an independent envelope. Cancelling the scalar t0 in a fixed basis does NOT cancel an arbitrary B_E. Physically increasing the interdot barrier can suppress the overlap of the instantaneous localized orbitals, and thereby suppress both their bare hopping and all bounded electrostatic source-assisted off-diagonal integrals. The Gaussian example explicitly has B proportional to the overlap s, while the diagonal charge-sensing Coulomb contrast stays finite as s approaches zero at fixed well locations. This supplies a constructive separation between a QND charge-sensing interval and a source-coupled hopping interval. The resulting h_off and the finite barrier ramp are charged over the FULL readout, loading, idle and control time. This statement calls for a proved or measured overlap/ramp bound in a particular device; it is not an exact switch inferred from the ON positivity condition.

The same expansion must be applied honestly to the nearby prepared Ramsey probe. Its Coulomb field also creates a data off-diagonal hopping term proportional to integral w_L w_R Phi_probe. This term vanishes with an empty probe; during readout it is included in h_off. A failed emptying can alter both data charge shifts and the hopping operator: enlarge the G bounds over that admitted occupied-probe domain, or charge the complete reset error separately. One cannot keep source-assisted hopping from this expansion while silently deleting the corresponding probe-assisted term.

The source can also exert a differential electrostatic force on the Ramsey probe. That is a bounded nuisance interaction on the finite source/probe domain and must fit the per-test contrast budget nu; geometric separation or screening is a control/design resource, not automatic exact isolation. Any data/source exchange term involving overlap of identical fermionic orbitals is a different interaction from the direct scalar potential and must be suppressed or charged. For localized wells direct Coulomb coupling is algebraic in separation while overlap-mediated exchange is exponentially small, providing a constructive geometric separation of those processes without proving a numerical device window.

3. A derivative-free pulse lemma

Fix j=L or R and set its known scalar detuning to zero. Write H0=I x H_E'(t)+Delta_other |D_other><D_other|. In its interaction picture the selected matrix element is sqrt(2) G_I(t), with G_I=U_E^dagger G U_E >=g I. The other hopping edge acquires only a scalar phase. The remaining interaction V_I has norm at most M=2h+d: the hopping block has norm <=2h and the residual charge shift <=d.

For the time-ordered unitary U_I(tau), Duhamel iterated once gives

 U_I(tau)=I-i integral_0^tau V_I(t)dt+R_2,
 ||R_2|| <= M^2 tau^2/2.

This bound uses unitarity of the exact inner propagator, and requires neither an exponential remainder nor a source bandwidth bound. Both selected charge-transition blocks obey

 sigma_min(<D_j|U_I(tau)|S>) >= sqrt(2) g tau - M^2 tau^2/2,
 sigma_min(<S|U_I(tau)|D_j>) >= sqrt(2) g tau - M^2 tau^2/2.

Indeed integral G_I >=g tau I. The statement is an operator singular-value bound, so it remains true with a correlated source and reference. Known unitary factors removed by the interaction picture do not change singular values.

Convenient parameters are

 tau = g/M^2,
 r = (sqrt(2)-1/2)^2 (g/M)^4.

Every selected transition has probability at least r, for every source state and every past history. For g=h,d=0, tau=1/(4g) and r>=0.05224. These are conservative guarantees, not measured rates. Stronger accurately known charge dynamics can be exploited by the conventional controller.

Finite switching ramps can instead be included in the integral: replace g tau by a lower bound on integral g_on(t) dt and M tau by the corresponding norm integral. The lower bound must remain positive after the second-order term. Detuning ramps may occur with the hopping barrier closed, where they preserve charge and spin exactly in the admitted model.

4. Complete adaptive cycle

The charge sensor reads n_L=0,1,2 with the interdot hopping switched off. It must supply a full finite-error QND charge instrument; a classical readout fidelity alone is not enough. Root's companion construction derives this sensor from a prepared orbital Ramsey probe, ordinary Coulomb density-density interaction, local helper controls, and irreversible port-resolved escape. Here its per-use complete instrument error is delta_Q and its actual duration t_Q, including all probe loading, gates, exposure, and disposal.

CAPTURE (input in (1,1)):
  (a) Set a selected double state, say D_R, to scalar resonance.
  (b) Pulse hopping for tau; close the barrier; read full charge.
  (c) Charge 0 or 2 certifies the singlet branch. Save that charge label and enter RETURN.
  (d) Charge 1 means repeat, up to N_c trials. If no double charge is found, report T.

All three triplet vectors remain at charge (1,1), with exactly the same fine source and sensor maps. This includes every probe outcome and escape record. On a singlet input, each capture trial succeeds with probability >=r even if its source was changed by all previous trials. Thus p_miss <=(1-r)^N_c.

RETURN (entered only after a doubled charge is measured):
  (a) If the latest observed double state is D_j, retune that j to scalar resonance with S.
  (b) Pulse hopping for tau; close the barrier; read full charge.
  (c) Charge 1 certifies return to |S> in (1,1). Report S.
  (d) Otherwise save the new D_L/D_R label and repeat, up to N_r trials.

The complete charge measurement makes each failed return start in a definite D_j, tensor an arbitrary conditional source state. Hence q_return <=(1-r)^N_r without source reset. A timeout is a heralded charge-return failure; it may be retained as an erasure or repaired by a separately paid confirmed singlet reload. Once S has been certified, the pair has rank-one spin state, so replacing it with a newly loaded singlet does not discard additional unknown pair spin information. Such replacement still has its own physical loading error and duration.

The main finite protocol uses no electron escape from the DATA dots. The outgoing measured particles belong to the prepared charge probe. Thus capture, readout, and return do not require a hidden spinful electron reservoir cycle on the data.

5. Full instrument error, histories, and duration

Use half the diamond norm as error, including a reference, the source, classical outputs, the returning data charge, and all retained fine measurement histories. With ideal charge reads the STP instrument has a source/history-dependent ideal comparison channel whose logical action is exactly P_S or P_T, with

 epsilon_ST <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss+q_return.

Reason: any history with doubled charge annihilates T, and after successful return acts as |S><S| tensor an auxiliary map. The never-captured history acts as P_T tensor an auxiliary isometry plus P_S tensor an auxiliary contraction B with ||B||<=sqrt(p_miss). Removing the cross term costs at most sqrt(p_miss); assigning its S probability to the correct classical label costs at most p_miss. Return failure costs at most its probability q_return because S has already been measured. This comparison retains the actual auxiliary maps; it does not replace the source by a fixed state.

With M_Q=N_c+N_r maximum charge reads, individual charge-instrument error delta_Q, and total other physical error eta_phys,

 epsilon_ST <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss+q_return+M_Q delta_Q+eta_phys.

The charge-error composition is an actual-history channel hybrid bound: the sensor theorem must apply to all admitted charges and all auxiliary histories, including error histories, or an explicit leakage envelope must be added. One must not invoke a code-only sensor theorem after an erroneous branch.

Maximum tunnelling exposure is M_Q tau. Actual wall time is bounded by

 T_ST <= M_Q [tau+t_switch+t_Q] + t_final_recovery,

where t_Q includes every repeated Ramsey probe, its loading, its calibrated one-electron gates, its Coulomb exposure, the escape/count duration, and resetting/reloading. The memory exposure of unaddressed data qubits is the full wall time, not only M_Q tau. Adaptive branches can be padded with safe idle operations if a fixed time is required.

For p_miss=O(epsilon^2), q_return=O(epsilon), N_c,N_r=O(r^-1 log(1/epsilon)). If a full QND charge instrument is obtained using O(log(M_Q/epsilon)) fixed-cost probes, the total readout-dominated duration is O(r^-1 log(1/epsilon) log(r^-1 log(1/epsilon)/epsilon)). This logarithmic-squared cost does not establish a device advantage: it purchases extra classical measurements and maintained prepared helpers, and real spin errors accumulate over that time.

6. Explicit routing without a calibrated SWAP

Given unknown spin 1 and a loaded singlet on adjacent spins 2,3, perform STP(1,2). A singlet outcome teleports spin 1 to 3 with no Pauli correction; the final pair (1,2) is a singlet. If the result is T, perform STP(2,3), then retry STP(1,2). Continue until an S outcome on (1,2).

The identity P_12 P_23 P_12=(1/4)P_12 holds on three spins. Throughout the protocol total J=1/2, and every operation acts on its two-dimensional multiplicity factor as a rank-one projector while leaving the unknown spin irrep unchanged. Thus every successful full history is a scalar times the same state-transfer isometry, including inputs entangled with an external reference.

First-try success probability is 1/4. Conditional on a failed STP(1,2), the next STP(2,3) has probabilities 3/4 and 1/4; the following STP(1,2) has success probability 1/4 or 3/4 respectively. Therefore each later attempt succeeds with probability 3/8. After K attempted (1,2) measurements,

 p_route_fail = (3/4)(5/8)^(K-1), K>=1,
 number of STP calls <=2K-1,
 expected number of STP calls =5.

Finite-accuracy physical STP calls add their full channel errors over the actual adaptive program. A routing timeout is heralded; it does not mean the unknown state has safely arrived. Long-distance motion requires a path of prepared singlet links and selectable neighboring pairs, or another explicitly provided routing architecture. The three-spin construction removes a hidden coherent-SWAP assumption; it does not make arbitrary pair connectivity free.

7. Loading and local resources

Required prepared/maintained resources are localized orbital occupation, isolated target edges, relative electrostatic detuning, an initially supplied spin ensemble or singlet pairs, an orbital probe with calibrated local operations, a counted irreversible escape outlet, and classical feedback. Their entropy and timing costs are not attributed to an unprepared source.

A same-orbital two-electron load is a spin singlet by antisymmetry. The following explicit OFF-LINE loading cycle earns that resource at the same finite-mode level as the data and probe Hamiltonians. This cycle is for fresh ancillas or an already certified rank-one S branch, not for an arbitrary unknown data pair.

Isolate one data orbital L, close interdot hopping, and first empty it into a prepared outgoing reservoir. Spin-independent removal jumps J_out,sigma=sqrt(kappa_out)c_L,sigma give d<n_L>/dt=-kappa_out<n_L>, so the residual occupied probability is <=2 exp(-kappa_out t_empty). This compulsory emptying avoids assuming a prior unknown charge state was successfully reset. A verified vacuum or a fresh empty slot can replace the emptying step.

Then expose the isolated L orbital to a filled unpolarized incoming reservoir, with injection jumps

 J_in,sigma=sqrt(kappa_in)c_L,sigma^dagger, sigma=up,down.

Pauli exclusion prevents two particles in one spin mode. Let h_sigma=1-n_L,sigma be a hole. Every spin-independent charge-diagonal Hamiltonian, including arbitrary quantum-source dynamics and charge-dependent source operators, commutes with h_sigma. The adjoint loading generator gives exactly

 d<h_sigma>/dt=-kappa_in<h_sigma>.

Hence, from vacuum or any empty/singly/doubly occupied input, the probability of not having both spin modes filled obeys

 p_load <= <h_up+h_down> <=2 exp(-kappa_in t_in).

The fully filled single orbital is the UNIQUE state D_L, a spin singlet. On every successful fine history its spin/orbital factor is therefore pure and factors from all source and spectator systems. There is no need to prepare reservoir spin polarization. Incoming spin, injection times, outgoing particles and all failed loading histories are included; a successful two-electron occupancy check or a complete two-injection record may herald completion. The physical input and output reservoirs and their occupancy/temperature are maintained resources.

Turn the loading reservoir off and apply the RETURN protocol from D_L. This creates a separated spin singlet without an adiabatic splitting assumption. An optional spin-independent disposal of one electron leaves the other exactly maximally mixed and independent of the retained source and other spins on the ideal successful branch. Thus the same loaded mode Hamiltonian supplies both singlet links for routing and independent maximally mixed input spins for the established STP consumer.

The stated injection model needs an actual addition-energy window: the incoming chemical potential lies above all intended first/second addition energies and below the first unwanted higher-orbital addition energy, with margins for the full admitted source transition band, charging shifts, reservoir temperature, and controls. The reservoir density/coupling must provide a rate lower bound over that complete window. Thermal backflow, third-electron loading, orbital excitation, probe/data exchange and finite reservoir bandwidth are explicit physical errors. A bare orbital gap or a bound only on the source's initial energy is insufficient. The low-temperature filled incoming and empty outgoing reservoirs are prepared resources. During an explicitly static microscopic segment a joint data/source gap may qualify the single-orbital truncation; an arbitrary external high-frequency drive cannot be excused merely by the static energy gap.

The duration of one fresh separated singlet is at most t_empty+t_in+t_load_switch+N_r(tau+t_switch+t_Q), plus any finite occupancy verification. Its data charge exposure, all reservoir particles, the source evolution and detector probes are charged. From a truly empty initial slot the ideal loading channel is number-block diagonal and its failure probability contributes directly to the state error; resetting an arbitrary coherent charge input instead requires the corresponding complete reset/instrument bound, not just a population statement.

Alternatively, independent unpolarized single-electron loads at zero field supply the maximally mixed spin resource used by the established STP universality theorem. Correlations, polarizations, and charge-loading failures must be bounded. An arbitrary dirty many-spin input is not promised to work: a fully symmetric ferromagnetic input has only triplet outcomes and cannot generate singlets by STP alone. This is a decisive resource counterexample.

For a supplied ideal STP program of L calls, count its ancilla production, off-line entanglement preparation, routing, and readout calls inside L. Choosing each physical call's complete error O(epsilon/L) gives a composed error O(epsilon) without preparing or estimating the barrier source. This finite-program simulation does not establish a fault-tolerance threshold for a fixed noisy device. Rudolph--Virmani explicitly do not supply such a threshold.

8. Two instructive obstructions

(i) At symmetric detuning with Hermitian G, |D_->=(|D_L>-|D_R>)/sqrt(2) is dark to hopping. A physical detector resolving D_L versus D_R can create D_- components. Monitoring only the return charge S while assuming an isolated S<->D_+ two-level model can leave those components trapped. The pulsed full-charge protocol explicitly handles this state, rather than ignoring it.

(ii) On a single two-site, two-electron, one-orbital problem the SU(2) representation is a triplet of multiplicity one plus three copies of the singlet. Every spin-independent Hamiltonian is scalar on the triplet. Consequently electrostatic controls and spin-blind dissipation alone cannot enact a nontrivial moving logical gate within that triplet. A holonomic construction requiring such a gate cannot be obtained just by renaming this protected subspace. Computational power is recovered by measuring DIFFERENT pairs in a larger register, where spin-representation multiplicity supports noncommuting operations.

9. Scope of the physical domain and perturbations

The one-orbital Hubbard model is an ordinary physical interaction model, not a proof that every semiconductor device meets it. The admitted source can be the full finite-dimensional barrier defect, while orbitals and electron-number sectors remain loaded and energetically constrained. A microscopic reduction requires the discarded orbital/valley/spin-dependent couplings, source excitation energies, control bandwidth, reservoir loading bandwidth, and thermal occupation to be compatible with the orbital gaps. The pulse theorem's independence of ||H_E|| does not erase this microscopic requirement. No numerical device window is claimed here.

If omitted terms have a proved bounded interaction-picture norm integral eta=integral||Delta H(t)||dt over the admitted joint domain, their full-channel half-diamond error is <=eta. Residual interdot hopping during all probe reads and detuning ramps contributes through this actual total-duration bound. So do spin-orbit terms, nonuniform magnetic fields, hyperfine gradients, and imperfect isolation of spectator edges. Spin-independent higher orbitals can spoil charge blockade and thus label selectivity by allowing double occupation with antisymmetric spatial states. They do NOT, by themselves, distinguish the three triplet orientations: full invariance under rotations of the DATA spins makes each fine operation identity on the J=1 spin irrep tensor an orbital/source map. This requires data-only SU(2) invariance, not merely simultaneous rotations of data and a spinful source. A rotationally invariant data-source spin exchange can preserve total angular momentum while disturbing data triplet orientations.

A global spin rotation identical on every electron is compatible with reference-free STP computation, but an independent field gradient is a different error. A fixed physical symmetry-breaking rate cannot be ignored while the logarithmic repetition count grows: a typical upper bound has a decaying statistical term plus an increasing eta_phys(T). The sensor controls and loading windows must be chosen before claiming a usable operating point.

10. Fair comparison

Calibrated exchange/ESR control is the strong conventional baseline when the Hamiltonian and phase references can be characterized. It can be far faster than this conservative repeated-charge protocol. The present proof answers a different information contract: only a common spin-independent hopping operator range and residual-shift bounds are supplied, while its source can be noncommutatively active. It does not prove that every robust coherent or holonomic controller fails under that same contract.

Conventional dissipative or latched Pauli-blockade readout already combines charge conversion, detectors, and loading in devices. Some versions destroy the data charge configuration or use spin-selective reservoirs. They should be compared against the complete nondestructive STP task, including triplet coherences, fine emissions, recovery, spin preparation, and the same source uncertainty. The pulse protocol is itself a conventional measurement-and-feedback strategy with a source-uniform guarantee. Calling it new hardware would be misleading.

Continuous monitoring can be more efficient when a Markov charge detector and its joint backaction are justified. The pulsed route pays extra measurement time to make the detector QND while hopping is off; this is what permits a direct physical sensor construction without assuming an exact charge-projector Lindblad jump during hopping.

Holonomic approaches protect encoded motion under their own qualified controls. In this mechanism two-spin electrostatic controls do not supply nontrivial triplet holonomies. The measurement-only consumer therefore changes the required physical operations rather than silently adding missing spin controls or a pair-star coupling.

The accompanying exact finite-matrix program verifies the fermionic reduction to numerical precision, 60 constant and 24 piecewise-changing noncommutative-source pulse cases, actual-history capture/return tails without source reset, the dark-charge counterexample, and the three-spin routing identities. The selected pulse bound was r=0.0013373 while the smallest sampled transition probability was 0.0031936. These checks validate calculations, not a microscopic device or a general worst-case optimum.
