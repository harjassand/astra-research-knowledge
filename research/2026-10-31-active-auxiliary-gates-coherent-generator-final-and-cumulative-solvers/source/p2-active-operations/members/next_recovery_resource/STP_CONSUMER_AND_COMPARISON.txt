SINGLET/TRIPLET CONSUMER, RETURN, AND FAIR COMPARISON
Research checkpoint, 2026-10-31

1. WHAT A PHYSICAL S/T INSTRUMENT WOULD BUY

Rudolph and Virmani, Nature Communications 14, 7800 (2023),
doi:10.1038/s41467-023-43481-y; primary full text:
https://arxiv.org/html/2107.03239v3
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10684540/

They establish universal quantum computation using only nondestructive
two-spin singlet/triplet measurements, an independent maximally mixed spin
supply, arbitrary-pair selection, and adaptive classical processing. They
build rotational reference resources and entanglement using the measurements
themselves. Their proof is polynomial, is not resource-optimal, and explicitly
does not establish fault tolerance. Arbitrary unprepared spins are insufficient
without a singlet-yield promise: a fully symmetric polarized ensemble can
produce only triplets. Arbitrary-pair routing, loading, and finite readout time
are physical costs, not consequences of the theorem. [Summary of this source]

The following calculations make one part of that consumer more explicit.
They are elementary derivations here, rather than a claim of a new
measurement-only universality theorem.

2. A CHARGED SYMMETRIC-STATE PREPARATION BLOCK

Let rho_sym,K be the normalized projector onto the fully symmetric space of
K spin-1/2 particles. Load a fresh independent I/2 spin. Put n=K+1. Repeatedly
choose a uniformly random pair of the n spins and measure s/t. On the first
singlet, discard the measured two spins and retain the other K-1. If m
measurements all yield triplet, retain all n spins as the candidate enlarged
symmetric source.

All needed dynamics are pair s/t measurements; no spin rotation, parity gate,
Bell gate, or polarization reset is added. Pair selection and disposal are
still required physical operations.

Use S=sum_i s_i and P_s,ij=1/4-s_i dot s_j. Then

 sum_(i<j) P_s,ij = n(n+2)I/8 - S^2/2.                  (1)

The maximum-spin J=n/2 sector is its kernel. The next sector J=n/2-1 has
eigenvalue n/2. Dividing by binom(n,2), a random pair has singlet effect at
least 1/(n-1) on every nonsymmetric total-spin sector.

The input rho_sym,K tensor I/2 has only the two total-spin sectors above,
with weights

 p_good=(K+2)/(2K+2),   p_bad=K/(2K+2).

Every s/t measurement preserves total spin. Thus the nonsymmetric component
survives m random triplet results with probability exactly

 q_K^m,  q_K=1-1/K,                                    (2)

where K=1 gives q=0 and one s/t measurement is sufficient. Its unnormalized
weight in a retained block is p_bad q_K^m. The good component is unchanged.
Consequently, conditional on all-triplet retention, the half-trace-distance
to rho_sym,K+1 is

 p_bad q_K^m / (p_good + p_bad q_K^m) <= q_K^m.           (3)

One can therefore choose m_K=ceil[K log(1/delta_round)] (at least one) to
make the false-symmetric event probability at most delta_round. The exact
q_K formula can give a slightly shorter block.

On a detected singlet, the other K-1 spins are exactly rho_sym,K-1: total
spin was (K-1)/2 and removing a spin-zero pair leaves the remaining spins in
their maximum-spin representation. Rotational invariance then forces their
state to be maximally mixed in that representation. This statement holds
for each fixed measurement-choice/outcome record.

3. EXPLICIT RANDOM-WALK COSTS

The ideal growth walk has states K=0,...,N, starts at 1, and moves K->K+1
with p_K=(K+2)/(2K+2), otherwise K->K-1. State 0 restarts with a freshly
loaded mixed spin. State N completes the resource. Elementary recurrences give

 success probability per attempt = (N+1)/(2N),
 expected steps per attempt = (N^2+3N-4)/6,
 expected sum of K over an attempt = (N^2-1)(N+2)/12.

After restart until success, these become

 E[steps] = N(N+4)(N-1)/[3(N+1)],
 E[sum K] = N(N-1)(N+2)/6.                              (4)

The mean remaining number of growth steps from any K>=1 is at most the
first quantity in (4). To get a fixed-duration protocol, let b=ceil(2E[steps])
and cap the walk after R=b ceil(log_2(2/delta)) growth steps. By the strong
Markov property, ideal failure to reach N by that time has probability
at most delta/2. Choose delta_round=delta/(2R); a union bound on false
symmetric blocks contributes at most the other delta/2. A sufficient bound
on primitive pair measurements is then

     R { ceil[(N-1)log(2R/delta)] + 1 }.

Its scaling is O[N^3 log(1/delta) log(N/delta)]. Expected pair-measurement
cost before this cap is bounded by the second expression in (4) times
log(2R/delta), plus the first expression in (4). Qubit loading/discard counts
are of order the number of growth steps, before routing costs.

This is only the first symmetric-source stage. Relative-axis localization,
cluster generation/fusion, algorithm execution and readout are additional
parts of the published STP consumer. It is not a complete practical compiler
or a threshold estimate. The calculation shows explicitly that a statement
like "only s/t measurements" still has a nontrivial resource bill.

If each physical s/t operation has uniform full-instrument error eta_j for
arbitrary retained source states, the capped consumer acquires an additional
error at most sum eta_j, plus loading, disposal and routing debits. An
inverse-polynomial per-measurement error can be sufficient for a polynomial
size ideal computation, but no fault-tolerance claim follows from this sum.

4. PHYSICAL RETURN AND WHAT MAY BE DISCARDED

The ideal singlet branch has rank one:

 (P_s tensor I) rho (P_s tensor I)
       = |S><S| tensor rho_rest,source (unnormalized).    (5)

Therefore, after a reliably heralded singlet, the pair carries a known state.
Charge return need only preserve that singlet. Alternatively, replacement
with a freshly supplied singlet gives exactly the same spin instrument,
provided the rest/source state is left alone. The singlet supply, loading,
charge verification and elapsed time must then be charged; replacement is
not a free recovery oracle.

The triplet branch has rank three. Its full state may remain entangled with
the rest of the computer. Every fine tunnel, sensor, emission and return
operation must therefore act as identity on that three-dimensional space.
Commuting with P_t, or merely obtaining the correct s/t label, is insufficient.

A spin-independent Hubbard pair in a single admitted orbital supplies the
right representation structure: data-only SU(2) rotations act irreducibly on
the triplet and all spin-independent orbital/source operations are scalar
there. The qualifier is DATA-ONLY symmetry. Invariance under joint rotations
of data and a spinful source allows, for example, a Heisenberg data-source
interaction which disturbs the data and does not certify the instrument.
Extra orbitals, spin-orbit tunneling and field gradients have to be bounded
in the admitted model rather than hidden inside an arbitrary source.

5. FAIR CONVENTIONAL COMPARISON

(a) Calibrated exchange is a strong alternative with related hardware.
Weinstein et al., Nature 615, 817-822 (2023),
doi:10.1038/s41586-023-05777-3, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03605,
demonstrate universal exchange-only encoded logic in silicon with an
unpolarized, unmeasured gauge spin per encoded qubit. It uses calibrated
partial-swap pulses and explicitly handles gauge freedom and leakage in
its gate sequences. Unprepared gauge auxiliaries by themselves are therefore
not a new advantage. The relevant proposed distinction is a guarantee under
coarse bounds on a dynamically noncommuting quantum gain, provided the full
new measurement/return cycle is actually earned. [Summary of this source]

(b) Ordinary Pauli-blockade charge readout already supplies the basic
spin-to-charge principle. The task distinction is full triplet-preserving
instrument versus a classical label or parity readout.
PRX Quantum 2, 010303 (2021), "Pauli Blockade in Silicon Quantum Dots with
Spin-Orbit Control", doi:10.1103/PRXQuantum.2.010303, experimentally and
theoretically distinguishes true singlet/triplet blockade from a regime
where T0 relaxes into S while T+ and T- remain blockaded. Their tuning of that
relaxation over four orders of magnitude is direct evidence that the
distinction matters in actual devices. [Summary of this source]

(c) Dissipative gates, holonomic gates, and ordinary calibrated charge
transfer can exploit the same extra source information if it is available.
A failure of one coherent hot-source path does not compare against all of
them. Under a known, stable scalar exchange pulse, coherent exchange may be
far faster than repeated charge checks. Under the same unknown-quantum-source
contract, an ordinary experimentalist may simply use the proved repeated
measurement protocol. That is reuse of a new reliability guarantee, not a
separate practical performance advantage.

(d) Finite readout matters. If one s/t decision takes R0 repeated trials and
each trial uses a charge discriminator of error delta_c, its total error
contains R0 delta_c. Logarithmic receiver settling/integration for that
discriminator can give a product of logarithms in the duration. Do not state
an overall logarithmic gate time without counting all trial readouts, ramps,
charge returns and the required per-trial error. An independently proved
continuous or one-sided herald detector can alter this accounting.
