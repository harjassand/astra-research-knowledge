EXPLICIT DARK-SPACE GATE CONTROLS AND THEIR PHYSICAL BOUNDARY
Complement to the root dissipative common-quantum-gain theorem.
31 October 2026. Ordinary derivations and numerical checks, not a device
qualification or a priority claim.

1. What is constructed

This note gives explicit finite-level, at-most-two-site control Hamiltonians
whose dark-space parallel transport implements arbitrary one-qubit gates and a
controlled-Z. The common gain is an arbitrary retained positive operator G_E(t)
with g_- I <= G_E(t) <= g_+ I. It may fail to commute with H_E(t). The root proof
supplies the dynamical following/error estimate using a damped excited level.
Here we construct the moving dark spaces and the actual finite list of coupler
matrix elements, rather than assume a programmable projector or a logical gate.

The result is at the specified few-level interaction model. A microscopic
implementation of every such matrix element, its common gain, spectral
isolation, and its loss channels has NOT been established. The distinction is
material. Holonomic universality is well established; the potential added
content is the root robustness theorem for noncommuting retained quantum gain.

2. One-qubit control

Each carrier has states |0>, |1>, |a>, |e>, |f>. The first two hold the logical
qubit. The helper |a> and excited |e> are initially unoccupied, and |f> is an
orthogonal error flag. These are not arbitrary unprepared receiver states: the
gate assumes the data carrier has been prepared in its computational code.
The uncontrolled retained source E can be in any state and can be entangled
with that code and with an external reference.

Pick any logical ray
  |u> = cos(beta)|0> + exp(i chi) sin(beta)|1>
and let |v> be its orthogonal logical companion. Classical amplitudes and
phases define
  |b(s)> = sin(theta(s)) exp(i phi(s)) |u> + cos(theta(s)) |a>.
Use the explicit Hamiltonian
  H(s,t) = I_data x H_E(t)
           + (|e><b(s)| + |b(s)><e|) x G_E(t),
with s=t/T, together with L=sqrt(kappa)|f><e| x I_E.
This means exactly three independently specified ground-to-e matrix elements,
all multiplied by the same quantum operator. Their ratios and relative phases
are controlled; their common scale is not.

Two dark vectors are
  |v>,
  |d(s)> = cos(theta)|u> - sin(theta)exp(-i phi)|a>.
Both H-H_E and L annihilate the dark space identically, including for a
noncommuting source G_E. At fixed s, the only coupled pair is |b>,|e>.

The connection is
  <d|dot d> = -i sin(theta)^2 dot phi,
  <v|dot d> = <d|dot v> = 0.
Thus a path beginning and ending at theta=0 gives
  U_logical = exp(i gamma)|u><u| + |v><v|,
  gamma = integral sin(theta)^2 d phi.
Explicit loop: smoothly increase theta 0->theta_0 at phi=0; increase phi
0->2 pi at fixed theta_0; smoothly decrease theta to 0. Then
  gamma = 2 pi sin(theta_0)^2.
Any gamma modulo 2 pi and any logical axis are available from those control
coefficients. The full time schedule is provided in holonomy_compiler.py.

A shorter sign loop implements a phase pi with real coefficients relative to
the fixed ray u: theta(s)=pi(3s^2-2s^3), phi=0. The dark vector starts at u and
ends at -u. Its Berry connection vanishes in this real frame, and the endpoint
frame changes sign. The physical code projector closes. This is a geometric
reflection, not a claim that an open path has an arbitrary Berry phase.

3. A two-site entangler with a finite list of two-body couplers

For sites A and B define local transition operators
  T_1^j = |e_j><1_j|,
  T_a^j = |e_j><a_j|.
The pair gate uses
  K_AB(s) = c_1(s) T_1^A x T_1^B
             + c_a(s) T_a^A x T_a^B + h.c.,
  H_AB(s,t) = I_AB x H_E(t) + K_AB(s) x G_E(t).
This is two specified products of local transitions plus their adjoints. It is
an at-most-two-site interaction on fixed finite-dimensional carriers. It is
not an arbitrary projector on an exponentially large register.

Take either a local decay on A,
  L_A = sqrt(kappa)|f_A><e_A| x I_B x I_E,
or local decays on both sites with rates summing to kappa. Initial support is
the four-dimensional logical code C=span{00,01,10,11}.

The exact accessible no-jump subspace is
  span{00,01,10,11,aa,ee} x E.
The first three vectors are spectators annihilated by the pair Hamiltonian.
On span{11,aa,ee}, the Hamiltonian is exactly a lambda:
  K_AB = |ee>(c_1<11|+c_a<aa|)+h.c.
The no-jump decay on |ee> is -i kappa/2. A decay sends it to |f_A,e_B> (or
|e_A,f_B>), outside the code and outside all driven transitions. This is an
absorbing error flag, not a successful measurement-conditioned gate branch.

Set real coefficients
  c_1(s) = sin theta(s), c_a(s)=cos theta(s),
  theta(s)=pi(3s^2-2s^3), 0<=s<=1.
The four moving dark vectors are
  |00>, |01>, |10>,
  |d_11(s)> = cos theta(s)|11> - sin theta(s)|aa>.
They are annihilated by every actual jump and by K_AB. The dark connection is
zero in this real basis, while |d_11(1)>=-|11>. Thus parallel transport is
  U_AB = diag(1,1,1,-1),
the controlled-Z gate.

The endpoint code equals the start code; all helper populations vanish in the
ideal gate. The gain's state and time history have not entered this algebra.
Combined with the one-qubit phase rotations, this is a scalable universal gate
set at the given two-site model, using a constant local dimension per qubit.
Only the addressed pair's two couplers may be active during its gate. Other
qubits are spectators, and spectator errors must be charged if that ideal
switching fails.

At s=1, c_a=-1 instead of its initial value +1. This is not silently treated as
the same physical Hamiltonian. Park c_1=0 and restore c_a from -1 to +1 over
a finite additional interval, for example c_a=-1+2(3r^2-2r^3), 0<=r<=1.
Throughout that reset the logical code is exactly decoupled, even where c_a=0
and the bright damping gap vanishes. The gain source evolves under H_E, and
residual leakage from the actual finite-time gate undergoes a CPTP evolution;
its previously charged distance cannot increase. The reset duration and source
maintenance are real operating costs. The root following theorem is needed
only on the moving-code interval, not on this fixed-code reset.

For the moving interval,
  max_s |theta'(s)| = 3pi/2,
  integral_0^1 |theta'(s)|^2 ds = 6pi^2/5.
The projector speed is ||P'(s)||=|theta'(s)|. These exact constants can be
inserted into the root finite-time estimate. A complex meridian-circle loop
with u=|11> also gives any controlled phase; its coefficients and endpoint
Hamiltonian close exactly.

4. Resources and state domains

The source E is retained and needs no purification or reset. Its coupling is
active during the gate, not frozen in the theorem. However, all source states
encountered must remain in the positive bounded-gain domain. A physical
confinement or higher-moment bound cannot be inferred merely from G>0.

The data helper levels |a>,|e> begin empty. Occupation of |a> is deliberately
used coherently during the gate, and it returns to zero ideally. The source can
be correlated with the input, but the initial carrier must lie in C x E. This
is standard encoded data loading, not an arbitrary-all-ancillas claim.

The engineered dissipative outlet is fresh and permits e->f decay. It is an
entropy resource. The ideal dark path emits no photon; finite-speed leakage
can emit one or more error photons depending on the actual loss design. For
the absorbing-flag construction each run has at most one primary e->f jump
because the flag decouples. Detecting that flag, replacing lost carriers and
performing fault-tolerant recovery require further operations. The unconditional
root channel error must include all such jumps even if no detector is used.

Arbitrary initial data is not provided for free. The scheme transforms a
supplied logical state; loading, preparation of a known code state, readout,
interconnect, and fault tolerance are distinct tasks. Neither a low no-jump
error nor a survival-conditioned fidelity is a full-device fidelity.

Circuit composition also needs care. A gate estimate only for code-supported
inputs cannot literally be reapplied to an actual prefix with leakage without
an extension argument. Uniform finite-source bounds allow telescoping with
ideal code prefixes and actual CPTP suffixes. If one instead relies on source
domains established only for actual histories, one must combine that with a
leakage-aware extension, a flag/return operation, or a suitable energy-domain
argument. The existing readout theorem's actual-prefix telescope should not
be copied without checking this distinct gate-entry condition.

5. What a microscopic pair coupler would have to supply

A direct resonant dipole or exchange interaction is one possible target,
because it is naturally a sum of products of single-site transition operators.
The pair-star requires both |11><->|ee> and |aa><->|ee>, with the same quantum
spatial/amplitude factor and separately controlled scalar coefficients. It
also requires all undesired computational transitions to be absent or bounded.

For an axial dipole tensor, conservation of total magnetic quantum number can
help: assign m(1_A)=+1, m(1_B)=-1, m(a_A)=-1, m(a_B)=+1 and m(e_A)=m(e_B)=0.
Both desired pair channels preserve total m=0; cross channels |1a>,|a1> have
total m=+/-2 and cannot connect to |ee> under exact axial symmetry. A logical
zero must be in a genuinely forbidden or suitably isolated storage state.
This is only a selection-rule target, not a selected atomic species or a
demonstrated operating window. The complete dipole manifold includes other
levels; Wigner-Eckart amplitudes, energy resonance, polarization, spontaneous
decays, and switching must all be worked out for a proposed species/device.

Making the pair transition resonant may require opposite energy ordering of
the local transitions (one site raises and one lowers) or a parametric drive.
A pair of optically excited states ordinarily has decay channels from the
would-be code and is not automatically a useful carrier. Microwave/hyperfine,
metastable, or driven circuit transitions may avoid that particular issue,
but none has been qualified here. Spectral/RWA elimination needs the local
orbit of G_E; a large detuning alone does not suppress an arbitrarily fast
noncommuting source which can supply a resonant frequency.

Likewise, a positive distance factor C/r^3 on a bounded fixed-axis domain is
compatible algebraically with noncommuting motional kinetic energy. It does
not establish that this domain is invariant, that transverse motion preserves
the tensor, or that control drives retain one exact common factor. These are
the same kinds of orbital qualifications that prevented promotion of the
earlier molecular architecture. They remain explicit.

The axial selection statement follows directly from the standard dipole
tensor V_dd proportional to (d_A dot d_B-3d_Az d_Bz)/R^3: its spherical terms
carry q on one atom and -q on the other, so total m is conserved. A primary
derivation and a warning about complete degenerate manifolds are in Walker and
Saffman, Phys. Rev. A 77, 032723 (2008), arXiv:0712.3438, Eq. (27). They show
that weak/Foerster-zero combinations can survive despite a large mean pair
coupling. This is why no g_- is inferred here from a selected scalar channel.

6. Strong alternatives and useful failed imports

Holonomic gates are established. Duan, Cirac and Zoller (Science 292, 1695,
2001; arXiv:quant-ph/0111086) give laser tripod one-qubit gates and a two-ion
lambda on {|11>,|aa>,|ee>} from bichromatic sidebands. Their pair Hamiltonian
contains products of local transition operators, and their motional domain
need not be the oscillator ground state if the Lamb-Dicke requirement holds.
The effective elimination also has Stark shifts and heating conditions. That
work is a strong baseline, not evidence for arbitrary noncommuting quantum
gain in the present theorem.

Wassner, Guaita, Eisert and Carrasco (Quantum 10, 2080, 23 Apr 2026;
arXiv:2502.17188v2) construct scalable holonomies using local tripod terms plus
a two-site Rydberg interaction. This does not transfer unchanged to a lossy
excited level: its entangling zero modes contain a doubly excited component.
Putting e->flag loss on that component destroys the intended dark code. Its
microscopic discussion also approximates the interatomic separation as fixed
during a gate. It is a substantive conventional alternative, not our damped
pair-star implementation.

Rudolph and Virmani (Nat Commun 14, 7800, 2023; arXiv:2107.03239) already prove
universal computation from singlet/triplet measurements and maximally mixed
spin inputs. An acquired sector-scalar SWAP measurement can therefore avoid
coherent gates logically, but the physical full-triplet instrument and its
return must actually be supplied. Its construction is polynomial and not
claimed to be an optimized fault-tolerant architecture.

Exchange-only computation already tolerates unpolarized spin gauge degrees of
freedom: Weinstein et al. (Nature 615, 817, 2023; arXiv:2202.03605) demonstrate
universal encoded silicon control and explicitly describe gauge-agnostic gate
sequences. This does not imply tolerance of arbitrary orbital-dependent
exchange amplitudes. In the three-spin J=1/2 sector, swaps act on the logical
multiplicity while collective-spin noise acts on gauge. Replacing a scalar
exchange J by J0+delta*tau_z on an independent mixed source produces logical
dephasing proportional to cos(2 delta t), even though total-spin symmetry is
preserved.

Kiefer et al. (Nature, 8 Apr 2026; DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10285-1;
arXiv:2507.22112) demonstrate a geometric SWAP based on lattice doublons and
fermionic exchange symmetry. Their entangling square-root-SWAP extension uses
an additional dynamical phase. A geometric SWAP by itself cannot create
entanglement from two product qubits. This is relevant contemporary physical
progress, but neither its scalar-control robustness nor the present theorem
establishes a practical advantage over the other.

7. Numerical evidence

holonomy_compiler.py constructs full 25-dimensional pair operators, verifies
exact reduction to the six-state no-jump sector, zero spectator coupling and
zero dark jump action, and integrates the Kato equation for one-qubit and
controlled-Z loops. It also checks the shorter real half-turn protocol.
The program writes holonomy_compiler_results.json. Floating-point errors are
diagnostics rather than certified enclosures. The algebra above supplies the
claimed identities; the root dynamical proof supplies finite-time following.
