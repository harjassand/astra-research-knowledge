COHERENT DARK-SPACE GATES WITH AN ACTIVE, UNPREPARED QUANTUM SOURCE
Research construction, 31 October 2026

This note is a mathematical and finite-level physical construction, not a
qualified laboratory device or a historical-priority claim.  It gives ordinary
proofs, explicit controls, resource costs, and useful failures.  The supplied
OUTPUT_MODE_THEOREM.md is preserved unchanged in this package.  Its measurement,
receiver, molecular, return, and actual-history claims keep their original scope.

MAIN RESULT

A shared quantum transition amplitude whose real part is uniformly positive,
together with an actual decay outlet, gives a uniformly contracting bright /
excited subsystem.  Its quantum source may remain mixed, correlated with the
data, and evolve arbitrarily rapidly and noncommutatively.  Moving an explicitly
constructed dark subspace then implements a coherent gate, with an O(1/T)
full-channel error bound and no source-Hamiltonian norm in that bound.

An explicit real tripod loop gives a logical reflection.  Two specified products
of local transitions give a controlled-Z.  An established real-amplitude compiler
then simulates Clifford+T circuits with one additional rebit and constant gate
overhead, before routing.  This last consequence is conditional on the specified
two-body couplers actually being available: their microscopic implementation has
not been earned by writing their finite-level Hamiltonian.

This is a different gate mechanism from the inherited receiver theorem.  It
does not assume an arbitrary projector measurement or a safe logical gate.  It
constructs the moving code from elementary couplings and derives its finite-time
dynamics.  It requires controlled relative coupling ratios, initially loaded
logical carriers, a decay outlet, and structural common-source coupling.  The
helper/excited levels do NOT have arbitrary initial populations.

1. TASK, INPUT, OUTPUT AND COST CONTRACT

Let the ground manifold have dimension m>=3.  An additional state |e> decays.
A normalized control vector b(s) in the ground manifold defines

  Q(s)=|b(s)><b(s)|,   P(s)=I_ground-Q(s),   0<=s<=1.

The code is P(s).  The source E is arbitrary finite-dimensional, or a separately
qualified infinite-dimensional extension.  Initially the state is supported on
P(0) tensor E, but may otherwise have arbitrary correlations with an external
reference.  Neither E nor any reference is prepared or measured by the gate.

The actual controlled Hamiltonian is, with hbar=1,

  H(t) = I_carrier tensor H_E(t)
       + |e><b(t/T)| tensor A_E(t)
       + |b(t/T)><e| tensor A_E(t)^dagger
       + |e><e| tensor D_E(t).                              (1)

Assume, as OPERATOR bounds on the admitted source space,

  g I <= Re A_E(t) <= h I,  g>0,
  D_E(t)=D_E(t)^dagger,  ||D_E(t)||<=d.                     (2)

Re A=(A+A^dagger)/2.  A need not be Hermitian.  The finite-dimensional operators
must have the regularity needed for their propagators (piecewise continuous is
enough for all constructions here); there is no derivative bound on A, D, or
H_E.  A bound a>=||A|| is useful for physical power, model errors, and the lower
bound below, but is not needed in the stated contraction constant.

Physical decay operators obey

  sum_mu L_mu^dagger L_mu = kappa |e><e| tensor I_E,
  L_mu P(s)=0,   kappa>0.                                 (3)

The output of a jump can be a loss flag or can return to the ground manifold.
All such jump histories are counted in the full error.  Absorbing flags are an
additional assumption only in the time lower bound and a particular pair model.
The decay reservoir has the Markov/vacuum or equivalent qualified irreversible
outlet assumed by (3).  It is a physical entropy resource, not part of the
arbitrarily disordered source E.

Output: the retained carrier/source channel after tracing the emitted field,
compared with the ideal logical holonomy tensor the source's free evolution.
One may instead keep a photon-counting record and compare it with the ideal
zero-count record.  The O(1/T) claim is not a distance statement for the entire
coherently retained radiation field; that stronger dilation distance can scale
as the square root of emission probability.

Cost: moving duration T, finite startup and control-restoration time, source
pump work, decay output, preparation/loading, final measurement, routing and
model errors.  No inverse H_E evolution or gain estimation is performed.

2. THE FIXED-BRIGHT CONTRACTION LEMMA

Fix b.  Remove the common H_E by a mathematical interaction picture.  This is
not a laboratory reversal.  Unitary conjugation preserves (2).  In a
zero-emission trajectory let u be the bright amplitude and v the excited
amplitude, both vectors in E and any reference.  They obey

  u'=-i A^dagger v,
  v'=-i A u-(kappa/2+iD)v.                                 (4)

Define

  N=||u||^2+||v||^2,  r=Im<u,v>,  V=N+alpha r,

  alpha=min{1, kappa/[2h+(kappa/2+d)^2/g]},
  C=sqrt[(2+alpha)/(2-alpha)],
  lambda=alpha g/[2(2+alpha)].                             (5)

All these constants are known from coarse structural bounds.  Direct
differentiation, with the usual conjugate-linear-first inner product, gives

  N'=-kappa ||v||^2,
  r'=-<u,Re(A)u>+<v,Re(A)v>
      -(kappa/2)r-Re<u,Dv>.                                (6)

The imaginary Hermitian part of A cancels from r'.  So does an arbitrary
common source Hamiltonian.  Put c=kappa/2+d.  Then

  V' <= -alpha g||u||^2-(kappa-alpha h)||v||^2
        +alpha c||u||||v||
      <= -(alpha g/2)||u||^2-(kappa/2)||v||^2.               (7)

The second step uses
c xy <= g x^2/2+c^2 y^2/(2g) and the definition of alpha.
Also |r|<=N/2 and alpha g<=kappa/2, so

  (1-alpha/2)N <= V <= (1+alpha/2)N,
  V' <= -alpha g/(2+alpha) V.

The exact time-ordered zero-emission propagator therefore satisfies

  ||U_BE(t,s)|| <= C exp[-lambda(t-s)]                     (8)

for every admissible time dependence.  In particular this is uniform in source
dimension, state, correlations, common source speed, and switching frequency.
This proves contraction; it does not infer it from the instantaneous spectrum
of an open-system generator.  Such an inference would not suffice for arbitrary
time-dependent nonnormal dynamics.

Bounded differential trapping/receiver shifts enter d.  An arbitrary unbounded
differential shift is not covered.  Large d gives a slow certified rate of order
kappa g^2/d^2.  For weak coupling and large kappa, lambda is of order g^2/kappa;
for d=0 and very small kappa it is of order kappa g/h.  Increasing loss without limit
does not make the gate arbitrarily fast.

3. MOVING DARK SPACES: A FINITE-TIME FULL-CHANNEL THEOREM

First take b(s) real and C^1.  Then <b,b'>=0 automatically.  Let R(s) be Kato
parallel transport,

  R'=[P',P]R,   R(0)=I_ground.

It maps P(0) to P(s), maps b(0) to b(s), and has no dark-dark or bright-bright
generator block.  Extend it by the identity on e.  The physically varied
couplings in (1) implement the path; R is a proof coordinate, not an assumed
physical gate.  Write

  q(s)=||P'(s)||,  A2=int_0^1 q(s)^2 ds,
  Ainf=sup_s q(s).                                        (9)

In the R and common-source interaction frame, let x be the dark amplitude and
y=(u,v) the bright/excited amplitude.  The geometric off-diagonal block B(t)
satisfies ||B(t)||=q(t/T)/T, and

  x'=-B(t)^dagger y,
  y'=M(t)y+B(t)x,     y(0)=0,                             (10)

up to immaterial signs/unitary factors.  M has the contraction (8).  The whole
zero-emission propagator is a contraction because its only anti-Hermitian loss
is -kappa|e><e|/2.  Thus ||x(t)||<=1 for any unit input, including its reference.

Variation of constants gives

  ||y(t)|| <= C int_0^t exp[-lambda(t-u)] q(u/T)/T du.

Young's convolution inequality on L2, or its direct integral proof, yields

  ||x(T)-x(0)|| <= d_x := C A2/(lambda T),
  ||y(T)|| <= d_y := C Ainf/(lambda T).                    (11)

If q(1)=0 and q(s)<=L1(1-s), then the stronger endpoint bound is

  d_y <= C L1/(lambda^2 T^2).                             (12)

This endpoint improvement differentiates only the known path, not the unknown
source or gain.  A finite fixed-projector holding interval can also suppress
the zero-emission bright/excited amplitude exponentially, with its real
duration added.  Emitted branches remain in the charged jump map.  Returning
decay can sustain unconditional bright population, so this is not by itself
a physical helper-reset theorem.

Let K0 be the actual zero-emission map restricted to the input code and let

  V0 = R(1)P(0) tensor U_E(T).

Then ||K0-V0||<=d_x+d_y.  Orthogonality of dark and bright components gives the
stronger probability bound

  p_any_emission <= 2d_x                                (13)

uniformly on code/source/reference inputs: the surviving dark norm is at least
1-d_x.  The bound is capped at one when necessary.  All histories with at least
one emission form a completely positive map J with output trace at most 2d_x;
arbitrary further jumps and return dynamics are included in J.

Using ||K0||<=1 and ||V0||=1,

  1/2 ||K0 rho K0^dagger-V0 rho V0^dagger||_1 <= d_x+d_y.

Adding half the trace of J proves

  epsilon_gate := 1/2 ||E_T - V0(.)V0^dagger||_diamond,code
       <= min{1, 2d_x+d_y}
       <= min{1, C(2A2+Ainf)/(lambda T)}.                 (14)

The reference can be arbitrarily large.  This is a complete reduced-channel
bound, not a success-probability statement or a normalized postselection claim.
The source is allowed to become disturbed on finite-time trajectories; (14)
controls that disturbance together with the useful logical action.

For a general complex path, a Kato-parallel bright vector can differ from the
laboratory one by a phase.  One must either set the physical common pulse phase
to the parallel gauge or co-rotate e in the proof and add the resulting known
phase derivative to D.  Ignoring that phase can rotate A out of its accretive
half-plane.  The real constructions below avoid this issue altogether.

4. ACTUAL ONE-QUBIT CONTROLS

Ground levels are |0>,|1>,|a>.  Pick a real ray

  u_beta=cos(beta)|0>+sin(beta)|1>.

Set theta(s)=pi(3s^2-2s^3), and physically apply the three relative transition
coefficients given by

  b(s)=sin(theta)u_beta+cos(theta)|a>.

The normalized dark vector

  d(s)=cos(theta)u_beta-sin(theta)|a>

is parallel, starts at u_beta, and ends at -u_beta.  The other logical ray is
unchanged.  The exact target gate is consequently the Householder reflection

  F_beta=I_2-2|u_beta><u_beta|.                            (15)

This path has

  Ainf=3pi/2,   A2=6pi^2/5,   L1=6pi.

Thus an explicit sufficient moving-time error is

  epsilon <= 12 C pi^2/(5lambda T)
             +6 C pi/(lambda^2 T^2),                    (16)

or the simpler (14).  These conservative analytic constants are not measured
error rates or optimized performance estimates.

The endpoint bright vector is -|a>.  Restore its control coefficient from -1
to +1 over a finite interval while the coefficients on |0>,|1> are zero.  The
entire computational code is exactly decoupled during this restoration, even
when the bright amplitude crosses zero.  Existing error evolves by a CPTP map,
so it need not be amplified in the comparison.  Startup ramps at fixed code
are equally legal, with their physical durations charged.
The comparison source propagator includes the ENTIRE moving, startup and
restoration interval, not just U_E(T) from the moving segment.

Relative ray calibration is a genuine resource:
||F_(beta+delta)-F_beta||<=2|delta|.  Common amplitude fluctuations affect the
certified speed; a deformation of the logical ray can change the desired gate.
These geometric gates are not generally immune to geometric control errors.

5. AN EXPLICIT TWO-BODY ENTANGLER, AND ITS HARDWARE BOUNDARY

Each carrier has states 0,1,a,e and an optional loss flag f.  Define local
transition operators T1=|e><1| and Ta=|e><a|.  Apply

  X_AB = sin(theta) T1_A tensor T1_B
        +cos(theta) Ta_A tensor Ta_B,
  H_AB = I tensor H_E+X_AB tensor A_E
        +X_AB^dagger tensor A_E^dagger,                    (17)

with the same common amplitude on the two pair transitions.  Add a qualified
e_A -> f_A loss at rate kappa.  From the loaded logical input, the exact
zero-emission accessible sector is

  {00,01,10,11,aa,ee} tensor E.

The first three vectors are spectators.  The last three have exactly the
bright/excited form already proved.  The moving fourth dark vector is

  cos(theta)|11>-sin(theta)|aa>.

The real half-turn therefore implements

  CZ=diag(1,1,1,-1).                                      (18)

The local e_A loss has L_A^dagger L_A=kappa|ee><ee| on this
accessible zero-emission sector.  That equality is not claimed globally on
all 25 two-carrier levels.

All ingredients in (17) are a finite list of two-site transitions.  No
high-dimensional projector oracle or exponentially large multilevel atom is
being hidden.  Nevertheless (17) is a Hamiltonian-level implementation
contract, NOT a proved microscopic coupler.  Dipole/exchange selection rules
can motivate these two channels, but do not by themselves provide tunable
coefficients, forbid every other level, or ensure a uniform gain.  The exact
physical operating window for this pair interaction remains open.  Existing
ion holonomies already use a pair-lambda idea; their elimination and motional
conditions cannot be silently inherited by an arbitrary active source.

The helpers a,e and flags must initially be unoccupied as part of loading the
logical carrier.  Loss flags require detection and replacement or an error
correcting consumer if they are to be reused after failure.  None is free.
At register level an addressed gate must act as identity on every inactive
logical carrier, apart from separately charged holding errors.  Inactive
carriers cannot be renamed as part of E, since an arbitrary H_E would then be
allowed to change their data.  Pair isolation and spectator protection remain
real control qualifications.

6. REAL CONTROLS SUFFICE FOR CIRCUIT SIMULATION

This uses established real-amplitude quantum simulation (Rudolph-Grover), with
an explicit compiler for the controls just constructed.  With Ry(alpha)=exp(-i alpha Y/2),

  H=F_(5pi/8),
  Ry(alpha)=F_(alpha/4) F_0,
  CNOT=(I tensor H) CZ (I tensor H).

For U=A+iB, with A,B real, introduce one additional rebit r and

  R(U)=A tensor I_r+B tensor J_r,
  J=[[0,-1],[1,0]].                                       (19)

The numerical encoding x+iy -> x tensor |0>+y tensor |1> respects composition
and computational-basis output probabilities.  An original T gate becomes controlled Ry(pi/2) on
r.  The identity

  CRy(alpha)=CNOT [I tensor Ry(-alpha/2)]
             CNOT [I tensor Ry(alpha/2)]                  (20)

gives at most ten dark loops for T/T^dagger, three for CNOT, and one for H.
An input Clifford+T circuit with h,c,t such gates therefore needs at most

  N=h+3c+10t                                             (21)

loops, before routing, loading, preparation and readout.  The finite ray
library is beta in {0, pi/16, -pi/16, 5pi/8}.

Every T accesses the added rebit.  Routing and serialization are real costs.
A synthesized SWAP costs at most nine loops.  On a specified connectivity
connected graph these must be inserted; no all-to-all device is inferred from (21).

This is computational universality from standard real initial states, not a
free physical realification of an unknown quantum input.  The map taking an
arbitrary vector to its real/imaginary parts is not complex-linear.  With the
convention (19), a separately prepared |-i> phase-reference qubit gives

  R(U)(|psi> tensor |-i>)=U|psi> tensor |-i>,

so it is a possible catalytic phase resource for arbitrary inputs.  It is not
preparable from real states using only real operations, and it has not been
supplied for free here.  Alternatively, complex tripod controls give direct
single-qubit phases with the gauge and calibration costs described above.

If the inherited logical Z readout is independently qualified on these same
carriers, measure Z and apply X=F_(-pi/4) on outcome 1 to initialize each loaded
logical qubit and the rebit.  That is at most one measurement plus one loop
per carrier.  It cannot repair arbitrary a/e/f loading errors.  The inherited
receiver theorem alone does not establish this carrier interface.

7. COMPOSITION, PHYSICAL TIME AND RETAINED AUXILIARIES

The source E can be retained across all gates, with its full prior correlations.
For GLOBAL source bounds (2), ordinary ideal-code-prefix / actual-suffix
telescoping applies.  Each ideal prefix remains in the intended code and has
an allowed source state; each actual suffix is CPTP.  Thus errors sum as

  total half-distance <= sum_j epsilon_j + other physical debits. (22)

One must not apply the code-restricted theorem directly to an actual prefix
that has leaked out of the code.  Nor may a source-energy domain certified
only on actual histories be assumed to hold on fictitious ideal prefixes.
For such restricted physical domains one needs a separate leakage/return
argument, an all-state extension, or explicit bounds on the appropriate
prefixes.  The inherited actual-history readout theorem remains intact; it
does not automatically supply those missing gate hypotheses.

For identical loop constants B=C(2A2+Ainf)/lambda and per-loop tolerance
epsilon_total/N, a simple sufficient choice gives

  T_j = B N/epsilon_total,
  total moving time = B N^2/epsilon_total.                 (23)

This is a sufficient construction, not an optimal full-circuit complexity or
a fault-tolerance threshold.  The endpoints, readout, preparation, routing and
reset intervals add to it.  A useful optimized allocation for differing B_j
is T_j proportional to sqrt(B_j), giving total sufficient moving time
(sum_j sqrt(B_j))^2/epsilon_total for the 1/T term.

The source has not been cooled or reset.  The data/code and empty helper levels
are nevertheless prepared resources.  The control fields and irreversible
outlet remain maintained resources.  If the source is motional rather than a
bounded spin, actual trapping, escape, higher moments and approximation
domains still require a joint physical budget.

8. THE INVERSE-ERROR TIME COST IS REAL IN A SPECIFIED SUBARCHITECTURE

Assume every emission goes to an invariant flag orthogonal to all ground
states; no direct ground-manifold Hamiltonian, flag recovery, or extra graph
edge is allowed; ||A(t)||<=a; and an untouched spectator |v> fixes the relative
phase of the target reflection |u> -> -|u>.  Fix kappa>0.  In this section T
means the total star-only evolution up to the reported output, including any
holding or restoration intervals used to achieve the stated tolerance.

Let K be the zero-emission map and n=<u|K|u> in a scalar-source instance.
K|v>=|v>.  For input (u+v)/sqrt(2), the logical X_uv expectation is Re n, while
the target expectation is -1.  If full half-diamond error <=epsilon<1, then

  1+Re n <= 2epsilon,
  ||P_ground K u-u|| >= 2(1-epsilon).                     (24)

The physical ground block (not the moving dark block) satisfies
||psi_ground'||<=a||psi_e||.  Cauchy-Schwarz and the first-emission identity give

  ||P_ground K u-u||^2 <= a^2 T int ||psi_e||^2 dt
                       = a^2 T p_u/kappa.

The flag outcome itself distinguishes the output from its target, so p_u<=
epsilon.  Combining gives the lower bound

  T >= 4 kappa(1-epsilon)^2/(a^2 epsilon).                 (25)

Thus 1/epsilon time is unavoidable in this fixed-loss star-only architecture,
at fixed coupling bound.  It is not a bound on all quantum gates.  In particular
returning jumps, recovery, other coupling graphs or direct lower-state controls
are outside this converse.  An upper bound on Re A alone is not a bound on a.

9. DECISIVE FAILURES AND AN EQUALLY SPECIFIED COMPARISON

9.1 Positive coherent gaps can fail because of quantum-source resonance.

Use the same tripod without loss:

  H0=I tensor (3/4)Z + (|e><b|+|b><e|) tensor (2I+X).

Rotate b with one dark vector d by theta=pi t/T.  The gain eigenvalues are 1,3.
Nevertheless dark |d,up> at energy 3/4 is degenerate with the lower source
eigenvector in the +(b+e)/sqrt(2) dressed block.  Their source overlap including
the bright fraction is 1/sqrt(10).  The geometric coupling in the degenerate
block integrates to pi/sqrt(10).  Therefore

  lim_(T->infinity) p_bright+excited = sin^2(pi/sqrt(10))
                                    =0.7021079104.        (26)

The opposite source eigenstate has the analogous resonance.  This failure is
not removed by making the source initially maximally mixed.  It does not say
that ordinary holonomic or hot-bus gates always fail: they can use stronger
spectral, motional, control or calibration information.

For the same finite matrices with kappa=2, an exact matrix-exponential
diagnostic at T=10000 gives maximum emission probability 0.0007892576 and
no-emission operator error 0.0007079627.  Their sum delta+p/2 yields the full
half-diamond upper bound 0.0011025915.  This number is a floating-point model
diagnostic with no rounding enclosure, not a measured gate fidelity.  Larger
kappa=8 gives a worse bound 0.00187637 at the same duration.

9.2 Separate positive leg gains do not supply a common dark subspace.

At a fixed nominal ray take couplings (2I+0.6Z)/sqrt(2) and
(2I-0.6Z)/sqrt(2), with common source H_E=0.75X and kappa=2.  Each unweighted
gain is positive with eigenvalues 1.4,2.6.  The nominal dark state (u-a)/sqrt(2)
couples to e through the differential gain.  No eigenvector of H_E in this
two-ground sector is dark: its X eigenvectors are sent to independent X
eigenvectors by the I and Z pieces, forcing both ground coefficients to vanish
if their coupling is zero.  The finite no-emission generator has no dark
eigenvector, hence all its eigenvalues have negative real part.  At T=50 the
calculated maximal survival is 0.00034998, and it tends to zero.

Thus positivity of every leg separately is not enough.  This is a structural
counterexample, not just a large perturbation bound or claimed physical floor.

9.3 Other retained limits.

Known pure-source/coherent hot gates may be faster and need no dissipative
outlet.  Extra loss does not automatically outperform them.  A missing amplitude
lower bound, source-dependent relative controls, unbounded differential shifts,
or photons that distinguish logical vectors can defeat this construction.
More gate time reduces (14) but increases uncontrolled accumulated errors.

10. WHAT PHYSICAL QUALIFICATION HAS AND HAS NOT BEEN EARNED

For a concrete bounded operator-valued spatial amplitude,

  A_E=g0[I+eta exp(ikX)],   0<eta<1,

Re A >=g0(1-eta)I and ||A||<=g0(1+eta) hold globally.  The bias removes the need
to confine motion to one positive lobe.  H_E can contain kinetic energy that
does not commute with A.  This algebra does not make a rotating-wave or
finite-level approximation uniform at arbitrarily high motional energy.

The companion physical_window/COMMON_MODE_TRIPOD_INTERFACE.txt derives a common-mode field design
on a one-dimensional axis and a complete 3-ground/9-excited LS-manifold angular
calculation.  It distinguishes scalar coherent light shifts from each physical
emission operator and retains the nondegenerate-manifold corrections.  Those
calculations are a physical route and an explicit qualification target, not a
completed atom-plus-trap-plus-pair-gate device window.  No small microscopic
remainder is merely chosen by fiat.

For an actual Hamiltonian remainder R(t), a sufficient half-channel debit is
int ||R(t)||dt on the common qualified domain, plus escape/tail errors.  For a
Lindblad-generator remainder, use half the integrated diamond norm.  These
sufficient upper bounds are not measured floors.  Source bandwidth, finite
transverse extent, higher atomic levels, differential traps, driving power,
outlet bandwidth and detector/reload operations must be counted where present.

An actual residual error rate r can give a sufficient budget B/T+rT.  Its best
upper bound is 2sqrt(B r) at T=sqrt(B/r), before other terms.  This optimization
does not turn a conservative norm estimate into a physical lower error floor.
It does show why the ideal theorem alone cannot justify increasing gate time
indefinitely in a device.

10.1 Complete optical closure supplies a separate reusable result.

For a declared L=0,S=1 ground manifold and full L'=1,S=1 excited manifold,
the spin-independent dipole is D(epsilon)v=epsilon tensor v.  All nine excited
states, split into J=0,1,2, are kept.  On a real polarization's dark plane
P=I-|epsilon><epsilon|, put r_j=(Delta_j-iGamma/2)^(-1).  The exact angular
identity for each second-order physical emission amplitude is

  D_l^dagger (r_1 P_1+r_2 P_2)D(epsilon)P
   = [(r_1+r_2)/2] epsilon_l P
     +[(r_2-r_1)/2]|epsilon><l|P.                         (27)

The effective coherent light shift is scalar on this plane, but the second
term in (27) carries logical information into the bright state.  After summing
all physical polarization channels its bright-leakage effect is bounded by

  gamma_bad <= 3 Gamma ||A||^2 |r_2-r_1|^2/4.             (28)

The two terms in one physical amplitude cannot be declared separately observed
events.  Using this resolvent as actual dynamics requires a justified
elimination with source local-orbit/differential-energy bounds.  A source-only
map on the dark plane is not automatically a harmless common map on the
bright/excited sector in the contraction theorem.

There is an exact static statement without elimination if Delta_1=Delta_2.
For a logical vector v perpendicular to epsilon*, embed the ground auxiliary
state as v and the excited auxiliary state as epsilon tensor v.  The complete
Hamiltonian and EVERY dipole-emission operator on this subsystem factor as
identity on v tensor an optical/source operation.  Arbitrary optical excitation
and repeated scattering can then coexist with intact logical information.
The detailed isometry and fine-jump proof are in the companion note.  Actual
helium fine levels are not degenerate; their measured splittings must be kept.

The full twelve-internal-state / two-source-state Lindblad diagnostic keeps
all dipole channels and a mixed noncommuting source.  With Gamma=g=1,
Delta_1=20, Delta_2=22 and a moving time T=400, it reports about 2.93 emitted
photons and a logical Choi-state trace distance about 0.0268 after an additional
12/Gamma drive-off settling interval.  This is a particular reduced-source
numerical example, not a diamond-norm theorem, monotonic asymptotic guarantee,
or device benchmark.  The return interval and initial/final dressing are real
parts of the comparison.  It preserves evidence for active optical auxiliaries
without quietly identifying this larger model with the simpler theorem (14).
This illustrative optical diagnostic switches the drive instantaneously;
finite experimental switching ramps are not simulated and remain an added
control time/model qualification.

10.2 Geometry and physical return with an occupied optical auxiliary.

In the exact degenerate-complement static model, take real epsilon(s) and an
orthonormal dark frame D(s).  In optical-auxiliary-first coordinates the
embedding is J(s)=diag[D(s), epsilon(s) tensor D(s)].  Direct differentiation
gives the additional organizing identity

  J^dagger J' = I_optical tensor D^dagger D'.               (29)

Thus the projected logical connection is independent of the optical auxiliary
population, including its mixtures and coherences.  For q=D^dagger epsilon'
and a_path=||epsilon'||, the transverse derivative B=(I-JJ^dagger)J' obeys

  B^dagger B=diag[qq^dagger, a_path^2 I+qq^dagger],
  ||B||=sqrt(2)||epsilon'||.                              (30)

This computes the needed control geometry without assuming a cold optical
auxiliary.  It does not prove dynamical following: the enlarged moving
subsystem needs its own uniform transverse coherence/population response,
including recycling emissions.  At Delta=0, full optical dynamics is identity
on the entire ground spin, so there is no attraction to the chosen dark plane
even though (29) holds.  This counterexample prevents promoting the connection
identity to an unsupported gate theorem.

Endpoint return within the static exact subsystem is constructive.  Freeze
epsilon, ramp the common drive off while preserving its factorization, and
then let the optical auxiliary decay for tau.  Every operation in this ramp
acts identically on logical vectors.  Amplitude damping gives

  half-diamond return error <= sqrt[2(1-sqrt(1-exp(-Gamma tau)))]
                            <= sqrt(2) exp(-Gamma tau/2).

Therefore tau>=Gamma^(-1) log(2/delta_return^2) suffices, plus the actual ramp
duration.  The optical auxiliary may initially be mixed and correlated, and
the source is not reset.  Recoil, trap differences and other excluded channels
must be added when passing beyond the declared dipole model.
