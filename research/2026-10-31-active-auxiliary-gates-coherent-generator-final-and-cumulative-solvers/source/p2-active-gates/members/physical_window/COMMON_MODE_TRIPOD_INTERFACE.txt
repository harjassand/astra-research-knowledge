COMMON-MODE TRIPOD: PHYSICAL INTERFACE AND COMPLETE ANGULAR CLOSURE
31 October 2026. Complementary working note for the parent research run.

Scope
-----
This note supplies an explicit field construction for an accretive quantum
amplitude and an exact angular-momentum calculation over a declared full
L'=1,S=1 optical manifold. It does not qualify an actual atomic gate window.
The 12-level numerical model is a model with physical dipole jump operators,
not the complete helium atom and not an experimental error estimate.

1. Common operator amplitude from actual electromagnetic fields
------------------------------------------------------------
Let the motion be along a coordinate z, with arbitrary retained source
Hamiltonian H_E. Set 0<eta<1 and

  A(z) = g0 [I + eta exp(i k_z z)].

Then, as operator inequalities on the entire axial Hilbert space,

  g0(1-eta) I <= Re A <= g0(1+eta) I,  ||A||<=g0(1+eta).

There is no positive-lobe truncation: the constant component provides the
strict accretive part. A(z) need not commute with p_z^2/(2m), a trap, or other
source dynamics. This directly fits the parent's accretive-amplitude extension.

This common field factor is compatible with Maxwell transversality on a line.
Write q=k_z/k and s=sqrt(1-q^2), with 0<=q<1. Three plane waves may have

  k1/k=(s,0,q),  p1=(0,1,0);
  k2/k=(0,s,q),  p2=(1,0,0);
  k3/k=(-s,0,q), p3=(q,0,s).

Each p_j is transverse to its own k_j. At x=y=0 all phases are exp(i k_z z).
For any controlled complex polarization vector e=(e_x,e_y,e_z), set

  c1=e_y, c3=e_z/s, c2=e_x-q e_z/s.

Then sum c_j p_j=e exactly. A second three-beam set with q=0 supplies the
constant axial component. Taking the amplitudes of both sets proportional to
the same e(t), with relative total strength eta, gives on the axis

  E^(+)(z,t)=E0 e(t) [1+eta exp(i k_z z)] exp(-i omega_L t).

The construction controls the ratios and phases determining the dark plane;
the motional quantum amplitude is common. It does not require freezing motion
or determining its trajectory. It uses real pump phase/amplitude controls,
independent beams derived from a common frequency reference, and finite power.
The polarization inversion is well-conditioned when s is bounded away from 0.

Costs and limitations: finite transverse motion changes the relative plane-wave
phases; finite waists and apertures change envelopes; a real trap may have
internal-state dependence. The common factor is exact on the declared line,
not throughout a finite three-dimensional volume. These corrections need a
joint motional domain or explicit full dynamics, and cannot be declared tiny
by assigning an arbitrary number. Optical rotating-wave and dipole truncations
also need actual local frequencies/transition matrix elements. The theorem's
uniformity in H_E does not qualify those approximations for an arbitrary source.

2. Complete spin-one optical manifold
------------------------------------
Take a metastable ground electronic orbital L=0, spin S=1. Its ground space is
V=C^3. The declared excited orbital manifold has L'=1,S=1, hence V_orb tensor
V_spin, of dimension nine, decomposed into J=0,1,2.

In a Cartesian basis the spin-independent electric dipole is

  D(e) v = e tensor v,        ||e||=1.

Let F swap the two Cartesian factors and |s>=vec(I)/sqrt(3). Then

  P0=|s><s|,
  P1=(I-F)/2,
  P2=(I+F)/2-P0.

Set b=e* and B=|b><b|. Direct multiplication gives

  D(e)^dag P0 D(e) = B/3,
  D(e)^dag P1 D(e) = [I-|e><e|]/2,
  D(e)^dag P2 D(e) = [I+|e><e|]/2-B/3.                 (1)

Their sum is I. Thus a field with overall coefficient sqrt(3) A has the
parent's desired resonant tripod amplitude A between b and the J=0 level.
Equation (1) includes all nine states of the declared excited manifold.

The full finite model used in the executable is

  H=H_E + Delta1 P1 + Delta2 P2
       + sqrt(3) [A D(e) + A^dag D(e)^dag],             (2)

where Delta0=0 and tensor identities are suppressed. For a physical complex A,
the two off-diagonal blocks are adjoints as usual. The drive operator in (2)
includes the ground-to-excited block only; its Hermitian conjugate returns.

All Cartesian electric-dipole emission channels are retained:

  L_l=sqrt(Gamma) D_l^dag,   D_l v=|l> tensor v,
  sum_l L_l^dag L_l = Gamma I_excited.                 (3)

A full angular continuum of emission directions and transverse polarizations
is equivalent after the appropriate angular weights; Cartesian channels are
a valid isotropic Lindblad representation of that nonselective emission.
They are not labels asserting a detector resolves Cartesian components.

The resonant J=0 state naturally decays to the spin-one ground manifold.
It is not an orthogonal classical flag. Core-theorem resonant emissions may
be counted as bad events for an unconditional error bound, or a real external
flagging/shelving mechanism must be added and charged.

3. Exact second-order angular identities, including every fine emission
---------------------------------------------------------------------
These identities are exact matrix algebra for a resolvent which is scalar on
each J block. Using them as an eliminated dynamics requires additional
adiabatic-elimination qualifications; the identities do not supply that step.

Write

  r_j=(Delta_j-i Gamma/2)^(-1),  R=r1 P1+r2 P2,
  P=I-|b><b|.

The off-resonant second-order Stark operator on the dark plane is proportional
to

  P D(e)^dag R D(e) P
   = (r1+r2)P/2 + (r2-r1) P|e><e|P/2.                (4)

For a Hermitian effective Hamiltonian take the real part with the conventional
minus sign and multiply by 3 A^dag A. The first term is a common source
operation. The second term can distinguish logical dark vectors when e is
complex. It is controlled by the DIFFERENCE of the two resolvents, not an
independently assigned 1/Delta remainder.

If e is real up to an overall phase, b=e in a suitable phase convention and
P|e>=0. The entire Stark operator is scalar on the dark plane, for arbitrary
r1,r2. This says nothing by itself about the fine emission maps.

For real unit e, each actual effective emission amplitude obeys

  D_l^dag R D(e) P
    = a e_l P + c |e><l|P,
  a=(r1+r2)/2, c=(r2-r1)/2.                           (5)

Proof: P1(e tensor v)=(e tensor v-v tensor e)/2 and
P2(e tensor v)=(e tensor v+v tensor e)/2 when e^T v=0.
Extracting the orbital l component yields (5).

The second term in (5) maps a logical vector to the bright vector e and
depends on its l component. An individual fine emission is NOT scalar if
c is nonzero. It would be incorrect to split the two terms into separate
observed jump histories; they are coherent pieces of one amplitude.

However, after summing the complete emission family, the cross terms vanish:
sum_l e_l <l|P=0. In particular,

  sum_l P L_eff,l^dag (I-P) L_eff,l P
     = [3 Gamma |c|^2] P tensor A^dag A.               (6)

Here A is the desired resonant coupling amplitude, and its source factors and
their order must be retained. Equation (6) is a bad-scattering effect in the
qualified second-order model. For ||A||<=a_plus its rate is at most

  gamma_bad = 3 Gamma a_plus^2 |r2-r1|^2 / 4.          (7)

The total off-resonant emission rate is
3 Gamma <A^dag A> (|a|^2+|c|^2). The common scalar piece can cause source
motion, entropy, recoil and repeated photons without directly distinguishing
dark logical vectors. That is an allowed source channel only after the full
fine-map calculation, not because the Hamiltonian happened to be scalar.

If r1=r2, every fine map is scalar on the dark plane for ANY complex e:

  D_l^dag r1(P1+P2)D(e)P = r1 e_l P.                 (8)

This follows from P0D(e)P=0 and D_l^dag D(e)=e_l I.
Thus complete closure supplies a real transparency mechanism in the degenerate
complement limit. It is not a claim that the actual atom has that degeneracy.

There is also an EXACT, non-eliminated static-subsystem statement at
Delta1=Delta2=Delta. Fix e and a logical vector v in D=ker(e^T). Embed

  J(v tensor |g>)=ground v,
  J(v tensor |r>)=excited (e tensor v).

Since P0(e tensor v)=0, the full Hamiltonian (2) preserves this four-dimensional
internal subspace and obeys

  J^dag H J = I_D tensor [H_E + Delta |r><r|
       + sqrt(3)(A |r><g| + A^dag |g><r|)].            (9)

Every full physical dipole jump, before elimination, satisfies

  L_l J = J [I_D tensor sqrt(Gamma) e_l |g><r|].       (10)

Thus arbitrary active optical auxiliary excitation and every fine emission
act identically on logical vectors in the declared static subsystem, even
with noncommuting H_E and A. This proof does not need a source bandwidth or a
large Delta. For time-varying e the embedding itself moves and introduces
additional derivative couplings; (9)-(10) alone are not a finite-time gate
theorem. For unequal Delta1,Delta2 this exact factorization is lost; replacing
them by equal values in a model of real helium would be unjustified.

3a. Moving-subsystem organizing lemma: one connection for every optical state
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
This lemma applies to the exact degenerate-complement static subsystem above.
It does NOT claim the remaining moving-subsystem dissipative theorem.

Let e(s) be a real, unit, differentiable vector and let D(s) be a real 3-by-2
orthonormal dark frame: D^T D=I and e^T D=0. In auxiliary-first ordering write

  J(s)=diag(D(s), e(s) tensor D(s)),
  K(s)=D^T D',  q(s)=D^T e',  a(s)=||e'||.

Because e^T e'=0, direct differentiation gives the exact connection

  J^dag J' = I_optical tensor K.                         (11)

Equivalently, in logical-first ordering it is K tensor I_optical. The
geometric connection is identical for the ground and optically excited
auxiliary and for arbitrary mixtures, coherences and source correlations.
It does not depend on the optical auxiliary population. This is stronger than
checking a Berry phase in the bare ground dark plane alone.

For the transverse derivative B=(I-JJ^dag)J', differentiating e^T D=0 yields

  D'=D K-e q^T,
  B_ground=-e q^T,
  B_excited=e' tensor D-e tensor e q^T.

The two excited terms are orthogonal. Hence, exactly,

  B^dag B = diag(q q^T, a^2 I+q q^T),
  ||B|| = sqrt(2) ||e'||.                                (12)

The occupied optical auxiliary therefore raises this uniform transverse
forcing norm by at most sqrt(2); it does not alter the logical connection.
The meridional case K=0 is not assumed. The executable checks a spherical
path with both polar and azimuthal velocities and nonzero K.

What is still required for a moving-subsystem gate: a uniform transverse
propagator/response bound for the FULL generator, including its physical
recycling emissions and source dynamics. That bound must control both
coherences with the code and population outside it, and specify how any
return into the code acts on logical vectors. The parent's zero-emission
tripod estimate cannot be imported unchanged: this enlarged subsystem allows
many harmless emissions and retains an optically excited auxiliary. Neither
the static factorization nor (11) supplies the required contraction.

There is a simple reason this extra condition is substantive. At Delta=0 all
three excited J sectors are degenerate. The full optical coupling and decay
then act as identity on the entire ground spin, so a bright input spin is
never attracted into the chosen two-dimensional dark plane. Equations (9)
and (11) remain true, but the necessary transverse contraction is absent.
Finite Delta distinguishes the resonant J=0 channel; its complete absorbing/
recycling dynamics still needs its own proof, with actual source conditions.

An exact endpoint return IS available within the static subsystem. Freeze
e(s) and switch off the common drive using any finite ramp that preserves
the common-factor structure. The ramp acts only on optical/source auxiliaries,
so it cannot change logical vectors. After the drive is zero, equations
(9)-(10) give ordinary amplitude damping of the two-state optical auxiliary
at rate Gamma. Put r=exp(-Gamma tau). Comparing its Stinespring isometry to
complete optical reset yields a uniform half-diamond bound

  distance_to_return <= sqrt(2[1-sqrt(1-r)]) <= sqrt(2r).   (13)

Thus waiting tau>=Gamma^(-1) log(2/delta_return^2) suffices for return error
delta_return, in addition to the actual ramp duration. This covers arbitrary
initial optical state and arbitrary correlations with logical/source/reference
systems. The source continues evolving. The statement uses the declared
isotropic dipole model, with no additional recoil/trap mismatch omitted by
fiat; additional common recoil channels require their actual source-channel
comparison. This endpoint return does not establish the missing transport
bound during the moving path.

4. What a physical finite window would have to pay
-------------------------------------------------
In the parent's weak-drive regime, the useful bright-removal rate scales as
lambda proportional to g^2/Gamma. A geometric gate with error budget epsilon
therefore needs T proportional to Gamma/(g^2 epsilon), with the actual path
and contraction constants included.

On complex paths a nonscalar Stark term from (4) can accumulate like
g^2 T |r2-r1|, hence Gamma |r2-r1|/epsilon after this substitution. On real
paths this particular coherent term cancels. The bad-scattering estimate (7)
instead scales as Gamma^2 |r2-r1|^2/epsilon, up to the actual path and gain
constants. Neither expression is an empirical error floor or a universal
lower bound. They are terms in a sufficient perturbative budget.

One must additionally include the initial and final optical dressing, drive
ramps, moving-polarization derivative terms, any finite source Doppler/local-
orbit bounds required in elimination, excitation of other principal/electronic
manifolds, deviations from LS dipole selection, trap-state dependence,
transverse mode mismatch, magnetic splitting, and spontaneous-decay channels
outside (3). For a motional source, [H_E,exp(i k_z z)] contains Doppler and
recoil operators; a prepared-state linewidth calibration does not bound them
on an arbitrary-energy domain. A full microscopic propagator calculation on
the admitted domain is a legitimate alternative to elimination.

The microscopic geometry does not provide state preparation, readout, an
entangling two-register operation or an error-correcting code. Those require
the parent's separately constructed operations and explicit physical ports.

5. A real atomic scale, without promoting it to a device
------------------------------------------------------
Metastable 4He has the relevant 2^3S1 to 2^3P0,1,2 multiplet at about 1083 nm.
Primary spectroscopy reports the J=0 to J=2 interval as 31.90813098 GHz and
the J=1 to J=2 interval as 2.29117756 GHz. A newer primary measurement gives
the J=1 to J=0 interval as 29.616955018 GHz. The excited lifetime used in the
primary line-shift calculation is approximately 98 ns.

Using angular frequencies and Gamma=1/(98 ns) gives

  Gamma/min|Delta_j| approximately 5.48e-5,
  |(r2-r1)/(r2+r1)| approximately 0.03724,
  |c|^2/(|a|^2+|c|^2) approximately 0.001385.

The last quantity is the conditional fraction of information-carrying bright
leakage in the qualified off-resonant effective scattering model for real
polarization. It is NOT an atom's gate error, measurement error or error floor.
The calculation omits all the device qualifications in section 4.

6. Ordinary executable checks
-----------------------------
Run python physical_window/atomic_closure_checks.py --full.
The program checks all three projector-strength identities, complete fine
emission cancellation at equal resolvents, and the real-polarization bad-jump
effect. Errors are near 1e-16 in ordinary floating point.

The optional diagnostic uses the full twelve internal states, a maximally
mixed two-state source, A=I+0.25 tau_z, H_E=1.5 tau_x, Gamma=1, Delta1=20,
Delta2=22, and a real pi polarization loop. It solves the complete Lindblad
equation by a constant co-rotating-frame matrix exponential. All emission
operators (3) are retained; no fine manifold is eliminated. The output is a
logical Choi-state trace distance after tracing the retained source, not a
diamond-norm certificate and not a worst-case correlated-source theorem.

An endpoint comparison to the bare ground code while the optical drive stays
on includes an O(g/Delta) optical-dressing amplitude. Indeed the initial run
gave about 0.094 Choi trace distance at T=400 despite Delta1 and Delta2 being
close. This remains about 0.096 at T=200 when Delta1=Delta2=20. It is wrong to
call this a logical error floor. The program now charges a drive-off plus
12/Gamma decay interval to return to the bare ground code and reports that
duration. The quantum gain source continues evolving and is not reset.
Results are in atomic_closure_results.json. Finite ramps are not simulated;
instantaneous switching is a declared feature of this finite model.

With the charged 12/Gamma endpoint settling interval, the exact-model results
for loop durations T=100,200,400 are respectively Choi trace errors about
0.05004,0.03153,0.02681 and total emitted counts 0.8138,1.5015,2.9303.
At T=200, making Delta1=Delta2=20 gives about 0.02189 error and 1.6207 photons;
using Delta1=20,Delta2=40 gives about 0.09285 error and 1.1829 photons.
These are illustrative finite-model diagnostics. They establish neither a
logical threshold nor a certified worst-case source guarantee for a device.

8. Preserved alternative: odd longitudinal circuit and the common force
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Richer and DiVincenzo, PRB93,134501 (2016), DOI10.1103/PhysRevB.93.134501,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.06138, construct an even-qubit/odd-receiver coupling
cos(phi_q/2) sin(phi_r/2). Its linear receiver expansion has no quadratic
operator pole. Projecting cos(phi_q/2), however, gives c0 I+cz Z; an unknown
quantum amplitude creates a common operator force as well as the useful force.
Subtracting a measured mean does not cancel that operator.

Two identical circuits with opposite receiver orientation and the SAME source
amplitude give G[cos(phi_1/2)-cos(phi_2/2)]sin(phi_r/2). On the dual-rail code
|01>,|10>, the common term cancels and the useful term is proportional to
Z_logical G sin(phi_r/2). The leading operator pole is absent by parity.
This is a structural alternative, not a completed device: cubic receiver
terms, higher same-parity qubit levels, imperfect common modulation, and
local source frequencies still require bounds. Josephson arrays reduce the
receiver expansion parameter but do not automatically suppress the ratios of
higher-level qubit matrix elements. These issues were not assigned arbitrary
small errors to create a claimed device window.

An echo is not a uniform substitute for structure. If a logical sign flip
and pump reversal leave the force fixed but toggle WD_E N by y(t), the source
interaction-picture term is y(t)D_E(t). A source with transition frequency at
the echo frequency can give a nonzero time average. Thus arbitrary fast
noncommuting D_E cannot be suppressed merely by naming a spin echo; a local
spectral/commutator condition or a direct propagator bound is required.
