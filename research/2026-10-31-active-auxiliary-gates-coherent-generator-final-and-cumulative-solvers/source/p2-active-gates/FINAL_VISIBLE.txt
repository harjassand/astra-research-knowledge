## 1. The mechanism and the theorem

Take three ground levels \(|0\rangle,|1\rangle,|a\rangle\), an excited level \(|e\rangle\), and the retained auxiliary \(E\). Controlled relative transition amplitudes select one normalized bright vector \(|b(s)\rangle\), where \(s=t/T\). The two-dimensional ground subspace orthogonal to it carries the logical qubit.

The interaction is

\[
H(t)=I\otimes H_E(t)
+|e\rangle\langle b(s)|\otimes A_E(t)
+|b(s)\rangle\langle e|\otimes A_E(t)^\dagger,
\]

with excited-state decay rate \(\kappa>0\). The proof also allows a bounded differential excited-state Hamiltonian.

The coupling condition is

\[
\boxed{gI\le \operatorname{Re}A_E(t)\le hI,\qquad g>0.}
\]

This includes positive Hermitian gains, but also complex quantum amplitudes with a uniformly positive real part. Neither the gain nor its dynamics needs to be learned.

### The key proof

For a fixed bright vector, remove the common source evolution as a mathematical interaction picture. The bright and excited amplitudes \(u,v\) then satisfy

\[
\dot u=-iA^\dagger v,\qquad
\dot v=-iAu-\left(\frac{\kappa}{2}+iD\right)v.
\]

I constructed the quadratic quantity

\[
V=\|u\|^2+\|v\|^2+\alpha\,\operatorname{Im}\langle u,v\rangle
\]

and proved, for an explicit positive \(\alpha\),

\[
\boxed{
\dot V\le
-\frac{\alpha g}{2}\|u\|^2
-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|v\|^2.
}
\]

The common source Hamiltonian cancels from this derivative. No derivative of the unknown gain appears. This gives a uniform exponential bound on the bright/excited propagator,

\[
\|U_{\mathrm{BE}}(t,s)\|\le C e^{-\lambda(t-s)},
\]

with explicit \(C,\lambda>0\) determined by the coupling, decay and differential-shift bounds.

Moving the dark subspace produces a known geometric forcing of size \(1/T\). Combining that forcing with the contraction yields

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\left\|
\mathcal E_T-
\bigl(\mathcal U_{\mathrm{hol}}\otimes\mathcal U_E\bigr)
\right\|_{\diamond,\mathrm{code}}
\le
\frac{C}{\lambda T}
\left[
2\int_0^1\|P'(s)\|^2\,ds+
\sup_s\|P'(s)\|
\right].
}
\]

Here \(P(s)\) is the moving code projector. The bound permits arbitrary initial code–auxiliary–reference correlations.

**This is a complete retained-channel bound.** All histories containing emissions are included in the error. It applies after the emitted field is discarded, or with a photon-counting record included. It does not claim the same distance bound for coherent retention of the entire radiation field.

The input must already occupy the logical code. The helper and excited levels are prepared resources; they are distinct from the auxiliary \(E\), which remains unprepared.

## 2. Why dissipation changes the result

We found a concrete failure of the tempting coherent alternative.

For the same tripod, take

\[
A=2I+X,\qquad H_E=\frac34 Z,
\]

and rotate the bright vector through a closed half-turn. The gain is strictly positive, with eigenvalues \(1\) and \(3\). Nevertheless, a source-dependent dressed state becomes resonant with the dark sector.

Without decay,

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{T\to\infty}p_{\mathrm{leak}}
=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{10}}\right)
\approx0.702108.
}
\]

Slowing the operation leaves approximately **70.2% leakage**. The failure also occurs with a maximally mixed source.

Adding the specified decay removes this failure:

| Finite-model calculation | Result |
|---|---:|
| No decay, \(T=10{,}000\): bright/excited population | \(0.7021079\) |
| \(\kappa=2,\ T=1{,}000\): full-channel half-diamond upper bound | \(0.0109937\) |
| \(\kappa=2,\ T=10{,}000\): full-channel half-diamond upper bound | \(0.00110259\) |

These are ordinary matrix-exponential calculations in dimensionless model units, without certified rounding enclosures. They are not measured gate fidelities.

This establishes the failure of an unqualified positive-gap argument. Conventional coherent gates can use additional spectral knowledge, calibrated controls and different trajectories; the example does not establish superiority over their best implementations.

A second counterexample identifies the crucial structural requirement: **separately positive gains on different control legs need not protect a common dark subspace.** With two different positive quantum gains and a noncommuting source Hamiltonian, the nominal dark state can eventually lose essentially all its population.
