\paragraph{Purpose and limits.}
This proves and executes an independent formula for the polynomial-time
scalar consumer used in the unsaturated source-free product construction.
It does not assume that raw circuits expose the matrices below. Acquiring
the full module-homomorphism space, its actual right-input subspace, the
dual character and the ranks belongs to the separately checked production
compiler. The present derivation uses row-space M\"obius inversion, while
the production consumer uses kernel-space inversion. Agreement is tested
against literal multiplication and exact Walsh transforms as well as
between the two formulas. Subspace-lattice inversion and matrix-rank
Fourier transforms are classical machinery.

\section{A polynomial formula with three rank invariants}

Let $q=2^a$, and write $\psi(x)=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}_{\F_q/\F_2}(x)}$.
For $Z\in\F_q^{p\times r}$ and $C\in\F_q^{d\times r}$ define
\[
 \mathcal W(Z,C)=
 \sum_{\substack{X\in\F_q^{p\times d},\,Y\in\F_q^{d\times r}\\XY=Z}}
       \psi\left(\sum_{i,j}C_{ij}Y_{ij}\right).
\]
Set
\[
 k=\rank Z,\qquad l=\rank C,\qquad t=\rank(CZ^T),\qquad v=k-t.
\]
The valid ranges include
$0\le k\le\min(p,r)$, $0\le l\le\min(d,r)$ and
$\max(0,k+l-r)\le t\le\min(k,l)$.
Set Gaussian coefficients to zero when the lower index is negative or
exceeds a nonnegative upper index, and also set them to zero for negative
upper indices.

\begin{theorem}
The value of $\mathcal W(Z,C)$ depends only on $p,d,r,q,k,l,t$ and equals
\begin{align}\label{eq:triple}
\sum_{s=k}^{\min(d,r)}q^{p(d-s)}
 \sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1)^{s-j}q^{\binom{s-j}{2}+dj}
 \sum_h &\gb{v}{h}\gb{r-l-v}{j-h}
 q^{(v-h)(j-h)}\\[-3pt]
 &\hspace{-15mm}\cdot\gb{r-k-j+h}{s-k-j+h}.\nonumber
\end{align}
It suffices to sum
$\max(0,j-(r-l-v))\le h\le\min(v,j)$.
All displayed powers then have nonnegative exponents.
The formula has polynomially many exact integer operations and polynomial
intermediate bit lengths in $a,p,d,r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $K$ be the row space of $Z$, of dimension $k$, and let
$H=\ker C\subseteq\F_q^r$, of dimension $r-l$.
The restriction of $C$ to $K$ has rank $t$, so
$\dim(K\cap H)=k-t=v$.
For a fixed $Y$ of rank $s$, the equation $XY=Z$ has a solution exactly
when $K\subseteq\operatorname{row}Y$; when it does, it has
$q^{p(d-s)}$ solutions $X$. Thus it remains to sum the character over
the matrices with exact row space $W$, for each $s$-space $W\supseteq K$.

For a subspace $T\subseteq W$, summing the character over all matrices
whose rows lie in $T$ gives
\[
 \sum_{\operatorname{row}Y\subseteq T}
   \psi\left(\sum C_{ij}Y_{ij}\right)
 =\begin{cases}q^{d\dim T},&T\subseteq H,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
\]
Indeed, each row is an independent additive-character sum. The
annihilator of a field-linear row space under the trace pairing is its
ordinary field-linear orthogonal complement, since field multiples are
also present. M\"obius inversion in the subspace lattice therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{\operatorname{row}Y=W}\psi\left(\sum C_{ij}Y_{ij}\right)
 =\sum_{T\subseteq W\cap H}
       (-1)^{s-j}q^{\binom{s-j}{2}}q^{dj},\qquad j=\dim T.
\]

To sum this expression over $W\supseteq K$, classify $T\subseteq H$
by $j=\dim T$ and $h=\dim(T\cap K)$.
Since $T\cap K=T\cap(K\cap H)$ and $K\cap H$ has dimension $v$,
the number of choices for $T$ is
\[
 \gb{v}{h}\gb{r-l-v}{j-h}q^{(v-h)(j-h)}.
\]
Choose the intersection, choose the image in $H/(K\cap H)$, then
choose its graph into $(K\cap H)/(T\cap K)$; the last choice gives
the displayed power of $q$.
For each such $T$, the sum $K+T$ has dimension $k+j-h$.
There are exactly
$\gb{r-k-j+h}{s-k-j+h}$ spaces $W$ of dimension $s$ containing it.
Multiplying these choices by the M\"obius weight and by
$q^{p(d-s)}$ gives \eqref{eq:triple}.
\end{proof}

No root of unity beyond $\{1,-1\}$ appears in the characteristic-two
case. There is no summation over field elements, matrices or subspaces
in the actual formula: only integer dimensions are enumerated.

\section{Independent executed comparisons}

The scalar code is \path{right_character_product_fiber.py}.

The audit \path{right_character_product_audit.py} directly enumerates
the original independent matrix entries, records the joint target and
right factor, and applies an exact binary Walsh transform to those
literal multiplicities. The character labels are converted using the
actual finite-field trace Gram matrix, so extension-field characters
are not mistaken for coordinate dot products.

Six literal families were executed:
\[
 (a,p,d,r)\in\{(1,1,1,1),(1,2,1,3),(1,2,2,3),
 (1,3,2,3),(2,2,1,2),(3,2,1,2)\}.
\]
Across these cases, all 9,508 original factor pairs and all 303,620
target--character combinations agree with \eqref{eq:triple} and with
the production kernel-space formula
\texttt{signed\_universal\_product} in
\texttt{unsaturated\_bilinear\_counter.py}.
The literal families include every field element of $\F_4$ and $\F_8$
at their stated shapes.

Separately, the two independently derived formulas agree on all 21,135
valid rank tuples with $1\le a\le5$ and $1\le p,d,r\le6$.
This grid comparison does not replace the proof or the literal tests;
it detects indexing, sign, boundary and exponent errors between the
two implementations. Results are preserved in
\texttt{right\_character\_product\_audit\_results.json}.

\paragraph{Interface review.}
The acquired forward and reverse Hom spaces are paired by a
nonzero binary functional on
$\End_S(U)$ that vanishes on commutators. Its trace space has binary
dimension $a$. The pairing is checked nondegenerate in the actual
binary coordinates. A different nonzero trace functional scales the
abstract character matrix by a nonzero field scalar; the three ranks
and the formula are unchanged. The embedded right-input annihilator
is acquired as a binary kernel, so a binary subspace need not be
field-linear. Each of its $2^c$ characters is handled individually.
The original target and the reverse-Hom operator give the three
rank invariants through actual represented ranks and orbit spans.
There is no division by a guessed module multiplicity or normalization
of a represented ordinary trace that may vanish in characteristic two.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
The resulting right-subspace count has a $2^c$ character cost, where
$c$ is the acquired binary codimension in the full Hom space. This
is polynomial for $c=O(\log L)$ and otherwise is a parameterized bound.
It does not establish efficient simultaneous arbitrary restrictions on
both factors, or polynomial counting for unrestricted Boolean circuits.
