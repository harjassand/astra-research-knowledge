\section{Statement and exact parameters}

This note verifies a family admitted by
\path|work_matrix_root/matrix_target_geometry.py|, including the raw polar
split, its orientation, the actual common scalar fields, and interacting
target carriers. No algebra decomposition or coordinate change is supplied
to the consumer. It also gives a direct formula specialized to this family.
The ingredients are classical matrix-module and finite-subspace identities;
the result asserted here is the complete manufactured interface and its
proved family, not historical novelty of those ingredients.

Let $F=\F_q$, $q=2^a$, $b\ge2$, $s\ge2$, $p=s$, and $d=2bs$. Partition
the $d$ coordinates into $2b$ consecutive blocks of size $s$. Define
\[
 \mathcal L=\{\operatorname{diag}(A_1,A_1,\ldots,A_b,A_b):
                 A_i\in M_s(F)\}\subset M_d(F).
\]
Use the nondegenerate pairing
$\langle A,Y\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\F_2}(\operatorname{tr}(AY))$.
The actual right input is
\[
 W=\mathcal L^\perp
  =\{Y\in M_d(F):Y_{2i-1,2i-1}=Y_{2i,2i}\ (1\le i\le b)\}.
\]
Every block not constrained by these $b$ equalities is free. In particular,
the two repeated diagonal blocks may vary independently between pairs.
Set
\[
 U=O=F^{p\times d},\qquad B(X,Y)=XY,\quad X\in U,\ Y\in W.
\]
Choose arbitrary binary bases for the independent coordinates. There are
\begin{align*}
 n&=a\{pd+d^2-bs^2\}=ab(4b+1)s^2,\\
 m&=apd=2ab s^2,\qquad c=abs^2.
\end{align*}
Here $n$ is the original independent input count, $m$ is the number of
binary product-output coordinates, and $c$ is the acquired binary
codimension in the full Hom space. For fixed $b=2$, $c=n/9$.

For any $C_0\in F^{p\times s}$ of rank $k$, consider the target
\begin{equation}
 Z=(C_0,0,C_0,0,\ldots,C_0,0)\in F^{p\times d}.
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
The rank $k$ can grow with $s$.

\begin{theorem}[Raw family admitted in polynomial time]
For the complete-register equality $XY=Z$ with the parameters above,
the paired-central-atom frontend manufactures the polar split; its two
global orientation trials recover the stated $U,W$ orientation. The
Hom-space and reverse-trace acquisition manufactures the actual dual,
and the matrix-dual consumer certifies its $b$ simple factors and their
one-copy target orbits. All target carriers coincide, so the complete-level
target-geometry test passes. The exact original-input population is
computed in polynomial time in the ordinary circuit description length.

These conclusions are invariant under arbitrary invertible binary input
changes, input translations, and invertible binary output mixing. They
also compose with the already recognized literal reversible suffix and
terminal complete-register equality syntax. The syntax qualification in
Section~\ref{sec:syntax} is part of the theorem.
\end{theorem}

\section{The actual idealizers, nucleus, and reverse dual}

Both $I_d\in\mathcal L$ and $I_d\in W$: in characteristic two the two
paired identity blocks have zero sum. For any $Z_0\in M_d(F)$,
nondegeneracy and cyclicity of the trace give
\[
 Z_0W\subseteq W\iff \mathcal L Z_0\subseteq\mathcal L,
 \qquad
 WZ_0\subseteq W\iff Z_0\mathcal L\subseteq\mathcal L.
\]
Either right condition forces $Z_0\in\mathcal L$ by applying it to $I_d$;
every $Z_0\in\mathcal L$ also satisfies it because $\mathcal L$ is an
algebra. Thus both actual idealizers of $W$ are exactly $\mathcal L$.

The full binary left nucleus of $B$ is $S=M_p(F)$. To see that this is an
equality, let binary-linear maps $A:U\to U$ and $C:O\to O$ satisfy
\[
 (AX)Y=C(XY)\quad\text{for all }X\in U,\ Y\in W.
\]
Putting $Y=I_d$ gives $A=C$. This common map commutes with all right
multiplications by elements of $W$. Every matrix unit between distinct
$s$-blocks, with any coefficient in $F$, belongs to $W$. Products of such
units generate all of $M_d(F)$: a within-block unit is a product through
any different block, and diagonal units are obtained in the same way.
Consequently the common map is in the binary commutant of the full right
$M_d(F)$ action, which is exactly left $M_p(F)$. This also forces
$F$-linearity; there are no additional binary semilinear operators.

The output span is all $O$ because $B(X,I_d)=X$. The two one-sided
radicals are zero, by the same identity and by varying $X$. The simple
nucleus therefore has the exact acquired parameters $a,p,d,r=d$.
Its full Hom space is the right multiplication space
$\Hom_S(U,O)\cong M_d(F)$.

The existing reverse-trace procedure chooses a nonzero binary functional
on this endomorphism algebra that annihilates commutators. In these
proof coordinates it has the form
\[
 T\longmapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{F/\F_2}
                         (\gamma\operatorname{tr}T),\qquad\gamma\ne0.
\]
This is the reduced algebra trace, not the trace of its represented
$apd\times apd$ binary matrix. Since $\mathcal L$ is $F$-linear,
its annihilator with this scalar factor is still $\mathcal L$.
Thus the acquired reverse dual is exactly the right multiplication
space $\{R_A:A\in\mathcal L\}$, in hidden coordinates.

Its full left multiplier inside $\End_S(U)$ is itself: composition of
right multiplications reverses matrix order, so the condition is
$\mathcal L Z_0\subseteq\mathcal L$, already proved equivalent to
$Z_0\in\mathcal L$. Abstractly it is a product of $b$ copies of
$M_s(F)$, up to the harmless opposite-algebra convention. Each simple
factor acts on its $2s$ column coordinates with natural multiplicity two.
Its actual center agrees with the restriction of the actual center of
$S$, not merely with a field of the same degree. Each dual component
has binary dimension $as^2$, and the common kernel of the full dual is
zero because it contains $R_{I_d}$. All these are exactly the conditions
that the consumer checks by actual binary linear systems and ranks.
No invertible element has to be found in an arbitrary matrix space.

\section{The complete raw polar-adjoint algebra}

The polar map on $U\oplus W$ is
\[
 \beta((X,Y),(X',Y'))=XY'+X'Y.
\]
For a joint adjoint pair $(P,Q)$ use the convention
$\beta(Pz,z')=\beta(z,Qz')$, with multiplication
$(P,Q)(\widetilde P,\widetilde Q)
=(P\widetilde P,\widetilde Q Q)$. Write the two binary-linear maps in
blocks relative to $U\oplus W$.

\begin{lemma}[No hidden off-diagonal adjoints]
Every joint adjoint is diagonal relative to $U\oplus W$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $c,c':U\to W$ be the two off-diagonal blocks of this type. Their
defining identity is $X'c(X)=Xc'(X')$. Put $X$ on a single output row
and $X'$ on a different output row, possible since $p\ge2$. The two
sides have disjoint row support. Varying the nonzero row of $X'$ forces
$c(X)=0$, and likewise for $c'$. Row decomposition and binary linearity
give $c=c'=0$ on all of $U$.

For the other two off-diagonal blocks $e,e':W\to U$ the identity is
$e(Y)Y'=e'(Y')Y$. Taking one argument to be $I_d$ shows
$e(Y)=X_0Y$ and $e'(Y')=X_0Y'$ with the same fixed $X_0\in U$.
It remains that $X_0[Y,Y']=0$ for all $Y,Y'\in W$.
If coordinates $i,j$ lie in different $s$-blocks, the two off-block
units $E_{ij},E_{ji}$ belong to $W$ and their commutator is
$E_{ii}+E_{jj}$. Therefore columns $i,j$ of $X_0$ vanish. Every
coordinate has a partner in another block, so $X_0=0$.
\end{proof}

For a remaining diagonal identity $(aX)Y=Xe'(Y)$, putting $Y=I_d$
forces $aX=XZ_0$, and then $e'(Y)=Z_0Y$. The requirement that $e'$ map
$W$ to itself is exactly $Z_0\in\mathcal L$. The other diagonal
identity gives an independent $Z_1\in\mathcal L$. All joint adjoints
are therefore
\[
 P=\operatorname{diag}(R_{Z_0},L_{Z_1}),\qquad
 Q=\operatorname{diag}(R_{Z_1},L_{Z_0}).
\]
Their multiplication is $\mathcal L^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal L$.
In particular its binary dimension is $2abs^2$, its ordinary center
is $F^{2b}$, and the adjoint involution exchanges the two atoms for
each block pair. The Frobenius square map is injective on this center.
Its Boolean fixed subspace has dimension $2b$ and is sufficient to
manufacture all primitive central idempotents by successive binary
splitting, without enumerating the center or its elements.

\section{Why the acquired orientation is consistent}

For each $i$, the two exchanged central atoms have actual images
\[
 U_i=\{X:\text{columns outside pair }i\text{ are zero}\},
 \quad
 W_i=\{Y:\text{rows outside pair }i\text{ are zero}\}.
\]
Their dimensions over $F$ are
\[
 \dim_F U_i=2ps=2s^2,\qquad
 \dim_F W_i=2sd-s^2=(4b-1)s^2.
\]
The subtraction of $s^2$ in the second expression is the one paired
diagonal-block equality in those rows; the other row blocks are free.
Since $b\ge2$, every $W_i$ is strictly larger than its paired $U_i$.
The implementation's rule selecting the larger actual image in each
exchanged pair therefore selects all of $W$, never a mixture of left
and right components. Under an arbitrary binary input conjugacy these
image dimensions are unchanged. Only the global transpose remains.

The first orientation, with left input $W$, has full nucleus isomorphic
to $\mathcal L$ up to opposite convention. Indeed an identity
$X A_W(Y)=C(XY)$ gives $C(X)=XZ_0$ at $Y=I_d$, and hence
$A_W(Y)=YZ_0$ for all $Y$. Its parameter is precisely the right
idealizer $\mathcal L$. Its center is $F^b$, which is not a field for
$b\ge2$, so the simple-nucleus test declines this orientation. The
global transpose recovers $U$ on the left and has the certified simple
nucleus $M_p(F)$ proved above. This uses two explicit global trials;
there is no search over the $2^b$ component orientation choices.

The common polar radical is zero. Hence a shared affine center, when
introduced by translating the inputs, is unique and is manufactured
by the frontend's binary linear system. A dense affine input hiding
cannot remove this center or create an alternative orientation.

\section{The actual target carriers coincide}

For the target in \eqref{eq:target}, a dual element with factor $A_i$
has the $i$th output pair $(C_0A_i,0)$. If $C_0\ne0$, the two-sided
span $M_p(F)C_0M_s(F)$ is the full $p\times s$ matrix space: multiply
a nonzero entry on either side by matrix units, then scale it.
The actual joint cyclic submodule generated by $D_i(Z)$ therefore
has binary dimension $aps$, exactly one joint simple-module copy.
For $C_0=0$ it has dimension zero, also admitted by the code.

For completeness, the consumer's target carrier is acquired directly
in the dual of the actual binary coordinate space of $S$. It is the
span of the rows of all evaluation maps
\[
 S\longrightarrow U,\qquad T\longmapsto T D_i(Z),
                  \qquad D_i\in\mathcal D_i.
\]
Its annihilator is the common kernel of those maps. In proof
coordinates that kernel is
\[
 K_i=\{T\in M_p(F):TC_0A_i=0\text{ for all }A_i\in M_s(F)\}
    =\{T:TC_0=0\}.
\]
It is independent of $i$. Thus all $b$ actual row spaces coincide,
and their common binary dimension is $apk$, where $k=\rank C_0$.
There is one distinct actual carrier, its natural dimension is $k$,
and its ambient natural dimension is also $k$. This is the complete
Grassmann level with cardinality ${k\brack k}_q=1$, including the
zero case ${0\brack0}_q=1$. The component activities are multiplied
within this actual carrier group, exactly as required by the code.

These are interacting targets. For $k\ge2$ and $b\ge2$, two rank-one
factor matrices can give either coincident or distinct nonzero lines
in $\im C_0$. The total target rank can therefore be one or two even
when both individual factor ranks are one and all other factors
vanish. No additivity of target ranks is assumed.

\section{An explicit reconstruction formula}

Put ${v\brack j}_q=0$ outside $0\le j\le v$. For $0\le j\le k$ define
\[
 R_j(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-k+j}
 {s-k+j\brack\ell}_q\prod_{u=0}^{\ell-1}(q^s-q^u)\,z^{2\ell}.
\]
For a fixed $j$-space $J\subseteq\im C_0$, the condition
$\im(C_0A_i)\subseteq J$ says
$\im A_i\subseteq C_0^{-1}(J)$, a space of dimension $s-k+j$.
The coefficient of $z^{2\ell}$ counts the rank-$\ell$ maps into
that space: choose their image, then a surjection onto it. The weight
is twice their rank because each matrix factor is repeated twice.
The polynomial for all $b$ independent factors with total target
image contained in $J$ is therefore $R_j(z)^b$.

Subspace-lattice M\"obius inversion gives the exact joint histogram
\begin{equation}
 H(t,\rho)={k\brack\rho}_q[z^t]
 \sum_{j=0}^{\rho}{\rho\brack j}_q(-1)^{\rho-j}
      q^{\binom{\rho-j}{2}}R_j(z)^b.
 \label{eq:hist}
\end{equation}
It counts actual dual elements having ordinary matrix rank $t$ and
target-product rank $\rho$. Indeed the actual dual is the block
matrix $\operatorname{diag}(A_1,A_1,\ldots,A_b,A_b)$, so
\[
 t=2\sum_i\rank A_i,\qquad
 \rho=\dim_F\sum_i\im(C_0A_i).
\]
In particular $\sum_{t,\rho}H(t,\rho)=q^{bs^2}$.

Let $F_{p,d,d,q}(k,t,\rho)$ be the already proved signed universal
evaluation-fiber formula in
\path|work_matrix_root/UNSATURATED_MODULE_EVALUATION_THEOREM.tex|.
Character orthogonality on the actual right slice now gives
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\#\{(X,Y)\in U\times W:XY=Z\}
    =q^{-bs^2}\sum_{t=0}^{d}\sum_{\rho=0}^{k}
           H(t,\rho)F_{p,d,d,q}(k,t,\rho).}
 \label{eq:count}
\end{equation}
This is the original-input population because the coordinate maps
are bijective, and $W$ is exactly the actual right input. No internal
gate or manufactured Hom coordinate is counted as an independent input.

For explicitness, in this square-right setting the imported scalar is
the following polynomial-size integer expression, with $w=t-\rho$:
\begin{align*}
 F_{p,d,d,q}(k,t,\rho)
 &=\sum_{h=0}^{d-k}
 \sum_{\ell=\max(0,h-(d-k-w),h+t-d)}^{\min(h,w)}
 {w\brack\ell}_q {d-k-w\brack h-\ell}_q (-1)^{t-\ell}\\
 &\qquad\cdot q^{(w-\ell)(h-\ell)+ph+\binom{t-\ell}{2}}
       \prod_{v=0}^{d-h-t+\ell-1}(q^d-q^{t-\ell+v}).
\end{align*}
The expression is used only on feasible rank triples; empty sums and
products have their usual values. Its proof is unchanged by the present
dual compression.

\section{Description, hiding, and arithmetic costs}\label{sec:syntax}

The input to the compiler is an ordinary fan-in-two Boolean DAG with
$n$ declared independent inputs, $G$ explicit gates, and one output.
Indices are binary. Let
$L=(n+G+1)\lceil\log_2(n+G+2)\rceil$ and $N=n+m+G+2=O(L)$
on the admitted family. The displayed matrix descriptions are proofs
of admission, not an extra input to the compiler.

The family permits dense invertible binary input maps and translations.
Such transformations conjugate the actual joint-adjoint algebra and
preserve all central image dimensions. Invertible binary output mixing
preserves the entire polar-adjoint system and conjugates the output
action of the nucleus. Thus the preceding conditions are manufactured
in the hidden coordinates. The targets are transformed by the same
output mixing; any output constants remain in the acquired affine
constant and are subtracted from the target.

The ordinary DAG must expose its initial product registers through the
frontend's exact degree-at-most-two ANF propagation. It must then have
a suffix of recognized literal NOT, CNOT, and Toffoli register updates
followed by a complete-register equality. The suffix can be arbitrarily
long; the actual $G$ remains in the cost. Peeling reverses these
bijections and changes the target, without creating independent input
bits. The theorem does not claim admission of every semantically
equivalent circuit encoding. Unknown high-degree intermediate ANFs
are never truncated or assumed quadratic.

Using an ordinary binary basis for $F$, one can construct initial product
circuits using $O(a^2pd^2)$ gates by direct multiplication, plus the
explicit linear input/output transformations and the reversible suffix.
This gives a polynomial-size ordinary description for every parameter
choice. The consumer is not supplied that basis, an irreducible
polynomial, field generators, matrix units, factor projectors, or a
decomposition. It obtains the representation by its binary systems.

All action-space dimensions are $O(N)$; all algebra and Hom dimensions
are $O(N^2)$. The existing naive elimination, multiplication, and full
sandwich-span certificates fit within the conservative $O(N^{14})$
bit acquisition bound. Each actual factor and carrier is examined;
there are $b\le d\le N$ of them. Formula~\eqref{eq:hist} uses $k+1$
polynomial powers, each of degree at most $2bs=d$. Binary exponentiation
uses $O(\log(b+1))$ polynomial products per power. At most
$(d+1)(k+1)=O(N^2)$ rank profiles are reconstructed. Gaussian binomials,
the M\"obius weights, and the signed-fiber expressions all have polynomial
bit cost; intermediate integer lengths are $O(N^2)$. The safe aggregate
bound $O(N^{16})$ bit operations and polynomial space consequently
applies to acquisition, geometry, and exact reconstruction. The final
count has at most $n+1$ bits.

No $2^c$ term occurs in this production bound. With $b=2$ and growing
$s$, it replaces a $2^{n/9}$ character enumeration by polynomial
arithmetic while allowing $k$ to grow. These are certified restrictions
on a meaningful family, not completeness conditions for arbitrary
Boolean formulas or circuits. No general polynomial-time counting
claim follows.
