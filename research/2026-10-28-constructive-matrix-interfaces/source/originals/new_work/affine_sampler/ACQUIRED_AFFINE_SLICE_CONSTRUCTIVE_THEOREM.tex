\begin{abstract}
The recovered source-free matrix-product compiler counts arbitrary affine
right-Hom slices with a $2^c$ character sum, where $c$ is their actual binary
codimension. Counting alone does not give a polynomial uniform sampler:
rejection from the full product fiber can have exponentially small acceptance
even at $c=1$, and adding arbitrary input pins need not retain the acquired
structure. We complete the bounded/logarithmic-codimension branch by counting
a carefully chosen sequence of acquired right-matrix row prefixes. Complete
rows are removed by a quotient. A partially fixed next row has only three
rank-update classes; their signed Fourier masses are evaluated by binary
elimination, without enumerating the growing prefix's characters. This gives
exact conditional counts, original-input witnesses, and exact uniform samples
at cost $2^c$ times a polynomial. Coordinates and the corrected affine input
map are actually manufactured. Arbitrary original-input conditioning,
unbounded-codimension polynomial sampling, and unrestricted Boolean counting
are not proved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Acquired input contract and statement}

Let $q=2^a$. The raw input is an ordinary indexed Boolean gate DAG, with $n$
declared independent input bits. The inherited frontend propagates exact
quadratic coefficients on the acquired frontier and reverses a recognized
literal reversible suffix ending in a complete-register equality. Its
shared-DAG traversal is memoized; internal wires are never new independent
inputs. Higher degree is a legitimate decline, not truncated information.

The actual polar/adjoint and affine-Hom certificates manufacture a split,
a checked simple nucleus $S\simeq M_p(\F_q)$, binary modules $U,O,V$, and an
injective $\iota:V\to H=\Hom_S(U,O)$. They certify the complete identity
\[
 (h_0+\iota(v))(u)=y,\qquad
 x=x_{\rm aff}+J(u,v),
 \tag{1}
\]
where $x$ is the original input, $J$ is a known binary isomorphism, and the
full marked output is $c_0+P_Oy$ for a known injective output map $P_O$.
Inconsistent output dependencies give zero. In particular,
\[
 \dim_2U=apd,\quad \dim_2O=apr,\quad \dim_2H=adr,\quad
 c=adr-\dim_2V,\quad n=apd+adr-c.
\]
Both orientations of the acquired split can be checked and the smaller
certified codimension chosen.

The inherited constructive matrix-coordinate procedure, applied to the
\emph{actual} simple algebra, manufactures field arithmetic and bijections
\[
 U\simeq\F_q^{p\times d},\quad O\simeq\F_q^{p\times r},\quad
 H\simeq\F_q^{d\times r},
\]
under which evaluation becomes $XY=Z$. The right affine slice is represented
by independent binary equations
\[
 \lambda_j(Y)=b_j,\qquad 1\le j\le c.                     \tag{2}
\]
No matrix decomposition, field basis, or supplied coordinate labels are
accepted in place of acquisition. The literal-coordinate class in the code
is an evaluator used by this native wrapper and by independent audits.

\begin{theorem}[Constructive completion of the character-enumerable branch]
Given this checked contract, a Las Vegas algorithm of expected cost
$2^c\operatorname{poly}(L)$, with $L$ the explicit raw input length,
manufactures a representation supporting:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the exact original-input count;
\item exact counts conditional on any prefix consisting of complete acquired
      rows of $Y$ and an initial sequence of binary coordinates of its next row;
\item a satisfying assignment on the original $n$ input bits, when one exists;
\item an exactly uniform random satisfying assignment on those same bits.
\end{enumerate}
The prefix constraints are exported as explicit binary affine equations on
the original input. There is no exponential dependence on prefix length in
addition to $2^c$. For $c\le K\log_2 L$ with fixed $K$, the expected cost is
polynomial. The implemented native admission uses $2^c\le L$.
\end{theorem}

This is not a closure theorem for arbitrary supplied original-input pins.
The right-row ordering is chosen after verified coordinate acquisition.
For unrestricted $c$, the explicit $2^c$ cost remains. The recovered
rank-one-dual sampler has a different, complementary admission: it can handle
unbounded $c$ when its actual rank-one certificate holds.

\section{Inherited signed full-product evaluator}

For $X\in\F_q^{p\times d}$, $Y\in\F_q^{d\times r}$ and a frequency matrix
$D\in\F_q^{d\times r}$, use
\[
 \chi_D(Y)=(-1)^{\Tr_{\F_q/\F_2}\sum_i D_iY_i^{\mathsf T}}.
\]
The sum
\[
 F_{p,d,r,q}(k,t,\rho)=\sum_{XY=Z}\chi_D(Y)                \tag{3}
\]
depends on $k=\rank Z$, $t=\rank D$, and $\rho=\rank(ZD^{\mathsf T})$.
This is the proved evaluator
\path|signed_universal_product| in the recovered
\path|work_matrix_root/unsaturated_bilinear_counter.py|, not an assumed
oracle. Its code stores the frequency transpose in the reverse-Hom
orientation. To make the dependency explicit, put $w=t-\rho$. Its finite
dimension sum is
\[
 \sum_{h=\max(0,r-d)}^{r-k}
 \ \sum_{\ell=\max(0,h-(r-k-w),h+t-r)}^{\min(h,w)}
 \gb{w}{\ell}\gb{r-k-w}{h-\ell}(-1)^{t-\ell}
 q^{(w-\ell)(h-\ell)+p(d-r+h)+\binom{t-\ell}{2}}
 \prod_{j=0}^{r-h-t+\ell-1}(q^d-q^{t-\ell+j}).
 \tag{4}
\]
All exponents are nonnegative on the summation domain; empty products equal
one. The original kernel-lattice derivation is in
\path|UNSATURATED_MODULE_EVALUATION_THEOREM.tex|, with an independent
row-space derivation and trace-Gram checks in
\path|RIGHT_CHARACTER_FIBER_MOBIUS_AUDIT.tex|. Gaussian coefficients and
all arithmetic are evaluated exactly. Formula (4) also covers a
zero-column quotient:
$F_{p,d,0,q}(0,0,0)=q^{pd}$.

Fourier inversion of (2) uses precisely the original characters
\[
 N=2^{-c}\sum_{\epsilon\in\F_2^c}
       (-1)^{\epsilon\cdot b}
       F_{p,d,r,q}\bigl(\rank Z,\rank D_\epsilon,
                                  \rank(ZD_\epsilon^{\mathsf T})\bigr),
 \tag{5}
\]
where $D_\epsilon$ is the actual trace-dual representation of
$\sum_j\epsilon_j\lambda_j$. All phase bits are retained. The old evaluator
already computes (5); the missing constructive operation is a useful sequence
of conditionals that does not replace $c$ by $c+$ number of assigned bits.

\section{Removing complete fixed rows}

Fix the first $t$ rows of $Y$ to a matrix $P$. Put
\[
 A=\row P,\quad \alpha=\dim A,\quad d'=d-t,\quad r'=r-\alpha,\quad
 \kappa=\dim(A+\row Z)-\alpha.
\]
Partition $Y=[P;B]$ and $X=[X_0\ X_1]$. In $\F_q^r/A$ the product equation
is $X_1\overline B=\overline Z$. For every lifted quotient solution,
\[
 X_0P=Z-X_1B
\]
has $q^{p(t-\alpha)}$ solutions. Each of the $d'$ quotient rows of $B$
has $q^\alpha$ lifts. Summing a character over those lifts is zero unless
its corresponding frequency row annihilates $A$; otherwise it contributes
$q^\alpha$.

\begin{lemma}[Complete-row quotient]
If every remaining frequency row annihilates $A$, then
\[
 \sum_{\substack{XY=Z\\Y_{<t}=P}}\chi_D(Y)
 =
 \chi_{D_{<t}}(P)\,
 q^{\alpha(d-t)+p(t-\alpha)}
 F_{p,d',r',q}(\kappa,\rank D_{\ge t},
                            \rank(ZD_{\ge t}^{\mathsf T})).
 \tag{6}
\]
If some remaining frequency row does not annihilate $A$, the sum is zero.
\end{lemma}
The preceding lift argument proves the formula. The two frequency ranks can
be evaluated in ambient coordinates: annihilation of $A$ makes the rows
faithful quotient functionals. In particular, (6) constructs no quotient
isomorphism as an uncharged oracle.

\section{The next row: three rank-update classes}

Suppose $s$ binary coordinates of the next row are fixed to
$\beta\in\F_2^s$, with $0\le s<ar$. The code uses low-coordinate prefixes;
the same lemma applies to any explicitly listed binary functionals on that
one row. Let $\delta_\epsilon\in\F_q^r$ be the trace-dual frequency
representing those functionals with coefficient vector $\epsilon$.
Their indicator is
\[
 2^{-s}\sum_{\epsilon\in\F_2^s}
       (-1)^{\epsilon\cdot\beta}
       (-1)^{\Tr(\delta_\epsilon Y_t^{\mathsf T})}.
 \tag{7}
\]
It changes the first remaining frequency to $u=D_t+\delta_\epsilon$.
We will \emph{evaluate}, rather than enumerate, this additional sum.

Let $D_{\rm rest}=D_{>t}$. If a row of $D_{\rm rest}$ does not annihilate
$A$, every summand vanishes. Otherwise define
\[
 K=\row D_{\rm rest},\qquad E=A^\perp,\qquad
 B=E\cap\{u:Zu^{\mathsf T}\in ZK^{\mathsf T}\}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Then $K\subseteq B\subseteq E$. Put
$\ell_0=\dim K$ and $\rho_0=\dim ZK^{\mathsf T}$.
There are exactly three possibly nonzero rank classes:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 u&\rank [u;D_{\rm rest}]&
       \rank(Z[u;D_{\rm rest}]^{\mathsf T})\\ \hline
 K&\ell_0&\rho_0\\
 B\setminus K&\ell_0+1&\rho_0\\
 E\setminus B&\ell_0+1&\rho_0+1 .
\end{array}                                                   \tag{9}
\]
Rows outside $E$ vanish by (6). Adding one row changes its span rank by at
most one. Its target image either lies in the previous image or adds one
dimension, proving the table.

For $T=K,B,E$, define the signed mass
\[
 \mu_T=\sum_{\epsilon:\ D_t+\delta_\epsilon\in T}
                    (-1)^{\epsilon\cdot\beta}.                \tag{10}
\]
Membership is an explicitly constructed binary affine linear system in
$s$ variables. If it has no solution, $\mu_T=0$. If its solutions are
$\epsilon_0+V$, then
\[
 \mu_T=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\beta\text{ is nonzero on }V,\\
 (-1)^{\epsilon_0\cdot\beta}2^{\dim V},&\beta|_V=0.
 \end{cases}                                                 \tag{11}
\]
Binary elimination and basis dot products evaluate this expression. The
number $s$ enters polynomially; no $2^s$ enumeration is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Partial-row formula]
Write $F(\kappa,\ell,\rho)=F_{p,d',r',q}(\kappa,\ell,\rho)$. The exact
one-character sum with the complete and partial row prefix is
\begin{align}
 2^{-s}\chi_{D_{<t}}(P)q^{\alpha(d-t)+p(t-\alpha)}
 \bigl[&
 \mu_K F(\kappa,\ell_0,\rho_0) \notag\\
 &+(\mu_B-\mu_K)F(\kappa,\ell_0+1,\rho_0) \notag\\
 &+(\mu_E-\mu_B)F(\kappa,\ell_0+1,\rho_0+1)\bigr].
 \tag{12}
\end{align}
Terms of zero mass are omitted, so impossible rank triples are never sent
to the inherited evaluator.
\end{lemma}
Substitute (7) into (6) and group by (9); the grouped sums are (10).
This proves (12), including all signs and the divisor $2^s$. The formula is
integral because it equals a signed sum over the original finite fiber.
The implementation also checks its exact divisibility.

Every space in (8) is computed by ordinary field elimination. For $K$ one
uses its annihilator; for $B$ one uses the equations for $A^\perp$ and
$Z^{\mathsf T}$ applied to an annihilator of $ZK^{\mathsf T}$. No isomorphism,
orbit, subspace-enumeration, or new count oracle appears.

\section{The exact conditional oracle and uniform construction}

Apply (12) to each of the original $2^c$ frequency matrices in (5), keep its
original affine phase, and divide by $2^c$. This is the conditional count.
The code checks integrality and nonnegativity. When the next row becomes
complete, it is moved to $P$ and the same rule is reused. At a fully fixed
$Y$, the count is zero if (2) fails or $\row Z\nsubseteq\row Y$; otherwise
it is exactly
\[
 q^{p(d-\rank Y)},                                           \tag{13}
\]
the size of the affine solution set for $X$.

Start at $N>0$. At each binary coordinate of $Y$, compute the zero-child
count $N_0$. The one-child count is $N-N_0$. For a uniform sample choose
zero with probability $N_0/N$, using an exact uniform integer in
$\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$. For a witness, take the zero child whenever $N_0>0$ and
otherwise the one child. Update the current count and continue.
There are $adr$ bit decisions. After the complete $Y$ is chosen, solve
each left-row system $xY=z$. For sampling, choose a uniformly random
element of its affine kernel translate; for search, choose a particular
solution. Each field coordinate is an $a$-bit uniform word, so there is
no enumeration of the field.

For a complete $Y$ with $M_Y$ left completions, the prefix probabilities
telescope to $M_Y/N$. Conditional left sampling chooses each completion
with probability $1/M_Y$. Every pair $(X,Y)$ therefore has probability
$1/N$. This proof uses the final positive conditional counts; individual
signed character coefficients are never treated as probabilities.
Integer interval draws can use rejection of bit strings with acceptance
at least one half. This is unrelated to rejection from a rare constrained
matrix-product fiber.

\section{Actual coordinate manufacture and original-input reconstruction}

The code reuses the proved constructive procedure
\path|ManufacturedMatrixCoordinates| from
\path|acquired_rank_one_slice_sampler.py|. This coordinate constructor
itself does not require the rank-one-dual hypothesis.
It acquires the center field from its actual multiplication table, splits
primitive corners by random elements and the binary-linear idempotent
equation in a generated commutative algebra, then constructs connecting
matrix units by linear equations. The inherited corner lemma gives success
probability at least $1/(8h)$ in a corner $M_h(\F_q)$, uniformly in $q$.
Thus this acquisition is Las Vegas with expected polynomial cost.
It is not a search for a rare invertible slice element. Module bases are
transported using the verified units, and all inverse binary coordinate
maps are constructed by elimination.

The field identity need not have binary coordinate word one. The code uses
the actual identity and the actual field trace. In a field basis $e_i$,
it forms the trace Gram matrix
$G_{ij}=\Tr(e_i e_j)$ and inverts it over $\F_2$ to obtain the dual basis
needed in (2), (5), and (7).

Given sampled matrices, convert $X$ to $u$ and $Y$ to the actual Hom vector
$h$. Because the constraints assert $h-h_0\in\im\iota$, its unique preimage

\section{Paid bit, memory, and output costs}

Use the acquired dimension measure
\[
 J_0=2+n+m+\dim_2S+\dim_2U+\dim_2O+\dim_2H+\dim_2V.
\]
All coefficient and field operations have explicit binary representations.
No step enumerates $q$ field elements. There are $2^c$ original characters,
three affine solves per character in (12), a constant number of calls to
(4), and at most $adr\le J_0$ decisions per witness/sample. Field elimination,
trace-dual construction, affine solves, the Gaussian sums, and exact integer
arithmetic are polynomial in $J_0$. Signed intermediate magnitudes have
$O(J_0^2)$ bits, covering the product-fiber sizes and all dimension-sum
terms, even where final cancellations are large.

A deliberately loose bound is
$O(2^cJ_0^{14})$ expected bit operations for preprocessing plus one sample;
it covers the inherited expected $O(J_0^{10})$ coordinate manufacture,
all $J_0$ prefix calls, and schoolbook integer arithmetic. Working space is
polynomial in $J_0$ when original characters are streamed. The scalar cache
has only polynomially many dimension/rank keys; it does not retain an
exponentially sized prefix tree. The answer count has at most $n+1$ bits,
and the sample explicitly contains $n$ bits. Multiple samples pay their
additional prefix and random-output costs.

For native input length $L$, $J_0=O(L^2)$ after the retained size guard.
The inherited deterministic frontend has a conservative $O(L^{14})$ bound.
Thus $2^c\le L$ gives the safe overall expected bound $O(L^{29})$.
This large exponent is a correctness bound, not a practical claim.
After a fixed coordinate certificate is obtained, conditional count queries
are deterministic. The expectation comes from coordinate manufacture and
exact random integer generation, not from a probability of a wrong answer.
An arbitrary random bit stream has no uniform finite termination bound.

\section{A one-condition exponential rejection obstruction outside rank one}

Let $X$ be $p$-by-$2$ and $Y$ be $2$-by-$2$ over $\F_2$, with target zero.
Of the sixteen $Y$, one has rank zero, nine rank one, and six rank two.
The full zero-product fiber has size
\[
 2^{2p}+9\,2^p+6.
\]
Impose the single affine equation $Y_{11}+Y_{22}=1$.
Exactly six of the eight such $Y$ have rank one and two have rank two.
The slice therefore has size
\[
 6\,2^p+2,
\]
so rejection from the full fiber accepts with probability
\[
 \frac{6\,2^p+2}{2^{2p}+9\,2^p+6}\sim6\,2^{-p}.             \tag{14}
\]
Its one-dimensional reverse dual is spanned by the rank-two identity, so
the earlier rank-one-dual certificate does not apply. Formula (12) samples
this family with a constant two-character outer sum.

The audit instantiates $p=64$ and obtains direct exact samples. This is a
literal-coordinate control, not a claim that its raw source-free
recognition was run. Separate native examples below verify the full
recognition-to-sampling interface outside the old rank-one admission.
The two-condition example $Y_{11}=Y_{22}=1$ has size $2^p+3$ and is also
retained as a useful control.

\section{Relation to established methods}

Character orthogonality, rank-metric MacWilliams transforms and generation
from completion counts are classical. Ravagnani's
\emph{Duality of Codes Supported on Regular Lattices, with an Application to
Enumerative Combinatorics}, especially Theorem 7 and Corollary 31, treats
finite-abelian-group character transforms and subgroup-chain strata
(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.02383}). His \emph{Rank-metric codes and
their duality theory}, Theorem 31 and Corollary 33, gives Gaussian moment
identities and recursive rank-distribution inversion
(\url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1333}). These are close antecedents for
the Fourier ingredients; a citation to them alone does not prove the
specific quotient product and prefix-closure formulas (6)--(12).

The count-to-generation connection is established in Jerrum--Valiant--Vazirani,
\emph{Random generation of combinatorial structures from a uniform
distribution}, Theoretical Computer Science 43 (1986), 169--188.
Here the additional work is to provide every conditional actually used by
that strategy without increasing the exponential parameter, and to recover
the actual coordinates and original inputs. Existing KUS and
\path|decdnnf_rs| implementations already support uniform generation after
knowledge compilation; that compilation cost remains part of a fair
same-circuit comparison. See the preserved primary-research notes.
No exhaustive novelty or historical-priority claim follows from this
focused literature check.

The substantial removed barrier is constructive closure for the generic
character-enumerable affine-slice counting branch. High-codimension
semisimple compressed counts still need a positive constructive mechanism;
the present theorem does not turn signed histograms into probabilities.
The civilisation-level objective remains a research objective, and
polynomial-time exact counting for arbitrary circuits remains unproved.
