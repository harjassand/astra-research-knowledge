# Exact continuous covariant-noise extension of the atomic spacetime theorem

2026-10-29. This closes the continuous jump/no-jump interface for a nontrivial noise class. It remains conditional on the existing deterministic D0/D1 contracts. It does NOT cover arbitrary noncovariant Hamiltonian noise at finite stabilizer-dephasing rate.

## Setting

The ideal atomic SSF memory and final decoder are as in `producer-workspace/SPACETIME_CPTP_MEMORY.md`. Let code distance d exceed the maximum noise support size s. Added local joint data-bath noise has generator

    L_cov(t)=-i[sum_Z h_Z,0(t),.] + sum_{Z,a} D[K_Z,a(t)] + L_bath(t),

where h_Z,0 commutes with every stabilizer, and each K_Z,a is a stabilizer-character eigenoperator with syndrome change a. Operators have at most s data sites but may share the same arbitrary retained bath. Bath-only evolution is unrestricted except for a well-defined joint dynamics. Joint norms, not reduced-data norms, bound strengths. Let

    R_Z(t)=sum_a K_Z,a† K_Z,a,
    ||R_Z(t)|| <= epsilon_Z,

and impose bounded primitive incidence as in the spacetime theorem. Ideal hub clocks remain independent exogenous clocks. Noise coefficients may be deterministic time-dependent; bounds are uniform. Initial logical state/reference factor from the bath as usual.

## 1. The no-jump evolution is exactly benign, not a small error

Every data Pauli appearing in h_Z,0 or R_Z has zero syndrome and support <=s<d, so it is a stabilizer up to phase. Therefore, on each full syndrome sector, h_Z,0 and R_Z act as identity on the logical subsystem tensored with a syndrome-dependent bath operator. The quantum-jump no-count propagator, generated by

    -i sum_Z h_Z,0 - (1/2)sum_Z R_Z

plus bath dynamics, preserves the syndrome and acts trivially on logical information. It may change bath amplitudes and correlations arbitrarily; it is not assumed TP by itself. Its duration and total norm need not be small.

For each nonzero K_Z,a select any data Pauli E_Z,a in its Pauli expansion. Every other data Pauli in that expansion differs from E_Z,a by a zero-syndrome Pauli supported on Z, hence by a stabilizer. Thus

    K_Z,a = E_Z,a B_Z,a(stabilizers,bath),

where B is syndrome-diagonal and logical-identity. A physical jump is exactly one bounded-support Pauli action followed/preceeded by a syndrome-controlled bath operation. Zero-charge jumps are benign too (they may be counted conservatively).

Consequently the usual serial Pauli support accounting is exact between jumps; there are no uncounted no-jump errors and no hidden Pauli twirl.

## 2. State-dependent quantum jump rates have the required positive tails

For a normalized conditional joint state, the conditional intensity of all Z-jumps is Tr(R_Z rho)<=epsilon_Z. More invariantly, tilt the positive record instrument by t^{N_Z}: its generator is the original Lindbladian plus (t-1)sum_a K_Z,a(.)K_Z,a†. Taking the trace gives

    d/dt_phys Tr(rho_tilt) <= (t-1)epsilon_Z Tr(rho_tilt).

The same argument for a selected set of supports yields

    E[t^{N(W,block)} | complete ideal hub clock schedule]
       <= exp[(t-1) tau sum_{Z:Z touches W} epsilon_Z].       (A)

This is uniform over logical input/reference/bath and remains valid with ideal hub interventions at prescribed times: each intervention is CPTP and leaves tilted trace unchanged. It therefore holds even after fixing the entire independent ideal-clock schedule. There is no claim that jump times are independent Poisson variables; (A) is the required dominating exponential moment.

Multiplying by t^{M_j^*(W)} and averaging over ideal clocks gives precisely the joint fixed-certificate estimate used in the spacetime proof, with primitive rate epsilon_Z. Thus its connected-animal counting and backward-chain tail estimates apply unchanged.

## 3. Quantum conclusion

The geometric good-record event uses only actual jump supports/times and ideal hub clock records. On it, every compatible history is corrected by the same final decoder; all no-jump and jump bath factors are logical-identity. The corresponding good-record CP map after decoding therefore has the form a id_L, with a independent of input. The complement is CP, and trace preservation makes its trace 1-a independent of input as well. Uniform record tail bounds give its diamond norm <=p_bad. Hence

    ||E_dec(t)-id_L||_diamond <= 2 p_bad,

with the same p_bad bound as the original positive-record spacetime theorem. State-dependent jump probabilities introduce no logical-input loophole: the uniform tilted trace bound controls the bad subinstrument, while exact good-record factorization supplies input independence where needed.

This proves an exact continuous local covariant Lindblad-noise extension, including arbitrary local no-jump loss and retained common bath, rather than only independent CPTP kicks.

## 4. Why it applies to the finite-dephasing base

For any arbitrary local K_Z, its stabilizer-character components satisfy

    sum_a K_Z,a† K_Z,a = Twirl_stabilizer(K_Z†K_Z),
    ||sum_a K_Z,a† K_Z,a|| <= ||K_Z||².

No 4^s rate penalty is necessary. Fourier components keep data support Z. The covariant base L_diag constructed in `FINITE_DEPHASING_RESOLVENT.md` therefore belongs to this theorem with small jump budget determined by the original joint Lindblad norms. Its zero-charge Hamiltonian part is benign regardless of norm (though locality and code-distance conditions remain necessary).

This leaves only the nonzero-charge Hamiltonian terms and cross-character dissipator terms to be controlled by finite-rate dephasing excursions. They cannot be discarded or identified with a positive Markov process without an additional all-orders argument.

## 5. Scope and physical implementation

This theorem treats ideal atomic SSF. Applying it to actual partial transactional gates needs their branchwise coefficient semantics and the positive fixed-certificate clock/emission bounds. It does not automatically establish that arbitrary continuous noise remains covariant in the physical dressed-data/controller coordinates. Added stabilizer dephasing is a new bounded-support engineered operation, not silently an existing onsite/two-body primitive.
