# Finite-threshold warning for the moving-fugacity proof

The proof is eventual outside a finite set, not a small-population drift claim. The 100-digit diagnostic intentionally retains failures:

- B=100, A=B^25 gives positive normalized adjoint defect about 0.2307; the O(1/log B) shift correction is not small yet.
- A up to 10^6 with B=0,1,2 also gives positive defect. The +2 logarithmic chart and finite reaction jumps mean that incoming neighboring states can remain in the high-fugacity plateau even when the target seems near the A-axis. For B=0, a predecessor can have B=36; all such neighbors must eventually enter the low plateau.
- The corresponding axis tests at A=10^60 are negative. The tested transition points at B=1000 and 10^6 are also negative.

These finite positive defects are compatible with the written eventual theorem. They forbid claims of a small threshold, a practically calibrated certificate, or universal finite-range numerical validation. The independent proof must retain the separate transition-error and neighbor-aware boundary thresholds.
