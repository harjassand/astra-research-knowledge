The strongest completed result is a construction that turns local observations of an unknown many-body plant into a globally optimized static controller. It separates three resources that the earlier work had not separated clearly: how far physical control effects propagate, how difficult the resulting control choices are to optimize, and how much prior structure reduces the observations needed.

This is a tractability result with an executed quantum example. The ordinary alternatives substantially outperform the generic acquisition method in the examples, so the evidence supports the principle more strongly than a new practical quantum advantage.

What now becomes constructible

Assume a finite alphabet of static controls, calibrated local measurements, and two uniform physical properties:

  • After a specified settling interval, each measured local observable is close to its stationary value from every initial state, including states left by earlier measurements.
  • Changing sufficiently distant controls has a bounded effect on that stationary local observable.

These properties must hold across the allowed control family. A few successful experiments or a spectral gap alone do not establish them.

The construction then:

  1. Uses a covering schedule of global settings to measure every relevant local control configuration on one retained plant.
  2. Builds simultaneous confidence intervals for the local responses, allowing measurement disturbance and temporal dependence.
  3. Converts the objective into a classical problem with local factors.
  4. Constructs the control field and a Bellman potential by dynamic programming, together with a computable lower/upper performance bracket.

The ordinary construction changed the conclusion

Giving the ordinary method the trusted circuit family and the same observations was decisive: fitting the unknown offsets from the first of the 64 acquisition settings already found the same controller. It used 147,456 acquisition cycles. Thus the generic response-table approach loses acquisition by a factor of 64 in this structured finite example. That comparison is conditional on the trusted family; it does not validate an arbitrary fitted model.

The complementary retained heat-bath experiments reached the same lesson. Using identical records and no new observations, the ordinary method combined structural fitting with confidence intervals and a uniform comparison theorem. In one 64-cell case it tightened the performance-gap bound from 0.06536 to 0.03460. Its selected point was slightly worse, so a tighter certificate must not be reported as a better controller. Early data prefixes found good policies, but their wider confidence bounds did not demonstrate equal-accuracy sample savings.

Local-observable learning across dissipative phases already has substantial primary machinery in Onorati and colleagues’ work. The present result builds on that direction rather than claiming its origin. arxiv.org The fidelity holdout uses the established stabilizer specialization of direct fidelity estimation. arxiv.org

The consequential principle is therefore: local physical response can make the control objective learnable, while control geometry makes global selection constructible. A compact trusted model can supply that objective much more cheaply. An ordinary method adopting the reduction is a success for the principle.

Obstructions preserved

The first attack derived exact physical Poisson-continuation equations for the earlier controller. Their algebra passed review, but a computable stable continuation region and the required response acquisition remain missing. A small residual can conceal a large correction when relaxation is slow. That route remains a partial mechanism, not an efficient general compiler.
