# What the new head construction does and does not remove

The actual holey-head construction removes three concrete technical restrictions:
1. Scalar first-up probability need not be small in every head phase; exact finite matrix elimination can earn phase offsets.
2. A uniform unit forcing is unnecessary; stronger negative margins can be allocated to the actual tail-entrance phases.
3. Conservative fibers need no quantitative mixing or polynomial absorption bound when a resource gap and an earned factorial collar allow a slowly growing cap.

The remaining structural problem is not simply a larger matrix. The present mechanism has a conservative finite fiber, a resource coordinate whose high-order rates accelerate clearing relative to births, an exact or monotone residual compatible with that clearing, and an outer factorial drift that pays the incoming cap forcing. A generic weakly reversible reaction network has not been shown to admit those jointly.

In a general Newton-face decomposition, internal face reactions may conserve one weighted count, but there need not be a separate resource coordinate whose large population outruns the worst finite-fiber absorption time. Multiple clearing directions may not preserve the same residual. Replacing them by an assumed inward barrier would supply the desired capability rather than prove it.

Likewise, the finite-head margin cycle criterion is sufficient and computable, not an established consequence of weak reversibility. A nonnegative margin cycle, an all-zero leading return mode, or incompatible residual shifts must trigger a new construction or an explicit failure report, not an assertion that a finite corner can be patched automatically.

These are the current reasons the work remains a restricted, increasingly reusable recurrence-certificate mechanism rather than a solution of general chemical recurrence.
