# Mechanical leading rectangles: what cavity-good boxes actually guarantee
2026-10-30. New deterministic interface lemma; not the unfinished QCA damage theorem.

## 1. Protected patch and good event

Enlarge every service cavity S_h to contain not just the Lyapunov witness, but the full native good-history projector B_h^perp, its forest factor, its full read/write footprint, and the sites of all its retirement/reset channels. This is a fixed enlargement; it changes v,K and packing constants, not the mechanism. A good (h,j) box is the genuine cavity event of at least two SUCCESS terminals during [jT,(j+1)T]. The cavity perturbation acts trivially on this entire protected patch.

All nominal terms preserve h's good range, and every complete h-retirement maps its entire source into that range. These are full-Hilbert individual-Kraus statements, including malformed tags and coherent prefixes. Outside cavity perturbations also preserve it because their system action is disjoint from its support. No Pauli or classical trajectory assumption is used.

## 2. Exact deterministic consequences

In every positive instrument term contributing to a cavity-good box:

(a) h is mechanically good at the END of the box, even if its source was arbitrary/dirty. The first SUCCESS is a complete retirement, and no subsequent term can recreate its bad history inside the protected interval.

(b) At least one SUCCESS in the box (the second, conservatively) implements the exact attempted update Phi_h. It may be a rejected marching-guard attempt; this lemma does not falsely call it an accepted algorithmic step.

(c) If h was good at the box's start, EVERY native terminal during the box is its exact intended attempted channel or an identity clean abort.

(d) If h was initially dirty, at most its FIRST complete retirement can be mechanically nonideal. That instrument has the fixed h payload footprint. It is not erased from the proof by the later good attempt.

These statements hold on arbitrary entangled inputs and for all nominal record branches kept by the positive cavity event. If two consecutive h boxes are good, the later box starts good and satisfies (c). At the initial boundary use actual supplied clean initialization rather than inventing an earlier good box.

## 3. Assignment of inherited dirty damage

For the signed exact-fault family E_R from the cavity expansion, every positive term has good boxes at all labels outside R. Therefore a mechanically nonideal retirement in a currently good box can only inherit from its immediately preceding BAD h box (or from an explicitly unclean initial boundary). Charge it to that earlier box and enlarge the declared damage support one time slab forward and by the fixed payload footprint. A long run of bad boxes is already a run of marked boxes; it is not excused as a single forever-persistent hidden episode. A single good box ends the owner's mechanical dirty episode.

This is a leading-rectangle statement, not an assertion that every bad coefficient has bounded DATA support forever. Errors introduced at a charged footprint can propagate through subsequent CORRECT computation channels; that propagation is the usual logical fault-tolerance problem. The signed Möbius combination preserves any established linear correct-gadget identities termwise, but it does not create identities not proved for its positive terms.

## 4. What remains

The source asynchronous QCA needs a precise valid-cut theorem, clock-repair/damage induction and progress statement. A fresh native attempt may fail its marching guard. Several attempts may occur in one physical box; a finite-rate point process has no deterministic upper event-count bound. Coherent controller timing is not a supplied synchronized schedule. Thus the mechanical leading-rectangle lemma is necessary but insufficient to identify good physical boxes with the QCA's prescribed virtual macro-locations.

A possible next finite refinement is to include upper bounds on fast causal chains of overlapping actual SUCCESS footprints in the good-box event. Nominal jump-intensity bounds control a prescribed length-L ordered chain by (kappa T)^L/L!, and bounded spatial branching makes sufficiently long fast chains rare. That needs its own joint mixed-instrument estimate and deterministic scheduling use; it is not silently included in the present norm theorem.

The final physical forest dressing also remains real. No ideal output frame inversion, clean retirement or readout is supplied by a successful service count alone.
