# Acquiring a reusable parity family: construction, rigidity, and failed advantage

## Status and actual question

The task is to compute many exact partition sums

    Z(T) = sum_{x in {0,1}^E} product_v T_v(x restricted to the three ports of v)

on a fixed loopless planar cubic graph. Each query supplies eight rational
coefficients per vertex, in the **original** edge coordinates. All local weights
may change. A hidden chart is not supplied. We seek a small acquired certificate
which admits new queries and permits polynomial exact execution.

The construction below works. However, its proposed four-task acquisition
advantage fails: a stronger retained one-instance method, derived explicitly
from the existing Astra N1077 construction, acquires the same family more
cheaply in the experiments. The retained ordinary variable-elimination plan is
also much faster on the executed small graph. No novel tractability class,
historical priority, practical gain, or general learning theory is established.

The useful residual operation is precise: a single generic member can identify
a reusable rational parity family; the retained certificate subsequently admits
arbitrarily singular members that the generic one-instance frontend would reject.
This is a source-derived extension, using established matchgate/Pfaffian theory.

## 1. Contract

Let V=Q^2, and let E_3 be the four-dimensional subspace of V tensor V tensor V
supported on 000, 011, 101, 110. Fix a cyclic port embedding of a planar cubic
graph G. A paired rational chart consists of invertible 2 by 2 matrices G_v,e,
one per incidence, such that

    G_v,e^T G_w,e = I                 for each edge e=vw.

At a vertex define H_v = tensor_{e incident v} G_v,e and S_v = H_v E_3.
The admitted query family is the product of the local S_v. It is a continuum
of new inputs, not a table of old answers. Contraction is bilinear, with no
complex conjugation. Entries and final answers may be signed; the result is
not automatically a probability.

The four-task route receives observations whose columns span S_v at every
vertex, without receiving any H_v or G_v,e. Four observations suffice only when
they are independent locally at every vertex. More observations may be needed.
An observed rank below four is INSUFFICIENT DATA, not a certificate of absence.
No distribution-independent sample-time bound is claimed: a distribution may
put mass 1-epsilon on one tensor and expose the other directions only rarely.
Its rank-acquisition time can grow as 1/epsilon, or be infinite.

The one-instance route instead receives one network satisfying N1077's generic
conditions (nonzero local hyperdeterminants and all four nonzero CP pairings on
every edge), and additionally requires the acquired parity-axis lines to split
over Q. This rational-splitting restriction is narrower than N1077's general
quadratic-field method. A failed generic/rational trial returns UNKNOWN.

Neither route assumes future queries remain in its family: every query is
checked. A changed topology, missing vertex, wrong tensor size, incompatible
edge axis, or failed parity-membership test is rejected, never silently solved
with the old chart.

## 2. Linear recovery from local variation

For a subspace S of V^tensor3 and leg i, define its one-leg preserver algebra

    A_i(S) = {X in Mat_2(Q): (I tensor ... tensor X tensor ... I) S subset S}.

**Lemma 1.** For S=E_3, A_i(S) is exactly the diagonal matrix algebra.

Proof. A diagonal operator preserves the parity of every coordinate basis
vector. Conversely, an off-diagonal matrix entry sends a suitable even basis
vector to an odd basis vector, so cannot preserve E_3. This applies separately
to both off-diagonal entries. QED.

**Lemma 2.** If S=(G_1 tensor G_2 tensor G_3)E_3, then

    A_i(S) = G_i {diagonal matrices} G_i^-1.

Proof. Conjugate the preservation condition by the tensor product of the G_i.
Only the factor at leg i changes. Apply Lemma 1. QED.

Thus A_i(S) is two-dimensional, containing I, and its traceless part is the
line generated by J_i=G_i diag(1,-1) G_i^-1. If B is any 8 by 4 basis matrix
for S and L is a 4 by 8 left annihilator, solve the homogeneous linear system

    L (I tensor ... tensor X tensor ... I) B = 0

in the four entries of X. This is a constant-size exact rational linear system,
not nonlinear search over growing collections of gauge parameters.

Take a nonzero traceless solution J. Cayley--Hamilton gives
J^2=-det(J)I. Under the rational-chart promise, -det(J) is a nonzero rational
square: J is a rational scalar multiple of G_i Z G_i^-1. Take its exact
rational square root, normalize to J^2=I, and obtain rational eigenvectors for
eigenvalues +1 and -1. Repeated or nonsplit roots are explicitly rejected by
this implementation.

At an edge require J_w,e = +/- J_v,e^T. The sign is irrelevant to the axis
line. Choose an eigenvector matrix G at one endpoint and G^-T at the other.
This gives edge duality exactly. Independently transform each learned local
subspace and check that it is entirely even or entirely odd. Since its rank is
four, pure parity containment is equality with that parity subspace.

Eigenvector ordering can leave odd local parities. Swapping the two eigenvectors
on one edge flips the parities at both endpoints. A spanning-tree solve removes
all odd vertices exactly when their parity sum on every component is even.
An odd total component contracts identically to zero for the whole retained
family. The prototype conservatively reports that case instead of installing
an even chart. For the even case it returns the paired chart and verifies all
edge dualities and all local basis identities.

**Theorem 1 (soundness and promised completeness).** With full local spans from
a paired rational even-parity family, the above procedure recovers a paired
rational chart for exactly those local subspaces, up to irrelevant diagonal
rescaling and label choices. Without that promise, a returned certificate is
still sound because the complete local subspace and edge identities are checked.
It may reject other tractable families.

Proof. Lemmas 1--2 force the recovered axes. Any two eigenvector bases of one
axis differ by diagonal rescaling and a possible swap. Such changes preserve
the parity subspace up to its even/odd label. Edge duality and the spanning-tree
argument complete the construction. The independent final identities prove
soundness directly, regardless of how the candidate axes were found. QED.

## 3. The stronger one-instance route: generic axis rigidity

This is extracted from the already-established necessity argument in N1077;
it is not claimed as a new tensor-factorization theorem.

For a generic tensor, use its simple 2 by 2 determinant pencil to obtain a
rank-two CP factorization with three nonsingular leg matrices A_v,e. Such a
factorization is unique up to simultaneous exchange and compensating column
scalings. Pull a hypothetical parity involution into these CP coordinates.
It must exchange the two summands at all three legs: preserving them would
give opposite products of the three traceless eigenvalues on the two nonzero
summands, so the tensor could not be a parity eigenvector. Thus every leg action
has the form

    S_v,e = [[0,r],[1/r,0]].

For W=A_v,e^T A_w,e, edge compatibility reads S_v,e^T W S_w,e=W. All four
entries are nonzero at the generic scope. Entrywise comparison gives

    r*s = W11/W00,    r/s = W10/W01,
    r^2 = W10*W11/(W00*W01).

Consequently the original-coordinate parity axis is the unique projective line

    [J_v,e] = [ A_v,e [[0,r^2],[1,0]] A_v,e^-1 ].             (1)

The two square-root choices negate the involution and do not change the line.
The CP chart ambiguities likewise leave the original line unchanged. N1077's
neighbor determinant quadratic computes r^2 inside the vertex's own formal
quadratic algebra, without assembling a global compositum.

**Theorem 2 (single generic member determines the reusable family).** If a
generic network admits a paired rational parity chart, each incidence axis
line is uniquely determined by the raw adjacent tensors. Once the axes and
local parity labels are fixed, the maximal product of local four-dimensional
parity spaces is determined. A retained chart therefore admits every later
member of that family, whether or not its hyperdeterminants or CP pairings
remain nonzero.

Proof. Equation (1) forces every possible compatible chart to have the same
axis lines. Each normalized axis has two eigenspaces; their choices alter only
the local parity labeling and diagonal rescalings, and hence not the resulting
accepted subspace after the first member's parity is imposed. Tensor-product
edge changes preserve contraction exactly. Later membership is a direct linear
identity and uses no genericity condition. QED.

The adapter computes (1) in the existing two-coordinate formal quadratic
algebra, divides by a nonzero-norm entry, and checks that every resulting ratio
is rational. It then applies the same rational split/duality/parity checks as
Theorem 1. This is an explicit strong retained comparator, not an oracle handed
the test generator's hidden chart. Tests compare the entire accepted subspaces,
not only sampled outputs.

This theorem defeats the proposed acquisition advantage of four independent
tasks. Variation can be useful when no generic member is available, but no
uniform benefit over one-instance recognition follows from it.

## 4. All-weight exact execution, including singular queries

For every new original tensor T_v compute F_v=H_v^-1 T_v. Check all four odd
coordinates vanish. The edge duality identities prove Z(T)=Z(F). This check is
exact and local. If a local tensor is zero, the answer is zero immediately.

For nonzero even F_v choose any even mask m_v with F_v(m_v) nonzero and put

    a_v = F_v(m_v),  f_v(y) = F_v(y xor m_v)/a_v.

Now f_v(000)=1 and f_v is even. A six-vertex planar city realizes it: make
three terminals t_i and three inner vertices u_i; put unit edges t_i-u_i and
a triangle on the u_i. Give u_i-u_j weight f_v(bit_i+bit_j).

Under the standard terminal-deletion signature convention, deleting no
terminals forces all three spokes and gives 1. Deleting terminals i,j leaves
the remaining spoke and the edge u_i-u_j, giving the desired pair coefficient.
Odd deletions leave odd order and have no perfect matching. These are all
eight signatures. No coefficient other than the chosen nonzero anchor is
divided by.

If the pivot flips agree at both ends of an original edge, join its two
terminals directly. The matching either uses this edge (both terminals deleted)
or does not (neither deleted). If the flips disagree, join them by a path with
one extra middle vertex. Exactly one endpoint then matches that middle vertex,
implementing disequality. This preserves planarity. The new graph has at most
6|V|+|E| vertices and its perfect-matching sum, multiplied by product a_v, is
exactly the original contraction.

An explicit structural reference perfect matching is available even if some
of its numerical weights vanish: use the terminal-deletion pattern induced by
the pivot masks on original edge assignment zero, remaining spokes, and the
appropriate inner-triangle edge. Every pivot is even, so this is a complete
matching. Its arc-crossing sign and orientation signs fix the Pfaffian sign.

The implementation checks the expanded rotation's Euler characteristic,
solves and checks the usual clockwise-odd face equations over F2, and evaluates
the exact rational skew Schur-complement Pfaffian. These are established FKT
operations, adapted from the pinned N1077 backend with credit. No floating
determinant square root, guessed sign, or promised nonzero partition is used.
Zero entries, signed weights, singular local tensors and global cancellations
are admitted after the chart has been acquired. Normalized marginals and
sampling are not implemented or claimed here.

## 5. Paid costs and limitations

For K observed tasks, n vertices and maximum expanded coefficient bit length B,
the span route reads O(Kn) coefficients and solves 3n constant-size rational
systems. Basis extraction, failed rank checks, eigenvectors, duality, chart
verification and storage are charged. Constant-size minors have O(B) bit
length up to fixed constants; there is no hidden large algebraic field.
The certificate has O(n) rational entries. It is not necessary to retain all
observations once an exact basis and certificate have been saved.

The time to obtain K informative tasks is an additional source/distribution
cost, not made small by the algebra. If those are user-requested tasks, the cost
of solving them before acquisition is also owed. Unlimited successful-data
arrival is not assumed. The one-instance route charges all rejected generic
trials; one such rejection occurs in the saved five-seed comparison.

For a query with expanded bit length L, reading, applying the chart and testing
parity are O(n) constant-size rational operations on polynomial-size numbers.
The expanded matching graph is linear in n. Orientation and the dense Pfaffian
take polynomial bit time; the Pfaffian uses O(n^3) rational arithmetic and O(n^2)
stored entries. Fraction sizes remain polynomial, by the standard minor-ratio
interpretation of exact elimination. The product of local anchor factors and
the final answer are explicitly represented. Nothing assumes unit-cost
arithmetic on unbounded integers. Repeated fixed pivot masks permit retaining
the orientation; the supplied test executor conservatively recomputes it.

The total lifecycle cost also contains the actual engineering, testing, tuning,
and failed scientific searches. The saved kernel timings do not measure all
those costs. **No positive all-in payback is claimed**, since the tested
candidate already loses to retained conventional execution before adding them.

The source family is exactly tractable only under the checked parity-chart
condition. Noise, arbitrary off-family perturbations, higher arity, general
nonplanarity, arbitrary observation maps, and black-box access without local
coefficients are not covered. A tiny off-family perturbation is rejected, not
approximately corrected. Uniqueness of a generic parity axis does not prove
that all useful representations are unique or that other tasks are hard.

## 6. Comparison and scientific conclusion

The actual execution records are in `strong_comparison_results.json`,
`all_weights_results.json`, and `execution_comparison_results.json`.

* Five independently generated hidden-chart fixtures: the four-task route and
  the conventional one-generic-member adapter have **identical accepted local
  subspaces**. The latter is roughly four times cheaper in acquisition here.
* Fifty new queries, including 25 singular queries, pass the same retained chart.
  Selected cases are checked by complete original-edge enumeration.
* Twelve full all-weights Pfaffian results match complete original-edge sums,
  including pivot flips, vanishing entries, cancellations and zero tensors.
* On the cube, an ordinary retained elimination plan stores 622 projection
  indices, has maximum bag size six, and executes in about 0.72--1.02 ms.
  The chart/Pfaffian prototype takes about 24.8--29.3 ms on the same new inputs.

Planar grids with growing width can distinguish polynomial Pfaffian execution
from a particular exponential-width elimination plan, but this is the established
matchgate advantage. It would not distinguish this construction from an equally
informed retained holographic solver. We did not run a useful nongenerated
large-width workload and do not claim that known asymptotics establish a
practical crossover.

The source-derived all-weights bridge is legitimate retained capital. The
proposed new acquisition advantage and practical consequence did not survive
the stronger comparisons. The civilisation-level objective remains unachieved.
