## 4. Worst caps versus executed private-event work

The companion EXPECTED_EXECUTION_SCOPE.md derives the ordinary energy estimate E N<=8E(0)/kappa+32r_E²T/kappa² and keeps actual source-control energy injection costs. An implementation that measures private physical first-event branches and dynamically executes the loop can have expected calls much smaller than its safety cap. A fixed statically padded circuit cannot claim this reduction.

For the localized chain, r_E²<=1, kappa=19/39 and arbitrary input E(0)<=n. This gives exact expected calls <=258+8n/kappa+32/kappa²; a finite counter-marginal error adds at most S*epsilon. The proof compares only that bounded integer counter through the common-uniform hybrid, not continuous versus discrete timestamp records. For an independently stationary classical task, a paid pure-reference representative can further set E(0)=0, but that is not a replacement for arbitrary correlated source inputs.

These expected-execution observations are separate from the main worst-cap tables and are not permission to measure inner source PREP registers or stop coherent rejection attempts for free.

## 5. Strong ordinary comparisons remain decisive

The original n4 ordinary dense CQ solver and effective model win numerically on their checked fixture. The microscopic direct-trajectory count and generic Lindblad stack action remain the SAME as the frozen audit; they are not lower bounds on all classical or quantum alternatives.

The source-only parent in this open zero-field chain also has ordinary Gaussian/free-fermion structure. The separately checked Gaussian kernel can replace generic LCHS by actual quadratic normal-form rotations and diagonal filters, without making the entire interacting system Gaussian. Its acquired preprocessing, work-qubit domain, kernel error and controlled rotations must be received explicitly before inserting a numerical cost. A conservative form is two Gaussian transforms plus O(n B_s) controlled filter rotations per kernel slot, with B_s determined by actual scaled-time and kernel-error budgets. Millions of kernel slots times that real cost can still mean billions of elementary rotations. No numerical crossover is claimed here.

The exact n-by-n spin-correlation closure and covariance solve already give an inexpensive ordinary effective-generator baseline. High damping at fixed accuracy is especially favorable to that baseline. The construction's finite-rate, retained-input/control and high-precision theorem is a different guarantee; that difference alone is not a practical advantage or a novelty claim.
