The source correctly invokes this established fixed-point amplitude-amplification construction. Its quoted analytical phases and failure expression match that primary source. This is not a new normalization algorithm.

Putting delta=sqrt(xi), the condition cosh(acosh(1/delta)/L)*sqrt(1-pmin)<=1 is exactly equivalent to the displayed lower bound on odd L. It guarantees failed probability at most xi for every admitted p>=pmin. The event race uses pmin=1/4; the other two factories use pmin=1/2. Upward certified odd rounding of L is sufficient.

In this application a generalized source reflection is implemented by the known proposal U_z, a phase on its initialized work registers, and U_z inverse. The success reflection is an actual flag phase. Thus l iterations plus initial preparation use 2l+1=L proposal-unitary/inverse calls. This is deliberately not identified with the primary paper's separate marked-oracle query convention. All phase/reflection, control, and work-register costs remain additional paid operations.

For each retained label z, the process acts in the span of its normalized success and failure projections. The successful vector has the original conditional seed law, multiplied by a scalar amplitude and a possibly z-dependent phase. In PREP dagger SELECT PREP, with SELECT diagonal in retained histories and zero on every failure, that phase cancels and only the squared amplitude remains. The selected contraction changes by at most xi per factory. For at most three adaptive-history factories, fresh retained flags and seeds give a conditional union bound of 3xi. This is not an assertion of a public state-isometry error xi: its failed vector norm can be sqrt(xi). Unpreparing history too soon, exposing source states, or allowing SELECT to mix histories would invalidate this particular argument.

Actual synthesis error is different from ideal failure probability. A total full-circuit operator error zeta changes the selected block by at most 2zeta for unitary PREP circuits and contractive SELECT. It is paid by telescoping across the actual O(L) proposal and reflection calls and their internal gates, rather than by scalar phase-insensitivity of the success probability.

The analytical phases require only finite classical arithmetic at polynomial bit complexity. A quadrant-correct atan2 expression avoids tangent poles; phases are interpreted modulo 2*pi. For odd L, the relevant trigonometric sample does not encounter a zero cosine and its separation is inverse-polynomial in L. Certified elementary-function arithmetic and conservative rounding therefore suffice; no generic polynomial factorization, coherent-duration table, acceptance oracle, or exact-real arithmetic is needed. Precision and proposal costs must still be included in any eventual numerical comparison.

The option supplies a valid, established alternative to the conservative polar polynomial. It does not prove a smaller total gate cost than three-call normalization plus scalar arithmetic, nor amend the frozen last-refresh receiver or any ledger. A further ordinary baseline is capped fresh-attempt rejection with a retained first-success index: its all-fail mass is at most (3/4)^R and SELECT zero on all-fail gives the same mean-only accuracy convention. That observation is not an implementation or an optimality claim.

## Binding finite-grid qualification

Continuous success floors do not follow automatically for arbitrarily rounded independent scalar descriptions. I checked the final author construction that preserves them structurally in the ideal finite-register circuit. Use one acquired f coin and its actual complement 1-f, acquired a0/pi coins in [0,1], and true interval-event flags of one clock. Repeated occurrences of survival x use the same prepared probability distribution (not necessarily the same random seed). Then the late event sum is exactly 1+x(1-f*a0)>=1; the early event Q weight is at least one. The high atom sum is x+1-pi*x>=1 and the low atom race has an always-accept arm. The dyadic conditional-clock floor follows from its exact geometric-bin law. Thus the finite ideal success floors remain 1/4 and 1/2, with changes to the target law charged separately. Subsequent actual gate synthesis is compared to this circuit by full operator-norm telescoping; its raw success need not obey an unperturbed exact inequality.
