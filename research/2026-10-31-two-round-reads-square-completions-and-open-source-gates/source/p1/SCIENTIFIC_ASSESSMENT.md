## What the result establishes

For a known Gaussian input and quadratic target, a fixed first batch pairs opposing eigendirections to reduce the unobserved quadratic part. One outcome-dependent linear measurement then reads the remaining linear term. The resulting two-round policy uses at most `k+1` exact scalar linear reads and has risk no greater than any adaptive policy with budget `k`, including expected budgets. Noninteger budgets give an expected-count construction. The lower bound is pointwise on almost every adaptive transcript; it does not treat the outcome-dependent observed projection as an unconditionally Gaussian vector. The final quadratic correction and cross-term averaging are included.

The original fixed-coordinate theorem required a potentially large multiplicative measurement increase. This is a much sharper feedback-depth principle **under a changed access contract**. Arbitrary programmable linear functionals are additional operations, not a consequence of the previous coordinate interface. The main construction need not be optimal at its own budget: the retained two-read threshold example improves on it.

The noisy inner-product theorem addresses an earlier weak point: absolute final accuracy. With unit-norm measurements, independent Gaussian noise of variance 0.01 and two 128-dimensional blocks, 129 adaptive reads have exact MSE 0.019704; the optimum over all 129-read fixed unit-row designs is 0.506023. Repeats, nonorthogonal and mixed-block directions are covered; 256 fixed reads match the adaptive risk. The tangent factorization proves the fixed optimum only for noise variance at most one. Achieved-MSE moment robustness is broader than the Gaussian Bayes-optimal benchmark. This is a real scoped information advantage, not a total runtime/device advantage or a general noisy-adaptive optimality theorem. The exact balanced reconstruction read-count saving approaches a factor of two, not an unbounded speedup.

The inertia classification concerns hard exact scalar-linear query counts and almost-sure zero-error evaluation. Expected exact counts are not classified. The coordinate-only vertex-cover companion keeps the original access and competes with hard-budget adaptive policies; its naive expected-budget extension fails.

The acquired 32-dimensional fixture pays 561 scalar model queries, dense input traffic, a numerical eigensolve and 17 reads per deployment. It supplies its quadratic and source-error contracts. The model-error bound is on **root MSE**, `sqrt(MSE) <= sqrt(R*)+2 tau`, not MSE plus `2 tau`. Finite stencil agreement cannot establish quadratic adequacy; the explicit invisible cubic preserves that failure. Dense measurement control, prior/noise acquisition and comparison with full-input/live-evaluator alternatives remain consequential. The Boolean grouped acquisition fixture's 1,140,791 calls versus 137 for interpolation is a failed efficiency result, retained as such.
