QUESTION CHECKED

For known f(x)=x^T A x+b^T x+c and X~N(0,I), the current construction allows
each scalar linear observation direction to depend on all earlier outcomes.
The candidate combined result is a two-round policy with at most k+1 reads
whose Bayes squared risk is no greater than every adaptive k-read policy.
Its signed-tail comparison is

  B(k)=2[sum_{j>k} lambda_j^2+sum_{j>k} mu_j^2],

where lambda_j and mu_j are decreasing positive and negative magnitudes.
A separate noisy claim optimizes over every fixed matrix with r unit rows,
including repetitions and nonorthogonal rows, for an isotropic bilinear goal.

FINDING

The matrix compression minimum underlying B(k) is a direct classical corollary
of Fan--Pall, whose primary theorem was inspected. Gaussian quadratic posterior
variance and the data-averaged fixed-design objective are explicit in recent
goal-oriented optimal experimental design work, also inspected. These ingredients
should be fully credited. The closest older multilinear-information paper remains
an unresolved contract check because its body could not be acquired. None of
the inspectable contracts below establishes the current combined k+1 domination
statement. This is a bounded finding about inspected sources, not priority
clearance or evidence that the combined result is historically new.

1. FAN--PALL: THE ENTIRE COMPRESSION MINIMUM IS CLASSICAL

Ky Fan and Gordon Pall, Imbedding Conditions for Hermitian and Normal Matrices,
Canadian Journal of Mathematics 9 (1957), 298--304.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1957-036-1
Primary publisher PDF, fully retrieved and inspected:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/AA7ACF7F360A1D44FE4B83007198F57C/S0008414X00044655a.pdf/imbedding_conditions_for_hermitian_and_normal_matrices.pdf

Theorem 1, printed p298, characterizes every possible spectrum of a Hermitian
compression. If alpha_1>=...>=alpha_n are A's eigenvalues, a dimension n-k
compression has ordered eigenvalues beta precisely when

  alpha_i >= beta_i >= alpha_{i+k},  1<=i<=n-k.

The end of Section 2, p302, explicitly retains the theorem with orthogonal
matrices for real symmetric inputs. Consequently, choose each beta_i as the
point nearest zero in its interval; these choices remain ordered. This gives

  min_{Q projection, codim(Q)=k} 2||QAQ||_F^2 = B(k).

This last display is our immediate application of their theorem. The current
positive/negative pairing constructs the minimizer explicitly. Neither the
minimum nor attainability should be presented as new spectral theory. The
paper concerns matrix compression, not Gaussian Bayes risk, adaptive transcripts,
measurement budgets or feedback rounds.

2. QUADRATIC GAUSSIAN DESIGN: THE FIXED-DESIGN RISK OBJECTIVE IS ESTABLISHED

J. Nicholas Neuberger, Alen Alexanderian, and Bart van Bloemen Waanders,
Goal oriented optimal design of infinite-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems
using quadratic approximations, arXiv:2411.07532v1 (2024).
Primary HTML inspected, especially Sections 1--4, Theorems 3.1/3.2, and Section 6:
https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07532v1
Journal record: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10915-025-03073-y

They use a known linear parameter-to-observable map, Gaussian prior and noise,
and a quadratic approximation to a scalar goal. Theorem 3.1 gives the Gaussian
quadratic variance formula; Theorem 3.2 averages posterior variance over the
prospective data. In current notation, its finite-dimensional specialization is

  R_fixed(M)=b^T C b+2 tr(A^2)-2 tr(APAP),
  C=Cov(X|MX+noise), P=I-C.

This specialization follows by setting their Hessian to 2A and prior covariance
to I. Thus the quadratic Gaussian fixed-design objective is existing machinery.
Their designed information consists of preselected sensor placements/weights;
their greedy algorithm optimizes that fixed design criterion. This is not an
outcome-dependent choice of later directions. Their theorem does not optimize
over arbitrary r unit-row directions with repetitions, or state a k+1 comparison
against unrestricted adaptive policies. No novelty is assigned here to the
posterior variance objective itself.

3. FOUCART: RECOVERY FROM A GIVEN LINEAR MAP, WITH A DIFFERENT LOSS CONTRACT

Simon Foucart, Worst-Case-Optimal Estimation of a Quadratic Form by Quadratic
Functionals of its Linear Observations. Author manuscript compiled 10 October
2026; full nine-page manuscript retrieved and relevant theorems inspected:
https://foucart.github.io/publi/OR_QuadraticQ_v6.pdf
Author publication list: https://foucart.github.io/

The manuscript starts with a fixed linear map Lambda and studies recovery of a
known quadratic quantity over an ellipsoidal model set. It gives semidefinite
constructions for local worst-case recovery and optimal quadratic global recovery.
Theorem 4 certifies optimality among all recovery maps under uniqueness of the
extremizers; Theorems 5/6 construct and certify in the ellipsoidal case. Additional
theorems allow deterministic observation errors. The optimized object is the
decoder for supplied Lambda, not an adaptive observation policy. Its loss is
worst-case absolute recovery error, rather than Gaussian average squared error.
This is a strong direct quadratic-functional recovery comparator, but its stated
contract does not imply the signed-tail adaptive-budget comparison.

The publisher metadata identifies the article as full open archive. Its PDF and
full article body remained unavailable through the attempted public routes.
Indexed publisher snippets mention adaptive information, but do not determine
its exact contract. The related Columbia dissertation is titled Worst and average
case complexity of multilinear problems and complexity of approximation with
relative error criterion; primary record https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/126629.
The dissertation body was also not acquired.

Precise unresolved questions for the actual paper, rather than presumed answers:

  (i) Is N a product of information maps N_j(f_j), where each map depends only
      on observations of its own argument? Or may information about f_j depend
      on previously observed values of the other arguments?
  (ii) Are arbitrary mixed linear functionals of the joint input allowed?
  (iii) Does the result optimize information, or only the decoder for given N?
  (iv) What cardinality allocation, stopping and error conventions are used?

These distinctions are decisive. For a bilinear target U^T V, reading U and then
the direction U/||U|| on V is legal under the current information model. A theorem
restricted to independently designed argument-wise information would have a
different comparator. Conversely, a cross-argument adaptive theorem may contain
the elementary m+1 bilinear construction or a stronger relevant result. Its
absence from the retrieved material is not evidence of absence from the paper.
The precise source should remain explicitly named as unresolved prior work.

5. CLASSICAL GAUSSIAN NO-ADAPTATION RESULTS DO NOT SETTLE THIS QUESTION

G. W. Wasilkowski and H. Wozniakowski, Can adaption help on the average?,
Numerische Mathematik 44 (1984), 169--190.
Primary author-institution abstract inspected:
https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/can-adaption-help-on-the-average/
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01410103

The abstract concerns approximation of linear solution operators under
orthogonally invariant measures, including Gaussian measures. It states
nonadaptive information can match adaptive information in that setting. The
current target is a quadratic solution functional. The linearity restriction
prevents using that theorem either to dismiss the current result or to infer
its novelty. Full body not acquired in this check.

Klaus Ritter, Average-Case Analysis of Numerical Problems (2000), Chapters VII/VIII.
Publisher previews retrieved and inspected:
https://page-one.springer.com/pdf/preview/10.1007/BFb0103941
https://page-one.springer.com/pdf/preview/10.1007/BFb0103942

statement under linearity of both the target operator and information. Chapter

optimization. The public chapter references and first two pages do not settle
quadratic functional approximation; the unseen pages are not certified empty of
relevant material.
