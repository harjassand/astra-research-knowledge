VERDICT

I independently reconstructed the central proofs and found no mathematical
counterexample or unresolved gap under the stated access/distribution model.
The signed-tail lower bound, k+1 two-round comparison, real expected-budget
interpolation, hard exact-inertia classification, and optimal fixed noisy
inner-product comparison are valid in the scopes below. This is an independent
mathematical audit, not a formal proof certificate or historical novelty review.

Two precision points sent to root were adopted before completion: the
conditional-law claim is for almost every transcript, and a finite-precision
first-vector error must be measurable from the first transcript/private seed,
before the second latent block or last noise is seen. The real-budget
construction already explicitly claimed an expected, not hard, query bound.

1. Adaptive Gaussian conditioning and stopping

Let the entire independent seed be in the initial sigma-field. Each new unit
direction u is measurable from prior observations and lies in the current
unobserved space. Conditional on that history, u is fixed. For Z~N(0,Q),

  u^T Z ~ N(0,1),
  Z-u(u^T Z) ~ N(0,Q-uu^T),

and the two are conditionally independent. This proves the induction for
predictable, possibly discontinuous measurable direction choices. Choosing a
direction as a function of already observed values adds no independent
restriction to the residual Gaussian. There is no missing Jacobian from an
adaptive global change of coordinates: the argument is conditional at each
step, not an assertion about an unconditional nonlinear transformation.

A stopping decision is measurable in the same history. Stopping does not add
information about the remaining residual. After removing redundant exact
reads, there are at most n innovations, so no unbounded optional-stopping
argument is needed. Given a terminal transcript, m=sum_t y_t u_t and Q are
fixed, and the residual law is N(0,Q), almost surely in the transcript.

Expanding f(m+Z), the centered linear term and centered quadratic Gaussian
term have zero covariance (odd Gaussian moments vanish). Consequently

  Var(f|H)=2||QAQ||_F^2+||Q(b+2Am)||^2.

This is not an unconditional assertion that the adaptive m is itself a
Gaussian vector with a deterministic covariance; the proof never requires
that false stronger assertion.

2. Signed-tail lower bound and expected budgets

For a codimension-t compression, Cauchy interlacing gives positive compressed
eigenvalues >=lambda_(t+j), j=1,...,(p-t)_+, and negative magnitudes
>=mu_(t+j), j=1,...,(q-t)_+. These two sets of retained modes are disjoint.
Squaring and summing proves, at every admissible terminal subspace,

  2||QAQ||_F^2 >= B(t).

The nonnegative linear residual cannot invalidate this lower bound. Since
the successive decreases of B are 2(lambda_(t+1)^2+mu_(t+1)^2), they are
nonincreasing; B's piecewise-linear extension is decreasing and convex.
Therefore for arbitrary adaptive stopping/randomization,

  R >= E B(K) >= B(E K) >= B(k)      when E K<=k.

The proof does not assume a fixed final subspace or an optimal policy exists.
B(k) need not be attained by an original k-read policy. It is the comparator
lower bound used by a k+1-read construction, not a claimed formula for the
same-budget adaptive optimum.

3. Paired first batch, final predictor, and the residual cross term

For a (+lambda,-mu) eigenpair, the stated orthogonal change of coordinates
has block matrix

  [[lambda-mu, sqrt(lambda*mu)],
   [sqrt(lambda*mu), 0]].

Thus reading its first coordinate removes both signed modes from the
unobserved quadratic compression, while leaving a linear coefficient on its
partner. Pairing the leading modes, followed by ordinary leading modes if
one sign is exhausted, gives 2||D||_F^2=B(k). The first batch may contain
fewer than k vectors if all relevant modes are exhausted; this only reduces
the charged count.

Conditional on the first batch, t=Q(b+2Am) is known. When t!=0, w=t/||t||
is a legal unit direction in the unobserved space. With z=w^T X=w^T Z,

  E[Z^T D Z|z]=tr D+(w^T D w)(z^2-1),

so the displayed predictor is exactly the conditional mean. Its quadratic
cross term has not been discarded. Put R=Q-ww^T. At the last transcript the
residual variance is

  2||RDR||_F^2 + 4 z^2 ||RDw||^2.

Since E[z^2|first batch]=1 and

  ||D||_F^2=(w^TDw)^2+2||RDw||^2+||RDR||_F^2,

averaging gives precisely 2||D||_F^2-2(w^TDw)^2 <= B(k). If t=0, omitting
the last observation leaves risk 2||D||_F^2. With b=0, t is supported only
on paired zero-compression directions, so Dw=0 and the achieved risk is
exactly B(k), including the t=0 case by direct interpretation.

For integer k, at most min(n,k+1) exact reads suffice, and comparison to an
expected-budget-k policy is valid because Section 2's lower bound covers
that larger comparator class. For real k, independent randomization between
the two neighboring first-batch sizes yields expected total count <=k+1
and average risk <=B(k). It does not yield a hard count bounded by a
noninteger k+1; the manuscript now states the distinction correctly.

4. Exact fixed comparator and two-read boundary

For fixed orthogonal projection P and b=0, the conditional-mean variance is
2||PAP||_F^2. The bound

  ||PAP||_F^2 <= ||PA||_F^2 = tr(P A^2)

is maximized by the r largest absolute eigenmodes, which also attain the
first inequality. Hence the fixed risk formula 2 sum_(j>r) s_j(A)^2 is
correct. For semidefinite homogeneous targets it coincides with B(k), so
the stated no-adaptivity-advantage conclusion follows.

I also independently checked the two-read example. For A^2=I and first
direction a with Rayleigh quotient rho=a^T A a, the residual gradient is
along a compression eigenvector of eigenvalue -rho and squared length
4y^2(1-rho^2). Optimizing a second direction reduces to the endpoints of
a convex quadratic: its conditional gain is

  max{2, 2 rho^2+4y^2(1-rho^2)}.

Together with first-read gain 2 rho^2, the expected total is

  2+E(4G^2-2)_+ + rho^2[2-E(4G^2-2)_+].

Since E(4G^2-2)_+>2, rho=0 is optimal. Both signs of unused unit eigenmodes
are available when m>=2, as assumed. This validates the displayed optimum
and the boundary that the compiled policy need not be optimal at its own
budget. No same-budget adaptive-optimum claim has been smuggled into (7).

5. Exact-inertia classification, including adaptive fibers

Fix a seed. Every terminal transcript freezes all its directions and values.
Every point on the resulting affine fiber follows the same transcript,
because all previous scalar outcomes and therefore all direction/stopping
decisions agree there. If f is constant on x+L, expansion in l in L gives

  l^T A l'=0 for all l,l' in L,
  l^T(2Ax+b)=0 for all l in L.

A totally A-isotropic subspace has dimension at most z+min(p,q), so at
least max(p,q) independent reads are necessary. At exactly that count, L
has maximum dimension and contains ker A; otherwise adding a missing kernel
vector would enlarge it. A nonzero nullspace component of b immediately
contradicts the second equation.

When b lies in range A, set y=x+A^dagger b/2. The second equation requires
y in L^(perp_A). Here is an elementary verification of the crucial cone
obstruction, rather than treating it as an unexplained inertia assertion.
Modulo ker A and after an invertible congruence, take A=diag(I_p,-I_q),
p>=q. A maximum isotropic L projects injectively and surjectively onto the
negative q-space: it has the form {(Tv,v):v in R^q}, where T^T T=I_q.
An A-orthogonal vector is (a,T^T a), whose quadratic value is

  ||a||^2-||T^T a||^2 >= 0.

Thus y^T A y<0 prevents ANY such terminal fiber, irrespective of how L
was chosen adaptively. The negative cone is nonempty and open when q>0.
Reverse signs for q>p. This proves the additional read for indefinite A.
In the semidefinite case, the maximum isotropic space is exactly ker A,
giving rank(A), or rank(A)+1 when b has a nullspace component. The stated
constructive upper bounds meet these lower bounds and cover A=0 as well.

Under a full-support Gaussian law, conditional residual Gaussian measures
have positive density on their affine fibers. A polynomial constant almost
everywhere there is constant everywhere there. The obstructing open cone
has positive probability, so Gaussian almost-sure exactness cannot evade
the lower bound. Conditioning on the private seed, or applying Fubini to
zero-error randomization, does not remove the obstruction. This audit
accepts the hard-count classification only, not an expected exact-count
classification, which the manuscript explicitly excludes.

6. All-noisy inner product and strongest fixed unit-direction experiment

For F=U^T V/sqrt(m), the first vector Y=U+eta has covariance (1+s)I.
Given Y, U has mean alpha Y and remains independent of (V,Z). The final
direction is now fixed, so E[V|Y,Z]=alpha Z Y/||Y||. Their conditional means
multiply, establishing the displayed predictor. Because E||Y||^2=m(1+s)
and E[Z^2|Y]=1+s, its variance is alpha^2 and Bayes risk is 1-alpha^2.
No assumption that a noisy first read equals the latent U is being made.

For ANY fixed r-row matrix M with unit row norms, including repeated and
oblique directions, H=M^T M is positive semidefinite, rank(H)<=r and
tr H=r. Gaussian conditioning gives posterior-mean covariance

  P=H(H+sI)^(-1).

Writing A=J/(2sqrt(m)), with J symmetric orthogonal, the target's explained
variance is

  2 tr(APAP)=tr(P J P J)/(2m) <= tr(P^2)/(2m).

This is genuine Frobenius Cauchy--Schwarz between P and J P J, whose norms
coincide; it does not assume that M commutes with A.

Pad the nonzero eigenvalues t_j of H to r entries. They sum to r. Direct
algebra verifies the manuscript's global tangent majorant

  g_s(1)+g_s'(1)(t-1)-g_s(t)
   = s(t-1)^2(2t+s-s^2)/[(1+s)^3(s+t)^2],
  g_s(t)=t^2/(s+t)^2.

For 0<s<=1 and every t>=0 the right side is nonnegative. Summing cancels
the tangent slopes and proves tr(P^2)<=r alpha^2. This relaxation covers
all repeated/nonorthogonal designs even though not every hypothetical
spectrum needs to be physically attainable. Orthogonal eigenvector rows
of A are feasible for r<=2m and achieve BOTH inequalities, proving the
claimed optimum, not merely a lower bound against a selected baseline.
An arbitrary estimator cannot outperform the conditional mean; randomizing
a fixed design independently of X/noise cannot improve its expected optimum.

The unit-direction constraint is essential: arbitrary scaling would change
the effective signal-to-noise ratio. Independent equal-variance Gaussian
readout errors and the Gaussian input law are essential to the optimal-fixed
claim. The sufficient range s<=1 is substantive: at s=2,m=1, repeating one
eigendirection twice gives explained variance 1/8, while the two orthogonal
directions give 1/9, exactly as stated. The two-round achieved-risk formula
continues to hold above that range; the fixed-optimum formula does not.
No comparison to the optimal noisy adaptive policy is established or claimed.

7. Further Section 5 checks and corrected finite-precision scope

For QAQ=0, after noisy first reads the observed coordinates have mean mu
and conditional covariance (1-alpha)I. The first conditional-mean variance
is 2 alpha^2||H||_F^2+alpha||b_U||^2. The final linear coefficient is
t=b_Q+2Cmu; its captured variance is alpha E||t||^2, equal to
alpha||b_Q||^2+4alpha^2||C||_F^2. Because
||A||_F^2=||H||_F^2+2||C||_F^2, subtraction from the prior variance gives
equation (15). Uncertainty in the observed first block has not been silently
discarded. Equality with full noisy orthonormal sensing is correct, while
optimal fixed sensing for unequal spectra is rightly left unclaimed.

The moment-only achieved-MSE calculation uses independent centered isotropic
blocks, not Gaussian fourth moments: E(FW)=1 and E W^2=(1+s1)(1+s2).
It is not a Bayes-optimality or optimal-fixed statement outside Gaussianity.

For the first-vector precision bound, e must be determined by Y and
independent implementation randomness before V and the last noise. Under
this condition, E(e^T V)^2=E||e||^2 and the coupled-noise calculation gives
the claimed sqrt(1+s2)*tau_Y bound. An arbitrary error merely satisfying an
L2 bound, but allowed to depend on V, would not justify that step. Root
made the necessary measurability condition explicit. The bounded final-read
error term follows directly from Cauchy--Schwarz and Minkowski.

AUDIT LIMITS

This review does not certify finite precision eigenvalue signs, the external
quadratic/Gaussian/channel promises, physical availability or cost of dense
linear measurements, numerical implementation correctness, or acquisition
and deployment claims in later sections. It does not resolve historical
priority. The accepted result is the explicit mathematical capability under
the declared interfaces, with the exact/noisy and hard/expected distinctions
preserved. No additional broad experiments were run for this audit.
