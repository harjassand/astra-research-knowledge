P1 CONTINUATION — TWO-ROUND COORDINATE ACQUISITION THROUGH INTERACTION COVERS
Independent construction and audit by adaptive_structure. 2026-10-30.
This is a companion result, not a historical priority claim.

1. Capability and exact claim

Let X_1,...,X_n be independent random variables, and let F(X) be a known
square-integrable target with pairwise interaction representation

  F(X) = c + sum_i u_i(X_i) + sum_{ij in E} v_ij(X_i,X_j).

The displayed factors are square-integrable under the product priors; the
factors and prior distributions are resources. A measurement reveals one
coordinate exactly. Measurements cost one; a fully adaptive policy may choose
the next coordinate after seeing earlier coordinates, use independent private
randomness, and stop early. Repeated exact reads can be deleted. Risk means
Bayes squared prediction risk E[(F-E[F|transcript])^2].

Fix an integer k >= 0 and ANY vertex cover H of the supplied interaction
graph G=([n],E). The following explicit two-round policy has risk no greater
than the infimum risk of every fully adaptive policy with at most k reads:

  Round 1: read every coordinate in H.
  Round 2: condition on those values and read the k remaining coordinates
           with the largest conditional additive variance contributions.
           Read all remaining coordinates if fewer than k remain.

Its total number of reads is at most min(n, |H|+k). No fourth moment, bounded
support, tail regularity, or sign symmetry assumption is required. For a
minimum vertex cover of size tau(G), the measurement overhead is tau(G).
A standard maximal-matching construction gives a cover of size <= 2 tau(G)
in linear graph time; on a bipartite graph an ordinary minimum vertex cover
algorithm can obtain the exact tau(G). Computing H is charged below.

This result converts a potentially long sequence of measurement/decision
cycles into two batches. It is a graph-structured resource augmentation
theorem, not an equal-budget adaptivity-gap theorem or a new graph algorithm.
It remains useful with arbitrarily rare or heavy-tailed coordinates.

2. Full proof

Posterior product fact. Conditional on any legal exact-query transcript, the
unread coordinates retain their independent prior laws. For a fixed private
seed, the transcript imposes restrictions only on the values it actually
reads. This fact also holds after averaging the seed. An equivalent formal
construction includes the entire independent seed in the initial sigma-field.

Fix a first-round realization X_H=h. Since H meets every edge, the conditional
target has the form

  F_h(X_R) = C(h) + sum_{i in R} g_i(X_i;h),    R=[n]\H.

There are no factors involving two coordinates in R. Define

  mu_i(h) = E[g_i(X_i;h)],
  v_i(h) = Var(g_i(X_i;h)).

Independence implies that after ANY further legal transcript with queried
subset S contained in R, its conditional residual variance is exactly

  sum_{i in R\S} v_i(h).

Crucially, the numbers v_i(h) do not depend on outcomes from R. Every policy
that reads at most k of R therefore leaves, pathwise, at least the sum of all
but the k largest v_i(h). Reading those k indices in a single batch attains
this lower bound. Thus adaptive choices among the remaining coordinates have
no advantage, even if they exploit additional private randomness or stop.

Now take an arbitrary original hard-k policy pi. After revealing X_H for
free, emulate pi on the same latent sample and seed. When pi requests a
coordinate of H, supply its already known value internally; when it requests
a coordinate of R, read that coordinate. Ignore free H values in pi's choice
rule until pi itself would have requested them. This emulator makes at most
k real reads in R. Its final information contains pi's original transcript
and X_H, so its Bayes risk is at most R(pi), by conditional expectation as
orthogonal projection. Conditional on each h, the second-round rule above
has no larger risk than the emulator. Integrating over h proves

  R(two_round_H,k) <= R(pi).

The constructed rule does not depend on pi. Taking an infimum over pi gives
the claim without assuming that an optimal adaptive policy exists. Zero risk,
k=0, empty H, isolated coordinates, and k >= n-|H| cause no exception.

The argument needs the hard bound on the number of original reads. An
unconditional expected-budget constraint is treated separately in Section 6.

3. Paid algorithm and representation

For a centered, variance-one independent-input quadratic model

  F = c + sum_i b_i X_i + sum_{i<j} a_ij X_i X_j,

the graph contains the nonzero a_ij. Reading an explicit sparse coefficient
table and constructing the graph costs O(n+|E|) arithmetic/storage operations.
A maximal matching costs O(n+|E|); taking its endpoints produces a vertex
cover H, because otherwise an edge with two unmatched endpoints could be
added. Any vertex cover meets every matching edge, hence |H|=2|M|<=2 tau(G).
This is established ordinary machinery, included here to make the rule
constructible rather than assume an optimal cover oracle.

After observing h, for each remaining i compute

  beta_i(h) = b_i + sum_{j in H} a_ij h_j.

All remaining interactions vanish, so v_i(h)=beta_i(h)^2. A scan of the sparse
coefficient table performs all updates in O(n+|E|). Select the k largest
scores in O(n) comparison time, or sort in O(n log n). The final Bayes mean
adds the observed terms and the conditional means of unobserved terms.
One further sparse scan suffices. The measurements themselves, all prior
calibration, and every model evaluation remain costs; none are supplied by
the theorem for free. With non-unit coordinate variances, multiply beta_i^2
by Var(X_i), or standardize once using known means and variances.

For general finite-state pairwise tables, compute g_i(x_i;h) by summing its
unary table and incident factors whose other endpoint is in H. Its mean and
variance then require an ordinary sum over the prior support of X_i. Charge
the table entries read and these support sums. For arbitrary analytic factors,
the ability to calculate these one-dimensional integrals is an additional
resource; the theorem alone supplies no integration algorithm.

Optional exact acquisition of an unknown Boolean quadratic model.
If an exact scalar value oracle for a degree-at-most-two multilinear F on
{0,1}^n is available, its interaction cover can be learned without reading an
entire n by n coefficient matrix. This is a separate intervention resource.

First obtain c=F(0) and b_i=F(e_i)-F(0), costing n+1 calls. For S subset [n],

  q(S)=F(1_S)-c-sum_{i in S} b_i = sum_{ij subset S} a_ij.

On the current unmatched vertices R, choose each vertex independently with
probability 1/2 to produce S. If q(S)!=0, there is an edge in S. A nonzero
multilinear Boolean polynomial of degree at most two is nonzero on at least
one quarter of the cube. A short proof is induction on degree: a nonzero
coefficient of a selected variable gives a nonzero derivative of one lower
degree, and for every assignment with nonzero derivative at least one of
the variable's two settings has nonzero value. Thus t independent zero tests
miss an existing edge with probability at most (3/4)^t, even for signed
coefficients and exact cancellations.

From q(S)!=0, bisect S. If one half has nonzero q, recurse there; otherwise
the cross sum between the halves is nonzero. Bisect the two cross sides in
turn, keeping a nonzero cross sum, until an edge is isolated. This requires
O(log n) additional q queries. Add the edge to a matching and remove both
endpoints. Stop after t consecutive zero tests at a stage. Use

  t = ceil(log((floor(n/2)+1)/delta) / log(4/3)).

A union bound over at most floor(n/2)+1 stages shows that with probability
at least 1-delta the final matching is maximal and its endpoints form a
vertex cover. If the actual minimum cover size is tau, there are at most tau
successful stages. Scalar model-call cost is

  O(n + (tau+1) log(n/delta) + tau log n).

Recover all coefficients touching H by exact two-coordinate finite
differences, requiring at most |H| n calls. Remaining interaction coefficients
are zero on the successful certification event. Thus an explicit model usable
for repeated acquisition is earned in O(n+|H|n+tau log(n/delta)) exact calls,
not assumed. Each call still submits an n-coordinate input and pays the
oracle's full execution cost. This is an elementary additive-query graph
construction; no improvement over the graph-learning literature is claimed.
It relies on exact answers/zero comparisons and a known degree-two promise.
No robustness to noisy value oracles is asserted. The model is correct with
probability at least 1-delta over reconstruction randomness; the acquisition
risk theorem then applies on that event. This is not by itself an unconditional
relative-risk guarantee after averaging over a potentially erroneous learned
model. The explicit known-model theorem has no such model-learning failure.

4. The rare-gate obstruction is repaired at linear cost

Let m>=2, p=1/m, L=2m-1. Independent B_i~Bernoulli(p) and uniform signs Y_ij
define the explicitly represented quadratic target

  F = L^(-1/2) sum_{i=1}^m B_i sum_{j=1}^L Y_ij.

There are n=m(L+1)=2m^2 independent coordinates and mL interaction terms.
Var(F)=1. The interaction graph is m disjoint stars, and H={all B_i} is a
minimum vertex cover of size m. Its construction is immediate from the
explicit graph; a generic matching-endpoint cover would use up to 2m instead.

Compare with EVERY fully adaptive policy capped at k=2m original reads.
The two-round rule first reads all m gates and then up to 2m leaves belonging
to active gates. Conditional on A=sum_i B_i, every active leaf contributes
variance 1/L and every inactive leaf contributes zero. Its exact risk is

  R_2 = E[(A L-2m)_+]/L
      = (1-1/m)^m - P(A>=2)/(2m-1),        A~Binomial(m,1/m).

Hence R_2 -> exp(-1). The theorem proves R_2 <= R_ad,opt(2m), even without
solving that adaptive optimum. The total read count is at most 3m, often
less because inactive leaves need not be measured. This is an upper bound
on the hard query count, not a claimed optimum two-round algorithm.

In contrast, any FIXED design with r measurements removes at most

  V_fixed(r) <= r/(mL)

of the unit prior variance. To see this exactly, let g_i in {0,1} indicate
whether its gate is included and let ell_i be the number of its leaves. Its
explained variance is

  (1/L) sum_i ell_i [g_i p + (1-g_i) p^2] <= p r/L.

At r=3m the fixed-design residual risk tends to one; the two-round risk tends
to exp(-1). Matching THIS ACHIEVED TWO-ROUND RISK within any fixed constant
relative inflation 1+epsilon with (1+epsilon)/e<1 requires Omega(m^2) fixed
reads, compared with 3m two-round reads. For comparison to the original
hard-2m adaptive optimum instead, the previous explicit sequential policy
below yields the same Omega(m^2) necessity for every fixed epsilon<1.
A fixed randomized design cannot improve this expectation bound.
This is an absolute-risk separation, not a gain ratio hiding vanishing total
benefit. It uses rare priors and an explicit O(m^2)-term input representation.
The separation concerns latent-coordinate measurements and feedback rounds;
one-time input reading/model construction can already cost Theta(m^2), and
no end-to-end computational speedup for a single instance is claimed.

For context, the previous sequential policy 'scan gates until the first
success, then spend the rest of 2m reads on that bank' has risk
(m-1)/(2m-1), tending to 1/2. It was not proved optimal. Our comparison to
the fully adaptive optimum follows from the theorem, not that policy.

5. A partial-cover extension when most inputs concentrate

The complete vertex-cover result can be excessive when the graph is dense
but its remaining interactions use well-behaved inputs. A clean intermediate
certificate is available for a centered, variance-one bilinear quadratic.
Mark as 'exceptional' coordinates whose standardized laws are not certified
to be uniform signs. Let H cover every interaction edge having an exceptional
endpoint. After observing H, every exceptional coordinate that remains is
isolated in the residual graph. The nontrivial residual interactions are all
between independent uniform signs.

For exact coordinate queries, the event A_i that coordinate i is ever queried
does not depend on X_i: varying its value cannot change the decision to query
it before it has been seen. If G_i=X_i g_i(X_-i) is the full fluctuation, then

  E[1_Ai G_i^2] = E[1_Ai g_i^2],

because E X_i^2=1 and A_i,g_i are measurable without X_i. Thus the own-tail
factor in E G_i^4 is unnecessary in the quadratic selection argument.
After conditioning on H, g_i is constant for an isolated exceptional i; for
every other i it is affine in independent uniform signs. Consequently

  P(g_i^2 >= u I_i) <= 2 exp(-u/2),    I_i=E g_i^2,
  E[1_A g_i^2] <= 2 I_i delta log(2e/delta),  P(A)=delta.

The sharp top-influence proof from the previous P1 report uses only this
event-energy bound, the posterior product fact, and the quadratic residual
identity. It therefore applies unchanged to this residual model, including
the arbitrary finite-variance isolated coordinates. A second batch of

  min(n-|H|, ceil((16 k/epsilon) log(32e/epsilon)))

largest CONDITIONAL influences attains risk <=(1+epsilon) R_ad,opt(k), for
0<epsilon<=1, after the first |H| reads. Use the hard-k free-H emulator and
condition pointwise on h before integrating. Conditional influences and
constant terms must be recomputed after H; initial kurtosis thresholds are
not a valid substitute for this structural certificate.

A coordinate with an arbitrary tail can remain unread until the second
batch, provided its remaining interactions have been removed. Thus H can
consist of a few ordinary hubs rather than every exceptional leaf. This is
the distinction from merely revealing every rare coordinate. The guarantee
is conservative: the cover is sufficient, not proved necessary.

6. Expected budgets: the cheap hard-k rule does not automatically transfer

Take one B~Bernoulli(1/m), m independent signs Y_j, and

  F=B m^(-1/2) sum_j Y_j.

An expected-budget-2 adaptive policy reads B and, only when B=1, reads every
leaf. It has zero risk. A rule that reveals B and then caps its second batch
at 2 leaves has strictly positive risk for m>2. Conditioning on B has changed
the comparator's conditional expected query count from 2 to m on the rare
event. This is why simply replacing a hard budget by its unconditional mean
inside the theorem is invalid.

An exact expected-budget variant EXISTS with an extra computation resource.
After H, let v_i(h) be the additive variance contributions as before. The
optimization over second-round selection probabilities z_i(h) in [0,1] is

  maximize E_h sum_i v_i(h) z_i(h),
  subject to E_h sum_i z_i(h) <= k.

Every fully adaptive free-H emulator induces such marginals and has exactly
this expected gain, because remaining contributions are additive. Conversely
these marginals can be implemented by one randomized batch. The linear
optimization is solved by a common cutoff lambda: select all pairs (h,i)
with v_i(h)>lambda, reject those below it, and randomize suitably on a tie.
This cutoff rule matches or beats every original expected-budget-k policy
using expected total reads <=|H|+k. It may read many more than k coordinates
on particular outcomes, as the example requires.

Unlike the hard-k rule, finding lambda requires the distribution of all
conditional variances v_i(X_H), or an integration/sampling procedure to
calibrate its expected count. Finite support enumeration can be exponential
in |H|. The implementation checks the counterexample, but does not implement
general cutoff calibration. No general cheap cutoff calibration or robust
sample-based relative-risk theorem is claimed. The principal paid algorithm
above is the hard-budget algorithm.

7. Relation to strongest established alternatives and limitations

Gupta, Nagarajan, Singla, 'Adaptivity Gaps for Stochastic Probing: Submodular
and XOS Functions', arXiv:1608.00673, Theorems 1.1-1.4, studies independent
element activation with submodular/XOS reward and prefix-closed probing
constraints. It proves constant submodular and logarithmic-width XOS gaps.
Their acquisition semantics and objective differ. Our quadratic Bayes gain
need not be submodular: for F=X_1 X_2, observing either coordinate alone has
zero gain while observing the pair has gain one. We do not claim to improve
those algorithms on their problem classes.
Primary URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00673

p1_research/source_notes/stochastic_probing.txt.

Ghuge, Gupta, Nagarajan, 'The Power of Adaptivity for Stochastic Submodular
Cover', PMLR 139 (ICML 2021), studies approximation relative to fully adaptive
solutions using only a few rounds, for independent and correlated stochastic
items. Their primary abstract explicitly describes nearly tight round/quality
tradeoffs. This is the correct established rounds-of-feedback comparison,
but its stochastic submodular cover objective is different. The paper's
abstract was read; a second attempt to fetch its PDF failed, so no precise
theorem constants or direct nonimplication beyond differing models are asserted.
Primary URL: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/ghuge21a.html

Segev and Singla, 'Efficient Approximation Schemes for Stochastic Probing and
Selection-Stopping Problems', arXiv:2007.13121 (current HTML), introduction
and Theorems 1.1-1.3, give EPTAS results for free-order prophets and both
adaptive/nonadaptive ProbeMax. They credit Fu et al. for a prior adaptive
ProbeMax PTAS using a constant number of adaptive rounds. ProbeMax maximizes
the expected largest independently revealed nonnegative value, not posterior
MSE of a coupled target. This is a stronger computational baseline on its own
class than a claim that 'few-round policies are new'.

Adaptive sparse-recovery papers were located (including Price/Woodruff's
limited-adaptivity work and arXiv:1205.3518), but the complete relevant primary
theorem text was not reliably retained/read. No precise comparison to their
measurement bounds is promoted. Their ability to choose general linear
measurements is a substantive additional resource relative to coordinate
queries. Root's separate Gaussian linear-measurement theorem handles that
different access model; this companion result does not establish it.

The new synthesis proposed here is: locate a small set of measurements whose
values remove the target's interactions, pay for those measurements, and then
use a conditionally additive exact design. Conditional independence, graph
vertex cover, selection by variances, and exact finite differences are all
ordinary machinery. A focused literature search did not locate this precise
Bayes-risk statement, but it was not an exhaustive novelty review. This is a
sound structural mechanism with a constant-risk rare-event consequence, not
evidence of a general learning or discovery advantage.

8. Executable evidence

feedback_checks.py uses exact fractions. It compares the two-round policy
against ordinary optimal adaptive Bellman recursion on independent finite
variables and general unary/pairwise factors, checks the rare family against
an exact nonadaptive allocation dynamic program, and validates the Boolean
value-oracle cover construction on signed graphs. These checks complement
the proofs. They are finite diagnostics, not a performance benchmark or a
formal proof assistant certificate. Query counts include every model-oracle
call in the reconstruction diagnostic.
