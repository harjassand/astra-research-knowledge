**PASS within the stated distinction of tasks.** I read the complete source `ANGULAR_OPTIMIZATION_BOUNDARY_CANDIDATE.md` and verified SHA-256 `9a93c71175bb297838f5550470c44398b1bf37afedc752ce9b29f8663fe06296`. Original unchanged. No parser or approximation-hardness theorem is inferred.

For U_G=sum z_i^4+2 sum_edges z_i^2 z_j^2, the substitution w_i=z_i^2 maps the Euclidean unit sphere onto the whole probability simplex. For an edge i,j in the positive support, fixing w_i+w_j makes their combined self/edge term constant, while all other incident terms are affine in w_i. At least one endpoint of this interval has no greater objective and removes a support vertex. Finitely repeating leaves an independent support. Cauchy–Schwarz then bounds sum w_i^2 by 1/|S|>=1/alpha(G), and uniform weights on a maximum independent set attain equality. Thus a_*=1/alpha(G), with the direction a_*<=1/k exactly equivalent to alpha(G)>=k for a positive integer k. This is the classical Motzkin–Straus complement mechanism, not a new graph-hardness result. The elementary proof here is sufficient independently of the citation; my DOI opening returned a tool internal error, so I do not claim a fresh full read of the 1965 article in this audit.

The cheap bound U_G>=sum z_i^4>=||z||^4/m is exact and adequate. At m>=2 the full homogeneous Gibbs tail condition 4m>p=4 holds. For unit parameters the reviewed radial capacity theorem gives factor 157/125 from this weaker coercivity, without an optimizer or Z_U. The parallel-channel theorem, separately received, may improve that factor without solving angular optimization either. Graph-dependent Z_U cancels from capacity/mass normalization; it has not been computed or proved easy to compute.

The rate-task complexity claim is read in the actual established numerical sense: polynomial input handling for the raw aligned sparse quartic and polynomial cost in m and inverse requested relative quadrature tolerance, with paid exact bit arithmetic and explicit refusal budgets. It is not a new polylog(1/tolerance) integration theorem or completeness claim for every finite resource limit. The target conductance can be tiny while scalar relative integration remains admitted; this does not establish arbitrary kinetic/sampling tractability.

Independent exact tests in the companion directory `fiber_stirring_capacity_20261031/reviewer_audit/check_stirring_and_graph.py` exhaust all 1,098 simple graphs with 2 through 5 vertices. For each graph they verify the maximum-independent-set witness value, perform rational positive-support compression, check nonincrease at every elimination, and verify the final bound. Together with three stirring identities the script reports 4,703 exact checks. These tests are not an NP-hardness proof; the explicit polynomial reduction is the proof, and maximum-independent-set hardness is standard prior knowledge.

The useful conclusion is task selection: demanding the optimal Euclidean coercivity would insert an unnecessary exact graph-optimization problem into this particular rate certificate. It is not a lower bound on competing rate algorithms, on approximate coercivity at a fixed factor, on partition functions, or on simulation. It does not imply that this implementation beats equally informed ordinary scalar/variational methods, and no historical novelty or practical advantage is accepted.
