A sharp law for the cost of unbiased distributed inversion

I preserved the earlier extraction and endpoint constructions, including their limits, and selected a different foundational question:

When can a distributed system compute an inverse field exactly in expectation, with both finite expected communication depth and finite output variance, without a spectral gap?

The result is a matching resource law for every local algorithm, including adaptive and nonlinear algorithms. It identifies a functional of the source that controls the best possible tradeoff between communication and second moment. I constructed an attaining procedure, removed its apparent dependence on an unknown probability normalizer, and implemented a finite-bit local version.

The strongest conventional comparator still wins the finite numerical example. The substantive result is the resource law and its necessity proof—not a demonstrated numerical speedup.

Executed checks

The implementation was checked through exact branch enumeration, not only sample averages. A distributed two-node example with different row denominators has 102 rounding branches; their probabilities sum to exactly one, and their exact conditional mean equals the importance-weighted polynomial target.

On a 17-node rational path, flipping the distant endpoint sign preserves outputs, received transcripts and private-bit consumption outside the reachable radius. The actual inverse changes there, as required by the lower-bound mechanism. A longer run responds once the sign becomes reachable.

Completed scope and preserved work

The main mathematical question is resolved in its stated communication model. The proof identifies the resource, constructs the operation when it is finite, and excludes every local workaround when it is infinite.

Its premises remain explicit: exact digital inputs, a source-class promise, known dimension metadata, local communication and input-independent public randomness. The protocol does not acquire those premises from a few successful observations. Fresh independent randomness is needed for inputs selected adaptively between calls; a revealed sampled polynomial cannot simply be reused against forcing chosen after seeing it.
