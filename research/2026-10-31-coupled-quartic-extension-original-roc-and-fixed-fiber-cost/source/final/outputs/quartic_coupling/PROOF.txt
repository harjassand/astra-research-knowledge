4. THE ORIGINAL NORMAL COUPLING IS A SMALL CONVEX PERTURBATION
   IN A DILATED METRIC

          ||R_a''(S,y)|| <= C_R |(S,y)|^2/sqrt(a).         (7)

The function |(S,y)|^4 has Hessian at least 4|(S,y)|^2 I. By (2),

           Q0(B)=R(B)+(delta/2)(|sym B|^2+a z^2)^2         (8)

                 F_a=H1(sym B)+Q,
                 Q=Q0+(delta/2)N_a.                     (9)

This step retains every coefficient of R. It handles its mixed signs,
its linear-normal part, and its dependence on a nonzero normal mean.
No assertion that R or the added penalty has nonnegative Jensen gap

         <J_r(A+G)> >= J_r(A), whenever U(G)=0,            (17)

for EVERY full affine mean A and EVERY actual periodic G in L4.
In particular U(A) need not vanish. A general-U failure of J_r in the
admitted r range must use a nonconstant normal component of the
perturbation; changing the affine background alone cannot evade (17).

Moreover (17) is strict for every nonzero G. To see the required
strictness of the P_r heat proof, suppose its twelve reduced features
vanish on a smooth periodic Hessian S. Their p h2,q h2,w h2 and
p h3,q h3,w h3 components force h2=h3=0, by continuity (a nonzero jet

constant and h0=0 as well. The Hessian is constant. The positive

for P_r over all means and fields; such a modulus is not inferred.

10. AN ATTAINED NONLOCAL NORMAL COST ON A FIXED HESSIAN FIBER

The coupling can also be measured without asserting that removing it
raises energy. Fix a smooth actual field S=M+Hess psi, with M symmetric,
and assume w(x)=|S(x)|^2 is bounded away from zero. Fix its Hessian Hodge
component. For a smooth mean-zero scalar z the ONLY normal Hodge
corrector on this fiber is

             E_z=-J Hess Delta^(-1)z,                    (18)

an actual gradient with curl z and zero Hessian Hodge component. Use
the actual family B_(a,z)=S+E_z/a in the coupled quartic (6). Exact
expansion, for each fixed z, yields

 lim_(a->infinity) a[<F_a(B_(a,z))>-<H(S)>]
             =<DF0(S):E_z>+2delta<w z^2>.                (19)

Its nonlocal forcing is

 K_S=Delta^(-1) div div[J DF0(S)],
                  <DF0(S):E_z>=<K_S z>.                 (20)

The Fourier multiplier in (20) is self-adjoint and the zero mode is
removed. An arbitrary added constant in K_S has no effect. The
forcing includes the actual Hodge response of the symmetric part;
it is not just the pointwise derivative in the normal variable.

The infimum of the coefficient (19) over ALL smooth mean-zero z is
ATTAINED. Define

 ell=<K_S/w>/<1/w>,
 z*=-(K_S-ell)/(4delta w).

This is smooth and mean zero, and (18) is its genuine compatible
attaining corrector. Completing the scalar square gives the exact cost

       inf_z (19) =-<(K_S-ell)^2/w>/(8delta).             (21)

It is strictly negative exactly when K_S is nonconstant. Thus the
normal interaction generally LOWERS energy on a fixed Hessian fiber.
The full coupled QC theorem controls the resulting total gap; it does
not assume this interaction has a favorable sign.

This is an attained infimum of FIXED-CORRECTOR limits. It is not an
interchange of an infimum with a limit over unbounded a-dependent
correctors, and no attainment assertion is made here if w vanishes.

There is a particularly transparent exact control for every r>0. Use
S=[[cos x,1],[1,0]], which has w=2+cos^2 x. For the ORIGINAL F0 in
Section 1, (20) can be represented, up to a constant, by

 k0=-(9r^2+16r+6)/[2r(r+1)^2].

Since <1/(2+cos^2 x)>=1/sqrt(6), formula (21) is

                 -k2^2(5/2-sqrt(6))/(8delta)<0.          (22)

The exact minimizer is
z*=-(k2/(4delta))[1-sqrt(6)/(2+cos^2 x)]. The reciprocal integral
follows directly by the substitution tan x on a quarter period.
Even the finite Fourier trial z=-(k2/(20delta))cos 2x has coefficient
