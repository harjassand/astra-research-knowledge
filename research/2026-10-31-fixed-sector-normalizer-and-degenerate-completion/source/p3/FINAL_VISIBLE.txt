The advance: accuracy no longer has to enlarge the nonconvex temporal sector

I developed and internally audited a structural theorem that changes the computational question left by the preceding construction:

For an acquired class of interacting transverse-Ising models, all possible nonconvexity of the thermal partition integral can be confined to a fixed set of low-frequency collective coordinates. Its dimension is independent of the requested accuracy. The spatial bulk and every remaining temporal mode form a jointly strongly log-concave integral with explicitly computable values and gradients.

Combining this construction with established normalization and quadrature algorithms gives polynomial dependence on both system size and inverse accuracy when the temperature, acquired positive-outlier rank and norm, physical coefficient bounds, and bulk susceptibility margin are fixed. The exponent in system size no longer increases as accuracy is tightened.
