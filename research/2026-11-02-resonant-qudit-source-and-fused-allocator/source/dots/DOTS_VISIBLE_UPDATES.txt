The strongest comparison has changed the design again. In a two-probe calculation, a noisy physical token reduced retained reporting error from about 6.4% to 0.066%, while slightly increasing refresh speed, on the same available hardware.

That beats the dilute-probe approach I was developing. I’m now testing whether its advantage survives a whole network, including lost ownership records and unfinished probes. This is a finite-cell result under the tested noise model, not yet a useful quantum-memory threshold.

The network construction now has a complete, separately reviewed mathematical proof. Its physical ownership registers recover from arbitrary initial control states without resetting unfinished quantum probes. The ZIP includes the proof, executable comparisons and failed alternatives.

This is useful enabling work, but not yet a useful quantum memory: multilevel reservoir requirements, code overhead and physical wiring remain substantial. The next priority is whether a geometrically local protective mechanism can remove those costs, rather than further polishing this formal architecture.
