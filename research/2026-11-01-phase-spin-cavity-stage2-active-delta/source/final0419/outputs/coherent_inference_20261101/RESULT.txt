What was actually derived and constructed

For initially empty receiver modes, exchange
 H=H_E+sum_i (a_i^dag b_i+b_i^dag a_i) tensor G_i(t)
can have arbitrarily correlated controllers, source backaction and arbitrarily
fast noncommuting H_E. If integral g_i=pi/2 and
 A_i=integral ||G_i-g_i I||<pi/2, then its observed count generating functions
are bounded by those of loss with transmission lambda_i=cos^2 A_i. In
particular, with collection eta and fixed m input photons,

 P(no photon detected) <=[1-eta cos^2 A]^m.

The constants are sharp, achieved by scalar gain error. A positive relative
gain interval 1+-delta gives A<=pi delta/2. Multiple simultaneous reads obey
product bounds for selected missed photons even when their gain operators
share a source and do not commute. The proof turns exact source propagators
into Bernstein finite differences; the Bernstein inequality is established
prior. It does not approximate the controller by a classical random number.

I built a full destructive dual-rail measurement instrument, carried a reused
quantum source through 34,992 encoded measurement histories, and checked the
source-retaining Choi matrix including an arbitrary reference. Then I derived
conditional joint error bounds from actual local thermal GKSL dynamics and
an exact charge-block Poisson representation. The parallel implementation can
have three-local interactions and bounded degree, with unreset correlated
controller spins. The observation duration stays fixed as encoding grows.

At delta=.1, collection .95 and the explicitly supplied thermal/noise rates,
the standard concatenated Steane decoder gives each logical flag/wrong bound
below 9.1e-14 at level four. Outward 80-digit arithmetic checks these formulas.
This uses 2401 encoded data dual-rail qubits, 12,005 total two-level sites
including controllers, 4802 fresh thermal receiver preparations and 4802
detector ports. The encoder, native Hamiltonian, baths, clock and detectors
are assumptions. Those numbers are model bounds, not measured device errors.

Why this is not promoted

Aliferis-Gottesman-Preskill already permits arbitrary shared quantum bath
memory and unrestricted bath dynamics. Its ordinary fault-set amplitude
bound, applied to the SAME virtual photons, measurement, baths and code,
gives missing probability A^2 instead of sin^2 A. At delta=.1 these are
.0246740110 and .0244717419. Both versions first meet total error <1e-8 at
the SAME level four and with the SAME hardware count. The exact constant
improves a bound; the comparison does not establish a new capability regime.
Existing dual-rail experiments are also much stronger practical incumbents
than an invented iid-fault baseline. See SOURCES_AND_COMPARISON.txt.

I also tried a full-quantum rescue: with exactly matched rail-source coupling,
a rail-blind occupation flag gives faithful heralded transfer for data initially
independent of the controller. Pre-existing correlations can steer a fine
conditional output; repeated trials with a coarse flag have a uniform vanishing
failure-effect bound instead. This distinction has an explicit countercontrol. A
Feynman edge carrying CNOT factorizes exactly into that operation times a
source operator. The full operator controls pass. But conclusive dual-rail
transfer is established prior, the flag must be physically supplied, and the
gate edge already hard-codes the desired CNOT with a four-local source term.
It does not improve the earlier allowed gate construction. Mismatched rails
give an explicit conditional Bell-fidelity counterexample near .813.

Optical correction and physical continuation

The supplied total-photon Gaussian comparator is valid: the specified cutoff
needs exactly 15,832 qubits, below the 32,768 total cap. Its 3000-block summed

intervals. It is a mathematical compressor, not an acquired cheap device.
The layered tree-precision correction is also accepted. Earlier files are
preserved; no claim of separation from Gaussian memories follows from a
one-qubit-per-mode slogan.

I removed both the artificial source-information cap and the fixed thermal
source restriction for the ordinary parallel classical competitor. The
standard multimode minimum-output-entropy theorem gives the energy-only
converse D<=eta*ln(1+1/b)*E, for arbitrary classical retained records and
arbitrary jointly prepared transmitted states. It yields a finite asymmetric
testing bound, without importing He's different interpolation/SDPI theorem.
This is established quantum-illumination theory, credited as such.

The continued source/capture/phase/spin-cavity calculations include finite
source release, storage decay, cold ports, phase-pump expenditure, saturation,
collection and actual conditional read noise. A separate Casimir bound excludes
thermal concentration by arbitrary returning controllers ONLY under the stated
collective symmetry. A constructed pure-controller counterexample shows that
number conservation alone is insufficient. These are retained model results.
The illustrative optical point requires write cooperativity 2500, 1024 capture
ports, thousands of spins and qualified detector/control conditions. It is
floating evaluation of conditional formulas, not a real operating region or
a demonstrated useful-dose improvement over the strongest quantum receiver.

Finally, I derived and tested direct three-level Lambda readout in the full
one-excitation manifold, including e->s branching emission. No Raman elimination
or substitution of independent effective loss is used. Collective bright-position
charge has noise drift at most (Gamma_es+2Gamma_phi)/ell. Higher excitation
sectors cost their actual per-block probability, <=ell(ell-1)p^2/2, and thermal
upward events are also charged per block. This repairs one physical reasoning
gap but leaves multiple-excitation scaling, capture and the Gaussian-memory
comparison unresolved. It is not another selected device certificate.
