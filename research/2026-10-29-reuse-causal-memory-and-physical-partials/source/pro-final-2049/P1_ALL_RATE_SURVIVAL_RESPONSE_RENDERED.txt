## 2. Ordinary primal and dual derivation

Define

\[
 T_s(w)=\sup_{f\in H_0^1(O)}
 \left(2\int fw-\int w|\nabla f|^2-s\int f^2w\right).
\]

Taking f=c v and optimizing c gives T_s>=L^2/(E+sV). Replacing f by |f|
preserves its energy and increases its positive load, so the supremum may
be restricted to nonnegative f. For such f, integral f w <= integral f u
= integral j dot grad f. Splitting the dominating load with a in [0,1]
and completing the gradient and reaction squares gives

\[
 T_s\le a^2R+(1-a)^2Q/s.
\]

For s>0 choose a=Q/(Q+sR), giving T_s<=RQ/(Q+sR). At zero the gradient
argument directly gives T_0<=R. The upper expression increases with R,Q,
so upper enclosures preserve it.

For the optional dual, the exact identity
integral f w = a integral j dot grad f + integral f(w-a u) holds for all
real a. Completing squares gives

\[
 T_s\le a^2R+(M-2aU+a^2Q)/s.
\]

Choose a=U/(Q+sR), obtaining [M-U^2/(Q+sR)]/s. The residual w-a u may
change sign; this identity needs no positivity assumption on it.
Normalize before enclosing: [1-U^2/(M(Q+sR))]/s increases with M,Q,R
and decreases with U. Thus this optional bound uses M_hi, while the basic
RQ/[M(Q+sR)] bound uses M_lo. Exact U^2<=M_hi Q_hi is checked.

The root's source comparison supplies

\[
 e^{-2\Delta}\frac{T_s(w)}M
 \le\Phi(s)\le\kappa e^{2\Delta}\frac{T_{\kappa s}(w)}M.
\]

Substitution yields the released curves. The pathwise integral bound
integral_0^tau exp(-st)dt<=1/s supplies the universal clamp. No estimated
global slow gap or rare-event lifetime enters a perturbation denominator.

The unclamped ratio is

\[

\]

Its derivative has constant sign, so its supremum is the larger of the
zero and infinite endpoints. Exact rational evaluation gives upper

all s>=0.

More generally, the same calculation turns two static witness defects

\[
 \frac{e2^2 M_{hi}}{M_{lo}L_{lo}^2}

The code also proves the universal-clamped factor. If B>C, r(s) and 1/s
