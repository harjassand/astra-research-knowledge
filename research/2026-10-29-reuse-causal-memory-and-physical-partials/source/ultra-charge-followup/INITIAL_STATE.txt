ACQUIRING THE REQUIRED FIXED-CHARGE GAUSSIAN INPUT
29 October2026. Ordinary Gaussian-state sampling, not a novelty claim.

The sector instrument need not restrict the target to a manually supplied
pure fixed-N state. A gauge-invariant Gaussian input with onebody covariance
C0 (0<=C0<=I) is an efficiently sampled mixture of pure Slaters: diagonalize
C0=V diag(p_j)V*, draw independent occupation bits b_j~Bernoulli(p_j), and
take the Slater determinant of the occupied columns of V. The average
state is exactly the original Gaussian density operator. This diagonalization
and sampling are paid matrix/random-number operations, not an arbitrary
convex-Gaussian-state decomposition oracle.

For several charges, require C0 and the diagonal onebody charge matrices to
commute. Diagonalize C0 within common charge blocks (resolve degeneracies
there), so each sampled orbital has definite charge. Every sampled Slater
then has a known q. The charge vectors C in the compiler are the SAME for
every trajectory, so its sufficient noise condition and U' construction do
not depend on the sampled charge value q. The quadratic gauge correction
g(q)=s/2+V_gauge q is applied separately to each trajectory. Since the
target physical generator preserves those charges, averaging the sector
simulations recovers its action on this block-diagonal input.

This is a classical representation change; q-conditioned gauge corrections
do not change the target model or demand a physical feedback apparatus.
The initial input is still supplied by its actual Gaussian covariance or
preparation. Arbitrary interacting thermal states, Gaussian mixtures with
an unacquired sampling law, and charge-coherent BCS/pairing components are
not licensed by this argument. In particular, the existence of a general
Gaussian Gibbs-mixture theorem does not by itself satisfy this interface.

Diagonal physical Gamma and exact finite-step identities are still ideal
mathematics. Finite precision, trajectory conditioning/QR, splitting error,
sampling cost and the requested observable must be paid by the consumer.
