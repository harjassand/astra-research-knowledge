## 2. Complete operator-valued coordinates, including negative tuples

For any Hermitian X on memory and caller/reference, each map

`X -> Tr_M[(F_i tensor I)X]`

is a CP trace-nonincreasing contraction: sandwich by sqrt(F_i) before tracing. For any Hermitian tuple x,

`||sum_j tau_j tensor x_j||_1 <= sum_j Tr(tau_j)||x_j||_1 <= ||x||_Sigma`.

Applying the first fact to each term in the signed inverse matrix gives

`||Pi(X)||_Sigma <= (sum_ji |H^-1_ji|)||X||_1 <= K||X||_1`.

These inequalities hold uniformly after every reference extension. They do not assume X or Lambda(x) is positive. Although stated on Hermitian arguments, they give the required complete norm bounds: an arbitrary operator can be embedded as an off-diagonal block of a Hermitian operator using one extra reference qubit, preserving the trace-norm ratio for Hermiticity-preserving maps.

The exact identity Pi Lambda=id follows from the raw Gram matrix H. Full span then gives Lambda Pi=id on **all** memory operator components, also when their coefficients are noncommuting caller operators. The second identity is the load-bearing use of a full core. Without it, coherent feedback could reach components invisible to a smaller scalar minor.

A complete physical source/controller segment P is CPTP on memory plus all caller registers, including all retained classical branches. Thus

`||Pi P Lambda||_(Sigma->Sigma),cb <=K`.

The bound is independent of the number of native calls in that exact segment. Interleaved caller controls act identically on every coordinate and have norm at most one. This stability is valid on the signed Hermitian intermediate inputs produced by perturbation terms; it is not a statement only about the actual positive occupied memory state.

## 4. Dyson expansion, feedback and the raw trace row

Each product term with m perturbations is separated by at most m+1 **exact** contiguous segments. Their complete norm is at most K even on nonpositive intermediate tuples, while each error costs delta. Summing the binomially many terms gives exactly

`K[(1+K delta)^H-1]`.

No error map is commuted through feedback. Controllers with at most H calls can be padded by inactive identity steps with zero error; all outcomes remain unnormalized and retained until their declared discard. A policy selected offline from calibration is harmless because the bound is uniform over every allowed physical policy. Feeding normalized signed pseudo-states into an undeclared nonlinear update would not be the linear controller represented here.

This is a finite-horizon perturbation certificate, not uniform infinite-time stability. For a useful fixed target, confidence must make HKdelta sufficiently small. Its advantage over multiplying one-step norms is that the true dynamics contributes K per exact segment, not a spurious K per time step even as delta tends to zero.

For the executable service, initial memory is the independently reset rho_initial=tau_1, product with the full caller payload/reference. The initial tuple e_1 tensor input therefore has norm one and is known without implementing Pi. The root explicitly removed any suggestion that the learner can initialize arbitrary correlated hidden-memory states. Abstract Pi/Lambda identities on correlated operators remain valid, but they do not supply such an operational initialization.

At the end, use the empirical **raw** mass row mhat, whose entries are in [0,1]. Its coordinate functional has norm at most one. The row error has norm at most delta_m, and the true final tuple has norm at most K. Splitting

`mhat (That_product-T_product) + (mhat-m) T_product`
