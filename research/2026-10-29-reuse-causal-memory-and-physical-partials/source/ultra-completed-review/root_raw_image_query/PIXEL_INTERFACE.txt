DETERMINISTIC PIXEL ERROR FOR A'S ANNULAR RAW-IMAGE QUERY
Root supporting derivation, 29 October 2026. Pending A whole intake.
A owns the image/particle covariance model, normalization, physical weak
balance, PSF/aperture qualification, source data and full receiver.

For complex planar coordinates let
 K(x,y)=-beta Im[y^2/x^2-2y/x], beta real.
On true-to-pixel-center segments staying in a padded region with
|x|>=a0>0 and |y|<=b0, holomorphic differentiation gives
 |grad_x K|<=2|beta|(b0^2/a0^3+b0/a0^2)=Lx,
 |grad_y K|<=2|beta|(b0/a0^2+1/a0)=Ly.
Indeed the complex derivatives of y^2/x^2-2y/x are
-2y^2/x^3+2y/x^2 and 2y/x^2-2/x; the Euclidean gradient norm of its
imaginary part equals the complex derivative modulus in either variable.

If location quantization errors are <=delta_0,delta_1 and those segments
meet the stated bounds, the fundamental theorem along both segments gives
 |K(Qx,Qy)-K(x,y)|<=Lx delta_0+Ly delta_1.             (P1)
For square physical pixels of sides h_j, delta_j=h_j/sqrt(2) suffices.
The bound applies to any normalized positive mixture of such position
pairs. In particular summing a constant pixel-center weight against an
integrated photon image equals integrating its quantized-location weight;
(P1) does NOT require uniform within-pixel photon locations. It does not
treat square pixels as an extra radial PSF.

Mask-boundary pixels require separate care: a discontinuous aperture mask
is not globally Lipschitz. Either admit a sufficient pixel/PSF margin or
charge the excluded boundary-strip mass with the bounded clipped kernel.
PSF tails crossing the Laurent pole or the aperture remain separate inputs,
as does converting connected intensity covariance to a positive pair law.
An estimated normalization ratio needs its own denominator/error bounds.
Quadratic-velocity interpretation divides this positional error by the
frame-lag squared and adds the finite-lag/model remainder. This derivation
does not itself supply those inputs or an actual torque measurement.

Mean-value, complex differentiation and deterministic quadrature error are
ordinary tools. Historical novelty, finite-sample performance and native
data advantage are not claimed by this supporting interface.
