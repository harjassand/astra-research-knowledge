Let Omega be a connected smooth bounded planar fluid domain with an outer
boundary and finitely many closed inner boundaries. Let w be single-valued
C2, smooth to its boundary, with div w=0 and Delta w=0. In complex notation
w=u+iv, z=x+iy,
 partial_z w=(div w+i curl w)/2.
The identity Delta w=grad div w+J grad curl w makes curl w=2alpha constant.
Hence w=i alpha z+overline{g(z)} for a SINGLE-VALUED holomorphic g on Omega.
No globally single-valued potential/stream function is assumed.

Take any mu>0. For constant-pressure stress sigma=2mu sym grad w-p I, put
a=sigma11+p,b=sigma12. Tracelessness gives
 a+ib=2mu overline{g'(z)}.
For any smooth closed curve in the fluid, or a boundary by smooth limit,
conjugate(n) ds=+/- i d conjugate(z). Its total viscous complex force is
therefore +/-2i mu conjugate(integral g' dz)=0, since g is single valued.
Constant pressure integrates to zero as well. EVERY individual closed
boundary force vanishes. Poles inside excluded holes do not affect the
integral of the exact differential dg. Nonzero circulation is permitted.

Suppose w=0 on the outer boundary and w=U_j, a constant translation, on
each inner boundary. Integration by parts now yields
 integral_Omega 2mu|sym grad w|^2=sum_j U_j dot F_j=0.
Thus w is a rigid motion, and its zero outer trace forces w=0. No nonzero
pure-translation loading trace meets these harmonic/incompressibility
conditions, for ANY such geometry. This strengthens the annulus example;
it does not assert torque traces exist on every geometry. Rotation can
do work against a torque even though the boundary's net force is zero.
