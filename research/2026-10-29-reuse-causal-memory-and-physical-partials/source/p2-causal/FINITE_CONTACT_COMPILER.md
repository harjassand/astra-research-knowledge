# A finite-channel compiler for bounded Weyl contacts

29 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane. Root construction. Draft pending actual
independent review. Predecessor proofs and reviews are not modified.

## Objective and scope

The output-port tail theorem in `../compact/` is useful only if the resulting
finite memory can be evolved by a finite, completely positive instrument.
This note constructs that bridge. It does not identify unknown parameters,
make an unknown initial state computable, or promise classical simulation
of a source register whose dimension grows exponentially with its qubits.

Take a contact with retained oscillator a, fresh oscillator b, arbitrary
source/controller/reference K, prescribed passive quadratic evolution that
preserves N=N_a+N_b, and a bounded interaction

    V(t)=sum_l B_l(t) tensor W(f_l(t)),
    sum_l ||B_l(t)|| <= a(t),  A=integral a(t),
    ||f_l(t)|| <= kappa.

The sum may be a finite-variation integral; V is self-adjoint. The Weyl
convention is W(f)=exp(i(a(f)+a(f)^dagger)). For a sine vertex, the two
Weyl coefficients have total variation equal to the source amplitude.
Several conjugate quadrature vertices are permitted; kappa bounds their
full two-mode feature norm. Put source-only controls in their common
interaction picture, or insert their complete isometries at their real
times. These preserve all number projections used below.

The finite contact input has a supported on 0,...,d-1, potentially
entangled with K and a reference. Fresh b is independent thermal with
mean n. Parameter uncertainty, preparation error, detector uncertainty and
physical errors are separate from the mathematical approximation here.

## 1. Displacement of a finite-number subspace

Let P_m project onto total number at most m, let Q_M project onto total
number at least M, and assume M>m. For any multimode displacement D(beta),
with b=||beta||, and z>1,

    ||Q_M D(beta) P_m||
      <= min(1, z^(-(M-m)/2)
          exp((z-1)b^2/2 + (sqrt(z)-1/sqrt(z)) b sqrt(m))).       (1)

Proof: normal ordering and z^(N/2) a^dagger z^(-N/2)=sqrt(z)a^dagger
give on the finite-number core

    z^(N/2) D(beta)
      = exp((z-1)b^2/2) D(sqrt(z) beta)
        exp((sqrt(z)-1/sqrt(z)) a(beta)) z^(N/2).

Here a(beta) has norm at most b sqrt(m) on P_m and lowers total number.
Its exponential series is finite on that subspace. Its norm is bounded by
exp((sqrt(z)-1/sqrt(z)) b sqrt(m)). The remaining displacement is unitary.
Also ||z^(N/2)P_m||=z^(m/2) and ||Q_M z^(-N/2)||=z^(-M/2).
This proves (1), with extension by boundedness from the finite core. It
does not apply the false identity sin(PXP)=P sin(X) P.

## 2. A uniform finite-time tail

In the passive interaction picture, each Dyson word of order k is a
source contraction times a Weyl word. The latter is one displacement of
norm at most k kappa, times a phase. The coefficient variation integrates
to at most A^k/k!. This remains true for arbitrary noncommuting source
controls. Therefore, uniformly throughout the contact,

    ||Q_M U(t) P_m|| <= T(M,m,A,kappa),                     (2)

where a computable upper bound is

    T = min(1, sum_(k=1)^K A^k/k! F(M,m,k kappa,z_k) + R_K(A)),
    F(M,m,b,z) = min(1, RHS of (1)),
    R_K(A) = sum_(k>K) A^k/k!.

The order-zero term is exactly zero because M>m. Different z_k>1 may be
used for different k. A convenient rational choice is z_k=r_k^2: all
prefactors are rational, and only a positive exponential and sqrt(m)
need directed enclosures. No finite Hamiltonian is used to prove (2).

## 3. Finite Hamiltonian, with a dynamics error

Let P=1-Q_M. The passive evolution preserves P. In its interaction
picture let U_P be the unitary generated by P V(t) P on P, extended by
identity on its complement. For an input vector psi in P_m, write

    z(t)=P U(t)psi,       u(t)=U_P(t)psi.

The difference z-u obeys a forced equation with force -i P V Q_M U psi.
Variation of constants gives ||z(T)-u(T)||<=A T(M,m,A,kappa). The omitted
orthogonal component has norm <=T. Thus

    ||(U(T)-U_P(T))psi|| <= (1+A) T(M,m,A,kappa).            (3)

The sharper sqrt(1+A^2) coefficient is possible, but is not needed.
Equation (3) controls half trace distance for all purifications of all
inputs supported in P_m. The same source instruments and references can
be retained. Source isometries commute P and introduce no new truncation
term. It also holds with prescribed piecewise time dependence; a numerical
time solver must still bound its own error in the finite system.

In the original picture, the finite Hamiltonian is the exact finite
restriction of the passive generator plus P V P. The passive boundary
does not create a leakage term because total number is conserved there.
For a single-mode sine, P sin(kappa X) P must be formed from the exact
displacement matrix elements, not by taking the sine of a truncated X.

## 4. Fresh thermal preparation in the finite model

Let q=n/(1+n), and replace the fresh thermal input by its normalized
restriction to b occupations 0,...,B-1. Its trace distance from the full
thermal input is exactly q^B. The finite input then has total number at
most m=d+B-2. Contractivity and (3) give the complete contact bound

    epsilon_contact <= q^B + (1+A) T(M,d+B-2,A,kappa).       (4)

The replacement is mathematical. It does not claim an experimentally
truncated thermal supply. The actual supply remains thermal; (4) compares
it with a finite simulator. If n=0, B=1 suffices exactly.

## 5. Completely positive memory compression

Let P_d retain a occupations 0,...,d-1 and Q_d=I-P_d. Define

    C_d(rho) = P_d rho P_d
             + |0><0|_a tensor Tr_a(Q_d rho Q_d).          (5)

This is a trace-preserving completely positive map into a d-dimensional
memory, including all source/reference correlations. It discards cross
blocks and resets only the omitted block. If p=Tr(Q_d rho)<=t^2 then

    d(rho,C_d(rho)) <= sqrt(p(1-p))+p <= t+t^2.             (6)

This follows from the off-diagonal block trace-norm inequality and the
orthogonal diagonal blocks. No normalization of rare outcomes occurs.

Let Phi_j be the true contacts including their declared causal source and
detector instruments. If their actual uncompressed prefixes have output
tail norms t_j, then

    d(Phi_J ... Phi_1(rho), (C_d Phi_J)...(C_d Phi_1)(rho))
       <= sum_j (t_j+t_j^2).                              (7)

Use telescoping with TRUE prefixes and compressed CPTP suffixes. It is
not necessary to prove the window tail theorem on artificial prefixes
that already contain C_d; doing so would introduce extra operations not
automatically covered by a Weyl-word argument.

Each C_d Phi_j restricted to finite a input is approximated by the finite
thermal preparation and finite unitary of (4), followed by the same C_d.
That approximate map is exactly CPTP in exact arithmetic. Its independent
per-contact allowance is epsilon_contact. Hence a finite computation has

    d_final <= epsilon_initial
               +sum_j(t_j+t_j^2)
               +sum_j epsilon_contact,j
               +epsilon_finite_solver.                  (8)

Model comparison, statistical confidence and physical noise can be added
only through their separate valid interfaces; none is part of (8).

## 6. What this makes possible

The port-regularization theorem supplies t_j with a memory cutoff that
grows only polylogarithmically with the desired finite workload accuracy
in its stated fixed-parameter regimes. Equations (1)-(8) convert that
compactness statement into finite, causal, completely positive transfer
maps. A fixed-dimensional source and specified finite controller can then
be simulated without an ever-growing bath Hilbert space, at a cost that
still depends on source dimension, requested outputs, and numerical input
regularity. An arbitrary growing quantum register or retained stream of
unmeasured quantum outputs remains a separate large resource.

A known finite-support initial a needs no initial allowance. A finite-mean
initial state can be compared to a compressed state with a separate
energy-tail allowance; arbitrary unknown incoming state is not acquired
by output regularity. After exact port regeneration, output compression
itself is uniform even over incoming states without finite mean.

The abstract theorem needs integrable bounded interactions. Effective
computation additionally needs computable finite matrix elements and an
effective integration rule for the supplied time dependence. Piecewise
constant contacts provide one concrete executable interface.

## 7. Close established results

Tong, Albert, McClean, Preskill and Su, Quantum 6,816 (2022),
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.06942 , establish polylogarithmic bosonic
truncation and Hamiltonian simulation for their low-initial-number,
finite-number-step interaction family; Eq.(24), Theorem5 and its proof
were read. Generic polylogarithmic truncation is not a novelty claim here.
The full sine has arbitrarily long number jumps, so that finite-step
hypothesis does not directly apply. This proof instead uses displacement
structure, and the separate port theorem replenishes low tails at a
retained output despite nonlinear operation.

The 2025 work “Effective descriptions of bosonic systems can be considered
complete,” DOI10.1038/s41467-025-64872-3, Theorems1-4, supplies broad
energy-constrained finite-dimensional approximations of physical bosonic
unitaries and universality constructions. The present result is narrower
in dynamics and more specific in causal repeated-port structure. It is
not a new proof that bosonic computations admit effective descriptions.
