3. WHY COVARIANCE RANK AT MOST TWO IS ALSO CONTROLLED

Let L=range S. Since <(v.G)^2>=0 for v in ker S, G(x) belongs to L
almost everywhere. Its real and imaginary Fourier coefficients also
belong to L. Every Fourier coefficient of a gradient has the form
u_k tensor k; its real and imaginary parts are real rank-one matrices.

If dim L=1 and G is nonzero, L must be a rank-one line. This is Section 2.
If dim L=2, restrict the determinant quadratic to L.

If det|L is identically zero, every value in L is rank one, so Section 2
again applies. A two-dimensional space of rank-one matrices has one
common output or spatial factor: the determinant of u1 tensor xi1
plus t u2 tensor xi2 is t det[u1,u2]det[xi1,xi2].

Otherwise the real projective zero set of det|L contains at most two
lines. It must contain two distinct ones, since the real and imaginary
Fourier coefficients span L. Choose rank-one generators

              R1=u1 tensor xi1, R2=u2 tensor xi2.

Both their output factors and their spatial factors are independent;
otherwise the determinant would vanish on all of L. Every Fourier
mode lies in one of these two rank-one lines. Its spatial frequency is
parallel to the associated xi_j. Hence the ACTUAL field has the form

           G=R1 a(k1.x)+R2 b(k2.x),                   (4)

with mean-zero scalar L4 profiles and independent primitive frequency
directions k1,k2. On a two-dimensional periodic cell the map to the
two circle phases preserves Haar measure and is surjective, so the
two scalar variables in (4) are independent. This also holds for a
general period lattice after choosing its dual lattice basis.

Write m_ij=<a_i^j>, j=2,3,4, for the two profiles. Use JUST TWO ordinary
rays R_i with

             w_i=m_i2, A_i=[m_i3/(3m_i2)] R_i.        (5)

Zero-variance branches are omitted. Covariance matching is immediate.
Independence and zero means give <C(G)>=sum m_i3 C(R_i) for EVERY
cubic C. Euler's identity proves that (5) matches all those targets.
Its cost is

 E=(2/3)sum_i F(R_i) m_i3^2/m_i2
       <=(2/3)sum_i F(R_i)m_i4,                       (6)

by Cauchy--Schwarz. The true quartic moment is

 f=sum_i F(R_i)m_i4
       +(1/2)m_12 m_22 F''(R1)[R2,R2].               (7)

The mixed term is nonnegative by ROC of the homogeneous F. Thus
E<=(2/3)f, and (1)-(2) prove (A). The exact replay includes eight actual
four-mode profile fields with nonzero third moments, verifies (5)-(7)
against their Fourier expansions, and matches all twenty cubics.

For the rank-one-plane case, finite ordinary attainment follows as in

4. A UNIVERSAL LOCAL POSITIVE COVARIANCE MAP IS IMPOSSIBLE

Consider scalar-Hessian fields first. Use symmetric value coordinates
r=(a,b,c) for (a,b;b,c), and their compatible derivative jet

       h=(psi_xxx,psi_xxy,psi_xyy,psi_yyy).

The raw heat covariance density is B1 B1^T+B2 B2^T, where
B1=(h0,h1,h2), B2=(h1,h2,h3). At a plane-wave symbol xi=(x,y),

       h=xi^3=(x^3,x^2 y,x y^2,y^3),
       r(xi)=(x^2,xy,y^2),
       S_raw(xi^3)=|xi|^2 r(xi)r(xi)^T.

Any positive allocation of symmetric rank-one covariance must have
S>=0 and S02=S11: the determinant is ac-b^2. Suppose a symmetric
3 by 3 matrix L(h), quadratic in h, obeyed

 L(h)>=0 and L02(h)=L11(h) for EVERY h,
 L(xi^3)=|xi|^2 r(xi)r(xi)^T for EVERY xi.              (9)

These assumptions allow every constant-coefficient quadratic correction
that vanishes on plane waves, not just a selected list of null forms.
They impose no restriction on how an allocation's individual rays are
chosen from h; only its resulting covariance is required quadratic.

Let A,B,C be the symmetric 4 by 4 matrices of the three nonnegative
quadratic forms L00,L11,L22. They are PSD. On h=(1,t,t^2,t^3), symbol
matching forces

       h^T A h=1+t^2,
       h^T B h=t^2+t^4,
       h^T C h=t^4+t^6.

PSD matrices with a zero diagonal have the corresponding zero row.
The t^6 and then t^4 coefficients force A's rows 3 and 2 to vanish.
The t^0 and t^6 coefficients force B's rows 0 and 3 to vanish.
The t^0 and then t^2 coefficients force C's rows 0 and 1 to vanish.
All remaining coefficients then give the UNIQUE possibilities

       A=diag(1,1,0,0), B=diag(0,1,1,0), C=diag(0,0,1,1).

At the compatible jet h=(0,1,0,0), these diagonal values are (1,1,0).
The trace constraint in (9) sets L02=L11=1, so the principal submatrix
on indices 0,2 is [[1,1],[1,0]], with determinant -1. This contradicts
L>=0. The receiver verifies all forced coefficient systems and this
exact negative minor. Therefore NO map satisfying (9) exists.

The full vector-gradient version is ruled out too. Restrict its jets
to h_full=(h0,h1,h2,h1,h2,h3). Plane-wave values lie in the symmetric
value subspace. Any normal variance of a positive quadratic output
would be a nonnegative quadratic form in h vanishing on every xi^3.
Four such coherent vectors span R4, so that quadratic form is zero.
PSD then forces the whole output covariance to stay in the symmetric
subspace, reducing to the contradiction above.

This forbids the instantaneous quadratic local covariance bridge from
the heat proof to positive allocations. It does NOT forbid nonlocal,
field-dependent or differently nonlinear constructions, and it does
not contradict the acquired heat QC proof or the nonlocal covariance
exchanges. Cubic matching and the cost budget would come AFTER this
already-impossible local covariance step.

Even continuous dependence on the current value matrix does not evade
this obstruction if the proposed instantaneous covariance integral is
preserved for every mean and every smooth perturbation. Linearize at
a constant symmetric mean M using A=M+epsilon D^2 psi and h=epsilon
D^3 psi. Quadratic homogeneity in h and continuity in A give, after
division by epsilon^2 and epsilon down to zero, a constant-M quadratic
map preserving the covariance integral for every psi. A single Fourier
wave then forces precisely (9) at that M. Thus the same contradiction
applies. Schemes that mix information nonlocally, change the jet
homogeneity, or preserve covariance only after an additional global
operation are not excluded by this argument.

6. WHY THIS CONIC OPTIMIZATION STILL TESTS GLOBAL ROC ENERGY

A conic Q has a quadratic extension Q_ext to all matrices. Let
ell_T(A)=(T^(-1)A)01-(T^(-1)A)10. For any epsilon>0 there is finite

 F+C+Q_ext+epsilon|A|^2+M_epsilon ell_T(A)^2           (13)

is globally ROC on ALL rank-one directions, provided (11) holds and
C belongs to the globally admitted V.

Proof. The completed kernel kappa_C(R) is continuous on normalized
rank-one matrices. On the conic ell_T(R)=0, (11) and its limits give
2Q_ext(R)>=kappa_C(R). Subtract 2epsilon from the residual on the
unit rank-one set. It is strictly negative in a neighborhood of that
conic. On its compact complement ell_T(R)^2 has a positive minimum,
so a sufficiently large finite M_epsilon dominates the residual.
The resulting completed ROC inequality is global. QED.

For actual G in L_T, the normal square in (13) has zero variational

global quadratic extension at zero margin is not claimed attained.
A strict negative conic energy would give a genuine global ROC
counterexample after choosing epsilon small enough and receiving a
