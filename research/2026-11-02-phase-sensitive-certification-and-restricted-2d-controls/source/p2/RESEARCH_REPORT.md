## 1. The precise physical assertion

For the spin-1/2 square-lattice Heisenberg antiferromagnet with `alpha=J2/J1=1/2`, decide whether infinitesimal field selection yields **nonzero columnar dimer order**. Set `J1=1` and, on even `L x L` periodic tori, define

\[
H_{L,\alpha}=\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j+\alpha\sum_{\langle\!\langle ij\rangle\!\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j, \quad
D_L=\sum_{x,y}(-1)^x\mathbf S_{x,y}\cdot\mathbf S_{x+1,y},
\]

\[
e(h)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{\lambda_{\min}(H_{L,1/2}-hD_L)}{L^2},\qquad
m_D:=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{e(0)-e(h)}{h}=-e'(0+).
\]

The per-site source norm satisfies `||D_L||/L^2 <= 3/4`. For the chosen thermodynamic subsequence, finite-range interactions support the energy-density limit; boundary-independent *phase identification* does not follow from this existence. `m_D>0` means spontaneous, field-selected bond modulation with wavevector `(pi,0)`; `m_D=0` only excludes this **particular** selected order, not other VBS vectors, plaquette texture, topological order, or gaplessness. The analogous vertical field requires a separate check. This is a more focused, consequential physical assertion than energy density alone.

The original trusted baseline at `alpha=1/2` is `-0.583212 <= e(0) <= -0.46909`; its rigorous width is `0.114122`. All prior witnesses, positive and negative, remain in the archived ZIP. In particular, fixed-patch eigenvalue sharpening and attractive stoquastic positivity fail as phase engines, and the baseline's sign-independent 9-spin certificate is already an instance of ordinary cluster methodology.

## 2. Most relevant established science: 2604.01555v1 and QMBCertify

The **linear ground-state identity** is `ell([H,f])=0`; the **matrix inequality** is generated by `ell(f H f^* -(H f f^* + f f^* H)/2)>=0`. Both are correct on an exact finite ground state. In frustrated 2D, the authors report numerical problems and omit *both* optimality families. This is empirical solver behavior, **not** a proof of invalidity, nor an established root-cause analysis. Their unfrustrated 16x16 energy lower bound is `-0.674580`/site vs QMC `-0.669976`, while their 10x10 frustrated `alpha=.5` SDP improves the earlier `-0.514628` lower to `-0.510813`/site; the comparison DMRG upper is `-0.4988`/site. At 16x16 unfrustrated, the maximum-separation spin-correlation SDP bounds `[-0.035612,0.065971]` still straddle zero although QMC reports `0.03839`; spatially local moment bases do not automatically resolve macroscopic order.

The linked [QMBCertify](https://github.com/wangjie212/QMBCertify) source includes the `GSB` hierarchy, U(1)-block reduced-density-matrix constraints, energy and correlation examples, and [2024 PRX](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.14.031006) methods. **Strongest ordinary comparator:** implement the same field perturbation and observable in the same structured SDP and the same PEPS/DMRG/NQS comparator; do *not* credit our programme for ordinary SDP observable bounds. ## 3. New organising question: which certificate can actually reach a phase?

Two distinct positive interfaces are tested:

1. **Construct compatibility when a local conserved/annihilating sector permits it.** A local quantum-marginal family is admitted or rejected using explicit support and coherence equations; prove the compatible global states and an order/gap theorem for a noncommuting 2D Hamiltonian in that sector. The structural condition must be *checked*, not granted, and fails on generic Heisenberg marginals.
2. **Avoid global marginal completion when the requested observable has a direct representation.** On weighted quantum-dimer Rokhsar–Kivelson Hamiltonians, local positive flip forms give a ground state whose diagonal observables are exactly the matching partition function derivatives. Determinants/adjugates replace a global quantum-state reconstruction. This is an established RK–Kasteleyn mechanism, independently reconstructed and replayed here, **not** an invention or a claimed improvement over existing Pfaffian solvers.

For the original Heisenberg question, a third tool is derived: **finite-field phase conversion with a *paid* susceptibility/curvature modulus**. It is a general thermodynamic consequence of concavity, not a free new solver. It identifies an obstacle more targeted than a generic quantum marginal extension algorithm.

## 4. Route I: exact quantum-marginal extension in a restrictive but complete sector

Let `G=(V,E)` be any connected simple graph with `|V| >= 3`, and at each vertex a qubit. On an edge `e=(u,v)` put

\[
Q_e=(I-Z_uZ_v)/2,\qquad P_G=\prod_{e\in E}(I-Q_e).
\]

The `Q_e` commute. Their common zero space on any connected graph is exactly `span{|0^V>, |1^V>}`. Suppose a *supplied* edge-marginal family `rho_e` is individually PSD, normalized, single-vertex consistent, and satisfies `Tr(rho_e Q_e)=0` exactly.

**Exact extension theorem (proved here; elementary and no priority claim).** These marginals have a global density-operator extension **if and only if**, in the edge basis `00,01,10,11`, every `rho_e` has form

\[
\rho_e=p|00\rangle\langle00|+(1-p)|11\rangle\langle11|,
\]

with the **same** `p in [0,1]` on all edges. If so, the explicit global extension is

\[
\rho_{\rm global}=p|0^V\rangle\langle0^V|+(1-p)|1^V\rangle\langle1^V|.
\]

*Proof.* If the marginals arise from a global `rho`, zero expectation of positive `Q_e` implies `Q_e rho =rho Q_e=0`. Hence `rho=P_G rho P_G`; all proper reduced states kill the cross coherence `|0^V><1^V|`. Therefore the displayed form follows with common `p`, which single-site consistency also enforces. The converse is the constructed global mixture. The graph may contain arbitrarily many 2D loops; the *sector* pays for tractability. Checking exact rational entries and consistency costs `O(|E|)` small-matrix operations, with a compact two-component output, **not** `2^|V|` dense-state construction.

**Non-extension falsifier.** Give each edge of a 3-qubit chain the Bell density `|Phi+><Phi+|`. Each edge has zero `Q` expectation and one-site marginal `I/2`, so pairwise overlaps agree. Yet no global extension exists: the `12` Bell density has `<X_1 X_2>=1`, the `23` Bell density gives `<Z_2 Z_3>=1`, while these two observables anticommute. Any state obeys

\[
\langle X_1X_2\rangle^2+\langle Z_2Z_3\rangle^2\le1.
\]

More generally, if `q=Tr(rho Q_{23})`, the same algebra gives the **quantitative necessary condition**

\[
|\langle X_1X_2\rangle|\le2\sqrt{q(1-q)}.
\]

This rejects incompatible edge data without optimizing a full global density matrix. For true global `rho` with `epsilon=sum_e Tr(rho Q_e)`, `1-Tr(rho P_G)<=epsilon`, so the gentle measurement bound gives an explicitly normalized state `sigma` supported on the 2D ground subspace with `||rho-sigma||_1<=2 sqrt(epsilon)+epsilon` (when `epsilon<1`). This approximate statement is **one-way**: arbitrary approximate edge inputs are not automatically globally compatible. For large volumes, `epsilon` must be small in *total*, not just per edge.

### A noncommuting 2D physical family with exact order and gap

On a periodic `L x L` square grid (`L>=3`) use the nearest-neighbour bonds `e`. For each bond pick the two sites `u_e,v_e` on the *opposite side of its adjacent unit plaquette* (disjoint from e), and define

\[
H_t=\sum_e Q_e\big(I-t X_{u_e}X_{v_e}\big),\qquad |t|<1.
\]

Each term is Hermitian and locally PSD because `Q_e` commutes with `X_{u_e}X_{v_e}`, and the latter is a Hermitian involution. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{H_t\succeq(1-|t|)\sum_e Q_e\succeq4(1-|t|)(I-P_G).}
\]

The second inequality uses the elementary square-torus edge-connectivity `4`: any nonconstant bit assignment has at least four disagreeing edges. Thus **every size** has precisely the two ferromagnetic product ground rays, a spectral gap at least `4(1-|t|)`, and ground-state longitudinal correlations `<Z_x Z_y>=1` across arbitrary distances. The model has global `prod_x X_x` symmetry, so the two product rays are symmetry-related pure ordered states; a zero-field mixture has vanishing one-point magnetization but retains long-range two-point order. Applying `-h sum_x Z_x` gives **exactly** `e(h)=-|h|` per site and field-selected magnetization `m=1` for `h>0`.

**This is not a commuting Hamiltonian:** already on the 3x3 torus with `t=2/5`, the exact computational-basis matrix element of `[sum Q_e,H_t]` for bit strings `s=1` and `s'=25` is `-8/5` (up to commutator sign convention), as independently enumerated. The two states have respectively 4 and 8 domain-wall edges. The four-spin defect-gated interaction is not simply the original ferromagnetic ZZ Hamiltonian; nevertheless, its **ground sector remains trivial**. An informed ordinary local-projector proof achieves the same result with less work, so this is a validated positive structural control, **not** a new frustrated Heisenberg phase or a new universality class.

Input contract/cost: explicit graph geometry, named opposite-plaquette sites, rational `t` and `|t|<1`; O(L²) local operator/rational checks, O(L²) sparse Pauli terms, and at most constant local matrix work per term. The global ground density is specified in compressed form. The estimate persists for `t_e` with `sup_e|t_e|<1`. It **does not** handle arbitrary physical perturbations which act nontrivially on the zero-defect sector or SU(2)-invariant antiferromagnetic Heisenberg bonds. Generic unknown ground-state marginals cannot be assumed to lie in the admitted sector.

**Observed finite test:** `L=3, t=2/5`, 9 qubits, 18 bonds, exact minimum nonzero defect count 4; exact gap *lower bound* `12/5`, not measured spectral gap. All 512 classical bit configurations and associated sparse kinetic transitions were enumerated in rational arithmetic. No large-size diagonalization is needed for the theorem.

**Exact exclusion for the actual Heisenberg target.** Every state admitted by this ferromagnetic zero-defect extension sector has Heisenberg pair correlation `+1/4` on every bond (`N>=3`), hence at `alpha=1/2` has **energy per site exactly `+3/4`**. The *separately certified* J1-J2 variational energy is `<= -0.46909`. Therefore the actual ground state is **provably not** in the admitted sector. This is a decisive non-transfer, not just an unexplored numerical compatibility issue. The signed gap between the two energy upper references is at least `1.21909` per site. It eliminates this exact local completion mechanism as a direct model of the J1-J2 ground state.

## 5. Route II: exact 2D quantum-dimer observables without global extension

Take a simply connected finite rectangular piece of the **square lattice**, with perfect-matchings Hilbert basis `|M>`. Supply positive rational `a_e` and physical classical weights `w_e=a_e^2`. Set `A_M=prod_{e in M}a_e` and `Z=sum_M A_M^2`.

For every elementary plaquette `p`, let `h` and `v` denote its two parallel-dimer configurations and set `u_p=prod_{e in h}a_e` and `v_p=prod_{e in v}a_e`. On the flippable local pair define the **rank-one local PSD** term

\[
H_p=(v_p|h\rangle-u_p|v\rangle)(v_p\langle h|-u_p\langle v|),
\]

acting identically across fixed outside occupations, and zero on unflippable configurations. The complete quantum Hamiltonian is `H_RK=sum_p H_p`, with off-diagonal resonance terms and noncommuting overlaps in general. The normalized vector

\[
|\Psi\rangle=Z^{-1/2}\sum_M A_M|M\rangle
\]

is **annihilated by every local flip form**: configurations differing only on `p` have amplitude ratio `u_p/v_p`. Hence it is a zero-energy ground state, and on any finite box whose plaquette-flip graph is connected the ground state is unique within this *perfect-matching Hilbert space*. Any disconnected sectors must be retained rather than silently discarded; periodic topology can have winding-sector multiplicity. This is the established RK detailed-balance mechanism, not novel.

For every diagonal physical dimer-occupation observable,

\[
\langle\Psi|n_e|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\sum_{M\ni e}\prod_{f\in M}w_f
= w_e\,\frac{\partial\log Z}{\partial w_e}.
\]

Now use the established Kasteleyn orientation for planar square-grid dimers: the bipartite K matrix has entry `w_e` on a horizontal edge, `i w_e` on a vertical edge. Then `Z=|det K|`, and edge/pair marginals follow from exact cofactors, using Gaussian-integer rational arithmetic. The *input includes actual graph and edge weights*, and this physical route never assumes an arbitrary quantum-marginal extension. Standard Pfaffian/Kasteleyn methods provide **exactly this resource advantage**, so our independent construction is a same-information tie, not an improvement. Generic 4-port interacting weights, frustrated Heisenberg physics, and generic spin-wavefunctions do not map to this sector.

**Exact finite execution:** `verify_mechanisms.py` enumerates 2x4 and 4x4 matching spaces solely for adversarial cross-checking, while separately evaluating the determinantal backend with fraction-free exact Gaussian-integer Bareiss elimination. It independently checks normalization, all RK local null equations, connectivity, and single-/double-edge insertion cofactors. Outcomes:

| Open square geometry | Weights | Configurations | `Z = |det K|` | Flip pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2x4 | all 1 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| 4x4 | all 1 | 36 | 36 | 70 |
| 4x4 | `a_e=1+(edge_index mod 3)` | 36 | 486972 | 70 |

For the weighted 4x4 corner horizontal edge, `<n_e>=91/167` from both brute matching and determinant/cofactor arithmetic. The code's determinant backend is `O(n³)` arithmetic operations, though bit complexity also depends on integer heights; brute-force enumeration is used **only** as a finite falsifier.

**Exact spin-to-dimer interface falsifier.** Distinct singlet valence-bond coverings of actual spin-1/2 particles are **not** the orthogonal matching configurations of the RK Hilbert space. Already with four physical spins, writing `|ij,kl>=|singlet_ij> tensor |singlet_kl>`, exact spin amplitudes give `<12,34|13,24>=1/2`, `<12,34|14,23>=-1/2` and the linear relation `|12,34>-|13,24>+|14,23>=0`. The three distinct RK matchings would instead be orthogonal and independent. This is an explicit **Gram-matrix/overcompleteness obstruction** to substituting the square J1-J2 spin Hilbert space by orthogonal dimer configurations. The code derives the overlaps with exact rational four-spin amplitudes. A valid Heisenberg-to-dimer reduction would have to acquire the overlap metric, nonorthogonal kinetic terms and couplings to omitted spin states, with a controlled error—none is presently acquired.

**What it does and does not determine:** The quantum-dimer model's **finite-volume** ground-state bond observables are exactly computable. To prove long-distance decay or VBS order in the bulk, one must additionally control limits of `K^{-1}` and geometry/sector dependence. The standard uniform square-lattice RK point is already known to have algebraic dimer correlations; the 2026 weighted quantum-dimer literature proves much stronger phase transitions, and a 2026 PRX example exhibits geometry-dependent bulk behavior. Neither our small checks nor formal `det K` alone establishes an unqualified bulk phase. Most importantly, the physical Hilbert space is *perfect matchings*, not the entire J1-J2 spin space. There is no acquired, controlled mapping from the latter to the RK Hamiltonian at `alpha=.5`; assuming one would replace the difficult operation rather than construct it.

## 6. Observable-specific theorem for the original target: finite field plus curvature

Let `e(h)` be the even concave thermodynamic ground energy density of `H-hD`, for `h>=0`. Define `m(h)=-e'(h)` where the derivative exists and `m_D=-e'(0+)`. Concavity implies `m(h)` is nondecreasing. Define the finite-field secant

\[
q(h)=\frac{e(0)-e(h)}{h}=\frac1h\int_0^h m(s)\,ds.
\]

**Finite-field phase transfer theorem (elementary but explicit).** Suppose, independently of any PSD global marginal completion, rigorous energy-density enclosures

\[
L_0\le e(0)\le U_0,\qquad L_h\le e(h)\le U_h
\]

are obtained. Then

\[
0\le m_D\le q(h)\le\frac{U_0-L_h}{h}.
\]

**To obtain a positive LOWER bound on spontaneous order from a finite `h`, an extra operation is indispensable.** If one **proves** the differential susceptibility bound `0<=m'(s)<=K` on `0<s<h` (distributionally, after the zero-field right-limit), then

\[
\boxed{m_D\ge\frac{L_0-U_h}{h}-\frac{Kh}{2}.}
\]

*Proof:* `m(s) >= m_D` and `m(s) <= m_D+Ks`; integrate over `[0,h]` and insert the energy enclosures. The factor `K` is a *separate physical certificate*, not an assumed convenient constant. Where a finite-size unique ground state has gap `Delta` and `Var(D_L)<=C L²`, the spectral perturbation formula yields `-e_L''(h)<=2C/Delta`; this is one possible provider, but its required **uniform field/volume scope** is not known for frustrated `J1-J2`, especially if there are near-degenerate broken-symmetry sectors. The old energy lower/upper band width `0.114122` alone gives an uncontrolled `11.4122` contribution when divided by a `0.01` dimer field, exceeding the trivial observable cap `3/4` by an order of magnitude.

**Non-order control against a false phase conclusion:** For a single transverse-field paramagnetic spin `H_0=(I-X)/2` probed by `-hZ`, one has exactly `e(h)=(1-sqrt(1+4h²))/2`. At `h=1/4`, the finite-field secant is `sqrt(5)-2 >0`, *but* `m_D=0`. Its susceptibility is bounded by `K=2` and the curvature correction `Kh/2=1/4` defeats the false positive. Conversely the ordered defect-gated control in Section 4 has `e(h)=-|h|`, `K=0` for positive fields and `m=1`, making the lower bound exact. This explicitly distinguishes *field-induced susceptibility* from **spontaneous** order.

A possible implementation is a shared proof-carrying source sweep: ordinary structured SDP supplies rigorous `e(h)` lower bounds, a separately normalized variational trial supplies upper bounds, and an **independently derived thermodynamic susceptibility/curvature modulus** converts finite fields to a phase result. This does not require the full set of quantum marginals to be globally reconstructed, but until a useful modulus is acquired on the actual frustrated Hamiltonian, this is a *conditional architecture*, not a phase theorem. The additional numerical issues associated with the omitted state-optimality constraints remain unresolved.

## 7. A quantified failure: local energy accuracy does not select long-range order

For the 2D ferromagnetic commuting reference `H_0=sum_e Q_e`, make alternating `+/-` vertical stripes of width `ell` on a periodic `4ell x 4ell` torus. Average equally over horizontal translations, producing a translation-invariant mixed state. The stripe state has **exact global magnetization zero** on every component and therefore `M²/N²=0`; the ground-state symmetric mixture has `M²/N²=1`. Nevertheless:

\[
\frac{\langle H_0\rangle}{N}=\frac1\ell,\qquad
\langle Z_{(0,0)}Z_{(r,0)}\rangle=1-\frac{2r}{\ell}\quad (0\le r\le\ell).
\]

A specified patch of horizontal diameter `R<ell` disagrees with the ferromagnetic ground marginal with total variation at most `R/ell` (trace norm at most `2R/ell`). Thus all **fixed-range** patch data and energy density can approach the ordered ground reference arbitrarily closely while the normalized **global** magnetization-square stays identically zero along the chosen finite-volume sequence. The failure is not a counterexample to convergence of local states, and it does not invalidate exact phase certification; it shows that a **finite local tolerance without a scale-sensitive criterion** cannot guarantee the global order parameter. The verification script checks `ell=4,8,16,32` exactly.

The attempt to use a noncommuting easy-axis XXZ/Clifford-dressed model as a novelty claim was rejected during the interrupted work: it reduces to an already understood ordered model. The defect-gated construction avoids that identical model but remains elementary via local PSD domination. Strong conventional alternatives are given full credit.

## 8. Reconciliation, scientific value, and required missing acquisition

**A real, positive but restricted capability was earned:** quantum-edge marginal data can be admitted/rejected and extended on a specified 2D loopy stabilizer sector in linear size; its noncommuting Hamiltonian has size-independent gap and exact long-range order. Independently, weighted RK quantum-dimer observables are reduced from a quantum ground state to exact polynomial-arithmetic determinant/cofactor queries, with a working finite verifier. The phase-field theorem proves exactly what must be added to ordinary finite observable certification to infer zero-field spontaneous order.

**It does not settle the J1-J2 assertion.** No original-field susceptibility bound, long-distance VBS observable certificate, globally admitted RK reduction, reliable finite-to-infinite sequence of optimality-constrained SDPs, or matching-resource numerical improvement was achieved. The exact ordinary methods already dominate the present physical controls. No extrapolated correlation curve or narrower energy interval is called a phase decision.

**Highest-value next operation (research task, not promise of delivery):** for `alpha=0.5`, construct a volume- and small-field-uniform bound on connected **dimer** response in the **selected symmetry sector**, sufficient to quantify `m'(h)` and combine with certified source-dependent energy differences. This is an observable-specific transfer problem. Alternatively, construct from the original Heisenberg interactions a checkable exact/controlled RK-like or tensor-matching representation with a *paid Hamiltonian and observable error*, including its gapless/sector limitations. A globally general quantum marginal solver is unnecessary for either route. A solver-only fix to Wang's Remark 6.1 is worthwhile *only if* it materially narrows the field-order certificate, and it must be compared to the existing unstrengthened SDP at equal expense.
