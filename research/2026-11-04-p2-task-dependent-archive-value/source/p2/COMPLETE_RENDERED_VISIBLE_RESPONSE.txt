I preserved the Gaussian ensemble construction and completed a different consumer: estimating one target expectation from an existing response archive. The strongest result is a theorem that determines when the archive can replace new measurements, how much response information it contains, and where additional measurements should be made.

It has two substantive consequences:

A calibration theorem: even when the actual old and new input laws agree, uncertainty about the old source can force essentially a full new measurement budget under an exact unbiasedness guarantee.
A positive construction: an exact finite count estimator that acquires its inputs, selects additional response locations, performs the actual response calls, and chooses its response budget from a requested mean-square-error bound.

This establishes a foundational result and a functioning estimator. A major practical scientific speedup is not established. Ordinary biased estimation can be substantially cheaper for some of the same expectation tasks, and that comparison changes how the theorem should be used.

The previous general-space and Gaussian results remain unchanged. In particular, the exact continuous Gaussian ensemble theorem remains separate from its approximate finite-grid implementation.

6. What this establishes

The completed result is that the value of a stored response depends jointly on source calibration, the requested functional, and the statistical guarantee.

For the stated guarantee, that dependence is now constructive:

Conditional domination decides whether zero-new-response estimation exists.
A quadratic density integral gives its exact fair-bit variance.
The paid-query extension prices additional response information even for nonlinear, adaptive procedures.
Gaussian conditional affinity converts calibration uncertainty into a response-budget lower bound and a positive residual construction.
The finite count implementation extends the construction to a uniform all-response MSE guarantee with actual acquisition and callback operations.

The classical ingredients—sufficiency, completeness, conditional reconstruction, importance and balance weighting, allocation optimization, and Gaussian density estimation—are credited in the report. Astra’s earlier trace-repair consumer and defeated posterior proposal remain inherited facts. The research establishes the assembled theorems and constructions; historical priority remains unestablished.
