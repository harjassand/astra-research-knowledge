The strongest continuation is a resource factory whose protection follows the actual charge motion

I developed a fixed-charge rail construction with unprepared spins, a native capacitive leakage check, and an accepted Bell-resource factory. Its strongest result is an exposure law:

In the qualified charge model, leading dipole noise needs to be bounded during transverse charge motion. Charge-diagonal dipole dynamics during holds can be included exactly, even when they are strong, time dependent and coupled to a retained quantum source. After the supplied check, the accepted error is second order in the active error action.

This changes the control problem. The controller does not need to reconstruct the source’s motion or freeze it throughout the operation. It needs a particular physical structure: fixed rail charge, spin-independent charge controls, charge-preserving holds, and a check that preserves coherence inside the accepted sector.

I carried the construction through finite gates, a reusable meter, a complete entangled-resource preparation sequence, microscopic Coulomb corrections, and a test with strong noise during extended holds. A complete device window remains unestablished. In particular, a microscopic correction that can be calibrated into the local gate can spoil the ideal meter if it persists during its interaction.

Residual interactions act throughout. The new exposure law removes only the qualified dipole contribution during charge-preserving holds. It does not remove detector integration time, even electric noise, orbital excitation, charge loss or unwanted noncommuting terms.

Nor has acquisition been made free. The pulse parameters, rail-number domain, scalar gain interval, reference fields and stability bounds must be qualified. The numerical controller solves do not establish a prepared-shot acquisition cost.

Established alternatives receive the same improvements. Charge-quadrupole proposals already use protection and pulse optimization; recent orbital proposals explicitly develop finite-bandwidth optimized controls and Coulomb coupling. Singlet-only approaches retain their magnetic-protection strengths. Optical heralding retains its loss-to-retry benefit with its real detector and waiting-memory costs. The present results establish no device-level superiority over those alternatives. Orbital charge-quadrupole proposal; SAGE singlet-only proposal; optical double heralding.

The substantive advance is a capability-specific protection rule: the source can remain unprepared, and its dynamics on rejected charge sectors can remain complicated, provided the physical control path and instrument prevent those dynamics from producing an accepted first-order logical error. Fixed local charge also makes the incident interaction compatible with an extensive array bound.

The remaining challenge is now concrete: preserving those operator properties through the multiband gate, meter, loading and detector cycle. The derived microscopic corrections identify where that challenge lies; a loose sufficient norm bound alone does not establish impossibility.
