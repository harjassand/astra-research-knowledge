A finite-contrast separation potential and constructive common controls

The result and its boundary
The independently important question is whether chemical species with the same equilibrium
interactions can be separated efficiently using only differences in their rates of motion or reaction.
Programmable ratchets and ordinary countercurrent separation already provide extensive sorting
capabilities. The new object developed here is a thermodynamic state function that charges the
complete sorting task: useful product, recovery, rejects, and retained internal information.
Foradeclaredcommon-affinitystochasticdynamics, afinite-contrastconvexpotentialgivesalower
bound on actual integrated entropy production under arbitrary time dependence, intermediate
storage, and numbers of stages. The proof covers both controlled diffusions and finite-jump
networks. A shared moving harmonic potential gives an exactly solvable finite-time construction,
including all driving work and residual free energy. Its weak separation limit has precisely the
local cost required by the potential. An exact two-state cell then gives a physical retained-history
construction approaching the global bound in an ideal common-control limit. Routing and reset
are explicitly included in the construction; finite apparatus losses remain a separate engineering
task.
The local efficiency function is classical Kedem–Caplan coupling algebra. The weak-contrast
potential is the established Dirac separation value. Neither is claimed as newly discovered. The
specific contribution constructed here is their finite-contrast, nonstationary endpoint connection,
its exact network extension, and a matching physical construction of the ideal infimum. The
selective primary-source search did not locate the same full theorem; historical priority is not
established. No separator apparatus, measured material response, or practical advantage has been
demonstrated.
The preceding rare-risk, source-likelihood, linkage, preparation, port-lifetime and complete-
excursion research is preserved unchanged and parked.

Step 6: finite implementations of the reversible operations. The preceding description
uses reversible-limit common transfers, splits, and compressions. None is a label-aware primitive.
The following elementary finite-state constructions justify their use in an infimum theorem.
Occupied-to-empty transfer. With an edge closed, place its empty destination higher by a common
energy L. Opening and equilibrating the pair costs log(1+e−L) per source particle when the
destination is exactly empty. Both labels have the same initial conditional state and the same
equilibrium. Slowly tilt the energy difference from +L to −L, then close the edge. The final source
leakage is O(e−L), and entropy produced by the slow tilt tends to zero. A finite permutation can
be decomposed into such transfers with an empty buffer. A split into an equiprobable pair uses
the same procedure but ends at zero energy difference.

8 Strongest comparisons and actual novelty boundary

from an Onsager transport framework, using small element separation and a specified separative
power definition [5]. The present result connects these ingredients to actual integrated entropy
production, finite contrast and arbitrary nonstationary networks. A search failing to find that
exact connection is not evidence of historical priority.

9 Failures that must remain visible
Trajectory information is different. At equilibrium, two species can have the same position
distribution and zero particle entropy production while different jump counts reveal their rates
to an external observer. Ideal Brownian quadratic variation can even reveal diffusivity. The
theorem bounds stored physical label–state correlations. A recording apparatus is covered only
after its states, affinities and costs are included. It is not a universal cost of knowledge or external
observation.
Bounded rate ratios alone are insufficient. If the two directions of an edge acquire species-
dependent affinities, arbitrarily slow common-looking protocols can create correlations through
energetic discrimination. The common affinity condition is essential. Selective binding, different
charges and species-specific pathways can supply precisely the resource the theorem excludes.
A restricted wash is not all separation. Uniform passive-chamber retention obeys a positive
spectral-mixture inequality Y(ρD,t) ≥ Y(D,t)ρ. It constrains a one-way retention task, but
ordinary stored-pool counterflow can outperform that restricted operation. Both survive as scoped
results, without turning either into a universal impossibility.
An ideal thermodynamic infimum is not throughput. The weak-operation construction has
divergingnumbersofoperationsandincreasingresolutionasitapproachesthebound. Finite-width
gates, leakage, interaction forces, controller erasure, finite density, solvent power and calibration
can dominate an actual apparatus. The simple shared-trap protocol is fully solved within its
model; the elaborate limiting network is not a fabricated machine.
