## Result: an exact cost of turning kinetic differences into physical selectivity

I developed a different research question:

> **Can programmable motion replace equilibrium selectivity in purification—and what is the least thermodynamic cost of doing so at useful purity and recovery?**

The return is a **sharp cost law and a construction that approaches it**, within a defined class of physical controls. It covers arbitrary sequences of shared controls, intermediate storage and retained information. The construction includes physical recording and reset; it does not require an external observer that identifies individual species.

The most consequential implication concerns dilute recovery: **when discrimination comes only from finite differences in kinetics, the ideal cost per recovered target grows inversely with its abundance.** More stages and slower operation cannot eliminate that penalty within the model.

This is a theoretical result with executable evidence. Historical priority and practical separator performance remain unestablished.

## 1. The use that selected the question

The motivating capability was high-purity, high-recovery extraction of valuable components from dilute mixtures using programmable fields and reusable controls.

My first construction used multiple traveling fields to program different species’ transport. The comparison showed that the central sorting capability was already substantially available: multiwave diffusivity-dependent transport and programmable multispecies ratchets have established constructions. Refining that mechanism would not have earned a new broad capability. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

The harder question was therefore the **complete cost of separation by rate differences**. A useful answer must charge product quality, recovery, rejected material, physical memory and reset. It must also permit intermediate pools—the operation that defeated an earlier restricted separation obstruction in the governing Astra construction.

The representation that survived is simple: **the target fraction \(r\) in a physical compartment**, together with a bound on kinetic contrast. Evaluating the resulting lower bound does not require reconstructing a separator’s entire network. Its physical hypotheses must still be warranted.

## 2. The precise result

Consider two conserved species with kinetic coefficients \(a,b>0\). On each corresponding transition, they share the same forward/backward affinity. A representative rate law is

\[
k^{(1)}_{xy}=a\,k_{xy},
\qquad
k^{(0)}_{xy}=b\,k_{xy},
\]

with the **same multiplier in the reverse direction**. Controls can change energies and gates over time, but a gate closes for both species together.

This is stronger than saying the final products have equal binding energies: corresponding affinities must agree throughout the process.

Define

\[
K=\frac{(a-b)^2}{ab}.
\]

Let \(f\) be the initial target fraction, and define a convex separation potential by

\[
\boxed{
\psi''(r)=
\frac{2\left[1+\sqrt{1+Kr(1-r)}\right]}
{Kr^2(1-r)^2},
\qquad
\psi(f)=\psi'(f)=0.
}
\]

If \(\Sigma\) is integrated entropy production divided by \(k_B\), the proved endpoint inequality is

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma\ge
\mathbb E[\psi(r_{\mathrm{final}})]
-\mathbb E[\psi(r_{\mathrm{initial}})].
}
\]

It holds for controlled diffusions and, **exactly rather than through a diffusion approximation**, for finite Markov networks. Edge-dependent kinetic contrasts are allowed if \(K\) bounds all of them.

For an initially mixed feed, desired product purity \(P>f\), and target recovery \(R\), put

\[
z=\frac{fR}{P},
\qquad
r_-=\frac{f(1-R)}{1-z}.
\]

Here \(z\) is the product fraction and \(r_-\) is the target fraction in the rejects. The complete-task bound becomes

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma\ge z\psi(P)+(1-z)\psi(r_-).
}
\]

**This expression is also the exact infimum in the ideal controlled-network class constructed here.** Finite protocols approach it while restoring their auxiliary history registers to arbitrarily good accuracy. Their size, duration, energy range and control precision need not remain bounded.

The lower-bound proof reduces entropy production minus potential growth to a positive-semidefinite \(2\times2\) quadratic form. Its determinant determines the displayed curvature. An exact interpolation of forward and reverse fluxes transfers that proof to finite jumps. The report contains the ordinary proofs.

### Why the stronger potential matters

A Shannon-information bound alone understates the cost of very accurate separation. The leading part of \(\psi\) is the classical Dirac separation value,

\[
V(r)=(2r-1)\log\frac{r}{1-r}.
\]

The contribution developed here is its connection to **actual integrated entropy production at finite kinetic contrast**, together with a matching admissible construction. The Dirac value and the local coupling-efficiency algebra are established prior art. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

For equal priors, the exact ideal cost of retained-state classification with Bayes error at most \(e\) is \(\psi(e)\). In particular,

\[
\Sigma_{\inf}
=
\frac{4}{K}\log\frac1e+O_K(1)
\qquad(e\downarrow0).
\]

Thus the complete accuracy requirement carries a logarithmically divergent cost that a bounded amount of Shannon information does not capture.

## 3. The consequential implication: dilute recovery has a bulk-processing penalty

Keep purity \(P\) and recovery \(R\) fixed, strictly below one, and let the feed fraction \(f\) decrease. The exact ideal cost satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{f\downarrow0}\Sigma_{\inf}(f,R,P)
=
\frac{4}{K}\log\frac1{1-R}.
}
\]

That is a positive constant **per input particle**. Consequently, per recovered target,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\Sigma_{\inf}}{fR}
\sim
\frac{4\log[1/(1-R)]}{KfR}.
}
\]

By comparison, the reversible mixing-work contribution per recovered target grows only as \(\log(1/f)\).

This distinction gives the theory a concrete design use. A separator relying entirely on finite kinetic differences cannot approach the reversible mixing-work benchmark merely by adding stages or slowing its controls. Energetic discrimination, selective accessibility, or another physical resource changes the problem.

The work accounting remains explicit:

\[
\frac{W}{k_BT}
=
\frac{\Delta F_{\mathrm{blind}}}{k_BT}
+I_{\mathrm{out}}+\Sigma.
\]

Here \(F_{\mathrm{blind}}\) includes free energy unrelated to species identity. Prepared packets, pressure, empty registers or fuel can supply that resource. When it is restored, the work is \(k_BT(I_{\mathrm{out}}+\Sigma)\).

The sharp per-particle result applies to **independent tracers or an ideal mixture**. I have not extended this stronger law to arbitrary cooperative processing of many interacting unknown labels. A weaker collective Shannon bound, under a specified many-body diffusion generator, is proved separately in the package.

## 4. The missing mechanism: retain the evidence physically

A locally efficient sorting step does not establish a complete separator. The decisive construction was a way to retain and reuse its output while charging the discarded information.

### An exact two-state operation

Use two physical states \(x=\pm1\), with a shared control \(F(t)\):

\[
k^{(s)}_{-+}=\frac{D_s}{2}(1+F),
\qquad
k^{(s)}_{+-}=\frac{D_s}{2}(1-F),
\qquad |F|<1.
\]

Both species experience the same affinity. Their conditional means satisfy

\[
\dot\mu_s=D_s(F-\mu_s),
\]

and their actual entropy-production rates are

\[
\sigma_s=
D_s(F-\mu_s)
\left[\operatorname{atanh}F-\operatorname{atanh}\mu_s\right].
\]

I first solved the corresponding shared harmonic control exactly. The binary operation then removed the need for a Gaussian approximation in the global construction.

For a weak operation producing mean difference \(\varepsilon\),

\[
\Sigma_{\mathrm{cell}}
=C(r)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4),
\]

\[
\mathbb E[\Delta\psi]
=C(r)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4),
\qquad
C(r)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+Kr(1-r)}}{K}.
\]

The physical cost and the separation-potential increment agree to leading order.

### Physical history, followed by an accounted reset

Each outcome becomes a physical child compartment. Its next control is predetermined by that compartment’s composition. No individual species measurement occurs.

The proof initially retains a full finite history tree. Branches stop when their compositions reach the product or reject thresholds. A drift identity gives an expected \(O(\varepsilon^{-2})\) active operations, so the accumulated local excess vanishes.

At the end, histories within each outlet are relaxed under a matched common potential. The irreversible reset cost is exactly

\[
\boxed{
I(S;\text{history}\mid\text{outlet}).
}
\]

Nearby terminal compositions make that cost small. The remaining label-independent history can then be compressed quasistatically, restoring auxiliary blanks while charging the reversible work.

This establishes global attainment in the stated limit. It also identifies the resource burden: the clean proof can require an enormous history tree. The numerical implementation instead merges nearby posterior compartments and explicitly charges the information lost by doing so.

### Finite controls were tested too

The common transfer/reset operation was tested with finite barriers, bounded transition rates, finite equilibration holds, and all state-energy work included.

For a transfer with energy gap \(L\) and sweep duration \(T\),

\[
\Sigma_{\mathrm{transfer}}
\le
\log(1+e^{-L})
+\frac{2L}{T}\mathbb E[D_s^{-1}],
\]

before separately bounded finite-barrier corrections.

At fixed \(L\), longer duration leaves a preparation and leakage floor. Both control range and duration must increase to approach the ideal limit. A tested reset required \(0.69351730\,k_BT\), close to the expected binary-erasure work. Reset was not supplied for free.

That calculation:

## 6. What is new, what is established, and what failed

The closest current task-level comparison I found is Hasegawa’s October 2026 *Thermodynamics of Classifiers*. It already bounds retained terminal-state classification using entropy production, for time-independent generators, through a functional of the entropy-production history. The result here imposes finite kinetic contrast and common affinities, allows shared time-dependent controls, and obtains an explicit attainable endpoint cost. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

A particularly useful counterexample comes from Kolchinsky–Wolpert’s constrained-control thermodynamics: correlations can be created with arbitrarily small entropy production under shared energies when transition activities can be independently switched off. In the present comparison, selectively freezing one species supplies unbounded kinetic contrast. **Shared energies alone are insufficient; the finite-contrast condition is essential.** :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Existing complete membrane-separation optimization also uses a stronger resource: perfectly species-selective membranes. Its quasistatic limit therefore addresses a different physical control class. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

The selective primary-source search did not identify the same finite-contrast potential plus matching arbitrary-control construction. That does not establish historical priority.

The useful failures remain preserved:

- The initial traveling-wave capability was already substantially known.
- An independent timed-gate construction was matched by ordinary countercurrent transport.
- Passive-chamber retention limits did not survive as universal separation obstructions once stored pools were allowed.
- External trajectory observation can reveal different rates at equilibrium with zero entropy production in the observed system. This theory concerns information retained in the modeled physical state; it is not a universal thermodynamic price of knowledge.
- The sharp binary theorem cannot simply be multiplied by particle count in an arbitrary interacting mixture.

The scientific return is an **operational cost law for a defined physical resource—finite kinetic discrimination—and a construction that reaches its ideal limit with recording and reset accounted for**. Its strongest immediate consequence is the dilute-recovery penalty. A useful device still has to earn acceptable throughput and control cost.
