# Ordinary computational comparator: affine sources with biased innovations

31 October 2026. Focused constructive comparison, not a novelty survey or an efficient-algorithm lower bound.

## 1. Finding and resource conventions

For a known invertible n-bit affine update x -> F x + G z + beta with iid k-bit innovations, bounded k makes calibration of the innovation distribution inexpensive, but does NOT by itself make integration of an arbitrary source-diagonal quantum Hamiltonian inexpensive. The main distinction is succinct system/source access versus full matrix dimension, and finite-rate controlled error versus a limiting averaged model. Removing lambda dependence alone is not a distinctive capability: an ordinary dense method already does this at fixed full dimension.

The remaining unearned ordinary interface is an algorithm polynomial in succinct input/circuit size, the physical interaction/action scales and prescribed control count, and logarithmic in switching rate and inverse numerical error, for the retained CQ process with general noncommuting couplings. Nothing below proves such an ordinary method impossible, nor proves novelty of the existing constructive quantum receiver.

All algorithms require the innovation law either supplied or separately estimated with an explicitly propagated error. They do not validate independence, stationarity, label access or a hidden physical coupling from a finite sample. Invertibility of F ensures that every fixed-z affine map is a permutation, so every innovation law preserves the uniform label distribution. This is a useful exact structural certificate, not an estimated stationary law.

## 2. A complete ordinary rate-independent comparator at full dimension

Let d=2^n be source dimension and D the explicit system dimension. Write the exact stochastic-Liouville generator on d operator blocks. Its matrix dimension is d D^2. It includes the actual finite-rate lambda(P-I), common H0 commutator and sqrt(lambda) source-diagonal commutators. There is no moment approximation here.

For an interval, dense scaling and squaring of this finite generator computes its channel. Choose 2^s to reduce the generator norm times interval length to a bounded scale. A Taylor or other certified base approximation to accuracy O(epsilon/2^s), followed by s squarings, controls final error: exact channels have complete trace norm one, and approximate power errors obey e_next<=2e+e^2. Conversion of matrix norms to channel norms costs full-dimension factors, which add logarithmically to required bits. Thus s and precision depend logarithmically on lambda and inverse epsilon, while storage/work are polynomial in d,D and the requested bit precision. If a physical channel representation is required, the previously reviewed dense Choi positivity and marginal repair provides one with dimension-paid errors.

Compose all prescribed CQ instruments explicitly and budget error per interval. Arbitrary retained references do not require building an unbounded reference matrix: use channel-norm bounds on the explicit base input/output spaces; actual record/control dimensions or their costs must still be charged. This is a genuine full controlled-process alternative, including source-label observations, not merely reduced-state averaging. It is exponential in n and system qubit count. There is no useful succinct-access conclusion from calling it a small stochastic-Liouville equation.

The stochastic-Liouville viewpoint is ordinary. For example, [Cheng and Silbey, 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312053) derive an exact dissipative equation for a specified Gaussian white-noise quantum-computation model. That inspected abstract is not a theorem about this arbitrary affine finite-source instance; the exact finite CQ construction here follows directly from conditioning on the classical labels.

## 3. Exact Fourier closure can be large, even with one innovation bit

For chi_v(x)=(-1)^(v dot x), the backward source operator obeys exactly

P^* chi_v = (-1)^(v dot beta) nu_hat(G^T v) chi_(F^T v),

where nu_hat is the k-bit innovation characteristic function. Hence source-only moment propagation is a weighted permutation of Fourier labels. This is an elementary and useful ordinary representation; no dense transition matrix is needed to apply it to one character.

There is nevertheless no universal polynomial-size EXACT character closure. Choose F with primitive degree-n minimal polynomial, so F^T cycles all 2^n-1 nonzero vectors. Choose any nonzero one-column G; irreducibility makes the single-input pair controllable. Set beta=0 and let the one-bit innovation have nonzero mean a with |a|<=1/(8n). Its Fourier factors are 1 or a, never zero. Thus successive (P^*)^j chi_v, 0<=j<2^n-1, are nonzero multiples of distinct linearly independent characters. The source Krylov space generated by this observable has dimension 2^n-1. One can take the bounded centered Hamiltonian H_x=chi_v(x) Z.

This example lies close to the uniform-innovation exact-reset chain: n consecutive uniform innovations generate the whole label space, so P0^n=Pi, and the kernel distance is |a|<=1/(4n). It therefore obeys the common-stationary robustness hypotheses. It refutes only the proposition that bounded innovation width automatically supplies a low-dimensional exact linear moment closure. It is NOT a computational hardness result, an approximation lower bound, or evidence that a clever closed-form evaluation is impossible.

## 4. Approximate source closure is often cheap; do not confuse it with Section 3

Controllability implies that along any n consecutive iterates of a nonzero Fourier label, at least one G^T v is nonzero. Otherwise that label annihilates span{G,FG,...,F^(n-1)G}. For one biased bit the product of Fourier weights therefore decays at least as |a|^floor(j/n). A source-only character can be propagated or its Poisson equation approximated by retaining only

J = O(n [1+log(1/epsilon)/log(1/|a|)])

steps when 0<|a|<1. Uniform noise a=0 terminates after at most n steps. For a general k-bit innovation law, replace |a| by the maximum nontrivial Fourier magnitude, provided it is strictly below one; that extra mixing property is not supplied merely by saying k is bounded.

There is also a direct source-only endpoint sampler under the reviewed common-stationary error condition. Approximate long discrete histories by a fresh uniform label after O(n log(1/epsilon)) steps, using the n-step contraction. For a Poisson number of updates, distinguish a short count and a long tail; only the short count needs actual affine updates. The cutoff and finite Poisson probability computation can be paid with polynomial cutoff and logarithmic rate bits. This skips source evolution alone; it does not integrate the Hamiltonian experienced during a skipped interval.

For an explicitly sparse Fourier/Pauli expansion H_x=sum_{v in S} chi_v(x) B_v, finite sums for source correlation or Poisson-equation coefficients can likewise be built by following these damped orbits. Starting from |S| specified labels, this costs polynomial |S|,n,J and supplied system-operator arithmetic. Thus the exponential exact orbit is emphatically not a reason to dismiss a useful ordinary approximation.

The difficulty begins after repeatedly inserting the actual driven coupling. Multiplication of characters shifts labels v -> v+w for w in S, and source propagation maps them through F^T. Repeated insertions can expand the active set. An uncompressed order-l expansion has up to |S|^l branches. This is an upper-cost observation, not a lower bound: cancellations, commuting operators, special algebra, tensor methods or better algorithms can reduce it. The exact source-only orbit argument does not prove that a polynomial approximation must visit exponentially many labels.

## 5. Strong ordinary multiscale route and its missing receiving obligations

Let A=P-I and Q=I-Pi. With centered V, the standard slow/fast block calculation suggests the second-order generator on the stationary sector

H0_super + Pi V R V Pi,   R=(I-P)^(-1)|_Q = sum_{j>=0} P^j|_Q.

Indeed A_Q=-R^(-1), the quasistationary fast block is approximately R QV Pi/sqrt(lambda), and its return to the slow block yields this expression. The common-centering condition removes the order-sqrt(lambda) mean drift. For sparse supplied character couplings, Section 4 makes many of these coefficients constructively inexpensive. For a general bounded diagonal circuit, neither the coefficient averages nor an efficient representation of the resulting generator follows just from bounded innovation width. Coherent mean/block constructions and classical sampling have distinct access/cost models; numerical expectation estimation is not free.

Ordinary heterogeneous multiscale methods explicitly use short fast simulations to estimate effective drift/diffusion rather than resolve every fast jump. [Vanden-Eijnden, 2003](https://www.math.pku.edu.cn/teachers/litj/notes/stoch_topics2015/cms_1_385.pdf), inspected at the primary paper, treats both deterministic slow limits and fluctuation-driven stochastic scales. [E, Liu and Vanden-Eijnden, 2005](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/analysis-of-multiscale-methods-for-stochastic-differential-equati/), inspected through the authors' institutional abstract, proves weak and strong convergence for multiscale numerical schemes. These are substantive ordinary alternatives, not naive step-by-step trajectory simulation.

Rigorous quantum singular-perturbation machinery also predates this work. The inspected primary abstracts of [Bouten–Silberfarb](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.0686) and [Bouten–van Handel–Silberfarb](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.2276) establish strong limiting quantum stochastic dynamics and approximation/singular-perturbation results. I have not inspected enough theorem hypotheses to claim they directly deliver this exact affine CQ finite-error interface or to claim they cannot be adapted to it.

A homogenized generator alone is not yet a comparable finite-rate controlled algorithm. A receiving proof would still need: a quantitative remainder uniform over the specified CQ inputs/references; treatment of the fast initial layer; source-label output and instrument effects; repeated prescribed interventions and records; a completely positive implemented approximation; coefficient acquisition and system simulation costs; and error dependence down to arbitrary epsilon. A fixed leading-order asymptotic error, even if small at very large lambda, does not provide logarithmic inverse-precision cost for all finite lambda. A hybrid resolving the remaining moderate-rate regime may be polynomial in 1/epsilon rather than logarithmic; no universal precise exponent is claimed here without an actual remainder theorem.

## 6. Explicit retained-source boundary against simply deleting the fast variable

Even the one-bit exact refresh chain demonstrates the issue with uniform control access. Prepare a known source bit by a CQ instrument and read it after time c/lambda. Its label distribution retains an e^-c fraction of the initial-label information. A model replacing the source instantly by its stationary distribution makes a constant error in that measurement, independent of how large lambda is. This example uses no Hamiltonian and is inside the allowed prescribed CQ-control task; the prescribed spacing may depend on lambda.

This does not defeat ordinary multiscale methods with boundary-layer correction or a retained classical label. It only rules out interpreting an effective system-only generator as automatically equivalent to the retained-source process. Source-only endpoint corrections are cheap as above; what remains to be earned is their conjunction with Hamiltonian integration and arbitrary allowed controls. No history-aware classical-memory method is excluded.

## 7. Consequence-focused conclusion

The strongest established ordinary route here is exact dense CQ propagation: it already has logarithmic switching-rate/precision dependence, but polynomial cost in full source/system dimension. Bounded-width innovation calibration and efficient source endpoint skipping are also ordinary and can be genuinely useful. Sparse Fourier couplings permit cheap approximate source correlation and leading effective-generator coefficient calculations. These facts reduce any claim of leverage substantially.

The narrow remaining candidate advantage is a fully acquired succinct controlled-process implementation for finite rate, noncommuting system couplings and arbitrary numerical precision, without expanding the source/system to a dense matrix or supposing a global low-dimensional moment closure. The hidden complexity is most likely in (a) the source-diagonal Hamiltonian/control representation, (b) repeated driven Fourier mixing rather than source mixing alone, (c) retained-label/initial-layer corrections, and (d) converting effective coefficients into an implemented CP process with paid errors. Small k and a small uniform kernel error do not discharge these obligations.

This analysis neither proves an ordinary lower bound nor establishes novelty. No denied source was retried, no alternate-copy access was used, and no new estimator, multiscale solver or controlled simulator was implemented. The explicit constructions and counterexamples above delimit what the available assumptions actually buy.
