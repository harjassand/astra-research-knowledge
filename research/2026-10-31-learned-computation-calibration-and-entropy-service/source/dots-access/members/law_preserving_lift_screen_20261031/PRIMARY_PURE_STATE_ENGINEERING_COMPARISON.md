# Primary comparison: pure-state engineering is an established ordinary alternative

31 October 2026. Narrow comparison; not an exhaustive literature search or a novelty judgment.

Kraus, Büchler, Diehl, Kantian, Micheli and Zoller, *Preparation of Entangled States by Quantum Markov Processes*, arXiv0803.1463v3, derives dissipative preparation for arbitrary multipartite pure targets, discusses uniqueness, and gives analytically solvable constructions. Special structured targets allow quasi-local jumps. Consequently the mere existence of a dissipative process attracting a prescribed pure state is not a new capability of the edge/random-map constructions here. Their extra requirement is preservation of a prescribed classical diagonal process together with acquired succinct access and a quantitative usable return bound. A state-preparation generator cannot simply replace the prescribed process without checking that requirement. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1463 .

Ticozzi and Viola, *Analysis and synthesis of attractive quantum Markovian dynamics*, arXiv0809.0613, develops finite-dimensional invariant/attractive-subspace criteria and constructive Hamiltonian/output-feedback stabilization strategies. This is a strong ordinary framework for the dark-state and absence-of-invariant-subspace analysis. It does not make an exponentially large supplied matrix representation or an unacquired conditional-fibre circuit cheap by definition. An equally informed ordinary designer may nevertheless exploit precisely the same algebraic structures; no advantage over it has been proved here. Primary text: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.0613 .

The concrete comparison question is therefore operational: can one acquire a law-preserving, rapidly pure-returning completion from the actual succinct classical program with less total work than ordinary retained classical/perfect-sampling and quantum state-preparation methods? The current reverse-map result identifies the conditional inverse-fibre operation, and the edge example exposes a dimension penalty. Neither is yet a general efficient answer.
