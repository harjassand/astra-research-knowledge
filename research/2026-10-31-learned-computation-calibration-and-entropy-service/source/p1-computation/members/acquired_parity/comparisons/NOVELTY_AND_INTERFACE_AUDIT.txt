# Novelty objection and a stronger planar query family

## Verdict

The present parity-coordinate result is a sound composition of established acquisition and inference algorithms. I do not see a justified claim of a new computational principle or an advantage over the strongest ordinary retaining algorithm. The closest literature is considerably closer than ordinary graphical-model learning: finite-field ICA already learns hidden XOR coordinates; minimally complex spin models learn a preferred parity representation and sample through its inverse; knowledge compilation explicitly evaluates representations by the queries and transformations they keep tractable.

What survives is a useful, auditable end-to-end theorem: scalar experiments can acquire a representation, the compiler can recognize a tractable structural class without receiving the hidden graph, and a specified family of future queries remains tractable. A planar cutspace subclass strengthens that theorem beyond low dependency dimension or narrow trellises. It remains a synthesis of classical algorithms, not a separation from them.

## Closest established analogues

| Work | What it already supplies | Remaining distinction here |
|---|---|---|
| Yeredor, *ICA in boolean XOR mixtures* (2007) | Hidden invertible GF(2) mixing of independent nonuniform bits; identifiability and an entropy-based separation algorithm. | We query a scalar response function rather than observing mixture samples, and allow dependent learned modes. |
| de Mulatier and Marsili, *Bayesian Inference of Minimally Complex Models with Interactions of Arbitrary Order* (2020 preprint; PRE 2025) | Learns high-order parity representations with independent components. Uses binary linear algebra, fills unused dimensions uniformly, and inverts the learned gauge when sampling. | Appendix G enumerates all parity operators for exact basis selection; larger problems restrict the candidate pool or use heuristics. It does not establish the same worst-case sparse membership-query theorem. |
| Darwiche and Marquis, *A Knowledge Compilation Map* (2002) | Chooses compiled representations by succinctness and the polynomial-time queries and transformations they support, including conditioning. | Acquisition from experiments is an additional charged stage, but the query-closure framing is established. |
| Johnson, Oyen, Chertkov, and Netrapalli, *Learning Planar Ising Models* (2016) | Learns a tractable planar Ising approximation from observed correlations. | Its spin coordinates are observed; our parity masks can hide the graph behind an unknown binary change of coordinates. |
| Sparse Walsh recovery + graphic-matroid recognition + planar Ising algorithms | Together, these already produce the proposed hidden-planar compiler. | I found no paper packaging this exact input/output contract, but absence of a matching title is not evidence of a novel algorithmic advance. |

The 2018 spin-model complexity work also explicitly studies gauge-equivalent high-order interactions and loop structure. Its “stochastic complexity” is an information-theoretic model-complexity measure, so it should not be conflated with our running-time parameter.

## Recover the graph rather than supply it

After acquiring the nonzero parity masks, let `A ∈ F₂^{s×n}`, `r=rank A`, and `K=im A ⊆ F₂^s`. Assume only that `K` is a planar graph's cutspace; neither the graph nor a useful spin basis is supplied.

1. Row-reduce `Aᵀ`, remove redundant rows, and choose a column basis. Permuting edge labels accordingly gives `[I_r | D]`.
2. Solve the classical graph-realization problem for `D`: find a tree or forest whose edge-path incidence vectors are the columns of `D`, or reject. Add one nonbasis edge for each returned path.
3. This produces an edge-labelled graph `G` with `Cut(G)=K`. Test planarity and construct an embedding.

Bixby–Wagner's 1988 algorithm solves the graph-realization step in almost-linear time in the number of nonzeros of `D`; Gaussian elimination and reading the acquired masks are separately charged. The CMR implementation exposes both graph construction and the graphic/cographic tests. This removes any graph-recovery oracle from the proposed theorem.

Why equality of cutspaces follows: over GF(2), a represented matroid's fundamental circuits determine its standard-form matrix `[I|D]`. The graph construction reproduces those circuits with the same edge labels, hence reproduces the row space of `Aᵀ`, which is `im A`. Graphic realizations can be nonunique; this recovers an equivalent computational graph, not necessarily the physical causal graph. Planarity is a matroid property here: graphic plus cographic, so ambiguity does not require searching exponentially many realizations.

If `R ∈ F₂^{s×r}` is a reduced vertex-edge incidence transpose of the recovered graph, solve `A=RT`. Then `u=Tx` gives rooted hidden spin coordinates. `T` has rank `r` and can be extended to an invertible `n×n` binary transformation by adding unused coordinates. This query translation is obtained by ordinary elimination.

## A closed, useful future-query family

Allow arbitrary positive rational edge weights `w_e(0),w_e(1)`, hard observations of any subset of the acquired parities `y_e=a_ex`, further such event-probability queries, MAP, and exact sampling. Neutral edge factors may also be deleted. Do not silently extend this contract to arbitrary new factors or arbitrary linear evidence.

For hard evidence `y_e=z_e` on an edge set `F`, the following compiler is exact.

1. Check consistency: the XOR of `z_e` around every cycle of the observed subgraph must be zero. Equivalently solve `τ_u+τ_v=z_e` for `e∈F`, by a parity union-find or linear-time graph traversal.
2. Write original vertex spins as `σ=τ+σ'`. For every remaining edge, replace its weight table by

   `w'_e(b)=w_e(b+τ_u+τ_v)`.

   This is just an entry swap when the gauge difference is one.
3. On every observed edge, `σ'_u=σ'_v`. Contract the connected components of `(V,F)`; multiply the fixed observed weights into a constant. Edges that become loops contribute fixed factors. Parallel edges may be merged by multiplying their two-entry tables, while retaining their original labels and offsets for later queries.

The resulting graph is a minor of `G`, so it is planar. There are still only pairwise difference factors and no external fields. The compiler can repeat these steps after each additional acquired-coordinate observation. Deleting a neutral factor is a graph deletion; recompute the cutspace rank/fiber multiplicity when deletion disconnects a graph.

### Normalization without hidden factors of two

For a graph with `c` connected components, define the cutspace sum

\[
Q_G(w)=\sum_{y\in\operatorname{Cut}(G)}\prod_e w_e(y_e).
\]

The ordinary zero-field spin partition is `Z_spin(G,w)=2^c Q_G(w)`, since each cut vector has `2^c` spin preimages. The raw input partition is therefore

\[
Z_x(w)=2^{n-r}Q_G(w)=2^{n-r-c}Z_{\mathrm{spin}}(G,w).
\]

Under consistent observed-edge conditioning, the same relation holds with the evidence-restricted cut sum. Signed contraction preserves the number of connected components if one only removes resulting loops and merges parallel edges. An absorbed constant multiplies the resulting spin partition. If neutral edges are deleted as well, recompute the relevant component/rank counts rather than carrying an old factor forward.

### Exact inference, MAP, and sampling

Classical planar zero-field Ising methods give polynomial partition and ground-state computations, even for frustrated interactions. With rational local weights, the partition has a polynomial-size exact rational representation: a common denominator is the product of edge denominators, and its numerator is a sum of at most `2^r` bounded products. Exact rational Pfaffian arithmetic therefore avoids an uncharged real-precision assumption.

MAP with rational local weights can be implemented using the conventional planar matching reduction. If log weights are used, `O(sL+log s)` bits of log precision suffice to distinguish distinct products of at most `s` rational weights with `L`-bit numerators and denominators. This precision must be charged rather than treating arbitrary real logarithms as unit-cost primitives.

Exact sampling needs no separately assumed sampler. Sequentially expose an uncontracted learned edge, compute the exact rational probability of its two values using two conditioned planar partition sums, sample that Bernoulli exactly, and contract the selected edge. At most `r` independent choices determine a cut vector. Lift the result through `Ax=y` using `n-r` fair bits on a kernel basis. The future-query family is closed under every conditioning used by this procedure. Polynomial expected random-bit cost follows from integer branch masses of `O(n+sL)` bits; no deterministic worst-case bound on rejection-sampling bits is asserted.

Total cost is the charged sparse acquisition plus polynomial graph recognition, plus polynomial work per future query. Planar grids give a materially broader positive class than the low-`d` or low-trellis-width theorem: their number of independent cycles can be linear and their width can grow, while this compiler remains polynomial. Dense unknown parity changes can make their raw interaction supports look completely nonlocal.

## Why arbitrary linear evidence is excluded

Start with `A=I_m`, whose image is the cutspace of a tree and whose unconstrained model has independent coordinates. Given any graph `H` with `m` edges, let `C` be a cycle-space basis of `H` and impose `Cx=0`. Then the allowed `x` are exactly `Cut(H)`. With local weights `w_e(0)=1`, `w_e(1)=2`, MAP returns a maximum cut of `H`.

Thus arbitrary linear evidence can encode general MaxCut even when the acquired starting graph is a tree. Evidence on individual acquired edges is a genuine and necessary closure restriction, not interchangeable with arbitrary parity constraints. General learned vertex fields likewise fall outside the zero-field planar guarantee.

## A sharp access-interface boundary: passive data contains LPN

Fix `0<ρ<1`, let `a=(s,1) ∈ F₂ⁿ`, and write `x=(u,b)` with `u∈F₂^{n−1}`. Consider

\[
p_a(x)=2^{-n}\bigl(1+\rho(-1)^{a\cdot x}\bigr).
\]

This is a positive normalized density: `a≠0` implies `Σ_x(-1)^{a·x}=0`. Summing over `b` gives `Pr(u)=2^{-(n−1)}`, and

\[
\Pr(b\ne s\cdot u\mid u)=\frac{1-\rho}{2}.
\]

Thus passive draws from this model are *exactly* LPN examples `(u,s·u XOR noise)` at noise rate `(1−ρ)/2`. Recovering the unknown mask `a` from polynomially many passive examples would solve search LPN for the same noise rate. The conventional search-LPN hardness assumption therefore rules out a blanket polynomial-time passive mask-recovery guarantee. This is an assumption-based computational boundary, not an unconditional NP-hardness theorem. Pietrzak's 2012 exposition defines this assumption and explicitly distinguishes average-case LPN hardness from worst-case NP-hardness.

Yet the energy is extraordinarily simple:

\[
\begin{aligned}
E_a(x)&=-\log p_a(x)=c+\theta(-1)^{a\cdot x},\\
c&=n\log 2-\tfrac12\log(1-\rho^2),\\
\theta&=-\tfrac12\log\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}.
\end{aligned}
\]

There is exactly one nonconstant Walsh mode, with dependency dimension zero. Once the mask is known, its weighted partition is `2^{n−1}(w(0)+w(1))`, and MAP and sampling are elementary. Trivial downstream computation therefore does not imply feasible acquisition from passive samples.

By contrast, scalar energy queries at `0,e₁,…,e_n` recover the complete mask in `n+1` queries:

\[
a_i=0\iff E_a(e_i)=E_a(0),\qquad
E_a(e_i)-E_a(0)=\log\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\quad\text{when }a_i=1.
\]

The contrast does not require infinite measurement precision. For fixed known `ρ`, the gap is a fixed positive constant; errors strictly smaller than one quarter of that gap permit deterministic threshold separation of the two cases. Preparing each full `n`-step experiment still costs `n`, if that is the physical experimental interface.

The distinction is computational rather than information-theoretic. For each nonzero mask `v`, `E_{p_a}[(-1)^{v·x}]` equals `ρ` when `v=a` and zero otherwise. An exhaustive search over all masks therefore identifies `a` using `O(ρ^{-2}(n+log(1/δ)))` samples with confidence `1−δ`, but takes exponential search time. No claim is made that every possible approximate generative-learning task for this family requires explicit mask recovery.

**Executed small check.** `lpn_interface_check.py` enumerates all 32 inputs for `n=5`, `s=(1,0,1,1)`, and `ρ=1/3`. The density takes value `1/24` at 16 inputs and `1/48` at 16 inputs, sums exactly to one, gives every `u` probability `1/16`, and gives conditional noise probability exactly `1/3`. Six energy queries recover the five-bit mask. An 80-decimal-digit evaluation checks the displayed energy formula with maximum residual `6×10^{-80}` and all other nonconstant Walsh coefficients below `3.125×10^{-80}`. The density and conditional checks use exact rational arithmetic; the logarithmic checks are numerical. This verifies the identity, not the LPN hardness assumption. The complete output is `lpn_interface_check.json`.

This is the decisive interface comparison: energy-query access buys information that the passive learner cannot obtain efficiently under a standard assumption, despite an identical one-mode representation and trivial subsequent inference. It is a repackaging of LPN for this benchmark, not a new hardness theorem.

## Honest research positioning

This extension proves more than “we can predict the energy”: it constructs a previously unknown graph and supplies an explicit, query-closed inference interface. It does not beat an ordinary retaining comparator. That comparator can use exactly the same sparse Walsh learner, graph-realization algorithm, Pfaffian/matching methods, and conditional sampler, and should tie our theorem.

I would retain this as a clean benchmark and a fully charged positive construction. I would reject a broad novelty claim unless subsequent work provides a new acquisition guarantee, a new efficiently recognizable query-closed class, improved robustness under a realistic experimental access model, or a theorem unavailable from these established components. A name for the composition or a comparison with episodic lookup would not suffice.
