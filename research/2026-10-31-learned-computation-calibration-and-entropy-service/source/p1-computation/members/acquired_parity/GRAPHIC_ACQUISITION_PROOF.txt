ACQUIRED GRAPHIC STRUCTURE FROM HIDDEN PARITY ENERGIES
Research checkpoint, 2026-10-31. Local executable: graphic_acquisition.py.

STATUS AND CONTRIBUTION

This is a constructive composition of established sparse Fourier acquisition,
binary linear algebra, graphic-matroid structure, and classical exact Ising
inference. It does not claim a new Pfaffian algorithm, a new general graphic
recognition algorithm, or historical priority for the composition. It earns an
actual hidden graph and embedding from the acquired parity support matrix,
without passing a generating graph or latent coordinate basis to the learner.
The local executable implements the restricted recognizer and an exact rational
Pfaffian backend itself. A strong ordinary retained variable-elimination solver
receives the same acquired graph and wins the compact execution comparisons.

1. INPUT INTERFACE AND AFFINE-CODE REDUCTION

Suppose paid chosen-input scalar experiments have acquired the distinct nonzero
Walsh masks a_1,...,a_s and coefficients of

  E(x) = theta_0 + sum_j theta_j (-1)^(a_j x),  x in F_2^n.

Let A have the a_j as rows. Acquisition is a separate, charged operation; this
file assumes its output is verified by the parent acquisition construction,
not supplied by the fixture. A later task supplies nonnegative rational factors
w_j(0),w_j(1), a linear evidence matrix C, and a right-hand side z. Its target is

  Z_C(z) = sum_{x:Cx=z} product_j w_j(a_j x).

The factors may be Boltzmann weights, but rational arithmetic is not free exact
exponentiation. For integer theta_j and rational fugacity t>0 one may use
w_j(b)=t^(-theta_j (-1)^b); the bit length of those explicitly supplied weights
is included. Binary-encoded huge exponents do not have free expanded outputs.

If Cx=z is inconsistent, Z_C(z)=0. Otherwise compute a particular x_0 and a
kernel basis N for C. Put y_0=A x_0 and L=A ker C, a binary code in F_2^s.
The image of the feasible x is exactly y_0+L. Every such y has exactly

  2^(n-rank([A;C]))

preimages. Therefore

  Z_C(z) = 2^(n-rank([A;C])) Q(L,y_0,w),
  Q(L,y_0,w) = sum_{y in y_0+L} product_j w_j(y_j).

Write K for any full-row-rank generator of L, and H for a full-row-rank
annihilator. Its dimension is r=rank([A;C])-rank C and its codimension is
d=s-r=s+rank C-rank([A;C]). Codeword enumeration costs 2^r, total-syndrome DP
costs 2^d, and minimal-trellis DP can be substantially smaller than either.
None of these ranks is a complete tractability classification.

2. EXACT GRAPH RECOGNITION WITHOUT A GRAPH OR DECOMPOSITION ORACLE

Restricted recognition theorem. Fix a constant Delta. There is a deterministic
algorithm taking an exact binary matrix K with s labeled columns which:

  (i) on acceptance produces a simple, 3-vertex-connected graph G with at least
      four vertices, exactly one graph edge for every input column, and an
      exact certificate rowspace(D_G)=rowspace(K), where D_G is incidence;
  (ii) accepts every K whose column matroid is that of such a graph of maximum
       degree at most Delta;
  (iii) runs in polynomial time for fixed Delta and uses no hidden vertex basis.

Its failure outcome is UNKNOWN outside the stated promise. It is not a complete
test for all graphic matroids. Loops and parallel edges, and graphs failing the
explicit 3-connectivity check, are outside this executed scope.

Algorithm. Compute an annihilator H of K. Enumerate every support S of size at
most Delta for which H 1_S=0. Thus 1_S belongs to rowspace(K). Let r=rank K.
Retain S as a cocircuit precisely when

  rank(K restricted to E\S) = r-1.

Indeed, the space of rowspace(K) vectors supported inside S has dimension
r-rank(K restricted to E\S). Since it contains 1_S, dimension one is precisely
support minimality over F_2. If its dimension were at least two, subtracting a
different nonzero vector would cancel some but not all coordinates of 1_S.

Retain such a cocircuit as a star candidate only if deleting S leaves the
represented matroid connected. This test is implemented by a column basis and
its fundamental circuits: join all elements in every fundamental circuit with
a disjoint-set data structure. The resulting components are exactly the matroid
components. Loops and coloops remain singleton components. An empty deletion is
not called connected by the implementation.

Make one prospective graph vertex per retained candidate. Every input column
must belong to exactly two candidates; those become its two endpoints. Reject
duplicate endpoint pairs, loops, fewer than four vertices, disconnected output,
or output that disconnects after deleting one or two vertices. Finally recompute
the binary incidence rowspace and compare its canonical reduced basis exactly
with that of K. This last check gates every acceptance, even on unpromised input.

Proof of completeness. In a simple 3-vertex-connected graph, every vertex star
is a bond. Deleting a vertex leaves a 2-vertex-connected graph on at least three
vertices, so its cycle matroid is connected. Thus every vertex star is a
nonseparating cocircuit. Conversely a non-star bond partitions the vertices
into two connected sides, each containing at least two vertices and therefore
at least one edge. After deleting that bond, the matroid has edge-bearing
components on both sides, so it is disconnected. Hence the nonseparating
cocircuits are exactly the vertex stars. Each has size at most Delta, and every
edge belongs to exactly its two endpoint stars. The recovered graph is the
original one up to a vertex relabeling, with the input edge labels preserved.
Triangles do not create an exception: K_4 is included, and deleting a vertex
leaves a triangle whose cycle matroid is connected.

Paid enumeration. The implemented version uses a meet-in-the-middle sieve.
For each weight k, split an ordered support after floor(k/2). Its two half
supports have the same H-syndrome exactly when their union is a rowspace word.
Require every left index to precede every right index to emit each word once.
Half lists have O(s^ceil(Delta/2)) entries, with exact O(s)-bit syndromes.
Worst-case matching-pair work is still O(s^Delta), not assumed small for all
inputs. Each candidate undergoes polynomial-time rank/connectivity checks.
A conservative O(s^(Delta+3)) bit-operation bound suffices. The program records
half subsets and half-pair comparisons, rather than silently charging only the
accepted stars. A failed attempt incurs these acquisition costs too.

3. ACQUIRING A PLANAR EMBEDDING IN THE EXECUTED SUBCLASS

For a simple 3-connected cubic planar G whose facial lengths are at most a fixed
Delta, run the preceding recognizer on K with cap 3 and on its annihilator H
with cap Delta. The latter recognizes the planar dual, which is again simple
and 3-connected. All feature/edge labels remain aligned.

For each recovered dual vertex, its incident edge labels must trace a simple
cycle in the recovered primal. These cycles are the prospective faces. Check
that every edge occurs exactly twice, their orientations can be chosen with
opposite traversal on shared edges, their vertex incidences induce a single
cyclic rotation at every vertex, and V-E+F=2. Check the face cycles generated by
that rotation. The result is an explicit connected orientable sphere embedding,
not a planarity flag. These conditions also make acceptance sound without
assuming either recognizer's promise in advance.

This bounded-face route is intentionally a self-contained executed substitute
for a full graphic recognizer plus a general planar-embedding library. It does
not say that graphs with large faces are hard. Established general algorithms
can remove the degree/face restrictions; Section 8 gives a concrete import.

4. THE GRAPHIC GENERATOR BRANCH IS A ZERO-FIELD ISING MODEL

For a graph incidence matrix D, rowspace(D) is its cut space:

  y_e = sigma_u xor sigma_v for e={u,v}.

If G has c connected components, each cut vector has exactly 2^c spin
representatives. Thus the acquired rowspace certificate gives

  Q(L,y_0,w) = 2^(-c) sum_sigma product_e
                     w_e(y_0,e xor sigma_u xor sigma_v).

Absorb y_0 by swapping the two edge weights where its bit is one. This creates
arbitrary frustrated edge interactions, but no vertex fields. For the connected
executed class c=1. Do not omit this factor of two, or the earlier physical-input
fiber multiplicity.

5. EXECUTED EXACT FISHER/PFAFFIAN KERNEL

For every adjusted factor let a_e=(w_e(0)+w_e(1))/2 and
b_e=(w_e(0)-w_e(1))/2. Nonnegative nonzero factors guarantee a_e>0, including
single-state clamps. Fourier expansion of the edge factor gives

  Z_Ising = 2^V product_e a_e * sum_{F even} product_{e in F}(b_e/a_e).

Here "even" means that every vertex has even F-degree. The ratios may be
negative; the polynomial identity is exact and no probabilistic interpretation
is imposed on the decorated dimer weights.

At every cubic vertex, put three port vertices around one center, forming a
planar K_4. Assign center-to-port weight 1 and port-triangle weight 1/3. Link
opposite ports across each original edge with weight b_e/a_e. If zero incident
external edges are selected by a perfect matching, the three internal perfect
matchings have total weight 1. If two are selected, the remaining port matches
the center with weight 1. One or three selected external edges are impossible.
Consequently the perfect-matching polynomial of the decorated graph is exactly
the even-subgraph polynomial above. The decorated graph has 4V vertices and
6V+E edges, with a sphere embedding constructed from the acquired rotations.

Acquire an odd clockwise orientation by correcting face parities along a dual
spanning tree. The code checks the final parity of every face. On an even-sized
closed planar graph, the outer parity is compatible as well. The classical
Kasteleyn theorem then makes all perfect-matching terms in the Pfaffian have
the same orientation sign. Calibrate that sign using the explicitly known
matching with no external edges and, in every gadget, center--port0 and
port1--port2. No true partition value is supplied for sign calibration.

The implementation computes the Pfaffian by exact rational 2x2 elimination,
including simultaneous row/column swaps when a pivot vanishes. It returns

  Q = 2^(V-1) product_e a_e * signed_Pfaffian(Kasteleyn_matrix).

The backend takes O(s^3) rational operations and O(s^2) stored rationals. With
explicit rational input weights of total bit length B, minors have bit length
polynomial in s+B; exact elimination uses ratios of such minors, so the bit
cost is polynomial as well. Intermediate signed cancellation is real; finite
precision numerical stability is not implied by this exact theorem.

6. A TASK FAMILY CLOSED UNDER LEARNED-FEATURE EVIDENCE

Evidence of the form a_e x=b fixes one acquired feature y_e. Set the disallowed
factor value to zero and keep the same graph, gadget and orientation. The same
Pfaffian construction remains valid because a_e>0 when at least one choice is
allowed. A factor with both values zero returns mass zero immediately. An
inconsistent collection around a cycle returns exactly zero through the kernel.
Thus any number of acquired-feature clamps is handled by a retained polynomial
kernel. Reweighting the existing features is handled by the same structure.

Conditional feature probabilities are ratios of two such sums. Exact sampling
can sequentially clamp features using those ratios, then solve Ax=y and draw a
uniform element of its affine fiber. The number of later operations is
polynomial; it has no inverse-event-probability rejection factor. Rational
Bernoulli draws must charge their integer-bit work. This says nothing about the
physical availability of an experimental sampler; it is a learned computational
sampler under the acquired model and scalar energy interface.

Arbitrary new physical-coordinate evidence can produce a different code L and
destroy graphical or planar structure. A fresh recognizer attempt or a general
code solver must then pay the resulting cost. Closure under acquired-feature
evidence is not closure under all questions about the original x variables.

7. OPERATION-SPECIFIC TRACTABILITY MAP AND STRONGER COMPARATORS

A. For any order pi of the s code coordinates, the minimal feasible syndrome
state space at a cut has dimension

  w_i = rank(H_prefix)+rank(H_suffix)-rank(H).

The states form an affine intersection im(H_prefix) intersect
(b+im(H_suffix)). Gaussian elimination computes the intersection and actual
transitions. Sum-product or min-plus on this trellis costs O(s 2^max_i w_i)
arithmetic operations up to polynomial bit overhead, improving total-d rank.
Finding the globally best order is not free; classical constructive FPT
algorithms exist, while optimizing trellis width generally is NP-hard.

Concrete connected example: independent hidden parities u_0,...,u_m plus terms
u_i xor u_(i+1). All terms are distinct and nonzero; s=2m+1, rank=m+1, d=m.
A hidden chain has constant-width exact transfer matrices even though both
dimension and codimension grow. No claim based solely on 2^d should omit this.

B. Generator graphic: rowspace(K)=cutspace(G). Planar G supports polynomial
partition and MAP algorithms, even for linear d and growing graph treewidth.
Nonplanar G does not in general give exact polynomial partition or MAP.

C. Check matrix graphic: rowspace(H)=cutspace(G), so the homogeneous code is
the cycle space of G. An affine coset has a prescribed odd-degree vertex set.
For rational local costs c_e(0),c_e(1), first pick the locally cheapest value q_e
and let delta_e=|c_e(1)-c_e(0)|. Correct the resulting vertex syndrome by a
minimum delta-cost T-join. Compute shortest-path distances between T vertices,
find a minimum-weight perfect matching on that metric graph, and xor the
corresponding paths. This is a classical polynomial MAP algorithm on ANY graph,
not requiring planarity. A particular coset representative translates the
original H-syndrome into the recovered incidence coordinates without needing
an unknown row-basis transformation. General cycle-space partition remains a
weighted even-subgraph sum, already containing the general Ising partition
problem. Efficient optimization does not supply efficient exact summation.

The local executable does not implement a general blossom algorithm and does
not claim to have executed this general T-join branch. The parent trellis solver
does execute MAP in its admitted families. The graph branch here executes
partition, feature clamps, and the graph/embedding acquisition.

D. Retained conventional variable elimination must receive the acquired graph.
The implemented min-fill baseline acquires its own elimination order and exactly
eliminates the binary spin factors. It is faster than this pure-Python rational
Pfaffian backend on all three compact examples. This does not remove the
Pfaffian branch's polynomial worst-case guarantee, and that guarantee does not
establish practical superiority on these examples.

8. PRIMARY SOURCES AND EXPLICIT SOFTWARE IMPORTS

All sources below were actually retrieved in this investigation. Full primary
implementation documentation was read where noted. No full third-party package
is bundled with this checkpoint.

  * Robert J. McEliece (1996), On the BCJR trellis for linear block codes:
    https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/rr13a-7te87
    Classical minimal trellis and edge-linear Viterbi runtime; abstract read.

  * Navin Kashyap, Matroid Pathwidth and Code Trellis Complexity:
    https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1384
    Abstract read; establishes the code/matroid relation and NP-hard ordering.

  * Jeong, Kim, Oum, The "art of trellis decoding" is fixed-parameter tractable:
    https://arxiv.org/html/1507.02184v4
    Constructive FPT layout algorithm, including paid layout acquisition.

  * Costalonga, Non-Separating Cocircuits and Graphicness in Matroids:
    https://arxiv.org/html/1211.5823v1
    Introduction explicitly records the classical vertex-star property used
    above and related graphic-recognition machinery. Its later conjectural
    research claims are not used in this proof.

  * Bixby and Wagner (1988), An Almost Linear-Time Algorithm for Graph Realization:
    https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.13.1.99
    Primary abstract read; the older Rice full-text mirror was inaccessible.

  * CMR 1.3.0 primary implementation documentation, read in full:
    https://discopt.github.io/cmr/graphic.html
    https://discopt.github.io/cmr/graphic_8h.html
    A concrete general import is: first reduce K over F_2 to [I|M], retaining
    the column permutation. Run `cmr-graphic M -G graph -T tree`, or call
    CMRgraphicTestMatrix with non-null graph, forestEdges and coforestEdges
    outputs. Forest edges are indexed by rows of M; coforest edges by its
    columns, preserving the original feature labels. For k nonzeros in M,
    documentation gives O(k alpha(k,r)) runtime for the Bixby-Wagner procedure,
    in addition to the paid binary Gaussian preprocessing. Recompute incidence
    rowspace equality before accepting. This complete algorithm can remove the
    restricted recognizer's degree/3-connectivity limitations. It was NOT
    executed in this environment, and its full runtime is not part of the
    measured table.

  * Sage 10.8 primary documentation:
    https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/matroids/sage/matroids/linear_matroid.html
    BinaryMatroid.is_graphic returns a boolean using Geelen-Gerards equations;
    its documentation warns it is not necessarily constructive, particularly
    in the presence of 2-separations. It is not a graph-acquisition substitute.

  * Schraudolph and Kamenetsky, Efficient Exact Inference in Planar Ising Models:
    https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.4401
    Full primary PDF retrieved and relevant Sections 3-4 read. It already
    gives exact planar MAP, partition and edge marginals; structural
    half-Kasteleyn matrices are explicitly reused across changes in edge costs.
    The associated implementation is at https://nic.schraudolph.org/isinf/ .
    It was inspected, not installed or redistributed.

  * Goldberg and Jerrum, Approximating the Tutte polynomial of a binary matroid
    and other related combinatorial polynomials:
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.5234
    Primary abstract read; preserves the distinction between general binary
    code inference and tractable graphical subfamilies.

9. EXECUTED EVIDENCE AND LIMITS

GRAPHIC_RESULTS.json contains full exact values, timings, and enumeration counts.
Fixtures construct a graph to generate a dense hidden matrix; only that matrix
is passed into the recognizer. The scalar oracle acquisition is performed by
the parent's separate end-to-end integration, not by this standalone fixture.

  cube: 12 terms, rank 7, d 5; acquired graph 8 vertices; Pfaffian dimension 32.
  truncated octahedron: 36 terms, rank 23, d 13; graph 24; Pfaffian dimension 96.
  truncated icosahedron: 90 terms, rank 59, d 31; graph 60; Pfaffian dimension 240.

The cube agrees with direct codeword enumeration and independent dual-code
enumeration. The 24-vertex case agrees with independent 8192-term dual-code
enumeration. All three agree with ordinary exact min-fill variable elimination.
Compatible clamps and an inconsistent face-parity clamp are verified on the
cube. The Fano matrix is rejected. All verification uses exact fractions.

Representative run: the 60-vertex case acquired its graph in 0.064 seconds and
its planar embedding in 0.181 seconds; Pfaffian contraction took 3.16 seconds,
while min-fill elimination took 0.113 seconds at width 12. The latter is the
compact execution winner. Timings are small single-process observations, not a
performance study. The saved JSON has the precise later run. No 2^31 dual sum
was attempted or portrayed as executed. General graph recognition, blossom
matching, noisy support acquisition, and physical experiments remain outside
the executed graph branch.

The reusable result is a certified route from acquired global energy characters
to a graph and a task family closed under feature clamps. It has consequential
scope as a computational possibility, but no demonstrated new scientific
application or superiority over retaining conventional inference is claimed.
