## 1. Shared baseline sample and unknown noise rank

For the promised kernel Y=Fx+beta+eta, every fresh eta is independent uniform on one fixed unknown linear subspace U. Conditional on the first zero-input output Y0, its eta0 is a fixed member of U. Each difference Dj=eta_j+eta0 is consequently independent uniform on U. Crucially this conditional joint distribution is the same for every Y0; the shared baseline is not introducing hidden dependence in this model. This argument would fail for general nonuniform or correlated noise and is not transferred to such models.

With d=dim U, failure of the sample span to equal U implies that one of the 2^d-1 nonzero linear functionals vanishes on all M samples. Each such event has probability 2^{-M}. Thus the union bound gives (2^d-1)2^{-M}<=2^{n-M}<=delta/2 for M=n+ceil(log2(2/delta)). If d=0, recovery is deterministic. No estimate of d or optional stopping rule is needed; n is the supplied upper bound and the sample count is fixed.

The zero-row quotient and the rank-zero sample-span branches are correctly specified. A right section is taken only on the quotient's actual row space, not by inverting a singular square matrix.

## 2. Exact global law on the recovery event

On E={Uhat=U}, the quotient removes the noise in every basis-state observation exactly. The learned quotient columns are RF e_i and the quotient offset is R beta. Any right-section lift therefore differs from the true F and beta only by vectors in U. The learned noise image is precisely U, with the same uniform distribution.

For every x, the learned and true transition laws are consequently the same uniform distribution on the same affine coset. This is global equality of the entire kernel on one event, not an approximation checked only at the queried states. The basis queries require no further confidence union bound because their noise is annihilated exactly on E. Original parameters inside a fibre and original coin labels are not identifiable and are not claimed recovered.

## 3. No false controllability, including failed recovery transcripts

Always Uhat is contained in U. Even off E, the quotient equations imply

    Fhat e_i - F e_i in U,   betahat-beta in U.

Indeed Zi+Y0=F e_i+eta_i+eta0, and the discrepancy between a chosen section lift and this vector lies in Uhat. Let V be the true controllability span generated by U under F. It contains U and is F-invariant. Since the image of Fhat-F lies in U, V is also Fhat-invariant. As it contains Uhat, it contains the learned reachable span Vhat. Thus the learned procedure cannot admit a truly uncontrollable kernel on ANY promised transcript.

On E, adding an image-in-U feedback perturbation does not change the smallest invariant space containing U: both F and Fhat generate the same reachable span. Therefore a truly controllable kernel admits on E, with admission probability at least 1-delta/2. Dividing the bad-event bound by this lower bound yields

    Pr(E^c | admission) <= (delta/2)/(1-delta/2) <= delta.

For a truly uncontrollable kernel the admission event is empty, so no conditional execution statement is asserted. Rejection of a controllable kernel can occur on E^c and is not a deterministic diagnosis of uncontrollability or model failure.

The final source correctly binds the essential statistical qualification: this is a probability over the admitted training transcripts for a fixed unknown promised kernel. It is not a posterior guarantee of correctness for EACH particular observed transcript, nor a confidence bound preserved by conditioning on an arbitrarily rare later outcome. Numerical execution error may be uniform per transcript; statistical model-recovery confidence is not thereby pointwise.

## 4. Public reuse and controlled-process error

The learned exact description is an algebraic generator for all states, not a cache that resamples only training outputs. On E, a consumer may publicly inspect the transcript and learned model and choose future allowed operations from them: equality of the full kernel still holds for every such selection. Prescribed-slot instruments must retain the diagonal-algebra requirements of the classical source task. This gives true classical controlled-process equality on E, including allowed system/reference correlations, without identifying an original coherent quantum dilation.

A single global recovery event supports arbitrarily many finite reuses without a factor equal to the number of future transitions or tasks multiplying delta. This is an event-level assertion, not zero cumulative numerical error for infinitely many simulations. Execution budgets still compose and must be allocated separately.

For a fixed finite consumer, compare experiments carrying the SAME training transcript and admission flag. On good admitted transcripts, only the numerical error epsilon_exec remains. On bad admitted transcripts, full trace-norm discrepancy is at most two. Averaging gives the stated conditional bound epsilon_exec+2delta. Without conditioning, using the same reject/accept convention, it gives epsilon_exec+delta from Pr(E^c)<=delta/2. Half-trace-distance or classical total variation uses the corresponding halved statistical term. This reasoning permits transcript-dependent consumers but does not permit rare-postselection amplification to be ignored.

## 6. General mean block versus numerical mean

The ordinary mean-block construction is valid. For an acquired block encoding of K/b and acquired preparation of psi, prepare an auxiliary source in psi, apply that block on system plus the auxiliary source, and unprepare. The simultaneous success block is Hbar/b, where Hbar=(I tensor <psi|)K(I tensor |psi>). This does not require numerical evaluation of the matrix entries or normalized postselection. A two-term LCU then encodes K-Hbar tensor I with normalization at most 2b.

Nevertheless the same physical Hamiltonian must retain sqrt(lambda)Hbar. The system action, access and approximation costs of that term remain real, unless an explicitly known scalar identity part is removed or another efficient implementation is separately earned. Compression does not automatically certify zero mean or provide a sharper normalization. The counting difficulty of numerical means of arbitrary Boolean diagonal functions does not prevent this mean BLOCK encoding when the function's coupling circuit is already acquired.

General block compression is an ordinary quantum operation on the newly chosen realization. It does not remove the label-diagonal coupling restriction needed to equate that realization with the original CLASSICAL source process. Non-diagonal couplings or arbitrary coherent source controls define a different quantum task unless an additional equivalence is proved.
