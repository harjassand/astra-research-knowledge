INDEPENDENT AUDIT: FINITE COLD-CARRIER PUMPING AND TWO CHARGE READS
2026-10-31. Audit by the photon/resource branch; no optical artifacts changed.

VERDICT

The proposed recurrence and the two-charge-read singlet/triplet cycle are
valid under the stated full-isometry and fine-operation hypotheses. No reset
of the unknown source or trace over earlier emitted helpers is required.
The first capture miss has an amplitude cost; final return failure has a
probability cost ONLY because the second charge read explicitly removes
success/failure charge coherence. One global isometry error bound permits
a slightly sharper pumping recurrence than the one proposed.

1. STATE SPACE AND FULL-HISTORY ASSUMPTIONS

Let P_B and P_F be orthogonal projectors onto transient and sink charge
spaces, respectively, on the active optical/electronic degrees of freedom.
All statements below tensor the identity on an arbitrary reference, the
quantum source, and the FULL collection of previously emitted helpers.
Those earlier helpers may remain coherent and entangled with the source.

The nth contact introduces a fresh helper in the known state |0>. Its
outgoing state is retained. Write V_n for the resulting ideal isometry and
W_n for the actual isometry. Assume, uniformly on the admitted input domain,

 P_B V_n P_F = 0,
 ||P_B V_n P_B|| <= a < 1,
 ||W_n-V_n|| <= e.

The projections at the output are understood to include the identity on
the newly emitted helper. The domain bounds must apply to the ACTUAL
source/reference/history state at each step, including states conditioned
by the subsequent charge reads. A source-domain invariance argument or
an explicit admitted-history envelope remains necessary where the
Hamiltonian estimate was originally proved only on a restricted domain.

These assumptions concern the full isometry, not only its reduced channel
or the helper's averaged transition probability. A reduced success
probability alone does not imply the coherent recurrence used below.

2. PUMPING RECURRENCE WITH COHERENT EMISSION HISTORY

Purify any input state, including the source and reference, and decompose

 |psi_n> = |psi_B> + |psi_F>,
 ||psi_B|| = sqrt(p_n),  ||psi_F|| = sqrt(1-p_n).

The proposed bound follows by applying the ideal/actual difference to the
two components separately:

 sqrt(p_(n+1)) <= (a+e)sqrt(p_n)+e sqrt(1-p_n)
                <= (a+e)sqrt(p_n)+e.

Hence, for r=a+e<1 and p_0<=1,

 p_N <= [r^N+e(1-r^N)/(1-r)]^2
      <= [r^N+e/(1-r)]^2.

Cap every probability bound at one. No intermediate charge measurement,
source reset, independent source samples, or helper trace was used.

SHARPER FORM. Because the stated hypothesis is a GLOBAL isometry norm
bound, one may instead keep the error action on the entire state:

 ||P_B W_n psi_n|| <= ||P_B V_n P_B psi_n||
                      +||P_B(W_n-V_n)psi_n||
                   <= a sqrt(p_n)+e.

Thus the stronger estimate is

 p_N <= [a^N sqrt(p_0)+e(1-a^N)/(1-a)]^2.

This only needs a<1. If the available microscopic calculation supplies
separate block error bounds rather than the global norm bound, retain the
original a+e recurrence. Neither result says arbitrary contact errors
disappear: they permit a coherent leakage floor of order [e/(1-a)]^2.

3. CAPTURE AND THE TWO-EDGE RETURN

For capture, use B=span{|S>} and F=span{|D_L>,|D_R>}. A fresh helper can
couple |S,0> to |D_L,1> while leaving |D_L,0> and |D_R,0> invariant in the
ideal contact. An ON contact need not be monotone during the contact:
Rabi recrossing is included in V_n, and only its endpoint contraction is
required. The fresh |0> of the next contact makes the already loaded sink
absorbing at the next step. The complete triplet spin factor is dark.

For return, a single coherent simultaneous coupling of both D states to
one |S,1> channel generally leaves a dark D superposition. This is the
previous charge-trap obstruction and must be preserved.

Use two ordered contacts instead:

 V_L: |D_L,0> -> transient D_L component + |S,1> component,
      |D_R,0> stationary, |S,0> stationary;

 V_R: |D_R,0> -> transient D_R component + |S,1> component,
      |D_L,0> stationary, |S,0> stationary.

The two contacts use different fresh helpers. Their successful outputs
are flagged by orthogonal helper states |1,0> and |0,1>. The transient
macrostep operator on span{D_L,D_R} is block diagonal: each input charge
undergoes its one contracting edge and the other stationary edge.
Consequently its norm is at most max(a_L,a_R), not a_L*a_R. This remains
true for arbitrary D_L/D_R superpositions entangled with the source.

The orthogonal route records permit a valid isometric transfer from two
orthogonal input charge states to the same S charge. They are not erased
or coherently added as a single outlet. This is why the D-minus trap is
absent. Telescoping the actual two isometries gives macrostep error

 e_macro <= e_L+e_R+e_ramps,

with e_ramps the explicitly bounded additional operations. Substitute
this e_macro and max(a_L,a_R) in the full-history recurrence.

4. WHY THE CAPTURE COST IS sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss

After the capture pump, perform a complete charge read. D_L or D_R implies
the singlet branch; the remaining (1,1) charge is labeled triplet. A true
triplet is (1,1), but a missed singlet also remains (1,1). Therefore that
charge outcome does NOT remove the coherence between the true triplet
and the missed-singlet amplitude. Its norm is at most sqrt(p_miss).

A safe complete-instrument comparison first removes that residual S/T
coherence, paying at most sqrt(p_miss), then relabels/completes the missed
singlet probability, paying at most p_miss. This yields

 epsilon_capture <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss.

Constants can be tightened for particular norm conventions and inputs;
the proposed expression is conservative. The argument includes every
fine helper record and arbitrary source/reference entanglement.

Each fine operation on the triplet factor must be I_T tensor M, with the
S branch a logical rank-one map. Merely preserving total-spin labels or
commuting with total spin does not give this property. Spin-dependent
phases, source spin exchange, and omitted orbitals require their separate
complete error allowances.

5. WHY RETURN COSTS p_return ONLY AFTER THE SECOND CHARGE READ

After a D outcome of the first charge read, the branch is already known
to be singlet. Pump back using the two-edge macrostep. Before the final
charge measurement a purified return output can have the form

 |Psi> = |S> |u> + |D_failure>,
 ||D_failure||^2 = p <= p_return,

where |u> and the failure vector retain all source, reference, and helper
degrees of freedom. Even though S is a rank-one logical state, the charge
coherence between these terms is physical. Its distance from a state with
that coherence removed can scale as sqrt(p), not p. Logical rank one
does not justify dropping charge coherence while retaining the field.

The SECOND complete charge read explicitly pinches the S-versus-D charge
sectors and records the outcome. Its ideal output has classical success
and failure branches:

 |success><success| tensor |S,u><S,u|
 + |failure><failure| tensor rho_D.

The failure branch has trace at most p_return. Replacing that branch by
an ideal valid-code singlet completion changes the full output by at most
p_return in half trace/diamond distance. Prior helper outputs can remain
coherent throughout; the newly measured charge outcome is intentionally
classical. One does not need to measure or erase the helper route records.

This comparison concerns the COMPLETE instrument, with its failure record.
Do not silently condition on success and normalize. A normalized
postselected channel needs its own success-probability denominator and
retained-source comparison. Nor does the probability-cost statement hold
if the final detector field is retained coherently without its measured
charge record: that would leave the success/failure charge coherence in
the global output and restore an amplitude cost.

6. TWO READS SUFFICE, WITH THE FOLLOWING RESOURCE CONTRACT

Let each physical charge read be within delta_Q of the ideal charge
instrument in half diamond norm, including its entire measured record,
the source, an arbitrary reference, and all prior coherent helpers.
Adaptive continuation after the first read is a channel, so contraction
and a two-term hybrid argument charge at most 2 delta_Q. No charge read
between the cold-carrier contacts is needed.

The complete unconditioned singlet/triplet cycle then has the safe bound

 epsilon_ST <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss+p_return+2 delta_Q+eta_other.

Here eta_other includes the actual loading, routing, helper preparation,
contact/ramp modeling not already paid in e, leakage outside the admitted
orbital/source domain, spin-symmetry breaking over the FULL elapsed time,
and any omitted source-independent-outlet errors. Avoid charging the same
contact approximation twice: its effect on pumping population has already
entered e, while any logical disturbance not controlled by the fine
factorization needs a separate term.

The ideal comparison permits source/helper backaction conditioned on the
singlet or triplet outcome. It does not assert an unchanged source. Its
meaning is identical action on every logical vector inside each retained
logical sector, on every fine history. The protocol is complete as an
approximate ST instrument with an explicit return-failure output; it is
not an exactly deterministic same-charge return and not a conditional
success claim with discarded failures.

The preparations of the fresh helper |0>, the two distinct return contacts,
their complete timing, and both finite charge reads are maintained
operations. They are compatible with an unprepared source, but are not
supplied by calling that source unprepared.

7. ADDITIONAL AUDIT: SHORT SCALAR TRANSFER WITH LARGE CONDITIONAL DETUNING

For an active two-charge block with scalar off-diagonal amplitude a>0 and
conditional source Hamiltonians H_E(t), H_E(t)+D(t), suppose ||D(t)||<=d.
Here d is the FULL conditional Hamiltonian difference. It is not the
half-difference which can be obtained by centering the perturbation in
the pi-swap comparison of section 7 of the main proof.

In the interaction picture of the two diagonal source propagators, the
relative propagator differs from I by at most d t. The first odd Dyson
term therefore has minimum singular value at least

 a tau-a d tau^2/2.

Every off-diagonal interaction-picture operator has norm a, so all higher
odd terms together have norm at most sinh(a tau)-a tau. Thus the stated
minimum transfer-amplitude bound is correct even for switched or
arbitrarily rapidly varying common H_E(t), and for bounded varying D(t).

For tau=1/[2(a+d)], put x=a tau, y=d tau. Then x+y=1/2. For 0<=x<=1/2,

 sinh(x)/x <= 1/(1-x^2/6) <= 1+x/2.

The first inequality follows term by term from (2k+1)!>=6^k; the second
is elementary on this interval. Consequently

 [a tau-a d tau^2/2-(sinh(a tau)-a tau)]/(a tau)
   =2-y/2-sinh(x)/x >=3/4 >=1/sqrt(2).

This verifies the proposed conservative probability bound

 r >= a^2/[8(a+d)^2].
