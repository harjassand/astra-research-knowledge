LOCAL ENERGY TRANSFER, PHYSICAL ADMISSION, AND A FINITE COLD-CARRIER CYCLE
Astra continuation, 2026-10-31

MAIN CHOICE

The strongest opening developed here is a way to qualify operations against a
large, disordered quantum source without bounding its entire many-body energy
band. The quantity that matters for finite-time energy isolation is the
source's ability to CHANGE THE COUPLING, measured by a local commutator. An
explicit cycle uses fresh lower-state orbital carriers to remove energy from
spin-selective charge transfer. It replaces repeated complete charge tests by
unobserved, spin-blind energy-removal contacts and two final charge tests.

The general isolation estimate and the finite-contact cycle are proved below.
The Coulomb matrix element and all accompanying projected terms are derived in
the companion physical-contact note. A screened source supplies a concrete
large-source domain. These results are not a demonstrated semiconductor device
window, a new universal-computation theorem, or a fault-tolerance threshold.
Repeated-interaction cooling, capacitive quantum-dot control, and spin blockade
are established mechanisms. The contribution is their source-uniform complete
operation and the admission criterion, with the actual resources exposed.

1. Why a whole-source bandwidth is the wrong default

A bare orbital or charge gap is insufficient: a source transition can resonantly
bridge it. The earlier split-manifold and orbital counterexamples remain valid.
But the sufficient condition 'gap exceeds the ENTIRE source spectrum' is often
far too strong. A source of N local cells has an extensive spectrum even when a
particular local operator can transfer only a small amount of energy efficiently.

There are two complementary formulations. The first is an exact finite-time
unitary estimate; it does not use a master equation, a secular approximation,
an initial source state, or any minimum source level spacing. The second is a
spectral sum rule useful for passive reservoirs. The first is the operative
admission proof for the cold-carrier construction here.

2. Exact commutator-sensitive block isolation

Let H_c=sum_a E_a P_a be a KNOWN carrier/orbital energy operator. Each P_a acts
only on carriers, not on the source. Distinct P_a label distinct known energies;
any intended degeneracies, including a resonant transfer pair, are grouped in
one P_a. Write the complete Hamiltonian during a contact as

  H = H_c + I_c tensor H_E + W + V,

where W preserves the P_a blocks and V has no diagonal blocks. W includes the
intended resonant transfer and all bounded charge-conditional source shifts.
No norm bound is imposed on H_E. Define the anti-Hermitian operator

  S_ab = V_ab/(E_a-E_b), a!=b; S_aa=0.

Then [S,H_c]=-V. Set

  s=||S||, c=||[S,I tensor H_E]||, w=||W||, v=||V||.

All these constants concern the full admitted source domain, not a selected
source eigenstate. The exact transformed Hamiltonian obeys

  exp(S) H exp(-S) = H_c + I tensor H_E + W + R,
  ||R|| <= exp(2s) [c + 2s w + s v].                           (1)

Consequently, for a contact of duration tau,

  ||U(tau)-U_ref(tau)||
    <= e_contact := 2s + tau exp(2s)[c+2s w+s v],             (2)

where U_ref is generated by H_c+H_E+W. Tensoring any reference changes none of
the norms. Every outgoing helper is included in U, so (2) is a coherent
Stinespring estimate, stronger than a reduced-state approximation.

Proof. With ad_S(X)=[S,X], combine the H_c and V BCH series BEFORE bounding:

  exp(ad_S)(H_c+V)-H_c
    = sum_{k>=1} [k/(k+1)!] ad_S^k(V).

Its norm is at most s v exp(2s), using
k/(k+1)! <= 1/[2(k-1)!]. The H_E series begins with [S,H_E], so its norm is
at most c(exp(2s)-1)/(2s) <= c exp(2s). The W series is at most
w(exp(2s)-1) <=2s w exp(2s). This proves (1) without ever introducing ||H_E||.
Unitary Duhamel contributes tau||R||. Undoing the two endpoint rotations costs
at most 2||exp(S)-I||<=2s, proving (2).

For a time-dependent common H_E(t), a fixed S gives the integral version with
int exp(2s)[c(t)+2s w(t)+s v]dt. No derivative of H_E is needed. Changing the
coupling or energy controls changes S: either treat piecewise contacts with
their endpoint costs or include the additional derivative/finite-ramp term.
An arbitrary fast ramp is not supplied by a static gap.

Useful explicit bounds are

  s <= max_a sum_{b!=a} ||V_ab||/|E_a-E_b|,
  c <= max_a sum_{b!=a} ||[H_E,V_ab]||/|E_a-E_b|.             (3)

These row-sum bounds are valid because S and [H_E,S] have the appropriate
adjoint symmetry. They keep different detunings and matrix elements explicit.

3. Orbital admission without a joint spectral gap

For two bare orbital bands P,Q, suppose the carrier Hamiltonian has an ordered
gap Delta_orb between them. The bounded off-band coupling is V, ||V||=v. The
Sylvester inverse gives S with

  [S,H_c]=-V, ||S||<=v/Delta_orb.

Since H_E acts on another factor and commutes with H_c, the same inverse gives

  ||[H_E,S]|| <= ||[H_E,V]||/Delta_orb = M/Delta_orb.

With a bounded block-preserving remainder W of norm w, (2) becomes

  e_orb(T) <= 2v/Delta_orb
     + T exp(2v/Delta_orb)(M+2vw+v^2)/Delta_orb.             (4)

This proves a finite-time admission statement even when the spectra of the
JOINT source-plus-orbital bands overlap. It is a dynamical estimate, not a claim
that those joint bands have a spectral gap. It remains meaningful when ||H_E||
is extensive or very large while M is bounded locally.

A genuinely fast NONCOMMUTING source is not exempt. For example, a source
splitting Omega with an X-type transition coupling gives M of order Omega v;
at Omega=Delta_orb the right-hand side correctly ceases to give isolation. The
earlier source-resonance counterexample therefore survives. Conversely a huge
diagonal source term that COMMUTES with V need not enter M at all.

If the discarded orbital Hilbert space is infinite, V must be bounded on the
specified domain (or a separate relative-bound argument must be supplied), and
the Sylvester/commutator domains must exist. Smooth electrostatic potentials
from separated charge regions are bounded on a confined carrier region. A
finite numerical orbital cutoff alone is not a proof of this requirement.
The same finite-time exposure T, including loading, ramps, sensing and waiting,
must be used when the corresponding unwanted interaction remains present.

4. An actual large-source domain

Consider a two-dimensional lattice of bounded-occupation charge cells, with a
metallic screening plane. The image-screened Coulomb potential is

  V_scr(r)=C[1/r-1/sqrt(r^2+4d_s^2)] <= 2C d_s^2/r^3.

Its absolute sum over a two-dimensional lattice converges. Therefore a source
Hamiltonian with bounded local hopping and screened density interactions has a
local interaction strength independent of total source area. All occupations,
coherences, correlations and total energies in the admitted many-body Hilbert
space are allowed; the source is not loaded into a code or a low-energy state.

In the density-coupling specialization, a data transition operator has

  G=t_0 I+sum_i b_i n_i,
  H_E=sum_i epsilon_i n_i + sum_ij V_ij n_i n_j
      + sum_<ij> t_ij(c_i^dagger c_j+c_j^dagger c_i).

The commutator is exactly

  [H_E,G]=sum_<ij> t_ij(b_j-b_i)
                    (c_i^dagger c_j-c_j^dagger c_i).

For spinless source carriers,

  ||[H_E,G]|| <= sum_<ij>|t_ij| |b_j-b_i|
              <= z t_max sum_i |b_i|.                       (5)

Arbitrarily large diagonal disorder epsilon_i and density-density interaction
energies do not appear in this first-commutator estimate. A known spin
multiplicity changes its constant, not its source-volume dependence.

The coefficient b_i is a projected transition-density Coulomb integral. Orbital
orthogonality removes its monopole. Reflection removes its dipole in the
symmetric construction; with screening its generic far contribution is of
order r^-5. A conditional charge shift has a dipolar data form factor and a
screened r^-4 tail. The relevant absolute sums therefore converge. The
companion screened-source note proves the summability and checks it on growing
lattices; it does not infer it from a finite table.

A COMPLETE Coulomb projection also has source transition-density terms
B_ij c_i^dagger c_j. They must be retained and assigned a local commutator
bound, or bounded by actual source orbital overlaps. Keeping source tunnelling
while silently deleting every such transition-density coupling is not valid.
Unscreened interactions, unbounded local occupation, and uncontrolled source
exchange can invalidate the uniform constants. Those are distinct physical
questions, not exceptions hidden in the word 'disordered'.

5. Exact spectral formulation and its limitation

For static source Hamiltonians H_S,H_D in two charge sectors, define

  G_omega=sum_{e_D-e_S=omega} Pi_D(e_D) G Pi_S(e_S),
  L(G)=H_D G-G H_S,
  D_HS(X)=sum_e Pi_S(e) X Pi_S(e).

Summing ALL transitions, including degenerate source eigenspaces, gives

  sum_omega G_omega^dagger G_omega = D_HS(G^dagger G),
  sum_omega omega^(2k) G_omega^dagger G_omega
      =D_HS[(L^k G)^dagger(L^k G)].                           (6)

If G^dagger G>=g^2 I, the first effect is >=g^2 I. Thus complete intermediate
closure cannot cancel this INCOHERENT sum of probabilities as it can cancel a
coherent molecular association amplitude. No individual matrix element has to
be nonzero in every source eigenbasis. For any W>0,

  sum_|omega|>W G_omega^dagger G_omega
      <= ||L^k G||^2/W^(2k) I.                               (7)

In an ADMITTED energy-resolved passive reservoir with rate J(E)>=J_min on
[Delta-W,Delta+W], Delta>W, the intended downhill rate effect is at least

  J_min [g^2-||L^kG||^2/W^(2k)] I.                          (8)

Reverse and wrong-charge transitions requiring a source energy transfer beyond
a specified threshold are bounded by (7) times the reservoir's upper rate
envelope. These are operator bounds, valid for all source states and reference
correlations. Conditional source Hamiltonians are allowed.

For bounded local source Hamiltonians and local G, iterated commutators have
factorial bounds independent of source volume, yielding exponential spectral
tails after optimizing k. This is established high-frequency/locality physics,
not a new claim of exponential heating suppression. Applying the operator
effect identity to both conditional charge Hamiltonians is useful here. Huge
diagonal disorder can invalidate a UNIFORM higher-moment constant even when
the first moment (5) stays small; do not infer exponential tails from (5) alone.

Crucial limitation: (6)-(8) do not bound a COHERENT sum of unresolved frequency
components by simply adding their probabilities. Nor do they establish a
finite-time, full-field Davies approximation for a many-body source with tiny
level spacings. Nonsecular coarse-grained master equations provide a relevant
route, but their reduced-state error is not automatically an emitted-instrument
error. The finite-contact construction below uses (1)-(4), not that shortcut.

6. Replacing the dissipative mode by finite energy carriers

The data are the full two-electron one-orbital Hubbard system, with triplets
T_+,T_0,T_- and singlet charge states S,D_L,D_R. Spin-independent hopping is

  K=sqrt(2)(|S><D_L|+|S><D_R|+h.c.).

Every triplet vector is dark to K and all fine operations act as I_3 on its
spin factor. Both double-charge states are retained throughout.

Use a fresh helper with two orbital energy levels |0>,|1>, splitting Delta.
Its spin can be unpolarized; the relevant prepared resource is its lower
ORBITAL state. A direct electrostatic interaction between the confined data
and helper densities contains

  H_DH = (known charge/helper diagonal terms)
          + lambda K tensor X_H
          + (all scalar and source-dependent single-transition terms).

The coefficient lambda is a transition-density times transition-density
Coulomb integral. It is not a product of two independently assumed equal gate
amplitudes. The same K on both spin channels follows from the data's ordinary
fermionic algebra. The companion microscopic note computes the complete
projected interaction, including larger unwanted terms and dangerous bias
resonances. Disjoint confined channels can remove data-helper particle
exchange exactly in the admitted hard-wall model; finite tails must be charged.

The helper need not be observed after contact. It is physically decoupled and
removed; a new lower-state helper is supplied. All outgoing quantum states can
remain in the fine output. The source E is NEVER reset. If one helper is reused,
its emptying/loading or cold reset, actual duration and complete error replace
the fresh-helper resource. A lower-state helper and its maintained energy gap
are not an unprepared auxiliary.

7. One selective capture contact

Ignoring known diagonal Coulomb corrections momentarily, bias D_R to energy
-Delta relative to S. The four relevant states have energies

  D_R0: -Delta; S0 and D_R1: 0; S1: +Delta.

The resonant block S0 <-> D_R1 has amplitude a=sqrt(2)|lambda|. After transfer,
removing the excited helper leaves D_R. A new helper in |0> begins in the
off-resonant sink D_R0; its reverse transition is detuned by 2Delta. The other
double-charge state and all other one-helper transitions are included in H_c
and V. Choose its charging energy so it is not accidentally resonant.

Known diagonal interactions must be included BEFORE defining the gap. For
example, in the parallel symmetric geometry H_DH contains chi z_D Z_H,
z_D=n_L-1 and Z_H=|0><0|-|1><1|. For a selected D with z_D=+1, resonance requires
E_D=-Delta+chi. Then D0=-Delta+2chi, S0=D1=0, S1=Delta. The unwanted single-
transition gap can be |Delta-2chi|, and the counterrotating gap is
2|Delta-chi|. Choosing Delta merely larger than chi is INSUFFICIENT. At
Delta=2chi the nominal sink is resonant with larger nuisance couplings.

In the perpendicular energy-doublet helper geometry, data reflection can
eliminate the entire differential-charge/helper interaction while leaving a
nonzero data-transition/helper-transition coefficient. That construction and
its actual bias/orbital limitations are given in the companion note. Neither
geometry licenses dropping source-helper fields or control-induced orbital
changes.

Construct H_c from the ACTUAL calibrated diagonal energy table. Put resonant
a(|S0><D_R1|+h.c.) and bounded conditional detunings in W. Put bare hopping,
source-assisted hopping, off-resonant helper flips, unwanted KX branches and
other admitted nuisance transitions in V. Equations (1)-(3) give e_contact
without a total source bandwidth. A separate orbital application of (4), or a
direct larger-mode calculation, pays for discarded orbitals on the same domain.

For a calibrated scalar a and bounded residual active-block perturbation d_swap,
tau=pi/(2a) gives the useful ideal-reference survival amplitude

  a_surv <= min(1, d_swap tau).                              (9)

This follows by Duhamel around the exact resonant swap with common H_E;
no source state or dynamics is calibrated. It is useful when d_swap tau<1.

A construction remains available without a small detuning-to-gain ratio. Put
d_rel=||H_D-H_S|| for the TWO CONDITIONAL SOURCE HAMILTONIANS in the resonant
block after the known scalar bias is removed. This differs from d_swap: a
centered swap comparison can have d_swap=d_rel/2. In an interaction picture of
the two conditional source Hamiltonians, their relative propagator differs
from I by at most d_rel t. The leading transfer amplitude is at least
a tau-a d_rel tau^2/2. All higher odd hopping terms total at most
sinh(a tau)-a tau. Hence

  sigma_min(transfer) >= a tau-a d_rel tau^2/2
                         -[sinh(a tau)-a tau].              (10)

Taking tau=1/[2(a+d_rel)] gives a transfer probability at least

  r >= a^2/[8(a+d_rel)^2],
  a_surv <= sqrt(1-r)<1.                                    (11)

The numerical constant 1/8 is a conservative simplification of (10). This
quadratic detuning-ratio guarantee uses a scalar supplied transfer amplitude;
it must not be substituted for the old arbitrary-gain pulse theorem without
that extra structure. The price when d_rel>>a is a slow pump and greater exposure.
For a direct constant check, put x=a tau,y=d_rel tau. Then x+y=1/2 and
sinh(x)/x<=1+x/2 for 0<=x<=1/2. The right side of (10), divided by a tau,
is at least 1-(x+y)/2=3/4>1/sqrt(2), which proves (11).

8. Uniform pumping with coherent past helpers

Let B be a transient carrier subspace and F its target sink. A fresh-helper
reference isometry V_0 satisfies

  ||P_B V_0 P_B||<=a_surv<1, P_B V_0 P_F=0.

Suppose the actual isometry V obeys ||V-V_0||<=e. For ANY input state, purified
with all previous helpers, the source, reference and earlier records, put
q=sqrt(Pr[B]). Then the triangle inequality gives the sharper recurrence

  q_next <= a_surv q + e.

Thus after N contacts, starting in B,

  q_N <= a_surv^N + e(1-a_surv^N)/(1-a_surv)
       <= a_surv^N + e/(1-a_surv).                           (12)

No repeated pinching or independent source state is assumed. This is why the
contact error does not have to be paid as N times a first-order state error.
The result concerns convergence to a target probability; it does not assert
that the complete auxiliary output equals that of N ideal contacts.

For CAPTURE, B is S in charge (1,1); F consists of BOTH D_L and D_R. The
reference contact only transfers S to the selected D, and leaves either
already doubled state in its own charge sector. The other D therefore does
not spoil (12). All exact charge-double events certify S, including an
unwanted route into the opposite double state, provided the single-orbital
spin-to-charge admission remains valid.

After N_c contacts, perform one complete charge measurement. Charge 1 is
reported T, with singlet miss probability

  p_miss <= [a_c^N_c+e_c/(1-a_c)]^2.                         (13)

9. Return without the dark-double trap

A single symmetric return bath can leave (D_L-D_R)/sqrt(2) dark. That decisive
counterexample is preserved. The new construction has TWO SEPARATE SELECTIVE
energy contacts, with fresh helpers, one for each double-charge state.

For contact j, bias D_j0 to resonate with S1; S0 is the sink. The opposite
D is off resonance. Run j=R and j=L in a fixed macrostep, using a new lower-
state helper for each contact and finite, isolated bias ramps between them.
Outgoing helpers distinguish which energy-transfer contact returned the pair.

In the reference macrostep the transient subspace is span{D_L,D_R}, and its
survival norm is at most a_r=max(a_L,a_R). A survivor of the first contact
remains in that D and is not coherently combined with the other edge; the
orthogonal outgoing helper labels remove the rank-one common-outlet trap.
This remains true for a coherent D_L/D_R input and source entanglement.

The actual macrostep's coherent error is at most

  e_r=e_L+e_R+e_ramp,

where e_ramp includes actual helper decoupling, barrier and bias operations.
Applying (12) to N_r macrosteps gives

  p_return <= [a_r^N_r+e_r/(1-a_r)]^2.                       (14)

Finally measure full charge again. Charge 1 certifies return to the unique
separated singlet. A remaining double charge is a flagged return failure;
retain it or use the preserved paid singlet reload/recovery construction.
Do not normalize away the failure. Without this final measured charge flag,
a coherent return error would generally cost sqrt(p_return), not p_return.

10. Complete singlet/triplet instrument and duration

Every exact fine operation is I_3 on the triplet spin factor. The only
single-orbital spin states in a double charge are singlets. The same
actual-history comparison used in the preserved cycle therefore gives

  epsilon_ST <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss+p_return
               +2 delta_Q+eta_preparation+eta_orbital
               +eta_controls+eta_spin.                      (15)

This half-diamond statement includes the source, reference, all removed
helpers, and retained measurement records. The ideal comparison retains the
actual auxiliary maps within each S/T sector. It does not pretend that the
source or the emitted helpers stayed unchanged. The charge instrument theorem
must apply after every allowed and error history, or a leakage envelope is
required. Coarse-graining a full charge read to S/T does not restore lost
within-triplet coherence if the apparatus did not preserve it.

If m=N_c+2N_r helpers are used, a conservative serial wall-time bound is

  T_ST <= m(tau+t_attach+t_detach+t_supply)
         +t_bias_ramps+2 t_Q+t_final_recovery.                (16)

Use separate contact durations where they differ. A prepared bank can pipeline
helper supply, but its startup, occupancy checks and throughput remain costs.
Two charge measurements replace N_c+N_r complete charge tests in the original
pulse construction. They do not replace the m prepared helper occupations.

The incoming helper's lower orbital and occupation need a cold loading or
verified preparation process. A single-electron charge window lies between the
first and second addition energies and below the upper orbital, including all
source shifts and thermal margins. Its spin need not be polarized. Fresh
helpers must be independent in the specified orbital state; a hot stream is
not the same resource. If an initial helper error is bounded as a complete
preparation-channel error delta_h, the safe allowance is m delta_h. A coherent
reset estimate must not be replaced by a population-only bound.

Preparation and removal can be performed in separate shielded regions while
the data barrier is isolated. Turning off their interaction must be a physical
operation; leaving old helpers coupled would invalidate the fresh-contact
recurrence. Their emitted/reset energy and entropy are an irreversible outlet,
not an unprepared part of E.

In the ideal sink model, a successful S capture and return leave two excited
outgoing helpers, with energy set by their gaps. The helper supply, relative
energy controls and switching work are maintained resources. The source can
remain highly excited; the helpers cannot.

Unwanted spin Hamiltonians of norm beta have a safe complete exposure allowance
beta T_ST. Data-helper/source exchange, spin-orbit and hyperfine terms must be
bounded over that FULL duration. The sensor can use the new recorded-current
or finite-contact isolation theorem. OFF hopping includes every source- and
helper-induced term while the barrier is closed. Sensor strength, charge
contrast and the local admission constants must refer to the same source domain.

For fixed a_c,a_r<1 and a chosen contact accuracy, the useful number of contacts
is logarithmic in the desired miss/return amplitude. At fixed hardware, (12)
has a certificate floor e/(1-a), while spin exposure grows with duration.
Increasing N indefinitely is not a solution. This is a floor in this upper
bound, not a proven lower bound on a better controller.

The first-order admission theorem typically requires energy separation growing
as the inverse target coherent error (times contact/exposure constants). It
does NOT give exponentially cheap accuracy in every resource. Higher-order
local prethermal bounds may improve the energy requirement under stronger
local assumptions; no such improvement is claimed for the complete cycle here.

11. Consequence and strongest comparisons

The new organizing result is that PHYSICAL ADMISSION CAN BE LOCAL IN THE
COUPLING EVEN WHEN SOURCE ENERGY IS EXTENSIVE. Equations (1)-(5) remove a
substantive global-band obstruction, while correctly rejecting a locally
resonant noncommuting source. The finite carriers implement energy directionality
without assuming a white bath, source-level secular spacings, or a bare local
oscillator-loss operator in an inconsistent frame.

The resulting primitive is the same complete singlet/triplet instrument used
by the preserved loading, singlet-link routing and measurement-only consumers.
For a finite program, all ancilla loading, routing and recovery calls remain in
its operation count. The preserved three-spin route has mean five STP calls;
it is not a free arbitrary-pair interaction. Universality with unpolarized gauge
spins and STP is established prior art, not the consequence newly discovered.

Strong conventional calibrated exchange can be much faster where pulse areas
and source effects are known or sufficiently stable. Strong frozen-blockade
readout already has wide barrier control and fast charge sensing. Ordinary
dissipative/latching readout or repeated-interaction cooling can use these same
carriers and bounds; this construction does not reserve an advantage for a new
architecture. Its additional guarantee is uniformity over an admitted quantum
source and complete within-triplet preservation, rather than label fidelity on
test states. Holonomic or calibrated controls supplied the same maintained
helpers and relative energies are legitimate comparators, not defeated by the
failure of one naive coherent pulse.

The Coulomb energy-transfer matrix element can be much weaker than the larger
diagonal electrostatic shifts and ordinary hopping. Source-helper fields and
spin exposure may dominate. A dimensionless compatible model and an exact
nonzero matrix element are not a measured usable window. A practical advantage
requires the JOINT numeric inequalities on lambda, detuning, source commutator,
orbital leakage, OFF hopping, readout contrast and beta T_ST in one apparatus.

In particular, tau is of order 1/|lambda| for a calibrated transfer. The local
admission cost is therefore M/(|lambda| Delta), not merely M/Delta; ordinary
virtual shifts contribute a conservative v^2/(|lambda| Delta) term. A small
lambda cannot be omitted from the compatibility test. The standalone budget
program makes the resulting energy-separation requirements visible rather
than reporting only a logarithmic contact count.
