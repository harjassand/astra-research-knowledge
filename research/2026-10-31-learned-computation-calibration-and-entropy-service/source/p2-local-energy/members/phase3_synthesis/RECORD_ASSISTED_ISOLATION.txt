RECORD-ASSISTED ISOLATION WITH AN ACTIVE QUANTUM SOURCE
Astra research continuation, 2026-10-31

Status: proved estimates for the explicitly stated continuous instrument and
finite-carrier Hamiltonian models. These are not a demonstrated material/device
window. All earlier results and their counterexamples are retained. No claim of
priority is made for continuous charge sensing, quantum Zeno suppression, or
singlet-triplet computation.

1. The physical question

How much residual charge hopping can a real measurement tolerate, when its
source is a quantum system that is neither prepared nor estimated? The relevant
answer involves the information in the retained detector record, not just the
unconditional dephasing rate. This distinction matters when the full emitted
history is part of the output of the operation.

The old pulsed Hubbard construction remains a valid construction. It used a
linear bound on OFF hopping over the whole sensing time. A maintained charge
detector provides a better isolation mechanism: a coherent boundary correction
of order h_off/Gamma and an accumulated correction of order h_off^2 T/Gamma.
The boundary term is real and must not be erased by calling the effect Zeno.

There is also an exact discrete version, based on fresh carriers and a finite
tunnel contact. It needs no Markov approximation for the detector reservoir.
The fresh carrier state, timed contact, collection and output measurement are
maintained resources, explicitly distinguished from the unprepared source.

2. Definitions, outputs, and norm

Let P_q, q=1,...,d, be charge projectors. A block may itself contain a source E,
all live reference/data systems, and all classical records accumulated so far.
Nothing is put into E merely to hide its computational disturbance. The norm

  ||X||_box = sum_{p,q} ||P_p X P_q||_1

includes integration/summation over retained classical records. Every estimate
below holds after tensoring an arbitrary reference. For a positive normalized
state, x=P X=sum_q P_q X P_q has ||x||_box=1. Its off-diagonal part y has

  ||y||_box <= d-1.

Indeed positivity gives ||X_pq||_1 <= sqrt(tr X_pp tr X_qq), and
(sum_q sqrt(tr X_qq))^2 <= d. This statement holds pointwise in a classical
record, and integration gives the same bound.

The no-hopping dynamics is charge diagonal. Its within-charge Hamiltonians,
source channels and detector amplitudes may be noncommuting and depend on
earlier records. Charge-diagonal Hamiltonian changes belong in this exact
reference dynamics; they are not counted as leakage.

Residual charge hopping V(t)=V(t)^dagger has P_q V P_q=0. Set

  h_box = sup_t max_q sum_{p != q} ||V_pq(t)||,
  v = 2 h_box.

The commutator -i[V,.] has induced box norm at most v. Hermiticity equates the
maximum row and column sums used in this estimate. For the full Hubbard
S,D_L,D_R star with both OFF edges sqrt(2)G_off and ||G_off||<=h_off,

  v <= 4 sqrt(2) h_off.

This bound includes source-assisted and detector-assisted hopping. Small
charge-changing dissipative jumps are not Hamiltonian V and need an additional
instrument error bound or an enlarged model.

3. What detector rate actually enters

For a scalar counting contact L=sum_q ell_q P_q, a recorded electron count
has Poisson rate |ell_q|^2 in charge sector q. The full classical-record
off-diagonal channel contracts in completely bounded trace norm by

  exp[-Gamma_pq t],
  Gamma_pq = (|ell_p|-|ell_q|)^2/2.

This is the Hellinger affinity exponent of the two count laws. It is not in
general |ell_p-ell_q|^2/2. Opposite amplitudes ell_p=-ell_q have identical
count distributions: they can cause unconditional dephasing while the complete
count record retains a known phase on the off-diagonal block. Gamma_pq=0.

The companion CONTINUOUS_CHARGE_INSTRUMENT.txt proves the more general result.
If L_q are source operators, the conditional intensities obey uniform bounds

  lambda_q^- I <= L_q^dagger L_q <= lambda_q^+ I,

and these rate intervals are ordered and disjoint, then

  Gamma_pq >= (sqrt(lambda_p^-)-sqrt(lambda_q^+))^2/2

for a higher-rate p and lower-rate q. Polar factorization of a general input
block and CP-instrument Cauchy-Schwarz reduce its norm to a Hellinger affinity.
The predictable intensity bounds hold on every actual quantum-source history.
No classical hidden-source interpretation or source reset is used.

Let Gamma=min_{p != q} Gamma_pq. For the no-hopping instrument propagator,

  ||Phi_qq(t,s)||_1,cb <= 1,
  ||Phi_pq(t,s)||_1,cb <= exp[-Gamma(t-s)] (p != q).

This concerns a classical measured record. Coherently retaining all output
fields gives an isometric dilation and does not have this contraction.

4. Continuous full-record isolation theorem

Suppose Gamma>v and put mu=Gamma-v. Compare the actual detector plus residual
hopping with an ideal initial exact charge projection followed by the ACTUAL
no-hopping within-charge dynamics and its full detector record. Write x_0(t)
for this comparison state, starting from x(0). Then

  ||y(t)||_box <= (d-1) exp(-mu t) + v/mu,

  ||x(t)-x_0(t)||_box <= v(d-1)/mu + v^2 t/mu.

Consequently the complete channel error, in half diamond norm, is at most

  E_iso(t) = 1/2 [ (d-1) exp(-mu t) + d v/mu + v^2 t/mu ].       (1)

Proof. Blockwise Duhamel with the no-hopping recorded instrument gives

  y(t) <= exp(-Gamma t)y(0)
       + v int_0^t exp[-Gamma(t-s)] [1+y(s)] ds.

Scalar Volterra comparison gives the first bound. Since P[-iV,.]P=0, the
diagonal difference is bounded by v int_0^t y(s)ds. Diagonal recorded channels
are trace contractions, including adapted future operations. The full trace
norm is bounded by the box norm, giving (1). The proof was performed with an
arbitrary reference; its bound is therefore a complete channel bound.

The ideal charge projection in this comparison is not a supplied physical
pinching step. The exp(-mu t) term pays for replacing the initial charge
coherence. The O(v/mu) boundary term pays for coherence generated by residual
hopping near the beginning/end of measurement. It cannot be discarded in a
general full-instrument claim. If a separate physical operation prepares a
charge-diagonal input and pinches the final charge, a sharper population-only
bound is possible, but that is a different resource contract.

Given a classifier of the same record whose worst-case wrong-charge probability
is p_count(t) on a fixed charge, the complete QND charge-instrument error is

  delta_Q(t) <= E_iso(t) + p_count(t) + eta_detector(t).          (2)

Here eta_detector is the complete error of any physical reduction made to
obtain the admitted detector model. Scalar known Poisson rates give a simple
maximum-likelihood bound p_count <= (d-1)exp(-Gamma t). Interval intensities
instead use the exponential-martingale thresholds in the companion note.
Finite receiver bandwidth, its initial condition, and amplifier imprecision
are charged there; a perfect electron-counting amplifier is not required.

5. Exact finite-carrier alternative

One collision uses one freshly supplied carrier in an incoming left mode and
an empty right mode. During a controlled interval Delta it evolves under a
charge-diagonal finite Hamiltonian, then the outgoing port is measured and
the carrier discarded. Both ports and all fine outcomes are retained. The
source may evolve arbitrarily rapidly and noncommutatively during this step.
The unprepared E is not this supplied carrier.

An explicit ordinary tunnel-contact specialization, on the occupied one-
particle carrier space, is

  H_q(t) = H_E^q(t) tensor I_c
         + [tau_q I_E+B_q(t)] tensor X_c,
  ||B_q(t)|| <= b.

The source-free carrier transition amplitude is -i sin(tau_q Delta). Duhamel
in the interaction picture of the arbitrary H_E^q gives an operator amplitude
error at most b Delta. Thus, on EVERY conditional source input,

  l_q = max(0, |sin(tau_q Delta)|-b Delta)^2,
  u_q = min(1, (|sin(tau_q Delta)|+b Delta)^2)

bound the transmission probability. Other bounded contact terms may be
included in b. This is a finite Hamiltonian estimate, not a white-noise source
approximation. The contact parameters/length and fresh packet are calibrated
resources; arbitrary gain without a contrast bound does not suffice.

For ordered disjoint probability intervals, the no-hopping output instrument
contracts a p,q coherence by at most their maximal Bernoulli affinity. For
u_p<l_q,

  c_pq = sqrt(u_p l_q)+sqrt[(1-u_p)(1-l_q)] < 1.

Use c=max_{p != q} c_pq. This cb bound follows from the same polar-factor/CP
Cauchy-Schwarz argument as the continuous theorem, and allows arbitrary
source states and reference entanglement at the start of each collision.

Now leave residual V present DURING the whole collision. Define

  s=v Delta, r=exp(s)-1-s, a=s+r=exp(s)-1,
  eta=c+a, gap=1-eta,

and require eta<1. Expand the exact collision superoperator in V. Its first
Dyson term has box norm at most s and maps a diagonal input to off-diagonal
charge blocks. The sum of all terms of order >=2 has norm at most r. These
are finite unitary Dyson estimates before the port measurement; no reservoir
Markov approximation is needed.

If x_n,y_n are the charge-diagonal and off-diagonal parts after n collisions,

  ||y_(n+1)||_box <= eta ||y_n||_box + a,
  ||x_(n+1)-Phi_0 x_n||_box <= a ||y_n||_box+r.

The initial off-diagonal norm is at most d-1. Summing the geometric series and
comparing with the ideal initially projected, no-hopping process gives

  E_discrete(N) <= 1/2 [ (d-1) eta^N
                   + d a/gap + N(r+a^2/gap) ].                 (3)

As s->0 this is a boundary O(v Delta/(1-c)) and an accumulated
O(N v^2 Delta^2/(1-c)) bound. In particular it is not N times a first-order
single-step hopping error. This is the discrete form of detector-assisted
isolation, proved for an explicit finite contact and fresh carrier resource.

Classify q by the empirical fraction of transmitted carriers, with thresholds
midway between adjacent admitted intervals. If zeta is the minimum distance
from an interval endpoint to its nearest threshold, martingale Hoeffding gives

  p_count(N) <= 2 exp(-2 N zeta^2).

There is no independence assumption for the source-conditioned outcomes. Thus
delta_Q <= E_discrete(N)+p_count(N)+eta_packet. The last term explicitly pays
any conversion of the finite contact to a flying-packet or Fermi-sea device.
For the finite Hamiltonian protocol itself eta_packet=0. Occupation loading,
contact switching, transit, output collection and record storage have their
actual durations and errors; the result does not claim an unprepared beam.

6. Physical contact and orbital admission

For a scalar frozen-charge contact a rectangular tunnel barrier supplies an
open set of distinct transmission probabilities. For incident energy E<U_q,

  D_q(E) = [1+ U_q^2 sinh^2(a sqrt(2m(U_q-E))/hbar)
                  / (4E(U_q-E))]^-1,
  U_q=U_0+q delta_U, delta_U>0.

The denominator increases strictly with U_q. Hence a finite energy interval
has ordered charge-dependent transmissions. Coulomb density coupling gives
the charge-dependent barrier. The same projection generates residual hopping
on the data and source-contact terms; these must appear in h_off and in the
contrast/domain bounds. They cannot be silently dropped.

For a metallic QPC one still needs a valid reservoir reduction, or a direct
finite-band scattering analysis. A short bath correlation time by itself is
not a quantified finite-time diamond error certificate. Scalar f(q) coupling
commutes with arbitrary charge-diagonal H_E^q, so internal source speed does
not enter the detector coupling's interaction-picture derivative. Direct
source-dependent contact amplitudes destroy that simplification: a fast
source can release energy into the transport current. The companion note
includes an explicit colored-reservoir counterexample.

The finite-carrier theorem separates this problem: it proves a complete
measurement for a bounded contact with prepared incoming occupations, without
claiming that every continuum junction realizes the contact automatically.

Spin preservation is stronger than a charge label statement. Every fine
operation must be the identity on the three-dimensional triplet spin factor.
Purely electrostatic carrier/source couplings have this property when source
exchange and spin-orbit/hyperfine terms are excluded or bounded. A common
total-spin symmetry involving source spins is insufficient. Extra data
orbitals can preserve spin while allowing triplet double occupancy; therefore
the single-orbital charge-to-spin conversion still needs the joint source-
orbital energy admission, not just a bare orbital gap.

7. Complete-cycle use and fair interpretation

Insert (2) or (3) for delta_Q in the preserved full singlet/triplet cycle:

  epsilon_ST <= sqrt(p_miss)+p_miss+p_return
                + (N_c+N_r) delta_Q + eta_other,

where p_miss <=(1-r_on)^N_c and p_return <=(1-r_on)^N_r.
All detector preparation/transport/integration, barrier ramps and loading
are included in T_ST. A symmetry-breaking Hamiltonian of norm beta has the
safe complete error allowance beta T_ST; stochastic loss/spin-flip channels
need their appropriate integrated complete generator bound. This is an upper
bound, not a universal lower bound on control or achievable fidelity.

The gain from the present theorem is earned isolation and a reusable physical
detector interface. It removes the inference that the old Ramsey-probe count
was a lower bound on a better apparatus. It neither proves a faster readout
than current rf sensors nor a fault-tolerance threshold. Strong conventional
calibrated exchange, full-triplet-preserving charge readout and measurement-
only control may use the very same detector and isolation estimates.
