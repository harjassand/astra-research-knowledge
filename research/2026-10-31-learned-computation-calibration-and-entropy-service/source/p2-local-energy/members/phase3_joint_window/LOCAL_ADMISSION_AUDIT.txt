INDEPENDENT AUDIT: FINITE COMMUTATOR-SENSITIVE ADMISSION
31 October 2026. Analytic proof and independent numerical checks.

Verdict
-------
The proposed bound is correct. It controls the complete unitary, not only
populations, and needs neither a source spectral gap nor the norm or total
spectral diameter of the common source Hamiltonian. It correctly fails to
give small error when a noncommuting resonant source can supply the bare
orbital energy. The extension to time-dependent common source evolution is
valid with a fixed dressing generator and an integral of the commutator
bound. No source-Hamiltonian derivative is required.

1. Statement and exact hypotheses
--------------------------------
Work first in finite dimension, with an arbitrary reference system implicitly
tensored throughout. Let

 H = H_c + I_carrier tensor H_E + W + V,
 H_ref = H_c + I_carrier tensor H_E + W.

Here H_c is source-independent and self-adjoint. Write its carrier spectral
resolution H_c=sum_a E_a P_a, allowing degeneracies. Let V=V^dagger have no
matrix elements between equal-energy sectors; those elements belong in W.
Define

 S_ab = V_ab/(E_a-E_b),  E_a!=E_b,
 S_ab = 0,               E_a=E_b.

Then S^dagger=-S and [S,H_c]=-V exactly. Put

 s=||S||,  c=||[S,I tensor H_E]||,  w=||W||,  v=||V||.

The exact conjugated Hamiltonian has the form

 exp(S) H exp(-S) = H_ref + R,

with

 ||R|| <= exp(2s)[c+2s w+s v].                         (1)

For t>=0, the exact propagators obey

 ||exp(-iHt)-exp(-iH_ref t)||
 <= 2s+t exp(2s)[c+2s w+s v].                          (2)

The trivial upper bound 2 may be applied to the right side. No small-s
assumption is needed for validity; small s is needed only for usefulness.

The hypothesis [W,H_c]=0 is NOT needed for the algebraic bounds. To interpret
(2) as admission into a chosen projector P, it is enough that the reference
Hamiltonian preserve P. For an orbital-band P this allows W to act within
the low and high bands without commuting with every bare orbital energy.
For a gate/contact reference, W contains the intended resonant operations.

2. Proof of the remainder bound
------------------------------
Write ad_S(A)=[S,A]. Since [S,H_c]=-V,

 exp(ad_S)(H_c+V)-H_c
 =sum_(k>=1) [k/(k+1)!] ad_S^k(V).                     (3)

The coefficient is k/(k+1)!, not 1/k!: the leading V cancels and the first
remaining term is [S,V]/2. Thus, using ||ad_S(A)||<=2s||A||,

 ||R_V|| <= v sum_(k>=1) [k/(k+1)!](2s)^k
          <=s v exp(2s).                              (4)

For the last inequality use
k/(k+1)!=1/[(k+1)(k-1)!]<=1/[2(k-1)!].

The source contribution starts with the actual commutator:

 R_E=sum_(m>=0) ad_S^m([S,H_E])/(m+1)!,
 ||R_E|| <= c [exp(2s)-1]/(2s) <=c exp(2s).             (5)

Interpret the ratio continuously as 1 at s=0. In particular, this estimate
does not first replace c by 2s||H_E||. Making that replacement would throw
away the central information in the theorem.

Finally,

 ||exp(ad_S)(W)-W|| <=w[exp(2s)-1]
                    <=2s w exp(2s).                  (6)

Adding (4)-(6) proves (1). A sometimes useful tighter version is

 ||R|| <= c[exp(2s)-1]/(2s)+w[exp(2s)-1]
          +v sum_(k>=1)[k/(k+1)!](2s)^k.

Alternatively replace 2sw in (1) by c_W=||[S,W]|| using the same argument
as (5). This can avoid paying a large part of W that commutes with S.

3. Propagator, instrument, and population consequences
----------------------------------------------------
The exact identity

 U(t)=exp(-S) exp[-i(H_ref+R)t] exp(S)

and unitary Duhamel give an interior error at most t||R||. For the endpoint
dressings,

 ||exp(S)-I|| <=s

because S is anti-Hermitian and exp(uS) is unitary for real u. The two
endpoint changes cost at most 2s, proving (2). No unknown initial source
state enters this operator-norm proof.

If H_ref preserves P and the input lies in P, the off-domain amplitude
satisfies ||(I-P)U(t)P||<=delta(t), where delta is the right side of (2).
The leakage probability is at most min(1,delta(t)^2). That population
statement must not replace the full channel statement: coherent shifts
inside P can already cause an error of order delta.

For any initial source/reference state, and any identical subsequent
discarding, fresh-helper attachment, outlet operation, or fine measurement,
the half-diamond distance between the two induced instruments is at most
delta(t), by Stinespring continuity and contractivity. Thus the theorem is
suitable for actual-history composition. Per-contact uniform errors can be
summed even when the retained source is changed by earlier contacts and is
correlated with the data/reference. Independence is not needed.

The endpoint term is a comparison in the BARE interface coordinates. It is
paid again for separately compared contacts unless a common dressed
interface has actually been constructed across their boundaries. The
finite-time result does not assert indefinite protection: even when c=0,
the usual coherent virtual shift of order v^2/gap can accumulate with time.

4. Two ordered bare orbital bands
--------------------------------
Let H_c=H_P direct-sum H_Q, with

 inf spec(H_Q)-sup spec(H_P)>=Delta>0,

and let V be strictly off-diagonal for P,Q. The operators H_P,H_Q act on
carrier coordinates only; the full source factor is untouched. There is a
bounded Sylvester inverse giving the off-diagonal part of S. For any a in
the interval between the two bands,

 S_QP=int_0^infinity exp[-u(H_Q-a)] V_QP
                         exp[u(H_P-a)] du,
 S_PQ=-S_QP^dagger.

It follows that

 s<=v/Delta.                                          (7)

The common H_E commutes with both semigroups and with P,Q, so its commutator
passes through this integral. Hence

 c<=||[I tensor H_E,V]||/Delta.                        (8)

If M=||[H_E,V]||, the practical sufficient bound is

 delta(t)<=2v/Delta
  +[t exp(2v/Delta)/Delta] [M+2v w+v^2].              (9)

This remains valid if the TOTAL source spectrum bridges or far exceeds the
bare orbital gap. The source must actually couple through the commutator to
make that energy available to the carrier. Formula (9) accounts for it.

For many arbitrarily placed carrier energy sectors, do not automatically
replace ||S|| by v divided by the smallest pairwise gap. The division is an
operator-valued Schur multiplier and needs its own norm estimate. Compute
S directly, sum the finitely many block norms, or use an applicable ordered
Sylvester estimate. The two-ordered-band statement has constant 1 as above.

5. Time-dependent source: fixed S is sufficient
----------------------------------------------
Let H_E(t) and W(t) be arbitrary piecewise continuous self-adjoint functions;
H_c and V, and therefore S, stay fixed. They can have rapid changes and the
Hamiltonians at different times need not commute. Define

 c(t)=||[S,H_E(t)]||, w(t)=||W(t)||.

Conjugation by the CONSTANT exp(S) gives the exact transformed generator
H_ref(t)+R(t). Time-ordered unitary Duhamel yields

 ||U(T,0)-U_ref(T,0)||
 <=2s+exp(2s) int_0^T [c(t)+2s w(t)+s v]dt.            (10)

There is no dH_E/dt term. In the ordered-band case, substitute
c(t)<=||[H_E(t),V]||/Delta. This remains a complete unitary bound.

If V or the chosen bare decomposition changes so that S=S(t), there IS an
additional inertial generator from d exp(S(t))/dt. It cannot be omitted.
For differentiable anti-Hermitian S(t), its norm is bounded by ||dot S(t)||;
endpoint dressings become ||S(0)||+||S(T)||. This extension is not needed for
the fixed-contact theorem but matters during a physical ramp.

For an unbounded H_E, the same argument requires the usual domain hypotheses:
a common invariant domain, well-defined unitary propagators, and a bounded
commutator in the sense used in the conjugation identity. The finite source
proofs are uniform in source size, so no unbounded-domain claim is needed to
obtain arbitrarily large finite admitted sources.

6. Physical local constant for a density-coupled source
-----------------------------------------------------
Use a spinless bounded-occupation source with

 H_E=sum_i epsilon_i n_i+sum_(i<j) V_ij n_i n_j
        +sum_<ij> t_ij(c_i^dagger c_j+c_j^dagger c_i),
 G=t_0 I+sum_i b_i n_i.

The onsite disorder and density interactions commute with G, regardless of
their norms or total energy bandwidth. Exactly,

 [H_E,G]=sum_<ij> t_ij(b_j-b_i)
                    (c_i^dagger c_j-c_j^dagger c_i).

Thus ||[H_E,G]||<=sum_<ij>|t_ij||b_j-b_i|. If the source graph has degree at
most z, this is at most z*t_max*sum_i|b_i|. Add the admitted multiplicity for
spinful source modes. For V_off=K_off tensor G, this gives M directly, with
the known norm of K_off. Conditional source shifts contribute through W or
through a bound on H_D G-G H_S, as appropriate to the chosen decomposition.

The physical qualifications from PHYSICAL_VALVE_AND_SCREENED_SOURCE.txt
remain: screened geometry gives summable coefficients and source interactions;
source transition-density terms and data/source exchange must be retained
or bounded. The simple density formula is not a license to omit them.

7. Independent numerical results
-------------------------------
check_local_admission.py uses exact finite matrices, standard fermionic
annihilation operators, and exact Hermitian matrix exponentials. It does not
derive the bound by fitting data. Numerical agreement is a check of the
algebra and examples; sections 1-5 are the proof.

A. Extensive diagonal source energy is harmless to the bound when it commutes
   with the gain.

   Four source sites; G=I+.3 Z_1, hence .7I<=G<=1.3I; nearest-neighbor source
   hopping .2; arbitrary onsite disorder and density interactions multiplied
   by a common scale L; bare orbital gap Delta=100; ||W||=.15.

   At L=0 the source spectral diameter is .8944. At L=10^6 it is 3.46e6,
   more than 34000 times the bare orbital gap. In all cases

     ||[H_E,G]||=.12, s=.013, c=.0012,
     exact BCH remainder approximately .017424,
     rigorous remainder upper bound .0225795.

   At t=5 the full unitary error is approximately .08786 and the bound is
   .13890. Increasing source size from 2 to 6 sites also leaves M and the
   bound unchanged, although the source spectral diameter grows.

B. A genuinely resonant source is correctly rejected.

   Take H_c=Delta|1><1|, H_E=(Delta/2)X, G=2I+Z, and
   V=X_carrier tensor G. Here G is POSITIVE: I<=G<=3I. Start in carrier |0>
   and source |+x>. The bZ part connects it resonantly to carrier |1> and
   source |-x>, despite arbitrarily large Delta.

   For Delta=1000 and t=pi/2:

     orbital leakage from the displayed input    .99998000057
     full unitary norm error                     1.41421356237
     correct commutator-sensitive bound          1.60047168064
     false bound obtained by dropping c          .02022224492.

   The source commutator has norm 1000 and c=1. The theorem identifies the
   dangerous resource. A bare-gap argument that omits source energy transfer
   fails by almost two orders of magnitude even with positive gain.

C. Arbitrarily fast changes require no derivative term for fixed S.

   Sixteen noncommuting piecewise source Hamiltonians, with diagonal source
   norm up to 54600, vary both hopping phase and diagonal energies. Total
   time is 3 and the dressing S is identical in every segment. The actual
   unitary error is .0538221; the integrated bound is .0891689.

D. Multiple carrier energy blocks.

   A complex four-sector example with carrier energies (-7,0,0,7) and a
   noncommuting two-state source verifies anti-Hermiticity, exact cancellation,
   and the stated remainder bound. The actual remainder is .886710 and the
   bound is 1.038276. This uses the actual computed ||S|| rather than an
   unjustified minimum-gap shortcut.

8. Consequence and limit
-----------------------
The theorem replaces an extensive source-bandwidth burden by the specific
energy-transfer commutator relevant to the unwanted coupling. It is a
finite-time organizing principle for an actual operation, not a claim that
all fast or all warm sources are harmless. Large source terms that commute
with the coupling can be tolerated. A resonant noncommuting component incurs
the correct cost. Its physical use still requires actual bounds on V, W,
the source commutator, ramps, spin-breaking terms, loading, and total duration.
