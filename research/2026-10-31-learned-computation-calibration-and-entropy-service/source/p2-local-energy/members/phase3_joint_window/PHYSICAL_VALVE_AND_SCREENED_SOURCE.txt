PHYSICAL VALVE, PASSIVE OUTLET, AND A SCREENED ACTIVE SOURCE
31 October 2026. Complementary calculation for the Astra continuation.

Scope
-----
This note earns an additive, spin-independent resonator coupling from an
electrostatic projection; identifies the exact singlet capture/return chain;
gives a bounded full-mode spectral admission construction; and supplies an
extensive-source domain in which local energy-transfer estimates can replace
a total source bandwidth. These are distinct levels of assertion.

The finite admitted valve is exact. The static system block reduction has
explicit bounds. A cold, passive, broad reservoir has a controlled limiting
description, but no numerical finite-band, full-record approximation error is
proved here. The numerical examples certify the specified matrices, not an
interacting semiconductor device. The inherited loading, source, S/T return,
triplet preservation, and charge-dark-state corrections remain in force.

Later same-session development: LOCAL_ADMISSION_AUDIT.txt proves a stronger
FINITE-TIME route using ||[H_E,V]|| rather than a total source bandwidth.
Consequently the global-band construction in section 4 is one sufficient
method, not a necessary source-resource condition. The new bound also keeps
complete unitary/instrument errors. Applying it to an unbounded oscillator
still requires its stated bounded-interaction/domain hypotheses; the finite
two-level carrier construction avoids that issue directly.

1. Microscopic electrostatic projection: additive is the earned coupling
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Let w_L,w_R be real orthonormal localized orbitals, with w_R(x)=w_L(-x).
A cavity/barrier-mode electrostatic potential is u(x)(a+a^dagger), with real
even u. Its exact projection on those orbitals is

 H_cav = (a+a^dagger)[d(n_L+n_R)+g_c K],
 K=sum_sigma(c_Lsigma^dagger c_Rsigma+c_Rsigma^dagger c_Lsigma),
 d=int |w_L|^2 u=int |w_R|^2 u,
 g_c=int w_L w_R u (real).

At fixed data number n_L+n_R=2 the diagonal term is a COMMON SCALAR force.
With b=a+2d/omega,

 omega a^dagger a+2d(a+a^dagger)+g_c K(a+a^dagger)
 =omega b^dagger b+g_c K(b+b^dagger)-(4d g_c/omega)K+constant.

Thus the common force is removed exactly by a known displacement, and the
static hopping acquires a known scalar correction. The mode to cool is b.
This does not require a source-dependent displacement.

An independent quantum electrostatic source gives instead

 H_static-hop=K tensor G_E,
 G_E=t_0 I+B_E,
 B_E=int w_L(x)w_R(x) Phi_E(x) dx.

The resulting interaction is K[G_E+g_c(b+b^dagger)]. It is NOT automatically
K G_E(b+b^dagger). Multiplicative quantum Peierls gain requires a physical
position-dependent tunnelling matrix and its derivative, or another actual
interaction; it cannot be inferred by multiplying two separately projected
couplings. The scalar sideband gain g_c I already suffices for the valve.
G_E can remain active, unknown, and noncommuting with H_E; it is off resonance
during the sideband dwell.

The common hopping identity survives arbitrary real electrostatic profiles:
both spin orientations see the same Hermitian projected amplitude. Equality
of the two onsite couplings is a separate reflection condition. Charge bias
can distort real orbitals and spoil this equality; the resulting conditional
source/mode forces must be retained or bounded. A bare two-orbital projection
does not by itself qualify such distortions.

For symmetrically orthogonalized Gaussian orbitals centered at +/-a with
width sigma and overlap s=exp(-a^2/sigma^2), choose u=u_0 exp(-x^2/b^2).
Writing c=u_0 b/sqrt(b^2+sigma^2), direct integration gives

 g_c = s c[1-exp(-a^2/(b^2+sigma^2))]/(1-s^2) > 0,
 d   = c[exp(-a^2/(b^2+sigma^2))-s^2]/(1-s^2).

The nonzero matrix element is a transition-density effect. A spatially
uniform voltage has g_c=0 by orbital orthogonality.

An actual 1D double-well projection is checked in explore_double_well.py:
H=-1/2 d^2/dx^2 + b(x^2-4)^2, x in [-7,7], 2001 grid points, u=exp(-x^2/.7^2).
The two lowest exact grid eigenstates define the localized orbitals. At b=.5:

 bare tunnel amplitude                  3.7912797221e-4
 first omitted one-particle gap         3.5040713932
 equal cavity diagonal coefficient      .0086251308162
 cavity hopping coefficient             .00014645346629
 ||Q u P||                              .036187005802
 diagonal equality residual             8.6e-14

This demonstrates a real profile with common force, nonzero gain, and a
separate orbital gap. It does not calculate two-electron Coulomb admission,
biased orbital distortions, or a reservoir error. Raising b from .5 to 1.5
reduces the bare hopping by about 1029 and the mode transition-density
coefficient by about 1888, while the first orbital gap increases. This is an
explicit mechanism for joint OFF suppression of ordinary and probe/mode
assisted hopping. A finite barrier ramp still needs its own orbital following
and spin exposure budget.

2. Full S/T chain: both double charges are retained
------------------------------------------------
Let C_i=U_i-V>0 and Csum=C_L+C_R. Relative to the singly occupied singlet S,

 E(D_L)=C_L+epsilon,  E(D_R)=C_R-epsilon.

The singlet matrix of K is

 K_S=sqrt(2)(|S><D_L|+|S><D_R|+h.c.).

K annihilates EACH of the three triplet states. Every electrostatic and
mode/outlet operation is the identity on triplet orientation. This is data
spin SU(2), not merely simultaneous rotations of data and a spinful source.

Capture: epsilon=C_R+omega gives E(D_R)=-omega and E(D_L)=Csum+omega.
The resonant scalar-mode chain is

 |S,0> <-> |D_R,1> -> |D_R,0>,
 coupling sqrt(2) g_c, outlet amplitude sqrt(kappa).

Return: epsilon=C_R-omega gives E(D_R)=+omega and E(D_L)=Csum-omega.
The chain is

 |D_R,0> <-> |S,1> -> |S,0>.

Csum>3 omega, with margins for all admitted shifts, keeps D_L above the
retained active bands in BOTH phases. The weaker condition Csum>2 omega
can exclude a particular resonance but is insufficient for the simple
ordered-gap construction used below. If a full charge read reports D_L,
use epsilon=-C_L+omega; then D_L returns by the same chain and D_R remains
at Csum-omega. No D_- charge-dark superposition is silently discarded.

After a fixed capture dwell, one ordinary full-charge read distinguishes
the absorbing D_R latch from a triplet in (1,1). A single-photon counter is
not necessary. The emitted field remains part of the fine Stinespring
history, but need not be collected: every such history acts in rank-one S
and identically on the complete triplet multiplicity. Return can also run
to a certified deadline. The failure probabilities, charge instrument error,
loading, bias ramps, mode cooling, and total spin exposure must all be paid.

The resonator is a maintained cold resource. An unprepared SOURCE is allowed;
an arbitrary warm sideband mode is not. Thermal photons can drive the reverse
chain. Cooling/reset must be performed with charge transfer isolated or
detuned and must be counted in elapsed time. Approximate thermal occupation
nbar produces additional exposure on the scale kappa*nbar*T, not a free
initial-state guarantee.

3. Derivative-free absorption estimate inside the admitted valve
--------------------------------------------------------------
This supplies an independent simple bound; the inherited source theorem may
be sharper. Let a(t),b(t) be the no-emission source/reference amplitudes in
the input and bright states. Remove any common H_E(t) interaction picture.
The off-diagonal coupling A(t) is Hermitian, mu I<=A(t)<=nu I, where for
the Hubbard sideband mu=sqrt(2)g, nu=sqrt(2)h. The two conditional Hamiltonians
may differ by norm at most d. The bright amplitude decays at kappa/2.

Put N=||a||^2+||b||^2, z=Im<a,b>, V=N+alpha z. Then

 N'=-kappa ||b||^2,
 z'<=nu||b||^2-mu||a||^2+(kappa/2+d)||a||||b||.

No derivative of A or H_E occurs. With c=kappa/2+d and

 alpha=min(1, kappa/[2(nu+c^2/(2mu))]),
 m=min(alpha*mu/2,kappa/2),
 lambda=m/(1+alpha/2),

V'<=-mN<=-lambda V. For an input-only initial amplitude,

 p_no-emission(t) <= [1/(1-alpha/2)] exp(-lambda t).

The proof is just alpha*c*x*y <= alpha*mu*x^2/2+
alpha*c^2*y^2/(2mu). It applies with an arbitrary reference and every source
state. For g=h and d=0, kappa=4g gives alpha=1/sqrt(2), lambda=.369398 g,
and prefactor 1.546919. Taking kappa arbitrarily large is counterproductive:
the bound then has the ordinary overdamped rate of order g^2/kappa.

4. A full oscillator admission bound with only coarse source bandwidth
---------------------------------------------------------------------
This section concerns a STATIC microscopic segment. Explicit external drives
can supply additional energy and must be admitted separately. The source
Hamiltonian can be noncommuting, unknown, and fast compared with the valve;
assume only a finite full source spectral diameter B after common shifts.

Analyze the singlet sector first. Triplets decouple exactly and stay in mode
vacuum, so a coincident unused triplet oscillator level cannot spoil a
singlet-sector spectral argument.

Capture retained P spans {D_R,0; S,0; D_R,1} tensor E.
Return retained P spans {S,0; D_R,0; S,1} tensor E.

All other singlet charge/mode states are retained in Q, including all D_L
states and arbitrarily high oscillator number. Let ||G_E||<=h. Since P has
at most one quantum, QHP is bounded despite the unbounded oscillator:

 ||QHP|| <= v := 2h+sqrt(10)|g_c|.

For capture alone the sharper sqrt(2)h+2|g_c| suffices. The displayed bound
also covers return. The quadratic-form inequality

 |g_c <K(a+a^dagger)>| <= (omega/4)<n>+16 g_c^2/omega

uses ||K_S||=2. Together with Csum>=4omega it gives a conservative common
ordered separation of the diagonal blocks,

 inf spec(QHQ)-sup spec(PHP)
 >= Delta := omega/2-B-4h-4|g_c|-16 g_c^2/omega.

The true gap can be substantially larger. Positivity of Delta is a sufficient
whole source-plus-mode condition, not a statement about a bare orbital gap.
Additional excited data orbitals require their own analogous full admission.

Here is a useful exact graph lemma. For H=[[A,C],[C^dagger,D]], D above A by
Delta, ||C||=v<=Delta/4, the Riccati fixed point

 X=-(D . - . A)^(-1)(C^dagger-X C X)

is a contraction on ||X||<=2v/Delta. Its invariant graph defines an exact
unitary block diagonalization; the near-identity direct rotation has norm
change <=2v/Delta (4v/Delta is a convenient conservative charge). If, in
addition, diam spec(A)<=5Delta/2, the canonically normalized low Hamiltonian
satisfies

 ||H_eff-A|| <=8 v^2/Delta.

For the last estimate write S=(I+X^dagger X)^(1/2) and
H_eff=S(A+C X)S^(-1). Bound the CX term by sqrt(1+r^2) v r and the commutator
with A by r^2 diam(A)/2, where r=2v/Delta<=1/2. Their sum is less than the
displayed bound. This explicit width condition prevents an unjustified
dimension-free effective-Hamiltonian bound for an arbitrarily broad low band.

The coherent correction is RETAINED in H_eff. Debiting v^2*T/Delta would
discard the advantage of a stable dressed band. A second finite-block
rotation separates its sink band (near -omega in capture, or 0 in return)
from its active band. This adds dressing O(h/omega) and coherent corrections
O(h^2/omega); they alter detunings and gain and must be bounded or calibrated.
A sufficient asymptotic hierarchy is

 h^2/omega << g_c,   |g_c|~kappa << omega,
 B << omega,        Csum>=4omega,
 plus all higher-orbital matrix elements small on their own full domains.

This hierarchy is nonempty: take h=eta*omega, g_c=c*eta*omega with fixed c>0,
and eta->0, while B/omega remains a fixed small number. Then h^2/(omega*g_c)
and all dressing vanish, yet B/g_c diverges. The source can consequently be
arbitrarily fast RELATIVE to the useful gate without assuming an unbounded
source spectrum. An exact finite-orbital theorem that tolerates arbitrary
source speed does not erase this separate physical spectral requirement.

5. Outlet passivity: the local annihilator is not the microscopic answer
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Let P_- and P_0 be the dressed sink and active bands. The physical outgoing
field couples through the LOWERING component

 A_out=P_- (a+a^dagger) P_0.

At zero bath temperature it cannot promote the system out of its lowest
band. In the admitted limit A_out approaches |sink><bright| tensor I_E.
All detailed source transitions within this band must be retained unless a
separate secular justification is available.

Blindly inserting a local dissipator kappa D[a] into the FULL non-RWA
Hamiltonian is not valid. Its exact dressed ground state contains virtual
oscillator population. Acting with bare a can leave an excited state and
spuriously deposit energy even with a vacuum outlet. This is verified by
check_resonant_valve.py and is an independent decisive counterexample.

A physical reservoir may be chosen with positive-frequency spectral support
around omega, width Lambda, cold input, and smooth coupling over the active
transition band. The useful hierarchy is

 B, |g_c|, kappa, conditional shifts << Lambda << omega.

The appropriate limiting argument separates positive and negative carrier
frequency bands, while retaining the source's internal evolution. It does
not resolve every unknown source Bohr spacing. A fully secular Davies
calculation is not a uniform physical error certificate when those spacings
can become arbitrarily small. The unified near-degenerate weak-coupling
construction of Trushechkin (2021) is relevant; his rigorous scaling keeps
near-degenerate splittings at the dissipative order. The extended weak-
coupling limit of Derezinski and De Roeck (2008) addresses unreduced unitary
dilations and reservoir observables, which is the relevant level for emitted
histories rather than just a reduced density matrix.

These primary results justify a serious controlled-limit route. This note
does NOT turn them into a numerical finite-band full-instrument bound for
the present device. In particular, no unsupported estimate such as
"error=O(kappa/Lambda)" is assigned to the entire long dwell. A proper
full-record convergence argument must specify the bath form factor, limiting
scaling, field-coordinate identification, Lamb shifts, and time interval.

actual retained-H correction                   2.0311327e-8
coarse conservative correction bound           8.6086506e-7
band-lowering operator change                  7.0710675e-6

The two active off-diagonal singular values are both about sqrt(2)*1e-5.

7. A concrete extensive-source domain for local energy-transfer laws
-------------------------------------------------------------------
A total source bandwidth grows with the number of bounded source sites and
can become an unnecessarily strong burden. Screened electrostatics gives a
physical alternative compatible with the same data hopping projection.

Take a two-dimensional source lattice of spacing a_s, with a bounded local
occupation n_i<=n_max, finite-range hopping, and a metallic screening plane
at distance d_s. For source charges in the same plane the image interaction is

 V(r)=C[1/r-1/sqrt(r^2+4d_s^2)] <=2 C d_s^2/r^3.

It is positive and summable on the two-dimensional lattice. A simple square-
lattice shell estimate gives

 sum_(j!=i) V(r_ij) <= (8 pi^2/3) C d_s^2/a_s^3.

Thus the local interaction norm

 J=sup_i sum_(X contains i)||h_X||

is bounded independently of source area for bounded onsite and hopping
terms; the Coulomb contribution is at most n_max^2 times the displayed sum.
Each interaction acts on at most two sites even though its range is long.
An arbitrary source many-body state, including one correlated with data and
references, is allowed. Its TOTAL energy can grow extensively.

The projected source gain has the form G=t_0 I+sum_i b_i n_i. Orbital
orthogonality removes its transition-density monopole. Reflection removes
the dipole. With image screening the far coupling falls at least as r^-5
in a generic fixed-height source plane; its coefficient norm
b=sum_i |b_i| n_max is finite. Exact far-field powers depend on geometry,
but summability already follows from the screened potential and bounded
data form factors. A charge-conditional source difference
V=H_D-H_S=sum_i v_i n_i has a dipolar leading data form factor and screened
far coefficients of order r^-4; v=sum_i |v_i|n_max is also finite.

For a source Hamiltonian with interaction support size <=2, a local operator
A on m sites obeys the elementary repeated-commutator bound

 ||ad_H^k(A)|| <= ||A||(2J)^k (m+k-1)!/(m-1)!.

Every nonzero commutator must overlap the current support; the next two-site
term adds at most one site. Its summed norm is at most 2J times the current
support size. Apply this termwise to the absolutely convergent one-site sum
in G. With H_D=H_S, the scalar t_0 disappears after one commutator and

 ||ad_H^k(G)|| <= b(2J)^k k!,  k>=1.

For the inter-sector transfer operator L(A)=H_D A-A H_S=[H_S,A]+V A,
where V is an absolutely summable ONE-SITE potential, a termwise support
argument instead gives

 ||L^k(A)|| <= ||A|| product_(j=0 to k-1)[2J(m+j)+v]

for initially m-site A. The identity part can be handled from support m=0;
for a convenient single bound use m=1 and total coefficient
|t_0|+sum_i |b_i|n_max. These factorial estimates are independent of total
source size. They provide the local microscopic constants needed by the
parent energy-transfer-tail theorem.

Screening is substantive. An unscreened two-dimensional Coulomb interaction
has a divergent absolute local interaction sum; an unscreened conditional
dipolar potential can also fail summability. Spatial separation alone does
not justify a uniform local norm. The screening plane's quasistatic range,
its own noise, and data/source exchange remain physical qualifications.

The gain coupling, conditional shift, and off-orbital transition operators
need not have the same local constants. In particular, a tail bound for the
S/T gain cannot be reused without calculation for orbital excitation or
data/source spin exchange.

For a finite-contact proof that uses only the FIRST commutator, the density
source gives a sharper explicitly microscopic constant. In the spinless
bounded-occupation model,

 H_E=sum_i epsilon_i n_i+sum_(i<j) V_ij n_i n_j
     +sum_<ij> t_ij(c_i^dagger c_j+c_j^dagger c_i),
 G=t_0 I+sum_i b_i n_i,

all density terms commute with G. Directly,

 [H_E,G]=sum_<ij> t_ij(b_j-b_i)(c_i^dagger c_j-c_j^dagger c_i),
 ||[H_E,G]||<=sum_<ij>|t_ij||b_j-b_i|
              <=z t_max sum_i|b_i|.

The last inequality assumes degree <=z. Add the specified spin/occupation
multiplicity for a spinful source. For a conditional source shift V,

 ||H_D G-G H_S|| <= ||[H_S,G]||+||V|| ||G||.

This is the directly useful constant for finite off-resonant contact bounds.
It can be much smaller than the bound obtained from the entire local
interaction strength J. Screening still supplies a robust bound on V and on
successive commutators; density commutation alone does not control them all.

This density-source specialization must not conceal source transition
densities. A full orbital Coulomb projection also contains coefficients
B_ij c_i^dagger c_j in G and in conditional source potentials. One may bound
these by the actual localized-orbital overlaps, or retain them and use the
general screened local commutator estimate. Keeping source tunnelling while
silently discarding all its transition-density couplings is not a physical
closure argument. Data/source particle exchange likewise needs its own
bound over the complete elapsed time; data-only spin symmetry is lost if
exchange with an uncontrolled spinful source is admitted without a bound.

check_screened_source.py evaluates the source couplings from orthonormalized
Gaussian data orbitals (a=.8,sigma=.4), source lattice spacing 2, data height
1, source height 2, image plane height 0, and Coulomb coefficient 1. At 81,
1089, and 16641 source sites respectively, the screened values are:

 sum |b_i|:           .00649859, .00656351, .00656500
 sum |v_i|:           1.21112,   1.29301,   1.29961
 source interaction:  3.37898,   4.24138,   4.49536.

In the same unscreened calculation, the conditional norm sum grows from
2.69421 to 6.93624 and the central-site interaction sum grows from 13.9407
to 225.446. The asymptotic convergence/divergence is proved by the powers
above; this table is a numerical witness, not the proof. A bare t_0 larger
than the convergent sum |b_i| gives a positive hopping floor independent of
source area and arbitrary occupation. These example constants are not a
claimed jointly optimized device window; the source geometry, data charging
energies, and helper detuning must still be chosen together.

8. Quadratic frequency response and what it does not prove
---------------------------------------------------------
For static source Hamiltonians H_S=sum_a e_a P_a and H_D=sum_b f_b Q_b,
let G_nu=sum_(f_b-e_a=nu) Q_b G P_a. Then

 sum_nu G_nu^dagger G_nu
 =sum_a P_a G^dagger G P_a.

If G^dagger G>=g^2 I, the full frequency-resolved transition effect is at
least g^2 I. A positive gain is sufficient but not necessary for this
quadratic identity. For L(G)=H_D G-G H_S,

 sum_nu |nu|^(2k) G_nu^dagger G_nu
 =sum_a P_a [L^k(G)]^dagger L^k(G) P_a
 <=||L^k(G)||^2 I.

Therefore an admitted spectral band |nu|<=W loses at most
||L^k(G)||^2/W^(2k) of its effect. In the screened source domain above, choose
k proportional to W/J to obtain exponential tails with constants that do
not depend on source area. A downhill carrier offset larger than W, with a
reservoir accepting the full window, retains a positive absorption floor;
reverse and wrong-charge events require high-frequency source transfers.

This is a quadratic, frequency-resolved statement. It does not directly
bound the norm of a COHERENT sum of unresolved G_nu. A finite-time or
nonsecular application must retain cross-frequency terms. In particular it
is not, by itself, an orbital block-diagonalization theorem for a huge source
whose total spectrum overlaps the omitted orbital band.

Nor can quadratic frequency response undo a microscopic cancellation that
has already made the actual transition vertex G vanish. The inherited
complete-intermediate-state association cancellation is preserved.

9. Comparison at the actual task
--------------------------------
Inelastic charge transfer and frozen/latching Pauli blockade are established
components. They should be used where they improve the apparatus. This note
does not claim those mechanisms as new. The comparison must concern a full
triplet-preserving, returned S/T INSTRUMENT on an arbitrary source/reference
state, including elapsed time and all outlet histories.

Calibrated exchange may be much faster when the same source uncertainty is
small enough or can be learned. Ordinary full-STP sensing can use the same
charge latch and cold outlet. Ordinary dissipative control can use the same
sideband and finite-band admission. The contribution developed here is a
source-uniform operational guarantee and a possible replacement of an
extensive source bandwidth by LOCAL energy-transfer resources. A new device
advantage must still be shown under identical source information and control
resources; neither the failed naive coherent route nor a very conservative
old probe count establishes it.

The valve removes repeated charge interrogation from the ideal capture and
return phases: a latch can be prepared in one dissipative dwell and read
once. It pays a cooled mode, engineered positive-frequency outlet, finite
ramps, passive-band qualification, and actual spin exposure. Nothing here
earns a fault-tolerant processor or a material parameter point.
