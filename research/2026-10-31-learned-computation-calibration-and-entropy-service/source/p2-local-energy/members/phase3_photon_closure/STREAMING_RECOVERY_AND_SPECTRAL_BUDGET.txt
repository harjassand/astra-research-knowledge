STREAMING RECOVERY, THE LOCATION OF THE LOGICAL ALGEBRA, AND A PHOTON BANDWIDTH BUDGET
2026-10-31. New work; the inherited optical and spin artifacts are unchanged.

RESULT AND DECISION

The split-manifold optical identity has a more economical consumer than a
stored pair of photons followed by a simultaneous Bell analyzer. A single
prepared matter qubit, one fixed controlled phase interaction with every
informative photon, X-resolved photon counting, and one final matter
measurement suffice. The corrections require only two accumulated classical
parities. They require neither time matching between the photons nor an
optical storage buffer. A predetermined drive-off time, followed by full
optical settling, suffices if the logical output may reside in either the
original emitter or the receiver memory, with its location reported.

The organizing law is where the noncommuting logical algebra resides.
For an even number N of informative photons, the two global polarization
Paulis commute and their classical outcomes can restore the emitter. For
odd N they anticommute: a qubit is still in the field, and some quantum
system must retain it. One receiver qubit is sufficient and necessary for
an online decoder that consumes the first photon before the return photon.

A second useful result closes a different apparent obstruction. In the
finite optical interaction with source-independent fine jumps, a bound on
source SPEED is not needed to obtain a finite emitted-light bandwidth
budget. Bounded coupling, loaded ground code, and a finite drive duration
give a uniform bound on the total photon spectral second moment. This is
an application of continuous-matrix-product-state regularity and its
commutator derivative formula, with an explicit source-independent bound.
It is not a claim to have discovered that general mathematical formalism.

This does not complete an optical computer. Efficient coherent collection,
a source-independent physical outlet including recoil, a sufficiently
good matter-photon interaction on the occupied optical modes, low loss,
receiver memory coherence, and logical operations after the handoff remain
paid resources. Single-photon cavity scattering is derived below; its
one-excitation calculation does NOT alone prove correct scattering of
overlapping photons. The strongest present contribution is the organizing
algebra and its constructive, audited resource reduction.

1. INHERITED EXACT INPUT, WITH NO REMOVAL OF HISTORIES

Use the model and notation of
next_recycling_optics/STATIC_SPLIT_PHOTON_PAIR_MEMORY.txt.
The carrier C is logical qubit tensor three optical states; the bright
sector B is three-dimensional. Their no-jump evolution and all z jumps
act identically on the logical vectors in C. The two informative fine
jumps have the exact form

 L_j = sqrt(Gamma)|b><j,t| + sqrt(Gamma/2)|j,g><q_B|, j=x,y,

tensored with I_source. Informative photons alternate C->B and B->C.
For each completed pair and every retained auxiliary history h,

 K_{k j,h} = |k><j| tensor M_h.

All noncommuting source operators, safe z emissions, waiting intervals,
and source backaction remain in their actual order in M_h. There is no
source-state average. The gain is bounded, and H_E(t) may evolve as rapidly
as desired on its admitted domain. Initial logical/reference/source
correlations are allowed by the positive recovery statements.

For reasoning about polarization alone, use normalized maps

 D: |j>_L -> |b>|j>_P,
 R: |b> -> (|0>_L|0>_P+|1>_L|1>_P)/sqrt(2),

with all scalar and source-dependent amplitudes assigned to the actual
history operator. The labels 0,1 denote the collected x,y channels; these
are abstract orthogonal radiation channels until the physical collection
optics is specified. An isotropic dipole outlet does not automatically
turn into two co-propagating polarization modes in a single fiber.

2. ONE MEMORY, THE SAME INTERACTION WITH EVERY PHOTON

Prepare receiver memory A in |+>. For every informative photon P apply

 CZ_AP = |0><0|_P tensor I_A + |1><1|_P tensor Z_A,

then measure P in the X basis. Record its bit m. There is no adaptive basis
change or memory rotation between photons. Let s be the XOR of all these
bits, and let N be the total number of informative photons.

When an odd photon leaves C, the resulting map from L,A to A is

 O_m = (1/sqrt(2)) sum_j (-1)^(m j) <j|_L tensor Z_A^j.

When an even photon returns from B, the map from A to L,A is

 E_n = (1/2) sum_k (-1)^(n k) |k>_L tensor Z_A^k.

The decisive identities are

 O_m (I_L tensor |+>_A) = (1/sqrt(2)) H_A Z_A^m,
 O_m E_n = delta_{m,n} I_A/sqrt(2).

The second identity says that each return-and-subsequent-departure relay
returns the stored qubit unchanged. In the time-resolved ideal record,
the measurement outcomes on each neighboring even/odd pair coincide.
These equal outcomes cancel in s.

After drive-off and complete drain:

 * N=0: the input remains in the emitter code, with identity logical action.
 * N odd: A contains H Z^s times the input. Apply or track Z^s H and use A.
 * N positive and even: measure A in X, obtaining q. The emitter contains
   Z^s X^q times the input. Apply or track X^q Z^s and use the emitter.

All equalities are up to irrelevant outcome-dependent global phases.
Every corrected fine Kraus operator is I_logical tensor an actual source
history operator. The output-location bit is part of the instrument.

There is one matter preparation per observation interval and one final
matter readout, rather than one reset per emitted photon pair. Every
informative photon still encounters a controlled phase interaction and a
detector. The memory may remain exposed for the entire interval.

3. GLOBAL PARITY PROOF: PHOTON ORDER AND TEMPORAL MATCHING ARE UNNECESSARY

Define P_X to swap the x,y polarization channels of every emitted photon
in every temporal/spatial mode. Define P_Z=(-1)^(N_y), the y-photon number
parity. On the N-photon sector,

 P_X P_Z = (-1)^N P_Z P_X.

At the end of an even informative history, write its polarization/source
isometry as V_N. Directly from D and R,

 P_X V_N = X_L V_N X_in,
 P_Z V_N = Z_L V_N Z_in.

One way to see this is to retain the first input photon, Bell pairs between
each intervening return/departure pair, and the final Bell pair between
last photon and emitter. The intermediate Bell pairs are stabilized by
both X tensor X and Z tensor Z. The same equations hold for the full
source-correlated wavefunction, not just a selected source vector.

If a fine field measurement vector has global parities (-1)^s,(-1)^q,
its induced emitter operator K obeys

 X_L K X_in = (-1)^s K,
 Z_L K Z_in = (-1)^q K.

The simultaneous eigenspace of these two conjugations on a qubit operator
is one-dimensional. Therefore K = Z^s X^q tensor M for some auxiliary M.
This proves recovery for EVERY fine refinement inside a joint parity
sector, including temporal-mode and detector records.

The memory interaction accumulated over the entire field is exactly

 U = |0><0|_A tensor I_field + |1><1|_A tensor P_Z.

Memory X readout measures P_Z. Local photon X readouts refine P_X, whose
eigenvalue is the XOR s of their bits. For even N these observables commute.
No pairing or temporal overlap measurement is needed.

For odd N, the emitter is bright and

 P_X V_N = V_N X_in,   P_Z V_N = V_N Z_in.

Thus the field itself carries a representation of the two anticommuting
logical Paulis. If a photon-X measurement vector has parity s, its row map
F satisfies F X_in=(-1)^s F and hence has the form M <s_X|. The controlled
P_Z interaction with |+>_A transfers this field qubit into A as H Z^s,
again tensor a common M. Reading A too would destroy an unknown qubit in
this odd sector, which is why its final measurement is parity-dependent.

Any polarization-independent unitary on hidden photon modes commutes with
P_X, P_Z, and N. It may cause arbitrary common dispersion or mix emission
times without invalidating these arguments. In particular a common
frequency-dependent reflection phase is harmless, and unknown temporal
mode matching is not a new premise. Polarization-dependent loss or
dispersion does not receive this exemption.

4. NECESSARY QUANTUM MEMORY AND THE FIRST-PHOTON LOSS LIMIT

Choose an initially maximally entangled reference/logical pair and a
product source state. Immediately after the first informative departure,
the original emitter is bright and the reference is entangled with the
departed photon. If that photon is consumed and only classical information
about it is retained, all future-accessible systems are separable from the
reference. Subsequent operations cannot restore entanglement. The best
qubit entanglement fidelity of such a measure-and-prepare channel is 1/2.

Exact online recovery therefore requires a quantum carrier of dimension
at least two across the interval between departure and return. Optical
delay, optical storage, a prepared matter memory, or a future system to
which the state was teleported all count. Pre-shared entanglement does not
evade the statement: if teleportation moves the qubit to a future system,
that system is the quantum carrier. One prepared qubit saturates this
dimension requirement for the present algebra.

Loss before ANY interaction or record is more severe than a classical
measurement. Conditioned on loss of the first informative photon, every
accessible system is independent of the input qubit. The channel is then
constant and its entanglement fidelity is exactly 1/4, even after optimal
later operations. If the first transfer occurs with probability p and
that photon is collected with efficiency eta, then for this product-source
test

 1-F_e >= (3/4) p (1-eta).

This is a worst-case necessary condition for uniform recovery. A known
erasure flag can tell the consumer that the input was lost, but cannot
restore an unencoded unknown input. Outer coding is a genuinely additional
resource and has not been constructed here.

A later detected photon can be the return photon after an undetected first
photon. Its arrival is not a trustworthy herald of transfer of the original
input. The duplicate ideal X outcomes detect some faults, not all losses.
Even a final QND C/B population measurement would only reveal the parity
of the number of missed informative photons; two missed photons evade it.
An exact loss-free counter cannot be supplied by ordinary destructive
counting. We therefore charge any missed informative photon conservatively.

5. FINITE DURATION WITHOUT A FAST STOP SIGNAL

Choose a drive-off time T, including the real ramp, independently of
detector feedback. Keep the fixed receiver interaction and photon counting
active while the emitter drains. In the inherited model, drive-off leaves
only the block-diagonal excited Hamiltonian and the source Hamiltonian.
There can be at most one additional spontaneous photon, and

 p_excited(T+s) = exp(-Gamma s) p_excited(T).

At complete drain, N parity decides which output carrier contains the
qubit. This removes the need to stop the drive within a small fraction of
an optical lifetime after a completed pair. A finite settling time s has
an explicit residual population tail p<=exp(-Gamma s); a full endpoint
instrument must charge an amplitude error, conservatively p+sqrt(p).
Known photon flight time and receiver ringdown must be included before
the final parity decision. The receiver's ringdown is a separate physical
calculation; it is not automatically Gamma^{-1}.

If the task REQUIRES the final qubit at the original emitter, an odd ending
still needs a return stage. The inherited bright-return bound applies on
its stated gain domain:

 Pr(no B->C by t) <= (9/2) exp[-11 Gamma t/180].

The streaming decoder can stay active during that stage, and its memory
need not be reset. Nevertheless, enforcing an even final count still
requires a latch, a qualified stop/retry strategy, or a later calibrated
state transfer. The variable-location output is the earned way to remove
the immediate stopping burden; it is a real change to the task contract,
not an implicit promise of same-site return.

For a drive interval T starting in ground, the total instantaneous emission
rate is at most Gamma. Therefore total emissions during the drive are
stochastically dominated by Poisson(Gamma T), and full drain adds at most
one photon. In particular

 E[N_informative] <= E[N_total] <= Gamma T+1.

With polarization-independent per-photon collection/detection efficiency
eta, a union bound gives

 Pr(any missed informative photon) <= (1-eta)(Gamma T+1).

Use min(1, right-hand side). A sharper apparatus calculation can retain
the actual count distribution; the union bound is not a lower bound on
what a better apparatus can achieve. The Poisson tail also provides an
explicit finite-photon-number cutoff for receiver error budgets.

Memory noise is charged over actual elapsed time, including drive, flight,
drain, and readout. For example, a memory with off-diagonal decay
exp(-gamma_M t) has phase-flip error (1-exp(-gamma_M t))/2. Such a maintained
memory is a prepared resource, unlike the unprepared common source.

6. SOURCE-UNIFORM SPECTRAL BUDGET FROM THE FULL EMITTED FIELD

This section does not assume that the source has a bounded frequency,
that its gain commutes at different times, or that its state is known.
It is a statement within the admitted finite optical Markov model, with
fresh incident vacuum in the emission channels.

Let H_0 be the excited fine-structure Hamiltonian, zero on ground, with
Delta=max(|Delta_1|,|Delta_2|). Let ||A(t)||<=a while the drive is on, and
zero afterward. The atom begins in ground; the source may be arbitrary.
Define

 G(t) = -i H(t) - (Gamma/2) P_excited,
 C_l(t) = [L_l,G(t)],
 d^2 = Delta^2+Gamma^2/4.

Because L_l acts as the identity on the source, [L_l,H_E(t)]=0. Equivalently
one may enter the source interaction picture: H_E disappears and A(t)
becomes an arbitrarily rapidly varying but still bounded operator.

For a complete ordered photon wavefunction

 Psi(t_1,...,t_n) = K(T_f,t_n)L_ln ... L_l1 K(t_1,0)|initial>,

differentiation with respect to t_i replaces L_li by C_li(t_i). Crucially,
this differentiates the neighboring propagators, not A(t_i); there is no
derivative-of-gain or source-frequency term. The following boundary facts
are needed:

 * L_l|initial>=0, because the loaded optical state is ground.
 * L_l L_m=0, so every two-emission coincidence boundary is continuous
   with zero amplitude.
 * After finite drive-off, the excited tail decays, so no hard final
   time truncation is made before evaluating the full field spectrum.

Consequently the full output wavefunction has a square-integrable first
temporal derivative. Summing its squared derivatives over all photon
numbers, labels, times, final system states and arbitrary reference/source
purifications gives the exact identity

 M_2 := sum_l integral d omega omega^2 <b_l^dagger(omega)b_l(omega)>
      = integral_0^infinity dt Tr[rho(t) sum_l C_l^dagger(t)C_l(t)].

The equality follows either directly by marking one jump in the complete
trajectory expansion and summing its future trace-preserving evolution,
or from the cMPS derivative/kinetic-energy formula [R1]. This is a moment
of the ENTIRE emitted field, not the spectrum of a chosen averaged
susceptibility or a no-return trajectory.

For the full Cartesian optical model, split C_l into its drive-free part
and its drive part. Their stacked operator norms obey

 ||(C_l^0)_l|| <= sqrt(Gamma) d,
 ||(C_l^drive)_l|| <= sqrt(3 Gamma) a.

To verify the second identity, write L_l=sqrt(Gamma)D_l^dagger and the
excitation coupling as sqrt(3)D_z tensor A. Then

 [L_l,H_drive] = sqrt(3 Gamma)
    (delta_lz P_ground - D_z D_l^dagger) tensor A,

and

 sum_l (delta_lz P_ground-D_zD_l^dagger)^dagger
       (delta_lz P_ground-D_zD_l^dagger) = I.

For a drive supported on [0,T], the drive-free tail contributes at most
d^2, since its excited population integrates to at most 1/Gamma. Thus

 M_2 <= Gamma (d+sqrt(3)a)^2 T+d^2 =: B_2(T).

More accurate state-dependent or time-dependent amplitude bounds may be
used in the integral identity. The uniform coarse bound is sufficient
for the new implication. Let N_out(B) count photons whose rotating-frame
detuning has magnitude greater than B. Then

 Pr(N_out(B)>=1) <= E[N_out(B)] <= B_2(T)/B^2.

This is a source-speed-independent admission bound for a finite-band
receiver. If a coherent field operation is only certified inside the
band, projection/gentle-measurement arguments give a conservative channel
error at most 2 sqrt(B_2(T))/B for replacing the field by its band-limited
part, in addition to the within-band operation error. The square root is
appropriate because off-band amplitudes may remain coherent with in-band
amplitudes. One must not charge only the small spectral probability when
claiming a coherent instrument bound.

The bound can be recentered at a chosen optical carrier c by replacing
H_0 in the drive-free commutator with H_0-c P_excited and using the largest
|Delta_j-c|, including the zero fine-structure eigenvalue. This can improve
constants without changing the source-independence argument.

This does not reverse the inherited sideband counterexample. A rapidly
evolving source can resonantly bridge a large optical detuning, producing
substantial harmful emission. The bound includes the full admitted
optical splitting Delta and constrains far-frequency weight; it does not
assert that bare detuning suppresses the relevant on-resonance process.

The price of uniformity can be large: B needed by this moment inequality
grows as sqrt(T)/desired amplitude error. A calibrated narrowband source
may need a much less demanding receiver. This sufficient bound is not a
practical-bandwidth lower bound.

7. WHY THE SPECTRAL LAW HAS REAL QUALIFICATIONS

An initially excited atom abruptly introduced at t=0 has a one-sided
exponential emission amplitude with a nonzero endpoint value. Its ideal
Markov Lorentzian spectrum has divergent second moment. It is excluded
by the loaded-ground condition; include the finite physical preparation
history if that condition is to be relaxed.

If the outlet changes the source, the commutator [L,H_E] can grow with
source speed. For example a source spin flip in the emission operator
can attach a source Zeeman splitting to the emitted photon. The present
bound then fails without an additional commutator/domain bound. The
source-independent outlet is therefore structural, not a cosmetic
calibration assumption.

Likewise, extrapolating a finite optical manifold, a Markov bath, or
recoil-free dipole collection beyond its physical validity is not justified
by the mathematical tail estimate. Real orbital, frequency, reservoir and
recoil admission remains separate, as in the inherited work. The result
removes a source-speed assumption WITHIN that admitted model; it does not
prove the microscopic admission of an arbitrary atom or a broadband cavity.

8. CONCRETE CAVITY CONTROLLED PHASE: WHAT IS DERIVED

A known strong component is a one-sided cavity with a long-lived matter
qubit. One memory state is optically uncoupled; the other couples to one
photon polarization through a transition of vacuum Rabi coupling g and
spontaneous linewidth gamma. Let the useful cavity port have linewidth
kappa and, for this calculation, no internal cavity loss. Arrange the
uncoupled photon polarization to have the same empty-cavity reflection
as the three uncoupled joint logical branches. This can use a polarization
degenerate cavity or a matched reference optical path; its matching must
be calibrated. Directional routing must also prevent the reflected field
from coherently feeding back into the emitter; otherwise the inherited
one-way outlet model has changed.

In the single-excitation input-output equations, with frequency detuning w,
the reflected amplitudes are

 r_0(w) = 1-kappa/(kappa/2-iw),
 r_1(w) = 1-kappa(gamma/2-iw)/D(w),
 D(w) = (kappa/2-iw)(gamma/2-iw)+g^2.

The spontaneous-loss probability for the coupled branch is

 ell(w)=kappa gamma g^2/|D(w)|^2,
 |r_1(w)|^2+ell(w)=1.

At resonance, r_0=-1 and r_1=(C-1)/(C+1), where
C=4g^2/(kappa gamma). Therefore the reflected operation is a controlled
phase in the large-cooperativity limit, after a common empty-cavity phase.
For |w|<=B and D_min=g^2+kappa gamma/4-B^2>0,

 |r_1(w)+r_0(w)| <= zeta_B
   = 2B/sqrt((kappa/2)^2+B^2)
     + kappa sqrt((gamma/2)^2+B^2)/D_min,

 ell(w) <= ell_B = kappa gamma g^2/D_min^2.

These are uniform operator bounds on a photon entangled with an unknown
source or other temporal modes; no wavepacket shape is substituted. The
common phase r_0 has modulus one and commutes with the global polarization
parities. It may therefore be retained exactly, rather than counted as
logical error. In the one-photon sector, the full reflected/lost instrument
has conservative half trace/diamond error <=zeta_B+ell_B/2 from the ideal
controlled phase, with loss included as a separate vacuum flag. Collection,
detector and internal cavity losses add their actual contributions.

For a genuinely isolated pair, ideal full Bell discrimination has no
structural 1/2 ceiling in this circuit. Its actual two-photon survival and
readout probability is bounded by the product of collection, scattering,
detection and memory-readout efficiencies. Successful survival is not a
reliable initial-input herald in an unflagged continuous stream with missed
earlier photons.

The model requires a prepared and controlled receiver qubit, not an
unprepared thermal receiver. It is the conventional cavity matter-photon
gate studied by Duan and Kimble [R2] and demonstrated by Reiserer et al.
[R3]. This component is not new. Its value here is that a single fixed
interaction can decode an indefinite stream with source-independent
parity algebra.

9. WHAT THE SINGLE-PHOTON CAVITY CALCULATION DOES NOT SUPPLY

A two-level optical transition saturates when photons overlap. Its
many-photon scattering operator is not automatically the product of the
one-photon reflection matrices. Therefore zeta_B is not, by itself, an
error bound for the entire streaming receiver. One must either qualify a
linear memory-controlled phase interaction on the occupied photon-number
domain, or bound the saturation/recovery error in the actual cascaded
emitter-receiver dynamics.

The source model does provide useful leverage. Immediately after ANY
emission, the emitter is in ground. For a gain bounded by a, the probability
of another emission within a short interval tau is bounded by

 Pr(next emission within tau | actual past) <= Gamma a^2 tau^3,

using the same ground-to-excited amplitude estimate as the inherited
stopping theorem. A union bound over the finite count budget controls the
weight of close-emission histories. The source speed is absent. To turn
that leverage into a complete receiver theorem, one still needs the
receiver's response tail, a bound on its nonlinear multi-photon scattering,
and coherent treatment of the exceptional temporal amplitudes. We do not
replace those with an unproved product-channel approximation.

For a receiver already qualified with a total controlled-parity error
epsilon_receiver on N<=n and the admitted spectral band, a transparent
whole-interval budget is

 epsilon_total <= epsilon_receiver + epsilon_memory(T_elapsed)
                  + epsilon_local_controls + epsilon_readout
                  + (1-eta)(Gamma T+1)
                  + Pr(Poisson(Gamma T)+1>n)
                  + 2 sqrt(B_2(T))/B
                  + epsilon_optical_settle + epsilon_receiver_drain.

Each term is capped as appropriate. Per-photon one-excitation errors may
be telescoped into epsilon_receiver only AFTER a valid many-photon or
isolated-pulse qualification. Actual-history factorization makes source
reset unnecessary. It does not grant loss-free photons, a noiseless
memory, or a qualified receiver by notation.

10. FAIR ESTABLISHED COMPARATORS AND THE CHOICE OF CONSEQUENCE

Environment-assisted correction already distinguishes what can be repaired
from a fixed classical environment record and what a coherent environment
measurement can repair; Gregoratti and Werner [R4] are a foundational
reference. Teleportation and memory-mediated Bell analysis are established.
The present contribution is their exact fixed decoder for this complete
source-correlated optical stream, the odd/even logical-algebra criterion,
and the explicit source-uniform receiver bandwidth budget.

Arenskotter et al. [R5] experimentally demonstrated the two constituent
steps of a single-ion-memory Bell analyzer: incoming photon-to-memory
mapping and a full memory-photon Bell projection via heralded absorption.
They demonstrated the two parts separately. Their free-space single-pass
absorption probability was 1.04e-3 and herald detection efficiency 1.64%,
giving a heralded absorption probability 1.71e-5. These are not a nearly
deterministic completed two-photon analyzer. The paper reports a mean
teleportation process fidelity 76(9)% without background/binning correction
and 81(5)% with those corrections. Its structure is a serious comparator;
its low-rate heralding does not protect an unknown input after the input
photon has already been lost. [All these figures come from its full paper,
not an inference about a future improved apparatus.]

Static passive linear optics with vacuum ancillas has a 50% unambiguous
Bell-discrimination ceiling in the usual dual-rail setting [R6]. This is
not a bound on all linear-optical apparatus. Additional photon ancillas
give 75% and near-unit constructions [R7]; squeezing, hyperentanglement,
memory interfaces and nonlinear interactions change the resource contract.
Their photons, detectors, mode control, loss and storage must be compared
on equal terms. A failed destructive Bell outcome is not a harmless
repeat-until-success step on an unknown input.

If the task permits relocating the logical state to a good matter memory,
the strongest conventional competitor is simply to transfer the first
informative photon into that memory and retain it. Returning the qubit to
the active emitter buys no automatic memory advantage. The streaming
scheme is useful specifically when continuous field collection, delayed
choice of endpoint, or maintaining an accessible emitter are required;
its extra interactions must earn their cost. No practical advantage over
equally informed calibrated control is claimed here.

RESEARCH CONCLUSION

The photon-pair identity is best understood as transport of a noncommuting
logical algebra. It can be decoded by one prepared qubit and two field
parities, while a bounded source-independent outlet gives a finite optical
bandwidth budget despite arbitrary source dynamics. These are useful
organizing results and concrete reductions of the inherited apparatus
requirements. Loss of the first exported qubit remains irreversible
without encoding, and a complete saturable-receiver/collection calculation
is still needed before claiming a physical active-emitter memory advantage.
