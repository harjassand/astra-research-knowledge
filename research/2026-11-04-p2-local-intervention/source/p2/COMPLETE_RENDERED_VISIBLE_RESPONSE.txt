Completed construction: local intervention without recovering the whole dynamical model

I chose the question:

Can we predict and simulate a local intervention in a large stochastic system when its microscopic rates and event history are unavailable—and determine exactly what information and operations make this possible?

I carried this through to three connected results:

An exact method for acquiring a local transition rate, with matching upper and lower bounds on its cost.
A positive simulation method for finite local interventions, including suppressing transitions to zero, with an explicit error bound and no requirement to learn the rates first.
An exact identification boundary: two reversible systems can have the same complete passive observed trajectory law, the same observed mobility, and the same linear responses, yet respond differently to the same finite force.

The proofs, independent audits, numerical checks and executed construction are complete for the stated classes. Historical priority and a civilisation-changing application remain unestablished.

1. Why I selected this opening

The larger scientific objective is reliable intervention: knowing what will happen when we alter a system, even when most of that system is inaccessible or too complicated to reconstruct.

The baseline comparison ruled out several easy claims.

Rare-event prediction already has strong constructive methods. Learned committors and transition-path sampling have substantial implementations and applications. One recent protein–ligand study reports recovering kinetics across systems spanning roughly 17 orders of magnitude in residence time; its code and supplementary data are available. That made another generic committor or variational-rate construction a weak choice without a distinct missing operation. 
Zenodo
+1

Nonlinear response without a complete microscopic model is also established. Müller, Basu, Sollich and Krüger show how weak active measurements of coarse path weights can determine second-order response, including time-dependent perturbations and situations without a separation of timescales. Their method acquires information through perturbation experiments. 
arxiv.org

Dynamical reweighting is a serious comparator. Girsanov methods provide likelihood-based relations between changed dynamics when the required microscopic quantities are available. Recent marginal Girsanov work addresses the variance growth of long trajectories, with an implementation deposited on Zenodo. 
arxiv.org
+2

Control through short native simulations predates this work. Equation-free computation already uses appropriately initialized microscopic simulations to obtain quantities needed for feedback control. Local error analysis for split stochastic dynamics is likewise established. 
arxiv.org
+2

The opening that survived was more precise:

Acquire or synthesize the particular local generator operation needed by an intervention, while retaining the exterior system in its actual conditional native state.

That led to useful Astra machinery. N1684 supplies a signed inverse that recovers microscopic generator actions from complete endpoint checkpoints, but its resource bounds use the total exit rate of the system. N1678 supplies local-balance and finite-clock inversion antecedents. I read their visible source accounts at repository revision 203ee3df1cf9a2a883da2725e0b30e3bb1e347fe; the report identifies their exact source paths and credits both.

The construction below changes the central operation that incurred the global-rate cost. It also supplies a positive finite-accuracy alternative when signed calculations become expensive.

The guarantee’s physical meaning

The result controls terminal states and the complete recorded grid of states. It does not automatically control every unobserved continuous-time event.

For example, a fully suppressed target site never jumps. A native site may jump temporarily and then be restored before the next observation. Those two paths can agree at every recorded time while differing in first-passage events or reaction counts.

The method also needs valid checkpoint and editing operations. It does not supply physical cloning, noninvasive measurements, perfect resets or their thermodynamic cost.

6. What the combined result changes

The completed organising principle is that passive prediction, local kinetic acquisition and intervention control have different information and resource requirements.

The passive counterexample identifies a genuine information deficit. Increasing the accuracy or duration of passive observation cannot remove it in general.

The local inverse supplies an active operation that acquires an instantaneous kinetic quantity at a sharp cost. Its cost is governed by the rate’s dependency neighbourhood, even when the surrounding system retains arbitrary correlations and mixes slowly.

The positive intervention construction then supplies the finite change itself, using native evolution and local edits. Its error depends on both incoming dependencies and outgoing influence.

That last distinction is necessary. I constructed a star example in which one controlled site affects arbitrarily many leaves. Every update has a tiny incoming neighbourhood, yet temporary excursions of the controlled site contaminate many leaves. A control bound based only on incoming neighbourhood size fails. The proved constants include the required forward-influence cost.

The resulting claims are narrower than a universal scientific simulator, but they are complete:

A sharp acquisition theorem with a matching lower bound.
An executable local intervention construction with positive samples and explicit finite-error guarantees.
An exact separation between complete passive observed knowledge and nonlinear response.
A working synthetic implementation with certified improvement and a matched comparator.

The civilisation-level consequence remains a further scientific obligation. It would require a consequential downstream system where the checkpoint, structural and editing resources are actually available, and where the strongest equally informed existing method does not already provide the required capability at lower complete cost. The present work establishes the operations and boundaries needed to evaluate such a case without assuming away the hidden dynamics.
