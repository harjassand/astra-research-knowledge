Two different possibilities merit a direct test. Astra’s fermionic contraction may help calculate correlated states, but its present theorem fixes one particle per site; I’m checking whether that blocks the proposed connection to superconducting charge order. The positive quantum-record construction has another possible opening: replacing its global cutoff with local cutoffs could remove a volume limitation. I’ll derive that step and compare it with conventional noisy-system simulation results.

The local-cutoff construction passes an exact test on every two-mode quartic basis term: it preserves the thermal Gaussian marginal, so its error estimate need not grow through a single global occupation tail. A 1,000-mode example gives a bound below \(9\times10^{-10}\), where the older global bound is vacuous. This is a continuous-law result; it does not yet certify a fast finite-precision simulator.

The memory extension also produced a useful failure: one physical face write can merge two harmless strips, defeating an additive potential built from separate components. That identifies what the next barrier must account for.
