# What the quantum-training receiver does, and what its comparators would require

This is an access-model clarification for `source_unknown_signed_choi_programmed_erasure_recovery_theorem.md`, not an additional optimality theorem.

The source-known polar receiver uses a classical description of V,Y and a compiled spectral projector/polar map on the received system. The new signed-Choi receiver removes that DESCRIPTION assumption but does not remove coherent control over the physical output registers. It consumes fresh quantum channel samples on every decoding attempt. Its finite training-query bound is independent of D, whereas storage and controlled-SWAP width depend on D. It neither returns a reusable classical decoder description nor verifies the assumed channel family/entropy promise.

An equally informed comparison cannot grant a transpose/Petz receiver an uncharged adjoint-channel oracle. With sigma=E(I3/3), the formal transpose map contains E* and sigma^-1/2 (on the supported output); these are not supplied as operations by black-box forward channel samples. A legitimate implementation under the present access model must acquire those operations, regularize any small spectrum, and charge all samples, heralds and errors. No claim is made here that the signed-Choi construction is better than an optimally implemented sample-based Petz recovery. It is simply an explicit feasible receiver under the stated resources.

The exact two-program antisymmetric construction in `two_program_unknown_isometry_inverse_and_noise_guard.md` is a transparent comparator: two noiseless Choi programs invert any unknown qutrit isometry with success1/27, charging three channel uses per attempt. For noisy programs, even the perfectly recoverable orthogonal-reset Delta0 family has accepted singlet fraction1/(5-4p). Thus quantum programmability alone does not supply a uniform entropy-controlled receiver; the reset-cancelling signed spectral construction pays a real additional step.

Finally, finite-copy programmable inversion is probabilistic/approximate and consumes its programs. It does not claim a deterministic exact universal processor from nonorthogonal finite program states. Any practical comparison must also price the output-register quantum memory and coherent SWAP implementation; ordinary classical recorded training data is a strictly different resource model.
