# Source-exact negative-stress mass exhaustion is sufficient

This is a receiving criterion for a future construction, not an obtained sequence.

Suppose S_n are finite symmetric matrix measures supported in the compact elliptope E, with exact identities J:S_n=0 and DivDiv S_n=μ-δ0. Let N_n be the total trace mass of the negative spectral part of S_n (defined using a scalar dominating measure). Assume N_n→0 with genuinely received bounds, not sampled eigenvalues.

Testing with |x|²/2 gives ∫tr S_n=Eμ|Z|²/2=3/4. Therefore their nuclear total variation is exactly 3/4+2N_n. Weak-* compactness gives a convergent subsequence. Every nonnegative matrix test has negative pairing bounded by its operator supremum times N_n, so the limit S is positive semidefinite. The J-null and full distributional endpoint identities pass to the limit because J is constant and Hessians of smooth tests are continuous. Thus S is an actual PSD J-null full-endpoint stress, and the previously received stress/Jensen/finite-tree-closure argument yields μ in the closed paid-law set.

This allows a finite-stage gauge optimizer to target integrated negative-eigenvalue mass rather than exact pointwise positivity at each stage. It does not make a finite small negativity value sufficient, and it does not supply a rate or an optimizing policy. Every finite-stage field must retain its boundary, vertex, and transition contributions. The two-jet collar and compact tensor gauges preserve the source exactly and therefore fit this framework; no vanishing sequence has been constructed.

Conversely, if such a PSD full-endpoint stress exists, its negative mass is zero. The criterion is therefore faithful to the actual target, unlike posterior moment floors that admitted nonlaminate bad labels. It changes the representation of the unresolved feasibility problem, not its truth value.
