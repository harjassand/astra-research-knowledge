Can the complete baseline law of an observed marked process identify its
response to a known multiplicative intervention when the unobserved
background is not identified? The following elementary phase-type example
answers NO for the general coarse-intensity contract, even with the exact
event time rather than only a histogram. It is not a RuO2 catalog realization,
a new theorem of hidden-process identification, or a physical impossibility.

STRONGER CONSTRUCTION: KNOWN IDENTICAL MICROSCOPIC RESET AND INTENSITY LIBRARY.
Use the same three transient states, common reset p=(0,1,0), and KNOWN
controlled mark-rate matrix D=diag(0,1,2) in both models. Marks absorb in
the same visible terminal state. The unobserved background generator is
Q_u=[[-2,2,0],[u/2,-u,u/2],[0,0,0]], u>=0. Only u is unknown. Its row sums
vanish and off-diagonal entries are nonnegative. Intervention multiplies
ONLY D by b; neither Q_u nor the reset changes. Visible record: time of
the first mark/censoring, with a constant visible signal before and after.

For z>0 the survival Laplace transform is
L_u(z,b)=p(zI-Q_u+bD)^(-1)1. Exact symbolic elimination gives

L_u(z,b)-1/(z+b)
 = b*u*(b-1)/[(z+b)(z+2b)(z^2+(2+u+b)z+u+2b)].

At baseline b=1 it is identically zero for EVERY u, hence the complete
visible law is the same Exp(1) mark time for all horizons. This includes
every statistic of the visible path, not just mean coverage or a histogram.
For 0<b<1 and u>0 the rational function is nonzero. More strongly the
quadratic factors as (z+r_minus)(z+r_plus) with positive real roots:
sum=2+u+b>0, product=u+2b>0, discriminant=(u+b)^2+4(1-b)>=0.
The inverse transform of the product of its four positive-rate reciprocal
factors is a strictly positive four-exponential convolution for t>0.
Thus survival_u(t,b)<exp(-bt) for all t>0. Model u=0 has exp(-bt).
Their third derivatives at0 differ by -u*b*(1-b), independently checking
that the target laws differ. At u=2,b=1/2,T=1, event probabilities are
about.3934693403 and.4148289642: difference.02135962394.

If strictly identical positive background support is required, compare
u=1 with u=2 instead. Both have exactly the same positive-rate edges.
Their baseline laws still coincide. At every z>0, the u-dependent term
contains u/[A+u(z+1)], A=(z+2)(z+b)>0, which is strictly increasing in u;
the coefficient b(b-1) is nonzero. Their target transforms therefore differ.
This stronger pair shares a known pure reset, a known hidden mark-rate
function and even exactly the same positive background transition support. It
refutes an unrestricted claim that more passive baseline data necessarily
closes the intervention interface. It is still NOT a realization of the
pinned RuO2 catalog, where native rules constrain the background further.
Independent review of this stronger construction is requested separately.

WARMUP CONSTRUCTION: TWO HIDDEN STATES WITH AN UNOBSERVED RESET MIXTURE.

Use time units lambda^-1. Model A has one transient hidden state, controlled
mark intensity1 and no background transitions. The first mark absorbs.
Model B starts equally in hidden states0,1, whose controlled mark intensities
are0,2. Its unchanged background transition0->1 has rate1. Marks absorb.
In both models the visible record is the first mark time, or censoring;
after the mark it is constant. They share the known coarse pre-mark
intensity bracket[0,2], post-mark intensity0 and repeatable initial laws.
The initial laws need only be the SAME before/after intervention within
each admissible model; they need not be a known microscopic preparation.

At baseline, B's transient generator is H1=[[-1,1],[0,-2]]. The reset row
p=(1/2,1/2) obeys p H1=-p. Thus both complete visible path laws have
survival exp(-t) for ALL t, not merely the same mean or one finite fixture.
No number of baseline visible records can distinguish these two models.

Multiplying ONLY the marked intensity by b leaves the background rate1
unchanged. A has survival exp(-bt); B has transient generator
Hb=[[-1,1],[0,-2b]]. At b=1/2 its survival is exp(-t)(1+t/2).
These differ for every t>0 because 1+t/2<exp(t/2).
At T=1 the event probabilities differ by about0.0547114980.
Both models meet the general intervention semantics: same initial law,
destination, unchanged background and bounded controlled intensity.

This excludes universal point identification from these observations in
that general class. It does NOT show that C's interval is sharp, exclude
identification with the full native catalog, rule out stronger observables,
or establish a lower bound for every controlled experiment. It also does
not by itself supply two models of the pinned catalytic graph/rate catalog.
Known phase-type / hidden-model nonidentification is the ordinary context;
no historical priority claim is made. The useful decision is specific:
increasing only baseline sample count cannot universally replace an
acquired microscopic activity or structural background contract.
