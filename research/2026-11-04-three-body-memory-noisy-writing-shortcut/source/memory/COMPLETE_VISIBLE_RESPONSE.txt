The next memory test exposed why noisy writing is harder than it first appeared: a fault in one auxiliary component can reverse a neighbouring component’s preferred state. That means the equilibrium storage proof cannot simply be extended by treating faulty components as missing checks. This is not a proof that the memory fails. It rules out a shortcut we were testing. The ideal preparation result still stands under its stated assumptions; I’m now working on whether real faults remain sufficiently contained during the full write-and-hold process.
