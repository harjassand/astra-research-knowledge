## 6. Joint admission must distinguish weak noise from a persistent broken apparatus

### Weak local faults and coherent calibration errors

The analysis keeps three ingredients separate:

1. Positive error components derived from the exact nominal instrument.
2. Completed calibration differences, including coherent terms.
3. Local data and control generator insertions over their actual duration.

For a connected same-axis gauge component within one snapshot, the diagonal algebra reduces to one representative modulo that component’s gauge. One local insertion surrounded by arbitrary diagonal apparatus evolution therefore has the correctable support required by the receiver, together with a possibly spoiled snapshot.

That reduction cannot absorb a second insertion or collapse several snapshots into one fault.

If \(b_j\) is a completed baseline-error budget and \(W_j\) is the integrated local generator-norm exposure, the audited expansion gives, with \(B=\sum_j(b_j+W_j)\),

\[
R(B)=e^B-1-B
\]

as a sufficient bound on terms of degree at least two. For repeated correction rectangles, a safe composition is

\[
\exp\!\left(\sum_r R(B_r)\right)-1.
\tag{10}
\]

The distinction from an ordinary probability union bound matters because the correctable signed sum of coherent terms need not be a completely positive map.

Similarly, a single correlated two-site jump of strength \(O(p)\) remains a first-order event. Its two sites do not earn a \(p^2\) suppression.

Calibration has also been treated at the channel level. Constant gain errors produce coherent angle errors. A selected actual-pole gate comparison gives a restricted-input norm contribution about \(1.42\times10^{-8}\), but that number is a pointwise calculation. It is **not** a certificate over the entire gate-calibration uncertainty region. The finite readout’s analytic uncertainty bound and this pointwise gate result have different scopes.

### Strong apparatus failures persist across uses

A readout-port outage or a stuck driver can violate the healthy force and damping envelopes. Such faults require a different treatment.

I constructed a physical local-switch model with bounded first-onset intensity. If

\[
\Lambda=\sum_i\int_{\text{actual lifetime}}\bar\lambda_i(t)\,dt ,
\]

then

\[
\Pr\{\text{more than }b\text{ ever-failed domains}\}
\le\frac{\Lambda^{b+1}}{(b+1)!}.
\tag{11}
\]

The proof does not assume independent faults in successive rounds. It does not supply the controller with a perfect flag. Once a domain fails, it can remain hot, wrongly driven, and correlated for the rest of the experiment.

The energy proof applies to never-failed sectors using

\[
V_i=P_{\rm never,i}e^{\theta n_i}+P_{\rm ever,i}I.
\]

The onset contribution to this observable is nonpositive. Already failed apparatus is handled by its physical support and reporter redundancy.

This requires private fixed domains and the specified longitudinal or axis-preserving behavior during sensing. A shared clock fault is one common event with its full support. Persistent off-axis dynamics that continually change syndromes do not inherit the same receiver theorem merely because they act near one edge.

A failed pair mediator can affect both endpoints during basis changes. In the present memory proof, tolerating one such persistent domain while also admitting an old residual error and a fresh weak fault requires a \(9\times9\)-type memory and redundant reporter graphs. The fixed pair-graph construction needs at least 252 edge modules. That is an expensive sufficient fixture, not an architecture lower bound.

Finite switch initialization is also modeled, but its physical repair rate has not been acquired. Its preparation time is additional to the \(4.15/g\) oscillator startup.

The resulting finite-life bound has the form

\[
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon_{\rm logical}\le{}&
\delta_{\rm start}+\delta_{\rm history}+\delta_{\rm force}
+\epsilon_{\rm common}\\
&+\frac{\Lambda_{\rm eff}^{\,b+1}}{(b+1)!}
+\exp\!\left(\sum_r[e^{B_r}-1-B_r]\right)-1 ,
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

with separately required detector/controller contributions included at their actual strengths. Its usefulness depends on acquiring those physical parameters, not choosing a convenient number of faults.
