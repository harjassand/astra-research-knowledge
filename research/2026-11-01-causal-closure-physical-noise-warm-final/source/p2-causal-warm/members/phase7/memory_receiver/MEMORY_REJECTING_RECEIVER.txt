Therefore a linear isometry W_z exists with

 V_z |psi> = |beta_s>_f tensor W_z |psi>              (2)

 V=sum_z |z><z| tensor [|beta_s>_f tensor W_z].       (3)

 q_th=Phi[-2sqrt(eta N_eff)/sqrt(1+2eta n)].          (6)

Colored input with long correlations, an apparatus fault continuing to

7. ROBUSTNESS COST AND AN EXACT CALIBRATION NO-GO

Suppose the implemented f or the true response matrices leave residual rows

 epsilon_z=int f*(t) C_m exp(A_true,z t)dt,
 max_z ||epsilon_z||<=epsilon.

For vacuum fresh fields and a conditional initial apparatus state with mean
occupation at most E, the unwanted measured-mode occupation is at most
epsilon^2 E. Its reduced-state trace distance from the intended coherent
pointer is at most epsilon sqrt(E), by the vacuum-overlap bound. Hence the
report probability can change by no more than epsilon sqrt(E), in addition
to displacement/model error. With correlated histories the energy bound
must hold conditionally or be averaged using the appropriate Cauchy bound;
a hidden unconditional average cannot certify every history.

The energy dependence is unavoidable. For any nonzero epsilon_z, an initial
coherent mode in its direction can shift the pointer by epsilon_z alpha.
Choosing alpha to reverse a signal beta takes energy4|beta|^2/||epsilon_z||^2.
It makes the nominally correct report wrong with probability approaching
1-q. Thus no nonzero calibration tolerance gives a uniform guarantee over
unbounded initial energy. Mathematical exact rejection and physically
bounded preparation/fault energy are different operating contracts.

There is also an exact no-go for arbitrary continuous unknown poles. If a
single homogeneous response may be exp[-(kappa/2+i delta)t] for every delta
in a nonempty open interval, a compact-time filter would need

 F(delta)=int_0^T f*(t)exp(-kappa t/2)exp(-i delta t)dt=0

throughout that interval. The transform is entire in complex delta. Analytic
uniqueness implies F identically zero, and Fourier uniqueness implies f=0.
Exact nonzero rejection is therefore possible for a known finite response
space, not an arbitrary continuous interval of unknown frequencies. Robust
finite-order derivative rejection can reduce small mismatch, but it consumes
signal dimensions and does not evade this result. Its acquisition and
precision costs must be charged if used.
