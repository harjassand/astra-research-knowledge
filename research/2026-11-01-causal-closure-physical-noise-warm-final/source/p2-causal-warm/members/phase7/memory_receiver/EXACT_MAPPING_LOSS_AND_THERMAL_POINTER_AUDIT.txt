PART I. MAPPING LOSS, COHERENT INPUT AND ARBITRARY-STATE EXTENSION

1. Physical mapping interval

Storage a has vacuum loss L=sqrt(kappa)a. The readout is decoupled during
this interval. The Hamiltonian is

 H(t)=chi(t) Z1 n+u(t)Z2(a+a^dag),
 theta(t)=int_0^t chi(s)ds,
 theta(T)=pi,
 u(t)=lambda chi(t)exp(-kappa t/2).

The real chi(t) may have finite smooth ramps. Define

 C=kappa int_0^T exp(-kappa t)[1-exp(-2i theta(t))]dt,
 K=int_0^T chi(t)exp(-kappa t)dt.                    (1)

Consider two data branches with the SAME parity s, with q=Z1=+/-1 and
Z2=sq. Start storage in the SAME coherent ket |beta> in those two branches.
Put w=beta+lambda s. The exact conditional trajectory is

 alpha_q(t)=exp(-kappa t/2)
             [exp(-iq theta(t))w-lambda s].         (2)

At the endpoint both branches have exactly the same retained coherent ket,
alpha_q(T)=-exp(-kappa T/2)(beta+2lambda s). Thus their final-mode overlap
is one. All distinction in this same-parity comparison is in phases and
the emitted storage-loss field, which must be retained.

2. Emitted-field overlap and the cancellation of the linear beta phase

Coherent-output overlap gives

 log <out_-|out_+>
   =-C|w|^2
    +i2kappa lambda s Re(w) int exp(-kappa t)sin theta(t)dt.  (3)

For a normal-ordered forced passive oscillator, the full coherent-dilation
phase obeys phi_q'=-Re[f_q* alpha_q], where
f_q=q s lambda chi(t)exp(-kappa t/2). Taking the two-branch difference gives

 phi_+-phi_-
   =2lambda^2 K
    -2lambda s Re(w) int chi(t)exp(-kappa t)cos theta(t)dt.  (4)

The last term cancels the imaginary linear term in(3), because

 d[exp(-kappa t)sin theta(t)]/dt
 =chi(t)exp(-kappa t)cos theta(t)
  -kappa exp(-kappa t)sin theta(t),

and sin theta is zero at BOTH endpoints. Consequently the COMPLETE mapping
overlap, including its coherent action phase, is exactly

 F_s(beta)=exp(i2lambda^2 K) exp[-C|beta+lambda s|^2]. (5)

This cancellation is not an assumption that beta is real or initially
uncorrelated with other bosonic modes. Equation(5) is evaluated on coherent
storage kets; the next section identifies the actual operator it determines.

3. Exact all-state relative-overlap operator

Let V_+,V_- be the full mapping isometries from the initial storage Hilbert
space into retained storage plus the complete vacuum loss field. Then

 Q_s=V_-^dag V_+
   =exp(i2lambda^2 K)
      D(-lambda s)(1-C)^n D(lambda s).              (6)

Proof: the coherent expectation of the operator on the right is precisely
(5), using <alpha|z^n|alpha>=exp[-(1-z)|alpha|^2]. Diagonal coherent-state
matrix elements determine a bounded operator uniquely (equivalently, compare
every coefficient in the expansion in beta,beta*). The operator is bounded:

 1-C=exp(-kappa T)
       +kappa int_0^T exp(-kappa t)exp[-2i theta(t)]dt,

which is a convex combination of points on the unit circle, so |1-C|<=1.
Hence ||(1-C)^n||<=1. This is also required because V_-^dag V_+ is a
contraction. Any readout mode evolving independently during mapping merely
adds an identity factor to this relative-overlap operator.

Equation(6) retains operator-valued memory dependence. For an arbitrary
same-parity cross-density operator rho_+,- on storage and a reference, its
coherence after tracing the map's storage/output is evaluated using Q_s,
not by replacing rho_+,- with a thermal scalar. It therefore exposes the
correct domain of the simpler thermal formula below.

After removing the known local phase exp(i lambda^2 K Z1), put z=1-C.
The elementary bound |1-z^n|<=n|C| for |z|<=1 gives, for any initial ket
or purification,

 ||(V_+-exp(i2lambda^2K)V_-)|psi>||^2
       <=2|C| <(a^dag+lambda s)(a+lambda s)>.

The shifted mean occupation is bounded by (sqrt(E)+|lambda|)^2 if the
initial mean occupation is <=E. This is an energy-constrained full-isometry
bound, not a state-independent probability for a classical loss fault.

For constant chi, T=pi/chi, the coefficient has the explicit form

 C=(1-exp(-kappa T)) * [2i chi/(kappa+2i chi)].       (7)

Its real part is nonnegative. Its imaginary part is higher order in kappa
for small kappa/chi; it must nevertheless be retained in exact coherence
and phase calculations.

4. Thermal initial state

For a thermal initial storage state of mean occupation nbar, independent
of the compared data coherence, use its positive coherent-state mixture

 rho_th=int d^2 beta exp(-|beta|^2/nbar)/(pi nbar) |beta><beta|.

The ordinary complex Gaussian integral of(5) yields

 F_th= exp(i2lambda^2 K)/(1+nbar C)
       * exp[-C lambda^2/(1+nbar C)].               (8)

The nbar=0 limit is continuous. Re(C)>=0 guarantees convergence and the
denominator cannot vanish for nbar>=0. After compensating the known phase
arg F_th, the same-parity dephasing probability is (1-|F_th|)/2.

The formula is independent of s because the thermal state is centered and
rotationally symmetric. A displaced, squeezed, nonGaussian or data-correlated
initial memory generally has a different operator expectation. It must use
(6), not an effective temperature inserted without justification.

No-jump effects have not been discarded: (5)-(8) compare the complete
Lindblad dilation, including the field's vacuum/no-count contribution. The
formula does not assert that each no-count trajectory is ideal or that all
logical disturbance is equal to the observed count probability.

6. Why the warm pointer factors from arbitrary initial memory

forces every other output row to be orthogonal to this chosen field mode.
Consequently the passive unitary factorizes into transmission of that
single field mode to f, and a passive unitary on its orthogonal complement
plus all initial apparatus modes.

The fresh homogeneous thermal field has covariance n_b I and no anomalous
covariance. It is invariant under this FIXED field-mode basis change and

9. Where this warm factorization fails or requires enlargement

The full result needs the common data-independent passive map, the exact
zero initial-mode row, and homogeneous thermal occupation of the relevant
fresh field modes. It does NOT automatically apply to:

 * the old Z1-dependent three-mode passive scattering;
 * nonzero storage loss during the mapping interval, whose scattering is
   individually Z1 dependent;
 * multiple fresh ports at unequal temperatures, where a selected mode can
   be correlated with remaining outputs;
 * colored, squeezed or temporally correlated input unless its covariance
   and purification are included in a separately proved factorization;
 * initial input-field correlations with the apparatus or past records;
 * unknown pole intervals or imperfect response rejection without an
   energy-dependent robustness budget.

Under some of those broader cases the report POVM still has a uniform
conditional Gaussian form, but that is weaker than(9)-(14). The weaker
POVM theorem must not be promoted to the complete CP mixture without proof.
