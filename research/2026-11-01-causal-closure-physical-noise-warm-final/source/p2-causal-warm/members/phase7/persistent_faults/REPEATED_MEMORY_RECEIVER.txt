REPEATABLE SUBSYSTEM RECOVERY WITH RETAINED QUANTUM APPARATUS MEMORY
1 November 2026

Primary result
==============

An apparatus need not return to vacuum between operations if the ACTUAL
joint data-memory channel stays in a correctable operator algebra and the
ACTUAL receiver supplies the syndrome information required to correct it.
This note makes those two conditions operational for Bacon-Shor recovery.

It gives three explicit consumer contracts:

 (1) Arbitrary nominal retained memory whose physical channel is gauge-only,
     plus at most one fresh localized fault per complete correction cycle:
     a 5x5 Bacon-Shor construction has an invariant across arbitrarily many
     cycles. It includes a previous surviving error and a new next-cycle fault.

 (2) In addition, an unknown fixed strong-memory region with one dispersive
     participant site, but intact calibrated reports: a 7x7 construction
     tolerates arbitrary strength, quantum correlations and reuse of that
     region, plus the same one-new-fault-per-cycle budget.

 (3) The strong region may also be ONE unknown permanently broken reporter,
     provided its data action preserves the declared axes/support. Redundant
     physical gauge edges, with an explicit polynomial classical decoder,
     remove the need for a perfect defect flag. A concrete 7-vertex, 11-edge
     graph supplies the required spatial redundancy.

These are deterministic support/fault-budget statements, not a threshold,
experiment, iid-noise hypothesis or total-resource advantage. They require the
physical memory/readout conditions proved separately by root and the receiver
collaborator. The original cold three-mode meter does NOT become gauge-only
on arbitrary warm states merely by adopting this decoder. Strong arbitrary
memory in every off-gauge module is explicitly shown to fail.

Executable checks: check_persistent_receiver.py.
Numerical/exact discrete ledger: persistent_receiver_checks.json.

2. PHYSICAL CONTRACT, INCLUDING WHAT CAN REMAIN HOT
=================================================

There are R rows and C columns of data qubits, with R,C odd. Horizontal
X_(r,c)X_(r,c') pairs generate the usual row XX gauge algebra. Vertical
Z_(r,c)Z_(r',c) pairs generate the column ZZ gauge algebra. Any connected
graph within each row/column generates the same Bacon-Shor gauge group as
nearest-neighbor path edges. Long-range edges are additional real hardware;
they are not present automatically in a nearest-neighbor device.

Each module has a fixed physical pair and a designated dispersive participant
i. Its second participant is j. Under active physical ZZ sensing, its data
dependence is diagonal in Zi,Zj. The inherited three-mode module has exactly
this support, even when the apparatus state and apparatus-only faults are
arbitrary. At the active check, S=ZiZj is a gauge of the current code frame.

The nominal module admitted by root's new warm construction must satisfy its
own joint-state theorem: its retained-memory action is gauge-only, and its
selected receiver mode supplies an accurate finite-strength gauge report
uniformly over the allowed initial data-apparatus-reference state. Nothing in
this note replaces that physical theorem. For the original cold construction,
only its actually proved cold renewal can be used as the nominal condition.

Let D be the set of dispersive participants of exceptional modules whose
retained-state backaction may be arbitrarily strong and off-gauge. D is a
mathematical support bound, NOT a measured or perfectly flagged set. The
decoder below does not need its identities. Write

 b_X = number of rows touched by D,
 b_Z = number of columns touched by D.

The actual set may be unknown. Its bounds must hold throughout the epoch to
which the theorem is applied. Apparatus states can be non-Gaussian, thermal,
entangled with one another and correlated with the data through earlier
admitted operations. No energy bound, closeness to vacuum, fresh-state coin,
or factorization between uses is required for this ALGEBRAIC support claim.
Such conditions may still be required by the microscopic Hamiltonian or by
the independent receiver construction; they cannot be erased by this note.

Whenever a module is not interrogating a gauge of the CURRENT code frame,
its longitudinal forces are actually zero. This includes all inactive modules
during a sensing block and all modules during basis-change gates. Exceptional
modules' dispersive couplings can remain on. No unearned ability to switch
their chi completely off is required in this bounded-support version.
Apparatus-only couplings/noise may remain arbitrary, provided they do not
introduce additional data support or different data axes.

For nominal warm modules outside D, the joint gauge-only contract must cover
idle and basis-change intervals as well as the active meter. If their hot
dispersive links remain on through H, their participant sites enter D even
when the active parity operation was gauge-only. An actually isolated idle
module can avoid that charge; isolation, its switching action and switching
faults must be earned by the physical construction. The bounded exceptional
always-on-chi result does not make arbitrary always-on nominal links free.

Strong retained-state backaction with an intact calibrated receiver is
different from a permanently changed response pole or a flipped readout
amplifier. The latter can give a wrong syndrome forever. Section7 supplies
an actual redundancy option for bounded persistent broken reporters; without
that option, such reports must be counted as fresh bad reported locations
on EVERY use, not as one old state fault.

3. JOINT DATA-MEMORY ALGEBRA — NO RESET IN THE INDUCTION
======================================================

Let G be the Pauli gauge group, ignoring scalar phases, and define

 A_D = span{E_D G : E_D is a Pauli supported on D, G in G}.    (3.1)

This is a finite-dimensional *-algebra on the data. A Pauli E_D normalizes
the gauge algebra: E_D^dag G E_D is a gauge operator up to a scalar sign.
Consequently,

 (E1 G1)(E2 G2)=E1 E2 (E2^dag G1 E2)G2 in A_D,

and adjoints remain in A_D. Tensoring (3.1) with arbitrary operators on the
JOINT memory, all baths and all old records gives a closed joint algebra.
Dyson expansions, arbitrary apparatus measurements, coarse graining and
conditional apparatus operations preserve this structure. No assumption of
independent apparatus states or independent successive channels is used.

For a module on its correct active gauge S=ZiZj,

 Alg(Zi,Zj)=span{I,Zi,Zj,S}=span{I,Zi}Alg(S).              (3.2)

Thus every arbitrary diagonal pair operation uses the SAME representative i,
even if a particular Pauli expression originally contains Zj. A module's
quantum memory may retain the earlier dependence on S and Zi; both factors
are retained explicitly in A_D and the joint memory coefficients. A later
use does not promote the earlier S dependence into an uncounted new error.

During a force-off interval, a module's data-dependent Hamiltonian is only
chi Zi n_c. It therefore acts at the single site i. During an actual local
Hadamard drive, this Hamiltonian need not commute with that drive. Nonetheless,
factoring out the ideal Hadamard leaves an arbitrary joint operator supported
on i and the apparatus. All other ideal single-qubit rotations factor out.
The full one-site Pauli algebra, including Xi and Yi, is already in A_D.

In the toggling frame of the ideal basis changes, the gauge group becomes
U G U^dag and E_D remains supported on the SAME sites D. Equations(3.1)-(3.2)
therefore apply at each stage. When the global Hadamard pair closes, the
original gauge frame is restored. A hot inactive dispersive module can
disturb its participant during the rotation, but cannot silently reach a
new participant merely because its state was correlated with an old gauge.

This proves the inherited always-on-chi issue is subtler than simply demanding
vacuum: it expands a persistent module from a Z-only column disturbance to
the full Pauli algebra of its dispersive site. The repeated code must charge
both that site's row and column, which the theorem below does.

Critical boundaries:

 * If longitudinal forces on j remain active during a basis change, an
   arbitrary two-site error on {i,j} can result. The same charge is needed
   when an inactive module retains a force on j while its pair product is
   not a gauge in the current code frame. Include both sites in D or count
   a larger fresh-fault support. The one-site theorem no longer applies.
 * Routing a hot module to a new pair can enlarge D. Reusing it on many
   pairs can turn one memory into a logical error source. Fixed physical
   pair assignment or an explicit propagated-support analysis is required.
 * Off-axis multi-data couplings and data leakage out of the qubit space
   are not covered by the stated Pauli-support algebra.

4. WHAT THE ALGEBRA PRESERVES, AND THE ALL-WARM COUNTEREXAMPLE
===========================================================

For an R x C Bacon-Shor block, a Pauli is characterized modulo gauge by its
X row-parity vector x in F2^R and Z column-parity vector z in F2^C. The
stabilizer syndromes are adjacent differences x_r+x_(r+1) and z_c+z_(c+1).
For odd R,C, minimum-weight decoding corrects

 |x|<=t_X=(R-1)/2,  |z|<=t_Z=(C-1)/2.                   (4.1)

If D misses at least one row r0 and one column c0, the logical operators

 Z_L = product_c Z_(r0,c),
 X_L = product_r X_(r,c0)

commute with every element of A_D and anticommute with each other. This is
an explicit protected subsystem for arbitrary-strength dynamics in A_D.
It is a conventional noiseless-subsystem observation. Unknown-support
recovery needs the stronger distance condition (4.1), because the receiver
must work without knowing which clean row/column to select.

On the code and all admitted correctable error sectors, subsystem
Knill-Laflamme supplies an isometry identifying logical data and syndrome/
gauge junk. Each admitted error word acts as I_L times an operator on that
junk. This is the reason an arbitrary joint memory state can be passed to
the next cycle: a successful physical recovery removes the logical action,
while the memory and syndrome junk may remain completely correlated.
The physical receiver used to realize that recovery is constructed below;
the algebraic isometry is only the proof's identification of the protected
subsystem, not an additional ideal operation appended to the circuit.

Arbitrary warm off-gauge memory in EVERY module is not harmless. Taking one
single-site Z from every column generates product_c Z_(r0,c)=Z_L. Each such
Z is permitted by a diagonal gauge module. Accurate parity reports cannot
distinguish an undetected logical Z from the intended encoded state. The
3x3 exact binary calculation checks this explicit inclusion. Thus 'memory
is gauge' cannot be declared after the fact. Either the nominal warm channel
must actually be gauge-only, or the strong off-gauge support must be bounded.

5. ACTUAL HALF-CYCLE RECEIVER
============================

All data are in one physical basis while each same-axis block is read.
For an X-syndrome block, apply physical H to all RC qubits, then measure
horizontal physical ZZ gauges, which are original-frame XX gauges. Do not
repeat the H gates for each repeated same-axis snapshot. Restore the basis
after the entire X-syndrome block. A Z-syndrome block then measures vertical
physical ZZ gauges. Each known nominal phase correction is a real counted
operation or a physically implemented equivalent.

During either sensing block, every active and inactive dispersive coupling
is diagonal in the current physical Z axis. Any arbitrary strong memory
backaction therefore COMMUTES with the current same-axis stabilizers. It
may create the other error component, but cannot change the stabilizer
component currently being read. Hot memory during the intervening H gates
can change either component at the fixed sites D, which is already charged.

A same-axis snapshot measures a connected gauge graph on each row/column
and computes the complete stabilizer vector for that axis. The path graph
is enough if every persistent module has its intact report contract. For
bounded broken reporters, use section7's redundant graph and actual decoder.

All edges in a same-axis snapshot may run SIMULTANEOUSLY when their distinct
modules, controls and output channels support that operation. Shared data
qubits do not themselves prevent this. For distinct apparatus Hilbert spaces,
each edge Hamiltonian is diagonal in the same physical Z operators, so
[H_e(t),H_f(t')]=0 for e != f even when the edges meet at a data qubit.
Arbitrary entanglement of the initial apparatus states does not alter this
operator commutation. Independent available control and receiver channels,
calibrated simultaneous couplings, power limits and crosstalk remain real
physical requirements. A common uncontrolled apparatus bus that propagates
one fault to many modules does not satisfy the local fault-domain contract.

The parallel schedule also preserves the fresh-data-fault support bound.
For a fault E_k on data qubit k at time tau, write the actual evolution as

 [U_after E_k U_after^dag] U_nominal.

Only edges incident on k contribute to the conjugated fault. Each such edge
has data algebra Alg(Z_k,Z_j)=Alg(Z_k,S_(k,j)), where S_(k,j) is a gauge of
the current frame. All the incident S commute. Their entire star therefore
satisfies

 U_after E_k U_after^dag in
 span{I,X_k,Y_k,Z_k} Alg({S_(k,j): j adjacent to k}) tensor B(memory).

This follows first for each Pauli E_k by conjugation and then by linearity
for an arbitrary one-site fault channel. A local data fault can corrupt many
incident reports in this snapshot, which the temporal receiver already treats
as one arbitrary whole-vector snapshot. It still creates only one data-error
representative modulo gauge. An apparatus-only fault confined to one edge
has the earlier one-edge support bound. An error mechanism spanning multiple
edge apparatus operators must instead be charged at its actual support.

Ordinary control and competing apparatus with the same simultaneous controls
receive this parallel schedule too. The number of meter invocations remains
the resource/noise count; it must not be confused with elapsed sensing time.

For each axis, the temporal receiver is:

 m := s+1
 repeat complete same-axis snapshots until m consecutive FULL syndrome
 vectors are identical, with a hard cap m^2 snapshots;
 on agreement, perform the ordinary component Pauli-frame correction;
 on reaching the cap without agreement, return FLAG and do not guess.

The frame is kept consistent across all snapshots. Relative gauge signs
must be translated into that frame before comparison. Frame updates need
no ideal quantum decoder; they are ordinary classical computation. Their
physical implementation/reliability is supplied in the companion addendum
CLASSICAL_REFRESH_AND_ACTUATION.txt: five actual decoder/history/frame pods,
five independent finite majority refresh circuits, encoded final outputs
and distinct local actuation voters. Its invariant includes one old bad
classical state and one new pod fault per update; seven pods also admit one
permanently bad classical pod. Actual failed local actuator voters enter
this quantum cycle's physical fault budget. Shared global failures remain
their actual larger fault domains; no perfect classical controller is used.

A fresh localized fault can cause one data Pauli component per axis and
make the containing entire snapshot arbitrary. The proof deliberately
permits that whole wrong vector: mixed pre/post-fault edge measurements
are not presumed mutually consistent. Faults between snapshots or during
basis changes are assigned to the adjacent event boundary and can change
the data but need not spoil a snapshot. No fault is duplicated or dropped.

If there are at most s fresh events during the complete X+Z cycle, there
are at most s corrupted snapshots and at most s target-syndrome transitions
within each same-axis block. Strong retained memory is not included in s;
it is admitted separately through its fixed support and commutation.

In any accepted run of m=s+1 equal snapshots, at least one snapshot is
uncorrupted. Its syndrome is the actual target component at that point.
Choose the last uncorrupted one in that run. Only fresh faults occurring
after it can produce residual target-component errors. Thus after the
component correction there are at most s such residual representatives.

The hard cap m^2 is sufficient under the promised budget. With f<=s fresh
events in a block, each event can interrupt at most one constant-syndrome
fault-free segment and can occupy at most one corrupted snapshot. Before
each of the f separators, at most m-1 clean snapshots can occur without
acceptance; the separator consumes at most one snapshot; after the last
separator, m clean snapshots suffice. Thus at most (f+1)m<=m^2 snapshots
are required. A FLAG is an actual observed failure of agreement, not an
oracle announcing every physical fault. Beyond the budget, a wrong run can
also be accepted; the flag is not perfect.

6. REPEATED-CYCLE THEOREM, INCLUDING OLD PLUS NEW FAULTS
======================================================

The following sufficient inequalities make the receiver repeatable:

 t_X >= b_X+2s,
 t_Z >= b_Z+2s.                                        (6.1)

The budget is AT MOST s FRESH LOCALIZED FAULTS PER COMPLETE X+Z CYCLE.
It is not s independently in each half-cycle. The latter would require a
larger distance bound. Fresh events include admitted data, local-gate and
sporadic readout faults. A fault that makes a new permanent strong module
enlarges D and must remain within its bound; it is not silently forgotten.

Maintain the following joint error-family invariant at the end of a cycle:

 X row-parity has representatives only from that cycle's late fresh faults;
 Z column-parity has support in columns(D), plus that cycle's late fresh
 faults after the accepted X-syndrome snapshot.                      (6.2)

In particular, X has at most s residual rows, while Z is a sum of a vector
supported on columns(D) and a vector of weight at most s. The bath, memory,
old syndrome records and gauge state remain arbitrary joint junk. The
statement is about the span of these errors, so coherent superpositions
and correlations are included.

Induction proof:

 * Before the next X-syndrome correction, the preceding-cycle residual Z
   component contributes at most s foreign columns, strong memory and the
   H gates contribute only columns(D), and new faults in the current cycle
   contribute at most s columns. Their total is at most b_Z+2s. By(6.1),
   any uncorrupted snapshot in the accepted run is correctly decodable.
   Correcting its syndrome leaves only fresh Z faults after that snapshot.

 * Before the next Z-syndrome correction, the previous-cycle residual X
   component contributes at most s rows, current strong memory contributes
   only rows(D), and ALL fresh current-cycle faults together contribute at
   most s rows. Their total is at most b_X+2s. Its accepted uncorrupted
   snapshot is therefore correctly decodable, leaving only later fresh X.

 * After the X correction, the intervening basis change and the Z-sensing
   block can regenerate arbitrary Z errors only on columns(D), plus the
   current cycle's fresh faults. After the Z correction, strong dispersion
   is Z-type and cannot add an X component. Therefore(6.2) is restored.

This proves the same invariant for any number of cycles under the declared
per-cycle budget. A previous surviving error and a new fault in the next
cycle are explicitly counted in the two separate s terms. The proof never
replaces the retained apparatus by vacuum, and never assumes its next
disturbance is an independent coin.

The starting boundary must satisfy(6.2), or its actual initial error counts
must be inserted into the first-cycle inequalities. A clean encoded input
with arbitrary nominal gauge-only warm memory satisfies it. A completely
arbitrary unknown logical error at the starting boundary cannot be corrected
by renaming it apparatus memory. Encoded state preparation remains a real
operation with its own fault budget.

For coherent noise, expand each allowed joint operator into Pauli/gauge
words with operators on the whole apparatus. The snapshot projectors commute
with the strong same-axis algebra. An uncorrupted snapshot projects the
target syndrome. Under(6.1), every Pauli component in that syndrome has the
same minimum-weight representative modulo gauge; the correction therefore
acts as identity on the protected logical subsystem. The remaining word
family is exactly(6.2). Linearity proves the joint-memory result for arbitrary
reference entanglement. This is the ordinary subsystem-QEC reasoning applied
to the physical, repeated receiver, rather than an ideal final recovery.

Concrete cases:

 * b_X=b_Z=0, s=1: R=C=5 suffices. All modules may retain arbitrary NOMINAL
   gauge-only memory. There are 25 data qubits, 40 path gauge modules, 20 meter
   uses per same-axis snapshot, 80 uses in a no-fresh-fault cycle, and at most
   120 uses with at most one fresh fault. The hard cap is 160 uses even if the
   budget is violated. There are 50 physical H gates per cycle, not per snapshot.

 * b_X=b_Z=1, s=1: R=C=7 suffices. With intact reporters there are 49 data
   qubits, 84 path modules, 168 no-fresh-fault meter uses and at most 252 uses
   with one fresh fault; 98 physical H gates per cycle. Section 7's fixed-broken-
   reporter version uses more modules and meters, stated there.

For a fully parallel same-axis snapshot with admitted duration T_meter,
elapsed sensing time is 2(s+1) T_meter when there are no fresh faults, at
most (s+1)(s+2) T_meter under the complete-cycle budget, and at most
2(s+1)^2 T_meter at the hard cap. For s=1 these are 4, 6 and 8 times T_meter.
Add two physical global H layers, plus actual switching, receiver/controller
latency and phase-control time. The H layers contain 2RC single-qubit gates.
Calling them parallel layers does not turn a common control failure on every
qubit into one localized fault; such a mechanism needs its actual correlated
noise analysis or a larger charged support.
All local physical-Z phase corrections commute within a same-axis block;
when that is their actual form, they may be accumulated and coalesced into
at most one phase correction per qubit at the block boundary. Any needed
noncommuting correction must retain its actual implementation and timing.

If the available hardware must serialize modules, its sensing time instead
uses the actual meter invocation count times the admitted duration; partial
parallel schedules use their actual layers. No cooldown is included because
this receiver's admitted semantics does not require vacuum. That does not
eliminate incoming-bath, bandwidth, isolation, control-action or microscopic-
validity costs in the physical module.

7. UNKNOWN PERSISTENT BROKEN REPORTERS: SPATIAL GAUGE REDUNDANCY
==============================================================

A permanently wrong reporter can defeat temporal repetition: it may output
the same incorrect syndrome indefinitely. Thus retained-state robustness and
parameter/electronics-fault robustness must not be conflated.

There is an explicit ordinary redundancy construction. In each row, measure
XX gauges on edges of a connected graph H; in each column, measure ZZ gauges
on the analogous graph. In a same-axis snapshot without a fresh opposite-axis
data transition, all those gauge operators commute and obey

 g_(u,v)=v_u+v_v in binary sign notation.

The allowed edge records are the binary CUT code of H. The Hamming distance
between two distinct allowed records is the size of the cut separating their
relative vertex labels. Its minimum distance is therefore the edge connectivity
lambda(H). If lambda(H)>=2b+1, up to b arbitrary wrong edge records can be
corrected, even if their sites are unknown and their values are correlated
across time. This requires distinct physical fault domains for the edges;
one routed broken apparatus must not impersonate multiple supposedly
independent graph edges.

An exponential nearest-cut search is unnecessary. Fix a root vertex. For
each other vertex, use unit-capacity max flow to precompute 2b+1 EDGE-DISJOINT
paths to the root. Each path parity estimates the same relative vertex sign.
At most b paths can contain a bad edge, so majority returns the correct sign.
Different vertices may reuse edges; within each majority vote the paths must
be edge-disjoint. Reconstruct every edge bit and compare with the measured
record. More than b discrepancies gives an actual classical FLAG. This
flag is guaranteed absent within the contract, but is not guaranteed to
detect every out-of-contract corruption.

This is a polynomial decoder, with a static polynomial max-flow preparation
and elementary path parity/majority operations at runtime. It uses acquired
graph structure, not a supplied computational oracle. For fixed small codes
the implementation is explicitly provided.

The quantum interpretation is also sound. Removing at most b edges from a
(2b+1)-edge-connected graph leaves a connected graph of good reporters.
Their actual ideal-component gauge measurements project onto all relative
vertex signs. The other-axis/gauge subsystem need not start in a known
state. Axis-preserving faulty modules commute with these gauges and cannot
change those relative signs during the snapshot. The decoded edge record
therefore is the actual projected gauge record, not a manufactured classical
pattern unrelated to the quantum state.

A fresh data fault during the snapshot may change a vertex sign partway
through the edge sequence. That snapshot may be arbitrary or flagged;
the temporal receiver already charges it as a fresh faulty snapshot. No
single-shot claim is made for arbitrary changing data during the snapshot.

For b=1 on 7 vertices, the explicit 11-edge graph is

 (0,1),(0,3),(0,6),(1,2),(1,4),(2,3),(2,5),
 (3,4),(3,6),(4,5),(5,6).

Its minimum cut is 3. The provided exact check verifies all 64 relative vertex
patterns and all 12 choices of no bad edge or one bad edge: 768 cases, zero
failures. The max-flow paths are saved in the JSON ledger. Two bad edges can
give a silently wrong valid cut; an explicit outside-contract example is
preserved, so the emitted flag is not being advertised as perfect.

This graph uses 11 physical pair modules per row/column. It is edge-count
optimal among simple seven-vertex graphs correcting one arbitrary bad edge:
minimum edge connectivity three requires every vertex degree at least three,
so 2|E| >= 3*7 and |E| >= 11. Triplicating each of the six path gauges uses
18 modules, while a complete graph uses 21. The graph therefore improves
that conventional redundancy count under equal pair-module cost, but it
adds non-nearest-neighbor interactions. Their physical cost cannot be
inferred from edge count alone.

For the 7x7 b=1,s=1 example: 154 modules, 77 meter uses per same-axis snapshot,
308 uses in a no-fresh-fault cycle, and at most 462 uses with one fresh fault.
The hard cap is 616 uses outside the budget. There are 98 H gates per cycle.
A two-mode module implementation would have 308 apparatus modes, before its
couplers, receiver and control hardware. These are sufficient explicit
costs, not a total-resource win over matched-qutrit or erasure architectures.

The b=1 bound here is GLOBAL for exceptional strong modules. Allowing one
arbitrarily strong faulty module in every row would touch every row and
destroy the data-error support guarantee, even though the classical cut
decoder alone could correct one bad edge in each row. Readout redundancy
does not replace the quantum fault-support condition.

8. FINITE MEASUREMENT STRENGTH, CORRELATIONS AND PROBABILITIES
===========================================================

The consumer uses the actual receiver theorem from root/receiver work. A
uniform POVM alone is not automatically a state-independent good/bad FULL
instrument decomposition. For the proved selected coherent output mode,
the joint isometry has a factor |beta_s> in that mode and a retained-memory
isometry W_s or W_(zi,zj). This factorization supplies the stronger statement.

If N is the signal photon number in that selected mode and eta is the real
detection efficiency, define q=Phi[-2sqrt(eta N)] and gamma=exp(-2N)/2. The
finite binary pointer instrument is exactly

 M0=(1-e) P+e B,  e=q+gamma^2/q,

with explicit positive residual Kraus operators given in the Phase6 proof.
Applying the actual retained-memory map W after it leaves a uniform mixture
on JOINT data and memory. The good branch is an actual projective gauge
measurement followed by the admitted gauge/support action. No step replaces
memory by a fresh state. This permits a fault-history expansion even when
the memory states are completely correlated from one use to the next.

If only a uniform conditional report bound is available, a classical tail
bound can still follow from conditional probabilities, but it does not by
itself establish the quantum good-branch model. Use the stronger physical
factorization where it was actually proved; do not infer it from first moments.

The deterministic cycle theorem does not assign probabilities to strong
persistent memory. For a finite run, its logical failure probability is
bounded by the probability that the declared support/fault conditions fail,
plus classical-controller failure and any separately quantified physical
approximation error. A probability bound on more than s fresh events requires
the ACTUAL noise model. Independent Bernoulli coins for the whole device
are not assumed. A state-uniform joint CP decomposition may earn particular
readout coin bounds, while non-Markov Hamiltonian faults require the usual
amplitude analysis or another explicit model.

The code's s-per-cycle deterministic tolerance is deliberately strong. It
does not imply a threshold or a competitive physical error rate without
charging all meter, local-gate, idle, input-bath and controller contributions
over the substantial cycle duration.

9. USEFUL GATES AND SUPPORT SERVICE
==================================

This is an actual repeated recovery consumer, not only a static noiseless
subsystem certificate. It can accept a protected-operation output satisfying
the explicit error-family boundary(6.2). Standard transversal CNOT between
equal Bacon-Shor blocks maps gauge operators to gauge operators and propagates
each Pauli component only to the corresponding physical index in the other
block. A hot module coupled during an indexwise physical gate may therefore
affect that index in both blocks; each block must charge the imported row/
column support. A sufficient gate transaction uses the union of the two
blocks' strong supports plus its actual gate/fresh-fault budget in(6.1),
then runs the constructed recovery on both blocks. No noiseless entangling
gate or fresh encoded ancilla is supplied by this statement. Its actual
physical gate, timing and faults must be provided by the operation owner.

One cannot reuse the one-block b bound after blindly spreading distinct
strong supports through many blocks. Recovery clears transient imported
data errors, but it does not repair a permanently changed module parameter.
Any change in module-to-data routing or a growing permanent defect set must
be charged by a propagated-support contract or a real service/replacement
procedure. The decoder does not secretly know D and cannot guarantee that
D stays bounded if physical hardware keeps accumulating permanent failures.

For root's nominal warm gauge-only operation, ordinary retained thermal or
faulted state need not enlarge D at all once the nominal physical channel
resumes: the theorem should use that earned joint map. A new off-gauge
parameter/control fault can enlarge D, and a new broken reporter can consume
one of the graph's bad-edge allowances. Those distinctions are consequential.

10. EXACT CHECKS AND INFORMATIVE FAILURES
========================================

The executable check records:

 * 3x3 gauge group 4096; single-site persistent algebra 16384; 229376 generator
   closure checks; logical operators on a clean row/column commute with every
   element; unrestricted all-column phase noise contains logical Z.
 * Graph minimum cut 3, explicit polynomial max-flow paths, 768 one-bad-edge
   record checks with zero violations; outside-contract two-edge failures.
 * 5-component b=0,s=1: 2304 scheduled old-plus-new fault/report histories,
   zero violations. Its complete cycle maps a 36-element boundary family
   into itself over 7344 aggregate cases.
 * 7-component b=1,s=1: 28672 scheduled histories, zero violations. Its full
   cycle maps the 112-element boundary family into itself over 329728 cases.
 * An insufficient 5-component b=1,s=1 construction fails explicitly:
   persistent column 0, old residual column 1, new fault column 2 give a

   pattern; standard decoding gives the all-ones logical operator. This
   preserves the missing '+ old error' term rather than shrinking the task.

The finite discrete checks verify the ordinary proof's abstraction. They do
not simulate a full bosonic device or estimate its hardware logical-error
rate. The material new connection is that a physically earned receiver can
pass retained quantum memory through this invariant without purity, reset,
or an independent-device-fault assumption. General correlated-noise QEC,
gauge codes and redundant classical decoding remain credited to their
ordinary theory.
