FINITE CLASSICAL RECEIVER REFRESH AND LOCAL ACTUATION
Addendum to REPEATED_MEMORY_RECEIVER.txt — 1 November 2026

Result
======

The repeated quantum receiver can use a finite classical construction instead
of a perfect classical controller. Five independent decoder/history/frame
replicas, each with its OWN majority-of-five refresh circuit, tolerate one
old erroneous replica state and one new arbitrary replica-domain fault at
every update. The post-update state again has at most one erroneous replica.
The final answer remains fivefold encoded, and physical commands are decoded
only in local actuation domains. A failed local actuator is an actual quantum
control fault, charged to the quantum cycle's fault budget.

Seven replicas tolerate one fixed permanently bad classical domain plus the
old-state/new-fault pair. These are ordinary deterministic redundancy results;
no hardware failure probability, asynchronous consensus result or new general
classical fault-tolerance principle is claimed.

Executable construction and checks: check_classical_refresh.py.
Exact ledger: classical_refresh_checks.json.

1. ORDINARY PRECEDENTS AND WHAT IS ACTUALLY CONSTRUCTED
=====================================================

Classical logic made reliable through redundancy is foundational work, not
an Astra novelty. See J. von Neumann, Probabilistic Logics and the Synthesis
of Reliable Organisms from Unreliable Components, Automata Studies (1956),
pp. 43-98; primary scan:
https://www.mriedel.ece.umn.edu/wiki/images/a/af/Von_Neumann_Probabilistic_Logics_and_the_Synthesis_of_Reliable_Organisms_from_Unreliable_Components.pdf

John Rushby, Reconfiguration and Transient Recovery in State-Machine
Architectures, FTCS 26 (1996), pp. 6-15, treats synchronized replicated machines,
transient recovery, majority-restored state, and the separate issues of
interactive consistency, clocks and membership:
https://www.csl.sri.com/papers/ftcs96/

The explicit circuit below is a local synchronous hardware construction with
direct wires, fixed update phases and a common actually recorded measurement
input. It does not claim to solve arbitrary network delay or Byzantine clock
agreement. The useful addition to the quantum work is supplying the finite
receiver algorithm, replicated state refresh, last-stage fault accounting and
resource ledger instead of leaving its classical controller perfect.

2. FINITE STATE MACHINE AND PHYSICAL FAULT DOMAINS
=================================================

Let S_t be the whole classical state required by the quantum receiver:
same-axis history, agreement counter, snapshot counter, Pauli frame, flag and
operation phase. Let u_t contain the actual newly recorded detector bits and
any finite physical-control words. A deterministic finite circuit computes

 S_(t+1) = F(S_t,u_t).

The supplied receiver_update function implements agreement, cap failure,
minimum-weight repetition decoding, frame translation and switching from the
X block to the Z block and back. path_snapshot_syndrome builds the stabilizer
vector from raw path-gauge detector bits. For the spatially redundant graph,
each replica uses the actual fixed edge-disjoint-path decoder already supplied
in check_persistent_receiver.py. There is no common trusted syndrome decoder
before replication.

For each replica i, physically provide:

 * Its own state register and candidate register, each W bits.
 * Its own copy of F, operating at a fixed bounded combinational latency.
 * Its own separately buffered inputs from every detector/source channel.
 * Its own majority circuit for EVERY bit of the refreshed state.
 * Its own output buffer and isolation from other replicas' supplies/signals.

Each such bundle is one replica, or pod, fault domain. A fault in its memory,
combinational gates, voter, local clock buffer or output driver may corrupt
its entire word and may send different wrong values to different recipients.
The proof never reduces a whole-word fault to one bit flip. A faulty source
still owns only one input position in each other pod's voter. Electrical
shorts or common control failures that corrupt multiple source positions
must be charged to their actual multiple domains.

All mutable state that affects future decisions belongs to W and is refreshed.
A stale program counter, unrefreshed lookup table or hidden state machine must
not retain a supposedly corrected error. F may be hardwired finite logic;
mutable program/configuration words must be included in the encoded state,
or their persistent failure must be treated as a permanently bad pod.

Refresh is a fixed hardwired operation independent of the possibly corrupt
application state: every candidate word is voted and latched on each refresh
phase. A corrupted program counter cannot disable its own repair. With a
software-only implementation that lets corrupt state skip voting or choose
the wrong destination addresses, the invariant below would not follow.

3. THE ACTUAL FIVE-POD UPDATE
============================

Use two local register banks and fixed physical phases:

 (a) Each pod i computes C_i=F(S_i,u_i) into its candidate bank.
 (b) Hold all candidate banks stable. Every pod j's separate refresh circuit
     computes each next-state bit as majority(C_1,...,C_5).
 (c) Each voter writes only its OWN state bank. Candidate and state banks
     are not a shared writable memory.

The candidate computation, source-to-voter fanout, vote and latch all lie
inside the update's fault window. A fault at the boundary is assigned to
one adjacent window. Continuous idle-memory exposure belongs to the next
window; it is not omitted by labeling the device idle.

Assume at the start of the window all but at most one pod store S_t, and at
most one fresh pod fault occurs in that window. The old bad pod and fresh
fault together can supply at most two wrong candidate inputs to any voter.
At least three source candidates still equal the SAME correct F(S_t,u_t).
Every nonfaulty voter therefore outputs that whole word exactly.

If the fresh event faults a voter, latch or a whole pod, at most its own
output bank can remain wrong; all other voters still see a correct majority.
If it faults only a source or old memory, its incorrect value is outvoted.
If it occurs after voting and corrupts a stored result, it leaves only that
one residual bad pod. Thus the next boundary again has at most one bad state.

This proves an invariant for an arbitrary number of updates with one fresh
pod fault per update. The old residual and next fresh fault are explicitly
distinct allowances. A whole-word input can be arbitrary and a bad source
can equivocate; majority is applied independently to every bit, where the
same three good words are identical. Correlations among failures are allowed
provided the stated deterministic domain count holds.

There is no single final refresh voter. Five different voters produce five
encoded output copies. A final logical measurement bit is retained as five
physical bits; no unprotected 'last reliable bit' is silently appended.

4. PERMANENT BAD PODS AND WHY FIVE IS SOMETIMES INSUFFICIENT
==========================================================

If b classical pods are permanently arbitrary and at most s new pod domains
fault per update, use

 N >= 2b+4s+1, N odd.

The boundary admits the b permanent bad pods plus at most s old residual
transient states. The next update sees at most b+2s bad source positions.
A strict majority remains correct. Only the b permanent voters and at most
s newly faulted voters/output registers can then leave bad states, restoring
the boundary family. The identity of the permanent set need not be known,
but its actual size must remain bounded throughout the epoch.

For s=1, five pods suffice when b=0; seven suffice when b=1. This is not the
same b as the quantum block's strong-memory support. They are separate
physical fault sets with separately stated budgets.

Informative counterexamples are retained:

 * Three replicas can fail with an old bad value 1, a new bad value 1 and the
   correct value 0: majority(1,1,0)=1.
 * Five replicas can fail with one permanent, one old and one new bad source:
   majority(1,1,1,0,0)=1 although the correct bit is 0.

A new permanent circuit/configuration fault must enlarge b, or be counted
as a fresh bad pod on every update it affects. There is no invisible service
process that removes accumulated permanent defects.

5. INPUT FANOUT, TIMING AND LOCAL PHYSICAL ACTUATION
===================================================

Detector information
--------------------

Each physical edge detector's recorded output is fanned out through isolated
branches to all pods. Each pod performs its own packing, graph/path decode,
history comparison and frame calculation. A local buffer/branch failure is
a pod input fault. A wrong detector bit common to all pods is still an ACTUAL
measurement-report error; classical replication cannot recreate information
that was never measured correctly. It enters the quantum receiver's report
fault analysis or its persistent-edge redundancy contract.

A shared unprotected aggregator for every detector bit would be a larger
common fault domain. A permanently corrupt such aggregator is not cured by
five replicas. Use direct per-detector input branches and per-pod aggregation,
or explicitly charge/protect the actual common aggregation hardware.

Control outputs
---------------

Every intended single-qubit pulse or local control word stays N-fold encoded
until a distinct local voter at that physical data port. A good local voter
sees a correct majority, even with an old bad controller state plus a new
controller-domain fault. The voter is ordinary finite logic and may fail.
Its failure can then produce an arbitrary operation at THAT physical port,
which is a physical data/control fault in the quantum analysis.

To claim one-site support, that port must actually control only its assigned
data qubit's pulse/couplings, with no shared voter feeding every qubit. Meter
apparatus-only controls have dedicated edge domains and use the proven edge
fault algebra. A faulty control that leaves both participants' forces on
during a basis change can have two-site support: it must be charged as such,
or the independently voted per-data-port controls/interlocks must physically
exclude that propagation. Calling an actuator 'local' does not earn locality.

The same applies to operation timing, abort/continue and flags: these remain
encoded control words and are consumed locally, not by one perfect global
command decoder. Under the admitted replica budget, their ordinary local
majorities agree. A failing local command/clock latch is an actual local
quantum fault. A shared control fault spanning many ports is a larger event.

Important budget separation
---------------------------

One corrected internal controller fault per classical update can occur
indefinitely without producing a data fault. It does NOT authorize one bad
actuator voter at every sub-update while retaining the quantum assumption
of only one new physical fault per COMPLETE correction cycle. All actual
failed final actuator domains, meter controls, data faults and detector
report faults must together satisfy the quantum cycle's s budget or its
separately earned probabilistic noise condition. No perfect final voter is
needed, but its real errors are not free.

Timing and common failures
---------------------------

This finite synchronous circuit uses a real clock/distribution network and
guard intervals exceeding its stated healthy-component skew, propagation
and register setup bounds. Candidate banks remain stable during vote/latch;
quantum actuation samples the encoded command only after the bounded settle
interval. For the five-input circuit below the vote depth is five gates.
The actual F latency, wire/fanout delay, latch setup and guard times must be
added; no zero-time processing is claimed.

A clock-buffer failure confined to one pod or one actuator is a local fault.
A shared oscillator, power rail or global clock-distribution failure that
disturbs many replicas is charged to those many domains and can violate the
contract. It is not asserted impossible or assigned zero failure probability.
This is the same ordinary locality boundary that must be respected for the
quantum controls. We do not claim an unconstructed asynchronous consensus or
Byzantine clock-synchronization mechanism from simple majority refresh.

Initialization and finite precision
----------------------------------

The initial known frame/history state is loaded independently into the pods;
at most one faulty initialization leaves the admitted five-pod boundary.
An arbitrary common wrong program or initial logical state is not corrected.
For an unknown classical application word, supply it in the encoded boundary
family or with an actual input error-correction contract.

The algorithm consumes finite recorded bits and finite control words. ADC
resolution, phase quantization, analog actuator accuracy and signal timing
remain real physical conditions. Quantization is not declared exact merely
because the resulting word is replicated. Store/replicate any finite control
calibration words needed by the chosen physical operation; charge their
residual physical approximation in that operation's error model.

6. EXPLICIT VOTER CIRCUITS AND RESOURCE COST
===========================================

For five inputs a,b,c,d,e, use only the following two-input AND/OR gates:

 p=a OR b, q=a AND b, r=c OR d, s=c AND d;
 A=(q AND r) OR (p AND s);           [at least three of a,b,c,d]
 B=(q OR s) OR (p AND r);            [at least two of a,b,c,d]
 M5=A OR (e AND B).

This is exactly majority of five, using 12 two-input gates at depth 5. Five
physically distinct copies per state bit cost 60 gates per state bit,
or 60W gates total. No sharing of internal voter gates between pods is used.
These are explicit upper bounds, not a claim of gate-count optimality.

For seven inputs, a fixed seven-layer odd-even transposition network sorts
the bits. Each comparator outputs AND and OR. There are 21 comparators,
42 two-input gates and depth 7; select the middle wire. Seven independent
voters cost 294W gates. No reliable external comparison oracle is required.

For the five-pod version the state/controller cost is:

 * Five copies of the actual F decoder, with their actual gate cost/latency.
 * 10W state-plus-candidate register bits, plus independently buffered input
   words and the actual program/configuration/physical-control resources.
 * 60W two-input gates for refresh, depth 5, with 25W source-to-voter bit paths.
 * One additional 12-gate local majority per locally decoded command bit;
   those gates' failures enter the physical actuation budget.

For seven pods replace these counts by seven copies of F, 14W register bits,
294W refresh gates at depth 7, 49W source-to-voter paths and 42 gates per local
command-bit voter. Wiring, loads, buffers, power and timing remain charged.

A concrete receiver-only state for n=5,s=1 uses 25 bits: axis 1, round counter 3,
run counter 2, last syndrome 4, valid 1, X/Z frames 10, flag 1 and pulse phase 3.
Its five-pod state/candidate banks contain 250 bits and its refresh circuit
uses 1500 two-input gates. The n=7 version uses 31 state bits. These counts
exclude ADC/control precision words, pulse-duration counters, calibration
tables, extra application payload, input queues and any state required by
the chosen physical module;
those actual bits and logic must be added, not assumed absent.

The hardware count is finite and explicit, but it does not imply a competitive
device area, energy, speed, reliability or temperature. The strongest ordinary
quantum route can use the identical classical construction.

7. CHECKS
=========

The executable verifies all 32 majority-of-five inputs and all 128 inputs of
the seven-input sorting voter. It exhaustively checks 910 receiving-voter
cases for five replicas and 24528 for seven replicas with one permanently bad
pod. Each nonfaulty receiver is checked against every possible assignment on
its bad source channels; this includes per-destination equivocation without
assuming all faulty fanout branches agree. Whole-word protection follows by
the same bitwise proof, with no independence between bits.

There are 17408 checks of the actual finite receiver's syndrome/frame update
for n=5 and n=7, covering every stored component frame and every correctable
component. The concrete input sequence 0,1,0,1 produces an observed cap flag.
Insufficient three-pod and five-with-permanent-pod examples are saved.

These checks certify the finite deterministic model and algorithm. They do
not assign probabilities to physical gates or shared failures. The quantum
and classical constructions are now connected by an explicit interface:
recorded detector bits, replicated finite history/frame state, and finite
local actuation whose remaining faults are counted where they physically act.
