FINITE CONTROL, UNKNOWN POLES, AND UNBOUNDED RETAINED MEMORY
Astra P2 / Phase 8. Root proof. 1 November 2026.

Purpose
=======
Explain why the new exact memory interface is consequential but cannot be
silently promoted to an all-state theorem for a miscalibrated apparatus.
The results below are elementary exact obstructions in stated classes, not
general impossibility theorems for quantum computing or for finite-energy
error correction. They also identify which replacement mechanisms escape.

1. Exact robustness on a continuous pole interval forces a trivial difference
=========================================================================
Let f be a complex integrable waveform supported in a finite interval [0,T].
For a fixed damped linear mediator, the incoming old-mode coefficient is

 c(kappa,Delta)=-i integral_0^T exp[(-kappa/2+i Delta)t] f(t)dt.

Suppose the same fixed waveform has c=0 for EVERY Delta in an open real
interval, at a fixed kappa, or for EVERY kappa in an open real interval at a
fixed Delta. Then f=0 almost everywhere.

Proof. F(s)=integral exp(st)f(t)dt is entire because f has compact support.
The assumed zeros have an accumulation point in its complex domain. The
identity theorem gives F identically zero. Restricting to the imaginary axis
and using uniqueness of the Fourier transform gives f=0. QED.

For several commuting data labels, apply the statement to each relevant
DIFFERENCE f_z-f_z'. A common data-independent force is unconstrained and does
not supply the intended entangling operation. For preservation within parity
sectors, only differences inside each preserved sector need be considered.
A Hamiltonian already in the exact parity/gauge algebra can therefore escape:
those differences vanish identically before optimization.

This theorem is about a fixed finite open-loop waveform with no actual pole
information. It does not exclude acquiring new information, adapting controls
to it, exploiting a true protected subsystem, or achieving arbitrarily good
accuracy on an admitted state family. An optimizer supplied with the actual
pole may construct its nullspace; competitors must get the same information.
Finite uncertainty after calibration still has to be charged.

2. Why a small nonzero coefficient is not a uniform small error
==============================================================
An old oscillator displacement difference xi contributes the characteristic
operator D(xi) to a data coherence. In an old coherent state |alpha>,

 <alpha|D(xi)|alpha>=exp(-|xi|^2/2+xi alpha* -xi* alpha).

For any nonzero arbitrarily small xi, an allowed coherent amplitude of order
1/|xi| changes the data phase by pi. The magnitude approaches one as xi goes
to zero. Thus an unrestricted old-state supremum can remain order one while
the nominal displacement residual tends to zero. A small number printed by a
pulse optimizer is not itself a state-uniform noise strength.

Finite input sectors, exponential-moment families, or bounded mediator
interactions remove this obstruction in different ways. The main Phase8
replacement lemma uses the first two together, over the actual joint history.
The finite dirty-qubit competitor supplies the third directly for bounded
physical error generators. It does not need an oscillator energy argument.

3. Higher-order nulling is useful, and its price must be explicit
===============================================================
At a nominal pole s0, enforce integral t^j exp(s0 t)f(t)dt=0 for j=0,...,p.
Taylor's theorem then gives the uniform scalar bound

 |F(s0+delta)| <= |delta|^(p+1)/(p+1)!
      integral t^(p+1) exp[Re(s0)t+|delta|t] |f(t)|dt.

This explains the actual finite derivative-null readout construction. It is
a finite-order residual bound, not exact closure on an interval. It consumes
signal, control degrees of freedom, bandwidth and finite integration time.
The actual integer coefficients and ADC receiver, rather than an exact
unknown-pole filter, determine the implemented bound in this packet.

Root's selected actual-pole gate comparison is an exact expression evaluated
at displayed scalar poles. The numerical grid is NOT an interval certificate.
The finite readout, in contrast, uses an analytic Taylor remainder covering its
stated uncertainty rectangle. Do not combine those two evidential strengths.

4. Mean energy does not imply a high-order joint loss tail
========================================================
For an old oscillator family with mean energy E, choose

 rho=(1-E/N)|0><0|+(E/N)|N><N|,   N>=E.

The mean is exactly E. During pure loss for time T, the number of emitted
photons from |N> has the binomial distribution with loss probability
1-exp(-kappa T). Taking N of order r/(kappa T) makes r emissions appreciable
on the rare high-energy component. Their unconditional weight is of order
E kappa T/r, rather than (E kappa T)^r. Normalizing on that component makes
its instantaneous loss intensity kappa N arbitrarily high.

This is a counterexample to an inferred independent-event or local-stochastic
admission, NOT automatically a logical failure. Many losses in one fixed
gauge/defect domain can be harmless to the implemented receiver. The physical
support theorem and the joint strength theorem must be kept separate.

5. The operational escape routes
===============================
(a) Enforce the exact interaction/record algebra of a protected subsystem.
    Strong retained state is then allowed on that support; reporting accuracy
    and propagation during basis changes still need an actual construction.
(b) Enforce incoming absence of influence on every preserved coherence and
    delivered record; outgoing closure can supply a stronger product channel.
(c) Admit finite tails over the entire actual experiment, and pay the complete
    replacement remainder once. Never normalize on favorable records.
(d) Use a finite mediator with bounded harmful generators, paying its actual
    decoherence, preparation/readout, switching and control exposures.
(e) Treat a physically localized hardware defect as a persistent domain, with
    its real onset intensity and a receiver correcting that entire support.
    Relabeling each subsequent use as a new independent fault is invalid.

These choices can be combined. The point is not that every mode must be near
vacuum. It is that physical information flow and the actual receiver determine
which part of a surviving state must be bounded, and why.
