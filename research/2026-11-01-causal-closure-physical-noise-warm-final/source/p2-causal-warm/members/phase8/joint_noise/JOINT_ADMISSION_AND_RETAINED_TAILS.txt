JOINT ADMISSION THROUGH RETAINED BOSONIC MEMORY
Phase8 independent mathematical construction and audit, 1 November 2026

Decision
========

The physical admission can be earned without bounding each normalized
measurement history. The correct object is an exponential number moment of
the full, unnormalized classical--quantum history. It gives a comparison of
the ENTIRE finite experiment with a model having bounded perturbations.
Only the perturbing operators should be capped. Capping the nominal oscillator
Hamiltonian would destroy the exact hot-bus identities at the Fock boundary.

This is useful for the new force-only loop: retain the exact damped nominal
operation, and cap only the small residual force or other calibration errors.
The number cost enters as sqrt(L) for a linear force, rather than L for an
uncancelled dispersive number coupling. It is much less attractive to treat
all storage loss in the inherited dispersive meter as a perturbation.

The construction supplies a genuine joint norm admission and a finite-life
error bound. It is NOT a universal device theorem, a fresh independent fault
coin, a claim that every conditional bus state is thermal, or a new generic
fault-tolerance threshold theorem. The actual local support of each admitted
fault still has to meet the implemented receiver's old-error-plus-new-error
contract. Initial preparation, force/rate envelopes and fresh fields remain
physical inputs that have to be earned by the architecture.

1. Physical model and actual histories
======================================

For one retained mode, n=a^dag a, use

 H(t)=H_N(t)+F(t)a^dag+F(t)^dag a,
 [H_N(t),n]=0, ||F(t)|| <= u(t),

 and fresh thermal Markov ports

 sum_l k_l(t)[(nbar_l(t)+1) D[a]+nbar_l(t) D[a^dag]].

F may act on arbitrary data, gauge, ancilla, controller and reference systems.
It need not commute with its values at other times. The number-preserving
part can include arbitrary data controls and frequency errors. Spatially
separate modes may share data but must have the declared local coupling
structure; a cross-mode interaction or collective bath is an additional
fault domain, not automatically a local error.

Assume 0<=nbar_l(t)<=n_* and k_-(t)<=sum_l k_l(t)<=k_+(t).
A bounded actuator error is included in u(t) at ALL times, including nominally
off periods. Bounds on the rate and force must hold on the admitted physical
histories. Omitting an entire cooling/readout interval, an uncontrolled
parametric pump, an unbounded drive fault, or a returning field is not covered
by silently reusing these envelopes.

Write rho_h(t) for each SUBNORMALIZED state conditioned on the actual
measurement record h. Define

 M_theta(t)=sum_h Tr[exp(theta n) rho_h(t)].                     (1)

The joint data/bus state can be nonGaussian and correlated. Measurement and
postselection can make a normalized branch arbitrarily more energetic than
this average. Equation (1) never divides by that branch's probability.
Measurements of outgoing fields, data, and controllers preserve the averaged
operator inequalities, and feedback is allowed when the same force/rate
bounds apply to every chosen action. This is a classical--quantum direct-sum
argument, not an independence assumption.

2. Operator exponential Lyapunov inequality
==========================================

Let V_theta=exp(theta n), 0<theta<log(1+1/n_*) (no upper endpoint for n_*=0).
Define

 d(theta)=(1-exp(-theta))[1-n_*(exp(theta)-1)],
 s(theta)=2 sinh(theta/2).

On the finite-Fock core, the exact thermal adjoint is

 L_thermal^*(V_theta)
 =[-k d_actual(theta) n + sum_l k_l nbar_l(exp(theta)-1)] V_theta.

For the drive, conjugating through V_theta gives a quadrature. Completing
the square, (a +/- iF/(u epsilon))^dag(a +/- iF/(u epsilon))>=0,
gives, for every epsilon(t)>0,

 L_drive^*(V_theta)
 <=u s(theta)[epsilon n+epsilon^(-1)]V_theta.                   (2)

This holds even for noncommuting, operator-valued F; only F^dag F<=u^2 I
and its commutation with a are needed. Hence

 dM_theta/dt <= [-k_- d(theta)+u epsilon s(theta)] partial_theta M_theta
              +[k_+ n_*(exp(theta)-1)+u s(theta)/epsilon]M_theta.  (3)

The number-preserving Hamiltonian contributes zero. One can first establish
(2)--(3) for finite-support states and bounded observables, then use the
positive moment bound to pass to the physical semigroup. This is the usual
energy-domain qualification; no bare formal interchange of infinite traces
is needed. The matrix audit checks the signs and factors independently.

Let theta(t),c(t) obey the scalar characteristic equations

 theta_dot=k_- d(theta)-u epsilon s(theta),
 c_dot=k_+ n_*(exp(theta)-1)+u s(theta)/epsilon,
 theta(0)=theta_0, c(0)=0.                                    (4)

Whenever theta(t)>0 stays in the stated thermal moment domain,

 M_{theta(t)}(t) <= exp(c(t)) M_{theta_0}(0).                   (5)

This is the main reusable tool. Real operation, waiting, switching and
readout envelopes enter the SAME equations. The auxiliary epsilon is a
proof parameter, not an additional control pulse.

3. Repetition and the rare-history tail
=======================================

Suppose a complete repeated physical period ends with theta(T)=theta_1>
 theta_0 and c(T)=C. Convexity of log M_theta, with M_0=1, gives

 log M_{theta_0}(after)
 <= (theta_0/theta_1)[C+log M_{theta_0}(before)].               (6)

Thus, from an initial moment B_0=log M_{theta_0}(initial),

 log M_{theta_0}(boundary) <= B=max(B_0,theta_0 C/(theta_1-theta_0)). (7)

No data-error count was used. In particular, a data error that remains after
one operation and another error in the next are already allowed in the
moment proof; arbitrary data controls obeying the envelope are allowed.
Whether those two errors are LOGICALLY correctable is decided by the actual
receiver, not by this scalar inequality.

A common all-time bound is obtained with any theta_*<=min_t theta(t):

 log M_{theta_*}(t)
 <= theta_* max_t[(B+c(t))/theta(t)] =: B_*.                  (8)

For every module, every time and the full unnormalized record ensemble,

 sum_h Tr[1_{n>=L} rho_h(t)] <= exp(B_*-theta_* L).            (9)

Equation (9) does NOT say that every normalized history has a small tail.
It is enough for the whole-experiment comparison below. Different modules
need not have independent occupation distributions; a sum over their
individual bounds is valid for arbitrarily correlated data and records.

Initialization is a real condition. A thermal marginal of mean n0 has
M_theta=[1-n0(exp(theta)-1)]^(-1), including when that marginal is correlated
with other systems. More generally use a measured/derived exponential-moment
family. Finite passive cooling cannot create a uniform exponential-moment
bound from completely unrestricted initial oscillator states: an arbitrarily
large Fock input remains arbitrarily energetic after any finite attenuation.
Our theorem therefore propagates an admitted preparation family; it does not
supply an unimplemented initial energy test or an infinite-time bath oracle.

4. The correct virtual cap: perturbations only
=============================================

Let the chosen exact nominal physical evolution already include the useful
hot-bus gate, its actual nominal damping and thermal noise, and the complete
receiver. Let a residual force have Hamiltonian

 delta H=delta F a^dag+delta F^dag a, ||delta F||<=v(t).

Set P_L=1_{n<=L} and replace ONLY this perturbation by

 delta H_L=P_L delta H P_L.                                  (10)

The nominal Hamiltonian and all nominal outlets are unchanged on the full
oscillator Hilbert space. In particular, exact canonical-commutator and
whole-space closed-loop identities remain true. The actual apparatus is NOT
measured, reset, filtered, postselected or truncated.

The capped perturbation obeys

 ||delta H_L|| <=2 v(t) sqrt(L).                              (11)

Writing Q=1-P_L, the removed linear-force operator is of the form aQ+Qa^dag
(with bounded data coefficients). The elementary quadratic-form bound is

 (delta H-delta H_L)^2 <=4 v(t)^2(n+1)1_{n>=L}.               (12)

For L+1>=1/theta_*, (8) implies

 ||(delta H-delta H_L) rho||_1
 <=2 v(t) sqrt(L+1) exp[(B_*-theta_*L)/2].                    (13)

Duhamel's formula plus complete trace-norm contraction then compares the
actual and noise-capped experiment, including all retained modes, data,
classical outcomes and any reference system:

 (1/2)||rho_actual-out-rho_capped-out||_1
 <= 2 sqrt(L+1) exp[(B_*-theta_*L)/2]
       sum_modules integral_actual_lifetime v_j(t) dt.       (14)

The bound applies to every admitted initial state/reference, so it is an
energy-family-constrained complete instrument bound. It is not an unrestricted
diamond norm on arbitrarily energetic inputs. The actual state is used on
the right-hand side of Duhamel; no moment assumption on every normalized
state in a fictitious fault expansion is needed. Classical records and
feedback can be retained explicitly, or measurements deferred; the same
Hamiltonian comparison bounds the full recorded history.

The crucial improvement is that the total action in (14) is the RESIDUAL
force difference, not the large nominal force. This makes an exact physical
reference at the actual unknown pole useful: if the implemented waveform is
close to a waveform with exact incoming-memory erasure at that pole, the
small difference is what must be capped. Such a reference is a proof
construction, not an oracle pulse supplied to the hardware.

For an uncancelled number error delta omega(t) n, the bounded perturbation
norm is L|delta omega|, and the comparison tail uses
(L+1)exp[(B_*-theta_*(L+1))/2]. This is more costly. Wherever the physical
reference absorbs the actual number-preserving frequency into its homogeneous
motion and changes only the force, that stronger comparison should be used.

5. Storage loss, no-jump drift and the original dispersive route
===============================================================

If a positive extra unobserved loss rate kappa is genuinely a perturbation,
replace its jump a by a P_L, leaving the nominal dynamics exact. Its bounded
superoperator norm is

 ||kappa D[a P_L]||_diamond <=2 kappa L.                      (15)

This includes the no-jump anticommutator; counting emitted photons alone
would omit it. For E=Tr(n rho), E_>=Tr(n Q rho), and
E2_>=Tr(n^2 Q rho), a direct expansion gives

 ||(D[a]-D[aP_L])(rho)||_1
 <=2 sqrt(E E_>)+E_>+sqrt(E2_>).                             (16)

Since E<=B_*/theta_* by Jensen, and for sufficiently large L,
E_><=(L+1)exp[B_*-theta_*(L+1)] and
E2_><=(L+1)^2exp[B_*-theta_*(L+1)],
(16) is an explicit all-history tail comparison. Sum its time integral over
ALL locations exposed to this noise. A warm extra upward Lindblad channel
has an analogous boundary term and cannot be deleted merely because the
incoming apparatus is called gauge.

Only positive extra Lindblad rates can be capped this way while retaining an
immediately evident CPTP generator. Subtracting a capped dissipator for a
negative rate drift is not generally a legitimate global Lindbladian. One
option is to choose a certified lower nominal rate. A better option in the
new force-only architecture is to keep the ACTUAL rate in the exact Gaussian
reference and cap only the corresponding small force correction.

A useful failure is preserved in exponential_tail_results.json. For the
Phase7 passive pre/post schedule (p=.42387235,q=.42384690,U=4.71219215),
a much sharper scheduled moment can be derived when those windows have the
certified passive scattering and no unmodeled forcing. A numerical example
needs a Fock scale about124 for 10^6 uses at a 10^-6 whole-history tolerance
when truncating the drive merely for simulation. Charging original intrinsic
loss kappa=10^-4 over a mapping at that scale gives the conservative
norm budget2*kappa*124*1.6707963=.04144; over the full6.7051 meter it is .16629.
Those are norm budgets, not predicted physical failure rates. They show that
this blanket sufficient admission is too expensive at the inherited point.
A consistent reference receiver must also be kept fixed when taking this
noise comparison; one cannot silently change its temporal filter with the
loss parameter and retain an unrelated exact report coin.

The earlier whole-Hamiltonian cutoff calculation is retained only as a
simulation-tail diagnostic. It is NOT the nominal FT gadget, because its
boundary breaks the exact hot-bus operation. The perturbation-only cap fixes
that failure.

6. Joint admission, no independent fault coin
============================================

For each physical location l, integrate the actual bounded perturbation
norm over its complete duration. For a residual linear force,

 eta_l <=4 sqrt(L) integral_l ||delta F(t)|| dt.               (17)

Add 2 integral||delta H_data|| for bounded data/control Hamiltonians, and
2 integral gamma||J||^2 for positive bounded Lindblad terms. Finite gates,
readout, waiting, switching and local actuator latency belong in that ledger.
A shared control error with large support is a large fault domain; the
number of affected sites cannot be relabeled away.

For a time-discretized CPTP circuit, write each actual location as its
nominal channel plus a difference with complete norm<=eta_l (Duhamel).
For a specified set of marked locations, the sum of terms with those
locations faulty and arbitrary unmarked locations is bounded by the product
of their eta_l, using actual channels of norm one at unmarked locations.
The continuous-time form follows from the time-ordered insertion expansion.
Equivalently, interpolate the bounded Hamiltonian perturbations and positive
Lindblad rates; mixed derivatives insert one required perturbation in every
marked region and the intervening maps remain CPTP. The time-order integrals
are bounded by products of the integrated strengths. No conditioning on a
normalized oscillator history and no fresh fault probability is used.

Use this admission with the REAL fault-support and recovery theorem. The
Phase7 receiver's boundary invariant counts an old surviving error plus a
new error, and the consumed-CNOT transaction shares its budget across the
gate, both recoveries and idles. The bounded-noise analysis does not license
replacing that by separate fresh promises for each substep. Exact fresh
Pauli/measurement channels that are already proved for the nominal reference
can retain their sharper CP probabilities; coherent residual strengths from
(17) must not be called such probabilities.

The final conclusion has the form

 actual logical error <= whole-history comparison (14)
                       + actual consumer's bounded-noise fault-path bound.

It is a reduction to an earned standard joint admission, not a proof of a
new threshold from an assumed number of faults.

readout coupling to fresh thermal modes. Let A be an actual complete slot and
N its nominal channel, both CPTP on data+retained apparatus+records. With
P=1_{n<=L} and Q=I-P on the INPUT apparatus only, define

 A^L(rho)=A(P rho P)+N(Q rho Q),
 N^L(rho)=N(P rho P+Q rho Q).

Both maps are CPTP. Their difference is
 B^L=(A-N)(P rho P).
Thus a complete restricted-input norm bound eta(L) for A-N becomes a uniform
complete norm bound for B^L on every input, including all reference systems.
The nominal N^L remains logically correct whenever arbitrary apparatus-only
channels before N are harmless. This follows from the proved all-state
semicausal or gauge contract, rather than from assuming the apparatus was
uncorrelated. The Hamiltonian is not Fock truncated; canonical identities
are unaffected. The pinching is a mathematical construction, not a physical
number measurement or a free herald.

For q=Tr(Q rho), the off-diagonal block obeys
 (1/2)||P rho Q+Q rho P||_1 <=sqrt(q(1-q)).
Replacing the Q block by a different CPTP slot costs at most q in half trace
norm. Hence
 (1/2)||A(rho)-A^L(rho)||_1 <=sqrt(q)+q.

Telescope using ACTUAL prefixes and CPTP capped suffixes. Every input tail
q_j is therefore bounded by the actual ensemble moment, rather than by a
postselected conditional model. For M compiled slots,

 delta_total <= M[sqrt(q_*)+q_*],

10. Finite preparation and a failed nonlinear shortcut
=======================================================

A legitimate initialization path starts with an explicit admitted finite
energy family, not an unmeasured claim of perfect Gibbs equilibrium. For a
thermal attenuator of transmissivity r^2, bath occupation nb and z=exp(theta),

 Phi^*(z^n)=[1-nb(1-r^2)(z-1)]^(-1)
    [1+r^2(z-1)/(1-nb(1-r^2)(z-1))]^n.

For an actually admitted initial Fock cap N_init this directly bounds the
output exponential moment after a finite conditioning interval. A supplied
initial exponential-tail bound can be used instead. Conditioning time,
thermal occupation, coupling ramps and any data/control exposure must be
charged. This supplies a calculation FROM a physical initial admission;
it does not establish that the hardware has acquired that admission. A
mean-energy estimate alone cannot be substituted for it.

An appealing attempted escape was two-photon loss D[a^2], whose effective
n(n-1) death rate can mathematically come down from arbitrarily high photon
numbers in finite time. That is NOT an earned microscopic initialization.
The usual finite buffer interaction a^2 b^dag+h.c. conserves n_a+2n_b;
with a linear buffer outlet, total energy leaves at at most a rate linear
in that total excitation. Its elimination cannot be extended to unbounded
initial energies while retaining the effective quadratic loss rate. The
apparent uniform initialization would exploit the elimination exactly where
its physical validity fails. This useful failed shortcut is preserved, not
used in the claimed operating regime.
