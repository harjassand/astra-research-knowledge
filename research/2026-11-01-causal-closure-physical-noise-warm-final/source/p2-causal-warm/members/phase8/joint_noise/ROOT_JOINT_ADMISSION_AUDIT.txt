INDEPENDENT AUDIT OF ROOT'S CAUSAL CLOSURE / JOINT ADMISSION
2 November 2026. Scope: root proof §§4,5,8,9 and analytic_all_interval_barrier.py.

Verdict
=======
The input-sector replacement and analytic photon barrier are sound under their
stated physical envelopes. The joint majorant needs an explicit mixed fault
expansion and correct support grading. Gross switch outages are outside the
barrier as originally written, but a finite-lifetime hybrid extension is
available without an observed flag or independent fault coins. Its strong
fault locality/axis conditions and larger consumer must be stated.

1. Input-sector pinching: passes
================================
A^L=A(P rho P)+N(Q rho Q) and N^L=N o pinch(P,Q) are CPTP.
B^L=(A-N)(P rho P) has complete norm bounded by the COMPLETE restricted-input
norm of A-N. This remains true with arbitrary reference systems. A physical
apparatus-only pinching before an all-state nominal logical interface cannot
change that logical interface; this does not truncate the oscillator's
Hamiltonian or canonical algebra.

For q=Tr(Q rho), the actual-versus-comparison half-trace distance is at most
sqrt(q(1-q))+q <=sqrt(q)+q. Actual-prefix/CPTP-suffix telescoping therefore
uses the ACTUAL unnormalized input tails. Adaptive schedules must be compiled
with their actual finite maximum/padding and all physical idle exposure.
Neither a postselection probability nor a perfect measured energy flag is
introduced. The same proof applies to the complete classical--quantum history.

3. The precise safe mixed CP / coherent expansion
================================================
The inclusive marked-set product bound is valid for completed maps
A_j=G_j+B_j, with actual unmarked A_j CPTP and ||B_j||<=eta_j. It does NOT by
itself establish that an entire B_j is one correctable physical event if it
contains several local data insertions.

A safe decomposition needs the following additional structure.

(a) Partition a fixed-basis gauge snapshot into its connected same-axis data
components, together with the fixed apparatus edges serving each component.
Within a component, the baseline physical data action is diagonal in physical
Z. All good and faulty apparatus amplitudes, loss trajectories, ancilla states
and records can remain arbitrary operators on their actual memory.

(b) For every vertex k of that connected component,
 Alg{Z_v:v in component}=span{I,Z_k} G_component.
Thus a local data insertion on k, surrounded by arbitrary diagonal baseline
operators, has one-site support modulo the actual gauge. Any number of
baseline apparatus effects within THAT component and THAT snapshot can be
absorbed into that insertion. The delivered component record may be arbitrary
for that snapshot. The temporal receiver handles one corrupted full snapshot
as part of that event. A second local data-site insertion is a second event.

(c) Different completed components/snapshots retain their own nominal CP bad
tags and calibration-map differences. Baseline faults cannot be absorbed
across several later snapshots. During basis changes and ordinary idles the
nominal data forces are off; residual local couplings must be counted as their
own admitted generators or actual enlarged support, not put into a harmless
unbounded baseline. For parallel star propagation, the connected-component
form above supplies a safe grouping; a nominal closure identity alone does
not prove one-fault support.

For component/snapshot macro j let its no-extra-local-insertion channel be
G_j+E_j, with G_j correct CP trace-nonincreasing and
||E_j||<=b_j=p_j+eta_j. Here p_j bounds the exact positive nominal bad-tag
subinstrument and eta_j bounds the completed diagonal calibration difference.
Expand additional bounded local generators in Dyson degree:

 A_j=G_j+E_j+sum_(r>=1) D_{j,r},
 ||D_{j,r}||<=W_j^r/r!,
 W_j=integral_j sum_a ||V_{j,a}(t)|| dt.

The physical baseline fragments remain CP contractions, even with arbitrary
retained apparatus state. Under(a)--(c), D_{j,1} is a correctable one-event
map. E_j has degree1; D_{j,r} has degree r, not degree1.

A coefficient-wise norm majorant for this macro is
 P_j(z)=e^(W_j z)+b_j z <=(1+b_j z)e^(W_j z).
Composing macros and the ordinary local-control slots gives
 product_j P_j(z) <= exp[B z], B=sum_j(b_j+W_j).
The total norm of degree>=2 terms is therefore at most

 R=exp(B)-1-B.                                             (A)

This proves the requested majorant with its physical support assumptions
visible. A general echo gate gives a counterexample without them: one local
insertion between a large entangling pulse and its inverse can become a
multi-site error even when the completed nominal channel is the identity.

A common physical two-site jump cannot simply be 'charged twice' and then
receive quadratic suppression. For example gamma D[X_i X_j] has first-order
strength O(gamma T). Either the consumer corrects that whole support, or its
integrated norm enters linearly among uncorrectable terms (a degree2 monomial
of coefficient O(gamma T), not O(gamma^2 T^2)). A larger common hardware fault
must retain its full domain.

The majorant bounds the BAD MAP norm. If all degree0/1 terms preserve the
logical subsystem/boundary invariant, the resulting HALF logical-channel
distance is at most R. A full diamond-norm statement can need a factor2.
Do not turn signed/coherent E_j or D_jr into ordinary probabilities.

4. Several rectangles: preserve the coherent normalization cost
==============================================================
For each physical rectangle A_r=Good_r+Bad_r with ||Bad_r||<=R_r,
Good_r need not be CP because it contains correctable signed terms.
Nevertheless ||Good_r||<=1+R_r. With actual CPTP suffixes,

 ||product_r A_r-product_r Good_r||
 <=product_r(1+R_r)-1 <=exp(sum_r R_r)-1.                    (B)

The inherited old-error-plus-new-error boundary theorem makes the product of
Good_r logically correct. Equation(B) is a safe finite-life composition.
The simpler sum_r R_r is its leading small-error approximation; it is not
automatically an exact CP union bound. Sharper standard FT resummations are
possible but must be proved for the chosen gadgets.

5. Gross switch outages: originally excluded
===========================================
The analytic barrier requires the flat readout port to remain in its declared
positive rate interval. A stuck-off/on port is not a small pole uncertainty.
One onset can corrupt several consecutive readouts and create a long hot
history. A two-state physical Markov switch with rates lambda(onset),mu(repair)
already shows why independent per-round coins fail:

 pi_bad=lambda/(lambda+mu),
 Pr(bad now AND bad after Delta)>=pi_bad exp(-mu Delta).

That can be much larger than pi_bad^2. The current5x5/no-persistent-reporter
consumer does not automatically contain such a history. A finite-life bound
charging ANY outage as failure is valid but grows linearly with lifetime and
usually wastes the inherited persistent-support construction.

6. Safe hybrid extension: strong onset domains plus weak noise
==============================================================
Use actual finite switch variables, initially in their admitted working state
(or include an explicitly bounded unknown initially bad set). For each local
failure domain alpha, use an onset jump

 L_on,alpha(t)=sqrt(lambda_alpha(t)) |bad><good|_alpha,
 0<=lambda_alpha(t)<=lambda_bar_alpha(t).

The rate may depend on old classical records, provided this remains a local
switch jump and the bound is uniform. It acts as identity on the quantum
apparatus/data at the instant of onset. Subsequent defective couplings may be
strong, but stay inside that fixed physical data/report domain. Real repair
may occur with arbitrary physical rates. For analysis, an unobserved virtual
register remembers the domain EVER having failed; repair does not erase it.
No controller receives that virtual flag.

For b allowed strong onset domains and
 Lambda=sum_alpha integral_actual_lifetime lambda_bar_alpha(t) dt,
the ordered CP-jump integral gives

 Pr(more than b first-onset domains)<=Lambda^(b+1)/(b+1)!.   (C)

This is a factorial-moment bound from physical conditional intensities, not
an independent fault coin. A common onset affecting several edges is ONE
event with its entire physical support/report domain. It must not be split
into independent single-edge probabilities. Repeated repair/onset on an
already ever-failed domain does not enlarge its support set.

The physical strong-domain requirements are essential:

 * Fixed endpoints and private apparatus/ports: no contamination of other
   actuators, modes, clocks or receiver channels outside the declared domain.
 * During each fixed-basis sensing block, defective data couplings remain
   diagonal in the physical measurement axis. Forces/rates may be arbitrary.
   During data basis changes their propagated error may be arbitrary on the
   fixed endpoints, which the strong support budget includes.
 * Bad reports are confined to the declared failed edges, and the actual
   spatially redundant graph decoder corrects that many unknown bad edges.
   Off-axis persistent dynamics can continually change the supposedly stable
   syndrome; fixed spatial support alone does not prove the existing receiver.
 * Common local data/control faults outside this longitudinal switch model
   retain their separate weak or strong support charge.

For an edge touching two physical data sites, one ever-failed module can have
up to2 row and2 column representatives after basis changes. With one old
surviving weak error and one new weak event, the inherited condition
 t_X>=b_X+2s, t_Z>=b_Z+2s (s=1) requires a9x9-type memory in the worst
orientation, not the original7x7 one-dispersive-participant promise. Unknown
wrong-edge reports also need3-edge-connected component graphs. In the fixed
pair-graph implementation a9-vertex3-edge-connected component needs at least
14 edges, hence at least252 edge modules for both axes of a9x9 block. This is
a resource consequence of that fixture, not a claimed architecture optimum.

7. Why the healthy-mode moments survive arbitrary strong histories
===================================================================
Never normalize on a strong history. For module i introduce the observable

 V_i(theta)=P_never,i e^(theta n_i)+P_ever,i I.

Its theta=0 expectation is one. On never-failed sectors the physical force
and damping envelopes hold, so the same operator moment inequality applies.
An onset of i contributes
 -lambda_i P_never,i(e^(theta n_i)-I)<=0.
After onset the observable is constant on that sector, so arbitrarily strong
local heating is irrelevant to this HEALTHY-SECTOR moment. Other strong
module operations commute with n_i because they act on their own apparatus
and data only; arbitrary data/memory correlations do not change this fact.
Consequently the SAME unnormalized moment barrier bounds
 Tr[P_never,i 1_(n_i>L) rho].

Apply the input-sector comparison only on never-failed sectors. Leave already
failed sectors untouched. A slot containing a first onset is a strong-domain
slot; its arbitrary longitudinal action is handled by that domain, not by a
small calibration bound. Healthy completed maps can still be separated from
strong diagonal dynamics during a sensing block because their data-axis
operators commute and their apparatus/ports are private. Local noncommuting
data insertions are handled by the component/snapshot expansion in Section3.

The total cap error is summed on actual unnormalized histories. AFTER this
comparison, weak errors have state-uniform complete norms on healthy sectors.
They can therefore be bounded in the presence of arbitrary strong-history
memory coefficients. Summing allowed strong histories uses CP trace-
nonincreasing maps; it never divides by their probability. This is the key
step that avoids reintroducing the rare-branch mean-energy fallacy.

If onset operators are instead allowed to measure an unbounded apparatus
observable, or a defective module directly heats a healthy mode, the argument
fails. Those are different physical fault models and require another bound.
If an onset jump has a non-scalar data-dependent effect operator, its no-jump
filter cannot be silently declared harmless; it must obey the same support
algebra or enter the bounded weak generator ledger.

8. Joint finite-life conclusion and limits
==========================================
Suppose the actual receiver corrects every path with at most b strong domains
and at most one fresh weak event per rectangle, including its admitted old
residual. Let R_r be(A), with all physical exposure counted and primitive
support as above. Then a safe complete finite-life HALF logical-channel bound
is, capped at1,

 delta_history
 + Lambda^(b+1)/(b+1)!
 + [exp(sum_r R_r)-1].                                    (D)

Additional first-order uncorrectable common-fault terms must be added; they
cannot be hidden in B and squared. Separate clipping or other genuinely
positive CP failure effects may be added with their proved complete weights.

This covers an OLD surviving switch defect, an old correctable weak residual,
and a NEW weak data fault without apparatus reset or a perfect defect flag.
For b=1 it does not claim to correct a second failed edge; that case is
explicitly in(C). If one wants to correct both onset domains, increase the
strong support and graph budget. The ever-failed construction deliberately
does not earn an indefinite-life repair advantage; doing that would require
an actual noisy service/requalification construction.

The hybrid support claim above is a MEMORY/RECOVERY extension. Phase7's
consumed7x7 CNOT transaction explicitly excluded persistentD. It cannot be
imported unchanged. For a useful Clifford transaction, propagate the strong
set through the actual ideal gate, then include the imported old residuals
and new faults. A conservative transversal-CNOT count would require
 t>=b_spread+3, compared with b+2 for memory; its receiver/control realization
still needs the corresponding transaction proof. No additional CNOT theorem
is asserted by this audit.

9. Residual-force ledger: distinguish input and operator caps
============================================================
The expression4 sqrt(L) integral v is a uniform generator bound for the
PERTURBATION-ONLY operator cap P_L deltaH P_L. It does not follow merely from
an input n<=L restriction: the nominal driven readout can raise occupation
within the slot and fresh thermal modes remain unbounded.

A clean implementation keeps nominal dynamics unchanged, caps only residual
forces on never-failed sectors, and adds the independently proved comparison

 delta_force <=2 A sqrt(L+1) exp[(B-theta L)/2],
 A=sum_modules integral_actual_lifetime v_i(t) dt.

Using the analytic barrier(theta=.4,B=64),L320, this is approximately
4.538e-13 A. It is small for many relevant finite ledgers, but is a real
additional term. Then4sqrt(320) integral v in root§9 is justified uniformly.
The cap and the input-sector replacement can be telescoped on the common
actual continuous/discrete history; actual prefixes and CPTP suffixes keep
the moment estimates on the physical state. Alternatively the capped force
itself satisfies the same number-ladder moment inequality, since its lowering
operator has a_L^dag a_L<=n.

Without that operator cap, a pure force-off idle slot with an input cap admits
2(sqrt(L)+sqrt(L+1)) integral v as a simple complete dilation bound, using the
nominal thermal damping energy<=L. For an actively driven slot, the reference
energy trajectory must be included too. This distinction is small numerically
for the displayed idle, but necessary for a correct joint theorem.

The healthy envelope also excludes a corrupted driver leaving a3g force on
during a nominal1e-7g idle. The hybrid strong-onset model may include such
local actuator-envelope violations, provided their onset hazards and subsequent
fixed support/axis/report locality are actually admitted. A shared power or
clock failure is one common domain with its full support. It is not many
independent small defects.

With the operator cap, add delta_force to(D). If a truly uncorrectable common
weak generator has integrated norm U, a safe one-rectangle majorant is
exp(B+U)-1-B, or charge U separately by a direct complete-map comparison.
Neither option replaces U by U^2.
