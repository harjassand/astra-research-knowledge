# Prior, acquisition, productive failures and decision

## Strongest ordinary construction was supplied first

Bruno–Pasqualotto, [2608.06367v1, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1#A1), already gives the complete left-and-right rotationally invariant quartic cone. In the notation T=|A|²,q=det A, its ROC members aT²+bTq+cq² satisfy either a=b=0,c≥0, or a>0, |b|≤4a, c≥−2a+3b²/(8a). They split as

    a W_t+mu q², t=b/(4a), mu=c+2a−3b²/(8a)≥0,
    W_t=T²+4tTq+(6t²−2)q².

The paper supplies polynomial exact-form heat dissipation for W_t and keeps the polyconvex q² term separate. This is the informed combined-method incumbent, not a new proposal. Its complete admitted symmetry scope was consumed before the endpoint attack. Cached full primary: `sol25_capability_frontiers_20261030/a_independent/heat_form/sources/Bruno_Pasqualotto_2608.06367v1.html`; it was refreshed at the primary URL in this wave.

## Decision at the actual boundary

The broad hoped-for PC-plus-angular-heat theorem has NOT been proved or refuted. The wave instead acquired an exact, full-dimensional obstacle to its natural polynomial implementation:

1. the positive ROC endpoint F* is extreme in the whole quartic polynomial cone and non-PC, so PC extraction cannot help;
2. all constant mean-converging exact-form flows for it reduce to ordinary heat;
3. no origin-C² closed correction, and therefore no polynomial primitive of ANY degree, can work.

This rules out a genuine combined method, not merely another flow for an already-PC energy. It also locates the unpaid replacement precisely: a nonpolynomial angular correction with unavoidable origin regularity loss, or a distributed all-angular dual for F*. The completed first-jet test is compatible, and all paid field/rational proposals are undecisive at that larger scope. The current result is a useful scoped failure/change in research decision; it is not a Morrey resolution, a general quartic QC algorithm, or evidence of practical/materials advantage. Historical priority of this combined endpoint/regularity theorem remains UNKNOWN.
