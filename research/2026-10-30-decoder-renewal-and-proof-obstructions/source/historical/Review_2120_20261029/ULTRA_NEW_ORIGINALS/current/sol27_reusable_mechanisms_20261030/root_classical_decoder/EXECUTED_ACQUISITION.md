# Executed ordinary acquisition consumers

30 October 2026. `acquire_parity_decoder.py` executed once with NumPy using
the existing solver runtime. Total script work after imports: 0.1082 seconds.
The input records were generated by a classical simulator, not by a quantum
source. Quantum source calls, quantum gates and physical runs executed: ZERO.
The exact command and full gate-list outputs are reproducible from the script
and `ACQUISITION.json`; no hidden source probability was provided to acquisition.

Two complete consumers take observed flags and labels, output secret bits,
then compile an explicit reversible parity-controlled phase-decoder circuit:

- Native iid noisy examples: signed frequency histogram plus a full exact
  integer Walsh-Hadamard transform over ALL 2^n parities. This strong ordinary
  algorithm pays exponential work/storage and polynomial observed samples.
- Explicit upgraded repeat-environment blocks: majority vote plus binary
  elimination. This pays for every repeated logical call and every environment
  hold; its stronger source access is not presented as a matched iid advantage.

The fixed cases use eta=0.1, failure-budget delta=0.01 and n=8,12,16,20. The
iid consumer used 543,681,820,959 classical simulated examples respectively;
the upgraded consumer used 432,540,648,756 simulated logical calls. Both
recovered the correct secret in every saved case. The n=20 iid transform
scored 1,048,576 parities and took 0.0279 seconds after record generation.
Its primary score array alone has 8,388,608 bytes; temporary arrays and
Python/NumPy/runtime memory are additional. Fast small cases do not establish
polynomial scaling or any cryptographic hardness statement.

A deliberately mismatched control holds the error bit fixed throughout each
environment block. It preserves each call's marginal phase-error rate but
violates the fresh-noise premise. Three saved cases then abort on inconsistent
equations; the n=12 case still learns the correct secret. No general success
rate, failure rate or uniform noise-robustness is inferred from these four
fixtures. This control preserves the distinction between repeatable environment
and repeatable noise: the former plus independent re-noising is the operation
proved useful, whereas repeated identical noisy labels supply no denoising.

No encoded payload or circuit was simulated or executed. Payload entanglement
fidelities written in the JSON are ANALYTIC values from the family theorem:
1-eta for the correct secret and 1/2 for any incorrect secret, since a nonzero
binary parity is balanced under the uniform flag. Successful classical
acquisition and literal gate-list construction do not prove laboratory access,
physical coherence, general Clifford calibration or practical quantum benefit.
