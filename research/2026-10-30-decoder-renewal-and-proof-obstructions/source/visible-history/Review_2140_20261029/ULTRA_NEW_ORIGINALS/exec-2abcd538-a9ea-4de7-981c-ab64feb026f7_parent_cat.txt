## 1. Additional control, specified as an operation

GIVEN factor bound alpha>=1. Hence its centered next-token logits satisfy

The ADDITIONAL service is a calibrated bias of ONE next outcome, followed by
readout after preparation of the SAME original prefix h. Command (h,y,u) gives
an independent categorical observation whose y-versus-rest probability is

    q_(h,y,u)=sigma(r_(h,y)+u+e_(h,y,u)),
    r_(h,y)=log[p_h(y)/(1-p_h(y))], |e_(h,y,u)|<=xi.   (1)

The systematic calibration error may depend on the context, chosen outcome and
command, but is fixed for a repeated-shot batch and has a warranted absolute
bound. Conditional independence of shots is supplied. A predictable bounded
drift version can instead use bounded martingale concentration, but is not
needed here. There is no access to r itself.

For ideal additive logits, (1) follows exactly from changing the raw outcome
weight p_h(y) to exp(u)p_h(y) while leaving all other outcome weights unchanged.
Uniform unknown cross-coupling is NOT free: (1), not a vague 'external field',
is the instrument contract. Bias is applied only to this next readout; a new
original prefix is prepared for every independent shot. No reweighted future
transition law is needed or claimed.

Set L=ceil(alpha^2) and the known integer command range

    R=2L+O+2.

Since ell_y in[-L,L],

    r_y=ell_y-log sum_(z!=y) exp ell_z,
    |r_y|<=2L+log(O-1)<R.                            (2)

Commands stay in[-R,R], with absolute actuator error xi. The RANGE grows as
alpha^2, not log alpha. Its physical actuation cost, settling time, reset cost

from passive shots, the rank promise, or the guarded source's known formula.

## 2. Find a balanced readout with uniformly bounded shots

Fix h,y and define u*=-r_y in[-R,R]. Maintain a bracket [a,b] initially[-R,R].
Let

    J=ceil(log2(8R))+1, delta0=1/64.

At each midpoint u=(a+b)/2 take n0 independent biased shots and estimate q by
the empirical indicator of outcome y. Use enough shots that every one of at
most J estimates differs from its actual mean by at most delta0 except with
probability beta0, for example

    n0>=2048 log(2J/beta0).                           (3)

The sigmoid is1/4-Lipschitz, and xi<=eta/128<=1/128. Thus every reliable estimate
is within at most 9/512 of the ideal sigma(r_y+u). If the estimate is below3/8,
the ideal probability is below1/2 and u*>u; replace a by u. If it is above5/8,
replace b by u. If it lies in[3/8,5/8], return u. In this last case the ideal
probability belongs to

    [183/512,329/512] subset [1/3,2/3].                (4)

All bracket updates therefore preserve the root. If no early return occurs,
after J updates the bracket has width<=1/8. Return its midpoint; then
|r_y+u|<=1/16<log2, which also gives (4)'s weaker [1/3,2/3] conclusion.
There is NO arbitrarily fine noisy sign decision at probability1/2. An
uncertainty band is explicitly accepted as the desired balanced experiment.

J depends logarithmically on the required FIELD RANGE. The entire search
terminates at its predeclared finite shot budget on every transcript; an
unreliable confidence event affects accuracy, not its runtime or validity.

## 3. Acquire the original log probability, not a temperature surrogate

At the balanced command take fresh shots estimating its actual probability
qtilde to delta1=eta/256, with failure probability beta1. It suffices to use

    n1>=32768 eta^-2 log(2/beta1).                    (5)

Both qtilde and the empirical qhat lie in[1/4,3/4] on the good events. The
derivative of logit on this interval is at most16/3<6. Therefore

    |logit(qhat)-u-r_y|<=6 delta1+xi<=eta/32.          (6)

Evaluate logit(qhat) to additional error<=eta/32, using logarithms on the
known positive interval[1/4,3/4]. The resulting dyadic rhat has
|rhat-r_y|<=eta/16. Now use

    log p_h(y)=g(r_y),   g(r)=log sigma(r), |g'|<=1.

Evaluate g(rhat) to additional dyadic error<=eta/16. The returned raw value
v_h(y) therefore differs from log p_h(y) by at most eta/8. Doing this for every
outcome and mean-centering gives

    ||Pi_1 v_h-ell_h||_infinity<=eta/4<eta.           (7)

This is the original source's approximate logit oracle, not an oracle for an
arbitrarily clipped, heated or reweighted target law. The nonlinearity of
log-probability centering is paid by the exact identity for g and its uniform
Lipschitz bound, not by a probability-rank assumption.

Large |rhat| is NOT a hidden scalar exponential bill. The stable expression

    g(r)=min(0,r)-log(1+exp(-|r|))                    (8)

allows one to discard the second term once |r| exceeds a known
O(log(1/eta)) threshold, with the allocated absolute error. Otherwise its
argument is bounded by that threshold. Rational Taylor/range-reduction
evaluation needs bits polynomial in log R+log(1/eta) and the input lengths;
it never needs relative resolution of an exp(-alpha^2) probability.
Each bisection command is dyadic with O(log R+log J) ordinary bits, while
the physical absolute calibration xi remains the separately supplied resource.
For literal validity on EVERY transcript, clamp the final empirical qhat to
[1/4,3/4] before these scalar calculations. This does not change it on the
good event, keeps all logarithms finite on bad events, and is estimator
completion rather than a clipped target-law or rank assumption. Compilation

## 5. The additional measurement cannot be emulated cheaply by native shots

The following exact two-point bound, independently supplied by root, prices
the control distinction. Let L>=2 be a known integer, a=2^L and

    p0=1/(1+a), p1=2p0, u=L log2.

A y-outcome odds bias of u transforms these probabilities to

    q(p0)=1/2, q(p1)=2a/(3a-1)>2/3.

Any native-coin-only algorithm whose output law is within TV1/48 of the desired
biased coin on BOTH inputs must have output separation>1/8. The input coins
differ by p0 per observation. A stopped adaptive maximal coupling gives

    TV(output_p0,output_p1)<=p0 E_p0[number of draws],

so it needs at least1/(8p0)=Omega(2^L) expected native draws. Additional shared
fair bits do not alter this bound. This is established two-point coupling
machinery, compatible with the earlier logit-pooling barrier, not a new generic
Bernoulli-factory theorem.

Consequently polynomial command amplitude and polynomial calibration bit length
do NOT imply polynomial implementation from ordinary native samples. Service(1)
must actually be an acquired actuator/readout operation. A known physical energy
or electronic threshold change can qualify it only if the original-prefix
reset, exact odds law and uncertainty bounds coexist in the same regime.
