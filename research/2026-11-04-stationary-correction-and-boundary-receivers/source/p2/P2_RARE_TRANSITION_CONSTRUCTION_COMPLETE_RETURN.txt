## 2. A coarse energy witness can be corrected using actual trajectories

This is the main proved reduction.

\[

Use unnormalized reversible weights and conductances

\[
w_x=e^{-\beta H_h(x)},\qquad
\kappa_{xy}=w_xc(x,y)=w_yc(y,x).
\]

Orient each configuration-space edge \(e=(x,y)\) toward adding one plus spin. For any trial function \(f\) with \(f|_A=0\), \(f|_B=1\), define

\[
W=\mathcal E(f)
=\sum_e\kappa_e(\Delta f_e)^2,
\qquad
\mathcal C=\operatorname{cap}(A,B)=\mathcal E(q).
\]

Suppose we can acquire the distribution

\[
\nu_f(e)=\frac{\kappa_e(\Delta f_e)^2}{W}.
\]

From the ordered endpoints \(x\le y\), run the **actual heat-bath chains**, using common update sites and uniforms. Record their respective indicators \(I_x,I_y\) of reaching \(B\) before \(A\). Attractiveness gives

\[
I_x\le I_y,\qquad
Z_e:=I_y-I_x\in\{0,1\},
\qquad
\mathbb E Z_e=\Delta q_e.
\]

If the two configurations coalesce before either absorbing outcome has been fixed, the experiment stops with \(Z_e=0\). Otherwise the absorbing labels must be determined correctly. All internal shapes, failed growth, and recrossings remain in these trajectories.

The estimator

\[
Y=\frac{WZ_e}{\Delta f_e}
\]

satisfies

\[
\boxed{\mathbb EY=\mathcal C,\qquad
\mathbb EY^2\le nW\mathcal C.}
\]

### Why the second-moment bound holds

Harmonicity gives

\[
\mathbb EY=\mathcal E(f,q)=\mathcal C.
\]

Because \(Z_e\) is Boolean,

\[
\mathbb EY^2
=W\sum_{\Delta f_e\ne0}\kappa_e\Delta q_e.
\]

The current \(j_e=\kappa_e\Delta q_e\) is nonnegative on increasing edges. With \(r(x)=N_+(x)\), every such edge has \(\Delta r=1\), so

\[
\sum_e j_e=\mathcal E(r,q)\le n\mathcal C.
\]

The last inequality follows because the source and sink boundary currents each have total mass \(\mathcal C\), while \(r\in[0,n]\). Equivalently, the **net electrical current** decomposes into increasing paths containing at most \(n\) flips. Physical trajectories can backtrack extensively.

Consequently, a certified bound

\[
W/\mathcal C\le P
\]

gives relative sample complexity

\[
O\!\left(nP\varepsilon^{-2}\log\delta^{-1}\right).
\]

It also earns an aggregate channel-coverage bound:

\[
\mathbb P_{\nu_f}(Z_e=1)
\ge \frac{\mathcal C}{nW}
\ge \frac1{nP}.
\]

Thus a polynomial energy ratio prevents all successful channels together from being exponentially invisible under this proposal. A pointwise approximation to the committor is unnecessary.

**What this has not earned:** the polynomial energy ratio, normalization \(W\), acquisition of \(\nu_f\), or physical running time. Those cannot be hidden inside the statement of the estimator.

## 3. The hardest operation now has a local dynamical certificate

\frac{\sum_{x\in D_*}w_xV(x)}{W}.

\tag{2}

\[

\sum_e\kappa_e(\Delta f_e)^2[v(x_e)+v(y_e)]

&\le\sum_{x\in D_*}w_xv(x)(-\mathcal L_hV)(x)\\
&=\sum_{x\in D_*}w_xV(x).
