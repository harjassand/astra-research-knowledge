## 2. The central construction

The useful change is to condition **the controller’s exogenous random seed**, rather than try to reconstruct or condition the hidden physical state.

Fix a causal program \(P\) that observes the sensor history and consumes at most \(H\) binary random bits. Its only tunable parameter is the coin probability \(\theta\). For deployment, draw

\[
B=(B_1,\ldots,B_H),\qquad
B_i\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta),
\]

and let the program read those bits as needed.

The entire seed can be committed before the experiment. **Unused bits remain part of the seed after success, stopping, or budget exhaustion.**

For each fixed seed \(b\), the program interacting with the apparatus defines an unknown conditional law

\[
K(dz\mid b)
\]

for the complete measured record \(z\). This law includes hidden molecular states, actuator lag, detector filtering, electronic noise, bath memory, and the causal feedback interaction.

The learner is never given \(K\). It is only a mathematical description of what the experiment does.

Let \(R=g(z)\in[0,1]\) be the prescribed measured outcome. Grouping seeds by their number of ones gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
p(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_\theta R
=
\sum_{n=0}^{H}
\binom Hn\theta^n(1-\theta)^{H-n}q_n .
}
\]

Here

\[
q_n
=
\mathbb E\!\left[
R
\;\middle|\;
\text{a uniformly chosen \(H\)-bit seed containing exactly \(n\) ones}
\right].
\]

**Each \(q_n\) is the mean of an executable experiment.** Choose a uniform \(n\)-subset of the \(H\) bit positions, supply that seed to the same controller, and measure the outcome.

### Why feedback does not break the identity

The commands can depend on earlier noisy measurements. That dependence is contained in \(K(\cdot\mid b)\). The distribution of a seed under the target controller is

\[
\Pr_\theta(B=b)
=
\theta^{|b|}(1-\theta)^{H-|b|},
\]

so all seeds with the same count have the same weight. Their conditional distribution is uniform and independent of \(\theta\).

This argument would generally fail if we stratified by **actual force changes**, successful pulses, or coins used before an event. Those quantities depend on the physical response. The acquisition stratum is the count in the **entire planned seed**.

There is another necessary restriction: the program must not use \(\theta\) anywhere except in generating its bits. If changing \(\theta\) also changes a threshold, actuator gain, stopping rule, or preparation, the common channel argument no longer applies.

### What is learned

For a binary outcome, the learned object contains \(H+1\) unknown numbers. For a recorded completion-time distribution, it contains one such vector for each requested deadline.

It does not contain:

- A hidden-state transition matrix.
- An assumed Markov representation of the observed coordinate.
- Thermal increments or force derivatives.
- Complete probability laws supplied before learning.
- A reconstruction of every hidden degree of freedom.

A useful consequence is that **\(H\) counts the program’s random decisions, not its sensor samples or hidden relaxation times**. A controller may monitor a long record but use only a few coins at detected trigger events. Its response as a function of the common coin probability still has degree at most \(H\). The long record’s time and sensing costs remain.

## 3. Finite acquisition and conditioning

Then

\widehat p(\theta)

\sum_{n=0}^{H}b_n(\theta)\widehat q_n,
\qquad
b_n(\theta)=\binom Hn\theta^n(1-\theta)^{H-n}.
\]

The estimate is automatically a valid probability. For binary outcomes, it is also exactly the **saturated joint Bernoulli likelihood estimator**.

That equivalence matters. An equally informed likelihood method using this representation obtains the same estimator. The construction earns a finite experimental object; it does not claim to defeat likelihood fitting through a different name.

2\exp\!\left[-\frac{2r^2}{V(\theta)}\right],

\[

\sum_{n=0}^{H}\frac{b_n(\theta)^2}{m_n}.
