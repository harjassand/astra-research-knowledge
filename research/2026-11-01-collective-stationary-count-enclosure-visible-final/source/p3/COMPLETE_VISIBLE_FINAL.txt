The result

I obtained a constructive way to enclose every probability in an interacting system’s stationary occupation-count distribution, with relative error bounds that remain useful when the probabilities are extraordinarily small. The construction starts from local birth, death and exchange rules. It does not require the stationary state, a learned predictor, a phase assignment or a rare-event sampling bias.

The decisive operation is a combination of two steps:

Acquire spatial correction functions from the physical generator, retaining the pair and higher-order interactions that a product approximation discards.
Use stationary flow to cancel the unknown probability of each count sector before estimating its conditional reaction rates.

For a 1,000-site cooperative system, the resulting executable calculation encloses all 1,001 count probabilities with relative interval width below 0.102%. It certifies a change in which of two separated population regions holds the majority of stationary probability under a small intervention, and it resolves tail probabilities near

The substantial physical condition is fast conservative exchange. The large calculation uses complete-graph exchange at a very high supplied rate. I also constructed the representation for arbitrary connected exchange graphs, including an exact implementation with inhibitory birth rules and interacting death rates. Its construction cost and required exchange strength depend on the graph. Useful prediction through spatial nucleation at fixed local diffusion rates remains the decisive unresolved obstruction.

1. The scientific question that selected the construction

The question is whether a cooperative population spends its stationary life near extinction or in a densely occupied state, and how a change in its cooperative reaction rate changes that balance.

This question requires collective correlations. A vacant site can receive the same spontaneous activation rate in two configurations with the same total population, while receiving very different cooperative activation rates because its neighbours differ. Counting occupied sites therefore loses information needed to predict the next reaction.

10. The boundary of the result

The completed capability is a finite-input method for acquiring and certifying collective stationary predictions, including all count probabilities, nonnegative count observables, separated population regions and intervention-induced majority changes. Passage-time predictions have their own independently checked generator construction.

The method succeeds because the conservative dynamics provide an inverse that can be acquired at the degree generated by the local physical rules, and because stationary sector flow removes the rare sector mass before error estimation.

The unresolved operation is equally concrete: an affordable representation of the relevant conditional spatial dynamics at fixed local transport, with bounds that remain useful when interfaces, droplets and nucleation control the outcome. The present graph inverse exposes that dependence but does not remove it. A thermodynamic phase description, microscopic conditional geometry and experimentally learned rate uncertainty have not been acquired.

The archive preserves the correlation representation, signed estimator, response construction, positive challengers, failed variants and exact audits. The held P3 materials were not transferred. The remaining scientific obstacle is to retain the geometry that controls local collective transitions without paying for the full configuration space or supplying that geometry as an oracle.
