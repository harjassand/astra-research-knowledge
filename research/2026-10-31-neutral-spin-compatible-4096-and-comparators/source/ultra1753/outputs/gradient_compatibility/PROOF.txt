2. DIFFERENTIAL TRANSVERSALITY AND STRICT LEADING QC

Lemma. If a smooth compatible matrix field A=M+Dphi on the connected
torus satisfies S_F(A,D A)=0 everywhere, then A is constant.

Proof. Suppose D A is nonzero somewhere. There is an open set on which
it remains nonzero. Some point in that set has A!=0, since a field
identically zero on an open set has zero derivative there. Shrink to
a connected neighborhood avoiding zero. By the zero-set description,
A lies in E1 or E2 throughout this neighborhood; continuity and the
opposite determinant signs fix which one. Its derivatives therefore
lie in that same linear plane. But (2) says its sole potentially
nonzero derivative is rank one. Identity (1) forces that derivative
to vanish, a contradiction. Thus D A=0 everywhere. QED.

The compatibility D A=(B1,B2) in this argument is essential. Merely
choosing independent A and h from the zero set does not imply rigidity.
Nor does this argument replace the gradient condition by det moments.

Now let G=Dphi be any nonzero mean-zero periodic L4 gradient and let
G_t be its heat evolution. At positive times it is smooth, compatible,
and nonconstant: Fourier multiplication by exp(-|k|^2 t) is injective
and its zero Fourier mode remains zero. The preceding lemma and the
nonnegative continuous heat density give

            <S_F(M+G_t,D G_t)> > 0,  t>0.             (3)

The retained exact form is d alpha, so its pullback integrates to zero
on the torus. The heat energy identity is therefore

 -d/dt <F(M+G_t)> = <S_F(M+G_t,D G_t)>.

The heat flow tends to zero as t tends to infinity. At t down to zero
it converges strongly in L4, so quartic energies converge. Integrating,
or simply using any positive-time interval in (3), proves

        <F(M+G)>-F(M)>0                               (4)

for EVERY finite M and EVERY nonzero actual G in this class. This also
handles nonsmooth L4 fields; a pointwise zero-set argument at time zero
was not needed.

Put H=<F''(G)>, v=<DF(G)>, f=<F(G)>. The exact quartic expansion is

        <F(M+G)>-F(M)=f+v.M+(1/2) M^T H M.            (5)

Nonnegativity for all M implies H>=0 and v in range H. Hence this
finite-dimensional quadratic has an attained minimum, even if H is
singular. Combining (4) and (5) gives the strict moment constraint

             a:=2f-v^T H^+ v >0,   G!=0.             (6)

No uniform quartic modulus has been acquired by this proof. Strict
positivity on each field does not give a positive infimum over all
oscillating or concentrating fields. For a strongly L4-compact family
with uniformly positive covariance, H stays positive definite and a
is continuous, so an infimum is positive if the family excludes zero.
This limited compactness observation is not a compactness theorem for
all gradients or a solution of the lower-face QC question.
