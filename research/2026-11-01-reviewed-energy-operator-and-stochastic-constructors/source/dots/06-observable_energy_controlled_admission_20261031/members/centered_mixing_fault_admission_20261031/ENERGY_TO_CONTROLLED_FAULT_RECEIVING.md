  ||(exp(t G^*) tensor id)(X)|| <= h(t)||X||,

This is a COMPLETE operator-norm statement on the ACTUAL generator, not scalar autocorrelation decay, entrywise contraction, a state-specific moment bound, or an assumed dilation. The source-only semigroup commutes with all source-blind data CP maps. Data maps preserve source coefficients in M and are unital-adjoint contractions. ## Essential locality and cost boundary

The source-decay estimate applies independently to each actual active patch's generator. A single shared source or cross-patch hopping cannot be split into independent generators merely because a global return estimate exists. A global source block attached to the entire computation does not establish bounded local fault support. The older occupation/clamped constructions have their own transport premises and must be retained separately.

Complete-return acquisition may itself be expensive, particularly for a large unknown block. The dark-source operator inequalities are one constructive sufficient route; exact observable eigenoperator checks can be much cheaper and can apply to nonprimitive sources that never fully return. Neither route makes unknown experimental operator norms or Markov validity free.

The resulting local-amplitude premise can feed an existing, fully specified fault-tolerance consumer when ALL its locations and other premises are covered. This is a scoped enabling implication, not proof that a useful hardware regime, a better source-control service, or historical novelty has been achieved.
