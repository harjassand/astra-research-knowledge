## 1. Deterministic uniform-word theorem

With uniform H=I/n, P=ZW is both stochastic and H-orthogonal. A true stochastic A preserves the sup-norm bound M on the full column trajectory even when it is not L2-contractive and does not share any stationary law with another action. Therefore both ||Px||_H and ||Qx||_H are at most M. Decomposing QAx yields t_(j+1)<=min(1,alpha*t_j+e_in), starting at zero because b=Pb.

The retained trajectory is Wx. Its discrepancy from a stochastic B replay is propagated in sup norm by B, with one-step terms dM and e_out||Qx||_H. The endpoint ell^TQ=0 and ||ell^TZ||_1<=||ell||_1 give exactly (M1). This argument is valid for every action word on the same operator event. It is not a state-only comparison, nor does it require the acquired coarse matrix C itself to be stochastic.

The implemented task-compatible partition is proposed from observed edges and public task labels, then checked. The public task does reveal its source and target regions; it does not supply the complete hidden partition. The threshold heuristic has no universal success guarantee. Its float-valued comparison can choose a poor partition without invalidating soundness, because subsequent certificates must still pass. No efficient complete search is asserted.

The fixed b, ell and maximum horizon are essential. Adaptive future selection among words using previous predictions is covered by the uniform event. Adaptive feedback into a realized Markov trajectory is a different task. For a microscopic initial state not preserved by P, the displayed certificate cannot simply be reused; the source explicitly charges or excludes that boundary.

## 3. Fresh finite-bit complement certificate

The integer probe argument is valid for a fixed leading eigenvector of F^TF. Conditional on all but a coordinate of magnitude at least 1/sqrt(n), the small-inner-product interval contains at most two allowed integers. Failure probability is at most 2/J. On its complement, ||F||^(2p)<=2sqrt(n)||(F^TF)^p g||.

The implementation normalizes C by a rational beta>=sqrt(||C||_1||C||_infinity). Q, C/beta and C^T/beta are all Euclidean contractions. Five rounded operations per power step accumulate at most 5p sqrt(n) times a half-grid unit. The final upward norm and rational power comparison implement (M7). The deterministic beta fallback is safe. It is not a deterministic certificate merely because the final comparison is rational: probe randomness is load-bearing.

A fixed mock seed is not an adversarial-source guarantee of a fresh random probe. The source correctly labels that distinction and retains an explicitly orthogonal-probe falsification. A genuine implementation of the statistical theorem must supply independent uniform probe randomness. The fixed 24-bit final bound and chosen grid can cause conservative refusal or lose relative tightness for extremely tiny norms; this is not an unsound PASS or a promise of universal finite-budget completeness.

Sparse count arithmetic uses actual count denominators and common integer accumulators. No dense n-by-n PSD decomposition, stationary distribution or hidden adjoint sampling oracle is called. ## 4. Quadratic statistics and confidence inversion

The reusable scalar inversion follows from v-S<=sqrt((av+b)/q), replacing S by max(S,0) and solving the resulting quadratic. Upward square roots preserve coverage. Odd independent-batch medians have the stated binomial-tail failure bound. Negative observed U-statistics are not silently interpreted as a negative true norm.

For outgoing pooled samples, X=Qe_J, mu=E X and Sigma=Cov(X). The centered U-statistic expansion gives exactly

    Var(S)=4n² mu^T Sigma mu/N + 2n² tr(Sigma²)/[N(N-1)].

Since Sigma<=Q diag(p)Q, its norm is at most p_max and trace at most 1. With v=n||mu||² this proves Q4. For the acquired block g, p_j<=B/|g|, hence kappa=B/pi_g. The collision count formula correctly subtracts diagonals and block-mean terms and needs no N² loop.

For incoming validation, two independent per-row halves give S=<QX,QY>/n. The linear terms have variance at most 2sigma*v/(nN). They are uncorrelated with the bilinear term because the other half has mean zero. The bilinear variance is bounded by sigma*T/(n²N²). Different row means and unequal block sizes do not invalidate the argument. The weighted count formula avoids storing square-root coordinates and matches the Frobenius quantity in Q5, which upper-bounds the induced incoming norm.

The covariance pilot is independent of subsequent U-statistic batches. Its simultaneous lower mass bounds may select the maximizing label on that same event. Weighted categorical covariance has trace at most (1-sum p_h²)/pi_min<=min(1,2(1-max p_h))/pi_min; norm is no larger than trace. Thus the acquired sigma,T are valid conditional envelopes. Bad block mass or a bad pilot remains expensive rather than becoming a hidden favorable promise.

The displayed small-leak completeness constants and sufficient N formulas check out. This is completeness under scaled L2 leakage v<=theta²/16, not a generic tolerant-TV promise. Bounded point masses and small TV alone do not imply that stronger quadratic condition. The square-root-n expressions also retain the at-least-one-sample-per-named-row floor, coarse spectral pass, block-mass and gap factors, and confidence costs.
