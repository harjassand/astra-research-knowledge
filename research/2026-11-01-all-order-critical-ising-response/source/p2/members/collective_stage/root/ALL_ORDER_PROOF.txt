1. Actual microscopic input and physical response

On the periodic n-site chain, n>=4, sigma_i is +/-1 and

  pi(sigma) proportional to exp[J sum_i sigma_i sigma_(i+1)],
  gamma=tanh(2J), t=tanh J,
  w_i(sigma)=(1-b sigma_(i-1)sigma_(i+1))/2
             *[1-gamma sigma_i(sigma_(i-1)+sigma_(i+1))/2].

Here 0<b<1/2, with gamma(1-b)>b sufficient for the uniform bounds below.
The prefactor is independent of sigma_i, so detailed balance is unchanged.
Every site's kinetics is modified. Its contrast is (1+b)/(1-b), fixed as n
grows. The zero-field symmetry and this particular neighbor dependence are
essential to the positive representation proved here.

Let K=-L be the positive generator, M=sum_i sigma_i, and

  chi=<M,K^+ M>_pi=integral_0^infinity Cov_pi(M(0),M(s)) ds.

This is the full target response, not a finite interaction-order coefficient.
The computed critical family is

  b=1/3, exp(2J)=n,
  t=(n-1)/(n+1), gamma=(n^2-1)/(n^2+1).

Its correlation length is order n; Var(M) is order n^2. The Glauber reference
has KM=(1-gamma)M. Form comparison by 1-b and 1+b proves chi/Var(M) is order
n^2 in the target, so M overlaps the collective slow dynamics. The small
spectral scale is not imposed through heterogeneous physical clocks.

All executable inputs for this family are rational. The earlier model with
kinetic multipliers A_same=1,A_opposite=2 is exactly 3/2 times this generator.
Its response is 2/3 of the response in this note. Comparisons must rescale time.

2. Exact positive representation of the target, at every order

For an odd subset S, phi_S=product_(i in S) sigma_i. Direct multiplication gives

  K phi_S=|S| phi_S
    -a sum_(i in S, +/-) phi_(S xor {i,i+/-1})
    -b sum_(i in S) phi_(S xor {i-1,i+1}),
  a=gamma(1-b)/2, kappa=(1-b)(1-gamma).

Thus the dual is a real positive branching-annihilating process: each particle
hops in either direction at rate a and toggles both neighbors at rate b;
overlaps annihilate. The residual killing rate is kappa |S|. Every odd subset
is allowed. The local packed triple is the exact state reached when a singleton
branches, not a cutoff in the number of particles or in perturbation order.

Write Kint for the bare internal process killed at first return to a singleton,
H=Kint+kappa N on nonsingletons, N=|S|. The diagonal of H is exactly N.
Let S0={0,1,2}. Define the acquired static reward

  alpha(S)=Cov_pi(M,phi_S)/Cov_pi(M,sigma_0).

Translation invariance identifies all singleton rewards. With a bare excursion
return time tau, Z_s=integral_0^s N_v dv, define

  U=E_S0 integral_0^tau exp(-kappa Z_s) N_s ds=[H^-1 N](S0),
  R=E_S0 integral_0^tau exp(-kappa Z_s) alpha(S_s) ds=[H^-1 alpha](S0).

The expectation of exp(-kappa Z_tau) is 1-kappa U. At a singleton, hopping
only translates the monomial and contributes no change to this uniform
observable. Integrating its branch/killing holding time exactly gives

  chi/Var(M)=(1+b R)/[kappa(1+b U)].                    (1)

In particular, this formula does not require observing the rare killing or
estimating 1 minus a probability near one. It also does not by itself certify
a useful variance: the raw excursion's second moment is a separate problem.

3. Static reward is acquired, including its precision

For any even spin set with ordered positions y_1,...,y_(2m), let
A=sum_j (y_(2j)-y_(2j-1)). The exact ring Ising moment is

  E product sigma_y=(t^A+t^(n-A))/(1+t^n).

Hence alpha(S) is available from microscopic equilibrium weights. It need not
be acquired by a physical measurement or supplied as a response coefficient.
There is a positive O(N)-term expression, avoiding an n-site sum. Sort S,
let g_j be its cyclic gaps, set A_0=sum_(j odd) g_j, and
A_(j+1)=A_j+(-1)^j g_j, indexing j from zero. Put

  l_j=min(A_j,A_j+(-1)^j(g_j-1)),
  u_j=max(A_j,A_j+(-1)^j(g_j-1)).

Then

  alpha(S)=sum_j [t^l_j+t^(n-u_j)](1-t^g_j)
             /[(1+t)(1-t^n)].                         (2)

All terms are positive. Repeated squaring and directed dyadic multiplication
enclose every power and the final division. The delivered alpha_interval
implements this with integers; no floating-point probability is used.

There is also a useful global bound. Let D be the pure rate-1/2 annihilating
hop operator and h the cubic in section4. Reference duality gives

  gamma D alpha+(1-gamma)(N-1)alpha=0,
  alpha(singleton)=1.

Feynman--Kac until pure-hop return, and Dh=(N-1)/2, therefore imply

  0<alpha<=1,
  1-alpha <= 2(1-gamma)h/gamma.                        (3)

For the critical family, (1+t)(1-t^n) is bounded below by an absolute positive
constant. A conservative absolute width for (2) is O(n^2 2^-p), so choosing
p=O(log n+log(1/delta)) is sufficient. Our reported calculations use p=100.
Terminal score enclosure widths are retained, and confidence is applied to
their actual dyadic endpoint variables, not to an assumed exact midpoint.

4. A positive output bound is not yet a work bound

For cyclic gaps g_0,...,g_(N-1), define F_m as the sum of products of m distinct
gaps whose successive cyclic index spacings are all odd, for odd m. Set

  h=F_3/n, h(singleton)=0, h0=h(S0)=(n-2)/n.

Collision compatibility is exact: when a gap vanishes, its two neighboring
gaps merge and the same polynomial becomes the one on N-2 particles.
The two relevant generator identities are

  Dh=(N-1)/2,
  B h=sum_i[h(branch_i S)-h(S)]=1-2N/n.

Put d=gamma(1-b), A=d/2+2b/n, C=d/2+b and
epsilon=(d-b)/3+2b/n. Then

  Kint h=A N-C >=epsilon N on N>=3.                   (4)

Thus E integral N ds <=h0/epsilon for a bare excursion. That finite first
moment does not ensure bounded reward variance. Even three pure annihilating
walkers have an infinite-line return-time moment threshold 3/2; a second
moment cannot be imported from positive recurrence. The original direct
regeneration route therefore stopped here until the next construction.

5. The terminal sampler and the separate cost potential

For any finite transient positive operator H with Hh>0, use transformed rates

  q^h_xy=q_xy h(y)/h(x),
  death rate=(Hh)(x)/h(x).

Their total rate is the original diagonal H_xx. The terminal distribution is

  P_x^h(Z=y)=H^-1(x,y)(Hh)(y)/h(x).

Consequently

  [H^-1 f](x)=h(x) E_x^h[f(Z)/(Hh)(Z)].               (5)

For our acquired rewards f=N and f=alpha, the terminal scores are

  U*=h0 N/(Hh),  R*=h0 alpha/(Hh).

Equation(4) gives 0<=U*<=h0/epsilon and 0<=R*<=h0/(3epsilon).
These are genuine uniform variance bounds. They can transfer the old heavy
tail into the transformed path length, so a distinct work proof is necessary.

The expected transformed event count is

  E_h T=[H^-1(Nh)](S0)/h0.                            (6)

We acquire a nonnegative explicit Wcost with H Wcost>=Nh. It is not an assumed
solution or a numerical inverse. The ingredients are

  Q=F_5/n,
  Ucost(S)=sum_(i<j) q_i q_j V(x_j-x_i), q_i=(-1)^(i+1),
  V(x)=x^5/(60n)-x^4/24+(n^2-1)x^3/(36n)+x^2/24,
  u0=(n-2)(n-1)/(6n).

The exact all-sector identities and inequality are

  D Ucost=h,       B Ucost=N u0-2h,
  DQ=(N-3)h/2,     BQ<=3h.                            (7)

Ucost is nonnegative because its explicit formula solves the finite pure-hop
Dirichlet problem DUcost=h, with zero singleton boundary. Q is nonnegative
term by term. No Poisson inverse is required to evaluate either formula.

The ordinary proof of the branching inequality is important. The identity

  tr product_j [[z g_j,1],[1,0]]=sum_(m odd) z^m F_m(g)

turns a branch into replacing adjacent gaps [a,b] by [a-1,1,1,b-1]. The exact
matrix difference is

  [[-z^2+z^4(a-1)(b-1), z^3(a-1)],
   [z^3(b-1), z^2]].

The z^5 coefficient, summed around the cycle, yields

  BF_5=3F_3-2 sum_(i<j)c_(j-i)g_i g_j+(N-1)n/2,
  c_l=l/2 if l even, (N-l)/2 if l odd.

All c_l>=1 and sum_(i<j)g_i g_j>=(N-1)n/2 for integer gaps>=1, proving BQ<=3h.
The full coefficient count, including zero-gap interfaces, is in the cited
companion proof. This is not an inference from a finite-order experiment.

Now the positive combination

  Wcost=(3Ucost+2Q)/d +[3b u0/(d epsilon)]h             (8)

obeys Kint Wcost>=Nh pointwise. Since the additional killing is nonnegative,
H Wcost>=Nh as well. At S0, Q=0, Ucost=u0, giving

  E_h T <= 3u0/(d h0)*(1+b h0/epsilon).               (9)

At fixed b<1/2 along the critical family this is O(n); at b=1/3 it tends to
3n. All particle counts and all branching histories are included. The work
bound prices the transformed chain whose score variance is bounded.

This is the reusable bridge: an output potential h and an independently
acquired cost potential Wcost. General Doob transforms and Lyapunov bounds
are established tools; the all-order physical branching identities in (7)
and their constructive combination are the substantive acquired interface.

6. Stable paired observable and complete confidence

The physical response can be estimated from U*,R* using (1). A useful exact
centering improves its numerical behavior. Let

  theta=kappa chi/Var(M), theta0=A/C,
  Z*=R*-theta0 U*+h0/C.

Since 1-theta0-b h0/C=0,

  theta=theta0+b E Z*/(1+b E U*).                     (10)

Equivalently,

  Z*=h0[alpha-1+kappa N h/C]/(Kint h+kappa N h).

Under d>b, equation(3) gives the lower bound Z*>=0: the sufficient inequality
gamma(1-b)N>=2C holds already at N=3. The upper bound is Z*<=h0/C.
Thus clipping its dyadic enclosure to these proved bounds is mathematically
licensed, not a numerical assumption.

The certificate uses four separate bounded random variables: the dyadic lower
and upper endpoints for U*, and the lower and upper endpoints for Z*. For
each, the relevant one-sided empirical Bernstein bound receives failure
budget eta/4. The four events jointly hold with probability at least1-eta.
The logarithm upper bound is rational, and square roots are rounded upward by
integer square roots. The sample sums and squares are exact integers.
Ratio monotonicity propagates [U_l,U_u],[Z_l,Z_u] to

  theta0+b Z_l/(1+b U_u) <=theta<=theta0+b Z_u/(1+b U_l).

Authoritative endpoints are the stored rational pairs. Printed decimals are
approximations unless rounded outward explicitly. Fixed sample counts avoid
optional-stopping misuse. An adaptive version needs a summable failure budget.

For a priori complexity, ordinary Hoeffding bounds suffice: all score ranges
are O_b(1), theta>=(1-b)/(1+b)>0, and the ratio has bounded derivatives.
O_b(delta^-2 log(1/eta)) independent samples give relative error delta.
An explicit sufficient statistical budget is obtained by writing
u_max=h0/epsilon, z_max=h0/C, theta_min=(1-b)/(1+b), and
B=b z_max(1+b u_max). With exact scores, any

  m>=B^2 log(4/eta)/(2 delta^2 theta_min^2)

suffices for the point estimator from two sample means, by Hoeffding and
the displayed ratio derivative bound. Allocate half the error to arithmetic
and replace delta by delta/2 in that formula when using rounded scores.
For the implemented b=1/3 family, p large enough that
1024 n^2 2^-p<=delta theta_min is a conservative arithmetic choice.
The empirical Bernstein implementation exploits small observed variance while
retaining a finite-sample guarantee. A PRNG reproduces the numerical runs;
the probability theorem, as usual, assumes independent uniform random bits.

7. The event backend is implemented, not delegated to a primitive

A transformed transition must not enumerate every particle candidate and
recompute a cubic sum. There is an explicit dynamic representation. For an
ordered set with alternating local charges, store signed moments

  P_j=sum_i q_i x_i^j, j=0,1,2,3,
  E_j=sum_(i<k)q_i q_k(x_k-x_i)^j, j=2,3.

Concatenating ordered segments X,Y with s=(-1)^|X| gives

  P_j(XY)=P_j(X)+s P_j(Y),
  E_j(XY)=E_j(X)+E_j(Y)
           +s sum_(r=0)^j (-1)^r binom(j,r)P_r(X)P_(j-r)(Y).

The potential is exactly

  n h=(3n E_2-2E_3)/6.                                (11)

An AVL tree maintains these constant-sized aggregates, height, count and rank.
Each insertion, deletion, rollback and uniform-particle selection costs O(log n)
integer operations. All integers in the potential tree have O(log n) bits.

The h-ratio after a hop is at most2. After a branch it is at most4 for n>=4.
This follows from positive gap polynomials, collision continuity and the
individual branch identity; the complete inequalities are in the companion
proof. Use a proposal envelope with weight1 for death and weight4(1-kappa)
for ordinary dual moves. Accept death with probability (Hh)/(Nh); accept a
proposed move with h(next)/(4h). These are rational exact comparisons. The
overall acceptance probability is exactly1/(5-4kappa), independent of state.

all_order_sampler.cpp implements this algorithm with integer bit-rejection
sampling. The declared n<=65536 bound keeps all intermediates within signed
128 bits; a general implementation substitutes multiprecision integers.
No continuous exponential holding times are sampled. The Python version is
a slower transparent reference. The C++ stream protocol and stream_response.py
retain only the current state, four sums/squares and a fixed512-entry cache;
the complete terminal histogram is optional reproducibility data.

8. Full cost and exact scope

Combining (9), constant rejection and the AVL backend gives, at fixed b away
from1/2 and gamma(1-b)-b bounded below,

  expected transition arithmetic
      O_b(n log n delta^-2 log(1/eta)).

The integer bit-operation cost multiplies this by the cost of O(log n)-bit
arithmetic. Terminal static scoring uses O(N log n) operations at
p=O(log n+log(1/delta)) bits, N<=n, once per sample; its worst-case bound fits
O_b(n log n M(p) delta^-2 log(1/eta)), where M(p) bounds arithmetic at p bits.
This is polynomial in requested accuracy; it has no n^order enumeration.

Working state space is O(n) words in the worst case, and only active dual
particles are stored. The theorem does not guarantee a uniformly small dual
population. It avoids the 2^n probability vector and a trajectory of all physical
spin flips. Individual paths have unbounded duration; the time bound is an
expectation. A hard abort budget can be charged separately with Markov's
inequality and its failure probability. No exponential runtime tail is claimed.

The critical collective relaxation time is order n^2. The work bound is order
n per bounded-score sample. At low temperature and fixed n the large physical
waiting time appears only through the input precision; the rarity issue itself
was already resolved by earlier conventional estimators and is not the new
claim here. The new bridge is extensive all-order nonlinear response.

This does not extend automatically to arbitrary kinetic multipliers, fields,
frustration, higher dimensions, conserved systems or arbitrary observables.
Positive duality, singleton closure of the uniform magnetization, and the
explicit polynomial drift identities are all acquired premises of this class.
The prior controlled-reference result remains useful on its broader promised
class and is preserved unchanged.

9. Strong conventional reconciliation

The explicit reference potential and feasible reference current already give

  chi0/E_mu A <=chi<=chi0 E_mu(1/A).

For the present chain they obtain the leading critical response, with O(1/n)
relative interval width, in essentially constant-size arithmetic. The acquired
three-defect reference scalar further supplies standard Gauss--Radau bounds.
At n128 that deterministic construction is much faster than the stochastic
algorithm for near4e-4 interval width. No contrary speed claim is made.

The all-order algorithm provides an accuracy parameter without a fixed moment
floor. The experiments compare the same chi after the required3/2 time
rescaling. This is not a proof of superiority over every possible conventional
algorithm. In fact the new Wcost also gives a second-moment bound for ordinary
regenerative excursions, so another estimator can reuse the same acquired
bridge. The scientific advance should be attributed to that bridge, not to
exclusive ownership of Doob importance sampling.

work, and leaves its continuous tilted-resolvent oracle unacquired. Its theorem
cannot supply our local transition law or polynomial potentials. We read that
governing source and its adverse comparison at commit
db0969a8586afaa38137a4371b3457c5576fadea. Here the missing discrete transition,
static reward, variance and work operations have all been explicitly acquired.
