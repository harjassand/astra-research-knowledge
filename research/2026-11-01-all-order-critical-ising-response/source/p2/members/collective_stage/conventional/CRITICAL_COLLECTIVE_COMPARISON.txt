COLLECTIVE NONLINEAR ISING RESPONSE: STRONG CONVENTIONAL CONSTRUCTIONS
2026-11-01

Question and exact microscopic inputs
------------------------------------
Periodic zero-field Ising chain of n spins, pi proportional to
exp[J sum_i sigma_i sigma_(i+1)], exp(2J)=n. All clocks are one.
The base heat-bath rate is
  w0_i = (1 - gamma sigma_i(sigma_(i-1)+sigma_(i+1))/2)/2,
  gamma = tanh(2J) = (n^2-1)/(n^2+1).
The target rate is w_i=A_i w0_i, with A_same=1 and A_opposite=2.
The source is M=sum_i sigma_i, with no normalization by sqrt(n).
The requested quantity is the complete integrated equilibrium response
  chi = <M,K^+ M>_pi.
All states, all target interactions and all times remain in this definition.

The target lies in the general nonlinear kinetic-Ising family with alpha=3/2,
delta=-1/3, eta=0 in Godreche--Luck's convention. It is not the free-fermion
Glauber line or the KDH uniform-magnetization closure. In domain-wall variables
creation=(1-gamma)/2, annihilation=(1+gamma)/2, hopping=1. The sum of reaction
rates is 1, whereas twice the hopping rate is 2. Thus the free-fermion condition
does not hold. Godreche--Luck Section 5.4 explains that the partial exact closure
at Metropolis concerns staggered quantities, not the ferromagnetic response here.

Our rates also equal ordinary Metropolis rates multiplied by n^2/(n^2+1) on
creation/annihilation moves, with hopping unchanged. Hence, by exact Dirichlet
comparison, chi_Metropolis <= chi_target <= (1+1/n^2) chi_Metropolis. Calling
this an unfamiliar kinetic family would be misleading.

Primary source: Godreche and Luck, Single-spin-flip dynamics of the Ising chain,
arXiv:1503.01661v3, especially equations (2.1), (2.8), (5.2), (5.10)--(5.21).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.01661


1. A complete conventional solution of leading collective response
-----------------------------------------------------------------
This construction needs no three-particle inverse.

General potential/current argument. Let K0 be a reversible reference on the
same equilibrium, c0 its conductances (one copy of each unoriented edge), and
u0=K0^+f an acquired reference potential. Put chi0=<f,u0>. The reference current
  J0(e)=c0(e) gradient(u0)(e)
has exactly the required divergence f (with the equilibrium density in the
ordinary unweighted graph convention). If c(e)=A(e)c0(e), define the probability
measure on reference-gradient energy
  mu(e)=c0(e) gradient(u0)(e)^2 / chi0.
Optimizing the scalar multiple of u0 in the Dirichlet variational principle
and inserting J0 in Thomson's principle gives
  chi0 / E_mu[A] <= chi <= chi0 E_mu[1/A].                    (1)
The potential and current are explicit; their full-state arrays need not be
stored if their local descriptions and energy sums can be evaluated.

For the uniform Glauber reference, K0 M=(1-gamma)M. Thus u0=M/(1-gamma).
Let t=tanh J. Exact periodic Ising transfer identities give
  V=Var(M)=n(1+t)/(1-t) * (1-t^n)/(1+t^n),
  chi0=V/(1-gamma),
  p=(1-t^2)(1+t^(n-2)) / [2(1-t^n)].
Here p is the fraction of the base Dirichlet energy of M carried by flips with
equal neighbors. For arbitrary positive same/opposite rates s,o,
  a=E_mu[A]=s p + o(1-p),
  E_mu[1/A]=p/s+(1-p)/o.
Consequently the complete target response obeys the closed interval
  chi0/a <= chi <= chi0 [p/s+(1-p)/o].                        (2)
Its relative width is exactly
  p(1-p)(s-o)^2/(s o).                                       (3)
All these are rational numbers when exp(2J)=n and s,o are rational.

Equivalently, if B=K0^(-1/2) K K0^(-1/2), b=K0^(-1/2)M,
then spectrum(B) lies in [m,Mmax]=[min(s,o),max(s,o)]. The normalized first
spectral moment is a. Jensen's inequality and the endpoint chord of 1/x give
  chi0/a <= chi <= chi0(m+Mmax-a)/(m Mmax).
The latter equals (2) because A takes exactly the two endpoint values. This is
ordinary first-moment Gauss--Lobatto / arithmetic-harmonic comparison.

At exp(2J)=n, t=(n-1)/(n+1), so
  p = 2 coth(1)/n + O(n^-2).
It follows already from (2), without a kinetic closure assumption, that
  chi/chi0 -> 1/o
with relative error O(1/n), for every fixed positive s,o. The collective scale
chi0~[tanh(1)/2] n^4 remains in this result. A direct trajectory or full state
has not been used. The same argument works for n/xi held at any fixed positive
value as xi grows, with a different explicitly known finite-size coefficient.

This is a decisive limit on novelty: leading low-temperature magnetization
response universality is already furnished by an inexpensive conventional
positive construction. The three-walker calculation is not needed to earn it.

0.00032024072834878104, taking about 0.11 seconds in the recorded run.
This has no accuracy extrapolation from small n. The displayed decimals are
summaries of exact rational calculations, and the stored exact recipe specifies
the endpoints without printing huge integers.


2. Stronger use of the acquired three-spin scalar: classical Gauss--Radau
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Root's microscopic construction acquires
  R=<r,K0^+r>, r=M-K u, u=V/<M,KM> M,
with exact rational integer-residual bounds on a sparse translation-orbit
three-particle solve. Let L=V^2/<M,KM>. This gives the exact identity
  chi-L=<r,K^+r>.
Even direct operator comparison supplies
  L+R/Mmax <= chi <= L+R/m.
Dropping R/Mmax from the lower bound discards information already available.

The optimal bounds based on the first two B spectral moments are stronger.
Normalize the spectral measure of B at b by chi0=||b||^2. Then
  mean a = chi0/L,
  variance v = a^2 R/chi0.
For endpoint c in {m,Mmax}, put
  x_c=a+v/(a-c),
  Q_c=chi0(c+x_c-a)/(c x_c)
     =chi0 (a-c+v/c)/(a(a-c)+v).
Classical two-node Gauss--Radau gives
  Q_Mmax <= chi <= Q_m.                                      (4)

Self-contained proof. Let q_c(x) be the degree-two polynomial that agrees
with 1/x at c and touches it at x_c. Its exact pointwise error is
  1/x-q_c(x)=-(x-c)(x-x_c)^2/[c x_c^2 x].
On [m,Mmax] this is nonpositive for c=m and nonnegative for c=Mmax.
Integration only uses the first two moments. The two-point measure at c,x_c
has those same moments and positive weights, and integrates 1/x to Q_c.
Thus (4) follows. The bounds are sharp among all measures with those moments.

For a rigorously acquired interval [Rlo,Rhi], both Q_c increase with v:
  d(Q_c/chi0)/dv=(a-c)^2/[c (a(a-c)+v)^2] >= 0.
Use v_lo=max(0,a^2 Rlo/chi0),
v_hi=min((a-m)(Mmax-a),a^2 Rhi/chi0), then lower Q_Mmax(v_lo),
upper Q_m(v_hi). All endpoint arithmetic in critical_radau.py is rational.
The microscopic compiler is explicitly shared from root/critical_response.py;
the script does not claim to have independently invented or acquired it.

With exact v, the normalized interval gap is
  (Q_m-Q_Mmax)/chi0 =
  v(Mmax-m)[(a-m)(Mmax-a)-v]
  / {m Mmax [a(a-m)+v] [a(Mmax-a)-v]} .                        (5)
The variance deficit in square brackets measures departure from an endpoint-
supported spectral measure. It explains why the same reference scalar gives
much better accuracy than a bare residual-norm upper bound.

Primary source for this established method: Gene H. Golub, Matrix Computation
and the Theory of Moments, Theorem 2 and its quadrature remainder.
https://web.stanford.edu/class/cme324/mmq2.pdf
Further primary development: Li, Sra and Jegelka, Gauss quadrature for matrix
inverse forms with applications, arXiv:1512.01904v2.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.01904

4. Independent local current construction and its useful failure
---------------------------------------------------------------
We also constructed actual divergence-free local cycle improvements of J0.
For a square formed by adjacent spin flips i,i+1, and exterior spins L,R,
assign signed circulation
  C_i(sigma)=pi(sigma)/pi(sigma_i,sigma_(i+1)|L,R)
             *sigma_i sigma_(i+1) h(L,R).
Its contribution is +C_i on flip i and -C_i on flip i+1. Conditional division
makes the circulation magnitude invariant when either internal spin flips.
Every square is therefore an antisymmetric, divergence-free current. Summing
translated squares preserves the exact required source divergence.

All spin-reversal-odd h on two exterior spins lie in span{L,R}. Optimizing the
two coefficients requires a 2x2 quadratic minimization whose entries are exact
five-spin marginal sums (32 local configurations). The optimum is proportional
to R-L, as reflection symmetry predicts. Separately, a primal Ritz solve in
span{M,T}, T=sum sigma_(i-1)sigma_i sigma_(i+1), uses the same local marginals
plus <M,T>=V-n(1-C2). There is no full-state or reference Green solve here.

At n128 this complete local primal/dual construction gives
  [51810385.78686532, 52143776.15922515], relative width .0064348174.
At n256 its width is .0033168632, versus .0001579596 from acquired R+Radau.
The local correction improves constants but retains O(1/n)-type behavior in
the tests. It does not acquire the nonlocal collision/cycle information present
in the reference inverse. This failure should be preserved, not dismissed as
a missing kinetic parameter or an assumed optimal helper.

5. Projection representation has strong prior machinery
--------------------------------------------------------
The two-rate decomposition gives a compression of a phase-indicator projection
by the reference gradient projection. Thus the spectral/Hodge interpretation
of (5) is structurally the same as the two-phase composite conductivity problem.
It is not a new abstract projection representation.

An accessible primary governing source is Murphy, Hallman, Cherkaev and Golden,
Spectral theory of effective transport for continuous uniaxial polycrystalline
materials, arXiv:2412.01105. It constructs self-adjoint
Gamma=grad(grad* grad)^(-1)grad*, compositions X Gamma X, and associated Stieltjes
measures, explicitly connecting them to Bergman--Milton and Golden--Papanicolaou.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.01105
Its material setting requires stationary/periodic homogenization assumptions;
the finite reversible graph adaptation here instead follows directly from
finite-dimensional gradient linear algebra. The common projection operation is
established; microscopic graph-specific moment acquisition is a separate cost.

Conclusion for selecting the scientific opening
------------------------------------------------
The target dynamics is genuinely nonlinear and the response genuinely collective.
Nevertheless its leading low-temperature response is already provided by an
explicit conventional reference potential/current construction. The newly
acquired microscopic three-spin inverse provides substantially sharper finite-
accuracy response intervals than local conventional competitors, when processed
by the strongest classical moment bounds. That is useful computational capital.
It is not by itself evidence for a new leading universality principle or a new
general moment/variational theory. A stronger opening must use the nonlocal
information in a regime where the requested complete service was not already
earned by energy-weighted rate concentration or an equally cheap conventional
construction. In particular the next comparator should remain the actual
potential/current bound (1), not the much weaker fixed-contrast scalar bracket.
