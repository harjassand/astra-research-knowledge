CONSTRUCTIVE CONVENTIONAL COMPARATOR: INFINITE-VOLUME NONGRADIENT DIFFUSION
1 November 2026

Result and decision
-------------------
Existing variational machinery already gives a strong, fully acquired answer
to the whole scalar transport task on a genuine nongradient exclusion gas.
At density 1/2 and microscopic rates c_(i,i+1)=1+eta_(i-1)eta_(i+2), the
construction below proves

  1.2277988910688737 <= D <= 1.227810257121883,

with relative interval width 9.2573e-6. The exact endpoints, rather than the
rounded decimal displays, are rational numbers in the saved JSON. No finite
physical system replaces the infinite gas, and no trajectory, slow manifold,
uniform infinite-volume spectral gap, or compressible solution tensor is used.

The same algorithms at rate contrast 10, c=1+9 eta_(-1)eta_2, prove

  2.3517170727218555 <= D <= 2.3576744492485107,

with relative width 0.00253321. The decisive remaining cost is exponential
growth with local support volume. Claiming only a trajectory-free thermodynamic
transport coefficient or only exact lower and upper bounds would not establish
an advantage over this comparator. A stronger opening must earn a substantive
complexity improvement, a broader physical class, or additional observables.

1. Full microscopic problem, and a nongradient check
--------------------------------------------------
On Z, exchange occupations at i,i+1 at rate

  c_i(eta)=1+alpha eta_(i-1)eta_(i+2), alpha>0.

The rate is independent of the exchanged occupations, is translation invariant,
and satisfies 1<=c<=1+alpha. Product Bernoulli(rho) is reversible. The conserved
gas has arbitrarily slow density modes: a uniform positive spectral gap is not
available. D is its bulk density diffusion coefficient, in the convention that
the simple symmetric exclusion process c=1 has D=1.

This model is not accidentally gradient. On the periodic six-site configuration
(1,1,0,1,0,0), sum_i c_i(eta)(eta_i-eta_(i+1))=alpha. A discrete spatial gradient
would telescope to zero on every periodic configuration. Equivalently the
nonlinear current contains distinct translation orbits of cubic monomials,
{0,1,3} and {0,2,3}.

Some seemingly nonlinear neighboring-rate examples ARE gradient. In particular
[a+b(eta_(-1)+eta_2)](eta_0-eta_1) equals h-tau_1 h for

  h=a eta_0+b(eta_(-1)eta_0+eta_0 eta_1-eta_(-1)eta_1).

Such a model would give no meaningful nongradient challenge. The quadratic
exterior-product term above is the first chosen obstruction.

2. Upper bound: acquire the complete local Ritz problem
------------------------------------------------------
Write chi=rho(1-rho), d=eta_0-eta_1, and
g_f=Delta_(0,1) sum_x tau_x f. The standard Varadhan--Spohn variational principle
in this normalization is

  D=inf_local f E[c(d+g_f)^2]/(2 chi).

Arita--Krapivsky--Mallick make this a finite-local-function Ritz construction.
Here the construction is executed in the Walsh basis at rho=1/2: s_i=2eta_i-1,
chi_S=product_(i in S) s_i. For every shape S of span at most R, min S=0 and
|S|>=2, include its translated gradient. There are 2^(R-1)-1 shapes. Constants
and singleton shapes have zero translated gradient and are removed.

Only shifts for which exactly one endpoint belongs to S+x contribute. All
matrix entries are exact products of finite Walsh polynomials, because
E[chi_S chi_T]=1_{S=T} and

  4c=(4+alpha)+alpha(s_(-1)+s_2+s_(-1)s_2).

The implementation compiles an integer sparse Gram matrix Gnum=2G and integer
driving vector bnum=4b, where G=2E[c g_S g_T], b=2E[c d g_S]. It solves the
finite quadratic proposal U=A+2b.x+x.Gx, A=1+alpha/4. The proposed minimizer is
quantized to denominator 2^80, then U is evaluated using Python integers and
Fraction. Any chosen vector is a valid trial; the upper bound therefore needs
no assumption about sparse LU accuracy. Floating arithmetic only chooses a
trial. The exact rational energy is the certificate.

An independent local-pattern enumeration at R=4,5 matched the compiled Gram
and driving matrices. It directly constructed the translated derivatives on
all 256 and 1024 required local assignments. This checks the normalization,
translation multiplicities, and sparse Walsh compiler against a different
representation, rather than merely comparing two sparse solvers.

3. Lower bound: construct feasible flows, including rounding repair
----------------------------------------------------------------
Use a local flow supported only on exchange bond {0,1}:

  q(eta)=(eta_0-eta_1) h(s_outside).

The exterior window is {-r,...,-1,2,...,r+1}, and h is an arbitrary Walsh
polynomial on these 2r sites. It has 2^(2r) coefficients. Endpoint independence
gives configuration-exchange antisymmetry exactly. Strict positivity of c
ensures admissibility.

Shapira's flow variational principle uses the translation covariance seminorm
of the divergence. Our flow has divergence q. If q=sum_A q_A chi_A, then

  sum_x E[q tau_x q]
    =sum_translation-orbits O (sum_(A in O) q_A)^2.

This follows immediately from Walsh orthogonality and finite support. Thus
vanishing divergence in that seminorm is an exact finite linear condition.
The h coefficient belonging to exterior set S gives an incidence column

  C_[S union {0}],S=+1,  C_[S union {1}],S=-1,

where brackets mean canonical translation orbit; coincident vertices cancel.
The omitted factor 1/2 from eta_0-eta_1 does not change feasibility. Hence Cx=0
is sufficient and necessary for this finite family, not an approximate local
continuity condition.

The current-flow overlap and flow norm are chi E[h] and chi E[h^2/c]. Shapira's
amplitude-optimized lower bound becomes exactly

  D >= (E h)^2 / E[h^2/c] = x_0^2/(x.Gx),

with G multiplication by 1/c in the exterior Walsh basis. It is not necessary
to know the true current corrector to evaluate this expression.

To optimize, set v=e_empty and solve

  (C G^(-1) C^T)lambda=C G^(-1)v,
  x=G^(-1)(v-C^T lambda).

Here G^(-1) is simply multiplication by c, an explicitly known four-term XOR
operator. Delete one incidence row per connected component to obtain an SPD
system. A spanning forest identifies these redundant rows constructively.

Quantization would normally destroy exact feasibility. The code avoids that
failure. Round each proposal coefficient to an integer numerator at denominator
2^80; calculate its integer divergence Cx. Process the spanning forest from
leaves toward roots, modifying the parent-edge numerator to cancel the leaf
divergence. Each component's total divergence is identically zero, so every root
also vanishes. The final integer vector is independently checked to satisfy
Cx=0. Its rational flow energy gives the certified lower bound. No approximate
divergence is silently accepted and no unknown inverse or norm is supplied.

For completeness, 4(1+alpha)/c has Walsh coefficients (4+3alpha,-alpha,-alpha,
-alpha) at the same four XOR masks. Both numerator and denominator of the lower
bound are therefore exact finite integer sums.

This local-flow compiler also works in d=2: include a flow polynomial for each
positive coordinate bond, and connect the two endpoint-augmented translation
orbits in the same incidence matrix. Different bond directions have separate
metric blocks. The support-volume exponential cost remains.

Important qualification: each displayed local-flow lower bound is rigorous.
Density of these particular exactly local kernels in the full infinite flow
space has NOT been proved here. The numerical bracket does not need that
assertion. The separate finite-box construction below supplies a general
convergent lower sequence when a guarantee of eventual termination is wanted.

5. Complete convergent finite-box fallback with acquired backend
---------------------------------------------------------------
Funaki--Gu--Wang define finite-volume Dirichlet and dual quantities (4.1) under
finite-range, uniformly elliptic, endpoint-independent reversible rates. Their
Proposition 5.1 proves algebraic convergence of BOTH bounds to infinite-volume
conductivity. Its C(d,lambda,r) and exponent gamma>0 are implicit rather than
numerically supplied. This prevents an explicit precomputed box size from the
stated theorem, but does not prevent adaptive certified stopping: increase the
box until its computed upper/lower interval meets the requested tolerance.

Our 1D dual backend is explicit. For Lambda={0,...,L-1}, Lambda+={0,...,L},
condition on the two exterior bits and on the particle number in Lambda+.
Build the finite weighted exclusion graph K_box and source f=eta_L-eta_0.
Let R=E[f K_box^+ f], averaging all sectors and exterior occupations. Then

  D_star(L)=chi L/R <= D.

The optimizer solves precisely this finite graph Poisson equation. There are
4*2^(L+1) states across exterior sectors, largest particle block
binomial(L+1,floor((L+1)/2)). A pinned sparse LU proposes the solution. For a
connected S-state graph whose edge rates are at least one, elementary path
Cauchy--Schwarz gives spectral gap >=1/S^2. If v is a dyadic trial, r=f-Kv,

  R_sector <= 2<f,v>-<v,Kv>+S^2<r,r>.

All residuals and quadratic expressions are integer sums. This bounds numerical
error before inversion to obtain D_star. It does not assume any spectral gap
for the infinite physical process. An exact rational Gaussian-elimination
backend is also implemented: it terminates for every finite block and removes
an arbitrary-precision oracle from the algorithm. For growing accuracy one may
use exact rational finite systems rather than hold float64 proposal accuracy
fixed. Ordinary rational elimination has cubic arithmetic cost in sector size;
bit growth is controlled by finite integer determinant bounds. It is plainly
exponential in box volume, not an efficiency breakthrough.

At alpha=0, every tested L=1,2,4,6,8,10 gives D_star=1. Exact rational solves
at L=1,2,4 match the certified numerical proposals. At alpha=1,L=4,
D_star=6965376/5980559; at alpha=9,L=4 it is
388421820800/275391689219. The L=10 numerical residual error bound is less than
3e-22 in R, but the physical finite-box lower is only 1.190407699 (alpha=1)
or 1.645321205 (alpha=9). Thus local Shapira flows are the much stronger measured
lower at comparable accessible sizes; finite-box convergence remains useful
as a guarantee, not as a falsely competitive measured result.
