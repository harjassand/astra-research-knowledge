LOCAL CONDITIONAL-SCHUR RESPONSE CONSTRUCTION
Independent interacting-system contribution, 1 November 2026

Status
------
This contribution combines the root's local bounded-overlap coupling estimate
with a positive-semidefinite Schur-complement refinement and a complete recursive
implementation. It is an explicit response construction for a separated hierarchy
of genuinely interacting Gibbs chains. Conditional averaging and Schur complements
are conventional machinery. No priority, arbitrary-clock uniform complexity, or
general superiority over strong conventional multiscale solvers is claimed.

1. A real obstruction to naive physical-support localization
---------------------------------------------------------
Take a free-boundary zero-field Ising chain sigma_0,...,sigma_L with homogeneous
J>0, t=tanh J, and ordinary heat-bath rates. Site 0 has clock epsilon; every other
site has clock one. Equilibrium is independent of the clocks. For f=sigma_L,

    Cov(f,sigma_0)=t^L,
    <sigma_0,K sigma_0>_pi=epsilon(1-t^2).

The variational Poisson principle therefore gives

    chi_f >= t^(2L)/[epsilon(1-t^2)].

Choosing epsilon=t^(4L) makes this lower bound grow exponentially with distance,
although equilibrium correlations decay exponentially and the microscopic rates
are an ordinary fixed local heat-bath rule. A fixed-radius physical neighborhood
around f misses the response-dominating remote spin. Spatial mixing alone is not
a clock-independent relative-response localization theorem.

The missing source projection is exactly acquired here:

    E[f | sigma_0]=t^L sigma_0.

Its slow averaged rate is epsilon(1-t^2); the displayed lower bound is the exact
response of that acquired conditional projection under the averaged slow chain.
A rate-ordered innovation basis might help encode such projections, but no
clock-uniform comparison or locality theorem for that general proposal has been
proved in this contribution. Do not substitute it as a finished primitive.

2. Exact conditional decomposition
----------------------------------
Work in L2(pi), where K=-L is the nonnegative self-adjoint generator. Let P be
conditional expectation given a selected set of slow spins, Q=I-P. Write

    K=K_F+K_S,

where K_F consists of fast updates and K_S of slow updates. Conditional fast
equilibration is exact: ker K_F=range P. On Q let F=K_F|Q>0. Define

    A=P K_S P,  B=Q K_S P,  C=Q K_S Q.

Then on mean-zero functions the block matrix is

    K = [ A    B*   ]
        [ B   F+C   ].

K_S is positive semidefinite. Its Schur complement consequently obeys

    C-B A^+ B* >=0,

with B vanishing on the constant kernel of A. Suppose an explicit local calculation
has earned

    B* F^-1 B <= theta A.                                  (1)

For centered source f=p+q with p=Pf, q=Qf, acquire

    a=<p,A^+p>,  b=<q,F^-1q>.

Eliminating A first gives the exact identity

    chi_f = a + <q-B A^+p, [F+C-B A^+B*]^-1(q-B A^+p)>.

Since the bracketed operator dominates F, (1) and Cauchy-Schwarz imply

    a <= chi_f <= (1+theta)a+b+2 sqrt(theta a b).             (2)

This holds for every theta>=0; there is no theta<1 restriction or 1/(1-theta)
denominator. In particular a purely slow source obeys

    a <= chi_p <= (1+theta)a.

The positivity of the COMPLETE slow generator is essential. An arbitrary block
matrix with the same off-diagonal norm does not have this bound.

3. Earning theta locally on a chain (root construction)
------------------------------------------------------
For a nearest-neighbor Gibbs spin chain, retain the even spins and update odd spins
as fast variables. Conditional on the retained spins, the fast spins are independent.
Their two-state relaxation gaps gamma_j(S) are known from the sum of their two
physical flip rates. Conditional centered normalized fast-spin monomials phi_U
form an orthonormal basis with

    F phi_U = [sum_(j in U) gamma_j(S)] phi_U.

For a slow observable h(S), the i-th slow update contributes

    Q K_i P h = [w_i-E_F w_i] [h(S)-h(S^i)].

Its nonempty conditional fast monomials use only the fast-neighbor set N_F(i).
On a chain a given nonempty set U belongs to at most d_*=2 such neighborhoods.
Thus, at each fixed S, Cauchy-Schwarz over this bounded overlap gives

    <B h,F^-1 B h>
    <= d_* sum_i E[ Var_F(w_i) (Delta_i h)^2 /
                         min_(j in N_F(i)) gamma_j(S) ].

The slow Dirichlet form is

    <h,A h> = (1/2) sum_i E[ E_F(w_i) (Delta_i h)^2 ].

Therefore the fully acquired bound is

    theta = 2 d_* max_(i,S_local)
       Var_F(w_i) / [E_F(w_i) min_(j in N_F(i)) gamma_j(S)].  (3)

A simpler conservative replacement uses Var_F(w_i)<=sup(w_i) E_F(w_i).
There is no total number of slow sites in (3). Each maximization in the chain
uses only the three neighboring retained spins and four fast-neighbor values.
Fixed-boundary configurations may be included unnecessarily; that only enlarges
the bound. Conditional eigenfunctions depend on S, which is allowed because
F is block-diagonal at fixed S. No derivative in S is taken in F^-1.

A sharper acquired estimate respects monomial-specific overlap. Write

    w_i-E_F w_i = u_L phi_L+u_R phi_R+u_LR phi_L phi_R.

The singleton overlap degrees d_L,d_R are at most2 (and1 at a fixed edge),
whereas the pair belongs to just one slow neighborhood. Hence one may use

    theta=2 max_(i,S_local) [ d_L u_L^2/gamma_L
         + d_R u_R^2/gamma_R + u_LR^2/(gamma_L+gamma_R) ] / E_F w_i.

The coefficients can be evaluated without dividing by a small conditional
variance. If p_L,p_R are conditional probabilities of the fast + spins,

    u_L=sqrt[p_L(1-p_L)] [E_R w_i(+,R)-E_R w_i(-,R)],
    u_R=sqrt[p_R(1-p_R)] [E_L w_i(L,+)-E_L w_i(L,-)],
    u_LR=sqrt[p_L(1-p_L)p_R(1-p_R)]
                          [w(++ )-w(+-)-w(-+)+w(-- )].

audit_fields_sources.py checks this sharper form directly against the largest
eigenvalue of the full normalized coupling operator.

4. Acquired fast response and a useful full-source lower bound
-----------------------------------------------------------
For a linear spin observable f=sum_i v_i sigma_i minus its equilibrium mean,

    q = sum_(j fast) v_j [sigma_j-m_j(S)],
    m_j(S)=tanh(J_(j-1) sigma_(j-1)+J_j sigma_(j+1)).

Thus

    b=sum_(j fast) v_j^2 E[(1-m_j(S)^2)/gamma_j(S)].          (4)

This is an explicit sum of two-spin Gibbs expectations. It retains the entire
fast conditional response, not just an assumed reduced source.

Let v=F^-1q, and acquire c=<v,K_S v>. A slow flip changes at most two neighboring
terms of v, so c is a sum of expectations over three retained and two fast spins.
All those distributions are obtained by Gibbs transfer matrices and the exact
two-spin conditional laws. No many-body inverse is used.

The variational trial A^+p+t v, together with (1), gives the additional bound

    chi_f >= a + max(b-sqrt(theta a b),0)^2/(b+c).           (5)

If the reduced response is known only within [La,Ua], the directly usable recursive
bounds are

    lower = La + max(b-sqrt(theta Ua b),0)^2/(b+c),
    upper = (1+theta)Ua+b+2sqrt(theta Ua b),                 (6)

with zero correction when b+c=0. This also handles a pure fast source: [b^2/(b+c),b].
No response coefficient in (6) is supplied; a is recursively bounded, and b,c,theta
are finite local calculations.

5. Closed recursion for genuinely interacting equilibrium
--------------------------------------------------------
Use an open chain of n=2^L-1 spins with fixed + boundary spins and zero bulk field.
Couplings may vary. Summing out every odd spin produces another nearest-neighbor
Ising Gibbs chain, with exact new couplings

    J'_k = atanh[tanh(J_(2k)) tanh(J_(2k+1))].

The averaged slow rate is the exact sum over its two fast neighbors, each with
its Gibbs conditional two-state distribution. It depends only on the retained
spin and its two retained neighbors; detailed balance with the new Gibbs law is
automatic and checked numerically.

Conditional means of a removed spin are linear in its neighboring retained spins:

    tanh(J_L s_L+J_R s_R)=a_L s_L+a_R s_R,
    a_L=[tanh(J_L+J_R)+tanh(J_L-J_R)]/2,
    a_R=[tanh(J_L+J_R)-tanh(J_L-J_R)]/2.

Consequently the projected magnetization source remains linear, with explicitly
updated weights. Boundary contributions are constants and disappear on centering.
Repeat until one spin remains; its variance divided by its two-state relaxation
rate gives the exact base response. Apply (6) in reverse order.

Each level uses O(number of current sites) finite truth-table and transfer-matrix
operations. Sizes halve, so arithmetic count is O(n), apart from arithmetic
precision. No configuration-space tensor, local response coefficient, or slow
manifold is assumed. Rates and static correlations are calculated anew at each
level. The total response interval itself determines whether the requested
accuracy was earned. Without sufficient acquired separation it may be broad;
this is not a uniform FPTAS for all arbitrary-clock Gibbs chains or all accuracies.

6. Executed physical instance and comparison boundary
---------------------------------------------------
The implemented original flip rates are

    w_i(s) = nu_i [.5+2 1{s_(i-1)=s_(i+1)=+1}]
                      /[1+exp(2 s_i(J_(i-1)s_(i-1)+J_i s_(i+1)))].

The multiplier is endpoint-invariant, lies between .5 and2.5, and is non-Gaussian
at every site. Clocks nu_i=alpha^(v2(i)) specify a hierarchy; no inferred clock
or measured kinetic coefficient is supplied. These inputs have genuine interacting
Gibbs equilibrium and extensive nonlinear kinetics. For large examples,
J=.5 log(n+1), so equilibrium correlation length is of the order of system size.
The normalized total magnetization has a computed O(1) fraction of its variance
in the final slow conditional projection. This checks that the source actually
overlaps the difficult collective dynamics.

conditional_decimation.py retains an earlier global-norm bound, which required
separation increasing with n. local_response_recursion.py uses the local estimate
(3), source response (4), local trial energy c, and (6), allowing fixed alpha as
n grows. No trajectory follows the fast events over the slowest clock.

Small-system response comparisons use an independently assembled spin generator
and a 75-digit Decimal Poisson solve. audit_local_schur.py checks (1)-(2) on15
disordered physical models and75 slow/fast/mixed sources, including large theta.
Every check passed; largest exact coupling theta divided by the acquired local
bound was0.29534. These are floating checks of ordinary proofs, not interval
certificates. The implementation uses resolved double inputs for the large runs;
extreme underflow or saturated hyperbolic functions require directed precision.

7. Fields, nearest-neighbor sources, and the acquired closure
----------------------------------------------------------
The root extends the executable recursion to arbitrary site fields H_i and sources

    O=c+sum_i a_i sigma_i+sum_i b_i sigma_i sigma_(i+1).

An eliminated site's source is sigma_j h_j(L,R), with
h_j=a_j+b_(j-1)L+b_j R. Its conditional mean is h_j tanh(H_j+J_L L+J_R R), an
arbitrary function of two binary boundary spins. A four-point Walsh transform
therefore gives a constant, two site terms, and one coarse edge term. This is exact
closure, not a low-rank assumption. The residual is

    q_j=h_j(L,R)[sigma_j-m_j(L,R)],

so (4) includes h_j^2 inside the Gibbs expectation. When calculating c, a retained
flip changes h_j as well as m_j and gamma_j; omitting that change is incorrect.

Similarly, integrating the Gibbs weight of an eliminated site gives

    log[2cosh(H_j+J_L L+J_R R)].

Its four-point Walsh coefficients are an additive constant, two boundary fields,
and one coarse coupling. Add them to the retained fields and couplings. Averaged
slow flip rates automatically satisfy detailed balance with these coefficients.

The independent field audit includes12 disordered chains n=3,5,7,9; fields,
arbitrary positive endpoint-invariant multipliers, and site/bond sources. It verifies
source closure, Gibbs closure, q, F^-1q, b, the monomial-specific coupling bound,
and the full-source response bounds against the independently formed full generator.
All pass. Max discrepancies: q6.67e-16 absolute, F^-1q5.91e-14 relative,
b1.36e-14 relative, source closure8.89e-15, Gibbs closure6.22e-15.

8. Interval blocks and the limit of this extension
------------------------------------------------
Conditional checkerboards are not essential to the theorem. Retain separating
spins of a 1D Gibbs chain and eliminate disjoint intervals of at most B spins.
Conditional on the retained spins, intervals are independent. Each 2^B-state
conditional generator and its gap is directly acquired by ordinary finite matrix
algebra. Fast-source Poisson problems are likewise finite conditional solves.
Each retained spin touches at most two intervals, so bounded overlap persists;
replace single-spin monomials by products of conditional interval eigenfunctions.
The variance/gap version (3) applies without constructing each eigenfunction.

The Gibbs partition and the conditional expectation of the constant/site/edge
source still depend on only the two boundary spins of each interval, so their
coarse representation closes exactly. The local c calculation uses two adjacent
intervals, costing O(4^B) finite-state work per retained site, plus the conditional
Poisson solves. A hierarchy that keeps B bounded has cost polynomial in n and
the finite block arithmetic, provided its computed response interval is narrow
enough. No theorem here says every clock pattern admits such a hierarchy. A large
required B, poor conditional gaps, or large local theta is an explicit obstruction.

This block extension is a finite-matrix algorithm specification; the executed
independent code in this directory uses B=1. The root's general field/source
backend is separately implemented. Do not claim the optional arbitrary-B backend
has been executed merely because its algebra is finite.

Conventional conditional averaging already produces the central reduced chain
under scale separation. Its known machinery is a successful comparator. The new
investigation must judge the useful contribution as the locally acquired finite
response operation and its reach, not as merely computing a huge lifetime. This
file does not assert that the operation establishes historical novelty or the
requested civilisation-changing capability.
