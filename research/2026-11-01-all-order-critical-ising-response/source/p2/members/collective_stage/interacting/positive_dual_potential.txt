POSITIVE ALL-ORDER DUAL: EXACT POTENTIAL AND WHAT IT DOES NOT YET BUY

Complement to critical_ring_proof.txt. The root owns the regenerative response
construction and implementation; this note proves the acquired excursion
operation, its elementary-event cost, and the remaining accuracy obstruction.

1. Actual nonlinear target has a positive dual

In the physical rate family of critical_ring_proof.txt, set eta=-b, 0<b<1.
The global odd monomial F_S=sum_x product_(i in S) sigma_(i+x), for an odd
subset S of the n-cycle, satisfies the exact target-generator identity

  K F_S = |S| F_S
       -gamma(1-b)/2 sum_(i in S, +/-) F_(S xor {i,i+/-1})
       -b sum_(i in S) F_(S xor {i-1,i+1}).

Thus the dual has real positive operations: a defect hops left or right with
rate gamma(1-b)/2, or branches by toggling its two neighbors at rate b.
Encountering an occupied site annihilates the pair. The number of defects
stays odd. The remaining mass is mu |S|, mu=(1-b)(1-gamma), a killing rate.
This is an exact representation of the entire target generator; no degree
cutoff, interaction-word enumeration, or sign cancellation is assumed.

The bare internal dual omits this final killing. For global observables,
singleton positions are equivalent under translation. Let tau be the first
bare return to a singleton from the packed triple.

2. The all-odd cyclic polynomial

For positive cyclic gaps g_0,...,g_(k-1), sum g_i=n, k odd, define

  h(S)=1/n sum g_i g_j g_l,

over triples i<j<l whose three cyclic index spacings j-i,l-j,k-l+i are odd.
Set h(singleton)=0. It is nonnegative, and is compatible with every collision:
a zero gap disappears together with its two coincident defects; its neighboring
gaps merge, and the polynomial becomes exactly the polynomial for k-2 defects.

For the symmetric rate-1/2 hopping generator D,

  D h=(k-1)/2.

For the branching operation B_i S=S xor {i-1,i+1}, the additional identity is

  sum_(i in S) [h(B_i S)-h(S)] = 1-2k/n.                (1)

3. A direct proof of the branching identity

Unwrap the ordered defects as x_1<...<x_k and give them alternating charges
q_i=(-1)^(i+1). Let W(d)=d^2/2-d^3/(3n). An equivalent representation is

  h(S)=sum_(i<j) q_i q_j W(x_j-x_i).                    (2)

It is independent of the cut: W(d)+W(n-d)=n^2/6, and the alternating odd
charges outside any cut particle sum to zero. Expanding (2) in gaps gives
the alternating-triple polynomial above.

A branch replaces charge q at x by q at x-1, -q at x, and q at x+1; coincident
opposite charges cancel, preserving (2). The new internal pair contribution is
W(2)-2W(1)=1-2/n. For every other charge at distance d, the second difference
of W is exactly 1-2d/n. Consequently

  h(B_i S)-h(S)
    =q_i -(2/n) -(2q_i/n) sum_j q_j |x_j-x_i|.

The sum over i of the last term is zero: for alternating odd charges,
sum_(i<j) q_i q_j |x_j-x_i|=0. Expanding each distance in gaps proves this,
because each gap has prefix charge either0 or1, and prefix*(1-prefix)=0.
Summing the first two terms proves (1). This derivation includes adjacent
annihilations; it is not restricted to widely separated defects.

4. First moments and elementary event cost are uniformly earned

Write d=gamma(1-b). The positive bare internal generator satisfies exactly

  K_int h = d(k-1)/2 - b(1-2k/n) = a k-c,
  a=d/2+2b/n, c=d/2+b.

On every non-singleton odd state, k>=3. If d>b, then

  K_int h >= epsilon k,
  epsilon=a-c/3=(d-b)/3+2b/n >0.

Stopping Dynkin's inequality at return gives

  E integral_0^tau k_t dt <= h(packed3)/epsilon,
  h(packed3)=(n-2)/n.

The total elementary hop/branch attempt rate is (d+b)k. Therefore

  E[number of elementary dual events before return]
       <= (d+b)(n-2)/(n epsilon).

This is independent of the physical n^2 critical relaxation scale at fixed
b<1/2 and sufficiently large gamma. For the root's rates same=1, opposite=2,
the normalized b=1/3, nu=3/2. At gamma tending1, this simple event upper bound
tends9. Restoring nu changes holding times, not the expected number of events.

Finite-state stopping makes the derivation unproblematic for every finite ring;
localization yields the corresponding inequality on the line. The inequality
is sufficient; equality of unbounded stopped-potential expectations is not
silently assumed.

5. A second moment is not supplied

Even without branching, three annihilating walkers on the infinite line have
return moments below3/2 but not above3/2. In particular, a uniform second
moment of the raw return reward or its particle-time is false. A bounded first
moment of computational work does not certify a bounded sampling variance.

The pure three-walker ring gives a sharper diagnostic. Put a+b+c=n,
h=abc/n, and q2=ab+bc+ca. Exact discrete identities are

  D[abc*q2/n]=12h-1,
  D[abc*(q2+1)/(12n)]=h.

At packed gaps(1,1,n-2), the latter potential equals
(n-2)(n-1)/(6n), which grows linearly with n. Thus an h-Doob transform that
transfers long excursions from reward variance to path duration does not have
uniform expected cost even in the pure three-walker case. This is a useful
failure, not a hidden assumption.

6. Governing prior, actually read

Balazs and Nagy, "Dependent Double Branching Annihilating Random Walk,"
Electronic Journal of Probability20(2015),84, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.00739 .
Their section1 describes the same parity-preserving neighbor-branching and
annihilation mechanism, its alternating charges, and its equivalence to a
voter/exclusion interface. Positive recurrence modulo translation is prior
machinery; their cited predecessors are Belitsky etal2001 and Sturm–Swart2008.

MacPhee, Menshikov, Volkov and Wade, "Passage-time moments and hybrid zones
for the exclusion-voter model," Bernoulli16(2010),1312–1342,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.0392 . Theorem1(ii) supplies the pure-voter3/2 moment
boundary. Theorem1(iii) includes recurrence of the symmetric exclusion mixture.
Their higher-moment Theorem6 requires exclusion parameter p>1/2. Our symmetric
branching corresponds to p=1/2, so that theorem is not an acquired higher-moment
bound for this route. No claim that this gap remains open in all later
literature is made.

The preceding first-moment argument alone does not prove arbitrary-accuracy
response costs. The following acquired construction addresses that gap without
asserting a false raw-excursion second moment.

7. Positive quintic bridge: complete ordinary proof

For any odd k, let F_m(g) be the sum of products of m distinct gaps whose
successive cyclic index spacings are all odd; m is odd. In particular h=F3/n.
Put Q=F5/n, setting F5=0 when k<5. Its coefficients are nonnegative.

Every F_m has the correct collision boundary: at a zero gap, terms containing
that gap vanish. Its two neighboring gaps have identical coefficients in the
remaining terms and never appear together. Merging them therefore gives
exactly F_m on the k-2 defect state. This includes the transition from5 to3,
where F5 becomes zero.

The hopping identity follows by differentiating the multilinear polynomial:

  DQ=(k-3)h/2.                                         (3)

Indeed the contribution of transfers across an adjacent gap pair i,i+1 is
partial_i partial_(i+1)F5. For a fixed alternating triple, its three cyclic
index arcs have odd lengths ell1,ell2,ell3 summing k. An adjacent pair can be
inserted into such an arc in (ell-1)/2 parity-compatible ways. Summing the
three arcs gives (k-3)/2.

For branching, the exact all-k identity is

  sum_i [F5(B_i g)-F5(g)]
     =3 F3(g)-2 sum_(i<j) c_(j-i) g_i g_j +(k-1)n/2,  (4)

where c_d=d/2 for even d and c_d=(k-d)/2 for odd d. These c_d also count the
alternating gap triples containing a specified pair. In particular c_d>=1.

Here is a coefficient proof of (4), including the cancellations rather than
assuming them from finite tests. Let

  T_z(x)=[[zx,1],[1,0]].

Then tr product_i T_z(g_i)=sum_(m odd) z^m F_m(g): choosing a gap contribution
occupies one index, while each unselected index must be paired with another;
equivalently successive selected indices have odd cyclic spacing. Branching
replaces adjacent gaps [a,b] by [a-1,1,1,b-1]. Its exact local matrix difference is

  T(a-1)T(1)T(1)T(b-1)-T(a)T(b)
    = [[-z^2+z^4(a-1)(b-1), z^3(a-1)],
       [ z^3(b-1),                  z^2]].             (5)

Thus the change in the z^5 coefficient has gap degree at most3; degrees4 and5
already cancel locally. Write the remaining ordered outside gaps in positions
1,...,k-2. The z^3 coefficient of their upper-left matrix entry is the sum
over odd-even-odd triples; the lower-right uses even-odd-even triples. The z^2
off-diagonal entries use odd-even or even-odd pairs. The z coefficient in the
upper-left entry is the sum of odd-position gaps. Equation(5) then gives the
following counts when summed around the ring.

* Cubic monomials: a nonalternating triple contributes zero, by the parity
  conditions just stated. For an alternating triple, let m be the number of
  its three index arcs of length1. Branch pairs containing exactly one selected
  gap contribute6-2m. Those containing two contribute m. Pairs containing none
  have alternating signs on each nontrivial odd arc, with sum-1 per such arc,
  contributing-(3-m). The total is3.

* Quadratic monomials: for a pair at cyclic index distance d, there are
  2c_d-2 interior cuts and2 endpoint cuts, all with coefficient-1. The total
  coefficient is-2c_d. The interior count is d-2 for even d and k-d-2 for
  odd d, from the even-length arc on which the branch cut can lie.

* Linear monomials: each gap occupies an allowed odd outside position for
  exactly(k-1)/2 branch cuts. There is no constant term.

These are all terms, proving(4). Zero-gap collision continuity makes the same
identity valid when a branch also annihilates particles.

Since every integer gap is at least1,
sum_(i<j)g_i g_j >=(k-1)n/2. Therefore(4) implies the useful uniform inequality

  sum_i [Q(B_i S)-Q(S)] <=3h.                          (6)

8. A positive quartic potential with its branching drift acquired

Give the ordered defects alternating charges q_i as before. Define

  U(S)=sum_(i<j) q_i q_j V(x_j-x_i),
  V(d)=d^5/(60n)-d^4/24+(n^2-1)d^3/(36n)+d^2/24.

Linear terms in V are irrelevant because sum_(i<j)qi qj dij=0. The symmetry
V(d)+V(n-d)=constant ensures independence of the cyclic cut. Annihilating
coincident opposite charges cancel, so U has the same collision interfaces.
The exact second difference is

  Delta^2 V(d)=-W(d)+(n/6)d,

with W from section3. Therefore

  D U=h.                                              (7)

The pure hopping process reaches a singleton in finite mean time, as section2
already proves. Its finite-state stopped Poisson identity gives
U(S)=E_S integral_0^tau h(S_t)dt>=0. Thus positivity is earned, despite the
signed charge formula. No numerical inverse is supplied.

The branch identity is equally explicit:

  sum_i[U(B_iS)-U(S)]=k u0-2h,
  u0=(n-2)(n-1)/(6n).                                 (8)

For a general pair kernel, branching gives a new internal contribution
V(2)-2V(1), plus the second difference against every external charge. Here
V(2)-2V(1)=u0. Summing the external contributions gives-2h; their linear
distance terms cancel. This proves(8).

On a three-defect state,
U=abc(ab+bc+ca+1)/(12n), agreeing with the independent calculation in section5.
In particular U(packed3)=u0 and Q(packed3)=0.

9. Explicit all-order elementary-work supersolution

Let d=gamma(1-b), epsilon=(d-b)/3+2b/n>0 as in section4. From(3),(6),(7),(8),

  K_int U=(d+2b)h-b u0 k,
  K_int Q >=[d(k-3)/2-3b]h.

The nonnegative, explicitly acquired function

  Wcost=(3U+2Q)/d + [3b u0/(d epsilon)]h

therefore satisfies the POINTWISE inequality

  K_int Wcost >= k h.                                 (9)

No limit interchange or unknown high moment is needed. At the packed start,

  Wcost0=3u0/d * [1+b h0/epsilon], h0=(n-2)/n.          (10)

For the killed target dual H=K_int+mu k, H Wcost>=k h too. A Doob transform
by positive h has transition rates q_xy h(y)/h(x) and terminal killing rate
(Hh)(x)/h(x). Since Hh>=epsilon k, the transform is a true absorbing process.
The original target-dual diagonal is exactly k in the normalized time units.
The transformed expected number of elementary event attempts is consequently

  E_h[event count] = [H^-1(kh)](packed3)/h0
                     <=Wcost0/h0=O(n).

At b=1/3 and gamma tending1 the explicit upper bound is asymptotic to3n.
This is linear in physical size, with no n^2 critical waiting-time factor.

For any acquired nonnegative reward r(S)<=k, the terminal-score representation

  [H^-1 r](packed3)=h0 E_h[r(S_terminal)/(Hh)(S_terminal)]

has score in[0,h0/epsilon]. Thus it has a uniform variance bound. This pays for
that bound with the proved O(n) transformed event count, and does not assert a
finite variance for raw excursions. The root combines two such bounded scores
in the exact regenerative response formula and supplies finite confidence and
finite arithmetic handling in its implementation.

10. Per-event ratios do not hide a growing rejection cost

For k>=3, h>=h0=(n-2)/n. To prove this, minimize F3 over integer gaps with fixed
sum and gaps>=1. Varying any pair gives a concave quadratic, since all multilinear
coefficients are nonnegative. A minimum therefore occurs with one gap n-k+1
and all others1. There F3=(k^2-1)(3n-2k)/24, increasing for3<=k<=n. The minimum
is attained at k=3, proving the claim.

A hopping move increases one gap by1 and decreases another by1. Each positive
monomial in F3 can increase by at most factor2. Collision continuity preserves
this bound, hence h(next)/h<=2 for a hop.

For a branch, the exact formula in section3 has
S_i=sum_j q_j|x_j-x_i| in[0,n]: it is a sum of selected positive gaps, at most
the unwrapped span. Thus |Delta_i h|<=1+2/n, and

  h(next)/h <=1+(n+2)/(n-2)=2n/(n-2)<=4, for n>=4.

These bounds permit constant-envelope rejection for transformed transitions.
The root earns the remaining particle-location/rank and aggregate update costs
through an ordered tree; this note does not substitute a constant-cost array
operation for that construction.

11. Important polynomial prior and the actual new interface

The cubic F3 and quintic F5 are established Herman-protocol polynomials, not
new objects. Bruna, Grigore, Kiefer, Ouaknine and Worrell, "Proving the
Herman-Protocol Conjecture," ICALP2016, article104, defines them and proves
their collision continuity and synchronous annihilating-token drift bounds.
The governing source was read, including Lemmas3–5 and8:
https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol055-icalp2016/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.104/LIPIcs.ICALP.2016.104.pdf .
Its protocol has synchronous one-direction-or-stay token moves and no target
branching operation. The present acquired interface is the exact neighboring
branch drift(4), the explicit U drift(8), and their positive combination(9)
which controls elementary work for the physical target's all-order dual.
No priority claim beyond this derivation has been established.
