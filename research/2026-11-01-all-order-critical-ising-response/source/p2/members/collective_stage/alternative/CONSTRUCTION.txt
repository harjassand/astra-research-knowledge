AN ALL-ORDER SIGNED OBSERVABLE DUAL WITH ACQUIRED VARIANCE
1 November 2026

Status
------
This is a positive alternative to deterministic interaction-word enumeration.
Its governing inverse-sampling operation is classical Neumann--Ulam machinery.
The contribution constructed here is its explicit observable-space realization,
source-aware relative second-moment proof, exact-integer physical implementation,
and a collective nonlinear kinetic test. Historical novelty is NOT established.
Common-equilibrium bounded contrast alone does not imply the absolute-row
condition required here. The root's support-cone method can cover cases this
signed dual cannot.

1. Algebraic algorithm and proof
-------------------------------
Let K=D+E be real symmetric and positive definite on an explicitly represented
mean-zero observable basis. D=diag(d_s), d_s>0. Assume an ACQUIRED nonnegative
symmetric component majorant B of E obeys

    |E_st| <= B_st,       sum_t B_st <= q d_s,       0<q<1.

One may retain separately signed components whose absolute values sum to B_st.
The matrix need not be written in full: row components, d_s and source coefficients
must be calculable from the current basis label at the cost charged below.

Set P_st=B_st/(q d_s), with a cemetery state for missing row mass. Then P is
substochastic and d_s P_st=d_t P_ts. The signed component on a sampled transition
has sign -sign(E_component). Its expectation realizes -D^-1 E/q, including any
cancellations among components.

For any nonzero finitely represented source f, define

    chi0 = sum_s f_s^2/d_s,       p0(s)=f_s^2/(d_s chi0).

Choose s from p0 and follow exactly j P-steps. If killed, output zero. If it ends
at t, output

    X_j = chi0 q^j sign(path) d_s f_t/(f_s d_t).

The source coefficient f_t must be obtained from its actual representation.
The starting f_s is nonzero by construction. Expanding the expectation proves

    E X_j = f^T (-D^-1 E)^j D^-1 f.

There is a useful dimension- and stiffness-independent SECOND-MOMENT calculation:

    E X_j^2
      = chi0 q^(2j) sum_t (f_t^2/d_t^2) sum_s d_s (P^j)_st
      <= chi0 q^(2j) sum_t f_t^2/d_t
      = chi0^2 q^(2j).

The inequality uses d-reversibility and substochastic row sums. It does not assume
that signs cancel harmlessly or that every trajectory has a bounded weight.

The symmetric Schur bound gives

    ||D^-1/2 E D^-1/2|| <= q,
    chi0/(1+q) <= chi=f^T K^-1 f <= chi0/(1-q).

Sample J with Pr(J=j)=(1-q)q^j, and return X_J/Pr(J). It is unbiased for chi, and

    E Z^2 <= chi0^2/(1-q)^2,
    E Z^2/chi^2 <= ((1+q)/(1-q))^2.

Thus ordinary means or median-of-means provide a complete confidence algorithm.
The sample cost is polynomial in inverse requested accuracy and 1/(1-q), without
an inverse-gap or system-size variance factor. Initial source acquisition and
lookup are charged separately. Clock ratios still cost their encoding bit length.
If leading coefficients are evaluated exactly, the same estimator can start at
any order h, with remainder second moment <=chi0^2 q^(2h)/(1-q)^2.

2. Local spin realization; no full observable matrix
---------------------------------------------------
Take uniform equilibrium spins sigma_i in {-1,1}, with flip rates

    w_i(sigma)=nu_i [1+sum_R a_iR product_(j in R) sigma_j],

where i is not in R, all R have bounded cardinality r, and

    sum_R |a_iR| <= q <1.

Each factor is invariant under the spin it flips, so uniform equilibrium and
detailed balance hold. Rates are between (1-q)nu_i and (1+q)nu_i. There may be
extensive nonlinear terms, on arbitrary explicitly specified interaction graphs.

Use orthonormal Walsh observables phi_S=product_(i in S)sigma_i. The empty set is
the stationary constant and is excluded. The physical positive generator obeys

    K phi_S = d_S phi_S
              + 2 sum_(i in S) nu_i sum_R a_iR phi_(S xor R),
    d_S=2 sum_(i in S)nu_i.

Because i is absent from R, i remains in S xor R; no nonempty source jumps to the
stationary constant. A component coefficient is symmetric under S<->S xor R.
Its absolute row sum is at most q d_S, acquired directly from the finite local
rate coefficients. These formulas earn D, row sampling and the majorant.

One step chooses a current-support site i with probability proportional to nu_i,
then a local Fourier term with weight |a_iR|/q; remaining mass kills the walk.
Toggle only R and track its sign. After j steps, support size is at most
|S_initial|+rj. No choice over all n physical sites or all perturbation words is
required. With elementary support lists, expected work is polynomial in r and
1/(1-q). A general local coefficient table can be Fourier transformed once at
cost O(2^r r), and that input cost is charged. Large graph adjacency lists cost
their full input size to acquire; a concise periodic formula needs no hidden list.

Finite precision: rational nu and a permit exact random choices using integer
random-bit rejection. The clock ratio appears through integer bit lengths, not
the number of physical events. The general estimator may have large weights,
but their second moment is bounded as above. Source coefficients must have finite
effective encodings. No unknown basis transform, spectral data or source norm is
supplied by an observer.

3. Collective executed instance
-------------------------------
For even n>=8 on a periodic chain, use

    w_i=nu_i [1+(9/20)sigma_(i-2)sigma_(i+2)
                -(3/20)sigma_(i-1)sigma_(i+1)],
    nu_even=1/B,       nu_odd=1,       B=10^40.

All n rates contain nonlinear interactions. The uniform equilibrium has 2^n
states. q=3/5 and contrast [2/5,8/5] are exact. The source is

    A=(n/2)^(-1/2) sum_(i even) sigma_i,

so Var(A)=1 and chi0=B/2. It overlaps all of the slow sublattice, whose own
distance-two nonlinear kinetic interactions remain extensive. The fast variables
are also coupled into the rates; they are not omitted in the algorithm.

E maps a singleton Walsh support to a three-site support. Therefore the first
inverse correction vanishes exactly for this source. The spectral Jensen
inequality consequently gives chi>=chi0. Start the random series at order two:

    Pr(J=j)=(2/5)(3/5)^(j-2), j>=2.

Initial support is a uniform even singleton. If the final support is an even
singleton, assign the product of transition signs; otherwise assign zero. Since
all nonzero source coefficients and endpoint clocks agree,

    chi/chi0 = 1+(9/10) E score,       score in {-1,0,1}.

EVERY order is included. This is not a low-order truncation. The exact zero
first correction is a control variate that improves variance.

Hoeffding gives, with N independent scores and a=9/10,

    Pr(|estimated chi/chi0 - chi/chi0| > delta)
        <=2 exp[-N delta^2/(2a^2)].

At N=64800 and delta=1/100 this is <=2exp(-4)<0.05. Since chi>=chi0, it is also
a one-percent RELATIVE response guarantee. The interval endpoints can be written
as exact rationals from integer score counts. There is no approximation in the
large-system sampling probabilities: use clock integer weights 1 and B, a 3/4
Fourier-term choice, and a 3/5 continuation probability.

Recorded n=4096 run: normalized response 1.076375, interval [1.066375,1.086375],
64800 samples, 227144 dual transitions, average order 3.5053, maximum observed
support 9, about 0.446 seconds. Hardware wall time is illustrative; transition
count and sampling guarantee are the portable quantities. This is an all-order
calculation of a model, not material calibration.

For n=8 and12, both B=1 and B=10^40 were checked against an explicitly assembled
full observable generator, symmetrically preconditioned and solved sparsely.
All four exact-solve values fall within the analytic sampling intervals. Those
small reference solves are floating point; the large-system estimator itself
uses exact integer choices and rational reported intervals.

4. Existing machinery, scope and a useful failure
------------------------------------------------
The decisive generic operation is classical Neumann--Ulam inverse sampling.
Ji, Mascagni and Li (2013), SIAM J Numer Anal 51,2107-2122, DOI
10.1137/130904867, analyze the second-moment matrix H*_ij=H_ij^2/P_ij. They show
that convergence of the deterministic Neumann series alone does not establish
finite variance; in particular, an absolute-kernel spectral radius exceeding one
can obstruct all standard transition choices. Primary full paper read:
https://www.cs.odu.edu/~yaohang/portfolio/SINUM2013.pdf

Our proof acquires a local absolute-kernel condition and a source-weighted scalar
variance bound. It does not create a new generic Monte Carlo inverse method.
The bounded source endpoint ratios make the executed example especially strong.

The absolute-row condition can fail while K remains uniformly comparable with D.
Local kinetic positivity constrains a rate function's values, whereas this dual
requires control of the sum of its Fourier coefficient magnitudes. These are
different norms. Thus this successful stochastic construction must not be promoted
to a solution of the full fixed-contrast problem. The root's deterministic support
cone construction is specifically valuable when it does not assume this l1 bound.

A concrete obstruction is A_i=1+(3/5)Maj(sigma_(i+1),sigma_(i+2),sigma_(i+3)),
where Maj(x,y,z)=(x+y+z-xyz)/2. Physical contrast remains exactly [.4,1.6], but
every nonempty Walsh row of |D^-1 E| sums to 6/5. Indeed, singleton Fourier terms
are all positive and triple terms are negative; different local terms leading to
the same XOR endpoint have the same sign. The constant vector is therefore a
Perron eigenvector with eigenvalue6/5. Standard Neumann--Ulam inverse sampling
cannot acquire finite variance by choosing a different transition matrix for
this splitting. Any positive diagonal rescaling also fails: its absolute row
sum is |1-x|+(6/5)x>1 for x=d_S/d'_S>0. This is not an impossibility claim for
all randomized algorithms, block resummations or non-diagonal preconditioners.

majority_abs_obstruction.py checks the exact row identity, the bounded symmetric
relative spectrum, and an independent physical-to-Walsh transform. This supplies
a discriminating positive-contrast family for the root's deterministic method.

Strong ordinary competitors for the concrete model include this same established
Neumann--Ulam route, few-scale elimination where its accuracy is acquired, and
local polynomial inverse iteration. The response is measured beyond the bare
reference, but no global historical novelty or civilisation-scale consequence is
claimed by this alternative.

5. Independent check of root's proposed support-cone principle
------------------------------------------------------------
For a product equilibrium and independent-refresh reference, D and all its powers
are diagonal in an orthogonal product-observable basis. They preserve each
monomial's support. A complete local reversible term annihilates the product
stationary vector, so it vanishes on a monomial with disjoint support. Each
application of D^-1/2 E D^-1/2 can therefore enlarge support only by a local update
neighborhood. After k applications, a source initially on a fixed finite support
lies in the radius-O(k) union of balls about that support. Merge equal resulting
subsets instead of enumerating histories.

For one-dimensional bounded-range geometry the number of such subsets is
2^O(k); for d-dimensional polynomial volume growth it is exp(O(k^d)). The source
can be a polynomial sum of local monomials: propagate each cone, or retain a
canonical aggregate dictionary, and pair with the original source. The global
positive scalar spectral theorem controls total relative error; there is no need
to sum independent local relative errors against possibly cancelling responses.

This argument does not require Fourier absolute-row contraction, and it does not
assume a low-rank solution tensor. Its main limitations are the product reference,
finite geometric growth, fixed contrast and source-support complexity. Claims
about arbitrary interacting equilibrium would need an earned reference inverse
that preserves or controllably localizes support; that property is not generic.
