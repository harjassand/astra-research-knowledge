## 1. The organising principle

The useful object is a **finite physical representation of an entanglement boundary**.

A Gaussian state may occupy infinitely many photon-number levels. Nevertheless, its separability across a fixed grouping of modes is controlled by a consistency condition on the quadratic coefficients of its Bargmann kernel. Local projection onto vacuum and one photon retains enough of those coefficients to preserve the answer exactly.

The important point is that this can be an operation on the **quantum state itself**. It does not require a preceding covariance estimate, a learned displacement correction, or a filter chosen for the particular input. The resulting finite system remains available for subsequent operations.

The failure flag has a precise mathematical role. It preserves a distinguishable coherent success sector even when unsuccessful events dominate. The lower-bound proof shows why a deterministic qubit output cannot universally absorb those events while retaining arbitrarily weak Gaussian entanglement.

This gives a fundamental dimension law. It does **not** preserve the amount of entanglement, produce an efficient universal readout, or establish a computational advantage.

## 2. The fixed channel and its exact guarantee

For one bosonic mode, define

\[
P_{01}=|0\rangle\langle0|+|1\rangle\langle1|.
\]

Let \(V_{01}\) identify this subspace with two levels of an output system, and reserve a third orthogonal state \(|e\rangle\) for erasure. For any \(0<\kappa\le1\), set

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_\kappa(X)
=
\kappa V_{01}P_{01}XP_{01}V_{01}^{\dagger}
+
\operatorname{Tr}\!\big[(I-\kappa P_{01})X\big]
|e\rangle\langle e|.
}
\]

This is completely positive and trace preserving. Its success Kraus operator is \(\sqrt{\kappa}\,V_{01}P_{01}\); the failure effect is \(I-\kappa P_{01}\). Every trial produces an output.

### Universal qualitative retention theorem

Let \(\rho\) be any state of finitely many bosonic modes with a Gaussian characteristic function, finite covariance and arbitrary finite first moments. For every fixed partition \(\Pi\) of the modes,

\[
\boxed{
\rho\in\operatorname{Sep}(\Pi)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_\kappa^{\otimes m}(\rho)
\in\operatorname{Sep}(\Pi).
}
\]

For every cut \(A|A^c\),

\[
\boxed{
\rho^{T_A}\succeq0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\big[\Phi_\kappa^{\otimes m}(\rho)\big]^{T_A}\succeq0.
}
\]

The same channel works for all these partitions simultaneously. Neither its implementation nor its parameters depend on the input’s unknown means.

“Per mode” matters. A party holding \(k\) modes retains dimension \(3^k\). The result does not compress an arbitrarily large party into one qutrit. Minimality concerns the smallest common bound on the local output dimension for this universal task.

The theorem also keeps **fixed-partition separability** distinct from mixtures over different partitions. I have not proved preservation of genuine multipartite entanglement or membership in a convex hull of varying separability partitions.

### Why retaining failures does not remove their cost

Projecting every output onto its success sector recovers

\[
\kappa^m V_{01}^{\otimes m}P\rho P
(V_{01}^{\dagger})^{\otimes m},
\qquad
P=P_{01}^{\otimes m}.
\]

Its weight is

\[
s=\kappa^m\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho)>0.
\]

Positivity is guaranteed because a finite Gaussian state has positive vacuum probability. There is, however, no uniform lower bound on \(s\). It can be extremely small as the number of modes, energy or displacement increases.

The complete channel is deterministic. Accessing its all-success qubit branch still costs \(1/s\) independent source attempts on average.

## 3. The hard converse: why the finite projection is faithful

Locality immediately proves that a separable input produces a separable output. The difficult direction is that the projection cannot erase all evidence of Gaussian entanglement across a fixed partition.

Write the Gaussian Bargmann kernel as

\[
F_\rho(z,w)
=
c\exp\!\left[
\ell^{\mathsf T}z+\ell^{*\mathsf T}w
+\frac12z^{\mathsf T}Nz
+z^{\mathsf T}Mw
+\frac12w^{\mathsf T}N^*w
\right],
\]

where \(M=M^\dagger\succeq0\), \(N=N^{\mathsf T}\), and \(c>0\). These are kernel coefficients, not ordinary physical second moments. This normal form has an established operator-theoretic treatment. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

For a centered state, retaining powers zero or one in each mode gives a finite qubit matrix \(Q(M,N)\). Its lowest entries include

\[
Q_{\varnothing,\varnothing}=1,\qquad
Q_{\{i\},\{j\}}=M_{ij},\qquad
Q_{\{i,j\},\varnothing}=N_{ij}.
\]

The exact condition is:

\[
\boxed{
Q(M,N)\in\operatorname{Sep}(\Pi)
\iff
\exists\,C=C^{\mathsf T}:
\begin{pmatrix}
M&C\\
C^*&M^*
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,
}
\]

with

\[
C_{ij}=N_{ij}
\quad\text{whenever \(i,j\) belong to different \(\Pi\)-blocks.}
\]

The entire within-party blocks of \(C\) are free. For full mode-by-mode separability, only its diagonal is free. This is the same kind of “one consistent classical covariance” requirement that made the complete mediation boundary stronger than separate cut tests.

The proof closes three distinct issues.

**First, separability of the finite state forces the completion.** Apply the bounded local attenuation

\[
F_\epsilon=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{diag}(1,\sqrt{\epsilon}).
\]

It maps \(Q(M,N)\) to \(Q(\epsilon M,\epsilon N)\). For a separable state, normal moments and cross-party anomalous moments admit a positive completed covariance: classical fluctuations of local means provide one part, and positive within-party quantum variances supply the remainder. Dividing by \(\epsilon\) and taking a bounded subsequence gives the displayed completion.

**Second, a feasible completion reconstructs an actual Gaussian product-state decomposition.** Draw a complex Gaussian vector \(\alpha\) with covariance and pseudocovariance \(M,C\), and put \(D=N-C\). Because \(D\) is block local, the entire functions

\[
f_{g,\alpha}(z_g)
=
\exp\!\left[
\frac12z_g^{\mathsf T}D_gz_g+\alpha_g^{\mathsf T}z_g
\right]
\]

factor across the chosen parties. Gaussian integration reproduces the parent kernel.

The crucial normalization step is earned. If \(b_k(\alpha)\) are their full Fock coefficients, then

\[
c\,\mathbb E\sum_k|b_k(\alpha)|^2
=
\sum_k\langle k|\rho|k\rangle
=1.
\]

Tonelli’s theorem therefore makes the vectors normalizable almost surely and supplies a normalized probability measure over genuine product states. There is no hidden assumption that an unbounded creation-operator exponential defines a physical vector.

**Third, unknown displacement does not require a physical correction.** The finite matrix obeys the exact identity

\[
Q_\ell=T_\ell Q_0T_\ell^\dagger,
\qquad
T_\ell=\bigotimes_i(I+\ell_i\sigma_i^+),
\]

with inverse \(\bigotimes_i(I-\ell_i\sigma_i^+)\). These are invertible local operators, so the separability and PPT answers are unchanged. This is a proof identity; the device does not need to know or apply \(T_\ell\).

The PPT statement follows by swapping the relevant ket and bra variables in the kernel. Its normal-block positivity machinery already appears in Wang–Matsumoto–Tomita. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

## 4. Why a deterministic qubit output cannot be universal

The lower bound applies to arbitrary fixed local quantum channels, including arbitrary internal local ancillas and operations.

### Lemma: pure outputs for every coherent input force a constant qubit channel

Suppose a bosonic-to-qubit channel maps every coherent state to a pure qubit. In a Stinespring dilation,

\[
U|z))
\in\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathcal E,
\qquad
|z))=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle,
\]

the output must be a product vector for every complex \(z\).

No two output qubit rays can be orthogonal, because

\[
((z|w))=e^{z^*w}\ne0.
\]

Choose one reference output as \(|0\rangle\). The dilation can then be written

\[
U|z))=|0\rangle u(z)+|1\rangle v(z),
\qquad
v(z)=h(z)u(z),
\]

where \(u(z)\) never vanishes. Local ratios of analytic environment components show that \(h\) is entire.

If \(h\) were nonconstant, its image would contain a disk around the reference value zero. Any sufficiently large attained value would have its orthogonal qubit ratio inside that disk, contradicting the absence of orthogonal output rays. Hence \(h\) is bounded. Liouville’s theorem makes it constant.

Taking Taylor coefficients shows that every input Fock vector has the same output qubit factor. The channel is a constant pure-state replacer.

### Weak Gaussian entanglement then defeats every other qubit encoder

Take any two fixed bosonic-to-qubit channels \(\Gamma_A,\Gamma_B\). A replacer already fails the task. Otherwise the lemma supplies coherent inputs whose outputs \(\tau_A,\tau_B\) are mixed qubits and therefore full rank. Let

\[
a=\lambda_{\min}(\tau_A)>0,\qquad
b=\lambda_{\min}(\tau_B)>0.
\]

Use the appropriately displaced two-mode squeezed vacuum

\[
|\psi_\lambda\rangle
=
[D(\alpha)\otimes D(\beta)]
\sqrt{1-\lambda^2}
\sum_{n\ge0}\lambda^n|n,n\rangle.
\]

It is entangled for every \(\lambda>0\), but its trace-norm distance from the chosen coherent product is exactly \(2\lambda\). Channel contractivity gives

\[
\|\omega_\lambda-\tau_A\otimes\tau_B\|_1\le2\lambda.
\]

Partial transpose preserves Hilbert–Schmidt norm, so

\[
\lambda_{\min}(\omega_\lambda^{T_B})
\ge ab-2\lambda.
\]

For \(0<\lambda<ab/2\), the output is PPT. In a two-qubit system, that means separable. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Thus **every fixed pair of qubit encoders loses some entangled Gaussian input**. The flagged three-level construction meets this obstruction exactly.

The lower bound uses arbitrary finite displacements. It does not establish the same minimum under a centered-only promise, state-adaptive encoding, multiple-copy encoding, general correlated LOCC encoding, or an uncounted external herald register.
