The result: three retained local levels suffice, and two cannot

I selected and resolved a new physical representation problem exposed by the mediation boundary:

What is the smallest fixed local output that can preserve the separability structure of every Gaussian quantum state, without first learning its covariance or displacement?

Three retained levels per mode suffice: a coherent vacuum–one-photon qubit sector and an orthogonal erasure flag. The same deterministic local channel preserves separability across every fixed partition of the modes, and PPT versus NPT across every cut. A matching impossibility theorem shows that no fixed pair of deterministic bosonic-to-qubit channels can preserve entanglement for all two-mode Gaussian inputs with arbitrary finite displacement.

I constructed the optical instrument, an explicit finite source, its measurement interface, and the complete output including failures. The result is a mathematical construction with executable synthetic validation; no laboratory experiment was performed.

1. The organising principle

The useful object is a finite physical representation of an entanglement boundary.

A Gaussian state may occupy infinitely many photon-number levels. Nevertheless, its separability across a fixed grouping of modes is controlled by a consistency condition on the quadratic coefficients of its Bargmann kernel. Local projection onto vacuum and one photon retains enough of those coefficients to preserve the answer exactly.

The important point is that this can be an operation on the quantum state itself. It does not require a preceding covariance estimate, a learned displacement correction, or a filter chosen for the particular input. The resulting finite system remains available for subsequent operations.

The failure flag has a precise mathematical role. It preserves a distinguishable coherent success sector even when unsuccessful events dominate. The lower-bound proof shows why a deterministic qubit output cannot universally absorb those events while retaining arbitrarily weak Gaussian entanglement.

This gives a fundamental dimension law. It does not preserve the amount of entanglement, produce an efficient universal readout, or establish a computational advantage.
