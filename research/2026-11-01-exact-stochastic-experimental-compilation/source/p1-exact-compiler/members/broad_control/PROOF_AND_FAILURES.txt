SUPPORT-ONLY REACHABILITY CERTIFICATES: A STRONG CONVENTIONAL COMPARATOR

Status: elementary constructive lemma, numerical/exact-rational executable
check, and decisive acquisition/execution separation. Not proposed as the new
main Astra capability: qualitative model checking and support learning already
give much of this power. No priority claim.

1. Desired operation and strongest conventional route

From imperfect transition observations, acquire reusable knowledge supporting
later infinite-horizon reachability and physical reset/steering questions. The
strong conventional route is learned support plus qualitative/robust Markov
decision process model checking, not a finely estimated transition matrix.

Primary references:
* P. Ashok, J. Kretinsky, M. Weininger, PAC Statistical Model Checking for Markov
  Decision Processes and Stochastic Games, CAV 2019, arXiv:1905.04403v3.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04403
  It treats unbounded reachability with a known lower bound on nonzero
  transitions, and also with known graph topology.
* S. Akshay, S. Bansal, G. Mahla, Certifying Almost-Sure Reachability in Unknown
  MDPs, arXiv:2610.18119v1, October 2026.
  https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18119v1
  Their certificate-transfer route computes hitting times of an estimated
  model and calibrates transition probabilities finely enough to transfer
  drift. Its worst-case sufficient calibration can be exponential in graph
  depth. The elementary support construction below avoids that calibration
  for producing some valid certificate, without estimating expected hitting
  times accurately. This does not refute a lower bound for estimating those
  hitting times or verifying an arbitrary candidate to a prescribed margin.
* PRISM official documentation, Uncertain Models:
  https://www.prismmodelchecker.org/manual/PropertySpecification/UncertainModels
  Robust interval-MDP verification already handles adversarial probability
  choices and policies; this is a strong executable conventional comparator.

2. Acquiring support

Known finite labelled state set S, |S|=n, known action labels, at most a actions
per state. A local generative request (s,u) returns a true successor distributed
as P(.|s,u). All nonzero transitions are known a priori to have probability at
least rational p in (0,1]. This is a structural observation-resolution promise,
not something finite observations certify. Trials at a requested row are fresh.

Draw m=ceil(p^{-1} log(n^2 a/delta)) samples from every state-action row, and keep
only the observed successor sets. A fixed positive edge is missed with probability
at most exp(-pm). There are at most n^2 a edges. A union bound gives exact support
recovery with probability >=1-delta. Number of source calls is at most nam.
Retained support is at most n^2 a bits/indices, and ordinary graph algorithms
operate on it. On this one event all later support-only questions, including
adaptively selected policies and targets, are correct. There is no per-query
statistical union bound after support acquisition.

This API does NOT earn access to each state-action row. Its preparation, reset,
state-label sensing, and physical transition durations have to be supplied and
charged separately. Section 6 proves why this qualification matters.

3. Explicit robust ranking certificate without a linear solver

Fix a later memoryless deterministic policy pi and a later nonempty target T.
In the acquired policy support graph, let d(s) be shortest directed distance to
T. If any state has infinite distance, a globally valid finite ranking
supermartingale of the kind below cannot exist. If all distances are finite,
put D=max_s d(s). For 0<p<1 define

    W_d = (1-p^d)/[(1-p)p^D],       V(s)=W_{d(s)}.

For p=1 use V(s)=d(s). For D=0 all states are targets and V=0 suffices.

Claim: For EVERY transition matrix with exactly this acquired policy support
and every positive transition at least p,

    V(s) >= 1 + E[V(X_next)|X=s],  s outside T,
    V(s)=0,                       s in T.

Proof. At every non-target state of distance d, a shortest path begins with a
successor of distance d-1. Its probability is at least p. All other successor
values are at most W_D. Since W_{d-1}<=W_D,

    E[V(X_next)|s] <= p W_{d-1} + (1-p) W_D.

Direct algebra gives

    W_d - p W_{d-1} - (1-p)W_D = 1.

This proves the claim. At p=1 each row has a single positive successor and its
distance is exactly d-1. Stop the chain on T; summing drift up to a finite
stopping horizon gives E[min(t,tau_T)] <= V(s). Monotone convergence gives
E[tau_T]<=V(s), hence almost-sure reachability. No unknown probabilities enter
the construction or its verification.

Finite-bit construction. If p=A/B is given in binary, W_d has a rational
representation with O(D(log A+log B)+log D) bits. Its numerical magnitude can be
exponential in D while its description and exact arithmetic are polynomial.
Breadth-first search acquires all d(s) in O(n+e) graph operations, followed by
ordinary integer/rational arithmetic. Floating subtraction of nearly equal
large V values can erase the unit drift; exact rational output avoids that
problem rather than treating a floating experiment as a rigorous certificate.

4. Policy synthesis when every state can reach the target in the union graph

Suppose every state has some action path to T. Compute shortest distances in
the union of action-labelled edges. At every non-target s choose an action
having at least one successor with distance d(s)-1. Other successors can have
any finite distance. The identical proof supplies a robust finite hitting-time
certificate for this constructed policy. This condition includes strongly
connected action-union graphs and every nonempty target in them. More general
winning subsets require the standard almost-sure reachability graph algorithm
to remove actions that leave the winning set; no claim of a new planning
algorithm is made.

5. Executed exact checks

Run: python3 broad_control/support_certificates.py
Results: support_results.json.

The source fixture has states 0,...,30, target 30, and one action. At s<30 it
moves to s+1 with probability 1/10, and otherwise returns to 0. State 30 is
absorbing. The learner gets only successor labels from source calls, recovers
support, constructs its ranking function, and verifies the worst compatible
row using exact rational arithmetic. It then executes 200 later target queries
on the retained support, without additional source calls. Additional random
support tests check the maximum admissible row expectation exactly, rather than
checking only one sampled choice of probabilities.

6. Decisive failure: cheap knowledge of reset possibility is not cheap reset

For the progress-reset chain in section 5, reaching state D requires D
consecutive forward successes. Its exact expected hitting time from state i is

    H_i=(1-p^(D-i))/[(1-p)p^D].

Verify H_D=0 and H_i=1+p H_{i+1}+(1-p)H_0. Thus H_0=W_D and the support-only
bound is sharp. At p=1/10,D=30, the actual expected physical time is about
1.111...e30 transitions. There is only one action, so no learner, controller,
reset strategy, better representation, or stronger inference solver can reduce
that physical execution cost. The source can have a 100-bit exact certificate
and still lack feasible physical reset.

If observations come from one uninterrupted trajectory, sampling a row near
the target is itself exponentially costly in this family. The generative
row-access abstraction would have hidden precisely the missing experimental
operation. This is why merely replacing Hoeffding calibration with support
learning does not complete the user's desired broader bridge.

7. Other routes considered and rejected as principal destinations

Reciprocal/bridge-class learning: path likelihoods modulo endpoint Doob gauges
are a natural representation invariant under bridge conditioning. But the core
equivalence and Schrödinger-bridge machinery are established. Learning such a
class does not automatically provide accessible bridge sampling or efficient
control; assuming the bridge oracle would import the desired operation.
Primary starting reference: Conforti and Leonard, Reciprocal Classes of Random
Walks on Graphs, SPA 2017, DOI 10.1016/j.spa.2016.09.012.

Shared tractability via polymorphisms: finite-valued CSP languages with binary
symmetric fractional polymorphisms are already solved exactly by BLP; ordinary
constraint-table acquisition and that theorem cover the naive learned-planning
endpoint. See Thapper and Zivny, The Complexity of Finite-Valued CSPs, JACM 2016,
DOI 10.1145/2974019, and the constructive BLP characterization arXiv:1207.7213.
Neither this route nor the support lemma alone is a new major capability.

Scientific lesson retained: the needed empirical accuracy depends on the
operation's invariant, not on the magnitude of an intermediate quantity.
Qualitative support may certify an infinite future with polynomial information
and arithmetic even when probability-fitting suggests an exponential bill.
But certificates, numerical answers, and physical execution have distinct costs.
