EXACT EXPERIMENTAL SAMPLING BY AN ADOPTED SHIFTED-REJECTION ROUTE

Status: constructive synthesis of established Bernoulli-factory components and
ordinary positive-weight sampling. This is a strong conventional comparator
for the proposed broader Astra exactification compiler. It covers bounded-width
positive factor models and monotone positive arithmetic circuits. It does not
establish a generic compiler for signed arithmetic formulas whose resulting
polynomials happen to have nonnegative coefficients.

Primary components:
[DHK N] Dughmi, Hartline, Kleinberg, Niazadeh, Bernoulli Factories and Black-Box
Reductions in Mechanism Design, STOC 2017 / JACM 2021, arXiv:1703.04143.
Author manuscript: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdk/papers/stoc17-rad.pdf
The empirical-maximum-in-expectation shift and exponential Bernoulli race are
established machinery. The key is that the shift coin's MEAN, not every noisy
empirical realization, has the deterministic required approximation property.
[Huber] Nearly optimal Bernoulli factories for linear functions, CPC 2016,
arXiv:1308.1562; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.1562 .
For input coin bias b, known c>1 and cb<=1-epsilon, the constructive scalar
factory uses <=9.5 c/epsilon input flips in expectation. Its simple algorithm
is implemented in shifted_rejection_demo.py; it is not an unknown solver.

1. Contract

There are M labelled primitive Bernoulli experiments with unknown fixed biases
p_i in [0,1]. Fresh chosen-label experiments are independent. Set
z_i=a+(1-a)p_i, where rational a in (0,1) is known. A later query has weights

    w(x)=L(x) product_{j=1}^m z_{i_j(x)},

where L(x) is a known zero-one constraint, and repeated factor occurrences are
allowed. Suppose the query's structural representation permits polynomial-time
exact sampling from the same weights with arbitrary supplied positive rational
numbers hat z_i. This numeric sampling routine must be constructed, not supplied
as an oracle. For bounded combined primal treewidth and fixed alphabet, ordinary
sum-product variable elimination with stored backward conditionals does this.
The combined constraint graph counts in the width. Finding a decomposition can
be paid using the earlier complete width search or a conventional FPT algorithm.
If L is infeasible, the rational sum-product computation detects that fact.

Knowledge of true scopes (or valid superscopes) is required for this instance.
Exact omitted dependencies are not identifiable from finite noisy data without
an additional promise. The earlier approximate-junta theorem alone cannot grant
an exact hidden factor graph. Every physical preparation/reset cost per primitive
experiment remains charged; this theorem gives no physical reset synthesis.

2. A bounded log coin using only primitive experiments

Let C=(1-a)/a; then -log a <= C. Given label i, generate K~Geom(a) on {1,2,...}.
With known probability 1/K, proceed and return 1 iff K fresh p_i coins are all
zero; otherwise return 0 immediately. The output mean is

 sum_{k>=1} a(1-a)^(k-1) (1-p_i)^k/k
 = -log(a+(1-a)p_i)/C =: ell_i.

All auxiliary distributions have known rational parameters. The expected number
of primitive flips is at most E[(1/K)K]=1, because the 1/K decision precedes the
physical flips and the all-zero test can stop at its first one. Internal geometric
sampling costs O(1/a) simple auxiliary trials; faster known methods are optional.

3. A learned rational proposal

Use fresh counts to estimate all p_i, then set hat z_i=a+(1-a)hat p_i. This always
lies in [a,1], regardless of estimation error. Define

    hat w(x)=L(x) product_j hat z_{i_j(x)},
    r(x)=log[w(x)/hat w(x)] on L(x)=1.

Choose enough pilot samples for all |p_i-hat p_i|<=a/[2m(1-a)] with probability
at least .9. Hoeffding and a union bound suffice with

    K_pilot=ceil(log(20M)/(2 eta_pilot^2)),
    eta_pilot=min(1,a/[2m(1-a)]).

Then all local |log z_i-log hat z_i|<=1/(2m), hence |r(x)|<=1/2 simultaneously
over every feasible assignment. Pilot cost is polynomial in M,m,1/a. Crucially,
correctness never assumes that this good-pilot event occurred.

Compute and retain the exact rational proposal pi_hat=hat w/sum hat w using the
actual bounded-width sum-product algorithm. Tiny evidence probability has no
rejection cost here: L is incorporated in the finite dynamic program.

4. Residual coins and an upper-envelope coin; no optimization oracle

Let B=max(mC,3), lambda=2B, epsilon=1/lambda. For every x define

    v_x=1/2+r(x)/(2B) in [0,1].

This coin is executable. Put h_i=-log(hat z_i)/C in [0,1]. With probability
alpha=mC/B choose an occurrence j uniformly and then, with equal probabilities,
return either a complement of an ell_{i_j(x)} coin or a known h_{i_j(x)} coin.
With remaining probability 1-alpha return a fair coin. The resulting mean is v_x.

Instead of maximizing r over exponentially many assignments, use the bound

    U(p)=m max(0,max_i log(z_i/hat z_i)),
    v_bar=(B+U(p))/(2B).

It obeys v_bar>=max_x v_x and v_bar<=1. On the good pilot U<=1/2, so U-r(x)<=1
for every feasible x. This is a coordinate bound, not a combinatorial solver.

Generate a fresh noisy estimate of every p_i for EACH upper-envelope coin. Set
tilde z_i=a+(1-a)tilde p_i and calculate the corresponding tilde v_bar. Choose

    eta_shift=min(1,a/[8m(1-a)]),
    K_shift=ceil(log(4M/epsilon)/(2 eta_shift^2)).

With probability >=1-epsilon/2 all coordinate errors are <=eta_shift. Then
|U(tilde p)-U(p)|<=1/8 and |tilde v_bar-v_bar|<=epsilon/8. Round the computed
known logarithms sufficiently finely that the final rational, clipped value
v_tilde differs from tilde v_bar by at most epsilon/8. Flip a known rational
v_tilde coin. Its unconditional mean z satisfies

    |z-v_bar| <= epsilon/4 + epsilon/2 < epsilon.

This is a deterministic statement about the mean of an executable subroutine,
valid for every possible pilot. It does NOT make a high-probability empirical
bound into an exact promise. Fresh calls return iid z-coins. Cost per z-coin is
O(M K_shift) primitive flips plus polynomial arithmetic on M rational/log values.

5. Exact acceptance from conventional linear and exponential factories

Let

    u_x=(1-2epsilon)(v_x+1-z).

Because z>=v_bar-epsilon>=v_x-epsilon,

    0<=u_x<=(1-2epsilon)(1+epsilon)<=1-epsilon.

Form a coin of bias b_x=(v_x+1-z)/2 by a fair mixture, and apply the Huber linear
factory with scale c=2(1-2epsilon) and gap epsilon. This yields exactly u_x.

Set lambda'=lambda/(1-2epsilon)<=1.5lambda. Draw N~Poisson(lambda'); return
acceptance iff N independent u_x coins are all one. The mean acceptance is

    A(x)=exp(lambda'(u_x-1))
        =exp(lambda v_x+lambda(1-z)-lambda')
        =c_pilot * exp(r(x)),

where c_pilot=exp(B+lambda(1-z)-lambda') is independent of x. Drawing x from
pi_hat and accepting with this coin thus returns the EXACT target law
w/sum w, for every pilot and without knowing c_pilot, the true weights, or the
normalizing constant.

On a good pilot,

 lambda v_x >= lambda v_bar -1,
 z <= v_bar+epsilon,

so

 log A(x) >= -1 + lambda(1-epsilon)-lambda'
          = -2 - 2/(1-2epsilon) >= -5

as epsilon<=1/6. The looser uniform constant A(x)>=exp(-8) is used below to
leave room for harmless conservative choices. On bad pilots acceptance may be
exponentially small, but each acceptance COIN still costs uniformly polynomial
expected flips: O(lambda^2(1+M K_shift)), using Huber and Poisson composition.

6. Capped stages remove bad-pilot runtime tails without bias

Try at most J proposals with a retained pilot (J is a fixed constant, e.g. 64 or
ceil(exp(8))). If no proposal was accepted, discard that pilot and learn a new
one, then repeat. Every accepted attempt under every pilot has the same target
law. Conditional on no earlier acceptance and on the current pilot, a successful
attempt's output distribution remains target. Therefore changing pilots after
failures leaves the output law exact.

A fresh pilot is good with probability at least .9. Conditional on a good pilot,
the stage succeeds with probability at least 1-(1-exp(-8))^J. Hence the number of
fresh stages is dominated by a geometric variable of a fixed positive parameter.
Each stage has J uniformly polynomial-cost acceptance trials plus polynomial
pilot/proposal setup. Total expected primitive flips and arithmetic operations
are polynomial in input/table size, m,1/a, and the numeric sampler's cost.

This argument holds after an arbitrary retained-pilot history: at most one
possibly bad retained stage is attempted, then a fresh stage has the same uniform
success guarantee. Consequently it gives a reusable exact service with uniform
conditional expected cost, not merely a prior-averaged runtime guarantee on a
single high-probability calibration event. Fresh random inputs yield iid outputs
for a fixed query. Adaptively chosen queries receive their exact requested
conditional law; the query description and combined width must remain within
the charged class.

7. Finite arithmetic

For rational a and counts, proposal weights are rational. Sum-product DP and
backward rational categoricals use polynomial bit lengths (products across m
factors add input bit lengths; sums add log state counts). The shift subroutine
uses only fixed-accuracy rational approximations to known logs, explicitly
budgeted above. Known h_i coins can be sampled exactly by comparing random binary
digits against successively refined directed rational log intervals; the expected
number of extra precision bits has geometric tails. Poisson with known rational
mean has conventional exact random-bit algorithms.

Huber's linear factory is explicit, not a real-probability oracle. Its arithmetic
can use rational C,epsilon at each stage and repeated known rational coins for
the stage survival probability (1+epsilon/2)^(-i), avoiding the formation of a
huge real exponent. For a conservative bit-complexity implementation one may
increase its stage cutoff constant, retaining polynomial cost while making the
probability of visiting stage k decay faster than any chosen polynomial factor
in the stage's rational operand size. The floating demonstration below does not
implement this entire directed-bit layer, and is not claimed bit-exact software.

8. Generality and the remaining generic compiler gap

The same route applies to a polynomial with nonnegative rational coefficients
presented by a monotone addition/multiplication circuit. At the positive pilot,
sample a monomial derivation by choosing each sum child in proportion to its
evaluated value, recursively expanding product children, and treating constant
subtrees as numeric coefficients. Degree D bounds the number of variable leaves;
one may pad lower-degree monomials with factors1. This costs polynomial in circuit
size and D and supplies the numeric proposal sampler. The coordinate envelope
uses D in place of m. A label identifying whether a monomial originated in F or
G turns this into an exact F/(F+G) factory.

Thus monotone circuits are NOT a remaining gap for a new positive-rational
compiler. Nor are instances such as spanning-tree or DPP polynomials automatically
new: those families have strong conventional numeric monomial samplers, which can
be paired with this correction construction.

For a signed arithmetic circuit or matrix formula that evaluates a polynomial
with nonnegative coefficients, no such generic positive monomial sampler follows
merely from efficient evaluation and a few low derivatives. Expanding coefficients
or repeatedly extracting mixed coefficients may be exponential. A proved factory
using only tame relative derivatives and efficiently evaluable finite-order jets,
with polynomial expected arithmetic as well as flips, would address this stronger
interface. I did not find a primary theorem already supplying that generic
computational conclusion. Absence from this search is not a priority proof.

9. Other conventional routes and their exact limits

* Naive product Bernoulli races or rejection from uniform feasible assignments
  can cost a^{-m}; this is a weak comparator and is superseded above.
* Separator rejection can be exponentially poor even at treewidth1 if opposing
  collections of leaf factors make two child separator marginals nearly disjoint.
  But this is an algorithm failure, not a hardness result; the shifted route fixes
  it, including arbitrary star degree.
* Fotakis, Kalavasis, Tzamos, Perfect Sampling from Pairwise Comparisons,
  NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2211.12868, combines learning and CFTP on explicit support.
  It is a strong comparator, but enumerating all global configurations is
  exponential here; exact pairwise comparison access is also not free.
* Morina et al., From the Bernoulli Factory to a Dice Enterprise via Perfect
  Sampling of Markov Chains, arXiv:1912.09229, gives constructive rational
  factories. Its expanded ladder/state-space representation and associated
  constants must be charged when applying it to a succinct growing family.
* Lazy brute-force perfect-sampling conversions assume a way to compute target
  and/or algorithm output probabilities, even if very slowly. Unknown primitive
  biases are not supplied by coin access, so this interface cannot simply be
  presumed in this problem.
* Stumpf-Fetizon and Goncalves, Scalable Bernoulli Factory MCMC for Intractable
  Marginalised Posteriors, arXiv:2505.05438v3 (29 August 2026),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05438 , proves polynomial divide-and-conquer cost for
  exact marginal MCMC transitions in factorizing models, under specified bounded
  unbiased likelihood/reciprocal/ratio estimators. This is stronger than naive
  product two-coin MCMC. It does not alone give exact iid posterior samples or a
  generic polynomial-arithmetic compiler from succinct rational-function jets.
* Nacu and Peres, Fast simulation of new coins from old, AAP 2005,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0309222 , relates univariate analyticity and
  exponentially tailed simulation. Holtz, Nazarov and Peres, New coins from old,
  smoothly, Constructive Approximation 2011,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1936 , relates univariate smoothness to achievable
  tail orders via compatible positive Bernstein expansions. Smoothness-to-factory
  existence and polynomial-envelope methods themselves are established. A new
  generic theorem needs to earn growing dimension, succinct descriptions,
  relative-scale-uniform bounds, and total arithmetic cost.

10. Demonstration

shifted_rejection_demo.py implements the scalar Huber factory, primitive log
factory, empirical-envelope coin, and capped-pilot exactness architecture on a
small factor chain with actual forward/backward proposal computation. Source
counts are iid binomial aggregates, with the represented physical flips charged.
The source probabilities are private to a fixture and are only used separately
for validation. Ordinary floating arithmetic is used for known probabilities;
the mathematical algorithm is exact, but the numerical demonstration is NOT a
validated exact-real implementation or a practical superiority claim.

Executed result: 240 outputs from a four-variable binary chain with endpoint
constraints, 14 local unknown parameters and a=.85. The empirical frequencies
had total variation .07119 from exact enumeration (a small Monte Carlo check,
not a rigorous coverage result). The implementation represented 114,414,587
primitive experiments, used 5,753 proposals and 21 pilots, and called the fresh
empirical-envelope coin 87,867 times. The independent scalar linear-factory check
returned mean .71725 from 20,000 trials for target .72. These large experimental
counts explicitly rule out presenting this proof-of-construction as a practical
sampling advantage on the fixture. The detailed counts are in
shifted_rejection_results.json.
