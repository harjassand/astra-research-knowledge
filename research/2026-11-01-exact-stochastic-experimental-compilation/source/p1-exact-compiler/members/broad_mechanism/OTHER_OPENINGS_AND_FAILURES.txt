Complementary routes considered before the exact local-bit compiler
===================================================================

1. Learned Gibbs conditionals with an acquired mixing certificate

Desired operation: reusable posterior sampling under arbitrary later pinnings
or external fields. If the interaction graph and quantitative Dobrushin or
spectral-independence contract are learned, ordinary Gibbs/cluster methods
already implement much of this. Learning Ising parameters from transition
data and even metastable data is established. A statement that simply fits
the model then invokes such a certificate would not earn a new operation.
Moreover, arbitrary reweighting can destroy the mixing certificate. This route
was not selected as an original destination.

Primary comparisons inspected:
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/dutt21a/dutt21a.pdf
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-75439-1
https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.09391

2. Recurrent Markov models, relative stability, and temporal planning

Desired operation: stationary statistics, rare reachability, and later
temporal specifications, including controllers. Markov-chain tree and forest
formulas already make many stationary/reachability quantities relatively
stable under multiplicative edge perturbations, independently of mixing time.
Ordinary linear systems, model-checking product automata and MDP linear
programs execute the finite-state consequences. PAC reachability learning has
strong established routes. The unsolved interface in a proposed compact
learner would be acquiring informative rare states, or acquiring the compact
state realization itself. A supplied generative reset oracle would hide that
cost. This was retained as a component, not claimed as a new theorem.

Primary comparisons:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1431
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v132/chan21a.html

https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/palasamudram26a.html

3. Conditional-stable fermionic resources

Possible broader capability: learn an unknown Gaussian bath plus a small
interacting core; execute later Gaussian controls, occupation observations
and restricted non-Gaussian operations. Operator-order accuracy would survive
postselection more strongly than trace-distance learning. But the strongest
baseline is substantial: Gaussian tomography, DPP learning, and t-doped
fermionic learning already discover compact simulation resources. The 2025
t-doped result compresses non-Gaussianity to O(t) modes and learns in
poly(n,2^t) cost. A mixed-state active-core discovery theorem with conditional
stability might remain interesting, but was not constructed in this branch.
It must not be represented as an earned capability.

4. Two failed shortcuts for the selected bit-oracle operation

The first shortcut was to use a Bernoulli race over entire assignments. With
target weights a product of m local probabilities bounded below by alpha,
the straightforward acceptance probability can be alpha^m. Rare probability
has merely been moved to another rejection step.

The second was to invoke a standard smooth-function Bernstein factory after
showing that the target marginal and its derivatives are cheaply evaluable.
Its reverse-time transition requires hypergeometric degree elevation, a sum
with (n+1)^m terms. The supplied RANDOMIZED_ELEVATION.txt resolves precisely
that computation under explicit relative smoothness and envelope-slack
conditions. It does not treat generic factory existence as a cost theorem.

5. Exact MCMC is not automatically exact independent sampling

The latest version of Stumpf-Fetizon--Goncalves constructs exact Barker
transitions. Its polynomial scaling theorem uses smoothness, posterior
concentration, IID structure and proposal sizes shrinking as n^{-1/2}.
Neither arbitrary-factor worst-case complexity nor independence from mixing
time follows from that theorem. Their elementary two-coin races are useful
existing components and should be adopted where appropriate.

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.05438

6. A lifetime information constraint

Fresh experimental coins are an honest continuing resource. A transcript of
m Bernoulli(p) observations cannot generate N>m jointly independent fresh
Bernoulli(p) outputs for every p without further p-dependent input: Fisher
information contracts under postprocessing, whereas those N outputs have
information N/[p(1-p)] and the transcript has m/[p(1-p)]. This does not forbid
a finite dataset from furnishing a fixed finite number of exact marginal
outputs; it rules out an unlimited exact IID service from finite retained
data. The proof assumes the usual differentiable Bernoulli family and
parameter-independent postprocessing.

Factories also do not remove calibration bias in the experimental coins.
They are exact for the stationary input law supplied. Unknown drift,
correlation, misprepared contexts, or adversarial bias must be separately
bounded or admitted into the task contract.
