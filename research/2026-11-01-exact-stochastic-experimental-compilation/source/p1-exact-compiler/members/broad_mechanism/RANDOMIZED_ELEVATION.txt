RANDOMIZED HYPERGEOMETRIC ELEVATION WITHOUT AN EXPONENTIAL COEFFICIENT SUM

Status and purpose
------------------
This note supplies the computational operation missing from a multivariate
Bernstein Bernoulli factory. It does not establish the coefficient construction
for the particular target function; that is a separate analytic obligation.
It proves that fixed-order relative Taylor control plus strict envelope slack
allows exact degree elevation to be used without evaluating an exponentially
large multivariate hypergeometric sum. The operation is an exact categorical
factory, not a numerical estimate of the elevated coefficient.

The useful finite-bit instantiation has envelope width n^{-3}, Taylor order 7,
and eighth-order remainder. Its stage work is polynomial times n^2 times
polylog(n), whereas stage survival is O(n^{-3}). The resulting expectation is
finite and polynomial in the fixed input parameters and starting level.

1. Conditional law at a doubling stage
-------------------------------------
There are m independent input coins with unknown biases p_1,...,p_m. At a
doubling stage we have 2n tosses of each and total success counts s_i. Put
x_i=s_i/(2n). Conditional on these totals, the counts K_i in the first n
tosses are independent and

 P(K_i=k)=binom(s_i,k) binom(2n-s_i,n-k) / binom(2n,n).

Thus Y_i=K_i/n-x_i is symmetric, has mean zero, has |Y_i|<=1/2, and satisfies
the sampling-without-replacement Hoeffding bounds

 E exp(tY_i) <= exp(t^2/(8n)),
 P(|Y_i|>=u) <= 2 exp(-2nu^2).

The exact variance is x_i(1-x_i)/(2n-1). Odd moments vanish. Every fixed-order
even moment is an explicitly computable rational polynomial in factorial
moments E(K_i)_j=(n)_j(s_i)_j/(2n)_j. This requires only fixed-order arithmetic;
one need not sum over all possible count vectors.

For use in the separate coefficient construction, with v=x(1-x),

 E Y^4=(3v^2-v/n)/[(2n-1)(2n-3)].

One elementary proof writes N=2n, s=Nx, Y=N^{-1}sum_{j=1}^s epsilon_j,
where epsilon is uniformly random among signs with N/2 positive and N/2
negative entries. Distinct pairs have mean -1/(N-1); distinct quartets have
product mean 3/[(N-1)(N-3)]. Expanding the fourth power gives
s(N-s)[3s(N-s)-2N]/[(N-1)(N-3)] before division by N^4.

For n>=3, the following deliberately loose numerical bounds follow directly:

 |E Y^2 - v/(2n) - v/(4n^2)| <= 1/(16n^3),
 |E Y^4 - 3v^2/(4n^2)| <= 5/(8n^3),
 |E Yi^2 Yj^2 - vi vj/(4n^2)| <= 1/(16n^3)  (i!=j).

The second one uses (2n-1)(2n-3)>=n^2 and v<=1/4. These identities make
the order-n^{-2} hypergeometric expansion explicit. For compatibility of
g_n=f-L1f/n+(L1^2-L2)f/n^2, it is enough to expand H_nf through degree 5,
H_n(L1f) through degree 3, and H_n[(L1^2-L2)f] through degree 1. Only
derivatives of f through order 6 are needed for that compatibility step.

Write H_n F(x)=E[F(x+Y)]. The ordinary deterministic way to compute this
quantity uses up to (n+1)^m values. We will instead generate bounded unbiased
estimators of the particular small differences needed by the factory.

2. Assumptions on the coefficient envelopes
------------------------------------------
Let a_n,b_n be rationally evaluable coefficient functions on [0,1]^m, with
0<=a_n<=b_n<=1 and b_n-a_n=2c n^{-s} h, where h>0 is a rationally evaluable
scale. Suppose for every doubling-stage count vector x, all n>=n0,

 d_+(x)=a_{2n}(x)-H_n a_n(x) >= c0 h(x)n^{-s},
 d_-(x)=H_n b_n(x)-b_{2n}(x) >= c0 h(x)n^{-s},
 d_+(x),d_-(x),b_{2n}(x)-a_{2n}(x) <= c1 h(x)n^{-s}.

The constants c,c0,c1 are positive and explicitly known. Strict slack c0 is
important: nonnegative means alone do not make the estimators below
pointwise nonnegative. Also assume the old elevated width is bounded below,

 H_n(b_n-a_n)(x) >= c2 h(x)n^{-s},

for known c2>0. This follows, for example, from the displayed width formula
and a bound H_n h >= h/2. It can alternatively be included in the analytic
coefficient lemma.

Choose an even integer 2r with r>s. For F=a_n and F=b_n let T_F(Y) be the
Taylor polynomial about x through degree 2r-1. Suppose that

 |F(x+Y)-T_F(Y)|
 <= C h(x) (sum_i |Y_i|)^{2r} exp(L sum_i |Y_i|)

for known rational C,L>=0, uniformly over the allowed Y. This follows from
relative derivative bounds on F through order 2r and an exponential
comparison h(x+u)<=h(x)exp(L||u||_1) along the segment. The constants may be
polynomial in the input size; they may not conceal exponential constants.

The exact Taylor expectation t_F=E T_F(Y) is available from the explicit
hypergeometric moments. There are O(m^{2r-1}) terms for fixed r. Since the Y_i
are independent and symmetric, terms having an odd power in any coordinate
vanish. For r=2 the only surviving terms are F(x) plus
one half the sum of Var(Y_i) F_ii(x). For r=4 only total degrees 0,2,4,6
survive.

3. A tractable product envelope for the Taylor remainder
-------------------------------------------------------
Take the integer K>=max(1,2L). For |y|<=1/2,

 exp(L|y|) <= (1+|y|)^K.

Indeed log(1+t)>=t/(1+t)>=2t/3 on [0,1/2]. Also the power-mean inequality
gives (sum |Y_i|)^{2r} <= m^{2r-1} sum_i Y_i^{2r}. Define

 B(Y)=C h(x) m^{2r-1} (sum_i Y_i^{2r})
       product_j (1+|Y_j|)^K.

Then |F(x+Y)-T_F(Y)|<=B(Y) for both coefficient functions. Crucially, B is a
sum of m product terms. Let

 Z_i=E(1+|Y_i|)^K,
 V_i=E[Y_i^{2r}(1+|Y_i|)^K].

Its exactly computable expectation is

 B0=E B=C h(x)m^{2r-1} (product_i Z_i) sum_i(V_i/Z_i).

No multivariate coefficient sum appears. Computing all Z_i,V_i needs O(mn)
univariate terms.

To sample Y from the tilted law Q(Y)=P(Y)B(Y)/B0:

 (i) choose I with probability proportional to V_i/Z_i;
 (ii) independently sample Y_I with mass proportional to
      P_i(Y_I)Y_I^{2r}(1+|Y_I|)^K;
 (iii) for each j!=I sample Y_j with mass proportional to
       P_j(Y_j)(1+|Y_j|)^K.

If some V_i=0, give that component zero selection weight. If B0=0, all
remainders vanish almost surely and use the exact Taylor expectation.

4. Size of the remainder envelope
---------------------------------
For n>=m^2K^2, the bounds above imply

 Z_i <= 1+K/sqrt(n),    product_i Z_i <= e.

One proof uses e^{K|Y|}-1<=K|Y|e^{K|Y|}, Cauchy--Schwarz, E Y^2<=1/(4n),
and E e^{2K|Y|}<=2e^{K^2/(2n)}. For n>=K^2 the resulting coefficient is
less than one.

The tail bound yields E|Y|^{4r}<=2(2r)!/(2n)^{2r}. A second
Cauchy--Schwarz bound therefore gives

 V_i <= 2 sqrt((2r)!)/(2n)^r exp(K^2/(4n)).

Consequently, a deliberately loose explicit bound is

 B0 <= 8 sqrt((2r)!) C m^{2r} h(x)n^{-r}.

For fixed r this is polynomial in m,C. Increase n0 until B0<=
(c0/2)h(x)n^{-s}. Since r>s, it suffices to take

 n0 >= [16 sqrt((2r)!) C m^{2r}/c0]^{1/(r-s)}

and n0>=m^2K^2 (round up, then to a doubling-grid start). This is polynomial
for fixed r,s with r-s fixed and positive.

5. Exact nonnegative estimators for the three decision masses
-----------------------------------------------------------
For a draw Y~Q define

 R_F(Y)=F(x+Y)-T_F(Y),
 Z_F(Y)=B0 R_F(Y)/B(Y),

with the zero-envelope case handled separately. Then

 E_Q Z_F = H_nF-t_F,    |Z_F|<=B0.

Generate

 U_+ = a_{2n}(x)-t_{a_n}-Z_{a_n}(Y),
 U_- = t_{b_n}+Z_{b_n}(Y)-b_{2n}(x),
 U_? = b_{2n}(x)-a_{2n}(x).

Their expectations are respectively d_+,d_-, and the new undecided width.
The deterministic centers differ from these expectations by at most B0.
Thus U_+,U_->=d_{+/-}-2B0>=0. Moreover each is at most
(c1+2B0/[h n^{-s}])h n^{-s}. Under the chosen n0 they are all bounded by

 M=(c1+c0)h(x)n^{-s}.

If convenient enlarge M to any computable rational upper bound of this
quantity. Notice that h can be extremely small. It scales every mass and
every bound equally; it is not a reciprocal probability cost.

Run an ordinary three-way Bernoulli race: choose +,-,? uniformly; generate
the corresponding U; flip a coin of known rational bias U/M; on failure
repeat, and on success return the selected symbol. The returned probabilities
are exactly the three desired masses divided by

 d_++d_-+(b_{2n}-a_{2n}) = H_n(b_n-a_n).

Expected attempts are at most 3(c1+c0)/c2, a constant in n. The race itself
is an established elementary construction. The new role of the Taylor
envelope is to supply its nonnegative bounded unbiased inputs at polynomial
arithmetic cost, with no exponential hypergeometric summation.

6. Why these are precisely the Bernoulli-factory transitions
-----------------------------------------------------------
At the starting level n0, after observing the count vector, accept 1 with
probability a_n0, accept 0 with probability 1-b_n0, and otherwise continue.
Suppose by induction that conditional on the n-count vector, the cumulative
probabilities of already returning 1, already returning 0 and continuing are
a_n,1-b_n,b_n-a_n. After acquiring n fresh tosses of each input, condition on
the 2n-count vector. Exchangeability gives the old cumulative masses
H_na_n,1-H_nb_n,H_n(b_n-a_n). On the branch that remains undecided, the race
above adds exactly d_+ to the unconditional accept-1 mass and d_- to the
accept-0 mass. The new cumulative masses are therefore a_{2n},1-b_{2n}, and
b_{2n}-a_{2n}. This proves the induction and requires no numerical knowledge
of the elevated coefficients.

If the associated Bernstein polynomials converge to f, and the width tends
to zero, the procedure terminates almost surely and outputs Bernoulli(f(p)).
Its continuation probability at level n is exactly E_p[b_n(S/n)-a_n(S/n)].
If h<=1, this is at most 2c n^{-s}. Thus the expected source toss count is
at most m n0 plus sum over doubling levels of O(mn)c n^{-s}, finite for s>1.

7. Finite bits: an elementary conservative implementation
---------------------------------------------------------
The integer K and even Taylor order are deliberate. All envelope weights are
rational, and a useful common denominator is visible. For k in the support,
put d=2k-s_i. The unnormalized integer weights for the base tilt are

 W_i(k)=binom(s_i,k)binom(2n-s_i,n-k)(2n+|d|)^K.

For the distinguished-coordinate tilt multiply by d^{2r}. The omitted
denominators are common across k: binom(2n,n)(2n)^K for Z_i and an additional
(2n)^{2r} for V_i. Thus computing the univariate normalizers involves integer
sums, not multiplying unrelated rational denominators. The bit lengths are
O(n+(K+2r)log n). Integer sampling from the weighted arrays uses a uniformly
random integer below the total (fair bits plus rejection, expected fewer than
two trials), then prefix sums or binary search.

The component probabilities V_i/Z_i are rational; multiplying their m
denominators produces only O(m[n+(K+2r)log n]) bits. Sampling the component
is therefore polynomial too. The envelope expectation B0 and the mass bounds
have comparable polynomial bit lengths, multiplied by the bit sizes of C,h.

Assume evaluating F and its fixed-order derivatives at rational grid points
uses poly(input size,log n) bit operations and output bits. This is true for
a positive bounded-width factor partition circuit of fixed polynomial degree
in the grid variables when the deterministic query coefficients are rational:
use the known global product denominator, and automatic differentiation of
fixed order. It is not an oracle for an arbitrary black-box smooth function.

Using fast integer arithmetic, the O(mn) univariate terms cost
poly(input size) n^2 polylog(n) bit operations. The remainder evaluations,
Taylor computations and categorical races add polynomial work; their input
integers can have O(poly(input size)n) bits but remain within this bound.

Therefore, with s=3 and r=4, expected post-starting-level bit work is bounded by

 poly(input size) sum_{j>=0} (2^j n0)^{-3}
                            (2^j n0)^2 polylog(2^j n0),

which converges. Starting-level source tosses and deterministic coefficient
work are polynomial as well. All random outcomes are finite discrete outputs;
there is no appeal to continuous-output total-variation comparisons.

The tempting s=3/2,r=2 version gives finite expected source tosses and ordinary
real-arithmetic work. The elementary exact-integer implementation above costs
n^2 rather than n, so it does NOT by itself establish finite expected bit work
for s=3/2. A directed-precision implementation may repair that, but is not
claimed here. The s=3,r=4 route avoids this unproved numerical step.

8. Scope and the retained failed shortcut
----------------------------------------
This lemma does not claim that every cheaply evaluable smooth function has a
cheap Bernoulli factory. It requires explicit compatible coefficient envelopes,
strict relative slack, and polynomial fixed-order relative derivative bounds.
In the intended application those must be derived from a positive factor
circuit with a known positive floor, and the circuit itself must be acquired.

The failed shortcut was: evaluate a compact inference circuit at empirical
coin frequencies, form Bernstein bounds, and cite the reverse-martingale
factory. This leaves an (n+1)^m degree-elevation sum unpaid. The present exact
randomized elevation replaces that sum; it does not conceal it in a numerical
subroutine or assume an exact conditional sampler.

Primary conventional comparison
-------------------------------
Stumpf-Fetizon and Goncalves, arXiv:2505.05438v3 (29 August 2026), provide
cascading 2-coin Barker MCMC. Their polynomial scaling result is under
posterior concentration, smooth likelihoods and shrinking proposal steps
(Assumptions 2--4, Theorem 1); it is not a worst-case perfect-sampling theorem
for arbitrary factor products. Their elementary Bernoulli-race components
are appropriate components here. The present lemma makes no priority claim
against all possible Bernoulli-factory or randomized-integration literature.
