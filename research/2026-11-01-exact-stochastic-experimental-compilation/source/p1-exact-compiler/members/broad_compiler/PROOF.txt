EXACT STOCHASTIC COMPILATION FROM IMPERFECT EXPERIMENTS
Working construction, 2026-10-31

Scientific claim and scope
==========================
The main object is an executable probability program, not an estimate of its
unknown numerical constants. Independent experimental bits supply those
constants during execution. A rational probability calculation with controlled
RELATIVE derivatives can be compiled into an exactly distributed output bit.
The construction has polynomial expected experimental, fair-bit, and ordinary
bit-computation cost. In particular its cost does not contain the reciprocal
of the output probability or of an intermediate positive partition function.

Existence of Bernoulli factories, smooth Bernstein envelopes, reverse-time
Bernstein algorithms, and Bernoulli races are established machinery. The
candidate new component here is a COMPUTATION-CHARGED multivariate lifting:
replace an exponentially large hypergeometric coefficient sum by a bounded,
nonnegative randomized correction, using a fixed-order Taylor control and a
tractable sum of product envelopes. Relative scale prevents rare outputs from
turning this correction into a rare rejection event. Third-order envelopes
pay for exact integer arithmetic, not only for input tosses.

The very strong conventional route is developed separately in
../broad_control/SHIFTED_REJECTION_PROOF.txt. Coarse positive proposals,
logarithmic coin factories, shifted exponential races, and capped relearning
already give the exact positive-factor endpoint, and extend to positive
arithmetic circuits and other models with an executable positive monomial
sampler. That route is ADOPTED, not claimed as a separation. The theorem below
does not require such a sampler or positive circuit representation. Its
arithmetic procedure may use subtraction. No priority claim is made for the
whole theorem; the literature comparison establishes the particular missing
operation investigated, not exhaustive novelty.

This is a theoretical construction with conservative polynomial constants.
The supplied programs verify the exact finite correction and a complete toy
factory; they are not a practical large-scale implementation of the generic
bound. A separate acquisition construction and actual numerical consumers
below prevent a hidden learned-representation or numerical-solver oracle.

1. Observation and arithmetic contract
======================================
There are m independent, stationary Bernoulli sources with fixed unknown
means p=(p_1,...,p_m) in [0,1]^m. One requested call returns one bit. All calls,
preparation/control costs, and fair random bits are charged. Bounded random
observations in [0,1] can be converted to bits by comparing to an independent
uniform variable; exact access to that observation must itself be specified.
Adversarial bounded numerical error is NOT this contract. A factory is exact
for the actual source means, not for an unidentifiable latent noiseless mean.

A later probability program is a rational function f:[0,1]^m -> (0,1), with
h=f(1-f). Rational coefficients have a finite binary description. A concrete
algorithm evaluates f and all partial derivatives through order 12 exactly at
rational arguments of bit length b in T(b) bit operations, with output lengths
at most T(b). T is a known polynomial in b and the program's declared size.
This charges operand growth; an operation-count-only straight-line program
does not suffice. Fixed-order automatic differentiation of an actually built
polynomial-bit arithmetic circuit is one implementation. No table of all
Bernstein coefficients is supplied.

A known integer K>=2 satisfies, everywhere on the cube,

 |partial^alpha f| <= |alpha|! K^|alpha| h       (1<=|alpha|<=12),
 |partial^alpha h| <= |alpha|! K^|alpha| h       (0<=|alpha|<=8).

These are certificate obligations, not a new empirical test for arbitrary
black boxes. Sections 7--9 derive them for explicit constructed programs and
give a paid discovery interface. Constant 0/1 queries can be handled separately
when their structural support is decidable by the constructed algorithm.

THEOREM. Under this contract an explicit algorithm outputs Bernoulli(f(p))
exactly, terminates almost surely, and uses expected poly(m,K,program size)
experimental calls, fair bits, and bit operations. The polynomial is fixed;
there is no reciprocal-minimum-f or reciprocal-minimum-(1-f) dependence.
Fresh independent executions support arbitrarily adaptive future programs:
conditional on each past transcript, the next output has its requested law.

2. Hypergeometric facts and relative Taylor control
==================================================
At a doubling step, 2n calls of each source have totals s_i. Put x_i=s_i/(2n).
The conditional first-half count K_i has law Hypergeom(2n,s_i,n); these laws
are independent over i. Write Y_i=K_i/n-x_i and H_n u(x)=E u(x+Y).

Every Y_i is symmetric and has mean zero. With v_i=x_i(1-x_i),

 E Y_i^2 = v_i/(2n-1),
 E Y_i^4 = (3v_i^2-v_i/n)/[(2n-1)(2n-3)].

Odd moments vanish. Fixed-order moments follow from
E(K_i)_j=(n)_j(s_i)_j/(2n)_j, so they require fixed-degree rational arithmetic.
Sampling without replacement gives
P(|Y_i|>=t)<=2 exp(-2nt^2), E exp(uY_i)<=exp(u^2/(8n)).
The fourth-moment identity and deliberately loose bounds used below are also
proved and checked in ../broad_mechanism/RANDOMIZED_ELEVATION.txt.

For S=sum_i |Y_i|, integration of the tail gives
E|Y_i|^{2r}<=2*(r!)/(2n)^r. For n>=256m^2 u^2, the elementary integral bound

 E exp(u|Y_i|)
 <=1+2u integral_0^infty exp(ut-2nt^2)dt
 <=exp(5u/sqrt(n))

implies E exp(uS)<=2 (the larger constant 4 is harmless). Applying this at
2u and using Cauchy--Schwarz gives, for each fixed integer r<=12,

 E[S^r exp(uS)] <= 2 sqrt(2*(r!)) m^r n^{-r/2},

provided n>=1024m^2 u^2. We will use a larger starting n if needed. No factor
2^m is introduced: the exponential moment is bounded near 1 before multiplying.

The first derivative bound on h gives
h(x+y)<=h(x) exp(K ||y||_1). Thus every fixed-order Taylor remainder is
bounded by a polynomial constant times h(x) S^r exp(KS). The same estimate
holds along the whole line segment in the cube. Jensen's inequality and
E S<=m/(2sqrt(n)) also give H_n h>=3h/4 at the starting levels used below;
the exponential-moment bound gives H_n h<=2h.

3. Explicit third-order coefficient envelopes
=============================================
All operators below act on the function to their right; coefficients are
differentiated when operators are composed. Set v_i=x_i(1-x_i) and

 L1 = (1/2) sum_i v_i partial_ii,
 L2 = (1/6) sum_i v_i(1-2x_i) partial_iii
      +(1/8) sum_{i,j} v_i v_j partial_iijj.

Define A=L1 f, B=(L1^2-L2)f and

 g_n=f-A/n+B/n^2.

For clarity the composed expression is

 B= -L1 f +(1/3)sum_i v_i(1-2x_i)f_iii
             +(1/8)sum_{i,j}v_i v_j f_iijj.

It is computable using fixed-order derivatives, including when f itself uses
subtraction. The hypergeometric expansion is

 H_n = I+L1/(2n)+M2/n^2+O_relative(n^-3),
 M2=L1/4+(1/32)sum_{i,j}v_i v_j partial_iijj.

Here O_relative means an explicit bound times h(x). Indeed, expand H_n f
through degree 5, H_n A through degree 3, and H_n B through degree 1. The
moment identities above and relative Taylor bounds yield

 H_n f=f+L1f/(2n)+M2f/n^2+R_f,
 H_n A=A+L1A/(2n)+R_A,
 H_n B=B+R_B,

with |R_f|<=3Q(m+1)^6 h/n^3,
     |R_A|<=2Q(m+1)^4 h/n^2,
     |R_B|<=3Q(m+1)^2 h/n.

One deliberately excessive computable choice is
Q=10^15 (m+1)^4 (K+1)^12.
Leibniz' rule, degree-at-most-four coefficients v_i v_j, and 12!<5*10^8
show that Q bounds every derivative of f of order 1--12, every derivative
of A through order 10, every derivative of B through order 8, and every
derivative of h through order 8, divided by h. The displayed remainders then
follow from the moment bounds. Enlarging Q is allowed; its exponent is fixed.

The cancellation is exact:
M2=(3/4)L2-(1/4)L1^2.
Substitution gives

 |H_n g_n-g_{2n}| <= R h/n^3,
 R=16 Q(m+1)^6.

This cancellation is essential. Plain Bernstein coefficients f(k/n) leave
an order-1/n defect and do not pay for finite expected work.

Set C=8R and

 a_n=g_n-C h/n^3,       b_n=g_n+C h/n^3.

For n sufficiently large, explicitly polynomial below, 0<a_n<b_n<1.
To see this without using min(f,1-f), note that h<=min(f,1-f). The relative
zero-order bounds

 |A|/h <= mK^2/4,
 |B|/h <= mK^2/4+mK^3/2+3m^2K^4/16

imply |g_n-f|<=h/4 once n>=8P, where
P=1+mK^2+mK^3+m^2K^4. Also C/n^3<=1/4 suffices. Hence a_n>=f-h/2>0 and
b_n<=f+h/2<1, even when f is exponentially small.

The two shrinkage increments have strict slack:

 d_+=a_{2n}-H_n a_n,
 d_-=H_n b_n-b_{2n},

 (C/2)h/n^3 <= d_+,d_- <=3C h/n^3,
 b_{2n}-a_{2n}=C h/(4n^3),
 (3C/2)h/n^3 <= H_n(b_n-a_n)<=4C h/n^3.

For example the lower increment bound is
-R+C(3/4-1/8)=4R=C/2. This is a true coefficientwise compatibility
statement, not merely uniform approximation of f by polynomials.

4. Randomized elevation: the computational ingredient
====================================================
Ordinary elevation would sum over (n+1)^m first-half count vectors. We do not
evaluate that sum. For F=a_n or b_n, form its Taylor polynomial P_F(Y) about
x through degree 7. Its expected value t_F is computed exactly from the
independent hypergeometric moments. Only O(m^7) terms are required.

At n with C/n^3<=1, the eighth derivatives of both coefficient functions are
at most T h, T=4Q. Taylor's theorem gives

 |F(x+Y)-P_F(Y)| <= T h(x) S^8 exp(KS).

The omitted 1/8! only enlarges the bound. Put J=2K and use the rational
envelope

 E(Y)=T h(x)m^7 [sum_i Y_i^8] product_j(1+|Y_j|)^J.

It dominates the remainder, since S^8<=m^7 sum_i Y_i^8 and
exp(K|y|)<=(1+|y|)^{2K} for |y|<=1. Its expectation E0 can be computed by
O(mn) UNIVARIATE terms. Define

 Z_i=E(1+|Y_i|)^J,
 V_i=E[Y_i^8(1+|Y_i|)^J].

Then E0=T h(x)m^7 (product_i Z_i) sum_i V_i/Z_i.
Sample its exactly normalized tilted law by choosing i proportionally to
V_i/Z_i, sampling that coordinate from its eighth-moment tilt, and the
others from their ordinary tilts. This is a mixture of m product laws.

For n>=1024m^2 J^2, use the coordinatewise bound
E[Yi^8 exp(J S)]<=sqrt(E Yi^16 * E exp(2J S))
<=2 sqrt(8!)/(2n)^4<26/n^4. Summing coordinates before multiplying by m^7
gives the loose explicit estimate

 E0 <= M h/n^4,       M=128T(m+1)^8.

Choose n>=8M/C. Then E0<=(C/8)h/n^3. If E0=0 the remainders vanish and
use their exact Taylor expectations. Otherwise draw Y from the tilted law
and put Z_F=E0 [F(x+Y)-P_F(Y)]/E(Y). It has the exact properties

 E Z_F=H_nF-t_F,       |Z_F|<=E0.

Consequently the random quantities

 U_+=a_{2n}-t_{a_n}-Z_{a_n},
 U_-=t_{b_n}+Z_{b_n}-b_{2n},
 U_?=b_{2n}-a_{2n}

have means d_+,d_-, and the new undecided width. They are pointwise
nonnegative and all at most 4C h/n^3. This is stronger than having an
unbiased signed estimate of a nonnegative number. Strict envelope slack is
what pays for pointwise positivity.

Race the three quantities: choose a label uniformly, generate its U, accept
with rational probability U/(4C h/n^3), and repeat on failure. Expected
attempts are at most 8, since their total mean is at least (3C/2)h/n^3.
The returned probabilities are exactly the desired increments divided by
H_n(b_n-a_n). Tiny h cancels from both acceptance and normalization.

One fully explicit starting level is the next power of two above

 max(3, 1024m^2J^2, 8P, ceil((4C)^(1/3)), ceil(8M/C)).

All constants and operations are computed, not supplied confidence oracles.
The constants are intentionally poor; no practical-efficiency claim rests
on them. Better coefficients can be substituted with their own proved bounds.

5. Full factory, correctness, and finite computation
===================================================
Initially take n0 fresh bits from each source. At x=counts/n0, return 1 with
probability a_n0(x), return 0 with probability 1-b_n0(x), otherwise continue.
On continuation take n additional bits of each source, obtaining total 2n.
Run the three-way race of section 4; + returns 1, - returns 0, ? doubles n.

Conditional on the counts at a stage n, the cumulative probabilities of
having returned 1, having returned 0, and remaining undecided are
a_n,1-b_n,b_n-a_n. This is true initially. Conditional on the doubled counts,
exchangeability makes the old cumulative masses H_n a_n,1-H_n b_n, and
H_n(b_n-a_n). The race adds exactly the two shrinkage increments on the
undecided branch. This proves the induction. Previous count vectors must
not be used to alter this transition; the degree-elevation identity averages
over them in the correct conditional law.

The continuation probability is exactly E_p[b_n(S_n/n)-a_n(S_n/n)] and is
at most C/(2n^3). Thus termination is almost sure. Since g_n converges
uniformly to f and binomial empirical means converge to p, the total
probability of output 1 is f(p). The expected source calls are bounded by

 m n0 + (mC/2) sum_{j>=0}(2^j n0)^(-2)
 =m n0 +2mC/(3n0^2)=O(m n0).

The bit computation requires a further argument. With d=2k-s_i, the tilted
unnormalized weights are integers

 W_i(k)=binom(s_i,k)binom(2n-s_i,n-k)(2n+|d|)^J,

and the moment tilt multiplies by d^8. Their shared denominators are
binom(2n,n)(2n)^J, with an additional (2n)^8 for the moment. Their bit
lengths are O(n+(J+8)log n). Adjacent binomial products are computed by
multiplication and exact division by O(log n)-bit integers; do not repeatedly
multiply unrelated O(n)-bit binomial coefficients at each entry. Summing
the O(mn) terms costs poly(m,J)n^2 polylog(n) bit operations, even with a
conservative elementary implementation. Products of the m normalizers and
rational race probabilities still have polynomial-times-n bit lengths.

Sampling a known rational probability uses a uniform integer below its
denominator, generated by fair-bit rejection. Expected trials are below 2.
Grid evaluations and fixed-order automatic differentiation cost
poly(program size,log n). Taylor coefficients, fixed moments, and the
constant-expected-attempt race add at most poly(input)n^2 polylog(n).

At level n the survival probability is O(C/n^3), so expected post-initial
bit work is bounded by a polynomial times
sum_j (2^j n0)^(-1) polylog(2^j n0), which is finite and polynomial in n0
and the other declared inputs. Fair-bit costs obey the same bound. This is
why the third-order envelope was retained instead of the simpler n^-3/2
envelope that pays only for source calls and unit-cost real arithmetic.

6. Why this is not an ordinary confidence-interval algorithm
==========================================================
No finite-sample confidence interval for p is treated as surely correct.
No randomized plug-in estimate of f(p) is asserted unbiased. The correction
identities hold under the exact binomial/hypergeometric laws. If a transcript
looks atypical, the algorithm remains valid; it can only change its runtime.
This is the mechanism that allows exact stochastic execution from imperfect
observations without estimating p exactly.

Fresh evidence is essential. If m0 retained Bernoulli(p) observations could
produce N>m0 jointly independent fresh Bernoulli(p) outputs for every interior
p, Fisher-information data processing would give
N/[p(1-p)]<=m0/[p(1-p)], a contradiction. A random stopping acquisition has
the corresponding expected-sample version under the usual score regularity.
Thus this is not a finite-data perpetual exact simulator. It is reusable
compiled knowledge with paid source calls during execution.

An interior zero is another decisive boundary: if nonconstant f has f(p*)=0
at an interior p*, no almost-surely finite classical factory valid throughout
an open neighborhood can output 1 anywhere. Every finite input-bit prefix
has positive probability at p*, so any positive-probability accepting leaf
would contradict f(p*)=0. The positive-relative-regularity condition has
substance. It also prevents the division-by-eta obstruction from being hidden
inside an unpriced constant K.

7. Positive partition quotients: a derived certificate, not a solver premise
===========================================================================
Let F,G be nonzero polynomials with nonnegative coefficients in primitive
affine factors z_j(p), positive throughout the cube, with z_j>=a>0 and
|partial_i z_j|<=1. Each factor depends on one input coordinate, which may
occur in several factors or with either sign. Suppose every monomial has
total degree at most D, counted with multiplicity. Then

 |partial^alpha F|<= (D/a)^|alpha| F,

and likewise G and Z=F+G. For f=F/Z, differentiate Zf=F. Combining the
highest derivative terms as (G partial^alpha F-F partial^alpha G)/Z shows
inductively

 |partial^alpha f|<=2^k k! (D/a)^k h,       k=|alpha|>=1.

Indeed the normalized constants satisfy c_k<=2+sum_{j=1}^{k-1}binom(k,j)c_j,
which is dominated by c_k=2^k k!. Differentiating h=f(1-f) gives the stated
jet certificate with K=8 max(1,D/a), rounded upward. The certificate is
independent of coefficient magnitudes and of tiny partition functions.

This argument can use a positive combinatorial identity even when the actual
polynomial-time evaluation uses a determinant or other signed arithmetic.
The identity does not license a free monomial sampler. Conversely, where
such a sampler IS constructible, the strong shifted-rejection route is an
adopted alternative and often much preferable.

Raw q-coins promised q>=alpha do not freely provide p=(q-alpha)/(1-alpha)
coins. The correct access options are (i) factors genuinely contain a known
mixture a+(1-a)p of directly observed p-coins, or (ii) reserve a=alpha/2 and
construct p=1-(1-q)/(1-a) with a standard linear Bernoulli factory, using
the slack 1-q<=1-alpha<1-a. Its polynomial alpha^-1 cost must be added.

8. A genuinely signed numerical consumer
========================================
The following is an explicit numerical operation covered without assuming a
positive proposal sampler. It is not claimed hard for every other method.
Let M(p)=M0+sum_i p_i M_i be a rational matrix pencil built from the acquired
experimental addresses. Its coefficient matrices may have either sign.
Choose a known rational R>=sup_cube ||M||_2, for example the sum of the
Frobenius norms replaced by rational upper bounds. Set

 A(p)=I+M(p)M(p)^T,  v(p)=b^T A(p)^(-1)b,
 f(p)=v(p)/(1+v(p)),    ||b||_2=1.

There is no supplied inverse oracle. Form A exactly, solve the rational
linear system by Gaussian elimination (or fraction-free elimination), and
differentiate this arithmetic through fixed order. A>=I on the entire cube;
v lies in [1/(1+R^2),1], so f lies in [1/(2+R^2),1/2]. All coefficient and
operand sizes are polynomial in matrix dimension, input coefficient bits,
and argument bit length. Determinants have the usual polynomial bit-height
bound. No n-dimensional analogue of an unbounded real oracle is present.

A direct derivative certificate is available: write L=max(1,sum_i ||M_i||_2)
using rational upper bounds. Each first derivative of A has norm <=a1=2RL;
each second derivative has norm <=a2=2L^2; higher derivatives vanish. Put
H=1+a1+a2. From A A^{-1}=I, uniform order-k derivative bounds satisfy
b0=1 and b_k<=k a1 b_{k-1}+binom(k,2)a2 b_{k-2}; hence b_k<=k! H^k.
The same bound holds for v. Differentiating (1+v)f=v gives
|partial^alpha f|<=k!(2H)^k for k>=1. Leibniz' rule then bounds the
derivatives of h by k*k!*(2H)^k. Since
h>= [1/(2+R^2)]*[1/2]=1/[2(2+R^2)], an integer
K>=8H(2+R^2) supplies all section-1 inequalities. This is an explicit
certificate, not an inference of smoothness from numerical behavior.

This consumer samples an exact nonlinear response/selection bit determined
by uncertain experimentally accessed means. It can be embedded in later
adaptive selection or accept/reject programs without an accumulating
parameter-estimation bias. It does not estimate a tiny response to relative
accuracy for free, make general optimal design cheap, or confer a practical
advantage over ordinary retaining matrix methods. Its purpose is to give an
actual signed arithmetic instantiation of the broader compiler, alongside
the conventional positive-model instantiations. Other scientifically useful
programs require their own earned arithmetic and regularity certificates.

9. Acquisition of the symbolic experimental dictionary
======================================================
It is possible to acquire a useful representation without estimating its
numerical constants. Here is a fully paid, nonmonotone example.

There are M controlled stateless component experiments. At chosen binary
input x in {0,1}^N, component j returns an independent bit of mean p_j(x).
Preparation of x and a fresh component trial is the physical primitive, with
its actual cost charged. This is a local test bench, not a free reset to an
unobserved state of a dynamical system. Suppose p_j is an UNKNOWN d-junta,
and every relevant variable i has maximum influence at least gamma:
max_x |p_j(x)-p_j(x xor e_i)|>=gamma>0. These are explicit source promises.
Neither the scopes nor their response tables are given to the learner.

Maintain a witnessed subset J of the true scope. For every assignment to J,
choose L=ceil(2^d log[2B 2^d/delta]) independent uniform completions outside J,
where B is a deterministic bound on the number of such tests over components
and d enrichments. With probability at least 1-delta/2, every assignment on
the remaining true parents occurs in every test. Estimate each chosen mean
to r=gamma/(256 max(1,d)) by repeated bit trials, with a union-bound budget
delta/2 over a deterministic query cap. Each estimate costs
ceil(log(4Q/delta)/(2r^2)) trials by Hoeffding. This use of Hoeffding is only
one sufficient discovery construction; it does not calibrate all later
numeric execution to an epsilon.

If all empirical completion ranges are <=gamma/2, no true parent can remain:
a missing parent's gamma-sized witness appears among the covered patterns,
and estimation error can shrink its range by at most 2r. Otherwise choose
two witnessed completions whose empirical values differ by >gamma/2.
Change their differing outside-J coordinates along a fixed chain. Bisect
chain intervals, querying midpoint means, and prune intervals with empirical
endpoint contrast <=t=gamma/(16 max(1,d)). Search both children if necessary.
Every unpruned interval has nonzero true contrast and hence contains a true
parent. At most d such intervals occur per depth. If no singleton survived,
at most d pruned terminal intervals contain parents, each contributing true
endpoint contrast <=t+2r; all other intervals contribute zero. The triangle
inequality would bound the original contrast by d(t+2r)<gamma/4, contradicting
the original true contrast >gamma/2-2r. A surviving singleton is a genuine
parent. Add it and repeat. At most d enrichments occur.

Thus the exact scopes are acquired on ONE event using
O(M[d 4^d log(Md/delta)+d^2 log N]) distinct mean assays, each with the
charged gamma^-2 d^2 concentration cost above. Caps and B,Q can be increased
conservatively; no claim of query optimality is made. Input-setting work
O(N) per trial must be counted when it is the actual preparation cost.

After scope discovery, do NOT fit a numeric table. Retain the canonical
source program: to request entry (j,c), set x_J=c and all other bits to 0,
and run a fresh experiment. There are at most M 2^d symbolic entries. On the
scope event these are exactly the relevant means at every full context.
All later probability programs are built on these addresses. Their input
coins are physically executable. Fresh execution randomness is independent
of acquisition. Consequently, on the one exact-scope event, an arbitrarily
long adaptive sequence of factories has its ideal transcript law exactly.
To compare unconditional laws, run the ideal service with the SAME acquisition
transcript and the same adaptive query policy. Conditional on every successful
acquisition transcript, both services have the same future transition laws.
They can therefore be coupled to agree forever on the one scope-success event.
Even the infinite transcript TV error is at most the discovery failure delta.
Numeric calibration errors do not accrue because no calibrated numeric
constants are used in the semantics. This statement does not condition an
unconditional query distribution on a conveniently selected discovery event.

For graphical-model queries, form the learned scopes, add the actual later
constraint scopes, discover an elimination order by complete width search
(the preserved XP algorithm is one concrete implementation), and construct
sum-product or max-sum arithmetic by elimination. All constraint sizes and
width costs are charged. This earns the numerical programs; it does not make
arbitrary constraints tractable. The strong conventional shifted-rejection
compiler already solves this positive endpoint and should normally be used.
Signed numerical queries can instead instantiate the explicitly constructed
matrix program in section 8 or another separately certified program.

The influence promise is necessary for uniform finite exact discovery. With
an arbitrarily weak omitted dependence, a finite noisy transcript cannot
reliably distinguish zero from nonzero influence. An approximate-scope version
may be used under an explicit uniform approximation promise, but it gives
approximate semantics, not lifetime exactness. If source means are perturbed
by Delta p, the relative derivative bound implies
|logit f(p+Delta p)-logit f(p)|<=K||Delta p||_1, hence a single output's TV
error is at most tanh(K||Delta p||_1/4). Repeated errors can accumulate.
This is a limit of the exact claim, not a new perturbation theorem destination.

10. What has and has not been earned
====================================
Earned mathematical operation: finite-bit, expected-polynomial stochastic
execution of the certified rational program, with no exponential coefficient
enumeration and no output-rarity penalty. The correction is explicitly
computable and the useful unknown addresses can be acquired by a finite
experiment procedure. Exactness refers to a distribution over finite output
bits, so there is no atomic-versus-continuous TV mismatch.

Not earned: arbitrary black-box smoothness certificates; unknown source
stationarity/independence from finite evidence; free local physical access;
exact hidden-scope discovery without a resolution promise; cheap arbitrary
planning, inference, or discontinuous decisions; practical constants for the
generic factory; numerical advantage over every existing exactification
method; unconditional novelty. A succinct numerical program may hide huge
bit growth unless the stated bit contract is checked.

The decisive failures are preserved alongside successful components:
plain Bernstein correction has insufficient tail decay; deterministic
multivariate elevation is exponential; an unbiased signed remainder is not
itself a valid probability; n^-3/2 survival does not pay the conservative
n^2 bit implementation; positive-factor inference already has a strong
conventional exact route; retained finite data cannot supply unlimited exact
fresh outputs; constant unheralded noise destroys the alternative Bell
quadratic kernel; cheap reachability certificates do not buy physical resets.

This construction changes what is retained: a finite experimental-address
dictionary, a computed program, and a regularity/cost proof. Their purpose is
to make future stochastic operations executable without silently replacing
unknown means by fixed estimates. The principal research opening is the
general compiler, not another special clock family or another numerical
solver benchmark.
