## Completed scientific construction

bath factors from future dynamics, while preserving the exact retained
mixed state, references and actual controller records. It charges the
entire remaining exposure. Source reset is never identified with bath reset.

Hidden parity randomization supplies one mechanism. Its ideal error is
quadratic in weak remaining overlap despite initial correlations and the
full nonlinear sine interaction. It requires only the first retired-number
moment and extends to a fixed separable retired subspace using total
occupation, without an explicit factor counting modes. This task supplies
no photon-count output. Fresh independent hidden seeds are required.

The difficult new bridge is control-compatible composition. A phase applied
to the wrong mode can disturb an active field immediately, even if the
intended retired mode is empty. We therefore acquired TOTAL second-number
moments from the actual sine action and lifted passive one-particle control
errors to complete multiphoton channel bounds. All wrong-mode operations
are compared on the full bath before any factor is removed. A chronological
hybrid proof then sums retirement allowances, with occupation contracts
valid on every intermediate comparison process.

The strongest tested conventional alternative is a finite near-swap into
a fresh vacuum dump. It meets the same model budget with a smaller bound
and looser pulse-area tolerance. No parity hardware advantage is claimed.
The reusable retirement framework can choose absorption, parity or retained
memory according to the actual resources.

## Useful failures and corrections

- Equal detector effects can give different future backaction; the exact
  bright-mode event gap exceeds 0.0045985. Terminal effects do not specify
  an intermediate instrument.
- Threshold dephasing can leave a linear effect: the exact example at
  epsilon=0.01 exceeds 0.00706. Zero scalar field mean also fails to remove
  joint odd source/bath correlations.
- Keeping the parity seed or reusing a correlated bit can expose or restore
  the linear term. Fresh independence and future inaccessibility are essential.
- Whole-state phase continuity gives about 4.131e-4 for a case whose future
  output bound is below 7.074e-7. A mean-only witness separates the metrics.
  Failure of the stronger bound is not a future-output lower bound.
- Wrong-mode control has a real retired-mean-only obstruction: retained
  distance can approach the nonidentity mixture weight even when the intended
  retired mean and future coupling vanish. The acquired total moment addresses
  the reachable-input problem without a photon cutoff.
- Dropping the linear Z term in the second-annihilation-row estimate fails
  an exact small-action full-sine check. That tempting false bound is preserved.
- Reused single-cut phase tolerances, geometric tolerance 10^-8 and a
  looser swap-area case fail their respective sufficient budgets. These are
  not measured errors or device floors.
- Optional feedback-dependent couplings require common pointwise or
  controlled-operator envelopes; separate history-integral bounds are insufficient
  for the stated purified proof.
- Negative near-swap transmission includes a residual passive phase.
  `root/PASSIVE_DUMP_PHASE_CLARIFICATION.md` corrects the channel description;
  the arbitrary-state retirement bound and all constants are unchanged.
  The nonempty preparation witness is PRE-absorption.
- Exact dark retirement is unavailable for the stipulated exponential
  continuum's dense future span. Approximate small remaining exposure still
  supports the constructed operational capability.
