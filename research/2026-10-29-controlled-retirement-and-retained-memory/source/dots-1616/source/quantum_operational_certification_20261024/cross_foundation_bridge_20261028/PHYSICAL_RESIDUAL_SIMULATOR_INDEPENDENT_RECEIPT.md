## Finite rank certification and boundary feasibility

For physical dimension `d`, the real Hermitian Liouville space has dimension `D=d^2`. Starting from a nonzero normalized state, the span of native words of length at most `n` either grows or is already invariant. Thus the reachable span stabilizes by `D-1`. Starting from trace and all terminal-effect seeds, the corresponding dual span also stabilizes by `D-1`. Pairing both saturated spans gives the full reachable observable rank. The finite Hankel minor test therefore checks the *entire declared future interface*, not just the measured core. Using only one side, omitting terminal seeds, or substituting a numerical rank threshold would not implement this certificate.

The invariant-kernel alternative is also sound and complete at the stated scope. Equation (16) puts the initial state in the core section, makes `span(K)` invariant and unobservable, and makes every core transition equal to its quotient transition modulo `span(K)`. Induction puts every reachable state in `span(G,K)` and all future probabilities in the `r`-dimensional representation. Independence of the columns of `K` is unnecessary for soundness. Conversely, when the candidate rank is `r`, choose a basis of the actual reachable unobservable subspace; its dimension is `dim(R)-r<=D-r`, and the required `M_z,L_z` exist. This establishes the finite enumeration over kernel sizes without requiring exact physical section intertwining.

State, Choi, POVM, finite word, discarded-outcome preparation, Hankel-minor and kernel-witness conditions are polynomial equalities/inequalities in real matrix entries. Rational confidence endpoints and isolated algebraic known constants suffice. PSD is non-strict, so singular Choi matrices and unitary branches are included. The source realization gives a real feasible point on the confidence event when its supplied dimension bound is valid. Embedding a smaller realization in dimension `d` with a separate TP completion on the unused block preserves every reachable law and the observable rank.

Exact real-algebraic feasibility/sampling then provides an algebraic feasible point. This is a standard real-closed-field consequence, not a rational-density argument. I checked the cited [Basu survey](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~sbasu/raag_survey2011_final-sep4-2014.pdf), Sections 2.1, 2.2 and 2.5, including effective elimination, algebraic number representations and sampling on singular sets. Its role is the general exact decision/sampling machinery; the physical encoding and finite-rank reduction are established above. Rational approximation need not preserve rank, PSD-boundary equalities or instrument normalization. As a small exact illustration, `rho=[[t,1/3],[1/3,1-t]]`, `det(rho)=0`, `t>1/2` forces `t=(3+sqrt(5))/6`, a valid pure density matrix with no rational solution for these rational-polynomial constraints. This illustration is not a run of the source's acquisition search.

The finite word list, principal minors and Hankel minors may be enormous; the compact kernel formula remains nonconvex. Exact algebraic isolation, comparisons, bit lengths and physical synthesis are real costs. The receipt accepts finite decidability and conditional eventual accuracy, not efficient search or an executable laboratory construction. Known dimension/rank bounds, inverse-core conditioning and a fixed horizon or positive clock remain essential. A sequential confidence scheme still needs its own summable allocation.
