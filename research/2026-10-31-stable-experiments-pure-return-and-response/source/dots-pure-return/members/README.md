# Pure-return certificates and known stochastic programs
31 October 2026

## What was established
A complete mathematical construction now turns an acquired source operation plus a uniform return certificate into a quantum simulation procedure for its coupled dynamics. The certificate bounds how quickly the source returns toward a known pure state. The procedure retains source memory and finite-rate effects without a loop proportional to every rapid source update.

A separately checked extension covers arbitrary initial joint source/system states, prescribed joint interventions and final source output. With the explicitly repaired window schedule, execution has near-linear dependence on accumulated coupling action, ordinary system-Hamiltonian action and intervention count, multiplied by polynomial return-horizon/circuit-size factors and logarithmic rate/precision factors. Actual source, coupling, reflection, preparation and consumer costs remain paid.

These are internally reviewed mathematical-existence results. They are not external peer review, an implemented full quantum circuit, a demonstrated practical advantage or a novelty determination. They have not achieved the civilisation-changing north star.

## A concrete acquired program interface
A source storing the last n fair random bits has 2^n possible histories, but its quantum shift-register realization resets exactly after n updates. Its source operation and reference-state reflection have explicit small circuits. The construction therefore receives known bounded-history colored-noise programs with genuinely nonlinear and noncommuting supplied output operators, without building a 2^n table.

This is not source learning. The update program and an actual coherent output-operator circuit must be acquired. Ordinary trajectories already store only n bits and source endpoints can often be sampled without every event. The consequential target is the coupled, chronologically ordered system process at high accuracy, not mere storage compression.

Under the separate invariant-algebra check, prescribed physical source-label snapshots and suitable label-based feedback can be public. The simulator's artificial jump times/colors are different objects and remain private. Requesting every physical event or a number of observations proportional to the event rate restores a corresponding cost.

## Assumptions that cannot be skipped
The abstract input includes an actual Stinespring circuit W, known pure fixed state psi and environment coefficient state c, a reflectable projector P=|psi><psi|, and an earned integer m with (Phi-adjoint)^m(I-P) <= (I-P)/16. An eigenvalue gap or ordinary mixing statement alone is insufficient. The system coupling is centered against P; subtracting an unknown mean is not free.

The optional fair-sign construction defines a different explicit centered noise model. It does not identify arbitrary unsigned noise with signed noise. The shuffle model has its specified top-to-random orientation. Its invalid binary encodings require the explicit flagged-reset extension, not an arbitrary identity extension.

All interventions occur at prescribed times. Adaptive choices of operations at those slots can use retained outcomes, but arbitrary intervention times, normalized rare postselection and artificial private-record disclosure are excluded. General quantum source operations are not automatically operations on the same classical source model.

## Evidence and limits
The included Python/JSON files check finite scalar normalizers, exact small shuffle identities, and small dense classical/quantum controlled-process comparisons. In the snapshot checks, stationary and fixed-label starts at two rates agreed to approximately 1e-15. These are diagnostics, not a scalable compiler implementation or benchmark.

Dense small-system methods, high-order elimination, reusable process tensors, ordinary quantum functional calculus and causal query compression remain serious alternatives. No general lower bound rules out a stronger acquired-memory method. Observable estimation and repeated-run measurement costs are additional and can scale as inverse powers of the desired statistical accuracy.
