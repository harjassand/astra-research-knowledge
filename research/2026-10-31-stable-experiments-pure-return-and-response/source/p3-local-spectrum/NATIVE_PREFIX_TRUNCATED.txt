## 1. A physical feature that survives an unknown exterior and coefficient uncertainty

Use the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt inner product and define

\[
R_A(\eta,\omega)
=
2^{-N}\operatorname{Tr}
\!\left[A^\dagger(\eta-i\omega-L)^{-1}A\right].

Equivalently,

\[
R_A(\eta,\omega)
=
\int_0^\infty e^{-(\eta-i\omega)t}

\]

The positive parameter \(\eta\) specifies the physical frequency smoothing. Numerical accuracy and frequency smoothing remain separate.

## 2. The representation is judged by its complete response residual

The central output certificate applies to several representation choices.

M=\eta-i\omega-L,

\[
\langle B,M^{-1}A\rangle

## 3. An exact compact repair for an exponentially large undamped sector

Local \(Z\)-dephasing leaves every diagonal operator undamped. There are \(2^N\) such Pauli words. At small \(\eta\), even a tiny diagonal residual can dominate a certificate through its \(1/\eta\) weight.

The new construction removes that residual **exactly**, using a representation whose size need not enumerate the diagonal sector.

### A bond-two right inverse

First consider

\[
H=\sum_i\Omega_iX_i+H_{\mathrm{diag}},
\qquad \Omega_i\ne0.
\]

Let \(P_0\) project onto traceless diagonal operators, \(Q\) onto off-diagonal operators, and define

\[
B=QKP_0,\qquad C=B^\dagger B.
\]

For a nonempty set \(S\),

\[
BZ_S
=
2\sum_{i\in S}\Omega_iY_iZ_{S\setminus\{i\}},
\qquad
CZ_S
=
4\sum_{i\in S}\Omega_i^2\,Z_S.
\]

The canonical minimum-norm inverse \(BC^{-1}\) appears to require reciprocals of all these nonuniform subset sums.

An exact constraint repair can be much simpler. Choose the first occupied input site \(j=\min S\), and set

\[
\boxed{
TZ_S=\frac{Y_jZ_{S\setminus\{j\}}}{2\Omega_j},
\qquad TI=0.
}
\]

Only the drive at \(j\) returns this word to the diagonal sector. Therefore

\[
P_0MT=I,\qquad P_0T=0,
\qquad
\|T\|=\frac1{2\min_i|\Omega_i|}.
\]

The rule needs only two automaton states: before and after the first \(Z\). Its matrix-product-operator bond is two. Finite-automaton compilation into matrix product operators is established technology; the contribution here is the particular physical right inverse and its exact constraint property. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

For any trace-zero trial \(x\),

\[
\boxed{
x'=x+TP_0(A-Mx)
}
\]

has exactly zero diagonal residual.

This is independent of frequency, dephasing strength, and diagonal interaction strength. It remains true for frustrated diagonal interactions. It is an algebraic property of the represented expression; an unqualified numerical compression afterward can destroy it.

### Approximate the expensive inverse while retaining an exact constraint

The simple repair can have a larger norm than the canonical repair. That suggests a composition rather than a choice between them.

For **any actually represented finite approximation** \(F\) to \(C^{-1}\), define

\[
\boxed{
T_F=BF+T(I-CF).
}
\]

Then

\[
P_0MT_F=CF+(I-CF)=I
\]

exactly. The correction uses the defect of the actual finite \(F\), including its approximation and coefficient errors.

Let

\[
a=4\min_i\Omega_i^2,\qquad
b=4\sum_i\Omega_i^2,\qquad
\kappa_C=b/a.
\]

If \(\|I-CF\|\le\varepsilon\), orthogonality to the canonical inverse gives

\[
\boxed{
\|T_Fd\|^2
\le
(1+\kappa_C\varepsilon^2)
\|BC^{-1}d\|^2.
}
\]

Thus an approximate separated inverse can deliver an almost minimum-norm repair while the physical constraint remains exact.

Exponential-sum inverse approximations and their separated tensor representations are established machinery. The useful connection here is that their remaining error can be retained inside a compact exact physical correction, instead of reopening the undamped sector. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

### Conditional drives and strong multiple-spin interactions

The more consequential extension allows

\[
H=
H_\varnothing+
\sum_j\bigl(X_jF_j(Z)+Y_jG_j(Z)\bigr)
+H_{\ge2},
\]

where \(F_j,G_j\) are real diagonal functions excluding site \(j\), and every term of \(H_{\ge2}\) flips at least two spins.

Suppose the input acquisition actually verifies

\[
F_j(z)^2+G_j(z)^2\ge a_j^2>0
\]

for every relevant local configuration. Replace the simple repair by

\[
\boxed{
TZ_S=
\frac{Y_jF_j-X_jG_j}
{2(F_j^2+G_j^2)}
\,Z_{S\setminus\{j\}},
\qquad j=\min S.
}
\]

These fractions multiply physical diagonal operators; they are not independent scalar weights on Walsh coefficients.

The matching single-flip term gives exactly the required diagonal commutator. Every other flip mask leaves a nonempty off-diagonal mask, so

\[
P_0MT=I,\qquad
\|T\|\le\frac1{2\min_j a_j}.
\]

The multiple-spin terms can be arbitrarily strong without invalidating this identity. They can still enlarge the final residual substantially.

Acquisition is explicit. If the conditional amplitudes at site \(j\) jointly depend on \(d_j\) variables, evaluating the tables, checking nonvanishing, computing reciprocals, and performing Walsh inversion costs

\[
O\!\left(\sum_j (d_j+1)2^{d_j}\right)

arithmetic operations, plus input reading and rational bit costs. An explicit arbitrary-layout matrix-product representation has bond at most \(2\sum_j2^{d_j}\). Bounded interaction order alone does not bound these union-neighborhood sizes.

Independent exact Pauli executions checked the construction with complex conditional drives and strong \(XX\), \(XY\), \(YY\), \(XYZ\), and \(XXX\) terms. The canonical subset-sum inverse guarantee was **not** imported into the conditional case: its Gram operator is generally different.

The organizing principle is therefore precise: **an exponentially large undamped constraint can have a cheaply acquired exact local right inverse, even when the useful approximate response remains difficult to acquire.**
