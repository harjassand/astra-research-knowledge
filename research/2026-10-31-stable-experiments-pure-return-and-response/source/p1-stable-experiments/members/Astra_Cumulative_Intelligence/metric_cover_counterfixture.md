# Exact counterfixture for metric covers and finite polytope compilers

## Result

A two-dimensional orbit generated by two self-adjoint Markov contractions can require order \(1/\rho\) centers for the proposed metric-cover compiler, while an ordinary exact Gram closure learner discovers a two-coordinate realization from 12 short scalar moments. Both learners retain their observations and use the same scalar source interface. This is an exact-moment diagnostic, not a finite-noise superiority claim.

The executable is `metric_cover_counterfixture.py`; it writes JSON and CSV results beside itself. It uses Python's exact `Fraction` arithmetic throughout source responses, nearest-center decisions, basis discovery, closure checks, and prediction verification. There is no floating-point threshold decision.

## A common invariant measure and self-adjoint Markov primitives

Let the source space be the circle with uniform probability measure. Put

\[
f(x)=\sqrt 2\cos x,\qquad
(Ag)(x)=g(-x),\qquad
(Bg)(x)=g(\theta-x).
\]

Both transformations preserve the uniform measure and are involutions. Their Koopman operators are positive, preserve the constant function, are self-adjoint, and are \(L^2\) isometries. Thus the example falls within a common-self-adjoint-Markov source class, not merely abstract rotations.

Define \(f_k(x)=\sqrt2\cos(x-k\theta)\). Then

\[
Af_k=f_{-k},\quad Bf_k=f_{1-k},\quad
\langle f_k,f_l\rangle=\cos((k-l)\theta).
\]

The reachable orbit spans exactly two dimensions when \(\sin\theta\ne0\). Take rational \(\rho=1/n\) and

\[
\theta=2\arctan\rho,\qquad
c=\cos\theta=\frac{1-\rho^2}{1+\rho^2},
\qquad s=\sin\theta=\frac{2\rho}{1+\rho^2}.
\]

Every return moment is rational, computed exactly by \(C_0=1,C_1=c,C_{k+1}=2cC_k-C_{k-1}\). A noisy version is also physically available by sampling a fresh uniform x and multiplying the two evaluated features; the sample is bounded by 2. We do not use that noisy version in this diagnostic.

## The strong ordinary learner

The ordinary learner is not given a basis or the two matrices. It starts with f, tests child-vector projection residuals from scalar Gram values, adds a child when its exact residual is nonzero, and stops when every basis child has zero residual. It discovers \(V=(f,Bf)\). With the convention that coordinate columns represent operator images,

\[
G=\begin{pmatrix}1&c\\c&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2c\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

All four basis-child closure residuals are exactly zero. The learner uses 12 distinct scalar moment values, total queried word length 26, and maximum queried pair length 4. These counts include fitting and closure verification, not just identifying c. Because the discovered span is exactly invariant, the representation predicts every subsequent word; the script checks ten fresh words including long compositions, with separately recorded verification cost.

The permanent representation consists of two basis-word identifiers, their Gram matrix, and two 2-by-2 transition matrices. Source acquisition is constant in the number of future tasks. Exact response bit lengths are also reported: short source values have O(log n) bits. Future exact arithmetic may grow with word length; this is not omitted from a purported constant-time prediction claim.

## Why the metric cover needs many centers

The compiler uses the stated exact threshold \(3\rho^2/4\) for squared distance. Distinct nearby phase states are farther apart than the merge radius because

\[
\|f_{k+1}-f_k\|=2\sin(\theta/2)
=\frac{2\rho}{\sqrt{1+\rho^2}}>\rho.
\]

Let N be the final number of centers. Every center has a source word of length at most N−1, so its phase index has absolute value at most N−1; a frontier child has absolute index at most N. If \((2N-1)\theta\le\pi\), every two distinct phase indices among a child and the centers have distance at least the adjacent distance above. Then all accepted transitions would have to be exact phase matches. A finite nonempty phase set cannot be invariant under both k↦−k and k↦1−k, because their composition is integer translation. Therefore

\[
N>\tfrac12(\pi/\theta+1)=\Omega(1/\rho).
\]

The usual circle packing bound gives \(N=O(1/\rho)\), since accepted new centers have a constant-times-\(\rho\) separation. Thus this family has \(N=\Theta(1/\rho)\). Exact center counts are affected by return-time arithmetic and need not increase smoothly with n.

## Precision limitation

For this basis,

\[
\kappa_2(G)=\frac{1+c}{1-c}=\rho^{-2}.
\]

The exact linear learner therefore does **not** establish a finite-noise sample advantage. Estimating a nearly dependent basis and extrapolating long words can require much higher precision. The comparison establishes that metric-cover size/cost is not controlled by linear rank alone, and that a retained ordinary linear estimator is a necessary strong comparator.

## A structural limitation of exact absolute-convex-hull compilers

Suppose a finite dictionary V spans the orbit plane and gives exact operators \(AV=VC_A\), \(BV=VC_B\), with every column of each C having l1 norm at most one. Then the centrally symmetric polygon

\[
P=\operatorname{absconv}\{v_1,\ldots,v_N\}
\]

satisfies \(AP\subseteq P\) and \(BP\subseteq P\). Since A and B preserve planar area, each inclusion must be equality. Their product is rotation by \(\theta\). If \(\theta/\pi\) is irrational, P is invariant under a dense group of rotations, hence must be a disk, impossible for a finite full-dimensional polygon.

For n≥2 in this rational-cosine construction, \(c=(n^2-1)/(n^2+1)\) is not one of 0, ±1/2, ±1; consequently \(\theta/\pi\) cannot be rational (equivalently, rational cosine of a rational multiple of pi has only those values). One can instead simply choose any irrational angle for the structural statement, without using this number-theoretic fact.

Thus a finite exact l1-stable polytope lift can be impossible even though a two-dimensional Euclidean-isometric realization exists. Approximation, an infinite dictionary, or another learned norm is required. This is a geometry tradeoff, not a failure of the proposed approximate theorem.

The construction uses known-coordinate state grids and state-transition/reachable-set computation. Its stability-based approximation theorem is clear prior art for symbolic approximation, but not the noisy hidden-Gram interface. Retrieved references: `turn27view1`, `turn29view2`.
- Devonport, Saoud, Arcak, *Symbolic Abstractions From Data: A PAC Learning Approach* (CDC 2021): https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13901 . It uses black-box state-successor evaluation and PAC behavioral guarantees. Unknown dynamics alone is not the distinction. Retrieved reference: `turn33academia24`.
- Hashimoto et al., *Learning-based Symbolic Abstractions for Nonlinear Control Systems* (Automatica 2022): https://people.kth.se/~dimos/pdfs/Automatica_Learning_Abstractions_2022.pdf . Algorithm 3 measures full successor-state coordinates and learns the unmodeled dynamics with Gaussian processes. Retrieved reference: `turn29view4`.
- Lavaei and Frazzoli, *Data-Driven Synthesis of Symbolic Abstractions with Guaranteed Confidence*: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09397 . It uses observed consecutive state pairs and a user-chosen function class for a bisimulation certificate. Retrieved reference: `turn33view2`.

Section 2 explicitly checks generator images of frontier vertices against the symmetric convex hull and adds images outside it. The paper assumes a fixed coordinate basis and input matrices. Its purpose is a common extremal norm/joint spectral radius certificate, with particular convergence conditions. The noisy source-only acquisition theorem would need to stand separately. Retrieved references: `turn36search0`, `turn37view0` (especially lines 23–25 and 149–185).

Quadratic moment fitting in a Hilbert space by conditional gradients is established prior art for the kernel/FW subroutine. Section 2 explicitly permits an implicit feature map accessible through its kernel and assumes bounded feature norms. The specific remaining claim is that adaptively acquired noisy return-word correlations suffice to construct and validate an approximately invariant compiler, with its source and numerical costs charged. Retrieved references: `turn36search6`, `turn37view1` (especially lines 87–101).

None of these checks proves that hidden-Gram finite-noise acquisition is novel. The safe claim is a proved synthesis for the explicitly stated source model, pending a wider novelty review.
