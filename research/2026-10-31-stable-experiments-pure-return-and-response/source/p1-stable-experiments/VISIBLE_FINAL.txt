## Result: acquiring a stable calculus of experiments

**I completed a construction that learns reusable operation rules from noisy scalar experiments without being given hidden coordinates, a useful basis, or a well-conditioned Gram matrix.** Once acquired, those rules predict an entire family of later operation sequences without further source calls, with an explicit error bound.

The construction also exposes a substantial limitation: **the smallest stable representation can be much larger than the smallest linear representation.** Strong ordinary retaining learners won important comparisons. This is a completed conditional mathematical mechanism; practical superiority and historical novelty remain unestablished.

## 2. The main theorem

### What the learner receives

There are unknown operators \(T_1,\ldots,T_m\) on a real Hilbert space, satisfying

\[
\|T_a\|\le 1.
\]

Starting from an observable feature \(f\), each finite operation sequence \(w\) defines a hidden feature

\[
v_w=T_wf.
\]

The learner cannot inspect \(v_w\). It can execute an experiment whose bounded, conditionally unbiased report has mean

\[
\langle v_u,v_v\rangle.
\]

This access has a concrete realization for reversible Markov controls sharing a stationary distribution: read an observable, execute the appropriate return word, read again, and multiply the endpoint readings. It does **not** require copying a hidden state.

The actual controls, stationary preparation, reversibility or adjoint implementation, and measurement validity remain substantive premises. Unknown systematic bias is not removed by repetition.

### What it acquires

The learner retains actual word features \(v_1,\ldots,v_N\), together with matrices \(M_a\) satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\|M_a\|_{1\to1}\le1,
\qquad
\left\|T_av_i-\sum_k(M_a)_{ki}v_k\right\|\le\rho.
}
\]

It also acquires the values of a specified finite set of readouts \(g\), to entrywise error \(\beta\).

For **every later word** \(w=a_1\cdots a_L\),

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\langle g,T_wf\rangle
-
\widehat h^{\,T}M_{a_L}\cdots M_{a_1}e_{\mathrm{start}}
\right|
\le L\rho+\beta.
}
\]

Numerical execution error receives an additional explicit budget.

This is one acquisition-confidence event covering the whole stated family. There is no new statistical union-bound penalty for each later query.

**Soundness of a completed certificate requires no rank promise.** A dimension or covering assumption is needed to guarantee termination and bound acquisition cost, rather than to justify the returned prediction certificate.

### Why the error remains controlled

A retained coefficient vector \(c\) always obeys \(\|c\|_1\le1\). Therefore, combining uncertain feature values cannot amplify their worst entrywise uncertainty.

If \(e_t\) is the discrepancy between the true feature after \(t\) operations and the learned representation, then

\[
e_{t+1}=T_ae_t+(T_aV-VM_a)c_t.
\]

Consequently,

\[
\|e_{t+1}\|\le\|e_t\|+\rho,
\]

which gives the \(L\rho\) bound.

The theorem concerns the specified scalar predictions. It does not automatically provide complete-record total variation, rare-event conditional probabilities, arbitrary new readouts, or feedback-policy laws.

## 3. The representation is actually constructed

The central operation is to test whether a newly encountered successor \(y=T_av_i\) can be represented by a **bounded signed combination** of acquired features:

\[
y\approx Vc,\qquad \|c\|_1\le1.
\]

Its squared error is

\[
R(c)=\|y-Vc\|^2
=\langle y,y\rangle-2k^\top c+c^\top Gc.
\]

All entries of \(G\) and \(k\) come from actual scalar experiments.

If every measured entry has error at most \(\tau\), then, simultaneously for every admissible coefficient vector,

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat R(c)-R(c)|\le4\tau.
}
\]

There is **no inverse smallest eigenvalue** in this inequality. It remains valid for coefficients chosen using those same measurements.

The coefficient search is explicit. An inexact Frank–Wolfe procedure scans the measured gradient, selects a signed coordinate, and updates a bounded coefficient vector. With

\[
\tau=\frac{\rho^2}{256},
\qquad
J=\left\lceil\frac{256}{\rho^2}\right\rceil,
\]

the proof gives:

- An accepted fit has true residual below \(\rho\).
- A rejected final fit is farther than \(\rho/\sqrt2\) from the old absolute convex hull.
- Rejection causes the learner to acquire and retain the actual new word feature.

The coefficients have a small-denominator rational representation,

\[
c_J=
\frac{\sum_{t=0}^{J-1}(t+1)s_t}{J(J+1)/2},
\]

so their construction does not hide an exponentially growing product of denominators.

I also repaired an expected-runtime issue. A single inaccurate old Gram entry could otherwise corrupt many later fits. An explicit nearest-feature check before convex fitting ensures that the algorithm still terminates almost surely, with finite expected work, under a finite covering bound.

## 4. What the complete cost says

Let \(N\) be the number of features actually acquired, and \(m\) the number of operations. Suppressing confidence logarithms, the general bounds are:

| Resource | Bound |
|---|---:|
| Distinct scalar Gram quantities acquired | \(O(mN^2)\) |
| Bounded experimental episodes | \(\widetilde O(mN^2\rho^{-4})\) |
| Primitive control executions, including word lengths | \(\widetilde O(mN^3\rho^{-4})\) |
| Coefficient-fitting arithmetic | \(O(mN^2\rho^{-2})\) |
| Later length-\(L\) prediction | At most \(O(LN^2)\) arithmetic |
| Later source calls for an already covered task | \(0\) |

Every episode also pays for stationary preparation and endpoint readout. Weighted return experiments can have much larger report ranges; their repetition costs grow accordingly.

For a requested horizon \(H\) and error \(\epsilon\), one can allocate

\[
\rho=\frac{\epsilon}{2H},
\qquad
\beta=\frac{\epsilon}{2},
\]

with a separate allocation for numerical rounding.

The difficult dependence is \(N\). If the reachable features lie in a real \(r\)-dimensional unit ball, the available general bound is

\[
\boxed{
N\le
\left(1+\frac{2\sqrt2}{\rho}\right)^r.
}
\]

**That is exponential in dimension.** This investigation has not proved efficient general acquisition merely from small rank.

Cumulative extension is nevertheless concrete. When a new operation arrives, the learner keeps old measurements and coefficient rules, tests the new operation on existing features, and acquires any newly activated features. Old coefficient columns remain valid after padding with zeros. A new readout or a stricter accuracy requirement has its own acquisition bill.

## 5. The comparisons changed the construction

### The first representation lost to an ordinary learner

The initial construction retained a table of nearby feature states. In a simulated source with 4,096 microscopic states, both learners received the same return experiments and the same signed-orthonormal-orbit promise.

| Quantity | Initial metric table | Ordinary retaining signed-frame learner |
|---|---:|---:|
| Retained states or directions | 24 | 12 |
| Scalar estimates | 878 | 224 |
| Simulated experimental episodes | 741,073 | 158,896 |
| Primitive control steps charged | 6,048,373 | 1,139,078 |
| Errors in the complete learned transition-table audit | 0 | 0 |
| Disagreements on 1,400 later programs | 0 | 0 |

The later programs included lengths up to 1,000. Both learners used no further source calls for those predictions.

**The ordinary learner won.** It reused an opposite direction through a minus sign, while the initial table stored it separately. I adopted that conventional signed-frame construction for its applicable source family and incorporated bounded signed reuse into the broader theorem.

These are simulated source counts, not physical device measurements. The simulation samples the exact aggregate endpoint-product law and charges the complete underlying experiment lengths.

### Bounded combinations improved the table, with a tradeoff

On a continuous rotation diagnostic at \(\rho=0.0375\):

- The metric table used **81 features and 6,725 Gram queries**.
- The bounded-combination compiler used **10 features and 152 Gram queries**.
- Its largest local residual was approximately **0.03166**, below the requested tolerance.
- All tested predictions satisfied the length-dependent theorem.

The smaller representation sometimes damped correlations and was less accurate than the larger table. This is an acquisition/storage improvement within that comparison, not pointwise prediction dominance. These continuous tests used exact-moment numerical inputs; the generic finite-noise guarantee is analytical.

### A new intervention really did require new information

A separate four-state reversible source initially had two controls that fixed the seed feature. Its entire baseline operation orbit therefore needed one retained feature.

Adding a third actual control activated missing directions. The compiler reused the old cache and rules, acquired the new correlations, and completed an enlarged model. This checks the intended causal distinction: the new response was learned through intervention experiments rather than inferred from unchanged baseline data.

It expanded to 25 features despite only four microscopic states. That is a successful acquisition diagnostic and a poor compression result. It establishes no efficiency advantage over a suitable small model.

## 6. A sharp obstruction: rank is not stable representation complexity

The strongest negative result concerns two self-adjoint Markov reflections whose product is an irrational planar rotation.

The process has an exact **rank-two Euclidean representation**.

Suppose our finite dictionary admitted exact operation rules with every coefficient column having \(\ell_1\)-norm at most one. Its absolute convex hull would be a finite polygon preserved by both reflections, hence by their irrational rotation.

Because the transformations preserve area, the polygon would have to be exactly invariant. Invariance under the dense rotation subgroup would force it to be a disk. A finite polygon cannot be a disk.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{An exact rank-two model can exist while no finite exact}
\ \ell_1\text{-stable dictionary of this form exists.}
}
\]

An ordinary exact Gram-closure learner in the accompanying counterfixture discovers its two-dimensional basis from **12 short moments and 26 primitive word steps**. It is not handed the basis.

Its Gram condition number grows as the relevant separation shrinks, so that exact-data win is not a claim of free noisy extrapolation. The comparison identifies the real tradeoff:

> Avoiding coordinate-conditioning costs can introduce representation-geometry costs.

The broad next problem is to acquire economical coordinates **and an appropriate stability geometry**, with their precision and discovery costs included.

## 7. A separate completed result: knowledge can make genuinely new evidence cheaper

A complementary construction isolates a different cumulative effect.

Consider a depth-\(H\) controlled experiment. Forward moves succeed with probability \(1/2\). After failure, one of two local recovery buttons returns to the current level; the other loses progress. Their identities must be learned through actual transitions.

After learning the recovery controls, one fresh target observation costs exactly

\[
\boxed{3H+1}
\]

primitive steps in expectation.

The explicit acquisition procedure pays for every failed test and replay. Its expected cost, ending at the experiment, is

\[
\boxed{
A_H=5H-2+\frac{3H(H-1)}4.
}
\]

Each later target bit is independent of the acquisition history. Old observations contain **zero information about its answer**. Their value is that they make obtaining new informative evidence cheaper.

A lower bound against **every retaining learner**, combined with the constructive controller, gives the complete lifetime minimax order

\[
\boxed{
\Theta(H^2+TH)
}
\]

for \(T\) fresh noiseless target bits.

An ordinary retaining model learner attains the same order. The comparison does not force a conventional learner to forget. Small numerical checks also allow a stronger Bayesian planner, which improves the simple construction’s constants.

Acquiring policies that expand reachable experiments has substantial established precedent in autonomous exploration and stochastic shortest-path learning. The appendix credits that work and treats this as a fully paid worked theorem, rather than a new field. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

- **Constructive acquisition of operation rules**, without a supplied useful coordinate system.
- **A uniform compositional error certificate** whose soundness does not require a rank bound.
- **Full acquisition, failed-fit, numerical, and deployment costs**, including exceptional-event runtime.
- **Strong retaining alternatives and structural counterexamples** that constrain the claimed consequence.

The main proof received internal mathematical reconstruction with shared construction disclosed. The package distinguishes that proof from finite simulations, exact rational checks, floating-point diagnostics, and unexecuted physical acquisition.

**The most consequential remaining opening is now precise:** discover a representation and stability geometry whose complete acquisition cost tracks the useful future tasks closely, while allowing ordinary retaining learners to use the same evidence and operations. The present construction supplies a complete, conditionally sound route into that problem. Its exponential geometric dependence and the rank-two obstruction show why an economical general solution is still missing.
