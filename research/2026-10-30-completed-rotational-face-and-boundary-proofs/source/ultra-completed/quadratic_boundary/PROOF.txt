                  2Q(R)>=grad C(R)^T H_F(R)^+ grad C(R)       (2)

with delta a strictly positive quartic on the entire real line. Put

                h_C(t,x,y)=U_u^T grad C(R),
                N_C=h_C^T adj(H(t)) h_C / t^2.              (3)

2. WHY THE ROOT PSEUDOINVERSE ALONE MISSES A BOUNDARY CONSTRAINT

In an orthonormal null-adapted basis near t=0 write

 H00=a t^2+O(t^3), H0perp=t b^T+O(t^2), Hperp,perp=D+O(t),
 h0=c t+O(t^2), hperp=g+O(t), D>0.

Then the limit of the cost in (2) contains

              g^T D^-1 g+(c-b^T D^-1 g)^2/(a-b^T D^-1 b).

The cost computed with H(0)^+ contains only the first term. The second
is nonnegative and is generally nonzero for the nontranslation cubic.
Thus checking the pseudoinverse exactly AT a root can miss the constraint
from nearby rank-one directions. Formula (4) retains it. This is why
the numerical construction used deflated acoustic charts, not a small
eigenvalue cutoff or only finite root evaluations.
