I chose programmable separation of nearly identical molecules, using isotope transport as the stress test.

The strongest result is an explicit finite transport construction that reverses which bare mobility travels faster, together with bounds showing what passive and periodically controlled gates can accomplish. I also carried the promising fluctuating-pore mechanism through a finite collector, throughput comparison and energy balance. The tested separators do not earn a performance advantage, and I have not established a transformative physical separator.

The useful outcome is more precise than the initial proposal: a distinction between mechanisms that can coexist thermodynamically, exact limits on their discrimination, and a construction whose finite output survives clock noise and leakage.

1. Where the two search directions met

Working backward, the desired capability was a separator whose discrimination could be programmed through dynamics. That would be useful when molecular size and ordinary chemical affinity offer little distinction. The obstructing operation is the conversion of a small microscopic difference into withdrawn product at useful recovery, throughput and energy cost.

Working forward, flexible pores and fluctuating barriers looked promising. Tang and colleagues’ 2026 study supplies a particularly concrete model and implementation. It predicts substantial amplification of a prescribed mass-dependent mobility difference, including a high-barrier extrapolation of approximately 2.05 for its Cu(IDB) model. Those predictions concern dilute self-diffusion; they do not themselves supply a complete membrane output or controller energy balance. 
Nature Communications

What is established and what should be retained

The retained results are:

A passive comparison law for both long-time diffusion and finite linear permeance under a common equilibrium measure.
A periodic conductance-control law permitting ordering reversal while bounding its magnitude.
An explicit local construction, with exact diffusivity and finite-reservoir output.
A simultaneous finite model containing a noisy clock, nonzero leakage, finite ports and a balanced collector.
A quantitative rejection of the tested efficient-separator proposals, after throughput, preparation and energy are included.

The verification used separate numerical routes: correctors versus tilted generators, closed expressions versus differentiated Floquet matrices, exact finite-port formulas versus affine phase propagators, spatial refinement, conserved face currents, and an independent entropy-production check.
