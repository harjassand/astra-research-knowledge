## 2. The passive limit: thermal gating cannot sharpen a shared scalar mobility contrast

Consider a periodic guest–gate system with transition rates

\[
k_e(d,\gamma)=d\,a_e+\gamma b_e.
\]

The first component scales guest motion; the second scales gate kinetics. Suppose both components separately satisfy detailed balance under the **same equilibrium distribution \(\pi\)**, which is independent of \(d\) and \(\gamma\).

The reversible corrector principle gives

\[
D(d,\gamma)
=
\frac12\inf_\phi
\sum_e
\pi_{i(e)}
(d a_e+\gamma b_e)
\left[\ell_e+\phi_{j(e)}-\phi_{i(e)}\right]^2.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
D(d,\gamma)=\inf_\phi\{dQ_A(\phi)+\gamma Q_B(\phi)\},
\qquad Q_A,Q_B\geq0.
\]

The comparison follows immediately:

\[
d_sQ_A+\gamma Q_B
\;\leq\;
r d_sQ_A+\gamma Q_B
\;\leq\;
r(d_sQ_A+\gamma Q_B).
\]

Taking the infimum yields

\[
\boxed{
1\leq\frac{D(d_f,\gamma)}{D(d_s,\gamma)}\leq r.
}
\]

The same nonnegative quadratic structure proves a finite-membrane conductance bound when the two species have the same equilibrium weights and reservoir activity mapping.

There is a useful interpretation of the derivative:

\[
\frac{\partial\log D}{\partial\log d}
=
\frac{dQ_A(\phi_*)}
{dQ_A(\phi_*)+\gamma Q_B(\phi_*)}
\in[0,1].
\]

The mobility response is the fraction of the optimized transport form carried by guest-rate transitions. A gate can become the bottleneck and suppress discrimination. Within these assumptions, it cannot amplify the supplied mobility contrast.

Another consequence is experimentally discriminating: **increasing a passive gate’s attempt frequency while holding its energies fixed cannot make long-time diffusion rise to a peak and then decline**. Such a turnover requires leaving this model class—for example by changing energies, introducing an external drive, retaining inertia or memory, or allowing species-dependent gate dynamics.

These statements specialize established reversible-network principles. I am not claiming that Rayleigh monotonicity or the underlying Dirichlet method is new.
