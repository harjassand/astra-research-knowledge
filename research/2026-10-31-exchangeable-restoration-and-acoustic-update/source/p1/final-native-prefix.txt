## The strongest construction is restoration through exchangeable probes

I have a new operation that moves the structural obstruction beyond the exponential decomposition LP.

**After a complete adaptive sequence of binary linear observations of an unknown product-distributed cache, the cache can be restored exactly using a number of fresh source calls controlled by the rank of the disclosed constraints.** The operation discovers its restoration set through fresh probes and elementary linear algebra. It does not estimate the source probabilities or search for a global certificate decomposition.

The main bound is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[\text{new source calls}\mid \text{old disclosure transcript}]
\le \frac{2r}{\eta}
}
\]

when the transcript has rank \(r\) and every unknown source bias lies in \([\eta,1-\eta]\). Both probes and replacement draws are counted. At fair inputs, the bound is \(4r\), although the algorithm does not need to know that the inputs are fair.

This changes the earlier obstruction substantially. A parity observation forces the previous one-pass copy-and-refresh mechanism to refresh every participating coordinate. The new operation restores after that same observation in fewer than four expected new source calls at fair inputs, regardless of the parity’s length.

The qualification is essential: **the released probe record contains unordered pairs, while their old/new orientations remain private.** Revealing those orientations can invalidate restoration. I have proved the result for the complete declared record, tested that identity exactly, and constructed explicit failures when the record is mishandled.

There is also an important comparison limit. A fresh factory can generate linear observations alone at comparable source cost. The distinctive operation here is **repair after an observation has already been made and must be preserved**, while retaining the rest of a cached world and its computations. I have not established an end-to-end advantage for a scientific learner merely from the inexpensive sketch-generation case.

## 1. The question and the required record

Let the paid cache be

\[
X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n),\qquad
X_i\sim\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)
\]

independently. The numerical \(p_i\) are unknown. A fresh independent draw from coordinate source \(i\) costs one call.

An analyst has already asked questions of this **same** cache:

\[
h_t=a_t^\top X\pmod 2.
\]

The next question \(a_t\), the decision to stop, and any subsequent model choice may depend on earlier answers and external randomness. All such decisions belong to the recorded protocol.

For a fixed realized transcript \(R_0\), collect its questions and answers as

\[
A_{R_0}X=b_{R_0}.
\]

After removing redundant equations, let

\[
r=\operatorname{rank}(A_{R_0}).
\]

The target is to retain the actual transcript and produce a new cache \(Y\) such that

\[
\mathcal L(Y\mid R)=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i),
\]

where \(R\) includes the old transcript **and the restoration metadata we release**.

This is a post-selection problem. Generating a different observation with the same marginal distribution does not repair the dependence created by the observation already on the record.

### Why adaptive questions still give affine constraints

Fix the external random choices used by the questioning algorithm. Any \(x\) satisfying all the recorded equations produces the same successive answers, therefore causes the algorithm to ask the same questions and stop in the same place.

Consequently, the likelihood of a particular complete transcript has the form

\[
\Pr(R_0\mid X=x)
=
c(R_0)\,\mathbf 1\{A_{R_0}x=b_{R_0}\},
\]

where \(c(R_0)\) does not depend on \(x\).

Thus an adaptive interrogation can be compressed into an affine constraint system **without discarding any part of its disclosure history**. The rank is the rank of the entire transcript, not merely the final question or final model.

This distinction matters. If an earlier observation selected the final matrix, omitting that observation can leave information about coordinates that the final matrix does not mention.

## 2. The new primitive: an unordered pair has a hidden fair orientation

Pair one cached value with one fresh draw from the same source:

\[
(X_i,Z_i),\qquad X_i,Z_i\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\operatorname{Bern}(p_i).
\]

Release only its unordered form, equivalently its count

\[
U_i=X_i+Z_i\in\{0,1,2\}.
\]

Conditional on \(U_i=1\),

\[
\Pr((X_i,Z_i)=(0,1)\mid U_i=1)
=
\Pr((X_i,Z_i)=(1,0)\mid U_i=1)
=
\frac12.
\]

The unknown \(p_i\) cancels because both ordered pairs have probability \(p_i(1-p_i)\).

This is the classical symmetry behind von Neumann’s unbiased-bit extraction. The use here is different: the orientation is left hidden so that it can remove the information a previously disclosed decision carries about untouched coordinates. The extraction principle itself is established machinery. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

### General principle

The useful object is an **exchangeable orbit under which the decision’s likelihood becomes constant across the untouched cache**.

For a selected set \(S\), obtain independent partners \(Z_i\) and retain unordered pairs \(U_S\). Conditional on those pairs, average the decision channel over all equally likely old/new orientations.

If that averaged probability,

\[
\overline A_h(U_S,x_{-S}),
\]

does not depend on \(x_{-S}\), then the disclosed decision and unordered-pair record do not change the law of the untouched coordinates. Freshly replacing the coordinates in \(S\) restores the whole product law.

The previous positive-cylinder construction obtained constancy pointwise, by making the complete selection probability depend only on selected coordinates. This construction obtains constancy **after averaging over exchangeable orientations**. Fresh probes supply the additional factors that make that averaging possible.

The principle applies to general product sources and exchangeable pairs. Binary linear observations give a particularly cheap, explicit way to verify its condition.

## 3. An executable rank-based restoration rule

Fix the complete old transcript and write its reduced system as

\[
AX=b,\qquad A\in\mathbb F_2^{r\times n}.
\]

Let \(a_i\) denote column \(i\).

Maintain:

- \(S\): coordinates probed so far;
- \(D\subseteq S\): coordinates whose old and fresh values disagreed;
- \(V=\operatorname{span}\{a_i:i\in D\}\).

At each step, choose an unprobed coordinate whose column is outside \(V\). Probe it once. A disagreement adds its column to \(V\).

Stop when

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}\{a_i:i\notin S\}\subseteq V.
}
\tag{1}
\]

Then replace every probed coordinate with a fresh independent draw from its original source.

A direct implementation is:

```python
def restore_after_linear_transcript(cache, columns, old_draw):
    probed = []
    unordered_pairs = []
    span = BinarySpan()

    while True:
        i = find_unprobed_column_outside_span(columns, probed, span)
        if i is None:
            break

        partner = old_draw(i)
        pair_count = cache[i] + partner

        probed.append(i)
        unordered_pairs.append((i, pair_count))

        if pair_count == 1:
            span.add(columns[i])

    for i in probed:
        cache[i] = old_draw(i)

    return cache, unordered_pairs
```

The selection rule uses the recorded matrix and previous unordered probe results. It does not inspect unprobed cache values to choose where to probe.

The public record is

\[
R=(R_0,\text{probe order},U_S,\text{stopping information}).
\]

Source-call counts and the selected set are determined by this record and may also be released.

### Exactness theorem

For every product law \(P=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Bern}(p_i)\),

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L(Y\mid R)=P.}
\tag{2}
\]

This holds for adaptive linear questioning of the original cache, not just a matrix selected independently of it.

### Proof

Fix a possible complete record \(R\).

For a probed coordinate:

- \(U_i=0\) fixes its old value to \(0\);
- \(U_i=2\) fixes its old value to \(1\);
- \(U_i=1\) leaves its old value as a fair hidden orientation.

Before imposing the old equations, these orientations are independent conditional on the unordered pairs.

Let \(c(U_S)\) be the contribution to \(AX\) from equal pairs whose old value is \(1\). The disagreement coordinates contribute a uniformly distributed element of \(V\). Therefore, for any untouched assignment \(z=x_{-S}\),

\[
\Pr(AX=b\mid U_S,X_{-S}=z)
=
2^{-\dim V}
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
b-c(U_S)-A_{-S}z\in V
\right\}.
\]

By the stopping condition, \(A_{-S}z\in V\) for every \(z\). The expression becomes

\[
2^{-\dim V}
\mathbf 1\{b-c(U_S)\in V\},
\]

which is independent of \(z\).

The probability of following the recorded probe path also depends only on the recorded transcript and unordered pairs. Hence

\[
\Pr(R,X_{-S}=z)
=
C_R\,P_{-S}(z)
\]

for a constant \(C_R\). Thus

\[
\mathcal L(X_{-S}\mid R)=P_{-S}.
\]

The final replacement draws give an independent \(P_S\), proving (2). ∎

### Why this leaves the previous characterization intact

The previous converse concerned a subset selected before fresh draws, through a parameter-independent selection kernel. Here the selected set depends on new source draws. Their probabilities introduce factors such as

\[
P_i(X_i)P_i(Z_i),
\]

whose exchangeability is precisely what the proof uses.

The new operation also has a different permissible auxiliary record. It supports unordered pairs; it does not support releasing every old selected value.

Under the common requirement \(Y\mid(H,S)\sim P\), however, the difference is substantive: parity requires all participating coordinates in the previous one-pass class, while the new mechanism can use constant expected work.

## 4. Source cost, including the conditional-history distinction

Every probed coordinate costs two new calls:

1. the partner draw;
2. the final replacement draw.

Thus

\[
C=2|S|.
\]

Each disagreement increases \(\dim V\) by one, because the algorithm only probes columns outside the current span. There can be at most \(r\) such increases.

### Bound after an arbitrary adaptive transcript

Suppose

\[
p_i\in[\eta,1-\eta].
\]

The old bit may have a strongly biased conditional distribution after the transcript. Nevertheless, conditional even on its particular value, a fresh partner disagrees with probability at least \(\eta\):

\[
\Pr(Z_i\ne X_i\mid X_i,\text{history})
\ge \eta.
\]

Therefore

\[
\eta\,\mathbb E[|S|\mid R_0]
\le
\mathbb E[|D|\mid R_0]
\le r,
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[C\mid R_0]\le 2r/\eta.
}
\tag{3}
\]

This uses a valid conditional bound. It does not assume that a posterior cached bit still has its original marginal law.

There is also the deterministic bound

\[
C\le 2n_{\mathrm{active}},
\]

where \(n_{\mathrm{active}}\) is the number of nonzero columns.

### Sharper bound when the matrix was fixed before inspecting this cache

If \(A\) was fixed conditional on the preceding public history, and the probing policy ignores the current answers, then an unprobed pair has disagreement probability

\[
d_i=2p_i(1-p_i).
\]

Writing \(d=\min_i d_i\),

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[C]\le 2r/d.
}
\tag{4}
\]

Under the same bias interval, this is

\[
\mathbb E[C]\le \frac{r}{\eta(1-\eta)}.
\]

Equation (4) is an expectation before conditioning on a particular current answer. Equation (3) is the robust bound conditional on a complete adaptive transcript. They should not be interchanged.

### Parity gives an exact formula

For one parity, use a fixed coordinate order. The first disagreement suffices to stop. If no disagreement occurs, all participating coordinates are replaced.

The exact expected cost is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E C
=
2\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\prod_{j=1}^{k}
\left[p_j^2+(1-p_j)^2\right].
}
\tag{5}
\]

At fair inputs,

\[
\mathbb E C=4(1-2^{-n})<4.
\]

Near deterministic inputs, the cost approaches \(2n\). The operation therefore does not contradict the earlier worst-case information lower bounds.

A full refresh remains a valid alternative. If an appropriate bias bound is supplied, the controller can choose between the proved bounds without estimating any \(p_i\). A capped probing attempt followed by full refresh is also exact; its fallback flag belongs to the record.

## 5. The structural witness is obtained by linear algebra

The rank condition is directly executable. It does not require solving the global decomposition LP.

For a stored matrix, a segment tree can retain a basis for the columns in each interval. To find an unprobed column outside \(V\), the search skips an interval whenever its stored basis is contained in \(V\).

Using a left-to-right probe order makes this particularly simple:

- previously skipped columns remain in \(V\), since \(V\) only grows;
- previously probed columns lie before the search cursor;
- an entire remaining interval can be skipped by one span-inclusion test.

A straightforward implementation has:

- preprocessing polynomial in the rank and linear in the number of columns, up to rank factors;
- roughly \(O(r^2\log n)\) bit-vector operations per probe;
- comparable update work for a changed matrix column;
- \(O(r|S|)\) work to update a cached linear sketch after replacing \(S\).

Large bit vectors have their usual word-length cost. These are computational costs in addition to source calls.

A newly supplied dense matrix still costs at least its input size to read. Repeated queries with a stored matrix, a stored family of matrices, or local column edits can reuse the preprocessing. This is a polynomial construction with an explicit acquisition bill; it is not a claim that arbitrary new structure is free.

### A broader algebraic extension

Suppose a decision channel has an explicit sparse Fourier representation on binary inputs,

\[
A_h(x)=\sum_T \widehat A_h(T)\chi_T(x),
\qquad
\chi_T(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in T}x_i}.
\]

A disagreement at coordinate \(i\) makes the hidden orientation fair and annihilates every Fourier term containing \(i\) when we average over orientations.

One can probe a coordinate from a still nonconstant surviving term until every surviving term is constant on the untouched coordinates. If there are \(K\) distinct nonconstant supports, every disagreement removes at least one, giving the analogous bound \(2K/\eta\).

The linear-transcript result is stronger than explicitly enumerating Fourier terms: a rank-\(r\) linear observation can have exponentially many relevant characters, while the span calculation represents all of them at once.

This identifies a broader mechanism: **use exchangeability to remove the parts of a decision channel that depend on untouched variables.**

## 6. Adaptive model updates and actual inference guarantees

### Model updates

After the full record \(R\), suppose the chosen new model has product law

\[
Q_R=\bigotimes_i Q_{i,R}.
\]

For probed coordinates, draw directly from \(Q_{i,R}\). For untouched coordinates, apply independent kernels satisfying

\[
P_iK_{i,R}=Q_{i,R}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{\mathcal L(Y\mid R)=Q_R.}
\]

The proof is immediate from the conditional product law of the untouched coordinates established above.

This makes the previous model-update wrapper usable after the new restoration mechanism. Unchanged coordinates can be copied. Changed coordinates can use an appropriate transport kernel, including the earlier source-only primitive where its access assumptions hold.

The source interface remains explicit: probes need access to the old coordinate source; replacements or transports need access to the selected new version. Dependent DAG nodes cannot be treated as independent coordinates merely because they are individually stored. Whole independent traces or a justified independent-source representation remain valid choices.

### Repeated use

If each restoration is completed before the next interrogation, the next cache has the correct product law conditional on the entire preceding released record.

Therefore:

- a fixed query produces independent output answers across rounds;
- an adaptively chosen query has the correct fresh-world conditional law;
- the full cache versions remain correlated.

The third point is unchanged. The construction creates valid future query opportunities; it does not turn every retained cache version into an independent dataset.

### Approximate structure with an inference-relevant guarantee

There is also a useful relaxation.

Suppose a real decision \(H=f(X)\) is approximated by a function of a linear sketch,

\[
H^\star=g(AX),
\qquad
\Pr_P(H\ne H^\star)\le\varepsilon.
\]

Run the restoration procedure for \(A\), with probing independent of which decision value occurred. Include the actual decision in the record.

Couple the actual and ideal procedures using the same cache, probes, and replacement randomness. Their records differ only on the approximation-error event. The ideal procedure restores exactly. A triangle inequality gives

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{TV}
\left(
\mathcal L(R,Y),
\mathcal L(R)\otimes P
\right)
\le 2\varepsilon.
}
\tag{6}
\]

This controls the **joint record-and-cache law**, which is the relevant object for subsequent adaptive use.

For a finite protocol, if the corresponding bounds hold at every allowed history, the errors compose to at most

\[
2\sum_t\varepsilon_t.
\]

A bounded test valid at level \(\alpha\) under the ideal protocol is then valid at level at most

\[
\alpha+2\sum_t\varepsilon_t.
\]

The approximation certificate must cover the complete released decision channel. A guarantee for only the last reported statistic does not cover earlier disclosures that influenced it. Acquiring or validating the approximation also has to be charged. Equation (6) does not supply free calibration, a stationary-law guarantee, or control of an unbounded estimator.

### Exact finite-state checks

I enumerated old configurations, probe outcomes, complete unordered-pair records, and untouched configurations using rational arithmetic.

For every valid record, I checked

\[
\Pr(R,X_{-S}=z)=\Pr(R)P_{-S}(z).
\]

Independent replacement then gives the full restored-cache identity.

| Test | Complete probe records | Exact factorization error | Expected new calls |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Five-coordinate parity, fair inputs | 94 | \(0\) | \(31/8\) |
| Rank-two sketch on five fair coordinates | 292 | \(0\) | \(55/8\) |
| Same rank-two sketch, heterogeneous rational biases | 292 | \(0\) | \(2112622/297675\) |
| Rank-three sketch, heterogeneous rational biases | 664 | \(0\) | \(1730045/190512\) |
| Branching adaptive question protocol | 310 | \(0\) | \(62722/8505\) |

The adaptive protocol first asked a parity of all five bits. Its answer selected the second question; the second answer determined whether a third question was asked. Its six possible old transcript leaves were retained in the check.

I also tested the approximate guarantee. A four-bit decision differed from parity on one of sixteen equally likely inputs. Thus \(\varepsilon=1/16\). The exact joint dependence after repair was

\[
11/128<1/8=2\varepsilon.
\]

It exceeded \(\varepsilon\), so replacing the proved factor \(2\) by \(1\) would not have been justified by that test.
