P1 COMPUTATIONAL REUSE BOUNDARIES — WORKING DERIVATIONS

Scope: local mathematical analysis and executable checks only. No external
writes, uploads, purchases, new helpers, or integration. Results below do not
claim priority. The conditional result depends on an unproved conjecture.

1. A FINITE COUNTEREXAMPLE TO FULL ADAPTIVE OPTIMALITY OF FIXED-BATCH DP

Let S,A,B be independent unknown probabilities with Beta(1/2,1/2) priors.
The scientific target is the read-once Boolean multiplexer mean

  theta = S A + (1-S) B.

Each observation is a fresh Bernoulli observation of one chosen probability.
The Bayes estimator is the posterior target mean; loss is squared error.
There are two observations available.

The prior variance of each probability is 1/8. The exact multilinear
variance formula gives

  Var(theta) = (1/4+1/8)(1/8+1/8) = 3/32 = 24/256.

All fixed allocations (order does not matter) have learned-mean variances:

  S,S: 0
  S,A or S,B: 5/256
  A,A or B,B: 1/48
  A,B: 1/32 = 8/256.

Hence the optimal fixed batch observes A and B once and has Bayes risk
24/256-8/256 = 16/256 = 1/16.

An adaptive policy first observes S. If that observation is one, it observes
A; if zero, it observes B. Each of the four two-observation transcripts has
probability 1/4. The final posterior target mean is 5/16 or 11/16, each with
probability 1/2. Its learned-mean variance is 9/256 and Bayes risk 15/256.
This is a 6.25% reduction of Bayes risk, or a 12.5% increase in learned
variance, relative to the exact best fixed batch. The gain is strict with
ordinary full-support positive Beta priors, without a limiting argument.

The exact script enumerates all 27 deterministic depth-two policies:
three first actions and three possible second actions after each first
outcome. It verifies this adaptive policy is globally optimal at budget two,
including all repeated-source choices. Randomizing policies cannot improve
their maximum because the expected gain is linear in the mixture.

More generally, for independent Beta(a,a) priors and s=2a,

  best fixed gain = 1/[8(s+1)^2],
  selector-first adaptive gain = (s+2)^2/[16(s+1)^4].

The latter is better for s<sqrt(2), i.e. a<1/sqrt(2).
This does not invalidate the proposed fixed-batch theorem. Independent fixed
experiments in disjoint subtrees produce independent posterior child means;
adaptive cross-subtree budget allocation destroys that ex-ante independence.
Posterior independence conditional on a transcript does not restore the
independence of the random posterior means across transcripts.

2. A ROBUST CONDITIONAL NO-GO FOR GENERAL SHARED BOOLEAN CIRCUITS

Assumption H (preprocessed OuMv): after arbitrary polynomial preprocessing
of an n by n Boolean matrix M, no randomized algorithm processes n online
pairs (u_t,v_t) of Boolean n-vectors and outputs all Boolean-semiring
products u_t^T M v_t before the next pair arrives in total online time
O(n^(3-eta)), for any fixed eta>0, with success probability at least 2/3.
This is the polynomial-preprocessing OuMv consequence of the OMv conjecture.
The 2015 original was identified but not newly read; an actually read
primary 2026 paper explicitly states this formulation (source below).

Theorem. Under H, no algorithm can, for every bounded-fan-in Boolean
scientific circuit of size N, use polynomial preprocessing and maintain
its expected output under arbitrary local root-probability changes with
both

  update cost U(N)=O(N^(1/2-epsilon)),
  fixed-output mean-query cost Q(N)=O(N^(1-epsilon))

for a fixed epsilon>0, while guaranteeing absolute mean error <=1/8 on all
queries with joint success probability >=2/3. This holds even when every
primitive probability stays in [1/4,3/4] and every queried scientific mean
lies in [1/4,3/4]. Gate truth tables and the final observable stay fixed.
Only local unary primitive probabilities change. Internal gates are
deterministic, as in an ordinary Boolean simulator; the bounded-bias
restriction applies to primitive independent sources, not all gate CPTs.

Proof. Start with

  F_M(u,v) = OR over (i,j) with M_ij=1 of (u_i AND v_j).

For every one of its 2n input bits b, substitute a majority of L independent
Bernoulli roots, each of probability 1/4 if b=0 and 3/4 if b=1. Choose odd
L >= 8 log(16n). Hoeffding's elementary Bernoulli tail bound gives each
decoder error probability <=exp(-L/8)<=1/(16n). Coupling all decoders to
their intended bits and applying a union bound gives

  |E F_M(decoded roots) - F_M(u,v)| <= 1/8.

The majority function has a bounded-fan-in monotone circuit of O(L^2)
gates, for example using the threshold recurrence
T(i,k)=T(i-1,k) OR [X_i AND T(i-1,k-1)]. The matrix circuit has O(n^2)
gates. Thus the whole circuit has O(n^2+n log^2 n)=O(n^2) gates; pad if
necessary to make N=Theta(n^2). Copy trees also bound fan-out if required.

Add independent roots C~Bernoulli(1/4) and D~Bernoulli(2/3), and use

  G = C OR (D AND F_M).

Then E G = 1/4+(1/2) E F_M. When the Boolean product is zero, E G<=5/16;
when it is one, E G>=11/16. All these means lie in [1/4,3/4]. Absolute
error 1/8 therefore determines the Boolean product by thresholding at 1/2.

For each arriving vector pair, change at most 2nL primitive probabilities
and make one G-mean query. Over n rounds the online time is

  O(n^2 log n U(Theta(n^2)) + n Q(Theta(n^2))),

which would be truly subcubic under the stated costs. Polynomial circuit
preprocessing is allowed by H. This contradicts H. The local updates in
this reduction are fully reversible, since arbitrary new vectors arrive.

A per-query bounded-error promise can be amplified using O(log n)
independent copies when its stated input/history contract permits such
amplification. The clean statement above uses a simultaneous trace
guarantee. A fixed MSE <=1/256 also separates the two ranges with error
probability <=1/9 by Markov's inequality, then admits ordinary independent
amplification. This conversion must not assume hidden independence of
reused randomized states.

Comparison with a strong ordinary incremental algorithm. For the unnoised
Boolean problem retain row counts r_i=sum_j M_ij v_j and the integer count
z=sum_i u_i r_i. A u_i update changes z in O(1). A v_j update changes all
r_i and z in O(n). A query tests z>0 in O(1). Preprocessing/storage are
O(n^2). For the noisy construction, on the promised query states where
each replica group has a common bias, the approximation
1/4+(1/2)F_M(u,v) has mean error <=1/16. Thus this ordinary retained
algorithm already matches the lower-bound exponent on the hard query
family. A complete resimulation after every local update is an inadequate
comparator: retained row sums already remove a factor n.

The reduction relies on shared root use in many terms. It does not rule out
the proposed read-once tree compiler, nor polylogarithmic updates on bounded
treewidth families. It is a worst-case, conjecture-conditional boundary,
not a lower bound for every individual matrix, trajectory, or application.

3. FIXED RESPONSE SPAN: EXACT GAUSSIAN REUSE IS ORDINARY WOODBURY

Let A0 be SPD, B be N by r, b0 be fixed, and C be N by m. Consider Gaussian
precision A=A0+B D B^T and information vector b=b0+B g, with A SPD.
The desired posterior means are C^T A^-1 b, and covariances C^T A^-1 C.
Retain

  K=B^T A0^-1 B, q=B^T A0^-1 b0, G=C^T A0^-1 B,
  y0=C^T A0^-1 b0, H=C^T A0^-1 C.

Then set z=(I+D K)^-1(g-D q). The exact answers are

  y=y0+G z,
  covariance=H-G(I+D K)^-1 D G^T.

Proof for means: x=A0^-1 b0+A0^-1 B z; substituting into Ax=b gives
(I+D K)z=g-Dq. The covariance formula follows by substitution or the same
identity on each column of C. These are deterministic identities, hence
valid for arbitrarily adaptive (D,g) choices that preserve SPD.

After the paid baseline factorization and r+m+1 solves, retained response
storage is O(r^2+mr+m^2). Mean queries cost O(r^3+mr); all m by m
covariances cost O(r^3+mr^2+m^2r). A rank-one D
update permits O(r^2) reduced inverse maintenance. No ambient-N cost is
required for these predeclared outputs. But this is exactly the
Schur-complement/Woodbury comparator, not a new reuse algorithm. When
arbitrary coordinate directions accumulate, r may reach N.

The script checks means and covariances by exact Fraction arithmetic on
12 adaptive updates, comparing retained r=2 responses with full N=4 solves.

4. A UNIFORM RESIDUAL CERTIFICATE FOR SPARSE LOCAL PARAMETER STATES

Fix A0 SPD, a dictionary B=[b_1,...,b_r], fixed right-hand side f0, a
reduced basis V of dimension k, and a scalar observable c. For a support
I of at most s active parameters, write

  A=A0+sum_{i in I} theta_i b_i b_i^T,
  f=f0+sum_{i in I} gamma_i b_i.

Assume A >= alpha I_N for a known alpha>0. This is automatic for
theta_i>=0 if a valid baseline eigenvalue lower bound alpha is known.
Let a be a reduced solution and x_tilde=V a. Its residual is

  r=f-A V a
   =f0-A0 V a+sum_{i in I} b_i(gamma_i-theta_i b_i^T V a),

so r belongs to L_I=span{f0, columns of A0 V, b_i:i in I}.
Each space has dimension at most h=k+s+1. There are at most binom(r,s)
spaces needed, since each smaller support is contained in a size-s support.

Draw a single d by N matrix S of independent N(0,1/d) entries AFTER
A0,B,V,f0 are fixed. For 0<epsilon<1, it suffices to take

  d >= (32/epsilon^2) [h log 9+s log(e r/s)+log(2/delta)]

(with the s log term zero when s=0). With probability >=1-delta,

  (1-epsilon)||w||^2 <= ||S w||^2 <= (1+epsilon)||w||^2

for EVERY w in EVERY L_I simultaneously. In particular, an unlimited
sequence of adaptively chosen supports, coefficients, and reduced
solutions can reuse the same certificate. No extra union bound over time
is needed. V or the dictionary must not be fitted to S without a new
uniform guarantee; prior data-dependent choice before S is allowed.

Self-contained probability proof. For a fixed unit vector w,
d||S w||^2 is chi-squared(d). Its moment generating function yields
Pr(| ||S w||^2-1 |>t)<=2 exp(-d t^2/8), 0<t<1. Each h-dimensional unit
sphere has a 1/4-net of at most 9^h points. Control each net quadratic
form to epsilon/2; the symmetric-matrix net argument enlarges this to
operator norm epsilon because the net approximation costs at most
2(1/4)||S^T S-I||. Union bound over nets and supports proves the formula.

Since A>=alpha I, the scientific-output error obeys

  |c^T(A^-1 f - V a)|
   <= ||c|| ||r|| / alpha
   <= ||c|| ||S r|| / [alpha sqrt(1-epsilon)].

Retain S f0, S A0 V, S B, B^T V, V^T A0 V, V^T f0, and c^T V.
Each local precision change is rank one in the reduced k by k matrix.
Reduced factor updates cost O(k^2); query solving costs O(k^2), and the
residual sketch costs O(d(k+s)+sk). The retained dictionary sketches
cost O(dr) storage; the offline applications and any basis discovery are
paid. For local coordinate directions B=I, S B is simply S.

This certifies a returned approximation if its observed bound passes the
tolerance. A low-dimensional certificate alone does NOT ensure that the
bound will be small or that all updates will be cheap: full solves,
enrichment, recompilation and rejection/fallback work must be charged.
If the sparse support assumption or the coercivity bound fails, the stated
certificate does not apply. The relevant s is the number of simultaneously
active deviations, not the number of coordinates changed at one instant.
The displayed certificate is an exact-arithmetic theorem. A floating-point
implementation must include its residual-evaluation and coercivity-bound
rounding errors; the Gaussian sketch theorem is not itself an interval-
arithmetic validation of a particular implementation.

Prior-art comparison: pyMOR already compiles residual Gram matrices offline,
evaluates their norms online, divides by a coercivity lower bound, and
multiplies by the output functional's dual norm. Its source explicitly
warns that an appropriate coercivity estimate is required for a certified
upper bound. The union-of-subspaces calculation here isolates a useful
adaptive local-update contract; it is not a claim to have invented
reduced-basis residual certification or randomized model reduction.

5. WHY LOCAL EDITS ALONE DO NOT JUSTIFY A SMALL FIXED SKETCH

For any fixed linear S:R^N->R^d with d<N, some nonzero v has S v=0.
Consequently no positive lower norm bound valid for all residual vectors
can be supplied from ||S r|| alone. An adaptive analyst given exact squared
sketch norms can reconstruct G=S^T S with N(N+1)/2 queries:

  G_ii=||S e_i||^2,
  G_ij=(||S(e_i+e_j)||^2-G_ii-G_jj)/2.

It can then choose a kernel vector. That vector can be reached from zero
by N separate single-coordinate evidence edits. Per-step locality therefore
does not prevent cumulative escape from the certified family.

Exact small witness: S has rows (1,0,0,0) and (0,1,1,1). It preserves
norms exactly on every span{e1,e_i}, i=2,3,4. Thus a fixed common direction
plus ONE active update direction is certified uniformly. Two local edits
can produce r=e2-e3, with ||r||^2=2 but ||S r||^2=0. The script reconstructs
S^T S from its ten norm queries and verifies this cancellation exactly.
This is a limitation of fixed linear certificates, not a lower bound
against all algorithms for the toy linear system (which is itself easy).

7. IDENTICAL LOCAL LEARNED VARIANCE DOES NOT COMPOSE ADAPTIVELY

This is a new exact finite derivation, requiring no external source.

Let S be a parameter with prior Pr(S=0)=Pr(S=1)=1/2. Two one-unit local
experiments have the following channels, for 0<a<1/2:

  Binary-symmetric: report Y=S with probability 1/2+a, otherwise 1-S.
  Erasure: reveal S with probability rho=4a^2, otherwise report '?'.

For M=E[S|Y], the binary-symmetric posterior mean is 1/2-a or 1/2+a,
equiprobably. The erasure posterior mean is 0,1/2,1 with probabilities
2a^2, 1-4a^2, 2a^2. Both experiments have exactly the same

  prior mean 1/2,
  prior variance 1/4,
  observation cost 1,
  learned variance Var(M)=a^2,
  expected remaining variance E Var(S|Y)=1/4-a^2.

Now insert the selector into a read-once multiplexer target

  theta = S P + (1-S) Q,

where P,Q are independent of S and the channel, have the same prior mean
m, and offer one local experiment each whose posterior mean has variance
u>0. After observing the selector channel, choose one of these two local
experiments. The selector observation alone leaves the target mean m
unchanged. Conditional on M, observing P gives learned target variance
u M^2, while observing Q gives u(1-M)^2. Hence the exact optimal total
gain is

  u E max(M^2,(1-M)^2)
   =u [1/4+a^2+E|M-1/2|].

For the binary-symmetric channel E|M-1/2|=a. For the erasure channel it
equals 2a^2. Thus the corresponding gains are

  G_binary = u(1/4+a+a^2),
  G_erasure = u(1/4+3a^2),
  G_binary-G_erasure = u a(1-2a)>0.

At a=1/4, both local selector gains are 1/16, but the exact continuation
multipliers are 9/16 and 7/16 respectively. With independent
P,Q~Beta(1/2,1/2), one ordinary Bernoulli observation of a branch has
learned variance u=1/16. The scientific target's prior variance is 1/8,
so the channel-plus-continuation comparison is

  binary: total learned variance 9/256, final Bayes risk 23/256;
  erasure: total learned variance 7/256, final Bayes risk 25/256.

For a completely finite latent-variable example, take P and Q themselves
to be independent fair {0,1} parameters. One Bernoulli sample reveals the
chosen branch, so u=1/4 and the target prior variance is 1/4. The gains
are 9/64 versus 7/64, and Bayes risks 7/64 versus 9/64.

The updated exact script derives posterior distributions directly by
Bayes' rule from both channel tables and exhausts all four binary-channel
continuation policies and all eight erasure-channel continuation policies.
It verifies both comparisons above in Fraction arithmetic.

Interpretation and scope. This proves that a local interface retaining only
the mean and learned variance (even also the prior variance and expected
remaining variance) cannot support every exact adaptive continuation.
The two local experiments are identical to that interface but have different
values in the same read-once parent context. A whole fixed-budget profile
can also be made identical by declaring each module one-use only, with
additional local budget yielding no new observations; then both profiles
are V(0)=0 and V(b)=a^2 for every b>=1.

This does not invalidate the fixed-independent-experiment Phi formula.
There the squared multiaffine target is integrated against independent
child posterior means, so the first two moments suffice. The adaptive
maximum here introduces an absolute-value term, requiring distributional
information absent from those moments. A richer exact Bellman interface
must retain information sufficient for the continuation's value function;
this example alone does not prove that every such interface must retain
the entire posterior distribution.

The channel comparison applies to a general local-experiment interface.
It is not a claim that two specific fixed-count Beta-Bernoulli experiments
with the same fully retained Beta hyperparameters are indistinguishable.
