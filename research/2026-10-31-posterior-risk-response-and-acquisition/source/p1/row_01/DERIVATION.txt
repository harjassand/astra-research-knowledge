EXACT TWO-STEP ADAPTIVE DESIGN FROM A RETAINED READ-ONCE RISK TREE
Independent derivation and implementation audit, 31 October 2026

Status
======

The parent's proposed ancestor/sibling formula is correct under the stated
product-posterior, read-once, direct-source contract. The exact implementation
returns a globally optimal two-step adaptive policy. The interval version
returns a concrete policy with a rigorous lower bound on its gain and an
upper bound on the exact optimum. The independent exhaustive checks below
found no implementation error.

This is a constructive capability, not an asymptotic separation from equally
equipped ordinary incremental inference. An ordinary method that retains the
same one-step risk compiler can enumerate hypothetical first observations,
update the affected ancestor path, read the best second experiment, and undo
the update. That method has the same O(n h) bounded-arity arithmetic cost.
The explicit derivative formula avoids rebuilding full node tables for each
hypothetical outcome, but no timing win is asserted by this audit.

No outside paper was retrieved or used for this audit. The proof is included
in full. No historical novelty or priority claim is made.

1. Contract and notation
=======================

There are L primitive Bernoulli sources with unknown probabilities p_i. The
current conditional distribution of their probabilities is an independent
product of Beta(alpha_i,beta_i) laws. Direct source observations have
likelihood p_i^x(1-p_i)^(1-x). Past source selections may have been adaptive;
conditional on the complete realized history, the product posterior and
its exact counts are retained. A policy-selection factor that depends only
on the observed past is constant in p and cancels from Bayes' rule.

The current target f(p) is represented by a supplied read-once tree. Every
gate g_v is multiaffine in its children, and separate child subtrees have
disjoint primitive leaves. The node target, denoted Theta_v, is the resulting
scalar functional of its primitive probabilities. Its current mean is m_v.
Bounded truth tables in [0,1] imply Theta_v in [0,1].

The target and observation model stay fixed during the next two observations.
Loss is squared error in f(p), and the estimator after these observations is
the posterior mean. The objective is the expected reduction in conditional
Bayes squared-error risk, equivalently the variance of the final posterior
mean. This is not a uniform frequentist MSE claim.

At each gate retain the derivatives

    d_v,a  = partial_a g_v(m_children),
    d_v,ab = partial_a partial_b g_v(m_children), a != b,

and the exact best one-observation gain V_v(1) in its subtree, with an
attaining primitive leaf. The existing risk compiler constructs these from
explicit local truth tables. No general decomposition or restoration oracle
is assumed. This audit needs budget one, not the fixed-budget-two table.

The root outside derivative at v is

    c_v = partial f / partial Theta_v

with the other subtree means held at their current values. It is the product
of derivatives on the path from v to the root. The root has c_root=1, and

    c_child = c_parent * d_parent,child.

It is independent of the mean m_v itself. All c_v are computed in one paid
top-down pass. They are not assumed to remain cheap to update after data
arrive elsewhere in the tree.

2. First observation and repeat gain
===================================

For a leaf i write a=alpha_i, b=beta_i, s=a+b and m_i=a/s. Its current best
one-observation learned variance is

    u_i = a b / [s^2 (s+1)^2].

Because f is affine in p_i, the first observation's root gain is

    I_i = c_i^2 u_i.

If the first observation is x in {0,1}, its predictive probability is m_i
for x=1 and 1-m_i for x=0. The new leaf posterior is Beta(a+x,b+1-x), so

    m_i^x = (a+x)/(s+1),
    Delta_i^x = m_i^x - m_i = (x-m_i)/(s+1).

The gain from a second observation of that same leaf is

    J_i^x = c_i^2 * (a+x)(b+1-x)
                         / [(s+1)^2 (s+2)^2].

The outside derivative c_i does not change after observing i, since it is
independent of the leaf's own parameter. This remains true if c_i=0.

3. Compressing all other second actions into sibling groups
=========================================================

Fix the first leaf i and its outcome x. Walk upward from i. At an ancestor v,
let child a contain i, and let b denote any different child. Every primitive
leaf j in subtree b is untouched by the first observation. Its internal
posterior, mean derivatives, and best one-call value V_b(1) are unchanged.

Only the mean of child a changes at v. If its mean change is Delta_a^x,
multiaffinity gives the exact identities

    m_v^x - m_v = d_v,a Delta_a^x,

    partial_b g_v(new means)
       = d_v,b + d_v,ab Delta_a^x.

The root derivative c_v is independent of the value at v. Consequently,
the largest conditional root gain from any second observation in subtree b
is exactly

    J_v,b^x = c_v^2 (d_v,b + d_v,ab Delta_a^x)^2 V_b(1).

The best leaf previously retained for V_b(1) attains this value. The new root
factor is common to every leaf in b, and it is nonnegative after squaring.

Every second primitive leaf other than i belongs to exactly one such sibling
subtree: take the lowest common ancestor of i and that leaf. Hence these
groups, together with the repeat-i option, exhaust all possible second
actions, without enumerating each pair of primitive sources.

Starting with Delta_i^x, the exact upward recurrence

    Delta_parent^x = d_parent,changed_child Delta_child^x

supplies every required change. No division by a derivative is used.
This matters: a first observation with zero immediate target gain can still
make a different second observation valuable. A selector in a balanced mux
is an explicit example. Algorithms that divide by a zero root sensitivity
or discard all zero-immediate-gain first actions would fail.

4. Exact optimal adaptive policy
===============================

The best conditional second-observation gain after x is

    B_i^x = max { J_i^x, J_v,b^x over the sibling groups on i's path }.

The value of starting with i and then choosing its optimal continuation is

    A_i = I_i + (1-m_i) B_i^0 + m_i B_i^1.

This is the conditional total-variance identity, or equivalently the
orthogonality of the two posterior-mean martingale increments. It does not
assume that the eventual adaptive observations are an independent product
experiment. Product independence is used separately at each conditioned
history, where the direct-source likelihood justifies it.

Finally choose i maximizing A_i and store the attaining second source for
each outcome. The resulting policy is a triple

    (first source, second source after 0, second source after 1).

This is optimal among all deterministic two-observation adaptive policies.
Randomized policies cannot improve the optimum: condition on their private
randomness and average deterministic policy values. Two observations always
are feasible; a source may be measured twice. A nonnegative-gain model makes
using at most two rather than exactly two observations equivalent if there
is no separate cost penalty.

The proof says nothing about an optimal three-step policy, arbitrary horizon,
or the consistency of repeatedly applying a short-horizon policy. The
read-once, fixed-current-target, and direct-source conditions are material.

5. Fully paid computational accounting
======================================

Let N be total nodes, L primitive leaves, h maximum leaf depth and k maximum
arity. Existing means, derivatives, and best one-step gains take a paid
O(N) build for fixed k, plus the explicit local truth-table construction.
The table has 2^k entries; it is not free when k grows. A real observation
or a target-gate edit affects its ancestor path and retains the existing
O(h) bounded-arity update cost for that base representation.

One exact adaptive-two-step query then costs

    O(N + sum_{leaf i} sum_{v on path(i)} degree(v))
        <= O(N + L k h)

arithmetic operations, including the outside-derivative pass, scanning each
first action, the two conditional outcomes, and recovering the second action.
Extra working storage is O(N), or O(N+k h) explicitly including a temporary
path's sibling candidates. A balanced bounded-arity tree gives O(N log N)
query work. A deep chain can have quadratic total path length.

Exact Fraction bit lengths can grow. These are arithmetic-operation bounds,
not a unit-cost exact-rational runtime claim. The interval implementation
offers an explicit numerical regret certificate instead of asserting exact
selection from binary64 arithmetic.

No O(h) maintenance theorem for the globally optimal adaptive first action
has been established. Current outside sensitivities can change broadly after
one observation, and the query deliberately recomputes them. Its entire
scan is charged each time adaptive-two-step planning is requested.

Strong ordinary comparison
--------------------------

Retain exactly the same ordinary product posterior and one-step compiler.
For each possible first source i and each outcome x:

  (a) hypothetically update i and its ancestor path;
  (b) read the retained root's one-step optimum;
  (c) restore i's prior counts and recompute the path.

Use total variance to evaluate the two-step gain. This costs O(L h) for
bounded arity, matching the derivative formula's asymptotic complexity.
No hypothetical outcome calls a physical source. The exact baseline is
implemented in the parent's adaptive_planner.py and verified here.

Therefore an n-squared pair scan is not the strongest retained baseline.
The shared enabling mechanism is the compiled conditional one-step value
and its cheap hypothetical maintenance. Ordinary adoption of this mechanism
inherits the capability. The explicit sibling formula provides a direct
closed-form execution path; a further wall-clock or asymptotic advantage
requires evidence beyond this audit.

6. Interval certificate
=======================

The parent code propagates outward binary64 enclosures through the same
formula. Each subtree's one-step entry has the invariant

    L_b <= gain of its selected leaf <= optimal one-step gain <= U_b.

For a sibling group, its true shared derivative factor and both its selected
gain and optimum are enclosed by the interval computation. The repeat-leaf
gain is enclosed directly. Selecting the candidate with greatest lower
endpoint yields an achievable lower guarantee; taking the greatest upper
endpoint over all groups bounds the true best second action.

For each possible first source, multiplying by enclosures of its predictive
outcome probabilities and adding its first-observation gain encloses the
value of the returned policy and the best policy with that first source.
Choosing the first source by greatest lower endpoint, and taking the greatest
upper endpoint over all first sources, proves

    L <= Gain(returned concrete policy) <= A_optimal <= U.

Correlations between interval quantities can widen these enclosures; they
do not invalidate them. Every true target and future posterior mean lies in
[0,1], so gain lies in [0,1/4]. Intersecting with that independently proved
range is sound. The displayed planning-regret bound U-L needs its subtraction
rounded upward, or needs to be represented as an exact rational difference.

This only covers roundoff for valid finite numeric inputs and the declared
Bayesian model. It does not cover observation-model misspecification or
hidden shared primitives. A certificate can be wide and uninformative.

8. Requested short audit: finite-feature experiment ordering
===========================================================

The following broader ordering theorem proposed by the parent is sound. It
is independent of the adaptive-two-step algorithm, and is recorded here to
make the scope check explicit.

Let X_1,...,X_n have a fixed common product prior. At coordinate i choose a
finite real feature vector phi_i(X_i) with E phi_i=0 and Cov(phi_i)=I. These
features are square-integrable. If the original covariance is singular,
remove its null directions before whitening. Augment psi_i=(1,phi_i).

A fixed product experiment E generates independent local data D_i, with
each D_i depending only on X_i. Put

    B_i^E = Cov(E[phi_i(X_i) | D_i]),
    M_i^E = diag(1,B_i^E).

Total covariance gives 0 <= B_i^E <= I in Loewner order. The posterior mean
of any tensor-feature target

    f(X) = sum_{j_1,...,j_n} c_{j_1...j_n}
                            product_i psi_i,j_i(X_i)

is obtained by substituting E[psi_i|D_i] into that expansion. The local
posterior feature-mean vectors are independent across i and have mean
(1,0,...,0). Their second-moment matrices are M_i^E. Therefore

    Gain_E(f) = c^T (tensor_product_i M_i^E) c - c_0^2,

where c_0 is the coefficient of the all-constant feature. The current prior
variance is ||c||^2-c_0^2, because the product basis is orthonormal.

For two fixed product experiments E,F under this SAME prior and SAME feature
system, the following statements are equivalent:

  (i) B_i^E >= B_i^F for every i in Loewner order.
  (ii) Gain_E(f) >= Gain_F(f) for every target in the full tensor-feature span.

To prove sufficiency, telescope the difference of the tensor products one
coordinate at a time. Each summand is a tensor product of positive
semidefinite factors and one positive semidefinite difference, and hence is
positive semidefinite. For necessity choose the single-coordinate target
f(X)=a^T phi_i(X_i); its gain difference is a^T(B_i^E-B_i^F)a for arbitrary a.

The scalar learned-variance fraction rho is the d_i=1 case after normalizing
the feature by its prior standard deviation. The matrix theorem covers
nonlinear finite feature classes as well as the raw multiaffine family.

Scope points:

* This is an order for squared-error risk on the declared full target span.
  It is not Blackwell order or dominance for arbitrary decisions.
* If a narrower target class is imposed, the single-coordinate test functions
  needed for necessity must still belong to that class. In particular, one
  should not silently claim necessity for a bounded subclass when features
  are unbounded and those test functions are excluded.
* Local channels must be fixed and independent. Outcome-adaptive channels
  can correlate the posterior feature means, invalidating the tensor product.
* Feature construction, whitening, local posterior calculation, and the
  experiment matrices are paid inputs or computations. This does not supply
  an oracle for acquiring them cheaply.
* Matrix-valued experiment profiles need not have a universal maximizer.
  For a concrete witness, one coordinate X consists of d independent fair
  signs, phi(X) is the d-vector of those signs, and a budget-k experiment
  reveals a chosen subset S of k signs. Then B_S is diagonal with ones on S
  and zeros elsewhere. All binomial(d,k) such profiles are pairwise
  incomparable in Loewner order. With k near d/2, a complete nondominated
  profile list is exponential in d. Thus the ordering theorem alone does
  not imply an efficient general budget-allocation compiler.

No new priority claim is made for the conditional-expectation, orthonormal
feature, or positive-operator identities used in this short audit.
