P1: COMPILING THE VALUE OF EXPERIMENTS
Retained inference representations, conditional Bayes risk, and adaptive planning
Working scientific result, 2026-10-31 branch

STATUS

This document gives proved identities, constructive algorithms, numerical
certificates, executed checks, and failures. It does not establish historical
priority, a general-purpose inference breakthrough, or practical superiority
over an equally equipped ordinary incremental method. The central algebra is
the centered-product / ANOVA variance identity. The proposed contribution is
the explicit experiment interface and its constructive, maintained optimization
compiler, with the statistical and numerical contracts kept intact.

The scientific question is now: given evidence about uncertain primitive
reliabilities, can retained computation determine which next experiments most
reduce uncertainty in a specified system property? The objective is useful
inference, rather than the number of repaired or generated sample bits.

The main positive result is an exact fixed-batch Bayes-risk optimizer on a
supplied read-once multiaffine tree. For bounded arity and a two-observation
budget it has linear construction and storage, ancestor-path updates, and
explicit recovered experiment choices. A second result solves the genuinely
adaptive two-observation problem in O(n h) arithmetic for bounded arity using
the same representation. Ordinary reversible hypothetical updates attain the
same adaptive complexity; that comparator is implemented and checked.

The broad structural principle is that fixed independent experiments compose
through their explained covariance on the target's feature space. A single
learned-variance number is sufficient for a scalar multiaffine interface.
Matrices are needed for multiple features, and adaptive continuation can need
more than either fixed-experiment scalar variance or a scalar budget profile.
The precise positive and negative statements follow below.

1. THE SCIENTIFIC CONTRACT AND THE COMPLETE RECORD

There are n stationary primitive sources. Conditional on p=(p_1,...,p_n), a
call to source i returns an independent Bernoulli(p_i) bit. Calling a source
does not produce a new system trajectory, and reusing a numerical circuit
does not acquire additional physical evidence. All calls are counted.

For the constructive implementation the declared prior is a product of
Beta(alpha_i,beta_i) distributions, with alpha_i,beta_i>0. After a complete
history H of direct source observations, the current posterior is the
corresponding product of updated beta laws. Loss is squared error in a
system reliability theta(p), so the Bayes estimate is E[theta|H], and its
conditional risk is Var(theta|H). This is a Bayesian statement under the
declared model, not a uniform frequentist MSE or confidence guarantee.

The target theta is supplied as a read-once tree. At each internal node v,
the child targets are combined by a scalar multiaffine function g_v. A gate
is supplied as its 2^k values on the Boolean cube, each in [0,1], and evaluated
by multiaffine interpolation. Therefore every subtree target lies in [0,1].
Child subtrees use disjoint primitive parameters. A Boolean read-once circuit
gives its ordinary reliability in this way. Soft gates with values in [0,1]
are also admitted. Shared primitive references are not silently duplicated.
Write N for the total number of nodes. Linear build/storage claims use N;
for trees with at least two children at every internal node, N=O(n). If unary
chains are retained, their nodes and actual height are paid separately.

The learner record includes the initial prior and circuit, every acquired
source identifier and bit, all predictable experiment choices, target edits,
and the deterministic maintenance trace and reported numerical certificates.
For a predictable randomized source policy pi, its likelihood factors as

  Pr_p(H=h) = k(h) product_i p_i^(success_i(h))
                                  (1-p_i)^(failure_i(h)),

where k(h) contains the policy randomization probabilities, whose arguments
are already observed portions of h. They do not depend on p once h is fixed.
Bayes' rule therefore gives the product beta posterior even with adaptive
source counts, adaptive stopping, and adaptive choices of the next target.
Every result below is conditional on the actual complete past history.

This factorization requires the direct-leaf likelihood. Observing a composite
system outcome, changing the primitive source law, or sharing a parameter
between purportedly independent child scopes may destroy it. The code's gate
edit changes the TARGET functional while keeping the observation model fixed.
It is not arbitrary likelihood repair or evidence assimilation in a general
graphical model. A target chosen from H is fixed when conditioning on H; it
must stay fixed over the contemplated future batch for that batch's objective.

The synthetic harness additionally retains generating p values to reproduce
and audit the source. Those values are not passed to the learner or any
compared inference method. The risk statements condition on the complete
learner filtration, not on an auditor's extra knowledge of ground truth. The
large execution measures computational work and checks the record; one drawn
truth does not establish frequentist calibration or an empirical Bayes-risk
average. No fresh-cache independence claim is made by this construction.

2. FIXED EXPERIMENTS HAVE A COMPLETE SCALAR COMPARISON

First consider independent scalar parameters X_i with known current means
m_i and finite variances v_i. A fixed product experiment consists of local
data D_i depending only on X_i, with independent experiment randomization
across coordinates. Set

  M_i = E[X_i | D_i],   u_i = Var(M_i),
  rho_i = u_i/v_i for v_i>0.

The law of total variance gives 0<=u_i<=v_i. Deterministic coordinates can
be removed. Let f be any multiaffine target fixed before these data arrive.
Its exact centered expansion is

  f(X) = sum_{S subset [n]} partial_S f(m)
                              product_{i in S}(X_i-m_i).

For distinct sets S and T, a coordinate in their symmetric difference occurs
to the first power in the product of the corresponding centered terms.
Independence makes that product's expectation zero. Thus these terms are
orthogonal, and

  Var(f(X)) = sum_{S nonempty} [partial_S f(m)]^2 product_{i in S} v_i.

Product posterior conditioning gives E[f(X)|D]=f(M). The M_i are themselves
independent, with means m_i and variances u_i. Applying the same expansion,

  Gain_D(f) := Var(E[f(X)|D])
             = sum_{S nonempty} [partial_S f(m)]^2 product_{i in S} u_i
             = sum_{S nonempty} A_S product_{i in S} rho_i,

  A_S := [partial_S f(m)]^2 product_{i in S} v_i >= 0.

By total variance, Gain_D(f) is exactly the reduction in expected squared-loss
Bayes risk. No posterior independence across different future transcripts is
asserted; this calculation uses independence of the random posterior means
across the fixed product channels before their outcomes are observed.

THEOREM 1 (complete scalar order for the declared decision class).
For two fixed product experiments E,F under the SAME product prior,

  Gain_E(f) >= Gain_F(f) for every multiaffine f

if and only if rho_i(E)>=rho_i(F) for every nondeterministic coordinate.

Proof. Sufficiency follows termwise from the nonnegative coefficient formula.
Necessity follows by taking f(X)=X_i. QED.

This gives a target-independent interface for comparing fixed local experiment
modules. It does not say that rho can be acquired cheaply, that each module
has an experiment dominating every alternative, or that knowing rho suffices
to update its posterior after actual data. The full local evidence and a
constructible local posterior/experiment model remain necessary resources.

3. THE FEATURE-SPACE VERSION AND ITS COMPUTATIONAL LIMIT

The scalar principle is a special case of a finite-dimensional covariance
statement. For independent X_i, fix real centered L2 feature vectors phi_i
with Cov(phi_i)=I. Remove null directions and pay any whitening construction.
Augment them as psi_i=(1,phi_i). For a fixed local channel D_i define

  B_i = Cov(E[phi_i(X_i)|D_i]),   0 <= B_i <= I in Loewner order,
  M_i = diag(1,B_i).

Let f lie in the tensor-product feature span, with coefficient tensor c:

  f(X) = sum_{a_1,...,a_n} c_(a_1,...,a_n)
                              product_i psi_i,a_i(X_i).

Then E f=c_0. Independence of the fixed local channels gives

  Gain_D(f) = c^T (tensor_product_i M_i) c - c_0^2.

Indeed, E[psi_i|D_i]=(1,E[phi_i|D_i]); its second-moment matrix is M_i because
the posterior-feature means have expectation zero. The second moment of the
conditional expectation of f is the displayed tensor contraction. Total
covariance proves the bounds on B_i.

THEOREM 2 (complete covariance order on a finite feature span).
Under a common product prior and fixed independent local channels, coordinate
Loewner dominance B_i(E)>=B_i(F) is necessary and sufficient for experiment E
to have at least as much squared-loss risk reduction for EVERY target in the
whole tensor-product feature span.

Proof. Necessity tests targets a^T phi_i. For sufficiency, telescope the
difference of the two tensor products, replacing one factor at a time. Each
summand is a tensor product of positive semidefinite factors, one of which is
the corresponding positive semidefinite difference. Every summand is positive
semidefinite, hence so is their sum. QED.

The scalar rho order is obtained with phi_i=(X_i-m_i)/sqrt(v_i). This is a
decision-class-specific order, not Blackwell dominance or equivalence for
arbitrary future decision problems. For unbounded features the theorem is
stated for all finite-L2 targets in the span; it does not silently substitute
a narrower bounded-target class in the necessity direction.

The theorem is not an efficient general tensor algorithm. Feature acquisition,
dimension, basis construction, and contractions are paid. Moreover, covariance
matrices are only partially ordered. For a two-feature module, two one-cost
experiments with B_E=diag(1,0) and B_F=diag(0,1) are incomparable. A parent
depending on the first feature prefers E; a parent depending on the second
prefers F. Maximizing one scalar such as trace cannot preserve all contexts.
An unrestricted matrix-valued design interface can require a large Pareto
frontier. The scalar positive recurrence below avoids that obstruction under
its explicit one-target-per-subtree, multiaffine-parent contract.

For a concrete list-size witness, let a module contain d independent fair
signs and use those signs as its features. A k-cost experiment may reveal
any k signs. Its covariance matrix is B_S=diag(1_S), and the binomial(d,k)
matrices are pairwise Loewner-incomparable. Thus an explicit nondominated-
profile list can be exponential near k=d/2. This is NOT a lower bound against
every compact representation or optimizer: the subset family has a compact
description, and optimizing a single linear-feature target here is just a
top-k selection. It does rule out representing every context by one best
matrix and cautions against replacing the original exponential LP by an
equally large enumerated profile list.

4. THE LOCAL VARIANCE POLYNOMIAL

At a node with k independent child targets X_j, means m_j, and gate g, define

  Phi_g(z_1,...,z_k)
    = sum_{empty != S subset [k]} [partial_S g(m)]^2 product_{j in S} z_j.

Two exact uses of the SAME polynomial are:

  current posterior variance = Phi_g(child posterior variances);
  fixed-experiment risk reduction = Phi_g(child learned variances).

The first follows from the orthogonal expansion. For the second, let each
child receive an independent experiment confined to its subtree. Its new
posterior mean has the original child mean and learned variance u_j. These
posterior means are independent. The parent's posterior mean is their
multiaffine combination, so its variance is Phi_g(u). This proof needs only
the first two moments of each child posterior mean; it does not approximate
the child's actual posterior distribution by two moments.

Crucially Phi has NONNEGATIVE coefficients, even for nonmonotone or signed-
coefficient gates. It is therefore coordinatewise nondecreasing on nonnegative
arguments. Increasing a child's learned variance cannot lower the parent's
fixed-experiment risk reduction, regardless of the other children.

5. THE CONSTRUCTIVE FIXED-BATCH OPTIMIZER

For a beta leaf with alpha,beta>0, put s=alpha+beta and

  v = alpha beta / [s^2(s+1)].

After b direct Bernoulli observations let K denote the success count. It has
beta-binomial variance b(s+b)v, and the new posterior mean is (alpha+K)/(s+b).
Therefore its exact learned variance is

  V_leaf(b) = v b/(s+b), including V_leaf(0)=0.

For a subtree v, let V_v(b) be the largest learned variance obtainable from
a FIXED batch of exactly b direct leaf observations inside it. Repeated
observations of one source are allowed. Then

  V_v(b) = max_{b_1+...+b_k=b} Phi_v(V_1(b_1),...,V_k(b_k)).       (DP)

THEOREM 3. This recurrence gives the exact global optimum over all fixed
b-observation batches and stores a concrete attaining allocation.

Proof. A batch induces a child budget allocation. Conditional on that
allocation, child observations are independent and disjointly feasible. For
any child, replacing its batch by the child-optimal batch at the same budget
can only increase Phi, by nonnegative coefficients. Thus the best value for
this allocation is exactly the displayed expression. Enumerate allocations
and induct over the tree. Randomizing a batch before its outcomes averages
the deterministic batch values and cannot beat their maximum. QED.

For budget two write a_i=partial_i g(m), a_ij=partial_ij g(m), u_i=V_i(1),
w_i=V_i(2). The recurrence simplifies to

  V_v(1) = max_i a_i^2 u_i,

  V_v(2) = max { max_i a_i^2 w_i,
                max_{i<j} [a_i^2 u_i+a_j^2 u_j+a_ij^2 u_i u_j] }.

The last term prices experimental complementarity. Two individually
uninformative child experiments can have a strictly positive joint value.
The recurrence requires O(k^2) candidates after local derivatives are known.

Actual representation construction, rather than an oracle:

* Each supplied 2^k gate table undergoes a Mobius transform to get monomial
  coefficients, and a shifted tensor transform to get all partial derivatives
  at the current child means. Each transform uses O(k 2^k) arithmetic.
* Retain each node's current mean, variance, derivatives, values V(0...B),
  and maximizing child-budget allocations. Retain all beta hyperparameters,
  observations, and structural gate tables as well.
* There are binomial(B+k,k) nonnegative child allocations across b<=B.
  Straight evaluation costs O(2^k) per allocation. For fixed k this is
  O(n 2^k B^k) arithmetic overall, with O(nB) budget-table storage plus gates
  and derivatives. Reading an arbitrary 2^k table is not made free.
* At B=2 and bounded k, build and storage are O(n). One new leaf observation
  or one local gate-table edit recomputes only ancestors, O(h) arithmetic.
  The actual leaf plan is recovered in O(Bh) work. A balanced tree has
  h=O(log n); an unbalanced chain can have h=Theta(n).
* With multiple edits, update the union of affected ancestor paths. There is
  no general guarantee of sublinear work for a broad or dense target edit.
* No global polynomial expansion, exponential restoration LP, decomposition
  oracle, or known numerical primitive probabilities are input to the code.

The stated costs count arithmetic operations. Exact Fraction numerators and
denominators can grow; exact bit operations are not constant-time. The large
execution instead pays finite floating interval arithmetic and certifies the
returned approximation to the optimization problem, as specified in section 7.

6. GENUINELY ADAPTIVE TWO-OBSERVATION PLANNING

Fixed-batch planning does not solve adaptive planning. Here there is a separate
constructive exact result for two observations. It uses the maintained
one-observation values V_v(1), means, and local derivatives.

Let c_v=partial theta_root / partial theta_v at the current means. A top-down
pass computes all c_v: c_root=1 and c_child=c_parent partial_child g_parent.
For a proposed first leaf i, let m_i be its current mean. Upon observing
x in {0,1}, let delta_i^x=(x-m_i)/(s_i+1) be its mean change. Propagate the
mean change along its path by

  delta_parent^x = partial_a g_parent(m) delta_a^x,

where a is the changed child. Multiaffinity makes this exact, without any
linearization remainder.

At an ancestor v, every possible second leaf in a sibling subtree b shares
the same changed outside derivative. If a is the child containing i, then
its group's optimal second-observation gain is

  c_v^2 [partial_b g_v(m)+partial_ab g_v(m) delta_a^x]^2 V_b(1).

The child-b posterior and its own best one-call experiment are unchanged by
observing i. Its internal maximum can therefore be reused. The root-to-v
factor c_v is unchanged because ancestor derivatives along that path do not
depend on the value of the path child. All other possible second leaves are
partitioned uniquely into these sibling groups, together with leaf i itself.

The repeat-i gain is c_i^2 times its updated leaf one-call gain. With
alpha'=alpha_i+x, beta'=beta_i+1-x, s'=s_i+1, it is

  c_i^2 alpha' beta' / [s'^2(s'+1)^2].

Let T_i^x be the maximum of the repeat and sibling-group terms. Then the
value of choosing i first and optimally adapting the second choice is

  A_i = c_i^2 V_i(1) + (1-m_i) T_i^0 + m_i T_i^1.

THEOREM 4. max_i A_i is the exact optimal two-observation adaptive Bayes-risk
reduction. The stored maximizers recover a concrete policy consisting of the
first source and one second source for each first outcome.

Proof. Total variance splits risk reduction into the gain of the first
observation plus the expected conditional gain of the second. The sibling
partition computes the exact best second observation at each of the two
possible posteriors. Maximize over first actions. A randomized decision
cannot improve a finite maximum at either stage. QED.

No division by a derivative is used. In particular, a selector experiment
with ZERO immediate target derivative can be optimal because it changes the
value of a later branch experiment.

The outside-derivative pass costs O(nodes). For each first leaf the algorithm
visits its ancestors and their siblings for both outcomes, costing
O(sum_i sum_(v ancestor i) arity(v)), or O(n h) for bounded arity. Extra
storage is O(n). All this query work is paid. The current code does NOT
maintain the globally best adaptive first action in O(h) after a real update.

The strongest elementary incremental comparator has the SAME complexity:
for each candidate i and x, temporarily update the product posterior with
that hypothetical bit, recompute its ancestor path in the one-step compiler,
read the root's best remaining gain, and restore the saved leaf posterior
and ancestors. No source is called for these hypothetical computations.
An exact reversible_hypothetical_baseline is implemented and agrees with
independent exhaustive enumeration. The explicit sibling formula avoids
repeated posterior mutations and makes the decision structure transparent;
no asymptotic separation from that ordinary baseline is claimed.

7. WHAT THE NUMERICAL CERTIFICATE MEANS

The Fraction backend implements the exact identities for rational input. The
large workload uses binary64 intervals rounded outward with nextafter after
each arithmetic operation. Squaring handles intervals straddling zero. Input
rationals are enclosed before arithmetic. Stored leaf prior parameters are
normalized to Fraction before observations increment their counts.

Because all target and posterior-mean values lie in [0,1], their variances
and learned variances lie in [0,1/4]. Intersecting arithmetic enclosures with
these independently proved ranges is sound. This clipping is not a generic
rule for arbitrary real gates or different objectives. Finite, valid IEEE
operations are required; malformed IV values, NaN/infinity, overflow, and
unrepresentably extreme inputs are outside the supported numeric scope.

For every node and fixed budget the maintained invariant is

  L_v(b) <= Gain(concrete returned plan) <= V_v^*(b) <= U_v(b).

To see it inductively, every candidate allocation is evaluated with intervals
that enclose both the gains of the chosen child plans and each child's exact
optimal value. Nonnegative coefficients ensure the independently optimal
child plans give that allocation's true optimum. Select the candidate with
largest LOWER endpoint, retaining its concrete allocation, and set the upper
endpoint to the maximum upper endpoint over all candidates. The lower bound
is achievable by the selected plan, while the upper bound covers the optimum.
The two endpoints need not describe the value of the same plan.

The adaptive two-step query uses the same invariant: choose a concrete
second-source branch by the largest lower endpoint for each outcome, and
retain the maximum upper bound across branches; then do the same across
first sources. Interval arithmetic also encloses the outcome probabilities
and changed derivatives. The resulting certificate is

  L <= Gain(returned adaptive policy) <= A^* <= U.

For either planner its optimization regret is at most U-L. The reported width
is rounded UP, including the endpoint subtraction. Floating selections are
not asserted exactly optimal merely because their intervals are narrow.

The reported posterior estimate is the actually rounded midpoint a of its
mean enclosure. Its distance from the true posterior mean is bounded by the
maximum distance to either endpoint, rounded up. Consequently

  E[(theta-a)^2 | H] <= variance_upper + numerical_mean_error^2,

with the square and final sum rounded up as well. The gain objective concerns
the ideal posterior-mean Bayes estimator; numerical estimation error is
accounted for separately. Intervals certify arithmetic under the declared
statistical model, not prior correctness or an unmodelled observation law.

An independent review found three implementation/reporting issues during
development: unnormalized floating prior parameters before count updates,
potential downward rounding of a displayed interval width, and acceptance
of negative child indices. All were corrected. The original checks and
findings remain as historical review records; the resolution note and
targeted resolution checks identify the corrected status.

9. THE FAIR COMPARISON

An ordinary product posterior sees exactly the same observations, counts,
priors, gate tables and maintenance record. Given the same retained moment
and allocation tables, it attains the SAME inference answers, sampling plans
and computational bounds. This compiler is compatible with ordinary Bayesian
inference; it is not a competing source of scientific evidence.

The actual improvement is a constructive representation of a coupled
acquisition objective. One-step importance alone can fail (below); computing
all candidate pairs independently is unnecessary; full recompilation after
every update is unnecessary. An ordinary algorithm adopting the proved
recurrence obtains the capability. That does not create an estimator-level
separation, nor does it establish historical novelty of the recurrence.

Direct fresh system simulation can estimate a known-parameter reliability.
For the present task the parameters are uncertain and the question concerns
posterior risk reduction from acquiring parameter evidence. Parameter draws,
nested predictive simulation, or numerical quadrature do not replace that
evidence. Strong direct-generation methods may use the same factorization
and compiler and then coincide with it. A comparison forbidding their access
to the representation would be artificial.

A fully adaptive optimizer can beat the fixed-batch result, as shown by the
exact examples below. The two-step extension handles precisely that horizon,
but it costs O(nh) per query. The fixed-pair optimum is maintained much more
cheaply. The appropriate choice depends on the price of real experiments,
planning, the horizon and the target. No universal tradeoff victory is proved.

10. IMPORTANT FAILURES AND SEPARATIONS

10.1 One-step greed can remain permanently blind to a useful interaction.

Let p_0,p_1,p_2 be independent Beta(1,1), and let

  theta = (1/10)p_0 + (9/10)(p_1+p_2-2p_1p_2).

The prior variance is 7/300. The immediate source gains are
  [1/3600, 0, 0].
Two observations of p_0 give gain 1/2400. Observing p_1 and p_2 once each
gives gain 1/400, six times as much. After ANY finite history sampling only
p_0 its next gain remains positive, while the other immediate gains remain
zero. Strict one-step greedy selection therefore keeps sampling p_0 forever,
leaving the parity contribution's variance 9/400 unlearned. The two-call
compiler discovers this complementarity without a global polynomial.

10.2 A fixed short horizon is not a consistency theorem.

Replace the parity term by the parity probability on d independent uniform
beta parameters:

  parity_d(p) = [1-product_(j=1..d)(1-2p_j)]/2.

Any transcript observing fewer than d distinct parity coordinates leaves
at least one posterior mean equal to 1/2. The posterior mean of parity_d
therefore remains exactly 1/2. This holds for fixed or adaptive experiments
of horizon B<d. Such a horizon gives this entire component zero value.
With the positive-gain decoy p_0, the optimal short-horizon planner can keep
allocating its whole budget to the decoy indefinitely. At d=3 and B=2 the
unlearned parity variance floor is 3/400; sixteen executed updates selected
the decoy twice every time. The general floor is 81/[400*3^d].

Forced exploration could restore posterior consistency for a fixed finite
stationary collection if every source is sampled infinitely often, by the
Bernoulli strong law and posterior concentration. That requires additional
paid exploration and supplies no optimal finite-cost rate here. It is not
part of the executed planner or a cure asserted without a budget.

10.3 Adaptive two-step allocation can beat the best fixed pair even for AND.

Let p_1,p_2 be independent Beta(1,1) and theta=p_1 p_2. The best fixed pair
observes each once, with gain 19/1296=76/5184. Observe p_1 first, then repeat
p_1 after a failure and observe p_2 after a success. First gain is 1/144;
conditional second gains are 1/288 and 1/81, each outcome having probability
1/2. The total is

  1/144 + (1/2)(1/288+1/81) = 77/5184.

The adaptive advantage is 1/5184. The exact adaptive compiler attains it.

A zero-immediate-gain example is the multiplexer theta=S A+(1-S)B with
independent Beta(1/2,1/2) parameters. Its prior variance is 3/32. Best fixed
gain is 1/32, while observing S and then A after a one or B after a zero
gives 9/256. The respective remaining risks are 16/256 and 15/256. All
27 depth-two deterministic policies were independently enumerated. The
selector's immediate derivative is zero, so pruning it for that reason
would incorrectly delete the optimal adaptive first action.

10.4 Identical local variance profiles do not support every continuation.

Let S be a fair binary parameter. For 0<a<1/2 compare two one-use channels.
The binary-symmetric channel reports S correctly with probability 1/2+a.
The erasure channel reveals S with probability 4a^2 and otherwise reports
an erasure. The posterior mean M is respectively

  1/2-a or 1/2+a, equally likely;
  0,1/2,1 with probabilities 2a^2,1-4a^2,2a^2.

Both have Var(M)=a^2, the same cost and prior variance, and the same expected
remaining variance. Declare each module one-use; then their whole fixed-
budget profiles agree: V(0)=0, V(b)=a^2 for b>=1.

Use the parent target S P+(1-S)Q, with equal-mean independent P,Q. Each offers
a one-call experiment with learned variance u. After observing M, choose P
or Q. The best total gain is

  u E max(M^2,(1-M)^2) = u [1/4+a^2+E|M-1/2|].

The two channels give u(1/4+a+a^2) and u(1/4+3a^2), differing by
u a(1-2a)>0. At a=1/4 and u=1/16 the gains are 9/256 and 7/256. Direct
Bayes conditioning of both channel tables and all continuation policies
was checked exactly. The adaptive maximum needs an absolute-value moment
absent from the shared variance profile. This does not prove that every
adaptive algorithm needs the full posterior law; it proves this scalar
interface insufficient for arbitrary adaptive contexts.

10.5 The experiment order is not Blackwell order.

Let X be uniform on {0,1/3,2/3,1}. Experiment E reveals X>1/2; F reveals
outer pair {0,1} versus inner pair {1/3,2/3}. The prior variance is 5/36.
E has learned variance 1/9 and rho=4/5; F has posterior mean always 1/2 and
rho=0. E is better for every affine squared-loss target. Yet F exactly
reveals X(1-X), while E learns nothing about that nonlinear target.

Even coordinate rho values need not compare adaptive policies: for two fair
binary parameters, reveal X_1 first and reveal X_2 only if X_1=1 (policy A)
or only if X_1=0 (policy B), using a redundant repeat otherwise. Both have
budget two and coordinate rho=(1,1/2). For target X_1 X_2 their gains are
3/16 and 1/16. Random posterior means are no longer independent across
coordinates, despite product posteriors conditional on each complete record.
