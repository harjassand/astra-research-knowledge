
I chose to remove spin-frame calibration from an entire encoded computation. The resulting construction has two linked parts:

A common unknown static spin frame can become part of the encoding. When loading, transport, charge interactions, measurement and return all share that frame, the complete computation can work without reconstructing individual spin-rotation angles.
Its native singlet/triplet measurement needs only one qualified charge resonance at a time. I proved a transfer-and-return bound that retains both double-charge states, allows unequal quantum source operators, and places no bandwidth or detuning bound on the other double-charge sector.

I also derived a microscopic improvement to the error model: in a qualified [110] semiconductor envelope model, choosing the encoding around the actual time-reversed band minima absorbs the linear part of the cubic Dresselhaus correction. The remaining spin-dependent error is cubic in momentum relative to those minima.

These are working theoretical results with executable checks. They establish a broader information/control capability; they do not yet establish a device operating window. The protected tunnel cycle and its qualifications remain preserved as a separate construction.
