
I developed a reusable parity-measurement construction with unequal quantum sources, including physical charge return, a finite tolerance to contact errors, and a charge-meter model that controls the full quantum instrument.

The central result is an organizing law:

When the actual interaction paths, readout and renewal preserve the information inside each measured sector, an uncertain quantum source may change the evidence stream and the time to completion without becoming logical damage inside that sector. Reliable computation then needs a uniform information margin and a real return procedure—not reconstruction or restoration of the source.

This remains a working theorem for a specified zero-mode, charging and control model. It gives a compatible operation cycle and a nonempty parameter region. It does not yet establish a superconducting device window or an advantage over available cavity and optical hardware.

The earlier balanced instrument, retained-source stopping law, three-cell construction, repaired controls and projection failures retain their status. The new proof is preserved in

1. The scientific choice: protect the entire measurement trajectory

I chose a tunnel interferometer because its physical paths can protect the measurement through real charge motion, sensing and return, rather than only at a favorable pulse endpoint.

There is substantial prior machinery here. Plugge and colleagues already supplied Majorana-box interference, charge readout and endpoint Pauli tracking. Karzig and colleagues supplied finite-charge contact models and the closed-loop joint-parity argument. Steiner and von Oppen explicitly developed symmetry-protected readout, including the distinction between a conserved combined parity and an informative measurement signal. Those constructions deserve the same improvements developed here; the parity loop and protected-readout idea are established. Plugge et al., Karzig et al., Steiner–von Oppen

The extension has four connected parts:

Exact preservation inside measured sectors, with operator-valued source amplitudes and actual charge excursions.
A finite information margin from unequal quantum arms.
Physical renewal using measured, resonant charge transfers.
A stopping theorem that controls the joint quantum instrument, including the retained source and arbitrary earlier correlations.

The last distinction matters. A protocol can return an accurate parity label while damaging the state that label was supposed to measure.

7. What this changes, and where the physical burden remains

The strongest consequence is that fine gain matching is no longer the condition for reusable quantum measurement. The necessary structure can instead be:

Physical paths and ports that preserve the measured-sector information.
A source-uniform finite contrast.
Actual charge-return moves with a finite progress floor.
A complete charge instrument for the adaptive controls.
Explicit accounting for the work that continues.

That is broader than a pulse reduction. It remains useful when the source changes state after every observation and retains correlations across repeated operations.

The comparison with established alternatives is also clearer.

Existing Majorana protocols receive the same operator-source, charge-return and stopping improvements. Calibrated scalar implementations retain their acquired information and can optimize their pulses. No advantage is claimed against them by withholding that knowledge.

Cavity dual-rail erasure checks can protect useful data by keeping the valid branch quiet. Making both outcomes bright can add ancilla exposure. Their actual mode-selective interactions and decay trajectories must be compared, rather than replacing them with a total-number operator because an endpoint unitary looks correct. Implemented cavity erasure check

Optical detection and storage offer different complete capabilities. A nondestructive-detection click need not certify survival through the last accepted interaction. If the task permits consuming the incoming photon, passive heralded Raman storage is a stronger existing interface because the herald accompanies storage itself. Both deserve their actual preparation, cooling, retrieval and source assumptions—and the same new stopping guarantees wherever their full instruments satisfy them. Photonic-qubit detection, Heralded Raman memory

The remaining physical burden is specific. Existing-contact errors with zero bare-low compression have a proved finite tolerance. Source-induced dot detuning, unwanted paths, detector differential backaction and continuum channels may not have that structure. Their full incident contribution over gates, sensing, return and holding must be derived from the same device.

The measurement coupling itself has now been supplied at the charge-Hamiltonian level, with its preparation, quantum-instrument error and renewal costs exposed. A fabricated implementation must still establish the zero-mode and orbital domain, the real contact/controller corrections, and a detector control path whose inactive differential coupling fits the bound. Neither a good projected pulse nor a correct classical label can establish those facts.
