# Compressed telegraph path: deterministic receiving lemmas

Working construction, 31 October 2026. Not an accepted full compiler or a novelty claim. The source samplers and complete finite-circuit resource accounting remain separate obligations. These results are new research after the delivered recovery archives.

## 1. Exact representation and random access
Let xi be a stationary symmetric two-state chain with flip rate lambda>0 and set c=sqrt(gamma lambda), W(t)=c integral_0^t xi(s)ds on [0,T]. Conditional on N flips, the N+1 chronological spacings divided by T have Dirichlet(1,...,1) law. The initial sign is independent and uniform.

Partition the spacings into positive and negative sign positions. Their total positive mass Z has Beta(nplus,nminus) law; conditional on Z, the normalized positive and negative vectors are independent Dirichlet vectors. If one group is empty its mass is deterministic. For each group, a balanced binary tree with independent Beta(left_leaf_count,right_leaf_count) splitting variables generates its Dirichlet vector. This follows recursively from the Dirichlet aggregation/conditioning identity. A prefix sum uses at most the tree depth many split values and arithmetic operations.

With initial sign positive, pair endpoints are T(P_j+M_j), where P_j and M_j are the first j positive and negative cumulative masses. Binary search over pair endpoints, then comparison with the intervening positive endpoint, locates any query time. Thus O(log^2(N+2)) node evaluations suffice, including a possible final unpaired positive interval. Reverse the sign labels for a negative initial sign. This is an arithmetic/random-node query bound, not yet a bit-complexity theorem.

## 2. Uniform path accuracy without a spacing gap
Couple exact and approximate splitting trees so every split, including the total positive mass, differs by at most delta. Approximate splits are dyadic numbers in [0,1]; complementary children are constructed exactly and all prefix operations use exact dyadic arithmetic. They define a genuine nonnegative spacing vector with total T. Rounded zero intervals are allowed.

At depth D the total variation distance of leaf probability vectors is at most D delta. Proof: couple the successive binary choices of a random leaf. Conditional on a common node, its next branch disagrees with probability at most delta; union over D levels. Therefore every prefix probability differs by at most D delta. Including the root group total, each positive and negative cumulative mass differs by at most (D+1)delta.

At each corresponding chronological breakpoint, the time coordinate is the sum of the two cumulative masses, and the signed-integral coordinate is their difference. Each coordinate consequently differs by at most E=2T(D+1)delta. Match corresponding intervals by their interpolation fraction. The resulting parametric paths have time and signed-coordinate differences at most E everywhere. The true signed-integral path is 1-Lipschitz in physical time. Every approximate time is represented by an interval (a zero-length interval can be replaced by its endpoint), hence

sup_t |W(t)-Wtilde(t)| <= 2cE = 4cT(D+1)delta.

No lower bound on an interval length, exclusion of switch-time queries, or summation of N independent switching errors is needed. In particular delta polynomially small in c suffices, so its binary precision depends logarithmically on lambda.

## 3. Interaction-frame stability for arbitrary retained controls
Let H(t)=H0+c xi(t) A for bounded Hermitian H0,A, with h>=||H0|| and a>=||A||. Define R_w(t)=exp(-i A w(t)). The exact propagator is U_w(t,s)=R_w(t) V_w(t,s) R_w(s)^dagger, where V is generated by H_I,w(t)=R_w(t)^dagger H0 R_w(t). This identity requires w'=c xi almost everywhere and w(0)=0; piecewise linear w is sufficient.

For any two continuous paths with sup difference d,
||R_w(t)-R_v(t)|| <= a d,
||H_I,w(t)-H_I,v(t)|| <= 2ah d.
Duhamel therefore gives ||U_w(t,s)-U_v(t,s)|| <= 2ad+2ah(t-s)d. The diamond distance of their unitary channels is at most twice this. For p intervention times and p+1 free intervals, telescoping through arbitrary quantum instruments, references and retained classical records yields a total controlled-process output trace-norm error at most

4a[(p+1)+hT]d.

The same bound holds for adaptive instruments at the prescribed times, because completely positive trace-preserving maps on the full record/register space are contractions. It does not grant direct access to the simulator's private random seed. For adaptively selected times, one must separately bound the time-selection circuit and ensure a fixed finite oracle budget; uniform path accuracy alone is not a complete implementation specification.

## 4. Clipping removes the large trajectory-amplitude gate cost
Stationary symmetric telegraph noise has
E exp(uW(t)) = exp(-lambda t)[cosh(t r)+(lambda/r)sinh(t r)],
r=sqrt(lambda^2+c^2u^2).
Since lambda/r<=1 and r-lambda<=gamma u^2/2, this is at most exp(gamma t u^2/2). Thus P(|W(t)|>x)<=2exp[-x^2/(2gamma T)] for t<=T.

Use M=ceil(cT) grid intervals, with the trivial cT=0 case handled separately. Lipschitz constant c makes the change from any time to its preceding grid point at most one. For eta in (0,1), set
B=1+sqrt(2gamma T log[2(M+1)/eta]).
Then P(sup_t|W(t)|>B)<=eta. Clipping w to [-B,B] is 1-Lipschitz as a scalar operation. On the exact-path good event it changes the exact path nowhere and does not enlarge the approximate-path error.

Generic block-encoding simulation of exp(-i A w) now only needs |w|<=B, rather than the worst-case cT. Its cost is polynomial in aB and logarithmic accuracy, without assuming fast-forwardability of A. The finite-grid transformed Hamiltonian is bounded by h and Lipschitz with constant at most 2ahc. Standard truncated-Dyson algorithms use only logarithmic dependence on this derivative through time-grid registers. Low and Wiebe, https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00675, supply this ordinary simulation machinery; it is not a new contribution here.

## 5. Finite random functions: correct order of reasoning
For a bounded quantum circuit making q primitive queries to a finite random-function oracle, the averaged output density matrix is a polynomial whose terms each involve at most 2q oracle values. A 2q-wise independent family therefore reproduces the iid function's averaged output exactly. This is a termwise statement, not a claim that a superposition ever touches only 2q addresses. Count every primitive node/random-word query, including uncomputation; q path queries are not q node queries.

First compare the finite seeded algorithm to the iid finite oracle by this exact query argument. Only then use a conceptual coupling of all iid nodes to the exact continuous tree, with per-node failure budget eta/O(Nmax). This coupling does not require actually generating all nodes. It does require each node sampler to have the declared finite worst-case bit cost and precision. Applying a global iid good-event probability directly to the k-wise dependent full tree would be invalid.

Poisson truncation/sampling, reversible Beta/Gamma construction, finite-field seed generation and evaluation, all finite-precision costs, and a closed noncircular overall query bound remain to be supplied and reviewed. No full polylogarithmic-rate simulation theorem is asserted by these lemmas alone.

## Normalized clipping completion for the resource theorem
The earlier unit-grid bound is valid, but to obtain a cost bound only through a*sqrt(gamma*T), use grid oscillation s0=sqrt(gamma*T). For gamma*T>0 choose J=ceil(sqrt(lambda*T))+1 grid points including endpoints and B=s0[1+sqrt(2log(2J/eta))]. Mesh T/(J-1) has c times mesh <=s0, and the same stationary tail/union argument bounds failure by eta. This removes an otherwise uncharged standalone a cost. Gamma*T=0 is deterministic. The digital rounded oracle is not itself differentiable; Lipschitz analysis concerns the underlying continuous clipped path and digital errors are charged separately.
