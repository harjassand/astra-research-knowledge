# Two-sector excursions for an explicit finite Markov reservoir

31 October 2026. Independent working construction; UNREVIEWED. Conditional on the exact pure stationary completion and activity theorem in `PURE_STATIONARY_COMPLETION_AND_ACTIVITY_BOUND.md`. This is not yet a released general-source compiler. No implementation or novelty claim.

## 1. Inputs and essential normalization qualification

Q is an explicitly supplied irreducible d-state generator with stationary vector pi, psi_x=sqrt(pi_x). The pure completion supplies reservoir jumps sqrt(lambda)L_e^0 and free no-jump matrix D0=-iH_env^0-C0/2, where C0=sum_e (L_e^0)^*L_e^0. Let P=|psi><psi|. Both D0 and D0^* annihilate psi, and C0>=c(I-P) for a supplied certified c>0. Hence the restriction D_Q to P-perp satisfies D_Q+D_Q^*<=-cI. All dependence on d, the explicit matrices, acquisition of pi/c, inverse c and inverse pi_min is charged.

The bounded Hermitian system coupling K is centered: PKP=0. Its controlled precision-capable block encoding has normalization b>0. Projecting that encoding yields each of K_PQ/b, K_QP/b and K_QQ/b as a contraction, with those actual encodings paid. If only component encodings are supplied, b must bound their accessible LCU normalization, not merely the unknown operator norm ||K||. A deterministic mean sqrt(lambda)PKP belongs to H0 and its physical simulation cost; centering cannot erase this cost. If K=0 the ground-source system simply evolves under H0.

The system Hamiltonian H0 commutes with the reservoir projections and is simulated by its supplied ordinary controlled interface. No ordered-noise-frame oracle is assumed.

## 2. Exact excursion expansion

Assume

    lambda >= 64 b^2/c^2.

Put

    g=sqrt(lambda)b, D=lambda*c/2, kappa=D-g.

Then D>=4g and kappa>=3D/4>=3g. Between coupling insertions the fast reservoir propagator can be written exactly as

    exp(lambda D_Q t)=exp(-Dt) F(t),
    F(t)=exp[lambda(D_Q+cI/2)t], ||F(t)||<=1.

The free system factor is exp(-iH0t). On the ground sector the reservoir factor is identity. Every coupling insertion contributes (-i)g times the appropriate projected K/b. Thus a Dyson word consists of ground-to-fast, fast-to-ground, and fast-to-fast insertions, with actual noncommuting system/reservoir contractions between them. No ground-to-ground insertion occurs.

Resum only the SCALAR weights of the fast-to-fast insertions. The endpoint scalar majorant is

    E(t)=exp(Mt), M=[[0,g],[g,-kappa]].

Let

    omega=sqrt(kappa^2/4+g^2),
    s=omega-kappa/2=g^2/(omega+kappa/2), d_down=omega+kappa/2=kappa+s.

Then s<=g^2/kappa<=8b^2/(3c), while d_down>=kappa>=3g. The positive Perron transform identifies each endpoint-normalized scalar path with a two-state CTMC bridge having upward rate s and downward rate d_down. Conditional on that bridge, place independent rate-g Poisson self-insertions along each fast interval. This is exact: their Poisson density includes exp(-g*fast_time), canceled by replacing exp(-D*fast_time) with exp(-kappa*fast_time). Their contractions K_QQ/b and all order information are retained. The scalar resummation does not replace K_QQ by a number.

The Doob transition formulas and full first-event CDF are the same two-state formulas used in the reviewed telegraph construction, with rates s,d_down. Endpoint conditioning is sampled by CDF inversion, not rare-endpoint rejection.

## 3. A rate-independent virtual count MGF

Let V count both off-diagonal transitions and self-insertions in the UNCONDITIONED Doob process on an interval of length t, starting in either sector. Potential upward transitions can be generated by a rate-s Poisson process on the entire time axis, accepting only when in the ground sector. Its count N is Poisson(st). The number of fast visits is at most N+1, and the number of off-diagonal transitions is at most2N+1.

For each potential visit independently draw an exponential dwell of rate d_down and its independent rate-g Poisson self-insertions. A final censored dwell can only remove insertions. Its full insertion count Z is geometric and

    E 2^Z = d_down/(d_down-g) <= 3/2 <=2.

These visit marks can be drawn independently of the potential upward Poisson process. The accepted history depends on them, but its total count is bounded pointwise by using all first N+1 marks. Therefore

    E 2^V <= 2 E[4^N 2^(N+1)]
           =4 exp(7st).

For an endpoint event of probability p_ij(t)>0,

    Pr(V>K | i,j) <= 4 exp(7st) 2^-K / p_ij(t).

Consequently

    K=ceil(log(4 exp(7st)/(eta*p_ij(t)))/log2)

is a sufficient virtual-tail cap. Worst-case endpoint bounds replace p_ij by its explicit minimum over allowed durations. Only log(1/p_ij) enters the cap.

Implementation detail: after sampling a bridge fast interval of length ell, sample its Poisson(g*ell) count in binary with the previously earned capped high-precision Poisson sampler. If this count exceeds the remaining global K budget, select zero good block immediately; never generate a long path before testing its count. Otherwise generate that many uniform times in the interval and sort them. The count sampler's dependence is polynomial in log(2+g*ell), log(1/error), not g*ell. Thus an exceptionally long bridge fast interval does not hide linear work.

## 4. Complete first-event envelopes

Let ell_e=||L_e^0||. Each edge jump is rank one, so sum_e ell_e^2=tr C0. The jumps annihilate the ground sector. Scalar entries satisfy

    E10(t) <= (g/kappa) exp(st),
    E11(t) <= (g^2/kappa^2) exp(st)+exp(-kappa t).

The high-rate assumption implies

    sqrt(lambda)[E10(t)+E11(t)]
       <= (4b/c)exp(st)+sqrt(lambda)exp(-kappa t).

Indeed sqrt(lambda)g/kappa<=8b/(3c) and sqrt(lambda)g^2/kappa^2<=8b/(9c); their sum is below4b/c. For no event use the simple four-sector-pair LCU normalization alpha0=4exp(s*tau). The projected contraction words already have the physical input/output sector supports; the auxiliary sector labels only organize the scalar LCU and do not replace physical fast-sector vectors.

For event e, use a uniform four-pair LCU (zero on impossible output pairs), multiply the final fast-sector word by L_e^0/ell_e, and choose

    alpha_e(t)^2 =1024 ell_e^2 exp(2s*tau)
                    [lambda exp(-2kappa t)+b^2/c^2].

This absorbs both the matrix-pair normalization and the displayed scalar bound. The resulting good block is the actual sqrt(lambda)L_e^0 K0(t)/alpha_e(t). Jumps need not reset the reservoir to its ground sector; their output is retained exactly.

Take tau<=c/(16b^2). Then s*tau<=1/6. The complete first-event normalization obeys

    alpha0^2+sum_e integral_0^tau alpha_e(t)^2 dt
      <=16 exp(2s*tau)
        +1024 exp(2s*tau) tr(C0)
           [lambda/(2kappa)+b^2*tau/c^2]
      <=32+2048 tr(C0)[4/(3c)+1/(16c)]
      <4096[1+tr(C0)/c].

Hence an enclosing power-of-two normalizer A>=64sqrt(1+tr(C0)/c), A<128sqrt(1+tr(C0)/c), is sufficient. The outer time density is a positive mixture of a truncated exponential with rate2kappa and a uniform density. All edge probabilities, source factors and normalizers are explicit finite-source data. The normalization is independent of lambda, but can be expensive in source conditioning.

Only the COMPLETE first-event isometry is amplified. K0 alone is a contraction, not an isometry. The previous attenuated Chebyshev/OAA argument gives O(A) calls, O(A^2 beta) good-block error and O(A sqrt(beta)) leakage. This is a conditional receiving reuse of that algebra, not a fresh proof of the whole numerical compiler.

## 5. Fast propagators are finite matrices, not free oracles

The exact matrices F(t) act only on a d-1 dimensional explicit reservoir. A constructive precision route is:

1. Set Z=D_Q+cI/2, which is dissipative. To approximate exp(hZ), h=lambda*t, choose n=2^j with h||Z||/n<=1. j=O(log(2+h||Z||)).
2. Compute the small matrix exponential by a truncated Taylor expansion at absolute error O(delta/n), with additional rounding guard bits. Its degree is O(log(n/delta)) at this bounded argument.
3. Square j times. Since the exact semigroup factors are contractions, perturbations grow at most linearly in the represented power, up to exp(O(delta)), rather than exp(h||Z||). Per-stage rounding O(delta/(n(j+1))) is sufficient. The arithmetic precision is polynomial in log n, log(1/delta), d and the input lengths.
4. If the approximant has norm at most1+delta, scale it down by1+delta. This produces an actual contraction within O(delta) of F(t).
5. A finite contraction can be implemented by its explicit Halmos unitary dilation using sqrt(I-B^*B) and sqrt(I-BB^*). Matrix square roots can be regularized by delta^2 I; Newton iterations starting at a scalar upper bound then require polynomial log(1/delta) iterations and guarded dense matrix inversions. Their eigenvalue condition numbers and inverse tolerances have only polynomial bit length. Approximate dilation entries can be compiled using finite-dimensional two-level rotations with all precision and reservoir gate costs charged. This final synthesis requires a written numerical stability ledger before a whole compiler receipt; it is not an assumed source oracle.

The factor exp(+Dt) must NEVER be separately multiplied by an underflowed exp(lambda D_Qt). Compute the shifted dissipative matrix directly. No reservoir matrix dimension scales with the system Hilbert-space dimension. Dense dependence on explicit d is permitted and paid; this is not a promise of efficiency for a succinct exponentially large Markov source.

The cost of each fast propagator and its inverse block encoding is repeated at every selected virtual interval. H0 variable-duration simulation is likewise paid for every slot. The pathwise sum of durations does not by itself earn an optimized coherent simulation cost.

## 6. What a finite-bit receiving proof must still bind

The following ingredients have concrete routes but are NOT declared reviewed here:

* Add the self-insertion rate g to the global event-intensity upper bound used for near-boundary and near-tie cuts. Condition only by the original bridge endpoint probability. Every recursive bridge duration denominator is then bounded as in the two-state source proof.
* A change delta ell in a fast interval changes its Poisson insertion-count law by TV at most g|delta ell| via Poisson coupling. Conditional uniform insertion positions and support changes must be coupled on the same private uniforms; the capped count and near-tie margin give additive polynomial losses, not an exponential recursion sensitivity.
* Word timing errors now involve ||H0||+lambda||D_Q+cI/2||, not only ||H0||, because the normalized fast reservoir contractions change with their durations. This increases logarithmic guard precision, but must be explicitly allocated.
* For event amplitudes divide out the edge scale ell_e analytically. The remaining normalized envelopes have a b/c floor. Exactly zero edges are removed; b=0 ground-source evolution is handled directly. Derivatives are polynomial in the supplied norms, lambda, inverse b and inverse c, with no exp(lambda*tau) term.
* The outer time label remains private. Comparison must use common-uniform L2 Kraus embeddings and the exact count-capped future channel's duration Lipschitz bound. Continuous and rounded timestamps are not close in TV if exposed.
* Retain the actual reservoir after jumps, no-event outcomes and consumer instruments. System-only adaptive instruments at prescribed times are external or have their full implementation costs added.

The activity theorem supplies the actual jump cap with its explicit dependence on c, pi_min and the ground-to-fast coupling. The number of windows is at most ceil(16 b^2 T/c)+p+1. The full physical generator norm, including H_env, C0 and sqrt(lambda)K, prices tiny-window and remaining-time errors. Its logarithm grows with encoded lambda and source norms. All cap comparisons must be against the exact count-augmented process, not an unproved approximate-process jump tail.

For lambda<64b^2/c^2, the stiffness parameter is bounded in terms of paid source/coupling parameters. Ordinary high-precision Lindblad simulation can be a separate low-rate branch; its precise access model, normalization and theorem must be cited/received rather than silently treating small lambda as zero cost.

## 7. Scope and consequential boundary

The two-sector decomposition, conditional path representation, virtual-count MGF and integrable first-event envelope above are actual finite-dimensional derivations. They remove the independent-mode tensor assumption at the level of a source-conditioned mathematical compiler candidate. They do not yet constitute a whole reviewed finite-bit controlled-process theorem.

The important costs are explicit-source dimension, acquisition/preparation of pi, the dissipative gap c, tr(C0), pi_min, accessible coupling normalization b, operator encodings and instruments. Arbitrary nonreversibility and correlations of a KNOWN explicit finite source are allowed by the conditional representation. Unknown, succinct, poorly conditioned or nonstationarily initialized source families are not fast-forwarded for free. Strong ordinary comparators remain exact finite sign/source-block matrix evolution for small systems and effective Lindblad elimination with a separately certified finite-rate controlled-process bias.
