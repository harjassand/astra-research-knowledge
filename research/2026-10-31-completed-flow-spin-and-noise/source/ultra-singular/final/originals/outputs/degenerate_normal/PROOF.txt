31 October 2026. Solo construction, full normal-infimum theorem and exact field.

1. WHAT THE ZERO-WEIGHT BOUNDARY CHANGES

The earlier attained normal coefficient at scale 1/a assumes the fixed
actual Hessian field S is bounded away from zero. Dropping that condition
is not a harmless use of a pseudoinverse. There are actual compatible
fields for which the coefficient infimum at scale 1/a is MINUS INFINITY.
The full normal relaxation has a different, sharp scale a^(-2/3).

This note proves a weak-L4 Gamma limit and convergence of the ENTIRE
normal-fiber infimum, including arbitrary a-dependent normal correctors.
Its limiting nonlinear cost is attained. The Hessian Hodge fiber remains
fixed: arbitrary simultaneous variation of that fiber is not asserted.

An explicit piecewise-polynomial example uses the original N901 coupling
at r=3/4 and its already admitted globally QC quartic completion. Its
actual normal-curl support is disjoint from the base Hessian support,
yet the Poisson/Hodge extension has a nonzero interaction with the base.
The receipt verifies a strict finite-a energy lowering and a POSITIVE
total Jensen gap. This is a structural relaxation theorem and obstruction
to a tempting approximation, not a Morrey counterexample.

2. SETUP AND EXACT RESCALED EXPANSION

Let F0 be any real homogeneous quartic on R^(2x2), H its restriction to
Sym(2), and delta>0. For A write z(A)=A12-A21 and define

 F_a(A)=F0(A)+2delta a z(A)^2 |sym A|^2+delta a^2 z(A)^4.  (1)

For the N901 application this is exactly the coupling-preserving family
proved globally ROC and QC in ../quartic_coupling/PROOF.txt, with all
coefficients of the original off-Sym coupling retained. The limit below
is algebraic/variational and does not assume a QC implication for F0.

Fix a bounded actual Hessian field S=M+D^2 psi, M symmetric, on a
normalized flat torus. Put w=|S|^2 and Z=L4_0, the real mean-zero scalar
L4 space. For z in Z set

 E_z=-J Hess Delta^(-1)z, Y_z=sym E_z, J=[[0,-1],[1,0]].   (2)

This is an ACTUAL mean-zero vector gradient with curl z, and its
Hessian Hodge component is zero. It parameterizes the entire normal
fiber at this fixed Hessian component. The retained elementary Hodge
bounds give ||E_z||_2=||z||_2, ||Y_z||_2<=||z||_2 and
||E_z||_4<=32||z||_4.

Let K=Delta^(-1) div div[J DF0(S)], with its zero mode removed.
K belongs to L^(4/3), and the exact actual adjoint identity is

                       <DF0(S):E_z>=<K z>.               (3)

Define the rescaled functionals on the WHOLE space Z by

 G_a(z)=a^(2/3)[<F_a(S+a^(-2/3)E_z)>-<H(S)>].           (4)

Their exact leading form is

 G_a(z)=<Kz>+2delta a^(1/3)<w z^2>+delta<z^4>+R_a(z).    (5)

The remainder is uniformly small on every bounded L4 set. More
explicitly let L=||S||_infinity, K4=32 and choose C such that
||F0''(A)||<=C|A|^2. With x=||z||_4,

 |R_a(z)| <=C L^2 a^(-2/3)x^2
       +4delta L a^(-1/3)x^3
       +(C K4^4 a^(-2)+2delta K4^2 a^(-1))x^4.           (6)

For the source remainder, Taylor's formula and
|S+t epsilon E|^2<=2L^2+2epsilon^2|E|^2 give the first and the
C K4^4 terms. The normal penalty contributes exactly

 4delta a^(-1/3)<z^2 S:Y_z>
                         +2delta a^(-1)<z^2|Y_z|^2>,

which gives the remaining terms by Holder. These estimates hold for
every z in L4, including unbounded a-dependent sequences; no bounded
corrector ansatz is substituted for the infimum.

3. THE FULL NORMAL-INFIMUM LIMIT AND ITS ATTAINER

Let E={x:S(x)=0}, up to null sets, and define

 G_infinity(z)=<Kz>+delta<z^4> if z in Z and z=0 off E,
              +infinity otherwise.                       (7)

Theorem. G_a weakly Gamma-converges in L4_0 to (7). The family is
equi-coercive for large a. Consequently

 lim_(a->infinity) a^(2/3)
  [inf_(t in L4_0)<F_a(S+E_t)>-<H(S)>]
                    =min_(z in L4_0) G_infinity(z).        (8)

This is an actual infimum over ALL normal fields t, because t=a^(-2/3)z
is a bijective rescaling for each a. It is stronger than an infimum of
fixed-corrector limits. The right side is attained by an actual normal
Hodge corrector via (2).

Proof. In the lower bound the last normal-penalty remainder is
nonnegative and may be dropped. By (6), for all sufficiently large a,
the source quartic error consumes an arbitrarily small fraction of
delta x^4. Young's inequality absorbs the quadratic, cubic and linear
negative terms, each into a further arbitrarily small fraction of
delta x^4 plus a constant independent of a. Thus

                         G_a(z)>=(delta/2)x^4-C0         (9)

for a sufficiently large threshold, with finite C0 depending on the
fixed source, S and delta. This proves equi-coercivity.

If z_a converges weakly in L4 and has bounded cost, (5)-(6) imply
<w z_a^2>=O(a^(-1/3)). Weak L2 lower semicontinuity of multiplication
by sqrt(w) forces w z^2=0 in the limit. The linear pairing in (3) is
weakly continuous; the L4 norm is weakly lower semicontinuous; (6)
tends to zero on this bounded sequence. This proves the Gamma liminf.
For any z supported on E, the constant sequence z_a=z has zero
weighted term and R_a(z)->0. It is a recovery sequence. This proves
the Gamma limit and (8) by weak compactness.

If |E|=0, the only finite-cost limit is z=0, and the limiting minimum
is zero. If |E|>0, let ell be the unique minimizer of

                         <1_E |K-ell|^(4/3)>.

The unique minimizer of (7) is

 z*=-1_E sign(K-ell) [|K-ell|/(4delta)]^(1/3),             (10)

where ell makes <z*>=0. The exact minimum is

 -3/[4^(4/3)delta^(1/3)] *min_ell <1_E |K-ell|^(4/3)>.     (11)

The minimizer is in L4 since K is in L^(4/3). Strict convexity gives
uniqueness. The cost is strictly negative precisely when K is not
constant on E. These are actual-space statements, not free-law costs.

Every rescaled sequence attaining the normal infimum up to o(1) in
(4) converges STRONGLY in L4 to z*. Indeed weak compactness and
uniqueness give the weak limit; (5), nonnegativity of the weighted
term and convergence of minimum values force convergence of the L4
norms. Uniform convexity of L4 gives strong convergence. The weighted
term itself tends to zero along such almost minimizers.

3A. THE REGULAR CASE ALSO ADMITS THE FULL NORMAL-INFIMUM LIMIT

When w>=rho>0, the same machinery upgrades the earlier attained
fixed-corrector coefficient to the complete infimum over a-dependent
normal fields. On mean-zero L2, extend the following functional by
+infinity outside L4:

 L_a(z)=a[<F_a(S+E_z/a)>-<H(S)>].                         (12a)

Then L_a weakly Gamma-converges in L2 to

                  L_infinity(z)=<Kz>+2delta<w z^2>.       (12b)

It is equi-coercive in L2 for large a. Thus the FULL normal infimum
has limit

 lim a[inf_(t in L4_0)<F_a(S+E_t)>-<H(S)>]
 =-< (K-ell)^2/w >/(8delta),
                    ell=<K/w>/<1/w>.                    (12c)

The limiting minimizer is z*=-(K-ell)/(4delta w). It is unique in L2;
for a smooth base field it is the earlier smooth attained corrector.
Every almost minimizing rescaled sequence converges strongly in L2.
This conclusion includes normal fields whose rescaled L4 norm grows
with a, rather than assuming the fixed-corrector expansion is uniform.

Here is the required compactness estimate. Exact expansion at this
scale gives

 L_a(z)=<Kz>+2delta<wz^2>+(delta/a)<z^4>
  +R_F+ (4delta/a)<z^2 S:Y_z>
                             +(2delta/a^2)<z^2|Y_z|^2>,

 |R_F|<= C L^2 a^(-1)||z||_2^2
                           +C K4^4 a^(-3)||z||_4^4.

The last displayed normal term is nonnegative. Also

 (4delta L/a)||z||_4^2||z||_2
 <=(delta/(4a))||z||_4^4+(16delta L^2/a)||z||_2^2.

For sufficiently large a, the source quartic error costs at most a
further delta/(4a) times the fourth moment, and the remaining quadratic
coefficient is at least delta rho. Holder and Young for <Kz> give

 L_a(z)>=(delta rho/2)||z||_2^2
                           +(delta/(2a))||z||_4^4-C1.   (12d)

K is in L2 because S is bounded and the actual Hodge adjoint is L2
bounded. Bounded-energy sequences consequently have bounded L2 norm
and ||z||_4^4/a bounded. On these sequences R_F->0, the cubic cross
term is O(a^(-1/2)), and the last normal term is O(a^(-1)). Retaining
the nonnegative delta<a^(-1)z^4> proves the weak liminf in (12b).
Fixed L4 functions give recovery; an ordinary diagonal approximation
gives recovery for every L2 function. This proves (12b)-(12c).
Strict quadratic convexity and convergence of minimum values give
strong L2 convergence of almost minimizers and <z^4>/a->0 on them.

Thus both the regular 1/a limit and the zero-set a^(-2/3) limit now
control the ENTIRE normal minimization at the fixed Hessian fiber.
Unrestricted simultaneous variation of the Hessian fiber remains a
separate problem in both statements.

3B. THE 1/a COEFFICIENT FOR EVERY BOUNDED BASE: AN EXTENDED-REAL LIMIT

There is also a useful statement requiring NO positive lower bound on w.
For every fixed bounded S in Section 2, put

 Q(z)=<Kz>+2delta<wz^2>, z in L4_0,
 q*=inf_(z in L4_0) Q(z), which belongs to [-infinity,0].

Then the FULL normal infimum always satisfies

 lim_(a->infinity) a[inf_(t in L4_0)<F_a(S+E_t)>-<H(S)>]
                              =q*.                       (12e)

The limit is understood in the extended real sense. In particular a
finite quadratic cost must not be asserted at a degenerate weight
without checking q*. This statement alone supplies no compactness or
attainment of Q when the weight degenerates.

Proof. Let x=||z||_4. The exact expansion in Section 3A, its source
remainder, and ||Y_z||_2<=||z||_2<=x give

 L_a(z)>=Q(z)+(delta/a)x^4
          -(C L^2/a)x^2-(4delta L/a)x^3
                                  -(C K4^4/a^3)x^4.

For a sufficiently large threshold this is bounded below by

 Q(z)+(1/a)[(delta/2)x^4-4delta L x^3-C L^2 x^2]
                                      >=Q(z)-C2/a,       (12f)

where C2 is finite, depends only on the fixed source, S and delta, and
is independent of z and a. If q* is finite, (12f) proves the lower
bound for (12e). Every fixed z gives L_a(z)->Q(z), proving the upper
bound. If q*=-infinity, the same fixed-z upper bounds force the full
infimum to tend to -infinity. This proves (12e) in both cases.

The finiteness gate itself has an exact weighted dual description:

 q*=-(1/(8delta)) min_(ell in R) <(K-ell)^2/w>.           (12g)

The integral uses b^2/0=0 for b=0 and +infinity otherwise. If no ell
gives a finite integral, q*=-infinity. If the minimum is finite it is
attained in ell, but attainment of the ACTUAL corrector requires a
separate check: z=-(K-ell)/(4delta w) on {w>0} must extend across {w=0}
to a mean-zero L4 function. Formula (12g) does not invent such a field.

To prove (12g), equip L4_0 with the seminorm ||sqrt(w)z||_2. A finite
lower bound for Q, applied to every real multiple of each z, says
exactly that z-><Kz> is a bounded linear functional for this seminorm.
In particular it vanishes on its null space. Its Riesz representative
h on the L2 closure of {sqrt(w)z:z in L4_0} satisfies

                       K-sqrt(w)h=ell a.e.,

because the annihilator of mean-zero L4 consists of constants. The
representative vanishes on {w=0}, so its squared norm is the integral
in (12g). Every other feasible ell supplies another representative;
the representative in the closure has the least norm. Completing the
square on that closure gives q*=-||h||_2^2/(8delta). Conversely any
finite integral supplies a bounded representative and a finite lower
bound. If none exists, the scaling test forces q*=-infinity. Equality
in the square gives exactly the stated actual-attainment condition.

The regular weighted formula in (12c) is a special case. The explicit
zero-set example below has no feasible ell in (12g), so its old-scale
coefficient is -infinity; (8) then resolves the sharper fractional rate.
For a zero set of measure zero, (8) alone has value zero. In that case
the finiteness and attainment tests (12g) still matter, and a universal
finite 1/a coefficient is not being inferred from the absence of a
positive-measure zero set.

4. WHY THE OLD QUADRATIC COST CAN BE UNBOUNDED

At the old scale S+E_z/a, the fixed-corrector coefficient is

                         <Kz>+2delta<wz^2>.             (12)

If there is a mean-zero z supported in E with <Kz><0, multiplying
that same ACTUAL corrector by arbitrary positive constants sends (12)
to minus infinity. Treating 1/w as a zero-valued pseudoinverse on E
would simply delete this real interaction and give a false finite cost.

At the new scale a^(-2/3), the transverse quartic term survives and
prevents that escape. Equations (8)-(11) resolve the resulting normal
minimization and identify the sharp rate whenever the cost is negative.
For an everywhere positive weight the a^(-2/3) limit is zero, consistent
with the earlier, smaller 1/a effect. No universal 1/a rate is valid
across zero-weight fibers.

5. AN EXACT ACTUAL FIELD WITH THE DEGENERATE INTERACTION

Use the periodic cell [-3,3]^2, normalized by its area 36. Put

 f(x)=(1-x^2)^4 on [-1,1], zero outside,
 psi(x,y)=f(x)f(y), S=D^2 psi.

This gives a C3 scalar potential and a bounded actual Hessian supported
on [-1,1]^2, with mean zero. Let chi=1 on [-3/2,3/2], zero outside
[-5/2,5/2]. On either transition interval write s=|x|-3/2 and set

                 chi(x)=1-10s^3+15s^4-6s^5.

Define eta=x y chi(x)chi(y), z0=Delta eta and E0=-J D^2 eta.
The potential eta is C2 and periodic after zero extension to the cell.
Since it equals the harmonic polynomial xy on a neighborhood of the
base support, z0 vanishes there. Consequently

                 w z0^2=0 everywhere,
                 <z0>=0, E0=E_(z0).                     (13)

The normal Hodge corrector has a NONZERO tail on the stressed region:
there E0=diag(1,-1). The nonlocal pairing (3) is therefore obtained
without evaluating a Green function:

                 L0=<DF0(S):E0> != 0.                   (14)

For the original N901 F0 at r=3/4, the exact rational receipt gives
L0 approximately -7.155639853 and base energy approximately 48.08589544.
It checks all interface derivatives, mean-zero identities, actual
potentials and polynomial integrals. In particular the nonzero (14)
proves K is nonconstant on E, since z0 is supported there and mean zero.

Let B=<z0^2|sym E0|^2>, C4=<z0^4>. Both are received exactly. The
source expansion is

 <F0(S+epsilon E0)>-<H(S)>
                =epsilon L0+epsilon^2 A2+epsilon^4 A4,  (15)

with A2>=0 and A4>0. The cubic coefficient is exactly zero: E0 is
constant on the source support and <S>=0. The new normal term has
no base-weight or cubic cross term because of (13).

The receipt chooses a rational magnitude tau and, if needed, the sign
of eta so that tau L0+delta tau^4 C4<0. Its EXACT finite-a lowering at
epsilon=a^(-2/3) is

 a^(-2/3)[tau L0+delta tau^4 C4]
    +a^(-4/3)tau^2 A2+2delta a^(-5/3)tau^4 B
                                      +a^(-8/3)tau^4 A4. (16)

At the already admitted a=2^186, epsilon=2^-124 is rational. Direct
exact evaluation of (16) is negative; adding it to the base energy
remains strictly positive. The density is globally QC by the coupled
theorem, so this is a coupling control and not a QC violation.

There is a gap between the base support and the cutoff transition.
Mollifying both potentials within that gap preserves (13), and by
continuity preserves a nonzero pairing (14). Thus genuinely smooth
examples exist as well. The literal executable fixture itself already
uses valid actual W^(1,infinity) gradients.

6. WHAT IS AND IS NOT RESOLVED

The first normal coefficient at a zero-weight fiber need not be finite.
The complete normal relaxation has a sharp a^(-2/3) regime, with an
attained nonlinear limit cost and control of arbitrary a-dependent
normal correctors. These conclusions follow from the variational
proof, not from the one fixture.

The underlying Hessian field is fixed throughout (8). This does not
settle arbitrary joint Hessian/normal relaxation or uncorrected general-U
quasiconvexity. It supplies a changed construction at the degenerate
boundary, rather than extrapolating the regular fixed-coefficient cost.
The older attained coefficient, N901 theorem, quartic completion,
ordinary heat obstruction, all-r ROC proof and capacity jump retain
their separate scopes.

Run `python3 outputs/degenerate_normal/receive.py`. Only standard-library
rational algebra is used. No numerical quadrature, field grid or
solver supplies a sign. Historical priority of this precise relaxation
formulation has not been established; no physical advantage is claimed.
