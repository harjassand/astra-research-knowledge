THE SINGULAR MEAN BOUNDARY OF ORIGINAL J_r
31 October 2026. Solo structural theorem for actual periodic gradients.

1. THE ALL-FIELD CLASSIFICATION

Fix ANY r>0 and retain the original homogeneous quartic J_r. For every
real mean-zero actual periodic G=Dphi in L4 set

 H_G=<J_r''(G)> in Sym_4, v_G=<DJ_r(G)>, f_G=<J_r(G)>.

Theorem. If G is nonzero, H_G has rank either THREE or FOUR. It is
singular if and only if

                 G(x)=u tensor e_j h(x_j), j=1 or 2,     (1)

where u is a fixed nonzero real output vector and h is a nonzero
mean-zero one-variable L4 profile. The coordinate line must support
periodic frequencies on the given torus; on the square torus (1) is
literal. There is no bandwidth, amplitude or concentration restriction.

For every such singular field, the minimum over ALL affine means is
finite and attained. If R=u tensor e_j and m_k=<h^k>, then

 inf_M [<J_r(M+G)>-J_r(M)]
       =J_r(R)[m4-(2/3)m3^2/m2] >=f_G/3>0.               (2)

Every minimizing mean is

             M=-(m3/(3m2))R+K, K in ker H_G.             (3)

In particular an arbitrarily large component of the mean along this
kernel cannot reveal a hidden negative energy.

Consequences. Covariance rank at least TWO already makes H_G positive
definite. Full four-dimensional covariance is unnecessary for ordinary
mean elimination. For EVERY actual field, including the singular ones,

 inf_M Delta_Jr(M,G)=f_G-v_G^T H_G^+ v_G/2               (4)

is attained; v_G belongs to range H_G. Any strict actual QC failure of
the original J_r must have H_G positive definite. These statements do
not assert a uniform modulus or continuity when fields approach the
singular set; the fourth-order angular contact is not erased.

2. EXACT ACOUSTIC KERNELS AND OPPOSITE DETERMINANT SIGNS

Put t_r=(2r+1)/3 and U_u=[[u1,-u2],[u2,u1]]. The independent all-r ROC
proof in ../quartic_coupling/original_roc.py gives positive definite
J_r''(u tensor xi) whenever xi is off the two coordinate axes.
On the axes its rank is exactly three, with kernels

 ker J_r''(u tensor e1)=span{U_u K1}, K1=[[0,t_r],[1,0]],
 ker J_r''(u tensor e2)=span{U_u K2}, K2=I.               (5)

The receiving code rebuilds the actual polynomial, all kernel equations
in r,u1,u2, and the positive complementary blocks. The transported null
matrices satisfy

              det(U_u K1)=-t_r |u|^2<0,
              det(U_u K2)=|u|^2>0.                      (6)

Thus a nonzero background cannot be a null matrix for rays from both
spatial axes. The matrices in either kernel are invertible.

3. THE ACTUAL FOURIER ARGUMENT

Quartic polarization gives J_r''(A)[R,R]=J_r''(R)[A,A]. For an actual
periodic gradient, each real and imaginary Fourier coefficient has
the real rank-one form u tensor k. Parseval expresses H_G as a
convergent positive sum of these acoustic Hessians. This is why H_G
is PSD, even though the pointwise matrix J_r''(G(x)) need not be PSD.

If a nonzero Z lies in ker H_G, every nonzero Fourier coefficient
must satisfy J_r''(u tensor k) Z=0. All non-axis coefficients vanish
by positive definiteness. Formula (6) forbids simultaneous nonzero
coefficients from both axes. On the remaining single axis, (5) says
U_u K_j is proportional to the same Z for every real and imaginary
coefficient. The map u->U_u K_j is injective and linear, so all these
output vectors are collinear. This is exactly (1). The converse follows
immediately from (5). Since one nonzero acoustic matrix already has
rank at least three, ranks one and two cannot occur for H_G.

The argument first applies to Fourier polynomials and then to all L4
gradients by L2 convergence and Parseval. No proposed moment law is
being treated as an actual field.

4. THE SINGULAR MINIMUM, WITH NO INVERSE ASSUMPTION

For a homogeneous quartic, zero mean of G gives the EXACT identity

 Delta_Jr(M,G)=f_G+v_G:M+M^T H_G M/2.                    (7)

For (1), H_G=m2 J_r''(R), v_G=m3 DJ_r(R), f_G=m4 J_r(R).
Euler's identity is

                       J_r''(R)R=3 DJ_r(R).

This proves v_G belongs to the range of H_G and directly supplies
the minimizing mean (3). Another Euler identity,
R^T J_r''(R)R=12J_r(R), gives (2). Cauchy--Schwarz gives
m3^2<=m2 m4. Both acoustic-axis values J_r(R) are strictly positive:
for unit e1 output they are alpha t_r^2 and alpha, respectively;
output SO(2) invariance and homogeneity cover every u.

The factor 1/3 is sharp as a profile-independent lower bound: take
h=1/p on a fraction p of its period and h=-1/(1-p) elsewhere. Its
primitive is a real periodic Lipschitz potential, and

 m3^2/(m2 m4)=(1-2p)^2/(1-3p+3p^2)->1 as p->0.

The code also receives literal two-level profiles at r=1/10,3/4,2,
both axes and arbitrary output rotation. It evaluates the original
energy at several means along the UNBOUNDED kernel line and obtains
exactly the same positive minimum. These validate the singular formula;
the all-profile result is the analytic argument above.

5. A COMPLETE REPRESENTATION OF THE RANK-THREE FRONTIER

The following elementary compatibility reductions further organize
the remaining search. They are proved here at their actual scope;
no priority claim is made for these classical reductions.

Every actual covariance-rank-at-most-two field obeys Jensen for any
globally ROC homogeneous quartic with positive rank-one values. To see
this, let L be its value subspace. Every real Fourier coefficient is
rank one and belongs to L. If det restricted to L is identically zero,
the field is pointwise rank one. For p(A)=J_r(M+A), the exact pointwise
identity on a rank-one R is

 p(R)-p(0)-Dp(0):R=J_r(R)/3+p''(R/3)[R,R]/2.

Integrating proves the lower bound f_G/3. If det|L is nonzero, the real
projective rank-one set in a two-dimensional L has at most two lines.
Fourier compatibility then puts the field on two independent spatial
frequency lines with independent output factors. It is the sum of two
independent one-variable profiles. Sequential rank-one Jensen, using
the product Haar law of the two phases, proves the inequality.

For covariance rank three let ell be the unique annihilating coefficient
matrix, so ell:G=0 pointwise. If rank ell=1, write ell=u tensor v.
The scalar potential component u.phi is invariant under translation
along v. Average over the closure of this torus translation flow.
That averaging leaves the u-row of the gradient unchanged. Its removed
part therefore lies in the common-output rank-one subspace u_perp
tensor R^2, on which every ROC integrand is convex. Conditional Jensen
reduces to the averaged field. The averaged potential is invariant
along v, so its gradient annihilates v and lies in the common-input
rank-one space R^2 tensor v_perp. Ordinary Jensen there finishes the
proof. On a rectangular normal form this is simply first-row Jensen
in x followed by second-column Jensen in y.

Thus a rank-three counterexample must have ell INVERTIBLE. Every such
actual field has the exact potential representation

                  G=T D^2 psi, T=ell^(-T) J.             (8)

Conversely each invertible T gives such a constraint. No covariance
surrogate is needed. For original J_r, proper output rotations and
positive scaling preserve the normalized all-mean problem. Polar
decomposition therefore reduces every invertible T to

 T=diag(1,epsilon) P, epsilon in {+1,-1},
 P=[[exp(x),y],[y,(1+y^2)exp(-x)]], x,y real.             (9)

P is positive definite with determinant one, and (9) covers all such
P. The whole rank-three unknown is therefore a scalar Hessian field
plus TWO real plane-shape parameters and one orientation sign. Its
mean matrix H_G is automatically positive definite whenever its actual
covariance rank is three, by Section 1. This replaces an unnecessarily
full-covariance parameterization; it is not a finite-dimensional proof
of the remaining universal field inequality.

capacity jump is unaffected: this theorem concerns only the actual
quartic's affine-mean minimization, not its nontranslation cubic charges
