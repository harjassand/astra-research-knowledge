# Selected research direction: Steering self-organized plasma confinement

**I would select a different physical programme: using a small, deliberately structured electromagnetic intervention to position and stabilize transport barriers that fusion-plasma turbulence already generates.**

The central idea is to control the *phase and location* of a protective collective mode, rather than supply all the energy required to create that mode.

If successful, this could allow magnetic-confinement reactors to reduce turbulent heat losses without paying the large power cost of externally generating the necessary plasma rotation. That would be a consequential advance toward sustained, economically useful fusion.

The first construction is complete at a reduced-physics level. It establishes an exact source-power requirement and an analytically solvable stochastic phase-control mechanism. It does **not** establish a working plasma controller, improved confinement or historical novelty.

## 1\. Why I selected this over continuing the molecular apparatus

I compared the complete intended capabilities rather than their most attractive component theorems.

|  | Molecular coherent discrimination | Fusion collective-flow control |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Whole success | Economical recovery of dilute, difficult-to-distinguish molecules | Substantially improved confinement and operating margin for fusion reactors |
| Existing physical opening | Derived chirality-dependent dipole interactions and internal-state-preserving phase operation | Experimentally observed protective zonal flows and derived electromagnetic momentum-transfer laws |
| Principal missing operation | High-throughput, whole-ensemble recognition and routing at acceptable coupling cost | Selective control of an existing protective mode without supplying its full amplitude |
| Strong ordinary competitor | Coherent state sorting with the same microwave interactions | Existing plasma heating, flow control and instability-avoidance systems |
| Current assessment | Valuable machinery; no complete-task advantage | Higher-consequence opening, with a tractable and falsifiable physical interface |

### Why the molecular route does not win this selection

The previous seven-state molecular construction remains a genuine microscopic recognition mechanism. Its balanced filter and shared-recorder interaction survive.

However, the retained single-molecule coupling in a representative cubic-centimetre microwave mode is only

\[
g_m/(2\pi)\approx0.3565\ \mathrm{Hz}.
\]

For an illustrative 1 cm traversal at 200 m/s, the interaction angle is only approximately \(1.1\times10^{-4}\) radians. The controlled ensemble also represents a limited fraction of the thermal rotational population, and the strong conventional coherent separator can preserve internal entropy too.

The most important resource—strong interaction with moving, internally mixed material—remains unearned. Further pulse optimization would not address it.

I therefore preserve this construction without making it the principal programme.

The distinction is important. Trying to generate a large flow directly is expensive. Steering an existing flow could be considerably cheaper if its spatial phase is a weakly constrained collective coordinate.

## 3\. My first construction: torque-neutral spatial wave control

Start from the known monochromatic-wave relation

\[
\mathcal Q_y=\frac{k_y}{\omega}P_{\mathrm{abs}},
\]

where \(P_{\mathrm{abs}}\) is absorbed wave power density and \(\mathcal Q_y\) is the transferred canonical poloidal momentum rate. The primary theory also contains a separate time derivative of stored wave-dressing momentum; that term cannot be silently discarded in a transient experiment. citeturn819083view0

I constructed a proposed actuator using two counter-propagating wave components. Their nonnegative deposited power profiles are \(p_+(x)\) and \(p_-(x)\)\.

In a specified effective momentum-transfer model,

\[
F_y(x)=
\eta\frac{k_y}{\omega}
K_\sigma*(p_+-p_-),
\]

where \(K_\sigma\) describes radial deposition width and \(\eta\) is the fraction transferred into the desired zonal-flow coordinate.

Choosing opposite-sign spatial deposits gives a shear-producing force while maintaining zero net applied momentum\:

\[
\int F_y(x)\,dx=0.
\]

This does not mean zero wave power. Both positive deposition profiles must be supplied and maintained.

### First result: supplying the whole flow is expensive

For a sinusoidal shear pattern at radial wavenumber \(k\), let the zonal-flow momentum equation be

\[
\partial_tU
=
D\partial_x^2U-\gamma U+\frac{F_y}{\rho}.
\]

Here \(D\) is effective momentum diffusivity, \(\gamma\) is damping and \(\rho\) is plasma mass density.

For an RMS shear \(S\), the least mean absorbed power within the continuously steerable Gaussian-deposition model is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{P_{\mathrm{abs}}}{V}
=
\frac{2\sqrt2}{\pi}
\frac{\rho\omega S}{\eta |k_y|}
\frac{\gamma+Dk^2}{k}
e^{k^2\sigma^2/2}.
}
\]

The expression is attained in this model by decomposing the deconvolved sinusoidal force into positive and negative wave-power profiles.

The optimal unconstrained radial wavenumber satisfies

\[
k_*^2=
\frac{2\gamma}
{D+\gamma\sigma^2+
\sqrt{(D+\gamma\sigma^2)^2+4D\gamma\sigma^2}}.
\]

This calculation has an important limitation: it assumes independently programmable deposition profiles. A real plasma wave dispersion relation and antenna geometry might not supply that freedom.

The resulting initial energy cost discouraged me from selecting full-amplitude wave pumping as the main programme.

## 4\. The stronger mechanism: steer the soft phase, not the amplitude

Now suppose turbulence itself maintains a propagating zonal mode,

\[
U(x,t)=A\cos(kx-\Omega t-\theta(t)).
\]

The amplitude \(A\) is supplied by the nonlinear plasma dynamics. The external wave need only control its phase \(\theta\)\.

Project a traveling wave-force pattern onto the phase-tangent mode

\[
\frac{\partial U}{\partial\theta}.
\]

The projection gives

\[
\boxed{
d\theta=
\left[\Delta\omega-\kappa\sin(\theta-\phi)\right]dt
+\sqrt{2D_\theta}\,dW_t,
}
\]

where \(\Delta\omega\) is drive detuning, \(D_\theta\) is phase diffusion and

\[
\kappa=\frac{F_0}{\rho A}.
\]

This is an Adler-type phase-locking equation, not a new general synchronization theorem. The positive construction is the specific coupling from signed wave momentum to a turbulence-sustained zonal mode, conditional on that mode possessing the assumed phase coordinate.

At zero detuning, its exact stationary distribution is

\[
p(\theta)=
\frac{\exp[(\kappa/D_\theta)\cos(\theta-\phi)]}
{2\pi I_0(\kappa/D_\theta)}.
\]

This allows an exact question: how much source power is needed to hold the barrier in a desired radial position?

For 95% probability within ±30° of the desired phase,

\[
\boxed{\frac{\kappa}{D_\theta}\ge14.6028.}
\]

The crucial new resource ratio is therefore not full flow strength divided by applied force. It is **phase diffusion divided by the available phase-control coupling**\.

For the same geometry and operating amplitude,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{P_{\mathrm{phase}}}{P_{\mathrm{amplitude}}}
=
\frac{\kappa}{\gamma+Dk^2}.
}
\]

A coherent mode with weak phase diffusion can be steered at a small fraction of the power needed to create it.

## 5\. Executable first test

I implemented the exact wave-power formulas, finite-width spatial deposition, a periodic phase-diffusion solver and a noisy Stuart–Landau amplitude/phase model.

The test uses a 50 m³ controlled region, ion density \(5\times10^{19}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}\), 120 kHz wave frequency, \(k_y=50\,\mathrm{m}^{-1}\), 12 mm deposition width, target shear \(10^5\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), 10% effective momentum transfer and 70% RF absorption. The large potential advantage is evident, but it disappears when phase diffusion is sufficiently strong.

The finite-time calculation also checked startup. Starting from uniformly distributed phases, a case with \(\kappa=100\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) and \(D_\theta=5\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) reaches approximately 95.1% within the target interval after 0.05 seconds.

The fuller noisy amplitude model produced 97.8% within the interval, with only a 1.5% increase in mean amplitude when amplitude restoration was strong. When that restoration was weak, the amplitude increased by about 71% instead. In that case, the apparent phase-control success partly came from generating additional flow. The phase-only description would be misleading.

Finally, spatial implementation matters. With sixteen idealized deposition ports, the chosen force generated spurious shear amounting to about 67% of the target shear norm. Thirty-two ports reduced that to approximately 0.073%; sixty-four reduced it below the reported numerical resolution. Those port counts are model discretizations, not a feasible antenna design.

## 6\. What would make this a consequential capability?

The benefit is not simply a stronger zonal flow. It is **placing already available shear where turbulent heat transport is most damaging**\.

For a localized heat-loss region, the phase model predicts that a strongly pinned sinusoidal mode can increase expected local squared shear by approximately 1.87 times compared with a uniformly wandering phase, at unchanged mode amplitude.

That is not a prediction of a 1.87-fold reduction in heat flux. A nonlinear plasma calculation must establish that mapping.

The distinction also supplies a strong falsifier. If the temperature gradient is spatially uniform, if the self-generated flow already occupies the best phase, or if moving the mode changes its nonlinear energy supply unfavorably, positioning may yield no benefit.

The equally informed ordinary comparator is existing RF, neutral-beam and feedback-based plasma control with the same actuator access. It may implement the same phase-locking law or outperform it. Recent work already combines zonal-flow control with plasma profile modification, and reinforcement-learning control has demonstrated instability avoidance in DIII-D. citeturn232880search8turn318270search3

No superiority over those systems is established.

## 7\. The live obstruction that should dominate the next investigation

The theoretical phase mechanism is not the main unresolved step anymore. The decisive missing operation is\:

**Can an actual antenna and plasma susceptibility produce a controlled, phase-selective force on a naturally sustained zonal mode without excessive heating, parasitic transport or loss of the mode itself?**

That requires connecting three measured or calculated objects in one experiment: the electromagnetic deposition map, the complex response of the naturally sustained mode, and the resulting nonlinear heat flux.

The most important failure conditions are physical rather than numerical: inadequate radial selectivity, large phase diffusion, frequency detuning, unfavorable mode response, or additional magnetic perturbations that damage confinement. Astra's source-specific field-line exit construction is a useful warning against treating an apparently small magnetic perturbation as harmless; it is not a proof about this RF system. urlAstra governing field-line argumenthttps://github.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/blob/db0969a8586afaa38137a4371b3457c5576fadea/research/2026-10-29-atomic-fields-confinement-and-contact/source/fusion/scientific_sources/parent/resonant_field_interface/PROOF.md\.

There is already useful public computational infrastructure. The url2026 Gkeyll TCV simulation datasethttps://zenodo.org/records/20146930 contains 256 gyrokinetic turbulence cases and full phase-space outputs, but does not supply a verified RF phase-pinning operation. It is a potential calibration and comparison source, not evidence that this controller works. citeturn989309search0

## Selection decision

I would commit the next investigation to **phase-selective control of naturally sustained protective zonal flows**, conditional on first earning the actual wave-to-mode interface. The alternative of creating the whole flow was tested and is substantially more expensive in the illustrative scenario. The molecular coherent-discrimination programme remains preserved but is not the strongest next expenditure of research effort.

The strongest scientific state reached is\:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Externally supplied control power}
\;\text{can scale with phase-restoration demand,}
\;\text{rather than full mode damping.}
}
\]

That is exact within the constructed soft-mode model and has a physically motivated source channel. It is **not yet an established reactor principle**\: the missing bridge is a verified, resource-accounted RF-to-zonal-phase response and a matched nonlinear confinement benefit.

The next turn should attack that bridge directly. A successful construction would turn externally positioned, turbulence-powered transport barriers from a synchronization idea into a potentially consequential new plasma-control capability.
