## Two results that make future claims harder to miscompose

### 1. Area, duty and reset enter one dissipation bound

I used the explicit convention

\[
j_C=\frac{F N_{\mathrm{delivered}}}
{T_{\mathrm{cycle}}A_{\mathrm{all}}},
\]

with one Faraday per delivered mole and **all installed separator area**.

For block \(i\), let \(z_i\) be absolute charge throughput per delivered carbon, \(s_i\) the number of counted sheets, \(r_i\) full-cell area resistance, \(f_i\) operating duty and \(u\) usable installed-area fraction. Then

\[
\boxed{
W_\Omega\ge
\frac{Fj_C}{u}
\left(
\sum_i z_i\sqrt{\frac{s_i r_i}{f_i}}
\right)^2 .
}
\]

Reset and parasitic charge belong in \(z_i\). Electrical recovery does not cancel \(I^2R\) dissipation. A reused separator is counted once physically, with its complete time and charge history.

This is a necessary bound, not a performance prediction. It also keeps **stored charge capacity** distinct from absolute charge passed: a simple charge/discharge cycle passes twice its stored charge window. The predecessor’s 0.680 M liquid carbon capacity establishes neither quantity.

### 2. Measured forward and backward fluxes give an exact operating gate

At one common membrane cross-section, suppose the measured pattern is:

- \(A\): forward carbonate carbon;
- \(B\): backward bicarbonate carbon;
- \(N\): backward neutral carbon;
- \(L\): unrecovered external carbon;
- \(O\): other forward charge, in electron equivalents.

Then

\[
D=A-B-N-L,\qquad
W_{\mathrm{cell}}
=FU\frac{2A-B+O}{D},
\]

where \(U\) is the **full terminal voltage**.

Writing \(b=B/A\), \(n=(N+L)/A\), \(o=O/A\) and \(X=FU\), a cell-work allowance \(W_{\max}\) requires

\[
\boxed{
(W_{\max}-X)b+W_{\max}n+Xo
\le W_{\max}-2X,\qquad b+n<1 .
}
\]

At a full terminal voltage of 0.150 V and a 35 kJ/mol cell allowance, this becomes

\[
20.5272b+35n+14.4728o\le6.0544.
\]

All leakage terms spend the **same** allowance. The same measurement must also establish sufficient absolute delivered flux after area and duty corrections.

This result does not assume a linear transport law. I also tested a linear Onsager formulation and found a nonlinear counterexample to treating its energy floor as universal; that limitation is preserved in the archive.
