# Complete radiation channels, an exact counterexample, and a matched ordinary comparator

27 October 2026. Continuation of `EXTERNAL_RADIATION_AND_EXACT_TRANSITION_MEASURE.md`. The principal theorems in this note concern the supplied number-conserving hard-core model. They must not be transferred unchanged to a non-RWA Hamiltonian coupled to a gapless external field.

## 1. A complete criterion for adding an external bath at finite engineered coupling

Fix an engineered coupling `g` for which the source has an even pair eigenstate `P_g` of energy `E_p`, isolated in its reflection-even two-particle sector. Let its isolation distance be `d_s > 0`. Let `h_sigma` be the **complete** one-particle source operator in parity `sigma`, including bound states and scattering states.

Add independent free bosonic external fields with parity-resolved one-particle supports `F_+` and `F_-`. Before coupling those fields to the memories, the additional reflection-even two-particle spectra are exactly the sets

\[
\bigcup_{\sigma=\pm}
\left[(F_\sigma+\sigma(h_\sigma))\cup(F_\sigma+F_\sigma)\right].
\]

The first terms include one engineered/source excitation and one external photon. The second terms include two external photons, including photons in different external bands of the same parity. Opposite-parity sums are odd and do not enter this particular sector.

**Sufficient complete-channel theorem.** Assume

\[
d_{\rm ext}:=\operatorname{dist}\left(E_p,
\bigcup_\sigma[(F_\sigma+\sigma(h_\sigma))\cup(F_\sigma+F_\sigma)]
\right)>0.\tag{1}
\]

Assume the external-memory coupling is number conserving, reflection preserving, and is generated by square-integrable one-particle form factors, so its restriction `lambda V_2` to the two-excitation sector is bounded. Then the enlarged uncoupled pair is an isolated eigenstate in its full even sector with gap at least

\[
d=\min(d_s,d_{\rm ext}).
\]

For sufficiently small `lambda`, Kato perturbation theory gives a unique normalizable even eigenbranch continuing that pair. For example, a Riesz circle of radius `d/2` remains valid when `||lambda V_2|| < d/2`. Its energy is real and there is **no external radiation channel**.

To retain the entire logical code, also require each source one-body bound energy `E_sigma` to have positive distance from `F_sigma`. Then both one-body bound states continue as isolated eigenstates in their parity sectors. Their ground-subtracted connected interaction is continuous in `lambda`, and remains nonzero when the starting interaction is nonzero and `lambda` is sufficiently small.

The original odd scattering interval `[4,8]`, generated by an upper and lower engineered photon, remains in the essential spectrum after these finite local couplings. Thus if the continued pair remains near 6 it is still globally embedded, despite its gap in the complete even sector. This is an exact combined-Hamiltonian statement, not a second-order LDOS statement.

Proof: The enlarged uncoupled two-particle Hilbert space decomposes into zero, one and two external-photon summands. On those summands its Hamiltonian is, respectively, the source hard-core two-particle operator, a source one-particle operator plus an external one-particle operator, and the free external two-particle operator. Taking even parity gives precisely the sumsets above. Condition (1) therefore supplies a genuine isolated eigenvalue. Bounded perturbation and a Riesz projection give the continuation. The bath one-particle essential supports survive finite-rank couplings; hard-core compression changes the harmonic two-particle resolvent by finite rank, so the opposite-parity two-photon essential interval persists.

Equation (1) is sufficient, not necessary. Special destructive-interference states or coincident bound-state poles can survive without such a gap. The point is that a controlled gap certificate must include **all** the listed channels.

All-sector nonnegativity is a separate condition. It is retained if the enlarged one-body Schur complement stays nonnegative. For finite inverse-frequency form integrals this follows from a strict inequality of the form

\[
3-\kappa_\sigma^2g^2\int\frac{d\nu_{A_\sigma}(\omega)}\omega
-\lambda^2\int\frac{d\nu_{F_\sigma}(\omega)}\omega>0.
\]

Second quantization and hard-core compression then preserve nonnegativity in every fixed particle sector. This does not assert an occupation-capacity law.

## 2. A finite support choice that does preserve the pair exactly

At zero couplings the engineered bath supports are

\[
A_+=[4,6],\qquad A_-=[0,2],\qquad \varepsilon=3.
\]

Choose additional external bands

\[
\boxed{F_+=[9/4,5/2],\qquad F_-=[7/2,15/4].}\tag{2}
\]

Despite having an external band on each side of the memory frequency, every even sum avoids 6:

| Even channel | Support nearest the target |
|---|---|
| `A_+ + F_+` | `[6.25,8.5]` |
| `F_+ + F_+` | `[4.5,5]` |
| `A_- + F_-` | `[3.5,5.75]` |
| `F_- + F_-` | `[7,7.5]` |
| `3 + F_+` | `[5.25,5.5]` |
| `3 + F_-` | `[6.5,6.75]` |

The new minimum even gap at zero coupling is `1/4`. The opposite-parity external sum `F_+ + F_- = [5.75,6.25]` actually adds another global continuum through the pair. The pair still persists exactly in the even sector for sufficiently small bounded couplings.

Each narrow band can be realized as the spectrum of a semi-infinite Jacobi chain with hopping `1/16`, with centers `19/8` and `29/8`. The even/odd assignment of those chains is a physical coupling resource; it is not supplied for free by ordinary vacuum.

The interaction remains nonzero on an open set. To see its leading dependence, take equal external coupling amplitude `lambda` to the two bands. If `m_F` is the lower narrow-band Weyl function, spectral reflection around 3 gives opposite values but equal derivatives for the two new band responses. In a joint small-coupling expansion,

\[
\Delta\Sigma(3)=-3ag^2+2m_F(3)\lambda^2,
\qquad \Delta\Sigma'(3)=-bg^2,
\]

\[
\chi=-\Delta\Sigma(3)\Delta\Sigma'(3)+O((|g|+|\lambda|)^6)
=-3abg^4+2b m_F(3)g^2\lambda^2+O((|g|+|\lambda|)^6).
\]

Thus small external coupling does not force the useful interaction to vanish. A tuned cancellation is possible, so protection alone still does not guarantee an interaction.

## 3. A memory-frequency notch that passes the entire one-emission check and still fails

Now put **both** narrow external bands into the even channel:

\[
\boxed{F_+=[9/4,5/2]\cup[7/2,15/4],\qquad F_-=\varnothing.}\tag{3}
\]

The external field has a clean gap `(2.5,3.5)` around the memory frequency. For the source pair throughout `0 < g <= 0.4`, the pair-to-even-bound frequency is inside this notch. Furthermore, an external photon plus an engineered upper-band photon has energy at least `4+2.25=6.25`, above the source pair. Therefore **every second-order external-emission term in the exact transition-measure formula vanishes**. This is stronger than merely checking the bare memory frequency.

Nevertheless,

\[
[9/4,5/2]+[7/2,15/4]=[5.75,6.25]
\]

contains the pair energy for all sufficiently small nonzero engineered `g`, by continuity from 6. Two even external photons conserve the pair's parity and can carry away its energy. The exact obstruction below is asserted on this small-`g` neighborhood; the broader source interval `g <= 0.4` is used above only for the second-order channel exclusion. This channel first appears at fourth order in the external coupling at a fixed nonzero engineered `g`.

### Exact obstruction to a real pair pole

Include the two external bands directly in the plus one-body operator. Its complete endpoint measure has strictly positive density on both narrow bands whenever `lambda != 0`. The even hard-core resolvent still has exactly the source form

\[
\langle p,(H_2-z)^{-1}p\rangle
=\frac{2L_+(z)L_-(z)}{L_+(z)+L_-(z)},
\]

now using the **combined** endpoint measures. Near the old pair energy, the external-external convolution supplies

\[
\operatorname{Im}L_+(E+i0)
\ge\pi\int\rho_{+,\lambda}(x)\rho_{+,\lambda}(E-x)\,dx>0.
\]

The minus channel is real there. Hence `L_+(E+i0)+L_-(E+i0)` cannot have a real zero in a neighborhood of the old pair energy. For fixed nonzero `g` and sufficiently small nonzero `lambda`, the source pair branch has no real eigenvalue continuation with nonzero bare-pair overlap. This conclusion comes from positive spectral density, without assigning a numerical lifetime.

A single double-bound pole does not rescue the physical pair: the harmonic-mean expression remains finite when only one `L_sigma` diverges. A coincident pole in both channels is a distinct special case, excluded here by fixing `g > 0` and taking `lambda` sufficiently small. At `g = 0`, coincident bound poles can indeed occur, so this argument must not be extended to that singular endpoint.

### Leading fourth-order external coefficient

Let `G_{+,g}(x)` denote the source plus endpoint resolvent at a point of the external bands, where it is real. Let `rho_F` be the sum of the two narrow-chain endpoint densities. The new endpoint density is

\[
\rho_{+,\lambda}(x)
=\lambda^2\rho_F(x)|G_{+,g}(x)|^2+O(\lambda^4).
\]

Define the positive quantity

\[
C_g(E)=\int \rho_F(x)\rho_F(E-x)
|G_{+,g}(x)|^2|G_{+,g}(E-x)|^2\,dx.
\]

Under the local analytic-continuation/resonance regularity assumptions, the pair population linewidth begins at

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_{\rm ext}
=\frac{2\pi C_g(E_p)}{L_{+,g}'(E_p)+L_{-,g}'(E_p)}\lambda^4
+o(\lambda^4)\quad\text{at fixed }g>0.}\tag{4}
\]

The exact positive-density obstruction above does not depend on promoting (4) to an exponential decay law.

For the stated Jacobi bands, the subsequent small-`g` coefficient is explicit. Since

\[
C_0(6)=\int\frac{\rho_F(x)\rho_F(6-x)}{(x-3)^4}\,dx
=\frac{16384}{27\pi^2},
\]

and `2/(L_+'+L_-')=9a^2g^4+O(g^6)`,

\[
\boxed{\Gamma_{\rm ext}
\sim\frac{16384a^2}{3\pi}\lambda^4g^4
=499.244981712\ldots\,\lambda^4g^4.}\tag{5}
\]

The limits in (4)-(5) are ordered: first external coupling tends to zero at fixed nonzero `g`, then the coefficient is expanded in `g`. No uniform fixed-`lambda`, `g -> 0` conclusion is asserted.

For completeness, the elementary integral follows by reflecting the upper band into the lower and setting `d=3-x`, so `d in [1/2,3/4]`. The square of the semicircle density is proportional to

\[
\frac{-d^2+(5/4)d-3/8}{d^4}.
\]

Its integral is

\[
\left[d^{-1}-\frac58d^{-2}+\frac18d^{-3}\right]_{1/2}^{3/4}=\frac1{54},
\]

which yields the coefficient above after including both ordered band assignments.

## 4. Can a common finite photonic gap close all these channels?

Within the number-conserving two-excitation problem, yes, but the required gap can be much wider than the memory notch. Add a carrier `Omega > 1`, giving engineered bands `[Omega,Omega+2]` and `[Omega+4,Omega+6]`, memory `Omega+3`, and bare pair `2Omega+6`. Suppose both external parities have supports contained in

\[
\boxed{F_\pm\subset[0,\Omega-1]\cup[2\Omega+7,\infty).}\tag{6}
\]

Then every high external photon already exceeds the pair energy. Two low external photons have total energy at most `2Omega-2`. A low external photon plus any engineered photon has total energy at most `2Omega+5`; plus a memory excitation it has energy at most `2Omega+2`. Thus all mixed and double-external channels stay separated from the bare pair by at least 1. The combined theorem applies for sufficiently small bounded couplings.

This is a **broad** external gap, stretching from below the lowest engineered band to above the total pair energy. It does not establish a practical electromagnetic structure or a useful advantage over ordinary bandgap shielding.

Crucially, if excitation conservation is removed and the external field retains a genuinely coupled continuum down to zero, arbitrarily many low-frequency external photons can have the pair's total energy and allowed excitation parity. Condition (6) then supplies no full-Fock sector gap. A non-RWA exact theorem needs an additional argument, such as a positive minimum frequency for every coupled external mode, or a genuine all-orders dark-state mechanism. The parent's carrier repair for the engineered bath does not automatically give the external vacuum this minimum frequency.

## 5. The ordinary single-band comparator is stronger for the currently demonstrated local CZ task

Use one semi-infinite bath with band `[0,2]`, keep both memories at energy 3, and couple both memories equally to its endpoint with amplitude `sqrt(3/2) g`. The bright memory mode then has endpoint coupling `sqrt3 g`; the dark mode is uncoupled. The total squared memory-bath coupling is

\[
\|v_+\|^2+\|v_-\|^2=3g^2,
\]

exactly matching the source's `2g^2 + g^2`. The leading pair bath occupation is also the same, `3bg^2`, and the leading one-particle exchange splitting has the same magnitude, `3ag^2`.

The general hard-core interaction formula gives

\[
\boxed{\chi_{\rm one\ band}=+9abg^4+O(g^6)
=+1.492267835786\ldots\,g^4+O(g^6).}\tag{7}
\]

Its magnitude is **three times** the source's `3abg^4`. This pair is isolated from the entire two-particle continuum, not only a symmetry sector. Both memories remain hard-core; no Kerr interaction is inserted. The same adiabatic/echo logic applies in the declared model. The sign difference has no cost for a CZ phase modulo `2pi`; an upper single band gives the opposite sign if needed.

The result is also visible directly inside the source's existing tunability formula. At fixed `t^2+s^2`, the magnitude of

\[
\chi(t,s)=-8ab\,ts(t^2+s^2)+O((|t|+|s|)^6)
\]

is maximized at `t=s` or `t=-s`. For `t=s=(sqrt3/2)g`, one parity coupling vanishes and the remaining one has magnitude `sqrt3 g`. The unused parity continuum can be retained as a spectator, making the same operative single-band mechanism globally embedded, or removed, leaving an ordinary globally bound gate. Thus global embedding itself does not supply the demonstrated interaction advantage.

This comparison matches total coupling strength, leading dressing population, leading exchange magnitude, locality at the same endpoint, and the hard-core resource. It does not establish an all-platform or all-noise dominance theorem. A use requiring the otherwise available global scattering channels could change the comparison, but no such extra capability has yet been acquired for this construction.
