ASTRA P2 — An interacting embedded pair in a local oscillator–spin ladder
27 October 2026. hbar=1; frequencies are measured in the original band scale B.

This is a continuation of the supplied symmetry-filtered hard-core pair. It
removes exact excitation-number conservation as an assumption, and constructs
a simpler port arrangement with a provably nonzero interaction. The bath is an
explicit positive nearest-neighbour spring network. The two-level memories
remain declared spins, not a derivation from a complete electromagnetic device.
External radiation, material loss, finite-temperature noise, pulse costs, and
composition with other active modules are not removed by the theorem.

1. THE EXACT LOCAL HAMILTONIAN

Take a semi-infinite two-rail oscillator ladder with canonical coordinates
q_{A,j},q_{B,j},p_{A,j},p_{B,j}. In oscillator normal ordering its Hamiltonian is

    H_b = (p.p + q.K.q)/2 - vacuum constant,

where K has diagonal W^2, nearest-neighbour rail entries -W, and rung entries
-4W. All other entries vanish. Equivalently, the potential is a sum of positive
springs: rail spring constant W, rung spring constant 4W, and grounding spring
constant W^2-6W, with an extra grounding constant W at the first site of each
rail to keep its diagonal equal to W^2. Thus W>6 suffices for a passive positive
bath. No rotating-wave approximation is used in this coordinate Hamiltonian.

Two spins L,R each have energy W n_i, n_i=(1+sigma_z,i)/2. Couple them by

    V = g sqrt(2W) [sigma_x,L (t q_A,0 + s q_B,0)
                  +sigma_x,R (t q_B,0 + s q_A,0)].

The useful simpler construction has t>0 and s=0: each spin couples only to its
own first-rung coordinate. All bath and coupling terms are local. The factor
sqrt(2W) fixes the coupling convention as W changes; changing W is a physical
change to the network and memories, not a harmless rotating-frame shift.

Rail/spin exchange S and excitation parity Pi commute with H=H_b+W(nL+nR)+V.
Pi is joint coordinate inversion times (-1)^(nL+nR), equivalently
(-1)^(N_normal_modes+nL+nR). Excitation NUMBER does not commute with H.

The symmetric bath branch is lower and the antisymmetric branch is upper.
This reverses the branch labels of the preserved positive-rung hopping model:

    A_l=[sqrt(W^2-6W),sqrt(W^2-2W)],  S=+;
    A_h=[sqrt(W^2+2W),sqrt(W^2+6W)],  S=-.

The frequency floor is physical. This model does not retain a bath with
arbitrarily small absolute photon frequencies.

2. SPECTRAL PROTECTION WITHOUT NUMBER CONSERVATION

At g=0 the bare pair |ee,0> has energy 2W in (Pi,S)=(+,+). Ground, symmetric
one-spin, and antisymmetric one-spin inputs occupy (+,+),(-,+),(-,-).
Within the pair sector, the other two-excitation configurations have energies

    W+A_l, W+A_h, A_l+A_l, A_h+A_h.

They all avoid 2W. The mixed sum A_l+A_h has S=- and is excluded from this
sector. The vacuum lies at zero. Every configuration with at least four total
excitations has energy at least 4m, where m=sqrt(W^2-6W) and each spin energy
W exceeds m. For W>8,

    4m > 2W.

Consequently the bare pair is a simple isolated eigenvalue in its joint
symmetry sector, with the valid gap lower bound

    d_p=min(W-max A_l, min A_h-W, 4m-2W, 2W)>0.

For the one-spin eigenvalues a corresponding valid gap lower bound is

    d_1=min(W-max A_l,min A_h-W,3m-W,W)>0.

These bounds are conservative. They enumerate all Fock sectors using Pi;
they do not truncate the oscillator occupation.

Because m>0, endpoint field operators are infinitesimally H0-bounded. The
spin matrices are bounded. Thus H(g) is self-adjoint on a common domain,
bounded below, and an analytic perturbation family near g=0. Completing the
positive coordinate square also gives a direct finite lower bound for every
finite g. Kato isolated-eigenvalue perturbation within each invariant sector
gives analytic branches E_G(g),E_+(g),E_-(g),E_P(g) for sufficiently small g.
They are even functions of g, since coordinate inversion maps g to -g.

The pair is still globally embedded. At g=0,

    2W is in the interior of A_l+A_h for W>8.

For example, at W=12 the mixed interval is approximately [21.44676,25.65139]
and the pair energy is 24. The same-symmetry pair gap is at least .96148,
while the four-photon threshold is 33.94113.

For small g, the full Hamiltonian has scattering Weyl sequences consisting
of its dressed ground state plus one lower-branch photon and one upper-branch
photon translated far from the endpoint. Their total (Pi,S) is (+,-) and
their energies fill E_G(g)+A_l+A_h. Endpoint coupling commutators vanish along
these escaping wavepackets. Since E_P(g)-E_G(g)=2W+O(g^2) remains in that
interval, E_P(g) lies in the global essential spectrum while remaining
isolated in (+,+). This establishes an exact embedded eigenstate of the
untruncated, non-number-conserving Hamiltonian.

3. CONNECTED ENERGY MUST INCLUDE THE DRESSED VACUUM

The gate-relevant connected energy is

    chi(g)=E_P(g)+E_G(g)-E_+(g)-E_-(g).

Omitting E_G would introduce a spurious second-order interaction. Exact
second-order cancellation gives chi(g)=C4 g^4+O(g^6). Define the spin basis
0,+,-,2. The coordinate couplers X_+=(sigma_x,L+sigma_x,R)/sqrt(2) and
X_-=(sigma_x,L-sigma_x,R)/sqrt(2) act as

    X_+: 0 <-> + <-> 2, with both matrix elements +1;
    X_-: 0 <-> -, matrix element +1; 2 <-> -, matrix element -1.

These signs are used in both the proof and the independent Fock computation.

4. AN EXACT FOURTH-ORDER SPECTRAL FUNCTIONAL

This part applies more generally to two identical spins coupled transversely
to independent parity baths. Let eta_+,eta_- be their coupling-squared spectral
measures BEFORE multiplication by g^2, including all endpoint form factors.
Assume their supports avoid the spin frequency epsilon, and the relevant
zero/two-excitation denominators admit the isolated-sector perturbation used
above. Set the signed measure delta=eta_+-eta_- and

    C(w)=1/(epsilon^2-w^2),
    G=integral w C(w) ddelta(w),
    H=integral C(w)^2 ddelta(w),
    J=double-integral C(w)C(v)/(w+v) ddelta(w) ddelta(v).

Then the exact fourth-order connected coefficient is

    C4 = 8 epsilon^2 G H - 4 epsilon^2 J.                 (1)

The last quadratic form has the representation

    J=integral_0^infinity [integral C(w) exp(-w u) ddelta(w)]^2 du >=0.

This is a fourth-order identity, not an all-orders energy formula. It retains
both rotating and counterrotating intermediate processes.

Derivation: put a_w=(epsilon-w)^-1, b_w=(epsilon+w)^-1, and for each parity
sigma write A_sigma=integral a d eta_sigma, A2_sigma=integral a^2 d eta_sigma,
B_sigma=integral b d eta_sigma, B2_sigma=integral b^2 d eta_sigma. Standard
nondegenerate fourth-order perturbation in each symmetry sector is

    E4=<n|V R V R V R V|n>-<n|V R V|n><n|V R^2 V|n>,
    R=Q(E0-H0)^-1 Q.

Starting with no bath photons, at most two photons occur in a returning
fourth-order path. Using the X signs above and summing the four connected
energies reduces the result to the same-parity quadratic functional applied
to delta. Its symmetric kernel is

    (a_w a_v-b_w b_v)/(2epsilon)
    -(a_w+a_v)(b_w+b_v)/(w+v)
    +[(a_w-b_w)(a_v^2+b_v^2)+(a_v-b_v)(a_w^2+b_w^2)]/2.

Algebra reduces this kernel to

    4epsilon^2 C(w)C(v)[w C(v)+v C(w)-1/(w+v)],

whose double integral is (1). Cross-parity terms have the negative of the
same polarized kernel; this is why only eta_+-eta_- survives. In particular,
identical parity measures, equivalent to independent identical local baths,
give zero connected coefficient as required.

5. AN ANALYTIC POSITIVITY CERTIFICATE THROUGHOUT W>8, WITH NO CROSS PORT

Take s=0. Write u in [1,3] for the reflection-paired band parameter, with
normalized endpoint density

    rho(u)=(2/pi)sqrt(1-(u-2)^2),
    w_l(u)=sqrt(W^2-2Wu), w_h(u)=sqrt(W^2+2Wu).

The coupling measures are d eta_l=t^2 rho(u) W/w_l du and
 d eta_h=t^2 rho(u) W/w_h du. Define

    a=integral rho(u)/u du=4-2sqrt(3),  integral u rho(u) du=2.

The signed quantity z=C delta is a positive measure on both bands. Direct
substitution gives

    G=t^2 a,
    H=t^2 integral rho(u)/[u(w_l+w_h)w_l w_h] du.

A stronger exact representation follows from

    1/[w v(w+v)]=(2/pi) integral_0^infinity
                         dxi/[(w^2+xi^2)(v^2+xi^2)].

Put A(y)=1+y^2 and

    F_W(y)=integral_1^3 [rho(u)/u] A(y)/[A(y)^2-4u^2/W^2] du.

Using the preceding identity in G,H,J and completing the square gives

    C4 = 2t^4 a^2/W
         -(8t^4/(pi W)) integral_0^infinity
                         [F_W(y)-a/A(y)]^2 dy.            (2)

No large-W approximation is made in (2). Let D=F_W-a/A. Since u<=3 and A>=1,

    0 <= D(y)
      = (4/W^2) integral rho(u)u/[A(A^2-4u^2/W^2)] du
      <= 8/(W^2-36) A(y)^(-3).

The elementary integral integral_0^infinity (1+y^2)^(-6)dy=63pi/512 therefore
proves the two-sided analytic estimate

    (t^4/W)[2a^2-63/(W^2-36)^2] <= C4 <= 2a^2 t^4/W.  (3)

For every W>8, the lower bracket is greater than
2a^2-63/784 = .494016... >0. Thus the interaction is strictly nonzero over the
ENTIRE W>8 region in which the pair has the stated joint-symmetry gap.

For example, with t=1,W=12, (3) gives

    .0474144102690 <= C4 <= .0478644803898.

The continuum quadrature gives .0475405924657, inside the analytic interval.
The interval, rather than the sampled decimal, establishes nonzero interaction.

Consequently, for every fixed W>8 and t>0, the exact globally embedded branch
of the full, untruncated Hamiltonian has chi=C4 g^4+O(g^6) with C4>0 for all
sufficiently small nonzero g. The allowed small-g radius is not uniform as W approaches 8 from above and has not been optimized
or made into a finite-time experimental tolerance.

6. WHY THE OMITTED TERMS CREATE THE INTERACTION

For general fixed t,s, set p=(t+s)^2, q=(t-s)^2 and
b=integral rho(u)/u^2 du=4/sqrt(3)-2. Expanding (1), uniformly on the compact
band parameter interval as W tends to infinity, gives

    C4(W)=ab(p^2-q^2) + a^2(p+q)^2/(2W) + O(W^-2).     (4)

Equivalently,

    C4(W)=8ab ts(t^2+s^2) + 2a^2(t^2+s^2)^2/W+O(W^-2).

The first term is the preserved number-conserving interaction with lower
and upper branch labels exchanged. The second is a physical contribution
from the full coordinate coupling. In the own-rail-only case s=0,

    chi(g)=t^4[2a^2/W-63/W^5+O(W^-7)]g^4+O(g^6).

In (2), F_W-a/A=8/[W^2 A^3]+O(W^-4), uniformly with an integrable
majorant, so the correction starts at 63/W^5 and the next order is W^-7.
The own-rail RWA-only coefficient, by comparison, is

    C4_RWA=4a t^4/W^3+O(W^-5).

The missing counterrotating terms therefore change the leading power of the
carrier scale in the connected interaction. Both expansions are at fixed
port factors, with the weak-g coefficient taken before the large-W limit.

The original exactly reflected, number-conserving own-rail model had chi=0
at all orders. That all-orders cancellation is not inherited by this different
physical oscillator Hamiltonian. For the passive spring spectrum, the
rotating-wave calculation alone has no own-rail 1/W term; the full transverse
processes produce the leading nonzero interaction.

7. EXECUTED CHECKS AND THEIR SCOPE

ownrail_spectral_functional.py evaluates (1) and (2) independently, compares
them with (3), and diagonalizes the actual own-rail-only Hamiltonian. At t=1,
W=12 and four modes per rail, chi/g^4=.0464931633 at g=.1, .0469484870 at
g=.075, and .0472755173 at g=.05, approaching its independently calculated
finite-mode coefficient .0475382315. This directly tests the new simpler
port construction. Its JSON output preserves the results.

nonrwa_pair.py performs two independent calculations:
(a) finite-mode fourth-order Rayleigh--Schrodinger recurrence, and
(b) sparse diagonalization of full transverse spin-boson Fock matrices in the
three symmetry sectors. The latter keeps counterrotating terms and includes
the dressed-vacuum energy in chi. A photon cutoff is used only for these
numerical checks, not in the theorem.

For t=(1+sqrt(2))/2, s=(sqrt(2)-1)/2, W=12, C4 converges as follows:

    modes/rail 8:  .601916099191040
    modes/rail 16: .601916105149633
    modes/rail 32: .601916105149636
    modes/rail 64: .601916105149635.

At four modes/rail its perturbative value is .601790009186343. Complete
Fock diagonalization yields chi/g^4=.587782625 at g=.1, .593862160 at g=.075,
and .598250846 at g=.05, approaching that finite-mode perturbative value.
Increasing the photon cutoff from four to six at g=.1 changes chi by about
7e-15; eigenpair residuals are around 1e-14 or smaller.

The compact functional (1) matches the independent recurrence in nine tested
(W,s) cases to below 2e-15. See compact_formula_check.json.

With s=0 and the same t, W*C4 is 1.21123551 at W=12, 1.21946665 at W=50,
and 1.21948825 at W=200, approaching the analytic 2a^2t^4=1.21948834. These
values and the corresponding RWA-only comparison are in
nonrwa_asymptotic_results.json. They are floating-point diagnostics, not
interval certificates. The proof of strict positivity is the analytic bound (3).

8. CONTROL CONSEQUENCE AND REMAINING PHYSICAL COSTS

The isolated branches and exact Pi,S symmetries permit slow symmetric on/off
ramps and the same two-cycle memory echo at g=0 as in the supplied proof.
The connected phase must use E_P+E_G-E_+-E_- and include ramp contributions.
The adiabatic claim is qualitative until constants and finite-time control
costs are bounded. Additional electric or magnetic fields can violate Pi;
memory asymmetry can violate S. A full electromagnetic vacuum has both
photon parities and can permit same-total-symmetry decay even when S survives.

Our separate microscopic derivation finds ordinary vacuum population loss
2lambda^2 Gamma0+O(lambda^2g^2), and an exact spectral measure for the residual
states. Notching the pair-to-bound transitions removes the leading external
channel at second order in that external coupling, but does not prove exact
protection at all electromagnetic orders. Uniform rail loss still samples
virtual oscillator occupation. A further exact counterexample adds two
same-parity external bands [2.25,2.5] and [3.5,3.75]: every second-order
external-emission term vanishes, but their two-photon sum contains the pair.
Its positive spectral convolution excludes a real continuation of the pair
for fixed sufficiently small nonzero engineered coupling and small nonzero
external coupling. Thus even an exact leading-order emission cancellation is
not an all-orders environmental certificate. Those results apply to the original RWA
ladder; their coefficients must not be silently transplanted to this model.

Two active modules can also radiate cooperatively while preserving both
individual parities; the isolated-unit gap does not compose freely. A sharper
abstract result now proves that tensor powers of a simple isolated target
have an N-independent gap on the orthogonal complement of its product state
if and only if its accessible complementary spectrum is entirely one-sided.
Two-sided spectral points can cancel under repeated sums; a continuum on one
side and just a discrete state on the other already cause finite-copy essential
spectrum overlap. This is spectral overlap, not a claim of decay for every
coupler. The density-coupled ladder example supplies a nonzero physical
transition coefficient. In particular, the lower dressed vacuum in this
non-RWA parity sector is a substantive composition cost, even if other nearby
continua were moved above the pair.
An ordinary single-accessible-band comparator is faster and more robust to
memory detuning at fixed leading endpoint coupling in the original gate
model. The present own-rail construction buys a simpler port arrangement
and exact non-RWA existence, not demonstrated gate superiority.

The physical deliverable is an exact local oscillator--spin construction and
a reusable fourth-order spectral identity, with a strict interaction bound.
It is not an experimental acquisition, a complete Maxwell/Josephson model,
a new fundamental force, or an established novelty/priority claim.
