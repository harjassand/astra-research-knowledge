The strongest matched optimizer governs the conclusion: ordinary multistart SLSQP gives IDENTICAL candidates and identical operational performance to the SOS-certified candidates, while the same-bank Nelder–Mead comparator is nearly equal. The new empirical improvement is relative to the final coarse-net Hedge distribution, not relative to equally informed conventional optimization. SOS adds a global empirical-objective certificate, not additional operational yield or a future-noise certificate.

All 27 frozen author files were hash-verified. The four main programs were replayed in a copied tree, preserving author originals. The new operation uses 155 one-qubit service slots to transmit 35 logical qubits through the received GF32 outer code, correcting up to six faulty symbols. At most six bad native slots affect at most six symbols. The guard aborts on the first validation failure and accepts the 155th service, including on its last allowed call, with a 1400-call cap. This is a single attempt, not repeated use of a lost payload.

## Finite guard and independent renewal check
The replayed finite DP tracks the full 2×2 bath, service count, and bad-count buckets 0…6/overflow. It conserves total acceptance/validation-abort/cap mass. It is not a binomial iid replacement.

A separate independent check sums every possible run of passing tests before the next service using the memory-channel resolvent V=(I−(1−s)P_pass)^−1. The next-service maps are sG V and sB V. Iterating these maps 155 times gives infinite-cap acceptance and accepted-overflow mass; summing Tr(V bath_j) gives expected calls. The finite-cap discrepancy is bounded by its remaining live mass (and a finite remaining-services factor for call cost). This non-scalar memory-channel calculation agrees for all eight policy/test combinations: maximum acceptance difference 1.18e-14 and expected-call difference 3.67e-12. Cap mass is negligible in this specified fixture; no general cap-negligibility assertion follows.

The reproduced SOS/SLSQP six-state acceptance is 0.3508147863 with 239.3421983 expected source calls, versus Hedge 0.0109274792 and 83.6291252 calls. Expected accepted-payload/call ratios are 0.0513010978 and 0.0045733083. Nelder–Mead achieves 0.0508876121. Matching is cheaper per test but lower yielding here. These are floating evaluations of a declared source model, not measured or directed-rounded confidence guarantees.

At R=1, no validation failures permitted and correction radius6, the inherited guard gives accepted-subnormalized error bound (1/2)^7=1/128. The smaller computed accepted-overflow mass is a model diagnostic, not a universal certificate. Abort is allowed to lose the unknown payload; there is no retry of the same unknown state.

Each of the 155-slot ledgers was converted into the received GF32 bit/phase convention; the corrected use of dx[:s] for the pre-Fourier phase syndrome is consistent with the received decoder. These samples are not 300 consecutive deployed attempts with a free bath reset, and they do not simulate a dense 155-qubit state. All 310 binary Pauli labels were checked under encoder followed by inverse. The four-spin encoder/inverse costs eight CNOT each; matching preparation/readout costs four. The canonical decoder also requires ancilla readout and conditional logical reset. The 155-qubit protected buffer and active source/test qubits are explicit.

## Required accepted-payload versus conditional-quality qualification
The tables count **accepted payload**, not exact logically successful payload. The universal 1/128 guarantee is subnormalized. Dividing by a very small acceptance probability can make its conditional guarantee weak or vacuous; a tiny accepted-payload ratio is not itself evidence of useful conditional recovery. For SOS-six, the model accepted-overflow mass is about 3.5861e-5 and acceptance0.350815, giving a conservative model-conditional overflow bound about1.02e-4. For Hedge-six the corresponding ratio is about0.177; uniform-net overflow is essentially all its tiny accepted mass. These are model-specific *upper bounds* on uncorrectable-event probability, not exact logical failure probabilities: some trajectories with more than six native faults can still correct. The 300 sampled zero-residual accepts do not replace either universal or model-tail bounds. No table entry should be relabeled exact successful/usable throughput without an explicit quality criterion and its proof.
