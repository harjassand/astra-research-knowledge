# Quantitative finite passive reset after a mediator fault

Model-specific addendum, 31 October 2026.
Code: finite_reset_bound.py. Results: finite_reset_results.json.

## 1. Scope and result

Keep the existing reciprocal network with g=1, Gamma=4, chi=2 and 0<=k_b,k_c<=0.0001, including the adopted k_b=k_c=0.0001 model. Turn both longitudinal forces OFF. Leave the dispersive chi Z1 n_c interaction ON and all three outlets coupled to COLD Markov baths. The data may be arbitrarily entangled with the three apparatus modes and with an external reference. Z1 must remain fixed as an operator during the cooldown: its two eigenvalue sectors may remain coherent, but there are no further X1 faults or noncommuting controls during this interval.

No ideal reset operation is introduced. Under these assumptions the actual passive dynamics gives

    ||E_±(t)||_2^2 <= (0.145)^floor(t/4),

where t is measured in units 1/g. This envelope is proved by a finite exact-arithmetic certificate described below.

If initial mean total apparatus occupation is bounded by E_max, then

    P_nonvac(t) <= min[1, E_max (0.145)^floor(t/4)].

For the SAME actual post-cooling data/reference marginal rho_RQ(t), the joint state obeys

    D[rho_RQM(t), rho_RQ(t) tensor |vac><vac|]
       <= min[1, sqrt(p)+p/2],

where p is the nonvacuum-probability bound and D=||.||_1/2.

For an optimized-pulse energy bound E_max=240, cooldown t=40 proves distance below 0.001; t=72 proves distance below 0.000001. The data dephasing caused by cooling is retained in rho_RQ(t), not silently removed by this reset approximation.

## 2. Exact linear dynamics and decay rate

For a fixed z=±1, define the zero-storage-loss reference matrix

    A_0,z = [[0,0,-i],
           [0,-2iz,-i],
           [-i,-i,-2]],

and the actual matrix

    A_z = A_0,z - diag(k_b/2,k_c/2,0).

Then E_z(t)=exp(A_z t). The two signs have identical singular values:

    A_- = D A_+* D,      D=diag(1,1,-1).

For the adopted k_b=k_c=0.0001 model, the numerical eigenvalues of A_+ are

    -1.219323169 +0.652419262 i,
    -0.514126183 -0.356967035 i,
    -0.266650648 -2.295452227 i.

Thus the adopted amplitude decay gap is 0.266650648 g and the asymptotic energy decay rate is 0.533301295 g. For reference, the k_b=k_c=0 amplitude gap is 0.266602757 g. An eigenvalue gap alone is insufficient for a finite-time singular-norm bound because A is nonnormal.

Direct matrix-exponential values for the ADOPTED model are:

The first short interval is a poor reset because energy can initially reside away from the damped mode. A large-Gamma intuition alone misses this finite propagation delay.

### Exact certificate for the simple envelope

The script realifies the 3x3 complex A_0 as a 6x6 real integer matrix. It evaluates the order-60 Taylor polynomial of exp(4A_0) using integer numerators and denominator 60!. Sylvester's criterion, with six strictly positive integer leading principal minors, proves the tightened bound

    ||Taylor_60(4A_0)||_2^2 < 0.1448.

Since ||4A_0||_2 <= 4||A_0||_F =4sqrt(12)<14, the Taylor remainder is bounded by

    r <= [14^61/61!] / [1-14/62]
       < 2.087e-14.

To transfer the certificate to the adopted storage losses, write Delta A=-diag(k_b/2,k_c/2,0). Both A_0 and A generate contractions because their Hermitian parts are negative semidefinite. Duhamel's formula gives

    ||exp(4A)-exp(4A_0)||
      <= integral_0^4 ||exp[(4-s)A]|| ||Delta A|| ||exp(sA_0)|| ds
      <= 4 max(k_b,k_c)/2 <= 0.0002.

Putting r_total=r+0.0002, the entirely rational inequality

    0.1448+2(0.381)r_total+r_total²
      < 0.144952440000016 <0.145

proves ||E(4)||²<0.145 for EVERY 0<=k_b,k_c<=0.0001. The semigroup property and contractivity then give the stated (0.145)^floor(t/4) bound, for both Z1 signs. Thus the certified cooldown times genuinely apply to the adopted model; no monotonicity assumption about adding damping was used. This certificate covers the stated storage-loss interval, not arbitrary calibration uncertainty in g,Gamma,chi or the delivered controls.

For the ZERO-STORAGE-LOSS reference model only, an entirely algebraic, looser continuous envelope is also available. The matrix

    P = [[7/4, i/2, 1/4-i/2],
         [-i/2, 9/4, 1/4-i/2],
         [1/4+i/2, 1/4+i/2, 3/4]]

satisfies A_0†P+PA_0=-I and (1/4)I<P<3I. Its Lyapunov inequality gives

    ||E(t)||² <= min[1,12 exp(-t/3)].

This reference-only Lyapunov envelope is not being transferred to the lossy model. The Duhamel-certified block envelope is the adopted reset bound.

## 3. Energy and joint decoupling proofs

For vacuum input, the normal-ordered one-particle density matrix evolves as

    C_z(t)=E_z(t) C_z(0) E_z†(t).

This identity holds for arbitrary initial bosonic states, not just coherent or Gaussian ones. Tracing the qubits removes their off-diagonal Z1 blocks when calculating total occupation. Consequently,

    <N_total(t)> <= max_z ||E_z(t)||² <N_total(0)>.

As I-|vac><vac| <= N_total,

    P_nonvac(t) <= <N_total(t)>.

This proves the energy/probability bound even when the apparatus is entangled with data and a reference.

For the trace-distance statement, write P0=I_RQ tensor |vac><vac| and p=Tr[(I-P0)rho]. The gentle-projection inequality gives

    (1/2)||rho-P0 rho P0||_1 <= sqrt(p).

The difference between rho_RQ tensor |vac><vac| and P0 rho P0 is positive with trace p, because partial trace over the apparatus kills vacuum/nonvacuum cross terms. A triangle inequality therefore yields sqrt(p)+p/2.

The simpler sqrt(p) bound DOES hold relative to the normalized vacuum-postselected data state. It need not hold relative to the SAME actual data marginal. For example, the pure joint state sqrt(1-p)|0,0>+sqrt(p)|1,1> has distance

    [p+sqrt(4p-3p²)]/2

from its actual data marginal tensored with vacuum, which exceeds sqrt(p) at small positive p. The bound used in this addendum therefore deliberately includes the extra p/2.

To guarantee joint distance <=delta, it suffices to require

    p <= p_delta = [sqrt(1+2delta)-1]².

The certified cooldown is

    t_cool = 4 ceil[log(p_delta/E_max)/log(0.145)]/g,

with zero cooldown if E_max<=p_delta.

## 4. Initial-energy budgets: distinguish sampled from uniform

The existing root/mediator_fault_results.json, whose stored model has k_b=k_c=0, has a largest residual energy of 0.807680168 photons among its TEN SAMPLED single-X faults, attained for X2 at T/2. This is a useful conditional numerical example, not a uniform certificate over all fault times, fault types, or the adopted lossy gate. The optimized adopted model uses the independent E_max=240 action-cap bound below.

A uniform bounded-control estimate needs no new fault-time scan. With vacuum initial modes and number-preserving passive terms,

    d sqrt(<N>)/dt <= sqrt(u²+w²),

so

    <N(T)> <= [integral_0^T sqrt(u²+w²)dt]²
             <= T integral_0^T (u²+w²)dt.

Qubit-only Pauli faults commute with apparatus occupation and do not invalidate this bound along a specified Pauli trajectory. It does not cover an apparatus fault that injects unbounded energy, and conditioning on a rare general nonunitary event can require a separate conditional energy bound.

Three distinct energy budgets are available:

* Original sin^6 pulse: numerical squared-drive action 19.20863446 at T=8, giving the numerical energy estimate 153.6690757. An ENFORCED action cap of 20 yields E_max=160. This budget must not be used for the optimized pulse.
* Stored optimized pulse: action_u=3.512284354 and action_w=24.114210810, total 27.626495164. An ENFORCED action cap of 30 at T=8 yields E_max=240.
* Explicit hard amplitude caps |u(t)|<=3 and |w(t)|<=3 for T=8 imply integral(u²+w²)dt<=144 and E_max<=1152. This is looser but depends only on directly enforceable amplitude and duration caps.

The optimized values were read from conventional_routes/finite_mediator_optimization_results.json, optimized[3]. Their quoted sampled peak magnitudes are about 2.36487 and 2.99990. To elevate the hard-cap bound from a numerical sample to a device specification, the actual delivered envelopes must obey the cap.

## 6. What this reset does and does not repair

The cooling output may acquire information from the already-faulty, entangled apparatus. The reset bound compares to the ACTUAL data marginal after that evolution, so it does not claim to reverse the data error. With the forces off, the cooling Hamiltonian and jump are diagonal in the data variables. A dedicated module therefore keeps cleanup noise in the same pair's diagonal operator algebra, rather than coupling the residual apparatus to a new data location. This is the appropriate interpretation for the proposed gauge-location treatment.

Keeping the module dedicated to its pair is material. Routing a hot module to another pair before the reset error is small can spread the original fault. Likewise, physically changing Z1 with noncommuting gates during the cooldown invalidates the fixed-sign contraction proof. Parallel operations can only be assumed when their commutation or an extended contraction analysis justifies them.

Additional data faults during the cooldown have not been eliminated. For independent Pauli-jump rates gamma_X1 and gamma_X2, the probability of at least one extra such fault during an interval t_cool is

    1-exp[-(gamma_X1+gamma_X2)t_cool].

This additional storage exposure must be charged to the full cycle. For generic faults use an appropriate generator norm or explicit circuit noise model rather than treating every channel as an input-independent classical mixture.

If a reset can be triggered only after a reliable fault/energy flag, its wall-clock cost may be probability weighted. The current random gauge/parity outcome is not automatically such a flag. Without a demonstrated flag or a justified architecture that confines residual memory, the serial reset time must be charged on every reuse.

## 7. Warm bath and unbounded-fault caveats

The cold baths are essential to the certificate. The following exact expression is restricted to the ZERO-STORAGE-LOSS case k_b=k_c=0, with the sole m bath at flat Markov occupation n_in:

    C_z(t)=E_z C_z(0)E_z† + n_in[I-E_z E_z†],

giving the simple bound

    <N(t)> <= ||E_z(t)||² E_max + 3 n_in.

In that reference model, the unique passive steady state has mean n_in in all three modes and vacuum probability (1+n_in)^(-3). Thus its apparatus-only trace distance from vacuum is

    1-(1+n_in)^(-3),

approximately 3n_in. For n_in=0.001 this reference floor is about 0.002994, already above a 0.001 vacuum-reset target.

With the ADOPTED k_b=k_c=0.0001 and vacuum b,c baths, n_in I is no longer the exact stationary covariance. Instead the covariance solves

    A C_inf + C_inf A† = -Gamma n_in |m><m|,

and the Gaussian stationary vacuum probability is 1/det(I+C_inf). A separate 3x3 numerical Lyapunov calculation gives Tr(C_inf)=2.99960006249 n_in and, at n_in=0.001, P_nonvac=0.00299361164. This warm-bath calculation is explicitly separate from the exact cold-bath reset certificate. Waiting longer cannot remove this warm steady-state floor and also exposes the data to ongoing thermal dephasing. The simple mean-energy upper bound ||E||²E_max+3n_in remains conservative when the added b,c ports are vacuum, but its equality formula from the zero-storage-loss case must not be reused.

No finite cooldown can guarantee a uniform error bound against an initial state with unbounded mean energy or against an arbitrary heating fault during reset. Nor does the fixed-sign proof cover adversarial rapid Z1 switching: switching can alter the effective bright/dark-mode structure. These are explicit boundaries of the finite-reset theorem, not hidden ideal reset assumptions.
