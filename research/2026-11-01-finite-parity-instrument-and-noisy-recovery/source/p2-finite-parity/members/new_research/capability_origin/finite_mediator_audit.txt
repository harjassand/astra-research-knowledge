# Independent audit of the exact finite three-mode parity mediator

31 October 2026. Target inspected: new_research/root/finite_mediator_parity.py.
The full Fock/Lindblad cross-density check is owned by finite_operation; this audit covers physical controls, initialization, thermal baths, resources and prior art.

## Verdict

The advertised coherent trajectories solve the explicit finite reciprocal network exactly. The output cancellation is not an adiabatic-elimination artifact. Under static Z1,Z2, vacuum modes and vacuum baths, calibrated controls, and no b/c loss, it is a legitimate exact full-parity instrument whose sole damped outlet carries only Z1 Z2. This is a useful finite construction.

The most important qualifications are substantive:

1. The apparatus has a thermal-preparation error floor. Smooth return of the COHERENT displacement does not return an unknown thermal initial state to a branch-independent vacuum.
2. A warm input bath produces an additional effect not covered by an initial thermal-mixture bound.
3. The two independently shaped Z2-dependent forces are real quantum interactions to engineer, not ordinary classical drive channels.
4. Exact cancellation for static Z values does not establish transparency to noncommuting data faults. Root's separate fault stress test already shows severe conditional errors.
5. Derivative pulse inversion, exact transient cancellation and terminal cavity emptying have strong prior art. The candidate novelty is the specific reciprocal network/control structure giving full parity equality before loss, not inverse engineering itself.
6. A matched-qutrit, flagged-retry parity measurement is a serious and apparently simpler conventional baseline. No optimized full-resource superiority has yet been shown.

## 1. Independent equation check

The Hamiltonian is

    H = g[(b†+c†)m + h.c.] + chi Z1 c†c
        +u(t) Z2(b+b†)+w(t) Z2(c+c†),

with damped outlets sqrt(Gamma)m, sqrt(k_b)b, sqrt(k_c)c.

For z1,z2 in {+1,-1}, let s=z1 z2. The proposed amplitudes are

    beta_b = s U + i z2 B,
    beta_c = s V - i z2 B,
    beta_m = i s A/sqrt(Gamma),

where U,V,B,A are real. Direct substitution gives

    A = sqrt(Gamma)/g [Udot+(k_b/2)U],
    V = -[Adot+(Gamma/2)A]/[g sqrt(Gamma)] - U,
    B = [-(k_c/2)V+g A/sqrt(Gamma)-Vdot]/chi,
    u = -Bdot-(k_b/2)B,
    w = Bdot+(k_c/2)B-chi V.

These are precisely the implemented relations. In particular,

    sqrt(Gamma) beta_m(t) = i s A(t)

at every time, in both parity sectors, for all finite positive g,Gamma,chi for which the drives are realizable. The unwanted local Z2 components cancel before the m output is emitted.

Choosing U proportional to sin^6(pi t/T) makes U through its fifth derivative vanish as needed at the boundaries. The drive expressions contain up to its fourth derivative, so both drives begin and end at zero. All coherent internal amplitudes close exactly. The independent finite_operation agent reports a converged all-coherence Fock-space check, so no duplicate large simulation was needed here.

For k_b,k_c>0, the individual b,c bath fields retain +/-i z2 B. The leading unobserved same-parity decoherence is consequently real, with exponent 2(k_b+k_c) integral B² dt. Recalibrating the drives can preserve parity purity in m while leaving this distinct intrinsic-loss cost. It cannot erase photons already lost separately from b or c.

## 2. Exact thermal-initialization calculation

### 2.1 Why coherent return is insufficient

At k_b=k_c=0, same-parity branches have the same complete coherent displacement: all internal modes end at zero and the entire emitted coherent wavepacket is identical. Their passive linear transformations nevertheless differ because mode c has frequency +chi or -chi. An initially occupied mode undergoes different scattering in these two branches. Unknown thermal excitations therefore induce dephasing even though the engineered signal is exactly parity-only.

Let

    A_± = -i h_± - (Gamma/2)|m><m|,
    h_± = [[0,0,g],[0,±chi,g],[g,g,0]],
    E_±(t) = exp(A_± t).

The one-particle isometry into the final internal modes plus all output temporal modes has columns S_±. Its cross overlap on the three initial mode coordinates is

    M(T) = S_-† S_+
         = E_-†(T)E_+(T)
           + integral_0^T E_-†(t) Gamma|m><m| E_+(t) dt.

Keeping the integral is essential: comparing only final cavity states misses the information emitted while they cool.

For an initial product of three thermal states with mean occupation matrix

    N_th = diag(n_b,n_c,n_m),

and vacuum input bath, the exact same-parity coherence factor after the deterministic vacuum phase correction is

    Lambda_th = 1/det[I + N_th(I-M)].

Derivation: the relative global passive transformation is U_-† U_+. Its vacuum-output compression is the bosonic second quantization of M. Summing its diagonal Fock elements against geometric thermal weights gives det(I-Q)/det(I-QM), with Q_j=n_j/(1+n_j), equal to the expression above. The common coherent displacement cancels before this trace, so Lambda_th does not depend on the chosen measurement-signal photon number.

An additional deterministic phase arg(Lambda_th) could be calibrated if the temperatures are known. After that compensation, the dephasing probability is

    p_Z,th = [1-|Lambda_th|]/2.

For root's g=1,Gamma=4,chi=2,T=8:

| Thermal initial modes | Mean occupation in each populated mode | Same-parity p_Z after phase compensation |
|---|---:|---:|
| b,c,m | 0.001 | 0.000671800 |
| b,c | 0.001 | 0.000584783 |
| m only | 0.001 | 0.0000871693 |
| b,c,m | 0.01 | 0.00665012 |

For all three modes at nbar=0.001, increasing T to 12 or 16 leaves p_Z approximately 0.000672. Waiting longer does not recover information that has already entered the output.

The calculation is independently implemented in audit_thermal_mediator.py and thermal_mediator_results.json. It uses a 3x3 single-particle ODE, includes the full output, and checks the determinant formula against the exact geometric Fock sum for each singly thermal input mode (residual 2.23e-16). This is not a finite-Fock cutoff approximation.

The result describes the raw corrected quantum channel with the vacuum phase correction, or its channel after additionally compensating the known thermal phase. It does not analyze additional continuous-record-dependent thermal-noise recovery. Such recovery would be another explicitly charged resource, not part of the current binary-sign instrument.

### 2.2 Conservative initial-mixture bound for a larger operation

For M independent initial thermal modes of equal nbar,

    p_nonvacuum = 1-(1+nbar)^(-M).

The input state is exactly a convex mixture of vacuum and a nonvacuum state with those weights. If the vacuum branch realizes the proven channel and nothing is assumed about the other branch, the half-diamond channel distance is at most p_nonvacuum, before adding the vacuum-branch imperfections. This is a legitimate conservative bound, independent of the unknown data input.

For six fresh modes at nbar=0.001 it is approximately 0.00598. It is not the calculated error of the thermal channel, and it does not include ongoing heating or correlations between reused modes.

### 2.3 A warm bath is a separate problem

If the m input line has thermal occupancy n_in, the Lindbladian is

    Gamma(1+n_in)D[m] + Gamma n_in D[m†].

With initially vacuum internal modes, its first-order contribution to same-parity coherence can be evaluated without a Fock truncation. In the undriven equivalent passive problem,

    D[m†]|vac><vac| = |1_m><1_m| - |vac><vac|.

A heating insertion at time tau therefore contributes M_mm(T-tau)-1. Hence

    Lambda_in = 1 - Gamma n_in integral_0^T [1-M_mm(s)] ds
                +O(n_in²),

and

    p_Z,in = (Gamma n_in/2) integral_0^T[1-Re M_mm(s)]ds
              +O(n_in²).

This independence from the coherent-drive amplitude follows from the same equal-displacement cancellation as above, and applies to the ideal sole-output model.

Numerical coefficients multiplying n_in are 1.12776 at T=4, 2.50686 at T=8, 3.88578 at T=12, and 5.26515 at T=16 for g=1,Gamma=4,chi=2. At T=8 and n_in=0.001, the leading raw same-parity dephasing is about 0.002507 per check. The input thermal bath also changes measurement noise; that assignment-error change has not been calculated here.

A more conservative heating-event count is n_in[Gamma T+N_signal] at leading order in the driven picture. For T=8,Gamma=4,N_signal=4.7748,n_in=0.001 it is 0.0368. This is not the actual logical dephasing and should not replace the more specific calculation. It does, however, show why an initial-state thermal-mixture bound alone is incomplete.

## 3. Preparation and reset protocol must be physical

Cooling while an unknown data qubit remains coupled through chi Z1 n_c can itself scatter thermal excitations in a qubit-dependent way. It cannot be called harmless initialization. A valid one-off channel experiment can cool first, then prepare/load the unknown data input. Scalable repeated use needs switchable coupling or a separately protected reset protocol.

There is also a dark-mode detail: if chi is switched off for safe cooling while the equal-g network is kept otherwise unchanged, (b-c)/sqrt(2) decouples from m. A sole m outlet cannot cool this dark mode. A qubit-independent detuning or mixing, sequential coupling to m, or extra gated reset ports must be added during initialization. The resources include their control lines, additional dwell time, residual occupations and isolation of the data.

The ideal pulse returns the coherent signal to vacuum and therefore eliminates a coherent ring-down wait in the already-cold model. It does not by itself reset initial thermal entropy, remove thermal photons entering during the operation, or guarantee harmless cleanup after a data fault that leaves residual cavity population.

## 4. Physical longitudinal forces

Parametrically modulated longitudinal interactions of the form Z(a+a†) are established proposals [A] and conditional displacement has been experimentally demonstrated [B]. The interaction itself is physically credible. Root's construction needs TWO independently calibrated envelopes, u(t) and w(t), acting coherently on the SAME data Z2 in distinct modes, while keeping the Z1 n_c dispersive interaction and the b-m,c-m beam-splitter couplings under control.

A route uses two tunable longitudinal couplers or two resolved pump channels, with phase/amplitude shaping at each mode frequency. Number-conserving intermode coupling may itself require parametric pumps when the bare mode frequencies differ. All pumps have bandwidth, crosstalk, Stark shifts and induced dissipative channels. Those are device-design requirements, not a mathematical obstruction.

An ordinary dispersive interaction plus an ordinary cavity drive is NOT automatically the assumed resource. Displacing chi Z2 a†a by alpha(t) produces the desired linear force chi Z2(alpha a†+alpha* a), but leaves chi Z2 a†a and the Stark term chi |alpha|² Z2. The residual Z2-dependent oscillator frequency changes the branch transfer functions and invalidates the stated waveforms unless it is canceled or incorporated into a new design. Echoing it costs pulses and adds its own noncommuting-error analysis.

At root's illustrative Gamma=4,g=1,chi=2, eta=0.5, assignment error 0.001, the signal has N=4.77477 photons. The current sin^6 examples require:

| T | peak u magnitude | peak w magnitude | peak n_b | peak n_c | integral(n_b+n_c+n_m) dt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 13.9817 | 8.51269 | 21.9750 | 21.4601 | 45.3420 |
| 8 | 1.75798 | 2.91206 | 2.44979 | 4.62178 | 14.3743 |
| 12 | 0.682229 | 3.33510 | 2.31835 | 3.92049 | 17.7620 |
| 16 | 0.398720 | 3.62790 | 2.77620 | 4.00695 | 25.4417 |

These are dimensionless examples from the stated pulse, not optimized lower bounds. They show why arbitrarily short mathematical pulses are not free: derivatives produce large peak conditional forces and intracavity energy. The independently optimized pulse family being developed by conventional_routes is the proper next comparison.

Known static parameter changes can be incorporated into inverse control. Unmeasured drift, relative pump phase noise, imperfect coupling equality, parasitic nonlinearities and qubit-state-dependent loss must instead appear in a robustness budget. A first-order amplitude mismatch produces a second-order distinguishability exponent, but the prefactor scales with the pulse energy and duration and may be large.

## 5. Strong lower-component-count comparator: matched qutrit

The established error-transparent/path-independent ancilla approach is not excluded by the capability. A concrete ideal parity-check model is

    H_q = chi(Z1+Z2)(|e><e|+|f><f|),

with the ancilla prepared in (|g>+|f>)/sqrt(2) and T_q=pi/(2chi). The conditional e/f data unitary is

    exp[-i chi T_q(Z1+Z2)] = -Z1 Z2.

Ramsey readout of g/f therefore measures parity. A dominant f->e relaxation at any time leaves the same conditional coupling active, so the resulting data unitary is still the fixed endpoint -Z1Z2. The e flag can be used to retry. The emitted transition frequency is data independent when the e and f couplings match, which is the physical reason no unknown-time phase escapes into the bath.

The no-jump branch is also safe inside parity sectors. If f decays with rate gamma_f, the two accepted Ramsey Kraus operators are proportional to

    I +/- exp(-gamma_f T_q/2) Z1Z2.

They are functions only of parity. Ancilla dephasing changes the reported parity statistics but likewise does not distinguish states within a parity sector under this ideal model. The single-relaxation flag probability is approximately gamma_f T_q/2, and the mean attempt count is approximately 1/[1-p_flag] if retries are independent and recovery/reset is included.

This comparator uses one multilevel ancilla plus its readout resonator, two matched data couplings, matching drive(s), two Ramsey rotations, level-resolved readout, and reset. It is not costless: matching must hold for both data couplings; g->e heating is generally not transparent; direct f->g channels, a second decay e->g, finite-duration Ramsey pulses, ancilla leakage and readout/reset errors remain. Rosenblum et al. experimentally demonstrated the corresponding hardware-efficient matching principle in a cavity setting [C], and Ma et al. formalized broader path-independent control [D].

At the SAME abstract chi=2, T_q=pi/4 is shorter than the current T=8 mediator pulse by about a factor of ten before counting Ramsey/readout/reset and differences in achievable coupling. That is only a component-count and coherent-interaction comparison, not a full experimental speed/fidelity ranking. The mediator could still win when finite-efficiency continuous readout, reusable output temporal modes, or a particular platform makes the qutrit resources expensive; this requires actual device parameters and a complete cycle model.

## 6. Prior art and what remains a candidate result

Exact smooth transient cancellation is established. Motzoi, Whaley and Sarovar (2015) derive compensation pulses enforcing equality of selected cavity outputs at all times beyond adiabatic approximation [E]. Their scheme is explicitly half-parity: it preserves the |01>,|10> sector, while distinguishing |00> and |11>. Motzoi, Buchmann and Dickel (2018) give derivative/transfer-function pulse synthesis with exact empty final cavities, including Purcell-filter modes and cascaded entanglement readout [F]. Their demonstrated entanglement condition again equates selected outputs, not the two full parity sectors of root's construction.

Therefore “we inverse-engineered a smooth pulse with zero terminal amplitude” is not a new principle. The more specific candidate result is:

    A finite reciprocal passive three-mode network, controlled by two
    independent longitudinal Z2 forces and one dispersive Z1 interaction,
    has an exactly parity-only sole output during the entire finite pulse,
    with both parity sectors preserved before arbitrary output inefficiency.

That is a precise theorem worth testing and comparing. It has not yet been established as an experimentally advantageous architecture or as a major advance over the strong ancilla alternatives. Initial vacuum, cold input, negligible individual storage outlets, coherent controls and static data observables are part of its scope.
