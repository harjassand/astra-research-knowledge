RESULT AND STATUS
=================

The strongest construction in this continuation is a finite-duration,
finite-strength parity measurement whose entire intended output field carries
only Z1 Z2. It uses three finite harmonic modes, two ordinary reciprocal
beam-splitter couplings, one dispersive qubit-mode coupling, and two actual
longitudinal qubit-mode drives. All auxiliary modes return to vacuum at the
end of the same operation. No stationary spectral approximation, eliminated
receiver, infinitely fast feedback, or unobserved final apparatus is used.

For vacuum initial auxiliaries and vacuum reservoirs, the controlled coherent
state dilation is exact at every finite drive strength within this Hamiltonian
model. It proves preservation of arbitrary superpositions within BOTH parity
sectors, not only correct reported parity or equality of basis-state outlet
marginals. Finite detection efficiency affects assignment, but does not by
itself leak within-parity information through the main output.

Intrinsic auxiliary losses are retained exactly. Their unobserved part gives
an explicit error functional. Accessible losses can instead be corrected from
their photon records with only local Pauli/Z rotations. Their no-click rates
are independent of the logical state. The full binned measurement instrument
is composed into a CNOT with nonzero helper, local-gate and readout errors.

This is a mathematically complete operation in a stated effective model. It
is NOT a demonstrated lower-resource fault-tolerant computer or a validated
hardware proposal. Ordinary optimized control improves its first waveform.
Thermal auxiliary populations and noncommuting data faults expose important
remaining errors. No hardware advantage, new threshold, or historical
priority is established. The architecture changes an inherited operational
consequence; the mission-level cost advantage remains open.

A further exact CP decomposition admits the finite-strength instrument as an
input-independent mixture of an ideal gauge check and a localized faulty
check. This supplies a direct link to a concrete repeated-syndrome recovery
protocol, rather than an ideal final decoder. The recovery proof, preparation
and reuse conditions, and a finite passive cooldown cost are stated below.

1. INPUT/OUTPUT/RESOURCE CONTRACT
================================

Input: two physical qubits, possibly entangled with an arbitrary reference;
three initially vacuum modes b,c,m; calibrated real positive g,Gamma,chi and
known auxiliary loss rates kb,kc. Physical Z1,Z2 are Pauli observables on the
qubits. This is not automatically logical Z in the inherited coherent-sign
cat encoding. In particular, a cat's photon number is not its sign Pauli.

Controls: actual real functions u(t),w(t) multiplying Z2 times the position
quadratures of b and c. A local calibrated Z1 phase rotation is available.
The two coherent links g are ordinary reciprocal mode couplings. The model
requires the longitudinal, dispersive and passive-link effective Hamiltonians
to hold over the drive amplitudes, photon populations and frequency bandwidth
actually listed below. A virtual frame update is not a longitudinal force.

Output: both qubits, a binary homodyne parity record, optional auxiliary-port
photon records, and the final auxiliary state. The theorem includes every
emitted field. All three modes return to vacuum on the ideal constructed
trajectory, so the device can be reused without a separately assumed perfect
reset. Initial preparation, nonvacuum perturbations and extra ports are
explicit separate costs.

Noise admitted exactly in the main theorem: vacuum Markov damping Gamma of m
and vacuum Markov intrinsic losses kb,kc of b,c, including every jump and
no-jump history; imperfect main homodyne efficiency eta. Known kb,kc affect
the designed controls, rather than being added after a lossless calculation.
Commuting data Z errors can be propagated through the operation as ordinary

nonlinear oscillator terms and calibration errors are not removed by the
theorem and are separately analyzed.

2. PHYSICAL HAMILTONIAN AND THE INVERSE CONSTRUCTION
===================================================

Set hbar=1 and S=Z1 Z2. The Hamiltonian is

 H = g[(b^dag+c^dag)m + m^dag(b+c)] + chi Z1 c^dag c
     + u(t) Z2(b+b^dag) + w(t) Z2(c+c^dag).

The physical outlets are

 L_m=sqrt(Gamma)m,  L_b=sqrt(kb)b,  L_c=sqrt(kc)c.

For fixed computational basis labels z=(z1,z2), s=z1 z2, a passive linear
bosonic system driven from vacuum stays a coherent state in its complete
system-field dilation. The conditional amplitudes solve

 dot b_z = -ab b_z - i g m_z - i u z2,
 dot c_z = -(ac+i chi z1)c_z - i g m_z - i w z2,
 dot m_z = -Gamma m_z/2 - i g(b_z+c_z),

where ab=kb/2 and ac=kc/2.

Choose an arbitrary real sufficiently smooth function U(t) with U and its
first four derivatives zero at both endpoints. Define, in this order,

 A = (sqrt(Gamma)/g)(dot U+ab U),
 V = -(dot A+Gamma A/2)/(g sqrt(Gamma)) - U,
 B = [-ac V+g A/sqrt(Gamma)-dot V]/chi,
 u = -dot B-ab B,
 w = dot B+ac B-chi V.                                  (2.1)

Then the exact solution from vacuum is

 b_z = U s+i B z2,
 c_z = V s-i B z2,
 m_z = i A s/sqrt(Gamma).                               (2.2)

Proof by substitution. The S coefficient of the b equation is
dot U=-ab U+g A/sqrt(Gamma), which defines A. The m equation is equivalent
to U+V=-(dot A+Gamma A/2)/(g sqrt(Gamma)), which defines V. The S coefficient
of the c equation is dot V=-ac V+g A/sqrt(Gamma)-chi B, which defines B.
The Z2 coefficients of b,c are exactly the last two equations in (2.1).
These identities hold at every time, not only after integration over a pulse.
Initial conditions and uniqueness give (2.2).

Consequently

 sqrt(Gamma)m_z(t)=i A(t)s                              (2.3)

is parity-only for the full finite waveform. The individual-qubit component
iB Z2 resides in the opposite b,c amplitudes, so it cancels before reaching
the common damped mode. This is physical coherent cancellation at an outlet.
It is not cancellation of a recorded classical average.

The endpoint assumptions imply A=V=B=0 and u=w=0 at both endpoints. Thus all
three finite modes return to vacuum. At kb=0, A has zero time integral;
its bipolar shape is read with a matched homodyne filter. There is no loss
of signal merely because the ordinary time average is zero.

An explicit simple family is U=U0 sin^6(pi t/T). Its Fourier representation
is [10-15 cos(2x)+6 cos(4x)-cos(6x)]/32, x=pi t/T. This supplies closed
form derivatives and finite controls. The normalization U0 sets the desired
N=int A^2 dt. Nonzero known kb is included by the ab U term, so finite-rate
loss does not spoil the return condition.

3. THE FULL QUANTUM STATEMENT, NOT JUST POINTER TRAJECTORIES
==========================================================

For a passive driven bosonic Hamiltonian, the full dilation maps vacuum to a
coherent state, with classical trajectories as above. The scalar quantum
phase has derivative -Re(sum_k f_k^* alpha_k), where f are the drive
coefficients. Here

 phi_z(T) = phi z1,
 phi = -int_0^T [u U+w V]dt.                            (3.1)

All dependence on z2 in this phase cancels because every drive is proportional
to z2 and z2^2=1. The phase is a physical single-qubit Z1 rotation, which may
be implemented once at the end or calibrated into subsequent local controls.
It is not a supplied arbitrary diagonal two-qubit correction.

With kb=kc=0, the exact dilation at time T is therefore

 |z1,z2>|0,0,0>|vac>
    -> exp(i phi z1)|z1,z2>|0,0,0>|i s A>_out.           (3.2)

By linearity, this establishes the full arbitrary-superposition and arbitrary
reference statement. After correcting phi, all states in a fixed S sector
have the SAME field ket and final apparatus ket. The environment cannot
learn any additional quantum information in that sector. The earlier
equality-of-basis-marginals loophole is absent because (3.2) is an explicit
controlled isometry, not a symmetry argument about traced basis inputs.

With kb,kc nonzero, include the actual additional coherent fields

 beta_b,z=sqrt(kb)(U s+iB z2),
 beta_c,z=sqrt(kc)(V s-iB z2).                           (3.3)

Equations (2.1) already include their damping. They do not change the exact
main parity field but they do disturb within-sector coherence.

Define IU=int U^2, IV=int V^2, IB=int B^2,
CU=int UB, CV=int VB, and K=kc CV-kb CU. Coherent-state overlap gives, for
two labels z,z', the hidden-field factor

 exp[-(kb IU+kc IV)(s-s')^2/2
     -(kb+kc)IB(z2-z2')^2/2
     +i K(s z2'-s' z2)].                                (3.4)

Inside one parity sector, the phase in (3.4) is a local Z1 difference.
The additional calibrated local correction phi-K removes it. The exact
remaining coherence and equivalent binary phase error are

 V_same=exp[-2(kb+kc)IB],
 p_Z=[1-exp(-2(kb+kc)IB)]/2.                             (3.5)

Main output detector inefficiency is absent from (3.5), because every photon
in that output has only the allowed parity dependence. It does appear in
the number of signal photons required for a reliable reported bit.

4. COMPLETE HOMODYNE INSTRUMENT
==============================

Use the main-output quadrature aligned with iA and its matched temporal
filter. Write N=int A^2dt. The normalized scalar record x has variance one
and mean 2s sqrt(eta N). Therefore the symmetric assignment error is

 q=Phi(-2 sqrt(eta N)),
 N=[Phi^{-1}(q)]^2/(4 eta).                              (4.1)

The binary threshold is x>=0 versus x<0. Crucially, the quantum operation
is not replaced by a projective measurement plus a wrong-bit probability.
For reported s_r=+1 or -1, the exact Schur multiplier on |z><z'|, after
the local correction phi-K, is

 G_{s_r}(z,z')=
 Phi[s_r(s+s')sqrt(eta N)]
 * exp[-(N+kb IU+kc IV)(s-s')^2/2
       -(kb+kc)IB(z2-z2')^2/2
       +i K{(z1-z1')+s z2'-s' z2}].                    (4.2)

This retains the continuous-record integration, missed main photons,
unobserved auxiliary emissions, no-click evolution, residual cross-parity
coherence, and all deterministic phases. Sum_s_r G has unit diagonal; each
G is positive semidefinite because it comes from an actual dilation and
measurement. The implementation checks these properties independently.

No thermal input or noncommuting data fault has been silently added to (4.2).

4.1 Exact admission to a noisy circuit, without a projective approximation.

Let Pi_+,Pi_- be the parity projectors, gamma=exp(-2N)/2, and q>0 the
actual symmetric report error. The main instrument has parity coefficient
matrix [[1-q,gamma],[gamma,q]] for report+, with diagonals interchanged for
report-. Physical positivity implies gamma^2<=q(1-q). Define

 e=q+gamma^2/q,  p=1-e,
 Kbad_+=(gamma/sqrt(q))Pi_+ +sqrt(q)Pi_-,
 Kbad_-=sqrt(q)Pi_+ +(gamma/sqrt(q))Pi_-.

Direct expansion proves the exact identity

 M0_r(rho)=p Pi_r rho Pi_r+Kbad_r rho Kbad_r^dag,
 sum_r Kbad_r^dag Kbad_r=e I.                           (4.3)

Thus the faulty branch is a normalized instrument with an input-independent
weight e. The residual parity matrix is positive and rank one. This is also
the largest uniform coefficient p of the ideal instrument that can be
subtracted in this two-term form; its determinant forces this bound.

The physical auxiliary channel Lambda commutes with the main instrument.
After the calibrated local phase, Lambda acting on an ideal parity projection
is exactly local Z2 dephasing with probability pZ from (3.5). Therefore

 M=p(1-pZ) P +p pZ(Ad_Z2 o P)+e(Lambda o B),             (4.4)
 e_total=1-p(1-pZ)=e+pZ-e pZ,

where P is the ideal correctly reported parity instrument and B is the
normalized bad main instrument. Every residual Kraus operator lies in
Alg(Z1,Z2). This is an exact completely positive mixture for the admitted
finite device, including its no-jump terms and cross-parity coherence. Its
weight is not an entanglement infidelity relabeled as a failure probability.

For the optimized waveform below, e=.00100126858272,
pZ=.000307090843994 and e_total=.00130805194630. Both actual four-dimensional
outcome Gram matrices pass the subtraction test, retaining every auxiliary

Independent location mixtures require fresh vacuum outputs at each use and

5. ACCESSIBLE AUXILIARY LOSS: CORRECTABLE UNITARIES, NOT FILTERS
==============================================================

For real U,V,B,

 U S+iB Z2 = sqrt(U^2+B^2) S exp[i atan2(B,U) Z1],
 V S-iB Z2 = sqrt(V^2+B^2) S exp[-i atan2(B,V) Z1].        (5.1)

Thus a resolved photon from either auxiliary port applies a scalar times a
product of local unitaries. Its instantaneous count rate, respectively
kb(U^2+B^2) or kc(V^2+B^2), is independent of the logical state. In this
counting unraveling, silence has no additional state-dependent logical
filtering. Multiple recorded emissions compose because all these diagonal
corrections commute. Their local phase angles can be accumulated and applied
once at the endpoint; instantaneous quantum feedback is unnecessary.

If only fractions eta_b,eta_c of these ports are physically collected and
counted, then the corrected unseen error uses

 D_unseen=[(1-eta_b)kb+(1-eta_c)kc] IB,
 V_same=exp(-2D_unseen).                                (5.2)

Material absorption is not assumed measurable. Detector false counts,
missed counts, finite time resolution and local correction errors must be
charged. If a registered time has error delta t, the applied local angle
differs by theta(t+delta t)-theta(t); the real waveforms determine that cost.
When the photon rate is zero, its formally undefined phase angle has no
event weight. The main detector remains an ordinary inefficient homodyne
detector; it is distinct from these optional counters.

9. FAILURES THAT REMAIN PHYSICALLY IMPORTANT
==========================================

9.1 Noncommuting data faults.

The prescribed cancellation assumes fixed Z labels during a pulse. An X/Y
fault changes a label while b,c,m still hold its earlier history. Replacing
that event by an ideal Pauli after a completed measurement is wrong.

The actual three-mode ODE is solved with one physical X inserted at different
times, for every logical branch, retaining emitted and terminal modes. At
the simple lossless illustrative pulse an X2 at T/4 gives near-complete
within-sector loss of visibility: equivalent pZ=.499999989 when the record
is discarded. Even only the inaccessible half of the main output yields
pZ=.499920032. An X1 at T/4 yields .499315337 and .477908831 respectively.

These conditional-on-a-fault numbers are not the unconditional gate error
and do not prove impossibility of an optimal syndrome/history decoder.
They do prove that the apparatus is not transparent to such data faults.
Every noncommuting fault probability and any correlated propagation must be
charged. For independent stochastic X-jump rates, an exact conservative
channel bound is the probability of at least one such event during the
full exposed interval. General T1/no-jump data noise requires its actual
channel or a norm/rate envelope, not the same stochastic-X mixture formula.

This also explains why the construction does not refute the recent
obstructions to continuous error correction with common finite-response
parity meters: those require protecting transitions between error sectors.
Here the finite closed operation is exact for fixed parity eigenbasis
histories, while a physical transition can still reveal forbidden data.

9.3 Thermal preparation.

Vacuum is a real resource. Equal thermal environment marginals do not imply
preservation of arbitrary logical superpositions. An initial thermal photon
can scatter differently under the two dispersive signs even when the
engineered coherent displacement is identical.

Let E_+(t),E_-(t) be the finite passive single-particle amplitude propagators
for Z1=+/-1, and C the actual outlet matrix. The overlap of their full
single-particle isometries is

 M=E_-^dag(T)E_+(T)+int E_-^dag(t)C^dag C E_+(t)dt.

For independent thermal initial occupations Nth=diag(n_b,n_c,n_m), vacuum
incoming fields and no coherent mismatch, the same-parity thermal overlap is

 Lambda=1/det[I+Nth(I-M)].                              (9.1)

This follows by tracing the passive bosonic transformation against the
geometric thermal distribution. It is separately checked against a converged
single-thermal-mode geometric series. It includes final oscillator and
emitted-field distinctions.

At g1,Gamma4,chi2,T8, equal initial nbar=.001 in all3 modes gives
pZ=(1-|Lambda|)/2 about .0006718, even with kb=kc=0. With nbar=.01 it is
about .00665. Heating b,c only at .001 still gives about .0005848.
The smooth long pulse does not remove this floor. These are initial thermal
populations, not a model of ongoing thermal input.

An independent first-order warm-input calculation is also preserved. For
constant occupation n_in in the main incoming bath, the extra same-parity
phase-error coefficient is

 (Gamma n_in/2) int_0^T [1-Re M_mm(s)]ds + O(n_in^2),

where M_mm(s) is the full emitted-plus-retained overlap caused by a single
initial photon in mode m over the remaining interval s. At the stated g1,
Gamma4,chi2,T8 parameters, the coefficient multiplying n_in is about2.50686.
Thus n_in=.001 gives approximately .002507 per check at first order. This
is not a complete finite-temperature binned-instrument simulation, and its
higher-order remainder has not been bounded. A crude heating-event count
n_in(Gamma T+N) is about .0368 there; it should not be confused with that
actual leading coherence error. The output bath must be physically cold,
not merely represented by an empty measurement-record buffer.

A conservative complete-instrument preparation bound is also available.
For three independent thermal modes, P(vac)=(1+nbar)^-3. The actual channel
is exactly P(vac) times the vacuum channel plus a valid channel with the
remaining weight. Thus half-diamond error from initial preparation is at

This is a separate conservative envelope, not an added thermal simulation
in the CNOT table.

Initial cooling should occur before unknown logical data are loaded, or
with data truly decoupled. Cooling an already entangled receiver can itself
emit logical information. A thermal floor, finite cooling time and the
physical ability to turn couplings off must be qualified. Exact vacuum
return in the admitted operation is earned; perfect physical initial vacuum
or perfect finite reset under arbitrary errors is not.

In particular, if chi is switched OFF during reset while both identical g
couplings remain on, (b-c)/sqrt(2) is a dark mode of the sole m reservoir.
Cooling through m alone then fails to prepare all three modes. One may
prepare them before unknown data are loaded while Z1 is in a known state
and chi remains on (the passive network then has no exact undamped dark
eigenmode), or supply qubit-independent detuning/mixing or gated reset
ports. Each choice requires its actual spectral gap, bath occupancy and
preparation time. No unearned instantaneous reset is part of the claim.

9.3.1 A finite reset construction with its actual waiting cost.

The existing network itself supplies a quantitative cold reset if the
forces are off, chi remains on, and no further noncommuting data controls
or faults occur during cooling. At g=1,Gamma=4,chi=2,kb=kc=0, its fixed-Z1

 Az=[[0,0,-i],[0,-2iz,-i],[-i,-i,-2]], z=+/-1.

An exact integer Taylor-polynomial/positive-definiteness certificate, plus
a contractive Duhamel bound, proves for EVERY 0<=kb,kc<=.0001, including
the adopted optimized instrument's actual losses,

 ||exp[(Az-diag(kb/2,kc/2,0))t]||_2^2
                  <= .145^floor(t/4).                  (9.2)

This includes finite propagation and the nonnormal transient; merely quoting
the amplitude eigenvalue gap .266602757 would not justify this envelope.
For any apparatus state, including nonGaussian states entangled with data
and a reference, with initial mean photon number <=Emax,

 p_nonvac(t)<=min[1,Emax .145^floor(t/4)],
 D(rho_RQM(t),rho_RQ(t) tensor |vac><vac|)
       <=min[1,sqrt(p_nonvac)+p_nonvac/2].              (9.3)

The comparison retains the SAME actual data/reference marginal after
cooling. Any dephasing emitted during cleanup has not been reversed or
silently discarded. The extra p/2 term is needed for this particular
comparison; the simpler sqrt(p) bound uses a postselected marginal.

Starting from vacuum, bounded real forces and passive couplings give
E(T)<=T int(u^2+w^2)dt for qubit-only Pauli fault trajectories. The actual
optimized squared-drive action is 27.626495. An enforced cap of 30 and T=8

below .001, and 72/g for distance below 1e-6. Directly enforced amplitude

Without a reliable fault/energy flag, a general reuse contract must charge

9.4 Repeated use and preparation/correction budget.

For deterministic calibration perturbations that preserve the conditional
linear model, retain the final modes in the output comparison. If after a
phase alignment the full coherent amplitude difference (all fields plus
final modes) is <=epsilon in Euclidean/L2 norm for every computational
branch, the controlled-isometry operator norm difference is <=epsilon.
The induced full-instrument diamond distance is <=2epsilon. This elementary
Stinespring bound is conservative but composes under future actual CPTP
operations without falsely assuming that residual receiver memory resets.
Tracing final modes and then silently starting the next pulse from vacuum
would not justify the same claim.

Unbounded thermal input, arbitrary qubit relaxation during the pulse,
correlated control faults, uncontrolled extra modes, Kerr terms and rotating-
wave/dispersive breakdown have no earned small budget here. Their physical
measurement or microscopic derivation is a remaining requirement.

10.1 A concrete connection to conventional fault tolerance.

Use this device to measure a weight-two GAUGE S=ZiZj of a subsystem code,
not an arbitrary logical parity whose entire degenerate sector must survive
each physical fault. Because all qubit-meter interactions commute with Zi,Zj,
an arbitrary apparatus-only fault (with arbitrary field record) induces data
Kraus operators in

 Alg(Zi,Zj)=span{I,Zi,Zj,S}=span{I,Zi} Alg(S).

On a subsystem code, S acts only on the gauge subsystem. Thus the logical
error span from that one location is a single-qubit phase-error span modulo
gauge, even if the meter's within-sector coherence was badly disturbed.

One X/Y event on participating qubit i at any time gives Xi D with D in
this diagonal algebra; regrouping yields span{Xi,Yi} Alg(S). It is still a
single-site error span modulo gauge. This precise operator containment
is compatible with the actual severe midpulse trajectory failures above:
those fail to preserve a bare pair, but need not irreversibly destroy a
logical qubit in a subsystem code.

The ordinary3x3 Bacon-Shor code supplies the relevant subsystem correction
structure. An exact binary-Pauli computation checks all100 errors X_i Z_j
where i,j each range over none or nine sites, all10000 ordered pairs, and
finds that every syndrome-zero E^dag F belongs to the4096-element gauge
group. The narrower participating-site fault span is included. This is a
subsystem Knill-Laflamme check, not a noisy recovery implementation.

Arbitrary coarse/noisy meter records do not invalidate the containment, but
they do not supply a trustworthy recovery decision. A concrete recovery
schedule is constructed next. The conventional code and its fault-tolerance
principles remain credited to their established literature.

10.2 A concrete noisy-syndrome recovery, and the cost of using it.

In the nine-qubit code, one round measures all six horizontal XX gauges,
then all six vertical ZZ gauges. The appropriate three-gauge products give
two X-type and two Z-type stabilizer signs. Keep their FULL four-bit vector.
Repeat complete rounds until two consecutive vectors agree, with a hard cap
of four rounds. On agreement, apply the ordinary row/column Pauli correction
as a frame. If there is no agreement by round four, report failure; do not
pretend that an arbitrary last vector is trustworthy.

For a clean encoded input and one localized fault, every complete pre-fault
round reports zero syndrome, every complete post-fault round reports the
syndrome sigma_E of the remaining correctable error, and the containing
round may report any four-bit vector. Two equal adjacent vectors necessarily
include a fault-free round, so the accepted syndrome is either zero or
sigma_E. The frame correction leaves respectively a correctable E or a
gauge operator. If a fault delays agreement beyond round two, rounds three
and four are fault free and agree. One input error with no new fault is
corrected after two rounds. Linearity and syndrome projection extend the
argument to coherent single-fault spans, not just sampled Paulis.

This avoids the invalid bitwise-majority rule: old 00, mixed 01 and new 11
would otherwise vote for the incorrect 01 syndrome. The exhaustive ordinary
Pauli/syndrome check includes 100 correctable X_i Z_j errors, 16 arbitrary
faulty-round vectors and four fault rounds: 6400 scheduled cases, 3200
reached before stopping, zero violations of either claimed one-fault
property. This is a finite recovery proof, not a Monte Carlo threshold.

Equation (4.4) now admits the actual finite optical checks to this schedule.
With independent cold-model renewals, each optimized check is an ideal
correctly reported gauge measurement except for an exact CP fault weight
e_total=.00130805194630. Every faulty component has the required gauge/local
algebra. Twelve checks per round imply at most 48 checks. With meters as the
only faulty locations and a clean encoded input, uncorrectable output can
occur only with at least two such location faults, hence the simple bound

 P_uncorrectable <= C(48,2) e_total^2 = .001930007881.   (10.1)

This is a CLEAN-INPUT RECOVERY-EPISODE bound modulo a correctable output
error. It is not an asymptotic logical error per cycle: a late fault may
leave one correctable error, and a new fault in the next episode is a second
fault across that boundary. Repeated computation requires the ordinary
extended-rectangle and apparatus-memory analysis. No threshold is claimed.

All controls must also be counted. Using physical H on all nine qubits
before and after the horizontal block costs 18 H operations per round,
at most 72, plus at most 48 local phase corrections. A fully dedicated
implementation uses twelve three-mode modules (36 modes) on the twelve
gauge edges and nine data qubits. Sharing or parallelizing modules requires
its actual routing, isolation and commutation contract. A conservative
serial schedule uses at most 48 sensing windows, plus those local gates,
controller time and any required cooldown before reuse/basis changes.

For illustration, adding independent H faults .001 each and local phase
faults .0001 each to the exact meter fault weights gives the pair-union
bound .009664890576 for the same clean-input episode. This excludes all
thermal, initialization, data-idle, correlated-control and classical-decoder
faults. It is a diagnostic sufficient bound, not an earned device error rate
or an improvement over unencoded storage for the same time.

The frame and comparison logic require a reliable classical controller;
its actual failure probability adds to the bound. No fault-free controller
or encoded-state factory has been supplied. This recovery has an explicit
encoded-input contract; the separate physical CNOT calculation above has
its own actual helper-preparation error. General code-state preparation and
repeated gates remain priced under a complete architecture, rather than
being inferred from this one correction episode.

The full protocol and its limitations are preserved in finite_operation/
NOISY_BACON_SHOR_RECOVERY.txt, check_bacon_shor_recovery.py and
bacon_shor_recovery_check.json. The severe bare-pair fault example is thus

12. CONCLUSION AND THE NEXT DECISIVE SCIENTIFIC OBLIGATION
========================================================

This continuation replaces stationary/tail or selected-coherence evidence by
an exact full finite-operation construction in a changed physical encoding.
It also replaces an ideal common dissipator/cascade with its explicit finite
receiver dynamics. The intended readout can be lossy without itself revealing
forbidden within-sector information. Accessible unwanted emissions have a
real local recovery; intrinsic unseen loss, thermal preparation and
noncommuting data faults have explicit costs/failures.

The remaining consequential obligation is a measured or microscopically
derived operating window in which the actual longitudinal drives, dispersive
coupling, mode lifetimes, thermal populations, calibration bandwidth and data
fault rates make the complete gate/code cost better than the strongest
established construction. The present evidence does not establish that
window. The exact operation and resource functional identify what must be
earned, rather than assuming its apparatus or lifetime as an oracle.
