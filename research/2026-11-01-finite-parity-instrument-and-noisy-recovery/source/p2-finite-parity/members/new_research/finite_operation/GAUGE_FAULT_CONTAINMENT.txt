FINITE-MEDIATOR FAULT CONTAINMENT IN A CONVENTIONAL SUBSYSTEM CODE
1 November 2026. Exact operator-span result; no noisy-syndrome threshold claim.

Purpose
-------
The finite mediator is not error-transparent to a data X/Y fault during its
pulse. Root's explicit stress calculation correctly found extra pair-sector
disturbance. That failure need not make the operation unusable for fault-
tolerant computation. When the measured weight-two parity is a GAUGE operator
of a conventional subsystem code, the actual propagated error can remain in
a correctable subsystem error span. This note makes that connection exact.
The Bacon-Shor code and its fault-tolerant extraction methods are established
machinery; the contribution is admission of this physical operation into their
error algebra under stated localization conditions.

1. EXACT MEASUREMENT-LOCATION ALGEBRA

During a check on qubits i,j, suppose every ordinary data-apparatus Hamiltonian
and every ordinary joint noise Kraus operator commutes with Zi and Zj. The
finite mediator has precisely this property: its physical data dependence is
only Zi in a dispersive term and Zj in the two longitudinal drives. Apparatus
operators can otherwise be nonlinear, time dependent, noisy, and arbitrarily
strong. The statement does not rely on the exact coherent-pulse solution.

A joint unitary dilation of any fixed fault/record history is block diagonal:

 W=sum_(zi,zj) |zi,zj><zi,zj| tensor W_(zi,zj).

Every apparatus-only fault, including a resolved or unresolved jump, preserves
this block structure. After an arbitrary apparatus measurement and classical
coarse graining, EVERY fine-grained data Kraus operator belongs to

 D_ij=span{I,Zi,Zj,ZiZj}.                                (1)

This follows by expanding any function of the two binary labels in its four
Walsh characters. No correct parity report is assumed. Finite-strength,
nonprojective measurements, faulty reports, and no-click branches all obey
(1), provided the physical couplings remain diagonal in the stated axes.
Initial apparatus state can be arbitrary and mixed, but may not already carry
uncounted logical-state correlations from earlier failed operations.

Now let S=ZiZj be an actual measured gauge generator. Because Zj=Zi S,

 D_ij={G0+Zi G1: G0,G1 in span{I,S}}.                    (2)

Thus an arbitrary diagonal two-data-qubit disturbance is a coherent sum of
identity and one single-qubit Z error, multiplied by gauge operators. It is
not necessary to demand that it act trivially on every state in a fixed
physical two-qubit parity sector.

2. ONE DATA FAULT DURING THE PULSE

Suppose a single fault may act jointly on the apparatus and ONE participating
data qubit i. Its data operator can be expanded in I,Xi,Yi,Zi. Pull Xi through
the diagonal evolutions before/after the fault using

 Xi f(Zi,Zj)=f(-Zi,Zj)Xi.

Consequently each resulting data Kraus operator is in

 D_ij + Xi D_ij
   =span{I,Xi,Yi,Zi} span{I,S}.                         (3)

For a fault known to have only an X/Y component, it is even narrower:

 Xi D_ij=span{Xi,Yi} span{I,S}.                          (4)

The same proof holds for a fault on participant j, choosing j as the
single-qubit representative. Thus the troublesome history-dependent phase
found after an in-pulse data flip is still at most ONE PHYSICAL-QUBIT Pauli
error modulo the measured gauge. This is stronger than merely asserting
one X and one Z at possibly different positions.

An arbitrary apparatus-only fault need not be small for (2), and an arbitrary
single participating-qubit-plus-apparatus fault need not be Pauli for (3).
The theorem is linear in their Kraus expansions. Computational leakage out
of the two-level data Hilbert space is not covered by a Pauli expansion.

A fault on an idle qubit outside the pair is an ordinary single-qubit error
when the measurement itself is nominal. Combining an external Y fault with
an additional arbitrary apparatus disturbance is TWO faults; it cannot be
silently counted as the one-fault case above.

3. SUBSYSTEM CORRECTABILITY, INCLUDING COHERENT SUPERPOSITIONS

Let P be the stabilizer-code projector and identify its range as H_L tensor
H_G. Gauge operators restricted to P act as I_L tensor g. If an error family
E_a satisfies subsystem Knill-Laflamme,

 P E_a^dag E_b P = I_L tensor g_ab,

then its linear span multiplied by arbitrary gauge operators on the right
is correctable as a logical subsystem. Indeed

 P (E_a G_a)^dag (E_b G_b)P
 =G_a^dag [P E_a^dag E_b P]G_b
 =I_L tensor(g_a^dag g_ab g_b).

The calculation also covers coherent sums with arbitrary gauge coefficients.
Equations (2)-(4) therefore imply correctability by any distance-three
subsystem code whose gauge group contains S, using its usual single-qubit
error-correction capability. For Bacon-Shor, the required gauges are physical
nearest-neighbor weight-two ZZ and XX operators.

This is an existence/containment statement about an IDEAL final recovery.
It does not supply that recovery as a free physical component. The finite
mediator must be incorporated into an actual repeated-syndrome extraction
and decoding schedule before a fault-tolerant gadget or threshold is claimed.

4. EXPLICIT 3x3 BACON-SHOR CONVENTION AND A STRONGER ERROR SET

Index qubits by row r and column c, each from1to3. Use gauge generators

 X_(r,c) X_(r,c+1),     horizontal XX,
 Z_(r,c) Z_(r+1,c),     vertical ZZ.

The center has four independent stabilizers:

 SX_c=product_r X_(r,c)X_(r,c+1), c=1,2,
 SZ_r=product_c Z_(r,c)Z_(r+1,c), r=1,2.

There is one protected logical qubit and four gauge qubits. X errors can be
moved within a row by horizontal gauge operators. Z errors can be moved
within a column by vertical gauge operators. The SZ syndrome identifies the
row of a single X component; the SX syndrome identifies the column of a
single Z component.

The entire coherent error span

 E=span{X_i Z_j: i,j each either absent or one of9 sites}  (5)

is correctable, including an X and Z at distinct positions. To prove this,
consider E_a^dag E_b. It has at most two X components and at most two Z
components. If its stabilizer syndrome is nonzero, P E_a^dag E_b P=0. If its
syndrome is zero, its X row-parity vector must be constant across all three
rows, and its Z column-parity vector must be constant across all columns.
Each vector has weight at most2, so the only possible constant vector is000.
The X part then is a product of horizontal XX gauges and the Z part is a
product of vertical ZZ gauges. Thus every syndrome-zero product is gauge,
which proves subsystem Knill-Laflamme for all of(5).

An explicit ideal recovery chooses an X on a fixed representative column in
the indicated error row, and a Z on a fixed representative row in the indicated
error column. The residual physical Pauli is a gauge operator. This is an
ordinary CSS/Bacon-Shor fact; it is not a new code construction.

5. RECORDS, WEAK GAUGE MEASUREMENTS, AND LATER IDEAL CHECKS

A finite-strength ideal measurement of S has Kraus operators f_r(S), with
arbitrary record r. These are in the gauge algebra even when neither parity
is projected exactly. They act as identity on the protected logical subsystem.
Wrong classical relabeling or coarse graining of records does not enlarge
that Kraus span. After an ideal subsystem recovery, each record subchannel
factors as identity on the logical subsystem times a channel on gauge/junk.
For a product of logical/reference state and initial gauge state, outcome
probabilities therefore carry no logical information. Existing logical-gauge
correlations can be conditionally steered by a gauge measurement; this is not
a claim that conditioning on a correlated gauge variable leaves every marginal
unchanged.

This containment survives later IDEAL gauge measurements. For a Pauli E and
a gauge operator G, G E=E(E^dag G E), and E^dag G E is still in the gauge
algebra. Therefore subsequent ideal gauge checks cannot convert the one-
location error span into a logical operator merely through propagation.
This remains true per record history and hence after coarse graining.

These facts do not make an arbitrary record stream useful for decoding.
A decoder that applies a wrong correction based on bad reports can add errors
and cause logical failure. In particular, do NOT assert that bitwise majority
of three complete syndrome vectors automatically tolerates an in-round fault:
pre-fault00, faulty01, post-fault11 gives majority01, potentially indicating
a different X-error row and producing two row errors after the wrong correction.
A proper fault-tolerant extraction/decoding schedule and its measurement-error
model are still required. At finite readout strength, measurement noise and
remaining cross-syndrome coherences must be carried in that analysis.

6. REQUIRED LOCALIZATION CONDITIONS AND ACTUAL LIMITS

The algebraic result assumes one check's physical disturbance stays on its
specified pair and apparatus during the location under analysis. A persistent
auxiliary fault that is reused on a different pair without containment/reset
can create multiple locations' worth of data errors from one underlying fault.
The ideal pulse's return-to-vacuum property does not prove return after a
fault; reset, isolation, a dedicated-pair arrangement, or a full memory-fault
analysis is needed. Arbitrary simultaneous noncommuting XX and ZZ interactions
on the same data qubits also invalidate the diagonal-block assumption unless
an additional joint analysis establishes containment.

Late apparatus faults are allowed within this support statement. They can
produce a residual correctable local Z error; no impossible perfect detector
at the final instant is assumed. What matters is the future physical support
and whether the fault is contained before the same apparatus reaches other
code qubits.

Off-axis control errors on multiple data qubits, uncontrolled cross-pair
coupling, leakage, or two independent faults need not fit(3). For example,
two X errors in two distinct Bacon-Shor rows have the same syndrome as an X
in the third row; correcting that syndrome creates the weight-three logical
X column. This is a real boundary of the one-location conclusion.

check_bacon_shor_error_span.py independently builds the nine-qubit binary Pauli
group and the conventional gauge generators. Results in
bacon_shor_error_span_check.json:

 12 gauge generators, gauge-group size4096 ignoring phases;
 4 independent stabilizer generators;
 100 distinct errors in(5);
 all10,000 ordered E_a^dag E_b pairs checked;
 ZERO subsystem Knill-Laflamme violations;
 all explicit row/column recoveries gauge-equivalent;
 all12 participant-fault cases across six vertical ZZ gauges reduce to a
 single-site Pauli times I or the measured gauge S.

This verifies the finite algebra; the ordinary proofs above supply the exact
statement. It is not a Monte Carlo logical-error-rate calculation or a
fault-tolerance threshold estimate.

8. PRIMARY PRECEDENT

Aliferis and Cross, Subsystem fault tolerance with the Bacon-Shor code,
Phys.Rev.Lett.98,220502(2007), https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610063.
Established fault-tolerant Bacon-Shor extraction uses nearest-neighbor two-
qubit measurements and avoids entangled auxiliary states. That is the strong
conventional framework to adopt here, not a weak comparison to supersede.

Atalaya et al., Error correcting Bacon-Shor code with continuous measurement
of noncommuting operators, Phys.Rev.A102,022415(2020),
https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08272.
Continuous finite-strength gauge monitoring and its logical error rates have
been studied directly. A proof that a single physical location is correctable
cannot substitute for that continuous decoding analysis.

The retained Astra ingredient is the finite physical parity-emission
construction and its all-output resource/error law. Conventional subsystem
QEC can absorb its earned localized fault algebra. No new code, full noisy-
syndrome protocol, architecture advantage, or threshold is claimed here.
