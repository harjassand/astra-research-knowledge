CONSTRUCTIVE NOISY BACON-SHOR RECOVERY USING THE FINITE GAUGE METER
1 November 2026. Complete bounded-round protocol and one-location proof.

Conclusion and exact scope
--------------------------
An explicit recovery episode can use the actual finite-strength, inefficient,
lossy gauge meters without assuming an ideal final syndrome decoder. Sequential
weight-two gauge rounds, stopped after TWO CONSECUTIVE COMPLETE FOUR-BIT
SYNDROME VECTORS AGREE, satisfy:

 (A) Clean encoded input plus at most one localized bad location leaves at
     most a correctable single-qubit error modulo gauge.
 (B) One correctable input error and no new bad location is corrected.
 (C) Under(A), acceptance occurs within at most four full rounds.

This is a concrete one-fault recovery route for the conventional 3x3 Bacon-Shor
code. It is not a new code, a threshold theorem, or an asymptotic repeated-cycle
logical error rate. A late fault can be left as a correctable residual error;
that residual and faults in the NEXT episode require an extended-rectangle
or memory analysis. The mathematical phrase 'modulo correctable output' does
not mean that an unimplemented ideal decoder is appended to the protocol.

1. THE CODE AND AN ACTUAL FULL ROUND

Use nine data qubits (r,c), r,c=1,2,3, with horizontal XX and vertical ZZ gauge
generators. The stabilizers are

 SX_c=product_(r=1..3) X_(r,c)X_(r,c+1), c=1,2,
 SZ_r=product_(c=1..3) Z_(r,c)Z_(r+1,c), r=1,2.

A full round performs these physical operations in order:

 1. Apply H to all nine data qubits.
 2. Sequentially measure the six horizontal physical ZZ pairs with the
    finite mediator, which measures the original-frame XX gauges. Apply each
    known local phase correction in the appropriate current frame.
 3. Apply H to all nine data qubits to restore the original frame.
 4. Sequentially measure the six vertical ZZ gauges, including their known
    local phase corrections.
 5. Multiply the three gauge outcomes defining each stabilizer, producing
    the COMPLETE vector (SX_1,SX_2,SZ_1,SZ_2).

Measurements in each same-axis group commute. The X and Z gauge groups need
not commute with one another, but every gauge commutes with every stabilizer.
Therefore a fault-free complete round reports the actual stabilizer syndrome,
regardless of the gauge state and regardless of changes of gauge state caused
by the other-axis measurements. The proof does not treat gauge degrees of
freedom as fixed classical bits across X/Z groups.

The round requires 12 physical gauge-meter uses, 18 physical Hadamards, and
up to12 known local phase corrections. If native X-axis couplings or safely
merged phase operations are available they may reduce these sufficient costs;
no such saving is assumed in the stated count.

2. THE CLASSICAL PROTOCOL AND ITS BOUNDARY ACTION

Keep the Pauli frame fixed while acquiring the episode, or translate every
reported gauge sign into the same comparison frame if optional monitored-jump
frames are updated during it. Use reliable classical storage and comparison.

 previous := none
 for round r=1,2,3,4:
     s := the complete four-bit syndrome from the full round above
     if previous exists and s==previous:
         update the Pauli frame by the standard Bacon-Shor recovery R(s)
         return ACCEPT
     previous := s
 return FLAG, leaving the frame unchanged

R(s) selects one X representative in the indicated error row and one Z
representative in the indicated error column. The syndrome of a single X
component identifies its row; that of a single Z component identifies its
column. Representatives can be chosen on a fixed column and fixed row.
This is a classical calculation followed by a tracked Pauli frame. It is
not an ideal quantum syndrome measurement supplied after the noisy meters.

If four rounds contain no adjacent equality, the device issues a classical
unreliable-syndrome flag and makes no speculative data correction. This event
cannot occur under the stated clean-input/one-localized-fault conditions.
With more faults it is counted as an episode failure, not declared a successful
quantum recovery. A larger architecture must decide its flagged-event policy.

A trusted classical controller and correct frame bookkeeping are explicit
assumptions, as in ordinary fault-tolerance constructions. Arbitrary persistent
corruption of the controller, or uncharged data gates selected from a bad
record, is not part of the one-meter-location fault model. Physical local
phase corrections and H operations remain counted quantum operations with
their own error budgets.

3. THE PHYSICAL ONE-LOCATION CONDITION

GAUGE_FAULT_CONTAINMENT.txt proves that, during one measured pair i,j,

 apparatus-only fault: K in span{I,Zi,Zj,ZiZj};
 one participating-qubit/apparatus fault:
   K in span{I,Xi,Yi,Zi} Alg(S), S=ZiZj.

The XX version follows by conjugating its two participating qubits. A fault
in a physical H or local phase correction is a one-qubit fault in this
reference model. Ideal later gauge checks do not spread this family beyond
single-qubit Pauli errors multiplied by gauge operators.

The condition includes an arbitrary reported result from the faulty meter.
Inside the round containing the fault, different stabilizer factors can be
measured before and after the data disturbance. We conservatively allow that
ENTIRE round's four-bit vector to be arbitrary; no time-consistent syndrome
is presumed for it. Only complete rounds outside that location are ideal
reference rounds. Persistent faults and auxiliary memory require the separate
containment provisions below.

4. ORDINARY ONE-FAULT PROOF

First consider a Pauli component E of the one-location error. Let sigma(E)
be its actual stabilizer syndrome. For initially clean encoded input:

 - Every complete round strictly before the fault reports0000.
 - Every complete fault-free round after the fault reports sigma(E).
 - The one round containing the fault may report ANY four-bit vector.

Any accepted pair of adjacent equal full vectors includes at least one
fault-free round. Thus the accepted vector is either0000 or sigma(E).
If0000 is accepted, the frame update is identity and E remains correctable.
If sigma(E) is accepted, the specified R(sigma(E)) leaves only a gauge
operator. No third, incorrectly combined syndrome can be accepted with only
one faulty round.

At most four rounds suffice. With no earlier fault, rounds1and2 would already
agree. A fault can delay the first agreement only if it occurs in round1or2.
If it occurs in round1, rounds2and3 are equal post-fault rounds unless the
protocol has already stopped. If it occurs in round2, rounds3and4 are equal
post-fault rounds unless it has already stopped. Later scheduled faults are
not encountered after earlier acceptance; a physical fault after acceptance
can leave one correctable residual error and belongs to the episode boundary.

For a coherent fault rather than a Pauli fault, expand it in the proven
correctable error span. If acceptance occurs before a complete post-fault
ideal round, the residual is still in that coherent span. Otherwise, the
complete ideal syndrome round projects onto a syndrome sector. Every error
component in that sector differs from the chosen R(s) by a gauge operator,
by subsystem Knill-Laflamme. Linearity therefore gives the same conclusion
for arbitrary quantum-reference entanglement and mixed apparatus states.

With one correctable input error and no new fault, the first two complete
rounds report its syndrome, and the prescribed recovery leaves only gauge.
The proof also works for the larger Bacon-Shor-correctable span containing
one X and one Z component at distinct sites. It does not promise correction
of one input error AND one new fault as if that were a single-fault case.

5. WHY BITWISE MAJORITY WAS REJECTED

In the two SZ bits, take pre-fault00, faulty01, post-fault11. Bitwise majority
is01, whereas the actual middle-row X error has syndrome11. Applying the
wrong representative X leaves two row errors. A subsequent ideal recovery
then produces the logical X column. The output was not merely a different
gauge representative. Requiring agreement of the COMPLETE syndrome vectors
avoids this mixed-vector failure.

6. ACTUAL FINITE-STRENGTH METERS HAVE AN EXACT CP GOOD/BAD ADMISSION

This section uses root's result, independently audited in
conventional_routes/AUDIT_BOUND_AND_FINITE_STRENGTH_INSTRUMENT.txt and
verify_exact_instrument_mixture.py. No channel norm is reinterpreted as a
stochastic error probability.

For the loss-free corrected binary homodyne instrument, put

 q=Phi[-2sqrt(eta N)], gamma=exp(-2N)/2,
 e=q+gamma^2/q, p=1-e, q>0.

In the two parity sectors, the reported+ Schur matrix is
[[1-q,gamma],[gamma,q]]. Subtract p times its correctly reported ideal
projective branch. The remainder is positive semidefinite, rank one, with
Kraus operator

 Kbad_+=(gamma/sqrt(q))P_+ +sqrt(q)P_-.

The reported- residual is

 Kbad_-=sqrt(q)P_+ +(gamma/sqrt(q))P_-.

Their sum Kbad^dag Kbad equals eI. Thus the ACTUAL finite-strength instrument
is exactly an ideal projective gauge instrument with state-independent weight
p, plus a normalized diagonal bad instrument with weight e. Its residual
cross-sector coherences are included; weak readout was not simply assumed
projective.

For the admitted calibrated auxiliary losses, after the known local phase
correction their channel on an ideal parity-projected branch is exactly a
local Z dephasing channel of probability pZ. Consequently the full actual
meter has exact ideal weight p(1-pZ), and total bad-location weight

 e_total=e+pZ-e pZ.                                    (1)

Every residual Kraus operator is in the diagonal pair algebra, which is the
correctable one-location family used above. These latent good/bad labels are
only a mathematical decomposition; the physical decoder does not need to
observe them. The actual nominal cold-bath instrument returns its auxiliary
modes to vacuum on all branches, including auxiliary photon losses and noisy
reports. Under fresh independent Markov baths, its next use therefore is a
new invocation of the same complete instrument, not a supplied perfect reset.

Root's optimized finite mediator has e_total=.001308051946299349. Its report
error q=.001 is not the whole location error: finite-strength coherence and
auxiliary loss both enter (1). This exact CP decomposition supplies a genuine
finite-noise route into the discrete single-location analysis.

For reference, there is also an exact HALF-diamond distance for the main
loss-free instrument relative to the ideal projective gauge instrument:

 delta_main=sqrt(q^2+gamma^2).

For a pure system-reference input with parity weights x,1-x, the two reported
blocks have summed trace norm2sqrt[q^2+4gamma^2x(1-x)], maximized atx=1/2.
The result is independent of gauge-sector degeneracy. Adding the calibrated
hidden channel gives delta_total<=delta_main+pZ by contractivity. This is a
norm bound, distinct from the exact stochastic CP weight(1).

7. CHARGED EPISODE COST AND A LIMITED NUMERICAL BOUND

Normal fault-free termination uses two full rounds:24gauge meters,36H gates,
and up to24local phase corrections. The four-round cap requires at most

 48 gauge meters,
 72 physical H gates,
 48 known local phase corrections,
 classical record retention, comparison and a final Pauli-frame update.

For sequential measurement duration T, the worst sensing duration is48T,
plus the H/phase gate durations, actual initialization/cooldown if needed,
routing and controller latency. At T=8 in the dimensionless model, this is
384 sensing-time units before those other costs. These are sufficient
protocol costs, not lower bounds or an overhead advantage.

There are twelve spatial gauge links. Dedicating a three-mode meter to every
link uses36auxiliary modes in addition to9data qubits. Sharing a meter uses
fewer modes only if real routing, isolation and reset make that sharing valid;
those operations cannot be assumed free. The illustrative decoder bound
below does not choose or validate a physical routing architecture.

For initially clean input, independent complete meter channels, and NO other
fault sources, the total weight of fault histories that are not certified
correctable by the one-location proof is at most

 Pr(at least2 bad meter locations)
    <= binom(48,2) e_total^2
    =1128 e_total^2 ~ .00193.                           (2)

This is a CLEAN-INPUT RECOVERY EPISODE bound on uncorrectable/logical damage
MODULO a correctable output error. It is NOT the repeated-cycle asymptotic
logical error rate. In particular, an accepted0000with a late fault can leave
one correctable error. That residual plus a fault in the next episode crosses
the boundary of the single-episode theorem and needs extended-rectangle or
memory analysis. No ideal decoder is added physically to claim(2); 'modulo a
correctable output' states the proved output-error family.

For a diagnostic that also assigns independent bad-location probabilities
.001 to each of72H gates and .0001 to each of48local phase corrections, the
pair union bound is

 sum_(a<b) p_a p_b
  =[(sum_a p_a)^2-sum_a p_a^2]/2
  =.0096648905755.

This deliberately exposes the quantum-control cost instead of silently
crediting noiseless basis changes. It still omits data-idle noise, state
preparation, thermal input, controller faults, routing and extra reset error,
and is not a full hardware logical-error prediction or a threshold estimate.

8. FAULT CONTAINMENT, RESET AND WHAT IS STILL REQUIRED

The exact nominal cold-bath channels in(1) have earned vacuum return even
on their noisy branches. Additional data X/Y faults or energy-injecting
apparatus faults can leave residual oscillator states, as root's stress
tests show. A single persistent fault reused across different pairs/rounds
is NOT one localized bad round in the proof. Before claiming the expanded
physical-fault model, those histories need bounded cooldown/isolation,
dedicated-pair containment, or a joint memory analysis.

The separate capability-origin FINITE_RESET_ADDENDUM supplies the constructive
finite-reset certificate and its costs. An independent illustrative data-only
cooldown calculation is preserved as finite_cooldown_bound.json; it is not
silently folded into the short sensing duration. Arbitrary unbounded-energy
apparatus faults require an explicit energy contract or a physically bounded
reset mechanism. Thermal baths impose their own residual floor.

Likewise, arbitrary simultaneous noncommuting couplings on the same data
qubits are not admitted without additional analysis. Basis rotations are
performed in separated groups with inactive apparatus isolated or in its
admitted vacuum state. The local phase corrections are real calibrated
quantum operations (or a physically implemented equivalent), not an arbitrary
perfect diagonal unitary oracle.

The episode accepts an encoded block at its stated input boundary. Reliable
encoded preparation, logical gates between episodes, leakage reduction, and
a scalable full decoder are separate architecture components. The constructed
protocol closes the particular missing noisy-recovery step under its explicit
channel and localization assumptions; it does not establish a full cheaper
fault-tolerant computer.

9. EXHAUSTIVE DISCRETE CHECK AND PRECEDENT

check_bacon_shor_recovery.py exhaustively checks6400scheduled cases:
100Pauli errors in the stronger one-X/one-Zcorrectable set,16arbitrary faulty
round vectors, and4possible fault rounds. Of these,3200faults are actually
reached before adaptive early stopping; later scheduled faults are correctly
classified as outside the completed episode. Reached histories terminate in
2,3,4rounds in200,1599,1401cases respectively. There are:

 ZERO clean-input/one-fault violations;
 ZERO one-correctable-input-error/no-new-fault violations;
 ZERO flagged outcomes within the admitted one-fault model.

It also verifies same-axis gauge commutation, gauge/stabilizer commutation,
actual noncommutation of X/Z gauge sets, and the bitwise-majority counterexample.
The complete output is bacon_shor_recovery_check.json. The discrete abstraction
allows an arbitrary entire faulty-round vector, so it safely covers rather
than assumes away mixed timing within the twelve sequential gauge meters.

This adopts the established Bacon-Shor fault-tolerance framework of Aliferis
and Cross, Phys.Rev.Lett.98,220502(2007),
https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0610063.
Continuous weak Bacon-Shor monitoring also has primary precedent in Atalaya
et al., Phys.Rev.A102,022415(2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08272.
The new Astra ingredient remains the physical finite-mode meter and its exact
complete noisy instrument; the code, gauge logic and classical repetition
strategy are conventional tools used constructively.
