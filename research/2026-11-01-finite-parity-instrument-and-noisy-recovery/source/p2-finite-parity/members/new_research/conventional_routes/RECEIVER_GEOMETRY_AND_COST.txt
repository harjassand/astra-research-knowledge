RECEIVER GEOMETRY, A STRONG CONVENTIONAL COMPARATOR, AND THE UPSTREAM LOSS COST
1 November 2026. Internal research derivation; no external validation or novelty claim.

1. Scope and decisions

This analysis began by constructing a stronger conventional receiver than a
binary homodyne result followed by an outcome-dependent phase correction.
Continuous measurement and feedback can sometimes erase the unwanted logical
information in the received field. This is established quantum-eraser machinery.
It does not erase information in fields lost before the receiver.

The selected root construction instead couples the emitted field back to a
data qubit before the final irreversible outlet. That can change the complete
output state; it is physically different from a passive transformation applied
only to an already emitted field. Cascaded parity measurement and coherent
compensation are established precedents. The new two-mode, data-conditional
compensation formula is a concrete construction assembled in this continuation;
historical novelty has not been established.

2. Exact coherent-output homodyne criterion

Consider an exactly specified coherent-state dilation on a finite set of
logical basis labels z:

  |z> -> exp(i phi_z) |z> |alpha_z>, alpha_z in C^M.

M can include orthogonal temporal output modes, provided all of them are
physically available to the receiver. No final meter memory is silently traced
away: it must have the same state within a sector, be included in M and measured,
or contribute its own overlap loss. Coherent-state vacuum covariance and pure
vacuum input are hypotheses; branch-dependent squeezing or thermal noise is
not included by specifying just alpha_z.

Let p(z) be the intended parity. Define the real vector space

  D = span_R {alpha_z-alpha_z' : p(z)=p(z')}.

A passive unitary optical network U, followed by an ideal homodyne measurement
of one quadrature of every mode, obtains no information about z inside any
parity sector if and only if

  Im <d,e> = 0 for every d,e in D.                         (1)

This is the isotropy condition for D under the real symplectic form.

Proof. Absorb all local-oscillator phases into U. Homodyne distributions have
the same vacuum covariance and means sqrt(2) Re(U alpha_z). Equality of the
distributions within sectors is equivalent to U D subset i R^M. Vectors in
i R^M have real mutual complex inner products, so (1) is necessary. Conversely,
if (1) holds, perform Gram-Schmidt over the reals using Re<d,e>. The resulting
real-orthonormal basis is also complex-orthonormal because its imaginary inner
products vanish. Extend it to a complex-orthonormal basis of C^M and choose U
to map its D basis vectors into imaginary coordinate vectors. This constructs
the required receiver. In particular dim_R D <= M.

This condition concerns erasure of intra-sector information. It does not
ensure that a useful parity signal survives in the measured quadratures. For
example, one mode with alpha_z = f(g1 z1+g2 z2) has D on the same real line as
the parity mean separation. Every zero-information homodyne quadrature then
also removes that separation. Photon counting can be stronger here because it
distinguishes amplitudes by magnitude while erasing their sign; the criterion
is not a no-go theorem for non-Gaussian receivers.

The conditional coherent-state wavefunction is

  <x|alpha> = pi^(-M/4) exp[-||x-sqrt(2) Re alpha||^2/2]
             exp[i sqrt(2) x.Im alpha - i Re alpha.Im alpha].

After an observed record x, its phase is explicitly known. Correcting it
requires the actual data-diagonal control with eigenvalues

  -phi_z - sqrt(2) x.Im(U alpha_z)
            + Re(U alpha_z).Im(U alpha_z).

This formula is not an assertion that an arbitrary high-weight diagonal gate
is free. The examples below have only one-qubit Z corrections. An arbitrary
coherent-output model can require non-native many-qubit phase corrections.

With equal collection efficiency eta, modeled as a beam splitter before U,
optimal phase correction gives pair-coherence magnitude

  C_zz' = exp[-(1-eta)||delta||^2/2
             -eta||Re(U delta)||^2/2], delta=alpha_z-alpha_z'.       (2)

The first term is inaccessible field information; the second is information
actually obtained by the chosen homodyne receiver. Equation (2) is exact for
the coherent-output dilation under these hypotheses. Feedback does not remove
the first term. Unequal efficiencies must be modeled before the mode mixer.

Non-isotropic example. Four same-sector outputs alpha={a,-a,ia,-ia} in one
mode have two differences 2a and 2ia with nonzero imaginary inner product.
At eta=1, for any homodyne angle theta the two antipodal coherences have losses
exp[-2a^2 cos^2 theta] and exp[-2a^2 sin^2 theta]. Their best worst-case value
is exp[-a^2], attained at theta=pi/4 modulo pi/2. This is a receiver-specific
obstruction: photon counting erases these four phases exactly, since every
alpha has the same magnitude and the number-resolved backaction is unitary.

There is a much older, broader classification relevant to this observation.
Kummerer--Maassen's rank-one dephasing generator theorem says a normal single
Lindblad operator has an essentially classical Markov dilation exactly when
its spectrum lies on a circle or a straight line. Fagnola et al. (2019),
section 5.1, reproduces this result. Line spectra support diffusive correction;
circle spectra support shifted photon counting. The present report does NOT
claim that classification as a new Astra theorem, or extend it without proof
to arbitrary finite-time channels and arbitrary non-Markovian receivers.

3. Strong one-resonator comparison with optimized ordinary control

An economical conventional comparison is a single vacuum resonator c with
fixed outlet sqrt(kappa)c and Hamiltonian

  H(t) = chi Z1 c^dag c + g u(t) Z2 (c+c^dag),
  a=kappa/2, chi real nonzero.

This requires the dispersive and longitudinal actuators on the same resonator.
It is a legitimate stronger hardware comparison if this co-location is
available; the root cascade's exact wiring does not automatically provide it.
An independently addressable Z2 force is preferable to an unoptimized equal
Z1+Z2 force. The latter has four times as much worst-sector loss at fixed
parity separation.

Write the branch amplitude

  alpha_z(t) = g[A(t) z1 z2 + B(t) z2].

For a general complex longitudinal envelope (with its conjugate in H),

  A'=-a A-i chi B,
  B'=-a B-i chi A-i u.

Consequently B=i(A'+a A)/chi. For a real chosen A, write B=i b with b real.
The x output quadrature is a pure parity signal and the p component is a Z2
nuisance. A fixed x homodyne receiver obtains parity while quantum-erasing
the nuisance in the collected fraction. The stochastic phase and all
deterministic within-sector phases require only local Z1/Z2 rotations:
P Z2=Z1 and Z2^2=I. No arbitrary diagonal phase oracle is needed.

Choose any twice differentiable real A with A=A'=0 at start and end. It is
implemented exactly by

  b=(A'+a A)/chi,
  u=-[A''+2a A'+(a^2+chi^2)A]/chi.                       (3)

The cavity begins and ends in vacuum for all four data states. A(t)=A0
sin^2(pi t/T) is a simple bounded-drive example; extra endpoint smoothness
can be obtained by a higher sine power. This is ordinary inverse response
engineering, closely related to derivative-shaped cavity reset pulses.

Define E_A=int|A|^2 dt and E_B=int|B|^2 dt over the COMPLETE pulse and tail.
If the endpoints vanish, integration by parts gives the exact identity

  E_B/E_A = [a^2 + ||A'||_2^2/||A||_2^2]/chi^2.          (4)

For any compact duration-T pulse, Wirtinger's inequality gives

  E_B/E_A >= [a^2+pi^2/T^2]/chi_max^2.                 (5)

The same lower bound applies to arbitrary complex envelopes and variable
chi(t) with |chi(t)|<=chi_max: use |B|^2=|A'+aA|^2/|chi|^2 before integration.
This is a bound for the specified fixed-linewidth, vacuum, two-actuator
class. Added conditional drives, tunable linewidths, shared-outlet networks,
or different encodings can escape its assumptions.

For A=sin^m(pi t/T),

  E_B/E_A = [a^2 + m^2 pi^2/((2m-1)T^2)]/chi^2.

Let N_sep=4 kappa g^2 E_A be the squared separation of the two parity output
amplitudes. Optimal matched homodyne discrimination has

  q = (1/2) erfc(sqrt(eta N_sep/2)).

After physically implementable continuous-record phase correction, the
remaining same-sector visibility is

  V = exp[-(1-eta) N_sep (E_B/E_A)/2].                 (6)

Thus even perfect pulse optimization cannot make inefficient detection
harmless in this one-cavity model. Equations (3)--(6) are stronger comparisons
than an unmodulated pulse with a discarded continuous record.

4. The root two-mode compensation construction: a necessary exposure cost

The root supplies the construction and its full proof. For the lossless
common cascade its real target pointer A and source output B obey

  B=-(A'+a_c A)/chi, a_c=kappa_c/2,
  source output = i B Z2,
  corrector amplitude = [A P-iB Z2]/sqrt(kappa_c),
  final outlet amplitude = A P.

This output is parity-only before it reaches an inefficient receiver.
Noisy final detection therefore lowers the measurement information without
revealing which vector inside a parity sector was present, within the ideal
linear, vacuum, conserved-Z model. It is the added *data-conditional* Z2 drive
on the corrector, not ordinary processing of a homodyne record, that cancels
the nuisance field.

For real A with A(0)=A(T)=0, put N_out=int A^2 and N_pre=int B^2. Exactly,

  N_pre/N_out = [a_c^2+||A'||^2/||A||^2]/chi^2
              >= [a_c^2+pi^2/T^2]/chi^2.               (7)

This is not a lower bound on all quantum measurements. It is a lower bound
on this compatible source/corrector realization with the stated actuators.
It charges the photons carrying Z2 information BEFORE cancellation.

If an interstage channel has transmission tau and controls are recalibrated
to deliver the same B to the corrector, the source emits N_pre/tau photons.
The inaccessible channel receives (1-tau)N_pre/tau and causes

  V_line = exp[-2(1-tau)N_pre/tau].                    (8)

With final homodyne efficiency eta, the two signals are +/-A, so

  q=(1/2)erfc(sqrt(2 eta N_out)).

Combining (7) and (8), for a target assignment q in (0,1/2),

  -log V_line >= [(1-tau)/(tau eta)] [erfcinv(2q)]^2
                 [a_c^2+pi^2/T^2]/chi^2.              (9)

Detector inefficiency does not directly cause within-sector leakage after
perfect cancellation. It still increases the required output photons and
therefore the upstream-loss burden when the assignment error is held fixed.

Internal source/corrector loss is analogous. With source external linewidth
kappa_e and inaccessible source loss gamma_e, source amplitude iB/sqrt(kappa_e)
gives a visibility exponent 2(gamma_e/kappa_e)N_pre. With inaccessible
corrector loss gamma_c, its amplitude above gives 2(gamma_c/kappa_c)N_pre.
These formulas use the actual calibrated dampings in the pulse equations;
one cannot just add internal loss to the lossless pulse without recalibration.
Additional parity-dependent attenuation in the lost corrector field can
reduce parity coherence, but intra-sector visibility is governed by B.

The corrector drive simplifies to

  w=-[A''+(chi^2-a_c^2)A]/[chi sqrt(kappa_c)].          (10)

At chi=a_c it is a pure second-derivative compensation. At chi much larger
than a_c, upstream information exposure falls as 1/chi^2, but maintaining a
fixed pointer requires a corrector force of order chi A/sqrt(kappa_c).
Finite amplitude, slew, photon criticality and data lifetime must be included
before using large chi or a very short pulse to claim low cost.

5. Qualifications that materially change the scientific claim

The output criterion assumes Z1 and Z2 remain conserved during the pulse.
An X error or relaxation event changes the sign while both resonators retain
their earlier conditional amplitudes. The precomputed compensation then
does not apply to that history. Root must retain the resulting instrument,
not replace an unknown change time with a known feedforward oracle.

The dispersive term chi Z1 c^dag c is a standard transmon-level model. In a
coherent-sign cat encoding, ordinary storage number a^dag a is approximately
logical identity, not logical Z. A claim that this directly implements
cat-logical Z dispersion requires a separate physical construction.

Kerr nonlinearities can turn branch-conditioned coherent fields into
noncoherent states. Equal means then do not guarantee equal output quantum
states. Static detuning errors, drive noise, warm input, counterrotating
terms and source/corrector internal ports also need explicit terms.

At equal acquired parameters and the same actuators, optimized conventional
quantum control can adopt equations (7)--(10). No honest comparison should
exclude it from doing so. The contribution is a useful explicit compatible
construction and its exposure law; a distinct architecture advantage or
historical novelty remains to be demonstrated.
