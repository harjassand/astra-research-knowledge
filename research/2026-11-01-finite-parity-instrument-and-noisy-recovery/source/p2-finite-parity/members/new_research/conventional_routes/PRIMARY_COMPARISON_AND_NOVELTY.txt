A. Methods that already solve substantial parts of the intended problem

1. A. Frisk Kockum, L. Tornberg, G. Johansson (2012),
   Undoing measurement-induced dephasing in circuit QED,
   Physical Review A 85, 052318.
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.85.052318
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.2386

   Full continuous-record homodyne or photodetection feedback can reverse
   measurement-induced stochastic phases under suitable collection. The
   article explicitly analyzes detector efficiency and bandwidth. Therefore
   arbitrary phase correction conditioned only on a final binary bit is not
   the strongest informed receiver comparison. Quantum-erasing readout itself
   is not new. Our exact coherent-output criterion makes the multi-output
   compatibility requirement explicit in the declared receiver class.

2. G. de Lange et al. (2014), Reversing Quantum Trajectories with Analog
   Feedback, Physical Review Letters 112, 080501.
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.080501
   https://publications.lib.chalmers.se/records/fulltext/195376/local_195376.pdf

   Experimental analog feedback reversed a measured stochastic phase, with
   a model efficiency near one half. This is a useful physical feasibility
   anchor for the conventional continuous-record comparison, not proof of
   the efficiency or latency of the root apparatus.

3. J. Kerckhoff, L. Bouten, A. Silberfarb, H. Mabuchi (2009),
   Physical model of continuous two-qubit parity measurement in a
   cavity-QED network, Physical Review A 79, 024305.
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.79.024305

   A coherent probe scatters sequentially from two atom-cavity systems and
   is read with fixed-quadrature homodyne. The work uses a full QSDE model,
   analyzes a limiting ideal parity measurement, and simulates finite
   parameters. Sequential conditional scattering as an eigenstate-preserving
   parity route is prior machinery. It is a stronger comparator than a
   standalone noisy sign-sensitive meter.

4. A. E. B. Nielsen (2010), Fighting decoherence in a continuous two-qubit
   odd- or even-parity measurement with a closed-loop setup,
   Physical Review A 81, 012307.
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.81.012307

   Repeated coherent interaction with the qubits can reduce within-parity
   decoherence compared with a single sequential pass. This prevents treating
   repeated field-data interaction or coherent-loop compensation as an
   unprecedented organizing principle.

5. D. P. DiVincenzo and F. Solgun (2013), Multi-qubit parity measurement in
   circuit quantum electrodynamics, New Journal of Physics 15, 075001.
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.1910

   The design imposes quantum-eraser conditions directly on scattering phase
   shifts of multimode microwave structures. It treats three/four-qubit
   measurements and finite-bandwidth coherent pulses. These are close
   predecessors to judging the complete field rather than its reported bit.
   A new stationary phase identity alone would not establish a new device.

6. F. Motzoi, K. B. Whaley, M. Sarovar (2015), Continuous joint measurement
   and entanglement of qubits in remote cavities,
   Physical Review A 92, 032308; corrected preprint version v2 (2016).
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.04766
   https://escholarship.org/uc/item/84m524mt

   The paper gives a full cascaded two-cavity model and exact shaped
   compensation fields. In section III.D, Eq. (37), the compensation drive
   enforces equality of the selected output states throughout the transient.
   Its main operation is half-parity: it protects |01>,|10> while distinguishing
   the even states. This is a direct prior for all-time compensation, but it
   does not establish the root's simultaneous equality of both parity sectors
   using a Z2-dependent longitudinal compensation force.

7. C. Dickel et al. (2018), Chip-to-chip entanglement of transmon qubits
   using engineered measurement fields, Physical Review B 97, 064508.
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.97.064508
   https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/128404/1/PhysRevB.97.064508.pdf

   The experiment implements compensation of selected-output transients and
   can choose either odd- or even-sector entanglement. Its fitted interchip
   loss was 11.8%; even matched transmitted fields retained loss-induced
   dephasing. This is empirical evidence that pre-compensation routing loss
   is a central physical burden, not an administrative footnote. It does not
   set a fundamental loss floor for an integrated shared-reservoir device.

8. F. Motzoi, L. Buchmann, C. Dickel (2018), Simple, smooth and fast pulses
   for dispersive measurements in cavities and quantum networks.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.04116

   Derivative pulse shaping gives finite-time measurement with cavity reset
   and extends to multiple states, Purcell filters, and cascaded cavities.
   The abstract was available; direct PDF/HTML retrieval failed in this run.
   Do not claim a detailed comparison to unread equations from this source.
   The broader inverse-response/reset methodology is plainly established.

9. M. Jerger et al., Dispersive Qubit Readout with Intrinsic Resonator Reset.
   https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04891v1

   The available full text experimentally implements derivative-engineered
   readout and reset, includes an iterative Kerr correction, and discusses
   why short pulses increase internal photon burden. The response products
   in Eqs. (6)--(7) implement reset for several state-dependent frequencies.
   Its reset and pulse-shaping results should be adopted as ordinary tools;
   reset is not a new contribution of the root's parity construction.

10. J. Govenius, Y. Matsuzaki, I. G. Savenko, M. Mottonen (2015),
    Parity measurement of remote qubits using dispersive coupling and
    photodetection, Physical Review A 92, 042305.
    https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.07653

    Two remote dispersive resonators, a beam splitter, number-resolving
    photodetection and record-dependent local phases give a complete parity
    protocol. Detector timing and efficiency are material resources. This
    supports finite_operation's monitored-counting route as an established
    baseline. Threshold-only or missed-count receivers do not inherit the
    ideal instrument automatically.

11. A. Sarlette and M. Mirrahimi (2017), Loss-tolerant parity measurement
    for distant quantum bits, Physical Review A 95, 032329.
    https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04490
    https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.95.032329

    Cat-state probe fields turn channel photon loss approximately into
    probe flips, weakening parity discrimination while largely preserving
    parity eigenstates. The finite-cat Kraus formulas retain a small residual
    intra-sector disturbance. The work assumes ideal local preparation,
    interactions and readout to isolate transmission loss. It is a strong
    route when transmission is the dominant problem, but the full local
    cat-source/control cost must be restored for a device comparison.

12. S. Rosenblum et al. (2018), Fault-tolerant detection of a quantum error,
    Science 361, 266--270.
    https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat3996

    Matching e/f dispersive shifts makes dominant ancilla decay commute
    through parity mapping. The inherited packet already derives the full
    matched-qutrit instrument and its no-click branch. That construction is
    retained as a strong whole-instrument baseline; it is not displaced by
    an exact noiseless network alone.

13. H. Putterman et al. (2025), Hardware-efficient quantum error correction
    via concatenated bosonic qubits, Nature 638, 927--934.
    https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13025v2
    https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08642-7

    A dissipative-cat repetition memory with noisy ancillas supplies a
    demonstrated multi-round computational protection baseline. The reported
    cycle is 2.8 microseconds, and the paper charges storage, ancilla and
    reconfinement errors. A replacement meter cannot claim architecture-level
    benefit by comparing only its conditional measurement kernel.

14. T. Tsunoda et al. (2023), Error-Detectable Bosonic Entangling Gates with
    a Noisy Ancilla, PRX Quantum 4, 020354.
    https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.020354

    Programmable beam splitters plus dispersion produce detected-error
    entangling gates. Appendix G explicitly distinguishes error closure from
    stronger error transparency, and handles no-jump effects separately.
    Its gate branch can compensate some no-jump imbalance by changing the
    initial ancilla pulse. A new closure or amplitude-balancing identity must
    be compared with these actual operations.

15. Q. Xu, P. Zeng, D. Xu, L. Jiang (2024), Fault-Tolerant Operation of
    Bosonic Qubits with Discrete-Variable Ancillae,
    Physical Review X 14, 031016.
    https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevX.14.031016
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.20578

    Path-independent and generalized path-independent quantum control are
    integrated with bosonic correction into fault-tolerant gadgets. Bosonic
    no-jump terms are neglected under a stated large-cat justification in
    part of the analysis. This is prior machinery for protected operations,
    not evidence that the root network's unmodeled faults are harmless.

16. An entangling gate for dual-rail erasure qubits (2026), Nature 656, 47--53.
    https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10822-y
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13441885/

    The demonstrated Swap--Wait--Swap operation uses temporary coupler
    excitation and strong dispersion. Reported gate duration is about 500 ns,
    erasure about 0.5%, and residual Pauli errors below 0.1%. These numbers
    belong to its stated device and characterization, not a threshold for
    the root model. It is a serious lower-overhead computation comparator.

B. General known theory adopted, not renamed as an Astra discovery

17. M. Gregoratti and R. F. Werner (2003), Quantum Lost and Found,
    Journal of Modern Optics 50, 915--933.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0209025

    Environment-assisted perfect quantum correction requires a suitable
    decomposition into isometric channels. In equal finite dimensions this
    is the random-unitary condition. Unitality alone does not suffice.

18. F. Fagnola, J. E. Gough, H. I. Nurdin, L. Viola (2019), Mathematical
    Models of Markovian Dephasing, Journal of Physics A 52, 385301.
    https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.11784
    https://doi.org/10.1088/1751-8121/ab38ec

    Section 5.1 reproduces Kummerer--Maassen's line-or-circle classification
    for a single normal Lindblad operator with an essentially classical
    Markov dilation. The paper separately characterizes a diffusive
    Hamiltonian obstruction. Our receiver geometry is a restricted
    operational construction, not a replacement for this classification.

19. A. Halaski and C. P. Koch (2026), Obstacles to Continuous Quantum Error
    Correction via Parity Measurements.
    https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02106v2
    https://doi.org/10.1103/8bkm-b48b

    The paper highlights that a parity-sector transition leaves a meter
    transient, which can reveal unwanted logical information. The new
    fixed-Z compensation construction does not dispose of that obstruction
    during X errors or relaxation. A full noisy trajectory calculation or
    an error-contained gadget is still necessary.

20. B. Royer, S. Puri, A. Blais (2018), Qubit parity measurement by parametric
    driving in circuit QED, Science Advances 4, eaau1695.
    https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aau1695
    https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6269160/

    A nonlinear parametric resonator distinguishes two-qubit parity while
    suppressing within-sector disturbance. Its bright signal and residual
    squeezed-vacuum dephasing can be tuned separately. For four qubits it
    explicitly adds a low-Q frequency-conversion filter to erase unwanted
    frequency information before emission. The resonant effective outlet
    is parity-only; off-resonant sidebands and internal resonator loss leave
    quantified leakage. Thus pre-emission information erasure by a mediator
    is established prior art. The root finite reciprocal construction's
    relevant distinction is exact all-time matching in a linear finite-mode
    model, including reset, without using a bifurcation or dropping those
    sidebands. This distinction alone does not establish device superiority.

21. Z. M. McIntyre, W. A. Coish (2024), Flying-cat parity checks for quantum
    error correction, Physical Review Research 6, 023247.
    https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.023247
    https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.023247

    Sequential conditional scattering writes parity into coherent signs.
    The full paper derives photon-loss data-error channels and the opposing
    photon-number requirements for measurement confidence and loss damage.
    It places weight-three checks in a subsystem surface code, where relevant
    hook errors are gauge-equivalent to local errors. Section V explicitly
    charges finite pulse bandwidth, cavity internal loss and data lifetimes.
    The matched dispersive scattering point chi=kappa_external/2 and
    coherent-sign parity emission are established tools, as is optimizing
    the resulting readout-versus-data-error tradeoff. The new finite-mediator
    bound must be compared to this informed architecture, not an uncorrected
    meter with a discarded record.

22. Yang Xiao et al. (2026), Precise parity measurement of N cat-state qubits
    based on steady states of a cavity mode, Physical Review A 113, 053702.
    https://doi.org/10.1103/3d46-1vn7

    Primary abstract verified; the article body required subscription and
    was not accessed. It reports a driven artificial-atom mediator, a
    parity-dependent effective Hamiltonian and dissipative auxiliary-mode
    steady states, with numerical robustness claims. Its abstract is not
    enough to certify a complete instrument or resolve exact actuator
    equivalence. It prevents asserting that many-cat steady-state mediator
    parity proposals are absent, but is not used for stronger technical
    conclusions in this work.

C. Current assessment

The strongest positive result developed in this continuation is exact
simultaneous full-parity output matching at finite time using two physical
Z2-dependent longitudinal drives and a dispersive Z1 coupling. Root replaced
the initial ideal cascade with three explicit finite reciprocal modes, one
of which is damped to the main receiver. Its endpoint reset is ordinary
inverse engineering. Coherent reinteraction and erasure before emission
have direct precedents above. Its value lies in the compatible explicit
finite-mode actuator recipe, all-output instrument and charged exposure
law, provided later fault analysis leaves a useful regime.

An optimized conventional controller given the same calibrated transfer
functions and all the same actuators can use that recipe. There is no fair
separate 'conventional optimized' baseline that is prohibited from doing so.
We explicitly implemented that fair comparison in
optimize_finite_mediator.py: ordinary constrained optimization of the same
root recipe improves logical-information exposure by about 29.3% over the
original sin^6 primitive at fixed duration, assignment and force caps.
It increases other photon/control costs. The optimized result is within
4.44% of a rigorous finite-time lower bound in the exact-output family;
neither a global total-error optimum nor hardware advantage is claimed.

The investigation must therefore earn its advance from a physical regime,
resource improvement, or extension to noisy histories that those established
constructions do not already deliver. No such total-resource advantage has
been established by this memo.
