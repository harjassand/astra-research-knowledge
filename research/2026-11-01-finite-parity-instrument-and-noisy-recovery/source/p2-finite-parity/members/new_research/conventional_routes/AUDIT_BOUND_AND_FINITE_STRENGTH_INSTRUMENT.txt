1. Re-audit of the .3080159594 exposure lower bound

Write a=kb/2=kc/2>=0. The actual b equation gives

 A=(sqrt(Gamma)/g)(U'+aU).

Multiplying by exp(at), integrating, and using U(0)=U(T)=0 gives
 int_0^T exp(at)A(t)dt=0 exactly. This is a weighted zero-area condition,
not an assertion that A has zero unweighted mean.

Exact mode reset gives A=0 because m=0, and A'=0 because b+c=0 in the
m equation. The construction is smooth enough that these endpoint traces
exist. Define f=exp[a(t-T/2)]A. Then f=f'=0 at both endpoints and int f=0.
Moreover,

 A''=exp[-a(t-T/2)](D-a)^2f,
 exp[-2a(t-T/2)] lies in [exp(-aT),exp(aT)].

Thus the direction of the weighted inequality is

 ||A''||^2/||A||^2 >= exp(-2aT) ||(D-a)^2f||^2/||f||^2.       (1)

Expand the numerator. The odd cross terms integrate to endpoint terms
in f^2 and (f')^2 and vanish. The remaining cross term is
2a^2 int f'' f = -2a^2||f'||^2, which combines with the +4a^2||f'||^2
from (-2af')^2. Consequently, with the signs and coefficients retained,

 ||(D-a)^2f||^2=||f''||^2+2a^2||f'||^2+a^4||f||^2.             (2)

The sharp clamped, zero-mean derivative inequality is
 ||f''||^2 >= (beta2/T)^4||f||^2, beta2=7.853204624095838.
It is not inferred by identifying zero mean with orthogonality to the
unconstrained ground mode. To prove it, center the interval at zero and
split f into even and odd parts. Their ordinary and second-derivative norms
are orthogonal. The odd part automatically has zero mean; the even part
inherits zero mean. Compactness gives a variational minimizer in each part.

On [-L,L], L=T/2, the odd minimizer has
 f=C sinh(kx)+D sin(kx), with tan(z)=tanh(z), z=kL.
The first nontrivial root z_o=3.926602312... is in (pi,3pi/2).

The constrained even Euler-Lagrange equation includes a constant multiplier:
 f''''=k^4 f+mu, giving f=C cosh(kx)+D cos(kx)+E.
Its derivative boundary condition, zero mean and value boundary condition
give respectively D=C sinh(z)/sin(z), E=-2C sinh(z)/z and

 F(z)=coth(z)+cot(z)-2/z=0.

If sin(z)=0 the same three conditions allow only the trivial solution.
For 0<z<pi, the convergent partial fractions give
 F(z)=4z^3 sum_{n>=1}1/(z^4-n^4 pi^4)<0.
For pi<z<=z_o<3pi/2, cot(z)>0 and coth(z)>1>2/z, so F(z)>0.
No even constrained eigenvalue is below the first odd one. Zero eigenvalue
is excluded by coercivity. The odd minimizer therefore supplies the sharp
constant beta2=2z_o. No unallowed projection is used.

Using the ordinary Dirichlet bound ||f'||^2>=(pi/T)^2||f||^2 in (2), then
(1), gives

 R4=||A''||^2/||A||^2
 >= exp(-2aT)[(beta2/T)^4+2a^2(pi/T)^2+a^4].                    (3)

For C=(Gamma/2)^2+a^2-4g^2>=0, the exact exposure functional and
||A'||^2/||A||^2>=(pi/T)^2 yield

 IB/N >= {exp(-2aT)[(beta2/T)^4+2a^2(pi/T)^2+a^4]
           +C(pi/T)^2+[(Gamma/2)a+2g^2]^2}/(chi^2g^2Gamma).

At g1,Gamma4,chi2,T8,a5e-5 this is .3080159593709674. It lower-bounds
the exact-output family; adding force caps cannot invalidate a lower bound
obtained by relaxing those caps. All weighted inequalities have been
checked in the correct direction. The numerical value stands.

2. Complete finite-strength binned instrument and a norm guarantee

Let P_+,P_- be the two parity projectors, q the symmetric report error,
delta=exp(-2N), and gamma=delta/2. The lossless-auxiliary main instrument is

 M0_+(rho)=(1-q)P_+rhoP_+ + qP_-rhoP_-
             +gamma(P_+rhoP_-+P_-rhoP_+),
 M0_-(rho)=qP_+rhoP_+ +(1-q)P_-rhoP_-
             +gamma(P_+rhoP_-+P_-rhoP_+).                       (4)

Here output includes the classical outcome register. The cross-parity
factor delta includes both detected and missed main photons; it is not
exp(-2 eta N). These expressions follow directly from the exact root Gram
instrument and therefore include arbitrary input/reference entanglement.

The calibrated auxiliary channel Lambda is an independent CPTP Schur
channel on the data, induced by the two hidden coherent fields and the
known local phase correction. The actual instrument is M=Lambda o M0.
On parity-diagonal inputs it is exactly a local Z2 dephasing channel D_pZ:

 Lambda o P = D_pZ o P,
 pZ=[1-exp(-2(kb+kc)IB)]/2,
 P_r(rho)=P_r rho P_r.                                       (5)

Let C_q flip the classical report with probability q. Dropping only the
cross-parity term in (4) gives C_q o P. The difference map has diamond norm
delta: the off-parity projection is (Id-Ad_S)/2, of diamond norm one, and
the two classical blocks each have coefficient delta/2. CPTP contraction
therefore yields the complete, dimension-independent bound

 (1/2)||M - D_pZ o C_q o P||_diamond <= delta/2.                (6)

A sharper exact norm for M0 versus the ideal instrument P is available.
For any pure input including an arbitrary reference, write
 |Psi>=sqrt(t)|Psi_+>+sqrt(1-t)|Psi_->.
The two parity components are orthogonal. Each classical outcome block
of (M0-P)(|Psi><Psi|) is a two-by-two Hermitian matrix, with diagonal
entries (-qt,q(1-t)) or their negatives and off-diagonal gamma sqrt(t(1-t)).
Each has trace norm sqrt(q^2+delta^2 t(1-t)). Maximizing at t=1/2 gives

 (1/2)||M0-P||_diamond = sqrt(q^2+delta^2/4),
 (1/2)||M-P||_diamond <= pZ+sqrt(q^2+delta^2/4).                (7)

Equations (6)-(7) distinguish reported-bit error from the complete operation.
In a code where Z2 is correctable modulo the measured gauge, D_pZ is in
that correctable algebra. That does not provide a physical recovery or
guarantee that a noisy syndrome schedule makes a correct recovery decision.

3. Exact CP mixture: a stronger connection to stochastic location faults

Root's proposed ideal-instrument weight is exactly valid. For finite N,
q>0. Define

 e=q+gamma^2/q,       p=1-e.

The physical positivity condition gamma^2<=q(1-q) guarantees 0<=p<=1.
Define unnormalized bad-branch Kraus operators

 Kbad_+=(gamma/sqrt(q))P_+ +sqrt(q)P_-,
 Kbad_-=sqrt(q)P_+ +(gamma/sqrt(q))P_-.

Their sum satisfies sum_r Kbad_r^dag Kbad_r=e I, and direct expansion gives

 M0_r(rho)=p P_r rho P_r +Kbad_r rho Kbad_r^dag.                (8)

Therefore M0=p P+e B, where B is a normalized CPTP instrument. The mixture
weight is input independent. Equivalently, subtracting p times the ideal
outcome-r projection leaves the positive rank-one parity coefficient matrix
[[gamma^2/q,gamma],[gamma,q]], with the diagonal entries reversed for r=-.
The physical four-dimensional Gram matrix repeats these two parity blocks;
positivity is equivalent to this two-dimensional coefficient positivity.

This p is the largest uniform ideal weight that can be subtracted from the
main instrument: the same two-by-two determinant gives p<=1-q-gamma^2/q.

Including the actual auxiliary loss, (5) and (8) yield the exact mixture

 M = p(1-pZ) P + p pZ (Ad_Z2 o P) + e (Lambda o B).            (9)

The latter two terms form a normalized bad instrument of total weight

 e_total=1-p(1-pZ)=e+pZ-e pZ.                                 (10)

Every Kraus operator stays in Alg(Z1,Z2). The CP mixture supplies an exact
stochastic-location model for this calibrated static-Z cold apparatus,
including finite measurement strength; the bad branch need not be observed
or flagged. Fresh modes and appropriate bath/control independence are
required to use independent location mixtures over repeated hardware uses.
Unmodeled thermal noise, parameter errors, actual midpulse X/T1 faults and
shared apparatus memory are not automatically included in e_total.
