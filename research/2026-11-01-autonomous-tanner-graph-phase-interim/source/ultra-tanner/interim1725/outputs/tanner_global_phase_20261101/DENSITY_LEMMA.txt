QUANTITATIVE LOCAL DESCENT FOR THE QUANTUM TANNER POTENTIAL
1 November 2026. Solo derivation; independent audit and priority review pending.

Purpose
-------
The Gu--Pattison--Tang decoder proves existence of an improving local move.
An autonomous process needs a RATE: enough local moves must be available
simultaneously. This derives that stronger statement by allowing active
vertices as exceptions in the local-minimum argument. The new density
claim is not attributed to the source's sequential runtime theorem.

Inputs and notation
--------------------
Use the double-covered left-right Cayley Tanner construction of
arXiv:2206.06557v1, with |V_0|=|V_1|=m, degree parameter Delta,
n=Delta^2 m/2 data faces and c=Delta^2 faces in every local view Q(v).
Every face belongs to two V_0 and two V_1 views. The row/column incidence
has the total-no-conjugacy property. We use these quantitative mixing bounds:

  G_1^square: degree Delta^2, nonconstant operator norm <=4 Delta;
  V_0--V_1 incidence: degree 2 Delta, nonconstant singular norm <=4 sqrt(Delta).

In particular the BIPARTITE bound is
 E(W,Y)<= (2 Delta/m)|W||Y|+4 sqrt(Delta) sqrt(|W||Y|).
The coefficient here uses m per side. We do not use a non-bipartite mixing
formula that forgets the negative trivial eigenvalue of the double cover.
These bounds follow respectively from the product and sum of the commuting
left/right Ramanujan averaging operators, on nonconstant functions.

Let C_A,C_B and their duals have minimum distance >=delta Delta. Both relevant
dual tensor codes are Delta^(3/2+epsilon')-robust, with
0<epsilon<epsilon'<1/28. Their corresponding product-code distance bound is
the standard robust tensor inequality of the source's Proposition 6.
The primary construction supplies an infinite positive-rate family with
distance d>=d_0 n>0. One may conservatively take
d_0=delta/(4 Delta^(3/2+epsilon')). Choose Delta sufficiently large below.

Work first with Z errors, detected by the V_1 X checks. For e subset Q set

 U_v(e)=distance(e|Q(v), C_1^perp),       U(e)=sum_(v in V_1) U_v(e).

This is a nonnegative integer function of the syndrome. For each v choose
any nearest local word c_v, put R_v^+=e_v+c_v, R=union_v R_v^+, and
y=e\R. Let Y={v:c_v!=0}. A vertex a in V_0 union V_1 is ACTIVE when some
F subset Q(a) strictly reduces U. Write A for that set and M=|A|.

Define
 alpha=min(d_0/4, delta/(16 Delta^(3/2-epsilon))),
 a_0=delta^2/8192,        B_0=1280/a_0.

Sufficient additional large-Delta conditions are

 Delta^(1+2epsilon)>=258,
 Delta^(2epsilon)>=max(32/a_0,3 delta B_0),
 Delta^(1/2-epsilon)>100/(a_0 delta^2),
 Delta^(epsilon'-epsilon)>=10/delta,
 Delta^(1/2-epsilon')>=2/delta^2.                         (D1)

These also imply the smaller dense-edge/mixing inequalities used below.
All exponents are positive, so the source's sufficiently large base-code
existence can be combined with (D1), keeping Delta fixed as n grows.

Claim
------
For every error reduced modulo Z stabilizers with |e|<=alpha n,

             M >= U(e)/(2 Delta^(3+2epsilon)).            (D2)

All predicates and the right side are syndrome functions. Thus a syndrome
admitting such a representative satisfies the same inequality, independently
of its Pauli representation. The X-sector statement uses the dual local codes.

1. Local facts require only LOCAL stalling
------------------------------------------
If a face lies in two R_v^+, flipping it decreases two local distances by
one each. Every center containing that face is therefore active. In a
nonactive view Q(a), the R edges have unique orientations: no face there
belongs to both endpoint corrections.

Take any F subset R intersect Q(a) with a nonactive. Keeping the old nearest
words as candidates, its changes in local residual weights sum to zero:
each selected edge is removed once and added once. New minimum distances
therefore have total at most U(e). Local stalling gives the reverse
inequality, so equality holds term by term. The old c_v remain valid nearest
words, the selected orientations reverse, and the union R stays unchanged.
Duplicates elsewhere are irrelevant. This is a LOCAL version of R-flipping.

For a nonactive a in V_1 this also gives the low-overlap condition

       |R intersect Q(a) intersect c|<=|c|/2
                  for every c in C_1^perp.               (D3)

Indeed first flip R_a^+ to make that residual empty; the change is neutral
by the preceding argument. If a word c has more than half its support in
R intersect Q(a), flipping those incident incoming residual edges removes
that many errors from the other local residuals. At a, using c as a new
candidate costs strictly less than that amount. The combined flip lies
inside Q(a) and improves U, a contradiction. Only this view was required
to be stalled, not all views or the subsequent intermediate configuration.

For any nearest-word choices, |c_v|<=2|e_v|. Thus

 |Y|<=4|e|/(delta Delta)<=m/(8 Delta^(1/2-epsilon)).        (D4)

Every y-face has both V_1 endpoints in Y. At a nonactive v in Y, (D3) and
c_v subset y_v union R_v imply |y_v|>=|c_v|/2>=delta Delta/2.

2. Few active vertices would leave many regular local words
------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose Y is nonempty and, for a contradiction,

                  M<=|Y|/Delta^(1+2epsilon).              (D5)

Let Y_e contain the vertices of Y whose y-degree exceeds
(1/2)Delta^(3/2+epsilon). From (D4) and the square-graph mixing bound,

 (1/2)Delta^(3/2+epsilon)|Y_e|
 <=(1/8)Delta^(3/2+epsilon)|Y_e|+4Delta sqrt(|Y_e||Y|).

Consequently |Y_e|/|Y|<128/Delta^(1+2epsilon). Put
B=Y_e union (Y intersect A). Equations (D1),(D5) give

 |B|<=129 |Y|/Delta^(1+2epsilon),
 |Y_g|>=|Y|/2,             Y_g:=Y\B.                     (D6)

At v in Y_g, (D3) implies |c_v|<=2|y_v|<=Delta^(3/2+epsilon).
Tensor robustness localizes c_v to at most t=Delta^(1/2+epsilon)/delta
rows and columns. Its supported decomposition into row and column codewords
shows that each row/column is within distance t of its base code and at
least one is within t of a NONZERO base word. This is precisely the local
robust tensor implication used in the source's Lemma 30; its hypotheses
hold here at each v in Y_g despite active vertices elsewhere.

Call the corresponding V_1--V_0 edge dense if its slice in c_v has weight
at least delta Delta-t, and let W be the V_0 endpoints of dense edges from
Y_g. Each v in Y_g has one such edge. Low overlap at v applied to the nearby
base codeword gives at least delta Delta/2-t y-faces along that slice.
Their other V_1 endpoints are in Y. Hence each w in W has at least
delta Delta/4 neighbors in Y, using (D1).

The correctly normalized bipartite mixing bound and (D4) now give

 |W|<=1024 |Y|/(delta^2 Delta),    |W|>=|Y|/(4Delta).       (D7)

For the upper bound, absorb (2Delta/m)|Y|<=delta Delta/8 and square
(delta Delta/8)|W|<=4 sqrt(Delta) sqrt(|W||Y|). The lower bound uses
|Y_g|>=|Y|/2 and maximum incidence degree 2Delta.

The number of dense edges is at least |Y_g|, so their mean degree on W
is at least delta^2 Delta/2048=4a_0 Delta. At least a_0|W|/2 vertices
therefore have >=a_0 Delta dense neighbors.

The same bipartite mixing bound, (D4),(D6),(D7), and sqrt(129)<12 give

 |E(W,B)|/|W|
 <=(258/8)Delta^(-1/2-epsilon)+8sqrt(129)Delta^(1/2-epsilon)
 <=160 Delta^(1/2-epsilon).                              (D8)

At most a_0|W|/8 vertices have more than B_0 Delta^(1/2-epsilon) neighbors
in B. Also M/|W|<=4Delta^(-2epsilon)<=a_0/8. Remove those two sets from
the dense-neighbor set. Some w remains with ALL these properties:

 w is nonactive; it has >=a_0 Delta dense neighbors from Y_g;
 it has <=B_0 Delta^(1/2-epsilon) neighbors in B.           (D9)

3. The local contradiction still works with B as the exceptional set
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Here are the needed inequalities explicitly, so an extension of the
source's globally stalled case is not merely assumed.

Inside Q(w), R has no duplicated face because w is nonactive. A face of
R_0:=R intersect Q(w) belongs to exactly one neighboring c_v; partition
R_0=R_row disjoint-union R_col accordingly. Let y_0=y intersect Q(w).
For every row and column, respectively,

 y_0[row] disjoint-union R_row[row]=c_v[slice],
 y_0[col] disjoint-union R_col[col]=c_v[slice].

These follow directly from e_v+R_v^+=c_v and the unique orientation on
Q(w). If c_v=0, the corresponding residual row/column is empty.

Flip e intersect R_0. Local R-flipping makes this a neutral move, retains
all nearest c_v, and gives a new error tilde-e with tilde-e_0=y_0. Its
neighboring minimum residuals in the shared slices are exactly R_row
and R_col. At a good neighboring v in Y\B, flipping the y_0 slice changes
its local potential by at most -|R_slice|+t, by using the nearby base
codeword as a candidate. A bad neighbor costs at most Delta. Since the
combined change is contained in Q(w), it cannot improve U. Therefore

 |R_0|<=2Delta t+3B_0 Delta^(3/2-epsilon)
       <=3Delta^(3/2+epsilon)/delta.                     (D10)

The factor three accounts for the possible bad-slice potential cost and
the omitted row/column residuals. The second inequality uses (D1).
Similarly, the distances of y_0 to the row and column codes sum to at most

                  10Delta^(3/2+epsilon)/delta.            (D11)

Indeed each direction costs at most Delta t, the bad slices cost
B_0 Delta^(3/2-epsilon), and replacing y_0+R_row or y_0+R_col by y_0 costs
at most (D10). Each direction is bounded by 5Delta^(3/2+epsilon)/delta.

At least half the a_0 Delta dense slices are rows or half are columns.
They are all good slices by construction, each of weight >=delta Delta-t.
Subtracting (D10) and using (D1) gives

                       |y_0|>=a_0 delta Delta^2/4.        (D12)

By (D1), (D11) is below the robust tensor range and that range is below
delta^2 Delta^2/2. The robust tensor inequality gives a product codeword
c_0 in C_A tensor C_B at distance <=15Delta^(3/2+epsilon)/delta from y_0.
It is a Z stabilizer supported in Q(w). Thus

 |e_0+c_0|<=18Delta^(3/2+epsilon)/delta
             <a_0 delta Delta^2/4<=|e_0|,

contradicting that e was reduced modulo stabilizers. This disproves (D5).

4. Convert the count to potential drift
----------------------------------------
Let D be the set of duplicated R-faces. Each endpoint of a duplicated face
is active, so 2|D|<=c|A intersect V_1|. Every nonduplicated R-face has an
endpoint in Y: if both nearest words are zero, its error value would put
it in both endpoint corrections. Therefore

 U=|R|+|D|<=c|Y|+2|D|<=c(|Y|+M).

If Y=0 this already gives M>=U/c. Otherwise the disproved (D5) gives
|Y|<M Delta^(1+2epsilon), and (D2) follows.

5. Correctability and confinement at a fixed fraction of n
-----------------------------------------------------------
Each local move writes <=c data faces, U<=2|e|, and every move lowers the
integer U by >=1. Starting with |e|<=alpha n/(1+2c), any descent sequence
has <=U(e) steps and stays at actual weight <=|e|+c U(e)<=alpha n. The
density lemma keeps supplying a move until U=0. The final zero-syndrome
error has weight <d, hence is a stabilizer. In particular, for the reduced
weight w in this range,

                            w<=c U.                    (D13)

Apply independently to the CSS error components. For their summed reduced
weight w_X+w_Z within this range, the TOTAL potential U_X+U_Z has
U>= (w_X+w_Z)/c, and the TOTAL number of eligible centers is at least
U/(2Delta^(3+2epsilon)). This is a statement about the full syndrome, with
no assumption that its quantum state or bath factors between sectors.

Verification boundary
-----------------------
local_density_controls.py exhausts the localized R-flipping and low-overlap
implications on several small linear factor systems and a 10-qubit genuine
cyclic Tanner CSS complex, including locally stalled views in globally
nonstalled configurations. It verifies the added parameter inequalities
with exact rationals. These are controls of the new reasoning. They neither
replace this all-size argument nor acquire the source's very large robust
base codes. The latter existence and spectral/robust tensor inputs remain
explicit primary mathematical imports.
