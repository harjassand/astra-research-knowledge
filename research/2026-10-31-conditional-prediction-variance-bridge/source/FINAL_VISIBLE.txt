The chosen opening: quantum prediction from conditional samples

I developed a general way to use an acquired conditional sampler directly for quantum prediction. The strongest result is a theorem: for a state with known phases, prefix-conditional samples suffice to estimate efficiently described sparse quantum observables with essentially the usual inverse-square Monte Carlo accuracy dependence, while charging the conditional computation.

The key construction is an importance-sampling estimator whose correction variance decreases with the squared amplitude error. That makes increasingly accurate—and increasingly expensive—conditional approximations useful without paying their full cost on every observable sample.

This gives the existing machinery a broader purpose. It can supply nonlocal coherent observables, reduced-state moments, conditional-model comparisons, and some entanglement witnesses through classical computation. The clean coherent interface remains available for outputs that require a quantum state or coherent composition.

I completed a finite implementation that certifies entanglement of its specified pure state at 95% conditional confidence. The constants are substantial: it consumed 931,905,440 conditional draws, and direct computation wins decisively on that small input. The earned advance is the reusable theorem and its verified output interface; a broad practical advantage remains unestablished.

5. What survived the strongest alternatives

The comparison became narrower and more credible as the work progressed.

Exact amplitude access already solves much of this output problem. For a normalized amplitude vector and a permutation, the ordinary amplitude-ratio estimator has second moment at most one. When a tensor representation, analytic formula or exact table makes amplitudes cheap, that method can be preferable.

Conditional amplitude-ratio estimation already exists. Chowdhury and colleagues use conditional auxiliary-variable sampling for neural quantum states, with a correction for finite-inner-sample square-root bias. Their model supplies a joint energy and clamped auxiliary conditionals, which are stronger primitives than an arbitrary next-bit prefix source. This work deserves direct credit. 
arxiv.org

Standard antithetic nested MLMC can already attain inverse-square accuracy cost under suitable range bounds. If the conditional ratio variable stays in a known interval bounded away from zero, the square-root functional is smooth, and the correction variance can decrease faster than the inner sampling cost grows. Its range, mixing and acquisition constants must be paid. 
people.maths.ox.ac.uk

The scalar square-root singularity is not an obstruction by itself. Even a one-bit plug-in estimator has uniform inverse-square sample complexity. I retained that counterexample and a randomized-series alternative. Therefore, an

The defensible general result is the bridge for arbitrary prefix-only sources and sparse/permutation outputs, with no minimum-probability or bounded-amplitude-ratio promise. Searches did not establish historical priority for this particular combination, and no best-method separation is claimed.

Other failures remain explicit:

Sparse-observable prediction does not simulate arbitrary adaptive quantum measurements.

Preserved result and remaining burden

The organizing principle earned here is that a validated conditional sampler can serve as a computational representation for quantum prediction. The receiver can spend accuracy where a particular observable needs it, with a proved variance law and complete conditional-access accounting.

The completed purity run demonstrates one such output through the full finite implementation.

The remaining scientific burden is a physically interesting interacting regime where the acquired source’s actual costs and a resolved nonlocal signal make this receiver competitive with informed direct methods. Positive competition, setup and signal resolution still determine that outcome.
