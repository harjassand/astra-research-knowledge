## The result: a lifetime guarantee that does not require identifying the hidden modes

I developed a concrete protocol for **unknown reciprocal, overdamped, linear Gaussian thermal systems**. It learns consequential deadline risks and mean time to a wrong decision from ordinary port measurements, without reconstructing the microscopic state, identifying transition support, counting internal modes, or knowing the slowest relaxation time.

The guarantee remains valid for a continuum of hidden modes with **infinite integrated memory**. That is the main advance in this run. Its present status is an exact mathematical construction with executable synthetic evidence. Physical validation and historical novelty remain open.

### 1. The question I chose

> **Can we change the input and readout so that every unobserved internal mode has a bounded effect on the consequential outcome, and then learn only the ordinary quantities that remain?**

I chose an actual functional outcome: the first wrong decision in a clocked stream of binary symbols passing through an inaccessible dissipative component. This supports both a lifetime and a deadline-risk question.

The hidden component is represented by its port response

\[
k(t)=\int_{[0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda t}\,\mu(d\lambda),
\qquad \mu\ge0.
\]

Reciprocal linear storage and dissipation earn this positive relaxation representation through the spectral theorem. The measure can contain arbitrarily many modes, a continuum extending to zero, or a static mode.

The learner never reconstructs \(\mu\). It acquires two scalars:

1. The variance \(v\) of a voltage difference across a chosen observation window.
2. The useful signal \(S\) produced by a finite charge pulse in that window.

Those two numbers alone would usually be insufficient. **The intervention supplies the missing bounds.**

This physical family is a substantive restriction. Gaussianity and the global positive relaxation law must be warranted by the architecture and bath model; finite passive observations cannot establish them.

## 2. The mechanism: difference the observation and return the charge

At the beginning of a window, read a reference voltage. Inject a finite current pulse. Read again after time \(h\), and decide from the difference.

Then return the injected charge using a prescribed sequence. For example:

\[
(+Q,-Q)
\quad\text{or}\quad
(+Q,-2Q,+Q).
\]

The first decision occurs before the return pulses. Thus the useful signal survives, while the later pulses control the state left behind.

Two exact bounds make the representation useful.

### Hidden noise dependence becomes uniformly bounded

Let consecutive decision windows start \(mh\) apart. For the stationary thermal component, the correlation matrix \(R_N\) of any \(N\) difference readings satisfies

\[
\boxed{
(1-1/m)I\preceq R_N\preceq(1+1/m)I .
}
\]

The proof sums the positive spectral contributions directly. Its bound contains neither the number of decisions nor the slowest relaxation time.

Slow modes need not disappear or equilibrate. Their effect on the chosen difference readings is bounded.

### Hidden signal memory becomes uniformly bounded

An order-\(p\) return sequence uses the binomial coefficients

\[
(-1)^j\binom pj,\qquad j=0,\ldots,p.
\]

For every previous bit history, every admissible hidden spectrum, and a finite positive pulse shape,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\lvert\text{interference from previous packets}\rvert}
     {\text{desired signal}}
\le
\binom{m}{p+1}^{-1}.
}
\]

This follows from retaining one nonnegative term in a binomial identity. Higher return order suppresses interference more strongly, while increasing charge, peak current, and energy.

The charge return also repairs an important failed route. **Differencing alone can hide unbounded stored charge.** An almost static mode can accumulate energy while successive differences remain apparently well behaved. Balanced returns give a uniform bound on the deterministic driven state.

### These bounds give every deadline and the mean lifetime

The probability step uses established Gaussian product inequalities, specifically the covariance-order theorem of Chen, Dafnis and Paouris. The new work here is acquiring the physical and statistical conditions needed to use that theorem without a supplied microscopic model. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

After accounting for calibration, interference, preparation, and retained-record conditioning, the construction produces two rates, \(\lambda_{\rm lo}\) and \(\lambda_{\rm hi}\), such that

\[
1-e^{-N\lambda_{\rm lo}}
\le
\Pr(\text{first error by decision }N)
\le
1-e^{-N\lambda_{\rm hi}}
\]

for **every** \(N\), and

\[
\frac{1}{1-e^{-\lambda_{\rm hi}}}
\le
\mathbb E[\text{first-error index}]
\le
\frac{1}{1-e^{-\lambda_{\rm lo}}}.
\]

The relative width is bounded independently of \(N\). This does not assume independent errors or an exactly geometric lifetime.

## 3. The completed experiment

The executable source was

\[
k(t)=\frac{1}{1+t}
=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}e^{-\lambda}\,d\lambda.
\]

It has a continuous spectrum reaching arbitrarily slow modes, and

\[
\int_0^\infty k(t)\,dt=\infty.
\]

The learner received quantized differences and commanded inputs. The source formula was used separately to generate and audit the experiment.

The completed run used a **non-equilibrium, nonzero initial state**, known only through an energy bound, and a calibration failure budget of \(10^{-12}\). It acquired 5,242,880 ordinary readings. No \(10^{15}\)-decision future was simulated.

For three-pulse packets and the same decision period \(32h\):

| Quantity | Charge \(Q=12\) | Charge \(Q=15.2\) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean first-error index | \(6.11\)–\(9.79\times10^9\) | \(1.21\)–\(2.44\times10^{15}\) |
| Risk by \(10^{12}\) decisions | Approximately one | \(4.11\times10^{-4}\)–\(8.25\times10^{-4}\) |
| Absolute charge per packet | 48 | 60.8 |
| Peak rectangular current, in model units | 120 | 152 |

The improved design’s mean lower bound exceeds the baseline’s mean upper bound by **123,944 times**. The increased signal costs 26.7% more pulse charge; the decision rate is unchanged. Full-precision calculations are in :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}[port_results.json](producer-workspace/port_results.json).

The uncertainty remains visible. For the improved design, the risk by \(10^{15}\) decisions lies between approximately **0.337 and 0.562**. The result is an informative bracket across the lifetime scale.

A separate moderate-risk diagnostic used 100,000 synthetic trajectories. Its observed failure fraction, \(0.32899\), lay inside the analytical interval \([0.30960,0.34991]\). That checks the implementation at observable probabilities; it does not experimentally establish an extreme physical tail.

## 4. Preparation and acquisition are now part of the theorem

Several initially convenient assumptions did not survive scrutiny.

**Equilibrium preparation was removed.** An unknown initial state with a globally valid, pathwise energy bound \(E_0\) produces a bounded transient in the difference readings. Averaging over an ordinary calibration record gives a stronger bound than waiting for every hidden mode to relax.

That energy bound still needs a physical basis. A bound only on port voltage is insufficient. It must cover the relevant stored energy through manufacturing constraints or complete preparation-energy accounting. This is a coarse global requirement, rather than access to the complete microscopic state.

**Adaptive design on the same device required another repair.** Calibration readings are correlated with future noise. Choosing a design from them does not justify an unconditional Gaussian calculation. I derived a conditional-record bound and included a paid guard period before deployment.

**Quantized records required a mixture argument.** The future law conditional on a quantized record is generally a mixture of Gaussian conditional laws. The bounds hold uniformly over compatible exact records, so they survive averaging over that mixture.

These repairs have substantial costs:

- Ordinary observation allocation: \(3.3554\times10^8h\).
- Guard period: \(2.0681\times10^{10}h\).
- First deployment start: \(2.1017\times10^{10}h\).
- The guard occupies about **98.4% of startup time**.

No hardware timescale has been assigned to \(h\). Source bandwidth, readout aperture, thermal backaction, and heat removal still require engineering qualification.

The confidence interpretation also matters: the future risk bounds hold on the calibration coverage event. **An unconditional operational guarantee must include the \(10^{-12}\) calibration failure budget.**

## 5. A final failure exposed the right source specification

Exact charge returns cannot simply be replaced by “small error per pulse.” A systematic imbalance accumulates on a static mode for an arbitrarily long mission.

I proved a useful repair. If the **cumulative delivered-charge error** remains bounded by \(r\), then, independently of the hidden spectrum,

\[
\|\text{additional signal state}\|
\le 2r\sqrt{k(0)},
\]

and each differential readout incurs at most

\[
\boxed{4rk(0)\le 4r/C_p}
\]

additional error, where \(C_p\) is a known port capacitance.

This gives a concrete specification for a charge-tracking source. It can tolerate causal, adaptive, non-Gaussian source error after calibration, because the proof uses pathwise bounds around the thermal process.

The tolerance calculation is executable. A cumulative radius \(r=10^{-6}\) barely changes the demonstrated risk interval; \(r=10^{-3}\) makes the design miss the illustrative deadline-risk target. A physical source maintaining that contract was **not** built. Errors occurring during calibration require further analysis.

## 6. The separately constructed nonlinear route

A separately briefed collaborator, without my Gaussian candidate, found a different representation.

For an unknown nonlinear passive capacitor network, positive stored charge obeys

\[
Q_+(t)\le Q_+(0)+\text{injected positive charge}.
\]

If every overvoltage trip requires at least a known amount of positive charge, this gives an **input-only event containing every possible trip**, including hidden pathways.

One can sample input schedules conditioned on that event and measure an ordinary conditional trip probability. Two physically realizable, imperfect reset procedures bracket every service starting state. Monotonicity then gives lifetime bounds without assuming identical resets or a Markov model of the observed variables.

Its completed synthetic test certified:

- At least a **259-fold** mean-lifetime improvement.
- Retention of **80%** of the input charge.
- About **55%** of each surviving cycle spent resetting.

The requirement is different and substantial: a common reset bus must physically reach the hidden storage, with a warranted decay rate. A loose charge cover can leave the conditional failure rare, destroying the acceleration.

The statistical estimator is conventional conditional importance sampling. **Given the same physical cover, the conventional method exactly ties.** The value of the construction is earning the complete cover and the preparation comparison.

## 7. What the comparison and failures establish

Correlated double sampling is established engineering. Its variance reduction, timing, bandwidth, and readout-design tradeoffs were studied long before this work. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"} Accelerated disturbance testing and certified importance sampling are also strong existing approaches to rare-event evaluation. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

The strongest matched conventional analyst can use the same physical inequalities and Gaussian theorem. I have **not** demonstrated superiority over that fully informed comparator, nor established priority for the complete protocol.

Current Astra material at [revision `26eeb009`](https://github.com/harjassand/astra-research-knowledge/tree/26eeb0093987ab60313707097acd4e2c93a46de2) materially shaped the construction. The governing N1069, N808, and N1338 sources forced attention to hidden slow spectral mass, same-temperature damping, global versus finite-band laws, initialization, and conditioning on retained records. Exact paths and hashes are preserved in the package.

The hidden-catastrophe impossibility retains its actual scope. An executable counterexample constructs two processes with the **same exponential covariance and matching linear response**, yet their relevant difference-tail probabilities are approximately

\[
6.22\times10^{-16}
\quad\text{and}\quad
6.32\times10^{-7}.
\]

Thus ordinary covariance and response measurements alone cannot warrant the far tail. Other retained failures show that general Gaussian filtering is too broad, finite spectral readings do not bound hidden DC memory, and unbalanced actuation can conceal growing energy. These are in :chatgpt-content-reference{index="9"}[failure_results.json](producer-workspace/failure_results.json).

**What was achieved is a constructive removal of microscopic identification for a precisely declared, arbitrarily large hidden family.** The operational representation is acquired from ordinary measurements, while physical intervention bounds what remains unidentified. The nonlinear construction shows a second way to earn that reduction.

The next scientific bottleneck is now concrete: engineer and validate the global physical, energy, and source contracts on an uncharacterized device, then seek comparable complete bounds in nonlinear systems with weaker reset access. A general or civilisation-changing physical capability remains to be earned.
