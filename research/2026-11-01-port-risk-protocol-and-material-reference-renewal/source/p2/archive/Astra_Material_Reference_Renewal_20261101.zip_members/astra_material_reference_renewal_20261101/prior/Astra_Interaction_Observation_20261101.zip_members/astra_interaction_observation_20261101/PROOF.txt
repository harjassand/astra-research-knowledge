STATUS AND CHOSEN QUESTION
=========================
The preceding returning-error sensor, its finite meter, complete estimator,
code and useful failures are preserved without modification. They eliminate a
static unknown noise-axis nuisance in their stated model. The two-rate ordinary
QEC competitor also earns quadratic information, so that scaling is not a basis
for selecting the next capability.

The next objective is a deterministic entangling gate on arbitrary unknown data,
including its correlations with an untouched reference, through a warm mediator
that continues exchanging noise with its environment throughout the interaction.
The observer and its noise are physical operations. Initial hot-state immunity,
small conditional classical variance, and high local sensing Fisher information
are not substitutes for that complete quantum-channel objective.

An independent collaborator built the strongest existing route before the main
construction: geometric force loops; dissipation-compensating/polychromatic
pulses; environmental quantum erasure and optimized Gaussian feedforward;
finite-efficiency circuit-QED trajectory reversal; physical thermal-output
unravelling; and encoded finite-angle gates. These sources already supply many
tempting claims. In particular, hot-noise saturation under actual environmental
access is established. This work does not claim to invent it.

The surviving foundational question is narrower and sharper:
Can measuring a separate cold output of a continuously hot mediator preserve
the desired interaction while removing the hot noise, at bounded force, time,
and observer cost? What is the exact optimal remaining channel when the gate
itself is constrained?

Results proved here:
1. The exact physical Gaussian measurement channel includes a coherent gate-phase
   shift as well as a conditional-variance reduction.
2. A necessary-and-sufficient finite-dimensional linear criterion tests whether
   a specified interaction can survive removal of all leading thermal noise.
3. For a Markov oscillator with inaccessible continuing hot input and observation
   of a separate cold output, preserving phase P costs J >= V_h kappa_h P^2/E.
   Here J multiplies a squared charge gap in the dephasing exponent, and E is
   integrated squared coupling, not joules. No noise-only estimator evades this.
4. The thermal component of that lower bound is sharp. A finite smooth force and
   explicitly synthesized homodyne filter attain it, while cold/detector costs
   remain explicit. The complete optimum is a constrained quadratic problem.
5. Native hot-output access supplies an established, different-resource escape;
   finite-angle encoded gates supply another. Neither is assumed free.

These are conditional mathematical/physical-model results, independently checked.
Historical priority of the particular bound and synthesis is unestablished.
No experiment, universal no-go theorem, fault-tolerance threshold, or practical
superiority over all conventional gates is claimed.

1. A RETAINED FAILURE: THE SENSOR IS NOT AN UNKNOWN-INTERACTION COMPUTER
=====================================================================
Let J_theta=cos(theta)X+sin(theta)Y, J_theta^2=I, and H=Z tensor B with B an
unknown bounded operator on a quantum target. On a returning error trajectory,
the noise-axis factors cancel, but the output is exp[-i A(T) Z tensor B], where
A(T)=integral_0^T (-1)^N(t) dt. This identity includes entangled target inputs.

An exact event record permits a first-passage snapshot: stop when A(t)=a>0.
The crossing occurs in even parity and gives exactly exp[-ia Z tensor B].
If even/odd rates are alpha,beta with beta>alpha, the mean stopping time is
a(alpha+beta)/(beta-alpha). A composable gate additionally requires a physical
way to stop/decouple the unknown interaction or transfer the state at that time.

Even perfect parity samples at spacing Delta leave hidden dwell times. For a
single interval with changed endpoint parity and spectral gap b, the conditional
coherence is

 c_odd(b)=gamma sinh[Delta sqrt(gamma^2-b^2)] /
          [sqrt(gamma^2-b^2) sinh(gamma Delta)],

with its continuous/sine continuation. Two such intervals return parity but
retain only |c_odd(b)|^2<1 when b is nonzero. No future parity-only Markov record
reveals those past hidden times. In the independent check at gamma=1, Delta=.6,
b=.8, the modulus is .92720819. For noncommuting target self-Hamiltonian H0,
equal signed clocks do not even imply equal time-ordered quantum operations.

This is not a defect in the completed local sensor theorem. It is the reason to
change the capability rather than relabel its Fisher information as coherent
quantum protection. Full derivations and executable examples are in the retained
warm_coherent_clock supplement.

2. STRONG CONVENTIONAL GATE AND ITS REAL FAILURE
==============================================
Use S=Z_1+Z_2 and a harmonic mediator a, in a rotating frame:

 H(t)=S[g(t)a^dagger+g(t)^*a].

The conditional mean beta solves

 beta_dot=-(kappa/2)beta-i g,  beta(0)=beta(T)=0.

A prescribed closed smooth beta is implemented by the bounded force
g=i(beta_dot+kappa beta/2). The geometric gate phase is

 Phi=-Re integral g beta^* dt = Im integral beta^* beta_dot dt.

For S=Z1+Z2, exp(i Phi S^2) equals a global phase times exp(i 2Phi Z1 Z2).
Phi=pi/8 is a maximally entangling unitary. The mediator need not initially be
in its ground state in the lossless closed-loop construction. This is established
geometric/hot-gate physics, not a new invention here.

Closing the conditional final bus displacement does not close all emitted
environmental displacements. They retain information about S. Moreover, if the
initial bus temperature differs from its baths, emitted modes can retain the
initial bus uncertainty even though beta(T)=0. The independent passive-dilation
check exhibits this explicitly. The actual free output covariance must be used.

The continuous model studied below has one inaccessible hot input and one
accessible cold input/output port:

 da=-(kappa/2)a dt-i S g dt-sqrt(kappa_h) db_h-sqrt(kappa_c) db_c,
 db_c,out=db_c+sqrt(kappa_c)a dt,
 kappa=kappa_h+kappa_c, kappa_h>0, kappa_c>0.

The hot input quadrature variance is V_h=n_h+1/2; the cold input has V_c=1/2.
The initial bus has variance V_b. Inputs and data are initially independent;
the data may be arbitrarily entangled with an untouched reference. The hot input
continues injecting independent Markov innovations during the gate. This is
essential: a merely hot INITIAL bus, a colored/switchable bath, or access to its
output is a different problem.

Allowed correction is a phase generated by S using Gaussian measurements of the
cold physical output. For S=Z1+Z2 this is two local rotations. No independent
S^2 interaction, protected ancilla, noiseless storage, bath purification, or
non-Gaussian encoded recovery is supplied.

3. EXACT TERMINAL GAUSSIAN INSTRUMENT
===================================
This finite-mode statement also applies to controlled discretizations of the
continuous fields. Let [R_j,R_k]=i Omega_jk and W(l)=exp(i l^T R). Vacuum
quadrature covariance is I/2. Weyl multiplication is

 W(l)W(m)=exp[-i l^T Omega m/2] W(l+m).

After a scalar-force protocol, write the actual joint operation as

 U_s=exp(i Phi s^2) W(s L) U_free.

The free environment is zero-mean Gaussian with covariance V. It includes the
actual initial bus and all physical bath outputs after free evolution. It is
not an invented pair of accessible vacuum purification ports.

Let a physical Gaussian output POVM obey

 integral exp(-i t^T y) M_y dy
       =exp(-t^T N t/2) W(-C^T t).

C describes actual measured outputs/modes and N their added detector noise.
For a genuinely joint general-dyne POVM its quantum compatibility conditions
must hold. Alternatively, a chosen scalar functional k^T C R can be acquired
by one matched homodyne measurement; that does not jointly measure incompatible
quadratures. Different k then specify different homodyne profiles.

Apply exp(-i S k^T y), discard the measured environment and average its record.
Every data/reference matrix element with charge labels s,s' is multiplied by

 exp[i P(k)(s^2-s'^2)] exp[-(s-s')^2 sigma(k)^2/2],                 (3.1)

 P(k)=Phi-(1/2)L^T Omega C^T k,                                   (3.2)
 sigma(k)^2=(L-C^T k)^T V(L-C^T k)+k^T N k.                       (3.3)

Proof. In the cyclic environmental trace the three Weyl operators are
W(-s'L) W[-(s-s')C^T k] W(sL). Their sum is
(s-s')(L-C^T k). Their multiplication phase is
-(s^2-s'^2)L^T Omega C^T k/2. The Gaussian characteristic function supplies
the remaining real exponent. This derivation uses the actual POVM, feedback,
and trace. It does not assume that an unmeasured latent quadrature was supplied
as a classical record. Equation (3.1) is an entire quantum channel, not a
single-state or classical covariance calculation.

A nonzero environment mean only adds a known/unknown linear S phase, which must
be calibrated or included as an additional nuisance. Record-conditioned states
can be nonunitary; (3.1) describes the delivered, record-averaged channel. It
does not assert that a retained record is independent of the data at finite error.

The independent Fock-space calculation integrates the heterodyne POVM explicitly.
It agrees with (3.1) to 1.69e-16 in its finite-dimensional fixture. Omitting
(3.2) gives an error of .0316 in the very same coherence.

4. PHASE-PRESERVING OBSERVABILITY: A COMPLETE LINEAR CRITERION
============================================================
Suppose V(n)=n V_H+V_0 for a fixed physical readout family, V_H>=0, V_0>=0.
All matrices, modes and detector noise are fixed and finite. Let A_H be a
square root with V_H=A_H A_H^T. A bounded-gain filter can remove all leading
thermal noise while retaining a specified phase P_* precisely when there is
a finite k satisfying BOTH

 A_H^T(L-C^T k)=0,
 (1/2)L^T Omega C^T k=Phi-P_*.                                   (4.1)

Sufficiency follows immediately from (3.2)-(3.3): the n term vanishes and all
remaining noise is finite. Necessity follows from nonnegativity of the n term.
For a bounded sequence of n-dependent gains, compactness gives a convergent
subsequence satisfying the same equations. If gain must diverge, its detector
noise, bandwidth and dynamic-range cost cannot be omitted. For positive detector
noise, bounded total sigma automatically bounds gain. Singular/noiseless detector
directions require their own physical resource analysis.

The first equation alone is classical hot-noise observability. The second keeps
the required quantum interaction. This is the useful added constraint. If the
first equation has solutions but every one violates the second, successful noise
estimation is not successful gate protection.

For a discrete charge spectrum the required phase can have equivalent
representatives. Apply (4.1) to each allowed representative, including any
permitted known local correction, before declaring a physical gate impossible.

Once (4.1) is feasible, the best remaining finite noise is a linearly constrained
positive quadratic minimization using V_0 and N. If it is infeasible, the same
matrices give the best thermal coefficient at a specified retained phase. Thus
the criterion is constructive and uses actually available modes and detector
noise. It does not import quantum coherent mixing of inaccessible outputs.

5. EXACT SAME-GATE GAUSSIAN COMPARATOR
=====================================
For a fixed environment and physical detector define

 Q=CVC^T+N, b=CVL, h=(1/2)C Omega^T L,
 k_W=Q^-1 b,
 sigma_W^2=L^T V L-b^T Q^-1 b,
 P_W=Phi-h^T k_W.

Assume Q>0. If h is nonzero, the optimum among the admitted linear filters
delivering EXACTLY P_* is

 k_*=k_W+Q^-1 h (P_W-P_*)/(h^T Q^-1 h),                          (5.1)

 sigma_min^2(P_*)=sigma_W^2+(P_*-P_W)^2/(h^T Q^-1 h).             (5.2)

Proof: complete the square sigma^2=sigma_W^2+(k-k_W)^TQ(k-k_W),
and minimize that positive norm on h^T(k-k_W)=P_W-P_*. This is standard
constrained quadratic optimization. Its scientific use here is to keep the
correct quantum phase constraint supplied by the full instrument.

If h=0, feedback does not change Phi: the requested phase is feasible iff it
equals Phi. Singular Q is handled on its support with the corresponding range
condition. A noiseless zero-cost direction is not assumed physical by default.

Noise-only Wiener optimization is already strong existing mathematics. It becomes
the wrong complete objective if P_W differs from P_*. Equations (5.1)-(5.2)
are the strongest comparator within these exact operations, not a deliberately
weak baseline. No numerical nonlinearity search is needed to find it.

For S=Z1+Z2, exactly identical channels have phases P_*+m pi/2. A known allowed
collective S rotation also removes a shift pi/4, since
exp[i(pi/4)S^2]=exp[i(pi/2)S]. Thus the most complete same-gate comparator minimizes
(5.2) over P=P_*+m pi/4, charging that known local rotation. For P_*=pi/8 in
our table, P_W is nonnegative and no larger than pi/8, so +pi/8 is the best
representative (tied with -pi/8 only as P_W tends to zero). The thermal bound
uses the minimum absolute representative, still pi/8. A local/identity gate
must not be assigned an artificial nonzero entangling cost by choosing a large
equivalent phase.

6. CONTINUOUS PHYSICAL REDUCTION AND THE HOT-PORT THEOREM
=======================================================
Choose a complex homodyne weight f(t) on the cold output and the local correction
exp(+i S Y_f), where

 Y_f=integral [f(t)b_c,out^dagger(t)+f(t)^*b_c,out(t)] dt.

The sign is chosen so that this record subtracts the force phase. A time-varying
homodyne local-oscillator phase aligned with f, followed by weighted integration,
acquires this one observable without retaining a coherent quantum field. A two-
quadrature heterodyne record is another available route but adds extra vacuum
noise. Actual finite detection efficiency is charged below.

Define the residual force h_f and backward response q:

 h_f=g-sqrt(kappa_c) f,
 q(s)=integral_s^T exp[-kappa(t-s)/2] h_f(t) dt,
 q'=kappa q/2-h_f,  q(T)=0.                                     (6.1)

Then the EXACT retained gate phase is

 P=Im integral_0^T g(t)^* q(t) dt.                               (6.2)

To prove it, use (3.2), input-output relations, and the closed forward response
beta. Equivalently P=-Re integral h_f beta^*. Integration by parts cancels the
damping terms:
 integral h_f beta^* = i integral q g^*,
because beta(0)=beta(T)=0 and q(T)=0. This also shows why a final measurement
of the closed bus or later smoothing cannot evade the hot term: a nonzero final
q boundary still multiplies beta(T)=0. Additional fresh-noise/detector costs are
nonnegative. The independent collaboration proofs give these boundary variants.

The complete dephasing coefficient J=sigma^2/2 is

 J[q]=V_b |q(0)|^2 + V_h kappa_h ||q||_2^2
      +(1/2)||f+sqrt(kappa_c)q||_2^2 + nu ||f||_2^2,              (6.3)

where
 f=[g-kappa q/2+q']/sqrt(kappa_c),
 nu=(1-eta)/(2eta)+nu_el >=0.                                   (6.4)

Here eta is efficiency after the actual cold output is collected, and nu_el is
additional input-referred Gaussian quadrature noise. Detector vacuum loss is
not a relabeling of the inaccessible hot bath. Equation (6.3) separately charges
initial bus, hot input, cold input and the detector. It follows by expressing
the residual phase in independent input quadratures. Hot coefficients are q(0)
and -sqrt(kappa_h)q(s); the cold coefficient is -(f+sqrt(kappa_c)q).

Let E=integral |g|^2 dt. Cauchy-Schwarz in (6.2) gives

                J >= V_h kappa_h P^2/E.                         (6.5)

For |g|<=F, E<=F^2 T, so J>=V_h kappa_h P^2/(F^2 T). A coherence whose
charge gap is Delta_s has exponent D=Delta_s^2 J and therefore

       D >= Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h P^2/(F^2 T).                   (6.6)

This bound already grants an optimal full cold-output record, arbitrarily good
classical processing, arbitrary finite real gain, and noiseless local phase
correction. Real readout and control errors only add costs in this operation
class. Thus merely buying a better detector does not remove the inaccessible
continuing hot input contribution.

In particular, removing all leading hot noise requires q=0 almost everywhere
since kappa_h>0. Then h_f=0 by the Volterra equation and P=0. Approximate
temperature-dependent gains cannot evade the quantitative bound at fixed nonzero
P and bounded E. This is the analytically proved version of the observed phase
collapse, not an exchange of an infinite-temperature and numerical time-step
limit.

If an unavoidable data coherence exponent aT is present, then

 D_total >= aT+Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h P^2/(F^2 T)
          >=2|P|sqrt[a Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h]/F.                  (6.7)

This rules out an infinitely slow, arbitrarily weak gate as a free exit. It is
a scoped statement for the specified unencoded commuting-force operation; it
does not invalidate fault-tolerant encoded gates or arbitrary nonlinear control.

======================================================
The thermal part of (6.5) is achievable. Take a smooth physically closed drive
g with g(0)=g(T)=0, set c=P_*/E, and choose

 q_*(t)=i c g(t),
 f_*(t)=[g(t)-kappa q_*(t)/2+q_*'(t)]/sqrt(kappa_c).              (7.1)

Then q_*(0)=q_*(T)=0, (6.1) is satisfied by construction, and

 P=Im integral g^* q_* = P_*,
 J_hot=V_h kappa_h P_*^2/E.                                     (7.2)

This is an explicit observation/control synthesis, not an assumed perfect
syndrome service. Its cold-input and detector terms are, respectively,

 (1/(2 kappa_c)) ||g+q_*'+(kappa_c-kappa_h)q_*/2||_2^2,
 (nu/kappa_c)||g+q_*'-kappa q_*/2||_2^2.                         (7.3)

They contain g', and can be large. Hence sharpness of the thermal component is
not a claim that the entire gate is optimal or accurate for arbitrary resources.
The full optimum below includes those derivative and detector costs.

A concrete finite smooth force is generated by

8. COMPLETE CONTINUUM OPTIMUM: A LINEAR BOUNDARY-VALUE PROBLEM
===========================================================
Equation (6.3) can be solved without discarding any of its four terms. Put
d=(kappa_c-kappa_h)/2, V_c=1/2,

 a=(V_c+nu)/kappa_c,
 b=(V_c d-nu kappa/2)/kappa_c,
 c=V_h kappa_h+(V_c d^2+nu kappa^2/4)/kappa_c.

For g(0)=g(T)=0, integration by parts gives

 J[q]=aE+a||q'||^2+c||q||^2+(V_b-b)|q(0)|^2
      +2Re integral (b g-a g')^*q dt.                           (8.1)

The quadratic form is the positive form inherited from (6.3). On q(T)=0,
its operator is A_op=-a d^2/dt^2+c, with natural Robin condition
a q'(0)=(V_b-b)q(0). Define h=b g-a g' and v=i g. The unconstrained optimum is
q_W=-A_op^-1 h. Set P_W=Re <v,q_W> and d0=<v,A_op^-1 v> (>0 for nonzero g).
Then

 q_P=q_W+A_op^-1 v (P_*-P_W)/d0,
 J_min(P_*)=aE-<h,A_op^-1 h>+(P_*-P_W)^2/d0.                    (8.2)

The inner product is integral x^*y. The phase constraint is
Re<v,q>=Im<g,q>=P_*. The proof is the same Hilbert-space completion of squares
as (5.2). A coercive form gives existence and uniqueness. A physical maximum
gain, bandwidth or slew restriction must be imposed if required; it is not
included automatically by solving this unconstrained quadratic problem.

The delivered executable instead supplies an EXACT finite smooth readout family
q=sin^2(pi t/T) sum_{m=-M}^M c_m exp(i2pi m t/T). It therefore has q=q'=0 at
both endpoints and a paid finite number of profile harmonics. Within that family
the constrained positive quadratic program is solved globally by one linear
solve and one rank-one correction. Every quoted cost is evaluated on that
actual finite profile. The code reports readout squared weight, peak weight and
RMS frequency; these are observer resources, not joules without a platform model.

The spectral calculation is a construction and convergence check, not a claim
of an experimentally demonstrated optimum. The analytic lower bound does not
depend on finite truncation.

The continuum optimum in (8.2) concerns output acquisition on [0,T] and q(T)=0.
Later smoothing or final-bus measurement still obeys the thermal lower bound,
but its finite-cost optimum needs an augmented observation/boundary model.
Finite Fourier-profile count and finite RMS frequency alone do not prove a hard
detector passband sufficient: time-windowed controls have spectral tails. The
initial table uses the stated flat-response Gaussian measurement model. The
explicit finite-response construction in Section10 below removes the need to
infer detector feasibility from profile count alone.

10. OBSERVER, INFORMATION AND PROTECTION COSTS
=============================================
An explicit finite-response detector construction.
Use two fixed-phase homodyne channels after a 50:50 splitter (heterodyne), with
each detector efficiency eta. Reconstruct quadratures input-referred to the
original cold output. The added vacuum quadrature variance is then
nu_base=1/eta-1/2, rather than the single-homodyne value(1-eta)/(2eta).
Explicitly j_Q=Q_out+xi_Q, j_P=P_out+xi_P, with added covariance
E[xi_mu(t)xi_nu(t')]=nu_base delta_mu,nu delta(t-t'). The reconstructed
observable is Y_f=sqrt(2) integral [Re(f)j_Q+Im(f)j_P]dt. At eta=1 the
heterodyne added variance is still1/2 per quadrature.

Let each photocurrent j pass through a physical first-order response
x_dot=-B x+B j, with finite B>0, followed by independent Gaussian electronic
noise of input-referred real-quadrature spectral density nu_e. For any chosen
smooth component of the desired weight f with f(0)=f(T)=0, integration by parts
gives the exact acquisition identity

 integral_0^T f j dt = integral_0^T [f-f'/B] x dt.                (10.1)

The unknown initial and final detector states cancel in the boundary term.
This weights the filtered record; it never differentiates a noisy record. The
added electronics contribution to J is

 nu_e ||f-f'/B||^2 = nu_e(||f||^2+||f'||^2/B^2).                 (10.2)

For the supplied smooth profiles, f endpoints do vanish. Since
f'=(g'-kappa q'/2+q'')/sqrt(kappa_c), the finite readout-family problem remains a
positive quadratic optimization including this derivative penalty. This is a
particular earned finite-response model, not a strict hard spectral cutoff:
the first-order transfer has no zero at finite frequency. A detector with an
actual stopband or a different transfer must use its real C,N or a bounded
mode-truncation error. The fixed-phase two-channel measurement is a valid POVM;
there is no joint noiseless q/p readout assumption.

The executable includes B=10, eta=.8 and nu_e=.01. At n_h=0,10,100,1000, it
preserves P=pi/8 with complete J=.08129335,.37208275,2.59443856,23.64971073,
respectively. The force and gate time are unchanged. At n_h=100, the actual
applied integration weight has squared norm .190581 and peak .432794; gain
and electronics amplification are paid explicitly. These are finite-response
model values, not experimental performance. They also make clear that a finite
observer does not evade the interaction bound.

Interaction. The bus coupling is finite and explicitly shaped. E and F, gate
duration T and force/readout RMS frequencies are separate resources. E has
dimensions inverse time in hbar=1 units; it is not electrical/optical energy.
The physical platform must supply an independently calibrated conversion.

Measurement. The actual cold output is collected. A matched local oscillator
measures the chosen temporal quadrature; eta and electronic noise appear in
(6.3). A weighted photocurrent integral yields the correction phase. No ideal
error-event times, bath purification, cold output from the hot bath, or perfect
stored pointer is substituted. Markov/linear-response/bandwidth assumptions
must hold over the actual force and readout spectra. A finite discretization is
a numerical approximation, not a claim of instantaneous physical pulses.

Calibration. The model uses actual port rates, force response, detector efficiency,
phase alignment and covariance. They must be acquired from device calibration.
Equation (3.1) gives the resulting channel when they are wrong; (4.1) is not a
self-certification theorem. An unmatched hot-noise residual delta r costs
n ||A_H^T delta r||^2/2 in J, so a temperature-uniform error claim needs relevant
amplitude/phase matching of order n^-1/2, or a separately proved symmetry.
Nothing in this work acquires that precision at a cost independent of n.

Digitization. For S=Z1+Z2, the final local phase frame is periodic modulo pi.
After a valid analog/digital integral is acquired, b phase bits give worst-case
half-diamond error at most pi/2^b. Acquisition itself still needs dynamic range:
for a Gaussian record with variance V_Y and bounded state-dependent mean m_max,
range m_max+sqrt[2V_Y log(2/delta_clip)] bounds clipping probability. A digital
integrator with fixed absolute precision therefore uses about(1/2)log2 n extra
range bits for an O(n) record variance. We do not assume a free analog modulo
operation, error-free ADC, or infinite-bandwidth stream.

Feedback latency. Because the correction commutes with the completed S^2 gate,
it may be applied after acquisition. It still requires finite local rotation
strength, delay and data storage. Phase periodicity bounds required rotation
angle, but does not remove errors in that rotation or storage. If later gates
commute with the frame it may be tracked classically; arbitrary subsequent
noncommuting gates require the frame/control to be resolved.

Data protection. No reference qubit or observer qubit is assumed noiseless.
The data qubits themselves must survive the finite gate/readout interval.
Commuting phase faults can be included exactly as above. A declared stochastic
noncommuting fault probability p contributes at most p to a worst-case
half-diamond error budget, while its actual propagation may be worse for useful
encoding. A general fault-tolerance threshold is not established here.

11. REAL ESCAPES AND WHY THEY DO NOT REFUTE THE BOUND
===================================================
A. Actual native-output quantum erasure (established mechanism).
If every relevant physical output displacement mode is accessible, measure its
generating quadrature rather than the quadrature carrying its S-dependent mean.
For a closed bus this makes C Omega^T L=0 and permits C^T k=L. The gate phase
is unchanged and all original thermal phase noise cancels. Detector vacuum loss
then leaves a finite residual sigma^2 such as
sum_j (1-eta_j)||L_j||^2/(2eta_j); J is half that variance.
This statement uses actual physical output collection, not access to a formal
purification. It can be implemented by local homodyne measurements and classical
addition when each port's profile is known; coherent quantum mixing across
unavailable frequencies or modes is not silently supplied.

For a single stationary thermal quadrature with variance V, detected through
vacuum admixture, optimum conditioning leaves
V_cond=V(1-eta)/[2eta V+(1-eta)]. This saturates with V. If the lost/added port
is itself hot, or independent hot outputs remain inaccessible, that conclusion
does not follow. General mixed-temperature correlations require the actual
covariance in Section3. The thermal erasure principle is prior work; the access
and phase-retention criteria distinguish its legitimate use from the cold-port
substitution that failed above.

B. Encoded fixed-angle gates (established different mechanism).
The four-level binomial code

 |0_L>=(|0>+sqrt(3)|2>)/2,
 |1_L>=(sqrt(3)|1>+|3>)/2

has P n P=(3/2)P and P n^2 P=3P, so {I,n} satisfies Knill-Laflamme. Yet
exp(i pi n^2/2) acts as logical S. Two blocks using n_A+n_B acquire
exp[i pi(n_A+n_B)^2/2]=(S_L tensor S_L) CZ_L on the code. Bus-induced phase
noise commutes with this COMPLETE finite-angle gate and can be suppressed by a
recovery after it, with the nonzero error below. The exact one-block Gaussian
phase-noise recovery error is

 p_L(v)=1/2-(9/16)e^(-v/2)+(1/16)e^(-9v/2)=9v^2/16+O(v^3).

This is a concrete correction to an overbroad continuous-HNLS inference: making
P n^2 P scalar forbids a particular continuously projected Hamiltonian, not every
finite-angle code-preserving gate. Rotation-symmetric/binomial code self-/cross-
Kerr gates are established primary research. State preparation, recovery,
charge range, native losses, noncommuting faults and controller protection remain
paid. The independent supplement checks a complete recovery and correlated
two-block channel. We do not advertise that ideal recovery as a completed device.

C. Other actual changes of premises.
Turning off the hot coupling, exploiting a truly colored bath's quiet spectrum,
raising available force with temperature, adding useful non-Gaussian operations,
or physically redirecting intrinsic noise into an acquired output can change
the problem. Their resources must be constructed. A second nominally cold probe
with no new access to the hot innovations does not by itself supply those changes.

12. RESEARCH CONCLUSION
======================
The main contribution of this continuation is a phase-preserving physical
observability test, a sharp thermal interaction-cost bound, and an explicit
finite observer synthesis evaluated against the strongest same-gate Gaussian
route. The bound changes the scientific question: a quiet inferred trajectory
can be obtained by removing the very response that mediates the gate.

The consequential engineering opening is therefore to create actual access to
the harmful environmental degrees of freedom, or a finite-angle protected
interaction outside this unencoded Gaussian class, without importing a more
capable hidden quantum service. Direct environmental erasure and encoded Kerr
gates supply real comparison constructions; their missing physical acquisition
and recovery costs, not their familiar scaling headlines, determine the next
credible advance.

This completed intermediate result does not assert that a civilisation-changing
device has been invented. It supplies an exact channel, a constructive optimum,
a provable obstruction, explicit escape conditions, and independently executable
evidence that future candidates must survive. Earlier sensor capital and useful
failures are retained in the companion archive.
