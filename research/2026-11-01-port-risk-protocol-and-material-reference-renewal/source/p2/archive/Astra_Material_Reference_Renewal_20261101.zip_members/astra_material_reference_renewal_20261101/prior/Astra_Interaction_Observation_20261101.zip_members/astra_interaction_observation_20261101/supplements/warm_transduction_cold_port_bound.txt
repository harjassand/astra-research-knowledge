# Exact feedback phase and a cold-output observation obstruction

Independent audit of the root's Gaussian feedback formula, 1 November 2026.

## 1. Exact Weyl-order formula

Let R be real environment quadratures with [R_j,R_k]=i Omega_jk and W(l)=exp(i l^T R). Let the free environmental state be zero-mean Gaussian with covariance V. The controlled mediated evolution is

    U_s=exp(i Phi s^2) W(sL) U_free.

Assume a physically valid Gaussian POVM with characteristic operator

    integral exp(-i t^T y) M_y dy
       = exp(-t^T N t/2) W(-C^T t).

A linear terminal local feedback exp(-i S k^T y) then multiplies target coherence s,s' by exactly

    exp{i[Phi-(1/2)L^T Omega C^T k](s^2-s'^2)}
    times exp{-(s-s')^2/2 [
       (L-C^T k)^T V (L-C^T k)+k^T N k ]}.

Proof: write Delta=s-s', c=C^T k, integrate the POVM characteristic function, and cyclically move the final W(-s'L). The three Weyl factors are

    W(-s'L) W(-Delta c) W(sL)
       = exp[-i(s^2-s'^2)L^T Omega c/2] W[Delta(L-c)].

Taking the Gaussian characteristic function proves the result. No classical conditioning approximation is used. In particular the feedback generally changes the deterministic S^2 gate phase as well as reducing dephasing.

The C,N pair must define a valid quantum POVM. Simultaneous ideal measurement of both noncommuting quadratures with N=0 is not an allowed instance. Heterodyne C=I,N=I/2 is a valid benchmark in the convention used here.

## 2. Physical model for the cold-port obstruction

One bus oscillator is driven through

    H(t)=S[f(t)a^dagger+f(t)^*a],

and damped into an inaccessible hot input field at rate kappa_h>0 and an observed cold input/output field at rate kappa_c>0. Let kappa=kappa_h+kappa_c. The field convention is

    a_dot = -(kappa/2)a - sqrt(kappa_h)b_h,in
            - sqrt(kappa_c)b_c,in - i S f(t),
    b_c,out=b_c,in+sqrt(kappa_c)a.

The initial bus and continuous hot input have independent isotropic variance V_h=n_h+1/2. The cold input has a specified finite covariance. Arbitrary linear Gaussian observation of the cold output and terminal feedback generated by S are allowed. There is no access to the hot output, no independent S^2 Hamiltonian, and no change of target encoding. The actual driven bus displacement beta obeys

    beta_dot=-(kappa/2)beta-i f,
    beta(0)=beta(T)=0.

These boundary conditions refer to physical bus closure.

Write the total scalar feedback observable on the cold output as

    Q_w = integral_0^T [w(t)b_c,out^dagger(t)+w(t)^*b_c,out(t)]dt.

Every linear combination of a Gaussian measurement record corresponds to such a w plus detector noise. Giving the observer the ideal Q_w quadrature can only strengthen it, so detector noise can be omitted for the lower bound.

Define

    u(t)=f(t)+sqrt(kappa_c)w(t),
    q(s)=integral_s^T exp[-kappa(t-s)/2] u(t)dt.

## 3. Residual hot noise

In the interaction picture the drive's linear noise generator is minus

    F=integral[f(t)a_0^dagger(t)+f(t)^*a_0(t)]dt,

where a_0 is the freely evolving bus. Replacing the cold output by b_c,in+sqrt(kappa_c)a_0 shows that the residual generator L-C^T k has hot-field coefficient sqrt(kappa_h)q(s), and initial-bus coefficient -q(0), up to the common harmless quadrature sign convention.

Consequently its hot contribution to the variance is exactly

    2 V_h [ |q(0)|^2 + kappa_h integral_0^T |q(s)|^2 ds ].

The corresponding contribution to the decay exponent of a coherence with gap Delta_s=s-s' is

    Delta_s^2 V_h [ |q(0)|^2
                   +kappa_h integral_0^T |q(s)|^2 ds ].

Detector noise and cold input noise add further nonnegative terms; neither cancels this independent hot component.

## 4. Exact relation to the remaining gate phase

The displacement-induced mean of Q_w is

    2 S sqrt(kappa_c) Re integral w(t) beta(t)^*dt.

Using the exact Weyl-order formula therefore gives

    Phi_eff = Phi - sqrt(kappa_c) Re integral w beta^*dt
            = -Re integral u beta^*dt.

The backwards Volterra equation obeys q'=kappa q/2-u and q(T)=0. Integration by parts, using beta(0)=beta(T)=0, yields

    integral u beta^*dt = i integral q f^*dt,
    Phi_eff = Im integral q(s) f(s)^*ds.

This identity is the central obstruction. It ties the remaining coherent interaction to the same residual hot-noise response that variance-only regression tries to remove.

## 5. Exact zero-noise consequence and quantitative bound

If the leading hot variance vanishes, kappa_h>0 implies q=0 almost everywhere. The Volterra operator is injective: u=kappa q/2-q'=0. Thus

    w=-f/sqrt(kappa_c),
    Phi_eff=0.

The resulting feedforward can cancel the hot force fluctuations, but it also cancels the mediated S^2 interaction. An independent S^2 correction could restore it only by supplying the entangling resource separately.

There is also a bound that rules out evasion through a sequence of ill-conditioned approximate gains. By Cauchy-Schwarz,

    |Phi_eff| <= ||f||_2 ||q||_2.

If |f(t)|<=F, the residual hot coherence-decay exponent is at least

    Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h Phi_eff^2 / ||f||_2^2
       >= Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h Phi_eff^2 / (F^2 T).

For a fixed nonzero target gate phase, bounded force and bounded duration, a strictly positive temperature-dependent error survives all such cold-port Gaussian observation and linear feedback.

If the same required target coherence has independent native decay exponent aT, then

    total exponent >= aT
       +Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h Phi_eff^2/(F^2 T)
       >= 2 |Phi_eff| sqrt[a Delta_s^2 V_h kappa_h]/F.

This comparison holds even before paying finite detector efficiency, detector noise, processing delay, or local-feedback error.

## 5a. Complete continuum noise functional and component sharpness

The residual complex amplitude on the cold input is sqrt(kappa_c)q-w. If the initial bus variance is V_bus, the hot input variance is V_h, the cold input variance is V_c, and the input-referred detector quadrature noise is nu, the full coherence exponent divided by Delta_s^2 is

    J(q)=V_bus |q(0)|^2 + V_h kappa_h ||q||_2^2
          +V_c ||sqrt(kappa_c)q-w||_2^2 + nu ||w||_2^2,
    w=[kappa q/2-q'-f]/sqrt(kappa_c),
    q(T)=0,    Phi_eff=Im integral q f^*.

The cold term can equivalently be written

    (V_c/kappa_c) ||q'+f+(kappa_c-kappa_h)q/2||_2^2.

This is a complete quadratic optimization problem for the stated independent Gaussian baths and ideal linear field access, not an assumed classical conditioning model. For physical homodyne attenuation eta with independent vacuum admixture, nu=(1-eta)/(2eta) after rescaling the record. More complicated physical detector noise must be represented by its actual covariance rather than this scalar.

The hot-component lower bound is sharp for smooth closed-loop drives satisfying f(0)=f(T)=0. Choose

    q(t)=i Phi_target f(t)/||f||_2^2.

Then q(0)=q(T)=0, the remaining phase is Phi_target, and the hot contribution exactly equals V_h kappa_h Phi_target^2/||f||_2^2. The cold-input and detector costs need not be small for this choice, so this sharpness statement does not establish an achievable low total gate error.

## 5b. Exact finite-dimensional noise-versus-gate frontier

The strongest same-task linear Gaussian comparator is available in closed form. Set

    A=C V C^T+N,
    b=C V L,
    h=(1/2) C Omega^T L,
    k_0=A^(-1)b,
    var_0=L^T V L-b^T A^(-1)b,
    Phi_0=Phi-h^T k_0.

For positive definite A and h nonzero, imposing a REQUIRED remaining gate phase Phi_target gives

    k_opt=k_0+A^(-1)h
          [Phi-Phi_target-h^T k_0]/[h^T A^(-1)h],

    var_min(Phi_target)=var_0
          +(Phi_target-Phi_0)^2/[h^T A^(-1)h].

This follows by completing the square and one Lagrange multiplier. If h=0, feedback does not change the phase; the constraint is either already met or infeasible. Singular A requires the usual support/pseudoinverse compatibility conditions.

The formula compares controllers at the same actual entangling gate. Optimizing variance without the phase constraint is a different task and may legitimately select a nearly identity target channel.

## 6. Exact scope and exits

This is an obstruction for an unencoded commuting-force gate, continuous inaccessible hot noise, linear Gaussian observation of only cold outputs, and terminal feedback generated by S. It is not a no-go theorem for all noisy-mediated quantum gates.

It does not cover:

* directly observing the relevant hot physical output;
* a hot initial bus without continuing hot input (kappa_h=0);
* encoded finite-angle Kerr gates with non-Gaussian recovery;
* a separately available entangling S^2 control;
* a changed nonlinear interaction or non-Gaussian physical measurement;
* correlated or engineered hot/cold inputs that violate the independent-bath model.

The explicit rotation-code comparator in `warm_transduction_encoded_competitor.md` is an important established exit. The retained passive transduction cut bound also continues to govern any proposed extra observer/transducer that is supposed to make the hot output accessible.

## 7. Numerical audit plan and finite discretization caution

The root's `warm_bus_observation_probe.py` uses an exact finite passive collision dilation, captures both cold-output quadratures with heterodyne noise N=I/2, and includes the initial bus in the hot covariance. Its Weyl phase is the correct quantity to track.

At fixed finite collision number, an arbitrarily large hot occupation can expose residual discretization-scale hot components. A continuum claim must not silently interchange the infinite-temperature and vanishing-bin-duration limits. The analytic bound above applies directly to the continuum input-output model and does not require that interchange.
