# A sharp thermal-noise/phase tradeoff for a cold observation port

Research continuation, 1 November 2026 (Brisbane). This note independently derives a continuum consequence of the root Gaussian-feedback instrument and checks it against a finite passive collision dilation. No hardware performance or historical novelty is asserted.

## 1. Model and precise question

A bosonic bus a mediates a scalar spin force

\[
H_F(t)=S[g(t)a^\dagger+g(t)^*a],\qquad S=Z_1+Z_2.
\]

It couples to an intrinsic hot Markov bath of rate \(\kappa_h>0\), occupation n, and an added vacuum port of rate \(\kappa_c>0\). The intrinsic hot output is inaccessible. The cold-port output may be processed by Gaussian measurements with arbitrary temporal kernels and followed by a scalar linear phase correction on S. Independent measurement ancillas and added detector noise are allowed. They cannot have prior correlations with the intrinsic bath. Write \(\kappa=\kappa_h+\kappa_c\).

Initially the bus is thermal with occupation n, independent of the hot and cold inputs. Equal initial and bath occupations simplify notation; the continuing-bath bound below does not rely on the initial occupation being large.

In an interaction frame with the free bus frequency removed,

\[
\dot\alpha=-\kappa\alpha/2-ig,
\qquad \alpha(0)=\alpha(T)=0.
\]

Closure is imposed on the actual damped displacement. The uncorrected scalar S-squared phase is

\[
\Phi=\operatorname{Im}\int_0^T\alpha^*\dot\alpha\,dt
=-\operatorname{Re}\int_0^T\alpha^*g\,dt.
\]

The target is a surviving scalar phase P, giving \(e^{iPS^2}\), equivalently a ZZ angle \(\Theta=2P\) up to global phase. We ask whether the added cold observation port can suppress all leading hot noise while preserving a nonzero P at finite force budget

\[
E=\int_0^T|g(t)|^2dt.
\]

E is integrated squared coupling, not joules without a hardware conversion.

## 2. Free input-output coefficients

Use input fields satisfying \([b_j(t),b_j^\dagger(s)]=\delta(t-s)\). The unforced bus evolves as

\[
a_0(t)=e^{-\kappa t/2}a(0)+
\sum_{j=h,c}\sqrt{\kappa_j}\int_0^t e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}b_j(s)ds.
\]

Choose the passive input-output convention

\[
b_{c,\mathrm{out}}(t)=b_c(t)-\sqrt{\kappa_c}\,a_0(t).
\]

Define the future response of the force

\[
u(s)=\int_s^T e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}g(t)dt,
\quad u'=\kappa u/2-g,
\quad u(T)=0.
\]

The input-picture linear noise generator has coefficients u(0) on the initial bus and \(\sqrt{\kappa_j}u(s)\) on input field j. A complex coefficient w means the Hermitian quadrature \(w a^\dagger+w^*a\), whose vacuum variance is \(|w|^2\).

Any scalar linear Gaussian feedforward from the cold output reduces to a complex temporal quadrature kernel f, plus independent nonnegative measurement noise. Define

\[
X_f=\int_0^T[f(t)b_{c,\mathrm{out}}^\dagger(t)+f(t)^*b_{c,\mathrm{out}}(t)]dt,
\]

and use correction \(e^{-iSX_f}\), with the measured outcome substituted for \(X_f\). Let

\[
v(s)=\int_s^T e^{-\kappa(t-s)/2}f(t)dt.
\]

On the initial bus, hot input, and cold input, the unforced measured observable has coefficient

\[
-\sqrt{\kappa_c}v(0),\qquad
-\sqrt{\kappa_c\kappa_h}v(s),\qquad
f(s)-\kappa_c v(s),
\]

respectively. Thus define the residual hot-response function

\[
\boxed{r(s)=u(s)-\sqrt{\kappa_c}v(s).}
\]

After feedback, the residual noise coefficients, up to a common irrelevant minus sign, are

\[
r(0),\qquad \sqrt{\kappa_h}r(s),\qquad
\sqrt{\kappa_c}r(s)+f(s).
\]

For ideal measurement of the single relevant quadrature, the exact variance therefore is

\[
\boxed{
V_{\rm res}=(2n+1)
\left(|r(0)|^2+\kappa_h\int|r|^2dt\right)
+\int|\sqrt{\kappa_c}r+f|^2dt.
}
\]

Independent detector/ancilla noise adds a nonnegative term. Full heterodyne adds another vacuum contribution \(\int|f|^2dt\). It is not needed if only the known scalar quadrature functional is required; a shaped homodyne local oscillator can acquire that commuting functional.

## 3. Feedback changes the gate phase

The corrected phase from the exact Gaussian instrument is

\[
P=\Phi+\sqrt{\kappa_c}\operatorname{Re}
\int_0^T\alpha^*(t)f(t)dt.
\]

The second term is the required half-commutator/backaction phase, rather than a freely ignorable calibration offset. It agrees with the root expression \(P=\Phi-\tfrac12c^Tk\).

There are two equivalent useful derivations of its relation to r.

First, Fubini gives

\[
\Phi=\operatorname{Im}\int g^*u,
\qquad
\operatorname{Im}\int g^*v
=-\operatorname{Re}\int\alpha^* f.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{P=\operatorname{Im}\int_0^T g(t)^*r(t)dt.}
\]

Alternatively, put \(h=g-\sqrt{\kappa_c}f\), so \(r'=\kappa r/2-h\). Then

\[
P=-\operatorname{Re}\int\alpha^*h
=-\operatorname{Re}\int(\dot\alpha^*+\kappa\alpha^*/2)r
=\operatorname{Im}\int g^*r,
\]

using \(\dot\alpha^*+\kappa\alpha^*/2=ig^*\) and the vanishing boundary term.

## 4. Sharp continuum lower bound

The component proportional to thermal occupation is

\[
V_{\rm hot}=2n\left(|r(0)|^2+\kappa_h\|r\|_2^2\right).
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the phase identity yields

\[
P^2\le E\|r\|_2^2.
\]

Hence every allowed Gaussian linear feedback that preserves phase P satisfies

\[
\boxed{V_{\rm hot}\ge\frac{2n\kappa_hP^2}{E}.}
\]

This statement allows arbitrary temporal Gaussian processing of the observed cold port. Extra independent detector noise cannot invalidate it. It is a lower bound on the actual quantum phase variance, not merely on a classical estimation error.

Several consequences are immediate:

- Eliminating the entire leading hot-input contribution forces r=0 almost everywhere and therefore P=0.
- At fixed nonzero P and finite E, corrected noise cannot remain bounded as n tends to infinity through continuously renewed hot input.
- Increasing the cold-port coupling alone does not remove the bound. It affects the force required for closure and the cold measurement cost; it does not erase \(\kappa_h\) from the lower bound.
- If \(|g(t)|\le G\), then \(V_{\rm hot}\ge2n\kappa_hP^2/(G^2T)\). Reducing that sufficient floor through longer time consumes real data coherence.

For S eigenvalues +2 and -2, the target S-squared unitary gives the same phase, while a Gaussian phase variance V attenuates their coherence by \(e^{-8V}\). Thus a diamond-norm gate error delta must obey

\[
\delta\ge1-\exp[-16n\kappa_hP^2/E].
\]

For a required \(\delta<1\), a necessary drive budget is

\[
E\ge\frac{16n\kappa_hP^2}{-\log(1-\delta)}.
\]

Equivalently, using the desired ZZ angle \(\Theta=2P\), the numerator is \(4n\kappa_h\Theta^2\). Half-diamond error is half the displayed delta convention.

If the same double-flip coherence has independent data dephasing rate Gamma, then its exponent is at least

\[
D\ge\Gamma T+\frac{16n\kappa_hP^2}{G^2T}
\ge8|P|\sqrt{\frac{n\kappa_h\Gamma}{G^2}}.
\]

This is a lower bound within the stated control/noise model. It does not account for error-correcting encodings, coherent quantum feedback, or extra observations of the native bath, which are different resources.

## 5. Sharpness with a finite feedback kernel

The leading-hot bound is attainable, not only asymptotically approached by singular filters. Assume \(g\in H^1[0,T]\), \(g(0)=g(T)=0\), and E>0. For any desired real P, choose

\[
r_*(t)=i\frac{P}{E}g(t),\qquad
f_*(t)=\frac{g(t)-\kappa r_*(t)/2+r_*'(t)}{\sqrt{\kappa_c}}.
\]

The future-response differential equations with terminal r(T)=0 show this filter produces r*. Then

\[
P=\operatorname{Im}\int g^*r_*dt,
\qquad
V_{\rm hot}=\frac{2n\kappa_hP^2}{E}
\]

exactly. The initial-hot term vanishes because r*(0)=0. The filter has finite norm because g and g' do. Its detector-vacuum cost depends on the derivative of the force, so bandwidth and gain remain charged.

At P=0 the simple filter \(f=g/\sqrt{\kappa_c}\) cancels all leading hot noise but also cancels the gate. Its remaining variance is E/kappa_c for ideal scalar homodyne and 2E/kappa_c for full heterodyne. This explains the numerical observation in the root probe: thermal-noise saturation accompanied by the corrected phase tending to zero.

The saturation example in `warm_bus_tradeoff.py` uses a smooth closed path

\[
\alpha(t)=A\sin^2(\pi t/T)e^{i\Omega t},
\]

with g and g' constructed from its actual damped dynamics. It retains P=0.1 with E=0.4323276, leading-hot coefficient 0.01387836, exactly \(2\kappa_hP^2/E\), and finite homodyne cold variance 0.06489575. The equation for the reconstructed filter response is checked to about 4e-9.

## 6. What the theorem does and does not rule out

### A final observation of the closed bus does not remove it

A final bus quadrature adds a terminal exponential to r and can make r(T) nonzero. Under actual closure alpha(T)=0, the integration-by-parts boundary still vanishes, so the same phase identity and continuing-hot-bath lower bound hold.

### Only an initially hot bus is different

If \(\kappa_h=0\), the hot part is finite-rank initial uncertainty \(2n|r(0)|^2\). Setting r(0)=0 does not require the whole function r to vanish, so a nonzero P may remain. Conventional cooling/waiting and direct or indirect measurement can then be serious competitors. The no-go above concerns a continuously replenished inaccessible hot input stream.

### Nonlinear measurements or coherent feedback need their own theorem

This proof covers the exact Gaussian linear measurement/feedforward family stated in the model. It is not a blanket impossibility theorem for all quantum instruments. Additional non-Gaussian operations, quantum memory, protected encodings, and feedback that changes the physical interaction lie outside it and must be evaluated with their own costs.

## 7. A physical escape: collect the native hot output

An added cold probe and access to the actual hot output are different resources. Suppose a nonzero fraction \(\eta_h\) of the native hot output is collected, in addition to fraction \(\eta_c\) of the cold output. In the exact Gaussian dilation,

\[
U=e^{i\Phi S^2}W(SL)U_{\rm free},
\]

actual bus closure means L has support only on output bath modes. For each output temporal mode j, measure the quadrature along its Weyl-generator vector \(L_j\), orthogonal to its S-dependent displacement. This is a no-information quadrature: rowwise

\[
C\Omega^T L=0.
\]

For a loss-limited detector, write \(C_j=\sqrt{\eta_j}L_j^T/\|L_j\|\), with added vacuum variance \((1-\eta_j)/2\). Set gain \(k_j=\|L_j\|/\sqrt{\eta_j}\). Then

\[
C^Tk=L,\qquad
\Phi_{\rm eff}=\Phi,
\]

and the physical thermal residual is exactly zero. The only residual phase variance is

\[
\boxed{V_{\rm det}=\sum_j\frac{1-\eta_j}{2\eta_j}\|L_j\|^2.}
\]

For the continuum two-port force model this is

\[
V_{\rm det}=\int_0^T
\left[\frac{1-\eta_h}{\eta_h}\kappa_h+
\frac{1-\eta_c}{\eta_c}\kappa_c\right]|\alpha(t)|^2dt.
\]

It is independent of thermal occupation for any fixed positive efficiencies. The result concerns full unknown inputs and their references: the measured quadratures commute with the conditional displacement, reveal no S information, and supply the random phase to be removed. Access to a purification of the thermal bath is unnecessary. At unit efficiencies the ideal matched instrument is exact.

This is an application of established environmental quantum-erasure/no-knowledge feedback principles, not a new principle of decoherence cancellation. The paid change is collection of the physical hot output, with the appropriate mode, bandwidth, and detector dynamic range. A new cold side port does not silently supply it.

### Calibration cannot be omitted

If actual gain or temporal-mode matching leaves residual vector Delta L, the hot part is proportional to \(n\|P_h^{1/2}\Delta L\|^2\). A fixed fractional gain error epsilon therefore produces an \(O(n\epsilon^2)\) contribution. Uniform error at increasing n generally requires matching accuracy of order \(n^{-1/2}\), unless a physical common-reference construction proves cancellation of the mismatch. Local-oscillator phase error can also reintroduce system information and a gate-phase shift. Finite bandwidth, temporal truncation, and calibration uncertainty must be inserted as actual mode errors, not ignored after taking the hot limit.

## 8. Executable evidence

`warm_bus_tradeoff.py` preserves the finite collision model and performs three checks:

1. Cold-output gains minimizing the hot coefficient while preserving phase. At 20,40,80,160,320 time cells, the ratio of the computed coefficient to \(2\kappa_hP^2/E\) is 1.071690, 1.032401, 1.015640, 1.007715, 1.003835. The unconstrained optimum cancels the phase to floating-point precision.
2. The smooth finite-gain saturation construction above, including its response differential equation and exact retained phase.
3. Native hot-output collection with efficiencies 0.4 (hot) and 0.7 (cold). At occupations 0,10,10^6, the corrected variance is the same 0.05528487567 and retained phase is 0.09828701185. The physical residual-vector norm is 1.83e-16. A fixed gain error 10^-3 adds a hot coefficient 8.70e-8, which becomes 0.0870 variance at n=10^6.

These checks support the algebra and continuum limit; they do not validate a hardware bath model or guarantee historical novelty.

## 9. Primary predecessors and scope

- Szigeti, Carvalho, Morley, Hush, *Ignorance Is Bliss: General and Robust Cancellation of Decoherence via No-Knowledge Quantum Feedback*, Physical Review Letters 113, 020407 (2014), https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.113.020407 . This supplies the broad prior principle of environmental noise monitoring and cancellation without measuring a system observable.
- *Time-delayed quantum feedback and incomplete decoherence suppression with a no-knowledge measurement*, Physical Review A 103, 022208 (2021), https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.103.022208 . It treats finite feedback latency and shows that noncommuting system dynamics change the cancellation. Here the post-gate S correction commutes with the intended S-squared phase, but data exposure during readout still costs time.
- *Environment-assisted quantum-information correction for continuous variables*, Physical Review A 81, 012325 (2010), https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.81.012325 . It is a close predecessor for removing thermal excess noise using small environmental access, but its coherent-state quadrature-information task is not identical to arbitrary-state exact quantum-channel recovery.
