# Geometric force gates: waveform and inaccessible-bath cost bounds

Complementary construction audit, 1 November 2026 (Brisbane). The inequalities below are elementary analytic derivations for a specified linear-boson model. They are not asserted to be historically new. The purpose is to compare conventional closed-loop shaping with output monitoring without hiding cost in very slow, increasingly rapid-winding loops.

## 1. Task and operations

Two data qubits A,B couple longitudinally to a damped bosonic mediator:

\[
H(t)=\sum_{j=A,B}Z_j[g_j(t)a^\dagger+g_j(t)^*a],
\qquad L=\sqrt\kappa\,a.
\]

The desired full two-qubit unitary is \(e^{i\Theta Z_AZ_B}\), up to known single-qubit phases. The displacement response to each force obeys

\[
\dot\alpha_j=-\kappa\alpha_j/2-ig_j,
\qquad\alpha_j(0)=\alpha_j(T)=0.
\]

The closure requirement is for the actual damped response. Closing a lossless trajectory instead does not meet it. Drive and loss parameters are treated as known here; calibration errors are additional costs.

For the branch-action phase, the ZZ coefficient is

\[
\Theta=\operatorname{Im}\int_0^T
(\alpha_A^*\dot\alpha_B+\alpha_B^*\dot\alpha_A)dt.
\]

This follows by substituting the driven coherent response into \(\dot\phi_z=-\operatorname{Re}(g_z\alpha_z^*)\), equivalently \(\dot\phi_z=\operatorname{Im}(\alpha_z^*\dot\alpha_z)\), and reading the coefficient of \(z_Az_B\).

For arbitrary independent force paths, bath overlaps can contribute the extra antisymmetric phase proportional to \(\kappa\operatorname{Im}\int\alpha_A^*\alpha_B\). It is not generally a ZZ unitary: it has opposite extra signs on A-flip and B-flip coherences. The theorem below applies directly when that term vanishes, including the common-force case \(\alpha_A=\alpha_B\). In a general design, all computational-basis coherences must satisfy the desired gate condition; the antisymmetric dissipative phase cannot simply be counted as additional desired entangling phase.

## 2. Complete waveform cost inequality

Define actual mediator exposure, trajectory-velocity cost, and squared-coupling budget

\[
J=\int_0^T(|\alpha_A|^2+|\alpha_B|^2)dt,
\]

\[
K=\int_0^T(|\dot\alpha_A|^2+|\dot\alpha_B|^2)dt,
\qquad
E=\int_0^T(|g_A|^2+|g_B|^2)dt.
\]

E has units of inverse time when the Hamiltonian is in angular-frequency units. It is an integrated squared interaction-strength budget, **not thermodynamic energy in joules** without a platform-specific conversion.

The cross term in \(\int|\dot\alpha_j+\kappa\alpha_j/2|^2\) is a boundary term and vanishes by closure. Therefore

\[
E=K+\kappa^2J/4.
\]

Cauchy–Schwarz, first between each pair of paths and then between the two-component vectors of their norms, gives

\[
|\Theta|\le\sqrt{J_AK_B}+\sqrt{J_BK_A}\le\sqrt{JK}.
\]

Combining the identities yields

\[
\boxed{\Theta^2\le J(E-\kappa^2J/4).}
\]

In particular \(E\ge\kappa|\Theta|\). For a prescribed finite E,

\[
\boxed{
J\ge\frac{2}{\kappa^2}
\left(E-\sqrt{E^2-\kappa^2\Theta^2}\right)
=\frac{2\Theta^2}{E+\sqrt{E^2-\kappa^2\Theta^2}}
\ge\frac{\Theta^2}{E}.
}
\]

The rationalized expression is numerically stable for small kappa. It tends to \(\Theta^2/E\) as kappa tends to zero.

Composite forcing cannot erase the nonnegative J exposure while retaining a fixed nonzero area at fixed E. It can improve constants, close the mode, suppress calibration derivatives, or redistribute the path, but it cannot cancel photons that have already escaped into an inaccessible channel.

### Finite peak coupling and finite bandwidth

If \(|g_A(t)|^2+|g_B(t)|^2\le P\), then \(E\le PT\) and

\[
J\ge\Theta^2/(PT).
\]

A finite RMS trajectory-frequency budget \(K\le\Omega^2J\) instead gives

\[
\boxed{J\ge|\Theta|/\Omega.}
\]

This is a bound on actual trajectory variation. It avoids making the mathematically impossible assumption that a nonzero pulse is both exactly compact in time and exactly band-limited. If a device uses another definition of bandwidth, the conversion to K/J must be shown and spectral tails charged.

## 3. A waveform-independent inaccessible-output loss floor

First take the favorable vacuum case: initially vacuum mediator and vacuum bath, with an inaccessible output loss rate \(\kappa_{\rm loss}\le\kappa\). For example, an ideal collection channel followed by efficiency eta has \(\kappa_{\rm loss}=(1-\eta)\kappa\). This is an optimistic model for monitoring; finite-efficiency homodyne or a particular detector need not recover everything in the accessible fraction.

Conditional on qubit Z eigenvalues z, the inaccessible output carries a coherent displacement \(\sqrt{\kappa_{\rm loss}}\,\alpha_z(t)\), where \(\alpha_z=z_A\alpha_A+z_B\alpha_B\). Tracing it multiplies the z,z' coherence by a modulus

\[
\exp\left[-\frac{\kappa_{\rm loss}}2
\int_0^T|\alpha_z-\alpha_{z'}|^2dt\right].
\]

No measurement of the accessible output followed by classical phase feedforward can restore its missing modulus deterministically. To see this, resolve accessible outcomes with amplitudes \(m_z(r)\). Even optimally aligning outcome phases gives a factor at most \(\int|m_z(r)m_{z'}(r)|dr\le1\), while the inaccessible coherent-state overlap remains multiplicative. Postselecting and renormalizing can change this statement only by accepting a probabilistic, possibly state-dependent instrument; that is not the requested deterministic gate on an unknown input.

For the two double-flip pairs (++ , --) and (+- , -+), the respective leakage exponents are

\[
2\kappa_{\rm loss}\int|\alpha_A+\alpha_B|^2dt,
\qquad
2\kappa_{\rm loss}\int|\alpha_A-\alpha_B|^2dt.
\]

At least one is at least \(2\kappa_{\rm loss}J\). Both pairs have equal ZZ eigenvalues within the pair, so the ideal entangling unitary adds no relative phase to them. The antisymmetric bath phase also vanishes for these pairs. A superposition of the worse pair witnesses

\[
\boxed{\|\mathcal E-\mathcal U_{ZZ}\|_\diamond
\ge1-e^{-2\kappa_{\rm loss}J}.}
\]

If the error convention is half diamond norm, divide this bound by two. Combining with the area bound gives

\[
\|\mathcal E-\mathcal U_{ZZ}\|_\diamond
\ge1-\exp\left[-\frac{2\kappa_{\rm loss}\Theta^2}{E}\right].
\]

With RMS bandwidth constraint, replace \(\Theta^2/E\) by \(|\Theta|/\Omega\). For common forcing, the (++ , --) exponent is actually \(4\kappa_{\rm loss}J\), so the corresponding lower bound is stronger.

This floor isolates inaccessible vacuum which-path information. A hot initial mediator, a thermal bath, residual displacement, detector noise, calibration error, and data-qubit noise require further analysis. The present statement does not substitute for the separate thermal-conditioning theorem, nor assert that every thermal model simply multiplies the vacuum bound by a particular factor.

## 4. Slower gates cannot be free when data also decohere

Suppose each single-qubit-flip coherence has independent data dephasing rate at least Gamma. The two double-flip coherences then decay at rate at least \(2\Gamma\). Combining that exposure with the general bath floor gives a worst coherence exponent

\[
D\ge 2\Gamma T+2\kappa_{\rm loss}\frac{\Theta^2}{PT}.
\]

Optimizing this lower bound over T gives

\[
T_* = |\Theta|\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{\rm loss}}{\Gamma P}},
\qquad
\boxed{D\ge4|\Theta|\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{\rm loss}\Gamma}{P}}.}
\]

The lower bound is subject to additional feasibility conditions such as \(PT\ge\kappa|\Theta|\), finite bandwidth, and calibration. It is not a claim that a waveform saturates all inequalities simultaneously. It does show why taking T arbitrarily large is not a complete hardware solution at fixed nonzero data decoherence.

If Gamma is exactly zero and arbitrarily large bandwidth and total E are allowed, pulse families can lower J indefinitely. That limiting statement imports unbounded physical control resources; the next explicit family exhibits the trade.

## 5. Explicit conventional loop family and its charged resources

Use identical closed paths

\[
\alpha_A(t)=\alpha_B(t)=r(e^{i\Omega t}-1),
\quad T=2\pi N/\Omega,
\quad\Theta=4\pi Nr^2.
\]

The exact damping-compensated force is \(g=i(\dot\alpha+\kappa\alpha/2)\). Its peak magnitude is

\[
g_{\rm peak}=r\left(\sqrt{\Omega^2+\kappa^2/4}+\kappa/2\right).
\]

For fixed target Theta and fixed peak force F, choose

\[
r^2=\Theta/(4\pi N),\qquad
\Omega=\sqrt{(F/r)^2-\kappa F/r},
\]

when the radicand is positive. Then

\[
J=4r^2T,\quad K=2r^2\Omega^2T,\quad E=K+\kappa^2J/4.
\]

As the number of windings N increases, J decreases as \(N^{-1/2}\), while T, E, and Omega grow as \(N^{1/2}\). Thus a smaller loss error is purchased with longer quantum exposure, more total drive, and larger waveform frequency. This is a real conventional route that must receive the same allowed budgets as output monitoring.

`warm_coherent_waveform.py` evaluates this family at \(\Theta=\pi/4\), \(\kappa=0.1\), F=1. At N=1,64,256 it finds, respectively:

| N | Duration T | Frequency Omega | Exposure J | Squared-coupling budget E |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.59081 | 3.94968 | 0.397702 | 3.10307 |
| 64 | 12.5861 | 31.9500 | 0.0491643 | 25.0936 |
| 256 | 25.1524 | 63.9500 | 0.0245629 | 50.2263 |

Adding double-coherence data decay rate 0.002 makes the total exponent in this family improve and then worsen: 0.16226 at N=1, 0.04484 at N=64, and 0.06013 at N=256. These are dimensionless mathematical examples, not platform forecasts.

The executable also verifies the closure identity \(E=K+\kappa^2J/4\), the area–cost inequality, and the exact quadratic lower bound on randomly generated closed Fourier paths.

### A stronger conventional two-tone family approaches the bound

The offset circular family is not the strongest possible competitor. Take common trajectories

\[
\alpha_A(t)=\alpha_B(t)
=c(e^{in\omega_0t}-e^{i(n+1)\omega_0t}),\qquad
T=2\pi/\omega_0.
\]

They close exactly and have zero time-average displacement. Direct integration gives

\[
\Theta=4\pi(2n+1)|c|^2,\quad
J=4T|c|^2,\quad
K=2T\omega_0^2[n^2+(n+1)^2]|c|^2.
\]

Consequently

\[
\frac{\Theta^2}{JK}
=\frac{(2n+1)^2}{2[n^2+(n+1)^2]}\longrightarrow1.
\]

This shows that the area–drive tradeoff cannot generally be improved by more than a vanishing multiplicative factor without additional physical constraints. For fixed T and Theta, increasing n lowers J but raises carrier frequency and E. At fixed E and Theta, approaching the bound instead requires increasingly long, weak gates; their data decoherence must still be charged.

At fixed T=10, \(\Theta=\pi/4\), \(\kappa=0.1\), n=1,4,16,64 gives Cauchy ratios 0.9, 0.987805, 0.999083, and 0.999940. The n=64 example uses E=31.8314 and maximum carrier frequency 40.8407, versus E=0.82455 and frequency 1.25664 at n=1. The near-saturation is therefore a real control-resource trade, not a free cancellation of leakage.

## 6. What could bypass the floor?

The proof assumes inaccessible Markovian loss, linear longitudinal forces, the stated gate acting on full unknown two-qubit inputs, and no additional error-correcting encoding of the data. Possible escapes change one of those resources: collect more output; provide a dark mediator mode with genuinely smaller inaccessible loss; use protected data encoding; or access a different nonlinearity that does not obey this force-area relation.

Two equal modes coupled to one common bath can have a dark difference mode. Driving only that mode evades a positive-loss bound because the relevant damping matrix has a zero eigenvalue. This is a protected mediator resource, not waveform cancellation of finite loss. Independent residual losses or imperfect mode matching reinstate a nonzero floor. Similarly, a decoherence-free subspace with fixed common-force eigenvalue removes its S-squared phase as well and does not implement the same full two-qubit entangler for free.

## 7. Strong primary conventional sources

- Haddadfarshi and Mintert, *High fidelity quantum gates of trapped ions in the presence of motional heating*, New Journal of Physics 18, 123007 (2016), https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.05814 and https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/18/12/123007 . The arXiv title uses “mediated by a dissipative bus mode.” It directly optimizes polychromatic driving to reduce bus entanglement and decoherence while retaining the mediated interaction.
- Müller, Luoma, and Strunz, *Geometric phase gates in dissipative quantum dynamics*, Physical Review A 102, 032611 (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.08033v2 . It constructs force compensation restoring the desired phase and final motional state in a dissipative model, while separately retaining environmentally induced loss of qubit coherence. Restoring phase and oscillator closure is not the same as recovering all leaked quantum information.
- Cross and Gambetta, *Optimized pulse shapes for a resonator-induced phase gate*, Physical Review A 91, 032325 (2015), https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.91.032325 . This work explicitly optimizes residual bus entanglement, photon-loss dephasing, duration, and robustness. The dispersive gate model differs from the strictly longitudinal force model here, so formulas should not be transferred without deriving the mapping.
- *Robust oscillator-mediated phase gates driven by low-intensity pulses*, Communications Physics (2023), https://www.nature.com/articles/s42005-023-01243-8 . It develops longitudinal oscillator gates with finite, low-intensity controls and protection against pulse imperfections, qubit noise, and mediator decoherence.
- *Error-Detectable Bosonic Entangling Gates with a Noisy Ancilla*, PRX Quantum 4, 020354 (2023), https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.020354 . Its finite-bandwidth shaping and noisy-ancilla treatment are relevant comparator machinery, but its operations and encoded task differ.
