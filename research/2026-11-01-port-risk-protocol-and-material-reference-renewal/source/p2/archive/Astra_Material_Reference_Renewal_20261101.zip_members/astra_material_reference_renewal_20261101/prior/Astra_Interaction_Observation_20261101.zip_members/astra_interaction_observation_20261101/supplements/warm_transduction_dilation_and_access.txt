# Warm coherent mediator: exact dilation, physical access, and two obstructions

Independent complementary construction for the root's warm continuation.
Date: 1 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane.

## Consequential quantum task

Implement an entangling gate on two unknown target qubits through a warm, lossy oscillator, preserving arbitrary target-reference correlations. A local sensing Fisher-information statement is insufficient for this task: every target coherence must have a controlled channel error.

The strongest ordinary starting construction is a spin-dependent force loop. Let S=(Z_1+Z_2)/2 and, in the bus rotating frame,

    H(t)=S[f(t)a^dagger+f(t)^*a].

The bosonic bus is coupled passively to Markov output fields with total rate kappa. The targets may additionally have their own stated noise. No noiseless quantum helper is assumed in the analysis below.

## Exact driven-bus dilation

Let U_0(T) be the passive free evolution of bus and all physical bath modes. Since the interaction-picture driven oscillator is linear in the initial bosonic operators, the commutator of two interaction-picture drive Hamiltonians is a scalar times S^2. Higher Magnus commutators vanish. Consequently the full unitary has the exact form

    U(T) = exp(i Phi S^2) D_E(S xi_out) U_0(T),

where D_E is a multimode displacement, including the retained bus and the outgoing fields. The classical displacement response is

    beta_dot = -(kappa/2) beta - i f(t),    beta(0)=0.

The final bus component of xi_out is beta(T); output port j has temporal displacement sqrt(kappa_j) beta(t), up to an immaterial port phase convention. The exact phase is

    Phi = -Re integral_0^T f(t) beta(t)^* dt
        = Im integral_0^T beta(t)^* beta_dot(t) dt.

Thus a chosen closed loop beta(0)=beta(T)=0 is implemented by f=i[beta_dot+(kappa/2)beta]. For S=(Z_1+Z_2)/2, exp(i Phi S^2) is an entangling ZZ gate when Phi/2 is a nontrivial ZZ angle; Phi=pi/2 gives exp(i pi Z_1 Z_2/4) up to global phase.

The controlled displacement on the outgoing environment is real physical which-path leakage. Closing the retained oscillator does not remove it.

## A subtle initial-state failure: physical closure is not interaction-picture closure

Even when beta(T)=0, the outgoing displacement can overlap the mode originating in the initial bus. For one Markov port, the freely emitted initial-bus wavefunction is sqrt(kappa)exp(-kappa t/2). The corresponding component of the interaction-picture displacement is

    xi_initial_bus = kappa integral_0^T exp(-kappa t/2) beta(t) dt

up to an overall port sign convention. This generally does not vanish.

If the initial bus has isotropic variance V_b=n_b+1/2 and the bath modes have common variance V=n+1/2, then tracing the environment multiplies a target coherence between S eigenvalues s,s' by

    exp[-(s-s')^2 { V ||xi_out||^2
                    +(V_b-V)|xi_initial_bus|^2 }].

Here ||xi_out||^2=kappa integral|beta|^2 for a physically closed loop. Therefore an arbitrary hot initial bus can still affect the unmonitored output through its emitted field. Claiming initial-bus independence solely from beta(T)=0 is incorrect under loss.

At common initial temperature V_b=V, passive free evolution preserves the isotropic covariance V I, and this simplifies to exp[-(s-s')^2 V kappa integral|beta|^2]. For nonstationary initial conditions or different port temperatures the actual output covariance must be used.

This failure does not invalidate ideal complete output erasure: if beta(T)=0 and all output quadratures generating D_E(S xi_out) are measured in their conjugate no-knowledge basis, their measurement can erase the displacement for arbitrary joint environmental states. That is precisely the environmental access the apparatus must supply.

## Collection and detector efficiency are different resources

The root's finite-efficiency conditional-erasure calculation concerns a physical thermal output mixed with a vacuum or otherwise specified detector-admixture mode. That model can be appropriate after the relevant physical output has actually been collected.

An inaccessible native bath port is not automatically equivalent to vacuum detector loss.

For an exact counterexample, take several independent bath ports with rates kappa_j, all initially at the same variance V, and the bus in thermal equilibrium at that variance. Passive free evolution leaves all final bath modes isotropic with covariance V I. Suppose only ports of total rate kappa_obs are observed. Even perfect observation of those ports leaves independent unobserved displacement noise with target-coherence exponent at least

    (s-s')^2 V (kappa-kappa_obs) integral_0^T |beta(t)|^2 dt.

There is no correlation in this example from which the missing hot-port quadrature could be inferred. Folding kappa_obs/kappa into a total efficiency and replacing the absent port by vacuum would remove a real temperature-dependent error term.

A cleaner physical implementation is a microwave resonator whose dominant damping is deliberately coupled to one accessible transmission line. Homodyne detection then needs a specified phase-sensitive amplifier or equivalent detector, its noise, its collection bandwidth, and a phase-stable local oscillator. A mechanical substrate bath is much less directly accessible: observing a mechanical mode through an added optical channel is not automatically measuring the outgoing native phonon bath. A proposed transducer for that bath is an additional physical subsystem, subject to the retained passive noisy-cut obstruction.

The observer's admixture need not be vacuum. A finite known detector noise covariance can be used, but the resulting gate floor is then paid for rather than described as a noiseless measurement resource.

## Obstruction 1: exact QEC of the force leakage can erase the gate

The intended mediated interaction is proportional to S^2, while leading bath which-path leakage induces errors in the span of {I,S}. Any exact code correcting {I,S} obeys the Knill-Laflamme condition

    P S^2 P = c P.

Therefore the intended S^2 Hamiltonian is trivial inside that code under ideal projected error correction. This is an immediate application of the Hamiltonian-in-Lindblad-span obstruction, not a new general QEC theorem.

It rules out a tempting free combination: use the same force-loop interaction as before, then claim to remove all collective which-path noise by exact QEC while retaining its projected entangling action. A construction evading this must change the operations, encoding, error model, or information access and show the resulting gate explicitly.

No-knowledge output erasure changes information access and does not contradict this obstruction. Critically, neither does a special finite-angle encoded gate: exp(i Phi S^2) can preserve a code nontrivially even though P S^2 P is scalar. The explicit binomial-code counterexample and ordinary Kerr-gate predecessors are in `warm_transduction_encoded_competitor.md`. The projection obstruction must not be presented as a no-go theorem for arbitrary fixed-angle protected gates.

## Obstruction 2: finite force and target noise impose a coherent-gate floor

Suppose |f(t)|<=F and define A=integral|beta|^2. The exact phase identity and Cauchy-Schwarz imply

    |Phi| <= F sqrt(T A),
    A >= Phi^2/(F^2 T).

This is independent of the shape of the damped closed loop. If one required target coherence has independent native-noise exponent aT and residual mediator/observer exponent bA, then its total exponent obeys

    aT+bA >= aT+b Phi^2/(F^2 T)
           >= 2 |Phi| sqrt(a b)/F.

Thus arbitrarily long, weak bus loops cannot give arbitrarily accurate coherent gates when both a>0 and b>0. Missing hot-port leakage supplies one concrete positive b; imperfect observed-bath erasure can supply another. A bound on actual gate accuracy still needs the relevant coherence gaps and the complete target channel.

Also |beta(t)|<=Ft gives the simple finite-control duration bound

    T >= sqrt(2|Phi|)/F.

These bounds need no adiabatic elimination or assumed number of faults.
