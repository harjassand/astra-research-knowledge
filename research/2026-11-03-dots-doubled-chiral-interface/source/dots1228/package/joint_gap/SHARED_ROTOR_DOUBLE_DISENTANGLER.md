# Shared-rotor doubled symmetry disentangler and concrete chiral test

2026-11-03. Algebraic completion of the symmetry-frame input to FINITE_AUXILIARY_COERCIVE_REGULATOR.md. Uses the TPF bosonic cochain intertwiner, but writes the fermionic dressing in a directly verifiable order instead of silently applying two independent-rotor disentanglers.

## Shared-rotor charge ledger

One doubled base has one rotor phi and two decoration copies. Its axial generator is

    Q_A,double = 2 s(phi) + K_1(phi) + K_2'(phi),
    K_2' = F_grade K_2^* F_grade†.

The second decoration retains axial charge; complex conjugating an exponential alone would reverse it and is incorrect. Its desired local rotation is exp(+i theta K_2'), equivalently graded INVERSE conjugation of exp(+i theta K_2). The vector-charge identification exchanges the free ± labels but does not add another rotor or another shear.

For base-stack charges (a_alpha,v_alpha), the actual bosonic cochain disentangler D_B has axial weights 2a_alpha and vector/shear weights v_alpha. The same theta ancilla is used across the stack. Its anomaly condition is sum_alpha 2a_alpha v_alpha²=0. The scalar cochain phase in TPF equations (59)–(60) is linear in axial weight, while the shear is applied ONCE: phi_alpha -> phi_alpha+v_alpha theta. Multiplying two complete bosonic disentanglers would double that shear and is wrong.

Fix the literal ket convention T_beta|theta>=|theta+beta>. In period-one angles, define D_B|u,theta>=exp(+2pi i sum_alpha omega_alpha(u,theta))|u+v theta,theta>, using omega of source Eq(60) with axial weights 2a. The PLUS sign is intentional; the source printed minus sign does not give the desired axial sign under these literal conventions. The resulting identity is

    D_B T_theta(beta) D_B† = U_B(beta),

where U_B simultaneously shifts phi_alpha by v_alpha beta and theta by beta, and supplies the scalar axial phase generated by sum 2a_alpha s_alpha. Use radians here; period-one formulas are rescaled by 2pi.

## Explicit periodic fermionic dressing, with correct ordering

For each rotor-controlled matching, put n_e=(1−i gamma_e gamma'_e)/2, a local integer-spectrum projector. Use its graded conjugate n'_e in copy2. The combined doubled K is the sum of these projectors plus a fixed INTEGER normal-ordering offset. For the literal source convention K=(1−sum_e i gamma_e gamma'_e)/2, with N_p pairs per copy,

    K_1+K_2' = sum_e(n_e,1+n'_e,2) + (1−N_p).

Other normal-ordering conventions simply change that known fixed integer. Distribute it as integer scalar countercharges over fixed reference local cells; for example one −1 per reference pair and one +1 on a chosen cell. No volume-sized charge needs to be concentrated at one site. Translation invariance is not required. This deals explicitly with the compact-angle seam: individual half-integer Majorana bilinears would not themselves define periodic theta gates.

Assign each local matching pair e a nearby ancilla theta_j(e), using a fixed local assignment rule. Define R(phi,theta) as the product over both copies and bases of

    exp[i a_alpha theta_j(e) n_e,alpha,c]

and the corresponding local integer countercharge phases. The two copies commute; within each matching all pair factors commute. Each gate is exactly periodic in theta and local. Importantly, the matching is that of the CURRENT/OUTPUT rotor phi, not a rotor silently shifted twice by the shear.

A common shift phi_alpha -> phi_alpha+v_alpha beta preserves every matching because pairings depend on rotor differences. Thus

    R(phi+v beta,theta+beta) R(phi,theta)†
       = exp[i beta sum_alpha a_alpha(K_1+K_2')].

Now set

    D = R D_B.

Acting on coordinate kets, the preceding cocycle identity proves

    D T_theta(beta) D†
      = exp[i beta sum_alpha a_alpha(K_1+K_2')] U_B(beta),

which is precisely the desired full doubled matter-plus-ancilla symmetry. Scalar bosonic phases commute with R, and the matching is invariant under the global vector shift, so no hidden ordering term remains. Applying D† makes the subgroup onsite. Fermion parity is preserved by every gate.

This is the actual TPF mechanism (bosonic shear/cochain phase plus local controlled matching rotations), with an explicit doubled compact-angle ledger. There is a literal-order caveat in the HTML rendering of TPF equations (58),(80),(81): Eq(81)'s projector shift and the displayed WW' order should be reconciled before claiming our formula is verbatim identical. The directly checked R D_B formula above does not depend on resolving that textual convention. The bosonic cocycle is checked directly below; no assumed fermionic intertwiner is used.

## Concrete 3+1D genuinely chiral test

Take two shared-rotor base layers:

    (a_1,v_1)=(1,2),  (a_2,v_2)=(-4,1).

Their original four-Weyl charge multisets are (3,−1,−1,−1) and (−3,−5,4,4). Sum a v²=4−4=0. The doubled construction duplicates EACH multiset; it is not an inverse stack. The actual D_B uses axial weights (2,−8), vector weights (2,1); its anomaly coefficient is 2*2²−8*1²=0. Each of the two fermion copies uses weights (1,−4).

The eight-charge original multiset has vanishing charge sum and cubic sum. After removing the ordinary vectorlike ±3 pair, the genuinely chiral six-charge set is

    {−5,−1,−1,−1,4,4};  sum q=0, sum q³=0.

The candidate therefore yields TWO copies of this six-charge set if that optional mass removal is implemented; retaining the ±3 pairs gives sixteen Weyls initially. This is an Abelian chiral test, not the Standard Model and not a 1+1D 3450 test.

There are two shared matter rotor coordinates plus ONE theta ancilla per coarse vertex on each relevant boundary. Thus the rotor factor after common cutoff is (2N+1)^3 per such vertex, not the factor for four independent matter rotors. For a two-interface test with separate vertex sets, add their coordinate counts. Decoration and free finite-fermion factors multiply this rotor dimension and retain the astronomical Wilson refinement costs.

D_B has fixed integer weights (2,−8;2,1). Its pulled-back floor hyperplanes and polynomial phase derivatives can be enumerated on each constant-size light cone. R adds only the original pair-difference rounding walls and smooth local theta phases, with integer countercharges bounded by the chosen local reference-cell assignment. ## Direct bosonic sign/cocycle proof and actual-cochain check
Let a denote the bosonic axial weight (2q_A for a doubled base), v=q_V, m=round(d(u+v theta)), b=round(theta), r=round(d theta), c=round(a u), and l=round(u). Under theta->theta+beta, m and r are unchanged. Put k=round(theta+beta)−round(theta), a 0-cochain. Expanding the literal Eq(60) gives, after integrating total coboundaries on a closed triangulation,

    integral[omega(u,theta+beta)−omega(u,theta)]
      = beta a integral m cup dm
        + a v integral[dtheta cup dk cup (m−dl)−theta cup dk cup dm].

The last integrand is exactly d[theta cup dk cup(m−dl)] by the simplicial cup-product Leibniz rule, and integrates to zero. Thus the phase difference has PLUS beta a S. The negative printed phase would yield the opposite scalar axial action in our explicit ket convention. Compact theta-periodicity uses the separate anomaly-cancellation identity sum a v²=0; changing the overall phase sign does not alter periodicity.
