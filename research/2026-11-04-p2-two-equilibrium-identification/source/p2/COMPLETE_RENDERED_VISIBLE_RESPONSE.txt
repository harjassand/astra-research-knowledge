Selected consequence: one calibrated force change can identify hidden dynamics

I preserved the three earlier results and selected a different operation: identify an otherwise ambiguous hidden physical mechanism well enough to predict its response to every subsequent visible force protocol.

The completed construction shows that, within a specified class of reversible switching diffusions, two complete equilibrium observation laws—before and after one known spatially varying potential is applied—determine the hidden dynamics up to relabelling. The second experiment fixes an ambiguity that unlimited unforced observations can leave unresolved.

This is a population identifiability theorem: its inputs are exact probability laws, including all multitime statistics. It does not establish that two finite recordings suffice, or that acquiring and reconstructing those laws is inexpensive. I carried the construction through explicit recovery formulas, exact failure examples, an information-acquisition bound, and numerical checks.

1. Why this became the strongest opening

The consequential gap is the passage from predicting what a system does naturally to predicting what it will do when physically changed.

The preceding passive-observation counterexample made that gap exact. Two systems could agree on the entire observed equilibrium trajectory law, their mobility, every static equilibrium response, and every linear endpoint response, yet disagree on nonlinear transient response. More accurate passive prediction would not resolve that ambiguity.

I compared three mechanisms for the next consequence:

Mechanism	Operation it could enable	Decisive limitation
Continue compiling interventions from native simulator endpoints	Generate altered dynamics inside an opaque simulator	Complete checkpoints, copying, editing and exterior simulation remain substantial resources; ordinary local-table acquisition is competitive.
Reuse trajectories through work identities or likelihood reweighting	Predict another driven ensemble from existing trajectories	The required microscopic likelihood ingredients or calibrated path information must actually be available. An unidentified hidden model cannot supply them automatically.
Change a physically known observable potential and use the resulting dynamics to identify the mechanism	Predict an entire family of future forces from a limited family of calibration conditions	Requires a precise physical actuator law, an identifiable model class, and sufficient temporal information.

The third mechanism offered the strongest constructive consequence.

This also survived comparison with established methods. Fukasawa already derives filtering, likelihood and EM machinery for switching diffusions with position-dependent hidden transition rates, the closely matched class here. That paper explicitly leaves suitable identifiability conditions and asymptotic statistical theory open. Thus there is a distinction between being able to evaluate and optimize a likelihood and knowing that the physical mechanism is uniquely determined by the observations.

Recent exact Bayesian methods provide powerful inference for discretely observed switching diffusions, with released implementation. Their exactness concerns the posterior target and removal of time-discretization bias; it does not resolve every structural ambiguity in the larger class of coupled hidden dynamics.

Completed theorem

If two models satisfying these assumptions have the same two observed sequence laws, then they have the same number of hidden states, and their equilibrium roots, drift functions and switching-rate functions agree up to one permutation of hidden labels.

It follows that they predict the same complete observed path laws under every subsequent regular visible-force protocol, including finite amplitudes, time-dependent potentials and finite sequences of feedback choices based on observed history.

Covered preparations include native equilibrium, equilibrium under another known potential, observable conditioning, and preparations generated by covered interventions. Arbitrary prescribed hidden-state preparations are additional operations.

The theorem requires no hidden-state measurement, physical state copying, checkpoint restoration, local state editing, or fast equilibration of the hidden variables.

8. Preserved capital and the completed consequence

The prior results remain intact:

Sharp local-rate acquisition: the matching endpoint-call and native-duration laws remain statements about those resources. Complete checkpoint handling and native backend work remain charged separately; ordinary whole-table inversion remains competitive.
Positive finite interventions: rollback and temporary copied runs retain their positive sampled-law guarantee, with valid local edits, complete restorable state, rate envelopes and outgoing-influence costs. No full continuous-path or laboratory-resetting guarantee has been added.
Exact passive-observation counterexample: its equality of linear endpoint responses is preserved precisely. The new two-time witness makes explicit why that equality does not extend to all first-order path responses.

Astra supplied useful cross-branch machinery, especially the idea that a physically specified operation can resolve an orthogonal ambiguity in a passive representation. The selected diffusion result carries that mechanism through a different physical interface, a complete identification proof, explicit reconstruction and exact limitations. Historical priority still requires specialist review.

The completed scientific consequence is that one calibrated change of a visible potential can make an entire hidden dynamics model—and therefore its responses to new forces—identifiable under stated structural conditions. The result also specifies when weak path calibration fails and proves that acquiring the missing information requires physical forcing and observation resources. Efficient finite-data reconstruction and a validated natural-system application remain separate achievements.
