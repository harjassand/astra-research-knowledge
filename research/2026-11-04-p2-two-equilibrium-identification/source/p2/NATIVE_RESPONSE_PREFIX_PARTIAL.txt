## 2. The precise scientific question

Consider a system with:

- an observed continuous position \(X\) on a circle;
- an unobserved internal state \(I\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\);
- smooth reversible dynamics;
- the same known diffusion coefficient and mobility in every internal state.

Use units in which the observed diffusion coefficient is one. The generator is

\[
(Lf)_i
=
f_i''+2\alpha_i(x)f_i'
+\sum_{j\ne i}k_{ij}(x)(f_j-f_i).
\]

The internal switching rates \(k_{ij}(x)\) may depend on position. Consequently, the observed position process can retain memory and need not be Markov.

Write the equilibrium density as

\[
\pi_i(x)=r_i(x)^2,\qquad r_i(x)>0,
\qquad \int\sum_i r_i(x)^2\,dx=1.
\]

Reversibility gives a useful symmetric representation:

\[
S=\partial_x^2 I+V(x),\qquad Sr=0,
\]

where

\[
\alpha_i=\frac{r_i'}{r_i},
\qquad
V_{ij}=k_{ij}\frac{r_i}{r_j}
\]

and \(V\) is symmetric.

The physical intervention is specified exactly. Adding a known dimensionless potential \(v(x)\), common to every hidden mode, changes the generator by

\[
L_v=L-v'(x)\partial_x I.
\]

The hidden switching rates at a given position and the mobility remain unchanged. This is a substantive actuator premise. Merely knowing the new equilibrium density would not establish it.

The structural condition is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{span}\{r(x):x\in\mathbb T\}=\mathbb R^m.}
\]

Equivalently, the positive functions \(r_1,\ldots,r_m\) are linearly independent. This condition restricts the admitted physical models. Fitting a full-rank model to finite observations does not certify it.

Choose a probe with \(v'(x)\ne0\) on a dense set. For example,

\[
v(x)=\lambda\cos x,\qquad \lambda\ne0.
\]

The observations are the complete stationary sequence laws

\[
\mathcal L_0(X_0,X_\Delta,X_{2\Delta},\ldots)
\]

and

\[
\mathcal L_v(X_0,X_\Delta,X_{2\Delta},\ldots)
\]

for one known positive frame interval \(\Delta\).

### Completed theorem

**If two models satisfying these assumptions have the same two observed sequence laws, then they have the same number of hidden states, and their equilibrium roots, drift functions and switching-rate functions agree up to one permutation of hidden labels.**

It follows that they predict the same complete observed path laws under **every subsequent regular visible-force protocol**, including finite amplitudes, time-dependent potentials and finite sequences of feedback choices based on observed history.

Covered preparations include native equilibrium, equilibrium under another known potential, observable conditioning, and preparations generated by covered interventions. Arbitrary prescribed hidden-state preparations are additional operations.

The theorem requires no hidden-state measurement, physical state copying, checkpoint restoration, local state editing, or fast equilibration of the hidden variables.

## 3. The central construction

### Passive observations determine a representation with a remaining rotation

Let \(M_f\) multiply every hidden component by the observed function \(f(x)\), and set

\[
P=e^{\Delta S}.
\]

A stationary observed multitime moment has the form

\[
\left\langle
r,M_{f_0}P M_{f_1}P\cdots P M_{f_n}r
\right\rangle.
\]

Because the system is reversible, \(P\) and the real multiplication operators are self-adjoint. The observed moments therefore determine inner products between all vectors formed by applying such operator sequences to \(r\). This constructs a canonical Hilbert-space representation of the observed process.

The full-span condition makes that representation complete. The key calculation is

\[
[S,M_f]=2f'\partial_x+f''.
\]

A projection onto an unobserved part of the representation would have to commute with every \(M_f\) and with \(P\). Commutation with observed multiplication makes it a matrix field depending on \(x\). Commutation with \(S\), through the displayed identity, forces that field to be constant. Full span then excludes any proper remaining projection.

This also explains why a fixed camera interval is sufficient **at the level of exact laws**. The reversible generator has the unique self-adjoint logarithm

\[
S=\frac1\Delta\log P.
\]

There is no structural temporal alias in this class. Estimating that logarithm from noisy finite data can still be extremely ill-conditioned.

Two identical passive laws consequently determine representations related by a constant orthogonal matrix:

\[
\widetilde r=Or,\qquad
\widetilde V=OVO^\top.
\]

That rotation need not be a relabelling. The preserved two-mode counterexample is an explicit case in which a nonpermutation rotation produces another physically valid model.

The new equilibrium root is

This is the crucial alignment step. The two experiments cannot choose unrelated hidden coordinate systems.

Subtracting their aligned symmetric generators gives

Since \(v'\) is nonzero on a dense set and the coefficients are continuous,

\[

### The remaining rotation must be a physical relabelling

Entry by entry,

\[

\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\widetilde\alpha_a(x)=\alpha_i(x)
\quad\text{for every }x.

If a row of \(O\) mixed two different modes \(i,j\), their logarithmic derivative functions would agree everywhere:

\[
\frac{r_i'}{r_i}=\frac{r_j'}{r_j}.

On the connected circle this implies \(r_i=c\,r_j\), contradicting full span. Thus every row has only one nonzero entry. Orthogonality makes \(O\) a signed permutation, and positivity of the roots removes the negative signs.

**The hidden drift and switching functions are therefore identified in their physical basis, up to labels.**

This is a generator identity, so its consequence extends to finite forces. It does not depend on truncating a weak-response expansion.

### A companion sufficiency theorem

Under **full passive cyclicity**—a weaker condition than full span—one known spatial potential probe also suffices through complete first-order path responses.

The operations can be kept concrete:

- apply the probe weakly during one frame interval of duration \(\Delta\);
- observe on the same \(\Delta\) grid before and after the pulse;
- acquire the first derivative of every observed path-cylinder expectation.

In the common passive representation, the possible control discrepancy is a bounded multiplication operator \(E\). The one-frame response determines

\[
T_\Delta(E)
=
\int_0^\Delta e^{sS}E e^{(\Delta-s)S}\,ds.

In an eigenbasis of the self-adjoint \(S\), each entry of \(E\) is multiplied by

\[
\frac{e^{\Delta\mu_a}-e^{\Delta\mu_b}}
{\mu_a-\mu_b},

This is again an exact-law result. Tiny divided differences make inversion statistically difficult. It does not justify extrapolating a small finite dataset to arbitrary strong forcing.
