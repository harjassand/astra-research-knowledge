8. The conventional construction won the timing comparison

I kept an ordinary route active: syntactic dependency analysis identifies state bits that may depend on inputs; degree bounds certify affinity in those bits; an affine symbolic interpreter then propagates original-input forms through the program and solves the same constraints.

It receives the same raw circuits and conditions.

On the sixteen-bit example, one local execution took approximately:

Route	Time
Semantic subspace acquisition	6 ms
Subsequent 80-step constrained solve	25 ms
Conventional certification, symbolic execution and solve	9 ms

These are illustrative local timings, not a performance study. The ordinary route won.

After the affine recoding, that particular syntactic recognizer declined, while the semantic recognizer still found the subspace. This establishes a difference in admission power for those implementations. It does not establish superiority over stronger program normalization or semantic compilation.

The broader conventional landscape is also substantial. Panini, for example, already implements reusable conditioning, model counting and uniform sampling through knowledge compilation. I did not run it and am not claiming a performance comparison against it.

The constructed result in this branch is the combination:

seed structure discovery with the program’s actual input differences;
acquire the canonical least translation subspace through forced witnesses;
test the exact conditions needed to exclude nonlinear interactions between inputs and state deviations;
establish complete admission and rejection for that global class;
obtain the coNP-completeness classification;
use a concrete nonlinear baseline to construct exact constraints on original input words at every finite horizon.

The existing comparator audited the all-state quantifier, minimality, query count and consumer. Its audit supplied the short-rejection-certificate argument that completed the coNP classification; I checked that argument and implemented its verifier.

The main remaining limitations are concrete. Admission proofs can be expensive. The global certificate can reject programs whose behavior is simpler on one reachable region. Arbitrary nonlinear output conditions and biased weighted counting need additional machinery. The tested programs do not establish a useful-workload speed advantage.

The resulting asset is a complete mechanism for acquiring reusable structure without first learning the entire nonlinear system. Its structure is forced by input effects, its successful certificates apply to every future horizon, and its consumer reconstructs the original input sequences exactly. The unresolved practical question is where that semantic acquisition admits useful programs that strong conventional analysis cannot already handle cheaply.
