The exact source is [the public primary repository](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Uniform-Permanence-in-Weakly-Reversible-Mass-Action-Systems-October-5-2026); its four governing TeX sections were retrieved read-only through the GitHub connector and preserved in sources/openai-permanence-20261005/.

The mechanism uses a finite concave affine minimum whose active slopes track logarithmic exponents, then orders complex levels and compares upward/downward cut fluxes. Fixed finite slopes give a nonzero monomial-order separation. Convex trapping plateaux at successive scales yield a common absorbing set in a fixed positive class. The arbitrary-tolerance affine lemma is credited there to the September 25 precursor; it is not an author invention here.

This changes the strongest deterministic comparator but not the missing stochastic boundary operation. It uses positive real monomials; a bounded-copy source can instead have an identically zero falling factorial. Moreover negating a concave affine minimum produces a convex maximum: the infinitesimal deterministic sign alone does not upper-bound its jump generator across switching facets. Bulk use can be legitimate after paying finite-jump curvature and sign-compatible collars, but it does not acquire a cold activation law, physical stopping time or receiving reward for the six-species process. No general stochastic recurrence conclusion is transferred from it.

The live construction therefore uses the raw normal-cell identity and genuine stopping/occupation bounds. The primary deterministic result remains a useful comparison, not a furnished global stochastic interface. The current source read is method-specific, not a new historical-priority audit.
