# A finite graph rule for joint slow-source repayment

Author synthesis. This is a conditional, checkable episode criterion with a completed six-species instance in BALANCED_NORMAL_CELL_PHYSICAL_EPISODE.md. It is not a theorem that every weakly reversible raw list has the required policy or reference barrier. It does not supply a global boundary receiver for free.

## 1. A bounded packet potential fixes the receiving contract

Let a finite auxiliary mode set M have fixed integer packet vectors tau_p. Let ell have strictly positive coordinates and define n=ell.(x-tau_p). Choose a fixed C so z=n+C>=1 at every admitted physical/mode state. The common receiver is U=D z^p; it is normlike because the packet set is bounded. Every raw reaction is counted ONCE: assign it either a normal mode update or an unexpected exit update. All chosen updates must be valid at their actual physical outputs.

Require each normal update to keep n fixed. At a fixed normal cell, positive ell and finite modes imply finite physical state space; extra conserved lattice coordinates can split it further. All unexpected updates have bounded |Delta n|<=B, directly from the finite raw and packet lists. Thus the receiver is acquired globally from finite data, even at outputs for which no return policy has yet been proved.

The six-species instance dispenses with auxiliary ambiguity: tau depends on actual capped anchors, its count is m(A)+m(D), and ell=1.

## 2. Acquiring a normal reference is a finite identity problem

A useful ansatz on a translated conservative fiber is

    mu(x,p)=a_p chi(residual)/x!,

where chi has fixed edge ratios along the conservative lattice. Substitute every NORMAL raw mass-action channel into mu Q=0. Multiplying a channel propensity by 1/x! leaves kappa/(x-source)!. Terms with the same residual background must balance. Equality of those finite background/mode coefficients gives a finite linear system in the a_p (and explicit conservative edge-ratio compatibility conditions).

Finding a positive solution and checking every background group acquires an invariant normal reference measure. Failure of this ansatz is a scoped refusal, not instability. A normal reference is not an equilibrium law of the physical entrance or of a surviving trajectory. A numerical implementation must actually find/check its positive coefficients and all physical boundary omissions. In particular, signed residual states supported by finite packet buffers must not be deleted.

In the completed instance the coefficient solution is a_(i,j)=1/(alpha_i beta_j), chi=2^k. Each normal block flux closes around its full translated cycle; the conservative pair balances exactly. No physical complex-balance condition on the full raw list follows.

## 3. The joint crossing rule is a graph decision, not an ordering assumption

On the finite normal mode graph select edge groups G,B,H. They count, respectively, opportunities controlling repaying, harmful and neutral exits. Suppose finite potentials phi_B,phi_H and constants K_B,K_H are acquired with

    1_B(e)-K_B 1_G(e) <= phi_B(target)-phi_B(source),
    1_H(e)-K_H 1_G(e) <= phi_H(target)-phi_H(source).        (F1)

Core edges that preserve mode have zero group weights. Summing along ANY actual normal path gives

    J_B<=K_B J_G+osc(phi_B),
    J_H<=K_H J_G+osc(phi_H).                              (F2)

There is a finite exact decision procedure: (F1) exists for a fixed K iff the corresponding weighted directed graph has no positive-weight cycle. Longest-path relaxation from a super-source constructs phi, or returns a positive-cycle obstruction. If every simple cycle containing a B edge also contains a G edge, K=|M| suffices for indicator groups; otherwise a B-positive/G-zero cycle is an exact obstruction. The same applies to H. This joint rule permits several interacting activation loops and requires no total source ordering.

For the two-block normal graph take G=the two phase2 edges, B=the two phase3 edges and H=the two phase1 edges. K_B=K_H=1 works. Explicit potentials are

    phi_B(i,j)=-1_(i=3)-1_(j=3),
    phi_H(i,j)= 1_(i=2)+1_(j=2).

All finite mode edges satisfy (F1) exactly. Starting at00 even removes the B additive remainder. These are raw crossing identities, not mean-profile comparisons.

## 4. Derive the actual slow episode

Run the genuine normal process until its first unexpected channel sigma. Let h be total unexpected hazard and G0=(-Q+diag h)^(-1). One must ACQUIRE the following quantitative ingredients from the raw cell:

1. a.s. absorption and physical E sigma<=C z^s, including every normal path;
2. an exact positive reference mu and a bound on reverse killed mean C' z^t;
3. a reference barrier giving a small actual occupation remainder epsilon(z) for any regions where the following hazard comparisons fail.

For example, (2) gives G0(i,j)<=C' z^t mu(j)/mu(i); summing actual hazards over a cold barrier earns (3). A finite raw word with polynomially small success probability and a positive killing hazard away from designated regeneration modes earns (1),(2). Neither a stationary fiber nor a mixing gap is required. These bounds are conditions to PROVE, not supplied oracle inputs; the completed instance proves all three explicitly.

Partition unexpected channels into repaying, neutral, harmful sets with respective mass increments <=-d, <=0, <=B. On the warm region suppose raw factorial comparisons give

    h_- >= a z^-r Lambda_G,
    h_+ <= b z^(-r-eta) Lambda_B,
    h_0 <= c z^-r Lambda_H,                               (F3)

where eta>0 and Lambda counts the corresponding genuine normal edge hazards. Cold remainders, including normal crossings there, are paid in epsilon(z). Apply genuine channel compensators and (F2):

    P_+ <= C z^-eta P_-+o(1),
    P_0 <= C' P_-+o(1).                                  (F4)

Here the additive potential remainders contribute O(z^-r) or O(z^(-r-eta)); require r>0, or explicitly pay a nonvanishing remainder when r=0. For the warm-only lower bound in (F3), the missing G occupation must also be in epsilon(z). Since every actual first exit is included and P_-+P_0+P_+=1, (F4) gives P_->=p0>0 and hence E Delta n<=-d0<0 for large z. The bounded raw increment gives E(Delta n)^2<=B^2.

Choose an integer p>=max(2,s+1). Direct expansion at the SAME actual stop yields

    E[(z+Delta n)^p-z^p] <=-p d0 z^(p-1)+O(z^(p-2)).

For large z, multiplying by an acquired D pays E sigma<=C z^s. Therefore

    E[sigma+U(actual output)]<=U(input).                  (F5)
