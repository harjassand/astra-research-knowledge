# Polynomial physical return bounds from the banked ordered paths

Author deduction from the completed ordered-cycle path estimates, using the weaker common physical contract. The recurrence theorem and its raw paths are banked; this is not a new reaction family or an independent audit. The new quantitative operation is a polynomial bound in the INITIAL population, replacing the previous exponential upper bound where a coupled launch needs it. Exponential physical return remains false.

## 1. Statement

For every ordered C_k block, every fixed positive raw rate list and every real p>=0, there is a normlike physical receiver V_p with

    M^(p+2)<=C V_p(x),   V_p(x)<=C_p(1+M)^(p+2),
    E_x integral_0^tau0 (1+M(X_t))^p dt
         <=C_p(1+M(x))^(p+2).                             (P2)

The finite constants have recipes from the banked raw words, overrun moment bound, finite profile and finite head trace. They are not numerically materialized here. In particular

    E_x tau0^r<=C_r(1+M)^(2r),
    E_x J0^r<=C'_r(1+M)^(3kr)                             (P3)

for integer r>=1, where J0 is the genuine jump count. At zero use positive return, including its first holding time and jump. No such activity bound is demanded for arbitrary weak reversibility; this particular autonomous block has earned it.

## 2. A receiver with the same outputs on every face

Use the banked section Z={A=0} union {B=C=0}. Fix a finite decreasing profile f_b, supported on b<B_*, and set

    W_p(M)=(1+M)^(p+2),
    V_p(x)=D_p[W_p(M)+1_(A=0)(1+M)^p f_B].                (P4)

All off-section states receive exactly D_p W_p; the A-axis also receives D_p W_p. The correction has finite width and size and is lower order than W_p. Thus (P4) is a single proper physical function, including every abort and protected-region exit. One need not first solve a Dirichlet receiver to the section: the weaker physical contract permits an off-section endpoint to be received at W_p and then use its own proved episode.

For any fixed p and any stopped path whose positive count overrun Y has a uniform exponential moment,

    E[W_p(M+Y)-W_p(M)]<=C_p(1+M)^(p+1).                  (P5)

This is the mean-value inequality followed by the earned moments of Y. If count drops by at least1, its W_p loss is at least c_p(1+M)^(p+1). A profile increase is at most f_max(1+M+Y)^p, so every no-overrun count decrease absorbs it for sufficiently large M. Zero radial change never decreases B in the banked no-overrun classification, so cannot increase f.

## 3. The banked off-section normalizer now pays polynomial reward

The actual at-most-k-jump normalizer decreases M by at least1 with probability 1-C/M outside a finite box, and never increases M by more than3k. The exact raw-source estimates of the completed theorem prove this; no new path is assumed. Hence

    E[W_p(M_out)-W_p(M)]<=-c_p(1+M)^(p+1)

for large M. A section landing adds at most O(f_max M^p). The actual physical reward integral(1+M_t)^p dt is at most C(1+M)^p, because there are <=k jumps, bounded prefix overrun and q>=kappa_0. Enlarge the finite head after f is acquired. Scaling D_p then proves the full common contract off the section.

## 4. Interior plane: retain all unbounded overruns

Start (0,b,c), b,c>=K+k. The banked B-deletion word has probability >=d/(c+1). Its W_p loss is >=c_p(1+M)^(p+1), after absorbing any finite profile term by the population threshold. Every other no-overrun branch has nonpositive receiver increment.

The first overrun probability is <=U/(bc). CONDITIONALLY on it, the banked actual overrun count has a uniform exponential moment, including all outward excursions and protected exits. Thus (P5) pays its positive receiver increment by

    C_p(1+M)^(p+1)/(bc)
       +C'_p f_max(1+M)^p/(bc).

The negative word supplies order (1+M)^(p+1)/(c+1). Choose K large from the first term, BEFORE choosing f. The second term is lower order in M once f is acquired: bc>=K M/2. Enlarge only the final population head to absorb it. This avoids circularly choosing K from f. The remaining receiver loss is >=c(1+M)^p, since M>=c.

The same overrun exponential moment pays the actual physical reward by C(1+M)^p: q>=kappa_0, each prefix M_t<=M+3J and E[J(1+3J)^p] is finite uniformly. Scaling D_p pays it. No exponential reward or stationary fiber is required.

## 5. Low-B plane: acquire the finite profile

For b<B_*, c large, use the exact banked two-branch body. Its ordinary return keeps (b,c); its good conversion gives (b+1,c-1), with probability >=p_b/2, where

    p_b=kappa_0/[kappa_0+kappa_1(b+1)].

Every later unexpected source is an actual off-section abort, with total probability <=D_C/c and bounded count overrun. Its positive W_p cost is <=C_p M^(p+1), so the COMPLETE expected bad cost is <=C'_p(1+M)^p for c>=B_*+1. An abort does not add a section profile. The good conversion keeps M exactly fixed and decreases the correction by

    (1+M)^p (f_b-f_(b+1)).

Acquire f_b-f_(b+1)=G_p/p_b, f_(B_*)=0, with G_p large enough that this conversion loss dominates the displayed bad coefficient and leaves a strictly positive multiple of (1+M)^p. The closed finite sum for f is the same raw arithmetic as in the completed theorem. No neighboring landing value is supplied. Its physical reward is bounded by C(1+M)^p because the body has bounded length.

## 6. Low-C plane and A-axis

For c<B_* and b large, the banked body has a negative count-one return with probability >=d_C>0. Its positive abort probability is O(1/b), with bounded count overrun; all aborts receive off-section W_p. Thus the loss is order M^(p+1), while positive receiver cost and physical reward are O(M^p). The good endpoints have B>=B_* after enlarging the head, so no finite profile is inserted.

On the A-axis the banked completed repayment word deletes one A with probability >=d_A/A, returns exactly the same axis point on all other good branches, and aborts with probability O(A^-2). An abort has bounded count overrun and lands off the section. Therefore the negative W_p contribution is order A^p and its positive contribution is O(A^(p-1)); the bounded-length physical reward is O(A^p). Choose D_p to pay its coefficient. This is the actual borrowed-inventory word, not its unpaid first switch.

## 7. Closing physical time and the moment hierarchy

The choices are noncircular: raw paths and overrun moment order; fixed interior K,B_*; finite low-B profile; one large finite head; common scale D_p. Every state outside that head has a proved actual episode paying integral(1+M)^p plus its actual endpoint at (P4). Episodes use a finite number of jumps a.s.; their physical reward is proved finite. The weak stopped-episode argument gives finite expected reward to the head. Finite irreducible head trace closes the reward to zero, with only a finite additive constant. Since (P4) is polynomially bounded, this proves (P2).

For time moments, induct on r using

    E_x tau0^r=r E_x integral_0^tau0 E_(X_t)tau0^(r-1) dt

and use (P2) with p=2r-2. For jumps, the same integer decomposition as in the cold-launch proof and q(x)<=C(1+M)^(3k-2) gives the recurrence of exponents a_r=a_(r-1)+(3k-2)+2=3kr. Localization justifies each step. This proves (P3) with raw finite constants.

## 8. What this changes in the live coupled problem

For a cold initial population R, the autonomous trial's physical moments now grow polynomially in R, as do its jump moments. The Poisson-input/excursion argument consequently gives POLYNOMIAL cold-launch population moment bounds, rather than merely finite constants on each head. This removes an exponential-cost artifact in the launch estimate.

It does not make the clean-overlap minorization uniform: forcing a growing cold state through zero can still have an exponentially small probability, and the number of central draining stages chosen from that probability can grow too fast. Nor does it cover high-anchor and large-difference faces of the coupled system. The full remaining operation is a joint activation/repayment rule for an UNBOUNDED cold subsystem, with a success/observation tradeoff that the same global receiver can pay. Improving a fixed finite-head probability is not a substitute for that rule.
