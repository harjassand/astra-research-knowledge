## 2. Closest inspected primary sources

### General slow/fast path actions

Kraaij and Schlottke, [A large deviation principle for Markovian slow-fast systems](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.05686), 2020. Sections 2–3 and Theorems 3.1–3.2 were inspected. Their framework has a finite-dimensional slow-state embedding and fixed compact fast space. Its action can be expressed through fast occupation measures or a principal-eigenvalue problem.

This is a substantial predecessor to any broad claim about predictable actions under fast hidden dynamics. The growing conservative field and one phase coordinate per bond require additional volume-uniform estimates. That is a scope distinction, not evidence of historical priority.

Yasodharan and Sundaresan, [Large deviations of mean-field interacting particle systems in a fast varying environment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.06855), version dated 2021. The model, Theorem 2.2, initial-LDP condition, and exponential-tightness framework were inspected. Their joint empirical-measure/environment-occupation LDP uses stochastic exponentials for finite-state mean-field particles and a finite fast environment.

It is another direct predecessor against broad novelty claims for particle-system rare histories in fast environments. Its mean-field state/rate structure differs from arbitrary bounded nonlocal bond amplitudes on a growing ring.

### Dynamic-conductance hydrodynamics

The model, Theorem 3.2, path-space scope, and self-duality discussion were inspected. Configuration-independent conductances allow a one-particle invariance principle to supply density hydrodynamics.

Here one-particle closure can fail exactly. Take one amplitude `alpha_i=a(2 eta_(i+2)-1)` and the others zero on a ring of at least five sites. It is invariant under its own swap, yet the generator applied to `eta_i` contains quadratic occupation terms when its phase cosine is nonzero. Thus the linear single-site occupation span need not be invariant. This calculation explains why the broader interface needs a separate phase-energy replacement argument.

## 3. Which assumptions buy this consequence?

The following are inferences from the constructed proofs, not summaries of a single source.

**Exact common phase mean:** it removes the unknown amplitudes from the averaged generator. Means that vary across frozen sectors are not equalized by phase agitation.

**Own-swap invariance:** it supplies a common reversible joint form despite nonlocal visible dependence. An amplitude-small directional error can instead create an order-one macroscopic field.

**An actual independent selective operation:** its additive phase energy suppresses the centered current at speed volume with a cost proportional to `1/kappa_N`. The process is physically changed; this is not a change of representation of unchanged kinetics.

**A one-dimensional conservative receiver:** exact conservation bounds the spatial current's total variation by circulation plus one. That compact-containment mechanism has not been supplied here in higher dimensions.

**Deterministic associated initial profiles:** the flat initial profile closes the simple time-zero smoothing construction and scalar contraction. Ordinary high-probability initialization need not preserve a specified rare-event exponent.

**Correct confidence scaling:** fixed-confidence calibration cannot simply be added to an exponentially small deployment probability. The root's explicit mixture obstruction is decisive on this point.
