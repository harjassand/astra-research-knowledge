## 1. The independent construction: compatible operations on the actual row sets

I started with the complete tables themselves.

For each relation \(R\subseteq D^k\), regard its explicitly listed rows as a finite set. Its coordinate projections

\[
\pi_i:R\longrightarrow D
\]

are known maps. Seek an operation \(m_D\) on \(D\), together with operations \(m_R\) on the row sets, satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\pi_i\bigl(m_R(r,s,t)\bigr)
=
m_D\bigl(\pi_i(r),\pi_i(s),\pi_i(t)\bigr).
}
\tag{1}
\]

Require the Maltsev identities on every set.

This is an exact formulation of the acquisition problem:

- If \(m_D\) preserves every input relation, applying it coordinatewise defines each \(m_R\).
- Conversely, an actual output row \(m_R(r,s,t)\) satisfying (1) certifies that the coordinatewise \(m_D\)-output belongs to \(R\).

No decomposition, group law, closure oracle, or solver is needed to construct this formulation.

Writing \(d=|D|\), and letting \(t_j\) and \(k_j\) be the row count and arity of \(R_j\), it has

\[
d^3+\sum_j t_j^3
\]

operation positions and

\[
\sum_j k_jt_j^3
\]

coordinate compatibility requirements. Its entire explicit representation is polynomial in \(N\).

The difficulty is exactly the global composition requirement: **the same domain triple occurring in different rows, coordinates, or relations must receive the same output everywhere.**

### Making every transport invertible would exclude valid Maltsev inputs

There is a useful general theorem here.

> **If a Maltsev polymorphism has every map \(x\mapsto m(x,b,c)\) bijective, then every nonempty pp-definable relation has equally sized nonempty fibres under every coordinate projection.**

To prove it, let \(R\) be such a relation and choose \(s,t\in R\). The coordinatewise map

\[
T(x)=m(x,s,t)
\]

preserves \(R\) and is injective, hence bijective because \(R\) is finite. Its induced map on any projected relation is also bijective, taking the projection of \(s\) to that of \(t\). It therefore maps the entire fibre above one projection bijectively onto the other.

But \(E\) above has first-coordinate fibres of sizes

\[
1,\;2,\;2.
\]

So no sufficient operation for this valid input can have every transport invertible.

A Maltsev witness for \(E\) is easy to exhibit. Let \(q(0)=0\), \(q(1)=q(2)=1\), and set

\[
m(x,y,z)=
\begin{cases}
z,&x=y,\\
z,&q(x)=q(y)\ne q(z),\\
x,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]

The identities hold, and the output block depends only on the three input blocks, proving preservation.

This rules out completing the general transport mechanism by silently imposing a group of bijections. General Maltsev compatibility must allow collapse and unequal fibres.
