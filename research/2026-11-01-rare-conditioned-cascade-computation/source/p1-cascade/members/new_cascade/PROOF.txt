LEARNING A SIMULATOR THAT REMAINS ACCURATE AFTER RARE CONDITIONING
31 October 2026. Root construction, with complementary native collaborators.

Scientific conclusion
=====================

An imperfect experiment can acquire more than an accurate predictor: it can
acquire a conservative generative model whose path law minorizes the real
one, and whose representation admits an explicitly constructed conditional
sampler. For the monotone irreversible systems below, this prevents the
usual inverse-event-probability amplification of error. A finite event-time
compilation then computes rare probabilities and generates conditioned
paths without waiting for the rare event and without stepping at the fastest
physical rate. Graph discovery, a usable decomposition, and the sampler are
constructed, not supplied as oracles.

This is a candidate theoretical advance at the stated scope, not a claim of
historical priority, a deployed scientific instrument, or a universal rare
event algorithm. Existing continuous-time graphical models, cumulative
exposure models, event-time encodings, junction-tree algorithms, relative
volume approximation and importance sampling are strong predecessors. The
10-node numerical rarity vignette is actually beaten by an ordinary tailored
importance sampler. The demonstrated acquisition has an enormous conservative
shot bill. Those facts are part of the result, not exceptions hidden from it.

The main new construction developed here combines (i) acquisition of a sparse
monotone minorant even when the true dependency graph is dense; (ii) a
conditional full-path law bound from one-sided exposure errors; and (iii)
rate-uniform relative time discretization followed by reconstruction of the
continuous random drivers. The generalized theorem is not intrinsically
Markov. Its regularity parameter is a scaling exponent of the threshold law.

1. The operation and experimental contract
=========================================

There are n labelled components. A fresh system starts with all components
untriggered. Each can trigger once, from 0 to 1. X(t) is the triggered set.
Independently draw thresholds E_v with known absolutely continuous densities
f_v on positive reals. Component v triggers when

    H_v(t) = integral_0^t a_v(X(s)) ds

first reaches E_v. Exposure ceases for that component after its trigger.
The fixed, unknown a_v is a nonnegative, coordinatewise nondecreasing
function of the OTHER component states. Directed feedback cycles are allowed.
No graph, local table, reaction coordinate, conditioned path, or solver is
given. There are at most n triggers, though the global state space has 2^n
states. For acquisition below use known 0<a_min<=a_v(x)<=a_max<infinity.

The basic clock is E_v~Exp(1). This gives an attractive irreversible CTMC.
The explicit non-Markov extension uses a known rational Weibull shape k_v>=1,

    P(E_v>x)=exp(-x^k_v),

with scale absorbed in a_v. These are ordinary stochastic physical model
contracts, not claims that finite data can verify a Markov/Weibull law or
independence in every unobserved context. Unknown clock shape cannot simply
be estimated to fixed precision and extrapolated to every future deadline.

A legal local experiment selects v, fixes all OTHER component labels to a
chosen 0/1 context, and uses a fresh v with zero accumulated exposure. For a
chosen duration t it observes only whether v triggered. The probability is

    1-exp(-a_v(x)t)                       [exponential],
    1-exp(-(a_v(x)t)^k_v)                 [Weibull].

Repeats are independent fresh preparations. The preparation/clamp cost,
number of resets, exposure duration and control precision are charged.
Forcing another component into state 1 is an explicit experimental control,
not a free sample from a rare conditional physical state. If that control
is unavailable, this acquisition theorem does not apply. In an aging system,
an 'alive' label does not by itself reset accumulated exposure.

Finite Bernoulli counts are the observations. There is no exact real-valued
rate or waiting-time oracle. Exact prescribed exposure duration is the
mathematical instrument idealization; a known small relative timer error can
be absorbed into the log-rate confidence budget, and the cost of achieving
that timing precision remains an instrument cost. Arbitrary unknown bias,
cross-trial dependence, drift or an unspecified reset mechanism is not covered.

Later tasks select a deadline T>0 and an increasing terminal event

    A_T = {X(T) in F},   x in F and y>=x imply y in F.

Requiring any selected subset to have triggered is the principal operation.
It is represented by unary event factors. More general events must be given
as explicit finite factors or a circuit whose auxiliary variables, domain
sizes and added graph width are paid. Cheap membership testing of F alone
does not promise cheap integration. Conditioning is requested only if p>0.

The returned computation is (a) a relative approximation to p=P(A_T), and
(b) a sample from a law close to the complete timed path conditioned on A_T.
Any bounded path statistic then inherits that law guarantee. The theorem
does not make arbitrary intervention optimization or partially observed
planning efficient. Known static exposure multipliers and fresh initial
trigger sets may be admitted under their explicit control semantics; the
same uniform table event then applies and their task width is charged.

2. Threshold scaling and the main execution theorem
==================================================

Assume known kappa_v>=1 satisfy, for 0<c<=1 and x>0,

    f_v(c x) >= c^(kappa_v-1) f_v(x).                    (S)

Use this inequality rather than density ratios at zeros. Put D=sum kappa_v.
Exp(1) has kappa=1. Weibull shape k>=1 has kappa=k because

    f(c x)/f(x)=c^(k-1) exp((1-c^k)x^k).

Gamma shape alpha>=1 likewise has kappa=alpha. The mathematical inequality
also holds with max(1,shape) for smaller positive shapes, but the supplied
generalized program implements only the stated Weibull shapes >=1. A general
density satisfying (S) does not automatically provide a computational CDF or
conditional sampler. The complete algorithmic claim here uses Exp/Weibull,
whose interval masses and inversions are explicit.

Suppose acquisition returns attractive local tables a_minus and scopes J_v
such that, simultaneously over ALL original contexts,

    exp(-rho) a_v(x) <= a_minus_v(x_Jv) <= a_v(x).       (M)

The next sections earn (M) from the binary experiments, without an exact
sparsity assumption on the real system. Fix N>n and Delta=T/N. The compiler
holds exposure inputs at their values at each bin's start. It computes the
probability p_N of A_T in that held model and samples its trigger bins.
It then reconstructs the original threshold vector conditional on those
bins, and runs the UNHELD acquired dynamics on that vector. Denote the
resulting ideal continuous path law by R_N. Then

    exp(-D rho) (1-n/N)^D p <= p_N <= p,                (1)

    TV(R_N, P(.|A_T))
      <= [1-exp(-D rho)] + [1-(1-n/N)^D]
      <= D rho + D n/N.                               (2)

No term in these bounds is 1/p, a_max*T, or a_max/a_min. Taking
rho<=epsilon/(3D), N>=3Dn/epsilon reserves another epsilon/3 for numerical
and finite-output errors. The ideal-law theorem is not a statement that
floating-point timestamps have small TV to an unrounded continuous law;
Section 9 gives the finite-output distinction and a constructive resolution.

For Exp clocks D=n. For bounded shape Weibull clocks D=O(n). If the acquired
factor graph plus event has width w, ordinary positive variable elimination
performs preprocessing in

    O(n (N+1)^(w+1))

arithmetic operations, up to local factor evaluation and the explicit event
description. Backward conditional bin sampling costs O(n(N+1)) per draw
after those tables are retained. Replaying a complete continuous cascade
requires at most n trigger updates. The exponent w is not disguised as a
constant unless a bounded-width regime is actually assumed or discovered.

3. A bit-observation rate assay
==============================

This is a conventional concentration and scale-bracketing component.
For clarity first take Exp clocks. Let a requested log-rate confidence
radius be r in (0,1/64). For a single unknown a in [a_min,a_max], expose
fresh trials at t_j=2^j/a_max until the empirical trigger fraction first
exceeds 1/4. Use

    m0 >= 128 log(2M/eta)

trials per coarse point, where M pays every possible coarse test in the
whole acquisition. Hoeffding gives error <=1/16 at every such point with
the allocated confidence. The first accepted true trigger probability lies
between 3/16 and 1-exp(-1). Indeed the initial a*t<=1; after a previous
failure to stop, the previous probability is <5/16, and doubling exposure
gives probability <1-(11/16)^2<1-exp(-1). The last possible duration is
O(1/a_min), and acceptance is guaranteed on the confidence event.

At this accepted t take fresh final trials to estimate the probability to
absolute error b=r/32. On [1/8,3/4], the inverse

    h(p)=log(-log(1-p))-log(t)

has derivative at most 9. Thus |h(p_hat)-log(a)|<r/2. Directed rounding and
known timer error can use the remaining r/2. O(r^-2 log(Q/delta)) final
trials suffice for Q adaptively selected rate assays. Conditional on each
chosen context, the new independent trials obey the same concentration
bound, so a union bound over a deterministic cap remains valid under
adaptive context selection. An anytime allocation is also possible.

For known Weibull shape k>=1 one may use t_j=2^(j/k)/a_max mathematically.
To use rational prescribed durations, the implemented protocol instead uses
t_j=s^j/a_max with rational s=1+1/(4 ceil(k)). The Weibull dose grows by
at most exp(1/4)<2, so the same probability bracket holds. The inverse is

    h(p)=log(-log(1-p))/k-log(t),

whose derivative is <=9/k. There are O(1+k log(a_max/a_min)) coarse stages.
The implemented b=min(1/64,k*r/32) is conservative and explicit.

With K=max k_v, total resets per assay are

    O(K log(a_max/a_min) log(Q K log(a_max/a_min)/delta)
      + r^-2 log(Q/delta)).                            (3)

The sum of the geometric coarse exposure durations is O(K/a) at the
particular queried rate a. Total exposure time per assay is therefore

    O(a^-1 [K log(Q K log(a_max/a_min)/delta)
                 + r^-2 log(Q/delta)]).               (4)

Multiply (3) by the actual fresh-preparation/clamp cost, and add (4).
There is no free physical parallelism in these totals. A tiny local rate
still costs inverse-local-rate exposure time. The result avoids waiting
for a conjunction/cascade whose rarity is compounded across many locally
resolvable mechanisms; it does not make an individually invisible rate
visible for free.

The shot constants in the prototype are intentionally simple worst-case
Hoeffding constants. Its aggregate binomial fixtures represent trillions
of resets. They are tests of the statistical algorithm and accounting, not
physical evidence or a practical acquisition claim. Sequential likelihood
intervals and precision adaptation are ordinary potential improvements,
not assumed improvements in the proved bill.

4. Acquire a useful local graph without exact sparsity or edge gaps
=================================================================

Let h_v=log(a_v). For a fixed proposed scope cap d, write d0=max(1,d) and
set r=rho/(64d0). Obtain every queried h_v value to error at most r by
Section 3. Correctness of a returned model needs only true monotonicity.
The following latent promise gives an acquisition-cost guarantee:

    There exists an UNKNOWN monotone d-junta h0_v with
    ||h_v-h0_v||_infinity <= b <= rho/(64d0).           (J)

The latent parent scopes and the h0_v are not received. Their graph can
have small width while the true dependency graph is complete. A dense
positive perturbation of total log-amplitude b is one such example.
There is no minimum size of a genuine influence to be recovered.

Maintain J consisting of witnessed latent parents, initially empty. For
each C subset J, query the two corner contexts C and C union U, where U
contains every still-unknown coordinate other than v. If all observed
log contrasts are <=rho/2, stop. True monotonicity then gives for EVERY x
with x_J=C,

    h_v(C) <= h_v(x) <= h_v(C union U)
                         <= h_v(C)+rho/2+2r.          (5)

Put l(C)=h_tilde_v(C)-r and compute the finite maximum closure

    h_minus(C)=max_{B subset C} l(B).

This is monotone and <=h_v(C)<=h_v(x). It is >=h_v(C)-2r, and hence

    0 <= h_v(x)-h_minus(C) <=rho/2+4r <rho.            (6)

Exponentiating proves (M). A finite rational retained rate may instead be
rounded downward from exp(h_minus) with an extra log error <=rho/4; the
slack in (6) keeps the final error below rho. The finite corner certificate covers all 2^n
original contexts; no uniform random-data claim is substituted for it.

If a corner contrast exceeds rho/2, find a new parent. Order the unknown
coordinates and consider the monotone chain formed by adding them one at
a time to C. Recursively bisect chain intervals. Prune an interval when
its OBSERVED endpoint contrast is <=q=rho/(8d0). A remaining singleton is
a witnessed parent. Search both children when needed; simply choosing the
larger child is not the proof and can lose a weak witness.

Why no spurious latent parent is added: an unpruned interval has true
contrast >q-2r=3rho/(32d0)>2b. Thus the h0 contrast on that interval is
positive, so its coordinates contain a genuine h0 parent. A singleton
therefore is a genuine latent parent. At every search depth, disjoint
visited intervals contain distinct latent jumps, so at most d survive.

Why the search finds a singleton: suppose all terminal intervals are
pruned. They partition the chain. At most d contain a positive h0 jump;
their total true contrast is <=d(q+2r). The remaining intervals group into
at most d+1 runs on which h0 is constant. On each run true h is monotone
and stays within b of that constant, so the total true contrast is <=2b.
Consequently the root contrast would be at most

    d(q+2r)+2b(d+1) <=7rho/32.

But its observed contrast exceeded rho/2, giving true contrast at least
rho/2-2r>=15rho/32. This contradiction proves discovery. At most d
enrichments occur. Once all latent parents have been found, every remaining
corner contrast is <=2b+2r<rho/2, so the procedure stops.

There are O(2^d+d^2 log n) distinct rate assays per component: sum the
corner scans across scope sizes and the O(d log n) split searches over
at most d enrichments. This remains true with the dense small perturbation
in (J). Learned scopes are subsets of latent scopes, so their moral factor
graph is a subgraph of the unknown latent moral graph. Exact dense physical
dependence has not forced an exact dense retained representation.

The cap d need not be supplied. Try d=0,1,..., with confidence allocation
delta_d=delta/[(d+1)(d+2)], charging every failed smaller attempt. An
attempt that exceeds its scope/assay cap returns incomplete, not a falsely
certified model. It stops by any d for which (J) holds. Summing trial caps
adds at most the explicitly charged factor d+1 (the powers-of-two part is
actually dominated by its final term). If no useful sparse approximation
exists, the procedure may eventually use all n-1 parents and exponential
work. There is no unconditional claim of cheap discovery.

Most importantly, even if (J) is false, a SUCCESSFUL completed corner
certificate is sound on the simultaneous confidence event. (J) supplies
completion and cost, not correctness by assumption. The essential global
assumption that remains is physical monotonicity of h itself.

5. Model acquisition preserves the rare conditional path law
==========================================================

Fix an ordered set of components that trigger before T, with ordered
times t_1<...<t_m. For each triggered v_j, its threshold is

    E_vj=H_vj(t_j)=sum_{ell<j} a_vj(S_ell)(t_(ell+1)-t_ell).

The map from ordered times to the triggered thresholds is triangular.
Its diagonal derivative for v_j is a_vj(S_(j-1)). Integrating the thresholds
of components that survive T gives the ordinary path-stratum density

    p(path) = product_triggered_v [a_v(S_before) f_v(H_v(t_v))]
              * product_surviving_v [1-F_v(H_v(T))].     (7)

This derivation works with cycles because each path has an actual chronological
order. It is not a false acyclic causal-graph assumption. It also applies
to the complete path through all n triggers when positive rates guarantee
completion, in which case there are no survivor factors.

For the SAME timed path under (M), every cumulative exposure satisfies
exp(-rho)H_v<=H_minus_v<=H_v. For a triggered v the Jacobian ratio is at
least exp(-rho), while (S) gives the density ratio at least
exp(-(kappa_v-1)rho). Every survivor factor increases. Therefore, as
measures over timed paths,

    P_minus >= exp(-D rho) P.                           (8)

Using common thresholds and attractiveness, a lower exposure model can
only delay triggers. Thus P_minus(A_T)<=P(A_T). Integrate (8) on A_T to
obtain exp(-D rho)p<=p_minus<=p. After conditioning,

    dP_minus(.|A_T)/dP(.|A_T)
       >=exp(-D rho) p/p_minus >=exp(-D rho).            (9)

Whenever Q>=cP for probability measures, Q=cP+(1-c)R for another probability
measure R, so TV(P,Q)<=1-c. This proves the acquisition part of (2).
This is why an arbitrary small absolute predictive error is not the
relevant certificate for rare conditioning.

For exponential clocks, an ordinary two-sided multiplicative model can
also be bounded with a slightly weaker constant using a path-likelihood
argument plus product-measure association (see the independent comparator
note). One-sided fitting is a clean sufficient construction, not a claim
that it is the only possible robust method. The generalized argument above
requires only (S), without stronger upper-tail regularity.

6. A time grid need not resolve the fastest clock
===============================================

Define the held process by evaluating each exposure rate on the failed
set at the beginning of a bin of length Delta. A trigger may occur inside
the bin, but its effect on others is held until the next bin. Couple this
and the unheld process using the SAME independent thresholds.

Let tau_v be a true trigger time, and tau_v^Delta its held trigger time.
Attractiveness gives tau_v<=tau_v^Delta. If v is the j-th true trigger,
inductively every earlier trigger u has propagated in the held system
by tau_u+rank(u)Delta rounded to the next bin. At held time t+jDelta,
the bin-start state contains every trigger present at true time t<tau_v.
Its exposure rate for v is therefore at least the true rate at t.
Integrating that shifted interval reaches the same threshold by
tau_v+jDelta. Hence

    tau_v <= tau_v^Delta <= tau_v+jDelta <=tau_v+nDelta. (10)

Equivalently one can delay every trigger itself to its bin endpoint and
prove the same endpoint bound inductively. The two constructions have the
same trigger-bin variables; deadline events at grid endpoints agree.
No rate upper bound appears in this comparison. The proof is distribution
free once the independent thresholds have been chosen.

All exposure rates depend on the discrete state, not explicit absolute
time. Therefore scaling all thresholds by c scales every trigger time by
c: tau(cE)=c tau(E). For any terminal event at a scaled deadline, a change
of variables and (S) give

    p(cT)=integral_(c A_T) product f_v(e_v) de
         >=c^D integral_(A_T) product f_v(e_v) de
         =c^D p(T),       0<c<=1.                       (11)

The generic shift-to-relative-accuracy idea has clear prior ancestry in
Ando's stochastic longest-path approximation. Here it applies to independent
threshold laws with (S) and nonlinear configuration-dependent exposure
rates, including feedback cycles and non-Markov clocks.

For an increasing terminal event, the shared-driver inclusions are

    A_(T-nDelta) subset B_T subset A_T,

where B_T is held success. With Delta=T/N, (11) proves

    (1-n/N)^D p(T)<=p_N(T)<=p(T).                        (12)

Also A_T subset B_(T+nDelta). Computing N+n bins at the SAME Delta gives
an actual computable upper enclosure p(T)<=p_held(T+nDelta). For uncertain
acquired rates, use lower a_minus for the lower calculation and upper
exp(rho)a_minus for the upper one. Both models are attractive. This can
tighten the worst-case bound without evaluating the true global process.

The n^2/N scale in the exponential case is not merely a proof artifact.
For a length-n activating chain with zero pre-parent rate and lambda*T
small, the ratio of held to true all-completion probabilities tends to

    (N)_n/N^n=product_(j=0)^(n-1)(1-j/N).

Its loss is asymptotic to n(n-1)/(2N). Positive baseline rates can approach
this limiting obstruction. The acquisition theorem does not pretend to
identify exact zero rates without a separate resolution/support promise.

7. The finite representation and the earned conditional sampler
==============================================================

Let K_v in {0,...,N-1,N} denote a held trigger bin, with N meaning survival
through T. Given a complete assignment K, a parent u contributes to the
rate in bin j exactly when K_u<j. Define

    H_v(k;K)=Delta sum_(j<k) a_minus_v({u in J_v:K_u<j}).

The independent-driver interval compatible with K_v=k<N is
[H_v(k;K),H_v(k+1;K)); for K_v=N it is [H_v(N;K),infinity).
The associated local factor is the interval mass under F_v.

These are genuine rectangular fibres, not a formal loopy factorization.
Induct on bins. If every threshold lies in its specified interval, the
previous-bin states agree with K; those assigned to the next bin cross
their thresholds there, and those assigned later do not. Conversely a
realized held path satisfies all the intervals. Thus these rectangles
partition threshold space up to null boundaries, even with directed cycles.
Their product masses normalize exactly, and their scopes are {v} union J_v.

For Exp clocks the factor for k<N is

    exp(-H_v(k)) [1-exp(-(H_v(k+1)-H_v(k)))],

and for survival it is exp(-H_v(N)). For Weibull shape k_v replace H by
H^k_v in this expression. It is the EXPOSURE that is held, not the
age-dependent Weibull failure hazard. Use expm1/log1p or directed interval
variants to avoid cancellation at small exposure increments.

Multiply the local factors and explicit event factors. Positive variable
elimination computes their sum, and retained elimination tables support
backward conditional draws of K. Factor entries can be evaluated from the
sorted at most d parent-change bins; no extra N loop is required per entry.
The code also implements a vectorized sum over the 2^d local contexts.

Conditional on K, sample the original E_v independently from their interval-
truncated threshold laws. For Exp, sample a shifted truncated exponential.
For Weibull, Z_v=E_v^k_v is exponential: transform the interval endpoints,
sample the truncated exponential, then take the k_v-th root. Run the unheld
acquired cascade on these SAME thresholds. This outputs exactly the acquired
path law conditioned on the driver event B_T of held success.

Since B_T subset A_T, the TV distance between driver laws conditioned on
B_T and A_T is exactly 1-P(B_T)/P(A_T). Pushing forward cannot increase TV.
Equation (12) therefore gives the grid part of (2). The probability bounds
multiply across model acquisition and the grid. For conditional-path TV we
use their SUM by the triangle inequality; no unproved product improvement
is asserted. Outputting K alone would not prove a continuous path-TV result.

8. Acquire the graph decomposition as well
=========================================

The acquired scopes explicitly construct the primal graph: make {v} union
J_v a clique for each local factor and add event-factor scopes. A min-fill
heuristic provides an actual order and its actual width, but has no optimal
width guarantee. The supplied complete search removes the decomposition
oracle from the theoretical contract as follows.

For a proposed width k, memoize states (S,C), where C is a connected component
whose entire outside neighborhood is S. A rooted partial decomposition has
boundary bag S. Select nonempty X subset C with |S union X|<=k+1. For each
connected component C_j of C\X, its boundary S_j is its actual neighborhood
in S union X. Recurse on (S_j,C_j), and attach the resulting decompositions
to bag S union X. Process components independently at the empty root.

Completeness: in any width-k decomposition of G[C union S] with S made a
clique, choose a bag containing S and root there. Since C is connected, its
vertices occupy one component below a root bag containing no C vertex.
Every member of S has a neighbor in C, so it persists along the route to
the first bag containing a C vertex. That bag contains S and a nonempty X.
Its remaining components yield exactly the smaller feasible subproblems.
Conversely gluing those subproblems at the selected bag is a valid tree
decomposition. This proves the recurrence by induction on |C|.

There are n^O(k) boundary/component states and n^O(k) candidate bags per
state, giving n^O(k) work. Postorder elimination of child variables followed
by X produces and verifies an order of width at most k. Try k=0,1,... until
success, charging failed widths. Faster established fixed-parameter treewidth
algorithms may replace this elementary routine; no improvement in their
complexity is claimed. In the code, exhaustive small-graph comparisons check
the recurrence against brute-force optimal elimination orders.

Under (J), the acquired graph is a subgraph of the unknown latent graph.
Thus fixed latent width w0 yields bounded acquired width, despite arbitrary
dense monotone perturbations within the stated uniform log-error budget.
This is an approximation of operational dependence, not recovery of the
true exact graph.

9. Arithmetic, random bits, and the actual output contract
=========================================================

The mathematical continuous-law theorem uses real threshold variables.
A program printing finitely many floating timestamps is atomic and can
have TV distance ONE from an absolutely continuous target. The numerical
programs are therefore functional checks, not certified implementations
of ideal continuous-law TV.

A finite output is available without assuming separated event times. Fix
a log-time resolution h>0. Map z=log(tau_v) to the adjacent integers
floor(z/h), ceil(z/h), using probabilities that linearly interpolate z/h.
This is randomized rounding, defined as part of the requested output law.
For two log-times z,z', its categorical output laws have TV at most
min(1,|z-z'|/h). Apply this independently by component. Initial triggers
at time zero are deterministic special labels; the positive-rate core
can output all completion times, avoiding an unadvertised censor boundary.

If thresholds are enclosed multiplicatively within exp(+-u), attractiveness
and tau(cE)=c tau(E) enclose EVERY output time within exp(+-u), even when
trigger orders change. Exact rational replay on rational threshold
approximations requires at most n updates and polynomial-bit rational
arithmetic. It follows that finite threshold/rounding evaluation contributes
at most O(nu/h) TV to this explicitly rounded output. This does not require
a minimum gap between trigger times or an anti-concentration promise.

For Exp/Weibull clocks, conditional interval inversion is explicit. A lower
bound for any nonempty increasing terminal event is obtained by requiring
all n components to trigger under their positive minimum exposures by T:

    p >= product_v F_v(a_min,v T).

The same bound applies to the held model at a grid deadline. For any future
known positive exposure multipliers, use the resulting minimum exposures.
Alternatively pay a discovered activating order and allocate T/n to its
steps. The log of this lower bound, not its reciprocal, controls the number
of precision bits needed in the following conservative implementation.

Threshold tails and values too near zero can be discarded with at most nu
probability by choosing their unconditional probabilities <=u*p_lower.
For Weibull this uses powers and logs of p_lower. On the retained compact
intervals, inverse CDF evaluation and relative threshold approximation take
polynomially many bits in the binary input sizes, log(1/p_lower), log(1/u),
the shape description and the requested output resolution. Random uniforms
can be generated as successively refined binary intervals. Bounds on the
explicit Weibull density over the retained interval give a fixed polynomial
precision sufficient except on the charged discarded mass. Approximate
rounding probabilities add their ordinary categorical variation budget.

Positive factor arithmetic is also stable without 1/p. Approximate every
nonzero factor multiplicatively to exp(+-eta/n), preserving exact zeros.
Every assignment weight is then within exp(+-eta) of the exact weight,
so the partition function has the same enclosure, uniformly over nonnegative
event factors. Normalized distributions differ by at most tanh(eta/2)
under this symmetric log envelope. Allocate additional directed rounding to
the finite positive sum/product circuit and the backward categorical draws.
If W arithmetic operations are performed, O(log(W/eta)) mantissa guard bits
above the needed input precision suffice for the usual positive-circuit
relative-error accumulation. Exp(-x) may require a large binary exponent;
charge its exponent bit length and range reduction, not a unit-cost exact
transcendental. Small 1-exp(-x) uses an expm1-style enclosure.

This specifies a finite-bit, rounded-output route for the explicit clock
families; it is not a completed interval-software verification. The supplied
code uses NumPy/SciPy floats. For arbitrary density functions, polynomial
special-function evaluation/inversion is an additional obligation and is
not asserted. A general 'known density' oracle has not been smuggled into
the algorithmic theorem.

10. Total costs, reuse, and what has actually been gained
========================================================

For fixed attempted d, let

    Q=O(n(2^d+d^2 log n)),  r=rho/(64 max(1,d)).

Equations (3)--(4), multiplied by Q, charge the experiments. Unknown-degree
discovery sums these bills over failed smaller caps. The finite table storage
is O(n2^d), apart from the recorded experiment transcript. Table monotonicity
closure costs O(nd2^d). Scope discovery and a complete width search are
explicit. Subsequent factor preprocessing costs O(n(N+1)^(w+1)) positive
arithmetic operations and storage of the actual retained tables; dense
tables can be impractical even at moderate w. Shape/call/event-factor costs
and all output writes are additional explicit costs.

For D=O(n), bounded known Weibull shapes, fixed d,w and rho=Theta(epsilon/D), N=Theta(Dn/epsilon), the
operation is polynomial in n and 1/epsilon, with the physical assay time
depending on inverse LOCAL minimum exposure. Neither acquisition nor query
execution contains an inverse GLOBAL event-probability factor. The grid does
not contain a fastest-rate factor. Bit costs still depend on the encoded
rate/duration ranges and the logarithm of a probability lower bound.

The one simultaneous statistical event proving (M) supports every later
adaptively chosen admitted deadline and event. This means no new confidence
union bound is needed for merely reusing the tables. It does NOT mean an
infinite sequence of approximate samples has fixed total transcript TV:
per-query TV errors can add under adaptive composition. Nor does fixed-rho
acquisition support arbitrarily tighter later accuracy without paying for
refinement.

The acquired object is useful because it supplies BOTH a robust conditional
law comparison and a finite computational representation. A compact predictor
alone, or a graph alone, supplies neither implication in general.

11. Strongest existing routes and remaining distinction
=======================================================

Continuous-time Bayesian networks already factor local intensities on directed
graphs, including cycles. One-shot event-time encodings and ordinary junction
trees already exploit structural width. Importance sampling, splitting and
Doob changes of measure already generate rare conditioned behavior when a
good proposal or progress interface is available. This work does not claim
those representations or methods as inventions.

Ando, 'The Distribution Function of the Longest Path Length in Constant
Treewidth DAGs with Random Edge Length', arXiv:1910.09791v4, proves a
deterministic relative FPTAS for independent uniform activity durations and
fixed treewidth, and an exact algorithm for standard exponential activity
durations in an exp-arithmetic model. Its staircase deadline shifts followed
by geometric dilation are a particularly close proof ancestor. The current
claim must be assessed beyond that established special case: configuration-
dependent cyclic exposure feedback, explicit non-Markov thresholds, acquisition
without a graph, and a full conditioned-path error bound under noisy learning.

For AND-precedence activity DAGs with logconcave duration densities, ordinary
convex/logconcave integration and sampling can be stronger than a width-
parameterized method. They are the appropriate baseline on that subcase.
The full present class has nonconvex success regions: let two root rates be
1 and a target's exposure be tiny before either root triggers and large
after the first. Threshold vectors with one early and one late root succeed;
their midpoint can fail. Disjoint OR gadgets give exponentially many
pairwise midpoint-incompatible successful vectors, obstructing a polynomial
EXACT convex-piece cover. This is not a lower bound against all nonconvex
integration algorithms. Stronger nonconvex samplers require their own warm
start, conductance/Poincare, kernel-volume or volume-growth conditions; none
follows from a mere monotone downset. Width supplies a different, acquired
and checked computational condition here.

The independent comparator note gives primary-source details. Its search did
not locate the complete theorem claimed here; a literature search is not a
proof of historical novelty. The useful scientific object is the ordinary
proof and executable construction, with the strongest known parts adopted.

12. Informative failures and boundaries
======================================

* Pure observation cannot bootstrap arbitrary rare-state access. A hidden
  length-L combination lock can have O(L) hidden states but require Omega(2^L)
  complete attempts if every earlier observation is identical. Our chosen
  local clamp interface is stronger and its costs are explicit.

* Tiny local rates still cost physical time. Distinguishing a from
  (1+c epsilon)a with constant confidence needs order epsilon^-2 informative
  events, hence order 1/(a epsilon^2) total at-risk exposure. The theorem
  concerns compounded rare events with locally resolvable mechanisms.

* Inhibition destroys the held-success subset. Two nodes initially have
  rate M; once one triggers, the other's rate falls to delta. For M*Delta
  large, a held bin can trigger both with probability near one; the true
  probability by T can be only order delta*T.

* Arbitrary conditioning destroys the rate-error guarantee. With independent
  rates (1,1) versus (0.99,1), condition at T=1000 on EXACTLY ONE trigger.
  The triggering identity changes from (1/2,1/2) to approximately
  (0.0000454,0.9999546). TV exceeds 0.4999 despite a 1% local change.

* Clock regularity is substantive. A threshold 1+Exp(1) can have positive
  success just above time 1 and zero success after an arbitrarily small
  downward exposure change. Lognormal lower tails generally have no finite
  global exponent in (S). No uniform all-deadline theorem is claimed there.

* Top-context edge tests miss OR interactions. Each of two parents may have
  zero marginal at the all-triggered context while both are genuinely
  relevant. The implemented corner/witness procedure repairs this failure.

* Exact support recovery is unnecessary and can be counterproductive. The
  first acquisition fixture retained a small but detectable extra edge.
  The strengthened theorem and dense-perturbation fixture instead learn
  a useful minorant without recovering the exact complete physical graph.

* Small physical width is not enough for arbitrary event formulas: their
  own coupling graph must enter the width. Likewise repeated repair events,
  unbounded event counts, unknown exposure-dependent memory, or correlated
  thresholds need new proofs, not relabelling of the current result.

* Finite timestamp laws are not continuous laws. The continuous theorem,
  randomized finite-output contract, and floating prototype have distinct
  scopes. No formal numerical certification was performed.

* The rare short-deadline demonstration is not a practical speed victory.
  A same-model ordinary event-time importance sampler is already faster
  on that fixture and provides an efficient rejection sampler. Its measured
  result is preserved beside the compiler. The proposed theoretical advance
  is the class-wide acquired guarantee, not rarity by itself.
