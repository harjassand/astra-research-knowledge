CONDITIONAL PATH LIFTING FOR ATTRACTIVE IRREVERSIBLE EXPOSURE CASCADES
Independent derivation and assumption attacks, 1 November 2026

This proof reviews the root-owned event-time construction. It does not claim
acquisition of the physical exposure tables or of a low-width decomposition
without the separate procedures and costs specified by the owner. It supplies
the exact threshold pullback, the probability/conditional-law argument, and
the one-sided model-error argument, including non-Markov threshold laws.

1. Model and threshold regularity
--------------------------------

There are n initially untriggered nodes. A node can trigger once and remains
triggered. Node v has a time-homogeneous exposure rate a_v(S)>=0 when the current
triggered set is S and v is not in S. For S subset S', a_v(S)<=a_v(S'):
interactions are attractive. All rates are finite. Each node has an independent
nonnegative threshold E_v drawn once at time zero. It triggers when its
integrated exposure first reaches that threshold. Previously triggered seeds
can be removed from the count n and incorporated into all rate tables.

For the general result, thresholds have known absolutely continuous laws with
densities f_v and survival functions barF_v. Assume numbers kappa_v>=1 such that

    f_v(y) >= (y/x)^(kappa_v-1) f_v(x)    for 0<y<=x,             (1.1)

and put D=sum_v kappa_v. The inequality form handles zeros better than writing
a ratio. In the positive Gamma and Weibull examples all ratios are well defined.
The algorithm must actually compute the required interval probabilities and
truncated draws at charged precision. A merely 'known density' is insufficient
as a computational interface.

Exponential thresholds have kappa=1. Gamma(shape alpha>=1, scale theta) satisfy
(1.1) with kappa=alpha because

    f(y)/f(x)=(y/x)^(alpha-1) exp((x-y)/theta).

Weibull(shape k>=1, scale sigma) satisfy it with kappa=k because

    f(y)/f(x)=(y/x)^(k-1) exp((x/sigma)^k-(y/sigma)^k).

Gamma or Weibull shapes below one also obey the weaker admitted bound kappa=1
when their density is decreasing; this proof does not use kappa<1. Allowing
kappa<1 in the owner's density comparison would require modifying the later
one-sided exposure argument, since the exponent then changes monotonicity.

Let A_T be an increasing predicate of the set triggered by deadline T. Its
computable representation is a separate premise: an arbitrary predicate oracle
does not give an inference algorithm. Assume P(A_T)>0 whenever a conditional
law is requested.

2. Exact local factorization of the held-input process
-----------------------------------------------------

Set Delta=T/N. In bin j=1,...,N each still-untriggered node uses the exposure
rate determined by nodes whose trigger bins are strictly earlier than j. Nodes
crossing their thresholds during bin j become effective inputs only at its end.
Let K_v in {1,...,N,infinity} be its trigger bin. Define, for a full bin assignment K,

    S_j(K)={u:K_u<j},
    H_v(k;K)=Delta sum_(j=1)^k a_v(S_j(K)).

For K_v=k finite, the allowed threshold interval is

    I_v(K)=[H_v(k-1;K), H_v(k;K));

for K_v=infinity it is [H_v(N;K),infinity). Endpoint conventions differ only
on probability-zero sets under the continuous threshold law.

Claim. The inverse image of K under the held-input simulator is precisely

    product_v I_v(K).                                          (2.1)

Proof. If K is produced, the cumulative exposure inequalities defining the
first crossing give every stated interval. Conversely take thresholds inside
those intervals. Induct on bins. Before the first bin the triggered set is
fixed. If the simulator agrees with K in all earlier bins, then its rates in
the present bin equal those used in H_v. A node with K_v equal to the present
bin crosses, a node with larger K_v does not, and a node with smaller K_v has
already triggered. Thus it agrees in every bin. The strict-earlier convention
is essential to this induction; cycles in the influence graph are harmless.
QED.

Independence of the thresholds now gives the exact probability

    P(K)=product_v w_v(K_v,K_parents(v)),                        (2.2)

where w_v is the mass of I_v(K). Consequently the factors are normalized
jointly even when the parent graph has cycles. Conditional on a complete K,
the threshold variables remain independent, with their original laws truncated
to the displayed intervals. This is an exact acquisition of the conditional
driver law, not an approximation by binned output times.

For exponential thresholds, a finite interval [L,U) has mass
exp(-L)(1-exp(-(U-L))) and admits the draw

    E=L-log(1-U0*(1-exp(-(U-L)))),  U0 uniform on (0,1).

For [L,infinity), draw E=L+Exp(1).

For Weibull(k,sigma), transform Z=(E/sigma)^k, which is Exp(1). Transform the
interval endpoints, apply the preceding truncated exponential formula, and set
E=sigma Z^(1/k). This explicitly earns both interval mass and truncated sampling
for a genuinely non-Markov family. Numerical cancellation in small interval
masses must use stable expm1/log1p or certified equivalent arithmetic.

3. Common-threshold path coupling and additive delay
---------------------------------------------------

Run the undelayed and held-input processes from the same thresholds. Write tau_v
for the undelayed trigger time and bar_tau_v for the held process's effective
grid-end trigger time. Attractiveness implies tau_v<=bar_tau_v whenever the
latter is finite. A first hypothetical reversal would encounter an undelayed
triggered set containing the delayed one, hence at least as much accumulated
exposure for the allegedly earlier delayed node, a contradiction.

Order the finite undelayed trigger times tau_(1)<=...<=tau_(m), m<=n. Except
on null boundary cases, ties may be ordered arbitrarily. Then

    bar_tau_(k) <= tau_(k)+k Delta.                             (3.1)

Proof by induction. For all s<tau_(k), each node triggered in the true process
by s has index j<k. By induction it has become effective in the held process by
tau_(j)+j Delta <= s+(k-1)Delta. Thus the held state at s+(k-1)Delta contains
the undelayed state at s. If node k has not already triggered in the held
process, integrating its exposure over this shifted time interval gives at
least its original threshold by tau_(k)+(k-1)Delta. Its next grid-end update
occurs within another Delta, proving (3.1). QED.

Therefore, for N>=n and c=1-n/N,

    A_(cT) subset B_T subset A_T,                              (3.2)

as subsets of the same threshold space, where B_T is the increasing event in
the held process. The left inclusion uses (3.1) for every node needed by the
predicate; the right uses attractiveness. With N<n only the trivial TV<=1
bound is asserted. At c=0 a positive approximation guarantee requires care;
normally N>n is chosen.

The code may report inside-bin crossing times rather than effective grid-end
times. Those crossing times are earlier than the grid endpoint, so (3.1) still
gives a valid (possibly loose) upper bound. They must not be confused with
actual undelayed CTMC/exposure-cascade times.

4. Relative probability and conditional path law without inverse rarity
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Time homogeneity gives an exact deterministic scaling identity:

    tau_v(cE)=c tau_v(E),                                      (4.1)

including infinite times. Thus A_(cT)=c A_T in threshold space. By change of
variables and (1.1), for c in (0,1],

    P(A_(cT))
      =c^n integral_(A_T) product_v f_v(cE_v) dE
      >=c^D P(A_T).                                           (4.2)

This calculation does not assume that a rare event is likely to be encountered
by simulation. Combining (3.2) and (4.2) gives

    c^D P(A_T) <= P(B_T) <= P(A_T).                            (4.3)

Now draw K exactly from the factor model (2.2) conditioned on the requested
held-process event. Draw independent thresholds from the intervals (2.1),
then rerun the undelayed process from those thresholds. This samples the
undelayed path map applied to the original threshold measure conditioned on B_T.

For nested events B subset A, their conditional input laws have exact TV

    TV(mu(.|B),mu(.|A))=1-mu(B)/mu(A).                         (4.4)

A deterministic output map cannot increase TV. Hence the lifted continuous
paths differ from the true conditional path law given A_T by at most

    1-c^D <= Dn/N.                                            (4.5)

The last inequality is Bernoulli's inequality, valid for D>=1 and c=1-n/N.
Every lifted path satisfies the event because B_T subset A_T. The result is
about the full continuous path law; outputting grid times themselves would be
singular with respect to a continuous-time law and could have TV=1.

For exponential thresholds D=n and the bound is <=n^2/N. There is no inverse
P(A_T) factor. Rarity disappears through a proved relative set-retention bound,
not through an assumed committor, posterior sampler, or final-state planner.

5. One-sided exposure-model error for full conditional paths
-----------------------------------------------------------

Let a be the true exposure rates and ahat the acquired model. Assume both are
attractive and, for every node and every admissible state,

    exp(-rho) a_v(S) <= ahat_v(S) <= a_v(S).                   (5.1)

Zero rates must be preserved. A finite noisy assay cannot generally certify
that an arbitrary rate is exactly zero without an additional support or gap
promise. The theorem does not supply that promise.

Let P and Q denote full path laws through T under a and ahat, respectively,
with the same independent threshold distributions. Fix a path stratum with
ordered triggered nodes v_1,...,v_k at times t_1<...<t_k<=T. Let S_j be the
first j triggered nodes and t_0=0. On this stratum,

    E_(v_j) = sum_(l=0)^(j-1) a_(v_j)(S_l)(t_(l+1)-t_l).

The map from (t_1,...,t_k) to the triggered thresholds is triangular: its j-th
coordinate depends only on t_1,...,t_j. Its diagonal derivative is
a_(v_j)(S_(j-1)). Integrating each untriggered threshold above its total exposure
H_v(T) gives the density

    p(path) = product_(j=1)^k [a_(v_j)(S_(j-1)) f_(v_j)(H_(v_j)(t_j))]
              * product_(v not triggered) barF_v(H_v(T)).       (5.2)

This formula includes the survival/censoring factors. It remains valid for
non-Markov threshold laws; exponential memorylessness is not used.

Evaluate q and p on the same ordered timed path. The cumulative exposures obey

    exp(-rho)H_v(t) <= Hhat_v(t) <= H_v(t).

For each triggered node, the derivative ratio contributes at least exp(-rho).
Since kappa_v>=1, the density ratio from (1.1) contributes at least
exp(-(kappa_v-1)rho). Each surviving node contributes a survival ratio at least
one. Therefore, on every positive-p path stratum,

    q(path) >= exp(-D rho) p(path).                            (5.3)

The inequality also covers strata with fewer than n triggers because the sum
of their kappa_v is at most D. Extra Q mass on strata of zero P mass is harmless.

Attractiveness and pointwise lower acquired rates give Q(A_T)<=P(A_T) for an
increasing terminal event. Conditioning (5.3) therefore yields

    dQ(.|A_T)/dP(.|A_T)
       >= exp(-D rho) P(A_T)/Q(A_T) >= exp(-D rho),

and consequently

    TV(Q(.|A_T),P(.|A_T)) <= 1-exp(-D rho) <= D rho.             (5.4)

Also exp(-D rho)P(A_T)<=Q(A_T)<=P(A_T). The lower probability bound follows
by integrating (5.3); it does not need an additional time-change comparison.

For the exponential convention (1-zeta)a<=ahat<=a, take rho=-log(1-zeta):

    TV <=1-(1-zeta)^n<=n zeta.

For ahat<=a<=(1+zeta)ahat, take rho=log(1+zeta):

    TV <=1-(1+zeta)^(-n)<=n zeta.

Combining this error with (4.5) by the triangle inequality gives a conditional
path error at most [1-exp(-D rho)]+[1-(1-n/N)^D]. The probability lower bounds
multiply; a product conditional-TV improvement is not asserted without proof.

6. Finite precision, graph scope, and earned numerical primitives
----------------------------------------------------------------

The exact bin-factor construction has one factor involving a node and its
physical parents. Its primal graph is the moral graph of that family. Factors
or auxiliary variables encoding the event add edges, domains, and fill. The
width governing variable elimination must be that of the combined actual
factor representation, not the physical graph alone. Cycles in the physical
graph do not invalidate factorization. A bad elimination order can still be
expensive even when a better order exists; computing the order is charged.

All-target-failed deadline events are unary masks on K_v. Cardinality predicates
can be handled with count variables or count-valued messages, whose polynomial
domain cost must be included. Arbitrary monotone Boolean constraints can encode
hard inference even with independent physical nodes, so no unrestricted event
query is implied by small physical width.

If each of the n nonzero local log factors is evaluated within tau/n, the full
bin weight is within exp(+-tau). This persists under every nonnegative event
weight. The partition error is exp(+-tau), and the normalized bin-law TV is at
most tanh(tau/2). This sharp bound is proved in PROOFS_AND_CONSTRUCTIONS.txt,
Section 4. It contains no inverse event probability. Arithmetic in elimination
and random sampling still needs a separate charged error allocation.

Weibull interval mass and truncated sampling are explicit above. General known
densities need a stated integration and inversion implementation with a cost
bound. Integer-shape Gamma laws also admit known finite-sum survival formulas;
large shape parameters must be included in the input and D costs. No generic
CDF/integration oracle follows merely from the density inequality.

7. Assumptions cannot be dropped
--------------------------------

Inhibition counterexample. Two nodes start with exposure rates one. The first
node switches the second's rate to zero. Thresholds are E_1=0.1,E_2=0.5 and
Delta=1. The undelayed first node triggers at 0.1 and the second never triggers.
The held process lets both cross in its first bin. Thus B_T subset A_T fails.

Non-upward event counterexample. Two independent exponential nodes have true
rates (1,1) and lower rates (0.99,1). At T=1000 condition on exactly one node
triggering. Under the true law the two possible triggered identities are equally
likely. Under the lower-rate law, node 2 is the one triggered with probability
1/(1+exp(-10)), approximately 0.9999546. Their identity marginal already has
TV approximately 0.4999546, far exceeding n zeta=0.02. The increasing-event
assumption is essential to the normalization step in (5.4).

Threshold-law counterexamples. A fixed positive incubation threshold permits
P(A_T)>0 but P(A_(cT))=0 near its minimal completion time. Lognormal lower tails
also have no finite global bound of the form (1.1). A finite D is a substantive
small-exposure regularity property, not an automatic property of lifetime laws.
Threshold atoms require a separate treatment and are not covered by (5.2).

Acquisition warning. Detecting parents by only their marginal effect at the
all-triggered context fails for OR-type interactions: each genuine parent's
top-context marginal can be zero while other genuine parents remain triggered.
The owner must implement witness-context/junta acquisition with a resolution
promise or account for a more expensive exhaustive search. The review theorem
does not manufacture that interface.

8. Targeted executable evidence
--------------------------------

check_cascade_lifting.py imports (and does not modify) the root-owned
new_rare_event/event_time_compiler.py. Its successful saved run checks:

  * All 28 positive bin assignments in a three-node cyclic example, including
    a zero baseline rate. Across 560 conditional threshold draws, an independent
    held-input simulator reconstructs exactly the original K every time.
  * Factor normalization is exactly one to the displayed floating precision.
  * Positive 96-point quadrature over all two-node path strata checks full timed
    path conditional TV for increasing events at deadlines 1e-5,0.1,2,10.
    This includes an event of probability about 1.15e-9. The proved bound is
    0.0396 for a two-percent one-sided error; observed TV is below 0.0077.
  * The inhibition and non-upward counterexamples above are retained.

check_weibull_fibres.py additionally checks all 28 positive bin assignments for
the same cyclic system with independent Weibull threshold shapes 1.4,2,3
(D=6.4). Its 560 conditional-threshold draws all return the original bin
assignment, and the total factor mass is one to displayed precision. The code
uses the explicit transformed-exponential interval primitive and saves
weibull_fibre_results.json.

The finite checks support the implementation and the identified assumptions.
They are not empirical certificates of total variation or substitutes for the
proofs. The generalized Weibull theorem above is proved analytically.

Disposition: no mathematical gap found in the root's conditional lifting or
one-sided exposure comparison at these stated assumptions. The acquisition,
efficient threshold arithmetic, combined graph decomposition, and total finite-
bit cost remain separate parts that the complete construction must earn.
