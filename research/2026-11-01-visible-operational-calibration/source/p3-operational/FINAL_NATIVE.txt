## 1. A receiver can tolerate what calibration cannot resolve

The target remains an unknown quantum register, possibly entangled with a reference, passed through a specified encoder, storage interval and quantum receiver.

On \(m\) physical or acquired coordinates, consider the channel

\[
\mathcal N_T(\rho)
=\mathbb E_\theta\,U_\theta\rho U_\theta^\dagger,
\qquad
U_\theta=\prod_{j=1}^{m}e^{-i\theta_j Z_j},
\qquad
\theta\sim N(0,TQ),
\]

where \(Q\) is any positive-semidefinite covariance matrix. Correlations can be dense and of arbitrary rank. The specified code corrects every phase-error pattern of weight at most \(t\); write \(h=t+1\).

An actual, qualified random \(X\) conjugation before and after storage makes the channel a phase-Pauli channel once the random key is retired. Conditional on \(\theta\), its error bits are independent with probabilities \(\sin^2\theta_j\). The physical randomization, key handling and receiver are part of the procedure.

For this channel, ordinary coding gives the useful baseline

\[
\delta_{\rm logical}
\le \frac{2m\bar p}{h},
\]

where \(\bar p\) is the average phase-error probability at the intended storage time. Here and below, \(\delta\) is the **full diamond norm**, including arbitrary reference entanglement.

That baseline is sharp for unrestricted correlated phase-Pauli noise. Improving it requires additional structure.

### The Gaussian construction avoids saturated-rate inversion

For centered Gaussian phases,

\[
p_j=\frac{1-e^{-2TQ_{jj}}}{2}.
\]

A small average \(\bar p\) does **not** bound the average variance: one coordinate can have an enormous variance while its observed error probability merely saturates at \(1/2\). Attempting to recover every rate from this average fails.

Instead, suppose the experiment acquires \(b\ge\bar p\). For any chosen \(u>0\), define

\[
p(u)=\frac{1-e^{-2u}}2,\qquad
d_{\rm hot}=\left\lfloor\frac{mb}{p(u)}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
h_{\rm rem}=h-d_{\rm hot},
\qquad
c(u)=\frac{u}{p(u)}.
\]

When \(h_{\rm rem}>0\), the complete ideal twirled receiver satisfies, for \(0<\eta<1\),

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm logical}
\le
2e^{-h_{\rm rem}(\log(1/\eta)-1+\eta)}
+
2\sqrt2\,
e^{-\eta h_{\rm rem}/(4c(u)mb)} .
}
\]

The mechanism is constructive:

1. There can be at most \(d_{\rm hot}\) coordinates with variance exceeding \(u\).
2. Charge every one of those coordinates as an error.
3. The remaining coordinates have bounded total Gaussian phase energy.
4. Apply a conditional error-count bound to the remaining correction capacity.

**The noisy set exists only inside the proof.** Neither its locations nor its individual rates need to be acquired. Correlations between that set and the remaining coordinates do not invalidate the argument.

For a fixed corrected fraction \(\alpha=h/m\), the resulting sufficient mean-error scale is

\[
b=O\!\left(\frac{\alpha}{\log(1/\delta)}\right),
\]

with a separately controlled finite-code term. This is a finite-horizon, accuracy-dependent guarantee; it is not a fixed-noise threshold whose error necessarily vanishes as the code grows.

The proof, finite confidence calculation and counterexamples are in :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}[MARGINAL_RECEIVER_COMPARATOR.md](producer-workspace/MARGINAL_RECEIVER_COMPARATOR.md).

## 2. A second theorem removes the register-size penalty without randomization

The same development produced a stronger result for the original coherent channel, without inserting a twirl.

Let

\[
\bar q=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}Q}{m},
\qquad
a_0=\frac{t+1}{4em},
\qquad
b_t=\frac43\,4^{-(t+1)}.
\]

Then the complete ideal encoder–storage–receiver channel obeys

\[
\boxed{
\delta_{\rm storage}
\le
4b_t+
2\sqrt2\exp\!\left[-\frac{a_0^2}{4T\bar q}\right].
}
\]

The proof replaces the previous requirement that **every** phase be small with a sufficient condition on **total phase energy**. The high-weight part of the product rotation is bounded by

\[
\sum_{\ell>t}\frac{\bigl(\sum_j|\sin\theta_j|\bigr)^\ell}{\ell!},
\]

while the Gaussian exponential moment satisfies

\[
\mathbb E\exp\!\left[
\frac{\|\theta\|^2}{4T\operatorname{Tr}Q}
\right]\le\sqrt2
\]

for every covariance spectrum. Exact correction of the low-weight error span supplies the full-channel conclusion.

At fixed corrected fraction and target error, this removes the earlier extra \(\log m\) penalty. **Noise rank is unnecessary for this receiver theorem.** Rank remains useful for proving that the acquisition procedure finds many protected coordinates.

## 3. Protection and coherent gates require different information

The result does not turn an average storage statistic into a universal control certificate.

For coherent gates on complementary encoded coordinates, the relevant noise envelope is

\[
R^2=\max_{z\in\{-1,+1\}^m}z^\mathsf TQz,
\qquad
\|\mathcal L\|_\diamond=2R^2.
\]

Two covariance matrices with the same average diagonal can give \(R^2=mq\) or \(R^2=m^2q\). Consequently, the receiver’s mean-error certificate cannot replace the active-gate envelope.

This gives a concrete division of information requirements:

| Intended operation | Information that suffices |
|---|---|
| Discover useful encoded coordinates | Certified pair-cancellation contrasts and the resulting sign trees |
| Preserve a register through a declared receiver | Mean phase energy, or the fixed-horizon mean-error certificate above |
| Execute finite coherent gates | Bounds on the complete active noise envelope |

## 4. The protected representation is acquired from measurement records

The cancellation hierarchy now has an operational acquisition procedure.

For current encoded vectors \(v_i,v_j\), simultaneous product Ramsey records supply

\[
\langle X_iX_j\rangle+\langle Y_iY_j\rangle
=e^{-2\tau\|v_i-v_j\|^2},
\]

and

\[
\langle X_iX_j\rangle-\langle Y_iY_j\rangle
=e^{-2\tau\|v_i+v_j\|^2}.
\]

The learner sees records, constructs a simultaneously certified graph of acceptable signed pairs, takes a **greedy maximal matching**, encodes those pairs and repeats. It receives no microscopic coupling list or latent orientation.

At fixed rank \(r\) and depth \(k\), the common-noise construction retains \(\Theta(n)\) coordinates in blocks of size \(2^k\), with rate scale \(O(n^{-2k/r})\). Independent residual noise contributes its actual uncancelled floor.

A useful physical closure theorem makes the probes less demanding: under additive Hermitian dephasing and disjoint block-local controls, spectators do not alter the tested local reduced dynamics. Constant-quality two-block calibration marginals suffice even when the whole probe register has poor fidelity. The working quantum data source retains its separate requirement.

Correlation spectroscopy and direct discovery of decoherence-free subspaces are established. The contribution here is the finite acquisition-to-hierarchy construction and its downstream receiver/control contracts. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

The cost remains substantial. At fixed tolerances and depth:

- Whole-register shots scale as \(O(\log(n/\beta))\) per level.
- Physical preparations and local readouts scale as \(O(n\log(n/\beta))\).
- Pair processing costs \(O(n^2\log(n/\beta))\).
- Sequential interrogation can cost \(O(n^{2k/r}\log(n/\beta))\).

Inverse-rate spectroscopy is established machinery, not a cost removed by this construction. Stationarity must survive acquisition and subsequent use. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}
