## Result and reading order

This checkpoint constructs a quantum simulation procedure for finitely many retained damped bosonic modes coupled through bounded, possibly noncommuting system operators. It preserves the accessible system process through instruments at prescribed times, retaining private reservoir memory. It receives thermal modes through an explicitly priced vacuum doubling.

With G = sum_i Gamma_i T and h = alpha_H0 T, overhead is polynomial in mode count, 1+G+h+number of prescribed interventions, supplied input/encoding/register lengths, logarithms of known damping/frequency magnitudes, and log(1/error). Small positive parameter scales, thermal occupation factors, supplied controlled operator access and ordinary system Hamiltonian simulation are paid. Bath acquisition and continuum fitting are not supplied.

## Evidence and limitations
The Python programs and JSON outputs are small numerical diagnostics of thermal doubling and bridge word averaging. They are not an implementation or benchmark of the complete finite-bit quantum compiler. The full claim is mathematical existence. No practical speedup, optimized gate count, literature priority, arbitrary adaptive intervention times, public bath trajectories, physical thermal heat statistics or free source preparation is established. Gaussian equivalence, thermofield doubling and several algorithmic ingredients have strong prior art, discussed and linked in the sources.

The domain construction supplies a canonical normal controlled process through finite-cutoff limits; it does not assert uniqueness of every formal unbounded generator extension. The virtual emission cap is not a physical thermal-detector count bound.
