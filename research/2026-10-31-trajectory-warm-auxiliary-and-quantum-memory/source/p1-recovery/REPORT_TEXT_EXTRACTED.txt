Cumulative learning through
acquired observation operations
A self-contained Astra investigation · 31 October 2026
Result. A learner can acquire exact executable functions from noisy observations, then use those
functions to align a different collection of noisy tasks into repeated observations of a new unknown
function. The relationships, useful numerical noise bounds, and task programs are acquired. The final
construction receives no hidden graph, task grouping, planted rank, or numerical noise rate.
The mechanism is conditional on a demanding but explicit observation model: uniform binary inputs,
parity tasks, and independently corrupted channel labels at the same input. It includes a procedure that
can decline when the observed evidence is insufficient. Its current-episode correctness does not require
past task associations to have been correct: fresh tests assess the actual executable functions.

1. The question and the supplied resources
The scientific question is whether acquired knowledge can change the computation needed to learn a
later capability. A useful answer must specify the earlier observations, the executable object retained,
the later observation interface, and the cost of finding and checking the connection. Retaining more
outputs or reusing a convenient latent structure without acquiring it does not answer that question.
What a source row contains
 X ∼ Uniform(F₂n),   Zi = χcᵢ(X) ξi,   χc(x) = (−1)c·x.
There are M fixed unknown vectors cᵢ. Every row contains the actual n input bits and all M channel labels
at that same input. The noise signs are independent across channels and rows, independent of X, and
have fixed unknown means βᵢ in (0,1]. Their law is stable across discovery, calibration, and decoding.
Positive orientation, the parity model, uniform inputs, and noise independence are supplied
assumptions. Finite calibration does not establish them.
The learner receives n, the number of observed channels, a sample callable, finite batch sizes, and
rational confidence budgets. It cannot reset a chosen input, request a direct repeated target label,
inspect hidden coefficients, or query a latent graph. If a source returns all channels together, all those
labels are charged even when only some tasks are eventually admitted.
What old knowledge means
At a later episode the learner retains a finite, frozen dictionary D of q explicit n-bit vectors. Each vector
defines an exact parity program. It may have been learned earlier or constructed by a paid XOR of
retained programs. D is a finite list, not free access to all 2ⁿ parities. Evaluating an old program is
computation; it is not a new observation.
A stored vector need not still carry a correct historical task name. Its execution nevertheless computes
exactly the parity described by those bits. The new experiment tests its current relevance. This
distinction is the basis of the error-containment result below.
Qualified recovery theorem
For any such history, dictionary, and current task collection, the three-phase procedure in Sections 2–3
acquires a sound graph of task contrasts, calibrates its actual groups, and returns exact programs for
the admitted tasks, except with probability at most δE + δcal + δD + δR. It may return no program. It
does not promise that arbitrary tasks supply useful redundancy or that a fixed budget suffices for
arbitrarily weak positive biases.
All claims concern exact coefficient recovery on the stated success event. On that event, a returned
parity program is correct on every future input, not just on held-out examples. The theorem is ordinary
mathematics, independently reconstructed by two helpers; it is not a proof-assistant certificate.

2. Acquiring the operation that aligns observations
Fresh tests discover true contrasts
For every channel pair i < j and candidate d in D, estimate the following moment on A discovery rows:
θij,d = E[ZiZjχd(X)]
= βiβj if d = ci + cj;   0 otherwise.
This identity uses uniform-input parity orthogonality and input-independent noise. There are Q =
q·choose(M,2) candidates. With εE = √[2 ln(2Q/δE)/A], retain only empirical means larger than εE.
Hoeffding and a union bound imply that every retained edge is true, except on an event of probability
δE. Independence between different candidate tests is unnecessary. Weak true edges may be absent.
Each retained edge carries its explicit vector d. Select a root r in every connected component and XOR
edge vectors along a spanning tree. On the sound-edge event, the resulting mask is tᵢ = cᵢ + cᵣ. Check all
remaining edges against these masks. Cycle checks establish internal consistency; their external truth
still depends on the statistical event.
Path length does not compound new measurement noise
 Wi = Ziχtᵢ(X) = χcᵣ(X) ξi.
The path mask is a computed program, not a product of fresh noisy edge observations. Each aligned
channel therefore retains just its own original error ξᵢ. All channels in a component now observe one root
bit with independent errors. The learner has acquired the correspondence that makes this operation
possible; it was not handed a grouping or an anchor assignment.
A target-free numerical calibration
For a discovered component C with ℓ ≥ 2, use R fresh rows to compute:
TC = [(Σi∈C Wi)² − ℓ] / [ℓ(ℓ−1)].
The unknown root sign disappears on squaring. The statistic has range contained in [−1/(ℓ−1),1], width
aℓ = ℓ/(ℓ−1), and expectation ρC = 2Σi<jβᵢβⱼ / [ℓ(ℓ−1)]. It costs a sum and square per row, not an
enumeration of all channel pairs. For h components fixed after discovery, a simultaneous lower bound is:
ρ̲C = max{0, T̄C − aℓ√[ln(h/δcal)/(2R)]}.
With conditional probability at least 1−δcal, every lower bound is valid. If μC is the mean of the channel
biases, Cauchy–Schwarz gives μC² ≥ ρC. The calibration therefore supplies a usable noise bound without
learning each channel’s rate or observing any true target label.

3. Decoding, finite qualification, and cumulative validity
For an independent decoding row, a wrong majority or tie is contained in Σξᵢ ≤ 0. Markov’s inequality,
independence, and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality give, for λ > 0:
P(Σξᵢ ≤ 0) ≤ Πi(cosh λ − βisinh λ)
≤ (cosh λ − μCsinh λ)ℓ.
Optimize at tanh λ = μC, with the noiseless endpoint handled by a limit. On the calibration event the
per-row failure probability is bounded by:
qC ≤ (1−μC²)ℓ/2 ≤ (1−ρ̲C)ℓ/2.
Choose N = n + ceil(log₂(1/δR)). Before acquiring the decoding batch, admit any set S satisfying the
actually checked risk budget:
N ΣC∈S (1−ρ̲C)ℓC/2 ≤ δD.
A union bound then controls every root label in the fresh batch. The common N×n input matrix fails to
have full column rank with probability less than 2n−N ≤ δR. One shared GF(2) elimination recovers the
selected roots, and cᵢ = cᵣ + tᵢ recovers their tasks. The rank event is shared across all targets; it is not
charged separately for each one.
Integer and rational decisions
The implementation replaces logarithms and square roots by conservative integer bounds. For edge
discovery take J = ceil(log₂(2Q/δE)) and s = ceil(√(2JA)); accept only a signed sum strictly exceeding s.
For calibration take K = ceil(log₂(h/δcal)) and u = ceil(√(2RK))/(2R), replacing the real radius by aℓu. The
actual code uses M as a conservative upper bound on h.
(1−ρ̲C)ℓ ≤ [δD/(Nh)]²
This squared comparison allocates an equal share to each calibrated component and is exact rational
arithmetic. It implies the preceding total-risk gate. Squaring is safe because both sides are nonnegative.
Integer bit growth, exponentiation, confidence calculations, and all failed qualification attempts are
computation to be paid. The rule is sufficient; it is not an optimal decoder or an impossibility threshold.
No uncharged inheritance of old errors
Condition on any past history, including wrong old task associations. The explicit dictionary is now fixed,
so the current moment identity and fresh-data proof still apply. Past errors can remove useful contrasts
and reduce coverage, but they do not add an uncharged current error term. A claim that all old and new
identifications are correct still uses the sum of their episode budgets. An indefinite sequence needs
summable allocations and may require increasing resources.
Freshness matters: the acquired subgraph and its admission decisions are data-dependent. This
theorem uses separate discovery, calibration, and decoding batches. Repeatedly inspecting a
fixed-sample interval until it passes, or voting on its own selection noise, is not covered.

4. Earning the first executable library
The initial procedure assumes arbitrary parity tasks under the same source model. It has no old
dictionary, hidden graph promise, numerical noise rate, supplied rank, or redundancy grouping. It
searches for a sound, possibly incomplete collection of short checks.
E[ZeZfZg] = βeβfβg
when ce + cf + cg = 0, and 0 otherwise.
Test all choose(M,3) distinct triples using the positive-confidence rule. For a target e, an accepted triple
{e,f,g} supplies a possible repair pair {f,g}. Compute an actual matching among these pairs, so the
selected pairs use disjoint primitive channels. A deterministic greedy scan suffices; it need not find the
maximum matching.
Ve,j = ZfⱼZgⱼ = χcₑ(X) ξfⱼξgⱼ.
Within this target, the product noises are independent and have positive means αⱼ = βfⱼβgⱼ. Apply the
same aggregate calibration and exact risk gate on fresh data. Use another fresh batch for majority
correction and a shared GF(2) solve. Different targets may reuse channels: their errors need not be
independent, because simultaneous calibration and decoding use union bounds.
The initial failure bound is δchecks + δcal + δdecode + δrank. The learner pays every triple considered,
matching construction, calibration, decoding, exact solving, basis selection, and retention. Its retained
basis is selected from actually recovered task programs.
A nonvacuous source, without a graph oracle
Let hidden vectors a₀,…,ak−1 form an affine independent frame, and let native tasks be the pair parities
cuv = au + av. The channels are shuffled. Triangles supply true checks, and each target edge has k−2
disjoint two-hop repairs. This proves that useful matchings can exist. The final learner neither receives
nor reconstructs the hidden vertex graph; it operates directly on discovered triples.
With homogeneous primitive bias β, a repair vote has bias β² and its aggregate correlation is β⁴.
Two-hop decoding and short parity-check recognition are established methods [1,2]. The new record
does not claim their noise dependence as an advance over stronger synchronization algorithms.
Where the bootstrap stops
If the initial task vectors are linearly independent, there are no triple identities. On the sound-check
event this procedure acquires none. Small matchings or weakly biased channels can also fail
qualification. Declining is a real outcome, not evidence that the tasks are unlearnable. This construction
is not a general solver for passive learning parity with noise.
A source with codeword-correlated errors can make every short check pass while preventing error
correction. That failure is preserved in Section 8. The existence of many relations does not itself create
independent evidence.

The initial learner found 22 disjoint repair pairs for each target. All 276 qualified. Its decoding bank had

The next source privately chooses a new vector outside that acquired span, combines it with 21 distinct

These 21 new true task vectors are linearly independent among themselves. Their output marginal is
uniform and has no nonempty internal parity check. The useful operation depends on executing retained
functions on the new inputs. This source is a deliberately constructed favorable task sequence, not
evidence that natural future tasks will supply the same relations.
Verification and boundaries
Models were written and hashed before hidden coefficients and rates were read by their verifiers. The
independent replay reproduced both frozen model JSON files byte for byte and every saved array entry.
It confirmed 472,845 native noisy labels and 152,880 observed input bits. Proof reviews checked the
mathematical interfaces separately from execution.
The original final run took about 13.17 seconds with Python allocation tracing; the isolated replay took
about 13.37 seconds. Those timings are diagnostics, not deployment benchmarks. The implementation
uses finite 64-bit input words for this n=48 fixture; the theorem permits ordinary arbitrary-length bit
vectors. The stated 0.03 bound is conditional on the ideal source model, not an estimated error
frequency from pseudorandom simulations.

6. Full costs and equally informed alternatives
The later theorem pays M(A+R+N) primitive labels and n(A+R+N) input bits whenever decoding is
attempted. With explicit dense vectors, direct work includes Aqn bit operations for dictionary evaluation,
Aq·choose(M,2) correlation operations, path and consistency calculations, (R+N)nM mask work, and
shared elimination. A naive shared solve for s roots costs O(Nn(n+s)) field operations. Packing bits
reduces word operations; it does not remove their widths or the observation bill.
Initial discovery costs O(A·choose(M,3)) scalar record-products, or packed equivalents. The final fixture’s
unpacked triple count is 1,774,643,200. The implementation scans every one of the 3,466,100
candidates. Derived repair votes require additional computation: 6,217,728 calibration XORs and
340,032 decoding XORs were counted in the final bootstrap.
Returning s dense task programs costs ns bits before task identities and bookkeeping. A basis alone
does not preserve each dependent task’s association: the corresponding coefficients or paths must also
be retained. Future execution costs a parity evaluation, O(n) bit work or O(ceil(n/w)) ordinary
packed-word work, without old-source calls. Enlarging D increases the discovery search unless a
separately earned index or selection method replaces it.
Final-data direct learner
An ordinary comparator retains the exact same final observations. It fits candidates from random
full-rank input subsets, shares each GF(2) factorization across all tasks, and selects each task’s best
candidate using the independent solve bank. Numerical noise rates and hidden graphs are not supplied.
The fixed 256-attempt run preserves 64/128/256 states before truth comparison.
All 276 initial programs and all 21 later programs were already exact at the 64-attempt checkpoint. That
checkpoint took about 3.50 seconds for the initial tasks and 0.48 seconds for the later tasks. The full 256
attempts were actually run and charged, taking about 14.14 and 1.98 seconds. They scored 70,656
initial and 5,376 later candidates, with 3,956,736 and 306,432 held-out parity evaluations. No new
source labels were needed.
An exact null test qualifies every selected function at both checkpoints. For a wrong training-frozen
parity, the mismatch count on N fresh uniform inputs is Binomial(N,1/2). Its exact lower-tail probability is
compared with δ/(256M); one union bound covers all 256 attempts and both checkpoints. Initial winners
had at most five errors against a nine-error admission threshold; later winners had at most five against
eleven. Thus the ordinary 64-attempt checkpoint matches exact-program confidence with the same total
0.03 allowance. Certification used no extra labels or search; 33,306 parity rechecks took about 0.016
seconds. This confidence mechanism is also ordinary statistical qualification.
Other controls and the consequence
Earlier retained-data candidate searches learned all tasks in a fixture where the graph rule declined
three. An exact Walsh–Hadamard maximum-likelihood learner at n=16 recovered all 120 tasks and was
faster than the specialized bootstrap in that fixture. A supplied-graph spectral decoder also succeeded;
stronger SDP synchronization is established [2]. Direct repeated-input target access, if available, permits
ordinary majority without alignment.
No full-cost advantage follows from these results. The final bootstrap costs about 19,104 labels per
acquired independent direction, while its next stage costs 33,453 for one direction. These are different
task ensembles, and their ratio is not an intrinsic efficiency measure. The constructive consequence is a
way for retained executable functions to enable later acquisition under a specified source, with strong
competitors free to use it.

7. What is cumulative, and what extends beyond parity
Define a true graph GD on the current tasks: i and j are adjacent when their actual contrast cᵢ+cⱼ belongs
to the finite dictionary. This graph measures a useful interaction between retained knowledge and the
new experiment. It is not determined by a representation’s dimension or its prediction accuracy alone.
A larger dictionary can join previously disconnected groups, exposing more independent observations of
a root. With a common supplied positive bias bound β, merging components decreases the conservative
risk expression NΣCexp(−|C|β²/2). That is an achievable certificate, not the optimal risk or a lower bound
on all learners. The unknown heterogeneous-bias whole-component rule is not necessarily monotone:
adding many weak channels can worsen an unweighted vote and its aggregate calibration.
The input coordinates already span every parity. New rank refers only to identified task descriptions
relative to the previously identified task library. Mathematical expressivity has not increased, and no
input information was manufactured. What has changed is that useful dense programs have been
located and can now be executed without repeating the search that identified them.
This gives a complete cumulative loop: discover a program; retain its exact description; test its
relevance to a future source; use it to reinterpret current measurements; recover another program.
Exact descriptions avoid accumulating approximation error in execution. Statistical claims about task
identities still carry their own episode budgets.
An explicit broader reduction
Let h₁,…,hk be arbitrary exact Boolean programs, and suppose their distinct pair products disagree with
probability at least γ under an input distribution. New opaque channels have the form Yᵢ = g(X)hπ(i)(X)ξᵢ,
with an unknown injection π and L≥3. Pair products cancel the arbitrary new target g. A finite correlation
search can identify the anchor pairs and their shared endpoint. Correcting by the acquired h programs
then yields independent noisy copies of g(X).
With a supplied positive bias bound β, L ≥ 2β−2ln(m/δ) makes an m-example cleaned batch entirely
correct except with probability δ. Any passive noiseless learner for g’s class can then be used on that
batch. This is a reduction to a stated noiseless learner, not a claim that every Boolean target is
efficiently learnable. The alignment search, separation qualification, old-program execution, labels, and
subsequent training are all paid in the accompanying ordinary proof.
The broader reduction requires exact anchors. Approximate anchors may share the same error c(X): pair
products cancel it, while every corrected label becomes g(X)c(X). Majority cannot repair a common
functional error. Thus the parity construction’s finite exact identification is a substantive reason its
cumulative execution is cleaner than repeatedly distilling an approximate predictor.

Dependence can be invisible to the calibration
If every later channel shares one fresh error ξ, correcting masks gives identical copies of χcᵣ(X)ξ. Pair
products cancel ξ, so calibration can look ideal. The bundle is equivalent to one ordinary noisy-parity
observation per X. In the executed misspecified fixture, qualification passed but the final GF(2)
equations were inconsistent and the program returned no task. That one rejection is not a theorem that
all such violations will be detected.
An additional counterexample preserves conditional independence given X. Let an input event E have
probability τ. Outside E, labels are noiseless; inside E, every channel is an independent fair sign. The
average pair correlation is ρ=1−τ. For odd ℓ, the actual majority error is τ/2, while incorrectly applying
the constant-bias formula would give τℓ/2. The gap grows with ℓ. Making inside-E biases small and
positive preserves a gap. Input independence or an appropriate uniform conditional guarantee is
essential.
Access, certification, and cost are different claims
Exact known masks do not create repeated inputs. With unpaired examples, multiplying by a known
mask simply converts each record into an ordinary passive noisy-parity example. This is an exact
experiment reduction; any hardness conclusion additionally requires the relevant LPN hardness
assumption. It does not establish a lower bound for equally informed learners given the paired data used
by our positive construction.
Nor does a failed confidence gate prove an information limit. It is a limitation of a sufficient finite rule.
Search failure, extra calibration, aborted solves, and rejected proposals remain in the resource ledger.
The evidence preserves these failures rather than rewriting them as successful acquisition.

9. Source-informed selection and preserved capital
Astra retrieval began at 00_START_HERE.txt, followed by task-first learning,
representation-acquisition, and program-synthesis navigation. Governing files were retrieved at commit
72f713bfca2d1de2f869a2ec7c289323e6f0a979. Seven exact sources, their repository paths, blob
identities, and SHA-256 hashes are preserved in the evidence bundle. Navigation cards were used as
routes, not substitutes for proofs.
The older ephemeral-procedure construction already learns a shared finite-field map from actual
expiring sources and tracks a shrinking affine uncertainty family. Its cumulative acquisition bound
charges anchor observations, guards, failed work, algebra, and execution. The guarded-family and
cohort constructions already distinguish accepted procedures from guaranteed future coverage and pay
fresh qualification or randomized self-correction. The retained-relation construction also distinguishes a
discovered invariant from an actually available conditional experiment.
These sources ruled out presenting ordinary representation retention as a new principle. Their failures
were equally useful: old raw data can determine a later generic fit; fixed reused guards invalidate
confidence; a conditional dimension optimum can lose to a joint query policy; and acquired operator
laws can lose to retained Krylov or tomography controls. The present comparison grants conventional
learners the same retained data and executable descriptions.
Primary precedents
Noisy short-check recognition is established [1], and disjoint two-hop voting appears explicitly in binary
synchronization work that also gives stronger SDP methods [2]. Lifelong metafeature acquisition has
formal precedents [3]; OOPS already treats reusable programs and charged incremental search [4].
Those facts guide the comparator and prevent a claim that the ingredients are historically new. The full
assembly’s priority remains unsettled.
Separate accessible compiler capital
The earlier signed predictive compiler was independently reconstructed and its four scientific checks
replayed. Its source-only acquisition returns an autonomous rational trajectory law with a
width-dependent KL guarantee. It uses actual conditional-source access and a retained dictionary that is
larger than the proof’s hidden witness. Support-safe escaped forests and normalization corrections
remain part of its governing result.
That compiler is valuable separate capital. Joint-law accuracy does not justify arbitrary rare conditional
experiments, and recursively applying a fourth-root mismatch bound is not a cumulative improvement
theorem. An ordinary retained Bayesian model pool supplies a strong competing reuse rule. Its finite
product identities were checked on 768 enumerated datasets. The new investigation keeps these facts
and failures without treating them as evidence for the parity construction.
The separate capital archive preserves the accessible p1_opening tree unchanged, including its audits,
stronger dyadic acquisition, obsolete predecessor drafts with their original names, and control/memory
obstructions. The archive index identifies the governing sources; it does not silently promote every
preserved intermediate draft to the final theorem.

10. What remains consequential and unresolved
The proved mechanism values retained knowledge by the new measurements it helps interpret. It
supplies one concrete route from an acquired program to another acquired program, even when the
later tasks have no output-only parity checks. Its strongest reliability feature is conditional
current-episode correctness under arbitrary historical task-label errors, obtained by testing executable
behavior on fresh data.
The next substantial advance must improve a load-bearing limitation, rather than merely enlarge a
favorable gain ratio. Three openings remain: discover or engineer useful independent task relations at
an acceptable native cost; acquire a compact searchable contrast dictionary whose use beats scanning
the retained library; or extend the operation to approximate programs while preventing common
functional errors from propagating. Each would need a full retained-data comparator and a source model
that supplies the promised observations.
