\[

### An explicit graph theorem

Consider

\[
H=\sum_i(\Omega X_i+h_iZ_i)+\sum_{ij\in E}J_{ij}Z_iZ_j,
\]

with uniform \(\Omega\ne0\), arbitrary real \(h_i,J_{ij}\), and local \(Z\)-dephasing of rate \(\gamma>0\). Thus a Pauli string with \(k\) transverse \(X/Y\) factors is damped at rate \(k\gamma\).

For \(A_b=\sum_i b_iX_i\), use the trial

\[
u_v=
\sum_i v_iX_i
+\sum_i\frac{h_iv_i}{\Omega}Z_i
+\sum_{ij}\frac{J_{ij}(v_i+v_j)}{2\Omega}Z_iZ_j .
\]

The longitudinal fields and the symmetric part of each interaction cancel in its commutator. What remains is exactly

\[
Ku_v
=
\sum_{ij}J_{ij}(v_j-v_i)
\left(Y_iZ_j-Z_iY_j\right).
\]

Consequently,

\[
\langle u_v,Du_v\rangle+
\|D^{-1/2}Ku_v\|^2
=
\gamma\sum_i v_i^2
+\frac2\gamma\sum_{ij}J_{ij}^2(v_i-v_j)^2.
\]

Let \(L_{J^2}\) be the graph Laplacian with edge weights \(J_{ij}^2\). Optimizing over \(v\) gives

\[
\boxed{
b^{\mathsf T}
\left(\gamma I+\frac2\gamma L_{J^2}\right)^{-1}b
\le R_{A_b}(0,0)
\le \frac{\|b\|^2}{\gamma}.
}
\]

All coupling signs and longitudinal fields are allowed. The remaining quantum system has not been replaced by a classical dynamics; the graph supplies a rigorous spectral enclosure.

An especially inexpensive consequence comes from simply choosing \(v=b\). With

\[
S=\sum_i b_i^2,\qquad
e=\sum_{ij}J_{ij}^2(b_i-b_j)^2,
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{1+2e/(\gamma^2S)}
\le
\frac{\gamma R_{A_b}(0,0)}S
\le1.
}
\]

This answers a useful class of queries in one pass over the input. The success condition is an **acquired small graph variation of the requested observable**. There is no uncharged eigenvector or optimizer.

This also identifies the limitation honestly: a local or rapidly varying observable may have a large \(e/S\), making the interval wide. Failure of that certificate does not establish computational hardness.

## 2. The finite-frequency obstruction was removable

At positive linewidth, a real trial containing undamped \(Z\)-strings pays

\[
\frac{\omega^2}{\eta}\|P_0u\|^2,
\]

where \(P_0\) projects onto \(\ker D\). That term made the first finite-frequency extension poor when \(\eta\) was small.

I retained the undamped coordinates and solved their constraint explicitly.

Define

\[
\mathcal B=(I-P_0)KP_0,\qquad
C=\mathcal B^\dagger\mathcal B.
\]

For a real trial \(v\) with \(P_0Kv=0\), let \(d=P_0v\), and suppose \(d\in\operatorname{ran}C\). Set

\[
w=\mathcal B C^+d,\qquad
u=v+i\omega w.
\]

Skew-adjointness gives

\[
P_0Kw=-d,
\]

so

\[
P_0(K+i\omega)u=0.
\]

The corrected denominator is therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal D(\eta,\omega)
={}&\langle v,E_\eta v\rangle
+\omega^2\langle w,E_\eta w\rangle\\
&+\|E_\eta^{-1/2}(Kv-\omega^2w)\|^2
+\omega^2\|E_\eta^{-1/2}(Kw+v)\|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Both residuals inside the inverse metric have **zero undamped component**. The artificial inverse-linewidth penalty disappears. The genuine finite-linewidth contribution \(\eta\|d\|^2\) remains.

The operation \(C^+\) is acquired locally. For a diagonal Pauli string \(Z_S\),

\[
\mathcal B Z_S
=
2\sum_{i\in S}\Omega_iY_iZ_{S\setminus i},
\qquad
CZ_S=4\sum_{i\in S}\Omega_i^2\,Z_S.
\]

Different supports have disjoint images. A trial involving \(Z_i\) and \(Z_iZ_j\) therefore needs only scalar divisions. There is no exponentially large undamped-sector inverse hidden in this step.

For the uniform-drive energy trial, \(w\) contains only \(Y_i\) and \(Y_iZ_j\) terms. Its full residual has Pauli weight at most three. Furthermore, the antisymmetric gradient in \(Kv\) is orthogonal to the symmetric edge correction in \(w\). The denominator becomes

\[
\mathcal D(\eta,\omega)
=
D_0+D_2\omega^2+D_4\omega^4,
\qquad
D_0>0,\quad D_2,D_4\ge0.
\]

All three coefficients were computed exactly from the supplied model.
