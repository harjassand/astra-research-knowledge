## 2. The central theorem: fixed-quality cooling can support arbitrarily sharp absorption

The difficult issue is conditioning.

An absorber preferentially removes the states it couples to. Consequently, unsuccessful histories can select hotter or otherwise less accessible states. A low mean energy before the first attempt does not establish a repeated-use guarantee.

I resolved this by tracking both survival probability and **unnormalized** surviving energy.

Let \(N\ge0\) be an excitation observable on the auxiliary/source domain. Let \(P\) select a useful low-energy subspace, with

\[
N\ge I-P.
\]

One cycle consists of a physical renewal channel \(\mathcal C\), followed by an accepted/unsuccessful instrument. Write \(\mathcal F\) for the unsuccessful map and

\[
A=I-\mathcal F^*(I)
\]

for the accepted effect.

The required qualifications are operator inequalities:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&0\le A\le I,
&&PAP\ge p_0P,\qquad p_0>0,\\
&\mathcal C^*(N)\le rN+bI,
&&0\le r<1,\\
&\mathcal F^*(N)\le uN+dI,
&&u,d\ge0.
\end{aligned}
\]

They must hold on the actual allowed source domain. They are stronger than rates measured on one prepared state, but they require no reconstruction of the complete channel.

### A sharp conversion from low-energy acceptance to global progress

For every \(0<c<p_0\),

\[
\boxed{
A\ge cI-\beta N,
\qquad
\beta=\frac{c(1-c)}{p_0-c}.
}
\]

This follows from a block decomposition of \(A\) into \(P\) and \(I-P\), using both \(A\ge0\) and \(A\le I\), followed by a Schur-complement bound. The coefficient is sharp already for a two-dimensional rank-one effect.

For an unnormalized surviving state \(\rho\), define

\[
q=\operatorname{tr}\rho,
\qquad
n=\operatorname{tr}(N\rho).
\]

The actual cycle then obeys

\[
\begin{pmatrix}q'\\n'\end{pmatrix}
\le
M
\begin{pmatrix}q\\n\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-c+\beta b & \beta r\\
ub+d & ur
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The first row accounts for acceptance. The second includes the energy change caused by surviving the measurement.

For this positive comparison matrix,

\[
\boxed{
\rho(M)<1
\iff
ur<1
\quad\text{and}\quad
c(1-ur)>\beta(b+rd).
}
\]

Under these conditions there are explicit \(\alpha>0\) and \(\lambda<1\) such that

\[
q_k+\alpha n_k
\le
\lambda^k(q_0+\alpha n_0).
\]

Thus both the probability of continued failure and its weighted energy decay exponentially. The theorem permits rare surviving histories to be very hot; their total joint weight is controlled.

Some choice of \(c\) in the sharp inequality works precisely when

\[
\boxed{
ur<1,
\qquad
b+rd<p_0(1-ur).
}
\]

This is an exact criterion for this certificate. It is not a necessary condition for every possible absorber.

## 3. Earning progress from a microscopic interaction

\[

\[

=
