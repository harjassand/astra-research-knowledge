There’s a useful improvement to the quantum-memory route: a different three-body qubit construction has an internally checked energy-scale guarantee more than 100 million times larger than the first one. It uses more connections between the auxiliary qubits, so that gain has a hardware cost. The important caveat is that the actual memory temperature requirement is still much colder than that number alone suggests, and we still haven’t established efficient, fault-tolerant writing or robustness to general hardware noise. So I’m not yet calling it a practical memory.
