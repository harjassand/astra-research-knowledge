# Astra fusion scout — completed investigation

**The strongest supported opening is a heating-balanced RF intervention that identifies, rather than assumes, the causal relationship between zonal-flow dynamics and turbulent transport.**

The investigation produced a physical wave-reachability calculation, a conditional wave-to-momentum construction, a finite-time controllability result, and executable nonlinear transport tests. It also exposed a serious limitation: the simplest realizable wave model couples extremely weakly to the desired fine-scale zonal structure.

**No net fusion-confinement benefit has been established.** The model calculations do not yet connect an actual tokamak antenna to a naturally sustained zonal mode and then to self-consistent ion/electron heat flux. The principal value is a better-defined physical operation and several decisive failures that prevent the earlier phase-locking model from being mistaken for a reactor capability.

## 1\. The transfer from wave physics

The starting point is Resonant and Ponderomotive Pumping of Zonal Flows by Turbulence, Alfvén Eigenmodes, and Radiofrequency Waves \(urlarXiv v1https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.36299v1\). Its governing construction separates stored wave-dressing momentum from resonantly transferred momentum. citeturn523457academia62

In its specified slab regime, the relevant distinction is

\[
\partial_t\Pi_y
=
\partial_t\mathcal P_{\mathrm{dress}}
+
\mathcal Q_y,
\qquad
\mathcal Q_y
=
\sum_s\int
\frac{k_y}{\omega}\,
p_{\mathrm{abs},s}\,d\omega\,d^3k.
\]

A steady wave can transfer canonical momentum through resonant absorption. During switching, however, the time derivative of dressed momentum is a separate physical contribution. Omitting it could produce an incorrect estimate of the phase-control impulse.

### The candidate operation

In a medium symmetric under \(k_y\mapsto-k_y\), consider two equal-frequency waves with opposite poloidal wavenumbers\:

\[
P_+=\frac P2(1+u),
\qquad
P_-=\frac P2(1-u),
\qquad |u|\le1.
\]

Their total launched power remains \(P\), while their signed canonical momentum contributions oppose one another.

This creates a potentially useful **heating-balanced torque probe**\: vary the direction of momentum input without varying total absorbed power in the symmetric model.

That is an established conditional transfer from wave symmetry and momentum accounting, not yet an experimentally calibrated actuator. In a real magnetized plasma, opposite-\(k_y\) waves may have different paths, polarizations, resonances and species heating. Even matching total absorbed watts would not guarantee matching *local* heating profiles.

Crucially, two such waves share the same radial force profile in the symmetric slab. They supply one signed spatial control channel, **not an arbitrary positive-and-negative radial deposition pattern**\.

## 2\. The physical reachability calculation was unfavorable

I constructed and executed a Maxwell boundary-value calculation for a cold O-mode wave incident on a uniform dissipative plasma.

For frequency \(110\) GHz, density \(2\times10^{19}\,\mathrm m^{-3}\), phenomenological collision frequency \(10^8\,\mathrm s^{-1}\), transverse wavenumber \(100\,\mathrm m^{-1}\), and a \(0.5\) m plasma slab, the results were\:

# 2.36%

Launched power absorbed inside the slab

# 0.076%

Absorbed-profile overlap with radial harmonic 5

# 2.60 nN

Formal mode-force component per 1 MW launched

These are calculated properties of the stated cold slab, **not tokamak measurements**\.

The central mathematical obstruction is transparent. For an exponentially absorbed wave with attenuation coefficient \(\alpha\), the normalized overlap with radial wavenumber \(q\), over a compatible integer-period interval, is

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal H_q|
=
\frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt{4\alpha^2+q^2}}.
}
\]

High radial wavenumbers receive little useful forcing when absorption varies only slowly across radius.

There is a second obstruction in the earlier illustrative ion-Bernstein parameters. At 74 MHz and \(k_y=4000\,\mathrm m^{-1}\), the vacuum wavenumber is only about \(1.55\,\mathrm m^{-1}\)\. Such a wave cannot propagate directly across vacuum as that transverse Fourier component.

This is **not a no-go theorem for mode-converted ion-Bernstein waves**\. That distinction changed the investigation. Existing RF work already demonstrates the production of substantial poloidal flows through ion-cyclotron and mode-converted wave physics. citeturn523457search1turn523457search11turn523457search8

### A more suitable existing tool

I inspected the actual MATLAB implementation of BORAY, including its urlpower-absorption routinehttps://github.com/hsxie/boray/blob/main/modules/boray_calpower.m\.

BORAY calculates ray propagation from cold-plasma dispersion and damping from hot kinetic dispersion. Its relevant operation is real, unlike an arbitrarily assigned deposition kernel. citeturn523457academia61turn523457search4

I constructed a downstream calculation that distributes the **same remaining ray power** among absorbing species\:

\[
P(t)=P_0\exp\left[-\int_0^t\sum_s\alpha_s(\tau)d\tau\right],
\qquad
dP_s=\alpha_sP\,dt.
\]

This avoids independently exponentiating species absorption and then adding fractions that need not form a conserved power partition. The executable passes its energy-conservation checks.

However, I did not execute a full BORAY equilibrium or acquire a hot, resonant deposition map for the target tokamak. Most importantly, the cold O-mode calculation uses phenomenological **collisional** absorption. Its formal \(k_yP_{\rm abs}/\omega\) momentum bookkeeping cannot simply be substituted for the **collisionless resonant** source in Gallaro–Dodin.

That remains a real missing bridge.

## 3\. Time-dependent control provides a stronger opening than spatial phase forcing alone

A single real spatial force profile generally changes both amplitude and phase of a complex zonal mode.

Writing the measured mode as \(z=Ae^{i\theta}\), with a force coefficient \(B\),

\[
\dot A_{\rm RF}
=
u\,\operatorname{Re}(Be^{-i\theta}),
\qquad
A\dot\theta_{\rm RF}
=
u\,\operatorname{Im}(Be^{-i\theta}).
\]

The controller cannot ordinarily choose those two changes independently at one instant.

But a different principle survives: **the plasma's own time evolution may rotate one spatial input into independent response directions**\.

For the linearized model

\[
\dot z=(-\lambda+i\Omega)z+Bu(t),
\]

the finite-horizon controllability Gramian has two nonzero eigenvalues when the mode rotates sufficiently during the observation interval. It becomes rank-deficient when the effective rotation vanishes.

This is established control theory. The new physical question is whether a turbulence-sustained zonal mode retains enough coherent response for that operation to be useful, without relying on a supplied Adler oscillator.

### Nonlinear executable test

I implemented a modified Hasegawa–Wakatani drift-wave model, allowed nonlinear zonal dynamics to develop, and measured complex responses to two separately timed perturbations.

The resulting two-pulse response matrices had condition numbers\:

\[
1.94,\qquad 6.14,\qquad 27.11.
\]

The finite-time response was therefore locally two-dimensional in all three tested states, though the last was poorly conditioned.

Using ordinary constrained linear control on those measured response columns, I requested a \(0.001\)\-radian phase change with approximately unchanged amplitude. Nonlinear replay gave\:

| Model state | Achieved phase change | Relative amplitude change |
|--------|--------|--------|
| 19 | 0.000998552 rad | \(1.53\times10^{-6}\) |
| 22 | 0.001000584 rad | \(0.64\times10^{-6}\) |
| 27 | 0.001053507 rad | \(21.21\times10^{-6}\) |

This is a substantive **finite-time local controllability result in the reduced model**, not an RF plasma control result. It also demonstrates why the instantaneous one-port rank restriction is not a universal prohibition on phase positioning.

The strongest ordinary two-pulse least-squares controller uses exactly the same measured information and achieves the same local objective. There is no demonstrated algorithmic advantage over that controller.

## 4\. The transport test changes the scientific decision

The recent Singh–Diamond work makes phase stability itself an uncertain premise. Its gyrokinetic simulations find intermittent nonlinear energy transfer from zonal modes back to turbulence, scattering both zonal phase and amplitude. citeturn523457search0

I therefore added a **passively advected temperature field** to the nonlinear drift-wave calculation\:

\[
\partial_tT+\{\phi,T\}
=
-\kappa_T\partial_y\phi
+\chi\nabla^2T-\mu T,
\]

with a model radial heat-flux observable

\[
Q_T=\langle-\partial_y\phi\,T\rangle.
\]

The temperature-budget identity and separate zonal-energy budget were checked to approximately \(10^{-16}\) residual in the tested state. A three-time-step short-horizon convergence test produced finite causal responses of heat flux, amplitude and phase.

This is more informative than squared shear, but still not a self-consistent gyrokinetic ion/electron heat-flux calculation: the temperature is passive and does not feed back on the turbulence.

| State | Heat-minimizing action | Phase-minimizing action |
|--------|--------|--------|
| 19 | Negative | Positive |

In two of the three cases, the phase-minimizing action slightly **increased** the model heat flux, while an equally available heat-minimizing action reduced it.

This is an offline best-of-three comparison; its future-response calibration is not free, and it is not a deployable online controller. Nevertheless, it is a direct discriminator of the proposed objective. **Better phase positioning is not sufficient evidence of better heat transport.**

Numerical failures are also retained. An overly coarse long-horizon run diverged; a refined finite-horizon run survived. Short-time derivative convergence does not justify extrapolating the reported flux differences to reactor confinement.

## 5\. Why the Gkeyll TCV dataset does not close the gap

I inspected both the urlZenodo dataset descriptionhttps://zenodo.org/records/20146930 and the urlpublished Gkeyll input implementationhttps://github.com/gkeyllorg/gkyl-paper-inp/blob/ae40ef73152de62bff9b5b4cb94cfc211ba123a1/2026_PRL_tcv_miller_scan/tcv_miller_scan_input.c\.

The dataset has 256 gyrokinetic runs varying elongation, triangularity and core heating. Its outputs include electrostatic potential, particle and heat-flux moments, and integrated diagnostics. citeturn523457search6

The source-code scan confirms that these runs do **not** vary a separately controlled RF momentum source. The large simulation archives were not downloaded or inspected.

There is an exact identifiability reason this matters. Two controlled dynamical models

\[
\dot X=F(X)+B_1(X)u,
\qquad
\dot X=F(X)+B_2(X)u
\]

produce the same passive trajectories when \(u=0\), even though their responses to RF intervention can differ arbitrarily.

Thus more passive Gkeyll data can help identify natural zonal structures, coherence and transport conditions. It cannot, by itself, determine an unknown RF-to-zonal causal susceptibility.

## 6\. Strongest scientific conclusion

The pilot supports three distinct findings.

**Established transfer\:** Wave symmetry gives a heating-balanced signed-momentum probe in a symmetric physical model. Full Maxwell propagation sharply restricts its spatial spectrum. Finite-time controllability permits two independent endpoint directions from one source when the dynamics rotates them.

**Promising but unearned transfer\:** A mode-converted resonant RF antenna could supply spatially useful, sign-reversible momentum perturbations to an actually sustained zonal mode. That requires source-resolved hot wave physics, transient dressing, species heating, and a calibrated complex response.

**Failed mechanism\:** Arbitrarily programmable bipolar deposition, harmless fine-scale RF phase forcing, or a favorable heat-flux benefit inferred from Adler locking or squared shear. The tested cold O-mode strongly disfavors affordable fine-scale steering, and the nonlinear model does not establish a phase-targeting transport advantage.

The more consequential target is therefore **not phase locking itself**\. It is a physically calibrated intervention that can test—and potentially exploit—the causal relationship among RF momentum deposition, zonal coherence, nonlinear energy back-transfer and actual thermal confinement.

Before treating phase steering as a technology programme, the first hard obligation is to calculate a nonzero, well-conditioned **resonant RF source-to-zonal-mode kernel** in a specific magnetic geometry, including wave dressing and all absorbed power. The second is to test that same source in nonlinear gyrokinetics and compare **direct heat-flux optimization** against phase targeting with identical sensing and RF hardware.

If the first kernel is too weak or poorly conditioned, this RF phase-steering route should not advance merely because the reduced oscillator can be controlled.

The work does not constitute a civilisation-changing fusion breakthrough. It substantially sharpens the physical interface, supplies executable causal discrimination, and identifies where new physics—not better phase-model tuning—is required.
