Coherent Service Rate
Quantum storage needs coherent service capacity

Author /root, 29 October 2026. New derivation; author checks and independent review are separate. This is a theorem for a specified heralded-erasure/service model, not an inferred device rate, a norm-to-probability conversion, or a universal physical memory limit.

1. Consequence and interface

An exactly closed mediator can remove initialization before each gate. It does not by itself make a serial processor a scalable memory. We grant arbitrary codes, classical records, adaptive scheduling and final recovery, while charging the rate at which quantum connections can be restored after storage loss.

There are N idle storage qubits. A coherent service region contains at most w source qubits at once; other quantum output/controller memory has dimension at most 2^b. For a stronger converse, service-region and b-memory noise may be zero. All other quantum systems supplied to the final decoder count in these dimensions: a returning bath cannot be a free output bypass.

An idle site undergoes independent Poisson replacement erasure at rate gamma>0. Its completed channel discards the old quantum state into a nonreturning environment, supplies a fresh local state independent of the other quantum systems, and appends an actual classical flag. A local environment that can later return the erased state is outside this replacement channel.

Arbitrary single-site operations, measurements and classical communication/feedback are free between service contacts. They cannot create entanglement between a reset site and the remaining apparatus. Operations that can do so belong to the service region or count as a new supplied quantum connection. A freshly supplied entangled storage qubit counts as a service completion. An address-controlled operation acting on many possible sites counts its actual source support; an abstract oracle name does not make that one physical contact.

A completion releases one source qubit from service to idle storage. Releasing r sites costs r incidences. This handles finite-duration interactions: a continuously coupled source occupies a service slot rather than creating arbitrarily many idle carriers. The number of completions in each declared interval of duration Delta is at most c. This is an acquired throughput limit, not a consequence of small memory dimension.

The task preserves K unknown qubits, possibly through a specified reversible computation whose known inverse can be included in the final decoder for this converse. Test the logical input with a maximally entangled reference of dimension d=2^K. Every actual classical record remains available. Postselected failures keep their probabilities; no free repeated starts are supplied.

2. A random causal cut after the last erasure

Mark all idle sites live initially. Erasure removes a mark; a new quantum connection or service completion can restore it. Entering service removes the site from the idle-live count A(t).

Conditioned on classical erasure/control history, an unmarked site has undergone independent replacement since its last quantum connection to the rest. Subsequent single-site operations and classical feedback leave it separable from the reference and remaining quantum apparatus. Refining unobserved local Kraus indices in a convex-decomposition proof does not assert that they were physically observed. A coherently accessible local environment must be counted in the quantum interface.

Thus all reference entanglement crosses at most A(t)+b+w quantum wires on each history branch. Unmarked sites and unlimited classical records do not enlarge this cut. This includes arbitrary nonstabilizer codes and coherent correction within service.

A state with Schmidt number r has overlap at most r/d with a d-dimensional maximally entangled state: prove it for pure states by Cauchy-Schwarz on Schmidt coefficients, then use convexity. 3. Adaptive service and idle loss

Put q=exp(-gamma Delta), p=1-q, and let A_m be the live idle count at interval m's end. Conditional on the available past at interval start, assign independent rate-gamma clocks to ALL initially live idle sites, before within-interval decisions. An old site still live and idle at interval end, with no intervening completion, must be among these clocks' surviving carriers. No independence is asserted conditional on an adaptively selected subset of sites that never entered service. A site entering and returning from service is included among at most c completions. Counting every completion as still live at interval end only helps the memory. Scheduling is nonanticipating: it can use past flags, but cannot know a future erasure clock value.

For a coupling proof, assign independent exponential lifetimes to currently live idle sites. When one enters service, retain its removed lifetime as a ghost. At a completion give it a fresh lifetime; for domination postpone the new ghost's losses until interval end and insert c fresh ghosts there. A single bus with fixed b,w,c,Delta cannot preserve an extensive quantum register for long times in this model. Larger source codes do not supply missing service capacity. The conclusion changes if loss decreases with size, coherent activity grows, additional protected memory is supplied, or a different self-correcting interaction acts across idle sites. Those are genuine additional mechanisms, not excluded by a replacement-erasure theorem.

Classical information can behave differently: measuring the input and retaining its classical value does not preserve entanglement with a reference and fails this quantum task.

5. Constructive response and strong alternatives

The bound motivates parallel bounded-support service. A conventional fault-tolerant circuit with bounded parallel scheduling degree can be compiled into distributed closed-mediator modules. If each ideal module is G_local tensor identity on the full admitted mediator support, old mediator errors cannot spread through later fault-free modules. This tranche's separate compiler derives that transfer with explicit full noise and record conditions.

Service activity proportional to the register removes this throughput obstruction. It does not prove a threshold by itself. Noisy preparation, syndrome acquisition, source control/storage and reuse still need the conventional circuit's actual fault-tolerance and acquisition conditions. Scheduling depth must remain bounded; replacing a parallel circuit by one serial bus reintroduces (8).

The strongest direct-coupling strategy receives the same loss and service allowances. A two-source gate can complete two incidences; a mediator pays its actual contacts/routing. Direct coupling can be cheaper. A teleportation/fresh-entanglement architecture also replenishes storage, but its supplied entangled sites and protected queues count in C or b,w. The theorem does not withhold those resources from the comparator.

A further same-port alternative resets the bus and uses three contacts per entangling gate, rather than a full-space four-contact closed loop. The avoided reset costs one extra contact. Qualified reset quality, leakage handling, source waiting time and parallel supply can change the preferred architecture. The compiler comparison, not (8), determines those operation costs.

6. Ancestry, tests and limits

Nayak, Chitambar and Varshney, 2406.05599, charge gates and measurements in reliable-memory rates and discuss noise accrued during decoder time. Shtanko et al., 2308.16233, bound autonomous memories using noise and recovery rates for their Markovian decoder models. Throughput is established scientific territory. The local contribution here is the last-erasure causal cut, the arbitrary-code/full-classical-record dimension bound, adaptive service domination and strong converse at this declared interface.

The current abstract of Baumann, Stein and Linnhoff-Popien, 2610.26228, reports a fresh-qubit/entropy-removal ceiling for noisy reservoirs without feedback. Attempted primary full-text routes were unavailable. No theorem from that abstract is imported or claimed validated. Our model allows adaptive classical feedback and charges quantum service, but specifically assumes complete replacement erasures. This limited comparison does not establish historical priority.
