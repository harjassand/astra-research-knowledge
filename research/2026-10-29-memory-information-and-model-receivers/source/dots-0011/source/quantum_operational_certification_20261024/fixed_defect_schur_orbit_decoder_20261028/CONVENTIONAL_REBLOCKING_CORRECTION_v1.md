Therefore even insisting on the exponent z rather than z/2 does not rescue a distinct asymptotic capability.

## Correct interpretation of the surviving contribution

The orbit construction implements the complete low-defect reference error space, including previously missing multiplicities, with explicit polynomial coefficient access and no dense environmental channel reconstruction. That remains a concrete algorithmic bridge inside the universal Schur code, conditional on its received front-end/design assumptions. It does not follow that this compiler improves the best distillation protocol for an iid dilute source.

A fixed prescribed block can have total impurity c and pure-block approximation error approaching 1−e^(−c), while the orbit decoder pays a much smaller defect tail. That is a finite-block comparison of mechanisms, not sufficient proof of practical or asymptotic superiority: the conventional method may subdivide the same physical input and distill jointly afterward. An advantageous task would need a naturally justified input/interface where reblocking fails, or a measured and fairly charged finite-resource improvement; neither has been established here. We do not impose an artificial indivisibility restriction to manufacture an advantage.

The outer-code construction remains useful as explicit conventional machinery and an implementation check. Its existence does not restore novelty to this dilute iid capability. Claims about fixed positive impurity, adversarially correlated sources, and hardware-tolerant operation remain unresolved.
