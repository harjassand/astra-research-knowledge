# Optional powered purity certificate with an explicit acceptance gap

28 October2026. Independent protocol-design addendum, not a performed acquisition experiment. The preserved source's zero-event rule is valid but has poor acceptance near its boundary. This standard binomial-test alternative exposes a useful regime and its copy bill without eigenvector or environment tomography.

Let q0=delta(1−delta)>0 and0<beta<1. For independent trusted SWAP signs, choose a fixed n satisfying n q0>=32 log(1/beta), and accept when the antisymmetric count X satisfies

    X <= floor(3 n q0/4).

For any actual q>=q0, binomial monotonicity and the multiplicative lower-tail Chernoff inequality give false acceptance at most exp(−n q0/32)<=beta. For any actual q<=q0/2, the upper-tail inequality at factor3/2 gives rejection at most exp(−n q0/20)<=beta. The integer rounding is harmless: rejection X>floor(3nq0/4) implies X>3nq0/4. The guarantees leave an honest transition region q0/2<q<q0; no statistical test is promised high power exactly at the null boundary.

Thus near delta=c/k, a factor-two-separated accepted purity regime can be acquired with O(k log(1/beta)) independent pair tests and twice that many source copies. Constants, successful yield and downstream copies still matter. No claim is made that this is sample-optimal. For calibrated observation bias, the null margin must be reduced accordingly and the alternative separated in OBSERVED probability as well; the displayed formula assumes the trusted ideal SWAP record law.

The accompanying exact integer binomial checks verify concrete counts for beta=1/1000. They establish both false-acceptance and power bounds at the two endpoints without floating-tail evaluation. No random record bank was generated, no source was accepted, and no experimental purity or throughput is claimed.
