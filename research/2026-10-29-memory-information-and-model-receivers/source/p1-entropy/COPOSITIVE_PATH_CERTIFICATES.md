# Copositive certificates for hidden dissipation

## 1. Scope and objects

Q is a finite irreducible row CTMC generator, pi>0 its stationary law.
Every microscopic state is even under the stipulated physical time
reversal. Let sigma be its usual stationary Markov entropy rate, in
nats per time. The claim is about this model class; a different odd-state
reversal or a non-Markov microscopic model is not ruled out by it.

A symmetric matrix C is completely positive (CP) if C=B B^T for an
entrywise nonnegative rectangular B. A symmetric H is copositive if
x^T H x>=0 for every x>=0. These cones are dual. Put

    h_minus=max(0,-min_ij H_ij).

A negative pairing with any nonnegative C requires h_minus>0.
All witness bounds below may use h_minus; max|H_ij| is a valid weaker
scale. No general efficient copositivity oracle is assumed.

For stationary lag data let E_xa be deterministic visible labels or a
nonnegative emission kernel with sum_a E_xa=1. The uncentered two-time
joint matrix is

    C(t)=E^T diag(pi) exp(tQ) E.

For stochastic emissions the two sample draws are conditionally independent
given the hidden path; correlated sensor memory is not described by E.
Use t>0 operationally. At t=0 this formula for stochastic E describes
two replicated emissions, not a single variable paired with itself.
Use C_sym=(C+C^T)/2. Arbitrary unnormalized nonnegative features need an
extra scale bound; the probability-normalized statements do not apply
unchanged to them.

## 2. Equilibrium positive geometry and constructive converse

At detailed balance, H_Q=-diag(sqrt(pi)) Q diag(1/sqrt(pi)) is symmetric,
and exp(-t H_Q/2) is entrywise nonnegative. Therefore

    C(t)=[E^T diag(sqrt(pi)) exp(-t H_Q/2)]
         [E^T diag(sqrt(pi)) exp(-t H_Q/2)]^T

is CP for every t>=0. This is the familiar midpoint/Markov factorization,
with nonnegativity retained rather than weakened to PSD. Reversible
two-step factorization and the CP/PSD distinction are classical; see
Caputo, Chen, Gu and Polyanskiy (2024), Section 2.3.

There is also a constructive converse IN CLOSURE for fixed t>0. Given
a normalized CP matrix C=sum_k v_k v_k^T, with v_k>=0 and sum_ab C_ab=1,
let s_k=1^T v_k, w_k=s_k^2, r_k=v_k/s_k, and p=C1. Delete zero s_k.

On hidden states (k,a) with stationary mass mu_ka=w_k r_ka, refresh
the label within its mode at rate R, and refresh the whole state from mu
at rate epsilon>0. Delete zero-mass states. Both operations obey detailed
balance and commute. The observed lag matrix is exactly

    C_(R,epsilon)=exp(-epsilon t) [
        exp(-Rt) diag(p)+(1-exp(-Rt))C]
        +(1-exp(-epsilon t)) p p^T.                         (1)

The chain is irreducible on its positive support and departure rates
are <=R+epsilon. Entrywise total-variation distance obeys

    TV(C_(R,epsilon),C)<=exp(-Rt)+epsilon t.

Thus the closure of lag matrices from finite irreducible reversible
even-state CTMCs is exactly the normalized CP cone. A supplied
factorization makes this constructive; its acquisition, factorization
cost, state count and increasing R as accuracy improves are not free.

## 3. Geometric path comparison

For an arbitrary Q, define the subgenerator

    Q_geo,xy=sqrt(q_xy q*_xy), x!=y,
    Q_geo,xx=q_xx,
    q*_xy=pi_y q_yx/pi_x.

The original and reversed total escape rates agree. Cauchy-Schwarz
gives sum_y!=x Q_geo,xy<=-q_xx, so Q_geo is a reversible generator with
additional killing. Its symmetrization is a symmetric M-matrix.

For every microscopic path gamma over [0,t], the subprobability path
weight generated by Q_geo, starting in pi, is

    sqrt(P[gamma] P^rev[gamma]).

Holding factors coincide under reversal; jump products telescope the
stationary weights. Consequently C_geo(t)=E^T diag(pi)exp(tQ_geo)E
is CP and

    Delta=C_sym(t)-C_geo(t)>=0 entrywise,
    sum_ab Delta_ab=1-affinity(P,P^rev).                    (2)

The equality uses normalized emissions. Let D denote this total deficit.

Because reversal is an involution,

    KL(P||P^rev)=sigma t
       =1/2 integral (p-q)log(p/q).

The elementary inequality (a-b)log(a/b)>=4(sqrt(a)-sqrt(b))^2 implies

    sigma t>=4D.                                          (3)

The usual Renyi/Hellinger bound also gives
sigma t>=-2log(1-D). If sigma is infinite the statements are immediate.

## 4. A quantitative lag witness

Let H be copositive and gamma=-<H,C_sym(t)> >0. Since C_geo is CP,

    gamma<=-<H,Delta><=h_minus D.

Combining this with (3) gives

    sigma >= 4 gamma/(t h_minus),                          (4)

and the alternative lower bound
sigma>=-2 log(1-gamma/h_minus)/t when gamma<h_minus.

This uses only a bounded bilinear label score H_{Y0,Yt} under stationary
preparation and a witnessed copositive H. It has no hidden-state-count
or microscopic rate ceiling. The statement does not require observed
time-reversal asymmetry. Nor does symmetry alone certify zero entropy:
a symmetric nonnegative PSD matrix can be outside the CP cone.

General thermodynamic inference from time-symmetric data is already
established (Skinner-Dunkel 2021; Dechant et al. 2023). The candidate
addition here is this explicit CP witness/path-affinity inequality and
its realization-sensitive scope, not the broad phenomenon.

## 5. Native excursion version

Use unnormalized stationary weights w, total W, port set B and hidden I.
Define f_xy=w_xq_xy and E=W sigma. Let rho_i=w_i(-q_ii), R=sum_i rho_i.
A native port departure either jumps directly to another port (zero
interior duration) or makes a hidden excursion with duration tau>0 and
next port j. Let mu be its stationary flux measure over FULL microscopic
excursions, including direct jumps. Its total mass is R.

Under the exact earlier hypotheses,

    KL_flux(mu||mu^rev)=E.                                 (5)

This is not an equality for the observed coarse path law. It follows
microscopically by telescoping w factors in the path ratio and using
stationary jump fluxes for every edge. The two flux measures have the
same total mass and are related by an involution.

Let K_ij(t) dt be the hidden excursion flux density, EXCLUDING direct
port-jump atoms. For any measurable 0<=phi(t)<=1 supported at positive
interior duration, retain

    C_phi,ij=integral phi(t) K_ij(t) dt.

Geometric symmetrization gives K_geo(t)=B_t B_t^T with B_t>=0, and hence
C_geo,phi is CP. The hidden killed matrix is transient: a zero-killing
closed geometric component would also be closed in the irreducible
original chain, a contradiction. Equivalently use a positive resolvent
regularization and its monotone limit.

Entrywise,

    C_phi,sym-C_geo,phi>=0,
    D_phi=sum entries <=1/2 integral (sqrt(dmu)-sqrt(dmu^rev))^2.

Equation (5) and the same scalar inequality give E>=4D_phi. Therefore

    E >= 4[-<H,C_phi,sym>]_+/h_minus.                      (6)

Normalize by R. A reset first chooses i with probability rho_i/R,
then retains one native excursion. The bounded score

    Y=H_ij phi(tau) 1{tau>0}

has expectation <H,C_phi>/R. If -EY=gamma>0, (6) yields

    entropy per native port departure, E/R >=4gamma/h_minus. (7)

For a stationary excursion rate R/W, divide by the mean native cycle
duration mu_cycle=W/R to get sigma>=4gamma/(h_minus mu_cycle).
The cycle includes native port hold plus interior duration. Returning
to the same port is a valid positive-duration excursion.

No tail bound is needed for (7). Native reset access, flux-weighted
selection, resolved endpoints, calibrated duration-bin readout and
independence are substantive access assumptions. Direct-jump atoms must
not be included in phi as if they were hidden excursions.

## 6. A first-moment witness with no clock ceiling

The earlier acquired entrance/exit residence matrix is

    M=integral t K(t) dt,
    2c=integral tau^2 dmu.

The corresponding geometric M_geo is CP. Set
D_1=sum entries(M_sym-M_geo)
   =1/2 integral tau(sqrt(dmu)-sqrt(dmu^rev))^2.

Cauchy-Schwarz, using (sqrt(a)-sqrt(b))^2<=a+b, gives

    D_1^2
    <=(1/4)[integral tau^2(dmu+dmu^rev)]
             [integral (sqrt(dmu)-sqrt(dmu^rev))^2]
    <=c E/2.

For gamma_M=-<H,M_sym> >0 this yields

    E >=2 gamma_M^2/(c h_minus^2).                         (8)

Thus a sound paired first-moment record plus c can bound hidden entropy
without a microscopic rate ceiling. It need not reveal directional
asymmetry in M itself. A finite-window score can be statistically much
cheaper because its range is bounded and it avoids tail acquisition.

## 7. Explicit symmetric positive-definite witness

Let C5 denote the adjacency matrix of the five-cycle and put

    M=I+(17/30)C5,
    H=11^T-2C5.

H is copositive: for x>=0, sum_edges x_i x_j<=(sum x)^2/4.
An elementary proof maximizes the edge sum on the simplex and merges
nonadjacent positive coordinates until a clique remains; a five-cycle

## 8. Strong alternatives and scope of the proposed addition

Hidden-model entropy minimization over waiting-time/transition statistics
is established and may produce a stronger bound. At the supplied tight
clock record, the ordinary exact star optimization gives the sharper
attained value. The proposed witness needs neither that clock equality
nor a chosen hidden dimension; it is a short analytic certificate usable
with a bounded native record. Existing methods can incorporate it.

The complete-positive cone, copositive duality, Horn witness, midpoint
factorization and Hellinger inequalities are established ingredients.
Their combination does not make cone membership polynomial, identify a
physical mechanism, or remove the need to warrant the even-state Markov
class. External priority remains unsettled.
