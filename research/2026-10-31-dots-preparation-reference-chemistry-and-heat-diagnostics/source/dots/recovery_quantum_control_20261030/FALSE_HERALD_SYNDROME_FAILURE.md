# A local false herald can leave an extensively wrong output sector
2026-10-30. Preserves root's decisive noisy-herald counterexample. Reviewed clean twirl/local-leg lemmas remain true but do not imply output warmth.

Suppose the full physical Z twirl is followed by ideal X-check extraction, and a single subsequent pointer flip changes a true singleton syndrome e_c to the accepted all-zero record. The actual accepted state lies in P_(e_c), not P_0. For the all-logical-zero input/module, its representative is Z^z|0bar>, where H_X z=e_c. All such representatives have weight at least fill_HX(e_c), even after quotienting by Z stabilizers and logical Z operators that fix this known state.

This can be macroscopic for a good code. The hard-singleton proof in review_factory/CQ_ATG_ANALYSIS.md shows: if H_X has full row rank, max column weight D, and nontrivial opposite logical distance d, at least one singleton has fill_HX(e_c)>=(d-1)/D. Consequently one localized faulty acceptance bit can admit a state with Omega(d) minimum Z-error weight.

The clean twirl gives all syndrome projectors the same probability, and the localized endpoint operator lemma controls their norms. Neither fact makes the accepted nonzero syndrome a low-weight DATA error. A shifted projector is not interchangeable with P_0 for the receiving code's warm-basin support condition. This is an output-support failure, not merely an inverse-success normalization penalty.

Repeated/redundant actual verification, a proved syndrome-filling property, or an actual fixed-wrong-baseline receiver could repair the interface. No such repair is implied by the single-readout twirl scheme. In particular all-plus acceptance may not be treated as a trustworthy physical predicate.

The ATG fixed-baseline alternative preserves sparse wrong baseline signs instead of forcing their arbitrary syndrome into zero via a dense correction. Its receiver theorem and protected capture still need actual proofs.
