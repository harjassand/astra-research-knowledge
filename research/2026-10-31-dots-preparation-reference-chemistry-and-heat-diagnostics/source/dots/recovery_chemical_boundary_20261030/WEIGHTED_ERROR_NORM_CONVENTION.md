# The large-B error bound is a tilted kernel norm, not a probability

30 October 2026. Clarification of the actual phase processor.

For arbitrary unbounded initial B, a long sequence of clearings can offer many chances for a rare local activation. Its UNWEIGHTED probability of seeing at least one such event need not be uniformly small. No such assertion is needed or intended in Section 3 of the full candidate.

Let M0=exp(theta r+lambda B). The small designated-exit bound is

`E[M0(X_stop); designated exit] <= epsilon(r0) M0(X_in)`.

The exp(-lambda q) or exp(-lambda) factors on ordinary continuing blocks make the sum of opportunities finite in this norm, uniformly in initial B. A local error kernel with norm o(1), composed with that earned phase resolvent, remains o(1). This is why the seed's unbounded B was not discarded.

To upgrade the error estimate to inverse profile f_s, use Cauchy–Schwarz with respect to the POSITIVE TILTED exit measure:

`E[M0 f_s; error] <= (E[M0; error])^(1/2) (E[M0 f_(2s); error])^(1/2)`.

The second factor is bounded by the stopped processor's transported f_(2s) norm. Dividing by M0(input) yields epsilon^(1/2) times f_s(input). The tilt need not be a probability measure; ordinary Cauchy–Schwarz applies to any finite positive measure. One must not substitute an unsupported unweighted error probability into this step.

The same finite exponent range can be selected in advance, with lambda chosen to contract all required ordinary profile kernels and r0 chosen afterward to absorb their error/Green constants. This avoids any circular choice of lambda depending on an unproved repair norm.
