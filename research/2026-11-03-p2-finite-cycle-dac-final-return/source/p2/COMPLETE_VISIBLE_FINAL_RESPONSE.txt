Strongest final result

I developed an explicit, finite-cycle architecture for electrochemical direct air capture, with a thermodynamic model that closes carbon, ions, water, charge, and reset losses—and identified the physical operation that prevents the present construction from establishing the full capability.

The completed result is mathematical and computational. It does not establish a working system that captures atmospheric CO₂ at ≤60 kJ/mol and the specified sustained throughput. No hardware was fabricated. The proposed electrode still requires unmeasured performance, and the attempted compact liquid-distribution geometry failed its final clearance audit.

1. The consequential opening

I selected the capability to extract CO₂ from ordinary air with low complete-operation energy and useful sustained electrochemical throughput.

The target was:

Requirement	Defined boundary
Feed	420 ppm wet CO₂, 25°C, 1 bar, 50% relative humidity
Removal	50% of incoming CO₂
Product	At least 99% CO₂ on a dry basis, 1 bar, ≤100 ppm water
Energy	≤60 kJ/mol delivered CO₂, including utilities, conversion, drying, and reset
Throughput	10 mA of carbon-equivalent current per cm², averaged over the complete cycle and all installed separator area

The central limitation is that improving one part of capture often makes another part worse. More air contact improves absorption but increases liquid circulation and oxygen exposure. Fine electrochemical staging reduces irreversible work but introduces more passages, electrical leakage paths, and retained liquid. Periodic electrode reversal then mixes liquids with different compositions unless the transition is physically recovered.

The strongest architectural leverage was to separate the number of air contacts from the number of electrical sections, and to recover the composition transition during electrode reversal:

Eleven coarse air contacts serve 176 fine electrical sections per module.
Capture and release liquids flow countercurrently, paired at matching alkalinity-flux coordinates.
Oxygen removal occurs between air exposure and the sensitive electrode.
A finite, graded-liquid regenerator handles retained liquid during role reversal.
Six electrically floating hydraulic modules are connected in series and phase-staggered through their real reset intervals.

This opening was formulated before consulting Astra. The subsequent Astra retrieval supplied relevant reversible-operation, actual-duty, and complete-process accounting ingredients; its exact source paths and pinned commit are preserved in the archive.

Earlier estimates for electrode-hole pumping, ionic connectors, measurement power, and hydrogen-generator standby add approximately 1.07945 kJ/mol, producing an illustrative incomplete account of 59.87306 kJ/mol.

That number has no earned margin for the missing physical interface.

The unresolved additions are coupled to the chemistry. A larger manifold changes retained liquid; that changes reset exchange and downtime; maintaining throughput then changes current. Additional pressure changes water transport and its reciprocal electrical work. Simply appending a pump-energy estimate would not close the operation.

5. The decisive failure: the liquid routing does not yet exist

The final geometric audit checked distances between finite three-dimensional channel segments and subtracted their radii. The attempted feed and return networks gave:

Backmanifold liquid inventory per side	Minimum surface clearance	Audit result
0.5 L/m²	+17.27 µm	Below the required 30 µm solid wall
0.8 L/m²	−29.10 µm	Channels intersect
1.2 L/m²	−87.89 µm	Channels intersect
2.0 L/m²	−117.46 µm	Channels intersect

Terminal combs also cross opposite-network fingers unless additional routing layers or vias are supplied.

None of these cases establishes a complete physical backmanifold. Their attractive hydraulic estimates are retained as conditional network calculations whose physical interpretation was rejected.

This is the hardest unresolved operation exposed by the work: supplying many small electrode passages with sufficiently little pressure loss and retained liquid, while preserving electrical isolation and a recoverable reset transition.

The nonideal thermodynamic implementation was checked against primary carbonate-solution measurements. Its RMS potential-difference errors were 1.362 mV at 5°C and 0.682 mV at 25°C. Independent solubility comparison differed by about 2% near the chosen temperature. Those calibration results and their source conventions are preserved.

I also reran the public Hamedi et al. computational notebook for 34 cases, preserving its scientific code while redirecting I/O. The maximum specific-energy difference was 0.5665%. The original input spreadsheet was not recovered; inputs were reconstructed from the published outputs and labeled accordingly. This provides reproducibility evidence for a computational reference, not experimental validation of the new architecture.

A strong recent physical comparator is Buchen et al.’s nickel-hydroxide system: a 9 × 300 cm² pilot stack reported 132 kJ/mol and 0.19 mol/m²/h over 48 hours, while a separate smaller device demonstrated 5,000 hours. Those boundaries must remain distinct.

The present throughput target corresponds to 3.731 mol/m²/h, about 19.6 times that pilot membrane-area flux. This is a target comparison, not demonstrated superiority. The proposed architecture also demands substantial passive resources. At one tonne/day, its calculations require approximately:

254 m² of main separator area;
326,000 m² of passive air-contact surface;
36 m³ of reset and conditioning storage;
9.18 m³/day of prepared water.

Those requirements prevent any inference that high active-area throughput automatically produces a compact or inexpensive plant.

What this work establishes

The completed contribution is a reproducible finite-operation theory and construction that connects staging, nonideal chemistry, water transport, retained-fluid memory, electrical control, drying, and utilities in one auditable account. It supplies a precise resistance–energy relation, a finite periodic liquid-regeneration construction, and a concrete falsification of the attempted physical routing.

The complete civilisation-scale capability remains unresolved. Its decisive missing requirements are jointly attainable cold-electrode performance and a complete low-inventory feed/return network, followed by their coupled pressure, reset, and delivered-throughput calculation. The report and archive preserve both the useful constructions and the evidence that prevents promoting them into a physical breakthrough claim.
