Astra Chemistry | Rooted product, local reanchoring, and conditional vacancy-frontier NCL
2026-10-27 | Original proposed integration; mathematical components checked as indicated.
No integrated peptide assembler has been demonstrated. Illustrative rates are not measured.

SCIENCE QUESTION
Can differently programmed rooted peptides undergo repeated NCL in a common reagent
bath and retry a missed coupling while an already-extended peptide progresses to its
own next instruction, without a DNA motor or perfect chemical bond detector?

STRONGEST PHYSICAL ANCESTRY
1. Niu, Hili, Liu, Nature Chemistry 5, 282-292 (2013), DOI 10.1038/nchem.1577:
   DNA-templated PNA-adapter oligomerization yields non-nucleic-acid polymers,
   as large as 26 kDa / 16 coupled blocks. It proves important prepositioned
   adapter chemistry, not asynchronous per-parent NCL retry.
2. Spaltenstein et al., Angew Chem Int Ed 64 e202413644 (2025),
   DOI 10.1002/anie.202413644: CAPTN controls thioester activation, demonstrates
   three-segment templated NCL, E. coli S17 (90 aa), and traceless linker removal.
   With simultaneous activation the three-segment model gives 70% desired and
   18% peptide 2 cyclization (RP-HPLC); selective activation gives 87% desired
   and 6.6% cyclization. Neither experiment is an autonomous retry controller.
3. Joshi, Romanens, Winssinger, JACS 2025 DOI 10.1021/jacs.5c00708:
   reversible terminator PNA primer extension, 256 four-base sequence options;
   chemistry is PNA backbone, not NCL peptide modules.
4. Mitra et al. JACS 2026 DOI 10.1021/jacs.6c03709:
   DNA assembly creates an internal toehold and a fuel drives template product
   dissociation; ~35-40 turnovers per template shown for a DNA dimerization.
   It does not prove amide-specific DNA fuel actuation.
5. Mechanism of NCL temporary S-acyl vs amide:
   DOI 10.1021/jacsau.4c00705. Transient S-acyl is covalently root connected;
   root connectivity alone cannot distinguish it from a stable amide.
6. Existing sequential externally scheduled DNA-toehold synthesis:
   He and Liu, JACS 2011 DOI 10.1021/ja201361t. It does not use per-bond
   product-qualified advancement; scheduled strand displacements are prior art.

PROPOSED PHYSICAL ARCHITECTURE (NOT DEMONSTRATED)
- Each C-terminal peptide root is covalently retained on its own encoding DNA
  template or shared solid support. The root presents one free N-terminal Cys.
- Donor j is a peptide module with protected N-terminal Cys, a C-terminal
  thioester and a positional PNA recognition tag covalently attached to the
  donor's peptide cargo via a cleavable sidechain linker. Correct NCL yields an
  internal amide, protected new N terminus, and a new rooted tag j.
- Donor complexes hybridize near the *first chemically available codon after
  the last rooted peptide tag*. Previous rooted tags can preferentially occupy
  the old codons, reducing their availability to competing unrooted donors.
  This alone does NOT ensure only the next codon is accessible: all downstream
  empty codons may bind free donors. Their wrong-amide kinetic rates must be
  bounded by geometry, blockers, or orthogonal address recognition.
- Global reagent pulses supply donor species; newly incorporated modules cannot
  incorporate again until global terminal deprotection.
- Crucial cleanup: resolve reversible S-acyl intermediates into permanent amide
  or truly detached competent parent by exchange, then denature/physically wash
  unrooted donors. This wash can deliberately dissociate PNA-DNA hybrids because
  the rooted peptide retains any covalently incorporated PNA tags even when
  undocked. Rehybridize product tags to their intended sites, then globally
  deprotect new N-terminal cysteines after all reactive donors have been cleared.
- A missed donor is not root-connected after cleanup, so its tag is washed away
  and the original first vacancy remains; a correctly ligated donor is retained
  in the product and changes the last-tag position. Two lineages can therefore
  progress independently under the same washes/pulses.
- Optional blockers at future sites and local tag-borne fuel can enforce a
  single exposed vacancy, but are NOT baseline: they require per-step reliable
  reblocking, no tag-induced premature blocker removal, and no activation by
  unresolved S-acyl intermediates. Their physical implementation is missing.

TOPOLOGICAL INVARIANT AND PROOF
Assume (i) rooted product remains linked to support throughout chemistry and
wash, (ii) only a correct amide can add a new rooted donor tag that survives
S-acyl challenge, (iii) all unrooted donor tags are removed before next fuel,
(iv) incorporated tags re-register at their own codons, (v) old-site binding
and downstream misbinding do not cause stable incorrect chemistry, and
(vi) reactive successor protection prevents two additions per macrocycle.
Let X_j be the rooted product carrying exactly a correctly ligated prefix of j
modules with tags 1..j. An unsuccessful reversible reaction restores X_j.
A correct ligation produces X_{j+1}. Thus, under these stated assumptions,
X_j -> X_j or X_{j+1} per cycle, and two X_j with different j can proceed
independently. Exhaustive finite-history tests in
astra_chemistry_vacancy_frontier.py check the state-machine invariant. The
assumptions are precisely the critical *physical* missing operations; abstract
prefix induction does not establish them. Wrong stable amides may add tag j and
falsely update the prefix. Covalent thioester intermediates survive denaturing
if chemical cleanup has not resolved them.

RESULT I: GEOMETRY REQUIRES LOCAL PRODUCT REANCHORING
Under an ideal-Gaussian independent two-rail model, for rooted peptide tip X_j
and target DNA site Y_j, X_j-Y_j~N(0,(v_p+v_t)j I3) for two flexible rails.
The contact density at zero separation is
  ((2 pi (v_p+v_t) j))^(-3/2).
If DNA stays extended at Y_j=j*d*e_z, contact density is
  ((2 pi v_p j))^(-3/2) exp[-j*d^2/(2v_p)].
For a small reaction capture volume the effective encounter rate is proportional
 to this density (if chemical conversion after encounter is not rate limiting).
An otherwise identical local tip tether at every successful step can reset the
relative-distance distribution to a single-module span and conditionally avoid
this length-dependent geometric decay.
At a_j=a_1 j^(-3/2), global irreversible aging d, and only competing
correct-versus-damage clocks, exact entire-chain success is
  F_L=prod_{j=1}^L (1+(d/a_1)j^(3/2))^(-1).
If renewed local tip anchoring gives constant a_j=a_1, then
  F_L^local=(1+d/a_1)^(-L).
At L=1024 and purely illustrative d/a_1=1e-6 these are 1.67014e-6 and
0.998977 respectively. No physical claim is made that either rate law applies
to a particular templated peptide. Crucially, Niu-style prepositioned PNA
adapters and CAPTN-style tethering already localize partners; do not apply the
unanchored penalty to those established alternatives.

RESULT II: THE MOST RECENT ATTACHED TAG NEED NOT BE PERMANENTLY BOUND
The latest rooted PNA tag may dock (B) and undock (U) repeatedly.
B->correct amide hazard a; B->U hazard u; B->irreversible wrong/damage
hazard w_B+d. U->B hazard v; U->irreversible wrong/damage hazard w_U+d.
Memoryless exact first-passage probability (starting B):
  P_B=a/[a+w_B+d+u*(w_U+d)/(v+w_U+d)].
Hence reversible undocking is NOT automatically a fatal failure. The effective
penalty is u*(w_U+d)/(v+w_U+d), which may be small when re-docking rate v is high.
With no incorrect or damage hazards and v>0, eventual reaction is certain and
mean waiting time is (1+u/v)/a. For 1024 hypothetical identical stages,
a=.05,u=.01,v=1,w_U=1e-4,w_B=0,d=1e-7 (s^-1) gives F=0.977706;
if v=.01 s^-1, F=0.131366. With w_B=1e-6 at v=1, F=0.957887.
This model does not establish PNA correct re-registration or physically measured
rate coefficients. Verified with 600 independently inverted Markov generators,
55,000 independent event-driven trajectories.

RESULT III: SIMULTANEOUS GLOBAL TAG REMOVAL HAS A BETTER-THAN-NAIVE SCALING
If L attached tags can each be cleaved independently at rate u with no wrong
cleavage, and whole-product irreversible aging has rate d independent of L,
then a fixed shared cleanup interval t yields exactly
  G_L(t)=exp(-d t) (1-exp(-u t))^L.
The optimum is t*=ln(1+L*u/d)/u for d>0. Its success is
  G_L*= (L*u/(L*u+d))^L (d/(L*u+d))^(d/u).
For fixed epsilon=d/u>0, G_L*~exp(-epsilon)*(epsilon/L)^epsilon;
this is POLYNOMIAL in L, not exponential, and t*=O(log L)/u.
At d/u=.001: L=64 gives G*=0.988006 (t*u=11.0667), L=1024 gives
G*=0.985270 (t*u=13.8392). These are not measured linker cleavage yields.
If each tag instead has competing permanent wrong cleavage hazard h, then
  G_L(t)=exp(-d t)[u/(u+h)*(1-exp(-(u+h)t))]^L,
  t*=ln(1+L*(u+h)/d)/(u+h),
  G_L*=(u/(u+h))^L times the analogous whole-product factor.
With L=1024,d/u=.001,h/u=5e-5, G* drops to 0.936096.
Therefore simultaneous cleavage helps only with *whole-product timing damage*;
per-tag covalent injury remains exponential. This is also contingent on every
tag's accessibility and the cleavage chemistry being compatible with the peptide.

RESULT IV: FUTURE VACANCY LEAK REINTRODUCES QUADRATIC INTERFERENCE
Passivating old addresses is not sufficient. A future uncommitted site r steps
past the current frontier may recruit an unintended donor. If its integrated
wrong-hazard-to-correct-hazard ratio is epsilon*r^(-alpha), the exposure sum
across an L-step programme is
  E_L=epsilon sum_{r=1}^{L-1}(L-r)r^(-alpha).
Scaling: alpha=0 -> Theta(L^2), alpha=1 -> Theta(L log L), alpha>1 ->
Theta(L). Independent competing first-event clocks give an exact success
F_L=prod_{j=0}^{L-1}[1+epsilon sum_{r=1}^{L-j-1}r^(-alpha)]^(-1).
For L=1024,epsilon=1e-5: alpha=0 gives F=0.00540743; alpha=1.5 gives
F=0.974837. To achieve 95% from this *one* failure mechanism alone, alpha=0
requires epsilon <=9.79331e-8, while alpha=1.5 allows <=2.01271e-5.
The intended frontier's actual spatial kernel must be measured; ideal flexible
polymer exponent 3/2 is a model assumption, not measured selective peptide NCL.
A DNA latch does not solve this without a measured leak/target-discrimination
ratio; it merely changes where a quadratic burden can arise.

RESULT V: FULL HISTORICAL REBINDING IS A QUADRATIC OBSTRUCTION
If a refresh independently succeeds in docking each of j retained product
adapters with probability theta, requiring ALL j historical adapters docked
on every subsequent cycle gives the exact joint probability
  P_strict(L)=theta^[L(L+1)/2].
At theta=.9999 and L=1024 this is 1.61096e-23; 95% requires
  theta >= exp(log(.95)/(L(L+1)/2)) = 0.99999990226.
It is therefore not physically acceptable to assume complete re-registration
of every historic adapter at each stage. The more defensible architecture
requires reversible RECAPTURE of the latest local anchor (Result II) and
spatial suppression of interference from older imperfectly bound tags
(Result IV). This is an exact counterexample to a naive "every rung must be
occupied" gate, under its named independence assumptions. Actual tags may
have correlated avidity, so the binomial numerical values are not measured.

DESIGN COMPARISON
A. Original chemically protected, independently retrying PNA primer extension
   (Joshi): strongest direct repeated-cycle demonstration but PNA-product scope.
B. Product-retained tagged peptide NCL + root-anchored wash + rehybridization:
   potentially peptide modules at own pace; difficult coherent integration of
   independent mechanisms, permanent wrong site amides, address register, and
   compatible chemistry unmeasured.
C. CAPTN and automated SPPS: much better established peptide synthesis, can
   coordinate reaction order with external operations; no demonstrated fully
   asynchronous same-bath programme progress; their demonstrated yield/cost
   must remain primary practical benchmark.
D. Future-address blocker latches: could suppress dangerous future-site access,
   but initial linker and blocker cost O(L), repeated replacement of freely
   diffusible blockers after whole-template denaturing could cost O(L^2).
   Tethered self-resetting hairpins would require new physical proof.

MOST DISCRIMINATING TEST (not performed)
Two covalently anchored roots with distinct DNA programmes. Use one first-pulse
success and one deliberately missed incorporation, then expose the same full
donor panel to both in the next pulse. Directly measure correct amide junctions,
unconverted N-terminal cysteine, transient thioesters, donor hydrolysis, free
and root-retained tag distributions, rehybridization fidelity to old/current
addresses, and wrong stable off-site reaction. Compare with a no-handle
unanchored control and an externally sequenced CAPTN-compatible control.
Critical before any scaling: demonstrate full denaturing washes can eliminate
unrooted donors while retaining correctly ligated rooted cargo, rehybridize the
last retained tag accurately, and preserve terminal protection. No physical
experiment was done here.
