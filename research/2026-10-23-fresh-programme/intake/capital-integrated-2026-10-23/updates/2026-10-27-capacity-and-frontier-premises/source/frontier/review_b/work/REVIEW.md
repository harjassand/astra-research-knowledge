## L42: accepted global objects and obstruction, with the asymptotic bound still missing

The Cartan space is a constructed representation, not a definition requiring unknown Werner positivity. Its coherent vectors correspond to every complex two-plane; their one-replica marginals are \(P/2\). The canonical highest-weight inclusion into degrees \(2\) and \(k-2\) is the inclusion required by [Christandl–König–Mitchison–Renner, Theorem II.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0602130v4). The paper uses half the full trace norm. Consequently the endpoint's full-norm error \(4(1-d_D(k-2)/d_D(k))\), rather than half that amount, is correct. I reconstructed the dimension ratio and its four-factor estimate, obtaining

\[
0\le g_n/4-\alpha_{n,k}\le16D(3/2)^n/k,\qquad D=3^n.
\]

Thus \(k=16\,9^n\) gives error \(2^{-n}\). The mixed-state extension is valid by convexity. The degree marginals retain the requisite Cartan support, so monotonicity is valid. The cost remains doubly exponential matrix dimension. N525's already-proved arbitrary-witness projection lift and both-parity persistent-gap argument then justify the stated equivalence with \(\alpha_{n,k(n)}\to0\). **The needed lower floor \(\alpha\ge-o(1)\) is not proved**; the approximation theorem compares against the still unknown minimum and cannot supply that floor.

The all-degree obstruction survives scrutiny. On three binary sites, \(\Theta^2=-I\), and the projector onto \(\operatorname{span}(u,\Theta u)\) is fixed by the linear matrix spin flip. The all-traceless projection changes sign, hence its value is zero. This zero mechanism is already in the supplied CT source. What L42 adds is the valid spanning argument: in a Grassmann chart the zero planes are \((x,M\bar x)\). Substituting \(x=a+ib\) gives \((a+ib,M(a-ib))\), an invertible complex-linear substitution of \((a,b)\). Therefore any holomorphic polynomial vanishing there vanishes identically. This proves Zariski density and the spanning of every finite Cartan power; it does not infer density from sampled zeros. A PSD compression would annihilate that spanning family, contradicting the positive symbol \(q_3(P_{000,011})/4=1/8\). Hence **\(\alpha_{n,k}<0\) for every finite \(k\ge2\) and \(n\ge3\)**, exactly as claimed. Embedding and pure-site padding preserve this conclusion.

The finite witnesses also replay exactly. In an orthonormal physical binary basis, the displayed Cartan vector has norm square \(432\) and numerator \(-162\), giving \(-3/8\); its column symmetries and zero total antisymmetrizer hold. The PSD four-replica mixture has all 24 required permutation moments and objective \(-13/32\). The physical invariant sectors have the stated eigenvalues and characters. It therefore excludes all zero-expectation corrections from the whole-permutation algebra, including noncentral ones. These are relaxed states, not negative Werner witnesses. N525 already had negative shadow-LP outer points, but not this exact PSD all-\(S_4\)-moment construction or the Cartan obstruction.

The weaker replica hierarchy is also valid: Theorem II.7 of the same primary paper gives full-norm error \(\min(2,12D^2/m)\); the spectral rounding lemma and moment constraints give the advertised convergence. It is standard de Finetti machinery applied with an actual stable flat-spectrum constraint, not newly invented de Finetti theory.

## L67: accepted sharp certificate thresholds and resource-dependent activation

The factorization \(C=UV^*\) retains all complex rank-at-most-two matrices. For complex variables, phase-averaging any real-polynomial SOS leaves squares of complex bilinear and sesquilinear forms. The Hermitian coefficient operator is uniquely determined by its values on complex product vectors, giving precisely the decomposable cone. For real variables, coefficient identification gives the partial-transpose symmetrization of a PSD Gram matrix. These identifications are necessary for interpreting the separators; they hold.

I independently reconstructed both normalized separators, their positive/PPT sector decompositions, and both attaining parameterized identities. They prove the full table for \(0\le t\le1\): actual positivity noise \(\max(0,t-1/2)\), real SOS noise \(\max(0,(3t-1)/4)\), and complex SOS noise \(\max(0,2(3t-1)/7)\). The positivity lower bound uses the nuclear-norm factorization inequality with the correct reversed sign when \(t\ge1/2\); a two-frame attains it. Nonnegative sector coefficients prove the SOS upper bounds on \(1/3\le t\le1\), while \(W(t)\succeq0\) covers the remainder. The trace-one dual states also certify the claimed operator-norm distances, so \(1/7\) and \(1/8\) are sharp at one copy, not numerical SDP guesses.

Product spectators preserve the negative pairings and factor norms at all copy counts. This is a correct uniform cone obstruction, also obtainable simply by restricting the larger free-variable polynomial to a padded one-copy instance. It is **not** an obstruction to all-copy positivity or constrained SOS. The endpoint's Stiefel-constrained Lagrange identity is correct and usefully prevents that overreach.

The qualitative one-copy non-SOS principle is already explicit in [Hiroshima (2010), Section III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.1830v1). Relative credit belongs to the explicit sharp quantitative thresholds, separators and attaining identities, not discovery of that principle. Historical priority for those exact formulas was not established by this bounded review.

The Bell contraction is correct: with the additional PPT state \(\alpha=\sigma_{\mathbb C}^{\Gamma}\), it produces \((8I-5F_2)/1890\), success probability \(11/945\), and normalized partial-transpose minimum eigenvalue \(-1/11\). The parameterized output is also correct. This is an explicit instance of established PPT-assisted activation, whose same local-Bell-filter architecture appears in [Vollbrecht–Wolf (2002), Section II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0201103). The activator is an added entangled resource; no production from Werner copies is supplied. The optional exclusion of producing it from one copy uses the current two-copy theorem at its actual boundary; [Fu–Gao–Park v3, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21367v3) has the required parameter range. I checked that statement and parameter translation, not its entire proof anew.
