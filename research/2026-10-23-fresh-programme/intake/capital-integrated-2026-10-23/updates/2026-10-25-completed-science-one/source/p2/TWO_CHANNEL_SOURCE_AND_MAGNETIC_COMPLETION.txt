TWO-CHANNEL SOURCE AUDIT AND EXACT CIRCULAR MAGNETIC COMPLETION
============================================================

This continues GAPLESS_DISPERSIVE_PHASE.txt.  The scalar evanescent-wave
intensity construction in EVANESCENT_TRANSFER_FILTER_INTERFACE.txt does NOT
implement the two-channel Hamiltonian used by that theorem.  The following
separates an analytic coherent-source construction, its failures, and a
stronger full-hyperfine magnetic completion.  No integrated device is claimed.

1. THE ACTUAL OPERATOR TO BE SUPPLIED

The target is H_M in both long-lived internal channels, relative offset
-Lambda in the auxiliary channel, and off-diagonal gG, where

 F=exp(-a q), G=F-cF^2, c=hbar*omega_M/(Lambda+hbar*omega_M).

An intensity modulation u(t)G(q) acting on one scalar motional potential is
not this operation.  Internal coherence, shared native potentials, source
waveforms and every additional scattering/field channel must be supplied.

2. NECESSARY MAXWELL/RAMAN COMPONENT CONSTRUCTION

At a planar dielectric interface an ideal evanescent field has amplitude
exp(-kappa q), with kappa=(omega_opt/c0)*sqrt(n^2 sin^2(theta)-1).
Two Raman pairs can therefore have leg amplitude decays a/2,a/2 and a,a.
Their effective products are F and F^2.  A common two-photon beat frequency
omega_ba+Lambda/hbar and relative Raman phase pi give gF-cgF^2, provided
the microscopic transition matrix elements actually permit these paths.

In an isolated Lambda approximation a pair j with leg amplitudes Omega_aj,
Omega_bj, common detuning Delta_j and spatial factor f_j=F^(r_j/2), r=1,2,
has the effective Hamiltonian

 -hbar/(4 Delta_j) * f_j^2 *
   [[|Omega_aj|^2, Omega_aj^* Omega_bj],
    [Omega_bj^* Omega_aj, |Omega_bj|^2]].

Equal dipole-weighted leg strengths make the diagonal Stark shifts common.
For positive detunings and coupling magnitudes g,cg the common shift is
-gF-cgF^2.  Native trapping powers can compensate it: with a red contribution
-A_r F and a blue contribution A_b F^2, use A_r=2D-g, A_b=D+cg so that the
total remains D F^2-2D F.  The constants in the potential are immaterial.
This is a constructive component calculation; actual multilevel atoms need
their full polarizability and Raman tensors.

There are two important extra interfaces:

(i) Different optical carrier frequencies generally generate cross-pair
Raman products F^(3/2), at transfer energies Lambda +/- hbar delta_carrier.
A positive cross-pair transfer can hit the unbounded motional continuum.
The condition delta_carrier >> g/hbar is not a global averaging proof.
One needs selection rules that really eliminate the unwanted products, or
their actual continuum form factors and finite-pulse tail bounds.  The
same issue affects scalar beat notes between pairs.  Orthogonal labels for
nominal Lambda paths do not suffice if each beam also drives the other
ground state.

(ii) Equal normal decay constants for two unequal optical frequencies
imply slightly unequal in-plane propagation constants.  Both pairs can be
oriented to have the same in-plane difference vector K, because each pair
allows |beta_1-beta_2|<=|K|<=beta_1+beta_2.  The off-diagonal is then
exp(i K.r_parallel)G(q).  Gauging out this phase changes the auxiliary
transverse kinetic energy by recoil/Doppler terms.  It is not the exact
one-dimensional theorem unless these terms are retained or bounded.

3. RAMAN SOURCE COST AND EMISSION: THE BALANCED FAILURE

For balanced isolated-Lambda legs, g_j=hbar Omega_j^2/(4|Delta_j|).
An input in channel a has remote excited-state occupation approximately
Omega_j^2 f_j^2/(4 Delta_j^2), hence scattering rate

 Gamma_j |g_j|/(hbar |Delta_j|) <f_j^2>.

The desired fixed dispersive phase takes T=hbar theta/(chi g^2).
Consequently the balanced scheme has integrated remote-source emission
proportional to 1/|g| at fixed optical detunings.  It does not inherit the
closed-Hamiltonian asymptotic error suppression merely by weakening g.
Its incident intensity scales as |g|, so incident energy likewise scales
as 1/|g|.  Photon number is not automatically logical error: actual recoil,
spin flips and differences of Rayleigh amplitudes determine the channel.

The equally equipped Raman comparator can do better.  Hold each Stokes leg
Omega_bj fixed and take Omega_aj=4 Delta_j c_j g/(hbar Omega_bj), where
g_j=c_j g.  With the ideal dressed state (n,g u1), the amplitude entering a
resolved spontaneous channel is, to first order,

 sqrt(Gamma_lj) g f_j(q) *
 [2 c_j n/(hbar Omega_bj) + Omega_bj u1/(2 Delta_j)],

with the actual branching/polarization matrix inserted for each channel.
The two terms must interfere before squaring when they feed the same emitted
photon mode.  Different resolved photon modes have separate jump operators.
This gives O(1), rather than 1/g, integrated emission as g->0, while the
held Stokes beam's incident energy grows as g^-2.

The held leg also makes an O(1) spatial differential Stark shift.  It must
be compensated to retain a common H_M.  One ideal-Lambda construction uses
an additional path at opposite single-photon detuning, equal leg powers,
and opposite relative Raman phase: diagonal shifts cancel, Raman terms add.
This uses extra fields and introduces cross-frequency terms and additional
spontaneous channels.  A real multilevel version must be solved explicitly.
Thus the 1/g emission divergence is a failure of the balanced realization,
not a universal impossibility theorem for Raman control.

4. A PRIMARY Yb CHECK THAT PREVENTS A FALSE NO-GO

Jenkins et al, Phys. Rev. X 12,021027 (2022), demonstrated nuclear-spin Raman
control and motional cooling in 171Yb tweezers.  Their full Appendix D treats
Raman and Rayleigh amplitudes separately, including interference through
both excited hyperfine manifolds.  It also reports a driven differential
light shift despite the small native differential trap shift.

Full published paper:
 https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=933949
Corrected arXiv record (v3 corrects polarization discussion):
 https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06732

The measured implementation uses an optical detuning around -180 MHz from
the selected 3P1 hyperfine manifold.  Appendix D gives, for optimized beam
polarization, spin decoherence/Rabi ratio proportional to

 Gamma_0/sqrt(6) * |Delta_hf/[Delta_0(Delta_0-Delta_hf)]|.

It can decrease at large detuning because amplitudes interfere.  A toy
sum of two positive total excited-state scattering rates divided by their
opposite-sign Raman amplitudes is not a universal logical-error floor.
Common Rayleigh photons still give recoil in the motional task here, and
the required incident intensity increases when the spin-flip matrix element
is suppressed.  No free large-detuning limit is inferred.

J=0,F=1/2 nuclear-spin states are attractive for approximately common scalar
optical potentials.  But actual ground-leg selectivity, bias-field-dependent
polarizability and the full set of excited states remain required.  The
existing tweezer demonstration does not integrate the two evanescent profiles
or the Morse continuum operation.

5. EXACT ROTATING FRAME FOR A CIRCULAR MAGNETIC FIELD

Root's stronger construction uses direct magnetic-dipole coupling.  Let an
alkali-like ground manifold have electronic spin S=1/2 and nuclear spin I.
Write dimensionless spin operators (commutators in units with hbar removed)
and

 H_at=A I.S,  F=I+S,
 D=mu_B(g_s S+g_i I),
 H_lab=H_M + H_at + B_c G(q)[D_x cos(omega_d t)+D_y sin(omega_d t)].

Here g_i includes the nuclear moment expressed in Bohr magnetons, with its
physical sign.  A static bias along z may be added.  An ideal common trap
commutes with F_z.  The exact transformation U=exp(-i omega_d t F_z) gives

 H_rot=H_M+H_at-hbar omega_d F_z+B_c G(q)D_x.

There is no rotating-wave approximation.  All magnetic hyperfine states
are retained.  This relies on circular polarization with the stated spatial
profile, not an arbitrary linearly polarized microwave field.

At zero bias choose

 a=|F_-=I-1/2,m_a=I-1/2>,
 b=|F_+=I+1/2,m_b=I+1/2>,
 Delta_hf=E_+-E_-=A(I+1/2)>0,
 hbar omega_d=Delta_hf+Lambda.

After subtracting the rotating energy of a, the internal offsets are

 lower manifold:  r hbar omega_d,       r=0,...,2F_-;
 upper manifold: -Lambda+r hbar omega_d,r=0,...,2F_+.

Thus a has offset0, b has offset-Lambda, and every other state has offset
at least hbar omega_d-Lambda=Delta_hf.  If E_n-E_0<Delta_hf, every spectator's
ENTIRE native motional spectrum lies above E_n.  This is a real gap for
the omitted spectator continua, not a clamped-coordinate electronic gap.
Only the intended b continuum is open.

The dipole is nonzero.  Clebsch-Gordan coefficients give

 |<b|D_+|a>|=mu_B |g_s-g_i| sqrt[2I/(2I+1)],
 d_ab=|<b|D_x|a>|=one half of that.

With B_c=g/d_ab, the desired a-b off-diagonal is gG.  For I=3/2,g_s=2,g_i=0,
d_ab=sqrt(3)mu_B/2.  The selected pair is magnetically sensitive; this is not
a clock transition.  Magnetic noise must be included.

6. WHY THE ORIGINAL BLOCK THEOREM SURVIVES THE FULL MANIFOLD

Divide the complete spin space by parity of the integer m-m_a.  Let S contain
even parity and B odd parity.  H_at and a z bias preserve m.  D_x changes m
by exactly +/-1 and therefore changes parity.  The exact full Hamiltonian is

 [[H_S, g V^dagger],[g V,H_B]],

where H_S contains a and only closed spectators; H_B contains b and closed
spectators.  The native target |a,n> remains isolated in H_S.  V is G(q)
times the finite normalized magnetic matrix D_x/d_ab between the parities.

For the first resolvent vector, the b component is the exact Morse u1 from
the main theorem.  Every other component is an ordinary closed-channel
resolvent applied to G|n>, with a genuine spectral denominator.  Therefore
u1 exists and is normalizable without truncating the b continuum.  V^dagger
u1 lies in S; its reduced resolvent exists because S is gapped at |a,n>.
This supplies u2 and the same order-g^3 residual as the original theorem.

The extra closed channels add finite dispersive shifts.  They do not require
an unnotched open-channel inversion at second order: the angular-momentum
parity prevents that.  The coefficient to calibrate is chi_full, including
those shifts.  This is the substantive improvement over an asserted
two-level microwave RWA.

check_circular_hyperfine.py verifies the entire eight-state I=3/2 manifold
at finite Delta_hf/D=100 and Lambda/D=.5.  It obtains offsets
{-0.5,0,100,100.5,200.5,201,301,401.5}; exact parity anticommutator norm0;
normalized target coupling1; squared couplings from a to closed spectators
sum1/3; and ||D_x/d_ab||=2/sqrt(3).

Consequently a simple bound on the spectator contribution is

 |delta chi| <= ||G n||^2/(3 gamma_spec),
 gamma_spec=min_j(C_j+E_0-E_n)>0.

The full repulsive-wall stability condition is strengthened to
|g| c ||D_x/d_ab||<D.  In the checked case this is |g|c(2/sqrt3)<D.

7. SOURCES AND NOISE: WHAT DIRECT MAGNETIC CONTROL REALLY IMPROVES

At fixed geometry, B_c is linear in current, g is linear in current, and
resistive source power is quadratic in g.  For a single port B_c=beta I_peak,

 g=d_ab beta I_peak,
 P=R I_peak^2/2=R g^2/(2 d_ab^2 beta^2),
 P T=R hbar theta/(2 d_ab^2 beta^2 chi_full).

Thus the ideal source energy can stay finite as g->0.  This removes the
balanced optical-Raman source's 1/g cost and remote excited-state scattering.
It does not remove fixed background or thermal chip noise.

Define the two-sided current spectrum S_I(omega)=integral dt exp(i omega t)
<delta I(t)delta I(0)>.  With current-to-field mode functions beta_j(q), a
transition rate contains

 (1/hbar^2) sum_jk <f|D.beta_j|i><i|D.beta_k|f>
 S_IjIk((E_f-E_i)/hbar),

summed over all final internal/motional states, including the continuum.
For a classical equilibrium resistor, this convention gives 2 k_B T ReY.
Distributed conductor noise supplies additional field modes; independent
noise in the two spatial Fourier components does not automatically inherit
the cancellation of their coherent mean fields.  A single noisily driven
common mode can retain the same G profile, but its spectral width and the
other thermal modes must still be assessed.  Fixed decoherence rates give
exposure proportional to g^-2.  Photon/spin/motional failures remain inclusive
data, not erased by survival conditioning.

8. THE REMAINING MAXWELL AND NATIVE-TRAP INTERFACES

Two orthogonal patterned current sheets in quadrature produce the required
circular magnetic field on their common symmetry axis.  Away from that axis
the cosine envelopes differ and longitudinal sine components occur.  Root's
separate Maxwell construction derives the finite lateral-mode errors; they
are not suppressed here by silently setting the lateral width to zero.

In particular, a longitudinal field multiplies D_z, which preserves m parity
and retains an oscillation at omega_d in the rotating frame.  Its diagonal
spin matrix elements can drive high-energy motional continua.  Similarly,
transverse ellipticity introduces opposite-helicity terms at twice the drive
frequency in that frame.  Large drive frequency alone is not a global
continuum-gap argument for either term.  Their state-action bounds or actual
continuum tails are part of the finite-width interface, even though the
ideal exactly circular spin completion above has no omitted spin channel.

Time-varying magnetic fields also have electric near fields.  A TE vector
potential A_y exp(-kappa q) cos(kx) exp(-i omega_d t) has B_x=kappa A_y and
E_y=i omega_d A_y, so |E|/|B|=omega_d/kappa on the axis.  Electric polarizability
therefore adds an O(g^2) spatial potential whose phase during T~g^-2 can stay
finite.  It must be included in chi or bounded.  It is not removed just by
weakening g.

There is also a global-domain warning: an attractive scalar electric Stark
term proportional to g^2G^2 has an F^4 tail, whereas the mathematical Morse
repulsive wall grows only as F^2.  The real half-space surface bounds F at
finite q; the ideal full-line Hamiltonian with the extra term is not globally
the same model.  An actual half-space trap needs its boundary and full
potential in the continuum calculation.

For neutral alkalis, equal optical trapping potentials of the two stretched
hyperfine states are not exact merely because both are ground states.  Scalar
hyperfine polarizability differences, vector shifts and material forces need
matching or a quantified mismatch.  Suitable polarization and extra fields
may compensate differential shifts, but that is an additional physical
construction.  The circular magnetic source itself does not establish it.

Ions provide a strongly state-independent electric charge potential, but
transverse confinement, image forces, finite electrodes and an actual Morse
escape profile would then have to be built and audited.  The existing ion
sideband demonstration does not provide those ingredients automatically.

9. OPERATION AND COMPARATOR

The useful surviving result is a dispersive phase accumulated through an
open auxiliary continuum, with a normalizable first divided-resolvent vector,
and an explicit finite-order error bound.  The full-hyperfine circular drive
adds a reusable microscopic selection principle: angular-momentum rotation
and parity place every unintended branch behind a real spectral threshold
while preserving the one deliberately open branch and its spectral zero.

This is not a generic superiority claim for a phase gate.  If an equally
equipped controller can reverse the detuning and place the auxiliary channel
below its threshold, ordinary dispersive control already performs the same
generic phase task and is a stronger practical comparator.  The theorem is
consequential only for a task that genuinely requires the auxiliary sector
to stay open, or as a design principle for coherent operation amid unavoidable
continua.  Matching the actual native potential and obtaining a finite noise
budget remain the physical decision points.
