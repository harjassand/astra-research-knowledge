25 October 2026

Independent construction and challenge for the acquired-boundary continuation.
Governing predecessor:
  research/acquired_local_control/boundary_response/transport/
  POSITIVE_PAULI_ERROR_EFFECTS_GIVE_EXACT_REFERENCE_UNIFORM_TRANSPORT.txt

This source proves a task-specific alternative to acquiring a worst-state
hidden-memory response width. It does not infer that width. The actual task is
one attempted transport of an unknown quantum payload, with an accept/reject
decision obtained during that attempt. Unknown quantum memory and the native
process may be arbitrarily complicated. Existing quantum authentication and
adversarial quantum-state verification are explicit prior capital.

The source also proves a scaling obstruction: fixed-strength weak tests with
constant ideal acceptance cannot reach accepted transport error o(n^-1/2)
using n unencoded carriers. Repeating the actual weak probes can recover the
O(n^-1) accepted-error bound, at O(log n) weak measurement time per audit.
The simulation/checks here operate on mathematical instruments, not hardware.

1. Why interleaved mean-risk acquisition alone is not authentication
===================================================================
The paired weak score for an actual fresh Pauli-framed native operation can
estimate a predictable current-use memory error even if the memory evolves.
That identity is useful. It must not silently become any of the following:

* A tail bound on the actual number of Pauli errors in a whole transmitted
  quantum stream. A persistent hidden error bit can make all outputs wrong
  together. Received-history mean error p does not imply independent errors.
* A fine-record posterior bound for earlier outputs after later observations.
  Later measurements can reveal a rare memory sector and condition a formerly
  low-error output onto its erroneous branch.
* A full-stream or frame-flagged distance bound. Even independent per-use
  dephasing of size p gives all-Bell-pair infidelity 1-(1-p)^n. Retaining a
  coherent-rotation frame flag can give an error of order sqrt(p).

The construction below uses a secret task location and a positive error-word
instrument to give a different, explicitly accepted-subchannel guarantee.

2. Actual physical interface and privacy requirements
=====================================================
There are n temporal input/output carrier ports. Each carrier is one qubit.
The complete native process between those ports is an unknown CPTP comb. It
may contain arbitrarily large physical quantum memory, noncommuting memory
maps, temporal correlations, and nonstationary operations. Its input/output
ports and local dimension are part of the physical interface.

The observer has:

1. Actual local Pauli pulses at each port, with a known matching of input and
   output axes. These do not supply the native transport gate itself.
2. Known efficient single-qubit weak Luedders instruments with contrast eta,
   including their backaction, in X, Y and Z. On an audited carrier a probe
   is applied before its input frame and another after its output frame.
3. A private random index J uniform in {1,...,n}, private fresh Pauli frame
   keys, and private audit settings/outcomes while the native block runs.
4. A trusted local loading/buffering operation that places the SAME unknown
   payload at temporal input J, and a trusted local output latch which keeps
   the corresponding received qubit isolated until the block ends. Dummy
   carriers fill the other slots. This is an actual coherent physical port;
   it is NOT acquired from a state-learning theorem. Sender/receiver storage
   is local and does not provide a bypass across the native transport gap.
5. Carrier isolation before insertion and after extraction. The native
   process cannot revisit an extracted output. Timing and other side channels
   do not reveal J, the audit axis, outcome, or a Pauli key while an attack or
   native interaction can still depend on them.

All slots use the same native interaction timing. An idle interval on the
payload slot must match the time charged for probes on audit slots. Unknown
free evolution in that interval belongs in the native comb when it acts on
the plant, and in a separately bounded storage channel when it acts on the
protected payload. Good storage is a premise with a physical error budget.

For each slot draw a fresh uniform Pauli R, apply R at input, call the native
port, then apply the matched R at output. The matching may be only a relabeling
of physical ports. No trusted entangling encoder is assumed. The Pauli keys
are averaged out of the certified output and never fed back to the native
process. They may be destroyed after use. Publishing them as quantum-output
flags is a stronger task not covered by the small-error theorem.

On every j != J choose an independent uniform axis P in {X,Y,Z}, weakly
measure P before the frame, obtaining y, and after the frame, obtaining z.
Audit passes iff yz=+1. Payload J receives no measurement. Initially the
payload may be entangled with an arbitrary external reference. The general
theorem below also permits correlations with physical native memory, but its
ideal accept operation must then include the same local environment filter.

3. Global Pauli reduction for arbitrary quantum memory
=====================================================
Because an unused input carrier has not yet interacted with the comb and an
extracted output cannot return to it, all input probes can be commuted to the
beginning and all output probes to the end for analysis. This is a mathematical
reordering of operations on disjoint systems, not an extra physical operation.
The complete native block is therefore a joint CPTP map on n carrier inputs
and physical memory M. Factor out the mathematical target wire matching.

Expand each native Kraus operator in the n-carrier Pauli basis:

  L_l = sum_e e_1 tensor ... tensor e_n tensor K_(l,e).

Fresh independent actual Pauli frames remove all cross terms e != e'. The
averaged map has product Kraus operators

  e_1 tensor ... tensor e_n tensor K_(l,e),                 (1)

with

  sum_(l,e) K_(l,e)^* K_(l,e) = I_M.                      (2)

These K are physical quantum-memory operators, not samples of an assumed
unknown classical model label. They can be noncommuting, and in a sequential
description their CP maps update physical memory. Their error-word labels are
mathematical unreceived Kraus labels. No syndrome has been supplied.

This is the same Pauli reduction used in quantum-authentication proofs, now
applied to the temporal weak-record interface. The unknown process need not
forget its past. The observer does not construct or retain the K operators.

4. Acquired weak audit has scalar success effect
================================================
For fixed Pauli error s and axis P, let chi_s(P) be +1 if they commute and -1
otherwise. Efficient weak Kraus operators K_y^P obey
  (K_y^P)^2 = (I+y eta P)/2.
The pass effect of the two actual measurements is

  sum_y K_y^P s^* (K_y^P)^2 s K_y^P
       = [(1+chi_s(P) eta^2)/2] I.                       (3)

It is scalar on the UNKNOWN input qubit. Thus tracing a tested carrier after
pass applies exactly that scalar times the appropriate memory CP map, even
when the carrier was entangled with other systems. No prepared Pauli eigenstate
is needed. Averaging the actual random axis gives

  a = (1+eta^2)/2               if s=I,
  b = (1-eta^2/3)/2             if s!=I.                 (4)

For any fixed nonidentity Pauli, one axis commutes and two anticommute. The
unknown preparation and measurement backaction have disappeared from this
specific effect, not from the physical experiment or its full fine records.

5. Accepted-subchannel theorem, including reference and memory
==============================================================
First accept only when all n-1 audits pass. Consider error word e of weight k,
meaning k nonidentity carrier errors. It has an erroneous payload exactly when
J is one of those k positions. For each such J there are k-1 erroneous audits
and n-k correct audits. Equations (3)-(4) give the bad accepted scalar weight

  c_(n,k) = (k/n) b^(k-1) a^(n-k),       1 <= k <= n.    (5)

Let beta_n=max_k c_(n,k). Positivity and (2) imply that the entire accepted
bad-payload CP map has effect at most beta_n I on all other quantum inputs.
There is no sum over the potentially enormous memory dimension.

For clarity, retain J, the audit axes and all weak outcomes as classical flags
r, but average out the Pauli frame keys. For a fixed error word and flag, the
actual accepted Kraus map is a Pauli s_J acting on the payload tensor a CP
map acting only on the dummy carriers/memory/environment. Define an ideal
accepted map by replacing EVERY s_J by I while leaving that same environment
map and classical flag. This ideal accepted map has form

  id_payload tensor E_accept,                             (6)

where E_accept is a trace-nonincreasing CP map on the environment. Actual and
ideal maps have exactly the same accept probabilities, since replacing a
unitary Pauli by I does not change a trace. For a positive branch of weight w,
the half trace distance between the Pauli-modified and unmodified state is at
most w. Correct-payload branches cancel. Hence, with diamond norm between the
entire joint input and declared joint output,

  (1/2)||A_accept - id_payload tensor E_accept||_diamond
     <= beta_n.                                          (7)

The inequality holds with an arbitrary untouched external reference and
arbitrary initial payload-environment correlations. It can include physical
memory output and the fine audit flags. In the correlated case E_accept may
steer the conditional payload state through its pre-existing entanglement;
the comparison preserves the payload relative to this SAME local environment
operation. It does not assert that postselection leaves its unconditional
input density matrix unchanged.

If payload+reference is initially independent of the environment, (7) is the
usual statement that the accepted unknown state is preserved with weighted
error <= beta_n. Dividing by the actual acceptance probability p gives
conditional trace error <= beta_n/p. One cannot set p=1 simply because one
attempt happened to accept. The unconditional accept/reject interface is the
clean composable object. With the output erased on rejection, the reject map
acts only on the environment and discards the payload; Pauli errors disappear
under that payload trace. This supplies an ideal rejection branch too.

To prove a complete cryptographic notion beyond this explicit map statement,
one must also declare which parties receive flags, keys, and timing. No key
recycling, ciphertext authentication, or device-independent metrology is
being claimed here.

When b/a <= 1/2, equivalently eta^2 >= 3/5, k(b/a)^(k-1) <=1 for every k>=1.
Therefore

  beta_n = a^(n-1)/n <= 1/n.                             (8)

The maximum is achieved by a process putting one fixed nonidentity Pauli at
one fixed temporal location. A maximally entangled payload-reference input
makes that payload error orthogonal to the target, so this worst-case scalar
bound is attained. The native process does not need to know J to attain it.

For a perfectly transporting native block, every audit passes with probability
a, independent of its unknown input. Ideal acceptance is exactly a^(n-1).
At fixed eta<1 this vanishes exponentially. That failure is consequential:
the correct soundness theorem alone does not make a useful high-yield port.

6. Actual repeated weak probes repair the completeness loss
===========================================================
Repeat the same known QND weak P instrument L times before the frame and L
times after it, with L odd. Decode each group by its majority sign. Define

  e_L = Pr[Bin(L,(1+eta)/2) <= (L-1)/2],
  eta_L = 1-2e_L.                                        (9)

This follows by diagonalizing P. Conditional on either P eigenvalue, each
QND weak sign has the corresponding Bernoulli distribution. Pairing each
fine word with its sign-complement cancels off-diagonal terms in the signed
instrument. The majority-signed instrument is exactly

  sum_word majority(word) K_word rho K_word^*
       = (eta_L/2){P,rho}.                               (10)

Consequently (3)-(8) hold with eta_L replacing eta. These are finite physical
weak measurement operations, not a silently added ideal projective meter.
The majority-coarsened instrument is generally NOT the Luedders instrument at
eta_L: its unsigned dephasing differs. Only the signed identity (10) and its
effect agree, and those are precisely what the present proof uses.
Hoeffding gives e_L<=exp(-L eta^2/2), and
  1-a_L=(1-eta_L^2)/2<=2 exp(-L eta^2/2).
Thus L >= (2/eta^2) log[2(n-1)/kappa], rounded up to an odd integer, suffices
for ideal acceptance >=1-kappa by a union bound. Choosing L large enough also
gives eta_L^2>=3/5. Native calls remain n; actual weak probe calls are
2(n-1)L. Native duration, local probe duration and quantum-buffer waiting time
are distinct costs. Data spends O(n times full slot duration) in protected
storage before/after its single native call.

Exact rational finite checks with base eta=3/5 and ideal acceptance >=0.99:

  n=25:   L=29,  1392 weak calls, ideal accept .9932164037,
          beta=.03972865615 <=1/25.
  n=100:  L=35,  6930 weak calls, ideal accept .9932457191,
          beta=.009932457191 <=1/100.
  n=400:  L=41, 32718 weak calls, ideal accept .9933507691,
          beta=.002483376923 <=1/400.
  n=1600: L=47,150306 weak calls, ideal accept .9934334295,
          beta=.0006208958934 <=1/1600.

These are calculated interface budgets, not acquired empirical records and
not claims that an actual native apparatus has that completeness.

7. A second construction: tolerate weak audit failures
=====================================================
If strengthening the probe is expensive, use one weak pair per audit and
accept whenever at most t audits fail. Set

  f_a=1-a=(1-eta^2)/2,
  f_b=1-b=(1+eta^2/3)/2,
  Delta=f_b-f_a=2eta^2/3.

For an error word of weight k, conditional on the payload lying in a bad
position, the coarse audit failures have the exact scalar generating function

  [(1-f_b)+f_b z]^(k-1) [(1-f_a)+f_a z]^(n-k).

This is a computation of positive branch effects. It does not impose an iid
quantum state or iid native errors. The scalar Bernoulli variables describe
only the independent randomized trusted tests conditional on a fixed word.
The sharp accepted-subchannel error is

  beta_(n,t)=max_(1<=k<=n) (k/n)
    Pr[Bin(k-1,f_b)+Bin(n-k,f_a) <= t].                  (11)

Equation (7) holds with beta_(n,t). A fixed Pauli error word at the maximizing
k and an entangled payload-reference attain the bound. Ideal acceptance is
Pr[Bin(n-1,f_a)<=t]. This permits nonvanishing ideal acceptance with fixed
weak contrast. It pays a worse soundness scaling.

For a transparent sufficient estimate, put N=n-1 and choose
  t=floor(N f_a+s), s>=0.
If k>1+(s+sqrt[(N/2)log(1/zeta)])/Delta, Hoeffding bounds the pass probability
by zeta. Otherwise k/n is already small. Hence

  beta_(n,t) <= max{[1+(s+sqrt[(N/2)log(1/zeta)])/Delta]/n,
                    zeta}.                             (12)

Round the right side upward or use (11) for finite certification. The ideal
rejection probability is at most exp[-2(s-1)^2/N] for s>=1, accounting for the
floor. Taking s=1+sqrt[(N/2)log(1/kappa)] ensures rejection <=kappa.
Thus fixed weak probes give O(eta^-2 sqrt(log n/n)) soundness at constant
ideal acceptance; direct exact optimization often improves the constants.

Floating SciPy evaluations for eta=.6 and ideal acceptance at least .99:
  n=25,   t=13:  ideal accept .993054, beta .559358, worst k=20.
  n=100,  t=43:  ideal accept .993638, beta .279475, worst k=40.
  n=400,  t=150: ideal accept .992252, beta .132352, worst k=77.
  n=1600, t=555: ideal accept .990241, beta .0625533,worst k=148.
These approximate numerical values are not exact finite-bit certificates.
The formula and small exact enumeration checks are separate evidence.

8. Fixed weak readout has a square-root soundness obstruction
=============================================================
Consider any acceptance rule based on n-1 single weak-pair records, allowing
all axis labels and both individual signs, with ideal acceptance >=1-kappa.
Take independent maximally mixed dummy inputs and allow an arbitrary unknown
payload entangled with a reference. This is an allowed controlled case.

Compare ideal transport with the iid Pauli channel that applies a uniformly
chosen nonidentity Pauli with probability q at every slot. For a dummy input,
both signs have joint distribution
  Pr(y,z|P)=[1+eta^2(1-4q/3) yz]/4.
Thus all information in the fine audit record about q is its parity; the first
sign is uniform and the axis gives no additional information for this attack.
Audit failure probability is f_1=f_a+Delta q. There are N=n-1 independent such
observations. The payload error is independent of their record and has
probability q. This is already a simple conventional channel, not an exotic
coherent-memory attack.

Let q=c/sqrt(N), with
  c=(1-kappa) sqrt[f_a(1-f_a)]/(2 Delta).
For sufficiently large N, f_1<=(1+f_a)/2 and q<=1. The Bernoulli KL bound gives
  D(Ber(f_a)||Ber(f_1)) <= (Delta q)^2/[f_1(1-f_1)]
                         <=2(Delta q)^2/[f_a(1-f_a)].
Pinsker therefore gives total variation between complete ideal and attacked
test records at most
  Delta c/sqrt[f_a(1-f_a)] = (1-kappa)/2.
Any rule accepting ideal with probability >=1-kappa must accept this attack
with probability at least (1-kappa)/2. Consequently its accepted erroneous
payload weight is at least

  (1-kappa)^2 sqrt[f_a(1-f_a)]/(4 Delta sqrt(N)).          (13)

A Bell-reference payload makes the nonidentity errors orthogonal, so this is
also an accepted trace-error lower bound against the ideal transport with
the same acceptance probability. For every fixed 0<eta<1 the coefficient is
positive. This proves that no processing of the same single weak-pair records
can improve the worst-case exponent beyond n^-1/2 at constant completeness.
The bound does not apply to stronger measurements, repeated weak probes,
entangling error-detecting encodings, altered native calls, or additional
trusted information. Strong conventional authentication codes use precisely
such additional resources and can achieve much better scaling.

8a. Revealing frame keys changes the scaling; revealing axes breaks the task
===========================================================================
The distinction between fine audit records and Pauli frame keys is necessary.
For an exactly controlled challenge, let the native channel apply the same
coherent Z rotation U=cos(theta)I-i sin(theta)Z in every slot. Take the ideal
projective limit eta=1. Each random-axis audit passes with probability
  p=1-(2/3)sin(theta)^2.
The keys flip the sign of the payload's coherent rotation. If those keys are
retained as readable output flags, the accepted payload-reference trace error
on a Bell input is
  |sin(theta)| p^(n-1).
When keys are traced, the corresponding error is
  sin(theta)^2 p^(n-1).
The flagged expression is maximized at sin(theta)^2=3/[2(2n-1)] for n>=2,
and scales as n^-1/2, rather than n^-1. With n=100, cos(theta)=399/401 and
sin(theta)=40/401, exact rational arithmetic verifies flagged error >1/100
and key-erased error <1/100. This is a fixed, simple native unitary; no large
memory or sophisticated adversary is needed for the failure.

If the audit axis is disclosed while the native call can depend on it, a
still stronger failure occurs: the native process applies that Pauli P. It
commutes with its two-time trap measurement, so every audit has precisely
its ideal pass probability a. On a hidden payload slot give the same interface
a random unused P label; the process applies a nonidentity Pauli to the payload
with certainty. Thus slot secrecy alone is insufficient. Axis secrecy and a
common native physical process before receiving private probe information are
part of the operation, not optional proof conveniences.

9. Bounded observer description and what is retired
===================================================
For fixed task precision the streaming observer retains a secret slot index,
slot counter, one currently needed frame, a small majority counter and an
all-pass bit or failure counter. It need not store a quantum posterior for M
or its growing past. Two local protected buffers suffice for the one payload:
one before insertion and one after extraction. Dummy carriers can be consumed
immediately. Raw audit signs can be retired after updating the counter.

The proof certifies a task using newly acquired marks. It does not predict
arbitrary future controls from a small memory model. Description remains
bounded because the operation restricts its requested consequence and pays
for fresh audit acquisition on each use. Past hidden information need not
decay: a secret randomized role and scalar audit effect make its entire
physical influence enter a bounded positive error budget for THIS operation.

Repeated blocks use fresh private keys and fresh task-specific acquisition.
The native process may retain memory across blocks; the proof is uniform in
that memory. Per-block accepted error is not a zero-error guarantee over an
infinite sequence. Composition pays the sum of the declared block errors.
Protected storage quality, random-key secrecy and trusted weak-instrument
quality are real interface premises, not learned consequences of the audit.

10. Complementary resource-state construction and its limit
===========================================================
If the physical source instead emits candidate Bell pairs, one can retain a
uniformly hidden pair J and locally test all others after isolation. Uniform
X/X, Y/Y (opposite sign), and Z/Z parity tests have verification effect
  Omega=Phi+(I-Phi)/3,
where Phi projects onto the target Bell pair. For arbitrary coherent joint
source state across n pairs and arbitrary quantum memory,

  (1/n) sum_J (I-Phi_J) product_(i!=J) Omega_i <= I/n.     (14)

Diagonalize the commuting Phi_i: a configuration with k bad pairs has
eigenvalue k 3^(1-k)/n, maximized at k=1. If all emitted pairs remain isolated
from the source, tests can be made immediately; their nonselective local
operations cannot signal into the source, and the joint experiment equals
deferred tests on the entire emitted state. One pair of quantum storage is
enough. Weak local tests can be sharpened using actual repeated QND probes.

This acquires a surviving entangled resource without any memory-width bound.
It does NOT by itself acquire a Bell measurement or input-coupling port needed
to teleport an unknown payload. A trusted local Bell measurement would be an
additional actual coherent operation. The direct secret-slot construction
above avoids that particular addition, while explicitly requiring temporal
loading and protected storage instead.

Adversarial quantum-state verification already studies such homogeneous
verification operators and obtains stronger ensemble-conditioned significance
formulas. Equation (14) is a deliberately simple acceptance-weighted bound,
not a claim to supersede that theory or establish its priority.

11. Strong conventional comparison and primary sources
=======================================================
Barnum, Crepeau, Gottesman, Smith and Tapp, Authentication of Quantum Messages,
FOCS 2002, https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0205128 .
Their work supplies the foundational quantum authentication/encryption
principle and efficient purity-testing codes. It provides exponentially small
error using additional encoded qubits and trusted encoding operations.

Dulek and Speelman, Quantum ciphertext authentication and key recycling with
the trap code, TQC 2018, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.02237 .
Section 3 and Appendix B show Pauli reductions with adversarial side
information. Stronger notions, ciphertext integrity, and key recycling have
separate hypotheses. This source does not acquire them merely by using traps.

Zhu and Hayashi, General framework for verifying pure quantum states in the
adversarial scenario, PRA 100,062335 (2019),
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.01943 .
Sections IV-VI analyze adversarial source verification, including homogeneous
operators, and give more efficient significance/fidelity tradeoffs than a
naive Markov conversion of (14).

Conventional filters, process tensors, stability methods and quantum
controllers receive the same local instruments, matched frames, hidden slot,
loading/latching and record. They can use every theorem here. An observer that
already has a trusted entangling encoder should compare against established
authentication codes, not be restricted to unencoded temporal traps.

The scoped programme gain is a usable weak-record acquisition interface for
accepted coherent transport, and the explicit tradeoff between fixed weak
information, measurement strengthening, acceptance and protected storage.
It replaces an unacquired hidden-memory width with a different physically
specified operation. No general economical quantum simulator, unknown-state
tomography, arbitrary-control port, or device advantage is established.
