ASTRA — CYCLIC TASK OBSERVERS AND THE RESPONSE-MEMORY LIMIT
25 October 2026, Australia/Brisbane

Question
========
Can the newly acquired syndrome/task representation be renewed indefinitely
with bounded observer state, and what prevents that statement from becoming
a claim of generic efficient prediction?

The answer separates three claims. A fixed-budget observer can repeatedly
acquire and retire calibration data. A fresh operator-uniform verifier can
certify each delivered accepted operation even when the learned law drifts.
Neither statement supplies a useful acceptance rate, a faithful unlimited
quantum output stream, or small response complexity as the task interface grows.
This source gives the precise finite-state renewal construction and a sharp
exponential response-memory obstruction. No new hardware operation is supplied.

1. A bounded cyclic observer with real acquisition
=================================================
Fix all of the following before execution:
* a physically constructed one-qubit communication macro with m terminal
  readout ancillas, which are actually weakly measured and discarded;
* its known terminal QND likelihood kernel, with repetition lengths bounded
  by L_max and elementary contrast bounded away from zero;
* a calibration budget N, quantization denominator 2^b, and validation length n;
* declared local meter, pulse, routing, privacy and buffer error allowances;
* the finite task and the allowed final-output Pauli repair family.

On each cycle:
  (a) acquire N new outer paired weak audit records;
  (b) accumulate the four positive candidate-pass counts in each majority
      context, as in ACQUIRED_LOGICAL_REPAIRS_FROM_TAGGED_WEAK_RECORDS.txt;
  (c) invert the known majority-readout kernel, project to positive task
      weights, and round to a probability vector with denominator 2^b;
  (d) freeze the resulting full-count recovery;
  (e) use fresh independent private settings for an n-slot accepted-operation
      validation of that entire actual macro and recovery;
  (f) retire the old calibration words and counters and renew the next cycle.

The required statistical message is bounded by
  O(4*2^m log(N+1) + 4*2^m (b+1) + m log(L_max+1)
    + log(n+1)) bits,                                 (1)
in addition to the fixed code, physical-interface description, working
arithmetic, and current settings. Integer accumulators and exact rational
intermediate arithmetic also have fixed finite bounds because N,L_max,m and
all declared rational constants are fixed. A dyadic numerator can equal 2^b,
so b fractional bits require at most b+1 numerator bits in a generic encoding.

For Steane's six final readout bits this is 256 acquired weights and bounded
calibration counters. It is independent of the number of completed cycles.
The physical channel/environment M may keep evolving; no small description of
M is inferred from (1). The code's exponential 2^m dependence remains explicit.

This is streaming acquisition followed by an actual finite representation
reset. Old records are dispensable for the next guarantee because that next
guarantee holds uniformly over the physical state they leave behind, not
because the old physical information is assumed to have disappeared.

2. What can be renewed conditionally on the complete past
=======================================================
Let H_k denote any actually retained classical history before validation block
k, and let the acquired frozen controller be f_k. Conditioned on H_k, the
physical quantum memory can be arbitrary and correlated with the next unknown
input/reference. The fresh outer setting must not be fed to the native macro
or decoder through an extra port. Retired frame keys remain retired.

The accepted-operation theorem supplies, for every such H_k, an appropriate
environment subchannel E_(k,H_k) with
  (1/2)||A_(k,H_k),accept
            - Ad_V tensor E_(k,H_k),accept||_diamond
       <= beta_n + delta_(trusted,k).                  (2)
This is an operator bound, not a confidence interval inferred from how good
the training fit looked. Its applicability does not depend on the acquired
weights being a future model, the training record being typical, or the
environment forgetting past cycles. The learned policy can be arbitrarily bad
and (2) still holds, at the possible price of poor acceptance/performance.

By contrast, the learned weights' finite-N confidence statement concerns the
actual CALIBRATION MIXTURE in that cycle. Reusing a failure budget delta in
each cycle gives a conditional per-cycle probability statement, not a single
1-delta event on which every estimate in infinitely many cycles is accurate.
A finite K-cycle union bound costs K delta. Summable risk allocation or stronger
uniform methods must be explicitly constructed and charged if requested.

Quantizing a fitted positive weight vector by largest remainder changes its
l1 norm by at most D*2^-b, D=4*2^m. This adds at most that quantity to the
corresponding plug-in MAP regret bound under the calibration mixture. It does
not add a new error term to the UNIVERSAL outer integrity theorem, because
that theorem accepts any fixed declared recovery algorithm. Inner likelihood
misspecification likewise threatens learned performance, not an independently
valid whole-macro outer proof; an error in the outer trusted meter is different
and belongs in delta_trusted.

3. No free infinite-record accuracy or useful delivery yield
==========================================================
For K sequential accepted/rejected task instruments, ordinary compositional
distance costs can add up to sum_k(beta_n+delta_(trusted,k)). Conditioning on
all acceptances can amplify a bound by the reciprocal of the joint acceptance
probability. Fixed n and fixed precision therefore do not give a uniform
small-distance guarantee for an arbitrarily long final quantum transcript.

A memoryless independent native error with nonzero accepted bad weight per
attempt is already a counterexample: the chance that no accepted error occurs
in K attempts decays to zero. No quantum-memory adversary is needed. This does
not invalidate a bounded-cost per-attempt service; it invalidates extrapolation
from that service to perfect accumulated information.

The specified finite unsharp wrapper DOES have a universal positive acceptance
floor. With b>0 its minimum one-audit pass probability, positivity gives
  A_accept^*(I) >= b^(n-1) I.
This exponentially small floor must not be replaced by a claim that an
arbitrary device can make this fixed wrapper reject with probability one.
An all-bad relative-Pauli instrument attains the bad-audit pass probability
b^(n-1) while its accepted payload can have conditional error one. Thus the
floor supplies neither economical yield nor a positive universal lower yield
of correctly delivered operations. The subnormalized integrity bound and this
example are compatible: conditioning divides by a small acceptance weight.

Empirical good yield over a finite calibration interval does not exclude a
later hidden activation. Storage time and consumed private distributed
randomness are physical resources in every cycle even when the retained
statistical state is bounded. A private classical control channel can stream
settings; otherwise pre-shared information-theoretic key stock is an additional
charged resource. A short pseudorandom seed does not establish the
unconditional theorem.

4. A sharp response-complexity obstruction with no continuing quantum memory
==========================================================================
Let K=2^m. For every bit table f in {0,1}^K define the MEMORYLESS channel
  G_f(rho)= (1/K) sum_(mu=1)^K
       X^{f(mu)} rho X^{f(mu)} tensor |mu><mu|_A.       (3)
The terminal m-bit ancilla reveals a fresh uniform context mu. There is no
continuing quantum-state memory. The unknown fixed response law f is a
classical device parameter; it is not renamed a physical quantum mode.

Give the observer unlimited prior calibration and even an ideal readout of
mu (stronger than the finite weak interface). Then retire calibration, retaining
only a controller message M of at most B bits. On a fresh context it must
recover an arbitrary unknown logical qubit, with mean entanglement infidelity
at most eps<1/2 uniformly over f. For an explicit Pauli repair this is exactly
its probability of guessing the wrong Pauli. A trace-distance task guarantee
of eps is also sufficient for this lower bound. No additional f-sensitive query,
model oracle, physical
encoder or f-dependent uncharged circuit is available after the message is
fixed. All accessible f-dependent description must be charged to M.

Choose F uniformly from the K independent random bits. A successful Pauli
repair provides an estimate of F_mu from (M,mu). If eps_i is the average bit
error for context i, Fano's inequality and conditional subadditivity give
  H(F|M) <= sum_i H(F_i|M)
         <= sum_i h2(eps_i) <= K h2(eps),
  B >= I(F;M) >= K[1-h2(eps)].                         (4)
Here h2 is binary entropy with base-two logs. Thus a uniform small-risk
controller can require exponentially many retained bits in m even though each
physical call is memoryless. Arbitrary channel-response complexity has not
been bounded by bounding physical-state lifetimes.

The wording matters: the state dynamics are memoryless, but information about
the unknown static response table can remain useful indefinitely. This is not
a zero-lifetime theorem about unknown model information. It is exactly why
state forgetting and acquired model complexity must remain separate.

5. Quantum controllers and rejection do not hide the accounting
==============================================================
The lower bound extends to total retained quantum/classical log-dimension B,
provided every accessible ancillary resource carrying information about F is
included. For a quantum message Q, each context supplies a measurement capable
of decoding F_i with error eps_i. Then
  I(F;Q) >= sum_i I(F_i;Q)
         >= sum_i[1-h2(eps_i)],
while the Holevo dimension bound gives I(F;Q)<=log2 dim(Q)<=B.
This is the standard quantum random-access-code argument, applied to the
physical task family (3). Primary source:
  A. Nayak, Optimal lower bounds for quantum automata and random access codes,
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9904093, Theorem 2.3.

Even an arbitrary quantum recovery program, rather than an explicit Pauli
guess, gives such a random-access decoder. Feed half of a known Bell state to
the recovery operation for context i, without a new call to G_f, and measure
the recovery's Choi output in the Bell basis. A recovery that corrects the
unknown X^{F_i} with entanglement infidelity eps yields the Bell state labeled
F_i with probability at least 1-eps. Thus allowing a coherent recovery does
not evade (4). The Bell experiment is a mathematical reduction for the lower
bound, not an additional physical port used by the proposed observer.

If rejection is allowed, a yield premise is essential. Suppose average accepted
fraction is at least a>0 and conditional accepted error at most eps, uniformly
over f. The same decoder may output an erasure. For each i, with accepted
fraction a_i and conditional error eps_i,
  H(F_i | decoded output) <= (1-a_i)+a_i h2(eps_i).
Weighted concavity gives the sharpened lower bound
  B >= a K[1-h2(eps)].                                (5)
At zero error this is attained by storing f on a fixed B-context subset and
accepting exactly that subset, with yield B/K. In this ABSTRACT class of
arbitrary rejecting controllers, without a positive yield requirement the
always-reject controller is allowed and no positive memory lower bound follows
from accepted integrity alone. This is not an assertion that the fixed finite
unsharp outer wrapper can always reject: its positive acceptance floor was
preserved in Section 3. That floor does not guarantee small conditional error.

6. What renewed acquisition can buy instead
==========================================
The timing of information acquisition changes the bound. A fresh oracle query
that reveals f(mu) for the CURRENT context can supply the missing bit with
constant retained memory. Such a query is a new physical interface and cannot
be replaced by a claim that arbitrary old calibration was compressed.

For a stricter controlled case, retain no information about F and allow Q new
auxiliary calls of (3), each with independent uniform context and even perfectly
revealed error labels. Only a query whose context equals the current mu can
reveal its independent unknown F_mu. The optimal unconditional repair error is
  eps_Q=(1/2)(1-1/K)^Q.                               (6)
If the controller accepts only when a matching context has been seen, it has
zero conditional error and yield 1-(1-1/K)^Q. Thus bounded retained memory can
be traded for exponentially large fresh acquisition time in this family.
The comparison grants the auxiliary queries stronger information than the
actual weak assay, so it is an honest obstruction for that weaker access too.

The useful principle emerging from the positive construction and this limit
is specific. An observer can safely retire old calibration while renewing a
fixed task certificate when the physical interface gives a state-uniform
accepted-error effect. Small predictive descriptions additionally require
bounded task-context complexity or exploitable structure in the response law.
Neither short state memory nor an inexpensive observed context alone supplies
that structure. The 256-weight construction is a fully obtained fixed-task
instance, with its exponential-in-m boundary visible.
