AN ORIENTED, ONE-SHOT REPAIR INTERFACE: THE OPERATION IS NOT YET ACQUIRED

Status: prospective narrow joint architecture; material compatibility UNKNOWN.
No physical capability, novelty, superior performance or autonomous arbitrary-
crack repair is claimed. The contact-time bounds in CONTACT_UNCERTAINTY_BOUND.txt
are internally checked conditional results, independent of this candidate.

Why this is a different physical question

A preset damage-triggered catalytic pulse has no way to know whether the faces
have met. A contact-dependent bond-forming reaction can have that information
in its actual chemical state. Ordinary furan-maleimide (F-M) Diels-Alder healing
already does this: mechanically opened links provide complementary groups that
react when the faces meet. It is an incumbent, not an invention here. In mobile
DA elastomers, separated faces can also recombine their own F/M groups and lose
healing capacity. An oriented interface, with different chemistries on its two
sides, could suppress that same-face loss. It deliberately gives up arbitrary
crack placement and reusable chemistry.

Prospective sequence

Side A of an intended epoxy bondline bears tethered F and photocaged SH; side B
bears tethered M. Form initial cross-interface F-M DA bridges while SH remains
masked. Then illuminate a sufficiently thin, optically accessible intact seam to
unmask SH, leaving M DA-masked. In service, a mechanically opened DA bridge can
expose M on B; after separation and recontact, SH on A captures M as a thiol-
maleimide adduct. The intended new link consumes one SH and one M. An eventual
hydrolytic ring-opening lock would be a further operation, not an assumed free
part of maleimide chemistry. This is a one-shot intended-joint upgrade; a second
cut through the new link does not recreate the original F/SH/M repair reservoir.

Chemical segregation is the contact conjunction: after full separation A has
SH/F and B has M, so neither isolated face contains its intended SH-M partner.
It does not by itself guarantee molecular contact, enough mobile reach, correct
load-path anchoring, clean mechanical DA opening, or a creep-resistant product.

The initial assembly constraint is real. Free SH and free M can react before DA
bridges form. The staged photocage operation addresses order conceptually, but
does not establish a compatible formulation. A version that merely declares SH
inaccessible before damage and accessible after recontact has introduced an
unacquired actuator unless a physical accessibility state is specified.

Actual components and missing integrations

The named strong-host anchor is the furan-functionalized epoxy-amine thermoset
used by Pratama, Peterson and Palmese. Their established repair supplies fresh
multimaleimide in solvent; it is not this oriented, preloaded interface. Their
approximately 90% initial-strength recovery after 20 days is a useful same-host
incumbent, not evidence for DA cleavage leaving registered partners or repaired-
joint creep. The proposed SH graft and B-side M attachment are NOT demonstrated
on that host in the checked sources.
 https://doi.org/10.1039/C3PY00084B

A named photocage handle is 4-arm PEG-S-DMNB from Mosiewicz et al. The study
demonstrates 405 nm thiol uncaging and PEG/vinyl-sulfone crosslinking. Its stated
30 mW / 50 scans lacks sample-plane irradiance and dwell, so no transferable
fluence is available. It does not establish thermal survival during DA/epoxy
assembly, DA photochemical orthogonality, or incorporation in the strong host.
An epoxy-tethered analogue is a new candidate synthesis. Nitrobenzyl photoproduct
reactivity toward thiols/dienes is an additional compatibility gate, not a
demonstrated failure of the DMNB/epoxy combination.
 https://doi.org/10.1039/C4BM00262H
 https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.joc.6c00332

Latent rDA-to-thiol capture is itself prior art. A 2015 cure-on-demand study
reports blocked maleimide/polythiol mixtures stable up to seven days at room
temperature and curing at 110 C; free maleimide formulations cure within 120 s.
Those mobile, low-Tg formulations do not demonstrate a mechanically triggered
oriented strong-epoxy joint or service-life storage.
 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eurpolymj.2015.03.044

Mechanical unmasking followed by thiol capture is also established in a different
material: 2025 PEG hydrogels with anthracene-M links were mechanically patterned
and captured thiol-bearing cargo. This shows a chemical primitive in a soft
solvated network, not restored structural cross-seam load transfer. Substitution
of anthracene for F also changes the assembly, force response and photochemistry;
it is not an acquired improvement by name alone.
 https://doi.org/10.1039/D4MH00761A

Thermal leakage is a lifetime cost, not an automatic impossibility

Let D be a closed DA bridge, U opened/unlocked M, and L the intended thiol lock:
 D -> U at k_-, U -> D at kF, U -> L at kS.
Writing K=kF+kS and beta=kS/K, direct first-exit locking from U by dwell tau is
 beta(1-exp(-K tau)).
For intact storage starting D, the exact survival against conversion to L is
 S_D(t)=[lambda+ exp(-lambda- t)-lambda- exp(-lambda+ t)]/(lambda+-lambda-),
 lambda+/-=[k_-+K +/- sqrt((k_-+K)^2-4 k_- kS)]/2.
The repeated-root limit is exp(-lambda t)(1+lambda t).

When k_- is much smaller than K, the slow leak is approximately k_- beta.
Thus a measured very small thermal opening rate can preserve the reservoir even
with identical capture branching intact and after recontact. A changed cage or
access state is an additional resource only if actual thermal leakage otherwise
fails storage. If kS is shared, achieving chosen beta_I and beta_R through a
changed F recombination rate requires
 kF_I/kF_R = [beta_R/(1-beta_R)]/[beta_I/(1-beta_I)].
This rate ratio must be earned by measured geometry/chemistry. It is not granted
because one specimen is called damaged. See luna_one_probe/JANUS_KINETIC_AUDIT.md
and its independently integrated check. No fitted rates are assigned here.

Complete costs and the nearest test

The operation pays manufacture of two chemically distinct faces, site density
and stoichiometric reserve, initial DA assembly time/temperature, photocage
synthesis and uncaging light, optical access and photoproduct disposal or
retention, possible catalysts, unloading and recontact pressure/registration,
repair dwell, and any water/pH/ring-opening/drying step used to stabilize the
product. Oxidation/hydrolysis of stored SH/M, thermal rDA leakage and loss of
reservoir during service must be counted. No replenishment/reset chemistry has
been supplied. Thiosuccinimide is not universally permanent; the actual service
humidity, temperature and thiol environment matter.

The next gate is chemical, before a full mechanical claim: in the SAME proposed
host-bound sequence, measure retained cage through assembly; usable SH and DA/F/M
mass balance before/after a calibrated light fluence; intact aging; and free-M
yield under controlled fracture versus intact high-load controls. Then compare
separated and recontacted faces at the same delay, temperature and pressure.
Only after those states are acquired should full-cut mode-I fracture recovery
and load-spanning repaired-joint creep be compared with the same host repaired
by fresh multimaleimide/solvent, a conventional two-part repair, and thermal
repair. A higher initial recovery fraction alone does not establish a stronger
or more creep-resistant repaired joint.

Current decision

This route supplies an explicit prospective contact/state operation and an exact
storage/repair screening model. It does not yet supply one demonstrated compatible
strong material or an executable validated full cycle. The acquired result in
this unit is the conditional control obstruction and its precise escape
requirements. The material operation remains a testable proposal, not a success.
