First candidate: perturbation transport memory, with an adversarial scale gate

Question: Can unsteady lateral concentration perturbations materially change
finite-wavelength growth beyond the best conventional transient-base stability
prediction? An internal concentration field is a genuine physical state, but
its mere existence is not a new mechanism; diffusion-history and lateral
screening are old incumbents. A useful new result must survive a quantitative
comparison against their time-scale separation and optimized waveform control.

Consider only a CONTROLLED REDUCED LIMIT first: supporting-electrolyte Cu
diffusion, locally planar front, negligible transport advection, uniform ionic
potential at a nonzero transverse Fourier mode k, finite reaction derivatives
A=partial r/partial c_s>0 and B=-partial r/partial curvature>=0, molar volume
Omega. This is not yet a quantitative model of the selected acidified bath.
Let the base concentration gradient at a frozen time be g>0. For a steady local
base Czz=0, the perturbation w(z,t) and height h(t) obey at leading dilute order

 w_t=D(w_zz-k²w), z>0; w(infinity)=0;
 D w_z(0)=A w(0)+(Ag-Bk²)h;
 hdot=Omega[A w(0)+(Ag-Bk²)h].                         (1)

The sign convention is z increasing from metal into electrolyte; r>0 deposits
Cu, j_F=2F r, and V=Omega r. The curvature of a convex protrusion is k²h and
its reaction correction is -Bk²h. Coefficients must come from an explicitly
specified thermodynamically consistent reaction law, not an arbitrary damping.
The O(Omega c_s) moving-domain solute term or the compensating solvent motion
must be included or bounded in a full mass balance; (1) states the dilute limit.

Eliminating w in Laplace frequency s gives the exact linear surface admittance
D sqrt(k²+s/D), not the quasistatic Dk. An exponential interface mode obeys

 s=Omega(Ag-Bk²) D sqrt(k²+s/D)/[A+D sqrt(k²+s/D)].      (2)

For unstable d=Ag-Bk²>0, the positive root s is larger than its quasistatic
counterpart s_QS=Omega d Dk/(A+Dk). But its correction has a strong bound.
Using d<=Ag and the dilute base flux Dg=r gives s<=V sqrt(k²+s/D).
For Pe_k=V/(Dk),

 0 <= s/(Dk²) <= Pe_k²/2+Pe_k sqrt(1+Pe_k²/4)=:E,
 1 <= s/s_QS <= sqrt(1+E).                              (3)

Thus this particular unsteady eigenmode correction is uniformly O(Pe_k) over
all positive finite reaction rates and stabilizing capillarity. With nanoscale
wavelengths and a Cu front speed of order55nm/s, ordinary aqueous diffusivity
would make Pe_k very small. Values of D/wavelength still require source matching.
If so, I should REJECT my own favored perturbation-memory explanation for an
order-one change in this local exponential growth rate, not rename the state.
Equation(3) does not bound arbitrary initial solute perturbations, pulse-induced
transients/non-normal finite-time gains, a time-varying base, electromigration,
space charge, convection, nonlinear geometry or nucleation noise. Those are
separate tests, not silently excluded by a frozen eigenvalue calculation.

Mass and moving-boundary accounting gate

For a stagnant dilute-solution Stefan model with metal concentration c_M=1/Omega,
normal metal-to-liquid gradient, exact species jump conservation is
 D partial_n c=(c_M-c_s) v_n and j_F=2F c_M v_n.
Equivalently D partial_n c=r(1-Omega c_s). If instead solvent motion follows
the interface, the appropriate advective relative flux must be included and
the usual D partial_n c=r form can follow. These conventions cannot be mixed.
For0.1M Cu, Omega c is expected small, but its value and any moving-film
inventory correction must be charged numerically. A 2D projected area becomes
metal volume only with independently justified thickness/porosity; Faradaic
efficiency and beam/chemical etching are not supplied by a total-current trace.

Superseded extension, preserved 24 October

After this frozen construction I sent root a proposed global periodic-pulse
Jensen theorem using only the Czz=0 expression. Root correctly identified the
missing moving-boundary displacement term for a transient base. The extension
is WITHDRAWN. The correct dilute perturbation boundary is
 D[w_z+Czz h]=A[w+Cz h]-Bk²h.
In the quasistatic perturbation closure its additional growth term is
Omega[A/(A+Dk)] D Czz. For the fixed-domain diffusion base D Czz=c_s,t,
so its cycle integral is Omega int [r/(r+Dk c_s)] dc_s, not generally zero.
The exact concentration/pulse problem must retain both that term and the
frequency-dependent DN map. A nonlinear periodic spectral check is now being
constructed; neither all-waveform DC optimality nor a physical pulse advantage
is claimed. The narrower frozen-linear-base bound (2)--(3) is unaffected.
