TRANSIENT BASE STATE: THE DISPLACED-FLUX TERM IN Cu STABILITY
24 October 2026. Root independent derivation, sent to sharp_statistics.

Scope: dilute, supported-diffusion, locally planar fixed-domain approximation.
This is a model consistency calculation, not a measured Cu mechanism or a
novelty claim. Motion/advection, migration, multiple ions, noise, and actual
liquid-cell geometry are not acquired by this calculation.

Let z point into liquid from the mean metal surface, C(z,t) the planar base,
D C_z(0,t)=r(t), c(t)=C(0,t), g=C_z(0,t), q=C_zz(0,t).
Write a small transverse Fourier displacement h(t) exp(iky), and a fixed-z
concentration perturbation w(z,t) exp(iky). Reaction derivatives A>0 and B>=0
mean delta r=A delta c_surface-B k^2 h. Evaluating both surface concentration
AND normal flux on the displaced interface gives

  delta c_surface=w(0,t)+g h,
  delta r=D[w_z(0,t)+q h]
         =A[w(0,t)+g h]-B k^2 h,
  hdot=Omega delta r.                                      (1)

Thus a quasistatic lateral perturbation w=w0 exp(-kz) yields

  w0=-(Ag-Bk^2-Dq)h/(A+Dk),
  Gamma=hdot/h=Omega [Dk(Ag-Bk^2)+A Dq]/(A+Dk).            (2)

The second term cannot be dropped solely because w is quasistatic. It
vanishes for a spatially linear base q=0. In fixed-domain base diffusion,
Dq=c_t, so for irreversible Tafel A=r/c, B=alpha*Gamma_cap*r, define
d=Dk and beta=alpha*Gamma_cap*k. Then

 Gamma=Omega*k*[(1+beta)r^2/(r+d*c)-beta*r]
       +Omega*[r/(r+d*c)]*c_t.                            (3)

The first summand is jointly convex in (r,c), as quadratic-over-linear plus
a linear term. For a periodic fixed film with C(L,t)=c_inf, base conservation
fixes mean(c)=c_inf-L*mean(r)/D. Jensen therefore bounds the FIRST summand
by its DC value. It does not bound the full (3) until the cycle integral

       Omega * integral_cycle [r/(r+d*c)] dc              (4)

is controlled. Mean(c_t)=0 alone is insufficient because the weight varies.

For example take a small harmonic flux around a positive DC state. Write
delta r=Re(R exp(i omega t)), delta c=-Re(Z(omega) R exp(i omega t)),
where Z is the causal surface diffusion impedance. With r0,c0>0 and
Q=r0+d*c0, the leading mean of the second summand is

  -Omega * [d*c0/Q^2] * |R|^2*omega*(-Im Z)/2.             (5)

For the half-space oscillatory problem Z=(i omega D)^(-1/2), this is negative.
That example concerns a stationary oscillatory part around a separately
maintained base; a nonzero DC half-space flux does not itself yield a
stationary base. A finite film gives Z=tanh(L sqrt(i omega/D))/sqrt(i omega D).

No pulse advantage follows from (5). The first summand has a positive
second-order convexity cost and the quasistatic perturbation approximation
itself requires the forcing bandwidth to be small relative to D*k^2.
For beta=0 its positive second-order cost is

 Omega*k*d^2*|c0+r0*Z|^2*|R|^2/(2 Q^3).                 (6)

In the formal half-space oscillatory impedance, set x=r0/(d*c0)>=0 and
y=sqrt(omega/(2 D k^2)). The sign of (5)+(6), after a positive factor, is

    1+x/y+x^2/(2 y^2)-(1+x)y.                            (7)

For 0<y<=1 this is nonnegative: 1-y>=0, x(1/y-y)>=0, and the
quadratic term is nonnegative. Capillarity beta>=0 adds a nonnegative
convexity contribution. This is only a local small-amplitude check in a
specified approximation, not a global periodic-control theorem. At y~1 the
quasistatic perturbation approximation is not automatically accurate.

A constructive next calculation is the full coupled periodic-base and
perturbation Floquet problem, with charge/mean flux/peak current/time and
concentration positivity matched, followed by a finite-bandwidth or other
rigorous limit if a clean inequality survives. Existing time-dependent
electrodeposition stability is the comparator, not an invention claim.

6. Full lateral-diffusion correction and an analytic sign example

Root's cu_periodic_boundary_check.py independently solves the full harmonic
Dirichlet-to-Neumann boundary problem at leading Omega, and also continues
the morphological Floquet branch at finite Omega in the SAME fixed-domain
dilute model. It does not restore omitted mean-front advection, exact Stefan
terms or actual bath transport. All numbers below are illustrative units.

For D=L=1, beta=0 and positive but small r0,k, the exact small-amplitude
coefficient of cycle growth divided by Omega has the limit

       coefficient -> Re[sech^2(sqrt(i omega))]/(2 c0)
                       as r0 -> 0+, k -> 0+.

To verify: A0 -> 0, A1 -> 1/c0, Z=tanh(z)/z, K1=z*coth(z),
and the first boundary harmonic tends to w1=-tanh^2(z), z=sqrt(i omega).
The second-order mean rate is [1+Re(w1)]/(2 c0).

Choose omega=pi^2/2, so z=(pi/2)(1+i). Then
cosh(z)=i*sinh(pi/2), and the limiting coefficient is exactly

              -csch^2(pi/2)/(2 c0) < 0.                 (8)

Continuity gives finite positive r0 and k with negative coefficient, while
a sufficiently small forcing amplitude compared with r0 keeps all currents
positive. The transverse ZERO mode itself is excluded: its galvanostatic
potential feedback differs. The limit uses nonzero k approaching zero on a
correspondingly wide finite periodic electrode. This is an analytic local
counterexample to a blanket DC-local-optimality statement in this reduced
finite-film model, not a universal pulse benefit or an optimized protocol.

An explicit numerical point uses D=L=c0=k=1, omega=10^0.75,r0=.001,beta=0.
The analytic small-amplitude coefficient is -.05739643353957228. Nonlinear
periodic boundary solves with 8/16/32 harmonics and amplitudes .0002,.0001,
.00005 reproduce it. At amplitude .0002, a finite-Omega continuation for
Omega=.001,.01,.1 gives about -.002297606 relative rate change from DC;
the nonzero height harmonics are retained. Residuals are <1e-12. This does
not establish a globally dominant branch outside the small-Omega continuation
regime, physical calibration, net energy advantage, or meaningful flux.
