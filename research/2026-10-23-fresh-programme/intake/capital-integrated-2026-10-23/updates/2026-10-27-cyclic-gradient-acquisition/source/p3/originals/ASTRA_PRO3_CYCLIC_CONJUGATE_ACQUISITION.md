# Astra Pro3 — Cyclic acquisition, dual potential transfer and anisotropic feedback

26 October 2026. This is a new mathematical/software research continuation of the retained response-family and inverse-service work. All equations and finite controls are conditional on explicitly supplied source and instrument contracts. In particular, **passing an interpolation test does not establish that the actual unobserved source satisfies the advertised physical class**. Smooth-convex interpolation, cyclic monotonicity, Fenchel conjugacy, max-plus path algorithms and the use of potential shifts are established mathematics. The contribution being investigated is their executable use as a source-to-new-experiment compiler, with revealing failure certificates. An equally informed classical retaining procedure can implement the same algorithms and tie every guarantee.

## 1. What was acquired and what remains supplied

Native apparatus: choose a position `x ∈ R^d` and receive only the full gradient `g=∇E(x)`. There is **no** native force-controlled inverse, energy measurement, Hessian oracle or source formula supplied to the learner. Historical position/gradient pairs `(x_i,g_i)` are retained exactly in the first construction. The physical contract, which must be independently warranted, is a single stable `C^{1,1}` potential with

`0 < μ I ⪯ ∇²E ⪯ L I`, interpreted a.e. for `C^{1,1}` functions.

The gradient-only data identify neither the additive constant of `E` nor the absolute value of its conjugate `G=E*`. They can, however, support exact finite **joint source-consistency tests**, sharp relative conjugate-energy ranges, previously unknown force-controlled equilibrium questions, and—in some circumstances—decisive falsification of the proposed conservative source class.

### Exact gradient-only interpolation theorem

For each old native observation, reverse the role of input and output: `z_i=g_i`, `h_i=x_i`. If the source contract is true, the dual potential `G=E*` has Hessian bounds `[α I, β I]`, where `α=1/L`, `β=1/μ`. Write `d=β−α>0`, `H_i=h_i−α z_i`, and define the exact rational directed edge weight

`w[j→i] = H_j·(z_i−z_j) + ||H_i−H_j||²/(2d)`.

The finite native record (or a portfolio extended by proposed future force/equilibrium pairs) can come from **one** source in the declared class if and only if there exist scalar dual-potential variables `φ_i` such that

`φ_i−φ_j >= w[j→i]` for every ordered pair.

Proof: subtract `α||z||²/2` from `G` to obtain a convex `d`-smooth `φ`. The Taylor–Hendrickx–Glineur finite smooth-convex interpolation theorem gives precisely these inequalities, with their potentials retained as free variables because energies were never measured. Conversely, every satisfying set of `φ_i` yields a global smooth, strongly convex dual interpolant and hence an original energy `E=G*` satisfying all native gradient observations. This is an exact **source-family** statement; not a fit or a claim about a narrower hidden microscopic class.

All such potentials exist iff **no directed cycle has strictly positive total weight**. Summing edge inequalities proves necessity; rational longest-path potentials prove sufficiency. A modified Bellman–Ford algorithm either constructs all `φ_i` or returns an explicit positive-cycle witness. Dense time is `O(N³)` rational operations on `N` sites, apart from coefficient-bit costs; no enumeration of exponentially many cycles is necessary for *consistency testing*. Duplicated locations and exact degeneracies are handled.

An explicit compatible-source constructor is available. Given feasible potentials, let

`ψ(y)=max_i { z_i·y − φ_i + ||y−H_i||²/(2d) }`.

Then `ψ` is `(1/d)`-strongly convex, `φ=ψ*` is convex and `d`-smooth, `G(z)=α||z||²/2+φ(z)` is `[α,β]`-curved, and `E=G*` is `[μ,L]`-curved. At each acquired point `H_i`, the `i`th quadratic in `ψ` is active by the interpolation inequalities; conjugacy yields `∇G(z_i)=h_i`. The scripts check these activation identities exactly. This is the classical constructive interpolation proof specialized to native scientific observations.

## 2. Decisive source-contract falsification

Three controlled position/force readings were generated by a stable, invertible but **nonconservative linear** source. Written in dual coordinates, its inverse map is

`h(z) = [[2/3, −4/15],[4/15,2/3]] z`.

The positions are the controlled native inputs; the forces are actually returned by the nonsymmetric matrix inverse, not assumed available through a native inverse oracle. Every pair of readings passes the smooth-strongly-convex interpolation condition for `μ=1,L=3`. Yet one directed three-cycle has exact positive weight **`11/75`**. Hence **no globally conservative `[1,3]`-curved potential can produce the entire transcript**. This rejects an untrue candidate physical contract without fitting any global model.

If each measured force coordinate has arbitrary deterministic error bounded by `η=10^-3`, the *primal* interpolation cycle remains positive. With `D=L−μ=2`, positions `x_i` exact, reported residual slopes `R_i=ĝ_i−μ x_i`, and directed cycle edges `j→i`, the change in cycle weight obeys

`|Δweight| <= η Σ_edges ||x_i−x_j||_1 + (2η/D) Σ_edges ||R_i−R_j||_1 + (2d_space η²/D) N_edges`.

This follows by expanding each quadratic edge term and bounding `||e_i−e_j||²<=4 d_space η²`. On the executed 3-cycle the certified downward perturbation is `7609/1500000`; the surviving incompatibility margin is **`70797/500000 ≈ 0.141594`**. Error correlations are unrestricted within the declared coordinate boxes.

A stronger four-site result defeats *every triple-only test*. On the native positions corresponding to dual force sites `(0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)`, use the nonconservative inverse map

`h(z) = [[2/3, −1/5],[1/5,2/3]] z`.

All **four** possible three-site subsets have feasible smooth-strongly-convex potentials; exact max-of-quadratics source witnesses are retained separately for each. Yet the complete four-site graph has a positive **four-cycle of weight `14/75`**. With coordinate-force error `10^-3` the surviving rejection margin remains **`67897/375000 ≈ 0.181059`**. Four native vector readings return eight force scalars. The result does **not** establish that four are universally minimal for detecting every nonconservative source; it establishes a specific transcript on which any triple-only acceptance rule is unsound.

**Fundamental converse failure:** no finite collection of successful gradient tests can *prove* that an unrestricted unknown smooth field is conservative everywhere. A smooth compactly supported rotational perturbation away from every sampled input matches the entire transcript, while introducing nonzero curl. Its amplitude can be made small enough to preserve quantitative strong monotonicity. The compiler therefore uses a positive cycle to **reject**, and uses a nonpositive-cycle result only to certify **compatibility conditional on the source contract**, never physical truth of that contract.

## 3. Sharp, previously unknown inverse equilibrium response

After only two native gradient calls at positions `x_0=(0,0)` and `x_1=(2/3,4/15)`, suppose a later task asks for the horizontal coordinate `u` of the unknown equilibrium at applied force `z_2=(0,1)`. The returned old gradients were `(0,0)` and `(1,0)`. Write the unknown equilibrium as `(u,v)`. The only pairwise cycle tests allow the **sharp** interval `u∈[-1/3,1/3]`.

Keeping the whole jointly consistent source instead yields four convex quadratic cycle constraints, including

`(3u²+3v²−4v+1)/2 <=0`, and

`(75u²−25u+75v²−120v+42)/50 <=0`.

The first disk gives `u<=1/3`; the second is a disk centred at `(1/6,4/5)` with radius `sqrt(97)/30`, giving `u>=(5−sqrt(97))/30`. Both limiting candidates satisfy every remaining cycle inequality. Hence the **exact sharp interval** over the stated source class is

`(5−sqrt(97))/30 <= u <= 1/3`, approximately `[-0.161629,0.333333]`.

Its width is about **25.8% smaller** than the pairwise-only width, with **zero additional native source calls** when the later question arrives. Sharpness means realizable by globally compatible smooth strongly convex interpolants, not only feasible in a numerical relaxation. One separate source-aware quadratic checker has true `u=4/15`, which lies inside the interval; its formula was never given to the learner.

### Executed general-purpose rational cycle cutter

For a future fixed force `z` and unknown equilibrium `h`, every directed cycle is a convex quadratic function `q_C(h)<=0`; its Hessian is positive semidefinite. Given an infeasible rational candidate, the positive-cycle checker finds a violated constraint and a supporting tangent. The resulting linear inequality holds for **every** compatible future equilibrium, giving a sound *outer* cutting-plane compiler. All LP vertices are computed by rational line intersections in this two-dimensional control.

The first unrestricted rational version failed by extreme coefficient growth near the irrational boundary. The repaired version rounds each tangent normal to a **24-fractional-bit dyadic grid**, then outward-inflates the right-hand side over the guaranteed initial equilibrium box. Each rounded inequality therefore remains rigorously valid. To avoid repeatedly cutting with negligible two-cycle violations, the two-dimensional implementation chooses the strongest violated cycle from the five simple candidates. General compatibility testing itself remains polynomial via Bellman–Ford; finding the maximally violated cycle in a large graph is **not** claimed efficient by this implementation.

**Actually executed:** 12 retained rational cuts give a source-valid lower bound `−150311252247/929971507927 ≈ −0.161629954214`, and a compatible rational global-source witness exists at `−40407/250000=−0.161628`. Thus the returned *certified bracket on the sharp lower endpoint* has width `≈1.9543×10^-6`, with maximum cut-coefficient numerator/denominator bit length **24**. The exact analytic sharp endpoint lies inside. The cuts are checked by their exact quadratic tangent remainders and safe rounding margins. This is an approximate computational certificate of a known sharp result, not an assertion of exact rational attainment of an irrational extremum.

## 4. Additional transfer: source energy differences without an energy sensor

For a **fixed** set of previously observed inverse pairs `(z_i,h_i)`, define `D_ij` to be the maximum sum of directed weights along any path `i→j`. No positive cycles ensures finiteness. With the dual energy shift `G(z)=φ(z)+||z||²/(2L)`, the exact sharp compatible-source range of any *relative* conjugate energy is

`D_ij + (||z_j||²−||z_i||²)/(2L) <= G(z_j)-G(z_i) <= -D_ji + (||z_j||²−||z_i||²)/(2L)`.

Each endpoint is attainable by setting the unknown potentials to longest-path distances from `i` or `j`, followed by the explicit globally compatible conjugate-source construction. This is a genuine no-new-source-call transfer to energy-like operations despite having **measured gradients only**.

In the exact rational two-port source control, native position inputs were initially `x0=0` and `x1=(2/3,4/15)`. Every later acquisition position was selected as a **known convex combination of these controlled inputs**, not by requesting an unavailable desired force. The native apparatus returned the corresponding gradient, which was retained. A later, previously unspecified dual-potential-difference task then yielded:

| Native position→gradient calls | Energy channels used | Sharp range of `G(g1)−G(g0)` | Width |
|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 2 | 0 | `[91/300,109/300]` | `3/50=0.06` |
| 3 (one midpoint) | 0 | `[191/600,209/600]` | `3/100=0.03` |
| 5 (three interiors) | 0 | `[391/1200,409/1200]` | `3/200=0.015` |

The independent quadratic checker gives the true difference `1/3`, inside all bounds. A conventional retaining interpolation method with the same gradient record obtains the same intervals. Adding **one separately charged exact energy measurement** at an old site anchors the otherwise arbitrary energy constant and converts these relative intervals into absolute dual-potential intervals. Repeating gradients alone cannot determine the absolute energy gauge.

## 5. Further construction: symmetric matrix feedback specified only later

After the source has expired, a new controller can request equilibria of

`∇E(x)−Kx=p`, with **symmetric** `K ≺ μ I` not specified during acquisition. Define `E_K(x)=E(x)−x^T K x/2` and the positive definite matrices

`A=μI−K`, `B=LI−K`, `C=B^-1`, `D=A^-1−B^-1`.

The dual `G_K=E_K*` has exactly the matrix Hessian interval `[C,C+D]`. Subtract `z^T C z/2` and transform by a conceptual square root of `D` to reduce its finite interpolation conditions to the same scalar 1-smooth convex theorem. **The executable uses only rational `D^-1`, not any irrational square-root interface.** Historical jets change algebraically to `z_i=g_i−Kx_i`, `h_i=x_i`. Define `H_i=h_i−Cz_i` and weights

`w_K[j→i]=H_j·(z_i−z_j)+(H_i−H_j)^T D^-1 (H_i−H_j)/2`.

No positive cycle is necessary **and sufficient** for a single global energy satisfying all stored native gradients and the proposed new equilibrium portfolio for this one fixed `K`. The same max-of-quadratics conjugate construction, with matrix metric `D^-1`, gives an explicit compatible source. This preserves full anisotropic controller information instead of bounding `K` by its largest eigenvalue. It also gives the same standard interpolation conditions when `K=kI`, confirmed by an exact regression test.

A further exact one-jet geometry: set `M=(μ+L)I/2−K`, `r=(L−μ)/2`, and `d=p−(g_i−Kx_i)`. Every compatible future equilibrium displacement `q=x−x_i` satisfies

`||d−Mq||² <= r²||q||²`.

This is an explicitly computable positive-definite ellipsoid when `K≺μI`. It is **sharp for one retained gradient jet**: every point in it is realized by some quadratic source with Hessian in `[μI,LI]`. With additional old jets it remains a valid outer bound, not necessarily the sharp full-history region. The saved script supplies its rational centre, metric and squared coordinate half-widths, and checks the independent source equilibrium against it.

**Executed:** three old native full-gradient readings (six returned scalars) were retained before controllers were chosen. Three later rational symmetric matrices `K` (two with nonzero off-diagonal entries) and two later loads each produced six force-controlled tasks with **zero additional learner native readings**. All true source equilibria passed exact rational anisotropic interpolation and one-jet ellipsoid checks. Checker evaluations are recorded separately. The physical source remains the same symmetric positive quadratic across all six checks.

**Boundary when future controllers differ:** exact per-controller compatibility does not justify taking a Cartesian product across different `K`. From the single old native gradient `g(0)=0`, a first later controller `k1=0` and load 1 can have equilibrium `x1=1` under `E1=x²/2`. A second controller `k2=1/2` and load 1 can have equilibrium `x2=2/5` under `E2=3x²/2`. Both are individually admissible with `1<=E''<=3`, but jointly imply `g(1)=1` and `g(2/5)=6/5`, contradicting monotonicity (indeed `(g1−g2)(x1−x2)=−3/25 < 9/25`). A single common source cannot realize them. The nonlinear joint mixed-feedback problem is not solved by the separate convex reductions. Nonsymmetric `K` likewise cannot generally be absorbed into a scalar energy; the earlier sector compiler is the appropriate different model.

## 6. Scope, costs, comparators, and genuine limitations

* The exact smooth-gradient conditions are **conditional on global source stability, conservative structure and independent μ/L curvature warrants**. Finite tests can sometimes falsify these claims but never infer them universally from their own finite success.
* Exact native gradient readings are used in the compatibility/energy-difference/feedback transfer results. The corrupted-source falsifiers specifically accommodate **coordinate-bounded force errors** in a conservative rejection test. General noisy-source *acceptance* and sharp noise-aware joint interpolation with uncertain dual force coordinates are not claimed; they can become nonconvex.
* Absolute conjugate energies are unidentifiable without an energy anchor. A new native energy channel cannot be relabeled as an existing gradient channel.
* Fixed-force future gradient unknowns yield a convex interpolation problem when historical dual coordinates are exact. The 2D cycle-cutter supplies a certified outer computational solution, not an efficient general optimizer for arbitrarily many future loads. Rational bit costs and cut selection are charged.
* Anisotropic exact transfer is **per fixed symmetric late controller**. Joint portfolios across changing controllers, nonsymmetric controllers, transient physical trajectories, hidden calibration gauges and microscopic constraints beyond `[μ,L]` remain separate problems.
* The controller/source matrices of synthetic fixtures are available only to checker code; they are **not retained by the learner**. Checker inversions and symbolic computations are recorded distinctly from charged native observations.
* This is not a priority claim for the classical interpolation, conjugation, cyclic monotonicity, graph algorithms or convex cutting-plane ingredients. An equally informed retainer can adopt the source-family representation and match the zero-new-call operations. Earlier scalar and vector sector compilers remain applicable under **different** physical interfaces.
