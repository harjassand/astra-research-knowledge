# Time-dependent nearest-neighbour path tail for a boundary-bond truncation

## Result

Let a finite 1D chain have a piecewise-continuous, time-dependent Hamiltonian

\[
H(t)=\sum_x g_x(t)+\sum_x h_{x,x+1}(t),\qquad \|h_{x,x+1}(t)\|\le J.
\]

Onsite terms can be removed by the product onsite interaction picture. The transformed bond terms remain supported on the same two sites and have the same operator-norm bound (J). Write (\tau_{t,s}) for Heisenberg evolution, and define

\[
F_d(z)=\sum_{n=d}^{\infty}\frac{z^n}{n!}.
\]

For local (A_X,B_Y), let (d_I(X,Y)=0) when (X\cap Y\ne\varnothing). Otherwise let (d_I(X,Y)\ge1) be the least number (n) of bond terms in a chain (Z_1,\ldots,Z_n) such that (Z_1\cap X\ne\varnothing), each consecutive pair overlaps, and (Z_n\cap Y\ne\varnothing). The time-ordered recursion and the degree-three bond-overlap count give

\[
\big\|[\tau_{t,s}(A_X),B_Y]\big\|
\le 2\|A_X\|\|B_Y\|\,F_{d_I(X,Y)}\!\left(\frac{6J|t-s|}{\hbar}\right).
\tag{1}
\]

For disjoint supports the series starts at (n=d_I\), because all shorter touching paths vanish. If the supports overlap, the (n=0) term covers the initial commutator. The bound is uniform in chain length and in the time dependence of the bond terms.

## Boundary-bond truncation

Take the interaction-picture boundary bond (A=h_{0,1}(t_*)), with \(\|A\|\le J\), and evolve it under the block Hamiltonian with that boundary bond omitted. Restrict this evolution to the (2\ell\)-site window

\[
\Lambda_\ell=\{-\ell+1,\ldots,\ell\},\qquad \ell\ge1,
\]
which contains \(\ell\) sites on each side of the cut. The only omitted terms touching the window are the two crossing bonds (h_{-\ell,-\ell+1}) and (h_{\ell,\ell+1}). For either term the interaction-path distance from the central bond is \(d_I=\max(0,\ell-1)\). Duhamel's formula, (1), and

\[
\int_0^T F_d\!\left(\frac{6Jr}{\hbar}\right)dr
=\frac{\hbar}{6J}F_{d+1}\!\left(\frac{6JT}{\hbar}\right)
\]

therefore give the explicit dressed-bond error

\[
\left\|\tau_{T,0}^{H_0}(A)-\tau_{T,0}^{H_{0,\Lambda_\ell}}(A)\right\|
\le \frac{2J}{3}\,F_\ell\!\left(\frac{6JT}{\hbar}\right).
\tag{2}
\]

This convention is important: with a (2\ell)-site window, the nearest omitted crossing-bond support is \(\ell-1\) bond steps from the central bond, and the Duhamel time integral raises the tail index to \(\ell\). At \(\ell=1\), use \(d_I=0\) and the same formula remains valid.

The factorial tail is explicit; for (d\ge0),

\[
F_d(z)\le e^z\frac{z^d}{d!}.
\]

Thus at fixed (JT/\hbar), (2) is superexponentially small in \(\ell\). If the interaction-picture gate is generated by a sum of (K) dressed cut bonds, Duhamel for the gate evolution gives

\[
\|U_I(T)-U_{I,\ell}(T)\|
\le \frac{K}{\hbar}\int_0^T\frac{2J}{3}F_\ell\!\left(\frac{6Js}{\hbar}\right)ds
=\frac K9F_{\ell+1}\!\left(\frac{6JT}{\hbar}\right).
\tag{3}
\]

The localized windows around cuts are disjoint when the intervening block has at least (2\ell) sites (use (2\ell+1) if a strict gap is desired); together with the exact within-block propagators, this gives a depth-two block circuit (C). The on-site frame and within-block propagators fold into the first layer. For a bounded observable (O),

\[
\|U^\dagger O U-C^\dagger O C\|
\le 2\|O\|\,\|U-C\|
\le \frac{2K}{9}\|O\|F_{\ell+1}\!\left(\frac{6JT}{\hbar}\right).
\tag{4}
\]

For (K=15), the prefactor in (4) is (10/3). A channel or measurement metric needs its own conversion factor.

## Derivation and source scope

Hastings defines the commutator envelope (C_B(X,t)), derives the recursive inequality (Eq. (24)), and iterates it into an ordered interaction-path series (Eq. (27)). In the finite-range, two-site-interaction case, Sec. II.B identifies the path sum and its factorial weights. The notes explicitly say the proof adapts to time-dependent Hamiltonians. Specializing that recursion here: at most three nearest-neighbour bonds touch a given bond, so the total norm weight of every length-(n) path is at most \((3J)^n\). The commutator recursion contributes \((2/\hbar)^n\); the ordered time simplex has volume \(|t-s|^n/n!\). This yields (1) with no dimension factor. Applying the same bound to the two Duhamel boundary terms gives (2).

The source is Matthew B. Hastings, “Locality in Quantum Systems,” Les Houches 2010 lecture notes, PDF pp. 3–5 (time-dependent-adaptation statement; Eqs. (24), (27), and Sec. II.B): [primary text](https://www.thphys.uni-heidelberg.de/~salmhofer/leshouches2010/leshouches2010_hastings.pdf). Hastings labels the notes preliminary and sketches the proof; the constants (3^n), (6J/\hbar), the (d_I\) convention, and (2) are the explicit specialization derived above, not constants quoted verbatim from the notes. The previously acquired Osborne source establishes the block-circuit architecture but does not instantiate these constants. No physical system or experiment is inferred here.
