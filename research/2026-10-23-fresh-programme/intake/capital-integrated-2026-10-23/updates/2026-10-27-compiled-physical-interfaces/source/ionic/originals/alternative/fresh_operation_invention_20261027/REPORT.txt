The locked question asked whether time-programmed finite-inventory ionic capture/release could improve useful recovery, purity and complete-cycle resources without assuming ion-specific equilibrium affinity. The shortest missing operation was not another optimized waveform: it was a source-depletion/recycling calculation with an equally informed conventional fractionation comparator.

Constructed results (full assumptions and proofs in DERIVATIONS.md):

1. Complete-stream conservation. If constant-composition feed capture has the common-factor form b_i=a_i c_i h(t), arbitrary finite-capacity saturation and timing cannot change the complete released product ratio from a_L c_L/(a_N c_N) on a closed bed cycle. Routing only selected fractions changes the operation and must be charged.

2. One-wash frontier. For an initially equal-affinity loading, arbitrary species-blind wash timing/fractionation has the sharp Neyman-Pearson frontier r_N=r_L^rho, rho=a_N/a_L>1. Here r_L is recovery of initially LOADED L, not uncharged whole-source recovery.

3. Finite-source turnover lower bound. A single fixed-volume source, bed capacity B, no other inventory/sink, initially/finally empty bed, and common-factor capture imply

  A_N >= rho max(0,N0 - R L0(1-p)/p - B) log[1/(1-R)].

This survives arbitrary shared waveforms, nonlinear vacancy, recycling, and even a granted ideal species-resolved product router. It is a gross capture-count bound, not an energy bound. If N release <= a_N k_off,max bound inventory, then T >= A_N/(a_N k_off,max B). The rate and capacity must be physically acquired before deriving a clock time. Other source preprocessing, sodium discard, additional pools, large stored inventories and spatial transport lie outside the proof.

4. Exact best ONE-SOURCE infinitesimal-cohort wash-and-recycle policy. Set gamma=1/(rho-1), X=log[1/(1-R)], V=log[N0/(N0-P_N)]/rho and

  I=(gamma/rho)(exp(rho V/gamma)-1).

When X/I>=1, the globally optimal gross background capture count is

  A_N,min = rho N0 X^(gamma+1)/I^gamma,

attained by ordinary adaptive wash staging with w(v)=(X/I)exp(rho v/gamma), u=1/w and H=log(w)/(a_N-a_L). All recycled wash returns to ONE well-mixed source; separate enriched or waste pools are excluded. This restriction is indispensable.

Illustrative consequence, NOT acquired Li/Na binding kinetics: a_L/a_N=103/133, initial L/N=1e-4, R=.9, p=.99. The one-source ideal optimum is 7.6887953e26 background captures per recovered target. A constant-wash conventional one-source policy differs by only 4.4465e-14 relative; ordinary adaptive one-source staging attains the exact optimum. One-wash recovery of initially loaded target at that purity is <=2.6001e-21. For the more general turnover theorem and B=100 L0, the separate lower bound is 32705.62 captures/target. No count was converted to joules, dollars or physical speed.

Countercurrent escape and comparator correction:

The root correctly challenged treating one-source recycling as ordinary multistage separation. A countercurrent cascade carries composition/history in separately enriched inventories and can avoid the enormous one-source penalty. The root reports a conditional 89-stage gambler's-ruin construction with >.909 target recovery, >.9955 purity and <=1.693e6 total captures/target, under complete nonselective cohort capture/elution and routed-pool interfaces. This worker did not audit or reproduce that result. It is a valid scope escape as described; those additional physical interfaces, beds, storage and full-cycle costs remain unacquired. Neither number establishes a matched-bed/hardware/work advantage. The one-source optimum is NOT a lower bound on countercurrent cascades or general ion separation.

The astronomical optimum also assumes sequential infinitesimal capture->wash->elute cohorts. It is not a global optimal-control theorem for concurrent capture/rebinding, arbitrary internal kinetic networks, or continuously operating spatial devices, even with one source. Only the weaker general turnover inequality extends to arbitrary common capture waveforms under its inventory/sink assumptions. That inequality alone does not establish practical impossibility. No broader negative theorem was constructed.
