## Candidate operation and boundary

The candidate is a single-use sequence: capture PFAS on strong-base anion-exchange resin; record breakthrough and matrix uptake; apply measured staged rinsing and deoxygenation; then irradiate the **wet loaded resin** in a thin cassette with an absorbed-dose map. Retain and assay the resin, rinse, regenerant, cassette walls, filters and gas. Close whole-fluorine recovery alongside parent PFAS, inorganic fluoride, residual organic fluorine, sulfur and products.

The source anchors capture with a reported PSR2 Plus chloride example. Its adsorption and breakthrough observations do not establish loaded-resin cleavage. Bound-resin fluoride yield, selective encounter after loading/rinsing, spent-material burden and capacity/selectivity after dose are all **unacquired**. The process remains a hypothesis; no physical operation, destruction yield or advantage is established.

## Rate transfer and material/energy accounting

The aqueous source reports a parent-disappearance coefficient of `0.0184 kGy^-1` for 0.1 mg/L PFOS in neutral, deaerated solution. Exponential transfer gives a conditional `D90 ≈ 125.14 kGy`; the corresponding `34.76 Wh/kg` and `≈34.76 kWh/m^3` at that dilute concentration are calculations for transferred parent loss. They are not a measured resin dose, fluoride-yield dose, full-mineralization dose or physical lower bound. The source's separate observations of high parent removal below 500 kGy and near-complete defluorination only around 1000 kGy under optimized conditions further show why parent disappearance cannot stand in for fluorine yield.

The full boundary includes missed PFOS, rinse loss, resin and wet-mass ratios, absorbed-dose nonuniformity, wall-plug efficiency, pumping, capture, transfer, regeneration, replacement and spent-resin treatment. Unresolved fluorine remains unresolved, not destroyed. The collision-share fixture `f_P = k_P C_P/(k_P C_P + Σ_i k_i C_i)` is conditional: uniformly scaling PFOS and scavengers leaves the share unchanged, so selective retention or scavenger removal is needed to improve it. Fixture values are model calculations, not resin measurements or productive-cleavage observations.
