# PFAS chemical-capability source check

Scope: primary evidence for actual C–F/C–S cleavage of PFOS or another perfluoroalkyl sulfonate in water. Checked 2026-10-23. This is a bounded source note, not a feasibility or novelty claim.

## Findings

**Best selective-encounter result at low feed concentration, but unresolved fluorine closure:** Chong et al., [“Complete defluorination of PFASs via photocatalytic reduction in water,” *Nature Communications* 17, 3081 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-69933-9), with its [supplementary information](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-026-69933-9/MediaObjects/41467_2026_69933_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

- **Material and operation:** potassium PFOS salt (98%) in water; 0.2 g/L TAPP aggregates; 6 h dark pre-equilibration, then 48 h visible irradiation (≥420 nm; 500 W Xe source, reported intensity 70 mW/cm²). The aggregates have positive zeta potential (+48.7 mV); the authors attribute capture to electrostatic/H-bond interaction with the sulfonate and van der Waals contact with the fluorinated tail. Initial pH at 0.1 ppm is reported as about 6.5 in SI Fig. 25. Dissolved oxygen was not controlled in the standard aqueous test; SI EPR indicates TAPP can reduce dissolved O₂ to OOH• and that adding PFOS suppresses that signal.
- **Evidence for transformation:** IC reports fluoride accumulation; LC-MS/MS and ¹⁹F NMR report loss of parent PFOS; TOF-SIMS reports no residual PFOS on the solid. The no-catalyst light control and TPP/TCyPP analogues have negligible fluoride; AgNO₃ suppresses defluorination to <5%, supporting an electron-mediated C–F cleavage mechanism. At early conversion, LC-MS/MS identifies fluorinated fragments and short-chain fluorinated acids. The paper reports 99.8% defluorination in 48 h and >90% over five cycles. It also reports spiked tap/NOM/IOM tests and a 0.5 L spiked natural-solar reactor (three days); these are not contaminated field waters.
- **Material caveat:** the text names PFOS-K as the starting salt, gives an initial 0.1 ppm without an explicit ppm basis, and reports 0.10 ± 0.01 ppm F⁻ as the “theoretical maximum.” If the input means 0.1 mg/L PFOS-K mass, its formula gives about 0.060 mg/L fluorine (about 0.0647 mg/L for 0.1 mg/L PFOS acid). Those are conditional mass-basis calculations; they do not establish the experiment's actual dose basis or an erroneous measurement. SI Note 3 identifies ¹³C₄-PFOS at 0.05 ppm as the LC-MS internal standard; it does not establish that labeled PFOS was in the reaction feed and cannot presently resolve an IC fluoride balance. SI Fig. 24 also says 0.1 ppm falls to 0.015 ppm and labels that 98.5% removal; those values arithmetically imply 85% removal, so preserve the raw values and flag the internal mismatch. The authors did check parent recovery from TAPP by dissolving the solid in 50:50 acetonitrile after reaction, then found no PFOS by LC-MS/MS. However, there is no whole-system total-F/organic-F balance or sulfur-product balance. The SI explicitly notes that background DIW organic carbon (0.811 ± 0.073 ppm) and catalyst-synthesis organics are comparable to the <0.8 ppm terminal-acid products; oxalate/lactate concentrations are below their reliable HPLC quantification limits. Thus the encounter-plus-cleavage mechanism is already published, but “complete mineralization” is not yet mass-closed on the reported record.
- **Cost/recovery:** five-cycle reuse is demonstrated, but catalyst recovery/regeneration burden and leaching are not quantified. The authors report 3.99 kWh/m³ as a normalized energy comparison; the actual bench experiment uses the 500 W lamp for 48 h, while the outdoor pilot uses natural sunlight. No metered full-scale energy, solvent recovery, or treatment cost is established.

**Strongest PFOS-specific closed-balance destruction in this set, for concentrated waste rather than dilute water:** Strijckmans et al., [“A generic solvothermal defluorination method for carboxylate and sulfonate PFAS,” *Chemical Science* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1039/D6SC03001G); [PubMed/PMC record and article text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13334274/).

- The table explicitly includes **PFOS**, not just PFOA: 0.5 M fluorine content, 8 M KOH, 220 °C, 6 or 4 h. Adding 50 vol% methanol raises reported inorganic-fluoride yield from 29% (no methanol) to 99%; reported carbon balance is 99% and sulfur balance >99%. IC shows concurrent fluoride and sulfate release for PFOS. The authors propose hydroxide attack at the α-carbon/C–S bond, followed by defluorination; methanol is assigned roles in retaining volatile intermediates and enabling hydride-assisted pathways. This is high-concentration PFOS feed (0.5 M F corresponds to roughly 0.029 M PFOS, derived from 17 F per molecule), in a sealed heated pressure reactor, not a dilute environmental-water test. The paper describes operation near 60 bar; no PFOS-specific oxygen dependence is established. It is the most convincing direct PFOS C–S plus C–F closure located here.
- No catalyst is required, but the process uses concentrated caustic and 50% organic co-solvent at 220 °C. No measured net energy, KOH/methanol recycle, solvent-regeneration, or treatment-cost result was found. It is an operation for a concentrate/destruction train, not demonstrated treatment of trace PFOS in natural water.

**Independent interfacial electrochemical route, with fluoride evidence but incomplete bulk C/S closure:** Meng et al., [“Complete electrocatalytic defluorination of perfluorooctane sulfonate in aqueous solution with nonprecious materials,” *Journal of Catalysis* 431, 115403 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcat.2024.115403).

- Potassium PFOS is treated at a laser-made [Ni₀.₇₅Fe₀.₂₅](OH)₂-coated carbon-fiber-paper anode in aqueous 8 M LiOH (measured pH 14.9), under UV irradiation and stagnant conditions. A cycle is 1 min at +1.6 V vs RHE, then 5 min open circuit; 60 cycles completely defluorinated 0.50 mM PFOS and yielded 158.6 ppm fluoride (close to its 161.5 ppm stoichiometric fluorine). At 0.05 mM (reported as 27 ppm PFOS), 20 cycles reached about 99% defluorination. The best conditions depend on high Li⁺/OH⁻, pulsing, and a thick interfacial layer; agitation and continuous electrolysis reduce performance. XPS sees C–F species, surface sulfate and deprotonated peroxide after operation; the authors propose UV-activated oxygen species made from anodic water oxidation. Oxygen atmosphere is not established as a controlled feed variable.
- Fluoride is measured with an ion-selective electrode after buffering the strong LiOH electrolyte. This shows substantial PFOS C–F cleavage, but the source does not provide a closed bulk carbon/sulfur balance or a whole-phase organic-fluorine balance; surface sulfate is suggestive of C–S cleavage, not a sulfur-product mass balance. The lowest demonstrated concentration is 27 mg/L, not trace-level drinking-water concentrations. Reported 5.256 kWh/m³ and ~$0.10M capital are scale-up estimates, not measured plant performance; no LiOH recovery/regeneration result is established.

## Operation judgment

The closest published selective-encounter-plus-cleavage operation is **TAPP dark capture followed by visible-light reductive treatment**. It is already published, so it is not a novel operation to claim; its dilute-feed conversion should remain conditional until the PFOS-K/F⁻ basis and whole-system F/S/C accounting are resolved. For a separate PFOS concentrate, sealed alkaline methanol solvothermal mineralization has stronger direct mass-balance evidence, but its 8 M KOH/50% methanol/220 °C conditions do not establish a low-cost dilute-water process. No new chemistry operation is supported by this source check.

## Official correction and current-version check

The [Nature Communications author correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-74789-0) (published 23 June 2026; *Nature Communications* 17, 5560; DOI [10.1038/s41467-026-74789-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-74789-0)) says the first paragraph of Results and Discussion had “unoccupied” where it should read “occupied.” The corrected sentence is: “Upon visible light irradiation, TAPP undergoes primary electron excitation from the highest occupied crystal orbital (HOCO) to the lowest unoccupied crystal orbital (LUCO).” The notice says the correction is applied to the article HTML and PDF. The official original-article page marks “An Author Correction” and “This article has been updated.” The web-accessible text did not expose a separate Crossmark record, so this status check relies on the publisher's current-page update flag and linked correction. The notice identifies no change to sample concentrations, salt basis, catalyst dose, fluoride values, calibration, or SI.

In the **current corrected** original article, the methods still name 98% heptadecafluorooctanesulfonic acid potassium salt (PFOS-K) as the PFOS source and say the initial concentration was 0.1 ppm, without stating whether that ppm is PFOS-K mass, PFOS-anion/acid equivalent, or fluorine-equivalent. The results still report 0.10 ± 0.01 ppm F⁻ as the “theoretical maximum from adsorbed PFOS.” Current SI Note 3 identifies ¹³C₄-PFOS at 0.05 ppm as an **LC-MS/MS internal standard** (“in all samples” in that analytical-method section), not as a documented pre-irradiation spike. Therefore the correction does not resolve the concentration/fluorine accounting question; the appropriate status remains **unresolved basis**, not a claim that the measurement is erroneous. The SI Fig. 24 text also still gives 0.1 ppm to 0.015 ppm and labels it 98.5% removal; those numbers do not arithmetically agree, so retain that as a separate textual inconsistency rather than use it to infer the experiment’s true starting dose.
