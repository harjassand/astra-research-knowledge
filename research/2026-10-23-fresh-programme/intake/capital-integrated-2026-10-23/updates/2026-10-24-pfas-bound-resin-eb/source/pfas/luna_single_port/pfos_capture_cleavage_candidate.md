# PFOS capture–cleavage: reject the coating; test a foamate-to-PER–EO relay

**Disposition:** the frozen cationic capture-film idea is subsumed by a much stronger direct Pd–TiO₂ photoelectrochemical PFOS system. No new cleavage material is justified. The only retained improvement candidate is a no-added-surfactant interfacial preconcentration front end that feeds a known Pd–TiO₂ photoelectrochemical reduction (PER) and boron-doped-diamond electrooxidation (EO) train. This is a falsifiable process candidate, not an invention or performance claim. No experiment was run.

## Frozen idea and why it fails as a new mechanism

The pre-literature concept is recorded, unchanged, in `idea_frozen_before_literature.md`: a thin quaternary-ammonium/guanidinium capture film on a high-overpotential BDD anode would concentrate PFOS at a cleavage site. The direct incumbent is stronger and already implements the essential encounter-plus-cleavage logic. Guan et al. report a Pd-decorated TiO₂ cathode where cathodic potential promotes PFOS adsorption on exposed Ti⁴⁺ sites; UV₂₅₄-excited Pd supplies hot electrons and hydrated electrons that drive C–S/C–F transformation. Under their model condition (10 µM PFOS, 100 mM Na₂SO₄, 15 mA cm⁻², 8 h), PER gives about 90% desulfonation but only 37% defluorination. A subsequent BDD EO step takes their measured defluorination to about 100%. Their 256 cm² mesh cathode was also tested in reverse-osmosis concentrate and diluted AFFF matrices. A new cationic film on BDD would repeat adsorption at a reactive interface while risking passivation of the BDD surface; putting it on TiO₂ would also risk masking the exposed Ti⁴⁺ coordination sites that the incumbent uses. It has no current evidence of better selectivity, turnover, or product release.

PFOS-specific product requirements matter, but fluorine and sulfur fate are separate endpoints. The 2026 plasmon-enhanced PFAS study reports the PFOS H/F-exchange series through \(\mathrm{C_8H_{17}SO_3^-}\), a fully hydrodefluorinated product that retains sulfonate. Thus C–S cleavage and sulfate formation are required for sulfur mineralization, not for complete defluorination. PFOA has a carboxylate head and no sulfur, so PFOA removal or defluorination alone cannot validate PFOS conversion. The published PER work found about 10% sulfur-balance gap after sulfate formation and a sulfonate-bearing H/F-exchange product; sulfate and remaining organosulfur still need separate accounting. At 0.4–1.2 µg L⁻¹ PFOS (about 0.8–2.4 nM), 1 mM chloride exceeds PFOS concentration by roughly 4×10⁵–1.3×10⁶. A simple competitive-site model would need a PFOS/chloride binding selectivity of at least that scale for PFOS to dominate occupancy against chloride alone; bicarbonate, sulfate, nitrate, and NOM add further competition. At 10 ng L⁻¹, the chloride/PFOS ratio rises to about 5×10⁷. The foam relay avoids claiming molecular receptor selectivity, but instead must quantify co-enrichment of other surfactants and NOM. The direct product observation is in [Özce et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44221-026-00690-1).

## Candidate operation beyond the coating

The candidate shifts the selective-encounter step from a binding film to PFOS's air–water interface affinity, then sends the recovered foamate directly to the established cleavage chemistry. A closed microbubble column fractionates a declared PFOS-bearing water without added surfactant or coagulant in the first test. Collect foamate and collapsed foam in a sealed receiver with an aerosol/off-gas trap. Route the measured foamate immediately across a Pd–TiO₂-coated titanium mesh cathode under the published PER conditions, then through a BDD EO polishing cell. Keep the captured matter in the liquid stream through cleavage rather than drying, storing, or regenerating a sorbent.

Use no-surplus-chemical feed first at 0.4–1.2 µg L⁻¹ PFOS and 2–19 µg L⁻¹ PFOS as two dilute source-water bands; bracket a complex matrix with pH 6.5–8.4, air-equilibrated dissolved oxygen near 4 mg L⁻¹ versus deoxygenated feed, nitrate from 0 to 37 mg L⁻¹, and natural organic matter from 10 to 50 mg L⁻¹ as TOC. Measure chloride, sulfate, hardness, conductivity, and other surfactants in each actual source. These conditions deliberately span the reported PER challenges: oxygen slowed reaction but did not stop it; 10–100 mg L⁻¹ nitrate competed for hydrated electrons; 10–100 mg L⁻¹ NOM slowed the rate, with complete PFOS removal reported within 8 h only below 50 mg L⁻¹ TOC; and the single-chamber design kept pH near 6–7 while reverse-osmosis concentrate was treated as received at pH 8.4. In a paired pH run, test 6.5, 7.0, and 8.4 without correcting pH unless correction is a separately metered process input.

The point of this operation is not to claim that foam is a selective PFOS receptor. It tests whether a low-cost air–water capture step can reduce the liquid volume sent to a destructive reactor while retaining PFOS mass, without increasing matrix competition enough to cancel the gain. A 2026 microbubble study reports 91–98% removal of PFOS/PFOA/PFHxS from one natural source water at 2–19 µg L⁻¹, lower performance in another source at 0.4–1.2 µg L⁻¹ that improved with coagulant, and 94–99% removal in semiconductor wastewater at 0.1–0.6 µg L⁻¹. It separately tested UV/sulfite on a 20 mg L⁻¹ model concentrate, where PFOS parent removal took about 110 minutes. This shows the front-end concentration idea is already being explored, but it does not establish a foamate→Pd–TiO₂ PER→BDD train with full product balances.

Measure the foam recovery \(\eta_{\rm foam}=m_{\rm PFOS,foam}/m_{\rm PFOS,in}\), foamate volume fraction \(\phi=V_{\rm foam}/V_{\rm feed}\), and concentration factor \(CF=\eta_{\rm foam}/\phi\). First measure the minimum PFOS concentration \(C_*\) at which the selected PER–EO reactor achieves decision-grade F/S closure for that same matrix. For inlet concentration \(C_0\), foamation is capable of reaching that concentration only when

\[
\phi\leq \eta_{\rm foam}\, C_0/C_* .
\]

This avoids assuming that a concentration factor automatically saves energy. The candidate passes only if the full-cycle electrical, lamp, gas-transfer, pumping, pH-control, off-gas capture, and wash energy per mole of PFOS-derived fluoride is lower than the matched baseline while PFOS-derived F and S are recovered in the expected products within uncertainty.

## Controls and decision-grade outputs

Run the following side-by-side on aliquots from the same feed and with the same PFOS input mass: (1) direct Pd–TiO₂ PER followed by BDD EO, (2) foamation only, (3) foamate-fed Pd–TiO₂ PER–EO, and (4) the best GIC adsorption/electrochemical-oxidation condition available for that matrix. Within the PER arm include bare TiO₂, Pd-free TiO₂, no-light, open-circuit, and no-cathode controls. Within the foam arm include gas-only blank and a closed aerosol trap. A PFOA run may be a separate contrast, but it is not a PFOS surrogate.

For every arm, collect and assay influent, bulk raffinate, foamate, reactor effluent, electrode washes/extracts, filters, vessel rinses, and aerosol/off-gas traps. Quantify PFOS and chain-shortened/other targeted PFAS by isotope-dilution LC–MS/MS; use LC–HRMS suspect screening for unknown fluorinated products. Measure F⁻ in all aqueous fractions by ion chromatography and total/organic fluorine in the liquid, foamate, spent mesh, and solids with matrix-matched CIC or another validated total-fluorine assay. The published PER study reports that high-salt ROC can suppress CIC recovery through stable fluoride formation, so use spike recovery/desalting controls and mark the F balance unresolved if it does not close. Measure sulfate and remaining organosulfur products; sulfate already present in the 100 mM PER electrolyte or source water must be baseline-subtracted with an uncertainty interval. If that interval is too wide to resolve PFOS-derived sulfur, do not claim C–S cleavage closure.

For the foam fractionator and the PER–EO reactor, close separate stepwise balances so the intermediate foamate is not counted twice. In a defined PFOS-spiked matrix, the fractionator balance is

\[
17n_{\rm PFOS,in}=17n_{\rm PFOS,raffinate}+17n_{\rm PFOS,foamate}+F_{\rm column/rinse/aerosol}+U_{F,\rm FF},
\quad
n_{S,\rm PFOS,in}=n_{S,\rm PFOS,raffinate}+n_{S,\rm PFOS,foamate}+S_{\rm column/rinse/aerosol}+U_{S,\rm FF}.
\]

The reactor balance starts from the measured foamate input and counts only terminal products and losses:

\[
17n_{\rm PFOS,foamate\ in}=\sum_j\bigl(F_{j,\rm PFOS}+F_{j,\rm other\ OF}+F_{j,\rm inorganic}\bigr)+U_F,
\qquad
n_{S,\rm PFOS,foamate\ in}=\sum_j\bigl(S_{j,\rm PFOS}+S_{j,\rm other\ organosulfur}+S_{j,\rm sulfate}\bigr)+U_S,
\]

where the reactor-stage sum starts with measured PFOS-derived fluorine/sulfur in the foamate, and \(j\) covers terminal reactor liquid, electrode/solids, washes, and gas/aerosol capture. On real water, report gross totals and matched unspiked-matrix blank subtractions with propagated uncertainty; if background subtraction is not precise enough, label PFOS-derived F/S closure unresolved. Report \(F^-/(17n_{\rm PFOS,in})\), net sulfate-derived S divided by PFOS input S, residual parent PFOS across all phases, non-target organic-F inventory, and unclosed \(U_F,U_S\). Do not infer destruction from aqueous parent removal.

The earlier proposed “10 cycles / 10× adsorption capacity” rule is removed: cumulative product beyond a sorption capacity does not rule out stoichiometric catalyst consumption and is not a general turnover criterion. A credible catalyst ledger would quantify total Pd and Ti initially and after operation; dissolved metals in every effluent, wash, and rinse by ICP-MS/OES; residual electrode/solid metal after digestion; electrode mass loss and surface-state/accessibility changes; and PFOS-derived F/S in every phase. Integrate the full cathodic and anodic charge, subtract matched blank/background current, and report Wh including lamps, pumps, gas transfer, and controls. For a pathway specifically assigned to C–F→C–H exchange, reconcile the expected 2 e⁻ per exchanged F against measured cathodic charge while explicitly reporting side-current uncertainty; do not impose that electron ratio on unassigned oxidation steps. If a chemical reductant or enzyme cofactor is used in any variant, measure its input, residual, oxidized form, and products. Report transformed PFOS and recovered product per total and accessible Pd inventory, but call turnover and catalyst stability “not established” unless the elemental, charge, and product balances jointly support them. Product inhibition should be tested separately by matrix-matched F⁻/sulfate/product additions; rising electrode inventory or falling conversion is not turnover.

## Strong incumbent and falsification gate

The closest direct incumbent is Guan et al.'s Pd–TiO₂ PER–EO train, which already includes cathodic target adsorption, C–S/C–F chemistry, a mesh-scale electrode, complex-matrix testing, and a BDD polish. The strongest adsorption/electrochemical comparator is Trzcinski and Harada's graphite-intercalation-compound process: PFOS adsorption followed by 5 adsorption/regeneration cycles removed >100 µg L⁻¹ parent PFOS to below 10 ng L⁻¹ at 28 mA cm⁻², but the real-water matrix reduced efficiency by 3–40%, and the report is principally a parent-removal result rather than a closed F/S mineralization balance. The foamate route must beat the best applicable incumbent on **whole-feed PFOS destruction and F⁻/sulfate recovery per total Wh and residual waste volume**, not on effluent PFOS alone.

The candidate fails if any of these occurs: measured foam concentration does not reach \(C_*\); foamation loses too much PFOS to walls/aerosols; co-enriched NOM/nitrate/salts increase total energy; PER products stay on the mesh or F/S closure misses threshold; or the total process cannot beat direct PER–EO/GIC under matched whole-feed accounting. A literature overlap search, acquired concentration factor, reactor energy ledger, independent repeat, and external reproducibility review remain open. No novelty, field efficacy, safe disposal, or cost advantage is established.
