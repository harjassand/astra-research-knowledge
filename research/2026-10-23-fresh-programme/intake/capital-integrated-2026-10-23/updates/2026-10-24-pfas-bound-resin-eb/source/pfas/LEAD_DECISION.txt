PFOS chemical capability unit — bounded decision
23 October 2026. Internal analytical work only; no experiment was performed.

No new acquired chemical capability or practical advantage is established.
The retained independent process hypothesis is selective anion-exchange capture,
measured scavenger-removing rinse/deoxygenation, then irradiation of a small wet
loaded-resin phase with an electron beam. Its central chemical interface — useful
fluoride yield from bound PFOS without an unacceptable material burden — remains
unacquired. This is not a new elementary reaction claim or a priority clearance.

THE OPERATION AND THE MISSING LINK

Use an actual specified exchanger (the quantitatively anchored example is PSR2
Plus Cl, a strong-base gel styrene/DVB exchanger), record PFOS breakthrough and
matrix uptake, and retain all fractions from a measured rinse. The intervention
changes both the irradiated mass and the pore environment; neither can be inferred
from aqueous PFOS removal alone. Dose-map the wet loaded phase. Measure parent,
inorganic F, residual organic F, and material changes across liquid, resin, rinse,
regenerant, walls, filters and gas collection. A regenerative version additionally
requires retained exchange capacity AND PFOS selectivity/breakthrough after dose.
For now it is a single-use hypothesis with unknown spent-phase burden.

An existing solution-phase radiolysis rate establishes a compatible source of
reducing species. It does not establish electron delivery to bound PFOS rather
than the exchanger, counterions or co-retained organics. Resin damage and PFOS
cleavage can coexist; damage defeats an inert/reusable-scaffold claim, not the
possibility of a simultaneous PFOS reaction. A freshly spiked resin control can
adsorb PFOS before irradiation, so preparation order alone does not isolate an
interfacial mechanism unless the initial partition and transfer times are known.

THE STRONGEST RETAINED DEDUCTION

Within a homogeneous first-encounter competition fixture,

  f_P = k_P C_P / (k_P C_P + sum_i k_i C_i).

Multiplying PFOS and all scavenger concentrations by a common factor leaves f_P
exactly unchanged. Therefore concentration reduces irradiated mass, but improves
this branching fraction only through selective retention or scavenger removal.
This is ordinary competition kinetics applied to the acquisition interface, not
a universal bound on bound-state chemistry, secondary radicals, or final F yield.

For the explicit assumed fixture PFOS=0.1 mg/L (anion basis), O2=0.25 mM and
nitrate=0.10 mM, literature initial-interaction rate endpoints give f_P between
2.56e-6 and 8.41e-5 before other sinks. Removing O2 alone does not remove nitrate.
These numbers are reproducible model calculations, not a measured reactor result.

The capacity data prevent the opposite exaggeration: small site occupancy need
not mean useless PFOS loading. A published PSR2 batch occupancy of 1.4% and an
apparent higher-loading value of 3.4%, combined with independently measured
Q=1.59 meq/g dry resin, imply about 11 and 27 mg PFOS/g dry resin. They do not
establish those loadings from a fast real-water column, but do justify keeping a
small-mass irradiation test plausible. No near-total PFOS site occupancy is a
necessary condition for treatment; area, throughput and turnover also matter.

If lambda is measured kg PFOS/kg dry resin, W is water/dry-resin mass, D is dose
in kGy, and y_F is the measured fraction of the captured PFOS fluorine converted
to inorganic F, absorbed beam energy per kg PFOS-equivalent defluorinated is

  D(1+W) / (3600 lambda y_F) kWh/kg,

before accelerator losses, capture, rinsing, transfer, regeneration, material
replacement and residual treatment. The formula is an accounting identity under
its definitions. Substituting a clean-water parent-loss fraction for y_F is not
allowed. A complete comparison must also count uncaptured PFOS and PFOS lost in
rinse. If those fractions receive no other treatment, overall F conversion is
the product of capture fraction, retained-after-rinse fraction and conditional
F conversion in the irradiation phase.

WHY THE 125-kGy EXAMPLE IS NOT A DESTRUCTION ADVANTAGE

The 2020 aqueous coefficient k_D=0.0184/kGy gives a transferred exponential parent
D90=125.14 kGy and 34.76 Wh/kg irradiated material. This is a conditional parent
disappearance projection, not a fluoride-yield dose or physical lower bound.
Dry-only resin arithmetic is especially not a claim of water-radiolysis efficacy
on dry material. The direct solution competitor must have the same PFOS loading,
gas condition and relevant matrix, in addition to a same-dose dilute-feed control.

A newer primary study is material to this distinction. Londhe et al. (2024),
Environmental Pollution 348, 123770, reports >90% PFOA/PFOS parent degradation
below 500 kGy at optimized conditions, but complete defluorination only at or
near 1000 kGy. Its mixture/groundwater tests show short-chain products increasing
and matrix-dependent performance. This was verified from the primary abstract;
publisher full text was not retrieved in this pass. It supplies neither a resin
result nor a transferable dose, but defeats the use of parent D90 as a mineral-
ization endpoint. At 1000 kGy, absorbed energy is 277.78 Wh/kg irradiated material
by unit conversion alone, with no predicted resin outcome.

INDEPENDENT ALTERNATIVES AND WHY THEY WERE NOT PROMOTED

1. Capture followed by mineral/metal-assisted milling was independently frozen,
   then retired as a distinct lead against the 2025 PFOS-loaded GAC/stainless-
   steel milling study. CaO/Fe or another monolith alone is a material change.
2. A head-anchored photo-electron-transfer coating and a host/H-transfer variant
   run into actual Pd/TiO2 and amine-silica/Pd(Pt) prior chemistry. Head-retaining
   H/F exchange is already reported. There is no newly acquired selective site.
3. Alpha-H/F exchange followed by SsuD/SsuE and aldehyde oxidation is a genuinely
   different conditional relay. It fails two necessary acquisition gates: the
   particular alpha-CH2 sulfonate isomer is not quantitatively established, and
   enzyme acceptance of its perfluoroalkyl tail is unshown. Ideal cofactor-
   compatible balances first yield PFOA, not mineralized carbon. Guan's direct
   desulfonation/EO sequence is the stronger demonstrated route to that task.
4. Foam fractionation into PER/BDD or field IXR/distillation into PER/BDD are
   conditional process integrations. Existing capture and cleavage mechanisms
   already own their components; no matched whole-train saving is established.

The source-matched incumbent for an actual dilute complex-water train remains
field IXR, regenerant recovery/distillation and Ti4O7 oxidation (Liang 2022).
The strongest closer molecular/interface incumbent is Guan 2026 Pd/TiO2 PER
followed by BDD, including complex matrices. The strongest directly loaded-solid
counterpart is PFOS/GAC milling. These comparisons must use whole-process F
conversion and material/energy boundaries, not unlike parent-removal EE/O values.
The current evidence does not support a head-to-head ranking.

OTHER BOUNDARIES RETAINED

Processivity is a useful distinction but not a new physical capability here.
An exact toy chain separates single-encounter completion from recapture and
finite resource budgets. Closed recycling can eventually complete despite poor
single-encounter success. Seventeen one-F steps are a toy, not an asserted PFOS
pathway. Neither a binding constant nor an eventual-completion theorem supplies
real rates, product pathways, energy, material renewal, or finite treatment time.

Product-specific balances are essential. Full H/F replacement can retain C-S
as C8H17SO3-, so sulfate is not required merely for all-F removal. The 34-electron
equation applies to that replacement endpoint only. Balanced formate or CO2
endpoints have different net redox ledgers; no sacrificial donor is not by itself
a stoichiometric impossibility. Balanced equations do not prove kinetics or
energetic feasibility.

A fresh tracer's post-treatment washout remains unable to identify historical
PFOS fate. Finite no-release can mean slowly retained parent. No effluent-parent
trace is substituted for the closed historical parent/organic-F/inorganic-F
inventory. Unresolved fluorine is not destruction. Complete carbon mineralization
and sulfur mineralization require their own product inventories.

TAPP is retained as a published selective encounter plus visible-light cleavage
incumbent, not as evidence for a new invention. physical_sources checked the
official June 2026 correction and current publisher update flag. The correction
changes the HOCO wording from unoccupied to occupied in the first Results and
Discussion paragraph, and says HTML/PDF were corrected. It lists no concentration,
dose, fluorine-calibration or SI amendment. A separate Crossmark record was not
exposed in the accessible page. Current Methods name PFOS-K and an initial
0.1 ppm without making the ppm basis explicit; the current F-concentration text
therefore remains a basis question. It is not an error allegation and supplies
no invention advantage. The exact receipt is appended to the existing source
note, rather than resolving a missing definition by inference.
