# PFOS capture → electron-beam cleavage: bounded resource/branching audit

**Scope.** A single hypothetical strong-base anion-exchange (AEX) capture, rinse, and wet thin-film electron-beam (EB) operation. This is an arithmetic and experiment-design audit, not a prediction that resin-bound PFOS is cleaved. Parent loss, fluorine release, complete defluorination, and mineralization remain distinct outcomes.

## The critical arithmetic: co-concentration does not improve electron branching

For a homogeneous initial-collision model, the fraction of hydrated electrons captured by PFOS is

`f_P = k_P C_P / (k_P C_P + Σ_i k_i C_i)`.

If capture-and-volume-reduction multiplies PFOS and every competing scavenger in the pore liquid by the same factor `a`, then `f_P` is exactly invariant: the numerator and denominator are both multiplied by `a`. Concentration helps the electron branching fraction only insofar as the operation **selectively** concentrates PFOS relative to scavengers or removes scavengers. The label “PFOS loaded on resin” by itself does not establish either condition. This is the sharpest interface test for the concept.

A transparent illustrative feed is the EB source concentration, 0.1 mg/L PFOS anion (0.20035 µM), with 0.25 mM dissolved O₂ and 0.10 mM nitrate. Use the measured initial hydrated-electron/PFOS rate-constant bracket `7.3×10⁷–2.4×10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹`; for the two named competing reactions use `k(eaq+O₂)=1.9×10¹⁰` and `k(eaq+NO₃)=9.7×10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹`. These give `k_i C_i = 4.75×10⁶ s⁻¹` for oxygen and `0.97×10⁶ s⁻¹` for nitrate. For an additional fixture of 2 mg-C/L NOM, the measured aquatic/terrestrial DOM range `0.51–2.11×10⁸ M_C⁻¹s⁻¹` gives a pseudo-first-order sink of `8.49×10³–3.51×10⁴ s⁻¹`. With O₂, nitrate, and that aqueous-NOM sink included, the joint-endpoint PFOS initial-capture share is `2.54×10⁻⁶–8.39×10⁻⁵` at 0.20035 µM, `1.27×10⁻⁷–4.19×10⁻⁶` at 10 nM, and `1.27×10⁻⁵–4.19×10⁻⁴` at 1 µM. The calculation still omits other matrix ions and resin, so it is an upper bound only in this named homogeneous competitor fixture.

Removing O₂ alone while leaving 0.1 mM nitrate gives at most `1.51×10⁻⁵–4.95×10⁻⁴` at 0.20035 µM PFOS in the O₂+nitrate-only model; including the fixture NOM lowers it slightly. A 1% initial PFOS share against the feed O₂+nitrate sinks would require 24.1–791.5 µM PFOS; including 2 mg-C/L free aqueous NOM changes this to 24.1–796.3 µM, using the favorable-to-adverse independent endpoints, **if O₂/nitrate/NOM concentrations remain fixed**. A 1,000-fold PFOS-only enrichment with competitors fixed raises the branch fraction; 1,000-fold co-concentration leaves it exactly unchanged. Actual solids/pore geometry and spur chemistry can depart from this homogeneous calculation, so it is a challenge fixture and measurement target, not a treatment model.

The PFOS rate range is particularly consequential: the lower value is an older pulse-radiolysis value, while the upper value is from Maza et al.'s nanosecond transient-absorption study. Both concern an initial PFOS/electron interaction, not productive cleavage. The calculation is an **initial encounter upper bound only within the named homogeneous competitor fixture**, because unlisted aqueous scavengers are omitted; it is not a general bound for electron transfer at a resin-bound interface. The NOM rate is from pulse-radiolysis measurements on aqueous isolates; no rates for the candidate resin, its bound counterions, or resin-associated NOM are available, so those remain unmeasured competitors.

## AEX capture can remove PFOS without producing a concentrated, clean irradiation target

For a simple one-site competitive equilibrium against chloride alone,

`θ_P/θ_Cl = (K_P/K_Cl)(C_P/C_Cl)`.

Thus, at `1 mM Cl⁻`, PFOS at 10 nM, 0.20035 µM, and 1 µM has PFOS:chloride solution mole ratios of `1:100,000`, `1:4,991`, and `1:1,000`. For PFOS to occupy 50% of sites in a two-species model would require `K_P/K_Cl` of those same ratios; 99% site occupancy requires 99 times those ratios. These are **site-utilization thresholds, not necessary conditions for high PFOS removal**: a large bed can remove trace PFOS at tiny PFOS occupancy.

That distinction is observed in a primary AEX study. With 0.2 mg/L PFOS and 5 mM chloride in a 42-day batch kinetic test, the three strong-base gel resins reported 99.995% PFOS liquid removal while PFOS occupied only 1.1–1.4% of measured resin exchange capacity. Their PFOS isotherms later plateaued around 3.4–6.2% of exchange capacity. This shows that high removal is possible and also warns that removal does not imply a small high-loading irradiation cassette. The cited resins are gel-type and the result is not a loading estimate for the proposed macroporous resin.

For a defined matrix of 1 mM chloride, 0.5 mM sulfate, 0.1 mM nitrate, and 2 mM bicarbonate, a cubic metre contains 4.1 equivalents of those inorganic anions, versus 0.200 mmol (`0.000200` equivalents) of PFOS at 0.1 mg/L: about 20,464 competing anion equivalents per PFOS equivalent. This is feed inventory, not an assertion that the resin captures all of it. A realistic capture ledger must measure actual inorganic-anion and NOM uptake, PFOS breakthrough, and usable PFOS loading. The bed mass/capacity cannot be assumed away when converting a water-volume energy estimate into a concentrated-solids estimate.

## Conditional absorbed-dose / concentration ledger

The primary Trojanowicz et al. paper reports an EB dose-normalized pseudo-first-order PFOS disappearance coefficient `k_D = 0.0184 kGy⁻¹` for 0.1 mg/L PFOS in neutral, deaerated solution. If that slope is treated as exponential and held fixed, `D90 = ln(10)/k_D = 125.14 kGy`. Since `1 kGy = 1 kJ/kg`, that is `34.76 Wh` absorbed per kg of irradiated material.

At 0.1 mg/L, one cubic metre contains 0.1 g PFOS anion. Applying the source slope unchanged to the water-only 1 m³ case gives a **conditional transferred-dose estimate** of `34.76 kWh/m³` absorbed, or `347,612 kWh/kg input PFOS`; per kg parent PFOS removed at 90% parent loss, it is `386,236 kWh/kg removed`. These are not physical lower bounds: the source parent-loss slope need not hold in the resin/matrix, and it does not establish any fluoride yield. The figures exclude accelerator wall-plug losses, pumping, capture, rinsing, bed transfer, regeneration, and any F⁻-yield requirement. If a 1 m³ feed were represented by only 1 kg of wet irradiated concentrate, the same nominal dose would be 0.03476 kWh absorbed—1000 times less irradiated mass. That is an arithmetic upper bound on the possible mass-concentration benefit, not a demonstrated saving: resin-bound kD, material dose distribution, capture selectivity, rinse loss, and full-phase destruction were never measured in the source study.

The tested operation should therefore weigh and meter **all** irradiated material. Report `D × M_irradiated` as absorbed energy, plus wall-plug EB electricity and auxiliaries. A measured bed that is mostly inorganic coion/NOM capacity, or that requires extensive water to remain hydrated, can erase some or all of the nominal mass reduction.

## Full-phase F and product-ion accounting

At 0.1 mg/L, a 1 m³ feed contains 0.20035 mmol PFOS and 3.406 mmol input fluorine atoms (64.71 mg F). A hypothetical *complete oxidation* endpoint would produce 17 mol F⁻ and one mol sulfate per mol PFOS; under that idealized endpoint, if products remained in a 0.1 L concentrate they would be about 34.06 mM F⁻ and 2.004 mM sulfate. Fluoride plus sulfate represent 19 negative charge equivalents per PFOS equivalent, so conversion products can load/compete for an anion exchanger very differently from the parent. This is an endpoint balance only, not the asserted EB pathway or product distribution. The actual product and electron ledger must follow measured products; do not infer a net electron demand from the number of C–F bonds.

For any claim of treatment, assay every boundary: feed; column effluent; rinse fractions; irradiation cassette liquid; resin extract/solid; regeneration eluate; headspace trap; vessel/filter/wall rinses. Close `F_input = F⁻ across all phases + organic-F across all phases + unresolved F`, and separately measure parent PFOS, shorter-chain PFAS, non-target organofluorines, and sulfur products. Adsorbed parent, resin-bound intermediates, or F-bearing gas are not destruction.

## Concrete falsification operation

Run one source-matched clean-feed trial and one defined-matrix trial. In the matrix trial, feed a 10 L bench column with 0.1 mg/L PFOS, 1 mM Cl⁻, 0.5 mM sulfate, 0.1 mM nitrate, 2 mM bicarbonate, 2 mg-C/L NOM, and measured air saturation; use an identified macroporous strong-base AEX resin. At breakthrough, separately quantify PFOS and each major anion in the effluent. Apply a measured, staged deoxygenated-water rinse; fractionate and assay each rinse so the PFOS mass sacrificed to purification is explicit. Measure the retained resin's water mass, pore-water O₂/nitrate, resin-bound PFOS, and co-retained organic fluorine before irradiation. Irradiate that wet loaded resin in a shallow sealed cassette at measured 125 kGy alongside (i) a source-relevant aqueous control at 0.1 mg/L, (ii) a concentration-matched resin-free solution control with the same PFOS and total wet sample mass as the loaded cassette, (iii) the unwashed loaded-resin sample, and (iv) beam-off controls. Use dosimeters across the cassette, not a nominal beam setting. Collect and assay liquid, rinse, resin, filters, wall rinse, and headspace for parent, total organic fluorine, fluoride, sulfate, and sulfur closure. Measure resin capacity/leaching after dose. If regeneration is proposed, quantify brine/water use, PFOS and product ions in the regenerant, resin capacity recovery after each cycle, and follow-on treatment energy for the PFAS-bearing regenerant; otherwise include spent resin in total-organic-F accounting.

The key discriminator is whether the rinse/load operation causes a **measured selective increase** in `k_P C_P / Σ k_i C_i` inside the wet cassette and whether that increase yields more inorganic fluoride per absorbed joule than the matched solution control. Stop the encounter-to-cleavage claim if AEX produces high water removal but no high PFOS loading or no improvement in whole-phase defluorination at equal dose. A positive must show fluoride yield and closed phase balance, not only PFOS disappearance.
