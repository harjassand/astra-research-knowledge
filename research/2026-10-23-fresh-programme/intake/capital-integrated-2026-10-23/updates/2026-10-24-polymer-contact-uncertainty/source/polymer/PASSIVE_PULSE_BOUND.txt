PASSIVE DAMAGE PULSE: CONTACT DELAY, HEALING DOSE, AND REPAIRED-SEAM CREEP

Status: exact result in the stated screening model; not a material law, chemical
compatibility claim, or priority claim. It exposes a control resource that must
be measured, rather than proving self-healing polymers impossible.

Model and interface

A damage event at t=0 releases an active species into a localized seam. Its
concentration is C(t)=C0 exp(-kd t), with finite C0<=Cmax and kd>0. This is a
passive first-order deactivation model, not a claim that any named catalyst has
this decay law. Faces recontact at tc and receive healing exposure for th.
Productive exchange hazard is kh C. Assume optimistically that healing requires
only dose H=kh integral_[tc,tc+th] C dt; for independent productive events a
target probability p gives H=-log(1-p). Steric or contact limitations increase
the dose requirement; they do not improve this optimistic screen.

The intended loaded-seam result assumes kh, kv, Cmax, H, h, Gh, s and u_max
strictly positive, and tc, tw nonnegative. Thus B>0 and x_min>0. If B=0 is
considered as a separate no-creep edge, f_d(0)=1 is a limiting value: with kd>0,
q=1 does not attain a positive-x solution. It is attainable only in an explicitly
extended kd=0 limiting model. No equality at x=0 is used for the loaded case.

The repaired layer has thickness h and modulus Gh. After healing it can be held
unloaded for an explicitly charged time tw, then put under constant stress s.
While catalyst persists, its Maxwell-like relaxation rate is kv C. The resulting
extra seam opening/slip is
 u=(h s/Gh) kv integral_[tc+th+tw,infinity] C dt.
There is no parallel permanent load path across this fully severed seam in the
model. A surviving permanent scaffold, irreversible cross-seam locking, or glass
freezing changes the constitutive model and must be included separately.

The exact screening criterion

Define
 x=kd th, d=tc/th, w=tw/th,
 q=kh Cmax th/H,
 B=(h s/Gh)(kv/kh) H/u_max.

For a fixed x, both healing dose >=H and subsequent opening <=u_max can be met
by some C0<=Cmax IF AND ONLY IF

 q >= f_d(x):=x exp(d x)/(1-exp(-x)),                 (1)
 exp(w x)(exp(x)-1) >= B.                            (2)

Proof. The smallest concentration achieving healing is
 C0*=H kd exp(kd tc)/(kh(1-exp(-kd th))).
It minimizes post-heal creep as well. Substitution gives
 u_min=(h s/Gh)(kv/kh) H exp(-w x)/(exp(x)-1).
The concentration and opening conditions are exactly (1)-(2). Increasing C0
cannot rescue violation of (2), because it increases creep along with dose.

For d>=0, f_d is strictly increasing on x>0:
 d(log f_d)/dx=d+1/x-1/(exp(x)-1)>0.
The left side of (2) is also increasing. Therefore, if kd is selectable, let
x_min be the unique nonnegative solution of (2) at equality. Feasibility is
equivalent to q>=f_d(x_min), with f_d(0)=1. For immediate return to load, w=0
and x_min=log(1+B).

A useful dimensionless counterexample to a free passive lifetime window is
B=1, w=0. Then x_min=log 2 and
 q_min=2^(d+1) log 2.
If recontact waits ten healing dwell times, q_min exceeds 1419; immediate contact
needs only 1.386. This is a conditional capacity ratio, not a fitted property of
any polymer. Waiting after healing can reduce the bound, but is an explicit
unloaded downtime resource. Continuous loading during repair only adds another
creep/contact penalty omitted by this optimistic model.

Consequences for mechanisms

* A short damage-generated radical/catalyst pulse is local and self-terminating,
  but useful chemistry may be gone before faces contact. Increasing lifetime
  trades against later seam relaxation; increasing dose costs finite trigger or
  reagent inventory. Diffusion can further dilute or spread the pulse.
* A persistent tethered catalyst avoids loss by migration, but kd=0 gives
  unbounded eventual Maxwell opening in this model under nonzero load. Tiny
  active volume does not supply an equilibrium cross-seam load path.
* Contact-timed external activation, explicit post-repair catalyst shutdown,
  vitrification after solvent removal, or formation of permanent cross-seam
  bonds can escape this passive-pulse model. Each is a different operation with
  inputs, optical access, heating/solvent/drying, stoichiometry, or downtime costs.

Primary context (not premises for the algebra)

Force-activated transesterification catalysis is established in molecular
systems, not an acquired structural repair interface:
 https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.167
Solvent-swollen epoxy-vitrimer repair is established, so solvent activation is
an incumbent, not our invention:
 https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-04257-x
Photochemical local shutdown of TMG-catalyzed thiol-thioester networks is also
established; its on-state kinetics depend strongly on the acid-generator cargo:
 https://doi.org/10.1002/marc.202500654
Architectured silicone healing uses a surviving permanent elastic path for
contact and creep resistance; it does not restore a completely cut permanent
scaffold:
 https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202306494

The decisive measurement is not activation color or scratch closure. On the
same chosen material, measure C(t), tc, productive cross-seam exchange, recovered
fracture energy, and constant-load healed-joint opening after a full cut. Compare
with intact high-load controls and a free-catalyst formulation matched for bulk
relaxation. The model is falsified, rather than repaired by assumption, if C does
not decay exponentially or if the seam has a measured nonzero equilibrium
modulus from permanent/locked bridges.
