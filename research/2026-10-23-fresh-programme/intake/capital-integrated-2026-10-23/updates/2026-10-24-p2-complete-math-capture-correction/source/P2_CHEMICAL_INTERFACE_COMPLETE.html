Decision at this checkpoint

I retained a specific molecular candidate: recyclable covalent DNA connections whose cleavage is suppressed by load, with an explicit strand-displacement step that releases cut partners. A stable scaffold carries the material while these additional connections are edited.

The work produced a domain-level chemical construction, a complete conditional cycle, and finite-cycle mathematics that includes unsuccessful output. It also exposed a substantial limitation: the broad principle of preserving loaded connections while recycling slack ones is already established, and the simplest two-junction application is dominated by ordinary programmed crosslinking. The remaining opportunity is narrower: a regenerable covalent connection that can preserve a selected configuration during an inactive storage period and subsequently permit useful partner exchange.

This is an independently developed mechanism candidate. Its integrated chemical and mechanical performance remains unvalidated. The earlier coordinator-preserved results are unchanged.

1. The molecular operation I chose

The architecture uses a stable scaffold with additional, chemically separate repair arms. A connection between two arms contains a DNA hairpin in its force path.

The proposed selection rule is:

  • A sufficiently relaxed hairpin folds and presents a double-stranded recognition site to a nicking enzyme.
  • Loading opens the hairpin and disrupts that recognition site, reducing cleavage.
  • A subsequent displacement reagent separates the partners of a cut connection.
  • An uncut connection remains joined by its continuous covalent backbone.
  • The displacement reagent is degraded, the released arms can bind partners again, and ligase restores covalent connections.

The molecule senses its local conformation. It does not receive a label saying that a connection is useful, belongs to the correct component, or permits global completion. The correspondence between conformation and usefulness must come from the actual scaffold geometry and loading.

The important advance: an explicit release operation

A nick does not necessarily release a network connection. Base pairing can keep the same partners together, allowing them to reseal without changing connectivity.

The RNA strand QQQ addresses that problem. It binds the exposed qqq toehold and displaces A’s rrr domain from B’s r∗r^*r∗ domain:

2. The complete cycle requires separate chemical phases

The compatible cycle is an externally sequenced process:

  1. Ligate while relaxed. Allow A/B docking and seal the DNA nick with T4 DNA ligase and ATP.
  2. Apply the mechanical probe. The intended useful bridges should preferentially open their recognition stems.
  3. Pulse the nickase. Cleave accessible folded substrates, then stop or remove nickase before unloading.
  4. Displace the cut partners. Add RNA QQQ, which strips the docking duplex.
  5. Clear the RNA and permit reassociation. RNase H degrades the invading RNA; obstructive fragments must clear before ligase is restored.
  6. Complete re-ligation and enter a hold period. Remove the active repair chemistry after the chosen number of cycles.

Two chemical constraints make the ordering substantive.

First, nickase must stop before unloading. Otherwise protected bridges can refold into accessible substrates while the enzyme remains active. Temporarily withholding a required cofactor is insufficient if a later phase restores that cofactor and reactivates residual enzyme.

Second, ligase must be absent during RNA stripping and clearance. The RNA strand’s 3′OH occupies the former A-arm ligation position. T4 DNA ligase can join an RNA3′OH to a DNA5′ phosphate on a DNA template—the precise class of unwanted reaction this geometry creates. Leaving ligase active could covalently attach the expendable RNA to B and corrupt the reusable port. PubMed

These are implemented control requirements, not a mathematical completion guard. The phase schedule needs no knowledge of network topology, but enzyme exchange, residual activity and fragment clearance are real parts of the proposed operation.

3. The completed theoretical result

I used a deliberately small motif to make the chemical and mechanical claims falsifiable.

Two scaffold junctions, uuu and vvv, each carry one additional A arm and one additional B arm. At fully ligated endpoints, the isolated motif has two possible pairings:

  • LLL: two local loops, AuBuA_uB_uAu​Bu​ and AvBvA_vB_vAv​Bv​.
  • XXX: two interjunction bridges, AuBvA_uB_vAu​Bv​ and AvBuA_vB_uAv​Bu​.

The scaffold’s own connections are separate from these repair arms. This changes the resource contract explicitly: the operation edits an additional network while a pre-existing structure maintains the load path. It does not establish repair of a severed scaffold.

What the annealing comparison establishes

A force-blind process with the same relaxed pairing probability ppp merely moves its occupancy toward ppp. Starting from loops, arbitrary scheduling of such unbiased resampling cannot raise its ideal bridge fraction above ppp. The selected process can exceed that level after finitely many cycles.

Under the additional assumption of independent per-link cutting, I also completed a comparison at matched expected cut/re-ligation counts. If the selected process has per-link cut probabilities qL>qXq_L>q_XqL​>qX​, a blind comparator can be assigned

qˉm=(1−xm)qL+xmqX.\bar q_m=(1-x_m)q_L+x_mq_X.qˉ​m​=(1−xm​)qL​+xm​qX​.

Both incur the same expected number of cuts in that round. The selected process still has at least as much useful occupancy after repeated rounds.

This comparison does not establish an energy advantage. Mechanical work, RNA consumption, enzyme exchange, transport, unsuccessful reactions and elapsed time remain costs. It also compares processes with the same available pairing information and the same ppp.

4. Incomplete cycles are now included

A fully ligated endpoint cannot be assumed merely because a chosen dwell time has elapsed.

They also establish why the performance claim should be finite. If irreversible loss continues during operation, an attractive bridge fraction conditioned on survival can coexist with declining total useful material.

5. The strongest alternatives substantially narrow the opportunity

The most consequential comparison was mobile catch-bond networks.

Mulla et al. experimentally showed stronger reconstituted actin networks with catch-bond crosslinkers than with the slip-bond comparator, even though the individual catch bonds were weaker. Their simulations explain the benefit through departure from low-stress regions and redistribution toward stressed regions. Requiring rebinding at the original location removes that modeled advantage. That directly challenges any claim that inverse selection plus partner exchange is itself new. PubMed+1

There are experimental DNA competitors as well. Yang et al. demonstrated tunable DNA catch bonds using single-molecule force spectroscopy, and van Galen et al. demonstrated DNA catch-bond-mediated rolling adhesion. These establish molecular force-dependent stabilization in a compatible class of material. A network built from mobile DNA catch links is therefore a serious comparator, although these papers do not demonstrate the proposed covalent storage-and-reactivation cycle. PubMed+1

The theoretical comparison became even closer. Wang, Galvani Cunha, Crocker and Liu, published on 15 October 2026, model rigidity and long-lived elastic memory with complete turnover using preferential low-tension disassembly, nonzero random disassembly and energy injection during assembly. The broad theoretical claim of retaining function through biased turnover is already occupied. PRX Life

A decisive limitation of our own testbed

For independently addressable two-junction motifs, ordinary sequence programming can aim directly for XXX, suppressing the local-loop pairing.

In the stated linear mechanical model, programming every independent motif to its bridge state gives a positive-semidefinite stiffness addition. Unknown future load direction does not rescue the selective-turnover application: those added bridges cannot reduce stiffness in that model.

I therefore retain the paired motif as a chemical testbed, and reject it as evidence of superiority over the strongest conventional construction.

A stronger application would require a substantive constraint such as shared valence, a limited connection supply, competing allocations, or a measured compliance/reconfiguration tradeoff. Introducing such a constraint requires new physical and mathematical work; it cannot be silently appended to the two-state proof.

6. Where the physical interfaces fail—and which results remain valuable

Several distinctions are now explicit.

A global completion guard still supplies no local actuator. The proposed hairpin is a local conformational selector with errors. Its possible usefulness is demonstrated only under stated geometry and transition assumptions.

Cleavage still does not imply reallocation. RNA displacement supplies a candidate separation operation, but a released A arm remains tethered near its old B partner. The original partner can have a very high effective local concentration. If the other junction is outside reach, the cross-pairing probability is effectively zero regardless of cleavage selectivity.

Chemical fuel consumption does not automatically supply the mechanical work used in a network model. In the new PRX Life model, insertion assigns an edge a rest length shorter than the current node separation,

ℓij=dij(1−ε),\ell_{ij}=d_{ij}(1-\varepsilon),ℓij​=dij​(1−ε),

thereby injecting prestress by construction. The authors motivate that coarse-grained operation through biological motor activity. A synthetic implementation needs an actual process that creates and maintains that shortened connection. Merely supplying ATP to a ligase does not establish the corresponding force–displacement work. The theoretical result remains valuable within its declared model. journals.aps.org

Two auxiliary results from this attack also remain useful:

  • Uniform reopening removes absorbing locks without supplying directed repair. In the declared permutation-pairing model with uniform lock/unlock rates and symmetric exchange, the equilibrium distribution can be calculated. For four junctions, the strong-lock limit has connected fraction 2/32/32/3. That is an equilibrium result for that model, not a bound on arbitrary driven protocols.
  • Connecting components can weaken the material. An abstract loaded/slack exchange rule can merge cycles when those labels are supplied. But inserting mmm equal springs into one arc of a two-arc load path changes its stiffness fromκ(1a+1b)toκ(1a+m+1b),\kappa\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right) \quad\text{to}\quad \kappa\left(\frac1{a+m}+\frac1b\right),κ(a1​+b1​)toκ(a+m1​+b1​),which is smaller. The connectivity construction survives as a graph result; it fails as a general stiffening argument.

These are structural limitations. By contrast, the absence of measured performance for our anchored cassette is an evidence gap. Lack of experimental access does not show that its operating window exists or that it fails.

7. The retained invention question

The surviving candidate is:

A regenerable covalent repair connection that uses local load to bias turnover, explicitly releases cleaved partners, restores the same reactive ends, retains a selected configuration during an inactive period, and can later be reactivated.

The chemistry has advanced beyond an unspecified “unlock and exchange” instruction. It now names the bond, recognition-site orientation, load path, release reagent, reset reaction and necessary phase exclusions. The proof also states what selective turnover would achieve and how much incomplete output could erase that advantage.

The unresolved question is whether one anchored construct can provide all of the following together:

  • preferential completed release of slack connections;
  • sufficient access to different partners;
  • re-ligation with acceptable missing-output and damage rates;
  • retained mechanical benefit after unloading and reagent clearance;
  • a useful later reconfiguration episode under a real connection-budget constraint.

That coupled result would justify further development. Molecular cleavage selectivity alone would not.

My priority decision is to retain this narrow chemical-interface candidate, retire the broad novelty claims, and treat the two-junction motif as a falsifiable mechanism test. The work has not yet established a distinct materials invention over mobile catch-link networks and programmed crosslinking. It has identified exactly what the proposed chemistry must add to them, and completed the argument needed to judge that addition without hiding failed cycles.
