Decision: pursue a catalytic junction with two mechanical controls

I am moving the main invention effort to a compact catalytic junction that can deliver mechanical work during a closed deformation cycle under steady chemical conditions. Yeast guanylate kinase is the strongest first physical testbed I found. The recyclable DNA connection remains a retained candidate, with its release/reset cycle and finite-output results intact.

The important question is whether a molecule can couple two externally accessible deformations so that its stationary mechanical response depends on the direction in which a deformation cycle is traversed. This would provide a route from chemical reaction pathways to an active constitutive element: a junction whose mechanical behavior cannot be represented by a stored elastic energy alone.

The investigation has produced three substantive results:

  • An explicit, thermodynamically consistent enzyme-cycle mechanism can generate this response.
  • The mechanism requires a particular relationship between reaction-pathway access and ligand-dependent force. Catalysis, conformational motion, and mechanically altered turnover do not separately establish it.
  • The physical interface is now sharply located. The kinase evidence supports catalysis under mechanical stress, but does not yet supply two independent, site-addressed mechanical coordinates or their effects on individual reaction pathways.

That last distinction determines the next attack. The opportunity is a molecular realization and a transferable design principle; a bulk material capability has not yet been demonstrated.

A nonzero Ω\OmegaΩ means that one traversal direction can extract chemical work through the mechanical coordinates. The molecule must return to the same statistical state after the cycle; accumulated winding, irreversible restructuring, or depletion of a stored conformational change must be included in the accounting.

The broad physics already exists. Nonequilibrium statistical-force theory establishes that driven internal dynamics can produce nonconservative forces. Odd-elasticity theory addresses work during closed strain cycles, and catalytic micromachine models already connect chemical cycling to such behavior. These precedents substantially narrow any claim of novelty. arxiv.org+2

I also examined materially different directions. Metastable-phase synthesis through topochemical ion exchange already has strong primary examples, including recent nitride synthesis. The catalytic-junction direction survived because it exposes a specific explanatory gap: which features of a chemical reaction network become useful mechanical nonreciprocity at its actual external attachments? That question connects chemistry, molecular mechanics, and material architecture.

Astra’s compatible piezoelectric gate analysis supplied a useful discipline here: the same physical description must support the actuator, gate, reset, and work balance. Its electrical actuator does not transfer to chemistry, and its static elimination does not establish dynamic tracking. I carried that requirement into the molecular construction.

2. Why yeast guanylate kinase is the better first testbed

The selected reaction is

ATP+GMP⇌ADP+GDP.\mathrm{ATP+GMP \rightleftharpoons ADP+GDP}.ATP+GMP⇌ADP+GDP.

Its chemical driving is the maintained imbalance

Δμ=μATP+μGMP−μADP−μGDP.\Delta\mu =\mu_{\rm ATP}+\mu_{\rm GMP} -\mu_{\rm ADP}-\mu_{\rm GDP}.Δμ=μATP​+μGMP​−μADP​−μGDP​.

The physical proposal is a three-attachment junction: two attachments address moving regions of the enzyme, while a third supplies a mechanical reference. Two independent changes in the attachment geometry would then act as xxx and yyy.

The evidence that changed the selection

Joseph and colleagues measured catalysis in a mechanically stressed yeast guanylate-kinase–DNA chimera, with DNA attached at sites 60 and 139. Comparing a gapped duplex spring with an intact duplex, their low-buffer-condition forward kcatk_{\rm cat}kcat​ fell from approximately 494949 to 25 s−125\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}25 s−1, while reverse turnover remained approximately 151515–17 s−117\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}17 s−1. These are apparent parameters from separate forward and reverse assays; their proposed alternative reaction paths are heuristic. PLOS One

That is stronger starting evidence for this task than a force experiment that resolves enzyme motion without establishing turnover in the loaded construct.

Two qualifications govern the interpretation. The reported reversal between “low Mg” and “high Mg” conditions also changes KCl and Tris, so it is not an isolated magnesium effect. The same coupling chemistry labels both cysteines, and the reported purification establishes arm stoichiometry without establishing which arm occupies which residue; the His-tag location is also unspecified. PLOS One

Those last details matter directly to the invention. An internal spring works without a known assignment of its two ends. A material assembled from separately addressed junctions needs a reproducible relation between chemical site and mechanical port. Swapping the order of two ports reverses the sign convention for a planar curl; balanced opposite assignments can cancel the useful ensemble response.

For a single-junction measurement, arbitrary external port labels are acceptable: a nonzero response can still be detected. For an oriented material, site assignment, structural calibration, or a separately validated orthogonal attachment scheme becomes a real requirement.

The three-attachment geometry remains a hypothesis

Native yeast structures support distinct domain motions: GMP binding moves its binding domain inward while the LID moves outward in the reported apo-to-GMP-bound comparison. That is more informative than a single scalar “open/closed” coordinate, but crystal structures do not establish that two external tethers can control two independent deformations in a functioning catalyst. PubMed

The proposed construction therefore has three unresolved interfaces:

  1. Mechanical independence. The second perturbation must deform a relevant molecular coordinate rather than disappear into rigid-body rotation or flexible linkers.
  2. Chemical addressability. The externally distinguished arms must have a reproducible relation to the protein sites.
  3. Loaded catalytic operation. Activity must persist under the simultaneous constraints imposed by all three attachments.

Simply adding a third anchor does not prove any of these. Its location, compliance, lifetime, and effect on catalysis belong in the physical model.

What remains useful from adenylate kinase

Adenylate kinase remains valuable as a mechanistic and computational comparison. Pelz and colleagues established useful attachment geometries and ligand-dependent mechanical states, but their large opening-barrier displacement belongs to the non-turnover inhibitor AP5A. Catalytic events were not resolved under the applied load, which the authors acknowledge could inhibit catalysis. researchgate.net

More recent nanopore observations show that ligand occupancy can stabilize the ATP lid even when the other lid’s closing events largely disappear. Thus biochemical occupancy and mechanical closure must remain separate variables. That evidence supports an allosteric gate, but does not establish that pulling the second lid controls it. pure.rug.nl

I am consequently keeping yeast GK, E. coli AdK, and Aquifex AdK distinct. Combining their structural, kinetic, and force parameters into a single nominal enzyme would manufacture an interface that no source supplies.

3. The chemical operation that can produce the capability

The central operation is:

Mechanically change access to one part of a driven catalytic cycle, while an independent deformation couples to the force associated with chemical occupancy.

“Access” here means a kinetic factor shared by the forward and reverse passage through a pathway—for example, how readily a binding pocket admits and releases a ligand. It is different from the energetic preference between the states on either side.

This separation is essential. A common acceleration of every reaction step changes the clock but leaves stationary populations unchanged. It therefore leaves the stationary force unchanged.

Restoring the gate’s own forces does not force cancellation

A rate-only construction can hide the forces associated with its activation pathway. I completed that consistency check.

Each reaction edge can be replaced by passage through a short-lived, high-energy pathway state. Give all three pathways a common large energy offset HHH, while retaining their coordinate-dependent differences. Then:

  • all effective reaction rates acquire the same factor proportional to e−βHe^{-\beta H}e−βH;
  • the reduced stationary populations and curl remain unchanged;
  • pathway-state populations and their corrections to the force and curl are O(e−βH)O(e^{-\beta H})O(e−βH).

Consequently, a strictly nonzero reduced curl survives in a finite, thermodynamically consistent realization for sufficiently large HHH. Including pathway forces does not impose a universal cancellation.

There is a real cost: the reaction and the permissible operating cycle become slower. Raising only one pathway barrier would instead create a bottleneck and can drive the response toward zero. The common scaling is essential to this particular proof.

This establishes a physically consistent mechanism class. It does not identify the necessary gate in GK.

4. A thermodynamic audit that must precede a work claim

The full-cycle simulation literature is useful, but a trajectory that reproduces turnover is not automatically suitable for calculating extracted mechanical work.

Consider a chemical transition at fixed microscopic configuration rrr,

S(r)⇌P(r),S(r)\rightleftharpoons P(r),S(r)⇌P(r),

with a fixed chemical free-energy contribution aaa. Local detailed balance requires

ln⁡kf(r)kr(r)=β[a−(UP(r)−US(r))].\ln\frac{k_f(r)}{k_r(r)} = \beta\left[a-\big(U_P(r)-U_S(r)\big)\right].lnkr​(r)kf​(r)​=β[a−(UP​(r)−US​(r))].

must be independent of configuration.

There is a short, decisive consistency test. Choose two configurations where both chemical directions are allowed and traverse

S(r1)→P(r1)→P(r2)→S(r2)→S(r1).S(r_1)\to P(r_1)\to P(r_2)\to S(r_2)\to S(r_1).S(r1​)→P(r1​)→P(r2​)→S(r2​)→S(r1​).

With passive conformational transitions on the two mechanical legs, the cycle’s log forward/reverse probability ratio is

A□=R(r1)−R(r2).\mathcal A_\square=R(r_1)-R(r_2).A□​=R(r1​)−R(r2​).

The cycle has one forward and one reverse chemical conversion. It therefore receives zero net chemical work from fixed reservoirs. A nonzero value identifies an additional driving contribution or an incomplete thermodynamic specification.

Thus a constant chemical rate ratio combined with a varying state-energy gap needs further justification. A common pocket-access factor cancels from the rate ratio and cannot fix this issue.

An explicit admissible completion is available. With

This preserves both reference rates and enforces the required ratio. The equal division of the energy change is one possible barrier convention; it is not an identified molecular mechanism.

There are legitimate alternatives. Chemistry could occur only on a surface with constant energy gap, or the transition could include coordinate remapping, resampling, or an energetic acceptance rule. Those possibilities require the actual transition kernel.

The public AdK example repository I inspected contains inputs and analysis data, but not the modified simulator kernel needed to settle that audit. I have no basis to allege that the published model violates thermodynamics. The completed result is the audit condition and a consistent completion family.

This shows why one-axis turnover measurements cannot determine the desired response. Even an excellent fit to those data leaves the transverse kinetic derivative unspecified.

For an actual enzyme, different ligand occupancies may also share the same observed conformation. Those states must remain distinct when they have different reaction pathways or forces. Collapsing them into a single optical “closed” state can erase the relevant chemical information.

5. From an active molecule to an active material

A molecular effect can disappear during mechanical transmission. I completed the corresponding local analysis rather than assuming that many active junctions automatically yield an active solid.

For a stable deterministic linear response, let b=(b1,b2)b=(b_1,b_2)b=(b1​,b2​) be the imposed boundary coordinates. After internal nodes relax, define

Re=∂qe∂bR_e=\frac{\partial q_e}{\partial b}Re​=∂b∂qe​​

for the two local coordinates of junction eee. Eliminating the internal coordinates gives

Ωboundary=∑eΩedet⁡Re.\boxed{ \Omega_{\rm boundary} = \sum_e\Omega_e\det R_e. }Ωboundary​=e∑​Ωe​detRe​.​

This has three immediate consequences.

Rank loss kills the contribution. If both boundary deformations move a junction along the same local direction, det⁡Re=0\det R_e=0detRe​=0. A chemically active element can then be mechanically invisible to the chosen operation.

Compliance can suppress it. Soft handles can absorb most imposed motion. Increasing stiffness may improve transmission while simultaneously suppressing the catalytic motion that generated the response. The loaded kinetics must change with the mechanical design.

Orientation matters. Junctions with opposite signed transmission determinants can cancel. This is why attachment-site assignment and oriented assembly are substantive requirements.

There is also a symmetry restriction. In a fully isotropic three-dimensional material described solely by symmetric strain and symmetric Cauchy stress, the ordinary local isotropic elasticity tensor has the usual major symmetry. Randomly dispersing chiral proteins does not, by itself, produce a classical local odd modulus. A planar or deliberately anisotropic architecture is the cleaner first material setting; rotational or couple-stress variables would define a different construction.

Molecular tethers require thermal averaging

The deterministic elimination formula is not an exact treatment of arbitrary fluctuating protein–DNA handles.

If a tether coordinate zzz equilibrates within chemical state iii before a reaction jump, the relevant external state free energy is

Ui(b)=−kBTln⁡∫e−βEi(z,b) dz.\mathcal U_i(b) = -k_BT\ln\int e^{-\beta E_i(z,b)}\,dz.Ui​(b)=−kB​Tln∫e−βEi​(z,b)dz.

The corresponding transition rates must be averaged over the same conditional ensemble. With that timescale separation, local detailed balance survives the averaging and the finite-state analysis can be reapplied at the external ports.

If chemistry and tether fluctuations occur on comparable timescales, the joint reaction-and-diffusion dynamics must be retained. A crystal displacement combined with a nominal linker stiffness does not supply that calculation.

The full cycle has an unavoidable chemical cost

At a fixed deformation, a driven junction generally continues consuming chemical free energy:

Pchem=j Δμ.P_{\rm chem}=j\,\Delta\mu.Pchem​=jΔμ.

For a completed periodic operation,

Wout≤Wchem.W_{\rm out}\le W_{\rm chem}.Wout​≤Wchem​.

As the imposed loop is made slower, its quasistatic work can approach a finite value while the chemical consumption needed to maintain the stationary driven state grows with the duration. In that limit, efficiency tends to zero if the average stationary chemical current remains positive.

The objective is therefore not an efficient engine operating arbitrarily slowly. The slow limit separates a stationary nonconservative response from delay and dissipation. Useful operation would require a finite-frequency regime compatible with catalytic relaxation and mechanical transmission.

No defensible modulus, power density, or cycle-life estimate follows from the present kinase data.

6. What would constitute a decisive result

The most informative next calculation or measurement is the load-resolved coupling, under the actual attachment constraints.

A decisive positive result requires:

  1. Two independent external coordinates with ongoing catalysis. The reference and active states must use the same mechanical constraints.
  2. A stationary cross-force difference. Measure or calculate both ∂xFy\partial_xF_y∂x​Fy​ and ∂yFx\partial_yF_x∂y​Fx​, retaining chemically distinct occupancies where necessary.
  3. A closed mechanical operation. Oppositely traversed small loops, together with a slow-frequency limit, must identify the stationary contribution while accounting for internal reset and chemical consumption.

Directional displacement transfer alone is inadequate. A passive elastic system can transmit displacements differently when different components are clamped and released, despite having reciprocal conjugate force derivatives. That distinction applies directly to interpreting the conservative “allosteric lever” construction. arxiv.org

Two further source corrections remain relevant to the benchmark. The PDZ3 addendum withdraws the earlier force-magnitude inference and corrects the large affinity claim. The piezoelectric metabeam work’s closed quasistatic work loops were finite-element results, whereas its directional wave response was experimentally implemented. Neither should be promoted into a molecular two-force measurement. Nature Communications+1

The failure cases are now interpretable:

  • If the third attachment merely fixes rotation, the proposed mechanical interface fails.
  • If all relevant rates change together, the proposed chemical gate fails to alter stationary force.
  • If the gate and state-force responses are parallel, the selected operation has zero curl.
  • If the molecular response exists but det⁡Re\det R_edetRe​ vanishes or orientations cancel, the material architecture fails.
  • If the necessary pathway derivatives are unmeasured, the conclusion remains undetermined.

These are distinct from unavailable experimental access. None is evidence that the proposed kinase junction has already been built and failed.

7. The retained repair candidate and the research priority

The covalent DNA cassette remains valuable as a chemical testbed. Its explicit operation still includes selective cutting in the permissive state, release of cut partners, removal of the release strand, and restoration of a ligatable connection. Nickase exposure, unloading, stripping, clearing, and re-ligation remain separate controlled stages; they have not become a demonstrated one-pot autonomous cycle.

Its finite-output theory also remains useful. Enrichment in the two-junction motif depends on a shared reset distribution, and unconditional benefit must exceed accumulated failure and collateral loss. Partner recapture, incomplete remixing, force-dependent chemistry, and load bypass remain physical limitations.

The comparison with programmed crosslinking continues to govern its inventive scope. Where the desired bridge state can be installed directly in independently addressable motifs, enrichment does not establish a superior material operation. Existing catch-link networks further constrain what stress-dependent retention alone can claim.

The stronger present opportunity is the coupled catalytic operation derived above. It has a clear physical candidate, a chemical interpretation of the required gate, a complete thermodynamic existence argument, and explicit conditions for transmission into a material.

The priority is now to determine whether an actual pair of mechanical perturbations changes pathway access and occupancy-dependent force in independent directions while catalysis continues. A positive result would connect ordinary catalytic chemistry to an experimentally specified active mechanical element. A negative result would still preserve the completed theory and identify which molecular interface must change, rather than sending the program back into routine motif or switching-parameter tuning.
