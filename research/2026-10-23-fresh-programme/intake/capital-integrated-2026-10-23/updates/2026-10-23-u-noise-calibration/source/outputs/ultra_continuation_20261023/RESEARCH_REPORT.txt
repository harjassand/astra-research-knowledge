Noise attribution, physical calibration and honest current certificates

The strongest retained outcome is an identification boundary and a constructive
way to make a narrower decision despite that boundary. Two passive Gaussian
converter models can have identical complete observed records under every
specified coherent and thermal control, yet opposite signs of a bath current.
The missing information is where excess fluctuations enter. A trusted physical
phase-switching plane can sometimes supply that information, but the proposed
decoder reduces to an ordinary phase-switched coherent receiver with the same
resources. It is not an established new instrument or a practical advantage.

Instead of assigning every fluctuation to the plant or detector, one can bound
the physical current over all allowed assignments. We derive an attainable
interval for an important exact observation class. For positive bath-number
weights, its positive-lower-endpoint decision is EXACTLY the ordinary conservative
raw-covariance current test, not new measurement information. The full interval
describes the remaining ambiguity; noncommuting energy weights require a signed
spectral calculation. These are population/model results. No measured experiment,
novelty, physical cooling, net energy benefit, or advantageous device is claimed.


1. Model and conventions

Let X be a zero-mean proper complex stationary Gaussian OU process, with
  dX = G X dt + dW,       G = -K/2 - iH,
  K = sum_i Q_i,         H=H*, Q_i>=0,
  D = sum_i n_i Q_i + D_p,   D_p>=0.
Here * denotes conjugate transpose and G is Hurwitz. Covariance C solves
  G C + C G* = -D,
and L_G(D)=integral_0^infinity exp(Gt)D exp(G*t)dt is its positive solution map.
Our forward lag convention is Gamma_X(t)=E[X(s+t)X(s)*]=exp(Gt)C for t>=0.

The bath NUMBER current into the system is
  j_i = n_i tr(Q_i) - tr(Q_i C).
For an actual energy observable E_h, the bath energy flux is
  J_i = n_i tr(E_h Q_i) - tr[W_i C],
  W_i = (E_h Q_i + Q_i E_h)/2.
Only a supplied, physically justified E_h turns these expressions into heat.
For a local single-frequency bath, multiply its number current by hbar omega_i.
Nothing here measures wall power, pump efficiency, absorption, or net cooling.

The instantaneous readout is Y=S X+Z, with unknown fixed invertible S and
independent stationary additive sensor Z unless a stronger code projection is
explicitly stated. Full quadrature readout is assumed. Hidden states, missing
baths, nonlinear dynamics, uncalibrated timing and drift of the readout map are
not silently included in a guarantee.

2. What all-bath thermal calibration does and does not identify

Because G+G*=-K and G is Hurwitz, L_G(K)=I. A known common occupation change
delta_n in every dissipative bath therefore gives Delta C=delta_n I, provided
G,Q_i,D_p remain fixed. With an invariant sensor covariance kernel,
  M = Delta Gamma_Y(0)/delta_n = S S*,
  U = M^(-1/2)S is unitary,
  M^(-1/2) Delta Gamma_Y(t) M^(-1/2)/delta_n = U exp(Gt) U*.
This calibrates the physical metric and propagator up to a unitary gauge.
An individual bath response then gives C_i=L_G(Q_i), hence
  Q_i,u = -(G_u C_i,u + C_i,u G_u*).
A clock and a justified logarithm branch, or a derivative/continuous lag curve,
are still necessary. A single sampling grid has rotational frequency aliases.

Three decisive limits are retained in thermal_bridge.py and discussed below.

(a) Only shifting controlled Q_c gives L_G(Q_c), not I. A partial-bath metric
    can look internally consistent: its inferred controlled couplings can sum
    to its inferred damping automatically. That closure is not a completeness
    test for hidden baths. Exact one-mode models with (Q_c,Q_u,S)=(1,1,1) and
    (1/2,3/2,sqrt(2)) have identical accessible observations but different
    physical metrics and controlled currents.
(b) Stable stationarity is needed. A passive network with an undamped dark mode
    has nonunique stationary covariance and need not satisfy the reference law.
(c) Active/squeezed dynamics do not obey the passive identity by default. With
    G=diag(-1/2,-3/2), a thermal diffusion increment 2 I produces covariance
    increment diag(2,2/3), rather than I. The active example is a quadrature
    response counterexample; it does not import the passive proper-mode model
    into a general Bogoliubov system. See robust_current_sign.py.

3. Exact ambiguity: an opposite current sign with identical records

Use two modes, S=I,
  G = -I/2 - i [[0,.3],[.3,0]],
  Q_1=diag(1,0), Q_2=diag(0,1), (n_1,n_2)=(.1,.02),
  C_A=L_G(diag(.1,.02)), P=.1 I.
Model A has physical covariance C_A, no excess pump diffusion, and an independent
sensor OU process of covariance P and the same drift G. Model B moves that OU
component into the physical plant: C_B=C_A+P, excess pump diffusion .1 I, and no
sensor component. Coupling the sources gives Y_A=X+Z=Y_B pathwise before any
physical phase intervention. Their stationary Gaussian path laws are identical.

Their bath number currents are
  A: (+.010588235294, -.010588235294),
  B: (-.089411764706, -.110588235294).
They disagree on extraction from bath 1. A positive local bath energy per quantum
preserves that sign, but a general multimode heat claim needs E_h as above.

The equality survives every common additive coherent plant force u(t), including
a causal feedback rule using only the same observed history: the observed sum
has drift G, input u and the same summed diffusion. Thermal occupation changes
also preserve it because the moved pump/sensor component is fixed across them.
More samples, exact time labels, ideal fitting, or more such controls cannot
resolve this constructed model class. This is an exact no-go, not an estimator
failure. It relies on allowing a sensor OU mode with the plant's drift, which is
enough to disprove a uniform identification claim over arbitrary colored noise.

4. An attainable current interval without noise attribution

Suppose G, the physical metric, Q_i and n_i are known and the observed process is
itself a same-G OU process with covariance C_tot. Write
  D_th=sum_i n_i Q_i, C_th=L_G(D_th),
  E_ex=-G C_tot-C_tot G* - D_th >=0.
Every decomposition 0<=P_d<=E_ex is a possible excess physical pump diffusion;
the independent sensor diffusion is E_ex-P_d. All these models have exactly the
same observed Gaussian law and the same stated coherent/thermal control laws.
Their physical covariance is C_th+L_G(P_d).

For any Hermitian current weight W, solve the adjoint Lyapunov equation
  G* R_W + R_W G = -W.
Then tr(W L_G(P_d))=tr(R_W P_d). Put
  B=E_ex^(1/2) R_W E_ex^(1/2).
If B_+ and B_- denote its positive and negative spectral parts, with B_-<=0,
the exact attainable current interval is
  [ J_th-tr(B_+), J_th-tr(B_-) ].
Proof: write P_d=E_ex^(1/2) A E_ex^(1/2), 0<=A<=I on the support. In a basis
diagonalizing B, tr(BA) is bounded between the sums of negative and positive
eigenvalues. The bounds are attained by the respective spectral projectors;
convexity fills the whole interval. Singular E_ex causes no difficulty on its
support. This is an elementary semidefinite/adjoint-Lyapunov reduction, not an
asserted new optimization theory.

For number currents W=Q_i>=0, R_W>=0. Thus the endpoints simplify to
  [ n_i tr(Q_i)-tr(Q_i C_tot),
    n_i tr(Q_i)-tr(Q_i C_th) ].
The same inequalities remain valid for an arbitrary independent stationary
additive sensor because C_th<=C_physical<=C_tot; outside the same-G class their
endpoints need not be attainable. Correlated sensor/plant noise invalidates
this covariance-order argument. With a separately certified covariance error
||C_hat-C_tot||_F<=epsilon, subtract ||Q_i||_F epsilon from the measured lower
endpoint. Uncertainty in G, metric, bath strengths and occupations must also be
propagated; those calibrations are not estimated in this demonstration.

Decision-level comparator: the lower endpoint is the ordinary uncorrected
observed-covariance current. Independent added sensor noise can conceal a
positive physical current, but cannot produce a falsely positive lower-bound
certificate once the physical metric and bath calibration are trusted. Therefore
interval-positive certification for these positive weights supplies no decision
beyond that established conservative test. Its useful contribution here is the
exact identified set and abstention boundary, not a new cooling detector.

A finite-error version is proved in ROBUST_CURRENT_INTERVAL.txt. For N independent
proper Gaussian zero-mean observed snapshots and m PSD bath weights, let
muhat_i=N^(-1)sum z_k*Q_i z_k and delta=sqrt(2log(m/alpha)/N)<1. Simultaneously
with probability at least 1-alpha,
  j_i in [n_i tr(Q_i)-muhat_i/(1-delta), j_i,thermal].
This follows from the Laplace transform of a PSD Gaussian quadratic form and
does not require estimating a full covariance matrix. It remains the same
conservative scalar decision with uncertainty attached. A continuous trace is
not N independent snapshots; no finite-trace measurement guarantee is supplied.
The general signed interval also has a deterministic outer enclosure under
certified errors in the excess diffusion, adjoint weight and thermal current.
No point estimate of gain/drift/energy silently supplies those certificates.

For the two-mode example with C_tot=C_th+sI:
  s=.005: bath-1 interval [.005588235294,.010588235294], sign positive;
  s=.02:  bath-1 interval [-.009411764706,.010588235294], unresolved;
  s=.1:   bath-1 interval [-.089411764706,.010588235294], unresolved.
Bath 2 has negative upper endpoint throughout these fixtures. These statements
are model-conditional population certificates, not experimental findings. The
point is to preserve an honest decision where available and abstain elsewhere.

For a noncommuting energy/coupling weight, testing only all-sensor and all-pump
assignments can be wrong. An exact two-mode fixture has E_h=diag(1,4),
G=-3I-iE_h, Q_1=[[1,1],[1,1]], Q_2=6I-Q_1, n_1=n_2=1 and E_ex=I. Its adjoint
weight is [[1/6,1/3-i/6],[1/3+i/6,2/3]], with eigenvalues
(5+sqrt(29))/12 and (5-sqrt(29))/12. All-sensor energy flux is 0 and all-pump
flux is -5/6, yet the negative-eigenspace pump assignment gives positive flux.
The spectral interval correctly includes it. This supplies a mathematical
decision caution beyond the positive-weight endpoint shortcut. Whether those
collective bath generators represent a particular physical thermal reservoir
must be established separately; a quadratic energy identity alone does not
validate a thermodynamic implementation. See the exact certificate discussion above.

5. What a physical phase program can identify

For the instantaneous model Y_k=epsilon_k S X_k+Z_k, choose iid balanced signs
independent of the entire pre-code joint (X,Z) process and demodulate
D_k=epsilon_k Y_k. For j!=k,
  E[D_j D_k*]=S E[X_j X_k*] S*.
Both sensor autocovariance and signal/sensor cross terms cancel by sign parity.
Cross-correlation between X and Z is allowed in this particular identity if
their joint pre-code process is independent of the signs. Zero-lag variance is
not removed. Merely multiplying stored data twice returns the original record
and supplies no intervention.

The useful extension is a causal discrete filter:
  Y_k=sum_{ell>=0} H_ell epsilon_(k-ell) X_(k-ell)+Z_k.
For j>k, the latest sign epsilon_j can pair only with the ell=0 term. The other
sign then forces the current tap at k. Therefore
  E[D_(k+m) D_k*]=H_0 Gamma_X(m) H_0*, m>=1.
For square invertible H_0 this retains full-state lag information despite long
causal detector memory. A known pure delay can be aligned with its earliest tap.
Stable L2 filters follow by finite-filter limits with bounded second moments.
Noncausal filtering retains an extra crossed term
  H_m Gamma_X(-m) H_(-m)*.
The exact finite sign-pairing checks and a 300,000-frame synthetic correlated
sensor example are in causal_filter_phase.py. They do not give a finite-sample
coverage theorem. In particular demodulated samples are neither iid nor jointly
Gaussian in general.

With a complete thermal shift and A_m=Delta Gamma_D(m), this discrete model gives
  T=A_(m+1) A_m^(-1),
  M_eff=T^(-m) A_m/delta_n=H_0 S S* H_0*.
The downstream noise may even change between thermal regimes: it is projected
out separately within each regime if each pre-code joint law is code independent
and the same signal transfer applies. The physical plant G and pump diffusion
must still remain fixed for the common thermal response.

6. Continuous exposure: preserve the lag ratio, repair the metric

The discrete formula is not a license to replace a continuously changing state
by a held sample. Let a sign be held during a frame of width h, and sample a
causal detector at the frame endpoint. Its current-frame contribution is
  V_k=integral_0^h H(u) S X((k+1)h-u)du.
Sign parity keeps Cov(V_(k+m),V_k) for m>=1. For nonoverlapping OU windows,
  Gamma_V(m)=L exp(Gmh) R_C,
  L=integral_0^h H(u)S exp(-Gu)du,
  R_C=integral_0^h exp(Gv)C S*H(v)*dv.
Consequently the lag ratio identifies L exp(Gh)L^(-1) when the factors are
invertible. A common shift produces delta_n L exp(Gmh)K*, where
K=integral_0^h H(u)S exp(G*u)du. Extrapolating to zero yields L K*, not a Gram
matrix. For scalar G=-1-i and unit-width boxcar this factor is
  2(cosh G-1)/G^2 = .988897705763 + .166269974869 i.
It is non-Hermitian even though the true instantaneous gain metric is 1.

There is a repair when H(u)=f(u)I is a KNOWN real scalar kernel. Define
  w(G)=[integral f(u)exp(-Gu)du][integral f(u)exp(Gu)du].
Then Gamma_V(m)=S exp(Gmh)w(G)C S*. If A=S G S^(-1) is recovered on its correct
generator branch, the metric is
  S S* = w(A)^(-1) T^(-m) Delta Gamma_V(m)/delta_n.
Similarly w(A)^(-1)T^(-m)Gamma_V(m) yields S C S* after code-invariant noise
has been removed. For a boxcar, w(G)=2(cosh(Gh)-I)G^(-2) (with the appropriate
exposure normalization); it is invertible for Hurwitz G, including defective G
by holomorphic functional calculus. An unknown matrix-valued detector response
does not inherit this calibration formula. Filter characterization, bandwidth,
switch timing and conditioning remain real acquisition requirements.

For known matrix-valued H there is a further conditional repair, rather than a
blanket impossibility. Given the correct-branch A=LGL^(-1), solve the linear
operator equation integral H(u)P exp(-Au)du=I for P=SL^(-1). Next invert
  R_(A,P)(B)=integral exp(Au) B P*H(u)*du.
It maps C_L=LCL* to T^(-m)Gamma_V(m), and maps M_L=LL* to the corresponding
common-thermal intercept divided by delta_n. If both operators are invertible,
this recovers covariance and metric; whitening M_L yields a unitary physical
gauge. Known H alone does not guarantee operator invertibility or good precision.

The continuous audit also gives two different NONNEGATIVE channel boxcar kernels
with the same G=-diag(1,2), S=I, all stationary bath settings and all positive-lag
covariances, yet bath-1 number currents +1/100 and -109/8100. The physical bath
and pump matrices are positive. This proves a limitation of the positive-lag
reduction without filter calibration. It deliberately does NOT match zero lag
or the full code-conditioned observation law, so it is not an impossibility for
all information in a receiver record. Delay-only ambiguity likewise changes
the effective L metric while leaving physical S, G and currents unchanged.
Both scopes are explicit in CONTINUOUS_RECEIVER.txt and its executable.

7. Strong conventional controls and exact receiver reduction

The same hardware, sign sequence, transfer, raw complex samples, calibration and
read budget define an ordinary phase-switched coherent receiver. The map
(epsilon,Y)->(epsilon,epsilon Y) is invertible; the proposed statistic is an
ordinary code-weighted correlation of those samples. A conventional receiver
granted the same interface can run the identical decoder, with identical laws,
risk and resource costs. There is no extra identifiable capability established
by renaming that operation. Restricting an incumbent to unswitched data would
grant our arm an additional intervention rather than demonstrate better use of
the same interface. The exact random-code/causal lag identity remains a useful
scoped lemma, with priority UNKNOWN.

King et al. (2014), sections 4.3.10, 5.2 and 6.2, explicitly model phase-switch
amplitude/phase errors and show measurements with switching. Residual correlated
amplifier noise is reported. This is relevant established receiver practice,
not proof that their power-channel apparatus supplies our full complex-voltage
interface or implements this thermal calibration. Source:
https://arxiv.org/html/1410.5469v1

Noise upstream of a trusted switching plane is signed with X and survives. More
strongly, epsilon(X+W)+Z and epsilon X+Z+epsilon W are pathwise identical. A
code-synchronous readout component can imitate intrinsic plant fluctuations
under every sign program; no parity or lag-consistency test can establish its
physical origin from those data alone. See SWITCH_ALTERNATIVE.txt in evidence.
The discriminating acquisition must constrain that component independently,
for example calibrated signal blocking that retains the electronics/switch law,
a reference injection on each side of the suspected noise source, or a changed
physical switch location. A successful consistency test cannot certify an
unconstrained code-dependent alternative absent such an exclusion premise.

An alternative conventional plant control also breaks the original relocation
if its sensor kernel stays fixed. In a calibrated gauge with known drifts G_0,
G_1 and E_j=exp(G_j t), define D_0=Gamma_0(0)-Gamma_1(0) and
D_t=Gamma_0(t)-Gamma_1(t). Then, when E_0-E_1 is invertible,
  C_1=(E_0-E_1)^(-1)(D_t-E_0 D_0), C_0=C_1+D_0.
No equality of physical diffusion between regimes is needed. With
G_1=G_0-delta I, delta>0, E_0-E_1=(1-exp(-delta t))E_0 is always invertible.
This avoids estimating a derivative. Small separation and long decaying lags
amplify absolute errors; a small scalar prefactor need not worsen the relative
condition number. With exact propagators the bound is
  ||error C_1||_F <= [||E_0^(-1)||_2 ||error D_t||_F
                      +||error D_0||_F]/|1-exp(-delta t)|.
Sensor changes violate the cancellation. Continuous exposure
requires its own calibrated response operators. This elementary linear rescue
is not presented as new control theory.

8. Matched numerical falsification of the tempting advantages

All numbers below are synthetic; six repetitions are descriptive, not evidence
of general estimator-risk ordering. Model classes, supplied calibrations and
physical costs matter as much as frame counts.

Phase versus dark reference, each using 100,000 frames at dt=.2, supplies known
G, the canonical metric, Q_i and n_i to every estimator. The dark arm uses half
signal frames and half signal-blocked sensor frames with invariant noise; its
allocation is not optimized. The innovation decoder uses the exact known AR(1)
propagator and inverts C-MCM*=innovation covariance. Cold-bath current RMS:
  model A: phase .00060649; dark innovation .00098399;
  model B: phase .00235781; dark innovation .00094024.
Phase wins A and loses B. Neither switches, settling, blocking nor hardware
complexity have been costed physically. No uniform advantage follows. A first
dark-reference run divided half-length moments by the full length; that error
was caught, fixed to len(record)-1, and the corrected results are retained.

The old covariance-noise programme also loses to a strong fixed-batch mean
comparator on its n=8 fixture: 170,000 raw snapshots per arm give mean RMS drift
error .38895 versus covariance RMS 1.60526. The mean arm uses 16 fixed +/-axis
force batches, so the result does not depend on cost-free per-sample switching.
Preparation, actuator amplitude and settling costs remain unknown. The original
fixture's tr(C_s)=tr(D_s)=8 is valid because its A is skew; a trace-mismatch concern was investigated and corrected. It need not hold for general real A.

9. A distinct destructive-pair construction and its obstruction

An early inherited mark U_0 and late full-state destructive measurement V_T can
obey D(t)=Cov(V_t,U_0)=exp(-Ft)S_0, without reading a single cell twice. This
requires conditional linear closure and inherited excitation; lineage labels
alone do not supply either. Independent Poisson partition can give S_0=0.
Equal-time isotropic Gaussian pedigrees can be blind to rotational drift.

For T uniform on [0,L], let
  a_q(t)=4^q/binom(2q,q) sin^(2q)(pi t/L),
  M_j=E[a_q^(j)(T)V_T U_0^T].
If 2q>=d, the endpoint derivatives needed for integration by parts vanish, all
weights are bounded, and M_j=F^j M_0 for j<=d. Also
  M_0=phi_q(F)S_0,
  phi_q(z)=q!^2(2pi/L)^(2q)/L (1-exp(-zL)) /
           [z product_(k=1)^q(z^2+k^2(2pi/L)^2)].
For Re z>0 this transform is zero-free. Hence
  H_0=[M_0,...,M_(d-1)], H_1=[M_1,...,M_d],
  rank(H_0)=d iff (F,S_0) is controllable,
  F=H_1 H_0^dagger when that rank holds.
One scalar inherited mark can therefore suffice at population level. The claim
does not give dimension-free precision: derivative weights and conditioning can
grow badly. This is an adaptation of classical modulating functions and Krylov
realization, not a new general paradigm. Shinbrot's 1954 primary report already
uses endpoint-vanishing sine-squared windows for system identification:
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930084000

The full-rank q=1 implementation uses bounded a=2sin^2(pi t/L), a'=2pi/L
sin(2pi t/L), rather than a singular score p'/p. For families jointly Gaussian
conditional on time, with stationary marginal laws independent of time,
conditional variance bounds u,v and bounded weight W, a conservative
subgamma product bound yields entry error
  W sqrt(uv) [sqrt(6 log(4d^2/alpha)/N)+2log(4d^2/alpha)/N].
The code splits an independent pilot for variance envelopes. With moment bounds
epsilon_0,epsilon_1 and stored-matrix singular lower bound s>epsilon_0,
  ||Fhat-F||_F <= [||Fhat M0hat-M1hat||_F+epsilon_1
                    +||Fhat||_2 epsilon_0]/(s-epsilon_0).
This is an analytic coverage statement under independent families, known zero
means, exact clock, correct conditional mean and variance envelopes. The saved
floating calculation is not a formal verified numerical coverage certificate.
The determinant-based lower bound only certifies the stored binary 2x2 matrix.
At 50,000 pairs and omega=1.5 it passes six times but radii are about 3.39-3.56;
it abstains on all the other displayed settings. It is not a useful tight bound.
The historical confidence claim is unconditional P(pass AND false bound)<=alpha,
not selective coverage P(false bound | pass)<=alpha.

Keeping full timestamps is the strong conventional comparator. Both methods get
the same rotor prior F=aI+omega J, .05<=a<=2, |omega|<=12, and unknown inherited
response fit from the data. At 50,000 pairs, full-rank weak/conditional regression
RMS errors are .06474/.02744 at omega1.5 and .53138/.03857 at omega8. With one
scalar inherited mark the corresponding errors are .23156/.06731 and
.23261/.03430. Conditional regression wins every displayed aggregate fixture.
Its implementation is variable-projection least squares with multistart, not a
claim of an efficient exact likelihood solver.

There is a full-law impossibility at missing excitation: S_0=(.7,0)^T,
F_1=diag(.7,1), F_2=diag(.7,9), late stationary covariance I in both models and
appropriate diffusion. Every independently sampled destructive-pair Gaussian
law agrees at every T. The second rate is unidentifiable. Even good semigroup
fits do not prove closure: shared inherited hidden OU H plus private stationary
OU Y can make all cross-kin moments see H's rate while the direct coefficient
of X=H+Y differs. Survival-dependent selection and nonlinear drift also change
the estimand. Actual biological assay validation is absent.

10. Statistical and software corrections to the historical checkpoint

The earlier 19-setting design has an all-estimator gain-calibration bottleneck.
For n=8, gamma1=17, gamma2=34 and 10,000 independent raw Gaussian snapshots in
each regime, the exact rational certificate gives two admissible drifts separated
by sqrt(49/50)=.989949494 in Frobenius norm. Their first 17 full observation laws
are identical, and complete KL is less than .491. Thus every estimator has
worst-case RMS at least .248634; a uniformly 95% confidence set must have diameter
at least .989949 with probability at least .404644 on one member. This is not
fixed by simply improving the covariance regression. The forward Wishart model
and shared-baseline efficient Fisher analysis are retained in evidence.
Changing the second damping and reference amplitude improves the local alias
information 7.51-fold but worsens its uniform settling bound 33-fold.

The old exact integer checker incorrectly accepted exponent zero, permitting a
nonidentifying malicious design. Saved positive-power designs still pass. The
version-2 checker rejects that fixture, checks literal integer data and valid
powers, and works with python -O because validation uses explicit errors. The
old files are preserved as historical bytes. Dense generation/eigen diagnostics
have O(n^4) matrix memory and conventional O(n^6) eigensolve work; a finite small
design certificate is not an efficient all-dimension construction.
