THERMAL CALIBRATION BRIDGE — adversarial scope check
23 October 2026. Fresh algebraic audit; no source outputs changed.

1. VALID BRIDGE, WITH NECESSARY ASSUMPTIONS

Take G=-K/2-iH, H=H†, K=sum_i Q_i, Q_i>=0, and assume G is Hurwitz.
Let L_G(Q)=integral_0^infinity exp(Gt)Q exp(G†t)dt, so
G L_G(Q)+L_G(Q)G†=-Q. Then C=L_G(sum_i n_i Q_i+D_p).
Assume fixed H,K,Q_i,D_p throughout bath-occupation interventions.

Measurement must be a fixed instantaneous invertible square matrix S, with
additive sensor process independent of the physical process. Its entire
covariance kernel R(tau), including R(0), is invariant under those interventions.
Use lag convention Gamma(tau)=E[Y(t+tau)Y(t)†], tau>=0. Then
Gamma(tau)=S exp(G tau) C S†+R(tau). Invariant sensor autocovariance alone is
insufficient if uncontrolled state-sensor cross-correlations are also present.

Since G+G†=-K, uniqueness gives L_G(K)=I. Therefore a known nonzero common
occupation shift Delta n of ALL noise/damping bath contributions gives
Delta C=Delta n I even when the fixed pump noise D_p is nonzero. Hence

  M=Delta Omega/Delta n=S S†>0, U=M^(-1/2)S is unitary,
  M^(-1/2) Delta Gamma(tau) M^(-1/2)/Delta n
      =U exp(G tau)U†.

For an individual bath define C_i=L_G(Q_i), T_i=M^(-1/2)
(Delta Omega_i/Delta n_i)M^(-1/2)=U C_i U†. With G_u=U G U†,
Q_i,u=-(G_u T_i+T_i G_u†)=U Q_i U†. This is exact calibration up to a unitary
coordinate gauge. No absolute physical coordinate basis has been identified.
The truly necessary reference intervention is Delta D=Delta n K; shifting every
bath occupation equally is one sufficient way to realize it.

Recovering exp(G tau) at one lag does NOT uniquely recover G. A calibrated local
derivative/continuous lag curve, or an adequate frequency/logarithm branch bound,
is needed. Even one mode has omega versus omega+2pi k/tau0 aliases on the grid
tau=m tau0. A calibrated time unit is also necessary to remove rate scaling.

Hurwitz stability/unique stationarity is essential, not implied by passivity:
H=0,K=diag(1,0),Q_1=K leaves a dark constant random mode. Shifting the only bath
can give Delta C=Delta n diag(1,0), with dark variance unchanged, rather than
Delta n I. Merely saying 'stationary' does not exclude this counterexample.

2. EXPLICIT PUMP/SENSOR COUNTEREXAMPLE WITH G,Q_i,M ALL KNOWN

One complex proper-Gaussian mode; choose any known omega>0 and
G=-1-i omega, K=2, Q_1=Q_2=1, S=1. Both bath occupations are controllable.
The physical stationary covariance is C=(n_1+n_2+D_p)/2.

Model A: D_p=0, C_A=(n_1+n_2)/2,
         R_A(tau)=(1/2)exp(G tau), tau>=0.
Model B: D_p=1, C_B=(n_1+n_2)/2+1/2,
         R_B(tau)=0.

Extend R_A to negative lags by R_A(-tau)=conjugate(R_A(tau)). It is a valid
positive covariance kernel: an independent stationary complex OU sensor with
drift G, diffusion 1, and variance 1/2 realizes it. Thus this is not an arbitrary
nonphysical choice of a covariance function. A common extra sensor-noise floor
can be added to both models if a particular readout requires it.

For every occupation setting both observed processes have
  Gamma_Y(tau)=[(n_1+n_2)/2+1/2]exp(G tau).
All stationary Gaussian observation laws, all lag covariances, all common and
individual occupation responses, M=1, G, and Q_1,Q_2 are identical. In fact summing
independent OU physical/sensor processes with the same G makes the whole observed
Gaussian process identical. More lag data cannot locate this noise internally.

Define bath number current INTO the system as j_i=Q_i(n_i-C). At n_1=1,n_2=0:
  Model A: C=1/2, (j_1,j_2)=(1/2,-1/2), pump number input 0.
  Model B: C=1,   (j_1,j_2)=(0,-1),       pump number input 1.
Both satisfy stationarity sum_i j_i+D_p=0. For this single fixed-frequency mode,
heat currents are hbar omega times these number currents, so absolute heat
currents also differ despite known G,Q_i,M and identical observation laws.

General form: for any Delta D>=0 let P=L_G(Delta D)>=0. Transfer covariance
P from an independent sensor OU component into the physical pump:
  D_p,B=D_p,A+Delta D, C_B=C_A+P,
  R_A(tau)=R_B(tau)+S exp(G tau)P S†.
This preserves every occupation-controlled observation. Bath number currents
j_i=Tr[Q_i(n_i I-C)] change by -Tr(Q_i P). Trace balance gives
sum_i Tr(Q_i P)=Tr(Delta D). This is an exact nonidentifiability, not poor fitting.

For general matrices, calling j_i a heat current requires a specified energy
observable. For a fixed one-particle energy E the bath energy flux is
Tr[E(n_i Q_i-(Q_i C+C Q_i)/2)]. Do not silently identify the number current with
heat in a multimode converter. If G/H and the energy per quantum are both rescaled,
the heat-current scaling factor need not equal the number-current scaling factor.

3. PARTIAL/UNCONTROLLED BATHS: WRONG METRIC CAN LOOK INTERNALLY CONSISTENT

If only Q_c=sum_(controlled i) Q_i is shifted, the response is
P=L_G(Q_c), with 0<=P<=I, and observed M_c=S P S† rather than S S†.
P=I iff Q_c=K under the Hurwitz assumption. If P is singular, full whitening fails.
If P>0, let U_c=M_c^(-1/2)S P^(1/2), which is unitary. The whitened lag is
  U_c exp(G_eff tau)U_c†, G_eff=P^(-1/2)G P^(1/2).
This is generally a similarity gauge, not a unitary gauge of physical G.
Moreover G_eff+G_eff†=-P^(-1/2)Q_c P^(-1/2). Consequently the inferred sum of
CONTROLLED bath responses automatically equals the inferred damping. That closure
test alone cannot establish that every physical bath was controlled.

Exact one-mode witness, even with D_p=R=0 and known G=-1-i omega:
  A: (Q_c,Q_u)=(1,1),   S=1,       n_u=0, C=n_c/2.
  B: (Q_c,Q_u)=(1/2,3/2), S=sqrt(2), n_u=0, C=n_c/4.
Both have K=2. Every accessible occupation setting and lag has observed
Gamma(tau)=(n_c/2)exp(G tau), and M_c=1/2. But true S S† is 1 versus 2.
Controlled bath number current is n_c/2 versus 3n_c/8. Whitening M_c falsely
assigns the entire effective K=2 to the controlled bath in both models.
Thus neither the physical metric, individual couplings, nor absolute currents
are generically acquired by an incomplete common shift with unknown S,Q_i.
Known aggregate proportionality Q_c=f K would repair the metric as M=M_c/f;
the obstruction concerns the absence of that extra calibration.

4. WHAT ACTUALLY REMOVES THE INTERCEPT AMBIGUITY

If sensor noise is temporally white so R(tau)=0 at a known positive lag, then
  C_u=exp(-G_u tau) M^(-1/2)Gamma(tau)M^(-1/2)
recovers absolute covariance. With absolute n_i and all Q_i known in that gauge,
D_p,u=-G_u C_u-C_u G_u†-sum_i n_i Q_i is then identified. A known nonzero R(tau)
at that lag works after subtraction as well. Conditioning may be poor; this is
an exact identifiability statement, not a low-sample method.

Alternatively a trusted reference covariance C_ref identifies R(0). A pump-off
reference suffices only if its physical covariance is known from calibrated bath
occupations/couplings and the relevant drift, and sensor covariance is invariant
between reference and target. Merely toggling a pump with an unknown reference
covariance or changed sensor noise does not suffice. Known D_p=0 as a structural
physical premise also removes that particular pump ambiguity.

Strong retained result: all-bath common occupation calibration plus time-resolved
differential lag can identify the physical Euclidean measurement metric and
drift/bath couplings up to unitary gauge. Arbitrary invariant colored sensor noise
still prevents absolute state/pump/current identification. Partial bath control
may fail even the metric step, and its failure can pass an internal closure test.
