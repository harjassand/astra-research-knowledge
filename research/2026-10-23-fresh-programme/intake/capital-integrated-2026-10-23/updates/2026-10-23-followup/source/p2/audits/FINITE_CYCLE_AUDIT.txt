MAIN CONCLUSION

The steady COP in the dossier is an asymptotic operating-rate comparison.
For a finite completed cycle, startup, shutdown, recovery, auxiliary control,
and any preparation/reset of consumed resources must be included. Restoring
the initial stored energy removes an endpoint term; it does not make charging,
switching, absorption, or reset free. The exact finite-cycle comparison below
does not require transient heat or work corrections to have a prescribed sign.

1. DEFINE A COMPLETED CYCLE AND ITS ENERGY PORTS

Let t=0 and t=T have the same device state, pump-store state, relevant control
store states, and Hamiltonian settings. A convenient contract has an empty
coherent pump and zero pump-induced signal coupling at both endpoints. Any
temporary thermal buffer or finite body used as a resource must also be reset,
or its energy/entropy change must remain explicitly in the ledger. A residual
charged battery, warm support, or cooled finite body is not a reset device.

Define Q_c and Q_h as integrated heat OUT OF the cold and hot reservoirs,
respectively; refrigeration has Q_c>0 and normally Q_h<0. Count optical heat,
support leakage, pump deposition, shutdown heat, and subsequent reset heat
in their actual destination reservoirs. At the external wall boundary, let
W_wall be net supplied work, including credited recovered work. Then

  Delta E_all_stores = W_wall + Q_c + Q_h + sum_r Q_r,other.          (1)

All source/driver losses must appear as heat at a named destination or as a
change in a store. For a fully reset cycle with only two thermal reservoirs,

  -Q_h = Q_c + W_wall,
  W_wall >= (T_h/T_c - 1) Q_c,                                     (2)

where the inequality is the cyclic entropy balance at fixed T_c,T_h. Thus
COP_cycle=Q_c/W_wall cannot exceed T_c/(T_h-T_c). Additional heat ports need
their own temperatures and entropy terms; do not apply the two-bath form
unchanged to an uncounted ambient sink. Finite reservoirs require integrated
entropy changes rather than assigning one fixed temperature to the process.

The exact heat definition is reservoir energy change. A reduced master
equation must reproduce that definition: during switching it can require
Tr[H(t) D_r rho], including interaction contributions. Bare oscillator
occupation currents alone are not a general transient calorimeter.

2. PUMP STORAGE, LOSS, RECOVERY, AND SOURCE EFFICIENCY

For a consistently chosen pump subsystem, its integrated balance is

  E_p,in - E_p,out + W_p,control
    = Delta U_p + E_p,abs + E_p,dump + W_p->signals.                  (3)

Here E_p,out is energy returned through a designated coherent recovery port;
E_p,dump is unrecovered outgoing energy, distinct from internal absorption.
W_p->signals is full energy delivered across the chosen pump/signal boundary,
including any assigned boundary interaction contribution. Such interaction
energy must be included once in the subsystem energy partition. At fully
decoupled reset endpoints no boundary interaction energy remains.

Equation (3) prevents two opposite errors: charging stored U_p repeatedly as
if it were steady dissipation, and declaring U_p free because Delta U_p=0.
If U_p is filled and later absorbed, the fill energy reappears in E_p,abs.
Do not add it a second time. If it is coherently returned, use its ACTUAL
recovery destination and conversion efficiency.

For positive delivered optical energy E_+, externally returned optical
energy E_-, source efficiency eta_src, and return-to-wall efficiency eta_ret,

  W_wall,pump = E_+/eta_src - eta_ret E_-.                           (4)

For variable efficiencies use the corresponding time integrals. A source
cannot generally be assigned the same efficiency for generation and return.
Other electrical/control/preparation ports are added with their own signed
inputs, recovered outputs, and efficiencies. If an optical store is recycled
internally and restored each cycle, only its net top-up crosses E_+; do not
also claim the recycled light as an external wall-energy credit.

For example, a pure U_s charge and return r U_s contributes
U_s/eta_src - eta_ret r U_s when it crosses the external source/recovery
interfaces. Internal optical reuse instead requires (1-r) U_s/eta_src of
top-up, provided r already includes recovery losses and the optical store
returns to its initial state. Both statements use different explicit port
boundaries. Charging loss during fill and all ramp-control loss are additional.

3. CONVERSION WORK AND TURN-ON/OFF INTERACTION ENERGY

The laboratory-frame prescribed-pump signal Hamiltonian is

  H_s(t) = hbar omega_c n_c + hbar omega_h n_h + V(t),
  V(t) = hbar [g(t) exp(-i Omega t) a_h^dagger a_c + h.c.],
  Omega = omega_h - omega_c.

Let C(t)=exp(-i Omega t)<a_h^dagger a_c>. Within this resonant rotating-wave
model, the conversion current and drive power are

  I(t) = 2 Im[g(t) C(t)],
  P_drive(t) = <partial_t V(t)>
             = hbar Omega I(t) + 2 hbar Re[dot g(t) C(t)].           (5)

Thus W_drive = W_conversion + W_ramp. The ramp term includes envelope and
phase changes and is not fixed by the steady photon current. The interaction
energy is E_int=2 hbar Re[g C]. With g(0)=g(T)=0, Delta E_int=0 exactly,
even if signal correlations persist. This endpoint cancellation does NOT
imply W_ramp=0. Ramp work can be balanced by interaction-energy contributions
to heat/other ports during the protocol. Its sign and recovery depend on the
actual protocol. Switching reservoir contacts can have additional work
integrals <partial_t H_contact>; driver dissipation must also be counted.

Use one of two consistent descriptions: (a) include the physical pump mode
and controllers in the dynamical energy ledger, or (b) prescribe g(t) and
charge the complete drive work in (5), together with pump generation losses
and storage. W_conversion is a transfer from the pump in (a), not an extra
external charge to add again to already measured source input. Likewise,
allocate W_ramp once to the actual pump/control port that supplies it.

No universal monotonicity or favorable sign of the ramp or relaxation terms
is assumed. The rotating-frame interaction Hamiltonian alone is insufficient
for laboratory-frame work accounting.

4. COLD-SIDE SHUTDOWN DEPOSITION AND NORMALIZED DWELL TIME

An explicitly solvable special shutdown is useful for bookkeeping. First
disable signal conversion, then let an isolated pump mode of energy U_off
ring down through fixed energy-decay rates kappa_abs, kappa_rec, kappa_dump.
Let kappa_tot be their sum, and let f_abs,c and f_dump,c be fixed fractions
of the corresponding deposited energies that ultimately reach the cold body.
For a finite ringdown interval t_d, let b_d=1-exp(-kappa_tot t_d). Then

  U_p(t)=U_off exp(-kappa_tot t),
  U_remaining = (1-b_d) U_off,
  E_abs,off  = (kappa_abs /kappa_tot) b_d U_off,
  E_rec,off  = (kappa_rec /kappa_tot) b_d U_off,
  E_dump,off = (kappa_dump/kappa_tot) b_d U_off,
  E_c,off   = lambda_c U_off,
  lambda_c  = b_d (f_abs,c kappa_abs + f_dump,c kappa_dump)/kappa_tot. (6)

Return-port inefficiency and delayed heat from warm material/supports must
be added at their actual destinations. If coupling remains on, rates vary,
or deposition fractions are not fixed, use their actual integrated currents;
equation (6) is a declared ringdown case, not a universal transient bound.
At every finite t_d, passive ringdown leaves U_remaining>0. A completed finite
cycle requires an explicit final recovery/dump operation for this remainder,
including its cold deposition and control work. Otherwise retain Delta U_p
and do not call the device reset. The b_d->1 limit is infinite passive time,
not an exact finite-duration completion.

For constant coherent stored energy U_s, linear absorptive loss obeys

  P_abs,ss = kappa_abs U_s,
  theta = kappa_abs tau,                                          (7)

where tau is the steady dwell time. kappa_abs is the ABSORPTIVE energy decay
rate, not a field-amplitude decay rate or total port linewidth. U_s denotes
coherent excess pump energy; any incoherent thermal pump fluctuations have
their own heat ledger. The total-energy relation would instead use net bath
loss kappa_abs(U_total-U_thermal) in a simple thermal Markov model.

When U_off=U_s, equation (6) gives the exact ringdown cold debit relative to
the useful steady dwell cooling:

  E_c,off/(Q_ss tau)
    = lambda_c (P_abs,ss/Q_ss)/theta.                              (8)

If the fraction r of U_s is recovered into a reusable optical store, the
unrecovered store energy divided by dwell absorption is (1-r)/theta. For
external generation/return as in (4), the charge-return WALL cost divided
by dwell absorption wall cost P_abs,ss tau/eta_src is

  [1 - eta_src eta_ret r]/theta.                                 (9)

These are particular overhead components, not bounds on every transient.
They show why a large absorption-to-cooling ratio can demand a long dwell
even when pump ringdown itself is fast. The signal-mode relaxation time,
thermal drift, contact switching, and preparation/reset must be checked
separately. Fast extraction is not assumed to have free control or loss.

5. PREPARATION, RESET, AND THE FINITE-CYCLE COMPARISON

Preparation/reset heat belongs in Q_c,Q_h or another named port; its supplied
work belongs in W_wall. For a finite equilibrium resource prepared using
a reference bath T_0 and work alone, the reversible work lower bound is the
increase in
F_(T0)=U-T_0 S (with the appropriate mechanical/chemical terms if needed).
Actual preparation can cost more. A one-time preparation amortized over N
cycles contributes its actual work divided by N, with N and final states
stated. If preparation is repeated every batch, charge it every batch.

The usual fixed-reservoir refrigeration COP treats the cold heat load as the
service being removed; it does not also classify that heat load as pump work.
If a comparison includes an external bath-conditioning plant, include its
incremental energy on the same boundary for both machines. Do not obtain an
apparent refrigerator by consuming an independently precooled finite body.

Choose a declared steady rate Q_ss>0 and wall rate P_ss>0, and dwell tau.
Both rates must use the SAME boundary and included losses as the cycle.
In particular, the dossier's model COP before source/control losses is not
an empirical wall COP. If P_ss omits a continuing source inefficiency or
auxiliary load that W_wall,cycle includes, E_w contains a term proportional
to tau; it cannot be amortized away by making the dwell longer.
Define the COMPLETE residuals by

  E_c = tau Q_ss - Q_c,cycle,
  E_w = W_wall,cycle - tau P_ss.                                  (10)

E_c includes cold pump fill/shutdown deposition, all cold leakage and reset
heat, and the signed difference between transient extraction and any chosen
steady reference. E_w includes the actual source/recovery accounting, ramp
and auxiliary work, preparation/reset, and signed transient differences.
These definitions are exact; neither residual is automatically nonnegative.

  COP_cycle = (tau Q_ss - E_c)/(tau P_ss + E_w).                    (11)

For a positive denominator, positive refrigeration requires tau Q_ss>E_c.
With COP_ss=Q_ss/P_ss, the exact comparison is

  COP_cycle <= COP_ss  iff  P_ss E_c + Q_ss E_w >= 0,
  COP_cycle - COP_ss = -(E_c + COP_ss E_w)/(tau P_ss + E_w).          (12)

Consequently, assuming finite cycles can only hurt the steady COP is not a
proof: both transient corrections must be obtained from the actual protocol.
If that protocol has CERTIFIED duration-independent bounds |E_c|<=B_c and
|E_w|<=B_w, then for tau>B_w/P_ss,

  |COP_cycle/COP_ss - 1|
   <= [B_c/Q_ss + B_w/P_ss]/[tau - B_w/P_ss].                       (13)

A sufficient condition for relative error <=epsilon is

  tau >= B_w/P_ss + [B_c/Q_ss+B_w/P_ss]/epsilon.                    (14)

This is conditional on the bounds; their values have not been measured or
simulated here. If transients, drift, or reset cost grow with dwell duration,
no duration-independent bound or automatic steady limit has been established.
