VERDICT

The proposed global threshold and unique-root construction are correct for every
finite d > 0 under the stated reduced model. The maximum is attained at finite,
strictly positive contact rates and drive. Positive net cold heat is possible in
that envelope exactly when 0 <= H < Hcrit(d). At H = Hcrit the best net cooling is
zero. This is an existence theorem for independently tunable parameters in the
saturated overlap model, and a necessary upper-bound screen for a real device;
it is not a sufficient material-feasibility certificate or a COP advantage.

The supplied implementation originally had an extreme-small-d numerical edge
from an absolute root bracket/tolerance. This does not invalidate the theorem.
A scale-normalized root construction, given below and communicated to root,
removes that particular issue. The synthetic example d = 1.371621172841498 was
already numerically accurate.

1. DEFINITIONS AND CORRECT PHYSICAL NORMALIZATION

Take A,B,x_dim > 0, Xi = xi N > 0, and assume the optimistic overlap equality

  g^2 = x_dim Xi = x_dim xi N.

The two positive occupation differences are

  Delta0 = n(w1,Tc) - n(w2,Th) > 0,
  Deltaa = n(w1,Th) - n(w1,Tc) > 0.

Define a=A/B, X=x_dim/B, z=4 xi N/B, d=Deltaa/Delta0 > 0, and
S = hbar w1 B Delta0. The audited gross cold heat, from the earlier exact
two-mode bath-current formula, is

  F(a,X,z;d) = Qc/S
    = a X [z(1-d X)-d(a+X+1)] /
      [(a+X+1)(a+X+z X)].                                (1)

Root's remote_cooling returns q=Qc/Q0 with Q0=S a/(a+1).
Consequently a normalized pump loss b z in q corresponds to H z in F, where

  H = b a/(a+1).

If the relevant dissipated pump power is hbar Omega kappa_p N and a fraction
f_c reaches the cold reservoir, then

  H = f_c (Omega/w1) kappa_p / (4 xi Delta0).             (2)

Equation (2) is independent of a and X only if f_c, kappa_p, xi, the bath
temperatures, and mode frequencies are held fixed when the contact rates are
varied. It also assumes that the cold heat deposited by pump loss is linear in
N. These are substantive assumptions, not consequences of the algebra.

Within those assumptions Qnet/S=F-H z, so positive cooling exists iff

  H < sup_{a,X,z>0} F(a,X,z;d)/z.                        (3)

2. ELIMINATE DRIVE EXACTLY

Set r=X/(a+X), t=a+X, and C=r z. Then 0<r<1, t>0, a=(1-r)t,
X=r t, and

  F/z = r(1-r) t/(t+1)
        * [1-d r t-d r(t+1)/C]/(1+C).                   (4)

If d r t >= 1, gross cooling is never positive and this point cannot beat any
H>=0. It suffices to consider 0<t<1/(d r). Put

  alpha=1-d r t > 0,
  beta=d r(t+1) > 0,
  alpha+beta=1+d r.

For C>0, direct differentiation gives

  max_C [alpha-beta/C]/(1+C)
    = [sqrt(alpha+beta)-sqrt(beta)]^2,

  C_star = sqrt(beta) /
           [sqrt(alpha+beta)-sqrt(beta)].              (5)

The derivative changes sign exactly once: its numerator is proportional to
beta+2 beta C-alpha C^2. The objective tends to minus infinity as C->0 and to
zero from above as C->infinity. Hence (5) is the unique global maximum.

After eliminating C,

  h(r,t) = r(1-r) t/(t+1)
           * [sqrt(1+d r)-sqrt(d r(t+1))]^2.            (6)

3. ELIMINATE TOTAL LOW-MODE CONTACT RATE EXACTLY

For fixed r, put u=d r>0 and v=sqrt(t+1). The nonconstant factor in (6) is

  J(v)=(1-v^-2)[sqrt(1+u)-sqrt(u) v]^2,
  1 < v < sqrt((1+u)/u).

Both factors are positive in the interior. Its logarithmic derivative is

  J'(v)/J(v)
    = 2/[v(v^2-1)] - 2 sqrt(u)/[sqrt(1+u)-sqrt(u)v].

Its sign is the sign of sqrt(1+u)-sqrt(u)v^3. There is exactly one stationary
point, it is a maximum, and both endpoints give J=0. Therefore

  t_star = [(1+u)/u]^(1/3)-1,

  max_t h(r,t)
    = r(1-r)[(1+d r)^(1/3)-(d r)^(1/3)]^3.             (7)

This proves the one-dimensional global envelope stated by root, including
its endpoint control. The apparent unbounded a,X domain has been eliminated
without assuming a finite search box.

4. UNIQUE GLOBAL CONTACT OPTIMUM

Set s=[d r/(1+d r)]^(1/3). This is a strictly increasing bijection from
r in (0,1) to s in (0,smax), with smax=[d/(1+d)]^(1/3). Then

  r=s^3/[d(1-s^3)],
  h(s)=s^3[d-(d+1)s^3]/[d^2(1+s+s^2)^3].              (8)

In the interior, logarithmic differentiation yields the stationary equation

  d(1-s^2)=(d+1)s^3(2+s).                             (9)

The left side strictly decreases with s>0, while the right side strictly
increases. At s=0 their difference is d>0. At s=smax their difference is
-d(1+smax+smax^2)<0. Thus exactly one interior root exists.

Equation (8) is positive inside and tends to zero at both endpoints. Its
derivative has the same sign as the difference of the two sides of (9), so
this root is the unique global maximum, not merely a candidate stationary
point. Recover

  t=1/s-1,  a=(1-r)t,  X=r t,
  C=s/(1-s),
  z=C/r=d(1+s+s^2)/s^2,                               (10)

  Hcrit=r(1-r)[(1+d r)^(1/3)-(d r)^(1/3)]^3
       =r(1-r)(1-s)^2/(1+s+s^2).                      (11)

All recovered parameters are finite and positive for every finite d>0.
For H<Hcrit, retaining exactly this operating point gives
Qnet/S=(Hcrit-H)z>0. For H>=Hcrit, (3) excludes positive cooling anywhere.
This completes necessity, sufficiency within the model, and uniqueness of
the threshold tangency. It does not assert that this tangency point maximizes
cooling power or wall COP when H is appreciably below Hcrit.

5. LIMITS, NUMERICAL CHECK, AND IMPLEMENTATION CONDITION

As d->0+, equation (9) gives s^3~d/2, r->1/2, and Hcrit->1/4.
Also t~(2/d)^(1/3)->infinity, so the d=0 boundary has a supremum 1/4 rather
than a finite-contact maximizing construction. Handle d=0 separately or
reject it explicitly; the finite-contact formulas divide by d.

As d->infinity, s->(sqrt(5)-1)/2 and

  Hcrit~(5 sqrt(5)-11)/(8d)=0.02254248594.../d.

For every d>0, 0<Hcrit<1/4. It strictly decreases with d: for every fixed
interior r the positive difference of cube roots in (7) strictly decreases
with d, and the maximum is attained at an interior r.

For root's synthetic d=1.371621172841498, independent substitution gives

  Hcrit = 0.013598075101448599,
  A/B   = 0.7238299407121469,
  X     = 0.11703165746627792,
  z     = 8.54469655176972.

Substitution into (1), followed by division by z, yields
0.013598075101448597. This is an algebraic/numerical diagnostic, not evidence
that a material supplies the required coupling or loss parameters.

An initial implementation using a fixed lower s bracket of 1e-14 and
brentq's default absolute tolerance returned Hcrit=9.9999899999997e-7 at
d=1e-36, with relative stationary-equation residual 0.999998. The correct
value there is near 1/4. Root was notified and supplied the following remedy:

  q=[d/(1+d)]^(1/3),  s=q y,
  solve 1-q^2 y^2-y^3(2+q y)=0 on [0,1],
  r=y^3/[1+d(1-y^3)].

This normalized equation has a root bounded away from both numerical
endpoints, including the small-d limit. Use (11) for the threshold and
require finite d>0. Other extreme floating-point overflow limits still
require a declared numerical domain if such parameters are supported.
The reviewer did not edit root's model.py.

6. PHYSICAL SCOPE, PUMP WORK, AND WHAT THE THEOREM DOES NOT PROVE

(a) The Cauchy overlap result is an upper bound g^2<=x_dim xi N. Equality
requires compatible field profiles and a well-defined dissipative operator.
Its use here is optimistic. The relevant dissipative operator must represent
coupling to the hot thermal bath; an arbitrary material-loss norm or driven
nonthermal noise source cannot simply be relabelled as x_dim. The support/
inverse-domain condition and local-master validity conditions recorded in
THERMAL_REVIEW.txt remain necessary.

(b) Real changes to A, B, or X generally also change field normalization,
xi, kappa_p, cold deposition fraction f_c, mode frequency, and parasitic
channels. The theorem is a universal upper bound only within the explicitly
specified independent-parameter family. It is not a bound on all possible
thermal converters, nor does H<Hcrit prove an attainable geometry. The
reachable set of simultaneously measured parameters must be supplied.

(c) Conversion work Wconv=hbar Omega I is already fixed by the resonant
two-mode first law, with Omega=w2-w1. It must be included in the power
denominator together with total pump absorption, unrecovered port power,
source inefficiency, and auxiliaries. If kappa_p denotes only absorption,
one consistent steady-state budget is

  Pwall=[hbar Omega I+hbar Omega kappa_p N+P_unrecovered]/eta
        +P_aux,
  COPwall=Qnet/Pwall.

Definitions must prevent counting a port loss twice. In the stated regime
the mixed occupation difference is Delta0(1+d r)>0, so I>0 and conversion
work is positive. Including this positive denominator does not change the
sign threshold (3), provided no omitted power is also deposited on the cold
body. It changes efficiency, the optimal operating point, and practical
utility. At H=Hcrit, COP is zero. Moving absorption to the hot side decreases
f_c but does not eliminate its wall-energy cost.

(d) Bounds on drive N, maximum g, linewidth ratios, achievable rate ranges,
control bandwidth, or heat rejection can exclude the point in (10). Extra
cold parasitic load lowers the gate further. Likewise, if pump heating
changes temperatures or material coefficients, H and d are not constants
and this fixed-parameter theorem is no longer the complete problem.

(e) Neither the theorem nor the numerical example establishes a new physical
principle, a material response, an executed experiment, or superiority to
conventional refrigeration. It is a rigorous loss-and-contact screen for
one existing frequency-conversion mechanism, useful for rejecting interfaces
whose measured dissipation/coupling ratio is too large.
