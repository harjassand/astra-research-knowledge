VERDICT
The stated resonant heat-flow formulas, special hot-overlap expression and
positive-cooling gate are algebraically correct under their explicit reduced

1. INDEPENDENT DERIVATION OF CROSS-BATH HEAT
Steady populations are n1=nbar1-I/K1 and n2=nbar2+I/K2.
For mode j, Qjc=hbar wj K_j f_j (njc-nj). Substitution gives
 Qc=hbar(w1 f1-w2 f2)I
    -sum_j hbar wj K_j f_j(1-f_j)(njh-njc).
Hot-reservoir heat, positive out of that reservoir, is
 Qh=hbar[w1(1-f1)-w2(1-f2)]I + the same leak sum.
Therefore Qc+Qh=-hbar(w2-w1)I. For resonant conversion the source must supply
Wconv=hbar(w2-w1)I, before absorption and unrecovered output ports.
Each term in the pump-off leak follows from coupling one mode to both baths;
it must not be omitted just because the converter is physically on the hot side.

2. SPECIAL HOT-OVERLAP EXPRESSION
For K1=A+x, f1=A/(A+x), K2=B, f2=0, g^2=x Xi,
 nbar1-nbar2=Delta0+x Deltaa/(A+x).
Direct elimination reproduces exactly
 Qc/(hbar w1)= A x [4Xi(B Delta0-x Deltaa)
                      -B Deltaa(A+x+B)]
              /[(A+x+B)((A+x)B+4xXi)].
For A,B,Xi>0, Deltaa>0 and Delta0>0, some x>0 gives positive cooling iff
 Xi>(A+B)Deltaa/(4Delta0),
and the stated strict upper bound on x follows by solving the numerator's
linear inequality. At equality only zero heat is possible. If the formula is
used as the saturation of an upper bound g^2<=xXi, passing the gate is necessary
but NOT sufficient for a realizable device. The x=0 endpoint has zero coupling
and zero heat; it is not a finite-power lossless refrigerator.

3. GENERIC LOSS-LAW THRESHOLD
For q(C)=C/(1+C)-b C^p-ell, C>0, b>=0, 0<p<=1, put s=C/(1+C).
Positive cooling requires b<(s-ell)[(1-s)/s]^p at some ell<s<1.
Differentiating gives s(1-s)=p(s-ell), hence the supplied positive root.
The domain must state 0<=ell<1; for ell>=1 there is no cooling. At
(p,ell)=(1,0), the formula has a removable boundary singularity and bcrit=1
as s->0; it is a supremum, not an interior tangency. For finite Cmax,
smax=Cmax/(1+Cmax); if smax<=ell no cooling exists, otherwise clip the
maximizing s to min(s*,smax). Root threshold() already handles these cases.

4. EXACT SCOPE OF THE LOSSY-OVERLAP CAUCHY BOUND
A rigorous abstract form is
 g=<u1,V u2>, x=<u1,Gamma_h u1>,
 Xi=<V u2,Gamma_h^+ V u2>,
where Gamma_h is positive semidefinite and V u2 lies in its range (or the
corresponding weighted inverse domain in infinite dimension). Cauchy-Schwarz
then gives |g|^2<=x Xi. If V u2 has a component in ker Gamma_h, there is no
finite such bound without another restriction: coupling in a lossless channel
cannot be bounded by a loss quadratic form that does not see it.
The normalization and frequency factors must be derived for the actual
electromagnetic energy norm; this abstract notation cannot supply them by fiat.
Gamma_h must describe loss thermally coupled to the HOT reservoir if x is used
as the hot-bath rate above. Total dielectric absorption in a differently anchored
layer is not interchangeable with x. A driven nonthermal loss channel requires
its noise spectrum, not a Bose bath at an assigned temperature.
Equality requires a mode-matching condition and may violate Maxwell geometry,
normalization, passivity or pump limits. Calling the saturated bound an optimistic
envelope is correct. It is not a universal material bound and Xi may depend on
pump amplitude, geometry and mode normalization.

5. PHYSICAL VALIDITY AND ENERGY LIMITS
The formulas assume independent Markov reservoirs, flat spectra/occupations
over the relevant hybrid-mode/sideband widths, positive damping, a stable
number-conserving difference-frequency interaction, undepleted or replenished
coherent pump, and controlled rotating-wave errors. For a simple real sinusoidal
modulation, counter-rotating conversion terms oscillate at twice the pump
frequency; smallness relative to the optical carrier alone is not sufficient
when the frequency difference is small. Coupling and linewidths relative to the
modulation frequency must also be checked unless single-sideband engineering
removes those terms. A global/Floquet treatment is needed when these assumptions
fail. Near threshold or near the Carnot limit, small neglected corrections can
exceed the claimed cooling margin.
Root's off-resonant covariance checks verify that stated approximate dynamical
model. They do not establish exact off-resonant heat/work thermodynamics; the
code comments correctly avoid that promotion. Resonant conclusions remain the
ones reviewed here.
Pump absorption, source efficiency, control electronics, support conduction,
other optical channels and any cooling needed by the pump source must be inside
the final device boundary. Displacing absorption to the hot stage can improve
cold heat but still increases wall work. Subtracting pump heat in Qnet and adding
the same total absorption to source work is correct, not double counting.
Coherent outgoing power can be credited only if recovered in the proposed
system. A held thermal reservoir is not a free auxiliary cooling apparatus.

6. CODE LIMITATIONS FOUND
The current positive-loss numerical tables do not show an algebraic error.
One unused helper edge case: best_remote(a,d,0,zmax=infinity) sets z=infinity
and evaluates inf/inf, producing NaN. It should reject the undefined unlimited-
drive limit, require finite zmax, or return an explicitly labeled supremum.
The outer scipy bounded minimization is numerical optimization. Without a
unimodality proof or interval/global certificate, its result should be called
numerically optimized, not an exact global optimum. The fixed-x elimination
and feasibility formulas are analytic. Unlimited-drive optima that exceed the
physical/RWA range are envelopes, not operating points.
Some synthetic random checks have coupling comparable to a linewidth by design;
this is fine for checking algebra, but it is not proof that all sampled parameters
are within a controlled electromagnetic approximation.

7. INDEPENDENT NUMERICAL RECEIPT
500 random two-mode examples were separately solved with a complex covariance
equation, using nondimensional hbar=kB=1. Maximum absolute cross-bath heat
discrepancy: 3.8858e-16. Maximum first-law residual: 5.5511e-17. Minimum sampled
resonant entropy production: 4.4670e-5 (positive). Special-form comparison error
relative to 1+|Qc|: 3.5747e-16. See thermal_audit_results.json.
These are consistency checks within the equations, not independent material
validation. No quantitative token or monetary receipt is exposed.

RECOMMENDATION
Retain the scoped formulas and the explicit missing-interface criterion. Do not
describe this as discovery of frequency-converting refrigeration, a universal
no-go result, or a measured technological improvement. The next decisive step is
one specific constitutive model with measured nonlinear response, absorptive
loss and thermal anchoring at all participating frequencies, followed by a
full-wave/Floquet calculation carrying an error margin into net cooling.
