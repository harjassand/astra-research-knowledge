VERDICT
The proposed construction is correct, including its removable nodes and
pointwise bound. For integer n=m/r>=1 it isolates exactly the requested
number-conserving edge and eliminates every resonant pair-creation edge.
Its target overlap is -pi/[4(2n+1)].

There is also a rigorous, tightly scoped optimality theorem: no bounded
scalar susceptibility |chi(theta)|<=chi0 can give a larger target overlap
while eliminating all other j<->j+r edges of the INFINITE, equally spaced
sin(j theta) ladder, with the same fixed cos(r theta) pump profile.
The full bounded nullspace is reflection-odd susceptibility. It allows
nonunique profiles but cannot improve the pointwise-norm bound.

This is not a universal fabrication or refrigeration optimum. Finite useful
spectral bands, dispersion, different pump profiles, different boundary
conditions, frequency-dependent spatial response, or a weighted material/
absorption budget change the optimization problem.

1. CONSTRUCTION, NODES, AND BOUNDEDNESS

Let m=nr, n>=1 and r>=1 integers; k=2n+1 and K=kr=2m+r. Define

  p0(theta)=chi(theta)/chi0=cos(K theta)/[k cos(r theta)]. (1)

There are no physical singularities. The exact finite cosine polynomial is

  p0(theta)=[(-1)^n
       +2 sum_(ell=1)^n (-1)^(n-ell)cos(2 ell r theta)]/k. (2)

At every pump node r theta=pi/2+q pi, its continuous value is (-1)^n.
For x=r theta and y=x-pi/2,

  p0=(-1)^n sin(k y)/[k sin(y)].

The identity sin(k y)/sin(y)=sum_(ell=0)^(k-1)
exp[i(k-1-2ell)y] and the triangle inequality prove |p0|<=1.
The limiting value at the nodes proves ||p0||_infinity=1, also in the
essential-supremum sense because p0 is continuous near each node.

2. EXACT RESONANT OVERLAPS

Define F(theta)=p(theta)cos(r theta) and
C_s=integral_0^pi F(theta)cos(s theta)dtheta. For j>=1,

  J_j=integral_0^pi p cos(r theta)sin(j theta)sin((j+r)theta)dtheta
     =[C_r-C_(2j+r)]/2.                                 (3)

For (1), F=cos(K theta)/k. Since K>r and cosine modes are orthogonal,

  C_r=0,
  C_(2j+r)=pi/(2k) delta_(j,m),
  J_j=-pi/(4k) delta_(j,m).                              (4)

For pair creation j+l=r, j,l>=1, its spatial coefficient is

  [C_(|j-l|)-C_r]/2=0,                                  (5)

because |j-l|<r<K. Degenerate pairs j=l are included. This is an exact
selection rule for resonant terms in the specified scalar model, not proof
that counter-rotating, detuned, tensor, or other-polarization channels vanish.

3. COMPLETE BOUNDED NULLSPACE OF THE NUMBER-CONSERVING MAP

Consider any bounded q(theta) for which every J_j is zero. Put
F_q=q cos(r theta). Equation (3) requires C_(2j+r)=C_r for all j>=1.
F_q is integrable, so the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma gives C_(2j+r)->0.
Consequently C_r=0 and every C_(2j+r)=0.

Let epsilon=(-1)^r. The reflection-parity projection

  F_parallel(theta)=[F_q(theta)+epsilon F_q(pi-theta)]/2

contains exactly the cosine indices with the same parity as r. All of its
indices >=r are zero. Thus F_parallel=P(cos(theta)), where P is a polynomial
of degree <r (only indices 0<=s<r with s congruent to r modulo 2 occur).
For r=1 this low-index space is empty.

Because cos(r(pi-theta))=epsilon cos(r theta),

  F_parallel=cos(r theta) q_even(theta),
  q_even=[q(theta)+q(pi-theta)]/2.

Bounded q makes q_even bounded. Therefore P must vanish at every one of the
r distinct pump nodes theta_l=(2l+1)pi/(2r), l=0,...,r-1. Otherwise the ratio
P(cos(theta))/cos(r theta) diverges on a neighborhood of that node. The r
values cos(theta_l) are distinct, and a polynomial of degree <r with these
r zeros is identically zero. Hence q_even=0 almost everywhere.

Conversely, every bounded reflection-odd q, satisfying
q(pi-theta)=-q(theta), gives J_j=0: each resonant overlap kernel is
reflection-even. The same reflection argument makes every resonant
pair-creation overlap zero. Therefore the complete bounded nullspace is
exactly the reflection-odd functions, up to irrelevant measure-zero values.

This extra boundedness argument is needed: Riemann-Lebesgue alone removes
the common C_r term but initially leaves finitely many low cosine terms.
Those low terms are eliminated by regularity at all pump nodes.

4. OPTIMAL COEFFICIENT AND NONUNIQUENESS

Suppose a bounded p, |p|<=1, has J_j=0 for every j!=m and target overlap J_m.
Set alpha=-4k J_m/pi. The known solution alpha p0 has the same complete
number-conserving map. Their difference is in the nullspace just proved.
Therefore

  p(theta)=alpha p0(theta)+q(theta),
  q(pi-theta)=-q(theta),
  p_even=alpha p0.                                      (6)

Reflection averaging cannot increase the essential supremum norm:

  |p_even(theta)| <= [|p(theta)|+|p(pi-theta)|]/2 <= 1.

Since ||p0||_infinity=1, this proves |alpha|<=1 and hence

  |J_m| <= pi/[4(2n+1)].                                (7)

This proof also applies to complex bounded p. It does not assume real or
mirror-symmetric fabrication. The proposed p0 attains the bound, so (7) is
sharp. For integer n, exact isolation of all number-conserving edges already
forces the same even part; all the pair-creation zeros then follow too.

The susceptibility is not unique. For real profiles, for example,

  q(theta)=eta [1-|p0(theta)|] cos(theta), |eta|<=1,

is reflection-odd and preserves |p0+q|<=1 while leaving every resonant
coefficient unchanged. Such nullspace components can change nonresonant
couplings and fabrication properties. They cannot increase the target
overlap under the uniform pointwise bound.

For completeness, if one also requires every resonant pair-creation zero,
integer m/r is necessary for a nonzero bounded exact single-edge solution
with this pump. The number-conserving and pair-creation constraints together
fix F_parallel=a cos((2m+r)theta). Bounded division by cos(r theta) requires
the numerator to vanish at the first pump node pi/(2r), so (2m+r)/r must be
an odd integer, equivalently m/r is an integer. This necessity statement
does not apply if pair-creation channels are allowed: low cosine terms can
then supply the node-cancelling remainder for a noncommensurate target.

5. PUMP PENALTY, VERIFICATION, AND PHYSICAL LIMITS

The uniform-chi target overlap is pi/4, so the isolated-edge amplitude is
smaller by exactly 1/k. For the SAME target mode pair, quantum frequency
normalization cancels from this ratio. Holding linear profiles, pump mode
energy normalization, and |chi0| fixed, the same target g requires k times
the pump field amplitude and k^2 times the stored pump energy. Absorption
power scales by k^2 only if pump damping is unchanged.

For m=10,r=2: n=5, k=11, J_m=-pi/44=-0.07139983303613166,
and the stored-pump/unchanged-absorption penalty is 121 relative to uniform.
Independent numerical quadrature using polynomial (2) checked j=1,...,80:
maximum absolute discrepancy from (4) was 2.313e-15. The j=l=1 pair-creation
integral was -5.26e-18. The pump-node value was -1. These are numerical
integral diagnostics, not physical measurements.

The optimality statement requires exact suppression over the infinite
equally spaced ladder. A finite transparent/useful band, detunings, or a
tolerable leakage budget leaves additional control freedom and can permit
larger target overlap. No bound of 121 is asserted for those broader designs.
If grading reduces material absorption, the actual heating penalty need not
equal 121 even though the fixed-profile stored-energy penalty does.

Equation (2) is a smooth finite harmonic prescription, so its apparent ratio
in (1) does not require an unbounded material response. How to realize its
signed, graded amplitude with adequate linear loss and thermal stability
remains UNKNOWN. Effective-medium domain mixtures or tensor rotation would
require their own field/loss calculation; this audit establishes neither.

If an implemented profile has uniform error |delta p|<=varepsilon, a simple
bound is |delta J_j|<=varepsilon*pi/2 by Cauchy-Schwarz. Relative to the
ideal target this is at most 2k varepsilon in raw overlap; frequency factors
and bath populations must still enter a heat calculation. Exact spectral
purity is therefore a demanding fabrication target, not a free consequence
of writing the analytic profile.
