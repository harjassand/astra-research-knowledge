STATUS AND STRONGEST RESULT

An omitted resonant LOWER hot sideband is an explicit conventional failure
mode of the two-mode refrigerator. An exact three-mode, time-periodic linear
model reverses cold heat while remaining stable, with positive pump work and
closed energy balance. It is not evidence that a specified real device has
this parasitic coupling. Its useful output is a quantitative spectral
rejection requirement.

A symmetric dark-mode converter suppresses intermediate thermal noise at
one analysis frequency but, at fixed intermediate damping, does not suppress
that noise integrated over its complete steady-state bandwidth. The exact
integrated coefficient increases with coupling and saturates. A genuinely
lossless intermediate does eliminate its own dissipative noise source;
however, it does not eliminate reciprocal thermal transmission from a hot
terminal. This result is restricted to the stated linear Markov chain, not
a no-go theorem for arbitrary filtered, nonreciprocal, or pulsed devices.

The initial counter-rotating attempt is retained below as a CORRECTED FAILED
CONSTRUCTION. Counting only bare occupation energy produced apparent

1. EXACT THREE-MODE LOWER-SIDEBAND COUNTEREXAMPLE

1.1 Physical assumptions and Hamiltonian

Use cold central frequency omega_c, hot upper frequency omega_h=omega_c+Omega,
and an unwanted hot lower frequency omega_l=omega_c-Omega>0. The specified
laboratory Hamiltonian is number conserving and explicitly periodic:

 H(t)/hbar = sum_j omega_j a_j^dagger a_j
     + [g_plus a_h^dagger a_c exp(-i Omega t)
        + g_minus a_c^dagger a_l exp(-i Omega t) + h.c.].

The desired process converts c -> h; the parasitic process converts l -> c.
Both consume one drive quantum when they proceed upward in frequency. The
lower hot mode has larger Bose occupation, so its upward conversion can
heat the cold central mode. This is an omitted resonant conversion channel,
not a counter-rotating pair-creation claim.

Assume independent local thermal Markov baths, occupations evaluated at each
mode carrier, and spectrally flat rates over the narrow linewidth/splitting
of each mode. Couplings and linewidths are small compared with carriers and
mode separations. The Hamiltonian above is exact as a declared model; its
derivation from a real permittivity modulation remains an implementation
obligation. In the frames rotating at the three mode frequencies, h is
constant and real:

 h = [[0, g_minus, 0], [g_minus, 0, g_plus], [0, g_plus, 0]].

With N_ij=<a_j^dagger a_i>, K_j=sum_r kappa_jr, and
D_jj=sum_r kappa_jr n_jr, the steady covariance is exactly determined by

 A N + N A^dagger + D = 0,   A=-diag(K_j/2)-i h.

The numerical calculation solves this continuous Lyapunov equation. There
is no time-stepping approximation to the rotating-frame steady state.

1.2 Heat, work, and thermodynamic closure

Positive Q_r means heat received by the three-mode system from bath r:

 Q_r = sum_j hbar omega_j kappa_jr (n_jr-N_jj).

Direct-edge covariances are imaginary at exact resonance, so <H_int>=0,
and the interaction contribution to Tr[H(t) D_r rho] vanishes for this
fixture. Thus bare-mode energy heat is also the full local-model heat here,
unlike the failed counter-rotating attempt in Section 3.

Define currents OUT of the cold mode:

 J_l=2 g_minus Im N_c,l,   J_h=2 g_plus Im N_c,h.

The explicit drive work is

 P_drive = hbar Omega (J_h-J_l),

since negative J_l denotes upward l -> c flow. At steady state,

 Q_c + Q_h + P_drive = 0.

The fixtures have nonnegative -Q_c/T_c-Q_h/T_h, stable drift, and positive
normal covariance. These checks establish consistency of this declared
model. They do not establish a microscopic material implementation.

1.3 Closed-form cooling condition

Initially take all three linewidths equal to kappa; the central mode couples
only to the cold bath and both end modes only to the hot bath. Let

 G^2=g_plus^2+g_minus^2,
 B=(g_plus a_h+g_minus a_l)/G,
 n_B=(g_plus^2 n_h+g_minus^2 n_l)/G^2,
 C=4G^2/kappa^2.

Only B couples coherently to c. The orthogonal hot combination is dark.
Although transforming unequal end occupations creates correlated B/dark
noise, the B-c population block closes and the following mean is exact:

 I = (kappa/2) [C/(1+C)] (n_c-n_B),
 Q_c=hbar omega_c I.

Therefore cooling requires

 g_plus^2 (n_c-n_h) > g_minus^2 (n_l-n_c).

For omega_c/(2 pi)=20 THz, Omega/(2 pi)=4 THz, T_c=300 K,
T_h=330 K, the Bose occupations are

 n_h(24 THz,330 K) = 0.031448808808129385.

Thus the exact equal-linewidth threshold is

 |g_minus/g_plus| < sqrt[(n_c-n_h)/(n_l-n_c)]
                  = 0.4106408212047886.

2. DARK-MODE BRIDGE: SELECTED FREQUENCY VERSUS INTEGRATED THERMAL FLUX

2.1 Precisely scoped chain

Consider three modes [a,m,b] on converted resonance, with equal real
couplings g along a-m and m-b, terminal linewidths kappa, and intermediate
linewidth gamma. These can be the interaction-frame equations of a driven
frequency converter. Coupling phases are removable in this loop-free
chain. Assume constant Markov occupations over the complete resonant
triplet. To isolate intermediate thermal noise by linearity, set terminal
source occupations to zero and intermediate source occupation to n_m.

The bright mode B=(a+b)/sqrt(2) couples to m with G=sqrt(2)g; the dark mode
D=(a-b)/sqrt(2) is uncoupled. Both have terminal damping kappa. Define

 C=4G^2/(kappa gamma)=8g^2/(kappa gamma).

With rate matrix K=diag(kappa,gamma,kappa) and A=-K/2-i h, the exact
input-output scattering matrix is

 S(nu)=I-sqrt(K)[-i nu I-A]^{-1}sqrt(K).

At the central analysis frequency nu=0,

 |S_ba(0)|^2 = C^2/(1+C)^2,
 |S_am(0)|^2 = 2C/(1+C)^2.

Increasing C therefore gives almost perfect coherent conversion and small
intermediate added noise in an arbitrarily narrow selected band.

2.2 Exact bandwidth integral

The integrated intermediate-source photon flux emitted through terminal a is

 Phi_m->a = n_m integral[-infinity,infinity] dnu/(2 pi) |S_am(nu)|^2
          = [kappa gamma/(2(kappa+gamma))] [C/(1+C)] n_m.

Proof: the B-m subsystem is an ordinary damped two-mode converter. With
terminal source occupation zero, its steady bright occupation is

 N_B= [gamma/(kappa+gamma)] [C/(1+C)] n_m.

The dark mode receives no intermediate source. Each physical terminal
contains half the bright occupation, so Phi_m->a=kappa*N_B/2. The independent
calculation integrates the exact scattering response over the real line,
including both bright peaks and the tail; it agrees with the covariance
calculation to 1.5e-15 or better for the recorded examples.

At FIXED nonzero gamma, the integrated noise increases with C and saturates
at kappa*gamma*n_m/[2(kappa+gamma)]. For kappa=1,gamma=0.1,n_m=1:

 1          .2500000000         .5000000000          .02272727273
 10         .8264462810         .1652892562          .04132231405
 100        .9802960494         .01960592099         .04500450045
 1000       .9980029960         .001996005992        .04540913632

The missing noise resides in the bright resonances; a central dark feature
does not eliminate its total heat contribution. To convert photon flux to
heat delivered to terminal a multiply by hbar*omega_a within the narrow
carrier-band approximation. Frequency conversion requires its own pump
work ledger; this isolated noise calculation is not a refrigerator COP.

2.3 What a truly lossless intermediate does and does not accomplish

At fixed g, reducing gamma reduces the intermediate thermal injection;
gamma -> 0 genuinely makes its own contribution vanish. This is a physical
loss-reduction benefit and must not be denied by the fixed-gamma result.
However, it is a material and interface requirement, not an inference from
the existence of a dark eigenmode.

Even at gamma=0, hot noise incident from terminal b is transmitted to a.
Since this real loop-free chain is reciprocal, S_ab(nu)=S_ba(nu). With unit
hot-terminal occupation and cold-terminal occupation zero,

 integral dnu/(2 pi) |S_ab(nu)|^2 = 2 kappa g^2/(kappa^2+4g^2).

This tends to kappa/2 at large g. For kappa=1 and g=0.1,1,10, the exact
integrals are .01923076923, .40000000000, .49875311721. A lossless mediator
cannot suppress that reciprocal input while maintaining the same desired
forward transfer kernel. A refrigerator may still work because the relevant
Bose occupations differ across converted frequencies; the point is that
reverse thermal transmission must be included, not set to zero.

Narrow spectral filtering that excludes bright peaks, real reduction in
intermediate loss, or additional controlled nonreciprocal architecture may
improve performance. Each changes this model and must be charged for its
heat throughput, residual noise, pump loss, and control requirements. This
calculation is not an exclusion of those possibilities.

6. BOUNDED CHECK OF ROOT'S SELECTIVE PUMP SYNTHESIS

Root proposed a physically conditional finite pump-control basis u in C^m,
with desired and undesired converted couplings

 g_plus=v_plus^dagger u,   g_minus=v_minus^dagger u,

and measured absorbed power P=u^dagger R u. For the following formula R
must be positive definite on the allowed control subspace. A null space of
R cannot be treated as a free physical control resource; pump-source work
and other loss metrics would then be needed.

For ideal bath separation and equal hot-end linewidth kappa_h, define

 A=[4 hbar omega_c/(kappa_c+kappa_h)]
      [Delta_plus v_plus v_plus^dagger-Delta_minus v_minus v_minus^dagger],
 B=4[v_plus v_plus^dagger+v_minus v_minus^dagger]/(kappa_c kappa_h),

where Delta_plus=n_c-n_h, Delta_minus=n_l-n_c. Then exactly within the
three-mode model,

 Q_c(u)=u^dagger A u/(1+u^dagger B u).

At FIXED absorbed P, write u=sqrt(P) w with w^dagger R w=1. The denominator
becomes w^dagger(R+P B)w. Therefore, if the fraction f of absorbed power
deposited as cold heat is independent of direction, and L is a fixed heat
leak, root's expression has the correct factors and conventions:

 max_direction Q_net(P)=P lambda_max[A,R+P B]-fP-L.

Linewidths here are energy/population damping rates; using amplitude decay
rates without changing the factor 4 would be inconsistent. The absorbed
power P is not automatically the total power supplied by the pump source.

Every eigenvector with a nonzero generalized eigenvalue lies in the span
of {R^-1 v_plus,R^-1 v_minus}. This follows directly from
A w=lambda(R+P B)w. The useful direction problem therefore reduces to an
absorption-weighted 2x2 Gram matrix, regardless of m.

For a specified nonzero desired g_plus and the hard constraint g_minus=0,
define p=v_plus^dagger R^-1 v_plus, s=v_minus^dagger R^-1 v_minus,
q=v_minus^dagger R^-1 v_plus. If s>0 and p s>|q|^2,

 P_min,null=|g_plus|^2/[p-|q|^2/s]
           =P_min,unconstrained/(1-rho^2),
 rho=|q|/sqrt(p s).

For rho=1, the desired nonzero coupling cannot survive the exact null. If
the undesired response is identically zero (s=0), no null constraint is
needed. Near rho=1, the amplification of pump cost also makes calibration
and control errors consequential; the algebra supplies no controllability
or field-stability measurement.

For a cross-coupled central mode, kappa_c must be TOTAL central damping.
Let alpha=kappa_cc/kappa_c and n_bar be its bath-weighted occupation. Replace
Delta_plus by n_bar-n_h and Delta_minus by n_l-n_bar, multiply A by alpha,
and include

 L_thermal=hbar omega_c*kappa_cc*kappa_ch/kappa_c*(n_hc-n_c).

B still uses total kappa_c and kappa_h. This reproduces Section 1.4.

The fixed-f assumption is equivalent to R_cold=f R across the controllable
pump subspace. If pump direction changes deposition, the cold heat load is
u^dagger R_cold u and the single generalized-eigenvalue formula no longer
applies unchanged. Acquiring only total R then is insufficient. Detuning,
linewidth changes, pump-induced occupation changes, nonlinear absorption,
or pump-dependent v also require an extended model.

target is the absorption-weighted Gram information p,s,q, plus the cold
