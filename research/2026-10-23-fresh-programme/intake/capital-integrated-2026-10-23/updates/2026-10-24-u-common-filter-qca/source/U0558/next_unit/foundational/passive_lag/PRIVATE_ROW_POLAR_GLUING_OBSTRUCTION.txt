1. The specific attempted extension and the missing interface
-------------------------------------------------------------
For a product local event, PRODUCT_LOCAL_FILTER_RECTIFICATION.txt uses
one ACTUAL Bob filter V_Y and the SAME mean Bob state/support for all
private Alice outcomes. Its normalized T_Y depends only on Bob's input.

For a JOINT predicate theta(alpha,gamma), a tempting extension is to fix
Alice's private coarse outcome alpha, select the accepted Bob instrument
V_(alpha,Y), compress it to the conditional program support P_alpha,
and polar-normalize each row separately. Even exact scalar compression
  P_alpha V_(alpha,Y)*V_(alpha,Y)P_alpha=p_(alpha,Y)P_alpha
does not supply a single legal Bob channel: both the accepted row and
its partial isometry may depend on Alice's inaccessible private outcome.

The counterexample below has zero dispersion in EVERY supported row,
unchanged referee law, actual noncommuting steering states, a positive
base commuting gap, and perfect conditional live payoff. The obstruction
is to gluing those row maps on the ACTUAL pre-joint-acceptance program,
including same-mass locally heralded implementation. It is not a claim
that every possible reconstruction from a new shared resource fails.

2. Exact two-state operator obstruction
----------------------------------------
Use Bob program states
  psi_0=|0>, psi_1=|+>, rho_alpha=|psi_alpha><psi_alpha|,
  |<psi_0,psi_1>|=1/sqrt(2),
and equal priors. They do not commute. Put
  q=(1+1/sqrt(2))/2=cos^2(pi/8),
  L_0=[I+(Z-X)/sqrt(2)]/2, L_1=I-L_0.                    (R1)
These are orthogonal rank-one Helstrom effects, with
  <psi_alpha,L_alpha psi_alpha>=q for BOTH alpha.
Keep the actual Bob fine outcome gamma. The accepted row alpha is
  V_alpha=|gamma=alpha> tensor L_alpha.
With P_alpha=rho_alpha,
  P_alpha V_alpha*V_alpha P_alpha=q P_alpha.              (R2)
Consequently each row's square-root dispersion is exactly zero. The
row-polar isometry sends psi_alpha to a normalized state in the retained
orthogonal fine-label sector gamma=alpha. It can be completed on its
kernel by the same local default construction used in the product theorem.

Suppose ONE Bob-local TP map on this actual program realized BOTH row
maps, without being given alpha. Reading its retained gamma label would
perfectly distinguish psi_0 from psi_1. For any such map, even a merely
positive TP map used only by pulling back its output POVM, let 0<=M<=I
be the input effect for guess 0. Its equal-prior correct probability is
  (Tr M rho_0+Tr(I-M)rho_1)/2
    =1/2+(Tr M(rho_0-rho_1))/2
    <=1/2+||rho_0-rho_1||_1/4=q.                         (R3)
Here rho_0-rho_1 has eigenvalues +/-1/sqrt(2). The bound is attained by
(R1). Thus the exact minimum mean fine-label error is
  1-q=(2-sqrt(2))/4>0.                                   (R4)
Input-independent ancillas/shared random seeds and complete Bob-local
preprocessing do not help: pull the final POVM back to the original
program. A seed correlated with alpha would itself be an extra side
channel; an already globally accepted gamma is an OUTPUT of the joint
selection and cannot be assumed available at the reconstruction input.

The obstruction remains when Bob may herald an abort. If wrong conclusive
labels are forbidden and row success probabilities are eta_0,eta_1, let
M_fail=I-M_0-M_1. No-error conditions give M_0 psi_1=0 and M_1 psi_0=0.
Therefore
  <psi_0,psi_1>=<psi_0,M_fail psi_1>,
  (1-eta_0)(1-eta_1)>=1/2.                               (R5)
AM-GM gives
  (eta_0+eta_1)/2<=s:=1-1/sqrt(2).                        (R6)
This unambiguous-discrimination optimum is attained by
  M_0=(2-sqrt(2))|-><-|,
  M_1=(2-sqrt(2))|1><1|.
After either correct label, Bob can prepare the desired row output state.
The externally checked correct Helstrom guesses have mass q>s. Thus
retaining their original mass while turning correctness into a local
herald is impossible on the actual private program.

3. A proper two-round checked-game realization
----------------------------------------------
Define ONE game G' with uniform free binary questions x,y and answer
triples (a,alpha,f_A), (b,gamma,f_B), all bits. Accept iff
  a xor b=xy, alpha=gamma, f_A=f_B=1.                     (R7)
Its tensor AND commuting value is exactly q. Winning implies ordinary
CHSH on the marginal bits a,b, whose contraction/vector Cauchy bound is
q. An optimal Bell CHSH strategy with constant matching metadata and
flags 1 attains q. Hence the own base gap is
  epsilon_co=epsilon_*=1-q>0.                             (R8)

Take n=2 independent rounds of G' and the ACTUAL checked set C={1}.
The source uses two independent Bell pairs and one shared uniform bit xi.
Both players receive their own complete question strings upfront.

On Bell pair 1, play optimal real CHSH at round 1: Alice measures Z or X
according to x_1, and Bob measures (Z+X)/sqrt(2) or (Z-X)/sqrt(2) according
to y_1. It wins with probability q for every x_1,y_1 and every fixed
Alice outcome a_1.

On Bell pair 2, Alice measures the complete PVM {P_(x_2),I-P_(x_2)},
where P_0=|0><0| and P_1=|+><+|. Set f_(A,1)=1 for the first outcome
and 0 otherwise, and alpha_1=x_2. Alice's pass has probability 1/2 for
each x_2 and steers Bob to psi_(x_2). Copying x_2 into an old answer is
legal because it is Alice's own input, already given to her.

Bob measures the FIXED PVM (R1) on his half of pair 2 and outputs its
actual outcome gamma_1; set f_(B,1)=1. He never receives x_2 or alpha_1.

At round 2 choose constant matching metadata/flags,
  (a_2,alpha_2,f_(A,2))=(xi,0,1),
  (b_2,gamma_2,f_(B,2))=(xi xor y_2 gamma_1,0,1).          (R9)
The source is a legitimate full-string local quantum strategy. All
success/failure, alpha/gamma, and xi records are retained explicitly.
No workspace erasure or coherent referee-question register is assumed.

The ACTUAL checked-game event W_1 is precisely
  (real CHSH at round1 wins) AND (Alice passes) AND
  (gamma_1=x_2).
Independence of the Bell pairs and the pointwise probabilities give
  P(W_1 | x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)=q^2/2=p                       (R10)
for all 16 full question tuples. The complete question law is unchanged.
On W_1, gamma_1=x_2, so (R9) wins the live G' with probability 1.
The live coarse a_2,b_2 table is uniform on the two winning CHSH pairs:
it is exactly PR at the coarse bit level, while the actual fine xi record
is still retained. This is postselection, not an unconditional quantum
behavior or a violation of the CHSH value bound.

4. Zero private-row dispersion in the actual checked source
-----------------------------------------------------------
Fix tested x_1,y_1 and Alice's supported row (a_1,alpha_1,f_(A,1)=1).
The Bob state BEFORE his row is selected has the form
  |phi_(a_1,x_1)><phi_(a_1,x_1)| tensor rho_(alpha_1)
                         tensor I_(xi)/2.                (R11)
The first factor is the actual real-CHSH steering state; the second is
the nonorthogonal private program. Bob's accepted-row effect is the
tensor product of his CHSH-winning effect, L_(alpha_1), and identity on
xi. Each pure factor accepts with probability q. Therefore its compression
to the actual row support is exactly
  P_row B_(accepted row) P_row=q^2 P_row.                 (R12)
Every supported row has exactly zero polar dispersion. Default/kernel
completion of each separate row is harmless. But changing alpha_1 while
holding a_1,x_1 fixed changes the program to a nonorthogonal state and
changes which retained gamma_1 label the row map must produce.

The common-channel contradiction (R3)-(R4) applies even if Bob is freely
told tested x_1,y_1 and a_1, because those are independent of the unknown
program bit. A same-mass, no-error locally heralded glue would need row
success q^2, while (R6) bounds the average by s<q^2, even if Bob can
prepare the correct real-CHSH fine output from the extra freely supplied
tested data. Thus this is an actual same-event-mass/fine-instrument
obstruction, not merely an arbitrary choice of row normalization.

If the old gamma label is forgotten but Alice's actual live answer xi and
the actual private program are held fixed, Bob must still guess x_2 when
y_2=1, since b_2 xor xi=x_2. Consequently ANY such Bob-local repair has
mean live loss at least (1-q)/2. This prevents a payoff-irrelevant-label
escape at that fixed-source interface. It does not optimize over changing
Alice's strategy and the shared resource; a legal fresh-resource base
strategy is instead bounded by the own game gap (R8).

5. What this decides and what it leaves open
-------------------------------------------
Rowwise scalar effects and zero rowwise dispersion are insufficient for
one all-input local normalization. An additional compatibility condition
must ensure that the row-dependent action can be chosen from the actual
locally available program, or that a payoff-preserving quotient removes
the action's dependence on the private row. In this example the row is
payoff-relevant, and binary program discrimination gives an exact cost.

The pre-joint-selection private record has
  chi(alpha:B)=h(q), H(alpha|B)=ln2-h(q),
  P_guess(alpha|B)=q, H_min,guess=-ln q.                  (R13)
Here h is binary entropy in nats. The average program state has spectrum
q,1-q. The operational guessing definition is explicit; no smooth-entropy
asymptotic statement is used. After the EXTERNAL comparison gamma=alpha,
Bob's conclusive record reveals alpha exactly. Using that output record
to justify a preselection local operation would supply the missing herald.

This is not a counterexample to the frozen projection theorem: (R7) has
a partial-matching predicate and that theorem may use a different legal
construction with a nonzero information cost. It is not a counterexample
to useful low-L/m selection. The matched example has n=2 and
  L/(n-|C|)=ln(2/q^2),
which is a fixed constant, not a quantity tending to zero. Even L/n is
a fixed constant. No claim is made that every helpful coordinate/history
is bad in a large repetition or that arbitrary fresh-resource extraction
is impossible. The general selection gate remains open.
