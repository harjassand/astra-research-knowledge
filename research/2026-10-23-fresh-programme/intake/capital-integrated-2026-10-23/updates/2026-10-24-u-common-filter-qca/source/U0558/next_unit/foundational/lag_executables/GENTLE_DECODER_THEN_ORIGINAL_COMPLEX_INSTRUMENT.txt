Gentle coherent decoder followed by the original complex fine instrument
=======================================================================

Purpose and scope
-----------------
This small exact fixture checks the localization step with the actual original
Bob fine measurement retained after the coherent decoder. It shows a changed
acceptance mass and changed joint fine law while satisfying the full-vector
gentle bound. It is a finite instrument calculation, not a general game or
low-parameter obstruction.

Complete Alice source instrument
--------------------------------
Alice has an input x uniform in {0,1} and one Bell pair Phi+. For x=0 she
measures the computational basis {|0>,|1>}; for x=1 she measures
{|+>,|->}. She outputs alpha=x and the actual outcome flag f=0 (pass) or
f=1 (complement/fail), with the convention below in the table: f=P denotes
the pass state and f=F the orthogonal state. Each branch has probability 1/2
and is retained; Bob is steered respectively to
  (x=0,P): |0>,   (x=0,F): |1>,
  (x=1,P): |+>,   (x=1,F): |->.
The two-outcome measurement is complete for each x. No branch is discarded.
For every x, averaging the two recorded branches gives Bob I/2.

Decoder and original Bob instrument
-----------------------------------
Let t=1/sqrt(2), d=(-t,0,t), and let the decoder POVM be the Helstrom PVM
for the two pass states |0>,|+>:
  D_g=L_g=(I+(-1)^g d dot sigma)/2,  g in {0,1}.
In the computational basis,
  L_0=1/2 [[1+t,-t],[-t,1-t]],  L_1=I-L_0.
It is a genuine decoder, not exact: on either pass state its correct-label
probability is q=(1+t)/2 and its error is r=(1-t)/2.

After coherently copying the decoder outcome with the prescribed square-root
isometry V_D|v>=sum_g |g> sqrt(D_g)|v>=sum_g |g>L_g|v>, Bob applies the
original fine PVM {N_0,N_1}, with N_gamma=(I+(-1)^gamma n dot sigma)/2 and
  n=(-1/2,t,1/2),   d dot n=t.
In matrix form,
  N_0=[[3/4, -1/4-i t/2],[-1/4+i t/2,1/4]],  N_1=I-N_0.
Thus the original fine instrument has nonzero complex amplitudes. Its
projectors do not commute with the decoder projectors: d cross n is
(-1/2,0,-1/2), so [L_0,N_0] != 0. The original instrument is the isometry
U_N|v>=sum_gamma |gamma> tensor N_gamma|v>; it is explicitly applied after
V_D and its fine output gamma and post-measurement qubit are retained.

True-label-copy comparison and full-vector defect
--------------------------------------------------
For a true source label alpha=x and a branch state |v>, compare the actual
full fine-output vector
  |A_v>=sum_(g,gamma) |g,gamma> tensor N_gamma L_g |v>
with the ideal mathematical copy followed by the *same original instrument*
  |I_(x,v)>=sum_gamma |x,gamma> tensor N_gamma |v>.
The original-instrument map U_N is an isometry, so it preserves the vector
difference. Orthogonality of the decoder register gives exactly
  ||A_v-I_(x,v)||^2
    = ||(I-L_x)v||^2 + ||L_(1-x)v||^2
    = 2 e(v,x),
where e(v,x)=1-<v|L_x|v> is the actual decoder error on this preselection
branch. In particular this is <=2e pointwise, not merely after coarse
graining gamma.

Average over the complete Alice source (uniform x and both equally likely
branches): pass error is r and fail error is q for each x. Thus the actual
preselection mean decoder error is e=(r+q)/2=1/2, and the mean squared full
fine-output vector defect is exactly 1=2e. If one explicitly conditions on
the retained pass flag before defining a new normalized source, the error is
r; that is a different, pass-conditioned preselection convention and must not
be substituted for the full-source e without saying so.

Acceptance mass and changed fine law
------------------------------------
Use the predicate theta(u,gamma)=1 iff u=gamma. The ideal true-label-copy
acceptance is gamma=x after the original N measurement. For the complete
source, each x mixture is I/2, so the original gamma bit is uniform and
  p=Pr(theta(x,gamma))=1/2.

In the actual sequence, the joint fine probability on a branch state rho is
  Pr(g,gamma|rho)=Tr(rho L_g) Tr(L_g N_gamma)
    = (1+(-1)^g d dot r)(1+(-1)^(g+gamma) t)/4,
where r is the Bloch vector of rho. This formula uses the decoder outcome and
the subsequent original N instrument, not a replacement decoder-only test.
Since Tr(L_g N_g)=q for both g and sum_g L_g=I, the actual accepted mass is
  p'=Pr(g=gamma)=q=(1+1/sqrt(2))/2 ~= 0.8535533906,
which differs from p. For example, on the x=0 pass branch rho=|0><0|, the
ideal table has only g=0, with gamma probabilities (3/4,1/4). The actual
table has
  P(0,0)=(1+t)^2/4,  P(0,1)=1/8,
  P(1,0)=(1-t)^2/4,  P(1,1)=1/8.
So the full fine law changes and the actual decoder has positive wrong-g
weight. The overall actual gamma marginal remains uniform for the complete
source; this does not restore the joint fine law.

Root/loss bounds for this fixture
---------------------------------
Here sqrt(2e)=1. The acceptance-root difference is
  |sqrt(p')-sqrt(p)|=cos(pi/8)-1/sqrt(2) < 1.
The rejected-mass (loss) root difference is
  |sqrt(1-p')-sqrt(1-p)|=1/sqrt(2)-sin(pi/8) < 1.
Both follow also directly by projecting the actual and ideal full vectors
onto the event or its complement; each projection is contractive. The
pass-conditioned comparison has e=r, p=3/4, p'=q, and both root differences
are likewise bounded by sqrt(2r)=sqrt(1-1/sqrt(2)). This conditional figure
is separate from the complete-source calculation above.

Replay
------
Run `python3 check_gentle_decoder_complex_instrument.py` from this directory.
The standard-library Decimal replay checks the complete branches, decoder
errors, noncommuting complex fine PVM, exact event masses, full fine-law change,
and the vector-defect identity. Formulae above give the exact algebra.
