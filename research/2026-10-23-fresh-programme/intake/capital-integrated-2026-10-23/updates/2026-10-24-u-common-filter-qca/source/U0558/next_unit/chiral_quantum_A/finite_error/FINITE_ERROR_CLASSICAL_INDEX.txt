FINITE-ERROR CLASSICAL LABEL RECORDS CAN FORCE TRIVIAL QCA INDEX
Constructive theorem, 2026-10-24. Frozen STATE_RETURN note unchanged.

Central result
--------------
Fix onsite dimension d>=2 and a known integer STRICT QCA radius R>=1 on the
infinite one-dimensional chain. Let alpha be the already established hidden
auxiliary automorphism; the exact unitary-data-channel/factorization premise
is retained. A product auxiliary preparation is diagonal in the tested local
bases, with tau_i >= p_* I and p_*>0 on the specified finite region.

Set
  C = 9+24 R d,
  delta_0 = 1/[1540 R d C],
  e_0 = p_*^(2R+1) delta_0^2.                                      (1)
Choose S=[0,4R-1], S_R=[-R,5R-1], and S_2R=[-2R,6R-1].
At every i in S_R, copy the input basis label to a classical reference and
compare it to the output label at the SAME site. Let e_ia be the JOINT
probability of input label a and a mismatch. If
  e_ia <= e_0 for every tested i and every label a,                   (2)
then the multiplicative GNVW index of alpha is exactly 1.
It suffices to bound each site's total mismatch e_i=sum_a e_ia by e_0.

The tests cover 6R contiguous sites; their local input density floors are
needed on the 8R-site region S_2R. No translation invariance is assumed.
The bound is conservative and can be extremely small. It is a structural
finite-probability theorem, not an acquired finite-shot experiment, general
noisy-channel certificate, or practical sample-complexity claim.

Mechanism: exact classical preservation need not preserve quantum phases.
Instead of trying to reconstruct those phases, the proof pairs alpha with
its mathematical transpose conjugate. The uncontrolled diagonal phases then
cancel approximately. The paired QCA is close to identity in the full local
operator norm required by an established index-stability theorem. Neither
transpose nor the proof's group averages are additional physical actuators.

1. Statistical contract and the local projector bound
-----------------------------------------------------
Let P_ia=|a><a|_i tensor I and alpha(X)=V^dagger X V in a finite-volume
description. The infinite-chain formulation uses the corresponding local
automorphism directly. Copying the label into C_i gives blocks P_ia tau P_ia
and leaves the entire B marginal unchanged, because tau is product diagonal.
The joint mismatch contribution is
  e_ia = Tr[tau_Lambda P_ia (I-Q_ia) P_ia],
  Q_ia=alpha(P_ia),  Lambda=[i-R,i+R].                              (3)
All traces in (3) are finite ordinary traces on Lambda. The operator inside
the trace is positive and Lambda contains the support of Q_ia. Put m=2R+1.
Since tau_Lambda>=p_*^m I,
  Tr[P_ia(I-Q_ia)P_ia] <= e_ia/p_*^m.                             (4)

Alpha preserves the canonical normalized trace: composing that trace with an
automorphism gives another normalized tracial state, and its restriction to
every finite matrix algebra is the unique normalized matrix trace. Thus
P_ia and Q_ia, regarded as projections on Lambda, have equal rank d^(m-1).
For two equal-rank finite projections P,Q, the principal-angle identity gives
  ||P-Q||^2 = ||P(I-Q)P|| <= Tr[P(I-Q)P].                          (5)
Consequently (2) implies
  ||alpha(P_ia)-P_ia|| <= sqrt(e_ia/p_*^m) <= delta_0.              (6)
The elementary Hilbert-Schmidt fallback is
  ||P-Q||_HS^2=2 Tr[P(I-Q)P] <= 2e_ia/p_*^m;
using only this weaker bound would replace e_0 by e_0/2.

Here e_ia is not a label-conditioned error. If the label probability is q_ia,
then the compressed density floor is q_ia p_*^(m-1), and a bound epsilon_ia
on the CONDITIONAL mismatch gives ||P-Q||^2<=epsilon_ia/p_*^(m-1).
No postselection is free: rare labels still need their actual occurrences.

2. A finite-group intertwiner bound
-----------------------------------
Let rho_1,rho_0 be unital *-representations of a finite-dimensional algebra,
and let a finite unitary group G linearly span that algebra. Suppose
  ||rho_1(g)-rho_0(g)||<=t<1 for every g in G.
Define the PROOF OPERATOR
  T=|G|^(-1) sum_g rho_1(g) rho_0(g)^dagger.
Then ||T-I||<=t, so T is invertible with ||T^(-1)||<=1/(1-t).
Left multiplication permutes the group average, giving
  rho_1(h)T=T rho_0(h) for every h in G,
and hence for every X in its linear span. Therefore
  ||rho_1(X)-rho_0(X)||
       <= [2t/(1-t)] ||rho_0(X)||.                               (7)
Indeed rho_1(X)=T rho_0(X)T^(-1), and the difference is
[T,rho_0(X)]T^(-1); replace T by T-I in the commutator.
No group-size/dimension factor occurs in (7).

Two uses below need only these finite groups:
 (a) products of diagonal Z_d powers on K sites, spanning their diagonal
     algebra; if each basis projector moves by <=delta, every group element
     moves by <=K d delta by expansion and telescoping;
 (b) the tensor product of finite Weyl groups generated by shift X_d, clock
     Z_d and scalar dth-root phases on L sites, spanning the full matrix
     algebra. Each onsite Weyl element has d nonzero matrix entries. If each
     onsite matrix unit moves by <=zeta, every group element moves by
     <=L d zeta. Central phases cause no problem: this is an actual finite
     group representation, and the average may contain repeated scalars.

3. From diagonal tests to a transpose-paired full operator bound
---------------------------------------------------------------
Temporarily suppose ||alpha(P_ja)-P_ja||<=delta for every j in S_R and a.
Fix i in S and one onsite matrix unit E_ab=|a><b|_i. Let K=2R denote the
number of neighboring sites, and X=alpha(E_ab), supported on [i-R,i+R].
The exact relations alpha(P_ia)X=X=X alpha(P_ib) imply
  ||X-P_ia X P_ib||<=2delta.
Write P_ia X P_ib=E_ab tensor B, with ||B||<=1 on the K neighbors.
For a neighbor j!=i, [X,alpha(P_jc)]=0, so
  ||[B,P_jc]||<=2delta.
Pinch B in every neighbor basis. Each site's pinching changes its argument
by at most sum_c ||P_jc B(I-P_jc)||<=2d delta. Subsequent pinchings are
contractions and commute. The resulting diagonal D has ||D||<=1 and
  ||X-E_ab D||<=u,  u=2(1+K d)delta.                             (8)
Since X^dagger X=alpha(P_ib), (8) also gives
  ||D^dagger D-I||<=delta+2u.                                   (9)

Let Theta be matrix transposition in the same product basis and put
  beta=Theta alpha Theta,  gamma=alpha beta.
Theta is a mathematical anti-automorphism, not a proposed physical channel.
Because E_ab is real and alpha(E_ba)=alpha(E_ab)^dagger,
  beta(E_ab)=overline[alpha(E_ab)].
Thus beta(E_ab) is within u of E_ab D^dagger. For diagonal D on the K
neighbors, use (7) and group (a). If Kd delta<=1/2, then
  ||alpha(D^dagger)-D^dagger||
       <=2 Kd delta/(1-Kd delta)<=4 Kd delta.                   (10)
Apply alpha to the approximation for beta(E_ab), replace alpha(D^dagger)
using (10), and replace alpha(E_ab) using (8). As D is diagonal,
DD^dagger=D^dagger D, so (9) yields
  ||gamma(E_ab)-E_ab||
       <=2u+4Kd delta+(delta+2u)
       =(9+12Kd)delta=C delta.                                (11)
Gamma has strict radius at most 2R. GNVW Lemma 11(2) gives index(beta)=
index(alpha); multiplicativity gives index(gamma)=index(alpha)^2.

4. A finite cut block suffices, including for nonuniform QCAs
-------------------------------------------------------------
Take the L=4R fine sites in S. By (11), every onsite matrix unit changes under
gamma by at most C delta. Group (b) therefore gives t=L d C delta on every
finite Weyl unitary. Applying (7) to the entire S algebra gives
  ||(gamma-id)|_(A_S)|| <=2t/(1-t).                              (12)
At delta<=delta_0 in (1), t<=1/385 and (12) is <=1/192. Also
Kd delta_0=1/(770 C)<1/2, so condition (10) is automatic.

Block the original chain into cells of 2R sites: gamma is a nearest-neighbor
QCA, and S is one adjacent pair of coarse cells. Ranard--Walter--Witteveen,
Proposition 4.10, says two such QCAs have equal index if the restricted map
norm on one adjacent two-cell block differs by at most 1/192. Taking the
second QCA to be identity in (12) gives index(gamma)=1. Hence the positive
multiplicative index of alpha obeys index(alpha)^2=1 and is exactly 1. QED.

This uses only tag/projector bounds on S_R: each estimate (11) centered at
i in S requires tags in [i-R,i+R]. Each such tag's density-floor conversion
(3)--(6) requires preparation control on [j-R,j+R], giving S_2R in total.
No global translation symmetry, all-site experimental coverage, or global
minimum eigenvalue of a many-body density matrix has been assumed.

5. Translation symmetry and a decisive sparse-coverage counterexample
---------------------------------------------------------------------
If alpha and the label basis are exactly one-site translation covariant,
the projector norms in (6) are the same at every site. Bounds on all labels
at one site then supply all projector bounds needed above. Translation
covariance is a trusted extra premise, not a conclusion of the tag test.

Without it, isolated or sparse tests do not suffice. Let S_right be a full
right shift and T=SWAP(c-1,c). The QCA V=S_right T fixes input/output site c
and routes c-1 directly to c+1; it has radius and inverse radius at most 2
and retains nontrivial index d. Every local label test at c passes exactly.
More strongly, take T=product_k SWAP(2k-1,2k). Then S_right T fixes EVERY even
site while shifting the odd sublattice by two positions. All even-site tags
pass, but index remains d. Homogeneous product tau returns as a state too.
This explains why a contiguous finite cut band, rather than tested-site
density or one chosen unverified cell, is part of the nonuniform theorem.

6. Consequence and limits
-------------------------
Contrapositive: a radius-R QCA with nontrivial index must have some joint
classical label mismatch >e_0 in every such tested cut band. Locality and the
known population floor prevent an arbitrarily tiny GLOBAL sector from hiding
all of that flow from these specified local tests. The threshold explicitly
degrades as p_* decreases or R increases. It says nothing gap-independent in
those parameters and does not make auxiliary marginal return robustly
equivalent to channel return. A diagonal controlled-phase circuit can pass
every tag perfectly while differing strongly from identity as a channel.

For application to the data/auxiliary conveyor, the exact data-unitary and
full-rank factorization premise must have been established separately. This
theorem does not infer it from classical tags. It also does not cover noisy
nonunitary maps, uncertain QCA radius, soft Lieb--Robinson tails, finite-ring
index claims without an appropriate limit, correlated/misaligned input
preparations, or unpriced finite-shot confidence/calibration. Classical
copy/readout, access to the selected sites, and blank/conditional-reset
resources are the same physical operations described in STATE_RETURN; this
proof does not acquire an entire hardware assay or implement Theta/gamma.
