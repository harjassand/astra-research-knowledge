RECIPROCAL LC GAIN FROM THE SAME STATIONARY STOCK
================================================

Governing scratch; DRAFT for full internal review. No frozen source is changed.
Claim: a finite, always-connected, lossless reciprocal LC MODEL has a finite-time
coherent-gain criterion with its full coupling/image energy retained. This is not
a completed electrode apparatus, finite-c field model, discharge or engine cycle.

1. Literal physical Hamiltonian and preparation contract
-------------------------------------------------------

Retain the actual common-mass particle coordinates and stationary phase-averaged
cube of HARMONIC_FIELD_SCALAR_PULSE.txt, frozen SHA256
e6a56d5e97f536ed86f6b2bdc8e4c48c84ce899adc0df2658442a3a55b41bbe8:

 Hb=sum_j nu_j I_j=sum_j[P_j^2/(2m)+m nu_j^2 X_j^2/2],
 nu_1>nu_2>0, X_j=q_j/sqrt(m nu_j), I_j=(q_j^2+p_j^2)/2.

The independent action density is h_*(I)=h(I/I_*)/I_*, with moments
mu_1=I_*/3, mu_2=7 I_*^2/45, mu_3=3 I_*^3/35 and uniform independent angles.
It is stationary for Hb, has covariance I_* I_4/3 and is not a thermal stock.

Let the dimensionless differential electrode weighting be

 w(X)=kappa (X_1 X_2^3-X_1^3 X_2),  kappa!=0,
 beta_w=kappa/[m^2 sqrt(nu_1 nu_2^3)], r=nu_2/nu_1,
 w=beta_w(q_1 q_2^3-r q_1^3 q_2).

Here kappa has units length^-4, beta_w has units action^-2. This w is harmonic
in physical X in a fixed aperture containing the free stock. A signed w needs
a differential multi-electrode mode; it is not the nonnegative 0/1 weighting
of one single-ended electrode. Exact circular P4 boundary data are an idealized
distributed mode. Finite equipotential segments approximate them and require
their own mode/capacitance/error calculation. No geometry has been acquired.

Grounded conductors also exert a static image potential U_im(X), generally not
equal to the circuit square term below. The actual hypothesis needed here is

 V_conf(X)+U_im(X)=sum_j m nu_j^2 X_j^2/2

throughout the trajectory aperture, with any constant fixed once. Thus an
independently supplied, calibrated compensating confinement is a premise.
Merely renaming an arbitrary U_im as part of Hb does not establish this equality.
The usual regularized grounded-boundary term is e^2 G_D^reg(X,X)/2; do not
double-count it if it is already in the chosen electrostatic decomposition.

For charge -e, choose the flux orientation with {Phi,Q}=1 and node voltage
V=Phi_dot. The reciprocal one-mode Lagrangian has circuit/interaction terms
C Phi_dot^2/2-Phi^2/(2L)+e w Phi_dot. Hence Q=C V+e w and

 H=Hb+Phi^2/(2L)+(Q-e w)^2/(2C).                         (1)

V=(Q-e w)/C, Q_dot=-Phi/L and the particle force from the mode is +e V grad w.
This is a quasistatic capacitance-mode Hamiltonian, not a prescribed voltage.
All static confinement and image energy is in the explicitly specified Hb.

Set omega=(LC)^(-1/2)=nu_1-nu_2>0 and use canonical load coordinates

 q=Q/sqrt(C omega), p=-Phi/sqrt(L omega), g=-e sqrt(omega/C).

Then (1) is exactly

 H=Hb+H_L+H_int,
 H_L=omega(q^2+p^2)/2,
 H_int=g q w+g^2 w^2/(2omega),
 H_L+H_int=omega[(q+g w/omega)^2+p^2]/2.                 (2)

The g^2 term is retained. A formal fixed-charge weak-coupling knob is large
finite total C with L adjusted to keep omega fixed. This does NOT make the
grounded image force disappear. A weak-charge limit is a different contract.

At t=0 supply an independent product of the stated particle stock and a compact
load distribution centered at (q,p)=(0,P), P>0, with a nonzero-width rotationally
invariant centered noise law. Define J_m=P^2/2 and J_v=E|delta(q,p)|^2/2>0.
In particular E(q_initial p_initial)=0 and E q_initial^2=J_v.
This is a declared prepared full initial state, not an acquired free preparation.
The load seed/noise costs omega(J_m+J_v). Because E w=0, its additional initial
coupling energy is exactly g^2 c0, where c0=S/(2omega), S=E w^2.
In general its microscopic voltage is not zero: Q=0 does not mean Q-e w=0.
A dressed zero-voltage preparation would instead correlate Q with the particle.
No coupling switch, reset or external current occurs during the operation below.

2. Exact finite-time perturbation data
-------------------------------------

Write w=sum_n w_n(I) exp(i n.theta), and group opposite pairs by positive
Omega=|nu.n|. Orient n so nu.n=Omega and define

 C_Omega=sum_(same Omega) integral |w_n|^2 n.grad[h_*h_*] dI,
 A_Omega=sum_(same Omega) E|w_n|^2 >=0.

The finite nonzero frequencies are the distinct positive values in
{nu_1+-nu_2, nu_1+-3nu_2, 3nu_1+-nu_2}; zero modes are kept in S but omitted
from C. Distinct angular vectors do not interfere when frequencies coincide.
The frozen six-branch calculation, with beta replaced by beta_w, gives

 C_res=C_omega=beta_w^2 I_*^3 times
 {11(1-r^2)/280, nu_1!=2nu_2;
  19/3360,      nu_1==2nu_2} >0.                         (3)

At ratio2, the extra (-1,3) resonant coefficient -beta_w^2 I_*^3/42 is included.
At ratio3 a zero branch contributes no response. All other degeneracies are
grouped in C_Omega and A_Omega.

Let q_a(t)=a cos(omega t)+b sin(omega t) be the free load trajectory, with
a=q_initial, b=p_initial. The stationary particle response is

 chi(t)=E{w_t,w_0}=2 sum_(Omega>0) C_Omega sin(Omega t),
 E_particle w^(1)(t)=integral_0^t q_a(s) chi(t-s) ds,       (4)

where w=w_0+g w^(1)+... . Equation (4) follows by the observable Poisson-bracket
variation and action integration by parts; only the first integrable derivative
of h_* is needed. Load evolution is exactly

 b_L=q+i p, b_L_dot=-i omega b_L-i g w,
 q^(1)(t)=-integral_0^t sin[omega(t-s)]w_0(s)ds.            (5)

The image term changes particle motion first at order g^2 and therefore changes
the load first at order g^3. It does not disappear from the energy ledger.

Take the finite observation times T=2pi N/omega, N a positive integer. The
resonant part for the mean seed is exactly

 E w^(1)_res(t)=C_res P[sin(omega t)/omega-t cos(omega t)],
 E p^(2)_res(T)=C_res P T^2/4.                            (6)

Set a_c=omega J_m C_res/2>0. The bare coherent load energy and its inductive
component are E_coh=omega[(E q)^2+(E p)^2]/2 and E_ind,coh=omega(E p)^2/2.
Both have the same leading gain at these times:

 Delta E_coh=g^2[a_c T^2+d_c(T)]+O_T(|g|^3),
 Delta E_ind,coh=g^2[a_c T^2+d_c(T)]+O_T(|g|^3).           (7)

In particular the initial seed omega J_m is subtracted, not counted as gain.
For Omega!=omega define

 I_Omega(T)=1/2[(1-cos((Omega+omega)T))/(Omega+omega)
                +(1-cos((Omega-omega)T))/(Omega-omega)].

The nonresonant term is exactly
d_c(T)=2 omega^2 P^2 sum C_Omega I_Omega(T)/(Omega^2-omega^2), and

 |d_c(T)|<=K_c=2 omega^2 P^2 sum_(Omega!=omega)
 |C_Omega|/|Omega^2-omega^2| [1/(Omega+omega)+1/|Omega-omega|].  (8)

Every sum below is over positive nonresonant Omega. These constants are finite
for the fixed supplied frequencies; none is asserted uniform near degeneracy.

Separate E_L=E_coh+E_fluc. Define

 Jcal_T(Omega)=1/2[(1-cos((omega-Omega)T))/(omega-Omega)^2
                  -(1-cos((omega+Omega)T))/(omega+Omega)^2],
 F_T(x)=integral_0^T exp(i x s)ds.

Amplification of the supplied seed NOISE adds 2omega J_v C_Omega Jcal_T(Omega)
to the g^2 fluctuation-energy coefficient. Spontaneous pickup adds
omega A_Omega[|F_T(omega-Omega)|^2+|F_T(omega+Omega)|^2]/2.
At resonance/strobe Jcal=T^2/4, F_T(0)=T and F_T(2omega)=0. Thus, with
a_f=omega[J_v C_res+A_res]/2>0,

 Delta E_fluc=g^2[a_f T^2+d_v(T)+d_sp(T)]+O_T(|g|^3),      (9)
 |d_v(T)|<=K_v=2omega J_v sum |C_Omega|
                 [(omega-Omega)^(-2)+(omega+Omega)^(-2)],
 0<=d_sp(T)<=2omega sum A_Omega
                 [(omega-Omega)^(-2)+(omega+Omega)^(-2)].

Zero-frequency w modes have F_T(omega)=0 at the selected times. Equation (9)
does not label pickup noise or amplified seed noise as coherent work.

3. Endpoint coupling is bounded; the full ledger closes
-------------------------------------------------------

The initial image expectation is g^2 c0. Its order-g^2 change is zero because
the free particle stock is stationary. At the chosen T the resonant part of
E[q_a(T) w^(1)(T)] is -C_res T E(a b)=0. The resonant part of
E[q^(1)(T) w_0(T)] is also zero. For other positive frequencies,

 E[q_a(T) w^(1)(T)]
   =sum 2J_v C_Omega Omega[1-cos(Omega T)]/(Omega^2-omega^2),
 E[q^(1)(T) w_0(T)]
   =-sum A_Omega[(1-cos((omega+Omega)T))/(omega+Omega)
                 +(1-cos((omega-Omega)T))/(omega-Omega)].

Zero modes also vanish here at a strobe. Consequently

 Delta E_int=g^2 b_int(T)+O_T(|g|^3),
 |b_int(T)|<=K_i=sum[4J_v |C_Omega| Omega/|Omega^2-omega^2|
                  +2A_Omega(1/(omega+Omega)+1/|omega-Omega|)]. (10)

The Hamiltonian is time independent, so at every coupling and time, exactly,

 Delta E_b+Delta E_L+Delta E_int=0.                         (11)

ONE FINITE CRITERION: choose a finite integer N sufficiently large that

 a_c T^2>K_c+2c0+K_i,    a_f T^2>K_v.                     (12)

This is possible because C_res>0. Choose this T FIRST, then a sufficiently
small nonzero g so all strict inequalities survive the fixed-T remainders.
It follows that

 Delta E_coh>|E_int(0)|+|E_int(T)|,   Delta E_fluc>0,
 Delta E_b<0,   Delta E_ind,coh>0.                         (13)

The gain cannot be attributed just to consuming the initial circuit-image
energy or the subtracted seed: even granting both endpoint coupling energies
as possible sources is insufficient for the coherent increment. The particles
lose energy by (11). This is finite-store transfer, not sustained instability,
large work, finite power, repeated operation or profitable preparation/reset.

For rigor at fixed T, extend w smoothly outside the aperture as in the frozen
scalar proof. The completed-square Hamiltonian, compact initial particle/load
supports and positive Hb keep trajectories in a common bounded region for g
near0. Smooth ODE dependence gives uniform C^3 parameter derivatives there and
the displayed O_T(|g|^3) energy/moment remainders. The g=0 trajectories have a
strict aperture margin; continuity makes the extension inactive for small g.
Constants can grow badly with T, seed energy and near-resonant detunings.
No joint T~1/g asymptotic or unproved long-time error estimate is used.

4. Matched baseline, primary ancestry and acquisition boundary
-------------------------------------------------------------

For the global-polynomial Gaussian response control h_G(I)=exp(-I/mu)/mu,
mu=I_*/3 (same covariance), C_n^G=-(n_1+n_2)A_n^G/mu. Thus the isolated
(1,-1) stimulated coefficient is zero, while the extra (-1,3) coefficient at
ratio2 is -beta_w^2 I_*^3/36<0. Empty-load pickup noise can still raise its
mean energy. This is a same-channel response comparison, not optimal-control
advantage. The Gaussian has unbounded support and is NOT a second stock already
contained in the finite aperture; no uncharged Gaussian tail limit is assumed.
