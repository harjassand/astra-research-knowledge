2. SUPPLIED PHYSICAL MODEL AND ALLOWED OBSERVATION
=================================================
Let theta>0 and m be a fixed finite number of bosonic modes. In units hbar=1,
  H_theta(t)=a^dagger h(t)a+(theta/2) N^T J(t)N,
  L_-,i(t)=sqrt(theta d_i(t))a_i,
  L_+,i(t)=sqrt(theta b_i(t))a_i^dagger.
Here h is Hermitian, J real symmetric, and d_i,b_i nonnegative. On each exposed
record prefix the coefficients are known piecewise bounded functions of time.
They may be selected by the actual implemented controller. Uniform envelopes
over its permitted prefixes are part of the theorem's data. The physical initial
state is supplied jointly with the accessible controller; arbitrary initial
correlations are not replaced by a product state.

Typical physical occupation is order theta^-1. The theta in each bath rate is
a physical scaling, not a conclusion about an unscaled bath. It leaves order-one
counts and order-one nonlinear frequencies on a fixed macroscopic interval;
the leading change in the macroscopic field due to each bath vanishes. This
does not cover finite unscaled bath damping without another derivation.

The directly implemented observation has a finite number M of bins and a finite
alphabet per bin: e.g. no event/at least one event, or no event/first registered
port. Registration efficiencies are known scalars in [0,1]. Within a bin,
controls may depend on time, completed exposed bins and the retained controller,
but not on an unread latch or a hidden first-event time. At the boundary the
observed symbol is exposed and the controller updated. The hidden latch does
not stop the physical evolution; all subsequent events are integrated out.

The construction also permits supplied finite linear combinations of annihilation
or creation modes for readout, after charging their coefficient norms. Local
mode formulas are used for the sharp quantum constants in Section 5.

4. EXACT LEADING PREDICTIVE QUOTIENT
====================================

  Q_-,i,0=sqrt(d_i)z_i,  Q_+,i,0=sqrt(b_i)bar z_i.
For a positive core with kernel K(z,z'), define p(z)=K(z,z). This is an ordinary
positive probability density (or subprobability on an unnormalized branch).

For a bounded multiplier M_f and multiplication jump M_q,
  J_q^*(M_f)=M_(|q|^2 f),
  D[M_q]^*(M_f)=0.
Koopman evolution also preserves the multiplier algebra. Induct backwards
through any allowed finite future instrument/feedback tree: every record effect
is a multiplier. Therefore its probability depends on the current core only
through p and the implemented controller. This is a predictive quotient for the
specified leading instruments, not a reconstruction of an arbitrary physical
state from p.

For a bin starting at y and controlled flow Phi_t, the registered intensity is
  lambda_r(t,Phi_t y)=eta_r(t)|q_r(t,Phi_t y)|^2.
For a pooled absorbing OR latch, the exact two likelihoods are
  ell_0(y)=exp[-integral_bin sum_r lambda_r(t,Phi_t y)dt],
  ell_1(y)=1-ell_0(y).
The current density is reweighted by the observed ell_b, normalized, and pushed
forward by the bin's flow. This integrates every later event in the same bin.
For a first-port latch with mark r,

                   exp[-integral_(bin start)^t sum_s lambda_s(u,Phi_u y)du]dt,
and ell_none=exp[-integral_bin sum_s lambda_s]. These nonnegative rows sum to one.

Across bins the intensities are controlled predictable intensities. Feedback
does not permit describing the whole record as independent observations of an
exogenous intensity path. The flow/control used after a prefix is the same
actual implemented policy in every compared model.

  p_+/-(q)=exp(-|q_1|^2-|q_2|^2)|q_1^2 +/- q_2^2|^2/(4 pi^2).
The two diagonals differ even though local number populations and covariance

The mixed quantum kernel is still necessary to justify the approximation, or

7. FINITE POSITIVE CURRENT GRID: WHOLE-RECORD COUPLING THEOREM
============================================================
Assume the smooth leading diagonal is supported on the radius-R ball. Partition
that ball into finitely many cells of diameter at most delta. Use one node in
each cell; equivalently round a Euclidean grid and project to the ball, since
metric projection does not increase distance to a point in the ball. Let the
exact cell masses define the initial discrete law. If their computed positive
normalized masses differ by TV epsilon_mass, charge that separately. Supplied
joint initial controller correlations require conditional cell masses, not a
product of marginal distributions. Conditioning on a known accessible initial
controller value is also valid. Averaging conditional bounds uses its supplied
law. No free discretization of an arbitrary controller distribution is implied.

Let z_t be a continuum leading signal and zhat_t its initial-node counterpart,
both driven by the SAME implemented controller on a common exposed record.
Before their first discrepant exposed event, their controls coincide. Gronwall
then gives
  |z_t-zhat_t|<=delta exp[integral_0^t L(s)ds].
For lambda_r(t,z)=gamma_r(t)|u_r(t)^dagger z|^2 and both paths in the radius-R
ball,
  |lambda_r(t,z)-lambda_r(t,zhat)|
       <=2 gamma_r(t) R ||u_r(t)||^2 |z-zhat|.
Use common Poisson clocks with shared rate min(lambda_r,lambdahat_r) and private
excess clocks. Before the first discrepancy the total hazard of a discrepant
registered marked event is at most the sum of these differences. Therefore
  TV(P_continuum_record,P_exact_node_record)
    <= epsilon_mass
       +2R delta integral_0^T g(t) exp[integral_0^t L(s)ds]dt,              (7.1)
where g(t) uniformly bounds sum_r gamma_r(t)||u_r(t)||^2. In particular the
second term is at most 2R delta exp(integral_0^T L) integral_0^T g.
Common initial randomness preserves all supplied correlations. Couple also the
randomized controller with the same internal coins if one is used.

This argument first controls a mathematical continuously timed marked-count
record. Coarsening it to the PERMITTED finite bin alphabet contracts TV, so it
covers OR and first-port latches. It is unnecessary to simulate or retain the
hidden times. The proof needs no continuity of the feedback policy in posterior
weights: it uses identical implemented policy on a common exposed prefix.

The online sufficient state is the current moving nodes, their positive weights,
and the actual controller (including its numerical state). In each bin transport
the nodes, compute the positive likelihood row, multiply the weights by its
observed component, normalize and update the controller. Node count is fixed;
raw exposed histories need not be stored beyond what that controller requires.
Computing the probability of every possible history still can be exponential
in M; online closure does not remove that distinction.

Node count is at most a dimension-dependent constant times (1+R/delta)^(2m).
Thus for a fixed m,T,R,rate/control envelope and target grid error it is finite
and independent of physical occupation. It can be exponential in m and in
instability/time through exp(integral L); no general economical many-body bound
has been derived. A grid is not being approximated in latent-density TV.

8. FLOW, LIKELIHOOD AND ARITHMETIC ALLOWANCES
============================================
Let a validated numerical flow have accumulated GLOBAL path discrepancy e(t)
relative to the exact node flow, uniformly over permitted prefixes. The initial

in radius R, numerical trajectory error adds

Per-bin local errors must first be propagated by the Lipschitz envelope; they
cannot be inserted into (8.1) as if global. If a numerical path has bound R_num

valid algorithm but its accumulated discrepancy must be certified.

For the finite alphabet of bin k, suppose its computed positive stochastic
likelihood row has a uniform row-TV error at most eta_k, over all nodes and
permitted prefixes. Then exact-kernel telescoping gives an additional
  epsilon_rows<=sum_(k=1)^M eta_k.                        (8.2)
For an OR bin it suffices to bound the error in its no-event probability. For a
first-port latch use the positive integral rows of Section 4; validated positive
quadrature and a paid stochastic normalization/completion produce (8.2).

An elementary implementation avoids uncontrolled rare-posterior roundoff.
Approximate initial node masses by nonnegative integers summing to 2^B0. At
each bin approximate each finite likelihood row by nonnegative integers summing
to 2^B. Store UNNORMALIZED integer node weights and multiply by the appropriate
likelihood numerator. Conditional prediction and normalization are then exact
rational ratios; no small-probability floating division is assumed accurate.
The integers require O(B0+M B+log(number of nodes)) bits. Finite-symbol row
rounding has a paid error of order alphabet_size times 2^-B, in addition to its
validated likelihood integral error. Strictly positive row smoothing may be
used with a paid floor allowance. Alternatively specify a bounded default
controller and normalized weight reset for any zero-denominator prefix.
Such prefixes may have positive continuum probability, so the policy must be
defined there even if the approximate model assigns them zero probability.

This arithmetic construction is for FINITE exposed alphabets. Rounding an
otherwise continuously reported event time to atoms does not converge in TV
to the continuous-time law on event branches. Time coarsening must be part of
the declared physical observation if such a time grid is used.

11. PRIMARY COMPARISON AND SCIENTIFIC BOUNDARY
=============================================
The waveoperator, purification gauge, Bopp operators, and Koopman classical
limit are established machinery: McCaul--Zhdanov--Bondar, arXiv2302.13208v4,
especially Eqs43--45. The present derivation does not rename that machinery as
a new general mathematical language.

Complete repeated-measurement record convergence is also established:
Borns-Weil--Oltman, arXiv2210.03224v3, Theorem1, obtains quantitative joint-record
TV convergence for quantized-torus evolution and smooth Kraus-symbol models.
Benoist--Fraas--Frohlich, arXiv2202.09558, Theorem3.1 and Remark3.2, gives
observation-sequence convergence and an open-system Markov-kernel interface.
The leading current law here is an instance of classical nonlinear filtering;
its Bayes update or controlled intensity interpretation is not a novelty claim.

Stellar/Gaussian-polynomial finite cores and strong-simulation algorithms are
actual incumbents, not merely formal alternatives: Chabaud--Mehraban,
arXiv2111.00117; Chabaud et al., arXiv2010.14363, Theorem2; Motamedi et al.,
arXiv2504.10455v3. The coherent low-degree witness is accessible to those methods.
Positive mixtures also remain valid; their number of components and quadrature
cost are independent resources, not erased by a bound on polynomial degree.

The scoped construction justified here is the explicit positive quantum lift
for simultaneous hopping/density interactions and weak gain/loss, its complete
adapted-record residual with a demonstrated necessary preparation premise, and
the finite positive current record evaluator with precision allowances and a
nonvanishing nonlinear prediction. The sources do not by themselves provide
this exact interface; a search for a still closer result remains incomplete.
Historical priority and comparative practical advantage remain UNKNOWN.

The consequential open obstruction is now specific: can the required control
of phase-space information and initial regularity be supplied or propagated
economically for many interacting modes, longer times, or an unscaled bath?
The present theorem isolates occupation as a removable cost in its stated
regime, but does not remove those other resources. Further refinement of one
integrable error curve is unlikely to answer that question.
