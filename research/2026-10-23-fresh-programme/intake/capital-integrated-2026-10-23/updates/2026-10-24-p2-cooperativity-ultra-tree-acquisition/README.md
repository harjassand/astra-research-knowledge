## Pro 2 — common-contact equilibrium cooperativity

The [native report](source/p2/P2_cooperativity_native_0718_20261024.md) compares a keyed, sliding three-collar guide with two active guest-binding collars and one passive spectator. Under the source's rigid aligned-guide model, the accessible fraction is `f(D) = (1-D/F)_+^3`. The single-site factor is `q=f(delta)=0.337043`; the double-bound factors are `0.0601662` and `0.337043`, giving coupling ratios `0.529641` and `2.966980`. This is a model-level common-contact operation with opposite cooperativity signs.

Exact equality of the single-site coefficients holds only in the aligned-guide limit. The source says sufficiently small positive guide and docking clearances preserve the strict opposite signs while making single-site coefficients arbitrarily close; it does not establish exact equality at finite clearance. The finite-compartment scaling limit also matters: separate-gap antagonism vanishes for two fixed contacts as the rail grows at fixed slack per collar, while a compatible shared-gap cooperative contribution remains finite. Strong antagonism therefore requires finite compartments in this model.

The source retains two decisive failures. A strict-clearance, fully occupied six-rail `K3,3` cage defeats the claim that suppressing repeated pair bonds necessarily selects an extended network; it does not show that the cage dominates equilibrium. Integrating the earlier freely swiveling contact over mobile geometries diluted the tested effect: about `0.03%` of proposed geometries made both neighbors restrict the same spectator, with about `0.036%` joint-weight suppression. The registered socket addresses that particular contact failure.

Registration has a substantial source-estimated cost. For `0.25 nm` transverse clearance and `0.5 nm` capture radius, a restricted orientation region contributes about `1.3×10^-8 nm^3`; if that region alone supplied binding at `1 μM`, the square-well estimate requires roughly `34 k_BT` stabilization. This is not a completed integral over every binding orientation, a measured affinity, or a dissociation time. The rigid-body result is not a fabricated or molecularly validated material. Retention lifetime, release-strand access, exchange kinetics, and finite-compartment realization remain open.
