ACQUIRING A TREE WITHIN THE CLASS DOES NOT VALIDATE RARE-EVENT PREDICTION
Lead exact observation boundary, 2026-10-24. No novelty claim.

Take any null P from UNKNOWN_TREE_ACQUISITION with a fixed admissible
tree and edge parameters, and let p=P(all +1)=P(all -1). At fixed
rho_max<1 this p is exponentially small in n. For n large enough p<=1/8.
Let S put equal probability on the all-plus and all-minus configurations.
Define a spin-flip-symmetric rare common-shock model

       Q=(1-2p)P+2p S.                                  (1)

Both laws give every individual spin mean zero. Q remains strictly
positive on every configuration because P does. It is within TV 2p of
the exact tree law (indeed TV(P,Q)=2p(1-2p)), and each pair correlation
changes by at most 4p. Thus all pair statistics used for reconstruction
can be exponentially close while the rare-event conclusion changes.
Q is not promised to be an exact Markov tree: this is precisely the
model-class enlargement being tested, not a counterexample inside the
positive theorem's assumptions.

The enlargement is genuine for n>=3 and 8p<gamma, where gamma is the
null tree's cut margin. Every pair correlation changes by at most 4p,
so T remains the unique maximum absolute-correlation spanning tree of
Q. Any positive zero-field Markov-tree representation of Q would
therefore have to use T. But on a length-two T path with original edge
correlations a,b, putting s=2p gives

 c'_edge1 c'_edge2-c'_nonedge
       =-s(1-s)(1-a)(1-b) != 0.

This violates the exact tree path-product identity. Thus Q is outside
that class. At fixed quantitative bounds, 8p<gamma holds for all large
enough n; if the bounds shrink with n this condition must be retained.
The TV and observation lower bounds below do not require this condition.

For the catastrophe A=all +1,

       Q(A)=(1-2p)p+p=2p(1-p).                          (2)

At p<=1/8, Q(A)/P(A)>=7/4, so relative-error-1/4 success intervals are
disjoint. Any method correct with probability >=2/3 under both models
therefore distinguishes them with transcript TV at least 1/3.

With N iid full snapshots, TV(P^N,Q^N)<=N TV(P,Q)<=2p N. Consequently

       N>=1/(6p)                                       (3)

is necessary for that rare-event-relative guarantee. The same bound in
expected N under P holds for adaptive stopping: couple each new full
snapshot maximally; before the first mismatch the entire history and
stop decisions agree, and mismatch probability is at most 2p per call.
All spins are observed perfectly. No sensor noise, hidden observed label
or restriction to pairwise computation is needed for this lower bound.

Thus passive polynomial snapshots can acquire the correct graph WITHIN
a promised identifiable class, while they cannot certify the validity
of the resulting exponentially rare forecast against an exponentially
small common-shock contamination. A good average or pairwise fit, or even
a small TV distance to a tree, is insufficient for relative rare-event
validation. A rigorous structural law, a rarer-mechanism intervention,
or an appropriate multiplicative model-error certificate is extra input.
The cost/possibility of acquiring that input is not solved by the MST.

This is the familiar contamination/two-point-testing obstruction in an
explicit model-preserving-symmetry wrapper. It is not a new statistical
law, and does not defeat the conditional positive acquisition theorem.
