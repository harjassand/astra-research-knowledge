Disposition

The exact event-preserving positive-factorization construction survives.
Its coefficient-mass entropy bound is needlessly weak for many predicates.
Retaining both actual one-sided effect masses gives a sharper theorem.
For fixed-question matching masks, including the binary line game, the
source's named S_actual/L premises now imply

  omega_co(G^n) <= exp[-2^-75 epsilon^2 n], epsilon=1-omega_co(G)>0.

The original exact event and conditional live table are preserved. The
large number of rectangle labels remains an accuracy/representation cost,
not an entropy-rate cost. This corrects the earlier conclusion that the
line's coefficient mass forces a cubic rate. That conclusion applies only
to the deliberately crude coefficient-mass bound.

This is a newly derived INTERNAL CONDITIONAL refinement. The full supplied
correlation protocol S_actual and accepted Lin finite-TABLE density are
still premises, not newly reconstructed here. No general quadratic theorem,
efficient finite-matrix or physical compiler, priority, or external validation
is claimed. The class below includes familiar matching/unique-type games;
comparison with their strongest prior theorems remains separate work.

Let mu be any joint within-round law; rounds are independent. For an
n-round strategy and tested C, let p=P(E)>0,t=|C|,m=n-t>0 and alpha be
the average conditional live loss. In the finite-tracial representation,
chi(F,H)=tr(sigma F sigma H), sigma>=0 bounded, tr(sigma^2)=1.

Fix an exact tested mask factorization
  theta_c(alpha,gamma)=sum_k lambda_{c,k} f_{c,k}(alpha)g_{c,k}(gamma),
  lambda>=0, 0<=f,g<=1,
depending only on the tested questions c. Merge effects as in the previous
note: F_j^k=sum_alpha f_k(alpha)F_j^alpha and H_j^k analogously. New live
fine effects sum to these coarse effects. They are effects, but their k
family need not be a POVM. The new local/opposite fine POVMs are the bounded
support/polar transports already given there. The original event mass and
full conditional live question/answer table are preserved exactly.

Write Lambda_C=E_c sum_k lambda_{c,k}. It is finite. It will still appear
in finite compression error. It need not appear in the entropy budget.

2. Weighted log-sum gives the actual one-sided masses

At exposure stage j define
  U_j=E_flags sum_k lambda_k chi(F_j^k log(F_j^k),H_j^k),
  Z_j=E_flags sum_k lambda_k chi(F_j^k,H_j^k log(H_j^k)).
For a term put h=chi(F,H), g_B=chi(I,H). Scalar Jensen with respect to
the positive functional chi(.,H) yields
  -chi(F log F,H)<=h log(g_B/h),
with zero-mass terms interpreted by continuity. Do NOT discard g_B here.
If g_B=0 then h=0 because 0<=F<=I.

Define
  G_B,j=E_flags sum_k lambda_k chi(I,H_j^k),
  G_A,j=E_flags sum_k lambda_k chi(F_j^k,I).
Their corresponding h sum is the same original p. The weighted log-sum
inequality (or Jensen after dividing by total h mass p) gives
  -U_j<=p log(G_B,j/p),
  -Z_j<=p log(G_A,j/p).                                   (W1)
Also p<=G_A,j,G_B,j, so all logarithms are nonnegative. Since F,H are
effects, U_j,Z_j<=0. These bounds retain actual state-dependent masses;
G_A,G_B can be much smaller than Lambda_C.

Only two endpoints are needed. U is nondecreasing and Z is nonincreasing
along the original fixed exposure. The resolvent Jensen inequality and
unchanged telescope give
  e_A <= [U_m-U_0]/m <= p log(G_B,0/p)/m,
  e_B <= [Z_0-Z_m]/m <= p log(G_A,m/p)/m.                  (W2)
Here e_A is the mean squared error from averaging Alice's effect; e_B
averages Bob's. There is no assumption of entropy lower bounds at every
flag stage in this argument.

In fact these two masses are stage-independent in the present construction.
Conditional on c, H_j^k is a finite classical conditional expectation of
the original Bob coarse effect, with coefficient g_k depending only on c.
The law of total expectation and linearity of chi give
  E_flags|c chi(I,H_j^k)
    =E_full questions|c chi(I,H_original^k),
independent of j. The corresponding Alice identity is identical. Thus
write G_A,G_B without j. This fact would require rechecking if a different
scheme let factorization coefficients depend on the live exposure itself.

3. Referee law is controlled independently of rectangle count

For the binary event E the original conditioned full question density is
P(E|full questions)/p <=1/p relative to the unchanged mu^n. Consequently
  D(P_full(.|E)||mu^n)<=log(1/p).
Discard tested coordinates, use product-entropy superadditivity and Pinsker:
  average_i dTV(Q_{X_iY_i},mu)<=sqrt(log(1/p)/(2m)).         (W3)
This is the arbitrary JOINT mu; no free/product-within-round assumption.

Optionally, let s_C be the original probability that the tested question
mask has any accepting answer pair. Tilt the tested-question prior by that
0/1 feasibility indicator. The same density proof gives log(s_C/p) in
place of log(1/p). In general p<=s_C<=1. For a bounded fractional filter,
one can instead use the mean over tested questions of its maximum entry.
No tilt is needed for the matching result below.

Set
  B_C=max{1,G_A,G_B}, tau_w=log(B_C/p)/m.                  (W4)
Then BOTH resolvent squared errors and the live-marginal drift have the
source bounds with tau_w replacing its old answer-count budget. A sharper
optional choice replaces 1 by s_C in B_C, using the previous paragraph.

Finite feature compression still obeys
  e_A,e_B <= their exact bound +32 Lambda_C epsilon_comp.
At fixed p and eta>tau_w choose
  epsilon_comp <=p(eta-tau_w)/(32 Lambda_C).
Norms, p, the conditional live table, and the local fine POVMs remain exact.
The coefficient mass can thus make the required resource accuracy severe,
but it does not change the mathematical rate after the source's ordered
infimum over finite protocols. No uniform resource bound follows.

The source's SAME local-history normalization and SAME all-input S_actual
protocol therefore yield, for every positive accuracy, a legal single-game
strategy of loss
  <=alpha+K[sqrt(alpha)tau_w^(1/4)+sqrt(tau_w)]+accuracy,
  K=65536.                                               (W5)
The accepted Lin TABLE closure extends this fixed-p scalar statement from
finite-tracial tables to all commuting tables. It does not approximate
the ambient algebra by matrices. If a bound on B_C is used for arbitrary
commuting strategies, it suffices that it hold uniformly on the chosen
finite-tracial approximating tables; the matching bound does.

4. Matching and disjoint-block predicates remove all answer entropy cost

Suppose that for each supported (x,y), the accepted answer pairs form a
PARTIAL MATCHING: each Alice answer has at most one accepted Bob answer
and each Bob answer has at most one accepted Alice answer. Each tested
mask is again a partial matching on the answer strings. Use a singleton
rectangle for every accepted tested pair. No accepted alpha or gamma
occurs twice. At every flag, therefore,
  sum_k F_j^k <=sum_alpha F_j^alpha=I,
  sum_k H_j^k <=sum_gamma H_j^gamma=I.
It follows directly that G_A,G_B<=chi(I,I)=1. In particular
  tau_w=log(1/p)/m.                                       (W6)
Equality/matching labels can remain numerous; their total one-sided
effect mass is one, which is the relevant entropy normalization.

The same reasoning applies more broadly when every fixed-question mask
is a union of vertex-disjoint complete rectangles. Use one rectangle per
block: each row and column belongs to at most one block. The sum of new
coarse effects on either side is again <=I. The block selection may depend
on BOTH tested question strings because those are sampled as shared
exposure before the fresh live questions. No response-equivalence under
all possible opposite live questions is required.

5. Complete quadratic consequence with the source constant

Assume the uniform matching/disjoint-block property above and all named
source premises. Let epsilon=1-omega_co(G)>0 and fix any n-round table.
If its all-wins probability theta is positive write L=log(1/theta).

Set gamma=2^-75. Suppose theta>exp(-gamma epsilon^2 n).
The original greedy lemma, testing while average conditional loss exceeds
epsilon/2, supplies a proper C with
  t<=2L/epsilon, p>=theta, alpha<=epsilon/2.
Indeed 2L/epsilon<2gamma epsilon n<n/2, so stopping must be proper.
Thus m>=n/2 and the refined budget is
  tau_w=log(1/p)/m <=2L/n
          <2gamma epsilon^2 <=2^-72 epsilon^2.
Insert this in (W5) and take the accuracy small. With K=2^16,
  legal loss/epsilon
    <=1/2+K(2^-72)^(1/4)/sqrt2+K(2^-72)^(1/2)
     =1/2+1/(4sqrt2)+1/(16K)<1.
This contradicts the base value. Hence
  omega_co(G^n)<=exp[-2^-75 epsilon^2 n].                  (W7)
Theta=0 is trivial, and epsilon=0 has the trivial bound one. The proof
works at every integer n; no asymptotic floor, attained optimum, fixed
matrix dimension, or independent answering assumption is used.

For the binary L-vertex line game, every supported mask is equality I_2
or an endpoint singleton. Hence it meets the hypothesis. Its one-round
commuting gap is 1/(2L-1), by the no-signaling telescoping proof in the
previous note. Equation (W7) gives a source-conditional gap-squared rate.
The previously proved optimal rectangle COEFFICIENT mass
  Lambda_C=[2-2/(2L-1)]^t
remains correct. It now enters only the choice of finite feature accuracy,
not (W6). Its lower bound is not a lower bound on G_A or G_B.

6. General local-load and state-dependent versions

For a chosen one-round factorization define its local loads
  a(x,y,u)=sum_k lambda_k f_k(u),
  b(x,y,v)=sum_k lambda_k g_k(v),
and maxima a_max(x,y),b_max(x,y). For tensor-product tested factorizations,
the corresponding tested loads are products of these one-round loads.
Completeness of the ORIGINAL coarse POVMs gives
  G_A <= [E_mu a_max]^t,
  G_B <= [E_mu b_max]^t.
Thus put
  rho_load=max{1,E_mu a_max,E_mu b_max}.
Then the source proof yields
  omega_co(G^n)<=exp[-2^-75 epsilon^3 n/
                                (epsilon+log rho_load)]. (W8)
This can be much stronger than the coefficient-mass bound: a matching
mask with arbitrarily many labels has loads one but coefficient mass
equal to its number of accepted pairs.

More sharply one may retain the ACTUAL G_A,G_B, with no worst-answer
maximum. A uniform proof that, on every selected near-perfect R/H instance,
  G_A,G_B<=exp(kappa epsilon t)
would give the quadratic rate exp[-2^-75 epsilon^2 n/(1+kappa)] while
preserving E exactly and using the old S. This is a concrete additional
R/H invariant target, distinct from fidelity repair or an approximate
filter with false-accept mass. It is NOT established in general.

7. A one-step obstruction to an automatic near-perfect weighted-mass bound

There is no bound G_A,G_B<=1+o(1) solely from p->1 for arbitrary positive
factorizations, even after optimizing the factorization. Consider M=C,
one fixed tested question, Alice probabilities P=(1-r,r), Bob
Q=(r,1-r), 0<r<=1/2, and winning mask
  theta=[[1,1],[0,1]].
Then p=1-r^2->1. Nevertheless every exact bounded positive factorization
has max(G_A,G_B)>=(3-r)/2, and equality is possible.

Proof: every nonzero atom f g^T must satisfy f1*g0=0, since the missing
cell cannot have positive weight. On such an atom,
  f0*g0+f1*g1+(1-r)*f0*g1
    <=(1-r)*f0+r*f1+r*g0+(1-r)*g1.
If f1=0, use f0*g0<=(1-r)f0+r g0 and f0*g1<=g1;
if g0=0, use f1*g1<=r f1+(1-r)g1 and f0*g1<=f0.
Summing the atom inequalities with lambda gives
  3-r<=G_A+G_B,
so max>=(3-r)/2. Attain it with two fuzzy rectangles of coefficient one:
  f=(1,0), g=(1,1/2);
  f=(1/2,1), g=(0,1).
Their sum is theta; both actual one-sided masses are (3-r)/2.

This is a sharp one-step obstruction, not a repetition lower bound.
The single-question game has value one and can be repaired to its constant
accepted pair (0,1), so semantic retraction can remove this particular
nuisance entirely. An independent automatic-reject flag makes the base gap
positive but retains that removable core. No lower bound for optimal
global tested factorizations of its tensor powers is asserted. The example
shows only that near-unit p and effect completeness do not automatically
prove the stronger general state-weighted-mass target.
