Atmospheric-air nitrogen oxidation support: primary evidence and accounting

Scope

This note compares three primary plasma studies that use air as the nitrogen source and make a NOx / nitrite / nitrate carrier. It does not treat wastewater-derived nitrate as fixed atmospheric N, and it does not count oxygen-enriched feed as air-only unless oxygen-production energy is included. For the gas-phase NOx studies below, each NO or NO2 molecule contains one fixed N atom, so mol NOx = mol N fixed. For aqueous NO3− and NO2−, each anion also contains one N atom. All power figures are kept on the paper's stated input boundary; missing compressor, pump, or power-supply costs are not silently set to zero.

Evidence table (three primary papers)

1. E. Vervloessem et al., “Sustainable NOx production from air in pulsed plasma: elucidating the chemistry behind the low energy consumption,” Green Chemistry 24 (2022), 916–929, DOI: 10.1039/D1GC02762J. Primary full text: https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docstore/d:irua:12363 .

   Feed and reactor: compressed dry air, stated purity ≥99.999%; needle / quartz-capillary / grounded outer-tube Soft Jet; 0.2–2 L min−1. Pulses are 0.74 μs, in 26.3 ms trains with 149.9 ms off time (14.9% train duty). Average gas temperature is reported as 300–330 K with transient high-temperature periods around 1750 K. NO and NO2 are measured online by calibrated FTIR; NO dominates, NO2 is secondary and low, and O3, N2O5, N2O3, N2O, and NO3 were not detected. See article pp. 2, 5–6 (lines 106–145 and 448–505 in repository PDF extraction).

   Best reported gas-phase NOx energy: 0.42 ± 0.03 MJ mol−1 NOx at 1.5 L min−1 and about 200 ppm total NOx; production rate is about 20–30 mg NOx h−1. This is the strongest low-specific-energy atmospheric-pressure air result among these three, but it is a dilute, small-output stream. The paper defines energy cost as plasma power divided by molar NOx output. The 0.42 MJ mol−1 uses 0.1 W absorbed plasma power; the applied power entering the system is reported as 0.76 W, and the paper explicitly says power-supply efficiency is excluded. Holding the measured product rate fixed, the same output normalized to 0.76 W is approximately 3.2 MJ mol−1 NOx, before any compressor or other auxiliaries. Thus 0.42 is a discharge-absorbed-power metric, not wall-plug system energy. See pp. 2 and 5–6 (lines 132–155, 474–495).

   Carrier capture / downstream boundary: this paper’s LNT adsorption/reduction experiment does not take the plasma outlet directly. It simulates that outlet with a gas mixture of 200 ppm NO, 20% O2, and 80% N2, then reduces the stored NOx with 5% H2 in N2. At 175 °C the reported NOx-to-NH3 selectivity is 78%; at 125 °C it reaches 82% but some NO breakthrough occurs. The paper’s overall ~2.1 MJ mol−1 NH3 is a process calculation that combines plasma energy, an assumed 80% LNT selectivity, and H2 electrolysis at assumed 70% efficiency; it is not an integrated atmospheric-air-to-ammonia run. This distinction matters if the oxidation leg is used as a real carrier supply. See pp. 3–5 (especially lines 218–245 and 307–345).

2. I. Tsonev et al., “Importance of geometric effects in scaling up energy-efficient plasma-based nitrogen fixation,” Sustainable Energy & Fuels 8 (2024), 2191–2209, DOI: 10.1039/D3SE01615C. Primary paper PDF: https://medialibrary.uantwerpen.be/files/7783/fef815ff-6b16-43ee-a8e8-b48824087088.pdf .

   Feed and reactor: compressed ordinary air, not oxygen-enriched air; the paper specifies N2:O2 ≈ 80:20. Air flow is measured in normal litres at 0 °C and 1 bar. The small DC arc reactor spans 5–20 ln min−1; the larger reactor spans 100–300 ln min−1. Gas NO and NO2 are measured with calibrated NDIR/NDUV analyzers; NO2 is <2% of NO under the reported conditions. They measure voltage and current at the plasma discharge terminals with a high-voltage probe, shunt and oscilloscope, average instantaneous V×I over three scopes, and normalize to measured NO+NO2 output. This is stronger input accounting than quoting a power-supply nameplate, but remains plasma-terminal power: PSU conversion loss and air-compressor consumption are outside the reported EC. See pp. 2–4 (lines 239–269, 294–308, 330–410).

   Practical air results: small reactor, 20 ln min−1, 1.72% total NOx, 2.8 MJ mol−1, 33 g NOx h−1. Large reactor in torch configuration, 300 ln min−1, 0.31% total NOx, 2.9 MJ mol−1, 80 g NOx h−1. Since NOx is predominantly NO, these correspond approximately to 1.1 and 2.7 mol N h−1 respectively (about 15 and 37 g N h−1); reported product-rate basis is NOx mass, not N mass. The paper attributes the improved throughput to the larger fraction of gas intersecting the freely moving torch arc. The high-flow outlet is still dilute relative to the small reactor; unreacted air dilution and rapid quenching reduce back-reaction while limiting concentration. See pp. 1 and 16–17 (lines 7–28, 1390–1435).

   Thermal / kinetic floor interpretation: the paper’s 2.74 MJ mol−1 “thermodynamic minimum” is computed for atmospheric-pressure air in the thermal-equilibrium model: heat the gas from 300 K to a chosen equilibrium temperature and divide by equilibrium NO yield. It is a thermal-equilibrium process benchmark, not a universal thermodynamic minimum for all nonequilibrium plasmas or final aqueous species. The same paper notes historical reduced-pressure microwave results of 0.2–0.9 MJ mol−1 at 50 Torr that were not reproduced, illustrating why there is no single demonstrated kinetic floor for all plasma regimes. The observed 2.8–2.9 MJ mol−1 is close to this paper’s particular thermal benchmark. See pp. 1–2, 5–6, and 16–17 (lines 120–132, 415–459, 1331–1348, 1441–1448).

3. S. Ghorui, N. Tiwari & H. Parab, “Atmospheric Pressure Portable Catalytic Thermal Plasma System for Fast Synthesis of Aqueous NO3 and NO2 Fertilizer from Air and Water,” Plasma Chemistry and Plasma Processing 45 (2025), 371–402, DOI: 10.1007/s11090-024-10514-3. Primary full text: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11090-024-10514-3 .

   Feed and capture: ambient air is brought by compressor into a thermal plasma torch; the plasma jet interacts with recirculating water mist and a surface-enhanced SS304 catalyst bed. The outlet product is measured in solution by ion chromatography (IC), rather than inferred from wastewater nitrate or gas-only NOx. The setup uses a 100 L water batch and a 65 L min−1 recirculating mist flow; the torch is operated at about 21 kW. After about 20 min, reported solution concentrations are approximately 200 ppm nitrate and 120 ppm nitrite; elsewhere the paper reports 207 ppm nitrate and product rates of 1035 mg min−1 nitrate and 635 mg min−1 nitrite. IC methods use standards and filtration, with stated maximum analytical error ±5%. See sections “Synthesis of Nitrogen Fertilizer” and “Ion Chromatography” (article lines 173–184, 175–178, 181–184, 228–232, 354–359).

   Specific energy on measured captured-ion basis: use the authors’ mass rates and one N per ion:
     NO3−: 1.035 g min−1 / 62.00 g mol−1 = 0.01669 mol N min−1.
     NO2−: 0.635 g min−1 / 46.01 g mol−1 = 0.01380 mol N min−1.
     Total measured fixed N = 0.03049 mol min−1 = 1.83 mol h−1 ≈ 25.6 g N h−1.
     21 kJ s−1 × 60 s min−1 / 0.03049 mol min−1 = 41.3 MJ mol−1 N.
   This independently reproduces the paper’s approximately 40 MJ mol−1 energy yield. By mole of captured measured N, the immediate output split is about 54.7% nitrate and 45.3% nitrite. The paper observes that nitrite continues oxidizing in the water after operation and reports about 97% conversion within five days, so speciation depends on the time at which the product is sampled.

   Crucial accounting limits: 21 kW is the reported torch operating power, not a documented wall-plug meter reading for the complete unit. The article does not report a metered ambient-air flow or total inlet N2 feed, so one cannot infer N2 conversion fraction or fix a closed nitrogen mass balance. The 65 L min−1 figure is water recirculation, not air throughput. Compressor, pump, and supply losses are not itemized in the stated ~40 MJ mol−1; treat that number as torch-power / measured aqueous-ion yield, not full-system energy. The immediate solution values and synthesis-rate claims are approximate and not identical (e.g. 120 ppm nitrite in 100 L over 20 min is not exactly the mass implied by 635 mg min−1); preserve the authors’ approximation and use the rate pair only for the independent SEC calculation. The data are described as contained in paper / available on reasonable request, not as a public raw dataset.

Thermodynamic lower scales and interpretation

- For NO(g), the 2026 Active Thermochemical Tables result gives ΔfH°(298.15 K) = +91.152 ± 0.066 kJ mol−1 for 1/2 N2 + 1/2 O2 → NO. This is the chemical enthalpy stored per mole of NO/N at standard conditions, not the electrical energy a practical process must consume and not a complete process bound for nitrate/nitrite capture. Source: https://atct.anl.gov/Thermochemical%20Data/version%201.222/reaction/?c1=-0.5&c2=-0.5&c3=1&species_number%5B%5D=5&species_number%5B%5D=51&species_number%5B%5D=6 .
- For the specific atmospheric thermal-equilibrium model, Tsonev et al. calculate 2.74 MJ mol−1 NOx. It includes heating a gas stream and its equilibrium product yield, so it is a useful floor for that thermal process class; it is not a universal kinetic floor.
- The pulsed plasma result of 0.42 MJ mol−1 shows that nonequilibrium or pulsed operation can report below that thermal benchmark on the plasma-absorbed-power basis. The result is low rate and dilute, and system-input normalization raises it to about 3.2 MJ mol−1 using the paper’s applied-power number. Therefore no universal practical kinetic minimum is established by these three studies. A number below 2.74 MJ mol−1 should be checked for pressure/feed composition, product concentration, whether NOx is gaseous or absorbed, plasma-only versus applied/wall power, and omitted compression/capture.

Decision-useful reading

- Lowest reported oxidation SEC in this evidence set: pulsed Soft Jet, 0.42 MJ mol−1 gas NOx absorbed power; about 0.76 W applied-system input gives ~3.2 MJ mol−1 at the same production rate, and output is only ~20–30 mg NOx h−1 at ~200 ppm.
- Best measured scale-up balance on ordinary air: DC arc torch, ~2.9 MJ mol−1 and 80 g NOx h−1 at 300 ln min−1, but the reported EC is at the plasma-terminal boundary, not complete plant power, and this study does not absorb the NOx into a carrier solution.
- Best direct aqueous carrier evidence among these three: atmospheric air-to-water thermal plasma, ~40 MJ mol−1 captured N and ~25.6 g N h−1 by the paper’s rate pair, with an immediate ~55:45 nitrate:nitrite mole split. The source shows direct solution capture and IC speciation but lacks inlet-air metering and full-system electrical metering.

No result above establishes an integrated, wall-plug-metered, continuous atmospheric-air → quantified NOx capture → downstream N-reduction system at the same operating point. The strongest follow-up discriminator is a closed N balance and common energy boundary across gas feed, plasma supply, compressor, absorber/capture, and carrier output, while reporting nitrate/nitrite speciation at a stated post-sampling time.
