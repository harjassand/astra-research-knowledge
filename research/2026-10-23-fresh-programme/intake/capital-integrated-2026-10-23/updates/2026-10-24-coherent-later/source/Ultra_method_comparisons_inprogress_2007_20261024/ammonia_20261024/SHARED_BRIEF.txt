SHARED UPSTREAM NEED — RENEWABLE NITROGEN-TO-AMMONIA TRANSFORMATION
Created 2026-10-24. Research evidence, not a declaration of a device or breakthrough.

Need and consequential outcome
Develop a scientifically credible operation that makes renewable ammonia production materially more useful at distributed scale: scalable output from atmospheric nitrogen and water-derived hydrogen/reducing power, with all carriers and reagents regenerated, useful product recovered, and the energy/material/control boundary closed. Success could open fertilizer production and chemical energy storage at scales or operating conditions poorly served by current systems. A new fundamental framework that identifies and enables the missing chemical operation is worthwhile even before a device exists. A different topic, catalyst list, optimization of a supplied favorable rate, or polished proposal is insufficient.

The same upstream need applies to both constructors. Neither room temperature nor atmospheric pressure is mandatory: those are possible means, not success criteria. Purified N2 or H2 may be used, but their production, storage and conditioning must be accounted for before comparing an air/water process. State product concentration, separation and nitrogen provenance. Do not credit sacrificial metal, alcohol, hydride, proton donor, stored charge or carrier degradation as a cyclic catalyst. Do not require an immediate apparatus to justify a consequential theoretical result.

Strong conventional route
Water electrolysis plus nitrogen separation and a modern Haber–Bosch synthesis/separation/recycle loop is the primary comparator, including heat integration, hydrogen buffering and partially flexible operation. Do not compare against fossil-derived H2 while granting renewable electricity to the candidate. Do not presume that only the new route can respond to variable power. Match feed/product, scale, power/heat quality, carrier recycling and purity; expose unresolved scale and capital tradeoffs instead of inventing values.
