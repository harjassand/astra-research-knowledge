OPEN-ARM SUPPORT: CONVENTIONAL GREEN HABER–BOSCH BASELINE
========================================================

Scope
-----
This note reads only shared-brief sources 4 and 5. Both are process/energy
models, not operating-plant demonstrations. They answer complementary questions:
Salmon & Bañares-Alcántara model renewable scheduling, flexibility and storage;
Izelaar et al. provide a fuller 91 tNH3/day material/energy and TEA comparison
of electrochemical routes against electrified and fossil-H2 Haber–Bosch (HB).
Their LCOA values cannot be combined: the systems, years, scale assumptions,
weather treatment, lifetimes and cost boundaries differ.

1. Flexibility and imperfect forecasts: Salmon & Bañares-Alcántara (2023)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Primary source: https://doi.org/10.1039/D3SU00067B
Publisher HTML: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2023/su/d3su00067b
Publisher PDF: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2023/su/d3su00067b
Oxford record/author version: https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:b2a3cc56-ced4-4be9-bf99-b751a509f55a

What it models. Section 2 uses a perfect-forecast linear program (LP) for plant
design and model-predictive control (MPC) for operation under finite forecasts.
The energy-system equipment includes solar PV, wind, electrolysers, HB (with
air separation), fuel cells, batteries and hydrogen storage. Weather is ERA5;
421 onshore locations are used for design sweeps, then three contrasting
locations (Algeria solar, UK wind, Morocco hybrid) are used for detailed
operation tests. The main article presents HB minimum operating rates (MOR)
and ramp-rate factors relative to rated HB capacity; it does not state one
common tNH3/day nameplate for all sites. Do not relabel the 421-site study as
the 91 t/day plant in source 5.

Scale, feeds, purity and system boundary. This is an islanded renewable-power
and dispatch/plant-sizing model, not a detailed ammonia mass-balance design.
Its equipment boundary does include H2 electrolysis and storage, air separation,
HB, renewable generation and backup/storage. The passages read do not specify
one common N2/H2 feed purity, NH3 product purity, water source/treatment, or
downstream ammonia separation/recycle specification. It therefore supplies
strong evidence that conventional HB can participate in an islanded flexible
renewable system, but cannot by itself serve as a matched product-quality or
full-process mass-balance estimate.

Numerical flexibility findings, Sections 3.1–3.3 and 4. The model tests MOR
20/40/60/80% and ramp factors 0.1/1/10 against baseline down/up rates of
20%/5% per hour; the constrained case is 2%/hour down and 0.5%/hour up. That
slow-ramp case changes average 2022 cost by about $15/tNH3, with less effect in
2050, and matters mainly near MOR=100%. In the average-site 2022/2050 design
cases, lowering MOR from 100% to 60% yields about four times the LCOA reduction
of lowering it from 60% to 20%; by 2050, the latter reduction is under 10% at
the average site, and smaller at top sites, even with storage-cycle constraints.
The article reports equipment improvements as a larger lever (cost reductions
on the order of 50% versus up to about 30% from flexibility in 2022 and about
10% for good sites in 2050). These are model results, not observed plant costs.

Forecast/control scope. Section 3.2 lets MPC see a 12/24/48-hour forecast; the
main real-cost comparison uses 24 hours. With enough oversizing and tuning,
solar and hybrid cases can approach LP production, while wind cases suffer from
multi-day weather variation; wind flexibility remains a useful lever. At the
wind case, no oversizing gives hundreds of annual failures; the hybrid case has
about two in 2022; the solar case can operate without oversizing. The model's
LCOA counts oversizing and production shortfall (§3.3). This is an important
counter to any assumption that only a new synthesis chemistry can handle
variable electricity.

Energy/cost accounting. The principal reported metric is LCOA, not a single
process energy-efficiency number. The article includes renewable generation,
electrolyser/HB sizing, storage and replacement/degradation assumptions; for
example, electrolyser electricity use is set to 53 kWh/kg H2 in 2022 and
46 kWh/kg in 2050, battery charge/discharge efficiency to 95%, membrane-related
O&M/replacement is included, and the project horizon is 30 years with 7%
discounting. The design model accounts for solar/wind and storage costs rather
than treating a continuously available, free H2 feed as its comparator. It is
the better of the two sources for the flexibility/storage issue, but the weaker
one for feed/product purity and detailed separation balances.

2. Integrated small-scale comparison: Izelaar et al. (2024)
---------------------------------------------------------
Primary source: https://doi.org/10.1039/D4EE03299C
Publisher article/PDF: https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/ee/d4ee03299c
Open full-text mirror: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11462118/
ESI file linked from article XML: EE-017-D4EE03299C-s001.pdf (the article DOI
is the primary persistent identifier; the ESI is linked there).

Scale and operating assumption. In “Process design assumptions and
descriptions,” all routes are compared at 91 tNH3/day, selected as the
smallest commercial SMR-HB plant serving local markets. Operation is continuous
at high capacity factor; variable renewable availability is explicitly outside
this study's scope. TEA assumes 333 operating days/year, 20-year lives for
electrolysis routes and 40 years for SMR-HB, and no electrolyser-stack
replacement. Thus 91 t/day is the strongest matched numerical full-process
comparator in these two sources, but it remains a local-market plant-scale
baseline rather than evidence for a much smaller unit.

Conventional green HB route and feed/product. Figure 1(a) and “Process design
assumptions and descriptions” model a water electrolyser (AEL or PEMEL) for H2,
an air separation unit (ASU) for high-purity N2, compression, the conventional
thermocatalytic HB loop, and downstream condensation/recycle. The text says
N2/H2 are compressed to 155 bar before a 400 °C reactor; reactor heat is
recovered to preheat feed. The N2/H2/NH3 stream is cooled to -5 °C and flashed
to 99.5 mol% NH3; the remaining gas has 4 mol% NH3 and is recycled. In the
energy-accounting passage the condenser is stated as typically -5 °C/145 bar,
consistent with a pressure drop after the 155-bar compression/reactor feed.
The article states that high-purity N2 is required because residual O2 poisons
the Fe catalyst. PSA is more economical below 500 tN2/day, cryogenic distillation
above; with stoichiometric N2 demand for 91 t/day NH3 below that threshold, the
PSA scale rule is relevant, but use the process-flow/SI table before claiming
which specific ASU choice or O2 coproduct revenue was used in a given scenario.
Argon from the ASU is explicitly excluded.

Water/H2 and boundary. In electrified HB, H2 is generated from water by AEL or
PEMEL; H2 is not a free boundary input. Main-text base assumptions assign water
a price of $7.5/m3. The modeled boundary includes ASU, electrolyzer, compressors,
HB synthesis/recycle/separation and associated process duties; electricity price
and electrolyser capital assumptions are projected for 2022–2050. AEL is
assumed at 75% and PEMEL at 65% efficiency on stoichiometric H2 LHV; the modeled
H2-generation electricity inputs are 28.4 and 32.8 GJ/tNH3, respectively.
PEMEL’s 35–50 bar outlet H2 can reduce compressor duty by up to 56%. The paper
excludes losses in power electronics, connections and cables. It prices water,
but the main text does not specify desalination or a site-specific water-supply
plant. Do not silently add one, or claim that it was included.

Energy versus cost. The article defines EE = NH3 LHV (18.6 GJ/t) divided by
total energy input. Electrified HB totals 33.9 GJ/tNH3 and is less energy
efficient than its cited best-available SMR-HB range, 27.4–31.8 GJ/t, because
water electrolysis dominates. This energy figure is distinct from LCOA. The
source's 91 t/day SMR-HB benchmark has $936M estimated investment and base-case
LCOA $555/tNH3; this is a fossil-H2 cost reference, not a climate-matched green
baseline. The clean conventional comparator is electrified HB with PEMEL, the
paper's preferred sustainable route on maturity/cost/energy efficiency. Under
its 2022-based projections, e-HB is still more expensive than SMR-HB; the paper
reports parity with SMR at PEMEL capex $615/kW and electricity $0.035/kWh, and
states this requires reductions of about 68% and 38%, respectively, from its
base-case projections. Treat this as a break-even condition, not as an observed
current LCOA. Electricity price is the largest LCOA driver; a $0.01/kWh change
changes PEMEL-HB LCOA by roughly $100/tNH3, while a $100/kW stack-cost change
changes it by about $60/tNH3 (~5%).

TEA construction and limitations. The authors say there were no NRR electrolyser
capital-cost data; those were inferred from commercial water-electrolyser data.
Standard equipment estimates use capacity correlations/engineering heuristics,
Lang factors for total capital, and per-equipment pressure/temperature/material
installation factors; electrolyser installation is assumed at 10% of stack
equipment cost. The article describes NPV=0 LCOA with capex at year 0, plus
operating costs, 25% tax, 25% salvage, linear depreciation and 4.28% discounting.
Those financing inputs differ from source 4's 7%/30-year islanded design.

3. Comparator decision and exact non-matches
--------------------------------------------
For a distributed-scale, renewable-N2/H2-to-NH3 comparison, use the 91 t/day
electrified-HB/PEMEL case from Izelaar et al. as the strongest detailed
conventional process model in this pair: it specifies water-derived H2, air-
separated N2, compression, synthesis, recycle, separation and a numeric product
purity, while accounting for whole-process energy and LCOA. Use the Salmon &
Bañares-Alcántara model to challenge any claim that conventional HB cannot
follow variable renewable power without very low MOR; it explicitly co-optimizes
generation, H2/battery buffers and plant operation.

Do not conflate the two. Source 5 assumes continuous high-capacity-factor
operation and has a chemistry/process-energy balance; source 4 explicitly
models weather-driven islanded operation and storage but does not establish a
common NH3 production rate, feed/product assay, water-treatment load or detailed
separation boundary. Nor should the $555/t fossil-SMR value be treated as the
renewable baseline. The appropriate matched conventional baseline is green
electrolysis plus HB with its electricity, ASU, storage/variability and product
separation costs stated at the same site, annual capacity factor, scale and
financial assumptions as the candidate.
