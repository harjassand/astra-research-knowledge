Model-first ammonia construction: detailed-balance support

Scope

This note gives thermodynamic constraints for a minimal A/B nitrogen-binding machine with a two-state redox/ligand gate. It does not assign any rate, especially the A-to-B transfer rate, and does not decide whether a complete construction is kinetically or materially realizable.

State and sign conventions

Let a machine microstate be x=(g,n_A,n_B), with g in {0,1} and n_A,n_B in {0,1}. Here n_A and n_B count bound nitrogen-atom equivalents; a more detailed mechanism can expand these labels to include protonation, oxidation, ligand occupancy, or multiple N-containing intermediates. F_x is the free energy of that internal state at the specified temperature, pressure, and standard-state convention. Any redox/ligand inventory that is part of the machine belongs in x and F_x.

For an elementary transition x -> y, let nu_{r,e} be positive when molecules, ions, electrons, or ligands are taken from reservoir r into the machine. Let w_e be positive work delivered to the machine. Define the generalized free-energy increment

  Delta G_e^gen = F_y - F_x - sum_r(mu_r nu_{r,e}) - w_e.

Local detailed balance (LDB) requires

  ln(k_{x->y}/k_{y->x}) = -beta Delta G_e^gen,   beta=1/(k_B T).

For charged reservoirs, mu_r is the electrochemical potential, including the electrical work under the chosen sign convention. A transition-state representation makes the kinetic limitation explicit: if k_{x->y}=a_e exp[-beta(B_e-F_x)] and k_{y->x}=a_e exp[-beta(B_e-F_y)] for a passive edge with no reservoir exchange or work, then LDB fixes their ratio but leaves the common prefactor a_e and barrier B_e free. A high transfer barrier can make both rates very small without violating LDB. Thus thermodynamics alone supplies no favorable central-transfer rate.

Gate transitions and their cost

For a controlled instantaneous switch at fixed occupation n=(n_A,n_B), the work on the machine is

  W_on(n) = F_1(n)-F_0(n)       for 0 -> 1,
  W_reset(n) = F_0(n)-F_1(n)   for 1 -> 0.

If activation occurs at n_on and reset at n_off, the net control work is

  W_gate = F_1(n_on)-F_0(n_on) + F_0(n_off)-F_1(n_off).

It need not vanish just because the gate label returns to 0: the gate is switched at different machine occupations. For a chemically regenerated gate, replace the controlled work term by the actual net reservoir exchanges (for example, reducing equivalents, oxidant, protons, or a consumed ligand) in the LDB sum. Do not count the same regeneration both as chemical fuel and as externally supplied work. If gate switching is spontaneous rather than controlled, its forward and reverse rates must obey LDB with those same state energies and reservoir terms.

Cycle constraint

Consider a complete forward turnover that begins and ends in the same internal state, produces one NH3, and restores the gate. Per NH3, use formal atom-equivalent bookkeeping: take 1/2 N2 and 3/2 H2 from their reservoirs and release one NH3. This is net stoichiometry, not a claim that half a molecule is an elementary event; a microscopic model can instead represent N2 activation explicitly or run two nitrogen turnovers per N2 molecule. Define

  Delta G_rxn = mu_NH3 - (1/2)mu_N2 - (3/2)mu_H2.

Let nu_gate denote all additional net gate-regeneration reservoir stoichiometries, and let W_ctrl be net externally delivered work over the closed protocol. Summing LDB over the cycle cancels every internal free-energy difference, including the A/B binding and gate energies, and gives the cycle affinity

  A_cycle = sum_e ln(k_e^+/k_e^-)
          = beta[-Delta G_rxn + sum_gate,r(mu_r nu_r) + W_ctrl].

Therefore, if each forward edge in the complete capture -> transfer -> NH3 release -> gate restoration cycle is strictly downhill in generalized free energy, then A_cycle>0 and necessarily

  Delta G_rxn < sum_gate,r(mu_r nu_r) + W_ctrl.

In particular, with a chemically and mechanically free restored gate (zero net gate-reservoir exchange and W_ctrl=0), all-forward-edges-downhill is impossible when Delta G_rxn >= 0. For an uphill net NH3 reaction, some step must be driven uphill by a net fuel/work input, or the cycle cannot run forward with every elementary step downhill. Conversely, if the net reaction is downhill under the actual reservoir chemical potentials, LDB creates no universal obstruction to all-downhill edges; it still does not establish adequate rates or selectivity.

The same statement can be expressed without assigning a mechanism to the middle step. Let the forward edge list include N capture at A, any gate activation, A-to-B transfer, B-site hydrogenation/protonation and NH3 release, and gate regeneration. The sum of their Delta G_e^gen is fixed by the net reservoir stoichiometry and control work above. Redistribution of free energy among A binding, B binding, gate-state stabilization, and transition barriers cannot change that sum.

Testable gates

1. For each proposed reversible elementary edge, measure or calculate both directional rates under the same fixed reservoir conditions and check the LDB ratio against the independently determined state free-energy and reservoir-potential differences. Check gate activation and reset edges separately.
2. Measure the full-turnover net stoichiometry: N2/NH3, H2 (or proton/electron equivalents), ligand/redox-regeneration species, and any external work. The measured cycle product of forward/reverse rate ratios should agree with exp(A_cycle) for a closed Markov-cycle model.
3. Verify that the terminal internal state has the original gate state and the intended A/B occupancies. If the gate is restored at another occupancy, include the occupancy-dependent W_gate or chemical regeneration exchange explicitly.
4. Determine the A-to-B forward and reverse rates or barrier independently. LDB predicts their ratio once the state and reservoir free energies are known; it does not predict their absolute magnitude.

These are thermodynamic and rate-ratio checks only. They do not imply a practical ammonia synthesis cycle, a particular active-state population, or favorable catalyst performance.
