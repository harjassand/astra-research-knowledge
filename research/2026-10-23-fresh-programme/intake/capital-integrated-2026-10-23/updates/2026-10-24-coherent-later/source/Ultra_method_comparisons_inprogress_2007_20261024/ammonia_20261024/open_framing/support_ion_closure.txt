# Ammonia arm: independent ion and thermodynamic closure

Status: bounded support calculation from the supplied net operation. The net
reaction and H2-anode/NO2-cathode assignment are supplied; the half-reactions,
membrane flux counts, and acid/base closure below are derived. Thermochemical
values are acquired from the cited evaluated tables / measurements. This note
does not specify a device or claim performance.

## 1. Redox and proton bookkeeping

The supplied neutral-ammonia reaction is

    NO2- + H+ + 3 H2 -> NH3 + 2 H2O.

Nitrogen changes from +3 in nitrite to -3 in ammonia, so the cathode accepts
six electrons per N atom. In acidic notation the half-reactions are

    Anode (H2 oxidation):  3 H2 -> 6 H+ + 6 e-
    Cathode (NH3):         NO2- + 7 H+ + 6 e- -> NH3 + 2 H2O.

Adding them gives exactly the supplied net reaction: six of the cathodic seven
proton equivalents are supplied by the anode reaction; one is consumed net.
Thus a closed process cannot count the anode's six regenerated protons as six
additional free acid equivalents.

If the nitrogen product leaves as ammonium, the cathode half-reaction is

    NO2- + 8 H+ + 6 e- -> NH4+ + 2 H2O,

and the net reaction is

    NO2- + 2 H+ + 3 H2 -> NH4+ + 2 H2O.

This is the same atom/electron closure plus one additional protonation
equivalent. In water, NH4+ <-> NH3(aq) + H+ has pKa about 9.25 at 25 C from
the standard Gibbs values below; the neutral fraction rises with pH. Gas-phase
NH3 is the neutral product in a different standard state. Stripping it changes
its chemical potential/fugacity and shifts the acid-base equilibrium; it does
not remove the proton or charge balance. If the feed is a nitrite salt MNO2,
the residual spectator ions must also be carried into an electrically neutral
effluent (for example, M+ pairs with the counteranion of the acid feed).

## 2. Membrane-by-membrane charge and acid/base closure

The faradaic charge is 6 F per mole of ammonia at unit selectivity. The
following ideal flux counts express the charge-carrying equivalents; they do
not assume that a real membrane has 100% selectivity or no water drag.

**PEM, proton transport anode -> cathode.** Six H+ equivalents cross per
six-electron reaction. The anode generates six H+ and the cathode consumes
seven for NH3, leaving one net H+ equivalent consumed from the cathode-side
feed. For NH4+ the cathode consumes eight, leaving two net H+ equivalents
consumed. With salt/acid feeds these equivalents appear in the neutral salt
and product inventory; they are not a free pH credit.

**AEM, hydroxide transport cathode -> anode.** In alkaline notation the
corresponding half-reactions are

    Cathode (NH3):  NO2- + 5 H2O + 6 e- -> NH3 + 7 OH-
    Anode:          3 H2 + 6 OH- -> 6 H2O + 6 e-.

Six OH- equivalents cross to the anode, exactly matching its six-OH
consumption. The net reaction is

    NO2- + 3 H2 -> NH3 + OH- + H2O.

The remaining OH- is one net base equivalent per NH3 (paired by the feed
counterion in a salt balance). For ammonium output the alkaline cathode is

    NO2- + 6 H2O + 6 e- -> NH4+ + 8 OH-,

so after six OH- cross to the anode the net is

    NO2- + 3 H2 -> NH4+ + 2 OH-.

These alkaline and acidic net equations are equivalent: neutralizing the
one or two OH- equivalents with one or two H+ gives the respective supplied
acid-form net reactions (and the water balance). If the base is retained as a
product, it must be counted as a coproduct; if pH is restored, its regeneration
or neutralization has a thermodynamic cost.

**Bipolar membrane, water-dissociation orientation.** For 6 F, ideal water
dissociation supplies six H+ equivalents to the cathode side and six OH-
equivalents to the anode side. Pair the acidic cathode half-reaction (which
needs 7 H+ for NH3, 8 for NH4+) with the alkaline anode half-reaction (which
uses 6 OH-). The six membrane-generated protons/hydroxides meet the six
electrode-transfer equivalents; one H+ (NH3) or two H+ (NH4+) remain net
consumed from feed. Summing also the membrane water-splitting reaction

    6 H2O -> 6 H+ + 6 OH-

recovers the same net acid-form chemistry. The BPM is not a source of free
acid/base: its separated H+/OH- flux and water dissociation are part of the
cell's electrical/chemical work, while the residual net proton consumption
remains. A different BPM orientation changes which compartment receives
which equivalent, not the overall elemental or charge balance.

## 3. pH terms and closed-cycle cancellation

For aqueous neutral NH3 product, with water activity near one, the net
reaction quotient is

    Q = a_NH3 / (a_NO2- a_H+ (f_H2/f°)^3).

For NH4+ product it is

    Q = a_NH4+ / (a_NO2- a_H+^2 (f_H2/f°)^3).

Since a_H+ = 10^(-pH), increasing pH by one unit at fixed other activities
raises reaction free energy by 2.303 RT per net proton for NH3 (and twice
that for NH4+). At 298.15 K these increments are 5.71 kJ mol-1 and 11.42 kJ
mol-1, respectively. In the six-electron Nernst voltage this corresponds to
9.86 mV per pH unit for NH3 and 19.72 mV per pH unit for NH4+. For a gas NH3
product, replace a_NH3 by its dimensionless fugacity; the pH coefficient is
unchanged.

The larger single-cathode pH coefficient is not the full-cell coefficient.
For the NH3 cathode, E_c contains 7 H+ equivalents and shifts by about
-69.0 mV/pH, while the H+/H2 anode reduction potential contains 6 and shifts
by about -59.2 mV/pH. Subtracting anode from cathode leaves only the net-one-
proton shift, about -9.86 mV/pH. The analogous 8-versus-6 count leaves the
net-two-proton coefficient for NH4+. Thus the individual electrode's apparent
pH voltage cannot be credited without the opposing electrode and membrane
terms.

More generally, pH is a chemical-potential state variable. A pH gradient can
temporarily shift a local electrode potential, but rebuilding the same acid,
base, and buffer inventories after each turnover costs the corresponding
chemical work. For example H+ + OH- -> H2O neutralization and its reverse
water-dissociation step cancel in a fully restored cycle at reversible
thermodynamic closure. The only uncanceled proton term is the one or two
equivalents consumed by the overall product stoichiometry (or the explicitly
counted acid/base coproduct). Neutralization can release free energy, while
regenerating separated acid/base costs the corresponding free energy. No
cyclic pH gain survives when all streams return to their initial states and
all acid/base regeneration work is counted.

## 4. Acquired thermochemical check (298.15 K)

Standard states here are 1 bar for gases and 1 molal ideal-dilute convention
for aqueous species; H+(aq) has the conventional zero reference. Evaluated
NBS/CODATA-style values give approximately:

| Species | Delta_f G degrees (kJ mol-1) | Provenance |
|---|---:|---|
| H2(g) | 0 | elemental reference |
| H2O(l) | -237.13 | NBS/Wagman compilation |
| H+(aq) | 0 | conventional single-ion reference |
| NO2-(aq) | -32.2 +/- 8.0 | 1982 NBS value, as discussed in IUPAC review |
| NH3(aq) | -26.50 | NBS/Wagman compilation |
| NH3(g) | -16.45 (about -16.4) | JANAF/CODATA; NIST WebBook reports underlying gas thermochemistry |
| NH4+(aq) | -79.31 | NBS/Wagman compilation |

Using these data, the acid-form reaction with NH3(aq) has

    Delta_r G degrees = -26.50 + 2(-237.13) - (-32.2)
                    = -468.56 +/- at least 8 kJ mol-1,

or E_rev = -Delta_rG/(6F) = +0.809 V (about +/- 0.014 V from the quoted
nitrite uncertainty). With standard-state NH3(g), Delta_rG degrees is about
-458.51 kJ mol-1 and E_rev about +0.792 V. With NH4+(aq) and two H+ reactants,
Delta_rG degrees is about -521.37 kJ mol-1 and E_rev about +0.901 V. These are
standard-state thermodynamic checks only, not operating voltages or device
predictions; concentrated-electrolyte activities, gas fugacities, product
speciation, and side products must be included for a real state.

For aqueous nitrous acid, a primary stopped-flow/spectrophotometric study
reports pKa = 3.16 at 25 C. Therefore its dissociation free energy is
2.303 RT pKa = 18.04 kJ mol-1. Combining that measured equilibrium with the
old NBS NO2- value yields an *inferred*, not independently measured, value
Delta_fG degrees(HNO2,aq) about -50.2 kJ mol-1, carrying at least the large
nitrite uncertainty. This inference is not promoted as a precise HNO2
thermochemical constant: the IUPAC review notes historical concern that NBS
HNO2 / nitrite thermochemical values may be inconsistent with HNO2 solubility
data at a level of roughly 4.6 kJ mol-1, still below the stated +/-8 kJ mol-1
nitrite uncertainty.

The ammonia values imply pKa(NH4+) =
[-26.50 - (-79.31)]/(2.303 RT) = 9.25, consistent with the usual aqueous
ammonium/ammonia partition. The gas-vs-aqueous NH3 standard chemical-potential
difference is about 10.05 kJ mol-1; treating a gas product as if it were
NH3(aq) would therefore shift the standard-state balance by about 17 mV per
six-electron reaction.

## 5. Scope boundary

All balances are stoichiometric/equilibrium accounting. They do not establish
selectivity to NH3 over N2, N2O, hydroxylamine, or other products, a membrane's
actual transference numbers, kinetics, overpotentials, transport losses,
product separation energy, or a process advantage. A pH-dependent half-cell
potential alone is insufficient to establish a cyclic voltage benefit.
