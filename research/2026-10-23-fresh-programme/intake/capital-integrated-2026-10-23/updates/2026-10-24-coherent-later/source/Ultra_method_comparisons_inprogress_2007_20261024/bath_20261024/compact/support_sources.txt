# Compact arm: primary source check

Scope: conventional partial-capture correction versus a true second thermal/native bath port; whether an input/reference quadrature alone removes the hot-dark residual. Sources were opened directly at the primary arXiv / publisher records below. No warm-arm output was read.

## Sabuncu et al., environmental-assisted correction

Metin Sabuncu, Radim Filip, Gerd Leuchs, and Ulrik L. Andersen, “Environmental Assisted Quantum Information Correction for Continuous Variables,” arXiv:0909.3546 (2009); Phys. Rev. A 81, 012325 (2010).

- Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/0909.3546
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.81.012325
- Opened: Secs. II–IV; Eqs. (1)–(5), (6)–(15), (17)–(22); experimental setup/figures and conclusion.

The signal and one noisy environmental mode are mixed at a variable beam splitter: \(X_{S,o}=\sqrt{\eta}X_{S,i}+\sqrt{1-\eta}X_{E,i}\), while \(X_{E,o}=\sqrt{1-\eta}X_{S,i}-\sqrt{\eta}X_{E,i}\), with analogous \(P\) equations (Eqs. 1–4). Their “partial access” is a loss element placed on this same leakage output: the recorded quadrature contains \(\sqrt{\gamma}X_{E,o}+\sqrt{1-\gamma}X_{vac}\) (Eqs. 6–9). Thus the uncollected fraction in this model is vacuum entering a detector/collection beam splitter, not a separately coupled hot bath.

For single-quadrature homodyne plus calibrated feedforward, the noisy environmental mode cancels from the corrected signal, leaving added vacuum noise \(V_{add}^{Hom}=(1-\eta)(1-\gamma)/\gamma\) (Eqs. 11–12). For simultaneous conjugate-quadrature heterodyne, the added noise is \(V_{S,add}^{Het}=(1-\eta)(2-\gamma)/\gamma\) (Eq. 15), even at ideal environmental measurement because the signal is phase-insensitively amplified. The feedforward gains require known channel transmission and detector-access fraction. Their experiment used coherent-state RF sidebands and an optically generated, externally modulated Gaussian-noise environment, not two independent native thermal ports (Sec. IV; 14.3 MHz sidebands; Gaussian noise generators). It demonstrated recovery of coherent-state information, not arbitrary-state identity or physical removal of the thermal bath.

Use as the strongest direct comparator for homodyne/Wiener-style environmental correction. Its ideal statement is port-specific: observing part of a given leaking mode reveals correlated information about that same mode. It does not imply that loss on a detector can stand in for an independent native port that remains coupled to the system.

## Genoni et al., filtering with a purified thermal reservoir

Marco G. Genoni, Stefano Mancini, Howard M. Wiseman, and Alessio Serafini, “Quantum filtering of a thermal master equation with a purified reservoir,” arXiv:1411.1435 (2014); Phys. Rev. A 90, 063826 (2014).

- Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1411.1435
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.90.063826
- Opened: Secs. II–IV; Eqs. (1), (4), (7), (10)–(12), (28)–(45), (43)–(55); abstract/conclusion.

The system oscillator couples to one thermal bath mode \(a\); a second mode \(b\) purifies it but does not couple to the system (Secs. II–III). They homodyne both bath modes. Their \(\gamma\) is a bath-correlation/purification parameter, not a detector efficiency. At \(\gamma=0\), \(b\) is uncorrelated and monitoring it gives no information about the system (Eq. 43). At \(\gamma=1\), the two modes form a pure two-mode squeezed purification, and joint access reaches the optimal conditional squeezing bound \(V_x=1/(2N+1)\) (Eqs. 44–45); partial purification gives intermediate performance and has a threshold for squeezing (Eq. 54).

This is a theoretical continuous-monitoring/unravelling result, not a demonstrated hardware protocol. It says that a *physical correlated purification* can help condition the system; a classical record of an unrelated thermal reference, or a single pre-interaction quadrature readout, is not that purification. It also does not say that measuring one of two independent hot native ports cancels the other port.

## Scope for the two-native-port topology

Algebraic consequence of the proposed two-port model (not a claim from either paper): for independent inputs \(b_1,b_2\), occupations \(N_1,N_2\), and couplings \(L_j=\sqrt{\gamma_j}a\), let \(\Gamma=\gamma_1+\gamma_2\). The interaction couples only to the bright input \(B=(\sqrt{\gamma_1}b_1+\sqrt{\gamma_2}b_2)/\sqrt{\Gamma}\); the orthogonal dark mode \(D=(\sqrt{\gamma_2}b_1-\sqrt{\gamma_1}b_2)/\sqrt{\Gamma}\) does not couple. Their input covariance has \(\langle B^\dagger B\rangle=(\gamma_1N_1+\gamma_2N_2)/\Gamma\) and \(\langle B^\dagger D\rangle=\sqrt{\gamma_1\gamma_2}(N_1-N_2)/\Gamma\). This elementary rotation makes the interface distinction explicit: if the two port occupations differ, the dark input can be correlated side information, but it is not detector vacuum; if it is not measured or purified, ordinary linear/Wiener conditioning generally leaves a Schur-complement residual. Exact cancellation requires a demonstrated accessible record of the relevant correlated mode(s), not relabeling the uncaptured native port as detector loss.

Decisive scope gate for any proposed advantage: compare the same two-port system under (i) one native output monitored with optimal linear conditioning, (ii) calibrated vacuum attenuation after a fully captured output, and (iii) both physical outputs or an explicitly prepared-and-accessible purification monitored. Report conditional covariance/residual per quadrature and include detector loss separately from the bath occupations. No source above establishes that a local measurement of a prepared thermal input quadrature alone removes the independent hot-port residual.
