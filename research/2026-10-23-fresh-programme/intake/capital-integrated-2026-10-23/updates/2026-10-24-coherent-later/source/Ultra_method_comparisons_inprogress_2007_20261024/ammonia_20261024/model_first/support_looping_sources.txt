Primary-source support: looping hydride/imide/nitride comparators
===============================================================

Scope
-----
This is a bounded acquisition note for the model-first A/B nitrogen-transfer
construction. It adds experimentally demonstrated carrier loops and their
regeneration boundaries; it does not rate the proposed construction or make a
novelty claim. The directly assigned 2017 Wang et al. Nature Chemistry
TM(N)-to-LiH comparator is covered by the other source owner and is not
repeated here.

1. Mn-nitride / alkaline-earth-imide composite loop (closest paired carrier)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
S. Feng et al., “A multi-functional composite nitrogen carrier for ammonia
production via a chemical looping route,” J. Mater. Chem. A 9 (2021), 1039,
DOI 10.1039/D0TA10519H. Publisher primary page and its listed SI:
https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2021/ta/d0ta10519h

The reported carrier is a composite of Mn nitrides and Li2NH or BaNH. The
authors assign a two-step loop: N2 fixation by N-poor Mn nitride together with
the alkali/alkaline-earth hydride; then hydrogenation of N-rich Mn nitride and
the imide to NH3. Mn nitride is described as both nitrogen carrier and
kinetic promoter, while the hydride also promotes the Mn-nitride steps. The
abstract reports ambient-pressure operation below 300 °C and says Mn2N–BaNH
gives an NH3 rate over ten times that of neat BaNH and neat Mn2N. The SI
index/search excerpt identifies a Mn–BaH cyclic test at 275 °C and 1 bar under
N2/H2. I did not acquire the complete SI experimental section here, so do not
infer an exact rate, cycle count, time-on-stream, or full product/material
mass closure from those excerpts.

For formula bookkeeping only, the simple BaH2/BaNH inventory can be balanced
as 2 BaH2 + N2 -> 2 BaNH + H2, followed by
2 BaNH + 4 H2 -> 2 BaH2 + 2 NH3. These are net stoichiometric balances, not
assertions that the composite follows only these elementary steps. The first
step exchanges N for lattice H and releases H2; the second restores BaH2
while producing NH3.

2. Li4NH alternative carrier with measured multicycle phase drift
------------------------------------------------------------------
M. Ravi and J. W. Makepeace, “Lithium–nitrogen–hydrogen systems for ammonia
synthesis: exploring a more efficient pathway using lithium nitride–hydride,”
Chem. Commun. 58 (2022), 6076–6079, DOI 10.1039/D2CC01345B. Open primary
article, including its SI link and data-availability statement:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9121210/
Released supporting data: https://doi.org/10.25500/edata.bham.00000827

This paper reports a different loop than the familiar LiH/Li2NH couple. The
authors print these idealized N2-fixation equations:

  4 LiH + N2 -> 2 Li2NH + 2 H2  [as printed]
  3 Li4NH + N2 -> 3 Li2NH + 2 Li3N.

The first printed equation is not hydrogen-balanced: its left side has four
H atoms and its right side has six. Formula-level balance gives
4 LiH + N2 -> 2 Li2NH + H2. This correction is elementary atom bookkeeping,
not a claim about the observed multiphase pathway. Their N2-TPR uptake
protocol (5 °C/min to 400 °C, then 20 min hold)
reported 6.7 µmol N mg−1 for Li4NH and 0.2 for LiH; ball-milled Co increased
the measured uptakes to 10.4 and 3.3 µmol N mg−1, respectively. These are
uptake measurements, not NH3 production rates. In a separate looping protocol
the authors used 60 sccm N2 then 75 sccm H2 at 1 barg and 450 °C; Co/Li4NH
showed more stable cycling over ten cycles than Co/LiH. Crucially, cycling
does not restore a single fixed Li4NH phase: the paper identifies
composition-dependent Li–N–H solid solutions, with spent Co/Li4NH showing no
LiH after ten cycles. Thus the demonstrated regeneration is within a changing
nitride–imide–hydride composition window, not exact recreation of the initial
Li4NH inventory on every pass.

The idealized LiH/Li2NH bookkeeping is 4 LiH + N2 -> 2 Li2NH + H2 and
2 Li2NH + 4 H2 -> 4 LiH + 2 NH3, net N2 + 3 H2 -> 2 NH3. These are not the
elementary steps of the Co/Li4NH loop, nor the Zn-promoted pathway below.

3. Zn-promoted LiH/Li2NH loop: changed carrier identity, faster N2 fixation
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R. Wang et al., “Zn Promotes Chemical Looping Ammonia Synthesis Mediated by
LiH–Li2NH Couple,” ChemSusChem 16 (2023), e202300813,
DOI 10.1002/cssc.202300813. Primary abstract:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37461801/

The abstract reports Zn addition changes the reaction pathway and forms
Li2NH, lithium-zinc intermetallics LixZny, and LiZnN; LiZnN together with
Li2NH is assigned as the nitrogen-carrier inventory. It reports an apparent
N2-fixation activation-energy decrease from 102 to 50 kJ mol−1 and
956 µmol NH3 g−1 h−1 at 350 °C, 19 times the neat LiH–Li2NH loop rate. The
retrieved primary abstract does not provide the exact time-resolved cycling
protocol, regeneration count, or a closed carrier mass balance. Do not reduce
this Zn-containing chemistry to the neat LiH/Li2NH net equations above.

4. Calcium nitride hydrogenation is a release half-cycle, not a demonstrated
   CaH2 loop
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
R. Michalsky et al., “Chemical looping of metal nitride catalysts:
low-pressure ammonia synthesis for energy storage,” Chem. Sci. 6 (2015),
3965–3974, DOI 10.1039/C5SC00789E. Primary article:
https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2015/sc/c5sc00789e
Full text is also indexed at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5707470/.

The paper reports H2-driven NH3 evolution from pre-nitrided Ca3N2 (and
Sr2N), with NH3 yield corrected for partial hydrolysis. Ca3N2 produces up to
12 mol% NH3 under the tested conditions, with delayed onset; the authors
identify Ca2NH as the principal solid product and report an apparent
activation energy of 42 ± 7 kJ mol−1 for the Ca3N2 hydrogenation sequence.
This demonstrates a hydrogenation/release half-cycle for preloaded nitride,
not an experimental Ca3N2/CaH2 nitrogen-loading and regeneration cycle. CaH2
appears in the paper's thermochemical/SI comparisons, which should not be
presented as observed CaH2 turnover.

A related but materially different comparator is F. Chang et al., “Alkali
and Alkaline Earth Hydrides-Driven N2 Activation and Transformation over Mn
Nitride Catalyst,” JACS 140 (2018), 14799–14806,
DOI 10.1021/jacs.8b08334; primary abstract:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30351925/. It screens CaH2 among hydride
promoters in Mn-nitride catalytic N2 conversion, but is a syngas-fed catalytic
system, not a separately operated CaH2/CaNH looping cycle. I acquired only
the abstract; quantitative cycle conditions are not asserted here.

5. Molecular Mo nitride photorecycling: explicit batch return, exact reset
   ledger not acquired
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
J. Kim et al., “Photodriven Ammonia Synthesis from N2 and H2: Recycling of a
Molecular Molybdenum Nitride,” J. Am. Chem. Soc. 147 (2025), 8215–8226,
DOI 10.1021/jacs.4c14966. Primary ACS abstract and SI listing:
https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jacs.4c14966

The abstract says visible-light irradiation of an N2-cleavage-derived Mo
nitride with an iridium photocatalyst under 1–4 atm H2 forms NH3 and cationic
Mo(VI) pentahydride as the major Mo product. Continued irradiation under N2
regenerates Mo nitride, which is reused for additional hydrogenation; the
authors call this superstoichiometric batch synthesis using N2 and H2 under
ambient conditions. This is the clearest acquired molecular example of
nitride-to-hydride hydrogenation followed by N2-driven catalyst return, but
the named inputs still include photons and an Ir photocatalyst. The publisher
lists an SI section “Photochemical Recovery of Mo1 and Super-stoichiometric
Ammonia Synthesis Cycle” beginning at S20, and lists 440 nm and 390 nm lamps
among methods; I did not retrieve the S20 experimental details. Consequently
the exact MoH5-to-MoN stoichiometry, number of repeat batches, measured
superstoichiometric yield/TON, and light/Ir conditions specific to recovery
remain UNKNOWN in this intake. Do not translate the abstract's qualitative
claim into a numeric turnover.

For reset context, the earlier same-group primary report—S. Kim et al.,
“Ammonia synthesis by photocatalytic hydrogenation of a N2-derived molybdenum
nitride,” Nat. Synth. 1 (2022), 297–303, DOI 10.1038/s44160-022-00044-1—gives
an explicit reagent-bearing three-step return. MoH5-derived cation is first
deprotonated with DBU to neutral Mo hydride 6; 6 is irradiated at 350 nm for
8 h under 1 atm N2 to form Mo dinitrogen complex 5 in 73% yield; then 5 is
oxidized by ferrocenium [Fc][BArF4] to regenerate the N2-derived nitride.
The 2022 paper explicitly identifies this as a three-step synthetic cycle.
This reset uses DBU, UV light, N2, and a ferrocenium oxidant in the
nitride-forming step, so it is not a reagent-free hydride-to-nitride return.
It is a distinct prior route and does not establish the exact 2025 direct
continued-irradiation recovery conditions. Primary article and author
manuscript: https://www.nature.com/articles/s44160-022-00044-1 and
https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1987945.

Comparison boundary
-------------------
These sources establish that N storage, transfer/hydrogenation, and carrier
return are experimentally accessible in several materially different
architectures: a Mn-nitride/alkaline imide composite; Li–N–H solid-solution
loops with Co or Zn pathway modification; hydrogenation of preformed Ca
nitride; and molecular Mo nitride photorecycling. They do not supply one
shared measured elementary A-to-B transfer rate, common conditions, energy
ledger, or proof that any proposed A/B pair in the model-first construction
works. In particular, “H2-only” in a nitride paper may describe only the
hydrogenation half-cycle. The complete stoichiometric loop must separately
replenish lattice nitrogen from N2 and account for all hydride/imide/nitride
composition changes and any non-H2 regeneration reagents.
