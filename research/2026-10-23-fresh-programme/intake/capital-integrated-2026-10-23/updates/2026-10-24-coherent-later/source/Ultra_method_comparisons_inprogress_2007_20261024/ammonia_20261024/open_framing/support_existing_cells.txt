# Existing H2/NOx-to-ammonia cells: bounded primary-source check

Scope: three primary papers, read for the literal reactants, operating point, and carrier/separator boundary. The strongest direct H2-powered result is nitrate reduction paired with H2 oxidation; it is an existence result for the electrochemical arm, not a demonstration of atmospheric N2 fixation or a closed end-to-end nitrogen process.

## Strongest direct match: nitrate reduction + hydrogen oxidation

**Zhang et al., “Exploiting underpotential deposited hydrogen enables energy-efficient nitrate electroreduction to ammonia,” Nature Communications 17, 4652 (2026), [DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-71299-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71299-x).**

- The paired cell uses AgRu-15 for cathodic NO3RR and commercial Pt/C for anodic HOR in a two-electrode, membrane-free flow cell at 25 °C. The authors describe it as “paired alkaline electrolysis.” The stability test uses 1 M KOH + 100 mM KNO3 (reported pH 13.96), 6 mL/min electrolyte flow, and supplied H2 at 10 sccm. At 200 mA cm−2, the paper reports stable NH3 production for >100 h and full-cell voltages below zero. The text does not give the numerical negative voltage or an NH3 FE measured at that exact paired-cell operating point; 81% FE is explicitly the techno-economic model assumption. Thus the evidence supports a reported net-output operating point by the paper’s voltage convention, while the exact power and full-cell selectivity at that point remain unspecified in prose. At 0.5–1 M nitrate, the cell reaches 2.0 A cm−2 at +0.75 V, an energy-consuming operating point, not fuel-cell output.
- Separate half-cell measurements in 1 M KOH show 100% NH3 FE at 0 V and 2.2 A cm−2 NH3 partial current at 1 M nitrate; the 53.7% NH3 energy efficiency is a half-cell result at 0.2 V vs RHE. These should not be transferred to the paired HOR cell as if measured there.
- No sacrificial metal anode or imposed acid/base gradient is present: H2 is the supplied anodic reductant, and the cell is membrane-free in alkaline electrolyte. However, the 100 h test renews the electrolyte nine times (every 11–12 h); the paper does not demonstrate a closed electrolyte / hydroxide-management loop, H2 production, or capture and regeneration of the nitrate feed. The nitrate is an input feedstock, not N2 fixed from air. The cost model assumes H2 from natural gas without CCUS (with an alternative scenario also discussed), so “green H2” is not established by that analysis.
- Primary article sections “Paired NO3RR-HOR systems” and Methods, plus Fig. 6: [Nature Communications full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-71299-x). The cell geometry, pH, flow, and renewal schedule are in Methods; the below-zero voltage, 200 mA cm−2 point and 2 A cm−2 / 0.75 V contrast are in the paired-cell results.

## Earlier H2/NO demonstration: open-circuit voltage without useful NH3-generating output

**Bunea, Coppens & Urakawa, “Electrochemical Conversion of NO to NH3 in a PEM Cell,” ACS Catalysis 13 (2023), 11345–11351, [DOI 10.1021/acscatal.3c01600](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.3c01600).**

- This is a gas-phase NO-reduction PEM cell with Ru/C cathode, Pt/C + H2 at the anode, and Nafion proton-conducting membrane (about 80 °C). The high-feed experiments use 4.8% NO/He; Fig. 7 identifies 50 mL/min H2. A PEM proton conductor is the relevant acidic carrier; a bulk electrolyte pH is not specified for this gas-fed MEA.
- With HOR, the paper gives an OCP of about 0.85 V in the introduction but about 0.6 V in Results (an internal reported-value discrepancy). The authors say reduction products were not detected in the cited 0–0.6 V operating range and current was very low; they then applied cathode potentials more negative than 0 V vs pseudo-RHE to drive conversion. Under those biased HOR-anode tests, NH3 FE peaks at 30% at −0.4 V vs pseudo-RHE, NO conversion at about 30% at −1 V, and NH3 selectivity at about 85% at −0.8 V. These are cathode-potential values, not a net-power full-cell operating voltage. Hence this paper establishes an H2-fed HOR architecture and open-circuit potential, but not demonstrated simultaneous NH3 production and electrical output at a useful load.
- The 97% NO conversion / 93% NH3 FE / 0.51 mmol cm−2 h−1 headline result is instead under water oxidation at the anode and 1.9 V cell voltage, i.e. an electrolyzer input. The authors also note H2 crossover through Nafion can alter cathode product FE (estimated crossover contribution up to 6% FE for H2 product), and flag gas humidification/flooding as unresolved operating factors.
- Primary article full text/PDF hosted by TU Delft: [ACS Catalysis paper PDF](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/158988899/acscatal.3c01600.pdf). See the HOR discussion and Fig. 7; compare with the water-oxidation abstract/result.

## High-power nitrate cell that is not an H2-powered closed-carrier example

**Zhang et al., “Electrochemical nitrate reduction in acid enables high-efficiency ammonia synthesis and high-voltage pollutes-based fuel cells,” Nature Communications 14, 8036 (2023), [DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-43897-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-43897-6).**

- The prominent 1.99 V OCV / 91.4 mW cm−2 result is an alkaline–acid hybrid Zn–NO3 battery: FePc/TiO2 cathode in 15 mL 0.1 M HNO3 + 0.4 M KNO3 (pH about 1), Zn-plate anode in 15 mL 6 M KOH, with a bipolar membrane. It gives peak NH3 FE 88.2% at 0.7 V and 12.3 mg h−1 cm−2 NH3 at 0.4 V; the text reports discharge up to 50 mA cm−2 and 10 h stability with FE falling from 84.7% to 81.8%. The source explicitly attributes the high OCV to both Zn oxidation and the acid/base pH difference across the bipolar membrane. The voltage is therefore not evidence for H2 as fuel or for a regenerated pH carrier.
- The same paper’s separately proposed/tested “fuel cell” replaces Zn with 0.3 M hydrazine in 1 M KOH and Pt/C HOR-like hydrazine oxidation; it reports 0.75 V at 1 mA cm−2, 11.5 mW cm−2 peak power, and roughly 80% cathodic NH3 FE. That is a hydrazine/nitrate pollutant cell, not a hydrogen/nitrate cell.
- The paper is useful as a warning against reading high voltage as a closed H2/nitrate cycle. Full text and Methods: [open-access PMC primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10698138/).

## Bounded conclusion

The 2026 AgRu-15/Pt-C study is the strongest found direct precedent for nitrate-to-ammonia paired with H2 oxidation and a reported negative full-cell voltage while NH3 is being produced. Its credible claim is specific to the reported point and its supplied nitrate/H2/alkaline feed boundary. It does not establish atmospheric nitrogen fixation, complete electrolyte/carrier regeneration, or a costed green-H2 process. The 2023 PEM NO study is an important architectural predecessor but its NH3-producing HOR operation is driven into cathodic bias; the 2023 high-power nitrate battery relies on sacrificial Zn and a maintained acid/base split.
