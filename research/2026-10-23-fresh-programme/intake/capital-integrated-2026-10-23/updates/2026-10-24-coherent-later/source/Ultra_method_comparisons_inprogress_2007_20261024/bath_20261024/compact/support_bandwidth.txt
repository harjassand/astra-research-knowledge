FINITE TEMPORAL SUPPORT: EXACT GAUSSIAN GATE

Scope. This is a conditional calculation for the compact arm's equal-hot, product-Gaussian bath model. It separates a true discarded bath subspace from a filter whose output is recorded. It does not establish that either native mechanical output is physically collectable. The only context read for this note was the compact arm's NEXT_CONSTRUCTION, COMPACT_HANDOFF, NEXT_QUESTION, and RETAINED_PRECOMPARISON_PROOF files; no new primary source was opened.

1. Wiener/Schur-complement form

Stack both native ports and all temporal modes into a real Gaussian vector R with mean zero and covariance Sigma. Choose the homodyne axes as in the opening's aligned Weyl identity, so the measured record distribution is independent of the spin branch and the relevant bath phase commutes with the recorded quadratures. For a fixed spin coherence let that phase be X = c^T R (so c includes the coherence difference and displacement scale). The available record is Y = L R + epsilon, where epsilon is independent Gaussian readout noise with covariance N. A linear local feedback phase g^T Y leaves X - g^T Y. Set

    A = c^T Sigma c,   b = L Sigma c,   S = L Sigma L^T + N.

Then its variance is A - 2 g^T b + g^T S g. The minimum is attained at g = S^dagger b (when b is in range(S)), and

    V_res = c^T [Sigma - Sigma L^T (L Sigma L^T + N)^dagger L Sigma] c.     (1)

The bracket is the conditional-covariance Schur complement. For a Gaussian bath, conditioning on Y leaves a Gaussian residual of this variance, independent of the record after its conditional mean is removed. Thus the optimally corrected coherence has visibility exp(-V_res/2), apart from its deterministic phase. Equation (1), rather than a count of detector channels, is the exact finite-mode criterion. Singular S is handled by the Moore-Penrose inverse; any component of b outside range(S) would signal an inconsistent noiseless observation model.

For equal-temperature independent bath modes, Sigma = nu I, nu = n + 1/2. If capture and homodyne select a nested orthogonal mode subspace M (M is the actually measured subspace inside the captured subspace C), then N=0 and (1) reduces to

    V_res = nu (||c||^2 - ||P_M c||^2).

For the displacement convention in the opening, c is the symplectic rotation of the displacement difference; isotropy makes its norm and captured fraction the same as those of v. If H is a homodyne subspace contained in C, P_M=P_H P_C. If C and H are not nested, P_H P_C need not be a projection: use the actual linear measurement map L in (1), or the projection onto the measured subspace, rather than calling P_H P_C an orthogonal projector. This is the precise content of the proposed nu(||v||^2-||P_H P_C v||^2) residual.

For the one-mode hot-dark example Y = sqrt(f) X + sqrt(1-f) D, with target phase qX, Var(X)=V_B and Var(D)=V_D, (1) gives

    V_res = q^2 V_B (1-f) V_D / [f V_B + (1-f) V_D].                 (2)

Thus V_D=V_B=nu gives q^2 nu(1-f): partial collection of one of two independent native hot ports leaves an O(n) residual. For downstream vacuum admixture V_D=1/2, exact Wiener feedback gives q^2 V_B(1-f)/[2 f V_B + (1-f)]. This is bounded by q^2(1-f)/(2f) for f>0 and tends to that constant at large V_B. The simpler signal-normalized gain q/sqrt(f) also gives the exact upper-bound residual q^2(1-f)/(2f), independent of V_B. This vacuum-loss comparison relies on the unmeasured quadrature being vacuum and on the common hot component being correlated with the observed record; it does not apply to an independent hot dark input.

2. Hard bandwidth cutoff: an irreducible hot component

Take the temporal-frequency mode space L^2([-B,B]), normalize v(omega)=1/sqrt(2B), and suppose all of C is physically captured but the homodyne chain records only H=L^2([-B/2,B/2]). Then ||v||^2=1, ||P_H v||^2=1/2, so

    V_res = q^2 nu/2.

The missing half of the displacement lies in ker(L); no offline filter or later feedforward can infer it. For n growing, its visibility penalty exp(-q^2 nu/4) tends to zero. This example is a hard inaccessible hot-frequency band, not ordinary inefficiency from vacuum injected after capture. The same conclusion holds for any positive-measure spectral set on which the physical capture/measurement transfer is exactly zero and v has nonzero energy.

3. Recorded invertible filter: recoverable in principle, noisy in practice

For each frequency let the recorded mode be Y(omega)=G(omega)X(omega)+epsilon(omega), with hot variance nu and independent readout-noise spectrum N(omega). The frequencywise form of (1) is

    V_res = q^2 integral |v(omega)|^2 nu N(omega) /
                         (|G(omega)|^2 nu + N(omega)) d omega.       (3)

If N=0 and G is nonzero on the support of v with a bounded inverse there, deferred offline deconvolution recovers the mode exactly: V_res=0. Example: compactly supported v on |omega|<=B and G=exp[-(tau omega)^2/2] has inverse norm at most exp[(tau B)^2/2]. This can be poorly conditioned but is finite on that support. With readout noise, (3) quantifies the residual; frequencies with |G|^2 nu << N are effectively unavailable. A zero of G on a positive-measure part of v-support restores the hard-cutoff residual on that part. Noncausal offline inversion is allowed in this statement; a real-time latency/causality constraint would be an additional restriction.

Decision rule: first establish whether the physical transfer has a nullspace on the hot displacement support. A nullspace gives a strict thermal residual even with ideal later processing. If it is injective, distinguish mathematical recoverability (bounded inverse and N=0) from stable finite-SNR recovery (use (1)/(3)); do not treat small nonzero gain as either free precision or an exactly missing mode. Native port access, spectral covariance, transfer function, and readout-noise spectrum remain unacquired inputs.

ADDENDUM: SHAPED HOMODYNE AND POST-DETECTION BANDWIDTH

The hard-cutoff example above applies when the quantum output mode is not captured or the measurement map has an actual null on its support. It must not be applied automatically to a finite-bandwidth electronics filter after a homodyne measurement.

Let the field quadratures obey [Q(t),P(t')]=i delta(t-t'). For the desired own-spin phase generator

    X_v = integral [v_Q(t) P(t) - v_P(t) Q(t)] dt,

use a shaped local oscillator with real amplitude a(t)=sqrt(v_Q(t)^2+v_P(t)^2) and phase chosen so cos(phi(t))=-v_P(t)/a(t), sin(phi(t))=v_Q(t)/a(t). In the convention I_0(t)=a(t)[Q(t)cos(phi(t))+P(t)sin(phi(t))], integrating the balanced current measures X_v directly. Equivalently the complex LO envelope is proportional to -v_P(t)+i v_Q(t). It selects this single temporal quadrature; it does not create access to a native output port that was never collected. The required LO shaping and photodetector quantum response must cover the mode's physical bandwidth.

Now put a stable linear electronics impulse response h after the balanced photocurrent: I_h=h*I_0. If h is integrable, I_0 has finite area, and the complete response tail is retained, Fubini gives

    integral I_h(t)dt = (integral h(t)dt)(integral I_0(t)dt) = H(0) X_v.

For H(0) nonzero, division by H(0) recovers the selected quadrature exactly in the noiseless model. A causal low-pass can delay and spread the response without deleting this area. Operationally, integrate a finite gate covering the LO pulse and the filter tail, or use a matched filter; an infinite all-time integral of a stationary electronics-noise baseline is not a finite-variance estimator. A finite gate leaves a tail error, and added electronics noise remains; both must be included in the actual estimator variance.

Concrete recoverable filter: h(t)=B_e exp(-B_e t) 1_{t>=0} has finite 3-dB bandwidth B_e and H(0)=1. For any compactly supported raw homodyne-current pulse, its filtered response has the same total area; recording through the exponential tail and integrating recovers X_v. Thus a slow electronics low-pass alone is a delay/tail and noise cost, not the projection loss in Section 2.

An AC-coupled filter with H(0)=0 makes this full-area statistic unusable, but does not by itself prove that the mode is inaccessible if the full time trace is retained. For a known deterministic record template s(t), the filtered template is s_h=h*s; its amplitude is identifiable when s_h is nonzero and the noise-weighted matched-filter norm is finite. For the stochastic bath quadrature, use the full observation map L and covariance in (1). In the ideal equal-hot, noiseless Hilbert-space case its residual is nu times the squared norm of the target component in ker(L). Exact loss therefore requires a genuine null component, not just a small gain. For an L2 temporal mode, a single zero H(0)=0 at one frequency point has measure zero and is not by itself a missing mode; it can still make inversion ill-conditioned near DC, and finite records can turn the DC bin into an actual lost mode. A hard missing band of positive measure is a true nullspace and leaves residual nu times the mode energy in that band. Thus H(0)=0 is a no-go for area-only readout, while a hard missing quantum-frequency band is a no-go for the corresponding mode component. A photodiode's finite quantum efficiency/bandwidth, a detector transfer zero over a band, finite-window truncation, and electronics noise are separate limits and cannot be collapsed into one "bandwidth" parameter.
