# Partial native capture: conditional instrument and scope of the loss floor

Assume fresh independent, centered, commuting Gaussian variables X,D with variances V_B,V_D. A fixed passive mixer exposes only

    Y = sqrt(f) X + sqrt(1-f) D,      0 <= f <= 1,

and the spin error is U_s=exp(-i q s X). The record is retained, followed by deterministic spin-Z feedback exp(+i g s Y). Let Δs=s-s'. The exact outcome-resolved coherence block is

    rho_ss'(y) = p_Y(y) rho_ss' exp[-i Δs q E(X|Y=y) + i Δs g y]
                          exp[-(Δs)^2 q^2 Var(X|Y)/2].

This follows by conditioning the commuting Gaussian X on Y; it is an instrument statement, not just an unconditional second-moment calculation. Writing M=Var(Y)=f V_B+(1-f)V_D and Cov(X,Y)=sqrt(f)V_B gives

    E(X|Y=y)=sqrt(f)V_B y/M,
    Var(X|Y)=V_B(1-f)V_D/M.

Thus g*=q sqrt(f)V_B/M removes the outcome-dependent phase, while no spin-Z phase choice can increase the conditional coherence modulus beyond exp[-(Δs)^2 q^2 Var(X|Y)/2]. Equivalently, the unconditional channel has multiplier

    Gamma_ss'=exp[-(Δs)^2 Var(qX-gY)/2],
    Var(qX-gY)=q^2 V_B(1-f)V_D/M

at g=g*. For s=±1 the off-diagonal factor is exp[-2 Var(qX-gY)].

For equal hot inputs V_B=V_D=v, M=v and g*=q sqrt(f); the residual is exactly q^2 v(1-f). This is a tight floor for access to the single fixed captured mode Y: any other deterministic feedback only changes the conditional phase, and any alternative homodyne quadrature of that same output has correlation with X reduced by |cos(theta)|, so cannot improve the conditional variance. This conclusion assumes the dark output is unavailable.

For dark vacuum V_D=1/2, the exact optimum at finite V_B is

    g*=q sqrt(f)V_B/[f V_B+(1-f)/2],
    Var_opt=q^2 V_B(1-f)/2/[f V_B+(1-f)/2].

The normalized gain g=q/sqrt(f) instead cancels X identically for every V_B, leaving qX-gY=−q sqrt((1-f)/f)D and residual q^2(1-f)/(2f), independent of hot X. It is the V_B→∞ optimal-gain limit; for finite V_B it need not be the variance-minimizing gain.

Scope counterexample: if both passive output ports are accessible, apply the inverse mixer and homodyne X directly. Then Y_alt=X, g=q, and the residual is zero. Thus the equal-temperature (1−f)v q² floor is not a universal passive-measurement limit; it is the limit for one fixed partially captured port (or an equivalent access restriction). Even selecting the dark output alone gives residual f v q², which beats the bright-port floor when f<1/2. Access to both commuting output Q quadratures lets one reconstruct X exactly; simultaneous noncommuting Q/P measurement is not needed to beat the floor.

The product/fresh-bath premise matters: if X,D are correlated with the spin initially, the posterior law P(X|Y,s) can depend on s and the displayed scalar dephasing multiplier does not follow from the marginal variances alone. The conditional Kraus/instrument then acts on spin-bath coherences; an initial independent product bath is the clean sufficient assumption. No physical hardware claim is made.
