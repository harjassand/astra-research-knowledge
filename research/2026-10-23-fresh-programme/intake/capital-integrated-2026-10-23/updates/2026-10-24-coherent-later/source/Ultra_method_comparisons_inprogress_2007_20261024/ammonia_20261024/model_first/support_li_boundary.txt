What the Zhou ~28% figure means
-------------------------------
The abstract calls the Li-mediated process's theoretical maximum energy efficiency “∼28%.” The paper's main text attributes the limit to the very negative cathode potential needed to electrodeposit Li metal: below about −3.0 V vs SHE (the parenthetical −2.9 V vs SHE refers to Ca in the Li/Ca phrase). The definition is in SI p. 2, not displayed in the main text.

Transcribed SI Eq. (S1), with its voltage symbols retained:

  EE_NH3,tot = [(V0_NH3syn + V0_H2syn) /
      ((V0_H2syn + eta_H2syn)/FE_H2
       + (V0_NH3syn + eta_metal + eta_NH3syn))] FE_NH3.

The SI then sets eta_H2syn = eta_NH3syn = 0 and FE_H2 = FE_NH3 = 1. It assigns V0_H2syn = 1.23 V to water oxidation and V0_NH3syn = −0.06 V to nitrogen reduction to ammonia, giving

  EE_NH3 = 1.17 / [1.23 + (−0.06 + eta_metal)].

For Li, use about 3.0 V as the *positive magnitude of the cathodic Li-deposition voltage cost*, consistent with the main text's roughly −3.0 V vs SHE. The denominator is 1.23 − 0.06 + 3.0 = 4.17 V and EE = 1.17/4.17 = 28.1%, reproducing the paper's approximate number. The SI calls eta_metal the metals' standard reduction potential but does not spell out the sign convention in words; inserting the signed negative Li potential into its additive denominator would be inconsistent with both the equation's energy-cost role and the quoted result. Treat eta_metal here as a positive voltage cost/magnitude.

Interpretation: the numerator is the reversible free-energy equivalent for the net water-to-ammonia chemistry, 1.17 V per electron equivalent (6 F per mol N2 for N2 + 3 H2O → 2 NH3 + 3/2 O2); this reaction balance is written here to make the boundary explicit. The denominator includes ideal water-to-H2 energy, Li deposition work, and N2-to-NH3 reduction voltage. The ideal estimate sets the water-oxidation and N2-reduction overpotentials to zero and both relevant Faradaic efficiencies to 100%; Li plating remains the large unavoidable voltage term in this particular mediator route. It is a conditional Li-mediated electrochemical energy estimate, not a universal ceiling for every eNRR chemistry.

The word “total” in the SI equation should not be mistaken for a plant-gate life-cycle or full process assessment. The equation does not, as written, add air separation/N2 compression, hydrogen drying/compression, solvent and proton-carrier manufacture/recovery, electrolyte replacement, ammonia recovery/separation, pumps, auxiliaries, heat, or capital. Nor does it establish that practical operation can attain its ideal FE and zero-overpotential assumptions. The paper's own argument is that a different stable catalyst with a less negative potential could improve efficiency, subject to its N2 dissociation, NH3 desorption, SEI/proton-transport, and water-tolerance requirements.

Why H2-oxidation cells need a boundary correction
------------------------------------------------
The 2024 Li et al. abstract explicitly describes continuous Li-mediated N2 reduction coupled to hydrogen oxidation (HOR). In that cell, H2 is an input chemical-energy carrier; OER and in-cell H2 generation are not the anode step. A reported cell efficiency based only on the HOR-coupled electrolyser therefore cannot be read as the water-to-NH3 efficiency in Zhou's Eq. (S1). For a common N2 + water boundary, charge the actual upstream H2-production energy and its conditioning, plus the other process terms, rather than treating fed H2 as free. Conversely, the ideal 1.23 V H2-synthesis term in Zhou's formula is a thermodynamic minimum, not the actual consumption of a practical H2 plant. The exact 2024 primary abstract gives current-to-ammonia FE, not an energy efficiency or cell voltage; I do not infer either from its FE.

Best honest comparison among the supplied current reports
---------------------------------------------------------
- Zhou et al. (2025): conditional ideal Li-route ceiling ≈27.8% on the above voltage boundary; not a measured device result.
- Li et al. (2024): 300 h continuous flow operation, 25 cm2 electrode, 1 bar and room temperature; 64 ± 1% current-to-ammonia efficiency and approximately 98% gas-phase ammonia content/distribution. These are important durability, FE, and product-phase results for an HOR-coupled Li-mediated cell. The 64% is Faradaic/current efficiency, not energy efficiency; “~98% gas-phase” is not a claim of 98% isolated product purity. The abstract does not give a cell voltage or energy-efficiency definition.
- Zhang et al. (2026): the primary abstract reports 98% Faradaic efficiency and 21% energy efficiency at 100 mA cm−2 in 2 M lithium difluoro(oxalato)borate; it also says 80% FE was sustained over 40 h, after which performance declined. This is the strongest explicit efficiency/current-density combination in the two supplied 2024/2026 abstracts, while the 2024 result is stronger on the stated 300 h operating duration and 25 cm2 area. The 2026 abstract alone does not expose the EE formula/system boundary, so 21% cannot be normalized against Zhou's 27.8% without full methods/SI. If, and only if, the definitions match, 21/27.8 ≈ 0.76 of that ideal ceiling; that ratio is conditional arithmetic, not a validated head-to-head result.

Thus there is no single honest scalar ranking: FE, energy efficiency, current density, run duration, area, and output handling are different metrics. None of these abstract-level results establishes full renewable water-to-ammonia performance including H2 production, feed purification, reagent regeneration, product separation, and balance-of-plant. The most direct numerical performance anchor among the supplied recent abstracts is the 2026 source-reported 21% EE at 100 mA cm−2, explicitly marked as method-specific and not independently reproduced here, alongside the 2024 long-run cell result; neither removes the need for a matched full-boundary comparator.

Conventional-route context in the Zhou article is also conditional: the main text cites the DOE REFUEL 60% target and says Haber–Bosch can operate around 75%, and it quotes cost estimates of $750/t NH3 for a single-stage electrochemical system versus $159/t for Haber–Bosch at 2,000 t/day, while suggesting a possible electrochemical cost advantage at 30 kg/day. Those are cited targets/estimates, not a same-boundary measurement or independent contemporary plant comparison in this audit. The fair system benchmark remains renewable water electrolysis + nitrogen separation + modern Haber–Bosch, with heat integration, H2 buffering, flexibility, recycle, and NH3 conditioning all included on both sides.
