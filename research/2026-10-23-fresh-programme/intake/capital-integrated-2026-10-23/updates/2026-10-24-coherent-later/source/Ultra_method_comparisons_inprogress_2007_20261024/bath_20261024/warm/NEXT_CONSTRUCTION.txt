Native operation and access contract

Their output waveguides are distinct. Only one hybrid output is measured; the other can remain hot and be discarded. If a native waveguide end is not physically routed to the collector, no unitary acting on already captured modes is allowed to rotate that absent output into the detector.

The first actual collector to construct is the bright-port hybrid

The retarded/feedback phase must be derived using gamma=gamma_1+gamma_2, not changed by the passive hybrid.

First decisive calculation: hot rank versus detector loss
For one self-spin displacement q_0 s times a real native-port vector c, let the common momentum covariance be V, and let the captured no-knowledge homodyne be Y=h^T P+z with Var z=w. Gaussian conditional spin feedback gives the exact minimum residual self-phase variance

This follows from the actual homodyne instrument/conditional characteristic, not an assumed measured force. In the equal-hot case V=v I, an unmeasured geometric component of c leaves v times its squared norm. No processing confined to a fixed captured subspace can remove that O(v) record.

In contrast, for one hot and one cold native direction, V=diag(v,u), c=(a,b), h=(cos theta,sin theta), and cos theta nonzero, the v->infinity residual is
  q_0^2 [u(b-a tan theta)^2+a^2 w/cos(theta)^2].
Hence small nonzero access can remove a single hot factor while leaving a paid cold/vacuum floor; it cannot do so when independent hot directions remain invisible. This is why a downstream vacuum beam splitter and a missing independent thermal output are different physical dilations. The cold covariance u is an explicit preparation resource, not assumed for a native hot dark port.
