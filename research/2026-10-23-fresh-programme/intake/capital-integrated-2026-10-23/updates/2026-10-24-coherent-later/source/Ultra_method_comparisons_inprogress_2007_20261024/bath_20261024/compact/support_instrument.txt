COMPACT-ARM TWO-PROBE HOMODYNE INSTRUMENT
Convention and scope

Use [Q_j,P_k]=i delta_jk, so vacuum variance is 1/2, and
D(q,p)=exp[i(p Q-q P)]. B and D are independent input modes before the
passive splitter. The system displaces B by

  q_s=q a+c b,       p_s=p b,

where a,b in {+1,-1} label the two probe eigenvalues; D is undisplaced by the
system. The measured output and discarded orthogonal output are

  C=sqrt(f) B+sqrt(1-f) D,
  E=sqrt(1-f) B-sqrt(f) D.

Thus the B displacement has C,E coordinates sqrt(f)(q_s,p_s) and
sqrt(1-f)(q_s,p_s). The input states are centered, independent, isotropic
Gaussian thermal states with quadrature variances V_B,V_D. The detector is a
pure-loss homodyne of C with efficiency eta>0. Define the normalized record
y=y_raw/sqrt(eta), where y_raw=sqrt(eta) P_C+sqrt(1-eta) P_v and P_v is an
independent vacuum quadrature. Its added variance is

  nu_eta=(1-eta)/(2 eta).

Exact outcome-resolved instrument

For s=(a,b), s'=(a',b'), let Delta a=a-a', Delta q=q_s-q_s',
Delta p=p_s-p_s', and define

  W=f V_B+(1-f)V_D+nu_eta,
  C_ss'=exp[-V_B(Delta q^2+Delta p^2)/2
             + i(q_s' p_s-p_s' q_s)/2],
  L_ss'=sqrt(f) V_B Delta q+i sqrt(f)(p_s+p_s')/2.

Before feedback, the instrument density multiplying |s><s'| is

  I_y^0(s,s')=C_ss'/sqrt(2 pi W) * exp[(L_ss'-i y)^2/(2W)].

The expression can be complex for s != s'. On the diagonal it is the
ordinary outcome density N(sqrt(f) p_s,W). Applying the local feedback
U_fb(y)=exp(i g a y) multiplies this density by exp[i g Delta a y]. Thus

  I_y^g(s,s')=exp(i g Delta a y) I_y^0(s,s').

One derivation is to Fourier transform in y. The lossy POVM obeys
integral dy exp(i k y) E_y=exp(-nu_eta k^2/2) exp(i k P_C). Moving the Weyl
operators around the trace gives

  integral dy exp(i k y) I_y^0(s,s')
    = C_ss' exp[-W k^2/2+k L_ss'].

Fourier inversion gives the displayed Gaussian instrument. This uses the
product thermal characteristic functions Tr[rho D(q,p)]
=exp[-V(q^2+p^2)/2]; no independent-output approximation is made after the
splitter.

After integrating over y, the exact spin coherence multiplier is

  M_ss'=C_ss' exp[-W h^2/2+h L_ss'],    h=g Delta a,

or, with its phase and attenuation separated,

  M_ss'=exp(i Phi_ss') * exp(-D_ss'),

  Phi_ss' = (p/2)[sqrt(f) g Delta a (b+b')+q(a'b-a b')],

  D_ss' = (V_B/2){[(q-sqrt(f)g)Delta a+c Delta b]^2
                       +p^2 Delta b^2}
            +(1/2){(1-f)V_D+nu_eta}g^2 Delta a^2,

where Delta b=b-b'. In particular, for arbitrary gain the phase may be
written

  Phi_ss'=(p/2){sqrt(f)g(ab-a'b')
                     +(q-sqrt(f)g)(a'b-a b')}.

The first term is the diagonal ZZ phase coefficient sqrt(f) g p/2. The second
is a residual measurement-induced coherence phase unless sqrt(f)g=q.

Normalized bright cancellation and its limits

For f>0 choose g=q/sqrt(f). Then the phase is exactly

  Phi_ss'=(q p/2)(ab-a'b'),

independent of f, and

  D_ss'=(V_B/2)(c^2+p^2)Delta b^2
       +q^2 Delta a^2/(2f) * [(1-f)V_D+(1-eta)/(2eta)].

The q-induced B contribution cancels. The unobserved input-D quadrature still
appears with coefficient q sqrt((1-f)/f) Delta a; for a hot dark input its
dephasing is V_D q^2(1-f)Delta a^2/(2f). Detector inefficiency adds
(1-eta)q^2 Delta a^2/(4eta f). Hence the bright coherent ZZ phase survives,
but partial collection of a hot native input leaves a thermal residual, and
eta<1 adds a separate vacuum-loss residual. At eta=1 the latter vanishes;
at f=1 the hot-dark residual vanishes. eta=0 has no normalized record and
the stated gain rule is undefined.

For comparison, if the unused D input is downstream vacuum, V_D=1/2, its
residual is q^2(1-f)Delta a^2/(4f), not a hot O(V_B) term. In the physical
two-hot-port case V_D is the native dark-port variance. The gain q/sqrt(f)
is a cancellation gain, not generally the variance-optimal estimator gain.
For c=p=0, minimizing D_ss' over g gives

  g_opt=sqrt(f) q V_B/[f V_B+(1-f)V_D+nu_eta].

This distinction is material when V_D is hot. All formulas above are for the
specified Gaussian two-mode model and normalized homodyne record.

Two-site mass swap, bins, and covariance check

For a second site with the same parameters and gain, swap the probe labels:
its branch is q_2=q b+c a, p_2=p a. Write gamma=sqrt(f)g. The first site's
coherence phase is

  Phi_1=(p/2){gamma(ab-a'b')+(q-gamma)(a'b-a b')}.

After a<->b and a'<->b', the second site's phase is

  Phi_2=(p/2){gamma(ab-a'b')-(q-gamma)(a'b-a b')}.

Therefore Phi_1+Phi_2=gamma p(ab-a'b') exactly. With a raw diagonal phase
theta_raw(ab-a'b'), the total coherent ZZ angle is

  theta_total=theta_raw+sqrt(f) g p.

The cancellation does not require q=gamma. It requires the paired sites to
have the same q,p,f,g and the stated exact label swap. If they differ, the
general sum is

  Phi_1+Phi_2=(p_1 gamma_1+p_2 gamma_2)(ab-a'b')/2
     +(p_1(q_1-gamma_1)-p_2(q_2-gamma_2))(a'b-a b')/2.

So mismatch in the weighted residual coefficients need not cancel.

For independent bath modes/bins indexed by mu, apply this identity termwise:
the residual coherence phase cancels in each matched pair, and the coherent
angle adds,

  theta_total=theta_raw+sum_mu sqrt(f_mu) g_mu p_mu.

In a continuum description the sum becomes the corresponding integral only
after the continuum quadrature and coupling normalization is fixed; for
normalized finite bins the coefficients are bin coefficients and their
products must have a convergent sum. Correlations between bins do not alter
the deterministic Weyl phase cancellation, but their attenuation is a full
quadratic form rather than a sum of single-bin attenuations.

For the classical Gaussian predictor underlying the gain check, set
Y=P_C+noise, Var(noise)=nu_eta. Then

  Var(Y)=W=f V_B+(1-f)V_D+nu_eta,
  Cov(P_B,Y)=sqrt(f)V_B,
  Var(P_B | Y)=V_B-f V_B^2/W.

Thus the minimum residual variance for estimating q P_B by a linear gain is
q^2 Var(P_B | Y), achieved by g_opt=q sqrt(f)V_B/W. This is the Schur
complement for the classical homodyne record; it is outcome-independent for
Gaussian inputs. For a general correlated Gaussian set of bins, use the full
covariance block Schur complement, V_R|Y=V_R-V_RY V_YY^{-1} V_YR, with detector
noise included in V_YY. This changes residual attenuation and can correlate
the residual phase noise across probes/bins, but those residual generators
remain commuting when they are linear combinations of the fixed Z_A,Z_B
couplings. The covariance update does not change the paired Weyl phase
identity above.
