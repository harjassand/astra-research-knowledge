Primary-source note: nitride transfer and hydrogenation loop evidence
====================================================================

Scope
-----
Two directly relevant primary studies: (i) reversible molecular nitride exchange between manganese porphyrins, and (ii) a heterogeneous transition-metal nitride/LiH relay that reports continuous ammonia synthesis and LiH regeneration. The first is only a transfer step; the second is a materially different catalytic-loop incumbent and must not be collapsed into the molecular result.

Source and access provenance
----------------------------
L. K. Woo, J. G. Goll, D. J. Czapla, and J. Hays, “Thermodynamic and Kinetic Aspects of Two- and Three-Electron Redox Processes Mediated by Nitrogen Atom Transfer,” *J. Am. Chem. Soc.* 113 (1991), 8478–8484, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1021/ja00022a040. The publisher landing record supplies the article metadata. Full article text was read from a Paperzz-hosted reproduction of the JACS paper: https://paperzz.com/doc/7953079/thermodynamic-and-kinetic-aspects-of-two--and-three. The latter is a mirror, not the publisher-hosted article; numerical and equation details below are attributed to the reproduced primary article.

Measured reversible transfer reactions
--------------------------------------

1. The paper’s Eq. 9 is a formally three-electron transfer between manganese porphyrins:

   (TTP)Mn≡N + (OEP)Mn ⇌ (TTP)Mn + (OEP)Mn≡N.

   Here TTP and OEP denote the porphyrin ligands used in the paper. The nitrido manganese donor is formally Mn(V); the non-nitrido manganese partner is Mn(II). Products show the nitrogen atom on the OEP complex, with the formal oxidation states exchanged. Spectroscopic/NMR measurements support reversible equilibration. Under anaerobic conditions in toluene at 20 °C, the forward bimolecular rate constant was (5.6 ± 1.2) × 10³ M⁻¹ s⁻¹ and K ≈ 1.2 ± 0.5. Reported reaction thermodynamics were ΔH° = 2.0 ± 0.2 kcal mol⁻¹ and ΔS° = 7.1 ± 0.6 cal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; activation parameters were ΔH‡ = 9.4 ± 0.7 kcal mol⁻¹ and ΔS‡ = −10 ± 2 cal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. This is fast, nearly thermoneutral atom transfer in an anaerobic organic solution, not hydrogenation.

2. The paper’s Eq. 10 uses a chloride-bearing Mn(III) acceptor and couples nitride transfer to chloride transfer:

   (OEP)Mn≡N + (TTP)MnCl ⇌ (OEP)MnCl + (TTP)Mn≡N.

   It is a formal two-electron Mn(V)/Mn(III) redox exchange; the chloride ends up on the original nitrido donor. In CDCl₃, followed by ¹H NMR, the reported 22 °C forward rate was 0.010 ± 0.007 M⁻¹ s⁻¹, with K = 24.3 ± 3.3, ΔH° = −7.0 ± 0.6 kcal mol⁻¹, ΔS° = −17 ± 2 cal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, ΔH‡ = 19 ± 2 kcal mol⁻¹, and ΔS‡ = −3 ± 6 cal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. The study reports starting concentrations in the 7.34 × 10⁻⁴–7.96 × 10⁻³ M range. At 50 °C, k was 0.150 ± 0.012 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ in CDCl₃; in THF k = 0.10 ± 0.02 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ and K ≈ 0.98; in N-methylformamide k = 20 ± 2 M⁻¹ s⁻¹. Thus solvent/ionization strongly affect the rate.

   Mechanistic evidence supports a coordination/redox gate: chloride dissociation precedes association of the cationic Mn(III) complex and a transient μ-nitrido species, followed by nitrogen transfer. Added chloride inhibited the reaction sharply: one equivalent relative to (TTP)MnCl slowed it by about 1,400-fold at 50 °C, and two equivalents by about 2,600-fold. Replacing chloride with iodide accelerated the reaction by about 500-fold at 22 °C; acetate was about 21-fold slower than chloride at 50 °C. These results make ligand lability and solvent part of the actual transfer operation, rather than a freely interchangeable N hand-off.

Why this is not a renewable NH₃ loop
------------------------------------
Both measured transformations keep nitrogen metal-bound as a nitride. Neither produces an N–H bond or NH₃, takes up N₂, nor demonstrates a hydrogenating acceptor B. The apparent reversibility is equilibrium exchange between two prepared metal complexes, with complementary formal oxidation states; reversing it returns the transferred nitrogen to its original metal partner rather than releasing ammonia. The Eq. 10 chemistry also transfers chloride and is strongly chloride-inhibited. The reported work therefore supplies a real intermolecular, redox/coordination-gated N-transfer precedent and quantitative kinetics, but no complete hydrogenation, nitrogen-feed, ligand/metal regeneration, or repeated product-turnover loop. Conditions are anaerobic organic solvents, not an aqueous catalytic process.

Direct heterogeneous relay incumbent: TM(N)–LiH ammonia synthesis
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Wang et al., “Breaking scaling relations to achieve low-temperature ammonia synthesis through LiH-mediated nitrogen transfer and hydrogenation,” *Nature Chemistry* 9 (2017), 64–70, DOI https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.2595. Official Nature record/abstract: https://www.nature.com/articles/nchem.2595; official PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27995914/. Balanced chemistry and proposed relay
-------------------------------------
The article distinguishes transition-metal sites that dissociate N₂ from LiH, which accepts activated nitrogen and hydrogenates it. Its elementary cartoon is schematic: N₂ + 2* → 2N*; N* + LiH → * + [LiNH]; [LiNH] + H₂ → LiH + NH₃. Here [LiNH] collectively denotes lithium imide/amide, so the middle and last expressions are not individually balanced molecular equations. The paper supplies balanced bulk-phase reactions for the Mn and Fe examples:

   2 Mn₂N + (3/2) LiH → Mn₄N + (1/2) LiNH₂ + (1/2) Li₂NH
   Fe₄N + (3/2) LiH → 4 Fe + (1/2) LiNH₂ + (1/2) Li₂NH

The separate alkali-imide/amide hydrogenation sequence is:

   Li₂NH + H₂ → LiNH₂ + LiH
   LiNH₂ + H₂ → NH₃ + LiH

Thus Li₂NH + 2 H₂ → NH₃ + 2 LiH, and LiNH₂ + H₂ → NH₃ + LiH. Combining the stated transfer stoichiometries with these hydrogenation steps gives the exact net reactions shown in the paper:

   2 Mn₂N + (3/2) H₂ → Mn₄N + NH₃
   Fe₄N + (3/2) H₂ → 4 Fe + NH₃.

The LiH is consumed in the N-transfer step and regenerated during hydrogenation; it is a relay reagent/catalytic phase in the proposed cycle, not a net stoichiometric reductant. Net nitrogen replenishment is assigned to N₂ dissociation on the metal/nitride sites. These equations establish an actual material-level chemical loop precedent, unlike the porphyrin exchange above.

What was measured, and under which conditions
----------------------------------------------
- For transfer, preformed Mn₂N or Fe₄N was mixed with LiH and heated under Ar to 548 K. XRD/FTIR of the collected solids showed Mn₄N or metallic Fe and an N–H vibration assigned to Li₂NH. The authors state the neat nitrides do not decompose below 600 K, supporting LiH-mediated reduction/transfer. No pressure is stated for this Ar treatment.
- For nitrogen uptake, metallic Mn–LiH and Fe–LiH mixtures were heated under N₂ to 548 K. XRD/FTIR showed Li₂NH, LiNH₂ and Mn₂N; LiH alone showed no reaction with N₂ at the same condition. Separate tests of Cr–LiH and Co–LiH were reported to show analogous activation/transfer products. The main text says “under a N₂ atmosphere” but does not state a pressure.
- The authors separately report LiNH₂ hydrogenation to NH₃ as low as 373 K, with apparent activation energy about 49 kJ mol⁻¹, and NH₃ formation from the N₂-treated solid on further H₂ treatment. The exact pressure/protocol for those supplementary-figure measurements cannot be recovered here because the SI fetch was blocked.
- Continuous catalysis used Cr–, Mn–, Fe–, and Co–LiH composites at a transition-metal:LiH molar ratio of about 1:5, under 10 bar syngas (N₂:H₂ = 1:3) and WHSV 60,000 ml g⁻¹ h⁻¹. The testing range was 423–600 K; Fe–LiH and Co–LiH gave reported constant rates of about 69 and 56 μmol NH₃ g⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 423 K. At 573 K the overall apparent activation energies were 63.6 ± 2.6 (Cr), 50.6 ± 1.7 (Mn), 46.5 ± 0.7 (Fe), and 52.1 ± 0.8 kJ mol⁻¹ (Co). These are overall-catalyst Arrhenius slopes, not isolated N-transfer barriers or rates.
- At 573 K, reported N₂ reaction orders were 0.43 (Cr), 0.12 (Mn), 0.37 (Fe), 0.48 (Co); H₂ orders were 0.62, 1.1, 0.88, 0.65 respectively. The paper interprets the low N₂ orders and the similarity of overall activation energies to LiNH₂ hydrogenation as evidence that the slow step can shift toward transfer/hydrogenation rather than N₂ dissociation. It explicitly says more surface-science characterization and theoretical modelling are needed for molecular-level mechanism.
- NH₃ production was measured from a continuous-flow fixed bed, with exhaust directed into diluted acid and measured by conductivity. Catalytic rates were reported relatively constant over a ten-hour testing period. XRD of catalysts collected at 598 K showed coexisting low- and higher-nitrogen phases (including Mn₄N with Mn₂N); this is compatible with concurrent activation/transfer but is not a time-resolved phase-cycle trace.

Cycle closure versus direct restoration
---------------------------------------
This paper is the strongest direct incumbent for the requested “nitrogen-affine A → hydrogenating B → regenerate B” idea: it explicitly assigns N transfer from transition-metal N sites/nitrides to LiH, N–H formation on the LiH-derived LiNHₓ phase, NH₃ release, and LiH regeneration. The empirical support is stronger than a thermodynamic proposal alone: separate transfer and uptake phase experiments, LiH-alone control, H₂ treatment, and ten hours of continuous NH₃ production are reported. However, the paper does not report isotope tracing of the transferred N in the accessible main text, nor an operando/quantitative experiment tracking the same nitride and LiH inventories through individual turnovers at the same gas pressure and temperature. The data therefore establish continuous catalytic activity and support the proposed loop, but do not directly time-resolve or mass-close each microscopic shuttle event. No elementary transfer rate constant or transfer activation barrier is measured separately; only overall composite-catalyst and isolated LiNH₂ hydrogenation apparent activation energies are given. The article also does not establish a renewable energy source for H₂ or process-level renewable-energy accounting: its catalytic feed is syngas under 10 bar. That limitation concerns renewable provenance, not whether the reported chemical cycle is catalytic.
