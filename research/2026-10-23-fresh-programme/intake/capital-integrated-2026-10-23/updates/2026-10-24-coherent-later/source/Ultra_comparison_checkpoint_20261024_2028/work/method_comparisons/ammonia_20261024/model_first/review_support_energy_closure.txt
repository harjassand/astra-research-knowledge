Independent support audit: alkaline carrier and energy closure
Date: 2026-10-24

Scope

This is a stoichiometric and thermodynamic closure check of the specified alkaline nitrite/nitrate carrier route and its energy arithmetic. It does not assess catalysts, transfer barriers, plant feasibility, or a complete ammonia construction. The equations below presume the same temperature, pressure, activities, and restored electrolyte/product states wherever a free-energy identity is used.

1. Exact KOH carrier closure and hydrogen stoichiometry

The balanced alkaline half-reactions are

  NO2- + 5 H2O + 6 e- -> NH3 + 7 OH-
  3 H2 + 6 OH- -> 6 H2O + 6 e-

and

  NO3- + 6 H2O + 8 e- -> NH3 + 9 OH-
  4 H2 + 8 OH- -> 8 H2O + 8 e-.

On adding cathode and hydrogen-oxidation anode balances, and including K+ as a spectator, one obtains

  KNO2 + 3 H2 -> NH3 + KOH + H2O,
  KNO3 + 4 H2 -> NH3 + KOH + 2 H2O.

Thus each mole of KNOx reduced returns one mole-equivalent of KOH. If the captured/reduced nitrogen consists of nitrite fraction 1-f and nitrate fraction f, where f is the nitrate fraction of the nitrogen that actually reaches NH3, the mixture balance per mole NH3 is

  (1-f) KNO2 + f KNO3 + (3+f) H2
      -> NH3 + KOH + (1+f) H2O.

The stoichiometric hydrogen use is therefore q=3+f mol H2 per NH3. This is also q=(z+3)/2 for mean initial nitrogen oxidation state z=3+2f, since reducing N(z) to N(-3) takes z+3 electrons and H2 supplies two electrons per molecule.

A matching carrier-preparation balance, with the same f and the same KOH state, is

  (1/2) N2 + (3/4+f/2) O2 + KOH
      -> (1-f) KNO2 + f KNO3 + (1/2) H2O.

Adding preparation and reduction cancels KOH. Splitting q water molecules,

  (3+f) H2O -> (3+f) H2 + (3/2+f/2) O2,

then cancels the H2 carrier and leaves, exactly for every f in [0,1],

  (1/2) N2 + (3/2) H2O -> NH3 + (3/4) O2.

The carrier, KOH, and mixed NOx fraction have disappeared from the net stoichiometry. This verifies the open-framing closure and q=3+f. It assumes the same KOH inventory/activity returns, full nitrogen conversion to NH3, no purge or side-product nitrogen, and no net change in water or other electrolyte inventories beyond the displayed balance. If f denotes feed composition but nitrite and nitrate have different conversion/selectivity, it is not necessarily the fraction of N reaching product and q=3+f must be recalculated from the product-weighted N flux.

2. Same-state Gibbs identity and dissipation signs

At 298.15 K, 1 bar ideal-gas standard states, pure liquid water, and NH3(g), the standard Gibbs energy of the net reaction above is

  G_T = mu_NH3 + (3/4)mu_O2 - (1/2)mu_N2 - (3/2)mu_H2O
      = Delta_f G°[NH3(g)] + (3/2) Delta G°[H2O(l)->H2(g)+(1/2)O2(g)]
      = -16.367 + (3/2)(237.141)
      = 339.3445 kJ mol-1 NH3.

For nonstandard streams, replace this by G_T°+RT ln Q, with

  Q = a_NH3 a_O2^(3/4) / (a_N2^(1/2) a_H2O^(3/2)).

For ideal gases a_i=p_i/p°, and pure liquid water has a_H2O=1. This makes the product state material: NH3 dissolved in electrolyte, NH4+ in an acid trap, and dry NH3(g) at a specified pressure have different chemical potentials. If a trap is used, include its acid/counterion state and the work or reagents needed to recover the stipulated NH3 product. For example, acid-base equilibrium obeys mu_NH3+mu_H2O=mu_NH4+ + mu_OH-. A source-cell voltage cannot simply be combined with G_T for dry NH3 unless the measured cell and the accounting cycle share the same product, water, gas, and electrolyte states or the state-transfer free energies are added.

Let g_ox be the signed Gibbs change for the carrier-preparation reaction, g_red the signed Gibbs change for the carrier-reduction reaction (negative means it can in principle export work), and g_H the Gibbs work of water splitting. All include the exact carrier composition, KOH, water, gas activities, and target NH3 state shown in the balanced reactions. State-function cancellation gives

  g_ox + q g_H + g_red = G_T.

This identity is sound when all intermediates close and no stream is silently changed. Define W_ox and w_H as signed work inputs, and W_return as positive electrical/mechanical work exported by reduction. Under a common isothermal work-only boundary with no uncredited nonambient heat or free-energy stream, the reversible bounds can be written

  W_ox = g_ox + D_ox,       w_H = g_H + D_H,
  W_return = -g_red - D_red,
  D_ox, D_H, D_red >= 0.

Then the net external work input is

  W_net = W_ox + q w_H - W_return + D_other
        = G_T + D_ox + q D_H + D_red + D_other.

The minus sign on W_return is essential: it is an exported-work credit. The definitions in the opening are algebraically consistent if W_total means inputs minus the positive return. Writing that relation explicitly avoids a sign ambiguity and prevents counting the returned electricity as an input. If heat is supplied at a nonambient temperature, include its exergy (and heat rejection) in the boundary; the work-only D_i need not remain nonnegative if an external heat/free-energy input is omitted. Likewise, D_other is nonnegative only after all useful coproducts, heat credits, and changed stream states have been included consistently.

For electrolysis with electrical work only, g_H=237.141 kJ mol-1 H2 and D_H=w_H-g_H>=0. Against direct N2/H2 synthesis, the extra hydrogen-splitting dissipation is (q-1.5)D_H=(1.5+f)D_H, equal to z/2 D_H. This is one component of the comparison, not a universal lower bound on every direct-electrosynthesis route and not a claim that all auxiliary energy is dissipation. With w_H=50 kWh kg-1 H2 as a sensitivity input, D_H=362.88-237.141=125.739 kJ mol-1 H2. The script's floors follow:

  G_T + 3 D_H  -> 11.687 kWh kg-1 NH3 for pure nitrite,
  G_T + 4 D_H  -> 13.738 kWh kg-1 NH3 for pure nitrate,
  G_T + 1.5 D_H ->  8.611 kWh kg-1 NH3 for direct N2/H2 reference.

The corresponding nitrite/nitrate excesses are 3.076/5.127 kWh kg-1 NH3. These are conditional reversible-other-stage floors at the stated sensitivity input and common product state; they are not acquired process energies. If a nonambient heat source, coproduct, or different product state is credited, the boundary must be modified before retaining these numerical comparisons.

A matched HB expression also needs one convention made explicit. If W_HB=1.5 w_H+H_HB, then H_HB must be the signed net non-H2 work/exergy for synthesis, air separation, recovery, storage, and control at the same product state; its reversible chemistry/heat credit must not be dropped or counted twice. With DeltaG_HB=G_T-1.5g_H=-16.367 kJ mol-1 under the displayed standard states, the route-beats-HB condition rearranges to

  H_HB > DeltaG_HB + (q-1.5)D_H + D_ox + D_red + D_other

when H_HB uses that signed convention. If H_HB is instead only positive auxiliary electricity and excludes the HB reaction/thermal balance, this inequality is not a valid like-for-like comparison without adding the omitted Gibbs/heat terms. The 91 t/day process figure or an unspecified “HB overhead” cannot be inserted without its exact configuration and boundary.

3. Faradaic-efficiency and measured-return accounting

For NH3 Faradaic efficiency eta_NH3 and nitrogen starting oxidation state z, the total charge passed per mole NH3 is

  Q_NH3 = (z+3) F / eta_NH3.

If (and only if) the anode current is supplied entirely by H2 oxidation, the Faradaic H2 consumed at the anode per mole NH3 is

  n_H2,anode = Q_NH3/(2F) = (z+3)/(2 eta_NH3).

For a nitrite/nitrate mixture with product-weighted fraction f and one common eta, this is (3+f)/eta. This is an anode charge stoichiometry, not the net fresh-H2 makeup: unused inlet H2, purge, cathodic H2 coproduct, other anodic reactions, and H2 recycle require separate measured balances. In particular, an 81% NH3 FE does not by itself identify the chemical energy or recoverability of the remaining 19% of charge products.

With a simultaneous full-cell consumed voltage V_cons (positive for input; negative for export), the electrical work per NH3 is

  W_cell,in = V_cons (z+3)F/eta_NH3.

Thus a negative V_cons gives a positive electrical-return credit of -V_cons(z+3)F/eta_NH3. The script's source-point arithmetic for nitrate at 0.1 V exported and eta=0.81 gives 4/0.81=4.938 mol Faradaic anode H2 per NH3, or 0.58455 kg H2 per kg NH3, and 8F(0.1 V)/0.81=95.3 kJ exported per mol NH3, or 1.554 kWh kg-1. At the explicit 50 kWh kg-1 H2 sensitivity, the anode-H2 electricity is 29.228 kWh kg-1, and subtracting the measured-voltage return gives 27.673 kWh kg-1 before oxidation, gas handling, recycle, and product recovery. The arithmetic is consistent. It is a partial benchmark, not a closed-cycle measurement: the paper's actual H2 feed, utilization, coproducts, and repeated electrolyte/carrier closure are not inferred from FE and voltage alone. Do not add an independent “FE penalty” on top of both Q/eta and the same measured voltage unless it represents a distinct measured loss; conversely, do not treat Q/eta as net fresh H2 without a coproduct/recycle balance.

The 10 mM nitrite current data are half-cell measurements, not a paired full-cell V_cons. Substituting the nitrate full-cell voltage or FE into a nitrite loop is a scenario, not an acquired nitrite energy result. Voltage and FE must be simultaneous at the same concentration, flow, electrolyte, and product state before evaluating a cell return per NH3.

4. Cycle affinity does not require every edge to be downhill

A stagewise “all forward steps are downhill” condition is sufficient for positive forward bias, but not necessary for positive steady-cycle current. For a reversible three-state ring with forward rates u_i on i->i+1 and reverse rates w_i on i+1->i (indices mod 3), define

  tau_0 = w_0 w_1 + w_0 u_2 + u_1 u_2,
  tau_1 = u_0 w_1 + u_0 u_2 + w_2 w_1,
  tau_2 = w_2 u_1 + u_0 u_1 + w_0 w_2,
  D = tau_0+tau_1+tau_2 > 0.

The exact stationary cycle current is

  J = (u_0 u_1 u_2 - w_0 w_1 w_2)/D.

Under LDB, ln[(u_0u_1u_2)/(w_0w_1w_2)] is the total cycle affinity. Its sign, not the sign of each edge affinity, sets the stationary-current direction. For example, u=(1,10,10), w=(2,1,1) has one reverse-biased edge u_0/w_0=1/2 but a forward current J=98/156>0. Thus an individual capture, transfer, or release step can be uphill while downstream product removal, other edge affinities, or fuel drive the total cycle forward. Any obstruction should be stated for the total closed-cycle affinity and explicit reservoirs/work, not as a necessity that every elementary edge be downhill. The three-state result is a kinetic-cycle identity; it does not assign the ammonia transfer rate.

Audit disposition

The KOH and mixed-carrier stoichiometry, q=3+f, same-state Gibbs identity, loss identity, FE conversion, and the displayed numerical arithmetic are internally consistent with the specified boundaries. The main precision guards are: state the sign convention W_net=W_inputs-W_return; keep D>=0 conditional on a complete isothermal/exergy boundary; treat FE-derived H2 as anodic Faradaic consumption rather than net feed absent a product/recycle balance; match the actual NH3/electrolyte/oxygen activities; and do not demand every edge be downhill for positive cycle current. The source nitrate full-cell point remains distinct from a closed captured-air nitrite/nitrate loop.
