## Verdict on the cold-preparation baseline

The existing paid_cold_input_and_mechanics_no_output_readout is a useful, deliberately strong all-cold preparation control. It sets both native input occupations to zero and the initial mechanical variance to vacuum, then runs the same pulse and damping without output readout. It tests whether a much broader conventional preparation can do at least as well; the replay says it can in this supplied fixture. It is not the strongest matched preparation comparison because it changes two resources at once and supplies more cold preparation than the dark-reference tap alone. The model specifies these cold states but does not price refrigeration work or the cost of maintaining cold bath streams.

The missing isolating comparison is cold native inputs with the initial mechanics held at the hot value, \(V_m=1000.5\), under the same pulse, damping, and no-output-readout rule. Evaluating the existing kernel with only occupancies=[0,0] changed gives:

The cold-input/hot-mechanics case is nearly identical to the all-cold case in this fixture: negativity differs by \(2.88\times10^{-6}\). The hot-input/cold-mechanics case remains PPT. ## Resources and boundary conditions

The replay's hot candidate starts from \(N_1=1000,N_2=500,V_m=1000.5\). Its nominal readout uses tap transmissions \((0.6,0.2)\), the finite-bandwidth matched-LO factor \(\eta_{\rm bw}\approx0.8340\), and detector efficiency \(0.9\). The script composes these as \(\eta_{\rm total}\approx0.25020\), with corresponding effective vacuum term \(w=(1-\eta)/(2\eta)\). Here \(\eta_{\rm bw}=1/J\), where the warm checkpoint's matched LO has \(J=\|H_B^{-\dagger}f\|^2=1+\|f'\|^2/B^2\) for normalized \(f\). The measured operator obeys \(P_g^{\rm out}=\sqrt{\eta_{\rm bw}}P_f^{\rm native}+\sqrt{1-\eta_{\rm bw}}P_{\rm vac}\); the filter loss is not modeled as residual hot temporal modes left unobserved by a mismatched filter. Each spatial tap, the passive filter's complementary port in this matched mode, and detector inefficiency contributes vacuum in the stated model. For this one observable, linear passive dilation lets the replay combine those independent vacuum contributions into a single effective term. This is exact bookkeeping for the observable, not a claim that the physical ports or their temperatures are identical: actual ports and any non-vacuum loss ancillas still have to be accounted for. The subsequently proposed dark-reference tap is absent from this replay; it adds a real cold input \(V\) and measures reflected record \(R\). These cold resources must not disappear into one generic detector \(\eta\).

The all-cold baseline supplies cold native bath streams for both channels plus a cold initial mechanical state, while using no output receiver. It is therefore a strong ordinary-preparation benchmark, but its cold-resource bill is larger and structurally different from the finite dark tap. The cold-input/hot-mechanics split is needed to separate these two preparation resources; the fixed-receiver table further distinguishes preparation from the output-loss penalty. For a fair capability comparison, report at least:

1. cold native bath inputs with the hot initial mechanics retained;
2. the existing cold-input/cold-mechanics case;
3. a single cold-dark replacement \(r=1\) using the same cold stream already paid by the tap, while retaining the hot bright input and mechanics;
4. the actual finite-\(r\) dark-reference tap and its measured \(P_R\) record.

If both physical outputs can be routed into the known bright/dark basis, direct bright-mode output sorting is also the same-interface incumbent: the existing replay's passive inverse-loss collector already encodes the bright hybrid. It should be compared before crediting the extra tap-and-reference route. The cold-resource cost of native baths, mechanical initialization, tap vacuum, filter loss modes, detector vacuum, and bandwidth must be charged separately per temporal mode or cycle.

## Primary/source and priority scope

The warm checkpoint explicitly recognizes the closest established ancestry: Sabuncu et al., “Environmental Assisted Quantum Information Correction for Continuous Variables,” PRA 81, 012325 (2010), https://arxiv.org/html/0909.3546, measures a same-mode environmental leakage and feedforwards; it is not a second independent hot native port. It also cites Trivedi et al., Phys. Rev. B 98, 144112 (2018), https://web.stanford.edu/group/nqp/jv_files/papers/trivedi2018few.pdf, for multiple independent Markov waveguides and \(L_j=c_j a\); the bright/dark rotation is elementary multiport input-output structure. Ogawa et al., PRL 116, 233602 (2016), https://arxiv.org/html/1507.06172, is cited for matched temporal-mode homodyne. These citations delimit conventional ancestry; this replay does not establish priority, physical implementation, or a new measurement principle.

The physical boundary is still open. Section 10 requires measured channel-resolved couplings/temperatures, output routing, and loss-ancilla temperatures before a gravity device. It also notes that the displayed one-period pulse does not satisfy the red-sideband/eliminated-cavity hierarchy \(B\ll\kappa\ll\omega\), and that current maglev/SQUID interfaces do not demonstrate access to their dominant intrinsic outputs. The script therefore establishes model-internal consistency only. The exact dark-input preparation is physically available only if two phase/mode-matched native lines are accessible upstream and can be mixed at the coupling-matched ratio; an internal Brownian reservoir does not provide that control by assumption.

## Recommended status

Keep the all-cold result as a strong paid-preparation benchmark, add the cold-input/hot-mechanics split to separate resources, and test the same-cold-stream \(r=1\) baseline before claiming that finite tap plus reference adds capability. No priority or experimental-advantage conclusion follows from these finite model comparisons.
