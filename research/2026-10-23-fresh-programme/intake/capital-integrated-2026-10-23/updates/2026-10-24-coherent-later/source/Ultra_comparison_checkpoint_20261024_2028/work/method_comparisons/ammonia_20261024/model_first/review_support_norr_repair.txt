# Bunea NO/OER point and feed-boundary audit

Scope: literal operating-point and feed-interface check for the section-4 NO/OER comparator only. I read the final published Bunea et al. main article (ACS Catalysis 13, 11345–11351, DOI [10.1021/acscatal.3c01600](https://doi.org/10.1021/acscatal.3c01600); open final-version [TU Delft PDF](https://pure.tudelft.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/158988899/acscatal.3c01600.pdf)) and the publisher-hosted [Supporting Information](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/suppl/10.1021/acscatal.3c01600/suppl_file/cs3c01600_si_001.pdf). This is not a whole-construction verdict.

## Operating-point correction

The section-4 tuple “1.9 V, 93% NH3 FE, 97% NO conversion, 0.51 mmol cm−2 h−1” is **not a demonstrated single operating point**. The main text explicitly places the 0.51 mmol cm−2 h−1 yield at **2.1 V and 57% NH3 FE** (main text, p. 3; results around lines 240–242 and 311–315 in the extracted PDF). In the full-cell voltage sweep, the 1 mg cm−2 IrO2 MEA has an NH3-FE maximum of about **78% at 1.9 V**, at about 64 mA cm−2 (p. 2, lines 139–162). In the later flow sweep, at **1.9 V and 8 mL min−1 of 5.2% NO/He**, the cell reaches **97% single-pass NO conversion**, but the authors explicitly say NH3 FE is *lower under these conditions* (p. 4, lines 328–342). Thus 0.51 definitely belongs to a different voltage/FE point, and the main text does not support pairing 93% FE with the 97%-conversion 8 mL min−1 point.

The abstract and conclusion do state 97% conversion and 93% FE for a roughly 5% NO/He feed, without specifying the flow rate for that paired claim. They do not reconcile it with the main text’s explicit “lower FE” at the 97%-conversion point or its 1.9-V voltage-sweep maximum of ca. 78%. Treat the exact 93%/97% co-occurrence and its flow condition as **UNRESOLVED**; do not use it as one joint data point in an energy/yield calculation. The 0.51-yield mismatch is not ambiguous: the main text specifies 2.1 V/57%.

The 1.9 V value is a **full-cell applied voltage for the water-oxidation/OER mode**, hence electrical input. The same paper’s HOR experiments report an OCP of ca. 0.6 V in Results (the Introduction says ca. 0.85 V); the authors state that no reduction products were detected over the cited 0–0.6 V range, current was very low, and they applied cathode potentials more negative than 0 V vs pseudo-RHE to drive NORR. The productive biased HOR data have maximum NH3 FE about 30% at −0.4 V vs pseudo-RHE and maximum NO conversion about 30% at −1 V. A pseudo-RHE cathode potential, the OCP, and the 1.9 V full-cell load voltage are different measurements and cannot be interchanged. No loaded positive-power NH3 point is established by the OCP.

## Cell and product boundary

This is gas-phase **NO reduction**, not direct electroreduction of captured aqueous nitrite. The cathode receives a premixed NO/He gas; the high-feed experiments use a nominal 4.8–5.2% NO/He composition (the article reports 4.8% for prior sections and 5.2% for the 8 mL min−1 conversion result/abstract). In OER mode, water is oxidized at the anode and protons cross an acidic-form Nafion™ 115 PEM (127 μm); the cathode is a Ru/C gas-fed MEA. The SI says the Nafion membrane was acid-activated in 1 M H2SO4 and rinsed/boiled in water before use. That is membrane preparation, not evidence for a bulk sulfuric-acid catholyte or acid supplied as an operating reagent. The reported 80 °C cell uses water oxidation at one side, which also hydrates the MEA/ionomer.

For the concentrated NO feed, the outlet NH3 is measured using an acid trap; the acquired main text does not identify a regenerable trap loop or a dry-NH3 recovery operation. For the separate 983 ppm NO/He test, the authors did **not** collect NH3 in the acid trap: a condenser was used to remove most water and ammonia because they interfered with the transmission-IR method, and they investigated NO conversion only. Therefore the dilute-feed conversion result is not an acquired NH3 FE or recovered-product result.

## Consequence for the captured-nitrite route

Switching the downstream cathode to Bunea-style NO/OER materially changes the upstream interface. An aqueous captured NOx carrier (in the opening, a nitrate/nitrite mixture) is not the gas-phase NO/He feed used in this PEM cell. Before applying these results, the process must supply a step that converts/releases the relevant captured nitrogen into NO gas, separates/conditions it to the required gas-feed composition and flow, and handles co-produced/remaining nitrogen species and capture liquor. Neither the Bunea paper nor its SI demonstrates that upstream conversion or purification, and the cell does not accept the captured nitrite solution as its feed. This is a missing process operation, not a claim that such conversion is impossible. Consequently the downstream OER point cannot be grafted onto the captured-nitrite ledger while treating the feed as unchanged or free.
