Primary-source check: Hollevoet et al. PNOCRA plasma accounting

Scope and source identity

This check is limited to the supplied Hollevoet_2022_PNOCRA author-version PDF/text and the static arithmetic in open_framing/analyse_operation.py. The supplied PDF is the archived peer-reviewed author version of Lander Hollevoet et al., “Energy-efficient Small-scale Ammonia Synthesis Process with Plasma-enabled Nitrogen Oxidation and Catalytic Reduction of Adsorbed NOx,” ChemSusChem 15:10 (2022), e202102526, DOI 10.1002/CSSC.202102526 (repository cover, extracted PAGE 1, lines 1–10; article title/authors on printed p. 1, extracted lines 12–20). The PDF has a repository cover, so PDF page number is one greater than the printed article page cited below.

Plasma figure and denominator

The paper reports a total NOx production rate “around 20–30 mg h−1” and defines EC as power divided by molar NOx production rate (printed p. 2 / PDF p. 3, extracted lines 141–152). It reports a lowest EC of 0.42 ± 0.03 MJ mol−1 at 1.5 L min−1 (printed p. 2 / PDF p. 3, lines 153–157). The outlet at that setting is described as about 200 ppm NOx and is “mainly NO,” with NO2 a smaller secondary product (printed p. 2 / PDF p. 3, lines 120–125, 208–212; Figure 2 on the same page). Thus the reported 0.42 denominator is mol NOx, not mol NH3. The text does not spell out the molecular-mass convention used to convert the plotted 20–30 mg h−1 mixed-NOx rate into molar NOx; the quoted EC and its stated molar denominator should be carried as reported rather than reverse-engineering the mass plot.

Power boundary and rescaling

The experimental section says the absorbed plasma power is 0.1 W and that the paper’s EC uses power absorbed in the plasma; displacement current is excluded (printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7, extracted lines 490–505). It separately gives 0.76 W as applied power into the system and says power-supply efficiency is not accounted for (printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7, lines 506–525). Therefore 0.42 MJ mol−1 NOx is an absorbed-plasma figure. At the same reported NOx throughput, the rounded applied/absorbed ratio is 0.76/0.1 = 7.6, so the corresponding applied-power-normalized estimate is 0.42×7.6 = 3.192 MJ mol−1 NOx (uncertainty propagated as ±0.03×7.6 = ±0.228 MJ mol−1). This is a boundary rescaling, not a separately measured EC, and still excludes upstream power-supply losses. Do not add 0.42 and 3.192: they are alternative accounting boundaries for the same plasma output.

The permitted analysis script makes exactly this alternative-boundary calculation at lines 191–199: p=0.42 and p=0.42×0.76/0.1, labelled respectively as absorbed-only and applied-power-normalized plasma. Its `kwhkg` conversion is kJ per mol NH3 divided by 3600×kg NH3 per mol (lines 24–25); `p*1000` treats p as MJ per mol N/NOx and uses one mol N per mol NH3 (lines 191–198). That stoichiometric bridge is valid only under the explicitly assumed complete one-N-per-product utilization for the direct-NO electrolysis scenario; an NH3 Faradaic efficiency is not itself a measurement of NO feed utilization. In particular, this script scenario is not the PNOCRA LNT train.

Carrier-gas and LNT evidence boundary

PNOCRA’s plasma feed is described as compressed dry air of purity at least 99.999%, at 0.2–2 L min−1 (printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7, lines 480–483); conditioning/compression energy is not included in the quoted absorbed-plasma EC. The plasma outlet selected for LNT work was about 200 ppm NOx (printed p. 2 / PDF p. 3, lines 208–212), but the LNT adsorption mixture was simulated gas: 200 ppm NO, 20% O2 and 80% N2, with 5% H2 in N2 for reduction (printed p. 3 / PDF p. 4, lines 239–248 and caption lines 254–256). The detailed protocol also uses 2% H2O during adsorption, an N2 purge, then 5% H2 plus 0.2% H2O in N2 (printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7, lines 552–558). This establishes separate plasma-output and simulated-feed LNT experiments, not a fully coupled plasma-to-LNT run. At 175°C the reported NH3 selectivity is 78%; its maximum is 82% at 125°C, where some NH3 remains adsorbed and requires additional heating for collection (printed p. 4 / PDF p. 5, lines 262–284; methods lines 561–563). The product is collected by aqueous washing (printed p. 4 / PDF p. 5, lines 301–306), not demonstrated as dry anhydrous ammonia.

What the 2.1 MJ mol−1 NH3 estimate means

The authors’ process estimate adds an assumed 80%-selective LNT plasma term to hydrogen from a 70%-efficient water electrolyzer. The plasma term is 0.42/0.8 = 0.525, rounded to 0.53 MJ mol−1 NH3; the paper gives about 1.57 MJ mol−1 NH3 for H2 generation and totals these as about 2.1 (printed p. 4 / PDF p. 5, extracted lines 334–370). The text’s phrase “20% more NOx” is loose if read as an input multiplier: 80% selectivity means 20% of input is lost, so fixed NH3 output needs 1/0.8 = 1.25 times the NOx, and the paper’s 0.53 calculation uses that divisor. The stated 4.6 mol H2 per mol NH3 is rounded: the listed stoichiometries give (0.8×4 + 0.2×2.5)/0.8 = 4.625 mol H2/mol NH3. The paper does not define the thermodynamic reference underlying “70% efficiency” in this paragraph; 1.57 is consistent with applying 70% to a roughly 237 kJ/mol-H2 water-splitting Gibbs reference, but that basis is not stated there. More materially, 2.1 is a modeled process estimate, not an integrated measured energy result: it combines absorbed plasma EC, assumed 80% NH3 selectivity, electrolyzer efficiency, and assumptions that the LNT and NH3 washing need no additional energy and LNT heat comes from exothermic reduction (same lines). The authors themselves say practical use should account for power-supply efficiency (printed p. 6 / PDF p. 7, lines 512–525).

For an apples-to-apples sensitivity using the paper’s own 80% selectivity and unchanged hydrogen term, substituting the applied-power-normalized plasma value gives 3.192/0.8 + 1.57 = 5.56 MJ mol−1 NH3 (about ±0.29 from the quoted plasma uncertainty alone). This arithmetic is not a revised experimental PNOCRA result; it is conditional on the 0.76/0.1 ratio holding at the chosen 1.5 L min−1 condition and retains all the paper’s other process assumptions. It also does not include the omitted power-supply losses, compressed-air preparation, or proof of integrated throughput/selectivity.

Boundary for the neighbouring NO-electrolysis calculation

The script’s direct-NO/OER case is a separate proposed construction: it combines a source-reported NO electrolysis point of 1.9 V and 93% NH3 Faradaic efficiency with an NO supply term p, while explicitly noting acid-trapped product and that full gas-NH3 recovery is not acquired (analyse_operation.py lines 175–202). Its two p values are useful boundary cases (absorbed-plasma-only and applied-power-normalized), but adding either p to that electrolysis point is not a reproduction of Hollevoet’s PNOCRA process. Keep the units visible: p=3.192 is MJ per mol NOx/N supplied; the PNOCRA 80%-selectivity bridge would instead be 3.99 MJ per mol NH3 for that plasma term. Do not silently substitute the script’s one-to-one NO-to-NH3 material-use assumption for Hollevoet’s 80% LNT selectivity, or treat the electrolysis FE as a feed-utilization measurement.
