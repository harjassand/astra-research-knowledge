The scalar thermal version illustrates the instrument; the general trace

6. A PAID INPUT-REFERENCE CONSTRUCTION WHEN ONLY C OUTPUT IS AVAILABLE

This is an actual covariance-producing operation, not a freely supplied bath
purifier. At each mass first rotate accessible incoming physical native lines
to B,D. Mix D with a real independent vacuum V_0 using a splitter of tap fraction
r, send D_t back through the inverse native input rotation, and homodyne R:

 D_t=sqrt(1-r)D+sqrt(r)V_0,
 R=sqrt(r)D-sqrt(1-r)V_0.

B still interacts with the mechanical oscillator, D_t still does not. Homodyne
of R destroys only the tapped field; the surviving D_t is actually returned to
the interface. For equal-temperature original ports D,B are independent. For
P quadratures, V_R=r v+(1-r)/2, k=Cov(P_Dt,P_R)/V_R, and

 Var(P_Dt-k P_R)= v/[2(r v+(1-r)/2)] =: V_D|R -> 1/(2r) as v->infinity.

Now C=sqrt(f)B_out+sqrt(1-f)D_t. Use the local two-record feedback
 exp{i a(q/sqrt(f))[Y-sqrt(1-f)k P_R]}.
Weyl multiplication cancels the bright own displacement EXACTLY, regardless
of B_out covariance. It gives the same feedback ZZ phase qp/2 per site as
full bright capture. It leaves only cross w, the dark innovation, and detector
noise. The own phase variance becomes

 V_self=(A/f)[(1-f)V_D|R+nu].

It is independent of the original hot occupation in the hot limit at fixed
r>0. For finite reference detector noise nu_R, replace V_R by V_R+nu_R in the
conditional covariance; for fixed r>0 the hot-limit innovation remains finite
but larger. Reference mode mismatch leaves O(n) leakage.

This construction needs coherent access to BOTH native input lines, a cold
ancillary input at the actual mode frequency, tap detection, and a physical
return path. It is invalid for an unaddressable material bath. Measuring an
unrelated thermal reference, or destructively measuring D and pretending the

low-frequency vacuum input can be a substantial refrigeration/conversion cost.

The strongest reservoir comparator with the same input control is r=1: replace

B. Passive QUANTUM filter versus electronic POST-HOMODYNE filter
A passive quantum output filter has a dilation in spectral modes
 C(omega)=H(omega)B(omega)+sqrt(1-|H(omega)|^2)V(omega),
where V must actually be vacuum for a vacuum-loss claim. Write the desired
bright phase kernel as h with ||h||^2=A. A shaped output LO/gain g satisfying
 H^dagger g=h cancels the entire bright phase, leaving vacuum variance

 (1/2) integral |h(omega)|^2(1-|H|^2)/|H|^2 d omega
     =(1/2)(||g||^2-||h||^2).

This gives a genuine effective vacuum-loss efficiency A/||g||^2, when g=h/H^*
is square-integrable and physically producible. It may require an INVERSE-
weighted LO, rather than simply matching the forward-filtered signal Hv.
If processor V is hot, multiply the respective spectral integrand by its
actual quadrature variance and it is not vacuum loss. A hard zero of H on a
positive-measure part of h's support leaves hot modes inaccessible. An isolated
zero is not itself an L2 nullspace, but can make the inverse cost infinite:
H~omega with h(0)!=0 has divergent integral |h/H|^2 near zero, whereas an h
vanishing sufficiently fast can avoid that divergence. Small transfer is not
free precision, and a pointwise inverse alone is insufficient.

An electronic filter after homodyne instead acts on the already measured
photocurrent, filtering its shot noise and signal together; it is not the
quantum beam-splitter dilation above and does not inject that vacuum term.
For mode selection performed by the LO before electronics, full response area
with H_e(0)!=0 preserves X_v up to gain. An isolated H_e(0)=0 destroys area-only
readout, but full-trace reconstruction may still work; it is not automatically
a positive-measure missing quantum band. Finite observation window, response
tail, calibration, and added electronics noise have to be included. Infinite
integration of a stationary electronics-noise baseline is not a finite-variance
estimator. These distinctions were independently checked by assigned support.
