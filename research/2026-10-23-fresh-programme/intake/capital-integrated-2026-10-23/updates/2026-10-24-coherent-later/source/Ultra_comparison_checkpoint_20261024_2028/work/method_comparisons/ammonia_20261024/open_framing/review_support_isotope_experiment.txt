Isotope/site audit of the proposed Mo-to-Ru nitrido handoff
2026-10-24 — bounded primary-source review

Scope and verdict

The 976 and 945 cm^-1 features are genuinely reported IR absorptions for the 14N and 15N isotopomers of terminal [RuN(PNP)] (complex 5). They are not predictions, but their provenance matters: the 15N complex was made with 1-15N-azide, not by transferring a nitride from Mo and not by using 15N2. The band pair is therefore a useful calibration/reference for assigning a terminal Ru-nitride isotopologue in a new crossover. It is not evidence that the proposed cross-metal handoff occurs. The 2011 Ru paper independently shows that Ru-nitride nitrogen becomes ammonia; the 2022 Mo paper independently shows a labeled Mo-nitride nitrogen becomes ammonia under H2-driven photocatalytic hydrogenation. Neither performs the proposed Mo-to-Ru crossover.

Primary experimental anchors

1. Askevold et al., “Ammonia formation by metal–ligand cooperative hydrogenolysis of a nitrido ligand,” Nature Chemistry 3 (2011), 532–537, DOI 10.1038/nchem.1051, https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.1051. The article reports a terminal Ru(IV) nitride that gives ammonia in high yield under H2 at ambient conditions and proposes rate-limiting heterolytic H2 splitting assisted by the PNP ligand. The author’s experimental chapter is publicly hosted by TUM: https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/download/1106483/1106483.pdf (chapter B-4, especially pp. 93–103; the matching Supporting Information is linked from the Nature article).

   The experimental chapter identifies [RuN(PNP)] (5), with PNP = N(CH2CH2PtBu2)2, reports 976 cm^-1 as the strong Ru≡14N IR absorption and 945 cm^-1 for Ru≡15N, and says a 1-15N-azide was used to prepare a sample 50% enriched in the 15N-5 isotopomer. It also reports a 15N NMR signal for the nitrido ligand at 832 ppm in C6D6. These are acquired isotopomer data and a site assignment, supported by the expected isotope shift and complementary NMR; the frequencies are not merely calculated markers. The isotope precursor is azide: do not describe the 945 cm^-1 reference as a Ru nitride made by Mo-to-Ru transfer.

   For hydrogenolysis, the same source reports 5 under 1 bar H2 at 50 °C for 48 h gives [RuH4(HPNP)] (7) in about 85% yield, with >80% ammonia by 1H NMR/indophenol analysis; small equilibrium amounts of [RuH3(PNP)] (8) occur under H2. It compares 14N-5 with a 1:1 14N/15N-5 mixture: the ammonia-region 1H NMR shows the expected 14NH3 triplet and 15NH3 doublet, with 1J(H-14N)=38.1 Hz and 1J(H-15N)=53.7 Hz. D2 hydrogenolysis removes the 14NH3 proton signal, and the source reports H2/D2 kinetics at 298 K. Quantitative ammonia workup used volatile transfer into HCl/ether followed by indophenol assay; the stated calibration range was 40–750 nmol NH3. This is strong evidence that the nitride ligand, not the PNP pincer nitrogen, furnishes the observed ammonia in that isolated Ru complex. It is still a stoichiometric Ru-nitride hydrogenolysis result, not the proposed Mo/Ru interface or an integrated carrier cycle.

2. Laplaza et al., “Dinitrogen Cleavage by Three-Coordinate Molybdenum(III) Complexes: Mechanistic and Structural Data,” JACS 118 (1996), 8623–8638, DOI 10.1021/ja960574x, https://doi.org/10.1021/ja960574x. The primary paper reports Mo[N(R)Ar]3, its μ-15N2 dimer, and terminal 15NMo[N(R)Ar]3; it states that 15N2 experiments established N2 as the source of the terminal nitride. It also follows μ-15N2 dimer cleavage to 15NMo products. This supports the basic source-label strategy for the Cummins-type Mo triamide platform, while also reminding us that its N2 cleavage proceeds through a μ-N2 dimer. The third amido ligand nitrogens are chemically distinct from the N2-derived terminal nitride and must be tracked separately in any product balance.

3. Kim et al., “Ammonia synthesis by photocatalytic hydrogenation of a N2-derived molybdenum nitride,” Nature Synthesis 1 (2022), 297–303, DOI 10.1038/s44160-022-00044-1, https://doi.org/10.1038/s44160-022-00044-1. Public author manuscript: https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1987945. This is a different Mo platform, [(depe)2MoN][BArF4], not MoL3. The paper reports hydrogenation of 15N-labeled Mo nitride to 15NH3 (39% yield per Mo), establishing nitride N as product N. With 4 atm D2 and an Ir-D photocatalyst under blue light it traps ND3 as ND3·BH3 and observes it by NMR, supporting H2 as the source of N-H hydrogen. It identifies MoH5 as the principal metal product and recycles it to the nitride in three steps. This is good precedent for independent N-source and H-source tracer measurements and a synthetic loop, but it does not test bare nitride transfer to Ru or the MoL3/Ru-PNP matched cycle.

4. Rütter et al., “Molybdenum(VI) Nitrido Complexes with Tripodal Silanolate Ligands. Structure and Electronic Character of an Unsymmetrical Dimolybdenum μ-Nitrido Complex Formed by Incomplete Nitrogen Atom Transfer,” Inorganic Chemistry 63 (2024), 8376–8389, DOI 10.1021/acs.inorgchem.4c00762, https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.inorgchem.4c00762. This is a chemically relevant warning, not a direct Mo/Ru precedent: attempted transfer to the Mo(NtBuAr)3 fragment often stops at isolable μ-nitrido adducts, with only small targeted terminal-product amounts detected. A 15N-sensitive Ru band by itself therefore cannot exclude a mixed-metal bridge, an N-containing adduct, or an incorrect assignment in the crossover mixture.

Atom and electron bookkeeping the experiment must test

For one proposed handoff, with AN = 15NMoL3 and B3 = RuH3(PNP):

T: 15NMoL3 + RuH3(PNP) -> MoL3 + Ru(15N)(PNP) + 3/2 H2.

For the downstream hydrogenolysis:

H: Ru(15N)(PNP) + 4 H2 -> RuH4(HPNP) + 15NH3.

For carrier dehydrogenation/reset:

R: RuH4(HPNP) -> RuH3(PNP) + H2.

Combining T+H+R consumes 3/2 H2 per NH3. With the established 2 MoL3 + N2 -> 2 15NMoL3 cleavage step and two repeats of T/H/R, the overall balance is N2+3H2 -> 2NH3. These equations are balanced bookkeeping, not evidence those three stages operate together. The exact Ru nitride hydrogenolysis source begins with RuN(PNP) under H2; it does not establish that the proposed B3-mediated transfer step T is favorable or selective.

What can masquerade as transfer

- Direct H/D delivery without N relocation: RuH3/D3 may protonate/deuterate the Mo nitride while the 15N remains bound to Mo. A later 15NH3 signal could then arise from Mo-bound nitride hydrogenation; it does not establish a Ru-15N intermediate. Before adding the high-H2 hydrogenation dose, assay the Mo-bound 15N species and any Mo-NH/ND products, and simultaneously assay the Ru site. The target observation is paired, stoichiometric loss of terminal Mo-15N and appearance of terminal Ru-15N together with recovered Mo(III) MoL3.
- μ-N bridge/adduct: a bridge can carry the 15N label and may be an on-path intermediate, but it is not the product of complete transfer. Resolve terminal versus bridging coordination with at least two orthogonal observables (for example, isotope-sensitive IR/Raman calibrated against authentic terminal isotopomers plus Mo/Ru EXAFS/XAS or an isolable X-ray/NMR-identified species). Do not use 945 cm^-1 alone as a universal unique-site fingerprint in a new matrix.
- Other nitrogen: MoL3 has three natural-abundance amido-N atoms in addition to the labeled terminal nitride. The Ru PNP contains one pincer N, which becomes HPNP in the reported Ru polyhydride product. Ligand-derived or released ammonia will generally be unlabeled and could inflate total NH3; natural-abundance background, residual ammonium/ammonia, azide-derived N, solvent/reagent impurities, and work-up contamination can also confound total yield. Quantify 14NH3 and 15NH3 separately, measure the initial isotopic enrichment, run no-Mo-nitride/no-Ru/no-H2/work-up blanks, and close the isotope balance over all Ru-, Mo-, bridge-, solution-, gas-, and product-N pools. A labeled gas/product alone is not enough to assign a metal site.
- H2/HD/D2 and hydride exchange: T predicts three H atoms leave B3 as 3/2 H2 if those hydrides pair without exchange. Starting from RuD3 ideally gives 3/2 D2 per transferred N, but HD, H2, N-H/D exchange at the pincer, proton donors, solvent, and residual H2 can change this distribution. Calibrated headspace GC-MS (or equivalent), headspace volume/pressure, dissolved-gas accounting, and 1H/2H NMR hydride inventories are needed. A gas signal alone is not specific to T because unrelated hydride/proton or decomposition chemistry can evolve H2. Measure the transfer stage before introducing high H2 for BN hydrogenation.
- Carrier reset: RuH4(HPNP) is the observed major product of direct hydrogenolysis, and RuH3(PNP) is a minor equilibrium component under 1 bar H2. The source also reports a pressure-dependent RuH3/RuH4(HPNP) manifold. The crossover must quantify Ru states during T, hydrogenation, and reset under the same solvent, temperature, and H2 potential; the existence of each isolated state is not a closed cycle or a free reset operation.

Minimum evidence thresholds

One N-handoff event (T) requires, in a single quantitative crossover time course or matched quenched aliquots:

1. A measured starting inventory and enrichment for terminal Mo-15N, intact MoL3 ligands, and B3 hydrides.
2. Time-correlated disappearance of Mo-15N and appearance of structurally assigned *terminal* Ru-15N, with recovered MoL3 / Mo(III) in the stoichiometric amount and no alternative μ-N or decomposition species accounting for the label.
3. A closed 15N balance over residual Mo nitride, terminal Ru nitride, μ-N species, dissolved/volatile nitrogen, and any NH3/NHx; and H/D plus H2/HD/D2 balance consistent with the proposed 3-hydride removal. Include authentic Ru-15N standards and matched control mixtures to calibrate frequencies, enrichment, and response.
4. Only after that T-stage readout, hydrogenate isolated/quantified Ru-15N to 15NH3 and B5, then quantify B5->B3 return. The sequential ordering prevents a fast downstream sink from erasing the handoff-site evidence.

A completed synthetic cycle additionally requires N2 uptake to regenerate the labeled/then fresh terminal Mo nitride, hydrogenation of Ru nitride, B5->B3 reset, recovery of both catalysts/ligands, and the balanced net N2+3H2->2NH3 over the same operating protocol. To call it catalytic turnover rather than a single stoichiometric round trip, run at least two successive N2-to-NH3 turnovers with the same Mo and Ru inventory, identify isotopically switched N2 feeds (e.g. a 15N2 first pass and 14N2 second pass) in the corresponding products, and report TON, complete N/H/metal/ligand balances, H2 potential, gas handling, and any photochemical/redox/gating inputs. The three-step MoH5-to-nitride regeneration in the 2022 paper is a precedent for a synthetic cycle but does not validate this different MoL3/Ru-PNP cycle.

Quantitative negative result: what a missing band can bound

A non-detected Ru-15N band bounds a population or rate only after calibrating a concentration-equivalent limit L for terminal Ru-15N in the actual crossover matrix and measuring its disappearance rate under the exact conditions. In the simplest explicit kinetic model, let v be a constant formation flux of terminal Ru-15N and let k_out be the sum of first-order consumption/back-transfer/hydrogenation/loss rates. Starting from zero:

[Ru-15N](t) = (v/k_out) (1 - exp(-k_out t)).

If no signal above calibrated limit L is seen at the end of an observation interval T, then, conditional on this model and the calibrated k_out,

v < L k_out / (1 - exp(-k_out T)).

For T >> 1/k_out this becomes v < L k_out. Thus a fast-lived BN species can have appreciable formation flux while its instantaneous concentration stays below the IR/NMR limit. If k_out has no independent upper bound, non-detection supplies no finite upper bound on v. An authentic terminal Ru-15N spike added to the same reaction matrix, followed at the same temperature, H2 pressure, and time resolution, can measure the survival/response and estimate k_out; one must also measure whether labeled product accumulates after the band disappears.

For a complementary event-level sampling design, suppose handoff events occur uniformly within fixed intervals of width Δ, each terminal-BN episode has an exponential lifetime k_out, and an instantaneous sample detects an episode that is present with probability p_det. The probability a single event is caught at the next sample is

eta = p_det * (1 - exp(-k_out Δ)) / (k_out Δ).

If n independent labeled-N handoff opportunities are assayed and none yields a positive BN observation, then at one-sided confidence 1-alpha the fraction q of opportunities that generate detectable terminal BN is bounded by

q <= min(1, [1 - alpha^(1/n)] / eta) ≈ min(1, -ln(alpha)/(n eta)).

At 95% confidence, the numerator is approximately 3/n. This does not bound all handoff attempts unless n is a defensible opportunity count, and it becomes weak when k_out Δ is large. It also requires p_det, Δ, and k_out to be calibrated; otherwise no finite numerical q bound follows. Endpoint 15NH3 analysis provides a separate cumulative bound on the fraction that reaches ammonia, but failure to find either BN or 15NH3 still requires a closed 15N balance to exclude other labeled sinks or label loss. Therefore “no 945 cm^-1 band” by itself is not “no handoff.”

Evidence boundary

The isotope strategy is strong because the relevant primary literature has independently demonstrated (i) site-specific 14N/15N IR and NMR assignment for isolated RuN(PNP), (ii) ammonia-N tracing from the Ru nitride, (iii) 15N2 tracing into the Cummins-type Mo nitride, and (iv) 15NH3 and deuterated ammonia after hydrogenation of a different N2-derived Mo nitride. These calibration and half-cycle results make the crossover experimentally falsifiable. None establishes the missing T reaction, a terminal Ru-15N species in a Mo/Ru mixture, full nitrogen/hydrogen closure, or a catalytic cycle on the proposed common operating conditions.
