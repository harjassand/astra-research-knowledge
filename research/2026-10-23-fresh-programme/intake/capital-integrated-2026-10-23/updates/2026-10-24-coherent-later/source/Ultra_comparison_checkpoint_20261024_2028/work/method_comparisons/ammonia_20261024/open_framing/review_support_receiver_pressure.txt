# Cross-review: Ru–PNP receiver inventory and conditional H2-pressure work

Scope: checked Sections 1–2 of `model_first/PHYSICAL_CYCLE_AND_OBSTRUCTION.txt` against the primary Ru–PNP hydrogenolysis article and the author’s thesis chapter from which it originated. The bookkeeping below is exact for the stated molecular formulas. It does not validate the proposed Mo-nitride-to-Ru transfer step.

## Receiver species and experimental basis

Askevold et al. report the terminal Ru nitride hydrogenolysis and assign it to ammonia formation by metal–ligand cooperative H2 activation ([Nature Chemistry 3, 532–537 (2011), DOI 10.1038/nchem.1051](https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.1051)). The author’s detailed primary chapter is available in the [TUM thesis, chapter 4, printed pp. 89–103](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/download/1106483/1106483.pdf).

The formulas in the checkpoint are correctly distinguished:

- `PNP = N(CH2CH2PtBu2)2` is the anionic, deprotonated amido pincer; `HPNP = HN(CH2CH2PtBu2)2` is its neutral N-protonated amine form.
- `BN = [Ru(N)(PNP)]` is the Ru(IV) terminal nitrido complex. With nitrido N formally −III and PNP −I, formal Ru is +IV.
- `B3 = [RuH3(PNP)]` is the Ru(IV) amido trihydride. Three Ru–H hydrides plus PNP− give a neutral complex.
- `B5 = [RuH4(HPNP)]` is the Ru(IV) tetrahydride with one pincer N–H. The thesis reports a 4H RuH signal at −8.48 ppm and a 1H NH signal at 4.54 ppm; the 1H is on the protonated pincer, not part of the four Ru hydrides. Thus B5 contains five H atoms outside the fixed pincer carbon skeleton, versus three in B3: `B3 + H2 ⇌ B5` is the correctly balanced one-H2 inventory change.

The primary chapter reports that `BN` under 1 bar H2 at 50 °C for 48 h affords B5 in about 85% yield; >80% NH3 is measured by NMR integration/indophenol assay, with isotope evidence that the nitrido nitrogen becomes NH3. It separately prepares B5 from B3 under 1 bar H2 quantitatively by NMR. On attempted isolation, B5 partially dehydrogenates to a B5/B3 mixture. In the nitrido hydrogenolysis mixture, a small equilibrium concentration of B3 is detected under H2. So the B5/B3 ⇌ H2 relationship is supported by qualitative bidirectional interconversion evidence; calling it only a one-way endpoint mixture would be too skeptical. However, there is no pressure-dependent equilibrium constant, quantitative low-pressure B5 discharge, or high-yield pressure-controlled reset demonstrated. The checkpoint’s phrase “directional complete reset unproven” is accurate if read narrowly this way.

## Exact atom and formal-redox audit

Use the checkpoint’s neutral Mo complexes with `L− = N(tBu)Ar−`: `A = MoL3` has formal Mo(III), and `AN = NMoL3` has formal Mo(VI) because the terminal nitride is N(−III). The proposed transfer equation

`AN + B3 -> A + BN + (3/2) H2`

balances all atoms and charge. Formal Mo(VI)→Mo(III) consumes three electron equivalents; the three B3 hydrides are oxidized from formal H(−I) to H2 H(0), supplying those three equivalents. Ru remains formally +IV on both B3 and BN; the transferred nitrido N remains −III. This is internal chemical redox bookkeeping, not an external three-electron current or evidence of an electrical work source.

The actual receiver hydrogenolysis balances as

`BN + 4 H2 -> B5 + NH3`.

The eight H atoms from four H2 appear as the four Ru hydrides plus one pincer N–H in B5 and three H atoms in NH3. Formal Ru remains +IV; the nitrido nitrogen remains at −III on conversion to NH3. The N–H inventory on the pincer must be carried through any regeneration calculation.

The observed B5/B3 composition change is

`B5 -> B3 + H2`.

It releases two H atoms: one hydride and the pincer N–H, leaving the three B3 hydrides. Adding transfer, receiver hydrogenolysis and this reset cancels BN, B3 and B5 and leaves `AN + (3/2)H2 -> A + NH3`. Combining twice with `2A + N2 -> 2AN` gives exactly `N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3`. The checkpoint’s total stoichiometry is correct.

## Pressure work and its assumptions

If transfer T and reset R are run at low H2 pressure `pL`, they release `3/2 + 1 = 5/2 mol H2` per mol NH3. Receiver hydrogenolysis H consumes 4 mol H2 at high pressure `pH`. Thus 2.5 mol is internal low-pressure gas to be returned to the high-pressure H step, and the net fresh H2 requirement is 1.5 mol per mol NH3. Under ideal-gas, reversible, isothermal compression at a common temperature T, the internal-recycle work is exactly

`Winternal = (5/2) RT ln(pH/pL)` per mol NH3.

This is a conditional work term for the stated pressure staging, not an acquired process penalty. If fresh H2 enters at pressure `pS < pH`, its additional ideal compression work is `(3/2) RT ln(pH/pS)` per mol NH3. More generally, the two separate terms are `RT[ (5/2)ln(pH/pL) + (3/2)ln(pH/pS) ]`; when `pS=pL`, this reduces to `4RT ln(pH/pL)` for the four H2 molecules delivered to H. If H2 is already delivered at `pH`, the fresh-feed compression term is zero, but internal recycle still costs the first term. Real compression, gas purification/separation, pressure drop, and nonideal fugacity can add costs.

Avoid double counting: only 1.5 H2 per NH3 is net fresh chemical feed, while 2.5 H2 is internally recycled. Do not charge source compression on 4 fresh H2 and then add the 2.5-mol recycle compression again. If an upstream H2 cost already includes delivery at `pH`, do not add its fresh-feed pressure term a second time. Conversely, the 2.5-mol internal term is not automatically included in a price for fresh H2. Any H2/NH3 separation needed to recover the low-pressure recycle is a distinct operation unless already included in another process boundary.

Crucial thermodynamic scope: the receiver paper establishes B3/B5 chemistry under 1 bar H2, not two distinct `pL` and `pH` operating windows. No `pH/pL` ratio, equilibrium constant, or pressure-switching cycle has been measured for the proposed Mo/Ru module. At a single pressure, the pressure-swing term may be absent, but the required B3 population and productive transfer at that same pressure must then be demonstrated. The existing estimate is therefore a correct conditional ideal-gas accounting identity, not a measured or necessarily unavoidable energy cost.
