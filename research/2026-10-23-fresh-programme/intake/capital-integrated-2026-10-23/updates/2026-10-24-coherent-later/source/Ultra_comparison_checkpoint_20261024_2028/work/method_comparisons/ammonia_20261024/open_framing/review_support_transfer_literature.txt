BOUNDED PRIMARY-SOURCE CROSS-REVIEW: NITRIDE DIRECTION, INVENTORY, AND RELAY SCOPE
2026-10-24

Scope and source handling
-------------------------
This note checks only the Mo-trisanilide/Ru-PNP transfer claim and its closest
nitride/hydride precedents. I read the pinned Rutter 2024 full text in the
provided PMC XML/TXT and checked its article passages against the primary PDF
text. I also read the cited Laplaza-Johnson-Cummins 1996 communication in a
full-text reproduction (the ACS DOI page itself exposed metadata, not full
text), the Askevold paper's primary author thesis treatment and article
abstract, and the published 2017 Wang et al. article in a full-text
reproduction (Nature's page was access-controlled in this retrieval). These
access limits affect source provenance, not the reaction-direction statements
below. The primary records are linked individually.

1. The transfer direction is unambiguous
----------------------------------------
There are two different Mo-to-Mo precedents in Rutter et al. (2024), and they
must not be conflated.

First, the older reaction Rutter cites is:

  [(tBuO)3Mo≡N] + [(tBuArN)3Mo] -> [(tBuArN)3Mo≡N] + 1/2 Mo2(OtBu)6.

Thus the *alkoxide-ligated Mo nitride* [(tBuO)3Mo≡N] is the N donor; the
low-valent trisanilide Mo(III) complex [(tBuArN)3Mo] is the N acceptor; and
the terminal trisanilide nitride [(tBuArN)3Mo≡N] is the product. The 1996
primary communication explicitly says N transfer is from NMo(OR)3 to
Mo(NRAr)3 and identifies Mo2(OR)6 as the coproduct. It also reports 83% and
74% isolated yields for the two terminal nitrides, respectively, and notes
that the wider N2/Mo system was cyclic but not efficient enough to be truly
catalytic. This paper does not show the trisanilide nitride donating N to a
different metal.

Second, Rutter's 2024 experiment used terminal *tripodal-silanolate* Mo(VI)
nitrides 3a/3b as donors and [(tBuArN)3Mo] (11) as acceptor. Reaction in
toluene at ambient temperature immediately produced heterodinuclear
μ-nitrido complexes 13a/13b. Attempts to drive them to complete transfer
mostly failed; the target terminal trisanilide nitride 10 and free aniline
ligand were only detected in small amounts. The paper's abstract itself
states the direction as transfer “from these novel terminal nitrides to
[(tBuArN)3Mo] ... as the potential acceptor.”

So for the candidate cycle, AN = [(tBuArN)3Mo≡N] is proposed to act as the
N donor in AN + RuH3(PNP) -> A + RuN(PNP) + 3/2 H2. That is the reverse
direction from the demonstrated Cummins transfer and is not established by
Rutter's experiment: its Mo-trisanilide complex A is the acceptor, and AN is
the acquired product. The 2024 work makes the exact *acceptor* scaffold
chemically relevant, but it does not establish AN as a transferable nitride
donor to Ru.

2. Ligand and dimer accounting; textual formula discrepancy
----------------------------------------------------------
With Ar = 3,5-dimethylphenyl, A is the three-coordinate Mo(III) complex
[(tBuArN)3Mo], carrying three anionic N(tBu)Ar amido/anilide ligands; AN is
its terminal Mo(VI) nitride [(tBuArN)3Mo≡N]. In the 2024 bridge complex,
the Mo[N] end is capped by this trisanilide ligand set, while the Mo[O] end
is the tripodal silanolate fragment. These are unlike the Ru-PNP ligand
inventory and should not be treated as interchangeable coordination shells.

For the older alkoxide transfer, the departing reduced fragment is
[(tBuO)3Mo]. Two such fragments form the Mo-Mo-bonded hexakis(tert-butoxide)
dimer Mo2(OtBu)6. Half a mole of that dimer per transferred N accounts for
three OtBu ligands per transferred donor Mo. The 1996 primary source gives
Mo2(OR)6 and independently characterizes the dimer; its cited 1977 dimer
reference is explicitly titled “Hexakis(alkoxy)dimolybdenum Compounds.”

There is a source-text inconsistency worth preserving rather than silently
explaining away: the Rutter 2024 PMC XML and extracted article text render the
old coproduct in one prose sentence as “[Mo2(OtBu)3]”, while the cited 1996
primary article gives Mo2(OR)6 and the atom/ligand balance requires
Mo2(OtBu)6. The 2024 source points to Scheme 3 as a graphic; the accessible
local package does not include that graphic at a readable resolution. Thus
the safest ledger is Mo2(OtBu)6, with the Rutter prose rendering flagged as a
likely subscript/typographical discrepancy—not claimed to be a proven OCR-only
loss.

The 2024 study identifies a separate, important bottleneck: for its
tripodal-silanolate-to-trisanilide reaction, it had hoped the Mo(III)
silanolate fragment could gain a Mo≡Mo bond as a driving force. Its podand
“fence” sterically impedes that dimerization, so the proposed driving force
could be absent. The reaction instead accumulates stable μ-N adduct 13. In
13a, the reported formal Mo[N]–N and Mo[O]–N bond orders are 2.5 and 0.5,
and a formal Mo–Mo bond order of 0.5 is also found; the authors conclude that
the nitrogen “tug-of-war” is won by the more oxidized Mo[N] center, consistent
with stronger π donation by amides than silanolates. This is direct evidence
that a μ-nitrido bridge can be a resting/trapped intermediate rather than a
productive handoff. It is not evidence that the distinct Ru/Mo pair must form
the same bridge.

3. Closest nitride/hydride relay evidence and its boundary
--------------------------------------------------------
The molecular Ru-PNP precedent is Askevold et al. (2011). Its starting
complex is already terminal Ru(IV) nitride [Ru(N)(PNP)]. Under 1 bar H2 at
50 °C over 48 h, it gives [RuH4(HPNP)] in about 85% yield and ammonia; the
primary thesis text reports a small equilibrium concentration of [RuH3(PNP)]
under H2 and notes solvent evaporation/dehydrogenation can form RuH3(PNP)
from RuH4(HPNP). This supports a Ru nitride-to-hydride/ammonia hydrogenolysis
manifold and the existence of the proposed hydride-state inventory. It does
not show RuH3(PNP) accepting N from Mo, does not show Ru nitride formation
from a Mo nitride, and does not close the candidate's cross-metal transfer
step. The proposed T step is not a routine inference from hydrogenolysis; it
requires the opposite functional role for the Ru complex plus the associated
Mo/Ru redox and H2-release accounting.

At the broader architecture level, Wang et al. (2017) is an established
nitride/hydride relay precedent: the authors assign activated N transfer from
transition-metal sites/nitrides to LiH-derived LiNHx, hydrogenation of the
LiNHx species to NH3, and regeneration of LiH. Their main text reports N2
treatment of Mn–LiH/Fe–LiH at 548 K, formation of Li2NH/LiNH2 alongside
Mn2N, no N2 reaction for the LiH-alone control, subsequent H2-driven NH3
formation, and continuous TM(N)–LiH catalysis for ten hours under 10-bar
syngas. This defeats any broad claim that relaying activated nitride nitrogen
to a separate hydrogenating carrier is itself a new concept. It is a
heterogeneous solid-state LiH/metal-nitride route, not a demonstrated
molecular Mo-trisanilide-to-Ru-PNP handoff. The authors themselves say
surface-science characterization and modeling are needed for molecular-level
pathway details; the available data do not supply a measured Mo-to-Ru
transfer rate or an exact molecular receiver cycle.

4. Defensible statement
-----------------------
The exact candidate stoichiometry can be written in a closed, atom-balanced
cycle, and the component ideas have real precedents: Cummins/Rutter for
Mo nitride formation and Mo-to-Mo N transfer/bridging; Askevold for Ru-PNP
nitride hydrogenolysis and hydride states; Wang for a heterogeneous
nitride-to-hydride-carrier ammonia relay. In the primary sources checked here,
however, there is no demonstrated transfer of an N2-derived Mo-trisanilide
nitride to a Ru-PNP hydride with Ru-nitride production, Mo(III) recovery, and
H2 release. State this as “not established in the audited sources,” not a
global novelty finding. The most discriminating feasibility result remains
direct observation of labeled Mo-derived N appearing as Ru–N while Mo A is
recovered; NH3 alone would not distinguish that handoff from H delivery to
Mo-bound N. Quantitative transfer yield/rate, competition with a stable
Mo–(μ-N)–Ru or Mo–NHx path, and complete B5/B3 reset under matched conditions
remain open.
