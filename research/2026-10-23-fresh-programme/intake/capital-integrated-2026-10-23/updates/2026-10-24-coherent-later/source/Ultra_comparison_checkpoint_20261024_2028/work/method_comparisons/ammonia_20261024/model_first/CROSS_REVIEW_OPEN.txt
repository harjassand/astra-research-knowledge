2026-10-24. Written after the model-first owned checkpoint was frozen.
Original checkpoints and original source XLSX were not modified.

Disposition

The peer's principal scoped result survives: a known nitrate/HOR cell really
produces NH3 while exporting electrical work, but that reduction-leg output
does not establish a competitive fresh-air/water ammonia process. Its
alkaline carrier closure and same-state electrolysis-loss identity check out.
Two material repairs are needed before reusing its numerical comparison:
gross anodic H2 throughput must not be called minimum fresh H2 without a
gas-recovery balance, and the Bunea NO/OER headline metrics must be split
into their actual operating conditions. No new enabling operation, complete
renewable loop, or method winner follows from the reviewed evidence.

1. Material energy-boundary repair: gross anodic H2 is not net fresh H2

The AgRu source SI pp57–59 reports about81% NH3 FE at consumed voltage-0.1V
and calculates H2 throughput from total anode current. I independently read
that source text and the main Methods. This is stronger evidence than the
older support_existing_cells note's statement that81% is only a TEA
assumption; the peer and root correctly overruled that older note.

At NH3 FE=eta for nitrate, total charge per mol NH3 is8F/eta. If the anode
current is entirely HOR, gross Faradaic anode consumption is4/eta molH2.
That gives4.93827mol/mol ateta=.81 and0.58455kg/kgNH3 using2.016g/molH2.
It is not automatically the minimum fresh makeup after closing gas streams.
Let chi be cathodic H2 Faradaic efficiency and rho its recovered fraction.
Under this restricted recovery model,

 q_fresh = (4/eta)*(1-rho*chi) molH2/molNH3,

before purge, crossover, chemical H2 use or other side-product regeneration.
Neither chi nor rho is acquired in this paper. An81% NH3 FE leaves19% charge
unassigned and does not identify it as HER. I checked the main Methods, SI
and original workbook; the bounded source checker likewise found no cathode
H2 FE/outlet balance/recovery assay. The positive cathodic potentials make
arbitrarily assigning the FE gap to HER especially unjustified. The endpoint
below is a conditional algebraic case, not a source-reported gas composition.

At the peer's explicit50kWh/kgH2 sensitivity and0.1V exported:
- No recovered cathodic H2 gives29.228kWh/kgNH3 H2 input minus1.554 exported
  =27.673 before nitrogen fixation and recovery.
- If, purely as a limiting recovery scenario, chi=.19 andrho=1, q_fresh=4,
  giving23.674 minus1.554=22.120kWh/kgNH3 before fixation and recovery.

Consequently18.796kWh/kgNH3 is a necessary HB-overhead threshold only for
the no-cathodic-H2-credit scenario. The corresponding algebraic full-recovery
threshold is13.242, using the identical HB H2-only term8.878. These values
are not an acquired range for the real plant: actual coproducts, recovery
and purges are unmeasured. The original27.673 and18.796 numbers are valid
conditional arithmetic but must not be a universal lower bound/necessity.
Root and the peer constructor have been informed. This correction does not
provide an attractive measured closed-loop energy point.

The source's imposed10sccm anode feed, with only about0.35sccm Faradaic H2
at0.05A, is another separate utilization/recycle question. It cannot resolve
cathodic H2 recovery or fresh makeup. Treat fresh input, gross anode reaction,
cathode coproduct and external gas recycle as four distinguishable fluxes.

3. The NO/OER repair is justified chemically but changes the feed interface

Replacing HOR by OER removes the extra electrolytic H2 round trip in the
displayed NO-fed reaction. It is a scientifically justified next branch,
and Bunea supplies real NO-fed PEM chemistry. It is not an acquired connection
to the preceding KNO2/KNO3 capture loop.

There are two distinct versions to state explicitly:
(a) retain alkaline capture, then acquire NO release/conversion from the
    captured nitrite/nitrate, with base/water/counterion restoration and all
    reducing reagents/thermal work counted;
(b) bypass that liquid carrier and condition the plasma's raw gas into a
    usable NO feed, including concentration and removal/tolerance of O2.

The demonstrated PEM feeds roughly5%NO/He. The plasma source gives about
200ppm NOx in air, mostlyNO, with abundantO2/N2. The source's separate983ppm
NO/He test reports conversion and does not acquire the full NH3 FE/product
stream there. Thus real low-concentration oxygen-containing plasma gas has
not been connected to the NO/OER cathode. A reductive NO-release conditioner
could reintroduce H2 use; avoiding that cannot be assumed. These are missing
operations, not a proof of impossibility. The peer already grants concentrated
NO for free in its optimistic energy calculations; keep that grant visible.

4. Verified scoped chemistry and numerical acquisition

The exact alkaline steps close KOH, potassium, charge and atoms:
 NO2-+5H2O+6e- ->NH3+7OH-,
 3H2+6OH- ->6H2O+6e-,
 KNO2+3H2 ->NH3+KOH+H2O.
Nitrate analogously needs4H2 and releases2H2O. With complete NH3 conversion,
product-weighted nitrate fractionf givesq=3+f. If nitrate and nitrite have
different conversion/selectivity, feed fractionf alone does not determine
that product-weighted flux. The ideal capture/loading formulas and water
electrolysis return exactlyN2+3H2O->2NH3+1.5O2 for both carriers.

With identical internal and external stream states,
 g_ox+q*g_H+g_red=G_target;
 W_net=W_ox+q*w_H-W_return
      =G_target+D_ox+q*D_H+D_red+D_other.
The signs and cancellation are correct. D>=0 requires the complete stated
isothermal/exergy boundary, including useful coproducts, heat credits and
actual product states; no free acid, ambient concentration change or external
high-grade heat may disappear intoD. The same-state standard-value arithmetic
G_target=339.3445kJ/mol and NO/OER reversible voltage0.523902V reproduce.
The50kWh/kgH2 ideal-other-stage floors11.687(nitrite),13.738(nitrate) and
10.662(NO) reproduce. They are optimistic conditional circuit floors and
do not measure an actual carrier preparation, rate or plant advantage.

5. Plasma/source ancestry and what remains genuinely established

I read the Hollevoet primary author text, including the experimental power
definition and process-energy paragraph. It is the2022 ChemSusChem PNOCRA
paper DOI10.1002/cssc.202102526, not a different plasma benchmark. It reports
0.42+-0.03MJ/molNOx using0.1W absorbed plasma, and explicitly distinguishes
0.76W applied system power. The7.6factor and3.192MJ/mol same-yield rescaling
are correct from rounded powers; it is not a separately metered full plant
or a second energy term to add to0.42. Further supply/conditioning losses
are not included. The20–30mg/h mostly-NO throughput is consistent in order
with that molar rate; no precise mixed-NOx mass convention was reverse-inferred.

Its LNT chemistry used simulated200ppmNO/air, humidity/purge and H2 mixtures,
not the same integrated plasma stream. The paper's2.1MJ/molNH3 is a summed
estimate with80% NH3 selectivity, H2 electrolysis efficiency and no extra LNT/
washing energy.0.42/.8=.525 is the correct plasma term for that scenario;
the text's loose20%more phrase must not replace the1/.8 factor. The peer's
directNO/OER combinations are a different scenario, not reproduced PNOCRA.
This source is close known-operation ancestry for the oxidative detour and
adsorbed-carrier regeneration. Neither oxidation plus reduction nor electrical
return is new merely because the present accounting makes it explicit.

The peer's finite-H competitive-adsorption derivative is algebraically correct
for its labelled oneH*/oneN* model. Its source0.64/0.97eV step energies are
not elementary barriers or acquired rate constants, which the peer correctly
states. Its strongest positive result is a physically supported known
reduction operation and a precisely narrowed integration/energy gate. Its
negative result does not exclude nitrate-waste valorization, future better
plasma/gas conditioning, or small-scale capital advantages; those questions
require their own matched evidence.
