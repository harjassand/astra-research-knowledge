6. UNEQUAL VACUUM TAPS AND THE NATIVE BRIGHT ROW

Let c_j=sqrt(gamma_j), gamma=sum_j gamma_j, and b_col,j=
sqrt(eta_j)b_out,j+sqrt(1-eta_j)v_j, with independent vacuum v_j and
0<eta_j<=1. A normalized passive single row is

  b_det=sum_j h_j b_col,j + sum_l u_l v'_l,
  sum_j |h_j|^2+sum_l |u_l|^2=1.

To reproduce the same native bright mode up to a scalar, require
h_j sqrt(eta_j)=lambda c_j. Then

  1=|lambda|^2 sum_j gamma_j/eta_j + sum_l |u_l|^2.

Writing S=sum_j gamma_j/eta_j, the largest possible |lambda|^2 is 1/S,
attained by using no extra vacuum ports and

  h_j=sqrt(gamma_j/eta_j)/sqrt(S).

The output is exactly

  b_det=sqrt(gamma/S) b_bright+sqrt(1-gamma/S) v_eff,
  b_bright=sum_j c_j b_out,j/sqrt(gamma),

where v_eff is a normalized combination of independent tap vacua. Thus
eta_sp=gamma/S. It lies in (0,1], and equals 1 when all eta_j=1. If any
gamma_j>0 has eta_j=0, no detected single row can reproduce the complete
bright vector.

This proves maximality only among passive single rows constrained to reproduce
the SAME bright mode. A row matched instead to the attenuated displacement
vector (components sqrt(eta_j)c_j) can have larger raw displacement overlap,
but generally mixes a native dark mode into the measurement and is not the
same bright-mode channel. No global SNR or arbitrary estimator optimality
follows from eta_sp.

7. ONE-POLE PASSIVE QUANTUM FILTER AND MATCHED LO

With Fourier convention F(omega)=integral f(t) exp(i omega t)dt, B>0,

  H(omega)=B/(B-i omega),   R(omega)=1-H(omega)=(-i omega)/(B-i omega).

The transfer matrix S=[[H,R],[R,H]] is unitary on real omega because
|H|^2+|R|^2=1 and H R*+R H*=0. The causal impulse responses are

  h(t)=B exp(-Bt) 1_{t>=0},
  r(t)=delta(t)-B exp(-Bt) 1_{t>=0}.

The poles are at -iB under this Fourier convention. The two eigenchannels
have transfers 1 and (B+i omega)/(B-i omega), a direct path and a stable
first-order all-pass path. The second input port must actually be vacuum for
the added noise below to be vacuum.

Let f have unit L2 norm, be supported in [0,T], and have a whole-line zero
extension in H1 (in particular f(0)=f(T)=0). Since the adjoint transfer is
H*=B/(B+i omega),

  (H^dagger)^(-1) f  <->  (1+i omega/B) F
                            = f-f'/B.

The cross term in its norm is zero:
Re integral f*(t) f'(t)dt=0. Consequently

  J=||(H^dagger)^(-1)f||^2=1+||f'||^2/B^2,
  g=(f-f'/B)/sqrt(J),   ||g||=1,
  H^dagger g=f/sqrt(J).

The sign is consistent with the stated Fourier convention, since
F[f']=-i omega F[f]. The adjoint action in time is

  (H^dagger g)(t)=integral_t^infinity B exp[-B(s-t)]g(s)ds.

Substituting g=(f-f'/B)/sqrt(J) and integrating by parts gives f(t)/sqrt(J)
with no tail outside [0,T]. Thus g is a compactly supported, predetermined
LO mode; it is not a causal active inverse inserted in the field path.

For a one-pole filter started with an arbitrary homogeneous intracavity
amplitude at or before t=0, the free output is proportional on [0,T] to
exp(-Bt). The LO is orthogonal to it:

  integral_0^T g*(t) exp(-Bt)dt=0,

by the same integration by parts and endpoint conditions. So the pre-window
ringdown does not contaminate this selected mode. This statement is specific
to the one-pole ringdown shape and those endpoints; other internal poles or
nonzero boundary terms require a new calculation.

The mode output obeys

  b_g(out)=sqrt(eta_bw)b_f(in)+sqrt(1-eta_bw)v_eff,
  eta_bw=1/J,

because H^dagger g has norm squared 1/J and the lossless two-port relation
gives ||R^dagger g||^2=1-1/J. The vacuum mode v_eff is from the independent
second input. This is a selected-mode attenuation identity, even though the
filter also emits the input packet's response into other output modes.

If spatial collection, this filter, and homodyne detection are serial,
independent pure-loss/vacuum stages, their mode amplitudes multiply and the
effective efficiency is

  eta_total=eta_sp eta_bw eta_det.

The combined ancillary noise is vacuum with variance (1-eta_total)/2, so the
normalized homodyne record has added variance
  (1-eta_total)/(2 eta_total).
This product needs each loss port and filter input to be vacuum; thermal
ancillas, mode mismatch, frequency-dependent tap loss before the hybrid, or
classical post-homodyne filtering are outside this exact reduction.

An electronic filter acts on the already-produced classical current and its
shot noise together. It may discard information or reweight a record, but it
does not implement the quantum two-port attenuation above and cannot be
assigned eta_bw from this LO calculation.

FINDING

No exact sign, normalization, causality, endpoint, or series-efficiency defect
was found in §§6–7 under their stated assumptions. The reusable repair is to
keep the scope explicit: eta_sp is the best single-row efficiency for exact
native-bright-mode reproduction, eta_bw is an inverse-domain matched-mode
efficiency of a specific passive quantum filter, and eta_total multiplies
only for the serial vacuum-loss model. Each still requires the corresponding
physical routing, vacuum state, LO bandwidth, and calibration resources.
