DONOR UNLOADING AND PRODUCT COMMITMENT ARE DIFFERENT OPERATIONS
2026-10-24. Scoped chemical/control gate, internally challenged.
Original OH_CHECKPOINT_OPERATION_OPENING and earlier frozen sources retained.

Actual need and acquired mechanism
The desired operation is one-pot, sequence-programmed assembly whose
next instruction becomes available only after the intended preceding
chemical connection has formed. It must retain the declared product and
identity, and must account for input charging, failed coupling, advance,
release and reset. The earlier HMSD custody result does not supply such
an authorization: a body can retain its original template contact until
an incorrect partner displaces that contact through an allowed port.

Bath--Turberfield2026 supplies a much stronger actual control mechanism:
blocked instructions, competing domains, a parallel-DNA reaction center,
and strand-displacing DNA-polymerase advancement. We use their design,
not a generic assigned cage or a hypothetical protected rate. Their
main text explicitly leaves aminoacyl transfer to future work. The
DNA actuator and organic coupling must therefore be assessed separately.

The literal branch that the checkpoint cannot identify by donorOH
Let D be the upstream DNA adaptor, P the accumulated chain, A the next
adaptor and B its incoming building block. The intended step is

 D-O-C(O)-P + H2N-B-C(O)-O-A
   -> D-OH + P-C(O)-NH-B-C(O)-O-A.                 [transfer]

Water can instead produce

 D-O-C(O)-P + H2O -> D-OH + P-C(O)-OH,             [hydrolysis]

with H2N-B-C(O)-O-A still available. After the SAME downstream adaptor
A binds its instruction and performs its blocker competition step,
both branches have the donor-DNA3'OH and the same DNA control domains.
The spent donor still has to anneal to the exposed blocker-primer site;
polymerase and dNTP substrate are also required. Those requirements do
not themselves inspect the new P--B connection. Hydrolysis before A
arrives is not immediate advancement: A binding remains a separate
necessary event. Once A is bound, the DNA graph admits the alias.

The organic outcome is substantially different. Transfer retains the
whole chain on A. Donor hydrolysis releases that accumulated chain;
continuing DNA progression could carry a new, shorter chain forward.
Thus this is not merely a missing nucleotide in a DNA progress record.
No frequency, dominant mechanism, or actual full-product yield follows
from the existence of the branch.

Exact scope of the conclusion
Donor unblocking alone is not a logically sufficient witness of correct
chain transfer. This is a statement about the DNA control projection,
NOT identity of all microscopic states: the organic payload changes
sterics, geometry, binding and potentially enzyme access. A measured
payload-sensitive effect could discriminate the branches. No such
effect is supplied merely by restoration of the donor3'OH, and none is
assigned favorably here.

The primary itself reports preliminary aminoacylated DNA subsequently
used as a primer AFTER HYDROLYSIS. Its Fig5 blocker/polymerase test uses
a truncated downstream adaptor with the parallel-duplex domain deleted.
That is a DNA-actuation experiment, not a coupled organic-chain transfer
or an order-resolved synthesis experiment. The source is explicit about
that boundary; we do not attribute a full-assembler success to it.

Minimal interpretability requirement
In a two-branch experiment conditioned on the same bound A, write p for
the transfer probability, qT for DNA-advance probability after transfer,
qH for DNA-advance probability after hydrolysis, and o for observed
advance probability. Then

 o = p qT + (1-p) qH.

Without a bound on qH, advance alone supplies no positive lower bound
on p. If an acquired qH<=eta<1 applies, qT<=1 gives

 p >= max(0, (o-eta)/(1-eta)).

This is a conditional observation ledger, not a fitted reaction model;
other chemical branches require their own terms. A product-sensitive
operation, or a bound on transfer-versus-hydrolysis together with the
failure outcome, is needed before using DNA advance as a coupling
certificate. A small hydrolysis hazard can make a finite-tolerance
device work; no universal assembler impossibility is claimed.

Strong complete synthesis comparator
He--Liu2010 actually performs autonomous, isothermal, DNA-track-ordered
oligoamide synthesis. The walker carries the growing organic product;
substrate docking promotes amine acylation and allows a 10--23 DNAzyme
to cleave an RNA segment and advance. That RNA cleavage substrate is
not the newly formed amide, so the source does not supply a direct
bond-completion checkpoint. Nevertheless it supplies actual organic
products and ordered-track controls, a stronger result than DNA routing
alone. The intended triamide was estimated at about45% of detectable
DNA-linked species, with omitted-step truncations. The readout is
mass spectrometry after desalting; the product remains DNA-linked.
Those finite synthesis results cannot be dismissed because an advance
mechanism lacks a logically exact chemical certificate.

The full comparator includes activated building blocks, track, walker,
cleaved substrate fragments and leaving groups. Ordinary staged
coupling/verification and enzymatic copying/translation remain stronger
alternatives for their supported substrates. No practical advantage or
novelty is inferred for the present gate analysis.

An actual product-sensitive operation, with its paid boundary
Product connectivity can be observed physically rather than inferred
from a leaving group. Chen etal2009 validates a DNA-ligation assay in
which a competitor plus thermal denaturation/reannealing removes the
template support. Only covalently joined dye/quencher strands then
fold intramolecularly and quench; unligated strands that had occupied
the same template are explicit controls. This is an acquired example
of testing connectivity AFTER removing common support, not an abstract
claim that covalent bonds are observable.

It is a STAGED DNA-phosphodiester assay, not an autonomous organic-bond
actuator in the Bath device. Transferring that principle would require
handles retained on opposite sides of the target organic connection,
an actual removal/challenge of template-induced proximity, and a
compatible product-dependent output. Heat, competitor, sample handling
and detection are paid in the source. A split-probe/avidity proposal
without that challenge may simply assemble the same two unreacted
halves and falsely signal; no perfect proximity sensor is assumed.
No such compatible autonomous handoff is acquired by this source.

Finite-resource boundary and current scientific status
All proposed Bath adaptors are present together, so charged inputs can
age before their codons are exposed. Hydrolyzed incoming adaptors may
also have free3'OH ends; the source's alternative binding geometries do
not establish polymerase rejection of them. Charging/recharging must
not be treated as a free purifier of input or spent-adaptor states.
Pay activated-substrate/adaptor preparation, any flexizyme charging,
polymerase/dNTP consumption, blocker wastes, lost accumulated chains,
retained DNA/product labels, and release/reset. No numerical hazard,
length scaling, complete yield, or buffer transfer is supplied here.

Outcome: the named DNA actuator is real; its intended organic transfer
remains unimplemented, and donorOH/control advance does not by itself
certify it. The missing physical interface is product-sensitive
authorization or acquired chemical selectivity WITH its failure/reset
contract. The paid post-support connectivity assay is a real comparison,
not a completed new autonomous mechanism.
