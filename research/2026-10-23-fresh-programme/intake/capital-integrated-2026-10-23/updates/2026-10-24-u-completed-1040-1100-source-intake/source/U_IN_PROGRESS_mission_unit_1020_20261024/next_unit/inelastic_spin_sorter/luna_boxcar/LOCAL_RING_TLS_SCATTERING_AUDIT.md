# Local nonmagnetic ring–TLS spin sorter: port and scattering audit

24 October 2026. Bounded check of one finite local Hamiltonian, not a molecular CISS explanation, a material prediction, or an experiment.

## Hamiltonian and physical locality

Use electron sites (1,2,3), spin (sigma=\pm1), and a two-state TLS (n=0,1). With the electron-site factor first, the spin block is
\[
 H_\sigma=h_\sigma\otimes I_2+I_3\otimes\Delta N
       +\eta |3\rangle\langle3|\otimes\tau_x,
 \quad N=|1\rangle\langle1|,
\]
where
\[
 h_\sigma=\sum_{j=1}^3\epsilon_j|j\rangle\langle j|
 +t_{12}(|1\rangle\langle2|+\mathrm{h.c.})
 +t_{23}(|2\rangle\langle3|+\mathrm{h.c.})
 +t_{31}(e^{i\sigma\phi}|3\rangle\langle1|+\mathrm{h.c.}).
\]
All on-sites and scalar couplings are real. The TLS is unchanged in either contact; the two ordinary leads attach only at sites 1 and 2. In an asymptotic channel labelled (n), the electron kinetic energy is (E_n=E-n\Delta). Thus each spin sector has four scattering channels (L0,L1,R0,R1), provided both (E_n) lie inside the lead band. No arbitrary four-channel (S) matrix is assigned: the matrix below is generated by this 6-state scatterer and its local contacts.

Time reversal is (\Theta=i\sigma_y K) on spin, with (K) also complex-conjugating the real TLS basis. Since (\tau_x,N), the on-sites, and the lead/contact parameters are real, (H_-=H_+^*\) and the full electron+TLS Hamiltonian is TR invariant. The two spin blocks conserve (S_z); there is no Zeeman term, magnetic contact, or spin flip. The link (e^{i\sigma_z\phi}\) is an explicit spin-orbit hopping with opposite phases for the two spins. Its loop product is (e^{i\sigma_z\phi}\): it cannot be removed from a ring by local site rephasings, while on a tree all such commuting link phases can be gauged away. This is a time-reversal-paired spin holonomy, not an applied electromagnetic flux. Its presence is still a model input, not a measured material coefficient.

## Current-normalized contacts and one-point result

For semi-infinite nearest-neighbor leads with hopping (t_\ell\), zero onsite, and contact hopping (v\), the retarded self-energy in sideband (n) is
\[
 \Sigma_n^r(E)=\frac{v^2}{2t_\ell^2}
 \left(E_n-i\sqrt{4t_\ell^2-E_n^2}\right),\qquad
 \Gamma_n=-2\operatorname{Im}\Sigma_n^r
 =\frac{v^2}{t_\ell^2}\sqrt{4t_\ell^2-E_n^2}.
\]
These are the propagating-channel values for (|E_n|<2t_\ell). The internal retarded Green matrix is (G_\sigma^r=[EI_6-H_\sigma-\Sigma^r]^{-1}), with the diagonal self-energy placed on site 1 and site 2 for each unchanged TLS label. Let (W) embed each of (L0,L1,R0,R1) into its contact site and multiply it by \(\sqrt{\Gamma_n}\). The flux-normalized Fisher–Lee matrix is
\[
 S_\sigma=I_4-iW^\dagger G_\sigma^r W.
\]
Because both sidebands are open and the finite TLS has no omitted decay bath, each (S_\sigma) is unitary. In the common real channel-phase convention, (S_-=S_+^T).

The executable check [`spin_sorter_check.py`](spin_sorter_check.py) uses the declared dimensionless point
\[
 E=0.5,\quad\Delta=1,\quad (\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)=(0,0.3,-0.2),\quad
 t_{12}=t_{23}=t_{31}=v=t_\ell=1,\quad\eta=0.4,\quad\phi=\pi/2.
\]
Here (E_0=0.5,E_1=-0.5), so all four channels are open. The numerical checks give
\[
 \|S_\uparrow^\dagger S_\uparrow-I\|=7.25\times10^{-16},\quad
 \|S_\downarrow^\dagger S_\downarrow-I\|=9.47\times10^{-16},\quad
 \|S_\downarrow-S_\uparrow^T\|=5.78\times10^{-16}.
\]
For an unpolarized left input with TLS ground state (n=0), summing over the final TLS channels gives
\[
 T_\uparrow=0.981408114714,\qquad T_\downarrow=0.960338073682,
 \qquad P_T=\frac{T_\uparrow-T_\downarrow}{T_\uparrow+T_\downarrow}
 =0.010851078868.
\]
Resolved by final TLS state, (T_{\uparrow,n=0}=0.981404074268), (T_{\uparrow,n=1}=4.04045\times10^{-6}), while (T_{\downarrow,n=0}=0.683614161016), (T_{\downarrow,n=1}=0.276723912666). This is a finite-point spin/direction/sideband correlation. It is a small total transmitted polarization at this point; the nearly selected excited-sideband channel is only one part of the transmitted beam.

The reflected probabilities are (R_\uparrow=1-T_\uparrow=0.018591885286) and (R_\downarrow=1-T_\downarrow=0.039661926318). Thus the opposite spin imbalance sits in reflection. Since the Hamiltonian conserves (S_z), and each spin input has (T_\sigma+R_\sigma=1), the total outgoing spin for a 50:50 unpolarized input, summed over both leads and both TLS outputs, is exactly zero. The device sorts the spin content among output ports/channels; it does not generate net spin or require a spin measurement in the Hamiltonian model. A practical readout/collector is not modeled.

Three degeneracy checks for the same point return zero polarization to numerical precision: η=0 (no TLS-changing path), (t_{31}=0) (tree, no loop holonomy), and φ=0 (spin-independent hopping). These support the stated dependence for this fixture, not a general theorem about arbitrary inelastic junctions.

## Scope and strongest incumbent

This is a closed single-electron plus two-level mode scattering calculation. The TLS ground state is supplied as the input; thermal occupation, mode relaxation, repeated-shot reset, material disorder, detector energy resolution, and spin-resolved collection are outside the point calculation. A repeated sorter would need a paid TLS reset/re-preparation cycle and an output implementation. The numerical parameters are chosen dimensionless settings; they are not fitted or sourced molecular hoppings, a measured vibronic coupling, or a validated material proposal.

A close theoretical incumbent already shows that time-reversal-symmetric two-terminal systems with multiple orbital channels can have spin-resolved currents while respecting paired transmission eigenvalues: Utsumi, Entin-Wohlman, and Aharony, [*Phys. Rev. B* 102, 035445 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.102.035445). Their mechanism uses spin-flip processes accompanied by orbital flips in a two-orbital helical-chain model; this finite ring instead conserves spin and mixes TLS sidebands. The result is therefore a conventional multichannel symmetry-compatible sorting construction, with no priority claim.

A distinct material caution is Upadhyay and Levy, [*J. Chem. Phys.* 164, 164303 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0324706): their self-consistent NEGF/SCBA study of helical tight-binding models finds negligible energy-integrated polarization in the weak electron–phonon regime they analyze. That does not calculate this three-site, closed two-level ring and is not a theorem against it. It does block extrapolating a finite toy parameter point to a sizable molecular CISS effect or a material advantage.
