# Primary prior check: inelastic spin sorter

## Scope and strongest boundary

The strongest exact comparator is already the general two-terminal, time-reversal-symmetric, multichannel spin-filtering result of Utsumi, Entin-Wohlman, and Aharony, [Phys. Rev. B 102, 035445 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.102.035445), [arXiv:2005.04041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.04041). It gives a unitary fixed-energy scattering matrix and finite z-spin conductance without magnetic contacts when each lead has two orbital channels. In its helical tight-binding realization, intra-atomic spin-orbit coupling flips spin together with orbital channel; the leads are spin-degenerate and the contact tunneling preserves spin and orbital labels. This owns the broad operation “TRS two-terminal spin filtering when a second channel is available.” Its extra channel is an elastic orbital mode, however: no electron energy changes, no initially-ground TLS, no phonon/TLS excitation probabilities or reset, and no excitation-energy lower bound are calculated. The proposed TLS construction should therefore be framed as a specific energy-resolved inelastic realization and its accounting theorem, not as the first general multichannel TRS filter.

## Closest vibrational CISS operation

Fransson, [“Vibrational origin of exchange splitting and chiral-induced spin selectivity,” Phys. Rev. B 102, 235416 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.102.235416), [open manuscript](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1521748/FULLTEXT01.pdf), is direct vibrational-spin-selectivity ancestry. Its phonon coupling is not a scalar Holstein term alone: the model includes spin-dependent electron-phonon coupling arising from SOC, which the Lang-Firsov transformation turns into spin-dependent onsite energies and exchange terms; the helical model also has phonon-assisted hopping and phonon-assisted SOC. The transport calculation couples the molecule to a ferromagnetic lead and a simple metallic lead, and extracts spin selectivity by reversing the left-lead polarization. It uses an equilibrium phonon self-energy rather than a complete unitary lead-times-phonon scattering matrix. The authors also plot an induced molecular spin current for nonmagnetic leads, but that is not a report of a ground-state-input, spin-polarized transmitted packet with every reflected and excited-phonon output counted. No per-electron excitation-cost theorem is given.

## Current weak-coupling benchmark

Upadhyay and Levy, [J. Chem. Phys. 164, 164303 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0324706), [arXiv:2601.15063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15063), re-evaluate vibrationally assisted CISS with self-consistent NEGF/SCBA. Their calculated proxy is the change in steady particle current when the left spin-polarized analyzer lead is reversed (spin-dependent broadening `Gamma^L_{up,down} = Gamma_0(1 +/- p)`, with `p = +/-0.5`), not spin-resolved transmission between two ordinary nonmagnetic leads. The setup has Fermi reservoirs at `mu_L = V/2` and `mu_R = -V/2` and integrates currents over energy; it includes a local Holstein-like interaction together with spin-dependent hopping/SOC terms. Phonon occupations are fixed at the Bose distribution `n_B(omega_0,T)`; nonequilibrium phonon heating is omitted. Their finite-linewidth variant phenomenologically broadens the phonon spectrum while retaining thermal occupations, so it is not an explicit reset dynamics. They do calculate an energy-resolved phonon-bath current and check current conservation, but derive no bound relating transmitted spin polarization to excited-mode probability or energy cost. Their result is a negative benchmark for weak-coupling steady-state CISS in this model class, not a test of the proposed finite TLS scattering problem.

## Decision boundary

The inspected sources do not combine the proposed ingredients: spin-conserving SOC ring, scalar local TLS coupling, nonmagnetic single-orbital leads, ground-state TLS input, fixed-total-energy electron-plus-TLS output channels, and a bound charging spin polarization against TLS excitation in the reflected branch. They do establish the two neighboring ideas—elastic TRS filtering by extra orbital channels, and vibrationally mediated CISS models with spin-dependent couplings and/or magnetic analyzers. This comparison is not a novelty clearance. In particular, any claimed cost bound must be stated for the explicit scattering assumptions and output convention; neither the citations nor their absence establishes a general thermodynamic heat/work lower bound.
