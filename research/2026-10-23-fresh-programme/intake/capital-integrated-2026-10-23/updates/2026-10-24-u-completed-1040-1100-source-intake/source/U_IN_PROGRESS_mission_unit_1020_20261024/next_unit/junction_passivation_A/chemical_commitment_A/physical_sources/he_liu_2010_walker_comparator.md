# He–Liu DNA-walker synthesis comparator

He and Liu, “Autonomous Multistep Organic Synthesis in a Single Isothermal Solution Mediated by a DNA Walker,” *Nature Nanotechnology* 5 (2010), 778–782, DOI [10.1038/nnano.2010.190](https://doi.org/10.1038/nnano.2010.190), PMCID [PMC2974042](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2974042/). I reviewed the official PMC full-text material returned by native search. The direct page presented a browser verification screen and direct official API retrieval did not yield a local copy, so no local primary PDF/XML is claimed.

## What the experiment actually does

The DNAsome uses a sequence-coded ssDNA track, an initiator, three DNA-linked substrates, and a DNA walker. Each substrate presents an amino-acid NHS ester, docking regions, two internal ribonucleotides, and an anticodon that places it at its track station. The walker carries a 3′ amine and a 10–23 RNA-cleaving DNAzyme. On hybridization at a station, the walker’s amine is positioned to acylate from the substrate’s NHS ester, transferring that amino-acid building block onto the walker; DNAzyme cleavage of the substrate’s rN-containing segment releases its 5′ fragment and enables the next step. The article describes both acylation and cleavage as consequences of walker–substrate assembly. The DNAzyme’s substrate is the rN-containing nucleic-acid junction, not the newly formed amide, so cleavage is not shown as a product-bond sensor. The main-text mechanism supplies no coupling that makes cleavage conditional on successful amide transfer. Figure 2 includes a no-NHS-ester control, but the accessible description does not make it a time-resolved test of cleavage conditioned on transfer; the extent of advance following a transfer failure is not directly quantified.

This is a substantial actual synthesis comparator, not merely a DNA-routing demonstration. The walker is covalently extended through successive amide-forming transfers, and the mass spectra identify the DNA-linked organic products. A three-step sequence programmed by track order gave the intended triamide as the major product, estimated at about 45% of detectable DNA-linked species; the paper also reports omitted-step truncation products. Reordered tracks gave products corresponding to their encoded order, while omission of the track yielded a complex mixture. The readout is high-resolution mass spectrometry after desalting, not a bare, detached organic product or a per-step checkpoint trace.

## Relevance and boundary

The closest relevant mechanism is DNAzyme cleavage used to advance a walker through an autonomous sequence of organic reactions. It shows that an actuator can be coupled spatially to a reaction center and that real ordered organic products can be measured. It does **not** establish that cleavage verifies bond formation: the cleavage recognition element and the aminoacyl-transfer chemistry are distinct, and the source does not report a direct conditional readout resolving transfer success before advance. Thus it is an incumbent for autonomous multistep synthesis, but it does not close the donor-3′-OH alias in the Bath–Turberfield proposal.

The operation is reported as a single isothermal solution after component preparation and combination, with no intervention during the walking/synthesis phase. The complete material boundary still includes the DNA track, initiator, walker/DNAzyme, each activated amino-acid substrate, cleaved substrate fragments, and NHS leaving groups. The product remains walker-DNA-linked; no walker/template regeneration or release of an isolated organic product is demonstrated. The reported product mixture and truncations are part of the comparator, not a claim of perfect fidelity.
