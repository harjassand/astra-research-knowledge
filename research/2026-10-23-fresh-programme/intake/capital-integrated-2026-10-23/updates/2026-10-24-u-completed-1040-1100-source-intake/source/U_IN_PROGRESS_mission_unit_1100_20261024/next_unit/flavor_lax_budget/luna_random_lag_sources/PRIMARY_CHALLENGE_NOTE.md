## The decisive source boundaries

1. **Fiorillo & Raffelt, “Slow and fast collective neutrino oscillations: Invariants and reciprocity,” PRD 107, 043024 (2023), corrected v3; erratum PRD 113, 069901(E) (2026).** [Corrected arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.09650v3), [published article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.043024), [APS erratum](https://doi.org/10.1103/js9s-fkfp). For the fast two-flavor equations, the paper gives the standard Lax vector and its evolution in Eqs. (19)–(20): away from an original discrete velocity, the Lax vector precesses and its complex square is invariant; at an original velocity, Gaudin invariants apply. Sec. III.3 explicitly describes the continuous family in spectral parameter. This is the closest direct ancestry for the gate's Cauchy-transform invariant. The paper does not give the gate's relaxed continuum set `K_G`, its conversion objective, or an all-time strict maximum gap. The 2026 note/erratum states that the two-flavor conclusions remain unchanged; the correction is material to the three-flavor invariant discussion, so use v3 plus the erratum rather than the original v1/v2 text.

2. **Fiorillo & Raffelt, “Flavor solitons in dense neutrino gases,” PRD 107, 123024 (2023), v2.** [Corrected arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12143v2), [published article](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.123024). The homogeneous infinite-speed limit is the fast-flavor pendulum “except that it swings only once instead of being periodic” (abstract); the paper also says the solitons are fragile because the asymptotic homogeneous state is unstable. This owns the exact one-pulse/one-swing result and its instability boundary. It does not bound later multimode evolution or conversion across all times. The capital dossier properly treats its first-pulse gap as narrower than the present gate.

3. **Fiorillo & Raffelt, “Quasilinear Theory of Fast Flavor Instabilities,” PRL 137, 171001 (published 21 Oct 2026).** [APS article](https://doi.org/10.1103/f1dk-yk6g), [full arXiv manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28854). This is the strongest relaxation-side comparison, but the model differs. The underlying QKE has spatial transport; QLT tracks spatial Fourier flavor waves around a slowly evolving homogeneous diagonal background and uses an adiabatic/random-phase closure. In its resonant prediction, affected angular bands relax toward `D_v=0`; the improved QLT includes off-resonant flavomon absorption and conserves the total integrated DLN. Its checks are numerical periodic-box QKE comparisons (100 velocity points, 700 spatial points, 20 random-phase realizations), with best agreement for weak instability. QLT omits wave–wave interactions; the QKE shows spectral broadening absent from QLT, and the authors caution that random-phase assumptions can fail for symmetric flavor pendula. It predicts a useful quasi-steady relaxation pattern, not an exact all-time theorem for the homogeneous length-preserving ODE or the gate's fixed-reference `C(D)` on `K_G`. Thus it neither contradicts nor establishes the gate. It does warn against presenting the homogeneous obstruction as a general statement about spatially fluctuating or coarse-grained relaxation.
