ASTRA — EXACT QUANTUM AGGREGATE-COUNT / CIR INTERTWINING
Independent derivation and check, 25 October 2026

STATUS AND PURPOSE
==================
This is an exact quotient of a specified quantum instrument. It upgrades the
gamma-root filter's aggregate-count corollary: for this interface, no quartic
diffusion bridge allowance is needed. It does not represent the full quantum
conditional state, resolve port marks, or solve the angular covariance test.
No novelty-priority claim is made for the Poisson intertwining.

Notation is deliberately separated:
  K = physical total photon number, sum_j a_j^dagger a_j;
  S = nonnegative classical CIR variable, in scaled intensity units;
  J = auxiliary gamma-mixture index used by the root filter.
In particular, J is not the physical photon number K.

1. PHYSICAL ASSUMPTIONS AND OBSERVATIONS
======================================
There are m bosonic modes, with m a positive integer, and theta,kappa,nu>0.
The supplied quantum evolution is

  d rho/dt = -i[H_t,rho]
      + sum_j [b D[a_j^dagger]rho + d D[a_j]rho],
  D[L]rho = L rho L^dagger - {L^dagger L,rho}/2,
  b=kappa nu/theta, d=b+kappa.

The Hamiltonian is well defined for the stated evolution and [H_t,K]=0.
It can include arbitrarily strong cross-Kerr terms, number-conserving hopping
and other interactions; the conclusion below follows from this commutator,
not from small nonlinear coupling. The bath coefficients remain constant and
isotropic. We assume the usual conservative evolution; the induced linear
birth-death number process is nonexplosive.

The observer receives every time of an aggregate detected loss, with no port
label. The aggregate count instrument is

  J_t(rho)=theta c_t sum_j a_j rho a_j^dagger,
  0<=c_t<=c_max, theta c_t<=d.

It is physically a monitored fraction of the existing loss bath. The
remaining unobserved loss coefficient is d-theta c_t. A complete unitary
mixing of the monitored mode outputs leaves this aggregate map unchanged,
provided the observer pools all those outputs. Monitoring only one mixed
output generally gives a different instrument and is excluded.

The control c_t is known and predictable from the received aggregate record
and any shared independent classical randomness. H_t may follow the same
controller, but must continue to commute with K. A number-preserving instantaneous feedback
unitary after a count also leaves the argument unchanged. Hidden port data
may not silently determine an unknown c_t.

The initial supplied preparation is the product thermal state with mean
nu/theta photons per mode. More generally, the same aggregate conclusion
holds for any initial density with this same distribution of K, regardless
of unobserved internal coherences. Arbitrary initial number distributions
need not admit the positive Poisson-mixture representation used below.

2. EXACT CLOSED NUMBER INSTRUMENT
================================
For a scalar function f of K, the adjoint quantum generator satisfies

  L^* f(K) = b(K+m)[f(K+1)-f(K)]
              +d K[f(K-1)-f(K)].

The Hamiltonian term vanishes. The identities behind this formula are
sum_j a_j^dagger f(K)a_j = K f(K-1) and
sum_j a_j f(K)a_j^dagger = (K+m)f(K+1).
They show that the closed instrument does not require a diagonal conditional
density or an assumption that the modes remain independent.

The detected-count adjoint is
  J_t^* f(K)=theta c_t K f(K-1).
Thus a detected physical photon is actually removed: K goes to K-1.

Let F(z)=Tr(z^K rho) for an unnormalized conditional density. The corresponding
forward number generator and count map are

  L_N F = b(z-1)[z F'(z)+m F(z)] + d(1-z)F'(z),
  J_N,t F = theta c_t F'(z).

Between observed counts the unnormalized update is L_N-J_N,t. At a count
it is J_N,t. Evaluating F(1) gives the likelihood mass of the record branch.
These equations close for every branch of the acquired aggregate history.

3. POISSON KERNEL AND GENERATOR IDENTITIES
========================================
Introduce the probability kernel

  K | S=s ~ Poisson(s/theta),
  P_s(z)=exp[(s/theta)(z-1)].

The classical signal generator is
  L_S f(s)=kappa(m nu-s)f'(s)+kappa nu s f''(s),
equivalently
  dS=kappa(m nu-S)dt+sqrt(2 kappa nu S)dW.

A direct calculation gives

  L_N P_s(z)
    =[kappa m nu(z-1)/theta
       -kappa s(z-1)/theta
       +kappa nu s(z-1)^2/theta^2] P_s(z)
    =L_S P_s(z),

  J_N,t P_s(z)=theta c_t partial_z P_s(z)=c_t s P_s(z).

Both identities are exact, including the count map. In particular, the
classical Cox count does not jump S; its likelihood multiplies the density
by c_t s. This matches physical photon subtraction because Poisson size bias
followed by removal of one photon returns the same Poisson kernel:
  k Pr(Poisson(lambda)=k)/lambda
is the law of 1+Poisson(lambda), with lambda=s/theta.

For a finite nonnegative measure mu on S, let
  P mu(z)=integral P_s(z) mu(ds).
Integration of the identities gives the unnormalized instrument relations

  (L_N-J_N,t) P mu = P (L_S^* mu-c_t s mu),
  J_N,t P mu = P (c_t s mu).

The kernel preserves total mass because P_s(1)=1. Probability generating
functions on 0<=z<=1 determine the corresponding finite measures on K.
Consequently these are identities of number instruments, including branch
likelihoods, rather than merely identities of first or second moments.

4. INITIALIZATION, TIME EVOLUTION AND FEEDBACK
============================================
Initially take S~Gamma(m,rate=1/nu). Its Poisson mixture has generating
function

  integral P_s(z) Gamma(m,1/nu)(ds)
       =[1+(nu/theta)(1-z)]^(-m)
       =[(1-r)/(1-r z)]^m, r=nu/(nu+theta)=b/d.

This is exactly the total photon distribution of the stated product thermal
preparation, a sum of m independent geometric photon numbers.

The no-count semigroups and jump maps inherit the generator identities.
One may establish this by uniqueness of the nonexplosive birth-death/CIR
equations, or by concatenating the Feynman-Kac no-count propagators with the
jump multiplication maps. Bounded c_t ensures finite aggregate counts on
finite intervals: the CIR path is continuous with finite integral of S, and
the physical detected rate is a fraction of a nonexplosive linear loss rate.

For an adapted controller, condition on a particular observed history and
shared random seed. The controls on the next no-count segment are then known
time-dependent coefficients. The same instrument identity applies, followed
by the same update at its next observed count. Induction over the record and
nonexplosion prove the joint equality in law

  (quantum aggregate count record through T, terminal K)
  = (CIR Cox count record through T, Poisson(S_T/theta)),

where the terminal Poisson draw is conditional on S_T. The same equality
holds for branch likelihoods and the conditional terminal distribution.
This is a generative representation of the observed quotient; it does not
assert that S is an additional physically measured coordinate or that the
whole photon-number path is conditionally independent Poisson draws.

5. DIRECT USE OF THE GAMMA-ROOT REPRESENTATION
============================================
Suppose the acquired classical posterior is
  S | J=j, record ~ Gamma(m+j,rate=beta),
and the auxiliary-index generating function is
  P_J(w)=exp[lambda(w-1)] product_i(1-p_i+p_i w).

Then the exact conditional physical total-number PGF is

  w(z)=beta theta/(beta theta+1-z),
  F_K(z | record)=w(z)^m P_J(w(z)).

Equivalently, each gamma component maps to a negative-binomial distribution
with shape m+j and success parameter beta theta/(1+beta theta). This formula
turns the stored root representation directly into a normalized physical
number prediction. J, its Poisson factor and its Bernoulli factors are
computational coordinates and are not additional measurement outcomes.

If a compressed root filter has a proved joint total-variation error
  TV(law(record,S_T), approximate_law(record,S_T))<=epsilon,
application of the same terminal Poisson kernel contracts total variation:

  TV(law_quantum(record,K), approximate_law(record,K))<=epsilon.

There is no extra quartic-diffusion bridge allowance in this statement. For
example, the fixed-threshold root-pruning theorem with threshold eta gives
the physical bound 2 c_max m nu T eta^2, with exactly the same hypotheses and
all its existing precision and runtime qualifications. Any separately
proved finite-precision error must still be added. The corresponding
anytime schedule transfers in the same way wherever its classical theorem
applies. The Poisson output kernel itself is exact; a finite sampler must
separately control its arithmetic and sampling implementation.

6. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT CONTROL
====================================
The reduction is independent of the strength or internal complexity of H_t,
but this has a precise physical reason: [H_t,K]=0 makes H_t incapable of
steering the distribution of K. In this quotient, c_t changes readout
efficiency and the acquired information; it does not change the unconditional
isotropic bath evolution. This is an exact predictive and sensor-control
result for the specified aggregate interface, not a general result about
controlling nonlinear quantum observables.

Port labels, interference observables, individual-mode numbers under general
hopping, and the full conditional quantum state remain outside this closure.
For those questions the retained covariance correction can change permitted
predictions, and the angular challenges remain. Fine aggregate count times
also supply more information than a bin's first-port latch. The root filter
cannot be applied to that coarser record by inventing its unobserved times;
the separate coarsening obstruction remains in force.

The physical preparation, constant bath model, detector calibration and
known controls are supplied assumptions here. This proof does not learn
them from an unspecified interface and does not make a hardware claim.
