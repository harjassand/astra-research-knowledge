BUILDING RARE-EVENT CERTIFICATES FROM LOCAL CALIBRATION
Lead constructive invariant, 2026-10-24. No novelty or device claim.
This concerns acquisition; no h, rare-event probability, or exact global
transition table is supplied as input.

Structured engineering model and actual input
A finite-horizon stochastic network is given as a topologically ordered
blueprint of M local gates. Wires take q values. A gate reads at most a
earlier wires, writes a new wire, and has an unknown row distribution
K_v(output | parent values). Known deterministic logic is allowed. The
event is a declared final bit/flag computed by gates included in M.
The initial frontier is a specified configuration; any random initial
variables must instead be source gates included in the blueprint and M.
The interaction/noise architecture is a MODEL PREMISE: at each gate use,
fresh private randomness is conditionally independent of all earlier
randomness given the parents. Shared environments must be represented
as extra parent/state wires, rather than assumed away after calibration.

For each local parent configuration the caller may prepare that component
configuration, obtain an independent outcome from its unchanged row,
and reset for another call. All these component preparations/calls have
their stated cost C_row; there is no free restoration of a hidden GLOBAL
state. This local intervention/simulator interface is stronger than
observing original whole-network trajectories. Each row entry is either
exactly 0 or at least a known p_min>0. The blueprint may give the support,
but this is not necessary: the SAME sample event below also learns it
with confidence 1-delta. The gap p_min, source independence and unchanged
rows remain premises, not facts established by these samples.
Arbitrarily tiny unknown positive probabilities are excluded.

The supplied execution order has a VERIFIABLE causal-frontier width w:
after any prefix at most w wire values are still needed by a later gate
or by the final event. Keep those values, then forget dead wires. Parents
are present when needed. Checking this schedule uses the known wire-use
list; discovering an optimal schedule is not a free subroutine.
The state is (gate index, values of these live wires), at most M q^w
possibilities. This is an acquired explicit representation bound, not an
assumption that the full physical network has only q^w microscopic states.

1. Paid local calibration
There are at most R=M q^a unknown rows and D=M q^(a+1) row entries.
Take N independent samples of each row and use its empirical distribution
hat K. Known deterministic rows need no sampling. For 0<eta<1, choosing

 N >= 3/(p_min eta^2) log(2D/delta)

gives, simultaneously on every allowed entry, with probability >=1-delta,

 (1-eta) K <= hat K <= (1+eta) K.                    (1)

This is multiplicative Chernoff plus a finite union bound. A true zero
entry is empirically zero with probability one. On (1), each true positive
entry is empirically positive. Thus the empirical support is the TRUE
support on that same event, even if it was initially unknown. All
support-based zero reachability claims below are correspondingly valid
with confidence 1-delta. The empirical rows are normalized distributions.
The total acquisition count is at most M q^a N, including configurations
that may be extremely rare in the original global process. Their paid
preparability, not original visitation, licenses this count.

2. Central invariant: nonnegative messages preserve multiplicative bounds
Let sigma be a frontier assignment before gate k, and F_k(sigma,b) the
known next-frontier update when its output is b. Compute an EXACT rational
backward table for the empirical model:

 hat h_M(sigma)=1{declared event holds},
 hat h_k(sigma)=sum_b hat K_k(b|parents(sigma))
                         hat h_(k+1)(F_k(sigma,b)).        (2)

Here h_true,k means the FUTURE event probability from a specified complete
causal frontier. Fresh future noises make this one future path sum; no
Bayes posterior normalization by a rare past-evidence probability occurs.
Arbitrary posterior queries A|B are not this interface and would require
bounding both numerator and denominator (with the corresponding extra
error budget). Any needed past-event flag must be kept in the frontier.

Define the two computable rational tables

 U_k=(1-eta)^[-(M-k)] hat h_k,
 L_k=(1+eta)^[-(M-k)] hat h_k.                           (3)

The same scale is allowed at known deterministic gates; it is conservative.
On the SINGLE event (1), for every frontier state and every k,

 U_k >= sum_b K_k(b|parents) U_(k+1)(F_k(sigma,b)),
 L_k <= sum_b K_k(b|parents) L_(k+1)(F_k(sigma,b)).        (4)

These follow directly from K<=hat K/(1-eta) and K>=hat K/(1+eta), with
nonnegative next messages. Backward induction, with the exact terminal
indicator, gives

 L_k(sigma) <= h_true,k(sigma) <= U_k(sigma).             (5)

This holds simultaneously even for rare reachable frontier assignments:
there is no union bound over global states and no division by their
visitation probabilities. When hat h_k>0, the COMPUTED bound ratio is

 U_k/L_k=[(1+eta)/(1-eta)]^(M-k).                        (6)

If hat h_k=0, support recovery on (1) gives true probability zero too.
With eta=epsilon/(8M), 0<epsilon<1, the right side of (6) is <=1+epsilon
(use log[(1+eta)/(1-eta)]<=4eta and exp(epsilon/2)<=1+epsilon).
Thus these are high-confidence global super/subsolution certificates,
BUILT from local samples, with nearly matching lower/upper scales.
They are not deterministic certificates that unknown probabilities equal
their empirical values. Their validity probability is 1-delta.

3. Building cost and what is actually obtained
There are O(M q^w) table entries and at most q successors/entry. The
construction uses O(M q^(w+1)) rational additions/multiplications after
acquisition. Uniform sample count N makes empirical denominators common;
rational message heights grow polynomially in M, log N and the encoded
eta/known-parameter bit sizes. No exponentially long trajectory product
or exact-real oracle is used. A coarse total acquisition count is

 O(M^3 q^a / (p_min epsilon^2) log(M q^(a+1)/delta)).

Here N times the row count counts source CALLS. Include all preparation,
reset and readout in C_row once, together with polynomial blueprint,
frontier-update, rational-arithmetic and table-access costs.
For fixed q,a and w=O(log M), p_min inverse-polynomial, and polynomial
C_row, the full acquisition time is polynomial in M, epsilon^-1,
log(1/delta) and the encoded parameter sizes. This is not polynomial in
log(1/epsilon) for an arbitrarily tiny requested relative tolerance.
The finite blueprint supplies an exact remaining-horizon time bound
M-k, rather than importing a spectral/absorption-time oracle.

At the start, (5) directly gives a relative rare-event probability
interval, even if that probability is exponentially small. DIRECT
MESSAGE PASSING IS THE STRONGER CONVENTIONAL OUTPUT; there is no reason
to run importance sampling after already computing this interval.
For exact, explicitly supplied row tables, the same DP computes h and p
exactly. For sampled UNKNOWN rows, the computed U/L are valid with the
declared confidence; they do not implement an EXACT true-model Doob
kernel, because its row probabilities are still unknown. No conditional
physical sampler is claimed from finite calibration alone.

4. Why the representation, not just local data count, matters
The positivity/factorization invariant supplies all-state confidence, but
does not make the exponentially many messages cheap if w is large.
The next structural test is whether spatial locality or a sparse physical
coupling graph itself forces small causal width. It does not automatically:
past wire values can remain necessary over a long computation. The
separate path-local SAT construction tests this sharply, with all local
probabilities already known and no tiny unknown row entries.

The result does NOT acquire the gate/noise architecture from local row
samples. Independent fair gates and a shared fair latent bit can have
the same individually calibrated rows but radically different joint
failure probabilities; the latter is outside the fresh-noise blueprint.
Without known support or a gap/testing contract, finite calibration
cannot certify an exact unknown zero against arbitrary tiny positives.
Here the known p_min gap and the declared failure probability provide
that statistical contract; they do not provide deterministic certainty.
