ONE PAID OVERDAMPED DIFFUSION REALIZATION -- FIXED MODEL, NOT A RESULT

1. Physical constitutive contract

The working medium is one overdamped Brownian coordinate on a circle of
length 3, with mobility mu=1, temperature kBT=1, and diffusion D=1:

  dX = [F - partial_x U_H(X,h)]dt + sqrt(2)dB,
  F = ln(3/64)/3,
  U_H(x,h) = H V(x) + v(x) - h f(x),
  V(x) = [1-cos(2 pi x)]/2.

The potential U_H and f are periodic.  F is an explicit nonconservative
force, and its work is a separate positive-input port when the drift runs
with F.  No hidden bath reset, uncharged saddle control, or stationary
preparation is part of the contract.  This is a stipulated overdamped
constitutive model; it is not a microscopic derivation or a device claim.

2. Fully specified offsets and one finite barrier

On the lift x in [0,3], interpolate w=v-Fx.  Nodes x=j/2 have values

  w = [0, 0, ln(4/25), ln160, ln(8/15), ln(1600/3), ln(64/3)],
  f = [1, 1/2, 0, 0, 0, 1/2, 1].

On half interval j let t=2x-j, S(t)=10t^3-15t^4+6t^5.  Set
w=w_j+(w_{j+1}-w_j)S(t).  Set f=f_j+(f_{j+1}-f_j)R(t), where
R=0 for t<=2/5, R=S(5t-2) for 2/5<t<3/5, R=1 for t>=3/5.
Thus f is exactly constant on radius 1/5 cores around each well and saddle.
Use the C^2 periodic v=w+Fx.  In particular w(3)-w(0)=-3F, not zero.

The tilted drift potential Phi=U_H-Fx=HV+w-hf has critical nodes exactly
at j/2 for every |h|<=.3, with second derivative +/-2 pi^2 H.  A sufficient
uniform no-extra-critical-point condition is

  H pi > (40/9) ln1000 + (45/16)/sin(2pi/5).

Indeed S'(t)/sin(pi t)<=20/9, max|delta w|=ln1000.  The f derivative is
supported at t in [.4,.6], where sin(pi t)>=sin(2pi/5), and has maximum
10*(1/2)*(15/8).  Multiplication by |h|<=.3 gives 45/16.  This bounds
the two perturbing slopes by the leading H pi sin(pi t) on every interval.
H=12 suffices even using pi>3, ln1000<7, sin(2pi/5)>.9:
the right side is less than 280/9+25/8<36<12pi.

ONE selected finite-barrier test uses H=12, h(t)=.3cos(.01 kappa_H t),
kappa_H=pi H exp(-H).  Its period is tau_H=200 exp(H)/H, about 2.71e6
physical time units.  The barrier, waveform, and profiles are not to be
retuned after a work-sign result.  Numerical spatial/time convergence is
required; this file contains no finite-H sign assertion.

3. Committed-transition limit, and exactly what remains to prove

Frozen 1D committed Eyring-Kramers rates have leading prefactor kappa_H.
The six baseline exponential factors exp(w_i-w_s) are exactly the column
generator offdiagonals

  Q0=(1/3000)[[-3120,480,3],[3000,-483,10],[120,3,-13]].

The saddle f value is the arithmetic midpoint of its neighboring well
values.  The rate multiplier is therefore exp[h(f_target-f_source)/2],
the extracting CTMC's stipulated geometric action.  The loop affinity
is 3F=ln(3/64), also fixed.  Killing at a saddle instead measures a first
hitting rate with twice the leading committed-transition prefactor; an
uncorrected killed-at-saddle QSD flux is NOT this transition rate.

These frozen statements do not alone prove the driven periodic density,
winding current, or full work convergence over tau_H=O(exp(H)/H).  The
needed gate is a relative rate/reduced-law error uniform in |h|<=.3 and
on a complete fixed slow-time period, plus control of the periodic law.
An absolute O(1/H) physical-time generator error is inadequate.  The
forcing is slow relative to intrawell equilibration, but has fixed speed
relative to interwell switching; no instantaneous stationary replacement
of the populations is allowed.

If the periodic well populations converge uniformly in slow time and
the probability outside the flat f cores vanishes, the exact field work
converges to the CTMC value: its error is bounded by total variation of h
(1.2) times the uniform observable error.  No factor tau_H multiplies
this work error.  A separate current estimate is needed for force work.
The existing CTMC periodic work-ON enclosure is strictly negative,
[-.000843880568,-.000648656748]; using that margin requires an actual
quantitative continuum error bound or the finite-H full PDE calculation.

4. Inclusive continuous energy and entropy ledger

For density rho, j=(F-U_x)rho-rho_x and rho_t=-j_x, normalized on [0,3),

  P_control = -hdot integral f rho dx,
  P_force   = F integral j dx,
  Q_bath,out rate = integral (F-U_x)j dx,
  d<E_U>/dt = P_control+P_force-Q_bath,out rate.

The full f is integrated across every barrier and transition strip.
The mean unwrapped displacement rate is integral j dx, so force work
counts winding rather than merely a housekeeping time surcharge.  With
S=-integral rho ln rho, Sdot+Q_bath,out rate=integral j^2/rho dx>=0.
At the unique periodic local density, DeltaE=DeltaS=0, hence

  W_control+W_force=Q_bath,out=Sigma>=0.

Negative W_control is a force-to-field transducer, not net extraction
from a single bath.  The current, force work, full field work, periodic
closure, heat, entropy, and numerical errors must all be reported.

5. Power, preparation, observation, and microscopic limits

An O(1) output per slow-clock cycle has physical power O(kappa_H),
which vanishes as H grows at fixed mobility/temperature.  Barrier height
and maximum restoring force grow with H.  There is no bounded-hardware
or positive-limiting-power claim.  A real fixed-mass, finite-memory bath
need not remain overdamped/white as intrawell time O(1/H) shrinks; the
mathematical high-H limit requires that constitutive assumption, or an
additional joint microscopic scale limit.  Finite H still needs actual
mass/friction/bath calibration for physical use.

A periodic density is not a free initial preparation.  A test must pay
or bound the warm-up; irreducibility proves existence/uniqueness, not a
short startup.  Maintaining force F, the field controller, observation,
and apparatus losses outside the medium are distinct resource costs.

The flat f=1 well core contains continuously many microstates.  A basin
herald is not the discrete unique A state used by the earlier exact
atomic-return null test.  That test remains discrete here.  Approximate
intrawell preparation, observation, and accumulated null-test errors
would require a new paid argument; a point event is not a replacement.

Conventional Kramers theory, stochastic pumps, driven Brownian baths,
and chemomechanical engines are strong comparators.  No novelty or
device-performance claim is made by specifying this construction.
