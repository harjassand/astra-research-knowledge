# Periodic metastability and full-ledger gate

## Finding

The high-barrier construction matches the intended six rates at *frozen*
control, and the chosen control scale is slow relative to intrawell
relaxation. That makes the time-dependent three-state limit plausible.
Frozen Eyring–Kramers rates alone, however, do not prove convergence of the
periodic diffusion law, its control work, or its mean fuel/winding. The
decisive missing result is a phase-uniform dynamic reduction with a
uniform current estimate for the selected `A=0.3`, `Omega_slow=0.01`
cycle. A separate static-resolvent route can establish a narrower negative-
work existence result at sufficiently small amplitude and sufficiently
small physical frequency, as recorded in `LOW_FREQUENCY_FRICTION.md`; it
does not certify this selected cycle or its full-cycle fuel. This file does
not claim the selected-cycle theorem for the model or a physical device.

## Frozen-rate construction

Use the SDE on the length-3 circle and its unwrapped lift
\[
dX_t=[F-V_H'(X_t)-v'(X_t)+h_{phys}(t)f'(X_t)]dt+\sqrt{2}\,dB_t,
\quad V_H(x)=\tfrac H2(1-\cos 2\pi x),\quad F=\tfrac13\log(3/64).
\]
Set `w=v-Fx` on the lift. Then `w(x+3)=w(x)-3F`, `v=w+Fx` is
periodic, and the drift is `-V_H'-w'+h_phys f'`. Let
`S(t)=10t^3-15t^4+6t^5`. On half-cell `[j/2,(j+1)/2]`, with
`t=2x-j`, interpolate `w` between the node values
`[0,0,log(4/25),log160,log(8/15),log(1600/3),log(64/3)]`
for `j=0,...,6`. Interpolate `f` from node values
`[1,1/2,0,0,0,1/2,1]` using `R(t)=0` for `t<=0.4`,
`R(t)=S(5t-2)` for `0.4<t<0.6`, and `R(t)=1` for `t>=0.6`.
Both profiles have zero first and second derivatives at every node; `f`
is flat within distance `0.2` of each. Thus the effective lifted potential
`V_H+w-hf` has exact node curvatures `+2 pi^2 H` at wells and
`-2 pi^2 H` at saddles. Bounded `w''` and `f''` ensure no additional
extrema for all sufficiently large `H`; no sharper finite threshold is
used here.
Use probability columns, `dot p=Qp`, so `Q_{j,i}` is the rate for
`i->j`.
The control is `h_phys(t)=h(kappa_H t)`,
`h(tau)=0.3 cos(0.01 tau)`,
`kappa_H=pi H exp(-H)`, and one slow cycle has length `P=200 pi`.
At a minimum and saddle, the leading curvatures are respectively
`2 pi^2 H` and `-2 pi^2 H`; hence the one-dimensional Eyring–Kramers
prefactor is `sqrt(2 pi^2 H * 2 pi^2 H)/(2 pi)=pi H`.

For a frozen field in `|h|<=0.3`, the local barrier formula on the lift gives
\[
\frac{k^H_{i\to i+1}(h)}{\kappa_H}
\longrightarrow e^{-[w(s_i)-w(x_i)]+h[f(s_i)-f(x_i)]}
=R_i e^{h(f_{i+1}-f_i)/2},
\]
and the reverse rate across the same edge is
\[
\frac{k^H_{i+1\to i}(h)}{\kappa_H}
\longrightarrow e^{-[w(s_i)-w(x_{i+1})]+h[f(s_i)-f(x_{i+1})]}
=L_{i+1}e^{h(f_i-f_{i+1})/2},
\]
where `L_k` is the zero-field rate from source `k` to `k-1`; the last
edge uses the state-0 lift at `x=3` in the reverse formula, and the
counterclockwise state-0 rate can equivalently use the saddle copy at
`x=-1/2`. These lift choices matter because `w` is not periodic. The
prefactor is `sqrt((2 pi^2 H)(2 pi^2 H))/(2 pi)=pi H`; uniformity over
compact `h` requires a uniform Eyring–Kramers remainder. In column
convention, the limiting triangle has
`q_{j,i}(h)=q^0_{j,i} exp(h*(f_j-f_i)/2)`.

The zero-field clockwise rates for `0->1, 1->2, 2->0` are
`R=(1,1/1000,1/1000)`; the counterclockwise source rates for
`0->2, 1->0, 2->1` are `(1/25,4/25,1/300)`. Their product ratio is
`prod(R)/prod(L)=3/64=exp(3F)`. The construction pins the extrema at
nodes (`V_H'=w'=f'=0`) with the stated curvatures. It does not by itself
prove a dynamic transition-current estimate.

## Exact conditional implication for the periodic law and work

Let `Y_H(tau)` be a well label defined by committed transitions between
fixed disjoint cores around the three wells; do not count every Brownian
recrossing of a saddle as a jump. Let `Q(h)` be the above tilted triangle
generator, and let `Phi(t,s)` be its time-ordered propagator. Since all six
rates are uniformly positive and bounded for `|h|<=0.3`, the one-period
kernel is strictly positive and has a total-variation contraction
coefficient `rho_Q<1`, uniformly in start phase. One explicit, loose bound
comes from `m=10^-3 exp(-0.15)` (a lower bound on each edge rate) and
`Lambda=(26/25)exp(0.15)` (a bound on total exit rate): over any unit slow
interval every entry of the transition matrix is at least
`m exp(-Lambda)`, so the Dobrushin coefficient is at most
`1-3m exp(-Lambda)<1`. Iterating over `floor(P)` units gives a uniform
one-cycle contraction.

The following are sufficient dynamic hypotheses for this specified
scaling:

1. **Uniform, time-dependent coarse graining.** After an intrawell
   relaxation layer, the phase-indexed law of committed well labels over
   every fixed slow-time horizon converges in total variation to the
   inhomogeneous chain generated by `Q(h(tau))`, uniformly in phase,
   `|h|<=0.3`, and the initial locally equilibrated well mixture. Equivalently
   for a law-level proof, the projected evolution has
   `p_H'=Q(h(tau))p_H+r_H(tau)` with
   `sup_tau ||r_H(tau)||_1 -> 0`, uniformly over those data. This remainder
   must include time-dependent transition-hazard, recrossing, and
   localization errors; pointwise frozen-rate asymptotics are not a
   substitute.
2. **Fast/slow spectral separation and periodic mixing.** There are exactly
   three slow modes; all intrawell modes relax on `O(H^-1)` physical time,
   and the projected one-period diffusion kernel has a phase-uniform
   contraction `rho<1` independent of large `H`. Equivalently the
   zero-mass slow evolution has a uniform bound
   `||Phi(t,s)|| <= C exp(-g(t-s))`, `g>0`. No other hidden mode may have a
   decay rate comparable to `kappa_H`.
3. **Observable decoration for work.** In the periodic diffusion law, the
   conditional well means of `f` converge uniformly to
   `(f_0,f_1,f_2)=(1,0,0)`, and the total slow-time occupation of transition
   regions tends to zero. With bounded fixed `f`, this controls the full
   work integral, including its barrier portion.
4. **Uniform integrability/current convergence for fuel.** Define the
   rescaled expected winding current
   \[
   J_H(\tau)=\kappa_H^{-1}\int_0^3
   [F-V_H'(x)-v'(x)+h(\tau)f'(x)]\rho_H(x,\tau/\kappa_H)\,dx.
   \]
   Require `J_H -> J_Q` in `L1([0,P])` in the periodic law, where
   \[
   J_Q(\tau)=\sum_{i=0}^2
   [q^+_i(h)p_i-q^-_{i+1}(h)p_{i+1}]
   \]
   is the clockwise-minus-counterclockwise edge flux, indices mod 3.
   An alternative sufficient condition is marked-transition convergence
   plus a uniform second-moment bound on the number of committed jumps per
   slow cycle. Weak convergence of well labels alone does not imply this
   mean-current statement.

Under hypothesis 1, the periodic projected law exists uniquely by
hypothesis 2. If the one-cycle projected kernel differs from the chain's
by at most `delta_H` in total variation uniformly over initial mixtures
and phase, the fixed-point perturbation inequality is
\[
d_{TV}(p_H^{per}(0),p_Q^{per}(0))\le \frac{\delta_H}{1-\rho_Q}.
\]
The same bound propagates through a cycle up to the within-cycle reduction
error. More directly, if the periodic residual satisfies
`sup_tau ||r_H(tau)||_1<=epsilon_H` and
`||Phi(t,s)||_{1,0}<=C exp(-g(t-s))` on zero-mass vectors, then periodicity
and variation of constants give
\[
\sup_\tau\|p_H^{per}(\tau)-p_Q^{per}(\tau)\|_1
\le C\epsilon_H/g.
\]

The diffusion's mean control work over one physical period is exactly
\[
W_H=-\int_0^{P/\kappa_H}\dot h_{phys}(t)\,E[f(X_t)]dt
    =-\int_0^P h'(\tau)E[f(X_{\tau/\kappa_H})]d\tau.
\]
The `kappa_H` factors cancel. If
`eta_H=sup_tau |E[f(X_{tau/kappa_H})]-f^T p_H^{per}(tau)| -> 0`, then
\[
|W_H-W_Q|\le \|h'\|_{L^1(0,P)}
\left[\eta_H+2\|f_{well}\|_\infty
d_{TV}(p_H^{per},p_Q^{per})\right].
\]
Here `||h'||_1=4(0.3)=1.2`. Thus the fixed negative chain work transfers
to the diffusion if the displayed right-hand side is eventually smaller
than `-W_Q`.

The chemical force work on the unwrapped coordinate is
`W_chem,H=F E[X_{P/kappa_H}-X_0]`. Ito's formula gives
`E[Delta X]=integral_0^P J_H(tau)d tau`, so hypothesis 4 implies
\[
|W_{chem,H}-W_{chem,Q}|
\le |F|\int_0^P|J_H(\tau)-J_Q(\tau)|d\tau\to0.
\]
This is the missing full-fuel condition; it is stronger than weak
convergence of the well-label paths.

## Why the missing conditions matter

**Warm-up.** Start at phase zero from a locally equilibrated density in
well 0 rather than from the unique periodic law. Its limiting label starts
at `e_0`, while the periodic chain law has all components positive. At
phase zero their total-variation distance is `1-p_0^{per}(0)>0`, independent
of `H`; the first-cycle law is not the periodic law even when every frozen
EK rate is exact. If the period map contracts by `rho`, `N` warm-up cycles
are needed to reduce a label-law error to `delta`:
`N >= log(delta)/log(rho)`. Their physical duration is
`N P/kappa_H = 200 N exp(H)/H`. Intrawell equilibration adds a smaller
`O(H^-1 log(1/delta))` transient. Starting in the periodic density avoids
this transient mathematically, but preparing it is an additional protocol.

**Weak convergence is not enough for fuel.** The generic random variables
`Z_H=1/kappa_H` with probability `kappa_H` and zero otherwise converge to
zero in probability while `E Z_H=1`. This is not claimed to be a trajectory
of the specified diffusion; it demonstrates why a weak path/label limit
cannot pass to an unbounded winding mean without uniform integrability or
current convergence.

**Driving corrections.** `|dot h_phys|=O(kappa_H)` is small relative to the
intrawell relaxation rate `O(H)`, but is not `o(kappa_H)`: the ratio of
driving frequency to a typical escape rate remains a fixed slow-time
number. Therefore one must retain `Q(h(tau))` as a time-dependent chain,
not replace it by an instantaneous stationary well distribution. One must
also bound any geometric/localization correction after rescaling by
`kappa_H`. The zero first derivatives pin the extrema and their basin
boundaries under the field; a uniform estimate on the actual nonreversible
Fokker–Planck evolution and its current remains to be shown.

**No discrete atomic return.** The continuum process is not in an atomic
state at sampled cycle boundaries. A one-dimensional diffusion can be
stopped when it hits a specified point and then has the strong Markov
property, but that is a separate continuous stopping protocol; the hitting
time is not a fixed-phase discrete return and synchronization/monitoring
costs need to be charged. A basin-core visit is not an automatic reset.

## Physical scale and claim boundary

If the conditions above hold and `W_Q<0` is the fixed work per slow cycle,
then physical output power is
\[
P_{out,H}=\frac{-W_H}{P/\kappa_H}
\sim \frac{-W_Q}{200}\,H e^{-H}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The affinity-work input per cycle can converge to a finite chain value,
but its power also vanishes on this clock. No physical force source,
controller, phase preparation, reset, or measurement system is built or
costed here. This is an ideal overdamped model and a conditional
per-cycle asymptotic only.

## Primary-source scope

* Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard and Klein prove sharp *frozen reversible*
  Eyring–Kramers asymptotics from capacities; that result supplies the
  static baseline but not this periodically driven current theorem:
  [JEMS 6 (2004), 399–424](https://ems.press/journals/jems/articles/172).
* Bouchet and Reygner derive the Eyring–Kramers prefactor for irreversible
  diffusions via the quasipotential. This is the relevant static extension
  for a tilted nonequilibrium system, but it does not supply the periodic
  three-well current estimate used above:
  [arXiv:1507.02104](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02104).
* Talkner and Łuczka derive time-dependent metastable master equations and
  explicitly separate frozen transition rates from a geometric term due
  to moving domains; neglecting that term requires a quantitative
  condition. Their formulation assumes frozen detailed balance, unlike
  the constant-force circle here:
  [Phys. Rev. E 69, 046109 (2004), arXiv:cond-mat/0307498](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307498).
  Their example also shows that the coefficient of the driving-frequency
  correction can grow with inverse noise, so merely saying the physical
  frequency tends to zero is inadequate.
* Berglund proves a time-periodic Eyring–Kramers result for a slowly forced
  bistable diffusion in a specified frequency range, but that range is
  described as large compared with the unforced transition rate; it is not
  the present three-well constant-force periodic-law/current result:
  [arXiv:2007.08443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.08443).

These references support the distinction between static escape-rate
asymptotics and a uniform driven-periodic reduction. None is cited here as
proving hypotheses 1–4 for this construction.
