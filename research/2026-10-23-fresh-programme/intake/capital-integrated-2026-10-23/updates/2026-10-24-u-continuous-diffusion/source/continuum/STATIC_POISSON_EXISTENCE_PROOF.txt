STATIC POISSON PROOF OF A FULL-CONTINUOUS-WORK EXISTENCE STATEMENT

Status: independent mathematical derivation for the fixed overdamped
constitutive model, with exact finite coefficient checks.  It establishes
existence for sufficiently large finite H and sufficiently small finite
amplitude/frequency.  It does NOT establish the selected H=12, A=.3,
Omega=.01*kappa finite-cycle result, physical validation, positive limiting
power, or uniform driven-periodic metastability at fixed slow controls.

1. Model and generator

Use precisely CONTINUOUS_CONSTRUCTION.txt / continuous_model.py.  Let
Phi_H,h=H V+w-hf on the lift, with Phi(x+3)=Phi(x)-3F and
C=exp(-3F)=64/3>1.  The forward generator on periodic densities is

  L_H,h u = -partial_x j_u,
  j_u = -partial_x u - (partial_x Phi_H,h)u.

The work-on port is the FULL continuous potential work
W_on=-integral hdot(t) integral f(x)rho(x,t) dx dt.  In particular f
is not replaced by a basin indicator in this definition.

2. Exact stationary and inverse formulas in one dimension

Choose base b=1/4, so the three wells at 1,2,3 and saddles at .5,1.5,2.5
are all strictly inside [b,b+3].  Every integral below is over this lift.
At fixed h put

  A_H = integral_b^(b+3) exp(Phi(y))dy,
  J_H(x) = integral_b^x exp(Phi(y))dy,
  U_1(x) = exp(-Phi(x))[A_H/(C-1)+J_H(x)],
  N_H = integral U_1,
  rho_H,h=U_1/N_H.

U_1 is positive and periodic.  Direct differentiation gives
U_1'+Phi' U_1=1, so its flux is -1; consequently L U_1=0.
This also proves that the normalized expression is the unique positive
stationary density.  Equivalently rho is proportional to
exp(-Phi(x)) integral_x^(x+3)exp(Phi(y))dy.

At h=0 let r_H=partial_h rho_H,h.  Then integral r_H=0.  Define

  R_H(x)=integral_b^x r_H,
  B_H=integral_b^(b+3)exp(Phi(y))R_H(y)dy,
  J_R,H(x)=integral_b^x exp(Phi(y))R_H(y)dy,
  U_R(x)=exp(-Phi(x))[B_H/(C-1)+J_R,H(x)],
  z_H=U_R-rho_H,0 integral U_R.

These formulas are exact, at each finite H.  Indeed
U_R'+Phi' U_R=R_H and the flux of z_H is c-R_H for a constant c.
Thus L_H,0 z_H=r_H, and integral z_H=0.  The monodromy C makes U_R
periodic, just as it does U_1.  No quasistatic replacement of a driven
density has been made.

For the equivalent window formula, differentiation yields

  r_H=rho_H[a_H(x)-integral rho_H a_H],
  a_H(x)=f(x)- [integral_x^(x+3)f(y)exp(Phi(y))dy]
                   /[integral_x^(x+3)exp(Phi(y))dy].

Since 0<=f<=1, |a_H|<=1 and |r_H|<=2rho_H.  Therefore |R_H|<=2,
|B_H|<=2A_H, and |U_R|<=2U_1.  After centering, |z_H|<=4U_1.
These uniform dominations prevent a hidden signed-mass or
inverse-operator assumption.

3. Elementary Laplace limit, including the Poisson inverse

At every well i, V(i)=0,V''(i)=2pi^2; at each saddle s,
V(s)=1,V''(s)=-2pi^2.  The locked offsets w and f are fixed C^2
functions with bounded derivatives, f is constant near these nodes,
and all relevant critical points are isolated.  Standard
one-dimensional Laplace estimates follow
by a quadratic rescaling in small fixed neighborhoods and exponential
suppression outside them.  Explicitly, with

  alpha_H=1/sqrt(pi H), beta_H=exp(H)/sqrt(pi H),
  kappa_H=pi H exp(-H), alpha_H beta_H=1/kappa_H,

the well mass coefficient is
lambda_i(h)=exp[-w(i)+h f(i)], and a saddle resistance coefficient is
b_s(h)=exp[w(s)-h f(s)].  The statements also hold after one h derivative:
the differentiated integrands merely have bounded factors f.  No
time-dependent metastability theorem is being invoked.

In the well order (1,2,3), at h=0 these coefficients are

  lambda=(25/4,15/8,3/64), b_s=(1,160,1600/3),
  f_well=(0,0,1), f_saddle=(1/2,0,1/2).

Let B=sum_s b_s, P_i=sum_{s<i}b_s, where s<i means a saddle preceding
the indicated well in the lifted interval.  Stationary density masses
converge to normalized

  v_i=lambda_i[B/(C-1)+P_i].

The same calculation after one h derivative gives weak convergence of
r_H dx to the derivative r_i=partial_h pi_i(h)|0.  Thus R_H converges
uniformly on each small saddle neighborhood to the cumulative preceding
well mass.  In saddle order these limits are

  R_s=(0,r_1,r_1+r_2).

Uniformity follows, for example, from |r_H|<=2rho_H and exponential
concentration of rho_H away from the wells.  Apply the saddle Laplace
estimate to B_H and J_R,H, followed by the well estimate.  Then
kappa_H U_R dx converges weakly to well masses

  t_i=lambda_i[B_R/(C-1)+sum_{s<i} b_s R_s],
  B_R=sum_s b_s R_s.

The domination |U_R|<=2U_1 and kappa_H integral U_1 tending to a finite
positive constant ensures tight bounded total variation; it justifies
these signed integrations and excludes barrier mass missed by the node
formulas.  Hence kappa_H z_H dx converges to

  u_i=t_i-pi_i sum_j t_j.

4. Identification with the intended kinetic action, not an assumed limit

The committed edge rates are 1/(lambda_i b_s), with the monodromy C
inserted on the wrapping edge.  These are exactly the intended Q0,
and the h-dependent rates are exactly
q_ji(h)=q_ji(0) exp[h(f_j-f_i)/2], because the saddle f is the midpoint.
This is also checked directly with the rational coefficients above.

For stationary v_i, each oriented edge current is -1, up to common
normalization, so Q0 pi=0.  For t_i, the oriented edge currents are
0,-r_1,-(r_1+r_2); their divergences give Q0 t=r.  Subtracting a
stationary vector changes no divergence, so Q0 u=r and sum u=0.
Thus u=Q0^# r.  Differentiating Q(h)pi(h)=0 gives
r=-Q0^# B0, B0=Q'(0)pi.  These equalities determine the limit from
elementary scalar integrals and finite linear equations, without
assuming a full driven coarse-graining theorem.

Define the exact continuous low-frequency friction

  zeta_H=-integral f z_H.

Because f is continuous and bounded, the established signed weak limit
gives

  kappa_H zeta_H -> -sum_i f_i u_i
      = f^T (Q0^#)^2 B0
      = -29239948720000/54023012580627 < 0.

static_limit_exact.py independently checks the rational limiting
stationary derivative, current coefficients, and Poisson inverse.

5. What finite continuous extraction follows

For every sufficiently large finite H, zeta_H<0.  At such a FIXED H,
the normalized forward elliptic generator has an invertible restriction
on zero-integral densities.  Its harmonic response to h=A cos(Omega t)
has susceptibility

  chi_H(Omega)=integral f (iOmega-L_H,0)^(-1) B_H,forcing,
  B_H,forcing=(partial_h L_H,h)|0 rho_H,0=-L_H,0 r_H.

The resolvent expansion on this zero-mass subspace gives

  Im chi_H(Omega)=-Omega zeta_H+O_H(Omega^2).

Choose sufficiently small fixed Omega>0, making this imaginary part
positive.  For that fixed H and Omega, the unique periodic normalized
parabolic solution depends analytically on sufficiently small A.  One
way to see this is to invert partial_t-L_H,0 on periodic zero-mass
functions; the smooth bounded-coefficient drift perturbation is
relatively bounded, so a small-A Neumann/implicit-function argument
applies.  The quadratic full work per period is

  W_on(A,Omega)=-pi A^2 Im chi_H(Omega)+O_H,Omega(A^3).

Therefore some sufficiently small FINITE nonzero A has W_on<0 in the
fully continuous medium, including barrier control work.  This is an
existence theorem inside the stated overdamped model, not a quantified
H/amplitude/frequency interval or a physical experiment.  Taking limits
successively is sufficient; no uniform-in-H remainder is claimed.

This proof does not predict the selected H12,A.3,.01*kappa cycle.  That
requires its own full-period calculation.  It also does not imply a
positive limiting physical power: periods compatible with the slow
kinetics grow exponentially, and the force/fuel ledger remains paid.
Nor does it transfer the discrete atomic-herald null test to a well.

6. The slow witness pays housekeeping fuel and has vanishing power

The same stationary formula gives j_H=-1/N_H.  Here
sum_i v_i=37803/61, so j_H/kappa_H -> -61/37803.  The lifted velocity
is 3j_H and the constant-force power is F*3j_H>0.  Near zero field,
a cycle of duration 2pi/Omega consequently spends leading fuel

  W_force ~ F*(-61/12601)*kappa_H*(2pi/Omega).

The weak-field work magnitude is instead approximately
pi A^2 |zeta_Q| Omega/kappa_H in the low-frequency regime.  Thus taking
Omega/kappa_H to zero spends increasing housekeeping fuel per cycle
while reducing output and efficiency; it cannot turn the existence
proof into an efficient engine.  The successive existence choices may
always be made with Omega_H/kappa_H tending to zero and A_H tending to
zero.  Their physical output power then tends to zero as well.  This
statement neither supplies a useful finite-power lower bound nor
relaxes the fixed-mass/bath-memory caveat of the constitutive model.
