COLLECTIVE OBSERVATION MAP FOR A FROZEN THERMOELASTIC FIELD
23 October 2026. Root derivation after independent aging openings.

Purpose and status

This is a constructive forward observation map and an identifiability gate, not
a newly established material mechanism. It replaces an invalid shortcut from
tagged-particle displacements to coherent XPCS. No measured material field,
Gaussian law, predictive advantage or priority is claimed. Bouchaud--Pitard's
dipolar displacement mechanism and mechanical scattering echoes are close prior
art, not inventions of this unit.

1. Start at the measured quantity

For point scatterers with real form factors f_j, an illumination amplitude w,
and a prescribed displacement field u_j(t), the single-scattering amplitude is

 A(q,t)=sum_j f_j w(r_j+u_j(t)) exp[-i q.(r_j+u_j(t))].
 I(q,t)=|A(q,t)|^2.

Absorption, polarization, coherence and detector integration would be additional
measured parts of a real apparatus. Even before those, homodyne intensity is a
pair observable. It does not equal a tagged-particle characteristic function.

With uniform illumination and affine motion r_j(t)=F_t r_j+b_t,

 A(q,t)=exp(-i q.b_t) A_0(F_t^T q),
 I(q,t)=I_0(F_t^T q).

Rigid translation is exactly invisible in this ideal intensity. An affine strain
moves speckles in reciprocal space. At a fixed detector pixel it can reduce a
correlation even when the density is related by a reversible affine map. An
actual comparison should therefore separate the measured reciprocal-space warp
from the residual decorrelation. A finite beam breaks the translation null when
different scatterers enter its weighted volume; changing w is a real observation
operation, not structural relaxation. A measured mean diffraction-peak position
does not specify a nonaffine displacement field.

2. Exact two-time coherent amplitude under a prescribed Gaussian field

Let u_j(t)=z_t U(r_j), where U is a fixed, zero-mean Gaussian random vector
field, independent of the initial configuration, with covariance
 B_jk=E[U(r_j) U(r_k)^T]. Define Q_jk=q^T B_jk q. For uniform illumination,
conditioning on the initial positions gives the exact finite sum

 E_U[A(q,t1)^* A(q,t2)]
 =sum_jk f_j f_k exp[i q.(r_j-r_k)]
  *exp[-(z1^2 Q_jj+z2^2 Q_kk-2 z1 z2 Q_jk)/2].       (1)

For a homogeneous field Q_jj=Q0, the attenuation in each term splits as

 exp[-(z2-z1)^2 Q0/2] exp[-z1 z2 (Q0-Q_jk)].        (2)

The self terms j=k have only the first factor. The distinct-particle terms
retain z1*z2 and pair-separation dependence. Independence of U from structure
does NOT eliminate these terms. Thus r_j=r_j0+U_j z is insufficient to derive
 F_coherent(t1,t2)=F_base(Delta xi) Phi(q Delta z).
Such a factorization needs an additional proved observation model and conditions
on pair structure, displacement correlations and normalization. It must not be
imported from the self intermediate scattering function.

3. An exact intensity map without invoking Siegert

Under the same frozen-Gaussian-field hypothesis, expand I(t1) I(t2) as a finite
four-index sum. Let particle labels i=(j,k,l,m), signs c=(+1,-1,+1,-1), and
associated z values h=(z1,z1,z2,z2), indexed by alpha,beta in {1,2,3,4}. Then

 E_U[I(q,t1) I(q,t2)]
 =sum_jklm f_j f_k f_l f_m
  *exp[-i q.(r_j-r_k+r_l-r_m)]
  *exp[-(1/2) sum_(alpha,beta) c_alpha c_beta h_alpha h_beta
                                      Q_(i_alpha,i_beta)].        (3)

Repeated particle indices are allowed. The real result follows by conjugate
pairing. Initial-configuration averaging requires its actual four-point
statistics or an independently justified approximation, not merely S(q).
Equation (3), combined with the same formula for mean intensities, is a complete
finite conditional prediction for the ideal intensity correlation. It retains
the operational distinction lost in a self-particle surrogate.

A useful control is U_j=V for every j, with random V. Then Q_jk=Q0 for all j,k.
In (3), sum_alpha c_alpha h_alpha=z1-z1+z2-z2=0, so the Gaussian attenuation is
identically
one: random global translation leaves every ideal intensity unchanged.
But (1) acquires exp[-(z2-z1)^2 Q0/2]. Applying Siegert to this mixture would
incorrectly predict intensity decorrelation. The joint field law is not thereby
joint circular Gaussian. Siegert is an extra observation assumption and must be
verified over the actual ensemble and protocol; random phases multiplying a
fixed speckle pattern provide a simple failure case.

4. What an echo can actually reject

If the full illuminated density configuration returns and the apparatus returns,
the ideal intensity returns. This sufficient condition is independent of the
motion's dissipated work. Plastic limit cycles can also return. Conversely,
intensity can return under translation, inversion or other homometric changes;
an intensity echo does not identify microscopic configuration return uniquely.
Returning only a scalar mean volume or FSDP position proves neither condition.

A constructive held-out test therefore needs a calibrated displacement-field
family U and state z, illumination/affine-warp corrections, and a forward map
such as (3), or a directly justified reduced version. Acquiring that model from
separate strain/control measurements is part of the scientific task. Fitting
z and a new kernel to the same test XPCS curves would supply the explanation.

5. Finite-core dipole screen (tagged-particle scope only)

For a separately assumed isotropic Poisson population of r^-2 displacement
sources of number density rho, core a, outer cutoff R, and A=|q Delta p|
with dimensions length^2, the scalar angular screen is

 Lambda=4 pi rho int_a^R r^2 [1-sinc(A/r^2)] dr
       =2 pi rho A^(3/2) int_(A/R^2)^(A/a^2)
                           (1-sinc v) v^(-5/2) dv.

At A/a^2 <<1,
 Lambda=(2 pi rho/3) A^2 (1/a-1/R)
        -(pi rho/150) A^4 (1/a^5-1/R^5)+O(A^6/a^9).
For a^2 << A << R^2,
 Lambda ~ (8 pi sqrt(2 pi)/15) rho A^(3/2).

The coefficient follows from int_0^infty(1-sinc v)v^(-5/2)dv
=4 sqrt(2 pi)/15. An actual dipole angular Green function need not be a sinc;
its tensor structure changes the coefficient. Finite-core crossover and the
3/2 dipolar regime are consequences of established physics, not a new aging
framework. Neither expression supplies the missing coherent four-point map.
