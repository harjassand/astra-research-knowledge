# Audit: a closed aging-coordinate echo versus established scattering echoes

**Scope.** This is a bounded audit of the lead-exposed kinematic candidate and primary scattering/echo studies, not a comprehensive literature review. The candidate is attributed to the lead, not to this audit. I distinguish an echo created by a controlled external deformation from a proposed echo driven by an independently measured internal aging coordinate.

## Finding

For the ansatz

\[
\mathbf r_j(t)=\mathbf r_{j0}+\mathbf U_j z(t),
\]
with fixed \(\mathbf U_j\), the displacement during an interval is \(\mathbf U_j\Delta z\), where \(\Delta z=z(t_2)-z(t_1)\). For a scattering-vector \(\mathbf q\), the reversible contribution to the intermediate scattering function is the characteristic function

\[
\Phi_{\mathbf q}(\Delta z)=\mathbb E_{\mathbf U}\!\left[e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf U\Delta z}\right].
\]

Thus \(z(t_2)=z(t_1)\) gives \(\Phi_{\mathbf q}(0)=1\): the reversible component rephases. If, in addition, independent irreversible events attenuate the field correlation by a factor \(M(\mathbf q;t_1,t_2)\), then

\[
g_1(\mathbf q;t_1,t_2)=M(\mathbf q;t_1,t_2)\Phi_{\mathbf q}(\Delta z),
\qquad
g_2-1=\beta|g_1|^2
\]

under the usual Siegert assumptions. A closed \(z\)-excursion therefore leaves any independent accumulated event-dose factor in place. This is a conditional model consequence, not a general property of every material: the conclusion requires fixed couplings \(\mathbf U_j\), stable scattering weights, and a well-defined scalar endpoint coordinate. If the couplings or structure evolve with dose, returning one scalar to its old value need not return the configuration.

The basic rephasing mechanism is already the central construction in oscillatory-shear DWS, DLS, microscopy/echo-DDM, and XPCS. The 1997 DWS paper explicitly says the correlation recovers when reversed shear returns scatterers to their initial positions; later XPCS papers use the same idea to separate affine reversible motion from cycle-to-cycle rearrangements. I found primary thermal XPCS studies of temperature scanning and cryogenic cycling, but in the inspected sources they measure evolving correlation/relaxation functions across those protocols rather than a prospectively predicted scattering echo at independently matched internal-clock endpoints. A temperature path that returns to its starting temperature is not, by itself, evidence that an internal aging coordinate or microscopic state has returned.

A thermal/aging echo could add a distinct result only if it validates a *material* coordinate \(z\), independently of the echo signal, and predicts rephasing at a held-out thermal history while an orthogonal measure records continuing irreversible change. Without those controls, it is the established echo idea with a different choice of drive coordinate. One further measurement limit matters: homodyne XPCS generally gives \(|g_1|^2\), so it sees \(|\Phi_{\mathbf q}|^2\), not the full complex characteristic function. Echo recovery tests endpoint phase coherence but cannot identify the full displacement distribution without additional assumptions or phase-/particle-resolved information.

## What would independently calibrate \(z\)?

First specify whether \(z\) is an externally set coordinate or an internal material coordinate.

* **Externally controlled strain:** use a calibrated encoder/actuator readout plus a direct local deformation map at the probed volume, with phase-synchronous acquisition. Verify wall slip, shear banding, and residual flow. Edera et al. show why the nominal boundary motion alone is insufficient. This case is already ordinary rheo-echo physics.
* **Temperature as an external coordinate:** log calibrated temperature at the sample (not only the stage setpoint), resolve heating/cooling lag and spatial gradients, and independently register the sample position/beam. Temperature alone may drive thermoelastic motion, but a same-temperature endpoint is not proof that an internal aging coordinate returned.
* **Internal aging coordinate:** predefine an independent observable and frozen map \(z=f(X)\), for example a suitably justified dilatometric, diffraction, or calorimetric proxy, then show the same mapping works over training and held-out histories. Calibrate the coupling distribution \(p(\mathbf U)\) in separate reversible, low-dose cycles or with an independent particle-/phase-resolved measurement; predict the q- and orientation-dependence of \(\Phi_{\mathbf q}(\Delta z)\) without refitting on the echo data. A state proxy such as mean peak position needs validation: it is not self-evidently a sufficient state variable for microscopic dynamics.

Do not define \(z\) from the same scattering echo whose refocusing is being tested. That would make “the echo returns when the fitted coordinate returns” circular. Also preserve the detector-level checks: normalize contrast \(\beta\) using a static reference, track beam/sample drift, and account for any simultaneous change in static structure factor or scattering weights. With intensity-only XPCS, report that the test constrains \(|\Phi_{\mathbf q}|\) under the Siegert relation; recovering the complex distribution generally needs extra information.

## Minimal discriminating experiment

1. Train and freeze \(z=f(X)\) and \(p(\mathbf U)\) from independent, non-echo data. Record local temperature and the independent proxy through the full protocol.
2. In matched initial states, compare (a) a closed thermal excursion with endpoints matched in independently measured \(z\), (b) an age-matched isothermal control, and (c) an open-path endpoint with the same temperature but a different measured \(z\). Record XPCS continuously or stroboscopically at fixed \(q\), with a static reference scatterer and independently monitored sample/beam position. Pre-register the expected \(q\)- and orientation-dependent rephasing curve.
3. Measure the proposed irreversible component independently (or state explicitly if it is only an XPCS memory-loss proxy). A successful result would show the reversible factor returns as the frozen \(z\)-model predicts while a separate non-affine/event-sensitive observable changes across the closed excursion. Repeat on a held-out thermal history without changing \(z\), \(p(\mathbf U)\), or contrast calibration.

This design distinguishes “temperature came back” from “the hypothesized aging coordinate came back,” and distinguishes sample rephasing from thermally driven beamline motion. Even a positive result would establish the factorization only for the calibrated material, observable pair, q-range, and tested histories; it would not by itself prove a universal material clock or causal response sufficiency.

## Exact novelty boundary

* **Already established:** a periodic externally imposed strain can produce speckle/correlation echoes on return to the same mechanical configuration; their cycle-to-cycle attenuation measures irreversibility or memory loss in the tested samples. Multiple primary studies now do this with DWS, XPCS and echo-DDM.
* **Not established by those mechanical echoes:** that an internal aging coordinate, inferred independently of scattering, is the state variable that drives reversible particle displacements during a thermal cycle.
* **Potential incremental result:** independently calibrated internal \(z\) predicts held-out q-resolved refocusing despite continued irreversible state evolution, after static-reference and instrument-motion controls. Without that, “thermal aging echo” is a reparameterized conventional echo claim.
