SMOOTH REFERENCE FILTERS AND THE DIRECT JOINT-ABORT ALTERNATIVE
2026-10-24. passive_lag. Finite operator construction; novelty UNKNOWN.

Purpose

The common-reference operation need not require exact domination of every
old coarse effect. This note gives an actual local clipping operation,
with a payoff-weighted rejected mass and a loss-aware event bound. It
also identifies a stronger direct strategy when the normalized joint
filter failure is already small. These are constructive sufficient
criteria, not a universal small-clipping theorem.

1. Local spectral clipping to a fixed reference

Fix full-rank reference POVMs F0^alpha,H0^gamma, independent of unchecked
questions, and numbers d_A,d_B>=1. Let original local coarse/fine POVMs
be F_u^alpha,G_u^(alpha,a) and H_v^gamma,K_v^(gamma,b), with fine sums
equal to coarse effects. For Alice define

  R_(u,alpha)=(F0^alpha)^(-1/2) F_u^alpha (F0^alpha)^(-1/2),
  F'_u^alpha=sqrt(F0^alpha) min(R_(u,alpha),d_A I) sqrt(F0^alpha). (S1)

The minimum in (S1) is spectral functional calculus of ONE positive
operator; it is not an unjustified Loewner minimum of two noncommuting
operators. It gives exactly
  0<=F'_u^alpha<=F_u^alpha,
  F'_u^alpha<=d_A F0^alpha.
Define H'_v^gamma analogously. Both are coarse sub-POVMs; their sums
are <=I. Full rank is an explicit finite premise here, so no unsupported
inverse or compression onto a noncommuting singular support is hidden.

Because F'<=F, bounded Douglas factorization gives, on supp(F),
  A_(u,alpha)=F^(-1/2)F'F^(-1/2)<=I,
  L_(u,alpha)=sqrt(A_(u,alpha)).
Extend L by identity off supp(F). Let C_(u,alpha)^a be the original
support-normalized fine POVM, so sqrt(F) C^a sqrt(F)=G^a. Define

  G'_u^(alpha,a)=sqrt(F) L C^a L sqrt(F).                     (S2)

It is positive and sums to F'. Bob uses the same construction, with
positive contraction M_(v,gamma), normalized fine D^b, and K'_v.
Complete the coarse/fine sub-POVM with a locally rejected failure outcome.
Thus this is a genuine instrument: a local positive contraction after
the original coarse Lueders operation, followed by the same fine POVM.
It depends only on own input and own coarse label, never on the other
player's actual fresh input or coarse label.

2. A payoff-weighted local rejection budget

Let theta_c(alpha,gamma) be the original tested acceptance relation.
All expectations below use the ORIGINAL unconditioned question/history
law. Let p>0, alpha be its accepted event mass and conditional fine loss.
Define

  delta_A=E sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c
                             chi(F_u^alpha-F'_u^alpha,H_v^gamma),
  delta_B=E sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c
                             chi(F_u^alpha,H_v^gamma-H'_v^gamma). (S3)

These are nonnegative ACTUAL rejection masses on the original accepted
history stack, not bounds on a purification chosen for convenience.
The operation that incurs them remains local even though their analysis
uses the game relation and the other effect. Put delta=delta_A+delta_B.

Write the original accepted stack vector as
  Psi=Pi_theta sum_(alpha,gamma)|alpha>_A|gamma>_B
                         tensor vec(sqrt(F_u^alpha)sigma sqrt(H_v^gamma)).
Orthogonal input/history records implement the stated averages. Its
squared norm is p, and the original block fine POVMs have loss mass
p alpha. The new vector is
  Psi'=calL calM Psi,
where calL,calM are the controlled local contractions above. They commute
with Pi_theta and with each other. Their product is a POSITIVE contraction.
Therefore, for p'=||Psi'||^2 and ell=p-p',

  0<=ell<=delta,
  ||Psi-Psi'||^2<=ell.                                      (S4)

The first inequality is the commuting union bound
I-calL^2 calM^2<=(I-calL^2)+(I-calM^2). The second is the scalar spectral
inequality (1-z)^2<=1-z^2 for 0<=z<=1 applied to calL calM. Positivity
of that product removes the factor-two loss of a triangle estimate.

Let 0<=J<=I be the SAME fine losing effect on this stack. Then
  ||sqrt(J)Psi'||<=sqrt(p alpha)+sqrt(ell),
so its new loss mass is at most (sqrt(p alpha)+sqrt(ell))^2. If delta<p,
the new accepted conditional loss alpha' obeys

  alpha'<=[alpha+2sqrt(alpha e)+e]/(1-e),
  e=ell/p<=delta/p.                                         (S5)

One may replace e by delta/p on the right. The clipping changes the
event and the fine law: alpha' belongs to the NEW event E', not the
original E. Equation (S5), rather than a silent identification of these
two conditional losses, is what relates them.

At the QUESTION level the new unnormalized event measure is pointwise
at most the old one, since F'<=F and H'<=H. Thus its conditional referee
law differs from the old one in TV by at most ell/p. Fine outcome masses
need not be pointwise smaller, because the filters can disturb the fine
POVM. The loss-aware bound (S5) accounts for that disturbance.

If delta=0, the accepted stack is unchanged exactly: each contraction
acts as identity on it. Hence all accepted fine entries, not only the
loss, are preserved. This is an explicit state-null local repair without
requiring nullity on every unaccepted branch or every possible state.

3. Apply the whole-coarse reconstruction to the clipped sub-POVMs

Here is the needed sub-POVM lemma, proved without applying a complete-
POVM domination premise to a nonexistent zero reference abort effect.
The reference F0,H0 are complete POVMs on the ORIGINAL label sets;
the input-dependent F',H' on those sets need only sum to <=I. For fixed
c put
  w0_(alpha,gamma)=chi(F0^alpha,H0^gamma),
  p0(c)=sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c(alpha,gamma)w0_(alpha,gamma).
When p0>0 prepare exactly the accepted-reference resource (R3) of
REFERENCE_POVM_DOMINATION_REBUILD.txt. On its alpha,gamma branch use
the positive local fine effects
  C'_(u,alpha)^a=(F0^alpha)^(-1/2)G'_u^(alpha,a)
                                     (F0^alpha)^(-1/2)/d_A,
  D'_(v,gamma)^b=(H0^gamma)^(-1/2)K'_v^(gamma,b)
                                     (H0^gamma)^(-1/2)/d_B.   (S3a)
Their sums are <=I because F'<=d_A F0 and H'<=d_B H0. Complete
these ACTUAL instruments by their positive leftover effects. For each
full u,v,a,b the same branch calculation, now written for the primed
effects, gives
  lambda_c P(1,a;1,b|u,v,c)
    =sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c(alpha,gamma)
                              chi(G'_u^(alpha,a),K'_v^(gamma,b)),
  lambda_c=d_A d_B p0(c).                                  (S3b)
No completeness of F' or H' was used in this identity.

The one-sided marginals obey exactly
  lambda_c P(s_A=1|u,c)
       =d_B sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c chi(F'_u^alpha,H0^gamma)
       <=d_B chi(sum_alpha F'_u^alpha,I)<=d_B,
  lambda_c P(s_B=1|v,c)
       =d_A sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c chi(F0^alpha,H'_v^gamma)
       <=d_A chi(I,sum_gamma H'_v^gamma)<=d_A.               (S3c)
The first inequalities use nonnegative terms and theta<=1; the second
use the sub-POVM sums. If p0=0, domination makes the primed accepted
table identically zero: take always-fail instruments and lambda_c=1.
Thus the lemma is everywhere defined and preserves the NEW table.

For clarity, one can append an abort coarse outcome perpendicular to
the original label sets, with zero theta row/column, to complete F',H'.
This is a bookkeeping completion of the original filtered instrument.
It is NOT part of the reference-domination hypothesis: assigning it
reference effect zero would not dominate its generally nonzero effect.
Equations (S3a)--(S3c) already give the actual complete replacement
instruments, without making that invalid assignment. Averaging (S3c)
under the original unconditioned c law gives
  G_A<=E_c d_B(c), G_B<=E_c d_A(c).

Define clipping at the FULL input family first. Then take its ordinary
conditional expectations in the R/H exposure. Do NOT clip separately
after each exposure: spectral clipping is nonlinear and would not
commute with those averages. Pointwise upper domination survives the
conditional expectations by positivity, and the fixed linear reference
transport applies to those new averages. This preserves source consistency.

For m unchecked coordinates, the standing source event inequality has
  tau'=log(max{1,E_c d_A,E_c d_B}/p')/m
and the new conditional loss alpha' from (S5). Therefore the final legal
one-round loss is at most
  alpha'+2^16[sqrt(alpha') (tau')^(1/4)+sqrt(tau')]+accuracy.   (S6)
The new event has ordinary referee density <=1/p' relative to original
mu^n. No table-only commuting transfer of the new operator hypotheses
is asserted. This is a finite-tensor construction with its named R/H/S
prerequisites, not a proof of universal small delta or small radii.

For fixed references and opposite original effects, delta_A(d_A) is a
finite nonnegative weighted sum of spectral tails (lambda-d_A)_+:
  F-F'=sqrt(F0)(R-d_A I)_+sqrt(F0).
It is decreasing, convex, and piecewise linear in d_A; Bob is symmetric.
This gives a concrete scalar radius/rejection tradeoff to optimize.
Its accepted weighting, not the dimension of a coarse-history alphabet,
is the relevant unproved control target. Input-copy tags still provide
large residuals unless a payoff-preserving redesign first removes them.

4. A direct strategy from ANY exact whole-table witness

Suppose actual finite local complete success/fine/failure POVMs on a
fixed resource zeta_c satisfy
  lambda_c P_zeta_c(1,a_J;1,b_J|u,v)=T_c(u,v,a_J,b_J)         (S7)
for the ORIGINAL accepted full unchecked table. Let
  Lambda=E_(c~mu^C)lambda_c, p=original accepted mass.
Then p<=Lambda. There is a legal direct one-round strategy as follows:
sample c with probability mu^C(c)lambda_c/Lambda and a uniformly
selected unchecked coordinate i OFFLINE. Sample the other unchecked
question pairs jointly from their original independent-round law before
the actual pair arrives. Prepare zeta_c. On the actual own fresh question,
run the local full-input POVM, read its coordinate answer on local success,
and output a fixed default on local failure. No player tests whether the
other succeeded, and no joint herald is supplied.

The both-success subtable equals the ORIGINAL conditional live table
times p/Lambda. All remaining branches are physically produced; charge
their loss at most one. Thus

  loss_direct <=(p/Lambda)alpha+1-p/Lambda.                    (S8)

This bypasses R/H/S entirely. It is useful when the JOINT-abort ratio
Lambda/p is close to one. It is distinct from one-sided masses G_A,G_B;
those can be small while Lambda is large. Conversely, with base own gap
epsilon in (0,1), every exact witness satisfies
  Lambda/p >=(1-alpha)/(1-epsilon),                          (S9)
because the legal strategy's winning probability is at least
(p/Lambda)(1-alpha). This is an operational compatibility constraint,
not a marginal/entropy condition alone.

For a CLIPPED table the direct statement uses p',alpha',Lambda' and
cannot silently substitute original p. Its nonabort winning mass has
the lower bound
  p'(1-alpha')>=p(1-alpha)-2sqrt(p alpha ell)-2ell.
This follows from (S4)--(S5) and is useful if the clipped residual is small.

5. A stronger comparator for two-sided small modulation

If an exact reference family has, for all full inputs and labels,
  (1-eta)F0<=F_u<=(1+eta)F0,
  (1-eta)H0<=H_v<=(1+eta)H0,
then its reference construction has lambda_c=(1+eta)^2 p0(c), while
the ORIGINAL mass satisfies p>= (1-eta)^2 E_c p0(c). Hence
  1-p/Lambda <=1-[(1-eta)/(1+eta)]^2
              =4eta/(1+eta)^2<=4eta.
Equation (S8) gives the elementary direct bound loss<=alpha+4eta.
Thus small eta relative to epsilon can already contradict an overly
small original conditional loss without entropy allocation or a sampler.
If the same two-sided condition holds at EVERY prefix C, each next-round
conditional loss is at least epsilon-4eta. For eta<epsilon/4 this yields
the stronger product bound
  P(all wins)<=(1-epsilon+4eta)^n.

This is a comparator that must be retained when discussing rates for
strongly controlled source families. A source quadratic rate is not an
advantage merely because eta=O(epsilon). The one-sided domination and
smooth payoff-weighted residual criteria are weaker; they do not supply
this linear-gap conclusion automatically.

Status

The local operations, exact residual identities, loss bound, and direct
joint-abort strategy are explicit. The unfinished general problem is to
control the payoff-weighted spectral tails or find a more semantic
reference/quotient at greedy-selected histories, with all-input local
instruments and bounded resources. No such universal control is claimed.
