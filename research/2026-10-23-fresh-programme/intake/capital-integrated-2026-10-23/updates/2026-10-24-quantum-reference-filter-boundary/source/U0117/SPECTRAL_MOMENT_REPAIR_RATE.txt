PAYOFF-WEIGHTED SPECTRAL MOMENTS GIVE A SMOOTH REPAIR RATE
2026-10-24. passive_lag. Strategy-class sufficient theorem; novelty UNKNOWN.

Disposition

Exact common-reference domination controls the worst operator direction,
including directions to which the actual state assigns negligible mass.
The smooth local operation can instead be controlled by payoff-weighted
spectral moments. This note derives the tail inequality and a quadratic
gap rate with an explicit prefactor under a UNIFORM MOMENT PREMISE at
the greedy selected histories. The moment premise is not proved for
arbitrary repeated strategies. The theorem therefore does not establish
an answer-free general-game repetition theorem.

1. A positive-functional spectral tail bound

Fix a finite original strategy, a tested set C with accepted mass p>0,
and complete full-rank reference POVMs F0(c),H0(c), independent of all
unchecked questions. For full own inputs set
  R_A(c,u,alpha)=F0^alpha^(-1/2) F_u^alpha F0^alpha^(-1/2),
  R_B(c,v,gamma)=H0^gamma^(-1/2) H_v^gamma H0^gamma^(-1/2).
All expectations below are under the ORIGINAL unconditioned question
law, before conditioning on the tested event. For s>0 define
  M_A(s)=E sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c(alpha,gamma)
       chi(sqrt(F0^alpha) R_A^(1+s) sqrt(F0^alpha),H_v^gamma),
  M_B(s)=E sum_(alpha,gamma)theta_c(alpha,gamma)
       chi(F_u^alpha,sqrt(H0^gamma) R_B^(1+s) sqrt(H0^gamma)). (M1)
These are nonnegative scalar functionals of actual operators and state.
They are not asserted to be Umegaki relative entropy or a familiar
Renyi information measure; the game weighting matters.

For r>=0 and d>=1, the scalar inequality
  (r-d)_+<=d^(-s)r^(1+s)
holds: it is zero for r<=d, while r>d gives r-d<=r<=r^(1+s)/d^s.
Spectral calculus of ONE operator, followed by congruence with sqrt(F0)
and the positive chi(.,H) functional, proves for the canonical clipping
of SMOOTH_DOMINATION_AND_DIRECT_FILTER.txt
  delta_A(d_A)<=d_A^(-s)M_A(s),
  delta_B(d_B)<=d_B^(-s)M_B(s).                              (M2)
No commutation of F_u with F0, or of two same-side effects, is used.
The opposite original effect in (M1) is deliberate: it is the original
accepted-stack rejection in the commuting union bound, not a newly
conditioned or already clipped opposite effect.

2. The one-event criterion

Let epsilon in (0,1] be the gap of the original one-round game in the
model being bounded. Suppose for this event
  alpha<=epsilon/2,
  M_A(s),M_B(s)<=M p, for M>=1.                              (M3)
Choose the same full-input clipping radius on both sides,
  d=(2048 M/epsilon)^(1/s).
It is fixed before all half-exposure averages. Equations (M2) give
  delta_A+delta_B<=p epsilon/1024.
Writing ell=p-p' for the actual loss of tested-event mass, the local
positive-contraction proof gives ell<=delta_A+delta_B and
  alpha'<=[alpha+2sqrt(alpha ell/p)+ell/p]/(1-ell/p).
In particular
  alpha'<=3 epsilon/5, p'>=p(1-epsilon/1024).               (M4)
One elementary check is to bound 2sqrt(alpha ell/p)<=epsilon/16.
The resulting ratio is <=(577/1023)epsilon<3epsilon/5.

The clipped conclusive families are SUB-POVMs on original labels.
Their leftover mass is an actual abort with zero theta row/column.
The sub-POVM reference lemma gives G_A,G_B<=d. For m unchecked
coordinates the source parameter is bounded by
  tau'<= [log(1/p)+K]/m,
  K=(1/s)log(2048 M/epsilon)+epsilon/512.                    (M5)
Here log(1/p')<=log(1/p)+epsilon/512, by -log(1-x)<=2x for
0<=x<=1/2. The half-exposure family is obtained by averaging the
ALREADY clipped full-input fine effects. Clipping is not claimed to
commute with those averages.

Under the standing R/H/S_actual event inequality, if
  [log(1/p)+K]/m<2^(-72)epsilon^2,                            (M6)
there is a legal one-round strategy of loss strictly below epsilon.
Indeed (M4) and the source bound give, before final accuracy,
  loss<=3epsilon/5
        +2^16 sqrt(3epsilon/5) (2^(-72)epsilon^2)^(1/4)
        +2^16 sqrt(2^(-72)epsilon^2)
       <(3/5+1/5+1/1000)epsilon<epsilon.
The available strict margin absorbs the arbitrarily small source
accuracy. Thus (M3) and (M6) cannot coexist for an actual strategy.

3. Restricted-strategy repeated-game consequence

Fix n, s>0 and M>=1. Consider only repeated strategies for which the
following is true at every proper tested set C encountered by the
standard frozen greedy selection: there are full-rank references,
independent of all unchecked questions, satisfying (M3)'s MOMENT bound
M_A(s),M_B(s)<=M p_C. References may depend on the fixed tested questions
c and the strategy, but not on a realized opposite fresh question.
The same s,M must control the actual selected set; a separate fixed-C
fixture is not evidence of this premise for a global repeated strategy.

Let theta be its all-wins probability, L=log(1/theta), gamma=2^(-75),
and K as in (M5). Then
  theta<=min{1,exp(K-gamma epsilon^2 n)}.                    (M7)
Equivalently the explicit prefactor is
  exp(epsilon/512)(2048 M/epsilon)^(1/s).
For fixed s,M this has quadratic gap order in n, with that prefactor;
it is not an improved uniform exponent for arbitrary games.

Proof. If L+K<gamma epsilon^2 n, the frozen greedy argument selects
proper C with t=|C|<=2L/epsilon, p_C>=theta, and alpha<=epsilon/2.
The assumed strict inequality implies
  t<2gamma epsilon n<n/2, m>n/2.
Apply the actual smooth local construction at THIS C. It gives
  tau'<=2(L+K)/n<2^(-74)epsilon^2<2^(-72)epsilon^2.
This contradicts Section 2 and the original one-round gap. Hence
L+K>=gamma epsilon^2 n, which is exactly (M7).

The finite-tensor statement uses the separate direct finite-matrix
R/S construction and the finite-tensor base gap. A commuting version
uses its OWN base gap and, additionally, the separate table-first
commuting-reference extension with accepted L. It does not compare
a commuting repeated value to a finite-tensor base gap.

4. Meaningful strictness and the resource boundary

The moment criterion can be strictly weaker than a small exact Loewner
radius, even after optimizing the reference. An input-copy scalar effect
rho I is NOT enough to establish this: an alternative reference can
upweight N such rare labels at total cost N rho. Instead use an orthogonal
state-rare sector with effect P_rare of operator norm one. If each of N
inputs places P_rare in a different coarse label, ANY dominating complete
reference must satisfy
  N P_rare<=d sum_(rare labels)F0^label<=d I,
so d>=N. Give that sector actual state probability rho=N^(-2).
A natural full-rank reference has spectral ratio O(N) there. Its moment
contribution is O(rho N^s), bounded for 0<s<=2, whereas discarding or
clipping that sector costs at most rho of original event mass.

An independent noncommuting qubit bulk can carry the constant-probability
L^tensor t event and parity-dependent fine measurements from the frozen
family. This makes the reference/moment distinction an actual operator
and fine-instrument distinction, rather than merely a classical table
calculation. Exact fixture details and preparation costs are to be read
with the separate bounded construction, not assumed from this paragraph.
Neither that fixed-event fixture nor its value-one coarse relation
establishes a positive-gap game family meeting the uniform greedy premise.

Moment evaluation, reference choice, spectra, and filter implementation
can all be expensive. Canonical clipping is an actual state-dependent
measurement operation, not a free estimate of p. The supplied S resource
also has no uniform cost bound here. The rate (M7) is mathematical under
the named source interfaces and moment premise, not an acquisition or
computational-efficiency theorem.

Status

Positive-functional tail inequality and the rate reduction derived.
The unproved general bottleneck is the normalized accepted moment bound
at greedy histories, or a semantic redesign that enforces it. No such
general bound, novelty, efficient compiler, or physical advantage claimed.
