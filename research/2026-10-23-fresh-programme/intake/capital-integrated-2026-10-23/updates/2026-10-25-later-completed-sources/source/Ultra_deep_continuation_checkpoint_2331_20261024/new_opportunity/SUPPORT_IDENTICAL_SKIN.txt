IDENTICAL ELASTIC SKIN WITH NO RESPONSIVE COMPACTION
Independent bounded comparator, 24 October 2026

Question and verdict

Set the channel-thickness derivative R_h to zero in the supplied skin
dispersion (S1). The skin is then an ordinary continuous ionic conductor with
elastic/capillary shape energy, but no pore-compaction feedback. It is not
automatically leveling: the exact long-wave coefficient is a competition
between capillarity (plus any signed membrane prestress) and current focusing
through the finite-depth anisotropic skin. For the requested illustrative
parameters, the coefficient is positive by factors of about 946, 9,463, and
94,630 for kappa_t/kappa_n=1, 10, and 100, respectively, even using the
optimistic L(0)=0 comparator. A finite lateral domain can remove the unstable
band only if its smallest allowed nonzero wavenumber lies above that band.
These are formula-level scenarios, not measured film performance.

Exact long-wave test

Write alpha=kappa_t/kappa_n, p=q*sqrt(kappa_n*kappa_t),
C=cosh(q*b*sqrt(alpha)), and S=sinh(q*b*sqrt(alpha)). Let

  chi(q)=A_m*q^2+nu_m*B*q^4,
  A_m=Gamma+nu_m*T0,
  B=E*b^3/[12*(1-nu_p^2)],
  nu_m=V_m/(zF).

Here T0 is the signed in-plane membrane force per length (N/m) under the stated
chi convention; if a source reports a stress in Pa, the conversion to a force
resultant needs the mechanically active thickness. Set T0=0 for the base
illustration. This separates the electrochemical coefficient nu_m from the
Poisson ratio nu_p.

For R_h=0, the supplied numerator is

  N0(q)=-G+(G-chi(q))*[C+L(q)*p*S],  G=j0/kappa_n.

Assuming L(q) has the finite limit L0 at q=0,

  C=1+alpha*b^2*q^2/2+O(q^4),
  p*S=b*kappa_t*q^2+O(q^4),

and hence

  N0(q)=q^2{G[alpha*b^2/2+L0*b*kappa_t]-A_m}+o(q^2).       (1)

The denominator tends to

  D0=r_ct+b/kappa_n+L0 > 0,

so the sign of (1) is the long-wave growth sign. Strict long-wave stability
requires

  A_m > G[alpha*b^2/2+L0*b*kappa_t].                       (2)

The reverse strict inequality gives unstable arbitrarily long mean-zero
modes. Equality is marginal at order q^2 and needs the q^4 expansion and the
actual small-q expansion of L(q); it is not a stability certificate. The
kinetic resistance r_ct slows response through D0 but cannot change this sign.
If only 0<=L(q)<=Lmax is known, replacing L0 by Lmax in (2) gives a robust
sufficient bound; replacing L0 by zero is the strongest ordinary no-feedback
comparator because it minimizes the skin's destabilizing impedance term.

With no residual prestress, the minimum capillary coefficient needed even
under the optimistic L0=0 comparator is

  Gamma_crit,0 = j0*b^2*kappa_t/(2*kappa_n^2).

This exposes a useful screening test before considering responsive compaction:
if Gamma is below Gamma_crit,0, no nonnegative upstream impedance can make the
continuum long-wave modes stable in this model. If T0 is retained, replace
Gamma by A_m in that comparison. A reported statement that compressive stress
helps crack resistance does not supply T0 or its sign in this morphology
dispersion; under the present sign convention a positive T0 raises A_m, while
a negative T0 lowers it and can also introduce a separate mechanical-buckling
problem.

Finite-domain comparison

For a finite lateral domain, let Q be its allowed nonzero Fourier wavenumbers
(excluding the conserved q=0 mean mode). The exact criterion is simply

  N0(q)<0 for every q in Q.                                 (3)

One geometry-independent sufficient cutoff is obtained from chi(q)>=G. Then
N0(q)<=-G<0 because C+L*p*S>0. For A_m>=0 and nu_m*B>0, the positive root is

  q_cap^2=[-A_m+sqrt(A_m^2+4*nu_m*B*G)]/[2*nu_m*B].

Thus q_min>=q_cap guarantees (3), though it can be conservative. For a
periodic square of side ell, q_min=2*pi/ell; for a one-dimensional
no-flux strip, q_min=pi/ell. If q_min lies below the positive band, a small
finite size alone has not removed the instability. Boundary conditions and
the actual spectrum matter; a guessed “device size” is not a substitute for
(3).

Illustrative substitution (not acquired properties)

Use only as a requested scenario: kappa_n=0.052 S/m, b=20 micrometres,
E=20 GPa, nu_p=0.3, nu_m=13e-6/96485=1.34736e-10 m^3/C,
j0=10 A/m^2, Gamma=5e-11 V m, T0=0, and the conservative constant
L=Lmax=4.4266667e-5 ohm m^2. This gives G=192.308 V/m,
B=1.46520e-5 N m, and q_cap=1.76663e4 m^-1. The same-film E and kappa_t
are not acquired; the three anisotropy ratios below are comparison inputs.

  alpha     kappa_t   Gamma_crit (L=0)   Gamma_crit (Lmax)   q_* (Lmax)   periodic side cutoff
            S/m       V m                 V m                m^-1         2*pi/q_* (mm)
  1         0.052     3.846e-8            4.731e-8           4,881        1.287
  10        0.520     3.846e-7            4.731e-7          13,249        0.474
  100       5.20      3.846e-6            4.731e-6          17,472        0.360

Here q_* is the nonzero root of N0(q)=0 before q_cap. Numerical root finding
and sign evaluation of the supplied formula with constant L=Lmax give N0>0
for 0<q<q_* and N0<0 for q_*<q<q_cap; for q>=q_cap the analytic bound
N0<=-G applies. Using Lmax is conservative because it maximizes N0 wherever
chi<G. Therefore the listed periodic-side bound is a sufficient finite-size
cutoff for any actual L(q)<=Lmax in this scenario. For a no-flux strip, the
corresponding listed length is half as large. The universal q_cap-only
periodic bound is ell<=0.356 mm for all three ratios. A domain larger than
the q_* side bound admits the first unstable mode for the constant-Lmax
periodic illustration; a smaller domain excludes the whole unstable band.
For an actual smaller L(q), the small-domain exclusion remains sufficient,
but the reverse implication does not: a larger domain may still be stable if
its actual impedance moves the band inward. Actual boundaries, L(q), E, T0,
and kappa_t must be inserted before applying it to a film.

For a common nonzero T0, (2) shifts by exactly nu_m*T0; use that same T0 in
chi when recomputing q_* and q_cap. Under the stated sign convention, the
long-wave T0 required just to reach marginality at L=Lmax is about 351,
3,511, and 35,116 N/m for alpha=1, 10, and 100. Even with L0=0 the required
values are about 285, 2,854, and 28,545 N/m. These are algebraic scenario
thresholds, not measured residual stresses. A negative signed T0 raises the
capillary burden rather than meeting it.

The long-wave margin is very large: even L0=0 gives Gamma_crit/Gamma of
769, 7,692, and 76,923 for alpha=1, 10, and 100. With L0=Lmax these ratios
are about 946, 9,463, and 94,630. Thus finite-domain exclusion, measured
surface tension/prestress, or an added restoring mechanism is required for
these illustrative inputs; calling the skin “elastic” alone does not close
the long-wave sign.

What R_h changes

For nonzero R_h the exact numerator is N=N0-j0*R_h. Positive R_h shifts every
mode downward and changes the q->0 limit from a neutral R_h=0 mode to

  j_b/h -> -j0*R_h/(r_ct+b/kappa_n+L0).

It therefore supplies a distinct long-wave restoring term, but it does not
automatically remove a finite-q band. On a specified finite domain with
discrete allowed set Q, define M_Q=max_{q in Q} N0(q). If M_Q>0, strict
stability requires and is equivalent to

  R_h > M_Q/j0.

If M_Q<=0, the R_h=0 comparator is already stable (with equality meaning a
marginal mode), and any positive R_h adds margin. On an unbounded continuum,
replace Q by q>0 and apply the modewise condition; a supremum of zero attained
only as q->0 does not by itself imply a nonzero mode is marginal. The R_h=0
calculation is the strongest ordinary identical-skin comparator for
isolating what the responsive channel term contributes.

Scope

This is linear, quasistatic ionic transport coupled to the given elastic
dispersion. It does not model pore-scale leakage, fracture, viscoelastic
relaxation, electrochemical side reactions, finite-amplitude shape change,
or a measured film. In particular, the source observation about residual
compression and crack resistance is not a numeric membrane-tension input and
does not establish stability of this plating morphology mode.
