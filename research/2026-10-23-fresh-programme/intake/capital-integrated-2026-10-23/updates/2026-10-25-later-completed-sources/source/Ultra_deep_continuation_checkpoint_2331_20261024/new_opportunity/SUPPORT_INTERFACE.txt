# Pore-mouth deposition defeats a local compression law; a conditional repair

## Scope

This is a bounded physical falsifier of the local law `d=d0-h` for a porous ionic layer. It keeps the useful mean plating current fixed and asks whether a roughness mode changes local resistance in the required direction. The result is an exact two-column ohmic counterexample and a small compliant-cell repair with its transfer condition and costs. Neither is an acquired lithium-cell result or a safety claim.

## Countermechanism: a rigid scaffold with pore-mouth deposition

Take two equal-area patches in parallel between a common current collector and a flat separator. A rigid porous scaffold is bonded to the current collector; its thickness `ds` and porosity `eps_s` stay fixed. Lithium grows at the scaffold mouths into the external electrolyte gap. A local height `h_i` therefore changes the gap `g_i=g0-h_i`, while it does not compress the scaffold. The patch's area-specific resistance is

    R_i = r_ct + ds/(kappa_l eps_s) + (g0-h_i)/kappa_g.

Here `r_ct` is the local linearized charge-transfer resistance, `kappa_l` the pore-liquid conductivity, and `kappa_g` the gap-electrolyte conductivity. The exact model is just Ohm's law in two parallel columns; all material properties are held fixed. For the zero-mean perturbation `h_+=hbar+a`, `h_-=hbar-a`, write `R= r_ct+ds/(kappa_l eps_s)+gbar/kappa_g`. Under fixed mean current density `J=(j_++j_-)/2`, the common voltage adjusts and gives, exactly while both gaps remain open,

    j_+ = J (1 + a/(kappa_g R)),
    j_- = J (1 - a/(kappa_g R)).

Thus, for `dot(h_i)=Omega_m j_i/(zF)`,

    dot(a) = [Omega_m J/(zF kappa_g R)] a > 0,
    d log(a)/d hbar = 1/(kappa_g R) > 0.

This is a positive roughness feedback at fixed useful mean deposition, not a change in total current. It comes from the ordinary geometric fact that a protrusion shortens the external ionic path. The porous-layer resistance in this geometry is constant. Here `eta=0` means a rigid scaffold or a bypass path where deposited metal consumes the outside gap without compressing the pore layer. A rigid backing by itself does not force `eta=0`: a compliant layer beneath a fixed top can still deform locally. The load path, bonding, and boundary kinematics decide the transfer.

The topology is physically relevant. The indexed primary abstract for Shin et al. reports that preferential lithium plating on top of a 3D host often causes early failure; their engineered bottom-lithiophilic/top-porous host is meant to redirect growth upward from the bottom. Zhao et al. report targeted filling of designed, interconnected carbon macropores (>50 nm), with deposition depending on pore size and local lithiophilicity. These are distinct regimes: mouth blockage is a concrete failure mode, while internal filling can be engineered. Neither makes local compressive transfer automatic. Zhang et al. (2019), identified in the parent source audit, already discusses external pressure, local pore closure, and deposition redirection; the compliant route below is therefore conventional mechanics, not a novelty claim.

## Local compliant repair and sign condition

Model a drained porous cell of local thickness `d` and fixed solid volume per projected area `s`, with `s<d<2s`. Its porosity is `eps=1-s/d`. Through-thickness ionic resistance is

    Rp(d) = d/(kappa_l eps) = d^2/[kappa_l(d-s)].

For a fixed-top series stack, local deposited advance is split between pore-layer compression `u` and outer-gap closure `x`: `h=u+x`, `delta d=-delta u=-eta delta h`, and `delta g=-delta x=-(1-eta)delta h`. This conserves the total shortened stack length. The transfer factor is a mechanical response, not a sensor. The total resistance is `Rtot=r_ct+Rp(d)+g/kappa_g`, and its roughness derivative is

    dRtot/dh = eta d(2s-d)/[kappa_l(d-s)^2] - (1-eta)/kappa_g.

Under fixed mean current, a mode is damped by this ohmic mechanism exactly when `dRtot/dh>0`, or

    eta > eta_crit = kappa_l(d-s)^2/[kappa_g d(2s-d) + kappa_l(d-s)^2].

If `eta=0`, the sign is destabilizing. For positive conductivities and `s<d<2s`, `eta_crit<1`; in the limiting case `eta=1`, there is no outer-gap shortening and compression raises pore resistance. For equal pore and gap conductivity and `d=1.5s`, the corrected fixed-top threshold is `eta_crit=1/4`. With `eta=1/2`, `dRtot/dh=+1/kappa_l`; with no compression it is `-1/kappa_g`. Adding a fixed positive `r_ct` reduces the growth/damping rate through the total-resistance denominator but does not change this sign criterion.

A small exact two-patch replay uses `s=1`, `d=1.5`, `g=1`, `kappa_l=kappa_g=1`, `r_ct=0`, mean `J=1`, and height perturbation `a=0.01`. With no local compression (`eta=0`), the resistances are `5.49, 5.51` and currents are `1.001818182, 0.998181818`. With `eta=0.5` and the fixed-top partition `delta g=-(1-eta)delta h`, resistances are `5.510202020, 5.490198020` and currents are `0.998181521, 1.001818479`. Both cases have exactly the same mean current `1`; the second reverses the feedback. The calculation is dimensionless and is not fitted to a battery.

One mechanical realization of the required transfer is a locally compliant, ion-permeable pore cell coupled in series with a mechanically supported separator. Let `u` be local pore-layer compression and `x` the remaining separator displacement, so deposited advance `h=u+x`. In a quasistatic tangent model with scaffold stiffness per area `Ks` and separator stiffness per area `Kb`, common pressure gives `Ks u=Kb x`, hence

    eta = u/h = Kb/(Ks+Kb),
    Keff = Ks Kb/(Ks+Kb),
    p = Keff h.

For equal conductivities at `d=1.5s`, the corrected resistance sign criterion needs `eta>1/4`, equivalently `Kb>Ks/3`. This is a real transfer condition for a locally responding cell. A rigid top may serve as a fixed boundary while an elastic porous layer compresses beneath it. A connected elastic film or scaffold needs its actual wavelength- and rate-dependent transfer `eta(q,omega)`, so this lumped cell must not be called a homogenized solution.

The repair is paid for. At mean deposited thickness `H`, stored mechanical work per area is `E/A=Keff H^2/2` and the pressure-induced reaction-potential increment is `Omega_m Keff H/(zF)`. The pore-layer ohmic term is `J^2 Rp` per area; even with the example's `d=1.5s`, `Rp=4.5s/kappa_l`. Pore-liquid volume `eta H` per area must be expelled. For a through-thickness Darcy drainage path of length `d`, permeability `Kperm`, viscosity `mu_l`, and average pore-volume speed `U=eta Omega_m J/(zF)`, the required pressure drop and dissipation are

    delta_p_D = mu_l d U/Kperm,
    P_D/A = delta_p_D U.

This adds a reaction-potential scale `Omega_m delta_p_D/(zF)`. A lateral drain changes the path length and requires its own flow geometry. The operation is feasible at the stated useful current only if the resulting ohmic, mechanical, and drainage voltage costs fit the cell's voltage budget and the pores remain connected. No numerical Li parameter values or such voltage budget were acquired here.

## Decisive interface test and scope

The central measurable is the local deformation transfer `eta(q,omega)=-delta d(q,omega)/delta h(q,omega)` under the actual stack load, together with the local pore ionic resistance during plating. At fixed mean current, a seeded mode should have `d log|a|/d hbar = - (dRtot/dh)/Rtot` in this coarse ohmic model, with the corrected fixed-top derivative above. If the scaffold does not compress or pore mouths seal first, the positive feedback above defeats the proposed local-compression argument. If a measured local response exceeds `eta_crit` while maintaining ionic connectivity and useful current, it supports this repair for that mode only. Curvature, SEI evolution, nucleation, stripping, contact loss, and fracture remain outside this two-column test.

## Continuous-film load path: what it does and does not enforce

A continuous elastic ion-conducting film can block metal/solution bypass only if it is dense, bonded to the intended load-bearing frame, and remains intact under plating pressure. Those conditions do not by themselves prescribe pore-column shortening. With a rigid pillar skeleton and a film that bridges its pore openings, deposited metal can deflect the film into pore voids; then pillar spacing and column thickness `d` stay fixed (`eta≈0`), while the aperture geometry and ionic resistance change by a separate mechanism that this scalar `Rp(d)` closure does not capture. A delaminated interface creates an even clearer bypass: a thin fluid/metal gap opens beneath the film and the deposit advances in that gap without compressing the pillars. Conversely, a sealed roof mechanically bonded to collapsible pore walls, with lateral restraint and a vented liquid path, can transmit pressure into pore closure; then `eta(q,omega)` is set by the combined film, wall, and boundary compliance. If trapped liquid has no outlet, pressure storage or fracture can replace the assumed drained compression.

Thus the film is a barrier only under acquired continuity/adhesion, and the compression feedback is a separate mechanical consequence. The exact load-path contract is: (i) no connected metal bypass at pore mouths; (ii) bonded contact from film through pore walls to the constrained frame; (iii) a deformation mode in which the local displacement consumes pore thickness rather than free pore volume or film deflection; and (iv) connected drainage that allows displaced electrolyte to leave. In general the kinematics are `h=u_pore+x_gap+w_film+x_upstream+delta_delam`; only `u_pore` changes `d`, so `eta=u_pore/h` must be derived from the actual mechanical boundary-value problem. An upstream fluid gap can absorb deposited volume without moving the skin; delamination adds another displacement reservoir. The mechanics and defect limits are unmeasured here. A film that merely coats the pillars or spans voids is not enough to infer `eta>eta_crit`.

For a thin bonded film spanning openings, one small-deflection Fourier model has restoring traction `p_q=(Df q^4+Tf q^2+Kpin)w_q`, where `Df` is bending rigidity, `Tf` in-plane tension, `Kpin` foundation stiffness from the pore-wall/frame contacts, and `w_q` the film deflection. The film compliance is `Cf(q)=1/(Df q^4+Tf q^2+Kpin)`. In a local series-compliance approximation, with pore-collapse compliance `Cs=1/Ks` and outer-gap compliance `Cb=1/Kb`,

    eta(q) = Cs/[Cs + Cb + Cf(q)].

This reduces to `Kb/(Ks+Kb)` only when film deflection is negligible. If the unsupported long-wave limit has `Kpin=0`, then `Cf(q)` grows as `q` decreases and local pore compression is not guaranteed; edge constraints or a load-bearing foundation must supply the missing stiffness. This plate relation is a conditional thin-sheet calculation, not a Li3N constitutive claim. If film deflection intrudes into a pore instead of the external gap, it changes the aperture resistance and requires a different transport model rather than the scalar `Rp(d)` formula.

## Historical draft claims superseded by this correction

The initial draft incorrectly combined `delta d=-eta delta h` with `delta g=-delta h`, shortening both series layers by a total `(1+eta)delta h`. Its resulting threshold `kappa_l(d-s)^2/[kappa_g d(2s-d)]` and its `eta=1/2` numerical currents apply only to that inconsistent double-shortened geometry; they are not the fixed-top result. The corrected model partitions the shortening as `h=u+x` and uses `delta g=-(1-eta)delta h`, giving `eta_crit=kappa_l(d-s)^2/[kappa_g d(2s-d)+kappa_l(d-s)^2]`. The initial draft also said a rigid common backing cannot produce nonzero-mode compression. That inference was too strong: a deformable layer beneath a fixed rigid top can compress laterally nonuniformly. The valid counterexample is specifically a rigid scaffold, bypass, or absent load transfer that leaves `d` unchanged.


For byte-level interpretive history, the superseded draft double-shortened finite example reported (at `eta=1/2`) `R+=5.505202020`, `R-=5.495198020`, `j+/J=0.999090578`, and `j-/J=1.000909422`. Those numbers came from setting both `delta d=-eta delta h` and `delta g=-delta h`; they are not the corrected fixed-top result above.
