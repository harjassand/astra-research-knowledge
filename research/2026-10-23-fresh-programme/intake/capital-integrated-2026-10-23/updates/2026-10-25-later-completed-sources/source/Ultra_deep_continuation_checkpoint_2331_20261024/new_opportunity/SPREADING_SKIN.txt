IONIC SPREADING SKIN: EXPLICIT PORE-TO-METAL TRANSFER
Owned continuation of CONSTRUCTION.txt, 24 October 2026

Purpose

The ideal column law in CONSTRUCTION.txt does not follow just from finding a
porous membrane. A metal tip can grow inside a pore without compressing the
skeleton. A continuous electronically insulating ionic skin under the channel
layer is a candidate way to span pore mouths and distribute ionic injection over
the metal surface. This note derives the actual competing transport effects;
it does not assume that a skin spreads microscopic current without bypassing
the intended larger-scale feedback. Ordinary protective artificial-SEI/solid-ion
films are allowed the same properties and are strong conventional comparators.

Geometry and linear regime

The metal is z=h(x); the lower ionic skin occupies h(x)<z<h(x)+b and has fixed
vertical thickness b and isotropic conductivity kappa_s. At linear order this
equals fixed normal thickness. The channel layer above ends at a fixed backing,
so its local thickness decreases by h. Its area-specific resistance has value
R and height derivative R_h. The remaining bulk, measured at the fixed backing,
has Fourier impedance Z_b(q)>0. Define L(q)=R+Z_b(q), assumed <=Lmax.

The skin has no electronic leakage. Concentrations are uniform, conduction is
quasistatic Ohmic, and the flat metal's differential charge-transfer resistance
is r>=0. Curvature raises the equilibrium potential by chi*h, chi=Gamma*q^2,
Gamma>0. Let j0>0 be the mean plating current, G=j0/kappa_s, q>0, p=kappa_s*q,
C=cosh(qb), S=sinh(qb). The potential perturbation in the skin solves
u''-q^2 u=0. Its evaluation on each moving face matters:

 u(0)=r*j_b+(chi-G)h,
 u(b)=W-G h,
 j_b=kappa_s*u'(0), j_t=kappa_s*u'(b).

Here W is the *physical* upper-face potential perturbation. The channels and
bulk give W=-L*j_t-j0*R_h*h. This includes the channel thickness/porosity change;
it must not be counted again as a shortened outer gap. Transfer across the skin
gives

 W=(r*C+S/p)j_b+[C*chi-(C-1)G]h,
 j_t=(C+r*p*S)j_b+p*S*(chi-G)h.

Therefore

 j_b/h=N(q)/D(q),
 N=-j0*R_h+G*(C-1+L*p*S)-chi*(C+L*p*S),
 D=r*C+S/p+L*(C+r*p*S)>0.                                   (S1)

The exact skin geometry term is destabilizing below its capillary cutoff. It
cannot be omitted when claiming that pore compression controls metal roughness.
At q->0, (S1) becomes

 j_b/h -> -j0*R_h/[r+b/kappa_s+R+Z_b(0)],

which recovers the column sign with the paid vertical skin resistance b/kappa_s.

A conservative all-wavelength linear certificate

Set ell_cap^2=kappa_s*Gamma/j0 and beta=b/ell_cap. For q>=1/ell_cap, chi>=G and
N=-j0*R_h-G+(G-chi)*(C+L*p*S)<0 whenever R_h>0. For q<1/ell_cap, ignore the
stabilizing chi term and maximize the increasing positive factors. Thus

 kappa_s*R_h > cosh(beta)-1
                 +Lmax*kappa_s/ell_cap*sinh(beta)             (S2)

is sufficient for every q>0. This is conservative, explicit, and uniform over
bulk terminations satisfying the stated impedance bound. It is not obtained by
sampling a dispersion curve. For a homogeneous finite bulk slab its Fourier
admittance increases from g=kappa_b/L_b, hence Z_b(q)<=1/g.

The inequality shows the resource tradeoff: low skin conductivity, excessive
thickness, or large upstream resistance can destroy the macro feedback even
when the ideal pore-column derivative is positive. Increasing r slows all
response and changes microscopic spreading, but cannot change N's sign.

Microscopic injection smoothing and forced roughness

For a flat skin driven by an imposed upper-face Fourier current I_q, with a
flat metal (h=0), the bottom current is exactly

 j_b(q)=T(q)*I_q,
 T(q)=1/[cosh(qb)+r*kappa_s*q*sinh(qb)] <=sech(qb).            (S3)

This is an exact linear conduction statement even when the imposed injection
contrast itself is large. For qb<<1, T is approximately
[1+r*kappa_s*b*q^2]^-1. Thus the kinetic spreading length is
ell_s=sqrt(r*kappa_s*b). For a conductance/voltage forcing arriving through
the channels, the complete denominator D adds the upstream resistance and
must be used instead of (S3). The skin's spatial smoothing is a paid finite
operator, not a new observation capability.

If a fixed microscopic injection forcing I_q is treated independently of h, the
same skin transfer gives

 j_b=[I_q+p*S*(G-chi)h]/[C+r*p*S].                           (S4)

For q>sqrt(2)/ell_cap, the capillary-stabilized forced equilibrium therefore has

 |h_q| <=2|I_q|/[kappa_s*Gamma*q^3*sinh(qb)].                 (S5)

The slope and deflection predicted by (S5) must be small enough to justify the
linear moving-boundary calculation. A pore mask has a Fourier spectrum, not one
sine mode; each relevant component must be propagated, and pore-current
dependence on the evolving interface remains a further coupled problem. This
does not yet prove nonlinear homogenization of an arbitrary pore geometry.

Mechanical load path, not just ionic smoothing

For a circular pore radius a and a clamped, thin elastic skin, bending stiffness
B_skin=E*b^3/[12(1-nu_p^2)] gives central deflection

 w0=Delta_p*a^4/(64 B_skin).

The actual restraint and thin-plate validity must be checked. A free film is not
automatically clamped by a porous skeleton. At a macroscopic shape mode, the
bending-strain scale is b*q^2*|h|/2 and bending pressure is B_skin*q^4*h;
stretching and residual stress add separate terms. The skin must span a pore
well enough that metal volume advances its lower envelope rather than intruding
through an opening; defects, fracture, delamination or electronic leakage defeat
that load path. A measured bulk E or kappa is not a measured 10-nm-film property.

Electrolyte must drain through an actual outlet. Straight cylindrical pores have
hydraulic permeability k_h=epsilon*a^2/8 and required pressure drop
Delta_p_D=mu*d*U/k_h for superficial expelled speed U. Fluid/wall storage sets
the finite response. Reaction pressure coupling depends on the metal and ionic
partial molar volumes; neither its sign nor its source is supplied by Faraday
height alone if concentration, solvation, swelling or SEI volume also changes.

Illustrative conjunction, not an acquired material

Take s=1 micrometre, d=1.6 micrometre, kappa_l=1 S/m in the ideal straight-pore
law. Then epsilon=.375, R=4.2666667e-6 ohm m^2 and R_h=1.7777778 ohm m. Set
j0=10 A/m^2, Gamma=5e-11 V m, kappa_s=.1 S/m, b=10 nm, r=1e-4 ohm m^2,
and a bulk 20 micrometres thick with kappa_b=.5 S/m. These are supplied design
specifications, not measurements of one material or a lithium cell.

Here ell_cap=.7071 micrometre, ell_s=.3162 micrometre, and Lmax=4.4266667e-5
ohm m^2. The left side of (S2) is .17778 while its right side is approximately
.08864, so the stated model has a nonvacuous all-q linear certificate. A 100-nm
periodic injection pattern is attenuated by about 2.37e-3 according to (S3).
The vertical skin drop is j0*b/kappa_s=1 microvolt; the actual charge-transfer
drop must use eta(j0), not assume a differential resistance is a DC resistance.
Mechanical span, electronic leakage, chemical compatibility, actual thin-film
conductivity and the interface-to-pore deformation transfer are unacquired.
LiPON with a much smaller actual kappa_s may fail (S2); one cannot combine an
unrelated stiff film with a different material's ionic conductivity.

What this does and does not close

(S1)--(S5) close the linear transport operation of this specific two-layer
geometry and expose a finite window in which microscopic current spreading and
macroscopic compressive feedback can coexist. They are not a finite-amplitude
free-boundary theorem. The finite-amplitude ordering theorem in CONSTRUCTION.txt
applies to its ideal local column interface; adding this skin changes that
interface and does not inherit that theorem automatically. Nor does the positive
plating certificate solve stripping, actuation, SEI or fracture. The next serious
step is a physically consistent coupled microgeometry/deformation closure or a
controlled nonlinear estimate, not another assumed current-smoothing layer.
