1. Rotation-only constitutive model: sign check

Use the ordered in-plane basis (x,z), with

  R(theta)=[[cos(theta),-sin(theta)],[sin(theta),cos(theta)]],
  K0=diag(kappa_t,kappa_n),  Delta_k=kappa_t-kappa_n.

For the Fourier displacement u_x=iU(z)e^{iqx}, u_z=w(z)e^{iqx}, the infinitesimal skew rotation in this ordered basis is

  theta=(partial_x u_z-partial_z u_x)/2
       =i(qw-U')/2.

The symmetric tensor push by this rigid rotation has

  delta K_xz=Delta_k theta,
  delta K_xx=delta K_zz=0

at first order. This sign is consistent with the stated R convention: its off-diagonal component is (kappa_t-kappa_n)theta. Reversing the definition of positive rotation (for example, using a right-hand +y angle with the x,z axes) reverses both theta and the R convention; changing only one would be a sign error.

Let the flat base potential gradient be G e_z, G=j0/kappa_n. In the spatial conductivity equation div(K grad phi)=0, the linearized Fourier equation is

  kappa_n phi''-kappa_t q² phi
       = -i q G delta K_xz
       = G Delta_k q(qw-U')/2.                              (A1)

Thus the proposed sign and factor 1/2 are correct. No derivative of delta K_zz appears for rotation alone because delta K_zz=0. This is an interior source; moving-boundary potential shifts and channel terms remain as separately derived in the prior elastic-layer audit.

For the thin conformal Kirchhoff displacement w=h, U'=-q h, theta=i qh and the right side of (A1) is the z-independent value G Delta_k q² h. The constant particular solution is

  phi_p=-G Delta_k/kappa_t * h
       =-G(1-kappa_n/kappa_t)h.

Writing rho=kappa_t/kappa_n, phi_p=-G(1-rho^{-1})h. The actual base field remains G=j0/kappa_n; only the effective geometric coefficient in the homogeneous boundary reduction changes. Let phi=phi_h+phi_p, with phi_h solving the homogeneous anisotropic slab equation. The lower kinetic boundary becomes

  phi_h(0)=r j_b+[chi-G/rho]h.

For top displacement v=m h, the physical top potential shift is W=phi(b)+Gv=phi_h(b)+G[m-(1-rho^{-1})]h. Using the same channel closure W=-L j_t-j0 R_v v and j_t=(C+r p S)j_b+pS(chi-G/rho)h gives

  j_b/h = N_rot/D,
  N_rot=(G/rho-chi)(C+L pS)+G(1-rho^{-1}-m)-m j0 R_v,
  D=r C+S/p+L(C+r pS).                                  (A2)

Here lambda=q sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n), p=q sqrt(kappa_n kappa_t), C=cosh(lambda b), S=sinh(lambda b). For m=1 and R_v=R_h, (A2) reduces to

  N_rot=(G/rho-chi)(C+L pS)-G/rho-j0 R_h,

so every geometric G in the old S1 numerator is replaced by G/rho, while G itself remains the amplitude of the material-rotation source and the actual base gradient. For rho=1, the rotation source vanishes and (A2) returns the nonconvected arbitrary-top-motion result. For m!=1, the correction G(1-rho^{-1}-m) must be retained; simply substituting G/rho into the whole S1 expression would be wrong.

This particular solution relies on theta being constant through the thickness. For the exact finite-thickness elastic two-port, U'(z) and w(z) generally vary; then (A1) has a z-dependent source. A constant phi_p is not a solution, and its contribution must be integrated through the Green/transfer problem along with the actual face displacements. The parent construction owner is doing that full coupling; (A2) is only the stated thin conformal limit.

2. Rotation is not a general deformation push-forward

A conductivity tensor whose principal values stay fixed in the *current* configuration and whose only change is material-axis rotation uses the rotation-only law above. That law intentionally has no strain-induced changes to kappa_t or kappa_n.

A different, common continuum construction starts from a reference conductivity K0 per reference volume and maps conserved flux into the deformed spatial domain. With deformation gradient F=I+grad u, Jacobian J=det F, reference flux j0=-K0 grad_X phi, and spatial flux j=J^{-1}F j0, the spatial tensor is

  K_sp=J^{-1}F K0 F^T.

Linearizing in two-dimensional plane strain gives

  delta K_xx=kappa_t(u_x,x-u_z,z),
  delta K_zz=kappa_n(u_z,z-u_x,x),
  delta K_xz=kappa_n u_x,z+kappa_t u_z,x
               =(kappa_t+kappa_n)eps_xz
                    +(kappa_t-kappa_n)theta,
  eps_xz=(u_x,z+u_z,x)/2.

The rotation-only term is recovered when the symmetric shear strain is zero, but the Piola push-forward also has strain/area factors, including diagonal perturbations. For the Fourier displacement above,

  delta K_xz=i(kappa_n U'+kappa_t q w),
  delta K_zz=kappa_n(w'+qU).

Substituting these into div(K_sp grad phi)=0 yields the distinct bulk source

  kappa_n phi''-kappa_t q² phi
       =-G[iq delta K_xz+delta K_zz']
       =G[kappa_t q²w-kappa_n w''].                        (A3)

For the conformal Kirchhoff field w=h, U'=-q h, the Piola source is G kappa_t q²h, whereas the rotation-only source is G(kappa_t-kappa_n)q²h. Their difference is G kappa_n q²h. This is a concrete demonstration that “axes convect” alone does not determine how a conducting microstructure responds to finite material strain.

The Piola rule is not automatically the right law for a real electrolyte film: a measured current-configuration conductivity may remain approximately constant in magnitude under small strain, may change with porosity/contact, or may require a different constitutive tensor. Conversely, if transport is derived in material coordinates with conserved reference flux, omitting the metric/Jacobian factors is not generally consistent. The correct choice is a constitutive acquisition/modeling decision, not an algebraic preference. A full moving-boundary model also needs the associated flux and geometry boundary terms; A3 reports the interior source only.

3. Scope and physical gates

The exact sign/factor check in A1 and the thin conformal result A2 are conditional on (i) a spatial Ohmic PDE, (ii) an anisotropic tensor with fixed eigenvalues and axes convected only by the local rigid rotation, (iii) the specified orientation convention, and (iv) a prescribed displacement field. They do not establish that the same skin has strain-independent ionic conductivity, that it remains connected or bonded, or that its top displacement follows the skeleton. A deformation-pushforward model changes the source as in A3 and needs matching boundary terms. No residual stress, pore-shape response, concentration dependence, electronic leakage or full exact elastic/transport solution is claimed here.
