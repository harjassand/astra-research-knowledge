SUPPORT BENDING AUDIT — anisotropic skin with conservative plate response

Bounded assignment

“Bounded new calculation, same existing support: audit the elastic ionic-skin extension to exact SPREADING_SKIN S1. Let tensor kappa_n,kappa_t>0, p=q sqrt(kappa_n kappa_t), C=cosh(q b sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n)), S=sinh(...), G=j0/kappa_n, and chi(q)=Gamma q²+nu B q⁴, B=E b³/[12(1-nu_p²)], nu=Omega/(zF)>0 from derivative of positive film bending energy, not hydraulic partial-molar-volume sign. Derive exact N/D and a sufficient all-q condition using q_* solving chi=G. Challenge finite-domain/fixed-normal-thickness conventions, mechanical-pressure sign and tensor conductivity assumptions. Independently derive small-q*b, plate-dominated optimal thickness and how it depends on kappa_t/kappa_n. No new agents or broad source audit. Save SUPPORT_BENDING_AUDIT.txt; distinguish continuum plate and material gates.”

1. Exact transfer formula

Keep the SPREADING_SKIN.txt S1 geometry and linearization: a planar Fourier mode of wavenumber magnitude q, a skin with fixed normal thickness b and constant positive conductivity tensor aligned with the interface normal/tangent, a channel/bulk response L(q)=R+Z_b(q), and charge-transfer resistance r>=0. Here the skin PDE is

  kappa_n u_zz - kappa_t q² u = 0,
  lambda=q sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n),
  p=kappa_n lambda=q sqrt(kappa_n kappa_t),
  C=cosh(lambda b), S=sinh(lambda b).

The transfer relation is the isotropic S1 relation with q replaced by lambda in hyperbolic functions and kappa_s q replaced by p. In the stated convention, the lower and upper boundary perturbations are

  W=(r C+S/p) j_b + [C chi-(C-1)G]h,
  j_t=(C+r p S)j_b + p S(chi-G)h.

The channel/bulk closure remains W=-L j_t-j0 R_h h. Eliminating W and j_t gives

  j_b/h=N(q)/D(q),
  N=-j0 R_h+G(C-1+L p S)-chi(C+L p S),
  D=r C+S/p+L(C+r p S)>0.                         (B1)

The algebraic extension is exact within the linear PDE/port model. `L` may depend on q; use 0<L(q)<=Lmax for a uniform sufficient bound. The denominator is positive for q>0, positive conductivities, r>=0 and passive positive L.

The added `chi` is a potential cost per unit interface height. For a small-slope Kirchhoff-Love plate of constant thickness b, the positive bending energy is

  E_bend[h]=(B/2) integral (Delta h)^2 dA,
  delta E_bend/dh=B Delta² h,
  B=E b³/[12(1-nu_p²)].

Thus for a Fourier mode the bending chemical-potential/voltage increment is

  chi_bend h = (Omega/(zF)) B q^4 h = nu B q^4 h.

It enters with the same suppressing sign as capillarity: it raises the equilibrium potential at a positive protrusion. This uses the cost of adding metal against a positive elastic energy, not an assumed hydraulic pressure sign. A restoring mechanical force is minus the energy gradient; confusing that force sign with the positive incremental reaction-energy sign would reverse the electrochemical term. A fluid pressure term requires its own signed partial-molar-volume/work derivation and is not included in `chi`.

Dimensional check: B has units N m, nu=Omega/(zF) has units m^3/C, so nu B q^4 has units V/m, like G=j0/kappa_n and Gamma q². `p` has units S/m², so L p is dimensionless, and each term in D has units ohm m².

2. All-wavelength sufficient condition

Assume j0>0, R_h>0, Gamma>=0 and nu B>0. The monotonically increasing

  chi(q)=Gamma q²+nu B q⁴

has one positive root q_* of chi(q_*)=G. Explicitly, with x_*=q_*²,

  x_*=[sqrt(Gamma²+4 nu B G)-Gamma]/(2 nu B).

For q>=q_*, rewrite the numerator exactly as

  N=(G-chi)(C+L p S)-G-j0 R_h<0.

For 0<q<q_*, drop only the nonpositive `-chi(C+LpS)` contribution, and bound the increasing C and pS by their values at q_*. If L(q)<=Lmax, then

  N(q) <= -j0 R_h + G(C_*-1+Lmax p_* S_*).

Therefore this is a sufficient all-q certificate:

  kappa_n R_h > C_*-1+Lmax p_* S_*,                  (B2)

where lambda_*=q_*sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n), C_*=cosh(lambda_*b), S_*=sinh(lambda_*b), and p_*=q_*sqrt(kappa_n kappa_t). The factor `kappa_n` follows from j0/G=kappa_n. Equality is not included in the strict sufficient condition. At q=0, which is the galvanostatically removed mean mode, N(0)=-j0 R_h; (B2) controls the nonzero modes as well as the continuum limit.

This is sufficient, not necessary: capillarity/bending were discarded in the lower band when bounding N, so actual stability can hold when (B2) fails. For a finite lateral domain, q belongs to its discrete Fourier/eigenmode set; (B2) remains sufficient but can be conservative, while the exact check is (B1) at each allowed nonzero q. The transfer formula assumes a laterally unbounded or periodic uniform flat base and a diagonal, constant tensor (`kappa_n`,`kappa_t`) aligned to the normal/tangent. A full tensor with normal-tangential cross terms changes the mode transfer and is not represented by this substitution.

3. Thin-transfer, plate-dominated asymptotics and thickness optimum

The small-transfer parameter is not just qb when conductivity is anisotropic. It is

  s_*=lambda_* b=q_* b sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n) << 1.

Expanding C-1=s_*²/2+O(s_*^4) and p_*S_*=kappa_t q_*² b[1+O(s_*²)] yields the right side of (B2):

  X(b):=C_*-1+Lmax p_*S_*
       =q_*² [kappa_t b²/(2 kappa_n)+Lmax kappa_t b]
         + higher orders in s_*.

For a plate-dominated cutoff, nu B q_*^4 >> Gamma q_*², equivalently Gamma² << nu B G, so

  q_*² ~= sqrt(G/(nu B))
       =sqrt(12 G(1-nu_p²)/(nu E)) b^(-3/2).

Treating kappa_n, kappa_t, Lmax, G, E, nu and nu_p as fixed while varying b, insertion gives

  X(b) ~= kappa_t sqrt(12 G(1-nu_p²)/(nu E))
         [ b^(1/2)/(2 kappa_n) + Lmax b^(-1/2) ].

For Lmax>0 this leading expression has its unique minimum at

  b_opt ~= 2 Lmax kappa_n,
  X_min ~= kappa_t sqrt(24 Lmax G(1-nu_p²)/(nu E kappa_n)).

Thus at this order b_opt is independent of kappa_t/kappa_n (with kappa_n and Lmax held fixed): both transfer contributions carry a common factor kappa_t, which cancels in their balance. The minimum required dimensionless feedback grows with kappa_t/sqrt(kappa_n); higher tangential relative conductivity makes the all-q sufficient condition harder. At the optimum, the two expansion terms are equal and X_min~=s_*². Small-transfer validity therefore requires

  s_*(b_opt) ~= [24 Lmax G(1-nu_p²) kappa_t²/(nu E kappa_n)]^(1/4) << 1,

and the plate model separately requires q_* b_opt<<1, small slopes/deflections and continuum wavelengths large compared with the film thickness. Large kappa_t/kappa_n can invalidate the small-transfer approximation even if qb is small. If Lmax=0 the expansion has no finite positive optimum (it decreases toward b=0), and the plate/continuum assumptions fail before that limit can be treated as a design result. The exact optimum outside these asymptotics must minimize the hyperbolic expression (B2) with q_* depending on b; no exact global optimum is claimed.

If Gamma is not negligible, use the exact root above. For fixed b, adding a positive bending term lowers q_* relative to the capillarity-only value sqrt(G/Gamma), so it shrinks the band in which geometric current focusing must be dominated. In the plate regime, since B is proportional to b³, thicker films lower q_* as b^(-3/4); simultaneously their transfer thickness lambda_* b scales as b^(1/4), so the thin-transfer approximation eventually breaks down as b grows.

4. Geometry and material gates

`B1`/`B2` assume that the skin maintains fixed normal thickness b while its lower surface follows h and its upper face translates accordingly; the channel layer above it loses thickness h relative to a fixed backing, which is already represented once by R_h. This is the fixed-normal-thickness convention in SPREADING_SKIN.txt. If instead the skin's upper face is held fixed in laboratory coordinates while the metal lower face moves, its thickness changes to first order. Then the transfer matrix, p/C/S factors, and possibly B and the conductivities acquire additional shape variations; merely replacing Gamma q² by chi(q) in S1 is not exact. Likewise, if b or E varies with compression, the constant-B plate term and fixed-coefficient skin PDE need relinearization. The mechanical boundary conditions (clamped/supporting frame versus free edge), residual tension, adhesion, stretching, fracture and load transmission determine whether the `B q^4` law applies. For finite plates, edge conditions select eigenmodes and may add tension terms; the continuum all-q Fourier certificate is a local/periodic-plane result, not the finite-device spectrum.

The derivation closes only a linear continuum implication: if a positive, constant-thickness elastic plate is genuinely forced to follow the metal profile, its positive bending-energy cost adds a q^4 stabilizing electrochemical shift and tightens (or can relax, through lowering q_*) the range of q needing resistance feedback. It does not establish a continuous elastic/ion-conducting material with the assumed tensor conductivity, mechanical attachment, pore-spanning operation, or simultaneous pore-level and macro-level stability. Those are separate material and interface acquisition gates.

5. Signed built-in membrane stress

If the film has a uniform in-plane membrane force T0 (N/m), positive in tension, its quadratic shape energy adds (T0/2) integral |grad h|². Its variational chemical-potential term is -T0 Delta h, hence +T0 q² h in Fourier space. With the same positive metal volume-per-charge coefficient nu, the appropriate coefficient is therefore

  A0 = Gamma + nu T0,
  chi(q) = A0 q² + nu B q^4.

This is the energy derivative in the deposition overpotential convention. Compressive T0<0 does not mean adding an always-stabilizing positive pressure: it can make A0 negative. The exact S1 extension (B1) remains valid with this chi if T0, B, b and the conductivities are fixed, the skin follows h, and the mechanics is quasi-static and represented solely by this quadratic energy derivative.

For B>0 and G>0, chi(q)=G still has exactly one positive root in x=q², even if A0<0:

  x_* = [-A0 + sqrt(A0²+4 nu B G)]/(2 nu B),  q_*=sqrt(x_*).

For A0<0, chi has minimum chi_min=-A0²/(4 nu B) at q²=-A0/(2 nu B), so set chi_neg=A0²/(4 nu B); for A0>=0 set chi_neg=0. On q>=q_*, chi>=G and N=(G-chi)(C+LpS)-G-j0R_h<0 when R_h>=0. On q<q_*, chi>=-chi_neg and H(q)=C+L(q)pS is positive and bounded above by H_*=C_*+Lmax p_*S_*. A convenient sufficient all-q condition is

  kappa_n R_h > (1+chi_neg/G) H_* - 1.              (B3)

It follows by bounding (G-chi)H <= (G+chi_neg)H_* below q_*; it reduces exactly to (B2) when A0>=0. A less conservative bound that is still sufficient replaces the product of separate maxima by the one-dimensional supremum

  kappa_n R_h > sup_{0<=q<=q_*} { [1-chi(q)/G]
                                      [C(q)+Lmax p(q)S(q)] - 1 }.

The latter can be evaluated from the actual parameters; it is not necessarily attained at q_* when A0<0. The simple (B3) remains a valid analytic certificate.

The sign of A0 also exposes a distinct mechanics gate. If A0<0, the quadratic energy coefficient A0 q²+nu Bq⁴ is negative for 0<q²<-A0/(nu B), with its most negative value at q²=-A0/(2nu B). This is a compressive-film buckling/shape instability in the bare plate-plus-interface energy, independent of electrochemical current focusing. A substrate foundation, adhesion, contact, residual stress relaxation or nonlinear post-buckling law could alter it, but none is in S1. Thus (B3) only certifies the *deposition current response* within a prescribed-shape linearization; it cannot establish mechanical stability of a film with compressive prestress.

The plate-dominated small-transfer optimum in section 3 survives to leading order with signed stress only under the stronger condition A0² << nu B(b) G near the candidate optimum, so that |A0|q_*² is negligible against the bending term at cutoff. Then q_*²~=sqrt(G/(nu B)) and b_opt~=2Lmax kappa_n as before. If that condition fails, the stress term changes q_*(b), the hyperbolic threshold and its optimum; no stress-independent thickness optimum follows. Built-in stress is not an uncharged stabilizing resource: T0 and its variation under cycling must be measured or physically derived.
