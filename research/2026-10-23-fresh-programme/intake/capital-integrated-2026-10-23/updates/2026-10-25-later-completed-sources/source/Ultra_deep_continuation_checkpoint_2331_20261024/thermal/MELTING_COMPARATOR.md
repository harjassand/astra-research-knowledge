# Quantum stability and the melting-velocity comparator

The closest conceptual ancestry identified by root is [Mousatov-Hartnoll, Nature Physics16,579-584(2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0828-6). The publisher page was inspected, and the primary [arXiv1908.04792v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1908.04792) main argument and AppendixA scattering derivation were read, especially equations2-10 and12-19.

Their governing construction is anharmonic high-temperature phonon diffusion. Equations6-7 combine a local vibrational-energy ceiling of order k_B T_M with uncertainty to obtain hbar sqrt(K/M) less than that melting scale. Equation8 turns this into an upper sound-speed scale. Equations3-4 and AppendixA estimate classical umklapp scattering, yielding the proposed lifetime relation in equation10. Lindemann displacement and zero-point stability are explicit conceptual ingredients. Their constants are parametric estimates and their transport observable is diffusivity/lifetime, not the intrinsic ballistic channel integral.

This antecedent means the broad claim that quantum displacement stability constrains thermal transport is established. The present work must not be presented as the first such principle, nor as a new derivation of the thermal-conductance quantum.

The current graph theorem asks a different precise question. A soft lowest branch over the Brillouin zone forces within-cell deformation on a set of wave numbers of finite measure. A cap on total Gibbs relative displacement therefore places a LOWER bound on the extent of an acoustic frequency interval carrying at least one right-moving channel. The proof counts every branch and crossing, permits arbitrary finite positive graph order and retuning, and gives explicit mass/range/stiffness constants. It assumes neither an atom's binding-energy ceiling nor a melting temperature or an anharmonic scattering law. Its oscillator zero-point ingredient is ordinary physics; the all-graph uniform coverage argument is the additional mathematical claim to compare.

The conventional explanation is therefore kept intact: stable localization constrains vibrational frequencies, and Landauer mode counting converts acoustic coverage into intrinsic conductance. The work sharpens that explanation under a declared graph/resource contract. Whether an earlier theorem already gives the same uniform quantitative statement remains UNKNOWN. A Lindemann-style choice of the input range d would require separate physical evidence; it is not acquired by this comparison.
