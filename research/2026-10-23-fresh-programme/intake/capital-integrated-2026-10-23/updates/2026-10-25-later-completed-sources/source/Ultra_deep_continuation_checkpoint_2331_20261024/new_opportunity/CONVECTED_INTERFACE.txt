MATERIAL-AXIS ROTATION, THE STRONGER ORDINARY COATING, AND THE PAID GAIN
Owned continuation of ELASTIC_INTERFACE.txt, 24 October 2026

Scientific change

Whole review exposed a load-bearing constitutive premise in the anisotropic
frozen-coefficient interface. A real crystalline skin can rotate its conductivity
axes as it bends. This note derives that different operator and repeats the
same-skin comparison. The frozen-axis all-q theorem remains a valid theorem for
its stated model; its positive ordinary-skin witness is not evidence that the
stronger rotating ordinary coating is unstable. Original proof/replay files are
preserved. No material or physical cell was acquired by either calculation.

1. Rotation-only constitutive law and exact moving-interface response

Retain the unstressed, shear-free, isotropic elastic two-port of E1--E2.
The ionic tensor has intrinsic eigenvalues k_t,k_n independent of strain, and
rotates by the material's infinitesimal rotation. This is a physical constitutive
assumption, distinct from assigning a fixed Eulerian tensor or a Piola-transformed
reference conductivity. The loaded tangent and strain-dependent intrinsic
conductivity of the actual film remain unacquired.

For a mode exp(i q x), write the horizontal displacement as i U(z) and vertical
displacement as w(z), w(0)=h, w(b)=m h. The infinitesimal rotation in the ordered
(x,z) coordinates is theta=i(qw-U')/2. Rotation of diag(k_t,k_n) gives

 delta k_xz=(k_t-k_n)theta.

Linearizing divergence of electrochemical current about the base gradient
G=j0/k_n therefore gives

 k_n phi''-k_t q^2 phi
       =G(k_t-k_n)q(qw-U')/2.                            (C1)

This source is first order in h. It cannot be removed by calling the conductor
quasistatic. The curl of the homogeneous isotropic Navier equation is harmonic.
With zero shear traction, U'+qw=0 on both faces. Thus

 Rrot(z)=(qw-U')/(2q h)
       =[sinh(q(b-z))+m sinh(qz)]/sinh(qb)>0,             (C2)

Rrot(0)=1 and Rrot(b)=m. Define phi_p=-G Rrot h. Because Rrot''=q^2 Rrot,
this is an exact particular solution of C1, for any k_t/k_n (including the
degenerate isotropic case where it is simply a homogeneous solution).

Let psi=phi+G Rrot h. It satisfies the homogeneous ionic ODE, while its physical
boundary potentials and currents are

 psi(0)=r j_b+chi h,  psi(b)=W,
 j_b=k_n psi'(0)-j0 Rrot'(0)h,
 j_t=k_n psi'(b)-j0 Rrot'(b)h,
 W=-L j_t-j0 R_v m h.

Consequently the same positive denominator D in E4 has numerator

 N_rot=-chi H-j0 m R_v
       -j0[(S/p+L C)Rrot'(0)-L Rrot'(b)],                (C3)
 H=C+L p S,
 Rrot'(0)=q[m-cosh(qb)]/sinh(qb),
 Rrot'(b)=q[m cosh(qb)-1]/sinh(qb).

For k_t=k_n this reduces algebraically to E4, not just in a numerical limit.
The nonzero-mode q->0 limit is also E5.

2. What the previous all-q proof still establishes

If rho=k_t/k_n>=1, the source in C1 is nonnegative for h>0 by C2. Variation of
constants across the skin adds the positive terms

 I_phi=integral_0^b [sinh(q_s(b-z))/p]
                   G(k_t-k_n)q^2 Rrot(z) dz,
 I_j=integral_0^b cosh(q_s(b-z))
                   G(k_t-k_n)q^2 Rrot(z) dz.

Relative to the frozen-axis numerator, N_rot=N_frozen-I_phi-L I_j. Thus

 N_rot<=N_frozen for rho>=1.                            (C4)

The rational all-q sufficient certificate already proved in E7 therefore also
certifies the responsive rotating-axis model, under this strain-independent
intrinsic-conductivity law. This is an analytic comparison of operators; no
frequency sampling is needed. For rho<1 the inequality reverses and one must
recalculate. Strain changes of the eigenvalues can also add different sources.

3. The ordinary identical coating is stronger than the first comparator

Let a=q b, t=tanh(a/2). Rewrite the geometric part of C3 as

 N_geo=j0 q t [S/p+L(C+1)]
   +j0(1-m)q/sinh(a) [S/p+L(C-cosh(a))].                 (C5)

For rho>=1 both displayed terms are nonnegative. The dependence on lateral
conductivity differs greatly from simply keeping the old G(C-1+LpS) term.
For a nearly conformal thin skin, the leading long-wave geometry coefficient
is j0[b^2/(2k_n)+L b], independent of rho. Axis rotation compensates the
spurious leading rho amplification of the frozen-axis comparison.

Here is a sufficient all-q test for the ordinary R_v=0 coating. Use the same
q_bar, k_plus_bar, k_minus_0, m_min, c_B and B as E6--E7. Let

 delta_bar=2(k_plus_bar+K_w)/k_minus_0,
 A_bar=(b/k_n) sinh(q_s_bar b)/(q_s_bar b),
 C_bar=cosh(q_s_bar b),
 J_bar=j0 delta_bar/b [A_bar+L_max(C_bar-1)],
 G2=j0 b/2 [A_bar+L_max(C_bar+1)],
 chi0_lower=nu_m K_w m_min^2,
 C4=nu_m c_B B.

Because 1-m<=delta_bar, q/sinh(qb)<=1/b, and
q tanh(qb/2)<=b q^2/2, C5 and E6 give, for q<=q_bar,

 N_ordinary<=J_bar-chi0_lower+(G2-Gamma)q^2-C4 q^4
 <=J_bar-chi0_lower+max(G2-Gamma,0)^2/(4 C4).            (C6)

If the final expression is negative, low-q stability is uniform. High-q
stability follows from the same chi>=G argument as E7. This is conservative
but explicit and uses all the ordinary coating's material-axis response.

For the original E9 fixture, K_w=1.583e11 Pa/m (mu=1MPa), the exact-rational
C6 upper bound is less than-17.9742V/m. Thus the rotating ordinary coating is itself
all-q stable in that declared model. The frozen-axis ordinary instability
witness is not transferred to this stronger constitutive law. The corrected
operation is faster leveling at fixed support, rather than a demonstrated
new stability region at those same parameters.

The distinction is not empty. Keeping the same ionic geometry but using a weak
solid-pillar modulus mu=10kPa gives K_w=1.583e9Pa/m and p0=13.422kPa.
At q=4000/m, an exact-rational lower bound on C3 with R_v=0 is positive;
the lower bound is greater than.69986V/m, equivalently N/G>.0036392.
the responsive model remains all-q stable by C4 and E7. This is a second
conditional parameter region, not a measured soft material. Contact amplitude,
pillar buckling, wet mechanics and the normal loaded tangent must still fit the
weaker preload. It is not evidence of an experimental advantage.

4. The surviving gain is an electrical stiffness paid by current and resistance

For q->0 the current numerator is E5 for both tensor conventions. Write

 -N0=m0[j0 R_v+K_w(nu_m-G b/M)], M=E/(1-v_p^2).

The pressure-like electrical restoring coefficient is

 K_electrical=j0 R_v/nu_m,                              (C7)

whereas K_w is the real conservative foundation. At the original fixture
K_electrical is about5.94e12Pa/m, roughly37.5 times K_w. The same support and
skin without R_v level on a mean-deposition length about0.35mm; including
the pore response gives9.095micrometres. The model predicts about38.5 times
faster long-wave leveling per useful mean deposited height. These are linear
operating-point quantities. They do not certify finite initial roughness or a
rechargeable cycle. Increasing an ordinary support stiffness could also improve
leveling, but its required preload, tangent, material and contact costs must
then be counted rather than holding only the new design to the pressure budget.

K_electrical is not a new passive elastic modulus. It is a biased transport
feedback, vanishes with j0 and changes sign on current reversal. Ohmic voltage,
deposited charge and the finite pore-volume range pay for it. This distinction
is why a reversible full plating/stripping stroke is not made stable by the
same R_v>0 law.

5. Closed pure-electrical design envelope with finite closure margin

The following optimization charges conductivity, resistance and geometric
range instead of using an arbitrarily large porosity gauge factor. It concerns
the long-wave local-column electrical contribution, not a nonlinear theorem
for the spatially conducting elastic skin.

Let A=d-s>0 be the height margin before closing the pore volume and let
epsilon=A/d<1/2. The straight-pore law E9 becomes

 d=A/epsilon,
 R=A/(k_l epsilon^2),
 R_v=(1-2epsilon)R/A.

If all other series differential resistance is R_fixed>=0, the pure-electrical
leveling length per mean deposited height is exactly

 ell=A[1+R_fixed/R]/(1-2epsilon)>A.                     (C8)

For fixed A,k_l,R_fixed and a pore-resistance budget R<=R_max, it decreases
as R increases. If R_max>4A/k_l, its infimum at that budget is

 ell_min=A[1+R_fixed/R_max]/
                  [1-2sqrt(A/(k_l R_max))],
 d_opt=sqrt(A k_l R_max).                              (C9)

The denominator is positive precisely on the compression-leveling branch.
An independent thickness bound d<=d_max adds epsilon>=A/d_max. Then use
epsilon_min=max(sqrt(A/(k_l R_max)), A/d_max), provided epsilon_min<1/2,
and the corresponding R=A/(k_l epsilon_min^2) in C8.

These formulas expose two hard prices: making ell approach A requires an
increasing resistance/thickness resource, and admitting a height perturbation
a needs A>a. A voltage budget j0R_max and a finite layer thickness therefore
exclude arbitrarily rapid robust leveling. For finite amplitudes the budget
must hold at the most compressed patch, R(d-h_max)<=R_max; applying it only
at the mean state would undercharge the device. Mechanical forces, kinetics,
mass transport, skin nonlocality and strain-dependent conductivity remain
separate additional constraints, not loopholes in C8--C9.

6. Constitutive alternatives and precise scope

A Piola law K=J^-1 F K0 F^T is not the same as rotation-only constant intrinsic
conductivity. Its first-order off-diagonal term contains strain as well as
rotation, and its diagonal terms alter the normal boundary flux. Merely using
a material coordinate transform does not select the actual physics of a
crystalline ionic conductor. Either law can be treated by the coupled interface,
but it must be stated and calibrated. No same-film piezoconductivity or loaded
elastic tangent has been acquired in this work.

The exact nonlinear column+conservative-energy theorem E10--E12 remains useful
in its separately declared interface limit and with the flat-critical condition.
The full rotating conducting skin has, so far, a linear continuum theorem,
not a proved finite-amplitude free-boundary basin. The positive construction
now contains a real reciprocal elastic load path and the first-order material
rotation, but initial stress, real pore contact/drainage, material conjunction,
nonlinear interface validity and stable stripping are still outstanding physical
gates. Conventional levelers, pressure control, artificial-SEI coatings and
gradient hosts retain their actual capabilities; no overall superiority claim
is made from these model operations.
