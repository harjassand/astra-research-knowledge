ROTATING-ANISOTROPY MATCHED COMPARATOR
Bounded exact reduction and scenario calculation, 24 October 2026

Verdict

For the prescribed finite-thickness rotating-axis correction, the ordinary
identical-skin numerator is no larger than the frozen-axis numerator when
rho=kappa_t/kappa_n>=1. This is an exact algebraic comparison, not a grid
claim. It can erase the former ordinary-skin positive witness: at q=10,000
m^-1, the requested strong-skeleton, R_v=0 scenario moves from
N_frozen/G=+0.1791 to N_rot/G=-0.2671. The long-wave leveling limit is
unchanged by this rotation correction. Under the weaker skeleton scenario,
the rotated R_v=0 mode at q=4,000 m^-1 remains slightly unstable
(N_rot/G=+0.00364), so the finite-q improvement does not establish ordinary
global stability. R_v=80 stabilizes all four requested sample wavenumbers in
both skeleton scenarios; this remains a finite-point comparison only.

Definitions and exact sign check

Let q be the lateral Fourier wavenumber, rho=kappa_t/kappa_n>=1,
lambda=q*sqrt(rho), p=q*sqrt(kappa_n*kappa_t)=kappa_n*lambda,

  C=cosh(lambda*b), S=sinh(lambda*b), H=C+L*p*S,
  C0=cosh(q*b), S0=sinh(q*b).

Use m(q)=v/h in the elastic two-port (0<m<=1), and k_eff(q) for its
positive bottom stiffness. This m is the mechanical top/bottom displacement
ratio, not the conductivity ratio rho. Let

  chi=Gamma*q^2+nu_m*k_eff,
  R0'=q*(m-C0)/S0,
  Rb'=q*(m*C0-1)/S0.

The stated rotating-axis numerator is

  N_rot=-chi*H-j0*m*R_v
         -j0*((S/p+L*C)*R0'-L*Rb'),

and the frozen-axis comparator is

  N_frozen=(G-chi)*H-m*(G+j0*R_v),  G=j0/kappa_n.

The transfer denominator D=r_ct*C+S/p+L*(C+r_ct*p*S) is shared and
positive, so numerator comparisons also compare growth signs and rates.
Substituting the two face derivatives gives

  (N_rot-N_frozen)/j0
   = m-C - [S/(sqrt(rho)*S0)]*(m-C0)
     -L*kappa_n*q*[sqrt(rho)*S
                   +(m*(C-C0)+1-C*C0)/S0].                (1)

Both terms on the right are nonpositive. For the first, put
a=sqrt(rho)>=1 and t=q*b. Then

  m-C-[sinh(a*t)/(a*sinh(t))]*(m-cosh(t))
   =(R-1)*(cosh(t)-m)-(cosh(a*t)-cosh(t)),
  R=sinh(a*t)/(a*sinh(t)).

Since sinh(x)/x increases, R>=1. Also
R*cosh(t)<=cosh(a*t) is equivalent to tanh(a*t)<=a*tanh(t), which follows
from concavity of tanh on x>=0 and tanh(0)=0. Using 0<m<=1 therefore makes
the first term nonpositive. For the L term, its bracket multiplied by S0 is

  a*sinh(a*t)*sinh(t)+m*(cosh(a*t)-cosh(t))
       +1-cosh(a*t)*cosh(t).

The m term is nonnegative. The remaining part
F(t)=a*sinh(a*t)*sinh(t)+1-cosh(a*t)*cosh(t) has F(0)=0 and
F'(t)=(a^2-1)*cosh(a*t)*sinh(t)>=0. Thus the bracket is nonnegative,
proving N_rot<=N_frozen for L>=0. Equality holds at rho=1; for rho>1 the
correction is strictly stabilizing at q>0 under these positive-layer
conditions.

At q->0, R0' and Rb' both tend to (m0-1)/b, while S/p->b/kappa_n and
C->1. The L pieces cancel in the derivative bracket, yielding

  N_rot(0)=N_frozen(0)
    =G*(1-m0)-nu_m*k_eff,0-m0*j0*R_v.                     (2)

So rotating the anisotropy does not change the leading capacity-normalized
long-wave leveling length for this exact boundary correction. With
c_e=(1-nu_p^2)/E and a positive uniform top foundation K_w,

  m0=1/(1+K_w*b*c_e),
  k_eff,0=K_w/(1+K_w*b*c_e),
  D0=r_ct+b/kappa_n+L0,
  ell_level=j0*D0/[-N_rot(0)]  when N_rot(0)<0.

Here ell_level is the mean deposited thickness needed for one e-fold
reduction in an asymptotically long-wave amplitude; it is not elapsed time.

Declared scenario inputs, not acquired properties

Use the requested same design geometry: s=20 um, d=s/0.9,
d_ref=2s, k_l=1 S/m, giving R=0.000222222 ohm m^2 and R_v=80 ohm m.
Keep the ordinary comparison at the same R and mechanics but set R_v=0.
Use kappa_n=0.052 S/m, rho=10, b=20 um, E=20 GPa, nu_p=0.3,
Gamma=5e-11 V m, nu_m=13e-6/96485 m^3/C, j0=10 A/m^2,
r_ct=1e-4 ohm m^2, and a 20 um bulk with k_b=0.5 S/m. These are model
specifications, not a conjunction measured in one film. In particular the
same-film E and kappa_t are not acquired.

For the exact finite bulk use
L(q)=R+tanh(q*d_bulk)/(k_b*q); L0=0.000262222 ohm m^2 and
D0=0.000746838 ohm m^2. The skeleton model gives
K_w=1.583e11 Pa/m for mu_skel=1 MPa, and 1.583e9 Pa/m for
mu_skel=10 kPa. At q=0, m0=0.999855968 and k_eff,0=1.582772e11 Pa/m
for 1 MPa; for 10 kPa they are 0.999998559 and 1.582998e9 Pa/m.

Using (2), frozen and rotating long-wave values coincide:

  mu_skel       R_v       N(0)/G       ell_level
  1 MPa         0         -0.110749     0.350662 mm
  1 MPa         80        -4.270150     9.09466 um
  10 kPa        0         -0.00110765   35.0612 mm
  10 kPa        80        -4.161102     9.33300 um

Thus the resistive response shortens the long-wave capacity length by about
39x with the 1 MPa support and about 3,757x with the 10 kPa support. The
weak-support R_v=0 case is technically leveling at q->0 in this specified
elastic model, but only after a very large mean deposit; a negative sign alone
does not imply useful leveling.

Finite-wavelength comparison

The exact two-port values below use the shear-free finite-thickness formulas
for m(q), k_eff(q), rather than extending the q^4 plate approximation. The
right-hand columns give N/G for frozen and rotating axes at the same geometry.
The last two entries are the rotating-axis capacity slopes
d(log|h_q|)/d(H_mean)=N/(j0*D), in m^-1, for R_v=0 and 80. A positive slope
means growth; a negative slope means leveling.

  mu_skel  q (m^-1)  frozen N/G (Rv=0,80)    rotating N/G (Rv=0,80)      rotating slopes (Rv=0,80) m^-1
  1 MPa       4000   -0.045775, -4.205169    -0.114366, -4.273760       -2,867.7, -107,164.9
  1 MPa      10000   +0.179077, -3.980049    -0.267096, -4.426222       -5,861.4,  -97,132.8
  10 kPa      4000   +0.072238, -4.087749    +0.003643, -4.156344       +91.3, -104,220.7
  10 kPa     10000   +0.343307, -3.816412    -0.102893, -4.262611       -2,258.0,  -93,542.4

The corresponding rotating-axis e-fold deposited thicknesses where stable
are: for 1 MPa, R_v=0 gives 0.349 mm at q=4000 and 0.171 mm at q=10000;
R_v=80 gives 9.33 um and 10.30 um. For 10 kPa, R_v=0 grows at q=4000 and
levels over 0.443 mm at q=10000; R_v=80 gives 9.60 um and 10.69 um.
At the same finite q values the rotation correction is always downward, but
it is not a substitute for the channel term in the weak-support q=4000 case.

Interpretation and limits

The old ordinary positive witness at q=10000 for mu_skel=1 MPa does not
survive the specified rotating-axis correction: its numerator changes from
+0.1791 G to -0.2671 G. The weaker support leaves a small positive rotating
witness at q=4000, so this comparison alone does not establish ordinary
all-wavelength stability. The responsive R_v=80 case is strongly negative at
the requested long-wave and finite-q points, but this note does not claim the
all-q certificate. The numerical values are conditional on the stated
elastic, transport, rotation-only constitutive model, exact skin attachment,
and design inputs. They do not include strain-induced conductivity changes,
prestress, fracture, nonlinear geometry, or a measured film.
