# Hydraulic pressure as a passive interface state

## Verdict

The proposed energy identity is exact, with no missing factor, provided the hydraulic and electrical couplings are reciprocal and the hydraulic storage energy is positive. It establishes passivity, not automatic flattening. A connected-to-reservoir (drained) pressure mode eventually vanishes, so it cannot change the DC shape equilibrium set by the electrical functional $F$. A trapped mode stores pressure and adds positive stiffness in the shape directions it couples to. Any leveling claim therefore needs either a trapped/compliant volume on the relevant time scale or an electrically stabilizing $F$, plus a material-specific sign and volume-conservation derivation.

## Identity and hypotheses

Work on $1^\perp$, let $P=P^T=P^2$, $P\dot h=\dot h$, and assume $K(h)=K(h)^T\succ0$ on this subspace, $S=S^T\succ0$, and $L_h=L_h^T\succeq0$. For

\[
K(h)\dot h/\nu+bPB^Tp=-P\nabla F(h),\qquad
S\dot p+L_hp=B\dot h,
\]

take the inner product of the first equation with $\dot h$. Since $P\dot h=\dot h$,

\[
\nabla F(h)^T\dot h=-\dot h^TK(h)\dot h/\nu-bp^TB\dot h.
\]

The second equation gives $p^TS\dot p=p^TB\dot h-p^TL_hp$. Thus

\[
\frac{d}{dt}\left(F(h)+\frac b2p^TSp\right)
=-\dot h^TK(h)\dot h/\nu-bp^TL_hp\le0
\]

for $b\ge0$. The two cross terms cancel exactly. This holds for state-dependent $K(h)$; no derivative of $K$ enters because the mobility multiplies velocity rather than appearing in the energy. It fails if pressure is inserted as an independent delayed resistance, if different maps appear in the two coupling terms, or if $K$ is nonsymmetric/nonpositive. If $b=0$, hydraulics are only forced by growth and do not feed back.

Dimensional reading: $Bh$ is a signed volume, so $B\dot h$ is volume/time; $S$ is storage in volume/pressure; and $L_h$ is conductance in volume/(pressure·time). Then $p^TSp$ is energy. If $F$ is dimensional free energy and $Bh$ is the actual conjugate volume, consistent normalization gives $b=1$. Otherwise $b$ is a positive energy scaling. Its sign cannot be assigned independently of the pressure-port definitions.

## Deriving the pressure source

For a saturated pore compartment, integrate fluid mass conservation,
$\partial_t(\rho_f\epsilon)+\nabla\cdot(\rho_f q)=0$. Linearize about a reference state and use Darcy flow $q=-(\kappa_h/\mu)\nabla p$. If positive $p$ means compression and $Bh$ is the signed volume displaced into hydraulic storage by interface motion, the compartment balances become

\[
S\dot p+L_hp=B\dot h.
\]

Here $S$ contains fluid compressibility $V_p/K_f$ and pore-wall/skeleton compliance. $L_h$ is the Darcy conductance matrix; a reservoir boundary gives a grounded (Dirichlet) conductance. A rigid pore filled by strictly incompressible liquid has no positive local storage: pressure is a constraint/Lagrange multiplier unless wall compliance or fluid compressibility supplies $S\succ0$. Thus $S\succ0$ is a constitutive assumption, not a result of Darcy's law.

Geometric intrusion gives $B_{ia}=A_a$ only if metal growth displaces that pore-liquid volume without compensating solvent redistribution. In liquid electrolyte, removing Li+ also changes solution composition and partial-molar volume. The net source then follows only after combining pore-volume balance with species balance, for example $\partial_t(\epsilon c_i)+\nabla\cdot N_i=R_i$, and including solvent flux and any swelling/SEI term. If those variables relax on the same time scale as pressure, they are extra states; Faraday's law alone does not determine $B$.

If positive $h$ grows into pore space and $Bh$ means volume displaced, positive pressure resists insertion, so $+bB^Tp$ belongs on the left of the growth law. If $Bh$ instead means remaining pore volume, both appearances of $B$ change sign. The same signed volume map must enter the pressure source and reciprocal force.

For Li plating, a thickness increment $dh$ over area $A$ consumes $dn=A\,dh/\bar V_{Li}$ moles. If the metal and electrolyte share pressure $p$, the pressure derivative of reaction free energy is approximately

\[
\Delta\bar V_{\rm rxn}=\bar V_{Li(m)}-\bar V_{Li^+(elyte)},
\]

with electron volume negligible. More generally the pressure term is $\bar V_{Li}p_m-\bar V_{Li^+}p_e$, because metal and electrolyte pressures need not match. If ionic pressure is fixed at reservoir pressure and only metal pressure changes, insertion costs $+\bar V_{Li}p_m$ per mole. Therefore “compressive pressure stabilizes plating” depends on which phase pressure enters the reaction.

For a shared reaction pressure and $h$ measured as metal thickness, one consistent signed volume map is $B_{ia}=A_a\Delta\bar V_{\rm rxn}/\bar V_{Li}$. If only geometric metal intrusion changes pore volume, it is $B_{ia}=A_a$. This distinction matters: mixing a positive geometric-area source in the pressure equation with an oppositely signed reaction-volume force is not the reciprocal passive model proved above.

The sign is material-specific. Li metal has molar volume about $13\,\mathrm{cm^3/mol}$. A primary carbonate-electrolyte densimetry study reports infinite-dilution Li-ion partial molar volume near $5\,\mathrm{cm^3/mol}$, giving positive equal-pressure $\Delta\bar V_{\rm rxn}$ in that example ([Naejus et al., 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0013-4686(97)00073-X)). A 2025 primary polymer-electrolyte study reports a high Li-ion partial molar volume of $108.5\,\mathrm{cm^3/mol}$ and uses it to reduce the Li/Li+ chemical-potential mismatch ([Chen et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.202500896)); under the same equal-pressure formula the sign reverses. That result does not measure pressure in a porous hydraulic layer, but it rules out assuming a universal reaction-volume sign. If the effective signed coupling is negative while the pressure source is fixed as $+B\dot h$, the stated positive-energy proof is unavailable. Re-derive both port coefficients or include distinct metal/electrolyte pressure states; do not impose $b\ge0$ by convention.

## Drained versus trapped modes

Assume $F$ is bounded below and trajectories are precompact. If every hydraulic component has a reservoir path, then $L_h\succ0$. With $b>0$, the identity dissipates both $\dot h$ and $p$. The invariant limiting states satisfy $p=0$, $\dot h=0$, and $P\nabla F(h)=0$. If $F$ is strictly convex on $1^\perp$, this is its unique minimizer. Otherwise the result is convergence to the stationary set, not necessarily to a single profile. Most importantly, drained pressure makes no static correction to the shape equilibrium.

If $L_h=0$, then $Sp-Bh=c$ is conserved. Substituting $p=S^{-1}(Bh+c)$ gives projected gradient descent for

\[
F_c(h)=F(h)+\frac b2(Bh+c)^TS^{-1}(Bh+c).
\]

The trapped modes add positive stiffness $bB^TS^{-1}B$. Strong convexity/coercivity of $F_c$ gives convergence within the fixed-volume class; final pressure is generally nonzero. For semidefinite $L_h$, each $z\in\ker L_h$ obeys $z^T(Sp-Bh)=\mathrm{constant}$, and equilibrium has $p\in\ker L_h$. Only exactly trapped modes supply DC elastic correction. A small leak can look trapped over a short test but gives no permanent static support.

A one-mode linearization makes the distinction sharp. Let $a=K/\nu>0$, $S=s>0$, scalar $B=q$, $L_h=\ell\ge0$, and $\kappa=F''(0)$. The characteristic equation is

\[
as\lambda^2+(a\ell+s\kappa+bq^2)\lambda+\kappa\ell=0.
\]

For drained $\ell>0$, an electrically unstable static curvature $\kappa<0$ makes the constant coefficient negative, so one real growth rate is positive for every $bq^2$: pressure can slow growth, not eliminate it. For trapped $\ell=0$, the nonzero eigenvalue is $-(s\kappa+bq^2)/(as)$; it is stable if $s\kappa+bq^2>0$, while the other zero eigenvalue records the conserved volume class. If $\kappa<0$ and $bq^2+s\kappa>0$, a small leak creates the slow unstable root

\[
\lambda_+\simeq-\frac{\kappa\ell}{bq^2+s\kappa}>0.
\]

Thus elastic-looking suppression crosses over to slow instability as trapped pressure drains.

## Pore scale and delayed-control contrast

For a cylindrical pore of radius $r$, length $L$, viscosity $\mu$, and fluid bulk modulus $K_f$, Poiseuille conductance and fluid-only storage are

\[
G=\frac{\pi r^4}{8\mu L},\qquad C_f=\frac{\pi r^2L}{K_f},\qquad
\tau_h=C_f/G=\frac{8\mu L^2}{K_f r^2}.
\]

As an illustrative water-like calculation, take $\mu=3\,\mathrm{mPa\,s}$, $K_f=1.5\,\mathrm{GPa}$, and $L=100\,\mu\mathrm m$. Then $r=100\,\mathrm{nm}$ gives $\tau_h\approx16\,\mu\mathrm s$, while $r=10\,\mathrm{nm}$ gives about $1.6\,\mathrm{ms}$. For $L=1\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $r=10\,\mathrm{nm}$, it is $0.16\,\mathrm s$. Wall compliance increases storage and time; tortuosity/constrictions increase resistance. This capillary scaling is not a measured separator. Drainage dissipates $\int p^TL_hp\,dt$ as Darcy viscous loss; sealed storage trades that loss for pressure load and fracture risk. Actual permeability, geometry, compliance, and outlet boundary are necessary for a material claim.

This passive pressure state differs from an assumed delayed conductivity controller. The nominally corrective law $\dot h(t)=-a h(t-\tau)$ has characteristic equation $\lambda+a e^{-\lambda\tau}=0$ and destabilizes after its first Hopf crossing at $a\tau=\pi/2$. No storage-energy cancellation prevents that. Pressure must come from volume conservation and reciprocal force, not an arbitrary lagged resistance.

## Scope against the incumbent

Pressure-dependent reaction thermodynamics, Darcy flow, and pressure-driven transport already appear in porous-Li models. Song et al. (2026) formulate a dual-1D porous electrode with Darcy-driven Li-metal transport and pressure-shifted equilibrium potential ([Journal of Energy Storage, DOI 10.1016/j.est.2026.120345](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.est.2026.120345)); Tu et al. (2020) include a metal-pressure term in the Li/Li+ Butler–Volmer overpotential ([Cell Reports Physical Science, DOI 10.1016/j.xcrp.2020.100106](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrp.2020.100106)). This note is not a novelty or performance claim. The decisive physical gate is whether a real layer has the signed reaction-volume map required for reciprocity, nonzero storage compliance, and either truly trapped pressure or an electrical $F$ already convex on the shape modes. With fully drained pressure and negative electrical curvature, this passive coupling cannot provide asymptotic leveling.
