PASSIVE SHAPE FEEDBACK FOR METAL ELECTRODEPOSITION
Owned construction in progress, 24 October 2026

Problem selected before consulting new Astra material: suppress geometry-driven
current focusing during metal deposition despite an imperfect moving interface.
Rechargeable metal anodes motivate the problem, but a one-stroke leveling theorem
does not certify rechargeable cycling, fracture resistance or lithium chemistry.

The first proposed physical mechanism, compression closing ionic pores at a
protrusion, is already in Zhang et al. 2019 (DOI10.1149/2.0701914jes). Root supplied
this close comparator after the first owned derivation was retained in
RESEARCH_NOTES.txt. The main body of the author manuscript was then read through
its conclusions. Its contact/creep/pore-collapse explanation is an incumbent,
including its explicit warning that trapped electrolyte can oppose collapse.
Haataja--Srolovitz--Bocarsly 2003 Part II supplies the strong independent chemical
leveling mechanism (adsorption, complexation and blocking), including its finite
feature-size and high-current limitations. The construction below is not a claim
to have invented pore closure, leveling additives, pressure control or consensus.

1. Why fixed transport grading is insufficient in a declared regime

Let a metal interface be z=h(x,t), the electrolyte z>h, with positive definite
fixed depth-dependent conductivity. Concentrations are fixed, transport is
quasistatic Ohmic, and charge transfer is locally monotone. For a Fourier mode
q>0, the bulk Dirichlet-to-Neumann admittance Y(q)>0 follows by integrating
the positive conduction quadratic form. If r_ct>=0 and Gamma=gamma*Omega/(zF),

 delta j/h = [j0/kappa_n(0) - Gamma q^2]/[r_ct+Y(q)^(-1)].       (1)

The denominator is positive. Fixed positive grading changes the rate and
operating voltage but cannot reverse the long-wave sign at fixed base field.
This is not a theorem about concentration-dependent transport, space charge,
charged pores or solid mechanical chemical potentials: those can change the
numerator and are serious conventional alternatives.

2. The full wavelength cost of a responsive layer

For a layer of thickness d, constant base normal/tangential conductivities
kappa_n,kappa_t, and a local thickness response uniform through each column,
write a=-d*(partial_h kappa_n)/kappa_n. Its metal boundary moves by h and upper
face is fixed. Put

 s=q d sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n), p=kappa_n*s/d,
 C=cosh(s), S=sinh(s), A=p C+Y_b(q) S,
 Y_in=p*(Y_b C+p S)/A, zeta=1-p/A,
 ell_cap^2=kappa_n*Gamma/j0.

Directly solving the two boundary conditions gives

 delta j/h = {Y_in*(j0/kappa_n-Gamma q^2)
                    -a*j0*zeta/d}/(1+r_ct Y_in).               (2)

The distributed conductivity term is essential; a perturbed lumped resistor is
not valid at arbitrary s. Let xi=Y_b/p and

 B(s,xi)=(C+xi S-1)/[s*(xi C+S)].

The sign is stabilizing precisely when

 1 - ell_cap^2 q^2 - a B(s,xi) < 0.                            (3)

For any positive passive termination,

 tanh(s/2)/s <= B(s,xi) <= tanh(s)/s < 1/s,
 B(s,xi) >= 1/(s+2).

The first bounds follow by differentiating B in xi; the last is the elementary
inequality exp(s)>=1+s. For an isotropic layer, complete all-q damping therefore
necessarily requires a >= [2/(3 sqrt(3))] d/ell_cap, and the stronger condition

 a > 2+[2/(3 sqrt(3))]d/ell_cap                                (4)

is sufficient for every passive bulk termination. A finite-DC-resistance bulk
also requires a>=1 from the long-wave limit. With anisotropy replace d by
d sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n) in these bounds. Thus a large logarithmic sensitivity
alone is not a stability resource: isotropic performance depends on kappa*a^2.
For a percolation closure kappa=kappa_p(epsilon-epsilon_c)^t and conserved solid
volume, a=t(1-epsilon)/(epsilon-epsilon_c). Then kappa*a^2 has a finite limit
when t=2, tends to zero when t>2, and mean resistance diverges at percolation.
The available compression range also vanishes. Near pore closure is not a free
route to arbitrary finite-wavelength stability. This calculation is a continuum
conditional result, not a measured material law or priority claim.

3. A finite-amplitude shape-voltage ordering theorem

Consider N equal-area patches of a working metal. H_i are deposited heights,
h_i=H_i-mean(H), and sum h_i=0. Let nu=Omega/(zF)>0. The imposed mean current
j0 may have either sign, and hdot=nu*(j-j0*1). The bulk ionic transport has a
top-node Dirichlet-to-Neumann matrix

 Y=g I+L,   g>0,   L=L^T graph Laplacian,   Z=Y^(-1).

This is a specific reciprocal, uniform-reservoir transport class, not arbitrary
hidden microstructure. Each local branch has polarization

 u_i=f(j_i,h_i),   partial_j f>0,
 j=g V*1-Y u,   sum j=N j0.                                  (5)

The function f includes local ionic resistance, monotone charge transfer and
declared local equilibrium-potential shifts. It must be derived from the actual
interface; it is not supplied as an unknown stabilizer. Set

 Q(h)=f(j0,h).

If Q is increasing on the entire initial height interval, then the maximum
height deviation cannot increase and the minimum cannot decrease. The result
is finite amplitude; no Fourier linearization or local height measurement is
used in its operation.

Proof. For any fixed V the node problem is
 Y u+f_i^(-1)(u_i)=g V*1.
Its Jacobian is an irreducible nonsingular M-matrix, so comparison applies and
the total current is increasing in V. Let H=max h and C=Q(H). At
V_C=C+j0/g the constant u=C*1 is a supersolution, because
f_i^(-1)(C)>=j0. Hence actual u(V_C)<=C*1 and total current at V_C is at least
N j0. The imposed-current voltage is therefore <=V_C and u_i<=C. A maximum-
height branch has f(j0,H)=C, so its current is <=j0. Its centered velocity is
nonpositive. The subsolution at the minimum gives the opposite inequality.
The reasoning also holds for negative j0 and time-dependent global operating
parameters whenever Q remains increasing at each instant.

There is a stronger common quadratic contraction. At the actual state define
positive secants

 D_i=[f(j_i,h_i)-f(j0,h_i)]/(j_i-j0), K=Z+diag(D),
 M=K^(-1), v=M*1,
 A=M-v v^T/(1^T v).

Then exactly delta j=-A Q(h), A*1=0. The identity

 M=D^(-1)-D^(-1)(Y+D^(-1))^(-1)D^(-1)

shows M_ij<=0 for i!=j, while
v=g D^(-1)(Y+D^(-1))^(-1)*1>0.
Thus A has nonpositive off-diagonal entries and is a graph Laplacian. Therefore

 d(||h||^2/2)/dt
 =-nu sum_(i<j) (-A_ij)*(h_i-h_j)*(Q(h_i)-Q(h_j)) <=0.           (6)

If Q'>=alpha_Q>0 and D_i<=Dmax on the invariant compact range, the restriction
of A to 1-perp is the inverse of P K P there. Its smallest eigenvalue is at least
1/(Dmax+||Z||), so

 ||h(t)||^2 <= ||h(0)||^2
   exp[-2nu integral alpha_Q/(Dmax+||Z||) dt].                 (7)

This remains a common variance estimate under programmed global phase changes;
there is no hidden switch of Lyapunov metrics. A monotone kinetic law without
bounded secants need not give exponential convergence. Heterogeneous reservoir
row sums or heterogeneous local branch laws need an explicitly compensated
target profile; flat leveling does not follow automatically.

4. Deriving a candidate R from pore geometry; the physical interface gap

An ideal separately conducting straight-pore cell has projected area A_cell,
constant solid volume per area s, length d and liquid fraction epsilon=1-s/d.
If liquid is the only ionic conductor, kappa_n=kappa_l*epsilon and

 R(d)=d^2/[kappa_l(d-s)],
 partial_h R = [s^2/(d-s)^2-1]/kappa_l,  d=d_mean-h.           (8)

Hence s<d<2s gives increasing R with protrusion height. Independent vertical
channels eliminate the responsive layer's lateral shunt; the positive bulk
network in (5) can still couple their top nodes. For f=eta(j)+jR(h), positive
plating has Q'>0 on this entire compression range. Its centered-height interval
is invariant and (7) gives a finite-amplitude leveling rate. Resistance and
voltage losses, stiffness/force path, electrolyte drainage and pore-scale
deposition have not disappeared.

In particular it is NOT enough to identify a porous separator: metal may grow
into pore mouths without compressing its skeleton. Then d does not follow h,
the R derivative in (8) is unavailable, and the ordinary external gap can focus
current at protrusions. The whole desired capability cannot be hidden in the
assumption d=d_mean-h. A candidate repair under active derivation is a continuous
ion-conducting spreading skin beneath the deformable channel layer: it must
smooth pore-scale injection while preserving the larger-scale resistance
feedback. Its thickness/lateral conduction and curvature response must be paid
and jointly derived. No material realizing this interface has been acquired.

5. Hydraulic response must be a reciprocal physical port

For a fixed operating phase with convex electrical shape potential F, suppose
actual pore-volume conservation and reaction-volume work give

 K(h) hdot/nu + b P B^T p = -P grad F(h),
 S pdot+L_h p=B hdot,   S>0, L_h>=0, b>=0.                    (9)

Then direct multiplication gives

 d[F+(b/2)p^T S p]/dt
   =-hdot^T K hdot/nu -b p^T L_h p <=0.                     (10)

Finite hydraulic storage/response does not destabilize this correctly paired
passive coupling. Fully drained modes vanish at equilibrium and cannot repair
a statically unstable F. Trapped modes retain volume invariants and add stiffness
inside that invariant class. Storage, permeability, outlet geometry and the
signed metal-versus-ion partial molar volume determine whether (9) describes a
material. A delayed resistance chosen independently of this volume/work pair
has no such guarantee. Switching external pressure forces the hydraulic port
and its work; (10) is not a free cycle theorem under arbitrary actuation.

6. Rechargeable-cycle gate and a derived global geometric control candidate

Root independently pointed out the hard reversal: with a fixed R'(h)>0 and a
reversible Ohmic law, changing the mean current sign reverses the shape flow.
An exactly charge-balanced plating/stripping cycle retraces its shape absent
irreversible mechanics/chemistry. Plating-only leveling is not stable cycling.

Equation (8), rather than a supplied local sign flip, offers a precise global
control candidate. For an initial height interval [h_-,h_+] of width <s, select

 s+h_+ < d_P < 2s+h_-     during plating,
 d_S > 2s+h_+            during stripping.

The SAME geometric law has R'_h>0 on the compressed plating interval and
R'_h<0 on the expanded stripping interval. Thus Q'=j0 R'_h>0 in both phases;
the ordering/contraction theorem applies to the declared quasistatic column
model. At zero imposed current, eta(0)=0 and no chemical-pressure offset, changing
the common gap produces j=0, so this ideal switch changes no heights. In a real
layer switching pressure generates hydraulic and elastic work and can redistribute
metal. Those terms, molecular interface transfer, contact loss on stripping,
actuator force/displacement/time and pore architecture must be derived before
this is a physical rechargeable-cycle claim. A priori roughness bounds are paid
inputs; no nanoscale feedback measurement is assumed. This is a possible next
constructive operation, not an already acquired device or an advantage claim.
