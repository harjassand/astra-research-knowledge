1. Elastic normal Dirichlet-to-Neumann map

Use x along the flat interface, z through an isotropic homogeneous layer 0<z<b, Fourier dependence exp(i q x), q>0, and plane strain perpendicular to (x,z). The material has Young's modulus E and Poisson ratio nu_p. Both faces are shear-free. Prescribe their normal displacements w0 and w1 in the same +z coordinate. Let the normal tractions be the outward tractions on the solid: t0=-sigma_zz(0), t1=+sigma_zz(b). These signs make the elastic energy per projected area (1/2) Re[w*·t] positive. The traction map is K(q)[w0,w1]^T.

A direct Airy-stress derivation fixes signs and constants. For an Airy amplitude Phi(z) exp(iqx), use

  sigma_xx=Phi'', sigma_zz=-q² Phi, sigma_xz=-i q Phi',
  (d²/dz²-q²)² Phi=0.

Shear-free faces mean Phi'(0)=Phi'(b)=0. Plane-strain compliance is

  eps_xx=c_e sigma_xx-d_e sigma_zz,
  eps_zz=c_e sigma_zz-d_e sigma_xx,
  gamma_xz=sigma_xz/mu,
  c_e=(1-nu_p²)/E, d_e=nu_p(1+nu_p)/E,
  mu=E/[2(1+nu_p)].

From eps_xx=iq u and gamma_xz=u'+iq w, the normal displacement at a shear-free face reduces to w=c_e Phi'''/q²; the term proportional to Phi' vanishes there. Center the slab at z=0, with faces at ±b/2 and l=qb/2. For equal face displacement (w0=w1), Phi is odd; one solution is Phi=A sinh(qz)+B z cosh(qz). Phi'(b/2)=0 gives A=-(B/q)(1+l tanh l). It yields w=2c_e B cosh l and top outward traction t1=-q²Phi(b/2)=Bq[sinh l-l sech l]. Thus t1=k_plus w, with

  k_plus = q/(2c_e) [tanh l-l sech² l].

For opposite face displacements (w0=-w1), Phi is even; take Phi=A cosh(qz)+B z sinh(qz). The same shear condition gives A=-(B/q)(1+l coth l). Then w=2c_e B sinh l and t1=Bq[cosh l+l csch l], giving

  k_minus = q/(2c_e) [coth l+l csch² l].

These are the two eigenvalues of the normal map. Hence

  K=[[a,b12],[b12,a]],
  a=(k_plus+k_minus)/2,
  b12=(k_plus-k_minus)/2.

The signs are not guessed: k_plus>0 because tanh(l)-l sech²(l) starts at zero and has derivative 2l sech²(l)tanh(l)>0. Also k_minus-k_plus=q/(2c_e)[coth(l)-tanh(l)+l(csch²(l)+sech²(l))]>0. Therefore a>0 and b12<0. The full elastic DtN map is positive definite for q>0.

2. Top foundation, effective stiffness, limits

Model the skeleton support only as a positive normal foundation energy (K_w/2)|w1|², K_w>=0. It has the same units as a and b12, pressure per displacement. Holding the bottom displacement h fixed and minimizing total elastic-plus-foundation energy over top displacement v gives

  (a+K_w)v+b12 h=0,
  v=m h,   m=-b12/(a+K_w),
  k_eff=a-b12²/(a+K_w).

Since -b12>0 and a+b12=k_plus>0, 0<m<=1; for every q>0 the upper bound is strict, including K_w=0. k_eff>0 by the Schur complement of the positive elastic-plus-foundation quadratic form. It is the bottom traction per h after the top has equilibrated. A support with negative tangent stiffness is outside this positivity result.

For l→0,

  k_plus ~ b³ q^4/(24 c_e)=B_plate q^4/2,
  k_minus ~ 2/(c_e b),
  B_plate=E b³/[12(1-nu_p²)]=b³/(12c_e).

Thus m→1/(1+K_w b c_e) and k_eff→K_w/(1+K_w b c_e). With K_w=0 this reduces to k_eff~2 k_plus=B_plate q^4, recovering the plate bending stiffness only at qb<<1. For q b→infinity, both eigenvalues tend to q/(2c_e)=E q/[2(1-nu_p²)], b12 tends to zero, and m→0 for fixed finite K_w; k_eff has the same half-space asymptotic. This avoids applying a thin-plate q^4 law at arbitrary q.

3. Coupling to the S1 ionic transfer

For the ionic skin linearize on the flat reference slab 0<z<b, with constants kappa_n,kappa_t>0, normal base field G=j0/kappa_n, p=q sqrt(kappa_n kappa_t), lambda=q sqrt(kappa_t/kappa_n), C=cosh(lambda b), S=sinh(lambda b), and L(q)=R+Z_b(q). Allow the metal bottom to move by h and the upper skeleton face by v=m h. Here b is the flat-state thickness; unequal h and v produce a first-order local thickness change v-h, represented by the moving-boundary terms below. The old fixed-normal-thickness case is the special choice v=h. Define R_v as the derivative of the channel resistance with respect to this *upper-face* displacement in the same signed convention in which positive protrusion increases resistance; do not silently identify it with a metal-height derivative when the geometry says otherwise. Let r>=0 be the differential charge-transfer resistance. With total stabilizing chemical-potential coefficient chi(q) per height, bottom kinetic boundary condition and upper channel closure are

  u(0)=r j_b+(chi-G)h,
  W=u(b)+Gv,
  j_t=(C+r p S)j_b+pS(chi-G)h,
  W=-L j_t-j0 R_v v.

Here W is the perturbation of physical potential at the moved top face; the Gv term is the base-field shift. Solving u''-lambda²u=0 gives W=(rC+S/p)j_b+[Cchi-CG]h+Gv. Eliminating W and j_t gives

  j_b/h = N/D,
  N=(G-chi)(C+L pS)-m(G+j0 R_v),
  D=rC+S/p+L(C+r pS)>0.                               (E1)

The exact signs follow directly from the moving-face base-potential terms. Setting m=1 and R_v=R_h recovers S1, since then N=(G-chi)(C+LpS)-G-j0R_h. At q->0, k_eff is the finite foundation/compression response above; if K_w=0, its leading q^4 term is the ordinary plate result. Formula E1 is exact only for the specified small-displacement, constant-coefficient ionic transfer plus elastic normal two-port and local channel-resistance law.

For a positive elastic energy, a protrusion costs k_eff h per unit projected area in normal traction; the incremental reaction-energy voltage is therefore chi_el h=nu_m k_eff h, nu_m=Omega/(zF)>0. Thus

  chi(q)=Gamma q²+nu_m k_eff(q)

has the suppressing sign in E1. This mechanical sign is the variational cost of adding material against the positive minimized elastic energy, not a hydraulic pressure sign inferred from partial molar volumes. Residual in-plane prestress is deliberately excluded here; it can add a signed q² term and must be handled separately.

4. Assumption boundary

The Airy derivation requires an isotropic linear elastic layer, plane strain, q>0, no residual stress, shear-free faces, and a uniform normal top-foundation tangent. Bonding, tangential shear transfer, slip/contact loss, finite lateral edges, viscoelasticity, nonlinear plate stretching, fracture and strain-dependent ionic conductivity are not represented. A physical bonded film often transmits shear; imposing zero shear on both faces is a deliberate boundary condition, not an automatic consequence of attachment.

The transport transfer is linearized on the flat reference thickness b. Unequal face displacements h and v are represented by the moving-boundary shifts G h and G v; the first-order thickness change v-h does not require an additional derivative of C,S with respect to b. In particular v=0 is allowed at this linear order if the top is fixed. E1 still assumes unchanged conductivity values/orientation and no extra strain-induced coefficient, contact or interior-source perturbation. If b or E varies as an independent material parameter, the tensor rotates with strain, or finite strain changes the transport domain, those effects require a new model; avoid double-counting the same moving-face terms with a second dC/db correction. If R_v is a derivative with respect to a different interface coordinate, its factor and sign need re-derivation. Finally, q->0 here is the limit of nonzero Fourier modes, whose in-plane displacement can relax over a long lateral wavelength; the exactly uniform q=0 loading may have a different modulus if finite-domain lateral edges constrain in-plane strain. The mean deposition mode is separately fixed by galvanostatic control.

This closes the exact unstressed continuum two-port and its conditional linear transport coupling, not a material capability. A physical instance still needs the elastic boundary/support law, ionic conductivity tensor of the same film, attachment to the metal and skeleton, channel response R_v, residual stress, and a check that the mechanical interface actually follows the assumed modes. The q=0 galvanostatic mean mode is removed; on a finite domain, allowed q values and edge conditions replace the infinite/periodic Fourier continuum.
