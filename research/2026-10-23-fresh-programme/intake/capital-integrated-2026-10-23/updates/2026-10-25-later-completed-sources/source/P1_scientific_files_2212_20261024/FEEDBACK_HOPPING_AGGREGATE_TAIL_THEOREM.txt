PRIVATE ASTRA P1 — LOCAL RECORD BOUNDS WITH FEEDBACK AND HOPPING
2026-10-24. New theorem and a retained failed stronger claim.

SCIENTIFIC RESULT
=================
A strong-damping condition on the ACTUAL hopping row budget controls aggregate
local-intensity tails under arbitrary permitted record feedback. This suffices
for an extensive local quantum-record error bound for density-density graphs
with hopping. It does not give a uniform tail at every named site: a finite
observation/controller example below defeats that stronger claim.

The useful invariant is therefore an aggregate mode-moment budget, rather
than an assumed stationary product intensity law or a uniformly thermal
conditional state. The proof retains the supplied joint preparation,
controller, observation access and numerical distinction.

1. SOURCE, QUANTUM INTERFACE, AND CONTROL PREMISES
================================================
Use the physical generator and coherent-mixture preparation of
GLOBAL_POSITIVE_QUARTIC_DIFFUSION_RECORD_BRIDGE.txt, with the density-edge
Hamiltonian and locally clipped covariance of
LOCAL_EDGE_POSITIVE_DIFFUSION_COROLLARY.txt. Add a Hermitian hopping matrix
h(C_t,t), now ALLOWED to depend on the permitted exposed record and retained
controller. All rates, quantum loss-subchannel constraints and common-policy
premises of those theorems remain. The reference is the continuous positive
diffusion, not the finite-jump numerical implementation.

For off-diagonal hopping assume the actual pathwise row budget

 max_i sum_(j!=i)|h_ij(C,t)|<=H                           (1)

uniformly over admitted controls and times. Hermiticity implies
|h_ij|=|h_ji|. Diagonal frequencies do not enter this budget. They remain
supplied evaluable coefficients. This H is max_i sup_C of the row sum; it is
NOT the generally larger sum_j sup_C|h_ij|.

For interaction edges e={i,j}, supplied envelopes |g_e|<=g_e,max, positive
bath allocations a_e with sum_incident a_e<=1, and 0<eta<1 define

 R_e=sqrt(I_i I_j),
 B_e=eta kappa nu a_e/(theta g_e,max),
 chi_e=min(1,B_e/R_e),
 Dhat=(kappa nu/2)I+sum_e chi_e Dq,e.                     (2)

The original hopping and density-interaction drift are retained. Local
covariance positivity and its factorization are unchanged by hopping.

The clean initial premise is product thermal positive coherent preparation
of mean scaled intensity nu per mode. A broader joint positive preparation
with arbitrary controller correlations is allowed if its ACTUAL initial
aggregate moments satisfy, for some supplied v0>=0,

 sum_i E I_i(0)^p <= m p! v0^p for every integer p>=1.     (3)

Product thermal preparation gives v0=nu. The joint law is retained in the
predictor; (3) does not acquire or eliminate the controller correlations.
The physical initial state must be its positive coherent lift, or a joint
delta_prep allowance is paid as before. A thermal total-radius marginal alone
does not imply (3) with v0=nu.

2. EXACT INTENSITY NOISE AND HOPPING CURRENT
==========================================
The local density-edge quantum covariance still has zero action on every
intensity drift and every intensity/intensity quadratic variation. Hopping
changes drift, not that covariance identity. The intensity equations have
the martingale representation

 dI_i=[kappa(nu-I_i)+J_i]dt+sqrt(2 kappa nu I_i)dBeta_i,
 J_i=2 Im[bar z_i sum_(j!=i) h_ij z_j],
 |J_i|<=2sum_(j!=i)|h_ij|sqrt(I_i I_j),                  (4)

where the Beta_i can be taken independent Brownian coordinates in an enlarged
filtration. Independence of the INTENSITY PROCESSES is not asserted: hopping
couples their drifts, and feedback can correlate them. For the proof it is
enough to use their exact diagonal quadratic variations, without choosing
particular Brownian drivers.

Density-interaction phases make no contribution to J_i. Hermiticity gives
sum_i J_i=0. Consequently the global total radius still has the exact CIR law
from the global bridge. At fixed finite m this supplies the moment domination
needed for stopped Itô formulas and passage to expectations below.

3. AGGREGATE MOMENT THEOREM
===========================
Define

 A_p(t)=sum_i E I_i(t)^p,       A_0=m,
 alpha=kappa-2H.

For every integer p>=1,

 A_p'(t)<=-p alpha A_p(t)+p^2 kappa nu A_(p-1)(t)          (5)

for almost every t, or in the equivalent integral/Dini sense.

Proof. Itô applied to I_i^p gives the exact bath terms

 -p kappa E I_i^p+p^2 kappa nu E I_i^(p-1)

and the current p E I_i^(p-1)J_i. Set a_ij=|h_ij| at a
given state, time and controller. Young's inequality gives the PATHWISE bound

 sum_(i!=j) a_ij I_i^(p-1/2)I_j^(1/2)
 <=sum_(i!=j)a_ij[(1-1/(2p))I_i^p+(1/(2p))I_j^p]
 =sum_i I_i^p sum_(j!=i)a_ij
 <=H sum_i I_i^p.                                       (6)

The equality uses a_ij=a_ji. It holds even when the chosen hopping coefficients
are correlated with the state through an arbitrarily complicated admitted
record. Equations(4),(6) bound the summed current by2pH A_p and prove(5).
No coefficient is moved out of an expectation by an illegitimate independence
assumption.

Let v(t) solve

 v'=kappa nu-alpha v,       v(0)=v0.                      (7)

Induction in p and the linear comparison inequality (5) show

 A_p(t)<=m p! v(t)^p.                                    (8)

Indeed m p!v^p has derivative -p alpha times itself plus
p^2 kappa nu [m(p-1)!v^(p-1)]. The initial inequality is (3).

If alpha>0, set

 V=max(v0,kappa nu/alpha).

Then v(t)<=V for all t. In particular thermal preparation yields

 V=kappa nu/(kappa-2H),
 A_p(t)<=m p! V^p for all t>=0.                           (9)

For a fixed finite horizon one can use the exact v(t), even when alpha<=0.
At alpha=0, v(t)=v0+kappa nu t; otherwise
v(t)=kappa nu/alpha+(v0-kappa nu/alpha)exp(-alpha t).
This extension charges the resulting time growth. Only alpha>0 gives the
uniform strong-damping envelope (9).

4. LOCAL EXCESS TAIL AND COMPLETE RECORD ERROR
==============================================
Let

 B=min_e B_e,
 d_g=max_i sum_(e incident i) g_e,max.                    (10)

The empty-interaction graph has zero quartic defect and can be omitted.
For nonempty graphs B>0 is a supplied constant. Convexity of positive part
and sqrt(I_i I_j)<=(I_i+I_j)/2 imply pointwise

 sum_e g_e,max(R_e-B_e)_+
 <=(1/2)sum_i [sum_(e incident i)g_e,max](I_i-B)_+
 <=(d_g/2)sum_i(I_i-B)_+.                                (11)

For any 0<s<1/V, positive series and (9) give

 sum_i E exp(s I_i)<=m/(1-sV).

Integrating the corresponding Markov tail yields

 sum_i E(I_i-B)_+ <=m exp(-sB)/[s(1-sV)].                 (12)

The exact local coherent-kernel defect, passive count/no-event identities,
and joint CQ contraction are unchanged from the local-edge proof. Combining
them with (11)--(12) proves, under arbitrary admitted feedback,

 TV(P_Q^T,P_hat^T)
 <=min{1,delta_prep+T m d_g exp(-sB)/[2s(1-sV)]},
                  0<s<1/V.                              (13)

The same bound applies to joint CQ half-trace distance. A convenient unoptimized
choice s=1/(2V) gives

 TV(P_Q^T,P_hat^T)
 <=min{1,delta_prep+2T m d_g V exp[-B/(2V)]}.              (14)

The exact v(t) may replace V inside the time integral, with any chosen
time-dependent admissible s(t). No optimization of a constant is needed for
the theorem.

A finite-moment alternative is useful for numerical composition. For p>1,

 (x-B)_+ <=[(p-1)^(p-1)/p^p] x^p/B^(p-1).

With c_p=(p-1)^(p-1)/p^p (and c_1=1), the record allowance can therefore also
be bounded by

 delta_prep+(T m d_g/2) inf_(integer p>=1)
                             c_p p! V^p/B^(p-1).         (15)

For a selected finite p, only the initial moment bounds through p and the
corresponding finite triangular comparison are needed. The exponential-moment
form(12)--(14) uses all orders; thermal preparation supplies them explicitly.

For maximum interaction degree Delta, |g_e|<=g0 and allocations a_e=1/Delta,
one may take d_g<=Delta g0 and B=eta kappa nu/(theta g0 Delta). Under thermal
preparation the exponent in(14) is

 B/(2V)=eta(kappa-2H)/(2theta g0 Delta).                  (16)

Thus the threshold is local in degree/coupling and the excess damping after
the admitted hopping budget. The complete-record cost still grows with m,T
and the interaction degree. This is not a dimension-free full-record claim.

5. THE STRONGER MAX-SITE CLAIM IS FALSE WITH ONLY ACTUAL ROW BOUNDS
================================================================
One tempting replacement is M_p=max_i E I_i^p in (5). That step is invalid
under adaptive choice of neighbors. A row sum bound inside an expectation
cannot bound E sum_j |h_ij(C)|I_j^p by H max_j E I_j^p: the selected neighbor
can have atypically high energy. The following compatible finite-model
experiment shows that the resulting claimed per-site envelope can fail.

Start with one unobserved receiver mode0 and K independent donor modes, all
in product thermal positive coherent preparation, independent of controller.
Take no quartic interaction and no hopping during an observation interval
[0,tau]. Observe each donor's own passive loss counts at a fixed positive
rate coefficient c fitting theta c<=d. A finite latch reports its count capped
at n+1. All physical noise and loss/gain dynamics continue during observation.

For one donor, its hidden I_t is stationary CIR with Exp(nu) marginal and
its count N_tau is Cox with integrated intensity
Lambda_tau=c integral_0^tau I_s ds. For fixed n,

 P(N_tau=n)=E[exp(-Lambda_tau)Lambda_tau^n/n!],
 E[I_tau 1_(N_tau=n)]=E[I_tau exp(-Lambda_tau)Lambda_tau^n/n!].

Continuity of CIR in every finite L^p at this stationary preparation yields

 P(N_tau=n)/(c tau)^n -> nu^n,
 E[I_tau 1_(N_tau=n)]/(c tau)^n ->(n+1)nu^(n+1)
                                      as tau decreases to zero.             (17)

One justification uses Lambda_tau/(c tau)->I_0 in L^p, I_tau->I_0 in L^p,
and uniform integrability from stationary CIR maximal moments on a fixed
short interval. The thermal moments are E I_0^n=n!nu^n. Hence

 E[I_tau|N_tau=n] ->(n+1)nu.                              (18)

Choose n=128 and a sufficiently small positive tau for the conditional mean
to be at least128nu. Let p_tau=P(N_tau=128)>0. Choose the finite donor count
K with (1-p_tau)^K<=1/2. At time tau the controller selects the first donor
with reported count128; if none exists it chooses any donor. Because donors
are independent and the criterion for selection uses their own count,

 E I_selected(tau)>=64nu.                               (19)

This is an acquired finite classical count label, not hidden-amplitude access.
All phases remain circular because these individual counts are phase blind.
The receiver is independent and thermal. The selection may require a very
large K and observation budget; they are genuine resources and are paid here.

Now couple ONLY the selected donor to the receiver by a real hopping of
strength h=kappa/4, for time1/kappa. The hopping matrix is Hermitian and every
actual row sum is at most H=h. The supposed max-site theorem would predict
V=kappa nu/(kappa-2h)=2nu.

The linear two-mode OU solution instead gives EXACTLY

 E I_receiver(tau+1/kappa)
 =nu+exp(-1)sin^2(1/4)[E I_selected(tau)-nu]
 >=nu[1+63 exp(-1)sin^2(1/4)]>2nu.                       (20)

The last inequality can be verified without floating arithmetic: exp(-1)>1/3,
sin(1/4)>=1/4-(1/4)^3/6=95/384, and
63*(95/384)^2/3=568575/442368>1. The cross coherent mean at the switch is zero
by circular phases and receiver independence, so no unobserved phase estimate
or phase-alignment control is being assumed.

This counterexample is also an exact physical quantum example: without a
quartic term the coherent-mixture OU/count interface is exact. It uses a
finite number of modes, a finite alphabet of count-latch values, finite
duration, physical passive loss access, and the admitted small hopping row
budget. The controller stores the selected donor and finite count counters.
Its required mode count is determined by the strictly positive p_tau; the
existence of a small enough tau follows from(18). There is no claim this is an
economical experimental protocol. It disproves a universal per-site bound
that did not charge this mode/access budget. A more restrictive fixed
observation class would need to be assessed on its own terms.

The aggregate theorem is consistent with the example. Hopping moves energy
between named modes and leaves total energy unchanged. A selected receiver
can exceed2nu while A_1=mnu and the aggregate higher-moment budget(9) hold.

5A. AN EXPLICIT FINITE OBSERVATION BUDGET FOR THE COUNTEREXAMPLE
==============================================================
The preceding limiting choice can be made fully explicit without a numerical
simulation. Take kappa=nu=c=1, n=128 and

 tau=1/66564,
 K=2*66564^128 donor modes.                              (21)

For a concrete physical instance set theta=1, so the initial mean photon
occupation is1, bath gain/loss coefficients are b=1,d=2, and the monitored
individual jump is a_i, a valid half of the loss bath. There is no quartic
term in this counterexample, so the coherent OU/count interface is exact.

Here is a direct bound establishing the required conditional donor energy.
For general kappa,nu,c write A=tau^(-1)integral_0^tau I_s ds and
a=(1-exp(-kappa tau))/(kappa tau). The stationary CIR is reversible because

 L f=kappa nu pi^(-1) partial_I[I pi partial_I f],
 pi(I)=exp(-I/nu)/nu.

Its reversible first-moment identity gives
E[A|I_tau]=a I_tau+(1-a)nu>=a I_tau. Conditional Jensen yields

 E[I_tau A^n]>=a^n(n+1)!nu^(n+1).

Holder and time Jensen give

 E[I_tau A^(n+1)]
 <=[E I_tau^(n+2)]^(1/(n+2))[E A^(n+2)]^((n+1)/(n+2))
 <=(n+2)!nu^(n+2).

Also P(N_tau=n)<= (c tau nu)^n. Since exp(-c tau A)>=1-c tau A,
the positive numerator lower bound divided by this upper denominator proves

 E[I_tau|N_tau=n]
 >=(n+1)nu[a^n-c tau(n+2)nu]
 >=(n+1)nu[1-tau(n kappa+c(n+2)nu)].                     (22)

The last step uses a>=1-kappa tau and Bernoulli, at kappa tau<=1. For(21),
66564=2*129*258, so(22) gives at least128.5 nu, and hence128nu.

Likewise Jensen gives E A^n>=a^n n!nu^n and
E A^(n+1)<=(n+1)!nu^(n+1). Therefore

 p_tau=P(N_tau=n)
 >=(c tau nu)^n[a^n-c tau(n+1)nu]
 >=tau^128/2.                                           (23)

The explicit K in(21) makes K p_tau>=1. The failure probability obeys
(1-p_tau)^K<=1/(1+K p_tau)<=1/2, proving(19) with entirely
specified finite inputs. The final one-unit coupling has h=1/4 and still
gives the strict violation(20).

This finite budget is astronomically large. It uses K individually accessible
loss ports, K counters with values0 through129, at most129K retained count
increments before those latches saturate, and a retained selected-donor index.
The controller need not retain count times or a continuous hidden amplitude.
Both the spatial access and observation cost are part of the counterexample,
not evidence that the selection is an economical capability. Its purpose is
to distinguish a false max-site theorem from the valid aggregate statement.

6. WHEN A UNIFORM SITE ENVELOPE IS AVAILABLE
==========================================
A stronger fixed edge envelope restores the max-site version. If supplied
numbers bar h_ij bound |h_ij(C,t)| individually, and

 H_env=max_i sum_(j!=i) bar h_ij,
 kappa>2H_env,

then Holder at every fixed i yields the upper Dini inequality

 D^+ max_i E I_i^p
 <=-p(kappa-2H_env)max_i E I_i^p
                         +p^2 kappa nu max_i E I_i^(p-1).

Thus max_i E I_i^p<=p![kappa nu/(kappa-2H_env)]^p from thermal prep. This
requires the union of the possible hopping contacts, not just the sum of the
contacts active on one branch. In the counterexample the receiver can choose
among K donors, so H_env=Kh rather than h. The stronger premise precisely
charges the previously unaccounted choice of neighbor.

7. IMPLEMENTATION AND SCIENTIFIC LIMITS
======================================
The aggregate proof closes the previously explicit local-tail gap for
density-density matter with feedback hopping in the strong-damping regime.
It leaves the general local non-density quartic feedback problem open:
individual intensity covariances can then have quantum corrections, so the
moment equation(4) is no longer the one used here. The open-loop local-quartic
invariant-Gaussian theorem remains a separate broader-Hamiltonian result.

The positive process still retains a full phase-space law when conditioned on
an acquired record, plus the real controller. No finite posterior family or
free observational acquisition has been proved. A generative sample path has
2m current real coordinates, but numerical precision, sampling variance,
conditioning rarity and controller storage remain real resources.

The finite positive jump sampler being constructed elsewhere does not share
the exact CIR/intensity moment generator. Its own aggregate or radial
recurrence must be supplied before using(13)--(15) for its law. The present
theorem concerns the continuous positive source and its exact coherent-kernel
comparison with the physical quantum instrument.
