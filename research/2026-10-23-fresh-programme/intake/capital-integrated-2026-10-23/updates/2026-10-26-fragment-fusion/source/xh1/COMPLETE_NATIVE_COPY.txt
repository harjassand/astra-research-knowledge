## Astra Chemistry — New construction: bond-qualified fragment assembly

The strongest successor to the earlier distributed-assembly model is hierarchical synthesis with covalent-junction verification and local replacement of failed fragments.

The essential change is to avoid making every irreversible error fatal to an entire long polymer.

The physical starting point is credible but incomplete. Niu, Hili and Liu demonstrated DNA-programmed synthesis of chemically diverse polymers containing up to 16 consecutive building blocks. Separately, DNA-templated click chemistry has been demonstrated with products that remain attached after denaturing washes remove noncovalently associated strands.&#x20;

### A product-qualified junction test

The proposed mechanism attaches temporary DNA identifiers covalently to two intended molecular fragments, A and B.

After attempting to join them, a denaturing separation removes noncovalent associations. The B identifier should remain associated with A's covalent anchor only when there is a persistent covalent path connecting them.

This avoids the original donor-release problem:

- Correct bond: the two identifiers remain connected through the product.
- Hydrolysis or missed reaction: the identifiers separate; the parent may be retried if its reactive site remains competent.
- Wrong stable bond: a covalent connection may still survive. Correct partner identity, reactive-site specificity and fragment purity must therefore be checked separately.

This is a topological test for persistent connectivity, not a perfect molecular bond sensor. Its important property is that hydrolysis cannot impersonate successful transfer merely by releasing an activated donor, provided the identifiers have no independent covalent connection or nonspecific route to the anchor.

The next construction problem becomes how to exploit this limited but physically meaningful certificate without losing scalability.

### Exact resource scaling under imperfect bond verification

Suppose every fragment-joining attempt has probability \\(p\\) of being accepted as chemically correct, probability \\(f\\) of accepting a wrong product, and probability \\(1-p-f\\) of rejection. A rejected join destroys both submitted fragments, which must be rebuilt. This deliberately charges a severe recovery cost.

For a balanced assembly tree producing \\(L=2^k\\) building blocks, if the acceptance probabilities remain independent of fragment length, the expected material cost \\(C_L\\), with unit-cost monomers and no additional joining overhead, obeys

\\[ C_1=1,\qquad C\_{2n}=\frac{2C_n}{p+f}. \\]

Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{C_L=L^{\log_2(2/(p+f))}.} \\]

The expected resource requirement grows polynomially rather than exponentially, even though any rejected fusion sacrifices both fragments.

However, the false-acceptance requirement becomes stringent. Under the same assumptions, achieving at least 95% correct final products requires

\\[ \boxed{f \leq 4.513\times10^{-5}\quad (p=0.9,\ L=1024).} \\]

Repeating the same denaturing selection cannot eliminate wrong covalent bonds that consistently pass its connectivity test. Their residual acceptance probability sets a fidelity floor.

### A serious failure mode

The polynomial advantage disappears if long fragments become progressively harder to join.

For example, if fusion success declines with fragment size as \\(q(n)=q_0e^{-\kappa n}\\), balanced assembly has an additional resource multiplier

\\[ \boxed{e^{2\kappa(L-1)}.} \\]

Even modest size dependence eventually restores exponential scaling. At \\(L=1024\\), a hypothetical \\(\kappa=0.001\\) makes material consumption about 7.74 times higher than the size-independent model predicts.

This turns length-independent reactive end accessibility into a requirement of the architecture, not an optional optimisation.

The resource recurrence, optimal assembly-tree algorithm, and independently checked stochastic bounds are implemented in the executable research model.

## New structural result: a cyclization-resistant assembly representation

I found a useful way to reorganize alternating AA/BB chemistry.

Let A denote a building block with two alkyne groups and B one with two azide groups. A correctly joined alternating fragment has the same type of reactive group at both ends exactly when its length is odd.

Consequently, even-length fragments can potentially cyclize through their complementary end groups. A standard doubling strategy creates intermediates of lengths 2, 4, 8, 16, and so on—all in the vulnerable class.

Instead, construct only odd-length completed intermediates:

A ··· A

A ··· A

A ··· A – B – A ··· A

Odd length; identical exterior reactive groups

By induction, every completed intermediate ends in two A-type reactive groups and cannot undergo intramolecular azide–alkyne cyclization between those ends. This does not exclude other side reactions.

### The crucial counterconstruction

During fusion, however, each input A fragment has two exposed terminal groups. A central B connector can attach to the wrong end or connect both ends of the same fragment.

Under an illustrative symmetric, equally reactive four-port model, exhaustive enumeration gives:

| Outcome                             | Probability |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Correct fragment-to-fragment fusion | 16.67%      |
| Intrafragment cyclization           | 33.33%      |
| Other wrong connections             | 50.00%      |

This is not an experimental prediction. It proves that safe stored fragments do not automatically give a safe joining reaction.

If each intended end has reaction propensity \\(\alpha\\) and each unintended exposed end has propensity \\(\eta\\), the model gives

\\[ P\_{\mathrm{correct}}= \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\eta} \frac{\alpha}{\alpha+2\eta}. \\]

Achieving 90% correct joining requires \\(\alpha/\eta\geq27.65\\); achieving 99% requires at least 297.67.

The most consequential missing operation is therefore selective exterior-end masking or an experimentally demonstrated equivalent geometric restriction. A DNA recognition sequence alone does not establish this selectivity.

## Improved mathematical bound

I also derived a tighter guarantee for the previous parallel reaction model with heterogeneous reaction rates. If \\(T_i\\) is the independent intended-bond formation time, \\(h_i\\) is irreversible damage hazard, and \\(w\_{ij}\\) is the wrong-bond hazard between unfinished sites, then

\\[ P\_{\mathrm{clean}} = \mathbb E\\!\left[ e^{-\sum_i h_iT_i-\sum\_{i\ne j}w\_{ij}\min(T_i,T_j)} \right]. \\]

Applying Jensen's inequality yields an explicit lower bound that accommodates unequal site rates and spatially restricted crosslinks. Under the earlier illustrative 999-bond parameters, it improves the certified lower bound from 98.48% to 98.74%. I verified its small-system cases against an independently implemented transition recurrence and stochastic calculations.

## What should survive this comparison

Iterative exponential polymer growth is already established, including protecting-group-free approaches and the 2025 synthesis of sequence-defined peptoids. Those are serious experimental comparators, not techniques Astra can claim to have invented.&#x20;

The distinctive unresolved synthesis operation is more specific: DNA-programmed fragments that can independently undergo bond-qualified acceptance, rejection, and rebuilding, while preserving chemical sequence and orientation.

The smallest discriminating construction is two independently identifiable A-ended fragments joined through a B connector, compared across exposed and protected outer termini. The critical measurements are correct junction orientation, cyclized side products, residual reactive-end competence, and whether a missed reaction permits recovery. Until these are established, the protected-PNA relay remains the better-grounded self-indexing construction, while hierarchical fusion remains a potentially broader but more conditional route.

The research artifacts are preserved:

- Mathematical proofs and chemical assumptions
- Verified fragment-fusion and fidelity model
- Verified polarity and cyclization counterexample
- 1,023-position DNA address library and its generator and checker

The address library is combinatorially checked, not biochemically validated. No integrated chemical synthesis or civilisation-level capability is established yet.
