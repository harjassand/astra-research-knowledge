A. COVALENT PATH CHECKPOINT -- EXACT SCOPE
Let covalent molecular graph G consist of rooted fragment F, donor D and their respective temporary *covalently attached* identifier handles h_F,h_D. Assume the rooted support connects covalently to F only; h_D initially belongs only to D; no other covalent paths join F and D; DNA splints and hybridization bonds are fully dissociated during the wash and no nonspecific attachment persists. After a reaction, h_D remains in the rooted connected component iff a new covalent path has been formed from F to D. Hydrolysis of an electrophile, donor disappearance or dehybridization alone cannot create this path. Therefore a retained donor identifier is a qualified CONNECTION report, unlike donor release.
Limitation: a WRONG covalent bond from D to an exposed side group on F passes this test. Similarly, misoriented joining of correct fragments passes. Connectivity and total mass cannot prove sequence or site identity. Inductive sequence correctness additionally requires verified correct leaf identities, only the two intended reactive ports being chemically competent during a join, known fragment orientation, and no alternative covalent path. Under these assumptions, concatenation correctness follows by induction over a contiguous binary assembly tree. All chemistry/protection assumptions remain to be measured. Periodic denaturing steps and reversible capture must preserve polymer, root attachment and temporary labels.
Primary physical precedent: Niu, Hili, Liu, Nat Chem (2013), doi:10.1038/nchem.1577 (macrocyclic PNA adapters, AA/BB CuAAC, cleavable cargo adapters, 16 consecutive units / 90 beta-amino acid residues). Direct product attachment after template-directed click and denaturing wash: Chem Commun (2014), doi:10.1039/C4CC02900C, with reported oxidative DNA damage risk; not a demonstration of our composite assembly.

B. STOCHASTIC CHAIN OF COVALENT REACTIONS -- REPRESENTATION IMPROVEMENT
E intended bonds i, each independent intended-reaction time T_i~Exp(a_i). While i remains unfinished, irreversible damage hazard h_i is active. For ordered wrong pair (i,j) both unfinished, wrong irreversible crosslink hazard w_ij is active until min(T_i,T_j); all Poisson clocks independent of one another and the intended clocks. Then exact no-error completion probability is the Feynman-Kac/Laplace identity:

 P_clean = E[exp(-sum_i h_i T_i - sum_(i!=j) w_ij min(T_i,T_j))].

Proof: conditional on every intended completion time, survival of each independent irreversible hazard contributes exp(-rate x available time); multiply and integrate. This equals Bellman first-event DP over subsets of unfinished bonds. Jensen immediately yields a rigorous lower bound

 P_clean >= exp(-sum_i h_i/a_i - sum_(i!=j) w_ij/(a_i+a_j)).

For a finite deadline t, the exact success probability is the same expectation times 1{max_i T_i<=t}. A Jensen lower bound results by conditioning the independent exponential clocks on Ti<=t. Define pi=1-exp(-a_i t), mi=1/a_i-t/(exp(a_i t)-1), and
 mij = integral_0^t [(exp(-a_i u)-exp(-a_i t))/pi]*[(exp(-a_j u)-exp(-a_j t))/pj] du.
Then P_clean_by_t >= (product_i pi)*exp(-sum h_i mi - sum_{i!=j} w_ij mij).
This is a mathematically useful certificate for arbitrary heterogeneous reactive sites, spatial couplings, and deadlines. It is NOT a measured yield. The independent test script compares random small-graph DP, Monte Carlo clocks and deadline bounds.

For homogeneous spatial wrong rates w_ij=b*r^(|i-j|-1), 0<=r<1, the new bound is
 P >= exp[-E h/a - (b/a) sum_(d=1)^(E-1) (E-d) r^(d-1)].
At E=999,a=.05/s,h=5e-7/s,b=1e-7/s,r=.25 it yields 0.987429236, improving the prior conservative 0.984798839 while remaining below no-wrong upper 0.990059784. All numbers hypothetical.

C. REJECTED-FUSION REBUILD THEOREM
There are L known, physically recoverable monomers. At each contiguous binary tree node, joining two accepted component fragments gives:
- correctly accepted product probability p;
- wrong accepted product probability f;
- rejected product probability 1-p-f;
with 0<p+f<=1. All rejected attempts irreversibly consume both submitted fragments, which are independently rebuilt; each attempt is IID for its node, and the probability does not depend on fragment size unless specified. No partial salvage is assumed. Leaf resource cost is 1; join cost is c>=0.
Expected consumed resource at node v is
 C_v=(C_left+C_right+c_v)/(p_v+f_v).
For uniform q=p+f, zero join cost and L=2^k, balanced assembly has exact C_L=(2/q)^k=L^{log2(2/q)}. Serial growth with total previous product destroyed on failure has exponential resource cost for q<1; the balanced scheme polynomial only so long as q stays bounded away from zero independent of fragment size. This computational resource statement does not establish a working physical assembler, useful time-to-product or purification cost.
If accepted wrong junctions occur independently with same p,f, conditional probability every junction of a fully accepted product is correct equals (p/(p+f))^(L-1). For L=1024,p=.9,f=1e-5, expected leaf resource 2936.4770 and conditional correct fraction 0.98869775. To attain 95% final correctness, the exact requirement is f <= p*(0.95^(-1/1023)-1)=4.51272e-5. Repeated testing using the same connectivity-only observable cannot reduce an undetected wrong COVALENT link that always satisfies that observable. Noncovalent background rejection is a different measurement.
If join acceptance drops as q(n)=q0 exp(-kappa*n) for joining n-monomer fragments, the balanced L=2^k cost becomes C_L=L*q0^(-log2 L)*exp(2*kappa*(L-1)). Thus size-dependent accessibility can destroy the polynomial advantage. For kappa=.001 and L=1024, penalty = 7.73689; for kappa=.01, penalty ~7.685e8. Need a size-independent reaction port, or different mechanism.

D. CHEMISTRY-AWARE ASSEMBLY TREE
If acceptance at boundary i|i+1 is q_i independent of child sizes, joining cost c_i and leaf costs c_leaf_i, exact minimum expected resource for a contiguous sequence is
 C(i,i)=c_leaf_i;
 C(i,j)=min_(i<=k<j) [C(i,k)+C(k+1,j)+c_k]/q_k.
Optimal substructure follows because rejected parent attempts rebuild each selected child; replace any child with a cheaper protocol without changing q_k to improve the parent. DP computes optimum in O(L^3) time O(L^2) space. Exhaustive Catalan enumeration independently verified small L. Example 32 monomers, all q_i=.95 except q_16=.35, c_i=.25, leaf=1: naive midpoint-balanced cost 138.3303; optimized 55.9646. Both numbers are resources of the abstract model, not real experimental throughput.

E. NEW POLARITY/TOPOLOGY OBSTRUCTION IN AA/BB CLICK FRAGMENT FUSION
Represent every A monomer as homobifunctional alkyne-alkyne and every B as homobifunctional azide-azide (or reverse). An alternating linear fragment of length n has same-type terminal ports iff n is odd, complementary ports iff n is even. Proof: port identity alternates once per monomer; two endpoints have the same type exactly when first and last monomer are of the same parity, equivalent to n odd. Thus even-length completed fragments permit intramolecular azide-alkyne closure if termini encounter one another. Every conventional doubling level 2,4,8,... is in this vulnerable parity class; no DNA code eliminates this chemical compatibility.

Constructive representation: maintain completed stored fragments only as odd-length A...(BA)*...A, ending in two A ports. Merge two valid A-ended odd fragments with one homobifunctional B connector:
  (A ... A)  +  B  +  (A ... A) -> (A ... A B A ... A).
The output is odd-length and A-ended by induction. For target length 2N-1, a binary tree with N A-monomer leaves and N-1 B connectors forms exactly the alternating intended sequence. Every *completed* intermediate is terminally incapable of intramolecular A--B CuAAC. For even intended lengths, a final B terminator must be treated separately and its remaining port consumed or passivated.

DECISIVE FAILURE OF THE UNMASKED BRIDGE: In the bridge step, each A-ended input fragment exposes two A ports, while B has two identical B ports. There are four candidate A ports; only two are intended: left fragment's right port and right fragment's left port. In a symmetric sequential without-replacement port-selection toy model, there are C(4,2)=6 equally probable endpoint pairs. One creates correct fusion, two consume both termini of the same fragment and make a ring, and three create wrong orientations/crosslinks. Therefore correct=1/6, ring=1/3, other wrong=1/2. This is not a prediction of real collision rates, but demonstrates that safe completed endpoints DO NOT ensure safe ternary fusion.

If each correct terminal is selected at per-port hazard alpha and each undesired exposed outer terminal at hazard eta, with stationary relative hazards after the first reaction, then the exact probability both clicked termini are correct is
 q_bridge = [alpha/(alpha+eta)] [alpha/(alpha+2eta)].
At alpha/eta=1 this is 1/6. To reach q>=.90 requires alpha/eta>=27.65097; q>=.95 requires >=57.65905; q>=.99 requires >=297.66518. Local exposure cannot be assumed from address-code combinatorics. After first covalent attachment, the same-fragment undesired outer terminus may react at much greater intramolecular effective molarity, making these toy requirements optimistic. This demands selective exterior-end masking, demonstrated geometric exclusion, or a more orthogonal chemistry before the representation yields a robust chemical operation.

If bridge acceptance is truly size-independent q=.9, a completed alternating 1023-unit product uses 512 A leaves and 511 B bridge monomers; with each bridge cost one B and rejected attempts destroying children, expected monomer input is 2522.07194 under the simplified model. With unmasked symmetric q=1/6, cost is 7.974e9, ruling out that configuration for practical use. Per-bridge q includes two required chemical bonds, so it cannot be imported from one-junction success rates.

F. RESEARCH STATUS AND EQUAL-INFORMATION ALTERNATIVES
Hierarchical iterative exponential growth is PRIOR ART, including Clapperton/Hood/Tran Chem Sci 2025 doi:10.1039/D5SC01296A and protecting-group-free multi-click IEG 2021 doi:10.1021/acsmacrolett.0c00812. Cabello-Garcia et al., Nat Chem 2025 doi:10.1038/s41557-025-01831-x, demonstrated a recyclable template for DNA dimerization, and embedding into a covalent-formation reaction; not an autonomously fully covalent long-polymer constructor.

A serious competing route is protecting-group-free IEG using orthogonal chemistries. Its advantage is an actual demonstrated soluble oligomer chemistry; the suggested DNA-programmed checkpoint's only conditional advantage is independent programmes and recovery of verified fragments. A positive bond-path capture will not measure wrong-site specificity, ring closure, selection purity or steric accessibility; all require separate controls. Final product identity needs direct junction-sensitive analytics such as MS/MS, not only DNA identifiers or total mass. DNA-cleavable handles must be retained through checkpointing then removed under cargo-compatible conditions.
