1. Literal input and control contract
------------------------------------

Use the original fixed canonical coordinates and

  Hb = (1/2) sum_(j=1,2) nu_j(q_j^2+p_j^2) = nu_1 I_1+nu_2 I_2,
  nu_1>nu_2>0,       I_j=(q_j^2+p_j^2)/2.

Independently average each oscillator angle of the original density
1_[-1,1]^4/16. Its stationary density is g(I_1)g(I_2), where

  g(I)=1/4                                      0<=I<=1/2,
      =1/4-arccos(1/sqrt(2I))/pi                 1/2<I<1,
      =0                                       I>=1.

Equivalently, the action variables have independent normalized densities
h(I)=2pi g(I), with independent uniform angles. The moments are

  mu_0=1,  mu_1=1/3,  mu_2=7/45,  mu_3=3/35.

The covariance is still I_4/3 and E(Hb)=(nu_1+nu_2)/3. Thus the earlier
linear-canonical passivity calculation still applies. The free stock is now
stationary, so the unaveraged cube's oscillating fourth moment is unavailable.
The stock is non-Gaussian and is not a decreasing function of Hb: in the
region I_1 in (1/2,1), I_2 in (0,1/2), changing actions by (-d,c d),
c>nu_1/nu_2, increases both Hb and the density for sufficiently small d>0.
The scalar protocol below, rather than an unrestricted rearrangement argument,
establishes actual work extraction in the stated control class.

Prescribe ANY finite T>0 and amplitude ceiling B>0. Allow a real smooth
position-only potential V(q,t), zero near t=0,T, with sup_(q,t)|V|<=B.
There is no momentum control or kinetic counterterm. Choose once a smooth
cutoff chi(q) in [0,1], equal to 1 on |q_j|<=2 and zero outside |q_j|<3, and put

  U(q)=chi(q) q_1 q_2^3,       V_epsilon(q,t)=epsilon u(t)U(q).

Hence ||U||_infinity<=81. The shape and temporal pulse will be fixed before
choosing one sufficiently small positive epsilon. No resolution limit is used.

2. Exact finite-time second-order response
----------------------------------------

Use q_j=sqrt(2I_j) cos(theta_j), p_j=-sqrt(2I_j) sin(theta_j), so theta_dot=nu.
For the polynomial on the cutoff plateau write U=sum_m U_m(I)exp(i m.theta).
Let omega_m=nu.m and u_hat(w)=integral_0^T u(t)exp(iwt)dt. If W denotes work
ON the particles, the exact endpoint identity and its small-epsilon expansion are

  W(epsilon)=E_final(Hb)-E_initial(Hb)
            =integral_0^T E[partial_t V_epsilon]dt
            =-(epsilon^2/2) sum_m omega_m C_m |u_hat(omega_m)|^2
              +O(epsilon^3),                                      (1)

  C_m=integral |U_m(I)|^2 [m.grad_I(h(I_1)h(I_2))]dI_1 dI_2.

Here f=h(I_1)h(I_2) with angle measure dtheta_1 dtheta_2/(2pi)^2; this is
the same physical density g(I_1)g(I_2) relative to dI_1 dI_2 dtheta_1 dtheta_2.
The first variation vanishes because the input is stationary. One derivation of
(1) is to use the interaction frame of the actual bare flow. Its first-order
Hamiltonian is G=sum_m U_m u_hat(omega_m)exp(i m.theta). The second-order
ordered term differs from (1/2)X_G^2 by a Hamiltonian commutator X_K. Its mean
first variation of Hb is zero, since integral f{Hb,K}=-integral K{Hb,f}=0.
For {F,G}=F_theta.G_I-F_I.G_theta,

  integral f{{Hb,G},G} = -integral {Hb,G}{f,G}.

Angle integration retains only opposite integer vectors, giving (1). Equal
temporal frequencies do NOT introduce cross terms between distinct m: the
angle integral still requires opposite vectors. Pairing m and -m removes the
1/2 when listing one representative per pair.

These integrations by parts are legitimate: on the nonnegative action
half-line h is continuous, compactly supported, and W^(1,1), with an integrable derivative singularity at I=1/2.
At I=1 it vanishes; the positive powers of I in every term remove the I=0
boundary. In particular integral I^k h'(I)dI=-k mu_(k-1), k>=1.

For U=q_1 q_2^3 the only Fourier vectors are (plus/minus 1,plus/minus 1)
and (plus/minus 1,plus/minus 3). Their squared coefficients are respectively
9 I_1 I_2^3/16 and I_1 I_2^3/16. All four paired branches are

       m       omega_m             C_m
     (1, 1)    nu_1+nu_2           -19/140
     (1,-1)    nu_1-nu_2            11/280
     (1, 3)    nu_1+3nu_2          -29/840
     (1,-3)    nu_1-3nu_2            1/42.

For example integral I_1 I_2^3(m.grad f)=-m_1 mu_3-3m_2 mu_1 mu_2.
Consequently the full quadratic coefficient, before filtering, is

  W_2 = (19/140)(nu_1+nu_2)|u_hat(nu_1+nu_2)|^2
        -(11/280)(nu_1-nu_2)|u_hat(nu_1-nu_2)|^2
        +(29/840)(nu_1+3nu_2)|u_hat(nu_1+3nu_2)|^2
        -(1/42)(nu_1-3nu_2)|u_hat(nu_1-3nu_2)|^2.                 (2)

Here W(epsilon)=epsilon^2 W_2+O(epsilon^3). The last term is absorbing when
nu_1<3nu_2 and emitting when nu_1>3nu_2. At nu_1=3nu_2 it is zero.

3. A finite smooth pulse with exact notches
-----------------------------------------

Set omega=nu_1-nu_2>0. Let Bad be the DISTINCT positive nonzero numbers among

  {nu_1+nu_2, |nu_1-3nu_2|, nu_1+3nu_2}

after removing omega if it occurs. Choose real nonzero eta>=0 in
C_c^infinity(0,T), supported on a short enough interval about t_c that
cos(omega(t-t_c))>0 on its support. Then eta_hat(omega) is nonzero. Define

  u_0 = product_(b in Bad)(partial_t^2+b^2) eta,
  u = u_0/||u_0||_infinity.

This is a real smooth pulse, zero near the endpoints, with ||u||_infinity=1.
Its Fourier transform is product_(b in Bad)(b^2-w^2)eta_hat(w), divided by
the nonzero normalization. Thus every unwanted nonzero branch in (2) vanishes
EXACTLY, and the target survives. No long-window or rotating-wave limit occurs.

The target can coincide with another absolute branch only at nu_1=2nu_2.
There the additional positive-frequency vector is (-1,3), whose C is -1/42.
Its sum with the target is 11/280-1/42=13/840>0. At nu_1=3nu_2 the zero
frequency contributes zero work and need not be notched. Therefore

  W(epsilon) = -epsilon^2 c + O(epsilon^3),
  c = omega |u_hat(omega)|^2 times
      {11/280,  nu_1 != 2nu_2;
       13/840,  nu_1 == 2nu_2} >0.                             (3)

4. Literal cutoff, remainder and fixed ceilings
-----------------------------------------------

The full scalar control is globally smooth and bounded, not an unbounded
polynomial. Let L=||grad U||_infinity<infinity. The bare flow R_t is orthogonal
in the given canonical metric. Duhamel for the actual full flow gives

  |z_epsilon(t)-R_t z_initial| <= epsilon T L,   0<=t<=T.

Every free initial-stock trajectory has |q_j|<=sqrt(2). Choosing
epsilon T L <2-sqrt(2) keeps every perturbed trajectory in the unit-cutoff
plateau. Hence the polynomial Fourier calculation is literally the calculation
on the entire prepared stock, not a collocation assumption or discarded tail.

For a fixed cutoff, T, frequencies and normalized pulse, the interaction-frame
equation is x_dot=epsilon u(t)R_-t X_U(R_t x). This field and its first three
spatial derivatives are globally bounded. Its flow has uniformly bounded first
three epsilon derivatives on a compact epsilon interval and the compact input
support, by differentiating the integral equation and Gronwall. The quadratic
observable Hb then has a bounded third epsilon derivative. Integrating against
the fixed bounded stock gives a finite C with

  |W(epsilon)-epsilon^2 W_2| <= C epsilon^3

for all sufficiently small epsilon>=0. This proof does not require a second
derivative of h. Choose one fixed epsilon>0 below the compact-flow threshold,
B/81, and c/(2C) (omit the last restriction if C=0). Then

  ||V_epsilon||_infinity<=B,        -W(epsilon)>=c epsilon^2/2>0. (4)

All controls are off at the fixed final T. Their force, spatial derivatives and
temporal slew are finite at this selected instance. The notch operator can make
u and its derivatives extremely large before normalization; the normalized
target amplitude and extractable work may therefore be very small. If an
additional positive slew ceiling S is prescribed, epsilon can also be chosen
below S/(||U||_infinity ||u'||_infinity). This preserves strict positivity,
without supplying a useful uniform power or work lower bound over changing B,T,
frequency uncertainty, or ceiling parameters.

5. Meaning, strongest incumbent and review boundary
--------------------------------------------------

This answers the literal scalar-control existence gate for the SAME stationary
phase-averaged stock: strictly positive extracted bare-particle work -W_on>0
is possible at any fixed
B,T>0 without momentum control, kinetic cancellation, fine spatial resolution,
or nonstationary angle coherence. It consumes the stationary non-Gaussian
distribution; it is not the intermode microcell target map, full Gardner optimum,
or fixed nonzero work with action tending to zero. The stock does not return.
Preparation, physical field/actuator energy, autonomous load and state reset are
not acquired by this prescribed-potential calculation.

Strong existing explanations are the known distinction between Gaussian/linear
passivity and unrestricted non-Gaussian ergotropy, and classical available
energy. Brown--Friis--Huber, arXiv:1608.04977v2 / NJP18 113028 (2016), concerns
quantum Gaussian operations and arbitrary-unitary non-Gaussian work, not this
classical finite scalar pulse. Campisi, EPL154 ae652c (2026), gives a classical
quench-to-f(rho) plus adiabatic-return prescription; that generally permits a
phase-space-dependent Hamiltonian and is not a fixed-B,T scalar implementation.
KPS arXiv:2509.24960v2 supplies mechanical controllability with unrestricted real
piecewise-constant coefficients, not the present fixed amplitude contract.
The bounded comparison does NOT establish priority or novelty of this pulse.
