Claim. In the literal stationary finite-meter/positive-cascade class below,
finite safe b-c experience can acquire the polynomial transfer coefficients
needed by the known-filter weak-operation procedure, to any finite requested
whole-episode tolerance and finite confidence. Neither an ideal output
sensor nor a calibrated b/c measurement is supplied. Repeated or coincident
poles are allowed. Detector contrast, finite preparation/tails and source
failure are accounted. The shared response ACROSS quadratures remains a
structural promise; no experiment, physical response data, hardware match,
optimal learning method, practical advantage or novelty is claimed here.

1. Literal class, prior information and exactly safe access

The specimen initially lies in c, with arbitrary g-independent controller
and reference state. Original a/e coupling gV and terminal gamma loss are
unchanged, but this acquisition uses ONLY direct b-c and the already allowed
private-meter b-c port. On their invariant sample span {b,c},
Z=-P_b, gV=0 and P_e=0. Hence specimen destruction is EXACTLY zero on every
calibration trajectory, including meter faults/reset/no-click outcomes.
This is one specimen, never replaced. Dangerous-port commands and their
actuator states start at zero and are never excited during acquisition.

Known model structure: fixed finite order m, unit DC gain, identical scalar
response on all later named quadratures, no cross-port mixing, and
 tau_j in [tau_min,tau_max], 0<tau_min<=tau_max<infinity.
Their values are UNKNOWN. In command-clock units,

 P(s)=product_j(1+tau_j s)=1+p_1s+...+p_ms^m,
 tau_1 x_1'+x_1=v, tau_j x_j'+x_j=x_(j-1), u=x_m.        (1)

The initial actuator state is zero; bounded subsequent commands imply a
known state envelope R0. More generally a supplied finite R0 is sufficient.
No uniform finite settling from an unbounded arbitrary actuator state is
claimed. Coefficients are stationary throughout source and target use.

Safe dephasing a=kappa/2 has known positive compact bounds; its value may
also be learned below by a DC experiment. The finite reset/dephasing and
control rates in the chosen meter model have supplied finite bounds, with
positive lower bounds where a gap/detection event is required. Commands,
controller couplings, waveforms and timing are implemented to the finite
errors budgeted below. Absolute SI time is not required: fitted poles and
later commands use the SAME stable command-clock units. Unbounded drift,
unknown error floors or an unknown common clock change are outside this
stationary model and are not acquired by coefficient inversion.

The causal kernel h_P is a convolution of positive exponential kernels.
It is nonnegative, integrates to one, and has uniformly bounded first
moments and exponential tails on the compact pole class, repeated poles
included. Thus bounded commands give a known bounded state envelope; a
zero-command wait O(log(R0/xi)) makes all actuator residues O(xi), with
remaining output L1 norm O(xi). This is a paid wait, not a state sensor.

2. One complete finite pointer instrument

Use a private qubit M and a finite pointer D with active states 0,1 and
absorbing recorded output states out0,out1. Initially M=D=0 only as a proof
input; Section 4 constructs finite preparations. The allowed sample port is

 K_R=|c,1_M><b,0_M|+h.c.,
 H_R(t)=u_R(t)K_R,

where u_R is the UNKNOWN output of (1) driven by a chosen nonnegative,
smooth, bounded finite command J_R w(t). Add the finite controller-only
exchange
 H_MD(t)=J_C chi_C(t)(|0_M,1_D><1_M,0_D|+h.c.).
All couplings/ramp durations are fixed and finite. Choose known chi_C
nonnegative everywhere and positive on a fixed interior interval. No instantaneous controller gate
or pole-dependent waveform is used.

Within the active space include finite unmonitored amplitude resets and
diagonal dephasing of M,D, and the inherited sample dephasing. No additional
diagonal Hamiltonian detunings are present in this chosen instance. Two
distinct finite monitored ports are

 L_i(t)=sqrt(lambda_d(t))|out_i><i_D|, i=0,1.              (2)

The activation is a finite known profile, positive on a fixed interval.
During a trial, pointer-active resets and H_MD vanish on out0/out1, so
the extracted pointer is absorbing. K_R and the private-meter reset may
continue on the safe sample/M factor after extraction; this is a TP suffix
that cannot change the recorded pointer outcome or recreate an active D.
K_R acts as identity on D: no new sample-to-D conditional port is assumed.
The classical label is stored separately. No-click is retained. A fixed
unknown symmetric flip rate r<1/2 is allowed as a FRESH, conditionally
memoryless channel on EACH extracted label, independent of prior records
and specimen input; no-click is unchanged. A single hidden persistent flip
bit does not satisfy this assumption, despite identical marginal r.
There is NO supplied gap below r=1/2. This concrete meter-noise instance
does not cover arbitrary state-dependent erasure or adversarial faults
that destroy all contrast. Such an observed contrast gate must fail.

Let N=P_b+P_1^M+P_1^D. Both exchanges preserve N. Diagonal dephasing is
covariant under its phase rotations; unmonitored resets lower N. Giving
the two extracted output branches their corresponding lost-excitation
charges makes the whole instrument covariant as well. Its classical
effects on initial M0D0 therefore commute with N, so their b-c
off-diagonals vanish EXACTLY. cM0D0 is a dark zero-excitation state and
cannot generate out1. Furthermore sum L_i^dag L_i=lambda_d(t)I_active;
the no-extraction trace is exp(-Lambda), Lambda=integral lambda_d.
Consequently the FULL three-outcome effects on sample b/c are

 E_out1=s(P)P_b,
 E_out0=(1-e^-Lambda)I-s(P)P_b,
 E_noclick=e^-Lambda I,
 E_y1=alpha I+d P_b,
 alpha=r(1-e^-Lambda), d=(1-2r)s(P).                    (3)

Every reset, lost excitation, out0 and no-click branch is included. The
private reset bath is NOT observed. This is a finite sample-to-controller
instrument, not a direct calibrated sample measurement.

There is an optional exact diagonalization independent of the stronger
charge-covariant meter-noise property: use a fresh fair sign for J_R w and
pool the outcomes. Sample b-sign conjugation changes only H_R's sign;
linear identical filtering preserves it, while controller-only generators
and safe sample dephasing are invariant. Thus E_-=Z_bc E_+ Z_bc, and the
pooled effect is diagonal. The random sign and all outcomes are paid.
This does not itself prove contrast for arbitrary meter faults; the
positive-contrast proof immediately below uses the explicit instance above.

3. Actual uniform quantum transfer contrast, separate from label margin

The filtered unit response bar u_P=h_P*w is uniformly positive on a fixed
interval I_R, by positivity/continuity of h_P and compactness of the pole
box. Choose later intervals I_S,I_D of positive length, with
sup I_R<inf I_S<sup I_S<inf I_D. Choose chi_C>=chi_*>0 on I_S and
lambda_d>=lambda_*>0 on I_D. All lie in a fixed finite readout duration T_D.

In the no-unmonitored-jump/no-prior-extraction component, the only exchange
chain from input b is
 B=bM0D0 -> M=cM1D0 -> D=cM0D1.
All no-jump damping is real nonnegative and bounded by a supplied d_max.
The second-order D amplitude is -J_R J_C times a positive ordered integral.
Restrict its pump time to I_R and its copy time to I_S. For t in I_D this
gives

 |A_D^(2)(t)|>=c0 J_R J_C,
 c0=u_* chi_* |I_R||I_S| exp(-d_max T_D)>0.               (4)

Every longer exchange walk from B to D contains both links and at least
two extra links. The fixed-time Dyson series therefore has the uniform
analytic remainder
 |A_D-A_D^(2)|<=C_D J_R J_C(J_R^2+J_C^2).
For example its coefficients are bounded by the ordered-simplex volumes,
the fixed uniform bar u_P/chi_C bounds and their convergent exponential
series; C_D is finite and computable from the supplied bounds. Choose
fixed small positive J_R,J_C so C_D(J_R^2+J_C^2)<=c0/2, also fitting
their actual command/control amplitude and slew limits. Then

 s(P)>=|I_D|lambda_* (c0 J_R J_C/2)^2=:s_min>0.           (5)

This is a positive contribution to the actual unconditional out1
probability; it is not a postselected experimental procedure. Reset/
dephasing/extraction damping is already in the amplitude. At a fixed
r<1/2, d=(1-2r)s(P)>0. There is no uniform positive d over all r<1/2;
Section 5 acquires an observed lower bound and pays its cost.

4. Finite safe preparation, mixing and a common detector channel

Intertrial reset acts on the FULL pointer basis, including out0/out1:
 L_prep,k=sqrt(lambda_prep)|0_D><k_D| for every k.
Its total reset intensity is lambda_prep I_D; the first reset event
replaces the pointer by 0 and erases old entanglement, without observing
that event. Controller exchanges/extraction are off during this finite
preparation. A wait O(log(1/xi)) gives a diamond replacement error O(xi).
Reset M similarly. Classical old records are retained in separate storage,
not erased by pointer reset. The finite on/off/ramp schedules are paid.

To prepare cM0, command a positive DC private-pump plateau. Its output
approaches the known unit-gain value J_R uniformly exponentially after
the filter transient. For constant J_R, the finite safe Lindbladian has
Lyapunov N_S=P_b+P_1^M with L^*N_S=-lambda_M P_1^M. A zero-derivative
invariant trajectory is supported on M0; the pump then excludes bM0.
Only cM0 remains. This proves strict finite-dimensional convergence to
the unique dark state. Compact positive reset/dephasing/control bounds
give uniform constants C,gap>0, including on arbitrary old references.

After a log-sized filter settling prefix, compare the remaining actual
generator to constant J_R by CP Duhamel; its integrated coefficient
difference is exponentially small. A further O(log(1/xi)) plateau yields
the channel cM0 tensor Tr_SM with diamond error O(xi). Turning off the
command and retaining its full safe tail cannot increase this error,
because cM0 is dark for every pump value. A further log-sized wait makes
the actuator residue O(xi). Neither reset events nor stationarity are
assumed observed. Starting in the safe sector is load-bearing.

For a second reference turn the private pump off, settle its tail, and
apply a fixed positive DC command to the DIRECT b-c port. With a=kappa/2,
the Bloch equations are
 x'=-ax, y'=-ay-2u z, z'=2u y.
For constant u=J_mix>0, the y/z characteristic polynomial is
lambda^2+a lambda+4J_mix^2. Positive compact a,J_mix give a uniform gap,
and I_bc/2 is the unique stationary state. The same filter-transient plus
finite-gap comparison gives diamond mixing to I/2 tensor Tr_S in
O(log(1/xi)) time. The complete command turnoff/tail is unital, so it
fixes I/2 and contracts the error. Meter/pointer preparation and actuator
settling run with all unwanted couplings off. All operations remain safe.

The residual actuator contribution to the NEXT complete detector has
L1 norm O(xi); finite-parameter Hamiltonian Duhamel changes its full
record probabilities by O(xi). Thus the same ideal zero-residue detector
(3) is approached after both reference preparations and after any probe.
All finite prep, turnoff, detector and settling errors are absolute and
uniform over the old conditional joint input. They can be chosen to make
each next binary conditional mean differ from its fixed reference
probability by at most a specified b_xi. No IID or survivor replacement
is asserted. All waits, resets and finite meters are paid.

5. Acquiring detector contrast with finite confidence

For the actual fixed ideal detector define q_c=alpha,
q_mix=alpha+d/2. For any sample state, including a coherent b/c state,
q=alpha+d p_b, so p_b=(q-q_c)/[2(q_mix-q_c)].
The exact diagonality in Section 2 is essential to this calibration.

If binary trials have |E[Y_i|past]-q|<=b_xi uniformly, conditional
Hoeffding--Azuma gives
 Pr(|qhat-q|>b_xi+t)<=2exp(-2nt^2).                        (6)
Preparation resets the required finite registers with the uniform
channel bounds above; no n-trial IID coupling is needed. If only a
nonuniform marginal prep promise were supplied, (6) would not follow.

For contrast acquisition use n_k=2^k reference trials of EACH type in
batch k. Allocate alpha_k=alpha_c/[4 zeta(2)(k+1)^2] to each reference
probability at each possible k, so both references over ALL potential
checkpoints consume alpha_c/2. Choose b_xi,k<=t_k/4 and
t_k=sqrt(log(2/alpha_k)/(2n_k)). The two intervals qhat +/- (t_k+b_xi,k)
give a lower confidence bound d_L,k on 2(q_mix-q_c). Stop this stage
only when d_L,k>0. All earlier trials, failed stages and reset time are
charged. For any fixed true d>0 this gate opens almost surely as the
intervals shrink: (6) and summability give eventual coverage almost surely,
while b_xi,k and t_k tend to zero. There is no uniform deterministic budget
as d->0.
On the simultaneous event of probability >=1-alpha_c/2, its declared
d_L is a valid positive lower bound. Subsequent sample counts use d_L,
not an assumed gap in r. An absent contrast leaves the gate unresolved.

After acquiring d_L, fix the fine batch sizes from it and use ENTIRELY
FRESH batches for q_c,q_mix and each probe probability. There is no optional
reuse of earlier contrast samples in these fixed-batch bounds. Conditional
on the contrast-stage record, acquire absolute error e_q with biases
<=e_q/4 and
 n>=2 e_q^-2 log(4K/alpha_c),
where K=m+3 counts two references plus DC and m nonzero probes. This
uses the remaining alpha_c/2 budget. Take e_q<=d_L/8. If dhat is the
fine contrast estimate, |dhat-d|<=4e_q and dhat>=d_L/2. The clipped ratio
 p_hat=(qhat-qhat_c)/dhat
then has absolute error <=12e_q/d_L. Errors of imperfect preparations,
tail settling, finite command approximation and timing must be inside
the conditional bias allowance; they are not ignored systematic errors.

6. Finite weak-drive population spectroscopy, including all filter tails

In a probe, use only the direct safe Hamiltonian u(t)X_bc, starting from
c. The command is epsilon times a known cosine of nonzero fixed omega
with a plateau of length T and fixed-duration smooth on/off ramps. Let
u=epsilon h_P*v_T, where |v_T|<=1 and the ramp support has fixed length.
The exact Bloch equations give the Volterra equation

 z'(t)=-4u(t) integral_0^t e^-a(t-s)u(s)z(s)ds.

Let E=integral u^2<=epsilon^2(T+C_r), using the cascade's unit L1 gain.
Young's inequality for the one-sided kernel of L1 norm 1/a gives
sup|z-1|<=4E/a. Integrating z' and putting p_b=(1-z)/2 yields

 p_b(infinity)=Q_P[u]+R,
 Q_P[u]=integral integral u(t)e^-a|t-s|u(s)dt ds,
 |R|<=8E^2/a^2.                                         (7)

This is a finite-exposure expansion, not the invalid bound epsilon*T<<1.
Every causal filter tail is included in Q and E. On a finite experiment,
stop the command and wait until the remaining output L1 tail is <=xi;
ignoring its future changes any complete detector probability by <=2xi.
After truncating that remaining tail, dephasing alone does not change p_b.
The finite wait is O(log(1/xi)) with the supplied state envelope.

For an explicit uniform long-plateau estimate set
 K_P=h_P^rev * e^-a|.| * h_P.
It has L1 norm 2/a and uniformly bounded first absolute moment, at most
(2/a)(2m tau_max+1/a). With a rectangular cosine window as a mathematical
comparison, product-to-sum gives

 Q_P[epsilon h_P*v_T]/(epsilon^2T)
 =a/(a^2+omega^2)|P(i omega)|^-2+O(1/T).                  (8)

The error is bounded using that first moment and 1/omega; replacing the
rectangle by the actual fixed smooth ramps adds only O(1/T), because
their L1 difference has fixed support and K_P has bounded L1 norm.
Thus (8) holds for the actual bounded-command/slew probe, not a switched
ideal field. All constants are uniform over the compact pole/a class.

At literal DC the window has mean-square ONE rather than the cosine's
one-half. Its coefficient is 2/a, independent of P since P(0)=1. It is
not the omega->0 limit after T->infinity. For s=epsilon^2T sufficiently
small, (7)--(8) therefore give

 p_b/s=c_j+O(s+1/T),
 c_DC=2/a,
 c_j=a/(a^2+omega_j^2)|P(i omega_j)|^-2, j=1,...,m.       (9)

Choose s=O(eta), T=O(eta^-1), epsilon=O(eta) with explicit constants from
(7)--(8), so the deterministic approximation error is <=eta/4. Choose
e_q=O(d_L s eta), with a sufficiently small fixed factor, so Section 5's
population error divided by s is also <=eta/4. The estimator c_hat_j is
then within O(eta) with the allocated source confidence. These are actual
finite command/probe/pointer observations. No waveform output trace is
assumed measured.

7. Direct polynomial coefficients; no separated pole estimates

Use the DC value to estimate a=2/c_DC if it was not already supplied.
All c_j have known positive lower bounds from the compact class and the
fixed distinct frequencies. On that range inversion is uniformly smooth,
so (9) estimates
 Q(omega_j^2)=|P(i omega_j)|^2
to O(eta). The real polynomial Q(x)=1+q_1x+...+q_mx^m is obtained from m
fixed distinct positive omega_j^2 by its known invertible Vandermonde
matrix. Its finite inverse conditioning is charged, not assumed one.

The stable factor P is identifiable and has a GLOBAL coefficient inverse
bound even at repeated poles. Let two candidate P0,P1 have constant term
one and write P1-P0=zR(z), deg R<m. Then exactly

 [P1(z)P1(-z)-P0(z)P0(-z)]/z
       =R(z)P1(-z)-P0(z)R(-z).                          (10)

The Sylvester map of P0(z) and P1(-z) is invertible: their roots lie on
opposite real half-lines. Its resultant magnitude is
 product_(j,k)(tau_0j+tau_1k)>=(2tau_min)^(m^2).
Coefficient norms are bounded on the compact class, so adjugate bounds
give a finite computable uniform inverse norm C_S. Applying it to
(R,-R(-z)) proves
 ||p(P1)-p(P0)||<=C_S ||q(P1)-q(P0)||.                   (11)
Changing from z to i sqrt(x) only changes the signs of even coefficients.
No separation between poles WITHIN either polynomial is needed.

Choose eta small enough, using the fixed Vandermonde, reciprocal and
Sylvester constants, that the resulting compatible P coefficient set has
diameter <=eta_P. This can be implemented directly in coefficient space:
intersect the measured Q constraints with the supplied compact semialgebraic
class P=product(1+tau_j z), tau_j in the given interval, and bound each
coefficient by validated polynomial feasibility/optimization. True P is
feasible on the confidence event. No individual roots are estimated or
given an artificial gap. A finite grid/quantifier-elimination implementation
and the required numerical accuracy have actual computation/storage
costs; no efficient or optimal fitting claim is made. The inverse command
uses the resulting polynomial coefficients directly.

9. Structural identification boundary and strongest conventional control

Safe trials identify P_bc only within the supplied class. Two plants that
agree on EVERY safe b-c/meter command but differ on an unexcited a/c or
b/e quadrature produce identical safe observation laws. No amount of this
safe experience can distinguish them. The common response across all
quadratures must therefore remain prior structural knowledge; it is not
validated by a fit at the single safe port. Stationarity and the stipulated
meter-noise model also remain class promises, not learned from finite data.

Measured-response predistortion is established in Gustavsson et al.,
PRL110,040502 (2013), https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.2175 , and Rol et al.,
APL116,054001 (2020), https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04818 . They are competent
equally informed acquisition/control baselines. The present acquired
object is the polynomial coefficient confidence set through this literal
safe finite instrument, not a new inverse-filter principle. Established
system identification or better experiment design may reduce these
conservative costs. Ordinary execution by a competent control does not
erase the supplied-versus-acquired distinction, and gives no architectural
exclusivity. No actual hardware response or physical source counts were
acquired in this mathematical derivation. Strict hard spectral bandwidth,
fixed precision floors, and generic depolarizing-noise sensing remain
outside the positive statement.
