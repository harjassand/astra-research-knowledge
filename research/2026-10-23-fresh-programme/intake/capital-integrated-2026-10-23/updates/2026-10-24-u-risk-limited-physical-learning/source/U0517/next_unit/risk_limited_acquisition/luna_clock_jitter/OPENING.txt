RISK-LIMITED ACQUISITION: INDEPENDENT SOURCE/CONTROL QUESTION
24 October 2026. Frozen before reading other worker material or source literature.

The response-versus-hazard exponent boundary is exact only inside its declared
statistical contract. The physical question is whether a real acquisition
channel has independently calibrated low-amplitude laws for (i) information
in live readouts, including noise floor and bandwidth, and (ii) irreversible
damage/death, including whether hazard is controlled by amplitude, elapsed
time, integrated dose, or latent cumulative state. A fitted power law over a
finite operating range cannot by itself justify extrapolating weak probes to
arbitrarily long duration.

My leading comparison is dose-fractionated imaging and sequential design:
many low-dose observations can reduce measurement noise, but for shot-noise
limited measurements signal and variance often scale together with accumulated
dose, while damage may depend on that same total dose. That can erase the
duration-only advantage of weaker exposures. Conversely, a fixed readout-noise
floor plus amplitude-squared hazard may support the mathematical weak-probe
tradeoff only if the apparatus can sustain the longer integration and the
damage law is actually validated there.

Bounded priority/control challenge: determine whether a primary experiment
measures one physical system in a regime where both the live-readout law and
irreversible hazard law are calibrated across probe amplitude and duration,
and whether it validates a fixed Gaussian noise floor with separable
per-exposure survival `exp(-kappa*a^s)`. If not, retain the exponent phase
boundary as a conditional statistical theorem and compare it with standard
optimal-design/sequential-likelihood controls, without inventing an exponent
or an application.
