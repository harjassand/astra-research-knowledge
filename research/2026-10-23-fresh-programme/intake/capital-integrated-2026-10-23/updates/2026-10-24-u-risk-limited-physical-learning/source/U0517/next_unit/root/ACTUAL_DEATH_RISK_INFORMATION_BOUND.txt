ACTUAL DEATH RISK, OBSERVED INFORMATION, AND DURATION
24 October 2026. Root derivation; Sol independent challenge requested.

Use the risk_limited_acquisition model exactly: an attempted exposure at
predictable amplitude a in (0,1] first survives with p(a)=exp(-kappa*a^s),
then, only if alive, supplies Y~N(theta*a^r,sigma^2). Death is absorbing;
there is at most ONE fatal exposure. The live/death kernel is parameter
independent given the same history and chosen action. Policies may adapt
and stop; all expectations below use the SAME reference law P_theta.

Let J_t indicate an alive attempt, B_t its survival, C=sum_t J_t B_t the
number of readouts, M=sum_t J_t attempts, and
E_obs=sum_t J_t B_t a_t^(2r). Let rho_theta=P_theta(ever dies).

The death indicators are disjoint, so predictability gives

 rho_theta = E_theta sum_t J_t(1-B_t)
           = E_theta sum_t J_t[1-p(a_t)].

For h=kappa*a^s, e^h>=1+h implies h*e^(-h)<=1-e^(-h).
Consequently

 E_theta sum_t J_t B_t a_t^s
   = E_theta sum_t J_t p(a_t)a_t^s
   <=rho_theta/kappa.                                    (R1)

This bounds OBSERVED exposure by actual event probability. It does not
identify attempted additive hazard with event probability, and it does
not need a pathwise or expected additive-hazard cap.

For s<=2r, a^(2r)<=a^s, so

                 E_theta E_obs <=rho_theta/kappa.        (R2)

For s>2r put q=2r/s in (0,1). Apply Holder to the probability-times-step
measure with density J_t B_t. Its total mass is E_theta C. Then

 E_theta E_obs
   <=(E_theta C)^(1-q)
                 (E_theta sum_t J_t B_t a_t^s)^q
   <=(E_theta C)^(1-q)(rho_theta/kappa)^q,
 E_theta E_obs<=E_theta C<=E_theta M.                     (R3)

Zero mass or zero risk is interpreted by the nonnegative limiting case.
Finite expected readout/attempt budgets suffice. For unbounded stopping,
truncate, apply the nonnegative inequalities, and pass by monotone
convergence; no survival conditioning is taken.

The lead's independently derived exact killed-law identity is

 D(P_theta||P_theta')
   =(theta-theta')^2 E_theta E_obs/(2 sigma^2).

Therefore (R2)-(R3) give direct actual-risk information bounds. If
E_theta C<=N and rho_theta<=rho, substitute N,rho on the right.
The same applies if only E_theta M<=N is charged. The reference law for
the probability and time budget must be the KL reference law.

For uniform unconditional completion-and-epsilon-accuracy at least
1-gamma, gamma<1/2, two parameters separated by d>2epsilon give
D>=k_gamma=(1-2gamma)ln[(1-gamma)/gamma]. Taking d down to2epsilon
under the appropriate fixed/uniform reference budgets gives
Q=sigma^2*k_gamma/(2epsilon^2), hence

 s<=2r: rho/kappa>=Q;
 s>2r: N>=Q and
        N>=(kappa/rho)^(2r/(s-2r))*Q^(s/(s-2r)).           (R4)

To keep the reference fixed in the limit, one may take theta0=-epsilon
and theta1=epsilon+delta, delta down to0; both remain in [-1,1] for
small positive delta when epsilon<1. The procedure's accuracy promise
is uniform, while resource budgets are needed at the chosen theta0.

At fixed amplitude a and committed horizon n, write h=kappa*a^s and
p=e^(-h). Direct calculation gives

 rho_n=1-p^n,
 E C = p(1-p^n)/(1-p),
 kappa E sum_t J_t B_t a^s =rho_n*h/(e^h-1).

Thus the conversion (R1) approaches equality as a goes to0. Holder in
(R3) is equality for constant amplitudes. The earlier fixed-horizon
Gaussian estimator supplies a constructive high-completion guarantee;
this note does not infer unbiased conditional estimation for adaptive
survivor-selected samples.
