## Decision

The proposed exponent boundary is a valid consequence of its stated Gaussian-plus-hazard contract, but at the level of exponents it is ordinary information-per-cost design. The potential theorem contribution is the exact stopped, adaptive converse with the stated reference-world/pathwise accounting—not the observation that one power law can dominate another.

For the model `Y|a ~ N(θa^r, σ²)`, live-readout Fisher information per exposure is `a^(2r)/σ²`, and the pairwise Gaussian KL for `θ,θ'` is `(θ−θ')² a^(2r)/(2σ²)`. Under the stipulated hazard `κa^s`, the information-to-hazard ratio is

`a^(2r)/(σ² κa^s) = a^(2r−s)/(κσ²)`.

It diverges toward zero amplitude exactly when `s>2r`. With a fixed number `N` of attempts and hazard cap `H≤h`, maximizing `Σa_i^(2r)` subject to `κΣa_i^s≤h` gives the stated Hölder frontier when `s>2r`; equal amplitudes attain equality in that regime. For `s≤2r` and `a≤1`, `a^(2r)≤a^s`, so `Σa_i^(2r)≤H/κ`: a fixed hazard cap cannot yield unbounded precision under this model. Imposing a minimum amplitude, nonzero per-readout/control overhead, a duration-dependent failure cost, or cumulative fatigue can remove the weak-probe limit. This is the conventional constrained-design interpretation of the boundary.

Chernoff’s primary work on sequential design is the closest classical control for the adaptive choice/stop structure: choose the next experiment using accumulated data and stop when the terminal inferential decision is adequate. The modern strong safe-exploration control is more specific: Turchetta, Berkenkamp, and Krause maintain confidence bounds on an unknown safety feature and only sample states/actions certified safe and safely reachable/returnable. Their formal guarantee depends on a safe seed, known transition function, RKHS/Gaussian-process regularity and high-probability confidence bands. It does not infer an arbitrary destructive hazard law from a record of survival. A competent baseline for the lead model is therefore a sequential likelihood/design policy over `θ` and any unknown hazard parameters, with an explicit resource state and a time-uniform confidence set or predeclared stopping rule; the hazard action is charged on every attempted exposure, and a failed exposure ends the run.

## What the physical imaging sources establish

The strongest direct interface audit in the small source set is the primary cryo-EM study by Karuppasamy et al. They exposed *Lumbricus terrestris* extracellular hemoglobin and compared dose-rate series: 1, 5, and 50 electrons Å⁻² s⁻¹, with 50–250 image sequences and equal integrated-flux comparisons. The control sample was 0.5–1 mg ml⁻¹ hemoglobin in 50 mM ammonium acetate (measured pH 6.5); they also tested 2 M ammonium acetate, 50% glycerol, and 0.2% glutaraldehyde. Acquisition used a 120 kV FEI Tecnai Biotwin and a 4k CCD. At matched integrated flux, the high-flux series had blur and earlier gas-bubble appearance; the authors report a dose-rate effect favoring lower flux. Their lowest-flux series also used many more frames, accumulated alignment error, and lost detail; ten-second acquisition imposed a stringent sample-drift requirement. Thus weakening flux can trade damage for elapsed-time and processing costs, but the measured response is specimen-, detector-, and protocol-specific.

The same paper says incident flux alone is a poor damage metric because interaction probability also depends on integration time, sample, and electron energy. It treats damage as beginning with the first exposure and varying with accumulated dose, and it discusses dose-rate effects, radiolysis, heating and composition. The study measured camera readout noise and dark current and found them relatively small even in its low-flux series. This does not establish a fixed Gaussian observation-noise floor: the useful image signal is extracted from electron-count data, and the primary tomography literature explicitly treats signal-dependent noise. It does show why “one Gaussian readout per unit exposure” must be calibrated for the actual detector/preprocessing pipeline rather than assumed from a generic analogy.

Hegerl and Hoppe’s original dose-fractionation result is also a close incumbent: for the same resolution and statistical significance, their electron-noise calculation gives a 3D reconstruction the same total integral dose as one 2D image of an isolated voxel, with the total divided among the projections. McEwen, Downing, and Glaeser later tested this theorem by simulation and extended it to high absorption, signal-dependent noise, varying contrast, and missing angular range. Its scope is tomographic reconstruction, not arbitrary repeated measurement of a scalar parameter. Still, it is a decisive counterexample to treating “many weaker exposures” as automatically more information per unit irreversible damage: under those imaging assumptions, fractionating the allowed dose does not reduce the total dose required for the target significance.

These sources neither validate nor universally refute the proposed `a^r`, fixed-`σ`, independent `exp(−κa^s)` family. They measure finite dose/flux ranges in specific materials, use image/Fourier statistics rather than one scalar readout, and show dose-rate and accumulated-damage effects. I found no primary experiment in this bounded audit that jointly fits a fixed Gaussian readout variance and an independent per-exposure power-law survival probability across arbitrarily weak amplitudes and increasing duration. That empirical interface remains an explicit premise, not a result supplied by dose-fractionation or low-dose imaging.

## Survival data and zero-failure control

Classical exact binomial inference already blocks “we survived, therefore this is safe.” For `n` independent identical fixed-amplitude exposures with zero failures, the one-sided `(1−δ)` Clopper–Pearson upper bound on failure probability is

`q ≤ 1 − δ^(1/n)`.

At 95% confidence and `n=100`, that is about 2.95% per exposure, not zero. This calculation assumes iid trials at the same condition. It cannot be pooled across adaptively selected amplitudes or extrapolated to an untested amplitude without the stated hazard model or additional regularity. Under a known model and fixed actions, zero deaths constrain cumulative hazard; they do not validate unknown `κ,s` or an arbitrary alternative hazard curve. If the empirical output is survival-only and unsafe/safe worlds differ only in hazard, the lead’s coupling lower bound is the right kind of accounting: a policy that obtains a strong permission decision only after surviving an unsafe discriminator necessarily pays the corresponding unsafe death probability.

The safe-MDP paper is a useful algorithmic control but also exposes the assumptions that make guarantees possible. It requires confidence intervals valid uniformly over all steps, a regular safety function, and an initially safe, connected/returnable set; action-dependent safety can be encoded by introducing action states. In the scalar acquisition model, the analogous certificate would need an independently justified parameter family or a validated envelope over amplitudes. A few successful shots alone cannot provide one for a completely arbitrary hazard function.

## Scope and stop point

The strongest conventional reading is: optimize a sequential design for information gain per unit of declared risk and time, use a confidence sequence or fixed sample size for the live signal, and maintain a separate conservative hazard bound. Under the lead’s exact parametric model, the power comparison and constant-amplitude frontier can be proved directly; a fully adaptive stopped-law KL bound is a compact extension of likelihood-chain-rule and Hölder arguments. Neither one supplies a physical power exponent.

The physical challenge is therefore sharply bounded: identify a specified instrument/sample pair, define what `a` controls, measure readout distribution and variance versus `a`, measure damage/failure versus both `a` and integration duration, and include drift, detector/readout overhead, and cumulative sample-state changes. Without that calibration, retain the theorem as conditional and do not transfer the exponent frontier to a device or claim an experimental weak-probe advantage. No broader source hunt or experiment is warranted here.
