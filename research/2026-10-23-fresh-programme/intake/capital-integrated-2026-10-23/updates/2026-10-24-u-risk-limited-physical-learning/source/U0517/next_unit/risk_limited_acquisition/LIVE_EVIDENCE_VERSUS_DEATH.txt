LIVE EVIDENCE VERSUS DESTRUCTIVE VALIDATION
24 October 2026. Lead proof; not a physical or novelty claim.

1. General permission experiment

Consider two possible worlds i=0,1 and a common sequential controller. At a
live history it chooses an action or stops to issue/refuse a permission. Action
selection and stopping may depend on all previous live outputs, successful
exposures and private randomness. One action either kills the system, ending
the source irreversibly, or produces its live output. The two worlds can have
different conditional hazards and different live-output kernels, possibly
depending on history. The controller cannot use the unknown world index.

A_i is the event that permission is issued alive under the actual killed law
P_i. D_i is source death before voluntary termination. The inferential demand
is P_0(A_0)>=1-beta in the permissible world, P_1(A_1)<=alpha in the impermissible
world. Refusing permission forever does not satisfy progress in world0.

Define Q_i as an ANALYTICAL immortal live-history law: always take the live
branch, use the specified conditional live-output kernel, and run the same
controller on that history of successes. Q_i is not a free observable simulator
given to the learner. Let A* be its permission event (a measurable event on
these live histories and the common private randomness).

Couple P_i to Q_i by the same live outputs/private randomness up to the first
death. If P_i reaches a permission alive, the corresponding Q_i also permits;
if Q_i would permit but P_i does not, P_i must have died before that time.
Consequently

        P_i(A_i) <= Q_i(A*) <= P_i(A_i)+P_i(D_i).              (1)

The coupling does not assume the world's hazard is constant, known, or ordered
relative to the other world's hazard. It uses the full conditional live kernel
specified by the model, not the survivor-selected law of an entire completed
experiment. Infinite immortal trajectories cause no issue: A* means permission
at some finite time. A controller that never outputs on them simply has A*=false.

2. Survival-only permission cannot be both powerful and uniformly nondestructive

If the two worlds have IDENTICAL conditional live-output kernels, then Q_0=Q_1.
Equation (1) yields

        P_1(D_1) >= [1-beta-alpha]_+.                        (2)

Proof: 1-beta<=P_0(A_0)<=Q_0(A*)=Q_1(A*)
                   <=P_1(A_1)+P_1(D_1)<=alpha+P_1(D_1).

No monotone ordering of the two hazards is needed: both are coupled to their
common immortal process, rather than demanding that unsafe survivors be a
subset of safe survivors at EVERY action. The earlier worker derivation imposed
monotonicity as a sufficient nested-coupling condition; (1) removes that extra
condition. If permission may be supplied after death, or if death is repairable,
this interface has changed and (2) is not the same claim.

Survival may genuinely update a posterior or support a zero-failure confidence
bound. Equation (2) does not deny that evidence. It says that an algorithm that
reliably obtains a strong safety license from such evidence must sacrifice the
impermissible world with corresponding probability. Discarding those source
failures and reporting survivor-only calibration hides that cost.

3. Informative live outputs supply the alternative route

For different Q_0,Q_1, total variation and (1) give

  1-beta <= Q_0(A*) <= Q_1(A*)+TV(Q_0,Q_1)
          <= alpha+P_1(D_1)+TV(Q_0,Q_1).

Thus low-risk licensing with P_1(D_1)<=delta requires

        TV(Q_0,Q_1) >= [1-beta-alpha-delta]_+.                 (3)

If alpha+delta<1-beta, applying data processing to the permission bit of the
IMMORTAL laws gives the sharper information requirement

 KL(Q_0 || Q_1) >= kl(1-beta || alpha+delta).                  (4)

The probabilities are ordered as needed: Q_0(A*)>=1-beta and Q_1(A*)<=alpha+delta,
so Bernoulli KL is minimized at these two boundaries. Equation (4) uses costs
under Q_0 when bounding that KL, not costs under an arbitrary actual killed law.
If Q_0 coincides with a no-death reference world, its cost is observable source
cost in that reference. Otherwise an explicit bound on the immortal reference
cost is an additional assumption; do not silently substitute killed costs.

This makes the acquisition criterion operational: reliable low-risk permission
needs enough nondestructive live discrimination, a weaker permission demand,
regeneration/replicas, or acceptance of the destructive source cost. It is not
a demand for assumption-free inference. Known physical laws and calibrated
model classes may legitimately link a live precursor to the later risk.

4. Relation to full killed-law likelihood

For a one-step model with survival probabilities q_i(a) and live kernels G_i,
the full observation kernel has exact KL

 kl(q_0(a)||q_1(a)) + q_0(a)*KL(G_0(.|a)||G_1(.|a)).           (5)

Adaptive full-transcript KL sums (5) under the same reference law. The first
term is hazard/death evidence; the second is actual live-readout evidence.
In the Gaussian source model of STOPPED_INFORMATION_FRONTIER.txt, hazards are
theta-independent given the action, so the first term is zero. That model's
positive weak-probe result is deliberately supplied by the live channel, not
by interpreting survival alone as free proof that the apparatus is safe.

5. Reusable calibration gate: completion probability must stay visible

Let C denote source completion alive and V={theta in I} the correctness of its
returned interval. A later controller may choose experiments from I and all
later observations. Suppose a separately justified risk envelope bounds the
total hazard by b on EVERY compatible no-death future path whenever V holds.
Then future survival conditional on any such source data is at least exp(-b),
so

      P(C and V and future survival) >= exp(-b)*P(C and V).    (6)

For fixed-a/fixed-N Gaussian calibration,
P(C and V)>=exp(-H_design)*(1-alpha), giving a total guarantee
exp(-(H_design+b))*(1-alpha). Conditional CI coverage is valid here because
completion is independent of that fixed Gaussian vector.

For a generic sequential confidence procedure with ONLY an unconditional bound
P(C and not V)<=alpha and completion probability P(C)>=c, one may infer only
P(C and V)>=c-alpha. The corresponding right side of (6) is (c-alpha)*exp(-b),
not c*(1-alpha)*exp(-b). Likewise a hazard cap alone does not guarantee C if
the immortal controller can refuse, fail to return an estimate, or never stop.
An a.s.-terminating no-death source with an output is needed to turn a hazard
bound into a source-completion lower bound.

A single valid interval can support many adaptively selected controls without
a new multiplicity factor when the risk functional is uniformly bounded over
that interval and the pathwise budget holds. Repeatedly replacing the interval
needs a confidence sequence or simultaneous coverage; estimating the envelope
needs its own uncertainty treatment. The later functional, damage law, actual
amplitude, detector model and future-task interface remain substantive premises.
