INDEPENDENT AUDIT: THE POINTWISE NEWTON INVERSE AT AN EXACT GAUSSIAN
27 October 2026. Checks of the parent's N687 affine calculation.

RESULT. The proposed Hermite coefficients, signs, exponential norm lower bound,
and bounded-C-infinity nonexistence example are correct. This rules out a
bounded arbitrary-residual L2 right inverse for this pointwise Newton equation.
It is NOT a target sampling lower bound. The distinction between arbitrary
residuals and residuals produced by actual admissible channels remains essential.

1. OPERATOR AND CONVENTIONS

Let G,Z be independent standard normals, e_n=He_n/sqrt(n!) the normalized
probabilists' Hermite basis, delta_Z=Z-partial_Z, and N_Z=delta_Z partial_Z.
Take 0<|lambda|<1 and a=sqrt(1-lambda^2)>0:
 X=lambda G+a Z, K=lambda, J_psi=a.
This is an exact standard Gaussian channel.

For a centered correction u=delta_Z J, the source's pointwise Newton linear
term is
 L_X(u,J)=lambda partial_G u+a partial_Z u+a J.
Because partial_G commutes with delta_Z and partial_Z delta_Z=N_Z+1,
 L_X(u,J)=[a(N_Z+2)+lambda delta_Z partial_G]J = A J.        (1)
Thus the equation for prescribed residual R is A J=-R. There is no missing
a or lambda factor, and the diagonal shift is +2.

2. EXACT CHAOS-BLOCK SOLUTION

Both N_Z and delta_Z partial_G preserve total chaos degree n. For R=e_n(G),
write
 J=sum_(j=0)^n J_j e_(n-j)(G)e_j(Z).
The recurrence is
 2a J_0=-1,
 a(j+2)J_j+lambda sqrt((n-j+1)j)J_(j-1)=0, j>=1.         (2)
Solving gives exactly
 J_j=(-1)^(j+1) a^(-1)(lambda/a)^j
             sqrt(binomial(n,j))/[(j+1)(j+2)].           (3)

For each n the block is triangular with nonzero diagonal a(j+2), hence
invertible. Formula (3) is the unique coefficient vector in that block.

3. NO BOUNDED L2 RIGHT INVERSE; STRONGER J-ONLY LOWER BOUND

The exact norm is
 ||J||_2^2=a^(-2) sum_(j=0)^n binomial(n,j)(lambda^2/a^2)^j
                                      /[(j+1)^2(j+2)^2].
Consequently
 ||J||_2 >= a^(-n-1)/[(n+1)(n+2)].                       (4)
This follows by replacing the denominator by (n+1)^2(n+2)^2 and using
1+lambda^2/a^2=a^(-2). Thus the inverse is unbounded already as an L2-to-L2
map; one need not demand a correction derivative to establish failure.

Since partial_Z u=(N_Z+1)J,
 ||partial_Z u||_2^2=a^(-2) sum_(j=0)^n
          binomial(n,j)(lambda^2/a^2)^j/(j+2)^2,
and hence
 ||partial_Z u||_2 >= a^(-n-1)/(n+2).                    (5)
The claimed derivative lower bound is exactly correct.

4. UNIQUENESS EVEN FOR WEAK L2 SOLUTIONS

Define a weak L2 solution by
 <J,A* phi>=-<R,phi> for every polynomial phi,
where
 A*=a(N_Z+2)+lambda delta_G partial_Z.
The adjoint also preserves each finite total-chaos subspace. Testing against
that subspace determines the projection Pi_n J by the invertible finite
matrix A_n. Therefore every weak L2 solution has exactly the coefficients
given by blockwise inversion. Orthogonality makes
 ||J||_2^2=sum_n ||Pi_n J||_2^2 necessary. If the forced sequence is not square
summable, there is NO weak L2 solution, not merely a failure of one preferred
formula. A homogeneous weak L2 solution has all projections zero and is zero.
A pointwise/Sobolev solution strong enough for Gaussian integration by parts
would in particular be such a weak solution.

5. BOUNDED SMOOTH RIGHT-HAND SIDE WITH NO L2 SOLUTION

For any
 0<beta<lambda^2/(2a^2)
let
 R_beta(G)=sum_(m=1)^infinity exp(-beta m^2) cos(mG).       (6)
The series and every real derivative converge uniformly, since
sum_m m^k exp(-beta m^2)<infinity for each finite k. Thus R_beta is bounded
C-infinity (indeed it extends to an entire periodic function).

Its normalized Hermite coefficients are
 r_n=0 for odd n,
 r_n=(-1)^(n/2)/sqrt(n!) sum_(m=1)^infinity
                   m^n exp[-(beta+1/2)m^2], for even n. (7)
This follows from E[exp(imG)e_n(G)]=(im)^n exp(-m^2/2)/sqrt(n!).
For a fixed even n all m terms have the SAME sign. In particular, for every
chosen m,
 |r_n| >= m^n exp[-(beta+1/2)m^2]/sqrt(n!).               (8)

If a weak L2 solution existed, (4) and orthogonality would require
 ||J||_2^2 >= a^(-2) exp[-(2beta+1)m^2]
        sum_(n even) (m^2/a^2)^n/[n!(n+1)^2(n+2)^2]       (9)
for EVERY integer m.

An elementary lower bound avoids asymptotic Hermite estimates. Put x=m^2/a^2.
For N distributed Poisson(x), P(N even)=(1+exp(-2x))/2>=1/2.
Chebyshev gives P(N>2x)<=1/x. Hence, for x>=4,
 P(N even and N<=2x)>=1/4.
On N<=2x, (N+1)^2(N+2)^2<=(2x+2)^4. It follows that
 sum_(n even) x^n/[n!(n+1)^2(n+2)^2]
      >= exp(x)/[4(2x+2)^4].                             (10)
Combining:
 ||J||_2^2 >= exp[(lambda^2/a^2-2beta)m^2]
                        /[4a^2(2m^2/a^2+2)^4].          (11)
The exponential coefficient is positive by the chosen beta. The right side
diverges as m grows. No weak L2 solution exists.

Subtracting the scalar mean of R_beta only removes n=0 and leaves the
nonexistence result unchanged. Multiplying the residual by any nonzero
constant also leaves nonexistence unchanged. Thus one may make any specified
finite collection of bounded derivative seminorms arbitrarily small while
preserving this failure. This does not assert failure in all stronger
Gaussian analytic or source-compatible spaces.

6. SCOPE AND WHAT A REPAIR WOULD HAVE TO PROVE

At the EXACT affine channel, the ACTUAL Stein residual is
lambda^2+a^2-1=0. Therefore R_beta is a prescribed right-hand side for the
linearized operator; it is not itself shown here to be the Stein residual
of a legal nearby g/psi channel. The theorem decisively defeats a claim of
an arbitrary-residual uniformly bounded pointwise Newton inverse, even in
d=1 and arbitrarily close to the Gaussian output law. It does not by itself
defeat a correction exploiting the special form of actually generated
residuals, a different Stein gauge, weak/output-law projection, or a
value-acquired positive update.

The next nontrivial obligation is to characterize a source-compatible range
on which inversion is controlled, or to replace the pointwise residual by a
law-relevant object and prove that the replacement can be acquired with legal
values and finite arithmetic/query costs. Conditional projection onto X may
remove invisible modes, but its existence as a conditional expectation is
not an acquisition algorithm.

7. ADDENDUM: THE PARENT'S ACTUAL ADMISSIBLE FIRST-ORDER FORCING

The scope caution above can be narrowed. Let
 f_k(x)=sin(kx)/k,
 g_eta,k(x)=lambda x+eta f_k(x),

The scalar Stein residual is exactly
 R_eta=K_eta g_eta,k'+a^2-1
       =eta lambda(f_k'+K_f)+eta^2 K_f f_k'.             (12)
Its actual first-order forcing is R_1=lambda(f_k'+K_f).

If c_n=(f_k')_n=(cos(kG))_n, Hermite differentiation gives
 (f_k)_(n+1)=c_n/sqrt(n+1).
Gaussian divergence gives (K_f)_n=c_n/(n+1). Therefore
 (R_1)_n=lambda[1+1/(n+1)]c_n,                           (13)
with no even-chaos cancellation. All factors and signs are confirmed.

More strongly take f_beta(x)=sum_(m>=1)exp(-beta m^2)sin(mx)/m.
Then f_beta'=R_beta from (6), and the actual first-order forcing has the
coefficients (13) with R_beta in place of cos(kG). Their magnitudes are at
least |lambda| times the coefficients used in (8)-(11). Thus for
0<beta<lambda^2/(2a^2), this ACTUAL first-order forcing has no L2 weak
solution under the affine pointwise Newton operator.

These are genuine globally Lipschitz conservative C-infinity fields:
 |g_eta'(x)|<=|lambda|+|eta|sum_m exp(-beta m^2).
Choose eta small to fit a fixed Lipschitz contract with slack above |lambda|.
A potential is lambda x^2/2 minus
eta sum_m exp(-beta m^2)cos(mx)/m^2. Derivatives converge locally and the
first derivative has the stated global bound.

This defeats differentiable perturbative pointwise correction even on actual
admissible forcings. It is still not a target-sampler or general nonlinear
correction lower bound: at eta!=0 the exact operator is different, and a
successful positive correction need not have an L2 derivative in this Stein
gauge. The projected finite-value repair remains live, provided its residual
can be acquired from permitted values.
