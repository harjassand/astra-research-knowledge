1. Arbitrary initial correlations
The Pauli expansion/twirl is a joint-channel identity on D+A with M and an
external reference carried along. It therefore applies to correlated inputs.
The virtual error probability depends on the A marginal through Tr(F_s rho_A).
It does not require a product input. Exact central effects imply that each
composed error-string memory map has scalar effect, so tracing memory only
at the end gives the product logical channel on the initial logical marginal.

2. K delta approximate centrality
The stated backward hybrid is valid. Replace the last task together with the
final ancillary trace; its error is bounded with every other logical wire as
a reference. Once that task is an independent Pauli map, the preceding
memory-only channel can be erased under the final trace. Repeat backwards.
This yields sum_j delta_j, hence K delta in the uniform case. It does not
require inventing an ideal continuing-memory state after an intermediate
approximate replacement.

Necessary scope clarification: all other logical registers, including future
input wires in the multi-use channel formulation, must remain outside the
intervening memory-only dynamics until their declared task use. The source's
isolated-output and untouched-reference assumptions convey the intended model;
an explicit sentence about future logical wires avoids ambiguity. Fine-record
dependent control does not inherit a fixed product law unless separately
covered by an appropriate channel/control comparison.

3. q fault bounds
F_bad<=qI gives conditional virtual bad probability<=q after every earlier
positive branch. Specified fault-set bounds q^|S| and binomial domination follow.
No iid microscopic model is required. Every deployed actual menu must obey
the same bound, and the private-mixture causal ordering must hold. Future
postselection or control that leaks the arm before A is fixed is outside the
claim. The same-environment-channel half-diamond comparison<=q also follows:
the actual and Pauli-replaced bad CP maps both have norm at most q.

4. Bell witness
For an exactly maximally entangled D-reference input, that pure input is
necessarily initially independent of the apparatus. Explicitly stating this
independence is preferable. Then the intended Bell weight is at least1-q for
every A/M state, so q<1/2 certifies entanglement. The arbitrary-correlations
channel theorem is broader, but it should not be used to describe an arbitrary
already-correlated input as a fresh Bell resource.

logical input independent of the apparatus, discarding/ignoring A gives
