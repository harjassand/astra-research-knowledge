AUTONOMOUS CYCLIC CTMC: EXACT RETURN, FUEL, AND A NORMAL-GENERATOR CEILING
Internal mathematical result and physical-interface audit, 2026-10-23.
No novelty or hardware-access claim. Primary comparison is classical stochastic

1. CONVENTIONS AND FOUR-STATE CONSTRUCTION

Probabilities are columns. A finite CTMC generator Q has Q_ij>=0 for i!=j
and 1^T Q=0. It is balanced when also Q1=0. Thus P(t)=exp(Qt) is doubly
stochastic. Label four consecutive cycle vertices A0,A1,B0,B1 and let C e_i
= e_(i+1 mod 4). For k_+>k_->=0 set

 Q=k_+(C-I)+k_-(C^T-I),  s=k_++k_-, d=k_+-k_->0,
 z=(1,1,-1,-1)^T, w=Cz=(-1,1,1,-1)^T.

Then Cz=w, C^T z=-w, Cw=-z, and

 Qz=-s z+d w, Qw=-d z-s w,
 exp(Qt)z=exp(-st)[cos(dt)z+sin(dt)w].                 (1)

The spectrum is {0,-2s,-s+id,-s-id}. At the prescribed sampling time

 T=pi/d, alpha=exp(-pi*s/d),  P(T)z=-alpha z.          (2)

Uniform binary memory is essential to the stated interface. Its two logical
basis embeddings are v_A=(1+z)/4 and v_B=(1-z)/4. Since P1=1, every logical
population q=(q_A,q_B) obeys

 P(T)[q tensor u_2] = [G_alpha q] tensor u_2,
 G_alpha = (1/2)[[1-alpha,1+alpha],[1+alpha,1-alpha]]. (3)

Consequently the success probability for NOT on either basis input is
(1+alpha)/2, and the same physical schedule works for all population inputs.
The memory marginal is uniform at every intermediate time, but it is generally
correlated with the logical bit: the w term in (1) is a correlation term.
Equation (3) asserts exact product return, not merely a uniform marginal.
It says nothing about quantum coherences.

Here alpha<=exp(-pi)=0.0432139182638, so success<=0.521606959132. The
algebraic maximum uses k_-=0. For k_->0 the inequality is strict.

2. WHAT DRIVES THIS, AND WHAT SETS THE TIME

This generator has a time-independent drive, but (3) is a gate at a chosen
time. A user still needs to prepare the input and read or disengage the device
at T. Keeping Q on gives damped oscillations; at mT the returned logical
contrast is (-alpha)^m. A finite irreducible CTMC has no persistent periodic
ensemble orbit. Thus a stationary circulation is not a reusable phase-stable
clock, and the construction does not internally stop or latch an output.

For k_->0 the stationary distribution is pi_i=1/4. Each oriented cycle edge
carries stationary current j=d/4, the cycle affinity is A_cycle=4 ell with
ell=log(k_+/k_-), and the stationary entropy-production rate is

 sigma_st = 4 j ell = d ell,
 Sigma_st(T)=pi ell.                                (4)

Entropy is dimensionless, with physical entropy k_B times these values. Under
an isothermal local-detailed-balance realization, medium free energy supplied
over T is k_B*T_bath*pi*ell. It is not supplied by the uniform memory.
Each state has total exit rate s, so the expected number of microscopic jumps
over T is sT=J=-log(alpha), and the expected net forward-minus-backward jump
count is dT=pi. This spectral half-period is not exactly two jumps or half
of a deterministic four-jump trajectory.

For a specified alpha<exp(-pi), J>pi and the ring can use

 k_+=(J+pi)/(2T), k_-=(J-pi)/(2T),
 ell=log[(J+pi)/(J-pi)], Sigma_st(T)=pi ell.            (5)

As alpha approaches exp(-pi), ell and the formal stationary entropy production
diverge. The one-way k_-=0 CTMC is mathematically well-defined; a finite-affinity
thermodynamic realization is not licensed by that limit. As k_+ approaches
k_-, alpha approaches zero and T diverges at fixed s, while the NOT advantage
vanishes. For a pure logical input the total entropy production is
pi*ell+h_2((1-alpha)/2), because the final logical population has gained that
Shannon entropy and the memory has returned. For an equiprobable input
ensemble the unconditional state is stationary and (4) is the total entropy.

Finite timing error has an explicit effect. At T+h, write
 beta=alpha*exp(-s h)*cos(dh), chi=alpha*exp(-s h)*sin(dh).
Then P(T+h)z=-beta z-chi w. The logical NOT success becomes (1+beta)/2;
the largest joint TV distance from the actual logical marginal tensor u_2 is
|chi|/2 (attained by a basis input). Hence timing precision, stopping work,
and rate calibration are explicit resources. For two balanced finite-rate
generators, Duhamel gives ||P_Q(T)-P_Q0(T)||_1<=T||Q-Q0||_1, so conditional
output TV changes by at most T||Q-Q0||_1/2. Literal exact return is a model
identity; finite numerical precision provides approximate return.

3. SHARP FOUR-STATE BOUND WITHOUT NORMALITY

Classical sector theorem: every eigenvalue lambda of an n-state CTMC
generator satisfies

 |Im lambda| <= -Re lambda * cot(pi/n).               (6)

For n>=3 this is stated, with the equal-rate directed-cycle equality case,
in J.Th. Runnenburg (1962), CWI Report S299, pp.2-3:
 https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/8592/8592D.pdf
It is an established Markov-embedding constraint, distinct from later
affinity-dependent spectral conjectures. The accompanying independent source
audit also points to Davies (2010), Eq.6.

If exp(QT) has an eigenvalue -alpha, spectral mapping gives a generator
eigenvalue with Re lambda=log(alpha)/T and Im lambda=(2m+1)pi/T. Equation (6)
implies alpha<=exp[-pi*tan(pi/n)]. In particular n=4 implies alpha<=exp(-pi),
for every four-state generator, including nonnormal and unbalanced ones.
Balance and the flat logical embedding are needed for (3), not for this
spectral obstruction. The one-way four-cycle attains the bound algebraically.

4. DIMENSION-FREE NORMAL-GENERATOR THEOREM, INCLUDING ALL ALIASES

Theorem. Let Q be a real, balanced, normal CTMC generator:
 QQ^T=Q^TQ, Q1=Q^T1=0, Q_ij>=0 for i!=j.
Let z in {+1,-1}^n have equal positive and negative counts, T>0, and
0<alpha<1. If

 exp(QT)z=-alpha z,                                  (7)

then alpha<=exp(-pi), independent of n. This includes any number of different
odd phase aliases in the contributing spectrum of Q.

Proof. Write Q=-L+A, with L=-(Q+Q^T)/2 symmetric positive semidefinite and
A=(Q-Q^T)/2 skew-symmetric. Normality is equivalent to [L,A]=0. An orthonormal
complex spectral decomposition shows that every nonzero component of z must
lie in a generator eigenspace with

 lambda=-a+i omega, a=-log(alpha)/T,
 omega T in {...,-3pi,-pi,pi,3pi,...}.

Consequently Lz=a z and

 ||Az||_2 >= (pi/T)||z||_2.                          (8)

Both Q and Q^T are row Markov generators because Q is balanced. For every
flat-sign coordinate, the maximum principle gives

 z_i(Qz)_i<=0 and z_i(Q^Tz)_i<=0.

Using Qz=-az+Az and Q^Tz=-az-Az yields |(Az)_i|<=a for every i. Hence
||Az||_2<=a||z||_2. Combining with (8) gives aT>=pi, proving the claim.
No single-frequency, diagonalizable-by-assertion, or generic-spectrum premise
is hidden in this proof: normality supplies the orthonormal decomposition.

Local-affinity extension. Suppose in addition every microscopic edge is
bidirectional and |log(Q_ij/Q_ji)|<=ell<infinity. Set rho=tanh(ell/2). Then

 alpha <= exp[-pi/rho] = exp[-pi*coth(ell/2)].         (9)

Proof. Set S_ij=(Q_ij+Q_ji)/2 off diagonal. Then |A_ij|<=rho*S_ij, A1=0,
and the flat-sign identity Lz=az gives 2*sum_(j: z_j!=z_i) S_ij=a. Thus

 |(Az)_i| = 2 |sum_(j: z_j!=z_i) A_ij| <=rho*a.

Replace the upper norm bound by rho*a||z|| in (8). The biased four-cycle
achieves equality in (9). This is a returned-flat-mode theorem under normality,
not a claim to prove a general affinity-dependent spectral conjecture.

Equality structure for (7) at alpha=exp(-pi). Equality in both norm bounds
forces w=Az/a in {+1,-1}^n, A^2z=-a^2z and Lw=aw. Also 1^Tz=1^Tw=z^Tw=0.
Hence the four classes indexed by (z_i,w_i)=(+,+),(+,-),(-,-),(-,+) have equal
size n/4. The equations Qz=a(w-z) and Qw=-a(z+w), combined with the flat-sign
maximum principle, prohibit every interclass transition except the four
successive cycle edges, with only one orientation present and total rate a.
The physical column-generator orientation is the reverse of its row-generator
orientation; both give the same four-cycle. Applying the identities also to
Q^T supplies the outgoing block rates. Thus the physical chain is strongly
lumpable on these four classes to a purely directed
equal-rate C4. Internal class transitions may remain. In particular n must be
divisible by four and any active interclass microscopic edge has zero reverse
rate. Thus finite standard stationary entropy production cannot attain equality.
For n=4 the generator itself must be the one-way equal-rate cycle, up to labels.

Nonuniform stationary memory extension. Let pi have strictly positive entries,
Q pi=0, and R=D_pi^(-1) Q D_pi be the generator for densities relative to pi.
Then R and its adjoint R*=Q^T in <x,y>_pi=sum_i pi_i*x_i*y_i are both row
Markov generators. If R is normal in this weighted inner product, repeat the
proof with weighted symmetric/skew-adjoint parts and ||z||_pi=1. It again gives
alpha<=exp(-pi) for any flat-sign returned density mode. For
pi=(1/2,1/2) tensor gamma, this permits any positive stationary memory law gamma
returned exactly, rather than only a uniform memory. The local-affinity version
uses |log(Q_ij*pi_j/(Q_ji*pi_i))|<=ell, the stationary flux affinity.
This extension does not cover a nondegenerate logical stationary law by the
same flat-sign argument: that logical contrast has unequal magnitudes.

5. SINGLE-FREQUENCY THEOREM WITHOUT NORMALITY, AND THE ALIAS BOTTLENECK

Even for nonnormal balanced Q, alpha<=exp(-pi) follows if the contributing
generator frequencies in (7) are only +omega and -omega. The spectral mapping
eigenspace contains no nontrivial Jordan-vector contribution: on a Jordan
block lambda I+N, exp(TN)-I=N times an invertible polynomial, so the kernel
is ker N. Thus z=Re v for an actual Q-eigenvector v (rescaled if necessary),
with eigenvalue -a+i omega.

Every coordinate v_i lies on one of the two vertical lines Re v_i=+1,-1.
Its coordinate convex hull has at most four vertices. Q is also a row generator.
Choose one original coordinate realizing each hull vertex and decompose every
other coordinate into a convex combination of hull vertices in

 lambda v_i = sum_(j!=i) Q_ij(v_j-v_i).

The resulting coefficients are nonnegative and define an m-state row generator,
m<=4, having the same nonreal eigenvalue lambda. Apply (6) to obtain |omega|<=a.
Since |omega|T>=pi, aT>=pi. This is the classical eigenvector-polygon mechanism.

Therefore a putative uniform-return binary gate with alpha>exp(-pi) must use
both nonnormality AND at least two distinct positive odd alias frequencies.
Each distinct frequency requires a conjugate pair of ordinary generator
eigenvectors; the negative propagator eigenspace has dimension at least four.
Equal generator eigenvalues with a repeated eigenspace do not count as distinct
aliases, since their eigenvectors can be added into one v in the argument above.

There is a sharper dimension requirement than eigenspace counting alone.
With r distinct positive odd aliases, max |omega|T>=(2r-1)pi, so (6) implies

 -log(alpha)>=(2r-1)pi*tan(pi/n).                    (10)

Already r=2 and alpha>exp(-pi) require n>=10. No balanced generator of dimension
4,6,or8, normal or nonnormal, can exceed the four-state ceiling while returning
uniform memory exactly. A possible escape needs at least five memory levels,
nonnormality, and simultaneous equal real decay at distinct odd aliases.
No such escape is constructed here. The dimension-free statement for arbitrary
nonnormal Q remains OPEN, and numerical failure to find an escape is not proof.

6. ROBUST NORMAL OBSTRUCTION WITH A FINITE RATE BUDGET

The exact statement is not silently promoted into an unbounded-rate robustness
claim. A simple explicit finite-budget estimate is available. Let Q be balanced
normal, z flat, T>0, alpha>exp(-pi), J=-log(alpha), B=T||Q||_2, and

 r0=alpha*min{(pi-J)/8,1/4}, g=(pi^2-J^2)/2>0.

Then the normalized endpoint residual satisfies

 ||exp(QT)z+alpha z||_2/||z||_2
       >= r0*sqrt[g/(B^2+g)].                       (11)

Proof. For any flat z, the two maximum principles give |(Az)_i|<=|(Lz)_i|,
and hence ||Az||_2^2<=||Lz||_2^2. In a normal spectral decomposition, let
(a,b)=(-T Re lambda,T Im lambda) and let mu be normalized squared spectral
weight of z. Then integral (b^2-a^2)dmu<=0. Every mode with
|exp(-a+ib)+alpha|<=r0 has a<=J+(pi-J)/4 and |b|>=pi-(pi-J)/4. This follows
from the modulus and argument of a point in the radius-r0 disk about -alpha;
we used -log(1-u)<=2u and arcsin u<=2u for u<=1/4. Such a mode has
b^2-a^2>=g. All other modes have b^2-a^2>=-B^2. Therefore at least
g/(B^2+g) spectral weight lies outside that disk, proving (11).

If D=max_i(-Q_ii), balance gives ||Q||_2<=2D, so B<=2DT. Thus at fixed
dimensionless rate-time budget, a normal controller cannot approach an exactly
returned target above the ceiling arbitrarily closely. The constants in (11)
are conservative; it is a rigorous obstruction, not an optimal cost formula.
For a basis input, the target joint TV distance is ||residual||_1/(2n), and
|residual_i|<=1+alpha gives the further bound TV>=r0^2*g/
[2(1+alpha)(B^2+g)].

7. A STOP-FREE LIMIT AND A CONSEQUENTIAL CONTROL OBSTRUCTION

Suppose a finite time-homogeneous CTMC is required to complete a gate and hold
its output with all auxiliary states returned. If its asymptotic map exists,
P_infty is a stochastic projection: P_infty^2=P_infty. An exact returned-memory
negative logical mode would imply P_infty z=-alpha z with alpha>0, impossible
for a projection. Even an exact finite-time stable endpoint is impossible:
Q exp(QT)p=0 implies Qp=0 because exp(QT) is invertible and commutes with Q,
so the endpoint was already stationary initially.

Thus an autonomous finite CTMC can show a transient oscillatory NOT advantage,
but a completed and latched NOT requires a changed completion flag, consumed
clock/phase state, continuing external clock/readout, or other operation outside
this returned-controller contract. Event-triggered first passage can remove a
fixed external time only by explicitly representing the event detector and its
initialization/reset, rather than calling its stopping rule free. This no-go is
not a ban on ordinary autonomous driven devices; it identifies the additional
state/resource their gate interface must expose.
