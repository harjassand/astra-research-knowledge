UNIFORM THERMAL MEMORY: A BLOCK-EXCHANGE CONVERSE
Internal specialization/proof audit with exact checks, 2026-10-23.
Independent proof review and present-day priority: NOT ESTABLISHED.

Result and scope
----------------
There are 2N classical joint states, divided into two physical blocks A and B
of N states each. Initially p=(1_A/N,0_B). Allowed elementary contacts replace
(p_i,p_j) by ((1-q)p_i+q p_j,q p_i+(1-q)p_j), 0<=q<=1/2. The pair can be any
two joint states. The protocol is a finite word of these contacts, or a limit
of such words. No measurements, feedback, postselection, nonuniform ancillary
resource, or extra uncounted states are allowed.

Claim: for EVERY such protocol,

    mass_B <= 1-e_N,       e_N = binom(2N,N)/4^N.                     (1)

This upper bound does not require memory return. In the closure, equality
is attained by the known N-by-N full-contact sweep, followed by uniformizing
within A and within B. This gives an exactly uniform product memory return
and the input-independent bit channel

    [[e_N,1-e_N],[1-e_N,e_N]].                                      (2)

Consequently the infimum bit-flip error in this model is exactly e_N. In
particular an O(1/N) error family is impossible: e_N~1/sqrt(pi*N). For a
finite-rate finite-contact exact-return implementation, (1) remains necessary;
attainment at its boundary is not asserted here. A separately checked finite
exact-return compiler can approach (2) with arbitrary positive error slack.

The state-space dimension N is the memory alphabet size, not the number of
memory bits. The result says log_2 N grows as 2 log_2(1/error)+O(1). It is a
classical uniform-reference result, without a quantum-coherence or general
finite-temperature claim. It does not imply heat, work, or controller costs.

Majorization notation and a wall lemma
-------------------------------------
For a nonincreasing vector v let V_k=sum_{j<=k} v_j, with V_0=0. Write v>=_maj u
if the total sums agree and V_k>=U_k for every k. A reciprocal contact is
bistochastic, so the sorted probability vector can only decrease in this
order along its continuously parametrized pulse.

Let F_i average the adjacent RANKED coordinates i and i+1, using indices
1,...,2N-1. It preserves nonincreasing order. All prefix sums are unchanged
except

    prefix_i(F_i v) = (V_{i-1}+V_{i+1})/2.                         (3)

Therefore F_i is majorization-monotone:

    v>=_maj u  implies  F_i v>=_maj F_i u.                         (4)

Also v>=_maj F_i v. If u is on the i-th ordering wall, u_i=u_{i+1}, and
v>=_maj u, then F_i v>=_maj u: for prefix i, use

    U_i=(U_{i-1}+U_{i+1})/2 <= (V_{i-1}+V_{i+1})/2;               (5)

all other prefix inequalities are unchanged.

Thus every crossing of adjacent ranks has a universal upper envelope:
replace the current envelope v by F_i v. This wall optimality is established
continuous-majorization machinery, not a new physical mechanism; see the
primary-source distinction at the end. Equations (3)-(5) give a complete
independent proof in the uniform-reference case needed here.

Finite galleries, including all ties
-----------------------------------
A contact can be parametrized affinely by q from 0 to its prescribed endpoint.
Only two coordinates change. For every pair of coordinates their difference
is affine, so there are finitely many distinct equality times, unless that
difference is identically zero. Between those times choose any fixed order of
the identically equal coordinates. At a boundary, any change between the two
valid sorted label orders is a permutation within equal-coordinate groups;
decompose it into adjacent swaps. Every such swap is at an actual tie.

Concatenating finitely many contacts therefore gives a finite adjacent-swap
word i_1,...,i_m, called its chamber gallery. Include necessary swaps at the
initial and final times as well. Multiple simultaneous ties cause no problem:
resolve the order difference into finitely many adjacent swaps at that same
time. These swaps describe equal coordinates; they are not physical controls.

Starting from the sorted initial vector v0=(1/N,...,1/N,0,...,0), equations
(4)-(5) inductively show that the sorted actual endpoint p^down satisfies

    p^down <=_maj F_{i_m}...F_{i_1} v0.                           (6)

Evolution between wall times also only decreases majorization. For a tie
swap, (5) applies even when the ENVELOPE itself has unequal coordinates at
those ranks. Thus degeneracies do not invalidate the induction.

Only the block exchange matters
------------------------------
First assume an exactly uniform product endpoint with mass_B>1/2. Choose the
initial chamber A_1,...,A_N,B_1,...,B_N. At the endpoint all B values are equal,
all A values are equal, and every B value exceeds every A value. Append swaps
at those within-block ties until its chamber order is

    B_1,...,B_N,A_1,...,A_N.                                     (7)

The gallery word then represents exactly this block exchange, including any
within-block changes the original trajectory made. Actual probabilities do
not change in the appended tie bookkeeping.

For completeness the same reduction works for ANY raw endpoint of mass_B>1/2,
without requiring an infinite memory reset. Append FINITELY many averages
within each physical block until every B coordinate exceeds every A coordinate.
These contacts preserve mass_B. To see finite separation, in each block an
average of its maximum and minimum decreases its squared deviation from the
block mean by (max-min)^2/2, at least 1/(2N) times that deviation. Repetition
therefore converges geometrically to the two different block means. A finite
number gives their strictly separated ordering.

Now choose the INITIAL label order within the exactly tied A block, and within
the exactly tied B block, to match the within-block order of this ACTUAL final
vector. These choices change no probability and no prescribed contact. The
initial chamber is allowed to be selected retrospectively as a proof device
because every A coordinate is exactly 1/N and every B coordinate is exactly 0.
Any departure into a different chamber at time zero is explicitly included
as swaps at the initial exact ties. No generic perturbation, assumption about
the propagation of perturbation orders, physical relabeling control, or
input-dependent protocol is being used. With these labels the final chamber
is again precisely (7). The rational-gallery executable checks this route
directly, including time-zero and simultaneous ties.

If mass_B<=1/2, (1) is already true. Thus it is enough to bound envelopes for
arbitrary adjacent-swap words representing (7).

Deleting redundant crossings: a self-contained reduced-word lemma
----------------------------------------------------------------
Draw the word as a wiring diagram, initially labeling the strands
A_1,...,A_N,B_1,...,B_N. If the same pair of original strands crosses twice,
delete the corresponding TWO swap letters, keeping every other rank index
unchanged. The resulting word has exactly the same final permutation:
deleting the first crossing exchanges the identities traveling on those two
downstream tracks; all intervening crossings still occur at the prescribed
positions; deleting their second crossing restores the identities. All other
strands have their original endpoints throughout. Equivalently, conjugating
each crossing by its preceding permutation yields the same transposition
of original labels at those two places, so their two factors cancel.

Repeat until no original strand pair crosses more than once. The final
permutation is unchanged. A pair's final relative order changes exactly
when it crosses an odd number of times. Thus in the remaining word a pair
crosses once iff its final order is reversed. For the block exchange (7),
these pairs are exactly the N^2 A/B pairs; no A/A or B/B pair remains. The
remaining word is a reduced block-exchange word occurring as a SUBSEQUENCE
of the original word. This is the specialized Coxeter deletion argument,
proved here without needing a coset or Bruhat-order assertion.

Deleting ANY F_i from an envelope product can only increase its output in
majorization order: immediately before that letter v>=_maj F_i v, and all
subsequent ranked maps are majorization-monotone by (4). Therefore (6) is
bounded above by the envelope of SOME reduced block-exchange word.            (8)

Every reduced word gives the same envelope
------------------------------------------
In a reduced block-exchange word, let event (i,j) be the unique crossing of
A_i and B_j. Since no same-block pair crosses, the events obey exactly the
rectangular partial order

    (i+1,j) precedes (i,j),    (i,j) precedes (i,j+1).              (9)

The first relation says B_j meets A_N,A_{N-1},...,A_1 in that order; the
second says A_i meets B_1,B_2,...,B_N in that order. Conversely, any linear
extension of (9) is feasible: when its next event is selected, all strands
originally lying between that A_i and B_j have already crossed the relevant
one of them, so the pair is adjacent. Hence reduced words are the linear
extensions of this grid.

Any two linear extensions of a finite poset are connected by exchanges of
neighboring incomparable events. Proof: move the first event of a desired
extension left across those preceding it in another extension; every such
preceding event must be incomparable to it; then repeat on the suffix.

Incomparable events in (9) share neither their A strand nor their B strand.
When neighboring in a gallery, their two adjacent rank pairs are therefore
disjoint. The corresponding F maps commute, since |i-j|>1. Consequently all
reduced words give EXACTLY the same vector, not merely the same block mass.
In Coxeter terminology this block exchange is fully commutative, but the
grid proof above is sufficient and self-contained.

Choose the canonical reduced word that moves B_1 through A_N,...,A_1, then
B_2 through A_N,...,A_1, and so on. This is the N-by-N full-contact sweep.
Combining (6)-(9), its sorted vector majorizes every separated endpoint.
Taking prefix N therefore bounds the B mass of every raw endpoint by the
sweep's top-N mass.

Computing the sweep error
------------------------
Scale probabilities by N. Let c_{r,j} be the common value after crossing the
r-th A encountered with B_j, so

    c_{r,j}=(c_{r-1,j}+c_{r,j-1})/2,
    c_{0,j}=0, c_{r,0}=1 (r,j>=1).                              (10)

The final A values are c_{1,N},...,c_{N,N}, divided by N. Solving (10), or
conditioning a fair coin sequence on its first step, gives

    c_{r,j}=2^{-(r+j-1)} sum_{k=0}^{r-1} binom(r+j-1,k).          (11)

Write S_N=sum_{r=1}^N c_{r,N}. From (10) and c_{N,N}=1/2,

    S_N=S_{N-1}+c_{N,N-1}-1/2,                 N>=2.             (12)

Symmetry of the binomial distribution in (11) yields

    c_{N,N-1}=1/2 + (1/2) binom(2N-2,N-1)/4^(N-1).             (13)

Starting at S_1=1/2, induction using (12)-(13) now gives

    S_N=N binom(2N,N)/4^N.                                    (14)

Thus the sweep's A mass is e_N=S_N/N, proving (1). The A and B labels in
the canonical reduced gallery have exchanged their whole rank blocks, so
its top-N mass is precisely the physical B mass 1-e_N.

Uniform return and the closure
-----------------------------
Within-block uniformization preserves the two block masses. For the pure-A
input it gives (e_N/N on A, (1-e_N)/N on B). Since every joint contact is
bistochastic and pure-A plus pure-B input vectors sum to the uniform vector
with coordinate 1/N, the same fixed protocol sends the pure-B input to the
complementary product vector. Linearity gives (2) for every bit input, and
also returns the memory independent of the bit for reference-correlated
inputs. Within-block reciprocal mixing approaches the required uniformizer,
so this protocol belongs to the closure of finite reciprocal words.

The lower bound (1) is a closed inequality on the raw output probabilities.
It therefore passes directly to limits of arbitrary finite words, whether
or not the approximating finite words already return their memories.
It is not necessary to assume exact finite-word implementation of a global
uniformizer. General reciprocal continuous-time generators on this fixed
finite space are also covered when their propagators are limits of products
of reciprocal contacts (e.g. the usual finite-rate piecewise-continuous case).
For a constant interval, the product formula approximates the exponential
of the sum of reciprocal edge generators by their individual exponentials;
each factor is a contact with q=(1-exp(-2*k*dt))/2. Piecewise-constant
approximation covers piecewise-continuous time dependence in finite dimension.
No approximation by reciprocal generators is asserted for a directed
balanced but nonsymmetric generator.

Consequences for acquisition and locality budgets
------------------------------------------------
The elementary exact inequality

    e_N >= 1/(2 sqrt(N))                                      (15)

follows by induction from e_1=1/2 and e_N/e_{N-1}=(2N-1)/(2N):
N(2N-1)^2 >= 4N^2(N-1). Therefore any error epsilon requires

    N >= 1/(4 epsilon^2),                                    (16)

with the sharper asymptotic N >= (1+o(1))/(pi epsilon^2). The exact discrete
condition e_N<=epsilon is better than (16). This closes the earlier gap
between the elementary geometric bound epsilon>=1/(2N) and the known sweep.

For reciprocal continuous-time controls let

    K_cut = integral sum_{edges crossing A|B} k_e(t) dt.

A stronger squared-norm argument than the entropy/activity estimate gives

    K_cut >= N(1-epsilon)/epsilon.                            (17)

Proof: the initial squared probability norm is 1/N and the final block
masses imply ||p_T||^2 >= [epsilon^2+(1-epsilon)^2]/N. Thus

    integral sum_all k_e(p_i-p_j)^2 dt <=epsilon(1-epsilon)/N.

The signed integrated cut flux is 1-epsilon. Cauchy-Schwarz bounds its
square by K_cut times the corresponding cut squared-difference integral,
which is no larger than the displayed all-edge integral. This proves (17)
even with residual memory correlations. It strengthens the existing
total-edge-rate argument in interface_invention to the physical cut alone.

If at most b cut edges are active simultaneously, each at rate<=kappa,

    T >= N(1-epsilon)/(kappa*b*epsilon)
      >= (1-epsilon)/(4*kappa*b*epsilon^3).                    (18)

For fixed b,kappa the stronger conclusion is Omega(epsilon^{-3}); the
entropy/log weakening is unnecessary. With a uniform prior on bit inputs,
the unconditional joint state stays uniform, so the expected number of
cut jumps is K_cut/N >=(1-epsilon)/epsilon. These are coupling, activity,
and time costs under specified controls. They are not Landauer heat claims,
and b must count actual S-changing edges for the physical encoding. No
N^2 physical contact-count lower bound follows from N^2 ranked crossings.

Parallel scheduling of the known rectangle
------------------------------------------
Write a physical sweep event as (r,j), acting on A_{N+1-r}, B_j. Its only
noncommuting predecessor requirements are (r-1,j) and (r,j-1). All events
with the same r+j form a matching: they use distinct A states and distinct
B states. Execute the antidiagonals r+j=2,...,2N in order. The depth is
2N-1, the maximum width is N, and the number of contacts remains N^2.
Any serial rectangle order and this schedule differ only by commuting
disjoint physical gates. Thus they give exactly the same full joint
propagator, including for arbitrary finite pulse parameters at each cell.

A finite contact with residual eta=1-2q and rate kappa takes
tau=log(1/eta)/(2kappa). At full width, the raw sweep time is

    T_raw=(2N-1)log(1/eta)/(2kappa).                            (19)

Replacing an ideal contact by residual eta changes its output TV by
eta*|p_i-p_j|/2 <=eta/(2N) on the capped intermediate states. Telescoping
N^2 contacts yields raw state TV error <=N*eta/2. Thus eta=2 delta_raw/N
suffices for raw error slack delta_raw, improving the generic operator
bound N^2 eta/2 when only uniform-memory bit inputs are needed. For
N=O(epsilon^{-2}) and delta_raw=O(epsilon), (19) is
O(epsilon^{-2} log(1/epsilon)/kappa), with width N explicitly charged.
This is an upper bound for the known schedule, not optimal parallel time.

Exact memory return costs are additional. The separately established
serial max/min mixer requires O(N log(N/delta_return)) finite contacts;
the subsequent exact correction uses at most2N-1 contacts and has total
rate area <=N delta_return/(1-2epsilon_raw-2delta_return). These stages
must be counted according to their own interface and concurrency budgets.
They are not hidden inside the raw antidiagonal depth. At an available
width b<N, subdividing each matching gives raw depth at most
N^2/b+(2N-1), still with N^2 individual contacts.
