1. EXACT MODEL AND CONCLUSION

For integer N>=1, start with p0=(1/N on A,0 on B), |A|=|B|=N. An elementary
contact on any physical pair i,j applies the symmetric stochastic block
[[1-q,q],[q,1-q]], 0<=q<=1/2. A protocol is a finite product, or an output
limit of such products, with no extra states, feedback, postselection, or
free physical permutations. Then

    sum_{i in A} p_i >= e_N=binom(2N,N)/4^N,
    sum_{i in B} p_i <= 1-e_N.

No return assumption is used in this upper bound. The known N^2 full-contact
sweep attains equality as an ideal q=1/2 word. Within-block uniformization
then supplies exact product return in the closure. The fixed channel on a
uniform-memory bit has error e_N. A finite-rate pulse has q<1/2, so equality
for finite-rate finite-contact exact-return implementations is NOT supplied
by this argument. The independently audited positive-slack compiler supplies
arbitrarily close finite exact-return implementations for N>=2.

2. WALL ENVELOPE, INCLUDING DEGENERATE TIES

For nonincreasing v, define prefix sums V_k. The ranked adjacent average F_i
leaves every prefix except i unchanged, and replaces prefix i by
(V_{i-1}+V_{i+1})/2. It preserves nonincreasing order. Therefore it is monotone
for majorization on ordered vectors and v majorizes F_i v.

If an ordered actual vector u is on wall i, then
U_i=(U_{i-1}+U_{i+1})/2. Consequently v majorizes u implies F_i v majorizes u.
This last implication is the key: it does not require the current envelope
itself to have equal entries i,i+1.

On one physical partial contact, parameterize q from 0 to its prescribed
endpoint. Every physical coordinate is affine in q. Every coordinate pair
has at most one isolated equality time, or is identically equal throughout
the pulse. Thus there are finitely many rank-change times for a finite word.
Inside an open interval choose any fixed order of identically equal labels.
At a boundary, the preceding and following sorted orders differ only within
equal-valued groups. Any permutation of such a contiguous group decomposes
into adjacent swaps, each at an actual tie. The same applies at time zero,
at pulse boundaries, and at the final time. Simultaneous ties require no
generic perturbation and add only finitely many swaps.

Between these times the sorted actual state only decreases in majorization:
it has undergone additional bistochastic mixing along the pulse. At a wall
swap use the wall implication above. Induction gives

    sorted(p_final) <=_maj F_{i_m}...F_{i_1} v0.

This induction remains valid for arbitrary finite tie bookkeeping. It does
not assume that envelope coordinates inherit the actual ties. It also does
not assume that the new reduced word found later is an actual subtrajectory.

The monotone-in-majorization path uses q<=1/2. Extending a contact past q=1/2
would allow the sorted coordinates to become less mixed after the equality
point and invalidates the proof. Already N=1,q=3/4 violates the claimed bound,
so that restriction is material rather than cosmetic.

3. REDUCTION OF EVERY RAW ENDPOINT TO A BLOCK-EXCHANGE GALLERY

Let raw B mass be beta>1/2 and A mass be 1-beta. Mix only inside A and inside
B until every B coordinate exceeds every A coordinate. This can be achieved
with finitely many strictly partial q=1/4 contacts, so no infinite waiting
time or exact block reset is hidden in this reduction.

For one block, let V=sum_i(p_i-mean)^2. Mixing its maximum and minimum with
q=1/4 decreases V by (3/8)(max-min)^2 >= (3/(8N))V. Thus V contracts
geometrically. Let eta=(2beta-1)/(4N)>0. Finitely many steps in each block
make V<eta^2; then all coordinates are within eta of their block mean, and
min_B p > max_A p. These appended contacts preserve beta exactly. Their
choices are an existence proof using the known model, not a measurement or
feedback resource. The candidate's q=1/2 version is also correct inside its
larger allowed-contact model.

Now list each block's physical labels in the within-block order they have
at this separated endpoint. Initially each block is exactly tied, so the
initial chamber can be chosen with those same within-block orders. If the
actual path leaves the initial tie in a different order, include the needed
time-zero tie swaps. The final order, relative to this chosen initial order,
is exactly the block exchange

    (A1,...,AN,B1,...,BN) -> (B1,...,BN,A1,...,AN).

This retrospective choice only names the sorted strands for the proof. It
does not permute probabilities or change any prescribed physical contact.
At the separated endpoint the top-N sorted mass equals beta. If beta<=1/2,
the desired inequality already holds since e_N<=1/2. Thus it suffices to
bound galleries whose endpoint permutation is this block exchange.

4. DELETING TWO CROSSINGS: PRECISE ALGEBRAIC JUSTIFICATION

The geometric claim is correct, but the candidate's sentence about identical
conjugated factors cancelling should be expanded: those reflections are not
adjacent factors in a naive product. Here is an exact identity that preserves
all remaining rank indices.

Write the permutation word, with simple rank swaps acting on the left, as

    U s_b X s_a W,

where s_a and s_b are two selected crossings of the same pair of ORIGINAL
strands and X is the unmodified word between them. The original-label
transposition at the first crossing is W^{-1}s_a W. At the second it is

    W^{-1}s_a X^{-1}s_b X s_a W.

Equality of these two transpositions gives

    s_b = X s_a X^{-1},   hence   s_b X s_a = X.

Therefore the whole endpoint permutation equals U X W: deleting exactly the
two selected letters, and leaving all other rank indices unchanged, works.
This does not require those selected crossings to be consecutive occurrences
of that strand pair. After a deletion the original labels on the new wiring
diagram MUST be recomputed before selecting a further repeated pair. The
candidate's executable does recompute them, and the mathematical repetition
should explicitly retain this convention.

Each deletion shortens the finite word. When no pair crosses twice, the
crossing count of each original pair is 0 or 1, and parity of relative order
shows it is 1 exactly for inversions of the endpoint permutation. For the
block exchange these are precisely the N^2 A/B pairs. Thus a reduced
block-exchange word is obtained as a literal subsequence of the original
rank-index word. There are no surviving same-block crossings.

Deleting a ranked map F_i can only improve its final output in majorization:
before that letter v>=_maj F_i v, and every following ranked map is
majorization-monotone. This assertion concerns ordered numerical vectors;
it does not require any reinterpretation of actual physical paths after
deletion. Repeated deletion consequently puts the original envelope below
the envelope of some reduced block-exchange word.

5. WHY ALL REDUCED BLOCK-EXCHANGE WORDS HAVE ONE ENVELOPE

Call the unique crossing of Ai and Bj event (i,j). Since no same-block
crossing survives, Bj must meet AN,...,A1 in that order, while Ai must meet
B1,...,BN in that order. These are exactly the rectangular-grid precedences.
Conversely, when all prerequisites of (i,j) have occurred, every Ak with k>i
has moved to the right of Bj, and every Bl with l<j has moved to the left of
Ai. All other same-block strands are outside the pair. Hence Ai and Bj are
adjacent, so every linear extension of the grid is feasible.

Any two linear extensions are related by swaps of neighboring incomparable
events: move the desired first event left across incomparable predecessors,
then induct on the suffix. Incomparable events share neither A nor B strand.
When they are consecutive, their rank swaps therefore act on disjoint pairs
of positions, with rank indices differing by more than 1. Swapping their
order leaves those rank indices unchanged and their two F maps commute.
This proves equality of the entire envelope vectors for all reduced words.

No three-term braid move is being silently used. In fact ordinary adjacent
averages need not satisfy such a braid identity. The rectangle's restriction
to commutations is exactly what makes this step work.

6. SWEEP FORMULA AND CLOSURE

Choose the word moving B1 through AN,...,A1, then B2 through them, and so on.
After scaling initial A coordinates to 1, the value at encounter (r,j) obeys

    c_rj=(c_(r-1),j+c_r,(j-1))/2, c_0j=0, c_r0=1.

Its solution is the fair-binomial tail

    c_rj=2^{-(r+j-1)} sum_{k=0}^{r-1} binom(r+j-1,k).

For S_N=sum_{r=1}^N c_rN, summing the recurrence gives

    S_N=S_(N-1)+c_N,(N-1)-c_NN
       =S_(N-1)+(1/2) binom(2N-2,N-1)/4^(N-1).

Here c_NN=1/2 and S_1=1/2. Thus S_N=N binom(2N,N)/4^N. This is the physical
A mass times N. The sweep's ranked top-N mass is its physical B mass 1-e_N.
Since this common reduced envelope majorizes every separated raw endpoint,
its prefix-N bound proves the claimed raw-output inequality.

The inequality depends continuously only on the endpoint probabilities, so
it passes to arbitrary limits of finite contact words. For attainment, the
ideal full-contact sweep followed by a limit of within-block symmetric
mixing has the stated product endpoint. The corresponding maps are
bistochastic. Since rho_A+rho_B=1/N on all physical states, the SAME limiting
map takes rho_B to the complementary product endpoint. Hence linearity gives
the fixed binary channel for every classical bit distribution and classical
reference correlation. Quantum-coherence preservation is not established.

Strict finite-rate continuous-time reciprocal generators are included when
their propagators are limits of these contact words. Directed balanced
generators, free permutations, and extra states do not follow from the proof.

8. INDEPENDENT ADDENDUM: THE STRONGER CUT-RATE-AREA BOUND

This useful bound also holds WITHOUT memory return. Let p(t) evolve under a
reciprocal continuous-time generator, from uniform A to any raw endpoint
with A mass epsilon and B mass 1-epsilon. Count unordered cut edges once and
define K_cut=integral sum_{a in A,b in B} kappa_ab(t) dt.

Squared-norm dissipation is exact:

    d||p||_2^2/dt = -2 sum_{unordered edges} kappa_ij (p_i-p_j)^2.

Initially ||p0||_2^2=1/N. By within-block Cauchy--Schwarz,
||pf||_2^2 >= [epsilon^2+(1-epsilon)^2]/N, regardless of correlations.
Consequently

    integral sum_all_edges kappa_ij(p_i-p_j)^2 dt
      <= epsilon(1-epsilon)/N.

The signed integrated current across the logical cut is 1-epsilon. Applying
Cauchy--Schwarz to its rate-weighted integral, with reverse currents allowed,

    (1-epsilon)^2
      <= K_cut integral sum_cut kappa_ab(p_a-p_b)^2 dt
      <= K_cut epsilon(1-epsilon)/N.

Thus for 0<epsilon<1,

    K_cut >= N(1-epsilon)/epsilon.

If at most b actual bit-changing edges are active at any time and each rate
is at most kappa, K_cut<=b*kappa*T, giving

    T >= N(1-epsilon)/(b*kappa*epsilon).

Combining with N>=1/(4epsilon^2) yields
T >= (1-epsilon)/(4b*kappa*epsilon^3), at fixed b,kappa. The exact discrete
central-binomial requirement on N gives the sharper finite-error statement.
This strengthens the earlier entropy/activity logarithmic bound without
making an energy or universal control-overhead claim. Ideal q=1/2 contacts
have infinite rate area and cannot be assigned zero action by this model.
