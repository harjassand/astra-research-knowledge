INDEPENDENT AUDIT: SINGLE-FREQUENCY REDUCTION AND ALIAS REQUIREMENTS
2026-10-23. Autonomous balanced classical generator; exact flat return.

Verdict. The proposed reduction and the dimension<=9 consequence are valid,
with the ordinary-eigenspace and orientation qualifications proved below.
No diagonalizability or normality of Q is required. Alpha>exp(-pi) therefore
requires at least two contributing positive odd aliases, Q nonnormality,
at least four real dimensions in the negative propagator eigenspace, and
at least ten physical states. These are necessary conditions, not a
construction or a claim that dimension ten suffices.

1. MODEL AND THE CLASSICAL SECTOR INPUT

Let Q be a fixed finite real D-state Markov generator in COLUMN convention,
with Q_ij>=0 for i!=j, 1^TQ=0, and Q1=0. Balance makes Q itself a row generator
as well. Assume T>0, z in {+1,-1}^D, and exp(QT)z=-alpha z with 0<alpha<1.
Put J=-log(alpha)>0 and a=J/T.

The classical generator sector bound is used:

    |Im lambda| <= (-Re lambda) cot(pi/D), D>=3.

It holds for all finite Markov generators, including reducible/nonnormal ones.
For D=2 all eigenvalues are real. A primary research account is E. B. Davies,
Embeddable Markov Matrices, Electronic Journal of Probability 15 (2010),
paper47, equations (5)-(6), page1479, Theorem9 proof:
https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/EJP-ECP/article/download/733/993.pdf
The source derives the sector from the relevant stochastic-matrix boundary
theorem. This audit checked the displayed sector statement directly. The
state-dimension constant is pi/D, not pi/(D-1). The directed D-cycle gives
the sharp sector boundary. No general affinity-sector conjecture is assumed.

2. WHY JORDAN BLOCKS DO NOT SUPPLY EXTRA RETURN MODES

Complexify the finite space and use Q's generalized eigenspace decomposition.
On a Jordan block Q=lambda I+N, exp(QT)=exp(lambda T)exp(TN). If
exp(lambda T)!=-alpha, exp(QT)+alpha I is invertible on that block and the
component of z there vanishes. If exp(lambda T)=-alpha, then

    exp(TN)-I = N [T I+(T^2/2)N+...].

The bracket is invertible because N is nilpotent and T>0. It commutes with
N. Therefore ker(exp(TN)-I)=ker N. Only ORDINARY Q eigenvectors contribute
to z, even when Q itself has nontrivial Jordan blocks. Components in distinct
generalized eigenspaces cannot cancel this equation against one another.

Consequently z is a sum of ordinary eigenvectors at eigenvalues

    lambda_k=-a+i(2k+1)pi/T.

Reality pairs the contributions at opposite frequencies. There are no real
contributing eigenvalues and no zero-frequency contribution. Repeated
eigenvalues or multiple eigenvectors at the SAME frequency do not create a
new alias; their sum is still an ordinary eigenvector at that eigenvalue.

3. ONE CONTRIBUTING CONJUGATE FREQUENCY PAIR

If only +/-omega contributes, omega>0, then

    (Q+aI)^2 z=-omega^2 z.

Set w=(Q+aI)z/omega and v=z-iw. Then

    Qz=-az+omega w, Qw=-aw-omega z,
    Qv=(-a+iomega)v.

The minus sign in v=z-iw matters for matching the chosen positive-frequency
eigenvalue, though not for the bound. Every coordinate v_j has real part
z_j=+1 or -1. Their convex hull K in the complex plane thus has at most FOUR
extreme points: on each of the two vertical lines only its highest and
lowest points can be extreme. This finite-hull statement uses flatness of z.

Choose indices i_1,...,i_h representing the distinct extreme points, h<=4.
Write every v_j as a convex combination sum_l t_jl v_i_l. For an extreme
point take its own trivial combination. For each extreme row i_k use the
row-generator relation

    lambda v_i_k = sum_{j!=i_k} Q_i_k,j (v_j-v_i_k).

Define, for l!=k,

    B_kl=sum_{j!=i_k} Q_i_k,j t_jl >=0,
    B_kk=-sum_{l!=k} B_kl.

Then B is a genuine h-state ROW Markov generator and
B(v_i_1,...,v_i_h)^T=lambda(v_i_1,...,v_i_h)^T. Its balance or normality is
not needed. Repeated coordinates do not change this construction. Since
lambda is nonreal, h cannot be 1 or 2. Applying the sector bound at h<=4,

    omega <= a cot(pi/h) <= a.

Thus J=aT>=omega T>=pi, proving alpha<=exp(-pi) without normality in the
single-frequency case. The construction does not preserve the original
generator's full process or stationary distribution; it is a spectral
witness sufficient for the sector obstruction.

4. MULTIPLE ALIASES AND THE DIMENSION CONSEQUENCE

If alpha>exp(-pi), Section3 excludes one contributing conjugate pair, so let
r>=2 distinct POSITIVE frequencies contribute. They are distinct positive
odd multiples of pi/T. In particular

    omega_max T >= (2r-1)pi >=3pi.

The D-state sector bound then gives

    J >= (2r-1)pi tan(pi/D).

This is a statement about the number of distinct contributing frequencies
in the particular flat vector z, not the number of all frequencies anywhere
in Q. Since D<=9 implies 3tan(pi/D)>1 for every possible D>=3, r>=2 would
force J>pi. Therefore every balanced autonomous generator on D<=9 states
obeys alpha<=exp(-pi) for this exact flat returned mode. D=1,2 have no such
negative mode at all. Flat balance itself forces D even, so in the uniform
bit-memory setting the first not-excluded dimension is D=10, N=5.

There are at least 2r linearly independent real directions in the ordinary
eigenspace ker(exp(QT)+alpha I), since distinct Q eigenvalues have linearly
independent eigenvectors and the real and imaginary parts of a nonreal
eigenvector are independent. Thus alpha>exp(-pi) forces its real geometric
multiplicity to be >=4, not just its algebraic multiplicity. The normal
autonomous theorem separately rules out normal Q at every dimension.

5. LIMITATIONS

The hull argument is for RIGHT eigenvectors of a ROW generator. In the
physical column convention this is available because Q is BALANCED. For a
general unbalanced column generator, merely transposing changes the right
flat mode into a left one; that would not justify the same argument.

All conclusions concern exact return under one autonomous Q. Approximate
return can have ill-conditioned spectral projections and nearby aliases;
Jordan-block cancellation and quantitative robustness need separate work.
Time-varying controls are not covered. A higher alias is an imaginary
generator frequency times T, not a free resource-independent count of time
steps or physical gates.

The proof leaves a real open target: a balanced nonnormal generator on at
least ten states with >=two contributing odd-frequency pairs and exactly
flat returned logical mode, attaining alpha>exp(-pi). No such generator is
constructed or implied by these necessary inequalities.
