# Astra XH2 — Closing the mechanical feedback-to-growth interface

## 1. The strongest new physical constraint

The previous construction established a mathematical comparator that can distinguish whether a growing repair has reached a target stiffness, largely independent of the magnitude of an applied tensile load.

However, detecting a mechanical difference is not equivalent to having enough energy to operate a physical nutrient valve.

This is the next interface to resolve.

Two recent primary results make the distinction important. Rajput and Pahlavan demonstrated bistable, flow-driven microfluidic elements in October 2026, with switching arising from fluid–structure feedback rather than electronic control. Separately, a 2026 strain-powered micropump demonstrated flow rates up to approximately 0.16 µL/s and pressures up to 11 kPa under its reported operating conditions. Neither experiment couples a mechanical repair comparator to living cellulose production.&#x20;

Phys. Rev. X

There is also an important biological constraint. Takahama and colleagues found that cellulose production in Komagataeibacter was highly sensitive to nutrient feeding and gluconic-acid accumulation. Their pH-controlled, fed culture produced approximately 7.59 g/L of nanofibrillated bacterial cellulose, versus 2.45 g/L in the unregulated batch comparison. Excessive sugar feeding could also worsen production.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.asm.org\&sz=32)

Applied and Environmental Microbiology



This means that a useful living-repair controller must solve two distinct problems:

- Supply enough fluid and chemical resources for effective construction.
- Stop or reduce delivery when the actual mechanical function has been restored, without assuming that greater nutrient delivery always improves cellulose production.

I am retaining the mechanical comparator as the sensing element, but challenging whether it can directly power a valve, or instead requires an independently powered fluidic switching element.

## 2. New exact result: a precise mechanical comparator may not have enough energy to operate the valve

I represented the earlier elastic comparator by two reference springs and two repair-side springs. Their free midpoint displacement difference is \\(\delta_0\\).

When an actuator is attached, the comparator behaves like a mechanical source with effective compliance \\(R\\). An actuator spring of stiffness \\(k\\) experiences

\\[ \delta=\frac{\delta_0}{1+kR}. \\]

The maximum energy that can be stored in such a spring is

\\[ \boxed{ E\_{\rm spring}^{\max}=\frac{\delta_0^2}{8R}, \qquad k\_{\rm optimum}=\frac1R. } \\]

The entire initial mechanical imbalance contains at most

\\[ \boxed{E\_{\rm available}=\frac{\delta_0^2}{2R}} \\]

of releasable elastic energy under fixed boundary displacement. This is four times the best energy captured by an ordinary linear spring.

I verified the formulas against direct matrix solutions for 1,000 randomized elastic systems.

### A dimensional comparison

Consider an illustrative 1 mm-long mechanical test region undergoing 20 µm of tensile displacement, with reference spring stiffnesses of 1,000 N/m.

Near the target repair stiffness, the available sensing energy becomes very small.

| Repair stiffness relative to target | Free midpoint difference | Total available energy | Best linear-spring energy |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------- | ------------------------- |
| 0.90                                | 526 nm                   | 135 pJ                 | 33.7 pJ                   |
| 0.95                                | 256 nm                   | 32.5 pJ                | 8.11 pJ                   |
| 0.98                                | 101 nm                   | 5.08 pJ                | 1.27 pJ                   |
| 0.99                                | 50.3 nm                  | 1.26 pJ                | 0.315 pJ                  |

These are calculated from the specified elastic geometry, not measurements of a constructed device.

Now suppose the valve must displace a 100 × 100 µm diaphragm through approximately 2.8 µm against a 1 kPa pressure difference.

The characteristic pressure–volume work is

\\[ W\_{\rm valve}\approx\Delta P A\_{\rm valve}h \approx28\ {\rm pJ}. \\]

At 98% of target stiffness, the comparator contains only about 5.1 pJ of available mechanical energy—roughly one-fifth of this illustrative valve requirement.

A perfect mechanical lever cannot resolve that deficit: it trades displacement against force without creating energy.

This does not rule out smaller valves, different geometries or stronger mechanical excitation. It rules out treating a precise passive comparator as a freely available power source.

The result also explains a conflict with the previous controller. Near completion, the comparator becomes less energetic precisely when the controller needs to make its final switching decision.

## 3. A second failure: hydraulic pressure can corrupt the mechanical decision

Using an external fluid-pressure source solves the actuator-energy shortage, but introduces another problem.

If the pressure-powered valve pushes back on the mechanical comparator, the sensor no longer responds only to repair stiffness.

Let \\(F_b\\) be an unbalanced force from the hydraulic mechanism. The comparator displacement becomes

\\[ \boxed{ \delta=\frac{\delta_0-RF_b}{1+kR}. } \\]

The additional force can imitate mechanical repair.

For the symmetric reference construction used above, the apparent switching stiffness becomes

\\[ \boxed{ d\_{\rm apparent} = \frac{K_0\Delta-3F_b} {K_0\Delta+F_b}, } \\]

where \\(K_0\\) is the reference stiffness scale and \\(\Delta\\) is the imposed tensile displacement.

This is exact for the stated linear mechanical model. I checked it against direct equilibrium calculations with the added force.

| External displacement | Unbalanced valve force | Apparent switching stiffness |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 20 µm                 | 0                      | 1.000                        |
| 20 µm                 | 10 µN                  | 0.998                        |
| 20 µm                 | 100 µN                 | 0.980                        |
| 2 µm                  | 10 µN                  | 0.980                        |
| 2 µm                  | 100 µN                 | 0.810                        |

The intended unperturbed transition is 1.000.

This is a serious limitation for a supposedly load-independent controller. A valve calibrated during substantial deformation may switch at a different apparent stiffness when the same material experiences a smaller external displacement.

### The revised physical interface

The hydraulic source should provide switching power without applying a significant net pressure force to the mechanical comparison element.

One possible construction uses two opposing, pressure-balanced diaphragms. Their static pressure forces cancel, while a differential mechanical displacement biases an independently powered snap-through element.

For a 1 kPa hydraulic supply and a 100 × 100 µm diaphragm, the full pressure force is 10 µN.

Balancing opposing diaphragms to leave no more than 1 µN residual force would restrict the apparent stiffness error to approximately 0.2% in the 2 µm displacement example.

This is a proposed mechanical design criterion, not a demonstrated device.

It also exposes an architectural distinction: the October 2026 fluidic hysteron obtains bistability from flow-induced deformation. Its demonstrated response is controlled by its flow conditions and geometry, not by the midpoint displacement of Astra's elastic comparator.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.aps.org\&sz=32)

Phys. Rev. X



Combining those two components requires a mechanical pilot interface that has both sufficient amplification and sufficiently little feedback force on the comparator. Simply drawing them next to one another does not establish that interface.

## 4. A third obstruction: the living material can clog its own control system

I sized an illustrative nutrient channel using rectangular-channel laminar flow:

\\[ Q\approx \frac{wh^3\Delta P}{12\mu L} \left(1-0.63\frac hw\right). \\]

The example uses a 100 µm-wide, 1 mm-long channel, water-like viscosity, 1 kPa pressure, 10 mM glucose and an assumed cellulose yield of 20% of the glucose-to-cellulose theoretical mass conversion.

To support 5 µg of cellulose per day, glucose conservation requires approximately 15.4 µL of nutrient medium per day.

The corresponding ideal open-channel height is approximately 2.79 µm.

| Channel height | Calculated nutrient flow | Maximum cellulose at assumed yield |
| -------------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| 2 µm           | 5.69 µL/day              | 1.84 µg/day                        |
| 2.5 µm         | 11.07 µL/day             | 3.59 µg/day                        |
| 3 µm           | 19.07 µL/day             | 6.18 µg/day                        |
| 4 µm           | 44.92 µL/day             | 14.57 µg/day                       |

These are optimistic transport calculations, not demonstrated growth rates. They exclude membrane resistance, oxygen limitation, maintenance and other nutrients.

The cubic dependence on channel height creates a serious vulnerability.

Starting from the 2.79 µm channel, a uniform deposit that reduces its height by just 0.58 µm cuts the nutrient flow in half.

For the full 100 µm × 1 mm channel, that deposit corresponds to only about 0.087 µg of dry cellulose if the obstructing volume is fully solid, or approximately 0.0087 µg if the deposit contains 10% cellulose by volume.

The latter is less than 0.2% of the example's desired 5 µg repair output.

A small amount of misplaced biological construction could therefore disable the supply of material needed to complete the repair.

This is not merely hypothetical as a class of failure. Experiments with flowing Komagataeibacter xylinus biofilms observed cellulose-network growth followed by reduction of flow as the microfluidic system became clogged.&#x20;

### A physical repair to the architecture

A much stronger choice is to place the precision valve upstream of, and physically separated from, the cellulose-producing compartment.

The 2025 microfluidic K. xylinus study provides a useful component: its culture and overlying flow chamber were separated by a 0.2 µm polycarbonate membrane, permitting controlled culture and imaging while retaining bacteria in the designated culture region.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov\&sz=32)

PubMed Central (PMC)



That does not prove a 2.79 µm valve can operate reliably or that the membrane provides adequate nutrient flux. The cited device used substantially larger flow rates.

It does, however, establish a credible architecture for keeping the biological constructors out of the delicate upstream flow regulator.

The extra membrane also adds hydraulic resistance. If its effective resistance equals that of the valve in the illustrated design, delivered nutrient flow falls from 15.4 to 7.7 µL/day at fixed pressure—halving the calculated cellulose supply.

The membrane, valve and culture chamber must therefore be designed and measured as one transport system.

## 5. The smallest physically coherent controller now has separate sensing and power paths

The calculations favour a different arrangement from directly connecting the elastic comparator to a nutrient slit.

Mechanical comparison

A reference-and-repair elastic bridge provides a signed displacement indicating whether the repair is below or above a calibrated functional-stiffness threshold.

Pressure-balanced pilot

A mechanically balanced trigger changes the state of a fluidic switching element without requiring the comparator to supply the full valve-opening energy.

Independent hydraulic supply

A pressure source drives nutrient flow through the selected route. A separate bypass maintains hydraulic drive where required for fluidic memory.

Separated culture chamber

A porous membrane keeps cellulose-producing cells out of the precision flow regulator, while delivering nutrients to the construction compartment.

Mechanical reconstruction

Cellulose grows within the damaged region and must become genuinely connected to the load-bearing host. The comparator responds to the resulting stiffness, not the quantity of cellulose secreted.

This is a proposed combination, not an experimentally integrated mechanism.

An important consequence of the October 2026 fluidic-hysteron work is that its bistability depends on hydrodynamic loading and flow redistribution. Cutting off every source of flow could eliminate the mechanism maintaining the switch state. A separately driven pilot or bypass is therefore a meaningful part of the architecture, not just a convenient drawing convention.&#x20;

### Could service motion power the switch instead?

Yes, in principle, if mechanical energy can be harvested and accumulated over repeated loading cycles.

The strain-powered micropump experiments establish an actual route for turning mechanical motion into directed fluid transport.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nature.com\&sz=32)

Microsystems & Nanoengineering



Using the earlier energy calculation, a valve requiring approximately 28 pJ could be operated near 98% repair completion after:

| Assumed energy capture                             | Minimum mechanical loading cycles |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Ideal capture of all available imbalance energy    | 6                                 |
| Ideal capture by the optimized linear-spring probe | 23                                |
| 20% capture of all available imbalance energy      | 28                                |
| 20% capture of optimized linear-spring energy      | 111                               |

This is an optimistic energy-accounting bound. A mechanism to accumulate, store and release that work has not been constructed.

More fundamentally, a material that receives no mechanical excitation cannot harvest this energy. The controller would become dependent on the structure's loading history.

For the current candidate, I would prioritize a pressure-powered, mechanically triggered pilot. The energy-harvesting alternative is relevant where periodic motion is naturally available.

## 6. A viable nutrient channel still needs an independent oxygen supply

The previous flow calculation establishes a glucose-delivery requirement. It does not establish a viable culture.

At 15.4 µL/day of air-equilibrated medium containing an illustrative 0.258 mM dissolved oxygen, the nutrient stream delivers only approximately

\\[ 3.98\ {\rm nmol\ O_2/day}. \\]

Producing 5 µg of cellulose corresponds to approximately

\\[ 30.84\ {\rm nmol/day} \\]

of anhydroglucose units in the final polymer.

Thus the nutrient stream can supply only about

\\[ \boxed{0.129\ {\rm mol\ O_2/mol\ cellulose\ unit}.} \\]

If actual cellular respiration and cellulose manufacture require more oxygen than that, the liquid nutrient stream cannot support the target production on its own.

The relevant oxygen requirement has not been measured for the proposed repair compartment. It cannot be inferred solely from the chemical formula of cellulose.

The table shows the consequences for different hypothetical requirements.

| Assumed oxygen demand per cellulose unit | Maximum cellulose supported by inlet dissolved oxygen |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 0.1 mol/mol                              | 5 µg/day, carbon-limited                              |
| 0.2 mol/mol                              | 3.23 µg/day                                           |
| 0.5 mol/mol                              | 1.29 µg/day                                           |
| 1.0 mol/mol                              | 0.645 µg/day                                          |

The calculated restriction is specific to the inlet's dissolved oxygen; it does not account for oxygen entering through a gas-permeable material or another independent route.

For example, with a hypothetical demand of 0.5 mol/mol, an additional 11.4 nmol oxygen/day is necessary. An idealized 100 µm-thick aqueous diffusion layer with a maintained 0.258 mM oxygen gradient would require approximately 0.026 mm² of exposed area to supply that difference under the stated diffusivity.

This is a physical feasibility calculation, not an engineered oxygen-transfer demonstration.

It suggests that oxygen should be supplied through a distinct interface, instead of requiring the mechanically gated nutrient flow to deliver both carbon and oxygen.

### The culture chemistry introduces another control variable

The October 2026 Komagataeibacter study found that glucose conversion into gluconic acid could lower medium pH and suppress cellulose synthesis. Its higher-yield systems used pH control and carefully regulated feeding, not unlimited sugar availability.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.asm.org\&sz=32)

Applied and Environmental Microbiology



The consequence is that the mechanical controller should not be assigned responsibility for the entire metabolic environment.

A physically coherent construction separates mechanical regulation of the repair-specific feed from oxygen delivery, medium exchange, pH management and continued survival of the producing population.

That is less autonomous than the earlier conceptual sketch, but it avoids conditioning successful repair on an unproved claim that a single nutrient valve automatically maintains the entire biological system.

## 7. A final obstruction: accurate sensing requires sufficient mechanical excitation

The elastic comparator reports a displacement proportional to the external mechanical excitation.

If the material is unloaded, its signal disappears. That cannot be corrected by a better bistable fluidic switch downstream.

I calculated the observation requirements for an illustrative displacement-measurement noise of 50 nm.

At 98% of target stiffness, with a comparator probe optimized for spring-energy transfer:

| Mechanical test displacement | Mechanical signal | Independent observations needed for 90% correct classification across 20 regions |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2 µm                         | 5.05 nm           | 642                                                                              |
| 10 µm                        | 25.3 nm           | 26                                                                               |
| 20 µm                        | 50.5 nm           | 7                                                                                |
| 40 µm                        | 101 nm            | 2                                                                                |

These are exact Gaussian-error calculations under an assumed independent-noise model. No 50 nm noise specification has been measured for the proposed physical controller.

Correlated viscoelastic noise would make repeated observations less informative.

An actively applied mechanical test is an alternative to relying on naturally occurring service loads.

But this introduces another physical cost.

For the illustrative elastic sample, a 20 µm diagnostic displacement requires approximately 20 mN of force and stores about 0.20 µJ of elastic energy.

Applying that force through a 1 mm² diaphragm requires roughly 20 kPa of pressure.

That is much larger than the illustrative 1 kPa nutrient-delivery pressure.

Consequently, the mechanically interrogated version of the system needs either adequate naturally occurring deformation or a separate diagnostic loading mechanism.

The latter is physically credible, but makes comparison with an externally controlled sensor-and-pump system especially important.

### The general physical limitation

Near the mechanical target, the comparator's free displacement vanishes linearly with stiffness error \\(e=1-d\\).

For the symmetric example,

\\[ \delta_0\approx\frac{\Delta e}{4}. \\]

Its available elastic energy therefore vanishes quadratically:

\\[ \boxed{ E\_{\rm available} \approx \frac{K_0\Delta^2e^2}{32}. } \\]

This establishes a useful general constraint:

A passive precision comparator cannot be expected to perform a fixed amount of switching work arbitrarily close to its balance point using only its instantaneous mechanical imbalance.

It needs independent actuation energy, stored energy accumulated over time, or a switching mechanism whose required work also approaches zero.

That is a more fundamental explanation of why the original direct-comparator-to-nutrient-valve construction was incomplete.

## 8. The architecture that survives

The strongest candidate is now a hydraulically powered, mechanically interrogated biological reconstruction system.

The required physical interfaces are:

| Interface                         | Best-supported mechanism                             | Remaining gap                                                 |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mechanical function to signal     | Differential elastic comparator                      | Mechanical excitation, drift and nonlinear calibration        |
| Signal to switching               | Pressure-balanced pilot acting on a fluidic hysteron | Direct coupling and reliable switching not demonstrated       |
| Switching to biological resources | Pressurized nutrient channel                         | Membrane resistance, fouling and low-flow performance         |
| Resources to living synthesis     | Separated Komagataeibacter culture                   | Oxygen, pH, viable-cell persistence and functional deposition |
| Synthesis to useful function      | Old–new cellulose reconstruction                     | Durable mechanical integration remains unproved               |

There is a useful simplification.

The cellular population does not need to measure its own cellulose secretion or genetically encode an exact numerical reward. The material's mechanical response can, in principle, determine whether additional construction resources are delivered.

But that feedback regulates how much useful reconstruction occurs, not automatically which molecular structure should be constructed.

The earlier cooperative-priming and functional-certification results therefore remain relevant. A system with no effective old-to-new load connection can consume nutrients without improving the mechanical signal.

### Strongest equally informed alternative

The appropriate comparator is an externally controlled system with the same cellulose-producing culture, membrane, oxygenation, nutrient supply and mechanical measurements.

It could use an electronic strain sensor and controlled pump instead of the elastic bridge and passive pilot.

The external controller would likely be easier to calibrate, could perform deliberate diagnostic tests, and could monitor the culture's pH and oxygen independently.

The proposed embedded mechanism has no established advantage over that system. Any advantage would need to arise from compactness, decentralization, low-maintenance operation or better adaptation to distributed damage—not superior information about mechanical function.

## 9. Scientific conclusion

The most important progress in this continuation is identifying that the previously assumed feedback-to-growth connection actually contains three distinct physical interfaces:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Mechanical information} \\;\longrightarrow\\; \text{Powered fluidic decision} \\;\longrightarrow\\; \text{Biologically effective resource delivery}. } \\]

The calculations establish several consequential results:

- Mechanical comparison accuracy and available actuation energy deteriorate near the repair target.
- Hydraulic amplification can provide the missing energy, but unbalanced pressure forces can corrupt the original load-independent decision.
- Very small amounts of misplaced biological material can obstruct a micron-scale nutrient valve.
- A culture-separated flow system is supported by existing primary experiments, but its low-flow performance is not established.
- Carbon delivery, oxygen delivery and metabolic pH regulation must be treated as separate operating requirements.
- When mechanical excitation is unavailable, the comparator needs a diagnostic probe or loses its ability to assess repair.

A stronger physical construction has emerged: a differential mechanical pilot, independently pressure-powered switching, a cell-excluding culture interface and separated metabolic support.

This is a more coherent mechanism than assuming that a small elastic displacement directly opens a productive bacterial-growth valve.

It is still a theoretical integration. In particular, no experiment has demonstrated that the proposed hydraulic pilot retains comparator accuracy while switching nutrient flow, or that its delivered nutrients produce mechanically useful old-to-new cellulose reconstruction.

The central experimental discrimination is now clear: a mechanically damaged material should control nutrient delivery according to its actual recovered stiffness, even when external load and fluid pressure vary, and the reconstructed region must demonstrably acquire load-bearing capacity.
