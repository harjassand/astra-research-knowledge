TERMINAL EMS SENSING: A MOTIONAL IDENTIFICATION GATE, NOT A GRAIN SENSOR
2026-10-24. Constructive/obstruction pass on an independently owned opening.

Provenance and purpose
An existing researcher independently chose the question whether the drive
coil of a 7150-Al electromagnetic stirrer could identify a useful reversal
window during solidification. Its original OPENING.md is preserved. The
lead's different contribution is the following literal terminal assay and
the exact representation boundary it exposes. Both retain extensive prior
inverse-problem/acquisition exposure. No apparatus, melt, calibration data,
casting test, useful grain prediction or new sensor has been acquired.

1. A one-mode equivalent circuit with a literal output

Use positive electrical carrier frequency omega and two spatial phase
sequences m=+1,-1. A rigid conducting rotor has angular speed Omega with
|Omega|<omega, resistance R>0, self-inductance L>0 and real mutual
inductance M!=0. These parameters are common to both probes at the same
frozen state. Write s_m=omega-m Omega. With RMS complex stator current I_m,

 (R+i s_m L) j_m=-i s_m M I_m,
 W_m=i omega M j_m/I_m
    =omega s_m M^2/(R+i s_m L).                          (I1)

W_m is the reflected load after subtracting the common stator/background
impedance Z_c from measured Z_m. A physical coupled-inductor model also
requires positive magnetic stored energy, e.g. L_s L>M^2 for stator
self-inductance L_s, and nonnegative stator resistance. The load formula
does not certify those hardware premises.

For ONE sequence at this fixed carrier,

 1/W_m=R/(omega M^2 s_m)+i L/(omega M^2).                (I2)

Thus even with fixed known L,M, speed and rotor resistance are exactly
confounded: replace s_m by any positive s'_m and R by R s'_m/s_m.
The reflected load is unchanged. Cooling or solid fraction may affect
resistance, so an unexplained single-tone change cannot be interpreted
as a change of speed. This is not a no-go for broadband/other controls.

For a paired measurement of the SAME rotor state with calibrated Z_c,
let a_m=Re(1/W_m). Then a_+,a_->0 and

 Omega=omega (a_+-a_-)/(a_++a_-).                        (I3)

Unknown R,L,M cancel. The equality Im(1/W_+)=Im(1/W_-)
is a necessary model-consistency check, not a sufficient validation of
the one-mode representation. Neither a source clock nor a noiseless
parameter/impedance oracle is being inferred from routine EMS telemetry.

This is a concrete identification operation under a model, rather than
a static impedance-to-grain correlation. Terminal-only speed estimation
is already conventional induction-machine practice; no novelty or total
cost advantage is inferred from the cancellation in (I3).

2. What calibration and finite readout must supply

Given complex estimates What_m with certified errors delta_m<|What_m|,
the actual a_m lies within the real interval centered at Re(1/What_m)
of radius

 e_m=delta_m/[|What_m|(|What_m|-delta_m)].                (I4)

The errors must include subtracting Z_c, current/voltage phase and gain,
drift between probes, finite electrical settling, and any state change
during readout. If the resulting [l_m,u_m] have l_m>0, monotonicity in
(I3) gives the rigorous speed interval

 [ omega(l_+-u_-)/(l_++u_-),
   omega(u_+-l_-)/(u_++l_-) ].                           (I5)

Otherwise this promised positive-slip algorithm abstains. Conditioning
deteriorates as reflected load or a_++a_- becomes small. Certified
intervals are supplied/calibrated inputs; no shot count or acquisition
cost is claimed before a detector/noise/bandwidth model is specified.

Subtracting an arbitrary UNKNOWN common complex Z_c does not cancel
inside the inverse in (I3). Pair differencing alone is not the same
estimator. Indeed, with A=M^2/R and tau=L/R,

 W_+-W_-=-2 omega A Omega /
 [1-tau^2(omega^2-Omega^2)+2i tau omega].                 (I6)

Two real difference data generically leave a continuum of positive
(A,tau,Omega) fits; adding the correspondingly changed common background
preserves the full measured pair. No uncalibrated-background speed
magnitude claim follows from (I3). Extra frequencies or independently
measured background would be extra information, not a contradiction.

3. A broader reciprocity witness, with a weaker output

In two real stator coordinates (for example a calibrated alpha/beta
transform of a three-phase winding), let V=Z I at one frozen linear
state. For circular e_+=(1,i)/sqrt(2), e_-=(1,-i)/sqrt(2),

 e_+^dagger Z e_+ - e_-^dagger Z e_-
        =i(Z_12-Z_21).                                 (I7)

A stationary linear reciprocal electromagnetic medium has Z=Z^T, so
the difference vanishes even with a spatially anisotropic symmetric
conductivity tensor or asymmetric geometry. This suggests measuring motional
nonreciprocity rather than assigning every loading change to motion.
A nonzero difference with a covered instrument/reciprocity error can
reject that reciprocal frozen-state null. It does NOT certify every
flow is detectable, identify a velocity field, or separate motion from
other nonreciprocal elements such as biased/hysteretic cores or active
electronics. Those apparatus alternatives must be excluded or bounded.

Sequential CW and CCW values from a drifting/cooling/moving state are
not automatically the two entries of one frozen Z. A general 2x2 Z
needs two independent input patterns or equivalent coded acquisition;
one phasor input does not determine it. Simultaneous sequence-resolved
identification requires suitable independent phase-current control and
readout. A relay that only swaps two AC phases does not by itself
supply that stronger operation. All settling, drift and demodulation
errors must enter (I4) or the null-witness error budget.

There is an exact symmetry blind class even for this weaker motion
witness. Suppose the vessel, scalar conductivity/permeability and the
prescribed frozen flow are axisymmetric. The two stator source/readout
mode patterns must form a matched real planar representation of
azimuthal rotations (a suitable dihedral doublet also suffices). Assume
the linear electromagnetic boundary-value problem is uniquely solvable.
For a no-swirl poloidal flow u=u_r(r,z)e_r+u_z(r,z)e_z, the whole physical
configuration also has meridional-reflection symmetry. Thus Z commutes
with planar rotations, so Z=aI+bJ, J=[[0,-1],[1,0]]. A meridional
reflection conjugates J to -J and forces b=0. This remains true with
complex frequency response. Magnetic field/winding moments transform
as axial vectors under reflection; their planar reflection matrix may
have the opposite overall sign, which gives the same conjugation.
Therefore (I7) is exactly zero although the flow can have nonzero strain.

A smooth finite example with no slip on a cylinder 0<=r<=R0,0<=z<=H is

 psi=A r^2(R0^2-r^2)^2 z^2(H-z)^2,
 u_r=-(partial_z psi)/r, u_z=(partial_r psi)/r, u_phi=0. (I7a)

The limits at r=0 are smooth in Cartesian coordinates; divergence is
zero and all velocity components vanish on the outer cylinder/endcaps.
For A!=0 this is nonzero recirculation, with finite positive viscous
dissipation. Maintaining it requires a paid mechanical/body-force input;
the example does not supply that input from the stated EMS phase relay.
It establishes a literal blind class for the differential witness, not
that the ENTIRE common impedance equals its no-flow value. Nor does it
prove that this flow is reachable in the original casting protocol.
A narrower verified reachable-flow prior could exclude the example.
Absent such a prior, nonreciprocity is not a general flow/strain assay.

4. The diagnostic actuation is paid

For (I1), real reflected electrical power, rotor Joule heat and torque
are, with RMS phasors,

 P_m=|I_m|^2 Re W_m,
 Q_m=R|j_m|^2=(s_m/omega)P_m,
 T_m=(m/omega)P_m,
 P_mech,m=Omega T_m,
 P_m=Q_m+P_mech,m.                                     (I8)

Actual terminal power additionally includes stator/cable/inverter
losses and transient stored energy. The electrical probe exerts torque
and heats the conductor. Its impedance estimate cannot be treated as
a free, nondisturbing observation.

In the one-mode model, simultaneously arranging P_+=P_- cancels net
electromagnetic torque while leaving positive rotor heating Q_++Q_-.
This does not supply a frozen-state promise in a real melt: temperature,
solid fraction, local forces and nonmodal motion can still change, and
the amplitudes needed for balance must themselves be measured/controlled.
Sequential equal-energy probes also change the intervening state; they
are not automatically two evaluations at the same Omega. No arbitrarily
weak probe is assumed to retain finite precision at no extra time.

5. The unclosed bridge in the actual owned question

A mushy solidifying alloy is not one conducting rigid rotor. It has
spatially varying conductivity, temperature, solid fraction and distinct
solid/liquid velocities. Reflected loads can sum distributed modes;
inverting that sum does not preserve (I2). A good one-mode fit would
not establish local shear, relative solid/liquid motion, fragment
generation/survival, or the final coarse-grain tail. Even ordinary
rotation need not imply the strain relevant to fragmentation.

Existing contactless inductive flow tomography is a stronger conventional
observer, but uses additional excitation directions and external
magnetic sensors. It does not license a terminal-only distributed
inverse map. Conversely, a supplied correct distributed constitutive
and flow model might support a richer estimator; this file proves no
universal terminal-sensing impossibility.

The original proposal remains CONDITIONAL/UNVALIDATED. The concrete
operation above breaks the conductivity-speed ambiguity only within
its calibrated frozen one-mode interface. The remaining consequential
gate is a justified observable/task relation for the actual distributed
mushy state, with diagnostic torque/heating, finite readout and the
fixed timer/temperature-stop incumbent matched. There is no newly
established grain-refinement capability, and no casting experiment or
sensor build is authorized by this analytical note.
