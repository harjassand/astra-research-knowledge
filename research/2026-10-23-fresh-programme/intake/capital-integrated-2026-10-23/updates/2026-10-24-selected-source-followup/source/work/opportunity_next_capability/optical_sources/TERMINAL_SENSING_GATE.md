# Narrow comparator gate: terminal loading versus flow sensing

## Verdict

The parent’s opposite-helicity terminal-loading identity is a plausible exact result for its stated single-rotor-mode equivalent circuit, but terminal-only rotor-speed estimation is already an established operation in induction-machine control. The targeted primary search did not establish that this exact parameter-canceling paired-sequence formula is already published; absence from this bounded search is not a novelty result.

More importantly, neither terminal-only motor speed estimation nor the closest CIFT experiments supply the 7150-Al endpoint in the opening. They estimate a rigid rotor's speed or reconstruct a liquid-metal velocity field. They do not observe dendrite fragmentation, solid/liquid slip, surviving fragment density, or a useful mushy-state window.

## Closest primary comparators

1. **Terminal voltage/current to rotor speed:** Zamora, García-Cerrada, and Zazo, “Rotor-speed estimator for induction motors using voltage and current measurements,” *Control Engineering Practice* 6 (1998), DOI [10.1016/S0967-0661(97)00022-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0967-0661(97)00022-1). The primary paper's accessible institutional record says it estimates rotor speed from stator voltage/current only, analyzes speed identifiability by operating point and sensitivity to motor-parameter and measurement errors, and validates by simulation and field tests. Its implementation computes rotor speed, slip frequency, and rotor-flux frequency using an induction-motor model plus filtering and recursive total least squares; the publisher abstract says model parameter sensitivity is explicitly analyzed. This is a close prior for terminal-only speed sensing, but the paper does not claim a model-free opposite-sequence invariant or conductivity-robust operation.

2. **Flow in a casting model with actuator variation:** Glavinić et al., “Contactless Inductive Flow Tomography for Real-Time Control of Electromagnetic Actuators in Metal Casting,” *Sensors* 22 (2022), DOI [10.3390/s22114155](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22114155), [primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9185332/). In an isothermal room-temperature GaInSn mini-caster, the actual measurement interface is two separate excitation coils plus fourteen external gradiometric receiver coils; CIFT measures flow-induced magnetic-field perturbations and reconstructs velocity. The paper develops real-time reconstruction and compensation for EMBr-induced magnetic hysteresis. It reports a few-mm agreement for jet-impingement position when reconstructing the same simulated velocity field; it also points to an earlier simple EMBr control loop for an artificial disturbance, using two predetermined EMBr current values. It does **not** measure rotor terminal loading, mushy-alloy motion, or fragmentation.

3. **Two-field excitation for full liquid-metal flow:** Sieger et al., “Two-Field Excitation for Contactless Inductive Flow Tomography,” *Sensors* 24 (2024), DOI [10.3390/s24144458](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24144458), [primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11280805/). A propeller-driven, stationary GaInSn flow in a 230-mm-diameter vessel is measured with fourteen external magnetic sensors and two independent orthogonal Helmholtz-like excitation field pairs. The authors obtain reconstructed average flow near 12 mm/s (maximum 40 mm/s); multiplexing gives 3 s temporal resolution and simultaneous 1/2-Hz fields give 1 s. Their forward relation has induced field proportional to conductivity and to an integral of the velocity cross excitation field, with an assumed homogeneous conductivity; multiple field directions are needed for general 3D reconstruction. This is flow tomography with independent sensors and excitation, not the terminal-only scalar of the rotor model.

## Exact transfer boundary for 7150 Al

The cancellation in the parent model requires paired measurements to share the same rotor-mode structure and common resistance, inductance, and mutual-coupling parameters, with one angular velocity entering the positive/negative slip terms. That is a legitimate contract for an induction machine's conducting rotor. A solidifying alloy is instead a spatially distributed liquid-plus-solid system: conductivity, temperature, geometry, velocity field, and solid fraction vary through the volume; the solid phase and remaining liquid need not share one angular speed; reversing the stirring field changes the flow and can change those internal parameters between measurements. A scalar terminal impedance could, at best, estimate a weighted aggregate unless a separate identifiability result establishes more.

For the 7150 target, Li et al. (2018; [DOI 10.2320/matertrans.M2017357](https://doi.org/10.2320/matertrans.M2017357)) invoke a resistivity contrast between primary Al solid solution and residual liquid to motivate faster solid motion, and discuss drag reducing the relative-velocity difference over longer reversal intervals. This is a proposed mechanism supported by a simplified calculation, not a direct measurement of solid/liquid velocity. Their measured outcome is post-solidification grain size/homogeneity. Thus the load observable needed for this controller is not merely bulk angular velocity: it must predict the state and relative solid/liquid motion that determine fragmentation/transport and the coarse-grain tail. CIFT shows that richer liquid flow is observable, but only with independent coils/sensors and with a homogeneous-conductivity model; it supplies no measurement bridge through the mushy zone or from velocity field to dendrite fragmentation.

**Decision:** Preserve the opening only as a conditional, unproven hypothesis. If pursuing it, first demonstrate the paired terminal assay on a rigid conducting rotor with independently measured speed, then separately test whether the exact melt terminal signal predicts the 7150 fragment-relevant window on held-out casts. Do not treat the rotor identity as already a flow or grain-quality sensor, and do not claim the general terminal-speed task is new.
