# Finite registry, conserved composition, and variation retention

24 October 2026. Frozen after internal consequential read by the lead/root, with bounded independent algebra, energy, transport, and primary-source checks. Accepted scope: the stated periodic continuous-valued continuum/finite-lattice energies, all-slip sufficient calibrations, deterministic negative-full-energy variation retention, and the separate fixed-curvature counterfamily. This is not external certification, a novelty conclusion, an acquired material, or a device advantage. No selected wavelength, thermal pathwise lifetime, or atomistic free-energy equivalence is asserted.

## 1. Scientific obstruction and the selected representation

A harmonic relative-displacement penalty has unlimited work capacity. A real registry interface can instead slip by one period and return to the same local potential minimum. Consequently its small-signal stiffness alone cannot justify the harmonic model's finite-amplitude exclusion of macroscopic composition separation. Composition can also change inside a slip core; a homogeneous mismatch threshold alone does not address that possibility.

The selected operation is conservative local transfer of a neutral composition field whose value changes a bond's natural length, coupled to a finite periodic registry interface. We supply an exact local energy and deterministic passive transport/mechanics, and ask whether a negative full-energy state must retain a nonzero density of composition variation even when the registry can slip. The answer below is sufficient and quantitative at that literal model scope. It is not a no-slip statement.

Permanent harmonic crosslinks preserve the previous screened-kernel model but do not answer finite registry capacity. Irreversible interface damage changes both topology and energy bookkeeping. Periodic registry is the selected distinct mechanism because it admits slips while retaining a conservative energy, so both the protective regime and a matched failure can be proved in one setting.

The strongest ancestry is already direct. Patrykiejew and Sokolowski's binary Ar/Kr Frenkel--Kontorova model has species-dependent natural bond lengths, harmonic nearest-neighbor springs, periodic substrate corrugation, and walls; their two-dimensional canonical Monte Carlo also exchanges atom identities. Pokrovsky--Talapov theory supplies commensurate--incommensurate mismatch and soliton ancestry. Neither ancestry licenses identifying the displayed quartic composition energy with an exact Ar/Kr free energy. The owned contribution here is the variable-composition calibration and its finite-lattice sufficient extension, with its failure discriminator, not a new registry material principle.

## 2. One complete specimen contract

Let the specimen be a one-dimensional periodic ring of length L. Composition phi is real, conserved, and has mean zero. It denotes a continuous mesoscopic deviation per reference cell; physically bounded occupancies would require an admissible interval or a measured chemical free energy. Displacements u and v are unwrapped, periodic, and have zero mean strain. A common rigid translation is immaterial. We retain the same fixed length and composition when comparing patterns and macroscopic domains.

At fixed parameters, take

\[
F=\int_0^L\left[
 f(\phi)+\frac\kappa2\phi_x^2
 +\frac C2(u_x-\alpha\phi)^2+\frac D2v_x^2+V(u-v)
\right]dx,\qquad
 f(\phi)=\frac a2\phi^2+\frac b4\phi^4,
\tag{1}
\]

with b,kappa,C,D,V0,d positive, alpha nonzero, and

\[
V(w)=V_0[1-\cos(\beta w)],\qquad \beta=2\pi/d,
\qquad K=V''(0)=\beta^2V_0.
\tag{2}
\]

Set

\[
S=\frac{CD}{C+D},\quad w=u-v,\quad r=\frac{Cu+Dv}{C+D}.
\]

The exact local identity is

\[
\frac C2(u_x-\alpha\phi)^2+\frac D2v_x^2
=\frac{C+D}{2}\left(r_x-\frac{C\alpha}{C+D}\phi\right)^2
 +\frac S2(w_x-\alpha\phi)^2.
\tag{3}
\]

Because mean phi is zero, the first square can be relaxed to zero by a periodic r. It remains nonnegative for nonrelaxed full dynamics. Thus every lower bound below applies to the actual full F, not only to mechanically minimized states.

The physical contract is periodic zero winding for w. Some calibration statements also hold for w(L)-w(0) equal to an integer multiple of d, because their corrector is d-periodic. That algebraic extension does not silently change the specimen's admissible winding or supply boundary atoms.

## 3. Linear instability and a genuinely nonlinear negative trial

The second variation about phi=u=v=0 agrees with the harmonic relative penalty K w^2/2. For each nonzero allowed Fourier mode,

\[
\lambda(k)=a+\kappa k^2+\frac{E_0}{1+\xi^2 k^2},\qquad
E_0=S\alpha^2,\quad \xi^2=S/K.
\tag{4}
\]

The exact uniform sector is excluded by mean phi=0; it is not identified with the k-down-to-zero limit. The fixed-length uniform composition sector, if separately allowed, has a different common-strain contribution.

Suppose a+E0>0 but an allowed nonzero mode has lambda=-nu<0. This is only a linear instability statement until a finite-amplitude energy comparison is made. For phi=A cos(kx), with the harmonic mechanical minimizer, the harmonic density is

\[
 -\frac\nu4 A^2+\frac{3b}{32}A^4.
\]

At A^2=4nu/(3b) this is -nu^2/(6b). Since

\[
1-\cos z\le z^2/2,
\tag{5}
\]

the same actual phi,u,v trial has periodic-registry energy no larger than its harmonic energy. The negative trial therefore survives in (1), with slips neither excluded nor assigned an infinite penalty.

Repeating a permitted trial cell gives the same negative density on larger commensurate rings with the same mean composition, mean strain, and material parameters. This is the comparison used for thermodynamic-size statements. It is not a dilation that changes the specimen boundary contract.

## 4. The direct continuum calibration allows arbitrary slip cores

Define

\[
q(w)=\sqrt{2S V(w)}=2\sqrt{S V_0}|\sin(\pi w/d)|,
\qquad
m_c=\frac{1}{Sd}\int_0^d q(w)dw
=\frac4\pi\sqrt{\frac{V_0}{S}}.
\tag{6}
\]

The familiar isolated continuum kink energy is Ek=S d mc=4d sqrt(S V0)/pi. Here mc will enter a pointwise calibration; we do not infer a variable-composition no-slip law from the homogeneous kink threshold.

The function q is globally Lipschitz. Let R be any bounded d-periodic primitive of

\[
R'(w)=q(w)-S m_c.
\tag{7}
\]

For a scalar mismatch m, write Em=S(wx-m)^2/2+V(w). The exact identity

\[
E_m-\frac S2m^2-\frac m{m_c}R'(w)w_x
=\frac S2\left(w_x-\frac{m q(w)}{S m_c}\right)^2
 +\left(1-\frac{m^2}{m_c^2}\right)V(w)
\tag{8}
\]

is nonnegative whenever |m|<=mc. No restriction is placed on wx or on the number, direction, or core composition of slips.

Assume the strict protective conditions

\[
a<0,\qquad a+S\alpha^2>0,\qquad
|\alpha|c_f<m_c,\qquad c_f=\sqrt{-2a/b}.
\tag{9}
\]

Choose a smooth cutoff chi(phi) between zero and one, equal to one on the whole interval |phi|<=cf, with compact support inside |alpha phi|<mc. Such a cutoff exists by strictness. Define

\[
B(\phi,w)=\frac{\alpha\phi\chi(\phi)}{m_c}R(w),\qquad
C_*:=\sup_{\phi,w}|B_\phi(\phi,w)|<\infty.
\tag{10}
\]

Since Em>=chi Em, (8) can be applied only on the cutoff support. Also

\[
f(\phi)+\chi(\phi)\frac{S\alpha^2\phi^2}{2}\ge0:
\tag{11}
\]

where f<0 the cutoff is one and a+S alpha^2>0; where f>=0 the added term is nonnegative. Using m=alpha phi and

\[
\frac{\alpha\phi\chi}{m_c}R'(w)w_x
=\partial_xB-B_\phi\phi_x,
\]

periodic integration cancels the B boundary term. Thus, for every admissible phi,u,v,

\[
\boxed{\quad F\ge\frac\kappa2\int_0^L\phi_x^2dx
 -C_*\int_0^L|\phi_x|dx.\quad}
\tag{12}
\]

The one-dimensional H1 chain rule suffices: q's cusps leave R continuously differentiable with Lipschitz derivative. The proof makes no global amplitude bound on phi; the cutoff turns off the calibration where f is already nonnegative. It permits decoration of every slip core by arbitrary phi.

Consequently every sequence with total variation TV(phi)/L tending to zero has nonnegative limiting lower energy density. This includes bounded-contrast macroscopic domains with a subextensive number of composition interfaces, even if their interiors have arbitrarily many registry slips. A negative-density trial excludes complete coarsening in this precise sense. It does not identify a unique periodic ground state or require a particular morphology.

## 5. Exact on-site lattice extension: no continuum kink cost is imported

For lattice spacing h, nodes i modulo N, L=Nh, and D_h z_i=(z_(i+1)-z_i)/h, define the exact local finite-dimensional model

\[
F_h=h\sum_i\left[
 f(\phi_i)+\frac\kappa2(D_h\phi_i)^2
 +\frac C2(D_hu_i-\alpha\phi_i)^2
 +\frac D2(D_hv_i)^2+V(u_i-v_i)
\right].
\tag{13}
\]

Composition is associated with a bond cell, while the two mechanical positions are node coordinates. This is a legitimate conservative nearest-neighbor mesoscopic Hamiltonian. It is not asserted to be the exact free energy of a binary atomic chain. Equation (3) is an exact discrete identity as well, with derivatives replaced by D_h.

An on-site atomic potential can be skipped between lattice sites by a large hop; hence Ek in (6) is not the discrete kink energy. Instead use the global bound

\[
L_q=\operatorname{Lip}q=\beta\sqrt{S V_0}=\sqrt{S K},\qquad
|R(w')-R(w)-R'(w)(w'-w)|\le\frac{L_q}{2}(w'-w)^2.
\tag{14}
\]

For Delta=w'-w, r_m=|m|/mc, and

\[
E_i=\frac S{2h}(\Delta-hm)^2+hV(w),\qquad
S_{\rm eff}=S-h r_mL_q,
\]

(14) gives

\[
\begin{split}
E_i-\frac{hS}{2}m^2-\frac m{m_c}[R(w')-R(w)]
&\ge\frac{S_{\rm eff}}{2h}\Delta^2
 -\frac{m}{m_c}q(w)\Delta+hV(w)\\
&=\frac{S_{\rm eff}}{2h}
 \left(\Delta-\frac{h m q(w)}{m_c S_{\rm eff}}\right)^2
 +hV(w)\left(1-\frac{r_m^2S}{S_{\rm eff}}\right).
\end{split}
\tag{15}
\]

In particular this is nonnegative for every Delta, including a jump across whole registry periods, if

\[
r_m^2+\nu_h r_m\le1,\qquad
\nu_h=\frac{hL_q}{S}=\frac h\xi,\qquad
r_h=\frac{\sqrt{\nu_h^2+4}-\nu_h}{2},\qquad r_m\le r_h.
\tag{16}
\]

S_eff is positive throughout this set. The value mc r_h is a conservative sufficient mismatch window, not the exact discrete commensurate--incommensurate threshold. It tends to mc as h/xi tends to zero.

For the exact lattice theorem replace the amplitude condition in (9) by

\[
|\alpha|c_f<m_c r_h.
\tag{17}
\]

Choose chi equal to one on f<0 and supported strictly inside this narrower interval. With A(phi)=alpha phi chi(phi)/mc, multiply (15) by chi_i and sum. The only corrector sum is

\[
\sum_i A(\phi_i)(R(w_{i+1})-R(w_i))
=\sum_i[A(\phi_{i-1})-A(\phi_i)]R(w_i).
\]

It is bounded below by -C_* sum_i |phi_(i+1)-phi_i|, where C_*=||R||_infinity Lip(A). Therefore

\[
\boxed{\quad F_h\ge\frac{\kappa}{2h}\sum_i
 (\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i)^2
 -C_*\sum_i|\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i|.\quad}
\tag{18}
\]

This is an exact finite-lattice result, not a small-hop expansion of its states. Taylor's inequality was global, and all finite slips remain admissible and paid by (13).

The lattice linear symbol is (4) with k^2 replaced by q_h=4 sin^2(kh/2)/h^2. An allowed negative mode provides the negative trial of Section 3, provided its sampled cosine has mean square 1/2 and fourth moment 3/8. If 2k or 4k aliases the lattice reciprocal period, use its actual moments; do not copy the continuum constants. Repeating an unaliased permitted cell supplies a fixed negative density on commensurate longer rings.

The sufficient nonlinear window is not empty merely by incompatibility with the finite-k instability: the latter is independent of b, while choosing sufficiently large positive b reduces cf. Once chosen, b stays fixed in the retention dynamics. This is an existence observation, not a material-parameter fit or a parameter search.

## 6. Retention under actual finite-rate passive dynamics

For either (1) or (13), retain the full mechanical variables. In the lattice define mu_i=h^(-1) partial F_h/partial phi_i, eta_ui=h^(-1) partial F_h/partial u_i, and eta_vi similarly. A compatible local deterministic law is

\[
\dot\phi_i=M\Delta_h\mu_i,\qquad
\Gamma_u\dot u_i=-\eta_{ui},\qquad
\Gamma_v\dot v_i=-\eta_{vi},\qquad M,\Gamma_u,\Gamma_v>0.
\tag{19}
\]

It conserves h sum phi_i and periodic winding. Discrete summation by parts gives

\[
\dot F_h=-Mh\sum_i(D_h\mu_i)^2
 -h\sum_i(\Gamma_u\dot u_i^2+\Gamma_v\dot v_i^2)\le0.
\tag{20}
\]

Thus, once the actual full state has F_h/L<=-q0<0, (18) implies at every later deterministic time

\[
\boxed{\quad \frac1L\sum_i|\phi_{i+1}-\phi_i|\ge q_0/C_*.\quad}
\tag{21}
\]

The continuum version follows from (12) and its analogous conserved/overdamped dissipation law. No instantaneous mechanical elimination or convergence to a global minimum is needed. This is composition variation-density retention, not the harmonic model's finite Fourier-band theorem. It is conditional on the declared fixed-parameter negative full-energy state. It neither proves generic formation from an exactly uniform deterministic state nor guarantees a finite temperature trajectory remains on that sublevel.

Preparation must supply the initial mixed state, any composition-conserving seed and mechanical controls, and the chosen thermodynamic parameter change. The exact fixed-temperature work identity is Fdot=W_param+W_external-D. For example the a, V0 and d contributions are

\[
W_{\rm param}=\dot a\int\phi^2/2
 +\dot V_0\int[1-\cos(2\pi w/d)]
 -\dot d\int\frac{2\pi V_0 w}{d^2}\sin(2\pi w/d),
\tag{22}
\]

with integrals replaced by h sums on the lattice; C,D,alpha,kappa changes have their own derivatives. Changing d physically can alter reference commensurability and boundaries, so (22) by itself is only a parameter-energy identity. A thermal quench additionally requires the heat/entropy balance. Resetting a conserved pattern or winding, substrate/support, and parameters is not free and is not supplied by retention alone.

## 7. Matched failure: identical K is insufficient

Keep S, alpha, a, b, kappa and K fixed, but change d and V0 together through V0=K d^2/(4pi^2). Every small-signal symbol (4), including its lattice version, stays unchanged. However mc and mc r_h scale linearly with d while r_h depends only on h/xi. The protective amplitude condition therefore eventually fails as d tends to zero.

There is an explicit same-contract macroscopic competitor. Choose balanced domains phi approximately plus/minus sqrt(-a/b), with two fixed-width transitions, so mean phi is zero. Choose wx=alpha phi and rx=C alpha phi/(C+D); both displacements are periodic. Mechanical strain energy vanishes. Since 0<=V<=2V0,

\[
\limsup_{L\to\infty}F/L\le -\frac{a^2}{4b}+2V_0.
\tag{23}
\]

The exact lattice analogue solves D_h w_i=alpha phi_i and the corresponding common strain on a mean-zero ring. Its finite number of composition interfaces has vanishing density, and the same bound holds. For sufficiently small d the right side is negative, tending to the chemical bulk minimum as d tends to zero. This disproves any universal nonlinear coarsening exclusion deduced from K and the finite-k linear symbol alone. It does not prove a unique coarsened ground state or a coarsening rate.

The comparison changes the registry period and barrier, so it is not an assertion of a free tunable material operation or equal fabrication cost. It identifies the independent nonlinear input that the harmonic representation omitted.

## 8. Physical compatibility and what remains unacquired

The local phi-to-natural-length coupling is conservative: changing composition changes the same bond energy whose derivative drives transport. It is not a separately commanded eigenstrain that can do uncharged work. Detailed-balance nearest-neighbor species exchanges are a microscopic ancestry, but their coarse-grained chemical potential, mobility, noise, and admissible composition interval have not been derived for a real material here. Equation (19) is an exact model operation, not a measured atomic rate law.

The substrate or second network takes reaction forces and belongs in the specimen inventory. In an FK interpretation, w is unwrapped; changing it by d at one node changes adjacent bond energies. Large slips do not grant free relabeling of neighbors, atom creation, fracture, or defect annihilation. A rigid substrate limit and two compliant networks are different material realizations even when their reduced scalar energies agree. Charge compensation, vacancy creation, layer detachment, creep, and finite-strain constitutive changes need additional variables and costs if present.

Actual mixed adsorbates can have species-dependent spring constants and registry barriers. A composition-uniform lower envelope of their mechanical/registry energies would be sufficient to reuse this calibration, but that envelope and the chemical-energy conditions must be established, not assumed. The quoted source's parameter choices are simulation inputs and its observed structures are registry walls with segregation; they do not establish a measured chemical binodal or the inequality (17).

The result supplies an exact nonlinear discriminator and passive model retention operation beyond the harmonic screened kernel. It does not supply an actual material with these coefficients, a transport/phase-slip timescale, a thermal lifetime, a selected wavelength, a full return-cycle advantage, or novelty clearance.
