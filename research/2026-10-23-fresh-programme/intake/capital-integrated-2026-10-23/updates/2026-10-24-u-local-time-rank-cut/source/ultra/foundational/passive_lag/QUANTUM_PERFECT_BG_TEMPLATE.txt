Main point

Small k-by-k answer-graph rectangles, even together with the global
density used for classical slow repetition, do NOT force an entangled
or commuting base gap. The construction below plants an actual finite
perfect magic-square strategy while retaining those graph properties
with fixed positive exponents. This goes beyond a perfect level-one SDP.

The exponents below are ADJUSTED positive constants. No claim is made
that the original BG numerical exponents .2/.1 are attained. The typical
UNPLANTED random graph and the general low-information selection gate
remain unresolved. This is not a slow-repetition counterexample with a
positive quantum base gap: the constructed quantum base value is ONE.

1. Exact statement
-------------------
There is an absolute constant c>0 such that for every sufficiently large
m divisible by 3, with k=3^m, r=k^(1/3), N=k^r, there is a bipartite graph
Gamma on [k]^r by [k]^r satisfying

 |E(Gamma)| >= N^2/(2 k^(2c)),
 e_Gamma(S,T) <= k^(2-c) whenever |S|,|T|<=k.              (P1)

Use the BG coordinate-block game: x,y are independent uniform [k];
answers are (s,ell),(t,ell') in [k]^r times [r]; win iff
 ell=ell', s_ell=x, t_ell=y, (s,t) is an edge of Gamma.
Then

 omega_class(Gamma_game) <= k^(-c/2),
 omega_class(Gamma_game^r) >=1/(2k^(2c)),
 omega_*(Gamma_game)=omega_co(Gamma_game)=1.               (P2)

Every ingredient below is finite. The existence of the random background
does not give an efficient graph generator or graph-description bound.

2. A finite perfect-quantum graph with no large complete rectangle
------------------------------------------------------------------
Let L consist of (x,a), x in {0,1,2}, a in {+1,-1}^3 with product(a)=+1.
Let R consist of (y,b), y in {0,1,2}, with product(b)=beta_y, where
 beta=(+1,+1,-1).
Join (x,a) to (y,b) iff a_y=b_x. Its 12-by-12 adjacency matrix is W.
Every vertex has degree 6. Its largest complete biclique AREA is

              B=max{|S||T|: S times T is in E(W)}=6.      (P3)

Direct proof: if S uses at most one answer at each input x, then |S|<=3.
One vertex has six neighbors; two vertices at distinct inputs have
exactly three common neighbors (one parity-completed answer per y).
Three vertices at distinct inputs have at most two common neighbors:
three would supply a 3-by-3 sign matrix with all row products + and
column products +,+,-, which is impossible.

If S contains two distinct answers at the same input, they agree at
only one of the three cells. Hence every common neighbor lies at one
Bob input y. If there is exactly one common neighbor, |S|<=6 by its
degree. If there are at least two distinct common neighbors at that y,
their answers agree at only one Alice input, forcing S to that input
with at most two answers, and there are at most two common neighbors.
Three distinct valid answers at a fixed input have no common neighbor.
Thus this case has area <=6 as well. A singleton and its six neighbors
attain 6. No SDP or quantum assumption enters this graph fact.

The actual perfect strategy uses |Phi_4>=(1/2)sum_j |j,j> and the grid

      Z tensor I     I tensor Z     Z tensor Z
      I tensor X     X tensor I     X tensor X
      Z tensor X     X tensor Z     Y tensor Y .

The three observables commute within each row and column. Row products
are +I,+I,+I; column products are +I,+I,-I. Alice measures the requested
row; Bob measures the transposes of the requested column. The matching
cell has equal signs with certainty by the maximally entangled identity.
All displayed two-qubit matrices are transpose symmetric, including YY.
This is a fixed finite strategy on two Bell pairs, with winning support W.

3. Endpoint entropy controls a tensor-compatible rectangle norm
---------------------------------------------------------------
Use uniform probability measure pi on 12 points and the Markov operator
 T=W/6. For 1<=p<=2 let p'=p/(p-1) (p'=infinity at p=1), and define

 C(p)=||T||_(L_p(pi) -> L_p'(pi)).

Positivity permits nonnegative test vectors. By homogeneity we may take
E_pi f=E_pi g=1 and maximize
 M(f,g)/(||f||_p ||g||_p),
 M(f,g)=E_pi f T g=(1/72)sum_edges f_s g_t.                (P4)
The set of such density pairs is compact. At p=1, M<=2, with equality
EXACTLY when supp(f) times supp(g) is contained in the winning graph.
Consequently C(1)=2.

Write Ent_pi(f)=E_pi f ln f, with 0 ln0=0. Uniformly on the compact
density simplex,
 ln||f||_(1+h)=h Ent_pi(f)+o(h) as h decreases to zero.   (P5)
This follows by differentiating (1/p)ln E f^p; f^p ln f
extends continuously through f=0 in a fixed neighborhood of p=1.
For maxima near p=1 one can restrict to M>=1, so ln M causes no
zero-denominator problem. The uniform expansion plus compactness gives
the right-envelope derivative

 (d/dp)_+ ln C(p)|_(p=1)
   =max_(M=2) [-Ent_pi(f)-Ent_pi(g)]
   =ln(B/12^2)=ln(6/144).                               (P6)

For the last equality, on any fixed biclique S times T the least
entropy densities are uniform on S and T, with entropy sum
ln(12/|S|)+ln(12/|T|). Maximize its area using (P3).
This is an explicit finite optimization argument, not an imported
hypercontractivity theorem with an unverified range.

For 1<p<=2, p<=p'. The operator norm tensorizes:
 ||T^tensor m||_(p -> p')=C(p)^m.                         (P7)
For clarity, the upper bound follows by applying the first operator in
one variable, then mixed-norm Minkowski
 ||g||_(L_p'(y) L_p(x))<=||g||_(L_p(x) L_p'(y)),
then the second operator bound. Iteration proves the claim; product
optimizers give the reverse inequality. This covers arbitrary functions,
not only product functions, on the tensor alphabet.

Define F(p)=8 C(p) 4^(-2/p). Equations (P6) and (P3) give
 F(1)=1,
 (d/dp)_+ ln F(p)|_1=ln(6/144)+2ln4=ln(2/3)<0.           (P8)
Therefore fix some p in (1,2) with F(p)<1, and let
 eta=-ln F(p)/ln3>0.
No numerical lower bound eta>=.1 is asserted.

For ANY two vertex sets S,T in W^tensor m with |S|,|T|<=k=3^m,

 e_(W^tensor m)(S,T)
  =72^m E_(pi^m) 1_S T^tensor m 1_T
  <=72^m C(p)^m (|S|/12^m)^(1/p)(|T|/12^m)^(1/p)
  <=k^2 F(p)^m=k^(2-eta).                               (P9)

This is the needed ALL-rectangles statement on the actual quantum
support graph. It does not make that support graph a classical strategy.

4. Coordinate-valid embedding and a random background
------------------------------------------------------
Map each row-input tuple xvec in {0,1,2}^m bijectively to x in [k].
For each x, inject its 4^m answer tuples into strings s in [k]^r whose
first coordinate is s_1=x. Do the analogous injection for Bob inputs
and parity-valid answers. This is possible because k^(r-1)>=4^m for
large m; two extra base-k coordinates already suffice. Plant precisely
the W^tensor m edges between these distinct embedded vertices.

Choose a fixed constant
                  0<c<min{eta,2/3}.                    (P10)
Independently add every ambient edge with probability q=k^(-2c).
For any fixed ambient S,T of size k, the number of random edges between
them has mean q k^2=k^(2-2c). A Chernoff bound at
u=(1/2)k^(2-c) is at most
 (e q k^2/u)^u =exp[-Omega(k^(2-c)ln k)].
There are at most N^(2k)=exp(2k r ln k) candidate rectangles. Because
c<2/3 and r=k^(1/3), the union bound tends to zero. The planted part
has at most k^(2-eta) edges in each such rectangle by (P9), including
rectangles using only a subset of planted vertices. Since c<eta, this
is at most (1/2)k^(2-c) for large k. Their union satisfies (P1)'s upper
bound with probability tending to one. Smaller sets can be padded.

The total random edge count is binomial(N^2,q). Its probability of
falling below qN^2/2 tends to zero. Thus with positive probability the
same graph also has the lower density in (P1). The lower target is HALF
the background mean, not the mean itself. Fix any such finite Gamma.

5. Actual values and what is decisively obstructed
---------------------------------------------------
For a deterministic classical strategy, partition its VALID input symbols
by the output label ell. If s_ell=x, distinct valid x at a fixed ell
give distinct strings. Write u_ell,v_ell for the sizes of the two input
parts. Then sum u_ell,sum v_ell<=k. Their winning edge count is at most
 min{u_ell v_ell,k^(2-c)}.
Using min{z,M}<=sqrt(zM) and Cauchy--Schwarz, the total is at most
 k^(1-c/2)sum_ell sqrt(u_ell v_ell)<=k^(2-c/2).
Shared randomness cannot improve the optimum of this finite classical
game. This proves the first inequality in (P2), directly, without a
quantum-to-classical rounding premise.

In r repeated rounds, the local strategy returns its entire own question
string in every round and the round index as ell. All r win whenever
the two uniform strings form an edge of Gamma. Its probability is the
density in (P1), proving the second inequality of (P2). These are iid
question pairs across rounds; no communication or correlated referee
oracle is inserted.

For the actual finite quantum strategy in ONE round, use m independent
magic-square pairs, produce the m answer tuples on the corresponding
input tuple, embed them into s,t, and always output ell=1. Coordinate
validity s_1=x,t_1=y is literal. The pair is a planted W^tensor m edge
with certainty, so omega_*=1 and hence omega_co=1. Only 2m shared Bell
pairs are required, although explicitly writing the whole ambient graph
or answer strings has enormous resource cost. The explicit finite
realization proves this equality.

Thus a theorem using ONLY the global-density/small-rectangle BG template
cannot infer a uniform positive entangled/commuting gap. Some additional
operator-sensitive property of a TYPICAL unplanted graph would be needed.
We have not proved that typical graph has or lacks such a property.
Nor does this example refute any repetition bound parameterized by its
own quantum gap: that gap is zero here. The original general selection
problem for positive-gap quantum games stays open.
