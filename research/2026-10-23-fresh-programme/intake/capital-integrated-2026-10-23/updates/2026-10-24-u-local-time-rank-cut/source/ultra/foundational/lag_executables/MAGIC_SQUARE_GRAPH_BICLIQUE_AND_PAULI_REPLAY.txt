Bounded replay: magic-square graph bicliques and a two-EPR strategy
===================================================================

Scope
-----
This is an exact finite check for the 12-by-12 magic-square answer graph and
the stated two-qubit-per-party Pauli realization. It establishes the maximum
complete-biclique area for this graph. It does not establish a tensor/planted
graph property, a BG-type-game theorem, or any optimized payoff constant.

Graph definition and degree
---------------------------
Left vertices are (x,a), with x in {0,1,2}, a in {+1,-1}^3 and
product(a)=+1. Right vertices are (y,b), with y in {0,1,2}, b in {+1,-1}^3
and product(b)=beta_y for beta=(+1,+1,-1). The edge predicate is a_y=b_x.
There are 4 legal answer triples for each of 3 settings on either side, hence
12 vertices per side.

For fixed (x,a), each column y has exactly two legal b with b_x=a_y: choose
one of the two other signs freely and the final sign is fixed by product(b)=
beta_y. Thus every left degree is 3*2=6. The transposed argument gives every
right degree 6.

Complete-biclique area
----------------------
For each left subset S, let N(S) be the common right neighborhood. A
complete biclique with left side S can have at most |N(S)| right vertices,
so its area is at most |S| |N(S)|; when this product is maximal, taking all
of N(S) attains it. The replay enumerates all 2^12=4096 choices of S using
exact bitsets. It finds

    max_S |S| |N(S)| = 6.

Every maximizing left subset, paired with all its common neighbors, has one
of these shapes (counts are numbers of maximizing left subsets/bicliques):

    (|S|, |N(S)|) = (1,6): 12     one left vertex and its full star
    (2,3):               48     two vertices in distinct rows; one common
                                    right vertex in each column
    (3,2):               48     one left vertex in each row; common right
                                    vertices in two distinct columns
    (6,1):               12     all six left neighbors of one right vertex

There are 120 maximizing bicliques total. The script also checks the stated
row/column support patterns for each enumerated maximizer. Since no S has
area greater than 6, no complete biclique (including one using only a subset
of N(S)) can have greater area.

Pauli-grid realization
----------------------
Use the 3-by-3 grid, with tensor factors ordered as two qubits:

    ZI   IZ   ZZ
    IX   XI   XX
    ZX   XZ   YY

Each row consists of commuting observables with product +II. Each column
consists of commuting observables with products +II,+II,-II respectively.
The script checks commutation and products exactly using Pauli phases. In
particular, ZX*XZ=YY, while ZZ*XX=-YY.

Let Alice and Bob share |Phi+> tensor |Phi+>. Alice measures the three
commuting observables in her input row. Bob measures the transposes of the
three commuting observables in his input column. Every grid entry is
symmetric under transpose (the YY entry has two Y factors), and
(P tensor P^T)|Phi+>^{tensor 2}=|Phi+>^{tensor 2}. Therefore the intersecting
cell outcomes agree with certainty. The operator products give Alice's row
parity +1 and Bob's column parities +1,+1,-1. This is a perfect strategy for
the usual magic-square consistency/parity game. It is a direct finite
realization check, not a claim about a broader graph construction.
