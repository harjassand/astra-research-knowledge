MAGIC-SQUARE PLANTING AGAINST BG-STYLE CLASSICAL GRAPH CONDITIONS
2026-10-24. Bounded independent proof check.

Verdict
-------
The proposed obstruction works, with the Lp spaces in C(p) explicitly
normalized by uniform probability measure on the 12 vertices. The endpoint
derivative is strictly favorable: log F has right derivative at most
log(2/3) at p=1. Tensorization, embedding, random-background sparsity, the
BG classical component bound, and the r-copy local strategy all check.
This shows that the induced-k-by-k sparsity condition used for a classical
upper bound does not by itself imply a quantum/commuting gap. Since the
planted base game has quantum value 1, this is not a slow-repetition
constant-gap example.

1. The fixed 12-by-12 gadget
----------------------------
Left vertices are (x,a), x in {0,1,2}, a in {+1,-1}^3 with product +1.
Right vertices are (y,b), y in {0,1,2}, b in {+1,-1}^3 with product
beta_y, beta=(+1,+1,-1). Join (x,a) to (y,b) iff a_y=b_x. There are
12 vertices per side. For each of the three y values, exactly two legal
b's match a_y, so the left degree is 6; symmetry gives right degree 6.

It is a perfect quantum kernel. On two EPR pairs use the observable grid

    ZI   IZ   ZZ
    IX   XI   XX
   -ZX  -XZ   YY

Rows commute and multiply to +II; columns commute and multiply to +II,
+II,-II. Each cell observable is symmetric under transpose (the last cell
has two Y factors), so the corresponding row and column outcomes agree
perfectly on the shared EPR pairs. The exact Pauli phase replay checks all
commutations/products. Independent tensor products give a perfect strategy
for W^m on every input tuple, hence both finite-tensor and commuting values
are 1.

The same exact replay enumerates every left subset S (4096 cases) and its
common right neighborhood N(S). It finds max |S||N(S)|=6, with maximizing
shapes (1,6),(2,3),(3,2),(6,1). Thus every complete biclique has area at
most 6. This finite check is also the input to the norm endpoint argument
below. Replay:

    python3 work/next_unit/foundational/lag_executables/check_magic_square_graph_pauli.py

2. Tensor edge bound and the endpoint derivative
------------------------------------------------
Let mu be uniform measure on either 12-point side and P=W/6 the row-
stochastic adjacency operator. For 1<=p<=2 put p'=p/(p-1) and
  C(p)=||P||_(L_p(mu) -> L_p'(mu)).
At p=1, C(1)=2: a point mass at one endpoint gives output 1/6 on its six
neighbors, whose L-infinity value divided by the input L1(mu) norm 1/12
is 2; no larger value is possible because max P_xy=1/6.

For nonnegative f,g with L1(mu) norms 1, write
  B(f,g)=<g,Pf>_mu = 2 Pr_(u~g/12,v~f/12)[(u,v) is an edge].
Hence B=2 exactly when the positive supports form a complete biclique.
If their sizes are s,t, the exact biclique-area bound gives st<=6.
For such f, with q_v=f_v/12 a probability distribution,
  d/dp log||f||_p at p=1+ = (1/12) sum_v f_v log f_v
                              = log 12 - H(q)
                              >= log(12/s).
The analogous bound for g gives, for every maximizer of C(1),
  d/dp log[B(f,g)/(||f||_p||g||_p)] at 1+
       <= log(st/144) <= -log 24.
The normalized L1 simplices are compact and this derivative is uniform
over them (finite dimension; z log z is continuous at zero), so the
one-sided envelope/Danskin bound yields
  C'_+(1)/C(1) <= -log 24.
For F(p)=8 C(p) 4^(-2/p), F(1)=1 and
  (log F)'_+(1) <= -log 24 + 2 log 4 = log(2/3) < 0.
Consequently F(p)<1 for some (indeed all sufficiently close) p>1. No
unverified numeric optimization of C(p) is needed.

For nonnegative P, its Lp-to-Lq norm tensorizes when p<=q. One direct
proof applies the factors successively: for a nonnegative matrix B and an
array z, Minkowski gives
  || (sum_j B_ij z_{j,.})_i ||_(l_q(i;l_p))
       <= ||B||_(p->q) ||z||_(l_p(j;l_p));
then apply the other factor coordinatewise. Product maximizing vectors
give the reverse inequality. Thus ||P^tensor m||_(p->p')=C(p)^m.

For subsets S,T of the two sides of W^tensor m with |S|,|T|<=k, uniform
measure on 12^m vertices gives
  e_Wm(S,T)=12^m 6^m <1_S,P^tensor m 1_T>
       <= 12^m 6^m C(p)^m (|S|/12^m)^(1/p)(|T|/12^m)^(1/p).
Set k=3^m. Rearranging the factors yields exactly
  e_Wm(S,T) <= k^2 [8 C(p) 4^(-2/p)]^m = k^2 F(p)^m.
This applies to arbitrary subsets, not just full question fibers or the
planted vertex list.

3. Injective planting in BG answer strings
-------------------------------------------
Take m divisible by 3, k=3^m and r=k^(1/3)=3^(m/3). Each left W^m
vertex is (x_tuple,a_tuple), with k possible x_tuples and 4^m legal
answer tuples for each. Map it injectively to s in [k]^r with
  s_1 = index(x_tuple),
using the remaining coordinates as an answer code. Capacity is enough:
  k^(r-1) >= 4^m,
for m>=3 (since k=3^m and r>=3). Make the analogous independent
right-side injection. Declare planted edges exactly the image of the
W^m edges. Both maps preserve distinct vertices and each planted edge
lies in the fixed coordinate-1 block with the encoded question symbols.

If A,B are any ambient subsets of at most k strings, their planted edges
are W^m edges between the inverse images A0,B0, which still have size at
most k. With eta=-log_3 F(p)>0, F(p)^m=k^(-eta), so the planted edge
contribution is at most k^(2-eta), uniformly for all such A,B.

4. Random background and simultaneous properties
------------------------------------------------
The ambient sides each have N=k^r strings. Add every possible bipartite
edge independently with probability q=k^(-2c), for fixed
  0<c<min(eta,2/3).
The random global density is at least q/2 with probability tending to 1
by Chernoff, since qN^2 is enormous.

For any fixed A,B of size k, the random background edge count has mean
mu=k^2 q=k^(2-2c). Let u=(1/2)k^(2-c). Chernoff gives
  Pr[X>=u] <= (e mu/u)^u
             <= (2e k^(-c))^((1/2)k^(2-c))
             = exp(-Omega(c k^(2-c) log k)).
The number of A,B pairs is at most (eN/k)^(2k), whose log is
O(k r log k)=O(k^(4/3) log k). Since c<2/3, 2-c>4/3, and a union
bound shows that every k-by-k pair has at most u random edges w.h.p.
The planted contribution is at most k^(2-eta)=o(k^(2-c)); for large k
it is <=u. Thus one graph simultaneously has density >=q/2 and at most
k^(2-c) edges between every two k-subsets. For sets of smaller size,
extend to size k; edge count only increases.

5. Generalized BG classical bound and repeated local block
----------------------------------------------------------
For a deterministic BG strategy on questions x,y in [k], answer strings
s,t in [k]^r, and labels ell_A,ell_B in [r], form the k-by-k win graph
on question pairs. An edge forces equal labels and s(ell)=x,t(ell)=y.
Along every connected component of the win graph the labels are constant;
the output strings on each side are distinct as the question varies.
Thus each component's winning edges lie in Gamma[A x B] for sets of at
most k ambient strings per side, so it contains at most k^(2-c) edges.
If a component has v_i vertices, it also has at most v_i^2/4 edges.
Combining the two bounds by min(a,b)<=sqrt(ab), and using sum_i v_i=2k,
  #win edges <= sum_i sqrt((v_i^2/4) k^(2-c))
             <= k^(1-c/2) (sum_i v_i)/2
             = k^(2-c/2).
Therefore the deterministic and randomized classical values obey
  val(Gamma-game) <= k^(-c/2).
This is exactly the BG component argument with delta=k^(-c), not an
assumption that the whole graph is sparse.

For an r-fold local block, on input tuples (x_1,...,x_r) and
(y_1,...,y_r), Alice outputs the same string s=(x_1,...,x_r) each
coordinate and label ell=i in coordinate i; Bob analogously outputs
t=(y_1,...,y_r), label i. The block wins all r coordinates iff
(s,t) is an edge of Gamma. For uniform inputs the probability is the
global graph density, at least
  q/2 = (1/2) k^(-2c).
Separately, the base game's fixed-label ell=1 strategy uses the planted
W^m quantum strategy and has value 1. So the BG graph hypotheses coexist
with a perfect quantum (and commuting) base game and a local r-block
success at least q/2. They cannot alone certify a quantum/commuting gap.

Scope
-----
This is a combinatorial counterexample to extending the BG classical
induced-subgraph hypothesis into a quantum upper bound. It does not claim
an independent slow-repetition theorem, and it does not contradict the
BG classical theorem. The construction is asymptotic along m divisible
by 3 and uses probabilistic existence for the background graph.

Parameter note: this verifies the stated generalized family with paired
parameters (density about k^(-2c), induced-edge cap k^(2-c)). The original
BG example fixes density about k^(-1/5) and induced cap k^(2-1/10).
Matching both exact exponents with this same one-parameter choice
requires c=1/10 and eta>1/10; the endpoint argument alone guarantees
only some eta>0. No exact-original-exponents claim is needed for the
generalized classical bound or for the quantum-obstruction conclusion.
