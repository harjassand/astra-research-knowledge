BG RANDOM GRAPH: FINITE-QUANTUM-DIMENSION GATE AND SELECTED-SOURCE CHECK
2026-10-24. Bounded independent proof check; no claim about unrestricted
quantum or commuting value.

Result
------
For the original BG random bipartite graph on N=k^r strings per side,
r=k^(1/3), with independent edge density q=k^(-1/5), the proposed net
argument is sound: for every fixed eta in (0,1/6), with probability
1-o(1), all strategies with local Hilbert dimensions at most
d=floor(k^(1/6-eta)) have value at most 1/2. This event intersects the
usual BG density and induced-k-by-k sparsity events with probability
1-o(1). The quantum-dimension restriction is essential to this statement;
it says nothing about unrestricted omega* or omega_co.

1. Extremal POVMs and valid answer labels
------------------------------------------
Fix finite k,r and answer alphabet A=[k]^r x [r], of size M=r k^r.
The strategy set of states on C^d tensor C^d and k local POVMs per
player is compact. A maximizing shared state can be chosen pure: for
fixed POVMs the payoff is linear in the density operator, so an extreme
rank-one state does at least as well as any mixed state.

At a global maximizer, fix the state and Bob's POVMs. Alice's objective
is linear in each separate POVM, so replace every Alice POVM by an
extremal maximizer of its own linear functional. Then, with Alice fixed,
do the same for each Bob POVM. The value cannot increase beyond the
global optimum, so the result remains optimal and both players' POVMs
are extremal. This sequential best-response step is the simultaneous
sparsification needed for the net; extremality of only one side would
not suffice.

An extremal POVM in dimension d has at most d^2 nonzero effects. If it
had more, its Hermitian effects would be linearly dependent: choose real
c_j, not all zero, with sum_j c_j E_j=0. For small positive t both
families (1 +/- t c_j)E_j remain positive and sum to I, expressing the
POVM as a nontrivial midpoint. Thus there are at most d^2 nonzero
outcome labels at each question. Keep their original labels in [k]^r x
[r]. In particular, no “invalid answer” is reassigned to a different
label or string; the support enumeration covers all original outputs,
including those that cannot win on that input.

Arbitrary shared classical randomness does not enlarge the bound: a
shared-random strategy is a convex mixture of dimension-d branches, and
its value is at most the largest branch value. The dimension parameter
here is the local quantum dimension on each branch; the amount of shared
classical randomness is unrestricted.

2. Net size and payoff approximation
------------------------------------
For each of k inputs and each player, choose at most d^2 labels from M.
The number of support patterns per POVM is at most
  sum_(j<=d^2) binom(M,j) <= exp(O(d^2 log M)),
so both players' full support-pattern count has logarithm
  O(k d^2 log M)=O(k d^2 r log k).

For a fixed support, a POVM is a tuple of at most d^2 Hermitian d-by-d
matrices, hence lies in a real space of dimension O(d^4). Cover the
compact set of valid POVMs by centers that are themselves valid POVMs,
using max-effect operator-norm resolution epsilon/d^2. Its log covering
number is O(d^4 log(d/epsilon)) per input, hence
  O(k d^4 log(kd))
for both players. A Euclidean epsilon-net for the unit pure-state sphere
adds O(d^2 log(d/epsilon)).

These nets approximate the game value uniformly. For fixed x,y, the
winning operator is a positive sub-sum of the product POVM, hence is a
contraction. Changing the state by Euclidean epsilon changes its
expectation by at most 2 epsilon. If Alice's effects move by at most
epsilon/d^2 each, then for each Alice answer a the sum of Bob's winning
effects is an operator between 0 and I; summing over at most d^2 Alice
answers bounds the winning-operator change by epsilon. The symmetric
Bob change is also at most epsilon. Thus choosing small absolute epsilon
(for example 1/32) yields a net strategy within 1/4 in value. Net
centers remain legal strategies, because the covering is of the compact
POVM/state sets rather than a raw grid of invalid matrices.

With d=k^(1/6-eta), the logarithm of the full net size is
  O(k^(5/3-2eta) log k + k^(5/3-4eta) log k + d^2 log k)
       = o(k^(5/3) log k).
Here log M=Theta(r log k), r=k^(1/3). Floors and factors for the two
players do not change the exponents.

3. Weighted-edge concentration for a fixed net strategy
--------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed legal strategy independent of the random graph, let
P_xy(a,b) be its output-pair probabilities. Group its potential winning
contributions by ambient graph edge (s,t):
  w_(s,t) = (1/k^2) sum_(x,y,a,b)
      P_xy(a,b) 1{a=(s,ell), b=(t,ell),
                    s_ell=x, t_ell=y for some ell}.
Then the strategy value on Gamma is exactly
  V_Gamma = sum_(s,t) w_(s,t) X_(s,t),
where X_(s,t) are independent Bernoulli(q) graph-edge indicators.
The weights are nonnegative and
  sum_(s,t) w_(s,t) <= 1,
because each output pair contributes to at most one ambient edge and the
total probability of output pairs passing the label/cell checks is at
most one. For fixed (s,t), each label ell fixes the only possible input
pair x=s_ell,y=t_ell. There are at most r such labels, each with
probability at most one, so
  max_(s,t) w_(s,t) <= Delta := r/k^2.

Weighted Chernoff (or its mgf proof using w<=Delta and sum w<=1) gives,
for fixed constant u>q,
  Pr[V_Gamma >= u] <= (e q/u)^(u/Delta).
Take u=1/4. Since q=k^(-1/5),
  -log Pr[V_Gamma >= 1/4]
      = Omega((k^2/r) log(1/q))
      = Omega(k^(5/3) log k).
This dominates the log net size for every fixed eta>0. A union bound
shows all net centers have value below 1/4 with probability 1-o(1).
The approximation bound then gives omega_d(Gamma)<=1/2.

This bound does not rely on reallocating output probability from invalid
answers. The actual label and string are preserved in the weights, and
invalid pairs simply contribute zero. The max-weight bound r/k^2 remains
valid even when several coordinates of s or t coincide.

4. Compatibility with the original BG random graph event
--------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed pair of k-vertex subsets, the random edge count has mean
k^2 q=k^(9/5). The original BG forbidden threshold is k^(19/10), whose
Chernoff exponent is Omega(k^(19/10) log k); the number of subset pairs
has log O(k r log k)=O(k^(4/3) log k). Thus induced-k-by-k sparsity
holds w.h.p. Also total density is q(1+o(1)) w.h.p. Intersect these
events with the dimension-net event above; all three fail with
probability o(1). Hence there are typical original-BG graphs satisfying
both the classical graph conditions and omega_d<=1/2.

5. Low-charge repeated classical source
---------------------------------------
For the r-fold repeated BG game, use the deterministic local strategy
that on the whole input tuple X^r outputs the string s=X^r and, in
coordinate j, label ell=j. Bob outputs t=Y^r and label j. Every
coordinate's win indicator is the same random variable
  xi_Gamma(X^r,Y^r)=1{(X^r,Y^r) is an edge of Gamma}.
Consequently, for any nonempty checked set C, the event that all C
coordinates win is exactly xi=1, and every remaining coordinate wins
with conditional probability 1. On a typical BG graph its probability
p is q(1+o(1)), so the total event log-cost per round is
  log(1/p)/r = ( (1/5)log k + O(1) )/k^(1/3) -> 0.

Thus any proposed complete one-round payoff extraction which both
preserves a local-dimension bound d=k^(1/6-eta) and turns this low-cost,
zero-live-loss source into payoff approaching one is impossible: the
one-round dimension-d value is at most 1/2. This is only a restriction
on omega_d. It does not establish any upper bound below one for
unrestricted omega* or omega_co, and does not invalidate an extraction
whose quantum dimension is allowed to grow beyond this regime.
