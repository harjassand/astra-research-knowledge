FOURFOLD ANISOTROPY: A COUPLED RESPONSE GATE, NOT A LIFETIME THEOREM
Root derivation, 2026-10-24. Uses the existing independent-q / massive
two-component field model. No peer's next opening has been read.

Let z=q1+i q2. The positive anisotropic potential is
  V=gamma |z|^4/4 + epsilon(q1^4+q2^4)/4.
Its complex restoring force is exactly
  (gamma+3epsilon/4)|z|^2 z + (epsilon/4) conjugate(z)^3.
Thus the anisotropy is not merely a new scalar Duffing coefficient.

Use temporal convention exp(-i omega t), and the clockwise reference
z0=A exp(-i Omega t). This conjugates the earlier counterclockwise orbit;
all energy/threshold statements use positive Omega. A first-order
isotropic amplitude adjustment can absorb 3epsilon/4 into the radial
coefficient at fixed Omega. This does not dispose of the anisotropic
forcing A^3 exp(+3i Omega t)/4.

The gamma force linearization about z0 is
  delta[gamma |z|^2 z]
    =2gamma A^2 delta z + gamma A^2 exp(-2i Omega t) conjugate(delta z).
Consequently the forced frequency -3Omega couples already at FIRST
ORDER in epsilon to +5Omega. A one-harmonic calculation is not the full
finite-amplitude susceptibility. Write the first-order nonfundamental
response as
  delta z=epsilon[b exp(+3i Omega t)+c exp(-5i Omega t)].

For an outgoing retarded response define
  d_ret(omega)=sqrt[omega_c^2-(omega+i0)^2],
with positive real value below the gap, negative imaginary value for
omega>omega_c, positive imaginary value for omega<-omega_c. Then
  u_hat(0;omega)=g z_hat(omega)/(2 c_field d_ret(omega)),
  K(omega)=omega0^2-omega^2+2gamma A^2
                         -g^2/[2 c_field d_ret(omega)].
Here c_field is the field wave speed, distinct from response coefficient c.
The coupled response equations are
  [ K(-3Omega)          gamma A^2 ] [ b           ] = [-A^3/4],
  [ gamma A^2   conjugate(K(5Omega))] [conjugate(c)]   [   0   ].
Real parameters imply K(-omega)=conjugate(K(omega)). The off-diagonal
coupling cannot be omitted at fixed finite A. If 5Omega exceeds the
continuum threshold while 3Omega does not, c can still radiate at order
epsilon through its coupling to b. Therefore the naive condition
3Omega>omega_c is NOT the only first-order radiative threshold. With
g,gamma,A nonzero and 5Omega>omega_c, the imaginary part on the second
diagonal, together with the nonzero off-diagonal, also prevents a real
frequency kernel of this 2-by-2 outgoing matrix. Threshold equality
and zero-coupling limits require separate treatment.

At later orders the natural frequency pairs are (1-4j)Omega and
(1+4j)Omega; whether an order really appears requires the nonlinear
recursion, not this bookkeeping alone. If both 3Omega and 5Omega lie
below the gap, internal Floquet resonances can also invalidate a
bounded first-order response, so no uniform analytic expansion is
claimed here.

For a single circular outgoing q-harmonic of complex amplitude h and
absolute frequency w>omega_c, the summed two-component outward power is
  P_w=w g^2 |h|^2/[2 c_field sqrt(w^2-omega_c^2)].
This is twice the earlier ONE-real-component peak-force formula. If
the formal b,c response exists, it predicts an order-epsilon^2 flux
with the appropriate radiative terms. This is a FORCED/STATIONARY
LINEAR RESPONSE calculation, not a proved unforced decay law.

To turn it into lifetime/metastability one still must justify modulation
of the prepared orbit, secular errors and backreaction, and distinguish
positive radiated energy from loss of H-Omega L, which is no longer
conserved after anisotropy. Exact nonexistence of finite-energy periodic
anisotropic orbits does not supply those estimates. No acquired device,
novelty or finite-horizon performance result is inferred.
