DIRECT TOMOGRAPHY OF A FIXED MACRO-INSTRUMENT FROM RAW JOINT COUNTS
2026-10-24

Question and verdict

For one predeclared finite macro-program, one can estimate its complete
success/abort terminal instrument directly, without composing noisy primitive
estimates. The following finite-shot theorem is a useful positive statement.
It needs: a true finite-dimensional operational quotient; a full core of
positive legal preparations and future effects; a nonsingular, quantitatively
conditioned H; legal repeated execution of each preparation protocol; a finite
terminal-outcome alphabet; and a true macro completion that preserves the
memory for the post-macro tests. It produces an observable linear instrument
estimate; it does not make the estimate CP/positive or provide a latent Kraus
gauge.

Setup

Let R/N be the real Hermitian operational quotient, of dimension r. N is the
common nullspace of all future action/outcome tests and is invariant under the
true maps. Choose r positive legal preparation histories rho_j (possibly
subnormalized, with Tr rho_j<=1) whose quotient classes form a basis. Choose r
future-test effects F_i spanning the dual, with F_1=I the empty future test.
Write f_i(X)=Tr(F_i X) and

  H_ij = f_i(rho_j),     sigma = sigma_min(H)>0.

For a quotient vector x, its core coordinates are h=Phi(x)=(f_i(x))_i,
and c=H^-1 h gives x=sum_j c_j[rho_j]. Define

  kappa = ||H^-1||_(ell_infinity -> ell_1).

Then

  ||Phi(x)||_infinity <= ||x||_Q <= kappa ||Phi(x)||_infinity,
  kappa <= r/sigma.

The first inequality follows because each F_i is an effect. The second uses
||[rho_j]||_Q<=Tr rho_j<=1. The stated r/sigma is a convenient bound from the
Euclidean singular-value inequality; the exact induced norm kappa can be
smaller. Nonsingularity alone is not enough unless the preparations span all
of R/N and the tests separate it.

Fix a macro-instrument with a finite terminal label b in B, including success,
abort and any other exposed terminal outcome. Let T_b be its true CP outcome
map on retained memory and controller state. The full terminal instrument is
complete: the sum over b is trace preserving. Measure F_i after branch b and
define the raw joint moments

  G_b[i,j] = f_i(T_b rho_j),     B_b = G_b H^-1.

Thus for any x in the quotient,

  Phi(T_b x) = B_b Phi(x).

All quantities G_b and H are *joint* probabilities. If rho_j is obtained as
an unnormalized past-event state, never divide by the frequency of that past
event. Repeated reset-to-start episodes estimate its joint event indicator;
rare preparation histories simply worsen H's conditioning/sample error.

Raw-count acquisition

For every pair (i,j), collect n_H independent baseline episodes: attempt the
legal past preparation j and then perform the binary test F_i. Record the
indicator of both the preparation event and test success. Its mean is H_ij.
For every (i,j), collect n_G fresh macro episodes: attempt preparation j, run
the fixed macro to its terminal label b, retain the memory, and perform F_i;
record the vector Y in R^|B| whose b-coordinate is one exactly when the past
event, terminal label b, and future-test success all occurred. In every shot Y
is either zero or one standard basis vector, and

  E Y = (G_b[i,j])_b,       sum_b G_b[i,j] <= 1.

This is an ordinary joint-event experiment, not a conditional-probability
oracle. The macro is run after the preparation protocol on every shot; the
indicator selects the desired past-event branch. The terminal label must be
physically observed, and F_i must be legally executable after that branch.

Let Hhat and Ghat_b be the empirical frequencies, and let alpha be the total
failure probability. Simultaneous Hoeffding and vector concentration give,
with probability at least 1-alpha,

  max_ij |Hhat_ij-H_ij| <= eta_H,
  max_ij sum_b |Ghat_b[i,j]-G_b[i,j]| <= eta_G,

where one sufficient choice is

  eta_H = sqrt(log(4 r^2/alpha)/(2 n_H)),
  eta_G = sqrt(|B|/n_G) + sqrt(2 log(2 r^2/alpha)/n_G).

For the second inequality, E||Yhat-EY||_1<=sqrt(|B|/n_G), since the outcome
vector is one-hot-or-zero with total mean mass at most one. Changing one shot
changes the empirical l1 error by at most 2/n_G, so McDiarmid's inequality
adds the displayed tail term. The union bound is over the r^2 (i,j) pairs;
it need not union-bound separately over b. For H, it is over r^2 Bernoulli
entries. Splitting alpha/2 between the two families gives the displayed
constants.

Conditioned operator error bound

Assume kappa eta_H<1. Since ||Hhat-H||_(ell_1 -> ell_infinity)<=eta_H,

  Hhat is invertible,
  ||Hhat^-1||_(ell_infinity -> ell_1) <= kappa/(1-kappa eta_H).

Set Bhat_b=Ghat_b Hhat^-1. For Delta H=Hhat-H and Delta G_b=Ghat_b-G_b,

  Bhat_b-B_b = (Delta G_b - B_b Delta H) Hhat^-1.

Each true branch is trace-nonincreasing, so it contracts Q; the complete
branch-summed true instrument is also contractive:

  sum_b ||T_b x||_Q <= ||x||_Q,
  ||B_b||_(ell_infinity -> ell_infinity) <= kappa.

For any coefficient vector c, the acquired joint-count bound implies

  sum_b ||Delta G_b c||_infinity <= r eta_G ||c||_1.

Indeed, for each j, sum_b max_i |Delta G_b[i,j]| is no larger than
sum_i sum_b |Delta G_b[i,j]|<=r eta_G. The H-error term benefits from the
branch-summed physical contraction:

  sum_b ||B_b Delta H c||_infinity
     <= ||Phi^-1(Delta H c)||_Q
     <= kappa eta_H ||c||_1.

Using ||Phi^-1 v||_Q<=kappa||v||_infinity and
||Phi(x)||_infinity<=||x||_Q gives the direct terminal-instrument guarantee

  epsilon_macro := sup_(x!=0)
      [sum_b ||Phi^-1((Bhat_b-B_b)Phi(x))||_Q]/||x||_Q
    <= kappa^2 (r eta_G + kappa eta_H)/(1-kappa eta_H).

This compares the whole learned success/abort instrument to the true whole
instrument in the direct-sum operational norm. The estimate is a linear map
on the identified quotient, but no positivity or CP property is implied.
Crucially, no primitive-model error is multiplied by macro depth: the target
macro itself is re-executed in each data episode. Depth appears in the
physical cost per episode, not as an algebraic L e error term.

Scalar prediction on arbitrary reachable inputs

For any normalized reachable positive input x, ||Phi(x)||_infinity<=1. Once
its core vector h=Phi(x) is available, the learned macro predicts each core
future-test probability after b by the entries of Bhat_b h. For any fixed
future event g after the macro (including a longer action/outcome test), its
effect satisfies |g(y)|<=||y||_Q. Therefore, if its linear functional on the
quotient is known, the absolute true-versus-learned scalar error for any
terminal event, including a union of branches with a declared post-test, is
at most epsilon_macro, uniformly over all normalized reachable x. This is a
distribution-free operation bound, not an average over the basis histories.

There is an access distinction: H and G for the finite core do not by
themselves provide numerical coefficients for an arbitrary unseen g. If g is
outside the core, acquire v_g[j]=g(rho_j) by r additional raw joint-event
experiments; then lambda_g^T=v_g^T H^-1 represents g on the quotient. If
lambda_g is supplied exactly, no further error is added. If v_g is estimated
with max-coordinate error xi, define

  kappa_dual = ||H^-T||_(ell_infinity -> ell_1) <= r/sigma.

When kappa_dual eta_H<1, the estimated row lambdahat has

  ||lambdahat-lambda_g||_1
    <= kappa_dual (xi+kappa eta_H)/(1-kappa_dual eta_H).

For a fixed finite family of m_g such future tests, r n_g samples per test
with xi=sqrt(log(2 r m_g/alpha_g)/(2 n_g)) suffices by a union bound. For an
unbounded test family, a complexity/uniform-convergence argument is needed;
finite core tomography alone does not give uniform numerical access to every
test functional. Similarly, if the new input x is not supplied as a known
predictive vector, estimating its r core probabilities needs repeated copies
of that input (or the corresponding legal preparation history); a single
unknown memory state cannot be measured in all noncommuting tests for free.

Explicit shot choice and costs

For a target epsilon_macro=epsilon, it is sufficient to choose

  eta_H <= min{1/(2 kappa), epsilon/(4 kappa^3)},
  eta_G <= epsilon/(4 r kappa^2).

Then epsilon_macro<=epsilon. Sufficient per-(i,j) counts are

  n_H >= log(4 r^2/alpha)/(2 eta_H^2),
  n_G >= 4(|B|+2 log(2 r^2/alpha))/eta_G^2.

The total baseline/macro shot counts are r^2 n_H and r^2 n_G respectively,
plus any g-row or new-input calibration. Each macro shot pays its actual
runtime and intervention cost: for fixed depth L it costs L primitive ticks;
for independent per-tick abort delta it has expected at most 1/delta ticks.
The macro is assumed to be physically re-executable from each legal
preparation protocol; no repeated access to its hidden state is assumed.

Primitive reuse comparator

If L true primitive instruments are contractive and each learned primitive
has direct-sum error at most e, telescoping composition gives the generic
worst-case bound (1+e)^L-1, approximately L e when Le is small; stopped loops
give the corresponding one-resolvent O(e/delta) bound under an exact abort
coin. Direct macro tomography replaces this model-error accumulation by the
statistical bound above, but spends r^2 n_G full macro executions and pays
the macro's entire physical runtime on every shot. It estimates only the
predeclared macro; it does not yield a free compositional model for unmeasured
programs or arbitrary post-hoc policy selection.

Uniformity and finite terminal alphabet

Freeze H's preparations/tests, the macro, its finite terminal labels, and the
future tests before collecting the certificate counts. If any are selected
adaptively from these same data, use fresh validation counts or a simultaneous
bound over a predeclared finite candidate family (with its log-cardinality in
the confidence term). An invertible empirical H chosen after searching an
uncontrolled dictionary is not covered by the fixed-core calculation.

The l1 terminal-outcome concentration uses finite |B|. If a geometrically
stopped program emits an unbounded transcript, either specify a finite
coarse-graining as the terminal instrument, or truncate at depth L and merge
the tail into a terminal bucket. With independent abort probability delta per
tick, the true probability of surviving beyond L is at most (1-delta)^L;
this truncation tail must be added to the operation/prediction error (and
learned tail handling must be specified). No finite-bin result certifies all
unbounded transcript branches without such a tail or an outcome-complexity
assumption.

Conclusion

PASS as a bounded operation-specific acquisition theorem. Raw joint counts
identify a finite complete macro-instrument with an explicit conditioning and
sample-cost bound, and CP completeness improves the branch-summed perturbation
estimate. Scope remains a fixed finite core/macro/test family and a finite
terminal alphabet (or paid truncation). The theorem does not show a learned CP
realization, arbitrary-macro generalization, or cheap physical access.
