# Reserve-cell energy and strongest comparator

Scope: adversarial analytical check of `../FIRST_CONSTRUCTION.md`. I use its reciprocal elastic-cell model, distinguish force from displacement control, and compare the proposed delayed shield with an idealized static shield and crack-triggered transformation toughening. This is not a materials-performance estimate.

## Exact energy accounting

Let the intact host stiffness be (H\succ0), the internal cell states be (y\in\mathbb R^m), their reciprocal force patterns be the columns of (A), and the dead load be (f). With

\[
E(u,y)=\tfrac12u^THu-f^Tu+V(y)-y^TA^Tu,
\]

force-controlled equilibrium gives (u=H^{-1}(f+Ay)). The minimized dead-load potential is

\[
\Phi_F(y)=V(y)-\tfrac12(f+Ay)^TH^{-1}(f+Ay)
=\mathrm{const}+\psi_F(y)-h^Ty,
\]

where (K=A^TH^{-1}A\succeq0), (h=A^TH^{-1}f), and (\psi_F(y)=V(y)-\tfrac12y^TKy). The host-mediated quadratic term is essential: the reserve is not just the local drop in (V). Branch stability requires (\nabla^2V-K\succ0) on that branch.

For a snap from (y_0) to (y_1), at the same dead load, the maximum energy available to fracture, dissipation, or a useful output is

\[
R_F=\Phi_F(y_0)-\Phi_F(y_1).
\]

The loading frame also does work (W_f=f^T(u_1-u_0)) during the event. The balance is (W_{\rm useful}+D=R_F), with (D\ge0) collecting fracture and other irreversible losses, if no kinetic energy remains afterward. This identity already includes the frame work because (\Delta\Phi_F=\Delta E-W_f). Do not add a second local cell-energy drop to (R_F).

Under fixed prescribed boundary displacement, the boundary performs no work during the instantaneous snap. If (E_D(q,y;\bar u_b)) is the host-plus-cell energy minimized over the free interior coordinates for damage state (q), the corresponding bound is

\[
W_{\rm useful}+D\le E_D(q_0,y_0;\bar u_b)-E_D(q_1,y_1;\bar u_b).
\]

If the right-hand side is nonpositive, the snap cannot run spontaneously at that fixed boundary without another energy source. With mixed force/displacement control, include work only from the force-controlled ports; prescribed-displacement ports do no work during the event.

For comparison, a scalar linear-elastic specimen with compliance (C(a)) has incremental crack-release energy

\[
G_F\,da=\tfrac12P^2C'(a)\,da\quad\text{(constant force)},\qquad
G_D\,da=\tfrac12\frac{\delta^2C'(a)}{C(a)^2}\,da\quad\text{(constant displacement)}.
\]

For a finite compliance increase (C_1>C_0), constant force (P) lowers the minimized potential by (P^2(C_1-C_0)/2), while the frame supplies work (P^2(C_1-C_0)) and the elastic strain energy increases by half that amount. At fixed displacement (\delta), the available strain-energy drop is (\delta^2(1/C_0-1/C_1)/2); taking the same initial force (P_0=\delta/C_0), this equals (P_0^2(C_1-C_0)C_0/(2C_1)), less than the force-controlled release. Thus a reserve that arrests a crack under fixed displacement cannot be credited with dead-load work from a force-controlled test, or vice versa.

For the opening's rank-one bond deletion (H_1=H-kbb^T\succ0), Sherman–Morrison gives

\[
f^T(H_1^{-1}-H^{-1})f=\frac{k(b^TH^{-1}f)^2}{1-kb^TH^{-1}b}.
\]

Half of this is the constant-force decrease in minimized potential; the full amount is the dead-load frame work. Under fixed boundary displacement, first eliminate free DOFs. If (b=(b_i,b_b)), (H_{ii}\succ0), (\tilde b=b_b-H_{bi}H_{ii}^{-1}b_i), and (r=b_i^TH_{ii}^{-1}b_i), the reduced boundary stiffness changes by

\[
K_{b,1}=K_{b,0}-\frac{k}{1-kr}\tilde b\tilde b^T,
\]

and the fixed-boundary release for prescribed (u_b) is (k(\tilde b^Tu_b)^2/[2(1-kr)]). These formulas require their stated positive-definiteness denominators. With a reserve cell present, evaluate the **combined** before/after potential using (H_0,H_1) and (y_0,y_1); adding independent deletion and snap releases double-counts their elastic coupling.

## What a static convex envelope proves—and does not

For a fixed damage state and fixed control (\lambda), write the fully relaxed cell potential as (\psi(y)-\lambda^Ty). The lower convex envelope (\psi^{**}) has the exact support-plane identity

\[
\inf_y[\psi(y)-\lambda^Ty]=\inf_y[\psi^{**}(y)-\lambda^Ty],
\]

because convexification leaves the Legendre–Fenchel conjugate unchanged. Under force control use (\psi=V-\tfrac12y^TKy); under fixed boundary displacement use the potential after eliminating the permitted free host coordinates. Therefore, if the material is assumed to remain in **global quasistatic equilibrium** at every load and a cost-free, compatible static microstructure can realize the convex envelope, no metastable reserve can improve the equilibrium potential or add energy beyond that envelope. This conditional statement is exact.

It does not establish dominance for a locally trapped metastable branch: that branch is deliberately above the global envelope until a trigger/spinodal, and its excess is what it can spend later. The maximum extra reserve at a given control is just the metastable excess over the lowest accessible final state, (\Phi(y_{\rm meta})-\min_y\Phi(y)\); it is finite and already paid for in stored free energy. Nor is a mathematical convex envelope automatically a realizable microstructure: interfacial energy, compatibility, nonlocal interactions (K), and equal-volume/energy constraints must be included. Thus convexification removes the claimed timing function only under the global-equilibrium and physical-realizability assumptions above; it is not a universal no-go for hysteretic materials.

## Static and transformation-toughening competitor

In the no-collateral-penalty limit, a static prestress that supplies the **post-snap shielding force from the start** pointwise dominates a delayed shield for a monotone tensile load: if the threat opening is (e(P,s)=e_0(P)-s\), the switched cell has (0\le s(P)\le s_{\max}\), and a constant admissible static inclusion can supply (s_{\max}) at the same stored-energy/resource cost without changing trigger strength, stiffness, or another failure mode, then (e_{\rm static}(P)\le e_{\rm switched}(P)) for every (P). For a single threatened crack and this objective, the static competitor arrests at least as well. The assumption “without changing another failure mode” is load-bearing; real prestress can create damaging stresses elsewhere, which this scalar inequality does not price.

If delayed activation is the intended distinction, it has a direct incumbent class: stress-induced phase transformation around a crack tip (e.g. tetragonal-to-monoclinic zirconia transformation toughening), which generates transformation eigenstrain and crack shielding. Clay et al. directly observed stress-induced, self-accommodated martensite pairs near propagating cracks in ceria-stabilized zirconia by AFM ([primary experimental paper, DOI 10.1016/j.jeurceramsoc.2004.07.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeurceramsoc.2004.07.029)). This is a mechanism-level comparator, not a matched equal-mass/equal-reserve test; no quantitative advantage for the proposed geometry follows from it.

A minimal passive *model* showing the timing function is two elastic ports and one bistable coordinate:

\[
E(t,v,y)=\tfrac12 k_t t^2+\tfrac12 k_v v^2+V(y)-gty+hvy-F_t t-F_v v,
\]

with (k_t,k_v>0), (V(y)) a double well, and (g,h>0). At fixed trigger displacement (t) and threat force (F_v>0), (v=(F_v-hy)/k_v); a transition (y_0\to y_1>y_0) reduces threat opening by (h(y_1-y_0)/k_v). It also lowers the trigger reaction by (g(y_1-y_0)), by reciprocity. Increasing (t) tilts the cell toward the larger-y state; a metastable branch can retain (y_0) until a threshold and then make this shield appear without a controller. The force-controlled threat load does work (F_v\Delta v=-F_vh(y_1-y_0)/k_v<0) during closing, so the cell must supply that work as well as dissipation/fracture costs. This proves only a passive staged response in a toy model; it does not beat the static or transformation-toughening competitor on equal resources, and no physical cell, attainable parameters, or fracture benefit is acquired.

## Decisive remaining comparison

The useful performance question is therefore not whether a reciprocal cell can create a shield—it can in the model—but whether, at equal mass, stored free energy, and failure criterion, its delayed two-port activation raises the measured work-to-catastrophic-fracture over (i) optimally placed static pre-stress/reinforcement and (ii) established crack-triggered transformation toughening, after charging collateral stresses, hysteresis, and any failed-cell interactions. The current construction has no such comparison. Status remains **model-only; advantage NOT_ESTABLISHED**.
