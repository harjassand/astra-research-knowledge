# Phonon-resonator prior-art and comparator check

Read against [`PHONON_CONSTRUCTION.md`](../PHONON_CONSTRUCTION.md). The construction's statement is an ideal periodic, passive, one-backbone-spring model with all internal bands counted. The sources below establish classical total-bandwidth transport and genuine finite-temperature local-resonance engineering, but none is a matched fixed-total-mass, fixed-longitudinal-\(K\) comparison over all internal passive networks.

## Closest exact high-temperature comparator

Chien, Kouachi, Velizhanin, Dubi and Zwolak derive the bands and reservoir-coupled thermal conductance for a classical 1D harmonic lattice with alternating masses, onsite springs and nearest-neighbour springs. Their total passband width is the sum over both bands, Eq. (13), and their ideal intrinsic conductance is

\[
\kappa_0 = \frac{k_B\,\Omega}{2\pi},
\]

Eq. (34). This makes total width, rather than the acoustic-band width or a gap alone, the right classical ballistic comparison. Their parameter scan reports that some dimerized settings have greater \(\Omega\) than nearby uniform settings; it does not give a universal minimum at fixed total cell mass and fixed backbone coupling. It also warns that finite end-reservoir conductance can differ materially from \(\kappa_0\): boundary modes and contact coupling change the measured device conductance even when bulk band properties are unchanged (Eqs. 26–29 and 34–35). Thus this is the strongest direct prior comparator I found for the trace-of-bands quantity, not the proposed rank-one all-internal-network inequality. [Chien et al., *Physical Review E* 95, 012137 (2017), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.95.012137; primary full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5473347/).

**Source result versus candidate result:** Eq. (34) translates \(\Omega\) into a high-temperature intrinsic conductance; Eq. (13) calculates \(\Omega\) for the authors' two-site alternating chain. I found no theorem there comparing arbitrary positive internal springs against a same-\(M_{\rm total}\), same-backbone-\(K\) rigidly attached cell. This bounded search is not a novelty or priority determination.

**Adjacent mass-ratio bandgap result.** Sugino, Leadenham, Ruzzene and Erturk derive a finite-beam local-resonator bandgap-placement rule in terms of resonator natural frequency and total resonator/host mass ratio, in the many-absorber approximation, and validate the framework against a bending-vibration cantilever experiment. Their source also treats the optimal finite number and placement of resonators as dependent on boundary conditions and excitation. This is prior art for mass-ratio-controlled stopband design and finite-resonator optimization, but its objective is a finite-beam vibration attenuation band, not complete phonon passband width or a thermal-conductance minimum at fixed total cell mass and longitudinal spring constant. [Sugino et al., *Journal of Applied Physics* 120, 134501 (2016), DOI 10.1063/1.4963648](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4963648).

## Strong finite-temperature local-resonance comparators

**Model prediction.** Davis and Hussein model a silicon thin film with periodic nanopillars on one or both free surfaces. A fitted lattice-dynamics calculation attributes spectral changes to hybridization between pillar local resonances and the membrane phonons, and predicts thermal conductivity as low as 50% of the corresponding uniform thin-film value, even though the pillars add modes and material. This is a direct prior for moving/scattering heat-carrying spectral weight using local resonators. It is a model prediction, not a measured device result; the comparator adds pillar mass and does not hold total mass or longitudinal stiffness fixed. [Davis & Hussein, *Physical Review Letters* 112, 055505 (2014), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.055505](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.112.055505); [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.6070).

**Measured material operation.** Spann et al. grew GaN nanopillars on suspended, device-scale silicon membranes by plasma-assisted molecular-beam epitaxy. The Si membranes were about 200 nm thick; the two sample sets used direct growth after surface nitridation or an 8 nm AlN buffer. Pillars were about 55 nm wide, with target heights from 0.5 to 4 \(\mu\)m. The suspended membranes were released by backside etching to buried oxide and removal of the oxide. For room-temperature thermal response, a 325 nm laser heated the samples and non-contact Raman thermometry measured temperature rise versus absorbed power; conversion used a bare-membrane reference near 60 W m\(^{-1}\) K\(^{-1}\). The paper reports a maximum 21% \(\pm\) 0.4% thermal-conductivity reduction and unchanged electrical power factor. A coalesced-pillar comparison and lattice-dynamics calculations support the local-resonance interpretation. This is a real thermoelectric-side incumbent: resonators lie outside the electrical conduction path while coupling mechanically to the heat-carrying membrane. However, the experiment compares pillared to bare membranes; the added GaN mass, geometry, and mechanical spectrum are not matched against a same-mass/same-\(K\) backbone. The thickness equivalence used in the thermal-conductivity conversion is an assumption, which the authors note tends to underestimate the reduction. It measures conductivity of a finite suspended material, not the ideal ballistic intrinsic conductance in Chien's Eq. (34). The paper reports source data in its supporting information. [Spann et al., *Advanced Materials* 35 (2023), DOI 10.1002/adma.202209779; NIST-hosted primary paper and experiment metadata](https://www.nist.gov/publications/semiconductor-thermal-and-electrical-properties-decoupled-localized-phonon-resonances).

## Consequence for the proposed comparison

There is already direct prior art for lowering finite-temperature conductivity by local resonances, including a measured GaN-pillar/Si-membrane implementation. A distinct result cannot be “local resonators suppress heat” or an acoustic stopband alone. The candidate's useful question is narrower: does a complete acoustic-plus-optical passband accounting impose the stated classical lower bound under fixed \(M_{\rm total}\) and fixed backbone \(K\), and can an admissible passive resonator beat the rigid-mass comparator for the full Planck-weighted conductance at a specified finite \(T\)? The latter needs a global optimization against resonator networks at those same resource constraints; the cited NPM experiment and model do not supply that benchmark.

No actual specimen, matched-mass thermal measurement, or hardware validation of the proposed cell was acquired in this source task.
