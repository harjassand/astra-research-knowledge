# Primary-source check: passive crack shielding and eigenstrain reserves

Read against `FIRST_CONSTRUCTION.md`. The two-port elastic-cell equations are a model construction; this source check found no experiment of that exact cell or a matched resource test against static reinforcement.

## Closest strong physical comparators

**Transformation toughening is direct prior art for stress-triggered eigenstrain shielding.** In MgO-partially-stabilized zirconia, Marshall et al. measured crack-tip transformation zones using optical-interference surface-uplift measurements and Raman microprobe maps. They found a spatially nonuniform transformed fraction, with the frontal zone extending about as far as the wake, then evaluated the crack-tip shielding stress intensity and compared it with measured fracture toughness. This is not just a proposed phase-field mechanism: crack-associated transformation and its shielding contribution were measured in specimens. [Marshall et al., *J. Am. Ceram. Soc.* 73, 2659–2666 (1990), DOI 10.1111/j.1151-2916.1990.tb06743.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1151-2916.1990.tb06743.x).

Gavalda-Diaz et al. later resolved the event in situ at single-grain scale. They FIB-machined wedge-driven double-cantilever beams from single-crystal 4YSZ (tetragonal) and 9YSZ (cubic), loaded them inside a TEM with a roughly 65° diamond wedge at 1 nm/s, and combined crack imaging with diffraction. The test region was electron-transparent (about 1 × 0.1 µm); cracks advanced roughly 1–2 µm. In 4YSZ, a persistent tetragonal-to-monoclinic zone formed at the crack, with monoclinic diffraction detected up to about 500 nm to one side; the authors inferred a 2–4 GPa transformation-trigger stress from their LEFM overlay. For cracks under 1 µm, their approximate measured toughnesses were 2.7 MPa√m (4YSZ) versus 1.8 MPa√m (9YSZ), with about half of the 4YSZ fracture energy attributed to transformation. This nanoscale geometry removes part of the 3D transformation zone, so it is mechanistic confirmation, not a bulk engineering-strength benchmark. [Gavalda-Diaz et al., *PNAS* 121, e2408292121 (2024), DOI 10.1073/pnas.2408292121](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2408292121).

**The strongest close architecture-level incumbent is a passive damage-programmed metamaterial.** Gao et al. fabricated 3D cells with oriented microfibers that program damage; combinations of type-1/type-2 cells create crack-bowing and crack-deflection regions, while type-3 cells form positive and negative shielding layers. Under fracture loading, XCT and digital image correlation showed crack shielding, deflection, trapping and dissipation. Their combined design was compared with a conventional metamaterial made with the same parent resin, printing technology, beam diameters and test method; it had about twice the normalized fracture strength and higher plateau load. Separately, the paper reports up to 1,235% greater fracture energy per unit crack propagation than conventional lattices at the same density. These are reported architecture-specific results, not a demonstrated advantage over an equal-energy metastable-cell design. The operation is damage programmed by geometry and microfiber failure, not a two-port rest-length snap or a separately actuated internal coordinate. Material/process: Tough 2000 resin, Form 3 SLA at 25 × 25 × 50 µm nominal resolution, post-cure at 80 °C under UV for 120 min; parent material modulus 1.3 GPa, ultimate tensile stress 36.3 MPa. Fracture tests, DIC and XCT provide the readout; source data are stated to accompany the article. [Gao et al., *Nature Communications* 15, 7373 (2024), DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-51757-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51757-0).

## What reciprocity establishes—and what it does not

For the proposed linear host, the cross-port opening change is
\[
\Delta e_v=b_v^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}a\,\Delta y.
\]
Because elastic stiffness is symmetric, \(b_v^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}a=a^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}b_v\): reciprocal elastic coupling can transmit an internal transition from one port to another, and geometry/sign can in principle make that transition reduce the threatened opening. This establishes a physically admissible coupling form, not that a realizable cell has the required sign, trigger threshold, snap direction, repeatability, or net fracture benefit. The internal state must release finite stored energy; a locally stable branch requires \(V''-a^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}a>0\). With many cells the coupling term \(-A^{\mathsf T}H^{-1}A/2\) can destabilize the reserve collectively, so isolated-cell calculations do not establish a stable array.

## Gate for a distinct result

The broad “damage-triggered eigenstrain can shield a crack” opportunity is already occupied by zirconia transformation toughening. The broad “spatially programmed passive crack shielding in a mechanical metamaterial” opportunity is also occupied by the Gao design. A distinct claim would have to specify a realizable reciprocal two-port cell and demonstrate, under matched flaw, loading, density/volume and available reserve energy, greater work of crack growth before catastrophic failure than the strongest static reinforcement/prestress and transformation-toughening comparators. It must also report cross-port response, switching/energy loss, repeatability and array interactions. None of those matched results is in the first-construction note or established by the sources above. Current evidence supports physical plausibility of the coupling and high-priority prior art; it does not support advantage or novelty.
