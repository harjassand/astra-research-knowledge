PROBABILISTIC SUPPORT AUDIT: PREFIX-FIBRE MEDIAN AND QUADRATIC CUBE SELF-CORRECTION
2026-10-24

Scope
-----
This audits only the finite-fibre median label and the randomized recovery of the
D_n interpolation values per output coordinate in FIRST_CONSTRUCTION.md. It
assumes the stated promised-class invariant: Q is a bijection whose coordinates
have degree at most two, and each already acquired prefix F is exact. It does
not establish recognition of that promise or compare whole-algorithm novelty.

1. Exact finite-N error, with replacement ties included
-------------------------------------------------------
Let N=2^d be the size of a fixed prefix fibre, d>=1, and put K=2m+1. Give its
objects ranks 0,...,N-1 in oracle order. Since Q is a bijection, the next Q bit
is 0 on ranks i<N/2 and 1 on i>=N/2. Draw K iid objects Y_j uniformly WITH
replacement in the fibre, let J be the rank of their sample median (multiplicity
is retained), and label x by L(x)=1{x is strictly above the median object}.

Conditioned on x having rank i, the number of sample draws strictly below x is
Bin(K,i/N). Thus ties to x are handled as follows: for upper-half x, median=x
also labels 0 and is an error, exactly as captured by the event that at most m
draws are strictly below x. The exact averaged error is

 e_{N,K} = (1/N) [ sum_{i=0}^{N/2-1} P{Bin(K,i/N)>=m+1}
                    + sum_{i=N/2}^{N-1} P{Bin(K,i/N)<=m} ].                 (1)

Equivalently, condition on median rank J. The predicted-one ranks are
J+1,...,N-1, while the true-one ranks are N/2,...,N-1. Their symmetric
difference has size |J+1-N/2|, so

 e_{N,K}= E |J+1-N/2| / N.                                                  (2)

This identity explicitly counts sample ties and strict target/median ties; it
does not assume a continuous sample or distinct Y_j.

For a clean uniform bound, couple each discrete draw to U_j~Unif[0,1) by
Y_j=floor(N U_j). Monotonicity gives J=floor(N U_(m+1)), where U_(m+1) is the
continuous sample median. Hence

 |(J+1)/N - 1/2| <= |U_(m+1)-1/2| + 1/N.

The order statistic is Beta(m+1,m+1), with mean 1/2 and variance
1/[4(K+2)]. Cauchy-Schwarz therefore yields, for every even N>=2,

 e_{N,K} <= 1/(2 sqrt(K+2)) + 1/N.                                         (3)

The 1/N term is a valid grid/tie correction; the bound need not be sharp. A
nonvanishing exact-sample tie effect explains why small fibres should be treated
separately: for N=2, (2) gives e_{2,K}=1/4 for every odd K. Increasing K alone
does not fix that fibre because the median remains the upper object with
probability 1/2, and the strict comparison then misses it.

2. Small fibres and explicit constants
--------------------------------------
For N<64, enumerate the queried prefix fibre, sort its N distinct objects by
the oracle comparator, and label x exactly by whether its position is >=N/2.
This uses the actual finite midpoint, so e=0; repeated sample points and median
ties disappear. The learned prefix gives the fibre description, and the stated
polar-rank algorithm enumerates it with O(poly(n)(2^{r_t}+N)) field work per
fibre, where r_t<=b.

For all remaining fibres choose K=1023 (m=511). With N>=64, (3) gives

 e <= 1/(2 sqrt(1025)) + 1/64 = 0.0312423762... < 1/28,
 7e < 0.218697 < 1/4.                                                       (4)

Numerical check of (1): e_{64,1023} ~= 0.013521256, below the conservative
0.031242376 bound. Also e_{2,1023}=0.25, confirming the need for exact small-
fibre handling. These figures are sanity checks; (1)-(4) are the proof.

3. One quadratic self-correction run
------------------------------------
For a Boolean polynomial f of degree <=2, the third additive derivative is
zero:

  XOR_{S subset {a,b,c}} f(x + sum_{v in S} v) = 0.

Therefore f(x) is the XOR of the seven labels at the nonempty vertices. Choose
a,b,c independently uniformly in F_2^n. For each fixed nonempty S, the sum
of its selected independent offsets is uniform, so x+sum_{v in S}v is uniform
for every fixed x. Applying the median routine with fresh samples at each
vertex gives each of the seven labels marginal error probability at most e.
The seven labels are dependent because they share offsets; independence is not
needed. A union bound gives per-run estimate error q<=7e<1/4 by (4).

Repeat a complete run with fresh offsets and fresh median samples. Conditional
on the learned prefix being exact, different runs are independent even though
the seven labels within one run are dependent. Hoeffding gives

  P[majority wrong] <= exp(-2R(1/2-q)^2) <= exp(-R/8),                      (5)

for odd R, since q<=1/4. Do not use seven independent-label Chernoff inside a
single cube; (5) applies across fresh complete experiments only.

4. Failure allocation under adaptive stages
--------------------------------------------
Each degree-two output coordinate is determined by the D_n=1+n+binom(n,2)
values at 0, e_i, e_i+e_j. Let M=n D_n be the total number of such requested
values for all n output coordinates. Set per-value failure budget delta_c=delta/M
and choose

  R = the smallest odd integer >= 8 ln(M/delta).                            (6)

Then (5) bounds each majority failure by delta/M. Coordinates are acquired
sequentially, and the next prefix sampler depends on previous recovered
coordinates. Independence across stages is not asserted. Instead, condition on
no earlier coefficient/value error: the current exact prefix is then valid,
and the per-value error bound is uniform in that prefix and in the requested
x. A union bound on the first erroneous value gives total failure <=delta.
After a value error, later conditional guarantees need not hold, but the global
failure event has already occurred.

5. Charged comparator count
---------------------------
For each one-run cube estimate, seven approximate labels are requested. For
N>=64, each label draws K=1023 exact uniform points from its prefix fibre,
selects their median, and compares x against that median. A deterministic
linear-comparison median-selection algorithm uses at most cK comparator calls
for an absolute constant c; the final x-versus-median comparison costs one
more. Thus each coefficient-value request uses at most

  7 R (cK+1) = O(R K)

oracle comparisons. For N<64, exact sorting of each queried fibre costs
O(N log N) comparisons per approximate label, a uniform constant because N<64;
indeed the label is then exact and repetitions can be skipped. The worst-case
comparison total over all output coordinates is consequently

  O(M R K) = O(n D_n * K * ln(M/delta))
  = O(n^3 ln(n^3/delta))

for the fixed K=1023, with a constant depending on the selection routine.
Sorting the K median samples instead costs O(K log K) per label; it is also a
constant here, but linear selection gives the cleaner bound. Prefix-fibre
sampling/linear algebra is an additional paid field-operation cost, not an
oracle-comparison cost; it is poly(n) times 2^{r_t} overhead and can be
exponential in the supplied rank bound b.

6. Separate cached-median variant (not used in the K=1023 proof above)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
The executable's cached-threshold variant has a distinct valid independence
argument. For eps=1/64, conceptually preassign an independent K-sample median
to each prefix at each stage (fewer than 2^n total), independently of every
correction-offset tape. Hoeffding and a union bound make all medians eps-close
to their fibre midpoint except with probability at most
2^(n+1) exp(-2K eps^2); lazy generation on first encounter preserves this joint
law. On that simultaneous event the deterministic quantile bound gives one
fixed label function uniform error <= eps+1/128=3/128. A fresh seven-offset
cube then errs with probability <=21/128. Conditional on the entire cached
median collection, fresh offset trials are independent even if their labels
and comparisons are retained, so majority error is <=exp(-2R(43/128)^2).
Choosing odd K,R with

  2^(n+1) exp(-2K(1/64)^2) <= delta/2,
  n D_n exp(-2R(43/128)^2) <= delta/2

and union-bounding gives total failure <=delta. This is a separate cached
implementation with K=O(n+log(1/delta)) median preparation on encountered
prefixes; it does not replace or alter Sections 1-5's independent fresh-sample
K=1023 analysis. In particular, its validity comes from conditioning on all
cached medians before exposing the fresh offset tapes, not from asserting that
reused labels are independent.

Conclusion
----------
The median error claim is valid with the stated conservative finite-grid term.
The only subtle probability issue is discrete tie mass, most visible at N=2;
exact sorting of short fibres repairs it. Cube self-correction needs only
marginal uniformity plus a within-run union bound; independent repetitions are
valid only when the whole offset-and-sampling experiment is refreshed. The
sequential adaptive acquisition guarantee follows from a uniform conditional
bound and first-error union bound, not unconditional independence of stages.
