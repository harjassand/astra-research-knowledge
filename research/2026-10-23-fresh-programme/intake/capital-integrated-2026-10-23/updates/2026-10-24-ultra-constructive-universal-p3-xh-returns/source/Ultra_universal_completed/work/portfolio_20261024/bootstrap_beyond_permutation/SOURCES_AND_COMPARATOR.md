# Governing sources and strongest comparison

24 October 2026. Primary texts were opened, and the governing statements/proofs below were read. This is not a priority certification.

The observable quotient and finite Hankel realization are established. Thon and Jaeger, Propositions 1–2 and Section 3.3 (Definitions 28–29), describe the linear continuation space, controlled action/outcome interface and predictive coordinates. Our basis exhaustion uses that algebra with a supplied global rank bound and an observed two-preparation gap. We do not claim to invent observable operators. [Primary paper, JMLR 2015](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume16/thon15a/thon15a.pdf).

The strongest nearby reset predecessor is Even-Dar, Kakade and Mansour. Definition 3.1 and Lemmas 3.1–3.2 give approximate expected-belief reset and amplification in connected finite POMDPs. Their random-walk existence proof uses randomized numbers of real actions through fictitious stays. Theorem 4.5 uses a homing strategy as input for no-reset learning, with system-dependent homing time. Thus approximate preparation enabling later learning is already a known function; its existence and the use of repeated homing are not new claims here. The present finite-count result supplies a quantitative contraction certificate from scalar observations for a rank-two interface and a fixed positive word library. This also keeps the common completion time and hidden word-record contract explicit. [Primary paper, IJCAI 2005](https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/05/Papers/0685.pdf), pp. 2–5.

Balle and Maillard, Theorem 6, give finite-sample Hankel estimation from a single trajectory under a known finite-state policy, exploration and geometric-mixing assumptions. This is a stronger alternative when the goal is prediction/model acquisition without reproducible preparations. It does not require us to call all correlated data unusable. The theorem has supplied prefix/suffix sets and mixing parameters and does not output this finite randomized reset certificate. A retaining learner can use either method, or the new preparation itself, under the same access rights. [Primary paper, ICML 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/balle17a/balle17a.pdf), pp. 6–8.

Chevalier, Hendrickx and Jungers study products from known stochastic matrix sets. Proposition 1 connects asymptotic consensus to a positive-column product; Theorems 4–5 bound/find such products. A positive column is weaker than a rank-one reset, and no noisy operator acquisition is supplied by those statements. A conventional controller that fits the same direct contrasts and searches their positive hull is the relevant strong comparator in the present rank-two problem; it need not build a full OOM. [Primary paper, 2015](https://arxiv.org/html/1505.00144v2).

That direct conventional controller **ties**: affine regression divides each measured macro contrast by the same measured preparation gap; cancelling its weighted slopes gives exactly the same rational weight as cancelling raw contrasts. `check_operation.py` verifies this exact equality, including the acquired integer counts. The finite fixture's lack of an exact fixed-word reset is a separation of legal intervention classes, not a separation from an equally informed randomized controller. Published work, old Astra source access, a successful simulation and support-agent agreement do not establish novelty.
