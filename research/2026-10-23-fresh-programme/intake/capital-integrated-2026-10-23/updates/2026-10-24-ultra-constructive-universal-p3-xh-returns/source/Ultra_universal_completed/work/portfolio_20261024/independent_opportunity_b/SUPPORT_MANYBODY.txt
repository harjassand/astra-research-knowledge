# Costed operation: Gibbs-state source for a shallow-dressed open XX chain

## Status and boundary

This is a closed, exact state-preparation construction for one explicitly specified family. It is **not a new algorithm or a generic interacting-Gibbs capability**: its engine is standard fermionic Gaussian/matchgate preparation, and the output Hamiltonian is related to a free chain by a known one-layer circuit. The useful point is a fully costed operation at inverse temperature growing with system size, with a local noncommuting Pauli Hamiltonian as its advertised target. Do not generalize it to unknown Hamiltonians or generic 1D systems.

## Input and target

Take an even number $n$ of qubits on an open line, with known $J>0$, $\mu$, and $\beta$, and
\[
 H_0=-\frac J2\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(X_jX_{j+1}+Y_jY_{j+1})
       +\mu\sum_{j=1}^n n_j,\qquad n_j=(I-Z_j)/2.
\]

Let $W=\prod_{j=1,3,5,\ldots<n}\mathrm{CZ}_{j,j+1}$, a single parallel matching layer, and set $H'=WH_0W^\dagger$. The advertised output is the exact Gibbs state
\[
 \rho_\beta(H')=W\rho_\beta(H_0)W^\dagger.
\]
Conjugation makes the Pauli terms of $H'$ noncommuting wherever the original adjacent-bond terms failed to commute. Each term has support on at most four consecutive sites: the CZ layer can add at most one neighboring $Z$ at either endpoint. This is a local noncommuting Hamiltonian, but a deliberately easy isospectral subclass; the shallow dressing is visible and known.

For a size-dependent low-temperature instance, choose $J=1,\mu=0,\beta=n$. The closest single-particle energy to zero has magnitude
\[
 \Delta_n=2J\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2(n+1)}\right)\sim\frac{\pi J}{n},
\]
so $\beta\Delta_n\to\pi$. This is not a fixed-temperature application of the 1D constant-depth Gibbs theorem.

## Exact preparation

With Jordan-Wigner fermions $c_j$, $H_0=\sum_{ij}c_i^\dagger K_{ij}c_j$, where $K_{jj}=\mu$ and $K_{j,j+1}=K_{j+1,j}=-J$. The known sine transform
\[
 V_{jk}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+1}}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi jk}{n+1}\right),\quad
 \epsilon_k=\mu-2J\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{n+1}\right)
\]
obeys $K=V\,\mathrm{diag}(\epsilon_k)\,V^T$. Thus, in the mode basis, the Gibbs state is the product
\[
 \bigotimes_{k=1}^n\big[(1-f_k)|0\rangle\langle0|+f_k|1\rangle\langle1|\big],
 \qquad f_k=(1+e^{\beta\epsilon_k})^{-1}.
\]

For each copy, draw independent classical bits $\eta_k\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(f_k)$, initialize the mode register to $|\eta_1\cdots\eta_n\rangle$, apply the second-quantized mode transform $\Gamma(V)$, then apply $W$. Averaging over the bits gives exactly $\rho_\beta(H')$; no bath, phase estimation, postselection, or quantum ancilla is used. The random record must be hidden from downstream conditioning or erased: conditional on a revealed record the output is a pure Slater determinant, not the Gibbs state.

The one-particle matrix $V$ is implemented by adjacent Givens rotations, each a nearest-neighbor matchgate. A standard elimination schedule uses at most $n(n-1)/2$ rotations and $O(n)$ parallel layers; a possible determinant sign is corrected by one local mode phase. The final dressing needs $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ nearest-neighbor CZ gates in one layer. At most $n$ X gates load the sampled occupations.

## Resource and precision ledger

- **Quantum controls per copy:** $M\le n(n-1)/2+1$ matchgates/phases, at most $n$ occupation-load X gates, and $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ CZ gates; total $O(n^2)$ two-qubit gates, $O(n)$ depth, $n$ qubits, no ancillas, no postselection. Repeat the full circuit once per output copy.
- **Classical randomness:** represent $f_k$ by $q_k=m_k/2^b$, with $b\ge\lceil\log_2(2n/\varepsilon)\rceil$ fresh bits per mode and independent thresholding. Then $|q_k-f_k|\le2^{-b}$, so the product-mode distribution has total-variation error at most $n2^{-b}\le\varepsilon/2$. The random bits or their seed must not leak to a consumer who conditions on them.
- **Gate precision:** write $G=M+n+\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. If each compiled gate differs in operator norm by at most $\varepsilon/(2G)$, telescoping bounds its contribution to output trace distance $D(\rho,\sigma)=\tfrac12\|\rho-\sigma\|_1$ by at most $\varepsilon/2$. For a Givens rotation generated by a norm-one operator, angle error at most $\varepsilon/(2G)$ suffices. The classical Bernoulli approximation plus control error therefore gives $D\le\varepsilon$. This is an ideal gate-error contract, not an acquired hardware calibration.
- **Classical computation:** $O(n)$ stable logistic evaluations and the known sine-transform decomposition; with $b_c$ bits of working precision, standard arithmetic is polynomial in $n,b_c,\log(1/\varepsilon)$ and input bit length. No Hamiltonian-learning or spectral-oracle calls occur. The homogeneous-chain eigenpairs are analytic.
- **Observed estimates, if requested:** for an observable of operator norm at most one, $N=O(\varepsilon_{obs}^{-2}\log(1/\delta))$ independent prepared copies suffice for additive error $\varepsilon_{obs}$ and failure probability $\delta$ by Hoeffding. This readout cost is in addition to preparation; the construction itself outputs the state and need not estimate an observable.

## Finite exactness check

An independent dense $n=4$ calculation used the determinant (exterior-power) lift of $V$ on each fixed-particle-number sector. It checked $V^TV=I$, $K=V\mathrm{diag}(\epsilon)V^T$, and equality of (i) the direct 16-dimensional Gibbs matrix $e^{-\beta H_0}/Z$ and (ii) the mode-product ensemble rotated by $\Gamma(V)$. It then repeated the comparison after the CZ dressing. At $J=1,\mu=0,\beta=4$, the reported trace distances were $3.89\times10^{-16}$ both before and after dressing; the one-particle diagonalization residual was $5.50\times10^{-16}$. These are floating-point fixture checks; the equations above give the exact proof for all $n,J,\mu,\beta$.

The following NumPy fragment reproduces the check. In the occupation-bit basis, the fermionic lift has matrix elements $\Gamma(V)_{S,T}=\det V_{S,T}$ on equal-particle-number sectors.

    import numpy as np
    n, J, mu, beta = 4, 1., 0., 4.
    V = np.array([[np.sqrt(2/(n+1))*np.sin(np.pi*j*k/(n+1))
                   for k in range(1,n+1)] for j in range(1,n+1)])
    eps = np.array([mu-2*J*np.cos(np.pi*k/(n+1)) for k in range(1,n+1)])
    K = np.diag([mu]*n) + np.diag([-J]*(n-1),1) + np.diag([-J]*(n-1),-1)
    D = 1 << n
    Gamma = np.zeros((D,D))
    for t in range(D):
        modes = [k for k in range(n) if t >> k & 1]
        for s in range(D):
            sites = [j for j in range(n) if s >> j & 1]
            if len(modes) == len(sites):
                Gamma[s,t] = 1 if not modes else np.linalg.det(V[np.ix_(sites,modes)])
    def hop(s,i,j):
        if not (s >> j & 1) or (i != j and s >> i & 1): return None, 0
        sign = (-1)**((s & ((1<<j)-1)).bit_count()); t = s ^ (1<<j)
        sign *= (-1)**((t & ((1<<i)-1)).bit_count()); t |= (1<<i)
        return t, sign
    H = np.zeros((D,D))
    for s in range(D):
        for i in range(n):
            H[s,s] += mu*(s >> i & 1)
            for j in range(n):
                if abs(i-j) == 1:
                    t, sign = hop(s,i,j)
                    if t is not None: H[t,s] += -J*sign
    E,U = np.linalg.eigh(H)
    rho = (U*np.exp(-beta*E)) @ U.T; rho /= np.trace(rho)
    rmode = np.zeros((D,D))
    for s in range(D):
        rmode[s,s] = np.exp(-beta*sum(eps[k] for k in range(n) if s >> k & 1))
    rmode /= np.trace(rmode); rho2 = Gamma @ rmode @ Gamma.T
    phase = np.array([(-1)**(((s>>0&1)*(s>>1&1))+((s>>2&1)*(s>>3&1)))
                      for s in range(D)])  # CZ(0,1) CZ(2,3)
    Hp = phase[:,None]*H*phase[None,:]
    E2,U2 = np.linalg.eigh(Hp)
    rhop = (U2*np.exp(-beta*E2)) @ U2.T; rhop /= np.trace(rhop)
    rhoout = phase[:,None]*rho2*phase[None,:]
    print("K residual", np.max(np.abs(K-V@np.diag(eps)@V.T)))
    print("bare and dressed trace distances",
          .5*np.linalg.norm(rho-rho2,ord='nuc'),
          .5*np.linalg.norm(rhop-rhoout,ord='nuc'))

## Strong incumbent and significance

Langer et al. give a direct matchgate construction for fermionic Gaussian state preparation with $O(n^2)$ nearest-neighbor gates and $O(n)$ depth, so the central implementation is already supplied by established methods. Their result is the controlling incumbent, not an advantage over matchgates. The 2026 1D Gibbs theorem of Pilatowsky-Cameo et al. supplies a broader constant-depth classical-mixture representation at fixed $\beta$; its depth constant is doubly exponential in $\beta$, and it does not make this $\beta=n/J$ case practically comparable. That distinction does not make this dressed free family generic or novel.

No genuinely new many-body operation was identified here. The surviving artifact is a verifiable, resource-bounded baseline for testing a future generic Gibbs/cooling claim: it has exact output, explicit local controls, and an honest low-temperature scaling, while every structural simplification is public and supplied.
