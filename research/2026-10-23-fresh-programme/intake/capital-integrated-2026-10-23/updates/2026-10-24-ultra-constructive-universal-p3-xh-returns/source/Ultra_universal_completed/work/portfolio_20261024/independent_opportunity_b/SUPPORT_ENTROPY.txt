# Bounded entropy/QKD comparator — closed alternative

This was a finite-energy, finite-memory secret-key operation considered as an
alternative opportunity. It is retained as a scoped calculation, not as a
capacity result or a novelty claim. The active work has moved to the separate
C3 trust-resource operation; this note does not extend that construction.

## One explicit model point

Take a thermal-loss channel with transmissivity `eta=0.55` and environment
occupation `nu=1`. Its entanglement-breaking boundary is
`eta=nu/(nu+1)=0.5`, so this point is just on the non-entanglement-breaking
side. With mean input energy at most one photon, prepare the finite-support
state `sqrt(0.64)|00> + sqrt(0.36)|11>` and send one mode through the channel.
This uses 0.36 photons per transmitted state. Project the received mode onto
`{|0>,|1>}` and keep successful events. In the finite-dimensional channel
calculation, this filter succeeds with probability about `0.845144`; after
Bell twirling its Bell probabilities are approximately
`(0.528123, 0.127321, 0.172278, 0.172278)`. The largest probability exceeds
one half, certifying an entangled two-qubit state.

As a deliberately simple follow-on, apply ideal two-level entanglement pumping:
five successful bit-pumping rounds prepare a helper, then 192 successful
phase-pumping rounds purify the retained pair. The resulting Bell vector is
approximately `(0.5001148, 6.1e-10, 0.4998852, 6.1e-10)`. Measuring in the
key basis and applying the Devetak–Winter expression gives about
`2.93e-10` secret bits per successfully pumped output pair. Counting retry and
helper generation in this particular renewal model consumes approximately
`5.02e55` accepted filtered pairs per output pair, or about `4.9e-66` secret
bits per channel use after the filter success probability. This is a finite,
positive arithmetic example with a uselessly small rate, not a practical
protocol. It is a model calculation, not an experiment. The local-memory
workspace must hold the current pair and a pumping helper; exact transient
workspace depends on the chosen recurrence implementation.

## The direct incumbents close the broad claim

Mele, Lami, and Giovannetti prove that a thermal attenuator has positive
two-way quantum/key capacity exactly on the non-entanglement-breaking side.
Their supplementary Theorem S9 already supplies a finite-energy construction:
a finite-Fock-support entangled input, Bob's `{|0>,|M>}` projection, Bell twirl,
P1/P2 recurrence, and an improved hashing rate. The energy constraint is
`(1-c^2)M <= Ns`; the displayed rate includes filter success, recurrence
success, and the batching factor. Thus a finite-energy positive-rate theorem
or this channel/filter operation is already incumbent.

The proposed small-workspace bit-then-phase purification is also standard.
Jiang, Taylor, and Lukin's two-level entanglement-pumping protocol first
reduces bit errors, then uses the bit-purified pairs to reduce phase errors;
Appendix A proves convergence to arbitrarily high fidelity under ideal local
operations, and Appendix B counts its success/failure Markov process. Dür et
al.'s earlier Scheme C is a related two-pair pumping protocol, though its
fixed-helper version alone has a non-unit fidelity fixed point. Devetak–Winter
classical post-processing after measurement is an ordinary key-extraction
step, not a new physical resource.

Therefore the exact finite-memory composition does not establish a new
capacity region, a near-boundary practical rate, or a new pumping mechanism.
It should remain an alternative benchmark only. No result here compares
favorably with the established MLG recurrence-plus-hashing construction at
matched rate, memory, or implementation cost.

The source check is limited to these direct protocol/capacity comparators and
the stated model point. No laboratory performance, channel capacity value, or
advantage over the incumbents is claimed.
