1. SIGNED STATE AND COMPONENT FACTORIZATION -- PASS

For symmetric diagonal-zero F, the pair creation generator is the sum over
unordered edges F_ij B^dagger_ij. Disjoint even bond creators commute. Single
occupancy retains precisely perfect matchings, and the m! orderings cancel the
exponential factorial. Moving to a physical site-ordered spin basis leaves
matching-dependent signs. Thus a positive-overlap replacement is invalid.

For an ordered pair (M,N), contract the reference dimers and partition the union
of selected weak edges into connected components. Each matching restricts to
the physical sites of each component; no selected edge crosses components.
The Fock reordering sign between disjoint site components is fixed by their
one-particle-per-site occupations, and is the same for bra and ket. It cancels
in the overlap. Equivalently the components have even fermionic parity. This
proves signed overlap factorization, including for interleaved site sets.
An inactive reference dimer contributes norm 2. Summing over matching pairs
inside each active component gives the stated polymer activity. Consequently
Z/2^m is exactly the disjoint-support gas. Adjacency alone is not incompatibility.
The two-dimer value 1-lambda^2+lambda^4 correctly has a negative activity.

2. ALL-SIZE ACTIVITY ESTIMATE AND KP TAIL -- PASS

For one matching with active dimer set A, both sites of every active dimer use
weak edges, so there are |A| weak edges. At each recursive first-unmatched-site
choice, the sum of absolute incident weights is at most rho. Induction bounds
the sum of weak matching magnitudes on these sites by rho^|A|, even when an
intermediate remaining set no longer consists of whole dimers.

On a size-s component the normalized unfiltered matching overlap is at most 1
by Cauchy--Schwarz, because each matching vector has norm 2^(s/2). The three
allowed active-copy statuses per dimer give

 |w(C)| <= sum_(A union B=C) rho^(|A|+|B|) = (2rho+rho^2)^s = h^s.

Connectivity restrictions only reduce this absolute sum. Rooted plane-tree
encoding bounds the number of connected s-sets through a vertex by
(4Dg)^(s-1). With x=4Dg exp(1+beta)h,

 sum_(C contains v) |w(C)| exp((1+beta)|C|)
 <= x^2/[4Dg(1-x)] <= 1/(8Dg) <= 1       if x<=1/2.

Summing over the vertices of a polymer proves KP for a(C)=|C| and
d(C)=beta|C|. Add zero-weight singleton polymers with a=1; they do not change
the partition. The KP anchored bound at each singleton is at most 1. Every
nonempty cluster meets some vertex, hence

 sum_(cluster total size >= K) |cluster coefficient|
 <= exp(-beta K) sum_v anchored_tilted_sum(v)
 <= m exp(-beta K).

This handles repeated polymers and complex/signed activities. The rational
test with 3^(beta+1) is sufficient since e<3. At rho_goal=1/(384Dg), h<=3rho_goal
gives the unfiltered test value <=9/32. Multiplication by (21/19)^4<3/2 leaves
the filtered value <=27/64<1/2. Thus beta=1 is genuinely available. The
m*2^(-beta K) selection is conservative because e^(-beta K)<=2^(-beta K).

Real activities, zero freeness along u in [0,1], and Xi(0)=1 fix the positive
real logarithm. No sign sampling or partition oracle is imported.

3. MATCHING AND GAUGE ACQUISITION / COMPLETENESS -- PASS

Any alternative reference matching changes k>=2 dimers. In a unit-reference
gauge it has k weak edges, so its magnitude product is at most rho^k<=rho^2.
Every perfect matching receives the same diagonal-gauge magnitude factor.
Thus the maximum-product matching is unique under the promise. Approximating
each edge log magnitude to 1/(100n) changes the comparison of two n/2-edge
matching weights by at most .01. The robust certified regime has a much larger
gap. Ordinary polynomial weighted perfect matching therefore acquires the
correct reference on the promised class; exhaustive enumeration in the small
checker is not the claimed general subroutine. A graph with no perfect matching
has zero projected state because each nonzero determinant term would supply one.

After a_first=1, a_second=1/F_ref, any further gauge preserving reference
amplitudes one has opposite log-magnitudes x_a,-x_a on the two sites. For each
weak-edge inequality sigma_i x_a+sigma_j x_b<=c, a feasible x gives signed-node
potentials u_(a,+)=x_a,u_(a,-)=-x_a. Conversely, adding the paired difference
constraints and dividing by two yields feasibility of
x_a=(u_(a,+)-u_(a,-))/2. Thus the signed-node construction is exact.

Integer difference weights give Bellman--Ford potentials bounded by the length
of a simple path times the maximum absolute edge weight. Rounding each x to an
integer raises a two-endpoint exponent by at most one. The ceil-log and floor
threshold then give each weak magnitude <=rho_goal/Delta, so each row is bounded
by rho_goal. For completeness, a continuous gauge with row bound
rho_goal/(8Delta) gives

 ceil(log2|C_ij|)+sigma_i x_a+sigma_j x_b
 < log2(rho_goal/(4Delta)) <= floor(log2(rho_goal/(2Delta))).

Hence its constraints are feasible; the subsequent rounding bound still holds.
This proves the declared robust sufficient recognition class, not recognition
of every useful gauge. Delta=0 / Dg=0 is the separately exact product case.

For Q(i) entries, squared magnitudes are rational. Their ceil-log comparisons
are exact integer/rational comparisons and require no real-number oracle.
Input bit lengths bound these exponents; simple-path potentials and actual
dyadic scalings therefore have polynomial bit length. The final manuscript now
explicitly uses rational rho_hat upper envelopes: per-edge dyadic bounds or
certified upward square-root intervals suffice. It does not require exact
efficient comparison of arbitrary sums of square roots. This resolves the one
precision wording gap found in the initial version.

4. EXPLICIT TRUNCATED-LOG ALGORITHM / BIT COST -- PASS

In log Xi_T(u), a cluster occurs iff its union U is contained in T. The Mobius
sum over T subset S annihilates it unless U=S. A connected incompatibility
cluster consists of intersecting connected polymer supports, so U is connected
in the native contracted graph. Its total marker degree is at least |U|.
Therefore summing ell(S;u) over connected |S|<K, modulo u^K and then setting u=1,
is exactly the required cluster truncation. Repeated polymers have the same
small union but larger marker degree; the formal log includes their cumulants.

For each S, the inactive-or-one-polymer recurrence computes Xi_T for every
T subset S. Subset enumeration, local matching pairs, explicit spin amplitudes,
and finite formal-log arithmetic take exp(O_Delta(|S|)) times polynomial K work.
There are m exp(O_Dg(K)) connected S to enumerate. Each local rational sum has a
common denominator formed from polynomially many local input denominators;
the log raises their powers at most K and introduces only integer divisors
through K. Bit growth is polynomial in input length, |S| and K. A common global
denominator can likewise use all input edge denominators to bounded O(K) powers,
so summing over S does not hide exponentially long exact rationals. This proves
the stated polynomial cost for fixed degrees and positive fixed beta, rather
than merely an arithmetic-oracle estimate. Exponentiation needs declared
relative precision; the known gauge/reference factors are paid.

5. LOCAL FILTERS / NORMALIZATION / ERROR -- PASS

On a size-s component with at most 2s filtered sites, ||A||<=(1+t)^(2s), while
the filtered reference norm is at least 2^s(1-t)^(4s). Cauchy--Schwarz therefore
bounds its normalized matching overlap by ((1+t)/(1-t))^(4s). Filters factor
across sites and components and preserve occupancy. This yields the claimed
filtered activity envelope and maintains KP without any conditional projection.

Expanding A_s^dagger A_s and taking the finite sign average selects exactly
the desired Pauli product. In the norm-error calculation,
Z_s/Z<=(1+t)^(2r) and |<P>|<=1. Relative errors at most delta in all norms give

 |estimated <P> - <P>| <= delta/(1-delta) * [((1+t)^2/(2t))^r+1].

At t=1/20 this is the claimed (441/40)^r factor. The supplied delta choice
suffices for the usual 0<epsilon<=1 (larger requested errors can be clipped).
Truncation and log-rounding errors <=delta/4 each imply relative norm error
below delta. Cost includes 2^r filtered norms and the amplification in required
precision; fixed r is essential. Summing Pauli-term errors gives the energy
certificate but no ground-state quality or optimization certificate.

Minor cost clarification, not a defect: for an arbitrary explicitly supplied
H=sum h_a P_a, the prescribed runtime depends on sum|h_a|/epsilon_E. Without a
coefficient normalization this may be exponential in coefficient bit length.
The proof correctly supplies this accuracy dependence; it should not be
retold as a bit-polynomial absolute-energy algorithm independent of that norm.
