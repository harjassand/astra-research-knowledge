# SUPPORT RECOVERY RESOURCES — cubic-phase kernel and sparse output-Pauli errors

## Scope and verdict

Consider a known diagonal third-level kernel

\[
U_f|x\rangle=(-1)^{f(x)}|x\rangle,
\qquad f:\mathbb F_2^n\to\mathbb F_2,\quad \deg f\le 3,
\]

available exactly once as a physical black-box operation, followed by a noise
channel whose Kraus operators lie in a specified sparse span of output Paulis.
The proposed operation is coherent pre/post Pauli checks that read a syndrome
and correct the known output Pauli error without a second kernel query.

The correction interface is established: it is a specialization of general
Pauli-check sandwiching / commutation-derived channel filtering, not a new
one-query error-correction primitive. The useful narrower result is a
structure-aware compiler for the prechecks. A literal monomial expansion gives
the claimed \(O(n^2)\) non-Clifford gates per check, but this is not tight:
quadratic-form reduction lowers the controlled-phase count to at most
\(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) CCZ gates per check, while the associated CNOT basis
change still costs \(O(n^2)\) Clifford gates in the worst case. For
\(r=O(\log n)\) checks the conservative total is therefore
\(O(n\log n)\) CCZ-equivalents plus \(O(n^2\log n)\) Clifford two-qubit gates,
in addition to ancillas, measurement, and the one noisy-kernel call.

This can be a resource niche versus a *literal* clean dense-CCZ expansion,
but it is not yet an advantage over an equally informed fault-tolerant
compiler. There is no general \(\Theta(n^3)\) gate-synthesis lower bound from
having \(\binom n3\) cubic monomials. More decisively, local faults inside a
termwise kernel need not remain in the assumed sparse output-Pauli span.
Consequently the favorable one-query result is conditional on a genuine
post-kernel Pauli-channel interface, not on circuit locality alone.

## Exact precheck algebra

Write an output Pauli as \(Q_{a,b}=X^aZ^b\), with \(a,b\in\mathbb F_2^n\).
Its backward image is

\[
U_f^\dagger Q_{a,b}U_f|x\rangle
 =(-1)^{q_{a,b}(x)}|x+a\rangle,
\qquad
q_{a,b}(x)=f(x+a)+f(x)+b\cdot x.
\]

Thus \(U_f^\dagger Q_{a,b}U_f=X^aD_{q_{a,b}}\), using the convention
\(X^aD_q|x\rangle=(-1)^{q(x)}|x+a\rangle\). Since the first difference of a
cubic Boolean polynomial is quadratic, \(q_{a,b}\) has degree at most two.
For Hermitian Pauli conventions that include a known phase on \(X^aZ^b\),
that phase is tracked as a known control phase; it does not change the gate
counts.
For a cubic monomial,

\[
\Delta_a(x_ix_jx_k)=
a_i x_jx_k+a_jx_ix_k+a_kx_ix_j
+a_ia_jx_k+a_ia_kx_j+a_ja_kx_i+a_ia_ja_k.
\]

If \(c_{ijk}\) is the coefficient of \(x_ix_jx_k\), the coefficient of
\(x_ix_j\) in the derivative's quadratic part is
\(\sum_{k\notin\{i,j\}} c_{ijk}a_k\) (with indices symmetrized). Hence the
direct ANF contains at most \(\binom n2\) quadratic terms, \(O(n)\) linear
terms, and a constant.

For one syndrome control bit \(s\), the direct implementation of the
controlled precheck uses at most:

| Operation | Direct ANF count per check |
|---|---:|
| Controlled quadratic monomial \((-1)^{s x_ix_j}\) | \(\binom n2\) CCZ gates |
| Controlled linear phase \((-1)^{s x_i}\) | at most \(n\) CZ gates |
| Conditional shift \(|x\rangle\mapsto|x+a\rangle\) | \(|a|\le n\) CNOT gates |
| Constant conditional phase | at most one Z on the control |
| Output postcheck \(Q_{a,b}\) | \(O(n)\) controlled Pauli gates |

The direct count is a valid upper bound, not an optimal CCZ count.

### Quadratic-form reduction

Define the polar form

\[
B_q(u,v)=q(u+v)+q(u)+q(v)+q(0).
\]

It is alternating over \(\mathbb F_2\), so its rank is \(2s\). A symplectic
basis for its nondegenerate part, followed by a basis of its radical, gives an
invertible linear coordinate change \(y=Mx\) under which

\[
q(M^{-1}y)=\sum_{j=1}^{s} y_{2j-1}y_{2j}+\ell(y)+c.
\]

The difference between the transformed quadratic polynomial and the displayed
pair terms has zero polar form, hence is linear plus a constant. Apply the
linear reversible circuit for \(M\), the controlled phase gates, and \(M^{-1}\)
to implement \(D_q\) in the controlled branch. This uses exactly \(s\le
\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) CCZ gates for the quadratic part, at most \(n\) CZ gates
for \(\ell\), and \(O(n^2)\) CNOT gates for the two basis transforms. The
controlled shift adds at most \(n\) more CNOTs. The same bound applies even
when the syndrome control is in superposition: the unconditional basis change
and inverse cancel in the control-zero branch.

Accordingly, for checks \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_r\), the exact, auditable upper bounds
are

\[
N_{\rm CCZ}\le \sum_{j=1}^r\frac{\operatorname{rank}(B_{q_j})}{2}
\le r\lfloor n/2\rfloor,
\qquad N_{\rm Clifford}=O(rn^2).
\]

If CCZ is priced by its standard exact Clifford+T realization, the T-count
bound is seven times the CCZ subtotal; device-specific CCZ factories and
fault-tolerant overhead are additional. The literal ANF compiler instead has
\(N_{\rm CCZ}\le r\binom n2\). Both are polynomial and correct, but only the
rank-reduced count reflects the best simple construction.

## Syndrome count and what is actually corrected

For the single-qubit Pauli set
\(\mathcal E_1=\{I,X_i,Y_i,Z_i:1\le i\le n\}\), every correctable error
must have a distinct syndrome, so any deterministic syndrome readout obeys

\[
r\ge\lceil\log_2(3n+1)\rceil.
\]

An \(O(\log n)\)-check construction exists. For example, take
\(r=2m\) with \(n\le(4^m-1)/3\), select \(n\) distinct two-dimensional
subspaces in \(\mathbb F_2^{2m}\) whose nonzero vectors are disjoint, and
assign the three nonzero vectors in each subspace to the site’s \(X,Y,Z\)
syndromes. A full spread realizes the bound when its size suffices. This uses
\(r\le\log_2(3n)+2\) checks. For an arbitrary specified finite error set of
size \(M\), random binary check rows separate all errors with positive
probability using \(r=O(\log M)\); the simple union bound gives a conservative
\(r>2\log_2(M+1)\).

For each check \(Q_j\), use its backward image \(P_j=U_f^\dagger Q_jU_f\)
before the kernel and \(Q_j^\dagger\) after it. Fix a check order and reverse
the order of the post-products: for control string \(s\), use
\(P_r^{s_r}\cdots P_1^{s_1}\) before the kernel and
\(Q_1^{-s_1}\cdots Q_r^{-s_r}\) after it. This cancels the ideal branch even
when the \(Q_j\)'s do not commute. In a controlled branch,
\(Q_j^\dagger U_fP_j=U_f\). If an output Pauli \(E\) occurs after the
kernel, the branch acquires the sign determined by whether \(E\) commutes
with \(Q_j\). Measuring all \(r\) control bits therefore produces the
symplectic syndrome of \(E\). When the supported errors have distinct
syndromes, apply the corresponding \(E^\dagger\) correction. This also works
for Kraus operators that are coherent linear combinations of the supported
Paulis: the syndrome measurement projects each branch onto one supported
Pauli, and the known correction leaves \(U_f\) on the data. If multiple
supported errors share a syndrome, the scheme needs the usual
Knill–Laflamme/degeneracy condition; “few Paulis” alone does not imply
correctability.

For \(r=2m=O(\log n)\) single-qubit-error checks, the check-stage resource is
therefore at most \(O(n\log n)\) CCZ-equivalents and \(O(n^2\log n)\) Clifford
two-qubit gates, with \(O(\log n)\) syndrome ancillas, plus a one-call noisy
\(U_f\). If the full noise set is larger, substitute its actual check count.
Ancilla preparation/readout/reset, control errors, latency, and decoding are
not zero-cost and are not included in these gate counts.

## Matched incumbents and fair cost boundary

1. **Pauli Check Sandwiching (PCS).** Gonzales et al. explicitly allow a
   general check pair \(C_1,C_2\) satisfying \(C_2UC_1=U\) for arbitrary
   target \(U\); choosing \(C_2=Q^\dagger\),
   \(C_1=U^\dagger Q U\) is exactly this construction. Their protocol
   postselects the check outcome to remove anticommuting output errors. The
   paper distinguishes this general version from its efficient version,
   which restricts checks to Pauli operators and therefore applies directly
   when \(U\) is Clifford. Its Section III-E reports that the general checks
   may be more complicated to synthesize. The derivative/rank compiler here
   is a family-specific resource refinement of that known freedom, not a new
   check principle. Turning separate postselections into a full syndrome and
   deterministic correction is standard syndrome-based recovery under the
   assumed known error set.

2. **Coherent Pauli Checks and quantum filters.** van den Berg et al. give
   the coherent one-ancilla, two-sided check for Clifford payloads and include
   noise in the check circuit. Das et al. give a one-query channel-filter
   framework; for Clifford \(C\), their precheck is the conjugated Pauli
   \(C^\dagger Q C\), and their Pauli-filter scheme corrects arbitrary noise
   with \(2n\) clean ancillas. They also show partial purification for
   non-Clifford T and CCZ gates. Thus the one-query filter premise, the
   conjugated-precheck algebra, and the Clifford/non-Clifford distinction
   are already explicit in the incumbent. The potential delta is solely a
   compact compiler for this cubic Boolean family and a carefully chosen
   sparse error set; compare its exact gates against a fully synthesized
   filter rather than counting a general controlled unitary as one gate.

3. **Fault-tolerant code/spacetime implementations.** These target a
   circuit-level noise model, where faults may occur at many locations and
   syndrome extraction is repeated around the implemented logical operation.
   They pay encoded-gate, ancilla, measurement, and spacetime-volume overhead,
   but they can address internal faults that invalidate an output-Pauli-only
   promise. This is a different interface from one post-kernel channel. A
   fair comparison must either (a) hold the kernel fixed as an atomic channel
   with certified output-Pauli noise, or (b) compare complete circuit-level
   fault-tolerant implementations including the kernel and check circuitry.
   Comparing the check layer to the literal \(\binom n3\)-CCZ expansion while
   ignoring check faults is a favorable upper-bound comparison, not a matched
   hardware benchmark.

4. **Gate teleportation / injection.** Teleporting a known \(U_f\) through a
   Choi or magic resource state is a standard alternative. If a clean verified
   resource is supplied, its preparation/factory cost is outside the
   one-noisy-kernel contract and must be charged. If the one available noisy
   kernel prepares that state, a post-kernel Pauli fault is transferred into
   the resource and then onto the teleported output; it has not been removed.
   Clifford-hierarchy byproduct correction is available, but it does not
   distinguish the unknown sparse output fault for free. Injection is not a
   lower-cost comparator unless the resource-state and verification budgets
   are stated.

### No unsupported \(\Theta(n^3)\) optimality claim

If every cubic monomial is implemented literally as its own CCZ, then the
kernel uses exactly \(\binom n3=\Theta(n^3)\) CCZs. That is a fair count for
that direct ANF implementation. It is not a proven lower bound for exact
Clifford+CCZ synthesis: CNOT basis changes can combine terms, and structured
cubic tensors can have compact decompositions. A general lower bound must
specify the gate set, ancillas, allowed parity computation, and whether
non-diagonal intermediate gates are allowed. No such lower bound is used
here. The only unconditional lower bound recorded above is the syndrome-bit
count for the specified error family.

## Circuit-level fault-spread counterexample

Take the fully dense phase

\[
f(x)=\sum_{1\le i<j<k\le n}x_ix_jx_k,
\]

implemented by its literal commuting CCZ list. Order first the CCZs whose
triples do not contain qubit 1, then the CCZs whose triples do contain qubit
1, and insert a single physical \(X_1\) fault between those two gate blocks.
The suffix phase is \(g(x)=x_1\sum_{2\le i<j\le n}x_ix_j\); the relative output
operator is

\[
U_fX_1U_f^\dagger|x\rangle
=(-1)^{f(x)+f(x+e_1)}|x+e_1\rangle
=X_1\prod_{2\le i<j\le n}\mathrm{CZ}_{ij}|x\rangle.
\]

The quadratic phase on the last line contains \(\binom{n-1}{2}\) pair terms.
For \(n\ge4\), this relative error is a non-Pauli Clifford operator, not one
of the assumed sparse output Paulis. It is a legal internal single-location
fault in this literal circuit model. A fault later in the list similarly
acquires the derivative of the remaining cubic suffix. Therefore the
one-query correction theorem is conditional on noise that truly occurs
*after* the entire physical kernel and lies in the advertised Pauli span.
Locality of primitive gate faults does not establish that condition.

## Decisive comparison gate

The candidate remains worth a matched resource test only if its contract
provides all of the following:

- a single physical kernel invocation with output noise certified to be in a
  known sparse Pauli span;
- trusted, fault-tolerant controlled-Clifford prechecks, including their
  \(O(n^2)\) CNOT basis changes per check, and controlled output-Pauli checks;
- an explicit syndrome set that separates the entire Kraus support and an
  error-correction rule for coherent Kraus superpositions;
- an all-in cost comparison against (i) general PCS/QF compiled using the
  same cubic structure, (ii) verified gate injection if resource states are
  available, and (iii) circuit-level QEC when the noise is internal;
- no assertion that literal \(\Theta(n^3)\) monomial count proves an optimal
  clean-gate lower bound.

Without a demonstrated post-kernel Pauli interface and a fully charged
compiler comparison, classify the result as **known one-query syndrome
recovery with an improved algebraic precheck upper bound**, not as a new
fault-tolerant kernel or established hardware advantage.
