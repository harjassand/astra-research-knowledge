I moved the active calculation from the kinase to an explicitly specified actomyosin crossbridge model and obtained a discriminating result: at fixed chemical drive, changing the mean filament gap by 2 nm reverses the work produced by the same small deformation cycle. The sign difference survives averaging over unknown axial registry between motors and binding sites.

That result survives thermodynamic and numerical checks, but it does not establish a new materials operation. The strongest prior work already contains two-coordinate crossbridge engines. What this attack adds is a concrete relation between deformation, pathway access, occupancy and force—and a sharper identification of the attachment mechanics that a realization must supply.

The catalytic-cycle construction, thermodynamic completion, force-transmission results and DNA cassette/release/reset work remain retained.

Why actomyosin was the stronger attainable testbed

The unresolved kinase attachment map would still require supplying transverse kinetics and force response that its one-axis measurements do not determine. Actomyosin offered a better starting point because published spatial crossbridge models explicitly relate axial offset and filament separation to head geometry, thermal binding access and forces in both directions. Independent muscle experiments also establish that different biochemical states have different radial mechanical responses. Those experiments support the physical relevance of the coupling; they do not calibrate the particular model used here. PLOS Computational Biology+1

The conventional comparison is decisive. Shankar and Mahadevan’s 2024 work already describes quasistatic work from axial–radial deformation cycles, including the need to account for both force components. Tune and Sponberg’s 2025 spatial model already shows gap-dependent changes between positive and negative axial work under phasic activation. Neither the broad engine principle nor “spacing can change motor/brake behavior” remains an invention claim. softmath.seas.harvard.edu+1

I therefore used a narrower question:

With a fixed chemical affinity and an explicit geometrical binding mechanism, does the predicted work survive energy-consistent forces, reverse reactions, confinement forces and averaging over competing binding sites?

It does, for the completed model below.

The sign difference survives unknown axial registry

The physical attachment remains the decisive boundary

The calculation requires more than two coordinates written on a diagram. It requires two independent relative translations and a defined reference frame for the myosin base and actin binding face.

The strongest retrieved actomyosin assembly example places motors on a DNA-origami thick-filament mimic. Its S2-mimicking connectors are 40 nm long and can pivot substantially; reported angular distributions differ between free and rigor conditions. This establishes a useful assembly and imaging platform, but scaffold placement cannot simply be substituted for a rigid motor pose. nature.com+2

A free swivel removes this transmitted torsional stiffness in the illustrative model. Recovering the specified crossbridge mechanics requires Kψ≫kθK_\psi\gg k_\thetaKψ​≫kθ​, together with sufficiently small motion under load. The proof does not need an invented numerical mobility. It does need control of force- and rate-weighted tails; local smoothness alone is insufficient. The infinite-target extension additionally needs a controlled truncation or corresponding countable-state bounds.

This completes a mathematical continuity argument. It supplies neither an attainable stiffness threshold nor an attachment chemistry. It also does not justify simply convolving the rigid force field with a Gaussian: finite compliance generally produces a joint nonequilibrium distribution of geometry and chemical state.

The stronger physical alternative—and its limits

The most relevant new boundary platform found is the MAESTRO DNA-origami force-spectroscopy preprint. It supplies real multidirectional molecular loading through ssDNA entropic springs and a rigid ring. Its four-arm Holliday-junction experiment uses separately assembled loading configurations, including nominal 3 pN loading along orthogonal axes. This is substantially better evidence for multiple molecular load paths than a proposed third anchor. It does not demonstrate two independently cycled displacement ports. PubMed+1

A source correction matters here: the T7 endonuclease cleavage experiment is single-axis, and the binding-state measurements use cleavage-suppressing calcium conditions. The accessible v2 cleavage method also lists calcium without clearly specifying the activation change. I could not verify the revised chemistry in v3. That is a reporting ambiguity, not grounds to dismiss the observed products. No release/reset catalytic-force cycle is established by these experiments. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

The useful physical variables of such a scaffold are anchor positions and spring contour lengths,

λ={Ai,Ni}.\lambda=\{\mathbf A_i,N_i\}.λ={Ai​,Ni​}.

Actual spring forces depend on molecular pose and conformation:

Therefore several nominal force labels cannot replace joint force and torque balance. Changing a spring’s contour length is also a different operation from moving a mechanical anchor, with different energy accounting.

The strongest independent computational alternative remains the chemically fueled particle motor developed by Albaugh and Gingrich: catalytic access, blocking occupancy and mechanical interactions arise from common particle coordinates. That is a sound route for avoiding arbitrary transverse rate laws. However, its published geometry-dependent current reversal is already prior work, and obtaining a two-attachment force response would require a new attachment potential and new stationary sampling. The inspected public sensitivity data do not supply those force observables. nature.com+3

Current scientific decision

Actomyosin remains the completed computational benchmark; the kinase remains a preserved candidate. The attained result is a fixed-affinity, geometry-based work prediction with explicit source corrections, competing-target normalization and a verified registry average. Its broad operation is conventional gap-controlled active traction.

The next development priority is now narrowly physical: a specified scaffold must retain two independent transmitted deformations after its swivels, rotations and tether extensions relax. The relevant outputs are the transfer map, conjugate forces and torques, and the geometry distribution experienced by each chemical state.

The retrieved myosin assembly does not yet identify that map. MAESTRO supplies a stronger example of actual multidirectional loading, but its existing measurements do not complete a continuously fueled mechanical junction. These are distinct limitations: neither is a universal impossibility, and neither is resolved merely by access to equipment.

The useful advance at this checkpoint is that the missing input is now explicit. Another turnover fit or stiffness sweep would not establish it. A calibrated attachment map could turn the present prediction into a test of a molecular realization; without that map, claiming a new material would outrun the evidence.
