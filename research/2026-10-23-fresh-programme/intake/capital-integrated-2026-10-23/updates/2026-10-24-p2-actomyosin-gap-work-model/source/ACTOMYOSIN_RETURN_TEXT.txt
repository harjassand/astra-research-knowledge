I moved the active calculation from the kinase to an explicitly specified actomyosin crossbridge model and obtained a discriminating result: at fixed chemical drive, changing the mean filament gap by 2 nm reverses the work produced by the same small deformation cycle. The sign difference survives averaging over unknown axial registry between motors and binding sites.

That result survives thermodynamic and numerical checks, but it does not establish a new materials operation. The strongest prior work already contains two-coordinate crossbridge engines. What this attack adds is a concrete relation between deformation, pathway access, occupancy and force—and a sharper identification of the attachment mechanics that a realization must supply.

The catalytic-cycle construction, thermodynamic completion, force-transmission results and DNA cassette/release/reset work remain retained.

Why actomyosin was the stronger attainable testbed

The unresolved kinase attachment map would still require supplying transverse kinetics and force response that its one-axis measurements do not determine. Actomyosin offered a better starting point because published spatial crossbridge models explicitly relate axial offset and filament separation to head geometry, thermal binding access and forces in both directions. Independent muscle experiments also establish that different biochemical states have different radial mechanical responses. Those experiments support the physical relevance of the coupling; they do not calibrate the particular model used here.

It also does not justify simply convolving the rigid force field with a Gaussian: finite compliance generally produces a joint nonequilibrium distribution of geometry and chemical state.

The stronger physical alternative—and its limits

The most relevant new boundary platform found is the MAESTRO DNA-origami force-spectroscopy preprint. It supplies real multidirectional molecular loading through ssDNA entropic springs and a rigid ring. Its four-arm Holliday-junction experiment uses separately assembled loading configurations, including nominal 3 pN loading along orthogonal axes. This is substantially better evidence for multiple molecular load paths than a proposed third anchor. It does not demonstrate two independently cycled displacement ports.

Current scientific decision

Actomyosin remains the completed computational benchmark; the kinase remains a preserved candidate. The attained result is a fixed-affinity, geometry-based work prediction with explicit source corrections, competing-target normalization and a verified registry average. Its broad operation is conventional gap-controlled active traction.

The next development priority is now narrowly physical: a specified scaffold must retain two independent transmitted deformations after its swivels, rotations and tether extensions relax. The relevant outputs are the transfer map, conjugate forces and torques, and the geometry distribution experienced by each chemical state.

The retrieved myosin assembly does not yet identify that map. MAESTRO supplies a stronger example of actual multidirectional loading, but its existing measurements do not complete a continuously fueled mechanical junction. These are distinct limitations: neither is a universal impossibility, and neither is resolved merely by access to equipment.

The useful advance at this checkpoint is that the missing input is now explicit. Another turnover fit or stiffness sweep would not establish it. A calibrated attachment map could turn the present prediction into a test of a molecular realization; without that map, claiming a new material would outrun the evidence.
