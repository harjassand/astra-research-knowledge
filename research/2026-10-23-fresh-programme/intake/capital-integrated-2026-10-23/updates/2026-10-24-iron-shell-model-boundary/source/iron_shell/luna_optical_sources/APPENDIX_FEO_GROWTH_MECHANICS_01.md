# FeO compatibility check: growth and mechanics on one specimen?

**Bounded result:** I did not find a primary experiment in this one-source check that co-measures Fe→FeO scale thickness/growth time and stress, creep, cracking, or delamination on the same Fe specimen during 600–900°C steam/H₂ oxidation. The closest direct FeO-mechanics experiment is Hidaka et al. (2000), but it does not provide the coupled growth-stress record needed to set a shallow pulse dose.

## What the closest primary source actually measured

[Hidaka, Nakagawa, Anraku & Ootsuka, “High Temperature Tensile Behavior of FeO Scale,” DOI 10.2320/jinstmet1952.64.5_291](https://doi.org/10.2320/jinstmet1952.64.5_291), *Journal of the Japan Institute of Metals* 64 (2000), 291–294 (open J-STAGE PDF).

- **Material/preparation:** 99.999% pure Fe test bars were completely oxidized in 3% H₂–10% H₂O–balance N₂: ramp to 1250°C over 30 min, then hold for 2 h. XRD found the resulting gauge section to be nearly 100% FeO. The authors describe a fully oxidized, porous FeO test specimen; this is not a thin FeO shell on a remaining Fe core.
- **Mechanical readout:** after oxidation, specimens were brought to the test temperature in 15 min and pulled in the same gas atmosphere at 600–1200°C. They recorded tensile stress–strain behavior, with surface observation by CCD/video. At 600°C the specimen fractured brittly at about 1.45% strain; FeO was ductile above 700°C. Stress-saturated steady deformation was reported above 1000°C, outside most of the proposed 600–900°C window.
- **Missing coupled observable:** the paper does not report a 600–900°C FeO thickness-versus-growth-time series or in-situ stress/crack/delamination while a FeO layer grows on a metallic Fe core. The 2 h treatment is a specimen-preparation step to full oxidation, not a calibrated shallow-layer dose-response.

## Consequence for the proposed operation

This source offers a useful material warning, not a pulse design rule: bulk porous FeO can be brittle at 600°C yet deform plastically above 700°C under tensile loading. Because the sample is fully oxidized rather than a constrained thin shell on Fe, these results do not establish whether a selective Fe/FeO-interface pulse will crack, creep-relax, or delaminate. For the proposed dose-limited FeO window returned to Fe, the key coupled growth/stress measurement remains **unavailable in this bounded check**; do not combine this tensile study with separate oxide-growth kinetics as if they came from one specimen or protocol.
