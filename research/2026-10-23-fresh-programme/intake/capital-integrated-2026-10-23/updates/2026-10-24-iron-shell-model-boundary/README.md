## Pressure pulse and trapped-water boundary

For \(\mathrm{FeO+H_2\rightleftharpoons Fe+H_2O}\), an ideal fixed \(p_{H_2O}/p_{H_2}\) ratio leaves the gas contribution to redox affinity unchanged when both partial pressures scale together. That does not fix kinetics, adsorption, hydrogen uptake, gas transport, residence time or local interface composition. Equal-permeance, fixed-volume bookkeeping gives no total-pressure amplification from the one-for-one \(H_2\)-to-\(H_2O\) conversion; a pressure step supplies at most its imposed differential in that model. Water partial pressure is not itself a shell pressure difference.

The source's thin-shell threshold \(\Delta P\ge 2\sigma_{eff}h/R\) is a necessary idealized condition, not a sufficient fracture prediction. Geometry, pore connectivity, stress concentration, temperature-dependent strength, creep and flaws remain unmeasured. Layered-sphere eigenstrain calculations report possible stress signs in perfectly bonded toy geometries; the moduli and strains are illustrative and do not predict cracking, pore opening or transport gain.

The primary-source audits distinguish observations from inference. Quenched atom-probe maps report deuterium-bearing water-like clusters, not high-temperature pore pressure or pressure-driven fracture. A separate single-crystal study reports pore topology and oxide rims; its 4–5 atm pore-water value is a phase-field output, while the authors attribute crack propagation to transformation stress. Neither result validates a pressure pulse in an ore pellet. Broad pressure cycling is also an incumbent: the packet records a 1973 pulsating-hydrogen pellet experiment and a 1984 pressure-cycling transport model. The narrower fixed-ratio shell-opening mechanism remains untested; no novelty or priority claim is made.

## Separate Fe/FeO excursion hypothesis

The isothermal audit treats a shallow Fe-to-FeO-to-Fe excursion as a distinct candidate, not as evidence for pressure-driven opening. It requires an actual-material phase-selective window, a spatially resolved growth/depth and phase/valence/isotope account, connected-path measurements, and matched final state, time, heat and gas costs. Bulk mass or isotope uptake alone cannot certify a shallow excursion; the supplied counter-ledger shows how concurrent oxidation and reduction can cancel in bulk records. The packet also notes that redox cycling and thermal-history control are established incumbents. The coupled FeO-growth/stress measurement needed to set a shallow dose is unavailable in this bounded review.

The exact-arithmetic and symbolic scripts, and the supplied elastic-screen result JSON, are retained as source-reported model artifacts. Their toy inputs do not represent measured Fe/FeO properties. No shell mechanism, material performance, physical advantage or new theory is established.
