# Astra XH2 — Enzymatic welding of newly grown cellulose

## 1. A stronger molecular mechanism has emerged

The previous construction depended on an unknown quantity: the effective shear strength between newly synthesized cellulose fibres and an existing wet cellulose network.

That was a serious limitation. Strong cellulose fibres do not automatically bond strongly to older cellulose, especially in water.

A more specific biochemical operation is now available.

Equisetum hetero-trans-β-glucanase (HTG) is a plant enzyme capable of cleaving cellulose chains and covalently transferring cellulose fragments to xyloglucan acceptors. This has been demonstrated both in biochemical assays and in native plant cell walls. The activity can also be enhanced by a bacterial expansin under particular conditions.&#x20;

More importantly, Franková and Fry published a study in January 2026 demonstrating the synthesis of multivalent xyloglucan-derived acceptors with up to four XXXG-derived groups. The compounds were accepted by HTG, and the authors identified possible cellulose-chain crosslinking as an application. They did not establish mechanically effective crosslinking across an old–new cellulose interface.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov\&sz=32)

PubMed



This provides a chemically specific route worth developing:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Living cellulose synthesis} \longrightarrow \text{Old–new fibre contact} \longrightarrow \text{Enzymatic covalent welding}. } \\]

The mechanism has an important advantage over the earlier SpyTag–protein-scaffold proposal: it could form covalent attachments directly involving cellulose, rather than depending entirely on an additional structural-protein network.

It also changes the crucial unknown from an unspecified adhesion coefficient to identifiable quantities: accessible cellulose donor sites, productive crosslink formation, retention of load-bearing cellulose after enzymatic cleavage, and mechanical performance in water.

### An essential geometric limit

These xyloglucan-derived molecules are short molecular linkers. They cannot bridge a 100 μm crack by themselves.

They can only help weld cellulose chains that have already approached molecular contact. Living cellulose growth must still create the larger structure crossing the defect.

This establishes a separation between two operations:

- Growth builds across distance.
- Enzymatic welding connects the new structure to the old one.

The second operation is promising because it addresses the major physical interface missing from the earlier model. Neither operation can substitute for the other.

## 2. Exact result: most molecular attachments need not be useful welds

The key distinction is between a molecule that attaches to cellulose and one that actually joins old cellulose to new cellulose.

Consider a linker with \\(v\\) reactive arms. Let \\(q\\) be the probability an arm successfully grafts, and \\(\eta\\) the probability that the transferred cellulose fragment remains mechanically connected to its original fibre network.

Write \\(u=q\eta\\). If a successful arm is equally likely to attach to the old or new network, the probability that a linker makes at least one mechanically retained attachment to each is

\\[ \boxed{ P_v=1-2(1-u/2)^v+(1-u)^v. } \\]

This follows by excluding linkers with no old-side attachment or no new-side attachment.

For illustrative values \\(q=0.7\\) and \\(\eta=0.8\\):

| Reactive arms | Probability of a two-sided weld | Useful weld probability per arm |
| ------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| 1             | 0%                              | 0                               |
| 2             | 15.68%                          | 7.84%                           |
| 3             | 33.87%                          | 11.29%                          |
| 4             | 50.00%                          | 12.50%                          |
| 5             | 62.95%                          | 12.59%                          |
| 8             | 85.70%                          | 10.71%                          |

An important result is that increasing molecular valence has diminishing returns. Under this model, four- or five-arm linkers make more efficient use of reactive arms than either two-arm or eight-arm linkers.

This is not evidence that four-arm XXXG compounds actually form 50% useful mechanical welds. The 2026 work demonstrated that multivalent compounds are chemically accepted by HTG; independent multi-arm reaction, two-sided loading and donor-fragment retention remain assumptions.

### An attempted improvement fails

I also tested a seemingly stronger strategy: expose the linker to old cellulose first, then introduce the new cellulose.

At a fixed total grafting probability per arm, the probability of completing an old–new connection is maximized when reaction opportunities are balanced equally between the two sides.

Simply dividing an unchanged reaction budget into two sequential stages offers no advantage in the independent-arm model.

Staging helps only if it actually increases productive reaction opportunities, suppresses same-side loops, or protects chemically available arms for subsequent contacts.

This rules out using timing alone as an explanation for improved repair.

## 3. New result: average chemical success can hide a severe repair-reliability failure

The next calculation revealed a more fundamental problem than insufficient enzyme concentration.

Suppose a small region of a crack remains accessible for a random time \\(T\\). During that time, HTG must complete at least two successful grafting reactions to turn a multivalent linker into a useful old–new connection.

If the entire local region loses access at once—for example, because transport shuts down as the crack seals—all its candidate linkers experience the same short opportunity.

Let the catalytic hazard of an individual arm be \\(k\\), the rate of common access loss be \\(d\\), and the number of candidate linkers in the region be Poisson with mean \\(N\\).

For a four-arm linker, the conditional probability of a useful connection depends on

\\[ q(T)=1-e^{-kT} \\]

through the two-sided reaction formula above.

The probability that the whole region contains no completed old–new connections is then

\\[ \boxed{ P\_{\rm failure} = \mathbb E_T\left[e^{-NP_4(q(T))}\right]. } \\]

By comparison, a model that ignores shared shutdown and uses only the average molecular success probability gives

\\[ P\_{\rm naive}=e^{-N\mathbb E[P_4]}. \\]

Jensen's inequality establishes

\\[ \boxed{P\_{\rm failure}\geq P\_{\rm naive}.} \\]

Thus averaging molecular reaction success can systematically overestimate structural reliability.

The discrepancy is important. A biochemical assay could report an apparently high number of successful molecular reactions while the material still contains numerous local regions with no structural connection.

### A stronger mathematical consequence

At very short exposure times, completing two reactions is a second-order event:

\\[ P_4(T)\sim3(\eta kT)^2. \\]

With exponentially distributed common access times, this gives

\\[ \boxed{ P\_{\rm failure} \sim \frac{d\sqrt{\pi}} {2\eta k\sqrt{3N}}. } \\]

Failure decreases only as \\(N^{-1/2}\\) when candidate-linker abundance increases.

That is dramatically slower than the exponential improvement predicted by the independent-exposure model.

To reach 90% probability that all 20 regions form at least one weld, the exact model requires approximately:

| Catalysis/access-loss ratio \\(k/d\\) | Independent-exposure estimate | Shared-shutdown requirement |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| 0.3                                   | 48 candidates per region      | 164,134                     |
| 1                                     | 17                            | 14,898                      |
| 3                                     | 10                            | 1,696                       |
| 10                                    | 8                             | 167                         |

Rounded up to whole candidate counts.

This result changes the proposed invention.

Overproducing crosslinker molecules is a poor substitute for keeping the repair interface accessible long enough to complete the chemistry.

A useful material architecture should decouple continued enzymatic welding from the immediate transport pathway that fracture closure progressively removes.

This also explains why externally maintained reagent access is such a strong engineering comparator. A conventional controlled repair system can preserve an adequate chemical-processing window even when the defect geometry changes.

## 4. Can molecular welding actually restore the required mechanical strength?

I connected the molecular model to the earlier fibre-stitching equations.

Each new cellulose fibre must span a 100 μm defect and transfer force into the old material at both ends. Successful enzymatic welds are modeled as discrete load-bearing connections along the fibre overlaps.

For a given overlap length, the number of useful connections is Poisson-distributed. I calculated the expected load capacity using the weaker of the two anchored ends, with an upper limit set by fibre rupture.

This replaces the earlier assumption of a perfectly continuous adhesive interface.

### Mechanical feasibility under restricted fibre growth

Consider an illustrative repair with a 1 mm² damaged area, 100 kPa required tensile traction, a 100 μm gap, and at most 200 μm of effective new fibre length.

The model assumes 10 kPa of pre-existing physical interfacial shear, 200 candidate linkers per micrometre of overlapping fibre, four reactive arms per linker, and 80% donor-fragment retention.

With no enzymatic welding, the minimum dry cellulose requirement is 1.50 μg/mm².

Suppose only 1 μg/mm² of replacement cellulose is available.

| Arm grafting probability | Sustained force per complete weld | Minimum fraction of fibre overlap in molecular contact |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| 70%                      | 1 nN                              | 1.67%                                                  |
| 70%                      | 0.1 nN                            | 16.03%                                                 |
| 10.3%                    | 1 nN                              | 44.94%                                                 |
| 10.3%                    | 0.1 nN                            | No feasible contact fraction                           |

Every numerical mechanical parameter in this table is illustrative, not a measured property of HTG-treated bacterial cellulose.

The 10.3% case is particularly important. A primary 2021 study found that HTG's cellulose-transfer activity on its tested cellulose-I preparations was approximately 9% of that on cellulose II.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov\&sz=32)

PubMed Central (PMC)



In a hypothetical fixed-exposure reaction model, reducing catalytic hazard to 9% of a reference hazard that produced 70% grafting success gives

\\[ q_I=1-(1-0.7)^{0.09}\approx0.103. \\]

This is a sensitivity calculation, not a conversion of the published enzyme assay into a measured bacterial-cellulose reaction probability.

Under this scenario, the probability of a four-arm linker completing a productive two-sided weld falls from 50.0% to approximately 1.86%.

The requirement for two productive attachments amplifies poor cellulose accessibility.

### A second biochemical failure: cutting cellulose may weaken it

HTG's reaction cuts a cellulose donor chain and transfers a portion to an acceptor.

If that transferred fragment is not mechanically retained in its original network, chemical grafting may fail to create a useful structural connection. Some reactions could also disrupt previously load-bearing cellulose.

I tested a conservative event-balance model that assigns one unit of benefit to a successful two-sided weld and one unit of possible damage to each graft whose donor fragment loses structural retention.

For the four-arm candidate with 70% grafting probability, the balance becomes positive only when

\\[ \boxed{\eta>0.81665.} \\]

The threshold rises to approximately 93.85% under the low-activity scenario.

These thresholds belong to the deliberately conservative equal-benefit/equal-damage model. Real fracture resistance will not be determined by counting molecular reactions with identical weights.

Nevertheless, the result identifies a decisive measurement: how much of the grafted cellulose remains mechanically integrated with the original load-bearing network?

Demonstrating covalent cellulose–xyloglucan formation alone does not answer that question.

## 5. A concrete repair: let extracellular chemistry continue after biological growth stops

The shared-shutdown result suggests a way to revise the architecture.

Instead of requiring cells to produce and attach every structural connection while nutrients enter the crack, the system could retain HTG and its acceptor molecules near the newly formed fibre overlaps.

Cellular activity would manufacture the new cellulose. The retained extracellular chemistry would then continue making covalent connections after nutrient delivery to the cells declined.

This is plausible in principle because HTG catalysis is an extracellular biochemical reaction and has been demonstrated on cellulosic substrates independently of living cellulose-producing cells.&#x20;

### Exact test of this mechanism

I extended the reaction model by adding a guaranteed period \\(T_0\\) of catalytic activity after the random shutdown of the original supply pathway.

The molecular success probability becomes

\\[ q(T)=1-e^{-k(T+T_0)}. \\]

For the same 20-region, 90%-reliability target, with \\(k=d\\):

| Guaranteed additional catalytic time, relative to mean shutdown time | Required candidate linkers per region |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 0                                                                    | 14,898                                |
| 0.05                                                                 | 272                                   |
| 0.10                                                                 | 104                                   |
| 0.20                                                                 | 41                                    |
| 0.30                                                                 | 25                                    |
| 0.50                                                                 | 15                                    |
| 1.00                                                                 | 10                                    |

This is a substantial result.

A guaranteed post-shutdown reaction period only 20% as long as the mean initial access period reduces the required number of candidate linkers by more than 360-fold in this model.

The deeper mathematical consequence is that a guaranteed nonzero reaction period restores exponential suppression of zero-weld probability with increasing linker abundance, rather than the slow \\(N^{-1/2}\\) decay caused by shared early shutdown.

This identifies a potentially useful form of extracellular construction memory: biological cells supply the reactants and catalyst, but the material retains enough chemical activity to finish structural assembly after the original biological stimulus disappears.

The physical implementation remains unproved. HTG, acceptor molecules, water and suitable cellulose contact must all survive at the interface. The calculation does not establish enzyme retention, long-term activity or mechanically functional grafting.

## 6. The remaining hard boundary: contact must exist before welding

I challenged the revised mechanism again.

If no newly synthesized cellulose reaches the old structure before the growth-supporting environment disappears, retaining an enzyme cannot create a useful old–new connection.

Let \\(g\\) be the rate of first establishing the necessary fibre contact, and \\(d\\) the rate at which the opportunity for further growth is lost. Suppose the competing times are independent exponentials.

Then

\\[ \boxed{ P(\text{contact before shutdown})=\frac{g}{g+d}. } \\]

Even perfect subsequent welding cannot exceed this probability.

For 20 independently damaged regions, the maximum overall reliability is

\\[ \boxed{ P\_{\rm all}\leq \left(\frac{g}{g+d}\right)^{20}. } \\]

To exceed 90% across all 20 regions, this particular model requires

\\[ \boxed{\frac gd>189.3.} \\]

This is a strong negative result. It is not overcome by increasing enzyme concentration, molecular valence or linker availability.

It is a model-specific reliability requirement, not a measured ratio for bacterial cellulose growth.

The architecture needs two kinds of persistence: enough biologically supported time to establish physical overlap, followed by enough extracellular chemical activity to weld it.

This rules out assuming that a single fracture-generated nutrient pulse will reliably accomplish both operations.

A stronger physical architecture would support cellulose growth through an independent porous feed network, while the damaged zone supplies exposed cellulose interfaces that determine where construction becomes mechanically integrated.

Such a design would be more constrained and engineered than an entirely autonomous material repairing arbitrary fractures. But it would address the actual failure instead of assigning cells an unspecified ability to find and repair damage.

## 7. Challenging HTG against another existing enzyme

The strongest biochemical comparator I found is AtXTH3, an enzyme from Arabidopsis thaliana.

Shinohara and colleagues demonstrated that recombinant AtXTH3 can transfer cellulose-derived chains onto cello-oligosaccharides. It also produced some insoluble cellulose-like material from soluble cello-oligosaccharides. Unlike the multivalent HTG construction, this route need not rely on chemically synthesized multivalent xyloglucan acceptors for the demonstrated reactions.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov\&sz=32)

PubMed Central (PMC)



However, the paper reported substantially reduced activity on crystalline cellulose compared with amorphous cellulose. Its strongest activity among the tested reactions was also on xyloglucan-based substrates, rather than cellulose-to-cellulose transfer.

The comparison therefore looks like this:

| Mechanism                                | Molecular advantage                                                                         | Main obstacle                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| HTG plus multivalent XXXG-derived linker | Documented cellulose grafting and demonstrated multivalent acceptor substrates              | Two-sided structural crosslinks are unproved; synthetic linker supply and cellulose-I accessibility |
| AtXTH3 plus cello-oligosaccharides       | Demonstrated cellulose-to-cello-oligosaccharide transfer without requiring multivalent XXXG | Structural bridging is unproved; strong preference for accessible amorphous cellulose               |
| Physical cellulose interpenetration      | Avoids the added crosslinking enzyme and acceptor chemistry                                 | Wet interfacial slip, contact topology and long-term mechanical retention                           |

I would retain HTG plus multivalent acceptors as the sharper covalent-welding hypothesis, while keeping AtXTH3 as a serious molecular comparator.

Neither has yet demonstrated functional wet welding between two previously separate bacterial-cellulose structures.

### A biologically compatible production architecture

There is one encouraging connection between the primary sources.

The 2026 Liu and colleagues platform already combines cellulose-producing Komagataeibacter with protein-secreting Pichia in a co-localized construction system. Meanwhile, earlier HTG research established recombinant enzyme production in Pichia.&#x20;

## 8. What has actually been established

The strongest new consequence is not a prediction that enzymatic welding will work. It is a more precise understanding of what must happen for it to work reliably.

The construction now separates four operations:

Biological growth

Generate new cellulose fibres capable of reaching the damaged host network.

Physical contact

Establish sufficiently extensive molecular-scale overlap between old and new fibres.

Covalent maturation

Produce mechanically retained old–new connections, potentially continuing after active biosynthesis declines.

Functional reconstruction

Restore wet load transfer and fracture resistance independently of whether the producing cells remain alive.

The first operation has strong biological precedents. A molecular reaction relevant to the third is experimentally established. Their integration through the second and fourth remains unproved.

A fair performance comparison must also account for existing approaches. A September 2026 study demonstrated 35% flexural-strength recovery through in-situ bacterial nanocellulose repair of concrete and up to 78% using premanufactured nanocellulose repair tape.&#x20;

A separate 2026 molecular-adhesion approach for cement reported up to 59% direct-tension strength recovery and rapid crack sealing without requiring a living cellulose-producing system.&#x20;

These are not identical substrate or loading conditions, but they establish that Astra cannot claim superiority merely by making a crack-spanning cellulose network.

The candidate needs to demonstrate additional functional value through localized biosynthetic replacement, controllable interface formation or renewed construction capability.

### The decisive unresolved experiment

The crucial distinction is between three outcomes:

Chemical grafting: enzyme-generated cellulose-derived material remains bound after appropriate washing and biochemical verification.

Mechanical welding: the new cellulose transfers load into the old network and improves wet interfacial strength or fracture energy over the same construction without active enzyme.

Regenerative repair: cells can produce new replacement material and the structure can repeat the operation after another injury.

A positive chemical assay establishes only the first. A positive mechanical assay establishes the second. Neither alone establishes autonomous biological regeneration.

The strongest discriminating comparison is therefore between active HTG with multivalent acceptors, inactive-enzyme and monovalent-acceptor controls, AtXTH3-mediated grafting, and an equally well supplied nonliving cellulose repair process.

The measured quantity that most directly tests the new hypothesis is the increase in cell-independent wet interfacial load transfer per unit of newly synthesized cellulose, alongside evidence that existing fibres were not weakened by the enzyme.

## Scientific conclusion

This continuation moves Astra from an unspecified biological adhesion mechanism to a specific candidate for covalent reconstruction of cellulose.

Three new mathematical results survive the changes in mechanism:

- Multivalent cellulose-grafting chemistry can make useful old–new connections, but attachment counts alone can dramatically overestimate the number of actual structural bridges.
- Shared loss of the reaction window creates a severe reliability penalty, with failure decreasing only as the inverse square root of linker abundance in the two-hit model.
- A retained extracellular catalytic period can overcome that penalty, but cannot replace the prior biological operation of creating physical old–new contact.

The immediate invention target is now:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Living fibre growth} \longrightarrow \text{Contact-dependent enzymatic welding} \longrightarrow \text{Cell-independent structural continuity}. } \\]

The science remains analytical and computational at the integrated-system level. The physical welding reaction and reliable regenerative lifecycle have not been demonstrated.

The source-grounded equations, exact Poisson calculations, independently checked molecular probabilities, closure-time analysis and competing enzyme-mechanism tests are preserved in the executable file:

Astra XH2 — Enzymatic cellulose-welding research model

The leading blocker is sharply defined: can multivalent enzymatic grafting create net-positive, mechanically retained connections between native wet cellulose fibres before the interface loses chemical access, without damaging the load-bearing cellulose already present?

Resolving that would establish an enabling operation for biologically manufactured matter to become a functional part of an existing structure—not simply more biological material deposited beside it.
