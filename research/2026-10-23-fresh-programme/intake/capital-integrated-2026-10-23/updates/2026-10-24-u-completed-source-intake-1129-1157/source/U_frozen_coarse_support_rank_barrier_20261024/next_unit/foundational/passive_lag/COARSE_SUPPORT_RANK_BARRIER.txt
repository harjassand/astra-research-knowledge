A coarse-support rank barrier for a public random relation
=========================================================

Parent full independent proof-level review and four bounded support
checks passed at the literal scope below. This is internal mathematical
review, not external validation or a priority determination. The draft
is preserved separately at SHA-256

This investigates an actual graph-aware quantum interface. The graph may
determine the shared state and every measurement. We do not erase the old
query, fix the apparatus before the graph, or flatten the Schmidt state.
The new interface is generic scalarization of the ACTUAL coarse POVM
support, followed by a sparse amplitude skeleton.

The counting method has strong established ancestry: Ronyai--Babai--
Ganapathy's zero-pattern argument and the sparse-basis/Cramer method in
Alon--Balla--Gishboliner--Mond--Mousset, arXiv:1809.01873, Definition 2.1
and Lemmas 2.3--2.4. The linear representative lemma below is the standard
Rado argument, with a proof included. Any scientific novelty would be in
the literal quantum interface/consequence; priority is UNKNOWN.

The result excludes perfect strategies with bounded rank per actual
coarse effect, including very large finite Schmidt rank and nonuniform
Schmidt coefficients. It does NOT prove a uniform constant gap, a robust
payoff estimate, or any unrestricted tensor/commuting repetition theorem.

1. Literal game, realization class, and finite bound
----------------------------------------------------
Let k>=2, M>=1 and 1<=h<=k be integers. Gamma is a bipartite relation on
[k]x[M] by a DISTINCT copy of [k]x[M]. Its k^2 M^2 ordered cross-edges
are independent Bernoulli(q), 0<q<1. The referee samples fresh independent
uniform questions x,y in [k]. Alice answers i in [M], Bob j in [M];
winning means Gamma_((x,i),(y,j))=1.

A finite tensor strategy is in the rank-h realization class if every
coarse POVM effect, compressed to its local marginal support, has rank
at most h. All state, measurement and classical choices may depend on
Gamma. This is a condition on the ACTUAL coarse effects, not on a chosen
rank-one refinement or its advertised outcome count. Local ancillas and
channels can be included by pulling their complete readout back to the
supplied local space before checking this rank condition.

Write

 B=e^2(1+2M)(1+k(M+1)),
 a=q B^(2h/k),
 b=(hM)^(2h) a^(k^2).                                    (R1)

If b<1, then a<1, and

 Pr_Gamma[there exists a PERFECT rank-h finite strategy]
 <= binom(k,h)^2 h b / ((1-a)(1-b)).                       (R2)

An upper bound above one is merely uninformative. The existential event
includes every Gamma-adaptive quantum strategy in this class, rather
than a preselected net/profile. The edge map is the fixed game alphabet.

2. One representative per COARSE effect preserves support
----------------------------------------------------------
A perfect mixed strategy has a perfect pure component, since all losing
probabilities are nonnegative. Compress it to its local Schmidt supports:

 |psi>=sum_(j=1)^d lambda_j |j,j>,
 S=diag(lambda_j)>0,  Tr S^2=1.                            (R3)

Let A_i^x,B_j^y be its complete POVMs on these d-dimensional spaces.
Their ranks are <=h; completeness implies d<=hM. For every coarse effect
choose one vector

 u_i^x in range(A_i^x),    v_j^y in conjugate(range(B_j^y)).

Use generic complex coefficients in bases of these subspaces. Set

 D_xy[i,j]=(u_i^x)^* S v_j^y.                              (R4)

This is a scalarized amplitude, not a probability or a new measurement.
The original probability is

 p(i,j|x,y)=||sqrt(A_i^x) S sqrt((B_j^y)^T)||_HS^2.

It is zero iff the sesquilinear pairing in (R4) vanishes identically on
the two displayed ranges. Write all representative coefficients in real
and imaginary coordinates. For every positive cell, |D_xy[i,j]|^2 is a
nonzero REAL polynomial. Outside their finitely many proper zero sets,
D_xy[i,j]!=0 iff p(i,j|x,y)>0 SIMULTANEOUSLY for all inputs and coarse
outputs. Zero effects simply yield zero vectors/rows.

No h^2 graph-bit penalty occurs: the scalarized matrix has exactly the
ORIGINAL COARSE support. It does not claim to preserve probabilities,
normalization, states, or any fine quantum instrument.

3. The load-bearing spanning statement
---------------------------------------
For ANY h distinct Alice input settings, generic representatives from
all their M coarse effect ranges span the whole Schmidt space C^d.
The same holds for any h Bob settings, including the conjugation in R4.
This is what permits a sparse skeleton despite coarse effect ranks >1.

Here is a self-contained representative argument. For a finite family
of subspaces W_1,...,W_m in a d-dimensional space, the following condition
suffices for choosing one vector per W_j whose union spans the space:

 for every subspace W, # {j: W_j not subseteq W} >= d-dim W. (R5)

To see this, let J be any subset of indices and put W=sum_(j in J)W_j.
Then (R5) implies dim W >= d-(m-|J|). Append the SAME auxiliary space
E of dimension m-d to every W_j. The enlarged family satisfies

       dim sum_(j in J)(W_j direct-sum E) >= |J|

for every nonempty J. The standard independent-representative proof is
short: induct on m. If some proper nonempty J has equality, solve that
subfamily and the complementary quotient by induction. Otherwise every
proper nonempty J has dimension at least |J|+1; choose a nonzero vector
in the last subspace and apply induction to the other subspaces in its
one-dimensional quotient. This supplies independent representatives in
the enlarged m-dimensional space. Their projections span C^d, proving
(R5). Equivalently the appropriate determinant polynomial is nonzero,
so generic representatives also span.

For our h POVMs, fix W of codimension c. In EACH input setting, the
effect ranges span C^d. Their images in C^d/W span dimension c; each
image has dimension at most h. Thus at least ceil(c/h) effect ranges
are not contained in W. Across h settings there are at least c such
slots, so (R5) holds. This includes d<h, zero/rank-deficient effects,
and identical measurement choices at different input labels. Settings
are distinct labels; their instruments need not differ.

For each h-setting subset choose one spanning minor whose determinant
is not identically zero. Its absolute square is a nonzero real polynomial.
These finitely many minor polynomials and the positive-cell polynomials
in Section 2 have a nonzero product in the real polynomial ring. Thus ONE
scalarization has the coarse support and every h-setting span property.
This is existential algebra, not a physically performed random filter.

4. Sparse input blocks and one anchor minor
--------------------------------------------
Let u be the total number of positive coarse cells in the actual table.
It is now the total scalar support size of D. Faithful local marginals
make every nonzero effect have positive marginal probability. Each input
has at least ceil(d/h) nonzero effects. For each such Alice outcome and
EVERY Bob input at least one joint probability is positive. Hence

                u >= k^2 ceil(d/h).                     (R6)

Choose the h input rows with smallest support and the h input columns
with smallest support. Their total supports are each at most h u/k.
By Section 3 the associated stacked representative frames have rank d.
The anchor hM-by-hM submatrix therefore has rank d, so choose a nonsingular
d-by-d minor C0=D[I0,J0] within it. In the GLOBAL kM-by-kM matrix put

 R=D[:,J0],   T=D[I0,:].

The common Schmidt factorization gives rank(D)=d, and exactly

                   D=R C0^(-1) T.                       (R7)

For example, factor D=U S V^* with both stacked frames full column rank;
if U0,V0 are the square row restrictions at I0,J0, then C0=U0 S V0^*.
Its inverse cancels all three invertible factors in (R7). No maximal
entanglement, square local basis, or entrywise complex-reality is used.

The skeleton R,T has at most 2kMd scalar locations and at most 2h u/k
NONZERO entries, because its rows/columns lie in the selected h input
blocks. Anchor sets can be chosen in at most binom(k,h)^2 ways; the
minor rows and columns in at most binom(hM,d)^2 ways.

Fix these choices and the nonzero skeleton locations. After multiplying
by det C0, every entry of (R7) is a polynomial of degree <=d+1 in the
free nonzero skeleton entries. On det C0!=0 its zero pattern is exactly
that of D. We overcount arbitrary complex values; no positivity,
normalization or quantum conditions are imposed in this algebraic count.

5. Fixed-weight polynomial support count
-----------------------------------------
For polynomials p_1,...,p_m of degree<=D in n complex variables, let F
range over realized nonzero support sets with EXACT cardinality u. The
products P_F=product_(j in F)p_j have degree<=Du. At a point realizing
another support F' of cardinality u, P_F is nonzero iff F=F'. Evaluation
therefore proves linear independence of these products. Consequently

                #support sets <= binom(n+Du,n).          (R8)

This is the fixed-weight version of the established polynomial-space
zero-pattern argument, proved here in the precise form needed.

Put t=floor(2h u/k). By R6, t>=h u/k>=1. Skeleton supports of size<=t
among 2kMd locations can be counted by binom(2kMd+t,t): inject a subset
into a multiset of size t by adding dummy zero symbols. Pad the free
variables to t. The inequalities

 (2kMd)/t <=2M,
 ((d+1)u)/t <=k(d+1)/h <=k(M+1)

and binom(n+t,t)<=[e(1+n/t)]^t give, using R8,

 N_(d,u) <= binom(k,h)^2 binom(hM,d)^2 B^(2h u/k).         (R9)

This counts all possible COARSE support profiles in the rank-h class.
The skeleton, generic representatives, Schmidt matrix, anchor and minor
may all depend on the realized public graph; every resulting support is
in the fixed family counted by R9.

6. Actual Gamma edges and the union bound
------------------------------------------
A coarse support profile with u cells consists of u DISTINCT ordered
Gamma bits, by the fixed map (x,i,y,j)->((x,i),(y,j)). For a perfect
strategy all those bits must be 1, of probability q^u. This is not a
claim that the strategies or skeletons are independent of Gamma.
Counting them before the random-graph containment test is precisely
what handles their full graph adaptivity.

Use R6 and R9, and sum u geometrically:

 P_bad <= binom(k,h)^2/(1-a)
          sum_(d=1)^(hM) binom(hM,d)^2 a^(k^2 ceil(d/h)).

Group d into intervals (j-1)h<d<=jh. There are at most h integers in
each interval, and binom(hM,d)^2<=(hM)^(2hj). The remaining geometric
series is h sum_(j>=1)b^j, proving R2.

7. The original shared-label BG game, with ranks retained
---------------------------------------------------------
Let N=k^r and M=N/k. The original game asks uniform x,y in [k], answers
(s,l),(t,j), and wins iff l=j, s_l=x, t_l=y and Gamma_st=1.
Here Gamma is bipartite independent Bernoulli(q) on [k]^r by [k]^r.
Suppose a PERFECT finite strategy has rank<=h for every ACTUAL coarse
answer effect, on the local marginal supports.

It has a perfect pure component; compress to faithful Schmidt supports.
Let E_l^x,F_l^y be its coarse label effects. Perfect matching labels
imply, for every x,y,l,

 <E_l^x>=<F_l^y>=<E_l^x F_l^y>.

Cross-party commutation and E^2<=E,F^2<=F give
||(E_l^x-F_l^y)psi||^2=0 and <E_l^x-(E_l^x)^2>=0 (similarly F).
Faithfulness makes each E_l^x and F_l^y a projection. Comparing two x
values against a common y gives E_l^x=E_l independent of x, and likewise
F_l independent of y. Also E_l psi=F_l psi.

Choose a positive branch psi_l=E_l psi/||E_l psi||. It is supported in
E_l tensor F_l. Each original fine answer effect of label l is supported
on its label projection and still has rank<=h. Restrict to the branch's
Schmidt supports: these effects are complete there. Invalid-coordinate
effects have zero branch probability and hence vanish on its faithful
local support. The branch is therefore a PERFECT, coordinate-valid,
fixed-l rank-h strategy. This is an existential pre-input resource
choice, not an LOCC claim about preparing the branch from a supplied
state. No Naimark dilation/rank inflation is used.

For each fixed l its fiber game is exactly Section 1 with M=k^(r-1):
the coarse cells map bijectively to the independent Gamma bits. Thus

 Pr[original BG has perfect rank-h finite strategy]
 <= r binom(k,h)^2 h b / ((1-a)(1-b)).                    (R10)

The different l partitions use the same graph; only a union bound is
used, not independence of the partitions.

8. What this establishes, and the remaining approximation gate
--------------------------------------------------------------
At r=floor(k^(1/3)), q=k^(-1/5), M=k^(r-1), fix eta>0 and take
integer h<=k^(2/3-eta). Then

 log B=O(r log k),
 log a=-(1/5-o(1))log k,
 log b=-(1/5-o(1))k^2 log k.

The prefactor in R10 has logarithm o(k^2 log k), so the bad probability
tends to zero. The theorem allows finite Schmidt rank up to hM in the
fixed-l branch, exponential in r, and an arbitrary Schmidt spectrum.
It therefore addresses an operator-compatibility restriction distinct
from the earlier small-Schmidt-dimension net bound.

For a fixed graph/rank cap, local marginal dimensions are bounded by the
number of coarse effects times h. The finite-dimensional POVM/state
parameter sets are compact (include singular states and compress their
actual supports when applying the proof). Hence if no perfect strategy
exists in this class, its supremal value is strictly below one. R2/R10
supply NO useful uniform numerical lower bound for that gap. Arbitrary
high-rank effects, unbounded commuting representations, and near-perfect
loss remain outside the result.

In particular the original classical r-round block source has all round
win indicators equal to the same Gamma-edge indicator, and on any
nonempty tested set has alpha=0 and log(1/p)/m->0 when m is a constant
fraction of r. A complete fresh-base extraction that preserved EXACT
zero live loss and the rank-h realization class would give a perfect
rank-h base strategy, excluded with high probability by R10. This is an
exact payoff obstruction despite arbitrary graph-aware state/measurement
choice and large Schmidt rank. It is NOT an obstruction to an O(sqrt(tau))
approximate extraction: the compactness gap above has no useful uniform
lower bound. Nor does it show that every useful selected coordinate lacks
an unrestricted repair, or promote a fixed quantum-output/full-instrument
comparison to an unrestricted payoff impossibility.
The present obstruction is exact zero support at the actual game
interface. The substantive next requirement is a WEIGHTED/ROBUST sparse
skeleton estimate that does not assume an inaccessible flat reference,
small Schmidt dimension, or an input-independent Gamma-blind apparatus.
