# Primary-source boundary: continuum compactness versus linear dephasing

Bounded readback of the already selected primary sources, 24 October 2026. This is a source-scope comparison, not a novelty or correctness determination.

## Finding

Neither corrected Fiorillo–Raffelt v3 nor Fiorillo–Raffelt v2 explicitly states the present boundary-saturation/strong-compactness consequence for the full continuous exact-reference Lax fiber. They establish the invariant ancestry and two adjacent, but different, kinds of dynamics: finite-beam integrability/coherent reductions, and linearized-continuum phase mixing. The latter does not contradict the present joint-limit statement, but it should not be described as the same result.

## What the sources cover

1. [Fiorillo & Raffelt, “Slow and fast collective neutrino oscillations: Invariants and reciprocity,” corrected arXiv v3 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.09650v3), corresponding to PRD 107, 043024 (2023), is the direct invariant antecedent. It gives the homogeneous fast equation (3), the fast Lax vector and its precession (19)–(20), and the conserved complex Lax square away from original discrete velocities (Sec. III.1). Sec. III.3 (30)–(32) explains that, for the discrete-beam system, these spectral invariants are algebraically generated by beam lengths and Gaudin invariants. Sec. III.5 describes finite-beam motions on an invariant torus and “degenerate”/“N-mode coherent” solutions as combinations of a smaller set of carrier functions. These sections do not derive the continuum real-boundary Cauchy saturation — that every point of the relaxed exact-reference fiber has |D(v)|=|G(v)| a.e. — or its strong-\( L^2 \) compactness consequence.

   Appendix B is an adjacent result, with an importantly different scope. It linearizes the continuum problem about a diagonal background and maps the linearized equation to the Vlasov problem (78)–(81). It separates discrete collective modes from the Case–Van Kampen continuum (82)–(84), then states that the integrated transverse lepton number can damp by phase mixing even while individual polarization perturbations have nondecaying terms (84)–(86). That is a statement about a linearized observable. It is not a theorem about weak-* cluster points of exact nonlinear trajectories preserving the full continuous Lax fiber.

2. [Fiorillo & Raffelt, “Flavor solitons in dense neutrino gases,” arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.12143v2), PRD 107, 123024 (2023), supplies exact coherent examples. Appendix C.3 gives the full-continuous-spectrum one-pulse trajectory (82)–(84). Appendix D constructs a three-beam surrogate by matching an unstable frequency and selected initial moments (up to order three), explicitly treating the three beams as a reduced representative of the continuous spectrum. Neither construction classifies all members or weak-* limits of the continuous exact-reference invariant fiber, nor proves boundary saturation or strong compactness for that fiber.

## Relation to the gate's deduction

The present gate's added step is: pass the exact Cauchy-square identity to real boundary values; use the pointwise ball bound to force equality of the boundary imaginary-part norms; infer |D|=|G| a.e. for every field in the relaxed fiber; then use equality of the (L^2) norms with weak (L^2) convergence to obtain strong (L^2) convergence (and, under the uniform bound on this finite interval, strong convergence in every finite (L^p)). This supplies compactness against an (O(1)) unresolved angular-variance defect when both seed size tends to zero and observation times may be arbitrary.

That conclusion does not imply decay of a fixed nonzero-seed orbit, a time-average result, or a result for a phase-averaged ensemble or spatially varying system. It also does not control the seed-rescaled linear perturbation: an (O(\epsilon)) transverse perturbation can have (O(\epsilon^2)) variance while disappearing in the unscaled joint limit, and the strong-convergence statement gives no uniform control after dividing by \(\epsilon\). Thus Appendix B's linear phase-mixing/decoherence result is a distinct phenomenon and is not refuted by this unscaled compactness claim. Conversely, that generic linear dephasing does not itself establish the nonlinear all-time fiber compactness step.

The source comparison therefore supports only this boundary: Lax/Gaudin invariants, finite-beam coherent families, exact soliton examples, and linearized continuum phase mixing are established prior art; the specific nonlinear continuum saturation-to-compactness implication is not explicitly supplied by these checked passages. This bounded readback makes no novelty claim.
