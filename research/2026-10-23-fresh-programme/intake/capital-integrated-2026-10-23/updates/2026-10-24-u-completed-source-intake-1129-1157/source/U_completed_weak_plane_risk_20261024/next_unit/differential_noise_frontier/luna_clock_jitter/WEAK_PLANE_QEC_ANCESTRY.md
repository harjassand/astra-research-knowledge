# Bounded ancestry check: weak-plane operation vs QEC metrology

24 October 2026. Primary-source priority boundary only. This is not an independent validation of the working weak-plane derivation and does not support a novelty claim.

## Closest established results

1. **Zhou, Zhang, Preskill & Jiang (2018), “Achieving the Heisenberg limit in quantum metrology using quantum error correction,” Nature Communications 9, 78, DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-02510-3.** This is the direct broad ancestry for using an ancillary logical code to retain signal while correcting Markovian noise. Its HNLS criterion is applicable algebraically here: with \(L_Z=\sqrt{\kappa}Z\), \(L_{\rm loss}=\sqrt{\gamma}|d\rangle\langle e|\), the Lindblad span is generated by \(I,Z,Z^2,|d\rangle\langle e|,|e\rangle\langle d|,|e\rangle\langle e|\) (and zero cross-products), so \(V=|a\rangle\langle e|+|e\rangle\langle a|\) lies outside it. However, the theorem is expressly in an ideal-control regime: noiseless ancillas, fast noiseless joint gates, perfect ancilla measurements/reset, and arbitrary joint probe-ancilla operations between short sensing intervals. It does not establish the proposed interface with bounded norm/slew, a forbidden \(a\)-\(e\) control block, an absorbing inaccessible dead state that cannot be recovered from, or a same-specimen destruction-risk budget. The HNLS theorem therefore establishes the broad possibility of QEC-enhanced sensing in an abstract Lindblad model, not the actual weak-plane operation or its vanishing-death construction.

2. **Zhou & Jiang (2020), “Optimal approximate quantum error correction for quantum metrology,” Physical Review Research 2, 013235, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.013235.** This is close in method: it constructs approximate codes and recoveries that trade some signal/noise suppression to attain the optimal SQL bound under general Markovian noise. Its setup again allows the most general sequential strategy, arbitrary CPTP maps every \(dt\), noiseless ancillas, and infinitesimally fast approximate recovery. Their central case is the HNLS-incompatible regime where exact correction would erase the signal. This is useful ancestry for “partially suppress/carry signal through an approximate code,” but it is not a finite-rate, bounded-control theorem and does not account for irreversible sample absorption.

3. **Finite-rate boundary.** Rojkov et al. (2022), “Bias in Error-Corrected Quantum Sensing,” Physical Review Letters 128, 140503, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevLett.128.140503, directly analyze finite-frequency/finite-strength QEC. They show residual decoherence and a systematic output bias from the delay between error and correction, with a correction procedure for the inferred signal. This makes finite-rate effects an established concern, not an untouched premise. Their setting is canonical sensor QEC (discrete detection/feedback or engineered correction jumps), not this no-\(a\)-\(e\)-control weak pulse with terminal, inaccessible specimen death.

   A very recent close discrete-time result is Liaubaitė, Ramacciotti & Raußendorf, “CSS codes for Quantum Metrology with Discrete-time Error Correction,” arXiv:2609.40022v1 (30 Sep 2026; preprint at inspection). For perpendicular Pauli noise it derives finite-\(\tau\) CSS-code sensing, a Heisenberg-like finite window and eventual SQL-like crossover at every fixed nonzero \(\tau\); recovery only at the end gives no advantage in that model. Its recovery operations are explicitly assumed ideal and instantaneous at fixed intervals. It is a useful warning against extrapolating finite-cadence QEC to infinite-time scaling, while differing from a sequence whose pulse strength and cadence themselves shrink with the asymptotic parameter and whose accumulated target phase is held fixed as duration diverges.

## Exact ancestry boundary

The broad ingredients—HNLS signal/noise separation, an ancillary logical degree of freedom, approximate/weak encoding, and signal retention in the presence of noise—have clear primary antecedents. The working claim is narrower and operational: for this \(V,Z,L_{\rm loss}\) model, a particular moving weak plane plus actual finite-rate safe reset, under the stated no-\(a\)-\(e\)-control and absorbing-death interface, allegedly accumulates a fixed \(g\)-dependent phase while the total probability of irreversible death tends to zero, at diverging paid duration. None of the checked primary results directly proves or disproves that assertion. Novelty remains UNKNOWN; the unresolved population, multiplier, and repeated-instrument gates in the working file remain decisive.

This comparison is not a validation of the model's physical realizability or of its endpoint, population, multiplier, reset, death-risk, or resource estimates.
