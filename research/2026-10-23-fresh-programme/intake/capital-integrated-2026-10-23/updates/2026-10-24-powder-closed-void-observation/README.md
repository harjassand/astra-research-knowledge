## Density gate and incumbent

Under the stated particle-mass, solid-density and excluded-volume premises, the sufficient bath setpoint is `rho_f,L >= rho_max,U (1-phi*)/(1-eta_U)`. For ideal pure Al with assumed `rho_max=2700 kg/m^3`, `eta=0`, and `phi*=0.01`, this gives `2673 kg/m^3`. The 2700 kg/m^3 upper bound is not acquired for the target alloy, so this arithmetic does not certify actual EN AC-43000 grains. A bulk average or alloy assay does not establish the maximum per-particle premises.

Dispersed-particle XCT is the strongest demonstrated operational incumbent for the accept/reject endpoint because it observes each particle's exterior and resolved internal pore geometry. Existing workflows show useful particle-scale scans, including 0.7-µm voxels, but do not bound accumulated sub-voxel porosity, segmentation error, or the unscreened maximum-tail coverage required for a one-sided certificate. A calibrated blinded same-particle challenge with pore references and held-out ground truth remains missing.

Two-fluid buoyant-mass sensing returns mass and fluid-excluded volume, not whether a pore is closed. A connected cavity that fails to wet or traps gas may resemble a closed void, and changing fluids can change wetting, chemistry or particle state. A 30-µm powder implementation is not shown by the cited bead/cell devices. The electric-response identity remains ideal-only and depends on fixed orientation and an acquired signed-field/trajectory measurement.

## Acquisition and false-acceptance limits

Settling direction alone does not certify density: startup momentum or downward fluid motion can produce downward travel for a bad grain. A finite-time one-sided test needs acquired bounds on fluid flow, residual startup slip and readout error; the source gives no uniform finite pass time at the threshold. Aggregation is also a max-tail failure: equal exterior volumes of dense Al and a grain with 1.5% closed void have average density `2679.75 kg/m^3` and can both sink at the ideal `2673 kg/m^3` cut. Singulation, wetting, coating preservation, material bounds, accepted yield and per-particle coverage remain unacquired. The density derivation is a conventional comparator, not a new separation technology or physical result.
