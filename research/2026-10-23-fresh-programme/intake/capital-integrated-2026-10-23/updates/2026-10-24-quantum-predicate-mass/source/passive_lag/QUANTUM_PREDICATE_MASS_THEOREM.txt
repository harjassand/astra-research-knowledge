QUANTUM PREDICATE FILTERS: A SOURCE-VALID MASS REFINEMENT
2026-10-24. passive_lag; root proposed the quantitative theorem target.
Internal mathematical construction. Novelty and external validity UNKNOWN.

Disposition

The exact ancillary-mask operation from the previous unit yields a useful
positive theorem, despite its established nonzero-overhead obstruction.
For a fixed FINITE dictionary of actual ancillary states and effects,
the source's answer-count term can be replaced by a distribution-weighted
one-sided mass parameter kappa. This is an operation on the original fine
effects, preserves the original tested event and every accepted live entry,
and uses ONE resource for the whole all-input family.

Subject to the already-frozen R/H/S_actual event reconstruction, the result
is the rate

  omega(G^n) <= exp[-2^-75 epsilon^3 n/(epsilon+log kappa)],       (T)

where epsilon=1-omega(G), logarithms are natural, and each model uses its
OWN base value. Finite matrix input gives the finite-tensor conclusion
directly, with no Lin premise. The separate commuting conclusion retains
the source's accepted finite-TABLE/finite-tracial passage; this note does
not supply or strengthen that premise.

For kappa=1 this recovers the frozen disjoint-block/projection result.
If log kappa=O(epsilon), the displayed exponent is of quadratic gap order.
For fixed nonblock kappa>1 it remains cubic. This is not general removal
of answer entropy, an optimal-rate theorem, or a physical resource bound.

1. The finite ancillary dictionary is concrete data

Write q=(x,y) for a supported one-round question pair and theta_q(a,b)
for its 0/1 acceptance mask. Select, for EACH q, finite-dimensional data

  normalized bipartite state eta_q,
  0<=E_a^q<=I on Alice's ancillary factor,
  0<=B_b^q<=I on Bob's ancillary factor,
  lambda_q>0,

satisfying the EXACT scalar identities

  <eta_q,E_a^q B_b^q eta_q> = theta_q(a,b)/lambda_q.             (1)

A mixed finite ancillary state can be purified on Alice's side before
using this notation. For a nonzero mask, lambda_q>=1. For the zero mask
take lambda_q=1 and all effects zero. Put

  e_a(q)=<eta_q,E_a^q eta_q>, f_b(q)=<eta_q,B_b^q eta_q>,
  k_A(q)=lambda_q max_a e_a(q),
  k_B(q)=lambda_q max_b f_b(q),
  kappa=max{1,E_(q~mu) k_A(q),E_(q~mu) k_B(q)}.                 (2)

The effects need NOT form POVMs as a varies or b varies. They are local
success effects, selected by the original coarse answer inside a positive
effect sum. Exact positive rectangle factorizations give finite classical
instances of (1); thus such dictionaries exist for every finite game.
No relaxation, abstract vector factorization, or NPA point is being used
as an ancillary realization.

All statements below hold for any chosen dictionary. One can subsequently
take an infimum over finite dictionaries by continuity of (T), without
assuming that the infimum is attained or that dimensions stay bounded.

2. One fixed resource and exact tested-mask tensorization

Fix the set C of tested coordinates, t=|C|, and enumerate its positions.
Prepare OFFLINE the finite ancillary pool

  eta_pool = tensor_(ell=1..t) tensor_(q in supp(mu)) eta_(ell,q).

Every factor is prepared before fresh live questions. For a hypothesized
tested question string c=(q_1,...,q_t) and coarse tested answer strings
alpha=(a_1,...,a_t), gamma=(b_1,...,b_t), set

  E_alpha^c = tensor_ell E_(a_ell)^(ell,q_ell),
  B_gamma^c = tensor_ell B_(b_ell)^(ell,q_ell),
  lambda_c = product_ell lambda_(q_ell),                       (3)

with identities on all unselected pool factors. Hence

  <eta_pool,E_alpha^c B_gamma^c eta_pool>
       = theta_c(alpha,gamma)/lambda_c,
  <E_alpha^c> = product_ell e_(a_ell)(q_ell),
  <B_gamma^c> = product_ell f_(b_ell)(q_ell).                   (4)

The tested predicate theta_c is the conjunction of its one-round masks.
The pool is the SAME for all c. A player's locally sampled/guessed history
may select a different c from the other player's history; both selections
still define local effects on this one resource. No claim that (4) holds
for mismatched guesses is needed. S_actual's existing all-input protocol
and its mismatch estimate handle those branches. A direct sum of states
depending on the actually realized pair would not establish this fact.

Although q contains BOTH tested questions, it belongs to the hypothesized
history r already used by R/H. It is not the opposite FRESH question. The
construction never chooses an ancillary state after observing that pair.

3. Exact local coarse and fine effects; no classical common flag

Use the frozen standard-form notation
  chi(F,H)=tr(sigma F sigma H), sigma>=0, tr(sigma^2)=1.
For a fixed flag/history, write F_x^alpha,H_y^gamma for the original coarse
tested-answer POVMs and G_x^(alpha,a),K_y^(gamma,b) for the fine effects
of a selected live coordinate. Thus
  sum_alpha F_x^alpha=sum_gamma H_y^gamma=I,
  sum_a G_x^(alpha,a)=F_x^alpha,
  sum_b K_y^(gamma,b)=H_y^gamma.
The original effects may be arbitrarily noncommuting on each player's side.

Represent the finite pool in Schmidt/positive standard form, with positive
unit vector sigma_pool.

In fixed Schmidt coordinates eta_q=sum_j s_j |j>_A|j>_B, use the positive
matrix diag(s_j) and represent the PHYSICAL Bob effect B_b^q by its
transpose (B_b^q)^T in the right/opposite algebra. The symbols B in (5)
denote these right representatives; e,f and (1)--(4) denote physical
probabilities. This fixed transpose is required for complex ancillary
effects, even though the real L fixture below does not expose it.
On the common tensor algebra and fixed vector sigma tensor sigma_pool,
define

  Fhat_x^c = sum_alpha F_x^alpha tensor E_alpha^c,
  Hhat_y^c = sum_gamma H_y^gamma tensor B_gamma^c,
  Ghat_x^(c,a) = sum_alpha G_x^(alpha,a) tensor E_alpha^c,
  Khat_y^(c,b) = sum_gamma K_y^(gamma,b) tensor B_gamma^c.        (5)

These are actual local/opposite positive effects. Since 0<=E,B<=I,
0<=Fhat,Hhat<=I. Fine sums are exactly Fhat and Hhat. Equations (1),(4)
and tensor factorization give, for EVERY flag,x,y,a,b,

  lambda_c chi_hat(Ghat_x^(c,a),Khat_y^(c,b))
    =sum_(alpha,gamma) theta_c(alpha,gamma)
                        chi(G_x^(alpha,a),K_y^(gamma,b)).      (6)

Thus the original event mass and FULL accepted live law are preserved,
including their x,y dependence. This is stronger than preserving just
acceptance probabilities or local marginals. It does not reproduce the
old fine tested history, and does not require a common classical rectangle
label. It also does not rebuild the whole coarse table; the known nuclear
norm obstruction to this conjunctive interface continues to apply.

For completeness, fine-POVM legality is an operator statement. If G_a>=0
and sum_a G_a=F, then bounded Douglas factorization supplies 0<=C_a<=I
on supp(F) with sqrt(F) C_a sqrt(F)=G_a and sum_a C_a=supp(F).
Complete one answer by I-supp(F). Do this for Fhat and Hhat. If R uses
polar factors A=V sqrt(Fhat), B=sqrt(Hhat) W, the transported POVMs are
  P_a=V C_a V*+1_(a=a0)(I-VV*),
  Q_b=W* D_b W+1_(b=b0)(I-W*W).
They have the exact fine expectation (6) on R's vector. These are the same
bounded support/polar operations already checked in the frozen proof;
they apply because (5) gives effects and positive fine subeffects, not
because of any abstract value assigned to kappa.

4. Normalization and the actual one-sided masses

Let p=P(E)>0 for the original tested-win event. Weight the c-history branch
by lambda_c outside chi_hat. Its original unconditioned flag measure is
still the one induced by mu^n. If a probability prior is wanted before
R/H, put
  Lambda_C=E_(c~mu^t) lambda_c,
  prior_tilt(dc,dflags)=lambda_c prior_original(dc,dflags)/Lambda_C.
Then the R event mass is p/Lambda_C. Its conditional event/live table is
EXACTLY the original one by (6). Dividing entropy errors by that R mass
cancels the common factor 1/Lambda_C. A hidden log Lambda_C term need
not be inserted into the actual weighted entropy estimate.

At exposure stage j, with its original unconditioned flags, define
  G_A,j=E_flags lambda_c chi_hat(Fhat_j^c,I),
  G_B,j=E_flags lambda_c chi_hat(I,Hhat_j^c).                  (7)
These are p-or-larger scalar masses. Pointwise in each flag with tested c,
let P_alpha be the original coarse one-party probabilities. Completeness
of the ORIGINAL coarse POVM gives

  lambda_c chi_hat(Fhat_j^c,I)
    =lambda_c sum_alpha P_alpha product_ell e_(a_ell)(q_ell)
    <=product_ell k_A(q_ell).                                (8)

The Bob formula uses Q_gamma and k_B. It does NOT require either original
coarse POVM or original answer distribution to factor across coordinates.
The flag average in (7) is ORIGINAL and unconditioned, not the posterior
law given E. Independent rounds imply c~mu^t for every fixed C, so

  G_A,j <= E_(c~mu^t) product_ell k_A(q_ell)
         =(E_mu k_A)^t <=kappa^t,
  G_B,j <=(E_mu k_B)^t <=kappa^t.                             (9)

In particular rare nonblock question pairs can have a small averaged
cost. Replacing E_mu k by max_q k is valid but unnecessarily coarse.
Arbitrary joint dependence within a round is fully retained in mu.
The same bound holds at every exposure stage; the telescope only needs
its two endpoints. C may be selected using the strategy's conditional
probabilities, but must be a fixed set of indices before actual questions
are drawn, as in the frozen greedy argument.

5. Entropy telescope, referee law, and loss estimate

For one term h=chi_hat(F,H), g_B=chi_hat(I,H), scalar Jensen gives
  -chi_hat(F log F,H)<=h log(g_B/h).
Weighted log-sum over flags with weight lambda_c therefore gives
  -U_j<=p log(G_B,j/p), -Z_j<=p log(G_A,j/p).
The existing monotone R telescope and (9) give normalized squared errors
at most

  tau_eff=[log(1/p)+t log kappa]/m, m=n-t>0.                 (10)

The actual conditional referee law has density P(E|questions)/p relative
to the ORIGINAL mu^n and therefore has KL<=log(1/p). Its average live
joint marginal TV is <=sqrt(log(1/p)/(2m))<=sqrt(tau_eff/2).
Tilting the hypothesized c prior above does not change this actual law.

Consequently the frozen same-protocol event inequality gives a legal
one-round strategy of loss, for every positive final accuracy,

  <=alpha+2^16[sqrt(alpha) tau_eff^(1/4)+sqrt(tau_eff)]
       +accuracy,                                           (11)

where alpha is the ORIGINAL conditional live loss. This is the original
Q/event, not an unconditional repeated-game value substituted for it.
The all-input resource, mismatched histories, fine effects, and source
timeout/graph approximation remain those already checked in that proof.

In the finite-matrix case, the enlarged effect family is finite and each
effect has finite spectrum. The exact scalar resolvent feature span then
removes compression error; S can likewise include all normalized spectra.
Only its positive smoothing and finite timeout limits remain. No Lin or
unbounded ancillary state is necessary for this finite-tensor statement.
In the general finite-tracial version, compression error is multiplied
by Lambda_C. It is finite for each fixed problem, so one can take enough
accuracy before the final limit; this is a representation COST, not a
new entropy term. No dimension/time/conditioning bound is asserted.

For the separate commuting extension, fix the finite ancillary dictionary
first. Each G_A,j,G_B,j is a finite linear functional of ORIGINAL marginal
table entries, with fixed scalar weights lambda_c e_alpha(c), etc.
Thus the accepted finite-TABLE density and scalar continuity transfer the
fixed-p inequality and the uniform bound (9). This does not represent
an arbitrary commuting ancilla by a finite-tracial one: the ancillary
dictionary used forward is FINITE from the outset.

6. Explicit greedy consequence and constants

Use either omega=omega_star or, with its separate source premise,
omega=omega_co. Set epsilon=1-omega>0, s=log kappa>=0. For a positive
n-round all-wins probability theta, write L=log(1/theta).
Suppose L<gamma epsilon^3 n/(epsilon+s), gamma=2^-75.
The frozen greedy argument provides a proper C with
  t<=2L/epsilon, p>=theta, alpha<=epsilon/2.
Indeed t<2 gamma epsilon^2 n/(epsilon+s)<=2 gamma epsilon n<n/2.
Consequently m>n/2 and

  tau_eff <=(2L/n)(1+2s/epsilon)
       <2 gamma epsilon^2 (epsilon+2s)/(epsilon+s)
       <=4 gamma epsilon^2=2^-73 epsilon^2
       <2^-72 epsilon^2.                                    (12)

There is no cancellation of the factor 2s. The last slack is sufficient
for the frozen scalar contradiction: substituting alpha<=epsilon/2 and
tau_eff<2^-72 epsilon^2 into (11) makes loss strictly less than epsilon,
since
  1/2+2^16*2^-18/sqrt(2)+2^16*2^-36
      =1/2+1/(4sqrt(2))+2^-20<1.
Take the positive final accuracy small. This contradicts the chosen base
value, without requiring any optimal strategy to be attained. Hence (T).
For epsilon=0 the bound is trivially one; theta=0 is already admissible.

7. A strict finite quantum improvement over classical local loads

For L=[[1,1],[0,1]], share eta=Phi+ and use real rank-one projections at
angles Alice(pi/6,pi/2), Bob(0,pi/3). Directly
  <E_a B_b>=(1/2)cos^2(angle_A-angle_B)=(3/8)L_ab,
and all four ancillary marginals equal 1/2. Therefore
  lambda=8/3, k_A=k_B=4/3.                                  (13)
This is an exact two-qubit realization, not a relaxation. No claim that
4/3 is optimal among all finite or commuting ancillary lifts is made.

For comparison, every classical response lift of this same mask has
max{k_A,k_B}>=3/2. With q_ab=E[e_a f_b]=t L_ab and 0<=e,f<=1,
q_10=0 implies e_1 f_0=0 almost surely. Pointwise on that support,
  e_0 f_0+e_0 f_1+e_1 f_1<=e_0+f_1.
Taking expectations gives 3t<=E e_0+E f_1, proving the bound. It is attained
by four equiprobable indicator rectangles: row0 x both columns; both
rows x column1; singleton00; singleton11. Their q is L/2, and the maximum
ancillary marginal is 3/4. Thus the exact classical balanced cap is 3/2.

If every supported nonzero mask is either disjoint-block or L-shaped and
the total mu-mass of L-shaped pairs is zeta, the separate finite dictionaries
with k_A=k_B=1 and 4/3 give
  kappa<=1+zeta/3, log kappa<=zeta/3.                         (14)
Thus zeta=O(epsilon) gives a quadratic gap-order conclusion within the same
source proof. The corresponding balanced classical dictionary gives
1+zeta/2. Equation (14) is a concrete positive consequence of the quantum
lift, while the worst-case nonblock constant still yields cubic order.

8. A nonblock class with answer-alphabet-independent classical cost

Root supplied the following useful positive corollary; the same exact
dictionary and source checks above apply. Suppose the REJECTED relation
R_q=1-theta_q is a union of vertex-disjoint complete bipartite blocks.
Assign a common finite label ell to all row and column vertices of each
nonisolated rejected block. Give every isolated row/column a private label,
with all such labels distinct from the block labels and from one another.
Then R_q(a,b)=1 exactly when ell_A(a)=ell_B(b).

Choose distinct codes code(ell) in F2^d, d=ceil(log2(number of labels)),
and share uniform r in F2^d and b0 in F2. Write
  h(ell)=r dot code(ell)+b0 mod2,
  E_a=1{h(ell_A(a))=0}, B_b=1{h(ell_B(b))=1}.                (15)
These are finite classical effects. Each hash bit is uniform. For distinct
labels the two bits are jointly uniform: their difference is the uniform
nontrivial linear form r dot(code(ell)-code(ell')), independently of b0.
Therefore
  <E_a B_b>=0 for equal labels, 1/4 for distinct labels,
  <E_a>=<B_b>=1/2.
Thus lambda_q=4 and k_A(q)=k_B(q)=2, independent of the number of labels.
When a mask is all accepting, one can instead use the trivial cap-one
dictionary. Zero acceptance masks likewise cause no difficulty.

In particular, an ordinary coloring-type game with equality masks on
same-question pairs and inequality masks on edge pairs admits
  kappa<=1+zeta, zeta=mu{edge-type question pairs}.             (16)
For positive OWN finite-tensor or commuting base gap epsilon, (T) gives
  omega(G^n)<=exp[-2^-75 epsilon^3 n/
                           (epsilon+log(1+zeta))].
This is answer-alphabet independent even when the number of colors grows.
For zeta=O(epsilon), it has quadratic gap order. For fixed positive zeta,
it has cubic gap order. A classical or chromatic base gap may not be
substituted for the model's own entangled/commuting gap.

The seed in (15) has d+1 bits for that ONE mask, rather than a random
table with one independent bit per label. This does not make the overall
history sampler or the full R/S protocol efficient; their other costs
remain uncontrolled. Strongest prior comparison and novelty for this
nonblock corollary are UNKNOWN.
