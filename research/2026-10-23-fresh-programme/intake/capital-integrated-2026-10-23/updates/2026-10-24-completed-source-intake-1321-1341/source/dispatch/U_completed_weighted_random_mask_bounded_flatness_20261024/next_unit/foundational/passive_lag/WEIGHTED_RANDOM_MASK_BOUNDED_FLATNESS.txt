The problem is to pass from an actual nearly perfect, graph-adaptive

Two proposed shortcuts are not used: a lower bound on Schmidt

1. Actual coarse effects and random representatives
---------------------------------------------------
Fix k input settings per side, M coarse outputs, and a pure finite
strategy on faithful Schmidt supports C^d:

  |psi>=sum_j s_j|j,j>,  S=diag(s_j)>0, Tr S^2=1.

Let A_i^x,B_j^y be COMPLETE POVMs with actual effect ranks <=h. In
particular d<=hM. No assumption is made about the ratio max s_j/min s_j.
The game predicate Gamma may already have determined the entire state
and the POVMs. In all probability below, Gamma and that strategy are
held fixed; the added representative randomness is merely algebraic.

For a nonzero Alice effect A of rank r choose z uniformly on the complex
unit sphere of range(A), and set a=sqrt(r)sqrt(A)z. Use independent
draws at every effect/input slot. For zero effects put a=0. Then

  E aa*=A,   aa*<=hI.                                    (F1)

For Bob independently use the same construction with the transposed
effects (B_j^y)^T, giving b_j^y. Set U_(x,i),:=a_i^{x*},
V_(y,j),:=b_j^{y*}, and

  D=U S V*,   D_xy[i,j]=a_i^{x*}S b_j^y.

Consequently

  E |D_xy[i,j]|^2=Tr(A_i^x S (B_j^y)^T S)
                =p_original(i,j|x,y).                   (F2)

The covariance in F1 is the INDIVIDUAL effect A, not I. Summing all M
outcomes of one complete input setting gives covariance I. Each vector
has norm squared <=h, and every scalar entry of D has absolute value
<=h. This is not a complete scalar POVM or a physical local operation.

For any fixed binary predicate, let delta be the original mean loss
under uniform independent x,y. With L_Gamma denoting entrywise masking
to losing coarse cells, F2 gives exactly

  E ||L_Gamma D||_F^2=delta k^2.                          (F3)

3. Sparse or energy-weighted cross sampling
-------------------------------------------
Let c_e(D)>=0 be ANY finite additive cost assigned to each entry of D,
chosen without seeing I,J. The cost may depend on D, on Gamma, or on a
fixed quantization rule. Put C(D)=sum_e c_e(D). For the twice-counted
cross and its anchor define

  C_cross=sum_(row in I,all columns)c_e
          +sum_(all rows,column in J)c_e,
  C_anchor=sum_(row in I,column in J)c_e.

Conditionally on D, each entry is included in a sampled input row with
probability t/k and in the anchor with probability (t/k)^2. Therefore

  E[C_cross|D]=2(t/k)C(D),
  E[C_anchor|D]=(t/k)^2 C(D).                           (F7)

For any A>0, Markov gives respective bad-event probabilities <=2/A
and <=1/A for the bounds A(t/k)C(D), A(t/k)^2 C(D). If C(D)=0, both
bounds hold exactly. Several predeclared costs can be handled by a
union bound. Conditioning on D for F7 does not assume that the frame
event is independent; its separate unconditional probability F4 is
combined by the ordinary union bound.

For example, take A=32, loss threshold 8 delta k^2, and
t>=(3h/epsilon^2)log(64d), t<=k. For ONE cost the sum of failure bounds
is at most 1/8+1/8+3/32=11/32. Thus some actual representative family
and whole-input anchor pair simultaneously satisfy F5, F6,

  ||L_Gamma D||_F^2<=8 delta k^2,
  C_cross<=32(t/k)C(D),
  C_anchor<=32(t/k)^2 C(D).                             (F8)

At delta=0 the losing energy is zero almost surely. F8 is a reduction
of the actual payoff, including arbitrary Schmidt spectra and public
graph adaptation, to an algebraic amplitude table with controlled
interpolation and cross cost. It is not itself a random-Gamma bound.

4. A mixed-anchor identity avoids inverse Schmidt factors
----------------------------------------------------------
Consider TWO amplitude tables D=USV* and D'=U'S'V'*, possibly of
different ranks, on the same coarse row/column alphabet and input-block
anchor I,J. Assume their selected U_I,V_J and U'_I,V'_J have full column
rank. Write

 C=D[I,J], R=D[:,J], T=D[I,:],
 Q_A=U U_I^+, Q_B=V V_J^+,

and use primes for the second table. Then D=Q_A C Q_B*,
R=Q_A C, T=C Q_B*, and similarly for the primed table. EXACTLY,

 D-D'=Q_A(T-T')+(R-R')Q_B'^*
                    -Q_A(C-C')Q_B'^*.                 (F9)

Expanding the right side cancels Q_A C'Q_B'^* and Q_A C Q_B'^*,
leaving D-D'. No inverse of S, S', C, or C' occurs. Hence

 ||D-D'||_F <= K_A||T-T'||_F+K_B'||R-R'||_F
                          +K_A K_B'||C-C'||_F,          (F10)

where K_A=||Q_A|| and K_B'=||Q_B'||. If BOTH tables satisfy the
corresponding F5 bounds, these interpolation norms are at most

           K=sqrt((1+epsilon)k/((1-epsilon)t)).          (F11)

The primed Bob frame bound is needed for K_B'; an unprimed bound cannot
be substituted silently. F9--F11 are comparison identities, not a
recipe for applying a remote frame transform to the original resource.
They apply to genuine two-table representatives of a quantization box,
even with different Schmidt ranks and no spectral floor.

9. Signed-amplitude max-norm entropy, with genuine legal centers

For a complex matrix A, gamma_2(A) is the infimum of
max_i||X_i||_2 max_j||Y_j||_2 over factorizations A=XY*. With Ucal,Vcal
the vertically stacked input isometries, factor

 A=(Ucal S^(1/2)/sqrt(k))(Vcal S^(1/2)/sqrt(k))*.

Every row of each factor has squared norm at most s/k. Therefore

                    gamma_2(A)<=s/k.                    (F26)

For real matrices, the Grothendieck inequality and finite-dimensional

This precise convex-hull inclusion is Srebro--Shraibman, COLT 2005,

 A=r0 T c,  ||c||_1=1,  r0=2 K_G s/k,

The coefficients can be nonnegative after including signed atoms.
Their entries are complex signed amplitudes, not nonnegative local

An empirical-average cover makes this quantitative. If a complex

                t>=C r0^2 eta^(-2) log(2N).

10. Multiscale quadratic sampling gives a bounded-flatness mask bound
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The needed sampling input is an IMPORTED primary theorem, not a generic
tensor conjecture. Haviv--Regev, arXiv:1507.01768v2, Theorem 3.1
(simpler analysis) states for ANY square complex matrix B of size H,
not necessarily unitary, that m iid uniform rows simultaneously satisfy

 |mean_sample |Bz|^2 - mean_full |Bz|^2|
 <=epsilon mean_full |Bz|^2
                 +eta ||z||_1^2 ||B||_infinity^2        (F31)

with sample threshold C epsilon^(-3) eta^(-1)
log H log^2(1/eta), and failure at most
exp[-c epsilon^(-2) log H log(1/eta)]. Absolute constants and sufficiently
small parameters are understood. Theorem 4.1 improves the epsilon
dependence. Neither statement assumes sparse z or orthogonal columns;
those assumptions enter only their subsequent RIP corollaries.
The proof's finite-net Chernoff bounds (Lemma 3.4, or Lemma 4.3) decrease
with m, so F31 holds with these bounds for EVERY m above the threshold.

For the rectangular L-by-K master dictionary in F27, form a square
matrix of size H=LK by repeating each original row K times and padding
the original K columns by H-K zero columns. Uniform rows push forward
exactly to iid uniform original coordinates, ||B||_infinity=1, and
log H=log L+log K=O(N). Padding c by zeros preserves its l1 norm.
In particular, the astronomical dictionary description is finite;
there is no efficient construction or calibration claim.

Set

       kappa=r0^2 L=4 K_G^2 M^2 s^2.                   (F32)

For every ACTUAL amplitude under the cap, F27 and ||A||_F=1 imply
mean_full |Tc|^2=1/kappa. No norm floor is assumed for other coefficients
of the master dictionary. Fix a sufficiently small constant
tau>0. Apply F31 with epsilon=tau/2, eta=tau/(2kappa). Then, on one
event simultaneous for all admissible c, including choices made after
seeing the sample,

       |mean_sample |Tc|^2-1/kappa|<=tau/kappa          (F33)

whenever

 m>=m0:=C_tau kappa log H log^2(2kappa).                (F34)

The failure bound can be written
delta_HR<=exp[-c_tau log H log(2kappa)]. This imports a multiscale
quadratic-norm theorem; it is stronger than merely applying a
Lipschitz contraction to an amplitude net.

The original graph has INDEPENDENT directed/bipartite Bernoulli(q)
edges on these L coordinates. There is an elementary upper-bound
transfer from iid row sampling to precisely that graph law. Let

 lambda=-log(1-q), Z_e independent Poisson(lambda),
 Gamma_e=1[Z_e>=1], m=sum_e Z_e.

The Gamma_e have exactly the required independent Bernoulli(q) law.
Conditional on m, the multiplicities Z_e are those of m iid uniform
coordinates. Assume lambda L>=2m0. Poisson Chernoff gives m>=m0 and
m<=(1+tau)lambda L except with probability at most
2 exp[-c_tau lambda L]. On the conditional F33 good event, for ALL
actual amplitudes at once,

 sum_e Gamma_e |A_e|^2
 <=sum_e Z_e |A_e|^2
 =r0^2 m mean_sample |Tc|^2
 <=(1+tau)m/L
 <=(1+tau)^2 lambda.                                   (F35)

Conditioning on m and integrating its distribution costs no union
over strategies or over m; the HR failure bound is uniform for m>=m0.
Thus with probability at least

 1-delta_HR-2 exp[-c_tau lambda N^2],                    (F36)

the supremum of the ACTUAL graph-adaptive capped-spectrum h=1 game
score is at most (1+tau)^2[-log(1-q)]. The comparison uses auxiliary
Poisson counts only to prove a statement about the Bernoulli graph.
It does not replace the graph, change the strategy, or offer a physical
repair map. A convex mixture of such pure capped-spectrum branches
obeys the same linear payoff bound, since it is already uniform over
each branch.

Here is the literal BG-scale conclusion. Put r=floor(k^(1/3)),
M=k^(r-1), q=k^(-1/5), and suppose M||S||_op^2<=C0 for a fixed constant
C0 (square maximally entangled projective strategies have C0=1).
Then kappa=O_C0(M), log H=Theta(kM), and log kappa=Theta(log M).
Condition lambda L>=2m0 follows for sufficiently large k because

 (lambda N^2)/m0 >=c_(tau,C0) qk/log^2 M
                       =Omega(k^(2/15)/log^2 k) ->infty. (F37)

Hence with probability tending to one, EVERY such graph-adaptive
actual strategy has beta_Gamma=O(q)=O(k^(-1/5)), which tends to zero.
The universal exceptional probability is bounded by F36. This is a
genuine weighted random-mask theorem for the stated spectral class,
not an exact-zero/support-only conclusion or a graph-blind-apparatus
bound. The general sufficient condition remains F34 with F32; in
particular the factor M||S||_op^2 cannot be dropped.

The theorem does NOT bound arbitrary Schmidt spectra, arbitrary
high-rank POVM effects, arbitrary entangled/commuting strategies, or
the unrestricted BG value. A maximally entangled state on d<M Schmidt
dimensions has M||S||_op^2=M/d, and pays that factor rather than being
silently treated as the square flat case. The high-alphabet perfect
permutation construction at log M of order k log(1/q) lies outside
F37's sampling regime. No minimum Schmidt coefficient is assumed.
There is no priority claim beyond the explicit Grothendieck and
Haviv--Regev imports, and no generic low-information selection theorem.

11. The additive theorem averages actual rank-h representatives
--------------------------------------------------------------
The same mechanism extends to actual coarse effects of rank at most
h. This is not an h^2-loss reduction to a refined random graph: the
original graph bits remain the same, one per COARSE outcome pair.

Fix a pure finite strategy in faithful Schmidt coordinates, with
Tr S^2=1 and the common cap ||S||_op<=s. For every actual effect use
the independent representatives a,b of F1, with zero effects assigned
the zero vector. Each draw obeys ||a||^2,||b||^2<=h. Define
A_rep=D_rep/k as in F2. Its row-factor bound gives

 gamma_2(A_rep)<=hs/k,
 E_rep ||A_rep||_F^2=1,
 E_rep sum_e Gamma_e |A_rep,e|^2=beta_Gamma_actual.       (F38)

The expectations are identities for each FIXED graph and its chosen
actual strategy, since F2 holds for every coarse probability and both
original POVMs are complete. No realization A_rep is asserted to be a
complete measurement table or to have unit Frobenius norm.

Use the SAME master sign dictionary from F27, now with

 r_h=2 K_G hs/k,   kappa_h=r_h^2 L=4 K_G^2 h^2 M^2 s^2.

Every representative has A_rep=r_h T c_rep with ||c_rep||_1<=1.
The dictionary is fixed independently of the graph, strategy, Schmidt
coordinates, and representative randomness. Zero can be represented
using the symmetry of the atom convex hull. Apply F31 with
epsilon=tau and eta=tau/kappa_h, without any image norm floor. After
multiplication by r_h^2 this gives, simultaneously for every such
representative on one HR event,

 sum_e Z_e |A_rep,e|^2
 <=(m/L)[(1+tau)||A_rep||_F^2+tau].                     (F39)

In particular, this is NOT an application of F33 to unnormalized
representatives. It uses the relative-plus-ADDITIVE statement F31.
The event holds for all coefficient vectors, so after fixing a good
graph/count realization we may choose a graph-adaptive actual strategy
and take its representative expectation in F39. Since Gamma<=Z,
F38 yields exactly

 beta_Gamma_actual<=(m/L)(1+2tau)
       <=(1+tau)(1+2tau)[-log(1-q)].                    (F40)

The sufficient sample condition and failure bounds are F34--F36 with
kappa replaced by kappa_h (and constants retuned for tau). In
particular it suffices that

 lambda N^2>=2 C_tau kappa_h log H log^2(2kappa_h).      (F41)

This is one event for ALL actual rank-h strategies satisfying the
spectral cap. The actual probability table, not a randomly normalized
scalar table, appears in F40. No representative is physically sampled
by the players; expectation is used solely in the proof.

In the BG scale a convenient sufficient asymptotic condition is

 h^2 M s^2 log^2(2kappa_h)=o(qk).                       (F42)

For example, a flat faithful Schmidt support d with d>=c hM, c>0,
has s^2=1/d and h^2Ms^2<=h/c. If h grows polynomially in k, then
log(2kappa_h)=Theta(log M), and
h=o(k^(2/15)/log^2 k) suffices. Fixed h and d comparable to hM are
included. The lower-dimensional case still pays the literal M/d
factor; no dimension or spectrum is flattened for free.

The rank cap is on the ACTUAL effects on the faithful supports, not a
rank-one refinement or a Naimark dilation. A convex mixture is covered
when it is explicitly a mixture of pure branches satisfying these
same caps; compressing original effects to a branch support cannot
increase rank. Arbitrary mixed states are not brought into this class
by a purification claim, and no bound on an average Schmidt spectrum
is substituted for the branchwise cap. The argument is finite matrix
theory; it supplies no unrestricted commuting/type-III extension.
