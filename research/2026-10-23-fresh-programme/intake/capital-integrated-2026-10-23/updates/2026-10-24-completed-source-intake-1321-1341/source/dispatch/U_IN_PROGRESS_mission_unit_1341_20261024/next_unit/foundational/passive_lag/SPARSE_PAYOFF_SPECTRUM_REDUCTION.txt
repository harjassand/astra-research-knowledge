The new
operation below reduces an actual positive game payoff to sparse
original-label sub-POVMs, while retaining the ORIGINAL shared state.
It removes the output-count overcharge in the previously established
rank-one maximally-entangled sampling bound. The spectral-layer step
is standard Junge--Palazuelos ancestry, not a new state-elimination
principle. The unrestricted quantum/commuting selection gate is OPEN.

1. Literal random-game and resource class
----------------------------------------
There are k>=2 uniformly independent questions x,y and M>=2 original
answer labels i,j. The bits Gamma_(x,i;y,j) are independent Bernoulli(q)
on distinct DIRECTED/BIPARTITE cells, with 0<q<=1/2. A fixed-label BG
fiber game is included after its fixed injective relabeling. If a
different game maps multiple coarse cells to one random graph edge,
that repeated-edge case is not silently included in this theorem.

The actual strategy is finite, pure, in faithful Schmidt coordinates
C^d tensor C^d, with actual POVM effects of rank at most one. Thus
d<=M. Write psi=sum_j s_j|j,j>, sorted s_1>=...>=s_d>0,
S=diag(s_j), and Tr S^2=1. No ratio, floor, or uniform cap on s_1 is
assumed in the eventual spectral conclusion. All states and apparatus
may be chosen after seeing the entire public Gamma. Inputs remain
fresh and each local POVM depends only on its own realized input.

Proof-only conclusive sub-POVMs are completed by PRIVATE abort flags.
The comparison score is the UNCONDITIONAL original winning predicate
times the event that both flags are conclusive. No original answer
is presumed universally losing. An abort may be mapped to an original
default answer physically, but its possible extra wins are excluded
from the comparison score. There is no conditioning on both flags.

2. Imported positive PSD sparsification with one scalar payoff
-------------------------------------------------------------
de Carli Silva--Harvey--Sato, arXiv:1107.0088v2, Theorem 3, states:
for Hermitian PSD matrices B_i with B=sum_i B_i and 0<epsilon<1,
there exist common weights w_i>=0, on at most C n/epsilon^2 indices,
such that B<=sum_i w_i B_i<=(1+epsilon)B, where n is matrix dimension.
The theorem explicitly permits arbitrary ranks and complex Hermitian
matrices. It is an imported established result.

Let E_i be one actual d-dimensional POVM and c_i>=0 one scalar payoff
contribution per ORIGINAL outcome. Apply the theorem to
B_i=E_i direct-sum c_i. For C0=sum_i c_i>0, their sum is
I_d direct-sum C0; if C0=0 omit the scalar block. It yields

 I<=F:=sum_i w_iE_i<=(1+epsilon)I,
 C0<=sum_i w_i c_i<=(1+epsilon)C0,                      (S1)

using at most C(d+1)/epsilon^2 original labels. Define

             G_i=w_i E_i/(1+epsilon).                 (S2)

Then sum_i G_i<=I, every conclusive effect retains its original rank
and label, and its score is at least C0/(1+epsilon). Complete with
the single abort effect I-sum_i G_i. The abort effect may have high
rank; it NEVER enters the active amplitude matrix or its rank-one
bound. There is no inverse-square-root normalization and no additive
epsilon loss. No fine postmeasurement workspace is preserved.

If fewer than the requested maximum labels survive, pad the selected
set by UNUSED distinct original labels with zero conclusive effect.
Repeated occurrences of an original label are merged by adding their
positive weights on the same original effect. They do not create
independent copies of a graph bit.

3. The two-sided operation keeps the original state and scalar payoff
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix an actual strategy and let beta be its mean positive game payoff.
For every Alice input x, let

 c_i^x=(1/k)sum_(y,j) Gamma_(x,i;y,j)
                          <psi|E_i^x tensor B_j^y|psi>.

These are nonnegative. Apply S1--S2 separately at each x, retaining
the same psi and Bob apparatus. The BOTH-CONCLUSIVE score after this
one-side replacement is at least beta/(1+epsilon), with Bob initially
always conclusive. Alice is now treated as a sub-POVM in the score.

For each Bob input y, compute his c_j^y against ONLY these conclusive
Alice effects. Sparsify and scale Bob in exactly the same way. It
follows that the final conclusive table satisfies

       beta_sparse >= beta/(1+epsilon)^2.              (S3)

Each side uses at most t=min{M,ceil(C(d+1)/epsilon^2)} distinct
conclusive original labels per input. The fallback t=M just keeps
the original POVM when the stated bound exceeds M. In either case
d<=t<=C_epsilon d. Positive rank-one effects summing to F>=I require
at least d nonzero slots; zero-payoff inputs may still be sparsified
on the effect block alone, so that statement also holds there.

The sequence is material: completing Alice to an original default
answer BEFORE forming Bob's costs could hide score in a high-rank
default, which is not counted by the active sampling model. Here
Alice's private abort is unscored throughout Bob's sparsification.
All weights are calculated offline from the declared strategy and
public game. They do not reveal a realized fresh opposite question.
No cheap calibration, same-instrument realization, or LOCC conversion
of a supplied unknown strategy is asserted.

4. Actual sparse sub-POVM amplitudes need no unit-norm premise
------------------------------------------------------------
For fixed selected label sets of size t, identify them increasingly
with [t]. A rank-one sub-POVM has effects a_i^x a_i^{x*}, with
sum_i a_i^x a_i^{x*}<=I and ||a_i^x||<=1, and similarly for Bob's
transposed effects with vectors b_j^y. Its active amplitude is

 A_(x,i;y,j)=a_i^{x*} S b_j^y/k.

It is an ACTUAL subnormalized measurement table, and

                 ||A||_F^2<=1,
                 gamma_2(A)<=||S||_op/k.               (S4)

The first inequality follows by summing the joint conclusive effects;
the second uses the S^(1/2) factorization and the vector-norm bounds.
For a maximally entangled psi=Phi_d, put s^2=1/d. The active dimension
is N'=kt and L'=(kt)^2. The fixed sign/imaginary-sign Grothendieck
dictionary gives A=r0 T c, ||c||_1<=1, with

 r0=2K_G/(k sqrt(d)),
 K_atom<=4 times 2^(2N'-1),
 kappa=r0^2 L'=4K_G^2 t^2/d.                           (S5)

The dictionary is independent of the graph, selected label pattern,
sub-POVMs, and any coefficient adaptation. It consists of signed
complex amplitudes, not nonnegative local probability tables.

5. Confidence-paid simultaneous sampling on all original-label sets
-------------------------------------------------------------------
There are at most

          P_t=binom(M,t)^(2k)                          (S6)

support patterns, with log P_t<=2kt log(eM/t). For each fixed pattern
the active bits restrict to DISTINCT original Gamma cells, so remain
independent Bernoulli(q). Correlations between different patterns do
not matter for a union bound.

Use Haviv--Regev arXiv:1507.01768v2 Theorem 3.1 (or 4.1), in its
literal arbitrary-matrix relative-plus-additive form. For a square
H-dimensional padded master dictionary with entries bounded by one,
m iid uniform rows give simultaneously for all ||c||_1<=1

 mean_sample |Tc|^2
 <=(1+tau)mean_full |Tc|^2 + tau/kappa,                 (S7)

once m>=C_tau kappa log H log^2(2kappa), with failure at most
exp[-c_tau log H log(2kappa)]. This is NOT a uniform absolute
tau/kappa approximation for all coefficients. Multiplying S7 by r0^2
and using the actual sub-POVM norm bound S4 is sufficient.

The square embedding can be made as large as required: repeat each
of the L' original rows equally R times, so H=RL'>=K_atom, and pad
columns by zeros. Uniform rows still push forward exactly to iid
uniform active coordinates. In addition to log H>=C N', choose

 log H>=C_tau (log P_t+u)/log(2kappa).                 (S8)

Taking the least such integer R costs no more than
log H=O_tau(kt+(log P_t+u)/log(2kappa)). This confidence payment
increases the sample requirement; it is not a free confidence boost.

Couple Gamma_e=1[Z_e>0] with independent Poisson(lambda) counts,
lambda=-log(1-q). For any one pattern, conditional on m=sum Z_e,
its active multiplicities are m iid uniform coordinates. The HR proof
has the same-or-better failure for every m above its threshold.
Therefore S7, S4, Gamma<=Z, and a Poisson upper-count bound imply

       beta_active<=(1+tau)(1+2tau)lambda              (S9)

simultaneously for all active strategies and all patterns except
probability O(exp(-u)), provided

 lambda L'>=C_tau [kappa log H log^2(2kappa)
                              +log P_t+u].             (S10)

The second term explicitly pays the union of Poisson-count tails.
No independence between the support events is asserted. This is an
UPPER-only Bernoulli statement; Gamma<=Z does not control signed
cross terms or furnish a two-sided Bernoulli RIP.

6. One event for all maximally-entangled ranks d<=M
---------------------------------------------------
Fix epsilon,tau>0 sufficiently small, independently of k,M,q. For
each d=1,...,M use t_d=min{M,ceil(C(d+1)/epsilon^2)}, so
d<=t_d<=C_epsilon d. By S5, kappa=Theta(t_d^2/d), hence
kappa<=C_epsilon t_d and log(2kappa)<=C_epsilon log(2M).
Take u_d=k log(2M)+log(2M), so that summing O(exp(-u_d)) over
d<=M still tends to zero. Equations S6--S10 show that a sufficient
condition, with constants depending only on epsilon,tau, is

               qk>=C_(epsilon,tau) log^2(2M).         (S11)

Indeed, substitution into the leading term gives

 kappa log H log^2(2kappa)
 <=C_(epsilon,tau) [k t_d^3/d]
          [log^2(2kappa)+log(eM/t_d)log(2kappa)]
       +C_(epsilon,tau) [t_d^2/d]u_d log(2kappa).

Dividing by k^2 t_d^2, using t_d/d<=C_epsilon and d>=1,
bounds this and the separate count term by
C_(epsilon,tau)log^2(2M)/k. This also handles d=1 and t_d=M;
log(2kappa), not log kappa, is used at small parameters.

Consequently there is one graph event of probability
1-O(exp[-k log(2M)]) on which EVERY maximally-entangled pure
rank-one-effect strategy of any faithful dimension 1<=d<=M has

 beta_ME<=(1+epsilon)^2(1+tau)(1+2tau)[-log(1-q)]
                      =:B_(epsilon,tau,q).             (S12)

This follows by first sparsifying the actual strategy with S3 and
then applying the simultaneous active bound. All states, local bases,
POVMs, and retained labels may depend on Gamma. The state has not
been flattened, and no original-space high-rank abort is inserted
into the active matrix. Mixtures of these stated ME branches obey
the same linear bound. This is a derived consequence of positive
PSD sparsification, Grothendieck, and HR, not a new generic RIP.

7. Standard positive-payoff spectral bridge, keeping original weights
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Now return to an arbitrary original Schmidt spectrum in the SAME
rank-one-effect class, dimension d<=M. Put s_(d+1)=0, let P_j be
the first-j Schmidt projection, and define

 a_j=(s_j-s_(j+1))sqrt(j)>=0,
 Phi_j=(1/sqrt(j))sum_(l<=j)|l,l>.

The original pure vector satisfies the exact identity

        psi=sum_j a_j Phi_j,
        L(S):=sum_j a_j
             =sum_j (sqrt(j)-sqrt(j-1))s_j.            (S13)

This is a mathematical linear decomposition of the ORIGINAL vector.
It is not a physical mixture or a local flattening procedure. For
each Phi_j, compress the SAME original local POVM effects to its
prefix Schmidt support. These are complete POVMs, with ranks at most
one, and produce exactly the Phi_j score of those original effects.
Thus S12 applies to every prefix on the common all-d event.

For fixed instruments and Gamma, the direct sum of winning amplitudes
is linear in the shared pure vector. Its squared norm is the actual
unconditional payoff. Minkowski applied to S13 therefore gives

                   beta_psi<=B_(epsilon,tau,q) L(S)^2. (S14)

The positive-operator equivalent is Junge--Palazuelos,
arXiv:1007.3043v2, Lemma 5.6/Theorem 5.7: a positive Bell functional
can be controlled by maximally-entangled restrictions at an O(log d)
loss. That primary standard principle is credited here. S13 is the
direct sorted-prefix instance and retains every original Schmidt
weight in the norm comparison; no flattening assumption is made.

8. What this acquires, and the genuine remaining case
----------------------------------------------------
Abel summation and Cauchy--Schwarz give

 L(S)^2<=sum_(j=1)^d (sqrt(j)-sqrt(j-1))^2
             <=1+H_(d-1)/4<=C(1+log d),                (S15)

where H_0=0. For harmonic coefficients
s_j=1/sqrt(j H_d), L(S)^2=Theta(log d), so this logarithmic factor
is not removable from this generic spectral decomposition bound.
That is a scalar limitation of the comparison, not a constructed
large-score random-graph strategy.

A two-level spike-plus-flat-tail spectrum has L(S)^2<=2, even when
d||S||_op^2 grows without bound. More generally S14 applies to every
actual spectrum, with its displayed L(S), rather than only a supplied
operator-norm cap. In the BG scale r=floor(k^(1/3)), M=k^(r-1),
q=k^(-1/5), condition S11 holds eventually for every fixed epsilon,tau
because qk/log^2(2M) tends to infinity. Thus

             beta_psi<=C q L(S)^2                     (S16)

uniformly over graph-adaptive actual rank-one-effect strategies on
one high-probability event. A specified positive constant payoff forces
L(S)^2=Omega(1/q) and hence Schmidt rank
d>=exp(Omega(1/q))=exp(Omega(k^(1/5))) by S15. This necessary-rank
consequence is confined to the literal effect-rank-one class.

There is a separate, direct mixed-state closure. Keep ACTUAL complete
rank-at-most-one POVMs on the original finite local Hilbert spaces,
and suppose the shared mixed state has Schmidt number at most D.
Choose an ensemble of pure states with Schmidt ranks d_l<=D. Compress
the SAME original effects to each component's local Schmidt supports;
rank cannot increase and completeness survives, so d_l<=M. The one
common graph event then gives, by linearity of the original payoff,

 beta_mixed<=B_(epsilon,tau,q) sum_l p_l L(S_l)^2
                         <=B_(epsilon,tau,q) H_D.      (S17)

For D=1 use H_1=1. Thus constant payoff also requires Schmidt number
exp(Omega(1/q)) within this actual-effect-rank-one class. This is an
ensemble/convexity argument, NOT a purification of the mixed state or
an inference about an averaged mixed marginal spectrum. No uncounted
classical flag changes the rank premise on the actual readouts.

This still does NOT close the original weighted gap: the allowed
log d can be of order log M=Theta(k^(1/3)log k), so q log d need
not tend to zero. Broad harmonic spectra and graph-adaptive signed
cross-band coherence remain a substantive unresolved case. Rank
separation alone does not force masked cross-band decay; the actual
bent-Walsh spike/tail example checked separately already defeats
that deterministic shortcut. Independent signed-mask cancellation
cannot be inferred from the positive Poisson domination.

9. Scope and sources
---------------------
The operation preserves one positive scalar payoff multiplicatively,
the actual shared state, original conclusive labels, and rank-one
conclusive effects. It does not preserve the entire fine instrument,
postselected law, or discarded quantum workspace. Its abort is private
and unscored in the comparison. It is not a uniform low-event-budget
acquisition theorem, a quantum advantage claim, or an efficient repair.
No general-rank coarse POVM, arbitrary mixed-state purification, or
unrestricted commuting/type-III extension is included. In particular,
rank-one refinement with duplicate coarse labels cannot turn this
into an independent-edge coarse-game result.
