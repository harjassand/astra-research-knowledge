EXACT UNIFORM GRAPH SAMPLING BY MOMENT-MATCHED ROW TAILS
Status: FROZEN, internally checked explicit WEAKER-TASK COMPLETION.
Retired as a substantive new-discovery claim after the primary comparison.
This is a weaker-order positive construction, not a resolution of Mao's
superlogarithmic explicit-sampler problem. Historical priority UNKNOWN.

1. Literal result and governing comparison

There is one uniform exact expected-polynomial-time sampler for graph laws
P_n on n labeled vertices with these properties:

 (i) P_n is invariant under every vertex permutation.
 (ii) Every set of at most k_n edges is exactly independent and fair, with
      k_n=Theta(log n/loglog n) -> infinity.
 (iii) After independent refresh-to-fair noise on each edge at any fixed
       rate 0<=epsilon<=1/2, maximum degree strongly distinguishes from
       G(n,1/2) in deterministic polynomial time.

The degree k_n=o(log n) is essential to the claim's scope. This does NOT
give agreement through log^(1+c)n, does NOT refute a superlogarithmic
low-degree conjecture, and does NOT solve the uniform-sampling question for
Mao's advised Reed--Muller graph law or its superlogarithmic alternative.
The construction uses conventional finite moment matching and binomial
concentration; the governing primary comparison is retained in Section 8.

It is outside a single invariant affine coset: its row laws modify the
probabilities of different Hamming weights, and retain full support. It does
not use iid finite-field latent evaluations, inverse prefixes, conditioning
on good lists, uncharged advice, or an approximate TV sampler.

2. A rational exchangeable row law

All logarithms in integer definitions below are base 2. Put

 ell=ceil(log_2 n), L=ceil(log_2 ell),
 k=max(1,floor[ell/(64 L)]), m=floor(n/2), l=n-m,
 R=ceil(sqrt(n ell)), c=floor(m/2), x=c+8R.

For the finitely many small instances with ell<2, L=0, x>m, or one of the
distinct-node conditions below failing, define P_n=G(n,1/2) instead. These
conditions all succeed for sufficiently large n. Their checks use integers.

Set u=floor(sqrt(m)), h=floor[u/(2(k+1))], and assume h>=1 and 1<=k<=m.
Define k+1 distinct central weights

                  w_j=c+(j-floor(k/2))h,  j=0,...,k.

Require 0<=w_0<...<w_k<x<=m, using the same null fallback otherwise. Let

 B(w)=2^(-m) binom(m,w), 0<=w<=m,
 lambda_j=product_{r!=j}(x-w_r)/(w_j-w_r).

For every polynomial f of degree at most k, Lagrange interpolation gives

                         f(x)=sum_j lambda_j f(w_j).               (1)

In particular sum_j lambda_j=1, so there is a positive lambda_j. Choose

              p=(1/2) min_{j:lambda_j>0} B(w_j)/lambda_j.           (2)

Define a nonnegative subprobability row-weight law

               Q(w)=B(w)-p sum_j lambda_j 1{w=w_j},
               B'(w)=Q(w)+p 1{w=x}.                              (3)

Indeed, at positive-lambda nodes Q(w_j)>=B(w_j)/2; at negative-lambda
nodes Q(w_j)>=B(w_j); all other entries are unchanged. Its mass is 1-p.
Also p<=1/2: sum_{lambda>0}lambda>=1 and
p sum_{lambda>0}lambda <= (1/2)sum_{lambda>0}B(w_j)<=1/2.
Thus B' is a probability law with full support.

To sample an m-bit row, first choose a tag I~Bernoulli(p). If I=1 use W=x;
otherwise use Q/(1-p). Conditional on W, choose uniformly an m-bit string
of Hamming weight W. Every ingredient is an exact rational/integer operation.

Equation (1) proves that B' and B have exactly the same moments through
degree k. For any t<=k distinct coordinates of the row,

 P(all selected bits are 1)=E[(W)_t]/(m)_t=2^(-t).

Inclusion--exclusion then gives every bit pattern probability 2^(-t).
The row is therefore exactly k-wise independent and fair.

3. The planted atom occurs often enough

The following elementary central-binomial estimate is sufficient: for some
absolute c_0>0 and all sufficiently large m,

 B(w)>=c_0/sqrt(m) whenever |w-m/2|<=sqrt(m)/4+1.                  (4)

For completeness, the central coefficient is Omega(m^(-1/2)) by Wallis or
the elementary Stirling bounds. For an offset s=O(sqrt(m)), the ratio to
the central coefficient is a product of s factors (m/2-j)/(m/2+j+1),
whose logarithm is bounded below by a constant when s<=sqrt(m)/4+1.
Our w_j all satisfy this range.

For sufficiently large n, h>=sqrt(m)/(8(k+1)), R<=2sqrt(n ell), and
n<=3m. Dropping the extra factorial denominators in lambda_j gives the
safe bound, with an absolute constant C,

              |lambda_j| <= [C(k+1)sqrt(ell)]^k.                  (5)

Here all k numerator factors are at most x-w_0<=9R; each of the k
denominator factors has magnitude at least h. For large ell, k+1<=ell,
and log_2 C <= (1/2)log_2 ell. Hence

 log_2 max_j |lambda_j| <= 2k log_2 ell <= ell/32.                 (6)

From (2), (4), and (6),

 p >= [c_0/(2sqrt(m))] 2^(-ell/32) >= n^(-3/4)                  (7)

for all sufficiently large n. The last inequality absorbs an absolute
constant and has ample exponent slack. The precise leading exponent is
not needed. In particular l independent tags contain at least one 1 with
probability at least 1-exp[-n^(1/4)/2].

Using k=Theta(loglog n) instead would give p=n^(-1/2-o(1)); that stronger
atom estimate is NOT asserted for the k in Section 2.

4. Uniform exact graph generation

Initially use a fixed bipartition with l left and m right vertices.
Independently for each left vertex, sample its cross-part row by Section 2.
All within-left and within-right edges are independent fair bits, independent
of the cross-part rows. Finally apply an independent uniform permutation of
the n vertices. This defines the SINGLE fixed target law P_n.

Before permutation, any set of <=k edges groups into independent row sets
and within-part fair edges. Each row group has size <=k, so Section 2 makes
the entire chosen set exactly uniform. A vertex permutation maps a fixed
edge set to another set of the same size; averaging preserves the exact law.
The final permutation also makes P_n vertex-relabel invariant.

This proof gives exact k-wise edge uniformity, not merely small Walsh bias
or closeness in total variation. Independent refresh-to-fair noise preserves
these exact marginals. Full support follows from Section 2 and the fair
within-part edges.

5. A strong detector after fixed positive noise

Let each edge independently be kept with probability beta=1-epsilon>=1/2,
and otherwise refreshed to an independent fair bit. Declare planted iff

                 max_v degree(v) >= (n-1)/2+2R.                    (8)

The half-integer threshold is exactly compared via 2degree(v)-(n-1)>=4R;
no floating-point square root or fitted threshold is used.

Under G(n,1/2), each degree is Bin(n-1,1/2). Hoeffding and a union bound give

             P_null(planted) <= n exp[-8R^2/(n-1)]
                              <= n exp[-8ell] -> 0.                (9)

For a left row tagged I=1, fix its actual original weight-x pattern. After
noise its cross-part bits are independent, with means 1-epsilon/2 or
epsilon/2 according to that pattern. Its within-left bits are independent
fair bits, also independent of the row and noise. Hence its entire degree
is a sum of n-1 independent bounded Bernoulli variables under this
conditioning, of mean

 (n-1)/2+beta(x-m/2) >= (n-1)/2+4R-1/4.

Its chance of falling below the threshold (8) is at most

                    exp[-2R^2/(n-1)] <= exp[-2ell]                (10)

for all sufficiently large n (in fact the mean gap is nearly 2R).
The independent tags give, without asserting independence of all degrees,

 P_planted(null) <= (1-p)^l + sum_left P(I_i=1, degree_i below threshold)
                 <= exp[-n^(1/4)/2]+n exp[-2ell] -> 0.             (11)

The detector is permutation invariant, so the hidden bipartition need not
be recovered or supplied. Equations (9)--(11) prove STRONG distinguishability.
No divergence of a low-degree L2 norm is being substituted for this test.

6. Exact access, time and bit costs

The integer logarithms and square roots are computed by integer comparison.
Each lambda_j has numerator/denominator bit length O(k log m). Binomial
weights have O(m) bits, and p has O(m+k log m) bits. The denominators of
all lambda_j divide h^k k! before reduction. Thus a common denominator for
the m+1 probabilities Q(w) has polynomial bit length, as do prefix sums.
The table is computed once and reused for all left rows.

Exact sampling from a rational finite law uses a uniformly sampled integer
up to its common denominator; rejection from the next power of two takes
at most two attempts on average. A uniform weight-W bit row is sampled by
exact binomial unranking or sequential hypergeometric decisions. Uniform
vertex permutation is standard exact integer sampling. These procedures,
their O(n^2) output bits, and rational arithmetic have expected polynomial
time in n and polynomial intermediate bit lengths. No real-valued sampling
oracle or hidden precision promise is used.

7. What this construction does and does not change

For every fixed d, all d-edge marginals are eventually exactly ER, while a
polynomial-time maximum-degree test succeeds even after 1/2 refresh. Thus it
answers the WEAKER exact uniform-generation task with agreement beyond each
fixed degree. It deliberately plants a rare row-tail event and amplifies it
over linearly many vertices. This is not Mao's superlogarithmic regime:
k_n=o(log n), and the detector's tail threshold is consistent with that gap.

The construction does not recover the old advised P_n, and does not use its
rank detector. It must be compared against conventional limited-independence,
moment-problem and heavy-tail constructions before assigning novelty. No
implementation fixture, physics output, new hardness claim or public release
has occurred. Root and three bounded independent readbacks passed the exact
moment/positivity, rational-cost, symmetry and strong-detector arguments.

Their Section 2 formulates limited-independence tail maximization by a
finite moment problem and permutation symmetrization; their Theorem 3
establishes the logarithmic independence scale for poor concentration.
The moment-matching/heavy-tail phenomenon here is therefore established
prior. A polynomial-size rational weight-law LP is a standard consequence
of symmetrization, rather than a newly supplied oracle. This note instead
gives an explicit Lagrange weight correction and checks its hidden-partition
graph/noise assembly. It does not claim that the cited paper states that
literal assembly. Direct priority of the assembly remains UNKNOWN.

No quantitative probability in Sections 2--5 is imported from a tail
existence theorem: the present weaker construction is proved directly.
Spreading nodes over sqrt(mk) and retaining factorial denominators can
remove the crude loglog loss toward k=c log n. That is not pursued here;
it stays within the governing limited-independence scale and does not reach
the superlogarithmic task.
