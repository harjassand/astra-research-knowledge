PRIVATE ASTRA P1 — finite positive physical state for binary gain/loss records
Independent quantitative audit: measurement_reduction_predecessors
Date: 2026-10-24
Scope: photon-added thermal cone; first-ANY-registered-event OR bins;
piecewise-constant supplied rates for the explicit quadrature bound.
No novelty claim or uncharged bit-complexity claim.

SUMMARY

Let sigma_(k,theta) have photon probabilities
  p_n=binom(n,k)theta^(k+1)(1-theta)^(n-k), n>=k,
  0<theta<=1, k=0,1,... .
The root-derived physical instrument identities below imply that m binary
clicked bins raise the maximal cone index by at most m. This audit supplies:

 (i) a boundary-safe PHYSICAL trace metric: with theta=sech(u)^2, u>=0,
       D_tr(sigma_(k,u),sigma_(k,v))<=sqrt(k+1)|u-v|;
 (ii) a gain-exposure bound on the finite u-grid range;
 (iii) positive first-event quadrature with a node bound depending on cone
       degree, number of supplied control pieces, u-span and accuracy, not
       on the possibly very large raw photon count;
 (iv) the exact places where scalar enclosure/root-isolation precision and
       stochastic-row arithmetic still have to be paid.

The state metric is proved directly on photon probabilities. A bound on the
Husimi density alone would not imply this physical trace bound.

1. GOVERNING PHYSICAL IDENTITIES AND NOTATION

Use total incoherent gain b>=0 and loss d>=0, registered rates
  0<=kappa_plus<=b,  0<=kappa_minus<=d.
All physical rates are specified by the accessible controller, not by unread
flag, cone index, rate-mixture label, or microscopic number. The physical
number rates are b(n+1) upward and dn downward. A bin exposes one bit: was
there any registered gain OR registered loss event? Registered type and event
times are not exposed. After the first registered event the bin flag stays1,
and all subsequent physical dynamics within that bin are unconditioned.

The positive family has generating function
  F_k(z)=theta^(k+1) z^k/[1-(1-theta)z]^(k+1).
Direct coefficient comparison gives the registered jump identities
  J_plus sigma_k = kappa_plus (k+1)/theta sigma_(k+1),
  J_minus sigma_k = kappa_minus[
       k theta sigma_(k-1)+(2k+1)(1-theta)sigma_k
       +(k+1)(1-theta)^2/theta sigma_(k+1)].               (1.1)
The k=0 downward term vanishes. The traces agree with gain rate
kappa_plus E(N+1) and loss rate kappa_minus E N, respectively.

Write
  c0=d-kappa_minus,
  g0=b-d+2kappa_minus,
  v=kappa_plus+kappa_minus.
The unnormalized no-registered-event evolution is positive index death plus
killing, sharing the deterministic rate coordinate
  theta_dot=v-g0 theta-c0 theta^2,
  qdot_k=c0 theta[(k+1)q_(k+1)-kq_k]
            -[v(k+1)/theta-kappa_minus]q_k.               (1.2)
The killing rate is nonnegative for theta<=1. At theta=1 the rate derivative
is kappa_plus-b<=0; theta remains in (0,1] for finite time.

For verification, the no-count photon generating-function operator is
  [(b-kappa_plus)z^2-(b+d)z+(d-kappa_minus)]partial_z
      +[(b-kappa_plus)z-b].
Substitution of F_k gives (1.2). This independently checks the root interface
on this diagonal physical cone. General non-diagonal preparation is outside
this particular statement.

Full unobserved evolution is obtained by setting both registered rates to0:
  theta_dot=-(b-d)theta-dtheta^2,
  index death rate from k to k-1 is dtheta k.             (1.3)
In particular, full evolution never increases k.

The exact click branch is the positive first-event integral
  K1(0,T)=integral_0^T K0(0,s) (J_plus+J_minus)(s) K(s,T)ds, (1.4)
with forward action on states. It follows from conditioning on the first
registered event and does not approximate the number of subsequent events.
Equations (1.1)-(1.4) imply k<=K0+m after m clicked bits from initial cone
degree <=K0. The total hidden number of events is absent from this bound.

2. EXACT PHYSICAL METRIC IN A HYPERBOLIC RATE COORDINATE

Define
  theta=sech(u)^2,  u=atanh(sqrt(1-theta))>=0.
For fixed k, summing the negative-binomial series gives the exact classical
Hellinger affinity (also the fidelity affinity of the diagonal states)
  sum_n sqrt(p_n(u)p_n(w))=sech(u-w)^(k+1).                (2.1)
Indeed the numerator of the series is
(sqrt(theta(u)theta(w)))^(k+1), and the denominator is
[1-sqrt((1-theta(u))(1-theta(w)))]^(k+1); their ratio is
sech(u-w)^(k+1).

The standard Cauchy-Schwarz factorization of |p-q| then gives
  D_tr(sigma_(k,u),sigma_(k,w))
    <=sqrt(1-sech(u-w)^(2(k+1)))
    <=sqrt(k+1)|u-w|.                                    (2.2)
For the last step use 1-(1-tanh^2 z)^(k+1)<=(k+1)tanh^2 z.
This includes u=0, theta=1, the pure number state, by continuity.

Equivalently, at u>0 the negative-binomial Fisher information in u is exactly
4(k+1); the direct affinity identity avoids a boundary regularity assumption.
For a positive mixture with k<=D, rounding each u by at most delta while
retaining its positive weight changes the physical state by at most
  sqrt(D+1)delta.                                        (2.3)
A grid of width h rounded to nearest points has delta<=h/2. This is a strong
state bound and contracts under any common subsequent physical instrument.

3. FINITE GRID RANGE FROM THE SUPPLIED GAIN BUDGET

For full dynamics and 0<theta<=1,
  theta_dot=-btheta+dtheta(1-theta)>=-btheta.
The difference between the no-count and full rate fields is
  Delta(theta)=kappa_plus+kappa_minus(1-theta)^2>=0.       (3.1)
Thus every rate appearing in (1.4), including after a first event, obeys the
same lower bound theta(t)>=theta_initial exp(-integral b).

If initially u<=U0 and total supplied gain exposure is G=integral b, then
  u<=arcosh(exp(G/2)cosh(U0)).                            (3.2)
An upward rounding of u by at most delta multiplies cosh(u) by at most
exp(delta). Consequently a safe global range after several projections is
  Umax=arcosh(exp(G/2+sum delta_j)cosh(U0))
       <=U0+G/2+sum delta_j+log2.                        (3.3)
The observation policy must supply a pathwise gain bound if one uniform
preallocated grid is required. Alternatively the accessible controller can
maintain the corresponding branchwise envelope. The state includes that
controller. The width depends on logarithmic excitation scale and gain
exposure, not linearly on mean photon number.

4. EXACT SCALAR FUNCTIONS FOR A FIRST-EVENT ROW

Fix one initial component sigma_(j,theta_initial), a bin of length T, and its
supplied piecewise-constant rates. Conditional on no registered event through
s, the index is Binomial(j,p0(s)), with common theta0(s). Initially p0=1, and
  theta0_dot=v-g0 theta0-c0 theta0^2,
  p0_dot=-p0[c0 theta0+(v/theta0)(1-p0)],
  S_j_dot=-[v(1+jp0)/theta0-kappa_minus]S_j,   S_j(0)=1.  (4.1)
Here S_j is the exact no-event probability. It decreases and remains positive
at finite supplied coefficients and theta_initial>0.

These functions are explicit through a positive 2x2 fundamental matrix:
  Mdot=[[-g0/2,v],[c0,g0/2]]M, M(0)=I,
  M=[[A,B],[C,D]], det M=1,
  P=A theta_initial+B, Q=C theta_initial+D,
  theta0=P/Q, p0=1/(A Q),
  S_j=exp(integral[-g0/2+kappa_minus])
             theta_initial^(j+1) A^j/P^(j+1).            (4.2)
Products of constant-segment matrix exponentials give the time-varying
piecewise-constant case. Thus first-event interval masses
  Pr(first event in [s0,s1])=S_j(s0)-S_j(s1)              (4.3)
are explicit known-model quantities, not supplied unknown mixture weights.
Validated evaluation and positive subtraction are still numerical tasks.

Let theta_f(s) be the full rate evolution from theta0(s) at time s to T, and
let p1(s) be the index-survival probability under that full evolution. Full
evolution thins index ell to Binomial(ell,p1). By (3.1) and monotonicity of a
scalar flow,
  d theta_f(s)/ds = partial_theta Phi_full(s,T;theta0(s))
                         Delta(theta0(s)) >=0.           (4.4)
Therefore u_f(s)=atanh sqrt(1-theta_f(s)) is nonincreasing. Its total variation
is exactly the finite endpoint span Delta_u=u_f(0)-u_f(T).

5. FIVE POSITIVE CONDITIONAL SHAPE COMPONENTS

On an interval where v>0, put
  eta=kappa_minus/v, theta=theta0(s), p=p0(s),
  H=1-eta theta+jp, q=p p1(s).
Define the five nonnegative raw weights
  a0=eta theta(1-theta),
  a1=1-eta+eta(1-theta)^2,
  a2=jp eta theta^2,
  a3=2jp eta theta(1-theta),
  a4=jp[1-eta+eta(1-theta)^2].                            (5.1)
Their sum is H. At a possible first-event time with nonzero hazard, normalize
wi=ai/H. If j=0, a2=a3=a4=0, and their components are omitted.

Before the remaining full propagation, the jump-index generating polynomial
is
  [a0 A(z)^j+a1 zA(z)^j+a2 A(z)^(j-1)
                 +a3 zA(z)^(j-1)+a4 z^2 A(z)^(j-1)]/H,
  A(z)=1-p+pz.
This follows by applying (1.1) to Binomial(j,p) and using
ell Bin(j,ell;p)=jp Bin(j-1,ell-1;p). It includes both registered marks.

After full propagation, the five conditional final index laws are respectively
  Bin(j,q),
  Bin(j,q)+Bern(p1),
  Bin(j-1,q),
  Bin(j-1,q)+Bern(p1),
  Bin(j-1,q)+Bin(2,p1),                                 (5.2)
where the displayed summands are independent. They all share theta_f(s), and
the maximal index is j+1. Let F_s be this normalized PHYSICAL state mixture.
The exact unnormalized click output is
  K1 sigma_j = integral F_s [-dS_j(s)].                   (5.3)

If H=0, the first-event hazard is zero and its interval carries no mass.
At an isolated boundary zero, formulas can be replaced by their one-sided
limits or the quadrature representative can be chosen inside the cell.
For physical constant controls, an interior zero H requires the event-free
vacuum case; it does not conceal positive first-event mass.

6. A STATE-VARIATION BOUND

Coupling binomial Bernoulli trials and using (2.2) gives, within one constant
control segment,
  D_tr(F_s,F_t)
   <=(1/2)sum_(i=0)^4 |wi(s)-wi(t)|
       +j|q(s)-q(t)|+2|p1(s)-p1(t)|
       +sqrt(j+2)|u_f(s)-u_f(t)|.                        (6.1)
The first term compares the five mixing probabilities. The next two compare
the binomial variables in (5.2). The last compares the physical states with
the same final index. In particular, the bound does not compare Husimi
densities and does not assume small photon excitation.

The following algebra bounds variation without a hazard-based time mesh.
Suppose a segment has constant coefficients, v>0, and length L. Write local
time t from the segment start and
  omega=sqrt(g0^2+4c0v)>0, xi=exp(omega t).
The strict inequality follows from the physical rate constraints when v>0.
The no-count rate is a Mobius function theta=P(xi)/Q(xi), with linear
polynomials P,Q. If p_* and theta_* denote values at segment entry, the
conditional index parameter has form
  p0=K xi/[Q(xi)R(xi)],                                  (6.2)
where R is linear and K>=0. For example, the segment matrix in (4.2), whose
entries are xi^(-1/2) times linear polynomials, gives
  p0=p_*theta_*/[(Ctheta_*+D)(Atheta_*+(1-p_*)B)].

Substitution in (5.1) shows that every normalized wi is a rational function
in xi with numerator and denominator degree at most4. Denominators are
positive where first-event mass is possible. Its derivative has polynomial
numerator degree at most7, unless identically zero. Consequently wi has at
most eight monotone pieces and, since 0<=wi<=1,
  Var_segment(wi)<=8.                                   (6.3)
This bound includes removable endpoint limits and vanishing components.

For the remaining full propagation write
  p1(s)=1/[1+theta0(s)V(s)].
On the same constant segment, V solves Vdot=(b-d)V-d, with its terminal value
supplied by the subsequent full evolution. If b-d is nonzero, V is a constant
plus one real exponential; if b-d=0, V is affine in t. Hence p1 is a ratio of
at most two exponential terms over at most four terms. Its derivative has
at most eight exponential terms before combining coincident exponents.
The product q=p0p1 has the cancellation
  q=K xi/[R(xi)(Q(xi)+P(xi)V(s))],                        (6.4)
whose denominator has at most six real exponential terms. Therefore the
derivative numerator has at most six terms after factoring a positive
exponential. It follows that
  Var_segment(p1)<=8,   Var_segment(q)<=8.                (6.5)
The latter bound is deliberately loose (six suffices).

Justification of the zero count: a nonzero sum of D distinct real
exponentials has at most D-1 zeros on an interval, counting multiplicity.
Divide by one exponential, differentiate, and apply Rolle's theorem
inductively. The confluent version sum P_i(t)exp(lambda_i t) has at most
sum_i(deg P_i+1)-1 zeros by the same argument. When b-d=0, the derivative
numerators above have at most three exponent groups with polynomial degree
at most1, so their dimension is at most6; the stated eight-piece bound still
holds. Coincident exponents and cancellations only reduce the dimension.

For S constant-control pieces within the bin, summing (6.1)-(6.5), and treating
the pieces separately at control discontinuities, yields the conservative
within-piece variation budget
  V_total <= (8j+36)S + sqrt(j+2)Delta_u.                 (6.6)
Pieces with v=0 have zero first-event mass and can be omitted from quadrature.
The endpoint u-span is global because u_f is monotone. A piece boundary need
not be crossed by a quadrature cell.

7. POSITIVE QUADRATURE WITH COMPUTED INTERVAL MASSES

Partition each constant-control piece so that the cumulative right-hand
variation in (6.1) over each subinterval is at most epsilon_quad. This can be
done using the finite monotonicity decomposition just proved, followed by
monotone scalar subdivision. Choose any representative s_i in each cell and
assign the exact nonnegative weight
  m_i=S_j(left_i)-S_j(right_i).
Then
  Q_click=sum_i m_i F_(s_i)
is positive, has exactly the true click probability, and obeys
  (1/2)||K1 sigma_j-Q_click||_1
      <=[1-S_j(T)]epsilon_quad<=epsilon_quad.             (7.1)
The required number of representatives is at most
  S+V_total/epsilon_quad,                                (7.2)
up to the harmless integer ceiling on each piece. This is independent of a
possibly very narrow first-event-time distribution at high excitation.
The exact interval probability in (4.3), rather than a rectangle approximation
to the event-time density, is what removes that stiffness from the node bound.

The no-click branch is already the explicit finite positive Binomial(j,p0(T))
mixture at theta0(T), weighted by S_j(T). Include it without quadrature.
Round every output rate to the physical u-grid by at most delta. The complete
binary-row physical error is then at most
  epsilon_quad+sqrt(j+2)delta,                            (7.3)
before certified arithmetic error. No-click and all click weights sum to1.

This is a constructive node bound, not an assertion that root isolation or
elementary evaluation has unit bit cost. Constant-degree rational critical
points, bounded-term exponential-polynomial critical points, positive 2x2
flows, monotone inversions, and the survival values must be enclosed. Nearly
multiple roots or tiny survival differences can require extra precision.
They can be handled by interval enclosures and charging unresolved parameter
variation; a full uniform bit-complexity theorem has not been proved here.

8. FINITE POSITIVE FILTER AND COMPLETE RECORD ERROR

Let B be the maximum number of binary bins, and D=K0+B. Use a grid on
0<=u<=Umax with maximum rounding distance delta. The stored positive state is
a probability vector over
  (k,u_grid), 0<=k<=D,
plus the supplied controller state. It embeds as an actual normalized
physical density sum w_(k,i)sigma_(k,sech(u_i)^2).

For each currently supported row (k,u_i), construct the positive binary
transition row of Section7 and aggregate all rounded output rates into this
same grid. Binomial probabilities in the five components require O(k)
arithmetic per first-event representative, since only convolution with zero,
one, or two Bernoulli variables is needed. There is no number-state cutoff.

If all rows of bin l have certified embedded error epsilon_l, physical CP
instrument contraction and composition on unnormalized record branches give
  TV(P_complete_record,Q_complete_record)
       <=D_initial+sum_(l=1)^B epsilon_l.                 (8.1)
The compared physical experiment uses the SAME implemented controller as a
function of the exposed record. It does not change action rules when replacing
an approximate predictor by an ideal one. This proof has no rare-normalization
denominator; it bounds the whole record, not every rare conditional posterior.
If an actually supplied bit has zero computed probability, the implemented
controller must specify a positive fallback state and action. Its branch
probability is charged in the unnormalized comparison; it is not silently
divided by zero.

For example choose epsilon_quad=epsilon/(3B),
delta=epsilon/[3B sqrt(D+1)], and row arithmetic allowance epsilon/(3B).
Then (7.3)-(8.1) give total error <=D_initial+epsilon. The number of stored
scalars is O((D+1)(1+Umax/delta)), polynomial in the observation budget,
initial cone degree, gain exposure/log excitation range, and inverse error.
The node-count bound (7.2) is likewise polynomial in these parameters and
the supplied control-piece count. Initial arbitrary rate mixtures require
their own acquired/known positive weights and initial projection allowance.

9. ARITHMETIC AND SCOPE BOUNDARIES

Every interval mass in the row algorithm is determined by (4.2)-(4.3). It is
not provided by assuming knowledge of an inaccessible posterior. Certified
row rounding can preserve nonnegative weights and total mass; its classical
row-TV error adds to (7.3). Errors in scalar component parameters can be
charged by (2.2) and (6.1). Actual kinetic calibration, preparation and physical
action errors require their own physical instrument allowance.

The stronger relative-word theorem for a CIR signal with classical Cox loss
observations is separate. Rate projection with exact mixture weights there
can have a multiplicative word certificate. The physical both-mark row
quadrature above supplies an ADDITIVE complete-record certificate. Approximated
shape weights do not automatically retain a relative guarantee on arbitrarily
rare future words. Near a pure number-state boundary, such a transfer can fail.

No scalar Husimi label is assumed to be a measured microscopic state. The
embedding and positive instrument identities (1.1)-(1.4) are the actual
interfaces used here. General quantum controls, spatial interactions, or
different observation logic need a compatible instrument theorem. The present
result is a complete scoped route to a finite positive physical predictor for
the specified single-mode binary instrument, with explicit numerical tasks
and remaining precision costs.
