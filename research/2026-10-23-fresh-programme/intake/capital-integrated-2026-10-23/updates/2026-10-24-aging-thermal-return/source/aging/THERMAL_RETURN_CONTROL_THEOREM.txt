THERMAL RETURN, VISIBLE RELAXATION MODES, AND CONTROL COMPLEXITY
23--24 October 2026. Lead derivation and root extension after independent openings.

Status and purpose

This is a structural consequence of an established distributed linear relaxation
model, with a constructive finite-mode control. It is not a newly identified
glass mechanism, a new Chebyshev theorem, or a claim that real metallic glasses
have an acquired continuous spectrum. It explains why one common material time
does not imply that one scalar mean or one returned temperature certifies a
returned microscopic displacement field. Failure to refocus is compatible with
ordinary passive relaxation, without a separate irreversible-event process.

The sign-change proof specializes classical Chebyshev-system orthogonality.
Musin, Proc. Steklov Inst. Math. 221 (1998), 236--246, Theorem 1, available as
arXiv:0903.1908v2, Section 2, explicitly gives that general result and its
sign-matched test-function proof. Finite diagonal-system controllability and
moment inversion are also conventional control ideas. Priority of this exact
thermal-return packaging is not established and no novelty claim is made.

1. The model and the observation gate

Let xi be a common, strictly increasing material time. Hold pressure/stress and
all other independent controls fixed. Let theta(xi) be the scalar temperature
deviation, or a calibrated scalar thermal forcing, relative to a fixed reference.
For visible rates r>0 suppose the normalized structural modes obey

  d x_r/d xi = -r [x_r-theta(xi)],       x_r(0)=0.               (1)

A nonzero rate-specific thermal coupling b_r can be included by replacing the
physical mode by b_r x_r; the conclusions concern modes with b_r nonzero. All
coefficients and the common-clock law are frozen over the tested small-amplitude
regime. A change of rate ratios, spatial temperature gradients, nonlinear mode
mixing, or an additional independent mechanical input is outside this model.

Here x_r(0)=0 is an equilibrated reference, or a control-induced difference from
a specified reference solution at the same material time. It is not an arbitrary
aging initial state silently declared equilibrated. If initial modes are unknown,
their decayed contribution must be separately bounded or included in a forward
prediction. Nor does comparing solutions at equal xi automatically give a
laboratory-time observation or a coherent-scattering factorization.

At a finite material duration 0<L<infinity with theta integrable,

  x_r(L)=r exp(-r L) int_0^L exp(r s) theta(s) ds.              (2)

For finite modes a mean proxy may be y=B x while an echo-relevant displacement
field is u=A x. Full field return implies all visible mode returns only if the
relevant mode map is injective on the reachable subspace (or an equivalent
observability condition has independently been established). More generally,
mean matching certifies field matching only if ker B is contained in ker A.
These maps are supplied model assumptions until calibrated. FSDP position or
dilatometric volume alone does not acquire them. A scattering echo can also be
invariant under motions that do not return the field; no converse is asserted.

Equation (1) can be passive: at fixed theta, each independent spring-like energy
E_r=k_r(x_r-theta)^2/2 with k_r>0 has dE_r/dxi=-k_r r(x_r-theta)^2.
This is a conditional realization. Cross-response weights in a measured volume
or dielectric signal need not define these positive energies.

2. Continuous visible spectrum: exact full return forces zero forcing

Theorem A. Suppose x_r(L)=0 for every r in a set S of distinct positive rates
having a finite accumulation point. Then theta=0 almost everywhere on [0,L].

Proof. The compact-interval transform

  H(z)=int_0^L exp(z s) theta(s) ds

is entire: derivatives of every order can be taken under the integral, uniformly
on compact complex z sets. Equation (2) gives H(r)=0 for r in S. The identity
theorem gives H identically zero. Hence int s^k theta(s) ds=0 for every k>=0.
Polynomial density in C[0,L] implies the finite signed measure theta(s)ds is zero,
so theta=0 almost everywhere. Equivalently use compact-support transform
uniqueness. The accumulation point may be zero; every sampled rate itself must
be positive. This proves the assertion.

If modes are indexed by a measure mu and return holds mu-almost everywhere,
continuity in r makes the transform vanish on the support of mu. Thus the same
conclusion holds when that support has a finite accumulation point. In particular
an interval of visible rates suffices. Infinite support with rates tending only
to infinity is NOT covered; completeness of its moment family is a separate
question. Neither approximate return at finitely many modes nor finite noisy
response data establish the hypotheses of this theorem.

The conclusion is specific to the initial/final reference in Section 1, finite L,
ordinary integrable scalar forcing, and (1). It does not exclude asymptotic
relaxation, independently controlled spatial/mode inputs, or a smaller observable
returning while hidden modes do not. History dependence of a positive common
clock does not invalidate the proof once the actual theta(xi) and finite L have
been specified. Acquiring that clock remains part of an experiment.

3. Finite visible rank: at least N forcing sign changes

Theorem B. Let r_1,...,r_N be distinct positive rates. If a nonzero integrable
theta returns all N modes in (1), theta cannot have fewer than N sign changes.
For piecewise-continuous forcing, count alternations between nonzero signs,
ignoring zero intervals. More generally, fewer than N sign changes means a
partition into at most N intervals on which theta has alternating weak signs
almost everywhere. The result uses that sign-partition definition.

Lemma. A nonzero real linear combination of m exponentials with distinct real
exponents has at most m-1 zeros, counting multiplicities, on a finite interval.
Proof by induction: divide by the lowest exponential. If the result has m zeros,
Rolle's theorem gives at least m-1 zeros of its derivative, which has at most
m-1 exponential terms and hence at most m-2 zeros by induction. A nonzero
constant is the trivial case. Terms with zero coefficients only improve the
bound. The exponentials are linearly independent by the same argument.

Proof of Theorem B. If theta has k<N sign changes at c_1<...<c_k, use any k+1
of the rates. A homogeneous k-by-(k+1) interpolation system supplies a nonzero
g(s)=sum_i a_i exp(r_i s) vanishing at all c_j. The lemma makes these exactly
its k zeros, all simple. Choose its overall sign so g and theta have the same
weak sign on all sign intervals. Then int theta*g>0: the product is nonnegative
and is positive on a set of positive measure. But (2) and return of the selected
modes give int theta*g=sum_i a_i int exp(r_i s)theta(s)ds=0. Contradiction.
Roots may be placed anywhere in an intervening zero interval. No continuity of
theta beyond its integrability/sign partition is needed for this proof.

In particular, N rectangular pulses or fewer have at most N-1 sign changes and
cannot return N distinct modes unless the entire forcing is zero. These are
sign changes of temperature deviation about the reference, not a count of
nonmonotone steps that remain on the same side of the reference.

4. A sharp N+1-pulse construction and its costs

Take N+1 equal MATERIAL-time widths delta>0 and L=(N+1)delta. Write
q_i=exp(-r_i delta) in (0,1). Let the pulse amplitudes be a_0,...,a_N. The exact
recursion is x_{i,k+1}=q_i x_{i,k}+(1-q_i)a_k, so

  x_i(L)=(1-q_i) P(q_i),
  P(q)=sum_(k=0)^N a_k q^(N-k).                              (3)

Choose

  P(q)=a_0 product_(i=1)^N (q-q_i),
  a_k=a_0 (-1)^k e_k(q_1,...,q_N),                          (4)

where e_k is the kth elementary symmetric polynomial and e_0=1. All e_k are
strictly positive. Thus all N modes return, every amplitude is nonzero, and the
forcing has exactly N sign changes. Scaling a_0 makes the peak pulse fit any
specified positive small-signal range. An additional visible rate q_* unequal
to every q_i has residual (1-q_*)a_0 product_i(q_*-q_i), generally nonzero.

The construction charges its control. If the protocol starts and ends at zero,

  sum_k |a_k|=|a_0| product_i(1+q_i),
  total variation(theta)=2 |a_0| product_i(1+q_i).             (5)

If the first pulse a_0 is fixed, later peak amplitudes can grow greatly. In the
slow-mode limit q_i near one, the coefficient shape approaches binomial(N,k),
with peak near 2^N/sqrt(pi N/2). To keep all amplitudes at most Theta, the first
pulse must satisfy |a_0|<=Theta/max_k e_k. Also max_k e_k>=product_i(1+q_i)/(N+1).
These dynamic-range and variation statements are for this equal-width minimal
pulse construction; they are NOT proved universal over arbitrary pulse timings
or other controls. The universal result established here is the sign-change
bound. Finite ramps, accessible temperature bounds, thermal equilibration,
clock estimation, and amplitude precision are additional physical costs.

Known material-time widths are an important requirement. Equal laboratory-time
pulses need not have equal material width when the clock changes with temperature
or history. Applying (4) to those pulses without clock acquisition is invalid.

Finite-mode exact return is not solely an artifact of discontinuous steps.
For known finite rates choose any nonzero smooth compactly supported phi inside
(0,L), and set theta=product_i(d/ds+r_i) phi. Integration by parts, with all
boundary terms zero, gives int exp(r_j s)theta(s)ds=0 for every selected r_j.
The resulting theta is not identically zero (a nonzero compactly supported
solution of that homogeneous constant-coefficient ODE cannot exist), is smooth,
and can be scaled to any positive amplitude/slew bounds. Its derivative sizes,
shape precision and sign changes remain charged; this existence argument gives
no practical calibration or universal efficiency claim.

5. Finite error and a protocol-specific witness

For a protocol with k<N sign changes construct the sign-matched g above. The
known forcing gives the strictly positive number

  D_g=int_0^L |theta(s)g(s)| ds.

Equation (2) implies

  D_g <= sum_i |a_i| exp(r_i L) |x_i(L)|/r_i.                 (6)

Consequently at least one selected |x_i(L)| is >=
D_g/[sum_i |a_i|exp(r_i L)/r_i]. This is a computable finite-error witness when
the forcing, rates, and reference are calibrated. It gives no uniform positive
bound over arbitrarily small/narrow inputs. If observations allow simultaneous
mode bounds |x_i|<=eta_i, return within those bounds is excluded when D_g exceeds
the right-hand side of (6) with eta_i. Initial-state and model errors enlarge
eta_i; a scalar proxy bound is not a mode bound without the observation gate.

For the constructive protocol, suppose it uses estimated qhat_i and scale a_0,
with true q_i in [qhat_i-d_i,qhat_i+d_i] contained in (0,1). Ignoring other errors,

 |x_i(L)| <= |a_0| (1-qhat_i+d_i) d_i
               product_(j!=i)(|qhat_i-qhat_j|+d_i).           (7)

For additional pulse-amplitude errors |delta a_k|<=eta_a at the same true equal
widths, positivity of the recursion adds at most
eta_a(1-q_i^(N+1))<=eta_a to the residual. This is a bound on mode prediction
within the frozen model, not a data-derived confidence statement. Rate intervals,
correct mode count/visibility, clock-width intervals, and observation-map
uncertainty must actually be acquired. Missed modes can retain residuals even
with exact cancellation of every fitted mode. Equation (7) does not cover
unequal/uncertain widths silently treated as the same q_i.

6. Exact examples and measured consequences

Mean return without mode return: q_1=1/2, q_2=3/4, equal mean weights, two pulse
amplitudes (1,-7/12). The final mode pair is (-1/24,+1/24): its mean is zero,
while the contrast is -1/12. The common clock and perfectly linear passive
relaxation have not failed. A field coupled to that contrast has not returned.
This is standard hidden relaxation memory in an explicit control form, not a
new physical explanation of a named glass.

Full two-mode return: the normalized three pulses (4/5,-1,3/10) give zero final
states for q=1/2 and 3/4. An unmodeled q=1/4 mode retains 3/40. Scale all values
by a physically admissible small temperature amplitude before interpreting them.

An operational alternative to inferring microscopic state from one endpoint is
to record its subsequent fixed-reference relaxation. With N finite calibrated
rates and a calibrated mean observable Y(v)=sum_i b_i x_i(L)exp(-r_i v), all
b_i nonzero, the N values at v=0,delta,...,(N-1)delta give a nonsingular weighted
Vandermonde observation matrix in the distinct q_i. They determine these N
visible residuals in exact arithmetic. With errors |delta Y|<=eta in infinity
norm, the mode error is <=||V^(-1)||_infinity eta, plus model/clock errors.
Ill-conditioning and unobserved modes cannot be removed by calling this a state
measurement. One scalar endpoint, by comparison, supplies just one constraint.
For the two-mode mean-return example above, the subsequent mean at v=m delta is
Y_m=[(3/4)^m-(1/2)^m]/48: Y_0=0 but Y_1=1/192. This is conventional Kovacs-type
memory, with an explicit finite-control/observation contract.

For a continuous spectrum the analogous identifiability statement requires a
finite signed residual measure nu(dr)=b_r x_r(L)mu(dr) with an injective Laplace
observation. If its full future trace int exp(-rv)nu(dr) is zero for every v>=0,
Laplace uniqueness gives nu=0. Nonzero b on the visible support then gives
mode return there. A finite noisy tail does not establish this infinite trace.
This observable statement still does not make the mean an instantaneous
microscopic field proxy or close the coherent-XPCS observation gate.

7. Classical infinite-discrete criterion and finite-resolution control

The finite-accumulation condition in Theorem A is sufficient, not a complete
classification. For an increasing unbounded sequence of distinct positive rates
r_n, Laurent Schwartz's interval Muntz Theorem III, Section 12, printed pages
60--61 of Etude des sommes d'exponentielles reelles (1943), gives the sharper
classical dichotomy. With u=L-s and f(u)=theta(L-u), set x=exp(-u), so

  int_0^L exp(-r_n u)f(u)du
   = int_(exp(-L))^1 x^(r_n) h(x)dx,   h(x)=f(-log x)/x.

If sum_n 1/r_n diverges, the powers x^(r_n) are dense in continuous functions
on this interval, which stays away from zero. Orthogonality of the finite signed
measure h(x)dx implies theta=0. If the sum converges, their span is non-dense
in L^2 on the interval. A nonzero real L^2 orthogonal vector h supplies a
nonzero L^2, hence L^1, exact-return input f(u)=exp(-u)h(exp(-u)). For example
r_n=n^2 admits a mathematical nonzero return input, whereas r_n=n does not.
Repeated copies of one rate add no independent constraint.

This does not produce a finite-pulse return of an infinite spectrum. By applying
Theorem B to every finite subset, any such nonzero return input has unbounded
sign complexity: no finite alternating sign partition is possible. Nor does
ordinary smoothness by itself forbid it. Apply the L^2 existence result on a
half-duration interval, extend the input by zero, and convolve with a nonzero
smooth compactly supported bump inside the other half. Laplace transforms
multiply, preserving all selected zeros. The convolution is nonzero, smooth and
compactly supported inside (0,L), and may be scaled to any positive amplitude
and slew bounds. It still has infinitely many sign changes. This is existence
in exact function spaces, not an acquired or finitely specified thermal waveform.

A finite-resolution hardware promise yields a useful separate cost. Suppose
theta starts and ends at zero, is absolutely continuous in laboratory time,
|dtheta/dt|<=v, and EVERY admitted nonzero sign lobe reaches at least amplitude
a>0 in magnitude. A waveform with k sign changes has k+1 alternating lobes.
Pick their consecutive extrema. The variation along 0, those extrema, 0 is at
least 2a(k+1), so

  v T_lab >= TV(theta) >= 2a(k+1).

Thus nontrivial full return of N distinct modes under those promises requires

  T_lab >= 2a(N+1)/v.                                      (8)

Increasing reparameterization preserves forcing order and total variation, so
this laboratory slew cost does not require a known clock SPEED. The mode model
and finite material duration still must hold. The minimum-lobe-amplitude promise
is essential: infinitely many lobes of vanishing amplitude evade (8). Finite
noise does not establish that an unmeasured waveform contains no such lobes.
This is a conditional actuator/control-resolution cost, not a universal aging
bound, an entropy-production statement, or a claim that heat is proportional to
variation. The equal-width construction's separate amplitude costs remain those
of Section 4.

Executable thermal_return_checks.py verifies the polynomial construction for
N=1,...,12, exact absence of any nonzero <=N equal-width pulse solution, the
mean-return example, the extra-mode residual, the total variation identity,
and explicit rational cases of the parameter/amplitude uncertainty bound. These
checks validate algebra in synthetic finite models. They are not acquired modes,
material measurements, or a proof of continuous support in a real specimen.

Operational consequence for an experiment: first calibrate a competent
small-amplitude TN/anelastic model, clock law, and independent observation map.
Freeze several thermal histories with successively more sign alternations and
predict all measured residual channels, including their uncertainties. A returned
mean plus a residual microscopic channel does not select plastic event dose over
distributed anelastic memory. A calibrated lower bound of N independently visible
rates makes a claimed nontrivial full-mode return with fewer than N sign changes
a falsification of this frozen single-input model (or its observation/control
calibration). Conversely, a scattering echo alone does not certify all-mode
return because of intensity invariances. Apply the exact collective observation
map and finite-beam/affine controls; do not substitute a self scattering factor.

In the Vit4 simultaneous mean-XRD/XPCS setting, mean FSDP and microscopic density
correlations are actual measured variables, but the mode-to-displacement map and
continuous visible spectrum are not currently acquired. The present structural
result identifies what must be learned before a thermal echo can decide between
the alternatives. It does not explain the reported data by itself.
