5. THE FULL PHYSICAL SECOND-ORDER SOURCE DECIDES THE OPERATION

For a periodic drive, let Q(t) be anti-Hermitian and solve

 V+[H0,Q]-i hbar dQ/dt=0.

For H(g,t)=H0+gV(t)+g^2W(t), transformation by exp[gQ(t)] gives

 H_transformed=H0+g^2{W+(1/2)[V,Q]}+O(g^3).

The average of this quadratic term is a candidate coherent interaction;
oscillatory terms, resonances, endpoints, and long-time errors must still be
handled. If the supplied physical family is an exact time-dependent unitary
image of H0 including its inertial term, its compulsory W=-(1/2)[V,Q] and
the apparent interaction vanishes. A vanishing linear absorption amplitude
alone cannot distinguish a useful actuator from a coordinate change.

The larger attack used B=(ad_H0^2-omega^2)A at frequency omega, hbar=1.
It cancels every linear matrix element at transfer +/-omega. For
H0=-h sumX+J sumZZ, A=sumZ,

 B=(4h^2-omega^2)sumZ+4hJ sum(XZ+ZX).

If B were independently supplied with W=0, its quadratic term would contain
8h^2J sum(ZZ-XX), a new XX interaction. But the easy physical construction,
tilting the native Ising axis by theta=4hg cos(omega t), has mandatory

Its average cancels the entire hoped-for new XX/ZZ interaction. Only a
one-body field correction remains. Independent positive/negative tilted

5b. A REUSABLE OPERATION TEST FOR THE NEXT SOURCE FAMILY

In a regime where a weak-coupling limit has actually been justified, write
the bright branch's second-order level shift as c^dagger Q c-i c^dagger Gamma c,
with Gamma>=0. Q must include the physical quadratic W, not only virtual
linear-source terms. At fixed phase theta, its limiting loss exponent is
2|theta| c^dagger Gamma c/|c^dagger Qc|. The supplied quadratic source action
is proportional to |theta| c^dagger Cc/|c^dagger Qc|. These ratios do not
improve just by weakening g.

Zero leading absorption with nonzero useful response requires that Q have
a nonzero restriction to ker Gamma, after removing the operation's trivial
global/native response directions. A positive definite acquired Gamma gives
a finite loss/phase tradeoff through generalized eigenvalues. This is an
operational design test, not a claim that Born/Markov assumptions or the
response matrices have been acquired. The code problem retains the whole
matrix-valued loss instrument; branch survival is generally input dependent.
Positive absorption measurements can constrain Gamma; dispersive information
and the full source family are still required to acquire Q. Equally equipped
tomography and system identification receive the same structural model and
control set. This test prevents an absorption null from being promoted to a
new interaction without examining what is actually left.

There is also a precise identification obstruction. Two physical source
families can have the same H0 and the same complete first derivative V, hence
the same leading absorption data, yet different quadratic W. Their order-g^2
coherent operations can differ by order one on the phase time g^-2. The
linear cross-axis Ising source and the axis-tilt completion provide the
explicit example above: one has the new XX term, the other cancels it.
Consequently leading absorption/rank data, even if exact, cannot certify the
coherent interaction. A full source-curvature model or phase-sensitive
forward information is needed. This complements the retained forward-
instrument logic; it does not deny equally equipped tomography its actual
source knowledge or reference controls.
