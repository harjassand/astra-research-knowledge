FINITE-POPULATION BOSONIC SIMULATION: ENSEMBLE AND NORMALIZATION BOUNDARIES
27 October 2026

Input contract
--------------
There are M<infinity lattice sites, two bosonic flavors b,c, beta>0, and
separately conserved particle numbers. The source-specific Hamiltonian is

 H = sum_ij [b_i^dag K_ij b_j + c_i^dag K_ij^* c_j]
     + U1 sum_i (n_bi-n_ci)^2 + U2 sum_i (n_bi+n_ci)^2,          (P1)

where K=K^dag, U1>=0, U2<=0. Source arXiv:2610.31541v1, Eq. S157,
has real frustrated triangular-lattice K and additionally allows a pairing
term. All canonical claims below set that pairing term Delta=0. They do not
apply to generic Kramers matrices mixing the two flavors, to unequal fixed
populations, or to the number-nonconserving paired model.

L is a finite positive integer and delta=beta/L. Let phi_i,l and psi_i,l
be independent standard real Gaussians. With g=-U2>=0, the HS factor is

 exp[-delta U1 (n_b-n_c)^2 + delta g (n_b+n_c)^2]
 = E exp[sqrt(2 delta g) phi (n_b+n_c)
          + i sqrt(2 delta U1) psi (n_b-n_c)].                 (P2)

The Gaussian measure is positive. At fixed fields the two single-particle
propagations are B and B^*. Symmetric kinetic splitting is used in the code.
These are ordinary HS and canonical-AFQMC operations; see Shen et al. 2020,
2023. The results below specify which ensemble makes their composition valid.


Proposition 1. The printed attractive grand-canonical Hamiltonian is unstable.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
For U2<0 and Delta=0, Tr exp[-beta(H-mu N)] is infinite for every finite real
mu, every M>=1 and beta>0, with the uncut local bosonic Fock spaces.

Proof. Fix one site j. The normalized Fock vectors v_n with n b-bosons and
n c-bosons on j and zero elsewhere are mutually orthogonal, lie in distinct
total-particle sectors, and obey

 <v_n,(H-mu N)v_n> = 2n K_jj + 4 U2 n^2 - 2 mu n.             (P3)

The U1 term vanishes. Every off-diagonal hopping term has zero expectation.
The negative quadratic term dominates the linear term. In each fixed-number
sector H is a finite Hermitian matrix. Convexity of exp gives

 <v_n,exp[-beta(H-mu N)]v_n>
 >= exp[-beta(2n K_jj+4 U2 n^2-2 mu n)].                       (P4)

The positive trace is bounded below by the sum of (P4) over n. That sum
diverges. This is an exact ensemble instability, not a statement about the
size of a Monte Carlo error bar. A finite-occupation reference or a physical
particle-number restriction changes the ensemble and must be specified.


Proposition 2. The uncut positive determinant measure is non-normalizable.
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Assume U1>0, U2<0, finite M,L, and use the printed number-conserving formula

 W_reg(phi,psi) = |det(I-B(phi,psi))|^(-2).                    (P5)

Then its integral against the full standard Gaussian HS measure is infinite,
for every finite real mu. This applies directly to the attractive-density
source family, including its 3x3 periodic lattice; it is not limited to a
one-site illustration.

Proof. Orthogonally decompose each of the two M L-dimensional field arrays
into its all-site/all-time uniform component and its orthogonal residual:

 Phi = (M L)^(-1/2) sum_i,l phi_i,l,
 Psi = (M L)^(-1/2) sum_i,l psi_i,l.                           (P6)

Phi and Psi are independent standard Gaussians and independent of all residual
coordinates. A scalar multiple of the single-particle identity commutes with
every hopping/field factor, so, conditional on arbitrary residual fields,

 B = exp[beta mu + a Phi + i b Psi] R,
 a = sqrt(2 beta |U2|/M)>0, b = sqrt(2 beta U1/M)>0.           (P7)

R is invertible: it is a finite product of invertible exponentials. Choose any
eigenvalue lambda!=0 of R. There are finite real Phi_0,Psi_0 such that

 exp[beta mu + a Phi_0 + i b Psi_0] lambda = 1.               (P8)

Writing x=Phi-Phi_0, y=Psi-Psi_0, the determinant has a zero of some algebraic
multiplicity m>=1. In a sufficiently small neighborhood it is a nonzero
analytic factor times (1-exp[a x+i b y])^m. Consequently, for some c>0,

 W_reg >= c (x^2+y^2)^(-m).                                  (P9)

The conditional Gaussian density has a strictly positive lower bound on a
small disk centered at (Phi_0,Psi_0). Integrating (P9) over that disk gives
integral_0^epsilon r^(1-2m) dr = infinity, including logarithmic divergence
at m=1. The determinant zeros themselves have measure zero, but their
neighborhoods have infinite weight. Every residual configuration has such a
root. Tonelli's theorem extends the conditional divergence to the full
positive integral. QED.

The normalization failure is not repaired by subtracting a finite part from
each occupation series and then using (P5) as an ordinary positive density.
Positive divergent contributions cannot cancel in that integral. This result
does not contradict the source's U2=0, trace-convergent bound (Eqs. S160-S163).
The premise of Sec. IV that the underlying GC trace is finite also fails in
the printed U2<0 benchmark, by Proposition 1. A different model or a different
explicitly defined regularized ensemble requires a separate justification.

Proposition 3. Total-number projection need not retain positive weights.
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Even a one-site, number-conserving conjugate-flavor model supplies a negative
total-N canonical coefficient for every chosen N>=1.

Proof. Let the two fixed-field eigenvalues be exp(i theta),exp(-i theta).
For real fugacity 0<z<1 the GC determinant weight is

 1/[(1-z exp(i theta))(1-z exp(-i theta))] > 0.                (P10)

Its coefficient of z^N is

 c_N(theta)=sum_k=0^N exp[i(2k-N)theta]
           = sin[(N+1)theta]/sin(theta).                     (P11)

Choosing theta=3 pi/[2(N+1)] makes (P11) negative. For N=2 and theta=pi/2,
the three occupation contributions are -1,+1,-1, totaling -1. The balanced
(1,1) contribution is instead +1. At U2=0 these phases occur in the ordinary
repulsive relative-density HS transformation, on open field neighborhoods.
The physical one-site total-N=2 partition is 1+2 exp(-4 beta U1)>0; individual
HS coefficients still have a sign problem. Positivity of a function on real
positive fugacity does not imply positivity of each Taylor coefficient. QED.

Thus the source's proposed canonical direction needs a conserved-sector
qualification. No claim is made that a general canonical formulation is new.


Proposition 4. Fixed balanced populations give a valid pole-free operation.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fix N_b=N_c=n<infinity in (P1). Let

 h_n(B) = Tr_{Sym^n(C^M)} Gamma_n(B)
        = [z^n] det(I-z B)^(-1).                              (P12)

The coefficient is a finite polynomial of degree n in B's entries. For every
field configuration, including nonnormal or defective B,

 W_{n,n} = h_n(B) h_n(B^*) = |h_n(B)|^2 >= 0.                (P13)

It has no determinant pole. Its positive Gaussian integral is the canonical
finite-L Trotter trace and tends to Tr_{n,n} exp(-beta H) as L tends to infinity.
This partition is finite even when U2<0.

Proof. The n-particle Fock space of one flavor has dimension
D_n=binomial(n+M-1,n). The trace of the symmetric-power representation is a
polynomial and, for a triangular B, equals the sum of all degree-n eigenvalue
monomials. Triangularization and polynomial continuation give (P12) for every
B, without diagonalizability assumptions. Complex conjugation gives (P13).
At any finite L the product of finite-dimensional Gaussian HS exponentials
is bounded by an exponential of a finite linear combination of absolute
field coordinates. This bound and every fixed power of it are integrable
against a Gaussian. One can therefore integrate the finite-dimensional HS
identity before taking the trace. It recovers exactly the product of
exp(-delta K/2), exp(-delta V), exp(-delta K/2) in that sector. Matrix-norm
Trotter convergence in this finite-dimensional sector gives the asserted
limit. A fixed particle sector is a physical ensemble restriction, not a
regularization of the divergent unrestricted GC partition. QED.

One implementation uses the ordinary Newton/Borrmann recursion

 h_0=1,  n h_n = sum_k=1^n Tr(B^k) h_{n-k}.                  (P14)

The code evaluates (P14) on matrix powers rather than assuming an eigenvector
basis is well-conditioned. Its per-field arithmetic work is
O((L+n)M^3+n^2), plus the Gaussian samples. This is not a bound on sampling
complexity, required precision, long-product stability, or total bit cost.
The known canonical recursions and paired-conjugate positivity are the strong
ordinary comparator; they are not inventions claimed by this packet.


Proposition 5. Uniform-mode integration and finite-variance thermal energy.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the fixed (n,n) sector, the spatially uniform relative-density HS mode in
every slice cancels exactly from the weight. Integrating each spatially
uniform total-density mode gives the known scalar

 Z_{n,n,L} = exp(4 beta |U2| n^2/M) E_perp |h_n(B_perp)|^2.   (P15)

Proof. In each slice write phi_l=x_l 1/sqrt(M)+phi_l^perp and likewise
psi_l=y_l 1/sqrt(M)+psi_l^perp, with independent standard-normal x_l,y_l.
Homogeneity gives h_n(sB)=s^n h_n(B). The weight acquires
exp[(2n sqrt(2 delta |U2|)/sqrt(M)) sum_l x_l]; the y_l phase cancels.
The Gaussian moment-generating function gives (P15). This is ordinary
Gaussian integration/mean-field subtraction, rather than a new QMC class.

It also gives an exact sampling-cost identity for raw IID Gaussian importance
integration. Define R=E W^2/(E W)^2. Then

 R_raw = exp(8 beta |U2| n^2/M) R_perp.                       (P16)

Consequently a strictly positive weight can have exponentially expensive raw
importance sampling. The factor in (P16) is removable exactly; it is not a
lower bound for optimized samplers. On a single site it is the whole cost:
R_raw=exp(8 beta |U2| n^2), while uniform integration is exact and noiseless.

For thermal energy, let f=h_n(B_perp), dot f=partial_beta f at fixed L and
standard Gaussian residual fields. Then

 E_L = 4 U2 n^2/M - E_perp[2 Re(f^* dot f)]/E_perp|f|^2.      (P17)

The derivative can be propagated through the finite matrix product and (P14),
as implemented. It does not require taking a singular resolvent. Under an
ideal importance distribution proportional to Gaussian*|f|^2, the random part
of the energy estimator is -2 Re(dot f/f). At zeros of f it is defined
arbitrarily on a set of sampling probability zero. Its second moment obeys

 E_target[(-2 Re(dot f/f))^2]
 <= 4 E_perp|dot f|^2 / E_perp|f|^2 < infinity.                (P18)

For finite M,n,L,beta, dot f is a finite sum of Gaussian-coordinate
polynomials times exponentials of linear field combinations, so its squared
norm is Gaussian-integrable. Thus this thermal estimator has finite variance
at that finite discretization. Arbitrary higher-order correlation estimators
do not inherit (P18); products of derivatives divided by |f|^2 can have new
singularities. No general mixing-time or asymptotic sample-complexity bound is
asserted. The numerical replay uses IID Gaussian proposals and ratio-of-means
integration, so it has no Markov-chain autocorrelation. Its reported standard
errors are finite-sample, floating-point Monte Carlo estimates, not directed
computer certificates for an ideal continuum Gaussian law.
