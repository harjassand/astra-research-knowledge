# CrSBr depth readout and the read-damage interface

**Status: NOT_ACHIEVED as a physical operation.** Actual multislice detector templates contain conditional single-layer information in 13 nm CrSBr. They do not establish that a real instrument can acquire layer identity before reading changes the specimen. Actual read error, any-move retention, defect-model coverage and full-cycle advantage remain UNKNOWN.

The useful route is ordinary finite-hypothesis likelihood testing of full diffraction counts, with calibrated nuisance models and a model-rejection gate. General 3D reconstruction is a stronger task. Conversely, a favorable self-template classifier is insufficient: an uncalibrated phonon sensitivity model makes the rigid classifier confidently report wrong depths. This is a computational interface result, without a novelty or practical-advantage claim.

[Klein et al., Nature (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10431-9) demonstrate substantial projected Cr vacancy/interstitial-column engineering. Their [peer-review response, PDF p10](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-026-10431-9/MediaObjects/41586_2026_10431_MOESM2_ESM.pdf) excludes demonstrated control of exact Cr number and depth in a column. Supplement S8A shows that repeated imaging moves the engineered interstitials again. These limits do not invalidate the demonstrated column operation.

**Executed discrimination.** The crystal and unrelaxed defect construction come from the authors' [pinned repository](https://github.com/KleinAtomLab/Mesoscale-atomic-engineering/tree/6844cc568271d448704a0eb6ee55ddfd5246d242). A six by four lateral cell, repeated 16 times vertically, contains 768 Cr atoms. The 17 hypotheses are pristine, or exactly one specified Cr transferred by +b/2 in-plane and −c/4 in depth at one of 16 column sites. All other atoms are known. The 16 defect states have identical projected elemental composition.

abTEM 1.0.11 propagates a 200 keV, 32 mrad probe through 1 Å slices. Forty-five positions use 0.35 Å by 0.5 Å scan cells, a nominal 7.875 Å² raster. Detector probabilities are squared complex-wave amplitudes divided by incoming probe power. Cropped probabilities are not renormalized. Counts up to 100 mrad are retained. All comparator processing receives the identical specimen, prior, dose and detector templates. Annular measurements are coarse grainings of the counts, not denied useful priors.

For state i, position s and pixel k, independent Poisson means are D a p(i,s,k), where D is nominal scan dose and a=0.175 Å². Define `B(i,j)=(a/2) sum_(s,k)(sqrt(p_i)-sqrt(p_j))²`. Maximum-likelihood classification satisfies `max_i P_i(error) <= max_i sum_(j!=i) exp(-D B(i,j))`. The sufficient doses below solve this worst-state error bound at 0.005, not merely a uniform-prior average.

A necessary dose comes from the closest restricted binary pair: its equal-prior optimal error is at least `[1-sqrt(1-exp(-2DB))]/2`. Thus every classifier with error ≤α in each state needs `D >= -log[4α(1-α)]/(2B)`, with α=0.005. Necessary and sufficient doses are distinct bounds, not an estimated exact threshold or an experimental resolution.

The first row costs about 11,526 incident electrons. Its closest pair is layers 15/16, 7.99416 Å apart. Exact compound-Poisson Monte Carlo draws detector events from these templates, without Gaussian-noise approximation. At D=1,463.6 it finds 2/3,000 and 0/3,000 binary errors; separate 95% Clopper–Pearson upper limits are 0.00241 and 0.00123. The analytical 17-way bound remains the multiclass statement; this binary check certifies no real specimen.

**Robustness and failure checks.**

- A 512 grid changes the closest-pair full-pixel B from 0.004005 to 0.004377, about 9.3%. This is a pair check, not global convergence.
- Periodic lateral boundaries repeat the defect every 21.04 by 19.08 Å. A 12 by 8 cell doubles these distances at the original real-space sampling. On identical fixed 2.5 mrad angular bins, B changes from 0.002655 to 0.002486, a 6.36% decrease. This pair signal is not solely a small-cell replica artifact. Two sizes do not establish the isolated-defect limit.
- A two-depth composite classifier receives 12 actual templates: layers 15/16, each nominal or with defocus ±8 Å, registration +0.1 Å along b, tilt 1 mrad, or 17 rather than 16 layers, one change at a time. Its minimum cross-class B is 0.003823 and sufficient worst-template dose 1,385.9 e/Å². It covers neither all depths nor continuous/joint nuisances.
- The phonon sensitivity averages four multislice intensities with Gaussian atomic displacement σ=0.1 Å and common random seeds between hypotheses. This σ is not measured for CrSBr; four configurations are not a converged thermal ensemble. Only pristine and layers 10,11,15,16 were checked. The source mixture averages nine neighboring probe positions with specified Gaussian weights, then bins detector pixels 2 by 2 and thins counts by 0.5; 21 center scan positions remain. These are explicit sensitivity settings, not source/detector calibration.
- Forcing the rigid 17-state classifier to interpret those phonon truths fails. Minimum expected Poisson divergence assigns pristine, layer 15 and layer 16 to layer 5. At D=3,834.1, exact count simulation produces 1,000/1,000 wrong forced reports for pristine and separately for layer 15, each with error interval [0.9963,1]. This is model mismatch, not indistinguishability: the poor fit can be rejected, and a calibrated phonon library still has conditional depth information.

No relaxed defect-coordinate library was acquired. Source supplement S9C/S31 describes adjacent-layer relaxation and feedback, making the unrelaxed-state prior substantive. The exactly-one-defect and all-other-atoms-known prior is also charged: extra Cr moves, Br/S loss, contamination and uncertain prepared atom number are outside the 17 classes. An unconstrained write-verification claim requires acquired rejection/coverage bounds for those states. This test does not supply them.

**Read versus damage.** Define q(D) for each complete protocol as the probability of no additional atomic move or damage anywhere in its exposed read region, including probe tails and collateral atoms. This is not only the selected Cr's survival. q(D) was not measured or identified. Optional notation `q(D)=exp(-D sigma_eff 1e-8)` expresses a protocol-dependent aggregate coefficient in barn, without fitting a microscopic rate.

Requiring q(D)≥0.995 gives `sigma_eff <= -log(0.995)/(D 1e-8)`, the table's ceiling. SI population cross sections, flux conversion, raw acquisition clock and any further division by 16 do not calibrate this interface and were not used. Different beam protocols can have different aggregate coefficients.

Under a common pre-first-move static counterfactual, `P(correct and unchanged) >= 1-epsilon_read-epsilon_move`; the illustrative 0.005+0.005 split gives 0.99 without assuming independent errors and damage. Model-coverage error needs its own extra budget. The static calculation assumes zero coverage error; the mismatch test prevents transferring that assumption into a physical claim. Repeated weak-write/read cycles must accumulate every native-state read's dose and risk, including reads before the first successful write. This is not perfect first-event stopping or a free feedback channel.

If independently correct any-move labels and identically prepared replicas existed, zero changes in 598 trials would bound change probability below 0.005 at 95% confidence for one condition. Covering 17 states separately with Bonferroni familywise 95% confidence needs 1,163 zero-event replicas per state, 19,771 total. No such preparation, labels or experiment were acquired. Pooling unlabeled layers or trusting an unchanged projected image does not establish worst-state retention.

**Ordinary alternatives and paid costs.** [Weile et al., PRX 15, 021080 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08100v1) classify bilayer CrSBr defects with 200 keV HAADF at a total 9×10⁴ e/Å². Labels combine possible layers; simulations average the two sites. Blind acquisition, focus post-selection and class averaging are paid parts of that workflow. Its SI S3 shows new Cr defects during imaging and lists 1.8×10⁴ e/Å² per displayed two-frame average. This demonstrates a read-damage concern, not a calibrated per-atom probability.

[Depth-sensitive low-angle ADF](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02640) already uses channeling and selected angles for depth inference. The 20–40 mrad test grants exact templates and calibration. The retained 78–100 mrad computation is a truncated annulus and does not rank the source's full 78–200 mrad HAADF, optimized through-focus methods or all conventional protocols.

[TCMEP](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56499-1) reports about 0.9 nm interface resolution in SrTiO3 at 8.4×10⁵ e/Å² with four tilts at ±1° and ±2°. Four 200×200 scans at 1 ms/frame imply 160 seconds of recorded acquisition before tilt/alignment overhead. The paper also reports a 15-minute reference-defect preparation step and about 30 hours on an A100 for that reconstruction. Its 300 keV/25 mrad settings, material and task differ. A 0.9 nm resolution metric neither proves nor disproves known-site discrimination at 0.8 nm spacing. [Multi-focus ptychography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01141) and [ptychographic tomography](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43634-z) strengthen the ordinary comparator set; no matched CrSBr retention demonstration from them was acquired. A full-count likelihood comparator with the same priors gets the same conditional information as the proposed route.

Present detectors do not force 1 ms acquisitions. [EMPAD-G2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.05889) reports 10 kHz, the [4D Camera](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11961) 87 kHz, and [event-driven Timepix3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02864) shorter scan dwell. Their best attributes cannot be combined into one assumed instrument. At 100 µs/frame and the declared scan-cell area, the 1,464-dose case requires about 0.410 pA, while the 40,460-dose case requires about 11.34 pA. At 20 pA their respective total beam-on electron budgets are about 92 µs and 1.19 ms. Detector noise/DQE, dead time, dynamic range, probe changes with current, focus and registration remain additional costs; none were acquired on CrSBr.

The recorded simulations propagated 55 specimen configurations in about 16.5 minutes summed across runs, with two-thread limits and the existing Python environment used read-only. Sources, metadata, templates and exact-count tests remain local. No physical experiment, paid service, outreach, CUA, public write or package installation was performed. A usable operation must pay for state preparation, beam/detector calibration, target focus/registration exposure, source/phonon/relaxation coverage, all read acquisitions, fitting/reconstruction, model rejection, independent retention validation, and recovery/replacement. Those full-cycle costs remain UNKNOWN.

The outcome is a conditional low-dose diffraction route and a concrete calibration obstruction. There is neither a general prohibition of single-Cr-layer readout nor a verified retained layer-read operation.
