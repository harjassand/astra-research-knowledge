# A retained, verified operation instead of direct-perfect writing

Failure of an illustrative99% first-event target does not exclude ordinary
heralding and retry. Suppose each attempt begins at the same verified native
state, proposes exactly one defect at the target with probability p, survives
perfect layer readout with probability q, and cleanly resets a rejected proposal
with probability r. All failed reads/resets terminate the run irreversibly.
These regenerative probabilities and one-defect/no-collateral contract are
unacquired assumptions, not conversions of fluence into physical fidelity.

Ordinary geometric-series reasoning gives

    S = qp / [1-q(1-p)r],    E attempts = 1/[1-q(1-p)r].

A goal S>=s requires r>=[1-qp/s]/[q(1-p)]. Even perfect reset needs
q>=s/[p+s(1-p)]. `heralded_interface.py`/`HERALDED_RESULTS.json` execute these
exact rational gates for explicitly declared illustrative inputs.

At p=.28,q=.995, even r=1 gives only .98237 eventual success. A .99 goal
needs q>=.997180 with perfect reset. At p=.68,q=.995, reset needs r>=.994239;
at q=.9999 the same proposal needs r>=.978848. False acceptance, write damage,
registry errors and unrecognized changes add costs. Repeated confirmation does
not suppress common calibration error as independent noise; extra dose counts.

The source's imaging-based erasure randomizes interstitial sites. Projected
reverse occupancy does not acquire a native layer reset. A conditional
counter-propagating lower-energy rollback candidate remains to be acquired,
including displacement thresholds, capture, other-species damage and flip/registry.
The missing operation is a joint proposal/read/rollback kernel on the same
specimen and envelope. Favorable component models do not establish that cycle.
An equally informed ordinary controller gets the same retry. No new principle,
physical no-go, or practical advantage is claimed.

## Finite pulses expose a stronger missing stopping interface

The preceding model silently proposes exactly one event. Separately, let a
write pulse generate a Poisson number with mean x and target fraction p. Each
full read survives with q,classifies perfectly,and resets a single wrong edit
with r=1. Zero-event pulses repeat; every multi-event pulse is detected but
terminates in failure. Fixed Poisson hazards after an edit, perfect rejection
and terminal damage are additional, unacquired assumptions.

S(x)=q p x exp(-x)/[1-q exp(-x)(1+(1-p)x)]. Its derivative has the sign of
1-x-q exp(-x), with one root in(0,1) for0<q<1: the global fixed-pulse optimum.
There S=p(1-x)/[p+(1-p)x]. To reach s, put x_s=p(1-s)/[p+s(1-p)]; read
survival must satisfy q>=(1-x_s)exp(x_s).

At declared p=.68,q=.995 the best mean is .0968119 and eventual success only
.863833, even with perfect reset/classification. Reaching .99 requires
q>=.999976625 per complete read in this model. These are not measured CrSBr
fidelities or a physical no-go. Weak pulses demand many native-state reads;
strong pulses create multiple edits outside the17-state template prior.
Continuous event-triggered stopping could change this, but its dose, signal,
latency and changed-state kernel must be acquired. Pulse results are in the JSON.
