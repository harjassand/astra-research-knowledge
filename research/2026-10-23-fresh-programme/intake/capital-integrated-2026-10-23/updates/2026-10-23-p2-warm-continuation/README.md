## Selected evidence

- The source gives an exactly gapped, bath-coupled independent-boson model with nonzero second-order response and zero zero-temperature subgap internal absorption and equilibrium noise. This is a scoped counterexample to a universal pointwise internal-loss floor derived from causality, passivity, or fluctuation-dissipation alone. It does not establish a practical converter, finite-temperature pointwise transparency, zero total electromagnetic extinction, or a refrigerator.
- The positive microscopic heat/loss lower bound is conditional on specified spectral loss floors, field constraints, and the source’s anharmonicity assumptions. Without those floors, the source does not infer a universal positive bound.
- The transport derivation retains thermal forward/backward bosonic fluctuations even for a number-preserving converter. Its noise/transport inequality applies under the stated independent thermal input, coherent-pump, resolved-port, and stable rotating-wave assumptions. It is not an internal material-absorption floor.
- The pump-cycle derivation conditions on the stated weak-coupling, isolation, control-capacity, and reset model. It charges reset work for the entropy of a freely evolving pump; it supplies no calibrated physical pump budget.
- For the source’s 2017 Josephson-device tuple at its stated setting, the report gives a converter-only extraction of about 1.7982 aW and net conversion work of about 1.2576 aW. The attempted passive two-series-RLC interface fails its full-frequency thermal-path check: the open-device-port model gives 59.0615 aW of hot–cold bypass near 4.890417 GHz, about 32.8 times the modeled extraction. This is a model result for that interface, not an assembled-device measurement. The distinct-port Josephson-ring architecture removes that specific cancellation loop; its full isolation and pump budget remain open.
